Upper moderate deviation probabilities for the maximum of a branching random walk
Louis Chataignier, Lianghui Luo

Abstract
Consider M n M_{n} the maximal position at generation n n of a supercritical branching random walk. In 2013, Aïdékon [ 1 ] obtained and described the convergence in law, as time n n goes to infinity, of M n − m n M_{n}-m_{n} , where m n m_{n} is an explicit function. Equivalently, he identified the limit of ℙ ⁡ ( M n > m n + x ) \mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x) , for any x ∈ ℝ x\in\mathbb{R} . More recently, Luo [ 33 ] gave an asymptotic equivalent for the upper large deviation probability, that is ℙ ⁡ ( M n > m n + x ​ n ) \mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+xn) , for x > 0 x>0 . In this work, we study an intermediate regime, called upper moderate deviation . We obtain, under close-to-optimal integrability conditions, an asymptotic equivalent for ℙ ⁡ ( M n > m n + x n ) \mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n}) , where x n x_{n} is such that x n → ∞ x_{n}\to\infty and x n = O ⁡ ( n ) x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n}) . Our proof is based on a strategy due to Bramson, Ding, and Zeitouni [ 14 ] . As a byproduct, we obtain information about the typical behavior of particles contributing to such deviations. Finally, we apply our main result to show the convergence in law of the centered maximum of a two-speed branching random walk in the mean regime and describe its limit.

Introduction

The branching random walk is a natural extension of the Galton–Watson tree, with the addition of a spatial attribute for each particle.
In one dimension, it is constructed as follows.
At time
n=0

At time
n=1

\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{L}

These new particles then restart the process, independently of each other, scattering children according to independent copies of
\mathcal{L}

The system continues indefinitely, with possible extinction if there is no particle at some generation.

Given a particle

Our object of interest is the maximal displacement

1.1
Main result

Here is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), (
4
), and let
(x_{n})
be any sequence such that
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})

(i)

If the branching random walk is non-lattice, then

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\sim C_{\mathrm{NL}}x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})},

(11)

C_{\mathrm{NL}}\coloneq\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\in(0,\infty).

(12)

(ii)

If the branching random walk is
(h,a)
-lattice and
x_{n}+m_{n}\in an+h\mathbb{Z}
for each

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\sim C_{\mathrm{L}}x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})},

(13)

where

C_{\mathrm{L}}\coloneq\frac{h}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{y\in(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})\cap[0,\infty)}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\in(0,\infty).

Our statements would play the same role as sharp estimates for solutions of the F–KPP equation, used e.g. to prove
[

One consequence would be a characterization of the decoration

as a branching random walk conditioned on a moderate deviation of its maximum.

1.2
Strategy of the proof

Let us discuss our strategy for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
A classical technique for studying branching processes is to compute expectations of certain functionals thanks to the many-to-one formula or the spinal decomposition (introduced in Section
2.2
below).
It allows to reduce the computation to an expectation involving only one random walk.
A naive idea for the upper bound in Theorem
1.1
would be to apply the union bound

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=n}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}}}\right],

and then the many-to-one formula.
This actually yields the correct asymptotic order, up to polynomial factors.
The expectation turns out to be inflated by an atypical event (more atypical than
\{M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}
) where some particles reach really high positions before time
n
.
As done by Mallein
[
39
]
, we can truncate the process by adding a suitable barrier: only count particles that are above
m_{n}+x_{n}
at time
n
while having stayed below
f_{n}(k)+x_{n}=m_{n}-m_{n-k}+x_{n}
for all times
k<n
.
This yields the correct asymptotic order.

To obtain an asymptotic equivalent, we need a refinement of this truncation method.
Such a refinement was developed by Bramson, Ding, and Zeitouni
[
14
]
to prove the convergence of
M_{n}-m_{n}
.
In this paper, we adapt their approach in the same way as
[
16
]
, but with additional technical difficulties due to our close-to-optimal assumptions.
We consider an intermediate time
\ell=\ell_{n}
such that
\ell\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
We denote by
\Lambda_{n,\ell}
the number of particles alive at time
n-\ell
whose ancestors have stayed below
f_{n-\ell}(k)+x_{n}
for all times
k\leq n-\ell
and have a descendant reaching
m_{n}+x_{n}
at time
n
.
Using the upper and lower bounds (
9
) and (
10
), we obtain
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\leq(1+o_{n}(1))\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]
.

Regarding the reverse inequality, note that, by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\geq\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{n,\ell}\geq 1)\geq\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]^{2}/\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}^{2}].

One idea is then to show that the first and second moments of
\Lambda_{n,\ell}
are asymptotically equivalent.
An issue is that the second moment of
\Lambda_{n,\ell}
may be too large, or even infinite, inflated by rare events where particles make too large a progeny.
To overcome this, we restrict
\Lambda_{n,\ell}
to particles that do not have “too many children too high”.
Our restriction provides a good approximation for which the above second moment method applies.

It then remains to compute an asymptotic equivalent for
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]
.
We achieve this by using results from Doney
[
23
]
and Pain
[
40
]
which provide, for a centered random walk
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with finite variance
\sigma^{2}
, asymptotic estimates of the form

\mathbb{P}(\forall k\leq n,S_{k}\leq x_{n},S_{n}\in(x_{n}-y-h,x_{n}-y])\approx C\frac{x_{n}yh}{n^{3/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})},

(16)

for some range of
y>0
and
h>0
.

Contrary to branching Brownian motion
[
16
]
, if
x_{n}/\sqrt{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=o(n)
, then, under our assumptions, the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})
cannot be described with a universal formulation such as in Theorem
1.1
.
This is due to the fact that, in this case, the left-hand side of (
16
) depends on the tail distribution of
S_{1}
.
The trajectories that give weight to this probability exhibit a full range of behaviors: it can be realized either through a collective contribution of all increments (the
Gaussian regime
), or through the contribution of a single large “jump” (the
one big jump regime
), or even through intermediate scenarios involving moderate jumps (see e.g.
[
7
]
).
In a word, there is no universality anymore.
This suggests that a higher moment condition than (
3
) is required.
We believe that it is possible to adapt the strategy of Bramson, Ding, and Zeitouni
[
14
]
for the regime where
x_{n}/\sqrt{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=o(n)
by assuming that there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that
\mathbb{E}[\sum_{|u|=1}\mathrm{e}^{\theta V(u)}]<\infty
for any
\theta\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
.
In this paper, in order to state the result under the close-to-optimal assumption (
3
), we only consider the regime where
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we first introduce the construction of a branching random walk and the spinal decomposition theorem.
Then we introduce some properties of the renewal functions and trajectory estimates of a random walk.
In Section
3
, we aim to prove the finiteness of constants
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
(though their existence is established later), and study the asymptotic behavior of
\int_{D_{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)
as
\ell\to\infty
, for appropriate domains
D_{\ell}\subset[0,\infty)
.
In Section
4
, we exclude some particle behaviors, which are much more atypical than the event
\{M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}
.
In Section
5
, we focus on proving that
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})
is asymptotically equivalent to
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]
.
In Section
6
, we give sharp upper and lower bounds for
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]
.
In Section
7
, we prove the existence of
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
, and complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Finally, we present two applications of Theorem
1.1
in Section
8
.
The first one is an identification of the region of integration that contributes most to
\int y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y
.
The second one is the convergence in law of the maximum of a two-speed branching random walk in the mean regime.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give a formal construction of branching random walks.
We then introduce the spinal decomposition theorem and the many-to-one lemma, which relate a branching random walk to a single random walk.
At the end, we gather sharp estimates for random walks.

2.1
Construction of a branching random walk

Define the set of finite sequences of positive integers

\mathbb{T}\coloneq\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{n},

with convention
\mathbb{N}^{0}\coloneq\{\varnothing\}
, where
\mathbb{N}\coloneq\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
.

Given a point process
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathcal{L}^{(u)}
be i.i.d. copies of
\mathcal{L}
.
If there exists some
\theta>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{e}^{\theta\ell}}}\right]<\infty,

which is the case with
\theta=1
under (
2
), we know that there are only finitely many atoms of
\mathcal{L}
located in
[x,\infty)
for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, we can rank the atoms, counted with their multiplicity, of
\mathcal{L}
in a non-increasing fashion.
We write
\mathcal{L}^{(u)}
as
(\ell_{i}^{(u)}:i\geq 1)
, where
(\ell_{i}^{(u)}:i\geq 1)
is a non-increasing sequence generated by the atoms in
\mathcal{L}^{(u)}
, with the convention that
\ell_{i}^{(u)}\coloneq-\infty
if
i>\mathcal{L}^{(u)}(\mathbb{R})
.
For
u\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we write
u=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(n)})
and denote by
|u|
the generation of
u
, i.e.
|u|=n
.
For
0\leq k\leq|u|
, we use
u_{k}\coloneq(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(k)})
to express the ancestor of
u
at time
k
, with the convention
u_{0}\coloneq\varnothing
.
With the help of the notation above, we can define a branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
as follows.
Let
V(\varnothing)=0
, for
u\in\mathbb{T}
such that
|u|\geq 1
,

V(u)\coloneq\sum_{i=0}^{|u|-1}\ell^{(u_{i})}_{u^{(i+1)}}.

We treat
u
such that
V(u)=-\infty
as dead particles, which do not contribute to the branching random walk.
In the rest of the article, we denote by
\{|u|=n\}
the set of particles alive at time
n
.

2.2
Spinal decomposition: a change of measure

Here we introduce the spinal decomposition.
This is an important notion for the study of branching processes that we use a lot throughout this paper.
The idea is to describe the law of the branching random walk with an infinite ray of descendants, called spine, and a change of measure.
The main theorem of this section is the spinal decomposition theorem.
Before stating it, we present a similar but more elementary tool, which is also very useful in this paper: the many-to-one formula.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathbb{P}_{a}
denote the law under which
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is a branching random walk started from
a
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{a}
denote the corresponding expectation.
We abbreviate
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
by
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}
.
Let
(S_{n}:n\geq 0)
be a random walk such that, for any non-negative measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
,

\mathbb{P}_{a}(S_{0}=a)=1\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}_{a}[h(S_{1}-S_{0})]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=1}h(V(u))\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}}\right].

We refer to
(S_{n}:n\geq 0)
as the random walk associated with the branching random walk.
Note that, under Assumptions (
2
) and (
3
), we have
\mathbb{E}[S_{1}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[S_{1}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}
.

Lemma 2.1

(Many-to-one formula, Theorem 1.1 of
[
41
]
)
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 1
and any non-negative measurable function
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty]
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=n}h(V(u_{1}),\ldots,V(u_{n}))\mathrm{e}^{V(u)-a}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[h(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})}}\right].

The many-to-one formula can be showed by induction.
It is also a consequence of the spinal decomposition theorem.
In order to state the latter, let
\mathscr{F}_{n}\coloneq\sigma(V(u):|u|\leq n)
and
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}\coloneq\sigma(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
, and define

W_{n}\coloneq\sum_{|u|=n}\mathrm{e}^{V(u)},\quad n\geq 0.

(17)

It is a martingale with respect to the filtration
(\mathscr{F}_{n}:n\geq 0)
and is usually called the (critical) additive martingale.
By Kolmogorov extension theorem, there exists a probability measure
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
such that, for any
n\geq 1
and
A\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
, we have

\bar{\mathbb{P}}(A)=\mathbb{E}[W_{n}\mathds{1}_{A}],

with
\bar{\mathbb{E}}
the associated expectation.
The branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
under
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
can be seen as a size-biased branching random walk.

Lyons
[
34
]
constructed the connection between the size-biased branching random walk and a branching random walk with a spine, which shows the genealogy structure and behavior of particles from a new point of view.
This method is an extension of the spinal decomposition introduced by Lyons, Pemantle, and Peres
[
35
]
for Galton–Watson process and Chauvin and Rouault
[
17
]
for branching Brownian motion.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
be a point process such that, for any non-negative measurable function
f
,

\mathbb{E}[f(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[f(\mathcal{L})\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\mathrm{e}^{\ell}}}\right].

A branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
with a spine
(w_{n}:n\geq 0)
can be described as follows.
At time
0
, particle
w_{0}\coloneq\varnothing
stays at
0
.
At each time
n
, all particles die and give birth to their children independently.
The children of spine particle
w_{n-1}
are scattered according to the law of
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
, with respect to
V(w_{n-1})
.
The children of normal particles
u
are distributed as
\mathcal{L}
, with respect to the position of their parents.
The spinal particle
w_{n}
is chosen at random among the children of
w_{n-1}
, by picking such a child
v
with probability proportional to
\mathrm{e}^{V(v)}
.
We denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
the law of the branching random walk with a spine, with
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
the associated expectation.
The following theorem tells us that the law of the size-biased branching random walk corresponds to the law of the branching random walk with a spine.

Theorem 2.2

(Lyons’ spinal decomposition theorem
[
34
]
)
.

For
n\geq 0
, the law of
(V(u):|u|\leq n)
is identical under
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
.
Moreover, for
|u|=n
, we have

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(u=w_{n}|\mathscr{F}_{n})=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}{W_{n}},

and
(V(w_{1}),\ldots,V(w_{n}))
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
is distributed as
(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})
under
\mathbb{P}
.

2.3
Random walk estimates

We consider a centered random walk
(S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{R}
with variance
\sigma^{2}\in(0,\infty)
.
In order to handle precise estimates, let us introduce some elements from fluctuation theory.
We define the strict ascending ladder epoch processes
(\tau_{k}^{+})_{k\geq 0}
and ladder height processes
(H_{k}^{+})_{k\geq 0}
as follows.
We set
\tau_{0}^{+}=0
,
H_{0}^{+}=0
, and for
k\geq 1
,

\tau_{k}^{+}=\inf\{n>\tau_{k-1}^{+}:S_{n}>S_{\tau_{k-1}^{+}}\}\quad\text{and}\quad H_{k}^{+}=S_{\tau_{k}^{+}}.

We denote by
R^{+}
the renewal function associated with
(H_{k}^{+})_{k\geq 0}
, defined for
t\geq 0
by

R^{+}(t)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\mathbb{P}(H_{k}^{+}\leq t).

By Feller
[
25
, Theorem XVIII.5.1 (5.2) and renewal theorem p. 360]
, since
(S_{n})
is centered with finite variance, we have
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[H_{1}}}\right]<\infty
, which implies that there exists
c^{+}>0
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{R^{+}(t)}{t}=c^{+}.

(18)

By Kozlov
[
30
, Theorem A]
, there exists
\theta^{+}>0
such that, for any
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}(\forall k\leq n,S_{k}\leq t)\sim\frac{\theta^{+}R^{+}(t)}{\sqrt{n}},

as
n\to\infty
.
Aïdékon and Shi
[
3
, Lemma 2.1]
have showed that constants
c^{+}
and
\theta^{+}
are related through

\theta^{+}c^{+}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\sigma^{2}}}.

(19)

We also consider the strict descending ladder variables
\tau_{k}^{-}
,
H_{k}^{-}
,
R^{-}
,
c^{-}
,
\theta^{-}
, the analogous quantities associated to the random walk
(-S_{n})_{n\geq 0}
.

(S_{k}-S_{k-1},\xi_{k})

\mathbb{R}^{2}

For any

\mathbb{P}(\forall k\leq n,S_{k}\leq x+f_{n}(k),\exists j<n,S_{j}>x+f_{n}(j)-\xi_{j+1},S_{n}\in x+f_{n}(n)-[y,y+1])\leq C\eta\frac{(1+x)(1+y)}{n^{3/2}},

(26)

where

\eta=\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}-S_{1}\geq 0)^{1/2}+\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\xi_{1}-S_{1})_{+}^{2}}}\right]^{1/2}+\mathbb{P}(\xi_{1}+1\geq 0)^{1/2}+\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\xi_{1}+1)_{+}^{2}}}\right]^{1/2}.

3
Asymptotic properties of the integral defining
C^{*}

The aim of this section is to study the asymptotic properties, as
\ell\to\infty
, of the integral and the sum defining constants
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
in Theorem
1.1
, or equivalently, of the integral defining
C^{*}
in Remark
1.3
, that is

\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(27)

To show that
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
are positive constants, we must prove that (
27
) converges in
(0,\infty)
as
\ell\to\infty
.
As a first step, we obtain that the
\limsup
and
\liminf
are indeed in
(0,\infty)
.
The proof of the convergence is postponed to Section
7
since it will follow from an argument developed in Section
6
.

It will also be useful to identify the main support of the integral in (
27
).
One can expect it to correspond to values of
y
that are of order
\sqrt{\ell}
, e.g. in view of equation (
8
) for the branching Brownian motion.
However, we do not need to be that precise for the proof of our main result Theorem
1.1
.
It will be sufficient to show that the integration domain can be restricted to
[\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell},B\ell]
, with
\varepsilon\to 0
and
B\to\infty
after
\ell\to\infty
.
This is achieved in the following two propositions.
The fact that we can restrict the domain to
[\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell},B\sqrt{\ell}]
is stated in Section
8.1
as a consequence of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
).
Then,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=0.

Proof.

According to (
9
), we know that there exists
C>0
such that, for any
\ell\geq 1
and
z\geq 0
, we have

\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>m_{\ell}+z)\leq C(1+z)\mathrm{e}^{-z}.

This implies that

\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq C\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}(y-m_{\ell})\mathrm{e}^{-y+m_{\ell}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{\ell^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell}}y^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y+\frac{C\log\ell}{\ell^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Making
\ell\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, this bound goes to
0
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
).
Then,

\lim_{B\to\infty}\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\int_{B\ell}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=0.

Proof.

By Fubini’s theorem, we have

\int_{B\ell}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[M_{\ell}\mathrm{e}^{M_{\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\{M_{\ell}>B\ell\}}}}\right].

And we know that

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[M_{\ell}\mathrm{e}^{M_{\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\{M_{\ell}>B\ell\}}}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=\ell}V(u)\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)>B\ell\}}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[S_{\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\ell}>B\ell\}}}}\right],

where the equality follows from the many-to-one formula.
By Markov’s inequality, we have

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[S_{\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{S_{\ell}>B\ell\}}}}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[S_{\ell}^{2}}}\right]}{B\ell}=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.

Therefore, we conclude that, for any
B>0
,

\sup_{\ell\geq 1}\int_{B\ell}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}.

Letting
B\to\infty
, this completes the proof.
∎

Up to the existence of constants
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
defined in Theorem
1.1
(proved in Section
7
below), the following proposition implies that they are finite.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
).
Then,

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

(28)

To prove Proposition
3.3
, we will need the following lemma, which will also be useful throughout the rest of the paper.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
).
Then, as
\ell\to\infty
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=O(\ell^{-1/4}).

Proof.

We split
[0,\infty)
into two parts
[0,a_{\ell})
and
[a_{\ell},\infty)
, for
a_{\ell}
that will be fixed later on.
On the one hand, according to (
9
), we have

\int_{0}^{a_{\ell}}\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{C}{\ell^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{a_{\ell}}(y-m_{\ell})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{Ca_{\ell}^{2}}{\ell^{3/2}}+\frac{Ca_{\ell}\log\ell}{\ell^{3/2}}.

On the other hand, we have

\int_{a_{\ell}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{M_{\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\{M_{\ell}\geq a_{\ell}\}}}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=\ell}\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)\geq a_{\ell}\}}}}\right]=\mathbb{P}(S_{\ell}\geq a_{\ell})\leq\frac{1}{a_{\ell}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[S_{\ell}^{2}}}\right]=\frac{\sigma^{2}\ell}{a_{\ell}^{2}},

where the first equality follows from the many-to-one formula.
Taking
a_{\ell}\coloneq\ell^{5/8}
, this completes the proof.
∎

Before turning to the proof of Proposition
3.3
, let us introduce some notation that will be useful there and in the rest of the paper.
For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
v\succeq u
if

is
u
or one of its descendants.
Besides, given

, we define

M_{n}^{(u)}=\max_{|v|=|u|+n,v\succeq u}(V(v)-V(u)),

(29)

the maximal position (with respect to

) of a branching random walk generated from

.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

Define, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
,
f_{n}(k)=-\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{n}{n-k+1}
and, for

and

\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x)=\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall j\leq n-\ell,V(u_{j})\leq f_{n}(j)\}}\mathds{1}_{\{M_{\ell}^{(u)}>m_{n}+x\}}.

We will control the left-hand side of (
28
) thanks to
\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x)]
.
First, let us state an upper bound for the latter quantity: by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x)]\leq\sum_{k=n-\ell+1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=k}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall j<k,V(u_{j})\leq f_{n}(j)\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)>f_{n}(k)+x\}}}}\right]\leq C(1+x)\mathrm{e}^{-x},

(30)

where the last inequality is shown by Mallein in the proof of
[
39
, Lemma 4.2]
, using Lemmas
2.1
and
2.7
.

Now, we apply successively the Markov property and the many-to-one formula to write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x)]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall j\leq n-\ell,V(u_{j})\leq f_{n}(j)+x\}}\mathbb{P}_{V(u)}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall j\leq n-\ell,S_{j}\leq f_{n}(j)+x\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{n-\ell}}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x)}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall j\leq n-\ell,S_{j}\leq m_{n}+x\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{n-\ell}}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x)}}\right].

Fix
h
any positive number if
S_{1}
is non-lattice, the lattice span if
S_{1}
is
(h,a)
-lattice.
Decompose the event

to write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x)]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<n-\ell,S_{j}\leq m_{n}+x,\\
S_{n-\ell}-m_{n}-x\in(-(i+1)h,-ih]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{n-\ell}}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x)}}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-m_{n}-x+ih}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<n-\ell,S_{j}\leq m_{n}+x,\\
S_{n-\ell}-m_{n}-x\in(-(i+1)h,-ih]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>(i+1)h)}}\right].

In view of applying Corollary
2.5
, we choose

such that

and

as

, and we restrict the sum to

, where

is such that

This, together with Fatou’s lemma, yields

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x_{n})]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}}\geq\sum_{i\geq A_{\ell}/h}\mathrm{e}^{ih}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>(i+1)h)\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{n^{3/2}}{x_{n}}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<n-\ell,S_{j}\leq m_{n}+x_{n},\\
S_{n-\ell}-m_{n}-x_{n}\in(-(i+1)h,-ih]\end{subarray}}}\right).

(31)

Applying Corollary
2.5
, it becomes

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x_{n})]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1+o_{\ell}(1))}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sum_{i\geq A_{\ell}/h}\mathrm{e}^{ih}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>(i+1)h)ih^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(1+o_{\ell}(1))}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\int_{A_{\ell}+h}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{y-2h}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)(y-2h)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

(32)

by monotonicity.
Letting

and applying successively (

3.4

\displaystyle\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Gamma_{n,\ell}(x_{n})]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{A_{\ell}+h}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{y-2h}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)(y-2h)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2h}}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{A_{\ell}+h}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-2h}}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

(33)

Combined with (

∎

Let us conclude this section with a straightforward consequence of the lower bound (

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (

), and (

Then,

\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y>0.

4
Non-contributing particles

Suppose that
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is such that
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
.
As in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, define, for
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq n
,

f_{n}(k)=m_{n}-m_{n-k+1}=-\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{n}{n-k+1}.

(34)

The main result of this section is Proposition
4.1
.
It tells that particles contributing to the upper moderate deviations typically stay below the barrier
x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)
until time
k=n-\ell
, for some large
\ell
.
To formalize it, we define, for
0\leq\ell\leq n
,

G_{n,\ell}=\{\exists|u|=n,V(u)>m_{n}+x_{n},\exists k\leq n-\ell,V(u_{k})>x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)\}.

(35)

Instead of taking
\ell
a large constant, it will be convenient to consider a sequence
(\ell_{n})
that goes to infinity arbitrarily slowly.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), and (
3
).
If
\ell_{n}\to\infty
and
\ell_{n}=o(n)
, then
\mathbb{P}(G_{n,\ell_{n}})=o(x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}})
.

Proof.

To simplify notations, let us abbreviate
\ell=\ell_{n}
.
Recall that
M_{n}^{(u)}
is defined in (
29
).
By the union bound,

\mathbb{P}(G_{n,\ell})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n-\ell}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=k}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<k,V(u_{j})\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(j),\\
V(u)>x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k),\\
M_{n-k}^{(u)}+V(u)>m_{n}+x_{n}\end{subarray}}}\right\}}}}\right]=\sum_{k=1}^{n-\ell}u_{n}(k),

where, by the Markov property at time
k
and the many-to-one formula,

u_{n}(k)=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{k}}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<k,S_{j}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(j),\\
S_{k}>x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{k}}(M_{n-k}>m_{n}+x_{n})}}\right].

Fix some sequence of integers
(k_{n})
such that
k_{n}\to\infty
and
k_{n}=o(x_{n})
.
First assume
k\leq k_{n}
.
Observe that, bounding the probabilities by
1
, we obtain
u_{n}(k)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)}
.
In particular, summing
u_{n}(k)
up to
k_{n}
yields

\sum_{k=1}^{k_{n}}u_{n}(k)\leq k_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k_{n})}=o(x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}).

Now, assume
k\in[k_{n},n-\ell]
.
By (
9
),

\mathbb{P}_{S_{k}}(M_{n-k}>m_{n}+x_{n})\leq C(1+(m_{n}-m_{n-k}+x_{n}-S_{k})_{+})\mathrm{e}^{-(m_{n}-m_{n-k}+x_{n}-S_{k})}.

Note that

m_{n}-m_{n-k}-f_{n-\ell}(k)=-\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{n}{n-\ell}+\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{n-k}{n-\ell-k+1}\leq\frac{3}{2}\log\frac{n-k}{n-\ell-k+1}=|f_{n-k}(\ell)|.

It follows that

u_{n}(k)\leq C(1+|f_{n-k}(\ell)|)\mathrm{e}^{-(m_{n}-m_{n-k}+x_{n})}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<k,S_{j}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(j),\\
S_{k}>x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)\end{subarray}}}\right).

By the Markov property at time
k-1
, the last factor is equal to
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\phi_{k}(S_{1})}}\right]
, where

\phi_{k}(y)=\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall j<k,S_{j}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(j),\\
S_{k-1}>x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-y\end{subarray}}}\right)\leq\frac{C(1+x_{n})(1+y_{+})^{2}}{k^{3/2}},

by Lemma
2.7
.
By Assumption (
3
),
S_{1}
admits a finite second moment and then
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\phi_{k}(S_{1})}}\right]\leq Cx_{n}/k^{3/2}
.
Hence,

\sum_{k=k_{n}}^{n-\ell}u_{n}(k)\leq Cx_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}\sum_{k=k_{n}}^{n-\ell}(1+|f_{n-k}(\ell)|)\frac{n^{3/2}}{(n-k)^{3/2}k^{3/2}}\leq Cx_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{k=k_{n}}^{n/2}\frac{|f_{n-k}(\ell)|}{k^{3/2}}+\sum_{k=\ell}^{n/2}\frac{|f_{k}(\ell)|}{k^{3/2}}}}\right).

Note that, for
k\leq n/2
, we have
|f_{n-k}(\ell)|\leq C
, and for
k\geq\ell
, we have
|f_{k}(\ell)|\leq(3/2)\log\ell
.
Hence,

\sum_{k=k_{n}}^{n-\ell}u_{n}(k)\leq Cx_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{k=k_{n}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{3/2}}+(\log\ell)\sum_{k=\ell}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{3/2}}}}\right)=Cx_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k_{n}}}+\frac{\log\ell}{\sqrt{\ell}}}}\right)=o(x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

5
An intermediate equivalent for the moderate deviation probability

Let
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be such that
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})

.
Recall that
M_{n}^{(u)}
is defined in (

) and

We set

\Lambda_{n,\ell}\coloneq\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq n-\ell,V(u_{i})\leq f_{n-\ell}(i)+x_{n}\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)+M_{\ell}^{(u)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}.

The main result of this section is the following proposition.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
).
As

, if
\ell_{n}\to\infty
and

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\sim\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell_{n}}].

Note that

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\geq\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_{n,\ell}\geq 1)\geq\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}^{2}]},

by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
Therefore, a way to obtain a lower bound for
\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]\sim\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}^{2}]

For

In other words, we restrict

More precisely, we consider, for

and

\displaystyle E(x)\coloneq\{u\in\mathbb{T}:\forall j\leq|u|,V(u_{j})\leq f_{|u|}(j)+x\},

\displaystyle F_{K}(x)\coloneq\{u\in\mathbb{T}:\forall j<|u|,\xi(u_{j})\leq K+(x+f_{|u|}(j)-V(u_{j}))/2\},

(37)

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}_{K}(x)\coloneq E(x)\cap F_{K}(x),

(38)

and

\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}\coloneq\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{u\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)+M_{\ell}^{(u)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
).
If
\ell_{n}\to\infty
and
\ell_{n}=o(n)
, then there exists

such that, for any

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell_{n}}]\geq cx_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]\geq\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})-\mathbb{P}(G_{n,\ell})
, it suffices to apply (
10
) and Proposition
4.1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
).
If
\ell>0
and
\ell=o(n)
, then

\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]}{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]}=1.

Proof.

Recall that
E(x)
and
F_{K}(x)
are defined in (
36
) and (
37
).
Using the branching property and then the probability distribution
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
introduced in Section
2.2
, we rewrite

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{u\in E(x_{n})\setminus F_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathbb{P}_{V(u)}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x_{n})}}\right]

\displaystyle=\bar{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=n-\ell}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}{W_{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-V(u)}\mathds{1}_{\{u\in E(x_{n})\setminus F_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathbb{P}_{V(u)}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x_{n})}}\right]

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-V(w_{n-\ell})}\mathds{1}_{\{w_{n-\ell}\in E(x_{n})\setminus F_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathbb{P}_{V(w_{n-\ell})}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x_{n})}}\right],

by the spinal decomposition theorem (Theorem
2.2
).
By decomposing the event
\{V(w_{n-\ell})\leq f_{n-\ell}(n-\ell)+x_{n}\}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]\leq\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}+i+1}\widehat{\mathbb{P}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}w_{n-\ell}\in E(x_{n})\setminus F_{K}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n}+m_{n-\ell}-V(w_{n-\ell})\in[i,i+1]\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}-m_{n-\ell}+i),

.
By Lemma
2.8
, applied with
\xi_{j+1}=2\xi(w_{j})-2K

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}w_{n-\ell}\in E(x_{n})\setminus F_{K}(x_{n}),\\
x_{n}+m_{n-\ell}-V(w_{n-\ell})\in[i,i+1]\end{subarray}}}\right)\leq C\eta(K)\frac{(x_{n}+1)(i+1)}{(n-\ell)^{3/2}},

\displaystyle\eta(K)

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{1}-V(w_{1})\geq 0)^{1/2}+\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\xi_{1}-V(w_{1}))_{+}^{2}}}\right]^{1/2}+\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(\xi_{1}+1\geq 0)^{1/2}+\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(\xi_{1}+1)_{+}^{2}}}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\widehat{\mathbb{P}}(2\xi(w_{0})_{+}-V(w_{1})\geq 2K)^{1/2}+\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(2\xi(w_{0})_{+}-2K-V(w_{1}))^{2}}}\right]^{1/2}

3

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[V(w_{1})^{2}}}\right]=\bar{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=1}V(u)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}{W_{1}}}}\right]=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=1}V(u)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}}\right]<\infty,

is defined in (
17

4
) together with

1

It follows that

Hence,

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]\leq x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}}o_{K}(1)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}(i+1)\mathrm{e}^{i+1}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>i-1)}}\right).

By Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
, the last sum in brackets is bounded by some constant.
In view of Lemma
5.2

Lemma 5.4
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (

and

Note that, by expanding

Lemma 5.5
.

Assume (
1
) and (
2
).
If
1\leq\ell=o(n)
, then there exists
C>0
such that, for any
K>0
and
n
large enough,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{e}^{K}\sum_{k=0}^{n-\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{n-\ell})\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-S_{k}}{2}}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i)\}}}}\right],

(39)

where
P_{n,\ell}(y)\coloneq\mathbb{P}_{y}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x_{n})
and
\psi(y)\coloneq(1+2y_{+})\mathrm{e}^{-y}
.

Proof.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, we denote by
u\wedge v
the most recent common ancestor of
u
and
v
, i.e.
u\wedge v\coloneq u_{k}
, where
k=\max\{j\geq 0:u_{j}=v_{j}\}
.
Recall that
\widetilde{E}_{K}(x)
is defined in (
38

We have

\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}^{2}=\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-\ell-1}\sum_{|u|=|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|u\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{u,v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(v)+M_{\ell}^{(v)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)+M_{\ell}^{(u)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}.

By the branching property, conditionally on
\mathscr{F}_{n-\ell}
, the random variables
M_{\ell}^{(u)}
, for
|u|=n-\ell
, are i.i.d. copies of
M_{\ell}
.
Therefore, recalling that
\bar{\mathbb{E}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{E}}
are defined in Section
2.2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|u\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{u,v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(v)+M_{\ell}^{(v)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}\mathds{1}_{\{V(u)+M_{\ell}^{(u)}>m_{n}+x_{n}\}}}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|u\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{u,v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(u))P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\bar{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=|v|=n-\ell}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}}{W_{n-\ell}}\mathrm{e}^{-V(u)}\mathds{1}_{\{|u\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{u,v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(u))P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad=\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-V(w_{n-\ell})}P_{n,\ell}(V(w_{n-\ell}))\mathds{1}_{\{w_{n-\ell}\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}\sum_{|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|w_{n-\ell}\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right],

where the last equality follows from spinal decomposition theorem (Theorem
2.2
).
By definition of
\Omega_{k}
,

\sum_{|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|w_{n-\ell}\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))=\sum_{u\in\Omega_{k}}\sum_{|v|=n-\ell,v\succeq u}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v)).

Let
\mathscr{G}_{n-\ell}\coloneq\sigma(w_{j},V(w_{j}),(u,V(u))_{u\in\Omega_{j-1}}:1\leq j\leq n-\ell)
.
Note that, conditionally on
\mathscr{G}_{n-\ell}
, for any
u\in\Omega_{k}
, the family
(V(v)-V(u):|v|=n-\ell,v\succeq u)
under
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
has same law as
(V(u):|u|=n-\ell-k-1)
under
\mathbb{P}
.
Therefore,

\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|v|=n-\ell}\mathds{1}_{\{|w_{n-\ell}\wedge v|=k\}}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))\middle|\mathscr{G}_{n-\ell}}}\right]=\sum_{u\in\Omega_{k}}\mathbb{E}_{V(u)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|v|=n-\ell-k-1}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right].

(40)

For
k^{\prime}=n-\ell-k-1
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|v|=k^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{a}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|v|=k^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{\{v\in E_{K}(x_{n})\}}P_{n,\ell}(V(v))}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq k^{\prime},S_{i}\leq f_{n-\ell}(k+1+i)+x_{n}-a\}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{k^{\prime}}+a)}}\right],

by the many-to-one formula.
By decomposing the event
\{S_{k^{\prime}}\leq f_{n-\ell}(k+1+k^{\prime})+x_{n}-a\}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq k^{\prime},S_{i}\leq f_{n-\ell}(k+1+i)+x_{n}-a\}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{k^{\prime}}+a)}}\right]\\
\leq\sum_{y\geq 0}\mathrm{e}^{-m_{n-\ell}-x_{n}+a+y+1}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i\leq k^{\prime},S_{i}\leq f_{n-\ell}(k+1+i)+x_{n}-a,\\
S_{k^{\prime}}-m_{n-\ell}-x_{n}+a\in[-y-1,-y]\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}-m_{n-\ell}+y).

For convenience, we define, for
0\leq j\leq n
and
x,y,z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\chi_{n,j}(x,y,z)\coloneq\mathbb{P}(\forall i<j,S_{i}\leq f_{n}(i)+x,S_{j}-f_{n}(j)\in[x-y-z,x-y]).

(41)

We write
\chi_{n}=\chi_{n,n}
for short.
Note that

f_{n-\ell}(k+1+i)=m_{n-\ell}-m_{k^{\prime}}+m_{k^{\prime}}-m_{n-\ell-k-i}=f_{n-\ell}(k+2)+f_{k^{\prime}}(i),

since
k^{\prime}=n-\ell-k-1
.
Thus, taking
n
large enough so that
m_{n}-m_{n-\ell}>-1
, we have

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{k^{\prime}}}\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq k^{\prime},S_{i}\leq f_{n-\ell}(k+1+i)+x_{n}-a\}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{k^{\prime}}+a)}}\right]\\
\leq\sum_{y\geq 0}\mathrm{e}^{-m_{n-\ell}-x_{n}+a+y+1}\chi_{k^{\prime}}(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k+2)-a,y,1)\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1).

Applying Lemma
2.7
and then Proposition
3.3
, we bound the above sum by

C(n-\ell)^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}+a}\sum_{y\geq 0}\mathrm{e}^{y+1}\frac{(1+(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k+2)-a)_{+})(1+y)}{(n-\ell-k)^{3/2}}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1)\\
\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}+a-f_{n-\ell}(k)}(1+(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k+2)-a)_{+}).

Tracing back our inequalities, we obtain that (
40
) is bounded by

C\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k)}\sum_{u\in\Omega_{k}}\mathrm{e}^{V(u)}(1+(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k+1)-V(u))_{+})\\
\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k)+V(w_{k})}(1+(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-V(w_{k}))_{+})\mathrm{e}^{\xi(w_{k})},

where we have used the basic inequality
1+(a+b)_{+}\leq(1+a_{+})(1+b_{+})
.
Now, by definition (
37
), on the event
\{w_{n-\ell}\in F_{K}(x_{n})\}
, we have

\mathrm{e}^{\xi(w_{k})}\leq\mathrm{e}^{K+(x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-V(w_{k}))/2}.

We deduce that

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]\leq C\mathrm{e}^{K}\sum_{k=0}^{n-\ell-1}\widehat{\mathbb{E}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-V(w_{n-\ell})}P_{n,\ell}(V(w_{n-\ell}))\mathds{1}_{\{w_{n-\ell}\in\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})\}}\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-V(w_{k})}{2}}}\right)}}\right].

Finally, the upper bound (
39
) follows from the definition of
\widetilde{E}_{K}(x_{n})
and the spinal decomposition theorem (Theorem
2.2
).
∎

Now we prove Lemma

.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

To simplify notations, let us abbreviate
\ell=\ell_{n}
.
By Lemma
5.5
, for
K>0
and
n
large enough, we have

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]\leq C\mathrm{e}^{K}\sum_{k=0}^{n-\ell-1}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{n-\ell})\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-S_{k}}{2}}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i)\}}}}\right],

where
P_{n,\ell}(y)\coloneq\mathbb{P}_{y}(M_{\ell}>m_{n}+x_{n})
and
\psi(y)\coloneq(1+2y_{+})\mathrm{e}^{-y}
.
By decomposing the event
\{S_{n-\ell}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(n-\ell)\}
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{n-\ell}}P_{n,\ell}(S_{n-\ell})\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-S_{k}}{2}}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\{\forall i\leq n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i)\}}}}\right]\\
\leq\sum_{y\geq 0}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}+y+1}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-S_{k}}{2}}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i),\\
S_{n-\ell}-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}\in[-y-1,-y]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}P_{n,\ell}(x_{n}+m_{n-\ell}-y)}}\right].

Then, by decomposing the event
\{S_{k}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)\}
and taking
n
large enough so that
P_{n,\ell}(x_{n}+m_{n-\ell}-y)\leq\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1)
, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(k)-S_{k}}{2}}}\right)\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i),\\
S_{n-\ell}-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}\in[-y-1,-y]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}P_{n,\ell}(x_{n}+m_{n-\ell}-y)}}\right]\\
\leq\sum_{z\geq 0}(2+z)\mathrm{e}^{-z/2}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i),\\
S_{k}-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k)\in[-z-1,-z],\\
S_{n-\ell}-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}\in[-y-1,-y]\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1).

k
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<n-\ell,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i),\\
S_{k}-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k)\in[-z-1,-z],\\
S_{n-\ell}-x_{n}-m_{n-\ell}\in[-y-1,-y]\end{subarray}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<k,S_{i}\leq x_{n}+f_{n-\ell}(i),\\
S_{k}-x_{n}-f_{n-\ell}(k)\in[-z-1,-z]\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall i<n-\ell-k,S_{i}\leq f_{n-\ell}(i+k)-f_{n-\ell}(k)+z+1,\\

\displaystyle\leq\chi_{n-\ell,k}(x_{n},z,1)\chi_{n-\ell-k}(z+1,y-1,2),

where
\chi_{n-\ell,k}
and
\chi_{n-\ell-k}=\chi_{n-\ell-k,n-\ell-k}
are defined in (

Tracing back our inequalities, this yields

\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}^{2}]-\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell,K}]\leq C\mathrm{e}^{K-x_{n}}(n-\ell)^{3/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-\ell-1}\sum_{y,z\geq 0}T_{n,k}(y,z),

(42)

where

Consider
\alpha\in(0,1)
that we will fix later on.
In the remainder of the proof, we treat separately the cases

,

and
\chi_{n-\ell-k}(z+1,y-1,2)
via Lemma
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{y,z\geq 0}T_{n,k}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}}{k^{3/2}(n-\ell-k)^{3/2}}\sum_{z\geq 0}(1+z)^{3}\mathrm{e}^{-z/2}\sum_{y\geq 0}(1+y)\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1)

where the last inequality follows from Proposition
3.3
and Lemma
3.4
.
Hence,

\displaystyle(n-\ell)^{3/2}\sum_{\ell^{\alpha}\leq k\leq n-\ell-\ell^{\alpha}}\sum_{y,z\geq 0}T_{n,k}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq Cx_{n}\sum_{\ell^{\alpha}\leq k\leq n-\ell-\ell^{\alpha}}\frac{(n-\ell)^{3/2}}{k^{3/2}(n-\ell-k)^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\leq Cx_{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\sum_{\ell^{\alpha}\leq k\leq(n-\ell)/2}k^{-3/2}+\sum_{(n-\ell)/2\leq k\leq n-\ell-\ell^{\alpha}}(n-\ell-k)^{-3/2}}}\right)

(43)

Now, assume
k\in[n-\ell-\ell^{\alpha},n-\ell]
.

\chi_{n-\ell,k}(x_{n},z,1)
via Lemma
2.7
, we obtain

\sum_{y,z\geq 0}T_{n,k}(y,z)\leq\frac{Cx_{n}}{k^{3/2}}\sum_{y,z\geq 0}(1+z)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{y-z/2}\chi_{n-\ell-k}(z+1,y-1,2)\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1).

\beta>0
that will be fixed later on.

\chi_{n-\ell-k}(z+1,y-1,2)\leq 1

.

\displaystyle\sum_{y>2\ell^{\beta}}\sum_{z\leq\ell^{\beta}}T_{n,k}(y,z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}}{k^{3/2}}\mathbb{P}(-S_{n-\ell-k}\geq\ell^{\beta}-2)\sum_{z\leq\ell^{\beta}}(1+z)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-z/2}\sum_{y>2\ell^{\beta}}\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}}{k^{3/2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[S_{n-\ell-k}^{2}]}{\ell^{2\beta}}\sum_{y>2\ell^{\beta}}\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y-1)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}}{k^{3/2}\ell^{2\beta-\alpha}},

where the final inequality follows from Lemma
3.4
.

\ell
large enough, using that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}\geq\frac{(1+o_{\ell}(1))h}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\sum_{k\in(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})\cap[A_{\ell},\infty)}k\mathrm{e}^{k}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>k).

Letting
\ell\to\infty
, it follows from Proposition
3.1
that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}\geq\frac{h}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{k\in(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})\cap[0_{\ell},\infty)}k\mathrm{e}^{k}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>k)=\bar{C}_{\mathrm{L}},

which concludes the proof.
∎

7
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now able to prove our main result Theorem
1.1
.
At first, we show that the equivalents (
11
) and (
13
) hold by replacing
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
C_{\mathrm{L}}
with
\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}
and
\bar{C}_{\mathrm{L}}
, defined in (
49
).
Once we know such an asymptotic, we deduce that the former constants are well defined and, subsequently, equal to the latter constants.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

First assume the branching random walk to be non-lattice and let
(x_{n})
be such that
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
.
By Propositions
5.1
and
6.1
, choosing
\ell_{n}
such that
\sqrt{\ell_{n}}/\log n\to\infty
and
\ell_{n}=o(\sqrt{n})
, we obtain

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell_{n}}]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}\leq\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}.

For the lower bound, we consider
\ell
constant in
n
and let
\ell\to\infty
after
n\to\infty
.
By Corollary
5.6
and Proposition
6.2
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}=\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell}]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}\geq\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}.

Besides, the constant
\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}
is in
(0,\infty)
, by Propositions
3.3
and
3.5
.
Hence,

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\sim\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}.

(56)

Now, as above, consider
\ell_{n}
such that
\sqrt{\ell_{n}}/\log n\to\infty
and
\ell_{n}=o(\sqrt{n})
, and further assume that it takes all integer values (e.g. set
\ell_{n}=\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
).
By (
56
) and Proposition
5.1
,

\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Lambda_{n,\ell_{n}}]}{x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})}}.

Therefore, taking the
\liminf
in (
54
) (recall that, in this context, we wrote
\ell=\ell_{n}
for short), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell_{n}}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Thus, the limit exists, i.e.
C_{\mathrm{NL}}
is well defined and is equal to
\bar{C}_{\mathrm{NL}}
.
This, together with (
56
), concludes the proof of (
11
).

Regarding the lattice case, the same argument shows that, for
(x_{n})
such that
x_{n}\to\infty
,
x_{n}=O(\sqrt{n})
, and
x_{n}+m_{n}\in an+h\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\mathbb{P}(M_{n}>m_{n}+x_{n})\sim\bar{C}_{\mathrm{L}}x_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-x_{n}-x_{n}^{2}/(2n\sigma^{2})},

and

\frac{h}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{y\in(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})\cap[0,\infty)}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\leq\frac{h}{\sigma^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{y\in(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})\cap[0,\infty)}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y),

which concludes the proof of (
13
) and of the theorem.
∎

8
Applications

In this section, we present two applications of our main result Theorem
1.1
.
The first is a refinement of the study of the integral defining
C^{*}
in (
15
).
In Section
3
, we showed that the domain of integration can be restricted to
[\varepsilon\sqrt{\ell},B\ell]
for
\varepsilon
small and
B
large.
Here, we show that it can even be restricted to a
\sqrt{\ell}
-neighborhood.
The second application concerns the two-speed branching random walk in the so-called
mean regime
.
This is a more general model than the branching random walk, for which we prove analogs of (
6
) and (
7
).

8.1
Main support of the integral defining
C^{*}

Here is the main result of this section.

Proposition 8.1
.

Assume (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), and (
4
).
For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
A,B\in(0,\infty)
such that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{[0,\infty)\setminus[A\sqrt{\ell},B\sqrt{\ell}]}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
By Proposition
3.1
, there exists
A>0
small enough so that

\limsup_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{A\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\leq\varepsilon.

In view of (
15
), it remains to show that

\liminf_{B\to\infty}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{B\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\geq\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\eqcolon\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sigma^{3}C^{*}.

(57)

Let us first assume the branching random walk to be non-lattice.
Applying Corollary
1.5
with
x=y-m_{\ell}
, we obtain that, for
\ell
large enough,

(1-\varepsilon)C^{*}\ell^{-3/2}y\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\leq\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\leq(1+\varepsilon)C^{*}\ell^{-3/2}y\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})},

uniformly in
y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}]
.
In particular,

\int_{0}^{B\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\geq(1-\varepsilon)C^{*}\ell^{-3/2}\int_{0}^{B\sqrt{\ell}}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y=(1-\varepsilon)C^{*}\int_{0}^{B}z^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

Letting
\ell\to\infty
and then
B\to\infty
, we deduce (
57
).

Now assume the branching random walk to be
(h,a)
-lattice.
By Corollary
1.5
and (
15
), for
\ell
large enough,

\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{h}{\mathrm{e}^{h}-1}C^{*}\ell^{-3/2}y\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})},

uniformly in
y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}-h]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\int_{y}^{y+h}x\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{h}{\mathrm{e}^{h}-1}C^{*}\ell^{-3/2}\sum_{y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}-h]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})}y\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\int_{y}^{y+h}x\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x.

(58)

By monotonicity,

\displaystyle\sum_{y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}-h]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})}y\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\int_{y}^{y+h}x\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}-h]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-y-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\int_{y}^{y+h}\mathrm{e}^{x}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{e}^{h}-1)\sum_{y\in[0,B\sqrt{\ell}-h]\cap(a\ell+h\mathbb{Z})}y^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\mathrm{e}^{h}-1}{h}\int_{h}^{B\sqrt{\ell}-h}(y-h)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-y^{2}/(2\ell\sigma^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y.

Inserting this lower bound in (
58
) and performing the change of variable
z=y/\sqrt{\ell}
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{B\sqrt{\ell}}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\geq(1-\varepsilon)C^{*}\int_{h/\sqrt{\ell}}^{B-h/\sqrt{\ell}}z^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z+o_{\ell}(1).

Letting
\ell\to\infty
and then
B\to\infty
, we deduce (
57
).
∎

Remark 8.2
.

A consequence of Proposition
8.1
is that, for any
\varepsilon,t\in(0,1)
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist
0<A<B<\infty
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\exists|u|=n,V(u_{\lfloor tn\rfloor})\notin[-B\sqrt{n},-A\sqrt{n}],V(u)>m_{n}+x)\leq\varepsilon.

(59)

We will prove it in Lemma
8.5
below for a more general model, namely the two-speed branching random walk.
This result shows that, with high probability, the ancestor at time
\lfloor tn\rfloor
of particles near
m_{n}
at time
n
has a position of order
-\sqrt{n}
.
This is in line with
[
19
]
, where Chen proved that the rescaled trajectory leading to the maximal position at time
n
converges in law to a Brownian excursion.
Similarly, Chen, Madaule, and Mallein
[
22
]
proved that the rescaled trajectory of a particle selected according to a supercritical Gibbs measure at time
n
(whose position is typically at distance
O(1)
from
m_{n}
) also converges in law to a Brownian excursion.
Subsequently, the result (
59
) can be interpreted as the “entropic repulsion” phenomenon: a Brownian bridge of length
n
conditioned to stay below a line for most of its lifespan, actually stays at a distance of order
\min(\sqrt{k},\sqrt{n-k})
below this line at each time
k\in[O(1),n-O(1)]
.
In the framework of branching Brownian motion, this property was already present in Bramson’s work
[
12
]
and it is precisely formulated in
[
4
, Theorem 2.3]
.

8.2
Convergence of the maximum of a two-speed branching random walk

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we prove the analog of (
6
) and (
7
) for a two-speed branching random walk, which was conjectured in
[
32
]
for the non-lattice case.

Given
t\in(0,1)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a two-speed branching random walk on
\mathbb{R}
is a time-inhomogeneous branching random walk, i.e. there is a change of reproduction law at time
\lfloor tn\rfloor
.
More precisely, there is one particle located at
0
at time
0
, and each particle reproduces according to the law of a point process
\mathcal{L}_{1}
independently from time
0
to
\lfloor tn\rfloor-1
, and reproduces according to the law of another point process
\mathcal{L}_{2}
independently between
\lfloor tn\rfloor
and
n-1
.
In particular, if the law of
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is equal to that of
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, then the two-speed branching random walk reduces to a time-homogeneous branching random walk.

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the maximal displacement
M_{n}^{(n)}
of particles at time
n
.
Fang and Zeitouni
[
24
]
showed the tightness of the centered
M_{n}^{(n)}
when the particle displacements are Gaussian.
Furthermore, Mallein
[
38
]
extended the tightness to a time-inhomogeneous branching random walk under general assumptions.
The weak convergence of the centered maximum has been proved by Bovier and Hartung
[
10
]
for a two-speed branching Brownian motion and by Luo
[
32
]
for a two-speed branching random walk in two different regimes, determined by a relation between
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
, referred to as the
slow
and
fast regimes
.
The former corresponds to the case where the maximal particle at time
n
descends from one of the maximal particles at time
\lfloor tn\rfloor
, the latter corresponds to the case where the optimal path is at a distance of order
n
below the maximum at time
\lfloor tn\rfloor
(see e.g.
[
38
]
).

In this section, we use Theorem
1.1
to prove that, in the
mean regime
, i.e. the frontier between the fast and slow regimes, the centered maximum converges in law to a standard Gumbel distribution with an independent random shift.
Before stating the main result, we introduce some notation.
For
i\in\{1,2\}
, let
(V_{i}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
be a branching random walk with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}_{i}
,
Z_{i,\infty}
be the limit of the associated derivative martingale (
5
),
M_{i,n}\coloneq\max_{|u|=n}V_{i}(u)
, and

\sigma_{i}^{2}\coloneq\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=1}V_{i}(u)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{V_{i}(u)}}}\right].

Recall that
m_{n}=-(3/2)\log n
.
In the remainder of this section, we aim to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 8.3
.

Assume that
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
and
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
satisfy the assumptions (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), (
4
), and fix
t\in(0,1)
.

(i)

If
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is non-lattice, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathbb{P}(M_{n}^{(n)}\leq m_{n}+x)-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{C_{2,\mathrm{NL}}Z_{1,\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}}{(1-t+t\sigma_{1}^{2}/\sigma_{2}^{2})^{3/2}}}}\right)}}\right]}}\right|=0,

(60)

where

C_{2,\mathrm{NL}}\coloneq\frac{1}{\sigma_{2}^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,\ell}>y)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\in(0,\infty).

(ii)

If
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is
(h_{1},a_{1})
-lattice,
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is
(h_{2},a_{2})
-lattice, and
h_{1}\mathbb{Z}\subset h_{2}\mathbb{Z}
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in A_{n}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathbb{P}(M_{n}^{(n)}\leq m_{n}+x)-\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\frac{C_{2,\mathrm{L}}Z_{1,\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}}{(1-t+t\sigma_{1}^{2}/\sigma_{2}^{2})^{3/2}}}}\right)}}\right]}}\right|=0,

(61)

where

A_{n}=\{x:m_{n}+x\in a_{1}\lfloor tn\rfloor+a_{2}(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)+h_{2}\mathbb{Z}\},

and

C_{2,\mathrm{L}}\coloneq\frac{h_{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\lim_{\ell\to\infty}\sum_{y\in(a_{2}\ell+h_{2}\mathbb{Z})\cap[0,\infty)}y\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,\ell}>y)\in(0,\infty).

Remark 8.4
.

The case where
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is
(h_{1},a_{1})
-lattice,
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is
(h_{2},a_{2})
-lattice, and
h_{1}\mathbb{Z}\not\subset h_{2}\mathbb{Z}
is more intricate and we do not treat it here.
The same holds for the case where
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is non-lattice and
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
is lattice.
In view of our proof, the issue in such a case is that, for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 1
, the family
\{x-V_{1}(u):|u|=t_{n}\}
is not included in a lattice of span
h_{2}
.
This prevents us to have a uniform control of the quantities
\mathbb{P}_{V_{1}(u)}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x)
, for
|u|=t_{n}
, via Corollary
1.5
.

Before proving Theorem
8.3
, we introduce a helpful lemma, which is a slight extension of (
59
).
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\in\mathbb{T}
such that
0\leq|u|\leq n
, we write
V^{(n)}(u)
the position of
u
and
t_{n}\coloneq\lfloor tn\rfloor
.

Lemma 8.5
.

Assume that
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
and
(V_{2}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
satisfy the assumptions (
1
), (
2
), (
3
), (
4
), and fix
t\in(0,1)
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist
0<A<B<\infty
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\exists|u|=n,V^{(n)}(u_{t_{n}})\notin[-B\sqrt{n},-A\sqrt{n}],V^{(n)}(u)>m_{n}+x)\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

One can show that, for
\gamma>0
, we have
\mathbb{P}(\exists u\in\mathbb{T}:V(u)>\gamma)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}
(see e.g.
[
20
, Lemma 3.2]
).
Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\exists|u|=n,V^{(n)}(u_{t_{n}})\notin[-B\sqrt{n},-A\sqrt{n}],V^{(n)}(u)>m_{n}+x)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}+\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\exists|u|=n,\forall k\leq t_{n},V^{(n)}(u_{k})\leq\gamma,\\
V^{(n)}(u_{t_{n}})\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma],\\
V^{(n)}(u)>m_{n}+x\end{subarray}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}+\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\exists|u|=t_{n},\forall k\leq t_{n},V^{(n)}(u_{k})\leq\gamma,\\
V^{(n)}(u)\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma],\\
\max_{v\succeq u,|v|=n}V^{(n)}(v)>m_{n}+x\end{subarray}}}\right).

By Markov’s inequality and the Markov property at time
t_{n}
, the last probability term is bounded by

\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{|u|=t_{n}}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall k\leq t_{n},V_{1}(u_{k})\leq\gamma,\\
V_{1}(u)\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{V_{1}(u)}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x)}}\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{1,t_{n}}}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall k\leq t_{n},S_{1,k}\leq\gamma,\\
S_{1,t_{n}}\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{1,t_{n}}}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x)}}\right],

where the equality follows from the many-to-one formula and
(S_{1,n})_{n\geq 0}
is a random walk associated with the branching random walk
(V_{1}(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
.
By decomposing the event
\{S_{1,t_{n}}\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma]\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathrm{e}^{-S_{1,t_{n}}}\mathds{1}_{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{subarray}{c}\forall k\leq t_{n},S_{1,k}\leq\gamma,\\
S_{1,t_{n}}\in(-\infty,-B\sqrt{n}]\cup[-A\sqrt{n},\gamma]\end{subarray}}}\right\}}\mathbb{P}_{S_{1,t_{n}}}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x)}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq\lfloor-\gamma\rfloor,\\
i\notin[A\sqrt{n},B\sqrt{n}-1]\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}^{i+1}\mathbb{P}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\begin{subarray}{c}\forall k\leq t_{n},S_{1,k}\leq\gamma,\\
S_{1,t_{n}}\in[-i-1,-i]\end{subarray}}}\right)\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x+i)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{n^{3/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq\lfloor-\gamma\rfloor,\\
i\notin[A\sqrt{n},B\sqrt{n}-1]\end{subarray}}(i+\gamma)\mathrm{e}^{i+1}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n}+x+i)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{C}{n^{3/2}}\int_{[-\gamma-1,A\sqrt{n}+1]\cup[B\sqrt{n}-1,\infty)}(y+\gamma)\mathrm{e}^{y}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,{n-t_{n}}}>m_{n}+x+y-1)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y,

where the second inequality follows from Lemma
2.7
.
With the change of variable
z=m_{n}+x+y-1
, we rewrite the above bound as

C\mathrm{e}^{x}\int_{([-\gamma-1,A\sqrt{n}+1]\cup[B\sqrt{n}-1,\infty))+m_{n}+x-1}(z-m_{n}-x+\gamma+1)\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z.

According to Lemma
3.4
, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,\infty)}(-m_{n}-x+\gamma+1)\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z=0.

(62)

Concerning the negative integration domain, we can bound

0\leq\int_{[-\gamma+m_{n}+x-2,0)}(z-m_{n}-x+\gamma+1)\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z\\
\leq C\log n\int_{-\infty}^{m_{n}/2}\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z+C\log n\int_{m_{n}/2}^{0}\mathrm{e}^{z}\mathbb{P}(M_{2,n-t_{n}}>m_{n-t_{n}}-m_{n}+\frac{3}{2}\log(1-t)+z)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}z,

(63)

since

Appendix A
Some analytic lemmas

In this section, we consider some function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as well as two sequences
(\beta_{n})
and
(\gamma_{n})
such that
0\leq\beta_{n}=o(\gamma_{n})
and
\gamma_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
The following analytic lemmas are useful throughout the paper (to derive Corollaries
1.4
,
1.5
,
2.5
,
2.6
,
4.2
, and
5.6
), with appropriate choices of
\beta_{n}
and
\gamma_{n}
.

Lemma A.1
.

Assume that, for any sequence
(x_{n})
such that
x_{n}\to\infty
and
x_{n}=O(\gamma_{n})
, we have
f(n,x_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then,
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}|f(n,x)|=0
.

Proof.

Assume that
\limsup_{x\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}|f(n,x)|>0
.
Then, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence
(y_{k})
such that
y_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
and, for any
k
,
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|f(n,y_{k})|>\varepsilon
.
This implies that, for any
k
, we can find
n_{k}
as large as we want so that
|f(n,y_{k})|>\varepsilon
.
In particular, we can set
n_{0}=0
and define inductively, for
k\geq 1
,

n_{k}=\inf\{n>n_{k-1}:\gamma_{n}\geq y_{k}\ \text{and}\ |f(n,y_{k})|>\varepsilon\}.

We set
x_{n_{k}}=y_{k}
.
Thus, we have
x_{n_{k}}\to\infty
,
x_{n_{k}}=O(\gamma_{n_{k}})
, and
f(n_{k},x_{n_{k}})\not\to 0
as
k\to\infty
, which contradicts the assumption of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Assume that, for any sequence
(x_{n})
such that
\beta_{n}\leq x_{n}\leq\gamma_{n}
, we have
f(n,x_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then, we have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}]}|f(n,x)|=0
.

Proof.

Similarly to Lemma
A.1
, if
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x\in[\beta_{n},\gamma_{n}]}|f(n,x)|>0
, then there exist
\varepsilon>0
and sequences
(n_{k})
and
(x_{n_{k}})
such that
n_{k}\to\infty
,
\beta_{n_{k}}\leq x_{n_{k}}\leq\gamma_{n_{k}}
, and
|f(n_{k},x_{n_{k}})|>\varepsilon
, which contradicts the assumption of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Assume that, for any sequence
(x_{n})
such that

(i)

either
\beta_{n}\leq x_{n}=o(\gamma_{n})
,

(ii)

or
x_{n}\asymp\gamma_{n}
,

we have
f(n,x_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then the latter convergence holds for any choice of
(x_{n})
such that
\beta_{n}\leq x_{n}=O(\gamma_{n})
.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a sequence
(x_{n})
such that
\beta_{n}\leq x_{n}=O(\gamma_{n})
and
f(n,x_{n})\not\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Then, there exist
\varepsilon>0
and a subsequence
(x_{n_{k}})
such that
\liminf_{k\to\infty}|f(n_{k},x_{n_{k}})|>\varepsilon
.
If
x_{n_{k}}=o(\gamma_{n_{k}})
, it directly contradicts the assumption of the lemma.
Otherwise, there exists a subsequence
(x_{n_{k_{j}}})
such that
x_{n_{k_{j}}}\asymp\gamma_{n_{k}}
.
Since
\liminf_{j\to\infty}|f(n_{k_{j}},x_{n_{k_{j}}})|>\varepsilon
, it still contradicts the assumption of the lemma.
∎

Lemma B.1

With the same notation as in Lemma

and setting

Our argument corrects a mistake from

The problem might come from an incorrect expansion of the expression
\mathds{1}_{\{\xi\geq 0\}}(1+X_{+}+\xi_{+})(1+\xi_{+})

Abstract
. Countably generated projective modules that are relatively big with respect to a trace ideal were introduced by P. Příhoda, as an extension of Bass’ uniformly big projectives. It has already been proved that there are a number of interesting examples of rings whose countably generated projective modules are always relatively big. In this paper, we increase the list of such examples, showing that it includes all right noetherian rings satisfying a polynomial identity. We also show that countably generated projective modules over locally semiperfect torsion-free algebras over h h -local domains are always relatively big. This last result applies to endomorphism rings of finitely generated torsion-free modules over h h -local domains. As a consequence, we can give a complete characterization of those h h -local domains of Krull dimension 1 1 for which every direct summand of a direct sum of copies of a single finitely generated torsion-free module is again a direct sum of finitely generated modules.



Introduction

For a long time, the study of projective modules was focused on the finitely generated ones. Outside the finitely generated setting, the classical theorem of Kaplansky
[
16
]
stating that every projective module is a direct sum of countably generated projective modules shows that one can just consider the countably generated case. Interesting monoids associated to infinitely generated projective modules have recently been studied: the monoid
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
of relatively big projectives defined, formally, in
[
10
]
and that is the main object of interest in this paper, but also the analogous of the Cuntz monoid for general rings defined in
[
3
]
, and the
\kappa
-monoids defined in
[
22
]
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
V(\Lambda)
(resp.
V^{*}(\Lambda)
) denote the set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of finitely generated (resp. countably generated) projective right
\Lambda
-modules. These sets form commutative monoids with addition induced by the direct sum of modules, with
V(\Lambda)
naturally embedded in
V^{*}(\Lambda)
.

The concept of relatively big projective module was introduced by Příhoda in
[
25
]
, extending Bass’ uniformly big projectives
[
4
]
. Let
J
be the trace of a countably generated projective module. A countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
is said to be
J
-big, if every countably generated projective
\Lambda
-module whose trace is contained in
J
is a homomorphic image of
P
. A countably generated projective module
P
is said to be
relatively
J
-big
if
P
is
J
-big and
P/PJ
is finitely generated. Therefore,
J
is maximal with respect to the property that
P
is
J
-big and minimal with respect to the property that
P/PJ
is finitely generated (cf. §
1.2
). A crucial property is that a relatively
J
-big projective module
P
is uniquely determined, up to isomorphism, by the trace ideal
J
and the isomorphism class of
P/PJ
(cf. Lemma
1.14
).

The relatively big projectives are the countably generated projective modules that are relatively
J
-big for some
J
. The important fact that boils down in the concept of relatively big projective is that projective modules modulo a trace ideal can be lifted to projective modules over the ring. If the projective module over the quotient is finitely generated, then there is a unique lifting to a relatively big projective module over the ring.

The monoid
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
of isomorphism classes of relatively big projective modules was introduced in
[
10
]
to capture the properties of such projectives. This monoid embeds in
V^{*}(\Lambda)
and can be thought of as the
disjoint union
of
V(\Lambda/J)
, where
J
runs through the trace ideals of the countably generated
\Lambda
-modules, see Corollary
2.5
.

In
[
25
]
, it was proved that over a noetherian ring
\Lambda
which satisfies a certain descending condition on two-sided ideals, which we call the
(*)
-condition,
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)=V^{*}(\Lambda)
(cf.
[
25
, Lemma 2.8]
and Theorem
1.23
). Commutative noetherian rings, finitely generated algebras over commutative noetherian rings of Krull dimension one, and noetherian semilocal rings satisfy the
(*)
-condition. Therefore, all countably generated projective modules over these rings are relatively big. These results were the main ingredients used to characterize
V^{*}(\Lambda)
for
\Lambda
a noetherian semilocal ring in
[
11
]
, or to study some infinite direct sum decompositions in
[
10
]
.

The main results of this paper provide new classes of rings for which
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)=V^{*}(\Lambda)
. We prove that right noetherian PI-rings satisfy the
(*)
-condition (see Theorem
4.8
), and this is one of the main ingredients in showing that all countably generated right (or left) projective modules over a right noetherian PI-ring are relatively big in Corollary
4.9
. In particular, this applies to module-finite algebras over commutative noetherian rings, such as group algebras of finite groups over commutative noetherian rings, or to endomorphism rings of finitely generated modules over commutative noetherian rings. This shows that the restriction on Krull dimension that we were imposing before was superfluous.

Beyond the noetherian setting, it is easy to show that for semiperfect rings, all countably generated projective right (or left) modules are relatively big (cf. Proposition
6.4
). In Theorem
6.17
, we show that the same conclusion holds for
\Lambda
a locally semiperfect torsion-free algebra, over an
h
-local commutative domain
R
with field of fractions
Q
, such that
\Lambda\otimes_{R}Q
is simple artinian.

The
h
-local domains satisfy strong noetherianity conditions on the maximal spectrum. More precisely, any non-zero ideal of
R
is contained only in a finite number of maximal ideals, and any non-zero prime ideal of
R
is contained in a unique maximal ideal. Noetherian domains of Krull dimension one are always
h
-local. The definition of
h
-local domain is attributed to Matlis. However, Jaffard in
[
15
]
introduced Dedekind-type domains as those domains
R
such that any non-zero ideal is a finite product of ideals that are contained in a unique maximal ideal of
R
. Jaffard proved that Dedekind-type domains coincide with
h
-local domains, cf.
[
7
, Theorem 2.1.5]
.

The class of
h
-local domains is ubiquitously associated with many classical problems in the study of modules over non-noetherian domains, see
[
20
]
and the recent survey
[
23
]
.
Non-finitely generated projective modules over an
h
-local domain are free
[
14
]
. Thus, what is of interest is the description of projective modules over algebras on such domains.

Our interest in this setting comes from our paper
[
1
]
, in which we studied domains for which the class of direct sums of finitely generated torsion-free modules is closed under direct summands. If
\Lambda
is the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated torsion-free module
M
over an
h
-local domain
R
, then
\Lambda
is torsion-free and
\Lambda\otimes_{R}Q
is simple artinian. If, in addition,
R
has Krull dimension
1
and all the direct summands of a direct sum of copies of
R\oplus M
are again a direct sum of finitely generated modules, it was proved in
[
1
]
that
\Lambda
is locally semiperfect. Therefore, our results show that over such
\Lambda
all countably generated projective modules are relatively big.

It is interesting to recall that Procesi and Small
[
27
]
proved that the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated module over a commutative ring or, in general, over a ring satisfying a polynomial identity, is also a ring satisfying a polynomial identity. As far as we know, this point of view has not been so much examined in the world of finitely generated modules, endomorphism rings and direct sum decompositions, and certainly, it seems it should be worthwhile to explore it.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
1
we give the basic definitions and notation. We introduce the needed background, presenting the concept of relatively big projective module, the
(*)
-condition and the main results relating both notions. In Section
2
we examine the monoid of isomorphism classes of relatively big projective modules, its algebraic properties and explore the right/left (non)symmetry of the monoids; check Example
2.7
and Corollary
2.9
.
In Section
3
we show that countably generated, projective right modules lift modulo a right
T
-nilpotent ideal, cf. Lemma
3.1
. We prove that for a right
T
-nilpotent ideal
N
of
\Lambda
there are monoid isomorphisms between
V^{*}(\Lambda)
and
V^{*}(\Lambda/N)
. This isomorphism restricts to isomorphisms between
V(\Lambda)
and
V(\Lambda/N)
, and between
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
and
V(\Lambda/N)\sqcup B(\Lambda/N)
, cf. Corollary
3.3
. In Section
4
, these results are an important ingredient to prove that right noetherian PI-rings satisfy the
(*)
-condition (Theorem
4.8
), and to conclude that
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)=V^{*}(\Lambda)
for
\Lambda
a right noetherian PI-ring in Corollary
4.9
. In particular, this holds for module-finite algebras over commutative noetherian rings (cf. Corollary
4.10
).

In the rest of the paper, we turn to algebras over
h
-local domains. Section
5
discusses Package Deal Theorems for projective modules over
\Lambda
, whenever
\Lambda
is an algebra over an
h
-local domain; our goal is twofold: recalling the results from
[
1
]
and proving some extensions needed in the subsequent sections. Section
6
focuses on semiperfect and locally semiperfect algebras. We observe in Proposition
6.4
that for semiperfect rings, all countably generated projective modules are relatively big.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
. In the remaining sections, we study locally semiperfect algebras
\Lambda
over
R
such that
\Lambda\otimes_{R}Q
is simple artinian. In Section
6
, we show that for such algebras, every trace of a projective module is the trace of a finitely generated projective module (cf. Lemma
6.7
) and that all countably generated projective
\Lambda
-modules are relatively big (cf. Theorem
6.17
).

In Section
7
, we study in detail the genus of countably generated projective modules over such algebras, that is, the isomorphism classes of countably generated projective modules over the localizations at the maximal ideals of
R
. This is a submonoid of the product of
V^{*}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})
, for
\mathfrak{m}
maximal ideal of
R
, that can be described quite explicitly in terms of solutions in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
of a system of linear equations with coefficients in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

In Proposition
7.1
, we show that two countably generated projective modules that are not finitely generated are isomorphic if and only if they have the same genus, that is, whenever all their localizations at maximal ideals of
R
are isomorphic. This is also true for finitely generated projective modules, provided
\Lambda
is a finitely generated algebra over a semilocal domain
R
, cf. Proposition
7.2
.

These tools allow us to study in Section
8
when every projective module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules. Finally, in Section
9
, we apply our results to
\Lambda
the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated torsion-free module
M
over an
h
-local domain
R
of Krull dimension
1
. In Theorem
9.3
, we give necessary and sufficient conditions that imply that any direct summand of an arbitrary direct sum of copies of
M
is a sum of finitely generated modules. These results complement and clarify the ones obtained in
[
1
]
.

Throughout the paper, rings are associative with
1
, and morphisms are unital. In each section, we try to be very precise about the setting we are working with. We mainly use
R
to denote a commutative ring and
\Lambda
to denote a non-necessarily commutative ring or an algebra over a domain
R
.

The set
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
and
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

1.
The monoid of infinitely generated projective modules

In this section we will cover the basic notions on projective modules and their trace ideals. We start by introducing the notation already used in
[
10
]
for monoids of projective modules.

Notation 1.1
.

For a given ring
\Lambda
, we write
V_{r}(\Lambda)
(resp.
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)
) for the set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of finitely generated (resp. countably generated) projective right
\Lambda
-modules. If
P
is a finitely (resp. countably) generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, we denote its representative in
V_{r}(\Lambda)
(resp. in
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)
) by
[P]
.

Similarly, we write
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
(resp.
V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)
) for the corresponding sets of representatives of the isomorphism classes of projective left
\Lambda
-modules and follow the same notation for the representatives of the isomorphism classes in these sets.

Unless stated otherwise, our modules will always be right modules, so we will write
V(\Lambda)
and
V^{*}(\Lambda)
instead of
V_{r}(\Lambda)
and
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)
, respectively, and leave the previous notation in cases where both right and left modules appear. All the results stated for
V(\Lambda)
and
V^{*}(\Lambda)
can be stated in the context of left
\Lambda
-modules for
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
and
V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)
, respectively.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that both
V(\Lambda)
and
V^{*}(\Lambda)
are commutative monoids with addition defined by
[P]+[Q]=[P\oplus Q]
. Note that
V(\Lambda)
is a submonoid of
V^{*}(\Lambda)
and that
V^{*}(\Lambda)\setminus V(\Lambda)
is a subsemigroup of
V^{*}(\Lambda)
.

An important object related to projective modules is the set of their trace ideals. We recall the definition and some basic properties.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module. Then the
trace
of
M
in
\Lambda
is the two-sided ideal of
\Lambda
defined as

\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)=\sum_{f\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)}f(M).

We will denote by
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)
(resp.
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
) the set of ideals of
\Lambda
that are traces of countably generated projective right (resp. left)
\Lambda
-modules. When we simply write
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
we mean
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)
.

Remark 1.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring and let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module with endomorphism ring
S=\End_{\Lambda}(M)
. Then
M
is also a left
S
-module. Since
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
is both a right
S
-module and a left
\Lambda
-module, the tensor product
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)\otimes_{S}M
is well-defined. Then
\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)
is the image of the
trace map
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau\colon}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)\otimes_{S}M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f\otimes x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f(x).}

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring.

(i)

Let
M,N
be right
\Lambda
-modules. If
M
is a homomorphic image of
N
, then
\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)\subseteq\Tr_{\Lambda}(N)
.

(ii)

Let
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in A}
be a family of right
\Lambda
-modules. Then

\Tr_{\Lambda}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in A}M_{i}\Big)=\sum_{i\in A}\Tr_{\Lambda}(M_{i}).

(iii)

[
17
, Proposition 2.40]
Let
J
be an ideal of
\Lambda
, and let
P
be a projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then
\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)\subseteq J
if and only if
PJ=P
. In addition, the trace of a projective module is always an idempotent ideal.

We recall the following properties of traces of projective modules.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, let
P
be a right
\Lambda
-module, and let
J
be an ideal of
\Lambda
. Then
(\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)+J)/J\subseteq\Tr_{\Lambda/J}(P/PJ)
. Moreover, if
P
is projective, then
(\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)+J)/J=\Tr_{\Lambda/J}(P/PJ)
.

Remark 1.7
.

For a commutative ring
\Lambda
, Vasconcelos showed that trace ideals of projective modules are exactly the pure ideals (see page 430 in
[
31
]
or Proposition 2.12 in
[
13
]
).

In
[
32
]
, Whitehead gave a characterization of ideals that are traces of projective modules for general rings. In particular, he showed that an idempotent ideal of
\Lambda
that is finitely generated as a left ideal is always the trace of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. We shall use this result throughout the paper, sometimes without prior acknowledgment.

An alternative approach to the characterization of the traces of projective modules can be found in the paper
[
13
]
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring with Jacobson radical
J(\Lambda)
, and let
I
and
I^{\prime}
be elements in
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
. Then

(i)

I=I^{\prime}
if and only if
I+J(\Lambda)=I^{\prime}+J(\Lambda)
.

(ii)

(I+J(\Lambda))/J(\Lambda)\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda/J(\Lambda))
, so the assignment defined by
I\mapsto(I+J(\Lambda))/J(\Lambda)
defines an injective map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}(\Lambda/J(\Lambda))
.

Proof.

(i)
is included in
[
13
, Corollary 2.9]
.

To prove
(ii)
, note that if
I
is the trace of the projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
then, by Lemma
1.6
,
(I+J(\Lambda))/J(\Lambda)
is the trace of
P/PJ(\Lambda)
. This shows that
\varphi
is well defined. The injectivity of
\varphi
follows from
(i)
.

The following lemma states that projective modules can be lifted modulo a trace ideal. This is a crucial result in the development of the theory of relatively big projective modules.

Lemma 1.9
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.1, Corollary 3.2]

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
I
be the trace of a projective right
\Lambda
-module. Let
P^{\prime}
be a projective right
\Lambda/I
-module. Then there is a projective right
\Lambda
-module
X
such that
I\subseteq\Tr_{\Lambda}(X)
,
X/XI\cong P^{\prime}
and
\Tr_{\Lambda}(X)/I=\Tr_{\Lambda/I}(P^{\prime})
.

Moreover, if
I
is an element in
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
and
P^{\prime}
is countably generated, then
X
can be taken to be countably generated.

1.1.
Relatively big projectives

First we introduce the auxiliary notion of big projective module with respect to an ideal. It was first introduced by P. Příhoda in
[
25
]
as a way of generalizing Bass’ uniformly big projective modules. We motivate the definition of this class of projective modules by comparing it with a characterization of the class of modules generated by a projective module.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module. We denote by
\mathrm{Gen}(M)
the class of modules generated by
M
, that is the class of right
\Lambda
-modules which are a homomorphic image of a direct sum of copies of
M
.

Lemma 1.10
.

[
10
, Lemma 2.10]

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
P
be a projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace ideal
I
. Then

\mathrm{Gen}(P)=\{M\in\mbox{\rm Mod-}\Lambda:MI=M\}.

Definition 1.11
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, and let
J
be an ideal of
\Lambda
. The module
P
is said to be
big with respect to
J
, or
J
-big
, if every countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
Q
satisfying
QJ=Q
is a homomorphic image of
P
.

The following remarks show that the significant case of Definition
1.11
is when
J
is a trace ideal of a projective module.

Remarks 1.12
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
J
be an ideal of
\Lambda
.

(1)

If
Q
is a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module such that
QJ=Q
then
\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q)\leq J
. Therefore, if
J
does not contain a non-zero element of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
then, by Lemma
1.5
, Definition
1.11
is trivially fulfilled, so any countably generated projective module is
J
-big or, equivalently,
0
-big.

(2)

If
P
is
J
-big, and
K
is a non-zero element of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
such that
K\subseteq J
, then
P
is
K
-big (cf. Lemma
1.5
).

Observe that

J^{\prime}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\leq J\\
K\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\end{subarray}}K

is again a trace ideal of a projective module by Lemma
1.5
. Then a countably generated projective module is
J
-big if and only if it is
J^{\prime}
-big.

We stress that
J^{\prime}
does not need to be the trace of a countably generated projective
\Lambda
-module. So it is not necessarily an element in
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
.

In the next lemma, we summarize what it means to be
J
-big with respect to
J\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
P,Q
be countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-modules. Let
J=\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q)
. Then

(i)

P
is
J
-big if and only if
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P
.

(ii)

If
P
is
J
-big and
P/PJ
is finitely generated, then
J
is the minimal ideal of
\Lambda
such that
P/PJ
is finitely generated.

Proof.

(i)
. Since
J=\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q)=\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q^{(\omega)})
, by Lemma
1.5
,
Q^{(\omega)}J=Q^{(\omega)}
. If
P
is
J
-big, then there is an epimorphism
P\to Q^{(\omega)}
. Since
Q^{(\omega)}
is projective, this epimorphism splits, so
Q^{(\omega)}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
P
. Write
P\cong P^{\prime}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
. Then,
P\cong P^{\prime}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P^{\prime}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
.

Conversely, if
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P
, then
Q^{(\omega)}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
P
, so there exists an epimorphism
P\to Q^{(\omega)}
.
Any countably generated projective module with trace contained in
J
is an epimorphic image of
Q^{(\omega)}
by Lemma
1.10
, so
P
is
J
-big.

Statement
(ii)
is
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
.

The following lemma states that the isomorphism class of a
J
-big projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
satisfying that
P/PJ
is finitely generated, is determined, up to isomorphism, by the ideal
J
and the isomorphism class of
P/PJ
.

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, let
J\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
, and let
P
be a
J
-big module such that
P/PJ
is finitely generated. Then:

(i)

If
P^{\prime}
is a projective module isomorphic to
P
, then
P^{\prime}
is
J
-big and
P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J
is finitely generated.

(ii)

[
10
, Proposition 3.4]
Let
P^{\prime}
be a countably generated projective module that is
J^{\prime}
-big for some
J^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
and such that
P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J^{\prime}
is finitely generated. Then
P\cong P^{\prime}
if and only if
J=J^{\prime}
and
P/PJ\cong P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J(=P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J^{\prime})
.

Proof.
We only need to prove
(i)
. Notice that a module isomorphic to a
J
-big module is also
J
-big. Moreover, if
P\cong P^{\prime}
then
P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J
is a homomorphic image of
P/PJ
. So if
P/PJ
is finitely generated, then so is
P^{\prime}/P^{\prime}J
.

Now we give the definition of
relatively big
projective which is crucial for the rest of the paper.

Definition 1.15
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. The module
P
is said to be
relatively big
if there exists a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
Q
with trace ideal
J
satisfying:

(1)

P
is
J
-big, that is,
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P
.

(2)

P/PJ
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/J
-module.

We will also use the terminology
P
is
relatively
J
-big
when we want to specify the ideal
J\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
.

Example 1.16
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Let
X
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, and let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then
X\oplus P^{(\omega)}
is relatively big: take
Q=P
and
J=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)
in the Definition
1.15
.

In particular, taking
X=\{0\}
, we see that
P^{(\omega)}
is relatively big for any countably generated projective module
P
. Also, taking
P=\{0\}
we see that any finitely generated projective module is relatively big.

Example
1.16
, is a particular instance of the following general construction of relatively big projective modules.

Lemma 1.17
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, and let
J
be the trace ideal of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
Q
. If
P/PJ
is finitely generated, then
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
is relatively
J
-big.

Proof.

Note that
(P\oplus Q^{(\omega)})/(P\oplus Q^{(\omega)})J\cong P/PJ
, which is finitely generated. Since
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
, by Lemma
1.13
,
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
is
J
-big.

Remark 1.18
.

Note that an indecomposable relatively big projective module must be finitely generated.

The following result characterizes when every relatively big projective module is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules.

Proposition 1.19
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Then every relatively big projective right
\Lambda
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules if and only if the following two properties are satisfied:

(1)

If
J
is the trace ideal of a projective right
\Lambda
-module, then there is a right
\Lambda
-module
P
, which is a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules such that
\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)=J
.

(2)

If
J
is the trace ideal of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module and
P^{\prime}
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/J
-module, then there is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
X
such that
P^{\prime}\cong X/XJ
.

Proof.

Assume that (1) and (2) are satisfied, and let
P
be a relatively big projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then there exists a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
Q
with trace ideal
J
such that
P
is relatively
J
-big.

By (1),
Q
can be taken to be a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules. By (2) applied to
P/PJ
, there exists a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
X
such that
X/XJ\cong P/PJ
. Let
P^{\prime}=X\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
. By Lemma
1.17
,
P^{\prime}
is relatively big, and by Lemma
1.14
,
P\cong P^{\prime}
, so it is a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules.

Assume now that every relatively big projective right
\Lambda
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules. Let
P
be a projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace
J
. By
[
16
, Theorem 1]
,
P=\bigoplus_{i\in I}P_{i}
is a direct sum of countably generated projective modules. By Example
1.16
,
P_{i}^{(\omega)}
is relatively big. Therefore,
P_{i}^{(\omega)}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules for each
i\in I
. Hence,
P^{(\omega)}
is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules and
J=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P^{(\omega)})
. This gives (1).

To prove (2), let
Q
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace
J
, and let
P^{\prime}
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/J
-module. By Lemma
1.9
, since
J
is the trace of a countably generated projective module, there exists a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
J\subseteq\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)
,
P/PJ\cong P^{\prime}
, and
\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)/J=\Tr_{\Lambda/I}(P^{\prime})
.

By Lemma
1.17
,
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
is relatively big, so it is a direct sum of finitely generated projective modules. Write
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i}
with
X_{i}
a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module for each
i\in I
. Since
(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i})/(\bigoplus_{i\in I}X_{i})J\cong P^{\prime}
is finitely generated, there is a finite subset
I_{0}\subseteq I
such that
(\bigoplus_{i\in I_{0}}X_{i})/(\bigoplus_{i\in I_{0}}X_{i})J\cong P^{\prime}
, so
X=\bigoplus_{i\in I_{0}}X_{i}
is the finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module we were looking for.

1.2.
Finite generation modulo an ideal and the
(*)
-condition

In this subsection, we take a closer look at condition (2) in Definition
1.15
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module.
Consider the set

\mathcal{I}(M)=\{I\unlhd\Lambda\mid M/MI\mbox{ is finitely generated}\}

We recall the following crucial properties of a descending sequence of right ideals associated with a column finite idempotent matrix. The result is taken from
[
13
]
, but all the necessary ideas are already in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 1.20
.

[
13
, Lemmas 5.1 and 5.2]

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Let
A=(a_{i,j})
be an
\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}
-matrix, with entries in
\Lambda
, that is a column finite matrix and such that
A^{2}=A
. Set
P=A\Lambda^{(\mathbb{N})}
. For any
k\geq 0
, let
I_{k}=\sum_{i>k,j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{ij}\Lambda
. Then,

(i)

\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)=\Lambda I_{0}
.

(ii)

For any
k\geq 0
and any
0<i\leq k
,

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}a_{ij}\Lambda+\Lambda I_{k}/\Lambda I_{k}=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq k}a_{i\ell}\Lambda+\Lambda I_{k}/\Lambda I_{k}.

In particular, if
0\leq n\leq k
,
I_{n}+\Lambda I_{k}/\Lambda I_{k}
is finitely generated.

(iii)

For any
k\geq 0
, there exists
n_{k}>k
such that
I_{n_{k}}I_{k}=I_{n_{k}}
. In particular, the descending sequence of two-sided ideals
(\Lambda I_{k})_{k\geq 0}
has a subsequence
(J_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of two-sided ideals satisfying that:

(a)

for any
n\geq 0
,
J_{n+1}J_{n}=J_{n+1}
;

(b)

for any
k\geq 0
, there exists
n
and
\ell
such that
J_{n}=\Lambda I_{k+\ell}\subseteq\Lambda I_{k}
.

(iv)

For any
k\geq 0
,
\Lambda I_{k}\in\mathcal{I}(P)
. Moreover, for any
I\in\mathcal{I}(P)
, there exists
k_{0}
such that
\Lambda I_{k_{0}}\leq I
.

(v)

\mathcal{I}(P)
has minimal elements if and only if there exists
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
such that
\Lambda I_{k_{0}}=\Lambda I_{k_{0}+\ell}
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case,
I_{k_{0}}=I_{k_{0}}^{2}
, and
\Lambda I_{k_{0}}
is the minimal element of
\mathcal{I}(P)
.

Statement
(v)
in Lemma
1.20
motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.21
.

[
25
]

A ring
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
provided that every descending chain of two-sided ideals

J_{1}\supseteq J_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq J_{k}\supseteq\cdots

such that
J_{k+1}J_{k}=J_{k+1}
, for each
k\geq 1
, is eventually stationary.

The following statement explains the role of condition
(*)
in our discussion.

Corollary 1.22
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. If
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
, then
\mathcal{I}(P)
has a minimal element, and this minimal element is an idempotent ideal.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.20

(v)
,
\mathcal{I}(P)
has a minimal element if and only if the descending sequence of two-sided ideals
(\Lambda I_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is stationary. By Lemma
1.20

(iii)
, this will happen if and only if the partial subsequence
(J_{n})_{n\geq 0}
given by that statement is stationary. Since such a partial subsequence satisfies that
J_{n+1}J_{n}=J_{n+1}
, for any
n\geq 0
, it is stationary because
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
.

The fact that the minimal element in
\mathcal{I}(P)
is an idempotent ideal follows from Lemma
1.20

(v)
.

Theorem 1.23
.

[
25
, Lemma 2.8]

Let
\Lambda
be a noetherian ring. Let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module such that
\mathcal{I}(P)
has a minimal element
J
. Then
J
is the trace ideal of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, and
P
is
J
-big.
In particular, if
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
, then any countably generated projective right module is relatively big.

Remarks 1.24
.

(1)
Let
\Lambda
be a semilocal ring, and let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then, by
[
13
, Proposition 5.6]
,
\mathcal{I}(P)
has a minimal element
J
. Then
J
is an idempotent ideal, but it does not need to be the trace of a projective module as it can even be contained in the Jacobson radical.

(2)
We do not know whether condition
(*)
is left-right symmetric, even for the case of noetherian rings.

(3)
There are noetherian rings with infinite descending sequences of idempotent ideals, so they do not satisfy condition
(*)
. Over such rings there may be countably generated projective modules that are not relatively big. See, for example, the last section of
[
13
]
.

2.
The monoid of relatively big projective modules

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. We denote by
B(\Lambda)
the subset of
V^{*}(\Lambda)
of isomorphism classes of relatively big projective right
\Lambda
-modules which are not finitely generated. We denote by
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
the disjoint union of
V(\Lambda)
and
B(\Lambda)
, which is also a subset of
V^{*}(\Lambda)
, and consists of the isomorphism classes of all the relatively big projective right
\Lambda
-modules.

It is important to observe that, by Lemma
1.14
, if
P
is a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module such that
[P]\in B(\Lambda)
, then
P
is relatively big, so there exists a non-zero ideal
J\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
such that
P/PJ
is finitely generated. Moreover,
J
and
P/PJ
uniquely determine
[P]
. We will use this fact freely throughout the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Then

(i)

B(\Lambda)
is a subsemigroup of
V^{*}(\Lambda)\setminus V(\Lambda)
.

(ii)

V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
is a submonoid of
V^{*}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

(i)
. Let
[P_{1}],[P_{2}]\in B(\Lambda)
. Then, for each
i=1,2
, there exists a countably generated non-zero projective right
\Lambda
-module
Q_{i}
with trace ideal
J_{i}
such that
P_{i}\oplus Q_{i}^{(\omega)}\cong P_{i}
and
P_{i}/P_{i}J_{i}
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/J_{i}
-module. Take
P=P_{1}\oplus P_{2}
and
Q=Q_{1}\oplus Q_{2}
. Then

P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\cong P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\oplus Q_{1}^{(\omega)}\oplus Q_{2}^{(\omega)}\cong P_{1}\oplus Q_{1}^{(\omega)}\oplus P_{2}\oplus Q_{2}^{(\omega)}\cong P_{1}\oplus P_{2}=P.

By Lemma
1.5
,
J=\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q)=\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q_{1})+\Tr_{\Lambda}(Q_{2})=J_{1}+J_{2}
, so

P/PJ\cong\frac{P_{1}}{P_{1}(J_{1}+J_{2})}\oplus\frac{P_{2}}{P_{2}(J_{1}+J_{2})}\cong\frac{P_{1}/P_{1}J_{1}}{P_{1}(J_{1}+J_{2})/P_{1}J_{1}}\oplus\frac{P_{2}/P_{2}J_{2}}{P_{2}(J_{1}+J_{2})/P_{2}J_{2}}

and
P/PJ
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/J
-module because it is a direct sum of quotients of finitely generated projective modules. Therefore, as
P
is not finitely generated,
[P]\in B(\Lambda)
.

Statement
(ii)
is proved similarly, taking into account that the modules in
V(\Lambda)
are the relatively
0
-big projective modules.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
. Then, there is a semigroup embedding
\iota_{I}\colon V(\Lambda/I)\hookrightarrow V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
. The image of
\iota_{I}
is all the isomorphism classes of relatively
I
-big projective right
\Lambda
-modules.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace ideal
I
. Let
P^{\prime}
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/I
-module. By Lemma
1.9
, there exists a projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
I\subseteq\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)
,
P/PI\cong P^{\prime}
, and
\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)/I=\Tr_{\Lambda/I}(P^{\prime})
.

By Lemma
1.17
,
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
is relatively
I
-big, and then
[P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]\in V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
. The isomorphism class of
P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
is uniquely determined by
I
and
P^{\prime}
. Hence, there is a well-defined embedding
\iota_{I}\colon V(\Lambda/I)\longrightarrow V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
defined by
\iota_{I}([P^{\prime}])=[P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]
.

The map
\iota_{I}
is a semigroup homomorphism because, for
P^{\prime}_{1}
and
P^{\prime}_{2}
in
V(\Lambda/I)
:

\displaystyle\iota_{I}([P_{1}^{\prime}\oplus P_{2}^{\prime}])

\displaystyle=[P_{1}\oplus P_{2}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]=[P_{1}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}\oplus P_{2}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]

\displaystyle=[P_{1}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]+[P_{2}\oplus Q^{(\omega)}]=\iota_{I}([P_{1}^{\prime}])+\iota_{I}([P_{2}^{\prime}]).

where for
i=1,2
,
P_{i}
denotes a countably generated projective module such that
P_{i}/P_{i}\cong P^{\prime}_{i}
, which exists because of Lemma
1.9
.

Let
P
be a relatively
I
-big projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then
P/PI
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/I
-module, and
\iota_{I}([P/PI])=[P]
.

If
I\neq 0
, the embedding
\iota_{I}
of Lemma
2.3
is not a monoid embedding because
[0]
maps to the isomorphism class of a non-finitely generated projective module.

Remark 2.4
.

Consider the set
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
and the disjoint union
\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V(\Lambda/I)
. We will denote the elements of
\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V(\Lambda/I)
by
([P],I)
with
[P]\in V(\Lambda/I)
. Note that if
I\subseteq J
, there is a morphism of monoids
f_{JI}\colon V(\Lambda/I)\to V(\Lambda/J)
defined by
f_{JI}([X],I)=([X/XJ],J)
with
[X]\in V(\Lambda/I)
. Therefore,
f_{JI}(V(\Lambda/I))\subseteq V(\Lambda/J)
. Define addition in
\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V(\Lambda/I)
by

\displaystyle([P_{1}],I)+([P_{2}],J)

\displaystyle=f_{I+J,I}([P_{1}],I)+f_{I+J,J}([P_{2}],J)

\displaystyle=([P_{1}/P_{1}J],I+J)+([P_{2}/P_{2}I],I+J)

\displaystyle=([P_{1}/P_{1}J\oplus P_{2}/P_{2}I],I+J)

for each
[P_{1}]\in V(\Lambda/I)
,
[P_{2}]\in V(\Lambda/J)
. Therefore, the universal property of the disjoint union and Lemma
2.3
imply that there exists a monoid embedding

\iota\colon\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V(\Lambda/I)\to V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda).

The following result states that
\iota
is an isomorphism of monoids.

Corollary 2.5
.

[
10
, Theorem 3.8]

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. Then

(i)

\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V(\Lambda/I)\cong V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
as monoids.

(ii)

\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}}V(\Lambda/I)\cong B(\Lambda)
as semigroups.

Proof.

The isomorphism in
(i)
is given by
\iota([X],I)=\iota_{I}([X])
, where
\iota_{I}
is the embedding from Lemma
2.3
. By Lemma
1.14
,
\iota
is bijective.

To prove
(ii)
, note that elements in
V(\Lambda)
can be thought of as elements in
V(\Lambda/I)
for
I=\{0\}
, so
(ii)
follows from
(i)
.

Remark 2.6
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring. It is well known that the functor
(-)^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(-,\Lambda)
induces a duality between finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-modules and finitely generated projective left
\Lambda
-modules. So that,
\varphi\colon V_{r}(\Lambda)\to V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
defined by
\varphi([P])=[P^{*}]
, is an isomorphism of monoids. Moreover,
P
and
P^{*}
have the same trace (cf.
[
2
, Exercise 22.1]
).

In the next example, we show that the canonical monoid isomorphism
V_{r}(\Lambda)\cong V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
of Remark
2.6
does not, in general, extend to an isomorphism between the monoids
V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)
and
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
. We will show that it does extend, provided that
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Example 2.7
.

In
[
12
, Example 3.6 (iv)]
it is given an example of a semilocal ring
\Lambda
such that
\Lambda/J(\Lambda)\cong D_{1}\times D_{2}
, with
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
division rings, such that any projective left
\Lambda
-module is free but this is not true on the right. Though, on the right, it is still true that
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)=V^{*}(\Lambda)
.

In such
\Lambda
, what happens is that one of the maximal ideals contains a non-zero trace of a countably generated projective right
R
-module. Therefore
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)=\{0,\Lambda\}\subsetneq\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)
. Then
V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)
and
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
are not isomorphic. This cannot happen if
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Assume
\Lambda
is a ring such that
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
. We define a map
V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)\mapsto V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
that extends the isomorphism between the monoids of finitely generated projective modules of Remark
2.6
by
([P],I)\mapsto([P^{*}],I)
for any
([P],I)\in V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)
. We must check that
([P_{1}],I)+([P_{2}],J)
is mapped to
([P_{1}^{*}],I)+([P_{2}^{*}],J)
.

The following technical lemma is crucial for our discussion.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
I,J
be ideals of
\Lambda
. Let
P
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/I
-module. Then

\Hom_{\Lambda/(I+J)}(P/PJ,\Lambda/(I+J))\cong\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)/((I+J)/I\cdot\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)).

Proof.

Note that we can identify
P/PJ
with
P\otimes_{\Lambda/I}\Lambda/(I+J)
. Then

\displaystyle\Hom_{\Lambda/(I+J)}(P/PJ,\Lambda/(I+J))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\Lambda/(I+J)}(P\otimes_{\Lambda/I}\Lambda/(I+J),\Lambda/(I+J))

\displaystyle\cong\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/(I+J)).

where in the second isomorphism, we have used the Hom-tensor adjunction. Now, we want to prove that
\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/(I+J))\cong\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)/J\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
as left
\Lambda/I
-modules.

Consider the map
\varphi\colon\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)\to\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/(I+J))
defined by
\varphi(f)=\pi\circ f
, where
\pi\colon\Lambda/I\to\Lambda/(I+J)
is the projection modulo
(I+J)/I
. Clearly, it is a homomorphism of left
\Lambda/I
-modules. Let
g\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/(I+J))
. Since
P
is projective, there is a lift
G\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
making the following diagram commute

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda/I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda/(I+J)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Therefore,
\varphi
is surjective. The kernel of this homomorphism is

\displaystyle\ker\varphi

\displaystyle=\{f\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)\mid\pi\circ f=0\}

\displaystyle=\{f\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)\mid f(x)\in(I+J)/I\text{ for every }x\in P\}

\displaystyle=\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,(I+J)/I).

It remains to prove that
\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,(I+J)/I)=(I+J)/I\cdot\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
.

Let
f\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,(I+J)/I)
. Consider
(I+J)/I
as a right
\Lambda/I
-module. Then there exists an epimorphism
\pi\colon(\Lambda/I)^{(A)}\to(I+J)/I
for some set
A
. Since
P
is projective, there exists a lift
F\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,(\Lambda/I)^{(A)})
such that
\pi\circ F=f
, that is, the following diagram commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Lambda/I)^{(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(I+J)/I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Since
P
is finitely generated, the image of
F
is contained in a finite number of copies of
\Lambda/I
, namely
(\Lambda/I)^{(A_{0})}
, with
A_{0}\subseteq A
finite. Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in A}
be the canonical basis of
(\Lambda/I)^{(A)}
. Then

f(x)=\pi\circ F(x)=\pi\Big(\sum_{i\in A_{0}}x_{i}a_{i}\Big)=\sum_{i\in A_{0}}\pi(x_{i})a_{i}=\sum_{i\in A_{0}}\pi(x_{i})\cdot\varphi_{i}(F(x)).

where
\varphi_{i}\colon(\Lambda/I)^{(A_{0})}\to\Lambda/I
denotes the projection into the
i
th
component,
i\in A_{0}
. Hence,
f=\sum_{i\in A_{0}}\pi(x_{i})\cdot(\varphi_{i}\circ F)\in(I+J)/I\cdot\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
.

Conversely, let
F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}+I)\cdot F_{i}\in(I+J)/I\cdot\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
for some
y_{1},\dotsc,y_{n}\in J
and
F_{1},\dotsc,F_{n}\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)
. Then, regarding the product of elements as a composition of homomorphisms,
F\in\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,(I+J)/I)
. Therefore, by the First Isomorphism Theorem,

\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I)/((I+J)/I\cdot\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/I))\cong\Hom_{\Lambda/I}(P,\Lambda/(I+J)),

so the statement is proved.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a ring such that
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
. Then there is an isomorphism of monoids between
V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)
and
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\colon\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V_{r}(\Lambda/I)\to\bigsqcup_{I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)}V_{\ell}(\Lambda/I)
be the map defined by
\varphi([P],I)=([P^{*}],I)
. By Remark
2.6
, it is well-defined and bijective. By Lemma
2.8
, it is a monoid homomorphism since

\displaystyle\varphi(([P_{1}],I)+([P_{2}],J))

\displaystyle=\varphi([P_{1}/P_{1}J\oplus P_{2}/P_{2}I],I+J)

8
) is stationary over
\Lambda/P
for any minimal prime ideal
P
of
\Lambda
. Then there exists
k_{0}\geq 1
such that
J_{k_{0}+\ell}^{2}=J_{k_{0}+\ell}
for any
\ell\geq 0
.

Proof.

(i)
. By
[
9
, Theorem 3.11]
,
N(\Lambda)
is nilpotent. Assume that (
7
) is stationary over
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
. Then there exists
k_{1}\geq 0
such that
I_{k_{1}}+N(\Lambda)=I_{k_{1}+\ell}+N(\Lambda)
for any
\ell\geq 0
. By Lemma
4.5
, this implies that
I_{k_{1}+1+\ell}^{2}=I_{k_{1}+1+\ell}
for any
\ell\geq 0
. Since idempotent ideals that are equal modulo a nilpotent ideal must be equal, the claim follows.

A similar argument works if (
8
) is stationary.

(ii)
. Since
\Lambda
is a semiprime right Goldie ring, it has a finite number of minimal prime ideals
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
, and its product is zero
[
9
, Proposition 7.1]
.

By hypothesis, there exists
k_{1}
such that
I_{k_{1}+\ell}+P_{i}=I_{k_{1}}+P_{i}
for any
\ell\geq 0
and any
i=1,\dots,n
.

As in
(i)
, we use Lemma
4.5
to deduce that
k_{0}=k_{1}+1
satisfies the conclusion of the statement.

Statement
(iii)
is proved in a similar way.

Robson and Small proved in
[
29
]
that PI-rings satisfying the ascending chain condition on two-sided ideals have a finite number of idempotent ideals.

We shall prove in Theorem
4.8
that, in addition, right noetherian PI-rings satisfy condition
(*)
and its left analog, which we call
(**)
. The proof is patterned after that of Robson and Small.

First, we provide some basic definitions needed to follow the proof.

Definition 4.7
.

A ring
\Lambda
is said to be a
polynomial identity ring
, a
PI-ring
for short, if there exists
n
such that
\Lambda
satisfies a monic polynomial of
\mathbf{Z}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
. That is, there exists a polynomial in non-commuting variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with coefficients in
\mathbf{Z}
, say
f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, such that:

(1)

f(r_{1},\dots,r_{n})=0
for any
r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\in\Lambda
,

(2)

the coefficient of one of the monomials of the highest degree of
f
is one.

The polynomials in
\mathbf{Z}\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
of the form
\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}a(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}
, where
\Sigma_{n}
denotes the permutation group of
n
elements and
a(\sigma)\in\mathbf{Z}
, are said to be multilinear polynomials or multilinear identities of degree
n
.

Of special importance in the theory is the
standard identity
of degree
n

s_{n}=s_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\varepsilon(\sigma)x_{\sigma(1)}\cdots x_{\sigma(n)}

where
\varepsilon(\sigma)
denotes the sign of the permutation
\sigma
. For example, if
R
is a non-zero commutative ring, a theorem of Amitsur and Levitzki shows that
M_{n}(R)
satisfies the standard identity
s_{m}
for any
m\geq 2n
and does not satisfy
s_{2n-1}
, cf.
[
21
, §13.3.2 and §13.3.2]
. Moreover, if a PI-ring satisfies
s_{m}
, then it satisfies
s_{t}
for any
t\geq m
, cf.
[
21
, §13.3.3]
.

A theorem of Kaplansky shows that if a ring satisfies a monic identity of degree at most
d
, then it satisfies a monic multilinear identity of degree at most
d
(cf.
[
21
, §13.1.9]
).

It is possible to define the
PI-degree
of a central simple algebra
\Lambda
(that is, a simple algebra that is finite dimensional over its center) as the minimum
n
such that
\Lambda
satisfies
s_{2n}
and not
s_{2n-1}
. In this case, we write
\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}(\Lambda)=n
.

Prime PI-rings
\Lambda
localized at the non-zero elements of the center are central simple algebras. Then, by definition, the PI-degree of
\Lambda
is the PI-degree of this localization. For details, check
[
21
, §13.6.7]
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\Lambda
be a right noetherian PI-ring. Then
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
and its symmetric
(**)
.

Proof.

Assume the statement is not true. By noetherian induction, we may assume that
\Lambda
is a right noetherian PI-ring that does not satisfy condition
(*)
, but so does any proper quotient ring of
\Lambda
.

By
[
29
, Theorem 4]
,
\Lambda
has only a finite number of idempotent ideals. By Proposition
4.6
, this implies that
\Lambda
is a prime, right noetherian, PI-ring. Therefore,
\Lambda
is (right and left) noetherian by a theorem of G. Cauchon
[
21
, 13.6.15 Theorem]
. Now we adapt the steps of the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 4]
to obtain a contradiction.

Let

\Lambda\supsetneq I_{1}\supsetneq I_{2}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq I_{k}\supsetneq\cdots

(9)

be a sequence of ideals of
\Lambda
satisfying that
I_{k+1}I_{k}=I_{k+1}
for any
k\geq 1
.
Let
P
be a prime ideal containing
I_{k}
for some
k
and assume that
\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda/P)=\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda)
. By
[
30
, Theorem]
, the set of elements that are regular modulo
P
consists of regular elements of
\Lambda
, forms an Ore set
S
, and the localization
\Lambda S^{-1}
is a local ring with maximal ideal
P\Lambda S^{-1}
. Since
I_{k}
is contained in
P
, so is
I_{k+\ell}
for any
\ell\geq 1
. But in the local ring
\Lambda S^{-1}
,
P\Lambda S^{-1}I_{k+\ell}\Lambda S^{-1}=I_{k+\ell}\Lambda S^{-1}
for any
\ell\geq 1
. As the ring is noetherian, Nakayama’s lemma implies that
I_{k+\ell}\Lambda S^{-1}=\{0\}
for any
\ell\geq 1
. Therefore,
I_{k+\ell}=\{0\}
for any
\ell\geq 1
. This contradicts our hypothesis. Therefore,
\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda/P)<\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda)
for any prime ideal
P
containing
I_{k}
for some
k
.

Let
n=\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda)
. Let
E(\Lambda)
be the non-zero ideal generated by the evaluations in
\Lambda
of
s_{2n-2}
(cf.
[
21
, 13.6.7 Corollary]
). For any fixed
k
, and for any prime ideal
P
containing
I_{k}
, the ideal
E(\Lambda)
is contained in
P
because
\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda/P)<\mathrm{PI\mbox{-}deg}\,(\Lambda)
. Thus,
\Lambda/P
must satisfy
s_{2n-2}
. Therefore,
E(\Lambda)
is contained in the prime radical of
I_{k}
. Then, for any
k
,
there exists
m_{k}
such that
E(\Lambda)^{m_{k}}\subseteq I_{k}
(recall that the prime radical of
\Lambda/I_{k}
is nilpotent by
[
9
, Theorem 3.11]
).

By hypothesis,
\Lambda/E(\Lambda)
satisfies condition
(*)
. This implies that there exists
k_{0}
such that
I_{k_{0}+\ell}+E(\Lambda)=I_{k_{0}}+E(\Lambda)
for any
\ell\geq 0
. By Lemma
4.5
, we deduce that
I_{k_{0}+\ell+1}
is an idempotent ideal for any
\ell\geq 0
. As a consequence, the sequence (
9
) contains infinitely many different idempotent ideals, which gives the desired contradiction with the statement of
[
29
, Theorem 4]
. This concludes the proof of the statement.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
\Lambda
be a right noetherian PI-ring. Then:

(i)

Every idempotent ideal of
\Lambda
is the trace of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module and the trace of a countably generated projective left
\Lambda
-module.

(ii)

V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)=V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)\cong V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)=V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Statement
(i)
follows from the fact that
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
, where
N(\Lambda)
denotes the prime radical of
\Lambda
, is two-sided noetherian
[
21
, 13.6.15]
, and that
N(\Lambda)
is nilpotent. Therefore, if
I
is an idempotent ideal of
\Lambda
, then
(I+N(\Lambda))/N(\Lambda)
is an idempotent ideal of
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
, so it is the trace of a countably generated projective right
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
-module and the trace of a countably generated projective left
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
-module (cf.
[
32
]
). By Lemma
3.1
,
I\in\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)
, and
I\in\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

By Theorem
4.8
,
\Lambda
and
\Lambda/N(\Lambda)
satisfy the
(*)
-condition and the symmetric
(**)
-condition. By Theorem
2.10
,
V^{*}(\Lambda/N(\Lambda))=V(\Lambda/N(\Lambda))\sqcup B(\Lambda/N(\Lambda))
, and from Corollary
3.3
, we deduce that
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)=V_{r}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{r}(\Lambda)
and
V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)=V_{\ell}(\Lambda)\sqcup B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Since, by
(i)
,
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
, the rest of the statement follows from Corollary
2.9
.

It is an interesting open question to determine whether there exists a right noetherian ring
\Lambda
with an idempotent ideal that is the trace of a (countably generated) projective left
\Lambda
-module but not the trace of a projective right
\Lambda
-module. Corollary
4.9
shows that if such an example exists, it cannot be a PI-ring.

The following corollary was our initial motivation for developing these results. As it shows, the basics of the machinery developed in
[
10
]
for the endomorphism ring of finitely generated modules over commutative noetherian rings of Krull dimension
1
are also available in arbitrary Krull dimension. See also §
4.3
for the transfer of information between direct sum decompositions of a finitely generated module and a projective module over its endomorphism ring.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring
R
, and let
\Lambda
be a module-finite
R
-algebra. Then
\Lambda
satisfies condition
(*)
and its symmetric condition
(**)
. Moreover, it has only a finite number of idempotent ideals, and projective modules satisfy the conclusions of Corollary
4.9
.

Proof.
If
\Lambda
is generated by
n
-elements as an
R
-module, then
\Lambda
is a sub-quotient of
M_{n}(R)
, so it is PI and noetherian (cf.
[
21
, 13.4.9 Corollary]
). Then, Theorem
4.8
implies that
\Lambda
satisfies the
(*)
condition and the symmetric
(**)
condition.

The statement about idempotent ideals is a consequence of
[
29
, Theorem 4]
.

Even for a two-sided noetherian ring
\Lambda
, there may be a strict inclusion
V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)\subsetneq V^{*}(\Lambda)
. See, for example, the last two sections of
[
13
]
.

4.3.
An application to direct sums of finitely generated modules

Let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module. By
\mbox{\rm add}(M)
we mean the full subcategory of
\Lambda
-modules that are isomorphic to a direct summand of
M^{n}
for some natural number
n
. By
\mbox{\rm Add}(M)
we denote the full subcategory of
\Lambda
-modules that are isomorphic to a direct summand of an arbitrary direct sum of copies of
M
. It is also convenient to consider the subcategory
\mbox{\rm Add}_{\aleph_{0}}(M)
of direct summands of
M^{(\omega)}
.

Note that
\mbox{\rm Add}(\Lambda)
(
\mbox{\rm add}(\Lambda)
) is the category of (finitely generated) projective right
\Lambda
-modules. While
\mbox{\rm Add}_{\aleph_{0}}(\Lambda)
is the category of countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-modules.

It is possible to transfer the information about projective modules over the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated module
M
to
\mbox{\rm Add}(M)
, via the following well-known result.

Proposition 4.11
.

[
6
, Theorem 4.7]

Let
\Lambda
be a ring, and let
M
be a right
\Lambda
-module. Let
S=\End_{\Lambda}(M)
. Then the functors
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,-)
and
-\otimes_{S}M
induce an equivalence between
\mbox{\rm add}(M)
and
\mbox{\rm add}(S)
.

Moreover, if
M
is finitely generated, then they induce an equivalence between
\mbox{\rm Add}(M)
and
\mbox{\rm Add}(S)
. This equivalence restricts to an equivalence between
\mbox{\rm Add}_{\aleph_{0}}(M)
and
\mbox{\rm Add}_{\aleph_{0}}(S)
.

Corollary 4.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring, and let
\Lambda
be a module-finite
R
-algebra. Let
M
be a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module. Then any indecomposable element of
\mbox{\rm Add}(M)
is finitely generated.

Proof.

As
\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda}(M)
is a module-finite
R
-algebra, we can apply Corollary
4.10
to deduce that all countably generated projective modules over
\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda}(M)
are relatively big. Hence, by Remark
1.18
, the indecomposable projectives over
\End_{\Lambda}(M)
are finitely generated. The conclusion follows from Proposition
4.11
.

Remark 4.13
.

In general, if
\Lambda
is a PI-ring, then the endomorphism ring of a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module is also a PI-ring (see the argument of
[
27
, Theorem 2]
).

5.
Projective versus locally projective modules

Let
R
be a commutative domain with field of fractions
Q
. An
R
-module
M
is
torsion-free
if the natural map
M\to M\otimes_{R}Q
is injective.

Notation 5.1
.

To ease the notation, we will usually write
M_{Q}
for the localization of an
R
-module
M
at
R\setminus\{0\}
.

In the following result, we recall that localization commutes with the
\mathrm{Hom}
functor for suitable torsion-free modules. This property is crucial for the rest of the paper.

Lemma 5.2
.

[
1
, Lemma 2.8]

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra. Let
\Sigma
be a multiplicative subset of
R
consisting of non-zero divisors. If
M
is a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module that is torsion-free as an
R
-module, then the canonical injective homomorphism

\varphi\colon\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,N)\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}\to\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma},N\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})

is an isomorphism for every
\Lambda
-module
N
which is torsion-free as an
R
-module.

The following result is an extension of
[
10
, Lemma 10.1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra. Let
R\to T
be a flat ring homomorphism.

(i)

Let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then
P\otimes_{R}T
is a countably generated projective right module over
\Lambda\otimes_{R}T
and

\Tr_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}T}(P\otimes_{R}T)=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)\otimes_{R}T.

(ii)

Let
T
be a commutative ring. If
M
is a finitely presented right
\Lambda
-module, then

\Tr_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}T}(M\otimes_{R}T)=\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)\otimes_{R}T.

(iii)

Let
T=R_{\Sigma}
, where
\Sigma
is a multiplicative subset of
R
consisting of non-zero divisors. Assume
\Lambda
is torsion-free as an
R
-module. If
M
is a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module which is torsion-free as an
R
-module, then

\Tr_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})=\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}.

Proof.

Statements
(i)
and
(ii)
are proved in
[
10
, Lemma 10.1]
.

To prove
(iii)
, let
x\in\Tr_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})
. Then there is an element
t\in\Sigma
, and elements
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n}\in\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma},\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})
and
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}\in M
such that
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\left(m_{i}\otimes\frac{1}{t}\right)
. Let

\varphi\colon\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}\to\Hom_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma},\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})

denote the canonical homomorphism. By Lemma
5.2
,
\varphi
is an isomorphism, so there is an element
r\in\Sigma
and elements
g_{1},\dotsc,g_{n}\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
such that
\varphi(g_{i}\otimes\frac{1}{r})=f_{i}
for each
i=1,\dotsc,n
. Then

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\left(m_{i}\otimes\frac{1}{t}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi\left(g_{i}\otimes\frac{1}{r}\right)\left(m_{i}\otimes\frac{1}{t}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}(m_{i})\otimes\frac{1}{rt}.

Therefore,
x\in\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}
.

To prove the reverse containment, let
x\in\Tr_{\Lambda}(M)
and
t\in R_{\Sigma}
. Then there are
f_{1},\dotsc,f_{n}\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
,
m_{1},\dotsc,m_{n}\in M
such that
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(m_{i})
. Therefore,
x\otimes t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(m_{i})\otimes t=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi(f_{i}\otimes 1)(m_{i}\otimes t)
. Hence,
x\otimes t\in\Tr_{\Lambda\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma}}(M\otimes_{R}R_{\Sigma})
. This concludes the proof of the statement.

It is easy to prove that the notion of relatively big projective module behaves well with respect to localization.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be an algebra over a commutative ring
R
. Let
J\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
. Let
\Sigma
be a multiplicatively closed subset of
R
consisting of non-zero divisors. If
P
is a relatively
J
-big projective right
\Lambda
-module, then
P_{\Sigma}
is a relatively
J_{\Sigma}
-big projective right
\Lambda_{\Sigma}
-module.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
,
J_{\Sigma}\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda_{\Sigma})
. Since
P/PJ
is finitely generated, so is
(P/PJ)_{\Sigma}\cong P_{\Sigma}/P_{\Sigma}J_{\Sigma}
.

Let
P^{\prime}
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace
J
. Since
P\cong P\oplus Q^{(\omega)}
as
\Lambda
-modules, also
P_{\Sigma}\cong P_{\Sigma}\oplus(P^{\prime})_{\Sigma}^{(\omega)}
as
\Lambda_{\Sigma}
-modules. This finishes the proof of the claim.

The following result is necessary to provide a version of the Package Deal Theorem for projective modules.

Corollary 5.5
.

[
1
, Corollary 4.3]

Let
R
be a commutative domain, and let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra. Let
N
be a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module which is torsion-free as an

-module. Then

is a projective
\Lambda
-module if and only if
N_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of

.

Definition 5.6
.

A commutative domain

is said to have

if any non-zero element is contained in only a finite number of maximal ideals. A commutative domain of finite character is said to be

-local
if, in addition, any non-zero prime ideal of

is contained in a unique maximal ideal.

The domain

has finite character if
R/I
is a semilocal ring for every non-zero ideal
I
of
R
. If

is

-local, then it also satisfies that

is a local domain for every non-zero prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

is an

-local domain if and only if

is semiperfect for every non-zero ideal

of

. For characterizations of

-local domains, the reader is referred to
[
23
]
.

In general, one has the following easy property for modules over torsion-free algebras over a domain.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
R
be a domain, and let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra. Let
\Sigma
be a multiplicative subset of
R
. Let
U
be a right
\Lambda
-module which is torsion-free as an
R
-module. If
V
is a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module that is torsion-free and such that
V_{\Sigma}
is isomorphic to a submodule of
U_{\Sigma}
, then
U
has a submodule isomorphic to
V
.

Proof.
By hypothesis, and because
V
is torsion-free,
U_{\Sigma}
has a submodule
V^{\prime}
that is isomorphic to
V
. Since
V^{\prime}
is finitely generated, there exists
d\in\Sigma
such that
V^{\prime}\subseteq\frac{1}{d}U\subseteq U_{\Sigma}
. Then
dV^{\prime}\leq U
is the module we are looking for.

If in Lemma
5.7
,
R
has finite character then the element
d
that appears in the proof is contained only in a finite number of maximal ideals. So one easily proves results like the following one, which are crucial in our discussion.

Lemma 5.8
.

[
1
, Lemma 4.2 and 5.1]

Let
R
be a commutative domain of finite character with field of fractions
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra, and let
M,N
be right
\Lambda
-modules which are torsion-free as
R
-modules. Assume that
M_{Q}\cong N_{Q}
. Then

(i)

If
M
and
N
are finitely generated, then
M_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong N_{\mathfrak{m}}
for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

(ii)

If
M
is finitely generated and
N\subseteq M
then
M_{\mathfrak{m}}=N_{\mathfrak{m}}
for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. So that, there exist
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}
maximal ideals of
R
such that
M/N=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}M_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/N_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
.

In the following result, we specialize in two-sided ideals of
\Lambda
and assume that
\Lambda_{Q}
is a simple artinian ring.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions

. Let
\Lambda
be a torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian, and let

be a non-zero two-sided ideal of

. Then:

(i)

;

(ii)

I_{\mathfrak{m}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of

;

(iii)

There exist
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}

such that
\Lambda/I=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
.

Proof.

(i)
Since
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian and
\Lambda
is torsion-free,

is a non-zero two-sided ideal of

. So

. Therefore, there exist
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in I
and

such that

. Thus

and this finishes the proof of

. Note that

.

Statement

is a direct application of Lemma

.

The following results, which are known as Package Deal Theorems, were proved in
[
19
]
for the case of algebras over noetherian rings of Krull dimension 1. The name of these theorems comes from the idea of having a “package” of modules over a localization and finding a module over the non-localized algebra whose localizations are those given in the “package”. In

Package Deal Theorem 5.10

(Localization of submodules)
.

[

Let

be an

-local domain with field of fractions
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be an
R
-algebra, and let

be a finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module, which is torsion-free as an

\mathfrak{m}
of

-submodule of

(i)

There is a
\Lambda
-submodule

, which is torsion-free as an
R
-module, and such that
N_{\mathfrak{m}}=X(\mathfrak{m})
for all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

(ii)

X(\mathfrak{m})=M_{\mathfrak{m}}
for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

Moreover, if each of the

Package Deal Theorem 5.11

(Localization of trace ideals)
.

[
1
, Theorem 5.13]

Let
R
be an

-local domain with field of fractions

. Let
\Lambda

\mathfrak{m}

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative domain of finite character with field of fractions
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be a torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. Let
S
denote a simple right
\Lambda_{Q}
-module, and assume that
\Lambda_{Q}\cong S^{k}
as right

-modules. Then any finitely generated projective right
\Lambda

By assumption

for suitable

. Therefore

(Localization of Projective Modules)
.

Let

be an

-local domain with field of fractions

. Let
\Lambda

\mathfrak{m}
of

\Lambda

\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

(ii)

P(\mathfrak{m})\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}^{r}
for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
. Let

be the finite set of maximal ideals of

.
Since

there exists
a submodule

of

such that

. For
\mathfrak{n}\in\mSpec(R)\setminus\mathcal{M}

, there is a finitely generated right

Also note that

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
Apply Lemma

.

6.
Semiperfect rings and locally semiperfect rings

We start this section by describing the monoids of projective modules for semiperfect rings. We follow the notation and conventions of
[
2
, §27]
.

6.1.
Projective modules over semiperfect rings and their trace ideals

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a semiperfect ring, and let
I
be an ideal of
\Lambda
. Let
P^{\prime}
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda/I
-module. Then, there exists a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
X
such that
X/XI\cong P^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Since
\Lambda
is semiperfect,
P^{\prime}
has a projective cover. That is, it fits in an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ker f}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

where
X
is a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, and
\Ker f
is a small submodule of
X
. Therefore, there are inclusions

XI\subseteq\Ker f\subseteq XJ(\Lambda)

which induce an exact sequence of
\Lambda/I
-modules

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ker f/XI}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/XI}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}

which splits because
P^{\prime}
is projective. Since
\Ker f/XI
is also a small submodule of
X/XI
, it must be zero. Therefore
X/XI\cong P^{\prime}
.

In the next proposition, we recall the behavior of traces of projective modules for semiperfect rings.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a semiperfect ring, and let
J(\Lambda)
denote its Jacobson radical. Then:

(i)

\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda):=\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
, and the injective map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\to\mathcal{T}(\Lambda/J(\Lambda))
defined in Lemma
1.8
is bijective.

(ii)

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
be a basic set of (primitive orthogonal) idempotents of
\Lambda
. Let
\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,t\})
denote the set of all subsets of
\{1,\dots,t\}
. Then the map

\Gamma\colon\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,t\})\to\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)

defined by
\Gamma(A)=\sum_{i\in A}\Lambda e_{i}\Lambda
, for any
A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,t\})
, is a bijection.

(iii)

Let
P
be a projective right
\Lambda
-module with trace ideal
I
. Assume that
I/(I\cap J(\Lambda))\cong\sum_{i\in A}\left((e_{i}+J(\Lambda))\Lambda/J(\Lambda)\right)^{\ell_{i}}
for suitable
\ell_{i}>0
and
A\in\mathcal{P}(\{1,\dots,t\})
. Then
P\cong\bigoplus_{i\in A}\left(e_{i}\Lambda\right)^{(L_{i})}
, for suitable non-empty sets,
L_{i}
.

(iv)

Keeping the notation from
(ii)
and
(iii)
,
\Lambda^{\prime}:=\Lambda/I
is a semiperfect ring and
e_{i}^{\prime}:=e_{i}+I,i\not\in A
is a basic set of idempotents of
\Lambda/I
. Moreover, let
P^{\prime}\cong\bigoplus_{i\not\in A}(e_{i}^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime})^{x_{i}}
be a finitely generated projective
\Lambda^{\prime}
-module, and let
P
be a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module such that
P/PI\cong P^{\prime}
. Then there are non-negative integers
s_{i},i\not\in A
such that
P\cong\bigoplus_{i\not\in A}(e_{i}\Lambda)^{x_{i}}\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in A}(e_{i}\Lambda)^{s_{i}}
.

Proof.

The proof of the four statements is more or less routine. One just has to take into account that any projective right (left)
\Lambda
-module is isomorphic to a unique direct sum of the indecomposable projective modules
P_{i}=e_{i}\Lambda
(
Q_{i}=\Lambda e_{i}
), for
i=1,\dots,t
(see, for example,
[
6
, Theorem 3.10]
). Moreover
\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}(P_{i})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}(Q_{i})=\Lambda e_{i}\Lambda
.

To give explicit descriptions of the monoids of projective modules over certain classes of rings, we introduce the monoid/semiring
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}
.

Notation 6.3
.

Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
, and let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\dots\}
. We denote by
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}=\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
the monoid with the sum
extending the one of
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and with the additional rule that
x+\infty=\infty+x=\infty
for any
x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}
. We also see
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}
as a semiring with the product extending the one of
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
together with
the rules
x\cdot\infty=\infty\cdot x=\infty
for any
x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}\setminus\{0\}
, and
0\cdot\infty=\infty\cdot 0=0
.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a semiperfect ring. Then:

(i)

The canonical isomorphism between
V(\Lambda):=V_{r}(\Lambda)\cong{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{t}
and
V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
extends to an isomorphism between
V^{*}(\Lambda):=V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)\cong({\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*})^{t}
and
V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)
.

(ii)

Let
P
be a projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then there exists a two-sided ideal
I
of
\Lambda
that is minimal with respect to the property that
P/PI
is finitely generated. Moreover,
I
is the trace of a finitely generated projective right module.

(iii)

Every countably generated projective module is relatively big, so that
V^{*}(\Lambda)=V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
be a basic set of idempotents of
\Lambda
. Then
P_{i}=e_{i}\Lambda
and
Q_{i}=\Lambda e_{i}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\Lambda}(P_{i},\Lambda)
, for
i=1,\dots,t
, are representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable projective right and left
\Lambda
-modules. Recall that any projective right or left module over
\Lambda
is a direct sum of indecomposables, uniquely up to isomorphism.

(i)
Let
P
be a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. Then there exist
A_{1},\dots,A_{t}
countable sets such that
P\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}^{(A_{i})}
.

Then the assignment

[P]=\left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}P_{i}^{(A_{i})}\right]\mapsto\left[\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}Q_{i}^{(A_{i})}\right]

gives an isomorphism of monoids
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)\to V_{\ell}^{*}(\Lambda)
that extends the canonical isomorphism
V_{r}(\Lambda)\to V_{\ell}(\Lambda)
from Remark
2.6
.

Moreover, the assignment
[P]\mapsto(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
, where for
i=1,\dots,t

n_{i}=\begin{cases}|A_{i}|&\text{if $A_{i}$ is finite}\\
\infty&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

induces an isomorphism
V_{r}^{*}(\Lambda)\to({\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*})^{t}
that restricts to an isomorphism
V_{r}(\Lambda)\to{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{t}
.

(ii)
If
P
is a projective right
\Lambda
-module, then
\{1,\dots,t\}=A\cup B
, where
A
and
B
are disjoint, and
P\cong\left(\bigoplus_{i\in A}P_{i}^{(A_{i})}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{i\in B}P_{i}^{(B_{i})}\right)
, where
A_{i}
is a finite set (possibly empty) for any
i\in A
, and
B_{i}
is an infinite set for any
i\in B
. Then, by Lemma
1.5
,

I=\sum_{i\in B}\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}(P_{i})=\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda}\Big(\bigoplus_{i\in B}P_{i}\Big)

is the ideal we are looking for.

(iii)
By Proposition
6.2
,
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
, so we can apply Corollary
2.9
to deduce that
B_{r}(\Lambda):=B(\Lambda)
is canonically isomorphic to
B_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Statement
(ii)
implies that any countably generated projective module is relatively big. Hence,
V^{*}(\Lambda)=V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
.

6.2.
Locally semiperfect algebras over
h
-local domains

Definition 6.5
.

Let
\Lambda
be an algebra over a commutative domain
R
. We say that
\Lambda
is
locally semiperfect
if
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
is semiperfect for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

From now on we are using the conventions of Notation
5.1
.

The following example explains a particular instance of the appearance of locally semiperfect algebras that, in addition, satisfy that they have a simple artinian ring of quotients. With the additional hypothesis that the domain
R
is
h
-local, they will be our main object of study in Section
9
.

Example 6.6
.

Let
R
be a commutative domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
M
be a finitely generated torsion-free
R
-module. Then
\Lambda=\End_{R}(M)
is an
R
-algebra and, by Lemma
5.2
,
\Lambda_{Q}=\Lambda\otimes_{R}Q\cong\End_{Q}(M\otimes_{R}Q)=\End_{Q}(M_{Q})
is simple artinian. By
[
6
, Proposition 3.14]
and Lemma
5.2
, for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
,
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a direct sum of modules with local endomorphism ring if and only if
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\End_{R_{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{m}})
is a semiperfect ring. In other words,
\Lambda
is locally semiperfect if and only if
M_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a direct sum of modules with local endomorphism ring for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. Then every trace of a projective right (or left)
\Lambda
-module is also the trace of a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module. In particular,
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Let
I
be a non-zero two-sided ideal of
\Lambda
which is the trace of a projective right
\Lambda
-module. Since
\Lambda
is torsion-free,
I_{\mathfrak{m}}\neq\{0\}
holds for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. By Lemma
5.3
and Lemma
6.2
,
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
is also the trace of a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module.

Let
S
denote a simple right
\Lambda_{Q}
-module, and assume that
\Lambda_{Q}\cong S^{k}
as a right
\Lambda_{Q}
-module. Since
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian and
I
is non-zero, by Lemma
5.9
, there exists a finite set
\mathcal{M}
of maximal ideals of
R
such that
I_{\mathfrak{m}}=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
if
\mathfrak{m}\not\in\mathcal{M}
. For any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}
, let
P(\mathfrak{m})
be a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module with trace ideal
I_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
P(\mathfrak{m})_{Q}\cong S^{t_{\mathfrak{m}}}
for a suitable positive integer
t_{\mathfrak{m}}\geq 1
. Let
t=\mathrm{lcm}\{t_{\mathfrak{m}}\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{M}\}
, write
t=\ell_{\mathfrak{m}}\cdot t_{\mathfrak{m}}
, and let
P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}):=P(\mathfrak{m})^{k\ell_{\mathfrak{m}}}
for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathcal{M}
. For each
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathcal{M}
, take
P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m})=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}^{t}
. Note that
P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m})_{Q}\cong S^{tk}\cong\Lambda_{Q}^{t}
and
\Tr_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}}(P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}))=I_{\mathfrak{m}}
hold for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. By Package Deal Theorem
5.13
applied to the package
\{P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m})\mid\mathfrak{m}\in\mSpec(R)\}
, there exists a finitely generated projective right
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
P_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m})
for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. By Lemma
5.3
,
\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)_{\mathfrak{m}}=\Tr_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}}(P^{\prime}(\mathfrak{m}))=I_{\mathfrak{m}}
for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. This implies that
I=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)+I=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)
.

A similar argument gives the conclusion for the traces of projective left
\Lambda
-modules due to the symmetry of the hypotheses.

The equality
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)
follows from the canonical bijection between finitely generated projective right and left
\Lambda
-modules induced by the
\Lambda
-dual, and because the trace of a finitely generated projective module coincides with the trace of its
\Lambda
-dual, cf. Remark
2.6
.

Corollary 6.8
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. If
P
is a countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-module, then
P^{(\omega)}
is a direct sum of finitely generated modules.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.7
, the trace of
P
coincides with the trace of a finitely generated projective module. To conclude, apply that if
P
and
Q
are countably generated projective modules then
P^{(\omega)}\cong Q^{(\omega)}
if and only if
P
and
Q
have the same trace.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
R
be a commutative domain of finite character with field of fractions
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian; so that there exist
k
and a division ring D such that
\Lambda_{Q}\cong M_{k}(D)
. Then, for almost all maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
, the ring
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
has the following properties:

(i)

There is exactly one indecomposable projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module up to isomorphism. Moreover,
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong M_{k}(T_{\mathfrak{m}})
, where
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a local ring such that
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{R}Q\cong D
.

(ii)

If
U
and
V
are projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-modules, then
U\cong V
if and only if
U_{Q}\cong V_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
S
be a simple
\Lambda_{Q}
-module and assume that
\Lambda_{Q}\cong S^{k}
. Let
X
be a finitely generated
\Lambda
-module, which is torsion-free as an
R
-module, and such that
X_{Q}\cong S
. By Lemma
5.8
,
X_{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}
is free for almost all maximal ideals. If
\mathfrak{m}
is such a maximal ideal, then
X_{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
gives an indecomposable decomposition of the semiperfect ring
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Hence
X_{\mathfrak{m}}
is the only indecomposable projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module up to isomorphism. As
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
is semiperfect, the endomorphism ring of
X_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a local ring that we will denote by
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Hence
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\mathrm{End}_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}}(X_{\mathfrak{m}}^{k})\cong M_{k}(T_{\mathfrak{m}}).
By Lemma
5.2
,
T_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{R}Q\cong D
.

Projective modules over
M_{k}(T_{\mathfrak{m}})
are always a direct sum of copies of the indecomposable projective
X_{\mathfrak{m}}
. Moreover,
U\cong X_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(I)},V\cong X_{\mathfrak{m}}^{(J)}
then
U\cong V
if and only if
I
and
J
have the same cardinality, if and only if
U_{Q}\cong V_{Q}
.

Remark 6.10
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian, so that there exist
k
and a division ring D such that
\Lambda_{Q}\cong M_{k}(D)
. In view of Lemma
6.9
, the maximum spectrum of
R
can be written as a disjoint union of two sets
A
and
B
such that for any
\mathfrak{m}\in A
,
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a ring of
k\times k
matrices over a local ring and
B
is a finite, non-empty set. In particular, for
\mathfrak{m}\in A
,
\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})=\{0,\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}\}
.

In view of Package Deal Theorem
5.11
and Lemma
6.7
, there is a bijective correspondence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}=\mathcal{T}_{\ell}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in B}\left(\mathcal{T}_{r}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})\setminus\{0\}\right).}

Corollary 6.11
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. Then
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
is finite. Moreover, if
P
is a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module, then
I=\Tr_{\Lambda}(P)
is a minimal non-zero element of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
if and only if
P_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a finite power of an indecomposable projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}}
-module
for any
\mathfrak{m}\in\mSpec(R)
.

Proof.

Finiteness of
{\mathcal{T}}(\Lambda)
follows from Remark

Proposition 6.16
.

R

-local domain with field of fractions

We have to prove that if

We prove the following claim: If

is a finitely generated submodule of

and

is a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module with trace contained in

such that

Assume we have proved the claim and let
U=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{N}}U_{i}
, with each
U_{i}
being finitely generated. Let
p_{1},p_{2},\dots\in P
be a countable set of generators of
P
. Consider the following construction: Let
f_{1}\colon P\to U_{1}
be an epimorphism, and let
P_{1}
be a finitely generated submodule of

and

and

are finitely generated submodules of

,

, and

(we can set

be a finitely generated submodule of

,

Thus, we are left to prove the claim. First, note that we may assume

Then

cannot be finitely generated. Hence

By Lemma

is onto and

. Let

be a finitely generated submodule of

containing

of

. Let

It remains to show that there are homomorphisms

and finitely generated submodules

of

(i)

T_{0}\subseteq T_{1}\subseteq T_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq T_{k}

(ii)

(g+\sum_{j=1}^{i}f_{j})_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}((T_{i})_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}})=V_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}

Then

. Hence,

is onto if

is not among

is onto for every

Assume
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell-1}
and

is a finitely generated projective module over a semiperfect ring
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}

is a finitely generated (semisimple module). Therefore, if
\pi\colon V_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}\to V_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}/V_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}J(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}})
is the canonical projection,
\pi((g_{\ell}(P))_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}})
is a finitely generated submodule of

. Let

be a finitely generated submodule of

containing

such that

By Lemma
6.13
, there exists

such that
(f_{\ell})_{\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}}
is onto and

7.
Rank and Genus of countably generated projective modules

We continue with the same setting as in §
6
. Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
. Let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free algebra over
R
such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. That is
\Lambda_{Q}\cong M_{k}(D)
, for
D
a division ring.

There are two natural monoid morphisms. The first one is the one associated with tensoring with
Q
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi\colon V^{*}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{*}(\Lambda_{Q})}

defined by
\Psi([P])=[P_{Q}]=[S^{r}]
, where
S
is a simple right
\Lambda_{Q}
-module. Then we have a monoid morphism that, by abuse of notation, we also call
\Psi

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi\colon V^{*}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*}}

defined by
\Psi([P])=r
if
P
is finitely generated and
P_{Q}\cong S^{r}
, and
\Psi([P])=\infty
if
P
is not finitely generated. We call
\Psi([P])
the
rank
of
P
, and we refer to
\Psi
as the
rank map
.

Associated with the localization at the different maximal ideals, we have the monoid morphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi\colon V^{*}(\Lambda)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mSpec(R)}V^{*}(\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}})}

defined by
\Phi([P])=([P_{\mathfrak{m}}])_{\mathfrak{m}\in\mSpec(R)}
. We shall refer to
\Phi
as the
genus morphism
, and to
\Phi([P])
as the genus of
[P]
.

We say that two countably generated projective right
\Lambda
-modules
P
and
P^{\prime}

are in the same genus
if
P_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong P^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{m}}
for every maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
, that is, if and only if
\Phi([P])=\Phi([P^{\prime}])
.

In this section, we want to describe the images of
\Psi
and
\Phi
.

At this point, we have plenty of information about
V^{*}(\Lambda)
. By Theorem
6.17
,
V^{*}(\Lambda)=V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)
, and by Lemma
6.7
, any ideal of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
is a trace ideal of a finitely generated projective module, and by Corollary
6.11
,
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
is finite.

In general, there may be plenty of finitely generated projective right modules over
\Lambda
in the same genus that are not isomorphic. For example, let
R=\Lambda
be a Dedekind domain. Then any localization of a projective module at a maximal ideal is free. So two projective modules are in the same genus if and only if they have the same rank. Certainly, two finitely generated projective modules over a Dedekind domain with the same rank are not, in general, isomorphic.

The situation changes for projective
\Lambda
-modules whose isomorphism class is in
B(\Lambda)
, or for finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-modules when
\Lambda
is a suitable algebra over a semilocal domain
R
.

Proposition 7.1
.

R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions

. Let
\Lambda

-algebra such that

The first part of the proof follows from Corollary
5.4

For the second part of the proof, we are going to use the notation of Corollary
2.5

By Lemma

of a system of linear equations and linear congruences with coefficients in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. However, it is not at all clear which invariants of the semilocal ring determine the coefficients of the system defining the monoid of relatively big projectives.

In our situation, by Proposition
7.2
,
\Lambda_{\Sigma}
where
\Sigma=R\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is a locally semiperfect, semilocal algebra over the semilocal domain
R_{\Sigma}
. We are proving that, at least for the genus, the system of equations that determines the monoid can be deduced from the structural properties of
\Lambda
and its localizations at the maximal ideals of
R
. Note that if
\Lambda
is finitely generated over
R
, then, by Proposition
7.2
,
V^{*}(\Lambda_{\Sigma})=V(\Lambda)\sqcup B(\Lambda)\cong\Phi^{\prime}(V^{*}(\Lambda))
.

8.
Infinite sums of finitely generated projective modules

In this section, we continue working with an algebra
\Lambda
over an
h
-local domain
R
, with field of fractions
Q
, and such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian.

We keep using the notation from the previous section. In particular, the one introduced in Notation
7.4
. As in Proposition
7.7
, let
\mathcal{A}=\Phi^{\prime}(V(\Lambda))
. If
\ell
is either
0
or
1
, then every projective module is a direct sum of finitely generated ones (cf. Remark
7.8
). So we assume
\ell\geq 2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be the image via
\Phi^{\prime}
of
B(\Lambda)=V^{*}(\Lambda)\setminus V(\Lambda)
in
\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(({\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{*})^{t_{i}}\right)
.

By Proposition
7.1
, the genus classifies the modules of
B(\Lambda)
up to isomorphism. Therefore, the modules in
B(\Lambda)
that are isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely generated modules are those such that the image of their isomorphism class via
\Phi^{\prime}
lies in

\mathcal{A}+\infty\cdot(\mathcal{A}\setminus\{0\})=\{a+\infty\cdot a^{\prime}\mid a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}\mbox{ and }a^{\prime}\neq 0\}.

In this section we will examine the question of whether any projective
\Lambda
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules. By Proposition
1.19
and Lemma
6.7
, we only need to characterize when finitely generated projective modules modulo an ideal of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
lift to finitely generated projective modules. Our next Lemma shows that we only need to ensure this property for the minimal elements in
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
R
be an
h
-local domain with field of fractions
Q
, and let
\Lambda
be a locally semiperfect torsion-free
R
-algebra such that
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian. The following are equivalent

(i)

For any
I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
and any finitely generated projective
\Lambda/I
-module
P^{\prime}
, there exists a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module
P

such that
P^{\prime}\cong P/PI
.

(ii)

For any
I
which is a minimal element of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}
and any finitely generated projective
\Lambda/I
-module
P^{\prime}
, there exists a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
P^{\prime}\cong P/PI
.

Proof.

Of course, only
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
needs a proof. To prove
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
, let
I
be a non-zero trace of
\Lambda
containing a minimal trace
X\in T(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}
.
Such an
X
exists by Corollary
6.11
.

By Lemma
5.9
, there exist
\mathfrak{m}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}
maximal ideals of
R
such that
\Lambda/X=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/X_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
. Thus, it is a finite product of semiperfect rings. Therefore,
\Lambda/X
is semiperfect.

Let
P^{\prime}
be a finitely generated projective
\Lambda/I
-module. By Lemma
6.1
, there exists a finitely generated projective
\Lambda/X
-module

P_{1}
such that
P_{1}/P_{1}I\cong P^{\prime}
. Now we can use
(ii)
to find a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
P/PX=P_{1}
. Then
P/PI\cong(P/PX)/(PI/PX)\cong(P_{1}/P_{1}I)\cong P^{\prime}
.

The next lemma will be useful for checking when the criteria of Lemma
8.1
is satisfied.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
2\leq\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell},s\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
m=\mathrm{lcm}(r_{1},\dots,r_{\ell-1})>0
. Then the system

\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&0&\dots&0&-s\\
0&r_{2}&\dots&0&-s\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&r_{\ell-1}&-s\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{\ell}\\
\vdots\\
r_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}

(12)

has a solution in
\mathbb{N}
if and only if the equation
my-sx_{\ell}=r_{\ell}
has a solution in
\mathbb{N}
. If
(y,s)
is such a solution, then

\left(x_{1}=\frac{m}{r_{1}}y,\dots,x_{\ell-1}=\frac{m}{r_{\ell-1}}y,x_{\ell}\right)

is a solution of (
12
). Therefore, (
12
) has a solution in
\mathbb{N}
if and only if
\gcd(m,s)
divides
r_{\ell}
.

Proof.

The claim follows because

\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\dots&0\\
-1&1&\dots&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
-1&0&\dots&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&0&\dots&0&-s\\
0&r_{2}&\dots&0&-s\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&r_{\ell-1}&-s\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&0&\dots&0&-s\\
-r_{1}&r_{2}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
-r_{1}&0&\dots&r_{\ell-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

so the system (
12
) is equivalent to the system

\begin{pmatrix}r_{1}&0&\dots&0&-s\\
-r_{1}&r_{2}&\dots&0&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&&\vdots\\
-r_{1}&0&\dots&r_{\ell-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\\
\vdots\\
x_{\ell}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}r_{\ell}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}

which has a solution in
\mathbb{N}
if and only if the equation
my-sx_{\ell}=r_{\ell}
has a solution in
\mathbb{N}
. Also, it is clear that the solutions are related, as claimed in the statement.

Proposition 8.3
.

Under the Notation
7.4
, the following are equivalent

(i)

Every projective
\Lambda
-module is a direct sum of finitely generated modules.

(ii)

For every family of natural numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{\ell}
,
1\leq a_{i}\leq t_{i}
, and for every
j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
1\leq b\neq a_{j}\leq t_{j}
, there are non-negative integers
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{\ell}
such that
r_{j,b}+x_{j}r_{j,a_{j}}=x_{i}r_{i,a_{i}}
for every

i\neq j
.

(iii)

For every family of natural numbers
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{\ell}
,
1\leq a_{i}\leq t_{i}
, and for every
j\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
and
1\leq b\neq a_{j}\leq t_{j}
,
\gcd(\mathrm{lcm}(\{r_{1,a_{1}},\dots,r_{\ell,a_{\ell}}\}\setminus\{r_{j,a_{j}}\}),r_{j,a_{j}})
divides
r_{j,b}
.

Proof.

Assume
(i)
is true, so finitely generated projective modules of
\Lambda
modulo a (minimal) element of
\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)\setminus\{0\}
can be lifted to finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-modules.

By Remark
6.10
,
\Lambda
has an ideal
I\in\mathcal{T}(\Lambda)
such that
I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=\Tr_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}}(U_{i,a_{i}})
for each
i=1,\dots,\ell
. As
\Lambda_{Q}
is simple artinian and
R
is
h
-local,
\Lambda/I\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
. Therefore, every (finitely generated) projective
\Lambda/I
-module is of the form
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}M_{i}
, where
M_{i}
is a (finitely generated) projective
(\Lambda/I)_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
-module. Note that
(\Lambda/I)_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}\cong\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
, which is a semiperfect ring, so projective modules over it are described in Proposition
6.2
(iv).

Consider a projective
\Lambda/I
-module
P^{\prime}
such that
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}^{\prime}\cong U_{j,b}/U_{j,b}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}
and
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}^{\prime}=0
if
i\neq j
. By assumption, there exists
P
a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module such that
P/PI\cong P^{\prime}
. Then
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}\cong U_{i,a_{i}}^{x_{i}}
if
i\neq j
and
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}\cong U_{j,b}\oplus U_{j,a_{j}}^{x_{j}}
. Since
(P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}})_{Q}\cong P_{Q}\cong(P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}})_{Q}
, we obtain
x_{i}r_{i,a_{i}}=r_{j,b}+x_{j}r_{j,a_{j}}
. So
(ii)
holds.

Conversely, let
I
be a minimal non-zero trace of
\Lambda
. Then
I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=\Tr_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}}(U_{i,a_{i}})
for some
a_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell_{i}\}
. Recall that
\Lambda/I\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
is a semiperfect ring. Hence, it is sufficient to lift an indecomposable projective
\Lambda/I
-module to a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module. So let
P^{\prime}
be a finitely generated indecomposable projective
\Lambda/I
-module. Regarding the decomposition of
\Lambda/I
,
P^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=0
for all
i
’s with exactly one exception. Assume
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}^{\prime}\cong U_{j,b}/U_{j,b}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}
, where
b\neq a_{j}
. Take
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that the equations in
(ii)
are satisfied. Let
r=r_{j,b}+x_{j}r_{j,a_{j}}
.

For
i=1,\dots,\ell
set
U(\mathfrak{m}_{i})=U_{i,a_{i}}^{x_{i}}
if
i\neq j
and
U(\mathfrak{m}_{j})=U_{j,b}\oplus U_{j,a_{j}}^{x_{j}}
. For
\mathfrak{n}\in\mSpec(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}\}
, let
U(\mathfrak{n})
be a projective
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}
-module such that
U(\mathfrak{n})_{Q}
has length
r
(it exists by Lemma
6.9
). Because of the choice of
x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}
, we can use Proposition
7.3
to show the existence of a finitely generated projective
\Lambda
-module
P
such that
P_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong U(\mathfrak{m})
for any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
. Then
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}=P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
if
i\neq j
and
P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}/P_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}\cong U_{j,b}/U_{j,b}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{j}}
. Therefore
P/PI\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}/P_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}I_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}\cong P^{\prime}
. This proves
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
.

The equivalence of
(ii)
and
(iii)
follows from Lemma
8.2
.

Example 8.4
.

Under the assumptions in Notation

Since

By Proposition

Example 8.5
.

Under the assumptions in Notation

, there are examples of algebras
\Lambda

,

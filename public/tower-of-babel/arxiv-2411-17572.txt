Log-concavity of polynomials arising from equivariant cohomology Thanks: Jacob Matherne received support from a Simons Foundation Travel Support for Mathematicians Award MPS-TSM-00007970.
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Yupeng Li, Jacob P. Matherne

Abstract
. We study the equivariant cohomology classes of torus-equivariant subvarieties of the space of matrices. For a large class of torus actions, we prove that the polynomials representing these classes (up to suitably changing signs) are covolume polynomials in the sense of Aluffi. We study the cohomology rings of complex varieties in terms of Macaulay inverse systems over ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . As applications, we show that under certain conditions, the Macaulay dual generator is a denormalized Lorentzian polynomial in the sense of Brändén and Huh, and we give a characteristic-free extension (over ℤ \mathbb{Z} ) of the result of Khovanskii and Pukhlikov describing the cohomology ring of toric varieties in terms of volume polynomials.

1.
Introduction

A sequence of real numbers
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is called
log-concave
if
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for all
1\leq i\leq n-1
.
Log-concave sequences naturally appear throughout algebra, combinatorics, and geometry; for thorough references on log-concavity and related concepts, we suggest
[
Bre89
,
Sta89
,
Brä15
]
.
Recently, the theory of
Lorentzian polynomials
was introduced by Brändén and Huh
[
BH20
]
(and in
[
AGV21
,
ALOGV24a
,
ALOGV24b
]
under the name of
completely log-concave polynomials
) and they have been instrumental in proving log-concavity and related results throughout mathematics, see, e.g.,
[
BH20
,
EH20
,
MS21
,
HMMSD22
,
BL23
,
BLP23
,
Ros23
,
ALOGV24b
,
BES24
,
HMV24
,
KMSD24
,
MMS24
,
RU24
]
.

The prototypical examples of Lorentzian polynomials are the volume polynomials of projective varieties
[
BH20
]
. Likewise, the
covolume polynomials
of Aluffi
[
Alu24
]
are the prototypical examples of the
dually Lorentzian polynomials
of Ross, Süß, and Wannerer
[
RSW23
]
. We introduce a new family of polynomials that specialize to a number of important polynomials in algebraic combinatorics, and we prove that they are covolume polynomials.

For two permutations
u,w\in S_{n}
with
w\geq u
in Bruhat order, we define the
double Richardson polynomial
as the product of double Schubert polynomials

\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\;=\;\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}w}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}^{\prime}),

where
{\textbf{s}}^{\prime}=(s_{n},\ldots,s_{1})
denotes the reverse of
{\textbf{s}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
.
The double Richardson polynomial represents the torus-equivariant class of matrix Richardson varieties in the cohomology ring
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}({\rm Mat}_{n,n})
of the space of
n\times n
matrices with the standard action of the torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
.
Double Richardson polynomials specialize to (double) Schubert polynomials:

\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\;=\;\mathfrak{R}_{w_{0}/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0})\;=\;\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\mathfrak{R}_{w_{0}/u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0}).

The
Richardson polynomial

\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})=\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0})
is closely related to the
skew Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
of Lenart and Sottile
[
LS03
]
since both polynomials yield the same class in the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}({{\mathcal{F}\ell}_{n}})
of the flag variety
{{\mathcal{F}\ell}_{n}}
.
But we point out that
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
and
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
may not be equal in general (see
Remark 5.9
).

Theorem A

(
Theorem 5.4
)
.

The
(
sign-changed
)
double Richardson polynomial
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
is a covolume polynomial.

Dually Lorentzian polynomials enjoy two nice combinatorial properties (see
Proposition 5.6
): their supports are M-convex, and they are discretely log-concave. A homogeneous polynomial
h=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}
of degree
d
with nonnegative coefficients is said to have M-convex support if
\mathrm{supp}(h)
is the set of integer points of a generalized permutohedron in the sense of
[
Pos09
]
, and it is said to be discretely log-concave if
a_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}\geq a_{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{j}}a_{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{j}}
for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and all
1\leq i,j\leq n
.

Corollary B

(
Corollary 5.8
)
.

The following polynomials have
M
-convex support and are discretely log-concave:

(i)

(
sign-changed
)
Double Richardson polynomials
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
.

(ii)

Richardson polynomials
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
.

(iii)

(
sign-changed
)
Double Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
.

(iv)

Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}})
.

We note that the M-convexity in
Corollary B
(iii) recovers a result of
[
CCRMMn23
]
and both the M-convexity and the discrete log-concavity in
Corollary B
(iv) recover results of
[
HMMSD22
]
.
To the best of our knowledge, the discrete log-concavity of double Schubert polynomials is new.

The proof of
Theorem A
follows from a general theorem that we prove in
Section 4
.
More precisely, we prove the following result regarding the equivariant cohomology classes of torus-equivariant subvarieties of the space of matrices.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 4.5
,
Corollary 4.6
)
.

Let
{\rm Mat}_{m,n}={\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
be the space of
m\times n
matrices with complex entries and consider the natural action of the torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
given by
(g,h)\cdot M=g\cdot M\cdot h^{-1}
for all
M\in{\rm Mat}_{m,n}
and
(g,h)\in T
.
Let
X\subset{\rm Mat}_{m,n}
be an irreducible
T
-subvariety and
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be the polynomial representing the class
[X]^{T}
of
X
in
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}\left({\rm Mat}_{m,n}\right)={\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]
.
Then we have that
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},-s_{1},\ldots,-s_{n})
is a covolume polynomial.

We point out that the result of
Theorem C
is sharp in the sense that for arbitrary torus actions we can find simple instances where the equivariant class of an irreducible
T
-variety is represented by a polynomial which does not even have
M
-convex support (see
Example 4.2
).

We also connect the theory of
Macaulay inverse systems
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
to the theory of Lorentzian polynomials.
More precisely, we study the cohomology rings of smooth complex algebraic varieties over
{\mathbb{Z}}
in terms of a suitable
generic version
of the Macaulay inverse system.
In this characteristic-free situation over
{\mathbb{Z}}
, we show that the respective Macaulay dual generator is a denormalized Lorentzian polynomial (see
Theorem D
below).

Over a field of a characteristic zero, say the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
, the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
of a smooth complex algebraic variety
X
is a classical example of an Artinian graded Gorenstein algebra (due to the Poincaré duality of
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
).
Therefore, the Macaulay inverse system stands as a powerful tool to study the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
because Artinian graded Gorenstein algebras can be defined in terms of the annihilator of a single polynomial in a dual polynomial ring (see, e.g.,
[
Eis95
, §21.2]
,
[
CLS11
, §13.4]
).
Perhaps the most spectacular application of this idea is the result of Khovanskii and Pukhlikov
[
KP92
]
describing the cohomology ring of a smooth complete toric variety in terms of the annihilator of the volume polynomial.
For more recent applications and related results, the reader is referred to
[
Kav11
,
HMM+13
,
KM21
,
KLM22
,
LMK22
,
MMSW22
,
HKM24
]
.

Our interest is to study the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
of a smooth complex algebraic variety
X
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra and to find suitable extensions of the aforementioned known results for
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
.
To substitute the fact that
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,\mathbb{Q})
is an Artinian graded Gorenstein algebra, we should have that the structure morphism
f\colon{\text{Spec}}\left(\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\right)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
is a finite Gorenstein morphism (i.e.;
f
is a finite flat morphism and all its fibers are Gorenstein rings); if this happens, we say
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is Artinian Gorenstein over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We also need to consider generic versions of the Matlis and Macaulay dual functors. (These generic versions have become of interest recently, see, e.g.,
[
Smi18
,
CCRS21
,
KK22
,
CR23
]
.)
Let
S={\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a positively graded polynomial ring and consider the inverse polynomial ring
T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
under the identification
y_{i}=x_{i}^{-1}
.
For any homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
such that the quotient
R=S/I
is a
finitely generated flat
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
, we have a successful
Macaulay inverse system

I^{\perp_{{\mathbb{Z}}}}=\{G\in T\mid g\cdot G=0\text{ for all }g\in I\}
that can be computed with the
graded Matlis dual

R^{\vee_{\mathbb{Z}}}={}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,{\mathbb{Z}})
.
We make the necessary developments of these notions in
§6.1
.
Our main result regarding cohomology rings is the following theorem.

Theorem D

(
Theorem 6.13
,
Corollary 6.16
)
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional smooth complex algebraic variety.
Suppose that the cohomology ring
R=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\text{H}^{2i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is a flat
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra
(
i.e., it is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free
)
.
Let
\rho\colon R_{d}=\text{H}^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
be the natural degree map.
Choose a graded presentation
R\cong S/I
where
S={\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
\delta_{i}=\deg(x_{i})>0
, and
I\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal.
Let
\delta=\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{n}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(ii)

We have the isomorphisms
\omega_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}=\text{Ext}_{S}^{n}(R,S(-\delta))\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong R(d)
.

(iii)

Consider the inverse polynomial ring
T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with the identification
y_{i}=x_{i}^{-1}
.
Then the ideal
I\subset S
is given as the annihilator

I\;=\;\{g\in S\mid g\cdot G_{R}=0\}

of the inverse polynomial

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\delta_{n}=d}\rho\big(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\big)\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\;\;\in\;\;T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}].

(iv)

Assume also that
X
is complete and that each
x_{i}
is equal to the first Chern class
c_{1}(L_{i})
of a nef line bundle
L_{i}
on
X
.
Then the normalization

N(G_{R})\;\in\;\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

of
G_{R}
is a Lorentzian polynomial.

(v)

Assume also that
X=X_{\Sigma}
is a complete toric variety and that each
x_{i}
is equal to the class of a torus-invariant nef Cartier divisor
D_{i}
on
X
.
Let
P_{i}=P_{D_{i}}
be the polytope associated to
D_{i}
. Then

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=d}\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\;\;\in\;\;T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}],

where
\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
is the mixed volume of
P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}
of type
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
.

Motivated by
Theorem D
, we say that
G_{R}\in T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
is the
Macaulay dual generator
of the cohomology ring
R=\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We perform several explicit computations with examples in
§6.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we set up the notation used throughout the paper. We also present some preliminary results necessary to prove our main theorems.

2.1.
Double Schubert polynomials

Let
p\geq 1
be a positive integer.
Let
[p]:=\{1,2,\dots,p\}
and
\binom{[p]}{k}
denote the set of size
k
subsets of
[p]
.
For
I=\{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}\}
,
J=\{j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{k}\}\in\binom{[p]}{k}
, we define a partial order on
\binom{[p]}{k}
where
I\leq J
if
i_{m}\leq j_{m}
for all
m\in[k]
.
We write
S_{p}
for the symmetric group of permutations on
[p]
.
For any
w\in S_{p}
, we use one-line notation, i.e.,
w=(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{p})
with
w(i)=a_{i}
.
For any
k\in[p]
, we write
w[k]:=\{w(1),w(2),\dots,w(k)\}
.
We say
(i,j)
is an
inversion
of
w
if
1\leq i<j\leq p
and
w(i)>w(j)
.
For every
i\in[p-1]=\{1,\ldots,p-1\}
, we have the transposition
\mathfrak{s}_{i}=(i,i+1)\in S_{p}
.
The
length

\ell(w)
of a permutation
w
is the number of inversions of
w
or, equivalently, the minimum number of transpositions
\mathfrak{s}_{i}
of which
w
can be written as a product.

We equip
S_{p}
with a partial order structure known as
Bruhat order
. For comprehensive references on Bruhat order and related topics, we point to
[
Hum90
,
BB05
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
u,w\in S_{p}
, we say
w\geq u
in Bruhat order if
w[i]\geq u[i]
for all
i\in[p]
.

Double Schubert polynomials were introduced by Lascoux and Schützenberger
[
LS82
]
and can be defined via divided difference operators.

Definition 2.2
.

The
i
-th divided difference operator
\partial_{i}
takes each polynomial
f\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\dots,t_{p}]
to

\partial_{i}f(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})\;=\;\frac{f(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})-f(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},t_{i},t_{i+2}\dots,t_{p})}{t_{i}-t_{i+1}}.

The
Schubert polynomial
for
w\in S_{p}
is defined by the recursion

\mathfrak{S}_{w}(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})=\partial_{i}\mathfrak{S}_{w\mathfrak{s}_{i}}(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})

whenever
\ell(w)<\ell(w\mathfrak{s}_{i})
, with initial data
\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\prod_{i=1}^{p-1}t_{i}^{p-i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\dots,t_{p}]
.
The
double Schubert polynomials

\mathfrak{S}_{w}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
are defined by the same recursion where the divided difference operators only act on the
t_{i}
’s, but starting from
\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})=\prod_{i+j\leq p}(t_{i}-s_{j})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\dots,t_{p},s_{1},\dots,s_{p}]
.

2.2.
Multidegrees

Here we recall the definition and basic properties of multidegrees as presented in
[
KM05
]
and
[
MS05
, Chapter 8]
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
R
-module and
F_{\bullet}
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded free
R
-resolution

F_{\bullet}\colon\;\cdots\rightarrow F_{i}\rightarrow F_{i-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow F_{1}\rightarrow F_{0}

of
M
.
Let
t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}
be variables over
{\mathbb{Z}}
and consider the Laurent polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\textbf{t}},\mathbf{t^{-1}}]={\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1}]
, where the variable
t_{i}
corresponds with the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
.
If we write
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
with
\mathbf{b}_{i,j}=(\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1},\ldots,\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
, then we define the Laurent polynomial

\left[F_{i}\right]_{\textbf{t}}\,:=\,\sum_{j}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j}}=\sum_{j}t_{1}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,1}}\cdots t_{p}^{\mathbf{b}_{i,j,p}}.

Definition 2.3
.

The
K
-polynomial
of
M
is the Laurent polynomial given by

\mathcal{K}(M;{\textbf{t}})\;:=\;\sum_{i}{(-1)}^{i}\left[F_{i}\right]_{\textbf{t}}.

We have that, even if the grading of
R
is non-positive and we do not have a well-defined notion of Hilbert series, the above definition of
K
-polynomial is an invariant of the module
M
and it does not depend on the chosen free
R
-resolution
F_{\bullet}
(see
[
MS05
, Theorem 8.34]
).

Definition 2.4
.

The
multidegree polynomial
of
M
is the homogeneous polynomial
\mathcal{C}(M;{\textbf{t}})\in{\mathbb{Z}}[{\textbf{t}}]
given as the sum of all terms in

\mathcal{K}(M;\mathbf{1}-{\textbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(M;1-t_{1},\ldots,1-t_{p})
having total degree
{\rm codim}(M)
, which is the lowest degree appearing.

2.3.
Lorentzian and covolume polynomials

In this subsection, we briefly recall Lorentzian polynomials, dually Lorentzian polynomials, and covolume polynomials.

A subset
J\subset\mathbb{N}^{p}
is called
\mathrm{M}
-convex
if for any
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{p})
and
r=(r_{1},\dots,r_{p})
in
J
, and any
i
where
q_{i}<r_{i}
, there exists
j
such that
q_{j}>r_{j}
and both points
q+{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{j}
and
r-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{j}
are also contained in
J
.
We point out that M-convex sets are equivalent to basis elements of
discrete polymatroids

[
Mur03
,
Sch03
]
and integer points of
generalized permutohedra

[
Pos09
]
.
A polynomial with M-convex support has
saturated Newton polytope
(SNP).
In
[
MTY19
]
, Monical, Tokcan, and Yong conjectured that many polynomials in algebraic combinatorics have saturated Newton polytopes.

Let
h(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in
\mathbb{R}[{\textbf{t}}]=\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
.

Definition 2.5

(
[
BH20
]
)
.

The homogeneous polynomial
h
is called
Lorentzian
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

The coefficients of
h
are nonnegative.

(ii)

The support of
h
is M-convex.

(iii)

The quadratic form
\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i_{1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i_{2}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i_{e}}}h
has at most one positive eigenvalue for any
i_{1},\ldots,i_{e}\in[p]
where
e=d-2
.

There are several linear operators discussed in
[
BH20
]
that preserve the Lorentzian property. In particular, the normalization operator

N\left(\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{a_{\mathbf{n}}}{{\mathbf{n}}!}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\qquad\text{ where }\qquad{\mathbf{n}}!:=n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!,

preserves the Lorentzian property
[
BH20
, Corollary 3.7]
.

In
[
Alu24
]
, Aluffi defined the notion of covolume polynomials.
These polynomials arise by considering the Chow classes of irreducible subvarieties of a product of projective spaces.

Definition 2.6

(
[
Alu24
]
)
.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
be a multiprojective space over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
be an irreducible subvariety of codimension
c
.
The class of
X
can be written as

[X]\;=\;\sum_{|{\mathbf{n}}|=c}a_{\mathbf{n}}\,H_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots H_{p}^{n_{p}}\;\in\;A^{\bullet}({\mathbb{P}})={\mathbb{Z}}[H_{1},\dots,H_{p}]/\left(H_{1}^{m_{1}+1},\dots,H_{p}^{m_{p}+1}\right).

Let
P_{[X]}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})=\sum_{|{\mathbf{n}}|=c}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
be the polynomial associated to the class
[X]
of
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
.
A polynomial
P(t_{1},\dots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
with nonnegative real coefficients is a
covolume polynomial

if it is a limit of polynomials of the form
cP_{[X]}
for a positive real number
c
and an irreducible subvariety
X
of
{\mathbb{P}}
.

We are interested in the family of dually Lorentzian polynomials introduced by Ross, Süß, and Wannerer
[
RSW23
]
.

Definition 2.7

(
[
RSW23
]
)
.

A polynomial
h\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\dots,t_{p}]
is
dually Lorentzian
if

N\left(t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}h\left(t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{p}^{-1}\right)\right)

is Lorentzian for some
{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})
sufficiently large (i.e., such that
t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}h\left(t_{1}^{-1},\dots,t_{p}^{-1}\right)
is a polynomial).

As shown by Aluffi

Alu24

, covolume polynomials form a subfamily of the family of dually Lorentzian polynomials.

Remark 2.8

For any monomial
x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{p}^{\alpha_{n}}
and any polynomial

Proof.

This follows directly from

Finally, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

For any polynomial
h=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
and any
{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}

{\mathbf{w}}
-truncation

h
is the polynomial given by

\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\leq{\mathbf{w}}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

3.
Multidegree polynomials of prime ideals in non-standard gradings

In this section, working over an arbitrary positive
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-grading, we show that the multidegree polynomial of a prime ideal is a covolume polynomial.
Our main tool is the technique of
standardization
that was used in
[
CCRMMn23
,
CCRC23
]
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be an algebraically closed field and
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a
positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
polynomial ring (that is,
\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
and
\deg(\alpha)=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
).
Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
R
-module.
Since
R
is assumed to be positively graded, there is a well-defined notion of Hilbert series

\text{Hilb}_{M}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\text{Hilb}_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;:=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[M\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\right){\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[M\right]_{\mathbf{n}}\right)t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}},

and then we can write

\text{Hilb}_{M}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\frac{\mathcal{K}(M;{\textbf{t}})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-{\textbf{t}}^{\deg(x_{i})}\right)}.

Below we discuss the case of a standard multigrading.
This case is of special importance since it deals with closed subschemes of a product of projective spaces.

Remark 3.1

(Standard multigradings)
.

Assume that
R
has a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-grading (i.e.,
|\deg(x_{i})|=1
) and that
R
is the coordinate ring of
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
Let
X\subset{\mathbb{P}}
be a
d
-dimensional integral closed subscheme with coordinate ring
R/P
, where
P
is an
R
-homogeneous prime ideal.
The class of
X
in the Chow ring of
{\mathbb{P}}
is given by

\left[X\right]\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\\
|{\mathbf{n}}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=d\end{subarray}}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot H_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots H_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}\;\in\;A^{\bullet}({\mathbb{P}})=\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[H_{1},\ldots,H_{p}]}{\big(H_{1}^{m_{1}+1},\ldots,H_{p}^{m_{p}+1}\big)},

where
H_{i}
represents the class of the inverse image of a hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
.
We say that
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is the multidegree of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
.
Then the multidegree polynomial of
R/P
is given by

\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=d}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}.

The
volume polynomial
of
X
(see
[
BH20
, §4.2]
) is given by

{\rm vol}_{X}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\int\big(H_{1}t_{1}+\cdots+H_{p}t_{p}\big)^{d}\cap\left[X\right]\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=d}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot\frac{d!}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!}\cdot t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}.

Due to a fundamental result of Brändén and Huh (see
[
BH20
, Theorem 4.6]
), we have that
{\rm vol}_{X}({\textbf{t}})
is a Lorentzian polynomial.

The following lemma tells us that multidegree polynomials in a standard multigrading are
dually Lorentzian

[
RSW23
]
.
This result already appeared in
[
Alu24
, Proposition 2.8]
(also, see
[
HMMSD22
, Theorem 6]
), but here we give a short self-contained proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Keep the same notations and assumptions of
Remark 3.1
.
Consider the polynomial

F(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}\cdot\mathcal{C}\Big(R/P;\frac{1}{t_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{t_{p}}\Big).

Then the normalization
N(F)
is a Lorentzian polynomial
(
i.e.,
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\textbf{t}})
is dually Lorentzian
)
.

Proof.

From
Remark 3.1
, we derive the following equalities

\displaystyle N(F({\textbf{t}}))

\displaystyle\;=\;N\Bigg(t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}\cdot\mathcal{C}\Big(R/P;\frac{1}{t_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{t_{p}}\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle\;=\;N\Bigg(t_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{m_{p}}\cdot\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=d}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot\frac{1}{t_{1}^{m_{1}-n_{1}}}\cdots\frac{1}{t_{p}^{m_{p}-n_{p}}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\;=\;N\Bigg(\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=d}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot{t_{1}^{n_{1}}}\cdots{t_{p}^{n_{p}}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p},\;|{\mathbf{n}}|=d}\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)\cdot\frac{1}{n_{1}!\cdots n_{p}!}\cdot t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{p}^{n_{p}}

\displaystyle\;=\;\frac{1}{d!}\cdot{\rm vol}_{X}({\textbf{t}}).

Therefore
[
BH20
, Theorem 4.6]
implies that
N(F)
is Lorentzian.
∎

We now describe some basic properties of the process of standardization as developed in
[
CCRMMn23
,
CCRC23
]
.
For the rest of the section, the following setup is fixed.

Setup 3.3
.

For
1\leq i\leq n
, let
\ell_{i}=|\deg(x_{i})|
be the total degree of the variable
x_{i}
.
Let

S\;=\;\mathbb{k}\left[y_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\text{ and }1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}\right]

be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring such that

\deg(x_{i})\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{\ell_{i}}\deg(y_{i,j})\quad\text{for all \;\;$1\leq i\leq n$.}

We define the
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra homomorphism

\phi\colon R=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{x}]\longrightarrow S=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{y}],\quad\phi(x_{i})=y_{i,1}y_{i,2}\cdots y_{i,\ell_{i}}.

For an
R
-homogeneous ideal
I\subset R
, we say that the extension
J=\phi(I)S
is the
standardization
of
I
, since
J\subset S
is an
S
-homogeneous ideal in the standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring
S
.
By a slight abuse of notation, we consider both multidegree polynomials
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\textbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\textbf{t}})
as elements of the same polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[{\textbf{t}}]={\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
.

The next theorem contains some of the basic and desirable properties of the standardization of an ideal.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
CCRC23
, Theorem 7.2]
,
[
CCRMMn23
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Let
I\subset R
be an
R
-homogeneous ideal and
J=\phi(I)S
be its standardization.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

{\rm codim}(I)={\rm codim}(J)
.

(ii)

I\subset R
and
J\subset S
have the same
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded Betti numbers.

(iii)

\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\textbf{t}})=\mathcal{K}(S/J;{\textbf{t}})
and
\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\textbf{t}})=\mathcal{C}(S/J;{\textbf{t}})
.

(iv)

R/I
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring if and only if
S/J
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring.

(v)

Let
>
be a monomial order on
R
and
>^{\prime}
be a monomial order on
S
which is compatible with
\phi

(
i.e., if
f,g\in R
with
f>g
, then
\phi(f)>^{\prime}\phi(g)
)
.
Then
{\rm in}_{>^{\prime}}(J)=\phi({\rm in}_{>}(I))S
.

(vi)

If
I\subset R
is a prime ideal and it does not contain any variable, then
J\subset S
is also a prime ideal.

Finally, we are ready to present the main result of this section.
It yields a large new family of covolume polynomials.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
P\subset R
be a prime
R
-homogeneous ideal.
Then
\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\textbf{t}})
is a covolume polynomial.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\{i\mid x_{i}\in P\}
be the set that indexes the variables belonging to
P
.
Write
P=P^{\prime}+\left(x_{i}\mid i\in\mathcal{L}\right)
with
P^{\prime}\subset R
only involving the variables not in
\mathcal{L}
.
Let
T=R\left[z_{i}\mid i\in\mathcal{L}\right]
be a positively
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring extending the grading of
R
and with
\deg(z_{i})=\deg(x_{i})
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}=P^{\prime}T+\left(x_{i}-z_{i}\mid i\in\mathcal{L}\right)\subset T
.
Notice that
\mathfrak{P}
is a prime ideal containing no variable and that

\mathcal{C}(T/\mathfrak{P};{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\prod_{i\in\mathcal{L}}\langle\deg(x_{i}-z_{i}),{\textbf{t}}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}(T/P^{\prime}T;{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\prod_{i\in\mathcal{L}}\langle\deg(x_{i}),{\textbf{t}}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}(R/P^{\prime};{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\textbf{t}}),

where
\langle\deg(x_{i}),{\textbf{t}}\rangle=a_{i,1}t_{1}+\cdots+a_{i,p}t_{p}\in\mathbb{N}[{\textbf{t}}]
after writing
(a_{i,1},\ldots,a_{i,p})=\deg(x_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
(see
[
MS05
, Exercise 8.12]
).
Let
W=S\left[w_{i,j}\mid i\in\mathcal{L},1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}\right]
be a standard
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded polynomial ring where we consider the corresponding standardization
\mathfrak{Q}\subset W
of
\mathfrak{P}\subset T
.
From
Theorem 3.4
(iii), we get

\mathcal{C}(R/P;{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\mathcal{C}(T/\mathfrak{P};{\textbf{t}})\;=\;\mathcal{C}(W/\mathfrak{Q};{\textbf{t}}).

Finally,
\mathfrak{Q}\subset W
is a prime ideal by
Theorem 3.4
(vi), and so the result follows by
Remark 3.1
.
∎

4.
Equivariant cohomology in multigraded commutative algebra

In this section, we study the equivariant cohomology of the irreducible varieties that appear in multigraded commutative algebra.
Here we show that, under suitable conditions, equivariant classes of irreducible multigraded varieties yield covolume polynomials (up to changing the sign of negative coefficients).
We follow the references
[
AF24
]
and
[
CG10
, Chapter 5]
for the basics of equivariant cohomology and equivariant
K
-theory.

Let
R={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring with a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-grading.
Let
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{p}
be a torus acting on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Any such
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-grading can be specified by the torus weights.
More precisely, if we have the action

\left(h_{1},\ldots,h_{p}\right)\cdot\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\right)\;=\;\big(h_{1}^{d_{1,1}}h_{2}^{d_{1,2}}\cdots h_{p}^{d_{1,p}}a_{1},\,h_{1}^{d_{2,1}}h_{2}^{d_{2,2}}\cdots h_{p}^{d_{2,p}}a_{2},\,\ldots,\,h_{1}^{d_{n,1}}h_{2}^{d_{n,2}}\cdots h_{p}^{d_{n,p}}a_{n}\big)

for all
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{p})\in T
and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, then we obtain the specific grading
\deg(x_{i})=\mathbf{d}_{i}=(d_{i,1},d_{i,2},\ldots,d_{i,p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
There is a bijective correspondence between
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
R
-modules and
T
-equivariant coherent sheaves on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Since
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is smooth, the Grothendieck ring of
T
-equivariant vector bundles coincides with the Grothendieck ring of
T
-equivariant coherent sheaves.
We denote this ring by
K_{T}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
.
The Grothendieck ring
K_{T}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
coincides with the Grothendieck ring of a point and the representation ring of
T
:

K_{T}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\;\cong\;K_{T}({\rm pt})\;\cong\;R(T)\;=\;{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1}].

Given a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
R
-module
M
(i.e.,
\widetilde{M}
is a
T
-equivariant coherent sheaf on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
), we denote by
[M]^{T}
the equivariant class of
M
in
K_{T}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p},t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1}]
.

We consider the
T
-equivariant cohomology ring

\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\;:=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}\big(\mathbb{E}T\times^{T}\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\big),

where
\mathbb{E}T
is contractible with
T
acting freely (on the right).
Then
\mathbb{B}T:=\mathbb{E}T/T
is a classifying space for
T
.
Since we can take
\mathbb{E}T=\left({\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{n}
and
\mathbb{B}T=\left({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{C}}^{\infty}\right)^{n}
, it follows that

\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\;\cong\;\Lambda_{T}:=\text{H}^{\bullet}_{T}({\rm pt})\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{B}T)\;=\;{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}].

Given a
T
-subvariety
X\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
, we denote by
[X]^{T}:=[\mathbb{E}T\times^{T}X]
the equivariant class of
X
in
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
.

The following remark allows us to apply our results in
Section 3
to equivariant cohomology.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
R
-module and
X\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be a
T
-subvariety with coordinate ring
R/I
.
Let
K_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
and
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
be the Laurent polynomial and the polynomial representing the classes
[M]^{T}\in K_{T}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
and
[X]^{T}\in\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
, respectively.
Then we have the equalities

K_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\mathcal{K}(M;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\;=\;\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}).

Proof.

See, e.g.,
[
KM05
, §]
or
[
KMS06
, Proposition 1.19]
.
∎

Then
Theorem 3.5
poses the question of whether the class
[X]^{T}
of any irreducible
T
-subvariety
X\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
yields a covolume polynomial.
The following example shows that it is not always the case for arbitrary
T
-actions.

Example 4.2
.

Consider
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and the torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{2}
with the action

(h_{1},h_{2})\cdot(a_{1},a_{2})\;:=\;(h_{1}h_{2}a_{1},h_{1}h_{2}^{-1}a_{2})

for all
(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
(h_{1},h_{2})\in T
.
Let
R={\mathbb{C}}[x,y]
be the coordinate ring of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.
The
T
-action above specifies the grading
\deg(x)=(1,1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
and
\deg(y)=(1,-1)\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
Consider the variety
X=\{(0,0)\}\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
given by the origin.
It is clearly irreducible, but the polynomial representative of the cohomology class

[X]^{T}\;=\;\mathcal{C}(R/(x,y);t_{1},t_{2})\;=\;(t_{1}+t_{2})(t_{1}-t_{2})\;=\;t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}\qquad\text{(see, e.g., \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{MS}{}{}, Proposition 8.49]})}

is not a covolume polynomial (its support is not
M
-convex) after changing signs.

For the rest of this section, we shall use the following setup that avoids the complications of the previous example.

Setup 4.3
.

Assume that the torus
T
is given as
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{q}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{p-q}
where “
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{q}
comes with positive weights and
({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{p-q}
comes with negative weights”.
More precisely, we require that

\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\quad\text{ for all $1\leq i\leq q$}

and

-\mathbf{d}_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}\setminus\{\mathbf{0}\}\quad\text{ for all $q+1\leq i\leq p$}.

In this case we say that the action of
T
determines a
twisted positive grading
on
R
.

The following lemma tells us that we can “flip” twisted positive gradings to positive gradings.
A version of this lemma appeared in
[
CCRMMn23
, Lemma 3.3]
.
Let
\widetilde{R}=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a polynomial ring with the same variables as
R
but with grading given by setting
\deg(x_{i})=\mathbf{d}_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq q
and
\deg(x_{i})=-\mathbf{d}_{i}
for
q+1\leq i\leq p
.
Notice that
\widetilde{R}
has a positive
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-grading.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
Setup 4.3
.
Let
I\subset R
be a
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded ideal, and also denote by
I
the corresponding
\mathbb{N}^{p}
-graded ideal in
\widetilde{R}
.
Then we have
\mathcal{C}(\widetilde{R}/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},t_{q+1},\ldots,t_{p})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},-t_{q+1},\ldots,-t_{p}).

Proof.

Let
r=p-q
and set
s_{i}=t_{p+i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Notice that, if
\widetilde{F}_{\bullet}
is a
({\mathbb{Z}}^{q}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})
-graded free
\widetilde{R}
-resolution of
\widetilde{R}/I
with
\widetilde{F}_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}\widetilde{R}(-\mathbf{a}_{i,j},-\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
, then there is a corresponding
({\mathbb{Z}}^{q}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{r})
-graded free
R
-resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
R/I
with
F_{i}=\bigoplus_{j}R(-\mathbf{a}_{i,j},\mathbf{b}_{i,j})
.
By definition, this yields the equality of
K
-polynomials

\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})=\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\mathcal{K}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},s_{1}^{-1},\ldots,s_{r}^{-1})=\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}^{\mathbf{-1}}).

From
[
MS05
, Claim 8.54]
, we have
\mathcal{K}(R/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})+Q({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
, where
Q({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
is the sum of the terms of degree at least
{\rm codim}(I)+1
.
Equivalently, we get
\mathcal{K}(R/I;{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})+Q(\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})
.
It then follows that

\mathcal{K}(\widetilde{R}/I;\mathbf{1-t},\mathbf{1-s})=\mathcal{C}(R/I;t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{1}},\ldots,1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{r}})+Q(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{1}},\ldots,1-\tfrac{1}{1-s_{r}}).

By expanding the right hand side of the above equality, the result of the lemma is obtained. ∎

We are now ready to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Assume
Setup 4.3
.
Let
X\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
be an irreducible
T
-subvariety and
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})
be the polynomial representing the class
[X]^{T}\in\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{n})
.
Then
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{q},-t_{q+1},\ldots,-t_{p})
is a covolume polynomial.

Proof.

The result follows by combining
Remark 4.1
,
Lemma 4.4
, and
Theorem 3.5
.
∎

Our main application of the above theorem is the following corollary.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
{\rm Mat}_{m,n}={\mathbb{C}}^{m\times n}
be the space of
m\times n
matrices with complex entries and consider the natural action of the torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{m}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
given by
(g,h)\cdot M=g\cdot M\cdot h^{-1}
for all
M\in{\rm Mat}_{m,n}
and
(g,h)\in T
.
Let
X\subset{\rm Mat}_{m,n}
be an irreducible
T
-variety and
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be the polynomial representing the class
[X]^{T}
in
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}\left({\rm Mat}_{m,n}\right)={\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]
.
Then we have that
C_{X}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m},-s_{1},\ldots,-s_{n})
is a covolume polynomial.

5.
Equivariant cohomology of matrix Richardson varieties

In this section, we study the equivariant cohomology of matrix Richardson varieties for a pair of permutations.
An interesting outcome of our approach is the definition of a new family of polynomials that we call
double Richardson polynomials
.
These polynomials specialize to many polynomials of interest.

We take the viewpoint of studying Schubert varieties and Schubert polynomials through a certain degeneracy loci problem that was introduced by Fulton
[
Ful92
]
.
Let
E_{\bullet}
and
F_{\bullet}
be two flagged complex vector spaces of dimension
n
with

F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{n}=F\qquad\text{ and }\qquad E=E_{n}\twoheadrightarrow\cdots\twoheadrightarrow E_{1},

where
\dim(F_{i})=\dim(E_{i})=i
.
By choosing bases, we identify
{\text{Hom}}(F,E)
with the space
{\rm Mat}_{n,n}\cong{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
of
n\times n
matrices with complex entries.
Consider the natural action of the torus
T=({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
given by
(g,h)\cdot M=g\cdot M\cdot h^{-1}
for all
M\in{\rm Mat}_{n,n}
and
(g,h)\in T
.
For a given permutation
w\in S_{n}
, we consider the degeneracy locus

D_{w}\;:=\;\big\{\varphi\in{\text{Hom}}(F,E)\mid\text{rank }(\varphi_{p,q})\leq\text{rank }(w_{p,q})\text{ for all $1\leq p,q\leq n$}\big\},

where
\varphi_{p,q}
is the composition of maps
F_{q}\rightarrow F\xrightarrow{\varphi}E\rightarrow E_{p}
and
w_{p\times q}
is the upper-left submatrix of size
p\times q
of the permutation matrix of
w
.
Under the identification
{\text{Hom}}(F,E)\cong{\rm Mat}_{n,n}
, we say that
D_{w}
is the
matrix Schubert variety
of
w
.

Choose a basis
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\}
of
F
such that
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}\}
is a basis of
F_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.
Let
\widetilde{F}_{\bullet}
be the opposite flag

\widetilde{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\widetilde{F}_{n}\qquad\text{ where }\qquad\widetilde{F}_{i}=\langle f_{n-i+1},\ldots,f_{n}\rangle.

For a given permutation
w\in S_{n}
, we consider the degeneracy locus

D^{w}\;:=\;\big\{\varphi\in{\text{Hom}}(F,E)\mid\text{rank }(\varphi^{p,q})\leq\text{rank }(w^{p,q})\text{ for all $1\leq p,q\leq n$}\big\},

where
\varphi^{p,q}
is the composition of maps
\widetilde{F}_{q}\rightarrow F\xrightarrow{\varphi}E\rightarrow E_{p}
and
w^{p\times q}
is the upper-right submatrix of size
p\times q
of the permutation matrix of
w
.
Under the identification
{\text{Hom}}(F,E)\cong{\rm Mat}_{n,n}
, we say that
D^{w}
is the
opposite matrix Schubert variety
of
w
.

A result of fundamental importance for us is the following geometric interpretation of double Schubert polynomials.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
FR03
,
KM05
,
AF24
]
)
.

The equivariant class
\left[D_{w}\right]^{T}
of the matrix Schubert variety
D_{w}
in the cohomology ring
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}({\rm Mat}_{n,n})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}]
is given by the double Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
.

The next lemma expresses the equivariant class of the degeneracy locus
D^{w}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

The equivariant class
\left[D^{w}\right]^{T}\in\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}({\rm Mat}_{n,n})
of
D^{w}
is given by the double Schubert polynomial

\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}w}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},s_{n},\ldots,s_{1})

of
w_{0}w
with a reverse ordering of the variables
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\colon F\rightarrow F
be the involution given by setting
\alpha(f_{i})=f_{n+1-i}
.
To simplify notation, let
\mathcal{M}={\text{Hom}}(F,E)
.
Let
\eta\colon T\rightarrow T
be the involution given by
\eta(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n},h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n},h_{n},\ldots,h_{1})
.
Then the induced involution
f\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M},\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\alpha
is an equivariant map with respect to
\eta
because

f\left((g,h)\cdot\varphi\right)\;=\;\eta(g,h)\cdot f(\varphi)

for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{M}
and
(g,h)\in T
.
Then the induced pullback homomorphism is given by

f^{*}\colon\;\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})={\mathbb{Z}}[{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}]\;\rightarrow\;\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M})={\mathbb{Z}}[{\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}],\qquad t_{i}\mapsto t_{i}\text{\; and \;}s_{j}\mapsto s_{n+1-j}

(see
[
AF24
, Exercise 3.2.3]
).
Let
\mathbb{E}_{m}=({\mathbb{C}}^{m}\setminus 0)^{n}\times({\mathbb{C}}^{m}\setminus 0)^{n}
.
Then
\text{H}_{T}^{i}(\mathcal{M})=\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{E}_{m}\times^{T}\mathcal{M})
for
i<2m-1

and the pullback map
f^{*}
can be constructed by taking the pullback in (usual) cohomology along the equivariant map

\mathbb{E}_{m}\times^{T}\mathcal{M}\;\xrightarrow{(\gamma,f)}\;\mathbb{E}_{m}\times^{T}\mathcal{M}

where
\gamma\colon\mathbb{E}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{m}
is the involution given by
\gamma(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},v_{n},\ldots,v_{1})
for all
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{E}_{m}
(see
[
AF24
, Exercise 3.2.1]
).
Since
\mathbb{E}_{m}\times^{T}\mathcal{M}
is smooth, it follows that

f^{*}\left(\big[D_{w_{0}w}\big]^{T}\right)\;=\;\big[f^{-1}(D_{w_{0}w})\big]^{T}=\big[D^{w}\big]^{T}

(see, e.g.,
[
AF24
, Proposition A.3.2]
,
[
Ful97
, §B.3]
).
The result of the lemma now follows by invoking
Theorem 5.1
.
∎

We are now ready to introduce the two main objects of this section.

Definition 5.3
.

For a pair of permutations
(w,u)
in
S_{n}
, we have:

(i)

D_{u}^{w}:=D_{u}\cap D^{w}
is the
matrix Richardson variety
.

(ii)

\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}):=\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}w}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}^{\prime})
is the
double Richardson polynomial
, where
{\textbf{s}}^{\prime}=(s_{n},\ldots,s_{1})
denotes the reverse of
{\textbf{s}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
.

We point out that the matrix Richardson variety
D_{u}^{w}
is a reduced and irreducible
T
-subvariety of
{\rm Mat}_{n,n}
.
We have that
D_{u}^{w}
is nonempty if and only if
w\geq u
in the Bruhat order, and when it is nonempty, it has dimension
\dim(D_{u}^{w})=\ell(w)-\ell(u)
.
For details on Richardson varieties, the reader is referred to, e.g.,
[
Ric92
,
Bri05
,
Spe23
]
.

Our main result in this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 5.4
.

For two permutations
u,w\in S_{n}
with
w\geq u
in Bruhat order, the following statements hold:

(i)

The double Richardson polynomial
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
represents the equivariant class of the matrix Richardson variety
D_{u}^{w}
in
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}({\rm Mat}_{n,n})
.

(ii)

The
(
sign-changed
)
double Richardson polynomial
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
is a covolume polynomial.

Proof.

(i) We have the equality
[D_{u}^{w}]^{T}=[D_{u}\cap D^{w}]^{T}=[D_{u}]^{T}\cdot[D^{w}]^{T}
in
\text{H}_{T}^{\bullet}({\rm Mat}_{n,n})
.
Therefore
Lemma 5.2
and
Theorem 5.1
imply that
\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}w}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}}^{\prime})=\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
represents the equivariant class of the matrix Richardson variety
D_{u}^{w}
.

(ii) By utilizing part (i) and
Corollary 4.6
, it follows that
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
is a covolume polynomial.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

Since the product of two covolume polynomials is a covolume polynomial (see
[
Alu24
, Corollary 2.14]
,
[
RSW23
, Proposition 4.9]
) and covolume polynomials are stable under certain linear transformations (see
[
Alu24
, Theorem 2.13]
), to show that
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})=\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})\mathfrak{S}_{w_{0}w}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}}^{\prime})
is a covolume polynomial, it suffices to prove that
\mathfrak{S}_{w}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
is a covolume polynomial for all
w\in S_{n}
.
In any case, we would need to apply
Corollary 4.6
to either matrix Richardson varieties or matrix Schubert varieties.

The double Richardson polynomial specializes to many polynomials of interest:

(i)

\mathfrak{R}_{w_{0}/u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})=\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},{\textbf{s}})
is the double Schubert polynomial.

(ii)

\mathfrak{R}_{w_{0}/u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0})=\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0})=\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}})
is the ordinary Schubert polynomial.

(iii)

We say that
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})=\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},\mathbf{0})
is the (ordinary)
Richardson polynomial
.

The next proposition shows that dually Lorentzian polynomials are discretely log-concave.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
h=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{n}}
is a dually Lorentzian polynomial, we have that

a_{\mathbf{n}}^{2}\;\,\geq\;\,a_{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{i}-{\mathbf{e}}_{j}}a_{{\mathbf{n}}-{\mathbf{e}}_{i}+{\mathbf{e}}_{j}}.

Proof.

Since
N\left({\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{m}}h(t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1})\right)=\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{a_{\mathbf{n}}}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}})!}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}
is Lorentzian for some
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
large enough, by
[
BH20
, Proposition 4.4]
we get the inequality in the statement.
∎

The following remark states that the truncation of a dually Lorentzian polynomial is dually Lorentzian.
This result also follows from
[
RSW23
, §3]
, but here we give a short self-contained proof.

Remark 5.7
.

Let
P({\textbf{t}})=\sum_{\mathbf{n}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}
be a dually Lorentzian polynomial.
Let
{\mathbf{w}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
.
Then the truncation

P^{\prime}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\leq{\mathbf{w}}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{n}}}

is dually Lorentzian.

Proof.

Take
{\mathbf{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{p}
large enough such that
{\mathbf{w}}\leq{\mathbf{m}}
and

Q({\textbf{t}})\;=\;N\left({\textbf{t}}^{\mathbf{m}}P(t_{1}^{-1},\ldots,t_{p}^{-1})\right)\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}}\frac{a_{\mathbf{n}}}{({\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}})!}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{n}}}

is a Lorentzian polynomial.
Then

\partial_{t_{1}}^{m_{1}-w_{1}}\cdots\partial_{t_{p}}^{m_{p}-w_{p}}Q({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\leq{\mathbf{w}}}\frac{a_{\mathbf{n}}}{({\mathbf{w}}-{\mathbf{n}})!}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{w}}-{\mathbf{n}}}

is also Lorentzian, and consequently

\sum_{{\mathbf{n}}\leq{\mathbf{w}}}a_{\mathbf{n}}{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{n}}+{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{w}}}\;=\;{\textbf{t}}^{{\mathbf{m}}-{\mathbf{w}}}P^{\prime}({\textbf{t}})

is dually Lorentzian.
Finally,
Remark 2.8
implies that
P^{\prime}({\textbf{t}})
is dually Lorentzian.
∎

We now obtain some consequences for certain polynomials of interest in algebraic combinatorics.

Corollary 5.8
.

The following polynomials have
M
-convex support and are discretely log-concave:

(i)

(
sign-changed
)
Double Richardson polynomials
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
.

(ii)

Richardson polynomials
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
.

(iii)

(
sign-changed
)
Double Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}},-{\textbf{s}})
.

(iv)

Schubert polynomials
\mathfrak{S}_{u}({\textbf{t}})
.

(v)

Any truncation of the above polynomials.

Proof.

By
Theorem 5.4
and
Remark 5.7
, these polynomials and their truncations are dually Lorentzian.

Since all these polynomials are dually Lorentzian and Lorentzian polynomials have M-convex support,
[
Sch03
, §44.6f]
implies that the support of all these polynomials is M-convex (also, see
[
Alu24
, Corollary 2.12]
).
The discrete log-concavity statement follows from
Proposition 5.6
.
∎

We now compare the Richardson polynomial
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
with the
skew Schubert polynomial
of Lenart and Sottile
[
LS03
]
(also, see
[
WY14
, Remark 2.16]
).

Remark 5.9
.

Let
w\geq u
be two permutations in
S_{n}
.
Lenart and Sottile
[
LS03
]
defined the skew Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}
as the polynomial representative in normal form of the class of the
Richardson variety

X_{u}^{w}=X_{u}\cap X^{w}
in the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}({{\mathcal{F}\ell}_{n}})
of the full flag variety
{{\mathcal{F}\ell}_{n}}
.
The Borel presentation

\text{H}^{\bullet}({{\mathcal{F}\ell}_{n}})\;=\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}]}{\left(e_{1}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}),\ldots,e_{n}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\right)}

(where
e_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
is the
i
-th elementary symmetric polynomial) yields a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis given by the monomials
t_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots t_{n}^{a_{n}}
with
a_{i}\leq n-i
.
We say that a polynomial representative is in
normal form
if it can be written as a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-linear combination of this distinguished monomial basis.
Let
n=4
and consider the permutations
w=3412>2143=u
.
The Richardson polynomial is given by

\mathfrak{R}_{3412/2143}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;\mathfrak{S}_{2143}({\textbf{t}})^{2}\;=\;t_{1}^{4}+2\,t_{1}^{3}t_{2}+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}+2\,t_{1}^{3}t_{3}+2\,t_{1}^{2}t_{2}t_{3}+t_{1}^{2}t_{3}^{2}.

However, the skew Schubert polynomial (the polynomial representative in normal form) is given by

\mathfrak{S}_{3412/2143}({\textbf{t}})\;=\;t_{1}^{3}t_{2}\,+\,t_{1}^{3}t_{3}\,+\,t_{1}^{2}t_{2}t_{3}.

Due to
Corollary 5.8
, if the skew Schubert polynomial
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
is a truncation of the Richardson polynomial
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
, then
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
is a dually Lorentzian polynomial.
However, as in
Remark 5.9
,
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
may not coincide with a truncation of
\mathfrak{R}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
.
Thus we should ask the question below.

Question 5.10
.

Let
w\geq u
be two permutations in
S_{n}
.
We ask the following:

(a)

Is the support of
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
an M-convex set?

(b)

Is
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
discretely log-concave?

(c)

Is
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
a dually Lorentzian polynomial?

(d)

Is
\mathfrak{S}_{w/u}({\textbf{t}})
a Lorentzian polynomial?

6.
Macaulay dual generators of cohomology rings

In this section, we study the Macaulay dual generators of cohomology rings of smooth complex algebraic varieties.
We prove that under certain positivity assumptions the Macaulay dual generators are denormalized Lorentzian polynomials.

6.1.
Gorenstein algebras over a base ring

Before presenting our results on cohomology rings, which is our main interest, we develop basic ideas regarding the notion of Gorenstein algebras over a base ring. (These developments are probably known to the experts but we could not find a suitable reference for us.)
For details on Gorenstein rings and duality results, the reader is referred to
[
Eis95
, Chapter 21]
,
[
BH98
, Chapter 3]
.
Throughout this subsection we use the following setup.

Setup 6.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring (always assumed to be commutative and with identity) and
R
be a positively graded finitely generated algebra over
R_{0}=A
.
Choose a positively graded polynomial ring
S=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with a graded surjection
S\twoheadrightarrow R
and write
R\cong S/I
for some homogeneous ideal
I\subset S
.
Let
{\mathfrak{m}}:=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\subset S
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
Let
\delta_{i}:=\deg(x_{i})>0
and
\delta:=\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{n}
.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we denote by
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
the residue field at
\mathfrak{p}
and we set
S(\mathfrak{p}):=S\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
R(\mathfrak{p}):=R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

We are interested in the following relative notion of Gorenstein.

Definition 6.2
.

We say that
R
is a
Gorenstein algebra over
A
if the natural morphism
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
is a Gorenstein morphism (see
[
Sta24
,
Tag 0C02
]
).
This means that
R
is
A
-flat and the fiber
R(\mathfrak{p})=R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a Gorenstein ring for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
. We also say that
R
is an
Artinian Gorenstein algebra over
A
if
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
is a finite Gorenstein morphism.

For a graded
S
-module
M
, we denote the
A
-relative graded Matlis dual
by

M^{\vee_{A}}\;=\;{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(M,A)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}{\text{Hom}}_{A}\left({M}_{-\nu},A\right).

Remark 6.3
.

Let
L,M,N
be finitely generated graded
R
-modules that are
A
-flat.
Then:

(i)

For all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, the graded part
M_{\nu}
is a finitely generated flat
A
-module, and thus
A
-projective.

(ii)

\left(M^{\vee_{A}}\right)^{\vee_{A}}\cong M
.

(iii)

\text{Ann}_{R}\left(M^{\vee_{A}}\right)=\text{Ann}_{R}(M)
.

(iv)

If
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
is a short exact sequence, then
0\rightarrow N^{\vee_{A}}\rightarrow M^{\vee_{A}}\rightarrow L^{\vee_{A}}\rightarrow 0
is also exact.

Remark 6.4
.

All the fibers
R(\mathfrak{p})=R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
of
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
have the same dimension in either of the following two cases:

(i)

f
is a finite morphism.

(ii)

f
is flat and
{\text{Spec}}(A)
is connected (e.g.,
A
is a domain).

Proof.

(i) In this case it is clear that all the fibers
R(\mathfrak{p})
have Krull dimension
0
.

(ii) Notice that each graded part
R_{\nu}
is a locally-free
A
-module of constant rank (see, e.g.,
[
Sta24
,
Tag 00NX
]
).
Thus the Hilbert function of the graded
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra
R(\mathfrak{p})
is the same for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module.
Then
M=0
if and only if
M\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

Remark 6.6
.

A Cohen–Macaulay ring
S
with a canonical module
\omega_{S}
is Gorenstein if and only if
\omega_{S}
is generated locally by one element (i.e.,
\omega_{S_{P}}\cong\omega_{S}\otimes_{S}S_{P}
is generated by one element for all
P\in{\text{Spec}}(S)
).

The next proposition is similar to
[
CR23
, Lemma 2.10]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

Assume that
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
is flat and that either
{\text{Spec}}(A)
is connected or
f
is finite.
Let
e
be the common dimension of the fibers of
f

(
see
Remark 6.4
)
.
Then
R
is Gorenstein over
A
if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
such that
i\neq n-e
.

(b)

\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}(R,S)
is
A
-flat and it is generated locally by one element as an
R
-module.

Proof.

Here we use the property of exchange for local Ext modules (see
[
LK79
, Theorem A.5]
,
[
AK80
, Theorem 1.9]
).
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, consider the natural base change maps

\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}\colon\;\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\;\rightarrow\;\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p})).

We have the following two important properties:

(i)

If
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}
is surjective, then it is an isomorphism.

(ii)

If
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}
is surjective, then
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i-1}
is surjective if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
-flat.

For all
i>n
, since each
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}
is surjective by Hilbert’s syzygy theorem, we obtain the vanishing
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
(see
Remark 6.5
).
For all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, recall that the fiber
R(\mathfrak{p})
is a Cohen–Macaulay ring if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(p),S(\mathfrak{p}))=0
for all
i\neq n-e
(this follows for instance by the local duality theorem).

Let
i>n-e
.
If each
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i+1}
is surjective and
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i+1}(R,S)=0
, it follows that
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{i}
is surjective, and this implies that
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p}))=0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Hence, by descending induction on
i
, we obtain
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
for all
i>n-e
if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p}))=0
for all
i>n-e
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

So we assume that
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
for all
i>n-e
(and, equivalently, that
\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p}))=0
for all
i>n-e
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
).

Since each
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n-e+1}
is surjective and
\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e+1}(R,S)=0
, it follows that each
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n-e}
is surjective.
In turn, this implies that
\gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n-e-1}
is surjective for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}(R,S)
is
A
-flat.
Similarly, by descending induction on
i
, we can show that
\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{i}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p}))=0
for all
i<n-e
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
for all
i<n-e
and
\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}(R,S)
is
A
-flat.

Therefore, in our current setting, we have shown that
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
is a Cohen–Macaulay morphism (see
[
Sta24
,
Tag 045Q
]
) if and only if
\text{Ext}_{S}^{i}(R,S)=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
with
i\neq n-e
and
\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}(R,S)
is
A
-flat.

Let
M=\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}(R,S)
.
To conclude the proof, we may assume that
f
is a Cohen–Macaulay morphism, and we need to show that
M
can be generated locally by one element if and only if
M\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\cong\text{Ext}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}^{n-e}(R(\mathfrak{p}),S(\mathfrak{p}))
can be generated by one element for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
This can be proved using basic properties of Fitting ideals (see
[
Eis95
, §20.2]
).
Indeed, the claim follows by
[
Eis95
, Proposition 20.6]
because
{\rm Fitt}_{1}(M)=R
if and only if
{\rm Fitt}_{1}(M\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))=\left({\rm Fitt}_{1}(M)\right)R(\mathfrak{p})=R(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
(again, see
Remark 6.5
).
So the proof is complete.
∎

Motivated by the above proposition, when
R
is Gorenstein over
A
and
e
is the common dimension of the fibers, we say that

\omega_{R/A}\;:=\;\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-e}\big(R,S(-\delta)\big)

is a relative canonical module of
R
over
A
.

Assume that
R
is a finitely generated
A
-module.
Let
d>0
be a positive integer and
\rho\colon R\rightarrow A(-d)
be a graded
A
-linear map (following tradition we call this map an
orientation
).
We say that
R
satisfies Poincaré duality with respect to the orientation
\rho
if

R\otimes_{A}R\;\rightarrow\;A(-d),\quad r_{1}\otimes r_{2}\mapsto\rho(r_{1}r_{2})

is a perfect pairing.
This means that the pairing induces a graded isomorphism
R\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)(-d)
of
R
-modules.
In particular, we have perfect pairings
R_{i}\otimes_{A}R_{d-i}\rightarrow A
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.
We also denote by

\rho\colon R_{d}\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}A

the induced isomorphism.
Since we are assuming that
R
is a finitely generated
A
-module, we actually have that
{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
coincides with
{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
(see
[
BH98
, Exercise 1.5.19]
), but we prefer the notation
{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
to stress our interest in the graded parts of
R
.

Remark 6.8
.

By the Hom-tensor adjunction, we have the isomorphism

{\text{Hom}}_{R}\left(R,{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A(-d))\right)\;\cong\;{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}\left(R\otimes_{A}R,A(-d)\right).

Thus having a perfect pairing
R\otimes_{A}R\rightarrow A(-d)
is the same as having
R\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)(-d)
.

We consider the inverse polynomial ring
T=A[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
where
y_{i}
is identified with
x_{i}^{-1}
.
The
S
-module structure of
T
is given by setting that

x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\,\cdot\,y_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\beta_{n}}\;\;=\;\;\begin{cases}y_{1}^{\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\beta_{n}-\alpha_{n}}&\text{if $\beta_{i}\geq\alpha_{i}$ for all $1\leq i\leq n$}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then
T
is a negatively graded polynomial ring with
\deg(y_{i})=-\delta_{i}
.
We have the natural isomorphisms

T\;\cong\;{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(S,A)\;\cong\;\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{n}\big(S\big)(-\delta)

of graded
S
-modules.
Recall the basic computation
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{n}(S)\cong\frac{1}{x_{1}\ldots x_{n}}A[x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{n}^{-1}]
.

The following theorem extends well-known results about Artinian Gorenstein algebras (over a field) to our current relative setting over a Noetherian base ring.

Theorem 6.9
.

Assume
Setup 6.1
and that
f\colon{\text{Spec}}(R)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
is a finite flat morphism and that
R
satisfies Poincaré duality with respect to an orientation
\rho\colon R\rightarrow A(-d)
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
A
.

(ii)

We have the isomorphisms
\omega_{R/A}=\text{Ext}_{S}^{n}(R,S(-\delta))\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)\cong R(d)
.

(iii)

Consider the inverse polynomial

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\delta_{n}=d}\rho\big(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\big)y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\;\;\in\;\;T=A[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}].

Then the presenting ideal of
R=S/I
is given as the annihilator of
G_{R}
:

I\;=\;\{g\in S\mid g\cdot G_{R}=0\}.

Proof.

Since
R=\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(R)
is
A
-flat and
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R)=0
for
i\geq 1
, the generic version of the local duality theorem given in
[
CR23
, Theorem A]
yields the isomorphisms
\text{Ext}_{S}^{n-i}(R,S(-\delta))\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(R),A)
for all
i
.
Therefore,
R
is Gorenstein over
A
by
Proposition 6.7
and we have the isomorphisms
\omega_{R/A}=\text{Ext}_{S}^{n}(R,S(-\delta))\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)\cong R(d)
.
So the proofs of part (i) and (ii) are complete.

Let
E=I^{\perp_{A}}=\{G\in T\mid g\cdot G=0\text{ for all $g\in I$}\}
be the (relative) inverse system of
I
.
We have the natural isomorphisms

E\;\cong\;{\text{Hom}}_{S}\big(R,T\big)\;\cong\;{\text{Hom}}_{S}\big(R,{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(S,A)\big)\;\cong\;{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)=R^{\vee_{A}}.

The isomorphism
E\cong R^{\vee_{A}}
can be obtained by dualizing the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow S\rightarrow R\rightarrow 0
and writing

0\;\rightarrow\;E\cong R^{\vee_{A}}\;\rightarrow\;T\cong S^{\vee_{A}}\;\rightarrow\;T/E\cong I^{\vee_{A}}\;\rightarrow\;0

(see
Remark 6.3
).
By part (ii), there is an inverse polynomial
G\in T=A[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
of degree
-d
that generates
E
as an
R
-module.
Then the dual short exact sequence
0\rightarrow I\rightarrow S\rightarrow E^{\vee_{A}}\rightarrow 0
shows that

I\;=\;\{g\in S\mid g\cdot G=0\}.

To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that
G_{R}
generates the
A
-module
E_{-d}
.
By construction, for any homogeneous polynomial
g\in S_{d}
, we have that

g\cdot G_{R}\;=\;\rho\left(\overline{g}\right),

where
\overline{g}
denotes the class of
g\in S_{d}
in
R_{d}=S_{d}/I_{d}
.
This implies that under the isomorphism
E_{-d}\cong{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R_{d},A)
, the inverse polynomial
G_{R}\in E_{-d}
corresponds to the isomorphism
\rho\in{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R_{d},A)
.
On the other hand, under the isomorphism
{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R_{d},A)\cong A
, the isomorphism
\rho
should correspond to a unit in
A
.
This shows that
G_{R}
is a generator of
E_{-d}
, and so the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

Definition 6.10
.

Following standard notation, we say that the inverse polynomial
G_{R}\in T=A[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
presented in
Theorem 6.9
is the
Macaulay dual generator of
R
over
A
.

It is well-known that, over a field
\mathbb{k}
, an Artinian graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra is Gorenstein if and only if it is a Poincaré duality algebra (see, e.g.,
[
HMM+13
, Theorem 2.79]
).
The following simple example shows that, over Noetherian base rings, we may not have the converse of
Theorem 6.9
.

Example 6.11
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and consider the reduced
\mathbb{k}
-algebra
A=\mathbb{k}[t]/\left(t(t-1)\right)
.
Consider the standard graded
A
-algebra

R\;=\;\frac{A[x,y]}{\left(tx,\;(t-1)y,\;x^{2},\;xy,\;\left(x,y\right)^{3}\right)}.

Since we have the natural isomorphism
R\cong A\oplus\frac{A}{(t)}x\oplus\frac{A}{(t-1)}y\oplus\frac{A}{(t-1)}y^{2}
, it follows that
R
is
A
-flat.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}=(t)\subset A
and
\mathfrak{p}_{2}=(t-1)\subset A
be the two points of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Then the two fibers

R(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{k}[x]}{\left(x^{2}\right)}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad R(\mathfrak{p}_{2})\;\cong\;\frac{\mathbb{k}[y]}{\left(y^{3}\right)}

are Artinian Gorenstein algebras over
\mathbb{k}=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{1})=\kappa(\mathfrak{p}_{2})
.
Therefore,
R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
A
.
Each of the fibers of
R
are Poincaré duality algebras, but we do not have a
global
Poincaré duality of
R
over
A
(as we introduced it).
Indeed,
R
cannot have a Poincaré duality over
A
because, for instance, the top nonvanishing graded part
R_{2}\cong\frac{A}{(t-1)}y^{2}
is
not
isomorphic to
A
.

The next proposition shows that we get the converse of
Theorem 6.9
when the base ring
A
satisfies a Quillen–Suslin type of theorem (i.e., every finitely generated projective module is free).

Proposition 6.12
.

Assume
Setup 6.1
and one of the following conditions:

(a)

A
is a principal ideal domain
(
e.g.,
A={\mathbb{Z}}
)
.

(b)

A
is a polynomial ring over a field.

(c)

Or, more generally, every finitely generated projective
A
-module is
A
-free.

Then,
R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
A
if and only if
R
satisfies a Poincaré duality over
A
.

Proof.

We only need to show the converse of
Theorem 6.9
.
Thus we assume that
R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
A
and that every finitely generated projective
A
-module is
A
-free.
The latter condition is satisfied in the case of PIDs and polynomial rings over a field (see, e.g.,
[
Lam06
]
).
Let
d
be the top nonvanishing graded part of
R
.
Due to our assumptions,
R_{d}\cong A
and
R(\mathfrak{p})=R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is an Artinian Gorenstein algebra for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Consequently, for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, the canonical module
\omega_{R(\mathfrak{p})}={}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(R(\mathfrak{p}),\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
of
R(\mathfrak{p})
is generated by its graded part of degree
-d
.
This implies that
{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
is generated by its graded part of degree
-d
(see
Remark 6.5
), and so by choosing a basis generator of the graded part
{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R_{d},A)
of degree
-d
, we obtain an isomorphism
\left(R/\mathfrak{a}\right)(d)\xrightarrow{\cong}{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
for some homogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset R
.
On the other hand, since
\text{Ann}_{R}\left({}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)\right)=0
(see
Remark 6.3
), we obtain the isomorphism
R(d)\xrightarrow{\cong}{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{A}(R,A)
.
The result of the proposition now follows from
Remark 6.8
.
∎

6.2.
Cohomology rings as Artinian Gorenstein algebras over
{\mathbb{Z}}

In this subsection, we apply our developments in
§6.1
to cohomology rings.
Our main result in this direction is the following theorem.

Theorem 6.13
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional smooth complex algebraic variety.
Suppose that the cohomology ring
R=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\text{H}^{2i}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is a flat
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebra
(
i.e., it is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free
)
.
Let
\rho\colon R_{d}=\text{H}^{2d}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
be the natural degree map.
Choose a graded presentation
R\cong S/I
where
S={\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
\delta_{i}=\deg(x_{i})>0
, and
I\subset S
is a homogeneous ideal.
Let
\delta=\delta_{1}+\cdots+\delta_{n}
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

R
is Artinian Gorenstein over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(ii)

We have the isomorphisms
\omega_{R/{\mathbb{Z}}}=\text{Ext}_{S}^{n}(R,S(-\delta))\cong{}^{*}{\text{Hom}}_{\mathbb{Z}}(R,{\mathbb{Z}})\cong R(d)
.

(iii)

Consider the inverse polynomial ring
T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
with the identification
y_{i}=x_{i}^{-1}
.
The ideal
I\subset S
is given as the annihilator

I\;=\;\{g\in S\mid g\cdot G_{R}=0\}

of the inverse polynomial

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}\delta_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}\delta_{n}=d}\rho\big(x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\big)y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\;\;\in\;\;T={\mathbb{Z}}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}].

(iv)

Assume that
X
is complete and that each
x_{i}
is equal to the first Chern class
c_{1}(L_{i})
of a nef line bundle
L_{i}
on
X
.
Then the normalization

N(G_{R})\;\in\;\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

of
G_{R}
is a Lorentzian polynomial.

Proof.

Parts (i), (ii), and (iii) follow from
Theorem 6.9
because
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
satisfies Poincaré duality and we are assuming it is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free.

We now concentrate on proving part (iv).
Notice that the normalization

N(G_{R})\;=\;\frac{1}{d!}\int\big(x_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}y_{n}\big)^{d}\cap[X]\;\;\in\;\;\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]

of
G_{R}
is the volume polynomial of
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
divided by
d!
.
By using Chow’s lemma, we can find a proper birational surjective morphism
f\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
where
X^{\prime}
is a projective variety.
Let
L_{i}^{\prime}=f^{*}(L_{i})
and
x_{i}^{\prime}=c_{1}(L_{i}^{\prime})
.
The projection formula yields the equality

N(G_{R})\;=\;\frac{1}{d!}\int\big(x_{1}^{\prime}y_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}^{\prime}y_{n}\big)^{d}\cap[X^{\prime}]\;\;\in\;\;\mathbb{R}[y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}].

Since each
L_{i}^{\prime}
is nef on
X^{\prime}
, we have that
N(G_{R})
is a Lorentzian polynomial due to
[
BH20
, Theorem 4.6]
.
∎

We close this subsection with some specific computations.

Example 6.14

(The flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{3}
)
.

By considering the Borel presentation

R\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}\ell_{3},{\mathbb{Z}})\;=\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]}{\left(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3},\,x_{1}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{3}+x_{2}x_{3},\,x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\right)}

of the flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{3}
, we obtain the Macaulay dual generator

G_{R}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\;=\;-y_{1}^{2}y_{2}+y_{1}^{2}y_{3}+y_{1}y_{2}^{2}-y_{1}y_{3}^{2}-y_{2}^{2}y_{3}+y_{2}y_{3}^{2}.

We know that the elements
\widehat{x}_{1}=x_{1}
,
\widehat{x}_{2}=x_{1}+x_{2}
and
\widehat{x}_{3}=x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}
correspond to nef line bundles on
\mathcal{F}\ell_{3}
(see, e.g.,
[
Ful97
, Chapter 10]
).
By considering the following alternative presentation

R\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}\ell_{3},{\mathbb{Z}})\;=\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[\widehat{x}_{1},\widehat{x}_{2},\widehat{x}_{3}]}{\left(\widehat{x}_{3},\,\;-\widehat{x}_{1}^{2}+\widehat{x}_{1}\widehat{x}_{2}-\widehat{x}_{2}^{2}+\widehat{x}_{2}\widehat{x}_{3},\,\;\widehat{x}_{1}^{2}\widehat{x}_{2}-\widehat{x}_{1}\widehat{x}_{2}^{2}-\widehat{x}_{1}^{2}\widehat{x}_{3}+\widehat{x}_{1}\widehat{x}_{2}\widehat{x}_{3}\right)},

we obtain the following Macaulay dual generator

G_{R}(\widehat{y}_{1},\widehat{y}_{2},\widehat{y}_{3})\;=\;\widehat{y}_{1}^{2}\widehat{y}_{2}+\widehat{y}_{1}\widehat{y}_{2}^{2}

whose normalization is a Lorentzian polynomial (as predicted by
Theorem 6.13
(iv)).

Example 6.15

(The Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
)
.

The cohomology ring of the Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
is given by

R\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}({\rm Gr}(2,4),{\mathbb{Z}})\;=\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},x_{2}]}{\left(x_{1}^{3}-2x_{1}x_{2},\,\,x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-x_{2}^{2}\right)},

where
x_{i}
is the
i
-th Chern class
c_{i}(\mathcal{S})
of the universal subbundle
\mathcal{S}
on
{\rm Gr}(2,4)
.
Then the Macaulay dual generator is given by

G_{R}(y_{1},y_{2})\;=\;2y_{1}^{4}+y_{1}^{2}y_{2}+y_{2}^{2}.

Finally, we provide a characteristic-free (over
{\mathbb{Z}}
) extension of the celebrated result of Khovanskii and Pukhlikov
[
KP92
]
showing that the cohomology ring (over
\mathbb{Q}
) of certain toric varieties can be expressed in terms of differential operators that annihilate the volume polynomial.
We follow the notations in
[
CLS11
]
.

We briefly recall the notion of mixed volume (for more details, the reader is referred to
[
Ewa96
, Chapter IV]
,
[
CLO05
, Chapter 7]
).
Let
P_{1},\dots,P_{n}
be lattice polytopes in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
and
{\rm Vol}(P_{i})
denote the Euclidean volume of
P_{i}
, where the unit hypercube has volume
1
.
The volume of the Minkowski sum of polytopes
{\rm Vol}(y_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}P_{n})
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
, and we write

{\rm Vol}(y_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}P_{n})\;=\;\sum_{|\alpha|=d}\,\frac{d!}{\alpha!}\;\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\dots,P_{n})\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}

where
\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\dots,P_{n})
is called the
mixed volume
of
(P_{1},\dots,P_{n})
of type
\alpha
.

Let
X_{\Sigma}
be a smooth complete toric variety and write
d=\dim(X_{\Sigma})
,
n=|\Sigma(1)|
, and
m={\rm rank}\left({\text{Pic}}(X_{\Sigma})\right)
.
Let
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{n}
be the one-dimensional rays in
\Sigma(1)
.
Let
D_{i}
be the divisor on
X_{\Sigma}
associated to
\rho_{i}
.
We have the following isomorphism

{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J})\;\;\xrightarrow{\;\cong\;}\;\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad x_{i}\mapsto D_{i},

where
\mathcal{I}=\left(x_{i_{1}},\dots,x_{i_{s}}\mid i_{j}\text{ are distinct and }\rho_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\rho_{i_{s}}\text{ is not a cone of }\Sigma\right)
and
\mathcal{J}
is the ideal generated by linear forms
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle m,u_{i}\rangle x_{i}
for all
m\in M
in the dual lattice
M
.
Moreover, the cohomology ring
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{Z}})
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-torsion-free.
This explicit description of
\text{H}^{\bullet}(X_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{Z}})
was obtained by Jurkiewicz
[
Jur80
]
and Danilov
[
Dan78
]
.
The
volume polynomial
of
X_{\Sigma}
is defined as

V(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\;:=\;\int_{X_{\Sigma}}\left(D_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+D_{n}y_{n}\right)^{d}.

Assume that the classes of the divisors
\overline{D}_{1},\dots,\overline{D}_{m}
on
X_{\Sigma}
give a basis of
{\text{Pic}}(X_{\Sigma})
.
Then the
reduced volume polynomial
of
X_{\Sigma}
is given by

\overline{V}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\;:=\;\int_{X_{\Sigma}}\left(\overline{D}_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+\overline{D}_{m}y_{m}\right)^{d}.

Below we have our extension of the aforementioned result of Khovanskii and Pukhlikov
[
KP92
]
.

Corollary 6.16
.

Let
R=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{d}\text{H}^{2i}\left(X_{\Sigma},{\mathbb{Z}}\right)
.
Under the Macaulay inverse system notation of
Theorem 6.13
, the following statements hold:

(i)

There are isomorphisms of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-algebras

R\;\cong\;{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/I\;\cong\;{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]/J

where

I\;=\;\left\{g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,\mid\,g\cdot N^{-1}\left(V(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\right)=0\right\}\;=\;\mathcal{I}+\mathcal{J}

and

J\;=\;\left\{g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]\,\mid\,g\cdot N^{-1}\left(\overline{V}(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\right)=0\right\}.

Here
N^{-1}
denotes the inverse of the normalization operator.

(ii)

If each
D_{i}
is nef and
P_{i}=P_{D_{i}}
is the polytope associated to
D_{i}
, then

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=d}\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

Proof.

(i)
We concentrate on the isomorphism
R\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/I
.
From
Theorem 6.13
(iii), we have
I=\{g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\mid g\cdot G_{R}=0\}
and that the Macaulay dual generator is given by

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=d}\left(\int_{X_{\Sigma}}D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\right)\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}.

Then the equality
I\;=\;\left\{g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,\mid\,g\cdot N^{-1}\left(V(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\right)=0\right\}
follows by noticing that

{\rm Vol}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;d!\,N\left(G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\right).

The isomorphism
R\cong{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]/J
follows verbatim.

(ii)
From
[
CLS11
, Theorem 13.4.3]
, we obtain the equality

V(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;{\rm Vol}(y_{1}P_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}P_{n}).

By comparing coefficients we get
\int_{X_{\Sigma}}D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}=\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})
.
Therefore, the equality

G_{R}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\;=\;\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=d}\mathrm{MV}_{\alpha}(P_{1},\ldots,P_{n})\,y_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots y_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}

follows from
Theorem 6.13
(iii).
∎

Example 6.17

(The Hirzebruch surface
\mathcal{H}_{r}
)
.

Consider the Hirzebruch surface
\mathcal{H}_{r}
and label the one dimensional rays in the fan as
\rho_{1}={\text{Cone}}(-{\mathbf{e}}_{1}+r{\mathbf{e}}_{2})
,
\rho_{2}={\text{Cone}}({\mathbf{e}}_{2})
,
\rho_{3}={\text{Cone}}({\mathbf{e}}_{1})
, and
\rho_{4}={\text{Cone}}(-{\mathbf{e}}_{2})

The cohomology ring of
\mathcal{H}_{r}
is given by

R\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{H}_{r},{\mathbb{Z}})\;\cong\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]}{(x_{1}x_{3},\,x_{2}x_{4},\,-x_{1}+x_{3},\,rx_{1}+x_{2}-x_{4})}.

The Macaulay dual generator is given by

G_{R}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})\;=\;-ry_{2}^{2}+ry_{4}^{2}+y_{1}y_{2}+y_{2}y_{3}+y_{3}y_{4}+y_{1}y_{4}

and the volume polynomial is given by

V(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})\;=\;-ry_{2}^{2}+ry_{4}^{2}+2y_{1}y_{2}+2y_{2}y_{3}+2y_{3}y_{4}+2y_{1}y_{4}\;=\;2!N(G_{R}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4})).

Alternatively, the cohomology ring of
\mathcal{H}_{r}
can be represented as

R\;=\;\text{H}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{H}_{r},{\mathbb{Z}})\;\cong\;\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}[x_{3},x_{4}]}{(x_{3}^{2},-rx_{3}x_{4}+x_{4}^{2})}.

The Macaulay dual generator is given by

G_{R}(y_{3},y_{4})\;=\;ry_{4}^{2}+y_{3}y_{4}

and the reduced volume polynomial is given by

\overline{V}(y_{3},y_{4})\;=\;ry_{4}^{2}+2y_{3}y_{4}\;=\;2!N(G_{R}(y_{3},y_{4})).

Since
D_{\rho_{3}}
and
D_{\rho_{4}}
are both nef divisors in
\mathcal{H}_{r}

[
CLS11
, Example 6.3.23]
, we have that
\overline{V}(y_{3},y_{4})
is a Lorentzian polynomial (as predicted by
Theorem 6.13
(iv)).

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB), caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis (M. tuberculosis), remains a major global health challenge, with approximately 10 million new cases and 1.5 million deaths reported annually. The emergence of multidrug-resistant (MDR-TB) and extensively drug-resistant (XDR-TB) strains has further complicated disease management and emphasizes the continuing need for new anti-tubercular agents targeting essential mycobacterial pathways and proteins.1, 2, 3, 4 The present study focuses on the preliminary in silico evaluation of coumarin–Schiff base derivatives against selected M. tuberculosis therapeutic targets and does not investigate their activity against drug-resistant strains or resistance-associated mutations.

The cell wall of M. tuberculosis is essential for bacterial survival and contributes to its ability to withstand environmental stress and maintain cellular integrity. Several proteins involved in mycobacterial cell wall biosynthesis and maintenance have consequently attracted considerable interest as potential therapeutic targets. In the present study, five cell wall-associated proteins—decaprenylphosphoryl-β-d-ribose 2′-epimerase (DprE1), enoyl-acyl carrier protein reductase (InhA), β-ketoacyl-acyl carrier protein synthase (KasA), mycobacterial membrane protein large 3 (MmpL3), and L,D-transpeptidase (LdtMt2)—were selected because they participate in distinct but complementary processes associated with cell wall biogenesis.5, 6, 7 DprE1 participates in arabinan biosynthesis, whereas InhA and KasA are involved in mycolic acid biosynthesis. MmpL3 is associated with mycolic acid transport, while LdtMt2 contributes to peptidoglycan cross-linking and cell wall integrity. The involvement of these proteins in essential aspects of mycobacterial cell wall formation makes them relevant targets for anti-tubercular drug discovery.5, 6 Evaluation against this panel of targets provides a broader computational assessment of ligand–protein interactions across multiple components of mycobacterial cell wall-associated pathways rather than restricting the investigation to a single molecular target.

Schiff base derivatives have attracted considerable interest in medicinal chemistry because of their structural diversity and broad range of reported biological activities.8, 9, 10 Their structural versatility permits modification with different substituents, providing opportunities to influence physicochemical characteristics and molecular interactions with biological targets.9, 11 Schiff base derivatives and their complexes have also been investigated for antimicrobial and anti-tubercular activities.10, 12 However, systematic computational evaluation of coumarin-based Schiff base derivatives against multiple M. tuberculosis cell wall-associated proteins remains limited.

Molecular docking and in silico ADMET prediction provide computational approaches for the preliminary assessment and comparison of potential lead compounds. Accordingly, the present study investigated novel aldehyde-derived coumarin–Schiff base derivatives against five M. tuberculosis cell wall-associated proteins (PDB IDs: 4P8T, 2NSD, 2WGD, 7NVH, and 5DC2). Molecular docking, protein–ligand interaction analysis, drug-likeness assessment, and in silico ADMET profiling were performed to compare the designed compounds, examine substituent-dependent interaction patterns, and identify promising computational candidates. The findings are intended to provide preliminary insights into the predicted binding characteristics and structure–activity relationships of the designed compounds. As the study is restricted to computational predictions, the results should be interpreted within the scope of the in silico methodology and should not be considered experimental evidence of biological efficacy, pharmacokinetic performance, or binding stability.

The present study aims to perform molecular docking investigations of novel Schiff base derivatives against selected M. tuberculosis cell-wall proteins to predict their binding affinities, interaction profiles, and inhibitory potential. The findings from this computational approach are expected to provide valuable insights into the structure–activity relationships (SAR) of Schiff base ligands and contribute to the rational design of new antitubercular agents capable of preliminary lead candidiates.

Methods

Materials used for the in silico study

In the present in silico study, a combination of computational tools was employed for molecular docking and visualization analyses. PyRx (Python Prescription) version 0.8, incorporating the AutoDock Vina engine, was used for ligand preparation, optimization, and molecular docking simulations. Protein structures were prepared using Discovery Studio by removing water molecules, adding hydrogen atoms, and performing energy minimization prior to docking. The resulting protein–ligand complexes were subsequently visualized in three dimensions (3D) using Discovery Studio, while Discovery Studio Visualizer was employed to generate two-dimensional (2D) interaction diagrams and to examine the molecular interactions between the ligands and the target proteins.

Selection and optimization of ligands

A total of thirty (30) Schiff base (Fig. 1) derivatives (Fig. 3) were designed using ChemDraw software. The chemical structures were initially generated in two-dimensional (.cdx) format and subsequently converted into three-dimensional (.pdb) structures for computational analysis. Ligand preparation and energy minimization were performed using Open Babel integrated within PyRx (Python Prescription) version 0.813 The optimized ligand structures were then converted into PDBQT format, with appropriate atom types and partial charges assigned during the preparation process, to enable molecular docking using the AutoDock Vina engine. The resulting energy-minimized ligand conformations were used for all subsequent docking analyses.

Drug likeness screening of ligands

The drug-likeness of the designed ligands was evaluated according to Lipinski's rule of five, considering molecular weight (≤500 Da), hydrogen bond donors (≤5), hydrogen bond acceptors (≤10), and lipophilicity (LogP ≤5). The physicochemical properties and drug-likeness parameters were predicted using the SwissADME14 and admetSAR web servers.15 In addition, the absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and toxicity (ADMET) profiles, along with toxicity predictions, were assessed using the admetSAR web-based platform16 For all analyses, the SMILES representations of the synthesized compounds were submitted to the respective web servers, and the predicted properties were recorded for comparative evaluation. These computational predictions were used solely for preliminary assessment of the pharmacokinetic and toxicity profiles and do not substitute for experimental validation.

Protein targets selection and preparation

Five essential Mycobacterium tuberculosis cell wall-associated proteins—Decaprenylphosphoryl-β-d-ribose 2′-epimerase (DprE1), enoyl-acyl carrier protein reductase (InhA), β-ketoacyl-ACP synthase (KasA), mycolic acid transporter MmpL3, and L,D-transpeptidase (LdtMt2)— were selected as molecular targets because of their critical roles in cell wall biosynthesis and maintenance.5, 6, 7 The three-dimensional crystal structures of these proteins were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (PDB) with PDB IDs 4P8T, 2NSD, 2WGD, 7NVH, and 5DC2, respectively (Fig. 2).17, 18 Protein preparation was carried out using Discovery Studio by removing crystallographic water molecules, co-crystallized ligands, and other heteroatoms, followed by the addition of hydrogen atoms to obtain the receptor structures suitable for docking. The prepared proteins (Fig. 4) were then converted to PDBQT format with the assignment of appropriate atom types and partial charges required for molecular docking using AutoDock Vina. The processed receptor structures were subsequently used for all docking simulations.

In silico docking protocol validation

The molecular docking protocol was validated through a re-docking procedure to assess its ability to reproduce the experimentally observed binding orientation of the co-crystallized ligands. The native ligands were extracted from the crystal structures of the selected target proteins and prepared for docking using Discovery Studio. Each ligand was subsequently re-docked into the corresponding binding site using the AutoDock Vina engine implemented in PyRx (version 0.8) under the same docking parameters used for the test compounds13, 19 The resulting docked poses were superimposed with the corresponding crystallographic ligand conformations, and root mean square deviation (RMSD) values were calculated to assess the agreement between the predicted and experimentally observed binding orientations. The RMSD values obtained for the respective target proteins are presented in Table 1. Protein–ligand interactions in the re-docked complexes were additionally analyzed using Discovery Studio Visualizer 2016 to compare the predicted interaction patterns with those observed in the crystallographic structures.

Molecular docking

Docking analysis of the selected Schiff base ligands with the target M. tuberculosis proteins was carried out using a flexible docking protocol. PyRx version 0.8,13 incorporating the AutoDock Vina docking engine19 was employed for all docking analyses. For each target protein, the docking search space was defined based on the binding pocket predicted by the docking software. The same binding region was used consistently for all ligands docked against a given protein to enable comparative evaluation of their predicted binding affinities and interaction profiles.

The prepared and energy-minimized protein structures were converted to PDBQT format by assigning appropriate partial charges and atom types, while the optimized ligands were similarly converted to PDBQT format. The protein receptor was treated as rigid, whereas rotatable bonds in the ligands were allowed to remain flexible. For each protein–ligand complex, ten binding poses were generated, and the corresponding docking scores were recorded. Docking calculations were performed using an exhaustiveness value of 10 and the number of modes set to 10. The predicted pose with the most favorable docking score was selected for subsequent interaction analysis using Discovery Studio Visualizer 2016. The resulting interactions were interpreted as computationally predicted binding interactions and not as evidence of experimentally confirmed binding stability.

Ligand optimization

Ligand preparation and energy minimization were carried out prior to molecular docking to obtain stable conformations for all designed compounds. The optimization was performed using Open Babel integrated within PyRx (version 0.8), and the resulting minimized structures were used for subsequent docking studies. The final minimized energy values obtained for the representative compounds SB5, SB13, and SB21 were 346.50, 167.38, and 179.26, respectively. These values are provided to document the ligand optimization process performed before docking.

ADME prediction

The absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion and toxicity (ADMET) properties of the top scoring compounds identified from the molecular docking studies were predicted using the SWISS ADME web server. The smiles representations of the compounds, obtained from PubChem, were submitted to the server to estimate their physicochemical properties, pharmacokinetic parameters, and drug-likeness characteristics. These computational predictions were used as a preliminary assessment of the pharmacokinetic profiles of the selected compounds and should be interpreted as supportive in silico data pending experimental validation.

The enhanced activity of the nitro-substituted derivative has been attributed to its strong electron-withdrawing nature and hydrogen-bond accepting ability, which may facilitate favorable interactions with active-site residues. Similarly, the fluoro substituent contributes to hydrophobic and electrostatic interactions while influencing the electronic distribution of the aromatic ring. The methoxy substituent, being an electron-donating group, may enhance binding through hydrogen-bond acceptor capability and additional hydrophobic contacts.

Among the synthesized derivatives, compounds containing nitro (–NO₂), fluoro (—F), and methoxy (–OCH₃) substituents exhibited comparatively favorable docking scores and interaction patterns across the selected Mycobacterium tuberculosis protein targets. Based on the docking results, the nitro-substituted derivative generally showed the strongest predicted binding affinity, followed by the fluoro- and methoxy-substituted derivatives. These observations suggest a possible influence of the nature of the substituent on the predicted binding behavior of the coumarin–Schiff base scaffold. However, the present findings are based solely on molecular docking and do not establish the mechanistic contribution of individual substituents to protein binding or biological activity. Therefore, the proposed structure–activity relationship should be regarded as preliminary and requires further validation through molecular dynamics simulations and experimental biological studies.

The prioritization of the designed coumarin–Schiff base derivatives was based not only on their molecular docking scores but also on their binding interactions with the target proteins, substituent effects, and predicted pharmacokinetic properties. The nature of the substituents on the aromatic ring appeared to influence the binding orientation and interaction profiles of the ligands within the active sites of the selected Mycobacterium tuberculosis proteins. SB5, containing three methoxy substituents, exhibited favorable binding interactions that may be attributed to the electron-donating nature of the methoxy groups, which can enhance hydrophobic contacts and contribute to ligand stabilization within the binding pocket. SB13, bearing a fluorine substituent, demonstrated effective binding through favorable hydrophobic interactions, while the fluorine atom may also contribute to improved molecular recognition because of its electronic characteristics. SB21, containing a nitro substituent, consistently exhibited strong binding interactions with several target proteins, which may be associated with the electron-withdrawing nature of the nitro group that facilitates favorable hydrogen-bonding and polar interactions with key active-site residues. Together with their interaction profiles and predicted ADMET properties, these substituent-dependent effects were considered during compound prioritization rather than relying solely on docking scores.

Schiff base coumarins and their derivatives are a class of phytocompounds known for their diverse pharmacological activities and potential in managing various diseases. In this in silico study, the designed compounds were carefully selected and docked against key proteins critical to the survival of Mycobacterium tuberculosis. Computational approaches such as molecular docking have proven valuable in the identification and development of novel small molecules in drug discovery.

The designed compounds, along with the standard drug, were evaluated for their oral druggability using Lipinski's rule of five, which stipulates that, for a molecule to be orally active, its molecular weight should not exceed 500 Da, lipophilicity (logP) should be below 5, the number of hydrogen bond donors should be ≤5, and hydrogen bond acceptors should be ≤10. Compounds that exceed these thresholds may exhibit poor absorption, metabolism, excretion, and increased toxicity, reducing their potential as effective drugs. Additionally, in silico physicochemical and toxicity assessments were performed to identify compounds with potential toxic effects, while drug-likeness was evaluated based on Lipinski's criteria.

Conclusion

Tuberculosis remains a major global health concern, particularly due to the increasing burden of multidrug-resistant strains. In the present study, molecular docking analysis was performed to evaluate newly designed aldehyde-derived coumarin Schiff base derivatives against five tuberculosis-associated protein targets (4P8T, 2NSD, 2WGD, 7NVH, and 5 DC2) implicated in bacterial survival and proliferation. The in silico analysis identified several compounds with favorable predicted binding interactions and pharmacokinetic properties. Among these, SB5, SB13, and SB21 exhibited comparatively favorable docking scores and predicted ADMET profiles, suggesting that they may be suitable candidates for further computational and experimental investigation. However, these findings are based solely on molecular docking and in silico predictions and should be considered preliminary until validated through molecular dynamics simulations and biological studies.

Among the evaluated compounds, SB21 demonstrated comparatively favorable binding energies across the selected protein targets, including notable affinity toward 2NSD. However, lead prioritization was based on an integrated assessment of docking performance, interaction profiles, and ADMET characteristics rather than binding affinity alone. Although SB5, SB13, and SB21 complied with Lipinski's Rule of Five, predicted variations in lipophilicity, gastrointestinal absorption, and CYP450 interactions suggest possible pharmacokinetic trade-offs and metabolic liabilities that require further investigation. The identified compounds demonstrated favorable binding affinities together with acceptable predicted ADMET profiles. However, the computational toxicity assessment indicated potential liabilities, including hepatotoxicity, CYP inhibition, hERG liability, and drug-induced liver injury (DILI) risk for some compounds. Accordingly, the present findings should be interpreted as preliminary computational predictions that provide a basis for comparative assessment of the designed compounds. The proposed lead candidates were prioritized based on their overall docking performance, interaction profiles, and predicted pharmacokinetic properties, while recognizing that the computational results alone are insufficient to establish their safety or therapeutic efficacy.

The molecular docking results demonstrated that coumarin-based Schiff base derivatives showed favorable binding interactions with key Mycobacterium tuberculosis targets, including DprE1, InhA, KasA, MmpL3, and LdtMt2, suggesting their potential anti-tubercular activity. Among all compounds, SB21 consistently exhibited the highest binding affinity (−8.1 to −10.5 kcal/mol), indicating greater predicted stability of the protein–ligand complexes which reflect the preliminary nature of docking based prediction.

SB21 occupied the same binding cavity as the native ligands in all targets and formed interactions with important active-site amino acid residues through hydrogen bonding, hydrophobic interactions, and van der Waals forces, which may contribute to effective target binding. Since these proteins are involved in cell wall biosynthesis, fatty acid synthesis, and transport processes, their inhibition may interfere with bacterial survival.

Overall, the favorable docking scores and interaction patterns observed for SB21 indicate that the coumarin–Schiff base scaffold warrants further investigation as a potential multi-target anti-tubercular candidate. However, these findings are based solely on molecular docking and should be considered preliminary. The ranking of SB21 is predictive rather than conclusive, as docking alone cannot reliably establish binding stability or biological efficacy. Therefore, molecular dynamics simulations, binding free energy analyses, and subsequent in vitro and in vivo experimental validation are essential to confirm its binding stability, pharmacological potential, and therapeutic relevance.

The present in silico investigation identified several coumarin–Schiff base derivatives with favorable predicted binding affinities, interaction profiles, and acceptable ADMET characteristics against selected Mycobacterium tuberculosis cell wall-associated targets. However, these findings are based exclusively on computational analyses performed using wild-type protein structures and should therefore be interpreted as preliminary predictions. The computational results provide a comparative assessment of the designed compounds and support their prioritization for further investigation but are not sufficient to establish their biological activity, safety, or therapeutic efficacy.

The present study provides a computational assessment of the designed coumarin–Schiff base derivatives against selected Mycobacterium tuberculosis targets. Although several compounds demonstrated favorable predicted binding interactions and pharmacokinetic profiles, these findings are derived solely from in silico analyses and should therefore be interpreted with appropriate caution. The conclusions of this study are limited to the identification and prioritization of computational lead candidates and do not constitute experimental evidence of efficacy or safety.

Although several compounds demonstrated favorable docking interactions with the selected protein targets, the absence of molecular dynamics simulations and experimental validation represents a limitation of the present study. Consequently, the reported binding modes and pharmacokinetic predictions should be considered computational hypotheses rather than definitive evidence of target inhibition. The conclusions of this study are therefore limited to identifying promising computational leads based on docking performance, interaction analysis, and predicted physicochemical and ADMET properties.

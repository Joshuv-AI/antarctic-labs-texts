Generalized conformal quantum mechanics as an ideal observer in two-dimensional gravity
Archi Banerjee, Tanay Kibe, Martín Molina, and Ayan Mukhopadhyay

Abstract
We obtain an action for a generalized conformal mechanics (GCM) coupled to Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity from a double scaling limit of the motion of a charged massive particle in the near-horizon geometry of a near-extremal spherical black hole. When JT gravity is treated in the classical approximation, the backreaction of the particle’s wavefunction on the time-reparametrization mode (and therefore the bulk metric) vanishes while the conformal symmetry in GCM is reparametrized in a state-dependent way. We also construct the semi-classical Hilbert space of the full theory by explicitly solving the general time-dependent normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation for GCM, and show that the time-reparametrization mode can be inferred from the measurement of suitable observables. Since the full theory of the GCM coupled to JT gravity is amenable to quantization, it can lead to a solvable model for a detector coupled to quantum gravity.

2.2

2.3
A nice form for the Hamiltonian of generalized conformal mechanics

The Hamiltonian of the generalized conformal mechanics can be expressed in a nice form. To see this, we first consider the Schwarzian theory

S=\int{\rm d}u\,{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)

(54)

which is invariant under the fractional linear transformations (
12
) of
f(u)
which give a representation of the SL(2,R) group. The conserved charges of this action are

\displaystyle Q_{0}(u)=\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)^{2}}{f^{\prime}(u)^{3}},

\displaystyle Q_{1}(u)=-2f(u)\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)^{2}}{f^{\prime}(u)^{3}}\right)+2\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)},

\displaystyle Q_{2}(u)=f(u)^{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)^{2}}{f^{\prime}(u)^{3}}\right)-2f(u)\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)}{f^{\prime}(u)}-\frac{f^{\prime}(u)}{f(u)}\right).

(55)

with
Q_{i}
obtained from Noether’s procedure considering the infinitesimal versions of the the fractional linear transformations (
12
):

f(u)\rightarrow f(u)+\epsilon v_{i},\quad v_{i}=\{1,-2f(u),f(u)^{2}\},

(56)

respectively. These Noether charges satisfy the Ward identities:

\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}Q_{i}=\frac{v_{i}}{f^{\prime}(u)}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u).

(57)

Under SL(2,R) transformations (
12
), the vector

\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{0},Q_{1},Q_{2}\}

transforms exactly like the conserved charge vector

\mathcal{G}=\{H_{f},D_{f},K_{f}\}

of generalized conformal mechanics under the transformations given by (
31
) and (
37
) (i.e. in the adjoint representation of SL(2,R)). If
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are two such adjoint vectors, then we can form a SL(2,R) invariant product

\mathcal{A}\bullet\mathcal{B}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{A}_{0}\mathcal{B}_{2}+\mathcal{A}_{2}\mathcal{B}_{0}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{A}_{1}\mathcal{B}_{1}.

(58)

We readily find that

\mathcal{G}\bullet\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{C},\quad\mathcal{Q}\bullet\mathcal{Q}=Q_{0}Q_{2}-\frac{1}{4}Q_{1}^{2}=2{\rm Sch}(f(u),u).

(59)

Above
\mathcal{C}
is the Casimir defined in (
53
). Note also
v\bullet v=0
.

After some algebra, it is possible to verify using (
46
), (
48
) and (
50
) that the Hamiltonian of the generalized conformal mechanics (
27
) (without the total derivative term proportional to identity) takes the remarkably simple form

\mathbb{H}=\mathcal{Q}\bullet\mathcal{G}.

(60)

It is to be noted that
\mathbb{H}
is not SL(2,R) invariant under the transformations of
\mathcal{G}
under (
31
) and (
37
) only. It is necessary also to transform
\mathcal{Q}
under (
12
) simultaneously for the SL(2,R) invariance of the dot product. As should be clear from the discussion in Sec.
2.1
, the latter transformations realize the isometries of the two dimensional gravitational metric (
9
). Therefore, (
60
) should be regarded as an invariant of the full theory combining the particle and JT gravity, which will be discussed in detail in the following subsection.
7
7

7

As shown in the previous subsection, the GCM action is separately invariant under the transformations given by (
31
) and (
37
), and transformations given by (
12
) where
f(u)
alone transforms. Furthermore, the Noether charges under these separate SL(2,R) transformations are equal in magnitude while opposite in sign on-shell. So if both the transformations are done simultaneously, as necessary for the SL(2,R) invariance of the Hamiltonian, then the total Noether charges vanish on-shell.

Another identity, which can also be verified using (
46
), (
48
) and (
50
), is

x^{2}=\frac{4}{f^{\prime}(u)}v\bullet\mathcal{G}

(61)

where

is a vector defined in (

\mathcal{G}
is conserved and that its SL(2,R) norm is the Casimir given by (

We can also check that

\frac{{\rm d}\mathbb{H}}{{\rm d}u}=\frac{{\rm d}\mathcal{Q}}{{\rm d}u}\bullet\mathcal{G}=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(u)}\left(v\bullet\mathcal{G}\right)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)=\frac{1}{4}x^{2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u).

(62)

Above we have used that
\mathcal{G}

2.4
The full theory

The action for the full theory of the particle coupled to two-dimensional gravity is simply

S_{\rm T}=S_{{\rm GCM}}[x(u),f(u)]+S_{{\rm JT-gen}}^{\rm on-shell}[f(u),J(u)]

(63)

where
S_{{\rm GCM}}
is the action for generalized conformal mechanics of the particle which depends on
x(u)
and the time reparametrization mode
f(u)
of the gravitational sector, and
S_{{\rm JT-gen}}^{\rm on-shell}
is the regularized action for two-dimensional JT gravitational theory coupled to arbitrary bulk matter where all bulk fields have been expressed in terms of the time reparametrization mode
f(u)
and
J(u)
, which denotes the sources of bulk matter fields collectively. As shown below,
S_{{\rm JT-gen}}^{\rm on-shell}
is a boundary action. Note that
f(u)
appearing in
S_{{\rm GCM}}
should be identified with the time reparametrization mode of JT gravity, and this is obvious from the derivation of
S_{{\rm GCM}}
presented in Sec.
2.1
. There is, however, an elegant reformulation of the action using auxiliary fields, where
f(u)
appearing in
S_{{\rm GCM}}
and the time reparametrization mode in
S_{{\rm JT-gen}}^{\rm on-shell}
get identified with each other automatically. This reformulation has been presented in Appendix
A
.

It is useful to also have a holographic perspective for the full action and rewrite it in the form

S_{\rm T}=S_{{\rm GCM}}+W_{\rm QT}\left[\phi_{r}(u)=\phi_{0}\right]

(64)

Here,
W_{\rm QT}
is the logarithm of the partition function (quantum effective action) of a putative large N quantum dot theory dual to a two dimensional gravity theory, where
N^{2}\sim G_{N}^{-1}
. Above,
\phi_{0}
is an arbitrary constant and
\phi_{r}(u)
, as explained below, is the source of the operator dual to the bulk dilaton. In the large N limit,

W_{\rm QT}=S_{\rm JT-gen}^{\rm on-shell}

(65)

with
S_{\rm JT-gen}^{\rm on-shell}
denoting the on-shell gravitational action of JT gravity coupled to matter, where the metric and other bulk fields are determined in terms of the time reparametrization mode via bulk equations of motion. As shown below, this action reproduces the constraint of the gravitational theory amounting to the equation of motion of the time-reparametrization mode (and thus, as expected, encapsulates the full gravitational theory). Generally,
S_{\rm JT-gen}
is given by

\displaystyle S_{\rm JT-gen}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}u\,\sqrt{-g}\,\phi\left(R+\frac{2}{L^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{8\pi G_{N}}\int_{\partial{\mathcal{M}}}{\rm d}u\,\sqrt{-\gamma}\phi_{b}K

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}{\rm d}z{\rm d}u\,\sqrt{-g}\mathcal{L}_{\rm matter}[g,\chi]+S_{\rm c.t.}.

(66)

Above,
\phi_{b}
is the boundary value of the dilaton, and
\mathcal{L}_{\rm matter}
stands for the Lagrangian density of generic bulk matter
\chi
(including the bulk gauge field) which couples only to the background metric but not to the dilaton.

Furthermore, the on-shell gravitational action is computed by imposing the following boundary conditions

\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}z\,\phi(z,u)=\phi_{r}(u),\quad\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}z^{2}g_{uu}(z,u)=-1

, the non-normalizable mode of the dilaton, to the value
\phi_{0}
as indicated in (
64

(78)

\displaystyle\phi_{r}^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)+2\phi_{r}^{\prime}(u){\rm Sch}(f(u),u)+\phi_{r}(u)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\left(T_{zu}(z,u)-z\partial_{u}T_{zz}(z,u)\right),

(79)

where
T_{\mu\nu}
is the energy-momentum tensor of the bulk matter. The final constraint (
79
) can be obtained from the extremization of the renormalized on-shell gravitational action (obtained by imposing (
68

77
), (
78
) and the matter equations of motion) with respect to

. Explicitly, the full action is

S_{\rm T}=S_{{\rm GCM}}+\frac{\phi_{0}}{8\pi G_{N}}\int{\rm d}u\,{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)+\,\,{\rm bulk\,\,matter\,\,contributions}.

(80)

The equation of motion of
f(u)
in this case is

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\left(T_{zu}(z,u)-z\partial_{u}T_{zz}(z,u)\right).

(81)

Therefore, even in the presence of bulk matter fields, the backreaction of
x(u)
on the time-reparametrization mode
f(u)
(and thus the bulk metric), the dilaton and also bulk matter fields vanishes. The time reparametrization mode

is determined only by the bulk matter fields.

We also note that the Hamiltonian of the full theory is simply

H_{\rm T}=\mathbb{H}+H_{\rm grav},

(82)

where
\mathbb{H}
is the Hamiltonian of the generalized conformal mechanics which takes the form (
60
) and

is the Hamiltonian of the gravitational theory. This split property of the Hamiltonian leads to the interpretation that the generalized conformal mechanics is a hair like degree of freedom
[
26

.

It is not hard to generalize to the case where we couple the quantized generalized quantum conformal mechanics to classical JT gravity. Following
[
26
]
, we should replace the

given by

S_{\rm GCQM}=\int{\rm d}u\,\bra{\psi}\left(i\partial_{u}-\hat{\mathbb{H}}\right)\ket{\psi},\quad\hat{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{p}^{2}+\frac{q}{2\hat{x}^{2}}+\frac{1}{4}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)\hat{x}^{2}.

S_{\rm T}=S_{{\rm GCQM}}+\frac{\phi_{0}}{8\pi G_{N}}\int{\rm d}u\,{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)+\,\,{\rm bulk\,\,matter\,\,contributions}.

(85)

The equation of motion for
f(u)

\displaystyle 2\pi G_{N}\frac{{\rm d}^{3}}{{\rm d}u^{3}}\langle\hat{x}^{2}(u)\rangle+4\pi G_{N}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}\langle\hat{x}^{2}(u)\rangle{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)+\left(2\pi G_{N}\langle\hat{x}^{2}(u)\rangle+\phi_{0}\right)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}u}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)

\displaystyle=\lim_{z\rightarrow 0}\left(T_{zu}(z,u)-z\partial_{u}T_{zz}(z,u)\right).

In the following section, we will obtain the general normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation satisfying appropriate boundary conditions. We will show that, although

\langle\hat{x}^{2}(u)\rangle=\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}f(u)+a_{3}f(u)^{2}}{f^{\prime}(u)},

(87)

in an arbitrary state satisfying the Schrödinger equation with

,
a_{2}
and

being (state dependent) constants. Recall that for a solution of the classical equation of motion we require that

as should be evident from (
43

,
a_{2}
and

are independent.

As should be clear from the results of Sec.
2.1
(recall (

)), the constraints for the dilaton (and thus the equation of motion for

) are automatically satisfied in pure JT gravity whenever

assumes the

) for arbitrary values of

,
a_{2}
and

. It can also be checked by substituting (

In the following section, we will explicitly study the normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation and discuss how the time-reparametrization mode of the gravitational theory can be detected via suitable measurements.

We conclude this section with two remarks. Firstly, the backreaction of the particle on the gravitational sector does not vanish away from the large

3
Quantization of generalized conformal mechanics and the full semiclassical theory

3.1
The normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation

Our first aim is to obtain the normalizable solutions of the Schödinger equation (
84
) for generalized conformal mechanics. This cannot be approached by solving the spectrum of the Hamiltonian as the Hamiltonian does not have normalizable eigenstates (including of course ground state) when
{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)\leq 0
. The case
{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)=0
is identical to usual quantum conformal mechanics analyzed in de Alfaro, Fubini and Furlan
[
17
]
. Since, the mass of the black hole in the JT gravity is
M_{BH}\sim-2\,{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)>0
, the Hamiltonian of the quantized generalized conformal mechanics has no normalizable eigenstate whenever the time reparametrization mode corresponds to a dynamical black hole solution of JT gravity. In what follows, we will assume that
{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)<0
and
f^{\prime}(u)>0
for
u\in(-\infty,\infty)
.

It is easy to see this in the case of the usual conformal mechanics (with
f(u)=u
), for which the Hamiltonian is
H_{0}
(given by (
46
)).
The commutation relation (
47
) implies that if
\ket{E}
is any finite energy eigenstate with energy
E
, then
e^{i\alpha D_{0}}
is also an energy eigenstate with energy
e^{2\alpha}E

[
19
]
. Thus the spectrum is continuous and the eigenstates are non-normalizable in this case. The case
q=0
is, in fact, just the free particle.

For a more general argument, consider

O=\mu_{O}H+\nu_{O}D+\omega_{O}K=\tilde{\mu}_{O}\tilde{H}+\tilde{\nu}_{O}\tilde{D}+\tilde{\omega}_{O}\tilde{K},

(88)

with both
\{H,D,K\}
, and
\{\tilde{H},\tilde{D},\tilde{K}\}
satisfying the sl(2,R) Lie algebra. Then

\Delta_{O}=\nu_{O}^{2}-\mu_{O}\omega_{O}=\tilde{\nu}_{O}^{2}-\tilde{\mu}_{O}\tilde{\omega}_{O}

and
O
has a discrete spectrum with normalizable eigenstates iff
[
17
]

\Delta<0.

The Hamiltonian (
27
) of generalized conformal mechanics can be written as

\mathbb{H}=\mathcal{Q}\bullet\mathcal{G}=H_{0}+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)\,K_{0}.

(89)

Above we have used (
60
) (with
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{0}(u),Q_{1}(u),Q_{2}(u)\}
, the conserved charges of the Schwarzian theory and
\mathcal{G}=\{H_{f},D_{f},K_{f}\}
, the conserved charges of generalized conformal mechanics) and (
46
) (we have ignored the term proportional to identity in (
27
)). Recalling (from (
47
)) that
H_{0}
,
D_{0}
and
K_{0}
furnish a representation of the sl(2,R) Lie algebra and (
59
), we note that
\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}
corresponding to the Hamiltonian is given by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}=\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{Q}\bullet\mathcal{Q}=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Sch}(f(u),u).

Therefore,
\Delta<0
is not satisfied if
{\rm Sch}(f(u),u)\leq 0
, and then, as claimed above,
\mathbb{H}
does not have normalizable eigenstates in this case.

It is therefore not appropriate to describe the Hilbert space using the energy eigenbasis. Following
[
17
]
, we will describe the Hilbert space using the eigenbasis of an operator that is a linear combination of the conserved charges for the action (
30
), namely
H_{f}
,
D_{f}
and
K_{f}
defined in (
50
). A generic operator of this form is

G=\mu_{G}H_{f}+\nu_{G}D_{f}+\omega_{G}K_{f},

(90)

where
\mu_{G}
,
\nu_{G}
and
\omega_{G}
are constants.
G
has a discrete spectrum with normalizable ground states iff

\Delta_{G}=\nu_{G}^{2}-\mu_{G}\omega_{G}<0.

It was suggested in
[
17
]
that such a
G
should be thought of as the true Hamiltonian of the system (which generates flow along a reparametrized time). The definition of
G
automatically introduces a length scale which leads to an energy gap and regulates the infrared problem of absence of normalizable eigenstates of the Hamiltonian (more below). This is similar to the case of confinement in gauge theories
[
35
,
36
]
where an energy scale emerges by spontaneously breaking scale invariance.

Crucially, we want to point out that instead of following
[
17
]
, we can use a conserved charge
G
with a discrete spectrum and normalizable eigenstates to find normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation (
84
) with the
original
Hamiltonian. This method of solving the time-dependent Schrödinger equation is known as the method of invariants
[
37
,
38
]
and has been used earlier in
[
26
]
in a similar context. To see how this works, note that since
G
is a conserved charge, it should satisfy

\partial_{u}G+i[\mathbb{H},G]=0

(91)

in the Schródinger picture also. Let us denote
\ket{G_{n}}
to be an eigenstate of
G
with eigenvalue
g_{n}
. Note that since
G
has explicit time-dependence, any eigenstate
\ket{G_{n}}
is also explicitly time-dependent. However, it can be easily shown that any eigenvalue
g_{n}
of
G
is constant (time independent) as follows. Firstly, plugging the state
e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}
into the time dependent Schrödinger equation (
84
) we obtain

\mathbb{H}e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}=i\partial_{u}(e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}})=i\left(ie^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\xi_{n}^{\prime}(u)\ket{G_{n}}+e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\partial_{u}\ket{G_{n}}\right).

(92)

Taking an inner product of the above with
e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}
we obtain

\xi_{n}^{\prime}(u)=\braket{G_{n}|i\partial_{u}-\mathbb{H}|G_{n}}.

(93)

Therefore,
e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}
solves the Schrödinger equation if
\xi_{n}(u)
satisfies the above equation. Secondly, we can differentiate the identity

Ge^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}=g_{n}(u)e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}

with respect to time (assuming that
g_{n}
are time-dependent) to obtain

-i(\mathbb{H}G-G\mathbb{H})e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}+G\partial_{u}(e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}})=g_{n}^{\prime}(u)e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}+g_{n}(u)\partial_{u}(e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}).

after utilizing (
91
). As
e^{i\xi_{n}(u)}\ket{G_{n}}
is a solution of the Schrödinger equation, using the eigenvalue equation itself we readily obtain that

g_{n}^{\prime}(u)=0.

(94)

Therefore, finding the normalizable solutions of the Schrödinger equation amounts to finding the time-independent spectrum of the invariant
G
, the corresponding time-dependent normalizable eigenstates and the corresponding time-dependent phases via (
93
).

Following
[
17
]
, we can consider the following dimensionless operators

\displaystyle R

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K_{f}}{\gamma}+\gamma H_{f}\right)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{K_{f}}{\gamma}-\gamma H_{f}\right)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}D_{f},

(95)

where
\gamma
is a constant with mass dimension
-1
.
9
9

9

Note that
H_{f}
,
D_{f}
and
K_{f}
have mass dimensions 1, 0 and -1, respectively. So

and

The above operators satisfy

[S,R]=-iT,\quad[R,T]=-iS,\quad[S,T]=-iR,

(96)

which is the

is a suitable invariant as it has normalizable eigenstates (
\Delta_{R}=-1/4<0
). Thus we can reduce the problem of finding solutions of the Schödinger equation to finding the spectrum of
R
, and the corresponding (time-dependent) eigenstates and phases. The energy scale
\gamma^{-1}
spontaneously breaks the classical scale invariance of generalized conformal mechanics.

Recall that the Casimir of the sL(2,R) Lie algebra is

\mathcal{C}=\tilde{H}_{f}\tilde{K}_{f}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{D}_{f}^{2}=R^{2}-S^{2}-T^{2}=\frac{q}{4}-\frac{3}{16}.

(97)

We can rewrite the Casimir in terms of a new variable
r_{0}
as

\mathcal{C}=\frac{q}{4}-\frac{3}{16}=r_{0}(r_{0}-1).

(98)

Solving this quadratic equation we get

r_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{q+\frac{1}{4}}\right).

(99)

For reasons described below, we choose
r_{0}
as the root with the

sign. We note that we also require that

for

to be real. However, we will see that we would require

(we will comment more on this later). We can define raising and lowering operators as

L_{\pm}^{R}=S\pm iT,

(100)

which obey

[R,L_{\pm}^{R}]=\pm L_{\pm}^{R},\quad[L_{+}^{R},L_{-}^{R}]=-2R.

(101)

The normalized eigenstates
\ket{r_{0},r}
of

L_{\pm}^{R}\ket{r_{0},r}=c_{\pm}(r_{0},r)\ket{r_{0},r\pm 1},

(103)

which implies that

r\geq r_{0},\quad{\text{o}r}\quad r\leq-r_{0}.

(104)

Note also that we can choose the phases appropriately so that

c_{\pm}(r_{0},r)=\sqrt{r(r\pm 1)-r_{0}(r_{0}-1)}.

We readily note that

c_{-}(r_{0},r_{0})=0,\quad{\rm and}\quad c_{+}(r_{0},-r_{0})=0

(105)

implying that

L_{-}^{R}\ket{r_{0},r=r_{0}}=0,\quad L_{+}^{R}\ket{r_{0},r=-r_{0}}=0.

(106)

We thus obtain the discrete series of eigenstates
\ket{r_{0},r_{n}}
with eigenvalues

(107)

by repeated action of
L_{+}^{R}
on
\ket{r_{0},r=r_{0}}
, and similarly another discrete series of eigenstates
\ket{r_{0},\tilde{r}_{n}}

on

.

The eigenstate

where

\psi_{0}(x,u)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\Gamma(2r_{0})}}\left(\frac{\gamma f^{\prime}(u)}{\gamma^{2}+f(u)^{2}}\right)^{r_{0}}\\
x^{2r_{0}-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}\gamma f^{\prime}(u)}{2(\gamma^{2}+f(u)^{2})}+i\frac{x^{2}}{4f^{\prime}(u)}\left(\frac{2f^{\prime}(u)^{2}f(u)}{\gamma^{2}+f(u)^{2}}-f^{\prime\prime}(u)\right)\right),

(110)

Note that this wavefunction is normalizable since

.

We recall that

\psi_{0}(0,u)=\partial_{x}\psi_{0}(0,u)=0.

(111)

As observed in

which corresponds to picking the positive root in (

(113)

We recall from Sec.

The other eigenstates of

4
Discussion

We have shown that generalized conformal quantum mechanics coupled to JT gravity naturally arises from considering a probe particle in the near-horizon geometry of a nearly extremal black hole, and leads to a solvable model for quantum gravity coupled with a detector. The full model is amenable to quantization, and can lead to a detailed understanding of how semi-classical spacetimes including black holes with entropies emerge from the point of view of a measuring device. In the future, we would like to study the full quantum theory of generalized conformal mechanics coupled to JT gravity, and explicitly understand the emergence of semi-classical spacetimes applying the theory of measurements.

Here, we discuss that our model can be possibly extended to higher dimensions leading to tractable quantizable models for black hole microstates. We first note that
n
-particle conformal mechanics arises from solutions in five-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity, as for instance, containing
n
static extremal black holes
[
40
,
19
]
. In such solutions, the centers of the throats of the extremal black holes are vectors
\vec{x}_{A}
in R
4
(with
A=1,2,\cdots,n
), and the full five-dimensional geometry reduces to AdS
2

\times
S
3
when zoomed near
\vec{x}_{A}
with the latter denoting the boundaries of the AdS
2
factors. When
\vec{x}_{A}
become slowly varying functions of the time coordinate only, the supergravity action reduces to a conformal mechanics action of
n
-particles on R
4
at the leading order. It would be interesting to study the case of
n
nearly extremal black hole solutions. We can expect that the conformal mechanics of the
n
particles is generalized via coupling to the time reparametrization modes of the
n
throats. With a single throat, the setup should reduce to the case studied in this work.

Another way of viewing the system of
n
nearly extremal throats whose centers slowly vary with time would be to consider
n
quantum dots whose motion in R
4
is governed by conformal quantum mechanics. Each quantum dot has internal degrees of freedom whose dynamics can be captured holographically by an emergent AdS
2
throat. Furthermore, the conformal mechanics couples to the time reparametrization modes of each AdS
2
throat. This can be called a
fragmented
holographic setup since the emergent geometries (AdS
2
throats) capture only parts of the degrees of freedom (the internal degrees of freedom of the quantum dots at
\vec{x}_{A}
). If the AdS
2
throats are described by JT gravity, then the full theory can be amenable to quantization.

Motivated by the fragmentation instability
[
23
]
of near horizon geometries of near extremal black holes in
D+1
dimensions, we can view such a setup of JT gravity theories of
n
two-dimensional throats (dual to the quantum dots) coupled to generalized conformal quantum mechanics of the motion of the center of the throats in R
D
(positions of the dual quantum dots) as a microstate model for such black holes. If the black hole has a horizon with radius
r_{h}
, then the motion of the throats are restricted to
|\vec{x}_{A}|\leq r_{h}
(see
[
19
]
for similar discussion). Quantum-mechanically, the multi-particle wavefunction should vanish whenever
|\vec{x}_{A}|=r_{h}
.

Such microstate models of black holes inspired by the fragmentation instability
[
23
]
of near horizon geometries of nearly extremal black holes has been studied in
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
. In this model the AdS
2
throats were restricted to end on a lattice which discretizes the horizon, and the AdS
2
throats were coupled by gravitational hair. The microstates are identified with the stationary solutions. It was shown that the relaxation and absorption properties of the microstates mimic those of a classical black hole. When quantum oscillators are coupled to one (or more throats), then they lose most of the energy, decouple from the throats and follow a quantum trajectory which retains a non-isometric copy of their initial state while another copy of the information of their initial state is transferred to the non-linear ringdown of the microstate as it transits to another
[
26
]
. Thus the model demonstrates replication of quantum information without paradoxes in a local semi-classical approximation realizing the black hole complementarity principle
[
27
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
25
]
microscopically. Furthermore, the model explicitly demonstrates quantum information mirroring
[
41
]
in consistency with the complementarity principle in which the explicit Hayden-Preskill decoding protocol of the infalling qubit from decoupled hair radiation does not depend on the state of the interior and only very limited information of the pre-existing hair radiation
[
24
,
26
]
. When the throats Hawking radiate after coupled to baths, the throats homogenize at very long time with the information of the interior transferred to the Hawking radiation and the decoupled hair radiation.

We can therefore expect that setups with JT gravity theories of
n
throats coupled to generalized conformal quantum mechanics of the motion of the center of the throats in R
D
, which are amenable to full quantization, can give deeper insights into the microscopic realization of the black hole complementarity principle without paradoxes, quantum information processing in black holes and also the emergence of the semi-classical geometries of black holes. In such models, the generalized conformal mechanics should play the role of the hair degrees of freedom and act as models of detectors for the interior of the quantum black hole.

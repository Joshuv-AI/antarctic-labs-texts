Bosonic Spin-1 SOPHY
Armando de la C. Rangel-Pantoja, I. Díaz-Saldaña, Carlos A. Vaquera-Araujo

Abstract
In this work we study the canonical quantization of a second-order pseudo-Hermitian field theory for massive spin-1 bosons transforming under the ( 1 , 0 ) ⊕ ( 0 , 1 ) (1,0)\oplus(0,1) representation of the restricted Lorentz Group and satisfying the Klein-Gordon equation.



1
Introduction

In recent years, there has been renewed interest in the study of pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theories, a topic pioneered by the study of symplectic fermions in
LeClair:2007iy
, where scalar fields that satisfy the Klein-Gordon (KG) equation evade the usual spin-statistics connection by relaxing the hermiticity requirement in favor of a less strigent one. The work in
LeClair:2007iy
was the first attempt to build a pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theory based on the success of pseudo-Hermitian quantum mechanics
Mostafazadeh:2001jk
;
Mostafazadeh:2008pw
, where a suitable redefinition of the inner product by means of an auxiliary operator guarantees that the energy eigenvalues are real and that the time evolution is unitary, generalizing the results of
PT
-symmetric quantum mechanics
Bender:1998ke
. Since then, several pseudo-Hermitian theories have been formulated. Notably, for most of them the dynamics are dictated by the KG equation and therefore can be classified as second-order pseudo-Hermitian theories, including massive spin-
1/2
fermions
Ferro-Hernandez:2023ymz
, spin-
1/2
Elko fermions and bosons with two-fold Wigner degeneracy
Ahluwalia:2023slc
, and a simple theory for massive spin-
1/2
bosons, built directly from Dirac spinors
Rangel-Pantoja:2025mhg
. The case of a first-order pseudo-Hermitian theory has also been studied in
Ahluwalia:2022zrm
, where a spin-
1/2
field satisfying the Dirac equation behaves as a boson.

In this paper, we study the first Second-Order Pseudo-Hermitian theorY (SOPHY) for massive spin-1 bosons transforming in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation of the Restricted Lorentz Group (RLG)
SO(1,3)^{+}
. The paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, The most general field in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation that satisfies the KG equation is built from first principles; in Section
3
, the Canonical Quantization of the theory is presented, showing that the resulting SOPHY is casual, the Hamiltonian is bounded from below with real eigenvalues, the global symmetries of the theory are identified, and its invariance under the discrete symmetries of Charge conjugation, Parity and Time-reversal is established; finally, in Section
4
we draw some conclusions about our results.

2
Fields in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation of the RLG

In order to make this paper as self-contained as possible, we build the fields from scratch, following the lines of
Napsuciale:2022usn
but adapting the notation to resemble that of
Langacker:2017uah
.

2.1
The
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
representations

The generators of the
SO(1,3)^{+}
group satisfy the commutation relations

\displaystyle[J^{i},J^{j}]=i\epsilon^{ijk}J^{k}\,,\quad[J^{i},K^{j}]=i\epsilon^{ijk}K^{k}\,,

\displaystyle[K^{i},K^{j}]=-i\epsilon^{ijk}J^{k}\,.

(1)

This algebra can be split into two independent
\mathfrak{su}(2)
algebras by defining the operators

A^{i}=\frac{1}{2}(J^{i}+iK^{i}),\quad B^{i}=\frac{1}{2}(J^{i}-iK^{i}),

(2)

that satisfy

\displaystyle[A^{i},A^{j}]=i\epsilon^{ijk}A^{k},\qquad[B^{i},B^{j}]=i\epsilon^{ijk}B^{k}.

\displaystyle[A^{i},B^{j}]=0.

(3)

Thus, the
SO(1,3)^{+}
irreducible representations (irreps) are characterized by the eigenvalues of the Casimir operators
\mathbf{A}^{2}
,
\mathbf{B}^{2}
while their vectors are labeled by the eigenvalues of the Cartan subalgebra operators
A^{3}
,
B^{3}
according to

\begin{split}\ket{a,m_a;b,m_b}&\equiv\ket{a,m_a}\ket{b,m_b}\\
\mathbf{A}^{2}\ket{a,m_a}&=a(a+1)\ket{a,m_a},\\
\mathbf{B}^{2}\ket{b,m_b}&=b(b+1)\ket{b,m_b},\\
A^{3}\ket{a,m_a}&=m_{a}\ket{a,m_a},\\
B^{3}\ket{b,m_b}&=m_{b}\ket{b,m_b}.\end{split}

(4)

The corresponding group elements for a column vector transforming under the
(a,b)
irrep become, in this basis

S(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi})=e^{-i(\mathbf{J}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathbf{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi})}=e^{-i(\mathbf{A}+\mathbf{B})\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}-(\mathbf{A}-\mathbf{B})\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}},

(5)

with rotation angles
\boldsymbol{\theta}=\theta\mathbf{\hat{m}}
and rapidities
\boldsymbol{\varphi}=\varphi\mathbf{\hat{n}}
.
In particular, for the
left-handed

(1,0)
irrep, with
\mathbf{B}=0
, the Lorentz transformation takes the form

\displaystyle S_{L}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi})=e^{-i\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}-\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}},

while for the
right-handed

(0,1)
irrep, with
\mathbf{A}=0
, we have

\displaystyle S_{R}(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\phi})=e^{-i\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}+\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varphi}},

where
\mathbf{J}^{(1)}
are the
SU(2)
generators for
j=1

\displaystyle J^{1\,(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J^{2\,(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}&0\\
\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}&0&-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\\
0&\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}&0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle J^{3\,(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

Using the fact that these generators satisfy the relation

[\mathbf{\hat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{3}=\mathbf{\hat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)},

(7)

it can be shown that the explicit form of the rotations is

\begin{split}R(\boldsymbol{\theta})&=e^{-i\theta\mathbf{\hat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}}\\
&=\mathbf{1}-i\sin\theta\mathbf{\hat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}+(\cos\theta-1)[\mathbf{\hat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}.\end{split}

(8)

while under boosts, left- and right-handed fields transform according to

\begin{split}B_{L,R}(\boldsymbol{\varphi})&=e^{\mp\varphi\mathbf{\hat{n}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}}\\
&=\mathbf{1}\mp\sinh\varphi\mathbf{\hat{n}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}+(\cosh\varphi-1)[\mathbf{\hat{n}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}.\end{split}

(9)

Since
SU(2)
is a pseudoreal group, there exists a matrix
\epsilon
that relates
R(\boldsymbol{\theta})
with its conjugate
R(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{*}
as

R(\boldsymbol{\theta})^{*}=\epsilon^{-1}R(\boldsymbol{\theta})\epsilon.

(10)

In our case at hand this operator is

\displaystyle\epsilon=

\displaystyle e^{-i\pi J^{2\,(1)}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(11)

satisfying
\epsilon=\epsilon^{-1}=\epsilon^{\dagger}=\epsilon^{T}=\epsilon^{*}
, and

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\widehat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{*}=-\epsilon[\mathbf{\widehat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]\epsilon,

\displaystyle[\mathbf{\widehat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}{}^{*}=\epsilon[\mathbf{\widehat{m}}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}\epsilon.

(12)

In terms of the momentum, we have
\cosh\varphi=\gamma=\frac{\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}{m}
,
\sinh\varphi=\gamma\beta=\frac{|\mathbf{p}|}{m}
, with
\omega_{\mathbf{p}}=+\sqrt{\mathbf{p^{2}}+m^{2}}
, thus the boosts can be written as

\begin{split}B_{L,R}(\mathbf{p})&=\mathbf{1}\mp\frac{\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}}{m}+\frac{[\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}}{m(\omega_{\mathbf{p}}+m)},\end{split}

(13)

showing that boosts are Hermitian

B^{\dagger}_{L,R}(\mathbf{p})=B_{L,R}(\mathbf{p}).

(14)

Besides, the boosts for left- and right-handed fields are orthogonal

\begin{split}B_{R,L}(\mathbf{p})B_{L,R}(\mathbf{p})=1,\end{split}

(15)

and therefore are their respective inverses

B_{R,L}^{-1}(\mathbf{p})=B_{L,R}(\mathbf{p}).

(16)

The squared left and right boost operators
B_{L}(\mathbf{p})^{2}
,
B_{R}(\mathbf{p})^{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle B_{L}(\mathbf{p})^{2}=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}{m^{2}}\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}+\frac{2}{m^{2}}[\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\tau^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}/m^{2}\equiv\tau(p)/m^{2},

(17)

\displaystyle B_{R}(\mathbf{p})^{2}=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}{m^{2}}\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}+\frac{2}{m^{2}}[\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{J}^{(1)}]^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}/m^{2}\equiv\bar{\tau}(p)/m^{2},

(18)

with

\displaystyle\tau^{00}=1,\qquad\tau^{0i}=\tau^{i0}=J^{i\,(1)},

\displaystyle\tau^{ij}=g^{ij}\mathbf{1}+\{J^{i\,(1)},J^{j\,(1)}\},

(19)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}^{00}=1,\qquad\bar{\tau}^{0i}=\bar{\tau}^{i0}=-J^{i\,(1)},

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}^{ij}=g^{ij}\mathbf{1}+\{J^{i\,(1)},J^{j\,(1)}\},

(20)

where
g^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
is the metric tensor of Minkowski space-time.
The product
\bar{\tau}(p)\tau(p)=\tau(p)\bar{\tau}(p)
reduces to

\bar{\tau}(p)\tau(p)=(p^{\mu}p_{\mu})^{2}=p^{4},

(21)

for an arbitrary momentum
p^{\mu}
, and on shell to
\bar{\tau}(p)\tau(p)=m^{4}
.
With the aid of
\tau(p)
and
\bar{\tau}(p)
, the left and right boost operators
B_{L}(\mathbf{p})
,
B_{R}(\mathbf{p})
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle B_{L}(\mathbf{p})=\sqrt{\tau(p)}/m,\quad B_{R}(\mathbf{p})=\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}/m.

(22)

In terms of
\tau^{\mu\nu}
and
\bar{\tau}^{\mu\nu}
the Lorentz generators for the
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
irreps are, respectively

\displaystyle M_{L}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{6}\left(\tau^{\mu\rho}\bar{\tau}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}-\tau^{\nu\rho}\bar{\tau}^{\mu}{}_{\rho}\right),

(23)

\displaystyle M_{R}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{6}\left(\bar{\tau}^{\mu\rho}\tau^{\nu}{}_{\rho}-\bar{\tau}^{\nu\rho}\tau^{\mu}{}_{\rho}\right).

(24)

The Lorentz generators of the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation in the chiral basis are given by

M^{\mu\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{L}^{\mu\nu}&0\\
0&M_{R}^{\mu\nu}\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

In this representation one can define
Parity
as the transformation
\Pi^{-1}\mathbf{A}\Pi=\mathbf{B}
,
\Pi^{-1}\mathbf{B}\Pi=\mathbf{A}
, and so it
exchanges
(a,b)\leftrightarrow(b,a)
irreps.
In this basis, the boost operator for this representation is given, in
3\times 3
blocks, by

B(\mathbf{p})=\begin{pmatrix}B_{L}(\mathbf{p})&0\\
0&B_{R}(\mathbf{p})\end{pmatrix},

(26)

while the parity operator is explicitly

\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(27)

and satisfies
\Pi=\Pi^{-1}=\Pi^{\dagger}=\Pi^{T}=\Pi^{*}
.
As shown below, in parity is a convenient tool in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
rep for the construction of the most general field that satisfies the KG equation.

2.2
Parity Eigenvectors, Charge Conjugation and Chiral Operator

In this subsection we build explicitly the most general spin-1 field satisfying the KG equation and transforming under the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
rep of the RLG. As a first step, we identify the eigenvectors of the parity operator
\Pi
, which are six-component column matrices, denoted here as
u^{s}_{\mathbf{0}}
and
v^{s}_{\mathbf{0}}

\Pi u^{s}_{\mathbf{0}}=u^{s}_{\mathbf{0}},\qquad\Pi v^{s}_{\mathbf{0}}=-v^{s}_{\mathbf{0}},

(28)

and they are given, in blocks, by

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{0}}^{s}\equiv

\displaystyle m\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{s}\\
\xi^{s}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{0}}^{s}\equiv

\displaystyle m\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{s}\\
-\chi^{s}\end{pmatrix},

(29)

where
\xi^{s}
,
s={1,0,-1}
are the unit column vectors

\displaystyle\xi^{1}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\xi^{0}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
0\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\xi^{-1}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix},

(30)

satisfying the orthonormality and completeness relations

\displaystyle\xi^{r\dagger}\xi^{s}=

\displaystyle\delta^{rs},

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\xi^{s}_{a}\xi^{s\dagger}_{b}=

\displaystyle\delta_{ab},

(31)

and
\chi^{s}=\epsilon\xi^{s\,*}
are given such that

\displaystyle\chi^{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\xi^{-1},\qquad\chi^{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\text{-}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}=-\xi^{0},

\displaystyle\chi^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}=\xi^{1},

(32)

or, equivalently

\chi^{s}=(-1)^{s+1}\xi^{-s}.

(33)

Boosting the parity operator we obtain

\begin{split}B(\mathbf{p})\Pi B^{-1}(\mathbf{p})=&\frac{p_{\mu}p_{\nu}}{m^{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&\tau^{\mu\nu}\\
\bar{\tau}^{\mu\nu}&0\end{pmatrix}\\
\equiv&\frac{1}{m^{2}}S^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\equiv\frac{1}{m^{2}}S(p),\end{split}

(34)

where we have defined the matrices

S^{\mu\nu}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&\tau^{\mu\nu}\\
\bar{\tau}^{\mu\nu}&0\end{pmatrix}.

(35)

In terms of
S^{\mu\nu}
the Lorentz generators for the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation are

\displaystyle M^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{6}[S^{\mu\rho},S^{\nu}{}_{\rho}],

(36)

with

\displaystyle M^{ij}\equiv\epsilon^{ijk}J^{k},\qquad M^{0i}=-M^{i0}\equiv K^{i}.

(37)

Notice that
S^{00}=\Pi
.

In order to obtain the vectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
with arbitrary momentum
\mathbf{p}
we have to apply the boost operator in Eq. (
26
) on the parity eigenvectors in Eq. (
29
). The boosted parity eigenvectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
are then

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=B(\mathbf{p})u_{\mathbf{0}}^{s}=m\begin{bmatrix}B_{L}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s}\\
B_{R}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\xi^{s}\\
\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}\xi^{s}\end{bmatrix},

(38)

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=B(\mathbf{p})v_{\mathbf{0}}^{s}=m\begin{bmatrix}B_{L}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s}\\
-B_{R}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\chi^{s}\\
-\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}\chi^{s}\end{bmatrix}.

(39)

Lorentz scalars can be built with the aid of the adjoints as follows

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,\dagger}S^{00}=\begin{bmatrix}\xi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}&\xi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\end{bmatrix},

(40)

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,\dagger}S^{00}=\begin{bmatrix}-\chi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}&\chi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\end{bmatrix}.

(41)

The charge-conjugate boosted parity eigenvectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
are defined as

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}\equiv

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,T},

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}\equiv

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,T},

(42)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
0&\epsilon\end{pmatrix},

(43)

satisfying
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{-1}=\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}=\mathcal{C}^{T}=\mathcal{C}^{*}
.
Explicitly

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle m\begin{bmatrix}\epsilon B_{R}^{*}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s\,*}\\
\epsilon B_{L}^{*}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s\,*}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle m\begin{bmatrix}-\epsilon B_{R}^{*}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s\,*}\\
\epsilon B_{L}^{*}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s\,*}\end{bmatrix}.

(44)

By combining Eq. (
12
) and Eq. (
13
), it can be shown that

\displaystyle\epsilon B^{*}_{R}(\mathbf{p})\epsilon=

\displaystyle B_{L}(\mathbf{p}),

\displaystyle\epsilon B^{*}_{L}(\mathbf{p})\epsilon=

\displaystyle B_{R}(\mathbf{p}),

(45)

and therefore, the charge-conjugate boosted parity eigenvectors become

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle m\begin{bmatrix}B_{L}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s}\\
B_{R}(\mathbf{p})\chi^{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\chi^{s}\\
\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}\chi^{s}\end{bmatrix},

(46)

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle m\begin{bmatrix}-B_{L}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s}\\
B_{R}(\mathbf{p})\xi^{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sqrt{\tau(p)}\xi^{s}\\
\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}\xi^{s}\end{bmatrix},

(47)

and their adjoints

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\ c}=

\displaystyle u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c\dagger}S^{00}=\begin{bmatrix}\chi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}&\chi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\end{bmatrix},

(48)

\displaystyle\overline{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c\dagger}S^{00}=\begin{bmatrix}\xi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\bar{\tau}(p)}&-\xi^{s\,\dagger}\sqrt{\tau(p)}\end{bmatrix}.

(49)

Note that, in contrast with the Dirac theory, here the charge-conjugation operator does not change parity

u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=(-1)^{s+1}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s},\qquad v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=(-1)^{s}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s}.

(50)

The chirality operator is defined as the Casimir

X=\frac{i}{8}\widetilde{M}^{\mu\nu}M_{\mu\nu},

(51)

where
\widetilde{M}^{\mu\nu}=(1/2)\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}M_{\rho\sigma}
are the dual Lorentz generators of the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation, with
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
being the totally antisymmetric Levi-Civita symbol in Minkowski space-time with
\epsilon^{0123}\equiv 1
. In the chiral basis, the chirality operator becomes

X=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},

(52)

and it can be seen that this operator anti-commutes with the
S^{\mu\nu}
matrices

\{S^{\mu\nu},X\}=0.

(53)

The vectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
can be written in terms of
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
using the chirality operator as follows

\displaystyle Xu_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=(-1)^{s}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s},

(54)

\displaystyle Xu_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=-v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=(-1)^{s+1}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s\,c},

(55)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}X=-\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=(-1)^{s+1}\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s}

(56)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}X=\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=(-1)^{s}\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{-s\,c},

(57)

and these vectors obey the following orthogonality relations

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=2m^{2}\delta^{rs},\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=-2m^{2}\delta^{rs},

(58)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=2m^{2}\delta^{rs},\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=-2m^{2}\delta^{rs},

(59)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=0,\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=0,\quad\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=0,\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=0,

(60)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=0,\quad\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=0,\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=0,\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=0,

(61)

\displaystyle\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=2m^{2}\chi^{r\,\dagger}\xi^{s},\quad\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=2m^{2}\xi^{r\,\dagger}\chi^{s},

(62)

\displaystyle\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r\,c}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=-2m^{2}\xi^{r\,\dagger}\chi^{s},\quad\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}}^{r}v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=-2m^{2}\chi^{r\,\dagger}\xi^{s},

(63)

together with the projections

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{s}u_{\mathbf{p}a}^{s}\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}b}^{s}=&\left[S(p)+m^{2}\mathbf{1}\right]_{ab}\\
\equiv&2\left(\Sigma^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\right)_{ab}\equiv 2\Sigma(p)_{ab},\end{split}

(64)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{s}v_{\mathbf{p}a}^{s}\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}b}^{s}=&\left[S(p)-m^{2}\mathbf{1}\right]_{ab}\\
\equiv&-2\left(\bar{\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}\right)_{ab}\equiv-2\overline{\Sigma}(p)_{ab},\end{split}

(65)

\displaystyle\sum_{s}u_{\mathbf{p}a}^{s\,c}\bar{u}_{\mathbf{p}b}^{s\,c}=2\Sigma(p)_{ab},

(66)

\displaystyle\sum_{s}v_{\mathbf{p}a}^{s\,c}\bar{v}_{\mathbf{p}b}^{s\,c}=-2\overline{\Sigma}(p)_{ab},

(67)

where we have defined the operators
Napsuciale:2015kua

\Sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{1}+S^{\mu\nu}),\qquad\overline{\Sigma}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}(g^{\mu\nu}\mathbf{1}-S^{\mu\nu}).

(68)

On shell, the boosted parity eigenvectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s},v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c},v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
satisfy the equations

\displaystyle\left[\Sigma(p)-m^{2}\right]u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left[\Sigma(p)-m^{2}\right]u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle 0,

(69)

\displaystyle\left[\overline{\Sigma}(p)-m^{2}\right]v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\left[\overline{\Sigma}(p)-m^{2}\right]v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}=

\displaystyle 0.

(70)

An important observation is that, for an off shell momentum
p^{\mu}
, the product
[\Sigma(p)-m^{2}][\bar{\Sigma}(p)-m^{2}]=[\bar{\Sigma}(p)-m^{2}][\Sigma(p)-m^{2}]
evaluates to

\begin{split}[\Sigma(p)-m^{2}][\bar{\Sigma}(p)-m^{2}]=-m^{2}(p^{2}-m^{2})\mathbf{1},\end{split}

(71)

thus, the boosted parity eigenvectors
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
,
u_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
,
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}
and
v_{\mathbf{p}}^{s\,c}
are all solutions to the on-shell condition
(p^{2}-m^{2})w_{\mathbf{p}}^{s}=0
, and therefore the most general field in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation of the RLG that satisfies the KG equation can be written as

\begin{split}\psi(x)=&\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2m\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s}\bigg\{\left[u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}}+v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]e^{-ip\cdot x}\\
&+\left[u^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{1s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}+v^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]e^{ip\cdot x}\bigg\}.\end{split}

(72)

3
Canonical Quantization

3.1
Pseudo-Hermitian Theory

The field in Eq. (
72
) can be successfully quantized as a pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theory, where the hermiticity of the Lagrangian is relaxed to the less stringent condition

\mathcal{L}^{\#}\equiv\eta^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\eta=\mathcal{L},

(73)

for some operator
\eta
.
The corresponding Lagrangian for our spin-1 SOPHY is given by

\mathcal{L}=\partial^{\mu}\widehat{\psi}\partial_{\mu}\psi-m^{2}\widehat{\psi}\psi,

(74)

where
\widehat{\psi}
is the redefined dual field that enforces the pseudo-Hermiticity of the quantum field theory
Sablevice:2023odu
, and is given by

\begin{split}&\widehat{\psi}(x)=\eta^{-1}\bar{\psi}\eta\\
=&\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2m\sqrt{\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s}\bigg\{\left[\bar{u}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{1s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}-\bar{v}^{s}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]e^{ip\cdot x}\\
&+\left[\bar{u}^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{1s}_{\mathbf{p}}-\bar{v}^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right]e^{-ip\cdot x}\bigg\},\end{split}

(75)

the action of the operator
\eta
is defined by the following relations

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\eta^{-1}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}\eta=(-1)^{j-1}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}},\qquad\eta^{-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\eta=(-1)^{j-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}},\end{array}

(76)

where
j=1,2
, and the explicit solution for this operator is found to be

\eta=\exp\left[i\pi\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{s}\left(a^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}+b^{2s\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{2s}_{\mathbf{p}}\right)\right],

(77)

this operator satisfies
\eta=\eta^{-1}=\eta^{\dagger}
, meaning that it is simultaneously Hermitian and unitary, yielding
\eta^{2}=1
.

If the conjugate momenta
\pi_{\psi}=\dot{\widehat{\psi}}
and
\pi_{\widehat{\psi}}=\dot{\psi}
satisfy the equal-time canonical commutation relations

\begin{split}\left[\psi_{a}(\mathbf{x},t),\pi_{\psi}{}_{b}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\right]&=\left[\widehat{\psi}_{a}(\mathbf{x},t),\pi_{\widehat{\psi}}{}_{b}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\right]\\
&=i\delta_{ab}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\end{split}

(78)

then the momentum-space field operators satisfy the following commutation relations

\begin{split}\left[a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}},a^{kr\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]&=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{jk}\delta^{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}),\\
\left[b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}},b^{kr\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}^{\prime}}\right]&=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{jk}\delta^{rs}\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime}).\end{split}

(79)

The resulting quantum field theory is causal, as can be verified explicitly

\begin{split}&[\psi_{a}(x),\widehat{\psi}{}_{b}(x^{\prime})]\\
&=\delta_{ab}\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega_{\mathbf{p}}}\bigg\{e^{-ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}-e^{ip\cdot(x-x^{\prime})}\bigg\}\\
&=\delta_{ab}\Delta(x-x^{\prime}),\end{split}

(80)

where
\Delta(x-x^{\prime})
is Schwinger’s green function.
Similarly we have
[\psi_{a}(x),\psi_{b}(x^{\prime})]=0
and
[\widehat{\psi}{}_{a}(x),\widehat{\psi}{}_{b}(x^{\prime})]=0
, and the Feynman propagator can be straightforwardly found as

\begin{split}&[S_{F}(x-y)]_{\alpha\beta}=\bra{0}T[\psi_{\alpha}(x)\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)]\ket{0}\\
&=\bra{0}[\theta(x^{0}-y^{0})\psi_{\alpha}(x)\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)+\theta(y^{0}-x^{0})\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(y)\psi_{\alpha}(x)]\ket{0}\\
&=\int\frac{d^{4}p}{(2\pi)^{4}}\left[\frac{i\delta_{\alpha\beta}}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i\epsilon}\right]e^{-ip\cdot(x-y)},\end{split}

(81)

as expected for a field that satisfies the KG equation.

3.2
Noether charges

The Hamiltonian and the momentum operator are given by

\begin{split}H&=:\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg\{\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\dot{\psi}+\nabla\widehat{\psi}\cdot\nabla\psi+m^{2}\widehat{\psi}\psi\bigg\}:,\\
\mathbf{P}&=:-\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg\{\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\nabla\psi+\nabla\widehat{\psi}\dot{\psi}\bigg\}:,\end{split}

(82)

where
:{}:
indicates normal ordering. Defining
P^{\mu}=(H,\mathbf{P})
, explicit calculation shows that

\begin{split}P^{\mu}&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}p^{\mu}\sum_{s,j}\left[a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}+b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}\right].\end{split}

(83)

As can be seen from the above expression, the energy is bounded from below and, written in terms of the momentum-space operators, the Hamiltonian is in fact Hermitian.

Since the field in Eq. (
72
) is complex, the theory has a global charge
\mathrm{U}(1)
associated with the transformation

\psi\rightarrow\psi^{\prime}=e^{-i\alpha}\psi,\qquad\widehat{\psi}\rightarrow\widehat{\psi}^{\prime}=\widehat{\psi}e^{i\alpha},

(84)

this conserved charge is given by

\begin{split}Q=&:i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\bigg\{\widehat{\psi}\dot{\psi}-\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\psi\bigg\}:\\
&=\int\frac{d^{3}\mathbf{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\omega_{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{j,s}\left[a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}-b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}\right].\end{split}

(85)

From the commutation relations of this operator with the creation and annihilation operators, one can conclude that
a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}
and
b^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}
have charge
+1
, while
a^{js}_{\mathbf{p}}
and
b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}
have charge
-1
.
The one-particle states can be labeled with this charge eigenvalue, and one can show that these states are twelve-fold degenerate

\begin{split}Ha^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\ket{0}&\propto H\ket{\mathbf{p},+,j,s}=\omega_{\mathbf{p}}\ket{\mathbf{p},+,j,s},\\
Hb^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}\ket{0}&\propto H\ket{\mathbf{p},-,j,s}=\omega_{\mathbf{p}}\ket{\mathbf{p},-,j,s}.\end{split}

(86)

Actually, the free theory has a larger symmetry that can be identified as follows: using the commutation relation
[\psi_{\alpha}(x),\widehat{\psi}_{\beta}(x)]=0
, following from Eq. (
80
), the Lagrangian Eq. (
74
) can be recast in the form

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}\partial^{\mu}\Psi^{T}\Xi\partial_{\mu}\Psi-\frac{m^{2}}{2}\Psi^{T}\Xi\Psi,

(87)

where
\Psi
is the column vector

\Psi(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\psi}^{T}(x)\\
\psi(x)\end{pmatrix},

(88)

and
\Xi
is a
12\times 12
matrix, written in
6\times 6
blocks as

\Xi=\begin{pmatrix}0_{6\times 6}&1_{6\times 6}\\
1_{6\times 6}&0_{6\times 6}\end{pmatrix}=\sigma^{1}\otimes 1_{6\times 6}.

(89)

The Lagrangian in Eq. (
87
) is invariant under global transformations of the form
\Psi\to\Psi^{\prime}=M\Psi
with
M^{T}\Xi M=\Xi
. This group turns out to be isomorphic to the group
O(12,\mathbb{C})
which has 66 independent generators
X
satisfying
X^{T}\Xi+\Xi X=0
. A convenient basis for these generators is

1_{2\times 2}\otimes A_{1},\qquad\sigma^{1}\otimes A_{2},\qquad\sigma^{2}\otimes A_{3},\qquad\sigma^{3}\otimes S,

(90)

where
A_{i},S
,
i,=1,2,3
are arbitrary antisymmetric, and symmetric
6\times 6
matrices, respectively.

It is important to remark that the theory is, by construction, a SOPHY for spin-
1
fields. One can explicitly show that the field indeed contains particles of spin
1

by evaluating the action of the spin operator

\begin{split}\mathbf{S}=:-i\int d^{3}\mathbf{x}\left\{\dot{\widehat{\psi}}\mathbf{J}\psi-\widehat{\psi}\mathbf{J}\dot{\psi}\right\}:,\end{split}

(91)

with

\mathbf{J}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{J}^{(1)}&0\\
0&\mathbf{J}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix},

(92)

on one-particle states with zero momentum in the chiral representation. The resulting relations are

S^{3}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{0}}\ket{0}=sa^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{0}}\ket{0},\quad S^{3}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{0}}\ket{0}=sb^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{0}}\ket{0}.

(93)

3.3
Discrete symmetries

Parity reverses the three-momentum and leaves the spin invariant. Thus, its action on creation and annihilation operators is

\displaystyle P^{-1}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}P

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}a^{js\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},

(94)

\displaystyle P^{-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}P

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}b^{js\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},

(95)

where the phases have been chosen by noticing that the inverse boost operator is

\begin{split}B(-\mathbf{p})=&\begin{pmatrix}B_{R}(\mathbf{p})&0\\
0&B_{L}(\mathbf{p})\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{\overline{\tau}(p)}&0\\
0&\sqrt{\tau(p)}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(96)

and therefore, the following relations hold

\displaystyle u^{s}_{-\mathbf{p}}=S^{00}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}},u^{s\,c}_{-\mathbf{p}}=S^{00}u^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}},

(97)

\displaystyle v^{s}_{-\mathbf{p}}=-S^{00}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}},v^{s\,c}_{-\mathbf{p}}=-S^{00}v^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}},

(98)

yielding

\displaystyle P^{-1}\psi(x)P=S^{00}\psi(\mathcal{P}x),

(99)

with
\mathcal{P}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
.
Similarly, the parity transformation for the dual field is

\displaystyle P^{-1}\widehat{\psi}(x)P=\widehat{\psi}(\mathcal{P}x)S^{00}.

(100)

Another feature is that charge conjugation interchanges particles and antiparticles

\displaystyle C^{-1}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}C

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}},

(101)

\displaystyle C^{-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}C

\displaystyle=(-1)^{j-1}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}.

(102)

Using the definition of Charge conjugation in Eq. (
42
), the corresponding transformation of the field and its dual reads

\displaystyle C^{-1}\psi(x)C=\mathcal{C}\widehat{\psi}^{T}(x),

\displaystyle C^{-1}\widehat{\psi}C=\psi^{T}(x)\mathcal{C}.

(103)

Notice that, in the chiral basis we have
\mathcal{C}=-S^{00}S^{22}=-S^{22}S^{00}=S^{11}S^{33}
.

Finally, time-reversal changes the direction of both momentum and spin

\displaystyle T^{-1}a^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}T

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}a^{j-s\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}},

(104)

\displaystyle T^{-1}b^{js\dagger}_{\mathbf{p}}T

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}b^{j-s\dagger}_{-\mathbf{p}}.

(105)

Using the identities

\displaystyle u^{-s*}_{-\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}\mathcal{C}u^{s}_{\mathbf{p}},

(106)

\displaystyle v^{-s*}_{-\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}\mathcal{C}v^{s}_{\mathbf{p}},

(107)

\displaystyle u^{-s\,c*}_{-\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}\mathcal{C}u^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}},

(108)

\displaystyle v^{-s\,c*}_{-\mathbf{p}}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{s+1}\mathcal{C}v^{s\,c}_{\mathbf{p}},

(109)

that follow from
B^{*}(-\mathbf{p})=\mathcal{C}B(\mathbf{p})\mathcal{C}
, and Eq. (
33
),
the time-reversal transformations for the field and its dual become

\displaystyle T^{-1}\psi(x)T=\mathcal{C}\psi(\mathcal{T}x),

\displaystyle T^{-1}\widehat{\psi}(x)T=\widehat{\psi}(\mathcal{T}x)\mathcal{C}.

(110)

where we have defined

\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
. Thus, our second-order theory is invariant under Parity (
\mathrm{P}
), Charge conjugation (
\mathrm{C}
) and time reversal (
\mathrm{T}
), and therefore under
\mathrm{CPT}
.

3.4
Interactions

The simplest
\mathrm{C}
,
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{T}
invariant pseudo-Hermitian interactions that can be introduced in this framework are self-interactions represented by a dimension-four renormalizable operator

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=&\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(\widehat{\psi}\psi\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\left(\widehat{\psi}X\psi\right)\left(\widehat{\psi}X\psi\right)\\
&+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\left(\widehat{\psi}M^{\mu\nu}\psi\right)\left(\widehat{\psi}M_{\mu\nu}\psi\right)\\
&+\frac{\lambda_{4}}{2}\left(\widehat{\psi}S^{\mu\nu}\psi\right)\left(\widehat{\psi}S_{\mu\nu}\psi\right).\end{split}

(111)

The renormalization of these interactions was studied in
Rivero-Acosta:2020hvy
, in the context of the naïve Hermitian second-order theory, with the six complex components of the field packed into a complex second-rank antisymmetric tensor. Nevertheless, all the results contained in
Rivero-Acosta:2020hvy
can be readily generalized to the SOPHY version of the theory presented here. Moreover, since
\psi
has mass dimension one, it cannot decay into standard model fields. Therefore, it is automatically stable and could play the role of a weakly interacting massive particle (WIMP) dark matter candidate, interacting with the standard model fields through a Higgs portal, along the lines of the spin-
1/2
SOPHY introduced in
deGracia:2024umr
.

4
Conclusions

In this work, we have studied the quantization of a massive field transforming in the
(1,0)\oplus(0,1)
representation of the RLG that satisfies the KG equation. We have shown that the canonical quantization is rendered consistent by an adequate choice of the field dual and the introduction of a unitary and Hermitian operator
\eta
that relaxes the Hermiticity condition into a less stringent pseudo-Hermitian one, dictated by Eq. (
73
). The resulting SOPHY is causal, and the Hamiltonian is bounded from below with real eigenvalues. For consistency, we have verified that the corresponding fields have spin
1
, and we have identified the global and discrete symmetries of the theory, which turns out to be invariant under
C
,
P
and
T
. Finally, we have sketched the plausible interactions, including a rich class of renormalizable quartic self-interactions.

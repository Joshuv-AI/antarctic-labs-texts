Forcing Axioms for Proper Posets Preserving a Topological Property: Consistency Results
Thomas Gilton

Abstract
. Forcing axioms are generalizations of Baire category principles that allow one to intersect more dense open sets and to do so in a wider variety of circumstances. In this paper we introduce two new forcing axioms related to posets which preserve topological properties of various spaces, specifically the properties of Lindelöf and countably tight. The focus in this paper is on using Neeman’s side conditions iteration schema ( [ 20 ] ) to prove the consistency of these two forcing axioms. In later work, we will discuss applications of these forcing axioms in more detail.



1.
Introduction

In a typical Baire category argument, one constructs an object of interest (say, an everywhere continuous, nowhere differentiable function) by refining approximations (dense open sets) and using features of the topology (say, being a compact metric space) to ensure that the dense sets “hone in” on the desired object (i.e., that any countably-many have a non-empty intersection). This is in contrast to constructions where one writes down an explicit formula for the object under consideration (say, the formula for the sawtooth function).

Forcing axioms generalize Baire category arguments by allowing the intersection of more (at least
\aleph_{1}
-many) dense open sets and under a wider variety of circumstances, namely, for posets with various desirable features. Martin’s Axiom was the first of these.
\mathsf{MA}(\kappa)
asserts that if
\mathbb{P}
is a poset with the countable chain condition (c.c.c.) and if
\left<D_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\right>
is a sequence of dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}
, then there is a filter
G\subset\mathbb{P}
so that for all
\alpha<\kappa
,
G\cap D_{\alpha}\neq\emptyset
. As another example, the Proper Forcing Axiom, abbreviated
\mathsf{PFA}
, is a substantial strengthening of Martin’s Axiom. It asserts that if
\mathbb{P}
is a proper poset (we will review this definition later) and if
\left<D_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right>
is a sequence of
\omega_{1}
-many dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}
, then there is a filter
G\subset\mathbb{P}
so that for all
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
D_{\alpha}\cap G\neq\emptyset
.

Models satisfying forcing axioms are normally obtained by reaching a closure point in a reasonably well-behaved iteration of forcings. Accordingly, constructing such a model involves at least the following two ingredients:

(1)

the appropriate notion of a “closure point” and

(2)

an iteration theorem, showing that a desirable property of posets is preserved under a specific kind of iteration.

For example, with Martin’s Axiom, one only has to deal with c.c.c. posets of cardinality below the targeted value of the continuum. Thus any regular cardinal
\kappa\geq\omega_{2}
can act as a closure point. Moreover, a finite-support iteration of c.c.c. posets is still c.c.c. – the classic result of Solovay and Tennenbaum
[
24
]
– giving us the desired iteration theorem. By contrast, in the case of
\mathsf{PFA}
, one needs a large cardinal (all known proofs to date involve a supercompact) in order to reach the right closure point. The relevant iteration theorem says that the property of being proper is preserved under countable support iterations (
[
23
]
).

A recent development is Neeman’s proof (
[
20
]
) of the consistency of
\mathsf{PFA}
using a forcing that has
finite
supports. This gives additional flexibility in constructing models of forcing axioms. A condition in Neeman’s forcing includes two components. The first component, known as the
working part
, is a finite support function analogous to a condition in a traditional iteration. The second component is a finite,
\in
-increasing sequence of models called
side conditions
. The two components are related by requiring the outputs of the working part to be (forced to be) generic conditions for plenty of the models in the side condition. This interaction between the two parts of a condition allows Neeman to argue that the entire poset is proper, and hence preserves
\omega_{1}
. For other examples of such “side conditions iteration theorems” see
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
.

The idea of using side conditions in order to help argue for properness goes back to Todorc̆ević (see
[
25
]
and later expanded to include preservation of
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
in
[
26
]
). The idea is remarkably fecund: by introducing models themselves into the conditions in a forcing, one can place natural constraints on how the models interact with the other components of a condition and thereby more smoothly argue for properness. Some other highlights include the following:
[
15
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
8
]
, and
[
16
]
, among many others.

In this work, we introduce two forcing axioms for proper posets preserving some topological property
\Phi
of a given space
X
, specifically, Lindelöf spaces as well as countably tight spaces. The models will be constructed using Neeman’s finite-support method. We view the situation as analogous to the results in
[
27
]
and
[
17

, where those authors focus on proper posets preserving some noteworty feature of a tree on
\omega_{1}

More specifically, we define forcing axioms of the form
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
, where
X
is a topological space satisfying the topological property
\Phi

\mathbb{P}
is a proper poset that preserves
\Phi
of

\mathbb{P}

\Phi

is a space of size less than a given supercompact cardinal, we provide proofs of the consistency of these axioms: see Theorems

\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
is the same as
\mathsf{PFA}

\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
for non-trivial

2.
Background Definitions

In this section, we review the basic definitions from forcing and from topology. For the former, we will review the definitions associated with proper and strongly proper posets, as well as some of their foundational features. For the latter, we will review the definitions of the relevant topological properties as well as review the definition of what it means for a forcing to preserve a topological property.

We begin with the notion of
properness
for a poset which has its roots in Shelah’s work (
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular uncountable cardinal,
\mathbb{P}\in H(\kappa)
a poset, and
M\prec H(\kappa)
with
\mathbb{P}\in M
.

A condition
q\in\mathbb{P}
is an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-
generic
condition if it forces that

M\cap\dot{G}\cap D\neq\emptyset

for each
D\in M
which is dense in
\mathbb{P}
.

\mathbb{P}
is
proper for
M
if for every
p\in M\cap\mathbb{P}
, there is an extension
q\leq p
so that
q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic.

Finally,
\mathbb{P}
is simply said to be
proper
if for some (equivalently, all) large enough uncountable regular
\lambda
, there is a club
\mathcal{C}
of countable elementary submodels of
H(\lambda)
so that
\mathbb{P}
is proper for each
M\in\mathcal{C}
.

The following lemma follows from applications of elementarity and the definition of properness. We will use it frequently throughout the paper:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\kappa
be a regular uncountable cardinal. Suppose that
M_{0}\in\dots\in M_{n-1}
is an
\in
-increasing sequence of countable elementary submodels of
H(\kappa)
and that
\mathbb{P}\in H(\kappa)
is a poset which is proper for each
M_{i}
. Next, let
p\in\mathbb{P}
, and suppose that for some
k<n-1
,
p
is
(M_{i},\mathbb{P})
-generic for all
i\leq k
and that
p\in M_{k+1}
. Then there exists
p^{\prime}\leq p
so that
p^{\prime}
is an
(M_{i},\mathbb{P})
-generic condition for all
i<n
.

The following are some useful equivalences to a condition being
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic; proofs may be found in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\kappa
,
\mathbb{P}
, and
M
be as in Definition
2.1
. Let
q\in\mathbb{P}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

q
is
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic;

(2)

q\Vdash M[\dot{G}]\cap V=M
;

(3)

for all
q^{*}\leq q
and all dense
D\subseteq\mathbb{P}
with
D\in M
, there exists a
p\in M\cap D
so that
p
is compatible with
q^{*}
.

Note that for a condition
q
to be
(M,\mathbb{P})
-generic, we only have to check a certain property with respect to dense subsets of
\mathbb{P}
which are members of
M
. A significant strengthening of this idea was first made explicit in
[
19
]
, namely, the idea of a
strongly
generic condition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\kappa
,
\mathbb{P}
, and
M
be as in Definition
2.1
. A condition
q\in\mathbb{P}
is said to be an
(M,\mathbb{P})
-
strongly generic
condition if
q
forces that
\dot{G}\cap M
is a
V
-generic filter for
\mathbb{P}\cap M
.

Note that for
q
to be
(M,\mathbb{P})
-strongly generic,
q
must force that
D^{\prime}\cap\dot{G}\cap M\neq\emptyset
for
all
dense
D^{\prime}\subseteq M\cap\mathbb{P}
with
D^{\prime}\in V
, not just ones which are the trace of some element of
M
.

In this paper, we shall be concerned with only the following two topological properties:

Definition 2.5
.

•

A space
(X,\tau)
is said to be

Lindelöf
if every open cover of
X
has a countable subcover.

•

A space
(X,\tau)
is said to be
countably tight
if for each
A\subseteq X
and
x\in X
with
x\in\operatorname{cl}(A)
, there is a countable
B\subseteq A
so that
x\in\operatorname{cl}(B)
.

We next review the definition of preserving a topological property by a forcing:

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(X,\tau)
be a topological space and
\mathbb{P}
a poset. Fix a
V
-generic filter
G
over
\mathbb{P}
. In
V[G]
, noting that
\tau
forms a basis for a topology on
X
, let
\tau_{G}
denote the topology on
X
generated by
\tau
.

Let
\Phi
be a topological property which
X
satisfies in
V
. We say that
\mathbb{P}

preserves that
X
satisfies
\Phi
if
\mathbb{P}
forces that
(X,\tau_{\dot{G}})
satisfies
\Phi
.

We give a few toy examples:

(1)

Let
x\in X
be a point of first countability in an arbitrary space
X
. Every forcing preserves that
x
is a point of first countability, as a countable neighborhood base at
x
in
V
remains such in any generic extension (since the ground model topology is used as a base in the generic extension).

(2)

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a forcing that adds a new real. Let
Z
be the unit interval
[0,1]^{V}
in the reals,
as defined in
V
. Naturally,
Z
is a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{V}
in
V
. However, in any extension by
\mathbb{P}
,
Z
is no longer a compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{V}
. Indeed, let
r
be a new real in
V[G]
, where we assume
0<r<1
. Then the following is an open cover of
[0,1]^{V}
in the space
\mathbb{R}^{V}
in the model
V[G]
:

\left\{[0,q)^{V}:q\in\mathbb{Q}\wedge 0<q<r\right\}\cup\left\{(s,1]^{V}:s\in\mathbb{Q}\wedge r<s<1\right\}.

However, this open cover has no finite subcover. We emphasize that we are working with rational intervals from
\mathbb{R}^{V}
, but we are using the new real
r
to define the open cover.

Now we define our forcing axiom schema:

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a topological space satisfying a topological property
\Phi
. The axiom
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
asserts that for any proper poset
\mathbb{Q}
so that
\mathbb{Q}
preserves that
X
has
\Phi
and for any collection
\left\{D_{\alpha}:\alpha<\omega_{1}\right\}
of dense subsets of
\mathbb{Q}
, there is a filter
H
so that
H
meets each
D_{\alpha}
.

3.
Neeman’s Model Sequence Poset

In this section, we will review some of the main results from Section 2 of
[
20
]
in order to make the current paper reasonably self-contained. We will not be providing proofs of most of these results, and the interested reader is heartily encouraged to carefully read at least Section 2 of
[
20
]
.

We begin with the basic set-up of the model sequence poset.

Notation 3.1
.

Let
\theta
be a large enough regular cardinal. Let
\cal{S}
denote a set of countable elementary submodels of
H(\theta)
and
\cal{T}
a set of transitive elementary submodels of
H(\theta)
.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that
\cal{S}
and
\cal{T}
are
appropriate
for
H(\theta)
if

•

all elements of
\cal{S}\cup\cal{T}
are members of
H(\theta)
, and

•

if
W\in\cal{T}
,
M\in\cal{S}
, and
W\in M
, then
M\cap W
is a member of
W
and a member of
\cal{S}
.

We assume for the remainder of this section that
\cal{S}
and
\cal{T}
are appropriate for
H(\theta)
. We will review the definition of the two-type model sequence poset
\mathbb{P}_{\cal{S},\cal{T},H(\theta)}
, which we abbreviate throughout this section by
\mathbb{P}
. We first define which objects are conditions, and then after a few remarks, we define the ordering.

Definition 3.3
.

Conditions in
\mathbb{P}_{\cal{S},\cal{T},H(\theta)}
are finite
\in
-increasing sequences of elements of
\cal{S}\cup\cal{T}
which are closed under intersections.

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

To say that a sequence
\left<M_{k}:k<m\right>
is closed under intersections means that if
k,l<m
, then
M_{k}\cap M_{l}
is also on the sequence.

(2)

One of the key places where closure under intersections is used is the proof of properness for small nodes.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\left<M_{k}:k<m\right>
is a condition and
k<l<m
, then the von Neumann rank of
M_{k}
is less than that of
M_{l}
.

Consequently, there is no loss of information in talking about the set
\left\{M_{k}:k<m\right\}
whenever
\left<M_{k}:k<m\right>
is a condition in the model sequence poset. This helps to define the order:

Definition 3.6
.

Conditions in
\mathbb{P}
are ordered by reverse inclusion:
\left<N_{l}:l<n\right>\leq\left<M_{k}:k<m\right>
iff
\left\{N_{l}:l<n\right\}\supseteq\left\{M_{k}:k<m\right\}
.

It will be helpful to have a slightly weaker notion than that of a condition, and the next item takes care of this:

Definition 3.7
.

A finite sequence
\left<M_{k}:k<m\right>
of elements of
\cal{S}\cup\cal{T}
is a
precondition
if it is
\in
-increasing.

Thus conditions in
\mathbb{P}
are precisely preconditions which are closed under intersections. However, it suffices to check a seemingly weaker condition than full closure under intersections to verify that a precondition is in fact a condition:

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
s
is a precondition and that
s
satisfies the following weak closure property:

(*)

if
W
and
M
are nodes in
s
of transitive and small type respectively and if
W\in M
, then
M\cap W
is in
s
.

Then
s
is outright closed under intersections, and therefore
s
is a condition.

We now state some results about amalgamating conditions in the model sequence poset.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
s
be a condition and
Q\in s
. The
residue
of
s
to
Q
, denoted
\operatorname{res}_{Q}(s)
, is the set
\left\{M\in s:M\in Q\right\}
.

Note that if
s
is a condition, so is
\operatorname{res}_{Q}(s)
(Lemma 2.18 of
[
20
]
). It will be helpful to have the following additional terminology when discussing amalgamation arguments.

Definition 3.10
.

Two conditions
s
and
t
are
directly compatible
if the closure of
s\cup t
under intersections is a condition.

Two compatible conditions needn’t in general be directly compatible. For example, suppose that
W\in\cal{T}
and
M\in\cal{S}
where
W\in M
. Then
M\cap W
is also a model in
\cal{S}
(Definition
3.2
). Note that the conditions
\left\{M\cap W\right\}
and
\left\{M\right\}
are compatible since
\left\{M\cap W,W,M\right\}
is a condition extending both. However, the closure of
\left\{M\cap W,M\right\}
under intersections is just
\left\{M\cap W,M\right\}
again, and this is not a condition since
M\cap W\notin M
(this in turn because
M\cap W\in W
).

In fact, any condition containing
M\cap W
and
M
must add a transitive node between these two models, as otherwise, we would have a finite
\in
-chain of
countable
models from
M\cap W
to
M
, which in turn would imply that
M\cap W\in M
.

The next lemma states the amalgamation result for the case when
Q
is transitive:

Lemma 3.11
.

Suppose that
s
is a condition and that
Q\in s
is transitive. Let
t\in Q
be a condition extending
\operatorname{res}_{Q}(s)
. Then
s\cup t
is a condition.

Note in this case that we don’t need to close
s\cup t
under intersections. However, the case when
Q
is a small node is much more involved. It is here that the closure under intersections is crucial. The next item “amalgamates” Lemma 2.21 and Corollary 2.31 of
[
20
]
.

Lemma 3.12
.

Suppose that
s
is a condition and
Q\in s
a small node. Let
t\in Q
be a condition which extends
\operatorname{res}_{Q}(s)
. Then
s
and
t
are directly compatible.

Furthermore, if
r
is the closure of
s\cup t
under intersections, then

•

\operatorname{res}_{Q}(r)=t
, and

•

the small nodes of
r
outside of
Q
are all of the form
N
or
N\cap W
where
N\in s
is small and
W\in t
is transitive.

The next (rather comforting) lemma tells us that if a condition is inside a model, then we can add that model to the condition.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{S}\cup\mathcal{T}
, and let
t
be a condition in
M
. Then there is a condition
r\leq t
with
M\in r
. In fact, the closure of
t\cup\left\{M\right\}
under intersections is such an

.

The following lemma (combining Claim 5.7 and Remark 5.8 of
[
20
]
) shows that under certain conditions, any transitive node can be added to any condition. We also include a helpful claim which extracts an additional feature of the proof. We will use these facts in later sections:

Lemma 3.14
.

Suppose that
\theta
is an inaccessible cardinal, and let
\left<H(\theta);A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\right>
be an expansion of

by finitely many predicates. Suppose that
\cal{T}
consists exactly of nodes

so that

has uncountable cofinality (equivalently,

is countably closed) and so that

Suppose further that every

is elementary in
\left<H(\theta);A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\right>
. Then for any condition
s
in
\mathbb{P}_{\cal{S},\cal{T},H(\theta)}
and any node
H(\alpha)\in\cal{T}
:

(1)

there is an
r\leq s
with

, and

(2)

one can find such a condition

where
N\in s
and

.

The next lemma extracts an additional feature of the proof of Claim 5.7 and Remark 5.8 of
[
20
]
.

Lemma 3.15
.

(Under the same assumptions as Lemma
3.14
) Suppose that
s
is a condition,
H(\alpha)\in\cal{T}
, and that
s\not\subseteq H(\alpha)
. Let
M
be the least member of

not in

, and suppose that
M\neq H(\alpha)
. Then

(1)

(M\cap\theta)\setminus\alpha\neq\emptyset
;

(2)

if

3.14
so that the transitive nodes of

are exactly

and those of

;

(3)

if
\alpha\notin M
, then setting
\alpha^{*}:=\min((M\cap\theta)\setminus\alpha)
, we have that
H(\alpha^{*})
is a member of
\cal{T}

4.
Basic Features of the
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
Poset

In this section, we define a “
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
-type” poset, where
X
and
\Phi
are parameters representing, respectively, a topological space whose size is below a given supercompact and a topological property of that space. In the case when
\Phi
is either “Lindelöf” or “countably tight”, we will show in later sections that forcing with the corresponding
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
-type poset will give a model of
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
. The purpose of this section is, after giving the definition, to highlight a number of general properties of this kind of poset. Most of our work in the current section involves translating Section 6 of
[
20
]
.

Assumption 4.1
.

V
is a ground model of the
\mathsf{GCH}
,
\theta
is a supercompact cardinal in
V
, and
J:\theta\to H(\theta)
is a Laver function for
\theta
. Finally,
X
is a space of size
<\theta
satisfying the topological property
\Phi
.

The forcing which we will use to create a model of
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
(see Definition
2.7
) is a slight variation of the finite conditions poset from
[
20
]
which creates a model of

7.2.
Putting it All Together

We are now ready to prove our first main theorem:

Theorem 7.3
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, suppose that
X
is a Lindelöf space. Then the poset
\mathbb{P}_{L}(X)
, where “
L
” denotes “Lindelöf”, forces that
\mathsf{PFA}_{L}(X)
holds.

Proof.

We first note, by Lemma
4.12
, that
\omega_{1}
and all
\lambda\geq\theta
are preserved and that
\theta
becomes
\aleph_{2}
in the extension.

The main thing that we need to show is that
X
remains Lindelöf after forcing with
\mathbb{P}:=\mathbb{P}_{L}(X)
. We prove by induction on
\beta\in Z^{*}\cup\lim(Z^{*})
that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf; note that we are including
\theta
itself in
\lim(Z^{*})
.

If
\beta=\min(Z^{*})
, then
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
is strongly proper, and hence by the results from
[
7
]
, it preserves that
X
is Lindelöf.

Next, suppose that
\beta\in\lim(Z^{*})
and that the result holds for all
\alpha
below
\beta
. By Proposition
7.1
,
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf.

Now we consider the successor cases. Fix
\beta\in Z^{*}\setminus\lim(Z^{*})
, and let
\alpha:=\sup(Z^{*}\cap\beta)
. Suppose first that
\alpha\in Z^{*}
. Recalling Notation
4.8
, Proposition
5.8
implies that there is a dense embedding from
D_{\alpha,\beta}
to
(\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\alpha)\ast(\dot{\cal{M}}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}(\alpha)))
. By induction,
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\alpha
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf. There are two options for the value of
J^{\prime}(\alpha)
: on the one hand, if
J^{\prime}(\alpha)
outputs the trivial poset, then
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\alpha
forces that
\dot{\cal{M}}_{\alpha}(J^{\prime}(\alpha))
is strongly proper (since it’s isomorphic to forcing with finite
\in
-chains of countable models), and so the results from
[
7
]
imply that it preserves that
X
is Lindelöf. On the other hand, if
J^{\prime}(\alpha)=J(\alpha)
(i.e., if
J(\alpha)
is forced to be the appropriate kind of poset) then by Lemma
6.1
, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\alpha
forces that
\dot{\cal{M}}_{\alpha}(J(\alpha))
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf. In either case, on account of the dense embedding, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf.

Second, suppose that
\alpha\notin Z^{*}
. By Proposition
7.2
and by our induction hypothesis, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf.

Since the case
\beta=\theta
was already taken care of as part of the limit case, we conclude that
\mathbb{P}
preserves that
X
is Lindelöf. Since
\mathbb{P}
also preserves
\omega_{1}
and forces that
\theta
becomes
\omega_{2}
, we may now apply the usual arguments to conclude that
\mathbb{P}
forces
\mathsf{PFA}_{L}(X)
.
∎

8.2.
Putting it All Together

We can now put together the previous pieces to complete the proof of the following theorem:

Theorem 8.3
.

Under Assumption
4.1
, suppose that
X
is a countably tight space. Then the poset
\mathbb{P}_{CT}(X)
, where “
CT
” denotes “countably tight,” forces that
\mathsf{PFA}_{CT}(X)
holds.

Proof.

This proof is almost identical to the proof of Theorem
7.3
, using the analogous preservation results for “countably tight” in place of “Lindelöf”.

In a bit more detail, we prove by induction on
\beta\in Z^{*}\cup\lim(Z^{*})
that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is countably tight.

If
\beta=\min(Z^{*})
, then
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
is strongly proper, and hence by the results from
[
7
]
, it preserves that
X
is countably tight.

Next, suppose that
\beta\in\lim(Z^{*})
and that the result holds for all
\alpha
below
\beta
. By Proposition
8.1
,
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is countably tight.

Next, consider the successor case where
\beta\in Z^{*}\setminus\lim(Z^{*})
. Define
\alpha:=\sup(Z^{*}\cap\beta)
. If
\alpha\in Z^{*}
, then we apply Proposition
5.8
and Lemma
6.2
to conclude that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is countably tight. On the other hand, if
\alpha\notin Z^{*}
, then we apply Proposition
7.2
to conclude that
\mathbb{P}\upharpoonright\beta
preserves that
X
is countably tight.

Since the case
\beta=\theta
was taken care of during our discussion of the limit case, we know that
\mathbb{P}
preserves that
X
is countably tight. Just as in the proof of Theorem
7.3
, we have that
\mathbb{P}
preserves
\omega_{1}
, preserves all cardinals at least
\theta
, and forces that
\theta
becomes
\aleph_{2}
. And finally, the usual arguments show that
\mathbb{P}
forces
\mathsf{PFA}_{CT}(X)
.
∎

9.
Comments on Applications and Questions

In follow-up work, we will discuss applications of these forcing axioms in more detail. For now, we make the following remark: any consequence
\Psi
of
\mathsf{PFA}
which can be forced by a strongly proper forcing is a consequence of the two
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
axioms discussed here. This is on account of the results from
[
7
]
that strongly proper posets preserve the Lindelöf property and preserve countably tight spaces.

The following “spectrum” question seems to be the most natural place to start:

Question 9.1
.

Let
\Phi
be either “Lindelöf” or “countably tight”. What are the consequences of
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
as we vary the space
X
?

The following contains some specific instances of the previous question:

Question 9.2
.

With
\Phi
as in the previous question, for which
X
does
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
imply the open graph axiom? The
P
-ideal dichotomy? The failure of square principles?

Another natural line of questions is the following:

Question 9.3
.

For which other topological properties
\Phi
and spaces
X
satisfying
\Phi
can we prove the consistency of
\mathsf{PFA}_{\Phi}(X)
? For instance, what if
X
is a Rothberger space? A Menger space? Or if
X
satisfies strategic versions of these covering principles?

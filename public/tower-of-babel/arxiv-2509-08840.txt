Abstract
We investigate the relation between cosmological time dilation (CTD), photon–baryon decoupling, and the Hubble tension in the minimally extended varying–speed–of–light (meVSL) framework. The generalized cosmological time (GCT) framework distinguishes cross-epoch temporal standardization from local interaction laws. For the diagnostic early-Universe calculation, the hydrogen recombination history is taken to be GR-like, while the homogeneous meVSL opacity prescription retains the correlated b b dependence of the dimensional Thomson cross section. This background scaling of the dimensional Thomson cross section does not imply that the locally measured dimensionless fine-structure constant α \alpha varies with cosmological epoch. Keeping the rapid variation of the free-electron fraction X e ​ ( z ) X_{e}(z) in the decoupling condition gives the local representation z ∗ ≃ 1090 − 315 ​ b z_{\ast}\simeq 1090-315b over the range relevant here. The baryon drag optical depth is determined by the Thomson momentum-transfer rate κ ˙ / R \dot{\kappa}/R , with R = 3 ​ ρ b / ( 4 ​ ρ γ ) R=3\rho_{b}/(4\rho_{\gamma}) , and accumulates from the present epoch to redshift z z . Positive b b then shifts the drag epoch slightly downward in redshift. For example, b = 0.03 b=0.03 gives z drag ≃ 1052.2 z_{\rm drag}\simeq 1052.2 and r drag ≃ 147.84 ​ Mpc r_{\rm drag}\simeq 147.84~\mathrm{Mpc} , compared with r drag ≃ 147.15 ​ Mpc r_{\rm drag}\simeq 147.15~\mathrm{Mpc} at b = 0 b=0 in the same calculation. The drag-epoch sound horizon remains nearly unchanged and does not yield the several-percent reduction required for a sound-horizon explanation of the Hubble tension. 3.1.1
Sound horizon from BAO

The comoving size of the sound horizon at the drag epoch (

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{\textrm{drag}}\equiv\int_{0}^{t_{\textrm{drag}}}\frac{\tilde{c}_{s}(t)}{a(t)}\,dt=\int_{0}^{a_{\textrm{drag}}}\frac{\tilde{c}_{s}(a)}{a^{2}\tilde{H}(a)}\,da=\int_{z_{\textrm{drag}}}^{\infty}\frac{\tilde{c}_{s}(z)}{\tilde{H}(z)}\,dz\,,

(8)

i.e.

For definiteness, the early-Universe calculation considered here takes the hydrogen recombination history to be GR-like,
X_{e}(z,b)=X_{e}^{\textrm{GR}}(z)

b

For the baryon–photon plasma, we use the meVSL sound-speed prescription

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{s}^{2}

4.1
Proper-time propagation and GCT population standardization

The geometrical redshift retains its standard Robertson–Walker definition,

1+z=\frac{a_{\textrm{obs}}}{a_{\textrm{emit}}}.

(28)

For two neighboring wave crests, or any same-event-to-same-event comparison along the comoving congruence, the proper-time dilation remains

\frac{\Delta\tau_{\textrm{obs}}}{\Delta\tau_{\textrm{emit}}}=1+z.

(29)

Thus a homogeneous lapse by itself does not modify the same-event proper-time propagation law.

The CTD observable considered here instead adopts a cross-epoch population-standardization hypothesis. Along the same comoving congruence let

d\tau=\mathcal{N}_{G}\,d\mathcal{T}_{G},\qquad\mathcal{N}_{G}=(U^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{T}_{G})^{-1},

(30)

where
\mathcal{T}_{G}
is homogeneous on each cosmological hypersurface. For the CTD application considered here, standardized intrinsic durations are compared in this common temporal representation. With
\mathcal{N}_{G}=N(a)=a^{b/4}
,
a_{\textrm{obs}}=1
, and
N_{\textrm{obs}}=1
, the population-level duration ratio is

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\mathcal{T}_{G,\textrm{obs}}}{\Delta\mathcal{T}_{G,\textrm{emit}}}

\displaystyle=(1+z)\frac{N_{\textrm{emit}}}{N_{\textrm{obs}}}=(1+z)^{1-b/4}.

(31)

Consequently, a phenomenological fit

\Delta t_{\textrm{obs}}=(1+z)^{n}\Delta t_{\textrm{emit}}

(32)

maps to

n=1-\frac{b}{4}

(33)

under the GCT population-standardization hypothesis. Equation (
33
) should be tested jointly with source evolution and selection effects; it is not a replacement for the proper-time result in Eq. (
29
).

4.3
DES-calibrated forecast for future CTD tests

A useful forecast can be constructed without assigning a toy per-object duration error or assuming that all population effects are independent. We instead calibrate the effective information directly to the published DES
i
-band CTD result, which used
N_{0}=1465
SNe Ia and obtained a statistical uncertainty
\sigma_{n,0}=0.008
on the exponent
[
31
]
. This choice is deliberately conservative for testing persistence of the
i

F_{n}(N)=F_{n,0}\frac{N}{N_{0}},\qquad F_{n,0}=\sigma_{n,0}^{-2},

(36)

so that

\sigma_{n,\textrm{stat}}(N)=\sigma_{n,0}\sqrt{\frac{N_{0}}{N}}.

(37)

This is an empirical information-scaling forecast, not a substitute for a full population likelihood.

To illustrate the requirement, suppose that the current
i

n_{\star}=0.988,\qquad\delta_{n}\equiv|n_{\star}-1|=0.012,\qquad b_{\star}=4\delta_{n}=0.048.

(38)

\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}

\sigma_{n}^{2}(N)=\sigma_{n,\textrm{stat}}^{2}(N)+\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}^{2}.

(39)

\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}
should be understood as the component that does not average down as

S(N)=\frac{\delta_{n}}{\sigma_{n}(N)}.

(40)

Solving for the sample size required for a target significance
S

N_{\textrm{req}}(S)=\frac{N_{0}\sigma_{n,0}^{2}}{\left(\delta_{n}/S\right)^{2}-\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}^{2}},\qquad\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}<\frac{\delta_{n}}{S}.

(41)

If the inequality in Eq. (
41

S_{\infty}=\delta_{n}/\sigma_{n,\textrm{sys}}
for a nonzero residual floor.

5
Conclusion

We have investigated the relation between cosmological time dilation and the Hubble tension in the meVSL framework, with the temporal interpretation formulated through generalized cosmological time (GCT). The homogeneous temporal scaling leaves the same-event proper-time law
\Delta\tau_{\textrm{obs}}/\Delta\tau_{\textrm{emit}}=1+z
unchanged, while the relation
n=1-b/4
follows conditionally when intrinsic durations are standardized across epochs in GCT. The baryon drag epoch, by contrast, is determined by local photon–baryon momentum transfer and is governed by the Thomson drag weighting
1/R
.

Keeping the rapid
X_{e}(z)
dependence in the photon-decoupling condition gives only a mild response of
z_{\ast}
to
b
, locally represented by
z_{\ast}\simeq 1090-315b
over the range studied here. Positive
b
also moves
z_{\textrm{drag}}
slightly downward. For a GR-like recombination history with the specified meVSL Thomson-cross-section scaling, the resulting sound horizon remains close to its standard value and increases slightly, from about
147.15
Mpc at
b=0
to
147.84
Mpc at
b=0.03
in the simplified calculation.

The early-Universe calculation therefore does not provide the several-percent reduction of
r_{\textrm{drag}}
required for a sound-horizon resolution of the Hubble tension. A simple inverse-ruler scaling moves
H_{0}
slightly downward rather than upward, although a precision cosmological constraint requires a full Boltzmann and likelihood analysis. CTD remains an independent empirical test of GCT cross-epoch temporal standardization and need not track the behavior of the early-Universe acoustic ruler.

A precision treatment should implement any physical
b
dependence of atomic microphysics self-consistently in a HyRec/CLASS-type calculation and analyze transient CTD with an explicit source-evolution and covariance model. These observables test different aspects of the framework and can be confronted with data separately before a combined interpretation is attempted.

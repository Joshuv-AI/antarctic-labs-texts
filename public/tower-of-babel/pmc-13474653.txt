Cluster bagging modifies flavonoid profiles in teinturier grape Garnacha Tintorera: Tissue-specific responses under different cultivation environments
Authors: Ming-Yu Li, Hui-Qing Li, Jia-Yi Deng, Hao-Cheng Lu, Meng-Bo Tian, Ning Shi, Jia-Yan Wei, Fei He, Keji Yu, Yi-Bin Lan, Chang-Qing Duan, Jun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Light plays a crucial role in grape berry ripening and flavor compounds metabolism. Garnacha Tintorera, a teinturier grape cultivar, accumulates flavonoids in both skin and pulp, exhibiting distinct tissue-specific characteristics. To investigate light effects on flavonoid accumulation across different tissues and growing environments, a three-year (2018–2020) cluster bagging experiment was conducted from pre-veraison to harvest in the open-field vineyard and greenhouse. Bagging significantly reduced anthocyanins and flavonol accumulation in the skin, while no consistent response pattern was observed in the pulp, and flavanol accumulation was only marginally affected. Cluster bagging reduced the proportion of F3’5’H-derived anthocyanins across all tissues. Flavonols showed a consistent result only in the pulp, whereas flavanols exhibited an opposing but relatively weak trend. Light-responsive biomarkers in the skin varied between cultivation environments, whereas pulp biomarkers remained consistent. The study revealed tissue-specific flavonoid responses to light exclusion, providing insights for optimizing teinturier grape cultivation.

Introduction

Flavonoids are crucial secondary metabolites in grape berries, recognized as quality-determining compounds in grapes and wines due to their contributions to color, flavor, texture, and astringency (Rienth et al., 2021). The flavonoid compounds in grape berries are predominantly composed of anthocyanins, flavonols, and flavanols. Anthocyanins are the primary compounds responsible for grape berry and wine color, and their type, content, and proportion are associated with color characteristics (Shi et al., 2024a). Flavonols function as co-pigments of anthocyanin in red wines, contributing to color stabilization, and also act as photoprotectants to reduce UV damage in grape berries (Li et al., 2025a). Flavanols are the basic building blocks of grape tannins and contribute to the bitter and astringent properties of wines (Koyama et al., 2017).

The concentration and composition of flavonoids are regulated by genetic factors and are also influenced by environmental variables, including light, temperature, water, soil properties, and cultivation practices (Shi et al., 2024b; Vilanova et al., 2015). Among these factors, light is a critical environmental regulator, directly modulating grape berry development and flavonoid biosynthesis (Li et al., 2024). Studies demonstrate that shading and low-light conditions can delay grape berry maturation (Ghiglieno et al., 2020; Zha et al., 2019). Flavonols are light-sensitive compounds, and light exposure promotes the expression of flavonol metabolic genes, thereby enhancing flavonol accumulation (Friedel et al., 2016). However, the effects of light on anthocyanin accumulation remain inconsistent, with some reporting that light reduced anthocyanin concentration or had no significant effect (Ristic et al., 2007; Sun et al., 2019), while others report that anthocyanin levels are predominantly regulated by non-light factors (Blancquaert et al., 2019b). Similarly, no consistent pattern has been observed for the effects of light on total flavanol accumulation (Blancquaert et al., 2019b; Lu et al., 2022). Furthermore, light also influences flavonoid composition, particularly through the differential regulation of the flavonoid 3’-hydroxylase (F3’H) and flavonoid 3’5’-hydroxylase (F3’5’H)-derived biosynthetic pathways (Guan et al., 2016; Li et al., 2024).

In viticulture, various vineyard management practices can modify light conditions and consequently affect berry metabolites, including canopy management (shoot toping, leaf removal) (Lu et al., 2023a; Torres et al., 2021), ground management (mulching, crop covering) (He et al., 2023), artificial light regulation (shading and supplemental lighting) (Li et al., 2025b; Scafidi et al., 2013), etc. Bagging treatment is a widely used protective field practice that can reduce mechanical damage and protect berries from pathogens, pests, and birds. In addition, bagging effectively alters fruit light exposure and can reduce the risk of skin sunburn (Ma et al., 2023; Pisciotta et al., 2020). The effect of bagging on grape berries depends on multiple factors, including bag material, timing and duration of bagging, external environmental conditions, and the phenological stage at which the bags are applied (He et al., 2022; Luca et al., 2023). Due to differences in light transmittance properties among bag materials, bagging provides an effective experimental approach for directly investigating the specific effects of light on fruit composition.

In most red grape cultivars, anthocyanins predominantly accumulate in the berry skin. However, teinturier grapes are capable of accumulating anthocyanins in both the skin and pulp (He et al., 2010a), resulting in deeper color and higher total anthocyanin levels compared to non-teinturier cultivars (Tian et al., 2021). Teinturier grapes contain similar flavonoid compounds in the skin and pulp tissues, but the proportions of certain anthocyanins are tissue-specific. The skin contains more trihydroxy-substituted anthocyanins, whereas the dihydroxy-substituted anthocyanins are enriched in the pulp (He et al., 2010b). In addition to anthocyanins, the pulp of teinturier grapes can also accumulate flavonols and flavanols (Chen et al., 2018). Therefore, teinturier grapes are primarily used to enhance color intensity and improve wine quality, exhibiting great potential for wine blending (Wang et al., 2019).

Garnacha Tintorera (Vitis vinifera L.), also known as ‘Alicante Bouschet’, is a teinturier grape cultivar and is primarily used for color enhancement in red wine blends (Falginella et al., 2012). Although the flavonoid composition of grape berries has been extensively studied, the tissue-specific flavonoid profiles of the skin and pulp in Garnacha Tintorera remain insufficiently characterized. Moreover, the effects of light on flavonoid accumulation and composition in different berry tissues remain poorly understood, particularly whether the pulp exhibits light-responsive patterns similar to those observed in the skin. Furthermore, it remains unknown whether these tissue-specific light responses are consistent under contrasting cultivation environments. In this study, bagging treatment was applied to Garnacha Tintorera grape clusters from the pre-veraison to harvest under two contrasting light environments (open-field vineyard and greenhouse) to modify fruit light conditions over three consecutive vintages (2018–2020). The study aimed to investigate the effect of cluster bagging on flavonoid accumulation and composition in the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera grapes, identify tissue-specific light-responsive flavonoid changes, and characterize flavonoid photoresponses under different growing conditions. By integrating tissue-specific analyses across contrasting cultivation environments and multiple vintages, this study provides new insights into the photoregulation of flavonoid accumulation in teinturier grape cultivars.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Sodium hydroxide, hydrochloric acid, ascorbic acid, acetone, phloroglucinol and sodium acetate were analytical grade and obtained from Tianjin Chemical Factory (Tianjin, China). Methanol, formic acid, and acetonitrile were HPLC grade and obtained from Honeywell (Charlotte, North Carolina, USA). Standards of flavonoids: malvidin-3-O-glucoside chloride, myricetin-3-O-galactoside, quercetin-3-O-glucoside, kaempferol-3-O-galactoside, syringetin-3-O-glucoside, isorhamnetin-3-O-glucoside, (+)-catechin, (-)-epicatechin, (-)-gallocatechin, (-)-epigallocatechin, and (-)-epicatechin gallate, with a purity of ≥95% were supplied from Sigma-Aldrich (St. Louis, Missouri, USA).

Plant materials and experimental design

The three-year experiment (2018–2020) was performed at Shangzhuang Experimental Station of China Agricultural University (40°14’N, 116°19’E, elevation 50 m), Beijing, China. The climate of the experimental field is a warm temperate semi-humid monsoon climate with loam soil. The Garnacha Tintorera grapevines were planted in the open field vineyard and glass greenhouse (with 80% light transmittance). For the open field vineyard, the Garnacha Tintorera vines were planted in 2014, oriented north-south with row and vine spacing of 2.5 m × 1.2 m. The grapevines were trained to the modified vertical shoot positioning (M-VSP) trellis system with 12–15 nodes per meter. For the greenhouse, the Garnacha Tintorera vines were planted in 2014, oriented north-south and trained to a high trunk (about 2.2 m) ‘T’ type horizontal trellis system with two bunches per shoot, with row and vine spacing of 4.0 m × 2.5 m. The cordons of the vines were extended in the east-west direction with about 80 nodes per vine. The management practices, including irrigation, nutrition, and pest control were carried out according to the local standards.

The bagging treatment was performed in both the vineyard and greenhouse after the collection of grapes at E-L 33 and continued until ripening, with untreated grapes serving as controls. The individual grape cluster was encased in a dual-layer kraft paper bag (38 cm × 26 cm), with a yellow outer layer and a black inner layer coated with wax. A straw was fixed underneath the bag to ensure ventilation. Fully exposed, non-bagged clusters throughout the experiment served as the control group. Both bagged and control clusters were selected from comparable canopy positions on vines with similar growth vigor and were located at similar heights above the ground to minimize potential canopy position effects. For each treatment, 90 clusters were randomly selected from both sides of the canopy with three biological replicates. Healthy berries from both bagged and control treatments were sampled at the following stages: green berries (E-L 33), veraison initiation (E-L 35), medium maturity (E-L 36), berries not fully ripe (E-L 37), and harvest (E-L 38). All the sampling dates are shown in Supplementary Table 1. For each replicate, 200 berries were randomly sampled from each sampling point. 100 berries were randomly selected for physicochemical analysis, and the remaining samples were immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored in a - 80 °C refrigerator for subsequent analysis.

Meteorological and microclimate data

For the meteorological data, the temperature (°C), growing degree days (GDD, °C), sunlight hours (h), and rainfall (mm) during the growing season were obtained from the China Meteorological Data Sharing Service System (http://cdc.cma.gov.cn/). For the microclimate, we installed solar radiation (SR) sensors (S-LIB-M003, Onset, USA), photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) sensors (S-LIA-M003, Onset, USA), and temperature-humidity sensors (S-THB-M002, Onset, USA) to monitor the microclimate of the grape cluster area. The microclimate stations were located under the canopy of the grape clusters, with the heads of the sensor probes placed pointing upwards. For the treatment group, the microclimate station was wrapped in the same bag as the grape clusters to detect microclimate changes inside the bags. An identical microclimate station located at the same height but without the bag served as the control group. Meteorological data were recorded at 15-min intervals using a HOBO micro station (H21-002, Onset, USA).

Determination of berry physicochemical parameters

For each replicate, 100 berries were randomly selected and weighed, and then the berries were pressed to obtain juice. The total soluble solids (TSS, °Brix) were detected using a digital hand-held refractometer (PAL-1, Atago, Japan), and the pH of the juice was determined by a pH meter (Sartorius PB-10, Germany). The titratable acidity (TA, g/L) of the juice was titrated using 0.05 mol/L NaOH, with pH 8.2 as the endpoint, and was expressed as tartaric acid equivalents.

Extraction and analysis of flavonoids in grapes

For each replicate, the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera grapes were separated under liquid nitrogen and then pulverized into powder and lyophilized under vacuum at -40 °C. The extraction of anthocyanins and flavonols in grape skin and pulp was according to a previous study (Downey et al., 2007). The dried powder (0.100 g) was accurately weighed into a 2 mL centrifuge tube and mixed with 1.0 mL of 50% (v/v) methanol aqueous solution. After shaking well, the methanol extract was sonicated in the dark for 20 min and centrifuged at 13,300 × g for 10 min. The supernatant was collected and the residue was extracted again, then the supernatant was combined and shaken well.

The flavanols in the skin and pulp were extracted following a previous study (Liang et al., 2012). For free flavanols, 0.100 g dried powder was accurately weighed and mixed with 1.0 mL of 70% (v/v) aqueous acetone solution containing 0.5% (w/v) ascorbic acid, shaken for 15 min, and centrifuged at 5,910 × g for 15 min. The supernatant was collected and the residue was extracted twice, then the supernatant was combined and mixed thoroughly. Then 400 μL of the supernatant was rapidly blown dry with nitrogen in the dark, and the dried sample was reconstituted in 200 μL of 1% HCl methanol solution and then neutralized with 200 μL of 200 mM aqueous sodium acetate. For the flavanol units, 0.050 g of dried powder was mixed with 0.5 mL of phloroglucinol buffer (50 g/L phloroglucinol, 0.5% (w/v) ascorbic acid, and 300 mM HCl in methanol). After incubation at 50 °C for 20 min, 0.5 mL of 200 mM sodium acetate was added to terminate the reaction. The extract was centrifuged at 5910 × g for 15 min, and then the supernatant was collected. The residue was extracted twice and the supernatant was combined.

Before analysis, all extracts were filtered through a 0.22 μm nylon membrane (MEMBRANA, Germany). Flavonoid compounds in grapes were analyzed using high-performance liquid chromatography/triple-quadrupole tandem mass spectrometry (HPLC-QqQ-MS/MS). An Agilent 1200 series HPLC system equipped with a Poroshell 120 EC-C18 column (150 × 2.1 mm, 2.7 μm) and an Agilent 6410 QqQ instrument equipped with an electrospray ionization (ESI) source were used. The detailed HPLC procedures and MS conditions were according to the previous study (Li et al., 2017), with some parameters modified. Anthocyanins were quantified using malvidin-3-O-glucoside chloride, flavonols were quantified using myricetin-3-O-galactoside, quercetin-3-O-glucoside, kaempferol-3-O-galactoside, syringetin-3-O-glucoside, and isorhamnetin-3-O-glucoside, flavanols were quantified using (+)-catechin, (-)-epicatechin, (-)-gallocatechin, (-)-epigallocatechin, and (-)-epicatechin gallate. The qualitative and quantitative information on identified grape flavonoids is shown in Supplementary Table 2. The concentration of all flavonoids was expressed as mg/kg FW (fresh berry weight).

Statistical analysis

Data processing was performed using Microsoft Excel 2019. One-way and two-way ANOVA (Duncan’s multiple range test) were performed using SPSS 26 (IBM, USA), t-test was performed using Microsoft Excel 2019. PCA and OPLS-DA were performed using SIMCA 14.1 (Umetrics, Umea, Sweden) software, and figures were prepared using Origin 2025 (OriginLab, Northampton, USA).

Results

Meteorological conditions and microclimate

The climate conditions at the experimental site in 2018–2020 are shown in Supplementary Table 3. In 2018, the temperature and GDD were higher than those in 2019 and 2020, but the sunlight hours showed an opposite pattern. The precipitation was mainly concentrated in July and August, with 2018 exhibiting much higher rainfall (up to 510.9 mm) than the other two years. The high precipitation in 2018 resulted in a relatively extended veraison period for vineyard-grown grape berries. While 2019 exhibited the highest sunshine hours and lowest precipitation, indicating relatively drier conditions compared with the other two years.

The microclimate of the vineyard and greenhouse is shown in Fig. 1. Cluster bagging completely excluded SR and PAR, resulting in complete exclusion of light within the bags. In the vineyard, the temperature ranged from 19.8 to 31.9 °C outside the bags, and it varied between 20.3 and 33.7 °C inside the bags. In the greenhouse, the temperature outside and inside the bag was 22.4–33.2 °C and 22.7–32.9 °C, respectively, providing better insulation compared to the vineyard. This also led to a pronounced advancement in phenological development in greenhouse-grown grapes. In the vineyard, the temperature inside the bag was generally higher than outside during most observation periods, whereas this pattern was not observed in the greenhouse. Humidity ranged from 34.1% to 100%, with no significant differences between experimental sites and treatments. Additionally, for phenological phases, the bagging treatment resulted in delayed grape development compared to the control, while the harvest date remained largely unaffected. In summary, the bagging treatments effectively block sunlight exposure while causing relatively limited changes in other microclimate parameters of grape bunches.Fig. 1Microclimate changes of different treatments during the growing season in the vineyard and greenhouse. Physicochemical parameters of grapes

The physicochemical parameters of the grapes in different treatments are presented in Fig. 2. At harvest, the grapes grown in the vineyard exhibited higher berry weight than the greenhouse-grown grapes, and the bagged grapes showed lower berry weight than the control in 2018 and 2020. For control grapes in the vineyard, the 100-berry weight in 2019 was significantly lower than in the other two years. Bagged grape berries exhibited higher TSS than those outside the bags during veraison, but the difference became less pronounced at harvest with a few exceptions. The pH increased consistently throughout development, with bagged grapes exhibiting higher pH values than control groups. The TA in all grape berries peaked at E-L 35 and then declined as the fruit matured. Additionally, bagged grapes exhibited lower TA than the control grapes, with more pronounced differences from E-L 35 to E-L 37, while these differences decreased at harvest. PCA separated samples according to developmental stages, and the loading plot indicated that flavanols were the predominant contributors at early developmental stages, while the content of anthocyanins and flavonols increased as the berry developed. The concentration of flavonols increased continuously with the development of the berries, as shown in Fig. 4, Fig. 5. At harvest, bagging significantly reduced skin flavonol levels compared to the control group, while no substantial differences were observed in the pulp. In the 2019 vineyard, significant differences in flavonol concentrations were observed since the beginning of bagging. At harvest, Garnacha Tintorera berries exhibited higher flavonol concentrations in the pulp compared to the skin.

Flavanols showed an overall decreasing trend during berry development, with the highest concentration observed at E-L 33. Bagging treatment had limited effects on flavanol accumulation in both the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera berries across developmental stages, and the differences between bagged and control berries became smaller at harvest. The flavanols in the skin showed high variability in concentration across years, with lower levels in 2018 than in the other two years, and the highest concentration observed in 2019. Nevertheless, pulp flavanols only showed lower concentrations at harvest in 2018 than in the other two vintages. Additionally, skin flavanols maintained higher levels than pulp throughout the developmental period in both 2019 and 2020, but this pattern was only observed at harvest in 2018.

Influence of bagging treatment on flavonoid composition of Garnacha Tintorera berries

To investigate the effect of bagging treatment on the concentration and composition of flavonoids in Garnacha Tintorera grapes at harvest, a two-way ANOVA was performed, as shown in Supplementary Table 7. A total of 20 anthocyanins were detected in Garnacha Tintorera grapes, which were categorized into five aglycones: cyanidins (Cy), delphinidins (Dp), peonidins (Pn), petunidins (Pt), and malvidins (Mv) based on their anthocyanidin unit (He et al., 2010a). Among the derivatives of anthocyanins, Mv derivatives were the most abundant components in the skin of Garnacha Tintorera, followed by Pn derivatives, while Cy derivatives showed relatively lower concentrations at harvest. In the pulp, the concentration of Pn derivatives was the highest, followed by Mv derivatives, while Dp and Pt derivatives only accounted for a small portion. As shown in Supplementary Table 7, both the vintage and bagging treatment affected the concentration of skin and pulp anthocyanin derivatives. In general, bagging treatment reduced the concentration of Dp, Pt, and Mv derivatives except for the skin of the 2018 vineyard, while Pn derivatives increased after bagging. Cy derivatives showed inconsistent results across vintages and tissues, and no significant differences were observed between control and bagging treatments in the vineyard. In addition, the concentration of anthocyanins in the F3’5’H pathway significantly decreased after bagging, whereas anthocyanins derived from the F3’H pathway showed the opposite response. Bagging treatment also altered the compositional ratio of anthocyanins. After bagging, both the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera exhibited a decreased proportion of Mv derivatives and an increased proportion of Pn derivatives, with minimal changes in other derivatives (Fig. 6a). Based on the biosynthetic pathway and molecular structure, flavonols were classified as follows: myricetin (My), laricitrin (La), syringetin (Sy) derived from the F3’5’H pathway, quercetin (Qu), isorhamnetin (Is) derived from the F3’H pathway, and kaempferol (Ka) derived from the flavanone 3-hydroxylase (F3H) pathway. My and Qu derivatives were the predominant flavonol components, with myricetin-3-O-galactoside, myricetin-3-O-glucoside, and quercetin-3-O-glucoside exhibiting significantly higher concentrations than other flavonol compounds. Tissue-specific differences in flavonol composition were reflected in the relative proportions of La and Is derivatives, with higher La derivative proportions in the skin and greater Is derivatives in the pulp. Both the concentration and proportion of Qu and Is derivatives increased after bagging, accompanied by an increased contribution of the F3’H-derived flavonol branch in the pulp, whereas other derivatives showed an opposite trend. However, the results differed between vineyard and greenhouse in the skin.

Six flavanol monomers (catechin (C), epicatechin (EC), gallocatechin (GC), epigallocatechin (EGC), epicatechin-3-O-gallate (ECG), and epigallocatechin-3-O-gallate (EGCG)) were detected in three forms: free monomers, terminal subunits and extension subunits. Catechin was the predominant component of flavanols, accounting for more than 90% of the total flavanol concentration. Compared with anthocyanins and flavonols, bagging had relatively limited effects on flavanols, mainly resulting in reduced concentrations of GC and EGC in the pulp and EGCG in the skin. Regarding the synthesis pathway, flavanols showed a slight decrease in the F3’H pathway contribution and a corresponding increase in the F3’5’H pathway, exhibiting a trend opposite to that observed in anthocyanins and flavonols.

Characteristic flavonoids with light-responsive in Garnacha Tintorera skin and pulp

To identify the light-responsive flavonoid compounds in the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera, orthogonal partial least square with discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed to analyze the flavonoid datasets of Garnacha Tintorera grapes at harvest in the vineyard and greenhouse, as shown in Fig. 6b. The result showed that bagging treatment and control samples could be well distinguished from each other, with compounds exhibiting VIP > 1.0 identified as light-responsive biomarkers (Supplementary Table 8), and were further visualized via UpSet plot (Fig. 6c) to identify condition-specific metabolites.

Anthocyanins and flavonols were the primary light-responsive compounds, while among flavanols, only GC and EGC exhibited light-dependent accumulation in the pulp. In the skin, light-responsive biomarkers exhibited distinct environment-dependent patterns, with flavonols predominating in the vineyard and anthocyanins in the greenhouse. Consequently, biomarker profiles differed markedly between the two experimental sites in the skin, unlike the essentially identical biomarkers observed in the pulp. My-gluc, Qu-gala, Qu-rut, La-gluc, and Pn-ace were identified as skin-specific biomarkers in both the vineyard and greenhouse, and the four flavonol markers exhibited decreased concentrations after bagging, whereas Pn-ace increased. GC, EGC, F3’H-flavanols, F3’5’H-flavonols, and Cy-caf were biomarkers specific to the pulp, all of which decreased following bagging treatment. Furthermore, distinct biomarkers were observed between different cultivation sites, Ka-glu, Qu-glu, Pn-glu, Kas, and F3H-flavonols were specific to vineyard-grown grapes, while My-gala and Dp-coum were characteristic of greenhouse-grown grapes. Notably, My-glu, Sy-glu, and Sys consistently responded to bagging treatment across experimental sites and tissues, with all showing decreased concentrations after bagging.

Correlation analysis of light indicators on the flavonoids in the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera berries

To investigate the effect of light on the flavonoids of Garnacha Tintorera skin and pulp, correlation analysis was performed on two light indicators, PAR and SR (Fig. 7). The flavonoids that significantly correlated with SR and PAR were primarily composed of anthocyanins and flavonols. Anthocyanins derived from the F3’5’H pathway were positively correlated with PAR and SR across all experimental sites and tissues, with their concentrations decreasing under bagging treatment. Whereas Pn derivatives and F3’H-derived anthocyanins showed a negative correlation with light indicators (except for pulp in the greenhouse). For flavonols, Is derivatives in both the skin and pulp were negatively correlated with PAR and SR. Additionally, in the pulp, Qus and F3’H-derived flavonols were also negatively correlated with PAR and SR, indicating enhanced accumulation of the F3’H-derived flavonols under light-exclusion conditions. In contrast, most other flavonols exhibited reduced concentrations under bagging treatment and showed a positive correlation with light parameters. Discussion

Bagging altered the cluster microclimate and physicochemical parameters of Garnacha Tintorera grapes

Bagging treatments influence grape berry growth and quality by regulating the microclimate of clusters, especially light exposure (Zhou et al., 2020). Bags of different materials and colors exhibit distinct light transmittance properties, thereby differentially influencing grape berries (Wang et al., 2022). In the present study, bagging treatment effectively induced the interception of SR and PAR, maintaining a light-free environment within the bags. Although bagging also influenced the microclimatic conditions within the bags, including temperature and relative humidity, the observed variations were relatively limited and not significant compared with the pronounced reduction in light availability. These results suggested that the primary environmental change induced by bagging was the reduction in light exposure, although the potential contribution of other bagging-associated microclimatic factors can not be completely excluded. The changes in light conditions could contribute to variations in the ripening process of the grapes, and previous studies have shown that shading could delay grape ripening (Miccichè et al., 2023). In the present study, bagging treatment resulted in a postponement of veraison, but did not significantly affect the final berry ripeness. Furthermore, all samples were collected at the same phenological stages, thereby minimizing the potential influence of developmental differences in berry developmental status.

In 2018 and 2020, bagging treatment reduced the berry weight at harvest, although no consistent pattern was observed before the ripening stage. Significant differences in TSS, pH, and TA were observed between bagged and control berries before ripening, but these differences diminished at harvest. Bagging treatment delayed veraison in Garnacha Tintorera grapes, consequently resulting in a relatively higher TSS and lower TA during veraison, consistent with the previous study (Li et al., 2024). Other studies that compared bagged and control berries at the same chronological stage reported that bagged berries exhibited lower TSS and higher TA in bagged berries (Nan et al., 2024; Scafidi et al., 2013), further supporting that bagging treatment delayed grape ripening to some extent.

Differential accumulation of flavonoids in Garnacha Tintorera grape tissues under distinct cultivation environments after bagging

The overall accumulation patterns of flavonoids in Garnacha Tintorera grapes were consistent across different cultivation environments, anthocyanin and flavonol concentrations increased during berry development, and flavanol concentrations gradually decreased, aligning with previous studies (Li et al., 2024; Lu et al., 2025). However, the accumulation of anthocyanins showed distinct tissue-specific patterns in Garnacha Tintorera grapes, with pulp showing rapid accumulation after early veraison, while skin anthocyanins increased significantly until mid-veraison. Similar tissue-specific anthocyanin accumulation dynamics were reported in teinturier grape Yan 73, in which anthocyanin biosynthesis occurred initially in the pulp before the skin (Xie et al., 2015). Most teinturier grape cultivars accumulate higher concentrations of anthocyanins, flavonols, and flavanols in the skin than in the pulp (Chen et al., 2018; Lu et al., 2023b). In contrast, Garnacha Tintorera grapes exhibited higher anthocyanin and flavonol accumulation in the pulp than in the skin, whereas flavanol concentrations were higher in the skin, consistent with previous reports.

Bagging treatment consistently reduced anthocyanin and flavonol accumulation in berry skin across vintages and environments, consistent with previous studies (Li et al., 2024; Sun et al., 2020; Zha et al., 2019). In the pulp, bagging showed inconsistent effects, possibly because the internal position of the pulp made it naturally shielded from direct sunlight regardless of bagging, leading to a more complex effect of regulation. For flavanols, bagging treatment exhibited limited effects in both skin and pulp, indicating a relatively limited responsiveness to changes in light conditions, consistent with the previous study (Valentini et al., 2019).

Compared to vineyard-grown grapes, greenhouse-grown grapes grown accumulated higher anthocyanin concentrations in the berry skin, and bagging treatment exhibited a more pronounced anthocyanin reduction effect. Previous studies have shown that a smaller diurnal temperature range can promote anthocyanin accumulation in grape berries (Cohen et al., 2008). Compared with vineyards, greenhouses provided better thermal insulation with smaller diurnal temperature differences, which might partially explain the higher anthocyanin content observed in greenhouse-grown grapes. In addition, skin flavonols exhibited a stronger response to bagging in the vineyard compared to the greenhouse. Flavonoid compounds exhibit different responsiveness to light induction, with flavonols being the most light-responsive because of their light-dependent and strong radical scavenging properties (Reshef et al., 2018). Compared to vineyards, the greenhouse exhibited different light intensities and spectral compositions because the glass covering of greenhouses filtered out portions of the UV and visible spectra, resulting in a generally weaker light environment. Bagging treatment reduced light parameters more drastically in vineyards, which might lead to a more pronounced reduction of flavonols.

Differential responses of flavonoids to bagging treatment in the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera grapes

As a teinturier grape cultivar, Garnacha Tintorera accumulated flavonoids in both the skin and pulp, although with distinct compositional profiles. The difference was mainly reflected in anthocyanin composition—the skin was dominated by trihydroxy-substituted anthocyanins, whereas the pulp predominantly contained dihydroxy-substituted anthocyanins, consistent with prior reports (He et al., 2010b; Kong et al., 2021). In the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway, F3H, F3’H, and F3’5’H regulate the hydroxylation patterns on the B ring (He et al., 2010a). Tissue-specific accumulation of dihydroxylated and trihydroxylated anthocyanins was mainly determined by the differential expression of F3’H and F3’5’H. Similar to Garnacha Tintorera, Gamay Fréaux showed consistent anthocyanin partitioning patterns, and F3’5’H expression predominated over F3’H in the skin, whereas the inverse pattern occurred in the pulp, explaining the tissue-specific anthocyanin distribution (Kong et al., 2021).

Climate has a significant influence on flavonoid composition in grapes, with light as a key climatic factor, playing a pivotal role in shaping the flavonoid profiles (Bavougian et al., 2013; Blancquaert et al., 2019a). Light intensity, quality, and duration all affect the flavonoid metabolism in grape berries (Blancquaert et al., 2019a; Sun et al., 2024). In the present study, bagging treatment was applied to block light exposure to grape berries, creating a light-deprived microenvironment inside the bags. The results indicated that the composition and concentration of flavonoid compounds were influenced by light exposure, exhibiting differential responses. Under bagging treatment, the proportion of dihydroxy-substituted anthocyanins increased in both the skin and pulp of Garnacha Tintorera after bagging, indicating that light had a significant impact on the hydroxyl substitution of anthocyanins. Similar increases in the ratio of dihydroxylated to trihydroxylated anthocyanins under shading have been reported previously (Guan et al., 2016; Ristic et al., 2007), consistent with our results. These results indicated that the F3’H- and F3’5’H-derived anthocyanin branches differed in their sensitivity to light. The altered hydroxylation pattern of anthocyanins observed after bagging treatment might result from changes in F3’H and F3’5’H enzyme activities. Koyama, Ikeda, Poudel and Goto-Yamamoto, 2012, Koyama and Goto Yamamoto, 2008 reported that shading downregulated F3’5’H expression and reduced the F3’5’H/F3’H transcript ratio in shaded berries during grape development.

Flavonols are highly light-sensitive compounds and are widely considered reliable indicators for assessing the exposure of grapes to solar radiation (Martínez-Lüscher et al., 2019). Different flavonols exhibited distinct responses to light, though most showed positive correlations with light parameters, consistent with previous findings (Quartacci et al., 2022; Tian et al., 2023). Martínez Lüscher et al. (2014) revealed that UV-B exposure changed the flavonol profile of grapes, with an increase in the proportion of disubstituted derivatives and a decrease in the proportion of trisubstituted forms. Similarly, in the present study, bagging increased the concentrations of Is and Qu derivatives in the pulp, resulting in a negative correlation between F3’H-derived flavonols and light parameters. Compared with anthocyanins and flavonols, flavanols exhibited weaker correlations with light parameters, and fewer light-responsive flavanol biomarkers were identified, suggesting a relatively minor photoregulatory effect on flavanol accumulation.

From a practical perspective, the reduction of skin anthocyanins and flavonols, together with the altered flavonoid composition observed under bagging conditions, may influence the potential contribution of Garnacha Tintorera to wine color and flavonoid-related quality attributes. However, bagging may provide benefits under specific cultivation conditions by reducing excessive light exposure and protecting berries from environmental stresses. Therefore, bagging strategies should be optimized according to cultivation conditions and production objectives.

Conclusion

In this study, the effects of cluster bagging treatment on flavonoids in different tissues of teinturier grape Garnacha Tintorera under contrasting cultivation environments were evaluated. Our results demonstrated that bagging treatment significantly delayed veraison progression but had no significant effect on the harvest date. Anthocyanins and flavonols exhibited strong light-dependent regulation, while flavanols were relatively less responsive to light. The concentrations of total anthocyanins and flavonols in the skin decreased after the bagging treatment, while no consistent pattern was observed in the pulp. Cluster bagging consistently reduced the proportion of F3’5’H-derived anthocyanins while increasing F3’H-derived anthocyanins in different tissues. Flavonols showed such a pattern only in the pulp, and the skin flavonols showed environment-dependent responses, whereas flavanols exhibited opposite results, though with minimal impact. In the skin, light-responsive biomarkers were predominantly flavonols in the vineyard, whereas anthocyanins dominated in the greenhouse, while pulp biomarkers remained relatively consistent across cultivation environments. Most flavonoids showed positive correlations with light partners SR and PAR, while negative correlations were observed for specific compounds: Pn derivatives, F3’H-anthocyanins, Is derivatives, and ECG in both skin and pulp, as well as Qu derivatives and F3’H-flavonols in the pulp. The study revealed the effects of cluster bagging on tissue-specific flavonoid profiles in Garnacha Tintorera grapes across contrasting environments, providing valuable references for optimized cultivation and utilization of teinturier grapes.

Optimal Dirac controls for time-periodic bistable ODEs, application to population replacement
Grégoire Nadin, David Nahmani, Nicolas Vauchelet

Abstract
This work addresses an optimal control problem on a dynamics governed by a nonlinear differential equation with a bistable time-periodic nonlinearity. This problem, relevant in population dynamics, models the strategy of replacing a population of A A -type individuals by a population of B B -type individuals in a time-varying environment, focusing on the evolution of the proportion of B B -type individuals among the whole population. The control term accounts for the instant release of B B -type individuals. Our main goal, after noting some interesting properties on the differential equation, is to determine the optimal time at which this release should be operated to ensure population replacement while minimizing the release effort. The results establish that the optimal release time appears to be the minimizer of a function involving the carrying capacity of the environment and the threshold periodic solution of the dynamics; they also describe the convergence of the whole optimal release strategy. An application to the biocontrol of mosquito populations using Wolbachia -infected individuals illustrates the relevance of the theoretical results. Wolbachia is a bacterium that helps preventing the transmission of some viruses from mosquitoes to humans, making the optimization of Wolbachia propagation in a mosquito population a crucial issue. Keywords: Bistable differential equation, time-periodic differential equation, optimal control, optimization, population replacement, Wolbachia MSC: 34H05, 92D25, 49J15

1.1
Biological motivation

The mathematical study of the present article was inspired by a population dynamics problem relevant in ecology. Assume that we want to replace a population of
A
-type individuals of any living species by a population of
B
-type individuals of the same species. This operation is called
population replacement
and can be achieved releasing
B
-type individuals among the initial
A
-type population. For the sake of simplicity in the study, we only consider variations in time, and assume that this whole problem is observed at a given location, therefore the space-dependency is ignored in this paper.

Our objective is then to mathematically optimize this strategy, namely to determine when and how
B
-type individuals should be released, such that the replacement eventually occurs, and, above all, in the most economic way, considering that this process involves a financial cost associated with breeding and releases logistics.

First, we will stick to this general optimization problem, in order to come up with mathematical results with the largest possible scope. Then, further in the paper, we will detail an application to a model coming from a specific public health issue: a biocontrol strategy for mosquito populations.

The proportion of
B
-type individuals in the whole population at time
t
is denoted
p(t)
and follows an intrinsic dynamics governed by the nonlinear differential equation
p^{\prime}=f(t,p)
.
We assume that the nonlinearity periodically depends on time, to take the seasonal variations of the habitat into account. Moreover, we consider nonlinearities of bistable type with respect to
p
. This assumption, which arises naturally in modeling population dynamics (see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
31
]
), takes the competition between the two populations into consideration (see
[
32
]
) and induces a negative growth rate at low densities,
\textit{i}.\textit{e}.
a strong Allee effect (see
[
34
]
).

To know about controllability of bistable – more general – reaction-diffusion partial differential equations, involving space dependency, the reader can refer to
[
1
]
and
[
29
]
.

We add to this dynamics a control part of the form
u(t)w(t,p)
, where
u(t)
denotes the released
B
-type population at time
t
, and
w
is a function that encompasses the time-dependency of the habitat and models the weight, depending on the value of
p
, of this control part on the overall equation.

Gathering the intrinsic dynamics and the control part, we study a control equation in which
f
and
w
satisfy some minimal hypotheses. The goal of this paper is to get information on the optimal released population in order to minimize a certain loss function.

Several mathematical works have been carried out on the optimization of such systems, and more specifically on models describing mosquito population dynamics. In all of them, the hosting capacity of the environment is assumed to be constant through time.

The work
[
7
]
studies the maximization of the proportion of
B
-type individuals at a fixed horizon of time, the total amount of released individuals through time being bounded.
The same type of problems is spatially addressed in
[
22
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
16
]
.
The article
[
3
]
studies the minimization of the total amount of released individuals such that the critical equilibrium of the differential equation is reached at the final time, considering that the number of released individuals at a given time is bounded. In
[
4
]
, the authors focus, with an epidemiological model, on the transmission of diseases from mosquitoes to humans, and intend to minimize the total amount of infected humans.
Finally, the paper
[
18
]
considers a system of two equations, and deals with the minimization of the time of intervention and the total amount of released individuals, such that the system gets to the good basin of attraction.

The main mathematical result obtained in
[
7
]
, and later in
[
3
]
, is that the optimal release function is bang-bang, and equal to its upper bound at the beginning of the time window, for as long as needed to reach the stated objective, and then equal to 0.

In the present work, we not only establish general results that are applicable to other periodic bistable phenomena, but we also consider that the hosting capacity is time-dependent, which adds a complexity to the problem.

To simplify the work, the profiles of the releases will be limited to scaled indicator functions, and asymptotically, to instant impulses. We will note further in the paper that this simplification has a biological relevance.

1.3
Optimal control problem

The control strategy we intend to study is population replacement, in other words replacing a population of
A
-type individuals by a population of
B
-type individuals. We will here aim the success of this strategy, with a mathematical point of view.

Let
p(t)
denote the proportion of
B
-type individuals and
K(t)
the environmental carrying capacity at time
t
. To analyze mathematically the success of population replacement strategy, let us investigate here a control equation, where one acts on the population, releasing a time-distributed amount of
B
-type individuals

.

Control equation.

We assume for now, the adequation with applications being detailed later, that this control equation is:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle p^{\prime}(t)=f\bigl(t,p(t)\bigr)+\frac{u(t)}{K(t)}g\bigl(p(t)\bigr)\quad\text{in $(0,+\infty)$},\\
p(0)=p_{0},\end{cases}

(2)

with
f
satisfying the hypotheses (H
1.2
) to (H
1.2
),
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following hypothesis:

(H5)

g
is Lipschitz continuous, decreasing,
g(0)=1
and
g(1)=0
,

and
K:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the following hypothesis:

(H6)

K
is continuous and
T
-periodic in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Moreover,
\displaystyle\min_{[0,T]}K>0
.

Indeed, since
K
stands for the largest amount of population that the environment can host, given available resources, it seems relevant to assume that it depends on the season of the year. This is why

is assumed to be a
T
-periodic function.

Since the control

can be discontinuous, the right-hand side is not continuous with respect to

2
) needs to be read in the sense of Carathéodory. In this sense, the function

is said to be a solution of (

is absolutely continuous and satisfies for all

,

p(t)=p_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl(f\bigl(s,p(s)\bigr)+\frac{u(s)}{K(s)}g\bigl(p(s)\bigr)\Bigr)\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, all the considered controls belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, therefore (
2
) has a unique solution

.

Form of the control term.

Given a total amount
C
of available
B
-type individuals, one wants to release them all at once, in other words, one considers a release taking the form of the largest Dirac impulse allowed, operated at a certain time
t_{0}>0
. The study of Dirac controls mathematically allows us to understand the behavior of solutions and consists in a first step towards studying more diverse forms of control terms.

In order to approach Dirac controls, let us first study controls defined as follows: there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varepsilon\ll 1
and the control term writes, for all
t\geqslant 0
,

u^{\varepsilon}(t)=\displaystyle\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{1}_{[t_{0}-\varepsilon,t_{0}]}(t).

As mentionned in
[
4
]
,
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
, this form of control term is relevant since releases are often operated for a short time with respect to the time window considered.
Moreover, the numerical simulations that we will expose further in the paper will show that limiting the theoretical study to scaled indicator functions, and asymptotically to Dirac impulses, is a natural choice.

Let
p^{\varepsilon}
be the solution (in the sense of Carathéodory) of the control equation associated with the control

:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle(p^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(t)=f\bigl(t,p^{\varepsilon}(t)\bigr)+\frac{u^{\varepsilon}(t)}{K(t)}g\bigl(p^{\varepsilon}(t)\bigr)\quad\text{in $(0,+\infty)$},\\
p^{\varepsilon}(0)=p_{0}.\end{cases}

(3)

The optimal control problem that we want to address is the following, denoting
S:=1/\varepsilon
:

\boxed{\begin{gathered}\text{Find the time $t_{0}$ at which the release should be operated}\\
\text{in order to ensure that the effort $C$ is minimal and to}\\
\text{guarantee population replacement in problem \eqref{controlsyst_g_wo_K_eps}.}\end{gathered}}

(
\mathcal{P}_{S}
)

The reader notes that the objective function to minimize is the total amount

of released individuals such that any greater amount “guarantees population replacement”,
\textit{i}.\textit{e}.
the solution of the control equation converges towards 1, whereas the works
[
7
]
and
[
16
]

In this paper, we tackle the issue of only steering the state to the good basin of attraction, to let the dynamics ensure by itself the convergence to 1. This question is closer to the ones addressed in
[
3
]
and
[
18
]
.

The mosquito

is one of the major vectors for transmitting dengue fever, chikungunya, Zika fever and yellow fever to the human being. None of those diseases can be fought with a specific treatment; moreover, there is no vaccine for Zika, and vaccines for chikungunya and dengue are only suitable for specific population groups (see

Scientists have been studying the endosymbiotic bacterium

It is therefore crucial to design release programs that pursue the goal of achieving the long-term persistence of

Many mathematical works (see

In this section, we explain how we can, from the models given by population dynamics, get to a scalar control equation that meets with theoretical results proven in the previous sections.

Note that another well-known vector control strategy is the sterile insect technique and that some mathematical works (see

).
Furthermore, one has, for all
(t,p)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1],\;\partial_{p}f^{\eta}(t,p)=\displaystyle\partial_{p}f^{0}(t,p)+\eta\bigl(f^{1}\bigr)^{\prime}(p)
.
Setting
\Delta:=\left\lVert\bigl(f^{1}\bigr)^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\infty}
, which is finite since
\bigl(f^{1}\bigr)^{\prime}
is continuous on [0,1], it yields, for all
(t,p)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0,1],\;\bigl|\partial_{p}f^{0}(t,p)-\partial_{p}f^{\eta}(t,p)\bigr|\leqslant\Delta\eta
. Denoting
\omega(\eta):=\Delta\eta
, one gets that
f^{0}
and the family
\bigl(f^{\eta}\bigr)_{\eta>0}
satisfy (
6
).

Therefore, by Proposition
6
, for
\eta
small enough, the ODE
p^{\prime}=f^{\eta}(t,p)
has a unique periodic solution between 0 and 1, which is not attractive.

Speed of convergence.

Proposition 6.2 in
[
14
]
provides the existence of a constant
C_{1}
such that for any
\eta>0
, one has
\left\lVert P^{0}-P^{\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}<C_{1}\Delta\eta
, where
P^{0}
and
P^{\eta}
respectively denote the Poincaré map associated with
f^{0}
and
f^{\eta}
.
Setting
\Phi:=P-\mathrm{Id}
, one immediately gets
\left\lVert\Phi^{0}-\Phi^{\eta}\right\rVert_{\infty}<C_{1}\Delta\eta
.

Therefore, for all
\gamma>0
, one gets:

-C_{1}\Delta\eta<\Phi^{0}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)-\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)<C_{1}\Delta\eta,

hence

\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)>\Phi^{0}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)-C_{1}\Delta\eta.

The reader notes that the function
\Phi^{0}
has the same regularity as the function
f^{0}
by a generalization of Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem, therefore, in this application,
\Phi^{0}
is at least
\mathcal{C}^{2}\bigl([0,1]\bigr)
. Hence, for
\eta
small enough, by Taylor remainder theorem, there exists
\theta_{\eta}^{+}\in\left(\bar{\theta}_{d},\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)
such that:

\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)>\Phi^{0}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)+\gamma\eta(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)+\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta^{2}}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})-C_{1}\Delta\eta=\eta\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})+\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)-C_{1}\Delta\right),

since
p\equiv\bar{\theta}_{d}
is an equilibrium state of
p^{\prime}=f^{0}(t,p)
.

Set
\gamma>\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}\Delta}{(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{d})}
, this lower bound being positive since
(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{d})=\exp\left(\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{p}f^{0}(t,\bar{\theta}_{d})\mathrm{d}t\right)-1>0
.

If
(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})\geqslant 0
, then for all
\eta>0,\;\eta\left(\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})+\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)-C_{1}\Delta\right)>0
. If
(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})<0
, let us take
0<\eta<\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)-C_{1}\Delta}{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\max_{[\bar{\theta}_{d},1]}\left|(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}\right|}
,
it gives
0<\eta<\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}\Delta-\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)}{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})}
, and we infer
\eta\left(\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{+})+\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)-C_{1}\Delta\right)>0
.

Finally,
\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)>0
for
\eta
small enough.

Similarly, for
\eta
small enough, by Taylor remainder theorem, there exists
\theta_{\eta}^{-}\in\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}-\gamma\eta,\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)
such that:

\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}-\gamma\eta\right)<\eta\left(\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{-})-\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)+C_{1}\Delta\right).

If
(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{-})\leqslant 0
, for all
\eta>0,\;\eta\left(\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{-})-\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)+C_{1}\Delta\right)<0.

If
(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{-})>0
, taking
0<\eta<\displaystyle\frac{\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)-C_{1}\Delta}{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\max_{[0,\bar{\theta}_{d}]}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}}
, we get
\eta\left(\displaystyle\frac{\gamma^{2}\eta}{2}(\Phi^{0})^{\prime\prime}(\theta_{\eta}^{-})-\gamma(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}\right)+C_{1}\Delta\right)<0
.

Finally,
\Phi^{\eta}\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}-\gamma\eta\right)<0
for
\eta
small enough.

We can now deduce, by continuity of
\Phi^{\eta}
, the existence of a real number
\theta_{\eta}\in\left(\bar{\theta}_{d}-\gamma\eta\;,\;\bar{\theta}_{d}+\gamma\eta\right)
such that
\Phi^{\eta}(\theta_{\eta})=0
,
\textit{i}.\textit{e}.
such that the solution of
p^{\prime}=f^{\eta}(t,p)
starting at
\theta_{\eta}
is a
T
-periodic solution, denoted
p_{\eta}^{T}
.

By the proof of Proposition 6.2 in
[
14
]
, one has, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{T}_{\eta}(t)|=|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{\eta}(t;\theta_{\eta})|\leqslant\displaystyle C_{1}\Delta\eta.

(21)

Moreover, considering
\eta
small enough and
t\in[0,T]
, one has

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\left|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{0}(t;\bar{\theta}_{d})\right|&=&\left|\theta_{\eta}+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f^{0}\left(s,p^{0}(s;\theta_{\eta})\right)\mathrm{d}s-\bar{\theta}_{d}-\int_{0}^{t}f^{0}\left(s,p^{0}(s;\bar{\theta}_{d})\right)\mathrm{d}s\right|\\
&\leqslant&|\theta_{\eta}-\bar{\theta}_{d}|+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left|f^{0}\left(s,p^{0}(s;\theta_{\eta})\right)-f^{0}\left(s,p^{0}(s;\bar{\theta}_{d})\right)\right|\mathrm{d}s\\
&\leqslant&|\theta_{\eta}-\bar{\theta}_{d}|+\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\partial_{p}f^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}\left|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{0}(t;\bar{\theta}_{d})\right|\mathrm{d}s.\end{array}

by the mean value theorem.

Therefore, Grönwall lemma implies, for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\bigl|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{0}(t;\bar{\theta}_{d})\bigr|\leqslant|\theta_{\eta}-\bar{\theta}_{d}|\exp\left(\left\lVert\partial_{p}f^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}t\right)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}\Delta}{(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\eta\exp\left(\left\lVert\partial_{p}f^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}T\right).

(22)

Finally, one has, for
\eta
small enough and for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\bigl|p^{T}_{\eta}(t)-\bar{\theta}_{d}\bigr|\leqslant\bigl|p^{T}_{\eta}(t)-p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})\bigr|+\bigl|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-\bar{\theta}_{d}\bigr|=\bigl|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{T}_{\eta}(t)\bigr|+\bigl|p^{0}(t;\theta_{\eta})-p^{0}(t;\bar{\theta}_{d})\bigr|,

that is, by (
21
) and (
22
),

\bigl|p^{T}_{\eta}(t)-\bar{\theta}_{d}\bigr|\leqslant C_{1}\Delta\eta+\frac{C_{1}\Delta}{(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\eta\exp\left(\left\lVert\partial_{p}f^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}T\right)=C_{4}\Delta\eta,

where
C_{4}:=C_{1}\left(1+\displaystyle\frac{\exp\left(\left\lVert\partial_{p}f^{0}\right\rVert_{\infty}T\right)}{(\Phi^{0})^{\prime}(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\right)
.

∎

4.4
Conclusions on the minimization problem in the general case

In the general case, one has
d_{1}=d_{2}-\eta
, with
\eta>0
. For a given
\eta
, let us denote
p^{T}_{\eta}
the maximal
T
-periodic solution strictly between 0 and 1.

Proposition 10
.

Assume that the function
K
satisfies (H
4.1.1
). For a given
\eta>0
, denote
p^{T}_{\eta}
the maximal
T
-periodic solution strictly between 0 and 1, and
t_{\eta}^{*}
a minimizer of the function
K(\cdot)G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\cdot)\bigr)
. This minimizer satisfies the two following assertions:

(i)

The sequence
\left(t_{\eta}^{*}\right)_{\eta>0}
converges, up to an extraction, to a minimizer of
K
in
[0,T]
, denoted
\tau_{i}^{*}
, when
\eta\to 0
.

(ii)

Assume moreover that
K^{\prime\prime}
is positive at each one of the minimizers of
K
. Up to the same extraction as in
(i)
, one has
|t_{\eta}^{*}-\tau_{i}^{*}|\underset{\eta\to 0}{=}O(\sqrt{\eta})
.

The interpretation of
(i)
is the following: the best time to release the whole amount of infected
mosquitoes can become as close as we want to a time when the carrying capacity
K
is minimal, as soon as
d_{2}-d_{1}
is small enough. A characterization of the speed of convergence is given in
(ii)
.

Proof.

(i)

The sequence of minimizers
\left(t^{*}_{\eta}\right)_{\eta>0}
is bounded, therefore it converges, up to an extraction, towards a limit denoted
t_{0}^{*}
.
And Lemma
2
implies that
p^{T}_{\eta}
pointwise converges to
\bar{\theta}_{d}
when
\eta\to 0
.

For all
t\in[0,T]
, the inequality
K(t)G\bigl(p^{T}_{\eta}(t)\bigr)\geqslant K(t^{*}_{\eta})G\bigl(p^{T}_{\eta}(t^{*}_{\eta})\bigr)
implies, making
\eta
go to 0,
K(t)G(\bar{\theta}_{d})\geqslant K(t^{*}_{0})G(\bar{\theta}_{d})
, that is, since
G
is positive,
t^{*}_{0}
is a minimizer of
K
.

(ii)

As
K^{\prime\prime}
is positive at each one of its minimizers, each minimizer is isolated. Borel-Lebesgue theorem allows us to prove that
K
has a finite number of minimizers.

Let us now denote
\left(\tau_{i}^{*}\right)_{i\in I}
the finite family of minimizers of
K
.
Let us introduce the bounded function
q
such that for all
t\in[0,T],\;p_{\eta}^{T}(t)=\bar{\theta}_{d}+q(t)\eta
, and set
M_{q}:=\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{\infty}
.

Since for all
i\in I,\;K^{\prime\prime}(\tau_{i}^{*})>0
, by continuity, there exists
\zeta>0
such that for all
i\in I
,
K^{\prime\prime}>0
in
[\tau_{i}^{*}-\zeta,\tau_{i}^{*}+\zeta]
. Denote
K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}:=\displaystyle\min_{i\in I}\min_{[\tau_{i}^{*}-\zeta,\tau_{i}^{*}+\zeta]}K^{\prime\prime}>0

Set
C_{5}>\displaystyle 2\displaystyle\frac{M_{q}}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})G(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\frac{\min_{[0,T]}K}{K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}}
. For
\eta>0
, one has

1+C_{5}\eta\displaystyle\frac{K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}}{\displaystyle\min_{[0,T]}K}>1+2\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})G(\bar{\theta}_{d})}.

Denoting, for
x>0,\;h(x):=\displaystyle\frac{1+\frac{M_{q}x}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}}{1-\frac{M_{q}x}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}}
, a first order Taylor expansion at 0 gives
h(x)\underset{x\to 0}{=}1+2\displaystyle\frac{M_{q}x}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}+o(x)
, therefore for
\eta
small enough, one then gets

1+C_{5}\eta\displaystyle\frac{K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}}{\displaystyle\min_{[0,T]}K}>h\left(\frac{\eta}{G(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\right)=\displaystyle\frac{G(\bar{\theta}_{d})+\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}}{G(\bar{\theta}_{d})-\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}},

which implies
\displaystyle\min_{[0,T]}K+C_{5}\eta K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}>\min_{[0,T]}K\displaystyle\frac{G(\bar{\theta}_{d})+\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}}{G(\bar{\theta}_{d})-\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}}
.

Hence, one has, for
\eta
small enough,

\Bigl(\min_{[0,T]}K+C_{5}\eta K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}\Bigr)\left(G(\bar{\theta}_{d})-\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\right)>\min_{[0,T]}K\left(G(\bar{\theta}_{d})+\frac{M_{q}\eta}{g(\bar{\theta}_{d})}\right),

which gives, even if it means choosing
\eta
smaller, by a first order Taylor expansion at 0,

\Bigl(\min_{[0,T]}K+C_{5}\eta K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}\Bigr)\;G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\bigr)>\min_{[0,T]}K\times G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}+M_{q}\eta\bigr).

(23)

Set
\nu:=\sqrt{2C_{5}}>0
.
Consider
\eta
which makes (
23
) true, and satisfying
\nu\sqrt{\eta}<\zeta
, and denote
\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}:=[\tau_{i}^{*}-\nu\sqrt{\eta},\tau_{i}^{*}+\nu\sqrt{\eta}]
.

Even if it means choosing
\eta
smaller, for all
t\notin\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
, one has
K(t)\geqslant\displaystyle\min_{i\in I}\;\min\Bigl(K(\tau_{i}^{*}-\nu\sqrt{\eta}),K(\tau_{i}^{*}+\nu\sqrt{\eta})\Bigr)
, thus by Taylor remainder theorem, there exists
i_{0}\in I
and
\xi_{i_{0}}\in(\tau_{i_{0}}^{*}-\nu\sqrt{\eta},\tau_{i_{0}}^{*}+\nu\sqrt{\eta})
such that

K(t)\geqslant K(\tau_{i_{0}}^{*})+\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{2}\eta}{2}K^{\prime\prime}(\xi_{i_{0}})\geqslant K(\tau_{i_{0}}^{*})+\displaystyle\frac{\nu^{2}\eta}{2}K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}=\min_{[0,T]}K+C_{5}\eta K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}.

Hence, by (
23
), one obtains, for all
t\notin\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
:

K(t)G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\bigr)\geqslant\Bigl(\min_{[0,T]}K+C_{5}\eta K^{\prime\prime}_{\zeta}\Bigr)\;G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\bigr)>\min_{[0,T]}K\times G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}+M_{q}\eta\bigr).

Furthermore,
\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\leqslant p_{\eta}^{T}(\cdot)\leqslant\bar{\theta}_{d}+M_{q}\eta
gives
G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\bigr)\leqslant G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\cdot)\bigr)\leqslant G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}+M_{q}\eta\bigr)
, which yields, for all
i\in I
,

K(t)G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(t)\big)\geqslant K(t)\;G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}-M_{q}\eta\bigr)>\min_{[0,T]}K\times G\bigl(\bar{\theta}_{d}+M_{q}\eta\bigr)\geqslant K(\tau_{i}^{*})G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\tau_{i}^{*})\bigr).

To conclude, for
\eta
small enough, one gets, for all
t\notin\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
,

K(t)G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(t)\bigr)>K(\tau_{i}^{*})G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\tau_{i}^{*})\bigr)
.
Denoting
\tau^{*}
the element of the family
\left(\tau_{i}^{*}\right)_{i\in I}
such that
G\left(p_{\eta}^{T}(\tau^{*})\right)=\displaystyle\min_{i\in I}\Bigl[G\left(p_{\eta}^{T}(\tau_{i}^{*})\right)\Bigr]
, one has, for
\eta
small enough, and for all
t\notin\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
,
K(t)G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(t)\bigr)>K(\tau^{*})G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\tau^{*})\bigr)
.

Therefore, any minimizer
t_{\eta}^{*}
of
K(\cdot)G\bigl(p_{\eta}^{T}(\cdot)\bigr)
in
[0,T]
belongs to
\displaystyle\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
.
Finally, given a converging subsequence
\bigl(t_{\sigma(\eta)}^{*}\bigr)_{\eta>0}
, there exists
i\in I
such that all the elements of the subsequence eventually belong to
\mathcal{V}_{\eta,i}
, which ends the proof.

∎

4.5
Numerical simulations

In this subsection, we implement an optimal control problem that aims to compute the control that minimizes its integral over time, remaining lower than a fixed upper bound, and allowing the solution to converge towards 1.
We use the package Gekko for Python (see
[
10
]
), that relies on IPOPT (Interior Point OPTimizer) as a main solver for large-scale non-linear optimization problems,
via
the optimization environment APMonitor.

Domain.

Denoting
T
the time period of the environmental carrying capacity, we set a time window of observation
[0,t_{f}]
such that
t_{f}=NT
, with
N\geqslant 2
an integer (
t_{f}
could simply be greater than
T
).

Numerical discretization.

The control equation is implemented through an explicit Euler method,
and the integral of
u
is computed with a composite trapezoidal rule.

Implemented functions.

To make things simple, the carrying capacity, which is
T
-periodic and two times differentiable, is modeled as a cosine function:

K(t)=K_{0}+A\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{T}t\right),

with
K_{0}
the mean value and
A
the magnitude, such that
K_{0}>A>0
.

The implemented expressions of
f
and
g
are given by (
20
).

Constraints.

We set an upper bound for
u
, denoted
M
.
The control
u(\cdot)
is set as constantly equal to 0 in
[T,t_{f}]
, to guarantee that the control occurs before
t=T
.
Moreover, in order to get a solution
p
converging to 1, we implement the constraint
p(2T)>p(T)
. Let us prove that this condition is sufficient.

We consider a situation where
the
T
-periodic solution of the ODE (
1
) strictly between 0 and 1 is unique
, which is the case when the Poincaré map has the form plotted in Figure
1
; let us denote it
p^{T}
.

As
u(\cdot)=0
in
[T,t_{f}]
, the solution
p
of the control equation (
2
) satisfies the ODE (
1
) in
[T,t_{f}]
.
Like in the proof of Proposition
3
, let us consider
P_{T}
the Poincaré map shifted at time
T
, defined, for all
p_{0,T}\in[0,1]
, by
P_{T}(p_{0,T}):=p(T+T;p_{0,T})
, with
p(t;p_{0,T})
denoting, for all
t\in[T,t_{f}]
, the evaluation at
t
of the solution
p
of (
1
) in
[T,t_{f}]
satisfying the condition
p(T)=p_{0,T}
.
Let us also denote
\Phi_{T}:=P_{T}-\mathrm{Id}
.
Note that the condition
p(2T)>p(T)
can be expressed as
\Phi_{T}\bigl(p(T)\bigr)>0
.
By uniqueness of
p^{T}
, we infer that
p(T)
belongs to the interval
(\theta_{T},1)
, with
\theta_{T}:=p^{T}(T)
, which gives, by Proposition
3
, that
p
would converge to 1, if it was defined in
[0,+\infty)
.

Objective.

One wants to minimize the integral
\int_{0}^{t_{f}}u(t)\mathrm{d}t
, given the aforementioned constraints.

Results.

The numerical value of the optimal control
u
is plotted in Figure
2
for three different values of the upper bound:
M=0.02,\,0.04,\,0.06
. Moreover, the resulting value of
C
is displayed, it is approximately equal to 0.01742, which appears, as expected, to be significantly close to 0.01739, the minimum of the function
K\times G\circ p^{T}
numerically obtained by a gradient descent. In Figure
3
, the numerical approximation of the corresponding proportion
p
, solution of the control equation (
2
) with the computed optimal control
u
, is plotted.

The optimal control
u
obtained by this algorithm happens to be a short impulse with the form of a scaled indicator function showing a plateau at its upper bound
M
. It means that the optimal control is also the one of shortest duration. The instant of this optimal appears to be quite close to the minimizer of the function
K
in the interval
[0,T]
, as established in Proposition
10
.

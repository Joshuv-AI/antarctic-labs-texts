On the 1-leg Donaldson-Thomas ℤ 2 × ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} -vertex
Yijie Lin

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of restricted pyramid configurations for computing the 1-leg Donaldson-Thomas ℤ 2 × ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} -vertex. We study a special type of restricted pyramid configurations with the prescribed 1-leg partitions, and find one unique class of them satisfying the symmetric interlacing property. This leads us to obtain an explicit formula for a class of 1-leg Donaldson-Thomas ℤ 2 × ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} -vertex through establishing its connection with 1-leg Donaldson-Thomas ℤ 4 \mathbb{Z}_{4} -vertex using the vertex operator methods of Okounkov-Reshetikhin-Vafa and Bryan-Young.



1.
Introduction

The topological vertex arising from the duality relating open string theory with Chern-Simons theory has attracted a lot of attention for computing invariants in enumerative geometry [
1
]. The mathematical theories for variant topological vertices have been established in several branches of counting theories such as (orbifold) Gromov-Witten theory and Donaldson-Thomas theory [
7
,
8
,
9
,
14
,
2
,
15
]. This paper is concerned with the orbifold DT topological vertex developed by Bryan, Cadman and Young [
2
]. They define the orbifold DT
G
-vertex
V^{G}_{\lambda\mu\nu}
, which is a generating function of 3D partitions asymptotic to a triple of partitions
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
colored by irreducible representations of a finite Abelian group
G
, see [
2
, Equation (3)]. It is well known from the classification of polyhedral groups that a finite Abelian subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
is either
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. The closed formula for
V_{\emptyset\emptyset\emptyset}^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}
is obtained by using the vertex operator method, while the explicit formula for
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\emptyset\emptyset}
is obtained by applying some addtional properties of the 3D combinatorial objects called pyramid partitions [
3
]. Moreover, an explicit formula for
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}_{\lambda\mu\nu}
with any triple of partitions
(\lambda,\mu,\nu)
has been derived in terms of Schur functions, see [
2
, Theorem 12]. It is natural and expected to have an explicit formula for the full DT
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vertex. But actually it is still open even for an explicit formula of the 1-leg DT
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vertex, i.e.,

V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\lambda\emptyset\emptyset}
,
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\mu\emptyset}
and

V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\emptyset\nu}
with nonempty partitions
\lambda,\mu,\nu
.

We will consider the 1-leg case of the DT
\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vertex in this paper. Now let us briefly recall the ocurrence of pyramid partitions in enumerative invariants and the proof of the explicit formula for
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\emptyset\emptyset}
by Bryan-Young. The non-commutative DT invariants of the conifold first appear in Szendroi’s definition of invariants counting framed cyclic
A
-modules [
16
]. By torus localization, he conjectures some formulas for generating functions of noncommutative DT invariants invloving the notion of pyramid partitions, which are proved by Young using the dimer shuffling algorithm [
17
] and interpreted by Nagao-Nakajima as a result of wall-crossing phenomena [
10
]. One may refer to the non-commutative DT theory in [
16
,
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
5
,
6
,
18
] for more details. The notion of pyramid partitions is refined and described as the finite set of bricks by some algebraic definition [
3
, Section 5]. Let
\mathfrak{P}
be the set of all pyramid partitions as mentioned above and
Z_{\mathfrak{P}}
be the generating function of pyramid partitions, denoted by the notation
Z_{\mathrm{pyramid}}
in [
3
, Definition 6.1]. Then the explicit formula for
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\emptyset\emptyset}
is derived by Bryan-Young through the following steps:

(\mathrm{I})

Z_{\mathfrak{P}}
is computed in two different ways. One is counting pyramid partitions along their antidiagonal slices and denote
Z_{\mathfrak{P}}
also by
Z_{\mathfrak{P+}}
in this way. The other is to count pyramid partitions along their diagonal slices and write
Z_{\mathfrak{P}}
also by
Z_{\mathfrak{P-}}
. Here
Z_{\mathfrak{P}}=Z_{\mathfrak{P+}}=Z_{\mathfrak{P-}}
.

(\mathrm{II})
Compute
Z_{\mathfrak{P-}}
directly by the vertex operator method of Okounkov-Reshetikhin-Vafa [
14
].

(\mathrm{III})
Establish a connection between
V^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}_{\emptyset\emptyset\emptyset}
and
Z_{\mathfrak{P+}}
using the vertex operator method as above together with introducing some new vertex operators such as
E_{\pm}(x)
in [
3

Actually every pyramid partition has the symmetric interlacing property of type
\emptyset

3.7
), i.e., the same interlacing properties (
3

4

Z_{\mathfrak{P+}}=Z_{\mathfrak{P-}}
in Step
(\mathrm{I})
by Remark
2.16

\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-vertex, the notion of pyramid partitions is not suitable for describing the interlacing property induced by the leg partitions. Therefore, we need a new notion of 3D combinatorial objects to describe the desired interlacing property.

We introduce a notion of restricted pyramid configurations, which are constructed from pyramid partitions by removing some finite bricks. We construct the special class of restricted pyramid configurations of antidiagonal (resp. diagonal) type
(\nu,l)

Let

(\mathfrak{I}_{1})
Establish a connection between

2.1.
Vertex operators

We briefly recall the relevant formulas for vertex operators in [
3

13

Let
\lambda\subset(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{2}
be a partition (or a 2D Young diagram) with its conjugate denoted by
\lambda^{\prime}
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\cdots)
and
\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\cdots)
be two partitions. We call
\lambda
interlaces with
\mu
, denoted by
\lambda\succ\mu
or
\mu\prec\lambda
, if the row lengths
\lambda_{i}
,
\mu_{i}
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\geq\mu_{0}\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\cdots.

Let
\lambda\succ^{\prime}\mu
or
\mu\prec^{\prime}\lambda
denote the interlacing relation
\lambda^{\prime}\succ\mu^{\prime}
if the column lengths
\lambda_{i}^{\prime}
,
\mu_{i}^{\prime}
satisfy

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{\prime}\geq\mu_{0}^{\prime}\geq\lambda_{1}^{\prime}\geq\mu_{1}^{\prime}\geq\cdots.

Any 3D Young diagram (or plane partition) has the following important property.

Lemma 2.1
.

([
3
, Definition 6])
The following are equivalent:

(i)

\lambda\succ\mu
;

(ii)

\lambda_{i}^{\prime}-\mu_{i}^{\prime}=0\mbox{ or }1
, for any
i\geq 1

(iii)

\lambda
and
\mu
are two adjacent diagonal slices of some 3D Young diagram.

Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the set of all partitions. First, we have the
\Gamma_{\pm}
-operators acting on elements of
\mathcal{Y}
.

Definition 2.2
.

([
3
, Section 3])
Let
x
be any variable. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}(x)\lambda=\sum_{\mu\prec\lambda}x^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\mu;\;\;\;\;\;\Gamma_{-}(x)\lambda=\sum_{\lambda\prec\mu}x^{|\mu|-|\lambda|}\mu;

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}^{\prime}(x)\lambda=\sum_{\mu\prec^{\prime}\lambda}x^{|\lambda|-|\mu|}\mu;\;\;\;\;\;\Gamma_{-}^{\prime}(x)\lambda=\sum_{\lambda\prec^{\prime}\mu}x^{|\mu|-|\lambda|}\mu.

They satisfy the following commutation relations.

Lemma 2.3
.

([
3
, Lemma 3.3])
Let
x
and
y
be two commuting variables, then we have

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma_{+}(x),\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}(y)\right]=1+xy,\;\;\;\;\;\left[\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}(x),\Gamma_{-}(y)\right]=1+xy;

\displaystyle\left[\Gamma_{+}(x),\Gamma_{-}(y)\right]=\frac{1}{1-xy},\;\;\;\;\;\left[\Gamma^{\prime}_{+}(x),\Gamma^{\prime}_{-}(y)\right]=\frac{1}{1-xy}.

Next, there are two type of weight operators acting on elments of
\mathcal{Y}

Definition 2.4

([

For

and

\displaystyle Q_{g}\lambda=\tilde{q}_{g}^{|\lambda|}\lambda,\;\;\;\;\;\;Q_{h}\lambda=q_{h}^{|\lambda|}\lambda,

\displaystyle Q_{h_{1}h_{2}}\lambda=q_{h_{1}}^{|\{(i,j)\in\lambda\,|\,i\equiv j(\mathrm{mod}\;2)\}|}\cdot q_{h_{2}}^{|\{(i,j)\in\lambda\,|\,i\not\equiv j(\mathrm{mod}\;2)\}|}\cdot\lambda,

Let
\alpha_{-n}
(

Definition 2.5

([

Let

Define

\displaystyle E_{\pm}(x)=\exp\left(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{x^{2k}}{k}\alpha_{\pm 2k}\right).

The following useful property follows from the definition of

\displaystyle\bigg\langle\emptyset\,\bigg|E_{-}(x)=\bigg\langle\emptyset\,\bigg|,

(2)

\displaystyle E_{+}(x)\bigg|\,\emptyset\bigg\rangle=\bigg|\,\emptyset\bigg\rangle.

And we have the following important properties for

-operators.

Lemma 2.6
.

([
3
, Lemma 7.5])
Let
x

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\pm}(x)=\Gamma_{\pm}^{\prime}(x)E_{\pm}(x),

\eta_{-\hbar-1}=\emptyset
and
\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1}(\nu;\frac{\hbar}{2})=\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1}(\nu;\frac{\hbar}{2}-1)=\rho_{2}(\nu)-\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1}(\nu;+\infty)
. This implies that
\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{-\hbar-1}(\nu)\succ\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{-\hbar-2}(\nu)
. And
\widetilde{\underline{\ell}}_{0}^{j}(\nu)-\widetilde{\underline{\ell}}_{1}^{j}(\nu)\in\{0,1\}
for any

due to the assumption
\eta_{1}\underset{\nu^{\prime}(-1)}{\prec\succ}\eta_{0}
. Then
\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{0}(\nu)\succ\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{1}(\nu)
.

With the pyramid partition
\pi=\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{k}
, we have if
-\frac{\hbar}{2}\leq k\leq 0
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\wp}}_{\pi}(\nu)_{2k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{2k}(\nu)\cap\widetilde{\underline{\pi}}_{2k}(\nu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\widetilde{\mathbf{S}}_{2k}(i,j)\,\bigg|\,0\leq i-\widehat{\varepsilon}_{2}(\nu;-k-1)<\Theta_{\{\eta_{\star}\}},\;0\leq j-\widehat{\varepsilon}_{1}(\nu;-k-1)<(\eta_{2k})_{i-\widehat{\varepsilon}_{2}(\nu;-k-1)}\right\}

which has the same 2D Young diagram as

\eta_{2k}=\widetilde{\bm{\wp}}_{\pi}(\nu)_{2k}
. The other cases are similar.
∎

&\varepsilon_{2}(\nu;+\infty)=0,\\

For the first case, we have

\rho_{1}(\nu)=0
, and hence

. Then

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu^{\prime}(t)=1,\;\;\quad\quad\quad\;\;\forall\,t<0,\\

&\nu^{\prime}(2t)=1,\;\;\quad\quad\quad\forall\;0\leq t\leq\ell-1,\\
&\nu^{\prime}(2t)=-1,\quad\quad\quad\forall\,t\geq\ell,\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 3.26
.

Now we have

Proposition 3.27
.

Let
\nu\neq\emptyset
be a partition. Then
\widetilde{\bm{\wp}}_{\pi}(\nu)=\bm{\wp}_{\pi}(\nu)
holds for any pyramid partition
\pi\in\mathfrak{P}
if and only if
\nu=(m,m-1,\cdots,2,1)
for some
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\widetilde{\bm{\wp}}_{\pi}(\nu)=\bm{\wp}_{\pi}(\nu)
for any
\pi\in\mathfrak{P}
. Since

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{\wp}}_{\pi}(\nu)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\cap\widetilde{\pi}_{k}=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)\bigcap\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\pi}_{k}\right)=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)\bigcap\pi\subseteq\pi,

\displaystyle\bm{\wp}_{\pi}(\nu)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\cap\pi_{k}=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)\bigcap\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{k}\right)=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)\bigcap\pi\subseteq\pi,

then we have

(24)

\displaystyle\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\right)\bigcap\pi=\left(\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\right)\bigcap\pi,\;\;\;\mbox{ for any $\pi\in\mathfrak{P}$.}

Let
[B]
be any brick in
\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}
. One can choose a pyramid partition
\pi
such that
[B]\in\pi
, then

[B]\in\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\right)\bigcap\pi
. Combined with (
24
), we have
[B]\in\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}
. Then we have
\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}
. Similarly, we have
\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\subseteq\bigcup\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}
. Hence

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}.

By Lemma
3.8
, we have

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{k}(\nu)\right)^{c}\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{2k}(\nu)\right)^{c}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{2k}(\nu)\right)^{c},\\
&\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\widetilde{\mathcal{S}}_{2k-1}(\nu)\right)^{c}=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{2k-1}(\nu)\right)^{c}.\end{aligned}\right.

By Lemma
3.25
, we have either

(25)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon_{1}(\nu;\flat-1)=\flat,\\
&\varepsilon_{2}(\nu;+\infty)=0,\\
&\varepsilon_{3}(\nu;+\infty)=0,\\
&\varepsilon_{4}(\nu;\flat)=\flat.\end{aligned}\right.\;\forall\,1\leq\flat\leq\rho(\nu)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu^{\prime}(2t)=1,\;0\leq t\leq\rho(\nu)-1\\
&\nu^{\prime}(2t)=-1,\;t\geq\rho(\nu)\\
&\nu^{\prime}(2t+1)=-1,\;t\geq 0\\
&\nu^{\prime}(-2t)=1,\;t\geq 1\\
&\nu^{\prime}(-2t+1)=-1,\;1\leq t\leq\rho(\nu)\\
&\nu^{\prime}(-2t+1)=1,\;t\geq\rho(\nu)+1\\
\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\nu=(2\rho(\nu)-1,2\rho(\nu)-2,\cdots,2,1)

or

(26)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\varepsilon_{1}(\nu;+\infty)=0,\\
&\varepsilon_{2}(\nu;\flat-1)=\flat,\\
&\varepsilon_{3}(\nu;\flat)=\flat,\\
&\varepsilon_{4}(\nu;+\infty)=0.\end{aligned}\right.\;\forall\,1\leq\flat\leq\rho(\nu)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu^{\prime}(2t)=-1,\;t\geq 0\\
&\nu^{\prime}(2t+1)=1,\;0\leq t\leq\rho(\nu)-1\\
&\nu^{\prime}(2t+1)=-1,\;t\geq\rho(\nu)\\
&\nu^{\prime}(-2t)=-1,\;1\leq t\leq\rho(\nu)\\
&\nu^{\prime}(-2t)=1,\,t\geq\rho(\nu)+1\\

\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\nu=(2\rho(\nu),2\rho(\nu)-1,\cdots,2,1)

where

. This implies that
\nu=(m,m-1,\cdots,2,1)
for some

.

Therefore, together with Lemma
3.21

Future work

4.6

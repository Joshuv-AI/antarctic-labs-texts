A scaling characterization of nc-rank via unbounded gradient flow
Hiroshi Hirai

Abstract
Given a tuple of n × n n\times n complex matrices 𝒜 = ( A 1 , A 2 , … , A m ) {\cal A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m}) , the linear symbolic matrix A = A 1 ​ x 1 + A 2 ​ x 2 + ⋯ + A m ​ x m A=A_{1}x_{1}+A_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+A_{m}x_{m} is nonsingular in the noncommutative sense if and only if the completely positive operators T 𝒜 ​ ( X ) = ∑ i = 1 m A i ​ X ​ A i † T_{\cal A}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}XA_{i}^{\dagger} and T 𝒜 ∗ ​ ( X ) = ∑ i = 1 m A i † ​ X ​ A i T_{\cal A}^{*}(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}^{\dagger}XA_{i} can be scaled to be doubly stochastic: For every ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 there are g , h ∈ G ​ L n ​ ( n , ℂ ) g,h\in GL_{n}(n,{\mathbb{C}}) such that ‖ T g † ​ 𝒜 ​ h ​ ( I ) − I ‖ < ϵ \|T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|<\epsilon , ‖ T g † ​ 𝒜 ​ h ∗ ​ ( I ) − I ‖ < ϵ \|T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|<\epsilon . In this paper, we show a refinement: The noncommutative corank of A A is equal to one-half of the minimum residual ‖ T g † ​ 𝒜 ​ h ​ ( I ) − I ‖ 1 + ‖ T g † ​ 𝒜 ​ h ∗ ​ ( I ) − I ‖ 1 \|T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|_{1}+\|T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|_{1} over all possible scalings g † ​ 𝒜 ​ h g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h , where ∥ ⋅ ∥ 1 \|\cdot\|_{1} is the trace norm. To show this, we interpret the residuals as gradients of a convex function on symmetric space G ​ L ​ ( n , ℂ ) / U n GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})/U_{n} , and establish a general duality relation of the minimum gradient-norm of a lower-unbounded convex function f f on G ​ L ​ ( n , ℂ ) / U n GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})/U_{n} with an invariant Finsler metric, by utilizing the unbounded gradient flow of f f at infinity.

Introduction

Given an
m
tuple of
n\times n
matrices
{\cal A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m})
over field
{\mathbb{K}}
,

Edmonds’ problem

[
8
]

asks to compute the rank of the matrix

A=A_{1}x_{1}+A_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+A_{m}x_{m}

(1.1)

for indeterminates
x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}
,
where the rank is considered in the rational function field
{\mathbb{K}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m})
.
A deterministic polynomial time algorithm for Edmonds’ problem is not known, and
is one of the prominent open problems in discrete mathematics
and theoretical computer science; see e.g.,
[
19
,
23
]
.
Recently, a noncommutative version of Edmonds’ problem was introduced
by Ivanyos, Qiao, and Subrahmanyam
[
17
]
and is bringing about new developments.
In this setting,
the indeterminates are noncommutative
x_{i}x_{j}\neq x_{j}x_{i}
, and the rank—the
noncommutative rank (nc-rank)

\mathop{\rm nc\mbox{-}rank}A
—is considered in the
free skew field

[
2
,
6
]
into which the nonncommutative polynomial ring
{\mathbb{K}}\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
embeds.
Recent groundbreaking results
[
11
,
13
,
18
]

show that the noncommutative Edmonds’ problem
can be solved in polynomial time.
The techniques used in this result have unexpected but fruitful interactions
with many areas of pure and applied mathematics (noncommutative algebra, operator theory, representation theory, invariant theory, differential geometry, statistics, optimization, quantum information,…),
and act as catalysts for interdisciplinary research; see e.g.,
[
5
]
and references therein.

One of the key ingredients of this development is the following characterization
of the nc-rank due to Fortin and Reutenauer
[
9
]
.
For a vector subspace
U
in
{\mathbb{K}}^{n}

(denoted by
U\leqslant{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
),
let
{\cal A}U
denote the vector subspace
that is the sum of
A_{k}U
over
k=1,2,\ldots,m
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm nc\mbox{-}rank}A=n+\min_{U\leqslant{\mathbb{K}}^{n}}\dim{\cal A}U-\dim U
.

The first polynomial-time algorithm of nc-rank by Garg, Gurvits, Oliveira, and Wigderson
[
11
]

is based on a connection to
operator scaling
(Gurvits
[
12
]
)—a quantum generalization of the classical
matrix scaling
(Sinkhorn
[
25
]
).
Suppose from now that
{\mathbb{K}}
is the field of complex numbers
{\mathbb{C}}
.
The matrix tuple
{\cal A}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{m})
is
associated with the completely positive operator

T_{\cal A}:{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
and its dual
T^{*}_{\cal A}:{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
by

T_{\cal A}(X):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}XA_{k}^{\dagger},\quad T_{\cal A}^{*}(X):=\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}^{\dagger}XA_{k}\quad(X\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}).

(1.2)

For
g,h\in GL_{n}=GL(n,{\mathbb{C}})
, consider the scaled tuple

g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h=(g^{\dagger}A_{1}h,g^{\dagger}A_{2}h,\ldots,g^{\dagger}A_{m}h)
, and the corresponding scaled operators

T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}
and
T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}
.
The tuple
{\cal A}
is said to be
(approximately doubly-stochastic) scalable

if for every
\epsilon>0
there are
g,h\in GL_{n}

such that

\|T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|^{2}_{2}+\|T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|_{2}^{2}<\epsilon^{2},

(1.3)

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the Frobenius norm. Then, Gurvits’ characterization
[
12
]
of scalability is the following:

Theorem 1.2

(
[
12
]
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

{\cal A}
is scalable.

(2)

\displaystyle\inf_{X\succ 0}\frac{\det\sum_{k=1}^{m}A_{k}XA_{k}^{\dagger}}{\det X}>0
.

(3)

\mathop{\rm rank}T_{\cal A}(X)\geq\mathop{\rm rank}X
for every
X\succeq 0
.

By
X\succ 0
(resp.
X\succeq 0
) we mean that
X
is a positive definite (resp. semidefinite) Hermitian matrix.
In (3),
one see that
\mathop{\rm rank}T_{\cal A}(X)=\dim{\cal A}U

for the vector subspace
U
spanned by column vectors of
g
with
X=gg^{\dagger}
.
Hence, the condition (3) is equivalent to
\dim{\cal A}U\geq\dim U
for all
U\leqslant{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. By Theorem
1.1
, we have:

Corollary 1.3

(
[
11
]
)
.

\mathop{\rm nc\mbox{-}rank}A=n
if and only if
{\cal A}
is scalable.

Thus, the optimization of the function in (2) links with
the nc-nonsingularity determination of
A
, as did in
[
11
]
.

The first main result of this paper is a more direct connection between nc-rank and scalability.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{1}
denote the
trace norm
, that is,

\|X\|_{1}
is the sum of singular values of
X
.
Then we prove that nc-corank is equal to one-half of
the minimum residual of a scaling with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
generalizing Corollary
1.3
.

Theorem 1.4
.

n-\mathop{\rm nc\mbox{-}rank}A=\frac{1}{2}\inf_{g,h\in GL_{n}}\|T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|_{1}+\|T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I\|_{1}.

(1.4)

This relation (
1.4
) is inspired by the duality theorem
of the minimum gradient-norm of a geodesically convex function on a Hadamard manifold
M
,
due to Hirai and Sakabe
[
16
]
.
They established that the infimum of the gradient norm of a lower-unbounded convex function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}

is equal to the supremum of
the negative of the
recession function

f^{\infty}
of
f
,
and that these infimum and supremum are attained by the unbounded gradient-flow curve of
f
at infinity.
In the operator scaling setting,
the residual matrices
T_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I,T^{*}_{g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h}(I)-I

of scaling
g^{\dagger}{\cal A}h
can be interpreted
as the (transported) gradient of a convex function
F
on
the product
P_{n}\times P_{n}
of the manifold
P_{n}
of positive definite matrices,
where
F
is a variant of the function in Theorem
1.2
(2).
From this,
they deduced a formula
[
16
, Theorem 4.19]
of the scalability limit with respect to
the Frobenius norm, i.e., the minimum possible
\epsilon>0
in (
1.3
).

Theorem
1.4

is viewed as a trace-norm version, and is
obtained by extending their gradient-flow approach.
To capture estimates by the trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
,
we consider a general
GL_{n}
-invariant norm on symmetric space

P_{n}\simeq GL_{n}/U_{n}
,
regard it as a Finsler manifold,
and consider the “gradient flow” of the function in Theorem
1.2
(2).
However, the norm in our setting may be non-differentiable,
and gradient flow cannot be defined directly.
We then utilize a general framework (Ambrosio, Gigli, and Savaré
[
1
]
) of gradient flows in metric spaces,
without differential structure,
and establish a duality theorem (Theorem
3.1
) of the minimum gradient norm of a convex function on
P_{n}

with respect to an invariant Finsler metric,
which is placed as the second main result of this paper.
Then, by adapting this duality, we obtain Theorem
1.4
.
Such an application of nondifferentiable gradient flows has never been seen before
and is interesting in its own right.
Discretizing this gradient flow to develop a new algorithm
and analysis for nc-rank and operator scaling
is an important future research.

In Section
2
,
we introduce necessary backgrounds on gradient flows in metric spaces and
symmetric space
P_{n}\simeq GL_{n}/U_{n}
endowed with an invariant Finsler metric.
In Section
3
, we establish the duality theorem (Theorem
3.1
)
of the minimum gradient norm of a convex function.
In Section
4
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

For positive integer

, let
[k]:=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
For an
n\times n
matrix
A
, let
e^{A}
denote the matrix exponential.
For a positive define matrix
X
, let
\log X
denote the unique symmetric matrix
Y
with

X=e^{Y}

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}

2.1
Gradient flow in metric space

We follow
[
1
, Part I]
.
Let
(M,d)
be a complete metric space.
An
absolutely continuous curve

is a curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to M
such that
there is a Lebesgue integrable function

m:(a,b)\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}m(r)dr

(2.1)

for any
s,t
with
a<s\leq t<b
.
A
locally absolutely continuous curve

is a curve such that each point on the curve
has an open interval on which the curve is absolutely continuous.
Note that the absolute continuity is stronger than uniform continuity.
For a (locally) absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to M
,
the
metric derivative

|\gamma^{\prime}|:(a,b)\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is defined as

|\gamma^{\prime}|(t):=\lim_{s\to t}\frac{d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t)))}{|s-t|}\quad(t\in(a,b)).

(2.2)

The limit exists for almost everywhere
t\in(a,b)
. The metric derivative
|\gamma^{\prime}|
can be chosen
as
m
in the above (
2.1
).
Particularly, it holds

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))\leq\int_{s}^{t}|\gamma^{\prime}|(t)dt\quad(a<s\leq t<b).

(2.3)

This inequality can be interpreted as the triangular inequality if the right-hand side is defined as the length of the curve.

A
d
-geodesic is a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
such that

d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=\frac{|s-t|}{|a-b|}d(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))\quad(a\leq s\leq t\leq b).

(2.4)

By a
d
-geodesic ray
we mean a curve
\gamma:[a,\infty)\to M

such that the restriction to every finite interval is a
d
-geodesic.
A function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is called
(geodesically)
d
-convex
if for any
x,y\in M

there exists a geodesic
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
such that

f(\gamma(t))\leq(1-t)f(x)+tf(y)\quad(t\in[0,1]).

(2.5)

The (global)
slope

|\partial f|:M\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
of a convex function
f
is defined by

|\partial f|(x):=\sup_{y\in X\setminus\{x\}}\frac{\max\{0,f(x)-f(y)\}}{d(x,y)}\quad(x\in M).

(2.6)

A
curve of maximal slope (a.k.a. gradient-flow curve)
of
f
with initial point
x_{0}\in M
is
a locally absolutely continuous curve
x:(0,\infty)\to X

satisfying
\lim_{t\to+0}x(t)=x_{0}
and

\frac{d}{dt}f(x(t))\leq-\frac{1}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}(t)-\frac{1}{2}|\partial f|^{2}(x(t))\quad({\rm a.e.}\ t\in(0,\infty)).

(2.7)

It is known
[
1
, Remark 1.3.3]
that the inequality becomes equality a.e. and it holds

|x^{\prime}|^{2}(t)=|\partial f|^{2}(x(t))=-\frac{d}{dt}f(x(t))\quad({\rm a.e.}\ t\in(0,\infty)),

(2.8)

and the energy identity holds

f(x(b))-f(x(a))=-\int_{a}^{b}\frac{1}{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}|\partial f|^{2}(x(t))dt\quad(0\leq a\leq b<\infty).

(2.9)

A curve of maximal slope of
f
is constructed by the limit of the implicit Euler scheme,
called a
generalized minimizing movement

[
1
, Chapter 2]
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 2.4.15]
)
.

Suppose that
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
is lower-semicontinuous
d
-convex and
every bounded subset of a sublevel set of
f
is relatively compact. Then, for every
x_{0}\in M
, there exists a curve of maximal slope
x:(0,\infty)\to M
of
f
with
\lim_{t\to+0}x(0)=x_{0}
such that

(1)

t\mapsto f(x(t))
is convex, and

(2)

t\mapsto|\partial f|(x(t))
is nonincreasing and right continuous.

2.2
Symmetric space with invariant Finsler metric

Let
v
be a norm on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
that is, it satisfies
v(x)\geq 0
,
v(x)=0\Leftrightarrow x=0
,
v(\alpha x)=|\alpha|v(x)
, and
v(x+y)\leq v(x)+v(y)
for
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The dual space
({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{*}
is identified with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
by

x\mapsto x^{\top}y

(y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.
The dual norm
v^{*}
of
v
is defined by

v^{*}(x):=\max\{x^{\top}y\mid y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:v(y)\leq 1\}
for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Suppose further that
v
is invariant under coordinate permutations, that is,
it holds
v(x)=v(\sigma x)

for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and every permutation matrix
\sigma
.
Then, the dual norm
v^{*}
is also invariant under coordinate permutations.

Let
S_{n}
denote the real vector space of
n\times n
Hermitian matrices.
The dual space of
S_{n}
is identified with
S_{n}
by
X\mapsto\mathop{\rm tr}XY

(Y\in S_{n})
.
For a permutation invariant norm
v
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
we obtain a unitarily invariant norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on
S_{n}
:

\|H\|_{v}:=v(\lambda)\quad(H\in S_{n}),

(2.10)

where
H=u\mathop{\rm diag}\lambda u^{\dagger}
for
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
u\in U_{n}
.
The dual norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{v}
of
\|\cdot\|_{v}

is defined by
\|H\|_{v}:=\max\{\mathop{\rm tr}HX\mid X\in S_{n}:v(X)\leq 1\}
.
The diagonal projection
D:S_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is defined by

D(H)_{i}:=H_{ii}\quad(H\in S_{n},i\in[n]).

(2.11)

The following are well-known, and can be found in
[
7
,
22
]
(for unitarily invariant convex functions).

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

\|\cdot\|_{v}
is a norm on
S_{n}
.

(2)

\|\cdot\|_{v}^{*}=\|\cdot\|_{v^{*}}
.

(3)

\|H\|_{v}\geq v(D(H))
for
H\in S_{n}
.

We give a proof in the Appendix for convenience.
If
v
is the
l_{p}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{p}
for
p\in[1,\infty]
,
then
\|\cdot\|_{v}
, denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
, is known as the
Schatten
p
-norm
.
Our particular interest is the trace norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
, which is the dual norm of spectral norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
(by above (2)).

\|\cdot\|_{1}=\|\cdot\|_{\infty}^{*}.

(2.12)

We next consider the manifold
P_{n}\subseteq S_{n}
of positive definite matrices.
We utilize elementary notions of Riemannian geometry; see e.g.,
[
3
,
24
]
.
The tangent space
T_{X}
at each point
X\in P_{n}
is identified with
S_{n}
, and the cotangent space
T_{X}^{*}

is also identified with
S_{n}
by
F\mapsto\mathop{\rm tr}FH

(H\in T_{X}=S_{n})
.
A differentiable function
f:P_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
gives rise
to a point
df(X)\in T_{X}^{*}
by
df(X)(H):=\frac{d}{dt}\mid_{t=0}f(\gamma(t))
, where
\gamma:[0,a)\to P_{n}
is
an arbitrary smooth curve with
\gamma(0)=X
and
\dot{\gamma}(0)=H\in T_{X}
.
It is called the
differential
of
f
at
X
.
The standard Riemannian metric
\langle,\rangle
on
P_{n}

is defined by
\langle H,H^{\prime}\rangle_{X}:=\mathop{\rm tr}X^{-1}HX^{-1}H^{\prime}
for
X\in P_{n}
and
H,H^{\prime}\in T_{X}
.
The corresponding norm is particularly denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{2,X}:=\sqrt{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{X}}
.

GL_{n}
acts isometrically and transitively on
P_{n}
by
(g,X)\mapsto gXg^{\dagger}
.
The stabilizer at
I
is the unitary group
U_{n}
.
So
P_{n}
is viewed as the symmetric space
GL_{n}/U_{n}
.
This is a representative example of
Hadamard manifolds
,
that is, simply-connected Riemannian manifolds having
nonpositive sectional curvature everywhere; see
[
4
, Part II, Chapter 10]
.
The Riemannian length of a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to P_{n}
is defined as

\int_{a}^{b}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{2,\gamma(t)}dt
, where

\dot{\gamma}(t)\in T_{\gamma(t)}
denotes the tangent vector of
\gamma
.
The distance
d_{2}(X,Y)
between
X
and
Y

is the infimum of the length of a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
with
\gamma(a)=X
and
\gamma(b)=Y
.
It is known that the infimum is (uniquely) attained by a curve with form
t\mapsto ge^{tH}g^{\dagger}
that is a
d_{2}
-geodesic.
Specifically, for
H\in T_{X}
the unique
d_{2}
-geodesic ray
t\mapsto\gamma_{X,H}(t)
with
\gamma_{X,H}(0)=X
and
\dot{\gamma}_{X,H}(0)=H

is given by

\gamma_{X,H}(t)=X^{1/2}e^{-tX^{-1/2}HX^{-1/2}}X^{1/2}\quad(t\in[0,\infty)).

(2.13)

\|H\|_{2,X}
is called the
speed
of
\gamma_{X,H}
.
If
\|H\|_{2,X}=1
, then
\gamma_{X,H}
is called a
unit-speed

d_{2}
-geodesic ray.
The distance
d_{2}(X,Y)
between two points
X,Y\in P_{n}
is explicitly calculated by

d_{2}(X,Y)=d_{2}(I,X^{-1/2}YX^{-1/2})=\|\log X^{-1/2}YX^{-1/2}\|_{2}.

(2.14)

Fix a unitarily invariant norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
on
S_{n}=T_{I}
,
we introduce a
GL_{n}
-invariant Finsler metric on
P_{n}
as follows.
For each
X\in P_{n}
,
define norm
\|\cdot\|_{v,X}
on
T_{X}
by

\|H\|_{v,X}:=\|X^{-1/2}HX^{-1/2}\|_{v}\quad(H\in T_{X})

(2.15)

The dual norm
\|\cdot\|^{*}_{v,X}
on
T_{X}^{*}
is given by

\|F\|^{*}_{v,X}:=\|X^{1/2}FX^{1/2}\|_{v}^{*}\quad(F\in T_{X}^{*}).

(2.16)

Observe that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{v}
is invariant under the action
(g,X)\mapsto gXg^{\dagger}
.
As in the Riemannian case, the length
L[\gamma]
of a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to P_{n}
is defined by

L[\gamma]:=\int_{a}^{b}\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{v,\gamma(t)}dt.

(2.17)

The distance
d_{v}(X,Y)
between two points
X,Y

is defined as the infimum of a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M

connecting
X=\gamma(a)
and
Y=\gamma(b)
.
Now we obtain a metric space
(P_{n},d_{v})
. Friedland and Freitas
[
10
]
studied this metric space and characterized the metric structure, as follows.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

(P_{n},d_{v})
is a complete geodesic metric space, where

d_{v}
is given by

d_{v}(X,Y)=d_{v}(I,X^{-1/2}YX^{-1/2})=\|\log(X^{-1/2}YX^{-1/2})\|_{v}.

(2.18)

In
[
10
]
, this theorem is stated for the Schatten
p
-norm
but its proof works for any unitarily invariant norm
(since
[
10
, p.7 (3.7)]
holds
for a permutation invariant convex function).

Corollary 2.4
.

Any
d_{2}
-geodesic in
P_{n}
is also
d_{v}
-geodesic.
Any
d_{2}
-convex function
f:P_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is also
d_{v}
-convex.

The metric derivatives and slopes in
(P_{n},d_{v})

are given as follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

(1)

For an absolutely continuous curve
\gamma:(a,b)\to P_{n}
, it holds

|\gamma^{\prime}|(t)=\|\dot{\gamma}(t)\|_{v,\gamma(t)}\quad({\rm a.e.}\ t\in(a,b)).

(2)

For a differentiable
d_{v}
-convex function
f:P_{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, it holds

|\partial f|(X)=\|df(X)\|^{*}_{v,X}\quad(X\in P_{n}).

Proof.

(1). We can assume that

\gamma(0)=I
and

\gamma
is differentiable at
0\in(a,b)
.
Also we can assume that
\gamma(t)=e^{H(t)}

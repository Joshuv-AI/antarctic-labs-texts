Integrability propagation for a Boltzmann system describing polyatomic gas mixtures
Ricardo Alonso, Milana Čolić

Abstract
. This paper reviews results on the scalar Boltzmann equation for a single-component polyatomic gas with continuous internal energy. For the space homogeneous problem, L 1 L^{1} -theory is established, for solutions with initial strictly positive mass and bounded energy, which enables to solve the Cauchy problem for initial data with L 2 + 1 L^{1}_{2^{+}} -moments using the comparison principle for ODEs. Then, deriving entropy-based estimates, L p L^{p} -integrability properties of the solution are explored, p ∈ ( 1 , ∞ ] p\in(1,\infty] . All these analytical results hold under a specific assumption on the collision kernel corresponding to cut-off and hard-potentials type. A mean to verify physical applicability of the model is to evaluate the corresponding Boltzmann collision operator and to derive models for transport coefficients in terms of the collision kernel parameters. Comparison with experimental data for polytropic gases determines values of these parameters showing the physical relevance of the collision kernel.



Keywords.
Boltzmann equation, polyatomic gases, Cauchy theory, integrability propagation, transport coefficients.

AMS subject classifications.
35Q20, 76P05, 82C05.

1.
Introduction

The collisional kinetic theory and the Boltzmann equation provide a robust framework for the description of non-equilibrium processes in gas flows
[
14
]
. Typically they occur at large Knudsen number, which is given by the ratio between the mean free path and an observation length scale. Some examples include rarefied regime (vacuum technology, high-altitude flights) or microscopic setting (Micro- and Nano-Electro-Mechanical-Systems devices)
[
29
]
, when the classical fluid dynamic models, such as Navier-Stokes-Fourier law, become inadequate.

While the Boltzmann equation is originally derived for the description of monatomic gases, it is clear that nowadays applications require to step out from this framework. For instance, Earth’s atmosphere is a mixture of mainly diatomic nitrogen (
\sim
78%) and oxygen (
\sim
21%), while monatomic argon as a third major constituent is present with only 0.93%. In that regard, the physical scenario for this paper is a single polyatomic gas (gas whose molecule is made up of more than one atom).

Polyatomic gases are characterized by the presence of an internal structure, typically described by an internal energy variable. There are two different pathways depending on the nature of this variable: (i) discrete internal energy approach
[
28
,
9
,
32
,
27
]
leading to a system of kinetic equations for each energy level and (ii) continuous internal energy approach
[
11
,
18
,
19
]
with a single Boltzmann-like equation. Recently, these two models have been unified by considering a general internal state
[
12
]
.

This paper will focus on the approach based on the continuous internal energy. Within this setting, the modelling and analysis of the Boltzmann-like equation with the nonlinear collision operator is an active field of research. For instance, in the perturbation framework, compactness properties of the associated linearized Boltzmann operator are studied in
[
7
,
13
,
8
]
. The global well-posedness for bounded mild solutions near global equilibrium on the torus is established in
[
24
]
, while
[
30
]
constructs large-amplitude solutions with small initial relative entropy and obtains convergence to the global equilibrium state with an exponential rate.

The goal of this paper is to review results on (i) analysis of the space homogeneous problem that proposes a suitable collision kernel of the Boltzmann operator and (ii) physical interpretation of such collision kernel through evaluation of the collision operator and modelling of transport coefficients enabling the comparison with experimental data. An outcome of such research is the analysis of the physically relevant Boltzmann model.

2.
Modelling of polyatomic gases in continuous kinetic approach

Due to its complex structure, a polyatomic molecule might posses nonnegligible rotational and vibrational energies, besides the translational one. A modelling choice for the continuous kinetic approach is to capture these peculiarities with one continuous variable, a microscopic internal energy
I\geq 0
. Thus, a molecule of mass
m>0
having the velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
has the total energy

\frac{m}{2}|v|^{2}+I.

Then, at any time
t>0
and space position
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the gas is modelled by a distribution function
f:=f(t,x,v,I)\geq 0
, which changes due to transport and particle collisions that we precise below.

2.1.
Collisions

A collision between two molecules of the same mass
m>0
, localized in time and space, affects the microscopic state of the involved molecules described by the velocity-internal energy pairs
(v,I)
and
(v_{*},I_{*})
, that change to
(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})
and
(v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime}_{*})
, respectively. If such a collision is assumed to preserve momentum and total energy of the two molecules,

(3)

v^{\prime}+v^{\prime}_{*}=v+v_{*},\qquad\frac{m}{2}|v^{\prime}|^{2}+I^{\prime}+\frac{m}{2}|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}+I^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{m}{2}|v|^{2}+I+\frac{m}{2}|v_{*}|^{2}+I_{*},

then it is possible to perform parametrization the collision using the so-called Borgnakke-Larsen procedure
[
11
,
19
]
, based on repartition of the energy
E
,

(4)

\displaystyle v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{R\,E}{m}}\sigma,\qquad I^{\prime}

\displaystyle=r(1-R)E,

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\sqrt{\frac{R\,E}{m}}\sigma,\qquad I^{\prime}_{*}

\displaystyle=(1-r)(1-R)E,\quad E:=\frac{m}{4}|v-v_{*}|^{2}+I+I_{*},

where
\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
is an
angular
variable and
r,R\in[0,1]
are
energy exchange
variables.

2.2.
Collision operator in a strong form

For suitable distribution functions
f(v,I)\geq 0
,
g(v,I)\geq 0
, and a parameter

(5)

Q(f,g)(v,I)=\int\left\{f(v^{\prime},I^{\prime})g(v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime}_{*})\left(\frac{II_{*}}{I^{\prime}I^{\prime}_{*}}\right)^{\alpha}-f(v,I)g(v_{*},I_{*})\right\}\\
\times\mathcal{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)\,d_{\alpha}(r,R)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}R\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\mathrm{d}I_{*},

where

(6)

d_{\alpha}(r,R)=r^{\alpha}(1-r)^{\alpha}\,(1-R)^{2\alpha+1}\,\sqrt{R},

and
the collision kernel
\mathcal{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)\geq 0
is invariant with respect to

(7)

\begin{split}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)&\leftrightarrow\left(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*},I^{\prime},I^{\prime}_{*},\hat{u},\frac{I}{I+I_{*}},\frac{m\left|u\right|^{2}}{4E}\right),\\
(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)&\leftrightarrow(v_{*},v,I_{*},I,-\sigma,1-r,R),\ u=v-v_{*},\hat{u}=\frac{u}{\left|u\right|}.\end{split}

\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{B}
is assumed to be factorized as an angular part
b\geq 0

\tilde{\mathcal{B}}\geq 0

\mathcal{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)=b(\hat{u}\cdot\sigma)\ \tilde{\mathcal{B}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},r,R).

As a byproduct, this choice enables to reduce domain for
\sigma

\mathbb{S}^{2}
to

\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+}=\left\{\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}:\hat{u}\cdot\sigma\geq 0\right\},\ \text{for the fixed}\ \hat{u}:=\frac{u}{|u|},

and to prescribe, without loss of generality,
b
supported on
\mathbb{S}^{2}_{+}
.

2.4.
H-theorem

The following theorem focuses on the properties of the entropy dissipation functional and characterizes the equilibrium distribution function

Theorem 2.1

(H-theorem)
.

Let the collision kernel
\mathcal{B}
be positive almost everywhere and

such that the collision operator

ii.

The three following properties are equivalent

(1)

{\mathcal{D}}(M)=0
,

(2)

Q(M,M)=0
for all
(v,I)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(3)

There exists

, such that

(19)

M(v,I)=\frac{\rho}{m(k_{B}T)^{\alpha+1}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi k_{B}T}\right)^{3/2}I^{\alpha}\ e^{-\frac{1}{k_{B}T}\left(\frac{m}{2}\left|v-U\right|^{2}+I\right)},

Lemma 3.11

First, notice that H-theorem (

) implies that a solution of the Boltzmann equation (

Moreover, the conservation of energy implies
\|f\|_{L^{1}_{2}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}
for the solution of the Boltzmann equation. Thus, the elementary inequality valid for any

|\log x|\leq\log(x)+(\alpha+4)\,x^{-\frac{1}{\alpha+4}},\ \alpha>-1,

together with Hölder’s inequality relate

to

and

\displaystyle H[f]

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}(f_{0})+(\alpha+4)\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-6{-2\alpha}}{I^{\alpha}}\|^{\frac{1}{\alpha+4}}_{L^{1}}\ \|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{\frac{\alpha+3}{\alpha+4}}.

It remains to prove that

implies

\mathcal{H}(f_{0})

When

implying

(60)

Hölder’s inequality applied to the

-norm of the first term in (

\begin{split}\|f_{0}^{\frac{3}{4}}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-6}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L^{1}}\ \|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}},\end{split}

and
applied twice to the

-norm of the last term in (

(r=\tfrac{p-1}{s-1},r^{\prime})

\begin{split}\|f_{0}\log I\|_{L^{1}}&\leq\left(\|f_{0}^{s(1-\theta)}\|_{L^{r}}\ \|f_{0}^{s\theta}\langle\cdot\rangle^{s}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}\right)^{1/s}\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-1}\log I\|_{L^{s^{\prime}}}\\
&\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{1-\theta}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{\theta}\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-1}\log I\|_{L^{s^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

Thus, integration of (
60
) gives

(63)

\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(f_{0})\leq 4\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-6}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{L^{1}}\ \|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+\tfrac{1}{2(p-1)}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+|\alpha|\|f_{0}\|_{L^{p}}^{1-\theta}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{\theta}\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-1}\log I\|_{L^{s^{\prime}}}.\end{split}

The statement follows adding estimates (
58
) and (
63
).

When

, in which case

, then the first term in the integrand of

Hölder’s inequality as in (

\ \begin{split}\|f_{0}\log f_{0}\|_{L^{1}}&\leq\|\langle\cdot\rangle^{-6}\|_{L^{1}}^{1/4}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}^{3/4}\max_{0\leq y\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}}(y^{\frac{1}{4}}|\log y|).\end{split}

-norm of second term in (

\begin{split}\|f_{0}\log I\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0}\left(\log I\mathds{1}_{I\leq 1}+\langle\cdot\rangle^{2}\mathds{1}_{I>1}\right)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{2}}.\end{split}

In conclusion, integration of (

4.
Physical relevance of the collision kernel

The analysis of the space-homogeneous Boltzmann equation (
20
) relies on the assumption (
8
) regarding the collision kernel. A natural question that arises is whether this assumption has a suitable physical interpretation. One approach to address it is to derive moment equations from the space-inhomogeneous Boltzmann equation and in particular to evaluate the collision operator for that specific collision kernel. The evaluation allows to derive models for transport coefficients that naturally depend on the collision kernel parameters. Consequently, the question of the physical validity of the collision kernel reduces to the question whether the values of these parameters can be determined to match the measured properties of gas transport.

In this Section, we review results of
[
20
,
21
,
23
,
34
]
that compute moments of the collision operator convexly combining the operator (
5
) with the frozen counterpart
[
4
]
accounting only for frozen collisions of polyatomic gases in which internal energy of each colliding particle remains invariant, when the energy law in (
3
) is replaced by
|v^{\prime}|^{2}+|v^{\prime}_{*}|^{2}=|v|^{2}+|v_{*}|^{2}
,
I^{\prime}=I
,
I^{\prime}_{*}=I_{*}
. More precisely, for a convex factor
\omega\in[0,1]
, the following collision operator is studied

(95)

Q^{\omega}(f,f)(v,I):=\omega\ Q(f,f)(v,I)+(1-\omega)\ Q^{\text{f}}(f,f)(v,I),

where the frozen collision operator is

(96)

Q^{\text{f}}(f,g)(v,I)=\int\left\{f(v^{\prime},I)g(v^{\prime}_{*},I_{*})-f(v,I)g(v_{*},I_{*})\right\}\\
\times\mathcal{B}^{\,\text{f}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}v_{*}\,\mathrm{d}I_{*},

and the primed quantities are defined by

(97)

v^{\prime}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}+\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\qquad v^{\prime}_{*}=\frac{v+v_{*}}{2}-\frac{|v-v_{*}|}{2}\sigma,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{S}^{2}.

The collision kernel
\mathcal{B}^{\,\text{f}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)\geq 0
satisfies the usual micro-reversibility rules,

\mathcal{B}^{\,\text{f}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)=\mathcal{B}^{\,\text{f}}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{*},I,I_{*},\hat{u})=\mathcal{B}^{\,\text{f}}(v_{*},v,I_{*},I,-\sigma).
The frozen collision operator is shown to play a critical role in recovering the correct value of the Prandtl number
[
21
]
. We refer to
[
4
]
for the study of some properties of the operator
Q^{\omega}(f,f)
.

4.1.
Choice of the collision kernel

The following collision kernel satisfying assumption (
10
) is prescribed, for
\eta\geq 0
and a dimension constant
K\geq 0
,

(98)

\mathcal{B}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma,r,R)=K\ \frac{2\Gamma(2\alpha+7/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha+1)^{2}}\ \\
\times\left[R^{\frac{\zeta}{2}}|v-v_{*}|^{\zeta}+\eta\left(r(1-R)\frac{I}{m}\right)^{\frac{\zeta}{2}}+\eta\left((1-r)(1-R)\frac{I_{*}}{m}\right)^{\frac{\zeta}{2}}\right],

where the multiplicative factor serves to achieve consistency with the constant collision kernel
[
21
]
or to normalize the effect of
d_{\alpha}(r,R)
. Its frozen counterpart reads, for
\eta_{\text{f}}\geq 0
,

(99)

\mathcal{B}^{\text{f}}(v,v_{*},I,I_{*},\sigma)=K\left[\frac{|v-v_{*}|^{2\zeta}}{\left(\frac{4}{m}E\right)^{\zeta/2}}+\eta_{\text{f}}\frac{I^{\zeta}+I_{*}^{\zeta}}{(mE)^{\zeta/2}}\right],

where
E
is the collisional energy (
4
).

4.2.
Transport coefficients as moments of the collision operator

Extended thermodynamics and Chapman-Enskog asymptotics
[
35
,
36
]
suggest model for transport coefficients – shear viscosity
\mu
, bulk viscosity
\nu
and thermal conductivity
\kappa
– in terms of moments of the collision operator, namely,

(100)

\mu=\frac{p}{P_{\sigma}},\quad\text{and}\quad\nu=\frac{4(\alpha+1)}{6\alpha+15}\frac{p}{P_{\Pi}},

while for
\kappa
we consider two distinct models: one in which the heat flux is divided into translational and internal components (the 17-moment model) and second one in which the heat flux is treated as a single variable (the 14-moment model),

(101)

\begin{split}\kappa^{(14)}&=\frac{k_{B}}{m}p\,\left(\alpha+\tfrac{7}{2}\right)\,\frac{1}{P_{q^{(tot)}}},\quad\text{and}\\
\kappa^{(17)}&=\frac{k_{B}}{m}\,p\frac{\tfrac{5}{2}(P_{s}^{(s)}-P_{s}^{(q)})+(\alpha+1)(P_{q}^{(q)}-P_{q}^{(s)})}{P_{q}^{(q)}P_{s}^{(s)}-P_{q}^{(s)}P_{s}^{(q)}},\end{split}

where
p=\tfrac{\rho}{m}k_{B}T
is the hydrodynamic gas pressure. Moments of the collision operator (
95
) appearing as coefficients

P_{\sigma}
,

P_{\Pi}
,

P_{q^{(tot)}}
,

P_{q}^{(q)}

P_{q}^{(s)}
,

P_{s}^{(q)}
,

P_{s}^{(s)}

are explicitly computed
[
16
]
for a given distribution function ansatz (corresponding to 14- and 17-moment models) and the collision kernel from Section
4.1
. They depend on the collision operator parameters, namely on
\alpha>-1
,
\zeta\geq 0
,
K>0
,
\omega\in[0,1]
,
\eta,\eta_{\text{f}}\geq 0
. The aim is to determine their values for a specific gas in order to match the measurements for the above mentioned transport coefficients
[
33
]
, which provides a physical interpretation to the Boltzmann operator and the collision kernel satisfying assumptions (
8
) of the space homogeneous problem.

4.3.
Comparison to experimental data

The parameter
\alpha>-1
appearing in the collision operator (
5
) is assumed constant, which physically means that the attention is restricted to gases with constant specific heats, also called
polytropic
or
calorically perfect
gases
[
34
]
. More precisely,
\alpha
is related to the dimensionless specific heat
\hat{c}_{v}
via

(102)

\alpha=\hat{c}_{v}-\tfrac{5}{2}.

Examples of gases which behave as polytropic over a significant temperature range around room temperature of
300
K are N
2
, O
2
, NO, CO, H
2
.

The next task is to provide physical values for the parameters of the collision kernel. We first consider
\zeta
and
K
, and then
\omega
and
\eta
,
\eta_{\text{f}}
.

For isobaric processes at pressures
p=
1bar and
p=
0.092bar=69mmHg, experimental data for temperature dependency of shear viscosity and thermal conductivity on the polytropic temperature interval is collected. At 300K, their values are denoted with
\mu_{0}^{(exp)}
,
\kappa_{0}^{(exp)}
. To determine
\zeta
, these data are then fitted with the power-law model for either (i) shear viscosity
\mu
or (ii) thermal conductivity
\kappa
, which in both cases (appropriately scaled) have the same shape for hard-potentials type of collision kernel
[
20
]
.
The constant
K
in the units of
\mu
Pa
\cdot
s
[
34
]
serves to match data at
T_{0}=
300K. Thus, two possible scenarios to determine
\zeta
and
K
are

(103)

\begin{split}&\text{Scenario}\ (i)\ \ \frac{\mu(T)}{\mu(T_{0})}=\left(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\right)^{1-\frac{\zeta}{2}}\!\!,\ K_{\mu}=\frac{\mu(T_{0})}{\mu_{0}^{\text{(}exp)}\times 10^{-6}},\\
&\text{Scenario}\ (ii)\ \ \frac{\kappa^{(14)}(T)}{\kappa^{(14)}(T_{0})}=\frac{\kappa^{(17)}(T)}{\kappa^{(17)}(T_{0})}=\left(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\right)^{1-\frac{\zeta}{2}}\!\!,\ K_{\kappa}=\frac{\kappa(T_{0})}{\kappa_{0}^{\text{(}exp)}\times 10^{-6}}.\end{split}

The remaining parameters
\omega
and
\eta
,
\eta_{\text{f}}
are determined to match the measurements at 300K of the Prandtl number defined by

(104)

\text{Pr}^{\text{(}exp)}=\left(\alpha+\tfrac{7}{2}\right)\frac{k_{B}}{m}\frac{\mu_{0}^{\text{(}exp)}}{\kappa_{0}^{\text{(}exp)}},

and/or the estimated value of ratio of bulk to shear viscosity
[
17
]
.
Transport coefficients (
100
)–(
101
) offer two Prandtl number models
[
22
]
,

(105)

\text{Pr}^{(14)}=\frac{P_{q^{(tot)}}}{P_{\sigma}},\qquad\text{Pr}^{(17)}=\frac{\left(\alpha+\frac{7}{2}\right)}{P_{\sigma}}\frac{\tfrac{5}{2}(P_{s}^{(s)}-P_{s}^{(q)})+(\alpha+1)(P_{q}^{(q)}-P_{q}^{(s)})}{P_{q}^{(q)}P_{s}^{(s)}-P_{q}^{(s)}P_{s}^{(q)}}.

Ideally, equalizing the right-hand sides of the Prandtl number model (
105
) and measurements (
104
), and the bulk viscosity (
103
) to data
[
17
]
, one gets a system of two equations in three variables
\omega
,
\eta
and
\eta_{\text{f}}
. The computations for the collision kernel chosen in Section
4.1
show that it is only possible for CO to find a combination of these parameters solving the given system. For other gases, one has to choose to match either Prandtl number (14- or 17-model) or bulk viscosity, which is possible for many combinations of
\omega
,
\eta
and
\eta_{\text{f}}
.

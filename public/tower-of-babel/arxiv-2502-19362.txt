Abstract
Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS), which can be realized with a photonic quantum computing model, perform some special kind of sampling tasks. In [ 4 ] , we introduced algorithms that use GBS samples to approximate Gaussian expectation problems. We found a non-empty open subset of the problem space where these algorithms achieve exponential speedup over the standard Monte Carlo (MC) method. This speedup is defined in terms of the guaranteed sample size to reach the same accuracy ϵ \epsilon and success probability δ \delta under the ( ϵ , δ ) (\epsilon,\delta) multiplicative error approximation scheme. In this paper, we enhance our original approach by optimizing the average photon number in the GBS distribution to match the specific Gaussian expectation problem. We provide updated estimates of the guaranteed sample size for these improved algorithms and quantify the proportion of problem space where they outperform MC. Numerical results indicate that the proportion of the problem space where our improved algorithms have an advantage is substantial, and the advantage gained is significant. Notably, for certain special cases, our methods consistently outperform MC across nearly 100% of the problem space.

Introduction

The Gaussian expectation problem
\mathcal{I}_{\Haf}
, introduced in
[
4
]
, involves computing the integral

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(x)g(x)\differential x,

(1)

where

g(x)=(2\pi)^{-N/2}(\det B)^{-1/2}\exp(-\frac{1}{2}x^\intercal B^{-1} x)

is the Gaussian probability density function with zero mean and covariance matrix
B
(real, symmetric and positive definite), and

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\left|I\right|=k}a_{I}x^{I},~~~~a_{I}\in\mathbb{R}

is either a multivariate polynomial (
K<\infty
) or a formal power series (
K=\infty
) with

\displaystyle I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N}),~~\left|I\right|=i_{1}+i_{2}\dots+i_{N},~~x^{I}=x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dots x_{N}^{i_{N}}.

Using Wick’s theorem,
\mu_{\Haf}
can be expressed as a weighted sum of matrix hafnians

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\left|I\right|=2k}a_{I}\Haf(B_{I}),

(2)

subject to certain integrability conditions for
K=\infty
(details in
[
4
]
). The
(\epsilon,\delta)
-multiplicative error approximation problem
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
is to find
e
such that for any
0<\epsilon,\delta<1
,

P\left(\lvert\mu_{\Haf}-e\rvert>\epsilon|\mu_{\Haf}|\right)<\delta,

(3)

provided that
\mu_{\Haf}\neq 0.

An important family of special cases
\mathcal{I}_{\Haf^{2}}
, is defined by the integral

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf^{2}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}{f}(p,q){g}(p,q)\,\differential p\differential q,

(4)

where

\displaystyle{g}(p,q)=(2\pi)^{-N}(\det B)^{-1}\exp(-\frac{1}{2} [p, q]^\intercal(B \oplus B)^{-1} [p, q])

(5)

is the Gaussian probability density function with zero mean and covariance matrix
B\oplus B
,
and

\displaystyle{f}(p,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\left|I\right|=k}a_{I}p^{I}q^{I},~~~~~a_{I}\in\mathbb{R}.

Again, using Wick’s theorem,
\mu_{\Haf^{2}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\left|I\right|=2k}a_{I}\Haf(B_{I})^{2}.

(6)

The
(\epsilon,\delta)
-multiplicative error approximation problem
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
is defined similarly as in (
3
).

In
[
4
]
, we presented solutions to
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
and
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
using samples from a Gaussian Boson Sampling (GBS) device
[
1
,
7
,
13
,
11
]
, provided that the eigenvalues of
B
are strictly less than 1. We introduced the probability estimator (GBS-P) for solving
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
and the importance estimator (GBS-I) for solving
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
. We proved that there exists a non-empty open subset of the problem space such that the GBS estimators achieve exponential speedup over the standard Monte Carlo (MC) method. Here, the speedup is defined in terms of the guaranteed sample size, as derived using Markov’s inequality or Chebyshev’s inequality, to reach the same accuracy
\epsilon
and success probability
\delta
.

In this paper, we enhance our original methods by optimizing the average photon number in the GBS distribution to match the specific Gaussian expectation problem. To this end, we first introduce a partition by degree of
\mu_{\Haf}
, given by

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf}=\mu_{\Haf,0}+\mu_{\Haf,1}+\mu_{\Haf,2}+\dots+\mu_{\Haf,K},

(7)

where

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf,k}=\sum_{\left|I\right|=2k}a_{I}\Haf(B_{I}).

For each degree
\mu_{\Haf,k}
, we define the corresponding multiplicative error approximation problem
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,k}(\epsilon,\delta)
. By De Morgan’s Law and the union bound, solving
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
reduces to solving each individual
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,k}(\epsilon,\delta)
(see Lemma
B.1
for details). We focus on
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,K}(\epsilon,\delta)
, with a slight abuse of notation where

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)=\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,K}(\epsilon,\delta)~~~~\textnormal{and}~~~~\mu_{\Haf}=\mu_{\Haf,K}.

The same partitioning strategy applies to
\mu_{\Haf^{2}}
, leading to the corresponding problem
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2},K}(\epsilon,\delta)
. Again, by an abuse of notation, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)=\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2},K}(\epsilon,\delta)~~~~\textnormal{and}~~~~\mu_{\Haf^{2}}=\mu_{\Haf^{2},K}.

We refine our estimator for each degree
K
problem by using samples from an optimized GBS distribution. The key idea is to select a distribution
P_{tB}
where the average photon number equals
2K
. Precisely, let
0<\lambda_{N}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{1}<1
be the eigenvalues of
B
and let
t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
. The GBS distribution for the scalar-multiplied matrix
tB
is given by

\displaystyle P_{tB}(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{t}}{I!}\Haf((tB)_{I})^{2},~~~~d_{t}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

Here,
(tB)_{I}
denotes the sub- or super-matrix of
tB
determined by
I
.
We define the
average photon number
of samples from
P_{tB}
to be

\displaystyle m_{tB}=\mathbb{E}_{I\sim P_{tB}}[|I|],

and we want

\displaystyle m_{tB}=2K.

Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
be i.i.d samples from
P_{tB}
. The GBS-P estimator is defined as a weighted average of the discrete approximation of the probability distribution

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}=\sum_{|I|=2K}a_{I}t^{-K}\sigma_{I}\sqrt{\tfrac{I!}{d_{t}}}\sqrt{\tfrac{S^{(I)}_{n}}{n}}

where
S^{(I)}_{n}
counts the occurrences of
I
and
\sigma_{I}
is the sign of
\Haf(B_{I})
. Here, we assume that there is an efficient method for computing
\sigma_{I}
. We will return to this part in a future publication. For
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
, we define the GBS-I estimator to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{I_{i}!}{d_{t}}a_{I_{i}}t^{-2K}.

As in
[
4
]
, GBS-I is an unbiased estimator. Therefore, it follows directly from the weak law of large numbers (WLLN) that
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
for a sufficiently large
n
. Using Chebyshev’s inequality, we compute the guaranteed sample size of GBS-I to be

\displaystyle n^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}=\frac{V^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf^{2}}^{2}},~~~~V^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}=\frac{t^{-4K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|I|=2K}a^{2}_{I}I!\Haf(B_{I})^{2}-\mu_{\Haf^{2}}^{2}.

The estimator, GBS-P, on the other hand, is biased. So, we cannot apply the WLLN directly. We instead perform a leading-order asymptotic analysis of the squared estimation error as
n
grows, using techniques very similar to the ones used in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}
and
\mu_{\Haf}
be defined as before, then the large
n
leading order asymptotics of the squared estimation error is given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]\sim\frac{1}{4n}\left(\frac{t^{-2K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|I|=2K}a^{2}_{I}I!-\mu_{\Haf}^{2}\right).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is provided in Section
3
.
We remark here that it is an interesting problem to derive the asymptotic expansion to all orders in
n
. We will likely obtain divergent series, but the techniques developed in
[
2
,
8
,
9
]
can give exact analytic expression. We shall in future work return to this part.
Using Theorem
1.1
, it’s straightforward that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]=0,

and therefore
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
for sufficiently large
n
.
Using Markov’s inequality, we can further derive an approximate guaranteed sample size, that is

\displaystyle n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}=\frac{V^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf}^{2}},~~~~V^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}\sim\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t^{-2K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|I|=2K}a^{2}_{I}I!-\mu_{\Haf}^{2}\right).

We compare the efficiency of the new GBS estimators with the standard Monte Carlo estimator, as defined in
[
4
]
. Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{MC}}_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}f(X_{i}),

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d samples from the Gaussian distribution
g
. Since MC is also unbiased,
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{MC}}_{n}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
or
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
for sufficiently large
n
. Furthermore,
Chebyshev’s inequality yields the following guaranteed sample sizes:

\displaystyle n_{\Haf}^{\textnormal{MC}}=\frac{V^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf}^{2}},~~~~~V^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|,|I^{\prime}|=2K\end{subarray}}a_{I}a_{I^{\prime}}\Haf(B_{I+I^{\prime}})-\mu_{\Haf}^{2}

\displaystyle n_{\Haf^{2}}^{\textnormal{MC}}=\frac{V^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf^{2}}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}|\mu_{\Haf^{2}}|^{2}},~~~~V^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf^{2}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|I|,|I^{\prime}|=2K\end{subarray}}a_{I}a_{I^{\prime}}\Haf(B_{I+I^{\prime}})^{2}-\mu_{\Haf^{2}}^{2}.

To estimate the percentage of the problem space where our improved GBS estimators outperform MC, we define

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}(N,K)&=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}(N,K)}H(n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}-n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf})\,d\mu}{\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}(N,K)}1\,d\mu},\\
P^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}(N,K)&=\frac{\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}(N,K)}H(n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf^{2}}-n^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}})\,d\mu}{\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}(N,K)}1\,d\mu}.\end{aligned}

(8)

Here,
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}(N,K)
is the subset of
a_{I}
’s and
B
for which
n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}
and
n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}
are well-defined, and
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}(N,K)
is the subset for which
n^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}
and
n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf^{2}}
are well-defined. Furthermore,
d\mu
denotes the volume form and
H
is the heavyside step function. The precise definition of the space and volume form is provided in Section
4.1
.
Numerical approximations of
P^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}(N,K)
and
P^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}(N,K)
are made for various
N
and
K
. A detailed description of the methods used for these computations is provided in Section
4.2
.

2
The GBS distribution and mean photon numbers

Let
B
be a real, symmetric, positive definite matrix given as in (
1
). Suppose the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{N}
of
B
are strictly less than 1. Then, GBS can be programmed to sample from the following distribution
[
7
,
10
]
:

\displaystyle P_{B}(I)=\frac{d}{I!}\Haf(B_{I})^{2},~~~~~I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}

(9)

where

\displaystyle I!

\displaystyle=i_{1}!i_{2}!\dots i_{N}!,

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

(10)

The
N
-tuples
I
’s correspond to the photon patterns output by the GBS device.
For a specific pattern
I=(i_{1},\dots,i_{N})
, the total number of photons in
I
is given by

\displaystyle|I|=i_{1}+i_{2}+\dots+i_{N}.

We are interested in the
average photon number
of samples from
P_{B}
, that is

\displaystyle m_{B}=\mathbb{E}_{I\sim P_{B}}[|I|].

From Lemma
B.2
,
m_{B}
can be computed explicitly from the eigenvalues of
B
, that is

\displaystyle m_{B}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

(11)

Consider
tB
with
t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
. It gives rise to a new GBS sampling distribution
P_{tB}
,

\displaystyle P_{tB}(I)

\displaystyle=\frac{d_{t}}{I!}\Haf((tB)_{I})^{2}

(12)

where

\displaystyle d_{t}=\prod_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

Let
m_{tB}
denote the average photon number of
P_{tB}
. In the next proposition, we show that
m_{tB}
can be set to any positive real number by tuning
t
over
(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
m>0
, there exists
t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
such that
m_{tB}=m
.

Proof.

Since
tB
has eigenvalues
t\lambda_{1}\geq\dots\geq t\lambda_{N}
, we compute that

\displaystyle m_{tB}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

(13)

We further note that
m_{tB}
as a function of
t
is surjective onto
(0,\infty)
, and this completes the proof.
∎

In practice, to find such a scalar factor
t
, a simple grid search over
(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
will give satisfactory results.

3
The GBS algorithms

3.1
The probability estimation method: GBS-P

The first estimator, GBS-P, uses a direct probability estimation. We begin by assuming that there is an efficient method for computing the sign of the matrix hafnians.
Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
be i.i.d. samples from
P_{tB}
where
m_{tB}=2K
. We further define

S^{(J)}_{n}=\sum_{l=1}^{n}1_{J}(I_{l}),~~~~~~~~~1_{J}(I)=\begin{cases}1&I=J\\
0&I\neq J\end{cases}.

Next, take a weighted average of the square root of
{S^{(J)}_{n}}/{n}
to define

\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}=\sum_{|J|=2K}\alpha_{J}\sqrt{\tfrac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}},

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{J}

\displaystyle=a_{J}t^{-K}\sigma_{J}\sqrt{\frac{J!}{d_{t}}},

\displaystyle\sigma_{J}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{sign}(\Haf(B_{J})).

We now prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
p_{J}=P_{tB}(J)=\frac{d_{t}}{J!}\Haf((tB)_{J})^{2}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\mu_{\Haf}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|J|=2K}a_{J}\Haf(B_{J})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|J|=2K}a_{J}t^{-K}\Haf((tB)_{J})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|J|=2K}\alpha_{J}\sqrt{p_{J}}.

For notational convenience, we define

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sqrt{\frac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\frac{S^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)\right].

(14)

Then,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|,|J^{\prime}|=2K}\alpha_{J}\alpha_{J^{\prime}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{S^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|J|,|J^{\prime}|=2K}\alpha_{J}\alpha_{J^{\prime}}e_{J,J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J|,|J^{\prime}|=2K\\
J\neq J^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{J}\alpha_{J^{\prime}}e_{J,J^{\prime}}+\sum_{|J|=2K}\alpha_{J}^{2}e_{J,J}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|J|,|J^{\prime}|=2K\\
J\neq J^{\prime}\end{subarray}}-\alpha_{J}\alpha_{J^{\prime}}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}+\sum_{|J|=2K}\alpha_{J}^{2}(1-p_{J})\right)

(15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n}\left(\sum_{|J|=2K}\alpha_{J}^{2}-|\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4n}\left(\frac{t^{-2K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|J|=2K}a^{2}_{J}J!-|\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right)

The leading order asymptotics (
15
) follows from
Lemmas
3.3
and
3.4
, presented near the end of this section.
∎

Theorem 3.1
.

The GBS-P estimator
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
with

\displaystyle n\geq\frac{V^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf}^{2}}=:n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}

where

\displaystyle V^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}\sim\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t^{-2K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|J|=2K}a^{2}_{J}J!-|\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right).

Proof.

It follows from Theorem
1.1
that
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
for large enough
n
, since

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]=0.

To determine the required size of
n
, we use Markov’s inequality.
We observe that

\displaystyle P(|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|>\epsilon|\mu_{\Haf}|)

\displaystyle=P(|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}>\epsilon^{2}|\mu_{\Haf}|^{2})

\displaystyle<\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]}{\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf}^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}\mu_{\Haf}^{2}}\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{t^{-2K}}{d_{t}}\sum_{|J|=2K}a^{2}_{J}J!-|\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

We refer to
n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}
as the
guaranteed sample size
for GBS-P to solve
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
.
Finally, we show that the optimal
t
that minimizes
n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}
is indeed the one that satisfies
m_{tB}=2K
.

Proposition 3.2
.

A real value
t_{0}
is the unique solution to

\displaystyle t_{0}=\argmin_{t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]

if and only if
m_{t_{0}B}=2K
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha_{J}=a_{J}t^{-K}\sigma_{J}\sqrt{\frac{J!}{d_{t}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]=\frac{t^{-2K}}{{d_{t}}}\sum_{|J|,|J^{\prime}|=2K}a_{J}a_{J^{\prime}}\sigma_{J}\sigma_{J^{\prime}}\sqrt{J!}\sqrt{J^{\prime}!}e_{J,J^{\prime}}

(16)

where
e_{J,J^{\prime}}
is defined as in (
14
).
To minimize (
16
) over
t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
, it is equivalent to compute

\displaystyle t_{0}=\argmin_{t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})}\gamma(t)

(17)

where

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=\log\frac{t^{-2K}}{{d_{t}}}=\log\frac{t^{-2K}}{\prod_{n=1}^{N}\sqrt{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}}=-2K\log t-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\log(1 - t^2 \lambda_n^2).

If
t_{0}
is a solution to (
17
), then it must be a critical point of
\gamma(t)
. In other words,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t_{0})=-\frac{2K}{t_{0}}+t_{0}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-t_{0}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}=0,

(18)

and therefore

\displaystyle m_{t_{0}B}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t_{0}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-t_{0}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}=2K.

To prove the other direction, we first note that if
0<t<t_{0}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}-1}<\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\frac{1}{t_{0}^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}-1}=m_{t_{0}}B=2K,

and therefore

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}(t)t=-2K+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-t^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}}<0

Hence,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)<0
. Similarly, we can check that
\gamma^{\prime}(t)>0
for all
t_{0}<t<\lambda_{1}^{-1}
. Therefore,
t_{0}
is the unique minimal solution in
(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})
.
∎

Now we state and prove the lemmas required by the proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p_{J}=P_{tB}(J)
and let
e_{J,J}
be defined as in (
14
). Then the large
n
leading order asymptotics of
e_{J,J}
is

\displaystyle e_{J,J}\sim\frac{1-p_{J}}{4n}.

Proof.

We start by computing that

\displaystyle e_{J,J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{m}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}P\left(\tfrac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}=m\right).

For convenience, let

\displaystyle t_{m}=\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{m}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}P\left(\tfrac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}=m\right).

Then, of course,

\displaystyle e_{J,J}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}t_{m}.

Note that

\displaystyle P\left(\tfrac{S^{(J)}_{n}}{n}=m\right)=\binom{n}{m}p_{J}^{m}(1-p_{J})^{n-m}.

At
m=0
or
m=n
, we obtain

\displaystyle t_{0}

\displaystyle=p_{J}(1-p_{J})^{n}

\displaystyle t_{n}

\displaystyle=(1-\sqrt{p_{J}})^{2}p_{J}^{n}.

Both decay exponentially in
n
. For the rest of the terms, we compute that

\displaystyle e^{\prime}_{J,J}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}t_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\left(\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}\binom{n}{m}p_{J}^{m}(1-p_{J})^{n-m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}(1-p_{J})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{\left(\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(n-m+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}\right)^{m}.

(19)

Here, (
19
) is obtained from expanding the binomial coefficient and rewriting
n!=\Gamma(n+1)
.

We now apply techniques similar to those in
[
3
]
for analyzing the values of colored Jones polynomials and quantum invariants, particularly using Cauchy’s Residue Theorem and the asymptotic analysis of oscillatory integrals.
We start by applying Cauchy’s Residue Theorem to the sum in (
19
). Let
\gamma_{n}
be a counterclockwise closed rectangular path on the complex plane, as illustrated in Figure
3
.
Let us further define
h\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
, where
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\textnormal{Re}(z)>0\}
, such that

\displaystyle h(z)=\frac{\left(\sqrt{z}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(z+1)\Gamma(n-z+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}\right)^{z}\cot(\pi z).

The poles of
h(z)
inside
\gamma_{n}
are at
z=1,2\dots,n-1
. Note that these poles are simple and the residue at each pole is given by

\displaystyle\text{Res}(h,m)=\frac{\left(\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(n-m+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}\right)^{m}\frac{1}{\pi}.

Cauchy’s Residue theorem implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{n}}h(z)\,dz

\displaystyle=2\pi i\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\text{Res}(h,m)

\displaystyle=2i\sum_{m=1}^{n-1}\frac{\left(\sqrt{m}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(m+1)\Gamma(n-m+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}\right)^{m}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle e_{J,J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2in}(1-p_{J})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)\int_{\gamma_{n}}h(z)\,dz.

(20)

Figure 3
:
The rectangular contour
\gamma_{n}
used in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Here,
\delta
and
\xi
are small positive real numbers which are both less than 1.

We now compute the leading order asymptotics of the contour integral in (
20
). Define

\displaystyle I_{n}=\int_{\gamma_{n}}h(z)\,dz.

(21)

We first make a change of variable
y=\frac{z}{n}
. Let
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
be the contour after the change of variable (see Figure
4
). We then get

\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle=n\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}h(ny)\,dy

\displaystyle=n\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{ny}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(ny+1)\Gamma(n-ny+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}\right)^{ny}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy

\displaystyle=n^{2}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma(ny+1)\Gamma(n-ny+1)}e^{\alpha ny}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy,

where

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\log\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}}.

(22)

Figure 4
:
The rectangular contour
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
is obtained from
\gamma_{n}
via a change of variable.

Recall the well-known Stirling’s approximation for the Gamma function when
\textnormal{Re}(x)>0
. There exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|\Gamma(x+1)-\sqrt{2\pi x}e^{x(\log x-1)}|<C\frac{|\sqrt{2\pi x}|e^{\textnormal{Re}(x(\log x-1))}}{|x|}.

When computing the leading order asymptotics of
I_{n}
, we can replace
\Gamma(x+1)
with the following asymptotics

\displaystyle\Gamma(x+1)\sim\sqrt{2\pi x}e^{x(\log x-1)},

(23)

since the error term decays with one more negative power of
n
, and thus can be ignored.
Plugging (
23
) into
I_{n}
yields

\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle\sim n^{2}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{2\pi\sqrt{ny}\sqrt{n(1-y)}}e^{\alpha ny-ny(\log ny-1)-n(1-y)(\log n(1-y)-1)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}e^{\alpha ny-ny(\log ny-1)-n(1-y)(\log n(1-y)-1)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{2\pi}e^{-n\log n}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{y(1-y)}}e^{n[\alpha y-y(\log y-1)-(1-y)(\log(1-y)-1)]}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy

For convenience, let us define

\displaystyle\psi(y)

\displaystyle=\alpha y-y(\log y-1)-(1-y)(\log(1-y)-1),

\displaystyle\rho(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{y(1-y)}},

where
\alpha
is given as in (
22
).
Then,

\displaystyle I_{n}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{n}{2\pi}e^{-n\log n}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy.

(24)

We now turn to examine the integral

\displaystyle H_{n}=\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy.

Notice that the contour
\hat{\gamma}_{n}
has four components:
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
,
\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
,
\hat{\gamma}_{n,3}
and
\hat{\gamma}_{n,4}
given as in Figure
4
, and we can evaluate the integral over each component. We define

\displaystyle H_{n}^{j}=\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy,~~~~j=1,2,3,4.

Then, clearly,

\displaystyle H=\sum_{j=1}^{4}H_{n}^{j}.

First, for
H_{n}^{1}
, notice that

\displaystyle H_{n}^{1}=-\int_{-\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\cot(n \pi y)\,dy,

where
-\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
denotes path with the opposite orientation of
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
and
-\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
has the following parametrization

\displaystyle y=x+\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}i,~~~~\frac{\delta}{n}\leq x\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}.

From Lemma
B.5
,

\displaystyle|\cot(\pi n y)-(-i)|\leq\frac{2}{e^{2\pi n\textnormal{Im}(y)}-1}=\frac{2}{e^{2\pi\xi\sqrt{n}}-1}.

(25)

Then, we can replace
\cot(n\pi y)
with following asymptotics

\displaystyle\cot(n \pi y)=\cot(n \pi x + \sqrt{n} \pi\xi i)\sim-i,

(26)

since the error term decays exponentially with
\sqrt{n}
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle H_{n}^{1}

\displaystyle\sim i\int_{-\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\,dy.

(27)

Next, we continuously deform
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
into a new curve as illustrated in Figure
5
. Since the contribution over the vertical components can be handled together with
H_{n}^{2}
and
H_{n}^{4}
, which we will soon prove negligible, we focus on the component over the real line. We then have

\displaystyle\int_{-\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\rho(y)e^{n\psi(y)}\,dy\sim\int^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}_{\frac{\delta}{n}}\rho(x)e^{n\psi(x)}\,dx.

(28)

Figure 5
:
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
can be continuously deformed into a new curve (shown in blue) with the same orientation and end points. The new curve has two vertical components and a horizontal component contained completely within the real line.

To analyze the asymptotic behavior of the real definite integral in (
28
), we apply the saddle point analysis, which says that the leading order asymptotics are determined by the behavior of the integrand near the critical points of
\psi(x)
. Towards this,
we find the critical point
x_{0}
by solving

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}(x)=\alpha-\log x+\log(1-x)=\log(\frac{p_J}{1-p_J})-\log(\frac{x}{1-x})=0,

and we get

x_{0}=p_{J}.

Note that
\gamma
is real along
\left[\frac{\delta}{n},1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right]\subseteq\mathbb{C}.

Further,
\psi^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x<x_{0}
, and
\psi^{\prime}(x)<0
for
x>x_{0}
. Thus,
\psi(x)
decays as
x
moves away from
x_{0}
.
We now expand
\rho(x)e^{n\psi(x)}
around
x=x_{0}
. We write

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}\rho(x)e^{n\psi(x)}\,dx=e^{n\psi(x_{0})}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}\rho(x)e^{n(\psi(x)-\psi(x_{0}))}\,dx.

(29)

Since
\rho(x)
vanishes to the second order at
x_{0}
, we have to go to higher orders in the standard stationary phase approximation to (
29
).
We thus introduce the following change of variable, where we write
\psi(x)-\psi(x_{0})
as the negative square of a formal power series. Precisely, let

\displaystyle z(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}(x-x_{0})^{k}.

(30)

We require that

\displaystyle-z(x)^{2}=\psi(x)-\psi(x_{0})=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\frac{\psi^{(m)}(x_{0})}{m!}(x-x_{0})^{m}.

(31)

The Taylor expansion starts only at
m=2
, since the first derivative of
\psi
vanishes at
x=x_{0}
.
Now we compute
z(x)^{2}
explicitly using the power series expression and get

\displaystyle z(x)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{\infty}c_{k_{1}}c_{k_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{k_{1}+k_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=2}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}c_{k}c_{m-k}\right)(x-x_{0})^{m}

(32)

Combining (
31
) and (
32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}c_{k}c_{m-k}\right)=-\frac{\psi^{(m)}(x_{0})}{m!}

and this allows us to compute the
c_{k}
’s explicitly. Particularly, for
m=2
, we have

\displaystyle c_{1}^{2}=-\frac{\psi^{(2)}(x_{0})}{2!}=\frac{1}{2(1-x_{0})x_{0}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle c_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1-p_{J})p_{J}}}.

We now define the inversion series of (
30
) using

\displaystyle x-x_{0}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{l}z^{l}.

(33)

Plugging (
33
) into (
30
), we see that

a_{l}
’s must satisfy

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{l}z^{l}\right)^{k}=c_{1}a_{1}z+(c_{1}a_{2}+c_{2}a_{1}^{2})z^{2}+(c_{1}a_{3}+2c_{2}a_{1}a_{2}+c_{3}a_{1}^{3})z^{3}+\dots

Hence, we must have

\displaystyle c_{1}a_{1}=1

\displaystyle c_{1}a_{2}+c_{2}a_{1}^{2}=0

\displaystyle c_{1}a_{3}+2c_{2}a_{1}a_{2}+c_{3}a_{1}^{3}=0

and so on and so forth. Specially, we can solve for
a_{1}
such that

\displaystyle a_{1}=\sqrt{2(1-p_{J})p_{J}}.

(34)

We further note that

\displaystyle dx=\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{l}lz^{l-1}\right)dz.

Then, apply the change of variable in (
33
) to (
29
), the integrand becomes

\displaystyle\rho(x)e^{n(\psi(x)-\psi(x_{0}))}\,dx

\displaystyle=\left[\rho(x_{0})+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{(m)}(x_{0})}{m!}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{l}z^{l}\right)^{m}\right]e^{-nz^{2}}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}a_{l}lz^{l-1}\right)\,dz

(35)

Now we organize the terms in (
35
) in ascending order of
z^{l}e^{-nz^{2}}
. Since

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{l}e^{-nz^{2}}\,dz=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{l+1}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{l}e^{-z^{2}}\,dz,

and since
\rho(x_{0})=\rho^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}\rho(x)e^{n\psi(x)}\,dx

\displaystyle\sim e^{n\psi(x_{0})}\frac{a_{1}^{3}}{n}\frac{\rho^{(2)}(x_{0})}{2!}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{2}e^{-z^{2}}\,dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{8}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{3}(1-p_{J})\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}

(36)

In (
36
), we have used

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}z^{2}e^{-z^{2}}=\sqrt{\pi}/2

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{(2)}(x_{0})}{2!}=\frac{1}{4p_{J}\sqrt{(1-p_{J})p_{J}}}

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0})}=\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}.

In summary, from (
27
), (
28
) and (
36
), we obtain

\displaystyle H_{n}^{1}\sim i\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{8}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{3}(1-p_{J})\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}.

The computation for
H_{n}^{3}
is completely analogous.
On
\hat{\gamma}_{n,3}
, we use
i
(instead of
-i
) to approximate for
\cot(n \pi y)
. With an additional minus sign coming from the parametrization of
\hat{\gamma}_{n,3}
, which is in the opposite direction of
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
, we get the same leading order asymptotic for
H_{n}^{3}
.

The contribution from
H_{n}^{2}
and
H_{n}^{4}
can be ignored. To see this, we parametrize
\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
by

\displaystyle y=\frac{\delta}{n}-ti,~~~~-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\leq t\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

From the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle|H_{n}^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\rho(y)|e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}|\cot(n \pi y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\rho(y)|e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}|\cot(n \pi y)|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dy|.

Of course,

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dy|=\frac{2\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

We focus on bounding the function
|\rho(y)|e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}|\cot(n \pi y)|
.
It is enough to show that there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\rho(y)|e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}|\cot(n \pi y)|\leq C_{0}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\frac{e^{n}\theta^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}},

(37)

since (
37
) clearly implies that
|H_{n}^{2}|
decays exponentially faster than
H_{n}^{1}
. Let
y\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
. From Lemma
B.6
,

\displaystyle|\cot(n\pi y)|\leq 1+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{1-\cos(2\pi\delta)^{2}}}.

We further compute that

\displaystyle|\rho(y)|\leq\frac{\left(\sqrt{\textnormal{Re}(y)}+\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{\textnormal{Re}(y)}\sqrt{\textnormal{Re}(1-y)}}=\frac{\left(\left(\frac{\delta^{2}}{n^{2}}+\frac{\xi^{2}}{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}+\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)^{2}}{\sqrt{\tfrac{\delta}{n}}\sqrt{1-\tfrac{\delta}{n}}}

where the right hand side is bounded above by a polynomial of
n
. Then, to prove (
37
), it suffices to show that for
n
large enough there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}\leq C_{1}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\frac{e^{n}\theta^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}.

(38)

From Lemma
B.7
, we get

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\textnormal{Re}(y)-\textnormal{Re}(y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(y))-\textnormal{Re}(1-y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(1-y))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2}|\textnormal{Im}(y)|+\frac{\pi}{2}|\textnormal{Im}(1-y)|+1.

Since
\textnormal{Re}(y)=\frac{\delta}{n}
,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\frac{\delta}{n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n})-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\pi\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}+1.

Hence,

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(y))}\leq\frac{e^{\alpha\delta+\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}e^{n}}{\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta}\left(1-\tfrac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n-\delta}},

which clearly satisfies (
38
). Therefore,
H_{n}^{2}
is negligible.
Using the same kind of techniques, we establish that
H_{n}^{4}
can also be ignored.

In total, we have

\displaystyle H_{n}\sim i\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}}\right)^{3}(1-p_{J})\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}.

(39)

Inserting (
39
) into (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle I_{n}\sim\frac{i}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}e^{-n\log n}(1-p_{J})\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J})^{n}}.

Finally, from (
20
), we get

\displaystyle e_{J,J}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2in}(1-p_{J})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1-p_{J}}{4n}.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
e_{J,J^{\prime}}
be defined as in (
14
)
with
J\neq J^{\prime}
. Then the large
n
leading order asymptotics of
e_{J,J^{\prime}}
is

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}\sim-\frac{\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}}{4n}.

(40)

Proof.

We begin by noticing that

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}=0\\
m_{1}+m_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{m_{1}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{\tfrac{m_{2}}{n}}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)P(S^{(J)}_{n}=m_{1},S^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}=m_{2}),

and

\displaystyle P(S^{(J)}_{n}=m_{1},S^{(J^{\prime})}_{n}=m_{2})=\binom{n}{m_{1}}\binom{n-m_{1}}{m_{2}}p_{J}^{m_{1}}p_{J^{\prime}}^{m_{2}}(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n-m_{1}-m_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(m_{1}+1)\Gamma(m_{2}+1)\Gamma(n-m_{1}-m_{2}+1)}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{m_{1}}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{m_{2}}.

For simplicity of notation, let us define

\displaystyle\alpha=\log(\frac{p_J}{1-p_J - p_{J'}}),

\displaystyle\beta=\log(\frac{p_{J'}}{1-p_J - p_{J'}}),

and

\displaystyle t_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\frac{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)}{n}\,\frac{\left(\sqrt{m_{1}}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{m_{2}}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha m_{1}+\beta m_{2}}}{\Gamma(m_{1}+1)\Gamma(m_{2}+1)\Gamma(n-m_{1}-m_{2}+1)}.

(41)

Clearly, we have

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},m_{2}=0\\
m_{1}+m_{2}\leq n\end{subarray}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}.

We now decompose the sum into the following components according to their asymptotic behavior

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle t_{0,0}+t_{0,n}+t_{n,0}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}=0\\
1\leq m_{2}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}=0\\
1\leq m_{1}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}+\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in P_{n}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}

where

\displaystyle P_{n}=\{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\mid 1\leq m_{1}\leq n-2,1\leq m_{2}\leq n-2,m_{1}+m_{2}<n\}.

The terms
t_{0,0},t_{0,n},t_{n,0}
decay exponentially with
n
. This follows from the facts that

\displaystyle t_{0,0}

\displaystyle=(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle t_{0,n}

\displaystyle=p_{J^{\prime}}^{n}\sqrt{p_{J}}(\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}-1)

\displaystyle t_{n,0}

\displaystyle=p_{J}^{n}\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}(\sqrt{p_{J}}-1).

The terms
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}=0\\
1\leq m_{2}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}
and
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{2}=0\\
1\leq m_{1}\leq n-1\end{subarray}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}
are effectively one-dimensional. By contour integral method similar to the proof of Lemma
3.4
, these terms also exhibit exponential decay in
n
. For details, see Lemma
B.8
and Corollary
B.9
.
The non-trivial contribution to the leading order asymptotics come from the two-dimensional sum over
P_{n}
. The calculation proceeds similarly to previous cases except we now get two dimensional contour integrals.
This suggests that we define

T_{n}={\gamma}_{n}\times{\gamma}_{n},

where
\gamma_{n}
is given as in Figure
3
.
Clearly,

\displaystyle T_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2}-\left((\mathbb{C}_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+})\cup(\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{C}_{+})\right).

Consider
h\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2})
such that

\displaystyle h(x,y)=\frac{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha x+\beta y}}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(y+1)\Gamma(n-x-y+1)}\cot(\pi x)\cot(\pi y).

(42)

and let

\displaystyle h_{n}(x,y)=h(nx,ny).

(43)

Define

\displaystyle I_{n}=\int_{T_{n}}h(x,y)\differential x\differential y.

Apply Cauchy’s residue theorem one variable at a time, and we obtain that

\displaystyle I_{n}=

\displaystyle\int_{\gamma_{n}}\int_{\gamma_{n}}h(x,y)dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{2i}\int_{\gamma_{n}}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{n-1}\frac{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{m_{2}}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha x+\beta m_{2}}}{\Gamma(x+1)\Gamma(m_{2}+1)\Gamma(n-x-m_{2}+1)}\cot(\pi x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{(2i)^{2}}\sum_{m_{1}=1}^{n-1}\sum_{m_{2}=1}^{n-1}\frac{\left(\sqrt{m_{1}}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{m_{2}}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha m_{1}+\beta m_{2}}}{\Gamma(m_{1}+1)\Gamma(m_{2}+1)\Gamma(n-m_{1}-m_{2}+1)}

Note that, for all
m_{2}+m_{2}>n

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(n-m_{1}-m_{2}+1)}=0.

We thus get that

\displaystyle I_{n}=

\displaystyle{(2i)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq m_{1}\leq n-1\\
m_{2}=n-m_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left(\sqrt{m_{1}}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{m_{2}}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha m_{1}+\beta m_{2}}}{\Gamma(m_{1}+1)\Gamma(m_{2}+1)\Gamma(n-m_{1}-m_{2}+1)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}\sim\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in P_{n}}t_{m_{1},m_{2}}=\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}\frac{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}\Gamma(n+1)}{n}\,I_{n}.

(44)

It remains to compute the leading order asymptotics for

. Towards this, we would like to use Stirling’s approximation to give asymptotics for
\Gamma(x+1)
,
\Gamma(y+1)

\Gamma(n-x-y+1)

into two parts based on the applicability of Stirling’s approximation and apply different strategies to each part.
For convenience, we first perform a change of variable

and
y\mapsto ny
to get

\displaystyle I_{n}=n^{2}\int_{\hat{T}_{n}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy.

Here,

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{n}=\hat{\gamma}_{n}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n},

\hat{\gamma}_{n}

4

Since
\hat{T}_{n}

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{n}=\bigcup_{i,j=1,2,3,4}\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j},

we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{T}_{n}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy=\sum_{i=1}^{4}\sum_{j=1}^{4}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy.

The non-trivial contribution to the leading order asymptotics comes from the integral over
\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}
where

, and the rest are negligible as we will prove below.

We focus on the integral over
\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}
with

.
First, let
\phi(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C}_{+}^{2})

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\frac{\left(\sqrt{nx}-\sqrt{np_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{ny}-\sqrt{np_{J^{\prime}}}\right)e^{\alpha nx+\beta ny}}{\Gamma(nx+1)\Gamma(ny+1)\Gamma(n-nx-ny+1)}.

(45)

From Lemma
B.5
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\cot(n\pi x)\cot(n\pi y)\,dxdy

\displaystyle\sim(-i)^{2}\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy,

Let us further define

\displaystyle W_{n,0}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\mid\frac{\delta}{n}\leq x,y\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right\},

\displaystyle W_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=\frac{\delta}{n}+si,\,y=t+si,\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\left.\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n},\,0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\right\},

\displaystyle W_{n,2}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=t+si,\,y=\frac{\delta}{n}+si,\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\left.\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n},\,0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\right\},

\displaystyle W_{n,3}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=1-\frac{\delta}{n}+si,\,y=t+si,\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\left.\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n},\,0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\right\},

\displaystyle W_{n,4}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=t+si,\,y=1-\frac{\delta}{n}+si,\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\left.\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n},\,0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\right\},

Since
\phi(x,y)
is holomorphic for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\textnormal{Re}(x)>0
and
\textnormal{Re}(y)>0
, and since
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
is homologous to
W_{n}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{l=0}^{4}W_{n,l}
relative to their common boundary.
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy=\sum_{l=0}^{4}\int_{W_{n,l}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy.

We further divide
W_{n,0}
into a lower triangular piece
L^{-}_{n}
, a thin strip
U_{n}
along the diagonal, and an upper triangular piece
L^{+}_{n}
. That is

\displaystyle W_{n,0}=L^{-}_{n}\cup U_{n}\cup L^{+}_{n},

where

\displaystyle L^{-}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in W_{n,0}\mid x+y\leq 1-\frac{1}{2n}\right\},

\displaystyle U_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in W_{n,0}\mid 1-\frac{1}{2n}<x+y\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}\right\},

\displaystyle L^{+}_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in W_{n,0}\mid x+y>1+\frac{1}{n}\right\}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{W_{n,0}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy=\int_{L^{-}_{n}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{U_{n}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy+\int_{L^{+}_{n}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy.

Below we compute the leading order asymptotics of the integral over
L^{-}_{n}
.
For
(x,y)\in L^{-}_{n}
, the Stirling’s approximation of the Gamma function (
23
) yields

\displaystyle\Gamma(nx+1)\Gamma(ny+1)\Gamma(n-nx-ny+1)

\displaystyle\hskip 7.22743pt\sim(\sqrt{2\pi})^{3}(\sqrt{n})^{3}\sqrt{xy(1-x-y)}e^{n\log n-n}e^{n(x\log x+y\log y+(1-x-y)\log(1-x-y)}.

We therefore obtain

\displaystyle\int_{L^{-}_{n}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\sim e^{n-n\log n}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\int_{L^{-}_{n}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}\,dxdy,

(46)

where

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log x-y\log y-(1-x-y)\log(1-x-y),

\displaystyle\rho(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}\right)\left(\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)}{\sqrt{xy(1-x-y)}}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{L^{-}_{n}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}\,dxdy=\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}-y}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}\,dxdy.

We proceed by applying the saddle point method to the integral above. Note that
\psi
is real on
W_{n,0}
.
The critical point
(x_{0},y_{0})
of
\psi(x,y)
is obtained from solving the following system of equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}=\alpha-\log(x)+\log(1-x-y)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}=\beta-\log(y)+\log(1-x-y)=0.

The system has a unique solution
(x_{0},y_{0})=(p_{J},p_{J^{\prime}}).
Since
p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}\leq 1
,
(x_{0},y_{0})\in L^{-}_{n}.

Furthermore, the Hessian matrix is given by

\displaystyle H=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x^{2}}&\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x\partial y}\\
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y\partial x}&\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial y^{2}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{1-x-y}&-\frac{1}{1-x-y}\\
-\frac{1}{1-x-y}&-\frac{1}{y}-\frac{1}{1-x-y}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
denote the eigenvalues of
H
. Since for all
x,y\in L^{-}_{n}
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}=\det(H)=\frac{1-y-x}{xy(1-x-y)^{2}}>0,

and since

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}=\textnormal{Tr}(H)=-\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{y}-\frac{2}{1-x-y}<0,

both
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are strictly negative, and hence the Hessian
H
is negative definite over
L^{-}_{n}
.
Therefore, the function
\psi(x,y)
attains its maximum value at the critical point
(x_{0},y_{0})=(p_{J},p_{J^{\prime}})
.

Let

\displaystyle z=(z_{1},z_{2})

be new coordinates on
L_{n}^{-}
near
(x_{0},y_{0})
,
where

\displaystyle z_{i}(x,y)=\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{\infty}c^{(i)}_{l_{1},l_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{l_{1}}(y-y_{0})^{l_{2}},~~~~~i=1,2.

(47)

Let

\displaystyle\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}-\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{0},y_{0})&-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x\partial y}(x_{0},y_{0})\\
-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y\partial x}(x_{0},y_{0})&-\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial y^{2}}(x_{0},y_{0})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1-p_{J^{\prime}}}{p_{J}(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}&\frac{1}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\\
\frac{1}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}&\frac{1-p_{J}}{p_{J^{\prime}}(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\end{bmatrix}.

We require that

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z=\psi(x,y)-\psi(x_{0},y_{0}).

(48)

Taylor expansion of the right-hand side of (
48
) yields

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)-\psi(x_{0},y_{0})=\frac{\psi_{xx}(x_{0},y_{0})}{2}(x-x_{0})^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.45377pt+\psi_{xy}(x_{0},y_{0})(x-x_{0})(y-y_{0})+\frac{\psi_{yy}(x_{0},y_{0})}{2}(y-y_{0})^{2}+\dots.

For simplicity, we write
\psi_{xx}=\psi_{xx}(x_{0},y_{0})
,
\psi_{xy}=\psi_{xy}(x_{0},y_{0})
and
\psi_{yy}=\psi_{yy}(x_{0},y_{0})
.
On the left hand side of (
48
), we have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}z_{1}^{2}+\psi_{xy}z_{1}z_{2}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}z_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{\infty}c^{(1)}_{l_{1},l_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{l_{1}}(y-y_{0})^{l_{2}}\right)^{2}+

\displaystyle~~~~\psi_{xy}\left(\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{\infty}c^{(1)}_{l_{1},l_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{l_{1}}(y-y_{0})^{l_{2}}\right)\left(\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{\infty}c^{(2)}_{l_{1},l_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{l_{1}}(y-y_{0})^{l_{2}}\right)+

\displaystyle~~~~\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(\sum_{l_{1},l_{2}=0}^{\infty}c^{(2)}_{l_{1},l_{2}}(x-x_{0})^{l_{1}}(y-y_{0})^{l_{2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,0}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{0,0}\right)^{2}+

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(1)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}\right)(x-x_{0})+

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(1)}_{0,1}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}\right)(y-y_{0})+

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{1,0}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{1,0}\right)^{2}\right)(x-x_{0})^{2}+

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,1}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{0,1}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{0,1}\right)^{2}\right)(y-y_{0})^{2}+

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(1)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}\right)+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{1,0}\right)(x-x_{0})(y-y_{0})+\dots

Comparing both sides term by term, we obtain from the constant term

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,0}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{0,0}\right)^{2}=0.

(49)

Since
\Sigma
is positive definite, the only solution to (
49

.
Therefore, the coefficients of the linear term on the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(1)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(1)}_{0,1}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}=0.

For the quadratic terms, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{1,0}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{1,0}\right)^{2}=\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,1}\right)^{2}+\psi_{xy}c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{0,1}+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}\left(c^{(2)}_{0,1}\right)^{2}=\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{xx}}{2}c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(1)}_{1,0}+\psi_{xy}\left(c^{(1)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{1,0}+c^{(1)}_{1,0}c^{(2)}_{0,1}\right)+\frac{\psi_{yy}}{2}c^{(2)}_{0,1}c^{(2)}_{1,0}=\psi_{xy}

One solution is given by

\displaystyle c^{(1)}_{0,1}=c^{(2)}_{1,0}=0,

\displaystyle c^{(1)}_{1,0}=c^{(2)}_{0,1}=1.

The rest of the coefficients can be computed in a similar way.

We now define the inversion series of (
47
) to be

\displaystyle x-x_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}

\displaystyle y-y_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}b_{l_{1},l_{2}}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}

Plugging this into (
47
), we get that

’s and

\displaystyle z_{1}

\displaystyle=c^{(1)}_{1,0}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}+\dots=c^{(1)}_{1,0}a_{1,0}z_{1}+c^{(1)}_{1,0}a_{0,1}z_{2}\dots

\displaystyle z_{2}

\displaystyle=c^{(2)}_{0,1}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}b_{l_{1},l_{2}}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}+\dots=c^{(2)}_{1,0}b_{1,0}z_{1}+c^{(2)}_{0,1}b_{0,1}z_{2}+\dots

Therefore,

\displaystyle a_{1,0}=b_{0,1}=1,

\displaystyle a_{0,1}=b_{1,0}=0.

The rest of the coefficients can be computed similarly.

We further note that

\displaystyle dx\wedge dy=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1\\
m_{1}+m_{2}\geq 1\end{subarray}}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}b_{m_{1},m_{2}}(l_{1}m_{2}-l_{2}m_{1})z_{1}^{l_{1}+m_{1}-1}z_{2}^{l_{2}+m_{2}-1}dz_{1}\wedge dz_{2}.

Particularly, when

and

, the coefficient is

\displaystyle a_{1,0}b_{0,1}+a_{0,1}b_{1,0}=1.

Then, using this change of variable, we can re-write the integral as

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}-y}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}-y}\rho(x,y)e^{n(\psi(x,y)-\psi(x_{0},y_{0}))}\,dxdy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}}\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{1}{2n}-y}(\rho(x_{0},y_{0})+\rho_{x}(x_{0},y_{0})(x-x_{0})+\rho_{y}(x_{0},y_{0})(y-y_{0})+

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{xx}(x_{0},y_{0})}{2}(x-x_{0})^{2}+\rho_{xy}(x_{0},y_{0})(x-x_{0})(y-y_{0})+\dots)

\displaystyle e^{n(\psi(x,y)-\psi(x_{0},y_{0}))}dxdy

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\rho(x_{0},y_{0})+\rho_{x}(x_{0},y_{0})\left(\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}z_{1}^{l_{1}}z_{2}^{l_{2}}\right)+\dots\right)e^{-\frac{n}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}

\displaystyle\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}\geq 1}\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}\geq 1}a_{l_{1},l_{2}}b_{m_{1},m_{2}}(l_{1}m_{2}-l_{2}m_{1})z_{1}^{l_{1}+m_{1}-1}z_{2}^{l_{2}+m_{2}-1}dz_{1}dz_{2}.

(50)

Now we organize the terms in (
50
) in ascending order of the exponents of
z_{1}^{r_{1}}z_{2}^{r_{2}}e^{-\frac{n}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}
. Since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}z_{1}^{r_{1}}z_{2}^{r_{2}}e^{-\frac{n}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}\,dz_{1}dz_{2}=\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{\frac{r_{1}+r_{2}}{2}+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}z_{1}^{r_{1}}z_{2}^{r_{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}\,dz_{1}dz_{2}

and since
\rho(x_{0},y_{0})
,
\rho_{x}(x_{0},y_{0})
,
\rho_{y}(x_{0},y_{0})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}\,dxdy\,\sim

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}\frac{\rho_{xy}(x_{0},y_{0})}{n^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}z_{1}z_{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}\,dz_{1}dz_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{2n^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}.

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}z_{1}z_{2}e^{-\frac{1}{2}z^{\intercal}\Sigma z}\,dz_{1}dz_{2}=2\pi\sqrt{\det(\Sigma^{-1})}\Haf(\Sigma^{-1})=-2\pi(\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}})^{3}\sqrt{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}=\frac{1}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}.

Finally, we obtain from (

\displaystyle\int_{L^{-}_{n}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\sim-\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}\frac{1}{n^{n}}.

over

In total, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\sim-\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}\frac{1}{n^{n}},

and thus

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\sim\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}\frac{1}{n^{n}}.

depending on whether

and
y
lie above or below the real axis, as well as the orientation of the surface.
The remaining
\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}
’s do not contribute to the leading order asymptotics. We provide proofs for a few representative cases to illustrate the key techniques. See Lemmas
B.14
,
B.15
, and
B.16
. The proof for the rest of are completely similar to the cases treated in these lemmas.

In the end, we see that

\displaystyle I_{n}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}\frac{e^{n}}{(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})^{n}}\frac{1}{n^{n}}.

(53)

Inserting (

) into (

), we get the leading order asymptotics is

\displaystyle e_{J,J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{(2i)^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}}{n}\Gamma(n+1)\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi n}}\frac{e^{n}}{n^{n}}

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{\sqrt{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}}{4n}.

∎

3.2
The importance sampling method: GBS-I

The GBS-I algorithm solves the special cases
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
using the idea of importance sampling.
Let
I_{1},\dots,I_{n}
be
n
independent draws from
P_{tB}
where
m_{tB}=2K
. We define the GBS-I estimator to be

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{I_{i}!}{d_{t}}a_{I_{i}}t^{-2K}.

It is straightforward to show that the GBS-I estimator is unbiased. In other words, for all
n

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}]=\mu_{\Haf^{2}}

By WLLN, we get

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P(|\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}]-\mu^{k}_{\Haf^{2}}|>\epsilon)=0.

(54)

Thus, GBS-I solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
for large enough
n
.
Furthermore, from Chevyshev’s inequality, a
guaranteed sample size

n_{\text{GBS-I}}
can be computed explicitly.

Theorem 3.5
.

The GBS-I estimator
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)

with

\displaystyle n\geq\frac{V^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}}{\delta\epsilon^{2}|\mu_{\Haf^{2}}|^{2}}:=n^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}

where

\displaystyle V^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}=\left(\sum_{|J|=2K}a^{2}_{J}t^{-4K}J!\Haf(B_{I})^{2}-|\mu_{\Haf^{2}}|^{2}\right).

Proof.

The key ingredient of this proof is to compute the variance of the GBS-I estimator.

\displaystyle\textnormal{Var}(\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}-|\mu_{\Haf^{2}}|^{2})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{d_{t}}\sum_{|J|=2K}a^{2}_{J}t^{-4K}J!\Haf(B_{J})^{2}-|\mu_{\Haf^{2}}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}V^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}.

The rest of the proof is straightforward from Chebyshev’s inequality.
∎

Similar to Corollary
3.2
, we can also show the optimal
t_{0}
for the GBS-I estimator is again when
m_{t_{0}B}=2K
.

Corollary 3.6
.

A real value
t_{0}
is a solution to

\displaystyle t_{0}=\argmin_{t\in(0,\lambda_{1}^{-1})}\mathbb{E}\left[|\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{n}-\mu_{\Haf}|^{2}\right]

if and only if
m_{t_{0}B}=2K
.

Proof.

The proof is omitted due to similarity to Corollary
3.2
.
∎

). For
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2},K}(\epsilon,\delta)

4
Percentage of GBS advantage

4.1
Theoretical formulation

We start by comparing GBS-P and MC. For an arbitrary
N
and
K
, let us denote
\sigma(N,K)=\#\left\{|I|=2K|\right\}
to be the size of the coefficient set. Let
\mathbb{S}^{\sigma(N,K)-1}
be the unit sphere such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{\sigma(N,K)-1}=\left\{(a_{I})_{|I|=2K}:\sum_{|I|=2K}a^{2}_{I}=1\right\}.

Let
M(N)
be the space of real, symmetric
N\times N
matrices whose eigenvalues are bounded strictly between 0 and 1,

\displaystyle M(N)=\left\{M\in\textnormal{Sym}(N):~\text{spec}(M)\in(0,1)\right\}.

The problem space
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}(N,K)
is thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}(N,K)=\mathbb{S}^{\sigma(N,K)-1}\times M(N).

To assign a volume form on
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}
, we first choose a normalized rotational invariant measure
dS
on
\mathbb{S}^{\sigma(N,K)-1}
. Recall that the Lebesgue measure on
\text{Sym}(N)
is given on the matrix entries as

\displaystyle dM=\prod_{i=1}^{N}dM_{ii}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\prod_{j=i+1}^{N}dM_{ij}.

A real symmetric matrix admits a diagonalization
M=U^{-1}\Lambda U
, where
U\in SO(N)
is an orthonormal matrix and
\Lambda=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
consists of all the eigenvalues of
M
.
The Lebesgue measure is invariant under conjugation by orthogonal matrices. Hence, via a change of variable, we obtain

\displaystyle dM=|\Delta(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})|\prod_{i}^{N}d\lambda_{i}dU.

(55)

Here,
\Delta(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})
is the Vandermonde determinant such that

\Delta(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq N}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})

and
dU
is the normalized Haar measure on
SO(N)
. We scale
dM
so as to make the measure integrate to 1. In other words,

\displaystyle\int_{M(N)}dM=\int_{SO(N)}\int_{[0,1]^{N}}c_{N}|\Delta(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})|\prod_{i}^{N}d\lambda_{i}dU=1,

which yields

\displaystyle c_{N}=\left(\int_{[0,1]^{N}}|\Delta(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})|\prod_{i}^{N}d\lambda_{i}\right)^{-1}.

The volume form on
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}
is thus the product
dS\times dM
, which we denote for convenience as
d\mu
.

Now we compute the percentage of GBS-P outperforms MC over the problem space
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}
as the following integral.

\displaystyle P^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}}H(n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}-n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf})\,d\mu

Here,
H(x)
denote the heavyside step function such that
H(x)=1
if
x\geq 0
and 0 otherwise. We think of
n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}
and
n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}
as functions of
\eta
where
\eta\in\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}
.
Since

\displaystyle H(n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}(\eta)-n^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}(\eta))=H(V^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf}(\eta)-V^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}(\eta)),

P^{\textnormal{GBS-P}}_{\Haf}
is
independent of the choice of
\epsilon
and
\delta
.

Similarly, for
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf^{2}}(\epsilon,\delta)
, we find that the problem space
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}
is the same as
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf}
. Therefore, we compute the proportion of
\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}
where GBS-I outperforms MC to be

\displaystyle P^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{P}_{\Haf^{2}}}H(n^{\textnormal{MC}}_{\Haf^{2}}-n^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}})\,d\mu,

and
P^{\textnormal{GBS-I}}_{\Haf^{2}}
is also independent of
\epsilon
and
\delta
.

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose
e_{k}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,k}(\frac{\epsilon}{K}\frac{|\mu_{\Haf}|}{|\mu_{\Haf,k}|},1-\frac{1-\delta}{K})
where
k=1,\dots,K
. Then,
e=\sum_{k=0}^{K}e_{k}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
.

Proof.

Let
A_{k}
denote the event
|\mu_{\Haf,k}-e_{k}|<\frac{\epsilon}{K}{|\mu_{\Haf}|}
and
B_{k}=\overline{A_{k}}
. Let
\tau=\frac{1-\delta}{K}
.
Then,
e_{k}
solves
\mathcal{I}^{\times}_{\Haf,k}(\frac{\epsilon}{K}\frac{|\mu_{\Haf}|}{|\mu_{\Haf,k}|},1-\frac{1-\delta}{K})
implies that
P(A_{k})>1-\tau
and
P(B_{k})\leq\tau
. Let
Q
denote the event
|\mu_{\Haf}-e|<\epsilon|\mu_{\Haf}|
. Note that
A_{1}\cap A_{2}\dots\cap A_{K}
implies
Q
. To see this, we note

\displaystyle|\mu_{\Haf}-e|=\lvert\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mu_{\Haf,k}-e_{k}\rvert\leq\sum_{k=0}^{K}\lvert\mu_{\Haf,k}-e_{k}\rvert\leq\epsilon|\mu_{\Haf}|,

which implies
Q
is true. Therefore,
P(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap\dots A_{K})<P(Q)
.

We further compute from De Morgan’s Laws and the union bound that

\displaystyle 1-P(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap\dots\cap A_{K})

\displaystyle=P(B_{1}\cup B_{2}\dots\cup B_{K})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}P(B_{k})=K\tau.

Therefore,

P(A_{1}\cap A_{2}\cap\dots A_{K})\geq 1-K\tau>\delta,

which implies that
P(Q)>\delta
and
e
solves
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{\Haf}(\epsilon,\delta)
.
∎

Lemma B.2
.

If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
are eigenvalues of
B
, then

\displaystyle m_{B}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}.

Proof.

[

]
that
the covariance matrix
\Sigma
of

must be of the form

\Sigma=\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_{1}&\Sigma_{2}\\
\Sigma_{2}&\Sigma_{1}\end{bmatrix},

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=U\left(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\cosh^{2}(r_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\right)U^{\intercal}

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=U\left(\bigoplus_{n=1}^{N}\cosh(r_n)\sinh(r_n)-\frac{1}{2}\right)U^{\intercal}.

In these expressions

and

\displaystyle B=U\Lambda U^{\intercal},~~~~\Lambda=\text{diag}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}),~~~~UU^{\intercal}=\mathbb{I}_{N}

and

From Eq. (27) of

\displaystyle m_{i}=U_{ii}-\frac{1}{2}.

\displaystyle m_{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\cosh^{2}(r_{n})-N

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-\tanh^{2}(r_{n})}-N

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}-N

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{1-\lambda_{n}^{2}}

Let

Lemma B.3
.

If
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N}
are eigenvalues of
B
and
m_{B}=2K
, then

\displaystyle p_{K}=\left(\prod_{l=1}^{N}{\sqrt{1-\lambda_{l}^{2}}}\right)\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\dots+k_{N}=K}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(2k_{l})!}{4^{k_{l}}(k_{l}!)^{2}}(\lambda_{l}^{2})^{k_{l}}\right)

(59)

\displaystyle p_{K}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}.

(60)

Proof.

We first prove (
59
).
From (
10
), we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\lambda_{l}^{2}}}.

Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}k!}(x^{2})^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{2k}(k!)^{2}}(x^{2})^{k},

we have that

\displaystyle\prod_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\lambda_{l}^{2}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{k_{l}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k_{l})!}{4^{k_{l}}(k_{l}!)^{2}}(\lambda_{l}^{2})^{k_{l}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\dots k_{N}=k}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(2k_{l})!}{4^{k_{l}}(k_{l}!)^{2}}(\lambda_{l}^{2})^{k_{l}}\right).

(61)

Since

is also the normalization factor to make the sum of the probabilities equal 1, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|I|=2k}\frac{\Haf(B_{I})^{2}}{I!}.

(62)

Equating (

) and (

\displaystyle\sum_{|I|=2k}\frac{\Haf(B_{I})^{2}}{I!}=\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\dots+k_{N}=K}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(2k_{l})!}{4^{k_{l}}(k_{l}!)^{2}}(\lambda_{l}^{2})^{k_{l}}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle p_{K}=d\sum_{|I|=2K}\frac{\Haf(B_{I})^{2}}{I!}=d\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}\dots+k_{N}=K}\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left(\frac{(2k_{l})!}{4^{k_{l}}(k_{l}!)^{2}}(\lambda_{l}^{2})^{k_{l}}\right).

We now prove (

). Let
\mu
be such that

\displaystyle 2K=\frac{\mu}{1-\mu}.

Then,

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{2K}{1+2K}.

Let

\displaystyle S=\left\{(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})\in[0,\mu]^{N}\Big|\,\sum_{l=1}\frac{\lambda^{2}_{l}}{1-\lambda^{2}_{l}}=2K\right\}.

In Lemma

, we see that

Therefore,

\displaystyle\partial S=S\cap\partial\left([0,\mu]^{N}\right)=\bigcup_{l=1}^{N}\left(S_{l}^{0}\cup S_{l}^{\mu}\right),

where

\displaystyle S^{0}_{l}

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in S\mid x_{l}=0\},

\displaystyle S^{\mu}_{l}

\displaystyle=\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in S\mid x_{l}=\mu\}.

\displaystyle D_{ij}=\lambda_{j}(1-\lambda_{i}^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{i}}-\lambda_{i}(1-\lambda_{j}^{2})\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{j}},

which is a vector field along

.
Now consider

\displaystyle f(\lambda)=f(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{N})=\prod_{l=1}^{N}\left(1-\lambda_{l}^{2}\right)

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\dots=\lambda_{N}=\lambda.

(63)

Hence, (

n
sufficiently large,
(x_{0},y_{0})\notin U_{n}
, and the maximum value of
\psi(x,y)
must be attained on the boundary of
U_{n}
. There are four components, and it suffices to prove that the maximum over each component satisfies (
70
).

1.

Along
x+y=1+\frac{1}{n}
, the maximum value of
\psi(x,y)
occurs at

x_{0}=\frac{p_{J}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right),~~~~y_{0}=\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)

as determined using basic calculus. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\psi(x_{0},y_{0})=\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(\log(\frac{p_J + p_{J'}}{1-p_J - p_{J'}})-\log\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)+1\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x,y)}\leq e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}=\left(\frac{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n+1}\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{-(n+1)}e^{n+1},

which clearly satisfies (
70
).

2.

Along
x+y=1-\frac{1}{2n}
, the maximum value of
\psi(x,y)
occurs at

x_{0}=\frac{p_{J}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right),~~~~y_{0}=\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\psi(x_{0},y_{0})=\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right)\left(\log(\frac{p_J + p_{J'}}{1-p_J - p_{J'}})-\log\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right)+1\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x,y)}\leq e^{n\psi(x_{0},y_{0})}=\left(\frac{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}\right)^{-(n-\frac{1}{2})}e^{n-\frac{1}{2}},

which clearly satisfies (
70
).

3.

Along
x=\frac{\delta}{n}
and
1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\leq y\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we see that

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)=\alpha\frac{\delta}{n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n})+\frac{\delta}{n}+\beta y-y\log(y)+y.

Since

\displaystyle e^{n\left(\alpha\frac{\delta}{n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n})+\frac{\delta}{n}\right)}=\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{\delta}\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{-\delta}e^{\delta}

is bounded above by a polynomial of
n
, we only need to give an upper bound for

\rho(y)=\beta y-y\log(y)+y.

The
maximum value of
\rho(y)
over the positive real line occurs at

\displaystyle y_{0}=\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}=\exp(\beta).

Since
p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}<\frac{1}{2}
,

\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}<1.

For
n
sufficiently large,

y_{0}\notin\left[1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n},1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right].

The maximum value of
\rho(y)
is attained at the end points of the interval.
At
y=1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we get

\displaystyle e^{n\rho(y)}\leq\left(\frac{ep_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\left(1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}.

At
y=1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we get

\displaystyle e^{n\rho(y)}\leq\left(\frac{ep_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{n-\delta}.

In both cases, (
70
) is clearly met.

4.

Along
y=\frac{\delta}{n}
and
1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n}\leq x\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, the analysis is completely analogous to Case 3 with
x
and
y
swapped.

This completes the proof of Lemma
B.10
.
∎

Lemma B.11
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{L_{n}^{+}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq C\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

As in Lemma
B.10
, the triangle inequality implies that it is enough to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{L_{n}^{+}}|\phi(x,y)|\leq C_{0}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Let
(x,y)\in L_{n}^{+}
. Recall that Euler’s reflection formula (
71
) states

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)},~~~~\forall z\in\mathbb{C}-\mathbb{Z}.

So, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)}=\frac{\sin(\pi z)}{\pi},~~~~\forall z\in\mathbb{C}.

(71)

Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-n(x+y-1))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sin(\pi n (x+y-1))\Gamma(n(x+y-1)).

Furthermore, it follows from the recursive formula (
68
) that

\displaystyle\Gamma(n(x+y-1))=\frac{\Gamma(n(x+y-1)+1)}{n(x+y-1)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+1)}=\frac{\sin(\pi n (x+y-1))\Gamma(n(x+y-1)+1)}{\pi n(x+y-1)},

and therefore,

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}||\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}|}{\pi|x+y-1|}\frac{e^{\alpha nx+\beta ny}\Gamma(n(x+y-1)+1)}{\Gamma(nx+1)\Gamma(ny+1)}.

Using Stirling’s approximation (
23
), we obtain that

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|\sim\frac{e^{n}}{n^{n}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\psi(x,y)}

for some
\rho(x,y)
bounded above by a polynomial of
n
and

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)=\alpha x+\beta y+(x+y-1)\log(x+y-1)-x\log x-y\log y.

Therefore, it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{L_{n}^{+}}e^{n\psi(x,y)}\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{\theta}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n}.

(72)

Towards this, we find the critical point
(x_{0},y_{0})
of
\psi(x,y)
by solving

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}=\alpha+\log(x+y-1)-\log x=0

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}=\beta+\log(x+y-1)-\log y=0

and we get a unique solution

\displaystyle(x_{0},y_{0})=(p_{J},p_{J^{\prime}})\notin L_{n}^{+}.

Therefore, the maximum of
\psi(x,y)
over
L_{n}^{+}
must be attained at the boundary. There are three components, and it suffices to prove that the maximum over each component satisfies (
72
).

1.

Along
x+y=1+\frac{1}{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)+\beta y+\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)-\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)-y\log y.

Define

\displaystyle\eta(y)=(\beta-\alpha)y-y\log y-\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right).

We find the critical point
y_{0}
of
\eta(y)
by solving

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}(y)=\beta-\alpha+\log\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)-\log y=0,

and we get a unique solution

\displaystyle y_{0}=\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}.

Since for large
n

\displaystyle y_{0}\in\left[\frac{\delta}{n},1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right],

and for all
y\in\left[\frac{\delta}{n},1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right]
,

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime\prime}(y)=-\frac{1+\frac{\delta}{n}}{y\left(1+\frac{\delta}{n}-y\right)}<0,

we see that
y_{0}
is a global maximum. Hence,

\displaystyle\eta(y)\leq-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log( 1 + \frac{1}{n})-\left(1+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log(\frac{p_{J'}}{p_J + p_{J'}} ),

and therefore

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x,y)}\leq\frac{1}{n}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{p_{J}}{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n+1}.

Clearly, (
72
) is satisfied.

2.

Along
x=1-\frac{\delta}{n}
and
\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\delta}{n}\leq y\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)+\beta y+\left(y-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(y-\frac{\delta}{n})

\displaystyle\quad-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1-\frac{\delta}{n})-y\log y.

Define

\displaystyle\nu(y)=\beta y+\left(y-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(y-\frac{\delta}{n})-y\log y.

From

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}(y)=\beta+\log(y-\frac{\delta}{n})-\log y=0,

we get

\displaystyle\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}=\frac{y}{y-\frac{\delta}{n}},

(73)

Lemma
B.3
implies that
p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}<\frac{1}{2}
, and thus

\displaystyle\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}<1.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\frac{y}{y-\frac{\delta}{n}}>1.

Therefore, there is no solution to (
73
) and the function
\nu(y)
has no critical points. The maximum of
\nu(y)
must be attained at one of the two end points of the interval
\left[\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\delta}{n},1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right].
At
y=\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\delta}{n}
, we see that

\displaystyle\nu(y)=\beta\frac{1+\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\log(\frac{1}{n})-\frac{1+\delta}{n}\log(\frac{1 + \delta}{n}),

and hence

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x,y)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}\left(\frac{1+\delta}{n}\right)^{1+\delta}.

Clearly, (
72
) is satisfied.
At
y=1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we see that

\displaystyle\nu(y)=\beta\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)+\left(1-\frac{2\delta}{n}\right)\log(1-\frac{2\delta}{n})-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1-\frac{\delta}{n}),

and hence

\displaystyle e^{n\psi(x,y)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\left(1-\frac{2\delta}{n}\right)^{n-2\delta}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}.

Lemma
B.3
implies that
p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}<\frac{1}{2}
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}<\frac{1}{2}.

Therefore, (
72
) is satisfied.

3.

Along
y=1-\frac{\delta}{n}
and
\frac{1}{n}+\frac{\delta}{n}\leq x\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, the analysis is nearly identical to the previous case after swapping
x
and
y
. We therefore omit its proof to avoid repetition.

∎

We now show that the integral of
\phi(x,y)
over
W_{n,1}
is negligible. Recall that

\displaystyle W_{n,1}

\displaystyle=\left\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\mid x=\frac{\delta}{n}+si,\,y=t+si,\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 72.26999pt\left.\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n},\,0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\right\}.

Lemma B.12
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

As before, the triangle inequality yields that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W_{n,1}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W_{n,1}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\max_{W_{n,1}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\int_{W_{n,1}}1\,|dxdy|.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{W_{n,1}}1\,|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}}1\,dsdt\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}},

it is enough to show that there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{W_{n,1}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\leq C_{0}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Let
(x,y)\in W_{n,1}
. It follows from the recursive formula (
68
) and the Stirling’s approximation (
23
) of the Gamma function that

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}||\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}||n(1-x-y)+1|e^{\textnormal{Re}(\alpha nx+\beta ny)}}{|\Gamma(nx+1)\Gamma(ny+1)\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+2)|}

\displaystyle\sim\rho_{n}(x,y)e^{\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))}

where

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}||\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}||n(1-x-y)+1|}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{3}|\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{n(1-x-y)+1}|)},

and

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log(x)-y\log(y)-(n-x-y+1)\log(n-x-y + 1)+n+1.

Since
\rho_{n}(x,y)
is clearly bounded above by a polynomial of
n
, it is then enough to show that there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle e^{\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))}\leq C_{1}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

(74)

From Lemma
B.7
, we get that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(\alpha nx+\beta ny)-\textnormal{Re}(nx)\log(\textnormal{Re}(nx) )-\textnormal{Re}(ny)\log(\textnormal{Re}(ny))

\displaystyle\quad-\textnormal{Re}(n(1-x-y)+1)\log( \textnormal{Re}(n(1-x-y) + 1))+n+1

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2}\left(|\textnormal{Im}(nx)|+|\textnormal{Im}(ny)|+|\textnormal{Im}(n(1-x-y)+1)|\right).

Since
x=\frac{\delta}{n}+si
,
y=t+si
with
\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
and
0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\delta+\beta nt-\delta\log\delta-nt\log(nt)

\displaystyle\quad-(n-\delta-nt+1)\log(n-\delta-nt +1)+n+1+2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi

\displaystyle=\alpha\delta+\beta nt-\delta\log\delta-(n-\delta+1)\log n-nt\log(t)

\displaystyle\quad-n(1-\frac{\delta}{n}-t+\frac{1}{n})\log(1-\frac{\delta}{n} -t +\frac{1}{n})+n+1+2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi.

For convenience, let us define

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(t)=\beta t-t\log(t)-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}-t+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log(1-\frac{\delta}{n} -t +\frac{1}{n}).

Then,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))\leq\alpha\delta-\delta\log\delta-(n-\delta+1)\log n+n+1+2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi+n\nu_{n}(t).

From elementary calculus, the maximum value of
\nu_{n}(t)
over the positive real line occurs at

\displaystyle t_{0}=\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\nu_{n}(t_{0})=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log(\frac{1-p_J}{1- p_J - p_{J'}})-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)\log\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle e^{\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{n}(nx,ny))}\leq\frac{e^{\alpha\delta+1}}{\delta^{\delta}}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\frac{e^{n}}{n^{n-\delta+1}}\left(\frac{1-p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta+1}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\right)^{n-\delta+1}

Clearly, (
74
) is satisfied and this completes the proof.
∎

Lemma B.13
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W_{n,3}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

From the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{W_{n,3}}\phi(x,y)\,dxdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{W_{n,3}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\,|dxdy|

Since

\displaystyle\int_{W_{n,3}}1\,|dxdy|

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{\delta}{n}}^{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}}1\,dsdt\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}},

it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{W_{n,3}}\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\leq C_{0}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

(75)

Let us further divide
W_{n,3}
into two parts

\displaystyle W_{n,3}^{-}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in W_{n,3}\mid\textnormal{Re}(x)+\textnormal{Re}(y)\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}\},

\displaystyle W_{n,3}^{+}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y)\in W_{n,3}\mid\textnormal{Re}(x)+\textnormal{Re}(y)\geq 1+\frac{1}{n}\}.

It is then enough to show that the maximum of
|\phi(x,y)|

75
).

Let
(x,y)\in W_{n,3}^{+}
and write

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\delta}{n}+si,

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=t+si

with
\frac{1+\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
and
0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}
.
It follows from Euler’s reflection formula (

) and the Stirling’s approximation (

) that

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|\sim\frac{e^{n}}{n^{n}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\textnormal{Re}\psi(x,y)}

for some
\rho(x,y)
bounded above by a polynomial of
n
and

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log x-y\log y+(x+y-1)\log(x + y-1).

Therefore, it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{W_{n,3}^{+}}e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}\leq C_{1}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{\theta}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n}.

(76)

From Lemma
B.7
, we see that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(\alpha x+\beta y)-\textnormal{Re}(x)\log(\textnormal{Re}(x))-\textnormal{Re}(y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(y))

\displaystyle\quad+\textnormal{Re}(x+y-1)\log(\textnormal{Re}(x+y -1))

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\pi}{2}\left(|\textnormal{Im}(x)|+|\textnormal{Im}(y)|+|\textnormal{Im}(x+y-1)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)+t\beta-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1- \frac{\delta}{n})-t\log t

\displaystyle\quad+\left(t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(t - \frac{\delta}{n})+\frac{2\pi\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

Define

\displaystyle\nu(t)=t\beta-t\log(t)+\left(t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(t - \frac{\delta}{n}).

One can easily verify that

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{\frac{\delta}{n}}{t\left(t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)}>0

for all
\frac{1+\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
.
Therefore, the maximum of
\nu(t)
must be attained at the boundary points. At
t=\frac{1+\delta}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\nu(t)=\frac{1+\delta}{n}\beta-\frac{1+\delta}{n}\log(\frac{1 + \delta}{n})+\frac{1}{n}\log(\frac{1}{n} ),

and thus

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{1+\delta}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}

\displaystyle\quad\left(\frac{1+\delta}{n}\right)^{-1-\delta}\frac{e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}}{n}.

Clearly, (
76
) is satisfied.
At
t=1-\frac{\delta}{n}

\displaystyle\nu(t)=\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\beta-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1 - \frac{\delta}{n})+\left(1-\frac{2\delta}{n}\right)\log(1 - \frac{2\delta}{n}),

and thus

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{-2n+2\delta}\left(1-\frac{2\delta}{n}\right)^{n-2\delta}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}.

Since Lemma
B.3
implies that
p_{J}+p_{J^{\prime}}<\frac{1}{2}
, we get that

\displaystyle\frac{p_{J}p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}<1.

Therefore, (
76
) is satisfied.

Let
(x,y)\in W_{n,3}^{-}
and write

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\delta}{n}+si,

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=t+si

with
\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq\frac{1+\delta}{n}
and
0\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}
.
It follows from Stirling’s approximation (
23
) that

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}||\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}|}{|\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+1)|}\frac{e^{\textnormal{Re}(\alpha x+\beta y)}}{|\Gamma(nx+1)||\Gamma(ny+1)|}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{e^{-n\log n\textnormal{Re}(x+y)}}{|\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+1)|}\rho_{d}(x,y)e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{d}(x,y))}

for some
\rho_{d}(x,y)
bounded above by a polynomial of
n
and

\displaystyle\psi_{d}(x,y)=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log x-y\log y+x+y.

Since
\textnormal{Re}(x+y)\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle e^{-n\log n\textnormal{Re}(x+y)}\leq\frac{1}{n^{n}}.

Using the recursive formula (
68
) and the Stirling’s approximation (
23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+1)|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|n(1-x-y)+1||n(1-x-y)+2|}{|\Gamma(n(1-x-y)+2+1)|}

\displaystyle\sim\rho_{g}(x,y)e^{\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{g}(x,y))}

for some
\rho_{g}(x,y)
bounded above by a polynomial of
n
and

\displaystyle\psi_{g}(x,y)=n(1-x-y)+2-\left(n(1-x-y)+2\right)\log(n(1-x-y) + 2).

Since

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{g}(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(n(1-x-y)+2)-\textnormal{Re}(n(1-x-y)+2)\log(\textnormal{Re}(n(1-x-y) + 2))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2}|\textnormal{Im}(n(1-x-y)+2)|

\displaystyle\leq(\delta-nt)+2-((\delta-nt)+2)\log((\delta- nt) + 2)+\pi\sqrt{n}\xi

\displaystyle\leq 1+\pi\sqrt{n}\xi,

we get that

\displaystyle e^{\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{g}(x,y))}\leq e^{1+\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}.

Therefore, it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{2}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{W_{n,3}^{-}}e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{d}(x,y))}\leq C_{2}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n}.

(77)

From Lemma
B.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{d}(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(\alpha x+\beta y)-\textnormal{Re}(x)\log( \textnormal{Re}(x) )-\textnormal{Re}(y)\log( \textnormal{Re}(y))

\displaystyle\quad+\textnormal{Re}(x+y)+\frac{\pi}{2}\left(|\textnormal{Im}(x)|+|\textnormal{Im}(y)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)+\beta t-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)-t\log(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)+t+\pi\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

Now let us define

\displaystyle\eta(t)=t\beta-t\log(t)+t.

Since the domain of
t
, given by
\left[\frac{\delta}{n},\frac{1+\delta}{n}\right]
shrinks as
n
grows, the maximum of
\eta(t)
over this interval must be attained at its end points. At
t=\frac{\delta}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\frac{\delta}{n}\beta-\frac{\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n})+\frac{\delta}{n},

and thus

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{d}(x,y))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{-\delta}e^{n-\delta}e^{\delta}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}.

Clearly, (
77
) is met.
At
t=\frac{1+\delta}{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\frac{1+\delta}{n}\beta-\frac{1+\delta}{n}\log(\frac{1 + \delta}{n})+\frac{1+\delta}{n},

and thus

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi_{d}(x,y))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}\left(\frac{p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\quad\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}\left(\frac{1+\delta}{n}\right)^{-1-\delta}e^{n-\delta}e^{1+\delta}e^{\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}.

Clearly, (
77
) is met. This completes the proof of Lemma
B.13
.
∎

We now turn to examine the integral of
h_{n}(x,y)
over
\hat{\gamma}_{n,i}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,j}
for a few representative cases in
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
.

Lemma B.14
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

From the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|h_{n}(x,y)|\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\phi(x,y)||\cot(n\pi x)||\cot(n\pi y)|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dxdy|.

From Lemma
B.5
, we see that for all
x\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}
,

\displaystyle|\cot(n\pi x)|\sim 1.

From Lemma
B.6
, we see that for all
y\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
,

\displaystyle|\cot(n\pi y)|\leq 1+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{1-\cos(2\pi\delta)^{2}}}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dxdy|\leq 2\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}},

it is therefore enough to prove that
there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\phi(x,y)|\leq C_{0}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Let us divide
\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
into two parts

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\right)^{-}=\left\{(x,y)\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\mid\textnormal{Re}(x)+\textnormal{Re}(y)\leq 1-\frac{1}{2n}\right\},

\displaystyle\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\right)^{+}=\left\{(x,y)\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\mid\textnormal{Re}(x)+\textnormal{Re}(y)>1-\frac{1}{2n}\right\}.

First, let
(x,y)\in\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\right)^{-}
. Then, we write

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=t+\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}i,

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{n}+si

with
\frac{\delta}{n}\leq t\leq 1-\frac{1}{2n}-\frac{\delta}{n}
and
-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\leq s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}
.
Using Stirling’s approximation (
23
), we get that

\displaystyle|\phi(x,y)|=\frac{e^{n}}{n^{n}}\rho(x,y)e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}

for some
\rho(x,y)
bounded above by a polynomial of
n
and

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log x-y\log y-(1-x-y)\log(1-x-y).

Hence,
it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\right)^{-}}e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}\leq C_{1}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{\theta}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n}.

(78)

From Lemma
B.7
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(\alpha x+\beta y)-\textnormal{Re}(x)\log(\textnormal{Re}(x))-\textnormal{Re}(y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(y))

\displaystyle\quad-\textnormal{Re}(1-x-y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(1-x-y))+\frac{\pi}{2}\left(|\textnormal{Im}(x)|+|\textnormal{Im}(y)|+|\textnormal{Im}(1-x-y)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha t+\beta\frac{\delta}{n}-t\log(t)-\frac{\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n} )-\left(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n} )+\frac{2\pi\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\nu(t)=\alpha t-t\log(t)-\left(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n} ).

From elementary calculus, we see that
\nu(t)
has a unique critical point at

\displaystyle t_{0}=\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J^{\prime}}}\left(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)

by solving

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}(t)=\alpha+\log(1-t-\frac{\delta}{n} )-\log(t)=0.

Since

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1-\frac{\delta}{n}}{t\left(t-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\right)}<0

for all
t\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{n}
, we know that the critical point is in fact a global maximum. Therefore,

\displaystyle\nu(t)\leq-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log( 1-\frac{\delta}{n} )-\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)\log\left(\frac{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right),

and hence,

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}\leq\left(\frac{p_{J}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{\delta}\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{-\delta}\left(1-\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{\delta-n}\left(\frac{1-p_{J^{\prime}}}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n-\delta}e^{2\pi\xi\sqrt{n}}.

Clearly, (
78
) is met.

The maximum value of the function
|\phi(x,y)|
over
\left(\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\right)^{+}
can be analyzed using similar techniques as in the case of
W_{n,3}^{-}
of Lemma
B.13
.
∎

Lemma B.15
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,4}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

The proof proceeds similarly to that of Lemma
B.14
. From the triangle inequality and the approximations of the
\cot
function, it suffices to prove that there exists positive
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,1}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,4}}|\phi(x,y)|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

The rest of the proof is entirely analogous to Lemma
B.13
, and we omit the details to avoid repetition.
∎

Lemma B.16
.

There exists positive constants
C
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\right|\leq Ce^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Proof.

Again, using the triangle inequality, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}h_{n}(x,y)\,dxdy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|h_{n}(x,y)|\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\phi(x,y)||\cot(n\pi x)||\cot(n\pi y)|\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dxdy|.

From Lemma
B.6
, we obtain for all
(x,y)\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}

\displaystyle|\cot(n\pi x)|\leq 1+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{1-\cos(2\pi\delta)^{2}}},

\displaystyle|\cot(n\pi y)|\leq 1+\tfrac{2}{\sqrt{1-\cos(2\pi\delta)^{2}}}.

Since

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}1\,|dxdy|\leq 2,

it suffices to prove that there exists positive constants
C_{0}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle\max_{\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}}|\phi(x,y)|\leq C_{0}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{e\theta}{n(1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}})}\right)^{n}.

Let
(x,y)\in\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}\times\hat{\gamma}_{n,2}
. We write

\displaystyle x=\frac{\delta}{n}-si,

\displaystyle y=\frac{\delta}{n}-ti.

with
-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\leq s,t\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

Then, from the Stirling’s approximation (
23
) of the Gamma function, we get that

\displaystyle\left|\phi(x,y)\right|\sim e^{n-n\log(n)}\rho_{n}(x,y)e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))},

where

\displaystyle\rho_{n}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{p_{J}}||\sqrt{y}-\sqrt{p_{J^{\prime}}}|}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{3}|\sqrt{xy}\sqrt{n(1-x-y)}|)},

and

\displaystyle\psi(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha x+\beta y-x\log(x)-y\log(y)-(1-x-y)\log(1-x-y).

It is straightforward that
\rho_{n}(x,y)
is bounded above by some polynomial of
n
. Hence, it suffices to prove that there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
\theta<1
such that

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}\leq C_{1}e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}\left(\frac{\theta}{1-p_{J}-p_{J^{\prime}}}\right)^{n}.

(79)

From Lemma
B.7
, we get that

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\textnormal{Re}(\alpha x+\beta y)-\textnormal{Re}(x)\log(\textnormal{Re}(x) )-\textnormal{Re}(y)\log(\textnormal{Re}(y))

\displaystyle\quad-\textnormal{Re}(1-x-y)\log( \textnormal{Re}(1-x-y))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\pi}{2}\left(|\textnormal{Im}(x)|+|\textnormal{Im}(y)|+|\textnormal{Im}(1-x-y)|\right).

Since
x=\frac{\delta}{n}-ti
,
y=\frac{\delta}{n}-si
with
-\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}\leq t,s\leq\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}
,

\displaystyle\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta}{n}(\alpha+\beta)-\frac{2\delta}{n}\log(\frac{\delta}{n})-\left(1-\frac{2\delta}{n}\right)\log(1- \frac{2\delta}{n})+2\pi\frac{\xi}{\sqrt{n}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle e^{n\textnormal{Re}(\psi(x,y))}\leq\frac{e^{\delta(\alpha+\beta)+2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}}{\left(\frac{\delta}{n}\right)^{2\delta}\left(1-\tfrac{2\delta}{n}\right)^{n-2\delta}}=\frac{n^{2\delta}\left(1-\tfrac{2\delta}{n}\right)^{2\delta}e^{\delta(\alpha+\beta)}}{\delta^{2\delta}}\frac{e^{2\pi\sqrt{n}\xi}}{\left(1-\tfrac{2\delta}{n}\right)^{n}}.

Clearly, (
79
) is satisfied and this completes the proof.
∎

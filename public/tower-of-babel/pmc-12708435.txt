NAP1L4 in hepatocellular carcinoma progression and treatment from gene expression to clinical impact
Authors: Junjie Lin, Hehe Yin, Zihan Xie, Meihui Liu, Lulu Kong, Ruixuan Yao, Guang Chen, Yuyao Li, Jiaojiao Shen, Qingling Wang, Kexing Han, Honghai Xu, Yufeng Gao

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) remains one of the deadliest malignancies globally, posing substantial health threats and economic burdens. Despite advances in therapeutic modalities—including surgical resection, liver transplantation, and targeted therapies—HCC prognosis remains unsatisfactory. This highlights an urgent need for novel biomarkers to improve prognostic stratification. Senescence-related genes (SRGs) have emerged as promising candidates for HCC prognostic exploration, making their association with clinical outcomes the focus of this study.​.MethodsComprehensive machine learning was applied to screen SRGs associated with HCC survival, identifying key prognostic genes. A clinical nomogram based on SRG scores was constructed and validated. NAP1L4 expression in HCC was verified using GEPIA2.0, UALCAN, HPA databases, and clinical samples from 40 patients at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. In vitro, NAP1L4 was knocked down in HCCLM3 cells to assess proliferation and migration changes. RNA-seq analyzed differentially expressed genes post-knockdown, followed by GSEA/KEGG enrichment to identify relevant pathways. Drug sensitivity and Mendelian randomization analyses explored therapeutic implications and gut microbiota-related HCC risk modulation.​.ResultsMachine learning identified NAP1L4 as a pivotal SRG, with the SRG score-based nomogram strongly associated with poor overall survival. High NAP1L4 expression in HCC was confirmed by multi-omics and clinical samples. NAP1L4 knockdown in HCCLM3 cells significantly inhibited proliferation and migration. RNA-seq revealed 129 upregulated and 101 downregulated genes post-knockdown, with GSEA/KEGG highlighting the NOD-like receptor pathway as critical for NAP1L4-mediated HCC effects. Elevated NAP1L4 correlated with adverse prognosis and tumor microenvironment alterations (increased M0 macrophages and regulatory T cells). Reduced NAP1L4 expression enhanced sensitivity to cisplatin and axitinib. Mendelian randomization showed ANGPT1 modulates HCC risk by increasing gut Faecalitalea cylindroides abundance.​.ConclusionSRGs, particularly NAP1L4, play critical roles in HCC progression. High NAP1L4 expression associates with poor prognosis, tumor microenvironment remodeling, and altered drug sensitivity. These findings support SRGs as potential prognostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets to enhance HCC treatment efficacy.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04016-7.Keywords: Senescence, Hepatocellular carcinoma, Machine learning, NAP1L4, Gut microbiota

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents a major worldwide contributor to cancer-associated deaths, resulting in major health and economic impacts on patients and healthcare systems [1]. The World Health Organization reports a concerning increase in HCC incidence and mortality, especially in developing countries with limited access to effective treatments [2, 3]. Existing therapeutic strategies for HCC, encompassing surgical resection, liver transplantation, localized ablation and targeted therapies, present considerable limitations, especially concerning early detection and the management of advanced-stage disease, which is often characterized by elevated rates of postoperative recurrence and subpar treatment outcomes [4–6]. Although a variety of combination therapies have been introduced to enhance patient survival, the outlook for individuals suffering from HCC remains grim [7, 8], accentuating the urgent requirement for innovative biomarkers that can improve prognostic precision and guide treatment strategies.

Cellular senescence, defined by a stable and persistent loss of proliferative capacity, is increasingly acknowledged as a fundamental mechanism contributing to tumorigenesis [9]. SASPs, linked to senescence, contribute to an inflammatory tumor microenvironment and promote tumor progression by supporting cellular proliferation, migration, and resistance to apoptosis [10, 11]. Particularly, the dysregulation of senescence-related genes (SRGs) has emerged as a pivotal factor in HCC advancement, influencing the tumor microenvironment and altering therapeutic responses [12–14]. Alterations in the expression of cell senescence-related genes (SRGs) can significantly influence the gut microbiota’s composition and function, potentially impacting liver cancer risk indirectly [15, 16]. Although there have been strides in recognizing senescence markers across various cancers, A more comprehensive understanding of specific SRGs in HCC is needed, as Dominik Saul defines a new gene set that identifies senescent cells. However, senescence also operates as a cell-intrinsic tumor-suppressive mechanism by halting the replication of damaged hepatocytes and recruiting immune-mediated clearance [17, 18]. The ultimate effect on hepatocarcinogenesis, whether restrictive or accelerative, hinges on the contextual equilibrium between these antagonistic forces, a perspective that has received limited attention in prior HCC SRG investigations.

While several studies have linked individual SRGs to HCC prognosis, they typically relied on a single machine-learning algorithm and transcriptomic data alone. This study integrates the most up-to-date machine learning approaches to identify and validate senescence-related genes (SRGs) that are closely associated with hepatocellular carcinoma prognosis. Another primary aim of our research is to develop a clinically relevant risk assessment model predicated on the identified SRGs with a specific emphasis on the significant recognition of NAP1L4 in HCC. We also conducted an extensive analysis of the expression discrepancies of NAP1L4 in HCC and its clinical relevance concerning the tumor microenvironment. Moreover, we explored how gut microbiota mediates the association between SRGs and HCC through Mendelian randomization analysis, leading us to identify ANGPT1 as a determinant that influences the abundance of Faecalitalea cylindroides in fecal matter, which subsequently elevates the risk of liver cancer. This approach clarifies senescence’s role in HCC and suggests potential therapeutic strategies targeting these pathways. The technical roadmap of this study is shown in Fig. 1.

Methods

Data acquisition

The mRNA expression, clinical characteristics, and somatic mutations for HCC patients of training cohort were sourced from The TCGA database, (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/). The mRNA expression, clinical information and somatic mutations for HCC of validation cohort were acquired through the ICGC database, (https://dcc.icgc.org/). Instances with missing information were omitted. Ultimately, cases with a survival time of 30 days or less were omitted from the prognostic analysis. 6540 single-cell liver cancer data are from GEO dataset GSE210679. 7525 single-cell normal liver tissue data are from GEO dataset GSE246662. This study sourced summary eQTL data from eQTLGen consortium (www.eQTLgen.org), comprising 25,482 samples across 37 datasets. Comprehensive gut-microbiota summary statistics (GCST90032172–GCST90032644) were derived from 5,959 European individuals; the dataset comprised 473 distinct taxa, including 11 phyla, 18 classes, 24 orders, 62 families, 145 genera and 213 species. The GWAS statistics pertaining to hepatocellular carcinoma psoriasis included 609 cases alongside 345,118 controls from the FinnGen consortium.

Machine Learning-Based selection of SRGs for HCC prognosis

To identify SRGs linked to overall survival in HCC, we utilized a comprehensive machine learning strategy, incorporating 101 model-building techniques across ten methodologies. The analysis leveraged various R packages tailored for survival analysis with unique approaches [19]. The glmnet package employed the LASSO method, incorporating least squares regression with a penalty term to shrink the coefficients of less important variables to zero, using an alpha value of one.The Elastic Net method, available in glmnet, Adjusts the alpha coefficient between 0 and 1 to balance LASSO and Ridge penalties. The Stepcox method, which relies on the survival package, employs a step function with Cox regression to pick models that reduce the AIC. The survivalsvm package is used to implement the Survival SVM, conducted survival analysis using support vector machines, thereby facilitating data segmentation within a higher-dimensional space. CoxBoost combines Cox regression with boosting techniques to improve prediction accuracy while maintaining interpretability. The SuperPC method integrates principal component analysis with supervised learning, forming a supervised principal component analysis framework. Ridge regression was used for selecting variables in glmnet. The PlsRcox function from the plsRcox package used partial least squares regression to construct Cox models for high-dimensional data. To predict survival outcomes, the Random Survival Forest (RSF) from the randomForestSRC package applied random forest models. Finally, using the gbm package, GBM sequentially creates weak learners to tackle the negative gradient of the loss function from the prior model. The integration of various machine learning methodologies significantly enhances the accuracy in identifying and validating crucial genes that are vital for prognostic evaluations across a spectrum of diseases. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to validate the prognostic significance of Senescence-Related Genes Signatures and identify relevant clinical variables. Following this, a nomogram was created utilizing the “rms” package in R, which integrated risk scores along with clinically significant variables that have prognostic relevance. The predictive efficacy of this nomogram was confirmed through discrimination and calibration assessments.The calibration curve for the nomogram was constructed to assess the alignment between predicted probabilities and actual observed rates [20].

Identification of DEGs

Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were determined utilizing the “limma” package, which is specifically designed for Bayesian data analysis. The criteria for significance were established with a FDR of less than 0.05 and a LogFC exceeding 1. The “pheatmap” package was employed to create volcano plots and heatmaps for visualizing the DEGs.

Identification of a core gene

For the identification of core genes, commonly employed machine learning techniques such as SVM, LASSO, DT, GBM, GLM, KNN, NNET, and RF, known for their robust classification performance, were utilized. These methods are frequently applied in biological research for the identification of characteristic genes. In the current study, a selection of these eight machine learning techniques was applied to filter characteristic genes, designating genes identified by more than four of these methods as critical genes [21].

Multilevel expression verification of NAP1L4

We conducted a multilevel analysis of NAP1L4 expression, focusing on its differential mRNA expression in HCC tissues using the GEPIA2 database [22]. We analyzed protein-level expression changes of NAP1L4 using the CPTAC data available via the UCLCAN database [23]. HPA database provided Immunohistochemical images of NAP1L4 in normal and HCC tissues, along with relevant tissue sample information [24].

RNA extraction and real-time quantitative PCR

Total RNA was isolated from the cells utilizing Trizol reagent (Invitrogen) and subsequently reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) with the aid of PrimeScript™ RT Master Mix (Takara Bio). The quantification of fluorescence was performed using TB-Green quantitative PCR (Takara Bio), with normalization against GAPDH expression levels. Supplementary Table 1 contains details of all designed primers.

Cell culture and transient transfection

All cell lines were obtained from the Laboratory of the Department of Infectious Diseases, First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. Total cell lines, encompassing the standard hepatic cell line MIHA as well as hepatocellular carcinoma cell lines, namely 97 H, Hep3B, HepG2, HUH7, HCCLM3 and SK, were maintained in DMEM augmented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin-streptomycin. Transient transfections were performed utilizing jetPRIME Transfection Reagent in accordance with the manufacturer’s instructions. HIPPOBIO Co, Ltd. designed the siRNA sequences. Refer to Supplementary Table 2 for the SiCTSA sequence.

Western blot analysis

Cells are lysed with RIPA buffer containing inhibitors, followed by sonication and heating at 95℃ for 5 min. Protein concentration is measured using a BCA kit and adjusted to an equal concentration. Proteins were separated by SDS-PAGE and transferred to PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk in TBST, incubated with primary antibodies(1:200; Proteintech, Wuhan)against target proteins and GAPDH, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies. Bands were detected using ECL. The full-length membrane images are provided in Supplementary Data 2.

CCK-8 experiment

Cells were inoculated at a density of 2 × 10^3 per well in a plate containing 96 wells with five replicates, maintaining cultures for 0, 24, 48, 72 and 96 h. After incubation, 10 µL of CCK8 reagent was added, followed by 2 h of incubation at 37 °C, and absorbance at 450 nm was recorded utilizing a TECAN reader.

Colony formation assay

A total of 200 cancer cells were plated per well in six-well culture plates, with culture medium and incubated at 37 °C. After 3 days, the medium was replaced with indicated CM, and cells were cultured for 14 days with medium changes every 5 days. At the end, cells were preserved using 4% paraformaldehyde and subsequently stained with a 0.1% crystal violet solution for a duration of 20 min, followed by washing with PBS, and images of colonies were captured and counted.

Wound healing assay

Cancer cells were seeded at 5 × 10^5 cells per well in 6-well plates. Upon reaching 90% confluence, a 200-µl pipette tip was used to create a central scratch wound.Following PBS washing, cells were incubated with specified CM without FBS for 12 h at 37 °C.Wound areas were monitored and imaged using a Leica microscope (Germany) at 0 and 12 h post-scratch.Cell migration rates were determined using ImageJ v1.46r (National Institutes of Health) with the formula: Relative migration rate = [(initial width at 0 h - width at 12 h) / initial width at 0 h] × 100%.

RNA sequencing

the NAP1L4 gene in the LM3 hepatocellular carcinoma cell line was knocked down using siRNA technology. Subsequently, RNA sequencing was carried out by Cosmos Wisdom Biotech Co., Ltd. (Hangzhou, China) to identify differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and potential downstream signaling pathways.

GO, GSEA and KEGG analyses

The analyses of Gene Ontology (GO), Gene Set Enrichment Analysis(GSEA) and the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) were performed utilizing the “clusterProfiler” package [25].

Single-cell RNA-seq data processing and quality control

Single-cell RNA-seq data processed with Seurat package. Low-quality cells were excluded according to the following thresholds: nFeature_RNA > 300; nCount_RNA > 1,000; percent.mt < 20%. Doublet detection was performed with DoubletFinder package. To integrate data across multiple samples, we applied Harmony package to correct for batch-specific technical variation. Cell type annotation was carried out with SingleR package using the Human Primary Cell Atlas (HPCA) reference.

Protein–protein docking

Rigid-body docking was performed with GRAMM, treating both proteins as rigid entities. Sequences retrieved from UniProtKB were modeled with SWISS-MODEL; top-ranked PDB structures were stripped and directly used for surface-to-surface docking.

Immunohistochemistry

Paraffin-embedded tissue sections were constructed from paired tumor and adjacent non-tumor liver tissues obtained from 40 HCC patients resected at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. Four-micron sections were deparaffinized, antigen-retrieved, and incubated overnight at 4 °C with rabbit anti-NAP1L4 antibody (1:200; Proteintech, Wuhan), followed by HRP-conjugated secondary antibody and DAB chromogen according to the manufacturer’s protocol.

Analysis related to immunity

Regarding the analysis of immunological properties, enrichment scores for 22 immune-related cell types were computed using the CIBERSORT algorithm, employing the packages “CIBERSORT” [26]. Additionally, thirteen immune-related terms in HCC samples were assessed with the ssGSEA algorithm from the “GSVA” and “GSEABase” packages. A comprehensive summary of common immune checkpoint molecule genes was compiled, and the correlation of expression between NAP1L4 and immune checkpoint molecules was analyzed. The IPS for patients with HCC was derived from the TCIA database. To further explore the immune microenvironment, the TIDE framework was utilized, incorporating an algorithm from i.harvard.edu to forecast and assess the response to immunotherapy in individuals diagnosed with HCC.

Sensitivity analysis of common drugs

We conducted a sensitivity analysis on commonly used pharmaceuticals employing the R package “oncoPredict” to evaluate drug sensitivities [27].

MR analysis

The objective of this research was to explore the causal connections among senescence-associated genes, the gut microbiome, and HCC. using a two-sample, bidirectional, two-step MR approach. the genetic variations employed as IVs, referred to as SNPs, must satisfy the following criteria: (i) robustly forecast the exposures; (ii) solely link to the outcome through the exposures; and (iii) exhibit no association with any confounding factors affecting the exposureoutcome relationship. The STROBE-MR guidelines aim to enhance the clarity and consistency of reporting in studies that apply Mendelian Randomization within observational research. The Mendelian Randomization investigation followed the STROBE-MR guidelines to ensure its validity (Supplementary Data1).

To meet stringent criteria and address horizontal pleiotropy based on the three main MR assumptions, Only SNPs that were independently significant across the genome-wide association studies were utilized as IVs for the exposure analysis. Initially, genetic variants were identified using association thresholds of p < 1 × 10^-5, consistent with prior MR research.We selected SNPs with genome-wide significance (p < 5 × 10^-8). In the absence of significant genome-wide SNPs as IVs, SNPs with a significance level below genome-wide (p < 5 × 10^-6) were considered as candidate IVs. We set r2 to 0.001 and kb to 10,000 to maintain the independence of selected IVs and minimize random allele distribution violations due to linkage disequilibrium. SNPs with an effect allele frequency greater than 0.01 were included, while those with F less than 10 were excluded.a measure of the strength of these IVs) to prevent weak instrumental bias.

We conducted MR analysis utilizing the “TwoSampleMR” package [28]. Initial bidirectional MR analyses were executed to examine the cause-and-effect relationship between senescence-related genes and HCC, referred to as the total effect.Effect estimation utilized the traditional MR approach of IVW analysis, presenting results as beta values with standard errors for continuous outcomes and OR with a 95% CI. The IVW method combined SNP-specific Wald estimates, calculated as the outcome estimate divided by the exposure estimate, using random effects to determine the overall causal effect estimate. A two-step MR mediation analysis was performed to assess whether gut microbiota mediate the causal effect of aging-related genes on HCC outcomes. The mediating effect was assessed by calculating the product of coefficients β1 and β2, where β2 signifies the causal influence of the mediator (gut microbiota) on HCC, and β1 represents the causal effect of the exposure (ANGPT1 on HCC in the primary MR analysis) on the mediator. The mediation effect percentage is calculated by dividing the indirect effect by the total effect.95% confidence intervals were calculated using the delta method as outlined earlier.Several additional sensitivity analyses were performed to identify horizontal pleiotropy and heterogeneity, which may enhance the reliability of the findings. Sensitivity analysis employed tests like Cochran’s Q, heterogeneity assessments, the leave-one-out method, RadialMR, and MR PRESSO, emphasizing the importance of identifying heterogeneity to determine the suitability of integrating individual results into a unified summary metric [29].

Statistical methods

The independent t-test assessed normally distributed variables, while the Kruskal-Wallis test detected differences among multiple groups. The Mann-Whitney U test compared two independent groups with non-normal distributions. The Kaplan-Meier method and log-rank test evaluated overall survival across subgroups. Analyses used R (Version 4.4.1) with a significance threshold of p < 0.05.

Result

Selection of HCC survival-related senescence genes using machine learning

We focused on the identification of HCC survival-related senescence genes through machine learning techniques.Recently, Dominik Saul et al.developed a 125-gene panel named the senescence gene set (“SenMayo”). Prior to machine-learning feature selection, genes with low expression in either normal liver or hepatocellular carcinoma tissue were removed by applying a TPM-based filter, yielding 77 high-confidence candidates for subsequent modeling. Overlap analysis with the recently published HCC senescence gene set (Liu S et al., J Adv Res 2024; n = 368) revealed: Shared genes: 26, Overlap percentage: 26/125 = 20.8%, Statistical significance was assessed by hyper-geometric testing against a background of 20,000 protein-coding genes: P = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵, indicating significant enrichment over random expectation. These data demonstrate that the liver-filtered SenMayo signature substantially and significantly overlaps with the HCC senescence signature, confirming its relevance in hepatocellular carcinoma research.

To further isolate senescence-related genes connected with overall survival in HCC, a total of 101 screening and modeling strategies were implemented.a combination of ten distinct machine learning methodologies was employed.The findings indicated that both Random Survival Forest (RSF) and the Stepwise Cox Regression (forward selection) combined with RSF exhibited the most superior predictive capability in the development of the model.The notable difference in c-index values between the training and validation cohorts led to the pursuit of a more reliable predictive model.A combination of Lasso for variable selection and Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM) for model construction was chosen for its balanced c-index values and high predictive performance. Training cohort (mean ± 95% CI): C-index = 0.780 (0.771–0.795), Validation cohort (mean ± 95% CI): C-index = 0.630 (0.624–0.635), Overall (mean ± 95% CI): C-index = 0.705 (0.691–0.717) (Fig. 2A).This selection process ultimately identified nine genes for model construction: ANGPT1, CSF1, HGF, ICAM1, KITLG, NAP1L4, SERPINE2, SPP1, and VEGFA.Utilizing this model, a senescence gene risk score (SRG score) was computed for all samples within the training dataset.Survival analysis revealed that an elevated SRG score was significantly correlated with diminished overall survival, a finding that was corroborated by the validation set (Fig. 2B). A meta-analysis utilizing univariate Cox regression analysis suggests that the SRG score serves as a prognostic indicator for HCC (Fig. 2C). The SRG score’s exceptional precision in predicting patient survival at 1-year, 1.5-year and 2-year intervals was validated through ROC curve analysis in both training and validation datasets (Figs. 2D).

Development and assessment of a clinical nomogram utilizing SRG score

The construction and assessment of a clinical nomogram based on the SRG score were discussed.A more applicable and consistent nomogram was developed by incorporating prevalent clinicopathological parameters, and stage. Univariate Cox regression analysis revealed that both tumor stage and SRG score significantly impact the prognosis of HCC patients (Fig. 3A). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further validated that both the stage of the tumor and the SRG score functioned as independent prognostic risk factors that influence the outcomes of patients with HCC. when adjusted for other clinicopathological parameters (Fig. 3B). Recognizing the strong correlation between SRG score and HCC prognosis, a novel nomogram integrating common clinicopathological parameters and the SRG score was constructed.The nomogram established on this basis further enhanced the precision of prognostic predictions for HCC patients (Fig. 3C).ROC curve analysis assessed the AUC for different prognostic indicators, demonstrating that the SRG score’s predictive ability for HCC patient prognosis significantly exceeded that of other clinicopathological features (Figs. 3D). The nomogram’s robust accuracy and reliability in predicting 1-year, 2-year, and 3-year patient survival rates were further validated using ROC and calibration curves (Figs. 3E, F).

Identification of key SRG

We performed a detailed analysis of the 47 previously identified common genes to pinpoint key signature-related genes (SRGs). Initially, we utilized eight machine learning techniques (Decision Tree, Gradient Boosting Machine, Generalized Linear Model, K-Nearest Neighbors, LASSO, Neural Network, Random Forest, and Support Vector Machine) to pinpoint marker molecules associated with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).The genes CXCL12, IGFBP3, NAP1L4, PGF, SEMA3F, and SPP1, which were identified by more than four of the eight machine learning methods, were classified as pivotal genes (Figs. 4E–H). Furthermore, we analyzed the expression disparities and the diagnostic and prognostic significance of these six SRGs.The differential expression analysis revealed that NAP1L4, PGF, SEMA3F, and SPP1 exhibited elevated expression levels in HCC, while CXCL12 and IGFBP3 were found to have reduced expression levels in the same context (Figs. 5A). ROC curve analysis revealed AUC values for NAP1L4, CXCL12, PGF, SEMA3F, SPP1 and IGFBP3 as 0.926, 0.932, 0.827, 0.993, 0.734, and 0.884 in the TCGA cohort, and 0.866, 0.940, 0.700, 0.905, 0.738, and 0.926 in the ICGC cohort, respectively (Figs. 5B, C). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that elevated NAP1L4 expression correlated with poor prognosis in both TCGA and ICGC cohorts (Figs. 5D, E), identifying NAP1L4 as a key SRG for further HCC research.

Multilevel expression verification and functional validation

In order to provide additional confirmation regarding the expression levels of NAP1L4 in HCC, we first utilized the GEPIA2.0 database, which confirmed a significant elevation in NAP1L4 expression in HCC (Fig. 6A).We analyzed NAP1L4 protein expression using the UALCAN database, which revealed significantly elevated levels in HCC tissues compared to normal tissues (Fig. 6B). The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) database and clinical samples revealed significant overexpression of NAP1L4 in HCC tissues compared to normal liver tissues (Fig. 6C). A series of experiments on NAP1L4 in HCC revealed significantly elevated expression of NAP1L4 in liver cancer cell lines (97 H, Hep3B, HepG2, HUH7, HCCLM3, and SK) compared to normal liver cell lines (MIHA) (Fig. 6D). We investigated the oncogenic role of NAP1L4 in HCC by analyzing the effects of its knockdown in HCCLM3 cells. RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated that siNAP1L4-1 and siNAP1L4-2 significantly suppressed NAP1L4 expression in HCCLM3 cells (siNAP1L4-1: 75.4% reduction, siNAP1L4-2: 80.3% reduction) (Supplementary Fig. 1),Western blot analysis demonstrated that the protein expression of NAP1L4 was significantly reduced following knockdown (Fig. 6E). Subsequent CCK8 assays, colony formation assays, and scratch assays showed that the reduction of NAP1L4 impaired the proliferation and migration abilities of HCCLM3 cell line (Fig. 6F- J).

NAP1L4 drive HCC progression via the NLRC5-NOD axis

To explore the potential mechanisms of NAP1L4 in hepatocellular carcinoma, RNA-seq was performed on LM3 cells with/without NAP1L4 knockdown to detect DEGs. The differential analysis results, presented in bar charts, volcano plots, and heatmaps, identified 129 up - regulated and 101 down - regulated genes (Fig. 7A-C). Additionally, GSEA and KEGG enrichment analyses both highlighted the NOD - like receptor signaling pathway (Fig. 7D-E), suggesting a crucial role in the biological processes of of NAP1L4 affecting hepatocellular carcinoma. Further analyses disclosed that NAP1L4 exhibits the strongest transcriptional correlation with NLRC5, a core component of the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway (Fig. 7F). Molecular docking revealed a binding free energy of -21.2 kcal/mol between NAP1L4 and NLRC5, indicative of a robust interaction (Fig. 7G).

Leveraging single-cell RNA-seq data from GEO comprising HCC and normal tissues, we first annotated major cell clusters (Fig. 8A). Violin plots (red, HCC; blue, normal) demonstrated that both NAP1L4 and NLRC5 were markedly up-regulated in tumor samples, with NAP1L4 showing pronounced differences in hepatocytes and immune cells, whereas NLRC5 displayed a preferential elevation in endothelial cells (Fig. 8B-C). Co-expression analysis further revealed a higher abundance of NAP1L4/NLRC5 double-positive cells (yellow) within the HCC microenvironment (Fig. 8D). Relationship between NAP1L4 and clinical parameters

Clinical data for hepatocellular carcinoma were obtained from TCGA database for subsequent analyses. As depicted in (Fig. 9A), the heatmap illustrating NAP1L4 alongside various clinicopathological parameters indicated significant differences in tumor stages among HCC patients categorized by NAP1L4 expression levels. To further validate the clinical significance of NAP1L4, we collected formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) specimens from 40 hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients treated at the First Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University. Kaplan–Meier analysis revealed that the high-expression cohort exhibited a significantly greater mortality risk than the low-expression cohort(Fig. 10D).

Relationship between NAP1L4 and the tumor microenvironment

We conducted an in-depth analysis of the interaction between NAP1L4 and the tumor microenvironment in HCC patients by assessing 22 immune cell types and 13 immune-related terms in tissue samples using CIBERSORT and ssGSEA methods. Results highlight the correlation between immune cells, revealing that high NAP1L4 expression is associated with increased levels of M0 macrophages, follicular helper T cells, resting dendritic cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), neutrophils, and eosinophils. Conversely, elevated NAP1L4 levels correspond to decreased counts of resting Mast cells, gamma delta T cells, memory resting CD4 T cells, and resting NK cells, as shown in (Fig. 11A, B). Immune-related metrics showed a significant increase in MHC_class_I levels in contrast to the low SRG score group, whereas Cytolytic_activity, Type_I_IFN_Response, and Type_II_IFN_Response were notably reduced (Fig. 11C). Given the critical role of immune checkpoint molecules in guiding immunotherapy, we investigated the relationship between NAP1L4 and 48 prevalent immune checkpoint molecules.The analysis identified a significant positive correlation between NAP1L4 and 36 immune checkpoint molecules (Figs. 11D). Furthermore, we evaluated the effectiveness of immune checkpoint inhibitor therapies in groups with high and low NAP1L4 expression using the Immune Prognostic Score (IPS). The study revealed that elevated NAP1L4 expression correlates with lower IPS scores, suggesting decreased sensitivity to CTLA-4 and PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade therapies.Ultimately, we assessed the responsiveness to immunotherapy in both high and low NAP1L4 expression groups, revealing that the TIDE score was elevated in the high NAP1L4 group (Fig. 11E, F), suggesting an increased likelihood of immune evasion.

Identification of drug sensitivity in HCC patients

To investigate the potential association between risk scores and drug sensitivity, the “oncoPredict” package was employed to forecast the drug responsiveness of HCC patients.The results indicated that HCC patients with lower NAP1L4 expression exhibited heightened sensitivity to Cisplatin, Axitinib, SB216763, among others (Figs. 11G). Conversely, those with elevated NAP1L4 expression demonstrated increased sensitivity to Gefitinib, Wee1 Inhibitor, MK − 2206, etc. Collectively, these findings offer valuable insights for clinical treatment strategies.

Mendelian randomization analysis

Given the multifaceted role of senescence in hepatocarcinogenesis, we extended our investigation with a Mendelian randomization framework to dissect whether senescence-related genes exert their oncogenic effects on hepatocellular carcinoma via modulation of the gut microbiota. Following the screening process, two specific SRGs and 17 distinct types of gut microbiota were identified as having potential causal relationships with HCC. The F-statistic for all IVs was greater than 10, suggesting no weak instrument bias.Subsequent Bonferroni correction revealed that all p-values fell below the Bonferroni significance threshold.In assessing the causal association of SRGs with HCC, two SRGs were identified to exhibit potential causal relationships: SERPINE2 and ANGPT1 (Fig. 12A, B, E, F). Conversely, when evaluating the causal influence of HCC on the SRGs, all p-values were above 0.05, showing that HCC does not exert an effect on the examined SRGs (Supplementary Table 3). A two-step MR analysis identified causal associations between 17 gut microbiota types and HCC. For instance, the abundance of Syntrophorhabdia, Poseidoniaceae, UBA8904, Elusimicrobiota, Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Faecalitalea cylindroides, etc. were positively correlated with HCC. Conversely, the abundance of Klebsiella, Eubacterium F, Proteus, Providencia, etc. were negatively associated with HCC, as illustrated in (Supplementary Fig. 2). Among the two SRGs identified as causally linked to HCC, one exhibited a significant association with a specific type of gut microbiota.Our study identified a correlation between ANGPT1 and increased Faecalitalea cylindroides abundance in stool, which is linked to a higher risk of HCC.As depicted in Figs. 12C, D, G and H, the analysis showed that the abundance of Faecalitalea cylindroides in stool contributed to 5.18% of the heightened risk linked to ANGPT1 with regard to HCC (proportion mediated = 12.3%; 95% CI = 3.36–21.2%; p = 0.007). These preliminary results suggest that ANGPT1 may partially elevate the risk of HCC by exacerbating the adverse effects of Faecalitalea cylindroides abundance in stool (Fig. 13A and B). To ensure the robustness of these causal effect estimations, multiple sensitivity analyses were performed to account for potential pleiotropic effects.Cochran’s Q-test and funnel plot analysis indicated no heterogeneity or asymmetry among the SNPs involved in the causal relationship (Supplementary Table 4, Fig. 3A). The effect of each SNPs on the causal estimates was confirmed by employing a leave-one-out analysis. (Supplementary Fig. 3B). Reassessing the Mendelian randomization study by excluding individual SNPs consistently produced similar results, suggesting each SNP significantly contributed to establishing the causal relationship.

Discussion

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) represents the most common type of primary liver cancer, substantially affecting cancer-related morbidity and mortality rates. The World Health Organization has raised concerns over the rising incidence and mortality rates associated with HCC, particularly in low-resource settings [4]. The current therapeutic strategies, which include surgical resection, liver transplantation, local ablation, and targeted therapies, face considerable hurdles, especially postoperative recurrence rates and less than optimal patient outcomes [30, 31], highlighting the critical need for innovative biomarkers to enhance prognostic accuracy and enable personalized treatment strategies. Cellular senescence, characterized by a permanent halt in cell division, is increasingly acknowledged as a crucial factor in the advancement of tumors. On the one hand, senescence acts as an intrinsic tumor-suppressive mechanism by stabilizing p53/p16-Rb signaling, thereby preventing the clonal expansion of damaged hepatocytes, on the other hand, the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP) can create a chronic inflammatory milieu that fosters proliferation, angiogenesis, and immune evasion, ultimately promoting tumor progression [32–34]. Previous work by Sicheng Liu et al. stratified hepatocellular carcinoma risk exclusively on the basis of a single machine-learning algorithm applied to senescence-related genes. Given the rapid evolution of machine-learning methodologies, a more comprehensive investigation is now warranted [12].

In this investigation, we clarified the role of SRGs in HCC prognosis through an extensive machine learning approach and Mendelian randomization analysis.We screened a total of 125 stress response genes (SRGs) and utilized machine learning techniques to create a robust prognostic index based on these genes. By analyzing the TCGA-HCC dataset, we identified nine prognostic SRGs: ANGPT1, CSF1, HGF, ICAM1, KITLG, NAP1L4, SERPINE2, SPP1 and VEGFA. We validated the SRG score, revealing that patients with higher scores had significantly worse prognoses compared to those with lower scores. Moreover, we validated the prognostic utility of the SRG score for outcomes in ICGC cohort [35]. The SRG score independently predicts HCC prognosis, as confirmed by multivariate Cox regression analysis and ROC curve demonstrated that the SRG score outperforms other clinicopathological parameters in predicting patient prognosis. To enhance clinical relevance and refine prognosis predictions, we integrated commonly used clinicopathological parameters with the SRG score to develop a nomogram.

Using eight different machine learning techniques, we identified potential marker molecules linked to hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). Our results underscore the significance of these genes, particularly NAP1L4, as crucial prognostic biomarkers. NAP1L4, implicated in chromatin remodeling and cellular senescence, has emerged as a pivotal factor in predicting outcomes for patients with HCC. The multilevel expression verification of NAP1L4 provides a comprehensive and robust validation of its role in HCC. The consistent elevation of NAP1L4 expression across different databases, including GEPIA2.0, UALCAN, and the Human Protein Atlas (HPA), as well as in clinical samples, leaves little room for doubt regarding its upregulation in HCC. The experimental validation in liver cancer cell lines further strengthens the evidence. The significantly elevated expression of NAP1L4 in liver cancer cell lines compared to normal liver cell lines, along with the functional assays showing that its knockdown impairs the proliferation and migration abilities of HCCLM3 cells, suggests that NAP1L4 may be a functional driver in HCC progression. These findings not only reinforce NAP1L4’s potential as a diagnostic and prognostic biomarker but also hint at its possible role as a therapeutic target [36, 37]. Recognizing NAP1L4 as a prognostic marker highlights the necessity for additional research into its role in HCC and its viability as a therapeutic target. The RNA sequencing analysis offers valuable insights into the potential mechanisms through which NAP1L4 exerts its influence in HCC. The identification of 129 upregulated and 101 downregulated genes in LM3 cells with NAP1L4 knockdown provides comprehensive data for exploring the downstream effects of NAP1L4. The Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses highlighting the NOD-like receptor signaling pathway open up new avenues for understanding the biological processes involved. This pathway’s involvement suggests that NAP1L4 may play a role in modulating immune responses and inflammatory processes, which are critical in the tumor microenvironment and HCC progression. We further explored its clinical value. Analysis of TCGA data revealed significant differences in tumor stages among HCC patients based on NAP1L4 expression levels. Multivariate analysis confirmed NAP1L4 as an independent prognostic risk factor. Clinical validation using immunohistochemical specimens from 40 HCC patients showed higher NAP1L4 expression in tumor tissues compared to adjacent non-tumorous tissues, with further elevation in advanced-stage tumors.

We also investigated the relationship between NAP1L4 and the tumor microenvironment, revealing that its relationship with specific immune cell populations provides critical insights into the immunological landscape of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). The interaction between M0 macrophages, Tregs and NK cells in relation to NAP1L4 expression underscores the tumor microenvironment’s complexity and its impact on immunotherapy approaches. Future studies should focus on elucidating the mechanistic pathways involved and examining potential therapeutic approaches to augment immune function in HCC patients with elevated NAP1L4 levels.

The exploration of immune checkpoint molecules represents a vital area in immunotherapy research, particularly in liver cancer, where the tumor microenvironment significantly impacts therapeutic outcomes. Our findings reveal a marked positive correlation between NAP1L4 and 36 immune checkpoint genes, suggesting that NAP1L4 may contribute to the immune evasion strategies employed by tumors. This observation aligns with established paradigms in immuno-oncology, where immune checkpoints are recognized as critical regulators of immune responses, facilitating tumor evasion from immune surveillance. Prior studies emphasize the critical role of immune checkpoints like PD-1 and CTLA-4 in determining the effectiveness of immunotherapy, underscoring the need to assess biomarkers that can predict treatment response [38–40].

Our study offers important insights into the drug sensitivity profiles of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients in relation to their NAP1L4 expression levels.We found that patients exhibiting low NAP1L4 expression demonstrated increased sensitivity to Cisplatin, a widely utilized platinum-based chemotherapy agent for various malignancies, including HCC. the pathophysiology of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). NAP1L4’s role in increasing cancer cell sensitivity to treatments like Cisplatin suggests its potential as a predictor of therapeutic responses. This aligns with previous studies emphasizing the importance of identifying molecular markers to tailor chemotherapy regimens for improved patient outcomes [41]. Additionally, the observation that HCC patients exhibiting elevated NAP1L4 levels are more responsive to Wee1 inhibitors and MK-2206 suggests that NAP1L4 may be integral to the modulation of cell cycle checkpoints and survival pathways [42–44], reinforcing its candidacy as a viable therapeutic target in HCC .

Moreover, our research unveils significant causal relationships between specific senescence-related genes (SRGs) and HCC, accentuating the role of gut microbiota through Mendelian randomization for mediation analysis.A pivotal finding is the identification of ANGPT1 as a crucial SRG associated with an elevated risk of HCC, primarily influenced by its relationship with the abundance of Faecalitalea cylindroides in fecal samples.This highlights the intricate interplay between host genetics and gut microbiota.ANGPT1, recognized for its involvement in angiogenesis and vascular remodeling, is linked to several malignancies, indicating that its elevated expression might facilitate tumor progression by altering the tumor microenvironment [45, 46]. The correlation between ANGPT1 and Faecalitalea cylindroides, a bacterium linked to Cellular senescence process [47], points to a potential mechanism whereby dysbiosis could elevate HCC risk.

It is essential to recognize the limitations inherent in this study. First, the prognostic value of the SRG score requires validation in larger, real-world cohorts. Second, the absence of classical senescence phenotyping (e.g., SA-β-gal, p16INK4a, p21, or SASP protein quantification) precludes a definitive assignment of NAP1L4 as a senescence-related driver of HCC. Third, although RNA-seq, molecular docking and single-cell co-expression analyses implicate the NLRC5-NOD-like receptor axis, causal evidence is lacking because rescue experiments and NLRC5 loss-of-function studies were not performed. Fourth, immune-microenvironment findings remain associative; functional assays such as Treg suppression or macrophage co-culture are needed to confirm that NAP1L4 actively orchestrates immune evasion. Finally, owing to budgetary and time constraints, in vivo validation of tumorigenicity and drug sensitivity in animal models was not undertaken. These gaps will be addressed in future investigations to establish the full therapeutic potential of targeting NAP1L4 in HCC.

In summary, we identified nine stress response genes (SRGs) associated with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) prognosis using machine learning and constructed a prognostic scoring system validated in TCGA and ICGC cohorts. We focused on NAP1L4, whose upregulation in HCC tissues correlates with tumor stage and prognosis and may predict immunotherapy and chemotherapy responses. Our study also revealed a potential link between gut microbiota and HCC risk, emphasizing the interplay between host genetics and gut microbiota. These findings provide new insights and potential targets for HCC diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment.

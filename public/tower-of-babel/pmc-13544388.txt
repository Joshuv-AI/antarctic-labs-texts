Protocol for functional characterization of adult hippocampal neural precursor cells under demyelinating injury conditions in mice
Authors: Shiva Nemati, Soheila Karimi-Abdolrezaee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryAdult hippocampal neural precursor cells (Hip-NPCs) are highly influenced by brain injury and diseases. Investigating the properties of Hip-NPCs is important for determining their reparative response. Here, we present a protocol for the functional characterization of Hip-NPCs under physiological and demyelinating conditions in adult mice. We describe steps for assessing of Hip-NPC self-renewal, activation state, proliferation, metabolism, and tri-lineage fate commitment. This workflow enables systematic evaluation of how pathological conditions and therapeutic interventions alter Hip-NPC functional states and adaptive responses.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Nemati et al.1Subject areas: Cell Biology, Neuroscience, Stem Cells

Before you begin

In this protocol, Hip-NPCs are isolated from adult mice under physiological and demyelinating conditions. We have induced brain demyelination in adult mice using cuprizone diet that is described in this protocol. Our primary focus in this protocol will be on multidimensional cellular and functional characterization of Hip-NPCs that include self-renewal and proliferation, cellular activity measurements, protein synthesis and metabolic profiling, and lineage specification analysis. Dissection and isolation of Hip-NPCs from adult mice were performed using similar procedures that were previously described by Ahmed et al. (STAR Protocols),2 with minor modifications to culture media composition provided in this protocol. For this protocol, all cell culture procedures must be conducted in a biosafety cabinet under sterile conditions, and cultured cells are maintained in a humidified incubator set at 37°C with 5% CO2. For complete experimental validation and application of this workflow in cuprizone mouse model of demyelination, refer to our previous study by Nemati et al, Stem Cell Reports 2026.1

Innovation

Hip-NPCs support hippocampal function and cognition across the lifespan through continuous neurogenesis.3,4,5 The intricate properties of Hip-NPCs are highly regulated by both intrinsic programs and extrinsic environmental cues. In the context of brain pathology, we have demonstrated that chronic demyelination results in quiescence of Hip-NPCs that contributes to repair failure, hippocampal degeneration, and enduring cognitive impairments in adult mice.1 This protocol provides a standardized framework for the functional assessments of adult Hip-NPCs under brain demyelinating conditions using a cuprizone (CPZ)-induced demyelination mouse model.6 Given the complexity of Hip-NPCs behaviour, the innovation of this protocol lies in its multifaceted approach for evaluation of self-renewal, proliferation dynamics, stem cell activity state, metabolic rate, and lineage commitment within a relevant in vitro platform. By integrating quantitative cellular and molecular readouts, the protocol enables reproducible evaluation of NPC plasticity, fate specification, and adaptive responses to demyelinating injury. Moreover, this protocol provides a versatile platform for mechanistic investigations of intracellular pathways that modulate the behaviour of Hip-NPCs under physiological and pathological conditions. Of note, in addition to demyelinating conditions, this protocol is also applicable to other neuropathological disorders such as neurodegenerative diseases, stroke and traumatic brain injury that can result in hippocampal pathology.

Institutional permissions

All animal procedures were approved by the University of Manitoba Animal Care Committee and conducted in accordance with the Canadian Council on Animal Care guidelines (protocol numbers #20-065 and #24-054). Animals were housed in standard plastic cages under controlled conditions (22°C, 12 h light/dark cycle) with ad libitum access to food and water.

All live-cell procedures must be performed under sterile conditions in a certified biosafety cabinet. Work involving primary neural tissue and cell culture was performed in compliance with Biosafety Level 2 (CL2) requirements according to the University of Manitoba Biosafety Committee and Environmental Health and Safety (EHS) program guidelines.

Establishment of CPZ-induced demyelination mouse model

Timing: 5–10 weeks

This section describes the induction of acute and chronic demyelination in mice using CPZ.

Note: Continuous exposure to a CPZ-containing diet induces a robust and reproducible demyelinating phenotype characterized by oligodendrocyte loss, demyelination, and reactive gliosis across multiple brain regions, including the hippocampus.1,6,7,8 We have demonstrated that brain demyelination induces a transient shift in Hip-NPCs from an active state toward a quiescent-like state.1 CPZ-induced demyelination is an established and widely used model to investigate mechanisms of demyelination and endogenous repair processes including remyelination and neurogenesis.

Note: The duration of CPZ administration determines the severity and stage of pathology. A 5-weeks CPZ regimen induces acute demyelination with spontaneous remyelination and recovery following cessation of CPZ exposure, whereas prolonged CPZ exposure for 10 weeks leads to chronic demyelinating pathology accompanied by impaired spontaneous remyelination, progressive neurodegeneration and cognitive decline. The CPZ model provides a reliable platform for studying cellular, molecular, and histopathological responses to demyelination. In this protocol, we describe a workflow to investigate Hip-NPC behavior within the demyelinating hippocampal niche, with particular emphasis on lineage commitment, and cellular dynamics in response to pathology-associated changes. Detailed characterization of CPZ-induced pathology and experimental outcomes has been reported in our original study.1

EdU and NESTIN co-immunolabeling to assess NPC proliferation

Timing: 2 days

This step describes EdU (5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine) labeling, which is a thymidine analog-based method used to assess cell proliferation by monitoring DNA synthesis.

Note: During S phase of the cell cycle, actively dividing cells incorporate EdU into newly synthesized DNA in place of thymidine. Because this incorporation occurs only in cells undergoing DNA replication, EdU serves as a direct marker of proliferating cells and allows tracking proliferative dynamics of NPCs and their progenies. Detection of incorporated EdU is performed using a copper-catalyzed “click chemistry” reaction, in which a fluorescent azide selectively binds to the ethynyl group of EdU. This provides a rapid, sensitive, and antibody-free method for identifying proliferating cells.12

Note: For cell type–specific analysis, EdU labeling is combined with lineage markers. In this protocol, EdU is paired with NESTIN immunostaining, a marker of NPCs, to evaluate NPC proliferation under both physiological and pathological conditions. Depending on the experimental design, EdU detection can be performed before or after immunocytochemistry (ICC) procedure. In this protocol, NESTIN staining is performed first, followed by EdU detection as the final step (Figures 3A).

CRITICAL: Mice must receive ethynyl deoxyuridine (EdU) prior to initiating this procedure. The timing of EdU administration, number of injections, and dosing concentration should be optimized empirically according to the specific experimental design. In our study that examined Hip-NPC proliferation under physiological conditions compared with CPZ-induced demyelinating pathology, mice received daily EdU injections at 12.5 mg/kg for one week before Hip-NPC isolation (refer to Nemati et al. Expected outcomes

The expected outcomes vary across the different functional assessments and are therefore presented separately for each assay. This section summarizes the anticipated results of Hip-NPC functional characterization under control and CPZ-induced demyelinating conditions in an assay-specific manner.

Expected outcomes for neurosphere colony-forming assay

In healthy control conditions, neurospheres are expected to be more abundant, relatively uniform in size, and generally larger in diameter. This reflects normal Hip-NPC growth and self-renewal capacity. In contrast, under CPZ-induced demyelination (pathological) conditions, a decrease in neurosphere number, smaller average size, and greater variation in size are expected. A shift toward smaller diameters in the size distribution would suggest reduced NPC proliferation and altered cell cycle. However, these outcomes may vary depending on the specific disease model or treatment paradigm. Different pathological contexts and interventions can differentially influence NPC self-renewal, leading to distinct neurosphere profiles. Therefore, interpretation of results should be made within the biological context of each experimental condition.

Expected outcomes for NPC activity assessment

In our experiment, mice were fed with a CPZ diet for defined period to induce brain demyelination.1 As demonstrated in our previously published study,1 CPZ-induced demyelination promotes a shift in Hip-NPCs from an active proliferative state toward a more quiescent phenotype. Consistent with this transition, Hip-NPC activity assessment is expected to reveal distinct differences between physiological and pathological conditions. Under healthy conditions, Hip-NPCs exhibit higher proliferative capacity, characterized by an increase in the number of EdU labeled cells. Additionally, CPZ-induced pathology is associated with reduced NPC activation, reflected by decreased proliferation rates and diminished EGFR expression. Quantitative analyses are therefore expected to show a lower percentage of EdU+ NESTIN+/DAPI+ proliferating NPCs and a reduction in the number of EGFR+/NESTIN+/DAPI+ NPCs and the NPC expression level of EGFR in CPZ-treated animals compared with controls. Together, EdU-NESTIN based proliferation measurements and EGFR–NESTIN co-labeling provides complementary functional readouts of Hip-NPC activity state, enabling evaluation of how demyelinating injury impacts the response of Hip-NPC and their regenerative potential.

Expected outcomes for NPC metabolic rate assessment

OPP fluorescence intensity normalized to the total number of NESTIN+DAPI+ cells is expected to be significantly reduced in Hip-NPCs under CPZ-induced demyelinated conditions compared with controls. In control mice, Hip-NPCs exhibit higher OPP signal consistent with the baseline metabolic and translational activity. In contrast, CPZ-induced demyelination is expected to markedly decrease OPP intensity within the NESTIN+/DAPI+ population, reflecting reduced protein synthesis and a shift toward a lower metabolic state. Overall, these results indicate impaired metabolic activity of Hip-NPCs in CPZ pathology relative to healthy controls.

Expected outcomes for NPC fate specification assessment

Using this differentiation method, we expect that multipotent Hip-NPCs will give rise to a heterogenous population of neurons (∼40%), astrocytes (40%–45%) and oligodendrocytes (10%–12%) under the control condition. In contrast, CPZ-induced demyelination induces a shift in lineage commitment that favours oligodendrogenesis at the expense of neuronal differentiation. This pattern is consistent with a pathology-associated alteration in Hip-NPC fate specification, as we reported in our original study.1

Limitations

The limitations of this protocol differ across the different functional assessments and are therefore addressed separately for each assay. This section outlines the potential limitations of Hip-NPC functional characterization under control and CPZ-induced demyelinating conditions in an assay-specific manner.

Neurosphere colony forming assay’s limitations

In vitro culture conditions do not fully recapitulate the in vivo NPC niche, including extracellular matrix interactions, vascular inputs, immune components, and inflammatory cues present during demyelination. As a result, neurosphere behavior observed in culture may not fully represent physiological NPC responses in vivo.

Neurosphere assays primarily provide structural and morphological readouts, such as sphere number and diameter, and therefore do not directly capture functional cellular states or underlying molecular mechanisms. As a result, interpretation of NPC behaviour solely based on these parameters remains limited. Without in vivo complementary approaches, including activity-based analyses (e.g., proliferation rate assessment, cell cycle profiling), as well as gene expression and signaling pathway analyses, a comprehensive understanding of NPC function and lineage dynamics cannot be achieved.

NPC activity assessment’s limitation

This protocol provides a robust approach for assessing Hip-NPC activity; however, several limitations should be considered. EdU labeling reflects S-phase entry within a defined time window and does not provide information on long-term outcomes. Similarly, NESTIN and EGFR are well-established markers of NPC identity and activation, but their expression may vary depending on cellular state and context; however, their combined use with EdU enhances the functional interpretation of NPC activity changes. In addition, immunofluorescence-based quantification may be affected by technical variability, although standardized imaging and analysis procedures help ensure reliable comparisons between groups. Overall, despite these considerations, the integrated EdU–NESTIN and EGFR-NESTIN approach provides a sensitive and complementary strategy for evaluating Hip-NPC activation dynamics under brain physiological and pathological conditions.

NPC metabolic rate assessment’s limitation

This approach provides a reliable readout of global protein synthesis in Hip-NPCs; however, several limitations should be considered. OPP labeling captures translational activity within a defined time window and therefore reflects a snapshot rather than long-term metabolic dynamics. Timepoint analysis is required to determine NPC metabolic activity over a time period. Quantification of fluorescence intensity may also be influenced by technical factors such as staining efficiency, imaging settings, and background signal, although standardized acquisition and analysis procedures minimize variability. Despite these considerations, the combined OPP–NESTIN approach provides a robust and sensitive method to assess relative changes in NPC metabolic activity under control and pathologic conditions.

NPC fate specification assessment’s limitations

NPC differentiation method in vitro does recapitulate key features of the brain microenvironment, including niche-derived signaling, cell–cell interactions, and inflammatory cues associated with CPZ-induced demyelination or other pathological conditions. Moreover, in vitro systems do not fully model the long-term maturation and functional integration of Hip-NPC derived neural cells in vivo. Nevertheless, this approach enables robust comparative analysis of NPC behaviour under homeostasis and pathology and provides a platform for high throughput screening and evaluation of candidate treatments.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Low cell viability after isolation of Hip-NPCs due to prolonged dissection time, harsh enzymatic dissociation and temperature fluctuations during processing. Related to Neurosphere colony forming assay (Step 4).

Potential solution

Perform the dissection quickly and efficiently while preserving tissue integrity. Keep cells on ice when not actively processing and avoid temperature fluctuation throughout the procedure. For full description, refer to the isolation method described by Ahmed et al.2 for optimizing dissociation steps.

Problem 2

Low neurosphere yield or poor sphere formation, which may be associated with excessive mechanical stress during tissue dissociation, incorrect seeding density or cell loss during transfer, and insufficient activity of growth factors in the culture media. Related to Neurosphere colony forming assay (Step 10).

Potential solution

Ensure gentle trituration to minimize mechanical damage to cells. Reconfirm accurate cell counting and maintain clonal density at 10 cells/μL to ensure consistent neurosphere formation. Prepare fresh growth factor–supplemented NPC proliferation media and verify appropriate storage conditions of growth factors (−20°C or −80°C, as applicable) to preserve their optimal activity.

Problem 3

Over-trituration or harsh pipetting during mechanical passaging of the Hip-NPCs can damage cells, reduce viability, and alter NPC phenotype. Related to Mechanical (enzyme-free) neurosphere passaging (Step 28).

Potential solution

Standardize trituration by defining a fixed number of gentle pipetting cycles (e.g., 8–12 slow passes) and avoid bubble formation.

Problem 4

Residual clumps during mechanical passaging of the Hip-NPCs can lead to uneven seeding density and biased neurosphere formation. Related to Mechanical (enzyme-free) neurosphere passaging (Step 28).

Potential solution

Perform a visual quality check under the microscope prior to seeding the cells. If clumps persist, apply an additional gentle trituration step or allow a brief settling period to remove larger aggregates before plating.

Problem 5

Uneven cell attachment or low NPC survival after plating, which may result from incomplete dissociation, inaccurate cell counting, or uneven/insufficient Matrigel coating. Related to NPC plating procedure (Steps 44 and 45).

Potential solution

Gently but thoroughly dissociate neurospheres into a single-cell suspension and accurately determine cell density before plating. Ensure uniform Matrigel coating and avoid drying of coated surfaces prior to cell plating. Plate 10,000 NPCs per well and gently shake the plate to ensure even distribution of the suspension before transferring it to the incubator. Minimize handling time and allow cells to attach undisturbed during the initial recovery period.

Problem 6

Lineage quantification can be affected by non-specific antibody staining and the partial overlap of early lineage markers, which may complicate the identification of transitional progenitor populations and introduce variability between experiments. Related to NPC fate specification assessment procedure (Step 74).

Possible solution

To minimize variability, validate antibody specificity using appropriate controls, optimize staining conditions and imaging parameters, and, when possible, use combinations of lineage markers to improve discrimination between closely related cell states. Maintaining consistent image acquisition settings and performing blinded quantification across all experimental groups can further improve reproducibility and accuracy. Moreover, other complementary approaches such as qPCR and western blotting can be used to validate immunofluorescent assessments.

. In the paper, we investigate the uniqueness problem of entire functions concerning their linear differential polynomial in shift and obtain three results which improve and generalize the recent result due to Qi (Ann. Polon. Math., 102 (2011), 129-142.) in a large extend.

1.
Introduction and main results

We assume that the reader is familiar with standard notation and main results of Nevanlinna Theory (see
[
10
,
34
]
). By
S(r,f)
we denote any quantity that satisfies the condition
S(r,f)=o(T(r,f))
as
r\to\infty
possibly outside of an exceptional set of finite linear measure. A meromorphic function
a
is said to be a small function of
f
if
T(r,a)=S(r,f)
and we denote by
S(f)
the set of functions which are small compared to
f
.
Moreover, we use notations
\rho(f)
and
\rho_{1}(f)
for the order and the hyper-order of a meromorphic function
f
respectively.
As usual, the abbreviation CM means “counting multiplicities”, while IM means “ignoring multiplicities”.

Let
k,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in S(f)
. Denote by
S_{(m,n)}(a_{1})
the set of those points
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
z
is an
a_{1}
-point of
f
of order
m
and an
a_{1}
-point of
g
of order
n
. The set
S_{(m,n)}(a_{2})
can be defined similarly. Let
\overline{N}_{(m,n)}(r,a_{i};f)
denote the reduced counting function of
f
with respect to the set
S_{(m,n)}(a_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

The research on the uniqueness problem of meromorphic function sharing values or small functions with its derivatives is an active field and the study is based on the Nevanlinna value distribution theory. The research on this topic was started by Rubel and Yang
[
31
]
. Now we state their result.

Theorem A
.

[
31
]
Let
f
be a non-constant entire function and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
such that
b\not=a
. If
f
and
f^{\prime}
share
a
and
b
CM, then
f\equiv f^{\prime}
.

Mues and Steinmetz
[
27
]
further generalized Theorem A from sharing values CM to IM and obtained the following result.

Theorem B
.

[
27
]
Let
f
be a non-constant entire function and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
such that
b\not=a
. If
f
and
f^{\prime}
share
a
and
b
IM, then
f\equiv f^{\prime}
.

Recently the uniqueness of meromorphic functions sharing values with their shifts or difference operators has become a subject of great interest. Now, we recall the following result due to Heittokangas et al.
[

Theorem C
.

[
12
]
Let
f
be a non-constant entire function of finite order,

and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
be distinct. If
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
a
and
b
CM, then
f(z)\equiv f(z+c)
.

In 2011, Qi
[

further improved Theorem C and obtained the following result.

Theorem D
.

[
28

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function of finite order,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
be distinct. If
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
a
and
b
IM, then
f(z)\equiv f(z+c)
.

We now introduce a linear differential polynomial as follows

\mathscr{L}_{k}(f)=b_{0}f+b_{1}f^{\prime}+\ldots+b_{k}f^{(k)},

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
and
b_{j}\in S(f)
such that
(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\not\equiv(0,0,\ldots,0)
.

In the paper, we have extended and improved Theorem D in the following three directions:

(1)

We replace
f(z+c)
by
\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
in Theorem D.

(2)

We consider
a
and
b
as the small functions of
f
in Theorem D.

(3)

We replace
\rho(f)<+\infty
by
\rho_{1}(f)<1
in Theorem D.

Now we state our results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in S(f)
be distinct such that either
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are
c
-periodic or
a_{1}^{\prime}-a^{\prime}_{2}
is non-constant. Let

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{\left((a_{1}^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime})(f-a_{1})-(a_{1}-a_{2})(f^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime})\right)\left(f-\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))\right)}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}.

(1.1)

If
f(z)
and
\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM, then either
f(z)\equiv\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
or
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\psi_{i}=-b_{0}
,
where
\psi_{1}(z)=(a_{1}^{\prime}(z+c)-a_{2}^{\prime}(z+c)-\phi(z+c))/(a_{1}(z+c)-a_{2}(z+c))
and
\psi_{i+1}=\psi_{i}^{\prime}+\psi_{1}\psi_{i},\;i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.

When
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
and
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}
are constant, we have the following.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
be distinct. Let
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})\neq(0,0,\ldots,0)
and
\phi
be defined as in (
1.1
).
If
f(z)
and
\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM, then either
f(z)\equiv\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
or
\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}\psi_{i}=-b_{0}
,
where
\psi_{1}(z)=-\phi(z+c)/(a_{1}-a_{2})
is a constant and
\psi_{i+1}=\psi_{i}^{\prime}+\psi_{1}\psi_{i},\;i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
.

When
(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})=(1,0,0,\ldots,0)
, we have the following.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in S(f)
be distinct such that either
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are
c
-periodic or
a_{1}^{\prime}-a^{\prime}_{2}
is non-constant. If
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM, then
f(z)\equiv f(z+c)
.

When
(b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})=(0,0,0,\ldots,1)
, we have the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in S(f)
be distinct such that either
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are
c
-periodic or
a_{1}^{\prime}-a^{\prime}_{2}
is non-constant. If
f(z)
and
f^{(k)}(z+c)
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM, then
f(z)\equiv f^{(k)}(z+c)
.

Following examples show that the condition “
\rho_{1}(f)<1
” in Theorem
1.1
is necessary.

Example 1.1
.

Let
f(z)=e^{\sin z}
,
c=\pi
,
a_{1}(z)=1
,
a_{2}(z)=-1
,
b_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{4}
and
b_{1}(z)=-\frac{3}{4\cos z}
. Clearly
\rho_{1}(f)=1
and
\mathscr{L}_{1}(f(z+c))=b_{0}(z)f(z+c)+b_{1}(z)f^{\prime}(z+c)=e^{-\sin z}
. Now from (
1.1
), we see that
\phi(z)=-2\cos z
and so
\psi_{1}(z)=(a^{\prime}_{1}(z+c)-a^{\prime}_{2}(z+c)-\phi(z+c))/(a_{1}(z+c)-a_{2}(z+c))=\cos z
, but
b_{1}(z)\psi_{1}(z)=-\frac{3}{4}\neq-b_{0}(z)
. Clearly
f(z)
and
\mathscr{L}_{1}(f(z+c))
share
1
and
-1
CM, but
f
does not satisfy any case of Theorem
1.1
.

Example 1.2
.

Let
f(z)=e^{e^{z}}
,
c=\pi i
,
a_{1}=1
and
a_{2}(z)=-e^{z}
. Note that
\rho_{1}(f)=1
. Clearly
f(z)
and
f^{\prime}(z+c)
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM, but
f(z)\not\equiv f^{\prime}(z+c)
.

Following example asserts that condition “
a_{1},a_{2}(\not\equiv\infty)
” is sharp in Theorem
1.1
.

Example 1.3
.

Let
f(z)=\sin z
,
c=\pi
,
a_{1}(z)=0
and
a_{2}(z)=\infty
. Clearly
\rho_{1}(f)=0<1
and
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
, but
f(z)\not\equiv f(z+c)
.

Following example shows that Theorem
1.1
does not hold for non-constant meromorphic function
f
with
N(r,f)\neq S(r,f)
.

Example 1.4
.

Let
f(z)=\frac{e^{z}+1}{e^{z}-1}
,
e^{c}=-1
,
a_{1}=1
and
a_{2}=-1
. Note that
\rho_{1}(f)=0<1
and
N(r,f)\neq S(r,f)
. Clearly
f(z)
and
f(z+c)
share
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
CM, but
f(z)\not\equiv f(z+c)
.

2.
Auxiliary lemmas

In the proof of Lemma
2.4
below, we make use of three key lemmas. For the convenience of the reader, we recall these lemmas here.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
35
]
If
f
and
g
are non-constant meromorphic functions, then

N\big(r,f/g\big)-N\big(r,g/f\big)=N(r,f)+N(r,0;g)-N(r,g)-N(r,0;f).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
9
]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
and
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Then

m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f(z)}{f(z+c)}\right)=S(r,f)

for all
r
outside of a possible exceptional set
E
with finite logarithmic measure.

Combining the lemma of logarithmic derivatives with Lemma
2.2
, the key result for meromorphic function
f
with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
is easily obtained

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)\leq m\left(r,\frac{f^{(k)}(z+c)}{f(z+c)}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f(z+c)}{f(z)}\right)=S(r,f)

(2.1)

for all
r
outside of a possible exceptional set
E
with finite logarithmic measure, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
32
]
Let
f
be a non-constant meromorphic function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
and
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Then
T(r,f(z+c))=T(r,f)+S(r,f)
and
N(r,f(z+c))=N(r,f)+S(r,f).

We define
g(z)=\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c))
and the following auxiliary functions

\displaystyle\psi=\frac{\delta(g)(f-g)}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})},

(2.2)

where
\delta(g)=(a_{1}^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime})(g-a_{1})-(a_{1}-a_{2})(g^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime})=(a_{1}^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime})(g-a_{2})-(a_{1}-a_{2})(g^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle H_{nm}=n\phi-m\psi\;\;\text{and}\;\;H=\frac{\delta(f)}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}-\frac{\delta(g)}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})},\;\text{where}\;m,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
f
be a non-constant entire function with
\rho_{1}(f)<1
,
c\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
and
a_{1},a_{2}\in S(f)
be distinct. If
f
and
g
share
a_{1},a_{2}
IM and
T(r,f)=T(r,g)+S(r,f)
,
then
f\equiv g
.

Proof.

If
f\equiv g
, then the proof is trivial. In the following, we assume that
f\not\equiv g
.
Using second fundamental theorem for small functions (see
[
33
]
), we get
T(r,f)\leq\overline{N}(r,a_{1};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};f)+S(r,f)
.
Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\overline{N}(r,a_{i};f)\leq N(r,0;f-g)
and so from (
2.1
), we get

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}_{i=1}^{2}}{\sum}\overline{N}(r,a_{i};f)\leq T(r,f-g)+S(r,f)\leq m(r,f-g)+S(r,f)\leq T(r,f)+S(r,f).

(2.3)

Therefore

\displaystyle T(r,f)=\overline{N}(r,a_{1};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};f)+S(r,f).

(2.4)

If possible, suppose
\delta\left(g\right)\equiv 0
. Then
\frac{g^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime}}{g-a_{1}}\equiv\frac{a_{1}^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime}}{a_{1}-a_{2}}
and so

g\equiv a_{1}+c_{0}(a_{1}-a_{2})
, where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Since
T(r,f)=T(r,g)+S(r,f)
, we have
T(r,f)=S(r,f)
, which is a contradiction. Therefore
\delta\left(g\right)\not\equiv 0
. Similarly
\delta(f)\not\equiv 0
. Consequently
\phi\not\equiv 0
and
\psi\not\equiv 0
. Also by the given condition, it is easy to get
N(r,\phi)=S(r,f)
. Note that

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{\delta(f)}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{1}{a_{1}-a_{2}}\left(\frac{\delta(f)}{f-a_{1}}-\frac{\delta(f)}{f-a_{2}}\right)\right)\leq S(r,f).

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(f)f}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}=\frac{\delta(f)}{f-a_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}\delta(f)}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}\;\;\text{and so}\;\;m\left(r,\frac{\delta(f)f}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}\right)=S(r,f).

Clearly

\displaystyle\phi=\frac{\delta(f)f}{(f-a_{1})(f-a_{2})}\left(1-\frac{g}{f}\right)

(2.5)

and so from (
2.1
), we get
m(r,\phi)=S(r,f)
. Therefore
T(r,\phi)=S(r,f)
. Again from (
2.5
), we get
m(r,0;f)=S(r,f)
.
Let
a_{3}=a_{1}+l(a_{1}-a_{2})
, where
l\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then in view of (
2.4
) and using second fundamental theorem for small functions (see
[
33
]
), we get

\displaystyle 2T(r,f)\leq\overline{N}(r,a_{1};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{3};f)+S(r,f)\leq 2T(r,f)-m(r,a_{3};f)+S(r,f),

i.e.,
m(r,a_{3};f)=S(r,f)
. Therefore

\displaystyle m(r,0;f)+m(r,a_{3};f)=S(r,f).

(2.6)

Also in view of (
2.4
) and using second fundamental theorem for small functions (see
[
33
]
), we get

\displaystyle 2T(r,g)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a_{1};g)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};g)+\overline{N}(r,a_{3};g)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a_{1};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};f)+T(r,g)-m(r,a_{3};g)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,f)+T(r,g)-m(r,a_{3};g)+S(r,f)=2T(r,g)-m(r,a_{3};g)+S(r,f),

from which we get

\displaystyle m(r,a_{3};g)=S(r,f).

(2.7)

Now from (
2.1
) and (
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)\leq m\left(r,\frac{\mathscr{L}_{k}(f(z+c)-a_{3}(z+c))}{f-a_{3}}\right)+m(r,a_{3};f)+S(r,f)=S(r,f),

i.e.,

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)=S(r,f).

(2.8)

Noting that
T(r,f)=T(r,g)+S(r,f)
and so using Lemma
2.1
, (
2.6
)-(
2.8
), we get

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)-N\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)-N\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)-N\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)+O(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N\left(r,\frac{g-a_{3}}{f-a_{3}}\right)-N\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N(r,a_{3};f)-N(r,a_{3};g)+S(r,f)=T(r,f)-T(r,g)+S(r,f)=S(r,f),

i.e.,

\displaystyle m\Big(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\Big)=S(r,f).

(2.9)

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\delta(g)(g-\alpha)}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})}=\frac{\delta(g)}{g-a_{1}}+\frac{(a_{2}-\alpha)\delta(g)}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})},

where
\alpha
is an arbitrary small function of
f
and so

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{\delta(g)(g-\alpha)}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})}\right)=S(r,f).

(2.10)

Then from (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle m(r,\psi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{\delta(g)(g-a_{3})}{(g-a_{1})(g-a_{2})}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{f-a_{3}}{g-a_{3}}\right)+O(1)\leq S(r,f),

i.e.,
m(r,\psi)=S(r,f)
. Since
f
and
g
share
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
IM, we get
N(r,\psi)=S(r,f)
and so
T(r,\psi)=S(r,f)
.
We now consider following two cases.

Case 1.
Let
H_{nm}\equiv 0
. Then (
1.1
) and (
2.2
) give

n\big(\frac{f^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime}}{f-a_{1}}-\frac{f-a_{2}^{\prime}}{f-a_{2}}\big)\equiv m\big(\frac{g^{\prime}-a_{1}^{\prime}}{g-a_{1}}-\frac{g^{\prime}-a_{2}^{\prime}}{g-a_{2}}\big)
.
On integration, we get
\big(\frac{f-a_{1}}{f-a_{2}}\big)^{n}\equiv c_{1}\big(\frac{g-a_{1}}{g-a_{2}}\big)^{m}
,
c_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. If
n\neq m
, then by Mohon’ko lemma
[
26
]
, we get

n\;T(r,f)=m\;T(r,g)+S(r,f)
, which is impossible as
T(r,f)=T(r,g)+S(r,f)
. Hence
n=m
and so

\frac{f-a_{1}}{f-a_{2}}\equiv c_{2}\frac{g-a_{1}}{g-a_{2}}
,
where
c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. Since
f\not\equiv g
, we have
c_{2}\neq 1
and so
\frac{1-c_{2}}{c_{2}}\frac{f-a_{4}}{f-a_{2}}\equiv\frac{a_{2}-a_{1}}{g-a_{2}}
,
where
a_{4}=\frac{a_{1}-a_{2}c_{2}}{1-c_{2}}
such that
a_{4}\not\equiv a_{1},a_{2}
. Since
f
is a non-constant entire function and
f
,
g
share
a_{2}
IM, we get
N(r,a_{4};f)=S(r,f)
. Also we have
F+\frac{c_{2}}{1-c_{2}}=\frac{f-a_{4}}{a_{2}-a_{1}}
, where
F=\frac{f-a_{1}}{a_{2}-a_{1}}.
Then in view of the second fundamental theorem and using (
2.4
), we get

\displaystyle 2T(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,\infty,F)+\overline{N}(r,0,F)+\overline{N}(r,1;F)+\overline{N}\left(r,-\frac{c_{2}}{1-c_{2}};F\right)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,a_{1};f)+\overline{N}(r,a_{2};f)+S(r,f)=T(r,f)+S(r,f),

which is impossible.

Case 2.
Let
H_{nm}\not\equiv 0
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
z_{m,n}\in S_{(m,n)}(a_{1})\cup S_{(m,n)}(a_{2})
such that

satisfies the recurrence formula defined by (
3.6
).

Now we divide the following two cases.

Case 1.
Let
\psi_{k}=\alpha_{k,2k}\not\equiv 0
for
p=1,2,\ldots,k-1
. Now following the same procedure as done in Sub-case 1.1 in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we get a contradiction.

Case 2.
Let
\psi_{k}=\alpha_{k,2k}\equiv 0
. Now from (
3.6
), we get
\psi_{k-1}^{\prime}+\psi_{1}\psi_{k-1}\equiv 0
.
On integration, we have
\psi_{k-1}(z)=c_{0}e^{\xi(z)}
, where
\xi(z)=-\int_{0}^{z}\psi_{1}(z)\;\text{d}z
is a non-constant meromorphic function and
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
. We know that if
\xi(z)
has a pole at the point
z_{0}
, then
z_{0}
is an essential singularity of
e^{\xi(z)}
. Since
\psi_{k-1}
is a non-constant meromorphic function, we deduce that
\xi
is a non-constant entire function and so
\psi_{1}
is also non-constant entire function. Now if
\psi_{1}
is a polynomial, then
\psi_{i+1}
is also a polynomial for
i=1,2,\ldots,k-1
. Therefore we immediately get a contradiction. Hence
\psi_{1}
is a transcendental entire function.
Now from (
3.64
), we get
b_{k}\psi_{1}^{k}=\tilde{R}_{k-1}(\psi_{1})
.
By Clunie lemma (see
[
6
]
), we get
m(r,\psi_{1})=S(r,\psi_{1})
. Since
N(r,\psi_{1})=0
, we have
T(r,\psi_{1})=S(r,\psi_{1})
, which is impossible.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

We can easily conclude that Theorem
1.1
and Corollaries
1.1
-
1.3
are still valid for non-constant meromorphic function satisfying
N(r,f)=S(r,f)
.

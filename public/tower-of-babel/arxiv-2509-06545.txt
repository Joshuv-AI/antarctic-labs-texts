Introduction

This paper is dedicated to anisotropic versions of lower-dimensional
Minkowski content
and
\mathcal{S}
-content
. It provides a generalization of the notions of isotropic lower-dimensional Minkowski content and
\mathcal{S}
-content introduced in
[
RW
]
.

The so-called
C
-anisotropic volume function
is defined at the point
r\geq 0
as the Lebesgue measure of the set
E\oplus rC\coloneq\{e+rc;\;(e,c)\in E\times C\}
, that is

V_{E,C}(r):=\lambda^{n}(E\oplus rC)\quad\text{for $r\geq 0$},

where
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a fixed compact set and
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a fixed
convex body
the interior of which contains
0
, i.e. a compact convex set containing properly the origin. We can understand the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
as a measurement of the volume of the
C
-anisotropic tubular neighborhood of
E
, i.e. the set
E_{r,C}\coloneq E\oplus rC
, and so

V_{E,C}(r)=\lambda^{n}(E_{r,C})\quad\text{for $r\geq 0$.}

This function is studied in depth in
[
CLV
]
. The authors characterize the set of points of differentiability of
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
, show that its complement is at most countable, give an explicit formula for its one-sided derivatives, and describe the second derivative of
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
in the distributional sense. We will use their results to prove that for a given compact
E
, the
C
-anisotropic volume function
of Kneser type of order n
, i.e.

(1)

\displaystyle V_{E,C}(tb)-V_{E,C}(ta)\leq t^{n}\big(V_{E,C}(b)-V_{E,C}(a)\big)\quad\text{ for any $t\geq 1$ and $b>a>0$}.

Kneser proved in
[
Kn
]
that
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
satisfies (
1
) in the isotropic case, i.e. for
C=B(0,1)
. Later,
Stachó showed in
[
St
]
that
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is Kneser for any symmetric
C
and arbitrary
E
. But the results of
[
CLV
]
enable us to provide a proof for a general convex body
C
containing properly the origin.

The
s
-dimensional (isotropic) Minkowski of content of a compact set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

(2)

\mathcal{M}^{s}(E)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(E\oplus B(0,r)\big)}{\omega_{n-s}r^{n-s}}=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,B(0,1)}(r)}{\omega_{n-s}r^{n-s}}

if it exists, where
s\in[0,n]
and

\omega_{t}\coloneq\frac{\pi^{\frac{t}{2}}}{\Gamma(1+t)}\quad\text{for $t\geq 0$.}

Here,
\Gamma(\cdot)
stands for the standard
Gamma function.
If
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\omega_{t}
is nothing but the volume of the
t
-dimensional unit ball.

Similarly, if
s\in[0,n)
, then the
s
-dimensional (isotropic)
\mathcal{S}
-content of a compact set

is given by

(3)

\mathcal{S}^{s}(E)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,B(0,1)}^{\prime}(r)}{(n-s)\omega_{n-s}r^{n-s}},

if the limit exists.

The properties and behavior of (
2
) and (
3
) are studied in
[
RW
]
, where the majority of the proofs rely on the fact that the function
V_{E,B(0,1)}
is of Kneser type. Since we prove that
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is of Kneser type for a general convex body
C
of full dimension, we can naturally extend the results of
[
RW
]
: we will study the convergence and asymptotic behavior of

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{V_{E,C}(r)}{r^{n-s}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{V_{E,C}^{\prime}(r)}{(n-s)r^{n-s-1}}

as
r
tends to zero from the right, where
s\in[0,n)
.

The
s
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
E
is defined as the limit of the first item of (
4
) as
r
approaches zero from the right, if it exists, i.e.

(5)

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,C}(r)}{r^{n-s}}.

The
s
-dimensional
C
-anisotropic
\mathcal{S}
-content of
E
is given by

(6)

\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)\coloneq\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,C}^{\prime}(r)}{(n-s)r^{n-s-1}},

if the limit exists, where
s\in[0,n)
.

We prove inequalities between limits superior and inferior as

tends to zero from the right of the fractions in (
5

Sierpinki gasket
for arbitrary

. We will find that all four values are in this case different.

Later, we introduce the
C
-anisotropic Minkowski dimension and
\mathcal{S}
-dimension
and prove inequalities between them thereafter. The isotropic case (i.e. for

) is treated in the article

, which we draw inspiration from. We will also discuss the role of

in the definitions of the dimensions mentioned above.

At the end of this paper, we provide a proof of a theorem that generalizes Theorem 3.4 from
[
LV
]
. It is a standard-type of an existence-theorem in this branch of mathematics—it basically says that the existence of a countable system of a given compact

formed by pairwise disjoint compact sets that covers the set

-anisotropic Minkowski content of the whole set

provided that there is a probability measure absolutely continuous with respect to

.

•

The

We denote the

.

•

The the symbol

and

s
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of

, where

, (note that for

•

The

We abbreviate

1.2.
Geometric Measure Theory

In this subsection, we formulate some useful results from geometric measure theory.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
k\leq n
and
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-measurable.

The set
S
is said to
k
-rectifiable
provided that there exists a bounded set
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{k}
and a Lipschitz map
f\colon F\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(F)=S.

The set
S
is said to be
countably
\mathcal{H}^{k}
-rectifiable
if there exists a sequence
\{f_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
of Lipschitz functions from
\mathbb{R}^{k}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\mathcal{H}^{k}\left(E\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}f_{i}\left(\mathbb{R}^{k}\right)\right)=0.

In fact, the definition of
k
-rectifiability of the set
S
is equivalent to the fact that
S
is contained in a finite union of Lipschitz surfaces.

Remark 1.2
.

Given an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(S)<\infty
, there exists a countable family of pairwise disjoint compact subsets of
S
that covers
S
up to an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible set and each element of which is a subset of a Lipschitz
(n-1)
-graph with finite
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
measure (see
[
Si
, Lemma 11.1 and Remark 11.7]
).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
E\subseteq\Omega
be measurable. The set
E
is said to be a
set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
if the derivative of
\chi_{E}
in the sense of distributions, denoted by
D\chi_{E},
is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure with finite total variation in
\Omega
, i.e. it is an
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued Radon measure with finite total variation in
\Omega
satisfying

\int\limits_{E}\nabla f\dif^{n}=-\int\limits_{\Omega}f\textup{ d}D\chi_{E}\quad\text{for all $f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)$}.

The
perimeter of E in
\Omega
is given by

\Per(E;\Omega)\coloneq\|D\chi_{E}\|(\Omega),

i.e. the
total variation of
D\chi_{E}
in
\Omega
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We define the
upper
and
lower Lebesgue density of E at x
as

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big|_{E}\big(B(x,r)\big)}{\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,r)\big)}

and

\displaystyle\Theta_{n}(E,x)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big|_{E}\big(B(x,r)\big)}{\lambda^{n}\big(B(x,r)\big)}.

Further, let
\Theta_{n}(E,x)
be their common value (i.e.
\Theta_{n}(E,x)\coloneq\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}
provided that
\Theta_{n}(E,x)_{*}=\Theta_{n}(E,x)^{*}
) which is referred to as the
Lebesgue density of E at x
.

Denote

E^{t}\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\Theta_{n}(E,x)=t\}\quad\text{for $t\in[0,1]$}.

The
essential boundary of E
is given by

\partial_{*}E\coloneq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\left(E^{0}\cup E^{1}\right).

Let us also introduce the
reduced boundary of E
defined by

\partial^{*}E\coloneq\left\{x\in E^{1/2};\;\exists\nu_{E}(x)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:\;\frac{E-x}{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;y\cdot\nu_{E}(x)\leq 0\}\right\},

where we consider the
\mathcal{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-convergence of sets. Moreover, if
x\in\partial^{*}E,
we call
\nu_{E}(x)
the
outer unit normal to E at x.

Remark 1.5
.

Let us state the following facts (see
[
AFP
]
, section 3.3).

•

For a set
E
of finite perimeter in
\Omega
, we always have

\Per(E;\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial_{*}E\cap\Omega\right)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega\right).

•

We have
\Per(E;\Omega)=\Per(F;\Omega)
whenever
\lambda^{n}\big((E\Delta F)\cap\Omega\big)=0.

•

If
E
is a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega,
then
\Omega\setminus E
is also a set of finite perimeter in
\Omega
with
\nu_{E}=-\nu_{\Omega\setminus E}

\left(\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\Big|_{\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega}\right)
-a.e.,
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\partial^{*}E\cap\Omega)=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\left(\partial^{*}\left(\Omega\setminus E\right)\cap\Omega\right)
and
\textup{Per}(E;\Omega)=\textup{Per}\big(\Omega\setminus E;\Omega\big).

Let us now recall the Besicovitch covering theorem (see
[
EG
, subsection 1.5.2, Theorem 2]
):

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\rho\colon A\to(0,\infty)
be a bounded function. Then there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
n
and
S\subseteq A
at most countable such that

\displaystyle A\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in S}B\big(x,\rho(x)\big)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in S}\chi_{B(x,\rho(x))}\leq N.

1.3.
Anisotropic (Outer) Minkowski Content

In this subsection, we will summarize the basic facts about anisotropic (outer) Minkowski content from
[
CLL
]
and
[
LV
]
.

Definition 1.7
.

A
convex body
is a non-empty, compact and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The system of all convex bodies the interior of which contains the origin will be denoted by the symbol
\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
and the system of all compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{K}^{n}.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. The
support function of
C
is given by the formula

h_{C}(y)\coloneq\sup_{x\in C}x\cdot y\quad\text{for $y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

We also introduce the
polar function of C
given by

h^{\circ}_{C}(x)\coloneq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
h_{C}(y)\leq 1\end{subarray}}x\cdot y\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

For
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
E\neq\emptyset
, we define the
C
-anisotropic distance function to the set
E
by

\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\coloneq\underset{y\in E}{\textup{inf}}\textit{ h}^{\circ}_{C}(x-y)\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.$}

Remark 1.8
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

•

We define the
polar of
C
by
C^{\circ}\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;h_{C}(x)\leq 1\}
. We see that
h^{\circ}_{C}=h_{C^{\circ}}
and
(C^{\circ})^{\circ}=C
.

•

The function
x\mapsto h_{C}(x)
is sublinear, hence convex, and Lipschitz with the property
h_{-C}(x)=h_{C}(-x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is also true that
h_{aC}=ah_{C}
for any
a>0
.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be with
0\in Q
,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open and
\varepsilon>0
. We define the
(\varepsilon,Q)
-
anisotropic outer Minkowski content (in
\Omega
)
as a functional defined on
\lambda^{n}
-measurable subsets
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
by the formula

\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((E\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon Q\big)\cap(\Omega\setminus E)\Big).

Further, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{SM}_{\varepsilon,Q}(E;\Omega).

The former formula defines the
lower
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of
E
(in
\Omega
)
, the latter the
upper
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content of
E
(in
\Omega
)
. If
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}=\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)^{*}
, we define the
Q
-anisotropic outer Minkowski content (in
\Omega
)
by the formula
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)_{*}
. If we write
\mathcal{SM}_{Q}(E;\Omega)
, we implicitly mean that the limit exists.

Let us define the so-called
C
-anisotropic Minkowski content, which is useful for
\lambda^{n}
-negligible sets (unlike
\mathcal{SM}_{C}
):

Definition 1.10
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be measurable and
Q\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in Q
. Putting

\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,Q}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\frac{1}{2{\varepsilon}}\lambda^{n}\Big(\big((S\cap\Omega)\oplus\varepsilon Q\big)\cap\Omega\Big),\quad\text{$\varepsilon>0$},

we define the
(\varepsilon,Q)
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
S
in
\Omega
.
We define the
lower
and
upper
Q
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
S
in
\Omega
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,Q}(S;\Omega)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon,Q}(S;\Omega).

If
\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)_{*}=\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)^{*}
, we define the
Q
-anisotropic Minkowski content of
S
in
\Omega
by
\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)\coloneq\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)_{*}
. If we write
\mathcal{M}_{Q}(S;\Omega)
, we implicitly mean that the limit exists.

In this work, we provide a generalization of the notions introduced in Definitions
1.9
and
1.10
for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Namely, we replace the
\varepsilon
in the denominators by
\varepsilon^{n-s}
, where
s\in[0,n].
Roughly speaking, the number
s
reflects in some sense the “dimension” of the set studied if the limit is neither zero nor infinity.

The meaning of the word
isotropic
is “having the same properties in all directions”, while the word
anisotropic
is its opposite, i.e. “not having the same properties in all directions” (see
the etymology of (an)isotropic
).

Definition 1.11
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. For
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
measurable we define the
C
-anisotropic perimeter of
E
in
\Omega
by

\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\Omega)\coloneq\begin{cases}{\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}}h_{C}\left(\nu_{E}\right)\difm^{n-1},&\text{ if $\Per(E;\Omega)<\infty$},\\
+\infty,&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We have a sufficient condition for the existence of the limit
\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

Theorem 1.12

(
[
LV
, Theorem 4.3]
)
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
and
E
be a closed subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\partial E
is a countably
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable and bounded set and such that there exists
\gamma>0
and a probability measure
\eta
absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
satisfying

\forall r>0\;\forall x\in\partial E:\eta\big(B(x,r)\big)\geq\gamma r^{n-1}.

Then we have

\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})+\int\limits_{E^{0}\cap\partial E}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E})\big)\difm^{n-1}.

Let us introduce the
anisotropic isoperimetric inequality
(see
[
Fo
]
, section 4).

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and of finite Lebesgue measure, and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. Then

\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E;\mathbb{R}^{n})\geq n\lambda^{n}(C)^{\frac{1}{n}}\lambda^{n}(E)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}.

Let us first introduce the notion of

This notion is further developed in
[

]
and
[

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E,C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be arbitrary subsets and

If

, we set

E_{r,C}\coloneq E\oplus rC.

The set

is called the

-anisotropic tubular neighbourhood of E.
Moreover, if
C
is convex, we define for

E^{\prime}_{r,C}\coloneq\bigcup_{0<s<r}(E\oplus sC).

Further, we set

V_{E,C}(r)\coloneq\mathcal{H}^{n}(E_{r,C}).

We will refer to the function
V_{E,C}\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
as the
C-anisotropic volume function of E
in the sequel.

In our setting, the set
E
will be compact most of the time and
C
will be a convex body of full dimension with
0\in\textup{int}(C)
. In these cases, the sets
\partial E_{r,C}
and
\partial E^{\prime}_{r,C}
are finite unions of Lipschitz surfaces
[
CLV
, Theorem 3.1]
for all
r>0
.

We will summarize the properties of the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
. The non-obvious ones are proven in
[
CLV
, section 5]
:

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. It is plain to see that the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is increasing and continuous from
[0,\infty)
to
[0,\infty)
(as follows from the continuity of the measure
\lambda^{n}
). Moreover, the left and right hand side derivatives of
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
, denoted by
V_{E,C}^{-}(\cdot)
and
V_{E,C}^{+}(\cdot)
respectively, exist at each point of
(0,\infty)
;
V_{E,C}^{-}(\cdot)\geq V_{E,C}^{+}(\cdot)
and are continuous from the left and from the right, respectively. Finally, they are given by the formulæ

V_{E,C}^{-}(r)=\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}(\nu_{E_{r,C}})\difm^{n-1}+\int\limits_{(E_{r,C})^{1}\cap\partial(E^{\prime}_{r,C})}\big(h_{C}(\nu_{E^{\prime}_{r,C}})+h_{C}(-\nu_{E^{\prime}_{r,C}})\big)\difm^{n-1}

and

V_{E,C}^{+}(r)=\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}(\nu_{E_{r,C}})\difm^{n-1}.

In particular, the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is first of all of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
on
(0,\infty)\setminus J_{E,C}
, where
J_{E,C}
is an at most countable set given by

J_{E,C}\coloneq\left\{r>0;\;\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\big((E_{r,C})^{1}\cap\partial(E^{\prime}_{r,C})\big)>0\right\},

and, secondly, is differentiable at a point
r>0
if and only if
r\notin J_{E,C}
. In this case,

(7)

(V_{E,C})^{\prime}(r)=\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}\left(\nu_{E_{r,C}}\right)\difm^{n-1}=\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

From the aforementioned properties, it easily follows that
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is
locally absolutely continuous on
[0,\infty)
, that is, absolutely continuous on each compact interval contained in
[0,\infty)
(see
[
He
]
, Theorem 17 and Corollary 20). Formula (
7
) inspires us to introduce the following definition:

Definition 2.2
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
and
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. We define

S_{E,C}(r)\coloneq\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n}),\;r>0.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

•

We always have
E_{r,C}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)\leq r\}
and
E^{\prime}_{r,C}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\;\textup{dist}_{C}(x,E)<r\}
for any
r>0
and nonempty
E
.

•

We have
S_{E,C}(r)=V_{E,C}^{+}(r)
for any
r>0
. In particular, the function
S_{E,C}(\cdot)
is continuous from the right.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
,
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
r>0
, we have

\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n})=\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

In particular,

\mathcal{S}_{E,C}(r)=\mathcal{SM}_{C}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof.

Since the set
E_{r,C}
satisfies all assumptions of Theorem
1.12
, we deduce that

\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n})=\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n})+\int\limits_{(E_{r,C})^{0}\cap\partial(E_{r,C})}\big(h_{C^{\prime}}(\nu_{E_{r,C}})+h_{C^{\prime}}(-\nu_{E_{r,C}})\big)\difm^{n-1}.

We will show that
(E_{r,C})^{0}\cap\partial(E_{r,C})=\emptyset,
which in turn implies

\mathcal{SM}_{C^{\prime}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n})=\textup{Per}_{h_{C^{\prime}}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Given
x_{0}\in(E_{r,C})^{0}\cap\partial(E_{r,C})
, we have on the one hand that there exists
y\in E
so that

x_{0}\in y+rC

and, on the other hand,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big(E_{r,C}\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)\big)}{\varepsilon^{n}}=0,

whence

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\big((y+rC)\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)\big)}{\varepsilon^{n}}=0,

which is a contradiction since each point of the convex body
y+rC
cannot have density zero.
∎

Let us introduce
s
-dimensional anisotropic Minkowski content
. Let us point out that it is a natural extension of Definition
1.10
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
,

and

. Putting

and

-anisotropic Minkowski content of

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\mathcal{M}^{s}_{r,C}(E).

Whenever the two quantities are equal, that is,
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}=\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}

s

C

The following lemma shows that the previous definition is of interest only for

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{n}_{C}(E)=\lambda^{n}(E)

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{0}_{C}(E)=\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\lambda^{n}(C).

Proof.

The first assertion follows easily, as

\mathcal{M}^{n}_{C}(E)_{*}=\mathcal{M}^{n}_{C}(E)^{*}=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}V_{E,C}(r)=V_{E,C}(0)=\lambda^{n}(E).

To prove the second part, suppose first that
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)=k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We see that in this case
V_{E,C}(r)=kr^{n}\lambda^{n}(C)
for small enough

. Hence

\mathcal{M}^{0}_{C}(E)_{*}=\mathcal{M}^{0}_{C}(E)^{*}=\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,C}(r)}{r^{n}}=k\lambda^{n}(C)=\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)\lambda^{n}(C).

If, on the other hand,
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E)=\infty
, we can find for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
a subset
E_{k}\subseteq E
with
\mathcal{H}^{0}(E_{k})=k.
But then

\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,C}(r)}{r^{n}}\geq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E_{k},C}(r)}{r^{n}}=k\lambda^{n}(C)\text{ for each $k\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Hence
\mathcal{M}^{0}_{C}(E)_{*}=\mathcal{M}^{0}_{C}(E)^{*}=\infty.

∎

3.
Minkowski Dimension and
\mathcal{S}
-Dimension

Let us start this section with the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The function
f
is a
Kneser function
(or
of Kneser type
, respectively)
of order
n
provided that it satisfies

f(tb)-f(ta)\leq t^{n}\big(f(b)-f(a)\big)\quad\text{for any $0<a\leq b$ and any $t\geq 1$.}

Kneser functions have the following properties (cf.
[
St
]
, Lemma 2). Interestingly, they share these properties with
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Any Kneser function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is locally absolutely continuous;
f^{\prime}
exists everywhere, safe for an at most countable set
J_{f}\subseteq(0,\infty)
; the left and right hand side derivatives of
f
(denoted by
f^{-}
and
f^{+}
, respectively) exist at each point of
(0,\infty)
;
f^{-}\geq f^{+}
; and
f^{-}
and
f^{+}
are continuous from the left and from the right, respectively.

A natural question arises: Is the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
of Kneser type of order
n
for any convex body of full dimension
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
0\in\textup{int}(C)
, and any compact set
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
? A well-known result of Kneser (see
[
Kn
]
) gives an affirmative answer to this question for
C=B(0,1)
. Few years later, Stachó showed that the volume function is of Kneser type for any
C
that is open, bounded, central symmetric, and convex (see
[
St
]
).

We will give a proof for a general
C
. But first, let us present Corollary 5.5 from
[
CLV
]
:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
,
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
, and set

\kappa_{E,C}(r)\coloneq\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}\left(\nu_{E_{r,C}}\right)\difm^{n-1},\;r>0.

Then for any pair
r_{0},r_{1}\in(0,\infty)\setminus J_{E,C}
with
r_{0}\leq r_{1}
we have
\kappa_{E,C}(r_{0})\geq\kappa_{E,C}(r_{1})
. Moreover, if
E\neq\emptyset
, we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\kappa_{E,C}(r)=n\lambda^{n}(C).

It is interesting that the limit at infinity is the same for all non-empty compacta
E
. It is quite natural to ask what the limit
\lim_{r\to 0_{+}}\kappa_{E,C}(r)
is, and we will compute it at the end of this chapter. But let us now return to our study of
V_{E,C}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
. Then the function
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is of Kneser type of order
n
for any
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
b>a>0
. We already know that
V_{E,C}(\cdot)
is locally absolutely continuous on the interval
[0,\infty)
. Hence, for any
t\geq 1
we have

\begin{split}V_{E,C}(tb)-V_{E,C}(ta)&=\int\limits_{ta}^{tb}V_{E,C}^{\prime}(r)\dif^{1}(r)\\
&=\int\limits_{ta}^{tb}\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}\left(\nu_{E_{r,C}}(x)\right)\difm^{n-1}(x)\dif^{1}(r)\\
&=\int\limits_{ta}^{tb}r^{n-1}\left(\frac{1}{r^{n-1}}\int\limits_{\partial^{*}(E_{r,C})}h_{C}\left(\nu_{E_{r,C}}(x)\right)\difm^{n-1}(x)\right)\dif^{1}(r)\\
&=\int\limits_{ta}^{tb}r^{n-1}\kappa_{E,C}(r)\dif^{1}(r)=\int\limits_{a}^{b}(t\rho)^{n-1}\kappa_{E,C}(t\rho)t\dif^{1}(\rho)\\
&=t^{n}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\rho^{n-1}\kappa_{E,C}(t\rho)\dif^{1}(\rho)\\
&\leq t^{n}\int\limits_{a}^{b}\rho^{n-1}\kappa_{E,C}(\rho)\dif^{1}(\rho)=t^{n}\big(V_{E,C}(b)-V_{E,C}(a)\big),\end{split}

where the inequality is a straightforward consequence of Lemma
3.3
.
∎

Observe that if
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
with zero Lebesgue measure and

and similarly

Remark 3.6
.

First, note that the notion of

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
f\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Kneser function of order
n
and let
h\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuously differentiable function with
h(0)=0
. Assume that
h^{\prime}
is non-zero in some neighborhood on the right of
0
. Let

\displaystyle S_{*}\coloneq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f^{+}(r)}{h^{\prime}(r)}

and

\displaystyle S^{*}\coloneq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f^{+}(r)}{h^{\prime}(r)}.

Then

(8)

S_{*}\leq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{h(r)}\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{h(r)}\leq S^{*}.

In particular, if
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
,
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
, and
s\in[0,n]
, we get the following chain of inequalities:

\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\leq\mathcal{SM}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}\leq\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}.

If, moreover,
\lambda^{n}(E)=0
, then

\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\leq\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\leq\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}\leq\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}.

Proof.

First, observe that the case
s=n
is trivial. So, we will suppose that
s<n
.

We claim that for each
r>0
there exist
0<t_{1}(r),t_{2}(r)<r
so that

(9)

\frac{f^{+}\big(t_{1}(r)\big)}{h^{\prime}\big(t_{1}(r)\big)}\leq\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{h(r)}\leq\frac{f^{+}\big(t_{2}(r)\big)}{h^{\prime}\big(t_{2}(r)\big)}.

To prove this, fix a positive number
r
and define

\Phi(t)\coloneq\big(f(r)-f(0)\big)h(t)-h(r)f(t),\;t\in[0,r].

The function
\Phi(\cdot)
is absolutely continuous on the interval
[0,r]
and
\Phi(r)=\Phi(0).
It follows

\int\limits_{0}^{r}\Phi^{\prime}(t)\dif^{1}(t)=\Phi(r)-\Phi(0)=0.

Consequently, either
\Phi^{\prime}=0

\lambda^{1}
-a.e. in
(0,r)
, or there exist
t_{1}(r),t_{2}(r)
in
(0,r)
with
\Phi^{\prime}\big(t_{1}(r)\big)>0>\Phi^{\prime}\big(t_{2}(r)\big)
, from which we conclude (
9
).

Now

\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{h(r)}\leq\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f^{+}\big(t_{2}(r)\big)}{h^{\prime}\big(t_{2}(r)\big)}\leq S^{*},

and similarly for the other inequality.

To prove the second part, set
h(t)\coloneq t^{n-s}
and
f(t)\coloneq V_{E,C}(t)
for

. We obtain

\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}=\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{S_{E,C}(r)}{(n-s)r^{n-s-1}}\leq\liminf_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{V_{E,C}(r)-V_{E,C}(0)}{r^{n-s}}=\mathcal{SM}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}.

The other inequality can be shown by analogy.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a Kneser function of order
n
. Then for any
s\in[0,n)
we have

\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{r^{n-s}}\geq\frac{n-s}{n}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f^{+}(r)}{(n-s)r^{n-s-1}}.

In particular, if
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
, then

\mathcal{SM}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}\geq\frac{n-s}{n}\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}.

If, in addition,
\lambda^{n}(E)=0
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}\geq\frac{n-s}{n}\mathcal{S}^{s}_{C}(E)^{*}.

Consequently,
\dim_{\mathcal{M}}(E)^{*}=\dim^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(E)^{*}
provided that
\lambda^{n}(E)=0
, and hence the value of
\dim^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(E)^{*}
does not depend on the choice of
C
.

Proof.

We find a sequence
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\in\left(J_{f}\right)^{\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{i}\searrow 0
as
i\to\infty
(due to Lemma
3.2
) so that

a\coloneq\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{i})}{(n-s)r_{i}^{n-s-1}}=\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f^{+}(r)}{(n-s)r^{n-s-1}}.

Fix
i\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
r\in[r_{i+1},r_{i}]\cap J_{f}
we have

\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-1}}\leq\frac{f^{+}(r)}{r^{n-1}}\leq\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{i+1})}{r_{i+1}^{n-1}}

(see
[
St
]
, Theorem 1). Now (using Lemma
3.2
)

(11)

\begin{split}f(r_{i})-f(0)&=\int\limits_{0}^{r_{i}}f^{\prime}\dif^{1}=\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\;\int\limits_{r_{j+1}}^{r_{j}}f^{\prime}\dif^{1}\\
&\geq\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\;\int\limits_{r_{j+1}}^{r_{j}}f^{\prime}(r_{j})\frac{r^{n-1}}{r_{j}^{n-1}}\dif^{1}(r)=\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}f^{\prime}(r_{j})\frac{r_{j}^{n}-r_{j+1}^{n}}{nr_{j}^{n-1}}\\
&=\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{j})}{(n-s)r_{j}^{n-s-1}}\frac{n-s}{n}\frac{r_{j}^{n}-r_{j+1}^{n}}{r_{j}^{s}}\\
&\geq\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{j})}{(n-s)r_{j}^{n-s-1}}\frac{n-s}{n}\left(r_{j}^{n-s}-r_{j+1}^{n-s}\right).\end{split}

If
b<a
, then there exists
j_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
j\geq j_{0}
we have

\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{j})}{(n-s)r_{j}^{n-s-1}}\geq b.

Thus, for
i\geq j_{0}
we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{f(r_{i})-f(0)}{r_{i}^{n-s}}&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{dr})}}}}{{\geq}}}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(r_{j})}{(n-s)r_{j}^{n-s-1}}\frac{n-s}{n}\frac{r_{j}^{n-s}-r_{j+1}^{n-s}}{r_{i}^{n-s}}\right)\\
&\geq b\frac{n-s}{n}\sum_{j=i}^{\infty}\frac{r_{j}^{n-s}-r_{j+1}^{n-s}}{r_{i}^{n-s}}=b\frac{n-s}{n}\left(1-\frac{\lim_{j\to\infty}r_{j}^{n-s}}{r_{i}^{n-s}}\right)=b\frac{n-s}{n}.\end{split}

Hence

\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{f(r)-f(0)}{r^{n-s}}\geq b\frac{n-s}{n}.

Since
b<a
was arbitrary, we get the desired inequality.

The second part of the statement is now clear: set simply
f\coloneq V_{E,C}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
E\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}^{n}_{0}
. Then for any
s\in[0,n]
we have

c(C,n,s)\mathcal{S}_{C}^{s\frac{n-1}{n}}(E)_{*}\geq\big(\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E)_{*}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{n}},

where
c(C,n,s)
is a positive constant depending only on
C
,
n
and
s
(and not on
E
). Consequently,
\dim^{C}_{\mathcal{S}}(E)_{*}\geq\frac{n-1}{n}\dim_{\mathcal{M}}(E)_{*}
provided that
\lambda^{n}(E)=0
.

Proof.

Let
r>0
. By the anisotropic isoperimetric inequality (Theorem
1.13
) we have

(12)

n\lambda^{n}(C)^{\frac{1}{n}}V_{E,C}(r)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\textup{Per}_{h_{C}}(E_{r,C};\mathbb{R}^{n})=S_{E,C}(r).

Fix
s\in[0,n]
. For brevity, define
t\coloneq\frac{n-1}{n}s
. If
\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}=\infty
, there is nothing to prove. So, let us assume
\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}<\infty
. The inequality in (
12
) gives us

\left(\frac{V_{E,C}(r)}{r^{n-s}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\frac{n-t}{n\lambda^{n}(C)^{\frac{1}{n}}}\frac{S_{E,C}(r)}{(n-t)r^{n-t-1}}.

Set

c(C,n,s)\coloneq\frac{n-t}{n\lambda^{n}(C)^{\frac{1}{n}}}.

We find a sequence
\{r_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
of positive real numbers converging to zero such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{S_{E,C}(r_{i})}{(n-t)r_{i}^{n-t-1}}=\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}.

For any real number
a>\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}
there exists
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for each
i\geq i_{0}
we have

\frac{S_{E,C}(r_{i})}{(n-t)r_{i}^{n-t-1}}\leq a.

Hence also

\left(\frac{V_{E,C}(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-s}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq c(C,n,s)a.

Thus

\big(\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq\liminf_{i\to\infty}\left(\frac{V_{E,C}(r_{i})}{r_{i}^{n-s}}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq c(C,n,s)a

Since
a>\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}
was arbitrary, we obtain exactly

\big(\mathcal{M}^{s}_{C}(E)_{*}\big)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\leq c(C,n,s)\mathcal{S}^{t}_{C}(E)_{*}=c(C,n,s)\mathcal{S}_{C}^{s\frac{n-1}{n}}(E)_{*},

which completes the proof.
∎

(and from the continuity of
\eta
). Define for
r\in(0,r_{\varepsilon})

E_{\varepsilon,r}\coloneq\left\{x\in E;\textup{dist}\left(x,\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\right)\geq 2ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right\},

where
r_{\varepsilon}<a^{-1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{n}}
and
\eta\Big((E\oplus r_{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}C)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\Big)<\varepsilon
as follows, again, from the continuity of
\eta
.

We can cover the compact set
E_{\varepsilon,r}
by a family of balls
\left\{B(i,\varepsilon,r)\right\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}
, where
B(i,\varepsilon,r)\coloneq B\left(x_{i},ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)
and
x_{i}\in E_{\varepsilon,r}
, for all
i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}
, such that for some
N\in\mathbb{N}

(15)

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\eta\big(B(i,\varepsilon,r)\big)\leq N\eta\left(\left(E\oplus r\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}C\right)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\right).\end{split}

The existence of such a family of balls can be justified as follows: We apply Theorem
1.6
on the compact set
E_{\varepsilon,r}
and the constant function
x\mapsto ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}
on
E_{\varepsilon,r}
and we get some
N\in\mathbb{N}
and a family
\left\{B(i,\varepsilon,r)\right\}_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}
consisting of balls centered at some point of
E_{\varepsilon,r}
such that

(16)

\displaystyle E_{\varepsilon,r}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}B(i,\varepsilon,r)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\chi_{B(i,\varepsilon,r)}\leq N.

Observe that the definition of the set
E_{\varepsilon,r}
and the inclusion
B(0,a)\subseteq C
ensure

\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}B(i,\varepsilon,r)\subseteq\left(E_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus B\left(0,ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\right)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\subseteq\left(E\oplus r\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}C\right)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}.

By integrating the second item of (
16
) with respect to the measure
\eta
over the set
\left(E\oplus r\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}C\right)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}
we obtain exactly (
15
).

By the assumption on
\eta
we get

\begin{split}\gamma a^{s}r^{s}\varepsilon^{\frac{s}{n}}\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon,r})&=\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\gamma\left(ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{s}\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\eta\Big(B\left(x_{i},ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\Big)\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{bez})}}}}{{\leq}}}N\eta\Big(\left(E\oplus r\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}C\right)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\Big)\leq N\varepsilon.\end{split}

Rearrangement of the terms gives us the following estimate

(17)

\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon,r})\leq\frac{N\varepsilon^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}{\gamma a^{s}r^{s}}.

Observe that since
C\subseteq B(0,b)
,

\begin{split}E_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus\left(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)rC&\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}B\left(x_{i},ar\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\oplus B\Big(0,b\left(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)r\Big)\\
&\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}B\Big(x_{i},br\left(1+2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\Big).\end{split}

Therefore,

(18)

\begin{split}\lambda^{n}\Big(E_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus\left(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)rC\Big)&\leq\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon,r}}\lambda^{n}\Big(B\Big(x_{i},br\left(1+2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)\Big)\Big)\\
&=\mathcal{H}^{0}(I_{\varepsilon,r})\omega_{n}b^{n}r^{n}\left(1+2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{n}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{haus})}}}}{{\leq}}}\omega_{n}b^{n}r^{n}\left(1+2\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)^{n}\frac{N\varepsilon^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}{\gamma a^{s}r^{s}}\\
&\leq r^{n-s}\frac{N\omega_{n}b^{n}3^{n}\varepsilon^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}{\gamma a^{s}}.\end{split}

We have the following chain of inclusions:

(19)

\begin{split}E\oplus rC&\subseteq\left(\left(E\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\right)\oplus rC\right)\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\left(E_{k}\oplus rC\right)\\
&\subseteq\Big(E_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus\left(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)rC\Big)\cup\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}\left(E_{k}\oplus rC\right).\end{split}

Finally,

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E)^{*}&=\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}(E\oplus rC)}{r^{n-s}}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{incl})}}}}{{\leq}}}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\Big(E_{\varepsilon,r}\oplus\left(1+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)rC\Big)}{r^{n-s}}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\limsup_{r\to 0_{+}}\frac{\lambda^{n}\left(E_{k}\oplus rC\right)}{r^{n-s}}\\
&\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\makebox[0.0pt]{\mbox{\tiny(\ref{kik})}}}}{{\leq}}}\frac{N\omega_{n}b^{n}3^{n}\varepsilon^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}{\gamma a^{s}}+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E_{k})\leq\frac{N\omega_{n}b^{n}3^{n}\varepsilon^{\frac{n-s}{n}}}{\gamma a^{s}}+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E_{k}).\end{split}

By sending
\varepsilon\searrow 0
, we get the desired inequality

\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E)^{*}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{M}_{C}^{s}(E_{k}),

which completes the proof.∎

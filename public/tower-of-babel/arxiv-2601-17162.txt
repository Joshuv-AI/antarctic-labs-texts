Varying Newton constant,entropy and the black hole evaporation law
Julia Haba, Zbigniew Haba

Abstract
In Einstein equations we represent the energy-momentum tensor as the one ( T μ ​ ν T^{\mu\nu} ) of an ideal fluid plus the cosmological term. We consider time-dependent Newton “constant” G G , the cosmological term Λ \Lambda and non-conserved T μ ​ ν T^{\mu\nu} . The Bianchi identity imposes a relation between the energy-momentum (non)conservation and the variation of G G and Λ \Lambda . If the energy-momentum T μ ​ ν T^{\mu\nu} is conserved then both the Newton “constant” G G and the cosmological term Λ \Lambda either do not change or both must depend on time. If the energy-momentum T μ ​ ν T^{\mu\nu} is not conserved then the Bianchi identity implies a relation between the energy-momentum and a variation in time of either G G or Λ \Lambda (or both). We apply thermodynamics in order to express the non-conservation of the energy-momentum of an ideal fluid by entropy and relate the time variations of G G and Λ \Lambda to a change of entropy. Using the relation between a varying Newton constant G and the black hole entropy we derive a modified formula for the Schwarzschild black hole evaporation (a slower evaporation). Its life time is 9 5 \frac{9}{5} times larger than the one for a constant G G . The average black hole’s density remains constant when the black hole’s radius shrinks to zero .

5
Summary and outlook

We have shown that the Bianchi identity of
classical Einstein equations admits a violation of the ideal fluid
energy-momentum conservation if the Newton “constant” G and the
cosmological term depend on time. As a generalization of the first
and the second laws of thermodynamics the covariant
energy-momentum divergence can be expressed by the covariant
divergence of the entropy density. Then, the entropy density
enters the Bianchi identity. In this paper we have applied the
formula for the change of entropy during the black hole
evaporation to determine the time-dependence of
G
in the Bianchi
identity. As a consequence of a growing
G
the black hole
evaporation is slower and the average black hole density is
constant in contradistinction to the one for a fixed
G
(this
could be subject to astronomical observations) .
The time-dependence of
G
is unavoidable if
we wish to describe the non-conserved energy-momentum of an
evaporating black hole by Einstein equations (2). The increasing

G
could have a physical stabilizing origin preventing the
appearance of an infinite density when the black hole’s radius
tends to zero. Another problem deserving a study concerns the
entropy of an expanding universe with a cosmological horizon. For
such a system the varying cosmological constant in the Bianchi
identity should be relevant.

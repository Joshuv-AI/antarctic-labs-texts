Soluble immune checkpoints as diagnostic biomarkers in bladder cancer
Authors: Anna Andrzejczak, Wojciech Krajewski, Emilia Jaskuła, Joanna Chorbińska, Anna Tomkiewicz, Krystian Sarat, Robert Mroczek, Bartosz Małkiewicz, Tomasz Szydełko, Lidia Karabon
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBladder cancer (BC) is a common malignancy with high recurrence and substantial monitoring costs. Limitations of current diagnostic tools: cystoscopy is invasive and expensive, urine cytology lacks sensitivity and existing urine biomarkers cannot replace cystoscopy, underscoring the need for improved non-invasive methods. Immune checkpoints (ICs) regulate immune activity, and newer ICs: T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain 3 (TIM-3), galectin-9 (Gal-9), B- and T-lymphocyte attenuator (BTLA), herpesvirus entry mediator (HVEM), cluster of differentiation 160 (CD160), and lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG-3), are involved in cancer immune evasion. This study demonstrated that levels of soluble ICs (sICs) are markedly elevated in patients with early-stage BC. Serum levels of sTIM-3, sGal-9, sBTLA, sHVEM, sCD160, and sLAG-3 were quantified in BC and controls. sTIM-3, sGal-9, and sBTLA were significantly elevated in BC, while their concentrations didn’t vary by tumor stage or grade, supporting diagnostic rather than prognostic utility. Whereas sHVEM, sCD160, and sLAG-3 showed no diagnostic value. Age, sex, and body mass index (BMI) had minimal influence on sIC levels. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses showed strong performance: sGal-9 was the best classifier (area under the curve (AUC) = 0.98; 91% sensitivity; 100% specificity), followed by sTIM-3 (AUC = 0.91) and sBTLA (AUC = 0.78). sHVEM, sCD160, sLAG-3 performed poorly. A multivariate model incorporating sGal-9, sTIM-3, and sBTLA achieved a cross-validated AUC = 0.982, with sGal-9 remaining independently predictive. Correlation analyses revealed two clusters: sGal-9/sTIM-3/sBTLA and sHVEM/sCD160, that relationships align with known receptor-ligand biology and may reflect shedding during T-cell exhaustion. Overall, sGal-9, supported by sTIM-3 and sBTLA, constitutes promising serum biomarker panel associated with early-stage BC, however, as these markers are not disease-specific and may be affected by inflammatory or other pathological conditions, further validation is required.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40364-026-00918-y.Keywords: Bladder cancer, Immune checkpoints, Soluble isoforms, Disease risk

TO THE EDITOR,

Bladder cancer (BC) is a common malignancy with high recurrence rates and substantial management costs due to surgery, lifelong surveillance, and ongoing treatment. Delayed diagnosis often leads to advanced disease and increased mortality [1]. Current diagnostic approaches have clear limitations. Cystoscopy, the clinical gold standard, is invasive, uncomfortable, and costly. Urine cytology, though non-invasive, lacks sensitivity, especially for low-grade tumors. Repeated cystoscopies also impose psychological stress on patients and financial strain on healthcare systems [2]. Urine biomarkers such as nuclear matrix protein 22 (NMP22), bladder tumor antigen (BTA), or UroVysion test offer some support but cannot replace cystoscopy [3], underscoring the need for improved diagnostic strategies.

Immune checkpoints (ICs) are regulatory molecules that maintain self-tolerance and modulate immune responses. In addition to well-known ICs such as cytotoxic T-lymphocyte associated protein 4 (CTLA-4) and programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/ programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), a new generation of ICs: T-cell immunoglobulin and mucin domain 3 (TIM-3), galectin-9 (Gal-9), B- and T-lymphocyte attenuator (BTLA), herpesvirus entry mediator (HVEM), cluster of differentiation 160 (CD160), and lymphocyte-activation gene 3 (LAG-3), plays important roles in cancer immune evasion [4–6].

This work demonstrates that soluble immune checkpoints (sICs) are significantly elevated in early-stage BC. Therefore, we propose that their soluble forms may represent minimally invasive biomarkers for early-stage BC, although further validation in independent and clinically diverse patient cohorts is required.

We quantified sTIM-3, sGal-9, sBTLA, sHVEM, sCD160, and sLAG-3 in BCs’ and healthy controls’ (HC) sera (supplementary material: patients characteristics (Table S1); methods; serum levels of soluble immune checkpoint molecules (Table S2). sTIM-3, sGal-9, and sBTLA were elevated ~ 1.6-, 4.4- and 2-fold in BC, respectively (all p < 0.0001) (Fig. 1). Demographic factors minimally affected sIC levels. Body mass index (BMI) showed no meaningful associations. Although age correlated with sTIM-3, sGal-9, and sBTLA in the pooled cohort, these correlations disappeared when BC and HC were analyzed separately, suggesting that the relationships reflected disease status rather than aging (supplementary materials). Gender had no influence in patients. HC women showed slightly lower sGal-9, a difference unlikely to affect clinical interpretation (Figure S1). Overall, circulating sIC profiles were largely independent of age, sex, and BMI.

Next, we examined whether marker levels tracked with disease severity. Across tumor stage (pTa-pT2) and grade (G1-G3), sTIM-3, sGal-9, and sBTLA remained consistently elevated but did not vary with progression (Fig. 1). Thus, these molecules function as diagnostic rather than prognostic markers, discriminating BC from healthy status but not distinguishing within-tumor heterogeneity. sHVEM, sCD160, and sLAG-3 lacked discriminatory ability.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis confirmed strong diagnostic performance. sGal-9 was the best single biomarker (area under the curve (AUC) = 0.98, 91% sensitivity, 100% specificity). Including gender as a covariate slightly improved performance (AUC = 0.994 vs. 0.984, p = 0.039; threshold 14.2 pg/mL), though its clinical simplicity supports using unadjusted sGal-9. sTIM-3 also performed well (AUC = 0.91; sensitivity 77%; specificity 96%; threshold 1332 pg/mL). sBTLA showed moderate accuracy (AUC = 0.78; sensitivity 83%; specificity 65%; threshold 134.4 pg/mL). By contrast, sLAG-3 (AUC ≈ 0.66), sHVEM, and sCD160 (AUC ≤ 0.55) lacked diagnostic value. Overall, sGal-9 emerged as the strongest candidate, with sTIM-3 and sBTLA serving as complementary markers. While the panel shows diagnostic potential in BC, these markers may reflect broader immune dysregulation, therefore, validation in additional BC cohorts and other tumors is required.

A multivariate logistic regression model incorporating log-transformed sGal-9, sTIM-3, and sBTLA achieved excellent performance (5-fold cross-validated AUC = 0.982). Within this model, only sGal-9 remained independently predictive (β = 6.7 ± 2.5; p = 0.007), while sTIM-3 and sBTLA lost significance, reinforcing the importance of sGal-9.

Pair-wise correlations revealed two functional clusters. The first centered on sTIM-3, strongly correlated with sGal-9 (ρ = 0.73, p < 0.001) and moderately with sBTLA (ρ = 0.59, p < 0.001). sBTLA also correlated with sGal-9 (ρ = 0.64, p < 0.001) and weakly with sLAG-3, while sTIM-3 correlated modestly with sLAG-3. The second cluster, comprising sHVEM and sCD160, showed a tight relationship (ρ = 0.61, p < 0.001) (Fig. 2B). These patterns align with known biology: TIM-3/Gal-9 and HVEM/CD160 form a receptor-ligand pair, BTLA and LAG-3 appear during intermediate phases of T-cell exhaustion, while LAG-3 and TIM-3 are co-expressed in the final phase [7–10]. Although the kinetics of sICs remain uncharacterized, the correlation architecture suggests that serum levels may mirror exhaustion-related shedding from TILs.

In summary, sGal-9, sTIM-3, and sBTLA robustly distinguish BC patients from HC, independent of BMI, sex, and tumor stage/grade. sGal-9 stands out as the strongest and independently predictive biomarker. However, upregulated sICs expression may reflect broader immune dysregulation in other pathological or inflammatory conditions (i.e. other tumors), therefore our findings needs validation in additional patients’ cohorts. The observed molecular clustering outlines a coherent sIC network that reflects known immune-regulatory relationships. These findings demonstrate a promising biomarker framework that could significantly reduce dependence on cystoscopy and support non-invasive BC detection.

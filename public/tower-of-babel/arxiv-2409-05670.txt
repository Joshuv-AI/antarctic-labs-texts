Emergent chirality and superfluidity of parity-doubled baryons in neutron stars
Shigehiro Yasui, Muneto Nitta, Chihiro Sasaki

Abstract
We propose novel superfluids induced by the parity-doubled baryons. The parity-doubled baryons, i.e., a nucleon N ⁡ ( 940 ) N(940) with mass 940 MeV and spin-parity J P = 1 / 2 + J^{P}=1/2^{+} and an excited nucleon N ∗ ​ ( 1535 ) N^{\ast}(1535) with mass 1535 MeV and spin-parity J P = 1 / 2 − J^{P}=1/2^{-} in vacuum, become degenerate at sufficiently high density where the chiral symmetry is restored. In this study, we extend the conventional U ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathrm{U}(1) chiral symmetry to the higher dimensional symmetries, dubbed emergent chiral symmetries, including the naive and mirror assignments as their subgroups. Starting with the Lagrangian up to four-point interactions among the neutron n n and its chiral partner n ∗ n^{\ast} , neutral components in N N and N ∗ N^{\ast} , in pure neutron matter, we investigate the properties of the ground state with a pairing gap generated by the n n and n ∗ n^{\ast} in the mean-field approximation. We find vector-type condensates that induce the dynamical breaking of a new class of internal symmetries, emergent chiral symmetries , as well as the baryon number and the rotational symmetries of the real space, indicating the appearance of massless Nambu-Goldstone bosons consisting of six quarks: emergent pions, superfluid phonons, and magnons, respectively. We also study the fermionic excitation modes at low-energy scales, and show that they exhibit a spatial anisotropy of the propagation at the Dirac cone in momentum space. Some phenomenological implications are advocated, shedding new light on the properties of neutron stars.

Naive and mirror assignments

II.2
Emergent chiral symmetry

Given our motivation that the two options of chirality assignments are to be unified,
we extend the usual chiral symmetry to higher symmetry: an emergent chiral symmetry.
Among possible candidates, we are naturally led to

\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{R}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{R}}

) is expressed by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\cal H}_{\delta}^{\ast\pm}(\boldsymbol{q})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma}&0&0&0\\
0&-\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma}&0&0\\
0&0&\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma}&0\\
0&0&0&-\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{\sigma}\end{array}\right).

Here, instead of
\boldsymbol{q}
, we define
\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}
by

\displaystyle\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}=\bigl(\tilde{q}_{1},\tilde{q}_{2},\tilde{q}_{3}\bigr)=\left(-\dfrac{q_{1}}{\sqrt{1+\dfrac{\mu^{2}}{|\delta|^{2}}}},-\dfrac{q_{2}}{\sqrt{1+\dfrac{\mu^{2}}{|\delta|^{2}}}},q_{3}\right),

(47)

for the convenience of the notation.
The momenta in the direction along the
x
and
y
axes,
\tilde{q}_{1}
and
\tilde{q}_{2}
, are scaled by the factor
1/\sqrt{1+\mu^{2}/|\delta|^{2}}
against the original momenta,
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
.
There is no modification for the
z
axis.
The submatrices,
\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
-\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, in the Hamiltonian (
III.4
) indicate the fermionic excitation modes with negative and positive parities, respectively, near the Dirac points.
The eigenvalues of the Hamiltonian (
III.4
) are given by

\displaystyle\pm\varepsilon_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}}=\pm|\tilde{\boldsymbol{q}}|=\pm\sqrt{\dfrac{q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}}{1+\dfrac{\mu^{2}}{|\delta|^{2}}}+q_{3}^{2}},

(48)

with four degenerate solutions in each
\pm
.
The dispersion relation (
48
) shows a special property of the fermionic excitation modes at the Dirac points:
the propagation speed of the fermionic excitations on the
x
-
y
planes is slower than the speed of light,
while the propagation speed in the direction of the
z
axis is the same as the speed of light.
The former is because the slope in the energy-momentum dispersion relation is very small (
|\delta|\ll\mu
) in the
x
and
y
directions.
Notice that the vector condensate is directed along the
z
axis in the present setting.
Therefore, the fermionic excitation modes have a tendency to propagate on the
x
-
y
plane rather than the
z
axis, because the energy cost of excitation is smaller on the
x
-
y
plane.
This result shows the anisotropy of the transport phenomenon in the emergent chiral superfluids.

IV
Conclusion and outlook

We have discussed the emergent chiral superfluids of neutron matter, where the neutron
n(940)
(mass 940 MeV, spin-parity

J^{P}=1/2^{+}
) and the chiral partner
n^{\ast}(1535)
(mass 1535 MeV, spin-parity
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
) are degenerate forming parity doubling at sufficiently high density.
Apart from the usual chiral symmetry, we have introduced the
G_{\mathrm{L}}\times G_{\mathrm{R}}=\mathrm{U}(1)_{(1-2)\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{(1-2)\mathrm{R}}

and
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{R}}
emergent chiral symmetries as proper symmetries in the parity-doublet model since the naive and mirror assignments are naturally included as their subgroups.
The emergent chiral symmetries are novel symmetries proposed in this study for the first time.
Introducing the four-point interactions for
n
and
n^{\ast}
, we have classified the Lagrangian in each emergent chiral symmetry and analyzed the Cooper pairings based on the Nambu-Gor’kov formalism.
The emergent chiral superfluids are characterized by several different condensates: the emergent-chiral-symmetric and vector condensate (
{\cal S}_{a}1^{-}
;
a=0,1,3
) and the emergent-chiral-asymmetric and axialvector condensate (
{\cal A}1^{+}
).
For the
\mathrm{U}(1)_{(1-2)\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{(1-2)\mathrm{R}}
emergent chiral symmetry, we have obtained (i) either
{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}
or
{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}
and (ii) either
{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
or
{\cal A}1^{+}
according to the relative strengths in the interaction terms.
For the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{SU}(2)_{\mathrm{R}}
emergent chiral symmetry, one of the
{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}
,
{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
,
{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}
and
{\cal A}1^{+}
condensates is formed.
The emergent chiral symmetry as well as the spatial rotational symmetry are broken by these condensates.
We have analyzed the patterns of symmetry breaking and the appearance of the NG bosons consisting of six quarks, and found that the emergent chiral superfluids exhibit several topological excitations according to the homotopy groups.
We have also shown the existence of the (massless) Dirac fermions at Dirac points in momentum space, indicating an anisotropy in the transport phenomena.

In this paper, we have considered
condensates of only one of
the vector condensations

\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{a}
(
a=0,1,3
) and
\boldsymbol{\delta}
for

{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}
,

{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
,

{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}
and
{\cal A}1^{+}
for simplicity.
We have further assumed for each case
a condensation of only
one spatial component of each three-dimensional vector
condensation,

\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{a}=(0,0,\Delta_{a})
or
\boldsymbol{\delta}=(0,0,\delta)
,
which is not the most general.
This situation
corresponds to the polar phase
in spinor BECs,
in which case depending on couplings
there is also another phase, the so-called ferromagnetic phase
where the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
rotational symmetry is completely
broken spontaneously
with magnetizations
[
87
]
.
Investigating a possibility of such a
ferromagnetic phase
with a possible origin of magnetic fields in neutron stars
is one of future directions.
It is also interesting to study whether
a simultaneous condensation of

{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}
and

{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}

and that of

{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
and
{\cal A}1^{+}

can occur in general.
In the
{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}
or

{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}
phase
in Fig.
5
,
the ground states of
the condensates

{\cal S}_{0}1^{-}

and

{\cal S}_{3}1^{-}

are energetically degenerated,
while
in the

{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
or
{\cal A}1^{+}
phase
those of

{\cal S}_{1}1^{-}
and
{\cal A}1^{+}

are degenerated.
Thus, there may exist domain walls
interpolating these two condensates in each phase.

As discussed in Sec.
III.3
,
there exists a rich variety of topological objects in dynamical symmetry breakings in our superfluids,
among which a particularly important class is given by superfluid vortices;
since neutron stars are rapidly rotating,
there appear a large number of
superfluid vortices along the rotation axis
and they may form an Abrikosov’s lattice.
In our case, a singly quantized vortex may
be split into a pair of
half-quantized Alice strings
[
96
,
97
]
.
Such superfluid vortices may be important for pulsar glitches,
as was proposed for
{}^{3}P_{2}
-
{}^{1}S_{0}
interface
[
54
]
.
If our superfluids are connected to

{}^{3}P_{2}
neutron superfluids
as illustrated in Fig.
1
,
half-quantized Alice strings in the former
may be connected to
half-quantized non-Abelian vortices
[
38
,
50
,
51
,
48
]

in the latter.

Anisotropic transport of Dirac fermions may have
an impact on neutron star cooling. It is an interesting subject to study the interaction between neutrinos and Dirac fermions. There we will also need to consider the NG bosons and study the coupled system among neutrinos, Dirac fermions and NG bosons.
The Berry phase around the Dirac cone will lead to a nontrivial topological charge showing properties of topological materials.

Studying the NG bosons consisting of six quarks in the
emergent chiral superfluids
may be an interesting subject for multiquark systems in the light of exotic hadrons
by the quark number counting.
Similar situations can be considered in the neutron
{}^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{3}P_{2}
superfluids.
Revealing the properties of these NG bosons, such as dispersion relations, interactions with medium and so on, at high density may give us a hint to understand multiquark systems in a way different from the conventional hadron spectroscopy in vacuum.
Investigating contributions of these NG bosons to neutron star cooling is also
an important future problem.

Our results in this paper are obtained exclusively with the specific part of the entire Lagrangian,
\mathcal{L}_{\rm com}
. The given classifications of symmetries and operators both in the naive and mirror assignments may suggest further richness of the phase structure in a more general situation.
It is of particular interest to explore the full theory to possibly pin down any characteristics as distinct properties of the given chirality assignment in high-density QCD.
Besides, it may also be intriguing to clarify a potential link of the emergent chiral symmetry to the chiral spin symmetry proposed for the chiral structure of hadrons in hot and dense matter
[
100
,
101
,
102
,
103
]
, as well as to the quarkyonic phase allowing the hadronic degrees freedom near the Fermi surface of quark matter
[
104
]
.
Those open problems are left for the future studies.

Appendix A
Naive and mirror assignments and extension of chiral symmetry

We consider two baryon fields denoted by
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
as chiral doublet.

\psi_{i}
(
i=1,2
) has the left and right components according to chirality,
\psi_{i\mathrm{L}}
and
\psi_{i\mathrm{R}}
.
Thus,
\psi_{i}
(
i=1,2
) is expressed by a direct sum of
\psi_{i\mathrm{L}}
and
\psi_{i\mathrm{R}}
:

\displaystyle\psi_{1}=\psi_{1\mathrm{L}}+\psi_{1\mathrm{R}},\quad\psi_{2}=\psi_{2\mathrm{L}}+\psi_{2\mathrm{R}}.

(49)

The left and right components,
\psi_{i\mathrm{R}}
and
\psi_{i\mathrm{L}}
(
i=1,2
), are expressed by the projection operators

\displaystyle\psi_{1\mathrm{L}}=\frac{1-\gamma_{5}}{2}\psi_{1},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{L}}=\frac{1-\gamma_{5}}{2}\psi_{2},\quad\psi_{1\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1+\gamma_{5}}{2}\psi_{1},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1+\gamma_{5}}{2}\psi_{2}.

(50)

In
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
, the left and right components are transformed under the chiral symmetry
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{R}}
.
The linear combinations of
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
make the basis for the fields of the nucleon (
N
) with spin-parity
J^{P}=1/2^{+}
and the excited nucleon (
N^{\ast}(1535)
) with
J^{P}=1/2^{-}
.

The chiral symmetry
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{R}}
indicates usually the simultaneous transformations as a global symmetry:

\displaystyle\psi_{1\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{L}}\psi_{1\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{L}}\psi_{2\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{1\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{R}}\psi_{1\mathrm{R}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{R}}\psi_{2\mathrm{R}},

(51)

for
U_{\mathrm{L}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{L}}
and
U_{\mathrm{R}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{R}}
.
In this case, the transformation rules are common to
\psi_{i\mathrm{L}}
and
\psi_{i\mathrm{R}}
(
i=1,2
), respectively, for each chirality:
the left component transforms under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{L}}
and the right component transforms under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{R}}
.
This is, however, not the unique transformation.
Another possible transformation in chiral symmetry would be given by

\displaystyle\psi_{1\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{L}}\psi_{1\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{R}}\psi_{2\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{1\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{R}}\psi_{1\mathrm{R}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{\mathrm{L}}\psi_{2\mathrm{R}},

(52)

where
\psi_{1\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{R}}
transforms under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{R}}
and
\psi_{2\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{R}}
transforms under
\mathrm{U}(1)_{\mathrm{R}/\mathrm{L}}
.
Notice in this case that the chiral transformation of
\psi_{1}
is different from that of
\psi_{2}
.
The chiral transformations (
51
) and (
52
) are called the
naive
and
mirror
assignments, respectively, in the chiral doublet formalism.
It is a nontrivial problem which of naive and mirror assignments are realized in the QCD, and this problem is researched by several researchers.

Including the naive and mirror assignments in the chiral symmetry, as discussed in Sec.
II.2
,
we consider the emergent chiral symmetry,
\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{L}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{R}}\times\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{R}}
, where
\mathrm{U}(1)_{i\alpha}
(
i=1,2
and
\alpha=\mathrm{L},\mathrm{R}
) indicates the
\mathrm{U}(1)
symmetry for each
\psi_{i\alpha}
.
In this symmetry,
\psi_{i\alpha}
is transformed in a completely independent manner by

\displaystyle\psi_{1\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{1\mathrm{L}}\psi_{1\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{L}}\rightarrow U_{2\mathrm{L}}\psi_{2\mathrm{L}},\quad\psi_{1\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{1\mathrm{R}}\psi_{1\mathrm{R}},\quad\psi_{2\mathrm{R}}\rightarrow U_{2\mathrm{R}}\psi_{2\mathrm{R}},

(53)

with
U_{1\mathrm{L}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{L}}
,
U_{2\mathrm{L}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{L}}
,
U_{1\mathrm{R}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{1\mathrm{R}}
and
U_{2\mathrm{R}}\in\mathrm{U}(1)_{2\mathrm{R}}
.
The naive symmetry is reproduced by setting
U_{1\mathrm{L}}=U_{2\mathrm{L}}
and
U_{1\mathrm{R}}=U_{2\mathrm{R}}
.
Similarly, the mirror symmetry is reproduced by setting
U_{1\mathrm{L}}=U_{2\mathrm{R}}
and
U_{1\mathrm{R}}=U_{2\mathrm{L}}
.

Protocol for reconstructing spatially aware receptor-TF-target signaling cascades using spatial transcriptomics
Authors: Yao Li, Xiaobin Liu, Xinkai Yan, Guangyi Fan, Mengyang Xu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryConventional protocols for inferring gene regulatory networks from spatial transcriptomics data often neglect spatial constraints. Here, we present a protocol using SpaGRN, a statistical framework integrating extracellular signaling and spatial dependencies, to reconstruct spatially aware receptor-transcription factor-target regulatory cascades. This protocol details steps for data preprocessing, spatial autocorrelation, co-expression construction, cis-regulatory motif enrichment, receptor-associated regulon inference, and 3D visualization. This approach enables the systematic mapping of signaling pathways in complex tissues such as developing embryos and tumors.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Li et al.1Subject areas: Bioinformatics, Single cell, Developmental biology, Systems biology, Computer sciences

Before you begin

Spatially resolved transcriptomics (SRT) technologies enable comprehensive mapping of gene expression within intact tissues, preserving spatial context essential for understanding biological systems. While these technologies capture both gene expression levels and their precise spatial coordinates, conventional computational methods for inferring gene regulatory networks (GRNs) exhibit critical limitations when applied to SRT data. Specifically, they often disregard tissue spatial heterogeneity and intercellular signaling mediated by ligand-receptor interactions, key mechanisms modulating transcription factor (TF) activity, and downstream gene expression.

To overcome these constraints, we developed SpaGRN, a computational framework that reconstructs dynamic and spatially aware GRNs by integrating intracellular regulatory causality with extracellular signaling cues. This approach leverages spatial co-expression patterns and cis-regulatory motif analysis to identify context-dependent regulons, providing a more comprehensive understanding of gene regulation in tissue microenvironments.

The protocol outlined below details SpaGRN’s application to SRT data analysis. It is benchmarked using mouse brain data generated via the Stereo-seq platform, as detailed in our associated research paper.1 Critically, SpaGRN’s versatility extends to other SRT platforms and tissue types, such as human colorectal cancer datasets and three-dimensional (3D) developing Drosophila embryos. This broad applicability positions SpaGRN as a robust tool for dissecting gene regulation across diverse biological systems.

Innovation

This work presents a fully automated, computationally integrated protocol for reconstructing end-to-end signaling cascades from receptors through transcription factors and their target genes within native spatial contexts. By coupling intercellular communication inference with intracellular motif-guided cis-regulatory network reconstruction in a unified spatial framework, our protocol overcomes a major limitation of existing methods, which typically analyze each spot or cell independently. It provides a cohesive spatial representation of gene regulatory events and demonstrates improved accuracy in identifying context-specific regulons, along with enhanced computational efficiency. Unlike prior scRNA-centric or correlation-only tools, this spatially aware, receptor-anchored GRN inference is delivered as an open-source Python toolkit featuring interactive 3D visualization. Designed for broad applicability, it supports data from multiple high-resolution spatial transcriptomics platforms, including 10x Visium, Stereo-seq, and MERFISH, thereby empowering systematic investigation of signaling mechanisms underlying complex biological processes such as organogenesis and tumorigenesis.

Expected outcomes

Upon completion of the analysis, an AnnData file (e.g., Mouse_brain_cell_bin.h5ad) that encapsulates all analytical results is generated in the specified output directory. Within this file, the.uns attribute stores the inferred regulons and their associated receptor-TF regulatory dictionaries. These represent putative transcriptional modules regulated by upstream receptors. Users can interpret these dictionaries to identify which TFs are linked to specific signaling pathways. Concurrently, the.obsm attribute contains the ISR activity matrix, which quantifies regulon activity per cell or spatial location. Higher ISR scores indicate stronger predicted activity of the corresponding regulon. Users should compare ISR score distributions across cell types or regions to identify cluster-specific or spatially restricted regulatory programs. These data can be visualized in multiple ways. For example, the spatial distribution of annotated cell types across the adult mouse hemisphere is shown in Figure 1A, and dimensionality reduction before and after gene filtering is illustrated in Figures 1B and 1C. These plots allow users to evaluate the effect of gene filtering on clustering structure. If clusters remain consistent while the number of retained genes is reduced, it suggests that filtering preserves biological signal while removing noise. The pie chart in Figure 1D illustrates the proportion of genes exhibiting significant spatial autocorrelation among all expressed genes. The impact of different spatial co-expression methods on FDR is depicted in Figure 1E. The density plots help assess the stringency of different spatial co-expression methods. A left-shifted FDR distribution indicates more confident regulon detection. Regulatory programs can be further explored through heatmaps of ISR scores (Figure 1F) and spatial maps of cluster-specific regulons (Figures 1G–1J). Heatmap of ISR scores highlights cell-type-specific regulons. Rows correspond to regulons, and columns to cell types; high values (warmer colors) denote strong activity. Users can identify which regulons are enriched in particular cell types. Spatial distribution maps visualize the spatial heterogeneity of selected regulons. Distinct regional enrichment patterns can reveal how microenvironmental cues shape regulatory programs in situ. Collectively, these outputs elucidate the intricate gene regulatory programs within the tissue that are fundamentally shaped by the spatial microenvironment. The code to generate Figures 1A–1E is available in the GitHub repository at https://github.com/BGI-Qingdao/SpaGRN-STARProtocols. The code for Figures 1F–1J can be found in Steps 11 and 13.

Quantification and statistical analysis

To validate SpaGRN’s accuracy in identifying spatial patterns, we employed an authentic mouse brain SRT dataset.

Regarding computational performance, the core regulon inference procedure (grn.infer()) represents the primary computational bottleneck in SpaGRN. Runtime scales with dataset dimensions (number of cells/spots and genes) and user-defined parameters (e.g., num_workers specifying parallel CPU cores). To facilitate resource estimation, we conducted benchmark tests using the same Stereo-seq mouse brain dataset (50,140 cells × 25,879 genes) (Figure 2). Firstly, we performed minimal filtering to remove genes that have zero expression values across all cells and cells that have zero genes. Following filtering, the dataset dimensions were reduced to 50,140 cells and 23,472 genes. As the number of cells (Figure 2A) or genes (Figure 2B) increases, both computation time and memory usage rise accordingly. On a Linux system (20 CPU cores, 256 GB RAM), SpaGRN completed inference in approximately 1 hour, demonstrating substantial efficiency for large-scale analyses.

Alternatively, users can perform basic topological and other network analyses on the GRNs, which are visualized in Cytoscape (Figure 3C) and built from the receptor-TF-target results in the AnnData object (adata.uns[‘regulon_dict’] and adata.uns[‘receptor_dict’]). This allows for the identification of key regulators, hub genes, and comparative analysis of network architecture.

The ISR regulon activity matrix (analogous to SCENIC’s AUC matrix) enables clustering analysis to group cells or conditions by regulatory activity patterns. This reveals key differences in signaling regulation across samples, mapping signaling hierarchies while identifying functionally distinct cellular states driven by pathway activation heterogeneity.

Furthermore, SpaGRN integrates SRT data across multiple tissue types and SRT platforms to construct an interactive 3D online atlas. This comprehensive resource encompasses diverse biological systems (including human brain, mouse brain, and Drosophila embryonic development), delivering a multi-tissue spatial atlas for researchers.

Limitations

While SpaGRN represents a powerful framework, users must acknowledge its inherent constraints. First, inferred regulatory relationships are prone to false positives and false negatives; thus, critical findings require experimental validation for confirmation. Second, SpaGRN’s performance depends critically on input data quality and the accuracy and completeness of underlying knowledge bases, such as TF motif and ligand-receptor databases. Inaccurate inputs or incomplete annotations may yield an insufficiently characterized regulatory network. Moreover, as these knowledge bases are primarily derived from model organisms like human and mouse, the framework’s applicability and accuracy may diminish for non-model organisms or poorly characterized tissues. Finally, the current design focuses on spatially proximate regulatory interactions and may not adequately capture long-range intercellular interactions or non-cell-autonomous regulatory mechanisms.

Troubleshooting

Problem 1

Users may encounter errors such as “A GDAL API version must be specified. Provide a path to gdal-config using a GDAL_CONFIG environment variable or use a GDAL_VERSION environment variable” and “Failed to build installable wheels for some pyproject.toml based projects (h5py)”. This issue is related to setting up the computational environment (related to Step 3, Method A of before you begin).

Potential solution

These errors are compilation issues caused by outdated versions of dependent packages in the environment. Update all dependent packages to their latest compatible versions, as outdated packages often lead to compilation conflicts. If the problem persists, install the problematic packages individually using conda, which can resolve version-specific compilation issues by managing dependencies more effectively within its environment.

Problem 2

Users may encounter dependency conflicts when installing the latest version of SpaGRN on GitHub (related to Step 3, Method B of before you begin).

Potential solution

First, confirm the Python version is 3.8. Next, identify any conflicting packages, create a new requirements.txt, and install the dependencies using pip install -r requirements.txt.

Problem 3

The intermediate file more_stats.csv contains numerous genes with zero values (related to Steps 9 and 14).

Potential solution

The more_stats.csv file stores p-values and adjusted p-values generated from spatial autocorrelation statistics for each gene. A small p-value indicates that the observed spatial gene expression pattern is statistically significant and unlikely to occur by chance.

Problem 4

A gene of interest is absent from the selected_genes.txt file (related to Steps 9 and 14).

Potential solution

The selected_genes.txt file lists genes exhibiting strong spatial autocorrelation. Omission of a specific gene suggests its spatial expression pattern lacks statistically significant spatial dependency. To increase gene retention during selection, modify the filtering strategy as follows.

•Union-based approach: Combine the output (union) of all spatial autocorrelation statistical methods instead of using their intersection (the default conservative approach).

Union-based approach: Combine the output (union) of all spatial autocorrelation statistical methods instead of using their intersection (the default conservative approach).

•Method reduction: Apply fewer statistical methods and take the intersection of genes selected by the remaining methods.

Method reduction: Apply fewer statistical methods and take the intersection of genes selected by the remaining methods.

Problem 5

Encountering AssertionError: Signatures dataframe is empty! (related to Step 9).

Potential solution

This error indicates that no regulons were detected. To reconstruct potential regulons, increase the rank_threshold or decrease the auc_threshold.

Problem 6

No regulons regulated by the specified TF were identified in the results (related to Step 9).

Potential solution

Regulon identification employs a rigorous multi-step validation process that distinguishes true regulons from simple co-expression modules. The workflow initiates with establishing TF-motif associations based on documented motif positions within the genome. Subsequently, motif enrichment analysis is performed on experimental data to detect potential target genes downstream of these motifs.

A TF-target set qualifies as a valid regulon only upon satisfying two stringent criteria: (1) demonstration of robust co-expression or association within the dataset, and (2) statistically significant enrichment of corresponding motifs, as quantified by the Normalized Enrichment Score (NES).

In the current analysis, genes of interest failed to satisfy the selection criteria during this process. This typically occurs when genes do not meet either the gene ranking threshold (rank_threshold) or the motif enrichment significance threshold (nes_threshold). To recover potential regulons, consider parameter adjustment.

•Increase rank_threshold to expand the pool of candidate genes.

Increase rank_threshold to expand the pool of candidate genes.

•Decrease nes_threshold to relax the requirement for motif enrichment significance.

Decrease nes_threshold to relax the requirement for motif enrichment significance.

While threshold adjustment may expand regulon detection, it inherently reduces result specificity by increasing false positives.

Problem 7

Regulons lack target genes (related to Step 9).

Potential solution

To recover potential target genes, adjust filtering thresholds.

•Increase rank_threshold to expand candidate gene inclusion.

Increase rank_threshold to expand candidate gene inclusion.

•Decrease nes_threshold to relax motif enrichment significance requirements.

Decrease nes_threshold to relax motif enrichment significance requirements.

Problem 8

ValueError: A gene signature must have at least one gene (related to Step 9).

Potential solution

This error occurs when lenient parameters (e.g., high rank_threshold or low nes_threshold) permit motifs without target genes to pass filtering. Resolve by: a. Applying stricter filtering thresholds, or b. Modifying the error-handling code to retain only non-empty “TargetGenes” columns while maintaining current thresholds.

Problem 9

When a step encounters an error causing pipeline termination, can analysis resume from the failed step rather than restarting entirely? (related to Step 9).

Potential solution

SpaGRN’s caching mechanism allows restarting from the point of failure when cache=True and save_tmp=True are enabled. The framework reloads intermediate files generated during prior executions, bypassing redundant computations through efficient reuse of previously calculated results.

Problem 13

The network inference step, grn.infer(), exhibits excessively slow computational performance (related to Setting up the computational environment).

Potential solution

This typically results from insufficient allocation of parallel processing resources. SpaGRN leverages parallel computing architectures. To accelerate computation, set the num_workers parameter to match the number of available CPU cores on your system (or slightly fewer to avoid resource contention). For example: num_workers=40.

Problem 14

Failure to detect regulons in rare cell types or small tissue regions (related to Step 9 and expected outcomes).

Potential solution

Signals from rare cell types or spatially restricted regions can be masked by dominant adjacent signals due to sample size imbalance. Mitigate this via.

•Prioritize the use of the ‘moran’ mode within the grn.infer() function (i.e., mode=‘moran’). Moran’s I spatial autocorrelation statistic demonstrates superior sensitivity for rare cell populations.

Prioritize the use of the ‘moran’ mode within the grn.infer() function (i.e., mode=‘moran’). Moran’s I spatial autocorrelation statistic demonstrates superior sensitivity for rare cell populations.

•Manually subset tissue data to the target region before analysis. This increases the relative abundance of target cells, enhancing detection sensitivity. Isolate regions of interest to increase target population representation.

Manually subset tissue data to the target region before analysis. This increases the relative abundance of target cells, enhancing detection sensitivity. Isolate regions of interest to increase target population representation.

Problem 15

The spatial patterns of inferred regulons lack specificity (related to Expected outcome).

Potential solution

This may arise from mismatched statistical methods or suboptimal parameterization.

•The selected statistical method for identifying spatial genes may be unsuitable for the current dataset. Test alternative methods parameter configurations (e.g., methods=[‘FDR_I’] or methods=[‘FDR_C’]) to identify optimal gene-expression spatial dependency modeling.

The selected statistical method for identifying spatial genes may be unsuitable for the current dataset. Test alternative methods parameter configurations (e.g., methods=[‘FDR_I’] or methods=[‘FDR_C’]) to identify optimal gene-expression spatial dependency modeling.

•The n_neighbors parameter may not accurately reflect the tissue’s underlying spatial architecture. Adjust this parameter (default is 10) based on the resolution and cell density of your data. High cell density: Use smaller values (e.g., 5–15) to prioritize local neighborhoods. Low cell density: Use larger values (e.g., 20–50) to capture broader spatial contexts.

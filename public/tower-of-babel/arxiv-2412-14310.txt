Equivariant cohomological rigidity for four-dimensional Hamiltonian 𝐒 𝟏 \mathbf{S^{1}} -manifolds
Tara S. Holm, Liat Kessler, Susan Tolman

Abstract
. For manifolds equipped with group actions, we have the following natural question: To what extent does the equivariant cohomology determine the equivariant diffeotype? We resolve this question for Hamiltonian circle actions on compact, connected symplectic four-manifolds. They are equivariantly diffeomorphic if and only if their equivariant cohomology rings are isomorphic as algebras over the equivariant cohomology of a point. In fact, we prove a stronger claim: each isomorphism between their equivariant cohomology rings is induced by an equivariant diffeomorphism.

1.
Introduction

For any algebraic invariant attached to a manifold, a key question is: To what extent does the invariant determine the manifold? In general, cohomology does not detect the diffeotype of a manifold. However, in recent years there has been substantial activity on establishing
when certain classes of manifolds are
cohomologically rigid
,
that is, determined by their cohomology.
In particular, Masuda and Suh conjectured that two toric manifolds (smooth toric varieties)
are diffeomorphic if their integral cohomology rings are isomorphic
[
20
]
;
this conjecture has been affirmed in many special cases
[
6
]
.

For manifolds with group actions, the analogous question is: To what extent does the equivariant cohomology determine the equivariant diffeotype?
For example, compact toric manifolds are
equivariantly cohomologically rigid
: two compact toric manifolds
whose equivariant cohomology rings are isomorphic as algebras are equivariantly diffeomorphic (indeed, this holds for the broader class of quasitoric manifolds)
[
19
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
7
, Theorem 6.6]
, and
[
13
, Remark 2.5(1)]
.
Moreover, if the algebra isomorphism preserves the first equivariant Chern class, then they are equivariantly isomorphic as varieties; see
[
19
]
and
[
13
, Remark 2.5(3)]
.
Similarly, for compact, connected, four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds,
if an equivariant cohomology algebra isomorphism
preserves the first equivariant Chern class, then
the manifolds are equivariantly biholomorphic (with respect to some compatible invariant complex structures); see
[
14
, Example 1.2 and Corollary 7.34]
.
Note also that the diffeotype of such a manifold
is determined by the cohomology ring
H^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
;
see Proposition
2.10
.

In order to state our results, we recall a few definitions.
A circle
action

In this paper we prove that equivariant cohomological rigidity holds for compact, connected, four-dimensional Hamiltonian

2.
Oriented dull graphs

In this section, we first give a formal definition of the dull graph associated to a compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold, and then describe the natural orientation on this graph. As we show, we can obtain any other orientation on the dull graph by a sequence of operations: If necessary, we take the “opposite orientation”, and then apply a finite number of “partial flips”. Finally, we prove that if the oriented dull graphs of two compact,
connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds are either isomorphic or have opposite orientations,
then there is an orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism between the manifolds.
This reduces the proof of Theorem
1.2
to showing
that there is an orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism between two manifolds if their oriented dull graphs differ by a partial flip.
Sections
3
and
4
will be devoted to this last step.

Let the circle
S^{1}
act on a compact, four-dimensional symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)

with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
.
Each connected component of the fixed set
M^{S^{1}}
is a symplectic submanifold, and hence is either
zero-dimensional or two-dimensional.
Every point with stabilizer
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
for some
k\geq 2

is contained in an
isotropy
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
-sphere
, that is,
an
S^{1}
-invariant symplectic two-sphere with generic stabilizer
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
.
Note that each isotropy sphere contains exactly two isolated fixed points, and
each isolated fixed point is contained in at most two isotropy spheres.
The moment map is a Morse-Bott function and the index of each fixed component is twice
the number of negative weights. As Atiyah showed, this implies that the level sets of
\Phi
are
connected, and so
\Phi
has exactly one local minimum and one local maximum
[
2
]
.
Hence, there are exactly two extremal fixed components and each non-extremal fixed point
has exactly
one positive weight and one negative weight.
In particular, every fixed surface is extremal.

We now introduce dull graphs and orientations
on them.

2.1 Definition
.

The
dull graph
associated to a compact, connected, four-dimensional
Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold
(M,\omega)
has one vertex for each fixed
component and an edge between two vertices exactly if the vertices correspond to isolated fixed points and
there is an isotropy
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
-sphere that
contains those points.
The dull graph has labels as follows:

•

“Thin” vertices represent the isolated fixed points; each such vertex is labeled by whether the moment
map value is extremal or not.

•

“Fat” vertices represent fixed surfaces; each such vertex is labeled by
the self-intersection
e
and the genus
g
of the surface.
These are always extremal.

•

Edges between vertices are labeled with integers
k\geq 2
where the label
k
indicates the vertices correspond to the fixed points
of an isotropy
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
-sphere.

We will denote the data of a dull graph as
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E,L)
.

Two dull graphs are
isomorphic
if there is a graph isomorphism that respects the labels.

2.2 Remark
.

An orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism between four-dimensional Hamiltonian

S^{1}
-manifolds induces an isomorphism
of dull graphs.
This is straight-forward to check once we note
that an isolated fixed point is extremal exactly if the product of the weights is positive.

In

to each compact, connected, four-dimensional

S^{1}

labels: the moment value of each fixed component and the (properly normalized) area

of each fixed surface.
Hence, the dull graph can be obtained from the decorated graph by forgetting the extra information.
Note that Karshon does not need to specify the “extremal” or self-intersection labels because they can be
obtained from the other data

15
, Lemma 2.18]
.

2.4 Theorem

(Karshon)
.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
and
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\Phi})
be compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds.
Let
f\colon M_{\min}\to{\widetilde{M}}_{\min}
be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism

\widetilde{M}

Given an isomorphism between the decorated graphs associated to
M

and
\widetilde{M}
, there exists an equivariant symplectomorphism

h|_{M_{\min}}

is isotopic to

Proof.

By Moser’s method, the orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
f\colon M_{\min}\to{\widetilde{M}}_{\min}
is isotopic to a symplectomorphism.
In
[
15

, Karshon prescribes how to extend such a symplectomorphism to an equivariant orientation-preserving diffeomorphism

The forms
\omega_{t}=(1-t)\omega+t\widetilde{\omega}

Moreover,
\omega_{t}
is nondegenerate for all

is equivariantly isotopic to an equivariant symplectomorphism

∎

Oriented dull graphs are an intermediate step between dull graphs and decorated graphs.

2.5 Definition
.

An
orientation

\mathcal{O}
on a dull graph
{\mathcal{G}}
consists of

•

Adding the label “mininum” to one of the extremal vertices and
the label “maximum” to the other; and

•

Giving
{\mathcal{G}}
the structure of a directed graph such that
the minimum is not the head of any edge, the maximum is not the tail of any edge,
and each non-extremal vertex is the head of at most one edge, and the tail of at most one edge.

Given a Hamiltonian
S^{1}
- manifold
(M,\omega,\Phi)
,
the moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathbb{R}}
induces a natural orientation on the associated dull graph
{\mathcal{G}}
. We
label the extremal vertices according to whether their moment image is maximal or minimal, and direct each edge so that
the moment image of the head is greater than the image of the tail. If a non-extremal isolated fixed point
p
is
contained in two isotropy spheres, then the moment image of one sphere lies above
\Phi(p)
and the
moment image of the other sphere lies below.
Hence, this gives an orientation
\mathcal{O}
on
{\mathcal{G}}
in the sense of the definition above.

As Holm and Kessler prove, one consequence of Theorem
2.4
is that oriented dull graphs distinguish
four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds up to equivariant diffeomorphism.

2.6 Lemma
.

(
[
14
, Remark 3.5 and Proposition 3.9]
)

Given an orientation-preserving isomorphism between
the dull graphs associated to two
compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds,
there is an orientation-preserving, equivariant diffeomorphism between the manifolds that induces the given isomorphism.

We say that a connected component
C
of the set of points with non-trivial stabilizer is a
free chain
if the component does not contain either the minimal or maximal fixed component.
A
free point
is an isolated fixed point that has isotropy weights
1
and
-1
.
Since a fixed point
p
is the maximum (resp. minimum) of an isotropy
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}
sphere exactly if one of the isotropy weights at
p
is
-k
(resp. +
k
),
every free point is a trivial free chain.
Similarly, let
{\mathcal{G}}=(V,E,L)
be the dull graph associated to the Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-action on
M
. By the discussion above, the dull graph
{\mathcal{G}}
has two “extremal” vertices and each vertex has degree at most two.
A
free chain
of the dull graph
{\mathcal{G}}
is a connected component
C
of
{\mathcal{G}}
that does not contain either extremal vertex.
A
free point
is a non-extremal point with no adjacent edges.

Given one orientation on a dull graph
{\mathcal{G}}
, there is another orientation on
{\mathcal{G}}
given by exchanging the “minimum” and “maximum” labels and reversing the direction on every edge of the graph. We call this the
opposite orientation
.
Additionally, given a free chain
C
that is not a free point, there is another orientation obtained by reversing the direction on each edge in the chain
C
and leaving all other aspects of the orientation the same. We call this the
partial flip along
\mathbf{C}
. These operations on orientations are shown in Figure
2.7
.

It is straight forward to see that any two orientations on
{\mathcal{G}}
with the same minimum and maximal labels differ by some number of partial flips.
We record this in the following lemma.

2.8 Lemma
.

Any two orientations on a dull graph differ by

•

possibly taking the opposite orientation; and then

•

applying finitely many partial flips.

We aim to show that two Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds are equivariantly diffeomorphic if their oriented dull graphs differ by either of these modifications.
We first observe that this is true
when the dull graphs have opposite orientations.

2.9 Lemma
.

Given a compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold
(M,\omega,\Phi)
, there exists
another compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\Phi})
with isomorphic dull graph
and an orientation-preserving, equivariant diffeomorphism from
M
to
\widetilde{M}
that induces an orientation-reversing isomorphism on the associated dull graphs.

Proof.

Let
(\widetilde{M},\widetilde{\omega},\widetilde{\Phi})
be
(M,-\omega,-\Phi)
and take the identity map.
∎

In the next two sections, we will show that we have an equivariant diffeomorphism between four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds if their oriented dull graphs differ by a partial flip along a free chain.

It is well-known that the diffeomorphism type of a compact,
connected four-dimensional
Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold
M
is determined by
the cohomology ring
H^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
. For the reader’s convenience,
we conclude this section with an explicit proof,
making use of Lemma
2.6
.

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
M
and
\widetilde{M}
be compact, connected, four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds. Then
M
and
\widetilde{M}
are diffeomorphic if and only if the cohomology rings
H^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H^{*}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})
are isomorphic.
In this case, we can choose the diffeomorphism to be orientation-preserving.

Proof.

By
[
15
, Theorem 6.3 and Lemma 6.15]
,
every compact, connected, four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold can either be obtained as a blowup of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
or a blowup of an
S^{2}
bundle over a compact, oriented surface
\Sigma
.
We will proceed by analyzing the possibilities depending on the rank of
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
If
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathbb{Z}}
, then
M
is diffeomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

Proceeding, we may assume that
\rank H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})>1
. Since the blow up of
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
is
an
S^{2}
-bundle over a sphere, this implies that
M
can be obtained as a blowup of
an
S^{2}
-bundle over a compact, oriented surface
\Sigma
. The genus
g
of the surface
is
\frac{1}{2}\rank(H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))
.
If
\rank(H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))=2
, then
M
is diffeomorphic to an
S^{2}
-bundle over
\Sigma
.
There are exactly two oriented
S^{2}
-bundles over a compact, oriented surface
\Sigma
.
If
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
with the intersection form is an even lattice, then
M
must be the
trivial bundle
S^{2}\times\Sigma
, and if
H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is an odd lattice, then
M
must
be the non-trivial bundle.

Finally, the one-fold blowups of the trivial and non-trivial oriented
S^{2}
bundles
over
\Sigma
are diffeomorphic by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism.
This can be seen, for example, by considering a circle action with fixed set
\Sigma\sqcup\Sigma
, blowing up at a fixed point, and applying Lemma
2.6
. Therefore, if
\rank(H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))\geq 3
, then
M
is a blowup of
S^{2}\times\Sigma
at
(\rank(H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))-2)
points.

We have thus shown that
\rank(H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))
,
\rank(H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}))
, and the intersection form on

H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
together determine
M
up to orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. The other implication is immediate: diffeomorphic
manifolds have isomorphic cohomology rings.
∎

3.
Corner manifolds

In this section we use the fact that a free chain of isotropy spheres
can be equivariantly blown down to a free point
to identify
neighborhoods of chains with smooth symplectic toric manifolds. We then explore the symplectic analogue of holomorphic blowup maps on such toric manifolds.
We begin by introducing the symplectic toric manifolds that arise,
which we call corner manifolds.

3.1 Definition
.

A
corner manifold

N
is a connected four-dimensional symplectic manifold
N
with a proper moment map
\Phi\colon N\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that the
moment image
\Delta:=\Phi(N)
is contained in the positive quadrant
\left({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\right)^{2}
and contains all
(w_{1},w_{2})\in\left({\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\right)^{2}
such that
w_{1}+w_{2}
is sufficiently large.

In particular,
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
with the standard action and symplectic form
(S^{1})^{2}\mathbin{\raisebox{-0.5pt}{\reflectbox{\begin{sideways}$\circlearrowleft$\end{sideways}}}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2},\omega_{0})
, and with the homogeneous moment map,
is itself a corner manifold.

The moment image of a corner manifold is a Delzant convex polyhedral set

\Delta=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mid\langle w,v_{i}\rangle\geq a_{i}\},

defines a non-trivial edge of

Here, a rational compact polyhedral set in

is

if the primitive inward-pointing normal vectors to each pair of adjacent edges form a basis of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}
.
For each

set

(3.3)

S_{i}:=\{p\in N\,|\,\langle\Phi(p),v_{i}\rangle=a_{i}\}

The

\displaystyle[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\mapsto\left(\frac{x_{1}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{\alpha_{n-1}}}{|x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots x_{n-1}^{\alpha_{n-1}}|},\frac{x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{\beta_{n-1}}x_{n}}{|x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{\beta_{n-1}}|}\right)

is a well-defined
(S^{1})^{2}
-equivariant symplectomorphism
that intertwines the moment maps.
Here,
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is equipped with the standard symplectic form,
(S^{1})^{2}
-action, and the homogeneous moment map.

Proof.

We will prove the claim by showing that
\psi
can be written as the composition of equivariant symplectomorphisms
that intertwine the moment maps.
If

, the map
\psi
is the identity map and the claim is trivial.
If

, we claim that there is at least one index

so that the set of points

with
\langle\Phi(p),v_{j}\rangle=a_{j}
is a two-sphere in
N
with self intersection
-1
, or equivalently such that
v_{j}=v_{j-1}+v_{j+1}
.
To see this, apply the argument in the proof of Lemma
3.13
to the anti-diagonal circle action on
N
.
Fix one such

and
define

\widecheck{\Delta}=\bigcap_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i=1}}{{i\neq j}}}^{n}\left\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ \Big|\ \langle w,v_{i}\rangle\geq a_{i}\right\}.

The fact that
v_{j}=v_{j-1}+v_{j+1}

guarantees that
\widecheck{\Delta}
is again a Delzant polyhedral set.
Let
\widecheck{N}\simeq M_{\widecheck{\Delta}}
be the associated corner manifold.

Before completing the proof, to
motivate the definition of
\psi
,
we recall the holomorphic blowup map.
Consider the holomorphic map

(3.18)

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\mapsto[x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-2},{x_{j-1}x_{j}},{x_{j}x_{j+1}},x_{j+2},\ldots,x_{n}]

from
N
to
\widecheck{N}
. The space
N
, together with the map (
3.18
), is the complex blowup
of
\widecheck{N}
at the unique point
[y]\in\widecheck{N}
that has
y_{j-1}=y_{j}=0
.
The map (
3.18
) sends
the exceptional sphere
\{[x]\in N\mid x_{j}=0\}
to
[y]
and is a biholomorphism on the complement of these sets.
Applying this blowdown procedure inductively and composing the blowup maps,
we get a holomorphic blowup map from
N
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]\mapsto\Big({x_{1}x_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{\alpha_{n-1}}},{x_{2}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots x_{n-1}^{\beta_{n-1}}x_{n}}\Big),

where
v_{i}=(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
;
moreover, the chain
D\subset N
is the preimage of the origin in
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
.

We now return to
completing the proof by analyzing the analogous symplectic blowup.
We claim that the map

(3.19)

\varpi\colon\left\{[x]\in N\ \Big|\ x_{j}\neq 0\right\}\to\left\{[y]\in\widecheck{N}\ \Big|\ \textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\alpha_{j}|y_{1}|^{2}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}\beta_{j}|y_{n-1}|^{2}>a_{j}\right\}

given by

\textstyle{\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}\right]\mapsto\left[x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-2},\frac{x_{j-1}x_{j}}{|x_{j}|},\frac{x_{j}x_{j+1}}{|x_{j}|},x_{j+2},\ldots,x_{n}\right]}

is a well-defined
(S^{1})^{2}
-equivariant symplectomorphism
that intertwines the moment maps.
To see this, consider the map

\displaystyle\widetilde{\varpi}\colon\left\{x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\ \Big|\ x_{j}\neq 0\right\}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n-1}

\displaystyle(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j-2},\textstyle{\frac{x_{j-1}x_{j}}{|x_{j}|}},\textstyle{\frac{x_{j}x_{j+1}}{|x_{j}|}},x_{j+2},\ldots,x_{n}\right).

Applying equation (
3.5
) to the
level sets
U_{\Delta}
and
U_{\widecheck{\Delta}}
,

\widetilde{\varpi}
maps

\{x\in U_{\Delta}\,|\,x_{j}\neq 0\}

onto

\left\{y\in U_{\widecheck{\Delta}}\ \left|\ \frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}|y_{1}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\beta_{j}|y_{n-1}|^{2}>a_{j}\right.\right\}.

Moreover, since
v_{j}=v_{j-1}+v_{j+1}
, we have that if

satisfy
x_{j}\neq 0\neq x^{\prime}_{j}
, then
x
and
x^{\prime}

are in the same
K
orbit if and only if
\widetilde{\varpi}(x),\widetilde{\varpi}(x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
are
in the same
\widecheck{K}
orbit.
Thus, the map
\varpi
defined in (
3.19
) is a
well-defined,
(S^{1})^{2}
-equivariant bijection that intertwines the moment maps.

Furthermore,
for
[x]\in N
we have

\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}|x_{j+1}|^{2}+\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}|x_{j-1}|^{2}=\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}|x_{j}|^{2}+\varepsilon_{j},

where
\varepsilon_{j}=a_{j}-(a_{j-1}+a_{j+1})
.
Thus

\varpi
has a smooth inverse given by

[y_{1},\dots,y_{n-1}]\mapsto\left[y_{1},\dots,y_{j-1},\big(|y_{j-1}|^{2}+|y_{j}|^{2}-2\varepsilon_{j}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},y_{j},\dots,y_{n-1}\right].

Therefore,
\varpi
is a diffeomorphism.
Finally, by a straightfoward computation in coordinates, the pull-back by the map
\widetilde{\varpi}
of the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{n-1}
is the standard symplectic form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
plus a multiple of
\operatorname{d}(|x_{j}|^{2}-|x_{j-1}|^{2}-|x_{j+1}|^{2}).
Therefore,

We now restrict to
N\smallsetminus D
;
applying this blowdown procedure inductively and composing the symplectic blowup maps, we see that

has the desired properties.
∎

4.
Partial flips and proof of Theorem

In this section we construct

-manifolds whose oriented dull graphs differ by a single partial flip.
We give the construction explicitly

4.1 Definition
.

Let

be a corner manifold whose moment image is the polyhedral set

\Delta=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\mid\langle w,v_{i}\rangle\geq a_{i}\},

\Delta^{\prime}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\left\{w\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\ \Big|\ \langle w,v_{i}^{\prime}\rangle\geq a^{\prime}_{i}\right\},

where

Let

denote the associated

.

the

of the manifold

.

is also the mirror of

and

4.4 Lemma
.

Let

and

and

(4.5)

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(-\overline{x}_{n},\overline{x}_{n-1},\ldots,\overline{x}_{1})

induces a diffeomorphism

and

M_{\Delta^{\prime}}

(\psi^{\prime}\circ F\circ\psi^{-1})(z_{1},z_{2})=(-\overline{z}_{2},\overline{z}_{1})

\psi\colon M_{\Delta}\smallsetminus D\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

\psi^{\prime}\colon M_{\Delta^{\prime}}\smallsetminus D^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

Proof.

By definition, the normal vectors

,

By equations (

4.7 Lemma

Let

be mirror corner manifolds with chains of isotropy spheres

and

Let

Since

Let

(\psi^{\prime}\circ{F}\circ\psi^{-1})(z_{1},z_{2})=(-\overline{z}_{2},\overline{z}_{1})

Moreover, the diffeomorphism

The map

and

Since the triangular neighborhoods

Let
S^{1}

\lambda\cdot(z_{1},z_{2})=(\lambda z_{1},\lambda^{-1}z_{2}).

The family

\varphi_{t}\colon{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{2};\,\,\,\varphi_{t}(z_{1},z_{2})=\Big(\cos{\big(\textstyle{\frac{\pi}{2}}t\big)}z_{1}-\sin{\big(\textstyle{\frac{\pi}{2}}t\big)}\overline{z}_{2},\cos{\big(\textstyle{\frac{\pi}{2}}t\big)}z_{2}+\sin{\big(\textstyle{\frac{\pi}{2}}t\big)}\overline{z}_{1}\Big)

-equivariant diffeomorphisms that satisfies

on

-equivariant diffeomorphism

Since

for all
k
that induces an orientation-preserving isomorphism from
({\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{O}_{k})
to the oriented dull graph associated to
M_{k}
for all
k
.
In particular, there is an orientation-preserving, equivariant diffeomorphism from
M
to
M_{n}

that induces an orientation-preserving isomorphism from
({\mathcal{G}},\mathcal{O}_{n})

to the oriented dull graph associated to
M_{n}
.
The theorem now follows from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The goal in this section is to prove our main result, Theorem
1.1
,
which states that every algebra isomorphism between the equivariant cohomology algebras of
compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds
is induced
by an equivariant diffeomorphism.
In the special case that the algebra isomorphism is orientation preserving and the equivariant cohomology of
the manifolds
vanishes in odd degrees, this Theorem – like Corollary
1.3
– follows nearly immediately from Theorem
1.2
and the results in
[
14
]
. Therefore, we will start by analyzing the provenance of
the odd-degree cohomology.

5.1 Lemma
.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a compact, connected four-dimensional
Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold. Let
R
be the subring of

H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
generated by
H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
. Then

(i)

H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated as a ring by
H_{S^{1}}^{2*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
and
H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.

(ii)

If
H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is non-zero,
then the minimum fixed component
M_{\min}
is a surface of
positive genus and the inclusion map
M_{\min}\to M\times_{S^{1}}ES^{1}
induces an isomorphism
from
R
to
H^{*}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Proof.

We may assume that
H^{2i+1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\neq 0
for some
i
.
The moment map
\Phi
is an equivariantly perfect Morse-Bott function whose
critical set is the fixed set,
and every fixed component has even index and coindex
[
3
,
12
,
18
]
.
Hence, it has a unique local minimum
M_{\min}
and
a unique local maximum
M_{\max}
, and so

H^{1}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})=H^{1}(M_{\max};{\mathbb{Z}}).

Since
M
is
4
-dimensional,

M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}
have dimension
0
or
2
, while other fixed points are isolated.
Since
H^{2*+1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\neq 0
, this implies that
M_{\min}
and
M_{\max}

are surfaces of the same positive genus.

As in
[
23
, §4]
, since
H^{*}(F;{\mathbb{Z}})
is torsion-free for each fixed component
F
, we
can adapt the arguments used to prove
[
22
, Lemma 1.13]
to get the following “canonical classes” in
H^{2*+1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
:

(1)

For each
\alpha\in H^{1}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})
, there exists
\widetilde{\alpha}\in H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

such that
\widetilde{\alpha}|_{M_{\min}}=\alpha
. Here, we use the isomorphism
H^{*}_{S^{1}}(F)=H^{*}(F)\otimes H^{*}_{S^{1}}(\pt)
to
identify
H^{*}(F)
with a subring of

H^{*}_{S^{1}}(F)
for each fixed component
F
.

(2)

For each
\beta\in H^{1}(M_{\max};{\mathbb{Z}})
, there exists
\widetilde{\beta}\in H^{3}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

such that

\widetilde{\beta}|_{M_{\max}}=\beta\cup e_{S^{1}}(\nu_{M_{\max}})

and
\widetilde{\beta}
restricts to zero on every other fixed component. Here,
e_{S^{1}}(\nu_{M_{\max}})
is the equivariant Euler class of the normal bundle to
M_{\max}
.

(3)

There exists
\varepsilon_{M_{\max}}\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M_{\max};{\mathbb{Z}})
such that

\varepsilon_{M_{\max}}|_{M_{\max}}=e_{S^{1}}(\nu_{M_{\max}})
and
\varepsilon_{M_{\max}}
restricts to zero on every other fixed component.

Moreover, as a module over
H^{*}_{S^{1}}(\pt;{\mathbb{Z}})
, the odd-degree cohomology

H^{2*+1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by the canonical classes of types
(
1
) and (
2
); hence,
H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by
H^{2*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
and the canonical classes of types
(
1
) and (
2
).

Applying the same lemma to
-\Phi
, for each
\beta\in H^{1}(M_{\max};{\mathbb{Z}})
there exists
a class
\beta^{\prime}
in
H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
whose restriction to
M_{\max}
is
\beta
.
The type (
2
) canonical class
\widetilde{\beta}\in H^{3}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})

is product of this
class
\beta^{\prime}
of degree
1
with the class
\varepsilon_{M_{\max}}\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
because

\varepsilon_{M_{\max}}
restricts to zero on every non-maximal fixed component and
H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M^{S^{1}};{\mathbb{Z}})
is
injective.
Claim (i) now follows immediately.

The inclusion map
M_{\min}\hookrightarrow M
induces a homomorphism

H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}}).

By the preceding paragraph, this map restricts to an isomorphism from
H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
to
H^{1}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Since
M_{\min}
is a surface of positive genus,
H^{*}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})
is generated by
H^{1}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})
. Hence, since
R\subset H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
is the subring generated by
H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
, the induced homomorphism
R\to H^{*}(M_{\min};{\mathbb{Z}})

is surjective.
To prove that this homomorphism is injective, consider a class

\gamma=\gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\gamma_{n}\in R
that restricts to zero on
M_{\min}
, where
n>1
and
\gamma_{i}\in H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
for all
i
.
To prove that
\gamma
is the zero class, it is enough to prove that its restriction to every fixed component
vanishes.
Since the odd-dimensional equivariant cohomology of a point is trivial, each
\gamma_{i}
restricts to the zero class on every fixed point, and so
\gamma
restricts to a class in ordinary cohomology on each fixed component.
If
n>3
, this implies that
\gamma
vanishes when restricted to any fixed component.
If
n=2
, this implies that
\gamma
vanishes when restricted to any fixed component except possibly the maximum;
moreover,

\int_{M_{\max}}\frac{\gamma|_{M_{\max}}}{e_{S^{1}}(\nu_{M_{\max}})}=0\text{ exactly if }\gamma|_{M_{\max}}=0.

Because the integral of a class of degree
2
over
M
is
zero, the ABBV localization formula
[
3
,
5
]
implies that

\gamma
restricts to zero on the maximum as well. This completes the proof of Claim (ii).
∎

Next, when the odd cohomology is not trivial, that is, when the minimal
fixed surface has positive genus, we need the following stronger version of Theorem
1.2
.

5.2 Lemma
.

Let
M
and
\widetilde{M}
be compact, connected,
four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds with
minimal fixed components that are symplectic surfaces
\Sigma
and
\widetilde{\Sigma}
, respectively, with positive genus.
Let
\psi
be an isomorphism from the dull graph of
M
to the dull graph of
\widetilde{M}

that sends
\Sigma
to
\widetilde{\Sigma}
, and let

\lambda\colon H^{*}(\widetilde{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})

be a ring isomorphism that preserves the orientations induced by the symplectic forms.
Then there exists an orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism
h\colon M\to\widetilde{M}
such that
h
induces
the given isomorphism
\psi
and
(h|_{\Sigma})^{*}=\lambda.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be an isomorphism from the dull graph of
M
to that of
\widetilde{M}
.
By Theorem
1.2
, there exists an orientation-preserving
equivariant diffeomorphism
f\colon M\to\widetilde{M}
that induces
the given isomorphism
\psi
.

The surface
\Sigma
is a minimal fixed component. Hence, if we use the symplectic orientations on
\Sigma
and
M

to orient the normal bundle
\nu(\Sigma)
to
\Sigma
, then
S^{1}
acts on
\nu(\Sigma)_{p}
with weight
+1
for all
p\in\Sigma
;
a similar statement applies to
\widetilde{\Sigma}
.
Therefore, since
f
is an orientation preserving equivariant diffeomorphism taking
\Sigma
to
\widetilde{\Sigma}
,
the pull-back map in cohomology

(f|_{\Sigma})^{*}\colon H^{*}(\widetilde{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})

preserves the symplectic orientations.
By
[
9
, Theorems 1.13 and 6.4]
, there exists an orientation preserving diffeomorphism
g\colon\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\widetilde{\Sigma}
such that
g^{*}=\lambda^{-1}\circ(f|_{\Sigma})^{*}.

Then by Theorem
2.4
, there exists an equivariant symplectomorphism

\widehat{g}\colon\widetilde{M}\to\widetilde{M}
that induces the identity map on the dull graph such that the restriction
\widehat{g}|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}

is isotopic to
g
.
Finally, the map
h=\widehat{g}^{-1}\circ f\colon M\to\widetilde{M}
is the desired equivariant diffeomorphism.
∎

A final ingredient is to construct explicitly some
orientation-reversing diffeomorphisms.

5.3 Lemma
.

Let
(M,\omega,\Phi)
be a compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifold with
b_{2}(M)=2
.
Then there exists an orientation-reversing
S^{1}
-equivariant diffeomorphism from
M
to itself.

Proof.

Assume first that either every fixed point is isolated or
M
has exactly one fixed surface.
Then by Karshon’s classification
[
15
]
,
M
is a Hirzebruch surface, and the circle action is
the restriction of a subgroup of the natural
(S^{1})^{2}
action.
Specifically, we can identify
M
with

({\mathbb{C}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\})\times_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},

where
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
acts diagonally on
{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, and acts on
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by
\lambda\cdot[w_{1},w_{2}]=[\lambda^{N}w_{1},w_{2}]
for some
N\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The compact torus
(S^{1})^{2}
acts on
M
by

(\alpha,\beta)\cdot[z_{1},z_{2};w_{1},w_{2}]=[\alpha z_{1},z_{2};\beta w_{1},w_{2}].

Hence the map from
M
to itself sending
[z_{1},z_{2};w_{1},w_{2}]
to
[z_{1},z_{2};\overline{w_{2}},\overline{w_{1}}]
is
an orientation-reversing
(S^{1})^{2}
-equivariant diffeomorphism.

Now assume instead that
M
has two fixed surfaces. Then by Karshon’s classification
M
is a ruled
S^{1}
-manifold.
More specifically, we may identify
M
with

M:=E\times_{{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}}{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},

where
E\to\Sigma
is a principal
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
bundle over a compact oriented surface
\Sigma
and

{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by
\lambda\cdot[w_{1},w_{2}]=[\lambda w_{1},w_{2}]
.
The circle
S^{1}
acts on
M
by
\alpha\cdot[e;w_{1},w_{2}]=[e;\alpha w_{1},w_{2}].

Hence the map from
M
to itself sending
[e;w_{1},w_{2}]
to
[e;\overline{w_{2}},\overline{w_{1}}]
is
an orientation-reversing
(S^{1})
-equivariant diffeomorphism.
∎

We now have all the ingredients needed to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
M
and
\widetilde{M}
be compact, connected four-dimensional Hamiltonian
S^{1}
-manifolds
and let

\Lambda\colon H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{S^{1}}^{*}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})

be an isomorphism of algebras. We want to show that
there exists an equivariant diffeomorphism
from
\widetilde{M}
to
M

that induces the algebra isomorphism
\Lambda
.

We begin with the case when
\Lambda

is orientation-preserving.
By
[
14
, Corollary 7.23]
,
there
exists an isomorphism
\psi
from the dull graph of
\widetilde{M}
to the dull graph of
M
such that
\Lambda(\varepsilon_{\psi(\widetilde{F})})=\varepsilon_{\widetilde{F}}
for each fixed component
\widetilde{F}\subset\widetilde{M}^{S^{1}}
, where the classes
\varepsilon_{\widetilde{F}}
are defined as follows:
Given a fixed component
\widetilde{F}\subset\widetilde{M}^{S^{1}}\!\!,

let
\varepsilon_{\widetilde{F}}
denote the class
whose restriction to
\widetilde{F}
is the equivariant Euler class
e_{S^{1}}(\nu_{\widetilde{F}})
of the normal bundle to
\widetilde{F}
, and whose restriction to every other fixed component is
0
.
We define
\varepsilon_{F}
for a fixed component
F\subset M^{S^{1}}
analogously.
Here, if
\widetilde{F}=\{p\}
is an isolated fixed point, we take the natural (symplectic) orientation on
\nu(\widetilde{F})=T_{p}\widetilde{M}
, while if
\widetilde{F}
is a surface, we orient the normal bundle
\nu(\widetilde{F})
to
\widetilde{F}
so that
S^{1}
acts on
\nu(\widetilde{F})_{p}
with weight
+1
for all
p\in\widetilde{F}
.
Moreover, if the minimal fixed component of
\widetilde{M}
is a surface
\widetilde{\Sigma}
then
we may assume that
\psi(\widetilde{\Sigma})
is a minimal fixed
surface, by replacing
\omega
by
-\omega
if necessary.

Let’s now also assume that
H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})=0
. By Theorem
1.2
there exists an orientation-preserving
S^{1}
-equivariant diffeomorphism
h\colon\widetilde{M}\to M
inducing the isomorphism
\psi
of dull graphs; in particular,
h(\widetilde{F})=\psi(\widetilde{F})
for all fixed components
\widetilde{F}\subset\widetilde{M}^{S^{1}}
.
Since
h
is an orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism,
this implies that

h^{*}(\varepsilon_{\psi(\widetilde{F})})=\varepsilon_{\widetilde{F}}=\Lambda(\varepsilon_{\psi(\widetilde{F})})

for all fixed components
\widetilde{F}\subset\widetilde{M}^{S^{1}}
. Hence, by the uniqueness claim in the first statement in
[
14
, Theorem 7.22]
, we must have

h^{*}\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{2*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})}=\Lambda\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{2*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})}.

Therefore, since
H^{2*+1}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})=0
by Lemma
5.1
(i),
we may conclude that
h^{*}=\Lambda
.

Now suppose instead that
H^{1}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\neq 0
, and let

R\subset H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{R}\subset H^{*}_{S^{1}}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})

be the subrings
generated by elements of degree
1
. Then, by
Lemma
5.1
(ii), the minimal fixed components of
M
and
\widetilde{M}
are
positive genus surfaces
\Sigma
and
\widetilde{\Sigma}
, respectively.
Moreover, the restriction maps

H_{S^{1}}^{*}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})\quad\mbox{and}\quad H_{S^{1}}^{*}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})\to H^{*}(\widetilde{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}})

restrict to isomorphisms between
R
and
H^{*}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})

and between
\widetilde{R}
and
H^{*}(\widetilde{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}})
, respectively.
Since

\Lambda
restricts to an isomorphism from
R
to
\widetilde{R}
, this implies that

\Lambda
induces an isomorphism in ordinary cohomology

\lambda\colon H^{*}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{*}(\widetilde{\Sigma};{\mathbb{Z}})

such that

\lambda(\alpha|_{\Sigma})=\Lambda(\alpha)|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}

for all
\alpha\in R\subset H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Moreover, since
\Lambda
is orientation-preserving and

\Lambda(\varepsilon_{\Sigma})=\varepsilon_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
,

(5.4)

\int_{M}\varepsilon_{\Sigma}\,\alpha=\int_{\widetilde{M}}\varepsilon_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\,\Lambda(\alpha)

for all
\alpha\in H_{S^{1}}^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
.
Since
\Sigma
is the minimal fixed component of
M
, the symplectic orientation on
\Sigma
and the orientation on
\nu(\Sigma)
described in the first paragraph combine
to give the natural (symplectic) orientation on
TM|_{\Sigma}
. A similar claim applies to
\widetilde{\Sigma}\subset\widetilde{M}.

Hence, by (
5.4
), the definition of
\varepsilon_{\Sigma}
, and the Atiyah-Bott and Berline-Vergne localization theorem,

\int_{\Sigma}\alpha|_{\Sigma}=\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\Lambda(\alpha)|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}

for all
\alpha\in H^{2}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}}).

Since the restriction map from
R\subset H^{*}_{S^{1}}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})
to
H^{*}(\Sigma,{\mathbb{Z}})
is surjective, this implies that

\int_{\Sigma}\beta=\int_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\lambda(\beta)

for any
\beta\in H^{2}(\Sigma;{\mathbb{Z}})
; in other words,
\lambda
is orientation preserving.
Hence, by Lemma
5.2
, there exists an
orientation-preserving equivariant diffeomorphism
h\colon\widetilde{M}\to{M}
inducing
the isomorphism of dull graphs
\psi
and such that
(h|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}})^{*}=\lambda.

As in the previous paragraph, we can now use
[
14
, Theorem 7.22]

to deduce from the fact that
the equivariant diffeomorphism
h
induces the isomorphism
\psi

that

h^{*}\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{2*}(M)}=\Lambda\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{2*}(M)}
.
Since
(h|_{\widetilde{\Sigma}})^{*}=\lambda
, Lemma
5.1
(ii) implies that
h^{*}\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M)}=\Lambda\big|_{H_{S^{1}}^{1}(M)}
.
By Lemma
5.1
(i), together these imply that
h^{*}=\Lambda.

Finally, we turn to the case that
\Lambda
is orientation-reversing. By
[
14
, Proposition 7.26]
, this implies that
\dim H^{2}(M;{\mathbb{Z}})=\dim H^{2}(\widetilde{M};{\mathbb{Z}})=2
. Therefore, by Lemma
5.3
,
there is an orientation-reversing equivariant diffeomorphism
g
from
M
to itself. By definition,
g^{*}\circ\Lambda
is orientation-preserving. So by the previous paragraphs there exists an equivariant diffeomorphism
h\colon\widetilde{M}\to{M}
such that
h^{*}=g^{*}\circ\Lambda
. Hence,
(g^{-1}\circ h)^{*}=\Lambda
.
∎

Collective Interference Phonon Spin Manifested in Infrared Circular Dichroism
Yizhou Liu, Yu-Tao Tan, Dapeng Liu, Jie Ren

Abstract
The classical field description of “phonon spin” relies on the invariance of a continuous elastic field under infinitesimal rotation. However, a local “medium element” in the continuous field may contain large numbers of vibrational particles at microscopic level, like for complex lattices with many atoms in a unit cell. We find this causes the phonon spin in real materials no longer a simple sum of each atom rotation, but a collective interference of many particles, since phonons are phase-coherent vibrational modes across unit cells. We demonstrate the many-particle-interference phonon spin manifested as the dipole moment rotating (DMR) of charge-polarized unit cell, by deriving the infrared circular dichroism (ICD) with phonon-photon interaction in complex lattices. We compare the DMR with the local atom rotation without interference, and exemplify their distinct ICD spectrum in a chiral lattice model and two realistic chiral materials. Detectable ICD measurements are proposed in α \alpha -quartz with Weyl phonon at Gamma point. Our study underlies the important role of many-particle-interference and uncovers a deeper insight of phonon spin in real materials with complex lattices.

For the description of phonon spin in solids, two different viewpoints are usually adopted: One is based on continuous elastic field theory; The other is based on discrete lattice models. However, a significant gap exists between these two different descriptions.
On one hand, the continuous elastic field theory offers a natural tool to introduce the elastic spin based on the infinitesimal rotational transformation invariance of the elastic Lagrangian density
Soper 1976
;
Ren 2022
;
Xin et al. 2025
. The derived elastic spin is
\bm{S}\propto\bm{u}\times\dot{\bm{u}}
with
\bm{u}
being the displacement field vector of a “medium element”, a macroscopically infinitesimal part of the medium. The macroscopically infinitesimal “medium element” can yet contain vast number of atoms at microscopic level, with the field vector
\bm{u}=\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i}\bm{u}_{i}
(
N
refers to the number of atoms). Thus the spin
\bm{S}\propto\sum_{i,j}\bm{u}_{i}\times\dot{\bm{u}}_{j}=\sum_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\times\dot{\bm{u}}_{i}+\sum_{i\neq j}\bm{u}_{i}\times\dot{\bm{u}}_{j}
, in addition to the simple sum of each atom rotation, naturally contains crossing interference terms between atoms
i
and
j
.

On the other hand, the continuous rotational symmetry, crucial for defining SAM, is broken in discrete lattice models, down to the discrete symmetries
C_{n}
(
n\in\{2,3,4,6\}
). Discrete
C_{n}
symmetries are preserved only at certain high-symmetry momenta of Brillouin zone (BZ) and its eigenvalues are used to define quantized pseudo AM with undetermined multiples of
n

Zhang and Niu 2015
;
Zhang and Murakami 2022
;
Zhang et al. 2025
. Otherwise, the phonon AM is actually not well-defined in the discrete lattice modes. As such, people just usually took the simple summation of each atom rotation
\bm{S}\propto\sum_{i}\bm{u}_{i}\times\dot{\bm{u}}_{i}
by hand as the phonon spin AM in discrete lattices and real material calculations.

However, this ad hoc and somehow arbitrary definition of phonon spin lacks both theoretical foundation and justification.
First, although the definition may be applied for the particle picture, its extension to phonon is questionable, because phonon is not a particle in real space but a collective vibration mode involving many particles. Second, as a collective motion, the phonon (the mode of many particle vibrations) is phase coherent, and this phase-coherent collective vibrations must bring interference among many particles.
Therefore, many crucial questions arise: what is the real physics picture of phonon spin in complex lattices? Does it contain the collective interference across particles at different sublattices? If so, what is the manifestation of the many-particle-interference phonon spin? Moreover, one should propose some detectable physics effect, by utilizing the interaction of phonon to the multiple physics field.

In this Letter, we uncover the many-particle interference picture of phonon spin in crystalline solids that can be viewed as the collective motion of many particles in the unit cell and measured by the infrared circular dichroism (ICD).
Theoretically, we revisit the phonon-photon interaction under circularly polarized (CP) infrared light excitation by a quantum perturbation method. We find the transition rates of photon absorption(emission) induced phonon creation(annihilation) depends on a physical quantity associated with the rotation of unit cell’s dipole moment, named dipole moment rotating (DMR), which shares the similar form as the spin AM of the rotating displacement polarization of the unit cell’s mass center. We first implement the calculation of DMR on a toy chiral model with three-fold screw rotation symmetry, to show that different charge distribution will cause different DMR distribution on the dispersion relation, leading to different selection rule of light.
We then implement first-principle calculation on real chiral materials tellurium(Te) and
\alpha
-quartz with chirality-induced DMR mapping on dispersion relations, which demonstrates the consistency of theoretical model and real materials. Finally, we propose a detectable ICD measurement based on DMR spectrum to demonstrate the many-particle-interference phonon spin in
\alpha
-quartz.

Circularly dichroism and dipole moment rotating (DMR).

Generally, phonon spin is able to couple with spin of other (quasi) particles, which gives rise to AM transfer processes. In the context of phonon-photon interaction, CP phonon modes can couple with external CP electromagnetic fields

For a deeper understanding of the physical insight of DMR in complex lattice, we exemplify three cases in diatomic lattice in Fig.
1

This is an ordinary case that nonlocal effect vanishes since there is no relative rotation between two atoms. The total DMR will vanish when two atoms’ motions are in phase opposition, shown as the second case in Fig.

In conclusion, we have demonstrated the significant role of collective motion of many particles in phonon spin of complex lattice, where the nonlocal crossing interference exhibits measurable effects. In particular, we have formulated the CP infrared photon-phonon process by quantum perturbation theory and obtained the ICD specturm in terms of a physical quantity DMR arising from the rotating motion of unit cell’s dipole moment. This DMR is strongly correlated with the nonlocal crossing interference phonon spin and has an pivotal impact on the measurable ICD spectrum in complex lattices.
Results have been exemplified in both theoretical chiral structures and realistic chiral materials.

Our study uncovers a deep insight of collective interference phonon spin in real materials with complex lattices. Although we focus on infrared absorption process at present, similar effects is expected to apply for the Raman scattering in chiral materials. Moreover, besides the spin AM transfer in photon-phonon process, the spin degree of freedom of phonon is able to interact with the spin of many other (quasi) particles as well, through such as magnon-phonon interaction
Holanda et al. 2018
;
Streib et al. 2018
;
Rückriegel and Duine 2020
;
Tauchert et al. 2022
and electron-phonon interaction
Hamada and Murakami 2020
;
Fransson 2023
;
Ohe et al. 2024
;
Funato et al. 2024
, which would exhibit more plentiful phenomena. The spin phononics with coupling to multiple degrees of freedom, such as electron spin, photon spin, magnon spin, and so on, can provide opportunity to develop solid-based devices for more promising functionalities.

M_{\alpha}
is the atomic mass, and
K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}=\frac{\partial^{2}U}{\partial u_{\mu\alpha i}\partial u_{\nu\beta j}}|_{\bm{u}=0}
is the second-order force constants at the equilibrium position. The lattice translation symmetry requires the force constants between different atoms only depending on their relative displacement,
i.e.

K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}=K_{\alpha i,\beta j}(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})
.

The lattice vibrational equation of motion can be derived by the Lagrangian equation
\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{u}_{\mu\alpha i}}\right)-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial u_{\mu\alpha i}}=0
as

M_{\alpha}\ddot{u}_{\mu\alpha i}=-\sum_{\nu\beta j}K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}u_{\nu\beta j}.

(S2)

Consider plane-wave solution

u_{\mu\alpha i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}}}\varepsilon^{\alpha i}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}

(S3)

we can derive the basic equation of lattice vibrations as

\begin{split}&D_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{k}}=\omega^{2}_{\xi\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{k}},\\
&(D_{\bm{k}})_{\alpha i,\beta j}=\sum_{\nu}\frac{K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})}.\end{split}

(S4)

D_{\bm{k}}
is the dynamical matrix and
\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{k}}
is eigenvector of
\xi
-th branch at Bloch wave vector
\bm{k}
.

Phonon.
In the language of second quantization, the lattice vibration
u_{\mu\alpha i}
is quantized as

\hat{u}_{\mu\alpha i}=\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2NM_{\alpha}\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}}}\left[\varepsilon^{\alpha i}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}+(\varepsilon^{\alpha i}_{\xi\bm{k}})^{*}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{-i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}\right].

(S5)

It is easy to check that the operators
\hat{u}_{\mu\alpha i}
satisfies the canonical commutation relation

\left[\hat{u}_{\mu\alpha i},\hat{p}_{\nu\beta j}\right]=\left[\hat{u}_{\mu\alpha i},M_{\beta}\hat{\dot{u}}_{\nu\beta j}\right]=i\hbar\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{ij}

(S6)

as long as the operators
\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}
,
\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}
satisfy the bosonic commutation relations

[\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}},\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}]=[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}]=0,~~~~~[\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}]=\delta_{\xi\xi^{\prime}}\delta_{\bm{kk}^{\prime}}.

(S7)

Substitute the second-quantization form of
u_{\mu\alpha i}
into the lattice vibrational Hamiltonian
\hat{H}=T+V
, we can get the familiar expression of phononic Hamiltonian in the second-quantization form as

H=\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}(\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}})=\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}+1/2).

(S8)

Derivation of Vibrational Circular
Dichroism (VCD).
Based on the notations introduced above, we are ready to derive the expression of VCD and discuss its relation with phonon spin. According to the second order quantum perturbation, so-called Fermi’s golden rule, the absorption rate of circularly polarized (CP) light is determined as

I_{L/R}=\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{i,f}p_{i}\left|\langle\Phi_{f}|H^{\prime}_{L/R}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\right|^{2}\delta(E_{f}-E_{i}\pm\hbar\omega).

(S9)

|\Phi_{i}\rangle
and
|\Phi_{f}\rangle
refer to the initial and final states, respectively, and
p_{i}
is the probability of initial state.
H^{\prime}_{L/R}
is the light-phonon interaction Hamiltonian for left- or right- handed light. The
\delta
function
\delta(E_{f}-E_{i}\pm\hbar\omega)
reflects the conservation of total energy, with the “
-
” and “
+
” signs correspond to photon absorption and emission precesses, respectively.

In the electric dipole approximation, the experssion of
H^{\prime}
can be expressed as

H^{\prime}_{L/R}=\sum_{\mu\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{E},

(S10)

where
Q_{\alpha}
is the electrical charge of
\alpha
-th ion, and
\bm{E}
is the electric field of the light. For CP light,

\bm{E}=\bm{E}_{\pm}=\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_{1}\pm i\bm{e}_{2})

(S11)

with “
+
” and “
-
” signs correspond to right- and left- handed cases, respectively.
\bm{e}_{1}
and
\bm{e}_{2}
are two unit vectors perpendicular to the light propagating direction
\bm{q}
satisfying
\bm{e}_{1}\times\bm{e}_{2}=\bm{e_{q}}
(
\bm{e_{q}}
is the unit wave vector
\bm{q}
of the light). For left-handed CP light,

\begin{split}|\langle\Phi_{f}|H^{\prime}_{L}|\Phi_{i}\rangle|^{2}=&\langle\Phi_{f}|\sum_{\mu\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{E}_{-}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\beta}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{E}_{+}|\Phi_{f}\rangle\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}\frac{1}{2}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\left[(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})-i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})\right][(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})+i(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})],\end{split}

(S12)

where
\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}=\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{\bm{u}}_{\mu\alpha}|\Phi_{i}\rangle
and
\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}=\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{\bm{u}}_{\nu\beta}|\Phi_{f}\rangle
. For right-handed case,

\begin{split}|\langle\Phi_{f}|H^{\prime}_{R}|\Phi_{i}\rangle|^{2}=&\langle\Phi_{f}|\sum_{\mu\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{E}_{+}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\beta}\hat{\bm{u}}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{E}_{-}|\Phi_{f}\rangle\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}\frac{1}{2}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\left[(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})+i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})\right][(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})-i(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})].\end{split}

(S13)

Therefore, the difference between left- and right- handed CP light is

\begin{split}|\langle\Phi_{f}|H^{\prime}_{L}|\Phi_{i}\rangle|^{2}-|\langle\Phi_{f}|H^{\prime}_{R}|\Phi_{i}\rangle|^{2}=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\cdot[i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})-i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{2})(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})]\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}[\underbrace{i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}-i\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}(\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}\cdot\bm{e}_{1})}_{(\bm{A}\cdot\bm{C})\bm{B}-\bm{A}(\bm{B}\cdot\bm{C})=(\bm{A}\times\bm{B})\times\bm{C}}]\cdot\bm{e}_{2}\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\cdot\underbrace{[i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta})\times\bm{e}_{1}]\cdot\bm{e}_{2}}_{(\bm{A}\times\bm{B})\cdot\bm{C}=\bm{A}\cdot(\bm{B}\times\bm{C})}\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\cdot i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta})\cdot(\bm{e}_{1}\times\bm{e}_{2})\\
=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\cdot i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta})\cdot\bm{e_{q}}\end{split}

(S14)

where two identities
(\bm{A}\times\bm{B})\times\bm{C}=(\bm{A}\cdot\bm{C})\bm{B}-\bm{A}(\bm{B}\cdot\bm{C})
and
(\bm{A}\times\bm{B})\cdot\bm{C}=\bm{A}\cdot(\bm{B}\times\bm{C})
are used in the above derivation.

Now we calculate the quantitiy
i(\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta})
by using Eq. (
S5
). We rewrite Eq. (
S5
) in a vector form as:

\hat{\bm{u}}_{\mu\alpha}=\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2NM_{\alpha}\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}}}\left[\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}+\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}a^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{-i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}\right].

(S15)

Here,
\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}=\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha x}_{\xi\bm{k}},\varepsilon^{\alpha y}_{\xi\bm{k}},\varepsilon^{\alpha z}_{\xi\bm{k}}\right)
is the component of eigenvector
\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{k}}
projected onto
\alpha
-th ion.
\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}
and
\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}
can be expressed as

\begin{split}\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}=&\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2NM_{\alpha}\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}}}\left[\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle e^{i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}+\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle e^{-i(\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}t)}\right],\\
\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}=&\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2NM_{\beta}\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}}\left[\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{i(\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}t)}+\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{-i(\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}t)}\right].\end{split}

(S16)

Therefore,
i\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}
can be calculated as

\begin{split}i\bm{u}^{fi}_{\mu\alpha}\times\bm{u}^{if}_{\nu\beta}=&\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\frac{\hbar}{2N\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}}\times\\
&\left\{\underbrace{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}+\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}+\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}}_{\textrm{①}}\right.\\
&\left.+\underbrace{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{-i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}+\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}+\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}}_{\textrm{②}}\right.\\
&\left.+\underbrace{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}}_{\textrm{③}}\right.\\
&\left.+\underbrace{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{-i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}}_{\textrm{④}}\right\}\end{split}

(S17)

For ① in Eq. (
S17
), a nonzero
\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle
requires the initial states
|\Phi_{i}\rangle
to annihilate a
\xi\bm{k}
phonon to get the final state
|\Phi_{f}\rangle
, and the final state to annihilate a
\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}
phonon back to initial state, which is impossible. Therefore ① is zero. Similarly, ② is also zero. ③ and ④ can be nonzero only if
\xi^{\prime}=\xi
,
\bm{k}^{\prime}=\bm{k}
.

Combining Eqs. (
S9
), (
S14
), and (
S17
), the whole expression of VCD can be expressed as

\begin{split}I_{L}-I_{R}=&\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{i,f}p_{i}\delta(E_{f}-E_{i}\pm\hbar\omega)\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\frac{\hbar}{2N\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}}}\\
&\left\{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}\right.\\
&\left.+i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle e^{-i[\bm{k}\cdot\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{k}^{\prime}\cdot\bm{R}_{\nu}-(\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\omega_{\xi^{\prime}\bm{k}^{\prime}})t]}\right\}\\
=&\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\frac{\hbar}{2N\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\frac{1}{\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}}\\
&\sum_{i,f}p_{i}\left\{\underbrace{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle\delta(E_{f}-E_{i}+\hbar\omega)e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})}}_{\textrm{CP light emission \& phonon absorption}}\right.\\
&\left.~~~~~~~\underbrace{+i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{k}}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle\delta(E_{f}-E_{i}-\hbar\omega)e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})}}_{\textrm{CP light absorption \& phonon emission}}\right\}\end{split}

(S18)

In Eq. (
S18
), the last two lines correspond to the processes of phonon absorption (CP light emission) and phonon emission (CP light absorption), respectively. The energy difference between final and initial states is
\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}
,
i.e.

E_{f}-E_{i}=\pm\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}
with the “
+
” and “
-
” signs correspond to phonon emission and absorption, respectively. The
\delta
function become
\delta(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\hbar\omega)
independent of the states
|\Phi_{i}\rangle
and
|\Phi_{f}\rangle
, thus can be taken out of the summation
\sum_{i,f}
.

At this moment, the only things remain unsolved are two summations:

\begin{split}\sum_{i,f}p_{i}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle=&\sum_{i}p_{i}\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle=\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}\rangle=f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}),\\
\sum_{i,f}p_{i}\langle\Phi_{f}|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{f}\rangle=&\sum_{i}p_{i}\langle\Phi_{i}|\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}|\Phi_{i}\rangle=\langle\hat{a}_{\xi\bm{k}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\xi\bm{k}}\rangle=1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}),\end{split}

(S19)

where
f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}})
is the occupation number of phonon at
\xi\bm{k}
. At thermal equilibrium,
f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}})=\left(e^{\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}/k_{B}T}-1\right)^{-1}
is the Bose distribution function. Therefore, the final expression of VCD is

\begin{split}I_{L}-I_{R}=&\frac{2\pi}{\hbar}\sum_{\mu\alpha}\sum_{\nu\beta}Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}|E_{0}|^{2}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\sum_{\xi\bm{k}}\frac{\hbar}{2N\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\delta(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}}-\hbar\omega)\\
&\left\{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})}f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}})+i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{k}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot(\bm{R}_{\mu}-\bm{R}_{\nu})}[1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{k}})]\right\}\\
=&N\frac{\pi|E_{0}|^{2}}{\hbar}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\sum_{\xi}\frac{\delta(\omega-\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})}{\omega_{\xi\bm{0}}}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\left\{i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha}_{\xi\bm{0}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta*}_{\xi\bm{0}}f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})+i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{0}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{0}}[1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})]\right\}.\end{split}

(S20)

The VCD of one single unit cell is

\begin{split}\frac{I_{L}-I_{R}}{N}=&\frac{\pi|E_{0}|^{2}}{\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}}}\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\sum_{n}\delta(\omega-\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\left\{-i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{0}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{0}}f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})+i\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{0}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{0}}[1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})]\right\}\\
=&\frac{\pi|E_{0}|^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\sum_{\xi}\delta(\omega-\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})\left\{\underbrace{f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{0}}}_{\textrm{CP light emission}}\underbrace{-[1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{0}})]\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{0}}}_{\textrm{CP light absorption}}\right\},\end{split}

(S21)

where
\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{k}}=\textrm{Im}[\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}(\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{k}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{k}})]
. By considering (
S3
), we can rearrange the formula of
\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{k}}
as

\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{k}}=\textrm{Im}[\sum_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\bm{u}_{\xi\bm{k}}^{\alpha*}\times\sum_{\beta}Q_{\beta}\bm{u}_{\xi\bm{k}}^{\beta*}]=\textrm{Im}[\bm{P}_{\xi\bm{k}}^{*}\times\bm{P}_{\xi\bm{k}}],

(S22)

with
\bm{P}_{\xi\bm{k}}=\sum_{\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\bm{u}_{\xi\bm{k}}
being the dipole moment of unit cell, so that
\bm{\mathcal{R}}
describe the rotating motion of dipole moment, which is called as rotational dipole moment (RDM).

The final result in Eq. (
S21
) is derived in the case of uniform light field
i.e.
the long wave length limit of light. In the presence of time-reversal symmetry (TRS), the net phonon spin at
\bm{k}=\bm{0}
is zero. By breaking the TRS, the VCD can be nonzero and depends on the direction of light (
\bm{e_{q}}
)
i.e.
by reversing the light direction, the VCD is also reversed. In the infrared range, the light wave length is approximately
\mu
m order which is much larger than the lattice constant. The final result can also be easily generalized to the case of finite wave vector of light,
i.e.

\bm{E}(\bm{r})=\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-i\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}}(\bm{e}_{1}\pm i\bm{e}_{2}).

(S23)

In this case, the final result can be got by replacing
\bm{0}
by
\bm{q}
in Eq. (
S21
) as

\frac{\Delta I}{N}=\frac{\pi|E_{0}|^{2}}{\hbar\omega}\sum_{\xi}\delta(\omega-\omega_{\xi\bm{q}})\left\{\underbrace{f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{q}})\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{q}}}_{\textrm{CP light emission}}\underbrace{-[1+f(\hbar\omega_{\xi\bm{q}})]\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{\mathcal{R}}_{\xi\bm{q}}}_{\textrm{CP light absorption}}\right\}

(S24)

Equation (
S24
) together with (
S22
)
are the final result of VCD.
\bm{q}
and
\omega
are the wave vector and angular frequency of light, respectively.

Discussions.
(i) According to Eq. (
S24
), the CP light emission is proportional to the phonon occupation number
N_{\xi\bm{q}}
whereas the CP light absorption is porportional to
1+N_{\xi\bm{q}}
, which reflects the fact that light absorption process is more likely to occur than light emission because the latter requires higher phonon occupation number. At zero temperature, only CP light absorption process exist.

(ii) According to Eq. (
S24
), the final expression of VCD is determined by
\sum_{\alpha\beta}\frac{Q_{\alpha}Q_{\beta}}{\sqrt{M_{\alpha}M_{\beta}}}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\textrm{i}\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}^{\alpha}*\times\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}^{\beta}

which can be rewritten into a compact form as
\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\langle\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}|\hat{\bm{S}}|\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}\rangle
with the matrix form of
\hat{\bm{S}}=\hat{O}\otimes\hat{\bm{s}}
where

\hat{O}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{Q^{2}_{1}}{M_{1}}&\frac{Q_{1}Q_{2}}{\sqrt{M_{1}M_{2}}}&\cdots&\frac{Q_{1}Q_{r}}{\sqrt{M_{1}M_{r}}}\\
\frac{Q_{2}Q_{1}}{\sqrt{M_{2}M_{1}}}&\frac{Q^{2}_{2}}{M_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{Q_{2}Q_{r}}{\sqrt{M_{2}M_{r}}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{Q_{r}Q_{1}}{\sqrt{M_{r}M_{1}}}&\frac{Q_{r}Q_{2}}{\sqrt{M_{r}M_{2}}}&\cdots&\frac{Q^{2}_{r}}{M_{r}}\end{array}\right)

(S25)

and
\hat{\bm{s}}=(\hat{s}_{x},\hat{s}_{y},\hat{s}_{z})
is the ordinary spin-1 operator,

\hat{s}_{x}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&-i\\
0&i&0\end{array}\right),~~~~~~~\hat{s}_{y}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&i\\
0&0&0\\
-i&0&0\end{array}\right),~~~~~~~\hat{s}_{z}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&-i&0\\
i&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right).

(S26)

The value of
\langle\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}|\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\hat{\bm{S}}|\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}\rangle
can be viewed as the expectation value of operator
\hat{\bm{S}}
for the polarization vector
|\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}\rangle
along the direction of
\bm{e_{q}}
. We first analyze the eigenvalues of
\hat{\bm{S}}
. Since
\hat{\bm{S}}=\hat{O}\otimes\hat{\bm{s}}
, its eigenvalue can be decomposed as the product of
\hat{O}
and
\hat{\bm{s}}
. The eigenvalues of
\hat{\bm{s}}
are
1,0,-1
along arbitrary direction. The eigenvalue of
\hat{O}
can be determined as follows. The matrix
\hat{O}
is a rank-1 matrix because it can be rewritten as

\hat{O}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Q_{1}/\sqrt{M_{1}}\\
Q_{2}/\sqrt{M_{2}}\\
\vdots\\
Q_{r}/\sqrt{M_{r}}\end{array}\right)\left(\frac{Q_{1}}{\sqrt{M_{1}}},\frac{Q_{2}}{\sqrt{M_{2}}},\cdots,\frac{Q_{r}}{\sqrt{M_{r}}}\right)=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|

(S27)

with
|\psi\rangle=(Q_{1}/\sqrt{M_{1}},Q_{2}/\sqrt{M_{2}},\cdots,Q_{r}/\sqrt{M_{r}})^{T}
,
T
stands for matrix transpose. The rank-1 property means that the
r\times r
matrix
\hat{O}
has at least
r-1
zero eigenvalues. The remaining nonzero eigenvalue can be derived by noticing that

\hat{O}|\psi\rangle=\langle\psi|\psi\rangle|\psi\rangle=\left(\sum_{\alpha}\frac{Q^{2}_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}\right)|\psi\rangle.

(S28)

The eigenvectors corresponding to the zero eigenvalue are those vectors
|\phi\rangle
perpendicular to
|\psi\rangle
, so that
\hat{O}|\phi\rangle=0
. Those vectors can be chosen as:

|\phi_{1}\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Q_{2}/\sqrt{M_{2}}\\
-Q_{1}/\sqrt{M_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
0\end{array}\right),~~~~~|\phi_{2}\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Q_{3}/\sqrt{M_{3}}\\
0\\
-Q_{1}/\sqrt{M_{1}}\\
\vdots\end{array}\right),\cdots,|\phi_{r-1}\rangle=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}Q_{r}/\sqrt{M_{r}}\\
0\\
\vdots\\
-Q_{1}/\sqrt{M_{1}}\end{array}\right).

(S29)

The above eigenvectors
|\phi_{1}\rangle
,
|\phi_{2}\rangle
, …, and
|\phi_{r-1}\rangle
are not orthogonal to each other. They can be orthogonalized by using Gram-Schmidt process.

Now we return to the expectation value of
\hat{\bm{S}}
. Since the eigenvalues of
\hat{\bm{S}}=\hat{O}\otimes\hat{\bm{s}}
are the product of
\hat{O}
and
\hat{\bm{s}}
, its expectation value satisfies

|\langle\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}|\hat{\bm{S}}|\varepsilon_{\xi\bm{q}}\rangle|\leq\sum_{\alpha}\frac{Q^{2}_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}.

(S30)

This gives the upper bound of VCD as

\left|\frac{\Delta I}{N}\right|\leq\frac{\pi|E_{0}|^{2}}{2\hbar\omega}\sum_{\xi}\delta(\omega-\omega_{\xi\bm{q}})\left[\underbrace{N_{\xi\bm{q}}\sum_{\alpha}\frac{Q^{2}_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}_{\textrm{CP light emission}}-\underbrace{(1+N_{\xi\bm{q}})\sum_{\alpha}\frac{Q^{2}_{\alpha}}{M_{\alpha}}}_{\textrm{CP light absorption}}\right].

(S31)

An especially interesting case is when
\frac{Q_{1}}{\sqrt{M_{1}}}=\frac{Q_{2}}{\sqrt{M_{2}}}=\cdots=\frac{Q_{r}}{\sqrt{M_{r}}}=\gamma
, so that

\hat{O}=\gamma\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&\cdots&1\\
1&1&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&1\end{array}\right)

(S32)

Suppose
\gamma=1
for simplicity, the eigenvalues of
\hat{O}
are
r
and
0
so that the total spin
\hat{\bm{S}}
has eigenvalues
r
,
0
,
-r
. That is to say the total spin
\hat{\bm{S}}
of a unit cell containing
r
atoms has eigenvalues of
0
and
\pm r
which resembles a spin-
r
particle with
r
being the minimum quantum.

(iii) Given that
\bm{q}
is a small quantity, compared to typical lattice wave number
\frac{1}{a_{0}}
(
a_{0}
is lattice constant), the phonon spin
\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{s}^{\alpha\beta}_{\xi\bm{q}}
with
\bm{s}^{\alpha\beta}_{\xi\bm{q}}=\textrm{Im}(\bm{\varepsilon}^{\alpha*}_{\xi\bm{q}}\times\bm{\varepsilon}^{\beta}_{\xi\bm{q}})
can be expanded as

\begin{split}\bm{e_{q}}\cdot\bm{s}^{\alpha\beta}_{\xi\bm{q}}=&\frac{1}{q}\sum_{i,j}q_{i}q_{j}(\nabla_{k_{j}}s_{i})\end{split}

(S33)

The
\nabla_{k_{j}}s_{i}
is the dipole of phonon spin in
\bm{k}
space which is even under TRS but is odd under inversion symmetry. Thus the VCD can occur only in inversion-symmetry breaking systems. If
i=j
, however, the nonzero elements
\nabla_{k_{i}}s_{i}
also require the system to be chiral because either inversion symmetry or a mirror symmetry forces
\nabla_{k_{i}}s_{i}
to be zero. For off-diagonal tensor elements
\nabla_{k_{j}}s_{i}
(
i\neq j
) only inversion symmetry breaking is required.

Phonon-photon interaction Hamiltonian in the potential
(\bm{A},\varphi)
gauge.
The lattice Hamiltonian in the presence of external electromagnetic field is

\begin{split}H=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\left\{\frac{[\bm{p}_{\mu\alpha}-Q_{\alpha}\bm{A}(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)]^{2}}{2M_{\alpha}}+Q_{\alpha}\varphi(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)\right\}+\sum_{\mu\alpha i}\sum_{\nu\beta j}\frac{1}{2}K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}u_{\mu\alpha i}u_{\nu\beta j}\\
=&H_{0}+H^{\prime}+\tilde{H},\\
H_{0}=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\frac{\bm{p}^{2}_{\mu\alpha}}{2M_{\alpha}}+\sum_{\mu\alpha i}\sum_{\nu\beta j}\frac{1}{2}K_{\mu\alpha i,\nu\beta j}u_{\mu\alpha i}u_{\nu\beta j},\\
H^{\prime}=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}Q_{\alpha}\varphi(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)-\frac{Q_{\alpha}}{2M_{\alpha}}[\bm{p}_{\mu\alpha}\cdot\bm{A}(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)+\bm{A}(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)\cdot\bm{p}_{\mu\alpha}],\\
\tilde{H}=&\sum_{\mu\alpha}\frac{Q_{\alpha}\bm{A}^{2}(\bm{R}_{\mu},t)}{2M_{\alpha}}.\end{split}

(S34)

Here
H_{0}
refers to the lattice vibration Hamiltonian without electromagnetic fields;
H^{\prime}
is the light-lattice interaction Hamiltonian;
\tilde{H}
is the remaining terms which are quadratic on
\bm{A}
, thus it is usually neglected

Now we derive the expression of
\bm{A}
and
\varphi
for the CP light wave

\bm{E}(\bm{r},t)=\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_{1}+i\varepsilon\bm{e}_{2})e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)},

(S35)

where
\varepsilon=\pm 1
refers to the right-handed and left-handed CP light, respectively. According to the Maxwell equation
\bm{\nabla_{r}}\times\bm{E}=-\partial_{t}\bm{B}
, i.e.
i\bm{q}\times\bm{E}=i\omega\bm{B}
, the magnetic field is

\bm{B}(\bm{r},t)=\frac{\bm{q}}{\omega}\times\bm{E}=\frac{qE_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}(\bm{e}_{2}-i\varepsilon\bm{e}_{1})e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)}.

(S36)

Suppose the plane-wave ansartz:

\begin{split}\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)=&(A_{1}\bm{e}_{1}+A_{2}\bm{e}_{2}+A_{q}\bm{e_{q}})e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)},\\
\varphi(\bm{r},t)=&\varphi_{0}e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)}.\end{split}

(S37)

By definition, the electromagnetic potential should satisfy

\begin{split}\bm{E}=&-\partial_{t}\bm{A}-\bm{\nabla_{r}}\varphi=i\omega\bm{A}-i\bm{q}\varphi,\\
\bm{B}=&\bm{\nabla_{r}}\times\bm{A}=i\bm{q}\times\bm{A}.\end{split}

(S38)

Substitute Eqs. (
S35
), (
S36
), and (
S37

S38

\displaystyle\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}(\bm{e}_{1}+i\varepsilon\bm{e}_{2})=i\omega(A_{1}\bm{e}_{1}+A_{2}\bm{e}_{2}+A_{q}\bm{e_{q}})-iq\varphi_{0}\bm{e_{q}},

(S39)

\displaystyle\frac{qE_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}(\bm{e}_{2}-i\varepsilon\bm{e}_{1})=iq(A_{1}\bm{e}_{2}-A_{2}\bm{e}_{1}).

(S40)

According to Eq. (

), we have

\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}=i\omega A_{1},~~~~~i\varepsilon\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}=i\omega A_{2},~~~~~0=i\omega A_{q}-iq\varphi_{0}.

(S41)

According to Eq. (

\frac{qE_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}=iqA_{1},~~~~~-i\varepsilon\frac{qE_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}=-iqA_{2}.

(S42)

The Eq. (

A_{1}=-i\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega},~~~~~A_{2}=\varepsilon\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega},~~~~~A_{q}=\frac{q}{\omega}\varphi_{0}.

(S43)

The Eq. (

A_{1}=-i\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega},~~~~~A_{2}=\varepsilon\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}.

(S44)

Combining Eqs. (

and

\begin{split}\bm{A}(\bm{r},t)=&-i\frac{E_{0}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}(\bm{e}_{1}+i\varepsilon\bm{e}_{2})e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)}+\frac{\bm{q}}{\omega}\varphi_{0}e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)},\\
\varphi(\bm{r},t)=&\varphi_{0}e^{i(\bm{q}\cdot\bm{r}-\omega t)}.\end{split}

(S45)

The above expressions of

(S46)

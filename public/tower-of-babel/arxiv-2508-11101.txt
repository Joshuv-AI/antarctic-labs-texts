Plancherel-Pólya’s Type of Instability in Vibration System with Multiple Frozen Arguments
Lung-Hui Chen, Chung-Tsun Shieh

Abstract
We discuss the problem of the inverse spectral problem of Sturm-Liouville operator with multiple frozen arguments at { a 1 , a 2 , … , a N } \{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{N}\} in ( 0 , π ) (0,\pi) . One may consider the characteristic functions as perturbation of sine or of cosine functions depending on the boundary problem prescribed. However, such perturbation is represented in the form of Fourier transform of certain function which may or may not bring in Riesz basis theory and classical perturbation theory in functional analysis. We shall demonstrate the spectral perturbation in Plancherel-Pólya’s type of inequality and connect to perturbation of related potential functions in L 2 L^{2} -functional norm. MSC: 34A55/34K29/65L03. Keywords: Sturm–Liouville operator; frozen argument; inverse spectral problem; sine type function; instability; interpolation theory.

Alternatively, one may state the following instability result when one uses the other spectral data. In applicational sciences, zeros of Fourier transform may play a better role than Fourier coefficients which is called zero-crossing problem in signal analysis.

Corollary 3.2
.

\displaystyle C^{\prime}\|\{c^{1}_{k}-c^{2}_{k}\}\|^{\,2}_{\,l^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|^{2}|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|^{2}+C_{3}^{2}\|\{c^{1}_{k}-c^{2}_{k}\}\|_{\,l^{2}}^{\,2}

\displaystyle-2C_{3}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|\|\{c^{1}_{k}-c^{2}_{k}\}\|_{\,l^{2}}

\displaystyle\vskip 3.0pts\hskip-2.0pt

\displaystyle\hskip-2.0pt\geq\hskip-2.0pt

\displaystyle\hskip-2.0pt\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|^{2}|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|^{2}-2C_{3}\|\{c^{1}_{k}-c^{2}_{k}\}\|_{\,l^{2}}\hskip-2.0pt\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})||\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|,

that is,

\displaystyle\|\{c^{1}_{k}-c^{2}_{k}\}\|_{\,l^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|^{2}|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|^{2}}{C^{\prime}+2C_{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|\,|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{4}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|^{2}}{C^{\prime}+2C_{3}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})|\,|\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|},

in which
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|(f^{1})^{\prime}(d_{k})||\beta^{1}_{k}-\beta^{2}_{k}|
in the denominator converges due to (
3.5
) and (
3.9
). This combines with Theorem
3.1
, and corollary is thus proven.

Appendix: Entire Functions

For reader’s convenience, we include a few mathematical linguistics.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
F(z)
be an entire function. Let

M_{F}(r):=\max_{|z|=r}|F(z)|.

An entire function of
F(z)
is said
to be a function of finite order if there exists a positive
constant
k
such that the inequality

M_{F}(r)<e^{r^{k}}

is valid for all sufficiently large values of
r
. The greatest
lower bound of such numbers
k
is called the order of the entire
function
F(z)
. By the type
\sigma
of an entire function
F(z)

of order
\rho
, we mean the greatest lower bound of positive
number
A
for which asymptotically we have

M_{F}(r)<e^{Ar^{\rho}}.

That is,

\sigma_{F}:=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln M_{F}(r)}{r^{\rho}}.

If
0<\sigma_{F}<\infty
, then we say

F(z)
is of normal type or mean type. For
\sigma_{F}=0
, we say
F(z)
is of minimal type.

Definition 4.2
.

If an entire function
F(z)
is of order one and of normal type,
then we say it is an entire function of exponential type (EFET).

Definition 4.3
.

Let
F(z)
be an analytic function of finite order
\rho
in the
angle
[\theta_{1},\theta_{2}]
. We call the following quantity as the
indicator function of function
F(z)
.

h_{F}(\theta):=\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\ln|F(re^{i\theta})|}{r^{\rho}},\,\theta_{1}\leq\theta\leq\theta_{2}.

The type of a function is connected to the maximal value of indicator function.

Lemma 4.4

(Levin
[
15
]
, p.72)
.

The maximal value of indicator function
h_{F}(\theta)
of

F(z)
on the interval
\alpha\leq\theta\leq\beta
is equal to the
type
\sigma_{F}
of this function inside the angle
\alpha\leq\arg z\leq\beta
.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
f(z)
be an integral function of order
1
, and let

N(f,\alpha,\beta,r)
denote the number of the zeros of
f(z)

inside the angle
[\alpha,\beta]
and
|z|\leq r
. We define the
density function as

\Delta_{f}(\alpha,\beta):=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N(f,\alpha,\beta,r)}{r},

and

\Delta_{f}(\beta):=\Delta_{f}(\alpha_{0},\beta),

with some fixed
\alpha_{0}\notin E
such that
E
is at most a
countable set
[
15
,
16
]
. In particular, we denote the density function of
f
on the open right/left half complex plane as
\Delta^{+}_{f}
/
\Delta^{-}_{f}
respectively. Similarly, we can define the set density of a zero set
S
. Let
N(S,r)
be the number of the discrete elements of
S
in
\{|z|<r\}
. We define

\Delta_{S}:=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N(S,r)}{r}.

(4.1)

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f
,
g
be two entire functions. Then the following two
inequalities hold.

\displaystyle h_{fg}(\theta)\leq h_{f}(\theta)+h_{g}(\theta),\mbox{ if one limit exists};

(4.2)

\displaystyle h_{f+g}(\theta)\leq\max_{\theta}\{h_{f}(\theta),h_{g}(\theta)\},

(4.3)

where the equality in (
4.2
) holds if one of the functions is of completely regular growth, and secondly, the equality (
4.3
) holds if the indicator of the two summands are not equal at some
\theta_{0}
.

Proof.

We can find
the details in
[
15
, p. 51, p. 52]
, and the sharpened results are discussed on page 159 and 160.

Definition 4.7
.

The following quantity is called the width of indicator
diagram of entire function
f

d=h_{f}(\frac{\pi}{2})+h_{f}(-\frac{\pi}{2}).

(Cartwright)
.

Let
f
be an entire function of exponential type with zero set

f

the function

\kappa

4.

the indicator

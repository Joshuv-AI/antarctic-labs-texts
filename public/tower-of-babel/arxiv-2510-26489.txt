An extraction of the Collins–Soper kernel from a joint analysis of experimental and lattice data Preprint: DESY-25-145; FERMILAB-PUB-25-0784-T; INT-PUB-25-026; MIT-CTP/5946
Artur Avkhadiev, Valerio Bertone, Chiara Bissolotti, Matteo Cerutti, Yang Fu, Simone Rodini, Phiala Shanahan, Michael Wagman, Yong Zhao

Abstract
We present a first joint extraction of the Collins–Soper kernel (CSK) combining experimental and lattice QCD data in the context of an analysis of transverse-momentum-dependent distributions (TMDs). Based on a neural-network parametrization, we perform a Bayesian reweighting of an existing fits of TMDs using lattice data, as well as a joint TMD fit to lattice and experimental data. We consistently find that the inclusion of lattice information shifts the central value of the CSK by approximately 10% and reduces its uncertainty by 40-50%, highlighting the potential of lattice inputs to improve TMD extractions.



I
Introduction

This work presents the first effort to incorporate lattice QCD data into a phenomenological extraction of transverse-momentum-dependent distributions (TMDs), to better constrain the Collins-Soper kernel (CSK). This shares the same idea as past combined analyses of lattice and experimental data towards a better understanding of the hadronic structure through the extraction of collinear distributions. These include analyses of unpolarized, longitudinally-polarized, and transversity parton distribution functions (PDFs)
Lin et al. 2018
;
Cichy et al. 2019
;
Del Debbio et al. 2020
;
Del Debbio et al. 2021
;
Bringewatt et al. 2021
;
Barry
et al. 2022
;
Hou et al. 2023
;
Karpie et al. 2024
;
Ablat et al. 2024
;
Cocuzza et al. 2025
;
Ablat et al. 2025
;
Good et al. 2025
;
Barry et al. 2025a
, and generalized parton distributions
Guo et al. 2023
;
Cichy et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
. However, this is the first time that a similar strategy is employed in the context of a TMD extraction (see also Ref.
Cridge et al. 2025
for a similar effort in this direction). In this context, the CSK plays a special role in that it encodes information on the QCD vacuum rather than on the actual structure of hadrons. In fact, there is no experimental observable directly sensitive to CSK, so lattice is the only source of direct information. Improved determinations of the CSK are important to further sharpen control and reduce model-dependence in TMD evolution, enhance the precision of QCD predictions of multiple processes, and test the validity of factorization in the nonperturbative regime.

Formally, the CSK
K
is the expectation value of a Wilson loop running along the light-front directions and characterized by a transverse displacement
\mathbf{b}_{\rm T}
. When
b\equiv|\mathbf{b}_{\rm T}|
is small enough, the CSK can be computed in perturbation theory. As customary, radiative corrections introduce ultraviolet divergences, which, upon renormalization, lead to the introduction of the renormalization scale
\mu
. The dependence of
K
on
\mu
is governed by the renormalization-group equation:

\frac{\partial K(b,\mu)}{\partial\ln\mu}=-\gamma_{K}(\alpha_{s}(\mu))\,,

(1)

where
\gamma_{K}
, a.k.a. cusp anomalous dimension, is computable in perturbation theory and is currently fully known to four loops
Moult et al. 2022
;
Duhr et al. 2022
. Introducing the scale
\mu_{b}=2e^{-\gamma_{\rm E}}/b
, where
\gamma_{\rm E}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant, the solution to Eq. (
1
) reads:

K(b,\mu)=K(b,\mu_{b})-\int_{\mu_{b}}^{\mu}\frac{d\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\gamma_{K}(\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime}))\,,

(2)

where
K(b,\mu_{b})
is also known to four loops
Moult et al. 2022
;
Duhr et al. 2022
. When
b
grows, such that
\mu_{b}\lesssim\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
, nonperturbative effects dominate and Eq. (
2
) becomes unreliable. Nonperturbative affects are accounted for by modifying Eq. (
2
) as follows:

K(b,\mu)=K(b_{*},\mu_{b_{*}})-\int_{\mu_{b_{*}}}^{\mu}\frac{d\mu^{\prime}}{\mu^{\prime}}\gamma_{K}(\alpha_{s}(\mu^{\prime}))-g_{K}(b)\,,

(3)

with
\mu_{b_{*}}=2e^{-\gamma_{\rm E}}/b_{*}
. Here,
b_{*}\equiv b_{*}(b)
behaves linearly for small values of
b
(
b_{*}\sim b
) and saturates to some
b_{\rm max}\ll\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{-1}
for large
b
. The purpose of
b_{*}
is to guarantee that the first two terms in the r.h.s. of Eq. (
3
) are evaluated in the perturbative regime where
\alpha_{s}\ll 1
. The role of the functions
g_{K}
is to encode nonperturbative effects that arise from the large-
b
region. A typical parametrization is:

g_{K}(b)=2g_{2}^{2}b^{2}\,,

(4)

with the parameter
g_{2}
to be determined from observables sensitive to the CSK. Other models for
g_{K}
, with at most two free parameters, have been used in recent TMD fits (see e.g. Refs.
Moos et al. 2025
;
Aslan et al. 2024
;
Bacchetta et al. 2020
). However, in this context, we found no evidence for the need of a more complicated functional form.
The purpose of this work is therefore to determine the parameter
g_{2}
from a joint analysis of experimental and lattice data.

II
Baseline TMD fit

A determination of
g_{2}
from experimental data implies an extraction of TMDs. TMD analyses have achieved a remarkable level of sophistication: they are often based on broad, multiprocess data sets, they achieve high perturbative accuracy, and employ advanced methodological frameworks (see, e.g., Refs.
Bacchetta et al. 2024
;
Bacchetta et al. 2022
;
Moos et al. 2024
;
Moos et al. 2025
;
Camarda et al. 2024
;
Barry et al. 2025b
;
Cuerpo et al. 2025
;
Kang et al. 2024
;
de Florian and Grazzini 2005
;
Camarda et al. 2025
).
In this work, we rely on the TMD extraction presented in Ref.
Bacchetta et al. 2025
. In that analysis, TMD parton distribution functions (PDFs) were extracted from a comprehensive set of Drell-Yan data using next-to-next-to-next-to-leading logarithmic (N
3
LL) accurate predictions. The nonperturbative part of TMD PDFs, which also involves
g_{2}
, was parametrized through a neural network (NN). The flexibility of the NN enabled a solid estimate of the nonperturbative parameters and their uncertainties. That analysis obtained:

g_{2}^{\rm Baseline}=0.186\pm 0.033\,.

(5)

In this Letter, we will use the numerical framework and the determination of Ref.
Bacchetta et al. 2025
as a baseline to quantify the impact on
g_{2}
of lattice data for the CSK in the context of a TMD phenomenological extraction.

IV
Joint extraction of the CSK

In this section, we studied the impact on
g_{2}
of including the CSK lattice data of Refs.
Avkhadiev et al. 2024
;
Avkhadiev et al. 2023
into the extraction of Ref.
Bacchetta et al. 2025
following two different strategies: reweighting and simultaneous fit.

IV.1
Reweighting

The impact of incorporating new data into an existing fit can be effectively estimated using the Bayesian reweighting procedure
Ball et al. 2011
. This method consists of assigning a weight to each of the
N=250
Monte Carlo (MC) replicas of the fit of Ref.
Bacchetta et al. 2025
according to its likelihood with respect to lattice data, which in turn is estimated through
\chi^{2}
.

We performed the reweighting both on the continuum-extrapolated and on the finite-lattice-spacing data. In the former approach, data for the CSK is provided as a set of
n=21
points
\{K_{j}\pm\sigma_{j}\}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, corresponding to as many values of
b\in\{b_{j}\}
. Since this data set comes without correlations between points, the
\chi^{2}
of the
\alpha
-th replica, with
\alpha=1,\dots,N
is computed as:

\chi^{2}_{\alpha}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\frac{K_{j}-K^{(\alpha)}(b_{j})}{\sigma_{j}}\right)^{2}\,,

(7)

where
K^{(\alpha)}(b_{j})
is evaluated using Eq. (
3
) with
g_{2}=g_{2}^{(\alpha)}
extracted from the
\alpha
-th MC replica. The weight to be associated with this replica is
Ball et al. 2011
:

w_{\alpha}=\mathcal{N}\left(\chi^{2}_{\alpha}\right)^{n/2-1}e^{-\chi^{2}_{\alpha}/2}\,,

(8)

where
\mathcal{N}
normalizes the weights so that
\sum_{\alpha}w_{\alpha}=N
. Weighted average and variance of
g_{2}
over the MC ensemble are then computed respectively as:

\langle g_{2}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}w_{\alpha}g_{2}^{(\alpha)}\,,\quad\sigma_{g_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N}w_{\alpha}(g_{2}^{(\alpha)}-\langle g_{2}\rangle)^{2}\,.

(9)

The result is:

g_{2}^{\rm Rew-cont}\equiv\langle g_{2}\rangle\pm\sigma_{g_{2}}=0.164\pm 0.020\,.

(10)

The associated number of effective replicas
Ball et al. 2011
,
N_{\rm eff}\simeq 150
, indicates that the reweighting was successful. Indeed,
N_{\rm eff}
is significantly lower than
N
but still large enough to produce a statistically sound ensemble after reweighting. Comparing the purely phenomenological determination in Eq. (
5
) with the lattice-reweighted one in Eq. (
10
), we observe a shift in central value of around 10% and a reduction in uncertainty of 40%.

Next, we consider the finite-lattice-spacing
data set. This is subdivided into three subsets corresponding to the lattice spacings discussed above, and counting
n_{1}=6
,
n_{2}=7
, and
n_{3}=8
points. Each subset
\{K_{j_{i}}\}
, with
i=1,2,3
and
j_{i}=1,\dots,n_{i}
, comes with its own covariance matrix
\Sigma_{i}
estimated in the lattice analysis of Ref.
Avkhadiev et al. 2024
. Therefore, the
\chi^{2}
of the
\alpha
-the replica is evaluated as:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\chi^{2}_{\alpha}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{3}\sum_{j_{i},l_{i}=1}^{n_{i}}\left(K_{j_{i}}-\overline{K}^{(\alpha)}(b_{j_{i}})\right)\\
\\
&\times&\displaystyle(\Sigma^{-1}_{i})_{j_{i},l_{i}}\left(K_{l_{i}}-\overline{K}^{(\alpha)}(b_{l_{i}})\right)\,,\end{array}

(11)

and the corresponding weight computed as in Eq. (
8
). However, each prediction for the CSK
\overline{K}
must now correspond to the appropriate lattice spacing
a
. This is done by replacing
g_{K}
in Eq. (
3
) with:

g_{K}(b)=2g_{2}^{2}b^{2}-k_{1}\frac{a}{b}-k_{2}\frac{a^{2}}{b^{2}}\,.

(12)

Since the parameters
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
were not available from the fit of Ref.
Bacchetta et al. 2025
, we generated a MC ensemble for each of them using as priors the values obtained in Ref.
Avkhadiev et al. 2024
:
k_{1}=0.22\pm 0.08
and
k_{2}=0\pm 0.1
.

k_{2}
was estimated to be negligible in Ref.

To consistently include these parameters in the reweighting, we first sampled

After reweighting, we find:

\begin{split}g_{2}^{{\rm Rew-fin}}&=0.165\pm 0.020\,.\end{split}

(13)

The reweighted value of

is in perfect agreement, both in terms of central value and uncertainty, with Eq. (

). We also find that the reweighted values of

and

agree with their respective priors. This proves the consistency of performing the reweighting using continuum-extrapolated and finite-lattice-spacing lattice data, and provides further evidence of the impact of this data on the CSK. For definiteness, we will take the value in Eq. (
13
) as our best estimate of
g_{2}
by reweighting.

V
Conclusion

We presented the first successful joint extraction of the nonperturbative Collins-Soper kernel (CSK) by combining experimental Drell-Yan data with lattice QCD calculations in the context of a TMD analysis based on a neural-network paratrization. The inclusion of lattice data consistently shifts the central value of the parameter
g_{2}
, which governs the behavior of the CSK in the nonperturbative region, by approximately 10% and reduces its uncertainty by 50%, leading to a significantly more precise determination. Two independent methodologies — Bayesian reweighting of an existing fit and a simultaneous fit — yield remarkably consistent results, demonstrating the robustness of the approach. The analysis finds no tension between experimental and lattice data, and the description of the Drell-Yan data remains stable. This study highlights the significant potential of lattice QCD inputs to substantially improve precision in the extraction of transverse-momentum-dependent distributions.

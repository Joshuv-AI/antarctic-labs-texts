Prognostic and Therapeutic Potential of Nicotinamide N-Methyltransferase and Hypoxia Inducible Lipid Droplet Associated in Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma
Authors: Mingyu Kim, Geehyun Song, Jae Young Joung, Ho Kyung Seo, Hyung Ho Lee, Jinsoo Chung
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundClear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is the most aggressive subtype of renal cancer, characterized by profound metabolic reprogramming and lipid accumulation. Despite therapeutic advancements, reliable prognostic biomarkers remain limited. This study investigates the prognostic and functional relevance of nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT), and hypoxia inducible lipid droplet associated (HILPDA) in ccRCC.MethodsRNA sequencing was performed on tumor and matched normal tissues from 14 ccRCC patients. Differential gene expression and pathway enrichment analyses were conducted. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to assess diagnostic value. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis using The Cancer Genome Atlas data evaluated prognostic significance. NNMT knockdown experiments were conducted in ccRCC cell lines (SNU1272 and Caki-1) to assess functional relevance.ResultsNNMT and HILPDA were significantly upregulated in tumor tissues. Pathway analyses revealed associations with lipid metabolism and biosynthesis. ROC analysis showed high diagnostic accuracy (NNMT area under the curve [AUC], 0.923; HILPDA AUC, 0.943). Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that high NNMT expression correlated with worse overall and disease-specific survival (P < 0.001), whereas HILPDA expression showed no prognostic impact. NNMT knockdown significantly reduced cell viability (P < 0.001), supporting its role in tumor progression.ConclusionNNMT is a promising prognostic biomarker and potential therapeutic target in ccRCC, supported by transcriptomic, clinical, and functional validation. While the prognostic relevance of HILPDA remains inconclusive, its metabolic associations suggest potential biological significance. Further studies with larger cohorts and in vivo validation are warranted.Keywords: Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase, Hypoxia Inducible Lipid Droplet Associated Protein, Clear Cell Renal Cell Carcinoma

INTRODUCTION

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC) is the most common and lethal subtype of renal cell carcinoma, accounting for 70–80% of cases and contributing significantly to kidney cancer-related mortality worldwide.1,2,3 Despite advancements in surgical resection, targeted therapies, and immune checkpoint inhibitors, ccRCC remains highly heterogeneous, with approximately 30% of patients presenting with metastatic disease at diagnosis and up to 40% experiencing recurrence after nephrectomy.4,5 These clinical challenges underscore the urgent need for reliable prognostic biomarkers and novel therapeutic targets to improve patient stratification and guide treatment decisions. A hallmark of ccRCC is its profound metabolic reprogramming, characterized by lipid accumulation, aberrant glucose metabolism, and mitochondrial dysfunction.6,7,8 This metabolic shift fuels tumor progression and contributes to resistance against conventional therapies.8,9 Lipid droplet accumulation within tumor cells defines the classic ‘clear cell’ morphology and highlights the critical role of lipid metabolism in ccRCC pathogenesis.10,11 Identifying key regulators of these metabolic pathways is essential for developing targeted interventions. Nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT) has emerged as a pivotal player in tumor metabolism by catalyzing the methylation of nicotinamide, thereby modulating nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide levels and altering redox balance.12,13,14 NNMT overexpression has been implicated in various malignancies, including pancreatic, gastric, and bladder cancers, where it promotes tumor growth, invasion, and chemoresistance.15,16,17,18 In ccRCC, Reustle et al.13 demonstrated that NNMT overexpression is associated with poor prognosis and aggressive tumor behavior. Mechanistically, NNMT has been shown to drive lipogenesis and enhance tumor progression through the IL6-STAT3 signaling pathway, reinforcing its role as a metabolic driver in ccRCC.19 Hypoxia inducible lipid droplet associated (HILPDA), a key regulator of lipid storage, is induced under hypoxic conditions and localizes to lipid droplets, modulating lipid accumulation and energy homeostasis.20,21,22 HILPDA overexpression has been documented in metabolically dysregulated tumors, including ccRCC, where hypoxia is a prominent feature of the tumor microenvironment.23,24 Although prior studies have explored the transcriptional landscape of NNMT and HILPDA in ccRCC, the integration of receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analyses to evaluate their diagnostic utility remains limited, highlighting the need for further investigation to strengthen their clinical applicability. Additionally, existing survival analyses are predominantly based on The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) datasets, lacking validation in patient-pecific cohorts. This gap highlights the need for studies utilizing RNA sequencing and functional assays to bridge the translational divide. In this study, we perform RNA sequencing on tumor and matched normal tissues from ccRCC patients to evaluate the prognostic and diagnostic potential of NNMT and HILPDA. Pathway enrichment and correlation analyses are conducted to elucidate their roles in tumor metabolism. Furthermore, NNMT knockdown experiments are performed to validate its functional significance in ccRCC cell viability. By integrating transcriptomic analysis with functional validation, this study aims to establish NNMT and HILPDA as clinically actionable biomarkers and therapeutic targets for ccRCC.

Paired normal and tumor tissues were collected during surgical resection. The demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients are presented in Table 1.

RNA transcriptome sequencing

Total RNA was extracted from tissue samples and quantified using the Quant-IT RiboGreen assay (Invitrogen, #R11490). RNA integrity was assessed using TapeStation RNA Screentape (Agilent, #5067-5576). Only samples with RIN > 7.0 were used for library preparation. Poly-A mRNA was enriched, fragmented, and converted to cDNA using the Illumina TruSeq Stranded mRNA Sample Prep Kit (Illumina, #RS-122-2101). Sequencing libraries were quantified using KAPA Library Quantification Kits (KAPA BIOSYSTEMS, #KK4854) and validated by TapeStation D1000 ScreenTape (Agilent, #5067-5582). Sequencing was performed on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform for paired-end (2 × 100 bp) sequencing, achieving a minimum depth of 30 million reads per sample. Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified using the edgeR package in R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Normalization was performed with the Trimmed Mean of M values method. DEGs were selected based on adjusted P values and log2-fold changes. Pearson correlation analysis was conducted to evaluate the relationship between NNMT and HILPDA expression using GraphPad Prism (San Diego, CA, USA), with significance set at P < 0.05. Scatter plots were generated to visualize correlations (R = 0.92, P < 0.001).

Protein-protein interaction (PPI) network construction

PPI networks were constructed using STRING (https://string-db.org/) and Metascape (http://metascape.org).25,26 Functional enrichment and pathway analysis were performed using Gene Ontology (GO) terms in DAVID (https://david.ncifcrf.gov).27 Additionally, Cytoscape (https://cytoscape.org/) was utilized to visualize and further analyze PPI networks and enriched pathways, allowing for the construction of interaction maps and clustering of functionally related genes.28 Enriched biological processes, molecular functions, and cellular components with P < 0.05 were visualized as bubble charts using RStudio and the ggplot2 package.29

Survival analysis and data acquisition

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed using TCGA Pan-Cancer Atlas (KIRC cohort) data. Clinical and transcriptomic information from 512 ccRCC patients was accessed through the Genomic Data Commons portal (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Patients were categorized into high and low NNMT or HILPDA expression groups based on median expression levels. Survival endpoints included overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (DSS). Statistical differences between survival curves were assessed using the log-rank test (P < 0.05).

Cell culture

Caki-1 and SNU1272 ccRCC cell lines were obtained from the Korea Cell Line Bank and cultured in RPMI-1640 medium (HyClone, Logan, UT, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum and 1% penicillin/streptomycin. Cells were maintained at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO2. Subculturing was performed every 2–3 days.

siRNA transfection

Small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting NNMT were synthesized by Bionics (Seoul, Korea), while negative control siRNA was obtained from the same manufacturer. Cells were seeded at a density of 3.9 × 105 cells per well in 60 mm dishes and incubated overnight. Transfection was performed with 20 nM siRNA using RNAimax (Invitrogen, Waltham, MA, USA) in Opti-MEM (Gibco BRL Inc., Grand Island NY, USA). After 48–72 hours, cells were harvested for further analysis.

Protein extraction and Western blot

Protein was extracted using RIPA buffer with protease and phosphatase inhibitors (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Grand Island NY, USA). Lysates were centrifuged at 22,000 × g for 15 minutes, and 30 µg of protein was separated by 12% NuPAGE gels. Proteins were transferred to polyvinylidene fluoride membranes and blocked with 5% skim milk for 1 hour. Membranes were incubated with primary antibodies against NNMT (1:1,000; Abcam, Cambridge,UK) and β-Actin (1:2,000; Santa Cruz, CA, USA) overnight at 4°C. After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibodies for 1.5 hours and visualized using enhanced chemiluminescence reagents.

RNA extraction and quantitative reverse transcription polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR)

Total RNA from ccRCC cell lines was extracted using TRIzol Reagent (Invitrogen) and reverse-transcribed into cDNA with PrimeScript RT Master Mix (Takara Bio Inc, Kusatsu, Japan). qRT-PCR was conducted using the GoTaq 1-Step RT-qPCR Kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) on the LightCycler® 96 system. Reactions (20 μL) contained 10 μL SYBR Green, 1 μL forward/reverse primers, and cDNA. Amplification conditions included 40 cycles at 95°C for 15 seconds, 58°C for 1 minutes, and 72°C for 15 seconds. 18S rRNA was used as the housekeeping gene, and data were analyzed using the 2-ΔΔCT method. Primer sequences used for NNMT, HILPDA, and 18S are provided for reference (Supplementary Table 1).

CCK-8 assay

Cell viability was measured using the CCK-8 assay (Dojindo Molecular Technologies, Rockville, MS, USA). Cells were seeded in 48-well plates at 2 × 104 cells/well and incubated overnight. After siRNA transfection, cells were incubated for 72 hours. Fresh medium mixed with CCK-8 (9:1) was added to each well, and absorbance at 450 nm was measured after 90 minutes.

Statistical analysis

All data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test was used to compare multiple groups. GraphPad Prism software was used, with significance defined as P < 0.05, P < 0.01, or P < 0.001. RNA-seq data were analyzed using edgeR, and pathway enrichment and scatter plots were generated to visualize gene expression and interactions.

Ethics statement

Ethical approval to report this data was obtained from the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of the National Cancer Center (IRB No. NCC2021-0147). It was confirmed that informed consent was obtained from all subjects, and all research was performed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and relevant guidelines and regulations.

RESULTS

Differential expression analysis reveals upregulation of NNMT and HILPDA in ccRCC

RNA sequencing analysis was conducted on tumor (T) and matched normal (N) tissues from 14 ccRCC patients to identify DEGs. Hierarchical clustering of gene expression profiles revealed clear segregation between tumor and normal samples, indicating distinct expression patterns (Fig. 1A). The clustering analysis demonstrated that tumor samples consistently exhibited higher expression of specific gene clusters compared to normal tissues, suggesting significant transcriptional reprogramming in ccRCC. Volcano plot analysis highlighted the top 10 genes with the most significant differential expression between tumor and normal tissues (Fig. 1B). Among these, five genes, including NNMT and HILPDA, were found to be consistently upregulated in tumor tissues. The magnitude of upregulation observed in these genes indicated their potential involvement in ccRCC pathophysiology. The overall expression levels of NNMT and HILPDA across tumor and normal tissues are summarized (Supplementary Table 2). Further investigation into the biological relevance of these genes was conducted in subsequent analyses. At this stage, the focus remained on identifying candidates for deeper exploration based on their differential expression patterns. NNMT and HILPDA were selected for additional analyses due to their consistent upregulation across patient samples and their potential involvement in metabolic processes, which are frequently dysregulated in ccRCC.

Correlation and network analysis of NNMT and HILPDA

To explore potential functional relationships between the five upregulated genes, STRING network analysis was performed. This analysis revealed direct and indirect interactions among the genes, with NNMT and HILPDA clustering within the same lipid metabolism-associated network (Fig. 2A). The network indicated that these genes share pathways related to lipid droplet formation, fatty acid metabolism, and cellular energy regulation. A strong positive correlation was observed between NNMT and HILPDA expression across all tumor samples using Pearson correlation analysis (R = 0.92, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2B). This correlation suggests potential co-regulation or functional synergy, warranting further investigation into their combined role in metabolic reprogramming within ccRCC. To further elucidate the biological relevance of NNMT and HILPDA, Cytoscape was employed to perform pathway enrichment analysis, focusing on Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and Reactome pathways. The enriched pathways were visualized as a pie chart to illustrate the distribution of genes involved in various metabolic networks (Fig. 2C). This visualization identified critical pathways linked to lipid metabolism and biosynthesis, reinforcing the potential involvement of NNMT and HILPDA in ccRCC-associated metabolic alterations. The network layout was generated using the yFiles orthogonal layout algorithm to ensure optimal visualization and interpretability. A detailed summary of enriched pathways associated genes, and their statistical significance is provided, further elucidating the metabolic clustering of NNMT and HILPDA and supporting their potential role in ccRCC progression (Table 2).

Functional enrichment analysis of NNMT and HILPDA

To validate the metabolic relevance of NNMT and HILPDA, comprehensive functional enrichment analysis was performed using GO and KEGG pathway databases. GO analysis revealed significant enrichment in biological processes such as lipid droplet organization, fatty acid metabolism, and cellular energy homeostasis, highlighting the metabolic functions associated with these genes (Fig. 3A). KEGG pathway analysis further identified lipid metabolism, nicotinamide metabolism, and the regulation of lipolysis as pathways significantly linked to NNMT and HILPDA (Fig. 3B). These findings underscore the role of NNMT and HILPDA in critical metabolic processes, reinforcing their involvement in ccRCC progression. Lipid metabolic reprogramming is a well-documented hallmark of ccRCC, and the identification of these enriched pathways supports the hypothesis that NNMT and HILPDA contribute to tumor growth by modulating lipid storage and biosynthetic pathways. This analysis complements previous network findings and highlights the potential of NNMT and HILPDA as key metabolic regulators in ccRCC pathogenesis.

Knockdown of NNMT inhibits tumor cell growth in ccRCC

To assess the prognostic significance of NNMT and HILPDA, two separate analyses were conducted. ROC curve analysis was performed using RNA sequencing data from the 14 ccRCC patients, demonstrating high diagnostic accuracy for distinguishing tumor from normal tissues. NNMT achieved an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.923, with a sensitivity of 91.7% and specificity of 89.5%. HILPDA achieved an AUC of 0.943, reflecting excellent discrimination capability between tumor and normal tissues (Fig. 4A and B). An AUC above 0.9 indicates excellent diagnostic performance, suggesting that NNMT and HILPDA could potentially be utilized as biomarkers for early diagnosis and postoperative monitoring in ccRCC patients. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was conducted using data from the Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma (TCGA, PanCancer Atlas) database to overcome the limited sample size of our cohort. Patients were stratified into high and low expression groups based on the median expression levels of NNMT and HILPDA. High NNMT expression was significantly associated with poorer OS (P = 3.33e-4) and DSS (P = 4.04e-5) (Fig. 4C and D). In contrast, HILPDA expression did not show a statistically significant association with survival outcomes (P = 0.125) (Fig. 4E and F). These results suggest that NNMT may serve as a valuable prognostic biomarker for ccRCC, providing insights into tumor aggressiveness and potential treatment stratification. The lack of significant association between HILPDA expression and survival highlights the complexity of ccRCC biology, suggesting that while HILPDA may play a role in metabolic processes, its prognostic value might be limited under certain clinical contexts. This finding reflects the multifactorial nature of ccRCC progression, indicating the need for further studies to delineate the functional contributions of HILPDA and its interactions with other oncogenic pathways.

NNMT knockdown reduces ccRCC cell viability

To investigate the potential role of NNMT in ccRCC, knockdown experiments were conducted in ccRCC cell lines (SNU1272 and Caki-1) using three independent siRNAs. Western blot and qRT-PCR analyses confirmed efficient silencing of NNMT expression across all siRNA-treated groups compared to scramble siRNA controls (Fig. 5A and B). Cell proliferation assays revealed a significant reduction in ccRCC cell viability following NNMT knockdown compared to the control group (P < 0.001) (Fig. 5C). This reduction in viability suggests that NNMT may play a role in supporting ccRCC cell growth, potentially through metabolic modulation or other survival mechanisms. While these results indicate NNMT’s involvement in promoting ccRCC cell viability, the absence of comprehensive functional assays and in vivo validation represents a limitation of this study. Further research, including functional studies examining downstream targets of NNMT and in vivo tumorigenicity assays, will be necessary to elucidate the precise role of NNMT in ccRCC progression. Nevertheless, the observed association between NNMT expression and reduced cell viability highlights its potential as a clinically relevant biomarker and possible therapeutic target for ccRCC.

DISCUSSION

Metabolic reprogramming is a hallmark of ccRCC, characterized by lipid accumulation and altered energy homeostasis.30,31 This shift not only drives tumor growth but also contributes to therapy resistance, underscoring the critical need to identify key metabolic regulators as potential biomarkers and therapeutic targets. NNMT and HILPDA have emerged as prominent players in lipid metabolism and hypoxia-driven pathways, suggesting their relevance in ccRCC pathogenesis.19,20 However, their prognostic significance and functional roles in ccRCC remain incompletely understood. The present study highlights the critical role of NNMT and HILPDA in the metabolic landscape of ccRCC. Our findings demonstrate that NNMT and HILPDA are significantly upregulated in ccRCC tumor tissues, reinforcing their involvement in tumorigenesis and metabolic reprogramming. NNMT emerged as a robust prognostic biomarker, with high expression correlating with poor overall and DSS. This association underscores the potential of NNMT as a clinically actionable target, providing valuable insights into patient stratification and therapeutic development. Network analysis further revealed that NNMT and HILPDA cluster within lipid metabolism-associated pathways, suggesting co-regulation and functional synergy in ccRCC progression. The STRING analysis indicated significant interactions between NNMT, and other metabolic genes involved in lipid droplet formation and fatty acid metabolism, emphasizing their role in driving tumorigenic metabolic alterations. These findings strengthen the hypothesis that metabolic cross-talk plays a pivotal role in ccRCC pathophysiology. The functional relevance of NNMT was further validated through siRNA-mediated knockdown experiments in ccRCC cell lines, which resulted in a marked reduction in cell viability. These results support the hypothesis that NNMT contributes to tumor cell proliferation and survival, likely through its role in lipid metabolism and cellular energy homeostasis. The ability of NNMT to drive metabolic alterations aligns with prior studies demonstrating its involvement in tumor progression across various malignancies. While prior studies, such as Reustle et al.13 and Nguyen et al.,19 have demonstrated the prognostic relevance and metabolic roles of NNMT in ccRCC, our study provides a distinct perspective by integrating RNA sequencing from matched tumor-normal tissues, functional knockdown assays, and ROC analysis. Unlike previous works that primarily relied on large-scale datasets or in vitro pathway elucidation, our study highlights early transcriptional dysregulation in patient-derived tissues and directly links NNMT to diagnostic potential and tumor cell viability. This integrative approach bridges mechanistic insights with translational applicability, reinforcing NNMT as both a metabolic regulator and clinically actionable biomarker. Such findings highlight the possibility of targeting NNMT as part of a metabolic intervention strategy in ccRCC, which could complement existing therapeutic modalities. HILPDA, while consistently upregulated in tumor tissues, did not exhibit a significant association with survival outcomes in Kaplan-Meier analysis. This result may reflect the multifaceted nature of HILPDA’s role in lipid droplet formation and hypoxia-induced metabolic shifts, suggesting that its impact on ccRCC progression might be context-dependent. ROC curve analysis, however, demonstrated that HILPDA has high diagnostic accuracy in distinguishing tumors from normal tissues, indicating its potential utility as a diagnostic biomarker. Despite this, the lack of prognostic significance complicates its clinical value as a reliable marker for survival prediction. This discrepancy highlights the need for further research to determine whether HILPDA’s diagnostic utility can be translated into meaningful prognostic insights when combined with other biomarkers. The diagnostic potential of NNMT and HILPDA was demonstrated by ROC curve analysis, which revealed high accuracy in distinguishing tumor from normal tissues. This suggests that NNMT and HILPDA could serve as valuable biomarkers for early detection and postoperative monitoring, enhancing current diagnostic frameworks. Given the metabolic reprogramming that characterizes ccRCC, incorporating metabolic biomarkers into clinical practice could provide a more comprehensive understanding of tumor behavior and treatment response Despite the promising findings, this study has several limitations. The small patient cohort and relatively short follow-up period limited our ability to perform statistically robust clinical outcome analyses. Although we observed consistent up-regulation of NNMT and HILPDA in tumor tissues, the absence of long-term survival data constrained our capacity to validate their prognostic relevance within our own cohort. To overcome this limitation, we conducted survival analyses using the TCGA (KIRC, PanCancer Atlas) dataset, which confirmed that high NNMT expression is significantly associated with poorer overall and DSS. In addition, while our in vitro experiments provide initial functional evidence supporting the role of NNMT in ccRCC progression, the lack of in vivo validation remains a limitation. Future studies incorporating animal models and larger, longitudinal patient cohorts will be essential to confirm the clinical utility of NNMT and HILPDA. Such efforts will facilitate the translation of these metabolic biomarkers into practical tools for patient stratification and targeted therapy development in ccRCC.

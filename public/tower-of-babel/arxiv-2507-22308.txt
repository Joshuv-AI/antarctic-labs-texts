Low-energy multi-photon scattering at tree-level and one-loop order in a homogeneous electromagnetic field
Ivan Ahumada, Patrick Copinger, James P. Edwards

Abstract
We study low energy photons coupled to scalar and spinor matter in the presence of an arbitrary homogeneous electromagnetic field in a first-quantised (worldline) approach. Utilising a Fock-Schwinger gauge for both the scattering photons and homogeneous background, simple compact expressions are found for both the photon- and background-dressed effective action and propagator in scalar and spinor quantum electrodynamics. The low-energy limit allows identification of the coupling of the scattering photons as one of an effective homogeneous superposition of their field strengths, with amplitudes following from application of a suitable linearisation operator. To treat the linearisation, several techniques are employed, including a functional expansion based on the proper time formalism and worldline Green functions, linearised vertex operators under a worldline path integral, and a matrix expansion in the field strengths. We find, in particular, that a replacement rule converting scalar amplitudes to spinor amplitudes at one-loop order can, surprisingly, be extended to tree level amplitudes in the low energy limit. Finally, we discuss a novel worldline representation of the momentum space matter propagators, obtaining a suitable worldline Green function for this path integral satisfying homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and momentum space vertex operators representing the scattering photons already in momentum space.

In the present manuscript we aim to develop a general approach to obtaining the low-energy limit of photon scattering amplitudes, including in the presence of an arbitrary homogeneous background electromagnetic field. First we will show how the expansion of the effective action with respect to a homogeneous component of a background electromagnetic field contains the full information on the low-energy photon amplitudes in the complement background. We will do this expansion in three ways: a direct expansion of the proper-time representation of effective action which simplifies the integral-structure of the resulting amplitudes, by employing the worldline representation to expand in the scattering photons’ momenta (this simplifies the Lorentz structure instead), and by directly performing a power series expansion about the Lorentz matrix form of the homogeneous photon field strength. We also extend this technique to the previously overlooked case of tree-level amplitudes, both in vacuum and in a constant field.

Along the way, we will show how – in the special case of low-energy scattering photons – it is possible to derive a novel worldline representation of the
momentum space
tree-level amplitudes that makes use of a coordinate space path integral with Dirichlet boundary conditions. This is, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the first time such a path integral representation has been presented (past efforts involve Fourier transforming the position space Master Formulae), and the resulting worldline theory is of intrinsic interest due to it having a non-diagonal kinetic operator.

II.2.2
Parameter integrals and matrix expansion

The expanded exponent in (
19
) is compact yet contains all information for arbitrary multiplicity of low-energy photon couplings. We now show how to perform the parameter integrals in a way complimentary to
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2024a
– they used a matrix decomposition for a specific configuration of (anti-)parallel fields; we however, keep our homogeneous field arbitrary. And, at the same time demonstrate an equivalent expansion resulting in a recursive evaluation. To maintain a compact final form, we wish to keep the field dependence explicit and under a trace.

To begin, we will need to employ the following identities for dual field strength
\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}F_{\alpha\beta}
, and likewise for
\widetilde{f}
, (these incidentally hold for arbitrary field in (3+1)-dimensions):

Ff-\widetilde{f}\widetilde{F}=-I_{fF}\,,\quad\widetilde{F}f+\widetilde{f}F=2I_{f\widetilde{F}}\,,

(28)

and their transposes, and the following corollaries:

\displaystyle[F^{2},f]=[\widetilde{F}^{2},f]=F\widetilde{f}\widetilde{F}-\widetilde{F}\widetilde{f}F\,,

(29)

\displaystyle\{F^{2}+\widetilde{F}^{2},f\}=4I_{f\widetilde{F}}\widetilde{F}-2I_{fF}F\,,

(30)

where the Lorentz invariants read
I_{fF}=\frac{1}{2}f_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}
and
I_{f\widetilde{F}}=-\frac{1}{4}f^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}_{\mu\nu}
.

Let us show how the above identities aid in the evaluation of the parameter integrals in (
19
). First we write for an
f
insertion

f=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\{f,F^{2}+\widetilde{F}^{2}\}+[f,F^{2}+\widetilde{F}^{2}]\bigr)\cdot\frac{1}{F^{2}+\widetilde{F}^{2}}\,,

(31)

where we can now see that the anticommutator part, according to (
30
), commutes with all other
F
and
\widetilde{F}
dependent elements under the trace of (
19
). Then for the anticommutator part of (
31
) we can simplify one parameter integral according to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\cdot\{f,F^{2}+\widetilde{F}^{2}\}\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau^{\prime\prime}}\cdot[2I_{f\widetilde{F}}\widetilde{F}-I_{fF}F]\,.

(32)

After performing many such operations (for anti-commutator insertions) one will be left with a number of folded integrals involving
\dot{\mathcal{G}}
, which we elaborate on below.

The other set of integrals we will encounter after applying (
31
) involve the commutator. Fortunately, one may show that the following identity holds for such cases:

e^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau^{\prime}\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}\cdot[f,F^{2}\!+\!\widetilde{F}^{2}]\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau^{\prime\prime}}=-2[\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime\prime}},\{eF,f\}]\,,

(33)

in effect reducing the rank of the Green functions from two to one. The above can be proven by first applying the defining differential equation, and its cousin following from the time translational symmetry of the worldline action, i.e.
(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-2eF\partial_{\tau})\mathcal{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}=2\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-2/T
and
\mathcal{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}(\overleftarrow{\partial}_{\tau^{\prime}}^{2}+2eF\overleftarrow{\partial}_{\tau^{\prime}})=2\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-2/T
and the periodic BC properties of
\mathcal{G}
, to find

\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau\,\mathcal{G}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}\cdot\overleftarrow{\partial}_{\tau}2[f,eF]\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime\prime}}=2[\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau^{\prime\prime}}\,,f]\,.

(34)

Then using the above, (
29
), and the fact that
[f,F^{2}]=F[f,F]+[f,F]F
, one arrives at (
33
).

In
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2024a
, the background field was chosen to consist of anti-parallel electric and magnetic fields to facilitate evaluation of the parameter integrals entering
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}
leading to a closed form expression. This, however, complicates the Lorentz structure of the result and required various projection operators to arrive at closed form results for the parameter integrals. We take an alternative route here, which is closer to worldline calculations in vacuum and simplifies the Lorentz structure (at the cost of complicating the parameter integrals where they do not appear under a trace). We claim that application of the above manipulations allow all parameter integrals to be reduced to ones over “closed cycles” of
\dot{\mathcal{G}}

\mathcal{J}^{(n)}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{i}\,\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{12}\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{23}\cdot\ldots\cdot\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{n1}

(35)

that, crucially, are factorised from the remaining Lorentz tensors (again, this holds under the trace). This is reminiscent of the worldline calculation of the
finite-
energy amplitudes
in vacuum
, but holds here only in the low energy limit. Before showing this, let us provide a formula for
n>1
such parameter integrals. Since

\frac{1}{[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-2eF]^{n}}=\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\Bigl(\frac{\partial_{e}}{2F}\Bigr)^{n-1}\frac{1}{\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-2eF}\,,

(36)

and using that

\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{Z}-\frac{\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-Z\cdot\dot{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}}}{\sinh(Z)}=\frac{1}{Z}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}2\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{[2j+1-\dot{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}]\cdot Z}\,,

(37)

one may readily find that

\displaystyle\langle\tau|\frac{1}{[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-2eF]^{n}}|\tau^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{-1}{T}\Bigl(\frac{-1}{2eF}\Bigr)^{n}

(38)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n-1)!}\Bigl(\frac{T}{2}[2j+1-\dot{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}]\Bigr)^{n-1}\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{[2j+1-\dot{G}_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}]\cdot eFT}\,.

Although a closed form solution for this series seems difficult, we can make progress for the functional trace,

\mathcal{J}^{(n)}=\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\langle\tau|\frac{2^{n}}{[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-2eF]^{n}}|\tau\rangle\,.

(39)

Since only even powers of
\dot{G}(\tau,\tau)
survive, we have
\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\dot{G}(\tau,\tau)eFT}=\cosh(eFT)
,
\mathcal{J}^{(1)}=T\big[\frac{1}{eFT}-\textrm{coth}(eFT)\big]
so that

\mathcal{J}^{(n)}=\frac{T}{(n-1)!}\Bigl(\frac{\partial_{e}}{F}\Bigr)^{n-1}\Big[\frac{1}{eFT}-\coth(eFT)\Big]\,.

(40)

We provide a closed form expression for this derivative in Appendix
C
.

It remains, then, to prove the claim that the parameter integrals in
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}
can indeed be reduced to a product of the cycle integrals of
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
and some (potentially complicated) function of the various field strength tensors. To do this we proceed by induction on

there is nothing to do. For the base case

\displaystyle\hskip-10.00002pt\mathcal{I}^{(2)}=

\displaystyle+\textrm{tr}\,\Big(\int_{0}^{T}d\tau_{1}\,\frac{1}{F^{2}+\tilde{F}^{2}}\cdot\big[\dot{\mathcal{G}}_{11},\big\{eF,f\}\big]\cdot\frac{f}{e^{2}}\Big)

A little further rearrangement finds

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(2)}=

III
Master Formulae in spinor SFQED

For the case of spin 1/2 fermions, we should add the contributions from the spin degrees of freedom factors. In the worldline formalism these are represented by “spin factors”
Feynman 1951
;
Strassler 1992
, described in more detail below. For the effective action this leads to a proper time representation

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\text{sp}}[A+a]

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\mathcal{V}_{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{dT\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}}{(4\pi)^{2}T^{3}}K_{\text{sp}}[F+f]\,,

(81)

\displaystyle K_{\text{sp}}[F+f]

\displaystyle=\det{\!}^{\!-\frac{1}{2}}\Big[\frac{\tanh(Z\!+\!z)}{Z\!+\!z}\Big]\,,

(82)

with an additional factor of
\det^{\frac{1}{2}}[\cosh(Z+z)]
entering the matrix determinant from the spin factor:

\displaystyle\textrm{tr}\,\mathcal{P}\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\frac{e}{2}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}}

\displaystyle=\det{}^{\frac{1}{2}}[\cosh(Z+z)]

(83)

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+2e(F+f)]}\,.

(84)

We have again written the determinant in its operator form and used
\sigma^{\mu\nu}=\frac{i}{2}[\gamma^{\mu},\gamma^{\nu}]
. As before we will need a Green function with which we can perform a suitable expansion. As spin degrees of freedom obey anti-periodic boundary conditions on the worldline, this is
\mathcal{G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|F)\eqqcolon\mathcal{G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=2\langle\tau|[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+2eF]^{-1}|\tau^{\prime}\rangle
, satisfying
\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{0\tau^{\prime}}=-\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{T\tau^{\prime}}
and
\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{\tau 0}=-\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{\tau T}
. Let us go ahead and provide its solution as
Copinger et al. 2024b

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\frac{\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ZG(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}}{\cosh(Z)}

(85)

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\frac{2Z}{T}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})}\bigl[\mathrm{sgn}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})+\tanh(Z)\big]\,.

(86)

With the anti-periodic Green function we again expand the logarithm in the weak field to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\textrm{Det}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+2e(F+f)\big]}{\textrm{Det}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+2eF\big]}

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\bigl[1+2e(\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+2eF)^{-1}f\bigr]}

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-ie)^{n}}{2n}\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F}}\,,

(87)

where

\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F}=\!(-i)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int^{T}_{0}\!d\tau_{i}\,\textrm{tr}\,(\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{12}\cdot f\cdot\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{23}\cdot f\cdot...\,\cdot\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{n1}\cdot f)\,.

(88)

Written in the form above we can see that this respects the loop replacement rule transforming between scalar and spinor matter already at the level of the exponent (see
Schubert 2001a
;
Edwards and Schubert 2019
), since we now acquire

K_{\text{sp}}[F+f]=K_{\text{sp}}[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-ie)^{n}}{2n}[\mathcal{I}^{(n)}-\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F}]}\,.

(89)

So, to determine the spinor extension to the complex scalar case one may simply replace
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}\to\mathcal{I}^{(n)}-\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F}
. This replacement rule usually holds in the worldline or string-inspired setting only after expanding the exponent to multi-linear order in the field strength polarisation vectors (and carrying out an integration by parts). It holds here at the level of the unexpanded exponent due to the projection onto the low energy limit of the scattering photons. In this sense the so-called “Q-representation” of the amplitudes (see
Schubert 2001a
) is the natural one for this expansion.

For the open line, we must also add the boundary information corresponding to the spin degrees of freedom. Indeed, the propagator can be compactly written in first quantised form as
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2017b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al.
:

\mathcal{S}^{x^{\prime}x}[A+a]=[-m-i\not{D}_{x^{\prime}}]\mathcal{K}^{x^{\prime}x}[A+a]\,,

(90)

where here
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ie(A+a)_{\mu}
and we have introduced the propagator spacetime “kernel” for a second-order formalism of the fermion degrees of freedom (see
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2022b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2020
) which has propertime representation

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{x^{\prime}x}[A\!+\!a]=

\displaystyle-i\!\int^{\infty}_{0}\!\frac{dT\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}}{(4\pi T)^{2}}\textrm{Symb}^{-1}\bigl\{K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F\!+\!f]\bigr\}

(91)

\displaystyle K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F\!+\!f]=

\displaystyle K_{\text{sp}}[F\!+\!f]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\frac{i}{4T}x_{-}\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot\coth(Z\!+\!z)\cdot x_{-}}

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\eta\cdot\tanh(Z+z)\cdot\eta}\,.

(92)

In the above we have introduced the inverse “symbol map” which maps the Grassmann variables,
\eta^{\mu}
to antisymmetrised products of
\gamma
matrices, which produces the Dirac matrix structure of the spinor propagator:

\displaystyle\textrm{Symb}\Big\{\gamma^{[\mu_{1}}\ldots\gamma^{\mu_{n}]}\Big\}=(-i\sqrt{2})^{n}\eta^{\mu_{1}}\ldots\,\eta^{\mu_{n}}\,,

(93)

where antisymmetrisation includes the combinatorial factor
\frac{1}{n!}
. See
Copinger et al. 2024b
for further details including explicit formulae for their operation in (3+1)-dimensions. The factor inside the inverse symbol map is generated in the worldline formalism from an operator inversion:

\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\eta\cdot\tanh(Z\!+\!z)\cdot\eta}=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\eta\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot\eta+\frac{1}{T^{2}}\eta\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}_{F}^{\circ}(Z\!+\!z)\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot\eta}\,.

(94)

Again, our strategy is to expand the exponent in powers of the dynamical photon field strength tensors. Doing this effects the replacement

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|F\!+\!f)=\mathcal{G}_{F}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-ie)^{n}\mathcal{G}_{F}^{(n)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\,,

(95)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{F}^{(n)}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=(-i)^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\int^{T}_{0}\!d\tau_{i}\,\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{\tau 1}\cdot f\cdot\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{12}\cdot f\cdot...\cdot\mathcal{G}_{F}{}_{n\tau^{\prime}}\,.

(96)

Using the above we can determine the expansion of the propertime kernel as

\displaystyle K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F\!+\!f]=K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F]\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-ie)^{n}}{2n}(\mathcal{I}^{(n)}-\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F})}

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\frac{i}{T^{4}}x_{-}\cdot\bigl[2z\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!\cdot Z+z\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!\cdot z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-ie)^{n}(Z\!+\!z)\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}{}^{(n)}{}^{\circ}\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\bigr]\cdot x_{-}}

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\eta\cdot z\cdot\eta+\frac{1}{T^{2}}\eta\cdot\bigl[2z\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{\circ}_{F}\cdot Z+z\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{\circ}_{F}\cdot z+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-ie)^{n}(Z\!+\!z)\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(n)\circ}_{F}\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\bigr]\cdot\eta}\,,

(97)

now ready for an expansion onto multi-linear order.

Remarkably we see from (
63
), (
97
) that a replacement rule persists even on the line, in the low-energy photon limit. We see that in addition to the loop replacement rule that
\mathcal{I}^{(n)}\to\mathcal{I}^{(n)}-\mathcal{I}^{(n)}_{F}
, we have the following accompanying rule on the line that incorporates the contribution from the end points as well:

\displaystyle x_{-}

\displaystyle...{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}{}^{(n)}{}^{\circ}...x_{-}\to x_{-}...{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}{}^{(n)}{}^{\circ}...x_{-}-iT^{2}\eta...{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(n)\circ}_{F}...\eta\,,

(98)

(for the
n=0
case replace
{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}{}^{(n)}{}\to{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!
and
{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(n)\circ}_{F}\to{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{\circ}_{F}
in (
98
)) alongside introducing the additional
-\eta\cdot z\cdot\eta
factor into the exponent.

As before we can readily determine the first few corrections. The first order correction is

\displaystyle\ K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}\,1}[F]

\displaystyle=-\frac{ie}{2}K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F]\,\bigl[\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{F}\bigr]\Big|_{\text{lin}1}\,,

(99)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{F}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{I}^{(1)}_{F}+2iT\eta\!\cdot\!f\!\cdot\!\eta

\displaystyle\;\;\;-\frac{2}{T^{2}}\eta\!\cdot\!\bigl[2iTf\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{\circ}_{F}\!\cdot\!Z+Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(1)\circ}_{F}\!\cdot\!Z\bigr]\!\cdot\!\eta\,.

(100)

And the second order correction becomes

\displaystyle K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}\,2}[F]=\frac{(-ie)^{2}}{4}K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}}[F]

(101)

\displaystyle\bigl[\frac{1}{2}(\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{F})^{2}+\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(2)}-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(2)}_{F}\bigr]\Big|_{\text{lin}2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(2)}_{F}=\mathcal{I}^{(2)}_{F}

(102)

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{T^{2}}\eta\!\cdot\!\bigl[T^{2}f\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{\circ}_{F}\!\cdot\!f-2iTf\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(1)\circ}_{F}\!\cdot\!Z-Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\!\mathcal{G}{}^{(2)\circ}_{F}\!\cdot\!Z\bigr]\!\cdot\!\eta\,.

Again, in Appendix

we give the full expansion of (

To convert these expressions into an expansion of the spinor propagator proper, we return to (

) which now contains two types of contributions. In analogy to
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2022b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2020
;
Copinger et al. 2024b
, we refer to the contributions in which all

of the scattering photons’ field strength tensors are taken from

as the “leading contribution” and the contributions in which one of the field strength tensors is taken from the leading covariant derivative as the “subleading contributions,” which form the distinct parts of the decomposition

\mathcal{S}_{N}^{x^{\prime}x}=-[m+i(\not{\partial}_{x^{\prime}}+ie\not{A}(x^{\prime}))]\mathcal{K}_{N}^{x^{\prime}x}+e\sum_{i=1}^{N}\not{a}_{i}(x^{\prime})\mathcal{K}_{N-1}^{x^{\prime}x}\,,

(103)

where in the

we omit photon

. In vacuum
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2022b
and in a plane wave background

, the subleading terms do not contribute to scattering amplitudes, as they do not survive the LSZ reduction which extracts them from
\mathcal{S}_{N}
, once matter legs go on-shell. In the homogeneous fields considered here, LSZ is troublesome as the fields do not vanish (the gauge potential does not become constant) asymptotically. Rather than performing LSZ, one may truncate the external lines of the coordinate propagator with asymptotic in and out wavefunctions in a constant field to arrive at an amplitude as is done in the standard approach to strong field QED. Therefore we halt at the above form in the coordinate representation (but see the momentum space formulae below).

III.1
Worldline photon vertices

For completeness we also show how the contribution from the spin factor can be obtained from low-energy photon vertex operators in the worldline approach, which are now augmented by the presence of the spin degrees of freedom. We do this for the open line, since we can always obtain the corresponding integrand for the one-loop amplitudes by fixing
x_{-}=0
(and change the measure on the
T
integral).

In analogy with the scalar case, for the spinor case we start from the path integral expanded about the straight line

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{N}^{x^{\prime}x}[A]=(-ie)^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}dT\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\frac{x_{-}^{2}}{4T}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{DBC}}\hskip-7.5pt\mathcal{D}q(\tau)\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,\bigl(\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+\frac{e}{2}q\cdot F\cdot\dot{q}-\frac{e}{T}\,x_{-}\cdot F\cdot q\bigr)}

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,\textrm{Symb}^{-1}\Bigl\{\!\int_{ABC}\!\mathcal{D}\psi(\tau)\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{iS_{\text{sp}}[F]}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}V_{\eta}^{x^{\prime}x}[f_{i}]\Bigr\},

(104)

where

stands for anti-periodic boundary conditions. The corresponding action in the path integral over Grassmann variables producing the Feynman spin factor reads

, and for brevity we write

Ahmadiniaz et al.
;
Copinger et al. 2024b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2017a
to linear order in the photon momentum (in fact the spin-coupling to the line already starts at this order). Explicitly this leads to

(105)

Similarly to the scalar case, the spinor propagator kernel can be written as a worldline expectation value as

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{N}^{x^{\prime}x}[A]

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dT\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}}{(4\pi T)^{2}}\textrm{Symb}^{-1}K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}\,N}[F]\,,

(106)

\displaystyle K^{x^{\prime}x}_{\text{sp}\,N}[F]

\displaystyle=(-ie)^{N}K[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\frac{x_{-}^{2}}{4T}}

\displaystyle\times\Big\langle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\frac{ie}{T}\int_{0}^{T}d\tau\,x_{-}\cdot F\cdot q}\prod_{i=1}^{N}V_{\eta}[f_{i}]\Big\rangle_{\mathrm{DBC},\,\mathrm{ABC}}\,,

where we write for the anti-periodic Grassmann expectation value

\langle\mathcal{O}\rangle_{\mathrm{ABC}}=\frac{\int_{ABC}\!\mathcal{D}\psi(\tau)\,\mathcal{O}\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{iS_{\text{sp}}[F]}}{\int_{ABC}\!\mathcal{D}\psi(\tau)\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{iS_{\text{sp}}[F]}}\,,

(108)

with the basic rule for Wick contractions of Grassmann fields being
\langle\psi^{\mu}(\tau)\psi^{\nu}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_{F}^{\mu\nu}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

We again focus on the one photon amplitude in the spinor case, choosing

\displaystyle-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(2)}_{F21}+(\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{1}-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{F1})(\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{2}-\mathcal{I}^{x^{\prime}x(1)}_{F2})\bigr]\,,

IV
Momentum space formulae

For the purpose of calculating scattering amplitudes with external matter legs, it is of course more convenient to work with the correlation functions in momentum space. In the worldline formalism this is usually achieved by first computing the path integral with vertex operator insertions to obtain a Master Formula in position space, which is then Fourier transformed to momentum space (see, for example
Edwards and Schubert 2019
;
Schubert 2001a
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2022b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2020
;
Copinger et al. 2024b
;
Ahmad et al. 2017a
). Since the scattering photons’ field,
a(x)
, appears already in the path integral exponent, for plane wave states at arbitrary energies the transition to momentum space is not immediate.

In the present case, however, the projection onto the low-energy limit of the photon scattering amplitudes allows this ordering to be reversed, so that we can
first
carry out the Fourier transform, thereby obtaining path integral representations of the dressed propagator directly in momentum space.

Let’s begin with the first approach – we take the Fourier transform of the coordinate space propagator directly,
viz.

\mathcal{D}^{p^{\prime}p}[A+a]=\int d^{4}x^{\prime}d^{4}x\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{i(p^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}-p\cdot x)}\mathcal{D}^{x^{\prime}x}[A+a]
. As before we need only examine the case of superposed homogeneous fields and then later implement the plane wave expansion of the scattering photons and subsequent projection onto the multi-linear contribution. Starting with (
68
) without vertices (
N=0
) we compute the Fourier transform before integrating out the fluctuations. After changing variables to
x_{+}=\frac{1}{2}(x^{\prime}+x)
and
x_{-}=x^{\prime}-x
and computing the Gaussian integrals we find (using
\hat{\delta}_{p^{\prime}p}=2\pi\delta(p^{\prime}-p)
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{p^{\prime}p}[A\!+\!a]=-i\hat{\delta}^{4}_{p^{\prime}p}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-5.0ptdT\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}K^{pp}[F\!+\!f]\,,

(121)

\displaystyle K^{pp}[F\!+\!f]=(4\pi T)^{2}\int_{\mathrm{DBC}}\hskip-10.00002pt\mathcal{D}q(\tau)\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau[\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+\frac{e}{2}q\cdot(F\!+\!f)\cdot\dot{q}]}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip^{2}T+i\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau\bigl[2ep\cdot(F\!+\!f)\cdot q-\frac{e^{2}}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau^{\prime}\,q(\tau)\cdot(F\!+\!f)^{2}\cdot q(\tau^{\prime})\bigr]}\,.

(122)

Unusually for worldline calculations, this path integral appears with a new non-local kinetic term – note that we are still integrating over trajectories with homogeneous DBC in coordinate space with the momenta appearing as parameters under the integral. Of course we may also perform the Fourier transform after having integrated over trajectories (i.e. from (
56
)) leading to the known result
Ahmad et al. 2017a

\displaystyle K^{pp}[F\!+\!f]

\displaystyle=\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip\cdot\frac{T\tanh(Z\!+\!z)}{Z\!+\!z}\cdot p}\det{}^{\!-1/2}[\cosh(Z+z)]\,.

(123)

We shall show that (
122
) reproduces this in the following section and develop a matrix expansion of (
123
) along similar lines to our above work in position space. At this point we remind the reader that the momentum space propagator can take on different functional forms, connected to gauge choices in the gauge covariant coordinate space propagator (the holonomy factor interacts non-trivially with the Fourier integrals). We explore this tangential point in the Appendix
B
.

For the spinor propagator “kernel” in momentum space, i.e.
\mathcal{K}^{p^{\prime}p}[A\!+\!a]=\int d^{4}x\int d^{4}x^{\prime}\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{i(p^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}-p\cdot x)}\,\mathcal{K}^{x^{\prime}x}[A\!+\!a]
, we will have by analogy with the scalar case (since the spinor degrees of freedom do not couple to the initial and final spacetime points):

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{p^{\prime}p}[A\!+\!a]=-i\hat{\delta}^{4}_{p^{\prime}p}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\hskip-5.0ptdT\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}\textrm{Symb}^{-1}\{K^{pp}_{\text{sp}}[F\!+\!f]\}\,,

(124)

\displaystyle K_{\text{sp}}^{pp}[F\!+\!f]\coloneqq(4\pi T)^{2}\int_{\mathrm{DBC}}\hskip-10.00002pt\mathcal{D}q(\tau)\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau[\frac{\dot{q}^{2}}{4}+\frac{e}{2}q\cdot(F\!+\!f)\cdot\dot{q}]}

\displaystyle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip^{2}T+i\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau\bigl[2ep\cdot(F\!+\!f)\cdot q-\frac{e^{2}}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau^{\prime}\,q(\tau)\cdot(F\!+\!f)^{2}\cdot q(\tau^{\prime})\bigr]}

\displaystyle\times 2^{-\frac{D}{2}}\,\int_{A}\!\mathcal{D}\psi(\tau)\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{iS_{\text{sp}}[F]}\,.

(125)

Therefore in this section we confine our attention to the scalar case, noting that the resulting formulae for spinors follow with minimal modification.

To compute this path integral we need to study the non-diagonal operator
\hat{\chi}
associated to the non-local kinetic term, defined with matrix elements

\displaystyle\mathcal{\chi}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|F)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big\langle\tau\big|\hat{\partial}_{\tau}^{2}-2eF\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-\frac{4e^{2}}{T}\hat{F}^{2}\big|\tau^{\prime}\big\rangle

(126)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-2eF\partial_{\tau}\big)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})-\frac{4e^{2}}{T}F^{2}\,.

(127)

The inverse of this operator,
\hat{\Xi}=\hat{\chi}^{-1}
, satisfies the “position-space” Green equation (there are no zero modes)

\big(\partial_{\tau}^{2}-2eF\partial_{\tau}\big)\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{4e^{2}}{T}F^{2}{}^{\circ}\Xi(\tau^{\prime})=\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\,,

(128)

where again we compactly write for the background field case
\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|F)\eqqcolon\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
, and borrow the derivative and integral notation introduced for
\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}
below (
58
). The Green equation is solved by (see Appendix
D
)

\displaystyle\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+\frac{4}{T^{3}}\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!{}(\tau)\cdot\frac{Z^{2}}{1-\frac{4}{T^{3}}{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!\cdot Z^{2}}\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}(\tau^{\prime})

(129)

\displaystyle=\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})+\frac{4}{T^{3}}\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}\!{}^{\circ}\!(\tau)\cdot Z\cdot\tanh(Z)\cdot{}^{\circ}\!\underset{{}^{\smile}}{\Updelta}{}(\tau^{\prime})\,.

(130)

We also require the path integral normalisation obtained from the functional determinant (also in Appendix
D
)

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}[F]

\displaystyle=\textrm{Det}{}^{\!-\frac{1}{2}}\Big[\hat{\partial}_{\tau}^{2}-2eF\hat{\partial}_{\tau}-\frac{4e^{2}}{T}\hat{\rm F}^{2}\Big]

(131)

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{(4\pi T)^{2}}\det{}^{\!-\frac{1}{2}}(\cosh(Z))\,.

(132)

With these results in hand, we now employ the path integral representation of the momentum space propagator to develop expansions in
f
of the background-dressed propagators analogous to those in sections
II
and
III
. Let us compute the Gaussian path integral in (
122
) to find

K^{pp}[F\!+\!f]\eqqcolon\widetilde{K}[F\!+\!f]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip^{2}T+i\frac{4}{T^{2}}p\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi{}^{\circ}\cdot(Z\!+\!z)\cdot p}\,,

(133)

where here
\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|Z\!+\!z)
. This already rederives (
123
) once we use the formulae for integrals of
\Xi
given in Appendix
D
.

As before we may now formally exponentiate the functional determinant factor

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}[F\!+\!f]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{K}[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Tr}\ln\big\{1-\hat{\Xi}[2ef\hat{\partial}_{\tau}+\frac{4e^{2}}{T}(\hat{F}\hat{f}+\hat{f}\hat{F}+\hat{f}^{2})]\big\}}\,,

(134)

where at this stage
\hat{\Xi}=\hat{\Xi}(F)
depends only on the background field. The presence of terms both linear and quadratic in
F
make a closed formula for the expansion in the low energy field strength tensors more challenging now; however we can provide results order by order.

Let us first expand the functional determinant. By expanding the logarithm in the weak field and introducing suitable resolutions of the identity we can produce the contribution corresponding to insertions of scattering photons. For example, at
\mathcal{O}(f)
in the exponent we get the following (reusing the notation
\Xi_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}=\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
)

\widetilde{K}[F\!+\!f]=\widetilde{K}[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{e\,\mathrm{tr}[\int^{T}_{0}\!d\tau(f\cdot{}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi{}_{\tau\tau})+\frac{4}{T^{2}}f\cdot Z\cdot{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!]+\mathcal{O}(f^{2})}\,.

(135)

Next we expand the momentum dependent exponent of (
133
). This involves a geometric series expansion in
f
for the Green function
\hat{\Xi}(F+f)
and a simple binomial expansion for the polynomial operators sandwiching the various
\Xi(F)
. We again restrict attention to the first order correction. For the Green function this is

\displaystyle\Xi(\tau,\tau^{\prime}|F\!+\!f)

\displaystyle=\Xi_{\tau\tau^{\prime}}+2e\int^{T}_{0}\!d\sigma\,\Xi_{\tau\sigma}\cdot f\cdot{}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi_{\sigma\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+\frac{4e}{T^{2}}{\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}}\!{}_{\tau}\cdot[f\cdot Z+Z\cdot f]\cdot{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi_{\tau^{\prime}}+\mathcal{O}(f^{2})\,.

(136)

Then using the above along with the expansion for the determinant to
\mathcal{O}(f)
in (

N=1

\displaystyle K^{pp}_{1}[F]

\displaystyle=eK^{pp}[F]\Bigl\{\mathrm{tr}\Bigl[\frac{4}{T^{2}}f\cdot Z\cdot{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!+\int^{T}_{0}\!d\tau f\cdot{}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi{}_{\tau\tau}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+\frac{8i}{T^{2}}p\!\cdot\!\Bigl[Tf\!\cdot\!Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!+\int^{T}_{0}\!d\sigma Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi_{\sigma}\!\cdot\!f\!\cdot\!{}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!{}_{\sigma}\!\cdot\!Z

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{T^{2}}Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!\!\cdot\!f\!\cdot\!Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!\!\cdot\!Z\Bigr]\!\cdot\!p\Bigr\}\,.

(137)

We shall verify this result by a worldline calculation below (one can, of course, compare this with a direct Fourier transform of the position space result, (

), where agreement is found after substituting the explicit form of
\Xi
from (

) and using its integral properties in ((

)).

IV.1
Momentum space vertex operator

We are now well-situated to derive the vertex operator for low-energy photon scattering amplitudes already in momentum space. In fact, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, this is the first time that a momentum space vertex operator has been derived on the worldline
using Dirichlet boundary conditions
(the obstacle to doing this for finite external photon momenta is that the worldline action ceases to be quadratic in the path integral variable); a momentum space vertex operator has been considered for a path integral with Neumann boundary conditions in
Bonezzi and Kallimani 2025
. Let us remark that the low-energy limit applies only to the photons and not to the matter momenta, where we can take
p
to be large.

To derive the vertex operator let us start from
K^{pp}[F+f]
before taking the path integral, (
122
). To derive the photon vertex operators we wish to consider
f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}
and take the part multi-linear in the
f_{i}
. To achieve this we first linearise the term in the action that is quadratic in
f
, we do this by introducing an auxiliary
field,
X(\tau)
, as in
Bastianelli et al. 2024
so we can rewrite
3
3

3

We observe that, in this case, a valid integral representation could be achieved by using an auxiliary
variable
,
X
(say). However, we ascribe to the worldline philosophy that all degrees of freedom should be represented by path integrals on equal terms.

\hskip-10.00002pt\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\frac{e^{2}}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau\int_{0}^{T}d\tau^{\prime}\,q(\tau)\cdot f^{2}\cdot q(\tau^{\prime})}=\int\mathcal{D}X(\tau)\,\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-i\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau(\frac{X^{2}}{4}+\frac{e}{T}X\cdot f\cdot Q)},

(138)

where
Q\coloneqq\int^{T}_{0}\!d\tau\,q(\tau)
, the Wick contractions for the auxiliary field are defined by
\langle X(\tau)X(\tau^{\prime})\rangle=2i\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
and its free path integral is normalised to unity. If we use the above equation in the momentum space worldline kernel, it is rewritten in terms of an expectation value of new, momentum space vertex operators

\displaystyle K_{N}^{pp}[F\!+\!f]

\displaystyle=(-ie)^{N}\widetilde{K}[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip^{2}T}

(139)

\displaystyle\Bigl\langle\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{2ie\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau p\cdot F\cdot q}\,\prod_{i=1}^{N}V^{p}[f_{i}]\Bigr\rangle_{\text{DBC, X}}\,,

where the expectation values are taken with respect to both
q(\tau)
and
X(\tau)
. Here the low-energy vertices read

\displaystyle V^{p}[f_{i}]:=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau_{i}\;\Big(\frac{1}{2}q_{i}\cdot f_{i}\cdot\dot{q}_{i}-2\,p\cdot f_{i}\cdot q_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}X\cdot f_{i}\cdot Q+\frac{2}{T^{2}}q_{i}\cdot f_{i}\cdot Z\cdot Q\Big).

Of course, the zero background field limit,

Considering the basic Wick contraction of the fields in momentum space

\displaystyle K^{pp}_{1}[F]=-ieK^{pp}[F]\int_{0}^{T}\!d\tau_{1}\,\Big[i\textrm{tr}\,({}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi_{11}\cdot f_{1})

(141)

\displaystyle-\frac{8}{T^{2}}\,p\cdot\bigl(Tf_{1}\cdot{\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}}\!_{1}\cdot Z+Z\cdot{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi_{1}\cdot f_{1}\cdot{}^{\bullet}\,\!\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}\!_{1}\cdot Z\bigr)\cdot p

\displaystyle+\frac{4}{T^{2}}\int d\tau_{2}\Bigl(i\mathrm{tr}(Z\!\cdot\!f_{1}\!\cdot\!\Xi_{12})-\frac{8}{T^{2}}p\!\cdot\!Z\!\cdot\!{}^{\circ}\,\!\Xi{}_{1}\!\cdot\!f_{1}Z\!\cdot\!{\Xi\,\!{}^{\circ}}\!{}_{2}\!\cdot\!Z\!\cdot\!p\Bigr)\Big]\,,

in agreement with (

For the spinor case, referring to (

), it is clear that we only need to append the spin degrees of freedom contractions inside the symbol map. These were, however, already worked out in (

), so it remains to take the linear part of either the scalar or the spinor contributions. At higher order, (e.g.

The formalism presented here produces all such contributions via a worldline path integral with vertex operator insertions.

IV.2
Matrix expansion

The matrix expansion technique may be applied to the momentum space propagator here without difficulty. Let us see how this expansion looks like for the worldline kernel (again here in this subsection we restrict our attention to the case of scalars). Let us perform the Taylor expansion of the exponent in (
123
) to find

\hskip-5.0ptK^{pp}[F\!+\!f]=K^{pp}[F]\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\!\frac{(f\cdot\partial_{F})^{n}}{n!}\big\{ip\cdot\frac{T\tanh(Z)}{Z}\cdot p-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\ln[\cosh(Z)]\big\}}\,.

(142)

The expansion of the functional determinant factor is essentially the same as for the spin contribution to the effective action, so we do not require any new results here. However to do the matrix derivative of the momentum dependent term we will require its Lorentz invariant decomposition for factors with
p
. This can, of course, be found using projection operators, as in Sec.
II.2.2
, as

\displaystyle p\cdot\frac{\tanh(Z)}{Z}\cdot p=p^{2}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\Bigl[\frac{\widetilde{Z}^{2}}{Z^{2}+\widetilde{Z}^{2}}\frac{\tanh(Z)}{Z}\Bigr]

\displaystyle+(p\cdot Z^{2}\cdot p)\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\Bigl[\frac{1}{Z^{2}+\widetilde{Z}^{2}}\frac{\tanh(Z)}{Z}\Bigr]\,.

(143)

So, for the
N=1
insertion, performing the matrix derivatives one may readily find an alternative representation of (

V
Conclusions

In this article, we have derived three different ways to calculate the effective action and propagator in a homogeneous background electromagnetic field additionally dressed by scattering photons in the low energy limit. These are associated to one-loop (respectively tree-level) photon scattering amplitudes in the constant background. We did this for both scalar and spinor matter in SFQED, and in both coordinate space and momentum space on the line. We showed that in the low-energy photon limit both the one-loop effective action and propagator can be related to their counterparts dressed by a superposition of a constant background field and constant field strengths produced by the scattering photons. The study of the scattering amplitudes is thus reduced to a multi-linear expansion of the dressed effective action or propagator in the scattering photons.

For each case we developed a functional expansion based on series representations of matrix elements associated to first quantised operators that arise in the worldline approach to QFT. This approach recovered existing results for the low energy one-loop amplitudes for scalars and spinors
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2024a
and extends them to arbitrary background configurations. For the propagator, we derived new compact Master Formulae that include all possible contributions from the expansion, which includes propagation endpoint dependence. En route we also discover a replacement rule from scalar to spinor quantities that extended
to the open line
(that holds in the low-energy limit). To treat the momentum space propagator case, we developed a novel approach using a worldline path integral over trajectories (with Dirichlet boundary conditions) for the momentum space correlation function.

We also corroborated the expansions (up to order
N=2
) through a direct computation of the effective action and propagator using the worldline formalism, with scattering photons represented by vertex operator insertions under the path integrals. Last, we presented a different but equivalent way to evaluate the expansion recursively using standard calculus, deriving formulae for a Taylor expansion of the path integral determinant, obviating the difficulty of evaluating parameter integrals for an arbitrary background field. Of particular significance we show that it is possible to evaluate the worldline parameter integrals in an arbitrary homogeneous field by reducing them to familiar cycle integrals; this required new identities and Lorentz invariants obtained in terms of the field strength tensors and their duals.

There are several avenues for future work. The closed-form evaluation of the parameter integrals for arbitrary multiplicity scattering off matter lines would be a naturally mathematical line of inquiry generalising existing studies of worldline integrals in vacuum
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2024b
;
Ahmadiniaz et al. 2022c
. Closed form expressions could provide access to important information on the behaviour of particle propagation and cascades in a strong homogeneous background field. Selecting a specific configuration for the electromagnetic field (parallel electric and magnetic fields or constant cross fields) relative to on-shell scattering photons could help simplify the tensor structure of the results presented here, and shed further light on the problem of scattering in this background
Lopez-Lopez 2025
. A merit of our low-energy approach is that higher-multiplicity (e.g.
\geq N=4
) tree-level scattering quantities are readily available – as opposed to the difficulty of carrying out such calculations in the Furry picture at generic photon energy. And it would be interesting to compare low-energy scattering with high-powered laser observations in a phenomenological setting, where vacuum birefringence, photon splitting and general light-by-light interactions are studied. Last, the evaluation of higher loop contributions to the propagator, while technically more challenging, is of high importance in SFQED in the context of the famous Ritus-Narozhny conjecture
Ritus 1985
;
Fedotov 2017
. It may be hoped that the known efficiency of the worldline formalism will allow easier access to higher loop calculations and their asymptotic behaviour.

Appendix B
Momentum space propagator and Fock-Schwinger gauges

The use of Fock-Schwinger gauge is particularly convenient in the worldline formalism, both in constant fields
Ahmad et al. 2017b
;
Dittrich and Shaisultanov 2000
;
Reuter et al. 1997
and in inhomogeneous fields

;
Fliegner et al. 1998
;
Auer et al. 2004
. In the constant field case, the advantage is that the gauge potential,

, where

vanishes, is taken to be the loop centre of mass (respectively initial point of the line) when calculating the effective action (respectively matter propagator). This may appear strange, since the loop centre of mass is integrated over, and likewise for the propagator endpoints when transforming to momentum space. Hence both the effective action and momentum space propagator so constructed receive contributions from path integrals corresponding to
different
choices of reference point, and hence different gauges. This is not problematic, since the effective action (propagator) are gauge (co-)variant, and so physical observables are not affected.

For completeness, let us present here the matter propagator (we restrict to scalar QED in
D=4
) instead calculated with a
fixed
reference point,
\hat{x}
. In configuration space, this is easily related to our results of the main text by the gauge holonomy

,
where

.
Note, then, that in contrast to shifting between two
constant

\hat{x}\rightarrow\hat{x}^{\prime})
, this holonomy factor is quadratic in the endpoints. Hence, a linear shift in the reference gauge already introduces a non-linear variation in the momentum space propagator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{p^{\prime}p}(\hat{x})=-i\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{ip_{-}\cdot\hat{x}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!dT\,(4\pi T)^{2}\hskip 1.00006pt\textrm{e}^{-im^{2}T}K^{p_{+}p_{+}}[Z]

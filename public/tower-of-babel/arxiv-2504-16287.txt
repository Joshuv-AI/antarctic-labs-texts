Deformations of reducible Galois representations with large Selmer p p -rank
Eknath Ghate, Anwesh Ray

Abstract
. Let p ≥ 5 p\geq 5 be a prime number. In this paper, we construct Galois representations associated with modular forms for which the dimension of the p p -torsion in the Bloch-Kato Selmer group can be made arbitrarily large. Our result extends similar results known for small primes, such as Matsuno’s work on Tate-Shafarevich groups of elliptic curves. Extending the technique of Hamblen and Ramakrishna, we lift residually reducible Galois representations to modular representations for which the associated Greenberg Selmer groups are minimally generated by a large number of elements over the Iwasawa algebra. We deduce that there is an isogenous lattice for which the Bloch–Kato Selmer group has large p p -rank.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main result

Let
h
be a normalized Hecke eigencuspform of weight
k\geq 2
and
p
be an odd prime number. Let
F
be the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
h
. Let
\mathfrak{p}|p
be a prime of
F
, set
\mathcal{K}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=F_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}
. Associated to
h
is Deligne’s
2
-dimensional representation

\rho_{V_{h}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(V_{h})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{K}).

Let
V
be a self-dual twist of
V_{h}
, i.e.,
V
is isomorphic to its Tate-dual
\operatorname{Hom}(V,\mathcal{K}(1))
. Choose a Galois stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
T
contained in
V
, and let

\rho_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(T)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})

be the integral Galois representation. Let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V/T
and let
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})
be the associated Bloch-Kato Selmer group. In this article we construct Galois representations for which
\dim_{\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})[\mathfrak{p}]
is arbitrarily large. Related results have been proven in various contexts for small prime numbers. Matsuno
[
Mat07
]
proved, using the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves that for primes
p\in\{2,3,5,7,13\}
and any number
N>0
, there exists an elliptic curve
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
for which
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\Sh(E/\mathbb{Q})[p]\geq N
. For the prime
p=2
, see the work by Flynn
[
Fly18
]
who proves this result for certain abelian surfaces. Bartel
[
Bar10
]
studied the growth of the Tate–Shafarevich group in certain dihedral extensions of the form
D_{2p}
. Related work of Česnavičius
[
Česna17
]
studies the growth of the Selmer group of an abelian variety over a number field
K
in
p
-cyclic extensions
K^{\prime}/K
. In particular, the
p
-torsion in the Selmer group over
K^{\prime}
can have large dimension. The distribution of Tate–Shafarevich groups in quadratic twist families of abelian varieties has been considered by Bhargava et. al.
[
BKLOS21
]
. Our main result is stated below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime number and
N
be a natural number. Then, there exists a Galois representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(T)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
arising from a
p
-ordinary Hecke eigen-cuspform of weight
2
and a choice of prime
\mathfrak{p}|p
, such that

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})[p]\geq N.

In fact, we prove that any reducible Galois representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
with
p\geq 5
and satisfying some additional conditions lifts to a modular representation
\rho
for which

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})[p]\geq N.

We refer to Theorem
5.7
for further details.

1.2.
Methodology

Matsuno’s construction focuses on the relationship between the Selmer groups of a pair of elliptic curves,
E
and
E^{\prime}
, over
\mathbb{Q}
that are related by a rational cyclic
p
-isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
. These Selmer groups are considered over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, where their algebraic properties are intertwined. Under specific additional local conditions, Matsuno demonstrates that the
p
-torsion in the Tate-Shafarevich group of
E^{\prime}
can become large. For Galois representations
A
arising from modular forms with ordinary reduction at
p
, similar results can be proven. This is achieved by adapting the methods of Hamblen and Ramakrishna
[
HR08
]
, who construct modular lifts of residually reducible Galois representations. The modularity of such representations follows from the results of Skinner and Wiles
[
SW99
]
.

In greater detail, we start with a residual representation

\bar{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi&*\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

which is then lifted to a modular Galois representation

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

that satisfies certain additional local conditions. Let
T
be a self-dual twist of the underlying
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module, and let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
be the associated Galois module. Since
\bar{\rho}
is reducible, there exists another Galois-stable lattice
T^{\prime}
containing
T
as a submodule of index
p
. Defining
A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, we obtain a rational
p
-cyclic isogeny
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\phi
is a
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
-equivariant surjective homomorphism whose kernel is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
be the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
and let
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
be the Greenberg Selmer group of
A
over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
. Let
S
be the set of primes at which
\rho
is ramified. There is an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})[p]\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}[p]\rightarrow\prod_{\ell\in S}\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell,A}[p]

relating the Greenberg Selmer group of
A
to the Bloch–Kato Selmer group of
A
and the local Tamagawa numbers
\operatorname{ker}h_{\ell,A}
. There is a similar sequence for
A^{\prime}
. We show that if the middle term of the above exact sequence is large for
A
, then it is large for
A^{\prime}
as well. The local Tamagawa number conditions on
A
ensure that the middle term is large for
A
. Hence the middle term for
A^{\prime}
is also large. Furthermore, the Tamagawa numbers of
A^{\prime}
are trivial for most
\ell\in S
. We deduce that
\dim\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q})[p]
is large.

1.3.
Organization

Including the introduction, the article consists of five sections. Section
2
is preliminary in nature. We discuss the Iwasawa theory of Bloch–Kato and Greenberg Selmer groups as well as their algebraic properties. We introduce the Iwasawa algebra and the Iwasawa invariants associated to the Greenberg Selmer group. In section
3
we discuss lower bounds for the dimensions of the
p
-ranks of the Selmer groups. We study the specific case when there are two lattices
A
and
A^{\prime}
which are related by a
p
-cyclic isogeny, and the relationship between their Bloch–Kato Selmer groups. In section
4
we discuss the deformation theory of reducible Galois representations and discuss a variant of the strategy of Hamblen and Ramakrishna. In section
5
, we prove Theorem
5.7
and the main result, i.e., Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Iwasawa theory of Selmer groups

In this section, we discuss the Iwasawa theory of Selmer groups associated to a Galois stable lattice that arises from a Hecke eigencuspform. We begin by introducing the Bloch–Kato and Greenberg Selmer groups. Then we introduce Iwasawa theoretic invariants associated to Greenberg Selmer groups considered over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. These invariants encapsulate the algebraic structure of Selmer groups, viewed as modules over the Iwasawa algebra. For further details, we refer to the original works of Greenberg
[
Gre89
,
Gre97
]
and Bloch–Kato
[
BK90
]
.

2.1.
The Bloch–Kato and Greenberg Selmer groups

Throughout, we fix an algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
and set
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
. Without further mention, any algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
considered in this article will be assumed to be contained in the chosen algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
. Given a prime
\ell
, set
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbb{Q}_{\ell})
, and choose an embedding
\iota_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
. Note that
\iota_{\ell}
induces an inclusion
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
\tau
be the variable in the complex upper half plane and set
q\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{exp}\left(2\pi i\tau\right)
. Consider a normalized Hecke eigencuspform
h=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}
on
\Gamma_{1}(N)
of weight
k\geq 2
and let
F_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\{a_{n}\mid n\geq 1\})
be the number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
h
. Fix an odd prime number
p
and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a choice of prime of
F_{h}
that lies above
p
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the completion of
F_{h}
at
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathcal{O}
be the valuation ring of
\mathcal{K}
. Let
\varpi
be the uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}
and
\kappa\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathcal{O}/(\varpi)
be the residue field of
\mathcal{O}
. Associated to
h
is the Galois representation
\rho_{h,\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{K})
, introduced by Eichler, Shimura and Deligne. Set
V_{h}\simeq\mathcal{K}^{2}
to be the underlying
\mathcal{K}
-vector space. Let
\chi
be the cyclotomic character and
\varepsilon
be the nebentype character. Note that there is a perfect pairing

V_{h}\times V_{h}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\chi^{k-1}\varepsilon).

We shall assume that there is a character
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}^{\times}
such that
\psi^{2}=\chi^{2-k}\varepsilon^{-1}
. Consider a self-dual twist
V\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{h}(\psi)
. Our constructions will depend on a choice of Galois stable
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
T\subset V
. Setting
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V/T
, we find that
A
is
p
-divisible and is isomorphic to
\left(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{O}\right)^{2}
. Let
\rho_{V}
(resp.
\rho_{T}
) be the Galois representation associated to
V
(resp.
T
)

\begin{split}&\rho_{V}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{K}}(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{K});\\
&\rho_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathcal{O}}(T)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}).\\
\end{split}

Let
\bar{T}
be the mod
\mathfrak{p}
reduction of
T
and
\bar{\rho}_{T}
be the associated Galois representation

\bar{\rho}_{T}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\kappa}(\bar{T})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\kappa).

The representation
\bar{\rho}_{T}
is referred to the
residual representation
associated to
\rho_{T}
. Given any two Galois stable lattices
T
and
T^{\prime}
in
V
, then
\bar{\rho}_{T}
is not always isomorphic to
\bar{\rho}_{T^{\prime}}
. However, if
\bar{\rho}_{T}
is absolutely irreducible, then, there is a unique Galois stable lattice
T
up to scaling. The case that shall interest us in this article is when
\bar{\rho}_{T}
is reducible.

Setting
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F_{h}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
to denote the natural inclusion, we assume that
h
is
\mathfrak{p}
-ordinary
, i.e.,
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{p})
is a unit in
\mathcal{O}
. Let
\operatorname{I}_{p}
be the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{p}
. There is a natural filtration

0\rightarrow V_{h}^{+}\rightarrow V_{h}\rightarrow V_{h}^{-}\rightarrow 0

of
\mathcal{K}[\operatorname{G}_{p}]
-modules, where
\operatorname{dim}_{\mathcal{K}}V_{h}^{\pm}=1
. Twisting by
\psi
, we obtain the exact sequence

0\rightarrow V^{+}\xrightarrow{\iota}V\xrightarrow{\pi}V^{-}\rightarrow 0,

where
V^{\pm}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V_{h}^{\pm}(\psi)
. This induces a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow T^{+}\rightarrow T\rightarrow T^{-}\rightarrow 0

of
\mathcal{O}[\operatorname{G}_{p}]
-modules. Here,
T^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T\cap V^{+}
and
T^{-}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi(T)
. As is well known,
T^{+}
and
T^{-}
are free
\mathcal{O}
-modules of rank
1
. Setting
A^{\pm}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V^{\pm}/T^{\pm}
, we find that (
2.1
) induces the following short exact sequence

0\rightarrow A^{+}\rightarrow A\rightarrow A^{-}\rightarrow 0.

As
\mathcal{O}
-modules,
A^{+}
and
A^{-}
are isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{O}
.

Given a natural number
n
, let
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}})
denote the cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
generated by the
p^{n}
-th roots of unity, set
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{n}\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n}})
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
denote the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. We refer to
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
as the
cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension
of
\mathbb{Q}
. Set
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q})
and
\Gamma_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^{n}}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{n}/\mathbb{Q}
be the extension contained in
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
such that
[\mathbb{Q}_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=p^{n}
, we find that
\Gamma_{n}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{n}/\mathbb{Q})
. The Iwasawa algebra is the completed group algebra

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathcal{O}[\Gamma_{n}],

where the inverse limit is taken with respect to natural quotient maps

\pi_{m,n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}[\Gamma_{m}]\rightarrow\mathcal{O}[\Gamma_{n}]

for
m\geq n
. Choose a topological generator
\gamma\in\Gamma
and set
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\gamma-1\in\Lambda
. We identify
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}\llbracket T\rrbracket
.

In order to define the Bloch–Kato (resp. Greenberg) Selmer group associated to
A
over
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, we must first introduce local conditions. Given a prime
v
of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, let
\operatorname{I}_{n,v}
denote the inertia subgroup of
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}_{n,v}}
. Let
\ell\neq p
be a prime number and
v|\ell
be a prime of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
. For
\ast\in\{V,T,A\}
, define

H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},\ast)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},\ast)\longrightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},\ast)\right\}.

The quotient map
V\rightarrow A
induces a map of cohomology groups

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A);

we set

\begin{split}&H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V),\\
&H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Im}\left\{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\right\}.\end{split}

It is easy to see that
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
is contained in
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
. This follows from the commutativity of the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},A).}

For a prime
\ell\neq p
, it follows from
[
Rub00
, Lemma 1.3.5 (i)]
that
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)
is the divisible part of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)
. In particular, the index
[H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)]
is finite.

Definition 2.1
.

At a prime
\ell\neq p
, the Tamagawa number of
A
at
\ell
is defined as follows

c_{\ell}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},A)].

On the other hand, if
v|p
is a prime of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, define the local condition at
v
as follows

\begin{split}&H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\mathbf{B}_{\operatorname{cris}})\right\};\\
&H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{im}\left\{H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},V)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\right\}.\end{split}

Definition 2.2
.

Define the Bloch–Kato Selmer group of
A
over
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
by

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n},A)\longrightarrow\prod_{v}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}\right\},

where
v
ranges over the primes of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
. The Bloch–Kato Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
is defined to be the following direct limit

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n}).

We come to the notion of the Greenberg Selmer group. Given a prime
v
of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
which lies above
p
, consider the composite

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},A)\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},A^{-})

where the first map is restriction to
\operatorname{I}_{n,v}
and the second map is induced by the natural quotient
A\rightarrow A^{-}
. For a prime
v|p
of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
, the local Greenberg condition is defined as follows

H^{1}_{\operatorname{ord}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)\rightarrow H^{1}(\operatorname{I}_{n,v},A^{-})\right\}.

Definition 2.3
.

With respect to notation above, define

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n},A)\longrightarrow\prod_{v\nmid p}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}\times\prod_{v|p}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{ord}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}\right\}.

The Greenberg Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
is defined as the following direct limit

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n}).

For ease of notation, let

J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\bigoplus_{v|\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}&\text{ if }\ell\neq p;\\
\bigoplus_{v|\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{\operatorname{ord}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}&\text{ if }\ell=p;\end{cases}

and set
J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})
with respect to natural restriction maps. Let
S
be the set of primes
\ell
which divide
Np
. The Greenberg Selmer group over
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
fits into the following exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}).

Likewise, we set

J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigoplus_{v|\ell}\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}{H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)}

and

J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n}).

Then the Bloch-Kato Selmer group fits into a natural short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in S}J_{\ell}^{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}).

For all
n
, there is a natural inclusion

\iota_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{n}),

see
[
Och00
, Proposition 4.1, part 1]
. In fact, for the prime
v
of
\mathbb{Q}_{n}
such that
v|p
, one has that
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
is a finite index subgroup of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{ord}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
, cf.
[
Fla90
, Theorem 3]
.
Recall that for
v\nmid p
,
H^{1}_{f}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
is the divisible part of
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{n,v},A)
. Thus the Bloch–Kato conditions are contained in the Greenberg conditions. Passing to the direct limit, we find that there is an inclusion

\iota_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}).

Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}
be the composite of maps

(2.2)

\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{BK}}(A/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}.

2.2.
Iwasawa modules and invariants

We introduce the algebraic Iwasawa invariants associated to the Greenberg Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})
. Given
\Lambda
-modules
M
and
M^{\prime}
a
pseudo-isomorphism
is a map of
\Lambda
-modules
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
whose kernel and cokernel are both finite. A polynomial in
\mathcal{O}\llbracket T\rrbracket
is

if it is monic and its non-leading coefficients are in
(\varpi)
. Let
M
be a finitely generated and torsion module over
\Lambda
. By the structure theory of such modules, there is a pseudo-isomorphism

(2.3)

M\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\Lambda/(p^{\mu_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\Lambda/(f_{j}(T)),\right)

where

are distinguished polynomials. The

-invariant and
\lambda
-invariant of

are defined as follows

\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mu_{i}\text{ and }\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{j=1}^{t}\operatorname{deg}f_{j}(T).

We set
\mu(M)=0
(resp.
\lambda(M)=0
) if

(resp.
t=0
). These quantities are well defined, i.e., independent of the choice of decomposition (
2.3
). Let
g(M)
be the minimal number of generators of
M
as a
\Lambda
-module. It follows from Nakayama’s lemma that

g(M)=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{M}{(\varpi,T)M}\right).

Given a
\Lambda
-module
M
, let

denote its Pontryagin dual. As is well known, it is a result of
[

]
that
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}
is torsion as a
\Lambda
-module. Denote by
\mu(A)
(resp.
\lambda(A)
) the
\mu
-invariant (resp.
\lambda
-invariant) of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}
. We mention here in passing that the Greenberg Selmer groups considered in this article are shown to be cotorsion over
\Lambda
, independent of Kato’s result (see Remark

). Set
g(A)
to denote the number of generators of
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}
. We note that

g(A)=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\frac{\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}}{(\varpi,T)\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\vee}}\right)=\dim_{\kappa}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})^{\Gamma}[\varpi]\right).

3.

In this section, we study the relationship between Selmer ranks and Iwasawa theory. We begin by recalling a rather general version of Cassels’ theorem.

\varphi_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\psi}
. The module
A[p]
sits in a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow A_{1}\rightarrow A[p]\rightarrow A_{2}\rightarrow 0,

where
A_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi_{i})
. From the above, we have the exact sequence

(3.11)

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{1})\xrightarrow{a}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])\xrightarrow{b}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{2}).

We note that since
\varphi\bar{\psi}
is ramified at
p
,
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},A_{1})=0
. On the other hand, since
\bar{\psi}
is odd,
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},A_{2})=0
. Since
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}
is a pro-
p
extension, it follows from
[
NSW08
, Corollary 1.6.13]
that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{i})=0
for
i=1,2
. It follows therefore that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])=0
. Consider the Kummer sequence

0\rightarrow A[p]\rightarrow A\xrightarrow{\times p}A\rightarrow 0

and the associated long exact sequence in cohomology:

\dots\rightarrow H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)\xrightarrow{\times p}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A).

Since
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])=0
it follows that
H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)=0
. As a result, we have an isomorphism:

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A)[p].

Thus we can view
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})[p]
as a subgroup of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])
. If
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})[p]
is infinite then it follows that either:

•

\mathscr{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Image}(a)\cap\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})[p]
is infinite, or,

•

\mathscr{B}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=b\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})[p]\right)
is infinite.

Thus in order to complete the proof it suffices to show that both
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are finite.

Since the character
\varphi_{1}
is even, it follows from
[
Gre99
, Lemma 5.9]
that
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A_{1})
is finite and we deduce that
\mathscr{A}
is finite. We show that
\mathscr{B}
is finite. Since
\varphi
is ramified and
\bar{\psi}
is unramified,
A^{-}[p]
is the unique
1

A[p]
. Thus we may identify
A_{2}
with
A^{-}[p]
. From our identifications, the Greenberg Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{\operatorname{Gr}}(A/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})[p]
consists of
\alpha\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},A[p])
such that

•

\alpha
is unramified at all primes
w\nmid p
of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
,

•

b(\alpha)
is unramified at all primes of
\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
.

Let
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))

H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))

\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}
. Since

belongs to
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))
, it suffices to show that
H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))
is finite. Let
K\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\psi})
be the abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which is cut out by
\bar{\psi}
and
K_{\infty}
the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of

. Consider the inflation-restriction sequence:

0\rightarrow H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}),H^{0}(K_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))\right)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/K_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi})).

Note that
H^{1}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty}),H^{0}(K_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))\right)
vanishes as
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/\mathbb{Q}_{\infty})

H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/K_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))
is finite. The action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/K)
on
\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi})
is trivial. Thus, a non-zero element
\beta\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{nr}}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/K_{\infty},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\bar{\psi}))
gives a surjective homomorphism
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}_{S}/K_{\infty})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
which is unramified at all primes of
K_{\infty}
. Let
\mathcal{L}/K_{\infty}
be the maximal
p

K_{\infty}
in which all primes of
K_{\infty}
are unramified. The homomorphism
\beta
factors as a surjective map
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\mathcal{L}/K_{\infty})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}
be the maximal abelian unramified pro-
p

, and set

asserts that the Iwasawa

∎

4.
Deformations of reducible Galois representations

The deformation theory of Galois representations is a powerful tool introduced by Mazur. Over the years, it has been employed crucially in establishing the modularity of Galois representations
\rho
in characteristic zero, when it is known that the residual representation
\bar{\rho}
is modular. We shall use this technique to construct modular Galois representations with prescribed local properties. Starting with a residual representation
\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, we study the functor of deformations
\rho_{R}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
of
\bar{\rho}
, where
R
is a local ring with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We begin by setting up some notation and recalling some well-known facts from Galois cohomology.

4.1.
Cohomological facts

Given a finite dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we set
V^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}\left(V,\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)
. Let
M
be a finite
\mathbb{F}_{p}[\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-module, denote by
M^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}\left(M,\mu_{p}\right)
its Tate dual. For
i\in\{1,2\}
and a finite set of primes
\Sigma
containing
p
and the primes which ramify for the Galois action on
M
, set

\Sh_{\Sigma}^{i}(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}H^{i}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)\right).

By the Poitou-Tate duality theorem

\Sh_{\Sigma}^{2}(M)\simeq\Sh_{\Sigma}^{1}(M^{*})^{\vee}.

For each prime
\ell\in\Sigma\cup\{\infty\}
, let
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
be a subspace of
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)
. Since
p
is odd,
H^{1}(\mathbb{R},M)=0
, and in particular, this forces
\mathcal{L}_{\infty}=0
. The data
\mathcal{L}=\{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\Sigma}
is known as a
Selmer datum
, and the associated Selmer group is defined as the following kernel:

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}\right)\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}\subseteq H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M^{*})
consist of classes that pair to
0
with
\mathcal{L}_{\ell}
. The dual Selmer group is defined as follows:

H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M^{*})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M^{*})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M^{*})}{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}^{\perp}}\right)\right\}.

Wiles’ formula
[
NSW08
, Theorem 8.7.9]
gives a relationship between the dimensions of the Selmer and dual Selmer groups:

(4.1)

\begin{split}\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M)-\operatorname{dim}H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M^{*})=&\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},M)-\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q},M^{*})\\
&+\sum_{\ell\in\Sigma\cup\{\infty\}}\left(\operatorname{dim}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}-\operatorname{dim}H^{0}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)\right).\end{split}

The Poitou–Tate sequence gives the following exact sequence:

(4.2)

\begin{split}0&\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M)\rightarrow H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M)}{\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}\right)\\
&\rightarrow H^{1}_{\mathcal{L}^{\perp}}(\operatorname{G}_{\Sigma},M^{*})^{\vee}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\Sigma}/\mathbb{Q},M)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\ell\in\Sigma}H^{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},M),\end{split}

see for instance,
[
Tay03
, p. 555, l.7]
.

4.2.
Deformation functors

A significant part of the theory is understanding how deformations behave locally at each prime
\ell
. There are local-global principles that can be formulated via Galois cohomological techniques. In order to understand global deformations of
\bar{\rho}
, it is enough to understand the local deformations at each prime
\ell
and ensure that these local deformations satisfy certain conditions encapsulated by subfunctors. Let
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=W(\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the ring of Witt vectors with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and note that
p
is the uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}
. Consider a character
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and the reducible Galois representation

\bar{\rho}=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi&*\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

The isomorphism class of the entry
*
can be viewed as a cohomology class in
H^{1}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F}_{q}(\varphi))
. Let
S
be the set of primes
\ell
such that either
\ell=p
or
\ell\neq p
and
\bar{\rho}
is ramified at
\ell
. Given a prime
\ell
and a Galois representation
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\cdot)
, we denote by
\rho_{|\ell}
the restriction of
\rho
to
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
.

Let
\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
be the category whose objects are complete noetherian local
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
R
with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, such that
R/\mathfrak{m}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
as an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. Given
R\in\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}
, there is a unique
\mathcal{O}
-algebra map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which induces the isomorphism
R/\mathfrak{m}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A morphism in this category is a map of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R_{1}\rightarrow R_{2}
. An important family of examples includes
\mathcal{O}/p^{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the group
\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}
or
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
(for a prime
\ell
), and let
\bar{\varrho}
denote
\bar{\rho}
or
\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}
respectively. A
lift

\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
of
\bar{\varrho}
is a homomorphism that reduces to
\bar{\varrho}
, as illustrated in the following commutative diagram:

\mathcal{G}
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
\bar{\varrho}
\varrho

The vertical arrow is induced by the quotient map
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The set of lifts
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
is denoted
\operatorname{Lift}_{\bar{\varrho}}(R)
. This association gives rise to a functor of lifts

\operatorname{Lift}_{\bar{\varrho}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{CNL}_{\mathcal{O}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sets}.

Given a prime
\ell
, and
\rho\in\operatorname{Lift}_{\bar{\rho}}(R)
, the restriction
\rho_{|\ell}
is a lift of
\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}
. This gives rise to a natural transformation

\operatorname{Lift}_{\bar{\rho}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Lift}_{\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}}.

Definition 4.1
.

We set
\widehat{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(R)
to be the kernel of the reduction map

\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}).

Two lifts
\varrho,\varrho^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
of
\bar{\varrho}
are said to be strictly equivalent if
\varrho^{\prime}=A\varrho A^{-1}
for some
A\in\widehat{\operatorname{GL}_{2}}(R)
. A deformation is a strict equivalence class of lifts.

Denote by
\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}}
and
\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}}
the associated functors of deformations. For each prime
\ell
, the restriction of a global deformation to
\operatorname{G}_{\ell}
gives a natural transformation

\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\ell
be a prime. A
deformation functor
at
\ell
is a subfunctor
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
of
\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}}
. For each coefficient ring
R
, the set
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(R)
is a subset of
\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\rho}_{|\ell}}(R)
. We say that a deformation
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\ell}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(R)
of
\bar{\rho}_{\restriction\ell}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
if
\varrho\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}(R)
.

4.3.
Tangent and obstruction spaces

The lifting strategy is based on a local-global principle, where local deformation functors are fixed and global deformations are required to satisfy these functors at the primes where they are allowed to ramify.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\varrho}
denote the Galois module whose underlying vector space consists of
2\times 2
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The Galois action is as follows: for
g\in\mathcal{G}
and
v\in\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\varrho}
, set
g\cdot v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\rho}(g)v\bar{\rho}(g)^{-1}
. Let
\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\varrho}
be the
\mathcal{G}
-stable submodule of trace zero matrices.

We note that
\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\varrho}
breaks up into a direct sum

\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\varrho}=\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\varrho}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot\operatorname{Id},

where the second summand is generated by the scalar matrices. Let
D\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{O}}
be the ring of dual numbers given by
D\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
. An infinitesimal deformation
\varrho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(D)
of
\bar{\varrho}
is a deformation of
\bar{\varrho}
to
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(D)
. We describe how infinitesimal deformations are in bijection with cohomology classes
f\in H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})
. Let
\widetilde{f}\in Z^{1}(\mathcal{G},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})
be a cocycle representing
f
. We check that the formula

(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f})\bar{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(D)

defines a homomorphism lifting
\bar{\rho}
. We see that

\begin{split}&(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{1}))\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1})(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{2}))\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{2})\\
&=(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{1}))(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1})\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{2})\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1})^{-1})\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1})\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{2})\\
&=(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{1})+\epsilon\sigma_{1}\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{2}))\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\\
&=(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}))\bar{\rho}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}),\end{split}

and it is clear from our definition that
(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f})\bar{\rho}
reduces to
\bar{\rho}
modulo
(\epsilon)
. The deformation represented by
(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon\widetilde{f})\bar{\rho}
depends only on

, and is referred to as the
twist
by

. We denote this deformation by
(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon f)\bar{\rho}
. The association

f\mapsto(\operatorname{Id}+\epsilon f)\bar{\rho}

gives a bijection

H^{1}(\mathcal{G},\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho})\leftrightarrow\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}(D).

The mod
(\epsilon)
reduction map

is a special case of what’s known as a

, and we can extend the above bijection to a generalized setting.

Definition 4.4
.

Let

and

be coefficient rings over

with maximal ideals

and

respectively. A surjective map of

•

•

Remark 4.5
.

We note that for

\pi_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{O}/(p^{n+1})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}/(p^{n})

is a small extension, with

generated by

.

Given a small extension
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R\rightarrow S
with kernel
(t)
, consider the reduction map at the level of deformations

\pi^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}(S)

.
Suppose that

\bar{\varrho}

. Let

Here, the product

is a well defined homomorphism satisfying the property that

by

Let

\pi_{\nu}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}^{\nu}(R)\rightarrow\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}^{\nu}(S)

We now discuss the cohomological obstructions to lifting. Let
\pi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}R\rightarrow S
be a small extension and
\varrho_{S}\in\operatorname{Def}_{\bar{\varrho}}(S)
. Let

c&-a\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\operatorname{Ad}\bar{\rho}
and choose a lift
\widetilde{A}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\widetilde{a}&\widetilde{b}\\
\widetilde{c}&-\widetilde{a}\\
\end{array}}\right)
with entries in

are lifts of

respectively. Then, we set

to denote the matrix

t\widetilde{c}&-t\widetilde{a}\\
\end{array}}\right)

is independent of the choice of

. Note that

we find that the association taking

Let

with determinant

The map

Lemma 4.11
.

The set of trivial primes has positive density.

Proof.

We note that a prime
\ell

splits in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p},\bar{\rho})

\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{2}})

\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p},\bar{\rho})

\mathbb{Q}_{1}
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p},\bar{\rho})
. We show first that
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho})
. The extension

is a prime to
p

is an extension of
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho})
of degree dividing

. Since
\mathbb{Q}_{1}
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p},\bar{\rho})

\mathbb{Q}_{1}
is contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho})
. We note that
\bar{\rho}
is an indecomposable representation of the form
\bar{\rho}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi&\ast\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
. Let
G
be the image of
\bar{\rho}
. The map
\sigma\mapsto\bar{\rho}(\sigma)
identifies
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\bar{\rho})/\mathbb{Q})

\ell\in X
, pick
f_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
such that

(4.6)

(\operatorname{Id}+p^{2}f_{\ell})(\xi_{3})_{|\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}.

Proposition
4.13
asserts that
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
preserves
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for small extensions
\mathcal{O}/p^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}/p^{n-1}
for

. It thus follows that any translate of
f_{\ell}
by an element in
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
will also satisfy (

). Thus, we can view
f_{\ell}
as an element in the quotient
\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\right)
. The tuple

(f_{\ell})\in\bigoplus_{\ell\in X}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\right)

arises from a global cohomology class

since the map (
4.5
) is an isomorphism. We set
\rho_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\operatorname{Id}+p^{2}f)\xi_{3}
and find that
\rho_{3}
satisfies the liftable deformation conditions
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
at all primes
\ell\in X
. Note that this argument does not apply to show
\rho_{2}
satisfies the
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
conditions for all
\ell\in X
since it is not known that
\mathcal{N}_{\ell}
preserves
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for the extension
\mathcal{O}/p^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}/p
. This is why
[

]
is necessary when constructing
\rho_{2}
.

Since
\rho_{3}
satisfies the liftable deformation conditions
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for
\ell\in X
, the obstruction to lifting
\rho_{3}
to

\xi_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/p^{4})

lies in
\Sh_{X}^{2}(\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
. This group is evidently
0
since
X
contains
T
. Hence, the lift
\xi_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/p^{4})
exists. There is a tuple

(g_{\ell})\in\bigoplus_{\ell\in X}\left(\frac{H^{1}(\mathbb{Q}_{\ell},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})}{\mathcal{N}_{\ell}}\right)

which is defined by the property that for all
\ell\in X
,

(\operatorname{Id}+p^{3}g_{\ell})(\xi_{4})_{|\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{\ell}.

This tuple arises from a global cohomology class
g\in H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{X},\operatorname{Ad}^{0}\bar{\rho})
since the map (
4.5
) is an isomorphism. We take
\rho_{4}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\operatorname{Id}+p^{3}g)\xi_{4}
and find that
\rho_{4}
by design satisfies the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
at all primes
\ell\in X
. In this way, we continue the argument inductively to obtain a compatible family of deformations

\rho_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}/p^{n})

which satisfy the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
for all primes
\ell\in X
. Take
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{X}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
to denote the inverse limit
\varprojlim_{n}\rho_{n}
. We find that
\rho
is odd, irreducible and satisfies the conditions
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
at the primes
\ell\in X
. In particular, it satisfies

, which makes it ordinary at
p
. If
\varphi_{|p}\neq 1
, then a result of Skinner and Wiles
[
SW99
,
Theorem
on p. 6]
implies that

is modular. We remark that recent work of Pan
[
Pan22
]
removes the condition

Main results

In this section, we prove the main result of this article, i.e., Theorem
1.1
. Let
p\geq 5
. We start with a mod
p
representation

\bar{\rho}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\varphi&\ast\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p}).

Assume that:

(1)

\bar{\rho}
is indecomposable,

(2)

\varphi=\bar{\chi}\beta^{2}
where
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is an odd character which is unramified at
p

(3)

\beta^{2}\neq 1

It is clear that
\bar{\rho}
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
4.6
with
q=p

\beta^{2}\neq 1
implies that
\varphi\neq\bar{\chi}

\beta
is unramified at

\varphi_{|\operatorname{I}_{p}}=\bar{\chi}_{|\operatorname{I}_{p}}

, it follows that
\bar{\chi}^{2}_{|\operatorname{I}_{p}}\neq 1

\varphi^{2}\neq 1
and
\varphi\neq\bar{\chi}^{-1}

\varphi_{|p}\neq 1
, one finds that
\bar{\rho}_{|p}
is not of the form
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ast\\
&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
.

Recall that
\Sigma
is the set of primes containing
p
and the primes at which
\bar{\rho}
is ramified. Let
\nu
be the character
\chi\widetilde{\beta}^{2}
. We extend the arguments of Hamblen and Ramakrishna to show that modular lifts of
\bar{\rho}
exist which also satisfy a number of local conditions at a prescribed set of primes. These local conditions will guarantee large
p
-rank in the Bloch–Kato Selmer group associated to a certain Galois stable lattice. Suppose that
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
is a
p
-ordinary modular lift of
\bar{\rho}
with determinant
\nu
and let

be the associated eigencuspform. Denote by

the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-lattice corresponding to
\rho
and set
V_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=T_{\rho}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. We set
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}
. Note that
\psi^{2}=\chi\nu^{-1}=\widetilde{\beta}^{-2}
.

Observe that
k=2
is the weight of
f
and
\widetilde{\beta}^{2}
is its nebentype. Let
V

and

\rho\otimes\psi

e_{1}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{-1}e_{1}
and
e_{2}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=e_{2}

(\rho\otimes\psi)^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(T^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

be the Galois representation associated to

with respect to the basis
(e_{1}^{\prime},e_{2}^{\prime})
. Since
\bar{\rho}\otimes\bar{\psi}
is upper triangular, we can write
(\rho\otimes\psi)(g)=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
pc&d\\
\end{array}}\right)

\end{array}}\right)\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\

In particular, the mod

is of the form
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{\psi}\varphi&\\
\ast&\bar{\psi}\\
\end{array}}\right)

We set

A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V/T=(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{1}\oplus(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{2}\text{ and }A^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=V/T^{\prime}=(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{1}^{\prime}\oplus(\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{2}^{\prime},

We now describe conditions at a prime

y&1\\
\end{array}}\right),

p^{2}|x
and
p||y

, with respect to the
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}

(\varrho\otimes\psi)^{\prime}(\sigma_{\ell})=\psi(\sigma_{\ell})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\ell
be a trivial prime. Assume that
\rho_{|\ell}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
of Type
II, then, we have that
\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0
. Furthermore, if
\beta_{|\ell}=1
, then
\delta_{\ell}(A)=1
.

Proof.

First we show that
\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0
. Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(A^{\prime})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}/(A^{\prime})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}_{\operatorname{div}}
, and recall from Lemma
3.2
that

\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\left(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}/(\sigma_{\ell}-1)\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\right)[p]\right).

We show that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=0
, i.e., that
(A^{\prime})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}
is divisible. From this we deduce that
\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0
. Recall from (
5.3
) that
\tau_{\ell}
acts on
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}e_{1}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}e_{2}^{\prime}
by the matrix
\psi(\tau_{\ell})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
y/p&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
, where
p||y
. Recall that
\psi=\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}
and thus
\psi(\tau_{\ell})=1
. Thus, the action of
\tau_{\ell}
on
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is via
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
y/p&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
. Since
y/p
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, it follows that
(A^{\prime})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}e_{2}^{\prime}
. In particular,
(A^{\prime})^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}
is divisible and thus,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=0
. We have thus proven that
\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0
.

Now we assume that
\beta_{|\ell}=1
and we show that
\delta_{\ell}(A)=1
. Since
\psi=\widetilde{\beta}^{-1}
, we have that
\psi_{|\ell}=1
, and in particular,
\psi(\sigma_{\ell})=1
and
\psi(\tau_{\ell})=1
. Let
\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=A^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}/(A^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}})_{\operatorname{div}}
, and recall from Lemma
3.2
that

\delta_{\ell}(A)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\left(\left(\mathcal{A}/(\sigma_{\ell}-1)\mathcal{A}\right)[p]\right).

By (
5.2
),
\tau_{\ell}
acts on
A
via the matrix
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
y&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
.
Since
p||y
, we thus find that as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-module

(5.4)

A^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}}=\left(p^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)e_{1}\oplus\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}e_{2}.

Thus,
(A^{\operatorname{I}_{\ell}})_{\operatorname{div}}=\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}e_{2}
and
\mathcal{A}=\left(p^{-1}\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)e_{1}
. On the other hand,

(\sigma_{\ell}-1)e_{1}=\left(\psi(\sigma_{\ell})-1\right)e_{1}+\psi(\sigma_{\ell})xe_{2}=xe_{2}.

We thus write

(\sigma_{\ell}-1)(p^{-1}e_{1})=\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)e_{2},

and find that
(\sigma_{\ell}-1)(p^{-1}e_{1})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{1}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})e_{2}
. Thus, we have shown that

(\sigma_{\ell}-1)\mathcal{A}=0
. Hence,
\mathcal{A}/(\sigma_{\ell}-1)\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}
and we conclude that
\delta_{\ell}(A)=1
.
∎

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
(\mathcal{C}_{\ell},\mathcal{N}_{\ell})
is of Type
III if it is the conjugate of
(\mathcal{D}_{\ell}^{\operatorname{ram}},\mathcal{P}_{\ell}^{\operatorname{ram}})
by
\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&1\\
\end{array}}\right)
.

The action of
\sigma_{\ell}
and
\tau_{\ell}
on
A
with respect to the basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
is given by:

(5.5)

\varrho\otimes\psi(\sigma_{\ell})=\psi(\sigma_{\ell})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
(x+1-\ell)&\ell\\
\end{array}}\right)\text{ and }\varrho\otimes\psi(\tau_{\ell})=\psi(\tau_{\ell})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
y&1\\
\end{array}}\right).

The action on

on the other hand is given by:

(5.6)

\varrho\otimes\psi(\sigma_{\ell})=\psi(\sigma_{\ell})\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

Note that since

and

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\ell
is a trivial prime and that
\rho_{|\ell}
satisfies
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}
of Type
III. Then, we have that
\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0

By (

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\

\end{array}}\right)

\delta_{\ell}(A^{\prime})=0

\psi(\sigma_{\ell})=1

\psi(\tau_{\ell})=1

∎

.

Proposition 5.5

\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta^{\prime}
. On the other hand, if
\beta^{\prime}
is even, set
\beta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\beta^{\prime}\alpha
where
\alpha
is an odd quadratic character which is unramified at
p
. Having chosen
\beta
, choose
\ast
to be a non-zero cohomology class in the infinite-dimensional vector space
H^{1}(\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{F}_{p}(\varphi))
, for
\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{\chi}\beta^{2}
. The representation
\bar{\rho}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
5.7
, from which the result follows.

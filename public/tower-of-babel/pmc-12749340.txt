An AM-CNN-BiGRU network with spatiotemporal feature fusion for industrial robot predictive maintenance
Authors: Zihao Zang, Yiwen Zhang, Shouxin Ruan, Lei Wang, Xinming Zhang, Linsen Song, Yibo Huang, Shuhong Ye
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to aging, high operational loads, and harsh working environments, industrial robots are prone to various failures. Predictive maintenance (PdM) offers a promising solution to this challenge. This study proposes a PdM method for industrial robots based on AM-CNN-BiGRU neural network. First, a convolutional neural network (CNN) and a bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU) are integrated to leverage the CNN’s ability to extract local spatial features and the BiGRU’s capability for learning global bidirectional temporal dependencies. This combination effectively captures the nonlinear and deep features within the data. Second, vibration, current, and torque data from the robot joints are input into the CNN as distinct channels, enabling multi-source data fusion. Concurrently, an attention mechanism (AM) is incorporated to assign adaptive weights to different data sources, enhancing the identification of critical fault signatures. Finally, ablation studies were conducted, and comparative experiments were performed between AM-CNN-BiGRU and several of the most representative and latest algorithms in the time series forecasting domain. During training, the Lion optimizer was employed for the deep learning models. The results demonstrate that AM-CNN-BiGRU achieved optimal performance across four evaluation metrics: R², MAE, MAPE, and RMSE, thereby validating its effectiveness and reliability.

Introduction

Industrial robots have been used to automate manufacturing processes due to their intelligence and high degree of integration to improve productivity, production quality, and safety1. However, with aging, high loads, and harsh operating environments, industrial robots are exposed to various failure problems such as accuracy degradation and component damage2. This can seriously affect the robot’s performance and efficiency, and can lead to unplanned downtime of the entire production line, resulting in significant economic losses3. The emergence of predictive maintenance (PdM) provides a new approach to address this problem. PdM is a maintenance methodology that continuously monitors equipment and issues alerts before equipment failure occurs. Its core function is failure prediction so that equipment maintenance can be performed before a failure occurs. Therefore, condition monitoring as well as failure prediction of industrial robots can significantly reduce maintenance costs and improve equipment utilization4,5. In the industrial field, there are three main approaches to predictive maintenance, including reliability-based PdM, model-based PdM, and data-driven PdM6.

Reliability-based PdM typically utilizes the statistical characteristics of historical failure data for fault prediction. Consequently, it requires neither the physical structural characteristics of the equipment nor extensive sensor data7. However, reliability-based predictive maintenance is better suited for structurally simple equipment or components and necessitates substantial long-term experimentation and statistical analysis. Model-based PDM typically analyzes the correct behavior of equipment to construct mathematical or physical models reflecting the physical laws underlying performance degradation8. Zhang et al.9,10 proposed a novel expectation maximization-unscented particle filter-Wilcoxon rank sum test (EM-UPF-W) method to adaptively estimate noise variables in degradation models and accurately detect capacity regeneration points under conditions of unlabeled small sample data. Additionally, they introduced an adaptive remaining useful life (RUL) prediction approach based on Kalman filtering and Rauch-Tung-Striebel expectation maximization (KF-EM-RTS) to address the uncertainty in parameter estimation and the lack of historical data for individual batteries. Nevertheless, establishing precise physical models for complex equipment is challenging, which adversely affects the accuracy of predictive maintenance outcomes. Therefore, neither approach is well suited for complex and high-value equipment, such as industrial robots. Data-driven PdM eliminates the requirement for detailed mechanistic knowledge of equipment. Instead, it only needs to start from the data and comprehensively mine the features of the large amount of production data, thus realizing the accurate prediction of faults11,12. Data-driven approaches primarily encompass machine learning and deep learning. Machine learning has been widely applied in PdM. Zhang et al.13 proposed a lifetime extension approach based on a Levenberg-Marquardt back-propagation neural network (LM-BPNN) and power routing in an interleaved DC-DC boost conversion system, aiming to accurately estimate the state of health without identifying a systematic mathematical model and to extend the system’s operational lifetime. Li et al.14 proposed a PdM framework for rotating motors based on machine learning classification and regression modeling and aimed to predict the specific types of faults that may occur in a given motor and determine its RUL. Baptista et al.15 introduced a PdM method combining an autoregressive moving average (ARMA) model with a support vector machine (SVM) regression model. This approach analyzes and extracts health indicators and performance degradation features hidden within equipment data to predict the risk of future failures in specific components or systems. Xu et al.16 proposed a method for detecting bolt loosening in industrial robot joints based on electromechanical modeling and motor current signature analysis (MCSA). They established a dynamic equation coupling motor current with bolt loosening, extracted time-frequency characteristics from the motor current, and subsequently employed an SVM algorithm for bolt loosening detection. Izagirre et al.17 presented a data acquisition and PdM network architecture for industrial robots. They utilized support vector regression (SVR) and extreme learning machine (ELM) algorithms to detect degradation states in robot joints. Validation was performed on a real automotive assembly line by extracting torque signals via a programmable logic controller (PLC) as model inputs. Raouf et al.18 implemented fault prediction for six types of failures in industrial robot drive systems using an SVM model with three-phase current signals as inputs. Pan et al.19 extracted features from vibration signals, which served as inputs to an artificial neural network (ANN) algorithm, achieving prediction of joint backlash faults in industrial robots.

Deep learning, as a special machine learning method, has excellent performance in big data processing, deep feature adaptive extraction, and fault prediction by virtue of the design of deep architectures20,21. Many researchers have made significant progress in the field of deep learning, especially in fault prediction. He et al.22 proposed a multiscale mixed convolutional neural network (MSMCNN) based on CNN, which can adaptively extract the depth features of the vibration signals of the robot joints and realize the fault prediction of the harmonic reducer in the robot joints. Long et al.23 proposed a multiscale convolutional capsule network (MCCN), which can effectively predict the fault of industrial robots by collecting attitude data, extracting multi-scale features using CNN, and identifying the spatial relationship in the attitude data using CapsNet. Chen et al.24 proposed a lightweight detection model (Micro-Net) based on a multifunctional local convolutional algorithm and used current signals as inputs to achieve fault prediction in industrial robots. Pan et al.25 proposed a method for predicting joint failures in industrial robots based on a Deep Residual Neural Network (DRNN). Hakami26 aiming to address the challenge of scarce fault data in predictive maintenance, employed Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to synthesize data and utilized Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks to extract temporal features. Joseph et al.27 developed a hyperparametrically tuned LSTM model for predictive maintenance of industrial robots. Park et al.28 constructed an edge device integrating data acquisition, processing, storage, and analysis, and implemented fault prediction for industrial robots based on LSTM. Qian et al.29 proposed an improved GRU network, which achieves excellent fault detection capability even with a limited sample size. Dai et al.30 proposed a new reliability assessment scheme based on BiGRU, Wavelet Kernel Network (WKN), and Wiener process model. Building on this foundation, recent research has begun to introduce multi-source data fusion and attention mechanisms to optimize PdM model performance. For instance, Gültekin et al.31 proposed a CNN-based data fusion method that fuses sound and vibration signals while utilizing the short-time Fourier transform to achieve accurate prediction of operational faults in automatically running vehicles. Liu et al.32 proposed a failure prediction method for chain jack hydraulic systems based on multi-source data fusion. The proposed method utilizes pressure, temperature, and flow data under different operating conditions. Huang et al.33 proposed a multi-scale convolutional neural network (CA-MCNN) with an attention mechanism. In CA-MCNN, the learned features are scored, and weights are assigned adaptively through the attention mechanism to improve the learning ability of the model. Tong et al.34 proposed a coordinated attention (CA) model suitable for one-dimensional vibration signals, and established a lightweight coordinated attention convolutional neural network ACNN to realize the fault detection of rolling bearings.

In summary, recent studies have shown that deep learning models such as CNN, GRU, and LSTM exhibit significant advantages in solving fault prediction by virtue of their excellent spatial feature extraction capabilities or temporal feature extraction capabilities. However, GRU and LSTM in essence process fault time series information in a unidirectional manner, ignoring the inverse information, which will ultimately affect the accuracy of the fault prediction model. Meanwhile, based on the literature analysis above, there is more limited research on the integration of spatial and temporal feature extraction capabilities in the predictive maintenance domain. In addition, most existing fault prediction efforts for industrial robots essentially use only a single data source, such as vibration, torque, or current, and extracting multidimensional features from multi-sensor data to provide accurate and reliable predictions remains a challenge.

To cope with these complex challenges, this paper proposes a PdM model for industrial robots featuring spatio-temporal feature fusion, named AM-CNN-BiGRU. This framework integrates the attention mechanism, the convolutional neural network (CNN), and the bidirectional gated recurrent unit (BiGRU). A combination of CNN and BiGRU. CNN can automatically extract local spatial features from the processed signals, and BiGRU utilizes its bi-directional time-series modeling capability to capture temporal features during the fault evolution process. Proposed methodology

Framework of the PdM system

In this paper, an overall PdM framework is first proposed for implementing the industrial robot predictive maintenance method, AM-CNN-BiGRU. As illustrated in Fig. 1, the framework comprises four phases. In the data collection phase, vibration signals, current signals, and torque signals are collected from each joint of the industrial robot.

Fig. 1The overall framework for the implementation of AM-CNN-BiGRU.

The overall framework for the implementation of AM-CNN-BiGRU.

In the data preprocessing phase, statistical features are first extracted from the collected raw time series signals, followed by standardization. The resulting feature matrix is then dimensionality-reduced using principal component analysis (PCA) to alleviate the model’s computational burden and accelerate processing. In this phase, the extracted statistical features include the maximum, mean, peak-to-peak, root mean square, kurtosis, waveform factor, and margin factor in the time domain, along with the centroid frequency, mean frequency, root mean square of frequency, and frequency variance in the frequency domain. Convolutional neural network

CNN has maintained its dominant position in the field of image processing. In recent years, it has also been applied to areas such as signal analysis and equipment failure prediction35. CNN performs convolution operations by applying convolution kernels in a sliding-window manner across the input data. Through multi-layer stacking, the shallow convolutional layers capture short-term local features of the data, such as transient impulses or abnormal fluctuations. The deeper convolutional layers then integrate these local features into more complex and global high-level representations. These high-level features exhibit stronger correlations with fault conditions.

CNN typically comprises an input layer, convolution layers, pooling layers, a flattening layer, and a fully-connected output layer. Figure 2 illustrates the CNN architecture.

Bidirectional gated recurrent unit

Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is a variant structure of the Recurrent Neural Network (RNN) model, which can effectively solve the defects of gradient explosion as well as gradient vanishing that the RNN is prone to when dealing with long sequences. GRU integrates the inputs from the “forget gate” and the LSTM neural network into the “update gate”. Compared with LSTM, the GRU network has one less gating unit, so the model parameter reduction is much more efficient36. The operational state of an industrial robot represents a typical temporal evolution process. Fault occurrence is often gradual and progressive, with its precursors being temporally correlated. The bidirectional gating mechanism of BiGRU enables it to simultaneously learn temporal dependencies from both directions. For instance, a subtle anomaly signal might only lead to significant performance degradation after an extended period. While the forward GRU may struggle to retain such long-term dependencies, the backward GRU can retrospectively trace the sequence, thereby more readily identifying early weak anomaly signals. This capability to capture bidirectional temporal dependencies is crucial for accurately determining the progression trend of faults, which remains unachievable by unidirectional network architectures.

Attention mechanism

Attention mechanism is a method proposed according to the human visual system and cognitive mechanism, which can improve the expressiveness of the deep learning model and increase the sensitivity of the model to key features37. Using the attention mechanism in the input data of CNN can link the different channels of the input data to calculate the degree of importance of each channel, assign weights to different channel features, and improve the network’s expressive ability in feature representation, which in turn improves the performance of the model38. Proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU structure

This section details the proposed methodology. Figure 4 shows the model structure of the proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU. The proposed method uses an architecture consisting of a deep stack of CNNs and BiGRUs along with an attention mechanism for feature extraction and time series prediction. CNN has significant advantages over traditional neural networks in terms of sensing the field-of-view and weight sharing between hidden layers. For the problem of fault prediction in industrial robots, where the time series is significantly nonlinear and stochastic, CNN can reduce the network complexity and the number of weights through the mechanism of weight sharing between layers and determine the local input space pattern that produces the strongest response by computing the same convolutional kernel. Before the CNN input data, it first assigns different weights to different channel feature data through the attention mechanism to determine the importance of different source data. BiGRU can further process the spatial features extracted by the CNN and continue to extract the deep temporal features to improve the accuracy of the model in predicting the time series.

Optimizer

Optimizers are algorithms that govern parameter updates during deep learning model training. During backpropagation, optimizers direct each parameter’s adjustment magnitude and direction, progressively minimizing the objective function toward a global minimum. Optimizer selection critically impacts both training efficiency and final model performance. Google Research (2023) proposed a method for discovering optimized algorithms for deep neural network training through program search. After thousands of TPU hours of arithmetic search, combined with human intervention, they obtained the Lion optimizer39.

Use the Lion optimizer to update the parameter expression as follows:22\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{t}+\mathrm{1}} = \beta _{\mathrm{1}}{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathrm{t}}+\text{}\mathrm{(}\mathrm{1} - \alpha \mathrm{(}\mathrm{s}\mathrm{i}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{n}\mathrm{(}{\mathrm{c}}_{\mathrm{t}+\mathrm{1}}\mathrm{)}+\text{}\lambda{\mathrm{W}}_{\mathrm{t}}\mathrm{)}\end{array}$$\end{document}24\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathrm{t}+\mathrm{1}} = \beta _{\mathrm{2}}{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathrm{t}}+\text{}\mathrm{(}\mathrm{1} - In contrast to Adam and various other adaptive optimizers that require the preservation of both first-order moments and second-order moments, the Lion optimizer requires only momentum and utilizes symbolic manipulation to compute the parameter updates, and the same magnitude is used for each parameter update. Its performance gain increases as the size of the training batch increases. It also requires a smaller learning rate due to the larger paradigm of updates produced by the sign function. Another key feature of the Lion optimizer is that it performs momentum updates after variable parameter updates, while eliminating the need to compute second-order moments. This significantly reduces memory consumption and enhances computational efficiency when training large deep learning models39.

The sign function can be regarded as a noise injection or regularization mechanism. Sensor time series data from industrial robots typically contain noise, abrupt changes, and complex nonlinear patterns. This parameter update approach in the Lion optimizer prevents predictive maintenance models from overfitting to noise in training data while theoretically aiding convergence to flatter minima regions in the loss function, thereby enhancing the robustness of predictive maintenance models.

The Lion optimizer has demonstrated superior performance and effectiveness compared to existing classical optimizers across various model training tasks, including language models, image classification models, diffusion models, and image-text matching models. Based on the various advantages of the Lion Optimizer for training predictive maintenance models, this paper pioneers the novel application of Lion to train industrial robot failure prediction models. To the best of our knowledge, this represents the first work to empirically demonstrate the effectiveness of the Lion optimizer for temporal data prediction tasks within the domain of predictive maintenance.

Experiment

To validate the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU model, our research team conducted two sets of experiments. One involved constructing an industrial robot test bench for data collection, building a dataset, and performing experiments. The other utilized the NASA turbofan engine dataset, the most commonly used dataset in the field of predictive maintenance.

Industrial robot dataset

1) Experimental platform construction.

We took the axis end accuracy faults of a six-axis industrial robot as the research object, and established an experimental platform in a high-end laboratory of an automobile factory. As shown in Fig. 5, the experimental bench consists of a six-axis tandem industrial welding robot (KR270 R2700 ultra) equipped with a C-type spot welder, a robot control cabinet (KR C4), a laptop computer (MECHREVO), a data collector (IN-SDG), six vibration acceleration sensors (310 A-20), and a laser accuracy tracker (Leica AT960-MR).

The training of a fault prediction model requires the simultaneous utilization of multiple operating cycles of continuous time-stamped data as input to the model. If the samples of original vibration signals, current signals, and torque signals of six joints from multiple operating cycles are directly inputted into the model for training at the same time, the dimension of the input data will be too large, which not only overly occupies the computer memory, but also affects the training of the model, and is prone to the problem of underfitting or overfitting. Therefore, the statistical features of the samples originally containing 1024 data points are extracted as inputs to the model. As mentioned in the previous section, the extracted statistical features mainly include a total of 11 features in the time and frequency domains. Afterwards, the same statistical feature values corresponding to each joint of the robot are normalized, and their average values are calculated, and a set of 11-dimensional feature matrices is obtained for each signal. Finally, principal component analysis (PCA) is used to downscale the obtained feature matrix. Figure 8 shows the contribution rate of principal components during the dimensionality reduction process of feature matrices corresponding to vibration signals, current signals, and torque signals using PCA. The top 6 principal components of each type of signal whose sum of information contribution is greater than 80% are selected as the input features of the AM-CNN-BiGRU model. As can be seen from the figure, the sum of the first 5 principal components of the current signal has reached 82%, but the first 6 principal components of the current signal are still chosen as input features in order to make the data dimension consistent for each type of signal. We conducted a preliminary experiment and compared the results between using the first six and the first five principal components (for the five-component case, the sixth dimension was padded with a zero). The results were essentially consistent, indicating that using six components does not introduce redundancy and is feasible.

After the statistical feature dimensionality reduction is completed, the input feature data of the model, as well as the repetitive localization accuracy data, need to be packaged and processed in timestamp order. NASA turbofan engine dataset

1) Dataset description.

The NASA Turbofan Engine Dataset is generated by the C-MAPSS (Commercial Modular Aero-Propulsion System Simulation) software to simulate the degradation process of turbofan engines40. This dataset captures the degradation progression of turbofan engines, recording sensor data throughout the entire process, and is widely used for the development and validation of algorithms in the field of predictive maintenance. Twenty-one sensors are deployed at various locations to monitor the engine’s condition. The dataset comprises four subsets, denoted as FD001, FD002, FD003, and FD004. Each subset differs in operational conditions and failure modes. Each subset consists of three parts: a training set, a test set, and RUL labels. The training set contains complete run-to-failure sensor data for each engine, from healthy operation to failure, while the test set contains engines with unknown health states, providing only a portion of the full lifecycle data. In this paper, the FD001 subset, with fixed operational modes and failure modes, is used to validate the fault prediction algorithm. The dataset includes a total of 21 sensor data types, encompassing temperature, pressure, flow rate, rotational speed, among others.

2) Data preprocessing.

Throughout the entire operational cycle, some sensor data exhibit little to no variation, indicating no correlation with engine degradation. In this study, data variance is used to represent the degree of change-smaller variance values indicate less variability41. The results of the variance analysis are shown in Fig. 9. It can be observed that the variance of sensor data s9, s14, s4, s3, and s17 demonstrates the most significant changes, hence these sensors are retained in the dataset. The data from all other sensors are discarded to reduce dimensionality and improve computational efficiency.

Fig. 9Variance of sensor data.

Variance of sensor data.

Since the sequence length of each engine varies and to increase the number of samples, the sliding window method was similarly employed to segment the time series data. The window length was set to 32 with a stride of 2. A total of 8800 samples were obtained. Subsequently, 11 statistical features were extracted from each sample to serve as model inputs, followed by data normalization. The mean value was also computed, resulting in an 11-dimensional feature matrix for each type of signal. Finally, PCA was applied to reduce the dimensionality of these feature matrices. Figure 10 shows the contribution rate plot of the principal components during the PCA dimensionality reduction process applied to the feature matrices corresponding to sensor data s9, s14, s4, s3, and s17. The top five principal components for each type of signal, which collectively account for over 80% of the cumulative information contribution rate, were selected as the input features for the AM-CNN-BiGRU model.

After completing the statistical feature dimensionality reduction, the RUL labels of the data were packaged using the same method as applied to the robot dataset. The final dimension of each input sample obtained is (5, 30, 5). Finally, the resulting dataset was divided into training, validation, and test sets following the same ratio as used for the robot dataset.

Evaluation metrics

Four model evaluation metrics, namely, the coefficient of determination R2, the mean absolute error MAE, the mean absolute percentage error MAPE, and the root mean square error RMSE, are used to evaluate the prediction results of different algorithmic models.

R² is a statistic that measures the goodness of fit of a regression model, which indicates how well the regression model fits the observations and represents the percentage of the dependent variable in the model that can be explained by the independent variable. MAE measures the average difference between the model’s predicted value and the true value in absolute terms. The closer the MAE value is to 0, the better the prediction is and the higher the model’s prediction accuracy is. Results and discussion

On the industrial robot dataset, the AM-CNN-BiGRU model was trained using three optimizers. Figure 11 shows the change in the loss function value of the model during the training process.

From the comparison of the convergence curves of the three optimizers in the figure, it can be seen that it takes about 50 iterations to reach the convergence state using the Adam optimizer, and about 40 iterations to reach the convergence state using the SGDM optimizer. Moreover, the two optimizers have poorer smoothness in the training process. However, the training using the Lion optimizer has converged after 25 iterations, and the trend is smooth during the whole training period. And it is more stable after convergence, and the final convergence effect is better than Adam and SGDM optimizers.

The above results demonstrate that the performance improvement of the AM-CNN-BiGRU model trained with the Lion optimizer in this experiment can be attributed to multiple factors. Firstly, its update mechanism based on the sign function introduces an intrinsic regularization effect, significantly enhancing the model’s generalization capability on noisy data, thereby improving training stability and overall performance while avoiding overfitting. Secondly, its algorithm eliminates the need to compute the second moment, resulting in smaller memory footprint and higher operational efficiency, which accelerates model convergence. Leveraging these advantages, the Lion optimizer provides novel insights and a powerful tool for addressing challenges in industrial predictive maintenance, such as poor data quality, the need for rapid model iteration, and edge deployment constraints. In subsequent experiments, the Lion optimizer was utilized for training all deep learning models.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a predictive maintenance method for industrial robots using an AM-CNN-BiGRU model with spatiotemporal feature fusion and multi-source data fusion. The model uses vibration, current, and torque data from each joint of the robot as input, avoiding the limitations of single-source data. First, an attention mechanism is proposed to assign different weights to multi-source data, determining the importance of different data sources. The weighted multi-source data sequences are then input into the CNN as different channels. The CNN extracts complex high-dimensional spatial features from the input sequences. Considering the variable periodicity of time series, the BiGRU is further employed to learn inherent temporal dependencies and extract high-dimensional temporal features. Additionally, the Lion optimizer is innovatively introduced into the training process of the predictive maintenance model proposed in this paper.

To validate the effectiveness and generalization capability of the proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU model, two sets of experiments were conducted. One involved building an industrial robot experimental platform for data acquisition to construct a dataset and perform experiments, while the other utilized the C-MAPSS dataset for validation. First, on the robot dataset, the AM-CNN-BiGRU model was trained using the Lion optimizer, Adam optimizer, and SGDM optimizer, respectively. The results confirmed the effectiveness of the Lion optimizer for training deep learning models on industrial datasets. Subsequently, ablation studies were performed on both datasets to compare the performance differences between the proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU and its variants: the model with the Attention Mechanism (AM) removed (CNN-BiGRU), the model with the CNN module removed (AM-BiGRU), and the model with the BiGRU module removed (AM-CNN). These results validated the rationality of the model’s architectural design. Finally, comparative experiments were carried out on both datasets, pitting AM-CNN-BiGRU against several popular algorithms in the field of time series prediction. These included two shallow learning algorithms (SVR and RF), three deep learning algorithms (TCN, LSTM, and Transformer), and two state-of-the-art fault prediction models (CNN-MMHA and BiLSTM-Trans). The results demonstrated that the proposed AM-CNN-BiGRU model achieved superior prediction performance on both datasets.

This study reveals the significant potential of integrated models in the industrial AI field, providing a powerful tool for transforming industrial robot maintenance strategies from “scheduled prevention” to “precision prediction”. More importantly, this research offers a universal spatiotemporal feature fusion framework that can be transferred to other industrial equipment fault diagnosis scenarios, facilitating the deep evolution of industrial operations towards intelligence. However, due to time constraints, data were collected only under a single operational condition of the robot. Research on model performance across different working conditions (including studies on the differential contributions of multi-source data to fault prediction under varying conditions) and lightweight deployment of the model will be our future research directions.

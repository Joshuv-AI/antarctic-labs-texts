Categorical spectra as pointed ( ∞ , ℤ ) (\infty,\mathbb{Z}) -categories
David Kern

Abstract
Lessard’s ℤ \mathbb{Z} -categories are an analogue of ω \omega -categories possessing cells in all positive and negative dimensions. Categorical spectra, developed by Stefanich, are an analogue of spectra obtained by replacing the suspension of pointed ∞ \infty -groupoids by that of pointed ( ∞ , ω ) (\infty,\omega) -categories. We give an ∞ \infty -categorical definition of weak ℤ \mathbb{Z} -categories (alias ( ∞ , ℤ ) (\infty,\mathbb{Z}) -categories), and show categorical spectra to be equivalent to pointed ( ∞ , ℤ ) (\infty,\mathbb{Z}) -categories. In particular, we show that the stable cells of categorical spectra coincide with the natural cells of ( ∞ , ℤ ) (\infty,\mathbb{Z}) -categories, and recover Lessard’s description of spectra as pointed weak ℤ \mathbb{Z} -groupoids.

(\infty,\omega)
-category
\mathfrak{C}
is
univalent-complete, then so is its desuspension
\Xi^{\ast}\mathfrak{C}
.

Proof.

We need to check that
\Xi^{\ast}\mathfrak{C}
is local with respect to
the functors
\Xi_{!}^{n}\mathfrak{eq}\to\Xi_{!}^{n}\ast=\mathfrak{D}_{n}
, for
all
n\geq 0
, that is that the induced functor

\hom(\mathfrak{D}_{n},\Xi^{\ast}\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi_{!}^{n}\mathfrak{eq},\Xi^{\ast}\mathfrak{C})
is an equivalence. By
adjunction, this map is equivalent to

\hom(\Xi_{!}\mathfrak{D}_{n},\mathfrak{C})=\hom(\mathfrak{D}_{n+1},\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi_{!}^{n+1}\mathfrak{eq},\mathfrak{C})

(22)

which is invertible because
\mathfrak{C}
is univalent-complete.
∎

Warning 2.3.6
.

The converse fails to be true, because univalence-completeness of

\Xi^{\ast}\mathfrak{C}
only implies that
\mathfrak{C}
is local with
respect to the functors
\Xi_{!}^{n}\mathfrak{eq}\to\mathfrak{D}_{n}
for

n\geq 1
, but does not recover the case
n=0
.

Thus the restriction of
\Xi^{\ast}
to univalent-complete Segal

{\Theta}^{\mathrm{op}}
-objects defines a functor

\Xi^{\ast}\colon\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,\omega)}\textnormal{-}Cat}\to\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,\omega)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
.

Lemma 2.3.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{I}
be an
(\infty,1)
-category, let

\mathcal{D\!}\,,\mathcal{D\!}\,^{\prime}\colon\mathfrak{I}\to\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,1)}\textnormal{-}Cat}
be two

\mathfrak{I}
-indexed diagrams of
(\infty,1)
-categories, and let

\alpha\colon\mathcal{D\!}\,\Rightarrow\mathcal{D\!}\,^{\prime}
be a
transformation of diagrams which is component-wise fully
faithful. Then the induced functor

\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\eta\colon\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{D\!}\,\to\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{D\!}\,^{\prime}

is also fully faithful.

Proof.

An
(\infty,1)
-functor is fully faithful if and only if it is
right-orthogonal to the class of functors that are essentailly
surjective on objects (
cf.

[
Lou24
, Proposition
2.2.1.75]
). Let us thus consider a
lifting problem as below-left

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{D\!}\,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\mathcal{D\!}\,^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{q\!}\,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{I}}\eta}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{c\!o\!n\!s\!t\!}\,\mathfrak{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D\!}\,}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{c\!o\!n\!s\!t\!}\,\mathfrak{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D\!}\,^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{c\!o\!n\!s\!t\!}\,(\mathcal{q\!}\,)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}

(23)

where
\mathcal{q\!}\,
is essentially surjective. Since the limit functor
is right-adjoint to the constant diagram functor
\mathcal{c\!o\!n\!s\!t\!}\,
,
this square corresponds to a lifting problem of diagrams as
above-right.

But
\mathcal{c\!o\!n\!s\!t\!}\,(\mathcal{q\!}\,)
is essentially surjective while

is fully faithful, so this square admits an essentially unique
dashed lift, which can be adjointed to a(n essentially unique)
solution to the original lifting problem.
∎

Definition 2.3.8
.

The
\infty
-category of

-categories

is the essential image of the functor

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a flagged

(\mathfrak{C}_{0},\mathfrak{C}_{1},\dots)
of flagged

for all

\mathfrak{C}

Definition 2.3.9
.

A flagged
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category is
stably
univalent-complete

\Xi^{\infty+n}_{!}\mathfrak{eq}\to\Xi^{\infty+n}\ast
for all

.

Remark 2.3.10
.

In contrast to

, a flagged

is an

-category if and only if its desuspension

Proposition 2.3.11
.

A flagged
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category
\mathfrak{C}
is an

(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category if and only if it is stably
univalent-complete.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathfrak{C}
is stably univalent-complete; in
particular for any
n,k\geq 0
the map

\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n-k}_{!}\ast,\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n-k}_{!}\mathfrak{eq},\mathfrak{C})
is an equivalence. This
map is equivalent to

\hom(\Xi^{n}\ast,H^{\infty-k}_{!}\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi^{n}\ast,H^{\infty-k}_{!}\mathfrak{C})
. Since

H^{\infty-k}_{!}\mathfrak{C}
is
\mathfrak{C}_{k}
, this shows that
\mathfrak{C}

is an
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category.

Now suppose that
\mathfrak{C}
is an
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category, and
let
n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{Z}}
. If
n\geq 0
, reversing the above
reasoning shows that
\mathfrak{C}
is local for the map

\Xi^{\infty+n}_{!}\mathfrak{eq}\to\Xi^{\infty+n}\ast
. If
n<0
, pick

\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n}\ast,\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n}\mathfrak{eq},\mathfrak{C})
is equivalent to

\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n+k}\ast,\Xi^{k}\mathfrak{C})\to\hom(\Xi^{\infty+n+k}\mathfrak{eq},\Xi^{k}\mathfrak{C})
as
\Xi^{k}
is in

\Xi^{k}\mathfrak{C}
is an
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category and

.
∎

Lemma 3.1.5

Let

\hom(\mathfrak{D}_{k+i},\mathfrak{C_{i}})\simeq\hom(\Xi^{i}\mathfrak{D}_{k},H^{\infty-i}\mathfrak{C})\simeq\hom(\Xi^{\infty-i+i}\mathfrak{D}_{k},\mathfrak{C})\text{,}

(35)

so all terms in the colimit are equivalent to

\hom(\Xi^{\infty}\mathfrak{D}_{k},\mathfrak{C})
. Because we are taking a
colimit in the
(\infty,1)
-category of
\infty
-groupoids, the
colimit of a constant diagram is the copower with the
groupoidification of the indexing shape category, which in our case
is
\mathbb{N}
so contractible.
∎

In particular, positive-dimensional cells of an

(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category are simply cells in its infinite
desuspension, whereas for categorical spectra it fails to be true that
positive-dimensional cells are cells in the infinite delooping.

Theorem 3.3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a categorical spectrum, and let

\ast\to\kappa\mathfrak{X}
be the corresponding pointed

(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
there is an
equivalence

\cells_{k}\mathfrak{X}\simeq\hom(\Xi^{\infty}\mathfrak{D}_{k},\kappa\mathfrak{X})
.

Proof.

By
lemma

3.3.2
, we have

\hom(\Xi^{\infty}\mathfrak{D}_{k},\kappa\mathfrak{X})\simeq\colim_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\bigl(\hom(\mathfrak{D}_{k+i},(\kappa\mathfrak{X})_{i})\bigl)
, and as the
equivalence
\kappa\colon\mathfrak{CatSp}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,\mathbb{Z})}\textnormal{-}Cat}_{\ast}

is compatible with the infinite deloopings this is equivalent to

\cells_{k}\mathfrak{X}
.
∎

Construction 3.3.4
.

The projection
\mathfrak{D}_{1}\to\mathfrak{D}_{0}
induces a degeneracy map

\cells_{k-1}\mathfrak{X}\to\cells_{k}\mathfrak{X}
for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}

and any
\mathfrak{X}\in\mathfrak{CatSp}
. A
k
-cell in a categorical spectrum

\mathfrak{X}
is said to be
invertible
if it lies in the image
of this degeneracy. More concretely,
[
Ste21
, Proposition
13.2.19]
shows that a
k
-cell

\varphi
of
\mathfrak{X}
is invertible if and only if some (and thus,
any) representative

\overline{\varphi}\in\hom(\mathfrak{D}_{k+i},\mathfrak{X}_{i})
defines an
invertible
(k+i)
-cell of the
(\infty,\omega)
-category

\mathfrak{X}_{i}
.

By
[
Ste21
, Remark 13.3.2]
,
categorical spectra all of whose cells are invertible coincide with
spectra.

Corollary 3.3.5

(
[
Les19
, Corollary
14.3.7]
)
.

The equivalence
\mathfrak{CatSp}\simeq\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,\mathbb{Z})}\textnormal{-}Cat}_{\ast}
restricts to an
equivalence between the
\infty
-categories of pointed groupoidal

(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-categories and of spectra.

Proof.

By univalence, a cell of an
(\infty,\mathbb{Z})
-category is
invertible if and only if it lies in the image of a degeneracy
map. Thus the notion of invertibility is preserved by the
equivalence
\mathfrak{CatSp}\simeq\mathfrak{\mathnormal{(\infty,\mathbb{Z})}\textnormal{-}Cat}_{\ast}
, so that restricting it
to groupoidal objects produces the sought-after equivalence.
∎

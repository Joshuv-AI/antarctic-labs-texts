MBUCF: a cross-fusion method for multimodal breast ultrasound based on multidimensional features
Authors: Ying Li, Renhe Liu, Xi Wei
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundBreast cancer (BC), characterized by high incidence and mortality rates, poses a severe threat to the life and health of women worldwide. Early, accurate identification is crucial for improving patient prognosis. Existing single-modal ultrasound techniques are insufficient to meet clinical needs. This study aims to establish a high-performance, lightweight multimodal ultrasound fusion model, providing a feasible method for distinguishing benign from malignant breast tumors.MethodsConventional ultrasound and elastography images of 227 patients with breast tumors were collected from Tianjin Cancer Institute and Hospital. After image registration, region of interest (ROI) annotation, and feature extraction, 581-dimensional features were extracted. A feature selection method of variance rough screening—information rough screening—sparse regression was adopted to select 10% of core features to achieve model lightweighting. Two models, light gradient boosting machine (LightGBM) and extreme gradient boosting (XGBoost), were selected. Scale_pos_weight and synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) were used to handle class imbalance, and relevant parameters were set empirically. In this study, metrics such as area under the curve (AUC), balanced accuracy (balanced Acc), and macro F1-score (F1-macro) were used to evaluate the performance of the model.ResultsThe proposed feature_cross fusion method in this study achieved the optimal performance. Under the full‑feature setting, the optimal model yielded an AUC of 0.8655 [95% confidence interval (CI): 0.7518–0.9539], a balanced Acc of 0.7652, and an F1‑macro of 0.7598. When only 10% of the features were retained, the model still maintained excellent performance with an AUC of 0.8258 (95% CI: 0.7083–0.9365), a balanced Acc of 0.7614, and an F1‑macro of 0.7608, and effectively achieved model lightweighting.ConclusionsThe multimodal ultrasound model based on feature cross-fusion can effectively improve the accuracy of distinguishing benign from malignant breast tumors and meet the lightweight requirement, providing a feasible tool for the early non-invasive diagnosis of BC.

Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is the second most prevalent cancer among women and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in females (1), posing a severe threat to women’s life and health. Despite its high incidence, the average 5-year survival rate of BC patients can reach 80.9% with systematic treatment (2), and the survival rate for patients diagnosed at stage I can reach as high as 99% (3). Early detection and clear diagnosis are crucial for improving patient prognosis. Pathological biopsy is the gold standard for BC diagnosis, but overuse imposes a burden. Thus, developing a high-accuracy, non-invasive technique for the early differentiation of benign and malignant tumors is an urgent clinical need.

Ultrasonography (US) is a routine method for differentiating benign and malignant breast tumors. It exhibits high sensitivity and specificity in BC detection, making it widely used in breast screening. Nevertheless, US has a relatively high false positive rate (FPR) (4), which may lead to over-biopsy due to misdiagnosing benign lesions as malignant (5). However, US lacks sensitivity to microcalcifications, hindering early detection. Although mammography compensates for this with high calcification sensitivity, its use is limited by patient discomfort, radiation risks, and low sensitivity in dense breasts (6).

Elastography, with the advantages of being painless, non-radiative, and sensitive to calcifications, overcomes the limitations of ultrasound and mammography, and is highly sensitive to malignant tumors (7). Tumor cells lead to high tissue density due to rapid proliferation, and elastography can visually reflect tissue stiffness through color mapping (8). Existing studies have confirmed that multimodal ultrasound can improve the diagnostic performance for breast tumors. Li et al. (9) found that conventional ultrasound combined with shear wave elastography (SWE) achieves a sensitivity of 96.1% in differentiating benign from malignant Breast Imaging-Reporting and Data System (BI-RADS) category 4 breast lesions. However, in clinical practice, multimodal ultrasound relies on manual BI-RADS assessment, which is subjective and has low efficiency in data integration. Thus, some researchers have introduced artificial intelligence (AI) technology (10) aiming to explore broader applications of multimodal ultrasound.

AI models based on multimodal fused ultrasound can provide accessible pathways for the early differentiation of breast tumors and treatment optimization (4,11). Studies have demonstrated their roles in predicting sentinel lymph node metastasis (12), neoadjuvant chemotherapy response (13), and lesion classification (14). Kubasik et al. (15) evaluated the potential of SWE and applanation tonometry in assessing and predicting the risk of capsular contracture (CC) after breast augmentation. Compared with tonometry, sonoelastography enables precise assessment of tissue stiffness across all layers and exhibits excellent capability in detecting subtle changes in breast tissue stiffness. This finding indicates that sonoelastography technology demonstrates superior performance in the evaluation of breast lesions. La et al. (16) used US and SWE based on machine learning models to differentiate solid breast lesions, with the model achieving an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.76, confirming its potential in breast tumor differentiation. However, its performance is still inferior to the diagnostic accuracy of senior physicians directly applying elastography, indicating that there is still room for improvement in the models.

Currently, AI research on multimodal ultrasound for BC faces two major limitations: first, the feature fusion methods are simplistic, mostly adopting simple feature concatenation or fixed-weight result fusion, which lacks deep feature interaction and makes it difficult to explore the complementary information between modalities. Second, high-dimensional features lead to time-consuming model training and high computational costs, hindering deployment on clinical devices. Based on this, this study proposes a novel fusion method that achieves lightweighting of the multimodal fusion model and improves its accuracy, stability, and clinical applicability through innovative feature interaction and feature selection approaches.

In summary, the main contributions of this study are as follows: (I) proposing a multidimensional feature cross-fusion strategy to explore the interactive value of features from conventional US and elastography, overcoming the limitations of traditional fusion methods; and (II) constructing a lightweight model that retains only 10% of the features while ensuring diagnostic performance, enabling rapid deployment of the model on ordinary devices and reducing the threshold for clinical application. We present this article in accordance with the STARD-AI reporting checklist (available at https://qims.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/qims-2026-1-0065/rc).

Data preprocessing

Image registration

This study adopted rigid and non-rigid registration methods to accurately quantify the displacement of breast tissue before and after pressure application by the ultrasound probe, thereby providing reliable deformation data for subsequent elastography. This method takes the ultrasound image before pressure application I(x, y) and the image after pressure application I(x’, y’) as input objects, and sequentially performs global and local transformation estimation.

First, a rigid registration model was used to correct the overall motion of the images. This process is achieved through an affine transformation, of which the parameters include rotation components (a1, a2), scaling components (b1, b2), and translation components (c1, c2). These parameters map the pixel coordinates (x, y) in the pre-pressure image to the initial corresponding positions in the post-pressure image, so as to eliminate global displacement caused by probe movement or patient respiration.

Then, a free-form deformation (FFD) model based on B-splines was applied for non-rigid registration to capture the local nonlinear elastic deformation of the tissue. In this model, the image domain is divided into a uniformly distributed control grid, and the displacement of each control point φi is interpolated using a cubic B-spline basis function, thereby constructing a smooth local transformation field Tlocal.

To evaluate the similarity between images, this study used the sum of absolute differences (SADs) as the registration criterion and introduced a smoothness constraint term based on the second derivative to ensure the physical rationality of the deformation field. Finally, the limited-memory Broyden-Fletcher-Goldfarb-Shanno (L-BFGS) optimization algorithm was used to iteratively solve the combined loss function, while optimizing the rigid and non-rigid transformation parameters.

After registration, the longitudinal displacement distribution map was obtained by calculating the difference in corresponding pixel positions before and after pressure application, laying a foundation for achieving high-precision elastography.

Region of interest (ROI) annotation

After registration, attending physicians with more than 5 years of clinical experience performed ROI annotation on the patients’ images, in a post-hoc fashion. The annotation scope fully covered the lesion boundaries, including the blurred areas at the lesion edges, to avoid missing the heterogeneous areas of the lesions. Meanwhile, normal breast tissues, fat, blood vessels, and other structures were excluded. Mask files of the tumors were obtained through manual annotation, which were used for subsequent feature extraction.

In this study, a consistent extraction process and parameter settings were applied to both elastographic ultrasound images and conventional ultrasound images. Feature extraction was performed using Pyradiomics (https://pyradiomics.readthedocs.io/en/latest/), an internationally commonly used radiomics toolkit, resulting in 581-dimensional features. This study innovatively proposes a novel multidimensional feature cross-fusion (feature_cross) approach. By deeply exploring the intrinsic correlations between conventional ultrasound (left-branch features Xleft) and elastography (right-branch features Xright), it breaks through the limitation of traditional fusion methods that only superimpose information at a shallow level. Based on the pathophysiological characteristics of BC and clinical diagnostic logic, this method designs 10 targeted cross-operation strategies, converting the structural features of conventional ultrasound and stiffness features of elastography into discriminative correlation features. Meanwhile, scientific operation rules are adopted to eliminate dimensional interference and amplify the specific signals of malignant samples, providing high-value inputs for the model’s accurate diagnosis. Lightweight design

To reduce dimensionality, remove noise, and achieve model lightweighting, this study adopted a combined screening strategy of “coarse screening + fine screening” to screen the 581-dimensional features obtained from feature extraction, ultimately retaining 10% of the core features. The specific process is as follows:

❖ First, coarse screening by variance: based on the variance selection method, the variance of all numerical features was calculated. The Variance Threshold tool was used to eliminate low-information features with near-0 variance, retaining only the top 50% of features.

❖ First, coarse screening by variance: based on the variance selection method, the variance of all numerical features was calculated. The Variance Threshold tool was used to eliminate low-information features with near-0 variance, retaining only the top 50% of features.

❖ Second, coarse screening by information: based on the correlation between features and labels, mutual information was used to quantify the statistical correlation between features and the benign/malignant lesion labels. The features retained in the first stage were sorted in descending order of mutual information values, with the top 50% retained to ensure that features weakly correlated with the labels were eliminated.

The features retained in the first stage were sorted in descending order of mutual information values, with the top 50% retained to ensure that features weakly correlated with the labels were eliminated.

❖ Third, sparse regression: ElasticNet, which integrates L1 and L2 regularization as an embedded screening tool, was adopted. Through regularization constraints, the coefficients of redundant features were automatically shrunk to 0, ultimately retaining 40% of features with non-0 coefficients.

Sample augmentation and loss optimization

In this study, the overall ratio of benign to malignant samples is 1.1:1.0, achieving class balance. However, clinically, BC exhibits significant heterogeneity in imaging features, and missed diagnosis can lead to severe consequences. Additionally, the ultrasound image data in this study contains multiple noise interferences and a small sample size. Even with a balanced sample ratio, there may still be an insufficient number of effective malignant samples. Therefore, this study adopted a weighting method and an oversampling method to improve the model’s ability to identify malignant samples and reduce the risk of missed diagnosis of malignant cases.

Evaluation metrics

Six metrics were used to evaluate the model performance: AUC, balanced accuracy (balanced Acc), macro F1-score (F1-macro), Precision-macro, Recall-macro, and Matthews correlation coefficient (MCC).

For the confidence interval (CI) estimation of the AUC indicator, the method of 1,000-time Bootstrap resampling was adopted for calculation; meanwhile, the Delong test or Bootstrap method was used to verify and supplement the AUC CI. The two methods corroborated each other, which improved the accuracy and robustness of the AUC CI estimation and effectively reflected the statistical fluctuation range of the AUC indicator.

For the comparative analysis of diagnostic performance among different fusion models, a combination of the paired Bootstrap method and the DeLong test was applied to conduct the inter-model comparison of the overall diagnostic performance.

In the multiple comparisons among multiple groups of models, to avoid false positive (FP) results caused by multiple hypothesis tests, the Benjamini-Hochberg method in the false discovery rate (FDR) correction approach was used to adjust the obtained P values.

Results

Research cohort

After screening, 227 patients were included in this study, including 119 benign samples (52.42%) and 108 malignant samples (47.58%), with an imbalance ratio of 1.1:1.0. Each patient had both conventional B-mode ultrasound images and elastographic ultrasound images.

In this study, stratified random sampling was used to divide the data of 227 patients into a training set and a test set at a ratio of 4:1, with 181 data in the training set and 46 data in the test set. The training set included 95 benign samples and 86 malignant samples; the test set included 24 benign samples and 22 malignant samples, with a ratio of approximately 1.1:1.0.

We used 10-fold cross-validation to ensure the consistent ratio of benign to malignant samples in each fold, so as to reduce the impact of sample distribution bias on the model.

Discussion

Effectiveness of the feature_cross fusion method

As can be seen from Table 2, in the LightGBM (scale_pos_weight) full-feature experiment, the AUC value of the feature_cross fusion method reached 0.8542 (95% CI: 0.7301–0.9500), ranking first alongside the stacking fusion method. Additionally, it achieved the optimal level together with the feature_concat fusion method in key metrics such as balanced Acc (0.7443), F1-macro (0.7371), Precision-macro (0.7560), and MCC (0.5001). These results were significantly higher than those of result-level fusion methods such as weighted fusion (AUC =0.8314) and gated fusion (AUC =0.8277), and far exceed the performance of conventional ultrasound alone (AUC =0.7538). This indicates that the correlation features constructed through 10 targeted cross-operations can effectively mine deep interactive information between bimodal data, and their information value is superior to simple feature concatenation or result weighting.

As can be seen from Table 3, in the LightGBM (SMOTE) full-feature experiment, the AUC value of the feature_cross fusion method was 0.8598 (95% CI: 0.7405–0.9500). Although slightly lower than the 0.8693 of feature_concat, it achieved the same optimal results as feature_concat in metrics including balanced Acc (0.7652), F1-macro (0.7598), Precision-macro (0.7729), and MCC (0.5380). Compared with elastography alone (AUC =0.8428) and conventional ultrasound alone (AUC =0.7595), it still maintained significant advantages.

As can be seen from Table 4, in the XGBoost (scale_pos_weight) full-feature experiment, the AUC value of the feature_cross fusion method was 0.8655 (95% CI: 0.7518–0.9539), second only to the 0.8693 of feature_concat. Its balanced Acc (0.7443), F1-macro (0.7371), Precision-macro (0.7560), and MCC (0.5001) achieved the optimal level together with the feature_asym fusion method, which were significantly higher than those of result-level fusion methods such as weighted fusion (AUC =0.8258) and attention fusion (AUC =0.8201).

As can be seen from Table 5, in the XGBoost (SMOTE) full-feature experiment, the AUC value of the feature_cross fusion method was 0.8580 (95% CI: 0.7269–0.9565). Although it did not reach the optimal level, it performed outstandingly in metrics such as balanced Acc (0.7633), F1-macro (0.7608), Precision-macro (0.7648), and MCC (0.5280), which were comparable to the deep_fusion method and significantly superior to single-modal models.

As shown in Figure 7, the model demonstrated clinically superior characteristics in distinguishing between benign and malignant breast tumors, with low missed diagnosis rates and manageable misdiagnosis rates. The model exhibited outstanding ability in identifying malignant samples while maintaining high precision in discriminating benign samples, reflecting the effectiveness and stability of the proposed fusion method.

Figure 8 shows that, from an overall trend perspective, the curves of all multi-modal fusion methods are significantly higher than the random baseline, indicating that combining complementary information from conventional ultrasound and elastography can effectively enhance diagnostic performance and avoid missed diagnoses or misdiagnoses due to insufficient single-modality information.

As can be seen from Figure 9, the AUC curves (left panel) show that all models exhibited a rapid increase in AUC during the early training phase (first 50 epochs), indicating that the models can quickly learn the core features of dual-modality breast tumor images. Notably, the feature_cross fusion model proposed in this study consistently maintained a dual lead in AUC on both the training and validation sets, with smooth curves and no significant fluctuations.

Examination of the convergence process of the loss curves (right panel) showed that both the training loss and validation loss of the feature_cross fusion model continuously declined and quickly stabilized, converging around 25 epochs. Moreover, the validation loss did not exhibit a significant rebound, indicating that the model avoids overfitting and possesses strong generalization ability.

In summary, the innovative feature_cross fusion method proposed by us can not only effectively mine the interactive information between features and improve the discrimination accuracy of the model, but also has stable performance, which can effectively reduce classification bias and the risk of misjudgment of benign and malignant samples. It has good performance in different model settings and is more in line with the clinical diagnosis demand for model stability.

Performance of the lightweight model

As can be seen from Table 6, in the LightGBM (scale_pos_weight) 10% feature experiment, the AUC value of the feature_cross fusion method was 0.8258 (95% CI: 0.7083–0.9365). Although slightly lower than that in the full-feature experiment, it was still significantly higher than that in most other fusion methods. Its balanced Acc (0.7614), F1-macro (0.7608), Precision-macro (0.7609), and MCC (0.5222) all ranked the highest, which were far superior to those of conventional ultrasound alone (AUC =0.6951, MCC =0.1326).

As can be seen from Table 7, in the LightGBM (SMOTE) 10% feature experiment, the feature_cross fusion method maintained optimal performance across key metrics: AUC =0.8371 (95% CI: 0.6511–0.9088), balanced Acc =0.7633, F1-macro =0.7608, Precision-macro =0.7648, and MCC =0.5280. These results were comparable to the core metrics of elastography alone and significantly outperformed other fusion methods such as feature_concat (AUC =0.8201) and stacking (AUC =0.7462).

As can be seen from Table 8, in the XGBoost (scale_pos_weight) 10% feature experiment, the feature_cross fusion method demonstrated more prominent performance advantages: its balanced Acc (0.8068), F1-macro (0.8043), Precision-macro (0.8086), and MCC (0.6154) were the highest among all fusion methods. Although its AUC value (0.8144, 95% CI: 0.6209–0.9039) was slightly lower than that of feature_concat (0.8239), its overall performance was significantly better, fully reflecting the efficiency and robustness of the model after core feature selection.

As can be seen from Table 9, in the XGBoost (SMOTE) 10% feature experiment (Table 10), the feature_cross fusion method achieved an AUC of 0.8220 (95% CI: 0.6806–0.9189), with balanced Acc (0.7633), F1-macro (0.7608), Precision-macro (0.7648), and MCC (0.5280) all remaining among the top performers. The performance gap between the 10% feature model and the full-feature model was minimal, verifying that the selection strategy can effectively retain core discriminative information while removing redundant and noisy features.

As shown in Figure 10, even when retaining only 10% of the core features, the confusion matrix of the feature_cross fusion model still maintains excellent classification performance: high proportions of TPs and TNs, with extremely low numbers of FNs and FPs. This indicates that the cross-feature set constructed by feature_cross possesses strong discriminative power, enabling accurate classification with only a small set of core features.

Figure 11 demonstrates that after lightweight configuration, the feature_cross fusion model maintained an AUC above 0.81, significantly outperforming other fusion methods and single-modality models. The performance degradation of all core metrics for the feature_cross fusion model after lightweighting was controlled within 5%, with AUC remaining above 0.81, still significantly higher than other fusion methods and single-modality models. In contrast, traditional methods such as weighted fusion and attention fusion show significant performance declines after lightweighting, indicating their heavy reliance on high-dimensional feature stacking and lack of effective refinement of core discriminative information. The performance deterioration of single-modality models is even more severe, further highlighting the necessity of multi-modal fusion.

Figure 12 shows that even when only 10% of features were retained, the model maintained good learning capability without overfitting, demonstrating strong generalization ability.

Table 10 presents a statistical evaluation of AUC, F1-macro, and balanced Acc for different fusion methods and single-modality models using the Bootstrap resampling method. It can be observed that across the three-core metrics—AUC, F1-macro, and balanced Acc—the mean values of feature_cross (feature level) (0.8258, 0.7608, and 0.7614, respectively) were the highest among all methods. Compared with single-modality methods (conventional_imaging, elastic_imaging), feature_cross showed clear performance advantages, with CIs almost entirely covering or exceeding those of single-modality approaches, demonstrating the value of multi-modal fusion. When compared with other multi-modal fusion methods (such as feature_concat, deep_fusion, stacking, etc.), feature_cross achieved leading positions in both mean values and CIs, exhibiting superior feature mining and discriminative capability.

In summary, a comparison of model performance between the full-feature and 10% feature settings showed that the lightweight model based on feature_cross fusion controlled the degradation of all core metrics within 5%, while reducing the feature dimension to only 10% of the original. This greatly lowers the model’s computational cost and storage requirements, laying a solid foundation for the rapid deployment of the model on ordinary clinical devices.

Comparison with existing SOTA models

As shown in Table 11, the AUC scores of SOTA models on similar tasks were 0.76 and 0.84, respectively. Our model achieved an AUC of no less than 0.81 across all configurations, with the model using SMOTE and all features reaching an AUC of 0.8580, which is significantly higher than 0.84. This demonstrates that our model performs excellently in extracting information from imaging modalities.

Limitations

Sample limitations

All samples in this study were collected from Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital, leading to potential single-center bias. The fixed ethnicity and equipment model may result in poor adaptability of the model when applied to data from other centers. Particularly for ultrasound data from primary hospitals with lower resolution, increased noise is likely to degrade model performance. Additionally, the sample size of this study is relatively small. Although 10-fold cross-validation was used to reduce bias, BC encompasses numerous subtypes, and the samples failed to cover all subtypes, especially the characteristics of rare subtypes may have been overlooked, leading to insufficient discriminative ability of the model for such subtypes. Multi-center studies are urgently required, with an expanded sample size, and including samples from different regions, equipment models, and subtypes to improve the model’s generalization ability.

Bias caused by dependence on manual annotation

The ROI (annotations in this study were performed by attending physicians with more than 5 years of experience, relying on manual labeling. Differences in physicians’ subjective judgments on lesion boundaries can lead to fluctuations in the extracted features. Moreover, manual annotation is time-consuming and labor-intensive, which limits the widespread application of the model and hinders large-scale data collection. Future work should prioritize the development of automatic ROI annotation algorithms to reduce manual dependence and improve annotation efficiency and accuracy.

Lack of clinical data and insufficient feature dimensions

This study only utilized elastography and conventional ultrasound data, without incorporating patients’ clinical information. Factors such as age, family history (19), hormone levels (20), and medication history (21) are closely related to the occurrence of breast tumors and partially determine the benignancy or malignancy of tumors, serving as important supplements to ultrasound data. In the future, a multi-dimensional feature set integrating clinical and imaging data can be constructed to fuse clinical features with imaging features, further optimizing model performance.

Insufficient clinical interpretability

Although this study conducted feature importance analysis and selected core features for feature-level fusion, it did not clarify the association between these features and tumor pathological mechanisms. This black-box characteristic may reduce clinicians’ trust in the model, thereby limiting its clinical application. Future research can perform correlation analysis between pathological biopsy results and tumor radiomic features to further clarify the relationship between ultrasound features and tumor morphology, thereby enhancing the model’s interpretability.

Conclusions

Focusing on the clinical pain points in the early discrimination of benign and malignant breast tumors, and addressing the limitations of single-modal ultrasound and traditional multi-modal fusion models, this study constructed and validated a multi-modal ultrasound fusion model. A total of 227 breast tumor patients from Tianjin Medical University Cancer Institute and Hospital were enrolled, with conventional B-mode ultrasound and elastography data collected. Rigid and non-rigid registration methods were used to align conventional ultrasound and elastography images. ROI annotations were performed by attending physicians with more than 5 years of experience to outline tumor boundaries and distinguish them from surrounding tissues. Radiomic features were extracted using the Pyradiomics toolkit. An innovative feature fusion method (feature_cross) was proposed, and a hybrid feature selection strategy (variance-based rough screening-information-based rough screening-sparse regression) was adopted to select 10% of core features for model lightweighting. Feature_cross effectively fuses complementary features of conventional US and elastography. The model maintains excellent performance after selecting 10% of core features and can be deployed on ordinary clinical devices, reducing the threshold for clinical application. This study provides an effective approach for the early discrimination of benign and malignant breast tumors.

On exactness of Semidefinite Programming relaxation for the Maximum Cut Problem
Avinash Bhardwaj, Hritiz Gogoi, Vishnu Narayanan, Abhishek Pathapati

Abstract
. Semidefinite programming provides a relaxation of the maximum cut problem on a graph. We investigate and present a few classes of graphs for which the semidefinite relaxation of the maximum cut problem is exact. For each of these classes, we determine the optimal objective value by explicitly constructing a maximum cut. Furthermore, we prove the uniqueness of the exact solution for two of these graph classes.



1.
Introduction

The Maximum-Cut (Max-Cut) problem asks the following : Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with non-negative weights assigned to its edges, find an optimal partition of the vertex set
V
into two disjoint subsets such that the total weight of the edges crossing the partition (i.e., edges with endpoints in different subsets) is maximized. Over the years, this problem has generated immense interest. It is one of the first demonstrations of NP-hardness of computational problems
[
9
]
and the first optimization problem where semidefinite relaxation was used to propose an algorithm with an approximation guarantee
[
6
]
. Apart from its theoretical significance, the Max-Cut problem finds applications across various disciplines and industries. Prominent areas include statistical physics, VLSI design
[
1
]
and machine learning
[
16
]
.

Goemans and Williamson 1995
introduced a semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxation for the Max-Cut problem, which yields a polynomial-time approximation algorithm. Given an input graph, the relaxation produces a positive semidefinite matrix as the optimal solution to the primal problem. A feasible cut can then be obtained by applying a hyperplane rounding technique to this matrix. The authors demonstrated that the expected value of the resulting cut is at least a factor of
\alpha_{\text{GW}}=\frac{2}{\pi}\displaystyle\min_{0\leq\theta\leq\pi}\frac{\theta}{1-\cos\theta}\approx 0.87856
times the value of the maximum cut. It was later shown that this approximation ratio is optimal under the assumption of the Unique Games Conjecture and the condition
P\neq NP

[
10
]
. The SDP relaxation is exact if and only if there exists a rank-1 positive semidefinite matrix as an optimal primal solution(see
2.3
).
One may, however, find it challenging to determine exactness solely from the obtained solution matrix. This limitation arises because the set of optimal solutions may include matrices of higher rank, and commercial solvers often return such high-rank solutions. Compounding this challenge, it is also possible to obtain sub-optimal feasible solutions even in cases where the relaxation is exact. Notably,
Karloff 1996
identified a class of graphs for which the semidefinite programming relaxation yields an exact solution; yet, the approximation ratio attained in these cases corresponds to the worst-case. Apart from the theoretical interests, these observations motivate the study of graph classes that guarantee the existence of a rank-1 optimal solution, thereby ensuring exactness of the relaxation. In this manuscript, we investigate and present a few such graph classes. For each of these classes, we determine the optimal objective value by explicitly constructing a maximum cut. Additionally, for two of these classes, we prove that the exact solution obtained via the relaxation is unique.

1.1.
Related work

A significant research direction in Max-Cut literature has focused on deriving upper bounds on the optimal cut value. An influential early contribution in this direction was made by
Mohar and Poljak 1990
, who derived an upper bound on the optimal value using the eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian.
Delorme and Poljak 1993a
improved this upper bound by introducing a correcting vector.
Delorme and Poljak 1993a
,
Delorme and Poljak 1993b
,
Delorme and Poljak 1993c
subsequently analyzed the performance of this improved bound (henceforth referred to as the Delorme-Poljak eigenvalue bound) on various graphs and graph classes. These studies also identified several classes of graphs where the Delorme-Poljak eigenvalue bound is tight (summarized in the next subsection). They further proved that the Exact Weighted Graph Problem— whether the Delorme-Poljak eigenvalue bound coincides with the actual value of Max-Cut given a weighted graph—is
NP
-complete
[
4
]
. Building on these developments,
Poljak and Rendl 1995
proposed a semidefinite formulation for the eigenvalue approximation problem.
Goemans and Williamson 1995
developed the semidefinite relaxation of the quadratic integer formulation of Max-Cut (now known as GW-relaxation) and demonstrated its equivalence to the formulation obtained from Delorme-Poljak eigenvalue relaxation. They also showed that the approximation ratio of the algorithm to be at least
\alpha_{GW}
.
Karloff 1996
showed that the approximation ratio of GW-relaxation is exactly
\alpha_{GW}
.
Sojoudi and Lavaei 2014
investigated the exactness of semidefinite relaxation for general quadratically constrained quadratic programs (QCQPs), of which the Max-Cut problem is a special case. More recently,
Hong et al. 2021
characterized conditions under which weighted cycle graphs yield rank-1 optimal SDP Max-Cut solutions. They also derived bounds on the rank of the optimal solution for the vertex-sum of two graphs.
Mirka and Williamson 2024
investigated the uniqueness of the optimal rank-1 solution for the GW-relaxation.

The Max-Cut problem can be formulated as a quadratic integer programming problem:

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{2}\Sigma_{i<j}w_{ij}(1-y_{i}y_{j})}

\displaystyle{y_{i}\in\{-1,1\},\forall i\in V{}}

Consequently, the optimization problem reduces to determining an assignment of the values 1 and -1 to the vertices that maximizes the objective. NP

3.
Main Results

We investigate a few classes of graphs for which an exact MAXCUT SDP optimal solution exists. Recall that any (non-negatively weighted) bipartite graphs are exact. We claim that, given a complete bipartite graph
K(n,n)

Proposition 1
.

Let

The following statement is equivalent to Proposition 1:

Corollary 1.1

The graph join of any two graphs of identical order is exact.

Corollary 1.1 shows that it is sufficient for two graphs to have identical order for their graph join to be exact. We extend this observation by noting that the graph join of two graphs can also be exact even when the graphs differ in order, provided that each contains a balanced complete bipartite spanning subgraph.

4
. For example, 3-gonal 3-cone graph, (2,3)-fan graph and (3,3)-fan graph are exact.

Lastly, we investigate exactness of lexicographic product of graphs. The lexicographic product of two graphs
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
can be explained informally as the graph obtained by associating a copy of
G_{2}
with each vertex of
G_{1}
, and then, for every edge
(u,u^{\prime})\in E_{1}
, connecting all vertices in the copy of
G_{2}
associated with
u
to all vertices in the copy associated with
u^{\prime}
, thereby forming a complete bipartite connection between the corresponding vertex sets. We show that the exactness of
G_{1}
is a sufficient condition for the exactness of the lexicographic product
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
, invariant of the structure of
G_{2}
.

Theorem 5
.

G_{1}(V_{1},E_{1})\circ G_{2}(V_{2},E_{2})
is exact if
G_{1}
is exact.

The exactness (or even bipartiteness) of
G_{2}
, however, does not guarantee the exactness of
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
. Consider the triangle graph
K_{3}
as
G_{1}
and the path graph
P_{3}
as
G_{2}
as an example. While
G_{2}
is bipartite,
G_{1}\circ G_{2}
yields an optimal solution of

, indicating non-exactness. Theorem 5, combined with the properties of the optimal MAXCUT SDP value

, enables us to prove the following:

Corollary 5.1
.

Consider a uniformly weighted exact graph
G
on
n
vertices, where each edge has weight
w

on

5.
Concluding Remarks

In summary, we identified the following classes of graphs for which the SDP relaxation of the Max-Cut problem is exact under the mentioned condition:

(1)

Graphs with balanced complete bipartite spanning subgraph.

(2)

Graph join of two graphs
G_{A}
and
G_{B}
, each with balanced complete bipartite spanning subgraph.

(3)

The lexicographic product of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
where
G_{1}
is an exact graph.

Let
G(V,E)
be a graph such that there exists a partition
(V_{1},V_{2})
of
V
, where
V_{2}
is an independent set and every vertex of
V_{1}
is adjacent to every vertex of
V_{2}
. Then,
G
was shown to be exact with unique solution if:

(4)

The size of the independent set
V_{2}
is atleast the size of
V_{1}
.

(5)

Any vertex of
V_{1}
has degree atmost
2
and
|V_{2}|\geq 4
.

Furthermore, we presented special cases of these graph classes in Section
3
and provided an explicit construction of their maximum cut in Section
4
. The special cases included conditions for exactness and uniqueness in complete
k
-partite graphs, weighted complete graphs,
m
-gonal
n
-cone graphs and
m
,
n
-fan graphs. Prior to this work, the only graph families known to exhibit both exactness and uniqueness of the Max-Cut SDP solution were appropriately weighted cycle graphs
[
7
]
and bipartite graphs with non-negative edge weights
[
11
]
. In this study, we expanded this list by identifying graph classes (4) and (5). Using Example 1, we demonstrated that our findings lay the groundwork for identifying the exactness of more intricate graphs. Furthering this, a natural direction for future research is to develop an algorithm that efficiently decomposes a graph and determine its exactness. As most of our results concern unweighted graphs, it would be worthwhile to investigate further the conditions under which semidefinite relaxation
yield exact solutions for weighted instances. Advancing these lines of inquiry could enhance the applicability of the theoretical framework and provide deeper insights into the structural properties that underlie exactness.

Quantum State Designs via Magic Teleportation
Hugo Lóio, Guglielmo Lami, Lorenzo Leone, Max McGinley, Xhek Turkeshi, Jacopo De Nardis

Abstract
We investigate how non-stabilizer resources enable the emergence of quantum state designs within the projected ensemble. Starting from initial states with finite magic and applying resource-free Clifford circuits to scramble them, we analyze the ensemble generated by performing projective Pauli measurements on a subsystem of the final state. Using both analytical arguments and large-scale numerics, we show that the projected ensemble converges towards a state k k -design with an error that decays exponentially with the k k -th Stabilizer Renyi Entropy of the pre-measurement state, via a Magic-Induced Design Ansatz (MIDA) that we introduce. We identify a universal scaling form, valid across different classes of magic initial states, and corroborate it through numerical simulations and analytical calculations of the frame potential. For finite-depth Clifford unitaries, we show that the timescales at which state designs emerge are controlled by the transport of magic. We identify a “magic teleportation” mechanism whereby non-Clifford resources injected locally spread through Clifford scrambling and measurements across distances beyond the lightcone. Our results demonstrate how a small and controlled amount of magic suffices to generate highly random states, providing a systematic route toward generating quantum state designs in early fault-tolerant devices.

II
MIDA for deep Clifford circuits

Let us first consider the asymptotic limit of deep Clifford circuits, where
C
is a global random Clifford transformation acting on
L
qubits.
In this limit, we uncover a direct connection between the design order of the projected ensemble and the magic of the initial state: the distance
\Delta_{A}^{(k)}
decays exponentially with the stabilizer entropy
M_{k}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
.

To corroborate this statement, we begin by presenting two analytically tractable regimes.
First, we consider a system with no magic resources, fixing
\ket{\psi_{0}}
without loss of generality.
After application of a global random Clifford, the pre-measurement state
|\psi_{\infty}\rangle\equiv\mathcal{C}|\psi_{0}\rangle
becomes a random stabilizer state.
Owing to the properties of stabilizer measurements, all post-measurement states
\ket{\psi_{A}(z_{B})}
share the same stabilizer generators up to signs (see e.g. Appendix 9D in Ref.
Bejan et al. 2025
or
Authors 2025
).
Consequently, the overlap between any two states
\ket{\psi_{A}(z_{B})}
and
\ket{\psi_{A}(z_{B}\textquoteright)}
in
\mathcal{E}
satisfies
\left|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}\textquoteright)\rangle\right|\in\{0,1\}
. Substituting into Eq. (
1
) then immediately yields
F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}=F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}
for all
k
.
Let us note that the frame potential for
k=1
corresponds to the purity of the reduced density matrix in
A
, namely
F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}=\Tr\left[\rho_{A}^{2}\right]
, where
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{tr}_{B}[\mathcal{C}|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}]
.
Since this expression involves two copies of the Clifford unitary, the average over Cliffords coincides with the Haar average, yielding

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}}[F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}]=\frac{2^{L-L_{A}}+2^{L_{A}}}{2^{L}+1}\;,

(2)

where
L_{A}=|A|
and
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}}[\bullet]
denotes the Clifford average. Asymptotically, for any initial state,
\lim_{L\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}}\left[F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(1)}\right]=2^{-L_{A}}
.
Consequently, in the absence of magic resources, the Haar distance of the projected ensemble is

\mu\equiv\Delta^{(k)}_{A}=\frac{1}{k!}\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(2^{L_{A}}+i\right)-1\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,k>1\,,

(3)

while
\Delta^{(1)}_{A}=0
.
Thus, for stabilizer states the projected ensemble is restricted to a 1-design
Bejan et al. 2025
;
Authors 2025
.
It is interesting to contrast this result with the global state
|\psi_{\infty}\rangle=\mathcal{C}|\psi_{0}\rangle
, which instead forms a 3-design on the full system
Zhu et al. 2016a
. In essence, projective measurements deteriorate this property, lowering the design order to one.

We now analyze the case where the system is doped with the minimal amount of magic resources: a single
T
-state
|\psi_{0}\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes L-1}\otimes|T\rangle
, where
|T\rangle=(|0\rangle+e^{i\pi/4}|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}

In this setting, the evolved state can be written as a superposition of two stabilizer states that differ by the sign of one of their stabilizer generators.
After measuring subsystem
B

(4)

where

is a constant that depends on the subsystem size

and can be determined from the second frame potential
F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(2)}

.
This means that once

is known, the behavior for all

In the large-

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}}\left[F_{\mathcal{E}}^{(k)}\right]\underset{L_{A}\gg 1}{=}2^{-L_{A}}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2^{k}}\Big).

(5)

III
Shallow Clifford circuits and magic teleportation

Our previous results provide a comprehensive picture of how much magic is required for a projected ensemble to approximate Haar random states under global Clifford evolution. This regime requires a depth
t=O(N)
, which is realistic for fault-tolerant devices but beyond the reach of present-day NISQ hardware. To connect with the latter, we next study shallow Clifford circuits. Fixing the initial magic content, we analyze how the projected ensemble approaches the asymptotic behavior as a function of circuit depth. A key finding is the emergence of
magic teleportation
, a phenomenon directly analogous to the entanglement teleportation explored in
Bao et al. 2024b
;
McGinley et al. 2025
;
Hoke et al. 2023
: the combination of unitary time evolution and measurements violates the typical light-cone locality and allows for transferring information via longer time-scales compared to simple unitary evolution.

We study two distinct spatial configurations for the initial magic resource. In the first case,

|\psi_{0}^{\mathrm{(left)}}\rangle=|\theta\rangle^{\otimes N_{P}}\otimes|0\rangle^{\otimes L-N_{P}}
,
the magic states are placed within or near subsystem
A
. In the second case,

|\psi_{0}^{\mathrm{(right)}}\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes L-N_{P}}\otimes|\theta\rangle^{\otimes N_{P}}
,
the magic resources are initialized on qubits located far from
A
. We begin by analyzing a standard brickwork circuit, where each layer is given by

U_{\mathrm{layer}}=\left(\prod_{x=1}^{L/2-1}U_{2x,2x+1}\right)\left(\prod_{x=1}^{L/2}U_{2x-1,2x}\right)\;.

(11)

We study how
\Delta_{A}^{(k)}(\ket{\psi_{t}})
evolves as a function of the circuit depth
t
. The results in Figs.
3
(a,b) reveal a sharp contrast in timescales. When the magic in the initial state is adjacent to
A
, the discrepancy
\Delta_{A}^{(k)}
decays rapidly and saturates after a constant depth, i.e.
O(1)
in system size. Curves of
\Delta_{A}^{(k)}(t)
for different
L
collapse onto one another, showing that only a finite scrambling depth is required for the ensemble to reach its asymptotic randomness when the initial state is supported within or near
A
(Fig.
3
(a)).
In contrast, when the magic is injected far away from
A
(case b), the approach to saturation slows down with increasing
L
. By fitting
\Delta_{A}^{(k)}\sim e^{-t/\tau}
(Fig.
3
(b, inset)), we extract a convergence timescale
\tau
that grows logarithmically with the distance of the magic from
A
, i.e.
\tau=O(\log L)
for a system of length
L
.
In the End Matter, we analyze an alternative protocol in which the system is initialized in
|\psi_{0}\rangle=|0\rangle^{\otimes L}
and the last
N_{P}
qubits are measured in
|\pm\theta\rangle=(|0\rangle\pm e^{i\theta}|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
, thereby injecting non-stabilizerness. Analytical and numerical arguments confirm that this setup yields the same behavior as initializing the system in
|\psi_{0}^{\mathrm{(right)}}\rangle
and subsequently performing projective measurements on
B
.

Lastly, we investigate the long-range Clifford circuits of
Bao et al. 2024b
, where we evolve under layers where
L\Delta t
random qubit pairs
(x,y)
are selected with probability
p(|x-y|)\propto|x-y|^{-\alpha}
(
x\neq y
), each acted upon by a random two-qubit Clifford
U_{x,y}\sim\mathfrak{C}_{2}
, with
\Delta t
the time step associated to each layer and periodic boundary conditions (see Fig.
3
(c,inset)).
We start from the state
|\psi_{0}\rangle=\ket{0}^{\otimes(L/2-2)}\otimes\ket{\phi_{r}(2)}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes(L/2-2)}
, meaning the magic is injected in the middle of the chain.
For strong long-range interactions (
\alpha\lesssim 2
), the projected ensemble converges to the stationary global Clifford value in constant depth,
O(1)
, while for suppressed long-range couplings (
\alpha\gtrsim 2
) the dynamics reduce to the local case with logarithmic saturation,
\tau=O(\log L)
, in agreement with the entanglement transition found in
Bao et al. 2024b
.
This is evident by analysing the finite-size shifts in Figs.
3
(c,d).
While in Fig.
3
(c), with
\alpha=1
, the saturation times rescalled by
\log L
shift towards the left, in Fig.
3
(d), with
\alpha=3
, the same rescalled saturation times converge with
L
.
Taken together, these results reflect a form of magic teleportation requirements.
In local circuits, magic must propagate to generate a global approximate design
Magni et al. 2025
;
Grevink et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2025b
so that measurements effectively induce a state design on
A
.
This mimics the case of random Haar circuits that was previously analyzed in
Lami et al. 2025c
, with the important difference that it applies to doped Clifford circuits, of relevance in present architectures.

IV
Discussion

We have characterized the emergence of randomness in projected ensembles generated by Clifford circuits doped with a finite amount of magic. Our analysis establishes a direct quantitative relation between the design order and the stabilizer entropy of the initial state: in the deep-circuit limit, the deviation from Haar randomness decays exponentially with
M_{k}
, interpolating smoothly between stabilizer states (1-designs) and ensembles with higher-order correlations. This behavior is captured by our MIDA formula supported by both numerical data and rigorous bounds derived from Clifford commutant theory.

Going beyond global circuits, we analyzed shallow architectures and identified the role of magic teleportation in randomness generation. In local circuits, saturation times grow logarithmically with system size, reflecting the need for magic to propagate across the system before inducing design properties on
A
. By contrast, in long-range architectures, nonlocal gates allow magic to “jump” across the system, collapsing this light-cone and enabling randomness generation in constant depth. These findings directly connect the behavior of doped Clifford circuits to the phenomenology previously observed in Haar random circuits, but now in a setting directly relevant to near-term fault-tolerant devices.

Theorem.
Consider the state
\ket{\psi(N_{P},D)}=\prod_{j=1}^{N_{P}}e^{i\sigma_{j}\theta_{j}}\mathcal{C}_{D}\ket{0}

\sigma_{j}
are local Pauli matrix operators acting on site

N_{P}

\ket{\psi(N_{P},D)}=\mathcal{C}_{D(N_{P})}\left[\otimes_{j=1}^{N_{P}^{\prime}}\ket{\phi(\theta_{j})}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes n-N_{P}^{\prime}}\right]

(13)

where
N_{P}^{\prime}\leq N_{P}
,
\ket{\phi(\theta_{j})}=\cos\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\ket{0}+\sin\frac{\theta_{j}}{2}\ket{1}
, and
D(N_{P})\leqslant 19D+9N_{P}
.

Proof.

We can write the state as

\ket{\psi(N_{P},D)}=\mathcal{C}_{D}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{P}}e^{is_{j}\theta_{j}}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}

(14)

where
s_{j}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
are obtained by the twirling of
\sigma
via
\mathcal{C}_{D}
.
Notice that all the
s_{j}
commute with one another.
Now, for each
s_{j}
, two situations can happen: (i) either it acts trivially on
\ket{0}
, which would effectively reduce the number of non-Clifford gates to
N_{P}^{\prime}\leq N_{P}
, or (ii) it does not act trivially.
Let us assume that
N_{P}^{\prime}

s_{j}
s act nontrivially. This necessarily means that there exists an element of the
\mathbb{Z}
group generating
\ket{0}
for which each
s_{j}
individually anti-commutes.
It follows that it exists a Clifford
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
such that

\mathcal{C}^{\prime\dagger}s_{j}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=X_{j}\quad,\quad\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}=\ket{0}^{\otimes n}

(15)

where
X_{j}
is the
X
Pauli matrix acting on the site
j
.
The existence of the above Clifford follows from the fact that all
s_{j}
commute with each other and all of them anti-commute with the
\mathbb{Z}
group.
This implies we can write

\ket{\psi(N_{P},D)}=\mathcal{C}_{D}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{P}^{\prime}}e^{iX_{j}\theta_{j}}\ket{0}^{\otimes n}

(16)

It just suffices to bound the depth of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
We notice that each
s_{j}
has been constructed by a single qubit Pauli matrix twirled by a depth
D
nearest-neighbor Clifford
\mathcal{C}_{D}

2D
many sites.
The Clifford
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is defined collectively on all the
s_{j}
, which altogether are different from the identity on a support
2D+N_{P}^{\prime}
. Hence, the Clifford
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
can be defined only on
2D+N_{P}
qubits.
To conclude the proof, we note that any Clifford circuit on
N

9N
in a nearest neighbor architecture

. Hence, the Clifford

Appendix D
Projected ensemble of a stabilizer state

Consider an
L

\ket{\psi}
with stabilizer group
S(\ket{\psi})=\langle s_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{L}\rangle
, where
s_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{L}
are the generators.
If we measure
\ket{\psi}
on site
i
in the
Z
-basis, there are two possible cases to consider:

1.

Z_{i}
commutes with all the generators,
[s_{j},Z_{i}]=0\,,\forall j\in\{1,\dots,L\}
.
In this case
Z_{i}\ket{\psi}
is stabilized by the same generators as
\ket{\psi}

Z_{i}\ket{\psi}=\pm\ket{\psi}
.
Measuring does not alter the state and
\pm Z_{i}
stabilizes the state
\ket{\psi}
(the sign agrees with the measurement outcome).

2.

There is one generator
s_{j}
which anti-commutes with
Z_{i}

\{s_{j},Z_{i}\}=0

The generators can be chosen such that only a single generator anti-commutes with

s_{l}
also anti-commutes with
Z_{i}

In this case, the measurement outcome probabilities are given by

p(+1)=\bra{\psi}\frac{1+Z_{i}}{2}\ket{\psi}=\bra{\psi}s_{j}^{\dagger}\frac{1+Z_{i}}{2}s_{j}\ket{\psi}=\bra{\psi}\frac{1-Z_{i}}{2}\ket{\psi}=p(-1)\ ,

(17)

so we can conclude that
p(+1)=p(-1)=1/2
and the post-measurement state is stabilized by
\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},\pm Z_{i},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{L}\rangle

Note that in both cases, the post-measurement state is stabilized by

, and the remaining generators, which are also generators of the pre-measurement state, can only be
\mathds{1}
or
Z
at site

If we iteratively measure many sites, the surviving generators of the pre-measurement state will always be diagonal in the measured subspace.
If we measure out a subsystem as in Fig.
1

S(\ket{\psi(z_{B})})=\langle s_{1},\dots,s_{L_{A}},\alpha^{\prime}_{1}(z_{B})Z_{L_{A}+1},\dots,\alpha^{\prime}_{L_{B}}(z_{B})Z_{L}\rangle\ ,\ \alpha^{\prime}_{i}(z_{B})\in\{-1,1\}\ \ ,

(18)

where the generators

are diagonal in

We can then get rid of the non-trivial diagonal elements of

Then the state can be trivially reduced to subspace

S(\ket{\psi_{A}(z_{B})})=\langle\alpha_{1}(z_{B})s^{A}_{1},\dots,\alpha_{L_{A}}(z_{B})s^{A}_{L_{A}}\rangle\ ,\ \alpha_{i}(z_{B})\in\{-1,1\}\ ,

\beta_{i}(z_{B}^{\prime})=\beta_{i}(z_{B})\ \forall i
, then
|\langle{\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B})}|\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=1
, meaning
|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|\in\{0,1\}
.
Else if
\alpha^{1}_{i}\beta_{i}(z_{B}^{\prime})=\alpha^{2}_{i}\beta_{i}(z_{B})\ \forall i
, then
|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=\sqrt{2}/2
.
Otherwise, if none of the previous conditions holds, all the stabilizers involved in the overlap are orthogonal and the overlap is zero.

Now that we have a good understanding of the measurement procedure, let us try to compute the overlap distributions of states in the projected ensemble.
We will first attempt to compute the probabilities of each of the following possible cases (which depend only on the choice of the global Clifford gate):

•

(i)

The measurements kill one of the stabilizers in
\ket{\psi}
.

•

(ii)

The measurements merge the stabilizers in
\ket{\psi}
.

•

(iii)

The stabilizers remain orthogonal after the measurements.

It is first useful to compute the probability of finding a deterministic measurement at a given site
i
(assuming all sites larger than
i
have already been measured),

P(i\in\{d_{j}\}_{j})=2\frac{\#_{S}(i-1)}{\#_{S}(i)}=\frac{1}{2^{i}+1}\ ,

(26)

where
\#_{S}(n)=2^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(2^{i}+1)
are the number of
n
-qubit stabilizer states.
Then the probability of getting no deterministic measurements is

P(N_{d}=0)=\prod_{i=L_{A}+1}^{L}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{i}+1}\right)=\prod_{i=L_{A}+1}^{L}\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{2^{i}}}\underset{L_{A}\gg 1}{\simeq}1\ .

(27)

Let us focus on the large
L_{A}
limit, where there are no deterministic measurements and (i) does not occur.
Since we act on the initial state with a global random Clifford
C
, the signs
\alpha_{i}^{\nu}
are fully random.
Moreover, the probability of finding (ii), which corresponds to the case
\alpha_{i}^{1}=\alpha_{i}^{2}\ \forall\ i\in\{1,\dots,L_{A}\}
is also exponentially surpressed at large
L_{A}
.
We are then left with (iii), with Born rule probabilities
p(z_{B})=2^{L_{A}-L}
, and need only compute the probability distribution of each overlap,

\begin{aligned} &P\left(|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=1\right)=\frac{1}{2}P\left(\beta_{i}(z_{B}^{\prime})=\beta_{i}(z_{B})\ \forall\ i\right)=2^{-L_{A}-1}\\
&P\left(|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=\sqrt{2}/2\right)=P\left(\alpha_{i}^{1}\beta_{i}(z_{B}^{\prime})=\alpha_{i}^{2}\beta_{i}(z_{B})\ \forall\ i\right)=2^{-L_{A}}\\
&P\left(|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=0\right)=1-3\left(2^{-L_{A}-1}\right)\end{aligned}\,.

(28)

Note that the factor of
1/2
in the first term comes from the fact that the condition
\beta_{i}(z_{B}^{\prime})=\beta_{i}(z_{B})\ \forall\ i
ensures that
\ket{\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B})}=\pm\ket{\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B}^{\prime})}
, but it is only when
\ket{\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B})}=+\ket{\psi_{A}^{\nu}(z_{B}^{\prime})}
that the
|\langle\psi_{A}(z_{B})|\psi_{A}(z_{B}^{\prime})\rangle|=1
.
After applying the overlap distributions to (
1
), the frame potential at large
L_{A}
gives (
5
) which agrees with the analytical prediction at
k=1
and with the numerical prediction in Fig.
7
, i.e.
\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{C}\sim\mathfrak{C}}[C_{2}]\underset{L_{A}\gg 1}{\rightarrow}2^{-L_{A}}
.

Appendix F
Upper bound on distance with generic initial magic states

In this section, we present analytical arguments that substantiate the upper bound (
10
) presented in the main text. For this purpose, let us define the usual twirling operators for the Haar and Clifford groups as

\displaystyle\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}(O)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim{\text{Haar}}}[U^{\otimes m}O(U^{\dagger})^{\otimes m}]

(29)

\displaystyle\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}(O)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{U\sim{\text{Cl}}}[U^{\otimes m}O(U^{\dagger})^{\otimes m}]\,.

It is possible to compute the twirling operators above by the commutant theory over the unitary group and the Clifford group respectively. In particular, the commutant of the unitary group is spanned by permutation operators
\sigma\in S_{m}
, and consequently, the twirling reads

\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}(O)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}c^{{\text{Haar}}}_{\sigma}(O)\sigma\,,\qquad c^{{\text{Haar}}}_{\sigma}(O)=\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\in S_{m}}{\text{Wg}}^{{\text{Haar}}}_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}\tr[\sigma^{\prime}O]

(30)

where the matrix
{\text{Wg}}^{{\text{Haar}}}
is given by the inverse of the Gram-Schmidt matrix
G_{\sigma^{\prime}\sigma}=\tr(\sigma\sigma')
and its components are called
Weingarten functions
.
A similar expression can also be written for the Clifford group. The commutant of the Clifford group is spanned by the set of Pauli monomials
\Omega

Bittel et al. 2025
;
Bittel and Leone 2025
. Following the notation of Refs.
Bittel et al. 2025
;
Bittel and Leone 2025
, we denote the set of these Pauli monomials as
\mathcal{P}=\{\Omega\}
. The twirling operators for the Clifford group can be expressed as:

\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}(O)=\sum_{\Omega\in\mathcal{P}}c^{{\text{Cl}}}_{\Omega}(O)\Omega\,,\qquad c^{{\text{Cl}}}_{\Omega}(O)=\sum_{\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}}{\text{Wg}}^{{\text{Cl}}}_{\Omega^{\prime}\Omega}\tr[(\Omega^{\prime})^{\dagger}O]\,.

(31)

where, similarly to the case of the unitary group, the matrix
{\text{Wg}}^{{\text{Cl}}}
is obtained by the inverse of the Gram-Schmidt matrix
G_{\Omega\Omega^{\prime}}=\tr(\Omega\Omega')
and its components are called
Clifford-Weingarten functions
. The number of Pauli monomials can be bounded as (see Lemma 17
Bittel and Leone 2025
)

|\mathcal{P}|=\prod_{j=0}^{m-2}(2^{j}+1)\leq 2^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}-\frac{3}{2}m+6}\,.

(32)

Now we have all the machinery to prove Eq. (10). In our setup (see Fig.
1
), the generalized frame potential (averaged over the global Clifford) reads

F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Cl}}}=\tr[Q^{(k,n)}\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)]\,.

(33)

where

Q^{(k,n)}=\sum_{z,z^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{N_{B}}}(|z\rangle\langle z|\otimes|z^{\prime}\rangle\langle z^{\prime}|)^{\otimes n}(|z\rangle\langle z^{\prime}|\otimes|z^{\prime}\rangle\langle z|)^{\otimes k}

(34)

and
m=2(n+k)
is the total number of replicas. The conventional frame potential is recovered in the limit
n\to 1-k
(
m\to 2
). We compare
F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Cl}}}
with the corresponding value for Haar, namely

F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Haar}}}=\tr[Q^{(k,n)}\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)]\,.

(35)

The deviation is

\left|F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Cl}}}-F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\right|=\left|\tr[Q^{(k,n)}\bigg(\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)-\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)\bigg)]\right|

(36)

which, by standard matrix inequalities, can be bounded as

\left|F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Cl}}}-F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\right|\leq\left\|Q^{(k,n)}\right\|_{\infty}\left\|\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)-\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)]\right\|_{1}\,.

(37)

Now notice that

Q^{(k,n)}=\sum_{Z=(z,z^{\prime})}(|Z\rangle\langle Z|)^{\otimes n}(|Z\rangle\langle Z|)^{\otimes k}\text{SWAP}=\sum_{Z}(|Z\rangle\langle Z|)^{\otimes(n+k)}\text{SWAP}

(38)

where
SWAP
is inserted to swap
z^{\prime}
and
z
in the last
2k
replicas. Using the unitary invariance of the Schatten norms, we find

\left\|Q^{(k,n)}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\sum_{Z}(|Z\rangle\langle Z|)^{\otimes(n+k)}\right\|_{\infty}=1\,.

(39)

Now we have to deal only with the term
\left\|\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)-\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)\right\|_{1}
. Bounds for this term can be found in Theorem 14 of
Bittel and Leone 2025
. In particular, for all
k\geq 4
, we have

2^{-2M_{3}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)}-\frac{1}{2^{L}}-\frac{16}{2^{2L}}\leq\left\|\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Cl}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)-\Phi^{(m)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\big(\rho_{0}^{\otimes m}\big)\right\|_{1}\leq 2^{\frac{m^{2}}{2}}2^{-M_{2}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)}+2^{2m^{2}-L}\,.

(40)

We are interested in the normalized deviation of the
k
-th frame potential, namely

\Delta^{(k)}_{A}=\frac{\left|F^{(k)}_{{\text{Cl}}}-F^{(k)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\right|}{F^{(k)}_{{\text{Haar}}}}\simeq\frac{2^{kL_{A}}}{k!}\lim_{n\to 1-k}\left|F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Cl}}}-F^{(k,n)}_{{\text{Haar}}}\right|

(41)

Using the above inequalities (with
m\to 2
), and neglecting all terms that are exponentially vanishing for large
N
, we arrive at

\Delta^{(k)}_{A}\leq 4\frac{2^{kL_{A}}}{k!}2^{-M_{2}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)}\equiv\overline{\Delta_{A}^{(k)}}\,.

(42)

If we define the stabiliser purities as

\Xi_{2k}(|\psi\rangle)=e^{\left[(1-k)M_{k}(|\psi\rangle)\right]}=2^{-L}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{P}_{L}}\langle\psi|s|\psi\rangle^{2k}\,,

(43)

where
s
are the Pauli strings and
\mathcal{P}_{L}=\{I,X,Y,Z\}^{\otimes L}
, we find

\frac{2^{kL_{A}}}{k!}\Xi_{4}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)\leq\overline{\Delta_{A}^{(k)}}\,.

(44)

Since
\Xi_{k}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)\leq\Xi_{4}(|\psi_{0}\rangle)
, then
\exp\left[(1-k)M_{k}(|\psi\rangle)\right]\leq\exp\left[-M_{2}(|\psi\rangle)\right]
for
k\geq 4
, which makes this inequality compatible with MIDA that reads, for
2^{L_{A}}\gg k
,

\Delta_{A}^{(k)}=\frac{2^{(k-1)L_{A}}}{k!}e^{(1-k)M_{k}(|\psi\rangle)}\leq\overline{\Delta_{A}^{(k)}}\,.

(45)

Although the inequalities derived above hold rigorously for all
n,k\geq 1
, the analytic continuation to the limit
n\rightarrow 1-k
is not guaranteed to preserve the validity of the bound. Nevertheless, the bound is confirmed by our numerics. Moreover, for sufficiently large
A
the measurement outcome probabilities
p(z_{B})
will be close to uniform as a result of anticoncentration; thus, the dependence of all relevant quantities on
n
will be parametrically weak. It is therefore unlikely that this non-rigorous step is crucial.

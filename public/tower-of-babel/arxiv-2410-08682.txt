Abstract

Let V Γ p ​ ( 𝒢 ) , 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ , V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G}),1\leq p\leq\infty, be the quasi shift-invariant space generated by Γ \Gamma -shifts of a function 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , where Γ ⊂ ℝ \Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}} is a separated set. For several large families of generators 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , we present necessary and sufficient conditions on Γ \Gamma that imply that the Γ \Gamma -shifts of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} form an unconditional basis for V Γ p ​ ( 𝒢 ) V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G}) . 1.
Introduction

1.1.
Paley–Wiener and Wiener amalgam spaces

Let
\hat{f}
denote the Fourier transform of a function
f
,

\hat{f}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-2\pi ixt}f(t)\,dt.

Given a set
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}
of finite positive measure, the classical Paley–Wiener space
PW_{S}
consists of all
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
whose inverse Fourier transform vanishes a.e. outside
S
.

The Wiener amalgam space
W
consists of measurable functions
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}:
satisfying

(1.1)

\|f\|_{W}:=\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(k,k+1)}<\infty.

The space
W
introduced by N. Wiener (see
[
27
]
) is proven to be important in harmonic analysis and time-frequency analysis, particularly in Gabor frame theory and theory of shift and quasi-shift invariant spaces, see e.g.,
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
, and references therein.

A trivial observation frequently used below is that condition
\mathcal{G}\in W
implies
\mathcal{G}\in L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}),1\leq p\leq\infty,
and that
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is a continuous function.

1.2.
Quasi shift-invariant spaces

A set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is called separated if

(1.2)

\Delta(\Gamma):=\inf_{\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Gamma,\gamma\neq\gamma^{\prime}}|\gamma-\gamma^{\prime}|>0.

Every separated set is countable.

Given a function
\mathcal{G}:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
with a ”reasonably fast” decay at
\pm\infty
and a number
p,1\leq p\leq\infty
, the shift-invariant space
V_{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
consists of all functions
f
of the form

f(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}\mathcal{G}(x-n),\quad\{c_{n}\}\in l^{p}({\mathbb{Z}}).

More generally, given a separated set
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, the quasi shift-invariant space
V_{\Gamma}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
consists of all functions of the form

(1.3)

f(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma),\quad\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{p}(\Gamma).

The function
\mathcal{G}
is called the generator for the space
V_{\Gamma}^{p}(\mathcal{G})
.

A classical example is the Paley–Wiener space
PW^{2}_{[-1/2,1/2]}
which is exactly the shift-invariant space
V^{2}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{G})
generated by the sinc-function

(1.4)

\mathcal{G}(x)={\rm sinc}(x):=\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}.

The (quasi) shift-invariant spaces have important applications in mathematics and engineering, particularly because they are often used as models for spaces of signals and images. For example, in time-frequency analysis, the well-known close connection between the Gabor frames and sampling sets for shift-invariant spaces allows one to obtain new information on the frame sets for different generators, see
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
23
]
.

1.3.
Main problem

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty.
We study a basic property of the quasi shift-invariant space
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
, specifically that the
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{p}
-stable, i.e. there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

(1.5)

C_{1}\|{\bf c}\|_{p}\leq\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\right\|_{p}\leq C_{2}\|{\bf c}\|_{p},\quad\mbox{for every ${\bf c}=\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{p}(\Gamma).$}

The
l^{p}
-stability means that
V^{p}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G})
is a closed subspace of
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
and that the system
\{\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
forms an unconditional basis in this space. For
p=2
, condition (
1.5
) means that the system
\{\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}
constitutes a Riesz basis for
V^{2}_{\Gamma}(\mathcal{G}).

In this paper, we consider the following

Problem 1.1
.

What assumptions should be imposed on
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{G}
to ensure that the set of
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
is
l^{p}
-stable?

The stability property of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-shifts is well-studied.
The following result is an immediate corollary of Theorem 3.5 in
[
11
]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}\in W
and
p\in[1,\infty]
. Then the integer-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{p}
-stable if and only if the Fourier transform
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{G}
does not vanish on any set
{\mathbb{Z}}+b,0\leq b<1.

However, results on the stability of
\Gamma
-shifts are scarce. We mention the paper
[
9
]
, which proves the
l^{2}
-stability of
\Gamma
-shifts for certain generators
\mathcal{G}
under the condition that
\Gamma
is a complete interpolating sequence for the Paley–Wiener space
PW_{[-1/2,1/2]}
.
We also mention the papers
[
8
,
25
]
which, in particular, study the stability of
\Gamma
-shifts for generators from certain classes. In particular, in
[
8
]
, it was shown that
\Gamma
-shifts of totally positive generators are
l^{2}
-stable for every separated set
\Gamma.

We will say that the
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are stable if they are
l^{p}
-stable for every
1\leq p\leq\infty.
We note that for a wide class of generators, the stability property of shifts does not depend on
p
.
Observe that the right-hand-side inequality in (
1.5
) is true for every separated set
\Gamma
, every generator
\mathcal{G}\in W
and every
p\in[1,\infty]
, see Lemma 8.1 in
[
25
]
. On the other hand, under a mild additional condition on the generator, it suffices to check that the left-hand-side inequality is true for
p=\infty
.
The following result follows from Lemma 8.1 and Theorem 1.6 in
[
25
]
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a separated set and
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
. If
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{\infty}
-stable, then they are stable.

In a more general situation, namely,
\mathcal{G}\in W,
the conclusion of this theorem remains true if one replaces
p=\infty
with
p=2
, see Theorem
2.11
below. Moreover, under additional assumptions on
\mathcal{G}
, the
l^{\infty}
-space can be replaced by
c_{0}
-space or even
c_{00}
-space, see Theorem
5.1
.

We conclude this section with a brief presentation of our two main results.
They show an intimate connection between the stability of general shifts, a certain interpolation property, and the existence of certain crystalline measures.

•

Theorem
2.5
(see sec. 2) states that, if
|\hat{\mathcal{G}}|^{2}\in W
, then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{2}
-stable if and only if there is a number
r>0
such that
\Gamma
is an interpolation set for
PW_{\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)|>r\}}
.

•

Theorem
3.3
(see sec. 3) states that, if
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
and the zero set
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
of
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is locally finite, then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{\infty}
-stable if and only no sequence of translates
\Gamma-x_{k},x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}},
may weakly converge to a set that supports a sparse crystalline measure with bounded coefficients whose spectrum lies in
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}).

See Sections 2 and 3 for necessary definitions and some results on interpolation in Paley–Wiener spaces and crystalline measures.
Using these results and the above theorems, we obtain new results on the stability of shifts: For the generators
\mathcal{G}
satisfying
|\mathcal{G}|^{2}\in W
and
{\rm Zer}{\,(\hat{\mathcal{G}})}={\mathbb{R}}\setminus(a,b),\,a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we completely describe the family of separated sets
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are stable. A similar result is obtained for the generators
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
such that
{\rm Zer}{\,(\hat{\mathcal{G}})}
is a finite union of separated sets, see Sections 2 and 3.

2.
l^{2}
-stability of
\Gamma
-shifts and interpolation

2.1.

When
\Gamma={\mathbb{Z}},
one may describe the set of all generators for which
(
1.5
) with
p=2
is true. The following result is known (see e.g.,
[
10
, Theorem 10.19]
) and not hard to prove:

Lemma 2.1
.

Given a non-trivial generator
\mathcal{G}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
. For every non-trivial sequence
{\bf c}=\{c_{n}\}\in l^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, we have

\essi_{t\in(0,1)}\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t-n)|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\|{\bf c}\|_{2}^{2}}\left\|\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{n}\mathcal{G}(t-n)\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\ess_{t\in(0,1)}\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t-n)|^{2}.

Both inequalities in (
2.1
) are sharp, with the right-hand side being the smallest possible value and the left-hand side the largest possible value that satisfies the inequalities.

Therefore, the conditions

(2.1)

\essi_{t\in(0,1)}\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t-n)|^{2}>0,\quad\ess_{t\in(0,1)}\sum\limits_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t-n)|^{2}<\infty

are necessary and sufficient for the
l^{2}
-stability of
{\mathbb{Z}}
-shifts.
See Section
4.1
for an extension of the right-hand-side inequality in Lemma
2.1
to a certain class of separated sets
\Gamma.

In this section, we will consider generators
\mathcal{G}
satisfying the condition

(2.2)

|\hat{\mathcal{G}}|^{2}\in W.

Condition (
2.2
) has already appeared in studying the stability properties of integer translates, see
[
10
, Lemma 10.24]
.
In particular, it implies the second inequality in (
2.1
). It also implies
\hat{\mathcal{G}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),
and so
\mathcal{G}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).
This condition is sufficient for the validity of the right-hand-side inequality in (
1.5
) with
p=2
:

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
\Gamma
is a separated set and
\mathcal{G}
satisfies (
2.2
). There exists
C>0
such that

(2.3)

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(\,\cdot-\gamma)\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq C\|{\bf c}\|_{2}^{2},\quad\mbox{for every ${\bf c}=\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{2}(\Gamma).$}

2.2.
Stability of shifts and interpolation sets for Paley-Wiener spaces

Definition 2.3
.

Let
S\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a set of positive finite measures. A separated set
\Gamma
is called an interpolation set for
PW_{S}
if for every
\{c_{\gamma}\}\in l^{2}(\Gamma)
there exists
f\in PW_{S}
satisfying
f(\gamma)=c_{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma.

It is well-known that every interpolation set
\Gamma
for a space
PW_{S}
is separated.

When
S=[a,b]
is a single interval, the interpolation property can be essentially described in terms of the upper uniform density of
\Gamma
, defined by

D^{+}(\Gamma):=\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\#(\Gamma\cap(x,x+r))}{r}.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
12
]
)
.

If
D^{+}(\Gamma)<b-a,
then
\Gamma
is an interpolation set for
PW_{[a,b]}.
If
D^{+}(\Gamma)>b-a,
then it is not.

A complete description of interpolation sets for the case of a single interval is known, but it is not expressed in terms of some density (see
[
24
]
). For a discussion on the interpolation problem for
PW_{S}
in the setting of disconnected spectra
S
, we refer the reader to
[
19
, Lecture 5]
.

We now formulate the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
\mathcal{G}
is a non-trivial generator satisfying (
2.2
).
Then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{2}
-stable if and only if there is a number
r>0
such that
\Gamma
is an interpolation set for
PW_{\{|\hat{\mathcal{G}}|>r\}}
.

Above we set
\{|\hat{\mathcal{G}}|>r\}:=\{t\in{\mathbb{R}}:|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)|>r\}
. This set is defined a.e.

Using Theorem
2.5
, we give two examples of generators
\mathcal{G}
for which it is easy to describe all separated sets
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{2}
-stable.

Example 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(x)={\rm sinc}(x)
. Then
\hat{\mathcal{G}}={\bf 1}_{[-1/2,1/2]}
. By Theorem
2.5
,
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are

-stable if and only if
\Gamma
is an interpolation set for

. Therefore, the interpolation sets
\Gamma
for

are exactly the sets

for which

-shifts of

are

-stable.

A similar result holds for every generator
\mathcal{G}
such that

|\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)|>\delta>0
a.e. on an interval
(a,b)
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)=0
a.e. for

.

Example 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}(x)={\rm sinc}^{n}(x),n\geq 2
. Then

is continuous and vanishes outside

Finally, the following result is a simple consequence of Theorems

and

Corollary 2.8
.

The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

(ii)

-shifts of

-stable, for every non-trivial

satisfying (

3.
Stability of shifts and crystalline measures

Below we consider the family of continuous generators
\mathcal{G}\in W
such that the
zero set Zer
(\hat{\mathcal{G}}):=\{t:\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)=0\}
of
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is
locally finite
, i.e.

(3.1)

\#\left(\mbox{Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})\cap(x,x+1)\right)<\infty,\quad\mbox{ for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

This condition is satisfied whenever
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is a real analytic (or a quasi-analytic) function.
In particular, every
\mathcal{G}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
with bounded support satisfies (
3.1
).

It turns out that the stability of
\Gamma
-shifts property for the generators satisfying (
3.1
) is equivalent to the existence of certain crystalline measures.

3.1.
Sparse crystalline measures

A crystalline measure is an atomic measure supported by a locally ﬁnite set whose distributional Fourier transform is also supported by a locally ﬁnite set. More precisely, following Yves Meyer (see e.g.
[
17
]
or
[
18
]
), we introduce

Definition 3.1
.

An atomic measure

\mu=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\delta_{\gamma}

is a crystalline measure if

(i)

\mu
is a tempered distribution
;

(ii)

The distributional Fourier transform
\hat{\mu}
is again an atomic measure

\hat{\mu}=\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}\delta_{s};

(iii)

The support
\Gamma
of
\mu
and its spectrum
S
are locally finite sets.

Measure
\mu
is called a sparse crystalline measure if the set
\Gamma
is separated.

Denote by
S({\mathbb{R}})
the set of all Schwartz functions. Given a crystalline measure
\mu
, the following generalized Poisson formula holds for every
F\in S({\mathbb{R}})
(see
[
18
]
, Section 3):

(3.2)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\hat{F}(\gamma)=\sum_{s\in S}a_{s}F(s).

A classical example of a sparse crystalline measure is the Poisson comb

\mu=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\delta_{n}.

The Poisson summation formula implies
\hat{\mu}=\mu.

More generally, every generalized Poisson comb

(3.3)

\mu=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}P_{k}(n)\delta_{an+x_{k}},\quad m\in{\mathbb{N}},

is a sparse crystalline measure, where
a>0
,
x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
P_{k},k=1,...,m,
are arbitrary exponential polynomials with purely imaginary frequencies. One can check that the Fourier transform of a generalized Poisson comb is also a generalized Poisson comb.

Crystalline measures are useful for studying mathematical structures and their applications in signal processing and crystallography.
They are essential for finding an adequate mathematical model for the physical quasicrystals discovered in the 1980s by Dan Shechtman.

We will say that a separated set
\Gamma
supports a sparse crystalline measure, if there exists a sparse crystalline measure
\mu
whose support lies in
\Gamma
.

3.2.
Weak limits of translates of separated sets

Given a sequence of separated sets
\Gamma_{k}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\inf_{k}\Delta(\Gamma_{k})>0,
where
\Delta(\Gamma)
is defined in (
1.2
), we say that the sequence converges weakly to some separated set
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
if for every
\epsilon>0
and
R>0
there is an integer
l=l(\epsilon,R)
such that

\Gamma_{k}\cap(-R,R)\subset\Gamma^{\prime}+(-\epsilon,\epsilon),\quad\Gamma^{\prime}\cap(-R,R)\subset\Gamma_{k}+(-\epsilon,\epsilon),\quad k\geq l.

Let
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be a separated set. We denote by
W(\Gamma)
the set of weak limits of all possible sequences
\Gamma_{k}:=\Gamma-x_{k},x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}.

We need the following lemma, see e.g.,
[
19
, Lecture 3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Every sequence of separated sets

\{\Gamma_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
satisfying
\inf_{k}\Delta(\Gamma_{k})=:d>0,
contains a subsequence
\Gamma_{k_{j}}
which converges weakly to some separated set
\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfying
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq d.

In particular, every sequence of translates
\Gamma-x_{k}:x_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}},
of a separated set
\Gamma
contains a subsequence
\Gamma-x_{k_{j}}
which converges weakly to some separated set
\Gamma^{\prime}
satisfying
\Delta(\Gamma^{\prime})\geq\Delta(\Gamma).

3.3.
Stability of shifts and crystalline measures

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a separated set and
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
satisfies (
3.1
).
Then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{\infty}
-stable if and only if no set
\Gamma^{\ast}\in W(\Gamma)
supports a sparse crystalline measure with bounded coefficients whose spectrum lies in
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}).

Recall that by Theorem
1.3
, under the conditions of this theorem, the
l^{\infty}
-stability of
\Gamma
-shifts implies the
l^{p}
-stability, for every
1\leq p<\infty.

A stronger result is true for the generators
\mathcal{G}
such that the set
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})

is a
finite union
of separated sets.
It is proved in
[
15
, Theorem 2.2]
that a sparse crystalline measure with such spectrum is a generalized Poisson comb

1

We also refer the reader to the paper
[

]
, where the same statement is proved for separated sets
\Gamma
.
. This and Theorem
3.3
imply

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is a separated set and
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
is such that the set

Then

This observation establishes the connection between the sets
\Gamma
and
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
. Moreover, if one of these sets has ”particularly nice” properties then the stability of shifts is ”universal”, i.e., it does not depend on the properties of the other set. More precisely, Corollary
3.4
leads to the following results:

•

We give new examples of generators
\mathcal{G}
such that
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{\infty}
-stable for every separated set
\Gamma
.

•

There exist separated sets
\Gamma=\{...<\gamma_{k-1}<\gamma_{k}<\gamma_{k+1}<...,k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
satisfying

(3.4)

\gamma_{k+1}-\gamma_{k}\leq d,\quad\mbox{for some }d>0,

such that
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are
l^{\infty}
-stable, for every generator
\mathcal{G}
satisfying the assumptions imposed in Corollary
3.4
.

The support
\Gamma
of a generalized Poisson comb defined in (
3.3
) is a subset of a separated periodic set, i.e.
\Gamma\subseteq a{\mathbb{Z}}+F,
for some
a>0
and a finite set

. Its spectrum (the support of its Fourier transform) is a subset of

where
\tilde{F}
is also a finite set.
Moreover, one may check that the support of a generalized Poisson comb
meets some arithmetic progression in
{\mathbb{R}}
in an infinite number of points. The same is true for its spectrum.
In this connection, observe that there exist sparse crystalline measures (even with integer coefficients) such that the intersection of their support with any arithmetic progression is either empty or finite, see
[
13
,
20
]
.

We see that if
\mathcal{G}
satisfies the assumptions of Corollary
3.4
and the intersection of
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
with every arithmetic progression is either finite or empty, then
\Gamma
-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are

Lemma 4.6

Let

-shifts of
\mathcal{G}
are not
c_{0}
-stable, i.e. for every
\epsilon>0
there is a sequence
{\bf c}\in c_{0}(\Gamma)
such that
\|{\bf c}\|_{\infty}=1
and

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon.

Choose
N
so large that
|c_{\gamma}|<\epsilon
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma,|\gamma|\geq N.
Since
\mathcal{G}\in W
, we get

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma,|\gamma|\geq N}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)\right\|_{\infty}<\epsilon\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W}.

We conclude that

\left\|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma,|\gamma|<N}c_{\gamma}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma)\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon(1+\|\mathcal{G}\|_{W}),

which proves property (iii).

Step 4.

(i)\rightarrow(iv)
. Write
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
.
By (i), for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
there is a sequence
{\bf c}^{(n)}=\{c^{(n)}_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\in l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that
\|{\bf c}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}=1
and

(5.3)

\left\|\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c^{(n)}_{k}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma_{k})\right\|_{\infty}<1/n.

For every
n
, choose an integer
j(n)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
|c^{(n)}_{j(n)}|>1-1/n
.
Clearly,
0\in\Gamma-\gamma_{j(n)}.

By Lemma
3.2
, passing to a subsequence, we may assume that

\Gamma-\gamma_{j(n)}
converges weakly to a non-empty set
\Gamma^{\prime}=\{\gamma^{\prime}_{k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}\in W(\Gamma)
. Passing again to a subsequence, we find a sequence
\{c_{k}\}\in l^{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
such that

c_{k+j(n)}^{(n)}\to c_{k},\quad n\to\infty,\ k\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Clearly,
|c_{0}|=1,
so that the limit sequence is non-trivial.
Since
\mathcal{G}\in W\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
, it follows from (
5.3
) that

\sum\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}c_{k}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma_{k}^{\prime})\equiv 0.

Step 5.

(iv)\rightarrow(iii)
.
Write

\Gamma=\{...<\gamma_{n-1}<\gamma_{n}<\gamma_{n+1}<...\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Gamma^{\ast}=\{...<\gamma^{\ast}_{n-1}<\gamma^{\ast}_{n}<\gamma^{\ast}_{n+1}<...\}.

Clearly,
(iv)
implies that
\Gamma^{\ast}
-translates of
\mathcal{G}
are not
l^{\infty}
-stable. By Steps 2 and 3,
they are not
c_{00}
-stable. Therefore, for every
\epsilon>0
there exists a finite sequence
{\bf c}=\{c_{n}\}
such that

\|{\bf c}\|_{\infty}=1,\quad\left\|\sum_{n}c_{n}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma^{\ast}_{n})\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon.

Since
\Gamma^{\ast}\in W(\Gamma)
, for every
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\delta>0
there exists
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that

|\gamma_{n+j}-\gamma^{\ast}_{n}|\leq\delta,\quad|n|\leq N.

Assumption
\mathcal{G}\in W({\mathbb{R}})\cap C({\mathbb{R}})
obviously
implies that

\left\|\sum_{n}c_{n}\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma_{n+j})\right\|_{\infty}<2\epsilon,

provided
\delta
sufficiently small. This proves Step 5.

Step 6.

(v)\rightarrow(iv)
. This follows from an approximation argument.
Choose any Schwartz function
H
such that

(5.4)

\hat{H}(0)=1,\ H(t),\hat{H}(t)\geq 0,\ t\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \hat{H}(t)=0,\ |t|\geq 1,

and put
H_{a}(t):=H(at),a>0.
Then
\widehat{H_{a}}(t)=\hat{H}(t/a)/a.

Let
\epsilon,\delta
be positive numbers, and set
\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}:=(1/\epsilon)(\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon})H_{\delta}
. Then
\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}\in S({\mathbb{R}})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}}=(1/\delta)(\hat{G}\hat{H}_{\epsilon})\ast\hat{H}_{1/\delta}.
Since
\hat{H}_{\epsilon}(t)=0,|t|\geq 1/\epsilon
and

\hat{H}_{1/\delta}(t)=0,|t|\geq\delta
, it follows that
\widehat{\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}}(t)=0

for
|t|\geq\delta+1/\epsilon.

We now apply the generalized Poisson formula (
3.2
) to the function
\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}(x-\cdot\,)
:

\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}(x-\gamma^{\ast})=\sum_{s\in{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}),|s|\leq 1/\epsilon+\delta}a_{s}e^{2\pi isx}\left((\hat{\mathcal{G}}\hat{H}_{\epsilon})\ast(1/\delta)\hat{H}_{1/\delta}\right)(s).

Using (
5.4
), we get

\left|\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}(x-\gamma^{\ast})\right|\leq H(0)\max_{|s|<1/\epsilon+\delta}|a_{s}|\max\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})\\
|s|\leq 1/\epsilon+\delta\end{subarray}}\max_{|t-s|\leq\delta}|(\hat{\mathcal{G}}\hat{H}_{\epsilon})(t)|,\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Since
\mathcal{G}\in W,
it is easy to check that
\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon}\in W.

Since
\hat{\mathcal{G}}\in C({\mathbb{R}})
and
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
vanishes on
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
, we see that the right-hand side of the above inequality tends to zero as
\delta\to 0,

so that

(5.5)

\left\|\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\mathcal{G}_{\epsilon,\delta}(x-\gamma^{\ast})\right\|_{\infty}\to 0,\quad\delta\to 0.

Condition
\mathcal{G}\in W
implies
\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon}\in W
. We now use Lemma 8.1 in
[
25
]
, which gives

(5.6)

\left\|\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon}(x-\gamma^{\ast})\right\|_{\infty}\leq C(\Gamma^{\ast})\|\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon}\|_{W}\|{\bf c^{\ast}}\|_{\infty},\quad{\bf c^{\ast}}=\{c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\}\in l^{\infty}(\Gamma^{\ast}).

Choose any small number
\rho>0
.
Write

\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon})(x-\gamma^{\ast})(H(0)-H_{\delta}(x-\gamma^{\ast}))=\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast},|\gamma^{\ast}-x|\leq R}+\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast},|\gamma^{\ast}-x|>R}=:S_{1}+S_{2}.

Since
\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon}\in W
, by (
5.6
), we may choose
R=R(\epsilon)
so large that
|S_{2}(x)|\leq\rho
, for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}.
Clearly, the functions
H_{\delta}(x)
are uniformly bounded and converge uniformly on compacts in
{\mathbb{R}}
to
H(0)\neq 0
as
\delta\to 0.
This and (
5.6
) shows that
|S_{1}|<\rho
for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, provided
\delta
is small enough.
By (
5.5
) we conclude that

(5.7)

\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{G}\ast H_{1/\epsilon})(x-\gamma^{\ast})\equiv 0.

Fix any small
\sigma>0.

Similarly to the argument on Step 2 (see (
5.2
)), one can find
\delta_{0}
such that

\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}|\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma^{\ast})-\mathcal{G}(x-s-\gamma^{\ast})|\leq\sigma,\quad\text{ for every }x\in{\mathbb{R}},|s|<\delta_{0}.

For sufficiently small
\epsilon
, the inequality

\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}\setminus(-\delta_{0},\delta_{0})}\frac{1}{\epsilon}H_{1/\epsilon}(s)ds<\frac{\sigma}{\|\mathcal{G}\|_{\infty}}.

holds. Since
\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1/\epsilon)H_{\epsilon}=1
, these two inequalities imply

\left|\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}(\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma^{\ast})-(\mathcal{G}\ast(1/\epsilon)H_{1/\epsilon})(x-\gamma^{\ast})\right|=

\left|\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma^{\ast})-\mathcal{G}(x-\gamma^{\ast}-s))(1/\epsilon)H_{\epsilon}(s)\,ds\right|\leq 2\sigma\|{\bf c^{\ast}}\|_{\infty}.

This and (
5.7
) prove
(iv)
.

Step 7.

(iv)\rightarrow(v)
. Condition
(iv)
means that the measure

\mu=\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\delta_{\gamma^{\ast}}

satisfies

\mu\ast\mathcal{G}(x)\equiv 0.

Since the coefficients
c_{\gamma^{\ast}}
are bounded and

\Gamma^{\ast}
is separated, this measure is a tempered distribution.

Claim 5.3
.

We have
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=0
, for every
\phi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support satisfying
{\rm supp\,}(\phi)\cap{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})=\emptyset.

Proof.

Since
\phi
has compact support, it suffices to prove this claim for the case when

\phi
vanishes outside a closed interval
I,I\cap{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})=\emptyset.

Let
\check{\phi}(x):=\hat{\phi}(-x)
be the inverse Fourier transform of
\phi
.
Then since

\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\hat{\phi}(\gamma^{\ast})=\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\check{\phi}(-\gamma^{\ast})=(\mu\ast\check{\phi})(0),

it suffices to prove that
\mu\ast\check{\phi}\equiv 0.

Since
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
is continuous and does not vanish on
I,
by the local form of the Wiener–Lévy Theorem (see
[
22
, Chapter 6]
) there is a function
h\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
such that
\hat{h}(t)=1/\hat{\mathcal{G}}(t)
on
I
.
Put
H:=h\ast\check{\phi},
then

\hat{H}=\hat{h}\phi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi/\hat{\mathcal{G}}&\mbox{on }I\\
0&\mbox{outside }I.\end{array}\right.

Hence,
\hat{\mathcal{G}}\hat{H}=\phi,
and so
\check{\phi}=\mathcal{G}\ast H.
Since
\mu\ast\mathcal{G}\equiv 0,
we conclude that

\mu\ast\check{\phi}=\mu\ast\mathcal{G}\ast H\equiv 0.

∎

Claim 5.4
.

Let
I_{0}
be an open interval and
t_{0}\in I_{0}
such that
I_{0}\cap{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})=\{t_{0}\}.

Assume
\phi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
vanishes outside
I_{0}
and satisfies
\phi(t_{0})=\phi^{\prime}(t_{0})=0.

Then
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=0.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
I_{0}=(-a,b)
, for some
0<a,b<1

and
t_{0}=0
. Fix a number
\epsilon,0<\epsilon<\min\{a,b\}/4
.

Choose any functions
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in S({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying

\psi_{1}(t)+\psi_{2}(t)=1,\quad|t|\leq 4,\quad\psi_{1}(t)=0,\ |t|\geq 2,\quad\psi_{2}(t)=0,\ |t|\leq 1.

Set

\phi_{j}(t):=\phi(t)\psi_{j}(t/\epsilon),j=1,2
. Then

\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}=\phi,\quad\phi_{1}(t)=0,\ |t|\geq 2\epsilon,\ \ \phi_{2}(t)=0,\ |t|\leq\epsilon.

By Claim
5.3
,
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}_{2}\rangle=0.
Let us now prove that
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}_{1}\rangle=0.

Below we denote by
C
and
C_{j}
positive constants.

Since
\phi(0)=\phi^{\prime}(0)=0,
there is a constant
C=C(\phi)
such that
\|\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}<C.

Using integration by parts, we get

\hat{\phi}_{1}(x)=\int_{-2\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}e^{-2\pi ixt}\phi_{1}(t)\,dt=-\frac{1}{(2\pi x)^{2}}\int_{-2\epsilon}^{2\epsilon}e^{-2\pi ixt}\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(t)\,dt.

This implies
|\hat{\phi}_{1}(x)|\leq C_{1}\epsilon/(1+|x|^{2})
, and so
|\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}_{1}\rangle|<C_{2}\epsilon.
Therefore,

|\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle|=|\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}_{1}\rangle|<C_{2}\epsilon.
Since
\epsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we conclude that
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=0
.
∎

Claim 5.5
.

Let
I_{0}
and
t_{0}\in I_{0}
satisfy the assumptions of Claim
5.4
. There is a constant
a_{0}
such that the equation

(5.8)

\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=a_{0}\phi(t_{0})

holds for every
\phi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
which vanishes outside
I_{0}
.

Proof.

We may assume that
I_{0}=(-a,b)
and
t_{0}=0
.
Fix any functions
\psi_{j}\in S({\mathbb{R}}),j=1,2,
which vanish outside
I_{0}
and satisfy
\psi_{1}(0)=1,\psi^{\prime}_{1}(0)=0,\psi_{2}(0)=0,\psi^{\prime}_{2}(0)=1.
Set

a_{0}:=\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}_{1}\rangle,\quad b_{0}:=\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}_{2}\rangle.

For every function
\psi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
which vanishes outside
I_{0},

the function
\phi(t):=\psi(t)-\psi(0)\psi_{1}(t)-\psi^{\prime}(0)\psi_{2}(t)
satisfies the assumptions of Claim
5.4
.
It follows that
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=0
and so

(5.9)

\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}\rangle=\psi(0)\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}_{1}\rangle+\psi^{\prime}(0)\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}_{2}\rangle=a_{0}\psi(0)+b_{0}\psi^{\prime}(0).

It remains to prove that
b_{0}=0.

Now, fix a small positive number
\epsilon
and set
\psi(t):=\psi_{2}(t/\epsilon)
. Then
\psi(0)=0,\psi^{\prime}(0)=1/\epsilon
and
\hat{\psi}(x)=\epsilon\hat{\psi}_{2}(\epsilon x).
By (
5.9
),

|b_{0}|/\epsilon=|\langle\mu,\hat{\psi}\rangle|=\left|\epsilon\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\hat{\psi}_{2}(\epsilon\gamma^{\ast})\right|\leq\|c_{\gamma^{\ast}}\|_{\infty}\epsilon\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}|\hat{\psi}_{2}(\epsilon\gamma^{\ast})|.

Claim 5.6
.

There is a constant
C=C(\psi_{2})
such that

\epsilon\sum_{\gamma^{\ast}\in\Gamma^{\ast}}|\hat{\psi}_{2}(\epsilon\gamma^{\ast})|\leq C,\quad 0<\epsilon<1.

This can be easily deduced from the assumptions that
\Gamma^{\ast}
is separated and
\hat{\psi}_{2}\in S({\mathbb{R}})
.

Finally, we see that
|b_{0}|\leq C\epsilon,
for some positive constant
C.

Since
\epsilon
can be chosen arbitrarily small, we conclude that
b_{0}=0.
The claim follows from (
5.9
).
∎

We now complete the proof of Step 7.
Similarly to (
5.8
), we have
\langle\mu,\hat{\phi}\rangle=a_{k}\phi(t_{k})
, for every point
t_{k}\in{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}})
and every Schwartz function
\phi
which vanishes outside a small interval around
t_{k}
which does not intersect
{\rm Zer}(\hat{\mathcal{G}}).

Choose any function
\phi\in S({\mathbb{R}})
with compact support. For every

, choose any numbers

such that
[t_{s}-\sigma_{s},t_{s}+\sigma_{s}]\,\cap\,{\rm Zer}\,(\hat{\mathcal{G}})=\emptyset
. Choose also a function
\phi_{s}\in S({\mathbb{R}})
satisfying
\phi=\phi_{s}
on

and
\phi_{s}=0
outside

Since

This means that
\hat{\mu}
is an atomic measure concentrated on the set

.

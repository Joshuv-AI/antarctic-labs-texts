Observational constraints on inflationary models with non-minimally derivative coupling by ACT
and Zhu Yi

Abstract
The most recent data release from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) reveals a larger value of the scalar spectral tilt n s n_{s} , ruling out a broad class of inflationary attractors. In this paper, we consider inflationary models including the power law potential, the hilltop model, the polynomial α \alpha -attractor and exponential α − \alpha- attractor, with non-minimally derivative coupling in the high friction limit, and show how the models can fit ACT data. We also derive constraints on the model parameters using the latest ACT data.

A widely studied class of inflationary models with nonminimal coupling involves a scalar field coupled to the Ricci scalar via a term of the form
f(\phi)R
,
which is a specific case of the more general scalar-tensor theory
F(\phi,R)

[
41
,
42
,
43
]
.
This type of coupling can be removed through a conformal transformation, reducing the theory to GR with a redefined scalar sector.
However, if the kinetic term of the scalar field is non-minimally coupled to the curvature tensor,
a conformal transformation cannot recast the model into a scalar-tensor theory
[
44
]
.
Generally, non-minimally derivative couplings introduce higher than second-order derivatives in the field equations and extra degrees of freedom,
potentially leading to the Boulware-Deser ghost
[
45

A particular choice, where the derivative coupling takes the form
G^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}
which is a special case of the most general Horndeski theory
[
46

, avoids this issue because the resulting field equations in Horndeski theory contain no higher than second derivatives
[
46
,
47
]
.
Moreover, the gravitationally enhanced friction associated with this coupling slows down the evolution of the scalar field
[
48
,
49
,
50
]
.
In this paper, we show that various inflationary models with such non-minimally derivative coupling are well consistent with the latest ACT data.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

we introduce the non-minimally derivative coupling model and present the expressions for the slow-roll parameters under the high friction condition.
We then review the scalar and tensor perturbations within this context.
In Section
3

n_{s}
and the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
for various inflationary models featuring non-minimal derivative coupling.
A detailed comparison between numerical results and theoretical predictions for both the non-minimally derivative coupling scenario and the standard GR case is provided.
Additionally, we use the latest ACT data release to constrain the model parameters. Finally, our conclusions are summarized in Section
4
.

2
The model with non-minimally derivative coupling

The action for the scalar field with the kinetic term non-minimally coupled to Einstein tensor is

S=\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi{\partial_{\nu}}\phi+\frac{1}{{{M^{2}}}}{G^{\mu\nu}}{\partial_{\mu}}\phi{\partial_{\nu}}\phi-2V(\phi)\right],

(2.1)

where
M
is the coupling constant with the dimension of mass and we take the Planck mass
M_{pl}^{2}=(8\pi G)^{-1}=1
.
From the action (
2.1
), we get the Friedmann equation and the
equation of motion for the scalar field
\phi

H^{2}=\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{3}\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2}\left(1+9\frac{H^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)+V(\phi)\right],

(2.2)

\frac{d}{{dt}}\left[{{a^{3}}\dot{\phi}\left({1+3\frac{{{H^{2}}}}{{{M^{2}}}}}\right)}\right]=-{a^{3}}\frac{{dV}}{{d\phi}}.

(2.3)

We see that the non-minimally derivative coupling
G^{\mu\nu}\phi_{,\mu}\phi_{,\nu}/M^{2}
which becomes
H^{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}/M^{2}
in Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
)
not only enhances the expansion rate, but also the friction of the motion of scalar field resulting slower roll of the inflaton. In the limit
M\rightarrow\infty
, the effect of the non-minimally derivative coupling is negligible,
Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) reduce to the Friedmann equations in GR.
In this paper, we take the high friction limit
F=H^{2}/M^{2}\gg 1
to take advantage of the gravitationally enhanced friction introduced by the non-minimally derivative coupling.

Under the slow-roll conditions,

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{9H^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll V(\phi),\\
|\ddot{\phi}|\ll|3H\dot{\phi}|,\\
\left|\frac{2\dot{H}}{M^{2}+3H^{2}}\right|\ll 1,\end{split}

(2.4)

Eqs. (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are approximated as

\displaystyle H^{2}\approx\frac{V(\phi)}{3},

(2.5)

\displaystyle 3H\dot{\phi}\left(1+\frac{3H^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\approx-V_{\phi},

(2.6)

where
V_{\phi}=dV/d\phi
. To quantify those slow-roll conditions (
2.4
) in the high friction limit
H^{2}\gg M^{2}
,
we introduce the following slow-roll parameters

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{1}{6}\left(\frac{V_{\phi}}{V}\right)^{2}\frac{1+9F}{(1+3F)^{2}}\approx\frac{M^{2}}{2V}\left(\frac{V_{\phi}}{V}\right)^{2},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{1}{1+3F}\frac{V_{\phi\phi}}{V}\approx\frac{M^{2}}{V}\frac{V_{\phi\phi}}{V},

(2.8)

where the approximation is valid in the high friction limit
F\gg 1
, and Eq. (
2.5
) was used to derive the approximation.
The scalar and tensor power spectra are
[
50
]

P_{\zeta}\approx\frac{1}{2\,\epsilon}\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{c_{s}k}{aH}\right)^{3-2\nu},

(2.9)

P_{T}\approx 8\left(\frac{H}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{c_{t}k}{aH}\right)^{3-2\mu},

(2.10)

where
c_{s}
and
c_{t}
are the effective sound speed,
\nu\approx 3/2+4\epsilon-\eta
and
\mu=3/2+\epsilon
.
The amplitude of scalar power spectrum, the scalar spectral tilt and the tensor-to-scalar ratio are

A_{s}\approx\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{H^{2}}{\epsilon}\approx\frac{1}{24\pi^{2}}\frac{V(\phi)}{\epsilon},

(2.11)

n_{s}=1+\left.\frac{d\ln P_{\zeta}}{d\ln k}\right|_{c_{s}k=aH}\approx 1-8\epsilon+2\eta,

(2.12)

r=\frac{P_{T}}{P_{\zeta}}\approx 16\epsilon.

(2.13)

When
H^{2}\ll M^{2}
, we recover the results in GR.

The number of e-folds before the end of inflation is

N(\phi_{*})=\int_{\phi_{*}}^{\phi_{e}}\frac{H}{\dot{\phi}}d\phi=\int_{\phi_{*}}^{\phi_{e}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon}}\sqrt{\frac{3H^{2}}{M^{2}}}d\phi,

(2.14)

where the subscript “
e
” means the value of inflaton field at the end of inflation and the subscript “
*
” means the value at the horizon crossing.
The value of
N
is around
\sim 50-60
.

3
Observational constraints

In this section, we show how the models with non-minimally derivative coupling match the latest data release P-ACT-LB-BK18.
We consider the power law potential, the hilltop model, the polynomial model and E/T-models in the high friction limit.

3.1
Power law potential

For the chaotic inflation with the power law potential
V(\phi)=V_{0}\phi^{n}

[
51
]
, the slow-roll parameters are
[
50
]

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{n^{2}}{2V_{0}}\frac{M^{2}}{\phi^{n+2}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=\frac{n(n-1)}{V_{0}}\frac{M^{2}}{\phi^{n+2}}=\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\epsilon.\end{split}

(3.1)

Note that
M^{2}\ll V_{0}
in the high friction limit, without loss of generality, we take
M^{2}/V_{0}=10^{-4}
in this paper.
The end of inflation is determined by
{\rm max}\{\epsilon(\phi_{e}),|\eta(\phi_{e})|\}=1
.
For
0<n<2
, inflation ends when
\epsilon(\phi_{e})=1
, so we have

\phi_{e}=\left(\frac{n^{2}M^{2}}{2V_{0}}\right)^{1/(n+2)}.

(3.2)

The number of e-folds before the end of inflation is

N=\frac{V_{0}\phi_{*}^{n+2}}{n(n+2)M^{2}}-\frac{n}{2(n+2)}.

(3.3)

For
n\geq 2
, inflation ends when
\eta(\phi_{e})=1
, so

\phi_{e}=\left(\frac{n(n-1)M^{2}}{V_{0}}\right)^{1/(n+2)},

(3.4)

and the number of e-folds before the end of inflation is

N=\frac{V_{0}\phi_{*}^{n+2}}{n(n+2)M^{2}}-\frac{n-1}{n+2}.

(3.5)

The value of the scalar field at the horizon exit is

\phi_{*}=\left(\frac{n(n+2)M^{2}\tilde{N}}{V_{0}}\right)^{1/(n+2)},

(3.6)

where
\tilde{N}=N+n/2(n+2)
for
0<n<2
and
\tilde{N}=N+(n-1)/(n+2)
for
n\geq 2
.
Substituting Eqs. (
3.3
) and (
3.5
) into Eqs. (
2.12
) and (
2.13
),
we obtain the scalar spectral tilt and the tensor-to-scalar ratio,

n_{s}\approx 1-\frac{2(n+1)}{(n+2)\tilde{N}},

(3.7)

r\approx\frac{8n}{(n+2)\tilde{N}}.

(3.8)

For
n=1
and
N=50
, we get
n_{s}=0.973
and
r=0.053
;
for
n=2/3
and
N=50
, we get
n_{s}=0.975
and
r=0.04
;
for
n=1/3
and
N=50
, we get
n_{s}=0.977
and
r=0.023
.
For
n=1
and
N=60
, we get
n_{s}=0.978
and
r=0.044
;
for
n=2/3
and
N=60
, we get
n_{s}=0.979
and
r=0.033
; for
n=1/3
and
N=60
, we get
n_{s}=0.981
and
r=0.019
.
The results are shown in Fig.
1
. We also show the GR results
[
52
]
in Fig.
1
.
With the help of non-minimally derivative coupling,
n_{s}
becomes larger and
r
becomes smaller.
In particular, the linear potential with
n=1
for
N=60
is now marginally consistent with the observation at the
2\sigma
confidence level.
Only the case with
n=1/3
and
N=50
is consistent with the
1\sigma
contour for both non-minimally derivative coupling scenario and GR.

3.2
Hilltop model

For the hilltop model with the potential
[
53
]

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left(1-\frac{\phi^{p}}{\mu^{p}}\right),

(3.9)

the slow-roll parameters in the high friction limit are
[
50
]

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{p^{2}M^{2}(\phi/\mu)^{2p-2}}{2V_{0}\mu^{2}\left[1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}\right]^{3}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=-\frac{p(p-1)M^{2}(\phi/\mu)^{p-2}}{V_{0}\mu^{2}\left[1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}\right]^{2}}=-\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\frac{1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}}{(\phi/\mu)^{p}}\epsilon(\phi).\end{split}

Since
\phi<\mu
, so
\epsilon(\phi)

In Fig.
1

As seen from Fig.
1
, the non-minimally derivative coupling increases

For

From Fig.

The attractor behavior can be understood through the following analysis.
In the limit of
\mu\gg 1
, we get
\phi_{e}\sim\mu
and
\phi_{*}\sim\mu
.
Taylor expanding the potential (
3.9
) to the first order, we get the linear potential
V(\phi)\sim\phi
.
Therefore, the attractors are given by Eqs. (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) with
n=1
which is independent of the parameter
p
.
Specifically,
n_{s}\approx 0.973
and
r\approx 0.053
for
N=50
, and
n_{s}\approx 0.978
and
r\approx 0.044
for
N=60
.

As discussed above,
\epsilon(\phi)
is smaller than
\eta(\phi)
by a factor
(\phi/\mu)^{p}
when
p\geq 3
, so inflation ends when
|\eta(\phi_{e})|=1
.
In the limit of
\mu\ll 1
, we get small field inflation,
\phi\ll\mu
,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\phi_{e}}{\mu}\right)^{p-2}\approx\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{p(p-1)M^{2}}.

(3.11)

The number of e-folds before the end of inflation when the pivotal scale
k_{*}
crosses the horizon is
N=f(\phi_{e}/\mu)-f(\phi_{*}/\mu)
, where

f(x)=\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{pM^{2}}\left(\frac{x^{-p}}{2-p}+\frac{x^{p}}{p+2}-1\right)x^{2},

(3.12)

for
p\neq 2
. In the limit of
\phi\ll\mu
, we get

N\approx\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{p(p-2)M^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{\phi_{*}}\right)^{p-2}-\frac{p-1}{p-2}.

(3.13)

Therefore, the scalar spectral index is

n_{s}\approx 1-\frac{2(p-1)}{(p-2)[N+(p-1)/(p-2)]},

(3.14)

and the tensor-to-scalar ratio is

r\approx\frac{8px_{*}^{p}}{(p-2)[N+(p-1)/(p-2)]}\ll 1,

(3.15)

where
x_{*}=\phi_{*}/\mu
.
For
N=50
and
p=4
, we get
n_{s}\approx 0.942
and
r\approx 1.83\times 10^{-10}
.
For
N=60
and
p=4
, we get
n_{s}\approx 0.951
and
r\approx 1.07\times 10^{-10}
.
These analytical estimations agree with the numerical results.

In GR, the slow-roll parameters are

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{p^{2}(\phi/\mu)^{2p-2}}{2\mu^{2}\left[1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}\right]^{2}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=-\frac{p(p-1)(\phi/\mu)^{p-2}}{\mu^{2}\left[1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}\right]}=-\frac{2(p-1)}{p}\frac{1-(\phi/\mu)^{p}}{(\phi/\mu)^{p}}\epsilon(\phi).\end{split}

(3.16)

When
p\geq 3
, the end of inflation is determined by
|\eta(\phi_{e})|=1
.
The number of e-folds before the end of inflation is
N=g(\phi_{e}/\mu)-g(\phi_{*}/\mu)
, where

g(x)=\frac{\mu^{2}}{p}\left(\frac{x^{2-p}}{2-p}-\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\right),

(3.17)

for
p\neq 2
. Again in the limit of
\mu\gg 1
,
\phi\sim\mu
and the potential behaves as the linear potential
V\sim\phi
,
i.e.,
n_{s}\approx 0.97
and
r\approx 0.078
for
N=50
;
n_{s}\approx 0.975
and
r\approx 0.065
for
N=60
.
When
\mu\ll 1
, the attractors are the same as those in non-minimally derivative coupling case given by Eqs. (
3.14
) and (
3.15
).

3.3
Polynomial
\alpha
-attractors

For the polynomial
\alpha
-attractors, the potential is

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left(1-\frac{\mu^{p}}{\phi^{p}}\right),

(3.18)

and the slow-roll parameters in the high friction limit are

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{p^{2}M^{2}(\mu/\phi)^{2p+2}}{2V_{0}\mu^{2}\left[1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}\right]^{3}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=-\frac{p(p+1)M^{2}(\mu/\phi)^{p+2}}{V_{0}\mu^{2}\left[1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}\right]^{2}}=-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\frac{1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}}{(\mu/\phi)^{p}}\epsilon(\phi).\end{split}

(3.19)

Without loss of generality, we take
p=3
.
By using Eqs. (
2.12
), (
2.13
) and (
2.14
),
we numerically calculate the observables
n_{s}
and
r
with
\mu
varying, and the results are shown in Fig.
1
with solid magenta lines.
With the help of non-minimally derivative coupling, the polynomial
\alpha
-attractors are consistent with the P-ACT-LB-BK18 data for
both
N=50
and
N=60
.
For
N=50
, the
1\sigma
and
2\sigma
constraints on
\mu
by the P-ACT-LB-BK18 data are

and

, respectively;

, the

and at the

can be arbitrarily large.

In the limit of
\mu\gg 1
, the same attractors (

) and (
3.8
) with

\phi\sim\mu
.
On the other hand, when
\mu\ll 1
, we have small field inflation.
From
|\eta(\phi_{e})|=1
, we get

\left(\frac{\mu}{\phi_{e}}\right)^{p+2}\approx\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{p(p+1)M^{2}},

(3.20)

and
N=h(\mu/\phi_{e})-h(\mu/\phi_{*})

h(x)=\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{pM^{2}}\left(\frac{x^{-p}}{2+p}+\frac{x^{p}}{2-p}-1\right)x^{-2},

for
p\neq 2
. Combining Eqs. (

) and (

), we get

N\approx\frac{V_{0}\mu^{2}}{p(p+2)M^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu}{\phi_{*}}\right)^{-p-2}-\frac{p+1}{p+2},

(3.22)

so

n_{s}\approx 1-\frac{2(p+1)}{(p+2)[N+(p+1)/(p+2)]},

(3.23)

and

r\approx\frac{8px_{*}^{p}}{(p+2)[N+(p+1)/(p+2)]}\ll 1.

(3.24)

For
p=3
, the attractors are
n_{s}\approx 0.969
and

for
N=50
,
and
n_{s}\approx 0.974
and
r\approx 3.32\times 10^{-7}
for
N=60
, these analytical results (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) are consistent with the numerical results.

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{p^{2}(\mu/\phi)^{2p+2}}{2\mu^{2}\left[1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}\right]^{2}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=-\frac{p(p+1)(\mu/\phi)^{p+2}}{\mu^{2}\left[1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}\right]}=-\frac{2(p+1)}{p}\frac{1-(\mu/\phi)^{p}}{(\mu/\phi)^{p}}\epsilon(\phi).\end{split}

The number of e-folds before the end of inflation is

(3.26)

The results for

and

with

The same attractors (

3.4
Exponential
\alpha
-attractors

The E model and T model share the same asymptotic form of the potential in the large field limit
[
6
,
9
]
,

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left[1-a\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi\right)\right].

(3.27)

In this section, without loss of generality we consider
a=1
for simplicity. The slow-roll parameters are

\begin{split}\epsilon(\phi)&=\frac{M^{2}\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi\right)}{3\alpha V_{0}\left[\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi\right)-1\right]^{3}},\\
\eta(\phi)&=-\frac{2M^{2}\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi\right)}{3\alpha V_{0}\left[\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi\right)-1\right]^{2}}.\end{split}

(3.28)

The number of e-folds before the end of inflation is

N=m\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi_{e}\right)-m\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3\alpha}}\phi_{*}\right),

(3.29)

where

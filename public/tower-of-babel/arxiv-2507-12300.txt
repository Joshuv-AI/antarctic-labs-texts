Sturm–Liouville operators with periodically modulated parameters. Part I: Regular case
Grzegorz Świderski, Bartosz Trojan

Abstract
. We introduce a new class of Sturm–Liouville operators with periodically modulated parameters. Their spectral properties depend on the monodromy matrix of the underlying periodic problem computed for the spectral parameter equal to 0 0 . Under certain assumptions, by studying the asymptotic behavior of Christoffel functions and density of states, we prove that the spectral density is a continuous positive everywhere function on the real line.

Introduction

A triple
(p,q,w)
of functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called
Sturm–Liouville parameters
if both
p
and
w
are positive almost
everywhere,
q
is real-valued, and
1/p,q,w\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
. Given Sturm–Liouville parameters one can consider
the differential expression

(1.1)

\tau u=\frac{1}{w}\big(-(pu^{\prime})^{\prime}+qu\big)

whose domain is

\operatorname{Dom}(\tau)=\big\{u\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty)):pu^{\prime}\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))\big\},

whereas
\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
denotes the space of locally absolutely continuous functions on
[0,\infty)
. Then the right-hand
side of (
1.1
) exists almost everywhere. Let
\mathbb{S}^{1}
be the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For any
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
one can define the operator
H_{\eta}
by setting

H_{\eta}u=\tau u.

Its domain is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}(H_{\eta})=\big\{u\in\operatorname{Dom}(\tau):u,\tau u\in L^{2}([0,\infty),w),\eta_{2}u(0)-\eta_{1}pu^{\prime}(0)=0\big\}.

The operator
H_{\eta}
is called
Sturm–Liouville operator
. The aim of this paper is to study spectral properties of
Sturm–Liouville operators under certain hypotheses on their parameters.

The topic of Sturm–Liouville operators is a classical one (see, e.g.
[
14
]
for historical development in the early
20th century). It has been thoroughly studied in numerous textbooks (see, e.g.
[
24
,

4

17
,

]
), which contain an extensive number of further references. Let us recall that taking

and
w\equiv 1
, the operator
H_{\eta}
is
1-dimensional Schrödinger operator
(on the half-line), which spectral

[

,

]
). We refer to
[
13
]
for a list of
important Sturm–Liouville parameters and some properties of the corresponding operators.

Spectral properties of
H_{\eta}
are intimately related to analysis of solutions of eigenvalue equation
\tau u=zu
for

z\in\mathbb{C}
. In fact, in view of
Weyl’s alternative
, exactly one of the cases holds:

•

For every
z\in\mathbb{C}
each solution to
\tau u=zu
belongs to
L^{2}([0,\infty),w)
(
limit circle case
).

•

There are
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
u\notin L^{2}([0,\infty),w)
such that
\tau u=zu

(
limit point case
).

Let us recall that the limit point case implies that, actually, for each
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the subset of

L^{2}([0,\infty),w)
consisting of solutions to
\tau u=zu
is one-dimensional. Therefore, the Weyl’s theorem asserts that

H_{\eta}
is self-adjoint if and only if
\tau
is in the limit point case.
If this is the case, then there exists a positive Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
,
\mu_{\eta}
, satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{1+\lambda^{2}}{\rm d}\mu_{\eta}(\lambda)<\infty
and a unitary operator
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}:L^{2}([0,\infty),w)\to L^{2}(\mu_{\eta})
such that
\mathcal{F}_{\eta}H_{\eta}\mathcal{F}_{\eta}^{-1}=M
where

(Mf)(\lambda)=\lambda f(\lambda),\quad f\in\operatorname{Dom}(M)

whereas
\operatorname{Dom}(M)=\{f\in L^{2}(\mu_{\eta}):\lambda\mapsto\lambda f(\lambda)\in L^{2}(\mu_{\eta})\}
(see, e.g.
[
4
, Theorem 4.3.7]
). Therefore, all
spectral information on
H_{\eta}
is carried by the measure
\mu_{\eta}
. Recall that the measure
\mu_{\eta}
comes from the Herglotz representation of Weyl–Titchmarsh
m

L^{2}([0,\infty),w)
solutions of equation
\tau u=zu
for
\Im z>0
. Since the measure
\mu_{\eta}
is canonical, it is of interest to study its properties in detail.

A well-studied example of Sturm–Liouville operators is the class with periodic coefficients (see, e.g.

In particular,

is in the limit point case. Then the so-called

It turns out that

In this article, we are particularly interested in a seemingly new class of Sturm–Liouville parameters. To be more precise,
we say that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters
if
(p,q,w)
are
Sturm–Liouville parameters satisfying: There are
\omega
-periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}{\rm d}t=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{q_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0,\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{p_{n}^{\prime}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0,

where

p_{n}(t)=p(t+n\omega),\quad q_{n}(t)=q(t+n\omega),\quad w_{n}(t)=w(t+n\omega),\quad n\geq 0,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We additionally assume that

(1.4)

\lim_{x\to\infty}p(x)=+\infty.

This class is inspired by a class of unbounded Jacobi matrices introduced in
[
20

Let us recall that the Liouville transformation (see, e.g.
[
13

H

on

with a real-valued potential

. Our interest in
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters
is partially motivated by the fact that these parameters sometimes lead to potentials being rapidly oscillating with unbounded
amplitudes. In particular, Example
3

(1.5)

V(x)=(1+x)^{a}\mathfrak{q}\big((1+x)^{(2+a)/2}-1\big),\quad x\geq 0,

for any

(1.6)

V(x)=\textrm{e}^{2x}\mathfrak{q}(\textrm{e}^{x}-1),\quad x\geq 0.

The function
\mathfrak{q}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
which appears in (
1.5

) is assumed to be real-valued
and
\omega
-periodic. Recently in
[

(1.7)

V(x)=x^{a}\sin(x^{b}),\quad x\geq 0.

They proved that if

is positive, then
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H)=[0,\infty)
. Observe that for
\mathfrak{q}(t)=\sin(t)

the potential (
1.5

such that for all

\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(H)=\mathbb{R}
. Moreover,

, then all self-adjoint extensions of

have empty essential spectrum.
In other words, we observe spectral phase transition (cf.
[
20
]
for a similar phenomenon in the discrete setting).
Consequently, the spectral behavior of

for

may be of particular interest. We note that potentials of the form
(

[

In this paper, we show that spectral properties of
H_{\eta}
for
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters
depend on the value of the monodromy matrix
\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)
for

. In fact we have the following cases:

(I)

|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|<2
;

(II)

If
|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|=2
, then we have two subcases:

(a)

\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)
is diagonalizable;

(b)

\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)
is
not
diagonalizable;

(III)

|\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|>2
.

It turns out that we can describe them in terms of the intervals
I_{k}
(cf. (
1.2
)). More precisely, we are in
Case
I
if
0\in\Lambda_{-}
; in Case

if
0
lies at the boundary of exactly two intervals;
in Case
IIII(b)
if
0
lies at the boundary of exactly one interval, and in Case
III
if

0\notin\operatorname{cl}(\Lambda_{-})
. In this article we shall consider regular cases: Case
I
and Case
III
. The critical
cases: Case
IIII(a)
and Case
IIII(b)
, are more involved and will be studied in the sequel.

A standard way of spectral analysis of Sturm–Liouville operators is the
method of subordinacy
initially introduced
by Gilbert and Pearson in
[
18
]
for one dimensional Schrödinger operators. Later, this method has been extended to the
Sturm–Liouville setting by Clark and Hinton in
[
7
]
. Let us summarize the method of subordinacy: Suppose that
\tau

is in the limit-point case. Let us denote by
K_{L}
the
Christoffel–Darboux kernels
that is (see, e.g.
[
23
]
)

K_{L}(z_{1},z_{2};\eta)=\int_{0}^{L}\mathsf{s}_{\eta}(t;z_{1})\overline{\mathsf{s}_{\eta}(t;z_{2})}w(t){\>\rm d}t,\quad L>0,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C},

where
\mathsf{s}_{\eta}(\cdot;z)
is the solution of the equation
\tau u=zu
satisfying
(u(0),pu^{\prime}(0))^{t}=\eta
.
By
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
, if for some compact interval with non-empty interior
K\subset\mathbb{R}
,

(1.8)

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in K}\sup_{\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)}{K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta^{\prime})}<\infty,

then
\mu_{\eta}
is absolutely continuous on
\operatorname{int}(K)
, and there are two positive constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
, such that the
density of
\mu_{\eta}
satisfies

(1.9)

c_{1}<\mu_{\eta}^{\prime}(\lambda)<c_{2}

for almost all
\lambda\in K
. In particular, for any
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, the operator
H_{\eta}
is absolutely continuous
on
\operatorname{int}(K)
and
K\subset\sigma_{\mathrm{ac}}(H_{\eta})
.

In our study we impose on Sturm–Liouville parameters the following regularity conditions: We say that a function
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}

belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};\mathbb{R})
, if

\sup_{n\geq 0}\int_{0}^{\omega}|f(n\omega+s)|{\rm d}s<\infty,

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big|f\big((n+1)\omega+s\big)-f(n\omega+s)\big|{\rm d}s<\infty.

These class has been introduced by Stolz in
[
30
]
. We assume that

(1.10)

\frac{q}{p},\frac{w}{p},\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};\mathbb{R}).

Additionally, by analogy with Jacobi matrices, the
Carleman condition
is defined as

(1.11)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}{\rm d}t=\infty.

We set

(1.12)

\varrho_{n}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{w_{j}(t)}{p_{j}(t)},\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{L}=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{w(t^{\prime})}{p(t^{\prime})}{\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Theorem A
.

Let
\omega>0

(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters, such that

\mathfrak{T}
corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})

\lvert{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}\rvert<2

Assume that (
1.10

\tau

1.11

Suppose that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varrho_{n}(t)=\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{w_{n+1}(t)}{w_{n}(t)}=1\quad\text{for almost all }t\in[0,\omega].

If there is
s\in[0,\omega]
such that

(1.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\frac{w_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0,

where

\gamma_{n}(t)=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t+t^{\prime})}{p_{n}(t+t^{\prime})}{\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(t^{\prime})}{\mathfrak{p}(t^{\prime})}{\rm d}t^{\prime},

and (
1.11

such that

(1.14)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)=g(\lambda;\eta)

(\lambda,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

\mu_{\eta}
is
absolutely continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, and its density

(1.15)

\mu_{\eta}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{|\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|}{\gamma\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}}\frac{1}{g(\lambda;\eta)},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

The proof of Theorem

consists of several steps. The main one is to determine the asymptotic behavior of

\mathsf{s}_{\eta}(\cdot;z)
for

(1.16)

\mathbf{u}(t;z)=T(t;z)\mathbf{u}(0;z)\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{u}(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t;z)\\
\partial_{t}u(t;z)\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in[0,\infty),z\in\mathbb{C},

and whereas the function

is the unique solution to

T(t;z)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t^{\prime};z)T(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad\text{where}\quad b(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\frac{q(t)-zw(t)}{p(t)}&-\frac{p^{\prime}(t)}{p(t)}\end{pmatrix}.

Let us define

\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}=\big\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\eta_{1}|^{2}+|p(0)\eta_{2}|^{2}=1\big\}.

\nu

,

2.
Sturm–Liouville equations

In order to understand spectral properties of
H_{\eta}
we need to study the eigenvalue problem

(2.1)

\tau u=zu

where
\tau
is defined in (
1.1
) and the equality is almost everywhere. In this paper we assume that Sturm–Liouville
parameters
(p,q,w)
are such that
p\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
is positive everywhere.
In view of
[
4
, Theorem D.2]
, the solution to (
2.1
) has absolutely continuous derivative. Therefore, setting

\mathbf{u}(t)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t)\\
\partial_{t}u(t)\end{pmatrix}

for given
z\in\mathbb{C}
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the equation (
2.1
) can be written in the following form

(2.2)

\mathbf{u}(t)=\mathbf{u}(t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}b(t^{\prime};z)\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime}){\>\rm d}t^{\prime},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

where

(2.3)

b(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\frac{q(t)-zw(t)}{p(t)}&-\frac{p^{\prime}(t)}{p(t)}\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},z\in\mathbb{C}.

By
[
4
, Theorem D.2]
, there is the unique solution to (
2.2
), and

\mathbf{u}(\cdot;z)\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
. Moreover, by
[
4
, Theorem D.5]
,
the mapping
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto\mathbf{u}(\cdot;z)
is entire with values in
\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
.
Let us observe that
u(t;z)=\langle{\mathbf{u}(t;z)},{e_{1}}\rangle
satisfies (
2.1
). Here
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
denotes
the standard base in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, that is

e_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix},\quad e_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

For any solutions
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}
of (
2.2
) we define their
Wronskian
by the formula

(2.4)

\operatorname{Wr}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})(t;z)=p(t)\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t;z),\mathbf{v}(t;z)\big),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},z\in\mathbb{C}.

By (
2.2
), Liouville’s formula (see e.g.
[
42
, formula (1.2.15)]
) and (
2.3
) we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t;z),\mathbf{v}(t;z)\big)

\displaystyle=\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t_{0};z),\mathbf{v}(t_{0};z)\big)\cdot\exp\bigg(\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\operatorname{tr}b(s;z){\rm d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle=\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t_{0};z),\mathbf{v}(t_{0};z)\big)\cdot\exp\bigg(-\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\frac{p^{\prime}(s)}{p(s)}{\rm d}s\bigg)

\displaystyle=\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t_{0};z),\mathbf{v}(t_{0};z)\big)\cdot\frac{p(t_{0})}{p(t)}.

Thus,

(2.6)

\operatorname{Wr}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})(t;z)=p(t_{0})\det\big(\mathbf{u}(t_{0};z),\mathbf{v}(t_{0};z)\big).

Taking
t_{0}=0
, for
z\in\mathbb{C}
, by
\mathscr{S}(\cdot;z)
and
\mathscr{C}(\cdot;z)
we denote two solutions of
(
2.1
), with boundary conditions

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\mathscr{C}(0;z)=1,\\
\partial_{x}\mathscr{C}(0;z)=0,\end{cases}\qquad\begin{cases}\mathscr{S}(0;z)=0,\\
\partial_{x}\mathscr{S}(0;z)=1.\end{cases}

Then
\mathscr{C}
and
\mathscr{S}
are jointly continuous on
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{C}
and entire with respect to
z
.
We define the
transfer matrix
as

(2.8)

T(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{C}(t;z)&\mathscr{S}(t;z)\\
\partial_{x}\mathscr{C}(t;z)&\partial_{x}\mathscr{S}(t;z)\end{pmatrix}.

Then it satisfies the matrix equation

(2.9)

T(t;z)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t^{\prime};z)T(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

If
\mathbf{u}(\cdot;z)
satisfies (
2.2
) then for each
t,s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(2.10)

\mathbf{u}(t;z)=T(t;z)T(s;z)^{-1}\mathbf{u}(s;z).

By (
2.5
), we get

(2.11)

\frac{\det T(t;z)}{\det T(s;z)}=\frac{p(s)}{p(t)},\qquad\text{for all }s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

A list of explicit examples of Sturm–Liouville equations can be found in
[
42
, Chapter 14]
and
[
13
]
.

2.1.
Classes of Sturm–Liouville parameters

Since the periodic case is well-understand, our aim is to study a class which is its certain perturbation. For
\omega>0
, we
say that
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
are
\omega
-periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters
if
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})

are Sturm–Liouville parameters satisfying

\mathfrak{p}(t+\omega)=\mathfrak{p}(t),\quad\mathfrak{q}(t+\omega)=\mathfrak{q}(t),\quad\mathfrak{w}(t+\omega)=\mathfrak{w}(t),\quad\text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

We assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
is positive everywhere.
Let

(2.12)

\mathfrak{b}(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
\frac{\mathfrak{q}(t)-z\mathfrak{w}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}&-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},z\in\mathbb{C}.

By
\mathfrak{T}(\cdot;z)
we denote the corresponding transfer matrix, that is

(2.13)

\mathfrak{T}(t;z)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};z)\mathfrak{T}(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
If
\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\in(-2,2)
then
\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\neq 0
.

Proof.

By (
2.13
), we have

\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(t;z)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}(s;z)\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(s;z)+\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z){\>\rm d}s.

Hence, by variation of parameters, we get

\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(t;z)=\mathfrak{T}(t;z)\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\partial_{z}b(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z){\>\rm d}s.

Suppose, contrary to our claim, that

(2.14)

\operatorname{tr}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)=0.

Setting

\mathfrak{T}(s;z)=\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}(s;z)&a_{12}(s;z)\\
a_{21}(s;z)&a_{22}(s;z)\end{pmatrix},

we can write

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)=-\int_{0}^{\omega}\big(

\displaystyle a_{11}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)a_{12}(s;z)-a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}

\displaystyle+a_{21}(\omega;z)a_{12}(s;z)^{2}-a_{22}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)a_{12}(s;z)\big)\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s)}{\mathfrak{p}(s)}{\>\rm d}s.

Since
\mathfrak{T}(\cdot;z)
is continuous, by (
2.14
) we conclude that there is
s\in[0,\omega]
such that

\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)-a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)a_{11}(s;z)a_{12}(s;z)=a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}-a_{21}(\omega;z)a_{12}(s;z)^{2}.

Thus

\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)-a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)^{2}a_{11}(s;z)^{2}a_{12}(s;z)^{2}=\big(a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}-a_{21}(\omega;z)a_{12}(s;z)^{2}\big)^{2}.

Since
\det\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)=1
, we have

\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)-a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)^{2}=\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)+a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)^{2}-4-4a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{21}(\omega;z),

therefore

\big((a_{11}(\omega;z)+a_{22}(\omega;z))^{2}-4\big)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}a_{12}(s;z)^{2}\\
=\big(a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}+a_{21}(\omega;z)a_{12}(s;z)^{2}\big)^{2}.

Now, in view of
\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\in(-2,2)
, we must have

a_{11}(s;z)^{2}a_{12}(s;z)^{2}=0

and

a_{12}(\omega;z)a_{11}(s;z)^{2}+a_{21}(\omega;z)a_{12}(s;z)^{2}=0.

Because
\det\mathfrak{T}(s;z)=1
, if
a_{11}(s;z)=0
, then
a_{12}(s;z)\neq 0
, and so
a_{21}(\omega;z)=0
.
Consequently,

1=\det\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)=a_{11}(\omega;z)a_{22}(\omega;z),

which leads to contradiction

\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)-a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)^{2}=\big(a_{11}(\omega;z)+a_{22}(\omega;z)\big)^{2}-4<0.

Analogously, we treat the case
a_{12}(s;z)=0
.
∎

We say that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters
if
(p,q,w)
are
Sturm–Liouville parameters such that there are
\omega
-periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
,
with properties that
p,\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
are positive everywhere
and

(2.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w(n\omega+t)}{p(n\omega+t)}{\rm d}t=0,

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{q(n\omega+t)}{p(n\omega+t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}(n\omega+t)}{p(n\omega+t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0.

We additionally assume that for each
t\in[0,\omega]
,

(2.18)

\lim_{t\to\infty}p(t+n\omega)=+\infty.

For
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
(see (
1.17
)), we denote by
\mathbf{u}
the solution to

(2.19)

\mathbf{u}(t;z)=\eta+\int_{0}^{t}b(t^{\prime};z)\mathbf{u}(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad t\geq 0,

where
b(t;z)
is given by the formula (
2.3
). Then we set

(2.20)

\mathbf{u}_{n}(s,\eta;z)=\mathbf{u}(s+n\omega;z),\quad s\in[0,\omega],n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We notice that by (
2.10
) we have
\mathbf{u}_{0}(s,\eta;z)=T(s;z)\eta
and

(2.21)

\mathbf{u}_{n+1}(s,\eta;z)=X_{n}(s;z)\mathbf{u}_{n}(s,\eta;z)

where

(2.22)

X_{n}(s;z)=U_{s;n}(\omega;z)

and whereas

(2.23)

U_{s;n}(t;z)=T(s+t+n\omega;z)T(s+n\omega;z)^{-1}.

Let us observe that for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
U_{s;n}(\cdot;z)
satisfies

(2.24)

U(t)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}b_{s;n}(t;z)U(t^{\prime}){\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad t\geq 0

where

b_{s;n}(t;z)=b(s+t+n\omega;z).

We set

p_{s;n}(t)=p(s+t+n\omega),\quad q_{s;n}(t)=q(s+t+n\omega),\quad w_{s;n}(t)=w(s+t+n\omega),

and

u_{n}(s,\eta;z)=\langle{\mathbf{u}_{n}(s,\eta;z)},{e_{1}}\rangle.

If
s=0
we just write
b_{n}(t)=b(t+n\omega)
, and

p_{n}(t)=p(t+n\omega),\quad q_{n}(t)=q(t+n\omega),\quad w_{n}(t)=w(t+n\omega).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\omega>0
. Suppose that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters,
and let
\mathfrak{T}
be the transfer matrix corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
. Then

(2.25)

\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{s;n}(t;z)=\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)

locally uniformly with respect to
s,t\in[0,\omega]
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, where

\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;z)=\mathfrak{T}(s+t;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}.

Moreover, for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
,

\sup_{s,t\in[0,\omega]}\sup_{z\in K}\big\|U_{s;n+1}(s;z)-U_{s;n}(t;z)\big\|\leq C\int_{0}^{2\omega}\big\|b_{n+1}(t^{\prime})-b_{n}(t^{\prime})\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Proof.

Since for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\cdot;z)
satisfies

\mathfrak{U}(t)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};z)\mathfrak{U}(t^{\prime}){\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad t^{\prime}\geq 0,

by (
2.24
), the variation of parameters gives

(2.26)

U_{s;n}(t;z)=\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)+\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)^{-1}\big(b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\big)U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z){\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Since
\|\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)\|
is uniformly bounded from above as well as from below, we have

\big\|U_{s;n}(t;z)\big\|\leq C\bigg(1+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|\cdot\big\|U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}\bigg).

Thus, by the Gronwall’s inequality

\big\|U_{s;n}(t;z)\big\|\leq C\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}\bigg).

By (
2.15
)–(
2.17
),
U_{s;n}
is locally uniformly bounded. Hence, by (
2.26
),

\big\|U_{s;n}(t;z)-\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)\big\|\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big\|b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Moreover,

\big\|b_{s;n}(t;z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t;0)\big\|\leq\lvert{z}\rvert\bigg|\frac{w_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{q_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{q}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{q}(p+t)}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\bigg|,

thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{\omega+s}\big\|b_{n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\int_{0}^{\omega}+\int_{\omega}^{s+\omega}-\int_{0}^{s}\bigg)\big\|b_{n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{2\omega}\big\|b_{n}(t^{\prime};z)-\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};0)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Consequently, by (
2.15
)–(
2.17
), we obtain (
2.25
). Lastly, by (
2.26
)

\displaystyle\big\|U_{s;n+1}(t;z)-U_{s;n}(t;z)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big\|b_{s;n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|\cdot\big\|U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq C\bigg(}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|\cdot\big\|U_{s;n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\big\|b_{s;n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq C^{\prime}\bigg(}+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|U_{s;n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}\bigg).

In view of the Gronwall’s inequality,

\displaystyle\big\|U_{s;n+1}(t;z)-U_{s;n}(t;z)\big\|

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|b_{s;n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\int_{0}^{2\omega}\big\|b_{n+1}(t^{\prime};z)-b_{n}(t^{\prime};z)\big\|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

and the proposition follows.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\omega>0
. Suppose that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters,
and let
\mathfrak{T}
be the transfer matrix corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
. If
\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)\in(-2,2)
, then for any compact
K\subset\mathbb{C}
there are constants
c>0
and
M\geq 1
such that for any
z\in K
,
t\in[0,\omega]
and
n\geq M

(2.27)

|u_{n}(t,\eta;z)|^{2}\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{n}(t,\eta;z)\|^{2}\leq c\big(|u_{n}(t,\eta;z)|^{2}+|u_{n+1}(t,\eta;z)|^{2}\big).

Proof.

Let us fix
z\in K
. Since the sequence
(\mathbf{u}_{n}(t;z):n\geq 0)
satisfies (
2.21
), we have

u_{n+1}(t;z)=[X_{n}(t;z)]_{11}\cdot u_{n}(t;z)+[X_{n}(t;z)]_{12}\cdot\partial_{t}u_{n}(t;z).

In view of (
2.22
) and Proposition
2.2
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{discr}X_{n}(t;z)=\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)<0,

hence there are
M\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that for all

\lvert{[X_{n}(t;z)]_{12}}\rvert\geq\delta>0.

Consequently, there is
c>0
such that for all

which easily implies (
2.27
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let us observe that

\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;z)=\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}.

Indeed, we notice that given
z\in\mathbb{C}
, both
\mathfrak{T}(\cdot;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)
and
\mathfrak{T}(\cdot+\omega;z)
satisfies

\mathfrak{U}(s)=\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)+\int_{0}^{s}\mathfrak{b}(s^{\prime};z)\mathfrak{U}(s^{\prime}){\rm d}s^{\prime},\quad s\geq 0,

which implies that

\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)=\mathfrak{T}(s+\omega;z),\quad s\in[0,\omega],z\in\mathbb{C}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\omega>0
. Let
p,\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{AC}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
be positive everywhere.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}
is
\omega
-periodic and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}(s)}{p_{n}(s)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(s)}{\mathfrak{p}(s)}\bigg|{\>\rm d}s=0,

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}{\bigg|\frac{p_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(0)}\bigg|}=0.

Proof.

Since

\log p_{n}(t)-\log p_{n}(0)=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}(s)}{p_{n}(s)}{\>\rm d}s,

we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\log\bigg(\frac{p_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}\bigg)-\log\bigg(\frac{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(0)}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}(s)}{p_{n}(s)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(s)}{\mathfrak{p}(s)}\bigg|{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{p^{\prime}_{n}(s)}{p_{n}(s)}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}(s)}{\mathfrak{p}(s)}\bigg|{\>\rm d}s

and the lemma follows.

\gamma_{n}(s)=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}{\rm d}t,

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\omega>0
. Suppose that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters, and let

\mathfrak{T}
be the transfer matrix corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
. Suppose that there is
s\in[0,\omega]

such that

(2.28)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\frac{w_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0,

then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\partial_{z}X_{n}(s;z)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;0)

locally uniformly with respect to
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

Since
U_{s;n}
satisfies (
2.24
), we get

\partial_{z}U_{s;n}(t;z)=\int_{0}^{t}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)\partial_{z}U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)+\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime},\quad t\geq 0.

By variation of parameters, we obtain

\partial_{z}U_{s;n}(t;z)=U_{s;n}(t;z)\int_{0}^{t}U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)^{-1}\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z){\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Analogously, we have

(2.29)

\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)=\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t;0)\int_{0}^{t}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)^{-1}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t^{\prime};0){\>\rm d}t^{\prime}.

Since

\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)=-\frac{w_{n}(s+t^{\prime})}{p_{n}(s+t^{\prime})}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

and

\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)=-\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t^{\prime})}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t^{\prime})}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

by (
2.28
), we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};0)\bigg|{\>\rm d}t^{\prime}=0.

Moreover, by Proposition
2.2
and the dominated convergence theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}

\displaystyle U_{s;n}(\omega;z)\int_{0}^{\omega}U_{s;n}(t^{\prime},z)^{-1}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z){\>\rm d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;0)\int_{0}^{\omega}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)^{-1}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}_{s}(t^{\prime};0)\mathfrak{T}_{s}(t^{\prime};0){\>\rm d}t^{\prime},

therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\partial_{z}X_{n}(s;z)-\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;0)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{s;n}(\omega;z)\int_{0}^{\omega}U_{s;n}(t^{\prime},z)^{-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\partial_{z}b_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z)-\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{b}(t^{\prime};0)\bigg)U_{s;n}(t^{\prime};z){\>\rm d}t^{\prime}=0

which in view of (
2.29
) completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

For each
s\in[0,\omega]
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
,

\operatorname{tr}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;z)=\operatorname{tr}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z).

Proof.

By Remark
2.4
,

\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;z)=\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}_{s}(\omega;z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}+\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\partial\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}.

Since

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}\Big(\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\partial\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\Big(\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\partial\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\Big)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{tr}\Big(\partial\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\Big),

and

\operatorname{tr}\Big(\mathfrak{T}(s;z)\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z)\mathfrak{T}(s;z)^{-1}\Big)=\operatorname{tr}\partial_{z}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;z),

the corollary follows.

2.2.
The Stolz class

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a semi-normed space. A sequence of elements
(x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
of
X
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}(X)
if

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|x_{n}\|_{X}<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\|\Delta x_{n}\|_{X}<\infty

where

\Delta x_{n}=x_{n+1}-x_{n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

For example
\mathcal{D}_{1}(L^{1}(I,X)))
consists of sequences
(f_{n})\subset L^{1}(I;X)
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{I}\|f_{n}(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{I}\|\Delta f_{n}(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s.

Given
\omega>0
, by
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(X)
we denote the set of functions
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow X
, such that

(f(n\omega):n\in\mathbb{N})
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}(X)
. The set
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};X)
consists of functions

f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow X
such that the sequence
(f(n\omega+s):n\in\mathbb{N})
belongs to

\mathcal{D}_{1}\big(L^{1}_{s}([0,\omega];X)\big)
. Next, we say that
f
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}\big(L^{\infty}L^{1};X)\big)
if

(f(n\omega+t+s):n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}\big(L_{t}^{\infty}([0,\omega];L^{1}_{s}([0,\omega];X))\big)
, that is

\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|f(n\omega+t+s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s<\infty,\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|\Delta f(n\omega+t+s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s<\infty.

Finally, we say that a collection
(f_{t}:t\in\mathcal{T})
belongs to
\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};X)
uniformly with respect to

t\in\mathcal{T}
, if and only if

\sup_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|f_{t}(n\omega+s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s<\infty

and the series

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|\Delta f_{t}(n\omega+s)\big\|{\>\rm d}s

converges uniformly with respect to
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

Let us notice that for
f:[0,\infty)\rightarrow X
, we have

(2.30)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big\|\Delta f(n\omega+s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{n\omega}^{(n+1)\omega}\big\|\Delta_{\omega}f(s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\big\|\Delta_{\omega}f(s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

where

\Delta_{\omega}f(x)=f(x+\omega)-f(x).

Hence,
f\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};X)
if and only if
\Delta_{\omega}f\in L^{1}([0,\infty);X)
and

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\omega}\|f(n\omega+s)\big\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s<\infty.

For
f\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};X)
we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\int_{0}^{\omega}\|\Delta f(n\omega+s+t)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\int_{t+n\omega}^{t+(n+1)\omega}\|\Delta_{\omega}f(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{n\omega}^{(n+2)\omega}\|\Delta_{\omega}f(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\|\Delta_{\omega}f(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s,

and

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\omega}\|f(n\omega+t+s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{n\omega+t}^{(n+1)\omega+t}\|f(s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{0}^{\omega}\|f(n\omega+s)\|_{X}{\>\rm d}s.

Therefore,
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\omega}_{1}(L^{\infty}L^{1};X)
. In particular,
f_{t}
, where

f_{t}(s)=f(t+s),\quad s>0,

belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{\omega}_{1}(L^{1};X)
uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,\omega]
.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{\gamma}\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)

which easily completes the proof.
∎

By (

4.
Turán determinants

In this section we introduce generalized Turán determinants, and study their asymptotic behavior in the case of

\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters. To be precise, assume that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically
modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters. If
u
is a non-trivial solution of (
2.1
),
then
generalized
\omega
-shifted Turán determinants
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}(t;z)

\displaystyle=\det\begin{pmatrix}u(t+\omega;z)&u(t;z)\\
\partial_{t}u(t+\omega;z)&\partial_{t}u(t;z)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=u(t+\omega;z)\partial_{t}u(t;z)-u(t;z)\partial_{t}u(t+\omega;z).

Given
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
(cf. (
1.17
)) and
s\in[0,\omega]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we set

\mathscr{D}_{n}(s,\eta;z)=\mathscr{D}(s+n\omega;z),\quad z\in\mathbb{R}

where
u=\langle{\mathbf{u}},{e_{1}}\rangle
and
\mathbf{u}
satisfies (
2.19
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\omega>0
. Suppose that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters, such that
the transfer matrix
\mathfrak{T}
corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
satisfies
\lvert{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}\rvert<2
.
Assume that

\frac{q}{p},\frac{w}{p},\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};\mathbb{R}).

Then for each solution
\mathbf{u}
of (
2.19
) the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}p_{n}(t)|\mathscr{D}_{n}(t,\eta;z)|

exists locally uniformly with respect to
(t,\eta,z)\in[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
, and it is a positive
continuous function.

Proof.

We write

S_{n}=p_{n+1}\mathscr{D}_{n}=\langle{E\mathbf{u}_{n+1}},{\mathbf{u}_{n}}\rangle

where

E=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since for each
X\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
,

(4.1)

X^{t}E=(\det X)EX^{-1},

by (
2.21
), we get

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=p_{n+1}\langle{EX_{n+1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+2}},{X_{n}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=p_{n+1}\langle{(X_{n}^{-1})^{t}EX_{n+1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+2}},{\mathbf{u}_{n+1}}\rangle

\displaystyle=p_{n+1}\det X_{n}^{-1}\langle{EX_{n}X_{n+1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+2}},{\mathbf{u}_{n+1}}\rangle.

Recall that by (
2.11
)

(4.2)

\det X_{n}(t;z)=\frac{p_{n}(t)}{p_{n+1}(t)}.

Consequently, for each
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=p_{n+1}\big(\det X_{n}^{-1}\cdots\det X_{n+k}^{-1}\big)\bigg\langle E\bigg(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}X_{n+j}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg)^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+k},\mathbf{u}_{n+k-1}\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle=p_{n+1}\frac{p_{n+k+1}}{p_{n}}\bigg\langle E\bigg(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}X_{n+j}\bigg)\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg)^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+k},\mathbf{u}_{n+k-1}\bigg\rangle.

Therefore,

S_{n}-S_{n+k}=p_{n+k+1}\bigg\langle EY_{n,k}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg)^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{n+k},\mathbf{u}_{n+k-1}\bigg\rangle

where

Y_{n,k}=\bigg(\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}X_{n+j}\bigg)-\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_{n}}\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg),

which by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality leads to

\big|S_{n}-S_{n+k}\big|\leq cp_{n+k+1}\bigg\|\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg\|\cdot\big\|Y_{n,k}\big\|\cdot\big\|\mathbf{u}_{n+k}\big\|^{2}.

Let
K
be a compact set in
\mathbb{R}
. By Proposition
2.2

(4.3)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in K}\sup_{t\in[0,\omega]}\big\|X_{n}(t;z)-\mathfrak{T}_{t}(\omega;0)\big\|=0,

thus there are
n_{0}\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
t\in[0,\omega]
and
z\in K
,

(4.4)

\operatorname{discr}X_{n}(t;z)\leq-\delta.

Since

\det\big(\operatorname{sym}(EX_{n})\big)=-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{discr}X_{n},

there is
c>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
t\in[0,\omega]
,
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, and
z\in K
,

\big|\mathscr{D}_{n}(t,\eta;z)\big|=\big|\langle{EX_{n}(t;z){\mathbf{u}_{n}(t,\eta;z)}},{\mathbf{u}_{n}(t,\eta;z)}\rangle\big|\geq c\|\mathbf{u}_{n}(t,\eta;z)\|^{2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{S_{n}}{S_{n+k}}-1\bigg|\leq c\bigg\|\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg)^{-1}\bigg\|\cdot\Bigg(\bigg\|\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}X_{n+j}-\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg\|+\bigg|1-\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_{n}}\bigg|\cdot\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}X_{n+j}\bigg\|\Bigg).

In view of Lemma
2.5
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{p_{n+1}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}=1

uniformly with respect to
t\in[0,\omega]
.
Since by Section
3.2
,
(X_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}_{0})
is uniformly diagonalizable on
[0,\omega]\times K
,

[
33
, Proposition 1 & 2]
implies that there is
c>0
such that for each
\epsilon>0
there is

n_{1}\geq n_{0}
, such that for all
n\geq n_{1}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{S_{n}}{S_{n+k}}-1\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq c\bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|D_{n+j}\|^{-1}\bigg)\cdot\bigg(\epsilon\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|D_{n+j}\|+\bigg|1-\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_{n}}\bigg|\prod_{j=0}^{k}\|D_{n+j}\|\bigg)

\displaystyle=c\bigg(\epsilon+\bigg|1-\frac{p_{n+1}}{p_{n}}\bigg|\|D_{n}\|\bigg)

uniformly on
[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. Consequently, for each
\epsilon>0
there is
n_{2}\geq n_{1}
such that
for all
m\geq n\geq n_{2}
,

(4.5)

\sup_{(t,\eta,z)\in[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K}\bigg|\frac{S_{n}(t,\eta;z)}{S_{m}(t,\eta;z)}-1\bigg|\leq\epsilon.

Since for all
u\in\mathbb{R}
,

\log\lvert{u}\rvert\leq\lvert{1-\lvert{u}\rvert}\rvert,

we obtain

\big|\log\lvert{S_{n}}\rvert-\log\lvert{S_{m}}\rvert\big|\leq\bigg|1-\frac{S_{n}}{S_{m}}\bigg|\leq\epsilon,

thus
(\log\lvert{S_{n}}\rvert:n\in\mathbb{N})
is a uniform Cauchy sequence of continuous functions on

[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. Therefore, it uniformly converges to continuous function on

[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. In particular,
(\lvert{S_{n}}\rvert:n\in\mathbb{N})
is uniformly bounded on

[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. Thus, by (
4.5
),
(S_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
is a uniform Cauchy sequence
of continuous functions on
[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem

\tau

\displaystyle u_{n}

\displaystyle=\left\langle C_{n-1}\Big(\prod_{k=L}^{n-1}D_{j}C_{j}^{-1}C_{j-1}\Big)C_{L-1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{L},e_{1}\right\rangle.

Next, by the proof of

, for all

(t,\eta,z)\in[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
,

\displaystyle\lvert{u_{n}(t,\eta;z)-q_{n;L}(t,\eta;z)}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq c\Big(\prod_{k=L}^{n-1}\|D_{k}(t;z)\|\Big)\sum_{k=L}^{n}\big\|\Delta C_{k-1}(t;z)\big\|\|\mathbf{u}_{L}(t,\eta;z)\|

\displaystyle\leq c\big(\sup_{[0,\omega]\times\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K}\|\mathbf{u}_{L_{0}}\|\big)\Big(\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n-1}\lvert{\lambda_{k}^{+}(t;z)}\rvert\Big)\sum_{k=L}^{n}\big\|\Delta C_{k-1}(t;z)\big\|

where the last estimate is a consequence of Claim

. Hence,

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{n}(t,\eta;z)-\psi_{n;L}(t,\eta;z)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\frac{u_{n+1}(t,\eta;z)-q_{n+1;L}(t,\eta;z)}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{+}(t;z)}\Big|+\Big|\lambda_{n}^{-}(t;z)\cdot\frac{u_{n}(t,\eta;z)-q_{n;L}(t,\eta;z)}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n}\lambda_{k}^{+}(t;z)}\Big|

(5.6)

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{k=L}^{n}\|\Delta C_{j-1}\|\leq c\epsilon.

Therefore, for all

is a Cauchy sequence it is enough to show that
(\psi_{n;L}:n\geq L)

converges. To achieve this, we first notice that

\displaystyle D_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{-}\operatorname{Id}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{n}^{+}-\lambda_{n}^{-}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{+}}\big(D_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{-}\operatorname{Id}\big)\Big(\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n-1}D_{k}\Big)=i\frac{\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}X_{n}}}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{L-1}\lambda_{k}^{+}}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{n-1}\lambda_{k}^{+}}\left\langle C_{\infty}\big(D_{n}-\lambda_{n}^{-}\operatorname{Id}\big)\Big(\prod_{k=L}^{n-1}D_{k}\Big)C_{L-1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{L},e_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=i\frac{\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}X_{n}}}{\prod_{k=L_{0}}^{L-1}\lambda_{k}^{+}}\left\langle C_{\infty}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}C_{L-1}^{-1}\mathbf{u}_{L},e_{1}\right\rangle.

Claim 3

,
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}

, we set

(6.7)

\rho_{L}=\int_{0}^{L}\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}{\rm d}t.

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
6.1
are satisfied. Suppose that there is
s\in[0,\omega]
such that

(6.8)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\frac{w_{n}(s+t)}{p_{n}(s+t)}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t.

Then

(6.9)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(z,z;\eta)=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{\lvert{\varphi(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}p_{M}(t)}{4-|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|^{2}}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\rm d}t,

locally uniformly with respect to
z\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. In particular,
\tau
is in the limit-point case at

\infty
.

Proof.

We shall start by proving the convergence

(6.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}K_{s+n\omega}(z,z;\eta)=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{\lvert{\varphi(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}p_{M}(t)}{4-|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|^{2}}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\>\rm d}t

locally uniformly with respect to
(\eta,z)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.

We apply Theorem
6.1
on
[0,\omega-s]
and
[\omega-s,\omega]
, together with the Lebesgue’s dominated theorem,
to get

(6.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\frac{1}{\varrho_{n}(s+t)}\sum_{m=M}^{n}\lvert{\langle{\mathbf{u}_{m}(s+t,\eta;z)},{e_{1}}\rangle}\rvert^{2}w_{m}(s+t){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{\lvert{\varphi(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}p_{M}(t)}{4-\lvert{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}\rvert^{2}}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\>\rm d}t

locally uniformly with respect to
(\eta,z)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
. Let us next observe that by Lemma
2.5
and
(
6.8
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}-\frac{\varrho_{n}(t+s)}{\rho_{n}(s)}\bigg|{\rm d}t\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\gamma_{j}(s)\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}-\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}(s)}\frac{w_{j}(s+t)}{p_{j}(s+t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t,

thus by the Stolz–Cesàro theorem we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}-\frac{\varrho_{n}(t+s)}{\rho_{n}(s)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0.

Next, we write

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}\frac{1}{\varrho_{n}(s+t)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lvert{u_{k}(s+t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w_{k}(s+t){\>\rm d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{\int_{0}^{\omega}}\qquad-\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lvert{u_{k}(s+t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w_{k}(s+t){\>\rm d}t\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}-\frac{\varrho_{n}(s+t)}{\rho_{n}(s)}\bigg|\cdot\frac{1}{\varrho_{n}(s+t)}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lvert{u_{k}(s+t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w_{k}(t)\bigg){\>\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\leq c\int_{0}^{\omega}\bigg|\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(s+t)}{\mathfrak{p}(s+t)}-\frac{\varrho_{n}(s+t)}{\rho_{n}(s)}\bigg|{\>\rm d}t.

Therefore, by (
6.11
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\int_{0}^{\omega}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lvert{u_{k}(s+t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w_{k}(s+t){\>\rm d}t=\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{\lvert{\varphi(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}p_{M}(t)}{4-|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|^{2}}\frac{\mathfrak{w}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\>\rm d}t

uniformly with respect to
(\eta,z)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. Since

\displaystyle K_{s+n\omega}(z,z;\eta)=\int_{0}^{s+n\omega}\lvert{u(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w(t){\>\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{\omega}\lvert{u_{k}(s+t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w_{k}(t){\rm d}t

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\int_{0}^{s}\lvert{u(t,\eta;z)}\rvert^{2}w(t){\rm d}t

we obtain (
6.10
)
uniformly with respect to
(\eta,z)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K
. Notice that, in view of (
6.8
), the function
u(\cdot,\eta;z)
does not belong to
L^{2}(w)
, which implies that
\tau
is in the limit-point case at
\infty
.

Let us turn to the proof of (
6.9
). Let
(L_{j}:j\geq 1)
be a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers. Then there is a sequence of positive integers
(n_{j}:j\geq 1)
such that
L_{j}\in[n_{j}\omega,(n_{j}+1)\omega)
. We obviously have
\lim_{j\to\infty}n_{j}=\infty
. Notice that by Corollary
4.2
there is a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{(\eta,z)\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\times K}\big|K_{L_{j}}(z,z;\eta)-K_{s+n_{j}\omega}(z,z;\eta)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{n_{j}\omega}^{(n_{j}+1)\omega}|u(t,\eta;z)|^{2}w(t){\rm d}t

(6.12)

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{n_{j}\omega}^{(n_{j}+1)\omega}\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}{\rm d}t.

Next, by (
6.2
) and (
6.7
)

(6.13)

|\rho_{L_{j}}-\rho_{n_{j}}(s)|\leq\int_{0}^{s}\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}{\rm d}t+\int_{n_{j}\omega}^{(n_{j}+1)\omega}\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}{\rm d}t

Since

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{\rho_{L_{j}}}K_{L_{j}}(z,z;\eta)-\frac{1}{\rho_{n_{j}}(s)}K_{s+n_{j}\omega}(z,z;\eta)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\rho_{L_{j}}}\big|K_{L_{j}}(z,z;\eta)-K_{s+n_{j}\omega}(z,z;\eta)\big|

\displaystyle+\frac{|\rho_{L_{j}}-\rho_{n_{j}}(s)|}{\rho_{L_{j}}}\frac{1}{\rho_{n_{j}}(s)}K_{s+n_{j}\omega}(z,z;\eta)

by (
6.12
), (
6.13
), (
6.10
), (
2.15
) and the Carleman condition, the conclusion (
6.9
) follows.
∎

7.
The density of states

Given
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
and
L>0
let us consider an operator
H^{L}_{\eta}:\operatorname{Dom}\big(H^{L}_{\eta}\big)\to L^{2}([0,L],w)

defined by
H^{L}_{\eta}f=\tau f
with the domain

(7.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Dom}\big(H^{L}_{\eta}\big)=\big\{f\in L^{2}([0,L],w)\colon

\displaystyle f,pf^{\prime}\in\operatorname{AC}([0,L]),\tau f\in L^{2}([0,L],w),

\displaystyle p(0)\eta_{2}f(0)-\eta_{1}pf^{\prime}(0)=0,f(L)=0\big\}.

Notice that according to
[
38
, Example, p.225]
the operator
H_{\eta}^{L}
is self-adjoint with the spectrum consisting
of simple eigenvalues only. In this section we shall be interested in asymptotic behavior of the family of the eigenvalue
counting measures

\nu_{\eta}^{L}=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(H^{L}_{\eta})}\delta_{\lambda},\quad L\in(0,\infty)

where
\delta_{\lambda}
denotes the Dirac’s delta at
\lambda
.

Let us begin with an adaptation of
[
37
, Lemma 3.1]
, which allows to link asymptotic behavior of Christoffel–Darboux kernel on the diagonal with the vague limit of
(\nu^{L}_{\eta}:L>0)
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
be given.
Suppose that there are an open subset
U
of the real line, a continuous function
\rho_{\cdot}:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that
\lim_{L\to\infty}\rho_{L}=\infty
and a non-zero function
g:U\to\mathbb{R}
such that

(7.2)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)=g(\lambda)

locally uniformly with respect to
\lambda\in U
. Then for each
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(U)

(7.3)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\nu^{L}_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\mu_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda).

Proof.

Since
g(\lambda_{0})\neq 0
for certain
\lambda_{0}\in U
, we have that
u(\cdot,\eta;\lambda_{0})
does not belong to
L^{2}(w)
, so
\tau
is in the limit-point case at
+\infty
.

Let us define a collection of positive measures

\sigma_{\eta}^{L}=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(H^{L}_{\eta})}\frac{\delta_{\lambda}}{K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)},\quad L\in(0,\infty).

By
[
40
, Theorem 10.1]
(see also
[
28
, formula (3.13)]
for a stronger statement) we have

(7.4)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(\lambda)\>\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(\lambda)\>\mu_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda),\quad h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

Notice that

(7.5)

\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)=\frac{1}{K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)}\nu_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda).

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(U)
and let
K=\operatornamewithlimits{supp}(f)
. By (
7.5
) we have

\displaystyle\bigg|\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\>\nu_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\>\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)\bigg|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\Big(\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)-g(\lambda)\Big)\>\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\lambda\in K}|f(\lambda)|\cdot\sup_{\lambda\in K}\bigg|\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta)-g(\lambda)\bigg|\cdot\sigma^{L}_{\eta}(K).

By (
7.4
) applied to
h\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
such that
h(x)=1
for any
x\in K
we get

\limsup_{L\to\infty}\sigma^{L}_{\eta}(K)<\infty.

Thus, by (
7.22
) and Corollary
6.2
we get

(7.6)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\>\nu_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)g(\lambda)\>\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda).

Since
f\cdot g\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
, by (
7.4
) we conclude that

(7.7)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\>\sigma_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\>\mu_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda).

Since
f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(U)
was arbitrary, by combining (
7.6
) with (
7.7
), the conclusion (
7.3
) follows.
∎

Recall that in Corollary
6.2
, under some hypotheses, we proved (
7.2
) (cf. (
6.9
)). We would like to express the value of
g
more explicitly. In view of (
7.3
), one way to do that is to compute the value of the left-hand side of (
7.3
) in a different way.

By
[
38
, Example, p.225]
,

\sigma(H^{L}_{\eta})=\{\lambda:u(L,\eta;\lambda)=0\}.

For any
L\in(0,\infty)
and any
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, by
[
4
, Theorem D.6]
, the function
\mathbb{C}\ni z\mapsto u(L,\eta;z)
is entire of
the order less or equal to
1/2
. Thus the Hadamard factorization theorem (see e.g.
[
4
, Theorem E.2.7]
)
implies that there are
A\neq 0,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
(\lambda_{j}:j\geq 1)
, such that

u(L,\eta;z)=Az^{k}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\frac{z}{\lambda_{j}}\bigg).

Since
u(L,\eta;\cdot)
has simple zeros, we must have
k\in\{0,1\}
. Thus,

(7.8)

-\frac{\partial_{z}u(L,\eta;z)}{u(L,\eta;z)}=\sum_{\lambda\in\sigma(H^{L}_{\eta})}\frac{1}{\lambda-z}=\mathcal{C}\big[\nu^{L}_{\eta}\big](z),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}

where for a positive measure
\nu
on the real line,
\mathcal{C}[\mu]
denotes its
Cauchy transform
, that is

\mathcal{C}[\nu](z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\lambda-z}\nu({\rm d}\lambda),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}

provided the integral exists. Motivated by
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]
we are going to analyze the asymptotic
behavior of the left-hand side of (
7.8
).

Theorem 7.2
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
5.3
are satisfied. Then for any
t\in[0,\omega]

(7.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{C}\big[\tfrac{1}{\rho_{t+n\omega}}\nu_{\eta}^{t+n\omega}\big](z)=\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}{i\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\varsigma}.

Proof.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{C}_{\varsigma}
with non-empty interior. By Theorem
5.3
for any
z\in K
, there are two linearly independent mappings
\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z)
and
\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)
solving (
2.2
). Moreover, for any
x\geq 0
the mappings
\mathbf{u}^{+}(x;\cdot)
and
\mathbf{u}^{-}(x;\cdot)

are continuous on
K
and holomorphic in
\operatorname{int}{K}
. Furthermore, there is
M\geq 1
such that the limits

(7.10)

\phi^{+}(t;z)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{u_{k}^{+}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<k}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)},\quad\text{and}\quad\phi^{-}(t;z)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{u_{k}^{-}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<k}\lambda_{j}^{-}(s;z)}

exist uniformly with respect to
(t,z)\in[0,\omega]\times K
and define non-vanishing functions.

Let
\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z)
be any solution to (
2.2
). By the linear independence of
\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z)
and
\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)
, there are functions
f,g:K\to\mathbb{C}
,
such that

(7.11)

\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z)=f(z)\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z)+g(z)\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z).

By computing the Wronskians we get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big)

\displaystyle=f(z)\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big)

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z)\big)

\displaystyle=-g(z)\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big).

Thus,

f(z)=\frac{\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big)}{\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big)},\quad\text{and}\quad g(z)=-\frac{\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{v}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z)\big)}{\operatorname{Wr}\big(\mathbf{u}^{+}(\cdot;z),\mathbf{u}^{-}(\cdot;z)\big)}.

In particular,
\mathbf{v}
is continuous on
K
and holomorphic on
\operatorname{int}{K}
if and only if functions
f
and
g
are continuous on
K
and holomorphic on
\operatorname{int}{K}
.

Next, by (
7.11
) we have

(7.12)

v_{n}(t;z)=f(z)u_{n}^{+}(t;z)+g(z)u_{n}^{-}(t;z),\quad t\in[0,\omega],n\geq 0.

Thus, for
n\geq M

\displaystyle\frac{v_{n}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}

\displaystyle=f(z)\frac{u_{n}^{+}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}+g(z)\frac{u_{n}^{-}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}

\displaystyle=f(z)\frac{u_{n}^{+}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}+g(z)\frac{u_{n}^{-}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{-}(s;z)}\cdot\frac{\prod_{M\leq j\leq n}\lambda_{j}^{-}(s;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j<n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}.

Now, by (
3.7
) and (
7.10
) we obtain

(7.13)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{v_{n}(t;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j\leq n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}=f(z)\phi^{+}(t;z)

uniformly with respect to
(t,z)\in[0,\omega]\times K
. Recall that

\phi^{+}
is a continuous non-vanishing function. Therefore, if
f(z)\neq 0
, then there is
\delta>0
,
M^{\prime}\geq M
,
such that for all
n\geq M^{\prime}
and all
t\in[0,\omega]

\delta\prod_{M\leq j<n}|\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)|^{2}\leq|v_{n}(t;z)|^{2}

By (
3.4
), (
2.22
), (
2.23
) and (
2.11
) we have

|\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)|^{2}\geq\det X_{j}(s;z)=\frac{p_{j}(s)}{p_{j+1}(s)}.

Thus,

\delta\frac{p_{M}(s)}{p_{n}(s)}w_{n}(t)\leq|v_{n}(t;z)|^{2}w_{n}(t).

By Lemma
2.5
there is
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that

\delta^{\prime}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}\leq|v_{n}(t;z)|^{2}w_{n}(t).

Therefore,

\int_{M^{\prime}\omega}^{\infty}\frac{w(x)}{p(x)}{\rm d}x\leq\int_{M^{\prime}\omega}^{\infty}|v(x;z)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x.

Consequently, by the Carleman condition (
1.11
), if
v(\cdot;z)\in L^{2}(w)
, then
f(z)=0
. Since
\tau
is in the limit point case, it yields
u^{-}(\cdot;z)\in L^{2}(w)
for any
z\in K
.

Let us turn to the proof of (
7.9
). Let us fix
t\in[0,\omega]
and define

(7.14)

\phi_{n}(z)=\frac{u_{n}(t,\eta;z)}{\prod_{M\leq j\leq n}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)},\quad z\in\operatorname{int}(K).

Then, by (
7.13
),

\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}(z)=\phi(z)

uniformly with respect to
z\in K
, where

\phi(z)=f(z)\phi^{+}(t;z),\quad z\in K.

Let us show that
\phi
is non-vanishing.
Since
\tau
is in the limit point case at
+\infty
, the operator
H_{\eta}
is self-adjoint. Thus, we have
\sigma(H_{\eta})\subset\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
K\cap\sigma(H_{\eta})=\emptyset
. Notice that since
u(\cdot,\eta;z)
satisfies the boundary conditions of
H_{\eta}
, it cannot belong to
L^{2}(w)
, otherwise it would be an eigenfunction of
H_{\eta}
. Consequently,
u(\cdot,\eta;z)
is linearly independent with
u^{-}(\cdot;z)
, which implies that
f(z)\neq 0
. Since
(\phi_{n}:n\geq M)
is a sequence of holomorphic functions on
\operatorname{int}(K)

and
\phi
is everywhere non-zero, we get

(7.15)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\partial_{z}\phi_{n}(z)}{\phi_{n}(z)}=\frac{\partial_{z}\phi(z)}{\phi(z)}

uniformly with respect to
z\in\operatorname{int}(K)
. Notice that

(7.16)

\frac{\partial_{z}\phi_{n}(z)}{\phi_{n}(z)}=\frac{\partial_{z}u_{n}(t,\eta;z)}{u_{n}(t,\eta;z)}-\sum_{j=M}^{n}\frac{\partial_{z}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}{\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}.

Since the Carleman’s condition (
1.11
) is satisfied,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\rho_{n}(s)=+\infty.

Therefore, by (
7.15
) and (
7.16
) we obtain

(7.17)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\frac{\partial_{z}u_{n}(t,\eta;z)}{u_{n}(t,\eta;z)}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\sum_{j=M}^{n}\frac{\partial_{z}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}{\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}.

To compute the right-hand side of (
7.17
), we are going to use the Stolz–Cesàro theorem. Namely,
we have

(7.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{n}(s)}\sum_{j=M}^{n}\frac{\partial_{z}\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}{\lambda_{j}^{+}(s;z)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\gamma_{n}(s)}\frac{\partial_{z}\lambda_{n}^{+}(s;z)}{\lambda_{n}^{+}(s;z)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}{i\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}}

where the last equality follows by Proposition
3.3
. By the Carleman condition we easily get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\rho_{n}(s)}{\rho_{t+n\omega}}=1

(cf. (
6.7
) and (
6.2
)). Therefore, by (
7.8
) we get (
7.9
). Since
t\in[0,\omega]
was arbitrary, it completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Theorem
5.3
are satisfied. Then for every
f\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\sup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}(1+\lambda^{2})|f(\lambda)|<\infty

we have for any
t\in[0,\omega]

(7.19)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{t+n\omega}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\>\nu_{\eta}^{t+n\omega}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\>\nu_{\infty}({\rm d}\lambda),

where the measure
\nu_{\infty}
is purely absolutely continuous with the density

(7.20)

\frac{{\rm d}\nu_{\infty}}{{\rm d}\lambda}\equiv\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{|\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|}{\gamma\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}}.

Proof.

Let us recall that
\varsigma=\operatorname{sign}({\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)})
. Now, the conclusion easily follows by
[
37
, Lemma 4.1]
together with Theorem
7.2
and
[
37
, Theorem A.3 and Remark A.4]
.
∎

Corollary 7.4
.

Suppose that the hypotheses of Corollary
6.2
are satisfied.
Then the measure
\mu_{\eta}
is purely absolutely
continuous on
\mathbb{R}
with the density

(7.21)

\mu_{\eta}^{\prime}(\lambda)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{|\partial_{z}\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|}{\gamma\sqrt{-\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}}\frac{1}{g(\lambda)},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

where

(7.22)

g(\lambda)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}K_{L}(\lambda,\lambda;\eta),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular,
\mu_{\eta}^{\prime}
is a continuous and positive function on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.1
we have

(7.23)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{\rho_{L}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\nu_{\eta}^{L}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\mu_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda),\quad f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

Thus, by (
7.19
) we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)\nu_{\infty}({\rm d}\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\lambda)g(\lambda)\mu_{\eta}({\rm d}\lambda),\quad f\in\mathcal{C}_{c}(\mathbb{R}).

Since
g
is positive everywhere, by (
7.20
) the formula (
7.21
) follows.
∎

8.
The case of empty essential spectrum

In this section we investigate the case when
|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|>2
. We seek for assumptions under which the operator

H_{\eta}
has the essential spectrum empty.

The proof of
[
26
, Lemma 5.13]
together with the subordinacy theory for Sturm–Liouville operators
(see e.g.
[
7
]
) yields the following lemma (cf.
[
40
, Theorem 8.1b]
).

Proposition 8.1
.

Suppose that
\tau
is regular at
0
and limit point at
+\infty
. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval with
non-empty interior. If for any
\lambda\in K
there exists a non-zero
u\in L^{2}([0,\infty),w)
such that

\tau u=\lambda u
, then for any
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
the operator
H_{\eta}
is pure point in
K
.

Taking into account Proposition
8.1
, the proof of
[
29
, Theorem 5.3]
yields the following result.

Theorem 8.2
.

Suppose that
\tau
is regular at
0
and limit point at
+\infty
. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval with
non-empty interior. If there exists a family
\{u(\cdot;\lambda):\lambda\in K\}
of non-zero functions such that

\tau u(\cdot;\lambda)=\lambda u(\cdot;\lambda)
, satisfying

(8.1)

\sup_{\lambda\in K}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x<\infty,

and

(8.2)

\lim_{L\to\infty}\sup_{\lambda\in K}\int_{L}^{\infty}|u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x=0,

and for all
L\in(0,\infty)
, and
\lambda\in\operatorname{int}(K)
,

(8.3)

\lim_{\lambda^{\prime}\to\lambda}\int_{0}^{L}|u(x;\lambda)-u(x;\lambda^{\prime})|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x=0.

Then for every
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, the operator
H_{\eta}
satisfies
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\eta})\cap\operatorname{int}{K}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. In view of (
8.1
) and Proposition
8.1
the spectrum of
H_{\eta}
is pure point on
K
.
Therefore, it is enough to prove that
\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(H_{\eta})
has no accumulation points in
\operatorname{int}{K}
.
Let us define a family of functions
(f_{L}:L\in(0,\infty])
by the setting

f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{L}u(x;\lambda)\overline{u(x;\lambda^{\prime})}w(x){\rm d}x,\quad\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in K.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

|f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})|\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{L}|u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{L}|u(x;\lambda^{\prime})|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x\bigg)^{1/2},

thus

\sup_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in K}|f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})|\leq\sup_{\lambda\in K}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x,

which by (
8.1
) is finite. Next, we claim that for each
L\in(0,\infty)
the function
f_{L}
is continuous.
Let us show that
f_{L}
is continuous with respect to the first variable. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality we have

\displaystyle|f_{L}(\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime})-f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\int_{0}^{L}|u(x;\lambda^{\prime\prime})-u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{0}^{L}|u(x;\lambda^{\prime})|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x\bigg)^{1/2}.

In view of (
8.1
) and (
8.3
), we obtain

\lim_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}\to\lambda}f_{L}(\lambda^{\prime\prime},\lambda^{\prime})=f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}).

Similarly one can show continuity of
f_{L}
with respect to the second variable. Finally, let us observe that

|f_{L}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})-f_{\infty}(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})|\leq\sup_{\lambda\in K}\int_{L}^{\infty}|u(x;\lambda)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x,

which by (
8.2
) tends to
0
as
L
approaches infinity. Consequently, the function
f_{\infty}
is also continuous.

Suppose, on the contrary to our claim, that
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\eta})\cap\operatorname{int}(K)\neq\emptyset
. Let

\lambda\in\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\eta})\cap\operatorname{int}(K)
. Then there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{n}:n\geq 0)\subseteq K
such that

(i)

\lambda_{n}\in\operatorname{int}{K}\cap\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(H_{\eta})
for any
n
;

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=\lambda
;

(iii)

all
\lambda_{n}
are different.

Then by the continuity of
f_{\infty}
, we have

0<f_{\infty}(\lambda,\lambda)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{\infty}(\lambda_{n+1},\lambda_{n})=0

where the last equality follows from the fact that for self-adjoint operators eigenspaces corresponding to different
eigenvalues are orthogonal. This leads to contradiction and the theorem follows.
∎

Let us state the main result of this section.

Theorem 8.3
.

Let
\omega>0
. Suppose that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-periodically modulated Sturm–Liouville parameters, such that
the transfer matrix
\mathfrak{T}
corresponding to
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
satisfies
\lvert{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}\rvert>2
.
Assume that

\frac{q}{p},\frac{w}{p},\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}^{\omega}(L^{1};\mathbb{R}).

If
\tau
is in the limit point case at
+\infty
, then for each
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
the operator
H_{\eta}
satisfies

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\eta})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact interval containing
z_{0}
in its interior. We consider the equation
(
2.21
) for
s=0
, that is

(8.4)

\mathbf{u}_{n+1}(0;z)=X_{n}(0;z)\mathbf{u}_{n}(0;z),\quad n\geq 0,z\in K.

Let us recall that
(X_{n}(0;\cdot):n\geq 0)
is a sequence of continuous mappings on
K
with values in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.
Since

\operatorname{discr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)=(\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0))^{2}-4>0,

the matrix
\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)
has two distinct eigenvalues

(8.5)

\lambda^{-}_{\infty}=\xi_{-}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}{2}\bigg),\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda^{+}_{\infty}=\xi_{+}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)}{2}\bigg).

Since
|\operatorname{tr}\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)|>2
, we have

(8.6)

0<|\lambda^{-}_{\infty}|<1<|\lambda^{+}_{\infty}|.

Moreover, by (
2.22
) and Proposition
2.2
,

(8.7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{n}(0;z)=\mathfrak{T}(\omega;0)

uniformly with respect to
z\in K
. Therefore, there are
M\geq 1
and
\delta>0
such that for all
n\geq M

\operatorname{discr}X_{n}(0;z)>\delta.

Hence, for all
n\geq M
and
z\in K
, the matrix
X_{n}(0;z)
has two distinct eigenvalues

(8.8)

\lambda^{-}_{n}(0;z)=\sqrt{\det X_{n}(0;z)}\xi_{-}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{tr}X_{n}(0;z)}{2\sqrt{\det X_{n}(0;z)}}\bigg),\quad\lambda^{+}_{n}(0;z)=\sqrt{\det X_{n}(0;z)}\xi_{+}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{tr}X_{n}(0;z)}{2\sqrt{\det X_{n}(0;z)}}\bigg)

see the formula (
3.4
). Since
|\lambda^{-}_{n}(z)\leq|\lambda^{+}_{n}(z)|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
z\in K
, we must have

(8.9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{-}_{n}(0;z)=\lambda^{-}_{\infty},\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda^{+}_{n}(0;z)=\lambda^{+}_{\infty},

uniformly with respect to
z\in K
. In view of Proposition
2.9
,

(X_{n}(0;\cdot):n\geq 0)\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(K;\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{R}))
. By
[
36
, Lemma 4.2]
there exists a compact interval

K^{\prime}\subset K
containing
z_{0}
in its interior such that the hypotheses of
[
36
, Theorem 4.1]
are satisfied.
Moreover, by the proof of
[
36
, Theorem 4.1]
for
r=1
there are a constant
M\geq 1
and a sequence

(\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;\cdot):n\geq 0)
of continuous mappings on
K^{\prime}
satisfying (
8.4
), and such that

(8.10)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}\bigg\|\frac{\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z)}{\prod_{j=M}^{n-1}\lambda_{j}^{-}(0;z)}-\nu^{-}\bigg\|=0

where
\nu^{-}
is a non-zero vector. Hence, there is a constant
c>0
such that

\|\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z)\|\leq c\prod_{j=M}^{n-1}|\lambda_{j}^{-}(0;z)|,\quad n\geq M,z\in K^{\prime}.

Notice that by (
8.8
) and (
2.11
) we have

\|\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z)\|\leq c\sqrt{\frac{p_{M}(0)}{p_{n}(0)}}\prod_{j=M}^{n-1}\bigg|\xi_{-}\bigg(\frac{\operatorname{tr}X_{j}(0;z)}{2\sqrt{\det X_{n}(0;z)}}\bigg)\bigg|,\quad n\geq M,z\in K^{\prime}.

By (
8.7
) and (
8.6
), for every
\epsilon\in(0,1-|\lambda_{\infty}^{-}|)
there are
M^{\prime}\geq M

and
c^{\prime}>0
such that

(8.11)

\|\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z)\|\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{\frac{1}{p_{n}(0)}}(1-\epsilon)^{n},\quad n\geq M^{\prime},z\in K^{\prime}.

For any
x\geq 0
define

\mathbf{u}^{-}(x;z)=T(x;z)\mathbf{u}^{-}_{0}(0;z).

Notice that since
\mathbf{u}^{-}_{0}(0;\cdot)
is continuous on
K^{\prime}
, the mapping
\mathbf{u}^{-}
is continuous on
[0,\infty)\times K^{\prime}
.
Using (
2.21
), (
2.22
) and (
2.23
) we easily get

\mathbf{u}^{-}(n\omega;z)=\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z),\quad n\geq 0.

Then by (
2.10
) we get for any
t\in[0,\omega]

(8.12)

\mathbf{u}^{-}(t+n\omega;z)=U_{0;n}(t;z)\mathbf{u}^{-}_{n}(0;z).

In view of Proposition
2.2
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}U_{0;n}(t;z)=\mathfrak{T}(t;0)

uniformly with respect to
z\in K^{\prime}
. Since
\mathfrak{T}(\cdot;0)
is continuous, it is uniformly bounded on
[0,\omega]
.
Therefore, in view of (
8.12
) and (
8.11
) there is a constant
c>0
such that for any
t\in[0,\omega]

and any
z\in K^{\prime}

\|\mathbf{u}^{-}(t+n\omega;z)\|\leq c\sqrt{\frac{1}{p_{n}(0)}}(1-\epsilon)^{n}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{M^{\prime}\omega}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}|u^{-}(x;z)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=M^{\prime}}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}|u^{-}_{n}(t;z)|^{2}w_{n}(t){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\omega}\sum_{n=M}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}|u^{-}_{n}(t;z)|^{2}w_{n}(t){\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\sum_{n=M^{\prime}}^{\infty}(1-\epsilon)^{2n}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}{\rm d}t.

Next, we notice that

\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}=\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}\frac{p_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)},

thus, by Lemma
2.5
and (
2.15

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}{\rm d}t=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(t)}\frac{\mathfrak{p}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(0)}{\rm d}t.

Because the function
\mathfrak{p}
is continuous, its uniformly bounded on
[0,\omega]
. Therefore, by (
2.15
) we obtain

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\frac{w_{n}(t)}{p_{n}(0)}{\rm d}t=0.

Consequently, we get

(8.13)

\int_{M^{\prime}\omega}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}|u^{-}(x;z)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x<\infty.

Since
u^{-}
is continuous function on
[0,\infty)\times K^{\prime}
, we get

(8.14)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sup_{z\in K^{\prime}}|u^{-}(x;z)|^{2}w(x){\rm d}x<\infty.

Observe that (
8.14
) implies (
8.1
) and (
8.2
). Since
u^{-}
is continuous, we also have (
8.3
).
Hence, if
\tau
is in the limit point case at
+\infty
, then Theorem
8.2
implies that for any
\eta\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}^{1}

the operator
H_{\eta}
satisfies
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\eta})\cap\{z_{0}\}=\emptyset
. Since
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
was arbitrary,
the theorem follows.
∎

Let us recall that if

In this section we provide a collection of examples where our method applies. The first example demonstrates that the assumptions
regarding the Sturm–Liouville parameters do not necessarily lead to them being regularly varying.

Example 2
.

Let
\omega>0
and let
\mathfrak{q}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}([0,\infty))
be a
\omega
-periodic real-valued function. For
0<a<b<1
let

be a strictly positive continuously differentiable function such that

p(t)=\exp\Big(\frac{a+b}{2}\log{t}+\frac{b-a}{2}\log{t}\cdot\sin\big(\log{\log{t}}\big)\Big),\quad t>\exp(\exp(1)).

Notice that

(9.1)

t^{b}\leq p(t)\leq t^{a},\quad t>\exp(\exp(1)).

Both bounds are attained infinitely many times. In particular,
p
is not regularly varying. Let us consider

q(t)=p(t)\mathfrak{q}(t),\quad w(t)\equiv 1,\quad t\geq 0.

Let
\mathfrak{p}(t)\equiv 1
and
\mathfrak{w}(t)\equiv 1
. Notice that there is a constant
c>0
such that for each
t>\exp(\exp(1))
,

\frac{w(t)}{p(t)}\leq\frac{1}{t^{b}},\quad\frac{q(t)}{p(t)}=\frac{\mathfrak{q}(t)}{\mathfrak{p}(t)},\quad\big|(\log p)^{\prime}(t)\big|\leq c\frac{1}{t},

which easily implies that (
2.15
), (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) are satisfied. Moreover, by (
9.1
)
the condition (
2.18
) holds true as well. Next, by direct computation with a help of (

In particular,

. Hence, by
[
30

1.10

is satisfied.

Appendix A
Asymptotically periodic parameters

Recently in
[
3
]
there has been studied another class of Sturm–Liouville parameters which is a perturbation of

\omega
-periodic case. We say that
(p,q,w)
are
\omega
-asymptotically periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters

if
(p,q,w)
are Sturm–Liouville parameters such that there are
\omega
-periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters

(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
satisfying

(A.1)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\omega}\big|w_{n}(t)-\mathfrak{w}(t)\big|+\big|q_{n}(t)-\mathfrak{q}(t)\big|+\bigg|\frac{1}{p_{n}(t)}-\frac{1}{\mathfrak{p}(t)}\bigg|{\rm d}t=0.

Let us notice that (
A.1
) implies

(A.2)

\int_{0}^{\infty}w(x){\rm d}x=\infty.

We aim to study solutions of (
2.1
). Let
u(\cdot;z)
be any solution of (
2.1
). We write

(A.3)

\mathbf{u}(t;z)=T(t;z)\mathbf{u}(0;z)\quad\text{where}\quad\mathbf{u}(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}u(t;z)\\
(p\partial_{t}u)(t;z)\end{pmatrix},\quad t\in[0,\infty),z\in\mathbb{C},

and whereas the function
T:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{C}\to\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, called here
transfer matrix
, is the unique solution to

T(t;z)=\operatorname{Id}+\int_{0}^{t}b(t^{\prime};z)T(t^{\prime};z){\rm d}t^{\prime}\quad\text{where}\quad b(t;z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&\frac{1}{p(t)}\\
q(t)-zw(t)&0\end{pmatrix}.

For
\omega
-periodic Sturm–Liouville parameters
(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q},\mathfrak{w})
we shall denote by
\mathfrak{T}
and
\mathfrak{b}
the mappings
T
and
b
, respectively.

For any
\eta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we denote by
\mathbf{u}
the solution to

(A.4)

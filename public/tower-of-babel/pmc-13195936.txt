Protective mechanism of sevoflurane preconditioning on myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury by regulating RMRP/miR-206 axis
Authors: Shuang Wu, Yuan Lu, Hao Chen, Shuai Wang, Yanyan Jiang, Dalong Wang, Quan Lin, Ke Liu, Shuai Zhang, Ying Cheng, Xiao Tian, Wenhan Ma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveMyocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI) is a major contributor to global disease mortality. This study aims to investigate the protective mechanism of the RMRP/miR-206 axis in MIRI following sevoflurane (Sev) preconditioning.MethodsIn this study, a hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) model was constructed by H9c2 cells. The optimal concentration of Sev was selected by assessing cell viability with a CCK-8 assay. After Sev preconditioning, flow cytometry was employed to detect cell apoptosis. The content of LDH was measured with a lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) assay kit. The SOD activity was examined by an SOD assay kit, and the levels of IL-6 and IL-10 were determined by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). RT-qPCR was utilized to detect the RMRP and miR-206 expression. The luciferase reporter assay and RIP assay were employed to demonstrate the interaction between RMRP and miR-206.ResultsIn this study, 2.0% was chosen as the optimal Sev concentration for subsequent experiments. After Sev preconditioning, cell viability increased, apoptosis decreased, LDH levels were reduced, SOD activity was enhanced, IL-6 was downregulated, and IL-10 was upregulated. Meanwhile, RMRP levels decreased significantly after Sev preconditioning. Overexpression of RMRP inhibited the protective effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced cells. Further analysis revealed that RMRP targeted miR-206. While miR-206 increased after Sev preconditioning, RMRP transfection suppressed miR-206 expression. Overexpression of miR-206 rescued H/R-induced cell damage caused by RMRP upregulation.ConclusionSev preconditioning protects cells from MIRI by regulating the RMRP/miR-206 axis.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40360-026-01129-9.

Introduction

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) are a major cause of illness and death worldwide. These diseases not only endanger the physical and mental well-being of patients but also impose a substantial economic burden on families [1]. Clinically, myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury (MIRI) is a severe condition that frequently occurring following treatment for myocardial injury. It typically manifests as myocardial hypofunction, cardiomyocyte necrosis, impairment of myocardial microvascular endothelial cells, or the onset of an inflammatory response [2, 3]. MIRI remains an urgent clinical challenge. New therapeutic approaches must be developed to mitigate MIRI progression and improve patient outcomes.

Anesthetic preconditioning is a protective mechanism, and the choice of anesthetics is critically important in mitigating MIRI during surgery [4]. Sevoflurane (Sev) is an inhalational anesthetic used to induce general anesthesia [5]. Evidence indicates that Sev preconditioning has a protective effect against heart disease [6]. In a rat model of myocardial infarction (MI), Wu et al. found that Sev protected cardiac function and alleviated myocardial injury [7]. Another study demonstrated that in a hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) model, Sev enhanced cell viability, reduced apoptosis, and mitigated injury [8].

In recent years, numerous studies have corroborated the pivotal role of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in MIRI [9]. For instance, JPX suppressed cell apoptosis by targeting miR-146b, thereby alleviating MIRI progression [10]. Inhibition of LINC00461 was shown to restore myocardial function and attenuate MIRI injury [11]. In addition, knocking down FGD5-AS1 promoted H/R cell viability and inhibited apoptosis, while FGD5-AS1 worsened MIRI by targeting miR-129-5p [12]. Subsequently, we found that RMRP induced cell injury under hypoxic conditions and might serve as a biomarker for treating acute MI [13]. In brain ischemia-reperfusion injury (IRI), RMRP interacted with miR-613 to promote IRI in mice [14]. However, the mechanism by which RMRP acts in MIRI remains unclear.

In this study, the function of the RMRP/miR-206 axis in MIRI was analyzed by constructing an H/R model. The goal was to reveal the molecular mechanism underlying MIRI development and provide new insights for treatment. We speculate that Sev preconditioning protects against MIRI by regulating the RMRP/miR-206 axis, which modulates oxidative stress, inflammatory response, and apoptosis.

Method and materials

Cell culture and the establishment of H/R model

H9c2 cardiomyocytes were obtained from Procell (Wuhan). Cells were cultured in DMEM (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Sigma Aldrich, USA) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin (MCE, USA) at 37 °C in a humidity atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Cells were divided into two groups. The H/R model was constructed according to previously published literature [15]. The control group was incubated under normal conditions. The experimental group was cultured in a milieu of 1% O₂, 94% N₂, and 5% CO₂ for 24 h. The medium was then replaced with fresh medium, and the hypoxia-treated cells were returned to normal conditions of 95% air, 5% CO₂, and 37 °C to construct the H/R model.

Cell treatment and cell transfection

The Sev experimental group was operated in an incubator equipped with a sevoflurane vaporizer (Kent, China) [16]. Cells were pretreated sevoflurane at concentrations of 0.5%, 1.0%, 1.5%, and 2.0% for 1 h. Cell viability of H9c2 was evaluated under each concentrations to determine the optimal concentration for Sev preconditioning. All plasmids (oe-NC, oe-RMRP), mimic-NC and miR-206 mimic were purchased from RiboBio (Guangzhou, China). H9c2 cells were seeded and cultured in 6-well plates. When cells reached the logarithmic phase, transfection was performed using Lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Invitrogen, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Cell viability

The effects of overexpressing RMRP and miR-206 on H9c2 cell viability under Sev preconditioning conditions were assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (Dojindo, Japan). H9c2 cells at a density of 1 × 10⁴ were plated in 96-well plates and cultured overnight. Cells were then transfected with plasmids, mimic-NC or miR-206 mimic according to the experimental requirements. At 0 h, 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h post-transfection, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well and incubated for 1 h. The cell viability was measured using a microplate reader.

Cell apoptosis

Using the Annexin V-FITC/PI Apoptosis Detection Kit (BD Biosciences, USA), the effects of overexpressing RMRP and miR-206 on the apoptosis of H9c2 cells under Sev preconditioning were assessed. After treatment, cells were collected and washed with precooled PBS. Following the product instructions, 5 µL of FITC and PI dyes were added separately, then incubated at 25 °C for 15 min. Samples were then analyzed using flow cytometer.

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), superoxide dismutase (SOD), interleukin-6 (IL-6) and IL-10 assay

The transfected H9c2 cells were incubated for an additional 24 h. The cells and their respective supernatants were then collected for subsequent analysis. Following the manufacturer’s protocol, the LDH levels were determined using the LDH assay kit (Abcam, UK). Cell supernatants were collected and transferred to new 96-well plates. Next, 60 µL of LDH reaction solution was added to each well, followed by incubation on a shaker at room temperature for 30 min.

The SOD assay kit from Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute was utilized to determine the SOD activity. Collected cells were lysed, and the supernatant was employed for the experiment. Each experimental step was executed strictly following the guidelines provided by the kit.

To detect IL-6 and IL-10 concentrations in cell supernatants, the experimental instructions of the IL-6 Quantikine ELISA Kit and the IL-10 Quantikine ELISA Kit (R&D Systems, USA) were strictly followed. The results of all experiments were measured by a microplate reader.

Cell RNA extraction and RT-qPCR

The extraction of total RNA from cell samples was carried out in accordance with the product instructions of TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA). RNA purity and quality were measured using a spectrophotometer. In accordance with the directions of the RT kit (Promega, USA), the RNA was reverse-transcribed into cDNA. RT-qPCR was performed on the synthesized cDNA using the SYBR Green kit (TIANGEN, Beijing). GAPDH and U6 served as internal reference genes. The relative expression levels of lncRNA, miRNA and mRNA were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt formula.

Western blot analysis

The collected cells were lysed using RIPA buffer (Solarbio, Beijing, China), and the protein concentration was quantified using a BCA assay kit (Solarbio). Equal amounts of protein samples were separated by sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (Solarbio) and transferred onto a polyvinylidene fluoride membrane (Beyotime, Shanghai, China). Following transfer, the membrane was blocked with 5% skimmed milk at room temperature for 2 h and then incubated with specific primary antibodies overnight at 4 °C. Primary antibodies against Bcl-2, Bax, NLRP3, Caspase-1, p53, p21, and the loading control β-actin were all obtained from Abcam (USA). Subsequently, the membrane was incubated with a horseradish peroxidase-conjugated secondary antibody at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit (Solarbio), and the band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

Bioinformatics analysis

The binding sites between RMRP and miR-206 were predicted using the Star Base online database. TargetScan, miRDB, TarBase, and miRWalk databases were combined to predict target genes, and Venn diagrams were used to determine their intersection. GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed on candidate target genes via the “Weishengxin” online platform, and a protein-protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed based on the STRING database. The binding site between miR-206 and its key target gene FN1 was predicted using the TargetScan database.

Luciferase reporter assay

The luciferase reporter gene assay was utilized to validate the interactions between RMRP and miR-206, and between miR-206 and FN1. Wild-type (WT) and mutant (MUT) RMRP and FN1 luciferase reporter vectors (Invitrogen) were constructed separately. Lipofectamine 3000 was applied to co-transfect cells with either WT-RMRP or MUT-RMRP along with miR-206 mimic/inhibitor. Similarly, cells were co-transfected with either WT-FN1 or MUT-FN1 along with miR-206 mimic/inhibitor. After 48 h of incubation, the cells were collected, and the luciferase activity of each group was measured using the Dual-Luciferase® Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA).

RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP)

The RIP experiment was performed according to the product instructions of the RIP Kit (Merck Millipore, DEU). H9c2 cells were collected and lysed with RIP lysis buffer, and the supernatant was retrieved. Magnetic beads were then added and incubated with mouse-derived Ago2 antibody (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) or control IgG antibody (Sigma-Aldrich, USA) at 4 °C overnight. The immunoprecipitated RNA was then isolated and analyzed by RT-qPCR.

Statistical analysis

Data were processed using GraphPad Prism 8.0 (GraphPad Software, Inc., USA) and SPSS 22.0 (SPSS, Inc., Chicago, USA). Data were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance was utilized to compare multiple groups, and the t-test was applied for two-group comparisons. A schematic diagram illustrating the experimental timeline and grouping was provided in Figure S1. P < 0.05 was defined as statistically significant difference.

Results

Role of Sev preconditioning on function, inflammation, and oxidative stress in H/R-induced cells

In this study, the H/R model was constructed, and all subsequent experiments were based on this model. As depicted in Fig. 1A, the impact on cell viability was assessed after H9c2 cells were treated with different concentrations of Sev (0.5%, 1.0%, 1.5%, and 2.0%). Cell viability increased in a concentration-dependent manner, with 2.0% Sev showing the most significant effect (P < 0.001). Therefore, 2.0% Sev was selected as the optimal Sev concentration for this research and used it for subsequent experiments. Furthermore, the effects of Sev and H/R treatment on H9c2 cells by detecting cell viability, apoptosis, inflammatory and apoptosis-related proteins levels, LDH levels, SOD activity, IL-6 and IL-10 levels. Compared to the control group, H/R injury reduced cell viability and increased apoptosis, while Sev preconditioning enhanced cell viability and reduced apoptosis (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B-C). As shown in Fig. 1D-E, the H/R cell model exhibited downregulated Bcl-2 protein levels and upregulated Bax, NLRP3, and Caspase-1 protein levels. Following Sev pretreatment, the expression trends of these proteins were reversed (P < 0.05). LDH measurements revealed that H/R injury markedly increased LDH levels, and Sev preconditioning reduced this elevation (P < 0.001, Fig. 1F). As illustrated in Fig. 1G, H/R injury significantly decreased SOD activity, while Sev preconditioning substantially increased it (P < 0.001). ELISA results indicated that H/R injury significantly increased levels of the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-6 and decreased levels of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10. Sev preconditioning reversed these changes (P < 0.001, Fig. 1H). These findings indicate that Sev preconditioning may protect against H/R-induced cellular injury by enhancing cell viability, inhibiting apoptosis, and modulating oxidative stress and inflammatory responses.

Effect of Sev preconditioning on RMRP expression in H/R-induced cells

To clarify the function of RMRP in H/R-induced cells following Sev preconditioning, we measured RMRP expression by RT-qPCR. As shown in Fig. 2A, preconditioning with different concentrations of Sev decreased RMRP expression in a concentration-dependent manner, with the lowest expression observed at 2.0% Sev (P < 0.001). In contrast to the control group, RMRP was remarkably elevated in H/R-induced cells and significantly reduced after Sev preconditioning (P < 0.001, Fig. 2B). Moreover, we discovered that RMRP overexpression upregulated RMRP levels in H9c2 cells, and further enhanced RMRP levels following H/R injury (P < 0.001, Fig. 2C-D). Nevertheless, RMRP overexpression reversed the Sev-induced reduction of RMRP in H/R-induced cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 2E).

Overexpression of RMRP reverses the protective effect of Sev on H/R-induced cells

To further investigate the protective role of RMRP in H/R-induced cells, we measured cell viability, apoptosis, inflammatory and apoptosis-related protein levels, oxidative stress levels, along with inflammatory factor levels. The results demonstrated that RMRP transfection notably reduced cell viability and increased apoptosis after H/R injury following Sev preconditioning compared with the H/R + Sev + oe-NC group (P < 0.01, Fig. 3A-B). In H/R cells pretreated with Sev, RMRP overexpression significantly weakened the protective effect of Sev on cell apoptosis. This was manifested as the decrease of Bcl-2 protein levels and increase of Bax, NLRP3, and Caspase-1 protein levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 3C-D). Similarly, RMRP overexpression reversed the inhibitory effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced oxidative stress, as evidenced by increased LDH and decreased SOD (P < 0.001, Fig. 3E-F). Additionally, the ELISA data in Fig. 3G showed that RMRP overexpression reversed the inhibitory effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced inflammatory, as indicated by increased IL-6 and decreased IL-10 (P < 0.001). In summary, the results suggest that RMRP overexpression impedes the protective effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced cells.

Validation of RMRP interaction with miR-206

This study examined the correlation between RMRP and miR-206 using luciferase reporter gene assay and RIP assay. The Star Base online database identified complementary sequences between RMRP and miR-206 (Fig. 4A). The luciferase reporter gene assay showed that miR-206 overexpression reduced luciferase activity in cells transfected with WT-RMRP, while miR-206 inhibition increased luciferase activity. However, neither manipulation affected cells transfected with MUT-RMRP (P < 0.001, Fig. 4B). RIP assay results revealed that miR-206 transfection significantly increased precipitated RMRP in the Ago2 antibody group compared to the IgG group, suggesting that RMRP associates with the Ago2 protein through miR-206 (P < 0.001, Fig. 4C). Additionally, RT-PCR results showed that miR-206 was significantly downregulated following H/R injury, and Sev preconditioning reversed this H/R-induced reduction (P < 0.001, Fig. 4D). Overexpression of miR-206 remarkably enhanced miR-206 levels in H9c2 cells (P < 0.001, Fig. 4E). Further research revealed that RMRP transfection suppressed miR-206, while miR-206 mimic transfection reversed the suppressive effect of RMRP overexpression on miR-206 levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 4F). Collectively, these findings demonstrate a direct binding interaction between RMRP and miR-206.

MiR-206 overexpression reverses the damaging effect of RMRP upregulation on H/R-induced cells

After transfecting RMRP, overexpression of miR-206 enhanced cell viability in Sev preconditioning H/R cells and reduced cell apoptosis (P < 0.05, Fig. 5A-B). In H/R cells pretreated with Sev, overexpression of RMRP and miR-206 significantly increased Bcl-2 protein levels while decreasing Bax, NLRP3, and Caspase-1 protein levels (P < 0.05, Fig. 5C-D). As depicted in Fig. 5E, overexpression of RMRP reduced the inhibitory effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced LDH release. However, further upregulation of miR-206 restored the inhibitory effect on LDH (P < 0.05). Overexpression of RMRP also weakened the promoting effect of Sev preconditioning on H/R-induced SOD activity. Transfection with miR-206 mimic restored the promoting effect on SOD (P < 0.001, Fig. 5F). Additionally, overexpression of RMRP diminished the inhibitory effect of Sev preconditioning on the H/R-induced inflammatory response. Upregulation of miR-206 decreased IL-6 levels and increased IL-10 levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 5G). In summary, overexpression of miR-206 reversed the detrimental effects of RMRP upregulation in H/R-induced cells.

Effects of the RMRP/miR-206 axis on the p53/p21 pathway in Sev-pretreated H/R cells

The study detected the protein expression of p53 and its downstream target p21 in each group via Western blot. As shown in Figure S2, Sev pretreatment significantly suppressed the H/R-induced upregulation of p53 and p21. In contrast, overexpression of RMRP promoted the upregulation of both genes (P < 0.001). Further upregulation of miR-206 inhibited this pathway, leading to decreased protein levels of p53 and p21 (P < 0.001).

Bioinformatics analysis

Potential target genes of miR-206 were predicted using the TargetScan, miRDB, TarBase, and miRWalk databases. A Venn diagram identified 145 overlapping genes from these databases (Fig. 6A). Further GO and KEGG enrichment analyses were performed on the overlapping target genes mentioned above (Fig. 6B-C). GO analysis indicated that target genes were significantly enriched in biological processes such as Golgi localization, negative regulation of muscle cell differentiation, and calcineurin-NFAT signaling cascade. In cellular components, they were significantly enriched in focal adhesion, cell-substrate junction, and transport vesicle. Regarding molecular functions, they were significantly enriched in cadherin binding, RNA polymerase II-specific DNA-binding transcription factor binding, and double-stranded RNA binding. KEGG pathway analysis revealed that these target genes primarily participate in the Adherens junction, PI3K-Akt signaling pathway, and Ras signaling pathway. Additionally, the PPI network was constructed using the STRING database (Fig. 6D), with the following parameters: number of nodes: 145; number of edges: 259; average node degree: 3.57; expected number of edges: 127; PPI enrichment P-value: <1.0e-16.

FN1 is a target of miR-206

The binding sequence of miR-206 to FN1 was shown in Fig. 7A. To validate this interaction, a dual luciferase reporter assay was performed. Results showed that co-transfection of WT-FN1 with miR-206 mimic suppressed luciferase activity compared to the control group, whereas co-transfection with miR-206 inhibitor enhanced luciferase activity (P < 0.001, Fig. 7B). Furthermore, FN1 expressions were upregulated in H/R-treated cells, and its expression was significantly suppressed following Sev pretreatment; however, overexpression of miR-206 reversed this inhibitory effect, leading to increased FN1 expressions (P < 0.001, Fig. 7C). The experimental results demonstrate the direct targeting of FN1 by miR-206.

Discussion

MIRI refers to a phenomenon where myocardial injury worsens upon the restoration of blood flow following myocardial ischemia [17]. This complex process involves multiple pathophysiologic mechanisms, such as oxidative stress response, inflammatory response, and cardiomyocyte apoptosis [18]. Sev has been discovered to possess protective effects against MIRI through its antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and anti-apoptotic effects, significantly improving MIRI progression [19]. Therefore, this paper explored how Sev preconditioning protects against MIRI by modulating the RMRP/miR-206 axis. The schematic diagram of the molecular pathway is shown in Figure S3.

In this research, we conducted in-vitro experiments using the H/R model to evaluate the protective mechanism of Sev preconditioning against MIRI. We assessed this by examining H9c2 cell viability and apoptosis. Compared to the control group, H/R injury decreased cell viability and increased apoptosis. However, Sev preconditioning reversed these changes, indicating that it inhibits myocardial injury. Similarly, Wang et al. found that Sev preconditioning elevated H9c2 cell viability and notably suppressed of H₂O₂-induced apoptosis [20]. LDH is a crucial indicator for assessing MIRI progression [21]. MIRI triggers an inflammatory response, releasing various inflammatory factors. As inflammatory response, inflammatory cells infiltrate myocardial tissue and generate reactive oxygen species. These directly damage cardiomyocytes and cause further LDH release [22]. Our preliminary research indicated that Sev preconditioning inhibited oxidative stress and inflammatory factors, as evidenced by decreased LDH, enhanced SOD, reduced IL-6, and increased IL-10. Consistent with Li et al., Sev preconditioning reduced LDH levels, boosted SOD activity, and suppressed IL-6 expression in mice following IRI [23].

Many studies have shown that ncRNAs a play a crucial role in MIRI. However, the function of RMRP and miR-206 in MIRI has not been studied. Thus, we evaluated the role of RMRP and miR-206 in protecting against MIRI after Sev preconditioning by establishing the H/R model. In this study, we found that RMRP and miR-206 were abnormally expressed in H/R-induced cells. Sev preconditioning reversed the increase in RMRP and the decrease in miR-206 following H/R injury. This suggests that Sev preconditioning can reduce MIRI by regulating the RMRP and miR-206 axis. We further confirmed that overexpression of RMRP reduced cell viability, increased apoptosis, raised LDH and IL-6 levels, and decreased SOD activity and IL-10 levels after Sev preconditioning following H/R injury. It demonstrates that overexpression of RMRP blocks the protective effect of Sev on cardiomyocyte injury. Moreover, RMRP expression was up-regulated in response to stimuli like ischemia, reperfusion, and H/R, which elevated apoptosis levels and worsened cell injury [24, 25].

To further explore the mechanism of RMRP in MIRI, we used the Star Base online database to detect complementary pairing sequences between RMRP and miR-206. Previous studies have shown that miR-206 plays a protective function in MIRI [26]. Yan et al. discovered that miR-206 reduced cardiomyocyte apoptosis and protected against acute MI injury by targeting PTP1B [27]. Moreover, Li et al. demonstrated that the upregulation of miR-206 decreased apoptosis, reduced IL-6 levels, and enhanced SOD activity. miR-206 also could reduce oxidative stress and ease MIRI by targeting MEKK1 [28]. As expected, our data suggested that miR-206 was downregulated in H/R-induced cells, while Sev preconditioning increased miR-206 expression. After the transfection of RMRP, overexpression of miR-206 elevated cell viability, reduced apoptosis levels, lowered LDH and IL-6 levels, and raised SOD activity and IL-10 levels. It implies that overexpression of miR-206 can reverse the damage to H/R-induced cells caused by upregulating RMRP.

In addition, this study identified potential target genes of miR-206 through bioinformatics analysis and further validated FN1 as its direct target. FN1, a key extracellular matrix protein, is involved in cell adhesion, migration, and tissue repair [29]. The experimental results confirmed that FN1 expression was significantly upregulated following H/R injury, while Sev pretreatment or miR-206 overexpression effectively suppressed FN1 expression. This suggests that miR-206 may alleviate MI by targeting FN1, thereby reducing abnormal extracellular matrix accumulation and inflammatory responses. Furthermore, KEGG enrichment analysis revealed that miR-206 target genes are significantly enriched in the PI3K-Akt and Ras signaling pathways, which are closely associated with cell survival, proliferation, and inflammatory responses [30, 31]. We speculate that miR-206 may exert its protective effects by modulating these critical pathways, collectively contributing to the alleviation of MIRI.

This study revealed that Sev pretreatment exerted its effects in MIRI by regulating the RMRP/miR-206 axis. Notably, a recent study suggested that overexpression of RMRP might exert a protective effect in MIRI by suppressing p53 activity, through the PFN1/p53 signaling pathway [32]. p53 is a nuclear transcription factor with pro-apoptotic properties [8]. This hypothesis provides an intriguing complement and contrast to our observation that “RMRP knockdown exerts a protective effect” at the level of p53 regulation. In our model, Sev-induced downregulation of RMRP alleviated the inhibition of miR-206. As a known tumor suppressor, miR-206 has multiple predicted target genes closely associated with cell proliferation, survival, and stress responses. We speculate that the elevation of miR-206 may directly or indirectly affect the stability or transcriptional activity of p53. Our preliminary protein level detection results support this association: Sev pretreatment suppressed H/R-induced p53 accumulation, and this effect could be reversed by RMRP overexpression. Furthermore, upregulating miR-206 reduced p53 protein levels. Based on the literature, we speculate that RMRP may play a complex role in MIRI, with its ultimate impact on p53 potentially depending on cell type, stress intensity, or specific upstream signaling pathways.

Further, this study found that following Sev pretreatment, levels of the proinflammatory cytokine IL-6 decreased while levels of the anti-inflammatory cytokine IL-10 increased. This phenomenon suggests that its anti-inflammatory mechanism may involve profound regulation of classical inflammatory signaling pathways. In recent years, NLRP3 inflammasome-mediated Caspase-1 activation has been demonstrated to be a core component of inflammatory injury in MIRI [33]. Activated Caspase-1 is responsible for cleaving pro-IL-1β and pro-IL-18 into biologically active mature forms, driving intense inflammatory responses [34, 35]. Research has found that NLRP3 inflammasomes play a significant role in cardiovascular diseases, exacerbating inflammation and IRI [36]. Combining these findings with our discoveries regarding the RMRP/miR-206 axis, we propose a plausible inference: Sev pretreatment may indirectly inhibit NLRP3 inflammasome assembly or activation by downregulating RMRP and upregulating miR-206. This, in turn, leads to reduced Caspase-1 activity, ultimately decreasing the maturation and release of proinflammatory cytokines.

This study also has certain limitations. First, the research only explored the interaction between RMRP and miR-206 and has not functionally validate the downstream targets of miR-206. Second, due to time and resource constraints, the study was conducted using in vitro cell models only. Although cell experiments have preliminarily revealed the regulatory role of the RMRP/miR-206 axis during sevoflurane pretreatment, the lack of validation in in vivo animal models may limit the translation of these findings to clinical settings. Based on this, we plan to establish rat or mouse models of myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury to systematically evaluate the functional role of this axis in sevoflurane-mediated cardiac protection within an in vivo setting, thereby providing critical evidence for clinical translation. Simultaneously, we will further investigate the downstream target genes of the RMRP/miR-206 axis and their regulatory networks to comprehensively elucidate its mechanism of action. Another limitation of the study lies in the issue of the availability of the original data. The untrimmed original scan files from the immunoblotting experiments cannot be obtained because these files were processed according to the standard image preparation procedure at the time of publication. We would like to emphasize that this does not affect the validity of the conclusions: all quantitative analyses are based on real and complete experimental data.

In summary, this study demonstrates that Sev preconditioning protects cells by alleviating MIRI through regulating the RMRP/miR-206 axis. These findings offer new directions for treating MIRI.

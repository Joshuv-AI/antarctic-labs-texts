Quantitative Quantum Zeno and Strong Damping Limits in Strong Topology
Robert Salzmann

Abstract
Frequent applications of a mixing quantum operation to a quantum system slow down its time evolution and eventually drive it into the invariant subspace of the named operation. We prove this phenomenon, the quantum Zeno effect, and its continuous variant, strong damping, in a unified way for infinite-dimensional open quantum systems, while merely demanding that the respective mixing convergence holds pointwise for all states. Both results are quantitative in the following sense: Given the speed of convergence for the mixing limits, we can derive bounds on the convergence speed for the corresponding quantum Zeno and strong damping limits. We apply our results to prove quantum Zeno and strong damping limits for the photon loss channel with an explicit bound on the convergence speed.

1.1
Summary of main results

Instead of focusing on the special case of
M
being a quantum operation on the Banach space of trace class operators
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
we allow for any contraction
M
on some general Banach space
X
, i.e. with operator norm satisfying
\|M\|=\sup_{\|x\|=1}\|Mx\|\leq 1
. Moreover, for proving the strong damping limit we consider
\mathcal{K}
to be a possibly unbounded generator of strongly continuous contraction semigroup
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
on some Banach space
X
(see Section
2.3
for more details).

We prove quantum Zeno- and strong damping limits in a unified fashion, while going beyond the above-mentioned spectral condition which most of the previous results in the literature relied on. Instead we merely assume that the mixing limits (
1.2
) and (
1.4
) hold in strong topology, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}x=Px,\quad\quad\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}x=Px,

(1.6)

for all
x\in X
and some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
In both cases we find for all
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
convergence to an effective dynamics as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}x=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}Px,\quad\quad\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}x=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}Px,

(1.7)

for all
x\in X
and
t>0,
compare Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
for formal statements of the results. Furthermore, both results on Zeno- and strong damping limits, provide bounds on the speed of convergence given control of the convergence speeds of the respective mixing limit in (
1.6
).

Due to this novel bound on the speed of convergence, our result on the quantum Zeno limit, Theorem
4.1
, can be seen as an improvement over
[
BDS21
, Theorem 2]
, which also applied for quantum operations
M
satisfying the mixing limit (
1.6
) in strong topology. Furthermore, Theorem
4.2
is, to the best of our knowledge, the first result of strong damping with unbounded generators
\mathcal{K}
and also without any spectral condition for the semigroup
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}.

For the unified proof of Zeno- and strong damping limits we consider a novel operator product, which we call the
generalised binomial product
. We analyse its asymptotic behaviour in Theorem
3.1
by providing a quantitative version of the pertubabition series technique used in
[
BDS21
, Theorem 2]
. We then show how both the Zeno product

and the damped evolution operator

can be written as such an operator product and conclude their respective convergence for

and

from the asympotics of the generalised binomial product.

To illustrate our results, we apply them to show Zeno- and strong damping limits for

In this section we review some basic definitions and facts about operators on complex Banach spaces, all of which can be found in one of the books

Let

be some complex Banach space with norm denoted by
\|x\|
for

For a linear operator
T:\mathcal{D}(T)\to X
with domain
\mathcal{D}(T)\subseteq X
we write

\displaystyle\ker(T)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathcal{D}(T)\,\Big|\,Tx=0\right\},

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(T)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{y\in X\,\Big|\,y=Tx,\,x\in\mathcal{D}(T)\right\}

to denote the

and

or

This is equivalent to say that the

\displaystyle\Gamma(T)\coloneqq\left\{(x,Tx)\Big|\,x\in\mathcal{D}(T)\right\}\subseteq X\times X

is closed or
\mathcal{D}(T)
equipped with the

\|x\|_{T}\coloneqq\|x\|+\|Tx\|
is complete.

We denote by

the set of bounded linear operators on

.
{\mathcal{B}}(X)
is a Banach space itself equipped with the

which for

is given by

\displaystyle\|T\|\coloneqq\sup_{\|x\|=1}\left\|Tx\right\|.

We denote by
{\rm{id}}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
the identity operator acting on

We call
T\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)

We say a map

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\to{\mathcal{B}}(X)

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle\mapsto T_{a},

with

being some topological space, is

is continuous, i.e. with respect to the operator norm in
{\mathcal{B}}(X)
, and

if for all

the map

is continuous. Similarly, we say a sequence
\left(T_{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)

For

we say a complex number

6

6

In this section we review some basic results about fixed point spaces of contractions on a Banach space

We say
x\in X
is a
fixed point
of a contraction
7
7

7

More generally, all statements in this section remain true if
M
is not a contraction but only

, i.e.
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|M^{n}\|<\infty

M\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
if
Mx=x
and hence the

of

shall be denoted by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(M)\coloneqq\Big\{x\in X\Big|Mx=x\Big\}=\ker\left({\rm{id}}-M\right).

(2.1)

By boundedness and linearity of
M
this denotes a closed subspace of
X.
We can define a projection onto
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
which, however, on a general Banach space might be unbounded. To obtain a bounded projection we may restrict to the so-called
mean ergodic subspace,
defined below. Moreover, this projection is then given by an explicit limit evolving the contraction
M.

For that construction, consider for
n\in\mathbb{N}
the
Cesàro mean

\displaystyle A_{n}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}M^{k}.

(2.2)

The

of
M
, which is the subspace of
X
on which (

) has a strong limit, shall be denoted by

X_{\text{me}}(M)=\Big\{x\in X\Big|\,x_{\infty}\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}x\text{ exists}\Big\}.

Using that the operators
A_{n}
are uniformly bounded, in fact even contractions, for all
n\in\mathbb{N},
it is easy to see that

is a closed subspace of

For
x\in X_{\text{me}}(M)
we immediately see that

which follows by

\displaystyle Mx_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}M^{k+1}x=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}M^{k}x=x_{\infty},

where for the second to last equality we have used that

is a contraction. This then immediately also implies

for all

and hence

Hence, we can define the linear map

\displaystyle A_{\infty}:X_{\text{me}}(M)

\displaystyle\to X_{\text{me}}(M)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto x_{\infty}=\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}x.

Furthermore, we have already established

) and the other is immediate by definition.
Note that since

we see by definition that
A_{\infty}
is a projection, i.e.
A^{2}_{\infty}=A_{\infty}.
Moreover,
A_{\infty}
is in fact a bounded operator, i.e.
A_{\infty}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X_{\text{me}}(M)),
as for
x\in X_{\text{me}}(M)
we have

\displaystyle\left\|A_{\infty}x\right\|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|M^{k}x\right\|\leq\|x\|.

By the same argument as in (
2.3
) we see that also
M
leaves its mean ergodic subspace invariant, i.e.
MX_{\text{me}}(M)\subseteq X_{\text{me}}(M),
and for all
x\in X_{\text{me}}(M)
we have
A_{\infty}Mx=A_{\infty}x.

Yosida’s Mean Ergodic Theorem
(cf.
[
Kre85
, Chapter 2]
or
[
Yos68
, Chapter VIII.3.]
) gives the following complete characterisation of the mean ergodic subspace:

\displaystyle X_{\text{me}}(M)=\mathcal{F}(M)\oplus\overline{\operatorname{ran}\left({\rm{id}}-M\right)}.

It is easy to see that
A_{\infty}(\overline{\operatorname{ran}\left({\rm{id}}-M\right)}\,)=\{0\},
and the above shows that
A_{\infty}
is the bounded projection onto the fixed point space
{\mathcal{F}}(M),
i.e. to summarise

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}(A_{\infty})

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}(M),\quad\quad\quad\quad\ker(A_{\infty})=\overline{\operatorname{ran}\left({\rm{id}}-M\right)},

\displaystyle A^{2}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=A_{\infty},\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,A_{\infty}M=MA_{\infty}=A_{\infty}.

We call an operator
M

mean ergodic
, if
X_{\text{me}}(M)=X.
Moreover, if
\lim_{n\to\infty}A_{n}=A_{\infty}
holds true even in operator norm, we say
M
is
uniformly mean ergodic
.
Lin’s uniform ergodic theorem

[
Lin74
]
gives full characterisation of uniformly mean ergodic contractions. It states that the following are equivalent for a contraction
M:

1.

M
is uniformly mean ergodic, i.e.
A_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}M^{k}
converges in operator norm as
n\to\infty
.

2.

\operatorname{ran}({\rm{id}}-M)
is closed and
X=\mathcal{F}(M)\oplus\operatorname{ran}\left({\rm{id}}-M\right).

3.

\operatorname{ran}({\rm{id}}-M)
is closed.

4.

\operatorname{ran}({\rm{id}}-M)^{2}
is closed.

We say
M
is
strongly power convergent
if

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}x=Px

(2.4)

for all
x\in X
and some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).

In that case we clearly also have that
M
is mean ergodic and
P
is the projection onto the fixed point space
{\mathcal{F}}(M)
, i.e.
P=A_{\infty}.

If
\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}=P
holds even true in operator norm, we say
M
is
uniformly power convergent
. In
[
BDS21
, Corolloray 3.2]
gives a characterisation of uniformly power convergent contractions. In particular, we see that if a contraction is uniformly power convergent, it already gives that the convergence
\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}=P
happens exponentially fast. Moreover, uniform power convergence is also equivalent to a certain spectral gap condition on
M,
which was at the core of most previous proofs of the quantum Zeno limit.

2.3
Dynamical semigroups

In the following we recall some general concepts from semigroup theory (see
[
EN00
]
for more details).
Let
X
be a Banach space: we say
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)
is an
one-parameter semigroup
if

1.

T(t)T(s)=T(t+s),
for all
t,s\geq 0,

2.

T(0)={\rm{id}}
.

A one-parameter semigroup is be
uniformly- or norm continuous
if it is at
t=0,
i.e.

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|T(t)-{\rm{id}}\|=0.

On the other hand, a semigroup is
strongly continuous
if for all
x\in X
we find

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|(T(t)-{\rm{id}})x\|=0.

For any such semigroup, we can define the densely-defined and closed
[
EN00
, Chapter II Theorem 1.4]
generator
\mathcal{L}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}x\coloneqq\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}T(t)x\coloneqq\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{T(t)-{\rm{id}}}{t\,}x

(2.5)

for all
x
in the domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subseteq X
, which is the set of
x
for which the strong limit on the right hand side of (
2.5
) exists. The generator is bounded if and only if the semigroup is uniformly continuous, in which case

T(t)=e^{t\mathcal{L}}

with right hand side being in terms of the usual exponential series.
For
contraction semigroups
, i.e. semigroups satisfying
\|T(t)\|\leq 1
for all
t\geq 0
, we can recover the semigroup from its generator as follows: The spectrum of the generator
\mathcal{L}
of a contraction semigroup is contained in the left half plane of
{\mathbb{C}}
and in addition the resolvents satisfy the bound
[
EN00
, Chapter II Theorem
3.5
]

\|\lambda\left(\lambda-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1}\|\leq 1,\quad\text{ for all }\lambda>0.

(2.6)

Hence, for each
s\in(0,\infty)
we can define the
s^{th}

Yosida approximant
of the generator by

\mathcal{L}_{s}=s\mathcal{L}\left(s-\mathcal{L}\right)^{-1},

(2.7)

which are bounded operators satisfying
\|\mathcal{L}_{s}\|\leq s
and in addition

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}x\xrightarrow[s\to\infty]{}\mathcal{L}x,\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}).

From the Yosida approximants, the semigroup can be recovered as the strong limit

\lim_{s\to\infty}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{s}}x=T(t)x=:e^{t\mathcal{L}}x\quad\forall\,x\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).

For
\mathcal{K}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})\subseteq X
being some generator of a strongly continuous contraction semigroup and
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
being some perturbation, also
\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
generates of a strongly continuous semigroup
\left(e^{t(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}\right)_{t\geq 0}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)

[
EN00
, Chapter III Theorem 1.3]
. Moreover, it satisfies the norm bound

\displaystyle\left\|e^{t(\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}\right\|\leq e^{t\|\mathcal{L}\|}

(2.8)

for all
t\geq 0
.

We say a contraction semigroup
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is
strongly mixing
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}x=Px

(2.9)

for all
x\in X
and some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
Similarly to the discussion of strongly power convergent contractions in Section
2.2
, it is easy to see that by (
2.9
) we directly get
e^{t\mathcal{K}}P=Pe^{t\mathcal{K}}=P
for all
t\geq 0
and by that also
P^{2}=P.
More precisely, (
2.9
) gives equality of all fixed point spaces, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}}(e^{t\mathcal{K}})={\mathcal{F}}(e^{s\mathcal{K}})
for all
t,s\geq 0
and that
P
is the projection onto that fixed point space
\operatorname{ran}(P)={\mathcal{F}}(e^{t\mathcal{K}}),
which equivalently is also given by
\ker(\mathcal{K}).
8
8

8

Note that in general we have
\bigcap_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{F}}(e^{t\mathcal{K}})=\ker(\mathcal{K}).
To see this note first that every
x\in\bigcap_{t\geq 0}{\mathcal{F}}(e^{t\mathcal{K}})
we clearly have
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{K}x=0
by simply calculating the derivative as in (
2.5
). For the other inclusion note for
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
with
\mathcal{K}x=0
and
t\geq 0
we have
e^{t\mathcal{K}}x=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{d}{ds}e^{s\mathcal{K}}x+x=\int_{0}^{t}e^{s\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{K}x+x=x.

Furthermore, we say the semigroup
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is
uniformly mixing
if the convergence in (
2.9
) does not only happen but also in uniform topology, i.e.
\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}=P
in operator norm. In
[
Sal23
, Corollary 4.1.2]
it is shown that in this case the convergence, in fact, happens already exponentially fast.

2.4
Operators, states and channels on Hilbert spaces

For some separable and complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
we consider certain Banach spaces consisting of operators on
\mathcal{H}
. To be able to distinguish the identity on
\mathcal{H}
with the one on the corresponding Banach space, we denote it by
\mathbbm{1}
instead of
{\rm{id}}.

We denote the
support
of a linear operator
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
by
\operatorname{supp}(x)\coloneqq\ker(x)^{\perp}.

A Banach space which is of particular importance to us is
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}),
the space of
trace class operators
on
\mathcal{H},
which is defined to be the set of
x\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
such that
\mathrm{Tr}|x|<\infty.
It is in fact a Banach space equipped with the
trace norm

\displaystyle\left\|x\right\|_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{Tr}|x|=\mathrm{Tr}\sqrt{x^{*}x}.

We say
\rho
is a
quantum state
(or often simply state in the following) if
\rho\geq 0
and it has unit trace
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)=1.

We say a state
\rho
is
pure
if it is a rank-1 projection and can hence be written as
\rho=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
for some normalised
\psi\in\mathcal{H}.

For
\rho
and
\sigma
being states, we denote the
trace distance
by
\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}\in[0,1].
It can be written as the variational expression

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho-\sigma\right\|_{1}=\sup_{\Lambda\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H}),\,0\leq\Lambda\leq\mathbbm{1}}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Lambda\left(\rho-\sigma\right)\right)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\pi_{+}\left(\rho-\sigma\right)\right),

where we denoted the orthogonal projection onto the support of
\rho-\sigma
by
\pi_{+}.
Moreover, for two pure states
|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|
and
|\varphi\rangle\!\langle\varphi|
the trace distance has the form

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\||\phi\rangle\!\langle\phi|-|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\right\|_{1}=\sqrt{1-|\langle\psi,\varphi\rangle|^{2}}.

(2.10)

We say an operator
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
is
positive
, if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\rho)\geq 0,\quad\text{ for all }\rho\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}),\text{ with }\rho\geq 0

Any positive linear map
\mathcal{N}
is in particular hermiticity preserving, i.e.
\mathcal{N}(x^{*})=\mathcal{N}(x)^{*}
for all
x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}).

Furthermore,
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
is called
completely positive
if
\left(\mathcal{N}\otimes{\rm{id}}_{d}\right)\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d}))
is positive for all
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
where we have denoted the identity map on the
d
-dimensional complex square matrices
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C}^{d})=\mathbb{C}^{d\times d}
by
{\rm{id}}_{d}
.

\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
is
trace-preserving
if for all
x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
we have
{\rm Tr}(\mathcal{N}(x))={\rm Tr}(x)
and
trace non-increasing
if
{\rm Tr}(\mathcal{N}(x))\leq{\rm Tr}(x)
for all
x\geq 0
.

We call a completely positive map
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
a
quantum operation
if it is trace non-increasing, and a
quantum channel
if it is trace-preserving. The set of quantum channels on
\mathcal{H}
, or often also simply channels for simplicity, is denoted by
{\rm{CPTP}}(\mathcal{H}).

Every completely positive map
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
can by
Stinespring’s dilation theorem
be written in the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(x)=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left(VxV^{*}\right),

(2.11)

for some linear bounded operator
V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E},

\mathcal{H}_{E}
being some
environment Hilbert space
and
\mathrm{Tr}_{E}
denoting the
partial trace
on
\mathcal{H}_{E}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{N}
is a quantum operation if and only if
V^{*}V\leq\mathbbm{1}
and a quantum channel if and only if
V^{*}V=\mathbbm{1}.
In the latter case
V
is therefore an isometry, which we henceforth call the
Stinespring isometry
of
\mathcal{N}.

Equivalently to (
2.11
) a completely positive map
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
can be written in
Kraus decomposition
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\mathcal{N}}}K_{i}xK^{*}_{i},

(2.12)

where
r_{\mathcal{N}}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
is called the
Kraus rank
of
\mathcal{N}
and
\left(K_{i}\right)_{i=1}^{r_{\mathcal{N}}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
are the so-called
Kraus operators
of
\mathcal{N}.
Note, for
r_{\mathcal{N}}=\infty
we have convergence of the series
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}K^{*}_{i}K_{i}
in strong topology which implies convergence of the series in (
2.12
) in trace norm. Moreover,
\mathcal{N}
is a quantum operation if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\mathcal{N}}}K^{*}_{i}K_{i}\leq\mathbbm{1}
and a quantum channel if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{r_{\mathcal{N}}}K^{*}_{i}K_{i}=\mathbbm{1}

For
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
we can denote the
diamond norm
or
completely bounded trace norm
by

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{N}\right\|_{\diamond}\coloneqq\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N},x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d})\,\|x\|_{1}=1}\left\|(\mathcal{N}\otimes{\rm{id}}_{d})(x)\right\|_{1}.

Note by the above we can easily see that any quantum operation
\mathcal{N}
satisfies
\left\|\mathcal{N}\right\|_{\diamond}\leq 1.
In particular
\mathcal{N}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}))
is a contraction as it has operator norm

\|\mathcal{N}\|=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\,\|x\|_{1}=1}\|\mathcal{N}(x)\|_{1}\leq 1.

Lastly, using the terminology of Section
2.3
,
we call an one-parameter semigroup of quantum channels
\left(\mathcal{N}(t)\right)_{t\geq 0}
a
quantum dynamical semigroup.

3
Generalised binomial product

In this section we consider an operator generalisation of the product
\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n},
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},
and prove convergence of named generalisation reminiscent of the well-known limit expression of exponential function

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n}=e^{\lambda}.

(3.1)

For that, let
R\subseteq\mathbbm{R}
be some compact interval and
\left(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N},r\in R}
be a family of bounded operators. We assume that, uniformly in
r\in R
, the sequence
(\mathcal{L}_{n,r})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in norm to some operator
\mathcal{L}_{r}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
. In other words we can denote some (abstract) speed of convergence
(s^{\prime}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbbm{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r}\right\|\leq s^{\prime}_{n}

(3.2)

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}^{\prime}=0.
We additionally assume that the map
r\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{r}
is strongly continuous.

Furthermore, let
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
and let
\left(M_{r}\right)_{r\in R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)
be a family of contractions which is, uniformly in
r\in R^{\prime}
, strong power convergent, i.e. for all
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}_{r}x=Px,

(3.3)

for some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
As discussed in Section
2.2
, this already gives that
P
is the projection onto the fixed point space
{\mathcal{F}}(M_{r})
and, in particular equality of all fixed point spaces, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}}(M_{r})={\mathcal{F}}(M_{r^{\prime}})
for all
r,r^{\prime}\in R^{\prime}
. Defining the sequence
(s_{n}(x))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbbm{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)\coloneqq\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n,r\in R^{\prime}}\|(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P)x\|,

(3.4)

we clearly have

\displaystyle\left\|(M_{r}^{n}-P)x\right\|\leq s_{n}(x),

(3.5)

and
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}(x)=0
. Moreover, the map
n\mapsto s_{n}(x)
is by definition monotonically decreasing and
x\mapsto s_{n}(x)
is continuous.
9
9

9

To see continuity of
x\mapsto s_{n}(x)=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n,r\in R^{\prime}}\|(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P)x\|
use for
x,y\in X
with
s_{n}(x)\geq s_{n}(y)
that
s_{n}(x)-s_{n}(y)\leq\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n,r\in R^{\prime}}\left(\|(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P)x\|-\|(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P)y\|\right)\leq 2\|x-y\|.

For such families
\left(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N},r\in R}
and
\left(M_{r}\right)_{r\in R^{\prime}},
we can now introduce the
generalised binomial product

\displaystyle\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}

(3.6)

and analyse its limit in the strong topology as
n
tends to infinity. In Theorem
3.1
below, we prove that this limit is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}x=e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}Px,

(3.7)

for all
x\in X.

Moreover, in (
3.12
) we provide a bound on the speed of convergence in terms of the convergence speeds
s_{n}^{\prime}
and
s_{n}(x)
defined in (
3.2
) and (
3.4
).

To give some intuition, let us try to connect (
3.7
) back to (
3.1
).
Consider the special case of
M_{r}=P
and
\mathcal{L}_{n,r}=\mathcal{L}
for some projection
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
and some operator
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
In that case (
3.7
) can be seen to be a direct consequence of (
3.1
). To convince ourselves, note first that

\displaystyle\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)^{n}=\left(P+\frac{P\mathcal{L}P}{n}\right)^{n}+\,\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right),

(3.8)

which can be seen by using
({\rm{id}}-P)\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)=\mathcal{O}(1/n)
,
\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)({\rm{id}}-P)=\mathcal{O}(1/n)
and
\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)({\rm{id}}-P)\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)=\mathcal{O}(1/n^{2})
and hence the sum of all error terms in (
3.8
) is of order
\mathcal{O}(1/n).

We can now employ the functional calculus for the operator
P\mathcal{L}P
on the Banach space
\operatorname{ran}(P)=\ker({\rm{id}}-P)
together with the sequence of functions
f_{n}(\lambda)=\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n},
for which
f_{n}(P\mathcal{L}P)=\left(P+\frac{P\mathcal{L}P}{n}\right)^{n},
giving in operator norm

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(P+\frac{\mathcal{L}}{n}\right)^{n}=e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P,

(3.9)

by using the continuity of the holomorphic functional calculus.
10
10

10

Note that the convergence
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{\lambda}{n}\right)^{n}=e^{\lambda}
is uniform over
\lambda\in K\subseteq\mathbb{C}
for
K
compact.

The reason for considering the generalised binomial product (
3.6
) is by that both the Zeno product,
\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}
, as well as the damped evolution operator,
e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})},
can be treated in a unified way as they can both be written in the form of (
3.6
). Hence, in Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
below, we see how the Zeno- and strong damping limit, respectively, can be deduced from Theorem
3.1
. Here, for the Zeno product, the
r
dependence of
\mathcal{L}_{n,r}
and
M_{r}
is trivial, i.e.
|R|=1
, whereas a non-trivial
r
dependence becomes necessary to also cover the strong damping limit.

Theorem 3.1
(Asymptotics of the generalised binomial product)

Let
R
be a compact interval and
(\mathcal{L}_{n,r})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)
be operators which, uniformly in
r\in R
, converge to some operator
\mathcal{L}_{r}
. Furthermore, assume that
r\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{r}
is strongly continuous.

Let
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
and
\left(M_{r}\right)_{r\in R^{\prime}}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(X)
be contractions such that for all
x\in X

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}_{r}x=Px,

(3.10)

uniformly in
r\in R^{\prime}
and for some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}x=e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}Px

(3.11)

uniformly in
r\in R^{\prime}
. Moreover, using the notation established in (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), we have

\left\|\left(\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}-e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq C\Big(s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})\Big).

(3.12)

for all
r\in R^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{N}=(N_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some
C\geq 1
independent of
n
and
\mathbf{N}.
Here, we have defined

s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})\coloneqq\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right).

(3.13)

Remark 3.2

Although the particular form of the bound on the speed of convergence

s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})
in (
3.12
) is rather involved, it can be shown to be well-behaved for some interesting examples - see Section
5
below where the respective Zeno and strong damping limits are discussed. Here, the idea is that if we have, for a given example, explicit control of the
s_{n}^{\prime}
and
s_{n}(x)
defined in (
3.2
) and (
3.4
), this leads to an explicit bound on the speed of convergence for the generalised binomial product.

Note that by the definition of
s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}),
there is a trade-off between the sequence
\mathbf{N}=\left(N_{l}\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}},
which can be choosen freely, and
n.
Hence, the idea is to choose an
n
-dependent
\mathbf{N}(n)=\left(N_{l}(n)\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
in such a way that both summands in (
3.13
) decay as
n\to\infty
, i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{N_{l}(n)}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)=0\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{N_{l}(n)}{n}=0,

and we furthermore have good control on the respective speeds of convergence to get

\lim_{n\to\infty}s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}(n))=0.

Since the convergence (
6.10
) is assumed to happen in strong topology, and not in uniform topology, note that due to the supremum over
l\in\mathbb{N}
in (
3.13
)
\lim_{n\to\infty}s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}(n))=0
might not be satisfied in general. In that case Theorem
3.1
still gives convergence of the generalised binomial product, but without an explicit (and decaying) bound on the speed of convergence.

We defer the proof of Theorem

4
Quantum Zeno- and strong damping limits from Theorem

Using Theorem

4.0.1
Quantum Zeno limit

For the Zeno product, we typically only consider the case

In that case, using the notation of (

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n}\|(M^{n^{\prime}}-P)x\|.

(4.1)

We choose
\mathcal{L}_{n}
to be the

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}\coloneqq n\left(e^{t\mathcal{L}/n}-{\rm{id}}\right).

(4.2)

By that choice, the Zeno product can be written as a generalised binomial product of

Theorem 4.1
(Quantum Zeno limit)

Let

be a contraction which satisfies the following: For all

(4.3)

for some operator

. Then for all
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
and

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}x=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}Px.

(4.4)

Moreover, using the notation established in (
4.1

\left\|\left(\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq C\,s^{\sup}_{n}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N}),

(4.5)

for all
\mathbf{N}=(N_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some
C\geq 1
independent of

and
\mathbf{N}.
Here, we have denoted

s^{\sup}_{n}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N})\coloneqq\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|t\mathcal{L}\|^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((t\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right).

(4.6)

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

In the following we omit the

. We define for
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}\coloneqq n\left(e^{\mathcal{L}/n}-\mathbbm{1}\right).

Note that we can write

\displaystyle n\left(e^{\mathcal{L}/n}-\mathbbm{1}\right)-\mathcal{L}=n\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}e^{s\mathcal{L}/n}\,ds-\mathcal{L}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(e^{s\mathcal{L}/n}-\mathbbm{1}\right)\mathcal{L}\,ds=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{s^{\prime}s\mathcal{L}/n}\mathcal{L}^{2}\,ds^{\prime}ds,

(4.8)

With that we can write the Zeno product as

\left(Me^{\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}=\left(M+\frac{M\mathcal{L}_{n}}{n}\right)^{n}.

(4.9)

) then directly follows from Theorem

where we have used the fact that, due to

4.3
), we have

) is immediate from (

) in Theorem

4.0.2
Strong damping limit

Let
\mathcal{K}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})\subseteq X
being the generator of a strongly continuous contraction group,
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, which is strongly mixing as defined and discussed around (
2.9
), i.e.

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}x=Px

(4.10)

for all
x\in X
and some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
.

Again, similarly to the definition of
s_{n}(x)
in (
3.3
), we define some
(abstract) speed of convergence

(v_{\gamma}(x))_{\gamma\geq 0}\subseteq\mathbbm{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle v_{\gamma}(x):=\sup_{\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma}\left\|\left(e^{\gamma^{\prime}\mathcal{K}}-P\right)x\right\|,

(4.11)

which clearly satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}-P\right)x\right\|\leq v_{\gamma}(x)

(4.12)

and
\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}v_{\gamma}(x)=0.
Moreover, we have by definition that the map
\gamma\mapsto v_{\gamma}(x)
is monotonically decreasing and
x\mapsto v_{\gamma}(x)
is continuous.

For
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
and
\gamma\geq 0
we want to analyse the perturbed dynamics generated by

\displaystyle\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}\quad\quad\text{with}\quad\quad\mathcal{D}(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K}),

(4.13)

in the strong interaction limit
\gamma\to\infty.
Note, as discussed in Section
2.3
, the operator (
4.13
) in fact generates a strongly continuous semigroup
\left(e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}\right)_{t\geq 0}.
In the proof of Theorem
4.2
below, the key step for understanding the behaviour for large
\gamma
is to rewrite
e^{t(nr\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in R^{\prime}\coloneqq[1,2]\subseteq[0,2]=:R
as a generalised binomial product (
3.6
) by choosing appropriate families of operators
\left(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N},r\in R}
and
\left(M_{r}\right)_{r\in R^{\prime}}.
In particular, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\coloneqq n\left(e^{t(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}-e^{tr\mathcal{K}}\right),

as well as the contractions
M_{r}=e^{tr\mathcal{K}}
, and find

\displaystyle e^{t(nr\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{t(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}\right)^{n}=\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}.

We can then use Theorem
3.1
to prove strong convergence in the limit
n\to\infty.
The freedom in choosing the parameter
r
and the fact that the convergence in the result of Theorem
3.1
happens uniformly in
r
, then also enables us to cover the more general convergence as
\gamma\to\infty.

Moreover, as discussed in more detail in the proof of Theorem
4.2
below, we see that
\mathcal{L}_{n,r}
converges in operator norm to some
\mathcal{L}_{r}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
which satisfies
P\mathcal{L}_{r}P=tP\mathcal{L}P.
Using the notation of (
3.2
), we show that we have good control on the corresponding speed of convergence as
s_{n}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(1/n).
Similarly as for the quantum Zeno limit discussed in Theorem
4.1
, this leads to a simplified bound on the speed of convergence for the strong damping limit compared to the one presented in (
3.12
) in Theorem
4.1
.

We are now ready to state and prove the result on the strong damping limit, outlined above, in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
(Strong damping limit)

Let
\mathcal{K}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})\subseteq X
be the generator of a strongly continuous contraction semigroup which satisfies for all
x\in X

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}x=Px,

(4.14)

for some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
. Then for all
t>0

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}x=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}Px.

(4.15)

Moreover, using the notation in (
4.11
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq Cv^{\sup}_{\gamma}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N}).

(4.16)

for all
\mathbf{N}=\left(N_{l}\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
and some
C\geq 1
independent of
\gamma
and
\mathbf{N}.
Here, we have denoted

\displaystyle v^{\sup}_{\gamma}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N})\coloneqq\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|t\mathcal{L}\|^{-l+1}v_{N_{l}}\left((t\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\|x\|\right).

(4.17)

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

In the following we will omit the
t>0
from the notation of Theorem
4.2
by redefining
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{L}
to be
t\mathcal{K}
and
t\mathcal{L}
respectively.
Let
r\in R:=[0,2]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
and define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\coloneqq n\left(e^{r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n}-e^{r\mathcal{K}}\right).

Note that for
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
we can write

\displaystyle\left(e^{r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n}-e^{r\mathcal{K}}\right)x=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{ds}\left(e^{s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}\right)x\,ds=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}e^{s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}x\,ds.

(4.18)

Note that we can bound

\displaystyle\left\|e^{s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}\right\|\leq e^{s\|\mathcal{L}\|/n},\quad\quad\left\|e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}\right\|\leq 1

(4.19)

for all
s\in[0,1]
and
r\in R
(c.f. (
2.8
)). Therefore, we see that both operators on the left-hand and right-hand side of (
4.18
) are bounded. Furthermore, using that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})
is dense in
X
(c.f. Section
2.3
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n,r}=\int_{0}^{1}e^{s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}\,ds.

(4.20)

Define now the family of bounded operators

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{r}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}e^{sr\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}\,ds.

Using the fact that
\mathcal{K}
generates a strongly continuous semigroup, together with the dominated convergence theorem, we see that the map
r\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{r}
is strongly continuous.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\left(e^{s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}-e^{sr\mathcal{K}}\right)\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}se^{s^{\prime}s(r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n)}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s^{\prime})sr\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}\,ds^{\prime}ds.

Therefore, using (
4.19
) again, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sup_{s\in[0,1/n]}e^{s\|\mathcal{L}\|}\|\mathcal{L}\|^{2}.

(4.21)

Defining the contraction
M_{r}=e^{r\mathcal{K}}
we can write

\displaystyle e^{nr\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}

\displaystyle=\left(e^{r\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}=\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}.

(4.22)

Let
x\in X
and
R^{\prime}=[1,2]\subseteq R
we see by (
4.14
) that for
r\in R^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[1,2]}\left\|\left(M^{n}_{r}-P\right)x\right\|=\sup_{r\in[1,2]}\left\|e^{n(r-1)\mathcal{K}}\left(e^{n\mathcal{K}}-P\right)x\right\|\leq\left\|(e^{n\mathcal{K}}-P)x\right\|\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

where we have used
e^{n(r-1)\mathcal{K}}P=P
and the fact that
\mathcal{K}
generates a contraction semigroup. Hence,
M_{r}
and
\mathcal{L}_{n,r}
satisfy the assumptions of
Theorem
3.1
and by applying this to the expansion (
4.22
) we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}e^{nr\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}x=e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}Px=e^{P\mathcal{L}P}Px,

(4.23)

with convergence uniformly in
r\in[1,2]
,
where we have used the following:

\displaystyle P\mathcal{L}_{r}P=\int_{0}^{1}Pe^{sr\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}Pds=P\mathcal{L}P.

To conclude the proof of (
4.15
) let now
\varepsilon>0
. By (
4.23
) we know that there exists some
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in[1,2]}\left\|\left(e^{nr\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq\varepsilon.

Let now
\gamma\geq N
be a real number and note that for
n_{\gamma}=\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor\geq N
we have
r_{\gamma}=\gamma/n_{\gamma}\in[1,2]
. This gives

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|=\left\|\left(e^{n_{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, this finishes the proof of (
4.15
).

We complete the proof of the theorem by showing the bound on the speed of convergence (
4.16
). For that we use (
3.12
) of Theorem
3.1
together with the fact that by (
4.21
) we have
s_{n}^{\prime}\leq c/n
for some
c\geq 0
, which gives for all
\mathbf{N}=\left(N_{l}\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some
C\geq 1

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(e^{n_{\gamma}r_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|=\left\|\left(M_{r_{\gamma}}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n_{\gamma},r_{\gamma}}}{n_{\gamma}}\right)^{n_{\gamma}}x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in[0,2]}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r_{\gamma}}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n_{\gamma}}\|x\|\right),

(4.24)

where we have denoted the speed of convergence for the operators
M_{r}=e^{r\mathcal{K}}
as defined in (
3.4
) by

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n,r\in[1,2]}\left\|\left(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P\right)x\right\|.

Note that

\displaystyle s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r_{\gamma}}P)^{l-1}x\right)

\displaystyle=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq N_{l},r\in[1,2]}\left\|\left(M^{n^{\prime}}_{r}-P\right)\int_{0}^{1}e^{sr_{\gamma}\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{L}\,ds\,(P\mathcal{L}P)^{l-2}x\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq N_{l},r\in[1,2]}\left\|\int_{0}^{1}\left(e^{(n^{\prime}r+sr_{\gamma})\mathcal{K}}-P\right)\,ds(\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\gamma^{\prime}\geq N_{l},}\left\|\left(e^{\gamma^{\prime}\mathcal{K}}-P\right)(\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right\|=v_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right),

with

being defined in (

Moreover, since

generates a contraction semigroup we have

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{L}_{r}\right\|=\left\|\int_{0}^{1}e^{sr\mathcal{K}}\mathcal{L}e^{(1-s)r\mathcal{K}}ds\right\|\leq\left\|\mathcal{L}\right\|

and hence

Substituting these in (

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L}}-e^{P\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|\mathcal{L}\|^{-l+1}v_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n_{\gamma}}\|x\|\right).

5
Discussion of speed of convergence in Zeno and strong damping limit

In this section we discuss cases and examples of contractions
M
and contraction semigroups
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
for which the provided bounds on the speed of convergence,
s^{\sup}_{n}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N})
in (
4.5
) of Theorem
4.1
and
v^{\sup}_{\gamma}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N})
in (
4.16
) of Theorem
4.2
, can be controlled explicitly. As outlined in Remark
3.2
, the idea is to have good control on the speed of the strong power convergence
M^{n}x\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}Px
and the strong mixing
e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}x\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{}Px
, i.e. using the notation of (
4.1
) and (
4.11
), on

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)=\sup_{n^{\prime}\geq n}\|(M^{n^{\prime}}-P)x\|\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad v_{\gamma}(x)=\sup_{\gamma^{\prime}\geq\gamma}\left\|\left(e^{\gamma^{\prime}\mathcal{K}}-P\right)x\right\|,

(5.1)

respectively. This can then be utilised to pick a reasonable
n
-dependent
\mathbf{N}(n)
(or
\gamma
dependent
\mathbf{N}(\gamma)
) ensuring that also the convergence speeds

\displaystyle s^{\sup}_{n}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N}(n))\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad v^{\sup}_{\gamma}(x,t\mathcal{L},\mathbf{N}(\gamma))

of the Zeno- and strong damping limits decay nicely as
n\to\infty
and
\gamma\to\infty
, respectively.

Firstly, we consider the case of uniformly power convergent
M
and uniformly mixing
\left(e^{t\mathcal{K}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, i.e. for which
M^{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}P
and
e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{}P
, does not only hold in strong topology but even in uniform topology. After that, we consider the so-called
(bosonic quantum limited) attenuator channel and semigroup.
In both cases we find that the sequences in (
5.1
) decay exponentially fast. This then implies bounds on the speed of convergence for the respective Zeno- and strong damping limits
of order
\mathcal{O}(\log(n)/n)
and
\mathcal{O}(\log(\gamma)/\gamma)
, respectively.

5.1
Uniform Zeno- and strong damping limits

Here, we consider the case in which the convergences

\displaystyle M^{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}P\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{}P

(5.2)

do not only hold in strong- but even in uniform topology. In that case, using
[
BDS21
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
Sal23
, Corollary 4.1.2]
respectively, we see that the convergences in (
5.2
) happen in fact even exponentially fast. In the language of Section
4
using the notation (
4.5
) and (
4.11
) established there, this would give for the abstract speeds of convergence

\displaystyle\sup_{\|x\|=1}s_{n}(x)\leq C\delta^{n}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\sup_{\|x\|=1}v_{\gamma}(x)\leq Ce^{-c\gamma}

(5.3)

for some
0<\delta<1
and
C,c>0.

In the proof of Theorem
5.1
and
5.3
below we combine this with Theorem
4.1
and
4.2
respectively to show bounds on the speed of convergence of the respective Zeno- and strong damping limits.

Theorem 5.1
(Zeno limit in uniform topology)

Let
M\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
be a contraction which satisfies in operator norm

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}M^{n}=P

(5.4)

for some operator
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
. Then for
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P.

(5.5)

More precisely, we have for all
n\geq 2

\left\|\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right\|\leq\frac{C\log(n)}{n},

(5.6)

for some
C\geq 1
independent of
n.

Remark 5.2

In
[
MR23
]
it was shown that for uniformly power convergent contractions
M,
the Zeno product converges with convergence speed
\mathcal{O}(1/n).
Moreover, the authors have proven that this is the optimal convergence speed. To contrast this, note that
Theorem
5.1
shows that using our bound on the speed of convergence of the Zeno product provided in Theorem
4.1
in this context gives a bound on the convergence speed of order
\mathcal{O}(\log(n)/n)
for the corresponding Zeno limit. Hence, the bound of Theorem
5.1
is optimal up to logarithmic factors in
n.

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

It suffices to prove (
5.6
). Using
[
BDS21
, Corollary 3.2]
we know that there exists
c\geq 0
and
0<\delta<1
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\left\|M^{n}-P\right\|\leq c\,\delta^{n}.

Hence, using the notation established in (
4.1
) we see that for all
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle s_{n}(x)\leq c\,\delta^{n}\|x\|.

Therefore, using (
4.5
) in Theorem
4.1
we see for all
(N_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some
C\geq 1

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|t\mathcal{L}\|^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((t\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(c\delta^{N_{l}}+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\right)\|x\|,

where we have used that
P
is a contraction. Picking now the constant sequence

\displaystyle N_{l}=\left\lfloor{\frac{\log(n)}{\log\left(1/\delta\right)}}\right\rfloor

for all
l\in\mathbb{N},
we see

\displaystyle\left\|\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right\|\leq C\left(c\delta^{\frac{\log(n)}{\log\left(1/\delta\right)}-1}+\frac{\log(n)}{\log\left(1/\delta\right)n}\right)=C\left(\frac{c}{n\delta}+\frac{\log(n)}{\log\left(1/\delta\right)n}\right).

Renaming the constant
C
yields (
5.6
).

Theorem 5.3
(Strong damping in uniform topology)

Let
\mathcal{K}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{K})\subseteq X
be the generator of a strongly continuous contraction semigroup which satisfies in operator norm

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}=P,

(5.7)

for some
P\in{\mathcal{B}}(X).
Furthermore, let
\mathcal{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}(X)
. Then for all
t>0

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\to\infty}e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}=e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P.

(5.8)

More precisely, we have for all
\gamma\geq 2

\displaystyle\left\|e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right\|\leq\frac{C\log(\gamma)}{\gamma},

(5.9)

for some
C\geq 1
independent of
\gamma.

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

The proof follows exactly the same lines as the one of Theorem
5.1
. But for completeness we give the details here explicitly.

It suffices to prove (
5.9
). Using
[
Sal23
, Corollory 4.1.2]
we know that there exists
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that for all
\gamma\geq 0

\displaystyle\left\|e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}-P\right\|\leq c_{1}\,e^{-c_{2}\gamma}.

Hence, using the notation established in (
4.11
) we see that for all
x\in X
we have

\displaystyle v_{\gamma}(x)\leq c_{1}\,e^{-c_{2}\gamma}\|x\|.

Therefore, using (
4.16
) in Theorem
4.2
we see for all
(N_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and some
C\geq 1

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\|t\mathcal{L}\|^{-l+1}v_{N_{l}}\left((t\mathcal{L}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\|x\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(c_{1}e^{-c_{2}N_{l}}+\frac{N_{l}}{\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\right)\|x\|,

where we have used that
P
is a contraction. Picking now for
\gamma\geq 2
the constant sequence

\displaystyle N_{l}=\left\lfloor{\frac{\log(\gamma)}{c_{2}}}\right\rfloor

for all
l\in\mathbb{N},
we see

\displaystyle\left\|e^{t(\gamma\mathcal{K}+\mathcal{L})}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\right\|\leq C\left(c_{1}e^{-\log(\gamma)+c_{2}}+\frac{\log(\gamma)}{c_{2}\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\right)=C\left(\frac{c_{1}e^{c_{2}}}{\gamma}+\frac{\log(\gamma)}{c_{2}\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\right).

Using
\frac{1}{\left\lfloor{\gamma}\right\rfloor}\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}
and renaming the constant
C
yields (
5.9
).

5.2
Bosonic quantum limited attenuator channel

In the following we discuss the example of the so-called
(bosonic quantum limited) attenuator channel
and the corresponding
attenuator semigroup
, which can be seen to model a beam splitter, as highlighted below. Notably, the considered channel and semigroup will respectively be strongly-, but not uniformly power convergent and mixing. Using our Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2
we can establish Zeno- and strong damping limits for the attenuator. Moreover, as we see in Lemma
5.5
and the discussion around (
5.26
), the attenuator is strongly power convergent and mixing with exponential convergence speed, provided that the input state has finite particle number (see (
5.10
) for definition). Similarly to the discussion in the last section, this can then be combined with the bounds on the speed of convergence provided in Theorem
4.1
and
4.2
to show convergence speed of the respective Zeno- and strong damping limits of order
\mathcal{O}(\log(n)/n)
and
\mathcal{O}(\log(\gamma)/\gamma)
respectively (see Proposition
5.6
).

We first give some basic definitions and facts corresponding to the attenuator channel in Lemma
5.4
and show its continuity properties in Lemma
5.5
. The latter will be essential to show the exponential strong power convergence and mixing mentioned above. Then we give the main result of this section in Proposition
5.6
.

For that, let
\mathcal{H}
be some infinite-dimensional, separable Hilbert space with orthonormal basis
\left(|n\rangle\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}.
We define the number operator
N:\mathcal{D}(N)\to\mathcal{H}
by its action on the basis vectors
|n\rangle
as

\displaystyle N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle

and then extended linearly. Here, we have denoted the dense domain
\mathcal{D}(N)\subseteq\mathcal{H}
which is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(N)=\left\{\psi\in\mathcal{H}\Big|\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n^{2}|\langle n|\psi\rangle|^{2}<\infty\right\}.

For a state
\rho
(or more generally
\rho\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
) we define its
(average) particle number
, by
[
BD19
,
Shi18
]

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\left(N\rho\right)\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n\langle n|\rho|n\rangle.

(5.10)

Note that the series as a sum of non-negative numbers is well-defined in
[0,\infty]
.
12
12

12

Note that
N
is unbounded and so
N\rho
in general not well-defined and especially not trace class. Therefore, the expression
\mathrm{Tr}(N\rho)
needs to be understood in the formal sense. However, we can make sense of it by formally evaluating the trace in the eigenbasis of the number operator
\left(|n\rangle\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as on the right hand side of (
5.10
).

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
we can define the
coherent state (vector)
, see e.g.
[
GK04
, Chapter 3]
or
[
Hol11
, Chapter 3.10]
,

\displaystyle|\alpha\rangle=e^{-\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}|n\rangle.

The coherent states satisfy the elementary relations

\displaystyle\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle=e^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-2\alpha^{*}\beta)}

(5.11)

for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha|d\alpha=\mathbbm{1},

(5.12)

where
d\alpha=d\Re(\alpha)d\Im(\alpha),
which can be checked by direct calculation. Note that due to (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) the coherent states can be interpreted as an
overcomplete basis.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\eta|\leq 1
, we can define the
(single mode bosonic quantum limited) attenuator channel

[
DPTG16
,
Win17
]
by its action on coherent states as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(|\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha|)=|\eta\alpha\rangle\!\langle\eta\alpha|.

(5.13)

The Lemma
5.4
below shows some basic statements about the attenuator channel which in some form have also been stated in
[
DPTG16
,
Win17
,
BD19
]
. In particular we show that the attenuator channel is already uniquely determined by the above relation (
5.13
), which essentially follows using that the coherent states form an overcomplete basis. Moreover, from the expression for its Stinespring isometry (
5.14
) it becomes apparent that the attenuator channel can be considered to model a beam splitter with transmitivity
\eta,
where one input beam is given by the channel input and the other is given by the
vacuum state

|0\rangle\!\langle 0|
(compare
[
GK04
, Chapter 6.2]
and particularly Equation (6.16) therein).

Lemma 5.4

For
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\eta|\leq 1
the relation (
5.13
) uniquely defines a quantum channel
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}:\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}).
Its Stinespring isometry
V_{\eta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
is, up to isometrically changing the enviroment Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{E}
, determined by its action on coherent states as

\displaystyle V_{\eta}|\alpha\rangle=\big|\eta\alpha\big\rangle\big|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha\big\rangle.

(5.14)

Moreover, the attenuator channel satisfies the semigroup property, i.e. for all
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|\eta_{1}|,|\eta_{2}|\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}\eta_{2}}=\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}}\circ\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{2}}.

(5.15)

Lastly,
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
can be written in Kraus decomposition as

\displaystyle\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}K_{l}(\eta)xK^{*}_{l}(\eta),

(5.16)

where
K_{l}(\eta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{{n+l\choose n}\left(1-|\eta|^{2}\right)^{l}}\,\eta^{n}|n\rangle\!\langle n+l|.

Proof of Lemma
5.4
.

We start by proving existence and uniquenes of the quantum channel
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}.
For existence note that (
5.14
) defines, after extending by linearity using the relation (
5.12
), a linear map

V_{\eta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
(where
\mathcal{H}_{E}\cong\mathcal{H}
) explicitly given for
\psi\in\mathcal{H}
by

\displaystyle V_{\eta}\psi=\frac{1}{\pi}\int V_{\eta}|\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha|\psi\rangle d\alpha=\frac{1}{\pi}\int|\eta\alpha\rangle|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\psi\rangle d\alpha.

(5.17)

Moreover,
V_{\eta}
is in fact an isometry as

\displaystyle\left\|V_{\eta}\psi\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\alpha\rangle\langle\eta\alpha|\eta\beta\rangle\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle\,d\alpha d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\alpha\rangle e^{-|\eta|^{2}(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-2\alpha^{*}\beta)}e^{-(1-|\eta|^{2})(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-2\alpha^{*}\beta)}\,d\alpha d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle\,d\alpha d\beta=\|\psi\|^{2},

where we have used the relation (
5.11
) twice. Hence,
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\,\cdot\,)=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}\left(V_{\eta}(\,\cdot\,)V^{*}_{\eta}\right)
defines a quantum channel, which by definition satisfies (
5.13
).

For uniqueness note that any channel
\Phi^{\prime}_{\eta}
satisfying (
5.13
), in particular mapping a set of pure states onto pure states, must have a Stinespring isometry
V^{\prime}_{\eta}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{E}
such that

V^{\prime}_{\eta}|\alpha\rangle=|\eta\alpha\rangle|\phi_{\alpha,\eta}\rangle

for some state vector
|\phi_{\alpha,\eta}\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_{E}.
By isometry, we already know for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
that

\langle\beta|\alpha\rangle=\langle\eta\beta|\eta\alpha\rangle\langle\phi_{\beta,\eta}|\phi_{\alpha,\eta}\rangle
and hence

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\alpha,\eta}|\phi_{\beta,\eta}\rangle=\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle/\langle\eta\alpha|\eta\beta\rangle=e^{-(1-\eta^{2})(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-2\alpha^{*}\beta)}=\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle.

From that we see that
V_{\eta}
and
V^{\prime}_{\eta}
are equal (up to isometrically changing the environment Hilbert space) and in particular the actions of the channels are equal, i.e. for
x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
we have

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}_{E}(V^{\prime}_{\eta}x{V^{\prime}}_{\eta}^{*})=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|x|\alpha\rangle\mathrm{Tr}_{E}(V^{\prime}_{\eta}|\beta\rangle\!\langle\alpha|{V^{\prime}}_{\eta}^{*})\,d\alpha d\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|x|\alpha\rangle|\eta\beta\rangle\!\langle\eta\alpha|\langle\phi_{\alpha,\eta}|\phi_{\beta,\eta}\rangle d\alpha d\beta=\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(x).

We proceed to prove the semigroup property (
5.15
). For that note that

\displaystyle\left(V_{\eta_{2}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{E}\right)V_{\eta_{1}}|\alpha\rangle=\left(V_{\eta_{2}}|\eta_{1}\alpha\rangle\right)|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}|^{2}}\,\alpha\rangle=|\eta_{1}\eta_{2}\alpha\rangle|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}|^{2}}\,\alpha\rangle|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{2}|^{2}}\,\eta_{1}\alpha\rangle,

where we denoted the identity on the environment Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{E}
by
\mathbbm{1}_{E}.

The semigroup property then follows using the same argument as above together with the fact that

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta_{2}|^{2}}\,\eta_{1}\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{2}|^{2}}\,\eta_{1}\beta\rangle=\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}\eta_{2}|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta_{1}\eta_{2}|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle,

which directly follows from (
5.11
).

Lastly we prove (
5.16
). First we convince ourselves that the right hand side of (
5.16
) indeed defines a quantum channel. Here, complete positivity is evident as the map is already written in Kraus decomposition. The trace preservation property follows from

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}K_{l}^{*}(\eta)K_{l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{n+l\choose n}\left(1-|\eta|^{2}\right)^{l}|\eta|^{2n}|n+l\rangle\!\langle n+l|

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose k}\left(1-|\eta|^{2}\right)^{m-k}|\eta|^{2k}|m\rangle\!\langle m|=\mathbbm{1}.

For the equality of the two channels in (
5.16
) it suffices to show equality
on coherent states by the established uniqueness. For that see

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}K_{l}(\eta)|\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha|K^{*}_{l}(\eta)

\displaystyle=e^{-|\alpha|^{2}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{{n+l\choose n}{m+l\choose m}}(1-|\eta|^{2})^{l}\,{\eta^{*}}^{n}\eta^{m}\frac{{\alpha^{*}}^{n}{\alpha}^{m}|\alpha|^{2l}}{\sqrt{(n+l)!(m+l)!}}\,|m\rangle\!\langle n|

\displaystyle=e^{-|\eta\alpha|^{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,{\eta^{*}}^{n}\eta^{m}\frac{{\alpha^{*}}^{n}{\alpha}^{m}}{\sqrt{n!m!}}\,|m\rangle\!\langle n|=|\eta\alpha\rangle\!\langle\eta\alpha|.

Hence, the quantum channel defined by the right hand side of (
5.16
) satisfies (
5.13
) and is therefore equal to
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
on all of
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H}).

As shown in
[
Win17
, Proposition 1]
the map
\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
is not uniformly continuous. In fact for all
\eta_{1}\neq\eta_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{2}}\right\|=2.

(5.18)

This can easily be seen by lower bounding the operator norm by the corresponding supremum over all coherent states as

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{2}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|x\|_{1}=1}\left\|\left(\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{2}}\right)(x)\right\|_{1}\geq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}\left\|\left(\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{1}}-\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta_{2}}\right)(|\alpha\rangle\!\langle\alpha|)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle=2\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{C}}\sqrt{1-|\langle\eta_{1}\alpha|\eta_{2}\alpha\rangle|^{2}}=2,

where for the second to last equation we used the expression of the trace distance for pure states (
2.10
) and the last equation follows by (
5.11
).

However, the map
\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
can be shown to be strongly continuous. Moreover, for input states which have a finite particle number we even get the explicit continuity bound (
5.20
) for continuity at
\eta=0.
This is the content of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.5

The map
\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
is strongly continuous, i.e. for all
x\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})
we have continuity of the map

\displaystyle\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(x).

(5.19)

Moreover, we have for
\rho\in\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})_{+}
the explicit continuity bound

\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)-P(\rho)\right\|_{1}\leq 4|\eta|\,\mathrm{Tr}\left((N+\mathbbm{1})\rho\right).

(5.20)

where we have denoted
P(\rho)=\Phi^{\text{att}}_{0}(\rho)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho)\,|0\rangle\!\langle 0|.

Proof.
We start by proving the strong continuity of
\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}.
For that note first that the map
\alpha\mapsto|\alpha\rangle
is continuous which can be seen by using (
5.11
) and therefore

\displaystyle\left\||\alpha\rangle-|\beta\rangle\right\|^{2}=2\left(1-\Re\left(\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle\right)\right)=2\left(1-e^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}-2\alpha^{*}\beta)}\right),

and by that
\lim_{\alpha\to\beta}|\alpha\rangle=|\beta\rangle.
By that we also get for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
continuity of the map

\displaystyle B_{1}(0)

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto|\eta\beta\rangle\!\langle\eta\alpha|\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle,

(5.21)

where we have denoted the unit ball in the complex numbers by
B_{1}(0)=\{\eta\in\mathbb{C}|\,|\eta|\leq 1\}
.
Now note that for any

we have

\displaystyle\iint|\langle\beta|n\rangle\!\langle m|\alpha\rangle|\,d\alpha d\beta=\iint e^{-(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})/2}\frac{|\beta|^{n}|\alpha|^{m}}{\sqrt{n!m!}}\,d\alpha d\beta<\infty.

In particular, this gives by the triangle inequality that for all
x\in\text{span}\left\{|n\rangle\!\langle m|\ \big|\,n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\right\}
the function
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto|\langle\beta|x|\alpha\rangle|
is integrable. Therefore, using continuity of the map (

\displaystyle\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint\langle\beta|x|\alpha\rangle|\eta\beta\rangle\!\langle\eta\alpha|\langle\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\alpha|\sqrt{1-|\eta|^{2}}\,\beta\rangle\,d\alpha d\beta

\eta\mapsto\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(x)

We know proceed to prove the explicit continuity bound (

\rho

\rho

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)-P(\rho)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle+\left\||0\rangle\!\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\right\|_{1}+\left\|(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\right\|_{1}.

\displaystyle\left|\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)-P(\rho)|0\rangle\right|

where for the inequality we have used

\displaystyle 1-\left(1-|\eta|^{2}\right)^{l}\leq l|\eta|^{2},

which follows by the mean value theorem.
For the second term in (

\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)
that

\displaystyle\Big\|(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\Big\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\Big((\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)\Big)=1-\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)|0\rangle

\displaystyle\leq|\eta|^{2}\mathrm{Tr}(N\rho),

(5.24)

where we have used that
\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}
is trace preserving and for the last inequality the estimate in (
5.23

For the cross terms we then use Hölder’s inequality and (

\displaystyle\left\||0\rangle\!\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\left\||0\rangle\!\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)^{1/2}\right\|_{2}\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)^{1/2}(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left\||0\rangle\!\langle 0|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)|0\rangle\!\langle 0|\right\|_{1}\left\|(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)(\mathbbm{1}-|0\rangle\!\langle 0|)\right\|_{1}}

The fourth term in (

\displaystyle\left\|\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta}(\rho)-P(\rho)\right\|_{1}\leq 2|\eta|\left(\mathrm{Tr}\left(N\rho\right)+\sqrt{\mathrm{Tr}\left(N\rho\right)}\right)\leq 4|\eta|\,\mathrm{Tr}\left((N+\mathbbm{1})\rho\right).

For

\eta(t)=e^{-t}
and define the

\Phi^{\text{att}}_{\eta(0)}=\Phi^{\text{att}}_{1}={\rm{id}},
we immediately see that this indeed defines an one-parameter semigroup. Furthermore, by Lemma

\mathcal{K}_{\text{att}}

5.2.1
Mixing-, Zeno- and strong damping limits of the attenuator channel

Note that from the semigroup property (

s^{\max}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})=\max_{l\in[k+1]}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right).

(6.13)

Proof.
Since
r\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{r}
is strongly continuous on
R
, which is a compact set, we have for all
x\in X
that
\sup_{r\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{r}x\|<\infty
and hence by the uniform boundedness principle
[
RS80
, Theorem III.9]
also
\sup_{r\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{r}\|<\infty.
Therefore, since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}=\mathcal{L}_{r}
uniformly in
r\in R
, we also have
C=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N},r\in R}\left\|\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\right\|<\infty.
Let
r\in R^{\prime}\subseteq R
and using that
M_{r}
is a contraction, we have for all
l\in[k]
and
i=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k})\in\Delta_{\text{disc}}^{k}(n)

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{l+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})\right\|\leq C\left\|\Pi_{m=k}^{l+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})\right\|\leq\cdots\leq C^{k-l+1}.

Hence, for all
y\in X
using the notion of (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{l+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})(M_{r}^{i_{l}}-P)(y)\right\|&\leq\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{l+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})\right\|\left\|(M_{r}^{i_{l}}-P)(y)\right\|\\
&\leq C^{k-l+1}s_{i_{l}}(y).\end{split}

(6.14)

Moreover, using the fact that
P
as the strong limit of the contractions
(M^{n}_{r})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a contraction itself and the notion of (
3.2
), we see by similar argument

\begin{split}&\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{l+1}(M^{i_{m}}_{r}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M^{i_{l}}(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r})(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}\right\|\\
&\leq\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{l+1}(M^{i_{m}}_{r}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M^{i_{l}}\right\|\left\|(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r})\right\|\left\|(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}\right\|\leq C^{k}s^{\prime}_{n}.\end{split}

(6.15)

We combine above inequalities using the triangle inequality to bound the quantity of interest. For that, first note that by using (
6.14
) we have

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{1}}-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})(M_{r}^{i_{1}}-P)x\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\Big(\Pi^{2}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})P-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq C^{k}s_{i_{1}}(x)+\left\|\Big(\Pi^{2}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})P-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|.

Estimating the second term in the last line by using (
6.15
) gives

\begin{split}&\left\|\Big(\Pi^{2}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})P-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|\\
&\leq\left\|\Big(\Pi^{3}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{2}}(\mathcal{L}_{n,r}-\mathcal{L}_{r})\Big)\right\|\|x\|\\
&\quad+\left\|\Big(\Pi^{3}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{r}P-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|\\
&\leq C^{k}s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\left\|\Big(\Pi^{3}_{m=k+1}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{r}P-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|.\end{split}

By iterating these estimates, we obtain

\begin{split}\left\|\Big(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|&\leq C^{k}ks^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}C^{k-l+1}s_{i_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)\\
&\leq C^{k}\left(ks^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{i_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)\right).\end{split}

(6.16)

Moreover, clearly we also have the bound

\begin{split}\left\|\Big(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})-\big(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\big)^{k}\Big)x\right\|\leq 2C^{k}\|x\|.\end{split}

Therefore, for every
0\neq\mathbf{N}=(N_{1},\dots,N_{k+1})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
we see

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\,\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in\Delta_{\text{disc}}^{k}(n)}\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{1}}-\frac{(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{k}}{k!}\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in\Delta_{\text{disc}}^{k}(n)}\left\|\left(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{1}}-(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{k}\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\frac{|\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n)|}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{k!}\right|\|(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{k}x\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in I_{n,k}(\mathbf{N})}\left\|\left(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})M_{r}^{i_{1}}-(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{k}\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\quad+2C^{k}\|x\|\left(\,\left|\frac{|\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n)|-|I_{n,k}(\mathbf{N})|}{n^{k}}\right|+\left|\frac{|\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n)|}{n^{k}}-\frac{1}{k!}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C^{k}}{k!}\left(k\,s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)\right)+\frac{2(2C)^{k}}{(k-1)!\,n}\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}N_{l}.

where in the last line we have used (
6.16
) together with the fact that
n\mapsto s_{n}(y)
is monotonically decreasing for every
y\in X
, the bounds presented in Lemma
6.1
and the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{|I_{n,k}(\mathbf{N})|}{n^{k}}\leq\frac{|\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n)|}{n^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{k!},

which follows from (
6.9
) and the fact that
I_{n,k}(\mathbf{N})\subseteq\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n).

Using now

\displaystyle\frac{C^{k}}{k!}\left(k\,s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)\right)+\frac{2(2C)^{k}}{(k-1)!\,n}\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}N_{l}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8k(2C)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\left(\,s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+\max_{l\in[k+1]}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right)\right)

(6.17)

and renaming the constant
C
gives the bound in (
6.12
).
Finally, we convince ourselves that from (
6.12
) we can conclude (
6.11
). Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since for all
l\in[k+1]
we have
\lim_{n\to\infty}s_{n}((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x)=0
and furthermore
n\mapsto s_{n}((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x)
being monotonically decreasing and
r\mapsto s_{n}((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x)
being continuous on the compact set
R
, we can pick by Dini’s Theorem
[
Rud76
, Theorem 7.13]
for all
l\in[k+1]
a
N_{l}\in\mathbb{N}
big enough such that
\sup_{r\in R}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)<\varepsilon
and then
N\in\mathbb{N}
big enough such that
s^{\prime}_{n}<\varepsilon
and
N_{l}/n<\varepsilon
for all
n\geq N.
Hence, all terms in (
6.17
) are, uniformly in
r\in R
, upper bounded by some
k
dependent constant times
\varepsilon,

which concludes the proof.

3.1
:

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

As above we use the convention
i_{k+1}=n-k-\sum_{l=1}^{k}i_{l}
and write
\Pi_{m={k+1}}^{2}(M_{r}^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r})=M_{r}^{i_{k+1}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}M_{r}^{i_{k}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\cdots M_{r}^{i_{2}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}.

We expand for all
x\in X
and
r\in R^{\prime}\subseteq R

\displaystyle\left(M+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}x

\displaystyle=M^{n}x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in\Delta_{\text{disc}}^{k}(n)}\Big(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}M^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\Big)M^{i_{1}}x.

For each
k\in[n]
, defining

\displaystyle y_{n,k}(r)=\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{i\in\Delta_{\text{disc}}^{k}(n)}\Big(\Pi_{m=k+1}^{2}M^{i_{m}}\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\Big)M^{i_{1}}x,

we see by Lemma
6.2
that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n,k}(r)=\frac{\left(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\right)^{k}}{k!}x

uniformly in
r\in R^{\prime}.

Moreover, by using the facts that
\|M_{r}\|\leq 1
and
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N},r\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{n,r}\|\leq C
for some finite
C>0
, together with the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Delta^{k}_{\text{disc}}(n)|}{n^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{k!},

proven in (
6.9
), we see that

\displaystyle\|y_{n,k}(r)\|\leq\frac{C^{k}}{k!}\|x\|

Hence, by the dominated convergence theorem we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big(M_{r}^{n}x+\sum_{k=1}^{n}y_{n,k}(r)\Big)=Px+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\right)^{k}}{k!}x=e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}Px,

with convergence again uniformly in
r\in R^{\prime}.

To prove the quantitative bound on the speed of convergence (
3.12
), we use (
6.12
) in Lemma
6.2
to note that for every
\mathbf{N}=(N_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
with
N_{1}\leq n
we have

\begin{split}&\left\|y_{n,k}(r)-\frac{(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{k}}{k!}x\right\|\leq\frac{kC^{k}}{(k-1)!}\Big(\,s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+s^{\max}_{n}\left(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}_{k+1}\right)\Big),\end{split}

(6.18)

for some
C\geq 1,
where
\mathbf{N}_{k+1}=(N_{1},\dots,N_{k+1}),

and

s^{\max}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}_{k+1})=\max_{l\in[k+1]}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right).

(6.19)

Using again
\|\mathcal{L}_{r}\|\leq C
this gives

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\left(M_{r}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{n,r}}{n}\right)^{n}-e^{P\mathcal{L}_{r}P}P\right)x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left(M^{n}_{r}-P\right)x\right\|+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|y_{n,k}(r)-\frac{\left(P\mathcal{L}_{r}P\right)^{k}}{k!}\right\|+\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\frac{C^{k}}{k!}\|x\|

\displaystyle\leq s_{n}(x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{kC^{k}}{(k-1)!}\Big(\,s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+s^{\max}_{n}\left(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N}_{k+1}\right)\Big)+\frac{Ce^{C}}{n}\|x\|

\displaystyle\leq 4C^{2}e^{C}\Big(s^{\prime}_{n}\|x\|+s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})\Big).

(6.20)

where we have used
n\mapsto s_{n}(x)
being monotonically decreasing and
N_{1}\leq n
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{kC^{k}}{(k-1)!}=C\frac{d}{dx}\Big|_{x=C}\left(x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\right)\leq 2C^{2}e^{C}.

and have defined

s^{\sup}_{n}(x,\mathcal{L}_{r},\mathbf{N})=\sup_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\left(\sup_{\tilde{r}\in R}\|\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{r}}\|\right)^{-l+1}s_{N_{l}}\left((\mathcal{L}_{r}P)^{l-1}x\right)+\frac{N_{l}}{n}\|x\|\right).

(6.21)

Renaming again the constant
C
in (
6.20
) gives the desired bound.

7
Summary and open problems

We proved quantum Zeno limits and strong damping limits in strong topology in a unified way (cf. Theorems
4.1
and
4.2
) and while merely demanding that the mixing limits
M^{n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}P
and
e^{\gamma\mathcal{K}}\xrightarrow[\gamma\to\infty]{}P
hold in strong topology. Both results are quantitative and contain a bound on the speed of convergence in terms of the convergence speed of the respective mixing limits. This is the first time that a bound on the speed of convergence for the quantum Zeno limit has been obtained in this setup. Furthermore, our result on the strong damping limit can be seen as the first for unbounded generators
\mathcal{K}.

For the unified proof we introduced a novel operator product, which we called the generalised binomial product, which generalises both the Zeno product and damped evolution operator. We then analysed its asymptotic behaviour in Theorem
3.1
.

Using these results, we were able to prove quantum Zeno- and strong damping limits for the attenutator channel and semigroup with a bound on the corresponding speeds of convergence (Example
1.1
and Proposition
5.6
).

An important open problem which could be the content of future research is the following:

The articles
[
BDS21
,
MR23
,
Mö24
]
generalised, under the spectral condition on
M
, the results on convergence of the Zeno product
\left(Me^{t\mathcal{L}/n}\right)^{n}
partially to unbounded generators
\mathcal{L},
though only under fairly strict constraints. It would be interesting to also provide extensions of our results on Zeno- and strong damping limits in strong topology, Theorem
4.1
and
4.2
, for unbounded
\mathcal{L}.
This could potentially be achieved under some form of energy constraint on the input state, reminiscent of the assumption of finite particle number in Proposition
5.6
. In the case of strong damping, it would be particularly desirable to obtain a rigorous analysis of the proposed paradigm for photonic dynamically error corrected qubits from
[
MLA+14
,
GM19
,
GCM23
]
.

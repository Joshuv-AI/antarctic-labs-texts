Single-layer compact dual-band dual-circularly polarized antenna based on proximity-coupling feed patches
Authors: Hung Tran-Huy, Hien Nguyen-Thi, Hung Nguyen-Manh, Muhammad Ikram
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a single-layer compact antenna with capability of realizing dual-sense circularly polarized (CP) radiation at two distinct frequency bands. The design employs multiple truncated-corner patches of different sizes operating in their fundamental modes, which are proximity-coupled through a probe-fed microstrip line. This configuration enables a single-layer structure, compact footprint, and flexible frequency-ratio tuning. This distinguishes the proposed design from the other related works, which are commonly multi-layer and large size due to the utilization of stack configuration or higher-order mode. Validation demonstrates that the proposed antenna exhibits dual-band operation at 5.2 and 6.5 GHz, with the operating bandwidths of 1.6% and 1.8%. Over these bands, the broadside gain values are higher than 6.0 dBi and peak at 6.5 dBi.Subject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

The demand for dual-band circularly polarized (CP) antennas is rapidly increasing due to the proliferation of multi-standard wireless systems1,2. These systems often operate at two frequency bands while requiring robustness to reduce multipath effects and polarization mismatch. Besides, the isolation between two channels can be significantly increased when two bands operate in the opposite sense, i.e., left-hand CP (LHCP) for the lower band and right-hand CP (RHCP) is for the upper band, or versa. Therefore, compact, low-profile dual-band dual-CP antennas are highly desirable for modern wireless applications3.

To achieve low profile and planar configuration, monopole or slot antennas are commonly used4–7. Despite having wideband operation, these designs suffer from bidirectional radiation with low gain. Dual-CP radiation with directional beam can be conveniently obtained with microstrip patch structure8. As reported in9–12, the main patch radiator is usually coupled with other radiators or slots to achieve dual-sense CP radiation. In13–15, the design approach of combination of separate radiators with feeding networks is reported. Alternatively, metasurface (MS) can also be employed to produce dual-band dual-sense CP radiation of patch antennas16–19. However, these designs have multilayered structures, which results in critical drawbacks of high profile and increased complexity. Besides, MS or feeding network also significantly increases the antenna footprint.

Simpler dual-band dual-sense CP patch antennas are realized based on a single patch radiator with a single feed20–23. Although single-layer design can be achieved, this approach relies on exciting higher-order modes of a single resonator, which require larger electrical dimensions and then, antenna size is significantly increased. To overcome this deficiency, studies in24–27 proposed various single-layer designs with dual-band dual-CP operation. These designs generally employ single patches connected with stubs or non-radiative structure. Nonetheless, in such designs, the two operating frequencies are determined by the modal resonances of the same radiator. Therefore, the frequency ratio is constrained by the inherent mode distribution and the physical size of the patch. As a result, independently tuning the two bands is difficult.

Single layer with flexible frequency ratio can be simply achieved when placing two radiating elements closely28,29. Since these resonators can be adjusted separately, the two operating frequencies can be tuned more independently, providing a more flexible frequency ratio while maintaining a single-layer configuration. However, large sizes and/or complicated feeding structure are the current disadvantages. In this work, a compact single-layer antenna capable of generating dual-band dual-sense CP radiation is proposed. The design is based on multiple truncated-corner patches of different sizes, which are proximity-coupled through a probe-fed microstrip line. This approach enables independent frequency tuning while maintaining a single layer and compact configuration. The fabricated prototype demonstrates dual-band performance at 5.2 and 6.5 GHz, with CP operation bandwidths (BW) of 1.6% and 1.8% for the lower and upper bands, respectively, and broadside gains exceeding 6.0 dBi across both bands.

Antenna design and performance

Antenna design

Fig. 1 presents the configuration of the proposed dual-band circularly polarized (CP) antenna. The antenna is implemented on a 1.52-mm-thick Taconic RF-35 substrate characterized by a relative permittivity of 3.5 and a loss tangent of 0.002. The design incorporates four radiating patches, each featuring truncations at two diagonally opposite corners to enable CP radiation. Dual-band functionality is achieved by intentionally employing patches of different sizes: the larger patches are responsible for CP radiation at the lower frequency band, whereas the smaller patches operate at the higher frequency band. Excitation is provided through capacitive coupling by means of a centrally located rectangular feed patch, ensuring effective coupling and a compact layout. It is noted that for each operating band, a pair of patches is employed to maintain a balanced layout around the centrally located proximity-coupled feed patch. This arrangement improves the excitation symmetry and stabilizes the broadside radiation pattern.

Antenna performance

Fig. 2 illustrates the simulated reflection coefficient of the proposed antenna. It can be seen obviously that the demonstrates two well-defined operating bands centered at approximately 5.2 and 6.6 GHz. The lower band achieves an impedance BW of 4.9–5.3 GHz, whereas the upper band spans 6.4–6.8 GHz. These results correspond to fractional BWs of 8.7% and 6.0% for the lower and higher bands, respectively.

Simulated of the proposed dual-band dual-CP antenna.

The far-field performance is assessed through the axial ratio (AR) and gain characteristics shown in Fig. 3. At the lower band, the antenna achieves a 3-dB AR BW from 5.19 to 5.27 GHz, while the upper band maintains an AR below 3 dB over 6.43–6.54 GHz. It is noted that such AR BWs are fully covered by the impedance BWs. The broadside gain response indicates gains of 6.4–7.0 dBi and 6.2–7.0 dBi for the lower and upper bands, respectively. Noted that the gains achieved in both bands are quite similar, demonstrating good performance of the proposed dual-band antenna.

Simulated AR and broadside gain of the proposed dual-band dual-CP antenna.

The simulated gain radiation patterns at 5.2 GHz and 6.5 GHz, corresponding to the two operating bands, are depicted in Fig. 4. Both co-polarized and cross-polarized components are presented in the x-z and y-z planes. Overall, stable broadside radiation characteristics are achieved at both frequencies. Owing to the structural asymmetry, a slight beam tilt from the exact broadside direction is observed. The antenna predominantly radiates LHCP at the lower band, whereas RHCP is dominant at the upper band, confirming the dual-sense CP performance.

Antenna operation characteristics

The realization of CP radiation is verified by examining the current distributions at the corresponding operating frequencies. Fig. 5 depicts the simulated current distributions at 5.2 and 6.5 GHz. Clearly, two orthogonal field components with a phase difference are observed at phase instants of and , confirming the CP generation mechanism. As the phase changes from to , the surface current vector rotates clockwise at 5.2 GHz and counterclockwise at 6.5 GHz. This behavior indicates that the antenna predominantly radiates LHCP at 5.2 GHz and RHCP at 6.5 GHz. Furthermore, the current distribution is mainly concentrated on the larger patches at 5.2 GHz, whereas at 6.5 GHz, it is primarily confined to the smaller patches, further validating the independent operation of the two bands.

In the proposed antenna, and control the capacitive coupling between the centrally located feed patch and the surrounding radiating patches. Therefore, these gaps directly influence the matching performance at these bands. For the CP radiation, the two orthogonal CP modes are controlled by the truncated corner. As a result, the CP radiation will be strongly affected by this parameter, rather than the coupling gaps. For demonstration, Figs. 8 and 9 present the simulated and AR characteristics for different values of and . It can be clearly observed that variations in these gaps strongly influence the impedance matching, whereas their effect on the AR response is relatively minor. The spacing between the feed patch and the surrounding radiating patches determine the coupling strength and field penetration. A stronger overlap or closer spacing increases the electric-field coupling, which can improve impedance matching. However, excessive interaction may over-couple the resonator, leading to poor matching performance. Overall, the antenna still maintains acceptable matching performance even under relatively large gap variations. Finally, the feeding position d is a critical parameter governing the impedance matching performance of the proposed antenna. As illustrated in Fig. 10, variations in d significantly affect the matching characteristics of both operating bands. Adjusting the feed location can improve the impedance matching at the lower band while simultaneously degrading it at the upper band, and vice versa. Therefore, an appropriate selection of d is essential to achieve a balanced impedance performance across both frequency bands.

Measurement results

To verify the proposed dual-band, dual-sense CP design, a prototype was fabricated and experimentally tested, as shown in Fig. 11. The reflection coefficient was characterized using an N5242A vector network analyzer, and the far-field radiation performance was measured in an anechoic chamber. The experimental results are in good agreement with the simulated ones. The slight differences observed are primarily due to fabrication inaccuracies and minor alignment errors during the measurement process.

Fabricated antenna prototype of the proposed antenna.

Fig. 12 illustrates the simulated and measured characteristics of the proposed antenna. The experimental results demonstrate impedance bandwidths of 9.3% (4.92–5.40 GHz) and 6.3% (6.46–6.88 GHz) for the lower and upper operating bands, respectively. The simulated and measured AR and broadside gain are presented in Fig. 13. Two distinct AR minima are observed at 5.2 GHz and 6.5 GHz. The corresponding 3-dB AR BWs are 1.6% (5.16–5.24 GHz) for the lower band and 1.8% (6.44–6.56 GHz) for the upper band. Within these frequency ranges, the measured broadside gain remains relatively stable, ranging from 6.0 to 6.5 dBi in the lower band and from 6.0 to 6.4 dBi in the upper band. In the measurement, the fabrication tolerance around the coupling gaps is mainly reflected in the measured , which shows some deviation from the simulated result. Meanwhile, the measured CP performance agrees reasonably well with the simulation. Therefore, although fabrication tolerance is unavoidable, its impact on the proposed design is not critical, and the measured results remain acceptable.

The simulated and measured radiation patterns are presented in Fig. 14. The results clearly demonstrate stable broadside radiation characteristics at both operating frequencies. Performance comparison

Performance comparison among dual-band dual-CP antennas shown in Table 1 is implemented to demonstrate the advantages of the proposed work. Noted that this paper just focuses on antenna with CP radiation, and only the overlapping bandwidth between impedance and AR bandwidths is considered. Using multi-layer structures11,12,18,19 suffers from high profile and complicated configurations for both radiating elements and feeding mechanisms. In fact, these drawbacks are not suitable for mass production. Dual-band realization with the aid of higher-order mode21,22 commonly requires large footprint. Single-layer designs in25,27 are just suitable for small ratio. Additionally, it is also noted that the principle for dual-band realization in21,22,25,27 cannot provide flexible frequency ratio. This feature can be conveniently achieved in28,29 when two separate patches are utilized, but these designs occupy large space for multiple radiating elements installation. In conclusion, the proposed design offers flexible frequency-ratio tuning, inherently using two distinct patches excited through a proximity-coupled feed. Moreover, the antenna exhibits a well-balanced performance in terms of overall dimensions, impedance bandwidths, and realized gains, demonstrating its effectiveness for compact dual-band CP applications.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a compact single-layer antenna that achieves LHCP radiation at the lower band of 5.2 GHz and RHCP radiation at the higher band of 6.5 GHz. Using proximity-coupled truncated-corner patches operating in their fundamental modes, the design enables flexible frequency-ratio tuning within a low complexity structure. It attains operating BWs of 1.6% and 1.8% for the lower and upper bands, respectively. Entire the operating BWs, the broadside gains are higher than 6.0 dBi. Future work will focus on BW enhancement using a thicker or lower-permittivity substrate, introducing additional parasitic patches or slots.

Prioritizing vaccine introduction to China’s National Immunization Program: a multi-criteria decision analysis
Authors: Jianming Guo, Bin Zheng, Tingting Chen, Shuyan Guo, Maobai Liu, Siping Dong
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo prioritize the introducing of new vaccines into China’s National Immunization Program (NIP) among 10 candidate vaccines across four classes.MethodsWe developed a vaccine value framework using Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) to simulate the introduction of new vaccines into NIP, covering 21 criteria encompassing six dimensions: safety, effectiveness, economy, innovation, accessibility, and appropriateness. Two decision scenarios were considered: Scenario One prioritized the four classes of vaccines, while Scenario Two identified specific vaccines within each class.ResultsIn the vaccine value framework, safety received the highest weight, while innovation received the lowest. The Human Papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine was identified as a top priority for inclusion in NIP, followed by Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (PCV), Haemophilus Influenzae Type B (HIB), and Rotavirus (RV) vaccines. The specific types are domestic bivalent HPV vaccine, imported 13-valent PCV vaccine, domestic HIB vaccine, and domestic RV vaccine. The results of the one-way and probabilistic sensitivity analysis demonstrated robustness.ConclusionsThis study provides a transparent, comprehensive, and quantifiable vaccine value framework to aid decision-making for introducing new vaccines into China’s NIP. According to the MCDA results, HPV should be considered the top vaccine candidate for the NIP.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-21013-7.Keywords: China, Vaccines, Multi-criteria decision analysis, National immunization program

Background

In the history of humankind’s fight against diseases, vaccination has been one of the greatest achievements and the most effective way to prevent infectious diseases [1, 2]. Vaccines have revolutionized public health by contributing to the eradication of devastating diseases such as smallpox and rinderpest [3]. In combating the global pandemic of COVID-19, vaccines also have once again highlighted their unparalleled effectiveness as a public health intervention.

Despite the critical role of vaccines, their global distribution is uneven, with over 75% of vaccinations occurring in high-income countries, while less than 5% of people in Africa are fully vaccinated [4]. This disparity is exacerbated by resource constraints in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), where weak health systems and financial barriers pose significant challenges [5]. Organizations such as the Global Alliance for Vaccines and Immunization (GAVI) have increased vaccination rates by providing essential support. Yet, these efforts are often hampered by inadequate infrastructure and the need for sustainable fiscal policies [6, 7]. Prioritizing vaccines for inclusion in the National Immunization Program (NIP) is crucial to maximize the impact and efficiency of immunization efforts in these regions [8, 9].

Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA) is a systematic approach used to evaluate and prioritize different options based on multiple criteria. It provides decision-makers with a structured framework to assess the trade-offs between competing alternatives, allowing for a more comprehensive evaluation of each option’s overall value. MCDA has been widely applied in various sectors such as transportation, migration, and energy, where it helps to balance diverse factors like cost, efficiency, and sustainability [10]. In the healthcare sector, MCDA is increasingly utilized to enhance decision-making processes. For instance, in a review involving the prioritization of healthcare interventions, MCDA was applied to evaluate pharmaceuticals and public health strategies in 18 different countries. The study found that MCDA facilitated more informed and balanced decisions by incorporating diverse criteria such as cost-effectiveness, patient outcomes, and equity considerations, ultimately supporting better allocation of resources and policy decisions [11]. The primary benefits of MCDA include its ability to provide a clear framework for value assessment, enhance transparency by clarifying criterion weighting, and promote stakeholder participation to achieve consensus in decisions [12].

This study selected China as a case to validate the use of the MCDA tool for prioritizing vaccine introduction within its NIP. This selection was characterized by several factors: (1) limited per capita medical resources compared to high-income countries, (2) not introducing or sustaining the introduction of new vaccines since 2010 [13], (3) high out-of-pocket costs of non-NIP vaccines, where the costs in China are often higher than comparable vaccines in the United States and Europe [14], and (4) low vaccine coverage in non-NIP vaccines and limited vaccine inclusion in the NIP. China’s number of diseases preventable by vaccines remains lower compared to middle- and high-income countries and some globally significant vaccines are yet to be included in the NIP [14, 15]. In China, there are two types of vaccines: those within the NIP, which are provided free of charge by the government (e.g., polio and hepatitis B vaccines), and those outside the NIP, which citizens must pay for voluntarily (e.g., Rotavirus (RV) and Human Papillomavirus (HPV) vaccines) [14]. The NIP has a history of significant accomplishments, exemplified by the inclusion of the live oral attenuated polio vaccine in 1961, which ultimately led to the eradication of polio in China by 2000 [16]. Despite this progress, the WHO’s Global Vaccine Action Plan 2011–2020 identified ten classes of vaccines for priority introduction in the NIP, of which China has yet to include four classes: specifically, RV, Pneumococcal Conjugate Vaccine (PCV), Haemophilus Influenzae Type B (HIB), and HPV vaccines, due to the high costs associated with these vaccines and the logistical challenges of implementing nationwide vaccination programs [15].

Previous studies on vaccine introduction decision-making have largely concentrated on high-income countries, often overlooking the unique challenges faced by LMICs [17]. Studies focusing on LMICs have frequently considered only a limited range of vaccines, which do not fully correspond with the WHO’s recommended list. Furthermore, these studies often did not explore variations within each vaccine class, such as different valences and manufacturers (e.g., 2-valent vs. 9-valent, domestic vs. imported), limiting their applicability and comprehensiveness [18, 19]. Moreover, many of these studies lacked transparency in their evaluation frameworks and processes, hindering their broader applicability [20, 21]. Recognizing these gaps, we conducted an MCDA study focused on prioritizing the introduction of four key vaccine classes—RV, PCV, HIB, and HPV—into China’s NIP. These vaccines were selected due to their notable absence from many LMICs’ immunization schedules, despite their potential to significantly mitigate disease burdens among children and adolescents. By providing health decision-makers with evidence-based recommendations, this study aims to optimize the use of China’s constrained immunization budget. The findings may serve as a valuable reference for other LMICs seeking to enhance their vaccine programs under similar resource limitations.

Methods

In accordance with the guidelines published by the International Society for Pharmacoeconomics and Outcome Research (ISPOR), this study followed eight MCDA steps. These steps included defining the decision problem, selecting and structuring criteria, measuring performance, scoring alternatives, weighting criteria, calculating aggregate scores, dealing with uncertainty, and reporting and examining findings [22, 23].

Due to the pandemic, a questionnaire was used to obtain the value preferences of relevant stakeholders for selecting and weighting criteria. We conducted an online meeting to explain the methodology of MCDA to the stakeholders before sending out the questionnaire. Additionally, the meaning of each criterion and instructions on how to fill out the questionnaire correctly were explained in detail within the questionnaire. To mitigate potential conflicts of interest, we omitted specific vaccine names in the questionnaire. All stakeholders affirmed they had no conflicts of interest related to the candidate vaccines.

Defining the decision problem

The decision problem we aimed to address was how to effectively prioritize and introduce new vaccines into China’s NIP given the constraints of budget and varying vaccine options. The challenge involves selecting which vaccines would provide the greatest health benefit and economic value, considering the diversity of available vaccine types and their respective impacts on public health.

To tackle this problem, our objective was to develop a reusable vaccine evaluation framework that could systematically assess vaccine alternatives. The alternatives considered included 10 vaccines categorized into four classes: RV vaccine (domestic and imported), PCV vaccine (domestic and imported), HIB vaccine (domestic and imported), and HPV vaccines (including 2-valent domestic, 2-valent imported, 4-valent imported, and 9-valent imported), all marketed in China.

Selecting and structuring criteria

We conducted a systematic search of multiple databases and institutional websites to identify criteria for a vaccine value framework. The databases searched included PubMed, EMbase, Web of Science, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wanfang Data, CQVIP, and China Biology Medicine (CBM). Search terms included vaccine types, safety indicators, efficacy indicators, economic indicators, and age-appropriate Chinese vaccine recipients. Institutional websites searched included WHO, The Institute for Clinical and Economic Review, and China Institute for Food and Drug Control. The information retrieved from these sources provided a comprehensive overview of vaccine value framework criteria both domestically and internationally.

To screen all possible criteria, we sent out a first round of questionnaires to stakeholders. In the questionnaire, stakeholders were asked to assess the importance of each criterion (very important: 5 points; important: 4 points; fairly important: 3 points; not important: 2 points; not at all important: 1 point) and to suggest any additions to the criteria based on their experience. The criteria included had to meet the following conditions: first, the mean value of the importance score should be greater than 3.5; second, the coefficient of variation should be less than 0.25. Additionally, the criteria had to meet the requirements outlined in MCDA, including completeness (all relevant aspects of the decision problem were covered, ensuring that no important factors were omitted), nonredundancy (each criterion provided unique information, avoiding any duplication or overlap that could skew the evaluation), preference independence (the preference for one criterion did not influence the preference for another, allowing for unbiased assessments), and nonoverlap (criteria were clearly distinct from one another, ensuring that each criterion addressed a separate aspect of the decision problem without ambiguity) [23].

Measuring performance

To measure performance for different criteria, we employed a variety of methods tailored to the type of data available, the sources of performance for each dimension are detailed in Table 1. Taking the imported HIB vaccine as an example, criteria within the dimensions of safety and efficacy were assessed by first searching for relevant systematic reviews and meta-analyses; if these were unavailable, we then proceeded to search for RCTs to gather necessary performance. The economic dimension was evaluated based on pharmacoeconomic evaluation studies. The Innovation dimension was assessed through stakeholder input, drawing on their expertise and experience. Accessibility was determined using information from healthcare authority websites, such as the National Bureau of Statistics and the National Institutes for Food and Drug Control. Lastly, the appropriateness dimension was evaluated based on the vaccine’s instruction manual, verifying its suitability for use. However, due to a scarcity of economic evaluations for the four types of HPV vaccines, we conducted our own analysis using the Papillomavirus Rapid Interface for Modelling and Economics (PRIME) to establish baseline values, please refer to Supplemental Fig. 1.

Sources of performance for each criteria

The principles identified for measuring performance were: (1) literature that involved the Chinese population was preferred; (2) a meta-analysis on the vaccine study containing multiple RCTs was performed; (3) the “antibody seroconversion rate” for the vaccine is the average of the multiple antibody seroconversion rates; (4) for vaccines that can prevent multiple diseases, the average of “vaccine efficacy” was selected as the performance source; (5) to ensure consistency, all the amount of money involved was discounted at 3% and presented in 2021 RMB.

Scoring alternatives and weighting criteria

To simulate the introduction of these vaccines into the NIP, we designed two decision-making scenarios. In Scenario 1, we aimed to determine the priority for introducing different classes of vaccines into the NIP by comparing the highest total scores among them. In Scenario 2, we focused on identifying the specific types within the four classes that should be prioritized for inclusion in the NIP by comparing their highest total scores.

We engaged a panel of 28 stakeholders, consisting of physicians (n = 7), pharmacologists (n = 7), pharmacoeconomists (n = 7), and healthcare officials (n = 7). Each stakeholder was tasked with assigning weightings and scores to assess the overall value of each vaccine alternative.

We defined five reference levels for each criterion: Level 1–5, where Level 1 stands for the worst level, Level 2 for a slightly worse level, Level 3 for a medium level, Level 4 for a slightly better level, and Level 5 for the best level. Based on the vaccine performance, the minimum value, medium value, and maximum value of performance were determined as Level 2, Level 3, and Level 4, respectively. The minimum value was reduced by 20% to define Level 1, and the maximum value was increased by 20% to define Level 5. To enable effective comparison, we standardized the scores to a uniform scale of 0 to 100, with the same unit.

In the second round of the questionnaire, stakeholders were asked to assess the relative importance between reference levels and between criteria (no difference: 1 point; very weak difference: 2 points; weak difference: 3 points; moderate difference: 4 points; strong difference: 5 points; very strong difference: 6 points; extreme difference: 7 points). This qualitative assessment aimed to provide an understanding of the attractiveness differences between each reference level and criterion. The resulting data were input into the MACBETH software to determine the value function and weight coefficients of each criterion.

The value function is essentially a piecewise function with five points, unique to each criterion. The x-coordinates of these points correspond to the five reference levels derived from the performance of alternatives, while the y-coordinates represent the scores (ranging from 0 to 100) based on stakeholder qualitative assessments. Unlike traditional scoring methods that assume equal gaps between levels (a straight line with equal slope), the value function better captures the specificity of different levels across criteria (a piecewise function with varying slopes).

Dealing with uncertainty

In this study, one-way sensitivity analysis and probabilistic sensitivity analysis were adopted to consider the uncertainty in the MCDA. Such uncertainty analysis incorporated all the variables in the MCDA, except for the vaccine performance. In the one-way sensitivity analysis, a range of variation was ± 20% of the base value. In the probabilistic sensitivity analysis, a Monte Carlo simulation was applied with a range of variation of ± 20% of the base value. For the weighting, the model was computed with a Beta distribution, and for the scores, a Triangular distribution was used.

Vaccine value framework

After a systematic literature search [24–26] and with reference to the six dimensions (safety, efficacy, economy, innovation, accessibility, and appropriateness) in National Drug Clinical Comprehensive Evaluation (2021 for Trial Implementation) issued by the National Health Commission [27], 28 criteria were initially identified for assessment. The vaccine value framework was constructed through a screening method (mean > 3.5 and coefficient of variation < 0.25), MCDA requirements, and expert opinions based on feedback from the questionnaire. Ultimately, 21 criteria were included in the value framework, consisting of 5 qualitative criteria (all in the innovation dimension) and 16 quantitative criteria, as presented in Table 2.

Vaccine performance matrix

The performance matrix for the 10 alternatives is presented in Supplemental Table 1. The results show that HPV and PCV-related diseases caused the highest number of annual deaths. The HPV and PCV vaccines outperformed the other vaccines in terms of the number of annual deaths prevented and quality-of-life improvements. However, the number of deaths prevented varied based on the different HPV vaccine valents available. All vaccines had antibody seroconversion rates and protective efficacy greater than 70%, with HPV and HIB vaccines demonstrating more than 90% vaccine protection. However, the HPV vaccine was associated with a higher incidence of local adverse reactions than the other three vaccine classes, while the imported RV vaccine had a higher incidence of serious adverse reactions than the other vaccines. Compared with no vaccination, all vaccination options are cost-effective (ICER < 3X GDP Per Capita). Oral administration is a benefit of the RV vaccine compared with other vaccines. The HPV vaccine has the potential to eradicate cervical cancer, but screening can also help prevent HPV-related diseases. Each vaccine has the greatest benefit for specific populations. None of the associated diseases have the potential for a pandemic.

Weighting

The qualitative judgments of stakeholders about the differences in attractiveness between each criterion were combined, and the weight coefficients of each criterion were obtained using MACBETH software. The six dimensions were weighted and ranked as follows: safety (0.30), effectiveness (0.29), economy (0.16), accessibility (0.13), appropriateness (0.08), and innovation (0.04). Of the 21 criteria, the three highest weighted criteria were the incidence of serious adverse reactions (0.179), the incidence of systemic adverse reactions (0.098), and the number of disease deaths averted per year (0.090). The three lowest weighted criteria were the presence of alternative public health measures (0.007), the greatest benefit to special populations (0.006), and the mode of administration (0.001). Uncertainty analysis

One-way sensitivity analysis

The study conducted a one-way sensitivity analysis to identify the top 10 factors that had the greatest impact on the total scores of the 10 vaccine alternatives. The results of Scenario 1 show that the order of vaccine priorities only changes when the “weighting of serious adverse reactions” exceeds 20%. At this level, the priority shifts to PCV, HPV, HIB, and RV vaccines. However, in all other cases, the priority remains unaltered. In Scenario 2, changes in the range of value scores and weighting factors did not alter the preferences for RV, PCV, and HPV vaccines. However, preferences for the HIB vaccine were affected by these changes (see Supplemental Fig. 3 for details).

Probabilistic sensitivity analysis

Table 4 shows the results of the probabilistic sensitivity analysis conducted for Scenarios 1 and 2. The purpose of the analysis was to determine the ranking probability of each alternative. In Scenario 1, the results showed that the HPV vaccine had the highest probability of ranking first (91.3%), followed by the PCV vaccine (90.8%) in second place, the HIB vaccine (96.3%) in third place, and the RV vaccine (96.8%) in fourth place. Furthermore, the probability of prioritizing the HPV vaccine, PCV vaccine, HIB vaccine, and RV vaccine was 77.3%, indicating that this preference is the best option with stable results. In Scenario 2, the preferred types and probabilities of the RV vaccine, PCV vaccine, HIB vaccine, and HPV vaccine were calculated, with the domestic RV vaccine having a 100% probability of being the preferred choice, followed by the imported PCV vaccine (97.9%), domestic bivalent HPV vaccine (98.2%), and domestic HIB vaccine (60.7%). Consistent with the result in the one-way sensitivity analysis, preferences for the HIB vaccine were unstable.

Discussion

The study employed the MCDA method to evaluate the priority and specific types of class II vaccines for inclusion in China’s NIP. MCDA is a comprehensive evaluation approach that integrates the value preferences of various stakeholders to assign value scores to different alternatives. To construct the value function and determine the weighting coefficients, the study engaged stakeholders from four fields: pharmacology, pharmacoeconomics, clinical practice, and public health. The involvement of stakeholders from diverse fields ensured that the value preferences of different stakeholders were considered in the decision-making process. The study showed that the three dimensions with the highest weight coefficients were safety, efficacy, and economy, occupying more than 75% of the total weight, indicating that safety, efficacy, and economy are the main factors in considering whether a vaccine should be introduced in the NIP.

Based on the total scores of the vaccine alternatives, the study determined the priority for introduction into China’s NIP as HPV vaccine, PCV vaccine, HIB vaccine, and RV vaccine. The specific types identified were the domestic bivalent HPV vaccine, imported PCV vaccine, domestic HIB vaccine, and domestic RV vaccine. Sensitivity analysis revealed that the selection of superiority between the two HIB vaccines was subject to change, primarily due to the lack of vaccine efficacy data for different HIB vaccines and the limited consideration of different effects brought by HIB vaccines in current economic evaluations. As a result, both HIB vaccines had similar overall scores (60.56 and 60.42), suggesting that if the NIP included HIB vaccine recommendations, both HIB vaccines could be included. According to the study, the priority for inclusion in the NIP was generally stable, i.e., HPV vaccine, PCV vaccine, HIB vaccine, and RV vaccine. Only when the weighting of serious adverse reactions incidence exceeds a certain level, the priority may change to PCV vaccine, HPV vaccine, HIB vaccine, and RV vaccine.

In China, the inclusion of drugs in the Essential Drugs List requires a comprehensive evaluation process based on six dimensions: safety, efficacy, economy, innovation, accessibility, and appropriateness. Similarly, this study used a similar approach to evaluate and introduce vaccines into NIP. The criteria for vaccine inclusion were screened and categorized into these six dimensions, offering a numerical advantage in criteria compared with previous studies [18, 19]. This improves the vaccine value framework, resulting in a more practical tool to mirror the decision-making process in China. Additionally, this study applied MACBETH software to construct the value function for vaccine scoring, promoting a more scientific and transparent process compared with other studies of MCDA application in healthcare policy decision-making.

The study underscores the critical importance of organizational aspects and transparency within the evaluation process. By actively involving all pertinent stakeholders, the evaluation becomes more comprehensive and systematic, benefiting from a diverse array of perspectives and expertise. Transparency is essential in fostering trust, as it allows the evaluation outcomes to be subject to open scrutiny and validation, thereby enhancing their credibility and acceptance. By focusing on these fundamental elements, the evaluation process is better positioned to support informed decision-making and effective public health interventions, thereby advancing our objectives to optimize strategies for vaccine introduction.

The study holds potential as a valuable reference for researchers interested in investigating vaccine inclusion in NIP. Firstly, one of the key advantages of the MCDA method is its ability to tailor the criteria for new vaccine introduction prioritization to the specific context and needs of a country. This adaptability is particularly relevant, as different countries may face varying public health challenges, resource constraints, and population health dynamics. By adopting and modifying the criteria and dimensions to suit specific contexts and data availability, various countries can develop their own version of the value framework to guide decision-making for vaccine introduction into their NIP. This approach helps align the evaluation framework more closely with real-world conditions and contributes to more informed and effective public health policies. In China, this method provides a structured framework that aligns with national health priorities, ensuring that the vaccines prioritized for inclusion in the NIP address the most pressing health needs and policy objectives. Secondly, for vaccine performance evaluation, it is recommended to follow a hierarchical approach, starting from high-value evidence and gradually moving to lower values. While literature is an important source, official websites, books, and expert input are also useful sources. In cases where high-value evidence is lacking, an independent study may be necessary. Thirdly, MACBETH software is a valuable tool in MCDA that enhances the transparency of evaluation processes. It allows for the conversion of stakeholders’ varying perceptions regarding different criteria into a specific value function associated with those criteria. Fourthly, performing uncertainty analysis on the vaccine value framework is an essential component of the final evaluation process. It serves to verify the stability and robustness of the final results.

Worth noting is that the suitability of using this method in other countries depends on their unique health priorities, population health dynamics, and decision-making structures. Different countries may prioritize different criteria based on their specific epidemiological needs, cultural values, and resource availability, which could lead to varying results. It is essential for each country to engage relevant stakeholders, such as National Immunization Technical Advisory Groups (NITAGs), health ministries, and other decision-making bodies, to ensure that the assessment reflects local priorities and contexts. Involving these bodies not only enhances the comprehensiveness of the assessment by providing expert guidance and facilitating interdisciplinary collaboration but also improves the effectiveness of implementation by aligning the evaluation with national health strategies. Additionally, involving a diverse range of stakeholders ensures that the evaluation process is inclusive and considers multiple perspectives, which is vital for achieving a comprehensive and balanced decision-making process.

To fully appreciate the strengths and limitations of the MCDA method, it is beneficial to compare it with other established tools like the CAPACITI Decision-Support Tool. The CAPACITI Decision-Support Tool, recommended by the WHO, is an Excel-based tool built upon the MCDA framework, providing a structured, user-friendly approach to decision-making in NIPs [28, 29]. While CAPACITI offers a standardized format, our study underscores the significance of the MCDA method’s adaptability to specific contexts, such as China’s unique health priorities and decision-making structures. This flexibility is essential for addressing unique public health challenges that may not be fully captured by standardized tools. By focusing on a tailored framework, our research complements CAPACITI by offering a flexible approach that can adapt to varying epidemiological needs and policy objectives, providing valuable insights for countries to refine their vaccination strategies.

The study has several limitations that may affect the generalizability of the findings. Firstly, the uncertainty of vaccine performance and the impact of performance evidence level on the model were not considered. Secondly, the value function and weight coefficients were based on a limited number of stakeholders, and the perspectives of other relevant parties, such as vaccine enterprises and vaccinators, were not included. Thirdly, this study included similar vaccines of different valencies in the vaccine value framework, thereby projecting a broader scope but a less comprehensive vaccine evaluation. Fourthly, the value framework consisting of 21 criteria makes it a practical tool for evaluating well-established vaccines. However, the comprehensive nature of the value framework may make it less suitable for evaluating new vaccine approvals (such as COVID-19 vaccines at the beginning of the pandemic). In the future, criteria with greater weighting can be selected for an expedited vaccine value framework to introduce new vaccines. Furthermore, this study did not conduct a budget impact analysis for the inclusion of vaccines in the NIP, which is a direction for future research.

The study attempted to address the challenge of balancing the baseline of performance entered into the MCDA framework by implementing the following measures: (1) for RCTs, selecting only those with vaccine alternatives in vaccination groups and placebos in control groups; (2) to control for other factors, the study selected economic evaluation literature containing multiple vaccine alternatives for vaccine efficacy evaluations. Additionally, due to the different publication times of economic evaluations, the ratio of ICER values to GDP per capita in the corresponding year was used as the performance metric; (3) in terms of the treatment cost of target diseases, vaccine affordability, and company capacity, the performance of the same year was used. Nevertheless, several challenges were encountered, including: (1) the performance of “annual number of deaths from disease” for the alternatives from literature published in different years added uncertainty to the scores; (2) the “quality of life improvement,” “number of deaths averted from disease per year,” and “ICER” for both PCV vaccines were obtained from two separate economic evaluation publications, which resulted in inconsistencies in parameters other than vaccine cost and protective efficacy; (3) in economic evaluations, Quality-Adjusted Life Years (QALYs) or Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) are frequently used as health utility indicators, and previous studies have demonstrated that QALYs obtained after vaccination should be greater than the DALYs avoided (56). However, treating QALYs and DALYs equally in this study may underestimate the value of the vaccine using DALYs.

Based on the results of this study, the following policy recommendations are proposed for all countries, including China, that plan to introduce vaccines into their NIPs:

Conclusions

This study rigorously followed the MCDA implementation steps to obtain the total value score of vaccine alternatives and prioritize their introduction into the NIP. The results indicated the priority for introduction as follows: HPV vaccine (domestic bivalent HPV vaccine), PCV vaccine (imported PCV vaccine), HIB vaccine (domestic HIB vaccine), and RV vaccine (domestic RV vaccine). The MCDA vaccine value framework developed in this study is adaptable for future use as new evidence emerges or new vaccines are introduced. Its strengths lie in its ability to incorporate diverse local health priorities, making it applicable across various settings. However, the model may require substantial data and resources, which could pose challenges in low-resource environments, and it necessitates adequate training for effective implementation. Overall, this study contributes to health decision-making research using MCDA and provides valuable insights for countries planning to introduce vaccines into their NIPs.

q q -Opers and Bethe Ansatz for Open Spin Chains I
Peter Koroteev, Myungbo Shim, Rahul Singh

Abstract
. In in a nutshell, the classical geometric q q -Langlands duality can be viewed as a correspondence between the space of ( G , q ) (G,q) -opers and the space of solutions of 𝔤 L {}^{L}\mathfrak{g} XXZ Bethe Ansatz equations. The latter describe spectra of closed spin chains with twisted periodic boundary conditions and, upon the duality, the twist elements are identified with the q q -oper connections on a projective line in a certain gauge. In this work, we initiate the geometric study of Bethe Ansatz equations for spin chains with open boundary conditions. We introduce the space of q q -opers whose defining sections are invariant under reflection through the unit circle in a selected gauge. The space of such reflection-invariant q q -opers in the presence of certain nondegeneracy conditions is thereby described by the corresponding Bethe Ansatz problem. We compare our findings with the existing results in integrable systems and representation theory. This paper discusses the type-A construction leaving the general case for the upcoming work.

The Geometric Langlands program has a multitude of layers with different levels of abstraction. In recent years there has been significant progress in understanding the geometric
q

[

The

Nevertheless, the ideas around

1.1.
q
-Opers,
QQ
-Systems and Bethe Ansatz

One such statement relates the space of
(G,q)
-opers for a simple simply-connected complex Lie group
G
and the space of solutions of
{}^{L}\mathfrak{g}
XXZ Bethe Ansatz equations for a closed spin chain with twisted periodic boundary conditions.
In full generality it was studied in
[
9
]
where an explicit
q
-difference oper connection was constructed as a meromorphic section
A\in\text{Hom}(\mathcal{F}_{G},\mathcal{F}^{q}_{G})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{G}
is a principal
G
-bundle over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{q}_{G}
is its pullback under the multiplicative
q
-shift of the base coordinate in the local trivialization
z\mapsto qz
. The
q
-oper condition imposes
A
to take values in a Coxeter Bruhat cell for the Borel subgroup
B_{-}\subset G
and the Miura condition ensures that
A
preserves the reduction to
\mathcal{F}_{B_{+}}
. The
q
-oper is then equipped with the set of regular singularities which are described by polynomials
\Lambda_{i}(z)
and twisted condition which requires
A
to be
q
-gauge equivalent to a regular semisimple element of the torus
A(z)=g(qz)Zg(z)^{-1}
, where
Z\in H\subset H(z)
.

In the presence of certain mild nondegeneracy conditions it was proved in
[
9
]
that the space of
(G,q)
-opers admits two equivalent descriptions. The former is the system of finite
q
-difference equations on the family of polynomials
\{Q_{\pm}^{i}(z)\}_{i=1,\dots,r}
referred to as the
QQ
-system for the Langlands dual group
{}^{L}G
. The Miura
(G,q)
-oper connection can thus be written as an element of
N_{-}(z)\prod_{i}\Lambda_{i}(z)^{\check{\alpha}_{i}}s_{i}N_{-}(z)\cap B_{+}(z)
. where
s_{i}
are lifts of the Weyl group of elements as

(1.1)

A(z)=\prod_{i}g_{i}(z)^{\check{\alpha}_{i}}\exp\frac{\Lambda_{i}(z)}{g_{i}(z)}\,,\qquad g_{i}(z)=\zeta_{i}\frac{Q_{+}^{i}(qz)}{Q_{+}^{i}(z)}\,.

The
QQ
-system equations can be viewed as obstructions to diagonalizing the above
q
-connection. These equations have been extensively studied in the literature on representation theory and quantum integrability.

The latter description is in terms of celebrated Bethe Ansatz equations for the XXZ spin chain which were studied in the representation theory literature
[
6
,
7
]
. The Bethe equations describe the spectrum of
U_{q}(^{L}\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
XXZ Hamiltonians (modulo some subtleties for non-simply laced groups, see
[
8
]
). The aforementioned Bethe Ansatz equations describe the spectrum of a
closed
quantum spin Hamiltonian which acts on the tensor product of evaluation modules of
U_{q}(^{L}\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
with
twisted periodic
boundary conditions.

On the left of Figure
1
tensor modules
V_{1}(a_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes V_{n}(a_{n})
from the Hilbert space of the XXZ Hamiltonians are represented with vertical arrows on a cylinder. One of the representations
V_{i}(a_{i})
is intertwined with all the others via
R
-matrices of
U_{q}(^{L}\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
(red circles) and in addition acted upon by the twist element
Z
. This action can be formalized in terms of the quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov equation (qKZ)
[
10
]

\Psi(q^{\sigma}a)=S_{\sigma}^{\text{closed}}\cdot\Psi(a)
where
\Psi(a)
is the state from the tensor product of the evaluation representations and
S_{\sigma}^{\text{closed}}
stands for the composition of the
R
-matrices and the twist element
Z
. Here
\sigma
labels the evaluation parameter which is being
q
-shifted. In the
q\longrightarrow 1
limit the qKZ equations become the eigenvalue equations for operators
S_{\sigma}^{\text{closed}}
and their spectrum can be obtained by solving Bethe Ansatz equations.

Orbifolding trick yields an open spin chain from a closed one.

In
[
20
]
and works that followed the qKZ connection and its solutions (as well as its mirror cousin dynamical equation where the twist variables are
q
-shifted) were understood geometrically through the equivariant quasimap counts to Nakajima quiver varieties.
This approach in part inspires our work as well. In fact, it would be an interesting question for enumerative geometry to find the setup which realizes our Bethe Ansatz equations for open chains.

1.3.
Folding

The left and right hand sides of

While the idea of folding has been discussed in the literature on integrable systems for a long time, to the best of our knowledge

-opers

-opers in the upcoming work.

In physics literature, in particular, in the context of the gauge/Bethe correspondence

13
,
5

2.
Reflection-invariant
(GL(2),q)
-Opers

We begin with developing our construction for
(GL(2),q)
-connections followed by the higher rank case in the next section. The beginning of this section goes along the lines of Section 3 of
[
14
]
. The construction follows the logic of
Z
-twisted Miura
q
-opers which were described using Bethe Ansatz for the XXZ spin chain.

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and not a root of unity. Consider the holomorphic vector bundle
E
of rank
2
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that is equivariant with respect to the scaling of the base
M_{q}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
that sends
z\mapsto qz
. Let
E^{q}
denote the pullback of
E
under
M_{q}
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a Zariski open dense subset
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
consider
V=U\cap M_{q}^{-1}(U)
. A meromorphic
(GL(2),q)
-oper on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a triple
(E,A,\mathcal{L})
, where
E
is a rank-two holomorphic vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\mathcal{L}\subset E
is a line subbundle, and
A\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{V}}(E,E^{q})
such that the restriction map

(2.1)

\bar{A}:\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\left(E/\mathcal{L}\right)^{q}

is an isomorphism on
V
.

The trivialization change of
E
via
g(z)\in G(z)
changes

A(z)
by the following
q
-gauge transformation

(2.2)

A(z)\mapsto g(qz)A(z)g(z)^{-1}.

giving
A
the structure of
(GL(2),q)
-connection.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\text{Span}(s(z))
in some trivialization on
V
. Then the oper condition (
2.1
) can be presented as

(2.3)

s(qz)\wedge A(z)s(z)=\Lambda(z)\,,

where roots of
\Lambda(z)
are the oper singularities.

Definition 2.2
.

A meromorphic Miura
(GL(2),q)
-oper on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a quadruple
(E,A,\mathcal{L},\hat{\mathcal{L}})
, where the triple
(E,A,\mathcal{L})
is the
(GL(2),q)
-oper as above and the line subbundle
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
is preserved by the
q
-connection
A(z)
.

We require that flags
\mathcal{L}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
are in generic relative position.

In local coordinates, in
\hat{\mathcal{L}}=\text{Span}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}
the
q
-connection takes the following form

(2.4)

A(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{1}(z)&\Lambda(z)\\
0&g_{2}(z)\\
\end{array}\right)

for some meromorphic functions
g_{1,2}(z)
and
\Lambda(z)
. In this gauge
\mathcal{L}=\text{Span}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The Miura
(G,q)
-oper is called
generalized
Z
-twisted
if there is a
q
-gauge transformation
v(z)\in B_{+}(z)
such that

(2.5)

A(z)=v(qz)Z(z)v(z)^{-1}

where
Z(z)\in H(z)
. In local coordinates,

(2.6)

Z(z)=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{1}(z)&0\\
0&\xi_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\,,

When
Z(z)\in H\subset H(z)
, we recover the
Z
-twisted
q
-oper.

In addition, we require that
Z(z)
is regular semisimple meaning that the locus where
\xi_{1}(z)=\xi_{2}(z)
should be excluded.

Now we chose the gauge where the
q
-connection has the form (
2.6
). Let the Miura gauge transformation be as follows

(2.7)

v(z)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}P_{1}(z)&\frac{P_{1}(z)Q_{-}(z)}{Q_{+}(z)}\\
0&P_{2}(z)\\
\end{array}\right)

where
P_{1,2},Q_{-}(z)\in\mathbb{C}(z)
such that in this gauge

(2.8)

s(z)=v(z)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Q_{-}(z)\\
Q_{+}(z)\end{pmatrix}\,.

Then the right hand side of the Miura condition for the generalized
Z
-twisted
q
-oper (
2.5
) is satisfied provided that

(2.9)

\frac{P_{2}(z)^{-1}P_{1}(qz)}{Q_{+}(z)Q_{+}(qz)}\left(\xi_{2}(z)Q_{+}(z)Q_{-}(qz)-\xi_{1}(z)Q_{-}(z)Q_{+}(qz)\right)=\Lambda(z)

as well as

g_{1}(z)=\frac{\xi_{1}(z)P_{1}(qz)}{P_{1}(z)},\quad g_{2}(z)=\frac{\xi_{2}(z)P_{2}(qz)}{P_{2}(z)}\,.

Since the right hand side of the above equation is a polynomial, so should be the left hand side. Our goal is to select functions
P_{1}(z)
and
P_{2}(z)
of the gauge transformation such that all poles cancel out. Indeed, if we assign

(2.10)

P_{2}(z)^{-1}=P_{1}(z)=Q_{+}(z)

this will be achieved so the
generalized
QQ
-system
reads

(2.11)

\xi_{2}(z)Q_{+}(z)Q_{-}(qz)-\xi_{1}(z)Q_{-}(z)Q_{+}(qz)=\Lambda(z)\,.

which is precisely the quantum Wronskian condition (
2.3
) for
s(z)
given by (
2.8
).
Here the adjective ‘generalized’ refers to the coordinate dependence of the twists.

This brings us to the following

Proposition 2.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of generalized
Z
-twisted Miura
(GL(2),q)
-opers and the set of solutions of the generalized
QQ
-system (
2.11
).

In the earlier work
[
14
]
line bundle
\mathcal{L}
was trivial on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\infty
so
Q_{\pm}(z)
could be chosen as (Taylor) polynomials. In this work, however, we study opers on a
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
orbifold of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
so we need to consider Laurent polynomials instead which have no poles other than
0,\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We shall discuss these matters momentarily and the orbifold condition will force the components of the section
s(z)
to belong to a subset of symmetric Laurent polynomials:
Q_{\pm}(z)\subset\mathbb{C}\left[z+\frac{1}{z}\right]\subset\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}]
.

2.1.
Reflection-Invariant
(GL(2),q)
-Opers

In order to achieve our goal of recovering Bethe Ansatz equations for the open spin chains, we need to supplement the above
q
-oper construction with the following
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
involution
T
acting on the base
\mathbb{P}^{1}
as
z\mapsto z^{-1}
.

Definition 2.5
.

A generalized
Z
-twisted Miura
(GL(2),q)
-oper is called
reflection-invariant
if there exists a
q
-gauge transformation
v(z)\in B_{+}(z)
such that
s(z)=v(z)\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}
is invariant under
T
:
s(\frac{1}{z})=s(z)
and the
q
-connection takes the form (
2.6
).

In other words, the reflection invariance of the
q
-oper assumes that there exists a gauge in which a section of the line
\mathcal{L}
is invariant under the reflection of the base local coordinate through the unit circle.

In particular in means that we can represent

(2.12)

Q_{+}(z)=\delta\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-s_{i})\left(\frac{1}{z}-s_{i}\right)

in this gauge. It is evident that
Q_{+}(z)\in\mathbb{C}\left[z+\frac{1}{z}\right]
.

We need the following lemma which is a consequence of the definition of determinants.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
F
be any field and let
M\in GL_{n}(F)
. For any
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in F^{n}
and
1\leq i\leq n
,
u_{1}\land\cdots\land u_{i-1}\land Mu_{i}\land u_{i+1}\land\cdots\land u_{n}
is equal to

(2.13)

\det(M)\left(M^{-1}u_{1}\right)\land\cdots\land\left(M^{-1}u_{i-1}\right)\land u_{i}\land\left(M^{-1}u_{i+1}\right)\land\cdots\land\left(M^{-1}u_{n}\right).

We will see below that the reflection invariance will impose certain transformation properties on the
q
-connection and
\Lambda(z)
which controls the oper singularities.

Proposition 2.7
.

For a generalized
Z
-twisted reflection-invariant Miura
(GL(2),q)
-oper we have the following condition on the oper singularities

(2.14)

\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=\Lambda(z).

Moreover, in the gauge where line
\mathcal{L}
is
T
-invariant, we have

(2.15)

A(z)A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=-\det A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)\cdot 1\,,

where
1
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix.

Proof.

The oper condition (
2.3
) at the point
\frac{1}{z}
reads

(2.16)

s\left(\frac{q}{z}\right)\land A\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)s\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{z}\right).

Since
s
is invariant under
T
, that is,
s\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=s\left(z\right)
, we have

(2.17)

s\left(\frac{q}{z}\right)\land A\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)s\left(z\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{z}\right).

Substituting
qz
in place of
z
, we have

(2.18)

s\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)\land A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)s\left(qz\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right).

By the invariance of
s(z)
under
T
, we get

(2.19)

s\left(z\right)\land A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)s\left(qz\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right).

The above lemma together with (
2.6
) gives

(2.20)

\left(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)^{-1}s\left(z\right)\land s\left(qz\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right),

or, equivalently

(2.21)

-s\left(qz\right)\land\left(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)^{-1}s\left(z\right)=\Lambda\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right).

i.e.,

(2.22)

-\left(\xi_{1}\xi_{2}\right)\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)A\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)^{-1}=A\left(z\right)\,,

and therefore
\Lambda(z)=\Lambda(1/qz)
in order to match the equation (
2.3
).
∎

The above proposition implies in particular, that the function
\Lambda(z)
has the following form

(2.23)

\Lambda(z)=\gamma\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-a_{i})\left(\frac{1}{qz}-a_{i}\right)\,,

where
\gamma
is some nonzero constant. The condition (
2.15
) takes the following form

(2.24)

\xi_{1}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=-\xi_{2}(z),\quad\xi_{2}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=-\xi_{1}(z)\,.

Notice that
Q_{+}(z)
in (
2.12
) is invariant under
T:z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}
whereas
\Lambda(z)
is invariant under
M_{q}\circ T:z\mapsto\frac{1}{qz}
.
In other words,
Q_{+}(z)\in\mathbb{C}[z+\frac{1}{z}]
whereas
\Lambda(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[z+\frac{1}{qz}]
.

2.2.
Nondegeneracy

Since each Laurent polynomial from
\mathbb{C}[z+\frac{1}{z}]
can be factored as
C\prod_{i}(z-t^{+}_{i})(1/z-t^{-}_{i})
, where
C
is a constant. We assume that no two zeroes of any of the functions that appear in the above theorem belong to the same integral
q
-lattice:
q^{\mathbb{Z}}t^{+}_{i}\cap q^{\mathbb{Z}}t^{-}_{j}=q^{\mathbb{Z}}(t^{+}_{i})^{\pm 1}\cap q^{\mathbb{Z}}(t^{+}_{j})^{\pm 1}=\varnothing
.
Notice that each such Laurent polynomial can be written as a Taylor polynomial
z^{-l}C\prod_{i}(z-t^{+}_{i})(1-t^{-}_{i}z)
divided by
z^{l}
for some positive
l
.

We shall refer to the
QQ
-system of the form (
2.11
) in the presence of the above nodegeneracy conditions for Laurent polynomials
Q_{\pm}(z)
and
\Lambda(z)
as well as rational functions
\xi_{1,2}(z)
as nondegenerate
QQ
-system.

2.3.
Bethe Ansatz Equations for Generalized
Z
-Twisted
(GL(2),q)
-Opers

Using the above nondegeneracy conditions we can formulate the following

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
Q_{+}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(z-s_{i})\left(\frac{1}{z}-s_{i}\right)
. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of nondegenerate generalized
Z
-twisted reflection-invariant Miura
(GL(2),q)
-opers and the set of nondegenerate solutions of the following generalized

XXZ Bethe equations

(2.25)

-\frac{\xi_{1}(s_{i})}{\xi_{2}(q^{-1}s_{i})}\frac{Q_{+}(qs_{i})}{Q_{+}(q^{-1}s_{i})}\frac{\Lambda(q^{-1}s_{i})}{\Lambda(s_{i})}=1\,,\quad i=1,\dots,k

Remark 2.9
.

Notice that without imposing the reflection invariance condition
Q_{\pm}(z)
and
\Lambda(z)
can be considered as arbitrary not necessarily symmetric Laurent polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[z,z^{-1}]
and the above theorem would also hold.

The statement is proved analogously in the presence of the above nondegeneracy conditions to Theorem 3.6 of
[

]
where one evaluates both sides of (

) at

.

Note that instead of evaluating the

Bethe equations (

-opers. At the moment any rational functions

that satisfy (

2.4.1.
Interlude: Differential Connections

The choice of these asymptotic conditions is motivated by similar analysis for differential connections
a(z)\in\text{End}(E)\otimes K
, where
K
is the canonical bundle. For a differential connection, one typically assumes mild (tame) singularity at
z=0
so that
a(z)=a_{-1}/z+a_{0}+O(z)
, where
a_{-1},a_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
for some Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}
. Then, if we also know the asymptotic behavior of the connection at infinity, then by Riemann-Roch theorem we can obtain some information about zeros and poles in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\backslash\{0,\infty\}
.

Let us first consider such a prototypical example. Assume that
a(z)=a_{0}dz+O(z)
as
z\longrightarrow 0
and that in a local trivialization
a(z)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}(z)&0\\
0&x_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}dz

for some rational functions
x_{1,2}(z)
satisfying a similar condition to (
2.24
), i.e.
x_{1}(z^{-1})=-x_{2}(z)
. In this case, we get
a(z)=a^{\prime}_{0}dz+O(z^{-1})
as
z\longrightarrow\infty
. However, in the punctured neighborhood of
\infty\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
the local coordinate is
w=\frac{1}{z}
thus
dz=-\frac{dw}{w^{2}}
so the connection has a double pole. Since the degree of any canonical divisor on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
should be equal to
-2
we are allowed to add equal number of zeros and poles. For instance,

(2.26)

x_{1}(z)=\frac{(z-a)(z-b)}{(z-c)(z-d)}

for nonzero complex
a,b,c
, and

On the other hand, if we assume that the connection has a simple pole at the origin

2.5.3.
Bethe Ansatz for
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
Open Spin Chains

Bethe equations (
2.34
) first appeared in the seminal work of Sklyanin
[
23
]
where he used the quantum inverse scattering method. Later in
[
24
]
the same equations were reproduced from the representation theory of quantum affine algebra
U_{q}(\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2})
. These equations describe the XXZ spin chain on
n
sites in the sector with
k
excitations with diagonal boundary conditions determined by parameters
\mu,\tilde{\mu}
. The Hilbert space of the corresponding quantum mechanical problem is the evaluation tensor module
\mathbb{C}^{2}(a_{1})\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}(a_{n})
where
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is the fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. The diagonal boundary conditions appear in a particular solution of the reflection equation.

In our geometric setting, (
2.34
) are realized from
q
-connections that approach a constant at
z=0
.

Yang, Nepomechie, and Zhang
[
26
]
derived equations (
2.35
) using the most general solution of the reflection equation which involves one additional parameter for the left and right boundary of the spin chain
b
and
\tilde{b}
. We provide the exact match of notations in Sec.
5
.

Notice that the is not direct limit like
b,\tilde{b}\longrightarrow 0
under which (
2.35
) turns into (
2.34
). One needs to additionally scale variables
\mu
and
\tilde{\mu}
to make it work.

In our geometric setting, (
2.35
) are realized from
q
-connections which have a simple pole at
z=0
. Therefore, generalized
Z
-twisted reflection-invariant
(GL(2),q)
-opers whose connections have at most
tame
singularities are described by
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
XXZ Bethe equations with generic open boundary conditions.

2.6.
Attenuation

In the above construction we had
a_{0}=q\mu\tilde{\mu}
for the constant asymptotic conditions of
A(z)
. In other words, the
q
-connection approaches diagonal matrix
Z=\text{diag}(1,a_{0})
at infinity. In other words, this limit reproduces the
q
-connection behavior for closed chains. This phenomenon is called
attenuation
in the spin chain literature
[
18
]
.

3.
Reflection-invariant
(GL(N),q)
-Opers

Now let us study the higher rank setup.

3.1.
(GL(N),q)
-Opers

Let
q\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
. Consider the holomorphic vector bundle
E
of rank
N
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
that is equivariant with respect to the scaling of the base
M_{q}:\mathbb{P}^{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
that sends
z\mapsto qz
. Let
E^{q}
denote the pullback of
E
under
M_{q}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A meromorphic
(GL(N),q)
-oper on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a triple
(E,A,\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})
where for an open Zariski dense subset
U\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
V=U\cap M_{q}^{-1}(U)
the
q
-connection
A\in\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}(V)}(E,E^{q})
satisfies the following conditions on the complete flag
\mathcal{L}_{\bullet}
of subbundles
\mathcal{L}_{1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{L}_{N-1}\subset E

i)
A\cdot\mathcal{L}_{i}\subset\mathcal{L}_{i+1}

ii) The induced maps
\bar{A_{i}}:\mathcal{L}_{i+1}/\mathcal{L}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{i}/\mathcal{L}_{i-1}
are isomorphisms.

Changing the trivialization of
E
via
g(z)\in SL(N)(z)
is given by the following
q
-gauge transformation

(3.1)

A(z)\mapsto g(qz)A(z)g(z)^{-1}\,.

Definition 3.2
.

We say that the oper is
generalized
Z
-twisted
if there is a gauge change
g(z)\in GL(N)(z)
such that
g(qz)A(z)g(z)^{-1}=Z(z)\in H(z)

Remark 3.3
.

At this point, we observe a deviation from
[
9
]
and previous work on
q
-opers and Bethe Ansatz. Indeed, a generalized
Z
-twisted
q
-connection can be diagonalized into a connection that takes values in loops valued in
H
, not just in constant loops.

Definition 3.4
.

A Miura
(GL(N),q)
-oper is a quadruple
(E,A,\mathcal{L}_{\bullet},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet})
where
(E,A,\mathcal{L}_{\bullet})
is a
(GL(N),q)
-oper and the complete flag
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}
of subbundles is preserved by the
q
-connection.

Definition 3.5
.

The Miura
(GL(N),q)
-oper is called
generalized
Z
-twisted
if there is a
q
-gauge transformation
v(z)\in B_{+}(z)
such that

(3.2)

A(z)=v(qz)Z(z)v(z)^{-1}

where
Z(z)\in H(z)
.

Let as denote
Z(z)=\text{diag}(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{N})(z)
where
\xi_{i}(z)\in\mathbb{C}(z)
.

The following statement generalizes a similar result for
Z
-twisted
q
-opers from earlier work.

Proposition 3.6
.

There are exactly
N!
Miura
(GL(N),q)
-opers for a given generalized
Z
-twisted
(GL(N),q)
-oper provided that
Z(z)
is regular semisimple.

At certian loci on the base of
E
this condition will fail and it will become important for the construction to follow. In particular,
\xi_{i}(z)
may have poles and zeros.

Definition 3.7
.

We say that a (Miura)
(GL(N),q)
-oper has regular singularities defined by a collection of Laurent polynomials
\Lambda_{i}(z)
for
i=1,\dots,N-1
if the induced maps
\bar{A}_{i}
in the the Definition
3.1
are isomorphisms away from the roots of these polynomials.

Remark 3.8
.

Another deviation from
[
9
]
– all components of sections of subbundles of
E
as well as
\Lambda_{i}(z)
need to be considered as Laurent polynomials. In what follows, unless specified, we will be dealing with Laurent polynomials.

The generalized
Z
-twisted condition of the Miura
q
-oper implies that in the gauge when
A
is equivalent to
Z(z)\in H(z)
the flag
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}
is formed by the standard basis
e_{1},\dots,e_{N}
. The relative position between flags
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\bullet}
is generic on
V\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
s(z)
to be the local section that generates
\mathcal{L}_{1}
according to the Definition
3.1
. The regular singularity condition implies that
q
-Wronskians, namely the determinants

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{k}(s)=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge{e_{N-k}}

\displaystyle\wedge s(q^{k-1}z)\wedge Z(q^{k-2}z)s(q^{k-2}z)\wedge Z(q^{k-2}z)Z(q^{k-1}z)s(q^{k-1}z)\wedge\dots\wedge Z(q^{k-2}z)\cdots Z(z)s(z)\,

have a subset of zeroes, which come from the following Laurent polynomials

(3.4)

\displaystyle W_{k}(s)=P_{1}(z)\cdot P_{2}(q^{2}z)\cdots P_{k}(q^{k-1}z),\qquad P_{k}(z)=\Lambda_{N-1}\Lambda_{N-2}\cdots\Lambda_{N-k}(z)\,.

Another subset of zeros of
\mathcal{D}_{k}(s)
corresponds to points on the base where the two flags are not in a generic relative position, i.e., when one of the vectors
Z^{k-j}s(q^{j-1})
becomes parallel to a vector from the standard basis. Therefore, the Miura condition is equivalent to the existence of nonzero constants
\alpha_{k}
and Laurent polynomials
\mathcal{V}_{k}(z)

for which the following equations hold

(3.5)

\mathcal{D}_{k}(s)=\alpha_{k}W_{k}\mathcal{V}_{k}(s)\,,

In components, the above functions can be represented as determinants of the matrix
M_{N-k+1,\dots,N}

(3.6)

\left(M_{N-k+1,\dots,N}\right)_{i,j}=\xi_{N-k+i}(q^{k-1}z)\cdots\xi_{N-k+i}(q^{k-j}z)\cdot s_{N-k+i}(q^{k-j}z)\,,\quad i,j=1,\dots,k

The boundary conditions for (
3.5
) are

(3.7)

\mathcal{D}_{N}(s)=W_{N}(s)\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{N}(s)=1\,,\qquad\mathcal{D}_{0}=e_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{N}=1\,.

However, for the purposes of this exposition, it will be sufficient to keep only
\Lambda_{N}(z)=\Lambda(z)
nontrivial whereas
\Lambda_{i}(z)=1
for
i=1,\dots,N-1
. It means that (
3.5
) can be viewed as

(3.8)

\mathcal{D}_{k}(s)(z)=\mathcal{V}_{k}(s)(z)\,,\quad k=1,\dots,N-1\,,\qquad\mathcal{V}_{N}(s)(z)=\Lambda(z)\,.

Generalizations for the complete set of singularities are straightforward.

The first determinant
\mathcal{D}_{1}(s)(z)=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{N-1}\wedge s(z)
is then equal to
\mathcal{V}_{1}(z)
, which will later be identified with
Q_{1}^{+}(z)
, if we put
\alpha_{1}=1
.

Example:
(GL(4),q)
-Oper

For the
(GL(4),q)

reads

\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}s_{1}\left(q^{3}z\right)&s_{1}\left(q^{2}z\right)\xi_{1}\left(q^{2}z\right)&s_{1}(qz)\xi_{1}(qz)\xi_{1}\left(q^{2}z\right)&s_{1}(z)\xi_{1}(z)\xi_{1}(qz)\xi_{1}\left(q^{2}z\right)\\

Notice that all columns are sections of

. One then has
\mathcal{D}_{4}(s)=\det M_{1,2,3,4}(s)
. At the same time, matrix
M_{3,4}
for
(GL(4),q)
-opers read

(3.9)

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}s_{3}(qz)&s_{3}(z)\xi_{3}(z)\\

3.2.
The
QQ
-System

As it was shown in
[
14
,
16
]
all
Q^{\pm}
functions can be understood as certain (generalized) minors of the matrix
M_{1,\dots,N}
. In the present setup we need to update that construction to iterated products of
q
-shifted twist elements.

The minors in question obey the famous combinatorial Jacobi/Lewis Carroll identity which for our matrices
M
can be written as

(3.10)

\det M^{1}_{1}\det M^{2}_{N}-\det M^{1}_{N}\det M^{2}_{1}=\det M^{1,2}_{1,N}\det M\,,

where
M^{a}_{b}
denotes a submatrix of
M
which is obtained from
M
by removing
a
th row and
b
th column.

We can see that if remove the first row and the first column of
M_{N-k+1,\dots,N}
we will get the matrix
M_{N-k+2,\dot{,}N}
whose
i
th row is multiplied by
\xi_{i}(q^{k-2})(z)
. Similar findings can be made about other submatrices from (
3.10
).

Let us denote

(3.11)

Q^{+}_{k}(z)=\det M_{N-k+1,\dots,N}(z)\,,\qquad Q^{-}_{k}(z)=\det M_{N-k,N-k-2\dots,N}(z)\,.

It is easy to see that

(3.12)

(M_{N-k,\dots,N})^{1}_{1}(z)=\xi_{N-k+1}\cdots\xi_{N}(q^{k-2})\cdot Q^{+}_{k}(z)\,,\qquad(M_{N-k,\dots,N})^{2}_{N}(z)=Q^{-}_{k}(qz)\,.

as well as

(3.13)

(M_{N-k,\dots,N})^{1}_{N}(z)=Q^{+}_{k}(qz)\,,\qquad(M_{N-k,\dots,N})^{2}_{1}(z)=\xi_{N-k}\cdot\xi_{N-k+2}\cdots\xi_{N}(q^{k-2}z)\cdot Q^{-}_{k}(z)\,.

At the same time

(3.14)

(M_{N-k,\dots,N})^{12}_{1N}(z)=\xi_{N-k+2}\cdots\xi_{N}(q^{k-2}z)\cdot Q^{+}_{k-1}(qz)\,.

This brings us to the following

Theorem 3.9
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
nondegenerate generalized
Z
-twisted Miura
(GL(N),q)
-opers and the set of nondegenerate Laurent polynomial solutions of the
QQ
-system

(3.15)

\xi_{N-k+1}(q^{k-1}z)Q^{+}_{k}(z)Q^{-}_{k}(qz)-\xi_{N-k}(q^{k-1}z)Q^{+}_{k}(qz)Q^{-}_{k}(z)=Q^{+}_{k-1}(qz)Q^{+}_{k+1}(z)\,,

where
k=1,\dots,N-1
and
Q^{+}_{N}(z)=\Lambda(z)

Note that under this identification, the last component of the section vector
s(z)
is
Q_{1}^{+}(z)
whereas the penultimate component is
Q_{1}^{+}(z)
. The other components can be obtained from
Q_{1}^{\pm}(z)
via so-called Bäcklund-type transformations
[
9
,
16
]
.

3.3.
Nondegeneracy

Each Laurent polynomial can be factored as
C\prod_{i}(z-t^{+}_{i})\prod_{j}(1/z-t^{-}_{j})
, where
C
is a constant. We assume that no two zeroes of any of the functions that appear in the above theorem belong the the same integral
q
-lattice:
q^{\mathbb{Z}}t^{+}_{i}\cap q^{\mathbb{Z}}t^{-}_{j}=q^{\mathbb{Z}}(t^{+}_{i})^{\pm 1}\cap q^{\mathbb{Z}}(t^{+}_{j})^{\pm 1}=\varnothing
.

Notice that each Laurent polynomial can be written as a Taylor polynomial
z^{-l}C\prod_{i}(z-t^{+}_{i})\prod_{j}(1-t^{-}_{j}z)
divided by
z^{l}
for some positive
l
.

Definition 3.10
.

Upon such nondegeneracy conditions, the system of functional
q
-difference equations (
3.15
) is called nondegenerate
GL(N)

QQ
-system.

3.4.
Bethe Equations

The correspondence between the
QQ
-system and the Bethe Ansatz equations follows, as in the story for the

-twisted

-opers, after evaluating both sides of (
3.15
) before and after a
q
-shift at zeros

of

.

Theorem 3.11
.

The solutions of the nondegenerate
GL(N)

QQ
-system are in one-to-one correspondence to the solutions of the Bethe Ansatz equations for rank-
N-1

(3.16)

-1=\frac{\xi_{N-k+1}(q^{k-2}s_{k,i,})}{\xi_{N-k}(q^{k-1}s_{k,i})}\frac{Q^{+}_{k-1}(qs_{k,i})Q^{+}_{k}(q^{-1}s_{k,i})Q^{+}_{k+1}(s_{k,i})}{Q^{+}_{k-1}(s_{k,i})Q^{+}_{k}(qs_{k,i})Q^{+}_{k+1}(q^{-1}s_{k,i})},

where
k=1,\dots,N-1
.

The exact form of the above Bethe equations depends on the functions
Q^{\pm}_{i}
and
\xi_{i}
. If
Q^{\pm}_{i}
are polynomials and
\xi_{i}
are nonzero constants then the Bethe equations are given in
[

]
. In the next section, we shall impose an additional condition on the section

of the line
\mathcal{L}_{1}
which will force

to be Laurent polynomials.

Reflection-Invariant Conditions

The following definition is a straightforward generalization of Def.

Definition 3.12

A generalized

(GL(N),q)
-oper is called

and the

The following is a straightforward generalization of the proof of Proposition

.

Proposition 3.13
.

For the reflection-invariant generalized

-twisted Miura
(GL(N),q)
-opers in a gauge where
\mathcal{L}
is

-invariant, the following conditions are satisfied

(3.17)

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{k}(z)=Q^{+}_{k}\left(\frac{1}{q^{k-1}z}\right),

\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq N,

and

(3.18)

\displaystyle-\xi_{N}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)\xi_{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=\xi_{i}(z)\xi_{i}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right),

\displaystyle k=2,\,1\leq i\leq N

and

(3.19)

\displaystyle\xi_{N}\left(\frac{1}{q^{k-1}z}\right)\xi_{N-1}\left(\frac{1}{q^{k-1}z}\right)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\xi_{N+1-k}\left(\frac{1}{q^{i}z}\right)}{\prod_{i=1}^{k-3}\xi_{N+2-k}\left(\frac{1}{q^{i+1}z}\right)},

\displaystyle 3\leq k\leq N.

Proof.

For
k=1
, we trivially have

(3.20)

\displaystyle Q^{+}_{1}(z)=Q^{+}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{z}\right).

For

.

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{2}(z)=e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{N-2}\wedge Z(z)s(z)\wedge s(qz),

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{2}\left(\frac{1}{qz}\right)=-\det(Z(1/qz))\xi^{-1}_{1}(1/qz)\cdots\xi^{-1}_{N-2}(1/qz)

(3.21)

\displaystyle\times\left(e_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{N-2}\wedge Z^{-1}(1/qz)s(z)\wedge s(qz)\right),

therefore

(3.22)

Z^{-1}(1/qz)=-\xi_{N}^{-1}(1/qz)\xi_{N-1}^{-1}(1/qz)Z(z),\qquad Q^{+}_{2}(z)=Q^{+}_{2}(1/qz),

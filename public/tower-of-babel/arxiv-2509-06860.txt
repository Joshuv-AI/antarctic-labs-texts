Automorphism groups of Inoue surfaces S ( + ) / S ( − ) S^{(+)}/S^{(-)}
David Petcu

Abstract
. We present the construction of Inoue surfaces of type S ( + ) / S ( − ) S^{(+)}/S^{(-)} in terms of data arising from real quadratic number fields. We then describe the automorphism group of such surfaces in terms of this data.

1.
Introduction

Inoue surfaces were introduced by Inoue as examples of minimal non-Kahler compact complex surfaces with Kodaira dimension
-\infty
and having no curves. Later, it was proven by Bogomolov
[
1
]
and Teleman
[
10
]
that they are the only surfaces with
kod=-\infty
, first Betti number equal to
1
, second Betti number equal to
0
and no curves.

The problem of studying automorphism groups of compact complex non-Kähler surfaces starts with Namba’s results on Hopf surfaces
[
7
]
(1974). In
[
4
]
(2005), Fujimoto and Nakayama classify compact complex surfaces admitting nontrivial surjective endomorphisms. More recently, Prokhorov and Shramov prove in
[
9
]
(2020) that automorphism groups of Inoue and primary Kodaira surfaces have the Jordan property.

In this paper we give a more explicit description of the automorphism group of Inoue surfaces of type
S^{(+)}/S^{(-)}
. To achieve this, we first present in Section
3.1
, a new way of constructing the Inoue surfaces. Our approach is closely related to that of Teleman
[
6
]
, but we adopt a slightly different formulation which we find more suitable for the study of automorphisms. This is because our formulation allows us to decide when an automorphism of the universal cover descends to an automorphism of the surface, by expressing the condition in terms of the data arising from the associated number field (see proposition
3.6
and proposition
3.7
).

For a surface of type
S^{(+)}
we first prove in Section
3.2
that the automorphism group is an extension of a discrete group
Q
by
\mathbb{C}^{*}
. This result follows relatively easily from the definition of the surface, the more challenging aspect being to understand the group
Q
. We next define in Section
3.3
a finite metabelian group
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}

3.5
(which depends on a quadratic real number field
\mathbb{Q}[X]/(X^{2}-\theta X+1)
and a fractional ideal
I
of it) and show that
Q
identifies canonically with a subgroup of it; in particular, it follows that
Q
is finite and metabelian. We also give precise conditions for a given element of
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
to belong to
Q
. We use these conditions to write a short algorithm for computing
Q
. Next, in Section
3.4
we present some worked out examples of explicit computation of the group
Q
. Eventually, the last section
4
is devoted to the study of surfaces
S^{(-)}
. For a surface of type
S^{(-)}
, we prove that the automorphisms group is an extension of a finite metabelian group
Q
by
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

2.
Basic facts

Inoue introduced three families of compact complex surfaces,
S^{0}
,
S^{(+)}
and
S^{(-)}
. They are examples of surfaces of Kodaira class
VII
(meaning that
b_{1}=1
and
kod=-\infty
) and have the property that they do not contain curves. In this paper we will focus on the latter two types. We begin by recalling the original construction of Inoue surfaces of type
S^{(+)}
/
S^{(-)}
, as they were introduced in
[
5
]
.

Surfaces of type
\mathbf{S^{(+)}}
:
Let
N=(n_{ij})\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
be a matrix with real eigenvalues
\alpha
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
\alpha>1
. Let
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2})
be eigenvectors of
N
associated to
\alpha
and
\frac{1}{\alpha}
, respectively. Let
p,q,r
be integers,
r>0
, and let
t
be a complex number. Let
G^{(+)}_{N,p,q,r;t}
be the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
generated by

(1)

\begin{split}&g_{0}(w,z)=(\alpha w,z+t)\\
&g_{i}(w,z)=(w+a_{i},z+b_{i}w+c_{i})\ \ \text{ for }i\in\{1,2\}\\
&g_{3}(w,z)=(w,z+d)\end{split}

where
d=\frac{b_{1}a_{2}-b_{2}a_{1}}{r}
and
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the unique solution of the system

(2)

(N-I_{2})\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\end{pmatrix}-d\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}

Here,
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are defined by

e_{i}=\frac{1}{2}n_{i1}(n_{i1}-1)a_{1}b_{1}+\frac{1}{2}n_{i2}(n_{i2}-1)a_{2}b_{2}+n_{i1}n_{i2}b_{1}a_{2}

One may show that the action of
G_{N,p,q,r;t}^{(+)}
on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
is properly discontinuous and without fixed points.

Definition 2.1
.

The compact complex surface obtained as the quotient of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
by the action of
G^{(+)}_{N,p,q,r;t}
is denoted by
S^{(-)}_{N,p,q,r}
and it is called an Inoue surface of type
S^{(+)}
.

Surfaces of type
\mathbf{S^{(-)}}
:
Let
N=(n_{ij})\in\text{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
,
\det(N)=-1
, with real eigenvalues
\alpha
and
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
,
\alpha>1
. Let
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2})
be eigenvectors of
N
associated to
\alpha
and
-\frac{1}{\alpha}
, respectively. Let
p,q,r
be integers,
r>0
. Let
G_{N,p,q,r}^{(-)}
be the group of automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
generated by

(3)

\begin{split}&g_{0}(w,z)=(\alpha w,-z)\\
&g_{i}(w,z)=(w+a_{i},z+b_{i}w+c_{i})\ \ \text{ for }i\in\{1,2\}\\
&g_{3}(w,z)=(w,z+d)\end{split}

where
d=\frac{b_{1}a_{2}-b_{2}a_{1}}{r}
and
(c_{1},c_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the unique solution of the system

(4)

(N+I_{2})\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}e_{1}\\
e_{2}\end{pmatrix}-d\begin{pmatrix}p\\
q\end{pmatrix}

Here,
e_{1},e_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
are defined by

e_{i}=\frac{1}{2}n_{i1}(n_{i1}-1)a_{1}b_{1}+\frac{1}{2}n_{i2}(n_{i2}-1)a_{2}b_{2}+n_{i1}n_{i2}b_{1}a_{2}

Once again, the action of
G^{(-)}_{N,p,q,r}
on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
is properly discontinuous and without fixed points.

Definition 2.2
.

The compact complex surface obtained as the quotient of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
by the action of
G^{(-)}_{N,p,q,r}
is denoted by
S^{(-)}_{N,p,q,r}
and it is called an Inoue surface of type
S^{(-)}
.

As explained in the introduction of
[
6
]
, the original notation for Inoue surfaces of type
S^{(+)}
may suggest that the biholomorphism class of the surface is independent of the choice of eigenvectors
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2})
. However, this is not the case. One can show that, up to biholomorphism, varying the choice for the eigenvectors
(a_{1},a_{2})
and
(b_{1},b_{2})
(while keeping the matrix
N
fixed) is equivalent to making a corresponding change in the parameters
p,q
and
t
. The analogous statement for surfaces of type
S^{(-)}
is also true.

Theorem 2.1

(Inoue
[
5
]
)
.

i) For
X
Inoue surface of type
S^{(+)}
,
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X})=1
.

ii) For
X
Inoue surface of type
S^{(-)}
,
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X})=0
.

Before proceeding, we recall the following well-known result.

Theorem 2.2

(Bochner, Montgomery
[
2
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold, then the group of biholomorphisms
\Aut(X)
has a natural complex Lie group structure. Moreover, the associated Lie algebra is
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{T}_{X})
(where
\mathcal{T}_{X}
is the holomorphic tangent sheaf of
X
).

From theorem
2.1
and theorem
2.2
above, we can see that the automorphism group of an Inoue surface is of dimension
1
(as a complex Lie group) when the surface is of type
S^{(+)}
and it is discrete when the surface is of type
S^{(-)}
.

We will denote by
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
the group affine maps
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
for which
f(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})=\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
. More precisely

\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}w\\
z\end{pmatrix}\to\begin{pmatrix}\mu&0\\
\lambda&\nu\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w\\
z\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}l\\
s\end{pmatrix}|\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{>};\ \lambda,s\in\mathbb{C};\ \nu\in\mathbb{C}^{*};\ l\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

Remark 2.1
.

All Inoue surfaces arise as quotients of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
under the action of a subgroup of
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})

[
5
]
. For Inoue surfaces of type
S^{(+)}
or
S^{(-)}
, this subgroup can be assumed to lie in the (real) Lie subgroup
\mathcal{G}\leq\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
defined as:

\mathcal{G}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\{\pm 1\}&\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{C}\\
&\mathbb{R}_{>}&\mathbb{R}\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}

where a matrix element of
\mathcal{G}
acts on
(w,z)\in\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
by left multiplication with the column vector
\begin{pmatrix}z&w&1\end{pmatrix}^{\mathsf{T}}
(compare
[
11
]
, pp. 146–147).

Next, we will give a different presentation of the group
\mathcal{G}
, see expression
5
below. This is precisely the presentation that is used in
[
8
]
, but it is written in such a way as to allow the case where
t\neq 0
. The group
\mathcal{G}
is the semidirect product:

\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{R}^{*}\ltimes\mathcal{H}

where
\mathcal{H}
is the mixed Heisenberg group

\mathcal{H}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\mathbb{R}&\mathbb{C}\\
&1&\mathbb{R}\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}

and
\mathbb{R}^{*}
is embedded as:

\alpha\to\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\alpha}{|\alpha|}&0&0\\
&|\alpha|&0\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}

The Lie algebra of
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathfrak{h}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}
with the bracket

[(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})]=(0,0,b_{1}a_{2}-a_{1}b_{2})

and exponential map

\exp(a,b,c)=\begin{pmatrix}1&b&c+\frac{ab}{2}\\
&1&a\\
&&1\end{pmatrix}

In this case the exponential map is a diffeomorphism, which allows us to identify
\mathcal{H}
with its Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{h}
. By this we mean that the elements of
\mathcal{H}
can be expressed in logarithmic coordinates via the inverse of the exponential map. With respect to these coordinates, the group law is given by the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff formula:

(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})\cdot(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})=\left(a_{1}+a_{2},\ b_{1}+b_{2},\ c_{1}+c_{2}-\frac{a_{1}b_{2}-b_{1}a_{2}}{2}\right)

Thus, we see that
\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{*}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
and the group operation may be explicitly written as:

(5)

\begin{split}&[\alpha_{1},(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})]\cdot[\alpha_{2},(a_{2},b_{2},c_{2})]=\\
&=\left[\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2},\ \left(a_{1}+|\alpha_{1}|a_{2},\ b_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}b_{2},\ c_{1}+\frac{\alpha_{1}}{|\alpha_{1}|}c_{2}-\frac{a_{1}b_{2}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}-b_{1}a_{2}|\alpha_{1}|}{2}\right)\right]\end{split}

This is our preferred way of presenting the group
\mathcal{G}
and it will be used throughout the following sections. The next result will prove very useful, since it reduces our search for the automorphism groups to algebraic computations in the group of affinities.

Theorem 2.3

(Khaled, Teleman
[
6
]
)
.

Let
S
be an Inoue surface,
f\in\Aut(S)
and
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
a lift of
f
. Then
\tilde{f}\in\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
.

3.
Surfaces of type
S^{(+)}

3.1.
A slightly different construction

In this section we will introduce an alternative way of presenting Inoue surfaces
S^{(+)}
. In definition
3.3
, we introduce a group of analytic automorphisms of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
, depending on a set of parameters arising from a real quadratic number field. Then, in definition
3.4
, we introduce the associated surface of such a group. In proposition
3.2
we show that the resulting surfaces are indeed of Inoue type
S^{(+)}
and in proposition
3.3
, we prove that our construction recovers all the possible examples of surfaces of this type. We begin by setting up some notations and making some remarks on the properties of the data that will be used for the construction.

Let
\theta
be an integer,
\theta\geq 3
, consider the polynomial

(6)

f_{\theta}(X)=X^{2}-\theta X+1

and the associated real quadratic number field
K=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(f_{\theta})
.
Let
u_{\theta}\coloneqq\hat{X}\in K
, it is clear that
u_{\theta}
is an algebraic integer and, in fact, a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}:K\to\mathbb{R}
be the two embeddings of
K
. We now fix the convention that
\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})>1
. The set

(7)

\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\coloneqq\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}|\sigma_{1}(u)>0\}

is a subgroup of the multiplicative group of units
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}
.
Since
K
is real quadratic, from Dirichlet’s unit theorem, we get that
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times}\simeq\{\pm 1\}\times\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\eta
be the fundamental unit of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(
\sigma_{1}(\eta)>1
), we then have that
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\simeq\langle\eta\rangle
and
\langle u_{\theta}\rangle\leq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}
is a subgroup of finite index. More precisely

(8)

[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}:\langle u_{\theta}\rangle]=\frac{\text{log}(\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta}))}{\text{log}(\sigma_{1}(\eta))}

Let
I
be a fractional ideal of the order
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
; then, as a group,
I
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Hence we may choose some
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
(x_{1},x_{2})
for
I
. The multiplicative action of
u_{\theta}
on the ideal
I
, with respect to the basis
(x_{1},x_{2})
, is expressed by a matrix
N_{\theta,x_{1},x_{2}}\in\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
with trace
\theta
, such that

(9)

N_{\theta,x_{1},x_{2}}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}=u_{0}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}

Definition 3.1
.

Let
K
be the real quadratic number field
K=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(X^{2}-\theta X+1)
for some integer
\theta\geq 3
. Let
u_{\theta}=\hat{X}\in K
and let
I
be a fractional ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. Denote by
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
the subgroup of
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}
(see
7
) containing those units that leave the ideal
I
invariant (as a set), that is:

{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\coloneqq\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}|uI=I\}

The condition that a unit
u
is contained in
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
is equivalent to
I
being a
\mathbb{Z}[u]
-fractional ideal.

We will use
\Norm(\cdot)
and
\Trace(\cdot)
for the norm and trace in
K
respectively and we will denote by
\varphi:K\to K
the nontrivial Galois automorphism of
K
. We will use
\chi
to denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-bilinear form
\chi:K\times K\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(10)

\chi(x,y)=\sigma_{1}(x)\sigma_{2}(y)-\sigma_{1}(y)\sigma_{2}(x)

Although the following properties of the form
\chi
are straightforward to verify, we record them here, as they will be intensively used in the upcoming computations:

\bullet

The form
\chi
is antisymmetric.

\bullet

\chi(xy_{1},y_{2})=\chi(y_{1},\varphi(x)y_{2})
, for all
x,y_{1},y_{2}\in K
.

\bullet

\chi(xy_{1},xy_{2})=\Norm(x)\chi(y_{1},y_{2})
, for all
x,y_{1},y_{2}\in K.

Proposition 3.1
.

There is a natural map from the set
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\times K\times\mathbb{C}
to the group
\mathcal{G}
(see
5
):

\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}:\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\times K\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{G}\ \ \ \ \ \ \iota_{\theta}^{(+)}([u,x,t])=[\sigma_{1}(u)\Norm(u),(\sigma_{1}(x),\sigma_{2}(x),t)]

The map
\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}
is injective and its image is a subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
.

Proof.

To prove that the image is a subgroup it is enough to observe that:

i)

\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}([u,x,t])\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}([v,y,s])=\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}\left(\left[uv,\ x+uy,\ t+\Norm(u)s-\frac{\chi(x,uy)}{2}\right]\right)

ii)

\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}([1,0,0])=[1,(0,0,0)]
, which is the identity of
\mathcal{G}
.

iii)

\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}([u,x,t])\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}\left(\left[\frac{1}{u},-\frac{x}{u},-\frac{t}{\Norm(u)}\right]\right)=[1,(0,0,0)]

The injectivity follows directly from the definition.
∎

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
be the group
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\times K\times\mathbb{C}
, with the group structure induced by the above embedding
\iota_{\theta}^{(+)}
in
\mathcal{G}
.

As seen in the proof of proposition
3.1
, the group structure on
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
is given by

[u,x,t][v,y,s]=\left[uv,\ x+uy,\ t+\Norm(u)s-\frac{\chi(x,uy)}{2}\right]

This implies that
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
is a group extension of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}\ltimes K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

where
\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}
acts on

by multiplication. Moreover, the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
coming from the inclusion of the left term is the center of
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
. Since the group
\mathcal{G}
embeds in
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
(remark

), so does
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
. Explicitly writing the left action of
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
on the complex domain

, we get

[u,x,t](w,z)=\left(\sigma_{1}(u)w+\sigma_{1}(x),\ \Norm(u)z+\sigma_{2}(x)\sigma_{1}(u)w+\frac{\Norm(x)}{2}+t\right)

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\theta
be an integer,
\theta\geq 3
, take
f_{\theta}
to be the polynomial defined by
6
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(f_{\theta})
and
u_{\theta}\coloneq\hat{X}\in K
. Let
r
be a positive integer and
I={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
, a fractional ideal of the order
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. Let

be an element in

\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}^{(+)}
the subgroup of
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}

g_{0}=[u_{\theta},t];\ \ g_{i}=[1,x_{i},\chi(x_{i},e)],i\in\{1,2\};\ \ g_{3}=\left[1,0,-\frac{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}{r}\right]

\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}^{(+)}

The following lemma gives an equivalent statement to the standard form condition. This formulation, in terms of the parameter

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{2},x_{2},e;t}^{(+)}
and
N=(n_{ij})\coloneq N_{\theta,x_{1},x_{2}}
, the associated matrix (
9
). Then, the standard form condition for
\Gamma
is equivalent to

(11)

\frac{1-u_{\theta}}{u_{\theta}}e+\frac{n_{21}n_{22}}{2}x_{1}-\frac{n_{11}n_{12}}{2}x_{2}\in Ir^{-1}

Proof.

We begin by observing that the center of
\Gamma
is generated by
g_{3}
and
g_{1}^{n_{i1}}g_{2}^{n_{i2}}(g_{0}g_{i}g_{0}^{-1})^{-1}
,

. Hence the standard form condition is equivalent to
g_{1}^{n_{i1}}g_{2}^{n_{i2}}(g_{0}g_{i}g_{0}^{-1})^{-1}\in\langle g_{3}\rangle
,

. In other words,
\Gamma
is in standard form if and only if there exist integers

and

, such that
g_{0}g_{1}g_{0}^{-1}=g_{1}^{n_{11}}g_{2}^{n_{12}}g_{3}^{p}
and
g_{0}g_{2}g_{0}^{-1}=g_{1}^{n_{21}}g_{2}^{n_{22}}g_{3}^{q}
. By direct computation, we see that the unique choice of

for which the above relations are satisfied is

(12)

e=\frac{u_{\theta}}{1-u_{\theta}}\left(\left(\frac{n_{11}n_{12}}{2}+\frac{p}{r}\right)x_{2}-\left(\frac{n_{21}n_{22}}{2}+\frac{q}{r}\right)x_{1}\right)

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group of type
\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}^{(+)}
, as defined by
3.3
. Assume that
\Gamma
is in standard form, then, when seen as a subgroup of
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
,
\Gamma
is a group of Inoue type
G^{(+)}
(
1
).

Proof.

Writing explicitly the action of the generators of
\Gamma
on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
, we get

\displaystyle g_{0}(w,z)=(\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})w,\ z+t)

\displaystyle g_{i}(w,z)=(w+\sigma_{1}(x_{i}),\ z+\sigma_{2}(x_{i})w+\frac{\Norm(x_{i})}{2}+\chi(x_{i},e))

\displaystyle g_{3}(w,z)=(w,z-\frac{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}{r})

Let
N=(n_{ij})
be the associated matrix of
u_{\theta}
(
9
).

N\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}=u_{\theta}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}\implies N\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i}(x_{1})\\
\sigma_{i}(x_{2})\end{pmatrix}=\sigma_{i}(u_{\theta})\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_{i}(x_{1})\\
\sigma_{i}(x_{2})\end{pmatrix}\text{, for }i\in\{1,2\}

In order to match Inoue’s original notation, we set

,

,

and

\chi((u_{\theta}-1)x_{2},e)\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}n_{11}n_{12}\\
n_{21}n_{22}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}{2}=\frac{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}{r}\begin{pmatrix}p\\

so, by lemma

∎

Definition 3.4
.

We will denote by

the Inoue surface obtained as the quotient of

by the left action of

Proposition 3.3
.

Any Inoue surface of type

Proof.

Let

,

. Let

, and let

. Clearly,

3.2.
The connected component of the identity

We are now ready to study the groups of biholomorphisms of Inoue surfaces of type

. In this section we will show that the connected component of the identity of the automorphism group of such surface is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Let
\theta\geq 3
and
r>0
be integers and
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(X^{2}-\theta X+1)
and
u_{\theta}\coloneq\hat{X}\in K
. Let

be a fractional ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
and let

.
Let

and

; we know that
\Gamma\leq\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
and, from the theorem

, we see that

\Aut(X)\simeq\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)/\Gamma

where
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is the normalizer of
\Gamma
in

.

Proposition 3.5
.

The connected component of the identity of the complex Lie group

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let

be the subgroup of translations of

in the

T_{\mathbb{C}}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}w\\

z\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
s\end{pmatrix}|s\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

It is easy to see that
T_{\mathbb{C}}\unlhd\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
, hence we have the following diagram of normal subgroups:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma\cdot T_{\mathbb{C}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T_{\mathbb{C}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Gamma\cap T_{\mathbb{C}}}}

Since

\frac{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}{(\Gamma\cdot T_{\mathbb{C}})}\simeq\frac{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)/\Gamma}{(\Gamma\cdot T_{\mathbb{C}})/\Gamma}

there is a short exact sequence of groups:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\frac{\Gamma\cdot T_{\mathbb{C}}}{\Gamma}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Aut(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\frac{\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)}{\Gamma\cdot T_{\mathbb{C}}}}}

∎

3.3.
The connected components of
\Aut(X)
(the group
Q
)

As before, let
\Gamma=\Gamma^{(+)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}

be the group defined in
3.3
, and let
X=S^{(+)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}
denote the corresponding Inoue surface. In this section, we develop an algorithm for determining the group of connected components of
\Aut(X)
, previously denoted by
Q
. The strategy is as follows: we begin by introducing a finite metabelian group
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
, which is constructed from the number-theoretic parameters defining

. We then prove that
Q
embeds as a subgroup of
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
, thereby showing that
Q
is itself finite and metabelian. Next, we give two easily verifiable conditions that characterize when a given element of
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
belongs to
Q
. Finally, we collect these steps into an algorithm: starting with the parameters
(\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t)
, one constructs
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
, applies the membership conditions, and thus obtains the desired description of
Q
.

Definition 3.5
.

Using the same notations as in definition
3.1
above, we define the following group:

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}\coloneq\frac{{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{\langle u_{\theta}\rangle\ltimes I}\simeq\frac{{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]}{\langle u_{\theta}\rangle}\ltimes\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}

where
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
acts on

by multiplication.

It is easy to see that, up to isomorphism, the group
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
depends only on the class of
I
in the ideal class monoid
\text{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}])
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
Q
denote the group of connected components of the automorphism group of the surface
X
. Then,
Q
embeds as a subgroup of
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
(definition
3.5
). In particular, it follows that

is finite and metabelian.

Proof.

In the previous section we have seen that

where
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
denotes the normalizer of
\Gamma
in
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
and
T_{\mathbb{C}}
is defined by
13
. It is straightforward to verify that

and hence

\Gamma/(\Gamma\cap T_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq\langle u_{\theta}\rangle\ltimes I

In the upcoming lemma (

) we prove that
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is a subgroup of

\left\{[\mu,l,s]\in\mathcal{G}^{(+)}_{\theta}\ |\ \mu\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}],\ l\in I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1},\ s\in\mathbb{C}\right\}

It follows that

\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)/T_{\mathbb{C}}\leq{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}

From (

) and

, we get that

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
be the normalizer of
\Gamma
in
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})

\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is contained in

\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)

There is a surjective morphism from

\frac{\lambda}{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\right]

. Then

is contained in the normalizer of

∎

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ (v-1)e+y\in Ir^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ (\Norm(v)-1)t+\chi((u_{\theta}-1)y,e-\frac{y}{2})\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{r}

Since the case where
r
is odd is more complicated, it is, in practice, easier to compute the automorphism group of the surface
S^{(+)}_{\theta,2r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}
, which is a
[2:1]
quotient of
S^{(+)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t}
, and then decide which automorphisms lift to the covering. We will now describe the algorithm for finding the group
Q
when one is given the parameters
(\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e;t)
(as in definition
3.3
).

Step 1
: Determine the fundamental unit
\eta
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
(such that
\sigma_{1}(\eta)>1
). Let
M_{1}
be the matrix with rational entries that represents the action of
\eta
on
I
with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
(x_{1},x_{2})
. That is:

M_{1}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\eta\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{pmatrix}

Step 2
: Let
j
be the smallest positive integer such that
M_{1}^{j}
is an integer matrix. We know that
j\leq[\langle u_{\theta}\rangle:\langle\eta\rangle]
, so its value can be determined by initially setting
j=1
and incrementing it until the integrality condition is satisfied. Then
u\coloneq\eta^{j}
is the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
.

Step 3
: Find a complete system of representatives
L
for the quotient
\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}
.

Step 4
: Let
n\coloneq[\langle u_{\theta}\rangle:\langle u\rangle]
. Denote by
\widetilde{v}
the class of
v\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
in
\frac{{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]}{\langle u_{\theta}\rangle}
and by
\widehat{y}
the class of
y\in I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
in
\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}
. We now have that

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\left\{\widetilde{u}^{i}|i\in\left\{0\dots n-1\right\}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{y}|y\in L\right\}

and by proposition (
3.7
),

Q=\left\{[\widetilde{v},\widehat{y}]\in\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}\ |\ [v,y]\text{ satisfies conditions 1) and 2)}\right\}

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
is finite, we may check its elements one by one against the two conditions.

3.4.
Computed examples

We will now use the method developed in the previous section to compute the group
Q
in some particular cases. Before we begin, we will make two remarks that will help with the ease of notation.

Remark 3.5
.

Let
K
be a real quadratic number field and
L_{1}={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
and
L_{2}={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle y_{1},y_{2}\rangle
two lattices in
K
. We will denote by
[L_{1}:L_{2}]
the rational number
|\frac{\chi(y_{1},y_{2})}{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}|
. If
L_{2}\leq L_{1}
, this is, of course, the usual group index.

Remark 3.6
.

When writing the elements of groups
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
and
Q
explicitly we will often identify an element of the field
K
with its image through the embedding
\sigma_{1}
.

We begin with an example where the parameter
r
is even and both
e\in K
and
t\in\mathbb{C}
are set to zero. Under these assumptions, conditions 1) and 2) of proposition
3.7
simplify significantly.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\theta=6
,
r=6
,
e=0
and
t=0
. Let
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=\eta
, where
\eta
is the fundamental unit of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\sigma_{1}(\eta)>1
. Thus, the fractional ideal is
I={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle 1,\eta\rangle
and the Inoue surface associated to these parameters is
X=S^{(+)}_{6,6,1,u,0;0}
.

Step 1
:
\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})=3+2\sqrt{2}
;
\sigma_{1}(\eta)=1+\sqrt{2}
. The minimal polynomial of
\eta
is
X^{2}-2X-1
. Thus

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=\eta\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}

and so

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix}

Step 2
: The matrix
M_{1}
is integral, so
j=1
and
u=\eta
is the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
.

Step 3
:
I=\mathcal{O}_{K}\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}]
and
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[2\sqrt{2}]
. Then

I(1-u_{\theta})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{2}](2+2\sqrt{2})={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle 2,2\sqrt{2}\rangle

and thus a complete system of representatives for
\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}
is

L=\left\{0,\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}\right\}

Step 4
:
n=2
and so

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\left\{\widetilde{1},\widetilde{1+\sqrt{2}}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\frac{1}{2}},\widehat{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}},\widehat{\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2}}\right\}

Let
[v,y]\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
representing an element in
Q
. In this example, the two conditions of proposition
3.7
become

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ y\in I6^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \frac{1}{2}\chi(u_{\theta}y,y)\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{6}

Both conditions are independent of
v
, so
v
may take any value in
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
. The second condition is equivalent to
\Norm(y)\in\frac{2}{6}[\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]:I]\mathbb{Z}
. Since
[\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]:I]=\frac{1}{2}
, we get that
\Norm(y)\in\frac{1}{6}\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that
y
is congruent to either
0
or
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
mod
I
and therefore

Q=\left\{\widetilde{1},\widetilde{1+\sqrt{2}}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}\right\}

Since the action of
u=1+\sqrt{2}
on
\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\sqrt{2}/2}\}
is trivial, we conclude that, in this case, the group
Q
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
. In this case the inclusion given by proposition
3.6
is strict.

For the following two examples we will consider situations where
r
is even and
[I]=1
in the ideal class monoid
\text{Cl}(\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}])
. Before we address the examples, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\theta\geq 3
and let
I
be a principal fractional ideal of the order
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. The only case where
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
is strictly larger than
\langle u_{\theta}\rangle
happens when
\theta=3
.

Proof.

The fact that
I
is principal means that, up to ideal class, we may assume that
I=\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. Let
u
denote the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
(with
\sigma_{1}(u)>1
), then
u_{\theta}=u^{n}
, for some integer
n
, so
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]\leq\mathbb{Z}[u]
. But this means that
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
is an integral
\mathbb{Z}[u]
-ideal and, since
1\in\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
, the only possibility is
\mathbb{Z}[u]=\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. We thus have that
\pm u=u_{\theta}+b
for some
b\in\mathbb{Z}
. The norm of
u_{\theta}+b
is
\Norm(u_{\theta}+b)=(u_{\theta}+b)(\varphi(u_{\theta})+b)=1+b^{2}+b\theta
. If
1+b^{2}+b\theta=1
, then
b=0
or
b=-\theta
, which implies that
u\in\{\pm u_{\theta},\pm\varphi(u_{\theta})\}
. We know that
u\in\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}
, so
u
can only be
u_{\theta}
or
\varphi(u_{\theta})=\frac{1}{u_{\theta}}
. So, in this case,
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]=\langle u_{\theta}\rangle
. If
1+b^{2}+b\theta=-1
, then
b(b+\theta)=-2
, so
\theta=3
and
b
is either
-1
or
-2
. We have that
\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})=\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and since
\sigma_{1}(u)>1
, we see that
u
is the exceptional unit
u_{\theta}-1
, so
\sigma_{1}(u)=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
. We have shown that this situation, where
\theta=3
, is the only case where the inclusion
\langle u_{\theta}\rangle\leq{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
is strict. ∎

Example 3.2
.

Let
\theta=4
,
r=6
,
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=u_{\theta}
(so
I=\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
). Let
t=0
and
e=\frac{1}{6(1-u_{\theta})}
.

It is not difficult to check that the group defined by these parameters is in standard form. Applying proposition
3.8
to this example, we expect the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
to be
u_{\theta}
.

Step 1
:
\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})=2+\sqrt{3}
. The fundamental unit
\eta
is equal to
u_{\theta}
and

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&4\end{pmatrix}

Step 2
: The matrix
M_{1}
is integral, so
u=\eta=u_{\theta}
is the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
.

Step 3
:
I=\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{3}]
. Then

I(1-u_{\theta})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{3}](1+\sqrt{3})={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle 1+\sqrt{3},2\rangle

and thus a complete system of representatives for
\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}
is

L=\left\{0,\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}\right\}

Step 4
:
n=1
and so

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\left\{\widetilde{1}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}}\right\}

Let
[v,y]\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
representing an element in
Q
. We have that
v\in\langle u_{\theta}\rangle
, and thus
(v-1)e\in Ir^{-1}
, so the two conditions of proposition
3.7
are

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ y\in I6^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \chi((u_{\theta}-1)y,\frac{1}{6(1-u_{\theta})}-\frac{y}{2})\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{6}

We only have to check if
y=\frac{u_{\theta}-1}{2}
(
\sigma_{1}(\frac{u_{\theta}-1}{2})=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}
) satisfies condition 2), which it does, as one can easily verify. We get that

Q=\left\{\widetilde{1}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}}\right\}

so in this case, the group
Q
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Example 3.3
.

As in the previous example, let
\theta=4
,
r=6
,
x_{1}=1
,
x_{2}=u_{\theta}
and
t=0
, but for this example take
e=0
.

Up until the last step everything is the same as in the previous example
3.2
, so again we have

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\left\{\widetilde{1}\right\}\ltimes\left\{\widehat{0},\widehat{\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}}\right\}

Let
[v,y]\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
representing an element in
Q
. This time, the two conditions of proposition
3.7
are

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ y\in I6^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \frac{1}{2}\chi(u_{\theta}y,y)\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{6}

Condition 2) is equivalent to
\Norm(y)\in\frac{1}{3}[\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]:I]\mathbb{Z}=\frac{1}{3}\mathbb{Z}
. The norm of
y=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{2}
is
-\frac{1}{2}
, so in this case, the group
Q
is trivial.

The last example of the section is one where the group
Q
is noncommutative.

Example 3.4
.

Let
\theta=7
,
r=10
,
e=0
and
t=0
. Let
x_{1}=1
and
x_{2}=\eta
, where
\eta
is the fundamental unit of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
with
\sigma_{1}(\eta)>1
. The fractional ideal is
I=\mathbb{Z}[\eta]
.

Step 1
:
\sigma_{1}(u_{\theta})=\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}
;
\sigma_{1}(\eta)=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
. The minimal polynomial of
\eta
is
X^{2}-X-1
. Thus

\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}=\eta\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\eta\end{pmatrix}

and so

M_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}

Step 2
: The matrix
M_{1}
is integral, so
j=1
and
u=\eta
is the generator of
{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
.

Step 3
:
I=\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[u]
and
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]=\mathbb{Z}[u^{4}]
. Then

I(1-u_{\theta})=\mathbb{Z}[u](3u+1)={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle u+2,5\rangle

and thus a complete system of representatives for
\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}
is

L=\left\{k\frac{1+3u}{5}\ |\ k\in\{0\dots 4\}\right\}

Step 4
:
n=4
and so

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\{\widetilde{u}^{i}\ |\ i\in\{0\dots 3\}\}\ltimes\left\{k\widehat{\frac{1+3u}{5}}\ |\ k\in\{0\dots 4\}\right\}

Let
[v,y]\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]\ltimes I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
representing an element in
Q
. The two conditions of proposition
3.7
are

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ y\in I10^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \frac{1}{2}\chi(u_{\theta}y,y)\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{10}

One then checks that all the elements of
\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}
satisfy the conditions. Therefore

Q=\{\widetilde{u}^{i}\ |\ i\in\{0\dots 3\}\}\ltimes\left\{k\widehat{\frac{1+3u}{5}}\ |\ k\in\{0\dots 4\}\right\}

so

is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})
, where the action of the generator of
(\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z})
on
(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})
is multiplication by
3
.

4.
Surfaces of type
S^{(-)}

In this chapter we will describe the automorphism groups of Inoue surfaces of type
S^{(-)}
. The results are similar to those of the previous chapter, so we will not prove them, instead we will only point the differences. We begin with the analogous construction.

Let
\theta
be an integer,
\theta\geq 1
and
g_{\theta}
the polynomial

g_{\theta}(X)=X^{2}-\theta X-1

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}[X]/(g_{\theta})
and
u_{\theta}:=\hat{X}\in K
. The group
\mathcal{G}^{(-)}_{\theta}
is defined in the exact same way as
\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(+)}
was defined in
3.2
, also its action on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
remains the same.

Definition 4.1
.

Similar to definition
3.3
, we consider the subgroup
\Gamma^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{1},e}\leq\mathcal{G}^{(-)}_{\theta}
generated by

g_{0}=[u_{\theta},0,0];\ \ g_{i}=[1,x_{i},\chi(x_{i},e)],i\in\{1,2\};\ \ g_{3}=\left[1,0,-\frac{\chi(x_{1},x_{2})}{r}\right]

We say that
\Gamma^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{1},e}
is in standard form if
\langle g_{3}\rangle=\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e}^{(-)}\cap T_{\mathbb{C}}
(see
13
).

Proposition 4.1
.

The quotient of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
by the left action of the group
\Gamma^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{1},e}
is an Inoue surface of type
S^{(-)}
. Moreover, any surface of type
S^{(-)}
is biholomorphic to one obtained in this way.

Definition 4.2
.

We will denote by
S^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e}
the Inoue surface obtained as the quotient of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C}
by the left action of
\Gamma_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e}^{(-)}
.

Let
X
be the be the Inoue surface
S^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e}
and
\Gamma=\Gamma^{(-)}_{\theta,r,x_{1},x_{2},e}
. Let
I={}_{\mathbb{Z}}\langle x_{1},x_{2}\rangle
, the fractional ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[u_{\theta}]
. The automorphism group of
X
is again
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)/\Gamma
, where
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is the normailzer of
\Gamma
in
\Aff(\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{C})
. As in the case discussed in the previous chapter, we have that
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{(-)}
and an element
h\in\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is of the form
h=[v,y,s]
with
v\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
,
y\in I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
[v,y,s]\in\mathcal{G}^{(-)}_{\theta}
with
v\in{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]
,
y\in I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}
and
s\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
(1-u_{\theta})y=ax_{1}+bx_{2}
and let the action of
v
on
I
(with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
(x_{1},x_{2})
) be given by the matrix
M=(m_{ij})
. Then, the condition that
[v,y,s]
is contained in the normalizer
\mathcal{N}(\Gamma)
is equivalent to the following two statements:

\displaystyle 1)

\displaystyle\ \ \ (v-1)e+y-\frac{m_{21}m_{22}vx_{1}-m_{12}m_{11}vx_{2}}{2}\in Ir^{-1}

\displaystyle 2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ 2s+\chi((u_{\theta}-1)y,e-\frac{y}{2})+\frac{\chi(ax_{1},bx_{2})}{2}\in\frac{\chi(I,I)}{r}

This implies that the automorphism group of
X
is finite and is an extension of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Aut}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}

where
Q
is again subgroup in

\mathcal{H}_{\theta,[I]}=\frac{{}^{I}[\mathcal{O}_{K}^{\times,>}]}{\langle u_{\theta}\rangle}\ltimes\frac{I(1-u_{\theta})^{-1}}{I}

The computation of the group
Q
for particular choices of initial data is done by similar methods to the
S^{(+)}
case.

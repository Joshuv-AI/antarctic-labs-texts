Abstract
. In this paper, we study Random Dynamical Systems (RDSs) of homeomorphisms on the circle without a finite orbit. We characterize the topological dynamics of the associated semigroup by identifying the existence of invariant sets which are finite unions of intervals. We describe the accumulation points of the average orbit of the transfer operator. For each ergodic stationary measure, we demonstrate interesting properties of its weight function on the circle. Relationships between the minimal sets of an RDS and its inverse RDS are also established.

2.
Basic notations

Let us introduce some preliminary notations, which we use throughout the text.

A
circular order
of a finite enumerated set of distinct points
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
n>2
, is a permutation
\phi:\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\to\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
such that: For any
i,j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
with
\phi(i)<\phi(j)<\phi(k)
, the triplet
\langle x_{\phi(i)},x_{\phi(j)},x_{\phi(k)}\rangle
appears in the order
x_{\phi(i)}\to x_{\phi(j)}\to x_{\phi(k)}
when traversing the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
counterclockwise.
If
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
are two circular orders of the same finite enumerated set
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
, then
\phi
is a translation of
\phi^{\prime}
, that is, for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
\phi=\phi^{\prime}+i\,(\text{mod }n)
.

We say that a
n
-tuple
\langle x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\rangle
,
n>2
, is
circularly ordered
when the identity map over
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
is a circular order of
\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\}
. Let
CO
denote the set of all
n
-tuples that are circularly ordered for all
n\geq 3
. In particular, a triplet
\langle a,b,c\rangle
is in
CO
if and only if it appears in the order
a\to b\to c
when traversing the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
counterclockwise.
Let us extend the concept of order to finite collections of sets. For
n\geq 3
A
n
-tuple
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\rangle
of pairwise disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is said
circularly ordered
if, any set
\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle\in\mbox{CO}
whenever
x_{i}\in A_{i}
.

Given
a,b\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
a\neq b
, the
open interval

(a,b)
is defined by

(a,b)=\{x\in\mathbb{S}^{1}\colon\langle a,x,b\rangle\in\mbox{CO}\}.

The
closed interval

[a,b]
is the union of the open interval
(a,b)
with the pair
\{a,b\}
. Here, we write
[a,a]=\{a\}
. These definitions follow the general notion of an interval (or, an arc) in the circle, identifying the circle as a counterclockwise real interval. For
a\neq b
and
I\in\{[a,b],(a,b)\}
, we write
x<y
for
x,y\in I
,
x\neq y
, if
\langle a,x,y,b\rangle\in\mbox{CO}
. And, we write
x\leq y
when
x=y
or
x<y
.

Now, we introduce the concept of monotonicity for functions defined on intervals of the circle. Note that the image of an interval under a homeomorphism is another interval.
Consider
a,b\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
a\neq b
, and
I\in\{[a,b],(a,b)\}
. For
f\in\mbox{Hom}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, we say that
f
is
monotone increasing
on
I
if for any
x,y\in I
,
x\neq y
, such that
x<y
then
f(x)\leq f(y)
. We call
f

monotone decreasing
on an interval
I\in\{[a,b],(a,b)\}
if for any
x,y\in I
,
x\neq y
, such that
x<y
then
f(y)\leq f(x)
. And, we say that
f
is
monotone
if it is increasing or decreasing.

3.
Invariant minimal sets

The results in this section are strictly topological and are established using the structure of the circle. Let
\mathcal{F}\subset\mbox{Hom}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}
be the collection of the

If

. For

as the family of the closed intervals such that

(b)

.

When

\mathcal{E}_{K}=\{\mathbb{S}^{1}\}
.
We have

(2)

K\subset\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}I.

In particular, note that
\mathcal{E}_{K}
is the

collection of closed intervals with the following two properties:

(a’)

If

, and

(b’)

\left(\cup_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}I\right)\cap K^{\prime}=\emptyset

That is, if

\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{E}}I\subset\bigcup_{I\in\mathcal{E}_{K}}I

Proposition 1

Assume that

and

Lemma 1

Assume that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mbox{Hom}(\mathbb{S}^{1})

Assume that

, there exists a sequence of closed intervals

\text{diam}(I_{n})\to 0

. We can assume (possibly passing to a subsequence) that if

then the sequences
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and

are convergent to a point

. By the definition of the collection

, there is

and

with
\langle a_{n},b_{n},a_{n+1}\rangle\in\mbox{CO}
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a finite set, we can find a subsequence
(n_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and

such that

for all

. Then,

is a sequence in

in
K
. Which contradicts the compactness of

. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}_{K}
is a finite set.

Assume that
\mathcal{F}\subset\mbox{Hom}(\mathbb{S}^{1})

6.
Proximal action

An important concept in the study of RDSs is
proximality
. Introduced by Furstenberg in his seminal paper
[
Fur67
]
, it has since been employed by many researchers in the field. Proximality measures the degree of similarity between two orbits in a dynamical system. Specifically, two points in a dynamical system are considered proximal if their distance remains small over time. See item (e) of Theorem
5
for further details.

Let us state the main result of this section, which further illustrates the usefulness of the collections of intervals defined in Theorem
2
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\nu)

or
\#\mathcal{B}_{i}=2\#\mathcal{A}_{i}

each interval in
\mathcal{A}_{i}

Abstract




Abstract.

It’s well-known that maximum distance separable codes (in short, MDS) and linear complementary dual (in short, LCD) codes are very important in coding theory and practice. In 2023, Yue et al.
[
25
]
constructed three classes of LCD MDS codes via
(*)
-TGRS codes. Recently, Wu et al.
[
27
]
generalized the results given by Yue et al. and constructed several classes of LCD MDS codes. In this paper, we unify their constructions by defining the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
twisted generalized Reed-Solomon (in short,
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-TGRS) code, give the parity-check matrix of
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
codes, and then construct four classes of LCD codes. Finally, some corresponding examples are given.

Keywords.

parity-check matrix; LCD code; twisted generalized Reed-Solomon code.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the
q
elements finite field and
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}=\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
, where
q
is a prime power. A
k
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with minimum (Hamming) distance
d
is called an
[n,k,d]
linear code over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The dual code of an
[n,k]_{q}
linear code
\mathcal{C}
is given by

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}=\left\{\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\right)=\bm{x}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\mid\langle\bm{x},\bm{y}\rangle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}=0,\forall\bm{y}=\left(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}\right)\in\mathcal{C}\right\}.

For an
[n,k]_{q}
linear code
\mathcal{C}
, if
n
and
k
are given, then the maximum value of the minimum (Hamming) distance
d
is determined, i.e,
d\leq n-k+1
. Especially, if
d=n-k+1
, then
\mathcal{C}
is maximum distance separable (in short, MDS); if
d=n-k
, then
\mathcal{C}
is almost MDS (in short, AMDS); if
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is also AMDS, then
\mathcal{C}
is near MDS (in short, NMDS).

The generalized Reed-Solomon (in short, GRS) code, as a well-known class of MDS codes, is defined as

\mathrm{GRS}_{k}(\bm{\alpha})\triangleq\left\{\left(v_{1}f\left(\alpha_{1}\right),\ldots,v_{n}f\left(\alpha_{n}\right)\right)|f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}[x]\right\},

where
\bm{v}=\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
,
\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}(i\neq j)
, and

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}[x]=\left\{f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f_{i}x^{i}\mid f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q},0\leq i\leq k-1\right\}.

If a linear code
\mathcal{C}
is not equivalent to any GRS code, then
\mathcal{C}
is the non-generalized Reed-
Solomon (in short, non-GRS) type and
\mathcal{C}
is called a non-GRS code.

Since MDS codes based on
\mathrm{GRS}
codes are equivalent to
\mathrm{GRS}
codes, it’s interesting to construct non-GRS MDS codes. Recently, several families of non-GRS MDS codes have been constructed
[
1

. In addition, in 2022, Beelen et al.
[

constructed some non-GRS MDS codes basing on the linear code

\mathrm{TGRS}_{k}(\bm{\alpha},\bm{t},\bm{h},\bm{\eta})\triangleq\left\{\left(v_{1}f\left(\alpha_{1}\right),\ldots,v_{n}f\left(\alpha_{n}\right)\right)|f(x)\in\mathcal{P}_{\bm{t},\bm{h},\bm{\eta}}^{n,k}\right\},

where
\bm{t}=\left(t_{1},\ldots,t_{\ell}\right)\in\left\{1,\ldots,n-k\right\}^{\ell},\bm{h}=\left(h_{1},\ldots,h_{\ell}\right)\in\left\{1,\ldots,k-1\right\}^{\ell},\bm{\eta}=\left(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{\ell}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell},\bm{v}=\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
,
\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}(i\neq j)
, and

\mathcal{P}_{\bm{t},\bm{h},\bm{\eta}}^{n,k}=\left\{f(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}f_{i}x^{i}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}\eta_{j}f_{h_{j}}x^{k-1+t_{j}}\mid f_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

(1.1)

Lemma 2.4

Theorem 3.1

Let
\bm{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{j}(i\neq j)
,
\bm{v}=\left(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right)\in\left(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\right)^{n}
and
\bm{\eta}=

\left(\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{\ell}\right)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\ell+1}\backslash\left\{\bm{0}\right\}
. Let
P=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{i}P_{i}
,
u_{i}=\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}\left(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j}\right)^{-1}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Let
2\leq k\leq n-(\ell+1),0\leq\ell\leq n-k-1
. Then the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
code has the parity-check matrix

\bm{H}_{n-k,*}=\begin{pmatrix}\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{n-k-(\ell+2)}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\left(\alpha_{j}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}-(-1)^{n-1}P_{j}\varOmega_{\ell+1}\right)&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\left(\alpha_{j}^{n-k-1}-(-1)^{n-1}P_{j}\varOmega_{1}\right)&\cdots\end{pmatrix}_{(n-k)\times n},

(3.1)

where

\varOmega_{r}=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}S_{t+1-r}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right),1\leq r\leq\ell+1.

Proof
. Note that
\bm{G}_{k,*}
is the generator matrix of the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
code. To prove that
\bm{H}_{n-k,*}
is the parity-check matrix of the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
code, we only need to check that
\mathrm{rank}(\bm{H}_{n-k,*})=n-k
and
\bm{G}_{k,*}\bm{H}_{n-k,*}^{T}=\bm{0}
.

Firstly, we prove
\mathrm{rank}(\bm{H}_{n-k,*})=n-k
. In fact, since the vectors

(\frac{u_{1}}{v_{1}},\cdots,\frac{u_{n}}{v_{n}}),(\frac{u_{1}}{v_{1}}\alpha_{1},\cdots,\frac{u_{n}}{v_{n}}\alpha_{n}),\cdots,(\frac{u_{1}}{v_{1}}\alpha_{1}^{n-1},\cdots,\frac{u_{n}}{v_{n}}\alpha_{n}^{n-1})

are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent, we know that the row vectors of the matrix

\bm{H}=\bm{H}_{n-k,*}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}&&&&\\
&\alpha_{2}&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&\alpha_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{2}&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\alpha_{j}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\cdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\left(\alpha_{j}^{n-k-\ell}-(-1)^{n-1}P\varOmega_{\ell+1}\right)&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\frac{u_{j}}{v_{j}}\left(\alpha_{j}^{n-k}-(-1)^{n-1}P\varOmega_{1}\right)&\cdots\end{pmatrix}_{(n-k)\times n}.

are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly independent, it means that
{\mathrm{rank}}(\bm{H})=n-k
, and so

{\mathrm{rank}}(\bm{H}_{n-k,*})\geq{\mathrm{rank}}(\bm{H})=n-k.

Note that
{\mathrm{rank}}(\bm{H}_{n-k,*})\leq n-k
, and then

{\mathrm{rank}}(\bm{H}_{n-k,*})=n-k.

Next, we prove
\bm{G}_{k,*}\bm{H}_{n-k,*}^{T}=\bm{0}
.
For convenience, we denote
\bm{g}_{i}
and
\bm{h}_{j}
be the
(i+1)
-th
(0\leq i\leq k-1)
row and the
(j+1)
-th
(0\leq j\leq n-k-1)
row of
\bm{G}_{n-k,*}
and
\bm{H}_{n-k,*}
, respectively. Then we have the following three cases.

Case 1.
For
0\leq i\leq k-1
and
0\leq j\leq n-k-(\ell+2)
, we have

\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\left(\alpha_{s}^{j}+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{k+t+j}\right),&\text{if}\ i=0,0\leq j\leq n-k-(\ell+2);\\
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{i+j},&\text{if}\ 1\leq i\leq k-1,0\leq j\leq n-k-(\ell+2).\\
\end{cases}

Note that

j\leq n-k-(\ell+2)\leq n-4<n-2,

k+t+j\leq k+\ell+n-k-(\ell+2)=n-2

and

i+j\leq(k-1)+n-k-(\ell+2)=n-\ell-3\leq n-3<n-2,

thus, by Lemma
2.2
, for
0\leq i\leq k-1
and
0\leq j\leq n-k-(\ell+2)
, we have
\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}=0.

Case 2.
For
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
n-k-(\ell+1)\leq j\leq n-k-1
, we have

\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{i+j}-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-1}u_{s}P_{s}\alpha_{s}^{i}\varOmega_{n-k-j}=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{i+j}-(-1)^{n-1}P\varOmega_{n-k-j}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{i-1}.

Note that

i+j\leq(k-1)+n-k-1=n-2

and

i-1\leq k-2\leq n-\ell-3\leq n-3<n-2,

thus, by Lemma
2.2
, for
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
n-k-(\ell+1)\leq j\leq n-k-1
, we have
\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}=0.

Case 3.
For
i=0
and
n-k-(\ell+1)\leq j\leq n-k-1
, for the convenience, we denote
j=n-k-r(1\leq r\leq\ell+1),
and then we have

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{k+t}\right)\left(\alpha_{s}^{n-k-r}-(-1)^{n-1}P_{s}\varOmega_{r}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\left(\alpha_{s}^{n-k-r}+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{n+t-r}\right)-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{k+t}\right)(-1)^{n-1}P_{s}\varOmega_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{n-k-r}+\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{n+t-r}-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}(-1)^{n-1}P_{s}\varOmega_{r}-\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{s}^{k+t}(-1)^{n-1}P_{s}\varOmega_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{n-k-r}+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{n+t-r}-\varOmega_{r}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-1}u_{s}P_{s}-(-1)^{n-1}\varOmega_{r}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{k+t}P_{s}.

Note that
n-k-r\leq n-3<n-2,
thus by Lemma
2.2
,
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{n-k-r}=0
. And by Lemma
2.3
, we have
\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-1}u_{s}P_{s}=1.
Furthermore, combined
P=\alpha_{s}P_{s}
and

k+t-1\leq n-k-(\ell+1)+\ell-1=n-k\leq n-2,

then by Lemma
2.2
, for
i=0
and
n-k-(\ell+1)\leq j\leq n-k-1
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{g}_{i}\bm{h}_{j}^{T}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{n+t-r}-\varOmega_{r}-(-1)^{n-1}P\varOmega_{r}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\sum\limits_{s=1}^{n}u_{s}\alpha_{s}^{k+t-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}S_{t+1-r}\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\right)-\varOmega_{r}=0.

Now by the above three cases, we can get
\bm{G}_{k,*}\bm{H}_{n-k,*}^{T}=\bm{0}
.

From the above discussions, we complete the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

In this subsection, we give the proofs for our main results.

4.2.1
The proof of Theorem 4.1

Note that
\bm{G}_{k,*}
and
\bm{H}_{n-k,*}
given by
(\ref{*LPTGRSgeneratormatrix})
and
(\ref{*LPTGRSparity-checkmatrix})
are the generator matrix and the parity-check matrix of
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
codes, respectively. Now by Lemma
2.1
, to prove that the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
code is LCD, it’s enough to prove that
{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{k,*}\\
\bm{H}_{n-k,*}\end{pmatrix}=n
. Note that
v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}(1\leq i\leq n)
, then the diagonal matrix
\mathrm{diag}\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}
is nonsingular over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and so it’s sufficient to prove that

{\mathrm{rank}}\left(\bm{D}\right)={\mathrm{rank}}\left(\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}&&&\\
&v_{2}&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&v_{n}\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{k,*}^{T}&\bm{H}_{n-k,*}^{T}\end{pmatrix}\right)=n.

For convenience, we denote
\bm{D}\triangleq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}&\bm{B}\end{pmatrix}
, where

\bm{A}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}^{2}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{1}^{k+t}\right)&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}^{k-1}\\
v_{2}^{2}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{2}^{k+t}\right)&v_{2}^{2}\alpha_{2}&v_{2}^{2}\alpha_{2}^{2}&\cdots&v_{2}^{2}\alpha_{2}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v_{n}^{2}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{n}^{k+t}\right)&v_{n}^{2}\alpha_{n}&v_{n}^{2}\alpha_{n}^{2}&\cdots&v_{n}^{2}\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}

(4.1)

and

\small\bm{B}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{0}&\cdots&u_{1}\alpha_{1}^{n-k-(\ell+2)}&u_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)1}\right)&\cdots&u_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}^{n-k-1}-\varDelta_{11}\right)\\
u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{0}&\cdots&u_{2}\alpha_{2}^{n-k-(\ell+2)}&u_{2}\left(\alpha_{2}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)2}\right)&\cdots&u_{2}\left(\alpha_{2}^{n-k-1}-\varDelta_{12}\right)\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{0}&\cdots&u_{n}\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+2)}&u_{n}\left(\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)n}\right)&\cdots&u_{n}\left(\alpha_{n}^{n-k-1}-\varDelta_{1n}\right)\\
\end{pmatrix}

(4.2)

with

\varDelta_{ij}=(-1)^{n-1}P_{j}\varOmega_{i}=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{P}{\alpha_{j}}\varOmega_{i}.

Note that
m(x)=x^{n}-\lambda=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x-\alpha_{i})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, thus we have

\alpha_{i}^{n}=\lambda

and

m^{\prime}(x)=nx^{n-1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\prod\limits_{j=1,j\neq i}^{n}(x-\alpha_{j}),

then

u_{i}=\frac{1}{m^{\prime}(\alpha_{i})}=\frac{1}{n\alpha_{i}^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{n\lambda}\alpha_{i}.

(4.3)

Thus by
v_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\left\{-1,0,1\right\}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
and
v_{i}\in\left\{-1,1\right\}
for
k\leq i\leq n
, then it’s enough to prove that

{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}=n,

where

\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}v_{1}^{2}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{1}^{k+t}\right)&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&v_{1}^{2}\alpha_{1}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
v_{k-1}^{2}\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{k-1}^{k+t}\right)&v_{k-1}^{2}\alpha_{k-1}&v_{k-1}^{2}\alpha_{k-1}^{2}&\cdots&v_{k-1}^{2}\alpha_{k-1}^{k-1}\\
1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{k}^{k+t}&\alpha_{k}&\alpha_{k}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{n}^{k+t}&\alpha_{n}&\alpha_{n}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{k-1}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4.4)

and

\bm{B}_{1}^{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\alpha_{1}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{1}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{1}^{n-k-\ell}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)1}\alpha_{1}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{1}^{n-k}-\varDelta_{11}\alpha_{1}}{n\lambda}\\
\frac{\alpha_{2}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{2}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{2}^{n-k-\ell}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)2}\alpha_{2}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{2}^{n-k}-\varDelta_{12}\alpha_{2}}{n\lambda}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{\alpha_{n}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{n}^{2}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}}{n\lambda}&\frac{\alpha_{n}^{n-k-\ell}-\varDelta_{(\ell+1)n}\alpha_{n}}{n\lambda}&\cdots&\frac{\alpha_{n}^{n-k}-\varDelta_{1n}\alpha_{n}}{n\lambda}\\
\end{pmatrix}

(4.5)

with
\varDelta_{ij}=(-1)^{n-1}P_{j}\varOmega_{i}=(-1)^{n-1}\frac{P}{\alpha_{j}}\varOmega_{i}.

Now, for the matrix
\bm{B}_{1}^{(1)}
, by multiplying
n\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
to the each row, we can obtain the matrix

\bm{B}_{1}^{(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}&\alpha_{1}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{1}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}&\alpha_{2}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{2}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{n}&\alpha_{n}^{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4.6)

where
\varPhi_{i}=(-1)^{n-1}P\varOmega_{i}.
And

{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(1)}\end{pmatrix}={\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
2\leq k\leq\frac{n-2\ell-1}{2}
, it means that

n-k-(\ell+1)\geq k+\ell\geq k>k-1.

Therefore, for the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(2)}\end{pmatrix}
, we can carry out the following elementary transformations. For convenience, we denote the vector
\bm{a}_{i,1}^{(1)}
and
\bm{b}_{j,1}^{(2)}
be the
(i+1)
-th
(0\leq i\leq k-1)
column and the
j
-th
(1\leq j\leq n-k)
column of
\bm{A}_{1}^{(1)}
and
\bm{B}_{1}^{(2)}
, respectively.

Step 1.
Replace
\bm{a}_{i+1,1}^{(1)}
with
\bm{a}_{i+1,1}^{(1)}-\bm{b}_{i,1}^{(2)}
for each
i=1,\ldots,k-1
, we can obtain the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(2)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(3)}\end{pmatrix}
and denote the vector
\bm{a}_{i,1}^{(2)}
and
\bm{b}_{j,1}^{(3)}
be the
(i+1)
-th
(0\leq i\leq k-1)
column and the
j
-th
(1\leq j\leq n-k)
column of
\bm{A}_{1}^{(2)}
and
\bm{B}_{1}^{(3)}
, respectively;

Step 2.
Replace
\bm{a}_{1,1}^{(2)}
with
\bm{a}_{1,1}^{(2)}-\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\bm{b}_{k+t,1}^{(3)}
, we can obtain the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(3)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(4)}\end{pmatrix}
;

Step 3.
put the first column of the matrix
\bm{A}_{1}^{(3)}
to the back of the last column of the matrix
\bm{A}_{1}^{(3)}
, we can obtain the matrix
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}
,
where

\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(v_{1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\left(v_{1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{1}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\left(v_{k-1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{k-1}&\cdots&\left(v_{k-1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{k-1}^{k-1}\\
0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(4.7)

and

\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}=\begin{pmatrix}\varTheta_{1}&\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{1}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\varTheta_{k-1}&\alpha_{k-1}&\cdots&\alpha_{k-1}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{k-1}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{k-1}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
1&\alpha_{k}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

with
\varTheta_{i}=\left(v_{i}^{2}-1\right)\left(1+\sum\limits_{t=0}^{\ell}\eta_{t}\alpha_{i}^{k+t}\right)+1.

Note that for any matrix
\bm{M}
, the rank of
\bm{M}
cannot be changed under the elementary transformations , and so it’s enough to prove that

{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}=n.

i.e.,
\det\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}\neq 0
. For convenience, we denote
\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}\triangleq\begin{pmatrix}\bm{K}_{1}&*\\
\bm{0}&\bm{L}_{1}\end{pmatrix}
, where

\bm{K}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(v_{1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{1}&\cdots&\left(v_{1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{1}^{k-1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\left(v_{k-1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{k-1}&\cdots&\left(v_{k-1}^{2}-1\right)\alpha_{k-1}^{k-1}\end{pmatrix}_{(k-1)\times(k-1)}

(4.8)

and

\bm{L}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_{k}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-\ell}-\varPhi_{\ell+1}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k}-\varPhi_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}_{(n-k+1)\times(n-k+1)}.

Note that

\det(\bm{K}_{1})=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\left(v_{i}^{2}-1\right)\cdot\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k-1}\alpha_{i}\cdot\prod\limits_{1\leq a<b\leq k-1}(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{a})\neq 0

(4.9)

and

\det(\bm{L}_{1})=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha_{k}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{k}^{n-k-\ell}&\cdots&\alpha_{k}^{n-k}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&\alpha_{n}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-(\ell+1)}&\alpha_{n}^{n-k-\ell}&\cdots&\alpha_{n}^{n-k}\\
\end{pmatrix}=\prod\limits_{k\leq a<b\leq n}(\alpha_{b}-\alpha_{a})\neq 0.

Hence

\det\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}=\det(\bm{K}_{1})\det(\bm{L}_{1})\neq 0,

it means
{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}=n
, thus

{\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{k,*}^{T}&\bm{H}_{n-k,*}^{T}\end{pmatrix}={\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{G}_{k,*}\\
\bm{H}_{n-k,*}\end{pmatrix}={\mathrm{rank}}\left(\bm{D}\right)={\mathrm{rank}}\begin{pmatrix}\bm{A}_{1}^{(4)}&\bm{B}_{1}^{(5)}\end{pmatrix}=n,

which means that
the
(*)
-
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P})
-
\mathrm{TGRS}
code is LCD.

From the above, we complete the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

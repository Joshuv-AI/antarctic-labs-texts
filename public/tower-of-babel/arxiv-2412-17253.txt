Deformations and homotopy theory of Nijenhuis associative algebras
Chao Song, Kai Wang, Yuanyuan Zhang and Guodong Zhou

Abstract
. This paper is the first in a series of works devoted to an operadic study of Nijenhuis structures, focusing on Nijenhuis associative algebras. We introduce the concept of homotopy Nijenhuis associative algebras and demonstrate that the differential graded (=dg) operad 𝔑 ​ 𝔧 ​ 𝔄 ∞ \mathfrak{NjA}_{\infty} governing these structures serves as the minimal model of the operad 𝔑 ​ 𝔧 ​ 𝔄 \mathfrak{NjA} for Nijenhuis associative algebras. Additionally, we determine the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of 𝔑 ​ 𝔧 ​ 𝔄 \mathfrak{NjA} . We construct an L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra that controls the simultaneous deformations of associative products and Nijenhuis operators. The Maurer-Cartan elements of this L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebra correspond bijectively to Nijenhuis associative algebra structures. From this, we derive a cochain complex (deformation complex) and an associated cohomology theory of Nijenhuis associative algebras. Finally, we explore the connection between homotopy relative Rota-Baxter associative algebras of weight 0 0 and homotopy Nijenhuis associative algebras. A sequel to this work will extend the study to Nijenhuis Lie algebras, with applications to Nijenhuis geometry.

Introduction

A Nijenhuis operator on a Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},[-,-])
is a linear endomorphism
P
satisfying the following Nijenhuis relation:

(1)

\displaystyle[P(a),P(b)]=P([a,P(b)]+[P(a),b]-P[a,b]),\text{ for }a,b\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

The concept of a Nijenhuis operator on a Lie algebra originated from the foundational notion of a Nijenhuis tensor, introduced by Nijenhuis
[
35
]
in the study of pseudo-complex manifolds. Nijenhuis operators on Lie algebras were later explored in
[
28
,
22
]
within the broader context of Poisson-Nijenhuis manifolds. On manifolds, Nijenhuis operators act as unary operations on the Lie algebra of vector fields and satisfy the corresponding Nijenhuis relation (
1
), which is the simplest geometric condition for
(1,1)
-tensors, as outlined in the philosophy of
[
1
]
. In 1957, Newlander and Nirenberg
[
34
]
proved that an almost complex manifold
(M,J)
is a complex manifold if and only if
J
is a Nijenhuis operator, as also noted in
[
18
, Theorem 1.2, Page356]
. This provides a significant example of a Nijenhuis operator.

The concept of Nijenhuis operators on associative algebras, introduced by Carinena et al.
[
4
]
to study quantum bi-Hamiltonian systems, is an analogue of the Nijenhuis operators on Lie algebras and manifolds (see Definition
1.1
).
In
[
11
]
, Ebrahimi-Fard interpreted the associative analogue of the Nijenhuis relation as the homogeneous version of the Rota-Baxter relation. Building on this perspective, he used the augmented modified quasi-shuffle product to construct free Nijenhuis associative algebras. Guo and Lei
[
25
]
provided an explicit construction of free Nijenhuis associative algebras using bracketed words, and applied this construction to derive the universal enveloping Nijenhuis associative algebra of an NS algebra. An important example of the Nijenhuis operator on associative algebras is given by Das
[
6
, 2.5 Proposition]
, which establishes a connection between relative Rota-Baxter operators of weight 0 and Nijenhuis operators. And this connection will be generalized to homotopy level in this paper.

We focus on the deformation and homotopy theory of Nijenhuis structures. Specifically, in this paper, we will study the deformation and homotopy theory of Nijenhuis associative structures. In subsequent work, we will consider these problems for Nijenhuis structures on Lie algebras and manifolds, as well as their applications in geometry.

A central philosophy in deformation theory, inspired by the foundational work of Gerstenhaber, Nijenhuis, Richardson, Deligne, Schlessinger, Stasheff, Goldman, Millson, and others, is that the deformations of a given mathematical structure can be described by a differential graded (dg) Lie algebra or, more generally, an
L_{\infty}
-algebra. The underlying complex of this algebra is referred to as the deformation complex. Lurie
[
27
]
and Pridham
[
36
]
formalized this idea rigorously, establishing it as an equivalence of infinity categories in characteristic zero. Consequently, an essential problem in deformation theory is the explicit construction of the dg Lie algebra or
L_{\infty}
-algebra that governs the deformation theory of the structure under study.

Another significant challenge in the study of algebraic structures is understanding their homotopy analogs, such as
A_{\infty}
-algebras for associative algebras and
L_{\infty}
-algebras for Lie algebras. Operad theory provides a systematic framework for this task, requiring the formulation of a cofibrant resolution of the operad describing the algebraic structure. Ideally, this resolution takes the form of a minimal model of the operad. If the operad is Koszul, Koszul duality theory
[
15
,
16
]
offers a powerful method to define the homotopy version of the structure through the cobar construction of the Koszul dual cooperad, which in this case is itself a minimal model.
However, for operads that are not Koszul, significant challenges emerge, and examples of minimal models in this setting remain scarce. This paper contributes to addressing these difficulties in the context of Nijenhuis structures.

These two problems, say, describing controlling
L_{\infty}
-algebras and constructing homotopy versions of algebraic structures, are deeply interconnected. Specifically, given a cofibrant resolution, particularly a minimal model of the operad in question, one can derive the deformation complex of the algebraic structure and endow it with an
L_{\infty}
-structure, as demonstrated by Kontsevich and Soibelman
[
21
]
and van der Laan
[
41
,
42
]
.
In practice, however, finding a minimal model or a small cofibrant resolution is often a nontrivial challenge. Recently, Wang and Zhou
[
43
]
successfully addressed both of these problems for Rota-Baxter associative algebras of arbitrary weight, while Chen, Guo, Wang, and Zhou
[
5
]
extended these results to differential algebras with nonzero weight.
In this paper and its sequel, we extend these methods, with significant modifications, to study the operad governing Nijenhuis structures.

In this paper, we exhibit the deformation complex of a Nijenhuis associative algebra as well as the controlling
L_{\infty}
-structure in the sense of deformation theory. We succeed in finding the minimal model and the Koszul dual homotopy cooperad of the operad for Nijenhuis associative algebras, providing justification for the deformation complex as well as the controlling
L_{\infty}
-structure.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section

We then combine this cochain complex with the usual Hochschild cochain complex of associative algebras to define a cochain complex for Nijenhuis associative algebras, whose homology controls the deformations of Nijenhuis associative algebras. Section

to homotopy level.

1.
Nijenhuis associative algebras and Nijenhuis bimodules

In this section, we recall some basic concepts and facts about Nijenhuis associative algebras and their Nijenhuis bimodules.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(A,m=\cdot)
be an associative algebra. A linear operator
P:A\rightarrow A
is a
Nijenhuis operator
if it satisfies

\displaystyle P(a)\cdot P(b)=P\big(P(a)\cdot b+a\cdot P(b)-P(a\cdot b)\big)

for any
a,b\in A
, or in terms of maps

(2)

\displaystyle m\circ(P\otimes P)=P\circ\big(m\circ(P\otimes{\rm Id})+m\circ({\rm Id}\otimes P)-P\circ m\big).

In this case,
A=(A,m,P)
is called a
Nijenhuis associative algebra
.

Let
B=(B,m_{B},P_{B})
and
A=(A,m_{A},P_{A})
be two Nijenhuis associative algebras. A morphism of associative algebras
f:B\rightarrow A
is called a
morphism of Nijenhuis associative algebras
, if
P_{A}\circ f=f\circ P_{B}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra and
M
be a bimodule over the associative algebra
(A,m)
.
Then
M
is said to be a
Nijenhuis bimodule
over Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
, if
M
is endowed with a linear operator
P_{M}:M\rightarrow M
such that the following equations

(3)

\displaystyle P(a)P_{M}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{M}\big(P(a)x+aP_{M}(x)-P_{M}(ax)\big),

(4)

\displaystyle P_{M}(x)P(a)

\displaystyle=P_{M}\big(P_{M}(x)a+xP(a)-P_{M}(xa)\big),

hold for any
a\in A
and
x\in M
.

Obviously, a Nijenhuis associative algebra
A=(A,m,P)
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over itself, called the
regular Nijenhuis bimodule
.

Let
(A,m)
be an associative algebra and
M
be a bimodule over it. It is well known that
A\oplus M
becomes an associative algebra whose product is given by

\displaystyle(a,x)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,y):=(a\cdot b,ay+xb),

for any
a,b\in A
and
x,y\in M
. This is called the
semi-direct product (or trivial extension)
of associative algebra
(A,m)
by bimodule
M
, denoted by
A\ltimes M
.
Write
\iota:A\rightarrow A\oplus M,a\mapsto(a,0)
and
\pi:A\oplus M\rightarrow A,(a,x)\mapsto a
, which are obviously associative algebra morphisms.

The following results are direct consequences of the definitions, whose proofs are left to the reader.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra and
M
be a bimodule over associative algebra
(A,m)
. Then the semi-direct product
A\ltimes M
of
A
by
M
is a Nijenhuis associative algebra such that
\iota
and
\pi
are both morphisms of Nijenhuis associative algebras if and only if
M
is a Nijenhuis bimodule over
A
.

This new Nijenhuis associative algebra will still be denoted by
A\ltimes M
, called the
(Nijenhuis) semi-direct product (or trivial extension)
of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
by Nijenhuis bimodule
M
.

Nijenhuis associative algebras and Nijenhuis bimodules have some descendent properties.

Proposition 1.4
.

[
4
]

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra. Define a new binary operation
m_{P}=\cdot_{P}
over
A
as:

\displaystyle a\cdot_{P}b:=P(a)\cdot b+a\cdot P(b)-P(a\cdot b),

for any
a,b\in A
. Then

(i)

the operation
m_{P}
is associative and
(A,m_{P})
is a new associative algebra;

(ii)

the triple
(A,m_{P},P)
also forms a Nijenhuis associative algebra and denote it by
A_{P}
;

(iii)

the map
P:A_{P}=(A,m_{P},P)\rightarrow A=(A,m,P)
is a morphism of Nijenhuis associative algebras.

One can also obtain new Nijenhuis bimodules from old ones.

Proposition 1.5

(Compare with
[
7
, 2.8 Example]
)
.

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra and
M=(M,P_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over it. We define a left action
``\rhd"
and a right action
``\lhd"
of
A
on
M
as follows:

\displaystyle a\rhd x:=P(a)x\quad\mathrm{and}\quad x\lhd a:=xP(a)

for any
a\in A
and
x\in M
.
Then these actions make
M
into a Nijenhuis bimodule over
A_{P}
, and we denote this new bimodule by
M_{P}
.

Let’s describe the operad for Nijenhuis associative algebras.

Definition 1.6
.

The
(nonsymmetric) operad for Nijenhuis associative algebras
, denoted by
\mathfrak{NjA}
, is generated by a unary operator
P
and a binary operator
m
with the operadic relations:

(5)

\displaystyle m\circ_{1}m-m\circ_{2}m

and

(6)

\displaystyle(m\circ_{1}P)\circ_{2}P-(P\circ_{1}m)\circ_{1}P-(P\circ_{1}m)\circ_{2}P+P\circ_{1}(P\circ_{1}m).

In other words,
\mathfrak{NjA}=\mathcal{F}({\bf k}m\oplus{\bf k}P)/I,
where for a collection
M
,
\mathcal{F}(M)
is the free operad generated by
M
, and
I
is the operadic ideal generated by (
5
) and (
6
).

Here, Relation (
5
) is the associativity axiom for the product
m
and Relation (
6
) corresponds to the defining relation (
2
) of the Nijenhuis operator
P
.

Remark 1.7
.

Since the defining relation (
6
) of the Nijenhuis operator is cubic, it is obvious that the operad
\mathfrak{NjA}
is not Koszul.
So the classical Koszul duality theory of operads
[
15
,
16
]
could not be applied directly to develop the operadic cohomology theory, minimal model, controlling
L_{\infty}
-structures, etc., for Nijenhuis associative algebras.

2.
Cohomology theory of Nijenhuis associative algebras

In this section, we will introduce a cohomology theory of Nijenhuis associative algebras. We will see later that this cohomology theory controls the deformations of Nijenhuis associative algebras.

2.1.
Hochschild cohomology of associative algebras

Let
M
be a bimodule over an associative algebra
(A,m)
. Recall that the
Hochschild cochain complex of associative algebra
(A,m)
with coefficients in bimodule
M
is the cochain complex

{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M),

where, for
n\geqslant 0
,
{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)={\rm Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
and its differential

\delta^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg},M}:{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\rightarrow{\rm C}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)

is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{Alg},M}^{n}(f)(a_{1,n+1})=a_{1}f(a_{2,n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}\cdot a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1,n})a_{n+1}

for any
f\in{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}\in A
, where for
1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant n
,
a_{i,j}=a_{i}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{j}
but for
i>j
,
a_{i,j}
is by convention empty.

The cohomology of the cochain complex, denoted by
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
, is called the
Hochschild cohomology of associative algebra
(A,m)
with coefficients in bimodule
M
.
When
M
is the regular bimodule
A
itself, we simply write the differential
\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg},A}
as
\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}
, and also simply denote
{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,A)
by
{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A)
and
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,A)
by
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A)
, called the
Hochschild cochain complex of associative algebra
(A,m)
and the
Hochschild cohomology of associative algebra
(A,m)
, respectively.

2.2.
Cohomology of Nijenhuis operators

Next, let’s introduce the cohomology theory of Nijenhuis operators.

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra and
M=(M,P_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over it. According to Propositions
1.4
and
1.5
, one can define a new Nijenhuis associative algebra
A_{P}=(A,m_{P},P)
and a new Nijenhuis bimodule
M_{P}=(M,\rhd,\lhd,P_{M})
over
A_{P}
.
Consider the Hochschild cochain complex of the associative algebra
A_{P}
with coefficients in bimodule
M_{P}

{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},{M_{P}})=\bigoplus\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},{M_{P}}),

where, for
n\geqslant 0
,
{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},{M_{P}})={\rm Hom}(A^{\otimes n},M)
and its differential

\partial^{n}:{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},{M_{P}})\rightarrow{\rm C}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},{M_{P}})

is given by

\displaystyle\partial^{n}(f)(a_{1,n+1})=

\displaystyle\ a_{1}\rhd f(a_{2,n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}\cdot_{P}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1,n})\lhd a_{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ P(a_{1})f(a_{2,n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\big(f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes a_{i}P(a_{i+1})\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})+f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes P(a_{i})a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-f(a_{1,i-1}\otimes P(a_{i}a_{i+1})\otimes a_{i+2,n+1})\big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1,n})P(a_{n+1})

for any
f\in{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A_{P},M_{P})
and
a_{1},\dots,a_{n+1}\in A

A=(A,m,P)

Definition 2.5
.

Let
M=(M,P_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over a Nijenhuis associative algebra
A=(A,m,P)
. We define the
cochain complex of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
with coefficients in Nijenhuis bimodule
M
, denoted by
({\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M),\delta_{\mathrm{NjA},M}^{\bullet})
, to be the mapping cone of
\Phi_{M}^{\bullet}
shifted by
-1
, that is,

{\rm C}^{0}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)={\rm C}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)={\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\oplus{\rm C}^{n-1}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A,M),\text{ for }n\geqslant 1,

and its differential
\delta_{\mathrm{NjA},M}^{n}:{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\rightarrow{\rm C}^{n+1}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{NjA},M}^{n}(f,g)=

\displaystyle\ (\delta_{\mathrm{Alg},M}^{n}(f),-\Phi_{M}^{n}(f)-\delta_{\mathrm{NjO},M}^{n-1}(g))

for any
f\in{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)
and
g\in{\rm C}^{n-1}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A,M)
.

The cohomology of
{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)
, denoted by
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{NjA}}^{\bullet}(A,M)
, is called the
cohomology of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
with coefficients in Nijenhuis bimodule
M
.
When
M
is the regular Nijenhuis bimodule
A
itself,
we simply denote the differential
\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA},A}
by
\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}
. We also simply denote
{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,A)
by
{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
and
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,A)
by
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
, called the
cochain complex of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
and the
cohomology of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
, respectively.

By properties of the mapping cone, there is a short exact sequence of cochain complexes:

\displaystyle 0\to s^{-1}{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A,M)\to{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\to{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A,M)\to 0,

which induces a long exact sequence of cohomology groups

0\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{0}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{1}(A,M)\to\cdots

\cdots\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{p}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}^{p}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}^{p+1}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\to\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{p+1}(A,M)\to\cdots

Here the symbol
s^{-1}
represents the desuspension of a cohomologically graded vector space. For a homologically graded space
V
, the suspension of
V
is the graded space
sV
with
(sV)_{n}=V_{n-1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Write
sv\in(sV)_{n}
for
v\in V_{n-1}
. The map
s:V\rightarrow sV
,
v\mapsto sv
is a graded map of degree
1
.
Similarly, the desuspension of
V
, denoted
s^{-1}V
, is the graded space with
(s^{-1}V)_{n}=V_{n+1}
. Write
s^{-1}v\in(s^{-1}V)_{n}
for
v\in V_{n+1}
and the map
s^{-1}:V\rightarrow s^{-1}V
,
v\mapsto s^{-1}v
is a graded map of degree
-1
. In the context of cohomologically graded spaces, as used above, the suspension
s
and desuspension
s^{-1}
have degree
-1
and
1
respectively.

2.4.
Coefficients from regular Nijenhuis bimodules to general Nijenhuis bimodules

Let
M=(M,P_{M})
be a Nijenhuis bimodule over a Nijenhuis associative algebra
A=(A,m,P)
.
According to Proposition
1.3
, the semi-direct product
A\ltimes M
of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
by its Nijenhuis bimodule
M
is also a Nijenhuis associative algebra. Hence, we get the cochain complex
({\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A\ltimes M),\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}})
of the Nijenhuis associative algebra

, where

\displaystyle{\rm C}^{0}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A\ltimes M)

\displaystyle={\rm C}^{0}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A\ltimes M)=A\oplus M,

\displaystyle{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A\ltimes M)

\displaystyle={\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A\ltimes M)\oplus{\rm C}^{n-1}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A\ltimes M)

\displaystyle={\rm Hom}((A\oplus M)^{n},A\oplus M)\oplus{\rm Hom}((A\oplus M)^{n-1},A\oplus M),\text{ for }n\geqslant 1.

In order to obtain the cochain complex of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
with coefficients in Nijenhuis bimodules
M
, one also needs to consider the obvious embedding

\iota:{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)\hookrightarrow{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A\ltimes M),\ f\mapsto\overline{f}.

Proposition 2.6
.

With above notations, the image
\mathrm{Im}(\iota)
is a subcomplex of
({\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A\ltimes M),\delta^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}})
.

Proof.

Let
(f,g)\in{\rm C}^{n}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A,M)
for
n\geqslant 0
. Direct calculation can obtain:

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{NjA}}^{n}(\iota(f,g))=

\displaystyle\ \delta_{\mathrm{NjA}}^{n}(\overline{f},\overline{g})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (\delta_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{n}(\overline{f}),-\Phi^{n}(\overline{f})-\delta_{\mathrm{NjO}}^{n-1}(\overline{g}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \iota(\delta_{\mathrm{Alg},M}^{n}(f),-\Phi_{M}^{n}(f)-\delta_{\mathrm{NjO},M}^{n-1}(g))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ \iota(\delta_{\mathrm{NjA},M}^{n}(f,g)).

∎

Let

, be the obvious projection. Using the fact

, we find that

is indeed a differential on the space

, which justify the Definition
2.5
. Moreover, Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
2.4
are direct corollaries as well.

4.2.
The minimal model

Let’s recall the notion of minimal models of operads.
For a graded collection

Definition 4.2
.

[

A

is a quasi-free dg operad

together with a surjective quasi-isomorphism of dg operads

\partial

(ii)

.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
10
]

When a dg operad

admits a minimal model, it is unique up to isomorphisms.

The following result is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.4

The dg operad

\mathfrak{NjA}

\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(V)\cong\mathbf{Hom}\big({\mathscr{S}({\mathfrak{NjA}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}})},{\rm End}_{sV}\big)^{\prod}.

{\mathscr{S}({\mathfrak{NjA}^{\mathrm{\textup{!`}}}})}(n)={\bf k}u_{n}\oplus{\bf k}v_{n}

5.2.
Realising Nijenhuis associative algebra structures as Maurer-Cartan elements

In this subsection, we will see that the cohomology of Nijenhuis associative algebras introduced in Subsection
2.3
can be obtained using the twisting procedure in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra introduced in previous subsection.

The following result follows from
[
5
, Proposition 3.9]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
V
be an ungraded space considered as a graded space concentrated in degree 0. Then a Nijenhuis associative algebra structure on
V
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
{\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}}(V)
.

Let us justify the cohomology theory of Nijenhuis associative algebras introduced in Section
2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
A=(A,m,P)
be a Nijenhuis associative algebra. Twist the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
by the Maurer-Cartan element corresponding to the Nijenhuis associative algebra structure, then its underlying complex is isomorphic to
s{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
, the shift of the cochain complex of Nijenhuis associative algebra
A
defined in Subsection
2.3
.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.4
, the Nijenhuis associative algebra structure on
A
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha=(\nu,\tau)
in the
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
with

\nu=-s\circ m\circ(s^{-1})^{\otimes 2}:(sA)^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow sA\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\tau=P\circ s^{-1}:sA\rightarrow A.

Via twisting procedure (see
[
5
, Proposition 2.4]
), this Maurer-Cartan element induces a new
L_{\infty}
-algebra structure
\{l_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
on the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
.
More precisely, for any
(sf,g)\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},sA)\oplus{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n-1},A)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)

, we have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(sf)

\displaystyle=l_{1}(sf)-l_{2}(\alpha\otimes sf)+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}\frac{1}{i!}l_{i+1}(\alpha^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)

\displaystyle=-l_{2}(\nu\otimes sf)-l_{2}(\tau\otimes sf)+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(-1)^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}\frac{1}{i!}l_{i+1}(\tau^{\otimes i}\otimes sf)

\displaystyle=-[\nu,sf]_{\mathrm{G}}+(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{1}{n!}l_{n+1}(\tau^{\otimes n}\otimes sf).

Note that,
-[\nu,sf]_{\mathrm{G}}
corresponds to
-(-1)^{n+1}\delta_{\mathrm{Alg}}^{n}(\widetilde{sf})
in
s{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Alg}}(A)

under the fixed isomorphism (
19
).
And

\displaystyle(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\frac{1}{n!}l_{n+1}(\tau^{\otimes n}\otimes sf)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}(-1)^{n(|f|+1)+n}\frac{1}{n!}l_{n+1}(sf\otimes\tau^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}s^{-1}\underbrace{(s\tau)\circ\cdots\circ(s\tau)}_{k}\circ sf\{\underbrace{s\tau,\dots,s\tau}_{n-k}\},

which corresponds to
\Phi^{n}(\widetilde{sf})
under the fixed isomorphism (
20
).
We also have

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(g)=

\displaystyle\ l_{1}(g)-l_{2}(\alpha\otimes g)-\frac{1}{2!}l_{3}(\alpha\otimes\alpha\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ -l_{3}(\nu\otimes\tau\otimes g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ s^{-1}\nu\{s\tau,sg\}-\tau\{\nu\{sg\}\}+(-1)^{n}\tau\{sg\{\nu\}\}

\displaystyle+s^{-1}\nu\{sg,s\tau\}-(-1)^{n}g\{\nu\{s\tau\}\}+(-1)^{n}g\{s\tau\{\nu\}\},

which corresponds to
(-1)^{n}\delta_{\mathrm{NjO}}^{n-1}(\widehat{g})
under the fixed isomorphism (
20
).

In conclusion, we have that the underlying complex of the twisted
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
by Maurer-Cartan element
\alpha
is isomorphic to the cochain complex
s{\rm C}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
.
∎

Although
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
is an
L_{\infty}
-algebra, the next proposition shows that once the associative algebra structure

on
A
is fixed, the graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
, which, after twisting procedure, controls deformations of Nijenhuis operators, is a genuine graded Lie algebra.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(A,m)
be an associative algebra. Then we have the following statements:

(i)

The graded space
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
can be endowed with a graded Lie algebra structure, and a Nijenhuis operator on
(A,m)
is equivalent to a Maurer-Cartan element in this graded Lie algebra.

(ii)

Given a Nijenhuis operator
P
on associative algebra
(A,m)
, the underlying complex of the twisted dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
by the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element is isomorphic to the cochain complex
{\rm C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}^{\bullet}(A)
of Nijenhuis operator
P
defined in Subsection
2.2
.

Proof.

(i) Consider
A
as a graded space concentrated in degree 0. Define

\nu=-s\circ m\circ(s^{-1})^{\otimes 2}:(sA)^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow sA.

Note that
\alpha=(\nu,0)
is naturally a Maurer-Cartan element in
L_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
.
By the construction of
\{l_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A)
, the graded subspace
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
is closed under the action of
\{l_{n}^{\alpha}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
. Note that
l_{1}^{\alpha}\equiv 0
on
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
, and the restriction of
l_{n}^{\alpha}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
is
0
for
n\geqslant 3
because the arity of
\nu
is 2. Thus
(\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A),\{l_{2}^{\alpha}\})
forms a graded Lie algebra.
More explicitly, for any
f\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},A)
,
g\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes k},A)
, we have

\displaystyle l_{2}^{\alpha}(f\otimes g)=

\displaystyle\ l_{2}(f,g)+l_{3}(\alpha,f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ l_{3}(\nu,f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (-1)^{|f|}\big(s^{-1}\nu\{sf,sg\}-(-1)^{|f|+1}f\{\nu\{sg\}\}+(-1)^{|f|+1+|g|+1}f\{sg\{\nu\}\}\big)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{|f||g|+1+|g|}\big(s^{-1}\nu\{sg,sf\}-(-1)^{|g|+1}g\{\nu\{sf\}\}+(-1)^{|g|+1+|f|+1}g\{sf\{\nu\}\}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ (-1)^{n}s^{-1}\nu\{sf,sg\}+f\{\nu\{sg\}\}+(-1)^{k}f\{sg\{\nu\}\}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{nk+k+1}s^{-1}\nu\{sg,sf\}-(-1)^{nk}g\{\nu\{sf\}\}+(-1)^{nk+n+1}g\{sf\{\nu\}\}.

Since
A
is concentrated in degree 0, we have
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)_{-1}={\rm Hom}(sA,A)
. Take an element
\tau\in{\rm Hom}(sA,A)
, then
\tau
satisfies the Maurer-Cartan equation

if and only if

s^{-1}\nu\{s\tau,s\tau\}-\tau\{\nu\{s\tau\}\}+\tau\{s\tau\{\nu\}\}=0.

Define
P=\tau\circ s:A\rightarrow A
. The above equation can be rewritten as

m\circ(P\otimes P)=P\circ m\circ(P\otimes{\rm Id})+P\circ m\circ({\rm Id}\otimes P)-P\circ P\circ m,

which means that
P
is a Nijenhuis operator on associative algebra
(A,m)
.

(ii) Now let
P
be a Nijenhuis operator on associative algebra
(A,m)
. By the first statement,
P
corresponds to a Maurer-Cartan element
\beta=P\circ s^{-1}
in the graded Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjA}}(A),l_{2}^{\alpha})

For
f\in{\rm Hom}((sA)^{\otimes n},A)\subset\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
, we compute
(l_{1}^{\alpha})^{\beta}(f)
as follows:

\displaystyle(l_{1}^{\alpha})^{\beta}(f)=

\displaystyle l_{1}^{\alpha}(f)-l_{2}^{\alpha}(\beta\otimes f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-l_{2}^{\alpha}(\beta\otimes f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{-1}\nu\{s\beta,sf\}-\beta\{\nu\{sf\}\}-(-1)^{n}\beta\{sf\{\nu\}\}

\displaystyle+s^{-1}\nu\{sf,s\beta\}+(-1)^{n}f\{\nu\{s\beta\}\}-(-1)^{n}f\{s\beta\{\nu\}\},

which corresponds to
(-1)^{n+1}\delta_{\mathrm{NjO}}^{n}(\widehat{f})
under the fixed isomorphism (
20
). So the underlying complex of the twisted dg Lie algebra
\mathfrak{C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}(A)
by the corresponding Maurer-Cartan element

is isomorphic to the cochain complex
{\rm C}_{\mathrm{NjO}}^{\bullet}(A)
of Nijenhuis operator
P
. ∎

6.
Homotopy relative Rota-Baxter associative algebras and homotopy Nijenhuis associative algebras

In this section, we will establish a connection between homotopy relative Rota-Baxter associative algebras of weight
0
and homotopy Nijenhuis associative algebras. This generalizes the following result of Das
[
6
]
, which relates Rota-Baxter associative algebras of weight
0
and Nijenhuis associative algebras, to the homotopy level:

Proposition 6.1
.

[
6
, 2.5 Proposition]

Let
(A,m)
be an associative algebra and
(M,l,r)
be a bimodule over it, where

(resp.

is a

on

is a Nijenhuis operator on the semi-direct product associative algebra

.

For a graded space

, it is well known that

being an

-algebra is equivalent to its suspension

being an
A_{\infty}[1]
-algebra, where the correspondence is given by

as described in Equation (

The concepts of left modules, right modules and bimodules over an

[

, Keller
[

[

[

Using the notion of brace operation, the concepts of

-algebras and

Let

be a graded space. It is called an

-algebra
if

is endowed with a family of graded linear operators
{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}:{\mathfrak{A}}^{\otimes k}\rightarrow{\mathfrak{A}},k\geqslant 1

, subjecting to the following identities: for

,

(21)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n+1}{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}\{{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}\}=0,

Let
({\mathfrak{A}},\{{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1})

be a dg space with differential

of degree

-bimodule structure

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i+j=n+1}\varrho_{j}\{{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}\}+\varrho_{j}\{\varrho_{i}\}=0.

Inspired by the construction of semi-direct product of an associative algebra by a bimodule over it, we have the following general construction:

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
({\mathfrak{A}},\{{\mathfrak{m}}_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1})
be an

-algebra and
({\mathfrak{M}},\{\varrho_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1})

Note that, for any

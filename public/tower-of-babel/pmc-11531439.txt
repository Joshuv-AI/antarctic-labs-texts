Intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics for preoperative prediction of neoadjuvant chemotherapy effect in breast cancer based on 18F-FDG PET/CT
Authors: Xuefeng Hou, Kun Chen, Xing Wan, Huiwen Luo, Xiaofeng Li, Wengui Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo investigate the value of 18F-FDG PET/CT-based intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics in predicting the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC) for breast cancer.Methods190 patients who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria from 2017 to 2022 were studied. Features were extracted from the PET/CT intratumoral and peritumoral regions, feature selection was performed through the correlation analysis, t-tests, and least absolute shrinkage and selection operator regression (LASSO). Four classifiers, support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbor (KNN), logistic regression (LR), and naive bayes (NB) were used to build the prediction models. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were plotted to measure the predictive performance of the models. Concurrent stratified analysis was conducted to establish subtype-specific features for each molecular subtype.ResultsCompared to intratumoral features alone, intratumoral + peritumoral features achieved higher AUC values in each classifier. The SVM model constructed with intratumoral + peritumoral features achieved the highest AUC values in both the train and test set (train set: 0.95 and test set: 0.83). Subtype-specific features improve performance in predicting the efficacy of NAC (luminal group: 0.90; HER2 + group: 0.86; triple negative group: 0.92).ConclusionIntratumoral and peritumoral radiomics models based on 18F-FDG PET/CT can reliably forecast the efficacy of NAC, thereby assisting clinical decision-making.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-024-05987-w.

Introduction

Breast cancer is the most common cancer type, with high heterogeneity. Breast cancer accounts for 30% and 15% of female cancer incidence and mortality respectively (Bray et al. 2018; Siegel et al. 2023). Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NAC), can facilitate breast-conserving surgery and improve patients’ quality of life (Connolly and Stearns 2013; Harbeck and Gnant 2017). Most breast cancer patients with pathologic complete response (pCR) can benefit from NAC (Spring et al. 2017). pCR patients’ overall survival and event-free survival were noticeably higher than non-pCR patients. Their five-year overall survival rate reached 98.1% (Asaoka et al. 2019; Chen et al. 2023a; Cortazar et al. 2014). However, some patients are not sensitive to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and even experience disease progression during neoadjuvant chemotherapy (Mao et al. 2022). For breast cancer patients who do not achieve a pCR after NAC, not only do they suffer unnecessary NAC toxicity, but also they may miss the timing of the optimal treatment regimen. However, the gold standard for assessing pCR depends on the pathologic findings of surgical specimens, which is an invasive procedure. For individualized treatment, a reliable and noninvasive approach to preoperatively predict the efficacy of NAC is urgently needed. In this study, we aimed to preoperatively and non-invasively predict whether patients would achieve pCR after NAC by PET/CT radiomics models.

Using molecular imaging, PET/CT is a crucial tool for the recurrence monitoring and treatment evaluation of breast cancer (Groheux 2022; Ming et al. 2020). PET/CT is increasingly indispensable for predicting the efficacy of NAC and has broad application prospects (Paydary et al. 2019). Compared to other imaging methods, PET/CT can detect disease earlier and has strong specificity in predicting the efficacy of NAC (Li et al. 2018).

2012 radiomics was initially proposed (Lambin et al. 2012). Radiomics can extract and screen valuable information from medical images for differential diagnosis and therapeutic evaluation of diseases. It can be used with genomes or pathomics data to address clinical problems and achieve precision medicine (Mayerhoefer et al. 2020). Several recently published studies have shown that PET/CT radiomics have the potential to predict the efficacy of NAC (Antunovic et al. 2019; Li et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2022). However, most of their studies have focused on the intratumoral region. Relevant studies on tumor biology have demonstrated that the peritumoral region is important for tumor growth (Hwang et al. 2019; Zhao et al. 2019). Therefore, quantifying both intratumoral and peritumoral areas enables better analysis characteristics of the tumor.

To the best of what we know, few studies have utilized PET/CT intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics to predict the efficacy of NAC in breast cancer. This study will further evaluate the value of PET/ CT-based intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics in predicting the efficacy of NAC in breast cancer.

Materials and methods

Patients

We retrospectively analyzed patients who had undergone PET/CT examinations at our hospital from 2017 to 2022. The following were the inclusion criteria: (1) Patients with pathologically confirmed breast cancer; (2)PET/CT was administered before NAC; (3) Preoperative neoadjuvant chemotherapy was not interrupted; (4) complete clinical and pathological data were available. Based on the above inclusion criteria, a total of 260 breast cancer patients were originally included. The following were the exclusion criteria: (1) multifocal breast cancer or combined with other malignant tumors; (2) neoadjuvant chemotherapy was interrupted; (3) no surgical and postoperative pathology data recorded; (4) negative FDG uptake of the primary tumor. Based on the above inclusion and exclusion criteria, a total of 190 breast cancer patients were finally included in this study and divided into a training set and a test set in the ratio of 7:3. The flow chart of patient enrollment is shown in Fig. 1. Patient informed consent was not required for this retrospective investigation, which was permitted by the institutional Ethics Committee.

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimen and the definition of pCR

All patients in this study preoperatively received 6 or 8 cycles of NAC according to NCCN guidelines. Neoadjuvant chemotherapy regimens included alkylator, anthracycline, and taxane. Patients with HER2 positivity were also treated with trastuzumab. pCR was defined as ypT0/TisN0, that is, no residual invasive cancer in the breast and axillary lymph nodes after NAC.

Image acquisition

Every patient was scanned using a PET/CT (GE Discovery Elite) scanner. Before scanning, patients fasted for at least 6 h and their blood glucose was controlled to < 11.1 mmol/L. Sixty minutes after the injection of 18 F-FDG (4 mBq/kg) into the cubital vein, scans were performed from the skull base to the distal femur. CT was utilized for attenuation correction, the tube current was 50–80 mAs, the thickness of the slice was 5 mm, and the tube voltage was 120 kV. The PET was scanned in eight-bed positions, each bed received a two-minute scan, and the increment was 16.2 cm (3D mode). After reconstructing the PET images using the ordered-subset expectation maximization (OSEM) iterative algorithm, the PET voxel’s final sizes were 5. 3 mm×5. 3 mm×2. 5 mm.

Image segmentation and feature extraction

CT lesions were manually segmented using 3D Slicer software (version: 4.11.20200930) to obtain regions of interest (ROI). To get more accurate results, PET and CT images were resampled to a 1.0 × 1.0 × 1.0mm3 isotropic voxel size before segmentation. PET images were registered with CT images to obtain the same intratumoral ROI. To quantify information about the peritumoral region, the previously drawn tumor boundary (intratumoral ROI) was automatically extended 2 mm outside to form the peritumoral ROI. We manually eliminated the chest wall included in the peritumoral ROI. Intratumoral and peritumoral ROIs of representative PET/CT images are shown in Fig. 2. Two experienced nuclear medicine doctors (Readers 1 and 2 with 6 and 11 years of experience in PET/CT image diagnosis) segmented the lesions on the CT images, and one of the doctors (Reader 2) performed two lesion segmentations on the CT images to assess the reliability of the extracted features. Two nuclear medicine doctors (Readers 1 and 2) were blinded to the clinical and pathological data when they segmented the lesions. Reliability was determined using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). To calculate the reliability of the extracted features, a 2-way random-effects model was applied for the inter-observer (Readers 1 vs. 2) ICCs and a 2-way mixed-effects model for the intra-observer (Readers 2 vs. 2) ICCs. Both inter- and intra-observer ICCs > 0.75 were defined as reliability features.

PET and CT images were pre-processed with wavelet filters and the Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG). After pre-processing the PET and CT images, features were extracted from intratumoral and peritumoral ROIs within PET and CT images (intratumoral region of CT, 2 mm peritumoral region of CT, intratumoral region of PET, 2 mm peritumoral region of PET) by using Pyradiomics, an open-source Python package compliant with the image biomarker standardization initiative (IBSI) standard. A total of 1932 intratumoral features and 1932 peritumoral features were extracted from the PET/CT images. The extracted intratumoral and peritumoral features were early fused to form 3864 intratumoral + peritumoral features. The types of radiomics features extracted are shown in Supplementary Material 1.

Feature selection and model construction

We performed the following screening steps to select the most relevant radiomics features. First, feature correlations were calculated by Pearson’s correlation coefficient (normally distributed data) or Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient (non-normally distributed data), and features with correlation coefficients > 0. 9 were removed. Secondly, features with significant differences between the groups were retained using t-tests. Ultimately, the features associated with the target variable were chosen using the least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) approach. To select the optimal prediction model, we built the following four classifiers: support vector machine (SVM), k-nearest neighbor (KNN), logistic regression (LR), and naive bayes (NB). Area under the curve (AUC) was computed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, which were used to assess the model’s performance. The procedures of image segmentation, feature extraction, feature screening, and modeling analysis are illustrated in Fig. 3.

Subtype stratified analysis

Patients were divided into three groups based on molecular subtypes. (1) Luminal group: luminal A (ER/PR+ and HER2− with low Ki-67) and luminal B (ER/PR+ and HER2− with high Ki-67; ER/PR+ and HER2+); (2) HER2+ group: ER/PR−, and HER2+; (3) Triple-negative group: ER/PR−, and HER2−. The same feature screening steps described above are then performed to screen the subtype-specific features of different molecular subtypes. ROC curves were drawn to assess the predictive power of subtype-specific features.

Statistical analysis

There is an imbalance between the pCR and non-pCR groups in breast cancer patients (56:134), so we used the synthetic minority oversampling technique (SMOTE) to address this category imbalance. Here was the specific process in detail: In SMOTE, the k-nearest neighbor algorithm was used to oversample the pCR group, until the numbers of the pCR and non-pCR groups were equal. This process was performed a thousand times to remove the impact of sampling randomness on the results (Chen et al. 2022). The average of the 1000 results was used as the mean AUC. The ROC curve of the best-performing model was drawn. The Delong test was used to compare the AUCs of different models. Using the t-test and chi-square test, clinical characteristics within the pCR and non-pCR groups were analyzed. R software (version: 4.3.1) and Python (version: 3.7.9) were used for all statistical analysis.

Radiomics features selected by feature screening

We built four classifiers to evaluate the predictive power of intratumoral features, peritumoral features, and intratumoral + peritumoral features. For each model in the test set, the mean AUC was computed. As seen in Fig. 4; Table 3, intratumoral features combined with peritumoral features yielded the highest AUC values in each classifier. When constructing models with intratumoral features, SVM had the highest AUC value (test set: 0.79, 95% CI: 0.697–0.874). The DeLong tests revealed statistically significant differences between SVM and NB (p < 0.001), KNN (p < 0.01), and LR (p < 0.01). When constructing models with peritumoral features, SVM had the highest AUC value (test set: 0.81, 95% CI: 0.718–0.893). The DeLong tests revealed statistically significant differences between SVM and NB (p < 0.001), KNN (p < 0.05), and LR (p < 0.001). When constructing models with intratumoral and peritumoral features, SVM had the highest AUC value (test set: 0.83, 95% CI: 0.746–0.906). The DeLong tests revealed statistically significant differences between SVM and NB (p < 0.001), KNN (p < 0.05), and LR (p < 0.01). Figure 5 displays the ROC curve for the best model (SVM).

The luminal group included 108 patients, the HER2 + group had 46 patients, and the triple-negative group included 36 patients. Discussion

NAC is becoming increasingly important because it benefits breast cancer patients. Compared with postoperative chemotherapy, the rate of mastectomy is decreased and the rate of Breast-conserving surgery is increased after NAC (“Long-term outcomes for neoadjuvant versus adjuvant chemotherapy in early breast cancer: meta-analysis of individual patient data from ten randomised trials,” 2018; Montemurro et al. 2020). After NAC, patients with pCR have a much better prognosis than those without pCR (Rastogi et al. 2008). However, approximately 30% of breast cancer patients don’t respond to neoadjuvant chemotherapy (Taleghamar et al. 2022). Preoperative predicting breast cancer patients who may receive NAC is important for implementing individualized treatment. In other words, a reliable and noninvasive method for predicting pCR is needed to prevent effective treatment delay in NAC-insensitive patients. Surgery or other optimal therapeutic strategy should be recommended for patients with predicted NAC insensitivity to minimize the toxicity of NAC (Mao et al. 2022). In the study, the baseline PET/CT images were retrospectively analyzed to construct a radiomic model to preoperatively predict the efficacy of NAC. In clinical work, PET/CT-based radiomics models can be used as a non-invasive method to assist clinical decision-making. The model not only helps to develop a personalized treatment plan but also avoids unnecessary surgery or suffering from unnecessary NAC toxicity.

Many studies have indicated that radiomics can forecast the effectiveness of NAC (Antunovic et al. 2019; Fan et al. 2017; Li et al. 2020). By analyzing PET images of 79 breast cancer patients, it was ultimately found that all radiomics models can predict pCR to NAC (Antunovic et al. 2019). Li et al. established supervised and unsupervised machine learning models after extracting radiomics features, and the AUC in the validation set reached 0.722 (Li et al. 2020). Analyzing the radiomics features of pretreatment breast DCE-MRI is valuable for the efficacy of NAC (Fan et al. 2017). However, previous studies have focused on the intratumoral region, ignoring the influence of the peritumoral region on tumor progression.

Tumor-associated fibroblasts, endothelial cells, immune cells of different kinds, and other cells constitute the peri-tumor microenvironment (TME). These cells were previously thought to be bystanders in the tumor development process, but research has now confirmed that they are integral to tumor development (de Visser and Joyce 2023; Ochi et al. 2019; Uematsu 2015; Yu et al. 2019). The tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte (TIL) levels within TME were significantly correlated with pCR. Consequently, TIL can serve as a biomarker for predicting the efficacy of NAC. Meanwhile, the decrease in TIL may also be related to tumor recurrence (Asano et al. 2018; Denkert et al. 2018). The peritumoral regions of breast cancer, lung cancer, and liver cancer have gradually been studied (Braman et al. 2017; Wang et al. 2019; Xia et al. 2023; Xu et al. 2022). After adding the peritumoral region, the AUC value of predicting lymphovascular invasion (LVI) of non-small cell lung cancer was significantly improved (Chen et al. 2023a, b). Huang et al. achieved better results in predicting the efficacy of NAC by combining intratumoral and peritumoral regions (Huang et al. 2021). Analysis within the recommended negative margins is considered a suitable alternative to the true peritumoral area (Braman et al. 2017). 2 mm is an appropriate margin width in ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) treated with whole-breast irradiation (WBRT), which is associated with a lower incidence of ipsilateral breast tumor recurrence (IBTR)(Morrow et al. 2016). Patients had a better prognosis when the margins of breast-conserving therapy (BCT) were ≤ 2 mm after NAC (Choi et al. 2018). Therefore, we finally included the 2 mm peritumoral region and the final results showed that the peritumoral region could improve the prediction ability.

Breast cancer heterogeneity is reflected in intratumoral heterogeneity and peritumoral microenvironment heterogeneity. Tumor invasion and tumor metastasis can cause changes in the peritumor area (Jia et al. 2010). These changes are difficult to identify with the naked eye while radiomics features can quantify the peritumor region to reflect changes in the peri-tumor microenvironment. In this study, by comprehensively analyzing both the intratumoral region and the 2 mm peritumoral region, it was shown that the intratumoral features combined with the peritumoral features had higher AUC values, both in the training set and the test set. Moreover, combined intratumoral features with peritumoral features achieved higher AUC values in each classifier compared to using intratumoral features alone. As listed in Table 3, in the test set, the AUC of 0.83 in the SVM classifier, 0.77 in the NB classifier, 0.79 in the LR classifier, and 0.79 in the KNN classifier, suggesting that including peritumoral features does improve the efficacy of predicting pCR after NAC.

In the subtype analysis, feature screening was re-conducted to select subtype-specific features. The luminal group achieved the maximum AUC value using the SVM classifier (0.90). The HER2 + group achieved the maximum AUC value using the NB classifier (0.86). The triple negative group achieved the maximum AUC value using the LR classifier (0.92). Subtype analysis further confirmed that subtype-specific radiomics features can improve AUC values (Huang et al. 2021).

In Table 1, we attempted to analyze the clinical and pathological features associated with achieving pCR. In PET/CT image analysis, SUVmax is a widely used quantitative parameter. The study revealed that SUVmax was not statistically different between the two groups, which is consistent with another study (Akimoto et al. 2018). We found that ER status, PR status, and HER2 + status were statistically different between the two groups, which was confirmed by another study (Omair et al. 2023).

This study has some limitations. CT image segmentation by hand is still a tedious and subjective process. In future studies, breast cancer lesion segmentation algorithms should be applied to build objective and universal models. Secondly, considering that this study was a single-center study, a multicenter study should be conducted in the future to validate the general applicability of the model.

Conclusions

From what we know, this is the first study to use PET/CT intratumoral and peritumoral radiomics to predict the efficacy of NAC in breast cancer and to show its strong predictive potential. PET/CT radiomics model can be used as a noninvasive method to help clinical decision-making.

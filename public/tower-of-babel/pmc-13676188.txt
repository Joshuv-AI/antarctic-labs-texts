Evaluating the introduction of a practical thyroid ultrasound teaching session for medical students
Authors: Isobel Austin, Catherine Denver, Twisha Girisankar, Titus Cvasciuc, Peter Ellis, Philip C Johnston
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and AimsThe use of thyroid ultrasound is gaining in popularity at postgraduate level. However, it has not been broadly incorporated into the UK undergraduate medical curriculum. Thyroid ultrasound can help augment students understanding of both anatomy and endocrinology. We aimed to embed a hands-on thyroid ultrasound practical teaching session as part of the QUB (Queen’s University Belfast) 3rd year medical students’ endocrine and diabetes rotation. Students completed a questionnaire on the effectiveness of the teaching.MethodsFifty 3rd year medical students completed a questionnaire (15 questions) assessing various aspects of teaching effectiveness. Mean Likert scale scores ranging from 1 (very good) to 5 (very poor) were calculated for each of the questions to gauge perceptions of competence, relevance, and alignment of assessments with learning objectives. The hybrid session consisted of didactic teaching followed by a practical thyroid ultrasound hands-on session. Thyroid ultrasound was performed using a GE V-scan Air wireless handheld ultrasound.ResultsNotably, students initially reported low confidence levels in their clinical knowledge of thyroid ultrasound indications (mean score: 3.10) and in using the ultrasound machine (mean score: 3.79). However, following the session, the mean confidence level in using the ultrasound machine significantly improved to 1.96. Positive scores were noted for the clarity of learning objectives (mean score: 1.50) and opportunities for practice (mean score: 1.74). Instructor competence and approachability both received relatively favourable scores (mean score: 1.65), and students felt they had sufficient opportunities to ask questions (mean score: 1.18). The course’s relevance to students’ future careers was also rated positively (mean score: 1.24).SummaryThe introduction of a novel thyroid ultrasound practical teaching session represents an advancement in integrating ultrasound imaging techniques into undergraduate medical education. It has the potential to offer an alternative to traditional teaching methods on thyroid anatomy and pathology. Students broadly welcomed the exposure to ‘hands on’ ultrasound which appeared to be beneficial for their procedural skills and confidence in interpreting thyroid images.

Introduction

Ultrasonography has become an invaluable tool in endocrinology, recognised for its safety and effectiveness in diagnosing various conditions1. This imaging technique can assist in procedures such as fine needle biopsy (FNA) of thyroid nodules and provide real-time insights into their structural and functional status. As a result, there has been a growing trend to integrate ultrasound techniques into medical school curricula to equip students with practical, hands-on skills early in their training, mainly in North American Universities and less so in the UK and Ireland2,3.

By utilising ultrasound, clinicians can assess the thyroid’s anatomy, detect abnormalities, and monitor conditions such as thyroid nodules and goitre. This real-time imaging capability enhances diagnostic accuracy and supports effective patient management, underscoring the importance of ultrasound proficiency in the field.

As medical education evolves, integrating practical ultrasound sessions have become increasingly recognised as a crucial component in bridging the gap between theoretical learning and clinical application4,5,6. The GMC’s ‘outcomes for graduates’ state that as a part of diagnosis and medical management, graduates should be able to “interpret the results of investigations and diagnostic procedures”7. However, ultrasound teaching has not been broadly incorporated into the UK undergraduate medical curriculum and is not included in the GMC’s ‘Practical skills and procedures’8,9. At Queen’s University Belfast, the integration of ultrasound training into the 3rd year medical curriculum reflects this shift in educational priorities. The university introduced a one-hour practical thyroid ultrasound session as part of the third-year medical students’ endocrine and diabetes rotation in 2023. This initiative was designed to bridge the gap between theoretical learning and practical application, allowing students to gain hands-on experience with ultrasound technology early in their medical education and as part of their endocrinology and diabetes rotation.

The decision to include this practical session stems from the recognition that early exposure to ultrasound technology is crucial for developing diagnostic and procedural skills. Furthermore at this stage of their medical training the students did not have any formal exposure to ultrasound teaching10. By engaging with state-of-the-art equipment, such as the GE Healthcare Vscan AirTM wireless handheld ultrasound, students can directly apply their theoretical knowledge in a clinical context. This approach not only reinforces their understanding of thyroid anatomy, pathology and physical examination skills but also enhances their confidence in using ultrasound as a diagnostic tool11,12.

The focus of the teaching session was specifically on thyroid applications, allowing students to gain targeted, hands-on experience with ultrasound, the teaching session ensured that students could directly apply ultrasound techniques to the assessment of thyroid anatomy, pathology, and the identification of common thyroid conditions such as goitre and nodules. This approach was designed to build a strong foundation in the use of ultrasound for thyroid-related diagnoses and augment knowledge on thyroid disorders.

We aimed to evaluate the impact of this educational intervention. Students provided feedback through a detailed questionnaire, assessing their perceptions of the session’s effectiveness in improving their ultrasound skills and understanding of the learning objectives.

Participants and Setting

The evaluation involved fifty 3rd year medical students participating in the endocrine and diabetes rotation at Queen’s University Belfast. Five groups of roughly ten students participated in a single, one-hour session consisting of an initial 30-minute presentation followed by a 30 minute hands-on practical using the GE V-scan Air wireless handheld ultrasound. This device was chosen for its portability and ease of use, which is beneficial for an educational setting.

Teaching Content

The session was designed to provide 3rd year medical students with a comprehensive introduction to both the theoretical principles and hands-on skills required for thyroid ultrasound examination. The session was structured to ensure a solid foundation in the fundamentals of ultrasound, thyroid anatomy, pathology identification and the development of practical scanning techniques.

To begin, students were provided with a 30-minute PowerPoint presentation that covered the theoretical aspects of thyroid ultrasound [Table 2]. The presentation began with a detailed overview of thyroid anatomy. Students were introduced to the normal structure of the thyroid gland, focusing on identifying the lobes, isthmus and surrounding anatomical landmarks on ultrasound images.

Following this, the presentation provided information on basic ultrasound principles, including probe handling techniques, the importance of proper orientation and the application of correct probe pressure and movement to achieve high-quality images.

The theoretical half of the session covered the identification of common thyroid pathologies. This included information on Hashimoto’s thyroiditis, Graves’ disease, discussing the typical ultrasound characteristics of an inflamed thyroid gland, such as changes in echogenicity and structural alterations. The session further guided students on how to differentiate between benign and malignant thyroid nodules, highlighting key ultrasound features such as nodule size, shape, margins and internal composition as well as vascularity. These characteristics that are essential for determining whether further investigation is warranted. The PowerPoint presentation also included a section on fine needle aspiration (FNA), providing students with an overview of its role in the diagnosis of thyroid pathologies. After the theoretical session, each student was able to participate in a hands-on practice session, where they applied the concepts learned during the previous presentation. Using the GE V-scan Air wireless handheld ultrasound device, students practised scanning each other’s thyroid glands in groups of two using a single handheld probe. This practical component allowed them to gain first-hand experience in identifying normal and pathological features of the thyroid, reinforcing their theoretical knowledge and boosting their confidence in performing ultrasound examinations independently and also gain real-time feedback from a single demonstrator on the ultrasound technique and examination.

Through this combination of theoretical instruction and practical application, the session aimed to equip students with the knowledge and skills necessary for integrating thyroid ultrasound into their clinical practice, particularly in the assessment of thyroid-related conditions.

Questionnaire Design

A detailed questionnaire was developed to assess various aspects of the teaching session. The questionnaire comprised fifteen questions, each designed to evaluate specific elements of the educational experience. These elements included students’ perceived competence in using the ultrasound machine both before and after the session, the relevance of the session to their learning objectives, the clarity of the teaching provided, and the overall effectiveness of the session in enhancing their practical skills. The questions were rated on a Likert scale ranging from 1 (very good), 2 (good), 3 (acceptable), 4 (poor) to 5 (very poor). N/A responses (not applicable) were added to the Likert scale survey results for descriptive purposes (Figure 1) and not included in assessing the Mean Likert score.

Mean rating of questions using the Mean Likert Scale

Data Collection

Students completed the questionnaire immediately following their practical session to ensure that their responses reflected their most recent experiences. This timing allowed for the capture of immediate impressions and perceptions, which are crucial for evaluating the effectiveness of the teaching session. The questionnaire was administered in a paper format to facilitate ease of completion and ensure high response rates.

Data Analysis

The responses were analysed to calculate mean Likert scale scores for each question, providing an overview of the students’ perceptions across different aspects of the session. Scores were interpreted to gauge overall effectiveness and identify areas for improvement. For example, a mean score close to 1 indicated a positive response, while scores approaching 5 suggested areas needing enhancement.

Evaluation Metrics

Key metrics included students’ confidence levels in using the ultrasound machine, the clarity of learning objectives, and their satisfaction with the opportunities provided for practice. Students also rated instructor competence and approachability and the relevance of the session to their future careers.

Results

The evaluation of the weekly thyroid ultrasound practical session yielded insightful results regarding its effectiveness and impact on third-year medical students. The data, collected through a detailed questionnaire administered to fifty students, highlights various aspects of the teaching session and provided a comprehensive overview of students’ perceptions. Likert survey results

Overall Effectiveness

The mean Likert scale scores reflected a generally positive reception of the practical session. Students reported substantial improvements in their confidence levels and practical skills related to ultrasound use. Notably, the mean confidence score for using the ultrasound machine improved significantly from an initial score of 3.79 to 1.96 by the end of the session. This substantial decrease in the score indicates a high level of student satisfaction with their ability to operate the ultrasound equipment effectively following the practical sessions.

Clarity of Learning Objectives

Students rated the clarity of the learning objectives positively, with a mean score of 1.50. This score suggests that the objectives were well communicated and understood, contributing to the effectiveness of the teaching session. Clear learning objectives are crucial for ensuring that students can align their practice with expected outcomes and apply their skills confidently in clinical settings.

Opportunities for Practice

The session was well-received in terms of providing adequate opportunities for hands on ultrasound practice. The mean score for this aspect was 1.74. indicating that students felt they had sufficient hands-on experience with the ultrasound equipment.

Instructor Competence and Approachability

The competence and approachability of the instructors received favourable scores, with a mean rating of 1.65. This suggests that students perceived their instructors as knowledgeable and supportive, which is vital for creating a conducive learning environment and fostering effective skill development.

Perceived Relevance and Opportunities to Ask Questions

The relevance of the session to students’ future careers was rated positively, with a mean score of 1.24. Additionally, students felt they had ample opportunities to ask questions, with a mean score of 1.18, highlighting the interactive nature of the session and the supportive learning atmosphere.

Considerations for Students Who Did Not Answer All Questions

Some students marked certain questions as “N/A” (Not Applicable), which could suggest a lack of relevance, incomplete engagement or unclear questions. Students may have found some questions not relevant to their personal learning experience or felt they did not have enough information to answer some of the questions. The highest proportions of “N/A” responses were responses to questions 7,11 and 12 which all relate to feedback and assessment of students’ use of the GE Vscan Air wireless handheld ultrasound.

Discussion

The introduction of a one-hour thyroid practical into the curriculum at Queen’s University Belfast represents a significant advancement in integrating advanced imaging techniques into undergraduate medical education. The evaluation results provide a comprehensive view of the session’s impact on third-year medical students, offering valuable insights into the effectiveness of this educational approach.

The marked improvement in students’ confidence levels, with a mean score reduction from 3.79 to 1.96, suggests the practical thyroid ultrasound session was effective. The positive outcomes from this training align with metaanalytic findings, which suggest that technology-enhanced simulations, including ultrasound training, significantly improve medical students’ procedural skills and confidence13.

The hands-on nature of the session was likely a critical factor in this improvement. The device’s user-friendly interface facilitated a smoother learning experience by allowing students to focus on gaining an understanding in ultrasound techniques rather than dealing with complex machinery. This direct experience with advanced technology effectively bridged the gap between theoretical knowledge and practical application. Students were able to visualise thyroid anatomy and practice identifying pathological features, which are essential for real-world clinical scenarios14. Clearly articulated objectives provide students with a roadmap of what they are expected to learn and how this knowledge will be applied in clinical settings. This approach aligns with the literature, which emphasises the role of structured learning goals in translating theoretical knowledge into practical skills7.

Students’ positive feedback regarding the ample practice opportunities, reflects the session’s success in providing practical, hands-on experience. Practical training is essential for consolidating theoretical knowledge and developing procedural skills15. The rating for instructor competence and approachability in our study indicates that the teaching was both effective and supportive. Competent and approachable instructors are crucial in creating a conducive learning environment where students feel comfortable asking questions and seeking clarification16.

An area for improvement was identified after the teaching session which included perceived overall knowledge of using the thyroid ultrasound probe. Addressing these concerns might involve extending the session duration or providing additional preparatory materials to enhance the learning experience. This need for comprehensive training is also highlighted by recent reviews emphasising the impact of practical ultrasound training on students’ learning outcomes17.

The positive impact of this practical session on students’ knowledge and performance on using thyroid ultrasound highlights the value of integrating advanced imaging techniques into medical training. The findings are consistent with those from a systematic review that concluded that technology-enhanced simulations, including ultrasound training, significantly enhance students’ procedural skills and confidence17. As medical education evolves, there is benefit in incorporating technologies such as ultrasound early in the undergraduate curriculum to help bridge the gap between theoretical learning and practical application, better preparing students for the increasing use of imaging technologies in clinical practice18.

Future iterations of the practical session could benefit from incorporation of simulated patients or simulated models of thyroid pathology. Extending the duration of the session, providing pre-session preparatory materials, or integrating additional simulation exercises could also enhance the learning experience.

Additionally, the spectrum of pathology covered in the session could be broadened to include more advanced cases, allowing students to gain experience with a wider range of thyroid conditions. Another area for improvement is that competence was not formally assessed during the session. To better gauge student progress and ensure a robust learning experience, future sessions should consider incorporating pre- and post-test assessments or OSCE-style evaluations. These measures would help assess both theoretical knowledge and practical proficiency, ensuring that students develop the necessary skills to confidently utilise ultrasound in clinical practice. Ongoing evaluation and adaptation of the educational approach will ensure it meets the evolving needs of medical students and effectively prepares them for their future clinical roles.

Conclusion

The introduction of a novel thyroid ultrasound practical teaching session represents an advancement in integrating ultrasound imaging techniques into undergraduate medical education. It has the potential to offer an alternative to traditional teaching methods on thyroid anatomy and pathology. Students broadly welcomed the exposure to use of ‘hands on’ ultrasound which appeared to be beneficial for their procedural skills and confidence in interpreting thyroid images.

George E. Andrews and Mohamed El Bachraoui

Abstract
. We consider some q q -series which depend on a pair of positive integers ( k , m ) (k,m) . While positivity of these series holds for the first few values of ( k , m ) (k,m) , the situation is quite unclear for other values of ( k , m ) (k,m) . In addition, our series generate the number of certain two-color integer partitions weighted by ( − 1 ) j (-1)^{j} where j j is the number of even parts. Therefore, inequalities involving these partitions will be deduced from the positivity of their generating functions.

1.
Introduction

Throughout let
q
denote a complex number satisfying
|q|<1

and let
m
and
n
denote nonnegative integers.
We adopt the following standard notation from the theory of
q
-series
[
1
,
6
]

(a;q)_{0}=1,\ (a;q)_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{j}),\quad(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{j}),

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{n}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j};q)_{n},\ \text{and\ }(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k};q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(a_{j};q)_{\infty}.

We will need the following basic facts

(a;q)_{n+m}=(a;q)_{m}(aq^{m};q)_{n},\ \text{and\ }(a;q)_{\infty}=(a;q)_{n}(aq^{n};q)_{\infty}.

(1.1)

A power series
\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}
is called positive if
a_{n}\geq 0
for any nonnegative integer
n
. Positivity results for
q
-series have been intensively studied in the past to some extent in connection with
Borwein’s famous positivity conjecture
[
2
]
.
For more on this, see for instance
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
10
]
.
Our main purpose in this note is to make clear that there are interesting
problems associated with positivity questions related to the following two
q
-series.

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+1)}\frac{(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

(1.2)

and

\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+2)}\frac{(q^{2n+4},q^{2n+2+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}},

(1.3)

where
k
and
m
are positive integers.
For instance, we will see below in Corollary
1
and Theorem
2
that
\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
is positive for

(k,m)\in\{(1,1),(2,1),(3,1),(2,2),(2,3)\}.

Furthermore, it is our conjecture that
\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,4,n)q^{n}

is positive as well, see Conjecture
2
. On the other hand,
\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,5,n)q^{n}

is oscillating and it is an open problem whether there exists
m>4
such that
the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,m,n)q^{n}
is positive.

In addition, inequalities involving integer partitions have received much focus in recent years,
see for instance
[
3
,
7
]
.
It turns out that the series we look at in this work are natural generating functions for weighted
two-color integer partitions with smallest part. So, we will also deduce inequalities involving two-color partitions through
the positivity of their corresponding
q
-series.

The rest of the paper is organized as follow. In Section
2
we focus on the two-color integer partitions where the smallest part is odd
and in Section
3
we discuss the case where the smallest part is even. Sections
4
-
7

are devoted to the proofs of the main theorems. Finally, in Section
8
we give some comments and suggestions for further research.

2.
Two-color partitions with odd smallest part

We start with the following natural interpretation for
C^{\prime}(k,m,n)
as two-color partitions.

Definition 1
.

Let
k
and
m
be fixed positive integers. For a positive integer
n
, let
\mathcal{C}(k,m,n)
denote the set of two-color partitions
\pi(n)
of
n

in which:

•

the smallest part
s(\pi)
is odd and occurs at least
m
times in blue color,

•

the even blue parts are at least
2k-1
more than
s(\pi)
,

•

the even parts of the same color are distinct.

By letting
q^{2n+1}\frac{(q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}
generate the blue parts and

\frac{(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}
generate the green parts, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{C}(k,m,n)=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+1)}\frac{(-q^{2n+2},-q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

Now let
C_{0}(k,m,n)
(resp.
C_{1}(k,m,n)
) be the
number of partitions from
\mathcal{C}(k,m,n)
wherein the number of even parts is even (resp. odd).
Then it is easy to check that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\big(C_{0}(k,m,n)-C_{1}(k,m,n)\big)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+1)}\frac{(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

(2.1)

That is,

C^{\prime}(k,m,n)=C_{0}(k,m,n)-C_{1}(k,m,n),

from which it follows that positivity of the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
means the partition inequality

C_{1}(k,m,n)\leq C_{0}(k,m,n)
.

We will achieve our main results through the following identity.

Theorem 1
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers. Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}}{(q^{2n+2k-1};q^{2})_{m-k+1}}&\ \text{if $m\geq k$}\\
\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}(q^{2n+2m+1};q^{2})_{k-m-1}&\ \text{if $m<k$}.\end{cases}

By virtue of Theorem
1
and simplification, for
(k,m)\in\{(1,1),(2,1),(2,2),(3,1)\}

we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(1,1,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n+1}}{1-q^{2n+1}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,1,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{q}{(1-q)^{2}}

(2.3)

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,2,n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n+2}(1-q^{2n+2})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+3})}=\frac{q^{2}}{(1-q)^{2}}-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{3n+4}}{1-q^{2n+3}},

(2.4)

and

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(3,1,n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n+1}(1-q^{2n+3})}{(1-q)(1-q^{3})}=\frac{q(1+q+q^{2}-q^{3})}{(1-q)(1-q^{3})^{2}}.

(2.5)

Then it is clear that for these values of
(k,m)
the series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
is positive.
This leads to the following inequalities involving two-color partitions.

Corollary 1
.

(a) For any nonnegative integer
n
we have
C_{1}(1,1,n)\leq C_{0}(1,1,n)
.

(b) If
m\in\{1,2\}
, then for any positive integer
n
we have
C_{1}(2,m,n)\leq C_{0}(2,m,n)
.

(c) For any nonnegative integer
n
we have
C_{1}(3,1,n)\leq C_{0}(3,1,n)
.

We now deal with the case
(k,m)=(2,3)
.

Theorem 2
.

The series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,3,n)q^{n}
is positive.

Corollary 2
.

For any positive integer
n
we have
C_{1}(2,3,n)\leq C_{0}(2,3,n)
.

We now state our second main result where we handle the case
(k,m)=(4,1)
.

Theorem 3
.

The series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(4,1,n)q^{n}
is positive.

Corollary 3
.

For any positive integer
n
we have
C_{1}(4,1,n)\leq C_{0}(4,1,n)
.

We close this section by the following positivity conjectures.

Conjecture 1
.

For any nonnegative integer
k
, the series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,1,n)q^{n}
is positive.

Conjecture 2
.

The series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,4,n)q^{n}
is positive.

3.
Two-color partitions with even smallest part

We now focus on the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
as in (
1.3
).
We start with the following natural interpretation for
D^{\prime}(k,m,n)
as two-color partitions.

Definition 2
.

Let
k
and
m
be fixed positive integers. For a positive integer
n
, let
\mathcal{D}(k,m,n)
denote the set of two-color partitions
\pi(n)
of
n

in which:

•

the smallest part
s(\pi)
is even, blue, and occurs exactly
m
times,

•

the even blue parts are at least
2k
more than
s(\pi)
,

•

the even parts of the same color are distinct.

By letting
q^{2n+2}\frac{(q^{2n+2+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}}
generate the blue parts and

\frac{(q^{2n+4};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}}
generate the green parts, we have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{D}(k,m,n)=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+2)}\frac{(-q^{2n+4},-q^{2n+2+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

Let
D_{0}(k,m,n)
(resp.
D_{1}(k,m,n)
) be the
number of partitions from
\mathcal{D}(k,m,n)
wherein the number of even parts that are greater than
s(\pi)
is even (resp. odd).
and let
D^{\prime}(k,m,n)=D_{0}(k,m,n)-D_{1}(k,m,n)
.
Then it is easily seen that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\big(D_{0}(k,m,n)-D_{1}(k,m,n)\big)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+2)}\frac{(q^{2n+4},q^{2n+2+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

(3.1)

That is,

D^{\prime}(k,m,n)=D_{0}(k,m,n)-D_{1}(k,m,n),

from which it follows that positivity of the series
\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
means the partition inequality

D_{1}(k,m,n)\leq D_{0}(k,m,n)
.

We will establish the following connection between
D^{\prime}(k,m,n)
and
C^{\prime}(k,m,n)
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k
and
m
be positive integers. Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}=q^{-m}\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}-\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}.

=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}}{(q^{2n+2k-1};q^{2})_{m-k+1}}-\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}&\ \text{if $m\geq k$}\\
\frac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}(q^{2n+2m+1};q^{2})_{k-m-1}-\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}&\ \text{if $m<k$}.\end{cases}

Our first application of Theorem
4
deals with
(k,m)=(2,1)
.

Theorem 5
.

The series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(2,1,n)q^{n}
is positive.

Corollary 4
.

For any positive integer
n
we have
D_{1}(2,1,n)\leq D_{0}(2,1,n)
.

We cannot prove any other positivity results for the series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
. However, we have the following
two conjectures.

Conjecture 3
.

The series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(2,2,n)q^{n}
is positive.

Conjecture 4
.

The only negative coefficients of the series
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(2,3,n)q^{n}
occur at
n=10
and
n=22
.

Also, we have the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5
.

If
k>m
, then
\textstyle\sum_{n\geq 0}D^{\prime}(k,m,n)q^{n}
is eventually positive.

4.
Proof of Theorem
1
and Theorem
4

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We will require Heine’s first transformation
[
1
,
6
]

{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{a,b}{c};q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muz\biggr]=\frac{(b,az;q)_{\infty}}{(c,z;q)_{\infty}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{c/b,z}{az};q\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0mub\biggr].

(4.1)

We have

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{m(2n+1)}(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{m}\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2mn}(q;q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{n}}

=q^{m}\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{q,q}{q^{2k}};q^{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muq^{2m}\biggr]

=q^{m}\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{(q,q^{2m+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2k},q^{2m};q^{2})_{\infty}}{}_{2}\phi_{1}\biggl[\genfrac{.}{.}{0.0pt}{}{q^{2k-1},q^{2m}}{q^{2m+1}};q^{2}\mathchar 59\relax\mkern 6.0muq\biggr]

=q^{m}\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{(q,q^{2m+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2k},q^{2m};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q^{2k-1},q^{2m};q^{2})_{n}}{(q^{2},q^{2m+1};q^{2})_{n}}

=q^{m}\frac{(q^{2k-1};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2m+1};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+2k-1},q^{2n+2m};q^{2})_{\infty}}

=\begin{cases}\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}}{(q^{2n+2k-1};q^{2})_{m-k+1}}&\ \text{if $m\geq k$}\\
\frac{q^{m}}{(q;q^{2})_{k-1}}\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n}(q^{2n+2};q^{2})_{m-1}(q^{2n+2m+1};q^{2})_{k-m-1}&\ \text{if $m<k$},\end{cases}

where in the third step we applied (
4.1
).

Proof of Theorem
4
.

By (
1.1
), we find

\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{m(2n+2)}(q^{2n+4},q^{2n+2+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}=q^{2m}\frac{(q^{4},q^{2k+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{2mn}(q^{3};q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(q^{4},q^{2k+2};q^{2})_{n}}

=q^{2m}\frac{(q^{4},q^{2k+2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{3};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\frac{(1-q^{2})(1-q^{2k})}{(1-q)^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2mn}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n+1}^{2}}{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{n+1}}

=q^{2m}\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\sum_{n\geq 1}q^{2m(n-1)}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{n}}

=\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}\Big(\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2mn}\frac{(q;q^{2})_{n}^{2}}{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{n}}-1\Big)

=\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{2mn}\frac{(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}

=q^{-m}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{m(2n+1)}\frac{(q^{2n+2},q^{2n+2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2n+1};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}-\frac{(q^{2},q^{2k};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}},

which gives the desired formula by (
2.1
) and Theorem
1
.

5.
Proof of Theorem
2

By Theorem
1
with
(k,m)=(2,3)
we find after simplification

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(2,3,n)q^{n}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{3}(1-q^{2n+2})(1-q^{2n+4})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5})}.

We want to prove that the coefficient of
q^{N}
in

\frac{q^{3}(1-q^{2n+2})(1-q^{2n+4})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5})}

is nonnegative.
To this end, we need some preparation. We start with a lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let for any nonnegative integer
n

\frac{1}{(1+q)(1-q^{2n+3})}=\sum_{N\geq 0}a(N)q^{N}.

Then

a(N)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{N}&\ \text{if\ }\lfloor\frac{N}{2n+3}\rfloor\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2),\\
0&\ \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We have

\frac{1}{(1+q)(1-q^{2n+3})}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{j}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{m(2n+3)}.

Then to find the coefficient of
a(N)
we need first to solve the equation

N=j+m(2n+3)
where
j\geq 0
and
0\leq m\leq\lfloor\frac{N}{2n+3}\rfloor
.
The solutions are

\begin{cases}j=N&\ \text{if $m=0$},\\
j=N-(2n+3)&\ \text{if $m=1$},\\
j=N-2(2n+3)&\ \text{if $m=2$},\\
\vdots&\ \vdots\\
j=N-\lfloor\frac{N}{2n+3}\rfloor(2n+3)&\ \text{if $m=\lfloor\frac{N}{2n+3}\rfloor$}.\\
\end{cases}

As these solutions alternate in sign, we have

a(N)=(-1)^{N}-(1)^{N}+(-1)^{N}+\cdots=\begin{cases}(-1)^{N}&\ \text{if\ }\lfloor\frac{N}{2n+3}\rfloor\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2),\\
0&\ \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

as desired.
∎

By shifting the coefficient from
N
to
N-A
, we get the following important consequence of Lemma
1
.

Corollary 5
.

Let for any nonnegative integer
n
and any positive integer
A

\frac{q^{A}}{(1+q)(1-q^{2n+3})}=\sum_{N\geq 0}a(N)q^{N}.

Then

a(N)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{N-A}&\ \text{if\ }\lfloor\frac{N-A}{2n+3}\rfloor\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2),\\
0&\ \text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

We need another lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For any nonnegative integer
n
we have

\frac{q^{3}(1-q^{2n+2})(1-q^{2n+4})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5})}

=\frac{q}{1-q}-\frac{q^{2}}{(1+q)(1-q^{2n+3})}-\frac{q}{1-q^{2n+5}}-\frac{q^{3}}{(1+q)(1-q^{2n+5})}

Proof.

Immediate by inspection.
∎

From Lemma
2
and Corollary
5
we see that the coefficient of
q^{N}
in

\frac{q^{3}(1-q^{2n+2})(1-q^{2n+4})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5})}

is
1-T_{1}-T_{2}-T_{3}
,
where

T_{1}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{N}&\ \text{if\ }\lfloor\frac{N-2}{2n+3}\rfloor\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\\
0&\ \text{otherwise},\end{cases}

T_{2}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{N-1}&\ \text{if\ }\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\rfloor\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\\
0&\ \text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

T_{3}=\begin{cases}1&\ \text{if\ }N\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2n+5)\\
0&\ \text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that it is not possible to have
T_{1}+T_{2}=2
because
(-1)^{N}+(-1)^{N+1}=0
. Hence, if
N\not\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2n+5)
, then
the coefficient of
q^{N}
is nonnegative. So there are only two possible cases where the coefficient of
q^{N}
might be
-1
:

N\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2n+5)
,
N
is even, and
\lfloor\frac{N-2}{2n+3}\rfloor
is even
or

N\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2n+5)
,
N
is odd, and
\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\rfloor
is even. We now deal with each of these two cases.

Case 1.
Write
N=k(2n+5)+1
. So for
N
to be even,
k
must be odd, say
k=2j+1
, but then we get
T_{2}=0
. So,

\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\rfloor
must be odd. But

\left\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{(2j+1)(2n+5)+1-3}{2n+5}\right\rfloor

\left\lfloor(2j+1)-\frac{2}{2n+5}\right\rfloor=2j,

which gives a contradiction. This yields a nonnegative coefficient for
q^{N}
.

Case 2.
Writing
N=k(2n+5)+1
and using the assumption that
N
is odd we see that
k
must be even, say
k=2j
. Thus

\left\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{(2j)(2n+5)+1-3}{2n+5}\right\rfloor

\left\lfloor 2j-\frac{2}{2n+5}\right\rfloor=2j-1,

contradicting the fact that
\lfloor\frac{N-3}{2n+5}\rfloor
is even. This yields a nonnegative coefficient for
q^{N}
as well.
The proof is complete.

6.
Proof of Theorem
3

By Theorem
1
with
(k,m)=(4,1)
, we find

\sum_{n\geq 0}C^{\prime}(4,1,n)q^{n}=\frac{q}{(1-q)(1-q^{3})(1-q^{5})}\sum_{n\geq 0}q^{n}(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5}).

So, to establish the desired inequality it is enough to show that
\frac{(1-q^{2n+3})(1-q^{2n+5})}{(1-q)(1-q^{3})(1-q^{5})}
has nonnegative coefficients.
This clearly holds if
3\mid 2n+3
or
3\mid 2n+5
. Now assume that
3\nmid 2n+3
and

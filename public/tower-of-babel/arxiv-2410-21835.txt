Ideal Magnetohydrodynamics Around Couette Flow: Long Time Stability and Vorticity-Current Instability
Niklas Knobel

Abstract
. This article considers the ideal 2D magnetohydrodynamic equations in a infinite periodic channel close to a combination of an affine shear flow, called Couette flow, and a constant magnetic field. This incorporates important physical effects, including mixing and coupling of velocity and magnetic field. We establish the existence and stability of the velocity and magnetic field for Gevrey-class perturbations of size ε \varepsilon , valid up to times t ∼ ε − 1 t\sim\varepsilon^{-1} . Additionally, the vorticity and current grow as O ⁡ ( t ) O(t) and there is no inviscid damping of the velocity and magnetic field. This is similar to the above threshold case for the 3 ​ D 3D Navier-Stokes [ BGM22 ] where growth in ‘streaks’ leads to time scales of t ∼ ε − 1 t\sim\varepsilon^{-1} . In particular, for the ideal MHD equations, our article suggests that for a wide range of initial data, the scenario “induction by shear ⇒ \Rightarrow vorticity and current growth ⇒ \Rightarrow vorticity and current breakdown” leads to instability and possible turbulences.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the ideal 2D magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{V}+\tilde{V}\cdot\nabla\tilde{V}+\nabla\Pi&=\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla\tilde{B},\\
\partial_{t}\tilde{B}+\tilde{V}\cdot\nabla\tilde{B}&=\tilde{B}\cdot\nabla\tilde{V},\\
\nabla\cdot\tilde{V}=\nabla\cdot\tilde{B}&=0,\\
(t,x,y)&\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}=:\Omega.\end{split}

They model the evolution of the velocity field
\tilde{V}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of a conducting fluid interacting with a magnetic field
\tilde{B}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The function
\Pi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
denotes the pressure. The MHD equations are derived from the Navier-Stokes and Maxwell equations and are a common model used in plasma physics and engineering
[
Dav16
,
Fre14
]
. A fundamental question of the MHD equations is understanding the behavior in the limit of high fluid and magnetic Reynolds numbers. The ideal MHD equations correspond to the limiting case and play a crucial role in modeling fusion reactors
[
Fre82
]
.

For the ideal MHD equations, global-in-time well-posedness is an open problem. Only local-in-time wellposedness has been established
[
Koz89
,
Sec93
,
MY06
,
CF23
]
. The maximal time of existence remains an important question and there exists a blow-up criterion on vorticity and current similar to the Beale-Kato-Majda criterion
[
CKS97
,
CCM07
]
. Under strong decay assumptions, small initial perturbations of the ideal MHD equations around a large constant magnetic field behave like the linearized system
[
BSS88
]
.

The (ideal) MHD equations exhibit several important physical and mathematical effects and an essential challenge is to understand the long time behavior of solutions. We investigate perturbations around the combination of an affine shear flow, called Couette flow and a constant magnetic field:

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}V_{s}(t,x,y)&=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
0\end{pmatrix},\\
B_{s}(t,x,y)&=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.\end{split}

The Couette flow leads to mixing by transporting perturbations and also leads to induction in the magnetic field. The constant magnetic field induces a strong coupling between velocity and magnetic field perturbations.
We are interested in perturbations around this stationary solution

\displaystyle V(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\tilde{V}(t,x,y)-V_{s},

\displaystyle B(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=\tilde{B}(t,x,y)-B_{s},

which satisfy the equations

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}V+y\partial_{x}V+V_{2}e_{1}&+V\cdot\nabla V-2\partial_{x}\nabla\Delta^{-1}V_{2}+\nabla\Pi=B\cdot\nabla B,\\
\partial_{t}B+y\partial_{x}B-B_{2}e_{1}&+V\cdot\nabla B\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,=B\cdot\nabla V,\\
\nabla\cdot V=\nabla\cdot B&=0,\\
(t,x,y)&\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}=:\Omega.\end{split}

The main result of this article is the existence, stability and growth of solutions:

.

Let

and
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>0

Then the corresponding solution of (

Remark 1

(On dissipation regimes)
.

In Corollary
1
we extend the ideal stability result to the dissipative MHD equations if the initial data is large compared to the dissipation parameters. For fixed
\varepsilon
the stability holds as
\kappa,\nu\to 0
. In other works on nonlinear stability of the MHD equations around Couette flow the initial data is chosen to be small in terms of the dissipation (see
[
ZZ24
,
Dol24
,
Kno24
]
or the overview written later). In particular, Corollary
1
this is the first result if
\max(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}},\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}})\ll\varepsilon
for the full dissipative case (compared to
[
WZ24
]
) and if
\kappa\ll\varepsilon
for the inviscid and resistive case (compared to
[

Dissipation is expected to significantly impact the dynamics at timescales of

but stability persists until
t\sim\min(\nu^{-1},\kappa^{-1})
. Furthermore, Corollary

. This destabilizing effect has been studied rigorously in
[

]
and also appears in
[
Kno24
,
WZ24
]
.

Remark 2

(On time of stability and comparison to
3D
Navier-Stokes equation)
.

The time scale
t\sim\epsilon^{-1}
is expected to be optimal for stability due to nonlinear effects. In the linear dynamics, the velocity and magnetic field are (global) stable, however, the vorticity and current grow in time. Due to the structure of the nonlinearity, nonlinear effects grow with the vorticity and current such that around the time
t\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
we expect that nonlinear effects dominate and secondary instabilities take over.

There are parallels to the stability of the 3D Navier-Stokes equations around Couette flow. In the above threshold case
[
BGM22
]
the velocity is linearly stable in its non-average part but due growth of ’streaks’ in the
x
-average, which yields increasing nonlinear effects, the stability only holds until
t\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
for Gevrey perturbations of size
\varepsilon
. They describe the destabilizing mechanism as “lift-up effect
\implies
streak growth
\implies
streak breakdown”.

For the ideal MHD equations around Couette flow, we describe the destabilizing effect as “induction by shear
\implies
vorticity and current growth
\implies
vorticity and current breakdown”. By induction by shear effect, we refer to the non-transport interaction of the magnetic field perturbation and the Couette flow (i.e.
b_{2}e_{1}=(b\cdot\nabla)ye_{1}
). Together with the constant magnetic field coupling, this leads to the growth of the vorticity and current perturbation.

Let us comment further on the result:

•

The nonlinear destabilizing mechanism is the ideal MHD equations’ equivalent of Orr’s observation
[
Orr07
]
. Fourier modes in nonlinearity exhibit a large interaction for specific frequencies and times, leading to growth in both modes. These resonances can lead to resonance chains which yield norm inflation in Gevrey regularity (see Subsection
2.4
).

•

Due to the structure of the ideal MHD equations and the growth of the vorticity and current, nonlinear effects are stronger than in related works on the Euler
[
BM15
]
and inviscid Boussinesq equations
[
BBZD23
]
around Couette flow (and affine temperature profile). In particular, for the Euler and Boussinesq equations, one expects stability in Sobolev spaces for times
t\sim\varepsilon^{-1}
.

•

We use carefully constructed tailored unknowns that match the equation’s linear and nonlinear dynamics such that the estimates reduce to their key elements. Furthermore, they allow us the choice of
\alpha\neq 0
where all constants degenerate as
\alpha\to 0
. These tailored unknowns are constructed in two steps: First, we adapt the velocity to cancel out possible growth in the magnetic field. Second, we change to symmetric variables to use cancellations in the equation.

•

The current and vorticity are important unknowns in the literature and in particular for wellposedness
[
CKS97
]
. The current and vorticity are expressed in terms of the tailored unknowns, making it sufficient to work with those alone.

For the MHD equations around Couette flow and constant magnetic field, previous stability results rely on the resistivity
\kappa>0
(or viscosity if
\nu\leq\kappa

[
CZ24

, Dolce proves stability for small initial data in Sobolev spaces
[
Dol24
]
. In
[
KZ23b
]
, Zillinger and the author prove stability for the case of horizontal resistivity and full viscosity,
\nu=\kappa_{x}>0
and
\kappa_{y}=0
. When resistivity is smaller than viscosity,

small initial data are stable in Sobolev spaces, but if resistivity is very small compared to the viscosity there is growth in the magnetic field leading to norm inflation of order
\nu\kappa^{-\frac{1}{3}}

[
Kno24
]
. This was generalized by Whang and Zang
[
WZ24
]
to all different full dissipation regimes
0<\nu,\kappa\leq 1
. In
[
KZ23a
]

\nu=0
and resistivity
\kappa>0

[
ZZ24

proved the almost matching nonlinear stability result for small perturbations in Gevrey

.
In these previous results for the MHD equations, the norm of the vorticity or current grows by

or

in the non-resistive limit. Theorem

holds for times

[

[

Resonances and Gevrey regularity:
In plasmas or fluids, it is an important question whether perturbations of a specific solution behave as under the linear dynamics. Echoes are an experimental and numerical observed effect
[
YOD05
,
MWGO68
,
Shn13
]
where nonlinear effects change the dynamics at a later time. Such echoes can appear due to resonances in nonlinear terms which lead to norm inflation in Gevrey regularity. In the following, we summarize existing results on related equations using Gevrey spaces. The use of Gevrey regularity to bound resonances mechanism was first established by Villani for the Vlasov-Poisson equations
[
MV11
]
. In fluid dynamics, resonances appear for perturbations of the Couette flow. Bedrossian and Masmoudi established the stability of the Euler equations for small initial data in Gevrey spaces
[
BM15
]
. Following this seminal work, many results for the Euler and Navier-Stokes equations have been established
[
BMV16
,
DZ21
,
DM23
,
IJ20
,
BGM20
,
BGM22
,
LMZ22
]
. For the inviscid Boussinesq equations, Gevrey perturbations of size
\varepsilon
are stable until times
t\sim\varepsilon^{-2}

[
BBZD23
,
Zil23b
]
. For the Boussinesq equations with viscosity but without thermal dissipation small Gevrey perturbations lead to stability
[
MSHZ22
,
Zil23a
]
. For the inviscid and resistive MHD equations, small Gevrey perturbations lead to stability
[
KZ23a
,
ZZ24
]
.

In the proof we do not change variables according to the average velocity and magnetic field. This is not necessary, since in lower regularity we improve the estimates on the average. Therefore, the contribution of the transport by the average is small.

Nonlinear Growth Mechanism

In this section, we explain the main nonlinear effect in (
10
) and (
11
). Since the linear terms in (

are comparable, it is sufficient to consider the nonlinearities of (

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{1}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}(b\nabla_{t}b-v\nabla_{t}v)_{\neq}=-\Lambda_{t}^{-1}(\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}p_{2}\nabla\Lambda_{t}p_{2}-\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}p_{1}\nabla\Lambda_{t}p_{1})_{\neq},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{2}

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{-1}_{t}\nabla^{\perp}_{t}(b\nabla_{t}v-v\nabla_{t}b)_{\neq}=\Lambda_{t}(\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}p_{1}\nabla\Lambda_{t}^{-1}p_{2})_{\neq}.

In the nonlinear terms, there arises a destabilizing mechanism, which is the ideal MHD equations’ equivalent of Orr’s observation
[
Orr07
]
. For the Euler equations (c.f.
[
BM15
]
), there is an interaction between high and low frequencies which leads to growth in specific modes and times which iterates to echo chains. These echo chains lead to loss of Gevrey regularity, such that Gevrey spaces are necessary for stability
[

,
DM23
]
.

For the ideal MHD equations, the growth of vorticity
w=\Lambda_{t}p_{1}
and current
j=\Lambda_{t}p_{2}
lead to an increase of strength of nonlinear effects. The following outlines the main growth model due to the high-low interaction on nonlinearities for the ideal MHD equations. We focus on
p_{1}
(dropping the
1
for simplicity) After a Fourier transform we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(k,\eta)

\displaystyle=-\Lambda_{t}^{-1}(\Lambda_{t}^{-1}\nabla^{\perp}p^{hi}\cdot\nabla\Lambda_{t}p^{low})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l}\int d\xi(\eta l-k\xi)\tfrac{\sqrt{l^{2}+(\xi-lt)^{2}}}{\sqrt{k^{2}+(\eta-kt)^{2}}\sqrt{(k-l)^{2}+(\eta-\xi-(k-l)t)^{2}}}p^{hi}(k-l,\eta-\xi)p^{low}(l,\xi).

For this nonlinearity, derive the main growth model for the ideal MHD equations. If we consider
\xi\approx 0
,
l=\pm 1
and
p(l,0)=\varepsilon
we will obtain on the modes
k
and
k-1
the model

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(t,k,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon\langle t\rangle\tfrac{\eta}{k(k-1)}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}\sqrt{(1+(\frac{\eta}{k-1}-t)^{2}}}p(k-1,\eta),

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p(t,k-1,\eta)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon\langle t\rangle\tfrac{\eta}{k(k-1)}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}\sqrt{(1+(\frac{\eta}{k-1}-t)^{2}}}p(k,\eta).

Close to resonant times
t\in\tfrac{\eta}{k}+\tfrac{1}{2}[-\tfrac{\eta}{k(k+1)},\tfrac{\eta}{k(k-1)}]=:I_{k}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\tfrac{\eta}{k(k-1)}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k-1}-t)^{2}}}\approx 1.

Therefore, for fixed
\eta
and
\varepsilon\langle t\rangle\approx c_{0}
the interaction model corresponds to

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}p(t,k)&=c_{0}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}}p(k-1,\eta),\\
\partial_{t}p(t,k-1)&=c_{0}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}}p(k,\eta).\end{split}

For
q^{\pm}(t)=p(t,k)\pm p(t,k-1)
the main growth model looks like

(13)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{0}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}}q^{\pm}.

For initial data
p(t_{k},k-1)=0
we obtain

\displaystyle q^{\pm}(t_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\pm c_{0}\int_{I_{k}}\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+(\frac{\eta}{k}-t)^{2}}}d\tau\right)

\displaystyle\approx\exp(\pm c_{0}\sinh^{-1}(\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}))p(t_{k},k).

Therefore, for the mode
p(t_{k-1},k-1)
we obtain that

\displaystyle p(t_{k-1},k-1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(q^{+}(t_{k-1})-q^{-}(t_{k-1}))

\displaystyle=\sinh(c_{0}\sinh^{-1}(\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}))p(t_{k},k).

Then we use that
\sinh(c_{0}\sinh^{-1}(\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}}))\geq\tfrac{c_{0}}{2}(\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}})^{c_{0}}
to infer

\displaystyle p(t_{k-1},k-1)

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{c_{0}}{2}(\tfrac{\eta}{k^{2}})^{c_{0}}p(t_{k},k).

Therefore,
p(t_{k},k)
yields growth of
p(t_{k-1},k-1)
which we can iterate
k\to k-1
. For
\eta\gg c_{0}^{-1}
and a mode

we obtain for the resonance chain

by Sterlings approximation the asymptotics

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq k\leq\sqrt{\eta}}(\tfrac{c_{0}\eta}{k^{2}})^{c_{0}}=(\tfrac{(c_{0}\eta)^{k}}{(k!)^{2}})^{c_{0}}\sim\eta^{-\frac{c_{0}}{2}}\exp(Cc_{0}\sqrt{c_{0}\eta}).

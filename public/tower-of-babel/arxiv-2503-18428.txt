Hilbert scheme of smooth curves of degree sixteen in ℙ 5 \mathbb{P}^{5}
Changho Keem

Abstract
. We denote by ℋ d , g , r \mathcal{H}_{d,g,r} the Hilbert scheme of smooth curves, which is the union of components whose general point corresponds to a smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curve of degree d d and genus g g in ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} . In this article, we study ℋ 16 , g , 5 \mathcal{H}_{16,g,5} for almost every possible genus g g and chasing after its irreducibility. We also study the natural moduli map ℋ ⌈ , } , ▽ → μ ℳ } \cal{H}_{d,g,5}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\xrightarrow{\ \ }}}\cal{M}_{g} and several key properties such as gonality of a general element as well as characterizing smooth elements in each component.

1.
An overview, motivation and preliminary set up

Let
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
be the Hilbert scheme of smooth, irreducible and non-degenerate curves of degree
d
and genus
g
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
.
We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,r}
the union of components of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
whose general element corresponds to a
linearly normal
curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

In this paper we study Hilbert schemes
\cal{H}_{16,g,5}
of smooth curves in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
of degree sixteen and genus
g
.
Specifically, we determine when
\cal{H}_{16,g,5}
is irreducible and study the moduli map

\cal{H}_{d,g,5}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\xrightarrow{\ \ }}}\cal{M}_{g}
for almost all possible genus
0\leq g\leq 21
. We also study key properties such as gonality as well as characterizing smooth elements in each component.

In general, determining the irreducibility of Hilbert schemes is rather a non-trivial task, which goes back to Severi
[
33
]
who asserted with an incomplete proof that the Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
is irreducible for
the triples of
(d,g,r)
in the range

(i)
d\geq g+r~~~~
or

in the following Brill-Noether range which is much wider

(ii)
\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)\geq 0
.

The assertion of Severi turns out to be true for
r=3,4
under the condition (i); cf.
[
17
,
18
]
. It is also known that
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,3}
is
irreducible in an extended range
d\geq g
; cf.
[
27
]
and references therein.
For
r=4
, irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{d,g,4}
also holds in the range
d\geq g+1
except for some sporadic small values of the genus
g
; cf.
[
23
,
29
,
28
]
.

For
r=5
the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,5}
is not known in general in the range
d\geq g+5
as conjectured by Severi. The best known result so far regarding the irreducibility of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,5}
is the result of H. Iliev who showed that

\mathcal{H}_{d,g,5}
is irreducible whenever
d\geq\max\{\frac{11}{10}g+2,g+5\}
; cf.

[
24
]
.

There have been attempts for a better understanding of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,5}
when the
degree of projective curves in question is relatively low. For example,
in recent works of the author jointly with E. Ballico,
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,5}
has been studied
rather extensively for
d\leq 15
; cf.
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
.
In this paper, we push forward one step further to the case
d=16
, which is the main object of our study and motivation.

1.1.
Notations and conventions

For notation and conventions, we follow those in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
; e.g.
\pi(d,r)
is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible, non-degenerate and reduced curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
which is usually referred as the first Castelnuovo genus bound. We shall refer to curves
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
d
whose (arithmetic) genus equals
\pi(d,r)
as
extremal curves
.
\pi_{1}(d,r)
is the so-called the second Castelnuvo genus bound which is the maximal possible arithmetic genus of an irreducible, non-degenerate and reduced curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
not lying on a surface of minimal degree
r-1
; cf.
[
19
, page 99]
,
[
3
, page 123]
.
We shall call curves
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
of degree
d
and (arithmetic) genus
g
such that
\pi_{1}(d,r)<g\leq\pi(d,r)

nearly extremal curves
.

Following classical terminology, a linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on a smooth curve
C
is denoted by
g^{r}_{d}
.
A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
(
r\geq 2
) on a smooth curve
C
is called
birationally very ample
when the morphism

C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{r}
induced by the
g^{r}_{d}
is generically one-to-one onto (or is birational to) its image curve.
A base-point-free linear series
g^{r}_{d}
on
C
is said to be compounded of an involution (
compounded
for short) if the morphism induced by the linear series
g^{r}_{d}
gives rise to a non-trivial covering map
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}
of degree
k\geq 2
.
Throughout we work exclusively over the field of complex numbers.

1.2.
Preliminary set up

We briefly recall a couple of fundamental results and basic frameworks for our study which are well-known; cf.
[
4
]
or
[
2
, §1 and §2]
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of smooth curves of genus
g
. Given an isomorphism class
[C]\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
corresponding to a smooth irreducible curve
C
, there exist a neighborhood
U\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
of the class
[C]
and a smooth connected variety
\mathcal{M}
which is a finite ramified covering
h:\mathcal{M}\xrightarrow{\ \ }U
, as well as varieties
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
and
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
proper over
\mathcal{M}
with the following properties:

(1)

\xi:\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathcal{M}
is a universal curve, i.e. for every
p\in\mathcal{M}
,
\xi^{-1}(p)
is a smooth curve of genus
g
whose isomorphism class is
h(p)
,

(2)

\mathcal{W}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the pairs
(p,L)
where
L
is a line bundle of degree
d
and
h^{0}(L)\geq r+1
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
,

(3)

\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
parametrizes the couples
(p,\mathcal{D})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is possibly an incomplete linear series of degree
d
and dimension
r
on
\xi^{-1}(p)
.

For a complete linear series
\mathcal{D}
on a smooth curve
C
, the residual series
|K_{C}-\mathcal{D}|
is sometimes denoted by
\mathcal{D}^{\vee}
.
Given an irreducible family
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
with some geometric meaning, whose general member is complete, the closure of the family
\{\mathcal{D}^{\vee}|\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{F},~\mathcal{D}\textrm{ is complete}\}\subset\mathcal{W}^{g-d+r-1}_{2g-2-d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
.

Let
\boldmath{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
(
\boldmath{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
, respectively) be the union of components of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
whose general element
(p,\mathcal{D})
corresponds to a very ample (very ample and complete, respectively) linear series
\mathcal{D}
on the curve
C=\xi^{-1}(p)
. By recalling that an open subset of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
consisting of elements corresponding to smooth irreducible and non-degenerate curves is a
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
-bundle over an open subset of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
,
the irreducibility of
\boldmath{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}}
guarantees the irreducibility of
\boldmath{\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}}
. Likewise, the irreducibility of
\boldmath{\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{L}}}
ensures the irreducibility of

\boldmath{\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}^{\mathcal{L}}}
.

We recall the following fundamental fact regarding the scheme
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
which is also well-known; cf.
[
19
, 2.a]
and
[
4
, Ch. 21, §3, 5, 6, 11, 12]
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For non-negative integers
d
,
g
and
r
, let

\rho(d,g,r):=g-(r+1)(g-d+r)

be the Brill-Noether number.
The dimension of any component of
\mathcal{G}^{r}_{d}
is at least

\lambda(d,g,r):=3g-3+\rho(d,g,r),

hence the dimension of any component of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g,r}
is at least

\mathcal{X}(d,g,r):=\lambda(d,g,r)+\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r}).

Moreover, if
\rho(d,g,r)\geq 0
, there exists a unique component
\mathcal{G}_{0}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
which dominates
\mathcal{M}
(or
\mathcal{M}_{g}
).

2.1.
Severi variety of nodal curves on Hirzebruch surfaces

We begin to recall the following generalities regarding the Severi variety of (nodal) curves on a Hirzebruch surface

Definition 2.1

(iii)

Let

Remark 2.2

(i)

For dimension count of certain families of curves under our study, we recall

Remark 2.3

For an irreducible and non-degenerate surface

(i)

Remark 2.8

Let

2.3.
On moduli map

Let
\mu:\cal{H}_{16,g,5}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{M}_{g}
denote the natural functorial map - which we call the
moduli map
- sending
X\in\cal{H}_{16,g,5}
to its isomorphism class

\mu(X)\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Remark 2.10
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
,
r\geq 2
, be a smooth curve of genus
g\geq 2
. Since
X
has only finitely many automorphisms, the set
G:=\{h\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})|h(X)=X\}
is a finite group. Hence the set

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})X:=\{Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}|Y=\sigma(X)\mathrm{~for~some~}\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})\}

consisting of all curves
Y\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
projectively equivalent to
X
is an irreducible quasi-projective variety isomorphic to
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})/G
. Thus

\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})X=\dim\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{r})=(r+1)^{2}-1

and
\dim\mu(\cal{H})\leq\dim\cal{H}-35
for every irreducible family
\cal{H}\subseteq\cal{H}_{16,g,5}
.

ramified coverings of curves of genus
\gamma
– is of pure dimension
2g+(2n-3)(1-\gamma)-2
; cf.
[
4

. In our case, the irreducible family of very ample
g^{5}_{16}
’s which is
5
-dimensional subseries of the residual series of
|\varphi^{*}(K_{E})|
on a double covering
of smooth plane quartics form a family
\cal{F}
with

\displaystyle\dim\cal{F}

\displaystyle=\dim\cal{X}_{2,3}+\dim\mathbb{G}(5,6)=2g-4+\dim\mathbb{G}(5,6)

\displaystyle=28<\lambda(16,13,5)=37,

hence
\cal{F}
does not constitute a
full component.

(i-B)

Suppose
\cal{E}=g^{2}_{8}
is birationally very ample. The Severi variety of plane curves of
degree
8
with
\delta=\binom{8-1}{2}-g=8
nodes has dimension

\dim\Sigma_{8,g}=\tbinom{8+2}{2}-1-\delta=36.

Therefore the family
\cal{F}_{\Sigma_{8,g}}
consisting of
g^{5}_{16}
’s arising this way has dimension

\dim\Sigma_{8,g}/\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{2})+\dim\mathbb{G}(5,6)=34<\lambda(16,13,5),

hence this family
\cal{F}_{\Sigma_{8,g}}
does not constitute a full component and this finishes the proof of
\cal{H}_{16,13,5}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{16,13,5}
.

Since
\rho(16,13,5)\geq 0
,
\cal{H}_{16,13,5}
dominates
\cal{M}_{13}
. By Brill-Noether theorem,
W^{1}_{7}(C)=\emptyset
,
W^{2}_{8}(C)=\emptyset
and
\dim W^{1}_{8}(C)=1
for general
C\in\cal{M}_{13}
. Thus
|K_{C}-g^{1}_{8}|=g^{5}_{16}
is very ample and therefore we have

\mu^{-1}\mu(C)\cong W^{1}_{8}(C)\times\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{5})

for a general
C\in\cal{H}_{16,13,5}
.

(ii)

The irreducibility and the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{16,12,5}
is shown in
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
by successive projection from the
canonical curve. To show that
\cal{H}_{16,12,5}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{16,12,5}

the previous case
\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{16,13,5}
.
Assume the existence of a component
\cal{H}\subset\cal{H}_{16,12,5}
consisting of non linearly normal curves. Choose general

C\in\cal{H}
and set
\beta:=\dim|\cal{O}_{C}(1)|\geq 6
,
\cal{E}=|K_{C}(-1)|=g^{\beta-5}_{6}.

We have

by Clifford’s theorem and since
C
is non-hyperelliptic.

(ii-A)

If
\beta-5=2
,
\cal{E}
is not birationally very ample since
\pi(6,2)=10<g
. Thus
\cal{E}
is compounded,

C
is trigonal and
\cal{E}=2g^{1}_{3}
. Note that
|K_{C}(-2g^{1}_{3})|
is very ample by Lemma
2.7
. The family
\cal{F}_{3}\subset\cal{G}^{5}_{16}
consisting of very ample

’s which are general subseries
of
|K_{C}(-2g^{1}_{3})|
on trigonal curves has

\dim\cal{F}_{3}=\dim\cal{M}^{1}_{g,3}+\dim\mathbb{G}(5,7)=37<\lambda(16,12,5)=39,

and hence does not contribute to a full component.

(ii-B)

If
\beta-5=1
,
|K_{C}(-1)|=g^{1}_{6}
. We recall a well-known fact
[
6

that
the Clifford index
c
of a general

-gonal curve
is computed by the unique pencil
g^{1}_{k}
and there is no
g^{r}_{d}

C

\dim|g^{1}_{6}+p+q|=1
for any

\cal{F}_{6}

has

\dim\cal{F}_{6}=\dim\cal{M}^{1}_{g,6}+\dim\mathbb{G}(5,6)=2g+13=37<\lambda(16,12,5)=39,

and hence does not constitute a full component.

Thus
\cal{H}_{16,12,5}=\mathcal{H}^{\mathcal{L}}_{16,12,5}
is irreducible and dominates
\cal{M}_{12}
.
A general
C\in\cal{H}_{16,12,5}
is
7=[\frac{g+3}{2}]
-gonal with
\dim W^{1}_{7}(C)=0
. Set
C^{r}_{d}(C)=\{D\in C_{d}:r(D)\geq r\}

g^{5}_{16}
on any curve

,

4.
Digression, remarks on
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
and
\cal{H}_{16,15,5}

We have not been able to determine the irreducibility of
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
(or
\cal{H}_{16,15,5}
). We even failed to locate any of its components. In this final section with loose end, we would like to list up some irreducible families
\cal{H}\subset\cal{H}_{d,g,r}
with
\dim\cal{H}\geq\cal{X}(d,g,r)
, which came out from our insufficient investigation on
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
and
\cal{H}_{16,15,5}
.

4.1.
Irreducible families in
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}

We only state intermediate results which may contribute toward a reasonable settlement for the study of
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
. We omit details.

Remark 4.1
.

It is easy to see that
\cal{H}_{16,16,5}\neq\emptyset
; cf. (ii) or (iv) below. Let
\cal{H}\subset\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
be a component.

(i)

There is no surface
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
with
\deg S=4
which contains a general element of
\cal{H}
.

(ii)

If
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a smooth del Pezzo, a smooth
C\subset S
with
(d,g)=(16,16)

is in
|(8;3,2^{2},1)|
, whose dual curve
C^{\vee}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
induced by
|K_{C}(-1)|
lies on a rational normal scroll
T\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
,
\deg C^{\vee}=13
,
|K_{C}(-1)|
has one base point.
C^{\vee}
is singular having two nodes with the strict transformation
\widetilde{C}^{\vee}\in|(8;3,2^{2})|
under the blow up

\mathbb{P}^{2}_{3}\xrightarrow{\ \ }T\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{1}
at the two nodes.
Furthermore,

C
is the image of the projection from
\widetilde{C}^{\vee}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{6}
with center of projection at the base point of
|K_{C}(-1)|
.

(iii)

The irreducible family of curves
\cal{H}_{1}\subset\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
lying on smooth del Pezzo surfaces has the expected dimension
\cal{X}(16,16,5)
.

(iv)

There is an irreducible family
\cal{H}_{2}\subset\cal{H}_{16,16,5}
consisting of curves which lie on a smooth surface
W\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{7}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\mathbb{P}^{5}
embedded by
|(4;2,1^{6})|
,
\deg W=6
. For a general
C\in\cal{H}_{2}
,

|K_{C}(-1)|
is base-point-free, birationally very ample and

is singular with
6
-nodes sitting on a rational normal scroll,
\widetilde{C}^{\vee}\in|(9;4,2^{6})|
,
\dim\cal{H}_{2}=\cal{X}(16,16,5)+1
.

(v)

However, it has not been possible to draw any meaningful conclusion from the above discussion. We may need to consider surfaces of another kind (probably of higher degrees) which
may contain a general
C\in\cal{H}
.

Hölder regularity for fully nonlinear nonlocal equations with gradient terms
J. Pablo Cabeza

Abstract
. In this paper we prove Hölder regularity results for viscosity solutions of fully nonlinear nonlocal uniformly elliptic second order differential equations with local gradient terms. This extends the nonlocal counterpart of the work of G. Barles, E. Chasseigne and C. Imbert in JEMS, 2011, to fully nonlinear extremal nonlocal operators.



1.
Introduction

In this chapter, we study interior Hölder regularity of viscosity solutions for nonlocal equations of the form,

(1.1)

\mathcal{F}u(x)+b(x)|Du|^{m}=f(x)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega,

where
u
is a real valued function,
Du
stands for the gradient of
u
, and
\mathcal{F}
is an integral extremal fully nonlinear operator of Pucci or Isaacs type. Also,
b\in C^{0,\tau}
with
\tau\in(0,1)
,
m\in(0,2]
, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain with
n\geq 1
.

We are interested in studying the regularity of viscosity solutions to equations of the form (
1.1
), under minor hypotheses about
b
and
f
.

Over the years, significant attention has been devoted to the study of integro-differential operators and fully nonlinear operators characterized by nonlinear growth in the gradient, largely attributed to their applications.

In general, two approaches are commonly used to study Hölder estimates for fully nonlinear equations. The first approach, based on the Harnack inequality, can be seen in the work of L. Caffarelli and L. Silvestre
[
4
]
and considers equations arising from stochastic control problems with Levy processes. The second approach is a method related to the theory of viscosity solutions introduced by Ishii & Lions
[
5
]
, initially designed to get comparison principles, that yields results for second-order nonlinear equations, possibly degenerate, but with continuous coefficients. Furthermore, Hölder estimates were established in
[
3
]
via probability techniques, and Silvestre in
[
8
]
proved Hölder continuity for a class of differential equations associated to jump processes, via growth lemmas.

As far as local drift terms are concerned, in the work
[
2
]
by G. Barles, E. Chasseigne, C. Imbert, mixed-type operators are studied, where the local part can be fully nonlinear, but the nonlocal part is linear. In this work, we will focus on exploring the nonlocal part of the operator, i.e., focusing on the nonlocal character of the equation. We will show that the methods used in
[
3
]
can be extended by combining the techniques of
[
1
]
to produce regularity results for Pucci operators.

We note that Hölder regularity provide a lot of applications, including regularity
C^{1,\alpha}
obtained by L. Caffareli & L. Silvestre in
[
4
]
and existence of eigenvalues for fully nonlinear integro-differential elliptic equations with a drift term
[
7
]
, just to mention a few. See also the parabolic counterparts with gradient growth equal to one in
[
9
]
.

Our main result reads as follows

Theorem 1
.

Let
u\in C\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
be a viscosity solution of the equation (
1.1
), assuming that
s\in(0,1)
,
b\in C^{0,\tau}\left(\overline{\Omega}\right)
with
\tau\in[0,1)
and
f\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)
. If
m\in(0,1)
, then
u
is
\alpha-
Hölder continuous with exponent

\alpha\leq\frac{2s-m+\tau}{1-m}.

If
m\in(1,2)
, then
u
is
\alpha-
Hölder continuous with exponent

\alpha\leq\frac{2s-m+1}{2-m}.

If
m=2
, then
u
is
\alpha-
Hölder continuos for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Remark 1
.

If
m\in(0,1]
the conclusion of the theorem holds for any
s\geq 1/2
with the only assumption of continuity over
b
; whereas it holds for any
s
if we assume that
b
is
C^{0,\tau}
with
\tau\geq 1-2s
. If
m\in(1,2)
the conclusion of the theorem holds for any
s\geq 1/2
with merely continuity over
b
; whereas it holds for any
s
if we assume that
b
is
C^{0,\tau}
with
\tau\geq 1-2s
. Note that if
m=2
, the conclusion holds for any
s\in(0,1)
with the only assumption of the continuity over
b
.

The rest of the paper follows this structure: the second section presents assumptions about the operators under study and definitions. The third section is dedicated to proving the main theorem in which we make use of the Ishii-Lion’s method.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will provide the basic concepts and assumptions used in this paper. First, we present the definition of the operators we will work in this article, followed by the definition of viscosity solution.

A linear nonlocal uniformly elliptic operator is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle L_{K}u(x)

\displaystyle=\text{P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x+z)-u(x))K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{B_{\epsilon}^{c}(x)}(u(x+z)-u(x))K(z)\mathrm{d}z

where
P.V.
denotes the principal value, and the kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfies:

(K.1)

is nonnegative, symmetric and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\min\{|z|^{2},1\}K(z)\mathrm{d}z<\infty;

(K.2)

for some
s\in(0,1)
and ellipticity constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
we have

\dfrac{\lambda}{|z|^{n+2s}}\leq K(z)\leq\dfrac{\Lambda}{|z|^{n+2s}}.

The most classical example of a nonlocal linear uniformly elliptic operator is provided by the fractional Laplacian by considering a kernel of the form
|z|^{-n-2s}
, that is,

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=C_{n,s}\text{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\hskip 10.00002pt,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
C_{n,s}>0
is a normalization constant. We observe that, via

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{u(x+z)-u(x)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{u(x-z)-u(x)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}z,

so the fractional Laplacian can be also written as

-(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=\frac{1}{2}C_{n,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{u(x+z)+u(x-z)-2u(x)}{|z|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}z.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{0}
be the class of all linear uniformly elliptic operators. In other words, we say that
L\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
if the operator
L
has the form (
2.1
), under the same above assumptions about
(\text{K.1})
and
(\text{K.2})
over
K
. We denote the nonlocal Pucci operators as

\mathcal{M}^{+}u(x)=\sup_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}Lu(x)\hskip 10.00002pt,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathcal{M}^{-}u(x)=\inf_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}Lu(x)

i.e., the maximum and minimum operators for the class
\mathcal{L}_{0}
, respectively. It is noteworthy that,
\mathcal{L}_{0}
,
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-}
depend on the parameters
\Lambda
,
\lambda
and
s
, in particular

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{+}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{S_{+}(u(x+z)+u(x-z)-2u(x))}{|z|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}z,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{S_{-}(u(x+z)+u(x-z)-2u(x))}{|z|^{n+2s}}\mathrm{d}z,

where
S_{+}(t)=\Lambda t^{+}-\lambda t^{-}
and
S_{-}(t)=\lambda t^{+}-\Lambda t^{-}
. Here,
t^{+}
and
t^{-}
are defined as
t^{+}=\max\{t,0\}
and
t^{-}=\max\{-t,0\}
, respectively.

Throughout this paper, we will fix the maximal Pucci extremal operator of the form

\mathcal{M}^{+}u(x)=\sup_{L\in\mathcal{L}_{0}}Lu(x)

where the operator
L
is of the form,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(u(x+z)-u(x))K(z)\mathrm{d}z,

and the kernel
K:\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
represents the frequency of jumps in every direction
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfies some conditions.

In proving Hölder regularity for the main problem, a suitable notion of a viscosity solution has to be considered. We recall the definition of viscosity solution for (
1.1
) as in
[
2
, Definition 2.1]
.

Definition 1
.

We say that
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity subsolution to (
1.1
) if, for any test function
\varphi\in C^{2}\left(B_{\delta}(x_{0})\right)
such that
u-\varphi
attains its maximum at
x_{0}\in\Omega
for some
\delta>0
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,\varphi,x_{0},\delta)+b(x_{0})|D\varphi(x_{0})|^{m}\geq f(x_{0})\text{ in }\Omega

where the operator
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is given by

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,\varphi,x,\delta)=\sup_{K}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{\delta}}(u(x+z)-u(z))K(z)\mathrm{d}z\right.\\
+\left.\int_{B_{\delta}}(\varphi(x+z)-\varphi(z))K(z)\mathrm{d}z\right\}.

In a similar manner, we say that
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity supersolution to (
1.1
) if, for any test function
\varphi\in C^{2}\left(B_{\delta}(x_{0})\right)
such that
u-\varphi
attains its minimum at
x_{0}\in\Omega
for some
\delta>0
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,\varphi,x_{0},\delta)+b(x_{0})|D\varphi(x_{0})|^{m}\leq f(x_{0})\text{ in }\Omega

A continous function
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) if it is both a viscosity subsolution and supersolution.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1

This section is devoted to the proof of the main theorem of this paper. For the proof, we will use the method introduced by Ishii-Lions
[
5
]
. Following this method, we fix
x_{0}\in\Omega
and introduce the function
\displaystyle\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\Phi(x,y):=u(x)-u(y)-\phi(x-y)-\Gamma(x),

where
\phi
is a radial function and
\Gamma(x)=L_{2}|x-x_{0}|^{2}
is a localized function around
x_{0}\in\Omega
. Since our goal is to attain Hölder regularity, we will choose the following function
\phi(t)=L_{1}|t|^{\alpha}
,
L_{1}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Our aim is to show that

(3.1)

\Phi(x,y)\leq 0.

The first observation to note is that if
\Phi
is nonpositive, then under suitable control conditions over
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
, and
\alpha
, we obtain the desired regularity result

u(x)-u(y)\leq L_{1}|x-y|^{\alpha}.

To prove (
3.1
), we will demonstrate that the maximum is only achieved when
x=y=x_{0}
. To establish this, from the continuity of the function
\Phi
, we observe that it attains a maximum point
(\overline{x},\overline{y})\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}
.

Suppose by contradiction that
\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})>0
.
As a consequence of this, it follows that,

(1)

\overline{x}\neq\overline{y}
, since otherwise
\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})=-\Gamma(\overline{x})<0
;

(2)

L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha},L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|^{2}\leq u(\overline{x})-u(\overline{y})\leq 2\|u\|_{\infty};

(3)

0<u(\overline{x})-u(\overline{y})
, this implies that
u(\overline{y})<u(\overline{x})
;

In particular, if
L_{2}\geq 8\|u\|_{\infty}[d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)]^{-2}
, we obtain

|\overline{x}-x_{0}|^{2}\leq 2\frac{\|u\|_{\infty}}{L_{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}[d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)]^{2}.

From this it follows that
|\overline{x}-x_{0}|\leq d(x_{0},\partial\Omega)/2
. Therefore, if
L_{1}
is sufficiently large, such that

\left(2\frac{\|u\|_{\infty}}{L_{1}}\right)^{1/\alpha}<\frac{1}{2}d(x_{0},\partial\Omega),

we can see that
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\Omega
.

On the other hand, we have that
\Phi_{\overline{y}}(x)=u(x)-\varphi(x,\overline{y})
reaches a maximum in
B_{\delta}(\overline{x})
at the point
\overline{x}
, where
\varphi(x,y)=\phi(x-y)+\Gamma(x)
. Then
\varphi\in C^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
is a test function in the definition of
u
as a viscosity subsolution of (
1.1
), so it follows

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,\varphi,\overline{x},\delta)+b(\overline{x})|D_{x}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}\geq f(\overline{x}).

Similarly,
\Phi_{\overline{x}}(y)=u(y)+\varphi(\overline{x},y)
reaches a minimum in
B_{\delta}({\overline{y}})
at the point
\overline{y}
; we obtain the viscosity supersolution inequality,

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,-\varphi,\overline{y},\delta)+b(\overline{y})|-D_{y}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}\leq f(\overline{y}).

Combining the previous viscosity inequalities, we obtain the following expression that accounts for both local and nonlocal terms,

(3.2)

\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,\varphi,\overline{x},\delta)-\mathcal{M}^{+}(u,-\varphi,\overline{y},\delta)\\
+b(\overline{x})|D_{x}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}-b(\overline{y})|D_{y}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}-f(\overline{x})+f(\overline{y})\geq 0.

We need to establish that the right-hand side of the above inequality is negative. To this end, we proceed to estimate both local and nonlocal terms. We denote the local terms in (
3.2
) by

\displaystyle T_{l}:=(b(\overline{x})-b(\overline{y}))|D_{x}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+b(\overline{y})(|D_{x}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m}-|D_{y}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})|^{m})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt-(f(\overline{x})+f(\overline{y})),

where the derivatives for the function
\varphi(x,y)=\phi(x-y)+\Gamma(x)
are given by

\displaystyle D_{x}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-2}(\overline{x}-\overline{y})+2L_{2}(\overline{x}-x_{0}),

\displaystyle D_{y}\varphi(\overline{x},\overline{y})

\displaystyle=-\alpha L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-2}(\overline{x}-\overline{y}).

To estimate the local terms in inequality (
3.2
), we must distinguish between two cases for the exponent
m
, in addition to making use of the fact that
b
belongs to the Hölder space
C^{0,\tau}
, so we can find a constant
C_{b}>0
such that
|b(\overline{x})-b(\overline{y})|\leq C_{b}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\tau}
. When
\tau=0
we only need to use that
b
is bounded.

First, let us note that if
m\in(0,1]
, then
|p+q|^{m}-|p|^{m}\leq|q|^{m}
. In this case, it follows from (
3.3
)

\displaystyle T_{l}\leq C_{b}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\tau}\left[(\alpha L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1})^{m}+(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m}\|b\|_{\infty}+2\|f\|_{\infty}.

On the other hand, if
m\geq 1
we know that for
|p|,|q|\geq 0
,

(|p|+|q|)^{m}\leqslant 2^{m-1}\max\{|p|^{m},|q|^{m}\}\leqslant 2^{m-1}(|p|^{m}+|q|^{m}).

Furthermore, the function
t\to|t|^{m}
is convex for
t\in(0,+\infty)
, this implies
|p+q|^{m}\leq|p|^{m}+m|p+q|^{m-2}\langle p+q,q\rangle
. Using the above inequality, it follows that

|p+q|^{m}-|p|^{m}\leqslant m2^{m-1}(|p|^{m-1}+|q|^{m-1})|q|.

In this case, of (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle T_{l}

\displaystyle\leq C_{b}2^{m-1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\tau}\bigg[\Big(\alpha L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}\Big)^{m}+\Big(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|\Big)^{m}\bigg]

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+m2^{m}L_{2}\bigg[\big(\alpha L_{1}|\overline{x}-\overline{y}|^{\alpha-1}\big)^{m-1}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt+\big(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|\big)^{m-1}\bigg]|\overline{x}-x_{0}|\|b\|_{\infty}+2\|f\|_{\infty}.

Given these inequalities, obtained for the local term when
m\in(0,1]
and
m>1
, note that
\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y})>0
implies that
|\overline{x}-x_{0}|<2\|u\|_{\infty}
, therefore, we can choose
L_{1}
to be sufficiently large in order to control the terms that depend on
\alpha
,
L_{2}
,
\|b\|_{\infty}
,
\|f\|_{\infty}
.

Now we need to estimate the remaining terms of (
3.2
), which involve the nonlocal terms. Following
[
1
]
, for given
\epsilon>0
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
fixed, we have

\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{x})\leq L_{K}u(\overline{x})+\epsilon

for some
L_{K}\in\mathcal{L}_{0}
. Indeed, this follows from the definition of supremum, since
\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{x})-\epsilon
is not an upper bound for the set
\{L_{K}u(\overline{x})\}
, since it is less than
\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{x})=\sup L_{K}u(\overline{x})
. Therefore, we can assert that there exists a kernel
K
such that

\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{x})\leq L_{K}u(\overline{x})+\epsilon\hskip 20.00003pt\text{ and }\hskip 20.00003pt\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{y})\geq L_{K}u(\overline{y}).

In other words, we only need to consider such a linear operator in the rest of the argument,

L_{K}u(\overline{x})-L_{K}u(\overline{y})+\epsilon\geq\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{x})-\mathcal{M}^{+}u(\overline{y}),

where the operators
L_{K}
are of the form (
2.1
).

Given that these integrals may be singular at the origin, we will partitionate the domain of integration into three parts:
A_{1}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}
, which relates to the nonlocal term
T^{1}_{nl}
;
A_{2}:=\{z\in B_{\delta}:(1-\eta)|z||\overline{x}-\overline{y}|\leq|\langle z,\overline{x}-\overline{y}\rangle|\}
, with
\eta\in(0,1)
small enough, is related to the nonlocal term
T^{2}_{nl}
; and
A_{3}:=B_{1}\backslash A_{2}
, which relates to the nonlocal term
T^{3}_{nl}
, so that

L_{K}u(\overline{x})-L_{K}u(\overline{y})=T^{1}_{nl}+T^{2}_{nl}+T^{3}_{nl},

where the nonlocal terms for
i\in\{1,2,3\}
are of the form,

T^{i}_{nl}(\overline{x},\overline{y})=\int_{A_{i}}(u(\overline{x}+z)-u(\overline{x}))K(z)\mathrm{d}z-\int_{A_{i}}(u(\overline{y}+z)-u(\overline{y}))K(z)\mathrm{d}z,

where
A_{i}
with
i\in\{1,2,3\}
describe each of the preceding domains. To avoid overloading the notation, we will denote
a:=\overline{x}-\overline{y}
.

For the initial term
T^{1}_{nl}
, there is no indeterminacy away from the origin. Consequently, we deduce that

(3.4)

T^{1}_{nl}\leq 4\|u\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{1}}K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq C_{1}.

where

C_{1}:=\Lambda\frac{2\sigma(S_{n-1})}{s}\|u\|_{\infty},

with
\Lambda>0
the ellipticity constant and
\sigma(S_{n-1})
the surface area of the sphere of dimension
n-1
.

Before proceeding with the estimation to evaluate
T^{2}_{nl}
, let us note that since
(\overline{x},\overline{y})
is the maximum point for
\Phi
, it follows that for any
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\Phi(\overline{x}+d,\overline{y}+d^{\prime})\leq\Phi(\overline{x},\overline{y}).

More precisely, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(u(\overline{x}+d)-u(\overline{x})\right)-

\displaystyle\left(u(\overline{y}+d^{\prime})-u(\overline{y})\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\phi(a+(d-d^{\prime}))-\phi(a)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\Gamma(\overline{x}+d)-\Gamma(\overline{x}).

Applying this inequality, first with
d=z,d^{\prime}=0
and then with
d=0,d^{\prime}=z
, we obtain

\displaystyle T^{2}_{nl}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{2}}\left(\phi(a+z)-\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{A_{2}}\left(\phi(a-z)-\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Now, applying Taylor’s formula with an integral remainder (for instance Theorem 9.9 in
[
6
]
) to the function
\phi(a+z)

\phi(a+z)=\phi(a)+D\phi(a)\cdot z+\int_{0}^{1}(1-s)\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\rangle\mathrm{d}s.

Analogously, we can derive a similar expression for
\phi(a-z)
,

\phi(a-z)=\phi(a)-D\phi(a)\cdot z+\int_{-1}^{0}(1+s)\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\rangle\mathrm{d}s,

adding these two equations, we observe that the linear terms in
z
cancel, leaving us with the following result

\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-2\phi(a)=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|s|)\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)z,z\rangle\mathrm{d}s.

Observe that the derivative of the function
\phi(t)=L_{1}|t|^{\alpha}
is given by

D\phi(a)=\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}a.

For the second derivative, denoting by
\widehat{a}=a/|a|
,

\displaystyle D^{2}\phi(a)

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}\left((\alpha-2)\frac{a}{|a|}\otimes\frac{a}{|a|}+I_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}\left((\alpha-2)\widehat{a}\otimes\widehat{a}+I_{n}\right).

In particular,

\displaystyle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z=\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-2}\left((\alpha-2)\widehat{(a+sz)}\otimes\widehat{(a+sz)}\cdot z+zI_{n}\right).

As we seek to estimate the Taylor expansion, we observe that

\displaystyle\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-2}\left((\alpha-2)\widehat{(a+sz)}\otimes\widehat{(a+sz)}\cdot z+zI_{n}\right)\cdot z

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-2}\left(|z|^{2}+(\alpha-2)|\langle\widehat{a+sz},z\rangle|^{2}\right).

Note that within the set
A_{2}
, which includes
z\in B_{\delta}
, the following bounds are established with
\delta=|a|\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{0}\in(0,1)

(3.7)

\displaystyle|a+sz|

\displaystyle\leq|a|+|s||z|\leq|a|+\delta=|a|\left(1+\rho_{0}\right)

\displaystyle|a+sz|

\displaystyle\geq|a|-|s||z|\geq|a|-\delta=|a|\left(1-\rho_{0}\right)

\displaystyle|(a+sz)\cdot z|

\displaystyle=|a\cdot z+s|z|^{2}|\geq|a\cdot z|-\delta|z|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\geq(1-\eta)|a||z|-\rho_{0}|a||z|

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=\left(1-\eta-\rho_{0}\right)|a||z|.

Through the preceding inequalities and the Taylor expansion (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-4}\left(|a+sz|^{2}|z|^{2}+(\alpha-2)|(a+sz)\cdot z|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-4}\left(|a+sz|^{2}+(\alpha-2)\left(1-\eta-\rho_{0}\right)^{2}|a|^{2}\right)|z|^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}(1+\rho_{0})^{\alpha-4}\left((1+\rho_{0})^{2}-(2-\alpha)(1-\eta-\rho_{0})^{2}\right)|z|^{2}.

From this estimate, we define

C_{2}:=(1+\rho_{0})^{\alpha-4}\left((2-\alpha)(1-\eta-\rho_{0})^{2}-(1+\rho_{0})^{2}\right)

then,

\int_{A_{2}}\left(\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-2\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq-C_{2}\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}\int_{A_{2}}|z|^{2}K(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Given
A_{2}\subset B_{1}
, we can observe that this set is indeed a cone, so if
z\in A_{2}
then
-z\in A_{2}
. Following the definition of
\Gamma
,

\int_{A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z=L_{2}\int_{A_{2}}(|z|^{2}+\langle\overline{x}-x_{0},z\rangle)K(z)\mathrm{d}z,

by using the symmetry of the kernel, we obtain

L_{2}\int_{A_{2}}(2\langle\overline{x}-x_{0},z\rangle)K(z)\mathrm{d}z=0,

that is,

\int_{A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z=L_{2}\int_{A_{2}}|z|^{2}K(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Given that we are in the
A_{2}
, we need to estimate the integral of the kernel. For this, note that

\displaystyle\int_{A_{2}}|z|^{2}K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq\sigma(S_{n-1})\Lambda\int_{0}^{\delta}r^{1-2s}\mathrm{d}r=\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2(1-s)}\delta^{2-2s}.

Hence, by defining this constant

C^{\prime}_{2}:=\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2-2s}\rho_{0}^{2-2s},

we can see that the second nonlocal term
T^{2}_{nl}
is bounded by

(3.8)

T^{2}_{nl}\leq-C_{2}C^{\prime}_{2}\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2s}+L_{2}C^{\prime}_{2}|a|^{2-2s}.

In order to estimate the final nonlocal term
T^{3}_{nl}
in
B_{1}\backslash A_{2}
, that is,

\displaystyle T^{3}_{nl}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\phi(a+z)-\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{B_{1}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\int_{B_{1}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\phi(a-z)-\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

we will write
B_{1}\backslash A_{2}=(B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta})\cup(B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2})
. In this way, in the annulus
B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}
, we can use a Taylor expansion for
\phi
, while in the domain
B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2}
, we utilize the concavity of the function
t^{\alpha}
for
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Let us consider
d\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then in
B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}

\displaystyle\phi(a+d)-\phi(a)

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}(|a|+|d|)^{\alpha}-L_{1}(|a|)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-1}|d|.

In this way, taking first
d=z
and then
d=-z
, we can conclude that in
B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}(\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-

\displaystyle 2\phi(a))K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-1}\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}|z|K(z)\mathrm{d}z.

Given the last integral with
s\in(0,1)
, we must separate the analysis in some cases. If
s\neq 1/2
, then

\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}|z|K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq\Lambda\sigma(S_{n-1})\int_{\delta}^{1}r^{-2s}\mathrm{d}r=\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{1-2s}\left(1-\delta^{1-2s}\right),

and we must again distinguish into two cases, as if
s<1/2

\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}|z|K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{1-2s}=\overline{C}_{3},

whereas if
s>1/2
with
\delta=|a|\rho_{0}

\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}|z|K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2s-1}\left(|a|\rho_{0}\right)^{1-2s}=|a|^{1-2s}\widetilde{C}_{3}.

If we define the following constant
C_{3}=\max\{\overline{C}_{3},\widetilde{C}_{3}\}
we obtain the following upper bound for
\phi
in
B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}
, when
s<1/2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}(\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-

\displaystyle 2\phi(a))K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq 2\alpha L_{1}C_{3}|a|^{\alpha-1},

and if
s>1/2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}(\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-

\displaystyle 2\phi(a))K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq 2\alpha L_{1}C_{3}|a|^{\alpha-2s}.

In the case
s=1/2
we get

\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}|z|K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq-\Lambda\sigma(S_{n-1})\ln(|a|\rho_{0}),

and if we define the constant

C^{\prime}_{3}:=\Lambda\sigma(S_{n-1})

we obtain the following upper bound,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-

\displaystyle 2\phi(a))K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq-2\alpha L_{1}C^{\prime}_{3}|a|^{\alpha-1}\ln(|a|\rho_{0}).

In
B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2}
we use a Taylor expansion, since
\alpha\in(0,1)
we see that

\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-2\phi(a)=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-|s|)\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)z,z\rangle\mathrm{d}s.

In particular,

\displaystyle\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-2}\left(|z|^{2}-(2-\alpha)|\langle\widehat{a+sz},z\rangle|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha L_{1}|a+sz|^{\alpha-2}|z|^{2}.

From (
3.7
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\langle D^{2}\phi(a+sz)\cdot z,z\rangle\leq\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2}(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}|z|^{2},

since the kernel
K
is bounded by constant
\Lambda>0
, the estimate of the integral over
B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2}}\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s-2}}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\sigma(S_{n-1})\int_{0}^{\delta}\frac{1}{r^{2s-1}}\mathrm{d}r=\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2(1-s)}\delta^{2-2s}.

Now, if we define

C_{4}:=\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2(1-s)}\rho_{0}^{2-2s},

we obtain the following bound for
\phi
,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\delta}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\phi(a+z)+\phi(a-z)-2\phi(a)\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq\alpha(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}L_{1}C_{4}|a|^{\alpha-2s}.

Finally, for the integral involving
\Gamma
, we can observe that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{1}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq L_{2}\int_{B_{1}}|z|^{2}K(z)\mathrm{d}z.

In a similar manner to the previous kernel estimation,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}\frac{1}{|z|^{n+2s-2}}\mathrm{d}z\leq\Lambda\frac{\sigma(S_{n-1})}{2(1-s)}=:C^{\prime}_{4},

we can conclude that the integral of
\Gamma
over
B_{1}\backslash A_{2}
is bounded by

\int_{B_{1}\backslash A_{2}}\left(\Gamma(\overline{x}+z)-\Gamma(\overline{x})\right)K(z)\mathrm{d}z\leq L_{2}C^{\prime}_{4}.

In conclusion, when combining all terms related to the nonlocal term
T^{3}_{nl}
, we must take into account the possible values of
s\in(0,1)
. If
s<1/2
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle T^{3}_{nl}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha L_{1}C_{3}|a|^{\alpha-1}+\alpha(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}L_{1}C_{4}|a|^{\alpha-2s}+L_{2}C^{\prime}_{4},

if
s=1/2
,

\displaystyle T^{3}_{nl}

\displaystyle\leq-2\alpha L_{1}\ln(|a|\rho_{0})C^{\prime}_{3}|a|^{\alpha-1}+\alpha(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}L_{1}C_{4}|a|^{\alpha-1}+L_{2}C^{\prime}_{4}.

and if
s>1/2

(3.10)

\displaystyle T^{3}_{nl}

\displaystyle\leq 2\alpha L_{1}C_{3}|a|^{\alpha-2s}+\alpha(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}L_{1}C_{4}|a|^{\alpha-2s}+L_{2}C^{\prime}_{4},

In the case
s=1/2
, we observe that
\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\ln(|r|)r^{\alpha}\to 0
. Therefore, we also obtain powers of
|a|
of the form
\alpha-2s
. Thus, (
3.10
) remains valid in this case as well.

3.1.
Estimates for local and nonlocal terms

We can now proceed to a joint analysis of the local and nonlocal contributions appearing in equation (
3.2
). To this end, we begin by considering
s\geq 1/2
and examine the result of combining the local terms (
3.3
) and the nonlocal terms (
3.4
), (
3.8
), (
3.10
), for both cases
m\in(0,1]
and
m\in(1,2]
.

For
m\in(0,1]
, the following estimate is obtained from inequality (
3.2
),

(3.11)

\begin{split}0&\leq\nu_{1}\big(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}\big)\\
&\qquad-\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2s}\Big(C_{2}C_{2}^{\prime}-(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}C_{4}-C_{b}(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-1}|a|^{\tau+2s-m}-2C_{3}|a|^{2s-1}\Big),\end{split}

where

\nu_{1}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}):=\begin{aligned} &2\|f\|_{\infty}+C_{b}|a|^{\tau}(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m}+\|b\|_{\infty}(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m}\\
&\quad+C_{1}\|u\|_{\infty}+L_{2}C_{2}^{\prime}|a|^{2-2s}+L_{2}C_{4}^{\prime}+\epsilon.\end{aligned}

The term
\nu_{1}
gathers all contributions that are negligible for small
|a|
or remain bounded independently of
L_{1}
. In order to conclude, it is sufficient to ensure that

(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-1}|a|^{\tau+(2s-m)}=o(1),

which holds if
\tau+2s-m+\alpha(m-1)\geq 0
and
m\in(0,1)
, that is,

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\frac{\tau-m+2s}{1-m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ if }m\in(0,1)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\tau+2s-1\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ if }m=1.

We choose
L_{1}
sufficiently large such that
L_{1}>2^{1+\alpha}d(x_{0},\Omega)^{-\alpha}\|u\|_{\infty}
and

L_{1}\geq\frac{\nu_{1}\big(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}\big)}{(C_{2}C^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}-(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}C_{4})\alpha}.

Then, based on the condition
L_{1}|a|^{\alpha}\leq 2\|u\|_{\infty}
, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, we deduce that
|a|^{\alpha-2s}>1
since these terms tend to
0
as
\overline{x}
approaches
\overline{y}
. It follows from
(
3.11
) that

0\leq\nu_{1}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},f)(1-|a|^{\alpha-2s})<0,

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
\Phi(x,y)\leq 0
.

Furthermore, in the case when
m=1
, if
s\geq 1/2
then the conclusion holds for any
\tau\geq 0
, that is,
b
it can be only a continuos function. Now, it follows for
m\in(0,1)
that

\alpha\leq\frac{\tau+2s-1}{1-m}.

When considering the case of
m\in(1,2]
, the following estimate is obtained from inequality (
3.2
),

(3.12)

\begin{split}0\leq{}&\;\nu_{2}\big(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}\big)\\
&\quad-\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2s}\Bigg(C_{2}C_{2}^{\prime}-(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}C_{4}-C_{b}(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-1}|a|^{\tau+2s-m}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-2C_{3}|a|^{2s-1}-m2^{m}\|b\|_{\infty}L_{2}(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-2}|a|^{2s+1-m}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|\Bigg),\end{split}

where

\nu_{2}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty}):=\begin{aligned} &2\|f\|_{\infty}+C_{b}2^{m-1}|a|^{\tau}\big[(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-1})^{m}+(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m}\big]\\
&\qquad+m2^{m}\|b\|_{\infty}L_{2}(2L_{2}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|)^{m-1}|\overline{x}-x_{0}|+C_{1}\|u\|_{\infty}\\
&\qquad\qquad+L_{2}C_{2}^{\prime}|a|^{2-2s}+L_{2}C_{4}^{\prime}+\epsilon.\end{aligned}

Similarly to the previous case, we need to ensure that

|a|^{\tau}(L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{(m-1)}|a|^{2s-m}=o(1)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt|a|(L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-2}|a|^{2s-m}=o(1),

which holds provided that
\tau+\alpha(m-1)+2s-m\geq 0
and

, from where we obtain

\alpha\leq\frac{1-m+2s}{2-m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ if }m\in(1,2)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }\hskip 10.00002pt\tau+\alpha+2(s-1)\geq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ if }m=2.

We choose
L_{1}>0
large enough to control the terms of
\nu_{2}
,

L_{1}\geq\frac{\nu_{2}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty})}{(C_{2}C^{\prime}_{2}-(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}C_{4})\alpha}.

Since
\alpha\in(0,2s)
, we have
|a|^{\alpha-2s}>1
. It follows that

0\leq\nu_{2}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty})(1-|a|^{\alpha-2s})<0.

This yields the desired contradiction.

Furthermore, in the case when
m=2
, from (
3.12
), we impose that
\tau-\alpha+2s\geq 0
and from the restriction
\tau+\alpha+2(s-1)\geq 0
, we obtain that
\tau\geq 1-2s
, however we know that
1-2s\leq 0
, so the conclusion holds for any
\tau\geq 0
, that is,
b
it can be only a continuous function.

Now we see what happens when
s<1/2

3.3
) and the nonlocal terms (

\begin{split}0\leq{}&\nu_{3}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty})\\
&\quad-\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha-2s}\Big(C_{2}C^{{}^{\prime}}_{2}-(1-\rho_{0})^{\alpha-2}C_{4}-C_{b}|a|^{\tau}(\alpha L_{1}|a|^{\alpha})^{m-1}|a|^{2s-m}-2C_{3}|a|^{2s-1}\Big),\end{split}

where, analogously, we have grouped all terms that are bounded or depend on
L_{2}
into
\nu_{3}
.
In order to conclude, it is sufficient to establish that

that is,

\alpha\leq\frac{\tau-m+2s}{1-m}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ if }m\in(0,1)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ and }s<\frac{1}{2},

while if

(3.14)

\begin{split}0&\leq{}\nu_{4}(L_{2},\|b\|_{\infty},\|u\|_{\infty},\|f\|_{\infty},|a|)\\

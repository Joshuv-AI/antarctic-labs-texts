First report on electrochemical sensing of ulipristal acetate in pharmaceutical formulations and biological fluids using reduced graphene oxide–CuO nanohybrid based sensors
Authors: Yasmeen A. S. Hameed, Ahmed Z. M. Al-Bassam, Omer A. Azher, Moneer A. Alrashidi, Kaseb D. Alanazi, Razan M. Snari, Ahmed M. Hameed, Nashwa M. El-Metwaly
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study illustrated for the first time the construction, characterization, and electroanalytical validation of reduced graphene oxide-copper oxide nanoparticles based screen-printed carbon electrodes (rGO-CuONPs-SPCEs) for sensitive differential pulse voltammetric quantification of ulipristal acetate (ULP) in pharmaceutical and biological samples. The rGO/CuONPs ratio within the nanohybrid was optimized with comprehensive characterization of the morphology and the electroanalytical features of the electrode surface. The fabricated sensors with the optimal composition exhibited enhanced performance towards the oxidation of ULP molecule at 0.72 V with a diffusion-controlled mechanism. Deep and comprehensive molecular orbital and electroanalytical studies sustained oxidation of the terminal nitrogen atom (N2) with the transfer of two electrons/one proton. The illustrated calibration graphs were linear within the ULP concentration ranged from 0.010 to 5.04 µgmL−1, with LOD value of 0.003 µgmL−1. The developed sensors showed prolonged lifetime, high mechanical stability, and measurement reproducibility. The presented voltammetric procedure has been introduced for sensitive monitoring of ULP residues in biological and pharmaceutical samples. Introduction

Ulipristal acetate (ULP), a selective progesterone receptor modulator drug, acts as an inhibitor for ovulation [1]. ULP is an effective oral medication approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as an emergency contraceptive. It is used to prevent pregnancy due to missed or failed contraception, lowering the incidence of abortion [2, 3]. In addition, it is approved for uterine fibroid treatment by the European Commission [4]. It is also being investigated for its potential to treat ovarian and breast cancer [5]. ULP has a steroidal structure with the chemical formula [17α-acetoxy-11β-(4-N, N-dimethyl amino-phenyl)-19-norpregna-4,9-diene-3,20-dione]. Side effects such as menstrual disorder, abdominal pain, nausea, and headache are reported [6]. ULP is not recommended for daily use as a birth control, and there is ongoing research about the drug’s safety profile for the management of uterine fibroids [7]. Therefore, for enhanced safety tracking, analytical methods are important to measure the concentration of ULP and to evaluate its pharmacodynamic profile.

Analytical approaches for monitoring of pharmaceutical residues in biological complex matrices are mainly chromatography and mass spectroscopy. Researchers evaluating ulipristal acetate show different detection limits, including 0.075 µgmL−1 by reverse phase high performance liquid chromatography (RP-HPLC), 9.6 ng/spot by high performance thin layer chromatography (RP-HPTLC), 0.025 ngmL−1 by liquid chromatography tandem mass spectroscopy (LC–MS/MS), 0.0062 µgmL−1 by UV spectrometric methods, and 0.0052 µgmL−1 by fluorescence sensors [8–10]. While highly sensitive, advanced analytical chromatographic methods suffer high cost, reliance on skilled operators, lengthy analysis times, high consumption of organic solvents, and operation complexity due to necessary preconcentration steps when analyzing real biological samples [11]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for rapid, reliable, and sensitive analytical methods suitable for ULP point-of-care (POC) continuous assessment and capable of accurately analyzing samples from both biological fluids and pharmaceutical formulations.

Recent trends in pharmaceutical analysis favor simplified and miniaturized methodologies. To overcome the existing challenges in conventional pharmaceutical analysis, recent research has focused on developing miniaturized electrochemical sensors. These sensors offer numerous advantages, including reduced sample and reagent volume, rapid analysis time, and suitability for automated processes [12–15]. Electrochemical sensor-based methods are considered promising alternatives to the aforementioned conventional techniques due to their high sensitivity and additional benefits such as portability, cost-effectiveness, rapid response, convenience, and ease of online monitoring. Selectivity can be readily adjusted through modification of the electrode surface with appropriate nanomaterials to selectively recognize the desired target analyte. For example, Liu et al. developed a highly sensitive electrochemical aptasensor that utilizes gold nanoparticles and aptamer recognition molecules, capable of monitoring broad-spectrum estrogen molecules in human serum [16].

Disposable screen-printed carbon electrodes (SPCEs) are increasingly utilized due to their excellent uniformity, high reproducibility, and suitability for mass production. Their disposable nature makes them reliable sensors for real-time and on-site drug analysis [17–19].A wide range of electroactive nanomaterials can be applied to the electrode surface to enhance sensing performance and enable the determination of diverse compounds. Reduced Graphene Oxide (rGO) is a valuable carbonaceous nanostructure that can be applied to the surface of SPCEs, imparting on them properties such as high surface area, mechanical strength, favorable optical, chemical, electrical, and thermal stability, along with being low-cost and biocompatible [20–22]. In addition, its stable and high surface area facilitates the high loading and immobilization of nanoparticles and electrocatalytic compounds [22]. Integration of nanoparticles can enhance the sensitivity and selectivity of electrochemical sensors, consequently improving their limits of detection (LOD) [23]. Notably, rGO features numerous oxygen functional groups, which impart hydrophobicity and negative charge densities, allowing the efficient binding of various nanoparticles, such as copper oxide nanoparticles (CuONPs) [24–27]. Recent advancements were focused on graphene-supported nanohybrid, particularly those integrated with metal oxide nanostructures [28, 29]. Functionalized graphene-metal oxide nanohybrid represents a compelling sensing platform for advancing electrochemical sensors, leveraging the synergistic effects of metal oxide nanostructure and graphene to achieve enhanced functionalities critical for diverse technological applications [29–34].

This study develops an electrochemical planner screen printed sensors enriched with a reduced graphene oxide-copper oxide nanoparticles nanohybrid to quantify trace amounts of ulipristal acetate (ULP) in the final pharmaceutical samples and biological fluids using differential pulse voltammetric (DPV) analysis. This detection method aims to monitor and prevent drug abuse in various clinical settings, making it well-suited for drug analysis required in biomedical, clinical, and environmental applications.

Experimental

Ulipristal authentic samples, chemicals, and reagents

Ulipristal acetate (ULP, C30H37NO4, molecular weight 475.62gmol-1, CAS 126784-99-4) pure standard was purchased from Sigma-Aldrich. To prepare the stock ULP solution, 0.025 g of the pure standard sample were dissolved in 5 mL methanol, sonicated for 30 min and the volume was completed to 25 mL with water. Graphene oxide (GO, Sigma), and copper oxide nanoparticles (CuONPs, < 50 nm, Alfa Aesar) were used as electrode modifiers. Ethyl cellulose (EC), graphite powder (2-µm, Aldrich), sodium dodecyl sulfate (SDS, Aldrich), dibutyl phthalate (DBP, Merck), and solvent-borne polyurethane resin (PU) were used to prepare the carbon-based printing ink. Britton-Robinson universal buffer solution was prepared by dissolving calculated boric acid, acetic acid, and phosphoric acid in water, where the pH was adjusted using NaOH solution. Biological fluids, free from ULP, were obtained from VACSERA (Giza, Egypt).

Instrumentation

Metrohm benchtop station (797 VA, Metrohm, Switzerland) accompanied by Pt wire auxiliary electrode, Ag/AgCl reference electrode, and the fabricated rGO-CuONPs-SPCEs was applied for cyclic and differential pulse voltammetric measurements. Electrochemical impedimetric spectroscopic measurements (EIS) were performed on CHI 660D electrochemical analyzer (CH Instrument Company, China). The morphological characterization of the fabricated electrodes was carried out onfield-emission scanning electron microscope (FE-SEM, Quanta FEG 250, Thermo Fisher Scientific/FEI), as detailed in the instrument documentation. Before imaging, the electrode surfaces were coated with a thin conductive gold layer using an Edwards S150A sputter coater to ensure charge dissipation in high-vacuum conditions. SEM micrographs were captured at different accelerating voltages to observe particle distribution, surface roughness, and the uniformity of CuONPs deposition on the carbon working electrode. Elemental analysis and confirmation of copper oxide loading were further conducted with an energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) detector (OCTANE PRO, EDAX/AMETEK) attached to the SEM chamber. The EDX spectra allowed for the identification of elemental composition and the mapping of Cu and O, confirming the successful functionalization of the electrode surface.

Construction of the working electrode

The nanohybrid-based sensors were fabricated as described in our previous publication with slight modification [34]. Graphene oxide (GO, with an initial concentration of 2 mgmL−1) was exfoliated in a binary aqueous-ethanol solvent mixture (volume ratio 1:1) by high-intensity probe sonication (power: 150 W; amplitude: 40%; 60 min) within an ice-water bath to reduce thermal-induced structural damage. GO was reduced to rGO chemically by dropwise addition of hydrazine monohydrate (25 µL; 80% aqueous concentration), followed by reflux heating at 95 °C for 120 min under continuous magnetic stirring in a sealed reaction vessel with a water-cooled condenser to avoid volatile losses. After the completion of the reduction process, the resulting black suspension was allowed to stand at ambient temperature, and centrifuged (8000 rpm, 10 min, three washing cycles with ethanol) to remove unreacted reagents and water-soluble by-products. Then, the isolated nanocomposite precipitate was redispersed in ethanol by brief sonication (5 min) to yield a final solid concentration of 5 mgmL−1. Meanwhile, the CuONPs were dispersed in an identical solvent system under identical sonication parameters. The two colloid suspensions were then mixed together and further sonicated for 30 min to achieve intimate mixing at the nanoscale and to prevent phase segregation.

The flexible substrate material chosen for electrode fabrication was transparent polyvinyl chloride film, 0.2 mm in thickness. Using a stainless-steel mesh template (200 µm thickness), the printing ink was deposited circular working electrode patterns (3 mm in diameter; geometric area: 0.071 cm2) on the cleaned PVC substrates. Immediately after pattern transfer, the mesh was removed, and stepwise thermal treatment under forced-air convection was used to achieve complete solvent removal. The fabricated electrodes were then stored under desiccated conditions at room temperature before surface functionalization. Aliquots (20 µL) of each formulated rGO-CuONPs nanohybrid were deposited onto the pre-fabricated electrode surfaces using calibrated micropipettes and allowed to dry yielding uniform modifier films, thereby giving a series of modified electrodes denoted as rGO(x%)-CuONPs-SPCEs, where x refers to the mass percent of rGO in the composite.

Method validation

The proposed voltammetric approach was validated by successive addition of known aliquots of the ULP standard solution to the measuring cell containing 10mL of BR universal buffer solution at pH 5. For each addition, differential pulse voltammogram (DPV) was recorded and the peak current (Ipa) against ULP concentration (µgmL−1). The electroanalytical parameters applied were: pulse width 100 ms; pulse amplitude 50 mV; pulse duration 40 ms; scan rate 60 mVs−1; voltage step 6 mV; and voltage step time 0.15 s.

Analysis of ULP samples

The applicability and suitability of the voltammetric method were tested by monitoring the ULP residues in the pharmaceutical tablets and biological samples compared with the chromatographic method [8]. Norpregna (5mg/tablet, Zeta Pharm, Nasr City, Cairo, Egypt) was purchased from local drug store. Two tablets were weighed, grinded to fine powder, and calculated amount equivalent to one tablet was dissolved in 25 mL of methanol. Sample solution was sonicated for 30 min, filtered through Whatman filter paper, and the volume was completed to 100 mL with distilled water. Suitable concentration of the sample solution were added to the measuring cell at pH 5, and the ULP was quantified under the optimized measuring conditions and calculated based on the linearity parameters of the standard curve. Three milliliters of the ULP free plasma sample were enriched with calculated concentrations of the ULP stock solution, mixed with 9.0 mL of acetonitrile, vortexed, and centrifuged to remove the sample protein. Known volumes of the clear enriched plasma sample solution were transferred to the measuring cell and assayed under the recommended procedures. The residual ULP concentration was quantified under the standard addition method to minimize the matrix effects and ensure the accuracy of method. Five milliliters of the blank urine sample were fortified with aliquots of the ULP solution and centrifuged to precipitate the particulate matter. The fortified sample was carefully filtered through a 0.45 μm syringe filter and analyzed as in the plasma samples.

Results and discussion

Electrode surface characterization

SEM analysis was carried out to examine the physical morphology and integrity of the rGO-CuONPs modified electrode surfaces to ensure even film thickness and the absence of micro-cracks (Fig. S1). The average thickness of the modifier films was found to be about 8 ± 1.5 µm, as revealed by cross-sectional SEM analysis and contact profilometry. The porous and hierarchical structure is ascribed to the synergistic combination of the two components, where the rGO nanosheets offer a high surface area scaffold with inherent porosity, and the CuONPs serve as spacers to ensure that the rGO layers do not irreversibly stack on top of each other. This synergistic combination ensures that the 3D network remains intact, which is in line with the reported morphologies of rGO-metal oxide nanocomposites, thus verifying the successful incorporation of the hybrid.

Energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (EDX) has been employed to confirm the successful deposition of copper oxide nanoparticles on the electrode surface by analyzing the elemental composition of the modified screen-printed electrode. The EDX spectrum is shown in Fig. S2. As observed in the spectrum, characteristic peaks for copper (Cu) and oxygen (O) indicate the presence of copper oxide nanomaterial. Quantitative EDX analysis reveals that carbon (C), originating from the screen-printed carbon substrate and the rGO in the deposited rGO-CuONPs nanohybrid, is the dominant element, accounting for approximately 80.0wt. %, consistent with the composition of the unmodified electrode (Table S3). Additionally, copper (Cu; 8.0wt. %) and oxygen (O; 12.0 wt. %) are present. The copper oxide loading levels are within an acceptable range to enhance electrochemical activity without compromising the electrode's mechanical strength or electrical conductivity. The lack of significant additional elemental signals indicates minimal impurities, confirming the high purity of the produced copper oxide nanoparticles. Overall, these results demonstrate that a uniform and well-controlled layer of CuONPs has been successfully deposited onto the SPE surface, producing a robust, high-performance working electrode suitable for sensing applications.

Electrochemical oxidation mechanism of ULP: Computational and voltammetric analysis

The electrooxidation of ULP at pH 5 was investigated using cyclic voltammetry coupled with molecular orbital calculations to elucidate the mechanism underlying the observed single anodic peak. Comprehensive analysis of MMFF94 and MOPAC charge distributions revealed that the dimethylamino nitrogen [N (2)] exhibits the highest negative charge density (− 0.2979 MOPAC, − 0.3093 MMFF94) among all heteroatoms in the molecule, including oxygen-containing functional groups, thereby establishing it as the primary oxidation site (Fig. S8, Table S9) [45]. This enhanced basicity and electron-donating capacity, attributed to the availability of a lone pair orbital on the secondary amine conjugated to the aromatic ring, accounts for the single electrochemical response observed in the voltammogram. The mechanism proceeds through a rate-determining two-electron oxidation involving electron transfer from the nitrogen lone pair to the electrode surface at pH 5, where the dimethylamino group exists in pH-dependent equilibrium between neutral and protonated forms. Formation of an iminium intermediate [R₂N⁺ = H] occurs via a transition state in which the nitrogen atom undergoes partial sp2 rehybridization, developing significant positive charge character (δ + ≈ 0.5–0.8) while maintaining connectivity to both methyl substituents. Stabilization of this transition state is facilitated by resonance delocalization of the developing positive charge into the conjugated aromatic benzene ring and extended steroid core structure, effectively distributing electrophilic character across the π-system and reducing the activation energy for electron removal. Subsequent deprotonation and hydrolytic rearrangement of the iminium species yield the oxidation products in aqueous solution. The absence of competing oxidation peaks from oxygen atoms and methylene carbons, despite their presence in the molecular structure, confirms that lone pair accessibility and orbital conjugation rather than functional group identity determine the electrochemical behavior, with the dimethylamino moiety representing a privileged site for electron abstraction. This single-peak response is therefore consistent with a unified oxidation pathway involving exclusive involvement of the nitrogen lone pair, with pH 5 providing optimal conditions for reproducible and kinetically well-defined electrochemistry through maintenance of the protonation equilibrium. The molecular orbital calculations thus provide compelling quantitative support for the structural basis of ulipristal acetate electrochemistry and validate the proposed two-electron oxidation mechanism involving iminium intermediacy and resonance-stabilized transition state geometry (Scheme 1).

Oxidation mechanism of Ulipristal molecule at rGO-CuONPs-SPCEs

Ulipristal undergoes an anodic oxidation through a nitrogen-centered ECE (electron-transfer–chemical–electron-transfer) mechanism in which the tertiary amine at N (2), identified by Gaussian calculations as hosting the HOMO and exhibiting the highest electron density, serves as the primary oxidation site (Fig. S10). The first electron-transfer step forms an N-centered radical cation, consistent with the experimentally observed anodic peak at + 0.67 V. Above pH 5, this radical cation rapidly undergoes a follow-up chemical step—such as deprotonation, α-carbon rearrangement, or N–C bond cleavage leading to iminium or demethylated intermediates—driven by the increased basicity and nucleophilicity of the nitrogen lone pair. These chemical transformations irreversibly convert the radical cation into electrochemically inactive products, accounting for the loss of the cathodic return wave at higher pH. In contrast, under more acidic conditions (pH < 5), protonation stabilizes the radical cation, suppressing the chemical step and enabling electron transfer in the reverse scan, producing the reversible or quasi-reversible couple near 0.66–0.67 V. The localization of the LUMO over the conjugated carbonyl–aryl framework further supports delocalization of charge in the oxidized species and explains the observed reduction peak near the same potential. Overall, the combined CV behavior, HOMO/LUMO distribution, Mulliken charges, and orbital shapes confirm a nitrogen-initiated, pH-dependent ECE pathway that controls whether ulipristal exhibits reversible or irreversible electrochemical oxidation.

The lifetime of the fabricated sensors was evaluated by contentious monitoring of the electrode signal in the presence of 2.7 µgmL−1 ULP after different time intervals (Fig. S11). Careful literature review for the previously reported analytical approaches for monitoring of ulipristal indicates the noticeable advantages of the introduced voltammetric approach based on the homemade disposable sensors based on rGO-CuONPs nanohybrid in terms of simplicity, sensitivity, and cost-effectiveness with short analysis time (Table S13). Specificity of the method

Residual manufacturing impurities, additives, and excipients that are usually present in the pharmaceutical formulation requires a novel, effective analytical technique capable of detecting the parent pharmaceutical compound in the presence of these interfernts [52]. Therefore, the interference of various interfering species was structured to rigorously demonstrate the selectivity of the developed rGO-CuONPs-SPCEs under realistic bioanalytical and pharmaceutical conditions (Table S14). Common excipients, biomolecules, co-administered drugs, and inorganic ions were added (100-fold excess). The biological interferents commonly present in biological fluids, such as uric acid, ascorbic acid, urea, glucose, creatinine, or BSA, were also included. Collectively, no noticeable interference of the aforementioned species was recorded as evidence of the high selectivity, and accurate quantification of ULP in the marketed samples and biological samples.

Conclusion

The present work introduced the construction and validation of a novel ulipristal acetate voltammetric sensor integrated with rGO-CuONPs nanohybrid. Optimization and full characterization of the modified electrode, electroanalytical parameters, and the electrooxidation mechanism of ULP at the electrode surface were discussed in detail. ULP molecule showed a single anodic oxidation peak at 0.72 V with a diffusion-controlled reaction mechanism in BR buffer pH 5. Calibration curves showed linear dynamic range from 0.010 to 5.04 µgmL−1 with LOD value 0.003 µgmL−1. The developed sensors showed long shelf lifetime with high measuring reproducibility and can be introduced as a complementary measuring approach for monitoring of ULP in various matrices with enhanced sensitivity and selectivity compared with the spectroscopic and chromatographic techniques.

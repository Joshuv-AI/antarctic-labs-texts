Global solutions for supersonic flow of a Chaplygin gas past a conical wing with a shock wave detached from the leading edges
Bingsong Long

Abstract
. In this paper, we first investigate the mathematical aspects of supersonic flow of a Chaplygin gas past a conical wing with diamond-shaped cross sections in the case of a shock wave detached from the leading edges. The flow under consideration is governed by the three-dimensional steady compressible Euler equations. For the Chaplygin gas, all characteristics are linearly degenerate, and shocks are reversible and characteristic. Using these properties, we can determine the location of the shock in advance and reformulate our problem as an oblique derivative problem for a nonlinear degenerate elliptic equation in conical coordinates. By establishing a Lipschitz estimate, we show that the equation is uniformly elliptic in any subdomain strictly away from the degenerate boundary, and then further prove the existence of a solution to the problem via the continuity method and vanishing viscosity method.

Lemma 3.1

Let

be an open bounded domain in the unit sphere that does not contain

composed of a finite number of curves

{\psi}_{\pm}>1
in
\overline{\Omega_{D}}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\psi_{-}\geq 0,\quad\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\psi_{+}\leq 0\quad

\displaystyle\text{in}~\Omega_{D},

\displaystyle\psi_{-}\leq\psi_{+}\quad

\displaystyle\text{on}~\Gamma_{1},

\displaystyle D_{\bm{\zeta}}\psi_{-}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{d_{j}}<D_{\bm{\zeta}}\psi_{+}\cdot\bm{\nu}_{d_{j}}\quad

\displaystyle\text{on}~\Gamma_{j},

{\psi}_{-}\leq{\psi}_{+}
in
\overline{\Omega_{D}}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{1}}

Let

\bar{\psi}:=\psi_{-}-\psi_{+}.

Since
{\psi}_{\pm}>1
in
\overline{\Omega_{D}}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{1}}

{\psi}(\bm{\zeta})=\cosh z(\bm{\zeta}).

A direct computation gives

\big(1+|D_{\bm{\zeta}}z|^{2}-\mu\partial^{2}_{\theta}z\big)\partial_{\theta\theta}z+(1+|D_{\bm{\zeta}}z|^{2}-\mu\dfrac{\partial^{2}_{\varphi}z}{\sin^{2}\theta})\dfrac{\partial_{\varphi\varphi}z}{\sin^{2}\theta}-2\mu\partial_{\theta}z\dfrac{\partial_{\varphi}z}{\sin\theta}\dfrac{\partial_{\theta\varphi}z}{\sin\theta}\\
+\cot\theta\big(1+|D_{\bm{\zeta}}z|^{2}+\mu\dfrac{\partial^{2}_{\varphi}z}{\sin^{2}\theta}\big)\partial_{\theta}z+(2+(1-\mu)|D_{\bm{\zeta}}z|^{2})\frac{1+|D_{\bm{\zeta}}z|^{2}}{\tanh z}=0.

\sup_{\Omega_{D}}\bar{\psi}\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega_{D}}\bar{\psi}\leq\sup_{\Gamma_{1}}\bar{\psi}.

Lemma 3.2

(|\nabla_{\bm{x}}\Phi|^{2}-1)\Delta_{\bm{x}}\Phi-\mu D^{2}_{\bm{x}}\Phi[\nabla_{\bm{x}}\Phi,\nabla_{\bm{x}}\Phi]=0.

) belong to
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}\big(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})\big)
. Moreover, the absence of lower-order terms in this equation implies that the conditions (1.4a)-(1.5d) in
[
26
]
are automatically satisfied. The uniform ellipticity of equation (
3.38
), combined with the angles at
P_{3}
and
P_{4}
lying in
(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
, guarantees that the remaining conditions (1.6a)-(1.7) in
[
26
]
are likewise satisfied.

In conclusion,
\phi_{m,\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1,\alpha_{2}}(\overline{\Omega})
with respect to
\mu\in[0,1]
. Then, we can seek a subsequence
\mu_{m_{k}}\subset\mu_{m}
such that
\phi_{m_{k},\varepsilon}\to\phi_{m_{\infty},\varepsilon}
in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
. Moreover, for any subdomain
\Omega_{sub}\subset\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})
, using the Arzela-Ascoli theorem, there must exist a subsequence
\mu_{m_{k^{\prime}}}\subset\mu_{m_{k}}
such that the corresponding functions
\phi_{m_{k^{\prime}},\varepsilon}
converge in
C^{2}(\Omega_{sub})
, which implies
\phi_{m_{\infty},\varepsilon}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{2}\big(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})\big)
. Then, the limit function
\phi_{m_{\infty},\varepsilon}
is a solution to problem (
3.1
)–(
3.2
) and
\phi_{m_{\infty},\varepsilon}\geq\sqrt{1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}+\varepsilon
in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}}
; that is,
\mu_{\infty}\in J_{\varepsilon}
.

Step
3.
J_{\varepsilon}
is open. Let
\mu_{0}\in J_{\varepsilon}
and
\phi_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon}
be the solution of the corresponding problem. We introduce a auxiliary function
s_{\mu,\varepsilon}
, given by

{\phi}_{\mu,\varepsilon}(\bm{\xi})=\sqrt{1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2}}\cosh s_{\mu,\varepsilon}(\bm{\xi}).

(3.39)

From the above relation and (
3.1
)–(
3.2
), a tedious but direct computation yields that
s_{\mu,\varepsilon}
satisfies

(1+n(\bm{\xi},Ds))(\Delta s+D^{2}s[\bm{\xi},\bm{\xi}])-\mu(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})D^{2}s[Ds+(Ds\cdot\bm{\xi})\bm{\xi},Ds+(Ds\cdot\bm{\xi})\bm{\xi}]\\
+2\Big(1+(1-\mu)n(\bm{\xi},Ds)\Big)Ds\cdot\bm{\xi}+\Big(2+(1-\mu)n(\bm{\xi},Ds)\Big)\frac{1+n(\bm{\xi},Ds)}{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})\tanh s}=0.

(3.40)

with

\begin{cases}s=\text{arcosh}\,(1+\varepsilon)\quad&\text{on $\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}$},\\
Ds\cdot(\bm{\nu}_{py}-\tan\sigma_{2}\bm{\xi})=0\quad&\text{on $\Gamma_{py}$},\\
Ds\cdot\bm{\nu}_{sy1}=0\quad&\text{on $\Gamma_{sy1}$},\\
Ds\cdot\bm{\nu}_{sy2}=0\quad&\text{on $\Gamma_{sy2}$},\end{cases}

where

n(\bm{\xi},Ds):=(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})(|Ds|^{2}+|Ds\cdot\bm{\xi}|^{2}).

Note that, in contrast to (
3.1
), the principal coefficients of (
3.40
) depend only on
\bm{\xi},Ds
. Moreover, the lower-order terms of this equation is non-increasing with respect to
s
. These structural properties are crucial for the solvability of the corresponding linearized equation (
3.42
) below.

Equation (
3.40
) can be rearranged into the following form:

\sum^{2}_{i,j=1}A_{ij}(\mu;\bm{\xi},Ds)\partial_{ij}s+L(\mu;\bm{\xi},s,Ds)=0,

(3.41)

Corresponding, the linearization of equation (
3.41
) at
s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon}
is

\sum^{2}_{i,j=1}A_{ij}(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})\partial_{ij}s+B_{i}(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})\partial_{i}s+\partial_{s}L(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})s=f,

(3.42)

where
s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon}
is defined by
\phi_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon}
and (
3.39
), and

\displaystyle B_{i}=\sum^{2}_{i,j=1}\partial_{p_{i}}A_{ij}(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})\partial_{ij}s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon}+\partial_{p_{i}}L(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})\quad\text{$i=1,2$}.

Here
(p_{1},p_{2}):=(\partial_{\xi_{1}}s,\partial_{\xi_{2}}s)
. Due to
\partial_{s}L(\mu_{0};\bm{\xi},s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon},Ds_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})\leq 0
, equation (
3.42
) satisfies the maximum principle. Then, it follows from
[
16
, Appendix]
that the corresponding oblique derivative problem is uniquely solvable. Similar to the argument in
[
16
]
, the linearized operator for the nonlinear mapping
\mu\mapsto s_{\mu,\varepsilon}
is invertible at
(\mu_{0},s_{\mu_{0},\varepsilon})
. By the implicit function theorem, there consequently exists a neighborhood of
\mu_{0}
where the mapping
\mu\mapsto s_{\mu,\varepsilon}
is well-defined; namely,
\mu_{0}
is an interior point of
J_{\varepsilon}
.

In conclusion, the set
J_{\varepsilon}
is both open and closed with
0\in J_{\varepsilon}
, i.e.,
J_{\varepsilon}=[0,1]
, which means the existence of
\phi_{1,\varepsilon}
.

We now establish the existence of solutions to problem (
2.14
) (i.e., Problem
2.1
).
From the estimates (
3.25
) and (
3.27
), we see that
\phi_{1,\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
Lip(\overline{\Omega})
with respect to the parameter
\varepsilon
. Hence,
\phi_{1,\varepsilon}
is convergent in the function space
C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
, with denoting
\phi
the limit. Similar to the argument in the
Step
2, it follows that
\phi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}})\cap C^{2}\big(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}}\cup\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})\big)
, which is a solution to problem (
2.14
).

Furthermore, estimates (
3.14
) and (
3.27
) imply the locally uniform ellipticity of equation (
2.9
). Then, from (
2.7
), (
3.6
) and Remark
3.1
, Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
can be applied to problem (
2.14
), yielding
\phi\in Lip(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}})\cap C^{2}\big(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}}\cup\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})\big)
. Using
[
20
, Theorem 6.17]
and
[
26
, Lemma 1.3]
, we further obtain
\phi\in Lip(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}})\cap C^{\infty}\big(\overline{\Omega}\setminus(\overline{\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}}\cup\{P_{3}\}\cup\{P_{4}\})\big)
.

The proof of our main theorem is complete.

Appendix A
The ellipticity of equation (
2.9
)

As discussed in subsection
2.2
, for the Chaplygin gas, equation (
2.9
) is hyperbolic outside the domain
\Omega
, and becomes parabolic degenerate on the boundary
\Gamma_{cone}^{\infty}
. This naturally leads to the question of whether parabolic bubbles exist inside
\Omega
. The question can be answered by the following lemma. Thanks to the rotational invariance of equation
(
2.1
)
, we can assume that the domain
\Omega
does not contain the origin
(0,0)
in conical coordinates.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Omega_{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open bounded domain that does not contain the origin
(0,0)
. Suppose that a function
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega_{D})
satisfies (
2.9
) with
0\leq L\leq 1
in
\Omega_{D}
. Then,

L^{2}<1\quad\text{in}~\Omega_{D}.

Proof.

If the conclusion would not hold, then there exists a point
\hat{P}(\xi_{1p},\xi_{2p})\in\Omega_{D}
, such that

L^{2}=1\quad\text{at}~\hat{P}.

(A.1)

Combing with the assumption
0\leq L\leq 1
in
\Omega_{D}
, we have

D(L^{2})(\hat{P})=\bm{0}.

(A.2)

Since equation (
2.1
) is rotation-invariant and the solution
\Phi
of (
2.1
) takes the form (
2.7
), we can assume without loss of generality that
\partial_{x_{1}}\Phi=\partial_{x_{2}}\Phi=0
and
\partial_{x_{3}}\Phi=|D_{\bm{x}}\Phi|
at the points
(\xi_{1p}x_{3},\xi_{2p}x_{3},x_{3})
with
x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then it follows from (
2.7
) and (
2.10
)–(
2.11
) that

D\phi=\bm{0}\quad\text{at}~\hat{P}.

(A.3)

Also using (
2.11
) and (
A.1
), we obtain

\phi^{2}=1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2}\quad\text{at}~\hat{P}.

(A.4)

Moreover, because equation (
2.9
) is also rotation-invariant, without loss of generality, we may choose
\xi_{1p}=-|\bm{\xi}|<0
and
\xi_{2p}=0
.

We will arrive at a contradiction for
(
A.2
)
. From
(
2.11
)
, one has

\begin{split}D(L^{2})&=\frac{D(|D\phi|^{2}+\chi^{2})-L^{2}Dc^{2}}{c^{2}}-\frac{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})D\phi^{2}-\phi^{2}D(|\bm{\xi}|^{2})}{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})^{2}c^{2}}\\
&=\frac{Dc^{2}}{c^{2}}(1-L^{2})-\frac{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})D\phi^{2}-\phi^{2}D(|\bm{\xi}|^{2})}{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})^{2}c^{2}}.\end{split}

(A.5)

Here, for the second identity, we have used the relation
Dc^{2}=D(|D\phi|^{2}+\chi^{2})
. Substituting the relations
(
A.1
) and (
A.4
)
into
(
A.5
)
gives

\begin{split}D(L^{2})&=-\frac{2(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})\phi D\phi-2\phi^{2}\bm{\xi}}{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})^{2}c^{2}}\quad\text{at}~\hat{P},\\
&=\frac{2\bm{\xi}}{(1+|\bm{\xi}|^{2})c^{2}}=-\frac{2}{(1+|\xi_{1p}|^{2})c^{2}}(|\xi_{1p}|,0)\quad\text{at}~\hat{P}.\end{split}

Namely,
D_{\xi_{1}}(L^{2})<0
at
\hat{P}
, which is contrary to
(
A.2
)
. The proof is complete.

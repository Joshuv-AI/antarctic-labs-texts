Chronic cardiorenal syndrome: cardio-renal protective effect of SGLT2i
Authors: Yixin Zhu, Chenxi Lv, Hanqi Yang, Qian Lu, XuChen Wang, Yueqi Zhang, Maojuan Guo, Bo Yang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesChronic cardiorenal syndrome (types 2 and 4) arises from chronic dysfunction of either the heart or kidneys, leading to progressive deterioration of the other organ. The coexistence of cardiac and renal dysfunction significantly complicates treatment strategies and is associated with high morbidity and mortality, with currently limited effective therapeutic options. Given the demonstrated cardiorenal protective effects of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors (SGLT2i), it is important to explore their potential mechanisms and clinical application value in the treatment of non-diabetic chronic cardiorenal syndrome.MethodsThis review systematically analyzes the multiple mechanisms of SGLT2i and integrates the latest clinical trial evidence to evaluate their potential application in the management of non-diabetic chronic cardiorenal syndrome. It specifically examines their cardiorenal protective pathways beyond glucose-lowering effects, including diuretic and natriuretic actions, improved hemodynamics, inhibition of neurohormonal overactivation, modulation of inflammation and oxidative stress, and enhancement of vascular endothelial function.ResultsRecent clinical studies confirm that SGLT2i exert cardiorenal protection through multiple mechanisms and demonstrate potential for improving prognosis in non-diabetic patients with cardiorenal syndrome. Combination therapy with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists shows synergistic therapeutic advantages.ConclusionsSGLT2i provide a novel therapeutic strategy for chronic cardiorenal syndrome, with their multi-target mechanisms offering new perspectives for combined cardiorenal protection. However, further clinical research is required to optimize treatment protocols and address practical challenges in their application.Keywords: SGLT2i, chronic cardiorenal syndrome, hemodynamics, eGFR, NT-proBNP

Introduction

Cardio-renal syndrome (CRS) is a broadly defined condition in which the heart and kidneys are interrelated, leading to a multi-organ disease, where acute or chronic dysfunction of one organ may trigger dysfunction of the other [1]. In 2023, the American Heart Association (AHA) introduced the novel concept of Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic Syndrome (CKMS) [2]. This integrated framework expands the traditional cardio-renal syndrome paradigm by emphasizing that metabolic dysregulation (such as insulin resistance and dyslipidemia) exacerbates the cardiorenal crosstalk in CRS. The core of this model is to highlight the significant role of metabolic disorders in cardio-renal syndrome.

CRS is categorized into five subtypes based on the acute or chronic nature of cardiac and renal dysfunction and the primary organ affected [3] (Table 1). Among them, type 2 CRS is characterized by progressive renal dysfunction in patients with CHF, while type 4 CRS is characterized by cardiac dysfunction resulting from primary chronic kidney disease (CKD). These two types of cardio-renal syndrome are considered chronic cardio-renal syndrome. It is noteworthy that the CKMS theory suggests that patients are more likely to develop cardio-renal syndrome due to metabolic disorders (such as disturbances in glucose and lipid metabolism, calcium and phosphorus metabolism, as well as volume and pressure overload) [7–9]. As CKMS progresses through disease stages (from stage 0 to stage 3), hyperglycemia and hyperuricemia further exacerbate the synergistic damage caused by CKD and Cardiovascular Disease (CVD), significantly increasing the absolute risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular events and heart failure [7].

Studies have shown that approximately 25%–50% of patients with CHF also have CKD, and these patients face a 56% increased risk of death [10]. Nearly half of all deaths in CKD patients are attributed to cardiovascular events. The mortality rate from CVD in patients with dialysis-dependent CKD is approximately 15 times higher than that in the general population. Acute myocardial infarction and sudden cardiac death are the most common causes [11,12]. Overall, in the context of metabolic disorders, cardio-renal syndrome significantly increases the mortality risk for patients, imposing a heavy burden on national and family healthcare costs. Therefore, research on the treatment of chronic CRS remains extremely important [10].

Currently, the popular drugs for the treatment of cardio-renal syndrome mainly include RAAS inhibitors [13], diuretics, and inotropic agents [14]. These drugs can alleviate volume overload, thereby partially relieving CRS. However, RAAS inhibitors may exacerbate renal dysfunction due to hypotension or hyperkalemia, and diuretics are prone to diuretic resistance and electrolyte disturbances, all of which limit the scope of use of these drugs. SGLT2i is different from traditional CRS treatment drugs. By inhibiting the sodium-glucose cotransporter in the proximal renal tubule, it promotes the excretion of urinary sugar/sodium, reduces volume load, and improves myocardial energy metabolism. Its multi-target effects beyond its glucose-lowering action (such as anti-fibrosis and improved endothelial function) may be more suitable for the complex pathophysiological mechanisms of CRS [15].

Currently, the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has approved five SGLT2is as glucose-lowering medications, which inhibit the reabsorption of glucose in the gut, thereby reducing postprandial hyperglycemia. The SGLT2i mainly include canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin [16], and sotagliflozin [17]. Since then, SGLT2i have demonstrated therapeutic effects beyond glycemic control, occupying a core position in cardiorenal protection, especially in their significant ability to reduce cardiovascular events and slow the progression of kidney disease [18]. Trials such as EMPEROR – REDUCED (Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction) [19] and DECLARE – TIMI 58 (Dapagliflozin Effect on Cardiovascular Events in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus) [20] have highlighted their substantial cardiorenal protective effects.

Therefore, based on the pathogenesis of chronic CRS, this review explores the improvement of cardiac and renal function by SGLT2i from multiple dimensions, including diuresis and natriuresis, regulation of neurohumoral factors, inhibition of sympathetic hormone activation, reduction of inflammatory responses, amelioration of vascular endothelial dysfunction, and inhibition of fibrosis and oxidative stress. This exploration is conducted in conjunction with levels of glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP), aiming to provide new therapeutic strategies for the treatment of chronic CRS.

Analysis of the mechanism of cardio-renal protective effect of SGLT2i

SGLT2is were initially developed as non-insulin-dependent antidiabetic drugs [16,19,21]. These drugs target SGLT2 to inhibit the reabsorption of glucose in the urine, thereby altering specific stages of renal metabolism to promote glycosuria and improve hyperglycemia [22]. The SGLT protein family plays a key role in regulating glucose transport across the cell membrane and is essential for maintaining blood glucose homeostasis. Among these, SGLT1 and SGLT2 are the primary subtypes involved in glucose transport. SGLT1 is primarily localized to the S3 segment of the proximal convoluted tubule, where it accounts for approximately 10% of glucose reabsorption [24]. In contrast, SGLT2 is predominantly expressed in the S1 segment of the renal proximal convoluted tubule [25]. It functions as a high-capacity, low-affinity glucose transporter, responsible for reabsorbing 80%–90% of the glucose from the filtrate [26]. This transporter facilitates the movement of Na+ and glucose into the basolateral cell membrane at a 1:1 ratio. Glucose then enters the interstitial space via the basolateral GLUT2 protein, while Na+ is actively pumped out by the Na+/K+ ATPase [27]. Moreover, the dual SGLT1 and SGLT2 inhibitor, sotagliflozin, is primarily employed to reduce the risk of cardiovascular mortality and heart failure in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) and CKD. Its dual inhibitory effect on SGLT1 and SGLT2 receptors not only reduces renal glucose reabsorption but also decreases glucose absorption in the gastrointestinal tract [28]. SGLT1 also plays a role in glycogen accumulation in muscle tissue under hyperglycemic conditions. Inhibiting the SGLT1 receptor can reduce the risk of hyperlipidemia, lowering the risk of myocardial infarction in patients [28,29].

The SOLOIST-WHF study [30] (Sotagliflozin in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Worsening Heart Failure) evaluated sotagliflozin’s effect on cardiovascular events in patients with type 2 diabetes and worsening heart failure, while the SCORED study [31] (Sotagliflozin on Cardiovascular and Renal Events in Patients with Type 2 Diabetes and Moderate Renal Impairment Who Are at Cardiovascular Risk) assessed its impact on cardiovascular and renal events in patients with type 2 diabetes and moderate renal impairment at cardiovascular risk. Both studies demonstrated that patients treated with sotagliflozin experienced a reduced risk of cardiovascular and renal mortality, providing compelling evidence of its significant cardiorenal benefits.

SGLT2 also exerts notable off-target effects on cardiomyocytes, which typically do not express SGLT2 [32]. Their natriuretic action is partly mediated through interference with the functional interaction between SGLT2 and NHE3 [33]. NHE3, which colocalizes with SGLT2 in the S1 segment of the proximal tubule, plays a dominant role in the tubular reabsorption of filtered Na+ [4,33,34]. The activities of these two transporters are interdependent: the reduction in Na+ transport mediated by NHE3 is thought to underlie the cardiorenal protective effects of SGLT2i [35]. In NHE3 knockout mice, downregulation of NHE3 protein in the kidneys reduces SGLT2 expression and inhibits the SGLT2i-induced urinary sodium excretion [23]. Additionally, canagliflozin can directly inhibit NHE3, selectively blocking sodium-dependent fluid transport and albumin uptake in mouse proximal tubule cells, which also modulates mitochondrial function upstream of the AMPK/mTOR axis [35].

NHE1 is predominantly expressed in the membranes of cardiac macrophages, where it regulates intracellular pH by exchanging intracellular protons for extracellular sodium at a 1:1 ratio [36]. It has been shown to exert protective effects on renal tubular epithelial cells in various CKD models [37]. Under hypoxic conditions, the accumulation of intracellular acid and a marked increase in H+ concentration can activate NHE1, leading to a significant rise in intracellular Na+ levels [36]. Studies have demonstrated that SGLT2i can still prevent heart failure in SGLT2 gene knockout mice by inhibiting NHE1. This suggests that their effects are not solely mediated through renal SGLT2 inhibition but also involve systemic metabolic regulation [38].

The NHE protein family maintains intracellular pH and ionic balance by mediating the antiport of protons and sodium ions. An increase in Na+ impairs the sodium-calcium exchanger (NCX), which in turn leads to elevated intracellular Ca2+ levels. This rise in Ca2+ activates NADPH oxidase, stimulating the production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which subsequently triggers inflammation via the NOD-like receptor protein 3 (NLRP3) inflammasome [39]. This cascade of events further activates renal tubulointerstitial fibrosis (e.g. Transforming Growth Factor-β, TGF-β1) and the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS) [40].

Notably, elevated mitochondrial Na+ levels compromise the fluidity of the inner mitochondrial membrane, thereby exacerbating ROS generation, while Ca2+ depletion induces a state of energy deficiency and impairs NADPH production [32]. Empagliflozin has been shown to effectively inhibit NHE in cardiomyocytes, reducing cytosolic Na+ and Ca2+ levels while concurrently increasing mitochondrial calcium concentration [41]. This dual action mitigates inflammation-induced ROS production and slows the progression of heart failure. Additionally, the release of Ca2+ from pyroptotic cells instigates the production of inflammatory mediators, such as interleukin-6 (IL-6) and tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α). These ROS and inflammatory mediators further activate sarcolemmal NHE-1, perpetuating a destructive cycle of Na+ overload [39].

SGLT2i multi-pathway therapy for cardiorenal diseases

Cardiorenal diseases are intricately interconnected through hemodynamics, neurohumoral factors, inflammation, and oxidative stress, forming a vicious cycle that accelerates the deterioration of both organ functions [5,15]. Based on the diuretic and natriuretic effects of SGLT2i through inhibition of SGLT2 and NHE proteins, and considering the pathogenesis of CRS, we hypothesize that SGLT2i may exert its effects through the following pathways: improving cardiorenal hemodynamics via Tubuloglomerular Feedback (TGF); inhibiting the overactivation of the RAAS and sympathetic nervous system (SNS); reducing inflammation and oxidative stress [9,42,43]; as well as improving mitochondrial function [44] and inhibiting organ fibrosis. These mechanisms may provide cardiorenal protection and ultimately bring clinical benefits in the treatment of chronic CRS (Figure 2).

SGTL2i treats chronic CRS by improving hemodynamics

Hemodynamics alterations (such as cardiorenal blood flow, perfusion pressure, and vascular resistance), are integral to the pathophysiology of CRS [27,45], predominantly manifesting as volume overload and glomerular hypertension. Volume overload, driven by sodium and water retention, culminates in venous congestion in both the heart and kidneys, facilitating the progression of CRS [46]. The increased metabolic burden on the kidneys exacerbates volume overload, elevating both central venous pressure and renal venous pressure, establishing a heart-kidney vicious cycle [45,47]. In the renal system, SGLT2i specifically target SGLT2 to restore abnormal Tubuloglomerular Feedback (TGF). By increasing Na+ delivery to the macula densa, they reverse this feedback loop, inhibit glomerular feedback, and induce afferent arteriolar constriction. This results in a reduction of intraglomerular pressure and reduces glomerular hyperfiltration, initially manifesting as a decline in glomerular filtration rate (GFR) [25]. Importantly, when SGLT2 inhibition is discontinued, this GFR decline is reversible, reflecting a hemodynamic response to the therapeutic intervention [48].

SGLT2i reduce preload by promoting natriuresis and osmotic diuresis [49], while decreasing afterload through blood pressure reduction and vascular function improvement, thereby ameliorating ventricular loading conditions [27]. This has the added benefit of improving subendocardial blood flow in heart failure patients [50]. SGLT2 inhibition in the proximal tubule increases natriuresis, reduces extracellular volume, and consequently enhances urinary chloride reabsorption, leading to elevated serum chloride concentrations [51]. Given that sodium and chloride are typically cotransported in renal tubules, the increased serum chloride concentration promotes further renal chloride reabsorption, stabilizing serum chloride levels and regulating electrolyte balance. This mechanism not only helps maintain plasma volume and enhance vascular tone but also avoids diuretic resistance, ultimately improving renal perfusion [52]. Notably, SGLT2i also lowers serum potassium levels by inhibiting renal sodium and glucose reabsorption. This inhibition results in a modest rise in aldosterone levels, which enhances urinary excretion of both sodium and potassium. This mechanism concurrently preserves renal function and maintains potassium homeostasis [51,53]. Additionally, SGLT2i increases strong organic acid metabolite concentrations (such as acetoacetate and ketone bodies) while reducing serum HCO3− concentration, indirectly raising serum chloride levels through buffering effects [51]. In the EMBRACE-HF (Empagliflozin Evaluation by Measuring the Impact on Hemodynamics in Patients with Heart Failure) trial [54], empagliflozin significantly reduced pulmonary artery diastolic pressure, with a mean 1.7 mmHg decrease after 12-week treatment (95% confidence interval: 0.3–3.2; p = 0.02).

In contrast to other diuretics that primarily exert natriuretic effects, the osmotic diuresis and glycosuria induced by SGLT2 inhibition facilitate greater electrolyte-free water clearance, alleviating congestion and reducing cardiorenal burden [44,55]. The EMPAG-HF [56] (Empagliflozin in Patients with Acute Decompensated Heart Failure) trial and EMPULSE [57] (Empagliflozin in patients hospitalized with acute heart faiLure who have been Stabilized) trials demonstrated that empagliflozin treatment significantly reduced cumulative diuretic doses in heart failure patients versus placebo, while improving diuretic efficiency. Notably, SGLT2i maintain clinical efficacy in patients with renal impairment (eGFR ≥ 20 mL/min/1.73 m2), as evidenced by reduction in diuretic requirements. These findings suggest that SGLT2i may more effectively alleviate congestion and potentially mitigate the risks associated with high-dose diuretic therapy in heart failure management.

The glycosuria induced by SGLT2i triggers a shift in energy substrate utilization, promoting the production of ketone bodies (such as β-hydroxybutyrate) in the liver. As highly efficient energy substrates, ketone bodies are more effective than free fatty acids in myocardial energy metabolism, reducing oxygen consumption and improving cardiac energy utilization efficiency [58]. Preclinical models have shown that SGLT2i increases ketone body utilization, transitioning myocardial cells from a free fatty acid–dependent metabolic pattern to a more efficient ketone body oxidation pathway [59]. This metabolic shift not only improves cardiac diastolic function but also enhances myocardial ATP production, with more pronounced effects in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF).

In the EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial (Empagliflozin Cardiovascular Outcome Event Trial in Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus Patients) [60], empagliflozin improved cardiorenal metabolic status by promoting the production of the energy substrate β-hydroxybutyrate. Additionally, β-hydroxybutyrate works synergistically with other SGLT2i mechanisms, such as increased oxygen delivery due to hemoconcentration and reduced cardiac load from decreased blood volume and pressure, exerting robust cardiorenal protective effects [60–62]. It is crucial to note that, in the absence of SGLT2 inhibition, the diabetic kidney functions as a ketogenic organ, and ketonemia can dilate afferent arterioles, promoting glomerular hyperfiltration and exacerbating its detrimental effects. Thus, the production of ketone bodies may exacerbate the progression of diabetic nephropathy [63].

SGLT2i treats chronic CRS by inhibiting the RAAS and SNS

In addition to regulating hemodynamics, SGLT2i further improves cardiorenal interactions by inhibiting the RAAS and SNS. Both RAAS and SNS are crucial homeostatic systems that maintain blood flow perfusion under low blood pressure conditions [64]. However, overactivation of RAAS can cause glomerular injury, exacerbate congestion, and ultimately result in structural and functional damage to the heart and kidneys [64–66].

In patients with heart failure, enhanced sodium reabsorption in the proximal tubules stimulates juxtaglomerular cells to increase renin secretion. This renin release leads to afferent arteriole dilation and glomerular hyperfiltration, which subsequently activates the RAAS, creating a pathophysiological vicious cycle [52]. SGLT2i provides long-term renal protection by reducing transglomerular pressure, a mechanism that stems from TGF-mediated afferent arteriolar constriction, rather than angiotensin II (Ang II) inhibition [25]. SGLT2i can also reverse Ang II-induced glomerulosclerosis, tubulointerstitial fibrosis, and proteinuria without significantly affecting systolic blood pressure. This suggests that their cardiorenal protective effects may involve interference with intrarenal RAAS activity, particularly in mitigating Ang II–induced renal injury in mice [67]. Additionally, increased sodium and chloride flow into the distal tubules activates TGF through ATP breakdown into adenosine, leading to afferent arteriolar constriction and RAAS activation inhibition, further enhancing cardiorenal protection [52].

Epicardial adipose tissue, which plays an active role in RAAS signaling, can be inhibited by SGLT2i [68], potentially contributing to their cardioprotective effects [44]. Studies in a subtotal nephrectomy rat model [69] have shown that these inhibitors do not exacerbate renal dysfunction in non-diabetic chronic kidney disease patients through RAAS activation, thereby preventing the worsening of RAAS-mediated cardiorenal events [40].

Overactivation of the SNS is associated with increased activity of the apical membrane NHE3 in the proximal tubules, leading to antinatriuretic and antidiuretic effects [70]. SGLT2i can reduce sympathetic activity [71], inhibit the expression of SGLT2 and NHE3 in the proximal tubules [72], increase fractional excretion of sodium in the short term, and ameliorate the pathological state of abnormally elevated renal norepinephrine levels. The antinatriuretic effects of renal sympathetic activation are particularly pronounced in the proximal tubules [73]. Studies have confirmed that dapagliflozin exerts cardiorenal protective effects by reducing renal sympathetic nerve activity in mice fed a high-fat diet [74]. In a neurogenic hypertension mouse model [75], the renal tyrosine hydroxylase staining positivity rate and norepinephrine content were significantly lower in the dapagliflozin-treated group compared with the control group.

Moreover, the natriuretic peptide system, which is regulated by Ang II and aldosterone [76,77], plays a central role in maintaining cardiorenal homeostasis and fluid and electrolyte balance [42,78]. Further research using a zebrafish heart failure model has found that SGLT2i can partially improve heart failure incidence and mortality by modulating the signaling pathways of atrial natriuretic peptide and brain natriuretic peptide [79].

SGLT2i treat cardiorenal syndrome by improving the inflammation-oxidative stress axis

SGLT2i ameliorates inflammatory response

Inflammation plays a key role in cardiorenal interactions, as both heart failure with congestion and chronic kidney disease are chronic inflammatory conditions characterized by elevated levels of circulating inflammatory mediators [25]. A meta-analysis of 18 randomized controlled trials demonstrated that, compared with placebo, SGLT2i significantly reduced IL-6 levels; notably, dapagliflozin was more effective than empagliflozin or canagliflozin in reducing IL-6 levels [80].

Beyond IL-6, SGLT2i also prevent the development of inflammation by inhibiting pro-inflammatory factors of the nuclear factor κB (NF-κB) family, CD68 macrophage aggregation, and the expression of p65, Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), and monocyte chemoattractant protein [81]. Additionally, SGLT2i also attenuates the activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome by activating the AMPK pathway and inhibiting NF-κB p65. This mechanism not only protects the heart from inflammatory damage [82] but also curtails high glucose-induced inflammation in human renal proximal tubular cells [6]. Notably, by reducing hyperglycemia and suppressing Ang II levels, SGLT2i induce beneficial effects such as vasodilation, natriuresis, blood pressure reduction, and antiproliferation [83]. Consequently, the AMPK/NF-κB/NLRP3 signaling pathway emerges as a key mechanism by which SGLT2i exerts anti-inflammatory effects in chronic cardiorenal syndrome.

SGLT2i regulates the oxidative stress response through mitochondrial function

In chronic CRS with heart and kidney failure, the RAAS and the SNS interact with inflammatory processes, leading to the activation of NADPH oxidase, mitochondrial dysfunction, and disrupted nitric oxide–reactive oxygen species (NO-ROS) balance. These pathological alterations ultimately trigger oxidative stress, further compromising organ function [84]. The primary sources of oxidative stress in this context are dysfunctional mitochondria and peroxisomes [73], reflecting an elevated ratio of ROS production-to-degradation ratio [85].

Given that the heart and kidneys are both rich in mitochondria, they are particularly vulnerable to mitochondrial dysfunction [86]. SGLT2i offers partial improvements in mitochondrial function via the AMPK/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway: AMPK phosphorylation or SIRT1 deacetylation directly activates PGC-1α, which upregulates genes responsible for mitochondrial biogenesis and function [87,88]. By inducing glucosuria and lowering pathogenic lipid levels, SGLT2i improve systemic glucose metabolism. This hypoglycemic state activates SIRT1, thereby protecting the heart and kidneys from oxidative damage [89,90]. Notably, empagliflozin has been shown to improve mitochondrial function and autophagy under hyperglycemic conditions by AMPK activation, which reduces mitochondrial fragmentation, suppresses ROS production, and inhibits mTOR in renal tubules [91].

The effects mediated by SIRT1 are associated with hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF-1α), a crucial regulator of inflammatory responses in cardiomyocytes [40]. HIF-1α primarily functions to activate the transcription of genes encoding metabolic enzymes, transport proteins, and mitochondrial proteins, resulting in reduced oxygen consumption [92]. Furthermore, HIF-1α inhibits mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative function while promoting the autophagic clearance of damaged mitochondria, significantly reducing oxidative stress in both glomerular and tubular cells [63]. Experimental studies using renal ischemia-reperfusion models have demonstrated that SGLT2i exert protective effects through three primary mechanisms: prevention of tubular cell apoptosis, enhancement of cellular adaptation to hypoxia, and retardation of renal fibrosis progression. These effects are achieved by elevating HIF-1 levels, restoring vascular endothelial growth factor expression, and enhancing the integrity of peritubular capillary endothelial cells [93].

The principal mechanism underlying SGLT2i-mediated oxidative stress inhibition appears to involve the suppression of AngII-induced SGLT2 overexpression, which consequently diminishes AngII-mediated ROS production [94].

SGLT2i ameliorates endothelial dysfunction and alleviates hypoxia in chronic CRS treatment

Endothelial dysfunction plays a critical role in CRS, contributing to cardiorenal dysfunction in type 2 CRS via impairment of the L-arginine-NO pathway. As a key vasodilator, NO’s bioavailability is reduced in this condition, leading to vasoconstriction, platelet aggregation, and the formation of thrombus, while simultaneously elevating ROS levels, which further exacerbate endothelial dysfunction [94,95]. Experimental evidence indicates that empagliflozin effectively reduces high glucose-induced ROS generation, modulates the expression of both Vascular Cell Adhesion Molecule-1 and tissue factor proteins, and restores NO synthesis [96], thereby significantly ameliorating endothelial dysfunction while reducing endothelial inflammation and oxidative stress parameters [97]. The antioxidant activity of SGLT2i in resting endothelial cells is, in part, mediated through NADPH oxidase-dependent mechanisms [98]. Under normal physiological conditions, endothelial cells play a crucial role in maintaining vascular homeostasis by precisely regulating vascular tone, coagulation balance, and inflammatory responses. However, in endothelial dysfunction, this balance is disrupted, leading to simultaneous platelet activation, procoagulant factor release (e.g. von Willebrand factor), and elevated inflammatory markers (including IL-6 and intercellular cell adhesion molecule-1). These changes work synergistically to increase thrombosis risk [99].

Highly selective SGLT2is (e.g. empagliflozin and dapagliflozin) primarily lower blood glucose by inhibiting renal SGLT2, with minimal SGLT1 activity. While these agents provide substantial benefits in enhancing endothelial function and offering cardiovascular protection, their antithrombotic efficacy remains limited [100]. In contrast, both sotagliflozin (a dual SGLT1/SGLT2 inhibitor) and canagliflozin (characterized by a 250:1 SGLT1-to-SGLT2 inhibition ratio) exhibit substantially greater SGLT1 inhibitory effects [101]. Their antithrombotic potential appears to be mediated through a constellation of mechanisms, including reduced intestinal glucose absorption, modulation of the gut microbiota, attenuation of inflammation and oxidative stress, inhibition of platelet activation, and enhancement of endothelial function - collectively contributing to superior prevention of myocardial infarction and stroke. The antithrombotic properties of canagliflozin’s SGLT1 inhibition may also involve activation of the GLP-1 pathway, given GLP-1’s known inhibitory effects on platelet aggregation and inflammatory cascades [102]. Emerging evidence suggests that these benefits may extend beyond GLP-1 mediation. The widespread expression of SGLT1 across the cardiovascular endothelium implies direct cellular actions, where thromboprotection may be mediated through dual mechanisms: enhanced myocardial antioxidant defenses and optimized endothelial performance [103].

Emerging data underscore the dual role of SGLT2i in mitigating renal hypoxia and optimizing energy utilization, which forms the basis of their nephroprotective effects. The therapeutic mechanism involves decreased proximal tubule glucose reabsorption, subsequently reducing cellular energy requirements, enhancing renal oxygen delivery and minimizing the generation of ROS. The improvement in proximal tubule cells function, coupled with a reduction in energy expenditure, lowers oxygen demand and increases cortical oxygen tension, enhancing the activity of renal cortical cells and improving the viability of the medulla [92]. Notably, in type 1 diabetic patients with proteinuria, SGLT2i rapidly enhance renal cortical oxygenation within six hours of administration [104].

By ameliorating hypoxic conditions, SGLT2i triggers nutrient deprivation signaling pathways that induce the transdifferentiation of myofibroblasts into EPO-producing fibroblasts. This improvement in hypoxia concurrently stimulates erythropoiesis while attenuating organelle dysfunction, inflammatory cascades, and fibrotic progression [63]. Crucially, this erythropoietic response is mediated through HIF-mediated pathways: SGLT2i suppress HIF-1α-driven metabolic reprogramming, shifting from lipid oxidation to glycolysis in tubular cells, thereby restoring renal glucose-lipid homeostasis [105]. This biphasic HIF modulation involves HIF-1α inhibition (reducing inflammation/fibrosis) and HIF-2α activation (promoting EPO synthesis), SGLT2i suppress HIF-1α to reduce inflammation, while activating HIF-2α to promote EPO synthesis, synergistically improving anemia. SGLT2i enhance the hypoxic state in the renal medullary junction (due to the shift of sodium/glucose transport load to the ascending limb of the loop of Henle), inhibit HIF prolyl hydroxylase, stabilize HIF-2α signaling, and thereby stimulate the synthesis of EPO in renal interstitial cells [106]. Clinical trials have confirmed that SGLT2i therapy elevates hemoglobin levels by week four, with sustained effects [107]. Strikingly, dapagliflozin achieves double the anemia correction rate versus placebo, demonstrating its capacity to boost serum EPO and ameliorate anemia. In CKD populations, SGLT2i consistently increase hemoglobin levels, correct anemia, and reduce cardiovascular mortality and heart failure hospitalization rates [108]. SGLT1 inhibition directly modulates endothelial inflammatory cascades (e.g. VCAM-1 downregulation) and platelet activation via GLP-1-independent pathways, offering superior antithrombotic effects compared to selective SGLT2i.

Clinical evaluation of SGLT2i in the treatment of CRS

The initial eGFR dip reflects hemodynamic adaptation via TGF-mediated afferent arteriolar constriction, a reversible mechanism correlating with long-term renoprotection rather than injury. This review primarily evaluates the cardiorenal protective effects of SGLT2i, as assessed by changes in eGFR and N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) levels, and further discusses how combining SGLT2i with mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists (MRAs) or glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) influences cardiorenal adverse outcomes in clinical practice.

Improvement of renal function by SGLT2i as estimated by eGFR

The GFR serves as the gold standard for assessing renal function and evaluating the pharmacotherapeutic efficacy. Clinically, patients with HF demonstrate a significantly heightened susceptibility to the development of CKD [42]. A sustained decline in eGFR decline below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 consistently predicts both renal dysfunction and an elevated cardiovascular risk [9].

Prominent studies, including EMPA-REG OUTCOME [109,110], CREDENCE [111,112] (Canagliflozin and Renal Endpoints in Diabetes with Established Nephropathy Clinical Evaluation), and the DELIVER [113] (Dapagliflozin Evaluation to Improve the LIVEs of Patients With Preserved Ejection Fraction Heart Failure) trials (Table 2), the rate of eGFR decline in the SGLT2i treatment group was significantly lower compared with that of the placebo group. This significantly reduced the risk of heart failure and kidney disease, thereby demonstrating the cardiorenal protective effect of SGLT2i. However, in patients with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) in the EMPEROR-Preserved (Empagliflozin Outcome Trial in Patients with Chronic Heart Failure with Preserved Ejection Fraction) [114,115], although the decline in eGFR was notably more gradual in the empagliflozin group compared to the placebo group, the relative risk reduction for events characterized by a sustained ≥40% decline in eGFR was only marginally lower (5%) in the empagliflozin group [116], This difference, however, failed to reach statistical significance. In the stratified renal outcomes of the DAPA-HF (Dapagliflozin And Prevention of Adverse-outcomes in Heart Failure) and DELIVER trials involving heart failure patients, dapagliflozin demonstrated superiority over placebo across six hierarchical renal endpoints, including all-cause mortality, end-stage kidney disease (ESKD) or eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m2, and ≥57% decline in eGFR, as measured using win statistics. The win ratio for the dapagliflozin group was 1.10 (95% CI: 1.06–1.15), indicating consistent and significant benefits across multiple renal outcome measures [125].

In EMPA-REG OUTCOME [117,126], as well as in EMPEROR-Reduced [117–119], empagliflozin treatment was shown to slow the decline of renal function in patients with heart failure with reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF), irrespective of whether the patients had diabetes or not. This decrease is typically attributed to the pharmacological properties of the drug and is generally reversible with continued treatment.

Improvement of cardiac function by SGLT2i as estimated by NT-proBNP

Elevations in BNP and NT-proBNP levels are strongly associated with poor prognosis, serving as critical biomarkers for the prognosis of CHF [78,127]. In patients with HFmrEF or HFpEF, the higher the NT-proBNP level correlates with an increased incidence of cardiovascular events, establishing a positive relationship between the two. Preliminary studies involving primary nondiabetic patients with CKD have shown that plasma concentrations of BNP and NT-proBNP rise in parallel with the decline in kidney function, correlating with an increased risk of progression to ESKD in individuals with mild to moderate CKD [127,128]. Studies such as EMPEROR-Preserved [114,115], DEFINE-HF [129,130] (Dapagliflozin Effects on Biomarkers, Symptoms, and Functional Status in Patients with Heart Failure with Reduced Ejection Fraction), DAPA-HF [115,120], and EMBRACE-HF [54] have demonstrated that patients in the SGLT2i group experienced varying degrees of NT-proBNP levels reductions, Dapagliflozin’s benefit in high NT-proBNP subgroups (>1,600 pg/mL) supports its preferential use in advanced CRS with biomarker-evident congestion (Table 3).

A meta-analysis examining the effects of SGLT2i on cardiac function and health status in patients with CHF [131] showed that in clinical trials such as DAPA-HF and EMPEROR-Reduced, where BNP and NT-proBNP were analyzed as outcome endpoints, the incidence of ≥20% reduction in NT-proBNP was 10% higher in the SGLT2i group compared to the placebo group. The study concluded that treatment with SGLT2i greatly reduced NT-proBNP and improved health in patients with congestive heart failure with or without T2DM. Additionally, the EMPA-HEART 2 CardioLink-7 trial (Empagliflozin and Cardiac Remodeling in People Without Diabetes) demonstrated [133] that in patients with neither diabetes nor significant heart failure but with risk factors for adverse cardiac remodeling, empagliflozin did not lead to a significant reduction in the left ventricular mass index (LVMi) after 6 months of treatment. This suggests that its effect on ameliorating cardiac remodeling could be limited in this patient population.

A negative correlation between Body Mass Index (BMI) and NT-proBNP was observed in the DELIVER study, with dapagliflozin treatment resulting in varying reductions in weight and blood pressure [134]. The outcomes of the DELIVER [124,135], DECLARE-TIMI 58 [132,136], and EMPEROR-Preserved trials were highly consistent, demonstrating that dapagliflozin significantly reduces the risk of cardiovascular hospitalization and death, irrespective of baseline NT-proBNP levels.

Combination of SGLT2i with MRAs and GLP-1 RAs

SGLT2i have demonstrated superior efficacy in mitigating glomerular hyperfiltration while concurrently reducing heart failure risk, improving existing heart failure through cardiac load reduction and preventing new-onset cases [137,138]. GLP-1 RAs significantly decrease proteinuria and atherosclerotic cardiovascular events by activating GLP-1 receptors on monocytes, macrophages, and lymphocytes, thereby enhancing atherosclerotic plaque stability [137,139]. MRAs exert therapeutic effects via dual mechanisms: reducing proteinuria and improving heart failure prognosis. When combined, SGLT2i and lower-dose GLP-1 RAs synergistically decrease proximal tubular sodium reabsorption, with the combination therapy providing additional benefits including HbA1c reduction, blood pressure control, body weight management, and uric acid lowering - collectively contributing to attenuated renal function decline [138]. The triple combination of these agents achieves a comprehensive reduction in metabolic, renal, and cardiovascular risks through complementary pharmacological mechanisms.

An observational study of patients with type 2 diabetes [140] revealed that combination therapy with SGLT2i and GLP-1 RAs produced significantly greater reductions in body weight and blood pressure compared to SGLT2i monotherapy, alongside an 83% relative reduction in the incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE). Supporting evidence from a meta-analysis examining MRA and SGLT2i combination therapy for chronic kidney disease [141] showed superior renal outcomes versus SGLT2i alone, including a clinically meaningful −33.6% reduction in urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR; p < 0.001) and a −6.1 mmHg decrease in systolic blood pressure. Most notably, compared to conventional therapy, the triple combination of SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA, and nonsteroidal MRA [142] was associated with a 35% lower risk of cardiovascular adverse events and a substantial 4.5-year extension in progression-free survival among chronic kidney disease patients, highlighting the synergistic benefits of multimodal therapeutic approaches.

Combination therapy offers enhanced protective effects while potentially reducing adverse events. A meta-analysis on diabetic nephropathy demonstrated that SGLT2i significantly decrease serum potassium levels during MRA coadministration, effectively reducing hyperkalemia risk [143]. Subsequent research revealed that MRA-SGLT2i combination therapy carries only 43% of the hyperkalemia risk observed with MRA monotherapy [144]. Importantly, while SGLT2i effectively reduce severe hyperkalemia episodes [145], their impact on MRA-induced mild hyperkalemia appears to be more moderate.

Barriers and challenges to SGLT2i in the setting of the clinic

Existing studies have clearly established the protective effects of SGLT2i in regulating blood pressure, managing body weight, and protecting cardiorenal function [146]. In 2024, the study by Iordan Liana et al. [97] showed that SGLT2i demonstrated significant protective effects in patients with HF, CKD, and diabetes, regardless of glycemic control status, GFR levels, or the severity of proteinuria. Therefore, the following discussion will explore the clinical utility, therapeutic limitations, and implementation challenges of SGLT2i from the perspectives of guideline-directed recommendations, pharmacoeconomic analyses, and safety considerations.

Guideline recommendations and economic cost analysis

The 2024 Standards of Medical Care in Diabetes [147] by the American Diabetes Association (ADA) advocate for the use of SGLT2i in patients with type 2 diabetes and CKD (eGFR ≥20 mL/min/1.73 m2) to slow CKD progression and reduce cardiovascular events. For patients with type 2 diabetes at high cardiovascular risk, the guidelines recommend combining SGLT2i with GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) to achieve glycemic control and reduce cardiovascular risks. This combination therapy has demonstrated superior efficacy in reducing HbA1c and cardiorenal risks compared with monotherapy [139]. Similarly, the 2024 KDIGO Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of Chronic Kidney Disease [148] also recommends SGLT2i as the first-line treatment for patients with type 2 diabetes, CKD, or heart failure, as well as for those with a urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) ≥200 mg/g (≥20 mg/mmol). These recommendations also apply to patients with an eGFR between 20 and 45 mL/min/1.73 m2 and a uACR below 200 mg/g (<20 mg/mmol). All of the above highlight the cardiorenal protective effects of SGLT2i.

However, the study by Kesari et al. (2024) [149] also found that the majority of patients with type 2 diabetes at risk of heart failure are not receiving the recommended SGLT2i therapy. Observational studies have shown [150] that patients hospitalized for heart failure or volume overload with reduced kidney function (eGFR 20–59 mL/min/1.73 m2) have low SGLT2i prescription rates at discharge, especially among older patients and those with worse renal function. This shortfall is primarily attributed to clinicians’ limited familiarity with these agents and concerns about potential safety issues, which have led to suboptimal utilization rates of SGLT2i. Further exacerbating this issue is the fact that the clinical use of SGLT2i is also restricted by their historical classification as antihyperglycemic agents. Moreover, according to a study by Chitpim et al. (2025) [151], the use of these drugs also increases the economic burden on patients. These combined factors have significantly overshadowed the cardiorenal protective benefits demonstrated by SGLT2i [152].

As the patent protection for current SGLT2i, including dapagliflozin, expires or approaches expiration in multiple regions, generic versions are increasingly being integrated into clinical practice. This transition is expected to significantly reduce drug costs, thereby enhancing the accessibility of SGLT2i to a broader patient population, especially in low- and middle-income countries [153]. Pharmacoeconomic evidence suggests that the introduction of generic SGLT2i could substantially lower the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio, from over $100,000 per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) to less than $10,000 per QALY, markedly improving the cost-effectiveness of the treatment [94]. While this development broadens treatment options for clinicians, it also necessitates addressing ongoing concerns among providers and patients regarding the efficacy and safety of generic medications. There is an urgent need for comprehensive regulatory policies to maintain stringent quality control standards during both generic drug approval and post-marketing surveillance.

Safety and adverse effects

The safety profile analysis of SGLT2i highlights AKI as a clinically significant adverse event, meriting prioritized evaluation. Epidemiological data from critically ill populations reveal markedly higher mortality rates in patients with hospital-acquired AKI (43.18%) compared to those without AKI (14.41%). Advanced age, preexisting comorbidities (including diabetes and ischemic heart disease), and concurrent infections collectively constitute primary risk factors for AKI development [154]. Regarding SGLT2i-associated AKI risk, current evidence indicates that factors such as age, baseline renal function, or concomitant RAAS inhibitor do not significantly alter this pharmacological relationship [155].

Existing evidence shows that the mortality rate in patients with hospital-acquired AKI (43.18%) is significantly higher than that in patients without AKI (14.41%) [154], highlighting the necessity to evaluate whether the use of SGLT2i increases the risk of AKI. It is worth noting that age, baseline renal function, or the use of RAAS inhibitors has not been shown to significantly alter the pharmacological relationship between SGLT2i and AKI risk [156]. A transient decline in eGFR and an increase in serum creatinine are common in the initial phase of SGLT2i treatment, with these hemodynamic changes stemming from its regulatory effect on glomerular pressure [157]. Although these changes are usually reversible and have no direct causal relationship with AKI [158], special attention is needed in the following high-risk populations: (1) Volume-depleted patients: The diuretic effect of SGLT2i may exacerbate renal hypoperfusion [159]; (2) Patients with eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2: A strict assessment of volume status is required; (3) Patients on concomitant diuretics: It is recommended to reduce the diuretic dose by 30–50% at the start of treatment to lower the risk of AKI [150].

Additionally, there are considerations for special populations. Although the use of SGLT2i in transplant recipients, dialysis patients, and individuals receiving intensive immunosuppression presents risks, it also offers significant therapeutic benefits for these populations. For patients on immunosuppressive therapy, while there may be an increased risk of urinary tract infections, SGLT2i can improve renal function and cardiovascular outcomes after transplantation [160]. For dialysis patients (especially those on peritoneal dialysis), SGLT2i enhances ultrafiltration volume and hemoglobin levels. Although SGLT2i treatment is associated with subclinical metabolic acidosis in the study population, it has no significant impact on overall patient survival rates [161]. Additionally, SGLT2i plays a significant role in mitigating common complications in patients with lupus nephritis, specifically by slowing the progression of chronic kidney disease and reducing atherosclerotic cardiovascular risk [162]. It is important to note that the potential renal protective effects of SGLT2i, primarily through the reduction of glomerular pressure, must be carefully balanced with the need for careful volume status assessment prior to treatment initiation. This precaution is particularly crucial for patients with preexisting renal impairment or concurrent diuretic therapy, where volume depletion-induced renal hypoperfusion may paradoxically increase the risk of AKI [163]. Therefore, volume status must be assessed before starting treatment, and renal function monitoring should be intensified in high-risk patients. These findings highlight the need for more research to clarify the optimal use of SGLT2i in different AKI risk populations, balancing its short-term hemodynamic effects with long-term organ-protective benefits [159]. Based on the above discussion of the adverse effects of SGLT2i, clinical decisions should be based on individual assessment, with particular attention to volume status and concomitant medications.

Conclusion

In summary, as an emerging drug, SGLT2i has demonstrated distinctive advantages, whether in the management of heart failure, renal disease, or the prevention of cardiovascular events. However, its therapeutic application has often excluded patients with severe renal insufficiency from the majority of clinical trials. While the ESC guidelines have recommended SGLT2i as a first-line treatment for CRS, the evidence supporting their efficacy in non-diabetic patients with advanced kidney disease remains insufficient and warrants further investigation. Meanwhile, as their use becomes more widespread, the long-term effects as well as potential risks associated with their practical clinical applications must be more thoroughly explored to facilitate a comprehensive evaluation of the efficacy and safety of SGLT2i in chronic CRS. Future studies should prioritize non-diabetic advanced CKD cohorts (eGFR < 20 mL/min/1.73 m2) to validate SGLT2i efficacy and explore long-term organ protection beyond glycemic control.

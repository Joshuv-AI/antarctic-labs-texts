DEPARTMENT OF TRANSPORTATION

Federal Aviation Administration


Proposed Advisory Circular 25.335-1A, Design Dive Speed

AGENCY: Federal Aviation Administration, DOT.

ACTION: Notice of Availability of Proposed Advisory Circular (AC) 
25.335-1A, and request for comments.

-----------------------------------------------------------------------

SUMMARY: This notice announces the availability of and requests 
comments on a proposed advisory circular (AC) which sets forth an 
acceptable means, but not the only means, of demonstrating compliance 
with the provisions of part 25 of the Federal Aviation Regulations 
(FAR) related to the minimum speed margin between design cruise speed 
and design dive speed for transport category airplanes.

DATES: Comments must be received on or before September 15, 2000.

ADDRESSES: Send all comments on the proposed AC 25.335-1A to: Federal 
Aviation Administration, Attention: James Haynes, Airframe and Cabin 
Safety Branch, ANM-115, Transport Airplane Directorate, Aircraft 
Certification Service, 1601 Lind Avenue SW, Renton, WA 98055-4056. 
Comments may be inspected at the above address between 7:30 a.m. and 
4:00 p.m. weekdays, except Federal holidays.

FOR FURTHER INFORMATION CONTACT: Jan Thor, Transport Standards Staff, 
at the address above, telephone (425) 227-2127.

    Interested persons are invited to comment on the proposed AC by

Discussion

    Section 25.335(b) requires the design dive speed, VD, of 
the airplane to be established so that the design cruise speed is no 
greater than 0.8 times the design dive speed, or that it be based on an 
upset criterion initiated at the design cruise speed, VC At 
altitudes where the cruise speed is limited by compressibility effects, 
Sec. 25.335(b)(2) requires the margin to be not less than 0.05 Mach. 
Furthermore, at any altitude, the margin must be great enough to 
provide for atmospheric variations (such as horizontal gusts and the 
penetration of jet steams), instrument errors, and production 
variations. This AC provides a rational method for considering the 
atmospheric variations.

The range of once-reinforced random walk on the half-line
Zechun Hu, Ting Ma, Renming Song, Li Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a once-reinforced random walk on the half-line, and give the limiting behaviors of all the moments of its range.



Keywords:
once-reinforced random walk; range process; half-line.

2020 MR Subject Classification (2020)
60K35

1
Introduction and main result

The once-reinforced random walk (ORRW) was introduced in Davis
[
9
]
and it follows a very simple rule: Initially every edge carries weight
1
, and the walker traverses an edge with probability proportional to its current weight. The first time an edge is traversed, its weight is permanently reinforced
by a positive parameter,
and remains unchanged thereafter. Inspired by the work of Pfaffelhuber and Stiefel
[
20
]
, we study the range of ORRW on the half-line
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and establish asymptotic behaviors of all the moments of the range.

Let
c>0
. An ORRW
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with parameter
c
is a process on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}(X_{n+1}=X_{n}+1\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})

\displaystyle=1-\mathbf{P}(X_{n+1}=X_{n}-1\mid\mathcal{F}_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{if }X_{n}=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }0<X_{n}<R_{n},\\
\frac{1}{1+c}&\text{if }X_{n}=R_{n},\end{array}\right.

where

R_{n}:=\max\{X_{0},X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}.

(1.4)

Note that
R_{n}+1
is the cardinality of the range
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:\exists m\in\{0,\dots,n\}\mbox{ such that }X_{m}=x\}
of the ORRW by time
n
.

The ORRW is closely related to
the linearly edge-reinforced random walk (LERRW). The LERRW
was introduced by Coppersmith and Diaconis
[
8
]
to model the behavior of a random walker who has a “preference” for traversing edges he has been visited before. In this process, each edge initially has weight 1, and each time an edge is traversed, its weight is increased by a fixed positive constant.
The LERRW
satisfies certain partial exchangeability.
A number of results concerning the recurrence and transience of
LERRW
have been obtained (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
11
,
23
,
24
]
). The model has found applications in areas such as biology (
[
19
]
) and social networks (
[
21
]
).

In contrast, the study of ORRW presents greater challenges due to the loss of partial
exchangeability.
Existing research on ORRW has mainly focused on three families of graphs: tree structures (e.g., regular trees, Galton-Watson trees, and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-like trees), ladder graphs (e.g.,
\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1\}
,
\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
with
d\geq 2
,
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
(
\Gamma
is finite)), and integer lattices (e.g.,
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
).

For tree structures, a rich body of work has devoted to phase transitions between recurrence and transience. Durrett et al.
[
10
]
showed that the ORRW is transient on regular trees for any reinforcement parameter
c>1
. This result was extended by Collevecchio
[
3
]
to supercritical Galton-Watson trees. Kious and Sidoravicius
[
15
]
further identified a phase transition on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-like trees. Subsequently, Collevecchio et al.
[
4
]
fully resolved the question of transience/recurrence for ORRW on trees and showed that the critical parameter for recurrence/transience of ORRW coincides with the so-called the branching-ruin number of the tree.
Collevecchio et al.
[
5
]
also studied the speed of once-reinforced biased random walk (ORbRW) on trees, proving that on Galton-Watson trees without leaves, the speed is positive in the transient regime.

For ladder graphs, Sellke
[
22
]
first investigated the ladder
\mathbb{Z}\times\{1,\ldots,d\}
with
d\geq 2
and established almost-sure recurrence for small reinforcement. Later, Vervoort
[
26
]
showed recurrence for all sufficiently large reinforcement parameters. Kious et al.
[
16
]
revisited Vervoort’s unpublished work
[
26
]
and proved recurrence on any graph of the form
\mathbb{Z}\times\Gamma
(with
\Gamma
finite) for sufficiently large reinforcement parameters. Huang et al.
[
14
]
observed that both Sellke
[
22
]
and Vervoort
[
26
]
proved that, for the simplest ladder
\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1\}
, the walk is almost surely recurrent for any reinforcement parameter

c>1/2
. Huang et al.
[
14
]
proved that the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}\times\{0,1\}
is almost surely recurrent for
any
c\in(0,1)
.

For integer lattices, Vervoort
[
26
]
proved that the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}
is almost surely recurrent for any reinforcement factor
c>0
.
For dimensions
d\geq 2
, however, the recurrence or transience of the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
remains an open problem. Sidoravicius
[
15
]
conjectured that the walk is recurrent on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
when
d\in\{1,2\}
. For dimensions
d\geq 3
, he suggested a phase transition: the walk should be recurrent for large reinforcement parameters, and transient for small ones. In addition to these recurrence/transience results, several results on the limit behavior of the ORRW have been obtained. Using Tauberian theory, Pfaffelhuber and Stiefel
[
20
]
obtained asymptotic
behaviors
for all the moments of the range of the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}
. Specifically, the first moment of the range grows asymptotically like
\sqrt{n}
. This confirms the diffusive character of the range on the full line. More recently, Collevecchio and Tarrès
[
6
]
extended
the analysis of
[
20
]

to general graphs via a change of measure formula. They derived large deviation estimates for the range and proved that on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(
d\geq 2
)
the cardinality of the range at time
n
is typically of order
n^{d/(d+2)}
.
However, the technique of Collevecchio and Tarrès
[
6
]

does not apply to
the setting with boundary, such as the half-line.
Recent study on ORRWs on the the half-line
has centerd on limit theorems. Collevecchio et al.
[
7
]
established a law of the iterated logarithm (LIL) for the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, showing

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{cX_{n}}{\sqrt{2n\log\log n}}=1\ \text{a.s.},

where
c>0

is a reinforcement parameter. For further results on reinforced random walks on the half-line, we refer the reader to
[
2
,
13
,
25
]
.

In this paper, motivated by
[
20
]
, we study the range of the ORRW on the half-line, and derive asymptotic behaviors for all the moments of its range.
In this article,
the notation
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
means that
a_{n}/b_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\longrightarrow}}1
. We use
\log
to denote the logarithm with base
e
.
The main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R_{n}
, defined in (
1.4
), be the range of the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with parameter
c>0
. Then,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)^{\ell}\right]\sim\frac{1}{2^{(\ell-2)/2}\Gamma(\ell/2)}\cdot J_{\ell}(c),\quad\ell=1,2,\ldots,

where

J_{\ell}(c):=2^{c}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x}+1}\right)^{c}dx.

In particular,

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}J_{1}(c)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{R_{n}^{2}}{n}\right]\sim J_{2}(c).

Remark 1.2
.

Compared with the asymptotic behaviors of the moments of the range of the ORRW on
\mathbb{Z}
obtained in
[
20
]
, one sees that the asymptotic orders of the moments of the range of the ORRW on

\mathbb{Z}_{+}
are the same as that on
\mathbb{Z}
,
but the coefficients are different. In
[
20
]
,

J_{\ell}(c)=2^{2c}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{e^{x}}{(e^{x}+1)^{2}}\right)^{c}dx.

Remark 1.3
.

The ORRW with
c=1
is just the
simple symmetric
random walk on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with the reflection at the origin 0. In this case, we have

J_{1}(1)=2\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x}+1}dx\stackrel{{\scriptstyle y=e^{x}}}{{=}}2\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{y^{2}+1}dy=2\arctan y|_{1}^{\infty}=\frac{\pi}{2}

and

\displaystyle J_{2}(1)

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x}+1}dx=2\int_{0}^{\infty}x\frac{e^{-x}}{1+e^{-2x}}dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-x}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}e^{-2nx}dx=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}xe^{-(2n+1)x}dx

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)^{2}}=2G,

where
G
is the Catalan constant. Hence

\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{R_{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right]\sim\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{R_{n}^{2}}{n}\right]\sim 2G.

2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Our treatment of the range process
R_{n}
is an adaptation of the technique of Pfaffelhuber and Stiefel
[
20
]
.

Let
S_{k}:=\inf\{n:R_{n}=k\}
denote the first time the range of the ORRW
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
reaches
k
. For
i=1,2,...
,
define
T_{i}:=S_{i+1}-S_{i}
as the time between the first instant the range
R_{n}
equals
i
and the first instant it equals
i+1
. Note that
T_{i}=1
with probability
1/(1+c)
.
Otherwise, the walk
X
returns to
i-1
, and subsequently moves within
\{0,...,i\}
until it again attains the range
i
. Denote this duration by
\tau_{i}
.

\tau_{i}
is the hitting time of
i
for a simple random walk with a reflecting barrier at
0
(i.e., the walk moves to
1
at the next step upon hitting 0) starting from
i-1
.
Upon reaching
i
, the walk moves to
i+1
with probability
1/{(1+c)}
, thereby increasing the range.
If instead it returns to
i-1
, and the procedure for again reaching
i
from
i-1
(as described above) repeats independently. The trial of advancing from
i
to
i+1
is thus repeated until it succeeds.
The number
Y_{i}
of independent attempts needed to first achieve a transition from
i
to
i+1
follows a geometric distribution
with a success probability
1/(1+c)
. That is,
\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}=j)=(\frac{c}{c+1})^{j}\frac{1}{c+1}
for
j=0,1,2,...
. In total, this gives

S_{k}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}T_{i},\quad k=1,2,\ldots\quad\text{with}\quad T_{i}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{Y_{i}}(1+\tau_{i}^{j}),\quad i=1,2,\ldots,

(2.1)

where we define the empty sum to be
0
. Here,
\tau_{i}^{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
are independent
copies of
\tau_{i}
and are also independent of the sequence
Y_{i},i=1,2,\ldots
.

2.1
Some preliminaries

Before proving Theorem
1.1
, we first make some preparations.
We begin by recalling the classical Tauberian result by Hardy and Littlewood

[
17
, Chapter I. Theorem 7.4]

(see also
[
18
, Proposition 12.5.2]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\geq 0
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}
converges for
|x|<1
. Suppose that for some
\alpha,A\geq 0
,

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle x\uparrow 1}}{{\sim}}\frac{A}{(1-x)^{\alpha}}.

Then,

\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\rightarrow\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{A}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}n^{\alpha}.

Moreover, if
\alpha>1
, and
n\mapsto a_{n}
is nondecreasing,

a_{n}\sim\frac{A\alpha}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}n^{\alpha-1}.

(2.2)

The following lemma gives the generating function of the first hitting time in a simple symmetric random walk with a reflecting barrier. Its proof adapts the classical “ruin problem” method (see e.g.,
[
12
, Chapter XIV.4]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a,b,x\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a\geq 0
and
-a\leq x\leq b
.
Consider a simple symmetric random walk
Z=(Z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
starting at
x

on the state space
\{-a,-a+1,...\}
with a reflecting barrier at
-a
, defined by the rule: if
Z_{n}=-a
, then
Z_{n+1}=-a+1
.
Let
T_{b}:=\inf\left\{n\geq 0:Z_{n}=b\right\}
denote the first hitting time of
b
. Then for any
s\in(0,1)

\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[s^{T_{b}}\right]=\frac{r_{s}^{x}+r_{s}^{-(2a+x)}}{r_{s}^{b}+r_{s}^{-(2a+b)}},

(2.3)

where

r_{s}:=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}{s}.

(2.4)

Proof.

Let

U_{x}(s):=\mathbb{E}_{x}[s^{T_{b}}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{x}(T_{b}=n)s^{n}.

Then for
-a<x<b
,

U_{x}(s)=\frac{s}{2}U_{x+1}(s)+\frac{s}{2}U_{x-1}(s)

(2.5)

and

U_{-a}(s)=sU_{-a+1}(s),\quad U_{b}(s)=1.

(2.6)

We now use the method of
[
12
, Chapter XIV.4]
to solve the difference equation (
2.5
).
We first look for particular solutions of the form
U_{x}(s)=r^{x}(s)
.
Substituting this expression into (
2.5
), we get
that
r(s)
must satisfy the quadratic equation

r(s)=\frac{s}{2}r^{2}(s)+\frac{s}{2},

(2.7)

which has two roots

r_{1}(s)=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}{s},\quad r_{2}(s)=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-s^{2}}}{s},\quad s\in(0,1),

satisfying
r_{1}(s)\cdot r_{2}(s)=1
. It is easy to check that

1-sr_{1}(s)={sr_{1}^{-1}(s)-1}.

(2.8)

The same identity also holds for
r_{2}(s)
.

According to
[
12
, Chapter XIV.2]
,
the general solution can be written as

U_{x}(s)=A(s)r_{1}^{x}(s)+B(s)r_{2}^{x}(s),

(2.9)

where
A(s)
and
B(s)
are
functions of
s
. The boundary conditions (
2.6
) yield

A(s)r_{1}^{-a}(s)(1-sr_{1}(s))=B(s)r_{2}^{-a}(s)(sr_{2}(s)-1),

(2.10)

and

A(s)r_{1}^{b}(s)+B(s)r_{2}^{b}(s)=1.

(2.11)

Using the relation
r_{2}(s)=1/r_{1}(s)
and (
2.8
), equation (
2.10
) simplifies to

A(s)=B(s)r_{1}^{2a}(s).

(2.12)

Substituting
r_{2}(s)=1/r_{1}(s)
into (
2.11
) gives

A(s)r_{1}^{b}(s)+B(s)r_{1}^{-b}(s)=1.

(2.13)

Solving the linear system (
2.12
) and (
2.13
) for
A(s)
and
B(s)
, we get

A(s)=\frac{1}{r_{1}^{b}(s)+r_{1}^{-(2a+b)}(s)},\quad B(s)=\frac{1}{r_{1}^{2a+b}(s)+r_{1}^{-b}(s)}.

(2.14)

Finally, inserting (
2.14
) into (
2.9
) and replacing
r_{2}(s)
by
1/r_{1}(s)
, we easily obtain (
2.3
) .
∎

(2.15)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\tau_{i}
and
T_{i}
be defined in (
2.1
),
g_{x}(\cdot)
and
G_{x}(\cdot)
be defined in (

for

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[s^{\tau_{i}}\right]=g_{i}(s),\quad\mathbb{E}\left[s^{T_{i}}\right]=G_{i}(s).

(2.16)

Moreover, the generating function of
S_{k}
is given by

\mathbb{E}\left[s^{S_{k}}\right]=s\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}G_{i}(s),\quad k=1,2,\ldots.

(2.17)

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2.2
with
a=0

and

For the generating function of

Y_{i}\sim\operatorname{geo}(1/(1+c))
is

\mathbb{E}\left[s^{Y_{i}}\right]=\frac{1}{c+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{cs}{c+1}\right)^{k}=\frac{1}{1+c-cs}.

Using the independence of
\{Y_{i}\}

\mathbb{E}\left[s^{T_{i}}\right]=s\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{Y_{i}}s^{1+\tau_{i}^{j}}\mid Y_{i}\right]\right]=s\mathbb{E}\left[\left(sg_{i}(s)\right)^{Y_{i}}\right]=\frac{s}{1+c-csg_{i}(s)}.

Finally, using (

) we get

\mathbb{E}\left[s^{S_{k}}\right]=s\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}s^{T_{i}}\right]=s\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[s^{T_{i}}\right]=s\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}G_{i}(s).

This completes the proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

For
s\in(0,1)

H_{\ell}(s):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}s^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[R_{n}\cdots\left(R_{n}+\ell\right)\right]=\frac{\ell+1}{1-s}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\cdots(k+\ell-1)s\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}G_{i}(s).

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
H_{\ell}
for

. Then,

H_{\ell}(s)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\uparrow 1}}{{\sim}}K_{\ell}(1-s)^{-(\ell+3)/2},

where

(2.19)

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
a_{n}:=\mathbb{E}[R_{n}\cdots(R_{n}+\ell)]

H_{\ell}(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}s^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s\uparrow 1}}{{\sim}}\frac{K_{\ell}}{(1-s)^{(\ell+3)/2}},

Combining (

, the coefficient

Consequently, for every integer

\mathbb{E}[R_{n}^{\ell+1}]\sim\frac{2^{c}}{2^{(\ell-1)/2}\Gamma((\ell+1)/2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}\left(\frac{e^{x}}{e^{2x}+1}\right)^{c}dx\cdot n^{(\ell+1)/2}.

The proof is complete.

20

. Recall
r_{s}
is defined in (

).
Let
d_{s}=\log r_{s}
.
We can then rewrite (

\displaystyle g_{x}(s)=\frac{e^{(x-1)\log r_{s}}+e^{(1-x)\log r_{s}}}{e^{x\log r_{s}}+e^{-x\log r_{s}}}=\frac{\cosh(xd_{s}-d_{s})}{\cosh(xd_{s})}=\cosh d_{s}-\tanh(xd_{s})\sinh d_{s},

\cosh(u+v)=\cosh u\cosh v+\sinh u\sinh v

\cosh d_{s}=\frac{r_{s}+r^{-1}_{s}}{2}=\frac{1}{s}.

(3.2)

(3.3)

\displaystyle f_{t}:=d_{1-t}

Applying the second-order Taylor expansion of

\displaystyle f_{t}

\displaystyle=t+\sqrt{t(2-t)}-\frac{t}{2}(2-t)+O(t^{3/2})

\displaystyle g_{x}(1-t)

\displaystyle=1-\phi(2\sqrt{2t}x)\sqrt{2t}+O(t),

\displaystyle G_{x}(1-t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-t}{1+c\phi(2xf_{t})\sqrt{t(2-t)}}

\displaystyle=1-c\phi(2\sqrt{2t}x)\sqrt{2t}+O(t).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{k-1}G_{i}(1-t)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log G_{i}(1-t)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log\left(1-c\phi(2\sqrt{2t}i)\sqrt{2t}+O(t)\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}-c\phi(2\sqrt{2t}i)\sqrt{2t}+O(t)\right\}.

We
now deal with the series in (

\displaystyle H_{\ell}(1-t)t^{\frac{\ell+3}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell+1}{2^{(\ell+1)/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\ell}\exp\left(-c\int_{0}^{x}\phi(2y)dy\right)dx\cdot(1+o(t)),

Using a simple change of variables, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\phi(2y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{e^{2y}-1}{e^{2y}+1}dy=\int_{1}^{e^{2x}}\frac{z-1}{z+1}\cdot\frac{1}{2z}dz=\frac{1}{2}\int_{1}^{e^{2x}}\left(\frac{2}{z+1}-\frac{1}{z}\right)dz

\displaystyle=\log(z+1)|_{1}^{e^{2x}}-\frac{1}{2}\log z|_{1}^{e^{2x}}=\log(e^{2x}+1)-\log 2-x.

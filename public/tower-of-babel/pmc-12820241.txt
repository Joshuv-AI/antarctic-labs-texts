E2F1-mediated PKMYT1 upregulation promotes prostate cancer progression by inhibiting the PPAR signaling pathway
Authors: Zengshun Kou, Yu Wang, Jiaxi Zhu, Shuaizhi Zhu, Zi’ang Si, Hai Zhu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractProtein kinase membrane associated tyrosine/threonine 1 (PKMYT1) is a protein-coding gene associated with cell cycle regulation and cancer development, but its specific mechanism in prostate cancer (PCa) has not been clarified. This study sought to elucidate the role of PKMYT1 in PCa. Expression patterns, prognostic significance and potential mechanisms of PKMYT1 were explored by the TCGA database. Single-cell sequencing was performed using the GSE137829 dataset. Multi-database prediction identified potential transcription factors regulating PKMYT1 expression. PC3 cell line with PKMYT1 knockdown was established. Functional analyses (CCK-8, Wound healing, and Transwell assays) were performed to investigate the changes in tumor malignant behavior after PKMYT1 silencing. Western blot experiments were performed to analyze the effects of PKMYT1 on epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) and PPAR signaling pathway. PKMYT1 was overexpressed in prostate cancer samples and its high expression was significantly associated with poor prognosis and Th2 cell infiltration. Knockdown of PKMYT1 could effectively inhibit the proliferation, migration, and EMT process of PCa cells. Mechanistically, E2F1 is an important factor regulating PKMYT1 expression, and PKMYT1 could inhibit the activity of the PPAR signaling pathway, thus ensuring the reinforcement of PCa progression. PKMYT1 can accelerate the progression of PCa by regulating the cell cycle, EMT process and PPAR signaling pathway. Introduction

Prostate cancer (PCa) is one of the most common malignant tumors of the male genitourinary system and the second leading cause of cancer deaths in men1. For limited prostate cancer, radical prostatectomy is considered the gold standard of treatment. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) is the basis of treatment for advanced PCa2. However, as the disease progresses, most patients treated with ADT inevitably develop castration-resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) after 2 years3. Therefore, there is an urgent need to find new therapeutic targets and potential mechanisms for prostate cancer to improve patient prognosis.

Protein kinase membrane associated tyrosine/threonine 1 (PKMYT1), a member of the WEE kinase family, prevents cells from entering mitosis from G2 phase by phosphorylating the threonine-14 (Thr14) and tyrosine-15 (Thr15) sites of cell cycle protein-dependent kinase 1 (CDK1), which inhibits CDK1 activity, or by binding to the CDK1/CCNB complex in order to limit the nuclear translocation of the complex, thereby preventing cells from moving from G2 phase into mitosis (M phase) and ultimately negatively regulates the cell cycle4,5. Abnormal regulation of the cell cycle plays a critical function in tumorigenesis and development6. As a key factor in cell cycle regulation, PKMYT1 has been found to be closely associated with the development of various cancers7. PKMYT1 modified by m6A of ALKBH5 can be recognized and bound by the “reader” protein IGF2BP3, which promotes gastric cancer metastasis8. PKMTY1 promotes hepatocellular liver cancer progression by activating β-connexin/TCF signaling through binding to GSK3-β. In addition, PKMYT1 promotes cancer stem cell maintenance by inhibiting β-TrCP1-mediated ubiquitin degradation of β-connexin, leading to enhanced tumorigenesis9. With the successful clinical application of CDK4/6 inhibitors (e.g., Palbociclib), targeting the cell cycle has become an effective anti-cancer strategy10. PKMYT1 inhibitor RP-6306 inhibits the growth of CCNE1-amplified tumor cells, and can be used to treat various solid tumors11,12. PKMYT1 plays an important role in prostate cancer occurrence and development13. However, the specific mechanism of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer has not been thoroughly explored, and further studies are needed to reveal its potential function.

Metabolic reprogramming is an important feature of tumor cells14. The peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor signaling pathway plays a key role in metabolic reprogramming by regulating fatty acid catabolism and energy homeostasis, as well as cellular differentiation, inflammation15. PPARA, PPARD and PPARG are the three subtypes of receptors. PPARA is induced by AR inhibition and is involved in intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) production via PKC signaling and increases cellular sensitivity to the antiandrogen enzalutamide in prostate cancer cells16. PPARD functions in both a DNA-binding-dependent and ligand-independent manner, and in prostate cancer it triggers the non-dependent inhibition of ligands of the trefoil factor family (TFF), thereby exerting tumor suppressor functions17. PPARG agonist pioglitazone not only regulates blood glucose, but also reduces the risk of prostate cancer18. However, the contribution of the PPAR signaling pathway to tumorigenesis is uncertain and has been controversial.

In this study, we found that PKMYT1 could promote PCa progression by regulating the cell cycle, EMT process and the PPAR signaling pathway. Experimental results showed that deletion of PKMYT1 could promote the expression of PPAR pathway-related proteins. This finding provides new clues for an in-depth understanding of the mechanism of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer and may provide new strategies for targeted therapy.

Materials and methods

Data sources and processing

RNA-seq data of prostate adenocarcinoma (PRAD) were downloaded and organized from the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) and extracted in TPM format. The data matrix was organized using R (v3.6.3), and the expression of PKMYT1 and corresponding clinical information were extracted. The PRAD samples were divided into two groups according to the median expression level of PKMYT1. Differential expression and correlation analyses of prostate cancer data in the TCGA database were performed using “limma” package. Meanwhile, the expression information of GSE46602 dataset was downloaded and analyzed for differential expression19. All results were visualized by “ggplot2” package.

Single-cell sequencing analysis

The data related to the single-cell sequencing matrix GSE137829 chip were downloaded from the GEO database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/), and the sequencing data were down-categorized and clustered using the UMAP technique20. In the UMAP plot, the position of each cell type reflected its expression characteristics and relationship with other cell types. Next, the expression data of PKMYT1 gene was visualized and analyzed. The color scale indicates the expression level of the gene, with red indicating higher expression and gray indicating lower expression. Each point in the graph represents an individual cell.

Survival analysis of PKMYT1

Kaplan-Meier (KM) survival analysis was performed on the clinical information data from the TCGA database using the “survival” package. ROC analysis was performed using the “pROC” package, and AUC values were calculated to evaluate the efficacy of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer. Meanwhile, proportional risk hypothesis testing was performed using the “survival” package, and further Cox regression analysis was performed. RMS package was used to create a Nomogram containing factors such as pathological T-stage, M-stage, Gleason score and PKMYT1 expression level.

Clinical samples and immunohistochemistry (IHC)

All prostate cancer tissues were obtained from surgical patients at Qingdao Municipal Hospital. All specimens were confirmed by pathologic diagnosis and stored on ice at -80 °C. This study was approved by the Ethical Review Committee of Qingdao University, and all patients signed informed consent (QDU-AEC-2024697). The clinical information of these patients is shown in Supplementary Material 1 Table 1.

All specimens were fixed (10% formalin-fixed for 24 h), embedded (paraffin-embedded), thin-sectioned (2–3 μm), and finally adhered to slides. After deparaffinization and hydration, antibody repair was performed, and the expression of PKMYT1 protein in the tissues was detected using anti-PKMYT1 antibody. The staining results were observed under a light microscope and images were captured.

Immune correlation analysis of PKMYT1

Based on the ssGSEA algorithm provided in the GSVA package, immune cell infiltration was computed using markers for 24 immune cells provided in the Immunity article21. Spearman correlation analysis was performed for PKMYT1 and immune cell infiltration. After grouping by median PKMYT1, enrichment scores for different immune cells were calculated.

Based on the CIBERSORT core algorithm, the markers of 22 immune cells provided by the CIBERSORTx website (https://cibersortx.stanford.edu/) were used to calculate the immune infiltration22. The percentage of different immune cells in each subgroup was calculated, and the statistical data were plotted in a stacked bar chart using the ggplot2 package.

Correlation analysis and enrichment analysis

PKMYT1 protein interaction data were extracted from the String database (https://string-db.org) and protein interaction networks were constructed using the Cytoscape (v3.10.1). PKMYT1 gene interaction data were extracted from the GeneMANIA database (https://genemania.org/), and gene interaction maps were constructed. We extracted 100 genes related to PKMYT11 from the GEPIA database, and performed geneontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) analyses of these genes using the “clusterProfiler, GOplot” package. Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed on the results of gene differential analysis of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, and the reference gene sets used for GSEA were KEGG and Hallmarks Pathway Database.

Prediction of transcription factors

The binding site of E2F1 to PKMYT1 was predicted using the Cistrome DB database (http://cistrome.org/db/#/). The promoter sequence of PKMYT1 was obtained from NCBI and compared in the Jasper database to explore the sequences where E2F1 might bind to the PKMYT1 promoter. Larger base letters in the graph indicate a higher likelihood of binding for that base.

Cell cultures and transfections

All prostate cancer cell lines were purchased from National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures (NCACC) and cultured according to the recommended medium. PC-3 cells were inoculated in 6-well plates and transfected when the cells grew to 30% confluence. An appropriate amount of lentiviral vector (Gikai, China) 100 µL was taken and mixed with an appropriate amount of transfection reagent Lipofectamine 2000 for infection treatment. The cell status was observed 12 h after infection, and gene expression was detected using fluorescence microscopy, and a fluorescence rate of 70% or more was considered successful for transfection. Sh-RNA design information is detailed in the Supplementary Material (Supplementary Material 1, Table 2).

Real-time qPCR and Western blot assay

Total RNA was extracted from cell lines using the Trizol kit, and the concentration and purity of each sample were determined using a spectrophotometer. The cDNA was synthesized using the Promega M-MLV kit (Protocol, China) and subjected to real-time quantitative PCR. The complete sequences of the primers used are shown in Supplementary Material 1, Table 3.

RIPA lysate was applied to fully lyse the cells and collect total protein. A 10% SDS-PAGE gel was prepared and 10 µg/well of protein sample was loaded into the gel wells. After performing electrophoresis, the proteins were transferred to a PVDF membrane and closed for 2 h. Incubation was performed using specific primary and secondary antibodies and subsequently visualized by chemiluminescent imaging system. The antibodies used for the experiments are detailed in Supplementary Material 1, Table 4.

CCK-8 assay

Stably transfected PC3 cells were inoculated in 96-well plates and incubated for 24 h. CCK-8 reagent (10 µL/well, BB-4202-500T; BestBio, Nanjing, China) was added, and incubated for another 2 h. Then, the absorbance (A) value was measured at 450 nm using an enzyme marker (Stat Fax-4200, USA). Survival curves were constructed using GraphPad Prism v. 9.0.

Wound healing assay

Cultivate PC-3 cells under suitable culture conditions until the cells are born to 90% fusion. Perform scratching, the width of the scratch should be consistent. Wash the cells gently with PBS and continue the culture. Record the healing of scratches at different time points (0 h, 8 h and 24 h). Migratory area was assessed using the Celigo cell analysis system to quantify the migratory capacity of the cells.

Transwell migration assay

A serum-free cell suspension was prepared and added slowly drop by drop to the center of the upper Transwell chamber, which was then placed in a 24-well plate (100 µL of serum-free medium was added to each well). Serum-containing medium was added to the lower chamber, and the upper and lower cultures were separated by a polycarbonate membrane. The whole device was incubated at 37 °C for 24 h. At the end of incubation, cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde fixative and then stained with 10% Giemsa solution. Finally, the number of invaded cells was observed and counted with a light microscope.

Results

PKMYT1 is overexpressed in prostate cancer

To explore the clinical manifestations of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, we performed expression difference analysis using TCGA and GEO databases. In the TCGA-PRAD data, PKMYT1 expression was significantly higher in the tumor group than in the normal group (Fig. 1A); this result was validated in PRAD and its paired paracancerous tissues (Fig. 1B). In the GSE46602 dataset, PKMYT1 expression was similarly increased in prostate cancer tissues (Fig. 1C). By single-cell sequencing analysis, we classified the cells in the GSE137829 data into 11 groups, and PKMYT1 was mainly expressed in malignant tumor cells (Fig. 1D-F). Next, we integrated the expression information from TCGA and GTEx databases to analyze the expression of PKMYT1 in different cancers. The results showed that PKMYT1 was highly expressed in 28 cancer types, with lower expression only in Acute Myeloid Leukemia (LAML) and Testicular Germ Cell Tumors (TGCT) (Fig. 1G).

PKMYT1 is overexpressed in prostate cancer: A: In the TCGA-PRAD data, PKMYT1 in the tumor group was significantly higher than that in the normal group; B: PKMYT1 expression in prostate cancer and its paired paracancerous tissues. C: PKMYT1 was significantly increased in the tumor group in the GSE46602 dataset; D-F: Cells in the GSE137829 data were categorized into 11 taxa by single-cell sequencing analysis, and PKMYT1 was mainly expressed in malignant tumor cells; G: The expression level of PKMYT1 in different cancers. * P < 0.05; ** P < 0.01; *** P < 0.001.

PKMYT1 is associated with poor prognosis in prostate cancer

Based on TCGA database, kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed that the high expression group had significantly lower overall survival (OS) and disease-specific survival (PFS) (Fig. 2A-B). Moreover, the expression of PKMYT1 gradually increased with the progression of tumor malignancy (e.g., clinical stage, PSA level and Gleason score) (Fig. 2D-F). Diagnostic ROC curve analysis showed that PKMYT1 had high diagnostic efficacy in TCGA-PRAD with an AUC value of 0.811 (Fig. 2C). To further investigate the application of PKMYT1 in prognosis, we constructed a column-line graph for predicting OS in patients with TCGA-PRAD. The Nomogram graph integrated the factors of pathological T and M staging, Gleason Score and PKMYT1, and was able to efficiently predict the probability of OS in patients at 1 year, 3 years, and 5 years (Fig. 2G). Immunohistochemical experiments confirmed that the expression level of PKMYT1 increased significantly with the elevation of clinical T stage (Fig. 2H, the clinical information is shown in Supplementary Material 1 Table 5).

PKMYT1 is associated with and poor prognosis: A-B: K-M survival curves for OS and PFS in high and low expression groups; C: Diagnostic ROC curves of PKMYT1 expression based on TCGA database; D-F: Expression of PKMYT1 in different clinical stages (e.g., clinical stage, PSA level, and elevated Gleason Score); G: Nomogram used to predict OS in patients; H: IHC of PKMYT1 in different clinical T-stage samples.

PKMYT1 is closely associated with Th2 cell infiltration

The tumor microenvironment (TME) plays a crucial role in cancer progression, and immune cells, as an important part of the TME, regulate tumorigenesis and progression23. We found that the correlation between Th2 cells and PKMYT1 was much higher than that of other immune cells, with a correlation coefficient of 0.523 (Fig. 3A-B). In addition, the enrichment score of Th2 cells was significantly higher in the presence of high PKMYT1 expression (Fig. 3C-D). Finally, we divided into two groups based on PKMYT1 expression and analyzed the percentage of different immune cells in each group (Fig. 3E). The oncogenic ability of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer may be closely related to the infiltration of Th2 cells.

Immune correlation analysis of PKMYT1: A: Lollipop chart of correlation between PKMYT1 and different immune cell infiltration; B: Scatter plot of correlation between Th2 cells and PKMYT1. C: Box plots of immune cell enrichment scores in PKMYT1 high and low expression groups; D: Box plots of enrichment scores of Th2 cells; E: Percentage of different immune cells in high and low subgroups.

Knockdown of PKMYT1 inhibits malignant behavior of prostate cancer cells

The expression of PKMYT1 was significantly elevated in a variety of prostate cancer cell lines, with the highest expression in PC-3 cell line (Fig. 4A). We constructed a PC-3 cell line with stable silencing of PKMYT1. CCK-8 experiments showed that the growth rate of PKMYT1 knockdown group was significantly lower than that of the control group (Fig. 4B). Wound-Healing Assay showed that the migratory ability of PKMYT1 knockdown group was significantly reduced (Fig. 4C). Transwell assay demonstrated that silencing of PKMYT1 resulted in a significant decrease in the invasive and migratory ability of PC-3 cells (Fig. 4D-E). The epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a critical initial step through which cancer cells acquire migratory and invasive properties. Next, the effect of PKMYT1 knockdown on the EMT process was assessed by Western Blot assay, and the expression of E-Cadherin protein was increased and the expression of Vimentin protein was decreased after PKMYT1 silencing (Fig. 4F). In summary, inhibition of PKMYT1 expression can effectively inhibit the malignant progression of prostate cancer.

Silencing PKMYT1 inhibits the malignant behavior of prostate cancer cells: A: PKMYT1 expression in various prostate cancer cell lines. B: CCK8 assay showed that the growth rate of PKMYT1 knockdown group was lower; C: Wound-Healing assay showed the migratory ability of PKMYT1 KD and NC group. D-E: Transwell assay showed that the invasion and migration ability of PC-3 cells was significantly decreased after silencing PKMYT1; F: PKMYT1 silencing increased the expression of E-Cadherin protein and decreased the expression of N-Cadherin and Vimentin.

E2F1 is a potential transcription factor for PKMYT1

GSEA analysis showed that PKMYT1 was significantly enriched in the E2F Targets pathway (Fig. 5A); therefore, we predicted that E2F1 might be a transcription factor for PKMYT1. In order to verify the regulatory effect of E2F1 on PKMYT1, firstly, we predicted the binding sites of E2F1 and PKMYT1 in Cistrome DB database. The results showed that E2F1 had a significant binding peak in the PKMYT1 promoter region (Fig. 5B). Next, we obtained the promoter sequence of PKMYT1 from NCBI and compared it in the Jasper database to find two possible binding sequences of E2F1 (Fig. 5C). Among them, the highest rated binding site was site 2, and its binding site is shown in Fig. 5D.

D: The binding site with the highest binding score. E: Scatter plot of correlation between E2F1 and PKMYT1 according to the UALCAN database; F: In TCGA-PRAD database, the expression of E2F1 was higher in the tumor group; G: The expression of E2F1 showed an increasing trend with increasing Gleason score; H-I: IHC of E2F1 in different clinical specimens.

According to the UALCAN database, we found that E2F1 was highly correlated with PKMYT1 in prostate cancer (Fig. 5E, R = 0.89). In the TCGA-PRAD database, the expression of E2F1 in the tumor group was significantly higher than that in the normal group, and the expression of E2F1 tended to increase as the Gleason score increased (Fig. 5F-G). In addition, by analyzing prostate cancer specimens, we confirmed that E2F1 stained more intensely immunohistochemically in tumor tissues (Fig. 5H-I).

E2F1 promotes prostate cancer progression by upregulating PKMYT1

Through a series of functional experiments, we verified the effect of E2F1 knockdown on prostate cancer. The results of CCK-8 assay showed that the cell proliferation rate in the E2F1 silencing group was significantly lower than that in the control group (Fig. 6A). In the Wound-Healing experiment, the healing degree of the knockdown E2F1 group was significantly slowed down (Fig. 6B-C). Transwell assay indicated that the ability of invasion and migration was significantly inhibited in the E2F1 knockdown group (Fig. 6D-E). The results of Western Blot experiments showed that the expression of PKMYT1 decreased after E2F1 silencing (Fig. 6F). In conclusion, knockdown of E2F1 may inhibit the growth, migration and invasion ability of prostate cancer cells by down-regulating the expression of PKMYT1.

F: Western Blot assay indicated that the expression of PKMYT1 was significantly decreased after E2F1 silencing.

Correlation analysis of PKMYT1

First, by using the STRING database and Cytoscape software, we screened 20 proteins closely related to PKMYT1 and constructed a protein-protein interaction network (Fig. 7A). Then, the 20 genes most closely related to PKMYT1 were obtained through GeneMANIA database and visualized (Fig. 7B). Finally, we performed a correlation analysis based on the TCGA-PRAD dataset, screened 20 genes that were positively correlated with PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, and constructed a correlation heat map (Fig. 7C).

Correlation analysis of PKMYT1: A: The protein-protein interaction network related to PKMYT1 was constructed using STRING database and Cytoscape; B: The 20 genes most closely related to PKMYT1 were obtained through the GeneMANIA database; C: The 20 genes positively correlated with PKMYT1 in TCGA-PRAD were screened and a correlation heat map was constructed; D: KEGG analyses were performed based on 100 genes associated with PKMYT1; E-G: GO analysis of PKMYT1.

We extracted 100 genes related to PKMYT1 from the GEPIA database and performed GO and KEGG enrichment analyses. GO analysis showed that PKMYT1 was closely associated with a variety of biological processes, especially in organelle fission, nuclear division, chromosome segregation, and DNA replication (Fig. 7D). In terms of cellular components, PKMYT1 is closely related to chromosomal region, spindle, condensed chromosome and kinetochore (Fig. 7E). In terms of molecular functions, PKMYT1 was closely related to tubulin binding, microtubule binding and ATP hydrolysis activity (Fig. 7F). KEGG enrichment analysis showed that PKMYT1 was closely related to cell cycle, DNA replication, Fanconi anemia pathway, p53 signaling pathway and other important biological pathways (Fig. 7G).

Gene enrichment analysis of PKMYT1

To further explore the specific mechanism of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, we performed GSEA on the PKMYT1 differential expression results in the TCGA-PRAD database. KEGG enrichment results showed that PKMYT1 exhibited significant enrichment in several pathways closely related to cell cycle and metabolic processes, including Cell Cycle, Porphyrin and Chlorophyll Metabolism, Pentose and Glucuronate Interconversions, Ascorbate and Aldarate Metabolism, and Starch and Sucrose Metabolism pathways (Fig. 8A). Therefore, PKMYT1 may influence the development of prostate cancer cells by regulating cell cycle progression and cell metabolism. HALLMAR enrichment analysis further revealed the association of PKMYT1 with a number of cell cycle-related pathways, specifically E2F Targets, G2/M Checkpoint, Mitotic Spindle, Myc Targets and mTOR Signaling pathway (Fig. 8B). Notably, we found that the PPAR signaling pathway showed significant enrichment and negative correlation when PKMYT1 was highly expressed (Fig. 8C).

PKMYT1 inhibits the expression of PPAR signaling pathway

Through the analysis of TCGA database, we evaluated the expression of PPAR signaling pathway related genes in prostate cancer. The results of expression difference analysis showed that the expression of PPARA, PPARD and PPARG was lower in the tumor group than that in the normal tissue group, suggesting that these genes may play an important role in inhibiting prostate cancer progression (Fig. 8D-F). Moreover, correlation analysis by GEPIA database showed that PKMYT1 was negatively correlated with the expression of PPARA, PPARD and PPARG in prostate cancer, which was consistent with the GSEA prediction (Fig. 8G-I). The results of q-PCR experiments showed that the expression of PPARA, PPARD and PPARG increased after silencing PKMYT1(Fig. 8J). The results of WB experiments again verified that the silencing of PKMYT1 was able to significantly up-regulate the expression of proteins related to the PPAR signaling pathway (Fig. 8K). These results suggest that PKMYT1 may affect the progression of prostate cancer by regulating the PPAR signaling pathway, which provides new insights into its potential function in carcinogenesis.

Discussion

Prostate cancer (PCa) is one of the most common malignant tumors in men, with a particularly high incidence in the elderly population24. Despite significant progress in early screening and treatment, the treatment of the disease still faces many challenges. Novel therapeutic approaches, such as immunotherapies and targeted therapies, are becoming a hot topic of research. Immunotherapies such as immune checkpoint inhibitors (e.g., PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors) and cancer vaccines have achieved initial success in certain cancer types, but their effectiveness in prostate cancer remains limited25. In terms of targeted therapies, the problem of resistance to ADT remains a major challenge in the treatment of prostate cancer26,27. An interesting research direction is targeted cell cycle therapy6. Cell cycle-related proteins and signaling pathways, especially CDK4/6, E2F transcription factors, and cell cycle checkpoints, are being extensively studied for treatment28. In addition, metabolic reprogramming, a feature of tumor cells, has been considered an important therapeutic pathway in recent years29. Therefore, diversified therapeutic strategies combining cell cycle regulation, metabolic reprogramming, and immunotherapy may open new horizons for the treatment of advanced prostate cancer.

PKMYT1 plays a crucial role in the regulation of the G2/M phase of the cell cycle5. PKMYT1 gene is located on human chromosome 16p13.3 and is a protein coding gene30. PKMYT1 protein is a kinase containing multiple conserved structural domains, consisting of a catalytic structural domain, a regulatory region, a membrane-bound region, a C-terminal tail and a phosphorylation site. Among them, the catalytic structural domain is a typical serine/threonine kinase structural domain located in the middle portion of the protein, which contains the ATP-binding pocket and catalytic function residues with phosphorylation function, and is a key region for cell cycle regulation7. Currently, the potential value of PKMYT1 is still unclear in Pca. Based on bioinformatics analysis and experimental validation, this study aims to delve into the molecular mechanism of PKMYT1.

First, we performed differential expression analysis of PKMYT1 by multiple databases and further validated it by immunohistochemistry with prostate cancer clinical samples. In the TCGA-PRAD and GSE46602 datasets, we observed that the expression of PKMYT1 was higher in tumor tissues than in normal tissues, this result is in line with the finding of PKMYT1 expression patterns in a variety of tumors (e.g., gastric cancer, hepatocellular carcinoma, etc.)8,31. Immunohistochemistry further validated this result. In order to more comprehensively understand the role of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, we also performed single-cell sequencing analysis, and found that PKMYT1 was highly expressed mainly in tumor cell group, which further suggests that the specific expression of PKMYT1 may be associated with the proliferation and survival advantages of cancer cells. Remarkably, the low expression of PKMYT1 in non-tumor cells further suggests that it may play a specific role in tumor cells and not be prevalent in normal tissues. Survival analysis showed that high expression of PKMYT1 was associated with worse prognosis. Moreover, the expression of PKMYT1 gradually increased with the progression of clinical T-stage and Gleason Scores. Therefore, PKMYT1 is closely associated with malignant progression of prostate cancer and can be used as a poor prognostic marker.

Tumor microenvironment (TME) is one of the key factors influencing tumorigenesis, progression and therapeutic response23. Immune cells, as an important component of the TME, play a double-edged role in tumor immune escape, tumor progression, and therapeutic resistance. In particular, Th2 cells in the CD4 + T cell subset, which play a critical role in the regulation of tumor inflammatory response and immune escape32. In this study, we found the infiltration of Th2 cells was significantly increased with high PKMYT1 expression, suggesting that PKMYT1 may mediate the immune regulation of prostate cancer by promoting the recruitment or activation of Th2 cells. Th2 cells predominantly secrete IL-4, IL-5, and IL-13, which are immunosuppressive cytokines that can accelerate tumor progression by down-regulating anti-tumor immune effects, inhibiting Th1 cell function, and promoting tumor cell survival and invasiveness33.Therefore, targeting Th2 cells may be a potential strategy for prostate cancer treatment. Combined with the role of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer, it can be hypothesized that simultaneous targeting of PKMYT1 and Th2 cells may produce synergistic effects and thus enhance therapeutic efficacy. Although these results provide new perspectives for understanding the interaction of PKMYT1 with TME, how PKMYT1 regulates the infiltration of Th2 cells and whether this regulation is influenced by other signaling pathways requires further validation.

In this study, we comprehensively revealed the critical role of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer progression through a series of functional experiments. The results of CCK-8 assay showed that the proliferative ability of PC-3 cells was significantly decreased after knockdown of PKMYT1, which directly proved that PKMYT1 had an important role in promoting the growth of prostate cancer cells. Wound-Healing and Transwell assays further confirmed that knockdown of PKMYT1 significantly inhibited the migration and invasion ability of PC-3 cells. This finding suggests that PKMYT1 may play a key regulatory role in tumor metastasis of prostate cancer, providing a theoretical basis for its targeted intervention in advanced cancer. In prostate cancer, epithelial mesenchymal transition (EMT) is a key biological process driving tumor cells to acquire invasive and drug-resistant properties34. To further explore the regulatory role of PKMYT1 on the EMT process, we analyzed the expression levels of EMT proteins by Western Blot experiments. The results showed that knockdown of PKMYT1 significantly up-regulated the expression of the epithelial marker protein E-Cadherin, while significantly down-regulating the expression of the mesenchymal marker protein Vimentin. This study not only revealed the multidimensional regulatory effects of PKMYT1 on prostate cancer cell proliferation, migration and invasion, but also clarified for the first time its potential mechanism of promoting tumor progression through EMT.

The transcription factor E2F1 plays an important regulatory role in prostate cancer development35,36. E2F1 can inhibit ubiquitination-mediated degradation of AURKA through sustained activation of HMMR transcription via the mTORC2/AKT pathway, thus forming a trigger for PCa progression37. In addition, E2F1 can also stabilize the expression of EZH2 by upregulating RACGAP1, thus promoting the progression of NEPC38. In this study, we proposed for the first time that PKMYT1 might be a potential target gene of the E2F transcription factor family by gene enrichment analysis, and pinpointed the potential binding site of E2F1 in the promoter region of PKMYT1 using Cistrome DB and Jaspar database. This finding not only revealed that PKMYT1 might be directly regulated by E2F1, but also suggested the key position of E2F1 in regulating the prostate cancer gene network. The expression of E2F1 in tumor tissues was significantly higher than that in normal tissues and was highly positively correlated with that of PKMYT1 (R = 0.89). Functional experiments verified the multiple roles of E2F1 in prostate cancer cells: knockdown of E2F1 significantly inhibited the proliferation, migration, and invasion of PC-3 cells. Western Blot experiments demonstrated that knockdown of E2F1 could lead to a significant decrease in PKMYT1 protein level, further revealing that E2F1 exerts a pro-cancer effect by regulating PKMYT1 expression. Combining experimental data and bioinformatics analysis, we propose that the E2F1-PKMYT1 axis, as an important regulatory pathway in prostate cancer, is not only involved in tumor cell proliferation, migration and invasion, but may also play a role in drug resistance and microenvironmental remodeling. Notably, E2F1 is extensively involved in cell cycle regulation, DNA repair and metabolic networks, and these functions may further amplify its pro-cancer effects through PKMYT1, an axis gene39,40. This study provides a new perspective for understanding the synergistic mechanism of E2F1 and PKMYT1, and future studies should further explore the regulatory mechanisms of the two and clarify whether E2F1 can work as a transcription factor to directly regulate the expression of PKMYT1.

Through multi-level correlation analysis, we comprehensively revealed the potential mechanism of PKMYT1’s role in cancer. First, the protein-protein interaction network constructed using the STRING database and Cytoscape software clearly demonstrated the direct or indirect relationship between PKMYT1 and several key proteins. Further, gene association analysis by GeneMANIA database identified genes closely related to PKMYT1, which may be involved in regulating the functional activities of the same signaling pathway. This networked analysis approach not only revealed the complex interactions of PKMYT1 at the molecular level, but also implied that it might serve as a regulatory core for cell biological processes. Finally, the correlation heatmap constructed by combining the correlation analysis of TCGA-PRAD dataset further visualized the close association between PKMYT1 and specific genes of prostate cancer, which provided important clues to analyze the function of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer. GO enrichment analysis deeply excavated the functional characteristics of PKMYT1, pointing out its key role in important biological processes such as cell division, chromosome segregation and DNA replication. These functional localizations were mainly focused on chromosome regions, spindle and other cell cycle-related cellular components, further supporting the role of PKMYT1 as a core factor in cell cycle regulation. KEGG analysis, on the other hand, revealed the extensive role of PKMYT1 from the perspective of signaling pathways, suggesting that it is involved in key pathways such as the cell cycle, DNA replication, Fanconi anemia pathway and p53 signaling pathway. These pathways are directly related to tumor proliferation, DNA damage repair, and cell survival, emphasizing that PKMYT1 may affect tumor progression through a multidimensional regulatory mechanism. In conclusion, these findings not only highlight the importance of PKMYT1 in cancer biology, but also provide a theoretical basis for exploring its potential as a therapeutic target.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of the TCGA-PRAD database further revealed the potential mechanism of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer. KEGG analysis showed that PKMYT1 was significantly enriched in cell cycle-related pathways and multiple metabolic pathways, including Cell Cycle, Porphyrin and Chlorophyll Metabolism, Pentose and Glucuronate Interconversions, Ascorbate and Aldarate Metabolism, and Starch and Sucrose Metabolism pathways. This suggests that PKMYT1 may promote tumor cell proliferation and growth through the synergistic effect of regulating metabolic pathways and cell cycle progression. The HALLMARK enrichment results further revealed that PKMYT1 was closely related to E2F target genes, G2/M checkpoint, Myc target genes and mTOR signaling pathway, indicating its central position in cell cycle regulation. In addition, PPAR signaling pathway showed significant enrichment when PKMYT1 was low expressed and negative correlation with PKMYT1. The expression of PPARA, PPARD and PPARG was lower in the tumor group than in the normal group, and the deletion of these genes may promote tumor progression. Our experimental results showed that silencing PKMYT1 significantly upregulated the expression levels of PPAR signaling pathway-related genes (PPARA, PPARD, PPARG) and their proteins, suggesting that PKMYT1 may drive tumor progression by inhibiting the activity of the PPAR signaling pathway.

PPAR signaling pathway has an important function in fatty acid metabolism, glucose metabolism and energy homeostasis, and its dysregulation may lead to metabolic reprogramming of tumor cells to adapt to rapid proliferation41. Metabolic abnormalities are one of the hallmark features of tumor development42. Prostate cancer cells usually exhibit unique metabolic properties, such as upregulation of lipid synthesis and enhancement of glycolysis43. By targeting cellular metabolic pathways, such as fatty acid metabolism, glycolysis, and oxidative phosphorylation, it is possible to disrupt the metabolic adaptations of prostate cancer cells, thereby effectively inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis44. Dysregulation of the PPAR signaling pathway may provide a metabolic advantage to PCa cells, driving their rapid proliferation and enhanced invasiveness45. Additionally, GSEA results suggest that PKMYT1 has an important regulatory role in carbohydrate metabolism. PKMYT1 may regulate the metabolic homeostasis of prostate cancer cells and promote the adaptation of cancer cells to abnormal metabolic environments by affecting the PPAR signaling pathway. In conclusion, PKMYT1 not only drives prostate cancer progression by regulating the cell cycle, but may also achieve precise regulation of tumors by affecting metabolic reprogramming. These findings provide new evidence for the role of PKMYT1 in tumor metabolic regulation. However, its specific impact in metabolic reprogramming still needs to be validated by further studies.

This study reveals the oncogenic function of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer and preliminarily links it to the transcription factor E2F1, elucidating its key role in promoting cell proliferation, migration, invasion, and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) process. However, several limitations remain, primarily concerning the incomplete elucidation of the regulatory relationship between E2F1 and PKMYT1. A core limitation is the lack of direct evidence demonstrating that E2F1 transcriptionally regulates PKMYT1. For instance, we were unable to confirm via a dual-luciferase reporter assay that E2F1 binding directly activates the transcriptional activity of the PKMYT1 promoter. Furthermore, experiments such as electrophoretic mobility shift assays (EMSA) or site-directed mutagenesis of key promoter regions to precisely identify the functional E2F1 binding site on the PKMYT1 promoter were not conducted. Despite these limitations, this study unequivocally establishes the critical role of PKMYT1 in prostate cancer progression and provides a novel, evidence-based hypothesis regarding its upstream regulatory mechanism. These findings lay a solid theoretical foundation for targeting PKMYT1 as a potential therapeutic strategy, while the unresolved mechanistic aspects will serve as a starting point for our subsequent research.

Conclusion

PKMYT1 is overexpressed in prostate cancer and is closely associated with tumor progression and poor prognosis and the infiltration of Th2 cells. PKMYT1 can promote the proliferation, migration and invasive ability of tumor cells through the EMT process. E2F1 is an important factor regulating PKMYT1 expression. Further studies revealed that PKMYT1 also promotes tumor growth by inhibiting the activity of PPAR signaling pathway. In conclusion, PKMYT1 accelerates the progression of PCa through multiple mechanisms. Targeting PKMYT1 provides a new perspective for the treatment of prostate cancer.

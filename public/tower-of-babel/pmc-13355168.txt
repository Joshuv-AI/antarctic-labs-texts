Phase I/II Study of NGGT001 Gene Therapy for Bietti Crystalline Dystrophy
Authors: Xiuju Chen, Lin Yang, Gang Wang, Yiting Liu, Guang Qu, Lixin Jiang, Yong Liu, Xiaoxin Li

Bietti crystalline dystrophy (BCD) is a rare autosomal recessive retinal dystrophy caused by variants in the CYP4V2 gene. It is characterized by crystalline deposits, progressive retinal pigment epithelium and choroidal atrophy, and photoreceptor degeneration1. Patients typically develop nyctalopia, reduced visual acuity, and constricted visual fields in early adulthood, leading to legal blindness by the fifth decade2.

CYP4V2 encodes a cytochrome P450 enzyme involved in lipid metabolism. Loss of function in CYP4V2 disrupts lipid processing, resulting in lipid accumulation and retinal degeneration3,4. Multimodal imaging studies have shown that structural abnormalities observed on OCT and fundus autofluorescence (AF) correlate with regional functional deficits on microperimetry5, supporting the use of macular sensitivity as a clinically relevant functional end point in studies of BCD.

Given its monogenic etiology and localized pattern of photoreceptor–retinal pigment epithelium involvement, BCD is well suited for gene augmentation therapy using recombinant adeno-associated viral vectors6, 7, 8, 9. In our previous investigator-initiated trial (IIT), NGGT001 was well tolerated. However, that study primarily enrolled patients with advanced disease, and several patients had off-chart vision at baseline. Additionally, all treated eyes were selected from the worse-seeing eye10. Thus, interpretation of apparent visual acuity improvements was confounded by potential test–retest variability and learning effects. Moreover, other functional end points, including microperimetry and visual field, were frequently unmeasurable in those patients, preventing a quantitative assessment of functional outcomes10.

In clinical practice and in the design of future trials, the performance of commonly used functional end points in patients with relatively preserved vision remains uncertain. Therefore, the present phase I/II study was designed to evaluate the safety and functional outcomes of NGGT001 in patients with BCD who retained measurable baseline vision, including individuals treated in the better-seeing eye. The study focused on changes in best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA) and microperimetry. Post hoc exploratory analyses were also performed to examine the association between foveal autofluorescence status and visual outcomes. These findings may help inform dose selection, end point design, and patient stratification in future randomized controlled trials.

Methods

Study Design

This was a phase I/II open-label, dose-escalation, and dose-expansion clinical trial evaluating the safety and preliminary functional outcomes of NGGT001 gene therapy in patients with BCD. The study was conducted at two tertiary ophthalmic centers in China (Chongqing and Xiamen, Fujian) between March and September 2024.

The trial consisted of three sequential dose cohorts (part A) followed by a dose-expansion phase (part B). In part A, participants were assigned to receive a single subretinal injection of NGGT001 at one of three dose levels: 7.5 × 1010, 1.5 × 1011, or 3.0 × 1011 vector genomes (vg) per eye. Three participants were initially enrolled in each dose cohort. Dose escalation proceeded after a minimum 4-week safety observation period without dose-limiting toxicities. In part B, 3 additional participants were enrolled in a mid-dose expansion cohort, which was selected based on safety and early functional observations in part A.

The study protocol was approved by the institutional ethics committees of Xiamen University Xiamen Eye Center (No. 2023-019-01) and adhered to the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants before enrollment. The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT06706427).

Participants

Eligible participants were adults with genetically confirmed BCD caused by pathogenic variants in CYP4V2. Key inclusion criteria included BCVA ranging from 0.05 to 0.3 (approximately 25 to 60 ETDRS letters)11 and presence of sufficient viable retinal tissue in the posterior pole, indicated by relatively preserved outer retinal structure on OCT volume scan and fundus AF imaging. When both eyes had similar structural preservation, the worse-seeing eye was generally preferred; if the worse-seeing eye showed structural features considered less favorable for treatment, such as disrupted retinal pigment epithelium–Bruch membrane integrity, the fellow eye could be selected.

Key exclusion criteria included ocular conditions that could interfere with surgical procedures or interpretation of functional outcomes, including choroidal neovascularization, active intraocular inflammation, uncontrolled glaucoma, or significant media opacity. Additional criteria are detailed in the study protocol (Supplementary Protocol).

Intervention

Each participant received a single subretinal injection of NGGT001 (0.3 mL total volume) under local or general anesthesia. Injections were performed using a standardized vitrectomy-free, two-port subretinal injection technique by experienced vitreoretinal surgeons12,13. Conversion to pars plana vitrectomy was permitted at the surgeon’s discretion in cases of increased injection resistance or evidence of vitreoretinal traction during subretinal delivery. Postoperative management included topical antibiotics and corticosteroid eye drops according to institutional protocols.

Follow-Up Assessments

Participants were evaluated at baseline (within 56 days before dosing), on day 1, and postinjection at days 7 and 14 and months 1, 2, 3, 6, and 12. Assessments included BCVA using standardized ETDRS charts by certified examiners, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, structural retinal imaging, including OCT and AF (Heidelberg Spectralis OCT), intraocular pressure measurement, microperimetry (MP-3; NIDEK), contrast sensitivity (CS) (Metrovision) testing, multiluminance mobility testing (MLMT), and patient-reported outcomes assessed by a vision-related quality-of-life questionnaire based on a modified version of the National Eye Institute Visual Function Questionnaire–2514, as well as adverse event documentation. Participants were subjected to long-term follow-up for up to 5 years.

Adverse events were graded using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 5.0, and classified according to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities, version 26.0. Ocular inflammation was assessed using the Standardization of Uveitis Nomenclature criteria. Dose-limiting toxicity was defined as any treatment-emergent adverse event (TEAE) occurring within 4 weeks after NGGT001 administration meeting prespecified ocular or systemic severity criteria. Detailed testing protocols for BCVA, microperimetry, and MLMT, as well as retinal integrity assessments, are provided in Supplement 1 (Appendix 1, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Outcome Measures

The primary end point was safety, defined as the incidence, type, and severity of TEAEs through month 12. Secondary end points included changes from baseline in BCVA, the proportion of responders (≥10-letter and ≥15-letter improvement in ETDRS letters from baseline), and mean macular sensitivity on microperimetry. Exploratory end points included MLMT performance, CS, and patient-reported outcomes.

Statistical Analysis

All participants receiving NGGT001 were included in the safety set, and all participants with at least 1 post-treatment efficacy assessment comprised the full analysis set. Continuous variables were summarized using mean (standard deviation) or median (interquartile range [IQR]), as appropriate. Categorical variables were summarized by frequency and percentage. Correlation analyses were exploratory in nature, and missing data at later visits were handled using the last observation carried forward method. Statistical analyses were performed using R software. Given the exploratory design and limited sample size, no formal hypothesis testing for efficacy was prespecified15.

Results

Demographics and Baseline Characteristics

A total of 26 Asian participants with genetically confirmed CYP4V2-associated BCD were screened for eligibility. Twelve participants were enrolled and assigned to three dose cohorts: 7.5 × 1010 vg/eye (n = 3), 1.5 × 1011 vg/eye (n = 6), and 3.0 × 1011 vg/eye (n = 3) between March and September 2024. Two participants (22006 and 23002) underwent vitrectomy during the procedure. All enrolled participants completed 12 months of follow-up except one. One participant in the high-dose cohort withdrew from the study because of personal reasons that was unrelated to treatment-related adverse events. Telephone follow-up reported that her visual acuity was unchanged compared with the month-6 visit (Fig 1).

Baseline demographic and disease characteristics were comparable across dose groups (Table 1). Mean age ranged from 39.2 to 49.0 years, and 5 of 12 (41.7%) participants were male. Eight study eyes were classified as disease stage 2 and four as stage 316. At baseline, mean BCVA was 49.1 (11.1) ETDRS letters in study eyes and 57.5 (19.3) letters in untreated fellow eye. In study eyes, mean macular sensitivity on microperimetry was 1.72 (1.20) dB, and median MLMT performance was 3.0 levels (IQR, 2.0–4.0) levels (Table 1).

Safety Evaluation

During the 12-month follow-up, no serious adverse events or treatment-related adverse events were observed in any dose cohort. All TEAEs were mild and self-limited (Table S1, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org). The most common ocular TEAEs were conjunctival hyperemia, occurring in 3 of 12 eyes (25.0%), and conjunctival hemorrhage occurred in 2 eyes (16.7%). Transient ocular hypertension (26 mmHg) and conjunctival edema were each observed in 1 eye (8.3%). Systemic TEAEs were infrequent, including toothache, influenza, and cervicitis (each 8.3%).

Across all visits, no anterior chamber cells or flare, keratic precipitates, vitreous haze, or retinal vasculitis were observed on slit-lamp biomicroscopy and dilated fundus examination. No cases of cystoid macular edema, macular hole, or procedure-related retinal detachment were detected on OCT.

One patient in the low-dose cohort developed choroidal neovascularization, which was attributed to a pre-existing lesion identified at baseline. The patient reported symptomatic visual decline without treatment approximately 5 years before enrollment. Baseline OCT and OCT angiography are shown in Supplementary Figure S2, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org. This event was classified as not treatment-related. No dose-dependent safety signals were identified.

BCVA Outcomes

Across all treated eyes, BCVA demonstrated modest within-eye increases from baseline during follow-up, with the most consistent numerical changes observed in the mid-dose cohort (1.5 × 1011 vg/eye). In this group, mean BCVA with a baseline of 47.8 (13) letters increased by 11.3 (5.9) letters at month 1, peaked at 14.8 (8.2) letters at month 3, remained 13.3 (8.1) letters at month 6, and declined to 9.1 (11.5) letters at month 12 (Fig 2A). In contrast, the low- and high-dose cohorts exhibited smaller and more variable mean changes, generally not exceeding 5 letters at most time points.

Exploratory analyses demonstrated an inverse association between baseline BCVA and the magnitude of BCVA gain across post-treatment visits, including month 12 (r = –0.62), indicating that eyes with poorer baseline visual acuity tended to exhibit greater numerical improvement (Fig S3, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Responder analyses using prespecified thresholds are shown in Figure 2B, C. In the mid-dose cohort, the proportion of eyes achieving ≥10 and ≥15-letter gains were highest at early visits (4/6 and 3/6 at month 3) and declined by month 12 (2/6 and 1/6). Individual patient trajectories (Fig 2D) indicated that eyes maintaining later improvements were generally those demonstrating early BCVA gains. No sustained ≥10 or ≥15-letter improvements were observed in the low-dose or high-dose cohorts beyond early visits (Fig 2B, C).

Best-corrected visual acuity fluctuations were also observed in fellow eyes, with mean changes of 7.5 (6.4) letters (median, 5.0; IQR, 4–9) at month 6 and 6.9 (6.6) letters (median, 5.0; IQR, 2–9) at month 12. Notably, three participants received NGGT001 in eyes that were not the worse-seeing eye at baseline; in these individuals, BCVA fluctuations were observed in both treated and fellow eyes during follow-up (Table S2, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Microperimetry Outcomes

Fixation stability during microperimetry was assessed as the proportion of fixation points located within the central 2° radius. Median fixation within 2° at baseline was 74%, 89%, and 86% in the low-, mid-, and high-dose cohorts, respectively, and remained similar at month 12 (78%, 91%, and 90%).

Mean changes in macular sensitivity from baseline varied across dose cohorts during follow-up (Fig 3). The mid-dose cohort showed consistent positive changes over time, with mean changes of 1.52 (2.49) dB at month 1, 0.98 (1.85) dB at month 3, 0.86 (2.73) dB at month 6, and 0.73 (2.82) dB at month 12. In contrast, the low-dose cohort showed minimal change throughout follow-up (mean change range, –0.32 to –0.15 dB), while the high-dose cohort demonstrated small and variable changes, reaching 0.45 (1.2) dB at month 12 (n = 2). Individual participant longitudinal microperimetry outcomes for both study and fellow eyes are provided in Supplementary Table S3 available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org. Fellow eyes also showed variability in macular sensitivity across follow-up, with mean changes ranging from approximately 0.3 to 1.5 dB, and a change of 0.60 (2.22) dB at month 12 (Table S3).

Exploratory analyses further revealed a positive association between changes in macular sensitivity and BCVA, with correlation strength increasing over time (r = 0.49–0.84 across months 1–12) (Fig S4, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Exploratory Analysis by Baseline Outer Retinal Structure

Building on baseline structure–function observations, we conducted an exploratory analysis within the mid-dose cohort stratified by baseline residual AF, with corresponding outer retinal preservation on OCT providing supportive structural context (Fig S1, Appendix 2, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Eyes with residual AF showed numerically larger and more sustained BCVA improvements across follow-up, with mean gains ranging from 13.7 to 18.0 ETDRS letters between month 1 and month 12. In contrast, eyes without residual AF demonstrated early BCVA gains through month 6 but smaller gains by month 12 (8.0 letters) (Fig 5A).

A similar pattern was observed for macular sensitivity. Eyes with residual AF showed numerically consistent improvements (1.2–1.4 dB), whereas eyes without residual AF exhibited fluctuations around baseline (–0.63 to 0.10 dB) (Fig 5B).

Additional Functional Outcomes: MLMT, Patient-Reported Outcome, and CS

Multiluminance mobility testing levels range from 0 to 8, with higher levels indicating better mobility performance under lower luminance. Across all treated eyes, median MLMT score increased from 3.0 (IQR, 2.0–4.25) at baseline to 4.5 (3.25–6.50) levels at month 12 across dose cohorts (n = 10). At month 12, 4 of 10 eyes (40%) achieved a ≥2-level improvement, with the highest proportion observed at month 3 (8/11, 72.7%). Interpretation of dose-level differences was limited by missing data at later visits (Fig S5, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Patient-reported outcome, as measured with mean changes in QoL total scores from baseline varied across dose cohorts over time. The mid-dose cohort showed sustained improvements, whereas low- and high-dose cohorts showed declining trends at later follow-up visits (Fig S6, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Results on CS showed heterogeneous changes without a consistent dose-dependent pattern (Fig S7, available at www.ophthalmologyscience.org).

Discussion

In this open-label phase I/II study, subretinal administration of NGGT001 was safe and well tolerated across all dose levels, with no treatment-related serious adverse events and no evidence of intraocular inflammation or retinal toxicity through month 12. Although 1 case of choroidal neovascularization occurred (Table S1), it was associated with a pre-existing subclinical lesion identified at baseline, underscoring the importance of careful baseline structural evaluation. Future studies may benefit from incorporating OCT angiography into screening procedures to facilitate early identification of such lesions.

No clear dose-dependent trend was observed in this study, and consistent with our prior IIT, the mid-dose cohort showed better visual function outcomes than the high-dose cohort, suggesting that efficacy may plateau within the tested dose range rather than increase proportionally with higher doses. Meanwhile, the less favorable responses in the low- and high-dose cohorts may be also explained by small cohort size, baseline heterogeneity. Specifically, 1 participant in low dose experienced choroidal neovascularization recurrence, and 1 participant in high dose withdrew at month-12 visit.

Across functional end points, BCVA and microperimetry demonstrated numerically favorable but heterogeneous trends, with more consistent functional patterns observed in the mid-dose cohort. BCVA gains emerged early after treatment and remained above baseline in most treated eyes through month 12, whereas macular sensitivity showed modest numerical improvements with substantial inter-individual variability. Improvements in MLMT were observed in a subset of participants, suggesting potential gains in mobility vision under varying luminance conditions. Together, these findings provide preliminary functional signals while reflecting the complexity of evaluating treatment response in BCD.

Compared with our prior IIT17, BCVA gains in the present phase I/II cohort appeared more modest and variable. This difference may be related to baseline visual function and interocular symmetry. In the IIT, treated eyes had markedly poorer baseline BCVA than fellow eyes (median, 34 vs. 60 ETDRS letters), which may have increased susceptibility to test–retest variability and learning effects near the lower limit of visual acuity measurement. In contrast, the current study enrolled participants with relatively symmetric baseline visual function (mean, 50 vs. 56 ETDRS letters) and included treatment of better-seeing eyes in three cases.

Similar patterns have been reported in other inherited retinal dystrophies. In a phase I/II gene therapy trial for choroideremia, participants exhibited minimal interocular differences in baseline vision and retinal structure. The treatment was assigned to the worse-seeing eye. Over two years of follow-up, visual acuity in the treated eyes was within 15 letters of baseline18. Likewise, in CEP290-associated Leber congenital amaurosis, clinically meaningful visual acuity gains were observed primarily in participants with off-chart baseline vision, whereas none of the participants with relatively preserved baseline acuity (defined as ≤1.4 logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution) demonstrated improvement in BCVA19. Another study of gene therapy for BCD reported visual gain of 14 letters in the low-dose group. It is worth noting that the participants enrolled in the low dose group had lower baseline BCVA (30 letters)10,20. These observations suggest that lower baseline visual acuity may be associated with greater apparent visual improvement. Interestingly, in these studies, fellow eyes also exhibited parallel BCVA fluctuations during follow-up, consistent with our findings. These findings indicate that baseline disease stage and interocular asymmetry can influence the magnitude and interpretability of observed visual changes in BCD.

Microperimetry provided complementary information regarding localized retinal function, and positive correlations between changes in BCVA and macular sensitivity support internal coherence across functional end points21. However, microperimetry responses exhibited substantial interindividual variability, and bidirectional fluctuations were also present in fellow eyes. This pattern likely reflects the inherent test–retest variability of microperimetry in advanced BCD, particularly in eyes with relatively unstable fixation and patchy residual retinal structure22. As such, while the observed microperimetry changes are biologically plausible and overall consistent with visual acuity outcomes, they should be interpreted as exploratory22 and a run-in phase with one or more practice microperimetry tests prior to baseline assessment is recommended to minimize learning effects.

Multiluminance mobility testing, which assesses functional vision under varying luminance conditions in various inherited retinal dystrophies23, showed improvement in a subset of participants, most notably in the mid-dose cohort, where 2 of 6 eyes achieved a ≥2-score gain at month 12 (Fig S4). Interpretation of dose-level patterns was limited by missing data in the low- and high-dose cohorts, as well as the variability of the test. Some participants with relatively preserved central vision reached max scores at the lowest luminance levels at the early time points during follow-up. Multiluminance mobility testing as an assessment of vision improvement in BCD needs further validation. It could provide a complementary measure of integrated visual function that is not fully captured by visual acuity or microperimetry alone.

An important insight from this study was the relationship between baseline structural preservation and functional stability. Residual foveal AF, supported by corresponding OCT findings, appeared to identify eyes in which functional measures were more stable and interpretable over time. Rather than implying treatment efficacy, this observation suggests that structural biomarkers may play an important role in future patient stratification and end point refinement for BCD gene therapy trials.

Interestingly, some early functional gains appeared to diminish over follow-up. This may reflect the progressive nature of BCD, limited residual retinal reserve in treated eyes, and variability or learning effects in functional testing. The magnitude and durability of response may depend strongly on baseline retinal preservation, with advanced structural loss limiting sustained improvement.

This study has several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. The small sample size limited cross-dose comparisons, and participant heterogeneity likely contributed to interindividual variability. The microperimetry protocol did not include a prebaseline run-in phase; therefore, early learning effects cannot be excluded. The absence of a randomized control group precludes definitive attribution of functional changes to treatment effects, and subgroup analyses based on structural markers require validation in larger controlled studies.

Conclusion

NGGT001 demonstrated a favorable safety profile in this phase I/II study. Functional outcomes showed preliminary numerical signals in BCVA and microperimetry, particularly in the mid-dose cohort, but should be interpreted cautiously in the context of bilateral variability and exploratory efficacy assessment. Randomized controlled trials will be necessary to more definitively characterize treatment effects and refine patient selection strategies in BCD gene therapy.

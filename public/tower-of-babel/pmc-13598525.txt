Multimodality imaging of high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma with diffusion MRI, PSMA PET and dual-layer spectral CT: A case report
Authors: Zane Sherif, Kirralee Sherif, Amanda Forbes
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Multiparametric MRI and prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography (PSMA PET) have become established imaging modalities for the diagnosis and staging of prostate cancer. Dual-layer spectral computed tomography (DLCT) permits retrospective material decomposition and iodine quantification, which may provide additional information regarding tissue iodine distribution and vascularity. The role of spectral CT in primary prostate cancer assessment remains incompletely characterized. We present the case of a 49-year-old man with a prostate-specific antigen (PSA) level of 140 ng/mL who underwent multiparametric MRI, PSMA PET and DLCT as part of his diagnostic workup. Dual-layer spectral CT demonstrated an irregular, approximately L-shaped region of increased iodine concentration within the prostate, with increased conspicuity on 40-keV virtual monoenergetic images (VMI). Subsequent multiparametric MRI demonstrated spatially corresponding restricted diffusion on high b-value imaging, while PSMA PET demonstrated concordant radiotracer uptake. This case illustrates concordant visualization of biopsy-proven high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma across three complementary imaging techniques: restricted diffusion on MRI, PSMA radiotracer uptake on PET, and increased iodine concentration on spectral CT. The observation supports further investigation of quantitative spectral CT parameters in prostate cancer.

Introduction

Prostate cancer is one of the most commonly diagnosed malignancies in men worldwide. Contemporary imaging assessment increasingly relies upon multiparametric magnetic resonance imaging (mpMRI; hereafter referred to as MRI) and prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography (PSMA PET), both of which provide information beyond conventional anatomical imaging. MRI evaluates tissue architecture and diffusion characteristics, with diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) playing a central role in the detection and PI-RADS assessment of clinically significant prostate cancer. High b-value DWI and corresponding ADC reduction reflect restricted water diffusion associated with increased tumor cellularity and altered tissue microstructure [1,2].

PSMA PET provides molecular imaging through assessment of PSMA receptor expression and has become an established tool for staging and assessment of recurrent disease [3]. Together, MRI and PSMA PET provide complementary information regarding tumor morphology and molecular biology.

Dual-layer spectral CT (DLCT) such as the Philips iQon, simultaneously acquires spectral information during routine CT imaging, permitting retrospective generation of material-specific maps, including iodine density maps, and virtual monoenergetic images without prospective selection of a dual-energy acquisition protocol [4]. Despite increasing interest in oncologic applications of spectral CT, its role in the detection and characterization of primary prostate cancer remains incompletely defined [5,6].

We present a case of biopsy-proven high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma with spatially concordant but variably conspicuous abnormalities on diffusion MRI, PSMA PET and retrospectively generated dual-layer spectral CT datasets. The purpose of this report is to illustrate the complementary imaging information provided by these modalities and to describe quantitative iodine measurements within tumor-associated and reference prostatic regions.

Case presentation

A 49-year-old man was referred for investigation following a six-month history of generalized malaise. Initial blood testing demonstrated a markedly elevated serum PSA level of 140 ng/mL. Contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, abdomen and pelvis was subsequently performed using a Philips iQon dual-layer spectral CT system. The CT examination had been requested as part of the investigation of his constitutional symptoms before the prostate malignancy was established.

Spectral CT was performed at 120 kVp using automatic tube current modulation, with an acquisition thickness of 0.8 mm and reconstruction thickness of 0.4 mm. Anteroposterior and lateral scout images were obtained before contrast administration to define the scan range and field of view. A single contrast-enhanced polychromatic helical acquisition was performed approximately 90 seconds after initiation of intravenous contrast administration. A multistage interdigitated triple-bolus protocol incorporating saline and iodinated contrast (Omnipaque 350 mg I/mL; total contrast volume 80 mL) was used to provide combined arterial and venous vascular enhancement within a single acquisition, with injection flow rates varying between 2.5 and 5 mL/s.

On conventional polychromatic CT images acquired at 120 kVp, the prostate abnormality was poorly conspicuous and could not be confidently distinguished from the surrounding gland. Retrospective 40-keV virtual monoenergetic images increased the conspicuity of the irregular L-shaped prostate abnormality, while iodine density maps demonstrated heterogeneous iodine concentration throughout its distribution.

Across the subsequent multimodality assessment, the dominant prostate abnormality demonstrated an irregular geographic configuration rather than a discrete rounded mass. On representative axial images it assumed an approximately L-shaped distribution, with components extending across contiguous regions of the gland. The relative conspicuity of individual components varied with slice level and imaging modality; however, the overall distribution was spatially concordant across diffusion MRI, PSMA PET and spectral CT.

Quantitative iodine analysis using fusion images on the Philips Advanced Visualization Workspace 15 (AVW) demonstrated spatial variation in iodine concentration across the irregular prostate abnormality and surrounding gland. The highest measured iodine concentration was 2.02 mg/mL within the dominant component of the L-shaped abnormality. Additional ROIs measured 1.64 mg/mL within another component of the abnormality, 1.23 mg/mL at its peripheral or marginal component, and 0.58 mg/mL within reference prostate tissue outside the dominant abnormality (Fig. 4 and Table 1). These measurements demonstrate regional heterogeneity in iodine concentration rather than a uniform quantitative separation between tumor-associated and reference tissue. Subsequent multiparametric MRI was performed at 3 T using a Philips Elition X and a dedicated prostate protocol. The protocol included high-resolution multiplanar T2-weighted imaging, diffusion-weighted imaging with high b-value images at b = 1400 s/mm² and b = 2000 s/mm², and corresponding apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) mapping. High-resolution T2-weighted imaging demonstrated relatively ill-defined low T2 signal within the corresponding region, without a discrete lesion of comparable conspicuity to that identified on diffusion-weighted imaging. DWI demonstrated an irregular region of marked high signal at b = 1400 s/mm² and b = 2000 s/mm², with corresponding low signal on the ADC map, consistent with marked diffusion restriction. ADC within the dominant component measured between 0.29 to 0.44 × 10⁻³ mm²/s. On representative axial images, the abnormality demonstrated an approximately L-shaped configuration, although the apparent extent and intensity of its individual components varied between adjacent slices. The extensive abnormality crossed conventional zonal boundaries, involving both peripheral and transition zones bilaterally, and was assessed as PI-RADS 5.

PSMA PET/CT was subsequently performed using 18F-DCFPyL-PSMA. A total activity of 173.5 MBq was administered intravenously, with imaging commencing approximately 57 minutes after tracer administration. The prostate abnormality demonstrated intense radiotracer uptake with an SUVmax of 11.2 and an irregular geographic distribution extending from apex to base, involving the bilateral peripheral and transition zones, with the most avid component at the mid gland just left of midline. Reference activity measured SUVmax 2.7 in the mediastinal blood pool, 8.2 in the liver and 16.8 in the parotid gland. The overall anatomical distribution corresponded to the dominant L-shaped abnormality identified on diffusion MRI. The precise margins differed because of the lower spatial resolution and different biological basis of PET imaging, but the overall anatomical distribution was concordant. Based on the reported lesion and reference-organ uptake, the primary prostate abnormality was retrospectively classified as miPSMA expression score 2 according to PROMISE criteria [3]. Staging PSMA PET/CT also demonstrated extensive PSMA-avid nodal and osseous metastatic disease, with additional pulmonary foci suspicious for metastases.

Transperineal biopsy confirmed bilateral high-grade acinar adenocarcinoma, supporting the interpretation that the irregular multimodality imaging abnormality represented spatially extensive tumor involvement rather than an isolated focal nodule. Histopathology demonstrated Gleason score 4 + 4 = 8 (ISUP Grade Group 4) disease within the right prostate and Gleason score 4 + 5 = 9 (ISUP Grade Group 5) disease within the left prostate. Cribriform architecture was present. Perineural invasion was identified and features suspicious for extraprostatic extension were noted. The imaging abnormalities demonstrated on spectral CT, diffusion MRI and PSMA PET corresponded to biopsy-proven prostate carcinoma.

Collectively, the modalities demonstrated complementary imaging characteristics of the same tumor-associated abnormality: restricted water diffusion on MRI, increased PSMA radiotracer uptake on PET and regional differences in iodine concentration on spectral CT.

Following confirmation of metastatic high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma, the patient was referred for specialist urological and oncological management. Longer-term treatment and oncological outcome were outside the period of observation available for this report.

Discussion

This case illustrates spatially concordant manifestations of high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma across diffusion MRI, PSMA PET and dual-layer spectral CT. Importantly, the tumor did not present as a discrete rounded mass. Rather, the dominant imaging abnormality demonstrated an irregular geographic, approximately L-shaped distribution, with its apparent extent and intensity varying between slice levels and modalities.

This variation is expected because the modalities interrogate different properties of tumor tissue and have different spatial resolution and image contrast. T2-weighted MRI demonstrated relatively ill-defined signal alteration, diffusion MRI more clearly delineated regions of restricted water diffusion, PSMA PET demonstrated the distribution of radiotracer uptake, and spectral CT demonstrated spatial variation in iodine concentration. The relevant observation is therefore not identical lesion morphology on each modality, but concordance in the overall anatomical distribution of abnormality.

Diffusion-weighted MRI demonstrated marked restriction of water diffusion, consistent with the increased cellular density and altered tissue microstructure associated with aggressive prostate adenocarcinoma (Fig. 1). The spectral CT findings are of particular interest. The prostate abnormality was poorly conspicuous on the conventional polychromatic CT reconstruction but became more apparent on 40-keV (MonoE40), while iodine density maps demonstrated regional differences in iodine concentration (Fig. 3). Quantitative iodine measurements demonstrated regional heterogeneity, with the highest measured concentration of 2.02 mg/mL within the dominant component of the L-shaped abnormality and additional measurements ranging from 0.58 to 1.64 mg/mL across other sampled regions (Table 1 and Fig. 4). A 40-keV VMI reconstruction was selected because low-energy virtual monoenergetic reconstructions increase iodine attenuation and may improve the conspicuity of enhancing lesions. There is no established optimal VMI energy level for prostate cancer assessment, and the 40-keV reconstruction used in this case should therefore be regarded as an illustrative spectral reconstruction rather than a standardized diagnostic threshold [5,7].

Recent studies have demonstrated improved lesion conspicuity and diagnostic performance of low-keV virtual monoenergetic imaging compared with conventional CT for prostate cancer detection [5]. The present findings are also consistent with phantom-based investigations demonstrating superior detectability of contrast-enhancing lesions using dual-layer spectral detector CT, particularly at lower virtual monoenergetic energy levels where iodine contrast is amplified [7]. While the role of spectral CT in primary prostate cancer assessment remains incompletely defined, these observations suggest that iodine density mapping and low-keV virtual monoenergetic imaging may provide clinically relevant physiological information beyond that available from conventional CT alone.

Oliva-Lozano et al. reported improved diagnostic performance of quantitative spectral CT parameters for prostate cancer and identified an iodine concentration threshold of approximately 1.5 mg/mL in their study population [6]. However, this value represents an ROC-derived population threshold rather than a deterministic biological cut-off. Direct application to an individual patient is limited by differences in acquisition technique, contrast timing, scanner platform, reconstruction method, ROI placement and normalization strategy. Accordingly, the iodine measurements in the present case are presented descriptively and are not intended to validate or independently apply the previously reported diagnostic threshold.

An additional advantage of dual-layer spectral CT is its ability to acquire spectral information during routine scanning without the need for prospective protocol selection. Spectral datasets, including iodine density maps and virtual monoenergetic images, can therefore be generated retrospectively from standard acquisitions, facilitating incorporation of spectral parameters into oncologic imaging workflows [4].

The pathological findings further support the imaging observations. The high-grade histopathological findings, including Gleason Grade Group 5 disease and cribriform architecture, were concordant with the overall imaging appearance of biologically aggressive prostate carcinoma. However, this individual case cannot establish a direct quantitative relationship between histological grade and the magnitude of diffusion restriction, PSMA uptake or iodine concentration.

The extensive PSMA-avid nodal and osseous metastatic burden further supported the biologically aggressive nature of the primary malignancy, although metastatic disease assessment was not the focus of this report.

This report has several limitations. It describes a single patient and therefore cannot establish the diagnostic sensitivity, specificity or predictive value of spectral CT for prostate cancer. Iodine measurements were obtained from selected regions of interest rather than whole-lesion segmentation and may be influenced by ROI placement, contrast timing and acquisition parameters. Direct one-to-one spatial correlation between individual spectral CT ROIs and biopsy cores was not available; consequently, individual ROI values should not be interpreted as independently histologically validated tumor-positive or tumor-negative thresholds. Histological microvascular density was also not available for direct correlation with iodine concentration. Quantitative spectral CT values may also vary between scanner platforms and reconstruction techniques, limiting direct application of thresholds derived from other studies. Finally, the spectral CT findings were complementary to, rather than a replacement for, established prostate MRI, PSMA PET and histopathological assessment.

MRI and PSMA PET remain established components of contemporary prostate cancer imaging. In this case, retrospectively available dual-layer spectral CT provided additional information regarding lesion conspicuity and regional iodine concentration. The observation is hypothesis-generating and supports further prospective investigation of quantitative spectral CT parameters in prostate cancer.

Conclusion

This case illustrates an irregular, approximately L-shaped region of biopsy-proven high-grade prostate adenocarcinoma with spatially concordant but variably conspicuous abnormalities across diffusion MRI, PSMA PET and dual-layer spectral CT. Although apparent lesion extent and conspicuity varied between modalities and slice levels, the overall distributions of restricted diffusion, PSMA radiotracer uptake and increased iodine concentration were concordant. The spectral CT findings are descriptive and do not establish diagnostic performance or a quantitative threshold for malignancy; however, they illustrate the complementary information that may be derived from spectral CT datasets and support further prospective investigation of quantitative spectral parameters in prostate cancer.

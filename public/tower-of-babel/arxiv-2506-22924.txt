Comultiplicative map on projective resolution for a family of algebras one of which is cluster-tilted type 𝔻 4 \mathbb{D}_{4}
Pratyush Mishra, Tolulope Oke

Abstract
. We construct a comultiplicative map on the projective bimodule resolution for a family of algebras one of which is cluster-tilted of type D 4 D_{4} . The comultiplicative map presented in terms of idempotents associated with vertices of the quiver and a chain homotopy map is used to describe the multiplicative structure on the Hochschild cohomology ring of the first member of the family. We define a star product used to describe the cup product structure on the Hochschild cohomology ring for all family members.

1.
Introduction

Let
A
be a finite dimensional
\mathrm{k}
-algebra. It is of interest in general to understand the representations of the algebra
A
. The use of homological algebra tools such as finding projective resolutions of
A
, constructing diagonal maps on resolutions, finding associated homology or cohomology groups and extracting meaningful information have provided researchers with several approaches towards understanding algebra representations. This often leads to several questions about how such resolutions may be constructed for example. If the associated projective modules used in the resolution are both left and right modules over the algebra, the resolution is regarded as a bimodule resolution. Several articles have been written to study algebras in which the associated modules in the projective resolution repeat at different degrees. Such algebras are called
periodic algebras
.

Examples of periodic algebras are preprojective algebras associated with Dynkin graphs
[
11
]
, trivial extension algebras of path algebras of Dynkin quivers
[
12
]
, and algebras associated with cyclic quivers and separable algebras
[
10
]
. A. B. Buan, R.J. Marsh and I. Reiten in
[
13
]
introduced cluster-tilted algebras as the endomorphism algebras of cluster-tilting objects in a cluster category. I. Assem, T. Brüstle, and R. Schiffler proved that an algebra
\tilde{C}
is cluster-tilted if there is a tilted algebra
C
(of global dimension at most two) for which
\tilde{C}
is the trivial extension of
C

[
3
]
. This suggests that cluster-tilted algebras also have periodic projective bimodule resolutions.

The minimal projective bimodule resolution of an algebra is used in determining the ring and Gerstenhaber algebra structure on its Hochschild cohomology. There are several techniques used to determine the ring structure on Hochschild cohomology. For instance, the Yoneda product and the Yoneda splicing
[
7
]
. The ring multiplication is usually referred to as the cup product and it is common to determine this product using a diagonal map on the projective bimodule resolution that one has chosen. We refer to the diagonal map as the comultiplicative map throughout this article. Basic homological algebra results ensure the existence of such a map under certain conditions. In practice, we often make a concrete construction of the comultiplication map for each algebra of interest.

T. Furaya
[
1
]
studied a class of algebras which are special biserial algebras one of which is a cluster-tilted algebra of type
D_{4}
. We construct a comultiplication map on the projective bimodule resolution for these class of algebras. Our construction is applicable to algebras of finite representation types having periodic projective bimodule resolutions or having
almost periodic
projective resolutions of period three. By
almost periodic
, we mean that the kernel of the first differential map has a periodic resolution. This can be a spring board for constructing explicit comultiplicative maps in terms of vertices of associated quivers for larger classes of algebras in future work.

We introduce quiver algebras and the family of algebras under study in Section
2
. We also present other preliminary results on Hochschild cohomology ring of algebras, the projective bimodule resolutions
\mathbb{Q}_{\bullet}
and useful module compatibility structure in
2
. In Section
3
, we present our main results describing the comultiplicative map for the family of algebras under study. In order to describe the multiplicative structure on Hochschild cohomology, we further present in Section
3
a star product using certain chain map.

More specifically, and using the resolution
\mathbb{Q}_{\bullet}
, we construct a comultiplicative map
\Delta:\mathbb{Q}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{Q}_{\bullet}\otimes_{\Lambda_{n}}\mathbb{Q}_{\bullet}
where the tensor product is taken over the algebra
\Lambda_{n}
. Throughout, we distinguish between the tensor product over the field and this tensor product by defining
\bar{\otimes}:=\otimes_{\Lambda_{n}}
. Our first result is presented in Lemma
3.2
where we defined the chain map
\Delta^{\prime}
lifting the map
\pi:\Lambda_{n}\rightarrow\Lambda_{n}
given as multiplication by two. We then state our main result in Theorem (
3.2

for some chain homotopy map

to describe the cup product structure on Hochschild cohomology

of the cluster-tilted algebra of type

. Using the constructed chain map

at the chain level for elements

for

in Theorem (

Preliminaries

Quiver algebras:
A quiver is a directed graph. The quiver
\mathcal{Q}
will sometimes be denoted as a quadruple
(\mathcal{Q}_{0},\mathcal{Q}_{1},\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{t})
where
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is the set of vertices in
\mathcal{Q}
,
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
is the set of arrows in
\mathcal{Q}
, and
\mathfrak{o},\mathfrak{t}:\mathcal{Q}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}_{0}
are maps which assign to each arrow
a\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
, its origin vertex
\mathfrak{o}(a)
and terminal vertex
\mathfrak{t}(a)
in
\mathcal{Q}_{0}
. A sequence of paths is written
a=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n-1}a_{n}
such that the terminal vertex of
a_{i}
is the same as the origin vertex of
a_{i+1}
, using the convention of concatenating paths from left to right. Let
\mathrm{k}
be a field. The quiver or path algebra
\mathrm{k}Q
is defined as a vector space having all paths in
\mathcal{Q}
as a basis. Vertices are regarded as paths of length
0
or trivial paths, an arrow is a path of length
1
, and so on. The multiplication on
\mathrm{k}Q
is concatenation of paths. Two paths
a
and
b
satisfy
ab=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{t}(a)\neq\mathfrak{o}(b)
. This multiplication defines an associative graded algebra over
\mathrm{k}
given by
\mathrm{k}\mathcal{Q}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\mathrm{k}\mathcal{Q}_{m}
. The path algebra is unital if and only if the quiver has only finitely many vertices.

What is known:

The Hochschild cohomology
of an associative
\mathrm{k}
-algebra
A
was originally defined using the following projective bimodule resolution known as the bar resolution. It is given by

(2.3)

\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(A):\qquad\cdots\rightarrow A^{\otimes(m+2)}\xrightarrow{\;\delta_{m}\;}A^{\otimes(m+1)}\xrightarrow{\delta_{m-1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{\;\delta_{2}\;}A^{\otimes 3}\xrightarrow{\;\delta_{1}\;}A^{\otimes 2}\;(\;\xrightarrow{\mu}A)

where
\mu
is multiplication and the differentials
\delta_{m}
are given by

(2.4)

\delta_{n}(a_{0}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m+1})=\sum_{i=0}^{m}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{m+1}

for all
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{m+1}\in A
. Each
A
-bimodule
A^{\otimes(m+1)}
is a left modules over the enveloping algebra
A^{e}=A\otimes A^{op}
, where
A^{op}\cong A
with reverse multiplication as an algebra. The resolution is sometimes written
\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}\xrightarrow{\mu}\Lambda
with
\mu
referred to as the augmentation map. The Hochschild cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
A
denoted
\HH^{*}(A)
is obtained by applying the functor
\HHom_{A^{e}}(-,A)
to the complex
\mathbb{B}_{\bullet}(A)
, and then taking the cohomology of the resulting cochain complex. That is

\HH^{*}(A):=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\HH^{j}(A)=\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}\text{H}^{j}(\HHom_{A^{e}}(\mathbb{B}_{m}(A),A)).

Let
\chi\in\HHom_{A^{e}}(\mathbb{B}_{m},A)
be a cocycle, that is,
(\delta^{*}(\chi))(\cdot):=\chi\delta(\cdot)=0.
There is an isomorphism of the abelian groups
\HHom_{A^{e}}(\mathbb{B}_{m}(A),A)\cong\HHom_{k}(A^{\otimes m},A),
so we can also view
\chi
as an element of
\HHom_{k}(A^{\otimes m},A)
.

The cup product:
We obtain the multiplicative structure on Hochschild cohomology using the following.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
A
be a
\mathrm{k}
-algebra. Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}:\mathbb{B}\xrightarrow{}\mathbb{B}\otimes_{A}\mathbb{B}
be the comultiplicative map lifting the identity map on
A\cong A\otimes_{A}A
. Let

and

be cocycles of degree

respectively. The cup product
f\smallsmile g
at the chain level is an element of
\HHom_{\mathrm{k}}(A^{\otimes(m+n)},A)
given by

f\smallsmile g=\tilde{\pi}(f\otimes g)\Delta_{\mathbb{B}},

where
\tilde{\pi}
is multiplication, and
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}
is given by

(2.5)

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(a_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i}\otimes 1)\otimes_{A}(1\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n+1}).

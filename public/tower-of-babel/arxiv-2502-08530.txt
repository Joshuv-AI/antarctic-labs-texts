Residually Dominated Groups in Henselian Valued Fields of Equicharacteristic Zero
Dicle Mutlu and Paul Z. Wang

Abstract
We introduce residually dominated groups in pure henselian valued fields of equicharacteristic zero as an analogue of stably dominated groups introduced by Hrushovski and Rideau-Kikuchi. We show that when G G is a residually dominated group, there is a finite-to-one group homomorphism from its connected component into a connected stably dominated group, and we study the functoriality and universality properties of this map. Moreover, we prove that there is a group homomorphism into a definable group in the residue field that witnesses the residual domination. In our proofs, we use the results of Montenegro, Onshuus, and Simon on groups definable in NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} -theories that extend the theory of fields. Along the way, we also provide an algebraic characterization of residually dominated types, generalizing the work by Ealy, Haskell and Simon for stably dominated types in algebraically closed valued fields and study the properties of residually dominated types.

We now show that if a type is residually dominated, then its realization does not add any new information to the value group.

Lemma 2.19
.

Let

be a tuple in
\mathrm{VF}(\mathcal{U})
and

be a subfield such that
\mathrm{tp}(a/C)
is residually dominated. Then
\Gamma(Ca)=\Gamma(C)
.

Proof.

Suppose, on the contrary, that there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma(Ca)\setminus\Gamma(C)
. First observe that
\mathrm{k}(C\gamma)=\mathrm{k}(C)
. Let
\alpha\in\mathrm{k}(C\gamma)
and let
\varphi(x,\gamma,d)
be formula witnessing this, where

is a tuple in
\mathrm{dcl}(C)
. By the orthogonality of
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{A}}
and Remark

, there is a formula

in the sort
\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{A}}
with

such that

is the unique realization. Hence,

. Then, the independence
\mathrm{k}(Ca)\mathrel{\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$|$\cr\raise-3.87495pt\hbox{$\smile$}\crcr}}}}_{\mathrm{k}(C)}^{alg}\mathrm{k}(C\gamma)

\mathrm{k}(C)=\mathrm{k}(C\gamma)
. By domination, we obtain
\mathrm{tp}(\gamma/C\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{A}}(Ca))\vdash\mathrm{tp}(\gamma/Ca)
, leading to a contradiction. In fact, by orthogonality again, if
\gamma\in\mathrm{dcl}(C\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{A}}(Ca))
, then
\gamma\in\Gamma(C)
, contradicting the assumption.
∎

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
C\subseteq L
be subfields of
\mathcal{U}
. Suppose

is a regular extension of

and
\mathrm{tp}(L/C)
is residually dominated. Then, for any maximal immediate extension

of

, we have
\mathrm{tp}_{0}(L/C)\vdash\mathrm{tp}_{0}(L/F)
in

.

Proof.

Fix a maximal immediate extension

of

. Notice that
\mathrm{k}(L)\mathrel{\raise 0.86108pt\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$|$\cr\raise-3.87495pt\hbox{$\smile$}\crcr}}}}_{\mathrm{k}(C)}^{alg}\mathrm{k}(F)
holds trivially, since
\mathrm{k}(F)=\mathrm{k}(C)
. Then, by domination,
\mathrm{tp}(L/C\mathrm{k}_{\mathcal{A}}(F))\vdash\mathrm{tp}(L/F)
holds. Since,
\mathrm{k}(F)=\mathrm{k}(C)
, in particular,
\mathcal{A}(C)=\mathcal{A}(F)
, it follows that
\mathrm{tp}(L/C)\vdash\mathrm{tp}(L/F)
.

We will show that every valued field isomorphism
\sigma:L\to L^{\prime}

, fixing

. By Lemma

,

and

and fixes

, it follows that

\mathrm{tp}_{0}(L/C)\vdash\mathrm{tp}_{0}(L/F)

. Note that

Proposition 2.24
.

Let

3.1

Throughout this section, we fix an arbitrary theory
T

\mathcal{U}
. Let
(G,\cdot)
be a type-definable group in

.

Let

1.

We say

-generic
of

does not fork over

-generic

such that any translation

of

does not fork over

.

3.

We say that

is a

of

over

is

-definable and, for all

-definable.

If

When

admits a global strongly

-generic type over

-generic type forms an ideal that is both

In any theory, let

When dealing with definable groups, and generics, one key question is constructing definable groups and group homomorphisms from generic data. The following result is well-known and uses the existence of definable generics; some form of it was already used in the proof of Proposition 6.2 in

Proposition 3.8
.

Let

be a complete theory, let

,

be type-definable groups, and

a sufficiently saturated model of

over which

and

are defined. Let

, with

. Assume that the types
\mathrm{tp}(c_{2}/Mc_{1})
and
\mathrm{tp}(c_{3}/Mc_{1})
are

-definable, that
\mathrm{tp}(g_{1}/M)

, and that

is in

Then, there exists

-type-definable subgroups

, and

, and an

,

\pi_{H}(\gamma^{{}^{\prime}}\cdot\gamma^{-1})

Proof.

For

\gamma
,

using the subgroup

Claim 1

The projection

of

to

.

Proof.

Since

is definable, and

Now, let

, and let

be the normalizer of

.

The group

Proof.

The fact that

Thus, since

, and so, for all

Let

Proof.

Lemma 3.15

In any theory, let

be stably dominated, where

be a sufficiently saturated model containing

denote the unique nonforking extension of

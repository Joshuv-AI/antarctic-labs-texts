TROP2 Expression in Bladder Transurethral Resection Specimens: Correlation With Histological Grade and Survival
Authors: ÖZGECAN GÜNDOĞAR, SİBEL BEKTAŞ, BURAK ARSLAN, PELİN AKBAŞ
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background/AimTROP2 has become a therapeutic target in urothelial carcinoma following the approval of TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs), yet its expression in transurethral resection (TURBT) material remains poorly characterized. This study aimed to evaluate TROP2 protein expression using immunohistochemistry (IHC) in bladder TURBT samples and to investigate its association with histological grade, tumor stage, lymphovascular invasion (LVI), carcinoma in situ (CIS), recurrence, and survival outcomes.Materials and MethodsA total of 79 bladder TURBT specimens were included. Histological grading was performed according to the WHO/ISUP two-tier classification system (low grade / high grade) and pathological staging was based on the AJCC/UICC TNM Classification, 8th edition. TROP2 expression was assessed using IHC and quantified using the H-score method. Statistical analyses were performed using the chi-square test, Fisher’s exact test, Mann-Whitney U and Kruskal-Wallis tests, Spearman’s correlation analysis, receiver operating characteristic analysis, Kaplan-Meier estimates with log-rank tests, and Cox proportional hazards regression.ResultsMean age was 65.87±11.46 years (91.1% male). Mean TROP2 H-score was 173.99±48.15 (median 160.0; range 30-280). A significant negative correlation was identified between TROP2 and histological grade (rho=−0.410; p=0.0003); median H-scores were 200.0 in low-grade versus 150.0 in high-grade tumors. Receiver operating characteristic analysis yielded an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.736 (sensitivity 92.3%, specificity 45.0%; cut-off 200). No significant association was found with pT stage, LVI, CIS, recurrence, disease-free survival (log-rank p=0.886), or overall survival (log-rank p=0.752). Cox regression confirmed no independent prognostic effect on survival.ConclusionTROP2 inversely correlates with histological grade in bladder TURBT specimens (AUC=0.736) and may guide pathologists in diagnostically challenging cases – limited tissue, cautery artifact, or incomplete resection – where a low H-score suggests high-grade disease. TROP2 may complement WHO/ISUP grading and serve as a candidate predictive biomarker for TROP2-targeted ADC therapies.

Introduction

Bladder cancer is the fifth most common malignancy worldwide, with approximately 573,000 new cases annually, and the fourth most frequent cancer in men. Urothelial carcinoma (UC) accounts for over 90% of histological subtypes, and 75-80% of cases present as non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) at diagnosis (1, 2). Although NMIBC is amenable to complete surgical excision, it follows a clinically challenging course, with five-year recurrence rates of 50-70% and progression to muscle-invasive disease in 10-15% of cases (1). Transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT) is the standard procedure for primary diagnosis and staging; the key histopathological parameters evaluated – histological grade, pathological stage (pT), lymphovascular invasion (LVI), and carcinoma in situ (CIS) – directly determine treatment algorithms (3), yet alone are insufficient to fully predict recurrence and progression risk, underscoring the unmet need for complementary molecular biomarkers in routine pathological evaluation (4, 5).

Trophoblast cell-surface antigen 2 (TROP2) is a type I transmembrane glycoprotein encoded by the TACSTD2 gene. Its cytoplasmic tail activates multiple signaling pathways through protein kinase C, contributing to tumor cell proliferation, migration, apoptosis resistance, and stem cell self-renewal (6, 7). TROP2 is expressed at low levels in normal tissues but is overexpressed in various epithelial tumors, positioning it as a targetable oncogenic molecule (8). While high TROP2 expression has been linked to poor outcomes in pancreatic, gastric, and colorectal cancers (9-11), the literature presents a contrasting picture in urothelial carcinoma: studies in TURBT specimens have associated TROP2 with low-malignant-potential papillary lesions (4), while upper tract urothelial carcinoma (UTUC) studies have linked high TROP2 expression to low histological grade, early pathological stage, and favorable clinical outcomes (12-14). However, the majority of these studies derive from cystectomy or nephroureterectomy specimens; systematic immunohistochemistry (IHC)-based evaluations specifically in TURBT material using H-score methodology remain limited (4, 5, 15).

The clinical relevance of TROP2 in urothelial carcinoma has been amplified by the emergence of TROP2-directed antibody-drug conjugates (ADCs). Sacituzumab govitecan (SG) received FDA accelerated approval in 2021 for locally advanced or metastatic UC progressing after platinum-based chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitor therapy; in the TROPHY-U-01 phase II trial, SG demonstrated a 27% objective response rate and a median overall survival of 10.9 months (16). Given that the WHO/ISUP two-tier grading system remains the primary reference for treatment decisions (17), the addition of an objective TROP2 IHC parameter could improve clinical decision-making both diagnostically and as a predictive biomarker for ADC eligibility. Accordingly, the primary aim of this study was to evaluate TROP2 protein expression using immunohistochemistry in bladder TURBT specimens and to determine its association with key histopathological parameters – particularly histological grade – along with pT stage, LVI, CIS, recurrence, and survival outcomes.

Materials and Methods

Study design and ethical approval. This retrospective cross-sectional study included patients who underwent TURBT at the Department of Pathology, Gaziosmanpaşa Training and Research Hospital, between January 2018 and December 2024, with histopathologically confirmed UC. The study was approved by the institutional Non-Interventional Clinical Research Ethics Committee (Decision No: 83, Date: 22.04.2026) and conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki (2013 revision); all data were fully anonymized.

Patient selection criteria. Inclusion criteria: primary bladder UC obtained by TURBT; a histopathologically confirmed diagnosis; and an available formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue block containing sufficient tumor for IHC analysis. Exclusion criteria: history of neoadjuvant therapy, non-urothelial histological subtypes (squamous, adenocarcinoma, small cell carcinoma, etc.), and insufficient material.

Histopathological evaluation. All hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) slides were re-evaluated by an experienced pathologist blinded to clinical data. Histological grading was performed according to the WHO/ISUP two-tier system (low grade / high grade) as defined in the WHO Classification of Tumours: Urinary and Male Genital Tumours (5th edition, 2022) (17). Pathological staging used the AJCC/UICC TNM Classification, 8th edition. The presence of LVI and concurrent CIS was recorded.

Immunohistochemistry protocol. TROP2 expression was assessed using IHC on 4-μm sections from FFPE tissue blocks using the VENTANA BenchMark ULTRA automated stainer (Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA) with a goat polyclonal anti-TROP2 antibody (AF650; 1:100; R&D Systems, Minneapolis, MN, USA) as the primary antibody (12). Each staining run included a positive control (TROP2-positive breast cancer tissue) and a negative control (isotype antibody) (Figure 1).

Assessment of TROP2 expression. TROP2 expression was evaluated using the H-score method, considering both membranous and cytoplasmic staining: H-score = (1 × %weak) + (2 × %moderate) + (3 × %strong). Slides were independently evaluated by two pathologists. The optimal cut-off for grade prediction was determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis.

Statistical analysis. Statistical analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Statistics v.26.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Categorical variables were compared using chi-square or Fisher’s exact test; TROP2 H-score was compared between groups using the Mann-Whitney U-test or the Kruskal-Wallis test as appropriate; correlations were assessed using Spearman’s analysis. ROC/area under the curve (AUC) analysis was performed for grade prediction; the optimal cut-off was determined using the Youden index (H-score=200; AUC=0.736, sensitivity 92.3%, specificity 45.0%). For survival analyses, patients were stratified by the median TROP2 H-score (160.0) into high-expression (H-score≥160, n=43) and low-expression (H-score<160, n=36) groups. Kaplan-Meier curves for disease-free survival (DFS) and overall survival (OS) with log-rank tests were used; independent effects were assessed using Cox proportional hazards regression. Significance was set at p<0.05.

Results

General patient characteristics. A total of 79 patients were included. Mean age was 65.87±11.46 years (median: 66; range=40-85 years); 72 patients (91.1%) were male and seven (8.9%) were female. Association between TROP2 and histological grade. The only variable showing a statistically significant association with TROP2 in this study was histological grade. Discussion

We identified a highly significant inverse correlation between TROP2 H-score and histological grade in bladder TURBT specimens (rho=−0.410; p=0.0003; AUC=0.736), with markedly higher median H-scores in low-grade (200.0) than high-grade tumors (150.0). The mean H-score of 173.99±48.15 across the cohort confirms widespread TROP2 positivity in urothelial carcinoma (1, 4). ROC analysis identified H-score=200 as the optimal cut-off for grade discrimination (sensitivity 92.3%, specificity 45.0%), supporting TROP2 H-score as an objective and reproducible adjunct to WHO/ISUP grading in routine pathological evaluation (17). To our knowledge, this is one of the few studies to systematically quantify TROP2 in TURBT specimens using the H-score and correlate it with the full spectrum of clinicopathological parameters.

No significant association was found between TROP2 H-score and pT stage (p=0.582), LVI (p=0.722), or CIS (p=0.194). The pTa and pT1 stages constituted 92.4% of our cohort, and LVI and CIS were present in only 12.7% and 20.3% of cases, respectively – features inherent to TURBT-based studies that limit statistical power for these comparisons. Müller et al. similarly reported that TROP2 loss was significantly associated with grade progression in pTa tumors but unrelated to disease outcome in pT2-4 carcinomas (5). This suggests that the discriminatory value of TROP2 is most clinically relevant in early-stage, TURBT-based settings, which correspond closely to the context of the present study. A potential mechanistic basis for a TROP2-LVI link has been proposed by Wen et al. through the integrin β1-RACK1-Src/FAK axis (6), although this has not been consistently demonstrated in clinical series with low LVI prevalence.

Despite recurrence in 53.2% of patients during follow-up, TROP2 expression was not associated with recurrence status (p=0.127), DFS (log-rank p=0.886; Figure 2A), or OS (log-rank p=0.752; Figure 2B). Cox proportional hazards regression confirmed no independent prognostic effect on either DFS [hazard ratio (HR)=0.991, 95% confidence interval (CI)=0.931-1.055, p=0.771] or OS (HR=0.971, 95% CI=0.890-1.059, p=0.501) per 10-point H-score increase (Figure 2C). The hazard ratios close to 1.0 with wide confidence intervals crossing unity confirm the absence of any clinically or statistically meaningful prognostic signal. These findings contrast with Zhang et al., who reported an association between high TROP2 and recurrence in NMIBC (15), and with Tomiyama et al. and Kobayashi et al., who identified high TROP2 as a favorable prognostic marker in UTUC (12, 14). The discrepancy likely reflects differences in disease context (NMIBC vs. UTUC), TROP2 scoring methodology, and the predominance of early-stage disease with generally favorable outcomes in our cohort. Dernbach et al. observed in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) that TROP2 expression at the tumor invasion front was associated with better DFS (18), suggesting that any prognostic signal in bladder UC may be spatially restricted – an analysis beyond the scope of the current TURBT-based study.

Our findings align with a growing body of literature demonstrating an inverse TROP2-grade relationship in urothelial tumors. Abbas et al. reported strong TROP2 associations with low-risk papillary neoplasms in 150 bladder UC cases (4); Kanaan et al. found 98.8% TROP2 positivity in UTUC with high intensity significantly associated with low grade (13); and Müller et al. demonstrated TROP2 loss as a predictor of grade progression in over 2,700 specimens (5). Mechanistically, Stoyanova et al. showed that TROP2 undergoes regulated intramembrane proteolysis, with the released intracellular domain co-localizing with β-catenin to maintain epithelial integrity (7); disruption of this pathway facilitates epithelial-mesenchymal transition, which may contribute to the high-grade morphological phenotype. In vitro, Zhang et al. demonstrated that TROP2 silencing in bladder cancer cell lines suppresses proliferation and induces apoptosis via the cyclin E1-p27 axis (2), supporting the view that TROP2 loss contributes to the aggressive phenotype of high-grade UC. Importantly, the biology of TROP2 in urothelial carcinoma differs fundamentally from its oncogenic role in pancreatic (10), gastric (11), and gallbladder cancers (19), where it activates PI3K/AKT and MAPK pathways and is associated with poor outcomes (9). In UC, TROP2 behaves instead as a luminal differentiation marker – present across all molecular subtypes except neuroendocrine (3), associated with FGFR3 mutation status (20), and exhibiting heterogeneous subcellular localization in variant histology subgroups (21) – underscoring the cancer type-specific nature of TROP2 biology (8).

Our findings have direct implications for TROP2-targeted therapy in urothelial carcinoma. Sacituzumab govitecan, a TROP2-directed ADC, received FDA accelerated approval in 2021 for locally advanced or metastatic UC progressing after platinum-based chemotherapy and checkpoint inhibitors, demonstrating a 27% objective response rate and a median OS of 10.9 months in TROPHY-U-01 (16). Routine IHC-based TROP2 quantification in TURBT specimens may help identify patients with preserved target expression who could be considered for TROP2-directed ADC strategies, while also highlighting the need to evaluate whether reduced TROP2 expression in high-grade tumors affects therapeutic responsiveness. Moreover, in diagnostically challenging cases where cautery artifact, limited tissue, or incomplete resection renders grade assessment uncertain on H&E alone, TROP2 H-score may provide an objective adjunct: a low H-score raises suspicion for high-grade disease, while a high H-score supports a low-grade designation.

Limitations. The retrospective single-center design limits causal inference and generalizability. The cohort size (n=79) provides adequate power for the primary correlation analysis but is insufficient for multivariate survival modeling incorporating all clinicopathological variables. The early-stage predominance (92.4% pTa/pT1) restricts inferences about TROP2 in advanced-stage disease. The low prevalence of variant histology (2.5%) and the universal absence of lymphocytic reaction precluded relevant subgroup analyses. The follow-up duration may have been insufficient to capture late recurrences. Future prospective multicenter studies with longer follow-up, molecular subtyping, and FGFR3 mutational profiling will be needed to validate TROP2 as a clinically actionable biomarker in bladder UC.

Conclusion

We demonstrate that TROP2 H-score is significantly and inversely correlated with histological grade in bladder TURBT specimens (AUC=0.736), representing an objective and reproducible IHC parameter that complements WHO/ISUP grading. No significant associations were found with pT stage, LVI, CIS, recurrence, DFS, or OS, and Cox regression confirmed the absence of independent prognostic effects. These findings support TROP2 as a differentiation-associated marker with potential grading utility, rather than as a standalone prognostic biomarker, in early-stage NMIBC. Its value as a diagnostic adjunct in challenging cases and as a candidate predictive biomarker for TROP2-targeted ADC therapies such as sacituzumab govitecan warrants prospective multicenter validation (5, 12-14, 16).

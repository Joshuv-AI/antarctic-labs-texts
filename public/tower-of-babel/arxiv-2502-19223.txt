On a theorem of Harder
Ivan Panin, Anastasia Stavrova

Abstract




1.
Introduction

In 1967 Günter Harder proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
H67
, Satz 3.3]

Let
G
be a simply connected semisimple group over a Dedekind domain
A
. Assume that
G

is quasi-split over the fraction field of
A
. Then
H^{1}_{Zar}(A,G)=1
.

Our aim is to generalize this theorem to isotropic simply connected semisimple groups.

We use the following definition of an isotropic group.
Let
G
be a reductive group (scheme) in the sense of
[
SGA3
]
over a commutative ring
R
.
A parabolic
R
-subgroup
P
of
G
is called
strictly proper
, if for each ring homomorphism

s:R\to\Omega(s)
, where

\Omega(s)
is an algebraically closed field,
the type of the parabolic subgroup
P_{\Omega(s)}
in the sense of
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXVI, §3.2]

does not contain any connected component of the Dynkin diagram of
G_{\Omega(s)}
.
We will say that

G
is
strictly isotropic
, if it contains a strictly proper parabolic
R
-subgroup.

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain.
Let
G
be a simply connected
semisimple group over
A
. Assume that
G
is strictly isotropic over the fraction field
L
of
A
.
Then the natural map

H^{1}_{\et}(A,G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L,G)

has trivial kernel.

In other words, let
E
be a
G
-torsor over
A
. If
E
is trivial over
L
, then
E
is trivial.

The following immediate corollary generalizes Harder’s theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain.
Let
G
be a simply connected
semisimple group over
A
. Assume that
G
is strictly isotropic over the fraction field
L
of
A
.
Then
H^{1}_{Zar}(A,G)=1
.

We note that Harder obtained Theorem
1.1
as a corollary of the following statement: if the
L
-group

G_{L}
satisfies strong approximation, then
H^{1}_{Zar}(A,G)=1

[
H67
, Kor. 2.3.2]
.
(In fact, this implication holds for any flat finite type
A
-group
G
, not just for simply connected semisimple groups.)
Strong approximation is known to hold for simply connected strictly isotropic groups
G
such that

G(\hat{L}_{p})
is
R
-trivial for every completion
\hat{L}_{p}
of
L
with respect a prime
p
of
A

[
G07
, Lemme 5.6]
.
If
L
is a global field, a simply connected group
G
over
L
satisfies strong approximation under somewhat weaker
isotropy conditions, thanks to the classical results of Kneser, Platonov, and Prasad
[
Kn
,
Pla
,
Pra
]

In all these cases the
conclusion of Corollary
1.3
follows from the above-cited result of Harder
[
H67
, Kor. 2.3.2]
.
Our proof does not use these results.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain and let
L
be the fraction field of
A
. Let
G
be a reductive group over
A
. If
G

is strictly isotropic over
L
, then
G
is strictly isotropic over
A
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be the smooth projective
A
-scheme of all parabolic subgroups of
G
in the sense
of
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXVI, Cor. 3.5]
. By
[
Gro61
, Corollaire 7.3.6]
the natural map
\mathcal{P}(A)\to\mathcal{P}(L)

is surjective. Since
G
is strictly isotropic over
L
, there is a strictly proper parabolic subgroup

P\in\mathcal{P}(L)
. Let
\tilde{P}\in\mathcal{P}(A)
be a preimage of
P
. We claim that
\tilde{P}
is a
strictly proper parabolic subgroup of
G
. Indeed,
since
\Spec(A)
is connected, by
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXII, Proposition 2.8, Lemme 5.2.7]

it suffices to check that
\tilde{P}
is strictly proper at any fixed point of
\Spec(A)
.
Thus,
\tilde{P}
is strictly proper, since
\tilde{P}_{K}=P
is strictly proper by assumption.
∎

For any commutative ring
R
, we denote by
E_{P}(R)
the subgroup
G(R)

generated by the
R
-points of the unipotent radicals of
P
and of an opposite parabolic subgroup

P^{-}
. Note that choosing a different opposite parabolic subgroup
P^{-}
does not change
E_{P}(R)
by
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXVI,
Cor. 1.8]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If
E
is not trivial, then, since
E
is trivial over
L
, there is a non-invertible element
f\in A
such that
E
is trivial over
A_{f}
.
Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
be all distinct maximal ideals of
A
dividing the ideal
(f)
.
Let
\hat{A}
be the completion of
A
with respect to the ideal
(f)
. Then
\hat{A}
is isomorphic to the direct product of rings

\hat{A}_{1},\ldots,\hat{A}_{k}
, where each
\hat{A}_{i}
is the completion of
A
with respect to the ideal
p_{i}
.
One has
A/f\cong\hat{A}/f
. Let
\hat{L}_{i}
denote the fraction field of
\hat{A}_{i}
. Then the localization

(\hat{A})_{f}
is isomorphic to the direct product
\hat{L}
of the fraction fields
\hat{L}_{i}
of the rings
\hat{A}_{i}
.

By the Serre–Grothendieck conjecture for
semilocal Dedekind rings
[
Guo
,
Ni84
]
the torsor
E
is trivial over
\hat{A}_{i}
, since
E
is trivial over
its fraction field
\hat{L}_{i}
which is a field extension of
L
. Consequently, the torsor
E
is trivial over
\hat{A}
.

Consider the commutative square

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A_{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hat{A}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\hat{L}}

This is a patching diagram for torsors. The torsor
E
is trivial over
A_{f}
and over
\hat{A}
. Then there is
an element
g\in G(\hat{L})
that patches together the two trivial
G
-torsors over
\hat{L}

to get the torsor
E
.

By Lemma
2.1

G
has a strictly proper parabolic subgroup
P
over
A
. Let
L
be a
Levi subgroup of
P
over
A
. Since
\Spec(A)
is connected,
by
[
G15
, Th. 7.3.1]
there is an
A
-group homomorphism

\lambda:\Gm_{,A}\to L
such that

L=\Cent_{G}(\lambda(\Gm_{,A}))
and
P
is one of the two opposite parabolic
A
-subgroups
P=P^{+}
and
P^{-}

of
G
uniquely determined by
\lambda
in the following way described in
[
SGA3
, Exp. XXVI, Prop. 6.1]
.
Let
X^{*}(\lambda(\Gm_{,A}))\cong\ZZ
be the lattice
of characters of
\lambda(\Gm_{,A})\cong\Gm_{,A}
. The adjoint action of
\lambda(\Gm_{,A})
on
\Lie(G)

defines a
\ZZ
-grading on
\Lie(G)
, such that
\Lie(L)=\Lie(G)_{0}
, and
P,P^{-}
are the unique parabolic subgroups of
G
such that

\Lie(P)=\Lie(L)\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\chi\in\ZZ_{>0}}\Lie(G)_{\chi}\mbox{\quad and\quad}\Lie(P^{-})=\Lie(L)\oplus\bigoplus\limits_{\chi\in\ZZ_{<0}}\Lie(G)_{\chi}.

This means that
P,P^{-}
are a pair of opposite parabolic subgroups of
G

that correspond to
S=\lambda(\Gm_{,A})
in the sense of
[
PSt1
, Lemma 2.4]
. These subgroups are strictly proper.
Then by
[
G07
, Lemme 4.5]
one has
G(\hat{L}_{i})=G(\hat{A}_{i})E_{P}(\hat{L}_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Since the base
\Spec(A)
is affine, normal and Noetherian, by
[
Th87
, Corollary 3.2]

G
is
A
-linear,
that is, there is a closed
A
-embedding
G\to\GL_{n,A}
for some
n\geq 1
. Clearly, we can assume that
n\geq 3

without loss of generality. Then all the conditions of
[
PSt1
, Lemma 3.2]
are satisfied, and it follows that

E_{P}(\hat{L})\subseteq G(\hat{A})E_{P}(A_{f})
. Summing up, we have

g\in G(\hat{L})=G(\hat{A})E_{P}(\hat{L})\subseteq G(\hat{A})E_{P}(A_{f}).

It follows that
E
is trivial over
A
.

∎

3.
Nisnevich conjecture

In 1989 Y. Nisnevich formulated the following

Conjecture 3.1
.

[
Ni89
, Conjecture 1.3]

Let
R
be a regular local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
, let
m
be the maximal ideal of
R
and let
u\in m\setminus m^{2}

be an element.
Let
G
be a strictly isotropic reductive group over
R
. Then
H^{1}_{Zar}(R_{u},G)=1
.

Nisnevich established this conjecture for
\dim R=2
and
G
quasi-split
[
Ni89
, Prop. 5.1]
.

Strictly speaking,
the initial formulation of this conjecture by Nisnevich did not include the isotropy condition
(although this condition was imposed on
G
in the text right before and after the formulation
of
[
Ni89
, Conjecture 1.3]
). However, R. Fedorov
[
F
]
provided an example of an anisotropic adjoint semisimple group for which the same
statement does not hold, so we include this condition in the statement.

Fedorov also established this conjecture under the assumption that
R
contains an infinite field. He also formulated
and proved a suitable extension of this statement to semilocal regular rings.

K. Česnavičius
[
Ce2
]
extended Fedorov’s result to the case where

R
contains a finite field, and under the weaker assumption that only
G_{R/u}
is strictly isotropic. On top of that,
Česnavičius proved the following mixed characteristic statement:

H^{1}_{Zar}(R_{u},G)=1
assuming that
R
is semilocal, geometrically regular over a Dedekind subring

D
such that
u\in D
, and
G
is quasi-split. Thus, the equicharacteristic case of the conjecture is completely settled.

Nisnevich conjecture is a direct generalization of the so-called Quillen’s question.

Conjecture 3.2
.

[
Q
, p. 170]

Let
R
be a regular local ring with maximal ideal
m
, and let
f\in m\setminus m^{2}
. Then
every finitely generated projective
R_{f}
-module is free.

On top of the cases mentioned above, Conjecture
3.2
is known for all 3-dimensional regular local rings

R

[
Gab1
]
.

Our theorem yields the following two cases of Conjecture
3.1
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(R,m)
be a 2-dimensional regular local ring with the fraction field
L
. Let
u\in m
be any non-zero element.
Let
G
be a strictly isotropic simply connected group over
R_{u}
. Then
H^{1}_{\et}(R_{u},G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L,G)
has trivial kernel. As a consequence,

H^{1}_{Zar}(R_{u},G)=1
.

Proof.

The ring
R_{u}
is a Dedekind domain. Thus it is subject to Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
D
be a discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic such that its residue field is perfect. Let
(R,m)
be a
3-dimensional local
ring, essentially smooth over
D
. Let
f\in m\setminus m^{2}
be a regular element of
R
. Let
G

be a simply connected strictly isotropic group over
D
. Then
H^{1}_{\et}(R_{f},G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L,G)
has trivial kernel,
where
L
is the fraction field of
R
.

For the proof of this theorem we need to establish some lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
A
be a discrete valuation ring and let
L
be the fraction field of
A
.
Let
G
be a strictly isotropic reductive group over
A
.
Then
H^{1}_{\et}(A[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L[x],G)
has trivial kernel.

Proof.

Since the Serre-Grothendieck conjecture holds for any reductive group over
A
, by
[
St22
, Lemma 5.3]

it is enough to prove the claim for every strictly isotropic simply connected group
G
over
A
.
Assume that
G
is simply connected.
Since
A(x)
is a Dedekind domain, by Theorem
1.2
the kernel of
H^{1}_{\et}(A(x),G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L(x),G)

is trivial. It follows that

\ker\left(H^{1}_{\et}(A[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L[x],G)\right)\subseteq\ker\left(H^{1}_{\et}(A[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(A(x),G)\right).

Let
E
be a principal
G
-bundle over
A[x]
and let
f\in A[x]
be a monic polynomial such that

E_{A[x]_{f}}
is trivial. Then
E
extends to a principal
G
-bundle
\tilde{E}
over
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{A}
such that
\tilde{E}
is trivial
at infinity. Then by
[
PSt2
, Theorem 1.7]

\tilde{E}|_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{A}}
is trivial. Hence
E
is trivial over
A[x]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A
be a Dedekind domain and let
L
be the fraction field of
A
.
Let
G
be a strictly isotropic simply connected group over
A
.
Let
S\subseteq A[x]
be a multiplicatively closed subset.
Then
H^{1}_{\et}(A[x]_{S},G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L(x),G)
has trivial kernel.

Proof.

Since a
G
-torsor
E
over
A[x]_{S}
is finitely presented, it follows that any such torsor
is defined over
A[x]_{f}
for a non-zero polynomial
f(x)\in A[x]
. Therefore, to prove the lemma, it is
enough to show that the map
H^{1}_{\et}(A[x]_{f},G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L(x),G)
has trivial kernel.

Set
U=1+fA[x]\subseteq A[x]
.
Then
(A[x]_{U})_{f}
is a regular ring of dimension
\leq 1
,
hence a Dedekind domain. Indeed, let
m
be a maximal ideal of
A[x]
not containing
f
. Since
(f,m)=A[x]
,
it follows that
m
contains an element of
U
. Therefore, no maximal ideal of
A[x]
survives in
A[x]_{Uf}
.

By Theorem
1.2

H^{1}_{\et}((A[x]_{U})_{f},G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L(x),G)
has trivial kernel.
Let
E
be a
G
-torsor defined over
A[x]_{f}
and trivial over
L(x)
.
Hence there is
h(x)=1+f(x)g(x)\in U
such that
E_{h}
is trivial. Consider the commutative square

\textstyle{A[x]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A[x]_{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A[x]_{h}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{A[x]_{fh}}

This is a patching diagram for torsors. The torsor
E
is trivial over
A[x]_{fh}
. Then it can be extended
to a
G
-torsor
E^{\prime}
over
A[x]
such that
E^{\prime}_{h}
is trivial. In particular,
E^{\prime}
is trivial over
L(x)
.
By
[
CTO
, Prop. 2.2]
it follows that
E^{\prime}_{L[x]}
is trivial, and hence
(E^{\prime}_{L[x]})|_{x=0}
is trivial.
Then by Theorem
1.2
it follows that
E^{\prime}|_{x=0}
is trivial.
Then by by a generalization of Quillen’s local-global principle
[
AHW
, Theorem 3.2.5]
, to show that
E^{\prime}
is trivial over
A[x]
, it is enough to show that it is trivial over
A_{m}[x]
for all
maximal ideals
m
of
A
.
By Lemma
3.5
the kernel of
H^{1}_{\et}(A_{m}[x],G)\to H^{1}_{\et}(L[x],G)
is trivial.
This proves the claim.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

Let
\pi\in D
be a prime element.
Let
E
be a
G
-torsor over
R_{f}
.
Since
E
trivializes over
L
,
there is
h\in R
such that
E_{hf}
is a trivial
G
-torsor. We can assume without loss of generality
that
h
and
f
have no common factors and
h\in m^{2}
, where
m
is the maximal ideal of
R
.
Moreover, we can assume that
\pi
does not divide
h
. Indeed,
if
\pi
divides
f
, then it is a common factor of
f
and
h
, so
\pi
does not divide
f
.
Then by the Grothendieck-Serre
conjecture for discrete valuation rings there is
h\in R_{f}
not contained in
(\pi)
such that
E_{h}
is trivial.

Since
D
is of mixed characteristic, it is excellent. Then by Dutta’s version of Lindel’s lemma
[
Dut00
, Theorem 1.3]

there exists a regular local subring
(B,n)
of
(R,m)
, with

B/n=R/m
, and such that

(1)

B
is a localization of a polynomial ring
W[x_{1},x_{2}]
at a maximal ideal

(\pi,\phi(x_{1}),x_{2})
, where
x_{2}=f
,
\phi
is a monic irreducible polynomial
in
W[x_{1}]
and

(W,(\pi))
is a subring
R
, isomorphic to
D[y_{1},\ldots,y_{l}]_{(\pi)}
for some
l\geq 0
(
in particular,
W
is a dvr);

(2)

R
is a localization of an étale
B
-algebra;

(3)

There exists an element
b\in B\cap hR
such that
B/bB\cong R/hR
is an isomorphism. Furthermore
hR=bR
.

Then
B_{x_{2}}/b\cong R_{f}/h
and we have a patching square for
G
-torsors

\textstyle{B_{x_{2}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R_{f}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{B_{x_{2}b}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{R_{fh}}

Consequently, there is a
G
-torsor over
B_{x_{2}}=\bigl(W[x_{1},x_{2}]_{(\pi,\phi(x_{1}),x_{2})}\bigr)_{x_{2}}
,
trivial over
B_{x_{2}b}
, that induces
E
. Denote this
G
-torsor
by the same letter

. It is enough to show that the
G
-torsor

over
B_{x_{2}}
is trivial.

Since the ideal
(\pi,\phi(x_{1}),x_{2})\subseteq W[x_{1},x_{2}]
intersects
W[x_{2}]
by the ideal
(\pi,x_{2})
, the ring

W[x_{2}]_{(\pi,x_{2})}[x_{1}]
is a subring of

. Consequently, the ring

. Since
W[x_{1},x_{2}]\subseteq X\subseteq B_{x_{2}}

and
B_{x_{2}}
is a localization of

, it follows that
B_{x_{2}}=X_{S}
is also a localization of
X

at a multiplicatively closed subset

.
Therefore,
B_{x_{2}}=X_{S}=A[x_{1}]_{S}
,
where

is a Dedekind domain. Since the torsor
E
over

is trivial

B_{x_{2}b}
, we conclude by Lemma

that

is trivial.
∎

On fractional semilinear wave equations in non-cylindrical domains
Mauro Bonafini, Van Phu Cuong Le, Riccardo Molinarolo

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate a class of semilinear wave equations in non-cylindrical time-dependent domains, subject to exterior homogeneous Dirichlet conditions. Under mild regularity and monotonicity assumptions on the evolving spatial domains, we establish existence of weak solutions by two different methods: a constructive time-discretization scheme and a penalty approach. The analysis applies to nonlocal fractional Laplacians and potentials with Lipschitz continuous gradient, and to vector-valued maps.



Keywords:
wave equations, non-local wave equations, non-cylindrical domains.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:

35L05, 35L85, 35Q74, 35R37, 74H20, 74K35.

1.
Introduction

Linear, semilinear, and non-linear wave equations have been widely investigated in the mathematical literature, with particular attention devoted to existence, well-posedness, regularity, and singular limits. Among the many significant contributions, we mention the classical monographs
[
29
,
32
]
and the seminal works on the topics
[
11
,
21
,
28
,
40
,
46
,
47
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we consider non-local equations on non-cylindrical domains. Hyperbolic PDEs on time-dependent domains model wave propagation, fluid-structure interaction, fluid dynamics, extensible beam dynamics, cavity resonators, and many other problems in mathematical physics (see the recent survey
[
31
]
). In particular, the problem addressed here naturally arises from elastodynamic models in mechanics, where a thin film is initially glued to a rigid substrate and gradually peeled off. In that context, it is natural to model the debonded region with a time-dependent, non-decreasing domain and to assume that the displacement satisfies a classical wave equation (see
[
15
,
33
,
34
]
and references therein). Hence, considering a non-local operator with possible interactions between the displacement and the external bonded region is a natural extension of this setup. Time-dependent domains also arise in dynamic fracture models, where the crack evolution induces an expanding time-dependent set (cf.
[
14
,
16
,
17
]
). Furthermore, at least for cylindrical domains, non-local non-linear wave equations with a double-well potential are relevant in the study of evolving interfaces or defects, such as minimal surfaces in Minkowski space (see
[
3
,
21
,
35
,
42
,
49
]
).

Our work is motivated by the following key objective. We aim to extend the existence results for fractional wave equations in
[
8
,
7
,
9
]
to the non-cylindrical setting, and, in doing so, to extend to the fractional setting the results in non-cylindrical domains presented in
[
33
,
34
]
.

The literature contains several results on hyperbolic equations in non-cylindrical domains, obtained through different methods and under various assumptions on the domain evolution. Among these contributions, in
[
4
]
the authors present an abstract formulation together with regularization techniques for the associated operators. Notable contributions include diffeomorphism-based methods that recast the problem in cylindrical domains (see
[
12
,
13
,
15
,
16
,
18
,
39
,
48
]
), with diffeomorphisms being a suitable option due to the local nature of the operators considered in those works.
Furthermore, we refer to
[
1
,
5
,
10
,
27
,
30
]
and references therein for various approaches on different evolution equations (parabolic, Schrödinger, Navier-Stokes, etc.) in non-cylindrical domains (see also
[
19
,
20
]
for shape analysis results for parabolic equations via potential theory).

In this paper, we focus on a semilinear fractional wave equation defined in a non-cylindrical domain
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
T>0
represents a finite time horizon. Time slices
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
of
\mathcal{O}
will describe our evolving domains. In this context, it is customary to impose some form of continuity on the domain evolution, such as Lipschitz continuity, Hölder continuity, or absolute continuity (see
[
6
,
43
,
45
]
, respectively). Moreover, monotonicity assumptions (most notably expanding domains) are typical (see
[
5
,
27
]
), and will be part of our working assumptions (for details on the geometrical framework, see Section
3
). We consider the general hyperbolic equation

(1)

\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)=f\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{O},

where
(-\Delta)^{s}
represents the fractional
s
-Laplacian,
W
is a potential such that
\nabla W
is Lipschitz continuous and
f
is a given forcing term. Our aim is to prove existence of suitably defined weak solutions to (
1
) coupled with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions and for given initial time conditions.

Two different existence proofs are presented. The first one is based on a time-discretization scheme, inspired by the approach of Calvo, Novaga, and Orlandi
[
10
]
(see also
[
33
]
). The second proof relies on the penalty method introduced by J.-L. Lions
[
37
]
, which has since been widely applied to evolution systems on time-dependent domains, including wave equations
[
38
,
50
]
and quasilinear hyperbolic
p
-Laplacian problems
[
24
]
.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we briefly recall fractional Sobolev spaces and the fractional Laplace operator to fix notation. In Section
3
we define the geometrical setting of the problem, we propose a definition of weak solution and we state our main existence result. Section
4
is devoted to the proof via the time-discretization method, while Section
5
provides the proof via a Galerkin-penalization approximation. Appendix
A
recalls a result for the problem in cylindrical domains that can be obtained as a simple adaptation of results in
[
7
,
9
]
.

2.
Preliminaries and notations

We denote by
\mathcal{L}^{d}
the
d
-dimensional Lebesgue measure, and the integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure by
dx
. We adopt standard notations and definitions for Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}
and Sobolev spaces
W^{n,p}
,
1\leq p\leq+\infty
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Given a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, we denote by
\mathcal{X}^{*}
its topological dual and by
\langle\varphi,x\rangle_{\mathcal{X}^{\ast},\mathcal{X}}
the duality pairing between
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
.

Bochner spaces.
For
T>0
, let
\{\mathcal{X}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
be a family of non-decreasing Banach spaces, i.e.,
\mathcal{X}_{s}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{t}
for every
0\leq s\leq t\leq T
, and denote by
\mathcal{X}:=\mathcal{X}_{T}
. With a slight abuse of notation, we say that a function
u
belongs to
L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{X}_{t})
,
1\leq p\leq+\infty
, if
u\in L^{p}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
and
u(t)\in\mathcal{X}_{t}
for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
. In particular, the map
t\mapsto\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{X}}
is in
L^{p}(0,T)
.
We denote by
C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\mathcal{X})
the subspace of
L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
consisting of functions
u
that are scalarly continuous, namely such that

the map
t\mapsto\langle\varphi,u(t)\rangle_{\mathcal{X}^{\ast},\mathcal{X}}
is continuous on
[0,T]
for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\ast}
.

In particular, we recall the following result
[
36
, Lemma 8.1]
: if
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are two Banach spaces,
\mathcal{X}
reflexive and
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathcal{Y}
with continuous injection, then

L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathcal{X})\cap C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\mathcal{Y})=C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\mathcal{X}).

Fractional Sobolev spaces.
Throughout this paper, we fix
s>0
and
d,m\geq 1
. Let us consider the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, i.e., the space of rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
functions from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For any
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
we define the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}u
of
u
as

\mathcal{F}u(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\textup{e}^{-\textup{i}\xi\cdot x}u(x)\,dx,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The fractional Laplacian operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
can be defined, up to constants, as

(-\Delta)^{s}\colon\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}),\;u\mapsto(-\Delta)^{s}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}u).

Given
u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we consider the bilinear form defined by

[u,v]_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(x)\cdot(-\Delta)^{s/2}v(x)\,dx,

and the corresponding Gagliardo seminorm

[u]_{s}:=\sqrt{[u,u]_{s}}=\|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})}.

We define the fractional Sobolev space of order
s>0
as

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})\,:\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2s})|\mathcal{F}u(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<+\infty\right\},

and for every
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})
the
H^{s}
-norm of
u
as

\|u\|_{s}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})}^{2}+[u]_{s}^{2}.

It is well-known that
(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m}),\|\cdot\|_{s})
is a Hilbert space (see e.g.
[
22
,
41
]
).

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary and define

\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{m})\,:\,u=0\text{ a.e. in }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega\right\},

endowed with the
H^{s}
-norm, and define its dual
H^{-s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}):=(\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}))^{*}
. One can prove, see e.g.
[
41
]
, that
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
corresponds to the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with respect to the
H^{s}
-norm. We have the continuous injections
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\subset H^{-s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. In particular, the duality product between
H^{-s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfies

\langle f,u\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}=\langle f,u\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\quad\text{for every }f\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\text{ and }u\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}).

In the following, for notational convenience, we usually drop the target space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
whenever clear from the context, and just write
L^{2}(\Omega)
,
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)
and
{H}^{-s}(\Omega)
. Moreover, each function
u\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is naturally interpreted as a function of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
considering it extended to zero outside of
\Omega
.

3.
Formulation of the problem and main result

In this section, we introduce the fractional semilinear wave equation on non-cylindrical domains, we provide a definition of weak solution and we state our main result.

Let
T>0
be a fixed final time. Consider a family
\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
of nonempty open bounded sets with Lipschitz boundary and define the domain
\mathcal{O}\subset[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by

\mathcal{O}:=\bigcup_{t\in(0,T)}\{t\}\times\Omega_{t}.

We will assume the following structural conditions:

(2)

\begin{split}&\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\tiny$\bullet$}\,\,\text{ the family }\{\Omega_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}\text{ is non-decreasing, i.e., }\Omega_{s}\subset\Omega_{t}\text{ for every }0\leq s\leq t\leq T,\\
&\raisebox{1.07639pt}{\tiny$\bullet$}\,\,\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\text{ is an open set with Lipschitz boundary}.\end{split}

For a function
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let us consider the problem

(3)

\begin{cases}\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)=f&\text{in }\mathcal{O},\\
u=0&\text{in }((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\mathcal{O},\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega_{0},\\
\dot{u}(0,x)=v_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega_{0},\end{cases}

where:

\bullet

\displaystyle W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\,\text{is a non-negative potential with Lipschitz continuous gradient, }

(4a)

\displaystyle\text{i.e., $\exists\mathrm{K}>0$ such that }|\nabla W(x)-\nabla W(y)|\leq\mathrm{K}|x-y|\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(4b)

\bullet

\displaystyle f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\text{ with }\supp f=\overline{\{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mid f(t,x)\neq 0\}}\subset\mathcal{O},

(4c)

\bullet

\displaystyle u_{0}\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})\text{ and }v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{0}).

Remark 1
.

Problem (
3
) consists of a fractional semilinear wave equation in the non-cylindrical domain
\mathcal{O}
with forcing term
f
. Note that in (
3
) the homogeneous Dirichlet condition is prescribed in
((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\setminus\mathcal{O}
and not simply on the parabolic boundary of
\mathcal{O}
, consistently with the non-local character of the operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
.

Remark 2
.

Since the PDE in (
3
) depends on
\nabla W
, the set of solutions of the problem is invariant under translation of the potential
W
. Hence, without loss of generality, we can always assume

W(0)=0.

In particular, condition (
4a
) implies at most linear growth of
\nabla W
: there exists
C_{W}>0
such that

(5)

|\nabla W(y)|\leq C_{W}(1+|y|)\quad\text{for all }y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Furthermore, one can prove that there exists two positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
such that the following holds (see also
[
7
, Prop. 8 (iii)]
):

(6)

|W(x)-W(y)|\leq\left(C_{1}(|x|+|y|)+C_{2}\right)|x-y|\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

We define a weak solution to problem (
3
) as follows.

Definition 3

(Weak solution)
.

We say that a function
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a weak solution to problem (
3
) if the following hold:

(i)

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t}))
and
\dot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{t}))
;

(ii)

u\in C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
and
\dot{u}\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
,
u(0)=u_{0}
and
\dot{u}(0)=v_{0}
;

(iii)

u
satisfies

(7)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}[u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
with
\supp\varphi\subset\mathcal{O}
.

Finally, we introduce the energy definition for a weak solution to problem (
3
).

Definition 4
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
3
). We define the energy of
u
as

E(u(t)):=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\frac{1}{2}[u(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,T],

and we set

E_{0}:=E(u(0))=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}.

Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 5
.

There exists a weak solution
u
to problem (
3
) in the sense of Definition
3
. Moreover, the following energy inequality holds:

(8)

E(u(t))\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau\qquad\text{ for every $t\in[0,T]$}.

4.
Time discretization method

In this section, we provide a proof of Theorem
5
based on a time-discretization method in the spirit of
[
10
]
and
[
33
]
(see also
[
34
]
for the radial setting). The idea is the following: for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we approximate
\mathcal{O}
from the interior by

\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}(t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n})\times\Omega_{t_{k-1}^{n}}\subseteq\mathcal{O},

where
\{t^{n}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is a uniform partition of the interval
[0,T]
. We seek, at each step
k=1,\dots,n
, a solution
u^{n}_{k}
of the PDE

\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)=f\quad\text{in }(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k})\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}

with initial data determined through the solution at the previous step
k-1
. An existence result in the cylindrical case was proved in
[
7
]
for the homogeneous case (i.e., without the source term
f
) and, for convenience, we extend such existence result to the non-homogeneous case in Appendix
A
. We then prove that the piecewise constant in time approximants
\{u^{n}\}_{n}
built from the families
\{u^{n}_{k}\}_{n,k}
yield, in the limit, a weak solution to (
3
) in the sense of Definition
3
.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Step 1. Discrete approximants and energy estimates.

For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{t^{n}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
be the uniform partition of the interval
[0,T]
given by

t_{k}^{n}:=\frac{kT}{n}\quad\text{for }k=0,1,\dots,n.

Upon defining
u^{n}_{0}(t,x)=u_{0}(x)+tv_{0}(x)
, we inductively define

u^{n}_{k}\colon[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}\quad\text{for }k=1,\dots,n,

as a weak solution to the fractional semilinear wave equation

(9)

\begin{cases}\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)=f&\text{in }(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k})\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}},\\
u=0&\text{in }(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k})\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}),\\
u(t^{n}_{k-1},x)=u^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1},x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}},\\
\dot{u}(t^{n}_{k-1},x)=\dot{u}^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1},x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}.\end{cases}

Indeed, since we are considering a fixed cylindrical domain
(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k})\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}
, Theorem
8
in Appendix
A
guarantees the existence of a weak solution
u_{k}^{n}
to (
9
) such that

(10a)

\displaystyle u^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k};\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))\cap C^{0}([t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}];L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))\cap C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}];\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})),

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k};L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))\cap C^{0}([t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}];H^{-s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))\cap C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}];L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})),

\displaystyle\ddot{u}^{n}_{k}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k};H^{-s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})),

with initial conditions satisfied as

(10b)

\displaystyle u^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1})\text{ in the sense of }\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}),

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\dot{u}^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1})\text{ in the sense of }L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}),

and with
u_{k}^{n}
satisfying

(10c)

\begin{split}&-\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}\langle\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}dt+\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}[u^{n}_{k}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt+\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}_{k}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}\,dt\\
&=\langle\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1}),\varphi(t^{n}_{k-1})\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}-\langle\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k}),\varphi(t^{n}_{k})\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}+\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t^{n}_{k}}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}\,dt\\
&\text{for every $\varphi\in L^{1}(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k};\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))\cap W^{1,1}(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k};L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}))$.}\end{split}

In particular,

(10d)

\displaystyle E(u_{k}^{n}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}+\frac{1}{2}[u^{n}_{k}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u^{n}_{k}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1})\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}+\frac{1}{2}[{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1})]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1}))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}

\displaystyle+\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}\,d\tau\qquad\text{for every $t\in[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]$}.

Given the sequence
\{u^{n}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
, we define the approximate solution
u^{n}:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
as

(11)

u^{n}(t,x):=\begin{cases}u^{n}_{k}(t,x)&\text{if }t\in[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k})\text{ for some }k\in\{1,\dots,n\},\\
u^{n}_{n}(T,x)&\text{if }t=T.\end{cases}

From (
10
), we deduce that

(12a)

\displaystyle u^{n}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})),\text{ and }\dot{u}^{n}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T})),

with initial time conditions

(12b)

\displaystyle u^{n}(0)

\displaystyle=u^{n}_{1}(0)=u_{0}\quad\text{in the sense of }\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}),

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{n}(0)

\displaystyle=\dot{u}^{n}_{1}(0)=v_{0}\quad\text{in the sense of }L^{2}(\Omega_{0}).

Summing (
10c
) for
k=1,\dots,n
, and using that
\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t^{n}_{k-1})=\dot{u}^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1})
to simplify the boundary terms, we obtain that

(12c)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}^{n}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\text{for every $\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega_{T})$ with }\supp\varphi\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}.

Furthermore, the energy estimate (
10d
) provides that

(12d)

\displaystyle E(u^{n}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\frac{1}{2}[u^{n}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u^{n}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau\qquad\text{for every $t\in[0,T]$}.

Indeed, let
t\in[0,T]
and let
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
t^{n}_{k-1}<t\leq t^{n}_{k}
. Then, we estimate

(13)

\displaystyle E(u^{n}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\frac{1}{2}[u^{n}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u^{n}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}+\frac{1}{2}[u^{n}_{k}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u^{n}_{k}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}+\underbrace{\|W(0)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t}\setminus\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})}}_{=0}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ICukn},\eqref{ineq: en bal u^n_k}}{\leq}\underbrace{E(u^{n}_{k-1}(t^{n}_{k-1}))}_{=E(u^{n}(t_{k-1}^{n}))}+\int_{t^{n}_{k-1}}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau\qquad\text{for every }t\in[t_{k-1}^{n},t_{k}^{n}],

where for every
\tau\in(t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]
we can interchange the spaces
L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})
and
L^{2}(\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}})
because we have
\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}\subseteq\Omega_{\tau}
and
\dot{u}^{n}_{k}(\tau)=0
in
\Omega_{\tau}\setminus\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}
. Hence, (
12d
) follows by iterating (
13
).

Step 2. Pre-compactness results.
It follows from (
12d
) that for every
t\in[0,T]
we have

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[u^{n}(t)]_{s}^{2}\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau.

We now proceed with a classical Grönwall’s argument. By Cauchy inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{E_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau}_{=:\,C_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau

\displaystyle=C_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau.

By Grönwall’s inequality (see, e.g.,
[
23
]
), we obtain

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C_{0}(1+T\textup{e}^{T})\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,T].

Hence, from (
14
) we get

(15)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert\dot{u}^{n}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+[u^{n}(t)]_{s}^{2}\right)\leq C,

for a suitable positive constant
C
independent of
n
. In other words,
\{u^{n}\}_{n}
is equi-bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
and
\{\dot{u}^{n}\}_{n}
in equi-bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
.

Furthermore,
\{\ddot{u}^{n}\}_{n}
is equi-bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
. Indeed, we first note that by (
11
) and (
10a
), we have that every
\ddot{u}^{n}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, we aim to establish the following inequality:

(16)

\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\ddot{u}^{n}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\right|\leq D\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))}\quad\text{for every }\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})),

for a suitable positive constant
D
independent of
n
and
t
. Hence, consider a smooth function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega_{0})
. Since
\varphi
is an admissible choice as test function in (
12c
), we obtain that

(17)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\ddot{u}^{n}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}^{n}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt.

Now we estimate the three terms in the second line above.

For the first term, one has the inequality

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left|[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\right|\,dt\\
\text{\footnotesize(by Cauchy Schwarz for $[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}$)}\qquad&\leq\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t)]_{s}\,[\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\\
\text{\footnotesize(by the inequality $[\cdot]_{s}\leq\|\cdot\|_{s}$)}\qquad&\leq\underbrace{\sqrt{C}}_{D_{1}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))},\end{split}

where we used (
15
) to get
[u^{n}(t)]_{s}\leq\sqrt{C}
for every
t\in[0,T]
.

For the second term, it suffices to consider the estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\right|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left|\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\right|\,dt\\
\text{\footnotesize(by Cauchy Schwarz in $L^{2}(\Omega_{0})$)}\quad&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\left\lVert\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\\
&\leq\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\\
&\leq D_{2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))},\end{split}

where the constant
D_{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ineq for nabla W(y)}}{\leq}\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left(C_{W}\int_{\Omega_{0}}(1+|u^{n}(t,x)|)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(using
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2a^{2}+2b^{2}
)

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\sqrt{2C_{W}}\left(\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{0})+\left\lVert u^{n}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{D_{2}}

where the uniform bound of
u^{n}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
follows again from (
15
).

For the third term, one has (again by Cauchy Schwarz in
L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
)

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\right|&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\left\lVert\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:f}}{\leq}\underbrace{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))}}_{D_{3}}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))}.\end{split}

Hence, the estimates above yield the existence of positive constants
D_{1},D_{2},D_{3}
such that

(18)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{1}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{3}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))}.

By the density of
C_{c}^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega_{0})
in
L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))
, (
17
) and (
18
) imply the validity of (
16
). Hence the equi-boundedness of
\{\ddot{u}^{n}\}_{n}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
holds.

Step 3. Convergence of the approximants.

By (
15
), there exist
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
and
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
such that

(19)

\displaystyle u^{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}u\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})),

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}v\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T})).

It is standard to prove that
v=\dot{u}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
and, since

\supp u^{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}\subset\mathcal{O},

we conclude that
u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{t}))
and
\dot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{t}))
(hence (i) in Definition
3
holds). Moreover, by the equi-boundedness of
\{\ddot{u}^{n}\}_{n}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
together with an Ascoli–Arzelà argument for the family
\{\dot{u}^{n}\}_{n}
in
C^{0}([0,T];H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))
and for the family
\{u^{n}\}_{n}
in
C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
, we conclude that

(20)

\displaystyle u^{n}\to u\text{ in }C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T})),

\displaystyle\dot{u}^{n}\to\dot{u}\text{ in }C^{0}([0,T];H^{-s}(\Omega_{0})).

Furthermore, by
[
36
, Lemma 8.1]
we have that

L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))\cap C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))\subseteq C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})),

hence we also obtain
u\in C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
. In a similar way, one observes that

L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))\cap C^{0}([0,T];H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))\subseteq C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{0})),

hence
\dot{u}\in C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))
.
In particular, using (
12b
), the convergence provided by (
20
) and the embeddings above, one obtains that (ii) in Definition
3
holds.

In order to conclude that
u
is indeed a weak solution, we are left to prove that
u
satisfies (
7
). We reason by density, hence proving the weak formulation for smooth functions. To this aim, we first observe that given a test function

(21)

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega_{T})\text{ with }\supp\varphi\subset\mathcal{O},

then there exists
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large such that for all
n\geq\bar{n}
it holds

\supp\varphi\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}}\subset\mathcal{O}

(for a proof of the Hausdorff convergence of the increasing union of cylindrical domains to the set
\mathcal{O}
, see, for instance,
[
10
, Lem. 3.4]
).
Hence, we can take
\varphi^{n}=\varphi
for all
n\geq\bar{n}
in (
12c
), namely

\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega_{T}),\text{ with }\supp\varphi\subseteq\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}[t^{n}_{k-1},t^{n}_{k}]\times\Omega_{t^{n}_{k-1}},

and then (
7
) holds for every
\varphi
as in (
21
) passing to the limit in (
12c
): indeed, we have that

(22a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}^{n}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

(22b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}[u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt,

(22c)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt.

As before we proceed by steps to verify (
22
).

For the first limit (
22a
) it suffices to consider the estimate

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}^{n}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right.&\left.-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}^{n}(t)-\dot{u}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|,\end{split}

and the convergence
\dot{u}^{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}\dot{u}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
(using
\dot{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega_{T})\subset L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
as test function for the convergence in (
19
)).

For the second limit (
22b
), we note that the regularity of
\varphi
implies

(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{T})),

hence we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}[u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u^{n}(t)-u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(t),u^{n}(t)-u(t)\rangle_{{H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})}}\,dt\right|

,

and the conclusion follows by the convergence
{u}^{n}\rightharpoonup^{*}{u}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
(using
(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}))
as test function for the convergence in (
19
)).

Finally, for the third limit (
22c
), it suffices to consider

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u^{n}(t))-\nabla W(u(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n}(t))-\nabla W(u(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq\sqrt{T}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n}(t))-\nabla W(u(t))\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leq C_{1}\sqrt{T}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left\lVert u^{n}-u\right\rVert_{C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))},

where we have used Cauchy Schwarz both in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
and on
(0,T)
and the positive constant
C_{1}
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u^{n}(t))-\nabla W(u(t))\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: nabla W}}{\leq}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{K}^{2}|u^{n}(t)-u(t)|^{2}\,dx\right)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\,\,\leq\underbrace{\mathrm{K}\sqrt{T}}_{C_{1}}\left\lVert u^{n}-u\right\rVert_{C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}.

Hence, the convergence of
u^{n}
to
u
provided by (
20
) yields the validity of (
22c
). Thus, (
22
) holds.

To conclude the proof of (
7
), we just note that the space

\{\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T)\times\Omega_{T})\colon\,\supp\varphi\subset\mathcal{O}\}

is dense in the space

\{\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))\colon\,\supp\varphi\subset\mathcal{O}\}

(a density result in the same spirit can be found in
[
18
, Lemma 2.8]
). Therefore, the validity of (iii) in Definition
3
has been established.

Finally, we are left to prove the energy inequality (
8
). We first note that by inequality (
6
) in Remark
2
, one can easily deduce that for a.e.
t\in[0,T]

(23)

\left\lVert W(u^{n}(t))-W(u(t))\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\leq\tilde{C}\left\lVert u^{n}(t)-u(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}

for a suitable constant
\tilde{C}>0
independent of
t
and
n
, due to the boundedness of
u^{n}
and
u
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
(see
[
7
, Prop. 8 (iii)]
).
We now integrate (
12d
) between arbitrary times
\alpha,\beta
with
0\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq T
. By (
19
) and (
20
) and by standard lower semicontinuity
arguments along with estimate (
23
), as
n\to+\infty
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t})}+\frac{1}{2}[u(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq(\beta-\alpha)\left(\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}\right)+\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}^{n}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau\,dt.

By the arbitrariness of
\alpha
and
\beta
, the above inequality yields the validity of (
8
) for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
.

In order to provide the energy inequality for every time, we note that the energy estimates can be replicated in order to obtain the following:
\dot{u}\in C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([\overline{t},T];L^{2}(\Omega_{\overline{t}}))
for every
\overline{t}\in[0,T)
(cf. the existence result in the Appendix
A
). Hence, we fix a time
\overline{t}\in[0,T)
and consider a decreasing sequence
t_{k}\searrow\overline{t}
along which the energy inequality is satisfied (which is possible since we already proved it holds a.e. in
[0,T]
). Then, again by weak lower semicontinuity arguments along with estimate (
23
), we deduce that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{u}(\overline{t})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\overline{t}})}+\frac{1}{2}[u(\overline{t})]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u(\overline{t}))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{\overline{t}})}\\
&\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{u}(t_{k})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{t_{k}})}+\frac{1}{2}[u(t_{k})]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u(t_{k}))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{t_{k}})}\right)\\
&\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{\overline{t}}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\tau})}\,d\tau.\end{split}

Hence, (
8
) holds for every
\overline{t}\in[0,T)
. Finally, the case
\overline{t}=T
can be treated just by considering a final time
\tilde{T}>T
, setting
\Omega_{t}:=\Omega_{T}
for every
t\in[T,\tilde{T}]
and arguing as above. This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Penalty method

In this section, we provide another proof of Theorem
5
by means of a penalty argument, whose idea goes back to
[
37
]
. The strategy consists of first considering a parameter
\varepsilon>0
and constructing an approximate solution
u_{\varepsilon}
(obtained by the standard Galerkin method) to problem (
3
), modified by adding a penalty term of the form
\varepsilon^{-1}\mathscr{P}\dot{u}
. Here,
\mathscr{P}
is the characteristic function of the complement of the set
\mathcal{O}
. The family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
admits a cluster point
u
, which solves (
3
); the penalization term forces
u
to vanish outside
\mathcal{O}
.
We mentioned that, in the same spirit, other types of penalties are possible: see for example
[
50
]
, where the (linear) wave equation for the Laplace operator in non-cylindrical domains is treated.

Proof of Theorem
5
.

Step 1. Penalized problems at level
\varepsilon
.

For every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, we consider the following formal penalized problem

(24)

\begin{cases}\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathscr{P}\dot{u}=f&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega_{T},\\
u=0&\text{in }(0,T)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega_{T}),\end{cases}

with initial conditions given by
u(0)=u_{0}
and
\dot{u}(0)=v_{0}
, and
\mathscr{P}
given by

\mathscr{P}(t,x)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }(t,x)\,\in\,\mathcal{O},\\
1&\,\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We aim to prove the existence of a weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
to problem (
24
), in the sense that

(25)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}[u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}
, where

\mathcal{X}:=\{\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))\colon\,\varphi(0)=\varphi(T)=0\}.

Moreover,
u_{\varepsilon}
is also required to satisfy the energy inequality

(26)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\left\lVert W(u_{\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(\tau,x)|\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau,

where
E_{0}
is given by Definition
4
. Existence of such a solution
u_{\varepsilon}
is obtained via a Galerkin approximation, hence we need to introduce a sequence of approximating spaces.

Step 2. Finite dimensional approximating spaces.
Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthogonal basis of
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
, and at the same time an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
. More specifically, we choose the eigenfunctions of the fractional Laplacian on
\Omega_{T}
, namely

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}w_{k}=\lambda_{k}w_{k}&\text{on }\Omega_{T},\\
w_{k}\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}),\end{cases}

which are orthogonal in
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
and orthonormal in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})

Note that, if
\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
, then for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
it holds

[\varphi,w_{k}]_{s}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}\varphi(x)(-\Delta)^{s/2}w_{k}(x)\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)(-\Delta)^{s}w_{k}(x)\,dx=\lambda_{k}\langle\varphi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

Hence, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(27)

\langle\varphi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}=\lambda^{-1}_{k}[\varphi,w_{k}]_{s}\qquad\text{for every }\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}).

In particular, (
27
) yields
[w_{k}]^{2}_{s}=[w_{k},w_{k}]_{s}=\lambda_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

For each
M>0
, define
V_{M}\subset\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
to be the subspace

V_{M}:=\textup{span}\,\{w_{1},\dots,w_{M}\}.

Let
\pi_{M}
be the
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
-orthogonal projection onto the subspace
V_{M}
, namely

\pi_{M}:\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})\to V_{M},\,\phi\mapsto\pi_{M}(\phi):=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\langle\phi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}w_{k}.

Note that for any
\phi\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
, it holds that

\begin{split}\left\lVert\phi-\pi_{M}(\phi)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}=\left\lVert\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}\langle\phi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}w_{k}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}|\langle\phi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}|^{2}\left\lVert w_{k}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\\
=\sum_{k=M+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}\lambda_{k}|\langle\phi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}|\langle\phi,w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}|^{2}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}[\phi]_{s}^{2}\,\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{s}^{2}\,,\end{split}

so that we have the decay estimate

(28)

\left\lVert\phi-\pi_{M}(\phi)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{M}}}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{s}\qquad\text{for every }\phi\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}).

Step 3. Galerkin-penalized approximants
u_{M,\varepsilon}
.

Now, let
\{a^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{b^{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the coefficients of
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
with respect to the given basis
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
. For every
M>0
, define

\displaystyle u_{0,M}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M}a^{k}w_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad v_{0,M}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M}b^{k}w_{k}.

We seek for a
V_{M}
-valued weak solution
u_{M,\varepsilon}
to (
24
), namely we seek for a coefficients function
d_{M,\varepsilon}\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{M},t\mapsto(d^{1}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dots,d^{M}_{M,\varepsilon}(t))
such that the function given by

(29)

u_{M,\varepsilon}(t,x)=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\,d^{k}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\,w_{k}(x)\qquad\text{for }t\in[0,T],x\in\Omega_{T},

is a solution of

(30)

\begin{split}\left\langle\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\phi]_{s}&+\left\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\\
&+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}=\langle f(t),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\end{split}

for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
and for every
\phi\in V_{M}
, coupled with initial conditions given by

(31a)

\displaystyle u_{M,\varepsilon}(0)=u_{0,M},

(31b)

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(0)=v_{0,M}.

By testing (
30
) with
\phi=w_{1},\dots,w_{M}
, the components
(d^{1}_{M,\varepsilon},\ldots,d_{M,\varepsilon}^{M})
of
d_{M,\varepsilon}
satisfy the following ODE system

(32)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\ddot{d}^{k}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)+\mathcal{A}^{k}(d_{M,\varepsilon}(t))+\mathcal{W}^{k}(d_{M,\varepsilon}(t))+\mathcal{R}^{k}(t,\dot{d}_{M,\varepsilon}(t))=\mathcal{F}^{k}(t)\quad k=1,\dots,M,\\
d_{M,\varepsilon}(0)=(a^{1},\dots,a^{M}),\\
\dot{d}_{M,\varepsilon}(0)=(b^{1},\dots,b^{M}),\end{cases}

where, for every
y=(y^{1},\ldots,y^{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
,
z=(z^{1},\ldots,z^{M})\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
and
k=1,\dots,M
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{k}(y):=[y^{k}w_{k},w_{k}]_{s}=y^{k}[w_{k}]^{2}_{s}=\lambda_{k}y^{k}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}^{k}(y):=\int_{\Omega_{T}}\nabla W\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\,y^{j}\,w_{j}(x)\right)\cdot w_{k}(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{k}(t,z):=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(t,x)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}z^{j}\,w_{j}(x)\right)\cdot w_{k}(x)\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{k}(t):=\int_{\Omega_{T}}f(t,x)\cdot w_{k}(x)\,dx.

We now prove that (
32
) admits a classical solution
d_{M,\varepsilon}\in W^{2,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{M})
. Let

g\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2M}\to\mathbb{R}^{2M},\quad(t,(y,z))\to(z,(g^{1}(t,y,z),\dots,g^{M}(t,y,z))),

with
g^{k}
be given by

g^{k}(t,y,z):=\mathcal{F}^{k}(t)-\mathcal{A}^{k}(y)-\mathcal{W}^{k}(y)-\mathcal{R}^{k}(t,z)\quad k=1,\dots,M.

We have that:

(i)

g\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2M}\to\mathbb{R}^{2M}
is a Carathéodory function;

(ii)

there exists a constant
C>0
(possibly depending on
M
and
\varepsilon
) such that

|g(t,y,z)|\leq C(1+|y|+|z|)\quad\text{for every }(t,(y,z))\in[0,T]\times R^{2M}.

Indeed, claim (i) follows by the observation that for almost every
t\in[0,T]
the map
(y,z)\mapsto g(t,(y,z))
is continuous, thanks to the assumption on the continuity of
\nabla W
and the linearity with respect to
y
and
z
of
\mathcal{A}^{k}
and
\mathcal{R}^{k}
, respectively. Secondly, for every fixed
(y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{2M}
, the map
t\mapsto g(t,(y,z))
is measurable: the measurability of the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{R}^{k}(t,z)
and the measurability of the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}^{k}(t)
follows from Fubini’s Theorem.

On the other hand, claim (ii) holds due to the following estimates:

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}^{k}(t)|\leq\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left\lVert w_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T],

\displaystyle|\mathcal{R}^{k}(t,z)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j=1}^{M}|z^{j}|\int_{\Omega_{T}}|w_{j}(x)||w_{k}(x)|\,dx\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j=1}^{M}|z^{j}|\quad\text{for any }t\in[0,T],z\in\mathbb{R}^{M},

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{W}^{k}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\Omega_{T}}\nabla W\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\,y^{j}\,w_{j}(x)\right)\cdot w_{k}(x)\,dx\right|\leq\int_{\Omega_{T}}C_{W}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left|y^{j}\,w_{j}(x)\right|\right)|w_{k}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{W}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{T})}+C_{W}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\left(|y^{j}|\int_{\Omega_{T}}|w_{j}(x)||w_{k}(x)|\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{C_{W}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{T})},C_{W}\right\}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{M}|y^{j}|\right)\quad\text{for any }y\in\mathbb{R}^{M},

where we have used the Cauchy Schwarz inequality in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
, the orthonormality of the system
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
and inequality (
5
) in Remark
2
. Hence, claim (ii) is proven, recalling the equivalence of norms on the finite-dimensional spaces.

Then, we apply the existence result provided by
[
26
, Thm. A.2]
(see also
[
25
, Chap. 1, Thms. 1 & 2]
), and we obtain the existence of a classical solution
d_{M,\varepsilon}\in W^{2,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{M})
to (
32
). In particular, the function
u_{M,\varepsilon}
given by (
29
) satisfies (
30
) with initial conditions (
31a
) and (
31b
). Moreover,

u_{M,\varepsilon}\in C^{0}([0,T];V_{M}),\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\in C^{0}([0,T];V_{M})\text{ and }\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;V_{M}).

Step 4. Energy estimates.
We first observe that, by
d_{M,\varepsilon}\in W^{2,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{M})
and by considering
\phi=\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}
in equation (
30
) (see also
[
7
, Remark 2]
), a direct computation yields

(33)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\,\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(t,x)|\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t,x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\langle f(t),\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

Then, integrating (
33
) between
0
and
t
,
0\leq t\leq T
, we obtain

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(\tau,x)|\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0,M}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0,M}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0,M})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(t),\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}.

We note that

\lim_{M\to+\infty}\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0,M}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0,M}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0,M})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}}_{E_{0}}.

Hence, there exists
M^{*}>0
large enough, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert v_{0,M}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{0,M}]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{0,M})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}\leq 2E_{0}\quad\text{for every }M\geq M^{*}.

In particular, for every
M\geq M^{*}
, we obtain that

(35)

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(\tau,x)|\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}\,dxd\tau

\displaystyle\leq 2E_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,d\tau.

By means of the same Gronwall’s argument used to derive (
15
) from (
14
), we can prove that there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and
M
, such that, for all
M>M^{*}
,

(36)

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}\right)\leq C.

In turn, there exists a positive constant
c>0
independent of
M,\varepsilon
and
t
such that, for all
M>M^{*}
,

(37)

\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{2}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{T})}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathscr{P}(\tau,x)|\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(\tau,x)|^{2}\,dxd\tau\leq c.

Step 5. Limits as
M\to+\infty
.
For every fixed
\varepsilon>0
, the uniform bound in (
36
) yields the existence of a function
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
with
\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
such that

(38)

\displaystyle u_{M,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}u_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})),

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{ in }L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T})).

By integrating (
30
) in time over
[0,T]
, we can deduce that, for each
M>M^{*}
, we have

(39)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)]_{s}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

for every
\varphi_{M}\in\mathcal{Y}_{M}
, where
\mathcal{Y}_{M}:=C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T;V_{M})
. Moreover, we note that
\{\mathcal{Y}_{M}\}_{M}
defines an increasing sequence of subspaces of
\mathcal{Y}:=C^{\infty}_{c}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
, with union dense in
\mathcal{Y}
.
Both
\mathcal{Y}_{M}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are clearly subspaces of
\mathcal{X}
.

In order to prove (
25
), we now pass to the limit in (
39
). To do so, fix a function
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
with
\supp\varphi\subset\mathcal{O}
. Let
\{\varphi_{M}\}_{M\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{Y}_{M}
be the sequence of functions given by

(40)

\varphi_{M}(t):=\pi_{M}(\varphi(t))=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\langle\varphi(t),w_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}w_{k}\in V_{M}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T],

for which we also have that

\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)=\pi_{M}(\dot{\varphi}(t))=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\langle\dot{\varphi}(t),w_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}w_{j}\in V_{M}\quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].

One can prove that, by construction and by Dominated Convergence Theorem, the sequence of functions
\{\varphi_{M}\}_{M\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
\mathcal{X}
to
\varphi
.
Then, we prove the following:

(41a)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

(41b)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)]_{s}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}[u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt,

(41c)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

(41d)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,

(41e)

\displaystyle\lim_{M\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt.

For the first limit (
41a
), it suffices to consider the estimate

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)-\dot{\varphi}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|+\sqrt{C}\left\lVert\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)-\dot{\varphi}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))},\end{split}

and the convergence
\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
(using
\dot{\varphi}\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
as test function for the convergence in (
38
)) and the convergence of
\varphi_{M}
to
\varphi
in
\mathcal{X}
along with the uniform bound for
\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}
provided by the constant
C>0
in (
36
).

For the second limit (
41b
), we note that
\varphi\in\mathcal{Y}
implies
(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}))
, hence by triangle inequality and Cauchy Schwarz inequality for the seminorm
[\cdot,\cdot]_{s}
and the inequality
[\cdot]_{s}\leq\|\cdot\|_{s}
in
\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})
, we get

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)]_{s}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}[u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(t),u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle_{{H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})}}\,dt\right|+\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)]_{s}\,[\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(t),u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle_{{H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T})}}\,dt\right|+\sqrt{C}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))},\end{split}

and the conclusion follows by the convergence
{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{T}))
(using
(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{T}))
as test function for the convergence in (
38
) and the convergence of
\varphi_{M}
to
\varphi
in
\mathcal{X}
along with the equi-boundedness of
u_{M,\varepsilon}
provided by the constant
C>0
in (
36
).

For the third limit (
41c
), by the equi-boundedness of
\{\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\}_{M}
in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
together with an Ascoli–Arzelà argument for
\{u_{M,\varepsilon}\}_{M}
in
C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))
, we deduce that

(42)

u_{M,\varepsilon}\to u_{\varepsilon}\text{ in }C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T})).

Then, we can proceed in a similar way and we can apply triangle inequality and Cauchy Schwarz inequality in
L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
, obtaining

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))-\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))-\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{T}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))-\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t))\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lVert\varphi_{M}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon})\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\sqrt{T}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\left\lVert u_{M,\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}+C_{2}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))},

where the two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
coming from the terms containing
\nabla W
in the last inequality are given by the following straightforward estimates (using the properties of
W
and the Cauchy Schwarz inequality):

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))-\nabla W(u_{\varepsilon}(t))\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq: nabla W}}{\leq}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\Omega_{T}}\mathrm{K}^{2}|u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\,dx\right)\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\,\,\leq\underbrace{\mathrm{K}\sqrt{T\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{T})}}_{C_{1}}\left\lVert u_{M,\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert_{C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))},

and

\displaystyle\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\lVert\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t))\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{ineq for nabla W(y)}}{\leq}\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}\left(C_{W}\int_{\Omega_{T}}(1+|u_{M,\varepsilon}(t,x)|)^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(using

)

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\sqrt{2C_{W}}\left(\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Omega_{T})+\left\lVert u_{M,\varepsilon}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}_{C_{2}}

Hence, summarizing the above estimates allows us to conclude that (
41c
) holds, thanks to the convergence provided by (
42
) and the convergence of
\varphi_{n}
to
\varphi
in
\mathcal{X}
.

For the fourth limit (
41d
), it suffices to estimate

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\mathscr{P}(t)\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|+\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\mathscr{P}(t)\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt,\\
&\leq\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)-\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(t),\mathscr{P}(t)\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|+\sqrt{C}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}-\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{T}))},\end{split}

and conclude by the convergence
\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup^{*}\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}
in

\varphi_{M}
to
\varphi
in
\mathcal{X}
along with the uniform bound for

Finally, for the fifth limit (

\begin{split}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right.&-\left.\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|\\

Therefore, passing to the limit in (

Step 6. Initial values for

In order to prove that
\dot{u}_{\varepsilon}(0)=v_{0}
, an additional uniform bound on second derivatives is needed, in our case a bound of the sequence
\{\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}\}_{M}\subset L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}))

\varepsilon>0

\mathcal{X}_{0}

\mathcal{X}_{0}:=\{\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))\colon\,\varphi(0)=\varphi(T)=0\}\subset\mathcal{X},

is dense in
L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))
, it suffices to provide an estimate for the action of every
\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}
against every
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}_{0}
. Since
\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\in L^{2}(\Omega_{0})
for almost every

, one has

(43)

\int_{0}^{T}\langle\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt.

We now rewrite the right hand side of the previous equation: using that
\varphi(t)=0
in
\Omega_{T}\setminus\Omega_{0}
, adding and subtracting the term
\varphi_{M}
(cf. (
40
)), using the orthogonality to
V_{M}
of the function
\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)-\dot{\varphi}(t)
, and finally using (
39

, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle\ddot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{H^{-s}(\Omega_{0}),\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0})}\,dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\underbrace{\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)-\dot{\varphi}_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt}_{=0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)]_{s}\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt.

Now, reasoning in a similar way as before and essentially replacing
\varphi_{M}-\varphi
simply with
\varphi
in the estimates above for proving (
41

5
) in Remark
2
, the regularity of

(see (

)) and the equi-boundedness of

such that the following inequalities hold:

(44)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}[u_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)]_{s}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{1}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u_{M,\varepsilon}(t)),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{2}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq D_{3}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega_{0}))}.

Note that we used the standard property
\left\lVert\varphi_{M}\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))}\leq\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))}

Furthermore, we can prove that there exists a positive constant

(45)

\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|\leq D_{4}\left\lVert\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega_{0}))}.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|}_{=0}

\displaystyle\,+\left|\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathscr{P}(t)\,\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t),\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\dot{u}_{M,\varepsilon}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\left\lVert\varphi_{M}(t)-\varphi(t)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega_{T})}\,dt

Appendix A
Fractional semilinear wave equations in cylindrical domains

In this appendix we recall a result on the existence of weak solutions to the fractional semilinear wave equation, which we generalize by adding a non-zero forcing term
f
. The proof can be done by a constructive time-discrete variational scheme whose main ideas date back to
[
44
]
(see also
[
2
]
for the minimizing movements approach introduced by De Giorgi) and which have, since then, been adapted to many instances of parabolic and hyperbolic equations. For instance, the homogeneous fractional wave equations (i.e., with no potential term and no forcing term) have been studied in
[
9
]
(see also
[
8
,
7
]
). The general case with a forcing term can be tackled by defining a suitable approximant for the function
f
(see
[
18
, Thm. 3.1]
).

Let
T,s>0
and
m,d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. For a function
u\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let us consider the problem

(48)

\begin{cases}\ddot{u}+(-\Delta)^{s}u+\nabla W(u)=f&\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{in }[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega,\\
\dot{u}(0,x)=v_{0}(x)&\text{for }x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

where:

\bullet

\displaystyle W\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{m})\,\text{is a non-negative potential with Lipschitz continuous gradient, }

(49a)

\displaystyle\text{i.e., $\exists\mathrm{K}>0$ such that }|\nabla W(x)-\nabla W(y)|\leq\mathrm{K}|x-y|\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{m},

(49b)

\bullet

\displaystyle f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\text{ with }\supp f\subset(0,T)\times\Omega,

(49c)

\bullet

\displaystyle u_{0}\in\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega)\text{ and }v_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).

We define a weak solution to (
48
) as follows.

Definition 6
.

We say that a function
u\colon(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a weak solution to problem (
48
) if the following holds:

(i)

u\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))
,
\dot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))

and
\ddot{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{-s}(\Omega))
;

(ii)

for every
\varphi\in L^{1}(0,T;\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,1}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
one has

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\dot{u}(t),\dot{\varphi}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}dt+\int_{0}^{T}[u(t),\varphi(t)]_{s}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\langle\nabla W(u(t)),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\\
=\langle\dot{u}(0),\varphi(0)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\langle\dot{u}(T),\varphi(T)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{T}\langle f(t),\varphi(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,dt\end{split}

with

u(0)=u_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{u}(0)=v_{0}.

Finally, we introduce the energy definition for a weak solution to problem (
48
).

Definition 7
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
48
). We define the energy of
u
as

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\|\dot{u}(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{2}[u(t)]_{s}^{2}+\|W(u(t))\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)},\quad\text{for every }t\in[0,T].

The following theorem can be proved following the same strategy used in
[
7
, Thm. 3]
, with the only difference consisting in the treatment of the forcing term
f
and taking into account
[
36
, Lemma 8.1]
.

Theorem 8
.

There exists a weak solution
u
to problem (
48
) in the sense of Definition
6
. Moreover, the following energy inequality holds:

E(u(t))\leq E(u(0))+\int_{0}^{t}\langle f(\tau),\dot{u}(\tau)\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,d\tau\qquad\text{for every }t\in[0,T].

Furthermore,

u\in C^{0}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega))\cap C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];\tilde{H}^{s}(\Omega))\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{u}\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{-s}(\Omega))\cap C^{0}_{\text{\tiny sc}}([0,T];L^{2}(\Omega)).

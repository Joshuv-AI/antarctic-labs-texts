Quantum Chemical Studies of Bendamustine and Melphalan in Water as Antiblood Cancer Agents
Authors: Roman Boča, Juraj Štofko, Miriam Ladická, Cyril Rajnák
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractA hybrid B3LYP version of the Density Functional Theory was applied in full geometry optimization followed by vibrational analysis of mustard-type molecules acting as antiblood cancer agents: melphalan and bendamustine. All calculations were performed with water as a solvent. In addition to the ground-state properties (dipole moment, quadrupole moment, dipole polarizability, solvated surface and volume, zero-point vibration energy, total entropic term), properties that characterize adiabatic redox processes (ionization energy, electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, electrophilicity index) together with the absolute oxidation and reduction potentials were evaluated. Vibrational frequencies were also calculated, and frontier orbitals were displayed and analyzed. Bendamustine and melphalan in their canonical (amino acid, A) forms were found to have very low ionization energies Ei = 4.85 eV and consequently low (in absolute value) oxidation potentials Eoxo̷ = −4.92 V based on the reaction Gibbs energies; this indicates considerable antioxidant capacity. The zwitterionic (Z) form of melphalan is more stable in water as opposite the bendamustine, for which the Z-form is less stable in water: ΔGZ-A = −2.7 and 2.9 kcal mol–1, respectively. The cationic residues of bendamustine-hydrochloride (bendamustinium) and melphalan-hydrochloride (melphalanium) have properties essentially analogous to those of the canonical forms, except for a very large dipole moment for the Bd-Z, p = 29.7 debye. All calculations were refined with the ab initio post-Hartree–Fock method DLPNO–CCSD(T), which is a variant of the “gold standard coupled cluster theory” CCSD(T) that accounts for the major part of the correlation energy (DLPNO–CCSD(T)–Domain Localized Pair Natural Orbitals–Coupled Cluster Singles + Doubles + Triples method). It is confirmed that (i) the canonical form of bendamustine is more stable in water compared to the zwitterionic form by ΔGZ-A = 4.8 kcal mol–1; (ii) low ionization energy Ei = 5.10 eV causes low absolute oxidation potential Eox* = −5.10 V and increased antioxidant capacity; and (iii) the redox properties of bendamustinium are analogous to the zwitterionic form of bendamustine and are significantly different from the canonical form of bendamustine. The energy profile of electron–proton coupled transfer shows that the SET-PT mechanism proceeds over lower barriers than the concurrent SPLET mechanism.

Introduction

The group of anticancer chemotherapy drugs is currently relatively rich. One structural motif is derived from nitrogen mustards (NMs): cytotoxic organic compounds with a bis(2-chloroethyl)amino ((ClC2H4)2NR) functional group, such as HN1, bis(2-chloroethyl)ethylamine (HN1), bis(2-chloroethyl)methylamine (HN2), and tris(2-chloroethyl)amine (HN3). These toxic compounds were later replaced by less aggressive drugs such as cyclophosphamide, uramustine, and chlorambucil (Figure 1). Other nitrogen mustards include melphalan and bendamustine.1

The bendamustine (Bd) molecule tends to hydrolyze in aqueous solutions and is therefore provided in the hydrochloride form Bd·HCl (bendamustinium chloride). There is a report on the X-ray structure analysis of Bd·HCl: it exists in three anhydrous polymorphic forms (I, III, and IV) and one monohydrate form (II).2 There are reports on the structure of related compounds such as 4-[bis(2-chloroethyl)amino]benzaldehyde and 2-[3-((Z)-2-{4-[bis(2-chloroethyl)amino]-phenyl}ethenyl)-5,5-dimethylcyclohex-2-en-1-idene]propane dinitrile.3,4

The studied molecules are structurally very flexible, as they contain a large number of rotatable C–C bonds (N = 4, 8, and 9) leading to 3N rotamers. Melphalan is the α-amino acid and therefore exists in aqueous solution in a zwitterionic form containing carboxyl residue −COO– and an ammonium group −NH3+. Bendamustine (Bd) crystallizes as hydrochloride (BdH)+Cl– with a protonated nitrogen atom of the benzimidazole ring −NH+.

Medical Aspects

The anticancer activity of these drugs lies in the binding of electrophilic alkyl groups, forming covalent bonds with electron-rich nucleophilic centers (such as DNA base guanine). This leads to DNA interstrand cross-links. Bifunctional covalent binding can lead to cell death through several pathways.

Bendamustine has properties of antimetabolites and alkylating agents. As a bifunctional alkylating agent, bendamustine contains two reactive groups that can separately bind to DNA sites. Compared with other alkylating agents, bendamustine has unique cytotoxic properties: it improves penetration and localization in DNA and causes multiple breaks in double-stranded DNA that persist longer. Bendamustine is administered intravenously (into a vein) in the form of a hydrochloride adduct (bendamustinium chloride) as a 30–60 min infusion, most often in a dosage of 90–200 mg/m2 (body surface) 2 days in a row in 21–28 day cycles, usually a total of 6 cycles.5 Dosage depends on the treatment regimen used. After administration, it is mainly bound to albumin and is mostly nonenzymatically hydrolyzed to the less active metabolites HP1 and HP2 and in the liver to the active metabolites M3 and M4. It is excreted by the kidneys. According to SPC (prescribing information), dosage adjustment is not necessary in case of mild liver damage (serum bilirubin <1.2 mg/100 cm3) and renal creatinine clearance above 10 cm3/min. Although it was initially administered to patients with multiple myeloma, it has now found application in the treatment of other lymphoproliferative diseases, especially indolent B-cell lymphomas, among which the most frequently occurring follicular lymphoma is included. Toxicity is generally low; therefore, it is the drug of choice in elderly patients. Hematological toxicity is the most common. Bendamustine may induce prolonged lymphopenia, thereby increasing the risk of infections.

Melphalan (alkeran) is a dialkylating agent with two alkylating groups. Melphalan is eliminated by spontaneous hydrolysis and renal excretion. Its excretion is increased by creatinine clearance, and renal insufficiency is associated with a higher risk of toxicity; therefore, dose reduction is necessary. In the past, it was used for various types of malignancies, including ovarian and breast cancers. Currently, it is mainly used in the treatment of multiple myeloma.6 Due to its ability to induce bone marrow ablation with minimal extramedullary toxicity and a significant immunosuppressive effect, it is part of preparatory regimens before autologous or allogeneic transplantation. It is administered intravenously or in tablets in various dosage regimens. The most common toxicity is hematological toxicity and mucosal toxicity of the digestive tract (mucositis), the intensity and frequency of which correlate with the dose.

Aim of the Study

Quantum chemical calculations are becoming popular tools in the study of molecular properties of small- and medium-sized biogenic molecules.7 Among them, the B3LYP variant of the Density Functional Theory plays a central role as it enables geometry optimization in a reasonable computational time.8−12

In this paper, we focus on evaluating the electronic properties of neutral melphalan and bendamustine molecules in their ground state as well as in the derived cations and anions. Adiabatic ionization energy and electron affinity are used to calculate molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, and the electrophilicity index. Absolute oxidation and reduction potentials are obtained from the reaction Gibbs energy after complete geometry optimization and complete vibrational analysis. These serve as criteria for antioxidant and antireduction capacity. The questions addressed are (i) which of the canonical and/or zwitterionic structures of melphalan and bendamustine is more stable in the aqueous environment; (ii) what are the unique electronic and redox characteristics and fundamental differences in their molecular properties; and (iii) what are the (dis)similarities of the bendamustine neutral molecule(s) compared to the cationic residue of the hydrochloride form (bendamustinium). In addition to electron transfer studied through redox reactions, proton transfer and electron–proton coupled transfer of bendamustine and melphalan are also investigated. These aspects have not been studied so far.

The results can help us to better understand the chemical stability of anticancer drugs in the aquatic environment. The mechanism of drug action is generally based on a set of different physicochemical events. A full-fledged quantum chemical description combined with the definition of selected physical quantities can be helpful in the synthesis of tailor-made drugs.

Methods

Quantum chemical calculations consistently follow the methodology described previously using the B3LYB-DFT and DLPNO-CCSD(T) methods.13 The B3LYP method enables geometry optimization and vibrational analysis in a short amount of time. The def2-TZVPD (triple-ζ-valence with polarization and diffuse functions) basis set consists of Cl: 15s10p4d1f contracted to the 6s6p3d1f pattern; O, N, and C: 12s7p3d1f contracted to 6s4p3d1f; H: 5s2p contracted to 3s2p.14

The DLPNO–CCSD(T) method (Domain Localized Pair Natural Orbitals–Coupled Cluster Singles + Doubles + Triples) is an ab initio post-Hartree–Fock variant that involves the major part of the correlation energy. The aug-cc-pVTZ basis set consists of Cl: 42s17p3d2f contracted to a 6s5p3d2f pattern; O, N, and C: 19s6p3d2f contracted to 5s4p3d2f; and H: 6s3p2d contracted to 4s3p2d. The augmented basis set is essential for a reliable description of molecular anions and is compatible with post-Hartree–Fock methods that include the correlation energy.15,16

The ORCA package was used with sufficiently large basis sets that include polarization and diffusion functions.17−19 The solvent effect was simulated using the CPCM model parametrized for water.20 Electronic properties such as molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, and electrophilicity index were evaluated according to the original sources.21−23

The quantum chemical methods used are compared in Table S1. The B3LYP method overestimates the total energy E0 which includes some correlation energy but to an uncontrolled extent. More exact are post Hartree–Fock methods in the order MP2 (2nd-order many-body perturbation theory with Mo̷ller–Plesset Hamiltonian partitioning), CCSD (coupled cluster with singles and doubles), and CCSD(T) (with triples correction); the latter contains almost the complete electron correlation energy. DLPNO–CCSD(T) is a simplified version of CCSD(T) with only a small loss of correlation energy but still tractable for medium-sized molecules: the computational time is proportional to the sixth-power of the basis set size, CPU ∼ M6.

Results and Discussion

Molecular Geometry

The molecular structures of mechlorethamine and melphalan in their canonical forms were retrieved from the PubChem database and used as a starting point for full geometry optimization, see Figure 2. Melphalan, as an α-amino acid Mp-A, was considered also in the zwitterionic form Mp-Z. Melphalan contains 37 atoms, and the def2-TZVPD basis set used includes 889 functions.

Geometries of mechlorethamine (1), melphalan (2), and bendamustinum chloride (3) as offered by Pubchem and CCDC databases used as a starting trial for the geometry optimization. Color code: green – Cl, red – O, blue – N, black – C, white – H.

For the hydrochloride form of the solid-state structure of bendamustine, the coordinates were extracted from the Cambridge Structural Database (Cambridge Crystallographic Data Centre, CCDC). The Cl– ion was removed to give the cation BdH+. This compound has been considered both in its canonical (acidic) form, Bd-A, and the zwitterion Bd-Z. Bendamustine contains 44 atoms, and the basis set used covers 1064 functions.

Geometry optimization was followed by vibrational analysis resulting in thermodynamic properties such as the internal energy, the zero-point vibration energy Ezpe, the entropic term ST along with its translational, rotational, and vibrational contributions, and finally the standard Gibbs energy.

The optimized geometry for melphalan Mp-A in water has the following characteristic bond lengths (hereafter referred to in Å): C–Cl = 1.820, 1.821; N–C = 1.391, 1.454; C–O = 1.211, C–O(H) = 1.341; O–H = 0.974; C–N(H2) = 1.463; N–H = 1.015, 1.016.

Melphalan (abbreviated 4B2CA-LPA or Alkeran) was studied by DFT-B3LYP methodology in an extended basis set 6-311++G(d,p). The geometry in the canonical amino acid form was optimized in vacuo, and then the molecular properties were evaluated in solvents of different polarities.24 The calculated characteristic bond lengths are C–Cl = 1.817, 1.817; N–C = 1.399, 1.455; C–O = 1.207, C–O(H) = 1.348; O–H = 0.97; C–N(H2) = 1.463; N–H = 1.015, 1.016; these are almost the same as those mentioned above in water. (However, the role of HOMO and LUMO in estimating vertical ionization energy, electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, and electrophilicity index is problematic.)

In the zwitterionic form of melphalan Mp-Z, the hydrogen atom migrated from the carboxyl group to the amine forming ammonium unit; the entire molecular skeleton remains conserved (see Figure S1). Relaxed bond lengths include C–O = 1.246, 1.258; C–N(H3) = 1.507; and N–H = 1.021, 1.021, 1.021 (in water). Comparison of the total electronic energy E0 shows that the Mp-Z form is more stable compared to Mp-A by ΔE = E(Z0) – E(A0) = −3.7 kcal mol–1 in water. The difference in standard Gibbs energy is ΔG = Go̷(Z0) – Go̷(A0) = −2.7 kcal mol–1. Note that isomers differing in the total energy of about 5 kcal mol–1 (20 kJ mol–1) are almost equally populated at room temperature.

The cationic fragment of melphalan hydrochloride MpH+ has C–Cl = 1.819; N–C = 1.386, 1.457; C–O = 1.210, C–O(H) = 1.346; O–H = 0.973; N(ring)–H = 1.011; these data are identical to the parent melphalan Mp-Z except the C–O bond lengths.

The optimized geometry for bendamustin Bd-A in water has the following characteristic bond lengths: C–Cl = 1.822, 1.824; N–C = 1.399, 1.452; C–O = 1.211, C–O(H) = 1.347; and O–H = 0.973; these are very similar to melphalan.

Bendamustine was studied using DFT methodology (MPW1PW91 and MPW1PW91-TD for the excited state, 6-311++G(d,p) basis set). The molecular geometry was optimized in vacuo, and the stability was compared for several solvents.25 The vertical electronic properties were evaluated using the calculated HOMO and LUMO. Another DFT calculations on bendamustine used the hybrid B3LYP functional and the 6-311++G(d,p) basis set in vacuo.26 After geometry optimization, the vibrational and electronic spectra were predicted and compared with experimental IR and Raman spectra with a detailed analysis. The frontier orbitals and NBO analyses were presented along with topological characteristics of electron density, atomic charges, molecular electrostatic potential, and molecular docking interpretations. The calculated characteristic bond lengths are C–Cl = 1.899, 1.899; N–C = 1.452, 1.477; C–O = 1.231, C–O(H) = 1.382; and O–H = 0.978; these are slightly longer than those calculated above in water as a solvent.

The zwitterionic form of bedamustine Bd-Z differs from its amino-acid counterpart in several aspects. First, the geometry of the carboxyl unit is oriented differently as seen from Figure S1. Second, the total electronic energy as well as the standard Gibbs energy in the optimized structure is lower for the amino acid form compared to the zwitterionic form by 1.4 and 2.9 kcal mol–1, respectively (Table S2). However, this applies only to the two investigated conformers. The characteristic bond lengths are C–Cl = 1.819; N–C = 1.386, 1.457; C–O = 1.260; and N(ring)–H = 1.011.

The cationic fragment of bendamustine hydrochloride (bendamustinium) BdH+ has C–Cl = 1.819; N–C = 1.386, 1.457; C–O = 1.210, C–O(H) = 1.346; O–H = 0.973; N(ring)–H = 1.011; these data are identical to the parent bendamustine Bd-Z except for the C–O bond lengths.

This table compares published data for melphalan and bendamustine using density functional theory but in a different environment and approximations. It can be seen that the passage from vacuum (gas phase) to aqueous solution causes a decrease in (vertical) ionization energy and electron affinity and then changes other global electronic characteristics. The most significant is the decrease in the chemical hardness of both bendamustine and melphalan to η = 44 kcal mol–1 (1.9 eV) in water as a solvent.

Bendamustine in its canonical form (Bd-A) shows a very low adiabatic ionization energy in water Ei = 112 kcal mol–1 which points to the ability of showing very high antioxidant capacity.27 Notice that small linear amino acids (AA: glycine, β-alanine, GABA, DAVA) in their canonical forms have Ei ∼ 136–147 kcal mol–1 and dopaminergic molecules (DM: dopamine, noradrenaline, adrenaline) have Ei ∼ 126–129 kcal mol–1; B3LYP/def2-TZVPD data in water.13,28 Consequently, the absolute oxidation potentials are Eoxo̷ = −4.0 V for Bd-A, from −5.8 to −6.3 V for AA, and from −5.5 to −5.6 V for DM. The electron affinity Eeg = −24 kcal mol–1 is not too far from values typical for canonical AA (−25 to −30 kcal mol–1) and DM (−20 to −21 kcal mol–1). Then the absolute reduction potentials are Eredo̷ = 1.2 V for Bd-A, 1.2 to 1.4 V for AA, and 1.0 to 1.1 V for DM.

The zwitterionic form of bendamustine (Bd-Z) has a higher ionization energy Ei = 124 kcal mol–1 and then more negative absolute oxidation potential Eoxo̷ = −5.4 V compared to Bd-A. A more negative electron affinity Eeg = −44 kcal mol–1 compared to Bd-A is reflected as higher absolute reduction potential Eredo̷ = 2.1 V. Small AA in their zwitterionic forms have Eredo̷ = 0.5–1.1 V and DM molecules have Eredo̷ = 1.0–1.1 V.

Bendamustinium (BdH+) has ionization energy and electron affinity close to the zwitterionic form of Bd-Z: Ei = 125 and Eeg = −46 kcal mol–1. For this reason, the absolute oxidation and reduction potentials are almost the same as for the Bd-Z form: Eoxo̷ = −5.4 and Eredo̷ = 2.1 V. This is an important finding, since the drug in common practice is bendamustine-hydrochloride, which contains bendamustinium unit.

Frontier Orbitals

The calculated frontier orbitals are shown in Figure 3. The HOMO for HN2 is semilocalized around the central core and involves the N–C–H atoms, not Cl atoms. In contrast, LUMO is delocalized throughout the molecular skeleton and includes a significant portion of the Cl atoms on both arms.

Frontier molecular orbitals for neutral molecules in water in their amino acid and zwitterionic forms: from top to bottom HN2, Mp-A, Mp-Z, Bd-A, and Bd-Z. Isosurface value: 0.03.

In bendamustine, the HOMO includes the aromatic ring of the benzimidazole moiety and the N atom of the mustard unit; neither the Cl atoms nor the distant amino acid unit is involved in the HOMO. The LUMO is even more localized in the aromatic part. This finding is consistent with a previous report on the frontiers orbitals of bendamustine in vacuo.26

In melphalan, the HOMO and LUMO are not only analogous to bendamustine but also include the amino acid residue. Note that the HOMO indicates the region from which an electron can be removed; however, the Koopmans theorem Ei = −E(HOMO) is weakly satisfied, and the adiabatic ionization energy requires geometry optimization of the molecular cation.

The frontier orbitals for the zwitterionic forms Mp-Z and Bd-Z are essentially analogous to those of Mp-A and Bd-A. (Different colors represent different signs of the lobes, and their choice is arbitrary since each orbital can be multiplied by −1.)

Molecular Electrostatic Potential

The molecular electrostatic potential on the electron density isosurface is plotted in Figure 4. As expected, the most negative domain lies on the carboxyl oxygen atoms. Melphalan is a molecule assembled of phenylalanine, which is a true alpha-amino acid. All amino acids are more stable in water than in their zwitterionic forms. This is due to charge polarization on the cationic and anionic domains, which produce much higher solvation energies compared to the electroneutral canonical form. Bendamustine is not an amino acid; the hydrogen atom in the zwitterionic form is attached to the N(ring) atom which saturates very high negative charge on N(ring): the Mulliken charge is q(Nr) = −0.91 for the canonical form vs −0.04 for the zwitterionic form (Table S7). As a result, the hydration energy for the zwitterionic form is lowered, and then the canonical form of bendamustine is slightly more stable in water.

Vibrational Spectra

The vibrational frequencies were evaluated in the harmonic approximation and for 1–3 are given in the SI. The spectrum starts with frequencies ν0 = 31, 20, and 5 cm–1 for 1–3 where torsion vibrations occur; the highest frequencies above 3600 cm–1 refer to the O–H stretching for 2 and 3. There is a gap between 1800 and 2800 cm–1 where the vibrations are absent. (Detailed assignment of gas-phase vibrations can be found elsewhere.26) The Bd-Z vibrational spectrum lacks the O–H vibration at 3700 cm–1 and is replaced by an intense N–H vibration at 3600 cm–1. In the cationic part of the bendamustine-hydrochloride Bd-C, the stretching vibrational frequencies of O–H and N–H are reproduced.

Note that the correlation energy is on the order of 3000 kcal mol–1.

Comparison of total energies shows that the canonical form of bendamustine is more stable in water compared to the zwitterionic form by 4.8 kcal mol–1; this result corresponds to the prediction of the B3LYP method. Note that at room temperature both forms would be equally populated. The zwitterionic form Bd-Z has a higher ionization energy Ei = 130 (vs 118) kcal mol–1 and a higher negative electron affinity Eeg = −42 (vs–22) kcal mol–1 compared to the Bd-A form. Bendamustinium BdH+ has adiabatic global electronic properties close to the zwitterionic form Bd-Z, e.g., Ei = 130 kcal mol–1 and also the other global electronic properties are similar to Bd-Z.

The antioxidant capacity of the AOH molecule is given by the donation of an electron to a reactive radical, which is possibly accompanied by proton transfer. Several mechanisms of electron–proton coupled transfer were proposed so far.27

The SET-PT mechanism (Single Electron Transfer followed by Proton Transfer) assumes the quenching of the free radical R· by the sequence A(OH) + R· → R– + A(OH)·+ → RH + A(O)·.

Conclusions

Melphalan and bendamustine—antiblood cancer agents—were considered in three forms: the canonical (amino acid, A) form, the zwitterionic form (Z), and the cationic residue of the hydrochloride form (C). A hybrid version of Density Functional Theory of B3LYP quality was used for full geometry optimization and complete vibrational analysis of the neutral species and their molecular cations and anions. For comparison, the basic mustard-type molecule mechlorethamine was also considered. All calculations refer to water as the solvent. A total of 21 molecular species were evaluated.

The zwitterionic form Mp-Z is more stable compared to the canonical (amino acid) form Mp-A by ΔE = E(Z0) – E(A0) = −3.7 kcal mol–1 in water. The difference in standard Gibbs energies is also small, ΔG = Go̷(Z0) – Go̷(A0) = −2.7 kcal mol–1, so both isomers are almost equally populated at room temperature. The opposite situation was found for bendamustine: the canonical form Bd-A is more stable compared to the zwitterionic form Bd-Z in water by ΔE = 1.4 and ΔG = 2.9 kcal mol–1.

The calculated bulk molecular properties (dipole polarizability, solvated surface and volume, zero-point energy, and the total entropic term) increase with increasing molar mass of the neutral molecule. These ground-state properties are similar for Mp-A, Mp-Z, and Mp-C as well as for Bd-A, Bd-Z, and Bd-C. However, the electric multipoles are very different. The dipole moment strongly depends on the separation of the barycenters of the negative and positive charges. The very large dipole moment for bendamustine Bd-Z in water, p = 29.7 debye, reflects the large spatial separations of the −NH+ and −COO– units. Also the quadrupole moment for Bd-Z is very large, |Q| = 180 ea02, which shows a serious deviation of the charge cloud from spherical symmetry.

Bendamustine Bd-A has a low energy of HOMO: −119 kcal mol–1 (−5.2 eV). This predisposition is reflected in the relatively low value of the adiabatic ionization energy, Ei = 112 kcal mol–1 (4.8 eV); this points to the ability to show a high antioxidant capacity. The chemical hardness of both Mp-A and Bd-A decreased to η = 44 kcal mol–1 (1.9 eV) and the electrophilicity index is the lowest for Bd-A, ω = 53, compared to Bd-Z, ω = 89 kcal mol–1.

All calculations were refined with the ab initio post-Hartree–Fock method DLPNO–CCSD(T) (Domain Localized Pair Natural Orbitals–Coupled Cluster Singlets + Doublets + Triplets) method, which accounts for the major part of the correlation energy. It is confirmed that (i) the canonical form of bedamustine is more stable in water compared to the zwitterionic form by ΔGZ-A = 4.8 kcal mol–1; (ii) low ionization energy Ei = 5.10 eV causes low absolute oxidation potential Eox* = −5.10 V and increased antioxidant capacity; (iii) bendamustine in acidic aqueous environments spontaneously produces bendamustinium—an agent used as a medicine; (iv) the global electronic properties such as adiabatic ionization energy, electron affinity, molecular electronegativity, chemical hardness, electrophilicity index, absolute oxidation, and reduction potentials of the bendamustinium BdH+ are analogous to the zwitterionic form of bendamustine and are significantly different from the canonical form of bendamustine. Electron–proton coupled transfer has analogous energy profile for SET-PT and SPLET mechanisms, however with two steps in the reversed order.

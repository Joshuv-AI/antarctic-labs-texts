Evaluating Six Apéry-like Series of Weight 5 5


Abstract
The main objective of this paper is to evaluate six new Apéry-like series of weight 5 5 in closed form. These series involve harmonic numbers and exhibit the characteristic reciprocal central binomial coefficient structure. Generating functions for the inverse sine and identities related to harmonic numbers are used to link each series to a variety of integrals containing ln ⁡ ( sin ⁡ ( x ) ) \ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right) and ln ⁡ ( cos ⁡ ( x ) ) \ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right) , which are evaluated using a range of methods and identities.

Introduction and Preliminaries

The central focus of this paper is to obtain the closed forms of the following series:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{\left(3\right)}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{2}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}}{k^{4}\binom{2k}{k}},

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}},\quad\operatorname{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{3}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}},

where
\binom{2k}{k}
is the
k
-th central binomial coefficient.

In these series,
H_{k}
denotes the
k
-th harmonic number, defined as:

H_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n},

and the
k
-th generalized harmonic number of order
m
, denoted by
H_{k}^{\left(m\right)}
, is defined as:

H_{k}^{\left(m\right)}=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\frac{1}{n^{m}},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}.

An Apéry-like series involving harmonic numbers, of weight
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{n}+n
, is expressed by the summand:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{\left(m_{1}\right)}H_{k}^{\left(m_{2}\right)}\cdots H_{k}^{\left(m_{n}\right)}}{k^{n}\binom{2k}{k}},

where
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{n}
and
n
are positive integers. Given this, we determine that our desired series all possess weight
5
. Next, we will introduce definitions, including special functions and other key identities, that will aid us in performing calculations and understanding the results clearly.

The polygamma function of order
m
is defined as:

\psi^{\left(m\right)}\left(z\right)=\frac{d^{m}}{dz^{m}}\psi^{\left(0\right)}\left(z\right)=\frac{d^{m+1}}{dz^{m+1}}\ln\left(\Gamma\left(z\right)\right),\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\wedge\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(z\right)>0,

(1.1)

where
\psi^{\left(0\right)}\left(z\right)
is the digamma function, and
\Gamma\left(z\right)
, the gamma function, is defined as:

\Gamma\left(z\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}e^{-x}\>dx,\quad\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(z\right)>0.

If
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(z\right)>0
, then the following integral representation for the polygamma function holds:

\psi^{\left(m\right)}\left(z\right)=-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{z-1}\ln^{m}\left(x\right)}{1-x}\>dx.

(1.2)

Additionally, the following identity holds:

\psi^{\left(m\right)}\left(z+1\right)=\left(-1\right)^{m+1}m!\left(\zeta\left(m+1\right)-H_{z}^{\left(m+1\right)}\right),\quad\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(z\right)>-1,

(1.3)

where
\zeta\left(m+1\right)
is the Riemann zeta function, defined as:

\zeta\left(m\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{m}},\quad\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(m\right)>1,

and
H_{z}^{\left(m+1\right)}
is the analytic continuation of the generalized harmonic number, defined as:

H_{z}^{\left(m\right)}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{k^{m}}-\frac{1}{\left(k+z\right)^{m}}\right),\quad z\notin\mathbb{Z}^{-}.

The beta function is defined as:

\operatorname{B}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)\Gamma\left(\beta\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta\right)}=\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}\left(1-x\right)^{\beta-1}\>dx,\quad\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\alpha\right)>0\wedge\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\beta\right)>0,

alternatively expressed as:

\operatorname{B}\left(\alpha,\beta\right)=2\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\sin^{2\alpha-1}\left(x\right)\cos^{2\beta-1}\left(x\right)\>dx,\quad\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\alpha\right)>0\wedge\operatorname{\mathfrak{R}}\left(\beta\right)>0.

(1.4)

The polylogarithm function of order
m
is defined as:

\operatorname{Li}_{m}\left(z\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{m}},\quad\left|z\right|\leq 1,

or, alternatively,

\operatorname{Li}_{m}\left(z\right)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\operatorname{Li}_{m-1}\left(x\right)}{x}\>dx,\quad z\in\mathbb{C},

where
\operatorname{Li}_{1}\left(z\right)=-\ln\left(1-z\right)
.

Lastly, the inverse tangent is defined in the following logarithmic form:

\arctan\left(z\right)=-\frac{i}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\left(1+iz\right)^{2}}{1+z^{2}}\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\wedge z\neq\pm i,

which can also be rearranged as:

\ln\left(1+iz\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+z^{2}\right)+i\arctan\left(z\right),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}\wedge z\neq\pm i.

(1.5)

The Lemmas and their Proofs

Lemma 1
.

The following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{i}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}x^{2k-1}}{k\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{\arcsin\left(x\right)}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}},\quad\left|x\right|<1;

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}x^{2k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}=\arcsin^{2}\left(x\right),\quad\left|x\right|\leq 1;

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}x^{2k}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}}{k^{4}\binom{2k}{k}}x^{2k}=\arcsin^{4}\left(x\right),\quad\left|x\right|\leq 1.

Proof.

The identities in points
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
and
\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)
are demonstrated in
[
2
, pp. 331-333]
. Furthermore, the identity in point
\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)
is established in
[
1
, pp. 107-109]
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let k be a positive integer. Then, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{i}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln\left(1-x\right)\>dx=-\frac{H_{k}}{k};

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln^{2}\left(x\right)\ln\left(1-x\right)\>dx=-2\frac{H_{k}}{k^{3}}-2\frac{H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k^{2}}-2\frac{H_{k}^{\left(3\right)}}{k}+2\zeta\left(2\right)\frac{1}{k^{2}}+2\zeta\left(3\right)\frac{1}{k};

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln^{2}\left(1-x\right)\>dx=\frac{H_{k}^{2}+H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k};

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln\left(x\right)\ln^{2}\left(1-x\right)\>dx=-\frac{H_{k}^{2}}{k^{2}}-\frac{H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k^{2}}-2\frac{H_{k}^{\left(3\right)}}{k}-2\frac{H_{k}H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}}{k}+2\zeta\left(2\right)\frac{H_{k}}{k}+2\zeta\left(3\right)\frac{1}{k};

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{v}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k-1}\ln^{3}\left(1-x\right)\>dx=-\frac{H_{k}^{3}+3H_{k}H_{k}^{\left(2\right)}+2H_{k}^{\left(3\right)}}{k}.

Proof.

The integrals in points
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
,
\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)
, and
\left(\operatorname{v}\right)
are evaluated in
[
2
, pp. 59-62]
. On the other hand, the integrals in points
\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)
and
\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)
are obtained by differentiating with respect to
k
the integrals in points
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
and
\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)
, as demonstrated in
[
3
, pp. 222-227]
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

The following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{i}\right)

\displaystyle-\ln\left(2\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\cos\left(2kx\right)}{k}=\ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right),\quad 0<x<\pi;

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)

\displaystyle-\ln\left(2\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}\cos\left(2kx\right)}{k}=\ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right),\quad\left|x\right|<\frac{\pi}{2};

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x\ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=\frac{7}{16}\zeta\left(3\right)-\frac{3}{4}\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x\ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=-\frac{7}{16}\zeta\left(3\right)-\frac{3}{4}\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{v}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x^{2}\ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=\frac{3\pi}{16}\zeta\left(3\right)-\frac{\pi^{3}}{24}\ln\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{vi}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x^{3}\ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=-\frac{93}{128}\zeta\left(5\right)+\frac{27}{32}\zeta\left(2\right)\zeta\left(3\right)-\frac{45}{32}\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(4\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{viii}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x^{3}\ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=\frac{93}{128}\zeta\left(5\right)-\frac{9}{8}\zeta\left(2\right)\zeta\left(3\right)-\frac{45}{32}\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(4\right).

Proof.

The identities in points

The integrals in points

Lemma 4
.

The following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{i}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}=3\zeta\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}}=-\frac{7}{2}\zeta\left(3\right)+6\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}=\frac{7}{2}\zeta\left(3\right)+6\ln\left(2\right)\zeta\left(2\right);

\displaystyle\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cot\left(x\right)\ln^{2}\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=\frac{1}{4}\zeta\left(3\right).

Proof.

We initiate with the series in point
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
. To derive it, we simply use the result from point
\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)
in Lemma
1
and set
x=1

Next, we will use the same result for the series in point
\left(\operatorname{ii}\right)
. We have

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}}=2\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}x^{2k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}\right)\frac{1}{x}\>dx=4\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\arcsin^{2}\left(x\right)}{x}\>dx.

Moreover, if we use integration by parts and apply the substitution
\arcsin\left(x\right)\mapsto x
, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}}{k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}}=-8\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x\ln\left(\sin\left(x\right)\right)\>dx.

Therefore, by applying the result from point
\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)
in Lemma
3
, we obtain the desired closed form.

For the series in point
\left(\operatorname{iii}\right)
, by considering the result from point
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
in Lemma
2
, it follows that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}=-\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}x^{k-1}}{k\binom{2k}{k}}\right)\ln\left(1-x\right)\>dx,

and by using the substitution
x\mapsto x^{2}
, applying the result from point
\left(\operatorname{i}\right)
in Lemma
1
, and then performing the substitution
\arcsin\left(x\right)\mapsto x
, we get

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4^{k}H_{k}}{k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}}=-8\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}x\ln\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\>dx.

Upon employing the result from point
\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)
in Lemma
3
, the desired closed form follows.

For the integral in point
\left(\operatorname{iv}\right)
, applying the substitution
\cos\left(x\right)\mapsto\sqrt{x}
leads to

\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\cot\left(x\right)\ln^{2}\left(\cos\left(x\right)\right)\>dx=\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\ln^{2}\left(x\right)}{1-x}\>dx.

Thus, by applying the geometric series and integrating by parts to evaluate the resulting integral, the desired result is obtained.
∎

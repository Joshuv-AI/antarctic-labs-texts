2.
Algebraic Stacks

2.1.
Affine schemes, schemes and algebraic stacks

Definition 2.1 (Ringed space).

A ringed space
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a topological space
X
together with a sheaf of rings
\mathcal{O}_{X}
on
X
. The sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is called the structure sheaf of
X
.

Definition 2.2 (Locally ringed space).

A locally ringed space is a ringed space
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
such that all stalks of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
are local rings (i.e. they have unique maximal ideals).

Notice: It is
not
required that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
be a local ring for every open set
U
; in fact, this is almost never the case.

Definition 2.3 (Affine scheme).

Let
R
be a commutative ring. An affine scheme is a locally ringed space
S
such that
S\cong\Spec(R)
.

Definition 2.4 (Scheme).

A scheme is a locally ringed space
S
admitting a covering by open sets
U_{i}
, s.t. each
U_{i}
(as a locally ringed space) is an affine scheme.

In particular:
S
comes with a sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{S}:U\mapsto\mathcal{O}_{S}(U)
for every open subset
U
, the ring of regular functions on
U
.

We think of a scheme
S
as being covered by “coordinate charts“ which are affine schemes; we obtain a scheme
S
by glueing together affine schemes using the Zariski topology.

Example: (Affine scheme)

\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}=
affine
n
-space over a field
k
, for
n\in\mathds{N}
,

by definition we have
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}:=\Spec(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
.

It can be defined over any commutative ring
R
:

\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}:=\Spec(R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
.

Example: (Scheme)

Every affine scheme
S:=\Spec(R)
is a scheme.

A polynomial
f\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
determines a closed subscheme
f\equiv 0
in affine space
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}
, called an affine hypersurface. Formally this is

\Spec(k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])/(f),

for example: let
k\cong\mathds{C}
, then
x^{2}=y^{2}(y+1)
defines a singular curve in
\mathbb{A}_{\mathds{C}}^{2}
, a nodal cubic curve.

\mathbb{P}_{R}^{n}
can be constructed as a scheme: glueing
(n+1)
copies of affine
n
-space over
R
along open subsets

\mathbb{A}_{R}^{n}\otimes_{n}\mathbb{A}_{R}^{n}\otimes_{n}\ldots\otimes_{n}\mathbb{A}_{R}^{n}\;\;\;\;(n+1)\;\text{copies}

Remark 2.5

An algebraic variety
X
over a field

can be defined as a scheme with certain properties. There are different conventions which schemes should be called varieties. One standard choice: a variety

A smooth scheme is a generalisation of the concept of a non-singular algebraic variety.

In particular: a smooth scheme of finite type over

Let

Definition 2.6

(
Algebraic stack
)

\mathcal{X}

3.
Étale cohomology

Let
S
be a
k
-scheme and let

H^{i}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{l})=\underset{k}{\underset{\leftarrow}{\text{lim}}}\;H^{i}(S,\mathds{Z}/l^{k}\mathbb{Z})

Remark 3.1

The identity
\mathbb{Z}_{l}=\underset{k}{\underset{\leftarrow}{\text{lim}}}\;\mathds{Z}/l^{k}\mathds{Z}
does not imply that the

as the cohomology does not commute with taking inverse limits.

In order to remove torsion subgroups from the

-adic cohomology groups and to obtain cohomology groups that are vector spaces over fields of characteristic

H^{i}(V,\mathbb{Q}_{l})=H^{i}(V,\mathbb{Z}_{l})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{l}

4.
Stratifications of Algebraic Stacks

Definition 4.1

(c-open and c-closed) A countable open (c-open) substack of an algebraic stack
\mathcal{X}
is the complement of a countable union of Zariski closed irreducible substacks, i.e. we have
\mathcal{U}_{0}=U\setminus\underset{i\in I}{\cup}\mathcal{A}_{i}
where
\mathcal{A}_{i}
are Zariski closed irreducible substacks of
U
and
I
is a countable index set. The complement of a c-open substack, i.e. intersections of a countable number of Zariski open substacks, is called a Zariski countable closed (c-closed) substack.

Remark 4.2

For the existence and construction of the c-open substack
\mathcal{U}_{0}
inside
U
see
[
PS24
]
for zero characteristic and
[
SW25
]
for positive characteristic, where the argument is given in detail in the scheme-theoretic language.
It uses set-theoretic ideas and in respect of Definition
2.6
carries over to the stack-theoretic formulation.

Definition 4.3

[
GL19
]

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack. A
stratification of

consists of the following data:

(a)

A partially ordered set

.

(b)

A collection of open substacks

satisfying

This data is required to satisfy the following conditions:

\bullet

For each index

\bullet

For every field

has a least element.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an algebraic stack equipped with a stratification

For each

A stratification

Let

We now prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
U:=\Sigma\setminus\Delta

\{\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
of

such that the monodromy argument applies in a uniform way for all

U=\underset{\underset{i}{\rightarrow}}{\cup}\;\mathcal{U}_{i}

Proof.

Let
d=\dim(U)
and let
\{\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
be a stratification of
U
by c-open substacks. The set
A
is at most countable (Remark
4.8
). By adding additional elements to

and assigning to each of those additional elements the empty substack of

, we may assume that
A
is infinite.

By assumption the set
\{\beta\in A:\beta\leq\alpha\}
is finite for each
\alpha\in A
, hence we can choose an enumeration

A=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots\}

where each initial segment is a downward-closed subset of
A
.

We can then write
U
as the union of an increasing sequence of c-open substacks

\mathcal{U}_{0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{2}\hookrightarrow\ldots

where
\mathcal{U}_{n}
is characterized by the requirement that if
m
is a field, then a map
\eta:\Spec(m)\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
factors through
\mathcal{U}_{n}
if and only if it factors through one of the substacks
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{0}},\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{1}},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{n}}
.

By hypothesis, there exists a finite collection
\{\mathcal{T}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq s}
of smooth algebraic stacks over
\Spec(m)
, where each
\mathcal{T}_{i}
has some fixed dimension
d_{i}
and for each
n\geq 0
there exists an index
i(n)\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
and a diagram

\mathcal{T}_{i(n)}\overset{f_{n}}{\rightarrow}\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{X}}_{\alpha_{n}}\overset{g_{n}}{\rightarrow}\mathcal{X}_{\alpha_{n}},

where
g_{n}
is a finite radicial surjection and
f_{n}
is an étale fiber bundle whose fibers are affine spaces of some fixed dimension
e(n)
.
The map
f_{n}
induces an isomorphism in
l
-adic cohomology. Applying the Lemma
4.10
to the finite radicial map

g_{n}:\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{X}}_{\alpha_{n}}\times_{\Spec(m)}\Spec(\overline{m})\rightarrow\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n}

we obtain fiber sequences

C^{*-2e^{\prime}_{n}}(\overset{\sim}{\mathcal{T}}_{i(n)};\mathbb{Z}_{l}(-e^{\prime}_{n}))\rightarrow C^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n},\mathbb{Z}_{l})\rightarrow C^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n-1};\mathbb{Z}_{l}),

where
e^{\prime}_{n}=e_{n}+d-d_{i(n)}
denotes the relative dimension of the map

. We have a canonical equivalence

\Theta:C^{*}(\overline{U};\mathbb{Z}_{l})\simeq\underset{\underset{n}{\leftarrow}}{\lim}\;C^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{n},\mathbb{Z}_{l}).

Passing by the fiber complexes (Gysin sequence, Lemmas

,

stacks, by the increasing sequence of c-open substacks

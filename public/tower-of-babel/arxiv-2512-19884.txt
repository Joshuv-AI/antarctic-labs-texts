Moderate-doubling sets in 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} intersect subspaces
Alex Cohen, Dmitrii Zakharov

Abstract
. We show that any set A A in 𝔽 2 n \mathbb{F}_{2}^{n} with | A + A | ≤ | A | 2 − η |A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\eta} must intersect a subspace of dimension O η ​ ( log ⁡ | A | ) O_{\eta}(\log|A|) in at least | A | η − o ⁡ ( 1 ) |A|^{\eta-o(1)} elements.



1.
Introduction

Given a set
A
in an abelian group
G
, the sumset is defined as

A+A:=\{a+a^{\prime}\,:\,a,a^{\prime}\in A\}.

The size of the sumset lives in the range
[|A|,|A|^{2}]
.
If the sumset is small, what can we say about the structure of
A
?
In this paper we focus on
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
In this context, Freiman’s theorem
[
4
,
8
]
says that if
|A+A|\leq K|A|
, then
A
can be covered by
O_{K}(1)
translates of a subspace with size
O_{K}(|A|)
.
Marton conjectured that the constants in this theorem can be taken polynomially large in
K
. This is also known as the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa (PFR) conjecture.
In a recent breakthrough work, Gowers, Green, Manners and Tao
[
5
]
solved this conjecture over
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
by proving

|A+A|\leq K|A|\Longrightarrow\text{$A$ can be covered by $2K^{12}$ cosets of a subspace with size at most $|A|$.}

It is easy to cover
A
with
|A|
many translates of the
\{0\}
subspace, so this theorem is interesting when
|A+A|<|A|^{1+1/12}
.
1
1

1

Liao
[
12
]
reduced the constant from 12 to 9 using a modification of their argument. On the other hand, it is known that the PFR exponent must be at least 1.4.

By contrast, not much is known in the
moderate doubling
regime where
|A+A|=|A|^{2-\varepsilon}
.

2

2

In
[
2
,
16
,
17
]
some structural results in the moderate doubling regime are obtained under certain assumptions on higher energies.

Over
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
, we prove moderate doubling sets have large intersection with a bounded size subspace.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
L>0
such that the following holds.
For any set
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
|A+A|=|A|^{2-\eta}
, there exists a subspace
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
such that
|V|\leq|A|^{L}
and

\mathsf{E}_{a\in A}\log|A\cap(V+a)|\geq(\eta-\varepsilon)\log|A|.

In particular, some translate of
V
intersects
A
in at least
|A|^{\eta-\varepsilon}
points. A related result was proven by Bateman and Katz
[
3
, Section 5]
: they showed that if
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\eta}
then
|A\cap V|\geq|A|^{\eta-o(1)}\dim V
for some subspace
V
of dimension at most
|A|^{\frac{1-\eta}{2}-o(1)}
. Our Theorem
1.1
improves the bound on the dimension from polynomial to logarithmic.

We consider three examples of sets with moderate doubling.

•

Random subset of a subspace
. Let
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be a subspace of size
N^{\frac{2}{1+\varepsilon}}
and let
A\subset V
be a uniformly random subset of size
N
. Then
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\varepsilon}
.

•

Union of cosets
. Let
V\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be a subspace of size
N^{\varepsilon}
, let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be a random set of size
N^{1-\varepsilon}
, and let
A=V+\Lambda
. Then
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\varepsilon}
.

•

Bernoulli
. Let
p\in(0,1/2]
and, for sufficiently large
n
, let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be the set of vectors with at most
pn
non-zero coordinates.
The size of
A
is estimated by

\log|A|=(1+o_{n}(1))nH(p)

where
H(p)=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p)
is the binary entropy function.
Vectors in
A+A
have at most
2pn
non-zero coordinates, so

\log|A+A|=(1+o_{n}(1))nH(2p).

For small
p
, we may approximate
H(p)=p\log(e/p)+o_{p}(p)
, leading to the doubling estimate

\log\frac{|A|^{2}}{|A+A|}=(1+o_{n}(1))(2p\log(e/p)-2p\log(e/2p)+o_{p}(p))=2p(\log 2+o(1)).

It follows that
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\varepsilon}
with
\varepsilon=\frac{2+o(1)}{\log_{2}(1/p)}
.

The first two examples play an important role in Gowers–Green–Manners–Tao’s proof of the PFR conjecture over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
The Bernoulli example represents a new behavior in the moderate doubling regime.
One can combine all three examples: take a bunch of cosets of some subspace
V
, put the Bernoulli example inside each coset, and then take a random subset of the result.
These are pretty much the only examples we know of sets with moderate doubling in
\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
All these examples have polynomially large intersections with subspaces, agreeing with Theorem
1.1
.

Quantitatively, the Bernoulli example shows that the smallest possible
L
in Theorem
1.1
must be at least exponential in
\varepsilon^{-1}
. On the other hand, our proof provides an upper bound
L\leq\varepsilon^{-C\varepsilon^{-1}}
(see Section
5
).

Theorem
1.1
follows from a more precise entropic statement, which we discuss next. The entropic approach to additive combinatorics was introduced by Ruzsa
[
15
]
and developed by Tao
[
19
]
and has proven to be a very useful tool in the field
[
7
,
9
,
11
]
and in its applications
[
10
]
.

Let
X
be a random variable taking values in an abelian group
G
.
The Shannon entropy of
X
, defined in (
2.1
), measures the size of
X
.
For example, if
A
is a subset of
G
and
\mathbf{U}_{A}
is a random variable uniformly distributed over
A
, then
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{A}]=\log|A|
.
The
entropic doubling constant
of
X
is defined by
\sigma=\mathsf{H}[X_{1}+X_{2}]-\mathsf{H}[X]
, where
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
are independent copies of
X
. In the case
X=\mathbf{U}_{A}
,

\mathsf{H}[X_{1}+X_{2}]\leq\log|A+A|\leq\log|A|+\log K=\mathsf{H}[X]+\log K,

so the entropic doubling constant
\sigma
is upper bounded by the logarithm of the usual doubling constant
K
. If we know that
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\varepsilon}
, this implies that
\mathsf{H}[X_{1}+X_{2}]\leq(2-\varepsilon)\mathsf{H}[X]
.
The following theorem is our main result about entropic doubling.
We denote the quotient map by a subspace
V
as
\pi_{V}:G\to G/V
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
L>0
with the following property.
Let
X
and
Y
be independent random variables on
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Then there exists a subspace
V\subset G
with
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq L(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y])
and such that

\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)+\pi_{V}(Y)]\geq\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(Y)]-\varepsilon(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]).

In words, after quotioning out by a subspace of dimension
O(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y])
we can kill off almost all of the additive interaction between variables
X
and
Y
. For example, one can take both
X,Y
to be independent copies of
\mathbf{U}_{A}
for some subset
A\subset G
.
Below are some corollaries of this theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
L>0
with the following property.
Let
X
and
Y
be independent random variables on
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and suppose that
\mathsf{H}[X+Y]=\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]-s
for some
s\geq 0
. Then there exists a subspace
V\subset G
with
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq L(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y])
and such that

(1.1)

\mathsf{H}[X|\pi_{V}(X)],\mathsf{H}[Y|\pi_{V}(Y)]\geq s-\varepsilon(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
A
be a set with
|A+A|\leq|A|^{2-\eta}
, and set
X
and
Y
to be independent copies of
\mathbf{U}_{A}
. Then we have
\mathsf{H}[X+Y]\leq(2-\eta)\log|A|=\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]-\eta\log|A|
.
So for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a subspace
V
such that
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq L\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{A}]
, i.e.
|V|\leq|A|^{L}
, and that
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{A}~|~\pi_{V}(\mathbf{U}_{A})]\geq(\eta-\varepsilon)\log|A|
. On the other hand, one can verify
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{A}~|~\pi_{V}(\mathbf{U}_{A})]=\mathsf{E}_{a\in A}\log|A\cap(V+a)|
giving the desired estimate.
∎

By writing
\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]=\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]
, (
1.1
) can be restated as

(1.2)

\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq\mathsf{H}[X+Y]-\mathsf{H}[Y]+\varepsilon(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]).

It was conjectured in
[
1
, Conjecture 3.1]
that for any
\varepsilon>0
and any identically distributed
X
and
Y
there exists a subspace
V
of dimension at most
e^{C\varepsilon^{-1}}\mathsf{H}[X]
such that (
1.2
) holds. Corollary
1
implies this with a slightly worse estimate
\varepsilon^{-C\varepsilon^{-1}}
.

Similar results hold for sums of more than two variables.

Corollary 1.4
.

For every
k\geq 2,\varepsilon>0
there exists
L>0
with the following property.
Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
be independent random variables on
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
. Then there exists a subspace
V\subset G
with
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq L(\mathsf{H}[X_{1}]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[X_{k}])
and such that

\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{1})+\ldots+\pi_{V}(X_{k})]\geq\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{1})]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{k})]-\varepsilon(\mathsf{H}[X_{1}]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[X_{k}]).

Taking
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}
all copies of the same random variable
X
, we get the following dichotomy: for any
\varepsilon>0
there is a subspace
V
of dimension
O_{\varepsilon,k}(\mathsf{H}[X])
, such that one of the following holds:

•

we have
\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]\leq\varepsilon\mathsf{H}[X]
,

•

or the variable
\tilde{X}=\pi_{V}(X)
is
(k,\varepsilon)
-dissociated, i.e.
\mathsf{H}[\tilde{X}_{1}+\ldots+\tilde{X}_{k}]\geq(k-\varepsilon)\mathsf{H}[\tilde{X}]
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
builds on the inductive strategy of Gowers, Green, Manners and Tao
[
5
]
.
Let
X,Y
be a pair of independent variables and suppose that
\mathsf{H}[X+Y]=(1-\eta)(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y])
.
Our goal is to find subspace structure as long as
\eta>0
.
The base case is
\eta=1/2
, where it is known that
X
and
Y
must be uniformly distributed over a subspace.
If
\eta
is just slightly smaller than
1/2
, the polynomial Freiman–Ruzsa conjecture implies that
X
and
Y
are close to a subspace
V
.
For
\eta<1/2
, we employ induction on
\eta
.
Let
X_{1},X_{2},Y_{1},Y_{2}
be independent copies of
X
and
Y
. Following
[
5
]
, we consider four moves that replace
(X,Y)
with a new pair of independent random variables:

•

First sumset
X_{1}+Y_{2},X_{2}+Y_{1}
,

•

First fiber
(X_{1}~|~X_{1}+Y_{2}=u),(Y_{1}~|~Y_{1}+X_{2}=w)
,

•

Second sumset
X_{1}+X_{2},Y_{1}+Y_{2}
,

•

Second fiber
(X_{1}~|~X_{1}+X_{2}=u),(Y_{1}~|~Y_{1}+Y_{2}=w)
.

If any of these four moves improves the doubling ratio of
X,Y
, then the inductive hypothesis lets us find subspace structure between the new variables.
This reduces the problem to the case when none of the four moves improve the doubling ratio.
In
[
5
]
, the authors use a
fibring identity
, Eq. (
2.10
), and a special identity valid over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, to show that the sums of fibers
X_{u}=(X_{1}|X_{1}+Y_{2}=u)
and
Y_{w}=(Y_{1}|Y_{1}+X_{2}=w)
have a much smaller doubling ratio and so their induction can be closed.
This last step is called the “Endgame” in their paper.

The key new difficulty in implementing this strategy for moderate doubling sets is the fiber move. Suppose we manage to prove Theorem
1.2
for each pair of variables
X_{u}=(X_{1}~|~X_{1}+Y_{2}=u),Y_{w}=(Y_{1}~|~Y_{1}+X_{2}=w)
, where
u\sim X_{1}+Y_{2}
and
w\sim Y_{1}+X_{2}
are arbitrary. This means that we obtain a subspace
V(u,w)
which explains the additive interaction between
X_{u}
and
Y_{w}
. To complete the inductive step we then need to show that there is a single subspace
V
explaining the interaction between
X
and
Y
.
In Lemma
5
we show that this is the case. The idea is to fix a typical
u
from
X_{1}+Y_{2}
, sample an independent sequence
w^{(1)},\ldots,w^{(k)}
from
Y_{1}+X_{2}
, and set

V=V(u,w^{(1)})+\dots+V(u,w^{(k)}).

For a carefully chosen value of
k
, we show that this subspace satisfies
\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(Y)]\leq(1-c)\mathsf{H}[Y]
, completing the inductive step.

In
Section

2
we recall basic definitions from entropic calculus and tools such as the fibring identity and Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers theorem. In
Section

3
we prove a version of the endgame from
[
5
]
. In
Section

4
we set up intermediate statements
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{B}
which will be used in the inductive proof. In
Section

5
we execute the inductive step and deduce the main result and its corollaries.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Definitions and basic identities

Throughout this paper we work in the group
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
.
We work with
G
-valued random variables, typically denoted
X,Y
.
Our convention is that
X_{j},Y_{j}
denote independent copies of
X
and
Y
.

The entropy of a
G
-valued random variable is defined by

(2.1)

\mathsf{H}[X]:=\sum_{x\in G}\Pr[X=x](-\log\Pr[X=x]),

where if
\Pr[X=x]=0
, the summand is set to zero.

For a set
V
, we denote by
\mathbf{U}_{V}
a random variable uniformly distributed over
V
.

For possibly dependent random variables
X
and
U
, we define

(2.2)

\mathsf{H}[X|U]:=\mathsf{E}_{u\sim U}\mathsf{H}[X|U=u]=\mathsf{H}[X,U]-\mathsf{H}[U].

The equality between the left and right sides is the
chain rule for entropy
.
Whenever we condition on random variables, we implicitly take an expectation over their values.

For two possibly dependent random variables
X
and
Y
, the mutual information is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}[X:Y]

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]-\mathsf{H}[X,Y]

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}[X]-\mathsf{H}[X|Y]=\mathsf{H}[Y]-\mathsf{H}[Y|X].

If
S
is a third random variable, the conditional mutual information is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}[X:Y|S]

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{E}_{s\sim S}\mathrm{I}[X:Y|S=s]=\mathsf{H}[X|S]+\mathsf{H}[Y|S]-\mathsf{H}[X,Y|S]

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}[X,S]+\mathsf{H}[Y,S]-\mathsf{H}[X,Y,S]-\mathsf{H}[S]\geq 0.

The mutual information is always nonnegative, and is zero if and only if
X
and
Y
are independent. The nonnegativity of the second line is known as
submodularity
.

We denote the quotient map by a subspace
V
as

\pi_{V}:G\to G/V.

We may also refer to this map by
\pi_{G/V}
.
We have

\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V}|\pi_{V}(X+\mathbf{U}_{V})]=\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}],

so

(2.5)

\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]=\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X+\mathbf{U}_{V})]=\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}].

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be two subspaces. Applying submodularity, Eq. (
2.4
), to

(S,X,Y)=(X+\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}},\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}},\mathbf{U}_{V_{2}}),

yields

\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}}]+\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V_{2}}]\geq\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}}+\mathbf{U}_{V_{2}}]-\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}}],

and subtracting off
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}}]+\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V_{2}}]=\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}+V_{2}}]+\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}}]
gives

(2.6)

\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V_{1}}(X)]+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V_{2}}(X)]\geq\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V_{1}+V_{2}}(X)]+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V_{1}\cap V_{2}}(X)].

Let
X
and
Y
be possibly dependent random variables, and let
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}
be mutually independent copies of
X
and
Y
. The
doubling mass
between
X
and
Y
is defined by

\mathsf{s}[X;Y]:=\mathsf{H}[X^{\prime}]+\mathsf{H}[Y^{\prime}]-\mathsf{H}[X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}].

By the chain rule for entropy, Eq. (
2.2
), if
X
and
Y
are independent then

(2.7)

\mathsf{s}[X;Y]=\mathsf{H}[X|X+Y]=\mathsf{H}[Y|X+Y].

There is a trivial upper bound

(2.8)

\mathsf{s}[A;B]\leq\min\{\mathsf{H}[A],\mathsf{H}[B]\}.

Let
(X,U)
and
(Y,W)
be pairs of random variables, the only dependencies being between
(X,U)
and
(Y,W)
. Let

X_{u}=(X|U=u),\qquad Y_{w}=(Y|W=w).

To be precise,
X_{u}
and
Y_{w}
are defined to be new random variables, independent of all the previous ones, with distributions equal to the conditional probabilities above.
The conditional doubling mass is defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{s}[X\mid U;Y\mid W]

\displaystyle:=\mathsf{E}_{u\sim U,w\sim W}\mathsf{s}[X_{u};Y_{w}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}[X|U]+\mathsf{H}[Y|W]-\mathsf{H}[X+Y|U,W].

Let
V
be a subspace and let
\pi=\pi_{V}
.
Applying the entropic chain rule to
(X,\pi(X))
gives the identity

(2.9)

\mathsf{H}[A]=\mathsf{H}[\pi(A)]+\mathsf{H}[A|\pi(A)].

Applying this identity to
X,Y
, and
X+Y
gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y]-\mathsf{H}[X+Y]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}[\pi(X)]+\mathsf{H}[\pi(Y)]-\mathsf{H}[\pi(X+Y)]+

\displaystyle\qquad\mathsf{H}[X|\pi(X)]+\mathsf{H}[Y|\pi(Y)]-\mathsf{H}[X+Y|\pi(X)+\pi(Y)].

By the definition of mutual information, the last term can be written as

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[X+Y|\pi(X)+\pi(Y)]=\mathsf{H}[X+Y|\pi(X),\pi(Y)]+\mathrm{I}[X+Y:(\pi(X),\pi(Y))\,|\,\pi(X+Y)],

yielding the fibring identity (cf. Proposition 4.1 in
[
5
]
):

(2.10)

s[X;Y]=s[\pi(X);\pi(Y)]+s[X|\pi(X);Y|\pi(Y)]-\mathrm{I}[X+Y:(\pi(X),\pi(Y))|\pi(X+Y)].

Often we will just use the non-negativity of mutual information to bound the left-hand side by the right-hand side.
The term
s[X|\pi(X);Y|\pi(Y)]
accounts for the amount of additive interaction between
X
and
Y
through the subspace
V
.

Let
W\subset V
be two subspaces. Take an expectation over
\pi_{V}(X),\pi_{V}(Y)
and apply the fibring inequality to find

(2.11)

\begin{split}\mathsf{s}[X|\pi_{V}(X);Y|\pi_{V}(Y)]&=\mathsf{E}_{t\sim\pi_{V}(X),r\sim\pi_{V}(Y)}\mathsf{s}[X_{t};Y_{r}]\\
&\leq\mathsf{E}_{t,r}\bigl(\mathsf{s}[X_{t}|\pi_{W}(X_{t});Y_{r}|\pi_{W}(Y_{r})]+\mathsf{s}[\pi_{W}(X_{t});\pi_{W}(Y_{r})]\bigr)\\
&=\mathsf{s}[X|\pi_{W}(X);Y|\pi_{W}(Y)]+\mathsf{s}[\pi_{W}(X)|\pi_{V}(X);\pi_{W}(Y)|\pi_{V}(Y)].\end{split}

In this inequality, the interaction of
X
and
Y
through
V
is bounded by the interaction through
W
, plus the interaction through
V\bmod W
.

2.2.
Tools from Gowers–Green–Manners–Tao
[
5
]

For a pair of variables
X,Y
we define the Ruzsa distance

\mathsf{d}[X;Y]=\mathsf{H}[X^{\prime}+Y^{\prime}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X^{\prime}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[Y^{\prime}],

where
X^{\prime},Y^{\prime}
are independent copies of
X
and
Y
, respectively.
There are trivial lower and upper bounds

\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[Y]\leq\mathsf{d}[X;Y]\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X]+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[Y].

Theorem 2.1

(Entropic Marton’s Conjecture
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.8)
.

Let
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and suppose that
X,Y
are
G
-valued random variables. There exists a subspace
V\subset G
such that

\max\{\mathsf{d}[X;\mathbf{U}_{V}],\mathsf{d}[Y;\mathbf{U}_{V}]\}\leq 6\mathsf{d}[X;Y].

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
and suppose that
X,Y
are
G
-valued random variables. There exists a subspace
V\subset G
such that
\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq 7(\mathsf{H}[X]+\mathsf{H}[Y])
and

\max\{\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)],\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(Y)]\}\leq 12\mathsf{d}[X;Y].

Proof.

Let
V
be the subspace provided by
Theorem

2.1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{d}[X;\mathbf{U}_{V}]

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}[X+\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:EntropyOfProjection}}}{{=}}\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X].

Using the trivial lower bound on Ruzsa distance,

\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X)]=\mathsf{d}[X;\mathbf{U}_{V}]+\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq 2\mathsf{d}[X;\mathbf{U}_{V}]\leq 12\mathsf{d}[X;Y].

The other trivial lower bound,
\mathsf{d}[X;\mathbf{U}_{V}]\geq\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[\mathbf{U}_{V}]-\frac{1}{2}\mathsf{H}[X]
, gives

(a)

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{k})]\geq\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{1}+\ldots+X_{k-1})]+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{k})]-\delta(\mathsf{H}[X_{1}]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[X_{k}])

\displaystyle\geq\ldots\geq\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{1})]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[\pi_{V}(X_{k})]-(k-1)\delta(\mathsf{H}[X_{1}]+\ldots+\mathsf{H}[X_{k}])

and by
\delta=\frac{\varepsilon}{k-1}
this gives us the desired bound.
∎

Double star arrangement and the pointed multinet
Yongqiang Liu, Wentao Xie

Abstract
. Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a hyperplane arrangement in a complex projective space. It is an open question if the degree one cohomology jump loci (with complex coefficients) are determined by the combinatorics of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . By the work of Falk and Yuzvinsky [ FY07 ] and Marco-Buzunáriz [ Mar09 ] , all the irreducible components passing through the origin are determined by the multinet structure, which is combinatorially determined. Denham and Suciu introduced the pointed multinet structure, which is combinatorially determined, to obtain examples of arrangements with translated positive-dimensional components in the degree one cohomology jump loci [ DS14 ] . Suciu asked the question if all translated positive-dimensional components appear in this manner [ Suc14A ] . In this paper, we show that the double star arrangement introduced by Ishibashi, Sugawara and Yoshinaga [ ISY25 , Example 3.2] gives a negative answer to this question.

1.
Background

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an arrangement of finite hyperplanes in
\mathbb{CP}^{d}
with
d\geq 2
.
The combinatorics of the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is encoded in its intersection lattice,
L(\mathcal{A})
; this is the poset of all intersections of
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by reverse inclusion, and ranked by codimension. Let
X(\mathcal{A})
denote the complement of the hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
. A fundamental problem in the theory of hyperplane arrangements is to decide whether various invariants of
X(\mathcal{A})
are determined by the combinatorics
L(\mathcal{A})
. For instance, Betti numbers and the cohomology ring of
X(\mathcal{A})
are combinatorially determined (e.g., see
[
OS80
]
), while the fundamental group of
X(\mathcal{A})
cannot be determined by
L(\mathcal{A})
due to Rybnikov
[
Ryb11
]
. See
[
Dim17
]
for an overview of the theory.

1.1.
Cohomology jump loci

In this paper, we focus on the degree one cohomology jump loci. We first recall its definition.

Assume that
X
is a connected finite CW complex.
Let
\mathrm{Char}(X)
denote the group of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-valued characters
\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathbb{C}^{*})
, which is a commutative affine algebraic group. Each character
\rho\in\mathrm{Char}(X)
defines a rank one
\mathbb{C}
-local system on
X
, denoted by
L_{\rho}
.

Definition 1.1
.

The degree one cohomology jump loci of
X
is defined as

\mathcal{V}^{1}(X)\coloneqq\{\rho\in\mathrm{Char}(X)\mid H^{1}(X,L_{\rho})\neq 0\}.

Since
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X)
only depends on the the fundamental group of
X
,
\mathcal{V}^{1}(G)
is well-defined for any finitely presented group
G
.

For hyperplane arrangement complement
X(\mathcal{A})
, we simply write
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
for
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X(\mathcal{A}))
.
Since we are only interested in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
,
using Lefschetz hyperplane section theorem,
we always assume that

\mathcal{A}
is an essential line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.

Being essential means that the intersection of all lines of
\mathcal{A}
is empty.
The following question is widely open, see e.g.
[
Suc14A
, Problem 3.15]
.

Is
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
determined by
L(\mathcal{A})
?

We now describe in more details about
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
. A celebrity result due to Arapura
[
Ara97
]
and Artal Bartolo, Cogolludo-Agustín and Matei
[
ACM13
]
shows that
\mathcal{V}^{1}(X)
are unions of torsion-translated
subtori. To explain their results, we recall the notion of orbifold maps.

An algebraic map
f\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\Sigma_{k}
is called an (genus 0) orbifold fibration, if
f
is surjective, has connected generic fiber and
\Sigma_{k}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}-\{k\text{ points}\}
with
k\geq 2
.
There exists a maximal Zariski open subset
U\subset\Sigma_{k}
such that
f
is a fibration over
U
. Say
B=\Sigma_{k}-U
(could be empty) has
s
points, denoted by
\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{s}\}
. We assign
the multiplicity
\mu_{j}
of the fiber
f^{*}(q_{j})
(the
\gcd
of the coefficients of the divisor
f^{*}q_{j}
) to the point
q_{j}
. Set
{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
. We only count such multiplicity if
\mu_{j}\geq 2
.

The orbifold group
\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
associated to these data is defined as

\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})\coloneqq F_{k-1}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{1}}\ast\cdots\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{s}},

where
F_{k-1}
is the free group with
(k-1)
generators.
The orbifold fibration
f
induces a surjective map to the orbifold group

\pi_{1}(X(\mathcal{A}))\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}),

which gives an embedding (see e.g.
[
Suc14A
, Proposition A.1]
)

f^{*}\colon\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}}))\to\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A}).

Set
\pi=\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})
,
A=\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{\mu_{s}}
and
\mathrm{Char}(A)=\mathrm{Hom}(A,\mathbb{C}^{*})
. Note that
\mathrm{Char}(\pi)=\mathrm{Char}(\pi)^{\circ}\times\mathrm{Char}(A)
, where
\mathrm{Char}(\pi)^{\circ}\cong(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{k-1}

is the identity component of
\mathrm{Char}(\pi)
. A Fox calculus computation shows that

\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi)=\begin{cases}\big(\mathrm{Char}(\pi)\setminus\mathrm{Char}(\pi)^{\circ}\big)\cup\{\mbox{\boldmath{$1$}}\},&\text{ if }k=2\text{ and }f\text{ has at least one multiple fiber,}\\
\mathrm{Char}(\pi),&\text{ if }k\geq 3,\end{cases}

where we call
f
small in the first case, and large
in the second case. See
[
Suc14A
, Section 3.4]
for more details.

Following
[
Suc14A
, Theorem 3.8]
, we summarize the results as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
Ara97
,
ACM13
]
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.
Then we have

\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})=\bigcup_{f\text{ large}}f^{*}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})))\cup\bigcup_{f\text{ small}}f^{*}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})))\cup Z,

where the unions are over the equivalence classes of orbifold fibration
f\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\Sigma_{k}
of the
types indicated,
and
Z
is a finite union of isolated torsion points. In particular,
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
has translated positive-dimensional components if and only if at least one of the orbifold fibration
f
appearing in the union has multiple fibers.

Remark 1.3
.

This theorem indeed holds for any
complex smooth quasi-projective variety, see
[
Ara97
,
ACM13
]
and also Budur and Wang’s work
[
BW15
]
for higher degree cohomology jump loci.

1.2.
Multinet

As shown by Falk and Yuzvinsky in
[
FY07
]
, the
irreducible components of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
passing through the origin can be described in terms
of multinet, which only depends on the intersection lattice
L(\mathcal{A})
.
Let us recall its definition.

Definition 1.4
.

A multinet on a line arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
is a partition of
\mathcal{A}
into
k\geq 3
subsets
\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k}
, together with an assignment of multiplicities,
m\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and a subset
\mathfrak{X}
of intersection points in
\mathcal{A}
, called the base locus, satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}m_{H}=\kappa
, independent of
i
;

(b)

for each
H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
and
H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
with
i\neq j
,
H\cap H^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}
;

(c)

for each
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, the sum
n_{x}\coloneqq\sum_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i},x\in H}m_{H}
is independent of
i
;

(d)

for every
1\leq i\leq k
and
H,H^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
, there is a sequence
\{H_{0},H_{1},\cdots,H_{s}\}
with
H_{0}=H
and
H_{s}=H^{\prime}
such that
H_{j-1}\cap H_{j}\notin\mathfrak{X}
for all
1\leq j\leq s
;

(e)

\gcd(m_{H}\colon H\in\mathcal{A})
=1.

Such multinet is called a
(k,\kappa)
-multinet.

The notion of multinet was simultaneously introduced by Marco-Buzunáriz in
[
Mar09
]
by the name of combinatorial pencil. Next we list some known results about multinet due to Pereira and Yuzvinsky as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

[
PY08
,
Yuz09
]
Let
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
be a multinet on
\mathcal{A}
with a base locus
\mathfrak{X}
.

(1)

If
|\mathfrak{X}|>1
, then
k=3
or
4
.

(2)

If there exists
H\in\mathcal{A}
such that
m_{H}>1
, then
k=3
.

Let
f_{H}
be the linear homogeneous polynomial which defines
H\in\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
. Given a multinet on
\mathcal{A}
, with parts
(\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k})
and
multiplicity vector
m
, set

g_{i}=\prod_{H\in\mathcal{A}_{i}}f_{H}^{m_{H}}

for
1\leq i\leq k
.
By definition of multinet,
\{g_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are homogeneous polynomials with the same degree.
Then it gives a map
f\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}

by

f(x)=[g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x)].

As shown in
[
FY07
]
, each
g_{i}
is a linear combination of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
for any
3\leq i\leq k
, hence
image of
f
is exactly
\Sigma_{k}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}-\{k\text{ points}\}
. In particular,
f
is a large orbifold fibration since
k\geq 3
for a multinet.

On the other hand, if
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{A}
is a subarrangement, then the inclusion
X(\mathcal{A})\to X(\mathcal{B})
induces an epimorphism
\pi_{1}(X(\mathcal{A}))\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(X(\mathcal{B}))
. Moreover, it induces an embedding
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, see e.g.
[
Suc14A
, Proposition A.1]
.
Components of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
that are not supported on any proper subarrangement are said to be essential.

Following
[
Suc14B
, Theorem 11.4]
, we summarize a series of works due to Falk, Libgober, Pereira and Yuzvinsky as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
LY00
,
FY07
,
PY08
,
Yuz09
]
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essential line arrangement in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
.
Then every positive-dimensional essential component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is obtained from
f^{*}(\mathcal{V}^{1}(\pi_{1}^{\mathrm{orb}}(\Sigma_{k},{\mbox{\boldmath{$\mu$}}})))
for some orbifold fibration
f\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\Sigma_{k}

and either

(1)

f
is small, i.e,
k=2
and f has at least one multiple fiber, or

(2)

k=3
or
4
, and
f
corresponds to a multinet on
L(\mathcal{A})
.

1.3.
Pointed multinet

Theorem
1.2
shows that
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
consists of three type components: the positive-dimensional components passing the origin, the translated positive-dimensional components and the finite union of isolated torsion points. The first one is well-understood by the multinet structure due to Theorem
1.6
.
The last one is still poorly understood.
Meanwhile, Denham and Suciu introduced a
combinatorial construction, called pointed multinet
[
DS14
]
,
to produce translated 1-dimensional component in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.
As shown by Suciu in
[
Suc02
]
, there do exist arrangements
\mathcal{A}
with isolated torsion
points and translated positive-dimensional component in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
.

Fix a hyperplane
H\in\mathcal{A}
. The arrangement
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}\setminus\{H\}
is then called the deletion of
\mathcal{A}

with respect to
H
. The pointed multinet structure is a variation of Definition
1.4
.

Definition 1.7
.

[
DS14
, Definition 5.5]
A pointed multinet on
\mathcal{A}
is a multinet structure
((\mathcal{A}_{1},\cdots,\mathcal{A}_{k}),m,\mathfrak{X})
,
together with a distinguished hyperplane
H\in\mathcal{A}
for which
m_{H}>1
, and
m_{H}\mid n_{x}
for each

x\in H\cap\mathfrak{X}
.

Note that Theorem
1.5
implies that the pointed multinet structure only exists for
k=3
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
H\in\mathcal{A}_{3}
.
By the multinet structure, we have a large orbifold map
f=[g_{1},g_{2}]\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\Sigma_{3}
. We can extend this map to
X(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, denoted by
f^{\prime}
. Since
H
is deleted from
\mathcal{A}
, the image of
f^{\prime}
equals to
\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
f^{\prime}
has at least one multiple fiber with multiplicity
m_{H}
.

.

[
DS14
, Proposition 5.6]
Suppose that
\mathcal{A}
admits a pointed multinet, and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is obtained from
\mathcal{A}

by deleting the distinguished hyperplane
H
. Then
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
supports a small orbifold fibration, and
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

has a component which is a 1-dimensional subtorus, translated by a character of order
m_{H}
.

Let

Suciu asked the following question
[
Suc14A
, Problem 3.12]
.

Question 1.10
.

Do all positive-dimensional translated tori in degree one cohomology jump loci of a line arrangement complement arise in the manner of pointed multinet structure?

In next section, we show that the double star arrangement introduced by Ishibashi, Sugawara and Yoshinaga in
[
ISY25
, Example 3.2]

admits a small orbifold map whose multiple fiber is not formed by lines. This example shows one of the difficult points to prove that the translated positive-dimensional component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
are combinatorially determined: the multiple fiber of the orbifold map may have irreducible fiber with degree greater than one.
In particular, it gives a negative answer to Question
1.10
.

Note that Theorem
1.6
implies that for a line arrangement
\mathcal{A}
a translated positive-dimensional component of
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
may have dimension 1, 2 or 3. The pointed multinet structure can only produce translated 1-dimensional component in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
. The double star arrangement we will studied later has translated 1-dimensional component in
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
not coming from the pointed multinet structure.
It is an interesting question if one can find examples of line arrangement
\mathcal{A}
such that
\mathcal{V}^{1}(\mathcal{A})
has a translated component with dimension 2 or 3.

Theorem 2.1
.

The double star arrangement is a counterexample to Question
1.10
.

Proof.

Consider
X(\mathcal{A})
as the complement of 10 affine lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.
Using computations by
Mathematica 12.0
(the codes are listed in the end of this paper),
we get that the product of the five red lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
has defining equation

h_{1}=c^{-1}\big(\frac{2x^{5}}{5}-4x^{3}y^{2}+2xy^{4}+\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}x^{4}+(1+\sqrt{5})x^{2}y^{2}+\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}y^{4}-(2+\sqrt{5})x^{2}-(2+\sqrt{5})y^{2}+\frac{11+5\sqrt{5}}{10}\big)

with
c=\frac{32(5+3\sqrt{5})}{125}\sin\frac{2\pi}{5}
and the product of the five blue lines in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
has defining equation

h_{2}=c^{-1}\big(\frac{2x^{5}}{5}-4x^{3}y^{2}+2xy^{4}+\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}x^{4}+(1-\sqrt{5})x^{2}y^{2}+\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}y^{4}-(2-\sqrt{5})x^{2}-(2-\sqrt{5})y^{2}+\frac{11-5\sqrt{5}}{10}\big).

Consider the map
h=\dfrac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}\colon X(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
. It is easy to see that
h
is surjective and has connected generic fiber.
For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\setminus\{1\}

has degree 5. It implies that the map
h

Let

and

Then we have

(1)

\overline{h_{1}}-\overline{h_{2}}=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{c}z\cdot(x^{2}+y^{2}-z^{2})^{2},

where
\{z=0\}
is the line at infinity.
This shows that the map
h
has a multiple fiber of multiplicity 2 at the point

.
So
h
is a small orbifold fibration with exactly one multiple fiber of multiplicity 2, which gives a translated 1-dimensional component of

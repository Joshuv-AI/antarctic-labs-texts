Joint Reconstruction of Activity and Attenuation in PET by Diffusion Posterior Sampling in Wavelet Coefficient Space
Clémentine Phung-Ngoc, Alexandre Bousse, Antoine De Paepe, Thibaut Merlin, Baptiste Laurent, Hong-Phuong Dang, Olivier Saut, Catherine Cheze-Le-Rest, Dimitris Visvikis

Abstract
Attenuation correction (AC)is necessary for accurate activity quantification in positron emission tomography (PET). Conventional reconstruction methods typically rely on attenuation maps derived from a co-registered computed tomography (CT)or magnetic resonance (MR)scan. However, this additional scan may complicate the imaging workflow, introduce misalignment artifacts and increase radiation exposure. In this paper, we propose a joint reconstruction of activity and attenuation (JRAA)approach that eliminates the need for auxiliary anatomical imaging by relying solely on emission data. This framework combines wavelet diffusion model (WDM)and diffusion posterior sampling (DPS)to reconstruct fully three-dimensional (3-D)data. Experimental results on simulated data show our method outperforms maximum likelihood activity and attenuation (MLAA)and MLAA-UNet with U-Net-based postprocessing, and yields high-quality noise-free reconstructions across various count settings with time-of-flight (TOF). It is also able to reconstruct non-TOFdata, although the reconstruction quality significantly degrades in low-count (LC)conditions, limiting its practical effectiveness in such settings. Nonetheless, a non-TOFBiograph mMR real data reconstruction with joint scatter estimation highlights the potential of the method for clinical applications. This approach represents a step towards stand-alone PETimaging by reducing the dependence on anatomical modalities while maintaining quantification accuracy, even in LCscenarios when TOFinformation is available. Our code is available on GitHub at https://github.com/clemphg/jraa-dps .

Introduction

Positron emission tomography (PET) is a nuclear medical imaging technique used to visualize functional mechanisms within the body. It involves injecting a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical as a tracer, designed specifically for the targeted function. This tracer concentrates in the regions of interest via the metabolism, and pairs of
\upgamma

Finally, an image of the activity distribution is reconstructed from the emission data. PEThas a wide range of applications in fields such as oncology, cardiology or neurology.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:

•

Building on our previous study, we propose a DM-based JRAAmethod, namely JRAA-DPS, which leverages a diffusion prior learned from activity–attenuation pairs for the joint reconstruction of activity and attenuation from emission data alone.

The DMprior itself is decoupled from the acquisition model. Consequently, the same trained model can be applied across different acquisition settings, including scan duration, TOFresolution and scanner geometry, without retraining, provided that the forward model is adapted to the measured data.

\bm{x}

During the forward process, an image

In denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs)

]

\bm{x}_{t}=\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}\bm{x}_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\alpha_{t}}\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\,,\quad\bm{\epsilon}_{t}\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{\mathcal{W}},\bm{I}_{\mathcal{W}})

(8)

where the

The reverse process consists in sampling
\bm{x}_{t-1}
from
\bm{x}_{t}
, starting from

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{t-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{t}}(1-\bar{\alpha}_{t-1})}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\bm{x}_{t}+\frac{\sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_{t-1}}\beta_{t}}{1-\bar{\alpha}_{t}}\bm{x}_{0}+\sigma_{t}\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle\bm{\varepsilon}_{t}

\displaystyle\sim\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{\mathcal{W}},\bm{I}_{\mathcal{W}})\,,

where

The mapping

where

III-D

Joint Scatter Estimation

PETreconstruction requires corrections for scanner sensitivity, scatter, and random coincidences. While sensitivity and random events can be estimated from the scanner and raw measurements
[
46
]
, most scatter estimation methods utilize the attenuation map, such as single scatter simulation (SSS)
[
47
]
and Monte-Carlo simulations
[
48
]
. However, in absence of a pre-estimated attenuation map, scatter must be estimated differently. AIapproaches have been developed
[
49
,
50
]
, but they require ACFs. Alternatively, an energy-based scatter estimation technique could be used
[
51
]
. In this work, we propose to deploy a joint scatter estimation method in which scatter is progressively refined during DPS, similarly to the approach proposed by
[
52
]

[
52
]
.

Each LOR
i\in\{1,\dots,n_{\mathrm{l}}\}
corresponds to a ring pair
\nu_{i}
, an angle of view
\varphi_{i}
and a radial position
u_{i}
.
The scatter vector
\bm{s}=[s_{1},\dots,s_{n_{\mathrm{l}}}]^{\top}
(the TOFindex was omitted for conciseness) can approximated by a collection of Gaussian-like functions of the radial position
u
, i.e.,

\hat{s}_{i}(\bm{\phi})=a_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}}\exp\left(-\frac{(u_{i}-m_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}}^{2}}\right)

(21)

where
a_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}}
is a scaling factor and
m_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}}
and
\sigma_{\nu_{i},\varphi_{i}}
are respectively the mean and the standard deviation (STD), and the parameters are regrouped in a vector
\bm{\phi}=\left\{(a_{\nu,\varphi},m_{\nu,\varphi},\sigma_{\nu,\varphi}),\,\nu=1,\dots,{n_{\mathrm{p}}},\varphi=0,\frac{\pi}{n_{\mathrm{a}}},\dots,\frac{(n_{\mathrm{a}}-1)\pi}{n_{\mathrm{a}}}\right\}
,
n_{\mathrm{p}}
and
n_{\mathrm{a}}
being respectively the number of ring pairs and angles of view.

The expected measurement and conditional PDFare then respectively rewritten
\bar{\bm{y}}(\bm{x},\bm{\phi})
and
p(\bm{y}\mid\bm{x},\bm{\phi})
to reflect the dependency in
\bm{\phi}
through
\hat{\bm{s}}(\bm{\phi})\triangleq[\hat{s}_{1}(\bm{\phi}),\dots,\hat{s}_{n_{\mathrm{l}}}(\bm{\phi})]^{\top}
. At each step
t
of the DPSprocess, we propose to compute
\bm{\phi}_{t-1}
from
\bm{\phi}_{t}
using a gradient ascent step of the form

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{t-1}={}

\displaystyle\bm{\phi}_{t}+\gamma_{t}\nabla_{\bm{\phi}_{t}}\log\,p\left(\bm{y}\mid\bm{x}=\bm{F}^{-1}\left(\hat{\bm{z}}_{\bm{\theta}}(\bm{z}_{t},t)\right),\bm{\phi}_{t}\right)

\displaystyle-\delta_{t}\nabla H(\bm{\phi}_{t})

(22)

where
\gamma_{t},\delta_{t}>0
and
H
is a quadratic regularizer that promotes smoothness along the angle
\varphi
axis.

The proposed framework can reconstruct data with diverse acquisition characteristics, provided that these are appropriately modeled. In particular, factors such as scan duration (i.e., noise level), scanner geometry, TOFinformation, normalization, and other corrections are explicitly incorporated into the forward model (

IV-B

IV-B
1
Normalization

Attenuation maps were linearly mapped to the
[-1,1]
range while PETimages were normalized as

\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{norm}}=3\cdot\sqrt[3]{\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}}}-1

(23)

where
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}=1\times 10^{5}
and
\bm{\lambda}_{\mathrm{norm}}
is the normalized PETimage. While attenuation map values occupy a relatively compact physical range, PETimages typically have a wide and a right-skewed intensity distribution. The cube-root transform compresses the wide dynamic range of PET, limiting the influence of a few high uptake voxels while retaining contrast in lower-activity regions. The resulting normalized PETrange of [-1, 2] aims to make the activity and attenuation histograms comparable, and to facilitate training on the activity–attenuation image pairs. This normalization strategy is similar to the logarithmic linear normalization proposed by
[
56
]

[
56
]
, which was shown to be effective for DMtraining on PETimages by compressing the dynamic range.

VI
Discussion

This paper introduces a framework for 3-DCT-less PETreconstruction based on JRAAusing DPS. We first simulated data by projecting the reference activity images and attenuation maps. We then evaluated JRAA-DPSagainst two other approaches, the classic MLAAand MLAA-UNet, a post-processing method to predict a pseudoattenuation map from MLAAreconstructions. JRAA-DPSoutperformed the comparison methods with regards to the selected metrics. It is also able to reconstruct non-TOFdata in higher count settings. In particular, it reconstructed clinical non-TOFdata acquired with a Siemens Biograph mMR scanner, recovering meaningful structures such as tumors. Overall, JRAA-DPSreconstructs accurate, noiseless 3-Dactivity images.

A key advantage of JRAA-DPSis that the learned prior is agnostic to scanner geometry, count level, and the presence of TOFinformation, as these factors are explicitly modeled through the forward operator. The framework does not require supervised training to accommodate variations in geometry, noise level, or the absence of TOFinformation. In addition, a joint scatter estimation strategy can be integrated into the framework at sampling time, without requiring additional training. Therefore, a single trained DMcan be applied to reconstruct data with diverse characteristics (e.g., different count levels, TOFor non-TOFacquisitions, and varying SFs).

However, the diffusion prior is learned from a specific image distribution and may be sensitive to domain shifts not captured by the forward model, such as differences in radiopharmaceutical uptake patterns. In this work, the DMwas trained and evaluated using data from a single tracer (i.e.,
18
F-FDG). Extending the approach to other imaging conditions may therefore require additional training. This limitation could be addressed by training the DMon multi-tracer data (e.g., with a tracer-conditioning mechanism), or by developing tracer-specific models through targeted training or fine-tuning.

The joint scatter estimation strategy we used was able to partially mitigate the impact of scatter on reconstructions, but tended to underestimate the scatter contribution, which led to overestimation of the activity in low-uptake regions. In the end, on the real patient dataset, MLAA-UNet yielded a higher-quality activity reconstruction than JRAA-DPSon the clinical data. However, the metrics used for comparison favored MLAA-UNet as they were computed with a reference image obtained with a similar method, as noted in Section
V-B
. Additionally, MLAA-UNet relies on the scanner-derived scatter estimates and the reference attenuation support, which are both obtained from the attenuation map. Conversely, JRAA-DPSreconstructs both the activity and the attenuation without relying on any attenuation information, which is a key advantage of the method. More advanced strategies could be applied during joint scatter estimation to improve the quality of the scatter estimate. For instance, the magnitude of the scatter contribution could be constrained based on an expected SF, which would mitigate the scatter underestimation.

Generative models are prone to hallucination, meaning they produce features that are not supported by the measurements. In our experiments, these were especially noticeable in the reconstructed attenuation maps: examples include missing bone detail, poorly recovered small air-filled regions, incoherent structures in the lungs, and shifted or extended arms. Although these artifacts did not appear to substantially affect the activity reconstruction in our experiments, especially when they were confined to peripheral or out-of-FOVregions, they remain a limitation of the method. However, such artifacts had little effect on the reconstructed activity images in our tests. JRAA-DPSwas able to recover high-quality activity images, even without TOFinformation, provided the count level was sufficient. In practice, small errors in the attenuation map may not significantly impact the reconstructed activity image, especially given the limited spatial resolution of PETsystems. The attenuation map obtained in PET/MRsystems is prone to errors (e.g., missing bones, simplified structures) due to the challenges of deriving accurate attenuation information from MRimages, yet is considered clinically acceptable
[
60
]
. In addition, activity–attenuation cross-talk is reduced when using TOFinformation, which improves robustness to attenuation map errors
[
61
]
. Nonetheless, quantifying the frequency, magnitude and clinical impact of such hallucinations on activity reconstructions is important to assess the clinical viability of diffusion-based reconstruction methods such as JRAA-DPS.

In TOFPET, attenuation information is theoretically identifiable from the emission data
[
2
]
, and the diffusion prior mainly acts as a regularizer. In contrast, attenuation information from non-TOFdata is more ill-posed, and the reconstructed attenuation map should be interpreted as a solution that is jointly consistent with both the measured data and the learned distribution of plausible
\bm{\lambda}
–
\bm{\mu}
pairs. Consequently, the prior plays a larger role in non-TOFreconstructions, which is reflected by the increased variability observed in Fig.
7
. This stronger dependence on the learned prior may also contributed to hallucinated activity and attenuation outside the object support. In addition, inaccuracies in the jointly estimated scatter may lead to overestimation of activity in low-uptake regions, as observed in the mMR study.

An important limitation of JRAA-DPSconcerns the reconstruction duration. Sampling from DMsis known to be time-consuming, particularly when using the standard DDPM, and reconstructing a sample of simulated data with JRAA-DPScurrently takes around 30 minutes. Employing the denoising diffusion implicit model (DDIM)with its accelerated inference variant could significantly reduce reconstruction time
[
31
]
. Additionally, the computation of the gradient of the data fidelity term can also be accelerated
[
34
,
35
]
. However, comparative evaluation would be necessary to assess the impact of this acceleration on reconstruction accuracy.

Finally, CT-less PETreconstruction methods are also attractive for respiratory motion compensation, since they can eliminate the need for gated-CTacquisitions. Previous studies have investigated joint PETreconstruction and motion estimation using a single, potentially mismatched, attenuation map
[
62
,
63
]
. Building on these works,
[
6
]
proposed two methods for the joint estimation of activity, attenuation and motion
[
6
]
. Such strategies could be integrated with our proposed JRAA-DPSframework to enable diffusion-based motion-compensated CT-less PETreconstruction.

VII
Conclusion

In this work, we introduce a novel method for 3-DCT-less reconstruction of PET. This approach reconstructs accurate and noise-free PETimages without attenuation information. The framework relies on a prior consisting of a DMtrained to generate activity-attenuation image pairs. Additionally, we used a WDMto process the large 3-Dvolumes. Overall, this method enables radiation dose reduction by eliminating the need for a CTscan for attenuation correction, and remains effective even in LCsettings. Coupled with a joint scatter estimation strategy, JRAA-DPSreconstructs real non-TOFclinical data, demonstrating its potential for clinical application. While these results are promising, the current joint scatter estimation tends to underestimate scatter, leading to increased uptake in low-activity areas. In addition, the framework can be affected by hallucinations due to the generative nature of the prior. Nevertheless, JRAA-DPSremains a promising approach to generate high-quality PETimages from low-dose or non-TOFPETdata without attenuation information. Future work will focus on improving joint scatter estimation, exploring sampling acceleration methods to reduce reconstruction duration and respiratory motion-compensated JRAA.

Macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the level sets of the Airy processes
Sudeshna Bhattacharjee, Fei Pu

Abstract
We study the Macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the upper and lower level sets of the Airy processes, following the general method developed in Khoshnevisan et al. [ 18 ] . For the Airy 1 process, the approach to macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of level sets hinges on some inequalities for its joint probabilities, while for the Airy 2 process, we make use of some quantitative estimates on the tail probabilities of its maximum and minimum over an interval.



MSC 2010 subject classification: 60A10, 60F05, 60H15.

Keywords: Airy processes, level sets, macroscopic Hausdorff dimension.

1
Introduction

The Airy
1
and Airy
2
processes are introduced by Sasamoto
[
26
]
and Prähofer and Spohn
[
24
]
respectively in the context of random growth models lying in the KPZ universality class. They are stationary stochastic processes whose finite-dimensional distributions are given in terms of the Fredholm determinant (see
[
27
, Section 4.1]
). Recently, the limit theorems for the Airy processes have been studied in
[
25
]
and
[
7
]
.
According to
[
25
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
7
, Theorem 1.1(i)]
, we see from stationarity of the Airy
1
and Airy
2
processes that

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)}{\frac{1}{2}((3\log t)/2)^{2/3}}

\displaystyle=1,\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)}{((3\log t)/4)^{2/3}}

\displaystyle=1,\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.2)

The limits in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) indicate that
t\mapsto\frac{1}{2}((3\log t)/2)^{2/3}
and
t\mapsto((3\log t)/4)^{2/3}
are gauge functions for measuring the tall peaks of the random height functions
t\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)
and
t\mapsto\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)
, respectively. We are interested in the upper level sets of the Airy processes and consider the random sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{1}(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}((3\log t)/2)^{2/3}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{2}(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)>\gamma((3\log t)/4)^{2/3}\right\},

for
\gamma>0
.

Denote by
{\rm Dim_{H}}(E)
the (Barlow-Taylor) macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
(see Section
2
for the definition and related information). Our first goal is to determine the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the upper level sets of the Airy processes.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{U}_{1}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=1-\gamma^{3/2},\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.3)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{U}_{2}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=1-\gamma^{3/2},\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.4)

The notion of macroscopic Hausdorff dimension is due to Barlow and Taylor
[
3
,
4
]
, which describes the large-scale geometry of a set. Khoshnevisan et al.
[
18
]
make use of the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension to study the
multifractal behavior of the peaks of the solution to stochastic PDEs. In light of the definition of multifractality in
[
18
, Definition 1.1]
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the tall peaks of the Airy processes are multifractal
with respect to their corresponding gauge functions.
We refer to
[
12
,
15
,
19
,
29
]
for the multifractal properties of the peaks for some random models related to stochastic PDEs.

We are also interested in the fractal properties of the valleys of the Airy processes. Basu and Bhattacharjee
[
7
]

have recently obtained precise limits for the asymptotic behavior of the minimum of the Airy processes. According to
[
7
, Theorem 1.1(ii), Theorem 1.2(ii)]
and by the stationarity of the Airy processes, we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)}{(3\log x)^{1/3}}=-1,\quad\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)}{(12\log t)^{1/3}}=-1,\quad\text{a.s.}

This motivates us to consider the lower level sets of the Airy processes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma):=\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)<-\gamma(3\log t)^{1/3}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(\gamma):=\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)<-\gamma(12\log t)^{1/3}\right\},

for
\gamma>0
.

Analogous to Theorem
1.1
, we have the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=1-\gamma^{3},\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.5)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{2}(\gamma))

\displaystyle=1-\gamma^{3},\quad\text{a.s.}

(1.6)

Theorem
1.2
suggests that the valleys of the Airy processes are multifractal
with respect to their corresponding gauge functions.
Note that the macroscopic Hausdorff dimensions of the upper and lower level sets are exactly the same for the Airy
1
and Airy
2
processes. This is because that the tail exponents are the same for the one-point distribution of both processes. We will make this point clear in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

2
Preliminary

In this section, we first follow Barlow and Taylor
[
3
,
4
]
and Khoshnevisan et al.
[
18
]
to introduce
macroscopic Hausdorff dimension. We will restrict to the one-dimensional case as the Airy processes have only one parameter. For a Borel set
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
, the
n
th shell of
E
is defined as
E\cap\{(-\mathrm{e}^{n+1},-\mathrm{e}^{n}]\cup[\mathrm{e}^{n},\mathrm{e}^{n+1})\}
.
Fix a number
c_{0}>0
. For
\rho>0
, we define
\rho
-dimensional Hausdorff content of the
n
th shell of
E
as

\displaystyle\nu_{\rho}^{n}(E):=\inf\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{{\rm length}(Q_{i})}{\mathrm{e}^{n}}\right)^{\rho},

where the infimum is taken over all intervals
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}
of length
\geq c_{0}
that cover the
n
th shell of
E
. The Barlow–Taylor macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}(E):=\inf\left\{\rho>0:\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\nu_{\rho}^{n}(E)<\infty\right\}.

The macroscopic Hausdorff dimension does not depend on
c_{0}
and hence we can choose
c_{0}=1
; see
[
18
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Khoshnevisan et al.
[
18
]
introduced the notion of thickness of a set, an import tool to give a lower bound on the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension which we now recall. Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
and define

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}(\theta):=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq\mathrm{e}^{n(1-\theta)+1}-\mathrm{e}^{n(1-\theta)}\\
i\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\left\{\mathrm{e}^{n}+i\mathrm{e}^{\theta n}\right\}.

(2.1)

Definition 2.1

(
[
18
, Definition 4.3]
)
.

We say that
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is
\theta
-thick if there exists an integer
M=M(\theta)
such that

E\cap[x,x+\mathrm{e}^{\theta n})\neq\emptyset

for all
x\in\Pi_{n}(\theta)
and
n\geq M
.

The following criterion on the lower bound of macroscopic Hausdorff dimension is due to Khoshnevisan et al.
[
18
]
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
18
, Proposition 4.4]
)
.

If
E\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is
\theta
-thick for some
\theta\in(0,1)
, then
{\rm Dim_{H}}(E)\geq 1-\theta
.

We will study the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the Airy
1
process using the association property. Recall from Esary et al.
[
14
]
that
a random vector
X:=(X_{1}\,,\ldots,X_{m})
is said to be
associated
if

\Cov[h_{1}(X)\,,h_{2}(X)]\geq 0,

(2.2)

for every pair of functions
h_{1},h_{2}:{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}

that are nondecreasing in every coordinate and satisfy
h_{1}(X),h_{2}(X)\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. A random field
\Phi=\{\Phi(x)\}_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
is

associated
if
(\Phi(x_{1})\,,\ldots,\Phi(x_{m}))
is associated
for every
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We remark that an associated random vector is also called to satisfy the FKG inequalities; see Newman
[
22
]
.

We recall some useful probability inequalities for an associated random vector
(X_{1}\,,\ldots,X_{m})
. Let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}
be real numbers. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j},1\leq j\leq m\}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\left(\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j},X_{k}\leq x_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{X_{k}\leq x_{k}\}\right).

(2.3)

The above inequality is proved in
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
, based on the Lebowitz’s inequality (see
[
23
, Theorem 1.2.2]
and
[
20
]
).
Similarly, using the Lebowitz’s inequality, we can derive that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}>x_{j},1\leq j\leq m\}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}>x_{j}\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\left(\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}>x_{j},X_{k}>x_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}>x_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{X_{k}>x_{k}\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad=\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq m}\left(\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j},X_{k}\leq x_{k}\}-\mathrm{P}\{X_{j}\leq x_{j}\}\mathrm{P}\{X_{k}\leq x_{k}\}\right),

(2.4)

where the equality holds obviously. Because the Airy
1
process is associated (see
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
), its marginal satisfies the above two inequalities. Moreover, since the one-point
distribution of the Airy
1
process, i.e., the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution has bounded and continuous density and finite second moment, we deduce from

[
23
, (6.2.20)]
that
there exists a constant
K>0
such that for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s,t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq s,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)\leq t\right\}-\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\leq s\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(y)\leq t\right\}\right)\leq K\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(x)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(y))\right]^{1/3}

(2.5)

(see also
[
25
, (6.11)]
).

Using the fact that certain centered and scaled passage times in the exponential last passage percolation (LPP) converge to the Airy processes, we will appeal to the exponential LPP to study the tail probability of maximum and minimum of the Airy processes. We state here the weak convergence results.
Let us introduce some notations of the relevant exponential LPP model.
Consider the last passage percolation on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with i.i.d.
\rm Exp(1)
passage times on the vertices. For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, let
u=u_{N}(s)=(N-\lfloor s(2N)^{2/3}\rfloor,N+\lfloor s(2N)^{2/3}\rfloor)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{r}

.
Let

denote the last passage time from
\bm{0}:=(0,0)
to
u
(i.e., the maximal total weight of an up/right path connecting
\bm{0}
and
u
excluding the last vertex).
Let
T_{N}^{*}(s)
denote the last passage time from
\mathcal{L}_{0}
to
u
(i.e., the maximal weight among all paths that start at some point in
\mathcal{L}_{0}
and end at
u
excluding the last vertex). We denote by
\Gamma_{u,v}
the almost surely unique geodesic (i.e, the up/right path with maximal passage time) between two points

.
\Gamma_{v}
will be used to denote the geodesic between
\bm{0}
and
v
.

]
and

As

\frac{T_{N}(s)-4N}{2^{4/3}N^{1/3}}\Rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{2}(s)-s^{2},

(2.6)

where

3
Macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the Airy
1
process

We first recall that the one-point distribution of the Airy
1
process is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq x\}=F_{1}(2x),\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
F_{1}
denotes the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution. By the asymptotic behavior of the GOE Tracy-Widom distribution (see
[
1
,
13
]
), we see that as
x\to+\infty

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)>x\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}+o(1))x^{3/2}},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)<-x\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{1}{3}+o(1))x^{3}}.

(3.2)

Moreover, according to
[
25
, Proposition 6.1]
, we have as
x\to+\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)>x\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}+o(1))x^{3/2}}.

(3.3)

Furthermore, Basu et al.
[
6
]
have shown that the covariance of the Airy
1
process
decays super-exponentially by showing that there exists
c^{\prime}>0
such that for all
t>1
,

\displaystyle\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(0))\leq\mathrm{e}^{c^{\prime}t^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}t^{3}},

(3.4)

(see
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
).

We first establish the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the upper level sets of the Airy
1
process in Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of (
1.3
).

Theorems 4.1 and 4.7 of
[
18
]
provide general macroscopic Hausdorff dimension estimates.
First, since the Airy
1
process is stationary,
we can combine
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
with (
3.3
) to deduce that for
\gamma\in(0,1)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}\left((3\log t)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}\leq 1-\gamma^{3/2},\quad\text{a.s.}

(3.5)

Theorem 4.7 of
[
18
]
cannot be applied directly to give a lower bound on the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension since we are not
able to construct a coupling process for the Airy
1
process which satisfies the conditions of Theorem 4.7 of
[
18
]
.
Instead, we will use the probability inequalities in Section
2
for the Airy
1
process to show that the upper level set is
\theta
-thick and
then derive a lower bound for the Hausdorff dimension by Proposition
2.2
.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
. We are aiming to show that

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}\left((3\log t)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}\geq 1-\gamma^{3/2},\quad\text{a.s.}

(3.6)

We will prove that

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}\left((3\log t)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}\geq 1-\theta,\quad\text{a.s. for all $\theta\in(\gamma^{3/2},1)$}.

Choose and fix
\theta\in(\gamma^{3/2},1)
. We also choose and fix sufficiently small positive constants
\delta
and
\eta
such that

\displaystyle\theta-\delta-(1+\frac{3}{4\sqrt{2}}\eta)\gamma^{3/2}>0.

(3.7)

Denote

\displaystyle x_{i,n}=\mathrm{e}^{n}+(i-1)\mathrm{e}^{n\theta}

(3.8)

for
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}:=\lfloor(\mathrm{e}-1)\mathrm{e}^{n(1-\theta)}+1\rfloor
. Recall the set
\Pi_{n}(\theta)
defined in (
2.1
) and we write

\displaystyle\Pi_{n}(\theta)=\{x_{i,n}:i=1,\ldots,L_{n}\}.

For
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}
, we define

\displaystyle z_{j,n}(i)=x_{i,n}+(j-1)\mathrm{e}^{n\delta}

(3.9)

where
j=1,\ldots,\ell_{n}(i)

with

\displaystyle\ell_{n}(i):=\lfloor\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)}\rfloor+1\in(\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)},2\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)}).

(3.10)

Since
\log s\leq n+1
for all
s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})
with
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{2/3}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{i,n,1}+\mathcal{P}_{i,n,2},

(3.11)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i,n,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\max_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{n}(i)}\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i,n,2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{n}(i)}\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}.

Since the Airy
1
process is associated (see
[
25
, Theorem 1.2]
), it follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i,n,1}

\displaystyle\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{k,n}(i)))\right]^{1/3}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}|z_{j,n}(i)-z_{k,n}(i))|^{3}}\leq K_{1}\ell_{n}(i)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq 4K_{1}\mathrm{e}^{2n(\theta-\delta)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}},

(3.12)

where the second inequality holds by (
3.4
) and the last inequality is due to (
3.10
).

In order to estimate
\mathcal{P}_{i,n,2}
, we first notice from (
3.1
)
that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)>\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}+\eta)(\frac{\gamma}{2})^{3/2}\frac{3(n+1)}{2}}=1-\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\frac{3\eta}{4\sqrt{2}})\gamma^{3/2}(n+1)},

where
\eta
is the fixed positive number satisfying (
3.7
). By stationarity of the Airy
1
process,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{i,n,2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3(n+1)/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}\right)^{\ell_{n}(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\frac{3\eta}{4\sqrt{2}})\gamma^{3/2}(n+1)}\right)^{\ell_{n}(i)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\ell_{n}(i)\mathrm{e}^{-(1+\frac{3\eta}{4\sqrt{2}})\gamma^{3/2}(n+1)}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)-(1+\frac{3\eta}{4\sqrt{2}})\gamma^{3/2}(n+1)}},

(3.13)

where the second inequality holds by the inequality
1-x\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x}
for all
x\geq 0

and the third inequality by (
3.10
).

Now, using the fact
L_{n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)}
, we see from (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{2/3}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq 4K_{1}\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)+2n(\theta-\delta)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}}+\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)-\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)-(1+\frac{3\eta}{4\sqrt{2}})\gamma^{3/2}(n+1)}}.

In light of (
3.7
), it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\sup_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{2/3}}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(3/2\right)^{2/3}\right\}<\infty.

Borel-Cantelli’s lemma ensures that almost surely, for all but a finite number of integers
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{2/3}}>\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{2/3},\quad\text{for all $1\leq i\leq L_{n}$}.

This implies that almost surely for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\gamma):=\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}((3\log t)/2)^{2/3}\right\}\cap[x,x+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})\neq\emptyset,\quad\text{for all $x\in\Pi_{n}(\theta)$}.

In other words, the upper level set
\mathcal{U}(\gamma)

is
\theta
-thick almost surely (see Definition
2.1
).
Therefore, we conclude from Proposition
2.2
that for
\theta\in(\gamma^{3/2},1)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)>\frac{\gamma}{2}((3\log t)/2)^{2/3}\right\}\geq 1-\theta,\quad\text{a.s.}

We let
\theta\downarrow\gamma^{3/2}
to complete the proof of (
3.6
).

Therefore, the equality in (
1.3
) follows from (
3.5
) and (
3.6
).
∎

We proceed to establish the macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the lower level sets of the Airy
1
process in Theorem
1.2
. The proof of (
1.5
) is similar to that of (
1.3
). In order to give an upper bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the lower level sets, we need to estimate the lower tail probability of the minimum of the Airy
1

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exists a constant
C>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
) such that for all sufficiently
large
x
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq-x\right\}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}.

(3.14)

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
By Lemma
5.1
below, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
), for
N
sufficiently large (depending on
x,\varepsilon
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{u\in I^{\delta}}T_{\mathcal{L}_{0},u}-4N\leq-x2^{4/3}N^{1/3}\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{6}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}.

Since
\min_{s\in[-\delta/8,\delta/8]}T_{N}^{*}(s)\geq\min_{u\in I^{\delta}}T_{\mathcal{L}_{0},u}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[-\delta/8,\delta/8]}T_{N}^{*}(s)-4N\leq-x2^{4/3}N^{1/3}\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{u\in I^{\delta}}T_{\mathcal{L}_{0},u}-4N\leq-x2^{4/3}N^{1/3}\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{6}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}

for any
x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
) and for
N
sufficiently large (depending on
x,\varepsilon
). By Theorem
2.4
and the continuity of the mapping
f\mapsto\min_{s\in[-\delta/8,\delta/8]}f(s)
in the topology of uniform convergence, we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{2^{1/3}\min_{s\in[-\delta/8,\delta/8]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(2^{-2/3}s)\leq-x\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{6}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}

for any
x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
). Using stationarity of the Airy
1
process, we see
that for any
x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,\delta/4]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(2^{-2/3}s)\leq-x\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}.

If
2^{-2/3}\delta/4\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq-x\right\}\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,\delta/4]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(2^{-2/3}s)\leq-x\right\}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}.

If
2^{-2/3}\delta/4<1
, then using again stationarity of the Airy
1
process,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq-x\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,\frac{\delta}{2^{2/3}4}\cdot(\lfloor 2^{2/3}4/\delta\rfloor+1)]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq-x\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(\lfloor 2^{2/3}4/\delta\rfloor+1)\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,\frac{\delta}{4}]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(2^{-2/3}s)\leq-x\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(\lfloor 2^{2/3}4/\delta\rfloor+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}}.

Therefore, we conclude that there exists
C>0
(depending on
\varepsilon
) such that for
any
x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\leq-x\right\}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}(1-\varepsilon)x^{3}},

which proves Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We are now ready to prove (
1.5
). We first give an upper bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the lower level set of the Airy
1
process.

Proof of (
1.5
): upper bound.

The proof follows from a modification of the proof of
[
18
, Theorem 4.1]
by using Lemma
3.1
.
We include the details for the sake of completeness.
Fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
and recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}({\gamma})=\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)<-\gamma(3\log t)^{1/3}\}.

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. According to Lemma
3.1
,
there exits a positive constant
C_{\varepsilon}
such that for all sufficiently large
m

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma)\cap[m,m+1)\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{s\in[m,m+1)}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)<-\gamma(3\log m)^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{s\in[0,1]}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)<-\gamma(3\log m)^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\varepsilon}}{m^{(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}}}.

Therefore, we can cover
\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma)\cap[\mathrm{e}^{n},\mathrm{e}^{n+1})
by intervals of length
1
to see that for all
\rho>0
and sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\nu_{\rho}^{n}(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma))\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
[m,m+1)\subset[\mathrm{e}^{n},\mathrm{e}^{n+1})\end{subarray}}\mathrm{e}^{-n\rho}\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma)\cap[m,m+1)\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{-n\rho}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\\
[m,m+1)\subset[\mathrm{e}^{n},\mathrm{e}^{n+1})\end{subarray}}m^{-(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{-n\rho}\mathrm{e}^{-n(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}}\mathrm{e}^{n+1}=C_{\varepsilon}\mathrm{e}^{-n(\rho+(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}-1)+1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\nu_{\rho}^{n}(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma))\right]<\infty,\quad\text{if $\rho>1-(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}$}.

This proves that
{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma))\leq\rho
a.s. for all
\rho>1-(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}
. Send
\rho\downarrow 1-(1-\varepsilon)\gamma^{3}
and then
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
to deduce that
{\rm Dim_{H}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}(\gamma))\leq 1-\gamma^{3}
a.s. This completes the proof of the upper bound in (
1.5
).
∎

We next give a lower bound on the Hausdorff dimension of the lower level set of the Airy
1
process.

Proof of (
1.5
): lower bound.

Fix
\gamma\in(0,1)
.
We will prove that

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)<-\gamma\left(3\log t\right)^{1/3}\right\}\geq 1-\theta,\quad\text{a.s. for all $\theta\in(\gamma^{3},1)$}.

Choose and fix
\theta\in(\gamma^{3},1)
. We also choose and fixed sufficiently small positive constants
\delta
and
\eta
such that

\displaystyle\theta-\delta-(1+3\eta)\gamma^{3}>0.

(3.15)

We define the points
x_{i,n}
and
z_{j,n}(i)
in the same way as in (
3.8
) and (
3.9
) with
\theta
and

\delta

satisfying the condition in (
3.15
).
For
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{1/3}}\geq-\gamma 3^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)\geq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\geq-\left(3\gamma n\right)^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,1}+\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,2},

(3.16)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left\{\min_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\geq-\left(3\gamma n\right)^{1/3}\right\}-\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{n}(i)}\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\geq-\left(3\gamma n\right)^{1/3}\right\},

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{n}(i)}\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\geq-\left(3\gamma n\right)^{1/3}\right\}.

Because the Airy
1
process is associated and its the one-point distribution
has a continuous probability density function, we derive from (
2.4
) that for
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,1}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\bigg(\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3},\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{k,n}(i))\leq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\leq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{k,n}(i))\leq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\left[\Cov(\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{j,n}(i))\,,\mathcal{A}_{1}(z_{k,n}(i)))\right]^{1/3},

where the second inequality follows from (
2.5
).
By (
3.4
) and the fact that
|z_{j,n}(i)-z_{k,n}(i)|\geq\mathrm{e}^{n\delta}
for all
1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)
,
there exists a constant
K_{2}>0
such that for
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,1}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq\ell_{n}(i)}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}|z_{j,n}(i)-z_{k,n}(i))|^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq K_{2}\ell_{n}(i)^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}}\leq 4K_{2}\mathrm{e}^{2n(\theta-\delta)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}},

(3.17)

where the last inequality holds by (
3.10
).
To estimate
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,2}
, we first see from that (
3.2
)
for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\geq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}=1-\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)<-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}\leq 1-\mathrm{e}^{-(1+3\eta)n\gamma^{3}},

where
\eta
is a fixed positive number satisfying (
3.15
).
Then, by stationarity of the Airy
1
process, for
i=1,\ldots,L_{n}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{i,n,2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{P}\left\{\mathcal{A}_{1}(0)\geq-\gamma\left(3n\right)^{1/3}\right\}\right)^{\ell_{n}(i)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\mathrm{e}^{-(1+3\eta)n\gamma^{3}}\right)^{\ell_{n}(i)}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\ell_{n}(i)\mathrm{e}^{-(1+3\eta)\gamma^{3}n}}\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)-(1+3\eta)\gamma^{3}n}},

(3.18)

where the second inequality holds by the inequality
1-x\leq\mathrm{e}^{-x}
for all
x\geq 0

and the third inequality by (
3.10
).

Because
L_{n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)}
, we deduce from
(
3.16
), (
3.17
) and (
3.18
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log x)^{1/3}}\geq-\gamma 3^{1/3}\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq 4K_{2}\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)+2n(\theta-\delta)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{e}^{3n\delta}}+\mathrm{e}^{1+n(1-\theta)-\mathrm{e}^{n(\theta-\delta)-(1+3\eta)\gamma^{3}n}},

which implies by (
3.15
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{L_{n}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\inf_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log s)^{1/3}}\geq-\gamma 3^{1/3}\right\}<\infty.

Borel-Cantelli’s lemma ensures that almost surely, for all but a finite number of integers
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\inf_{s\in[x_{i,n},x_{i,n}+\mathrm{e}^{n\theta})}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{1}(s)}{(\log x)^{1/3}}<-\gamma 3^{1/3},\quad\text{for all $1\leq i\leq L_{n}$}.

This means that the lower level set
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
is
\theta
-thick a.s. (see Definition
2.1
). Therefore, we conclude from Proposition
2.2
that for
\theta\in(\gamma^{3},1)

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{1}(t)<-\gamma\left(3\log t\right)^{1/3}\right\}\geq 1-\theta,\quad\text{a.s.}

We let
\theta\downarrow\gamma^{3}
to complete the proof.
∎

4
Macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the Airy
2
process

As we have seen in Section
3
, the approach to Macroscopic Hausdorff dimension of the level sets of the Airy
1
process relies on the exponential decay rate of the covariance of the Airy
1
process. This method does not apply to the Airy
2
process since
its covariance decays polynomially; see Widom
[
28
]
. We will make use of the exponential LPP to give some quantitative estimates on the tail probabilities of the
maximum and minimum of the Airy
2
process. First we state the estimates about the maximum and minimum of the Airy
2
process over an interval which can be obtained using the weak convergence of the point-to-point passage times in exponential LPP (
2.6
) and assuming the corresponding exponential LPP estimates. The exponential LPP estimates will be proved in Section
5
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
\varepsilon,\delta>0,x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
) and
t
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon,\delta
) there exists
c>0
such that the following holds:

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\mathcal{A}_{2}(s)\leq x\right)\leq\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{4}{3}(1+\varepsilon)x^{3/2}}t^{1-\delta}}+\mathrm{e}^{-c(\log t)^{2}}.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Proposition
5.4
below and Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\varepsilon,\delta>0,x
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon
) and
t
sufficiently large (depending on
\varepsilon,\delta
) there exist

such that the following holds:

This is a consequence of Proposition
5.5
below and Theorem
2.3
.
∎

4.1

) and (

)

First, according to

Using triangle inequality and the fact that (see
[
1
,
13
]

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{\mathcal{A}_{2}(0)>x\}=\mathrm{e}^{-(\frac{4}{3}+o(1))x^{3/2}},\quad\text{as $x\to+\infty$},

\varepsilon>0

(4.2)

Then we can combine (
4.2

[
18

to obtain that for

(4.3)

We next show that
for

\displaystyle{\rm Dim_{H}}\left\{t>\mathrm{e}:\mathcal{A}_{2}(t)>\gamma\left((3\log t)/4\right)^{2/3}\right\}\geq 1-\gamma^{3/2},\quad\text{a.s.}

Fix

. Analogous to the proof of the lower bound for (

2

with

This completes the proof.
∎

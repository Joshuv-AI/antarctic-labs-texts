Laurent type expansion of multiple polylogarithms at integer points
Pawan Singh Mehta and Biswajyoti Saha

Abstract
. In this article, we study the local behaviour of the multiple polylogarithm functions at integer points, in the s s -aspect. This is done by writing a Laurent type expansion at integer points, involving certain power series and rational functions. The coefficients of these power series are the regularised values of the multiple polylogarithm functions at certain related integer points.

11M32, 32Dxx

1.
Introduction

Throughout this article, we denote the set of all non-negative integers by
{\mathbb{N}}
.
For positive integers
r
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
and complex numbers
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
with
|z_{i}|<1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
,
the multiple polylogarithm (of depth
r
) is defined by the convergent power series

\mathrm{Li}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r};z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}):=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{a_{r}}}.

This definition can be extended for
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, if
a_{1}\geq 2
.
Further, this series can be considered as a multiple Dirichlet series by fixing complex numbers

z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
and replacing
a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
by complex variables
s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}
such that
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
, i.e.,

(1)

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}},

where

U_{r}:=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}~:~\Re(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i})>i~\text{ for all }~1\leq i\leq r\}.

The above series converges normally on compact subsets of
U_{r}
and
therefore defines a holomorphic function there (see
[
5
, Proposition 2]
).
The depth
0
multiple polylogarithm function is taken to be the constant function
1
.
In our recent work
[
4
]
,
we have shown that the series in (
1
) converges (conditionally) on a larger open domain, depending on
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})
,
by treating it as

(2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{N>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

For an integer
r\geq 1
and
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, where
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, let

q({\bf z})
be the smallest positive integer such that
1\leq q({\bf z})\leq r
and
z_{q({\bf z})}\neq 1
.
If no such
q({\bf z})
exists, then we set
q({\bf z}):=r+1
. Consider the open set
U_{r}(\mathbf{z})
of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
defined as follows:

(3)

\begin{split}U_{r}(\mathbf{z}):=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}:&\ \Re(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i})>i\text{ if }1\leq i<q({\bf z})\\
&\text{ and }\Re(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i})>i-1\text{ if }q({\bf z})\leq i\leq r\}.\end{split}

We proved the following in
[
4
, Theorem 2]
.

Theorem 1
.

The multiple Dirichlet series in (
1
) converges on
U_{r}(\mathbf{z})
.
Moreover, the series in (
1
) defines a holomorphic function on
U_{r}(\mathbf{z})
.

This theorem therefore extends the classical result for the depth
1
Dirichlet series
\mathrm{Li}_{(z)}(s)
.
However, this open domain of convergence is not optimal in general.
For example, as
N\to\infty
, one has

(4)

\sum_{N>n_{1}>n_{2}>0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{2}}}{n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{-1}}=\frac{\log 2}{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}+o(1).

Clearly,
(2,-1)\notin U_{2}(1,-1)
. In fact, one can see that for
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Re(s)>1
, the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{N>n_{1}>n_{2}>0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{2}}}{n_{1}^{s}n_{2}^{-1}}

exists. In
[
4
, Theorem 3]
, we established a more accurate and larger open domain
where the series in (
1
) (viewed as the limit in (
2
)) converges at integer points.
To recall the statement, we need a few more notations. Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}

be such that
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are roots of unity. For each
1\leq i\leq r
, define

(5)

J_{i}(\mathbf{z}):=\{j:1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }z_{[j,i]}=1\},\ \text{ and }\ Q_{i}(\mathbf{z}):=|J_{i}(\mathbf{z})|,

the number of elements in
J_{i}(\mathbf{z})
, where
z_{[j,i]}:=\prod_{k=j}^{i}z_{k}
for
1\leq j\leq i\leq r
. Consider the open set

(6)

V_{r}(\mathbf{z}):=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}:\Re(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i})>Q_{i}(\mathbf{z})\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq r\}.

It is easy to see that
V_{r}(\mathbf{z})=U_{r}
if and only if
\mathbf{z}=(1,\ldots,1)
. Moreover,

U_{r}(\mathbf{z})\subseteq V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
.
We proved the following in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\in{\mathbb{C}}
be roots of unity. The multiple Dirichlet series in (
1
)
converges at integer points of
V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
. Moreover, for an integer point
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in V_{r}(\mathbf{z})

and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
, the series

(7)

\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}(\log n_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}(\log n_{r})^{k_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

converges when we consider it as the limit

(8)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{N>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}(\log n_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}(\log n_{r})^{k_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

This theorem was proved by devising a regularisation process for the series defining the multiple
polylogarithm functions (see
[
4
, §2]
). However, the question of holomorphicity of
the multiple polylogarithm functions at integer points of
V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
is a delicate one and
was not addressed in
[
4
]
.

In this article, which is a follow-up of
[
4
]
, we use the regularisation process devised in
our article
[
4
]
to address this
problem by writing explicit Laurent type expansion for the multiple polylogarithm functions around
all the integer points in
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, for any positive integer
r
. A prototype of the desired Laurent type expansion is

\zeta(s)=\frac{1}{s-1}+\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k}\gamma_{k}}{k!}(s-1)^{k},

where for
k\geq 0
,
\gamma_{k}
is the Stieltjes constant defined by

\gamma_{k}:=\lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{N>n>0}\frac{\log^{k}n}{n}-\frac{\log^{k+1}N}{k+1}\right).

In
[
6
]
, the second author provided explicit Laurent type expansion for the multiple zeta functions around
integer points. Our work here therefore extends the work of
[
6
]
to the case of the multiple polylogarithm
functions. This requires us to study the regularised multiple polylogarithm functions, which we define as follows:

For a positive integer
r
, consider
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
and
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, such that

z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are roots of unity. Then we consider the formal power series

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}{(\mathbf{z})}(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}},

where the constant
\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
is the regularised value of the sequence of partial sums

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{N>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}(\log n_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}(\log n_{r})^{k_{r}}}{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{a_{r}}},

as defined in
[
4
, Definitions 2, 3]
. For the sake of completeness, we recall from
[
4
]
that as
N\to\infty
,
the series (
10
) can be expressed as a sum of four different types of terms, namely, one which goes to
\infty
in
modulus, one which is bounded but oscillates, one which remains constant and one which goes to
0
. The constant term is then
defined to be the regularised value
\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
of the series (
10
).
For example,

\sum_{N>n>0}(-1)^{n}=-\frac{(-1)^{N}}{2}-\frac{1}{2},\ \sum_{N>n>0}(-1)^{n}n=-\frac{(-1)^{N}N}{2}+\frac{(-1)^{N}}{4}-\frac{1}{4},

and further, as
N\to\infty
, we also have

\sum_{N>n_{1}>n_{2}>0}\frac{(-1)^{n_{2}}n_{2}}{n_{1}}=-\frac{\log N}{4}+\frac{(-1)^{N}}{4}+\frac{1-\log 2-\gamma}{4}+o(1),

where
\gamma
is the Euler’s constant. Thus,
\ell_{[0]}^{(0)}(-1)=-1/2
,
\ell_{[0]}^{(-1)}(-1)=-1/4
and

\ell_{[0,0]}^{(1,-1)}(1,-1)=(1-\log 2-\gamma)/4
. For more details on the above regularisation process we suggest the reader to see
[
4
, §2]
.

Now for the formal power series (
9
), we prove that it
converges in a neighbourhood of
{\bf a}
and it can be extended to a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
We denote this meromorphic function by

\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and call it
the (depth
r
) regularised multiple polylogarithm function for
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{a}
.
The depth
0
regularised multiple polylogarithm function is taken to be the constant function
1
.
If
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap U_{r}(\mathbf{z})
, then as meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
we have

\mathrm{Li}_{\mathbf{z}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}).

But for other integer points of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, this is not necessarily true.
For example, if
r=2
and
\mathbf{z}=(1,-1)
, then around the point
\mathbf{a}=(1,1)
, we have the
following equality between meromorphic functions:

(11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\mathbf{z}}(s_{1},s_{2})=\frac{\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(-1;1)}{(s_{2})}}{s_{1}-1}+\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},s_{2}).

Hence
s_{1}=1
is the only polar hyperplane of
\mathrm{Li}_{\mathbf{z}}(s_{1},s_{2})

which passes through the point
(1,1)
.

The formulation of our theorem is more delicate for general integer points, particularly for those lying outside

\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
. Hence we first consider the case of integer points in
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
.
This has been stated in Theorem
4
in §
2
and the proof is given in §
4
. The intermediate steps involved in the proof of Theorem
4
are discussed in §
3
and the required proofs are given in §
5
.
In §
6
, we discuss the required setup of asymptotic expansions of sequences of germs of holomorphic and
meromorphic functions with variable coefficients, which we use to prove our theorem for the general integer points in
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}

(see Theorem
6
below). This theorem and its proof appear in §
7
. Theorem
6
also allows us
to discuss the question of holomorphicity of the multiple polylogarithm function at integer points of
V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
,
which we have mentioned above (see Corollary
1
below).

2.
Behaviour of the multiple polylogarithms at the integer points of
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer.
In this section, for
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, where
z_{i}
’s are fixed roots of unity for all
1\leq i\leq r
,
we discuss the local behaviour of the multiple polylogarithms at the integer points of
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
.
Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
. Then from the definition of
U_{r}(\mathbf{z})

(see (
3
)), we can deduce that the only possible polar hyperplanes of the multiple polylogarithm function

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
passing through a point
\mathbf{a}
of
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}

are of the form
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}=i
or
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}=i-1
for some
1\leq i\leq r
, depending on the complex
tuple
\mathbf{z}
and the point
\mathbf{a}
. To understand this in detail, we recall the following theorem
(see
[
2
, Lemma 1.2]
and
[
5
, Theorem 9]
).

Theorem 3
.

The function
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

extends to a meromorphic function on the whole of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
If
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, then

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
Otherwise, if
i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}
denote all the indices such that
z_{[1,i_{j}]}=1

for all
1\leq j\leq m
, then the set of all possible polar hyperplanes of

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
can be given as follows:

(a)
If
i_{1}=1
, then
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is
holomorphic outside the union of the hyperplanes given by the equations

s_{1}=1;~s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i_{j}}=n\ \text{ for all integer }n\leq j\ \text{and }2\leq j\leq m.

(b)
If
i_{1}\not=1
, then
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is
holomorphic outside the union of the hyperplanes given by the equations

s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i_{j}}=n\text{ for all integer }n\leq j\text{ and }1\leq j\leq m.

From the above theorem, we can deduce that the plane
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}=i
is a
polar hyperplane of
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
passing through the point

\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
only if
z_{j}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq i
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i
.
Now suppose that
z_{j}\neq 1
for some
1\leq j\leq i
. Then the necessary condition for the plane

s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}=i-1
to be a polar hyperplane of
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

passing through the point
\mathbf{a}
is
z_{[1,i]}=1
and
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-1
.
Note that this can happen only if there exists exactly one integer
i_{0}
such that

1\leq i_{0}<i
and
z_{[1,i_{0}]}\neq 1
and
z_{[1,i]}=1
. Hence
z_{j}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq i
,
except for
j=i_{0},i_{0}+1
. This can also be equivalently written as the condition that there exists exactly
one integer
j_{0}
such that
1<j_{0}\leq i
and
z_{[j_{0},i]}\neq 1
and
z_{[1,i]}=1
. In fact,

j_{0}=i_{0}+1
.

This prompts us to define an indexing set
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})

which is the collection of all integers
0\leq i\leq r
and satisfies the following properties:

(I)

z_{[1,i]}=1
,

(II)

there is at most one integer
t_{i}
such that
1\leq t_{i}\leq i
and
z_{[t_{i},i]}\neq 1
,

(III)

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-a(\mathbf{z})_{i}
, where
a(\mathbf{z})_{i}:=

\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }z_{j}=1\text{ for all }1\leq j\leq i,\\
1&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

By convention, we set
a(\mathbf{z})_{0}=0
and hence
0\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
. Therefore,

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
is non-empty for all
\mathbf{z}
and
\mathbf{a}
.
So for example, if
r=2
and
{\bf a}=(1,1)
, then
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\{0,1,2\}
for
{\bf z}=(1,1)
,

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\{0,1\}
for
{\bf z}=(1,-1)
, and
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\{0\}
for both

{\bf z}=(-1,-1)
and
{\bf z}=(-1,1)
.

Remark 1
.

In condition (II) of the above definition, if for some
i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
, there is no
t_{i}

such that
1\leq t_{i}\leq i
and
z_{[t_{i},i]}\neq 1
, then we take
t_{i}=0
.

For
i\geq 1
in
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
, we define the function
H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})

in
s_{j},\ldots,s_{i}
, for each
1\leq j\leq i
, as follows:

(12)

\displaystyle H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]}):=\begin{cases}s_{j}+\cdots+s_{i}-(i-j)&\text{ if }1\leq j<t_{i},\\
1-z_{[t_{i},i]}&\text{ if }j=t_{i},\\
s_{j}+\cdots+s_{i}-(i-j+1)&\text{ if }t_{i}<j\leq i.\end{cases}

With these notations in place, we are ready to state our main theorem of this section.

Theorem 4
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be such that
z_{i}
’s are roots of unity for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
. Then the power series

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}

converges in a neighbourhood of
{\bf a}
and can be extended to a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
Moreover, if this meromorphic function is denoted by
\mathrm{Li}^{\rm{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
,
then we have the following equality between the meromorphic functions:

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\rm{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})}(-1)^{i}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})}.

For an integer point
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
, in order to get a Laurent type
expansion of
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
around
\mathbf{a}
from (
14
), we need an

inversion process
. For this we first note that for every
i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
, the point

(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r-i}\cap\overline{U_{r-i}(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}
. This is obvious for
i=0
. For
i\geq 1
and

i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
, we have
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-a({\bf z})_{i}
.
Let
k
be an integer such that
i\leq k\leq r
. Note that by definition, we have

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=i-a({\bf z})_{i}+a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{k}\geq k-a({\bf z})_{k}.

Therefore,
a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{k}\geq(k-i)-\left(a({\bf z})_{k}-a({\bf z})_{i}\right)
. Note that
0\leq a({\bf z})_{k}-a({\bf z})_{i}\leq 1
,
as
a({\bf z})_{i}=1
implies
a({\bf z})_{k}=1
. Now we only need to study the case
a({\bf z})_{k}-a({\bf z})_{i}=1
, i.e.,

a({\bf z})_{i}=0
and
a({\bf z})_{k}=1
. This happens if and only if
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{i}=1
and there exists an integer
j

such that
i<j\leq k
and
z_{j}\neq 1
. Thus,
a({\bf z})_{k}=a(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{k})_{k-i}=1
and in this case

a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{k}\geq(k-i)-a(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{k})_{k-i}.

Therefore, we get
(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r-i}\cap{\overline{U_{r-i}(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r}})}
.

For
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
, we write
(
14
) for every point of the form
(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})
, where

i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
. All these equations can be concisely written
in the following matrix form:

{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}={\bf M}{\bf V},

where
{\bf V}
and
{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}
are column vectors indexed by the elements of

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
, such that for each
i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
,
the corresponding entries of
{\bf V}
and
{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}
are
\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})

and
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r};a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})
, respectively,
and
{\bf M}
is an upper triangular matrix with entries in the field of rational functions

{\mathbb{C}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
, with the diagonal entries as
1
. Hence
{\bf M}
is an invertible matrix with entries in

{\mathbb{C}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and we can write

{\bf V}={\bf M}^{-1}{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}.

Now the first entry of the above matrix equation gives an expression of the form

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})}R_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r};a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r}),

where
R_{0}(\varnothing):=1
and for
i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z,a})
with
i\geq 1
,
R_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})

is a rational function in
s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}
. This is the desired Laurent type expansion of

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
around
{\bf a}
.
This can be seen in the following examples.

Example 1
.

For
r=2,\mathbf{z}=(1,-1)
and
\mathbf{a}=(1,1)
, we have seen that

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\{0,1\}
and hence by (
14
), we have

\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},s_{2})=\mathrm{Li}_{\mathbf{z}}(s_{1},s_{2})-\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{(-1)}{(s_{2})}}{s_{1}-1}.

Again, around the point
s_{2}=1
, we have
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(-1;1)}{(s_{2})}=\mathrm{Li}_{(-1)}{(s_{2})}
, and thus
we recover (
11
).

A more involved example is as follows:

Example 2
.

For
r=3,{\bf z}=(1,-1,-1)
and
{\bf a}=(1,1,0)
, we have
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\{0,1,3\}
. In this case, we have

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{({\bf z};{\bf a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(-1,-1;1,0)}(s_{2},s_{3})\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\frac{1}{s_{1}-1}&-\frac{1}{2(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)(s_{3}+s_{2}+s_{1}-2)}\\
0&1&\frac{1}{2(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)}\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\\
\mathrm{Li}_{(-1,-1)}(s_{2},s_{3})\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore,

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\\
\mathrm{Li}_{(-1,-1)}(s_{2},s_{3})\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{s_{1}-1}&-\frac{1}{2(s_{1}-1)(s_{3}+s_{2}+s_{1}-2)}\\
0&1&-\frac{1}{2(s_{3}+s_{2}-1)}\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{({\bf z};{\bf a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(-1,-1;1,0)}(s_{2},s_{3})\\
1\end{bmatrix},

and hence we get the Laurent type expansion around the point
{\bf a}=(1,1,0)
, given by

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{({\bf z};{\bf a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})+\frac{\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(-1,-1;1,0)}(s_{2},s_{3})}{s_{1}-1}-\frac{1}{2(s_{1}-1)(s_{3}+s_{2}+s_{1}-2)}.

In the next section, we setup the intermediate steps required to prove Theorem
4
.

3.
Intermediate steps for Theorem
4

We first recall the definition of the multiple polylogarithm-star function. Let
r\geq 1
be an integer. In what follows,

{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
is fixed such that
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, unless otherwise stated. The multiple polylogarithm-star
function of depth
r
is defined on
U_{r}
by

(15)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}):=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq 1}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

This series converges uniformly on every compact subset of the open set
U_{r}
.
Therefore, it defines a holomorphic function on
U_{r}
. Since the multiple polylogarithm-star function of
depth
r
can be written as a linear combination
of the multiple polylogarithm functions of depth
\leq r
(can be seen using an analogue of
[
3
, eq. (33)]
),
it can be extended as a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. As before,
the depth
0
multiple polylogarithm-star function is taken to be the constant function
1
.

Similarly, for an integer
N\geq 1
and
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
, consider the tail

(16)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}=\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}:=\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

Again for each integer
N\geq 1
,
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
is holomorphic on

U_{r}
and can be extended to a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. As usual, the depth
0
analogue of this function
is taken to be the constant function
1
. We first note that the tail of the multiple polylogarithm-star function

\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
satisfies a translation formula, which is proved in the following section.

Proposition 1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
r\geq 1
be integers and
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
. Then for
r=1
, we have

(17)

\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1})}(s_{1}-1)_{\geq N}+\frac{z_{1}^{N-1}}{N^{s_{1}-1}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1})}(s_{1}+k)_{\geq N},

and for
r>1
, we have

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}-1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}+\frac{1}{z_{1}}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
&=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N},\end{split}

where for any complex number
s
and non-negative integer
k
,
(s)_{k}
denotes the Pochhammer symbol

s(s+1)\cdots(s+k-1)
.

Remark 2
.

In (
17
) and (
18
) above, the first term on the left-hand side is considered to be
0
if
z_{1}=1
and when

z_{1}\neq 1
, note that
(s_{1}-1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}({\bf z})
and hence the series defining

\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}-1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
converges (as can be seen from
[
4
]
).
The identities (
17
) and (
18
) also extend to
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, as equality between the meromorphic functions.

Now to prove Theorem
4
, the key step is to find the relation between
the multiple polylogarithm functions, the tail of the multiple polylogarithm-star functions
and the partial sum of the series defining the multiple polylogarithm functions.
Let
r\geq 1
and
N\geq 2
be integers. Consider the holomorphic function

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N}=\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N}:=\sum_{N>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. Then we have the following analogue of
[
6
, Theorem 4]
.

Theorem 5
.

The following equality holds between the meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
:

(19)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{i},\ldots,z_{1})}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{1})_{\geq N}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r}).

Proof.

The proof can be deduced following the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 4]
, as it uses only the combinatorics between the indices.
∎

In view of the above combinatorial formula we need to study

\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{r},\ldots,z_{1})}(s_{r},\ldots,s_{1})_{\geq N}
around the integer points in
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}

for
r\geq 1
. Several cases pertaining to this are covered in the following lemmas, whose proofs require Proposition
1
.
The proofs of these lemmas are given in the following section.

Lemma 2

Let

be an integer.

Lemma 3
.

Let

Assume that there exist at least two integers

\left\|P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{r},\ldots,z_{1})}^{\star}(s_{r},\ldots,s_{1})_{\geq N}\right\|_{D}\rightarrow 0.

Lemma 4

Let

\mathbf{a}

4.
Proof of Theorem
4

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4
. We need the following notations.
Suppose
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1
for some
1\leq i\leq r
. Then clearly
i\notin\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
.
Also, in that case
a(\mathbf{z})_{i}=1
. Now we define the set

\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
which is the collection of all the indices
1\leq i\leq r
such that

(I)

z_{1}\neq 1
and
z_{j}=1
for all
2\leq j\leq i
,

(II)

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-1
.

Remark 3
.

It is clear that
\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})\cap\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})=\varnothing
and the set

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})\cup\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
is the collection of all the indices
0\leq i\leq r

such that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-a(\mathbf{z})_{i}
and there exists at most one integer
1\leq t_{i}\leq i
with
z_{[t_{i},i]}\neq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Since
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
, we have
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{i}\cap\overline{U_{i}(\mathbf{z})}

for each
1\leq i\leq r
.
Hence
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}\geq i-a({\mathbf{z})_{i}}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Now applying Lemma
1
,
2
and
3
to each of

\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i},\ldots,z_{1})}^{\star}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{1})_{\geq N}
for each
i=1,\ldots,r
, we can find a common polydisc
D_{1}
around
{\bf a}

and a common polynomial
P_{1}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

with the desired property. Furthermore, we can find a common polynomial
P_{2}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and a polydisc
D_{2}
around
{\bf a}

such that the function
P_{2}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})
is holomorphic on
D_{2}
for each
i=0,\ldots,r-1
.
Hence, in view of Theorem
5
, we consider
D=D_{1}\cap D_{2}
and
P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=P_{1}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})P_{2}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

to see (using Remark
3
) that the sequence of holomorphic functions
(u_{N})_{N\geq 1}
on
D
, given by

\displaystyle u_{N}:=

\displaystyle P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N}

\displaystyle-P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})}\frac{(-1)^{i}N^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}+a({\mathbf{z}})_{i}-i)}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})}

\displaystyle-P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})}\frac{(-1)^{i}z_{[1,i]}^{N}N^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}+1-i)}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})},

converges normally to
0
on
D
, as
N\to\infty
.

Note that for
i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})\cup\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
,
the Taylor series expansion of
N^{-(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{i}+a({\mathbf{z}})_{i}-i)}
around the point
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})
is

\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}\geq 0}\frac{(-\log N)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{i}!}(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{i}-a_{i})^{k_{i}},

as for such
i
, we have
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}=i-a({\mathbf{z}})_{i}
. Therefore, applying Lemma
4
to the sequence of functions

(u_{N})_{N\geq 1}
, we get that the formal power series

P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}{(\mathbf{z})}(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}

converges on
D
, and it is equal to

P(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(-1)^{i}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})}

on
D
, taking only the contributions from the constant terms. Note that the indices

i\in\mathcal{I}{{}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})
do not takes part in
this expression because
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1
, for such
i
. This implies that the
formal power series (
13
) converges on
D
. As it is denoted by

\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
, therefore in the polydisc
D
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{z},\mathbf{a})}(-1)^{i}\frac{\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq i}H_{j}^{(i)}(s_{j},\ldots,s_{i};z_{[t_{i},i]})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
4
.
∎

5.
Proof of the intermediate steps

Since the proofs of Lemma
1
,
2
and
3
, require Proposition
1
,
we first prove the following lemma which is required to prove Proposition
1
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
r\geq 1
be integers. Let
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be such that
|z_{i}|\leq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.
Let
K
be any compact subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
and
k_{0}
be the smallest non-negative integer such
that
(s_{1}+k_{0},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
for all
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in K
. Then the sequence of functions

\left(\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right)_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N,k\geq k_{0}}

converges normally on
K
. Moreover, the sum is
O(N^{-\epsilon})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Remark 4
.

For any point
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, there exists a non-negative integer
k_{0}
such that

(a_{1}+k_{0},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})\in U_{r}
. Therefore, we can find a suitably small polydisc
D

around the point
(a_{1}+k_{0},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})
in
U_{r}
such that
\overline{D}\subseteq U_{r}
. Hence,
there exists a polydisc
D
around
{\bf a}
such that for all
k\geq k_{0}
,
the function
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
is holomorphic on
D
.
Moreover, applying Lemma
5
, we have

\left\|\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right\|_{D}=o(1),

as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

5.1.
Proof of Lemma
5

Let
A:=\sup_{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in K}|s_{1}-1|
, then for any positive integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
, we have

\left\|\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right\|_{K}\leq\frac{A(A+1)\cdots(A+k)}{2^{k-k_{0}}(k+1)!}\left\|\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k_{0}}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right\|_{K}.

Since
\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\frac{A(A+1)\cdots(A+k)}{2^{k-k_{0}}(k+1)!}<\infty
,
it is enough to prove that there exists
\epsilon>0
such that the sum,

\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\left\|\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right\|_{X}=O(N^{-\epsilon})
as
N\rightarrow\infty
, for any compact subset
X
of
U_{r}
.
Since
X
is a compact subset of
U_{r}
, there exists
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\cap U_{r}

such that
X\subseteq D(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})
, where

D(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}):=\{(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}:\Re(s_{i})\geq\sigma_{i}\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq r\}.

Then,

\left\|\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right\|_{D(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})}\leq\frac{1}{n_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{\sigma_{r}}}

for any positive integers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
. Therefore,

\sum_{n_{1}\geq n_{2}}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}}\leq\frac{1}{n_{2}^{\sigma_{1}}}+\int_{n_{2}}^{\infty}x^{-\sigma_{1}}dx=\frac{1}{n_{2}^{\sigma_{1}}}+\frac{1}{1-\sigma_{1}}\frac{1}{n_{2}^{\sigma_{1}-1}}.

Using this inequality (and its avatars), we can write

\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{\sigma_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{\sigma_{r}}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{T_{i}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i})}{N^{\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{r}-i}},

where for each
1\leq i\leq r
,
T_{i}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i})
is a rational function whose denominator
is made of the factors of the form

\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{j}-k
for
1\leq k\leq j\leq i\leq r
and
T_{0}:=1.

Since

there exists

for all

5.2.
Proof of Proposition
1

We prove this by induction on
r
. First we assume that
z_{1}\neq 1
. Let
r\geq 1
and
n_{1}\geq 2
be integers. Then for any
s_{1}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

(20)

\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}-\frac{1}{(n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}-1}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k}}.

Now for
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
, if we multiply both sides of the above equation by

z_{1}^{n_{1}}\frac{z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}
and sum for
n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N
, we get

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}-\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{(n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}-1}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}\right)\\
=&\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k}(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.\end{split}

When
r=1
, then the left-hand side of the above equation is,

(22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{n_{1}\geq N}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}-\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{(n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}-1}}\right)&=\sum_{n_{1}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\sum_{n_{1}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}+\frac{1}{z_{1}}\frac{z_{1}^{N}}{N^{s_{1}-1}}\\
&=\frac{z_{1}^{N-1}}{N^{s_{1}-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\sum_{n_{1}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}\\
&=\frac{z_{1}^{N-1}}{N^{s_{1}-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1})}(s_{1}-1)_{\geq N}.\end{split}

Note that if
z_{1}=1
, this is nothing but
1/N^{s_{1}-1}
.
Since
s_{1}\in U_{1}
, using Lemma
5
, the right-hand side of (
21
), for
r=1
, becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\sum_{n_{1}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}+k}}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{1})}(s_{1}+k)_{\geq N}.

This completes the proof in the case
r=1
. Now assume that
r\geq 2
. Then as before,
the left-hand side of the equation (
21
) is

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\sum_{n_{1}\geq n_{2}}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}-\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{(n_{1}+1)^{s_{1}-1}}\right)\frac{z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\left(\frac{z_{1}^{n_{2}-1}}{n_{2}^{s_{1}-1}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\sum_{n_{1}\geq n_{2}}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}}\right)\frac{z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{1}}\sum_{n_{2}\geq\cdots\geq n_{2}\geq N}\frac{(z_{[1,2]})^{n_{2}}z_{3}^{n_{3}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{2}^{s_{1}+s_{2}-1}n_{3}^{s_{3}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}+\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}-1}n_{2}^{s_{2}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{z_{1}}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{2}\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}+\left(1-\frac{1}{z_{1}}\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}-1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}.

Note that if
z_{1}=1
, this is nothing but
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
.
Again, using Lemma

, the right-hand side of the equation (

\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
1
.

a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}=r-1>(r-1)-1
, we can apply Lemma
1
to get the desired conclusion in this case.
This completes the proof of Lemma
3
.

5.6.
Proof of Lemma
4

For every integer
N\geq 1
, let the Taylor series expansion of

u_{N}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
around
{\bf a}
be written as

\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\alpha_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})}(N)(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}.

Then for every
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
,
\alpha_{\bf k}(N)=P_{\bf k}(N,\log N)+o(1)
, as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Let the Taylor series expansion of the function
u(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
around
{\bf a}
be written as

\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\beta_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})}(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}.

Since
u_{N}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
converges uniformly to the holomorphic function
u(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
on
D
,
we get that
P_{\bf k}(N,\log N)+o(1)
converges to
\beta_{\bf k}
as
N\rightarrow\infty
.
Now given that
P_{\bf k}(N,\log N)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{\bf k}^{(i,j)}(N)N^{i}(\log N)^{j}
,
the sequence
P_{\bf k}(N,\log N)
is convergent if and only if we have
a_{\bf k}^{(i,j)}(N)=0
for all
pair
(i,j)\neq(0,0)
and
(a_{\bf k}^{(0,0)}(N))_{N\geq 1}
is an eventually constant sequence of

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
. Assume that for
N
large enough,
a_{\bf k}^{(0,0)}(N)=a_{\bf k}
.
Then
a_{\bf k}
is same as
\beta_{\bf k}
, which is exactly the
{\bf k}
-th
coefficient in the Taylor series expansion around
{\bf a}

of the function
u(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
. This completes the proof of Lemma
4
.

6.
Asymptotic expansions of sequences of germs of holomorphic
and meromorphic functions with variable coefficients

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the comparison scale,
on the set
{\mathbb{N}}
, filtered by the Fréchet filter and formed by the sequences

\left((\log n)^{l}n^{-m}\right)_{n\geq 1},

where
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Also, let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
let
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be such that
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are fixed roots of unity.
As before, let
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
be the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
generated by the constant sequence
(1)_{n\geq 1}
and the sequences of the form
(z_{i}^{n})_{n\geq 1}
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Note that as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-vector space,
{\mathcal{C}}_{\bf z}
is finite dimensional since the set

\mathcal{S}:=\{z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{k_{r}}:k_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ for }1\leq i\leq r\}

is finite (see
[
4
, §2]
). For every element
\xi\in\mathcal{S}
, there are infinitely many
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}

such that
\xi=z_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{k_{r}}
. By the exponent of
\xi
, we mean the smallest such
element in
{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, as per the dictionary ordering. Let
S
denote the set of exponents of elements of
\mathcal{S}
.
Then
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
has a basis of the form

(24)

\mathcal{B}_{\bf z}=\{(z_{1}^{k_{1}n}\cdots z_{r}^{k_{r}n})_{n\geq 1}:(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in S\}.

As
(1)_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{B}_{\bf z}
, we have
(0,\ldots,0)\in S
. It is immediate that
\mathcal{B}_{\bf z}

is a spanning set for
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
. Moreover,
as we are only taking
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in S
, we get the linear independence
using a Vandermonde determinant trick.

6.1.
Asymptotic expansions of sequences of germs of holomorphic functions with variable coefficients

Majority of the notations used here are taken from
[
6
, §7.1]
, where the notion of asymptotic expansions of sequences
of germs of holomorphic functions is explained. We extend that to the notion of asymptotic expansions of sequences
of germs of holomorphic functions with variable coefficients as follows:

For fixed
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
, let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}
denote the

{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra of germs of holomorphic functions at
\mathbf{a}
. For simplicity, we use the same
notation for the function and its germ at
{\bf a}
.
For
A\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, we say that a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of germs of holomorphic functions at
\mathbf{a}
has an

asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
,
if the following conditions holds:

(a)

Suppose that in a neighbourhood of
{\bf a}
, the Taylor series expansion of
f_{n}
is written as

f_{n}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}c_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})}(f_{n})(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}.

Then for each
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
, there exists a family of sequence

((u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(n))_{n\geq 1})_{l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}

such that the sequence
(c_{\mathbf{k}}(f_{n}))_{n\geq 1}
has the asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
relative to

\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
(in the sense of
[
4
, §2]
) of the form

c_{\mathbf{k}}(f_{n})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
m\leq A\end{subarray}}u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(n)(\log n)^{l}n^{-m}+O(n^{-A}),

as
n\to\infty
, where the sequence
(u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(n))_{n\geq 1}
is the zero sequence for all but finitely many pairs
(l,m)
;

(b)

there exists
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(n)=0
for all
n\geq 1
,
\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
,
l\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
m\leq m_{0}
;

(c)

for each
(l,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
, if
u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}
denotes the coordinate corresponding to the
constant sequence
(1)_{n\geq 1}
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}_{\bf z}
for the sequence

(u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(n))_{n\geq 1}
,
then the power series

(25)

\displaystyle g_{(l,m)}:=\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}u_{(\mathbf{k},l,m)}(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}},

converges in a neighbourhood of
\mathbf{a}
.

When these conditions hold, then the formal series

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\\
m\leq A\end{subarray}}g_{(l,m)}L^{l}X^{m},

is called the
formal asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
(at
{\bf a}
) relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
of the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

Example 3
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}
be such that there exists an
open neighbourhood
D
of
\mathbf{a}
such that
\|f_{n}\|_{D}=o(n^{-A})

for some integer
A
, as
n\to\infty
. Then
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
and its formal asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}

relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
is the Laurent polynomial
0
.

We say that a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of elements of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}
has a

complete asymptotic expansion
(at
{\bf a}
) relative to
\mathcal{E}
with
coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
if it has an asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}

relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
, for all
A\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
In this case, there exists a unique Laurent series

G=\sum_{(l,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{(l,m)}L^{l}X^{m},

in the indeterminate
X
with coefficients in the formal power series ring
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}[[L]]
such that the
Laurent polynomial obtained by truncating
G
to degree
\leq A
in
X
is the formal asymptotic expansion of

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
to precision
n^{-A}
relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
.
We call
G
to be the
formal complete asymptotic expansion
(at
{\bf a}
) of

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
).

Example 4
.

Let
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. For each integer
n\geq 1
, consider the germs of holomorphic functions
f_{n}
defined by

s\mapsto z^{n}n^{1-s}
, where
z
is a root of unity. So around
a
,
f_{n}
is defined by the power series,

f_{n}(s)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k}(\log n)^{k}z^{n}n^{1-a}}{k!}(s-a)^{k}.

When
z=1
, we get,
g_{(l,a-1)}=\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}(s-a)^{l}
and
g_{(l,m)}=0
for all
m\neq a-1
. But
when
z\neq 1
, we get
g_{(l,m)}=0
for all
l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, in this case, the formal
complete asymptotic expansion of
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is zero.

To conclude this segment we need to also define the notion of asymptotic expansions of sequences
of germs of meromorphic functions with variable coefficients. For that we need the following lemma,
which is an extension of
[
6
, lemma 7]
. Since the proof follows analogously, we omit the proof.

Lemma 6
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}
and
f
be a
non-zero element in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{a}}
. If the sequence
(ff_{n})_{n\geq 1}
has an
asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
, for a given integer
A
(resp. a complete asymptotic expansion)
relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
, then the same
holds for the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

6.1.1.
Asymptotic expansions of sequences of germs of meromorphic functions with variable coefficients

denote the

\mathbf{a}

(resp. a

)

if there exists a common denominator
f
of

(i.e., a non zero element

.

.
In this case, the formal complete asymptotic expansion of the sequence

(26)

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f^{-1}g_{(l,m)}L^{l}X^{m},

in the indeterminate

\sum_{l\in{\mathbb{N}},m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{(l,m)}L^{l}X^{m},

(at
{\bf a}
) relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
. Clearly,
restricting

in (

), we get the formal asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}

of the sequence
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

.

6.2.
Asymptotic expansion of
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}

Let
z
be a root of unity.
The formal asymptotic expansion of the sequence (of germs) of functions
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

relative to
\mathcal{E}
with variable coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{(z)}
, depends on
z
.
If
z=1
, then
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}=\zeta^{\star}(s)_{\geq N}
.
Hence, in this case the formal complete asymptotic expansion (at an integer
a
) is given in
[
6
, Remark 17]
,
namely, it is the formal Laurent series

\sum_{k\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq 0}h^{\star}_{(l,k)}L^{l}X^{a+k-1},

where
h^{\star}_{(l,k)}(s)=\frac{(-1)^{l}B_{k}^{\star}}{l!k!}(s)_{k-1}(s-a)^{l}
and

B_{k}^{\star}
denotes the star Bernoulli numbers, defined by

\frac{x}{e^{x}-1}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k}B_{k}^{\star}}{k!}x^{k}.

So, we are now interested in the case when
z\neq 1
and we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 2
.

Let
a
be an integer and
z\neq 1
be a root of unity. Then the sequence (of germs at
a
) of the meromorphic functions

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
has a complete asymptotic expansion relative to

\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{(z)}
. Moreover, the associated formal complete asymptotic
expansion is equal to zero.

Proof.

Let
A
be a positive integer. We prove this by induction on the smallest non-negative integer
k_{0}
such that
a+k_{0}>A+1
.
Let
D
be a disc around
a
with small radius
\rho<1
. So if
k_{0}=0
, we have
a\geq A+2
and
hence
\|\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}\|_{D}=o(N^{-A})
, as
N\to\infty
and we are done by Example
3
.
Now from the translation formula (
17
), we can write

\frac{z^{N-1}}{N^{s}}=\left({\bar{z}}-1\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}+\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+k+1)_{\geq N}.

For
k_{0}\geq 1
, following the proof of Lemma
5
,
D
can be so chosen that as
N\to\infty
, we have

\left\|\sum_{k\geq k_{0}-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+k+1)_{\geq N}\right\|_{D}=o(N^{-A}).

Hence, by Example
3
, we get that the sequence (of germs) of holomorphic functions at
a

\left(\left({\bar{z}}-1\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}+\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-2}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+k+1)_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
, with the same formal asymptotic expansion
to precision
N^{-A}
as that of the sequence (of germs) of
(z^{N-1}N^{-s})_{N\geq 2}
,
which is given in Example
4
.
By the induction hypothesis,
for any
0\leq k\leq k_{0}-2
, the sequence (of germs) of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
at
a+k+1

has asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
. Hence for each
0\leq k\leq k_{0}-2
, the sequence of germs

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+k+1)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
at
a
has asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}

and therefore the sequence (of germs) of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
at
a
has an asymptotic
expansion to precision
N^{-A}
.

To write down the corresponding formal asymptotic expansion, we again need an inversion process.
From the above arguments we have that for each
0\leq j\leq k_{0}-1
,
the sequence (of germs) of meromorphic functions
(z^{N-1}N^{-s-j})_{N\geq 2}
at
a
has the same formal
asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
as that of the sequence (of germs) of meromorphic functions

\left(\left({\bar{z}}-1\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+j)_{\geq N}+\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-j-2}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s+j)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+j+k+1)_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

at
a
. Writing this in matrix from, we have

(27)

\mathbf{W=AU},

where
\bf{U}
and
\bf{W}
are column vectors, whose entries are the formal asymptotic expansion
at
a
to precision
N^{-A}
of the column vectors

(28)

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N}\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+1)_{\geq N}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s+k_{0}-1)_{\geq N}\end{bmatrix}\text{and}\begin{bmatrix}z^{N-1}N^{-s}\\
z^{N-1}N^{-s-1}\\
\vdots\\
z^{N-1}N^{-s-k_{0}+1}\end{bmatrix},

respectively, and
\mathbf{A}
is the square matrix whose entries are the germs at
a
of
the rational functions of
s
as per the matrix given below:

\mathbf{A}(s)=\begin{bmatrix}{\bar{z}}-1&\frac{(s)_{1}}{1!}&\frac{(-1)^{1}(s)_{2}}{2!}&\cdots&\frac{(-1)^{k_{0}-2}(s)_{k_{0}-1}}{(k_{0}-1)!}\\
0&{\bar{z}}-1&\frac{(s+1)_{1}}{1!}&\cdots&\frac{(-1)^{k_{0}-3}(s+1)_{k_{0}-2}}{(k_{0}-2)!}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&{\bar{z}}-1\end{bmatrix}.

Note that as
z\neq 1
, the matrix
\mathbf{A}(s)
is invertible in
\mathbf{T}({\mathbb{C}}(s))
, the ring of upper
triangular matrices of order
k_{0}\times k_{0}
with elements in the field of rational functions
{\mathbb{C}}(s)
.
To find the inverse, we use the idea from
[
5
, §5.2]
with suitable changes. Note that

\mathbf{A}(s)={\bar{z}}\mathbf{I}-\exp(-\mathbf{M}(s)),

where
\mathbf{I}
denotes the identity matrix of order
k_{0}\times k_{0}
and
\mathbf{M}(s)
is the
nilpotent matrix over
{\mathbb{C}}(s)
defined by

\mathbf{M}(s)=\begin{bmatrix}0&s&0&\cdots&0\\
0&0&s+1&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&0\end{bmatrix}.

Now we need to invert the power series
c-e^{-x}
in
{\mathbb{C}}{[[x]]}
for some
c\neq 1
. For this,
we recall the definition of Eulerian polynomials. The Eulerian polynomials
A_{n}(t)
for
t\neq 1
,
are defined by the following generating series:

\frac{1-t}{e^{(t-1)y}-t}=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}(t)\frac{y^{n}}{n!}.

Hence

\frac{1}{c-e^{-x}}=\frac{1}{c-1}\sum_{n\geq 0}(-1)^{n}\frac{A_{n}(c)}{(c-1)^{n}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

It can be checked that
A_{0}(t)=1
. Now define
A^{\star}_{n}(c):=(-1)^{n}A_{n}(c)
for all complex
numbers
c\neq 1
and integers
n\geq 1
. Then the inverse of
{\bf A}(s)
in
\mathbf{T}({\mathbb{C}}(s))
is given by

\displaystyle{\bf A}(s)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{{\bar{z}}\mathbf{I}-\exp(-\mathbf{M}(s))}=\left({\bar{z}}-1\right)^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{k_{0}-1}\frac{A^{\star}_{k}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)^{n}}\frac{(\mathbf{M}(s))^{n}}{n!},

i.e.,

(29)

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(s)^{-1}=\left({\bar{z}}-1\right)^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{(s)_{1}A_{1}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)1!}&\frac{(s)_{2}A_{2}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)^{2}2!}&\cdots&\frac{(s)_{k_{0}-1}A_{k_{0}-1}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)^{k_{0}-1}(k_{0}-1)!}\\
0&1&\frac{(s+1)_{1}A_{1}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)1!}&\cdots&\frac{(s+1)_{k_{0}-2}A_{k_{0}-2}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)^{k_{0}-2}(k_{0}-2)!}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&1\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
{\bf A}
is invertible and from (
27
) we have

\mathbf{U}=\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{W}.

By comparing the first entries on both the sides, we get that the sequence
(of germs at
a
) of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
has the same formal asymptotic
expansion to precision
N^{-A}
as that of

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\frac{(s)_{k}A_{k}^{\star}({\bar{z}})}{({\bar{z}}-1)^{k+1}k!}z^{N-1}N^{-s-k}\right)_{N\geq 2}.

Since
z\neq 1
, therefore by Example
4
, this formal asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}

(relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{(z)}
)
is zero. This implies that the formal asymptotic
expansion to precision
n^{-A}
(of germs at
a
) of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z)}(s)_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

(relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{(z)}
)
is also equal to zero. Since the positive integer
A
is arbitrary, this completes the proof.
∎

6.3.
Asymptotic expansion of
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer. Recall that for an integer
N\geq 2
and
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in U_{r}
,

\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}
is defined by the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1}\geq\cdots\geq n_{r}\geq N}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{s_{r}}}.

It is a holomorphic function on
U_{r}
and can be extended to a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
We need to define some notations to state our next proposition. For each
1\leq i\leq r
,
define
q_{[1,i]}:=
1 if
z_{[1,i]}=1
, else put
q_{[1,i]}=0
. Consider the integer
Q_{[1,i]}

which counts the number of indices
1\leq j\leq i
such that
z_{[1,j]}=1
, i.e.,

Q_{[1,i]}:=q_{[1,1]}+q_{[1,2]}+\cdots+q_{[1,i]}.

Let the sets
I_{i}
and
I^{\prime}_{i}
be defined as follows:

I_{i}=\{i:1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }z_{[1,j]}=1\}\\
\text{ and }\\
I^{\prime}_{i}=\{j:1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }z_{[1,j]}\neq 1\}.

Clearly
Q_{[1,i]}=|I_{i}|
and
I_{i}\cup I^{\prime}_{i}=\{1,\ldots,i\}.
Also, for
\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
,
|\mathbf{s}|

denotes the sum of its coordinates, i.e.,
|\mathbf{s}|=s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}
. Then, we prove the following proposition which
generalises Proposition
2
and
[
6
, Remark 17]
for
r\geq 2
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
. The sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of the
meromorphic functions
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
has a complete
asymptotic expansion relative to
\mathcal{E}
with coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
. Moreover, the associated formal
complete asymptotic expansion is the formal Laurent series

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}}\sum_{l\geq 0}h^{\star}_{(l,\mathbf{k})}L^{l}X^{|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{k}|-Q_{[1,r]}},

where for
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}
,
h^{\star}_{(l,\mathbf{k})}
is the germ at
\mathbf{a}
of the
holomorphic function defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mapsto

\displaystyle\ \frac{q_{[1,r]}(-1)^{l}}{l!k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\prod_{j\in I^{\prime}_{r}}\frac{{\overline{z_{[1,j]}}}A^{\star}_{k_{j}}({\overline{z_{[1,j]}}})}{({\overline{z_{[1,j]}}}-1)^{k_{j}+1}}\prod_{j\in I_{r}}B^{\star}_{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times(s_{1})_{k_{1}-q_{[1,1]}}(s_{1}+s_{2}+k_{1}-Q_{[1,1]})_{k_{2}-q_{[1,2]}}\cdots

\displaystyle\times(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}-Q_{[1,r-1]})_{k_{r}-q_{[1,r]}}(|\mathbf{s}|-|\mathbf{a}|)^{l}.

Proof.

To prove the proposition, we will use induction on
r
. Let
r=1
. If
z=1
,
then we have the desired result by
[
6
, Remark 17]
. If
z\neq 1

then,
q_{[1,1]}=0
and hence by Proposition
2
,
h^{\star}_{(l,k)}=0
for all
(l,k)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{N}}
.
Therefore, we have the desired result.

Next assume that
r\geq 2
. Let
A
be a positive integer and
k_{0}
be the smallest non-negative integer such that

(a_{1}+k_{0}-A,a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})\in U_{r}
. Let
D
be a polydisc around
{\bf a}
with small polyradius
(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r})

such that
\rho_{1}+\cdots+\rho_{r}<1
. If
k_{0}=0
, then
(a_{1}-A,a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})\in U_{r}
. Therefore,

\lVert\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\rVert_{D}=o(N^{-A})
as

N\to\infty
. Hence by Example
3
, the sequence (of germs at
{\bf a}
) of the holomorphic functions

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}

(relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
).

Now assume that
k_{0}\geq 1
. We will use induction on
k_{0}
. First, consider the case when
z_{1}=1
. Then from (
18
) we have,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}=\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}.

Applying Lemma
5
,
D
can be so chosen that as
N\to\infty
, we have

\sum_{k\geq k_{0}}\left\lVert(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right\rVert_{D}=o(N^{-A}).

Therefore, by the induction hypothesis on the depth and Remark
5
, the sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
)
of the meromorphic functions
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3}\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
has an
asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
). Hence, this
together with Example
3
yield that the sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of the meromorphic functions

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
).
Note that for
1\leq k\leq k_{0}-1
, the sequence (of germs at

(a_{1}+k,a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})
) of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
, by the induction hypothesis on
k<k_{0}
. Thus for

1\leq k\leq k_{0}-1
, the sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of meromorphic functions

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
has an asymptotic
expansion to precision
N^{-A}
. This implies that the sequence (of germs at

\mathbf{a}
) of the meromorphic functions
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
).

Now to write down the corresponding formal asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
,
we note that for
0\leq j\leq k_{0}-1
, the sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of the meromorphic functions

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-j-1}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1}+j-1)_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+j+k,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}
,
and
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}+j-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

have the same formal asymptotic expansion to precision
N^{-A}
, by the above arguments.
This can be written as the following matrix identity:

(30)

\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{BX},

where
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}
are column vectors whose entries are the formal asymptotic expansion at

\mathbf{a}
to precision
N^{-A}
of the column vectors

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+1,\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k_{0}-1,\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\end{bmatrix}\ \text{and}\ \begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}+k_{0}-2,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\end{bmatrix},

respectively, and
\mathbf{B}
is the square matrix, whose entries are the germs at
\mathbf{a}
of the rational
functions of
s_{1}
as per the matrix given below:

{\bf B}(s_{1})=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{(s_{1}-1)_{1}}{1!}&\frac{(-1)(s_{1}-1)_{2}}{2!}&\frac{(-1)^{2}(s_{1}-1)_{3}}{3!}&\cdots&\frac{(-1)^{k_{0}-1}(s_{1}-1)_{k_{0}}}{k_{0}!}\\
0&\frac{(s_{1})_{1}}{1!}&\frac{(-1)(s_{1})_{2}}{2!}&\cdots&\frac{(-1)^{k_{0}-2}(s_{1})_{k_{0}-1}}{(k_{0}-1)!}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\frac{(s_{1}+k_{0}-2)_{1}}{1!}\end{bmatrix}.

The matrix
\mathbf{B}(s_{1})
is invertible in the ring of upper triangular matrices with entries in the field of rational functions

{\mathbb{Q}}(s_{1})
, denoted by
\mathbf{T}({\mathbb{Q}}(s_{1}))
and is given by the following square matrix (see
[
6
, Remark 17]
):

{\bf B}(s_{1})^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{1}{s_{1}-1}&\frac{B_{1}^{\star}}{1!}&\frac{(s_{1})_{1}B_{2}^{\star}}{2!}&\cdots&\frac{(s_{1})_{k_{0}-2}B_{k_{0}-1}^{\star}}{(k_{0}-1)!}\\
0&\frac{1}{s_{1}}&\frac{B_{1}^{\star}}{1!}&\cdots&\frac{(s_{1}+1)_{k_{0}-3}B_{k_{0}-2}^{\star}}{(k_{0}-2)!}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\cdots&\frac{1}{s_{1}+k_{0}-2}\end{bmatrix},

where for integers
n\geq 1
,
B_{n}^{\star}
denote the star Bernoulli numbers, as before.
Hence the matrix
{\bf B}
is invertible and we can rewrite equation (
30
) as

\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{B}^{-1}{\bf Y}.

Comparing the first entries on both the sides, we get that the formal asymptotic expansion (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}

to precision
N^{-A}
is same as that of

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\frac{B_{k}^{\star}}{k!}(s_{1})_{k-1}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}+k-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right).

Now by the induction hypothesis on depth
r
, we get that for
k\geq 0
, the formal complete asymptotic expansion of the
sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
) of

(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}+k-1,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
is

\sum_{k_{2},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq 0}h^{\star}_{(l,k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}L^{l}X^{|\mathbf{a}|+k+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r}-1-(q_{[1,2]}+\cdots+q_{[1,r]})},

where
h^{\star}_{(l,k,k_{2},\ldots,k_{r})}
is the germs at
\mathbf{a}
of the function

\displaystyle\mathbf{s}\mapsto

\displaystyle\ \frac{q_{[1,r]}(-1)^{l}}{l!k_{2}!\cdots k_{r}!}\prod_{j\in J{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{{\overline{z_{[1,j]}}}A^{\star}_{k_{j}}({\overline{z_{[1,j]}}})}{({\overline{z_{[1,j]}}}-1)^{k_{j}+1}}\prod_{j\in J}B^{\star}_{k_{j}}

\displaystyle\times(s_{1}+s_{2}+k-1)_{k_{2}-q_{[1,2]}}(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+k+k_{2}-1-q_{[1,2]})_{k_{3}-q_{[1,3]}}\cdots

\displaystyle\times(s_{1}+\cdots+s_{r}+k+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r-1}-1-q_{[1,2]}-\cdots-q_{[1,r-1]})_{k_{r}-q_{[1,r]}}(|\mathbf{s}|-|\mathbf{a}|)^{l},

with
J
being the set of integers
2\leq j\leq r
such that
z_{[1,j]}=1

and
J^{\prime}
being the set of integers
2\leq j\leq r
such that
z_{[1,j]}\neq 1
.
Note that
z_{1}=1
, implies that
q_{[1,1]}=1
. Hence
I_{r}=J\cup\{1\}
and
I^{\prime}_{r}=J^{\prime}
. Hence from the
above argument we get that the formal complete asymptotic expansion of the sequence (of germs at
\mathbf{a}
)
of the meromorphic function
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
is given by

\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{r}}\sum_{l\geq 0}h^{\star}_{(l,\mathbf{k})}L^{l}X^{|\mathbf{a}|+|\mathbf{k}|-Q_{[1,r]}},

as
Q_{[1,r]}=1+q_{[1,2]}+\cdots+q_{[1,r]}=q_{[1,1]}+\cdots+q_{[1,r]}
. So, we have desired result when
z_{1}=1
.

Now suppose
z_{1}\neq 1
. In this case, as before we write (
18
) as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&{\overline{z_{1}}}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\\
&=\left({\overline{z_{1}}}-1\right)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}+\sum_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\frac{(s_{1})_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k+1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}.\end{split}

Again similar to the previous case, using the Lemma
5
, induction on depth
r
, Remark
5

and then induction on
k_{0}
, which is the smallest non-negative integer such that
(a_{1}+k_{0}-A,a_{2},\ldots,a_{r})\in U_{r}
,
we get that the sequence (of germs at
{\bf a}
) of the meromorphic functions

\left(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

has an asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficients in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
). Moreover, the sequence (of germs at
{\bf a}
) of

\left({\overline{z_{1}}}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2},s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

and

\left(({\overline{z_{1}}}-1)\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}+\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-2}\frac{(-1)^{k}(s_{1})_{k+1}}{(k+1)!}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1}+k+1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 2}

have the same formal asymptotic expansion to precision
n^{-A}
(relative to
\mathcal{E}

and coefficients in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
).
Now we write down this expansion. As before, the above statement is true if we replace

(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})
by
(s_{1}+j,s_{2},\ldots,s_{r})
and
k_{0}
by
k_{0}-j
for each
0\leq j\leq k_{0}-1
.
Again, we write this as the following matrix identity:

(31)

{\overline{z_{1}}}\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{AX},

where
\mathbf{X}
is as before,
{\bf Z}
is obtained from
\mathbf{Y}
by replacing
s_{1}
with
s_{1}+1
,
and
\mathbf{A}
is the square matrix as in (
27
) (with
s,z
replaced by

s_{1}
and
z_{1}
, respectively). Therefore, we can write

\mathbf{X}={\overline{z_{1}}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}{\bf Z}.

Now comparing the first entries on both the sides, we get that the formal asymptotic expansion (of germs at
{\bf a}
)
of
(\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N})_{N\geq 2}
to precision
N^{-A}
is the same as that of

\left(\sum_{k=0}^{k_{0}-1}\frac{\overline{z_{1}}A_{k}^{\star}({\overline{z_{1}}})}{({\overline{z_{1}}}-1)^{k+1}k!}(s_{1})_{k}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{[1,2]},z_{3},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{1}+s_{2}+k,s_{3},\ldots,s_{r})_{\geq N}\right)_{N\geq 1}.

Note that
q_{[1,1]}=0
in this case. Hence the proof follows from the induction hypothesis on depth,
as in the case of
z_{1}=1
.
∎

7.
Behaviour of the multiple polylogarithms at general integer points

As before, let
r\geq 1
be an integer and
let
{\bf z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be such that
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
are fixed roots of unity.
In §
2
, we have studied the local behaviour of
the multiple polylogarithms at integer points of
\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
. In this section, our aim is to study the
local behaviour of the multiple
polylogarithms at general integer points of
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
.
To state our theorem we first need some notations.

For each
1\leq j\leq i\leq r
, define
q_{[j,i]}=
1 if
z_{[j,i]}=1
, otherwise put
q_{[j,i]}=0
.
Consider the integer
Q_{[j,i]}
which counts the integers
k
such
j\leq k\leq i
and
z_{[k,i]}=1
, i.e.,

Q_{[j,i]}:=q_{[j,i]}+q_{[j+1,i]}+\cdots+q_{[i,i]}.

Let
J_{i}
be the set of integers
1\leq j\leq i
(as in §
1
) such that
z_{[j,i]}=1
and
J^{\prime}_{i}

be the set of integers
1\leq j\leq i
such that
z_{[j,i]}\neq 1
, i.e.,

J_{i}=\{j:1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }z_{[j,i]}=1\}\\
\text{ and }\\
J^{\prime}_{i}=\{j:1\leq j\leq i\text{ and }z_{[j,i]}\neq 1\}.

These notations are set in the reverse ordering when compared to the notations in §
6.3
, as we need
to use the combinatorial identity (
19
) where the indices in the tail of the multiple
polylogarithm-star function are in the reverse order.
In this context, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Given
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
, the formal power series

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})(s_{1}-a_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots(s_{r}-a_{r})^{k_{r}}

converges in a neighbourhood of
{\bf a}
and extends to a meromorphic
function on
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. If we denote it by
\mathrm{Li}^{{\rm Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
,
then we have the following equality between meromorphic functions

(32)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{{\rm Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}),

where
C_{0}(\varnothing):=1
and for
1\leq i\leq r
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}):=q_{[1,i]}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}\geq 0\\
\sum_{1\leq t\leq i}(k_{t}+a_{t})=Q_{[1,i]}\end{subarray}}&\frac{1}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{i}!}\prod_{j\in J_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{\overline{z_{[j,i]}}A_{k_{j}}^{\star}(\overline{z_{[j,i]}})}{(\overline{z_{[j,i]}}-1)^{k_{j}+1}}\prod_{j\in J_{i}}B_{k_{j}}^{\star}\\
&\times(s_{i})_{k_{i}-q_{[i,i]}}(s_{i}+s_{i-1}+k_{i}-Q_{[i,i]})_{k_{i-1}-q_{[i-1,i]}}\cdots\\
&\times(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{1}+k_{i}+\cdots+k_{2}-Q_{[2,i]})_{k_{1}-q_{[1,i]}}.\end{split}

As a key corollary, we first prove the holomorphicity of the multiple polylogarithm function at integer
points of
V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
.

Corollary 1
.

Given
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
, the function
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
is
holomorphic at
\mathbf{a}
and its value at
\mathbf{a}
is equal to the regularised value

\ell_{[0,\ldots,0]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
of the series (
1
) at
\mathbf{a}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
. Since
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
, then for
every
1\leq i\leq r
, we have
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}>Q_{i}({\bf z})=Q_{[1,i]}
. Hence, for every
1\leq i\leq r
,
there are no non-negative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}
such that
\sum_{1\leq t\leq i}(k_{t}+a_{t})=Q_{[1,i]}
, i.e.,
for every
1\leq i\leq r
,
C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})=0
.
So by (
32
), around the point
\mathbf{a}
, we have

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r}).

Hence

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}({\bf a})=\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}({\bf a})=\ell_{[0,\ldots,0]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
.
∎

We recall that in
[
4
, Theorem 3]
, we proved that for

\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap V_{r}(\mathbf{z})
, the series (
1
), treated as (
2
), converges
at
{\bf a}
to
\ell_{[0,\ldots,0]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
.
But such phenomenon can occur at certain points outside
V_{r}({\bf z})
as well, even if the limit
in (
2
) does not exist. In view of
[
5
, Theorem 9]
, if
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be
such
z_{i}
’s are roots of unity and
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq r
, then
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})

is holomorphic everywhere in
{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
. Moreover, we have the following corollary which is immediate from
(
32
).

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
be such that
z_{i}
’s are roots of unity and
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1

for all
1\leq i\leq r
. Then, for any
\mathbf{a}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
, the value of the holomorphic function

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
at
\mathbf{a}
is equal to the regularised value

\ell_{[0,\ldots,0]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z})
of the series (
1
) at
\mathbf{a}
.

Remark 6
.

We also note that we can recover (
14
) from (
32
).
Let
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\cap\overline{U_{r}(\mathbf{z})}
. If
1\leq i\leq r
is such that

i\notin\mathcal{I}({\bf z},{\bf a})
, then
i
does not satisfy one of the defining conditions of

\mathcal{I}({\bf z},{\bf a})
. If
z_{[1,i]}\neq 1
, then
q_{[1,i]}=0
. Next if there exist

1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq i
such that
z_{[i_{1},i]},z_{[i_{2},i]}\neq 1
, then

Q_{[1,i]}=q_{[1,i]}+\cdots+q_{[i,i]}\leq i-2
. But since
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}\geq i-1
,
there are no non-negative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}
such that
\sum_{1\leq t\leq i}(k_{t}+a_{t})=Q_{[1,i]}
.
Next let
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}>i-a({\bf z})_{i}
. Note that
a({\bf z})_{i}=0
if
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{i}=1
, else

a({\bf z})_{i}=1
. Also,
Q_{[1,i]}=i
if
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{i}=1
, else
Q_{[1,i]}\leq i-1
.
In any case,
Q_{[1,i]}<a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}
and hence
there are no non-negative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}
such that
\sum_{1\leq t\leq i}(k_{t}+a_{t})=Q_{[1,i]}
.
So if
i\notin\mathcal{I}({\bf z},{\bf a})
, then
C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})=0
.

Next let
i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z},{\bf a})
and
i\geq 1
. Note that similar computations as above
shows that the admissible choice of
k_{1},\ldots,k_{i}
in this case is
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{i}=0
.
Now if
t_{i}=0
, i.e.,
z_{1}=\cdots=z_{i}=1
,
we have
J_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=\varnothing
and
Q_{[j,i]}=i-j+1
for
1\leq j\leq i
. Hence

(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-Q_{[j+1,i]})_{-q_{[j,i]}}=\frac{1}{s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-(i-j+1)}.

So we are done in this case. Next let
t_{i}\geq 1
. In this case, we note that

J_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}=\{t_{i}\}
,
q_{[t_{i},i]}=0
and
q_{[j,i]}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq i
such that
j\neq t_{i}
.
Thus,
Q_{[j,i]}=i-j+1
for
t_{i}<j\leq i
and
Q_{[j,i]}=i-j
for
1\leq j\leq t_{i}
.
Now, as before, we note that for
t_{i}<j\leq i
,

(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-Q_{[j+1,i]})_{-q_{[j,i]}}=\frac{1}{s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-(i-j+1)},

for
1\leq j<t_{i}
,

(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-Q_{[j+1,i]})_{-q_{[j,i]}}=\frac{1}{s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-(i-j)},

and for
j=t_{i}
,

(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{j}-Q_{[j+1,i]})_{-q_{[j,i]}}=1.

Therefore, we see that (
14
) can be recovered from (
32
).

Remark 7
.

Let
\mathcal{I}({\bf z})
denote the set
\{0\}\cup\{i:1\leq i\leq r\text{ and }q_{[1,i]}=1\}
.
We can write (
32
) for all the points of the form
(a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})
for
i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z})
.
As in §
2
, all these equations can be concisely written
in the following matrix form:

{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}={\bf N}{\bf V},

where
{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}
and
{\bf V}
are column vectors indexed by the elements of

\mathcal{I}({\bf z})
, such that for each
i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z})
,
the corresponding entries of
{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}
and
{\bf V}
are

\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r};a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})

and
\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})
, respectively,
and
{\bf N}
is an upper triangular matrix with entries in the field of rational functions

{\mathbb{C}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
, and the diagonal entries as
1
. Hence,
{\bf N}
is an invertible matrix with entries in

{\mathbb{C}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
and we can write

{\bf V}={\bf N}^{-1}{\bf V}^{\text{Reg}}.

Now the first entry of the above matrix equation gives an expression of the form

\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})=\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z})}D_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r};a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r}),

where
D_{0}(\varnothing):=1
and for
i\in\mathcal{I}({\bf z})
with
i\geq 1
,
D_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})

is a rational function in
s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}
. This equation, therefore, gives
the desired Laurent type expansion of
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
around
{\bf a}
.

Example 5
.

Let
{\bf z}=(-1,1,-1,1)
and
{\bf a}=(0,1,0,1)
. Then
\mathcal{I}({\bf z})=\{0,3,4\}
. From
(
32
), we have

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{{\rm Reg}}_{(\mathbf{z};\mathbf{a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(1;1)}(s_{4})\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&-\frac{1}{4(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)}&\frac{1}{4(s_{4}-1)(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}-2)}\\
0&1&-\frac{1}{s_{4}-1}\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\\
\mathrm{Li}_{(1)}(s_{4})\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore,

\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\\
\mathrm{Li}_{(1)}(s_{4})\\
1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&\frac{1}{4(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)}&\frac{1}{4(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}-2)}\\
0&1&\frac{1}{s_{4}-1}\\
0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{({\bf z};{\bf a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})\\
\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(1;1)}(s_{4})\\
1\end{bmatrix},

and hence we get the following Laurent type expansion around the point
{\bf a}=(0,1,0,1)
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{({\bf z};{\bf a})}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4})+\frac{\mathrm{Li}^{\text{Reg}}_{(1;1)}(s_{4})}{4(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}-1)(s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+s_{4}-2)}.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

We first recall the combinatorial identity (
19
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Li}^{\star}_{(z_{i},\ldots,z_{1})}(s_{i},\ldots,s_{1})_{\geq N}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r}).

Note that
(\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N})_{N\geq 2}
is a sequence of holomorphic functions around

{\bf a}
and its sequence of the
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
-th Taylor coefficients at
{\bf a}
is given by

(33)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\sum_{N>n_{1}>\cdots>n_{r}>0}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}(\log n_{1})^{k_{1}}\cdots z_{r}^{n_{r}}(\log n_{r})^{k_{r}}}{n_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{a_{r}}}\right)_{N\geq 2}.

Now using Proposition
3
, Remark
5
and combinatorial identity (
19
), we get that the sequence (of
germs at
{\bf a}
) of holomorphic functions (
\mathrm{Li}_{\bf z}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})_{<N})_{N\geq 2}
has a complete
asymptotic expansion (relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficient in
\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
). Therefore, if the
associated formal complete asymptotic expansion is given by
G=\sum_{(l,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}}g_{(l,m)}L^{l}X^{m}
, then by
definition for each
(l,m)\in{\mathbb{N}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}
,
g_{(l,m)}
is the germ of a holomorphic function at
{\bf a}
. This implies that the
sequence in (
33
) has an asymptotic expansion to the arbitrary precision (relative to
\mathcal{E}
and coefficient in

\mathcal{C}_{\bf z}
). In particular, by definition and the above argument, we get, for each
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})
,

c_{\mathbf{k}}(g_{(0,0)})=\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}}}{k_{1}!\cdots k_{r}!}\ell_{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}]}^{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})}(\mathbf{z}).

Since
g_{(0,0)}
is a germ of holomorphic function at
{\bf a}
, therefore the power series (
9
)
converges in a neighbourhood of
{\bf a}
.

Now to get the required equality between the meromorphic functions, we apply Proposition
3

and Remark
5
in (
19
), and deduce that the formal Laurent series
G
is

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\sum_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 0}\sum_{l\geq 0}h_{(i,l,{\bf k})}(\mathbf{z})L^{l}X^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{i}+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{i}-Q_{[1,i]}},

where for each
0\leq i\leq r
,
h_{(i,l,{\bf k})}(\mathbf{z})
is the germ of the function at
\mathbf{a}
which maps
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})
to

\displaystyle\ \frac{q_{[1,i]}(-1)^{i+l}}{l!k_{1}!\cdots k_{i}!}\left(\prod_{j\in J_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}}\frac{\overline{z_{[j,i]}}A_{k_{j}}^{\star}(\overline{z_{[j,i]}})}{(\overline{z_{[j,i]}}-1)^{k_{j}+1}}\prod_{j\in J_{i}}B_{k_{j}}^{\star}\right)\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})(s_{i})_{k_{i}-q_{[i,i]}}

\displaystyle\times(s_{i}+s_{i-1}+k_{i}-Q_{[i,i]})_{k_{i-1}-q_{[i-1,i]}}\cdots(s_{i}+\cdots+s_{1}+k_{i}+\cdots+k_{2}-Q_{[2,i]})_{k_{1}-q_{[1,i]}}(|{\bf s}|-|{\bf a}|)^{l}.

Since the power series (
9
) is same as
g_{(0,0)}
, taking
l=0
and

\sum_{1\leq t\leq i}(k_{t}+a_{t})=Q_{[1,i]}
for each
1\leq i\leq r
, we get that
g_{(0,0)}
is the germ of function for which

\displaystyle(s_{1},\ldots,s_{r})\mapsto

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}\mathrm{Li}_{(z_{i+1},\ldots,z_{r})}(s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{r})C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}),

where
C_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{i})
’s are the desired rational functions. This completes the proof of the identity
(
32
) and the meromorphic extension of the power series (
9
).

Restricted graph Lie algebras in even characteristic
S. Blumer

Abstract
. We investigate restricted Lie algebras arising as analogues of (twisted) right-angled Artin groups and right-angled Coxeter groups over fields of characteristic two. These algebras are defined via quadratic relations determined by decorated graphs. We compute their cohomology rings with trivial coefficients and uncover phenomena specific to characteristic two: unlike in zero/odd characteristics, where quadratically defined ordinary and restricted Lie algebras have equivalent cohomology theories, the characteristic 2 2 case exhibits dependence on the base field. In particular, we prove that the ground field being the prime field 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}_{2} characterizes when a Lie-theoretic analogue of the twisted Droms theorem [ 1 ] holds. Generalizations of graph Lie algebras are also discussed.



1.
Introduction

Restricted Lie algebras, introduced by Jacobson
[
9
]
, provide a natural framework for studying Lie-theoretic structures in positive characteristic. Their additional
p
-power operation
x\mapsto x^{[p]}
mimics the action of the Frobenius map on associative algebras of characteristic
p
, and endows them with richer algebraic properties than ordinary Lie algebras.
Restricted Lie algebras naturally arise as the graded objects associated to distinguished filtrations of pro-
p
groups, and they also play a crucial role in the study of algebraic groups.

On the other hand, quadratic algebras, namely those defined by relations that are quadratic in the generators, form an important class of algebras which serve as a natural first approximation to the study of more general graded algebras. Among these, Koszul (restricted) Lie algebras are determined by their cohomology ring, and have been studied by Weigel
[
20
]
(see also
[
4
]
). The primary motivation for this paper stems from current research in Galois theory. Specifically, it follows from the Norm residue isomorphism theorem by Rost and Voevodski (formerly known as the Bloch-Kato conjecture; see Weibel’s survey
[
19
]
) that the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{F}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
of maximal pro-
p
quotients of absolute Galois groups
G_{\mathbb{F}}
of certain fields is a quadratic algebra. To improve this, Positselski
[
17
]
and Weigel
[
21
]
have conjectured that the restricted Lie algebras associated to the Jennings-Zassenhaus filtration of such groups are Koszul, and quadratic dual to
H^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{F}}(p),\mathbb{F}_{p})
.

If
k
is a field of positive characteristic
p
, and
\mathscr{L}
is an ordinary Lie algebra with a given presentation over
k
, then there is an associated restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
defined by the same presentation, or, equivalently, by taking the primitive elements of the universal enveloping algebra
U(\mathscr{L})
(cf.
[
13
]
). The assignment
\mathscr{L}\mapsto\mathfrak{g}
gives rise to the restrictification functor, which, provided that
p
is odd, defines an isomorphism between the category of Lie algebras whose relations are quadratic in the generators, and restricted Lie algebras with the same property. Moreover, the cohomology theories of these objects are the same. This case has already been studied in the author’s paper
[
4
]
.

However, in characteristic
2
the situation fundamentally changes, as the “squares” of some generators can appear in the quadratic defining relations of restricted Lie algebras. Consequently, the restrictification functor is reduced to a mere embedding, producing strong consequences both on a cohomological side, and on the structure of the subalgebras.
People working on modular algebraic structure in characteristic
2
ironically say that “
2
is the
oddest
prime number”. Despite these complexities, working in characteristic
2
also opens up the possibility of considering a richer and broader range of examples.

Right-angled Artin groups (RAAGs), also known as partially commutative groups, or graph groups, form a well-studied class of combinatorially defined abstract groups whose properties are encoded within simple graphs.

A distinguished classifying complex of a RAAG can be constructed by attaching tori of various dimension (see Salvetti
[
18
]
). By additionally including Klein bottles into this construction, one gets a modified version of RAAGs, called twisted right-angled Artin groups, or T-RAAGs. These groups are characterized by defining relations that are either standard commutators
[x,y]=1
, or Klein-type relations
yxy^{-1}=x^{-1}
between the generators. Notably, the Klein-type relation can also be expressed as
[x,y]=xyx^{-1}x^{-1}=x^{2}
, which allows us to consider the restricted Lie algebra defined by the analogous presentation. These objects are similarly encoded in a graph which also has some directed edges.

Droms
[
7
]
determined the class of graphs whose associated RAAGs are locally RAAGs. Specifically, he showed that the (finitely generated) subgroups of a RAAG are all RAAGs if and only if the defining graph is a Droms graph, that is, it contains neither a square graph nor paths of length
3
as induced subgraphs.
Prompted by Droms’ work, a recent work of Foniqi, Quadrelli, and the author
[
1
]
extends this result to T-RAAGs.

The aim of the present paper is to study the Lie algebraic counterparts of these groups. Associated to a graph with some directed edges, we define a restricted Lie algebra, called the T-RAAG Lie algebra, that is generated by the vertices of the graph subjected to a specific defining relation for each plain/directed edge. Under mild assumptions on the defining graph, the associated T-RAAG is a Koszul restricted Lie algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph, and
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
be the associated T-RAAG restricted Lie algebra over a field
k
of characteristic two. Then, each of the following implies that
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph:

(1)

All standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are T-RAAGs;

(2)

All standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are quadratic restricted Lie algebras;

(3)

The cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is universally Koszul.

Moreover, these conditions are equivalent to the fact that
\Gamma
is a mixed Droms graph if, and only if,
k
is the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Recall from
[
4
]
that a standard subalgebra of a graded, restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
is a restricted subalgebra generated by elements of degree
1
of
\mathfrak{g}
(see §
2.1
for the definitions).

We also give a complete characterization of those graphs for which all the standard subalgebras of the associated T-RAAG Lie algebra over a field
k\neq\mathbb{F}_{2}
are T-RAAG Lie algebras.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
k
be any field containing the field
\mathbb{F}_{4}
of four elements. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph, and all the directed edges in any connected component of
\Gamma
have a common origin.

(2)

All standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are T-RAAGs;

(3)

All standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are quadratic restricted Lie algebras;

(4)

The cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is universally Koszul.

Analogously, we examine the Lie algebraic version of right-angled Coxeter groups, which are specific quotients of RAAGs where all the canonical generators have order two. To achieve a Droms-type result for these, it becomes necessary to extend our study to a broader class of restricted Lie algebras. These are defined by simple graphs whose vertices are labelled by elements of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
The corresponding generators of the restricted Lie algebra have torsion depending on their labelling. For these Lie algebras, the analogue of Droms’ theorem proves to be independent of the ground field.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a graph endowed with a function
\theta:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}=\{0;1\}
, and let
k
be a field of characteristic
2
. Let
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,\theta)
be the associated extended right-angled Lie algebra (ERA) with presentation

\left\langle{v:\ v\in V}\,\big|\,{\theta(u)u^{[2]},\ [v,w]:\ u,v,w\in V,\ \{v,w\}\in E}\right\rangle

Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a Droms graph that does not contain any path-graph of length
2
on which
\theta
is non-constant, and where the middle vertex
v
has
\theta(v)=0
;

(2)

The graph
\Gamma
is Droms, and, for all connected induced subgraphs
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
, either
\theta|_{V(\Lambda)}\equiv 0
, or there exists a central vertex
v
of
\Lambda
with
\theta(v)=1
;

(3)

All the standard subalgebras of the ERA Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
are ERA Lie algebras.

We also consider the Bloch-Kato property for restricted Lie algebras. A restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is defined as weakly Bloch-Kato if all of its standard restricted subalgebras are quadratic, and Bloch-Kato if all of its standard restricted subalgebras are Koszul. Such property is intended to mimic the behavior of subgroups of maximal pro-
p
quotients of
G_{\mathbb{F}}
for some field
\mathbb{F}
. Unlike the zero or odd characteristic case, where (restricted) Lie algebras are Koszul when all of their subalgebras generated in degree
1
are quadratic (see
[
4
]
), in characteristic two this is unknown. Nevertheless, we demonstrate that within the classes of restricted Lie algebras under consideration, this property holds true.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be either a T-RAAG Lie algebra or an ERA Lie algebra over an arbitrary field of characteristic
2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is weakly Bloch-Kato;

(2)

The restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is Bloch-Kato.

2.
Restricted Lie algebras of characteristic two

If not otherwise stated, henceforth we denote by
k
an arbitrary field of characteristic two, and by
\mathbb{F}_{2}
its prime field.

2.1.
Restricted Lie algebras

Let
V
be a vector space over
k
.
The
free restricted Lie algebra
on
V
is the (ordinary) Lie subalgebra of the commutator Lie algebra structure of the tensor
k
-algebra
T(V)=k\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes_{k}V)\oplus\dots
that is generated by the
2^{n}
-th powers

v^{2^{n}}=\underbrace{v\otimes\dots\otimes v}_{2^{n}\mbox{ times}}

of elements
v\in V
, for
n\geq 0
.
We denote it by
\mathfrak{f}(V)
, and, for elements
x,y\in\mathfrak{f}(V)
, we write
[x,y]
for their commutator
x\otimes y+y\otimes x
, and
x^{[2]}
for the element
x\otimes x
; we also set
x^{[2]^{n+1}}:=(x^{[2]^{n}})^{[2]}
. It follows from our definition that
[x,x]=0
for all
x\in\mathfrak{f}(V)
.
Considering
T(V)
as a Hopf algebra in the usual way,
\mathfrak{f}(V)
is also the set of its primitive elements.

The following identities hold true for all
\lambda\in k
and
x,y\in\mathfrak{f}(V)
:

(1)

(\lambda x)^{[2]}=\lambda^{2}x^{[2]}
;

(2)

[x^{[2]},y]=[x,[x,y]]
;

(3)

(x+y)^{[2]}=x^{[2]}+[x,y]+y^{[2]}
.

These define the notion of a
2
-
operation
on an arbitrary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. A Lie algebra over
k
with a
2
-operation is called a
restricted Lie algebra
(of characteristic two). Notice that, in the light of (
3
), the Lie brackets are determined by the
2
-operation.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a restricted Lie algebra.
A subalgebra (resp. an ideal) of the ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called a restricted subalgebra (resp. a restricted ideal) if it is closed under the
2
-operation. If
\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
-graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
such that
[x,y]\in\mathfrak{g}_{i+j}
and
x^{[2]}\in\mathfrak{g}_{2i}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{j}
, then we call
\mathfrak{g}
a (positively) graded restricted Lie algebra. Clearly,
\mathfrak{f}(V)
can be given a natural grading induced by the tensor degree of the tensor algebra. For instance, if
V=kx\oplus ky
has dimension
2
, then
\mathfrak{f}(V)_{1}=V
, and
\mathfrak{f}(V)_{2}=kx^{[2]}\oplus k[x,y]\oplus ky
.
An ordinary Lie homomorphism
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
between two restricted graded Lie algebras will be simply called a restricted homomorphism if it satisfies
f(\mathfrak{g}_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{h}_{i}
, and commutes with the
2
-operation. The kernel of a restricted homomorphism
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{h}
is a restricted ideal of
\mathfrak{g}
, and the image of a restricted subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is a restricted subalgebra of
\mathfrak{h}
.

If
R
is a subset of
\mathfrak{f}(V)
, then the quotient of
\mathfrak{f}(V)
by the restricted ideal generated by
R
is denoted by
\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle
(or by
\left\langle{v_{i}}\,\big|\,{r_{i}}\right\rangle
if
(v_{i})
and
(r_{i})
are bases of
V
and
R
, respectively). If
R
is a homogeneous subspace of
\mathfrak{f}(V)
, then
\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle
is a graded restricted Lie algebra generated in degree
1
, and, conversely, any graded Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
generated in degree
1
is naturally isomorphic with
\left\langle{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle
, where
R
is the kernel of the natural map
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to\mathfrak{g}
. Graded restricted Lie algebras generated in degree
1
are said to be
standard
. For an arbitrary standard restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we call the natural map
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\to\mathfrak{g}
the
free cover
of
\mathfrak{g}
; by abuse of language,
\mathfrak{f}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})
is also called the free cover of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
R=R_{2}\oplus R_{3}\oplus\dots
is the homogeneous decomposition of the kernel of the free cover of
\mathfrak{g}
, then the restricted Lie algebra

\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{g}:=\left\langle{\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\,\big|\,{R_{2}}\right\rangle,

together with the natural map
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, is called the
quadratic cover
of
\mathfrak{g}
. We say that
\mathfrak{g}
is
quadratic
if the map
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

is an isomorphism.

The restricted (universal) envelope
u(\mathfrak{g})
of a
2
-restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the quotient of the universal enveloping algebra of the ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by the ideal generated by the elements
x^{[2]}-x^{2}
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. One can realise
u(\mathfrak{g})
as the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(\mathfrak{g})
by the ideal generated by the following elements

(a)

[x,y]+x\otimes y+y\otimes x
, where
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

(b)

x^{[2]}+x^{2}
, where
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

By the Milnor-Moore theorem
[
13
]
, the algebra
u(\mathfrak{g})
is an augmented Hopf algebra, whose set of primitive elements is
\mathfrak{g}
.

If
\mathfrak{g}
is a graded restricted Lie algebra and
M
is a graded restricted
\mathfrak{g}
-module, that is
x\cdot m\in M_{i+j}
, and
x^{[2]}\cdot m=x\cdot(x\cdot m)
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{i}
and
m\in M_{j}
, we define the (bigraded restricted)
cohomology groups
of
\mathfrak{g}
as

H^{ij}(\mathfrak{g},M)=\operatorname{Ext}^{ij}_{u(\mathfrak{g})}(k,M)

where
k\simeq u(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{g}u(\mathfrak{g})
is the trivial
1
-dimensional module of
u(\mathfrak{g})
concentrated in degree
0
. For
M=k
, the whole cohomology
\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0}H^{ij}(\mathfrak{g},k)
, simply denoted
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
, can be endowed with a multiplication mapping (called the cup-product), making it a bigraded connected algebra over
k
. The cohomological dimension
\cd\mathfrak{g}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the supremum of the integers
n
such that
H^{n}(\mathfrak{g})\neq 0
(cf.
[
20
]
).
Recall that
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a
Koszul
restricted Lie algebra if
H^{ij}(\mathfrak{g})=0
unless
i=j
.

Henceforth, restricted Lie algebras are all assumed to be finitely generated, e.g., for a standard restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, we assume
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{1}<\infty
.

A standard restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is (
weakly
)
Bloch-Kato
if all of its standard restricted subalgebras are Koszul (resp. quadratic). The proof of
[
3
, Thm. A]
still applies to the restricted context, implying that a standard restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is Bloch-Kato iff its cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
is universally Koszul, which amounts to saying that all the quotients by ideals generated by elements of
H^{1}(\mathfrak{g})
admit a linear resolution as
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules (see
[
5
]
for the original definition in the commutative context).

It is worth noting that the restricted cohomology of a restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is not the same as the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the underlying ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, which is the
\operatorname{Ext}
-group of its ordinary universal enveloping algebra, i.e.,
H^{\bullet}_{o}(\mathfrak{g},M):=\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(k,M)
. However, the epimorphism
U(\mathfrak{g})\to u(\mathfrak{g})
induces a natural map
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g},M)\to H^{\bullet}_{o}(\mathfrak{g},M)
.

Example 2.1
.

In this example we consider quadratic restricted Lie algebras generated by a single element.

(1) Let
\mathfrak{k}
be the free restricted Lie algebra on one generator. Its cohomology ring is the
2
-dimensional algebra
k[T]/(T^{2})
. As an ordinary Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{k}
is the countable dimensional abelian Lie algebra, which is thus infinitely generated, i.e., its ordinary first cohomology group is non-zero in each internal degree
j
that is a
2
-power. The Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of
\mathfrak{k}
is thus the exterior algebra over the vector space
k^{\mathbb{N}}
.

(2) Consider the
1
-dimensional restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}=\left\langle{x}\,\big|\,{x^{[2]}}\right\rangle
. The (ordinary) enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
of the ordinary Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}\simeq k
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
k[T]
, and hence the ordinary cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{g}
is the dual ring
k[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^{2})
. In particular, it is a finite dimensional algebra.

On the other hand, the restricted envelope
u(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to the quotient ring
k[S]/(S^{2})
, proving that its cohomology ring is the polynomial ring
k[\delta]
, which is infinite dimensional.

We say that an element
x
of a restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a torsion element if
x^{[2]^{n}}=0
for some
n\geq 1
. Thus
\mathfrak{g}
is
torsion-free
if the zero vector is the unique torsion element. Torsion-free abelian Lie algebras are called
free abelian
; for instance, the restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
from Example
2.1
is free abelian. On the contrary,
\mathfrak{g}
is
elementary abelian
if
x^{[2]}=0
holds for every
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. In fact, notice that this also implies that
[x,y]=0
, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

If
V
is a
k
-vector space, the
exterior algebra

\Lambda(V)
of
V
is the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(V)=k\oplus V\oplus(V\otimes_{k}V)\oplus\dots
by the ideal generated by the elements
x\otimes x
, for
x\in V
. Notice that, since
k
has characteristic two, that ideal properly contains the ideal generated by
x\otimes y+y\otimes x
, for
x,y\in V
, which is, instead, the defining ideal of the
symmetric algebra

S(V)
as a quotient of
T(V)
.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the direct product
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}
of the restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\left\langle{v,w}\,\big|\,{[v,w]+v^{[2]}}\right\rangle
with the free abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}=\langle{z}\rangle
of rank
1
, and its subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{v+z,w}\rangle
.

One has
[v+z,w]=v^{[2]}
, and
(v+z)^{[2]}=v^{[2]}+z^{[2]}
, which imply that
\mathfrak{h}_{2}=k\cdot v^{[2]}+k\cdot z^{[2]}+k\cdot w^{[2]}
has dimension
3
. In particular, the quadratic cover
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{h}
is a free restricted Lie algebra of rank
2
. However,
[[v+z],w^{[2]}]=[v^{[2]},w]=[v,v^{[2]}]=0
is a non-trivial relation of
\mathfrak{h}
, proving that it is not quadratic.

By the Corollary
4.5
below,
\mathfrak{g}
is Koszul, and the proper standard subalgebras are all free abelian, hence Koszul. In particular,
\mathfrak{g}
is BK but
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}
is not. Notice that
\mathfrak{g}
is not the restrictification of an ordinary quadratic Lie algebra.

In particular, unlike the case of zero/odd characteristic, the direct product of a BK Lie algebra with a free abelian Lie algebra is not always BK.

Remark 2.3
.

Since the cohomology of a direct product of restricted Lie algebras is the wedge product of the cohomology rings of the factors, we see that the twisted extension of a universally Koszul algebra is not always universally Koszul, in contradiction with Proposition 31 of
[
14
]
. The flaw sits at the end of the proof where the authors define the map
\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\xi$}}:B\to B
by
a+a^{\prime}x\mapsto a+a^{\prime}(x-l)
for
a,a^{\prime}\in A
, claiming that it is an automorphism. Nevertheless, this is not well-defined in general as
\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\xi$}}(x)^{2}=(x-l)^{2}=x^{2}+l^{2}=tx+l^{2}
and
\mathbin{\rotatebox[origin]{270.0}{$\xi$}}(tx)=t(x-l)
. It is well-defined only if
tl=l^{2}
.

We now show that the BK property is preserved if the second factor is elementary abelian instead of free abelian.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a (weakly) BK restricted Lie algebra. Then
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
is (weakly) BK.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
, and fix a non-zero element
z
of
\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
. If
\mathfrak{m}
is a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, then it is Koszul (resp. quadratic) as
\mathfrak{g}
is BK (resp. weakly BK). On the other hand, if
\mathfrak{m}
is not contained in
\mathfrak{g}
, there exist elements
x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
such that
\mathfrak{m}=\langle{x_{1},\dots,x_{n},x_{0}+z}\rangle
.
The standard subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}\rangle
of
\mathfrak{g}
is Koszul (resp. quadratic) as
\mathfrak{g}
is (resp. weakly) BK. We can suppose that
x_{0}
is independent over
k
from
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
, as otherwise
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}}\rangle
, whence we would get that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{h}\times\langle{z}\rangle
is Koszul (resp. quadratic). Now, the map

\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{m}:\quad x_{0}\mapsto x_{0}+z,\quad x_{i}\mapsto x_{i}\ (i=1,\dots,n)

is a well-defined homomorphism. Indeed, if
r=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}[x_{i},x_{j}]+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}^{[2]}
is a relation in
\mathfrak{h}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}

\displaystyle[\phi(x_{i}),\phi(x_{j})]+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta_{i}\phi(x_{i})^{[2]}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{0j}[x_{0}+z,x_{j}]+\beta_{0}(x_{0}+z)^{[2]}+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}[x_{i},x_{j}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}^{[2]}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{0j}[x_{0},x_{j}]+\beta_{0}(x_{0}^{[2]}+[x_{0},z]+z^{[2]})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}[x_{i},x_{j}]+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}^{[2]}=

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n}\alpha_{ij}[x_{i},x_{j}]+\sum_{i=0}^{n}\beta_{i}x_{i}^{[2]}=r=0.

The restriction to
\mathfrak{m}
of the natural projection
\pi:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the inverse of
\phi
.
∎

The same proof, with the omission of the
2
-powers of elements, shows that if
\mathfrak{g}
is the restrictification of an ordinary BK Lie algebra, then
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{k}
is BK. Moreover, for these Lie algebras there is no distinction between the weak and strong BK properties.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
A
be a commutative algebra over
k
. Then the symmetric tensor product
A\wedge k[T]
is universally Koszul.

We finally provide another example of a Bloch-Kato restricted Lie algebra that is not the restrictification of an ordinary Lie algebra.

Example 2.6
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}=\left\langle{x,y}\,\big|\,{x^{[2]}+y^{[2]}}\right\rangle
is quadratic, and, being
1
-relator, its cohomological dimension is
2
, proving that it is Koszul. Moreover, all the proper subalgebras are free abelian, hence Koszul, which implies that
\mathfrak{s}
is Bloch-Kato.

Equivalently, notice that its quadratic dual admits a presentation as a connected algebra

A=\left\langle{\xi,\eta}\,\big|\,{\xi\eta,\eta\xi,\xi^{2}+\eta^{2}}\right\rangle_{\text{alg}},

and hence it is universally Koszul as the annihilator of
a\xi+b\eta
,
(a,b)\in k^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, is generated by the degree-
1
element
b\xi+a\eta
(see
[
14
]
).

2.2.
HNN-extensions

Following Lichtman and Shirvani
[
12
]
, we consider HNN-extensions of graded restricted Lie algebras in characteristic
2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a graded restricted Lie algebra over
k
, and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a restricted homogeneous subalgebra. If
n
is a positive integer, a degree-
n
derivation of
\mathfrak{h}
into
\mathfrak{g}
is a
k
-linear map
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that, for every homogeneous element
x\in\mathfrak{h}_{i}
, one has

(1)

\phi(x)\in\mathfrak{g}_{i+n}
;

(2)

\phi(x^{[2]})=[x,\phi(x)]
.

It follows from (
3
) that the usual Leibniz product formula holds true, i.e., for
x,y\in\mathfrak{h}
,

\phi([x,y])=[\phi(x),y]+[x,\phi(y)].

The aim of HNN-extensions is to embed
\mathfrak{g}
into a larger Lie algebra where the derivation
\phi
extends to an inner derivation.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
n
be a positive integer, and let
\phi:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
be a degree-
n
derivation of a homogeneous subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of a graded restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. A graded restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}
together with a homogeneous restricted homomorphism
\iota:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{e}
, and an element
t\in\mathfrak{e}
such that
\ad(t)|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\iota\phi
is an HNN-extension of
\phi
if it satisfies the following universal property: For all restricted Lie algebras
\mathfrak{m}
, all homogeneous restricted homomorphisms
\jmath:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{m}
, and all
s\in\mathfrak{m}
such that
\ad(s)|_{\mathfrak{h}}=\jmath\phi
, there exists a unique homogeneous restricted homomorphism
\psi:\mathfrak{e}\to\mathfrak{m}
such that
\psi\iota=\jmath
.

The HNN-extension exists and is unique up to (unique) isomorphism; we denote it by
\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathfrak{g},t)
.
An explicit presentation for
\mathfrak{e}
is given in terms of a presentation
\mathfrak{g}=\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{R}\right\rangle
as follows

\mathfrak{e}=\left\langle{V,t}\,\big|\,{R,[t,x]+\phi(x):\ x\in\mathfrak{h}}\right\rangle.

It is clear that the additional generator
t
, which is called the stable letter of the extension, is a homogeneous element of
\mathfrak{e}
of the same degree as the derivation
\phi
.
Moreover,
[
12
]
proves that
\mathfrak{g}
naturally embeds into the HNN-extension
\mathfrak{e}
.

The proof of Proposition 2.2 by Kochloukova and Martínez Pérez
[
11
]
adapts verbatim to the graded, restricted context.

Theorem 2.8

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{e}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathfrak{g},t)
be the HNN-extension of a graded restricted Lie
k
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to a degree-
n
derivation
\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{g}
. Then, there is a short exact sequence of left
u(\mathfrak{e})
-modules

0\to u(\mathfrak{e})\otimes_{u(\mathfrak{h})}k\overset{\alpha}{\to}u(\mathfrak{e})\otimes_{u(\mathfrak{g})}k\overset{\beta}{\to}k\to 0

where, for
x\in u(\mathfrak{e})
,
\alpha(x\otimes 1)=xt\otimes 1
,
\beta(x\otimes 1)=\varepsilon(x)
, and
\varepsilon:u(\mathfrak{e})\to k
is the augmentation map. The maps
\alpha
and
\beta
are homogeneous maps of degree
n
and
0
, respectively. In particular, by the bigraded Eckmann-Shapiro lemma
[
3
, Thm. 1.4]
, if
M
is a graded restricted
\mathfrak{e}
-module, there is a long exact sequence

\dots\to H^{ij}(\mathfrak{e},M)\to H^{ij}(\mathfrak{g},M)\to H^{i,j-n}(\mathfrak{h},M)\to H^{i+1,j}(\mathfrak{e},M)\to\dots

Remark 2.9
.

In the theory of quadratic (restricted) Lie algebras of characteristic
\neq 2
,
[
4
]
proves that if
\mathfrak{g}
is a quadratic Lie algebra, and
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal proper standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, with
t\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}\setminus\mathfrak{m}
, then
\mathfrak{g}=\operatorname{HNN}_{\phi}(\mathfrak{m},t)
, where
\phi:=\ad(x)|_{\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{m}
, and
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{x\in\mathfrak{m}_{1}:\ [t,x]\in\mathfrak{m}}\rangle
. Using this general decomposition, an easy inductive procedure shows that weakly BK Lie algebras of characteristic
\neq 2
are BK. The bijection established by the restrictification functor also shows that restricted weakly BK Lie algebras of characteristic
\neq 2
are BK.

Unfortunately, in characteristic two, the above-mentioned decomposition is not always available; for instance, the quadratic Lie algebra
\left\langle{x,y}\,\big|\,{(x+y)^{[2]}}\right\rangle
, as well as the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{s}
of Example
2.6
, does not split as an HNN-extension over
\langle{x}\rangle
.

Question 2.10
.

Is it true that all weakly BK restricted Lie algebras are Koszul?

An affirmative answer would imply that there is no distinction between BK and weakly BK Lie algebras, like in zero/odd characteristic.

2.3.
A generalized Witt formula

Let
\mu:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be the Möbius function, and consider its mod-
2
version (see Petrogradsky
[
15
]
)

(2.1)

\mu_{2}(n)=\begin{cases}\mu(n),&\text{if }n\text{ is odd,}\\
2^{s-1}\mu(m),&\text{if }n=2^{s}m,\text{ and }m\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

It satisfies

(2.2)

\sum_{d|m}(-1)^{d+1}\mu_{2}(m/d)=\delta_{1,m}

The generalized necklace polynomial mod-
2
of degree
k\geq 1
is defined similarly to the classical one, where the Möbius function is replaced by
\mu_{2}
, namely,

M_{2,k}(t)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j|k}\mu_{2}(k/j)t^{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!]

Now, recall that, given a graded, connected associative algebra over a field
k
such that
\operatorname{Ext}^{\bullet}_{A}(k,k)
is finite dimensional, one defines the
eigenvalues
of
A
as the complex numbers
\lambda_{i}
such that

\chi_{A}(t):=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\dim\left(\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i,j}(k,k)\right)t^{j}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+\lambda_{i}t).

The left-hand side is the Hilbert series of the
\operatorname{Ext}
-algebra of
A
with respect to the internal degree. If
A
is the restricted envelope of a graded, restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, then the numbers
\lambda_{i}
are called the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a graded, restricted Lie algebra, with finite dimensional cohomology and eigenvalues
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n}
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\dim\mathfrak{g}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{2,k}(\lambda_{i}).

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Weigel
[
20
]
to the restricted world.

Let
A=u(\mathfrak{g})
. By the Poincaré-Birkhoff-Witt for
p
-restricted Lie algebras, the Hilbert series of
A
is

h_{A}(t):=\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim(A_{i})t^{i}=\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1-t^{kp}}{1-t^{k}}\right)^{\ell_{k}}

where
\ell_{k}=\dim\mathfrak{g}_{k}
. By Fröberg’s formula (see
[
20
, Prop. 2.2]
), one has
\chi_{A}(-t)h_{A}(t)=1
in
\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!]
, and for
p=2
we deduce that

\prod_{k\geq 1}(1+t^{k})^{\ell_{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_{i}t)^{-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{Z}[\![t]\!].

By taking
-\log
from both sides,

-\sum_{k\geq 1}\ell_{k}\log(1+t^{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log(1-\lambda_{i}t)

and then, using the definition of logarithm of power series

\log(1-y)=\sum_{j\geq 1}y^{j}/j
,

\sum_{k\geq 1}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{\ell_{k}}{j}(-1)^{j+1}t^{kj}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{(\lambda_{i}t)^{m}}{m}

By rewriting the left-hand side as

\sum_{m\geq 1}\sum_{j|m}\ell_{m/j}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}}{j}\ t^{m},

we compare the coefficients of
t^{m}
and get, for all
m\geq 1
,

(2.3)

\sum_{j|m}\frac{\ell_{m/j}}{m/j}(-1)^{j+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\lambda_{i}}^{m}

The result thus follows from Equation
2.2
and the generalized Möbius inversion formula. Indeed, if
\alpha(k)=(-1)^{k+1}
, and
\beta(k)=\ell_{k}/k
, then the left-hand side of (
2.3
) is the Dirichlet convolution
(\alpha\star\beta)(m)
. By (
2.2
),
\mu_{2}
is the Dirichlet inverse of
\alpha
, i.e.,
\mu_{2}\star\alpha\star\beta=\beta
, and hence

\ell_{k}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{d|k}\mu_{2}(k/d)\lambda_{i}^{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}M_{2,k}(\lambda_{i})

∎

For the standard free restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
on
n
generators we recover the restricted Witt’s formula
[
15
, Cor. 2.1]
:
\mathfrak{f}
has a single eigenvalue,
\lambda_{1}=n
, whence

\dim(\mathfrak{f}_{k})=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu_{2}(k/d)n^{k}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

2

\theta(v)=1

Proof.

Since
\Lambda
is a connected Droms graph, it contains a central vertex
v
.
Suppose that all the central vertices of
\Lambda
have value
0

u
is not a central vertex, then either
\theta(u)=0

such that the induced subgraph spanned by

is one of the path graphs of Definition
3.1
, which is impossible since

is a Droms labelled graph.
∎

4.

We first consider restricted Lie algebras associated to mixed graphs.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

isomorphic to
\operatorname{gr}kA(\Gamma)
?

(Q2) Is
\operatorname{gr}A(\Gamma)
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
?

If
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph with a partition
V=V_{+}\cup V_{-}
, the signature
\theta:V\to k
extends to a restricted Lie homomorphism
\theta:\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)\to k
, where
k
is seen as an elementary abelian, standard, restricted Lie algebra, i.e.,
k\simeq\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
. We call
\theta
a signature of the mixed graph
\Gamma
. For all edges
e=\{v,w\}\in E
, one may rewrite the associated relation of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
as
[v,w]=\theta(v)w^{[2]}+\theta(w)v^{[2]}
, which provides an equivalent presentation for the T-RAAG of a special mixed graph
\Gamma
:

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)=\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{[v,w]+\theta(v)w^{[2]}+\theta(w)v^{[2]}:\ \{v,w\}\in E}\right\rangle.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\Lambda
is an induced subgraph of a special mixed graph
\Gamma
, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
naturally embeds into
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

First of all notice that the natural map
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)\to\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
induced by the inclusion
\Lambda\to\Gamma
is a restricted homomorphism.

We argue by induction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
. It is enough to suppose that it is connected and that
\Lambda\neq\Gamma
. The case when
\Gamma
consists of a single vertex is obvious, so suppose that
\Gamma
has more than one vertex. If
v
is a vertex in
\Gamma
but not in
\Lambda
, let
{\Gamma_{v}}
be the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
with vertex set
V\setminus\{v\}
. By induction,
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
naturally embeds into
\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
.

If
v\in V_{+}
, the map
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)\to\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
defined on generators by
v\mapsto 0
and
x\in V({\Gamma_{v}})\mapsto x
is a restricted Lie map, and is a left inverse to the natural map
\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})\to\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
constructed above, which is thus injective.

On the other hand, if
v\in V_{-}
, and
W
is the set of vertices that are adjacent to
v
, one can split
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
into the HNN-extension of
\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
with respect to the derivation
\phi:\mathfrak{b}\to\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
defined by sending each canonical generator
w
to
w^{[2]}
, where
\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{a}(\Pi)
is the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
generated by
W
, and
\Pi
is the subgraph induced by
W
. It follows that
\mathfrak{a}({\Gamma_{v}})
embeds into
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
by
[
12
]
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

If
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is a Koszul restricted Lie algebra.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the number
n
of vertices of
\Gamma
. If
n=1
, then
u(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))=k[T]
is clearly a Koszul algebra, and hence so is
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
by definition.
Suppose now that
n>1
. If
V_{-}=\emptyset
, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is Koszul by Fröberg
[
8
]
(see also
[
4
]
).
On the other hand, if
v\in V_{-}
, then one recovers the same HNN-decomposition of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
as in Proposition
4.4
, in terms of subalgebras
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
and
\mathfrak{b}
which are Koszul by induction. By Theorem
2.8
we conclude (see
[
4
, Lem. 2.2]
).
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

If
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph, then the cohomology ring of the T-RAAG Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the commutative connected
k
-algebra presented by

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma),k)=\left\langle{\xi_{i}}\,\big|\,{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\ ,\ \xi_{p}^{2}+\xi_{p}\xi_{q}:\ \{v_{i},v_{j}\}\notin E;\ (v_{p},v_{q})=(o(e),t(e)),\ e\in D}\right\rangle_{\text{alg}}

where
\xi_{i}
denotes the dual basis element of the canonical generator
v_{i}\in V
.

In particular, the cohomological dimension

\cd\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
equals the clique number of
\Gamma
, that is the maximal integer
n
such that
\Gamma
contains an
n
-clique.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is a Koszul Lie algebra, its cohomology ring is isomorphic to the quotient of the tensor algebra over
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\ast}=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\mathfrak{a}_{1},k)
by the ideal generated by the elements
\alpha\in(\mathfrak{a}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{a}_{1})^{\ast}
such that
\alpha(v\otimes w-w\otimes v+\theta(v)w\otimes w+\theta(w)v\otimes v)=0
for all
\{v,w\}\in E
, where we have identified
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\ast}\otimes\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\ast}
with
(\mathfrak{a}_{1}\otimes\mathfrak{a}_{1})^{\ast}
. Let
(\xi_{i})
be the dual basis of
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\ast}
relative to
(v_{i})
, i.e.,
\xi_{i}(v_{j})=\delta_{ij}
.
The second assertion follows from the fact that
\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
, vanishes in
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a})
when
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\notin E
,
i\neq j
, and
\xi^{3}=\xi_{i}^{2}\xi_{j}=\xi_{i}\xi_{j}^{2}=0
when
(v_{i},v_{j})\in(o,t)(D)
, and
\xi\in H^{1}(\mathfrak{a})
.
∎

By Example
2.1
(2), the T-RAAG associated to a special mixed graph is torsion-free for it has finite cohomological dimension. On the other hand, if
\Gamma
is not a special graph, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
might have torsion.

Example 4.7
.

Consider a non-special complete graph
\Delta
with an oriented cycle, e.g.,
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
,
E=\left\{{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}}\ \big|\ {i\neq j,\ v_{i},v_{j}\in V}\right\}
,
D=\left\{{\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}}\ \big|\ {i=1,\dots,n}\right\}
, and
(o,t)\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}=(v_{i},v_{i+1})
(indices mod
n
).
In the associated T-RAAG
\mathfrak{a}(\Delta)
, one has

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\right)^{[2]}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{[2]}+\sum_{i<j}[v_{i},v_{j}]=2\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}^{[2]}=0,

that is, the sum of all canonical generators is a torsion element.

Unlike the simplicial case, if
\Gamma
is a mixed graph and
\Lambda
is an induced subgraph, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
might not be a retract of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
in a natural way.

Example 4.8
.

Consider the single-edge graph
\Gamma=

u
v

The quotient of the T-RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)=\left\langle{u,v}\,\big|\,{[u,v]+u^{[2]}}\right\rangle
by the ideal generated by

is elementary abelian, and hence not a T-RAAG.

Nevertheless, we can avoid the situation of the example if we only consider subgraphs containing all the negative vertices of
\Gamma

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\Gamma
be a special mixed graph. Let

be a set of vertices of
\Gamma
not containing the terminus of any edge, i.e.,

. Then,
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)/(X)
is the T-RAAG Lie algebra associated to the induced graph of
\Gamma
spanned by

.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be the graph spanned by

.
We prove that the natural map
\iota:\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)\to\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
admits a retraction, namely, a left inverse. Let
\sigma(v)=0
for

, and
\sigma(v)=v
for any vertex
v
of
\Lambda
. A computation of
\sigma
on the relations shows that
\sigma
extends to a well-defined restricted homomorphism, which is thus a retraction of
\iota
.
∎

The same proof shows that
X
can also contain a negative vertex
v
, provided that it also contains the whole
star
of
v
, namely, the set of its adjacent vertices.

In the unidirected case, the graph is determined by its associated RAAG Lie algebra, namely, if
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)\simeq\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
, then the graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic (see Kim and Roush
[

]
, and Droms
[

]
for the group theoretic case). As for twisted right-angled Artin groups (cf.
[
1
]
), this is no longer true in the directed case.

4.1.
Hilbert series of T-RAAGs

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a special mixed graph. Denote by
\Gamma^{\circ}
the simple graph with vertex set
V
and edge-set
E^{\prime}=E\setminus D
. We compute the Hilbert series of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
from that of the RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ})
.

In fact, one clearly has a surjective restricted homomorphism
\pi:\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ})\to\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
, which is an isomorphism in degree
1
. Since these T-RAAG Lie algebras are Koszul, the inflation
\pi^{\ast}:H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))\to H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ}))
is surjective, and we need to compute its kernel.

For this, fix an ordering on
V
and let
\mathfrak{X}
be the set of cliques of
\Gamma
properly containing the terminus of some edges in
D
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{X}=\left\{{\mathcal{O}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}\subseteq V}\ \big|\ {\mathcal{O}\cap V_{-}\neq\emptyset,\ |{\mathcal{O}}|=n\geq 2}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{X}
. As
\Gamma
is a special graph,
|{\mathcal{O}\cap V_{-}}|=1
, and we let
v_{\mathcal{O}}
be the minimal vertex of
\mathcal{O}\cap V_{+}
(with respect to the chosen ordering).

By Corollary
4.6
the ideal
\ker\pi^{\ast}
is generated in
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
by the elements
(v^{\ast})^{2}
, where
v\in o(D)
and
v^{\ast}
denotes the Kronecker dual of the canonical basis element
v
, i.e.,
v^{\ast}(w)=1
if
w=v
, and
v^{\ast}(w)=0
if
w\in V\setminus\{v\}
.
Moreover,

\ker\pi^{\ast}=\sum_{\mathcal{O}\in\mathfrak{X}}k\cdot\left[v_{\mathcal{O}}^{\ast}\prod_{v\in\mathcal{O}\cap V_{+}}v^{\ast}\right]

Indeed, if
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
are pairwise-adjacent vertices, then there are only two possible cases:

(1) If
v_{1}
is not the origin of any edge
e\in D
, then
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}
already vanishes in
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
.

(2) If
v_{1}
is the origin of an edge
e\in D
, with
v=t(e)
, then
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}v_{2}^{\ast}\dots v_{m}^{\ast}=v_{1}^{\ast}v^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\dots v_{m}^{\ast}
does not vanish in
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
only if each
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v
; if this is the case, then
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}v_{2}^{\ast}\dots v_{m}^{\ast}=v_{1}^{\ast}(v_{2}^{\ast})^{2}\dots v_{m}^{\ast}=\dots=v_{1}^{\ast}v_{2}^{\ast}\dots(v_{m}^{\ast})^{2}
, and
\{v,v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}\in\mathfrak{X}
is a clique containing a negative element.

On the other hand, if
v_{1},\dots,v_{m}
are not all pairwise adjacent, then
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}v_{2}^{\ast}\dots v_{m}^{\ast}=0
.

Example 4.11
.

(1) Consider the single-edge
\Gamma=

u

\Gamma^{\circ}=

,
and thus

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))=k[u^{\ast},v^{\ast}]/((u^{\ast})^{2}+u^{\ast}v^{\ast},(v^{\ast})^{2}),

and

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ}))=k[u^{\ast},v^{\ast}]/((u^{\ast})^{2},u^{\ast}v^{\ast},(v^{\ast})^{2}),

which gives
\ker\pi^{\ast}=k\cdot(u^{\ast})^{2}
.

(2) Consider the graphs

\Gamma=
v_{1}
v_{2}\ \ \ \
c
w_{1}
\ \ \ \ \ w_{2}
d
\Gamma^{\circ}=
v_{1}
v_{2}\ \ \ \
c
w_{1}
\ \ \ \ \ w_{2}

The cohomology rings of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
and of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ})
are quotients of the polynomial algebra

k[v_{i}^{\ast},w_{i}^{\ast},c^{\ast},d^{\ast}:i=1,2]

by the ideals generated respectively by
\Omega_{\Gamma}
and
\Omega_{\Gamma^{\circ}}
, where

\displaystyle\Omega_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Span}_{k}\{(c^{\ast})^{2},c^{\ast}v_{i}^{\ast}+(v_{i}^{\ast})^{2},c^{\ast}w_{i}^{\ast}+(w_{i}^{\ast})^{2},c^{\ast}d^{\ast}+(d^{\ast})^{2},d^{\ast}v_{i}^{\ast},d^{\ast}w_{i}^{\ast},v_{i}^{\ast}w_{j}^{\ast}:\ i,j=1,2\},

The homogeneous components of the kernel

are

\displaystyle I_{j}=\begin{cases}0,&j<2\text{, or }j>3\\

Indeed,
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}v_{2}=(v_{2}^{\ast})^{2}
, and
(v_{1}^{\ast})^{3}=(v_{1}^{\ast})^{2}c^{\ast}=v_{1}^{\ast}(c^{\ast})^{2}=0
, etc.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
\Gamma
be a special mixed graph. For
i\geq 2
, let
n_{i}
be the number of
i
-cliques containing a negative vertex. Then, the Poincaré series of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
, i.e., the Hilbert series of
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
, is

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim H^{i}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))z^{i}=\operatorname{C_{\Gamma^{\circ}}}(z)+\sum_{i\geq 2}n_{i}z^{i}

where
C_{\Delta}(z)
denotes the clique polynomial of the simplicial graph
\Delta
.

Proof.

Consider the natural surjection
\pi^{\ast}:H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))\to H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ}))
. Recall that the restricted cohomology of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ})
is isomorphic to the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the ordinary Lie subalgebra
\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma^{\circ}}
of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ})
generated by its elements of degree
1
(cf. the restrictification functor in
[
4
]
). It follows that

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim H^{i}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))z^{i}=\sum_{i\geq 0}\left(\dim H^{i}(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\circ}))+\dim(\ker\pi^{\ast})_{i}\right)z^{i}.

From the computations performed above, we get
\dim(\ker\pi^{\ast})_{i}=n_{i}
, which gives the desired formula, as the Poincaré polynomial of an ordinary RAAG Lie algebra is the clique polynomial of the defining graph.
∎

The next result thus follows at once.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
\Gamma
be a special mixed graph, and let
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
be the associated T-RAAG Lie algebra. Then, the Poincaré series of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the clique polynomial of the underlying simple graph
\bar{\Gamma}
.

Since the restricted envelope
u(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
is Koszul, it follows from Froberg’s formula that the Hilbert series of
u(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
is

(4.2)

\sum_{i\geq 0}\dim(u(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))_{i})z^{i}=C_{\bar{\Gamma}}(-z)^{-1}

By the main theorem of Petrogradsky
[
15
]
, the Hilbert series of the T-RAAG
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is

\sum_{i\geq 1}\dim\mathfrak{(}a(\Gamma)_{i})z^{i}=\mathcal{L}_{2}(\phi):=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\mu_{2}(n)}{n}\ln\phi(z^{n})

where
\phi
is the formal power series of the Hilbert series (
4.2
) of
u(\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma))
, and
\mu_{2}
is the mod-
2
Möbius function described in §
2.3
.

5.
Subalgebras of T-RAAGs

In this section we provide a complete characterization of Bloch-Kato T-RAAGs in terms of the structure of the defining graph.

To begin with, we exclude non-special graphs from the discussion.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a non-special mixed graph. Then,
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
contains a non-quadratic, standard subalgebra.

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is not special, it contains an induced subgraph
\Lambda
on three vertices
u,v,w
such that
\vec{uv}\in D
and either
\vec{vw}\in D
or
\overline{vw}\in E\setminus D
. We put
\theta=1
in the first case, and
\theta=0
otherwise, so that
[v,w]=\theta v^{[2]}
.
Consider the subalgebra
\mathfrak{b}
of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
generated by the vertices of
\Lambda
. We can assume that
\mathfrak{b}
is quadratic, and hence isomorphic to
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
generated by
u+w
and
v
. Since
[u+w,v]=u^{[2]}+\theta v^{[2]}
, the quadratic cover
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{m}
is a free restricted Lie algebra, and hence it is a proper extension of
\mathfrak{m}
as
[u+w,v^{[2]}]=0
.
∎

Example 5.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
be a special mixed graph, and let
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
be the associated T-RAAG over
k
.

(1)

Let
\Gamma=\Lambda_{s}
, i.e.,

and

. Denote the canonical generator of
\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda_{s})

.

Consider the subalgebra
\mathscr{b}
generated by

and
x_{2}

[x_{2},x_{1}+x_{3}]=x_{1}^{[2]}+x_{3}^{[2]}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(x_{1}+x_{3})^{[2]}=x_{1}^{[2]}+x_{3}^{[2]}+[x_{1},x_{3}],

proving that
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{b}
is free. Since for

[x_{2}^{[2]},x_{i}]=[x_{2},[x_{2},x_{i}]]=[x_{2},x_{i}^{[2]}]=[[x_{2},x_{i}],x_{i}]=[x_{i}^{[2]},x_{i}]=0,

it follows that
[x_{2}^{[2]},x_{1}+x_{3}]=0
, and hence
\mathfrak{b}
is not quadratic. Notice that, for RAAG Lie algebras associated to simple graphs, the existence of non-quadratic standard subalgebras generated by

[
4

(2)

Let

be the square graph, i.e.

(a)

If

(b)

Suppose that
\vec{12}\in D

. Then the subgraph
\Lambda

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
.

(3)

Suppose that
\bar{\Gamma}=P_{4}
is the path of length

V=\{1,2,3,4\}
and

(a)

If

\mathfrak{b}

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)

(b)

If

One has

\displaystyle[[x_{1}+x_{2},x_{3}],x_{4}]

Lemma 5.3
.

If
\Gamma
is a simple graph, i.e.,

are RAAG Lie algebras if, and only if,

Let
\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}
be the ordinary RAAG Lie algebra over

is
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)

∎

5.1.
The prime field

For

Proposition 5.4
.

If
\Gamma
is a mixed graph and
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the associated T-RAAG restricted Lie algebra over the prime field
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a mixed Droms graph;

(2)

All standard restricted subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are T-RAAGs;

(3)

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is weakly BK, i.e., all standard restricted subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are quadratic restricted Lie algebras;

(4)

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is BK, i.e., all standard restricted subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
are Koszul restricted Lie algebras;

(5)

The cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is universally Koszul.

Proof.

The equivalence (4)
\iff
(5) is clear, and obviously (4) implies (3). Example
5.2
, together with Proposition
5.1
, shows that (3) implies (1). Moreover, by Corollary
4.5
, (2) implies (4). It remains to prove that (1) implies (2) .
The proof is based on that of the group theoretic analogue
[
1
]
. Suppose that
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph. We argue by induction on the number of its vertices
|{V}|
.

The case
|{V}|=1
is trivial, so suppose that
|{V}|>1
. If
\Gamma
is disconnected, say
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
, then
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the free product
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)=\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{1})\amalg\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{2})
of restricted Lie algebras. In particular, the cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the direct sum of those of the
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{i})

\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is BK. From the Kurosh subalgebra theorem
[
3
]
,
(3)
follows.

Now suppose that
\Gamma
is connected, and hence a cone
\nabla_{\theta^{\prime}}(\Gamma^{\prime})
, where
\theta^{\prime}
is the signature of
\Gamma^{\prime}
and
v
is the tip of the cone, i.e.,
V=V(\Gamma^{\prime})\cup\{v\}
. The unique signature
\theta
for

is the extension of
\theta^{\prime}
with
\theta(v)=0
.
Hence,
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is the semidirect product
\langle{v}\rangle\rtimes\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
. By induction, we suppose that all the standard subalgebras
\mathfrak{c}
of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
are T-RAAG restricted Lie algebras with signature
\theta^{\prime}|_{\mathfrak{c}}
. Let
\pi:\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)\to\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
be the natural projection.
If
\mathfrak{b}
is a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
, then the image
\pi(\mathfrak{b})
in
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
is a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma^{\prime})
, and hence a T-RAAG Lie algebra by induction, i.e., there exists a mixed graph
\Lambda=(V(\Lambda),E(\Lambda),D(\Lambda),o_{\Lambda},t_{\Lambda})
such that
\pi(\mathfrak{b})\simeq\mathfrak{a}(\Lambda)
, with signature
\theta^{\prime}|_{\pi(\mathfrak{b})}
. Put

\pi(x_{i})=v_{i}
.
For all

.
We need to show that the assignment
\sigma(v_{i})=v_{i}+\alpha_{i}v

\pi(\mathfrak{b})\to\mathfrak{b}
giving a split exact sequence

5.1
) splits. We deduce that
\mathfrak{b}=(\mathfrak{b}\cap\langle{v}\rangle)\rtimes\pi(\mathfrak{b})
is a T-RAAG Lie algebra with canonical generators
x_{i}
(
i=1,\dots,r
), and possibly
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}\cap\langle{v}\rangle
.
Finally, the map
\tilde{\theta}(x_{i})=\theta^{\prime}(v_{i})
,
\tilde{\theta}(x)=0
, for
x\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}\cap\langle{v}\rangle
, is a signature for the defining graph of
\mathfrak{b}
and is the restriction of
\theta
. Since
\mathfrak{b}
is a T-RAAG Lie algebra, we deduce that
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is BK, and hence its cohomology ring is universally Koszul.
∎

5.2.
Non-prime field

In the following example we show an unexpected behavior of T-RAAGs over larger fields: if
k
contains the field with four elements
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, then the T-RAAG Lie algebra of a particular Droms graph with
3
vertices is not BK, and its cohomology is not universally Koszul. This is essentially due to the fact that the map
\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}
of a torsion-free, abelian, restricted Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}
over a field
k
sending each element to its
2
-power is not linear, unless
k=\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Indeed, if
\alpha\in k\setminus\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and
x\in\mathfrak{a}
is non-zero, then
(\alpha x)^{[2]}=\alpha^{2}x^{[2]}\neq\alpha x^{[2]}
.

Example 5.5
.

For the sake of concreteness, we work on the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{4}
, but the same computations can be performed over any ground field
k\neq\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Realize the field
k=\mathbb{F}_{4}
as the quotient
\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]/(T^{2}+T+1)
, and denote its elements by
\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{1}T
,
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
.
Consider the special graph
e
consisting of two adjacent vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
, where
V_{+}=\{v_{1}\}
, and
\Gamma=\nabla_{\theta}(e)
; then the associated T-RAAG restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is

\mathfrak{a}=\left\langle{v,v_{1},v_{2}}\,\big|\,{[v_{1},v],[v_{2},v_{1}]+v_{1}^{[2]},[v_{2},v]+v^{[2]}}\right\rangle

Let
\mathfrak{b}
be its standard subalgebra generated by
v_{1}+Tv
and
v_{2}
.
Because
[v_{2},v_{1}+Tv]=v_{1}^{[2]}+Tv^{[2]}
and
(v_{1}+Tv)^{[2]}=v_{1}^{[2]}+T^{2}v^{[2]}
are linearly independent, one has
\mathfrak{b}_{2}=\mathbb{F}_{4}\cdot v^{[2]}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{4}\cdot v_{1}^{[2]}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{4}\cdot v_{2}^{[2]}=\mathfrak{a}_{2}
. Therefore
\mathfrak{b}_{2}
has the same dimension as the degree-
2
component of the free restricted Lie algebra on two generators, and we deduce that
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{b}
is free. Nevertheless, the equality
[v_{2},(v_{1}+Tv)^{[2]}]=0
implies that
\mathfrak{b}
cannot be free, and
\operatorname{q}\!\mathfrak{b}\neq\mathfrak{b}
. Notice that, since
(v_{1}+Tv)^{[2]}=v_{1}^{[2]}+T^{2}v^{[2]}
, and
[v_{1},+Tv,v_{2}]=v_{1}^{[2]}+Tv^{[2]}
, one has
\mathfrak{b}\cap\langle{v}\rangle=\langle{v^{[2]}}\rangle
, i.e., it is zero in degree
1
and contains
v^{[2]}
.

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is Koszul, its cohomology is the associative algebra with presentation

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{F}_{4})=\left\langle{a,b,c}\,\big|\,{ab+ba,ac+ca,bc+cb,c^{2},ac+a^{2},bc+b^{2}}\right\rangle_{\text{alg}}

where
(a,b,c)
is the dual basis to the basis
(v,v_{1},v_{2})
. Although we already know that
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{F}_{4})
is not universally Koszul, as
\mathfrak{a}
is not Bloch-Kato, we prove this explicitly. Consider the ideal
I
generated by the element
x=Ta+b
, i.e.,
I
is the ideal generated by the elements
f\in H^{1}(\mathfrak{a},\mathbb{F}_{4})=\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\ast}
such that
f|_{\mathfrak{b}_{1}}\equiv 0
.

The elements
xa,xb,xc
are linearly independent, whence
I
has no relation of degree
2
. Nevertheless,

\displaystyle a(Tb+c)x

\displaystyle=(Tab+ac)(Ta+b)=

\displaystyle=T^{2}a^{2}b+Tab^{2}+Ta^{2}c+acb=

\displaystyle=(1+T)acb+Tabc+Tac^{2}+acb=

\displaystyle=2abc=0.

Since
a(Tb+c)\neq 0
, we deduce that
\operatorname{Tor}_{1,3}^{A}(I,\mathbb{F}_{4})\neq 0
, and hence
I
is not a quadratic
A
-module (of degree
1
). The same computations show that the T-RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{4}}k
over
k
contains non-quadratic standard subalgebras.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a simple Droms graph, and let
Y
be a mixed graph consisting of a single vertex
y
, which is a negative vertex not lying in
\Gamma^{\prime}
. Consider the special cone
\Gamma=\nabla_{\theta}(\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup Y)
on the mixed graph
\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup Y
, where
\theta:V(\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup Y)\to k
is the signature with
\theta(y)=1
and
\theta|_{V(\Gamma^{\prime})}\equiv 0
. Then, the T-RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
over
k
is Bloch-Kato.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, all the standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
are T-RAAGs, where
v
is the tip of the cone
\Gamma=\nabla(\Gamma_{v})
, and
\Gamma_{v}=\Gamma^{\prime}\sqcup Y
.

Let
\mathfrak{m}
be a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
. If
\mathfrak{m}
is contained in
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
, then
\mathfrak{m}
is a T-RAAG Lie algebra. So, assume that
v
appears with non-trivial coefficient in the linear expression of some element of degree
1
of
\mathfrak{m}
. By Lemma
4.9
,
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
is the quotient
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)/(v)
; let
\pi
be the corresponding projection.

We can choose the generators
v+u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
of
\mathfrak{m}
so that the
u_{i}
’s are elements of
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
. Consider the Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{u_{0},\dots,u_{n}}\rangle
of the restricted RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma_{v})
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{h}=\pi(\mathfrak{m})
. Clearly, if
u_{0}
lies in the linear span of the
u_{i}
’s for
i\geq 1
, then
\mathfrak{m}\simeq\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{k}
. We can thus focus on the contrary.

Since Droms theorem holds true for restricted RAAG Lie algebras over arbitrary fields (Lemma
5.3
),
\mathfrak{h}
is a restricted RAAG Lie algebra. Moreover, up to a Gauss elimination procedure, we can assume that
y
only appears in one of the
u_{i}
’s, say
u_{i_{0}}
. In particular, by Kurosh theorem
[
3
]
,
\mathfrak{h}
is the free product of the free restricted Lie algebra generated by
u_{i_{0}}
and its subalgebra
\langle{u_{0},\dots,\hat{u}_{i_{0}},\dots,u_{n}}\rangle
, where
\hat{u}_{i_{0}}
means that the generator
u_{i_{0}}
is omitted.

Consider the map
f:\mathfrak{h}\to\mathfrak{m}
given by
f(u_{0})=v+u_{0}
and
f(u_{i})=u_{i}
for
i>0
. We need to check well-definedness. After that, notice that
f
is the inverse of
\pi|_{\mathfrak{m}}
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{h}\simeq\mathfrak{m}
.

If
i_{0}=0
, then
\mathfrak{h}=\langle{u_{0}}\rangle\amalg\langle{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}}\rangle
, and
f
is well-defined by the universal property of the free product.

If
i_{0}>0
, then
f
is well-defined as
[v,u_{i}]=0
for all
i\neq i_{0}
, and no relation involves
u_{i_{0}}
.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
k
be a field containing the field
\mathbb{F}_{4}
of
4
elements, and let
\Gamma
be a mixed graph. Then the T-RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
over
k
is BK if, and only if,
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph, and all the directed edges in any connected component of
\Gamma
have a common origin.

Proof.

First suppose that
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is BK. Then
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph by Proposition
5.1
and Example
5.2
.

Let us assume, without loss of generality, that
\Gamma
is connected and has two different directed edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in D
. If, by contradiction
o(e_{1})\neq o(e_{2})
, then there are two cases to consider.

(1) If
t(e_{1})=t(e_{2})
, then either
\Lambda_{s}
or the graph of Example
5.5
is induced in
\Gamma
, proving that
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is not BK.

(2) If
t(e_{1})\neq t(e_{2})
, then
e_{1}\cap e_{2}=\emptyset
. Moreover, there exists a positive vertex
v
that is adjacent to all the other vertices of
\Gamma
. We may assume that
v\notin e_{2}
, so that the induced subgraph spanned by
v
and
e_{2}
is the graph described in Example
5.5
, contradicting the assumption on
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
.

Now assume that
\Gamma
is a Droms mixed graph, and all directed edges in any connected component have a common origin. We argue by induction as usual. If
\Gamma
is disconnected, by induction,
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is a free product of BK Lie algebras, and hence it is BK.

We can thus suppose that
\Gamma
is connected, and hence there is a positive vertex
v
that is central in
\Gamma
. Clearly, if
D\neq\emptyset
, then
v
is the common origin of all the edges
e\in D
. Since
\Gamma
is a special mixed graph, the graph
\Gamma_{v}
induced by the vertices
\neq v
is a disjoint union of a simple Droms graph and a discrete set of negative vertices, i.e.,
\Gamma
is obtained by attaching a star
\vec{\Sigma}_{n+1}
at
v
to the subgraph spanned by all the positive vertices. In view of Example
4.10
, we can assume that
\Gamma
has a single negative vertex, and hence, by induction and Lemma
5.6
, we deduce that
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
is BK.
∎

Example 5.8
.

The graph
\Gamma
with realization

is obtained by taking the cone of the disjoint union of two simple Droms graphs (the fish-shaped graph
\Phi
, and the complete graph
K_{4}
on
4
vertices), and then attaching a directed star
\vec{\Sigma}_{4}
to the tip of the cone, i.e.,
\Gamma=\nabla(\Phi\sqcup K_{4}\sqcup 4N)
, where
4N
is the discrete mixed graph consisting of four negative vertices. The associated T-RAAG Lie algebra over a field containing
\mathbb{F}_{4}
is Bloch-Kato, and all of its standard subalgebras are T-RAAGs.

6.
RACG Lie algebras

For a simple graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
we define the
right-angled Coxeter

Lie algebra
, or RACG Lie algebra:

\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)=\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{u^{[2]},\ [v,w]:\ u\in V,\ \{v,w\}\in E}\right\rangle

It can be seen as the quotient of the T-RAAG Lie algebra on the mixed graph
\Gamma=(V,E,D,o,t)
with
D=\emptyset
, with respect to the ideal generated by
v^{[2]}
, for all canonical generators
v
. The ordinary Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
generated by its canonical generators is the ordinary RAAG Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}
.

Example 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be complete. Then,
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)=\left\langle{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}}\,\big|\,{v_{i}^{[2]},[v_{i},v_{j}]}\right\rangle
is elementary abelian, and hence
u(\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma))
is the exterior algebra on an
n
-dimensional vector space. In particular,
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is Koszul, and its cohomology is the symmetric algebra on the dual vector space
c(\Gamma)_{1}^{\ast}
. Notice that the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of the underlying ordinary Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is the exterior algebra on
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)^{\ast}
.

It follows from Lemma
2.4
that if
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is BK, then so is
\mathfrak{c}(\nabla(\Gamma))\simeq\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
.

For RACG, the BK property is not equivalent to the fact that all standard subalgebras are RACG. For instance, if
\Gamma
is discrete with two vertices, then the subalgebra generated by the sum of the canonical generators is not elementary abelian, as it should be if it were a RACG (on a singleton graph).
For this we introduce a new class of restricted Lie algebras interpolating between RAAG and RACG Lie algebras.

If
\Gamma
is a graph, and
\theta:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
is a vertex labelling of
\Gamma
, the
extended right-angled
Lie algebra (or ERA) over
k
associated to the pair
(\Gamma,\theta)
is the restricted Lie
k
-algebra with presentation

\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,\theta)=\left\langle{V}\,\big|\,{\theta(v)v^{[2]},[v_{1},v_{2}]:\ v\in V,\ \{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E}\right\rangle

For instance, if
\theta\equiv 1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,1)=\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
. On the contrary, when
\theta\equiv 0
, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,0)
is the (restricted) RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a simple graph and
\theta:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
a
2
-labelling of
\Gamma
. Let
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
be the associated ERA Lie
k
-algebra.

(1)

If
W\subset V
, then the subalgebra of
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
generated by
W
is the ERA Lie algebra associated to the induced subgraph spanned by
W
, with signature
\theta|_{W}
;

(2)

The ERA Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is Koszul.

Proof.

Let
W
be a proper subset of
V
. Clearly, the map induced by
v\in W\mapsto v
and
v\in V\setminus W\mapsto 0
is a retraction of that induced by the inclusion
W\hookrightarrow V
; hence (1) follows.

We argue by induction on the number of vertices.
If
\Gamma
is complete, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is the abelian restricted Lie algebra
k^{n}\times\mathfrak{k}^{m}
, where
n=|{\theta^{-1}(1_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})}|
. In particular,
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma))
is the symmetric tensor product of a symmetric algebra and an exterior algebra, which is thus Koszul. Moreover, the subalgebra of
e(\Gamma)
generated by
W
is the ERA Lie algebra on the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by
W
.

So, suppose
\Gamma
is not complete, and let
v\in V\setminus W
. Denote by
\Gamma_{v}
the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by the vertices
\neq v
. By induction,
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})
is a Koszul restricted Lie algebra.

If
v
is a central vertex of
\Gamma
, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)\simeq\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})\times\langle{v}\rangle
, proving that
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})
naturally embeds into
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
. Moreover,
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma))
is the symmetric tensor product of
H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v}))
and
H^{\bullet}(\langle{v}\rangle)
— which are generated in degree
1
by induction—, implying that
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is Koszul by
[
16
]
.

If
v
is not a central vertex, let
\Delta
be the induced subgraph of
\Gamma
spanned by the link of
v
, i.e.,
V(\Delta)
consists of the elements
v^{\prime}\in V\setminus\{v\}
such that
\{v,v^{\prime}\}\in E
. Let
w
be a vertex of
\Gamma_{v}
that does not lie in
\Delta
.
By induction,
\mathfrak{e}(\Delta)
naturally embeds into
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})
. Moreover, the map
\phi:\mathfrak{e}(\Delta)\to\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})
defined on the canonical generators by
\phi(u)=[w,u]
is a derivation of degree
1
, and the associated HNN-extension is isomorphic with
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
. This proves both that
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v})
naturally embeds into
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
, and that, by induction and Theorem
2.8
,
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is Koszul.
∎

This allows us to compute the cohomology ring of ERA Lie algebras.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a finite simple graph, and let
\theta:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}\subseteq k
be a vertex
2
-labelling of
\Gamma
.
Then,

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,\theta),k)\simeq k[V^{\ast}]/(v^{\ast}w^{\ast},(u^{\ast})^{2}:\ \{v,w\}\notin E,\ v\neq w,\ u\in V\text{ s.t. }\theta(u)=0)

In particular, for
\theta\equiv 1
, we get the cohomology ring of RACG Lie algebras.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a simple graph. Then, the cohomology ring of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is the Stanley-Reisner ring of
\Gamma
, i.e.,

H^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma),k)=\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)^{!}\simeq k[\Gamma]:=k[V^{\ast}]/(v^{\ast}w^{\ast}:\ \{v,w\}\notin E,v\neq w)

Since the RACG Lie algebras (of non-empty graphs) contain non-trivial torsion elements, their cohomological dimension is infinite.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph with a
2
-labelling map
\theta:V\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a Droms
2
-labelled graph;

(2)

The graph
\Gamma
is Droms, and, for all connected induced subgraphs
\Lambda
of
\Gamma
, either
\theta|_{V(\Lambda)}\equiv 0
, or there exists a central vertex
v
of
\Lambda
with
\theta(v)=1
;

(3)

The ERA Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is BK;

(4)

The ERA Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is weakly BK;

(5)

All the standard subalgebras of the ERA Lie algebra
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
are ERA Lie algebras;

Proof.

The implication (1)
\implies
(2) is Lemma
3.2
.

Clearly, (5) implies both (3) and (4) by Proposition
6.2
(2).
If
\Gamma
is not a Droms labelled graph, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
contains a non-quadratic standard subalgebra by Examples
7.1
,
7.2
. This proves that any one of (3)-(5) implies (1).

It remains to show that (2) implies (5).

We argue by induction on the number of vertices of
\Gamma
. The base case being trivial, we suppose that
\Gamma
has at least two vertices.

Let
\Gamma
be disconnected with one connected component
\Gamma_{1}
, and disjoint decomposition
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
. By induction, both the
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{i})
’s satisfy (2) and (3), and
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)=\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{1})\amalg\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{2})
.
It thus follows from the Kurosh subalgebra theorem
[
3
]
that if
\mathfrak{m}
is a standard restricted subalgebra of
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
, then there exist graphs
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, maps
\theta_{i}:V(\Lambda_{i})\to\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and a free Lie algebra
\mathfrak{f}
, such that
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{f}\amalg\mathfrak{e}(\Lambda_{1},\theta_{1})\amalg\mathfrak{e}(\Lambda_{2},\theta_{2})
. Finally, notice that
\mathfrak{f}
is the ERA Lie algebra associated to a discrete graph
\Delta
with
\theta_{\Delta}:V(\Delta)\to\{0\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and hence the disjoint union
\Delta\sqcup\Lambda_{1}\sqcup\Lambda_{2}
is a defining graph of
\mathfrak{m}
, where the signature is defined in the obvious way.

Suppose now
\Gamma
is connected. If
\theta\equiv 0
, then
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma)
is the RAAG Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}(\Gamma)
, which is BK when
\Gamma
is a Droms graph.
On the other hand, if
\theta\not\equiv 0
, then there exists a central vertex
v
of
\Gamma
with
\theta(v)=1
, and hence
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,\theta)\simeq\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v},\theta^{\prime})\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
, where
\theta^{\prime}
is the restriction of
\theta
to the induced subgraph
\Gamma_{v}
spanned by the vertices
\neq v
.
If
\mathfrak{m}
is a standard subalgebra of
\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma,\theta)\simeq\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma_{v},\theta^{\prime})\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
, then we argue as in Lemma
2.4
, and prove that there exists a standard subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{e}(\Gamma_{v},\theta^{\prime})
such that
\mathfrak{m}
is isomorphic to either
\mathfrak{h}
or
\mathfrak{h}\times\mathfrak{k}/\mathfrak{k}^{[2]}
. By induction,
\mathfrak{h}
is an ERA Lie algebra, proving that the same is true for
\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

In particular, this applies to RACG Lie algebras.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
be the RACG Lie algebra over
k
associated to a simple graph
\Gamma
. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\Gamma
is a Droms graph;

(2)

\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is a BK restricted Lie algebra;

(3)

All the standard restricted subalgebras of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
are ERA Lie algebras.

(4)

All the ordinary standard subalgebras of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
are RAAG Lie algebras (
[
3
]
).

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
V
be a
k
-vector space with basis
(v_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}
, and let
\lambda:T(V)\to\Lambda(V)
and
\sigma:T(V)\to S(V)
be the natural projections. Let
E\subseteq\left\{{v_{i}\otimes v_{j}}\ \big|\ {1\leq i,j\leq n}\right\}
. Then,

\Lambda(V)/\lambda(E)
is universally Koszul iff
S(V)/\sigma(E)
is universally Koszul.

Proof.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E^{\prime})
, where
E^{\prime}=\left\{{\{v_{i},v_{j}\}}\ \big|\ {v_{i}\otimes v_{j}\in E}\right\}
. Then, the RACG
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is a Bloch-Kato restricted Lie algebra if, and only if, the RAAG
\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}
is a Bloch-Kato (ordinary) Lie algebra. In other words, the restricted cohomology of
\mathfrak{c}(\Gamma)
is universally Koszul precisely when the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of
\mathscr{L}_{\Gamma}
is universally Koszul.
∎

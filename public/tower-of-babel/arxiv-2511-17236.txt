The Star Product of Uniformly Random Codes
Johan Vester Dinesen, Ragnar Freij-Hollanti, Camilla HollantiBenjamin Jany, Alberto Ravagnani

Abstract
We consider the problem of determining the expected dimension of the star product of two uniformly random linear codes that are not necessarily of the same dimension. We achieve this by establishing a correspondence between the star product and the evaluation of bilinear forms, which we use to provide a lower bound on the expected star product dimension. We show that asymptotically in both the field size q q and the dimensions of the two codes, the expected dimension reaches its maximum. Lastly, we discuss some implications related to private information retrieval, secure distributed matrix multiplication, quantum error correction, and the potential for exploiting the results in cryptanalysis.

Introduction

The
star product
, also called the
Schur
or
Hadamard product
, between subspaces
U,V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is the subspace spanned by the component-wise products of vectors from
U
and
V
; see
[
31
]
. This operation is attracting increasing attention because of its connections with several areas of coding theory, information theory, and cryptography.

In the context of quantum error correction, star products have been used to construct stabilizer codes
[
38
,
39
,
4
]
and to give an algebraic characterization of binary CSS-T codes
[
6
]
. In secure distributed matrix multiplication (SDMM) and private information retrieval (PIR), the algebraic structure of star products controls communication complexity and security guarantees
[
16
,
26
]
. The star product also appears naturally in secret sharing and secure multiparty computation
[
12
,
11
]
, and in linear exact repair schemes for distributed storage
[
17
,
24
]
. In code-based cryptography, star products appear in attacks through distinguishers
[
9
,
20
,
29
]
, mostly via squares of codes, i.e., a star product of a code with itself. These rely on certain structured codes like generalized Reed–Solomon (GRS) codes typically having a much lower dimension than random codes when starred with themselves. A well-known exception to this is the (binary) Goppa code that can withstand such distinguishing attacks with carefully chosen parameters
[
29
]
.

The properties of star products have been studied in various algebraic settings
[
35
]
. For special classes of codes, particularly evaluation codes such as Reed-Solomon codes and algebraic geometry codes, star products are well understood and have proven useful in the construction of decoding algorithms
[
32
,
10
]
. A notable contribution in this direction is the analysis of the square of a random linear code,
\mathcal{C}^{2}=\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{C}
, where asymptotic estimates of its dimension were derived
[
7
]
.

In this paper, we study the expected dimension of the star product of two independent, uniformly random codes.
More precisely, we investigate the typical behavior of
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})
,
where
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are independent, uniformly random linear codes of prescribed dimensions.
Extending the ideas of
[
7
]
,
our approach establishes an explicit formula for the expected size of the kernel of a linear map that takes a bilinear form to a star product of codes.
Based on this formula, we obtain an explicit lower bound on
\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]
for all parameter choices. We then determine the asymptotics of the bound both as field size
q
and the dimensions of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
grow.

In the second part of the paper, we discuss the implications of these results for private information retrieval applications and secure distributed matrix multiplication, as well as the potential of the results obtained to be utilized in distinguisher-type attacks in code-based cryptography.

The remainder of this paper is outlined as follows. Section
2
recalls the coding theory concepts needed throughout the paper. Section
3
develops the connection between star products and bilinear forms. In Section
4
and Section
5
, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of the star product, first as the field size grows and then as the code dimensions increase. In both cases, we conclude that the expected dimension attains its maximum value. Finally, in Section
6
we discuss the implications of these results for certain applications.

2
Preliminaries on linear codes

Throughout this paper,
\mathbb{F}
denotes a field,
q
a prime power, and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the finite field of
q
elements. We denote subsets by
\subseteq
and subspaces by
\leq
. Vectors are row vectors unless otherwise specified.
We let
A^{(i)}
denote the
i
th column
of a matrix
A
. The natural numbers are
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
q
-binomial coefficient
is

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{q}=\frac{(1-q^{n})(1-q^{n-1})\ldots(1-q^{n-k+1})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\ldots(1-q^{k})},

which evaluates to 0 when
k>n
. When both the numerator and denominator are empty products, we set the quantity to 1. We will also allow negative inputs for both
q
-binomials and binomials, but in that case they are defined to be 0.

We start by recalling some notions from coding theory. For general reference, we refer the reader to
[
23
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
linear code
is a non-zero
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. The
minimum Hamming distance
of
\mathcal{C}
is

d(\mathcal{C})=\min\{w(c)\mid c\in\mathcal{C},\;c\neq 0\},

where
w
denotes the Hamming weight on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
.

For the remainder of this paper, all codes are assumed to be linear. We recall some other coding theory concepts in passing.

The
support
of a code
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C})=\{i\in[n]\mid\text{ there exists }c\in\mathcal{C}\text{ with }c_{i}\neq 0\}.

\mathcal{C}
is called
degenerate
if
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C})\neq[n]
. For
I\subseteq[n]
,
\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C})
is the code obtained by projecting
\mathcal{C}
onto the coordinates indexed by
I
, that is,
\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C})=\{(c_{i})_{i\in I}\mid c\in\mathcal{C}\}
.

A
generator matrix
of a code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with dimension
\dim\mathcal{C}=k
is a
k\times n
matrix
G
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\mathrm{rowsp}\,G=\mathcal{C}
. A generator matrix is in
systematic form
if
G=[I_{k}\mid A]
for some matrix
A
. A
parity-check matrix
of
\mathcal{C}
is a matrix
H\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{(n-k)\times n}
such that
\mathcal{C}=\ker H^{\top}
. We say that codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
are
monomially equivalent
if there exists a monomial
n\times n
matrix
M
such that if
G_{1}
is a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, then
G_{1}M
is a generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

All codes
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
satisfy the
Singleton bound
:
\dim\mathcal{C}\leq n-d(\mathcal{C})+1
. Codes that meet the Singleton bound with equality are called
Maximum Distance Separable
(
MDS
) codes. Note that MDS codes are always non-degenerate.

The key player of this paper is the star product.

Definition 2.2
.

The
star product
of
vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is their component-wise multiplication, denoted by
x\star y=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\star(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=(x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{n}y_{n})
.
The
star product

of linear codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}

is the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear span of the pairwise star products of two codewords, that is,

\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}=\langle x\star y\mid x\in\mathcal{C}_{1},y\in\mathcal{C}_{2}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}.

The star product is also called

Schur product
or
Hadamard product
.
In general, it is difficult to determine the dimension of the star product of two codes. A trivial upper bound is
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq\min\{\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\dim\mathcal{C}_{2},n\}
since fixing bases of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, and then taking all pairwise star products between these basis vectors yields a spanning set for
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
(and in general not a basis).
Other bounds were derived with additional assumptions on either of the two codes.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
33
]
, Lemma 5)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be non-degenerate codes. Then,

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\geq\min\{n,\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}+d(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp})-2,\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}+d(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp})-2\}.

This lower bound highlights the role of the dual distance of one code in affecting the dimension of the star product. In the special case where one of the two codes is MDS, the dual distance attains its maximum possible value, allowing for a stronger bound. Since the dual of an MDS code is also MDS, the following result holds.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
35
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be non-degenerate codes. If at least one of the codes is MDS, then

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\geq\min\{n,\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}+\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}-1\}.

For sufficiently large field sizes, we expect the conditions of Proposition
2.4
to hold with high probability, since random codes are then likely to be MDS. This result will be necessary to prove Proposition
4.4
.

3
Star product and bilinear forms

Star products and bilinear forms are closely related to one another, as shown in
[
35
]
. In this section, we show that the star product of two codes, denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
, is the evaluation of all bilinear forms at points corresponding to the columns of the generator matrices of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. To make this relation precise, we first recall the definition of a bilinear form.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
and
W
be
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces. A map
\varphi\,:\,V\times W\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
is a bilinear form if for all
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
,
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
, and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}
, the following hold:

1)

\varphi(v_{1}+v_{2},w_{1})=\varphi(v_{1},w_{1})+\varphi(v_{2},w_{1})
,

2)

\varphi(v_{1},w_{1}+w_{2})=\varphi(v_{1},w_{1})+\varphi(v_{1},w_{2})
,

3)

\varphi(\lambda v_{1},w_{1})=\varphi(v_{1},\lambda w_{1})=\lambda\varphi(v_{1},w_{1})
.

We let
\mathrm{Bil}(V,W)
denote the set of bilinear forms on
V\times W
. It is well known that
\mathrm{Bil}(V,W)
is an
\mathbb{F}
-vector space of dimension
\dim(V)\dim(W)
.

In the sequel we work with positive integers
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
and let
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\mathrm{Bil}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}},\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}})
. Recall that there is a one-to-one correspondence between bilinear forms
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}
and matrices
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}\times k_{2}}
. We now state the relation between star products and bilinear forms, which was established in
[
35
]
. We provide a proof for completeness.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
35
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with generator matrices
G_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}\times n}
and
G_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}\times n}
. Define the linear map

\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}\,:\,\mathcal{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n},\quad\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}(\varphi)=\left(\varphi\left(G_{1}^{(1)},G_{2}^{(1)}\right),\ldots,\varphi\left(G_{1}^{(n)},G_{2}^{(n)}\right)\right).

Then,
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}=\mathrm{im}(\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}})
and

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=k_{1}k_{2}-\log_{q}(|\ker\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}|).

Proof.

It is immediate from the definition that
\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map. Furthermore,
\mathcal{B}
is generated by the bilinear forms
\Omega=\{\varphi_{ij}\,:\,1\leq i\leq k_{1},\,1\leq j\leq k_{2}\}
, where
\varphi_{ij}(v,w)=v_{i}w_{j}
. Let

G_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\\
\alpha_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{k_{1}}\end{bmatrix},\quad G_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\beta_{1}\\
\beta_{2}\\
\vdots\\
\beta_{k_{2}}\end{bmatrix},

where
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{j}
denote the
i
th row of
G_{1}
and
j
th row of
G_{2}
, respectively. Then for any
\varphi_{i,j}\in\Omega
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}(\varphi_{ij})=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{i,1}\beta_{j,1}&\alpha_{i,2}\beta_{j,2}&\ldots&\alpha_{i,n}\beta_{i,n}\end{bmatrix}=\alpha_{i}\star\beta_{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2},

so that
\mathrm{im}(\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

Now consider
c\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
be such that
c=\alpha\star\beta
. Then, for some scalars
\lambda_{i,j}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we can write

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k_{1}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq k_{2}}\lambda_{ij}(\alpha_{i}\star\beta_{j})

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k_{1}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq k_{2}}\lambda_{ij}\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}(\varphi_{ij})

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}\left(\sum_{1\leq i\leq k_{1}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq k_{2}}\lambda_{ij}\varphi_{ij}\right),

where (
3.2
) follows by (
3.1
), and (
3.3
) follows by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearity of
\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}
.
Hence
c\star c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{im}(\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}})
, which shows that
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathrm{im}(\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}})
and thus the two spaces are equal.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Note that different pairs of generator matrices
(G_{1},G_{2})
and
(G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2})
for the codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
induce in general different maps
\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}
and
\psi_{G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}}
, as defined in Theorem
3.2
. However, the image of those two maps remains the same since they are both equal to
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is monomially equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime}
, then
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
is also monomially equivalent to
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Therefore, in the rest of the article we always choose a generator matrix in systematic form and denote the corresponding map introduced in Theorem
3.2
by
\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}
.

By Theorem
3.2
, one can determine the dimension of the star product between two codes by counting the number of elements in the kernel of the map
\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}
, for some given generator matrices
G_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}\times n}
and
G_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}\times n}
both in systematic form. To estimate this kernel, we can count the number of bilinear forms
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}
of rank
r
such that
\varphi(e_{i}^{1},e_{i}^{2})=0
for
i\in[k_{1}]
, or equivalently the number of
k_{1}\times k_{2}
matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of rank
r
with zero diagonal. This is a known problem in enumerative combinatorics
[
22
,
36
]
.
We introduce notation that
will be used in the remainder of the article.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
r,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}
,
I\subseteq K\coloneqq[k_{2}]\setminus[k_{1}]
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We then define the following sets:

\displaystyle S_{r}^{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{A\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}\times k_{2}}\,\middle|\,\mathrm{rk}(A)=r,\;A_{i,i}=0\text{ for all }i\in[k_{1}]\right\},

\displaystyle S_{r,I}^{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{A\in S_{r}^{k_{1},k_{2}}\,\middle|\,A^{(i)}=0\text{ for all }i\in I,A^{(\ell)}\neq 0\text{ for all }\ell\in K\setminus I\right\},

\displaystyle S^{k_{1},k_{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigcup_{r=0}^{k_{1}}S_{r}^{k_{1},k_{2}}.

Since
|S_{r,I}^{k_{1},k_{2}}|=|S_{r,J}^{k_{1},k_{2}}|
for
I,J\subseteq K
when
|I|=|J|
we let
S_{r,\ell}^{k_{1},k_{2}}\coloneqq|S_{r,I}^{k_{1},k_{2}}|
, where
I\subseteq K
with
|I|=\ell
. The subscripts and superscripts
k_{1},k_{2}
may be omitted if they are clear from context.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
r,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
r\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}
and
I\subseteq K
. Let

\theta(i,j)=\binom{k_{1}}{i}(q-1)^{i}(-1)^{r-j}q^{jk_{2}+\binom{r-j}{2}}.

Then

|S^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{r}|=q^{-k_{1}}\sum^{k_{1}}_{i=0}\left(\sum^{k_{1}}_{j=0}\theta(i,j)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}\right),

and

|S^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{r,I}|=\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}q^{-k_{1}}\Bigg(\sum^{k_{1}}_{i=0}\Bigg(\sum^{k_{1}}_{j=0}\theta(i,j)q^{-j|J|}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}\Bigg)\Bigg).

Proof.

The first part of the statement is
[
36
, Corollary 60]
.
For the second part, define

\displaystyle f(I)\coloneqq|S^{k_{1},k_{2}}_{r,I}|,\qquad g(I)\coloneqq\left|\left\{A\in S_{r}^{k_{1},k_{2}}\,\middle|\,A^{(i)}=0\text{ for all }i\in I\right\}\right|.

Clearly
g(I)=\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}f(J)
for any
I\subseteq K
. Hence, by Möbius inversion we obtain

f(I)=\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}g(J).

Using the first part of the statement we determine
g(J)
for any
J\subseteq K
as

\displaystyle g(J)

\displaystyle=|S_{r}^{k_{1},k_{2}-|J|}|

\displaystyle=q^{-k_{1}}\sum^{k_{1}}_{j=0}\binom{k_{1}}{j}(q-1)^{j}\left(\sum^{k_{1}}_{l=0}(-1)^{r-l}q^{l(k_{2}-|J|)+\binom{r-l}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{l}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-l}{k_1-r}_{q}\right),

and the result follows.
∎

The next result counts the number of zeros of a bilinear form, defined as follows.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}
.
An element
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}
is
a zero
of
\varphi
if
\varphi(v_{1},v_{2})=0
.
Denote by
Z(\varphi)
the number of zeros of
\varphi
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}
be of rank
r
. Then
Z(\varphi)=(q^{r}+q-1)q^{k_{1}+k_{2}-r-1}
and
Z
is a monotonically decreasing function in
r
.

Proof.

Let
A
be the matrix representation of
\varphi
. Then
\mathrm{rk}(A)=r
.

\displaystyle Z(\varphi)

\displaystyle=|\{(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\,\colon\,v_{1}Av_{2}^{\top}=0\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}}|\{v_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\,:\,(v_{1}A)v_{2}^{\top}=0\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\\
v_{1}A=0\end{subarray}}|\{v_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\,:\,(v_{1}A)v_{2}^{\top}=0\}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\\
v_{1}A\neq 0\end{subarray}}|\{v_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\,:\,(v_{1}A)v_{2}^{\top}=0\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\\
v_{1}A=0\end{subarray}}q^{k_{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\\
v_{1}A\neq 0\end{subarray}}q^{k_{2}-1}

\displaystyle=q^{k_{1}-r}q^{k_{2}}+(q^{k_{1}}-q^{k_{1}-r})q^{k_{2}-1}=(q^{r}+q-1)q^{k_{1}+k_{2}-r-1}.

In the third equality
|\{v_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\,:\,(v_{1}A)v_{2}^{\top}=0\}|=q^{k_{2}-1}
for
v_{1}A\neq 0
, since the linear map
v_{1}A\,:\,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
v_{2}\mapsto v_{1}Av_{2}^{\top}
is surjective of rank
1
, and thus has a kernel of dimension
k_{2}-1
.
In the last equality, we have that
\{v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}}\,:\,v_{1}A=0\}
is the left kernel of
A
, which has dimension
k_{1}-r
since
A
has rank
r
. Lastly,
Z
is monotonically decreasing as
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}r}Z=-\ln(q)(q-1)q^{-r+k_{2}+k_{1}+1}
is negative.
∎

We now proceed to compute the expected size of the kernel of the map
\psi_{G_{1},G_{2}}
, where the generator matrices
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are chosen at random according to the model defined below.

Definition 3.8
.

In this paper, a uniformly random code
\mathcal{C}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
of dimension
k
is constructed by choosing uniformly at random and independently the last
n-k
columns of a systematic generator matrix.

The authors of
[
7
]
explicitly discuss alternative probabilistic models for choosing a random code and argue that their results apply more broadly than just the systematic generator matrix model used in Definition
3.8
. Specifically, as noted in their Remark 2.1 and in the discussion leading to Section 6, they show that the main conclusions regarding the square of a random code remain essentially unchanged under other standard distributions, such as choosing codes or generator matrices uniformly at random. Since our analysis for pairs of random codes (i.e., counting bilinear forms) follows the same combinatorial and algebraic principles, their arguments translate directly to our setting. Consequently, our results exhibit the same robustness with respect to the choice of random model, and we therefore confine our proofs to the model in Definition
3.8
for technical convenience.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Let
\gamma(j)=(q^{j}+q-1)q^{-j}
. Then the expected value of
|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|
is

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{r-j}\gamma(r)^{n-k_{2}}\gamma(j)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}(q-1)^{i}q^{jk_{2}-n+\binom{r-j}{2}}\binom{k_{1}}{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}\times n}
and
G_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}\times n}
be systematic generator matrices of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
, respectively. Let
\mathcal{X}_{j}
denote the set of all
k_{j}\times n
generator matrices in systematic form. For
\varphi\in\mathcal{B}
and
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}
we use a slight abuse of notation and write
\varphi(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=0
to mean
\varphi\left(\pi_{1}^{(i)},\pi_{2}^{(i)}\right)=0
for all
i\in[n]
. Then

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker(\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}})|\big]

\displaystyle=\sum^{q^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}_{i=0}i\cdot\mathbb{P}(|\ker(\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}})|=i)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{q^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}i\left|\big\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\,\colon\,|\{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}\,\colon\,\varphi(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=0\}|=i\big\}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}}|\{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}\,\colon\,\varphi(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=0\}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{B}}|\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{X}_{1}\times\mathcal{X}_{2}\,\colon\,\varphi(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})=0\}|

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{I\subseteq K}\sum_{A\in S_{r,I}}\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{k_{1}+k_{2}-r-1}\right)^{n-k_{2}}q^{k_{1}|I|}(q^{k_{1}-1})^{k_{2}-k_{1}-|I|}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{I\subseteq K}\sum_{A\in S_{r,I}}\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{k_{1}+k_{2}-r-1}\right)^{n-k_{2}}q^{(k_{2}-k_{1})(k_{1}-1)+|I|}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{(k_{1}+k_{2}-1)(n-k_{2})+(k_{2}-k_{1})(k_{1}-1)}}{q^{(n-k_{1})k_{1}+(n-k_{2})k_{2}}}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{I\subseteq K}\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{-r}\right)^{n-k_{2}}q^{|I|}|S_{r,I}|

\displaystyle=q^{k_{1}-n}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{I\subseteq K}\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{-r}\right)^{n-k_{2}}q^{|I|}\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}q^{-k_{1}}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}\sum^{k_{1}}_{i=0}\binom{k_{1}}{i}(q-1)^{i}\left(\sum^{k_{1}}_{j=0}(-1)^{r-j}q^{j(k_{2}-|J|)+\binom{r-j}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}\right),

(3.5)

\displaystyle=q^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}}\Bigg(\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{-r}\right)^{n-k_{2}}\binom{k_{1}}{i}(q-1)^{i}(-1)^{r-j}q^{jk_{2}+\binom{r-j}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}=}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}\sum_{I\subseteq K}\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}q^{|I|}q^{-j|J|}\Bigg),

where (
3.4
) follows by Lemma
3.7
and (
3.5
) follows by Lemma
3.5
. The last two summands of (
3.5
) can be further simplified using the Binomial Theorem:

\displaystyle\sum_{I\subseteq K}\sum_{I\subseteq J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|-|I|}q^{|I|}q^{-j|J|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|}q^{-j|J|}\sum_{I\subseteq J}(-1)^{|I|}q^{|I|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|}q^{-j|J|}\sum_{t=0}^{|J|}\binom{|J|}{t}(-q)^{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{J\subseteq K}(-1)^{|J|}q^{-j|J|}(1-q)^{|J|}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\binom{k_{2}-k_{1}}{t}(-q^{-j}+q^{-j+1})^{t}

\displaystyle=((q^{j}+q-1)q^{-j})^{k_{2}-k_{1}}.

Thus, we have that

\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker(\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}})|\big]=q^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}}(-1)^{r-j}\left((q^{r}+q-1)q^{-r}\right)^{n-k_{2}}((q^{j}+q-1)q^{-j})^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\\
(q-1)^{i}q^{jk_{2}+\binom{r-j}{2}}\binom{k_{1}}{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q},

and the result follows.
∎

In the case where
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
have the same dimension, we get the following value for
\mathbb{E}[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|]
.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimension
k=k_{1}=k_{2}
as in Definition
3.8
. Let
\gamma(j)=(q^{j}+q-1)q^{-j}
. Then

\mathbb{E}[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|]=\frac{1}{q^{n}}\sum_{r=0}^{k}\sum^{k}_{i=0}\sum^{\min\{r,k-i\}}_{j=0}(-1)^{r-j}\gamma(r)^{n-k}(q-1)^{i}q^{jk+\binom{r-j}{2}}\binom{k}{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k-j}{k-r}_{q}.

To conclude the section, we use Theorem
3.9
to give a lower bound on the expected dimension of two uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
. We also experimentally compare this bound to the approximate expected value, sampled by Monte-Carlo, for some parameters in Table
1
. See Appendix
A
for a comparison of the lower bound of Corollary
3.11
to the expected dimension for values
k_{1}=2,3
;
k_{2}=3,4
;
n=7,11,15
; and
q=2,3,5,7
. As we can see, even for low values our bound is appears to be good, and approaches the approximate expected value as any of the parameters increases.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Let
\gamma(j)=(q^{j}+q-1)q^{-j}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(3.6)

\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]\geq k_{1}k_{2}-\log_{q}\Bigg(\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}}(-1)^{r-j}\gamma(r)^{n-k_{2}}\gamma(j)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}(q-1)^{i}\\
q^{jk_{2}-n+\binom{r-j}{2}}\binom{k_{1}}{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-i}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}\Bigg).

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
we have
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=k_{1}k_{2}-\dim(\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}})=k_{1}k_{2}-\log_{q}\left(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\right)
. We then have by Jensen’s Inequality that

\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]=k_{1}k_{2}-\mathbb{E}\big[\log_{q}\left(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\right)\big]\geq k_{1}k_{2}-\log_{q}\left(\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\big]\right),

and the result then follows by Theorem
3.9
.
∎

4
Asymptotic behavior as the field size grows

In this section, we establish that the expected dimension of the star product is maximal with respect to the upper bound
\dim(C_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq\min\{n,\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\}
, when we let the field size
q
tend to infinity. We begin by computing the asymptotic behavior of the expected size of the kernel of the map
\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}
for two random codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
each of fixed dimension.
Recall that functions
f,g\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are
asymptotically equivalent
, denoted by
f\sim_{x}g
, if
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=1
;
see for instance
[
13
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|]=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}(1+q^{k_{1}k_{2}-n})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }k_{1}k_{2}<n,\\
2&\text{ if }k_{1}k_{2}=n,\\
\infty&\text{ if }k_{1}k_{2}>n.\end{cases}

Proof.

By Theorem
3.9
we know that

\mathbb{E}[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|]=q^{-n}\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}}\gamma(r)^{n-k_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{r,k_{1}-i\}}(-1)^{r-j}\binom{k_{1}}{i}f(i,j,r),

where
\gamma(r)=(q^{r}+q-1)q^{-r}
and

f(i,j,r)=\gamma(j)^{k_{2}-k_{1}}(q-1)^{i}q^{jk+2+\binom{r-j}{2}}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-1}{j}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_1-j}{k_1-r}_{q}.

We treat the summand for
r=0
and the others differently. For
r=0
only
j=0
contributes to the sum, so

\displaystyle q^{-n}\gamma(0)^{n-k_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}f(i,0)=q^{-n}q^{n-k_{2}}q^{k_{2}-k_{1}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{i}(q-1)^{i}=1.

For
r\geq 1
, note that the
q
-binomial satisfies
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{q}\sim_{q}q^{k(n-k)}
. Using this, one can verify that

f(i,j,r)\sim_{q}\begin{cases}q^{k_{2}-k_{1}+i-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r+k_{1}r}&\text{if }j=0,\\
2^{k_{2}-k_{1}}q^{i+jk_{2}-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r-\frac{1}{2}j^{2}+\frac{1}{2}j-ij+k_{1}r}&\text{if }j=1,\\
q^{i+jk_{2}-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r-\frac{1}{2}j^{2}+\frac{1}{2}j-ij+k_{1}r}&\text{if }j\geq 2.\end{cases}

Define

X(i,j,r)=\begin{cases}k_{2}-k_{1}+i-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r+k_{1}r&\text{if }j=0,\\
i+jk_{2}-\frac{1}{2}r^{2}-\frac{1}{2}r-\frac{1}{2}j^{2}+\frac{1}{2}j-ij+k_{1}r&\text{if }j\geq 1.\end{cases}

The leading term of

\sum_{r=1}^{k_{1}}\gamma(r)^{n-k_{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{1}}\sum_{j=0}^{\min\{r,k_{1}-i\}}(-1)^{r-j}\binom{k_{1}}{i}f(i,j,r),

comes from the maximum of
X(i,j,r)
over all allowed indices, which is

\max\big\{X(i,j,r)\mid 1\leq r\leq k_{1},\;0\leq i\leq k_{1},\;0\leq j\leq\min\{r,k_{1}-i\}\big\}=X(0,k_{1},k_{1})=k_{1}k_{2}.

Therefore we get

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|]

\displaystyle=1+\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}q^{-n}\gamma^{n-k_{2}}(-1)^{k_{1}-k_{1}}\binom{k_{1}}{0}q^{k_{1}k_{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}q^{k_{1}k_{2}-n},

which yields the desired result.
∎

With the above result, we are able to show the first main result of this section.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\})=1.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{im}(\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}})=\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
, we have that
|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|<q^{t}
if and only if
|\mathrm{im}\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|>q^{k_{1}k_{2}-t}
.
Hence
\mathbb{P}(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})>k_{1}k_{2}-t)=\mathbb{P}(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|<q^{t}).

Moreover, by Markov’s inequality, we get

\mathbb{P}(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|<q^{t})\geq 1-\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|\big]}{q^{t}}.

We now consider two cases. If
k_{1}k_{2}\leq n
, let
t=1
and observe that by Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|<q)

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}1-\frac{2}{q}=1.

If
k_{1}k_{2}>n
, let
t\coloneqq k_{1}k_{2}-n+1
and observe that, again by Theorem
4.1
,

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(|\ker\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}|<q^{k_{1}k_{2}-n+1})

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}1-\frac{1+q^{k_{1}k_{2}-n}}{q^{k_{1}k_{2}-n+1}}=1.

Putting everything together gives the desired statement.
∎

In the sequel, we show that when fixing the code
\mathcal{C}_{1}
to be MDS and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
to be uniformly random of a certain dimension relative to
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, the expected dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
behaves as if
\mathcal{C}_{1}
was a uniformly random code. We start with a lemma that can be shown with a standard application of Möbius inversion.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
n,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\leq n
and
I\subseteq[n]
. Then the number of
\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
\dim\mathcal{D}=\ell
and
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})=I
is

\sum^{|I|}_{i=\ell}(-1)^{|I|-i}\binom{|I|}{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{i}{\ell}_{q}.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be MDS with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}=k_{1}
. Fix
k_{2}\leq n
such that either
k_{2}=1
, or
k_{2}\geq n-k_{1}+1
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be a uniformly random code with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}=k_{2}
. Then

\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q^{n}-1}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}\min\{k_{1},i\}&\text{ if }k_{2}=1,\\
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}^{-1}_{q}\sum_{s=k_{2}}^{n}s\binom{n}{s}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s-k_{2}}(-1)^{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{s-i}{k_2}_{q}\binom{s}{s-i}\right)&\text{ if }k_{2}\geq n-k_{1}+1.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k_{2}=1
then
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=\min\{\dim\mathcal{C}_{1},|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|\}
. The result then follows by counting the number of
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with
|\mathrm{supp}(v)|=i
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, up to a scalar multiple.
For the rest of the proof assume
k_{2}\geq n-k_{1}+1
and let
I=\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})
.

Suppose
|I|>k_{1}
. Then
\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{1})
is an MDS code of dimension
k_{1}
and
\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2})
is non-degenerate with
\dim(\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))=k_{2}
. By Proposition
2.4
we have

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=\dim(\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{1})\star\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))\geq\min\{|I|,\dim(\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{1}))+\dim(\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2}))-1\}=|I|.

Furthermore,
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=\dim(\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime})\star\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}))\leq|I|
as
\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),\pi_{I}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{|I|}
, which proves
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|
for
|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|>k_{1}
.

Now suppose
|I|\leq k_{1}
. For
j\in I
choose
c^{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
such that
c^{j}_{j}=1
and
c^{j}_{i}=0
for any
i\in I\setminus\{j\}
. This is possible as
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is MDS with
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1})=k_{1}\geq|I|
. Similarly, choose
d^{j}\in\mathcal{C}_{2}
with
d^{j}_{j}=1
. Then
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}=\langle c^{j}\star d^{j}=e_{j}\;|\;j\in\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})\rangle
, so
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|
.

Lastly, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
let
\varphi(s)
denote the number of subspaces
\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
of dimension
\ell
and
|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|=s
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}=\ell\end{subarray}}|\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{C}_{2})|=\sum^{n}_{s=\ell}s\varphi(s),

and the result then follows by Lemma
4.3
.
∎

Note that Proposition
4.4
does not cover the cases where
2\leq\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq n-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}
. In the following example, we provide two monomially inequivalent MDS codes which yield different expected dimensions with respect to the star product for a value not covered by Proposition
4.4
. This also shows that the expected star product dimension is not an invariant under combinatorial equivalence, where by combinatorial equivalence we mean that the induced matroids of the linear codes are equivalent
[
30
]
.

Example 4.5
.

Consider the two
[6,3]_{7}
codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
defined by the generator matrices

G_{\mathcal{C}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&4&5&2\\
0&1&0&6&1&1\\
0&0&1&5&6&5\end{bmatrix},\quad G_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0&1&1&6\\
0&1&0&4&1&4\\
0&0&1&6&2&4\end{bmatrix}.

Both codes are MDS, so their underlying matroids are both uniform with the same parameters. If
\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{7}^{6}
is any code chosen uniformly at random with dimension
\dim\mathcal{D}=2
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{D}]=\frac{13138498}{2288417},

\displaystyle\quad\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\star\mathcal{D}]=\frac{13154050}{2288417}.

However, for the case of
\dim\mathcal{D}=3
, which is also not covered by Proposition
4.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{D}]=\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\star\mathcal{D}]=\frac{72051027}{12044300}.

The expected dimension under the star product when
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is fixed is however an invariant under monomial equivalence, as shown by the proposition below.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be monomially equivalent and
\ell\in[n]
. Let
\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be any code chosen uniformly at random with
\dim\mathcal{D}=\ell
. Then
\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{D}]=\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\star\mathcal{D}]
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is monomially equivalent under
M
, so
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{2}M
. Then,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim\mathcal{D}=\ell\end{subarray}}\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{D})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim\mathcal{D}=\ell\end{subarray}}\dim(\mathcal{C}_{2}M\star\mathcal{D})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim\mathcal{D}=\ell\end{subarray}}\dim(\mathcal{C}_{2}\star\mathcal{D}M)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\\
\dim\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=\ell\end{subarray}}\dim(\mathcal{C}_{2}\star\mathcal{D}^{\prime}),

as
M
is generalized permutation matrix.
∎

Lastly, we show that when
\mathcal{C}_{1}
is a fixed MDS code and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is a uniformly random code whose dimension satisfies either
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}=1
or
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}\geq n-\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}+1
, the expected dimension of their star product behaves asymptotically in
q
as it does when both
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
are uniformly random, as stated in Corollary
4.2
.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let the setup be as in Proposition
4.4
.
Then

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\})=1.

Proof.

We first show that
\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]=\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}
. Consider first the case
k_{2}=1
. By Proposition
4.4
then

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{q^{n}-1}\sum^{n}_{i=1}\binom{n}{i}(q-1)^{i}\min\{k_{1},i\}

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{i=1}\binom{n}{i}\min\{k_{1},i\}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(q-1)^{i}}{q^{n}-1}

\displaystyle=\min\{k_{1},n\}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(q-1)^{n}}{q^{n}-1}

\displaystyle=\min\{k_{1},n\}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{(1-\frac{1}{q})^{n}}{1-\frac{1}{q^{n}}}

\displaystyle=\min\{k_{1},n\}.

Similarly, for
k_{2}\geq n-k_{1}+1
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}^{-1}_{q}\sum_{s=k_{2}}^{n}s\binom{n}{s}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s-k_{2}}(-1)^{i}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{s-i}{k_2}_{q}\binom{s}{s-i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum^{n}_{s=k_{2}}\sum^{s-k_{2}}_{i=0}s\binom{n}{s}(-1)^{i}\binom{s}{s-i}\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{s-i}{k_2}_{q}{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}_{q}}^{-1}

\displaystyle=n\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}_{q}^{-1}

\displaystyle=n=\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}.

Indeed, the last equality follows from the observation that the assumption

k_{2}\geq n-k_{1}+1
implies
k_{1}k_{2}\geq n
, so
\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}=n
.

Define the non-negative integer-valued random variable
\mathbf{X}\coloneqq\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}-\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})
.
Then

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}]=\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}-\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]=0,

so by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\neq\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\})=\mathbb{P}(\mathbf{X}\geq 1)\leq\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{X}],

and the result then follows by letting
q
tend to
\infty
.
∎

5
Asymptotic behavior as the code dimensions grow

We now consider the asymptotic behavior of the dimension of the star product of two uniformly random codes, in the case where the dimensions
k_{1},k_{2}
of the respective codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}
are strictly increasing functions of the same variable
t
, while assuming
k_{2}
does not grow too quickly compared to
k_{1}
. Before proving the main result of this section, we introduce some new notation and prove a few lemmas we will need.

Notation 5.1
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{1}\leq k_{2}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Define

\displaystyle S^{-}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{A\in S^{k_{1},k_{2}}\,\middle|\,0<\mathrm{rk}(A)\leq\alpha k_{1}\right\},

\displaystyle S_{k_{1},k_{2}}^{+}(\alpha)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{A\in S^{k_{1},k_{2}}\,\middle|\,\mathrm{rk}(A)>\alpha k_{1}\right\}.

The subscripts
k_{1},k_{2}
will be omitted when they are clear from context. For instance, we will simply write
S^{-}(\alpha)
and
S^{+}(\alpha)
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be strictly monotone increasing functions such that
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Fix
\alpha\in(0,1)
.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S^{-}(\alpha)|}{|S|}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S^{+}(\alpha)|}{|S|}=1.

Proof.

We use

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{k_2(t)}{r}_{q}\leq q^{r(k_{2}(t)-r+1)}

and bound
|S_{r}|
by the number of
k_{1}(t)\times k_{2}(t)
matrices of rank
r
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.
|S_{r}|\leq q^{r(k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t))-r^{2}+r-k_{1}(t)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle|S^{-}(\alpha)|=\sum^{\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}_{r=1}|S_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}_{r=1}q^{r(k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t))-r^{2}+r-k_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor q^{\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor(k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t))-(\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor)^{2}+\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor-k_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha k_{1}(t)q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)(k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t))-(\alpha k_{1}(t)-1)^{2}+\alpha k_{1}(t)-k_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle=q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+(3\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{-}(\alpha)|}{|S|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+(3\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}}{q^{k_{1}k_{2}-k_{1}}}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=q^{(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+3\alpha k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{as}\quad t\rightarrow\infty,

where (
5.1
) follows as
\alpha<1
and
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
\frac{|S^{-}(\alpha)|}{|S|}\geq 0
as well, we get that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S^{-}(\alpha)|}{|S|}=0
. Finally, since
|S^{+}(\alpha)|=|S|-1-|S^{-}(\alpha)|
, the latter limit of the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be strictly monotone increasing functions such that
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then for any
\alpha\in[0,1)
we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{r=\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell}}{\sum_{r=\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)k_{2}(t)}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}\right).

Proof.

We begin by proving the claim for
\alpha=0
, and then show that this implies it for any
0\leq\alpha<1
. Note that
\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}(t)}S_{r,\ell}=q^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)-k_{1}(t)\ell}
. This allows us to rewrite the LHS of the statement as

\displaystyle\frac{q^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}q^{-\ell(k_{1}(t)-1)}}{q^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}q^{-\ell k_{1}(t)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{-(k_{1}(t)-1)}+1)^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}}{(q^{-k_{1}(t)}+1)^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{q^{k_{1}(t)}+q}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}\right)^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}\right)^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)},

where the first equality follows from the Binomial Theorem.

We distinguish two cases according to the asymptotic behavior of
k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)
, which will be exhaustive by the assumed properties of
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
. For the first case suppose
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)=\infty
. It is well known that for functions
h,p\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}h(t)=0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}p(t)=\infty
, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}(1+h(t))^{p(t)}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp({h(t)p(t)}).

Hence, letting
h(t)\coloneqq\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}
and
p(t)\coloneqq k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)
,
we get the desired identity for
\alpha=0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)=\infty
.

For the second case, suppose
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)=C<\infty.
Since
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}k_{1}(t)=\infty
the result follows as

\displaystyle 1=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}\right)^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left({\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}\right).

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left({\frac{(q-1)k_{2}(t)}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}}\right).

Suppose now
0<\alpha<1
and let

N=\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell},

and

D=\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}=|S|.

Additionally, partition the sums of
D
and
N
as follows:

\displaystyle N^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell},

\displaystyle\Delta N

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell},

\displaystyle D^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell},

\displaystyle\Delta D

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\lfloor\alpha k_{1}(t)\rfloor-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}.

Note that when
r=0
, we have
\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{0,\ell}=1
. Hence, when considering
\frac{\Delta D-1}{D}
, we can apply Lemma
5.2
and its proof to obtain
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta D}{D}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta D-1}{D}=0
as

\frac{\Delta D-1}{D}\leq q^{(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+\alpha k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}.

Furthermore, as
1\leq q^{\ell}\leq q^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}
for all
\ell\leq k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)
, we have

\frac{\Delta N-1}{N}\leq q^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\frac{\Delta D-1}{D}\leq q^{(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}.

This allows us to conclude that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta N}{N}=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Delta N-1}{N}=0
. The result then follows as

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N^{\prime}}{D^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N-\Delta N}{D-\Delta D}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{D}\left(\frac{1-\frac{\Delta N}{N}}{1-\frac{\Delta D}{D}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{N}{D}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(\frac{(q-1)k_{2}(t)}{q^{k_{1}(t)}+1}\right)

where the last equality follows from the case
\alpha=0
.
∎

We will now consider the asymptotics with respect to the dimension of the codes
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
. Specifically, if
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}=k_{1}
and
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}=k_{2}
then we will sample the codes from the ambient space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}k_{2}}
, as we then expect asymptotically almost all such pairs of codes to have a star product equal to the entire ambient space. This is motivated by the fact that when taking the star product of two random codes then a generating set of
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
will have size

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1})\dim(\mathcal{C}_{2})-\frac{\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2})(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2})-1)}{2},

where the expected intersection will tend to 0, as proven in the following result.

Lemma 5.4
.

Fix
n,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq n
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
be uniformly random codes of dimension
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2})]=\left({{\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_1}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k_2}_{q}}}\right)^{-1}{{\sum\limits^{k_{1}}_{i=1}i\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-i}{k_1-i}_{q}q^{(k_{1}-i)(k_{2}-i)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k_1}{k_2-i}_{q}}}.

Proof.

Fix a subspace
W\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
U^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}/W
with
\dim(U^{\prime})=k_{1}-i
. We then count the number of
V^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}/W
with
\dim(V^{\prime})=k_{2}-i
such that
U^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}=\{0\}
. There are
\prod^{k_{2}-i-1}_{j=0}(q^{n-i}-q^{k_{1}-i+j})
ordered bases spanning a subspace of dimension
k_{2}-i
which intersects
U^{\prime}
trivially, which we divide by the number of different bases for the same subspace:

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{n-i}-q^{k_{1}-i})(q^{n-i}-q^{k_{1}-i+1})\ldots(q^{n-i}-q^{k_{1}-i+k_{2}-i-1})}{(q^{k_{2}-i}-1)(q^{k_{2}-i}-q)\ldots(q^{k_{2}-i}-q^{k_{2}-i-1})}=q^{(k_{1}-i)(k_{2}-i)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n-k_1}{k_2-i}_{q}.

Lastly, we multiply (
5.2
) by the number of ways to choose
U^{\prime}
given a fixed
W
, and the number of ways to choose
W
. The result then follows as

|(U,V)\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\colon U\cap V=W|=|(U^{\prime},V^{\prime})\leq(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}/W)\times(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}/W)\colon U^{\prime}\cap V^{\prime}=\{0\}|.\qed

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be strictly monotone increasing functions such that
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
n(t)=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively. Then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2})]=0.

Proof.

From
[
21
]
we have the bounds

q^{k(n-k)}\leq\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n}{k}_{q}\leq\frac{7}{2}q^{k(n-k)},

from which we obtain

(5.3)

\displaystyle q^{k_{1}(t)(n(t)-k_{1}(t))+k_{2}(t)(n(t)-k_{2}(t))}\leq\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)}{k_1(t)}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)}{k_2(t)}_{q}.

For
1\leq i\leq k_{1}(t)
we have

\displaystyle\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-i}{k_1(t)-i}_{q}q^{(k_{1}(t)-i)(k_{2}(t)-i)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-k_1(t)}{k_2(t)-i}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^{3}q^{i(k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-2k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t)(n(t)-k_{1}(t))+k_{2}(t)(n(t)-k_{2}(t))},

so we obtain

\displaystyle\sum^{k_{1}(t)}_{i=1}i\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-i}{k_1(t)-i}_{q}q^{(k_{1}(t)-i)(k_{2}(t)-i)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-k_1(t)}{k_2(t)-i}_{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;\sum^{k_{1}(t)}_{i=1}k_{1}(t)\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)}{i}_{q}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-i}{k_1(t)-i}_{q}q^{(k_{1}(t)-i)(k_{2}(t)-i)}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{0}{n(t)-k_1(t)}{k_2(t)-i}_{q}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;k_{1}(t)(k_{1}(t)+1)\left(\frac{7}{2}\right)^{3}q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-2n(t)+k_{1}(t)(n(t)-k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)(n(t)-k_{2}(t))}.

Combining (
5.3
) with (
5.4
) and Lemma
5.4
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap\mathcal{C}_{2})]\leq q^{-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)+\log_{q}(k_{1}(t)(k_{1}(t)+1)\frac{343}{8})},

and the result follows as
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t
.
∎

We now prove that the kernel of
\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}
is particularly well-behaved when the dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
does not grow too quickly compared to that of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
. We specify this in the following definition.

Definition 5.6
.

We say that functions
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
are
admissible
if they are strictly monotone increasing such that
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
, and for all
\alpha\in(0,2-\log_{q}(2q-1))
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}=0.

This assumption reflects a technical limitation of our approach rather than an intrinsic necessity, and we do not claim it to be optimal. The proof of Theorem
5.7
relies on it as the number of zeros of a non-zero bilinear form in
\mathcal{B}
is at most
(2q-1)^{k_{2}-k_{1}-2}
. Note that for
q=2
we have
\alpha<0.415
, and that
\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}2-\log_{q}(2q-1)=1.
Note moreover that
strictly monotone and increasing functions
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
k_{1}(t)\leq k_{2}(t)
and that both grow polynomially in
t
are admissible for all
q
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be admissible. Let
n(t)=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|]=2.

Proof.

The expected kernel size can also be written as

\mathbb{E}[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|]=\sum_{r=0}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))},

where
Z(B)
is the number of zeroes of a bilinear form in
S_{r,I}
, where
I\subseteq[k_{2}]\setminus[k_{1}]
is any subset of size
|I|=\ell,
and
S_{r,\ell}=|S_{r,V}|
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,2-\log_{q}(2q-1))
. By Lemma
5.2
and its proof we have that, for
t\rightarrow\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{|S^{-}(\alpha)|}{|S|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}}{q^{k_{1}k_{2}-k_{1}}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=q^{(\alpha-1)k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}(t)^{2}+\alpha k_{1}(t)+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}\rightarrow 0,

and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S^{+}(\alpha)|}{|S|}=1
. Let
B\in S^{+}(\alpha)
, so
r\geq\alpha k_{1}(t)
. Then

Z(B)\geq(q^{k_{1}(t)}+q-1)q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)-1}\geq q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-1}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}\right),

which yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}

\displaystyle\geq q^{-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}\frac{1}{q^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)}}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}.

Since
n(t)=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
, we obtain

\sum_{B\in S^{+}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}\geq\frac{|S^{+}(\alpha)|}{|S|}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}\right)^{k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1)}.

Then

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}\right)^{k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1)}=\;\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}(k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1))\right)=1,

so

(5.6)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{B\in S^{+}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\geq 1.

Thus, we need to show that (
5.6
) is also bounded from above by 1. Note that

\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}=\frac{(q^{r}+q-1)q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-r-1}}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}=q^{-1}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}}\right)\leq q^{-1}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right),

which yields

\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}=q^{-(n(t)-k_{2}(t))}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left((n(t)-k_{2}(t))\ln\left(1+\frac{q-1}{q^{r}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left((n(t)-k_{2}(t))\frac{q-1}{q^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\exp\left((q-1)\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right)

\displaystyle=1.

Thus, for sufficiently large
t
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}\leq q^{-(n(t)-k_{2}(t))}\exp\left((q-1)\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right),

which in turn yields

\displaystyle\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\>q^{-(n(t)-k_{2}(t))}\exp\left((q-1)\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right)\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}

(5.7)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>q^{-(n(t)-k_{1}(t))}\exp\left((q-1)\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right)\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell}.

Taking the limit of (
5.7
) it then follows by Lemma
5.3
that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}q^{-(n(t)-k_{1}(t))}\exp\left((q-1)\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{\alpha k_{1}(t)}}\right)\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(q^{-(n(t)-k_{1}(t))}\right)\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}q^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{1}{|S|}\right)\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum_{r=\alpha k_{1}(t)}^{k_{1}(t)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}\binom{k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)}{\ell}S_{r,\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|S^{+}(\alpha)|}{|S|}=1.

We conclude

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{B\in S^{+}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}=1.

Let
B\in S^{-}(\alpha)
. By Lemma
3.7
,
Z(B)\leq(2q-1)q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)-2}
and since
q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}\leq 1
for all
\ell\leq k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in S^{-}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}(t)+k_{2}(t)}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}q^{\ell-(k_{2}(t)-k_{1}(t))}\leq\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{2}(t)}|S^{-}(\alpha)|.

Substituting
n(t)=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
it then follows by (
5.5
) that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{k_{2}(t)(k_{1}(t)-1)}|S^{-}(\alpha)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\;q^{(\alpha-2+\log_{q}(2q-1)k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-\alpha^{2})k_{1}^{2}+(2-\log_{q}(2q-1)k_{2}(t)+(\alpha-1)k_{1}+\log_{q}(\alpha k_{1}(t))}.

Since
\alpha<2-\log_{q}(2q-1)
we obtain

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{B\in S^{-}(\alpha)}\left(\frac{Z(B)}{q^{k_{1}+k_{2}}}\right)^{k_{1}k_{2}-k_{2}}q^{\ell-(k_{2}-k_{1})}=0,

which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.8
.

Note that by Theorem
5.7
we have that
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|\big]=\infty
when
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}=\infty
. This follows directly from the proof when determining the lower bound. Furthermore, the proof and numerical experiments suggest the conjecture that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|\big]=\exp\left(\frac{q-1}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}(k_{2}(t))(k_{1}(t)-1)\right)+1,

independently of the value of
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}{q^{k_{1}(t)}}
.

Having established the concentration properties of the kernel size and the growth behavior of the relevant combinatorial quantities, we are now ready to study the asymptotics of the expected star-product dimension as both code dimensions increase. The following results formalizes this by showing that, under the assumed admissible growth conditions, the expected kernel size stabilizes and the star product almost surely attains full dimension.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be admissible. Let
n\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
n(t)\geq k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then, for sufficiently large
t
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)\right)\geq 1-\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}.

Proof.

For any codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
as in the statement, let
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}
be the corresponding codes obtained by puncturing
\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\mathcal{C}_{2}
in the last
n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
coordinates.

Let
\mathcal{N}
denote the event that
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
, and for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{E}_{j}
be the event that
|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}}}|=j
.
Note that
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
if and only if
|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|=1
, which occurs if and only if, for all non-zero
\varphi\in\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}}}
, there exists
i\in\{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+1,\ldots,n(t)\}
such that
\varphi(G_{1}^{(i)},G_{2}^{(i)})\neq 0
.

To study the event
\mathcal{E}_{j}
, let

\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}}}\setminus\{0\}=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{j-1}\}
.
Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\overline{\mathcal{N}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}\left\{\varphi_{i}(G_{1}^{(k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+1)},G_{2}^{(k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)+1)})=\cdots=\varphi_{i}(G_{1}^{(n(t))},G_{2}^{(n(t))})=0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathbb{P}\left(\varphi_{i}(x,y)=0\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}

\displaystyle=(j-1)\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)},

where
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}(t)}
and
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{2}(t)}
are drawn independently and uniformly at random.
Note that (
5.8
) follows from Lemma
3.7
, since each
\varphi_{i}
is nonzero. By the law of total probability,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\overline{\mathcal{N}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{j})\,\mathbb{P}(\overline{\mathcal{N}}\mid\mathcal{E}_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{j})(j-1)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{n(t)-k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}\left(\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\prime}}}|\big]-1\right).

The result then follows from Theorem
5.7
.
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be admissible. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
t_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
t\geq t_{\varepsilon}
, for every non-negative integer
\ell
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\geq k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-\ell\right)\geq 1-\frac{2+\varepsilon}{q^{\ell+1}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.7
, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that, for all
t\geq t_{\varepsilon}
,
\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|\big]\leq 2+\varepsilon.

By Markov’s inequality, for
t\geq t_{\varepsilon}
we have

\mathbb{P}\left(|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|<\delta\right)\geq 1-\frac{\mathbb{E}\big[|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|\big]}{\delta}\geq 1-\frac{2+\varepsilon}{\delta}.

The result follows by taking
\delta=q^{\ell+1}
, since

\mathbb{P}\left(|\ker_{\psi_{\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}}}|<q^{\ell+1}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})\geq k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)-\ell\right).\qed

Theorem
5.9
addresses the regime in which the ambient length is no smaller than the product of the code dimensions. By applying puncturing arguments, one obtains an analogous result when the ambient space is smaller.

Theorem 5.11
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be admissible. Let
n\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
n(t)<k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then, for sufficiently large
t
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=n(t)\right)\geq 1-\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{t}.

Proof.

For
i=1,2
, let
G_{i}
be the systematic generator matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{i}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
. Set
m(t)=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
and extend
G_{i}
by concatenating
m(t)-n(t)+t
additional columns sampled uniformly and independently from
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{i}(t)}
. Let
\tilde{G}_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k_{i}(t)\times(m(t)+t)}
be the resulting matrix and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i}=\mathrm{rowsp}(\tilde{G}_{i})
.

If
\pi\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m(t)+t}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
denotes the projection onto the first
n(t)
coordinates, then
\pi(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{i})=\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be the event that
\dim\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\star\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}=k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t)
. By Theorem
5.9
for sufficiently large
t
we have

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N})\geq 1-\left(\frac{2q-1}{q^{2}}\right)^{t}.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the event
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})=n(t)
. We then show that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}
implies
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
. Suppose that
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})<n(t)
, so there exists a non-zero
v\in(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})^{\perp}
. Consider
\tilde{v}=(v,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m(t)+t}
. Since
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\star\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
is spanned by
\tilde{c}_{1}\star\tilde{c}_{2}
, where
\tilde{c}_{1}
and
\tilde{c}_{2}
are basis elements of
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2}
, respectively, we have

\langle\tilde{v},\tilde{c}_{1}\star\tilde{c}_{2}\rangle=\langle v,\pi(\tilde{c}_{1})\star\pi(\tilde{c}_{2})\rangle=0.

Thus,
\tilde{v}\in(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\star\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{2})^{\perp}
and
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M})\geq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{N})
.
∎

We then conveniently summarize the conclusions of Theorem
5.9
and Theorem
5.11
in a single result.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2}\colon\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be admissible. Let
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n(t)}
be uniformly random codes of dimensions
k_{1}(t)
and
k_{2}(t)
, respectively, as in Definition
3.8
. Then, for sufficiently large
t
,

\mathbb{E}[\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2})]=\min\{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t),n(t)\}+o(1).

Remark 5.13
.

In the final stages of preparing this paper, we became aware of
[
34
]
, a work that appears to have been largely overlooked by the community and whose results partially overlap with ours. In this paragraph, we describe the relationship between our findings and those of
[
34
]
. Their methods rely on estimating the span of random rank 1 matrices, whereas ours estimate the kernel of a linear map whose image equals
\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{2}
. The authors conclude that the star product between two uniformly random linear codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is expected to have dimension
\min\{\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\dim\mathcal{C}_{2},n\}
for certain parameters as defined in (
5.9
). Specifically, let
\varepsilon,\kappa\in(0,1)
such that
q^{(1-\kappa)^{2}}\geq\frac{2q-1}{q}
and define the parameter space

(5.9)

\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon,\kappa)=\left\{(k_{1},k_{2})\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\;\middle|\;2\leq k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon q^{\kappa k_{1}}}{(q-1)k_{1}}\right\}.

Then
[
34
, Theorem 16]
yields the same conclusion as our Theorem
5.9
when
(\dim\mathcal{C}_{1},\dim\mathcal{C}_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon,\kappa)
. However, our result is stronger in the sense that any sequence of dimensions of codes in
\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon,\kappa)
will satisfy the conditions of Theorem
5.9
. Similarly,
[
34
, Theorem 17]
comes to the same conclusion as Theorem
5.11
when
(\dim\mathcal{C}_{1},\dim\mathcal{C}_{2})\in\mathcal{P}(\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2})
. In this case their result is only stronger for the case
q=2
as
\alpha<2-\log_{q}(2q-1)<0.415
, while for
q\geq 3
then
2-\log_{q}(2q-1)\geq 0.535
. Furthermore, our Corollary
4.2
, which provides an asymptotic result in the field size, is entirely independent of the relationship between
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}
and
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}
.

6
Implications to secure communications and quantum error correction

As mentioned in the introduction, the star product appears in a variety of applications, including cryptanalysis, linear exact repair schemes, quantum error correction, private information retrieval, and secure distributed matrix multiplication. In this section, we briefly discuss some of these applications and the connection to our results.

Private information retrieval (PIR).

Private information retrieval considers the problem of downloading a data item or file from a (public) database without disclosing the identity of the retrieved item to the database owner
[
8
]
. Recently, PIR from coded distributed storage systems has gained a lot of attention, see
[
41
,
3
,
16
,
43
]
among many others.

Star products are natural objects to study in linear PIR and its quantum extensions due to the response structure, which is essentially just a vector of inner products between the query vectors and the stored data vectors. The utility of star products in PIR is showcased by several extensions of the basic setting: the first star product PIR scheme
[
16
]
has been extended to cover, e.g., stragglers and errors/adversarial nodes
[
42
]
, non-MDS codes
[
15
]
, secret sharing framework for secure PIR with algebraic geometry codes
[
27
]
, and quantum PIR
[
2
]
. It is also the key ingredient in proving and achieving the capacity of PIR from coded storage with colluding servers
[
18
,
19
]
.

A figure of merit for any PIR scheme is its
retrieval rate
, defined as

R_{\mathrm{PIR}}=\frac{\mathrm{size\ of\ retrieved\ file}}{\mathrm{size\ of\ total\ download}}\leq 1,

which should be maximized. The star product retrieval scheme
[
16
]
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d(\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{D})-1}{n}\leq R_{\mathrm{PIR}}\leq\frac{\dim((\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{D})^{\perp})}{n}=1-\frac{\dim\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{D}}{n}.

Suppose now that both
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
are chosen randomly (according to Definition
3.8
) such that
\dim\mathcal{C}=k_{1}
and
\dim\mathcal{D}=k_{2}
. Then by applying Corollary
4.2
we see that

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[R_{\mathrm{PIR}}]\leq 1-\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\dim\mathcal{C}\star\mathcal{D}]}{n}=1-\frac{\min\{k_{1}k_{2},n\}}{n}.

Thus, when instantiating such a PIR scheme with random codes, a non-zero upper bound on the expected rate is obtained when
\dim\mathcal{C}\dim\mathcal{D}<n
.
Now suppose
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}=k_{1}(t)
and
\dim\mathcal{C}_{2}=k_{2}(t)
according to Corollary
5.12
. Then we see

\mathbb{E}[R_{\mathrm{PIR}}]\leq 1-\frac{\min\{k_{1}(t)k_{2}(t),n(t)\}}{n(t)}+o(1),

so we obtain the same conclusion as for the asymptotics in
q
. Contrasting these with the star product PIR scheme utilizing GRS codes
[
16
]
that achieves a rate

R_{\mathrm{PIR}}=1-\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}{n},

we see that random codes will typically not yield constructions with a high PIR rate.

Secure distributed matrix multiplication (SDMM).

Secure distributed matrix multiplication is a protocol for computing a large matrix product using a set of helper servers, such that the content of the matrices remain information-theoretically secure against specified subsets of colluding servers. In
[
26
]
, a general framework for linear SDMM using star products was established and shown to capture most of the previous SDMM schemes in the literature as special cases, including
[
14
,
1
,
28
,
25
]
. In this framework, a user holds two private matrices
A
and
B
and wishes to compute
AB
, while having access to
N
servers. The user constructs encoded shares and distributes them to the servers using two codes
\mathcal{C}_{A},\mathcal{C}_{B}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
.

We define the
recovery threshold

R_{\mathrm{SDMM}}
as the smallest number of server responses needed to guarantee reconstruction of
AB
, and the
straggler tolerance

S_{\mathrm{SDMM}}
as the maximum number of unresponsive servers that can be tolerated while still able to reconstruct the product. For a linear SDMM scheme that is decodable, that is, able to reconstruct
AB
, there exist corresponding bounds on the recovery threshold and straggler tolerance.

Instantiating a linear SDMM scheme with random codes (according to Definition
3.8
)
\mathcal{C}_{A},\mathcal{C}_{B}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{N}
yields with high probability a decodable scheme for sufficiently large
q
. According to
[
26
]
, such a scheme has a recovery threshold of
R_{\mathrm{SDMM}}=N-d(\mathcal{C}_{A}\star\mathcal{C}_{B})+1
and can tolerate at most
S_{\mathrm{SDMM}}=d(\mathcal{C}_{A}\star\mathcal{C}_{B})-1
stragglers. Consequently,

\displaystyle N\geq\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[R_{\mathrm{SDMM}}]

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\dim\mathcal{C}_{A}\dim\mathcal{C}_{B},N\}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}[S_{\mathrm{SDMM}}]

\displaystyle\leq N-\min\{\dim\mathcal{C}_{A}\dim\mathcal{C}_{B},N\}.

Thus, if
\dim\mathcal{C}_{A}\dim\mathcal{C}_{B}<N
we get a non-maximal lower bound on the expected recovery threshold and a non-zero upper bound on the expected straggler tolerance. However, if
\dim\mathcal{C}_{A}\dim\mathcal{C}_{B}\geq N
, then the expected recovery threshold is
N
and consequently there will be no straggler tolerance. Again, similarly to PIR, we see that random constructions are not likely to possess the desirable properties of low recovery threshold and high straggler tolerance.

Binary CSS-T quantum error-correcting codes.

Quantum error-correcting codes are essential for protecting quantum information against noise and decoherence. A particularly important class is the Calderbank–Shor–Steane (CSS) family, where two classical binary linear codes with specific inclusion properties allow the construction of a quantum code
[
5
,
40
]
. In the setting of transversal T gates, further structural conditions are imposed on the pair of classical codes
[
37
]
. The resulting CSS-T codes can then be characterized via the star product
[
6
]
. In particular, binary CSS-T codes are pairs of binary non-zero linear codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\mathcal{C}_{2})
satisfying
\mathcal{C}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{1}\cap(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{1})^{\perp}.
Furthermore, the resulting binary CSS-T code will have a minimum distance
d^{\prime}\geq d(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp})
.

For a uniformly random code
\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}=k
, it was shown in
[
7
]
that for sufficiently large
k
,

\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{1}=\min\left\{n,\binom{\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}+1}{2}\right\}

with high probability. Consequently,

\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{1})^{\perp}=n-\min\left\{n,\binom{\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}+1}{2}\right\},

Thus, for a uniformly random code
\mathcal{C}_{1}\leq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
of sufficiently large dimension, we expect with high probability that no binary CSS-T code using
\mathcal{C}_{1}
exists.
When
\dim(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{1})
is large but still smaller than the ambient space, as is typical for random codes when
\dim\mathcal{C}_{1}
is not too large, the orthogonal complement
(\mathcal{C}_{1}\star\mathcal{C}_{1})^{\perp}
becomes small. As a result,
\mathcal{C}_{2}
is forced to have low dimension. Consequently, the resulting CSS-T code offers only a weak lower bound on the minimum distance,
d^{\prime}\geq d(\mathcal{C}_{2}^{\perp})
. Conversely, codes with a smaller star product leave more room for choices of
\mathcal{C}_{2}
and may yield quantum codes with larger distance.

7
Conclusions and future work

We have shown that the expected dimension of the star product of two uniformly random linear codes asymptotically reaches its maximal value, unconditionally in the field size and, under appropriate assumptions, also in the code dimensions. It would be interesting to analyze the non-asymptotic cases further and to derive lower bounds for the expected dimension, thereby providing a more detailed understanding on the distribution of the probability mass.

The behavior of star products underpins the performance of many applications such as private information retrieval and secure distributed matrix multiplication, which we also briefly discussed in this paper. One could keep exploring how the derived star product properties extend to other settings where star products play a central role. In particular, the results suggest potential applications to linear exact repair schemes for distributed storage, where componentwise operations naturally arise
[
17
,
24
]
, and to code-based cryptanalysis, where deviations from maximal star product dimension can serve as structural distinguishers. For instance, in
[
29
]
it is shown that the dimension of the square of the dual of a Goppa code is significantly smaller than that of a random code, providing a rigorous upper bound for its dimension. However, the findings concern parameter ranges that do not threaten the current McEliece Goppa code system parameters, see
[
29
, Table 1]
. It would be interesting to try using different codes in the star product instead of the Goppa code or its dual itself. This would enable changing the dimension of the other code while keeping the Goppa code dimension fixed, which could potentially reveal some differences versus the same code starred with a random code in new parameter ranges relevant to cryptography.

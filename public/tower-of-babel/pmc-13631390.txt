Cross-sectional associations of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 and neurofilament light chain in dementia-free older adults in the INSPIRE-T baseline cohort
Authors: Claudie Hooper, Emmanuel González, Leonor Nogueira, Julien Delrieu, Nicola Coley, Maria Soto, Bruno Vellas, Sophie Guyonnet, for the IHU HealthAge INSPIRE/Open Science study group
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLoneliness is the subjective feeling of social isolation and evidence suggests that it increases the risk for cognitive decline and Alzheimer's disease (AD). However, studies examining the associations between loneliness and AD biomarkers are limited and inconclusive.ObjectiveWe sought to investigate the relationships between loneliness and plasma tau phosphorylated at threonine 217 (p-tau217) and plasma neurofilament light chain (NfL): peripheral biomarkers of central AD pathology.MethodsThis is a cross-sectional analysis of the ‘INStitute for Prevention’ ‘healthy agIng’ and ‘medicine REjuvenative’ ‘Translational’ (INSPIRE-T) baseline data. Participants were dementia-free (Mini-Mental State Exam score ≥ 24) community-dwellers aged ≥ 65 years (n = 434). Loneliness was assessed using the Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS®) Item Bank v2.0, Short Form 8a questionnaire and plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL were measured using Lumipulse immunoassays. Multiple linear regression was conducted to evaluate the relationships between loneliness (exposure) and plasma biomarkers (outcomes).ResultsLoneliness was not associated with plasma p-tau217 (β = 0.00458 [95% CI: −0.00328, 0.01243], p = 0.253) or plasma NfL (β = 0.00138 [95% CI: −0.00418, 0.00693], p = 0.626) in multiple linear regression models adjusted for age, sex, education, cognitive performance, depressive score, eyesight, hearing, and apolipoprotein ε4 (APOE ε4) status (model 2). There was no significant moderating effect of sex, depression, eyesight, hearing or APOE ε4 on these associations.ConclusionsOur findings suggest that loneliness is not associated with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL in dementia-free older adults. Perspective studies are warranted to examine these associations further.

Introduction

Loneliness is the subjective feeling of being socially isolated and social isolation is the objective state of possessing few social relationships and experiencing limited social contact. 1 Approximately 1 in 4 older adults worldwide experience loneliness, which emphasizes that loneliness is a major public-health issue. 2 This is of particular significance in the context of the rapidly ageing population. 3 In older adults loneliness has been associated with cognitive impairment,4–6 cognitive decline,5,7–9 and Alzheimer's disease (AD),6,7,9–11 the primary cause of dementia. 12 Consistent with this, neuro-imaging studies have reported that loneliness is associated with increased cerebral amyloid-β (Aβ)13–15 and tau deposition, 15 the principle hallmark characteristics of AD. 16 However, other imaging studies have conversely reported no associations with these cerebral biomarkers.17,18 Evidence to date linking loneliness to plasma biomarkers of AD is based on data from a single study in cognitively unimpaired older adults. 17 This study reported no associations between loneliness and plasma Aβ42/40, tau phosphorylated at threonine 181 (p-tau181), neurofilament light chain (NfL), or glial acidic fibrillary protein (GFAP) at baseline and no consistent or robust associations of loneliness with changes in these biomarkers over time using mixed models (mean age of the 2 populations studied ∼ early to mid-60 s, with 53 and 54.5% female subjects and 46.6 and 83% of subjects reporting college level education in the two studies, respectively). 17

The relationship between loneliness and biomarkers of AD merits further research considering that loneliness is a modifiable risk factor and such factors are receiving great interest due to their potential to provide cost-effective, non-pharmacological targets for dementia intervention.19,20 Hence, the aim of this current study was to examine the cross-sectional relationships between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL, a downstream marker of neurodegeneration. 21 To our knowledge, this is the first study to investigate the relationships between loneliness and plasma p-tau217, which is a plasma biomarker that has shown potential as a robust indicator of cerebral Aβ burden outperforming plasma Aβ and other p-tau epitopes. 22 We hypothesized that loneliness would be positively associated with these plasma biomarkers in our analytic population of dementia-free older adults (mean age: 76.6 (7.2) years with 54.6% female subjects), exhibiting poorer cognitive performance and more advanced age than that of the previous plasma biomarker study. 17 In addition, we explored whether sex, depression, eyesight, hearing, or apolipoprotein E ε4 (APOE ε4) carrier status moderated the associations between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL.

Methods

Study design and approval

This cross-sectional analysis used baseline data from the ‘INStitute for Prevention’ ‘healthy agIng’ and ‘medicine REjuvenative’ ‘Translational’ (INSPIRE-T) study. INSPIRE-T is an ongoing 10-year observational study designed to enable the investigation of intrinsic capacity changes (global and by domain) across the life course. 23 INSPIRE-T was approved by the French Ethics Committee located in Rennes (CPP Ouest V) and was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT04224038). 23 This current study within INSPIRE-T conformed to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines.

Participants

The total baseline population of INSPIRE-T comprised 1120 male and female participants aged between 20 and 102 years. INSPIRE-T did not specify strict inclusion criteria; however, participants were required to be ≥ 20 years old and affiliated to a French social security scheme. Exclusion criteria pertained to short life expectancy due to medical conditions or comprised legal protection status (guardianship, curatorship, or safeguard of justice). Subjects were recruited to the study through methods such as geriatric department outreach and media campaigns. Our analytic sample of the baseline cohort consisted of 434 subjects aged ≥ 65 years (an age group often classified as older adults) 24 with a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score ≥ 24 (dementia-free) with data on loneliness and plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL. The sample size for the present analysis was defined a posteriori and was based on data availability in INSPIRE-T.

Assessment of loneliness

The subjective feeling of loneliness was assessed using the Social isolation Patient-Reported Outcomes Measurement Information System (PROMIS®) Item Bank v2.0, Short Form 8a questionnaire. 25 This questionnaire contains 8 items that interrogate perceived social isolation (i.e., loneliness) including four shared questions with the UCLA loneliness questionnaires26,27: ‘I feel left out’, ‘I feel that people barely know me’, ‘I feel isolated from others’ and ‘I feel that people are around me but not with me’. 28 Note that instruments that assess social isolation typically assess the number of social ties or the frequency of social interactions. The PROMIS questionnaire is scored out of 40 (raw score) using a 5-category Likert rating scale from ‘never’ to ‘always’ (giving 1–5 points per item). Raw scores were converted to T-scores using the Health Measures Scoring Service web application. T-scores are normalized to the U.S general population as an inherent feature of the instrument (mean = 50, standard deviation (SD) = 10). Higher T-scores represent greater loneliness.

Assessment of plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL

Plasma was collected into EDTA tubes at study baseline and frozen at −80 °C in polypropylene cryotubes and samples were not thawed prior to biomarker measurement. Lumipulse (Fujirebio) chemiluminescent immunoassays were used to quantify p-tau217 and NfL in plasma samples. Biomarker concentrations were determined in pg/ml against a standard curve and raw values were log transformed to correct for skewness. The intra-assay and inter-assay coefficients of variation (CVs) were < 7% for both assays. The mean (SD) time interval between blood collection and the assessment of loneliness was 0.11 (5.5) days.

Covariates

Covariates used in the regression models were selected a priori based on scientific literature1,12 and included age (years), sex (male or female), education (3 groups: no diploma or primary school certificate, secondary education, and university level or higher), cognitive performance (MMSE 29 continuous score), depressive score (Patient Health Questionnaire-9: PHQ-9 30 continuous score), eyesight (binary variable operationalized as normal/anomaly on the WHO simple eye chart), hearing (binary variable operationalized as normal/anomaly on the whisper test), APOE ε4 status (carrier versus non-carrier) and time interval between blood collection and the assessment of loneliness (days). Eyesight and hearing were included as covariates in regression models due to the association of vision and hearing loss with increased dementia risk 19 and loneliness. 31 Multicollinearity was evaluated using variance inflation factors (VIFs) and all values were < 2. This suggests no evidence of substantial multicollinearity of loneliness with covariates (mean VIF: 1.13).

Statistical analysis

Descriptive characteristics are shown as mean ± SD or absolute values and percentages. Multiple linear regression models were used to evaluate the cross-sectional associations between baseline loneliness T-scores (exposure) and baseline levels of plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL (outcome variables). The exposure and outcomes were all modelled as continuous variables in the main analysis. Regression models included an unadjusted model and 2 adjusted models. Model 1 was adjusted for age, sex and education only. Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, education, plus cognitive performance, depressive score, eyesight, hearing and APOE ε4 carrier status. As a supplementary analysis, the time interval between blood collection and the assessment of loneliness was added as a covariate to regression model 2. Three sensitivity analyses were also conducted with and without adjustment for covariates according to the aforementioned model 1 and 2. Firstly, loneliness T-scores (exposure) were categorized into tertiles and multiple linear regression was run. The reference group was the lowest tertile and biomarker outcomes were modelled as continuous variables. Secondly, plasma p-tau217 values (outcome variable) were dichotomized according to a positivity threshold of > 0.285 pg/ml, which is based on the prediction of cerebral Aβ positivity using positron emission tomography (PET). 32 Logistic regression was then conducted with loneliness T-scores (exposure) modelled as a continuous variable with plasma p-tau217 negativity set as the reference group. Thirdly, the association between loneliness T-scores (exposure) and cognitive performance measured as MMSE score (outcome) was evaluated using multiple linear regression with loneliness and MMSE modelled as continuous scores. For this sensitivity analysis cognitive performance was omitted from the covariates in model 2. To explore the moderating role of sex, depression (PHQ-9 score > 4 indicative of depression versus ≤ 4 indicative of the absence of depression), eyesight, hearing and APOE ε4 carrier status (all modelled as binary variables) on the associations between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL, interaction terms between each potential moderator and loneliness were added to adjusted regression model 2. Loneliness (the exposure), plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL (the outcomes) were all modelled as continuous variables in the interaction terms. Version 2025.3 of the INSPIRE-T clinical database was used for the analysis presented here and statistical analyses were performed using Stata version 18 (StataCorp LLC, USA) with a p-value < 0.05 (two-sided) considered statistically significant.

Results

Sample characteristics

Demographic and clinical characteristics of our analytic population of dementia-free older adults (n = 434) and the total baseline INSPIRE-T population (n = 1120) are shown in Table 1. The mean (SD) age was 76.6 (7.2) years in the analytic sample and 62.5 (19.0) years in the total baseline INSPIRE-T population, with 54.6% and 61.8% female participants, respectively. Subjects possessed a high educational level in the two populations. The mean MMSE score was 28.5 in our analytic sample and 28.6 in the total baseline population with a minimal difference in SD. The proportion of APOE ε4 carriers was marginally lower in the analytic population (16.4%) than in the total baseline population (18.5%). Loneliness T-scores were comparable between the two groups with mean (SD) values of 40.6 (7.4) in the analytic sample versus 40.3 (7.3) in the total baseline population. Mean (SD) concentrations of plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL were 0.2 pg/ml (0.2) and 26.0 pg/ml (15.4) in our analytic population and 0.1 pg/ml (0.2) and 19.8 pg/ml (21.6) in the total baseline INSPIRE-T population.

Main analysis

Linear regression analyses demonstrated a significant positive association of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 (β = 0.01014 [95% CI: 0.00276, 0.01751], p = 0.007) and plasma NfL (β = 0.00984 [95% CI: 0.00426, 0.01541], p = 0.001) in the unadjusted regression models (Table 2). However, these associations were no longer significant after adjusting for covariates according to models 1 and 2 (Model 2: plasma p-tau217: β = 0.00458 [95% CI: −0.00328, 0.01243], p = 0.253, plasma NfL: β = 0.00138 [95% CI: −0.00418, 0.00693], p = 0.626). The addition of the time interval between blood collection and the assessment of loneliness (as a covariate) into regression model 2 did not affect the magnitude or the significance of the associations between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 (β = 0.00461 [95% CI: −0.00326, 0.01247], p = 0.250) or plasma NfL (β = 0.00140 [95% CI: −0.00416, 0.00696], p = 0.621). The estimates for all covariates in the main regression models assessing the associations of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL (unadjusted model and adjusted model 1 and 2) are provided in Supplemental Tables 1 and 2.

Logistic regression demonstrated that loneliness was not significantly associated with plasma p-tau217 positivity in either the unadjusted or the two adjusted regression models (Model 2: OR = 1.01749 [95% CI: 0.96589, 1.07184], p = 0.514) (Table 3).

There was a significant inverse association between loneliness and cognitive performance (MMSE) in both the unadjusted (β = -0.02531 [95% CI: −0.04350, −0.00713], p = 0.006) and adjusted regression model 1 (β = -0.02049 [95% CI: −0.03804, −0.00294], p = 0.022). However, this association was no longer significant after adjusting for further covariates according to model 2 (β = -0.01913 [95% CI: −0.03991, 0.00166], p = 0.071).

Exploratory analysis: moderation analysis

Sex did not moderate the association between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 (β-coefficient interaction = −0.00446 [95% CI: −0.01840, 0.00949], p = 0.530) or the association between loneliness and plasma NfL (β-coefficient interaction = −0.00473 [95% CI: −0.01458, 0.00512], p = 0.346). Similarly, depression did not moderate the association between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 (β-coefficient interaction = 0.00156 [95% CI: −0.01584, 0.01895], p = 0.860) or the association between loneliness and plasma NfL (β-coefficient interaction = −0.00453 [95% CI: −0.01685, 0.00780], p = 0.470). Likewise, the interaction between loneliness and eyesight was not associated with either plasma p-tau217 (β-coefficient interaction = 0.00425, [95% CI: −0.01028, 0.01878], p = 0.566) or plasma NfL (β-coefficient interaction = 0.00275, [95% CI: −0.00723, 0.01272], p = 0.589). Furthermore, hearing did not moderate the association between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 (β-coefficient interaction = 0.01212 [95% CI: −0.00133, 0.02558], p = 0.077) or the association between loneliness and plasma NfL (β-coefficient interaction = 0.00170 [95% CI: −0.00756, 0.01097], p = 0.718). Lastly, APOE ε4 carrier status did not moderate the association between loneliness and plasma p-tau217 (β-coefficient interaction = −0.00829 [95% CI: −0.02628 0.00971], p = 0.366) or the association between loneliness and plasma NfL (β-coefficient interaction = 0.00141 [95% CI: −0.01132, 0.01414], p = 0.828).

Discussion

In this cross-sectional analysis we found that loneliness was not significantly associated with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL in dementia-free older adults in the adjusted multiple linear regression models. More specifically, in the main analysis, the initial positive associations observed in the unadjusted regression models were attenuated and rendered non-significant following a minimal adjustment for age, sex and education (model 1). The further addition of cognitive performance, depressive score, eyesight, hearing and APOE ε4 carrier status as covariates (model 2) corroborated the attenuation of the associations, which remained non-significant, moving further from statistical significance, after this full adjustment. Furthermore, sex, depression, eyesight, hearing or APOE ε4 status did not significantly moderate the associations of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL in our study.

Our null findings on p-tau217 are in accordance with the previous study on loneliness and AD plasma biomarkers that reported no associations between loneliness and plasma p-tau181 at baseline or with changes in plasma p-tau181 levels over time (albeit a different p-tau epitope) in two large samples of cognitively unimpaired older adults. 17 PET imaging studies have also reported no cross-sectional associations between loneliness and cerebral tau and Aβ in cognitively unimpaired 17 and dementia-free subjects. 18 However, it should be noted that some cross-sectional neuro-imaging reports have demonstrated a positive relationship between loneliness and cerebral tau 15 as well as cerebral Aβ burden13–15 in cognitively normal older adults of comparable age to the INSPIRE-T analytic population. These discrepant reports could reflect differences in sample size as the neuroimaging studies demonstrating positive findings contained fewer subjects, therefore as previously reasoned, 17 potentially increasing the likelihood of false-positive results (in the largest of these studies n = 117). Nevertheless, numerous studies have consistently reported positive associations between loneliness and cognitive decline,5,7,8 dementia6,8,10,11,33 and AD.6,7,10,11 Our sensitivity analysis on cognitive performance demonstrated that the cross-sectional association of loneliness with MMSE score, approached but did not reach statistical significance (p = 0.071) in the fully adjusted regression model (model 2), although a significant inverse relationship was observed in the unadjusted regression model and the model adjusted for age, sex and education only (model 1). These findings could be attributable to the insensitivity of the MMSE to detect subtle cognitive impairment particularly in highly educated subjects 34 and/or the fact that the participants of this analysis were dementia-free and only exhibited a 6-point range in MMSE score, skewed towards the upper limit of the scale (24–30), thereby potentially limiting the power to detect a significant association between loneliness and cognitive performance.

In this current analysis, we also observed that loneliness was not associated with plasma NfL after controlling for covariates, which is in keeping with the previous findings in plasma. 17 NfL is a non-specific marker of neurodegeneration and therefore could potentially be associated with loneliness independently of AD-related changes. 35 Indeed, loneliness has been associated with lower total brain volume 36 and lower white matter volume in dementia-free older adults 37 and reduced regional brain volume specifically in frontal white matter and in the putamen and globus pallidus in a population predominantly comprising of cognitively normal older individuals. 38 These structural differences may reflect increased vulnerability to neurodegenerative processes associated with loneliness, although causality remains to be determined. Thus, further investigation is required to elucidate the relationships between loneliness and biomarkers of neurodegeneration.

Consistent with previous findings, 17 we observed that sex did not moderate the associations of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL, despite evidence suggesting that women might be more susceptible to loneliness than men 39 and the risk of AD is higher in women. 12 Likewise, depression did not serve as a moderator of the associations between loneliness and plasma biomarkers. Depression represents an independent risk factor for dementia according to the Lancet Commission on dementia, prevention and care report.19,40 Loneliness, however, appears to be associated with cognitive decline and dementia independently of depression,6,8 even though evidence suggests that loneliness and depression are bidirectionally correlated. 41 Visual and auditory impairment are other risk factors for dementia outlined by the aforementioned Lancet commission on dementia 19 and have been associated with increased loneliness, 31 yet we observed that eyesight and hearing did not moderate the associations of loneliness with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL. The interaction between hearing and loneliness in relation to plasma p-tau217 levels did, however, show a trend towards statistical significance (p = 0.077). Furthermore, we found that APOE ε4 carrier status did not moderate the associations of loneliness with plasma biomarker outcomes. Accordingly, no moderating effect of APOE ε4 status on the association of loneliness with cerebral tau PET 15 and plasma p-tau181 17 has also been reported. It should be highlighted though, that some studies have found that there is a stronger association of loneliness with cerebral Aβ PET imaging measures in APOE ε4 carriers (versus APOE ε4 non-carriers).14,15

Considering the multi-factorial nature of AD pathogenesis, it is plausible that loneliness might be linked to cognitive decline and dementia via other mechanisms distinct from Aβ and tau. Loneliness has been associated with increased waking cortisol, acute phase proteins,42,43 natural killer cell responses to stress 42 and cardiovascular disease. 44 Given the latter, loneliness might be associated with dementia through vascular mechanisms. Indeed, loneliness has been associated with vascular dementia 6 and white matter hyperintensities (WMH). 36 However, evidence to the contrary should be acknowledged. Other studies have reported that loneliness is unrelated to cerebral infarcts at autopsy 9 and small vessel disease markers (including WMH).37,38 More research is needed to fully characterize the range and magnitude of neurobiological changes associated with loneliness and to understand how these pathological processes influence cognitive function and dementia risk.

Despite evidence to suggest that loneliness acts as a risk factor for AD, 6 it should also be borne in mind that AD neuropathology might contribute to feelings of loneliness, which is indeed the direction that some authors have framed the reported cross-sectional findings on neuropathology.14,15 This notion is corroborated by a recent study that showed an interaction between cerebral tau and living alone to heightened increases in loneliness during the COVID-19 pandemic in cognitively unimpaired older adults. 45 Interestingly, behaviors encompassed within the Mild Behavioral Impairment (MBI) framework, such as apathy, irritability, agitation and social inappropriateness are increasingly being recognized as potential early biomarkers of AD. 46 In this context, loneliness may not simply represent a risk factor for dementia, but could itself constitute an early behavioral biomarker reflecting underlying disease-related changes.

The main strength of the current study is the availability of data on loneliness using a multi-item scale, plasma p-tau217 and plasma NfL in a relatively large sample of older adults. Plasma biomarkers were also quantified using high precision immuno-assays. The limitations of the study include its cross-sectional design preventing the investigation of the associations of loneliness with changes in plasma AD biomarkers over time. This limitation is particularly relevant given the current paucity of longitudinal studies examining such relationships. Our current study is also limited by the unavailability in the INSPIRE-T database of other biomarkers relating to AD such as plasma Aβ species and GFAP and neuro-imaging measures of cerebral Aβ and tau, thereby precluding analysis with such outcomes. It should be noted as well, that PROMIS® T-scores are standardized to the U.S general population, which does not affect internal comparison, but may limit interpretation of absolute levels. In addition, we assessed loneliness in late life, which may fall outside the relevant window for detecting its associations with AD-related pathological processes. Lastly, the predominance of well-educated subjects of high socio-economic status in INSPIRE-T could have introduced bias into our findings. 23

In summary, we found that loneliness was not associated with plasma p-tau217 or plasma NfL in dementia-free older adults from the INSPIRE-T baseline cohort. Perspective studies are required to validate these preliminary cross-sectional findings in order to help clarify whether loneliness is associated with AD neuropathology. Studying loneliness in the context of AD is important because of its modifiable nature.

Abstract
. Let ℓ \ell and n n be positive integers with ℓ \ell prime. 2.1.
Conventions

Throughout
k
denotes a number field and
\operatorname{Gal}_{k}
is used to denote the absolute Galois group of
k
. We use
\ell
to denote a prime number and
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
for the ring of
\ell
-adic integers.

By curve we mean a projective nonsingular
1
-dimensional scheme over
k
. For a curve
C/k
, we define the degree of a closed point
x\in C
to be the degree of its residue field
k(x)
as an extension of
k
.

An elliptic curve is a curve of genus
1
with a specified base point. We say an elliptic curve
E
over
k
has complex multiplication, or CM, if its geometric endomorphism ring is strictly larger than
\mathbb{Z}
. For
P\in E(\overline{k})
, we use
k(P)
to denote the field extension of
k
generated by the
x
- and
y
-coordinates of
P
.

For any open subgroup
G\leq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, we define its level to be the smallest integer
\ell^{n}
such that
G=\pi_{n}^{-1}(G(\ell^{n}))
, where
G(\ell^{n})
is the image of
G
under the projection map
\pi_{n}:\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

Subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
appearing as the image of the mod
\ell
Galois representation of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
are described using the notation of Sutherland
[
30
]
. Subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
appearing as the image of the
\ell
-adic Galois representation of a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
are denoted by the notation of Rouse, Sutherland, and Zureick-Brown
[
26
]
. For an open subgroup
G\leq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, this notation is of the form

N.i.g.n

where
N
denotes the level,
i
denotes the index,
g
denotes the genus of the modular curve
X_{G}
, and
n
is an integer used to distinguish groups having the same level, index, and genus.

2.2.
Galois Representations

Let
E/k
be an elliptic curve and let
\ell
be a prime number. By fixing a basis for the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
, we obtain the
\ell
-adic representation given by

\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}:\operatorname{Gal}_{k}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}).

This describes the Galois action on all points in
E(\overline{k})
with order a power of
\ell
. Similarly for any integer
n\geq 1
, we also have the mod
\ell^{n}
representation

\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}:\operatorname{Gal}_{k}\to\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/{\ell^{n}}\mathbb{Z}),

which describes the Galois action on the
\ell^{n}
-torsion subgroup
E[\ell^{n}]
of
E(\bar{k})
. We denote the image of
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
and
\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}
by
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
and
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}
, respectively. By Serre’s Open Image Theorem
[
28
]
, we know that
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
is open in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
for any non-CM elliptic curve
E/k
, so its level is well defined.

2.3.
Modular Curve
X_{1}(n)

For a positive integer
n
, the modular curve
X_{1}(n)
is an algebraic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
whose non-cuspidal points correspond to isomorphism classes of elliptic curves with a distinguished point of order
n
. We may understand the complex points of
X_{1}(n)
by first constructing
\mathbb{H}/\Gamma_{1}(n)
, where
\mathbb{H}
is the upper half plane and the group

\Gamma_{1}(n)\coloneqq\left\{{\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):c\equiv 0\pmod{n},\,a\equiv d\equiv 1\pmod{n}\right\}

acts via linear fractional transformations. By adding a finite number of cusps, we obtain a compact Riemann surface whose complex points agree with
X_{1}(n)(\mathbb{C})
. The non-cuspidal points of
X_{1}(n)(\mathbb{C})
correspond to pairs
(E,P)
where
E/\mathbb{C}
is an elliptic curve and
P\in E(\mathbb{C})
is of order
n
, up to
\mathbb{C}
-isomorphism. If
n\geq 4
, the same holds true when
\mathbb{C}
is replaced by any number field
k
. That is, for these
n
the curve
X_{1}(n)
is a fine moduli space.

If
E/k
is an elliptic curve with
P\in E(\overline{k})
of order
n
, then
(E,P)
induces a
k
-valued point on
X_{1}(n)
by the moduli interpretation. However, as explained in
\lx@sectionsign 2.1
we define the degree of
(E,P)
to be the degree of the residue field of the corresponding closed point
[E,P]
. This degree can be computed explicitly using the following result.

Lemma 1
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
, and let
P\in E
be a point of order
n
. Then the residue field of the closed point
x\in X_{1}(n)
associated to
(E,P)
is given by

\mathbb{Q}(x)\cong\mathbb{Q}(j(E),\mathfrak{h}(P)),

where
\mathfrak{h}\rightarrow E/\operatorname{Aut}(E)\cong\mathbf{P}^{1}
is a Weber function for
E
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
8
, Lemma 2.5]
.
∎

Our study of isolated points makes frequent use of the natural maps between modular curves. We record the degree of one such map below.

Proposition 2
.

For positive integers
a
and
b
, there is a natural
\mathbb{Q}
-rational map
f\colon X_{1}(ab)\rightarrow X_{1}(a)
sending
[E,P]
to
[E,bP]
with

\deg(f)=c_{f}\cdot b^{2}\prod_{p\mid b,p\nmid a}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right).

Here,
c_{f}=1/2
if
a\leq 2
and
ab>2
and
c_{f}=1
otherwise.

Proof.

The degree computation follows from
[
12
, p.66]
.
∎

2.4.
Modular Curve

The modular curve

is an algebraic curve over

whose non-cuspidal points correspond to isomorphism classes of elliptic curves with a distinguised cyclic subgroup of order

as the compactification of the Riemann surface given by

, then

gives a point in

-isomorphism. That is,

For a non-CM elliptic curve

with a cyclic subgroup

of order

is precisely the extension over

over which

,

]

is given below.

2.5.
Isolated Points

Let
C/k
be a curve with a point
P\in C(k)
. For a positive integer
d
, let
C^{d}
denote the direct product of
d
copies of
C
. We denote the
d
-th symmetric product of
C
by
C^{(d)}
, i.e., the quotient of
C^{d}
by the symmetric group
S_{d}
. We have a natural map

\phi_{d}\colon C^{(d)}\to\operatorname{Jac}(C)
given by
(P_{1},\ldots,P_{d})\mapsto[P_{1}+\ldots P_{d}-dP]
. Let
x\in C
be a closed point of degree
d
, which we may identify with a point in
C^{(d)}(k)
by viewing it as a Galois orbit of points in
C(\overline{k})
having length
d
. We say
x
is:

•

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-parameterized
if there is
x^{\prime}\in C^{(d)}(k)
such that
\phi_{d}(x)=\phi_{d}(x^{\prime})
. Otherwise we say
x
is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-isolated
.

•

AV-parameterized
if there exists a positive rank subabelian variety
A
of
\operatorname{Jac}(C)
such that
\phi_{d}(x)+A\subset\operatorname{im}(\phi_{d})
. Otherwise we say
x
is
AV-isolated
.

We say
x
is
isolated
if it is both
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-isolated and AV-isolated.

Any curve
C/k
contains only finitely many isolated points, and it has infinitely many points of degree
d
if and only if there exists a degree
d
point which is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-parameterized or AV-parameterized; see
[
4
, Theorem 4.2]
. In particular, if
C
has genus at least 2, then any point in
C(k)
is isolated by Faltings’ Theorem
[
14
]
. Thus one can consider the study of isolated points as a kind of generalization of the study of rational points. See recent work of Viray and Vogt
[
33
]
for more discussion.

One key result that can be used to rule out the existence of isolated points is the following.

Theorem 3

(Bourdon, Ejder, Liu, Odumodu, Viray
[
4
]
)
.

Let
f:C\rightarrow D
be a finite map of curves over
k
, and let
x\in C
be an isolated point. If
\deg(x)=\deg(f(x))\cdot\deg(f),
then
f(x)\in D
is isolated.

The following theorems characterize both

-isolated

associated to known

]
. They can be deduced from a slightly modified version of the algorithms used in

]

[

]

]

with

. In particular, if the output is the empty set, then there is no isolated point on

associated to

, even as

[

]
worked in an analogous way for the modular curve

. By modifying these algorithms to take as input a given

-isolated

Let

be a non-CM elliptic curve, and suppose

]

with

unless

and

or
-7\cdot 137^{3}\cdot 2083^{3}

Given
\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
, we will apply a streamlined version of the algorithm of

7
]

\{\langle\ell^{a_{1}},d_{1}\rangle,\langle\ell^{a_{2}},d_{2}\rangle,\dots,\langle\ell^{a_{r}},d_{r}\rangle\}
such that there exists an isolated

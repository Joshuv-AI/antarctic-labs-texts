A prospective, randomized, controlled, open-label blinded end point clinical study of Ayulite in comparison with vitamin E for efficacy and safety in patients with non-alcoholic fatty liver disease
Authors: Vyankatesh Shivane, Sanjay Tamoli, Amol Hartalkar, Kamlakar Gajre, Hema Shah, Ameet Mandot, Sheetal Padalkar, Rohit Barnabas, Swapnali Padalkar
Published: 2026

Introduction

Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is defined as the presence of excessive amount of fat in the liver evidenced by imaging or histology. NAFLD excludes significant alcohol consumption, long term steatogenic medication, or monogenic hereditary disorders by etiology [1]. NAFLD is further classified in to non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) and non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) depending upon the presence or absence of hepatocellular injury or fibrosis. Presence of ≥5% hepatic steatosis without the evidence of hepatocellular injury (e.g., ballooning) is called as NAFL and the presence of ≥5% hepatic steatosis and inflammation with hepatocyte injury, with or without any fibrosis is called as NASH [2].

Globally, NAFLD affects about 30% of the population. In the United States, NAFLD is the most common form of liver disease affecting about 30% of adults [2]. In India, about 38.6% of adults and 35.4% of children are estimated to be affected with NAFLD [3]. The incidence of NAFLD is found higher in males and in overweight or obese people. Also, there was 3-fold increase in the incidence rate of NAFLD between 2000 and 2015 [4]. Most often NAFLD is associated with other health conditions like obesity, diabetes, insulin resistance and dyslipidemia. About 37% to 93% of those undergoing bariatric surgery was reported to have NAFLD and 26% to 44% had NASH in the US. NAFLD often accompanied by advanced fibrosis develops in about 33% to 66% of patients with type 2 diabetes. The prevalence of NAFLD was 54% in patients with dyslipidemia, which was increased to 78% in those with highest triglyceride to high-density–lipoprotein cholesterol ratios [2]. NASH if left untreated can lead to advanced fibrosis, cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma [5].

Diet, weight loss and physical activity are the most important recommendations in effective management of NAFLD. Dietary interventions play a pivotal role in the management and prevention of NAFLD. Evidence indicates that dietary patterns with lower food insulin index, higher total antioxidant capacity, and adherence to the Mediterranean diet are associated with reduced odds of NAFLD and improved liver health [[6], [7], [8]]. Herbal and functional foods play a beneficial role in the management of NAFLD by improving hepatic enzymes, lipid profile, oxidative stress, and inflammation. Clinical trials using pomegranate peel, soy milk, purslane, spinach extract, sumac, and extra virgin olive oil have demonstrated these effects, especially when combined with weight reduction strategies [[9], [10], [11], [12]].Treatment with pharmacological drugs is reserved for NASH patients with significant fibrosis (≥F2) and for patients with high risk of disease progression (i.e., having comorbidities such as metabolic syndrome, diabetes) but with less severe disease. In such cases, only vitamin E and Pioglitazone are recommended by the European- and American Association for the Study of the Liver [5].

Vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE) is a non-invasive technique used to assess liver fibrosis. It is also known by the brand name FibroScan®, which is a commonly used device for performing transient elastography. Ayulite is a registered Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine, licensed by the FDA and manufactured at WHO GMP certified manufacturing facility. Ayulite is a liver tonic consisting of a 11 herbal ingredients to help maintain healthy liver and is available as capsules in the market. Ayulite has been clinically studied for liver health, but has not been specifically tested in patients with NAFLD. Therefore, the objective of the study was to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Ayulite in comparison to vitamin E in patients with NAFLD wherein the patients were assessed for fibrosis using VCTE.

Methods

Trial design

The study was designed as a prospective, randomized, open-label blinded end point (PROBE) controlled clinical study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of Ayulite in patients with NAFLD where the patients were treated for a duration of 90 days as per CONSORT statement. Vitamin E was used as a active control to compare with Ayulite. The patientswere randomly assigned to Ayulite group and Vitamin E group. The treatments were known to both the patients and doctors but the experts who evaluated the outcome of the treatments were blinded to the treatment allocation. The patients were called at site every four weekly for safety and efficacy assessment.

Study population

The patients of either sex aged between 20 and 60 years with grade 1 and grade 2 NAFLD diagnosed by ultrasonography willing to participate in the study after signing informed consent form were recruited. The patients with active jaundice or hepatitis, uncontrolled diabetes (HbA1c ≥ 10%), chronic alcoholism, biliary obstruction, cirrhosis, malignancy or drugs causing hepatic steatosis were excluded.

Interventions

The patients in the Ayulite group were instructed to take 2 capsules of Ayulite (each weighing 440 mg) along with water twice a day after food for 90 days and patients in the vitamin E group were instructed to take 1 softgel of vitamin E (Evion 400 mg, manufactured by Procter & Gamble Hygiene and Health Care Ltd., India) along with water once a day after food for 90 days. Each Ayulite capsule contained extracts of Andrographis paniculata, Tecomella undulata, Tephrosia purpurea, Phyllanthus niruri, Eclipta alba, Holarrhena antidysenterica, Glycyrrhiza glabra (licorice), Fumaria parviflora, Tinospora cordifolia, Hedychium spicatum and Boerhaavia diffusa. Each capsule of vitamin E contained 400 mg of vitamin E. Ayulite is a registered Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine, licensed by the FDA and manufactured at WHO GMP certified manufacturing facility of Welex Laboratories Pvt Ltd., Mumbai.

Manufacturing process: The above ingredients are first tested and approved by quality control and then taken in the mentioned quantity and proportion as per the required batch size. Dry blending of these ingredients are done in an octagonal blender. Preservatives and lubricants are added into the mass and blending is done to ensure proper mixing. The blend is then sent for capsule filling in HPMC capsules 0 size capsules. After thorough in-process QC checks, the capsules are sent for bottle/blister packing. The final product is further tested as per specifications. The Ayulite capsules contain a blend of 11 herbal extracts which support liver function and digestion.

Outcome measures

Comparative changes in fatty liver stage (using Ultrasonography(USG)/VCTE) between the two groups was taken as the primary outcome measure. Liver Stiffness measure (LSM) obtained using VCTE was used to assess the liver fibrosis. The ratio of interquartile range (IQR) to median (IQR/M) of LSM was found to be lesser than 30% indicating the quality and reliability of the LSM score obtained using VCTE [13]. The primary outcome assessments were carried out on baseline and at the end of study. Comparative changes between the two groups on liver function, lipid profile, glycosylated haemoglobin, fasting serum insulin, fasting blood sugar, HOMA-IR, body weight, BMI and safety assessments were considered as the secondary outcome measures.

Safety measures like Liver function tests viz., ALT, AST, serum total bilirubin, serum alkaline phosphatase, and serum albumin were carried out at baseline and every four weekly. Lipid profile, Fasting serum insulin, glycosylated haemoglobin, fasting blood sugar, HOMA IR, Body weight & BMI were done at baseline and end of the study. HbA1c was measured using the D-10 HbA1c Testing System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Inc., Hercules, CA, USA) with HPLC method while biochemical parameters were analysed using a fully automated biochemistry analyzer (BioSystems A25; BioSystems S.A., Barcelona, Spain). Assessment for tolerability and safety was carried out using laboratory parameters, vital signs and adverse events.

Implementation

The study was conducted from June 2021 to January 2022 at two sites after due approval by respective Institutional/Independent Ethics Committees. The study was registered with CTRI (CTRI/2021/06/034244) and was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the Helsinki Declaration of 1975, as revised in 2008.

The patients attending the out-patient department and were willing provide written informed consent were included in the screening. USG was carried out on all subjects to assess fatty liver (steatosis) and VCTE was carried out on selected subjects to assess fatty liver and fibrosis (liver stiffness). USG and VCTE of liver were performed by trained and experienced sonologist, who was blinded to the allotted study group/treatment.

The patients meeting the inclusion criteria were randomized to Ayulite group or vitamin E group using a computer-generated list. General and systemic examinations of subjects were carried out for vitals, body weight, body mass index and clinical symptoms at each visit. Diet and exercise counselling was done at each visit. All the subjects who took at least one dose of the study drug and who gave at least one post baseline efficacy measurement were considered for safety evaluation in the study. All cases that completed the study as per the protocol were considered as “Per Protocol Population” and were randomised to 1:1 ratio.

Participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to either the intervention group or the control group using a simple randomization procedure. The random allocation sequence was generated using a computer-based random number generator. Each participant was assigned a unique study identification number, and the allocation was concealed in sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes, which were opened only after the participant was enrolled in the study.

Statistical methods

Sample size calculation: A total of 38 participants (19 per arm) were included based on a non-inferiority design using change in liver stiffness measurement (LSM) by FibroScan as the primary endpoint. LSM is a validated, non-invasive marker of liver fibrosis suitable for NASH trials. The design compared the herbal product with vitamin E, with a non-inferiority margin of 1.0 kPa, 80% power, and a one-sided 2.5% significance level, based on standard statistical assumptions.

Categorical variables were expressed as actual numbers and percentages, whereas continuous variables were expressed as the median, range or mean, standard deviation as applicable. Statistical tests were carried out to compare study groups as per the distribution (normality), Student's t-test (normative), Mann-Whitney statistic (non-parametric), Chi-square statistic (categorical) and ANOVA as required. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analysis were carried out using GraphPad InStat Version 3.6.

A total of 85 patients were screened of which 17 patients did not meet the inclusion criteria and one patients withdrew from the study. A total of 67 patients were randomized, Ayulite group (n = 33) and vitamin E group (n = 34). Six patients dropped out of the study with 4 patients from Ayulite Group and 2 patients from vitamin E group. Finally, there were 29 evaluable patients in the Ayulite Group and 32 subjects in the vitamin E Group.

Assessment of effect on liver fat

Liver fat was assessed using USG and from Controlled Attenuation Parameter (CAP) score obtained using VCTE. Four out of 25 patients in the Ayulite group and 7 out of 32 patients in the vitamin E group were completely free from fatty liver as assessed by using USG on Day 90. Three patients with grade 1 fatty liver and 1 subject with grade 2 fatty liver in the Ayulite group were completely recovered. Similarly, 4 patients with grade 1 fatty liver and all 3 patients with grade 2 fatty liver in the vitamin E group were completely recovered. There was no significant difference between the two groups on fatty liver at end of study. This indicates comparable efficacy of Ayulite in treating fatty liver to vitamin E. Effect of treatment on fatty liver along with details of the number of patients with fatty liver of different grades at baseline and day 90 assessed using USG. Notably all patients with grade 2 fatty liver were recovered with Vitamin E at the end of study.

There was a significant increase in the CAP score compared to baseline in the Ayulite group (p < 0.05) and non-significant decrease in the CAP score compared to baseline in Vitamin E group at the end of the study (Table 2, Table 3). Overall, the reduction in the CAP score was significant in Vitamin E group compared to Ayulite group (p < 0.05) at the end of the study. Vitamin E significantly reduced CAP score within 90 days (p < 0.05).

Assessment of effect on liver fibrosis

There was a significant reduction in the LSM score at the end of study compared to baseline within the Ayulite group (p < 0.05) and vitamin E group (p < 0.05) indicating the effectiveness of both the substance in reducing fibrosis at the end of study. Whereas, the reduction in LSM was not significant when compared between the Ayulite and Vitamin E groups (Table 2, Table 3).

Effect on other parameters (Table 3)

Within the group and between the group analysis of the results of liver function tests, lipids, glycosylated haemoglobin, fasting serum insulin, fasting blood sugar, HOMA-IR, body weight and BMI did not show any significant difference except for AST and HDL-C levels, wherein AST was found significantly (p < 0.05) reduced from 23.20 ± 6.06 U/L to 21.13 ± 5.95 in the Ayulite group and HDL-C was found significantly (p < 0.05) increased from 49.28 ± 15.81 mg/dL to 54.58 ± 19.90 mg/dL in the Ayulite and from 46.06 ± 11.81 mg/dL to 51.32 ± 18.21 mg/dL in the vitamin E group at the end of the study. Both Ayulite & Vitamin E group showed non-significant reduction in HbA1c, Fasting Insulin Levels, HOMA IR, Body weight and BMI at the end of the study compared to baseline.

Safety and tolerability of Ayulite (Table 3)

Eight adverse events were reported in the Ayulite group and 10 adverse events were reported in the vitamin E group. The reported adverse events include increased sweating, stomach ache, vomiting, heel pain, dry cough, fever, throat pain, cold, weakness, benign paroxysmal positional vertigo, loose motion, constipation, headache and body pain.There was no significant difference observed in the safety parameters such as blood pressure (diastolic and systolic), creatinine, RBC, WBC and platelets within Ayulite and vitamin E group indicating the safety of Ayulite and vitamin E at the tested doses and duration.

Discussion

NAFLD is caused primarily due to high fat diet coupled with lack of physical activity [14]. In the present study, mean BMI in the Ayulite and vitamin E groups at the baseline were 31.65 ± 7.04 kg/m2 and 31.43 ± 7.16 kg/m2 respectively indicating the obese nature of the study population. In this study, 13.8% of patients were found to be free from fatty liver as assessed using USG after receiving 2 capsules Ayulite twice a day for 90 days. This effect of Ayulite was numerically (p NS) comparable to vitamin E, which showed recovery in 21.9% of the population when administered at a dose of 400 mg per day for a period of 90 days.

Previous studies have reported that Ingredients of Ayulite have been reported to be effective against fatty liver. A polyherbal formulation containing Tephrosia purpurea and Phyllanthus niruri was found to reduce fatty liver in a clinical study [15]. Glycyrrhiza glabra (licorice) has reduced hepatic steatosis in women with NAFLD [16]. Andrographolide, isoandrographolide and 14-deoxy-11, 12-didehydroandrographolide from Andrographis paniculata [[17], [18], [19]], Phyllanthus niruri [20], Eclipta alba [21], Glycyrrhiza glabra (licorice) [22] and constituents of licorice such as licochalcone A [23], glycycoumarin [24] and glycyrrhizic glabra were reported to reduce hepatic steatosis in various preclinical studies. Tecomella undulata was reported to reduce hepatic fat and also inflammation [25].

Effectiveness of Ayulite was comparable with vitamin E against hepatic steatosis as assessed using VCTE as a diagnostic modality. No published studies seem to have reported effect of any Ayulite ingredients against fatty liver using VCTE. Evaluation using VCTE showed that Ayulite significantly (p < 0.05) reduced fibrosis (LSM) in patients with NAFLD, which was comparable to vitamin E. A polyherbal formulation containing Tinospora cordifolia, Eclipta alba and Phyllanthus niruri was also reported to reduced fibrosis in patients with NAFLD as assessed by transient elastography in a single arm clinical study [26]. Phyllanthus niruri alone has also shown fibrosis reducing effect in rats fed with HFD diet [20].

Ayulite was very well tolerated as there were no adverse events that are related to the treatment and there were no statistically significant difference in any of the other parameters such as liver function tests, blood pressure, lipids, HbA1c, haemoglobin, total leukocyte counts, platelets, creatinine, body weight and BMI either within group or between the groups at the baseline and post treatment on Day 90. The strength of the study was the study design being a randomised control trial and the limitation was small sample size and shorter duration, because of which subgroup analysis was not done with respect to comorbidities like T2DM, hypertension, hypothyroidism, hyperlipidemia etc, detailed dietary and physical activity status was not adjusted for the outcomes. in addition to NAFLD. Further studies are needed with Ayulite on a larger sample size and longer duration.

Conclusion

Ayulite was a safe and efficacious therapy when administered as 2 capsules twice daily for a period of 90 days and comparable to vitamin E 400 mg per day in NAFLD with grade 1 and 2 fatty liver. Ayulite capsules can be recommended as a safe and effective agent for the management of NAFLD in addition to Vitamin E.

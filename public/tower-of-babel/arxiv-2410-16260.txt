Multi-product Zeno effect achieving higher order convergence rates
Tim Möbus

Abstract
The quantum Zeno effect is a fundamental mechanism for implementing the effective dynamics of projected Hamiltonian and Lindbladian systems. It approximates the target projected evolution by interleaving Hamiltonian or Lindblad dynamics with quantum operations associated with the desired subspace. In contrast to the related Trotter product formula, the best-known convergence rate of the quantum Zeno effect is typically limited to order 1 / n 1/n . In this work, we improve this convergence rate by employing a multi-product formula, thereby achieving arbitrarily high-order convergence of the form 1 / n K + 1 1/n^{K+1} . This yields an improved approximation scheme for Zeno-like expectation values via an efficient post-processing method. The approach combines a modified Chernoff lemma, an adapted Dunford-Segal approximation, holomorphic functional calculus, and Chebyshev interpolation. We illustrate the method with the bosonic cat code and also consider the broader class of systems governed by the Bang-Bang decoupling method.

2
Preliminaries

Before presenting the results, we introduce the mathematical framework and notation. Throughout,
(\mathcal{X},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}})
denotes a Banach space and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
the Banach space of bounded linear endomorphisms (operators) on
\mathcal{X}
with operator norm
\|A\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\coloneqq\sup_{\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}=1}\|A(x)\|_{\mathcal{X}}
for
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and identity
\mathbbm{1}
. We call an operator
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
a projection if
A^{2}=A
and a contraction if
\|A\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq 1
. The resolvent set of
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
is defined by
\mathrm{res}(A)\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbbm{C}\;|\;(z-A)\text{ is bijective}\}
, its spectrum by
\textrm{spec}(A)\coloneqq\mathbbm{C}\backslash\mathrm{res}(A)
, and the resolvent of
A
is defined by
R(z,A)\coloneqq(z-A)^{-1}
for all
z\in\mathrm{res}(A)
.

Next, an operator-valued map
I\ni t\mapsto X(t)\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
defined on an interval
I\subset\mathbbm{R}
is continuous if it is continuous with respect to the operator norm, strongly continuous if the vector-valued map
I\ni t\mapsto X(t)x\in\mathcal{X}
is continuous w.r.t. the norm on
\mathcal{X}
, and differentiable at
t\in I
if it is Fréchet differentiable at
t
with respect to the operator norm. If the operator-valued map is (strongly) continuous on
[a,b]\subset I
, the Bochner integral
\int_{a}^{b}X(t)dt
(resp.
\int_{a}^{b}X(t)xdt
for
x\in\mathcal{X}
) is well-defined and extends the Lebesgue integral to operator-valued maps. It satisfies the usual properties of linearity, the triangle inequality, additivity of disjoint sets, dominated convergence, and the fundamental theorem of calculus can be proven:

X(b)-X(a)=\int_{a}^{b}\frac{d}{dt}X(t)dt\,,

assuming that
X(t)
is differentiable for all
t\in(a,b)
with continuous derivative
[
28
, Thm. 3.7.4]
. Next, a strongly continuous (
C_{0}
-)semigroup is defined by an operator-valued map
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that satisfies the following properties for all
t,s\geq 0
and
x\in\mathcal{X}
:

T_{t}T_{s}=T_{t+s},\quad T_{0}=\mathbbm{1},\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\downarrow 0}T_{t}x=x\,.

Note that the latter continuity condition is in the strong operator topology, i.e., pointwise convergence for
x\in\mathcal{X}
, and not in the uniform operator topology with respect to the operator norm. Every
C_{0}-
semigroup is uniquely determined by its generator given by

\mathcal{L}(x)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}(T_{t}(x)-x)\,,

for any
x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:t\mapsto T_{t}(x)\text{ is differentiable on }\mathbbm{R}_{\geq 0}\}
, which is an unbounded, densely defined, and closed operator, justifying the notation
T_{t}=e^{t\mathcal{L}}
. An unbounded, densely defined operator is a linear map
\mathcal{L}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subset\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
defined on a domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
dense in
\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, it is called closed if its graph
\{(x,\mathcal{L}(x))\,|\,x\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\}
is a closed set in the product space
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
. The generator is bounded if and only if
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is continuous in the uniform topology
[
17
, Thm. II.1.4]
. By convention, we extend all densely defined and bounded operators by the bounded linear extension theorem to bounded operators on
\mathcal{X}

[
31
, Thm. 2.7-11]
.

Quantum mechanical systems are defined on a separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
over the complex field
\mathbbm{C}
with an inner product
\braket{\cdot\,|\,\cdot}
and induced norm
\|\psi\|\coloneqq\sqrt{\absolutevalue{\braket{\psi\,|\,\psi}}}
. The adjoint
A^{\dagger}
of
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is uniquely defined by
\braket{A\phi\,|\,\varphi}=\braket{\phi\,|\,A^{\dagger}\varphi}
for all
\phi,\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, and
A
is positive if
A=B^{\dagger}B
for some
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then, the trace norm of
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined by
\|A\|_{1}=\tr[|A|]
with absolute value
|A|\coloneqq\sqrt{A^{\dagger}A}

[
48
, Thm. 2.4.4]
and trace
\tr[|A|]=\sum_{j\in\mathbbm{N}}\bra{\varphi_{j}}|A|\ket{\varphi_{j}}
for any orthonormal basis
\{\ket{\varphi_{j}}\}_{j\in\mathbbm{N}}
. The Banach space of trace-class operators is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{1}=\{A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\|A\|_{1}<\infty\}

[
48
, Sec. 3.6]
. The subset
S_{1}\subset\mathcal{T}_{1}
of positive operators with trace
1
is called density operators or quantum states, which evolve via quantum operations in the Schrödinger picture. In the Schrödinger picture, a quantum operation
\mathcal{E}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
is a completely positive, i.e.
\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathbbm{1}(X)\geq 0
for all
X\geq 0
, and trace non-increasing, i.e.
\tr[\mathcal{E}(X)]\leq\tr[X]
for all
X\geq 0
, linear map, and a quantum channel if equality holds in the latter. Then, a
C_{0}
-semigroup defined on
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{T}_{1}
is called a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup if it is a quantum operation as well. To connect the first operator theoretical part and the quantum mechanical part of this section, note that every quantum operation is a contraction on
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{T}_{1}
with respect to the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{1\rightarrow 1}
(or diamond norm)
[
56
]
.

Remark 2

. The proof uses the holomorphic functional calculus to define the perturbed eigenprojections on the unit circle (see
Lemma

3.6
). This is then used to decompose the Zeno sequence into a part related to the spectrum in
\mathbbm{D}_{\delta}
, which converges to
0
, and another part, for which we follow the proof of
Theorem

3.4
. Finally, the expansion of the perturbed eigenprojection is used to conclude the proof.

4
Multi-product Zeno effect

With the above proven higher order error expansion for the generalized quantum Zeno effect, we can construct a multi-product formula for the quantum Zeno effect similar to the multi-product trotter formulas
[
13
,
13
]
. Due to the strong quantum mechanical motivation, we construct the Multi-product Zeno effect for quantum operation
M
and quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroups
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
defined on trace-class operators
\mathcal{T}_{1}
of an separable Hilbert space, which are by definition contractions. However, the the following also works for operators, as discussed in
Section

3
.

4.1
Multi-product scheme

For a given vector of real values
c\in\mathbbm{R}^{K+1}
, we define the multi-product Zeno effect by

(7)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\left(Me^{\frac{t}{n_{\ell}}\mathcal{L}}\right)^{n_{\ell}}\,.

The goal is to choose the coefficients such that the higher-order error terms found in
Theorem

3.7
cancel out. This is first demonstrated using the first-order expansion of the projective Zeno effect.

Projective
2
-product Zeno effect

Recall that
Proposition

3.3
proves that for any projective quantum operation
P
, with
\overline{P}=(\mathbbm{1}-P)
, and a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{t\mathcal{L}})_{t\geq 0}
, where
P\mathcal{L}
,
\mathcal{L}P
, and
P\mathcal{L}^{2}
are bounded, there is an explicit bound such that for
n\to\infty
and
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\left\|(Pe^{\frac{t}{n}\mathcal{L}})^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P-\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\tau_{1}P\mathcal{L}P}P\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{L},P]Pe^{t(1-\tau_{1})P\mathcal{L}P}d\tau_{1}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{}-\frac{t}{n}e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\mathcal{L}\overline{P}\right\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\,.

Then, we search for a
c\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}
such that the multi-product formula for
K=1
and
n_{\ell}=\ell n
satisfies

\sum_{\ell=1}^{2}c_{\ell}(Pe^{\frac{t}{n\ell}\mathcal{L}})^{n\ell}-c_{\ell}e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P-c_{\ell}\frac{1}{n\ell}E_{1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)

where

E_{1}\coloneqq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\tau_{1}P\mathcal{L}P}P\mathcal{L}[\mathcal{L},P]Pe^{t(1-\tau_{1})P\mathcal{L}P}d\tau_{1}-te^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\mathcal{L}\overline{P}\,.

This is satisfied if the zeroth order is unchanged and the first-order error cancels or equivalently if the following system of linear equations is satisfied:

\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
\frac{1}{1}&\frac{1}{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}

which is solved by
c_{1}=-1
and
c_{2}=2
. This gives the following
2
-product scheme:

Corollary 4.1

(2-product Zeno effect)

Let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}))
be the generator of a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup,
P\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{T}_{1})
be any projective quantum operation such that
P\mathcal{L},P\mathcal{L}^{2},\mathcal{L}P\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
are bounded,
O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be an observable, and
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
. Then for all
n\geq 1
and
t\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|2(Pe^{\frac{t}{2n}\mathcal{L}}P)^{2n}-(Pe^{\frac{t}{n}\mathcal{L}}P)^{n}-e^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\,,

in particular,

\displaystyle\Bigl|2\tr[O(Pe^{\frac{t}{2n}\mathcal{L}}P)^{2n}(\rho)]-\tr[O(Pe^{\frac{t}{n}\mathcal{L}}P)^{n}(\rho)]-\tr[Oe^{tP\mathcal{L}P}P(\rho)]\Bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\,.

This means that running two Zeno effects in parallel with different step sizes can improve the convergence rate to
\frac{1}{n^{2}}
, while only linearly increasing the number of channel usages.

Generalized Multi-product Zeno effect

In the following, we generalize the above scheme, similar to the multi-product Trotter formulas discussed, for example, in
[
13
,
11
]
, using the higher-order expansion of the generalized quantum Zeno effect (see
Theorem

3.7
). That is, there exists a sequence of bounded operators
\{E_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
such that

\Bigl\|(Me^{\frac{t}{n}\mathcal{L}})^{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}^{n}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}^{n}E_{k,j}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\right)\,.

For a quantum operator
M
admitting the uniform power convergence assumption

\Bigl\|M^{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}^{n}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\leq c\delta^{n}

given a
c\geq 0
,
n\in\mathbbm{N}
, peripheral eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\subset\partial\mathbbm{D}_{1}
, and a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup with generator
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}))
, so that
M\mathcal{L}^{k}
,
\mathcal{L}M
are bounded for all
k\in\{1,\dots,K+1\}
. Additionally, assume that the peripheral eigenvalues are periodic, i.e., the phases are rational:

\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\subset\{e^{2\pi iq}\,|\,q\in\mathbbm{Q}\}\,.

Therefore, there exists a common period
p
such that
\lambda_{j}^{p}=\lambda_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,...,J\}
. Then, choosing
n_{\ell}=m_{\ell}pn
, we obtain

(8)

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}pn}\mathcal{L}})^{m_{\ell}pn}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\right.

\displaystyle\left.{}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{(pn)^{k}}\Bigl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}\frac{c_{\ell}}{m_{\ell}^{k}}\Bigr)\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}E_{k,j}\right\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\right)\,.

if

(9)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}m_{\ell}^{-k}=0\quad\text{for }k=1,...,K\,.

For the choice
m_{\ell}=\ell
, the multi-product Zeno effect converges with a rate of order
\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}
, provided that the vector
c\in\mathbbm{R}^{K+1}
satisfies the following system of linear equations:

(10)

\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\ldots&1\\
\frac{1}{1}&\frac{1}{2}&\ldots&\frac{1}{K+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\frac{1}{1^{K+1}}&\frac{1}{2^{K+1}}&\ldots&\frac{1}{(K+1)^{K+1}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\\
\vdots\\
c_{K+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}\,.

Note that the system of linear equations in Eq. (
10
) is a Vandermonde system and therefore invertible. However, this equidistant-grid choice is typically ill-conditioned for large
K
(see
[
34
,
11
]
). More importantly for the present setting, the coefficients grow exponentially: solving the Vandermonde system yields

c_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell-1}\binom{K+1}{\ell}\ell^{K},

and thus
S_{K}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}|c_{\ell}|
satisfies

(11)

S_{K}\in\Omega(e^{K}),

as discussed in
[
34
]
. Since
S_{K}
is part of the error bound, this limits the scalability of the multi-product Zeno effect for large
K
.

Corollary 4.2

(Equidistant-grid case)

Let
M
be a quantum operation satisfying the uniform power convergence (
3
) with periodic eigenvalues, i.e.,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\subset\{e^{2\pi iq}\,|\,q\in\mathbbm{Q}\}
, with period
p\in\mathbbm{N}
, and let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}))
be the generator of a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup satisfying
M\mathcal{L}^{k}
and
\mathcal{L}M
bounded for all
k\in\{1,...,K+1\}
. For
m_{\ell}=\ell\in\{1,...,K+1\}
, any
\varepsilon>0
, all observables
O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, states
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\frac{t}{n}<\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\bigl(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}np}\mathcal{L}}\bigr)^{m_{\ell}np}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,,

in particular,

\displaystyle\tr\biggl[O\biggl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\bigl(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}np}\mathcal{L}}\bigr)^{m_{\ell}np}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\biggr)(\rho)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,.

Here, the coefficients
c_{\ell}
are given by
c_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell-1}\binom{K+1}{\ell}\ell^{K}
so that
\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}|c_{\ell}|\in\Omega(e^{K})
and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}m_{\ell}=\frac{(K+1)(K+2)}{2}
.

Well-conditioned multi-product grids

To overcome this limitation, we choose
m_{\ell}
in
n_{\ell}\coloneqq m_{\ell}\,p\,n
following the approach discussed in the Hamiltonian-simulation literature
[
13
,
34
,
11
]
. Recall that by
Theorem

3.7
each sequence satisfies the error bound in
Equation

8
under the condition
Equation

9
, i.e.

\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}m_{\ell}^{-k}=0\quad\text{for }k=1,...,K\,.

is satisfied. This linear system has a
(K+1)\times(K+1)
matrix determined by
\{m_{\ell}\}
as observed in
Equation

10
. Next, we follow
[
34
]
(see also
[
11
]
) and choose
m_{\ell}
as an approximate Chebyshev grid.

Theorem 4.3

Let
M
be a quantum operation satisfying the uniform power convergence (
3
) with periodic eigenvalues, i.e.,
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{J}\subset\{e^{2\pi iq}\,|\,q\in\mathbbm{Q}\}
, with period
p\in\mathbbm{N}
, and let
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}))
be the generator of a quantum dynamical
C_{0}
-semigroup satisfying
M\mathcal{L}^{k}
and
\mathcal{L}M
bounded for all
k\in\{1,...,K+1\}
. Then for all
\varepsilon>0
and all observables
O\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, states
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{1}(\mathcal{H})
, and
\frac{t}{n}<\varepsilon
, there exist pairwise distinct integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{K+1}\in\mathbb{N}
and a coefficient vector
c\in\mathbbm{R}^{K+1}
solving the linear system (
9
) such that the multi-product Zeno effect converges with rate
\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}
:

\displaystyle\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\bigl(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}np}\mathcal{L}}\bigr)^{m_{\ell}np}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,,

in particular,

\displaystyle\tr\biggl[O\biggl(\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\bigl(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}np}\mathcal{L}}\bigr)^{m_{\ell}np}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\biggr)(\rho)\biggr]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

First, recall that by
Theorem

3.7
, for each fixed
\ell\in\{1,\dots,K+1\}
and
n_{\ell}=m_{\ell}pn
(compare to
Equation

8
), there exist bounded operators
E_{k,j}
(independent of
n
) and a remainder
R_{\ell,n}
such that

\Bigl\|(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}pn}\mathcal{L}})^{m_{\ell}pn}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{(m_{\ell}pn)^{k}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}E_{k,j}-R_{\ell,n}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\right)\,.

Then, summing over
\ell
and using the condition (
9
) yields

\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}(Me^{\frac{t}{m_{\ell}pn}\mathcal{L}})^{m_{\ell}pn}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}e^{tP_{j}\mathcal{L}P_{j}}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\right)\,.

The argument above holds for every pairwise distinct integer grid
\{m_{\ell}\}
for which (
9
) is solved. It remains to explain, in detail, how to choose such a grid with good conditioning. For

x_{\ell}\coloneqq m_{\ell}^{-1}\in(0,1],\qquad\ell=1,\ldots,K+1\,,

Equation

9
is equivalent to the polynomial interpolation problem of finding
c_{\ell}
such that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}x_{\ell}^{k}=\delta_{k0},\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,K.

In
Equation

11
, we observed that the equidistant-grid choice
m_{\ell}=\ell
yields exponentially growing
S_{K}
(Vandermonde instability). The key idea of
[
34
, Thm. 1, Eqs. (8-9)]
is to first relax
x_{\ell}
to real values so that
\widetilde{m}_{\ell}=x_{\ell}^{-1}
and start from a Chebyshev-type node family: The real nodes are

x_{j}^{\star}=\sin^{2}\!\Bigl(\frac{\pi(2j-1)}{4(K+1)}\Bigr),\qquad j=1,\ldots,K+1,

These are the images of the Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto interpolation points; their use minimizes the Lebesgue constant and hence the polynomial interpolation error (see
[
51
, Chap. 5]
). Solving the moment system on these nodes gives coefficients with logarithmic norm growth instead of exponential growth:

\sum_{j=1}^{K+1}|\widetilde{c}_{j}|=\mathcal{O}(\log(K+1))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}\widetilde{m}_{\ell}=\mathcal{O}(K\log K)\,.

However, we require integer step counts, which is why we convert real exponents to integers by scale-and-round:

m_{\ell}\coloneqq\lceil\kappa\widetilde{m}_{\ell}\rceil\in\mathbbm{N},

with
\kappa
chosen so that the rounded values remain pairwise distinct (this is exactly the role of the scaling parameter in
[
34
, Eq. (10)]
). The perturbation estimate of
[
34
, Eq. (11)]
shows that rounding changes each coefficient by only a constant relative factor, provided the pre-rounded nodes are sufficiently separated. Therefore, one can choose pairwise distinct integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{K+1}
such that (
9
) is satisfied and

S_{K}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}|c_{\ell}|=\mathcal{O}(\log(K+1)).

This finishes the proof by choosing
n_{\ell}=m_{\ell}pn
.
∎

Remark 3

(Time dependency)
.

From the proof of Theorem
4.3
, one obtains the time dependence
\mathcal{O}(t^{2(K+1)}/n^{K+1})
. Hence, to achieve a target accuracy
\eta>0
, it suffices to choose
n=\mathcal{O}(t^{2}\eta^{-1/(K+1)})
. Thus, increasing
K
improves only the accuracy dependence (from
\eta^{-1}
to
\eta^{-1/(K+1)}
), while the
t
-dependence remains quadratic.

This shows that the convergence of the quantum Zeno effect can be improved to order
\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}
by post-processing the probability distributions of
K+1
parallel Zeno sequences with step counts
n_{\ell}=m_{\ell}pn
. Hence, the accuracy scaling improves exponentially in
K
, while the overhead remains moderate: the number of parallel sequences grows only linearly in
K+1
, and for well-conditioned grids the coefficient sum satisfies
S_{K}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}|c_{\ell}|=\mathcal{O}(\log(K+1))
.

4.2
Applications

Finally, we present two brief examples demonstrating the scheme’s broad applicability, which is formulated with sufficient generality to encompass most instances of the Zeno effect.
This means that every convergence rate of the Zeno effect in the above form (
6
), even in infinite-dimensional systems, can be improved using the presented multi-product scheme.

Dynamical decoupling

The so-called ‘Bang-Bang’ method is closely related to the quantum Zeno effect (see
[
19
,
8
,
27
]
). In a bipartite system with one part being the bath, the idea is to apply frequent strong ‘kicks’ at short intervals to reduce the influence of the bath on the system, thereby decoupling the system from the bath
[
53
]
. The quantum operation acting on the bipartite system is
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}\otimes M
, where
M
is an ergodic quantum channel on system
2
. By
[
27
, Thm. 16]
, the peripheral eigenvalues of
M
are
\lambda_{j}=e^{2\pi ij/J}
for
j=0,\ldots,J-1
, with spectral gap
\delta
equal to the spectral radius of
M
on the complement of its peripheral eigenspace. A concrete example is the qubit amplitude-damping channel
M_{\gamma}(\rho)=K_{0}\rho K_{0}^{\dagger}+K_{1}\rho K_{1}^{\dagger}
with
K_{0}=\outerproduct{0}{0}+\sqrt{1-\gamma}\outerproduct{1}{1}
and
K_{1}=\sqrt{\gamma}\outerproduct{0}{1}
for
0<\gamma<1
: it is ergodic with unique fixed point
\outerproduct{0}{0}
, has
J=1
(only peripheral eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=1
), and
\delta=\sqrt{1-\gamma}
.

More formally, the quantum dynamical semigroup is defined on a bipartite system of two finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2}
and a Hamiltonian, which can always be represented by a set of self-adjoint operators
H_{1},H_{1,j}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and
H_{2},H_{2,j}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
for all
j\in\{1,...,d\}
so that

H=H_{1}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes H_{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}H_{1,j}\otimes H_{2,j}\,.

The goal is to interact with the dynamics in such a way that the sum over the interaction terms with the bath is transformed into a weighted sum over
H_{1,j}
in the effective dynamics (see
[
27
, Def. 15]
). Here, we focus on the recent results of
[
27
]
, which states in Theorem 16 that dynamical decoupling using a quantum channel
\mathbbm{1}\otimes M
is possible if and only if
M
is ergodic, i.e. the channel admits a unique fixed point. By
[
27
, Thm. 16]
, this property is equivalent to the assumption that the eigenvalues given in
Equation

3
are not degenerated and of the form
\lambda_{j}=e^{2\pi ij/J}
for all
j\in\{1,...,J\}
. In particular the phases are rational so that
Theorem

3.7
improves the convergence rate of the decoupling scheme
[
27
, Thm. 16]
, i.e. for a fixed
K\in\mathbbm{N}
, fixed point
\rho^{*}=M(\rho^{*})
, there is a
c_{\ell}\in\mathbbm{R}^{K+1}
so that with
H_{dec}=H_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\tr[H_{2,j}\rho^{*}]H_{1,j}

\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\bigl((\mathbbm{1}\otimes M)e^{-\frac{t}{\ell nJ}i[H,\cdot]}\bigr)^{\ell nJ}-e^{-ti[H_{dec},\cdot]}\otimes\sum_{j=1}^{J}\lambda_{j}P_{j}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,.

Bosonic cat code

Another example is the bosonic cat code
[
2
,
26
,
25
]
. Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbbm{R})
be the Fock space of a single-mode bosonic system endowed with an orthonormal (Fock-)basis
\{\ket{n}\}_{n\in\mathbbm{N}}
. The annihilation and creation operators are then defined by
a\ket{n}=\sqrt{n}\ket{n-1}
and
a^{\dagger}\ket{n}=\sqrt{n+1}\ket{n+1}
, satisfying for all
n\in\mathbbm{N}
the so-called canonical commutation relation (CCR):

[a,a^{\dagger}]\ket{n}=\ket{n}\,,\quad[a,a]\ket{n}=0\,.

The number operator is defined as
N\ket{n}=a^{\dagger}a\ket{n}=n\ket{n}
, and coherent states are the eigenvectors of
a
, denoted by
\ket{\alpha}
, such that
a\ket{\alpha}=\alpha\ket{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in\mathbbm{C}
. The code space of the bosonic cat code governed by the two-photon driven dissipative process for a given
\alpha\in\mathbbm{R}
is
\mathcal{C}_{2}\coloneqq\textrm{span}\left\{\outerproduct{\alpha_1}{\alpha_2}:\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\{-\alpha,\alpha\}\right\}
. Note that larger code-spaces are desirable due to their error-correcting properties, but they are not relevant to demonstrate the multi-product Zeno effect. The Schrödinger cat states, which represent the logical qubits, are defined by

\displaystyle\ket{CAT_{\alpha}^{+}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\ket{\alpha}+\ket{-\alpha}}{\sqrt{2(1+e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}})}}\,,

\displaystyle\ket{CAT_{\alpha}^{-}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{\ket{\alpha}-\ket{-\alpha}}{\sqrt{2(1-e^{-2|\alpha|^{2}})}}\,.

Then, the
X(\theta)
gate, described in
[
36
]
and experimentally realized in
[
50
]
, is defined by

X(\theta)=\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)(P^{+}_{\alpha}+P^{-}_{\alpha})+i\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)X_{\alpha}\,,

with
P^{\pm}_{\alpha}=\ket{CAT^{\pm}_{\alpha}}\bra{CAT^{\pm}_{\alpha}}
and
X_{\alpha}=\ket{CAT^{+}_{\alpha}}\bra{CAT^{-}_{\alpha}}+\ket{CAT^{-}_{\alpha}}\bra{CAT^{+}_{\alpha}}
. One possible construction is obtained by intersecting the driving Hamiltonian
H=a+a^{\dagger}
with the projections
P_{\alpha}=P^{+}_{\alpha}+P^{-}_{\alpha}
(see
[
40
]
), because the Zeno dynamics is

e^{itP_{\alpha}HP_{\alpha}}P_{\alpha}=\cos(t(\alpha+ \alpha^\dagger))P_{\alpha}+i\sin(t(\alpha+ \alpha^\dagger))X_{\alpha}\,,

which constructs the rotation
X(\theta)
for appropriate
t
. Since
P_{\alpha}H^{k}
and
H^{k}P_{\alpha}
are bounded for any
k\in\mathbbm{N}
(as
P_{\alpha}
is a projection onto a finite-dimensional subspace), the quantum operation
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}=P_{\alpha}\,\cdot\,P_{\alpha}
has a single peripheral eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}=1
(i.e.
J=1
) and
\delta=1
, with all non-peripheral eigenvalues equal to zero.
Theorem

4.3
improves the convergence rate to any power
K\in\mathbbm{N}
, i.e., for
\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}=P_{\alpha}\cdot P_{\alpha}
, we have

\Bigl\|\sum_{\ell=1}^{K+1}c_{\ell}\left(\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}e^{-i\frac{t}{\ell n}[H,\cdot]}\right)^{\ell n}-e^{-ti\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}[H,\cdot]\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}}\mathcal{P}_{\alpha}\Bigr\|_{1\rightarrow 1}=\mathcal{O}\Bigl(\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}\Bigr)\,.

Remark 4

(Multi-mode systems)
.

The analysis applies only to single or few-mode systems. Extending to multi-mode dynamics encounters fundamental obstructions related to information propagation, resulting in bad dependence on the number of modes: Lieb–Robinson bounds (LRBs), which control information spread, exist only weakly for the Bose–Hubbard model
[
16
,
32
]
. Establishing strong locality tools for dissipative bosonic systems (see
[
41
,
45
]
) is an important open problem for scalable bosonic quantum error correction.

5
Conclusion

This work proposes a multi-product scheme for the quantum Zeno effect by deriving a higher-order error expansion. The approach involves calculating higher-order error terms in the Zeno sequence using a modified Chernoff Lemma, a modified Dunford-Segal approximation, and the holomorphic functional calculus. A multi-product scheme is then applied to improve the convergence rate from order
\frac{1}{n}
to
\frac{1}{n^{K+1}}
for a given
K\in\mathbbm{N}
. Due to the generality of the result, we are able to apply the result to the ‘Bang-Bang’ method, which is used to decouple systems from their environment, and the bosonic cat code to engineer the
X(\theta)

\Gamma
for all

[

, we obtain that

defines, for all

\Gamma

\displaystyle 2\pi iP(t)

\displaystyle=\oint_{\Gamma}\Bigl(R-tR(t)B(t)R\Bigr)\,dz

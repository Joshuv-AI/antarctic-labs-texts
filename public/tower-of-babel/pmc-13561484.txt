Unity is strength: Multidisciplinary team rounds in psychiatry
Authors: Sharmishtha Shailesh Deshpande, Pradip Dubale
Published: 2026

Sir,

Patient care in Psychiatry is often challenging in terms of diagnostic complexity, time management, and bio-psycho-social management as per needs. For delivering high quality care for Psychiatry patients a coordinated approach from a multidisciplinary team (MDT)[1] is highly useful. This team approach with the psychiatrist in leadership role is essential to implement in Indian practice of psychiatry,[2] and has become very important in the recent times after MHCA 2017.

A tertiary care center psychiatry team usually consists of Senior Psychiatrist, Junior Psychiatrist, and Trainees in Psychiatry and Psychology, Clinical Psychologist, Psychiatric social worker, and Psychiatry nurse. In addition, such teams may include occupational therapists and special educators with expertise in mental health. They assess the patient together but preparing a comprehensive case record of assessment, psychosocial needs, and division of the work accordingly for effective team management is often lacking. Formulating practically easy basic guidelines for this practice is necessary, which would make it feasible.

We propose a simple format for this round, which is followed by us. We confirm the type of admission as per MHCA 2017 and capacity assessment. Thereafter, understanding the case summary to establish clinical working diagnosis, identifying the main problem areas, allocating the skilled professional to deal with the respective area and final discussion for comprehensive understanding and management are the four main headings to be considered. Record is maintained in a separate register dedicated for these rounds. Psychiatrist and psychologist together consider the history, and evolve the working diagnosis as per ICD-11. Senior psychiatrist in the team approves the pharmacological management. Clinical psychological tests if needed are then administered. Psychiatric social worker can undertake family environment and medico legal assessment and counselling. Junior psychiatrist would to psychoeducation and compliance related counselling. The patient and his primary relative are also given an opportunity to express their view about the illness and the treatment given. The method is time saving and repetition of work is avoided. We found this method to be useful as well as effective for inpatient care at our tertiary hospital. This leads to better case management and improved patient and relative satisfaction.

This has been reported in various research articles. Objective plan developed during MDT rounds has been proven to be useful for the team and it adds to the patient satisfaction as well.[13] The patient needs to be listened to with empathy and involved in the decision making during these rounds.[45]

We need to explore on four main issues: What brought the patient here? What is the patient’s present condition? What must occur before the patient is discharged? Where will the patient go, and are there any obstacles that might prevent him or her from transitioning? Team members should be aware of the specific duties they are responsible for and be ready to contribute the effective amount of information necessary to help the group accomplish its goals.[3]

There is added benefit of increased staff collaboration, mutual trust, and job satisfaction, leading to better environment for both patients and providers.[4] Clear task delegation and streamlined processes, enables better resource use and objective monitoring of patient progress. However, establishment of clear clinical diagnosis in the beginning is very important. Psychiatrist is the leader of the team, who can be formally trained in leadership skills, which can facilitate this activity. There is dearth of adequately trained clinical psychologists and psychiatric social workers in India that makes such rounds even more challenging.[2]

The practice of active listening, refraining from interruptions, and seeking clarification when appropriate helps in improving quality of care during these rounds. Eliminating distractions is also essential, keeping cell phone on silent mode and avoiding side chats with other team members, especially when someone else is speaking are other necessary things.

Although ward rounds are a relatively small aspect of the inpatient experience, they remain pivotal, as it is a meaningful engagement and decisions should be made in collaboration with the patient about his/her treatment and future management.[6] Patient preparation for participation in such rounds is an important role of the nursing staff of the Psychiatry ward.[7] Multidisciplinary rounds with this simple format can thus make better and clearer decisions, for the betterment of the patient and his family.

On the geometric fixed points of the real topological cyclic homology of ℤ / 4 \mathbb{Z}/4
Thomas Read

Abstract




Abstract

We study the homotopy groups of the geometric fixed points of the real topological cyclic homology of

\mathbb{Z}/4
. We relate these groups to the values of the non-abelian derived functors of
the functor
M\mapsto(M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}/4}M)^{C_{2}}
at the

\mathbb{Z}/4
-module
\mathbb{Z}/2
, which we precisely calculate with computer assistance up to degree
6
,
and calculate in general up to slight remaining ambiguity. Using these results
we compute
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
exactly
for
i\leq 1
, up to an extension problem for
2\leq i\leq 5
, and describe the asymptotic growth of this group for large
i
.
A consequence of these computations is that there exists some
0\leq i\leq 5
such
that the canonical map comparing the
genuine symmetric and symmetric
L
-theory spectra of
\mathbb{Z}/4
is not an
isomorphism on degree
i
homotopy,
and moreover this comparison map is never an isomorphism on homotopy in sufficiently large degrees.

Let us first recall the relevant flavours of

We work with the flat

For a

1.2
Dold-Kan equivalence

Let
R
be a commutative ring. The Dold-Kan equivalence
[
Dol58
]

is an equivalence between the category of connective chain
complexes of
R
-modules
\mathrm{Ch}_{\geq 0}(R)
and the category of simplicial

R
-modules
\mathrm{sMod}_{R}
.
Let us briefly recall the definitions of these functors, since these
constructions will be an essential part of our calculations and we will need to
reason about the concrete details later on.

We denote the functor from chain complexes to simplicial modules by

\sigma:\mathrm{Ch}_{\geq 0}(R)\to\mathrm{sMod}_{R}\mathrm{.}

Let
M_{\ast}
be a connective chain complex. Then the simplicial
R
-module

\sigma M_{\bullet}
is given level-wise by

\sigma M_{n}=\bigoplus_{[n]\twoheadrightarrow[k]}M_{k}

where the sum is taken over all surjections out of
[n]
in the simplex
category
\Delta
; that is, all surjective weakly increasing maps
[n]\twoheadrightarrow[k]
for
0\leq k\leq n
. The simplicial structure is as
follows. Given a map
[m]\to[n]
in
\Delta
, we need to produce a map

\sigma M_{n}\to\sigma M_{m}
. It suffices to describe how to map out of each
direct summand of
\sigma M_{n}
, and so for each surjection
[n]\twoheadrightarrow[k]
we will give a map
M_{k}\to\sigma M_{m}
. Factor the composite
[m]\to[n]\twoheadrightarrow[k]
as a surjection followed by an injection
[m]\twoheadrightarrow[m^{\prime}]\hookrightarrow[k]
, then we take the map
M_{k}\to M_{m^{\prime}}
from the chain complex structure of
M
, and compose it with the
summand inclusion
M_{m^{\prime}}\subseteq\sigma M_{m}
corresponding to
[m]\twoheadrightarrow[m^{\prime}]
.

The inverse functor from simplicial modules to chain complexes is the normalised
chain complex functor

N:\mathrm{sMod}_{R}\to\mathrm{Ch}_{\geq 0}(R)\mathrm{.}

Given a simplicial
R
-module
M_{\bullet}
, we define
NM_{\ast}
as follows: let

NA_{n}\coloneq\bigcap_{i=0}^{n-1}\text{ker}(d_{i})
be the subgroup of
A_{n}
that is killed by all the face maps
d_{i}
for

i<n
, and let the differential
NA_{n}\to NA_{n-1}
be given by
(-1)^{n}d_{n}
.

Theorem 1.3

(Dold-Kan equivalence

[
Dol58
]
)
.

The functors
\sigma
and
N
give an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{Ch}_{\geq 0}(R)\simeq\mathrm{sMod}_{R}\text{.}

Proof.

See
[
GJ09
]
Section III.2 (and note the correction to
the proof in the errata).
∎

Additionally recall the Moore complex of a simplicial
R
-module. Given a
simplicial
R
-module
A_{\bullet}
, the Moore complex is the chain complex

A_{\ast}
which is given by
A_{n}
in degree
n
, and has boundary map
\partial:A_{n}\to A_{n-1}
given by the alternating sum of the face maps
\Sigma_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}d_{i}
. The Moore complex
A_{\ast}
and the normalised chain complex

NA_{\ast}
are naturally homotopy equivalent, and so in practice we can use
either in situations where we only care about the result up to homotopy.

1.3
Real topological Hochschild homology

In this subsection we briefly recall the construction of
\mathrm{THR}(A)
and
\mathrm{TCR}(A;p)
in the
special case where
A
is a discrete commutative ring with trivial involution
(this is the only case we will need). These constructions were introduced in

[
HM15
]
,
[
Dot12
]
,

[
Høg16
]
; good sources for
full details include
[
DMPR21
]
and
[
DMP24
]
.

Let
HA
be a flat model for the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum of
A
, in
orthogonal spectra. The cyclic nerve of
HA
becomes a dihedral spectrum if we
give it an appropriate involution. We denote this construction
N^{di}HA
.
In level
n
we have
(N^{di}HA)_{n}=HA^{\otimes n+1}
, which we visualise as
n+1
copies of
A
tensored
together in a circle, equipped with rotation and reflection actions. We define the real topological Hochschild
homology of
A
to be the geometric realisation

\mathrm{THR}(A)\coloneqq\left\lvert N^{di}A\right\rvert\mathrm{.}

This is an orthogonal spectrum with an
O(2)
-action, so we can regard it as a
genuine
O(2)
-spectrum.
Note the underlying cyclotomic spectrum of
\mathrm{THR}(A)
is
\mathrm{THH}(A)
, while
the underlying genuine

\mathbb{Z}/2
-spectrum is

A\otimes_{N_{e}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}A}A

(where
A
has
N_{e}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}A
-module structure given by

N_{e}^{\mathbb{Z}/2}A\xrightarrow{\mu}A
). In particular we find that the geometric fixed points are

\mathrm{THR}(A)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\simeq A^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\otimes_{A}A^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathrm{.}

Let
p
be a prime. We define the
p
-typical truncated real topological
restriction homology of
A
by

\mathrm{TRR}^{n+1}(A;p)\coloneqq\mathrm{THR}(A)^{C_{p^{n}}}\mathrm{,}

where
C_{p^{n}}\leq D_{p^{n}}\leq O(2)
. We can consider this as a

\mathbb{Z}/2
-spectrum, since the Weyl group of
C_{p^{n}}
inside
D_{p^{n}}
is

\mathbb{Z}/2
. There is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-equivariant map
R:\mathrm{TRR}^{n+1}(A;p)\to\mathrm{TRR}^{n}(A;p)
coming from the real cyclotomic structure of

\mathrm{THR}(A)
; using this we define the real topological restriction
homology

\mathrm{TRR}(A;p)\coloneqq\mathrm{holim}\left(\dotsb\xrightarrow{R}\mathrm{TRR}^{n+1}(A;p)\xrightarrow{R}\mathrm{TRR}^{n}(A;p)\xrightarrow{R}\dotsb\xrightarrow{R}\mathrm{TRR}^{1}(A;p)\right)\mathrm{.}

The inclusion
C_{p^{n-1}}\leq C_{p^{n}}
induces a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-equivariant restriction map

F:\mathrm{TRR}^{n}(A;p)=\mathrm{THR}(A)^{C_{p^{n}}}\to\mathrm{THR}(A)^{C_{p^{n-1}}}=\mathrm{TRR}^{n-1}(A;p)\mathrm{,}

known as the Frobenius. This in turn induces an endomorphism
F
on
\mathrm{TRR}(A;p)
. Finally we
define the real topological cyclic homology of
A
as the

\mathbb{Z}/2
-equivariant spectrum

\mathrm{TCR}(A;p)\coloneqq\mathrm{hoeq}(\mathrm{TRR}(A;p)\,\begin{subarray}{c}\xrightarrow{F}\\[-1.99997pt]
\xrightarrow[\mathrm{id}]{}\end{subarray}\,\mathrm{TRR}(A;p))\mathrm{.}

This construction of
\mathrm{TCR}
from
\mathrm{THR}
is analogous to the
usual construction of
\mathrm{TC}
from
\mathrm{THH}
, and indeed the underlying spectrum of
\mathrm{TCR}(A;p)
is
\mathrm{TC}(A;p)
.

As mentioned in the introduction, we will not work directly with the above
construction of
\mathrm{TCR}
, but instead use the following formula of Dotto,
Moi and Patchkoria.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
DMP24
]
2.14)
.

We can describe topological cyclic restriction homology by the following
homotopy equaliser

\mathrm{TCR}(A;2)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\simeq\mathrm{hoeq}\left((\mathrm{THR}(A)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}\,\begin{subarray}{c}\xrightarrow{f}\\[-1.99997pt]
\xrightarrow[r]{}\end{subarray}\,\mathrm{THR}(A)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\right)\mathrm{.}

(see
[
DMP24
]
for descriptions of the maps, and we will later
give an explicit description in the case
A=H\mathbb{Z}/4
).

See also Appendix A of
[
QS22
]
, which gives a
similar description of the geometric fixed points of
\mathrm{TCR}
as an equalizer.

In this section we compute the low degree terms of the long exact sequence in homotopy

2.1
Decomposition of the equaliser formula for
\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}

In order to compute the homotopy groups of the terms in the equaliser, we want to apply Lemma 4.3 of
[
DMP24
]

to decompose the copies of
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
. But to
satisfy the conditions of the lemma we first need to show that

H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
, endowed with the Frobenius
H\mathbb{Z}/4
-module structure,
splits as the direct sum of its homotopy groups.

Lemma 2.1
.

For an abelian group
A
we have

\pi_{i}(HA^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\begin{cases}\mathrm{coker}(A\xrightarrow{2}A)\quad&\text{$i\geq 0$, $i$ even,}\\
\mathrm{ker}(A\xrightarrow{2}A)\quad&\text{$i\geq 0$, $i$ odd,}\\
0\quad&\text{$i<0$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The spectrum
HA
is connective, so its geometric fixed points are
too. Now consider the cofibre sequence

HA_{h\mathbb{Z}/2}\to HA\to HA^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\text{.}

Since
HA
is concentrated in degree
0
, the long exact sequence in homotopy groups gives us an exact sequence

0\to\pi_{1}(HA^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\to\pi_{0}(HA_{h\mathbb{Z}/2})\to\pi_{0}(HA)\to\pi_{0}(HA^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\to 0

(3)

and isomorphisms

\pi_{i}(HA^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\pi_{i-1}(HA_{h\mathbb{Z}/2})

for
i\geq 2
.

But we have

\pi_{i}(HA_{h\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong H_{i}(\mathbb{RP}^{\infty};A)\cong\begin{cases}A\quad&\text{$i=0$,}\\
\text{coker}(A\xrightarrow{2}A)\quad&\text{$i>0$, $i$ odd,}\\
\text{ker}(A\xrightarrow{2}A)\quad&\text{$i>0$, $i$ even.}\end{cases}

And the map
A\cong\pi_{0}(HA_{h\mathbb{Z}/2})\to\pi_{0}(HA)\cong A

appearing in the exact sequence (
3
) is
the multiplication map
A\xrightarrow{2}A
. The result follows.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

We have a splitting of

-modules

H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\simeq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Sigma^{n}H\pi_{n}(H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\Sigma^{n}H\mathbb{Z}/2\mathrm{.}

where we consider
H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
as an

H\mathbb{Z}/4
-module via the Frobenius

1.1
and consider
H\mathbb{Z}/2
as an

H\mathbb{Z}/4
-module via the quotient map

H\mathbb{Z}/4\to H\mathbb{Z}/2

Proof.

First note that by Lemma

we indeed have

.

Recall Lemma
1.2

(4)

We claim that

Let the ideal

be the kernel of the quotient
\mathbb{Z}/4\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/2

Considering

\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\simeq\bigoplus_{n,m,k\geq 0}\Sigma^{n+m+k}H\mathbb{Z}/2

and the result follows.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

The homotopy groups of the off-diagonal part (i.e. the second summand) of
(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
under the decomposition of Corollary

are given by

\pi_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n<m}\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2\right)\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\

Proof.

Similarly to Proposition

, we have an equivalence of spectra

\bigoplus_{0\leq n<m}\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2\simeq\bigoplus_{0\leq n<m}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{n+m+k}H\mathbb{Z}/2

∎

So we just need to focus on calculating the homotopy groups of the diagonal
summands of
(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
, which are of the form

\left(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/2)\right)^{C_{2}}

for
n\geq 0
. We approach this using the concept of non-abelian derived functors,
as defined in
[
DP58
]
. First let us recall the definition.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and let
T:\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to\mathrm{Ab}
be a functor with

T(0)=0
. Let
i\geq 0
and
n\geq 0
be natural numbers. Given an
R
-module
M
, let
M_{\bullet}
be a level-wise free
simplicial
R
-module such that the geometric realisation of the
HR
-module spectrum
HM_{\bullet}
is

\Sigma^{n}HM
(in the following we will sometimes abuse terminology and refer to

\left\lvert HM_{\bullet}\right\rvert
as the geometric realisation of
M_{\bullet}
, implicitly
taking the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum levelwise). Then we define a functor

L_{i}^{(n)}T:\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to\mathrm{Ab}

via

L_{i}^{(n)}T(M)\coloneqq\pi_{i}(\left\lvert HT(M_{\bullet})\right\rvert)

where
T(M_{\bullet})
means the simplicial abelian group produced by applying

T
level-wise to
M_{\bullet}
. Note one can always produce such an

M_{\bullet}
, for example by taking a free resolution for
M[n]
in chain complexes of
R
-modules, then
applying the Dold-Kan equivalence of Theorem
1.3
.

Definition 2.8

(Non-abelian derived functor)
.

We call
L_{i}^{(n)}T
the
i
th non-abelian derived functor of type
n
of
T
.

Remark 2.9
.

Observe that
T(M_{\bullet})
is zero in degrees less than
n
, so
L_{i}^{(n)}T(M)=0
for
i<n
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
R
be a commutative ring and
M
an
R
-module. Let
M_{\bullet}
be a free
simplicial
R
-module with geometric realisation
\Sigma^{n}HM
. Then

\displaystyle\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM)

\displaystyle\simeq\left\lvert H\left(M_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}M_{\bullet},w\right)\right\rvert\mathrm{,}

where
w
is the involution on
M_{i}\otimes_{R}M_{i}

given by swapping the two copies of
M_{i}
. This equivalence is natural in

M_{\bullet}
(if we take
M
to depend functorially on
M_{\bullet}
).

Proof.

First rewrite as

\displaystyle\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM)

\displaystyle\simeq HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(\Sigma^{n}HM)

\displaystyle\simeq\left\lvert HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM_{\bullet})\right\rvert\mathrm{.}

To see the first equivalence, write the suspension
\Sigma^{n}({-})
as a smash product with the
sphere spectrum
S^{n}\wedge({-})
, then note the norm is monoidal by

[
HHR16
]
Proposition A.53 and
N^{C_{2}}_{\{e\}}(S^{n})\cong S^{n\rho}
by

[
HHR16
]
Proposition A.59. The second equivalence uses
the fact that geometric realisation commutes with the norm and tensor products
(recall by
[
HHR16
]
Proposition A.53 the norm commutes with
sifted colimits).

Let
B_{i}
index a basis of the free module
M_{i}
, i.e.
M_{i}\cong\bigoplus_{B_{i}}R
.
We first establish some notation. Consider the set
(B_{i}\times B_{i})_{C_{2}}
, where
C_{2}
acts on
B_{i}\times B_{i}
by swapping the two factors. Denote the diagonal part by
B^{d}_{i}=\{(u,u)\in(B_{i}\times B_{i})_{C_{2}}\}\cong B_{i}
and denote the off-diagonal part by
B^{o}_{i}=(B_{i}\times B_{i})_{C_{2}}\setminus B^{d}_{i}
.
Then we have a series of equivalences of simplicial
C_{2}
-spectra

\displaystyle\phantom{{}\simeq{}}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\simeq HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}\left(\bigoplus_{B_{\bullet}}HR\right)

\displaystyle\simeq HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}\left(\bigoplus_{B^{d}_{\bullet}}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)\oplus\bigoplus_{B^{o}_{\bullet}}\left(\Sigma^{\infty}(C_{2})_{+}\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{B^{d}_{\bullet}}HR\oplus\bigoplus_{B^{o}_{\bullet}}\left(\Sigma^{\infty}(C_{2})_{+}\otimes_{\mathbb{S}}HR\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H\left(\bigoplus_{B_{\bullet}\times B_{\bullet}}R,w^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq H\left(M_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}M_{\bullet},w\right)\mathrm{,}

where
w^{\prime}
is the involution on
\bigoplus_{B_{i}\times B_{i}}R

given by swapping the two copies of
B_{i}
, and which under
\bigoplus_{B_{i}\times B_{i}}R\cong M_{i}\otimes_{R}M_{i}
becomes the involution
w
described in the
lemma statement.

To see naturality, consider the map of spectra

HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM_{i})\to H(M_{i}\otimes M_{i})

obtained as the canonical map from the left hand side to the Eilenberg-MacLane spectrum
of its degree zero homotopy, and observe that taking geometric realisations gives the
map in the statement of the lemma; from this definition of the map it is clear
that it is natural in
M_{\bullet}
.
∎

The immediate consequence of this theorem is that we can describe some homotopy
groups of interest using non-abelian derived functors. We present these
generally but note the case we will be interested in is when

R=\mathbb{Z}/4
and
M=\mathbb{Z}/2
.
First we consider the
homotopy groups of the genuine
C_{2}
-fixed points; this appeared as
Lemma
A
in the introduction.
Define
F_{R}:\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to\mathrm{Ab}
on an

R
-module

by
F_{R}(M)=(M\otimes_{R}M)^{C_{2}}
, where

has the
C_{2}
-action
given by swapping the factors.

Corollary 2.11
.

We have

\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}\Big(\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N^{C_{2}}_{\{e\}}(HM)\Big)\cong L^{(n)}_{i}F_{R}(M)

where
L^{(n)}_{i}F_{R}
denotes the
i

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}\Big(\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM)\Big)

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{i}\big(\left\lvert HF_{R}(M_{\bullet})\right\rvert\big)

\displaystyle\cong L^{(n)}_{i}F_{R}(M)\mathrm{.}\qed

on an

We have

\pi_{i}\Big(\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N^{C_{2}}_{\{e\}}(HM)\Big)\cong L^{(n)}_{i}G_{R}(M)

denotes the
i

of

.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\pi_{i}\Big(\Sigma^{n\rho}HR\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HR)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(HM)\Big)

\displaystyle\cong L^{(n)}_{i}G_{R}(M)\mathrm{.}\qed

However this time when

and

.

induced by the natural transformation

given at a module

by inclusion of fixed points

Proof.

Let

be a free simplicial

We can straightforwardly extend our computer program to compute this map on non-abelian

be

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left(H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\sigma M_{\bullet})\right)^{C_{2}}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\to\left\lvert\left(H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\sigma M_{\bullet})\right)^{\phi C_{2}}\right\rvert

(5)

\displaystyle\simeq\left\lvert H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi C_{2}}\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\sigma M_{\bullet}\right\rvert

We start by simplifying the map from genuine to geometric fixed points.

Lemma 2.16
.

Write
\sigma M_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{B_{\bullet}}\mathbb{Z}/4

Then we have a commutative diagram of simplicial spectra

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left(H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\sigma M_{\bullet})\right)^{C_{2}}}

where

We have

(6)

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{B^{d}_{\bullet}}H\mathbb{Z}/4^{\phi C_{2}}

Finally observe that under the above equivalences of spectra, the map from genuine to geometric fixed points of

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
are modules over the ring
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/4
hence
must be
4
-torsion.
∎

2.4
Resolving the extension problem in degree 1

We would prefer to actually compute the homotopy groups, not just fit
them into exact sequences. The above shows
\pi_{-1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
and
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/4
. The following analysis lets us additionally
compute
\pi_{1}
, and makes some progress in simplifying the problem for higher homotopy groups.

The key observation is that the map
r-f
almost splits as a direct sum. Recall
how the maps
f
and
r
are defined in Corollary
2.5
in terms of the decompositions of

\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
and

(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
described in
Corollary
2.3
. The map
f
sends the diagonal summand indexed by

n
to the diagonal summand indexed by
n
, and sends the off-diagonal summand indexed
by
(n,m)
into the off-diagonal summands indexed by
(n,m)
and
(m,n)
. The
map
r
is zero on the off-diagonal summands, and sends the diagonal summand indexed
by
n
into the summands indexed
(n,m)
for
m\geq 0
. So
r
preserves
the
n
index, and so does
f
if we restrict to the diagonal summands. We
record this observation in the following lemma.

Let

D_{n}\coloneqq\left(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/2)\right)^{C_{2}}

denote the
n

(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
, and let

D\coloneqq\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}D_{n}\text{.}

Lemma 2.25
.

The restriction
(r-f)|_{D}:D\to\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}

is a direct sum of maps

\gamma_{n}:D_{n}\to\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2\mathrm{,}

where the right hand side
is the sum of the
(n,m)
-indexed summands of
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
for
m\geq 0
.

Proof.

Immediate from Corollary
2.5
.
∎

Homotopy fibres preserve direct sums, so we have

\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D})\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n})\mathrm{.}

Our computer program computes the action of
r-f
on
homotopy in terms of the
decomposition of Corollary
2.3
, so for each
n
we can easily restrict
to obtain the action of the map
\gamma_{n}
, and from this calculate a long
exact sequence of homotopy groups for
\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n})
.

Let us record the resulting short exact sequences involving
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n}))
.

Lemma 2.26
.

We have

\displaystyle 0\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\to

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to 0

\displaystyle 0\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{1}))\to 0

and
\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n}))=0
for
n>1
.

Proof.

From the long exact sequence of homotopy groups we get short exact sequences

0\to\text{coker}(\pi_{i+1}(\gamma_{n}))\to\pi_{i}(\text{hofib}(\gamma_{n}))\to\text{ker}(\pi_{i}(\gamma_{n}))\to 0\text{.}

Immediate from the output of the computer program, we can read off the relevant
restrictions of
r-f
and compute kernels and cokernels. For example, recall our
description of the action of
f
and
r
on
\pi_{1}
in the proof of
Theorem
2.22
. We have
\pi_{1}(D_{0})\cong L_{1}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
corresponding
to the subgroup generated by
\{x_{1},x_{2}\}
in our previous computation,
and
\pi_{1}
of the sum of the
(0,m)
-indexed
components of
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
is

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}
corresponding to the subgroup generated by
\{y_{1},y_{2}\}

previously. The map
f
takes
x_{1}\mapsto 0
and
x_{2}\mapsto y_{1}
respectively, and the map

r
takes
x_{1}\mapsto y_{1}
and
x_{2}\mapsto 0
. So
\gamma_{0}
is given by
x_{1}\mapsto y_{1}
and
x_{2}\mapsto-y_{1}
, and we conclude that
\pi_{1}(\gamma_{0})

has kernel
\mathbb{Z}/2
.

The full set of kernels and cokernels needed is as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{coker}(\pi_{2}(\gamma_{0}))

\displaystyle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{ker}(\pi_{1}(\gamma_{0}))\cong\mathbb{Z}/2

\displaystyle\mathrm{coker}(\pi_{2}(\gamma_{1}))

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\quad\quad

\displaystyle\mathrm{ker}(\pi_{1}(\gamma_{1}))\cong 0\text{.}

For
n>i
the short exact
sequences are evidently all zero.
∎

Now we need to relate the homotopy groups of the restricted homotopy fibre

\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D})
to those of the
original homotopy fibre
\mathrm{hofib}(r-f)\simeq\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
. Ultimately this will allow us to compute

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
.

We first note a small homological algebra lemma that we will need in the
subsequent proposition.

Lemma 2.27
.

Suppose we have a map of short exact sequences in an abelian category

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where the rightmost vertical map is equality. Then the left hand square is a
pullback. If the map
A\to C
is an epimorphism then the left hand square is also a pushout.

Proof.

First note that a square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}

with the top horizontal map a monomorphism and the right
vertical map an epimorphism is a pushout iff it is a pullback. Indeed if it is
a pushout then we have an exact sequence

L\to M\oplus N\to P\to 0\text{,}

but since
L\to M
is monic so is the map
L\to M\oplus N
and so

0\to L\to M\oplus N\to P

is exact and the square is a pullback. Dually if the square is a pullback then
using
M\to P
epic we see that the square is also a pushout.

Now consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\text{.}}

Since
X
is the cokernel of
C\to D
, the bottom square is a
pushout. As
C\to D
is monic and
D\to X
is epic, the bottom
square is also a pullback. Similarly as
X
is the cokernel of
A\to B
the outer square is a pushout and a pullback. So by the
pasting law for pullbacks the upper square is a pullback.

If
A\to C
is epic then so is
B\to D
by the
4
-lemma applied to the
original map of short exact sequences, so we
deduce the upper square is also a pushout.
∎

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
(r-f)_{i}
denote the map induced by
r-f
on homotopy groups

\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\to\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
. Then we have a pushout
and pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1}|_{\pi_{i+1}(D)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}(\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})}

where the horizontal arrows come from the long exact sequences for the homotopy
fibres of
(r-f)|_{D}
and
r-f
, and the vertical maps are induced by the
inclusion of
D
into
(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
.

Proof.

We have a map of fibre sequences:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\mathrm{,}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(r-f)|_{D}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r-f}

giving a corresponding map of long exact sequences in homotopy, and so maps of
the short exact sequences involving the homotopy groups of the homotopy fibres.
Note that since

D
is a summand of
(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
,

\pi_{i}(D)
is a summand of
\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})
, and so the map induced by
(r-f)|_{D}
on degree
i
homotopy
groups is
(r-f)_{i}|_{\pi_{i}(D)}
.
Then we have a map of short exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1}|_{\pi_{i+1}(D)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}(\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i}|_{\pi_{i}(D)})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

The map

\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1}|_{\pi_{i+1}(D)})\to\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{i+1})
is evidently
surjective. We claim that the map
\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i}|_{\pi_{i}(D)})\to\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i})
is an isomorphism; that is, we claim that

\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i})
is contained within
\pi_{i}(D)
. Let
0\leq n<m
. If we post-compose

(r-f)_{i}
with the projection map onto the off-diagonal component of
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
indexed by
(n,m)

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{i}(\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2)

then we get the projection map onto the off-diagonal component of
(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}
indexed by
(n,m)

\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{i}(\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2)

(observe that this is what happens
if we post-compose

with the projection, and on homotopy
r_{i}
only hits non-diagonal
components indexed by
(n^{\prime},0)
for some
n^{\prime}
). So
\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{i})
is
entirely contained within the kernel of every projection onto an off-diagonal
component, i.e. is contained in the diagonal part of the decomposition. We
conclude the right hand vertical map is an isomorphism.

Now we are done by Lemma
2.27
.
∎

Remark 2.29
.

Our earlier computer calculations suffice
to compute the left hand vertical map of the pushout square, so we’ve
reduced the problem of computing
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
to that of computing

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D}))\cong\bigoplus^{i}_{n=0}\pi_{i}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n}))

and the pushout.
In fact as we will see in the following calculation, even if
while computing
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{n}))
we run into extension problems
that we are unable to solve, sometimes we can still compute the pushout.

Theorem 2.30
.

We have

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\mathrm{.}

Proof.

We apply the above proposition to compute
\pi_{1}
. Taking the direct sum of the

n=0
and
n=1
short exact sequences from Lemma
2.26
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{2}|_{\pi_{2}(D)})

\displaystyle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\text{,}

\displaystyle\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}((r-f)|_{D}))

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))\oplus\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{1}))\cong\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\text{,}

\displaystyle\mathrm{ker}((r-f)_{1}|_{\pi_{1}(D)})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\oplus 0\text{.}

We saw earlier that
\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
, and explicitly examining the action of
r-f
on
\pi_{2}
shows
that the quotient

\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{2}|_{\pi_{2}(D)})\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{coker}((r-f)_{2})

is the projection of

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2
onto the second summand. Now
Proposition
2.28
gives us a pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\text{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}/2}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\text{.}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ulcorner}

Considering this pushout we find that

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
is the cokernel of the composition

(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\to\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\text{,}

where the first map is the map

\pi_{1}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathrm{hofib}(\gamma_{0}))/(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\mathrm{.}\qed

Remark 2.31
.

Unfortunately the same approach is not enough on its own to compute higher
homotopy groups of
\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
. For example
we run into an extension problem when computing
\pi_{2}

of the homotopy kernel of
\gamma_{1}
, and
this time we would actually need to solve this extension problem
in order to compute
\pi_{2}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
. Nevertheless these techniques can potentially constrain the set of possible homotopy
groups. For instance applying this analysis to
\pi_{4}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
gives a second proof that this group is

-torsion (note we
already knew this since it is a
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
-module).

3
Asymptotic growth estimates

Our results so far show that the first few homotopy groups of

\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
do not obey a
4
-periodic
pattern. We would like to further prove that the homotopy groups grow arbitrarily large,
and so there is no point beyond which they start repeating with any period. To
show this we will make asymptotic estimates that show that the logarithm of
the size of the group
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})

grows quadratically in
i
. In order to do so we almost completely
compute the groups
L^{(n)}_{i}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
for all
i
and
n
; this is a
result that may be of independent interest.

Recall as in Theorem
2.22
the long exact sequence
associated to the homotopy fibre of
r-f
and the resulting short exact
sequences. If we can estimate the sizes of the homotopy groups involved in the
long exact sequence, then this gives
us constraints on the sizes of the kernels and cokernels appearing in the short
exact sequences, and so gives bounds on the size of

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
. In particular note we get
these estimates without needing any control over the maps in the long exact
sequence. We describe this
general technique in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

A\xrightarrow{u}B\to C\to D\xrightarrow{v}E

be an exact sequence of finite abelian groups. Then we have bounds

\mathrm{max}\left(1,\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\right)\cdot\mathrm{max}\left(1,\frac{\left\lvert D\right\rvert}{\left\lvert E\right\rvert}\right)\leq\left\lvert C\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert B\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert D\right\rvert\text{.}

Proof.

We have a short exact sequence

0\to\text{coker}(u)\to C\to\text{ker}(v)\to 0

so

\left\lvert C\right\rvert=\left\lvert\text{coker}(u)\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\text{ker}(v)\right\rvert\text{.}

And we have bounds

\text{max}\left(1,\frac{\left\lvert B\right\rvert}{\left\lvert A\right\rvert}\right)\leq\left\lvert\text{coker}(u)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert B\right\rvert

and

\text{max}\left(1,\frac{\left\lvert D\right\rvert}{\left\lvert E\right\rvert}\right)\leq\left\lvert\text{ker}(v)\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert D\right\rvert\text{,}

where note that if
\left\lvert B\right\rvert<\left\lvert A\right\rvert
we take the trivial lower bound
\left\lvert\text{coker}(u)\right\rvert\geq 1
, and similarly if
\left\lvert D\right\rvert<\left\lvert E\right\rvert
we take
the trivial lower bound
\left\lvert\text{ker}(v)\right\rvert\geq 1
.
∎

Applying this lemma to the long exact sequence for
\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
, appropriate bounds on the sizes of
\pi_{j}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})
and
\pi_{j}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
will give us upper and lower bounds for the size of
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
.

First recall that we entirely understand the non-equivariant homotopy groups of
\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The group
\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})
has order
2^{\frac{1}{2}(i+1)(i+2)}
.

Proof.

Recall from Proposition
2.6
that

\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n+m+k=i\end{subarray}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathrm{.}

The number of
\mathbb{Z}/2
-summands is the number of ways of writing
i
as
the sum of three non-negative integers
i=n+m+k
. For each
0\leq n\leq i
,
there are
i-n+1
possible choices of non-negative integers
m,k
such that
m+k=i-n
. So in
total we have

\sum_{n=0}^{i}i-n+1=\frac{1}{2}(i+1)(i+2)

summands.
∎

As before, analysing
\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})
takes more work.

Lemma 3.3
.

The group
\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})
has order

2^{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\left\lfloor(i+1)^{2}/4\right\rfloor}}{{\phantom{.}}}}\cdot\prod_{0\leq n\leq i}\left\lvert L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert\mathrm{.}

Proof.

Recall from Corollary
2.3
that

\displaystyle(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}}\simeq

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/2)\right)^{C_{2}}

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{0\leq n<m}\Sigma^{n+m}H\mathbb{Z}/2\otimes_{H\mathbb{Z}/4}H\mathbb{Z}/2\mathrm{.}

By Corollary
2.11
we know that the
i
th homotopy
group of the
n
-indexed diagonal summand is computed using non-abelian
derived functors as
L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
, and by general properties of
non-abelian derived functors these groups are zero for
n>i
. So the diagonal part has order

\prod_{0\leq n\leq i}\left\lvert L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert\mathrm{.}

As we observed in Proposition
2.7
the homotopy of the
off-diagonal terms is given by

\pi_{i}\left(\bigoplus_{0\leq n<m}\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\Sigma^{n+m+k}H\mathbb{Z}/2\right)\cong\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}n,m,k\geq 0\\
n<m\\
n+m+k=i\end{subarray}}\mathbb{Z}/2\mathrm{.}

Let us count the number of

\mathbb{Z}/2
summands appearing here. For each
0\leq k\leq i
, there are

\lfloor(i-k-1)/2\rfloor+1
ways to pick non-negative integers
n,m
with
n<m
and

n+m=i-k
, so the total number of summands is

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{i}\lfloor(i-k-1)/2\rfloor+1

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}i(i+2)/4&\text{if $i$ is even}\\
(i+1)^{2}/4&\text{if $i$ is odd.}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\left\lfloor\frac{(i+1)^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor

Multiplying the orders of the diagonal and off-diagonal parts gives the result.
∎

3.1
Non-abelian derived functor computations

To make further progress we need to study
L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
. As a
warm-up, let us compute
L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/4)
; this will come in useful later.

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/4)\cong\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}/4&\text{if $n$ is
even and $i=2n$}\\
\mathbb{Z}/2&\text{if $n\leq i<2n$, or if $n$ is odd
and $i=2n$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

By the same reasoning as Corollary
2.11
, Lemma
2.10
gives

L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/4)\cong\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}\left(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4\otimes_{N_{\{e\}}^{C_{2}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)}N^{C_{2}}_{\{e\}}(H\mathbb{Z}/4)\right)\mathrm{.}

But

so

L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/4)\cong\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}\left(\Sigma^{n\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4\right)\mathrm{.}

This is precisely the Bredon homology of
S^{n\rho}
with coefficients in the
Mackey functor of the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}/4
with trivial involution.
For
n
even this is the homology of the chain complex

\dotsb 0\to\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\dotsb\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}/4\to 0\dotsb

where the non-zero groups are in degrees

to

. For

chain complex looks like

\dotsb 0\to\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{0}\dotsb\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{0}\mathbb{Z}/4\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb{Z}/4\to 0\dotsb

with non-zero groups in the same degrees. The homology of these complexes
gives the claimed result.
∎

Definition 3.5

(Cross effect, originally introduced in
[
EL53
]
)
.

Let
T:\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to\mathrm{Ab}
be a functor with
T(0)=0
. The second
cross-effect is the functor
T_{2}:(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})^{2}\to\mathrm{Ab}
defined
on objects by

T_{2}(A_{1},A_{2})\coloneqq\text{Im}\big(\text{id}_{T(A_{1}\oplus T_{2})}-T(i_{A_{1}}p_{A_{1}})-T(i_{A_{2}}p_{A_{2}})\big)

where
i_{A_{i}}
,
p_{A_{i}}
are the canonical injections and projections of
A_{i}

into and out of
A_{1}\oplus A_{2}
. The functor
T_{2}
is defined on morphisms in
a natural way; see
[
ST74
]
Section 4 for full details.

We define higher cross effects
T_{k}:(\text{Mod}_{R})^{k}\to\text{Ab}
inductively via

T_{k}(A_{1},\dotsc,A_{k})\coloneqq\big(T_{k-1}(A_{1},\dotsc,A_{k-2},{-})\big)_{2}(A_{k-1},A_{k})\text{.}

Lemma 3.6
.

The functor
F_{R}(M)\coloneqq(M\otimes_{R}M)^{C_{2}}
has second cross-effect

A_{1},A_{2}\mapsto A_{1}\otimes_{R}A_{2}\text{,}

and third cross-effect zero—accordingly we say
that
F_{R}
is quadratic.

Proof.

We compute

\left(F_{R}\right)_{2}(A_{1},A_{2})\coloneqq\text{Im}\left(\text{id}_{F_{R}(A_{1}\oplus A_{2})}-F_{R}(i_{A_{1}}p_{A_{1}})-F_{R}(i_{A_{2}}p_{A_{2}})\right)\cong A_{1}\otimes_{R}A_{2}\text{.}

For the third cross-effect we have

\left(F_{R}\right)_{3}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{3})\coloneqq\left(\left(F_{R}\right)_{2}(A_{1},{-})\right)_{2}(A_{2},A_{3})\text{.}

But
\left(F_{R}\right)_{2}(A_{1},{-})=A_{1}\otimes_{R}({-})
is additive, so has second cross-effect
zero. Hence
F_{R}
has third cross-effect zero.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

L^{(n+1)}_{2n}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to\mathbb{Z}/2
is zero.

Now we look at the part of the exact sequence of
Corollary
3.15
leading up to the section in
(
9
), and split the argument
according to the parity of
n
. Let
n=2k+1
be odd, then we get

\displaystyle 0\to L_{4k+3}^{(2k+1)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to

\displaystyle L_{4k+3}^{(2k+2)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\oplus\mathbb{Z}/2\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to

\displaystyle L_{4k+2}^{(2k+1)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to L_{4k+2}^{(2k+2)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to 0

using that
\pi_{i}^{C_{2}}(\Sigma^{(2k+1)\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4)
is zero for
i>4k+2
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2
at
i=4k+2
(by Lemma
3.4
), and as we just observed the map into that group
for
i=4k+1=2n-1
is zero. Filling in the values of
L^{(n)}_{i}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)

that we have already computed gives

0\to L_{4k+3}^{(2k+1)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+3}\to\mathbb{Z}/2\to L_{4k+2}^{(2k+1)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+1}\to 0\mathrm{.}

This shows
L^{(2k+1)}_{4k+2}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
has order at most
2^{2k+2}
, so
considering the list of possibilities in (
8
) we
must have

L^{(2k+1)}_{4k+2}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+2}\mathrm{.}

Then the rest of the exact sequence shows

L^{(2k+1)}_{4k+3}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+3}\mathrm{.}

Finally consider the case where
n=2k
is even. We obtain a very similar exact sequence with the
difference that now
\pi^{C_{2}}_{4k}(\Sigma^{2k\rho}H\mathbb{Z}/4)\cong\mathbb{Z}/4
, so we get

0\to L_{4k+1}^{(2k)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+2}\to\mathbb{Z}/4\to L_{4k}^{(2k)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k}\to 0

There is no exact sequence
\mathbb{Z}/4\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+2}\to(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k}\to 0
, so considering our list of possibilities we must have

L^{(2k)}_{4k}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\in\{(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+1},(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4\}\mathrm{.}

If
L^{(2k)}_{4k}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+1}
then the map
from
\mathbb{Z}/4
has kernel
\mathbb{Z}/2
, so we find

L^{(2k)}_{4k+1}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+1}\mathrm{.}

Otherwise
L^{(2k)}_{4k}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4
; in this case the map from
\mathbb{Z}/4
is injective and we find

L^{(2k)}_{4k+1}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2k+2}\mathrm{.}\qed

Remark 3.17
.

Note this result agrees with the output of our computer program, which
we have used to compute
L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}
for
i,n\leq 6
. In the
cases where the above theorem does not give a definite answer, the
program gives us

\displaystyle L_{0}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{Z}/4,\quad\quad

\displaystyle L_{1}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\text{,}

\displaystyle L_{4}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)

\displaystyle\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{2}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4,\quad\quad

\displaystyle L_{5}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{4}\text{.}

3.2
Application to size estimates

Combining Theorem
3.16
with Lemma
3.3
gives
us lower and upper bounds for
\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert
.

Theorem 3.18
.

We have bounds

2^{(i+1)(5i+7)/8}\leq\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert\leq 2^{(i+2)(5i+8)/8}\text{.}

Proof.

We work with the base 2 logarithms of the orders of the groups to make the
notation easier. Recall Lemma
3.3
, which says that

\log_{2}\,\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert=\left\lfloor(i+1)^{2}/4\right\rfloor+\sum_{0\leq n\leq i}\log_{2}\,\left\lvert L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert\text{.}

We apply the results of Theorem
3.16
to analyse the sum

\sum_{0\leq n\leq i}\log_{2}\,\big|L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\big|
.
For the range
0\leq n\leq\lfloor i/2\rfloor
we know
L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)
takes a constant value; we have

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq n\leq\lfloor i/2\rfloor}\log_{2}\,\left\lvert L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=(\lfloor i/2\rfloor+1)\log_{2}\,\left\lvert L_{i}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert

where
\log_{2}\,\left\lvert L_{i}^{(0)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert\in\{\lfloor i/2\rfloor+1,\lfloor i/2\rfloor+2\}
, giving lower and upper bounds for this
term. For the range
\lfloor i/2\rfloor<n\leq i
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lfloor i/2\rfloor<n\leq i}\log_{2}\,\left\lvert L_{i}^{(n)}F_{\mathbb{Z}/4}(\mathbb{Z}/2)\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lfloor i/2\rfloor<n\leq i}(i-n+1)

\displaystyle=(\lceil i/2\rceil)(\lceil i/2\rceil+1)/2\text{.}

Combining
these calculations and for convenience eliminating the floors and ceilings (at the cost of
very slightly loosening the bounds) gives the result.
∎

Finally we have enough ingredients to use Lemma
3.1
to get
asymptotic bounds on the order of the homotopy groups of

\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
. The following appeared as
Theorem
E
in the introduction.

Theorem 3.19
.

The groups

\pi_{i}\left(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\right)

grow rapidly: the logarithm of the order asymptotically grows quadratically in
i
.

More precisely, for
i\geq 0
we have upper and lower bounds

2^{(i-1)(i+1)/8}\leq\left\lvert\pi_{i}\left(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}\right)\right\rvert\leq 2^{(i+2)(9i+20)/8}\text{.}

Proof.

Apply Lemma
3.1
to the long exact sequence for

\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2}
, giving

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert}{\left\lvert\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert\cdot\left\lvert\pi_{i+1}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert\mathrm{.}

Now by Proposition
3.2
computing

\left\lvert\pi_{i}(\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert
and
Theorem
3.18
proving bounds on

\left\lvert\pi_{i}((\mathrm{THR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})^{C_{2}})\right\rvert
, we find

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left(\left\lvert\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\geq(i+1)(5i+7)/8-(i+1)(i+2)/2

\displaystyle=(i-1)(i+1)/8

and

\displaystyle\log_{2}\left(\left\lvert\pi_{i}(\mathrm{TCR}(\mathbb{Z}/4)^{\phi\mathbb{Z}/2})\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\leq(i+2)(5i+8)/8+(i+2)(i+3)/2

\displaystyle=(i+2)(9i+20)/8\qed

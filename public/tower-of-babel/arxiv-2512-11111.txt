Analysis of a Discontinuous Galerkin Method for Diffusion Problems on Intersecting Domains
Miroslav Kuchta 1 , Rami Masri 2 , and Beatrice Riviere 3

Abstract
. The interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin method is applied to solve elliptic equations on either networks of segments or networks of planar surfaces, with arbitrary but fixed number of bifurcations. Stability is obtained by proving a discrete Poincaré’s inequality on the hypergraphs. Convergence of the scheme is proved for H r H^{r} regularity solution with 1 < r ≤ 2 1<r\leq 2 . In the low regularity case ( r ≤ 3 / 2 r\leq 3/2 ), a weak consistency result is obtained via generalized lifting operators for Sobolev spaces defined on hypergraphs. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results. Keywords. hypergraphs; enriching map; Kirchoff condition; Poincaré’s inequality; low regularity; error estimates. MSCcodes. 65M60, 65N30, 53Z99, 57N99



1.
Introduction

There is a need for the numerical analysis of elliptic partial differential equations (PDEs) in non-standard computational domains, namely, networks of manifolds of lower dimensions. This type of problem arises in many application domains, for instance, from geosciences, where the network of planar surfaces may represent natural fractures in the subsurface (see
[
6
]
and references therein). Another application is in
biomedicine, where the network of one-dimensional lines is a representation of the vasculature of an organ: indeed, each blood vessel is reduced to its centerline
[
27
,
1
,
29
,
11
]
. One main advantage of using topological model reduction to obtain PDEs on manifolds of lower dimension is the gain in computational efficiency.

In this work, we formulate and analyze a discontinuous Galerkin method for solving the diffusion problem in two types of networks: (i) a network made of connected one-dimensional lines and (ii) a network made of connected two-dimensional planar polygonal surfaces. In either case, the computational domain is a hypergraph with an arbitrary but fixed number of graph edges and graph nodes including bifurcation nodes.
Standard interior penalty discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods assume that an
interior facet is shared by exactly two elements. However, hypergraphs lead to meshes where this assumption is no longer valid.
To discretely enforce the conditions at the bifurcation graph nodes, a special flux is constructed that generalizes the discontinuous Galerkin fluxes usually used on standard computational domains. With a generalized definition of jumps and averages at the bifurcation nodes, the interface conditions are seamlessly written in the DG form. We introduce an enriching map that is the key ingredient in the proof of the discrete Poincaré’s inequality on hypergraphs, used to obtain the stability of the numerical solutions.

Convergence of the scheme is established for the case of a smooth solution and for the case of a low regularity solution. The numerical analysis for the low regularity solution is non-standard as Galerkin orthogonality is no longer valid. The proof utilizes and extends to hypergraphs the functional analysis tools introduced in
[
19
]
for classical domains. They include the generalization of lifting operators and mollification operators to networks of planar surfaces. By defining a weak notion of the normal traces, we extend the DG bilinear form so that the first argument belongs to a space on the hypergraph of low regularity functions. We then obtain a weak consistency result and we derive a priori error bounds.

As mentioned above, this work is relevant to the study of flows in fractured porous media for the geosciences applications. In that case, flow inside the network of fractures is coupled with the flow in the surrounding rock matrix. The literature on mixed-dimensional models of Darcy flow in fractured porous media is vast, specially in the simpler case of one single fracture. Over the last decade, there has been an increase on the numerical investigation of the more challenging case of networks of fractures embedded into a porous medium, see for instance the
works
[
34
,
4
,
9
,
8
,
30
,
2
,
3
]
and the references therein.
Our work can also be placed in the broader context of numerical analysis of surface PDEs. The literature on the Laplace-Beltrami PDE is significant: for instance, finite element method
[
15
,
22
,
7
]
, discontinuous Galerkin methods
[
14
,
13
,
10
]
and other discretization methods have been studied. On one hand, because the domains are one dimensional lines or two dimensional planar polygons, the use of the Laplace-Beltrami operator is not needed here and this facilitates the numerical analysis of the PDEs. On the other hand, the fact that the lines or surfaces intersect and form a hypergraph introduces challenges in the numerical analysis. Additional constraints on the bifurcation nodes are required and special treatment of the interface conditions is needed to ensure well-posedness and convergence of the numerical methods.

The literature on theoretical numerical analysis of PDEs on hypergraphs remains scarce. In
[
33
,
25
]
, the authors analyze a mixed hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin method for elliptic equations on hypergraphs (network of lines or network of planar surfaces). In
[
33
]
, the Kirchoff interface condition at the bifurcation nodes of the hypergraph is relaxed by the addition of a concentrated source. Another DG method is formulated in
[
28
]
for networks of 1D lines: in that work, hybridization is occurring only at the bifurcation nodes and only existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution is shown. The work
[
23
]
presents a
continuous finite element method to solve the diffusion problem on composite surfaces. The authors propose a Nitsche formulation of the Kirchoff law at the interfaces of the composite surfaces that can be viewed (after some algebraic manipulation) as a generalization of our approach for handling the Kirchoff condition. With Galerkin orthogonality,
error bounds are derived for smooth enough solutions, namely functions in
H^{1+s}
on each surface for
1/2<s\leq 1
. Conservation laws on networks of 1D lines are numerically discretized by a finite volume method in
[
20
]
; convergence of the numerical solution to a unique entropy solution is proved. PolyDG methods are analyzed for coupled flows in
[
3
]

under the assumption of smooth enough solutions, and for the simpler case of a hypergraph with only one bifurcation node.

2.
Continuous problem

We consider two types of networks or hypergraphs (see Fig.
1
):
(i) an edge-network made of one-dimensional edges
\Omega_{i}
connected by a set
\Gamma
of bifurcation nodes (i.e. points) and (ii) a plane-network made of planar polygonal surfaces
\Omega_{i}
connected by a set
\Gamma
of bifurcation nodes (i.e. segments).

2.1.
Notation

Edge-network

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a directed graph made of edges
\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. The graph
\mathcal{G}
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
For each
i
, denote by
\gamma_{\mathrm{in}}^{i}
and
\gamma_{\mathrm{out}}^{i}
the two vertices of
\Omega_{i}
and define the values
n_{i}(\gamma_{\mathrm{in}}^{i})=-1
and
n_{i}(\gamma_{\mathrm{out}}^{i})=+1
.
Let
\Gamma
be the set of bifurcation points; this means that each vertex
\gamma\in\Gamma
belongs to at least three distinct graph edges.

Plane-network

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a hypergraph made of planar polygonal surfaces
\Omega_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
for
1\leq i\leq N
. The hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Let
\Gamma
be the set of bifurcation segments (also called interfaces in this work) that result from the intersection of the planar surfaces; this means that each segment
\gamma\in\Gamma
belongs to at least three distinct planar surfaces. Since each domain
\Omega_{i}
is embedded into a two-dimensional plane, we can uniquely define the unit normal outward vector
\boldsymbol{n}_{i}
to
\Omega_{i}
.

.

2.2.
Model problem

For each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, we associate the set
I_{\gamma}
of indices
i

such that
\gamma
belongs to
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
. In other words, we have

\gamma=\bigcap_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\overline{\Omega_{i}}.

We also associate to each bifurcation
\gamma
the set
D_{\gamma}
of ordered pairs of indices corresponding to the domains
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
that contain
\gamma
:

D_{\gamma}=\{(i,j)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}i<j,\quad i,j\in I_{\gamma}\}.

We assume that the cardinality of
I_{\gamma}
(resp. of
D_{\gamma}
) is uniformly bounded.
We denote by
\partial\mathcal{G}
the union of the boundaries of the domains
\Omega_{i}
, that do not contain the bifurcations.

\partial\mathcal{G}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\partial\Omega_{i}\setminus\Gamma.

For the edge-network,
\partial\mathcal{G}
is the set of nodes of the graph that are connected to one edge only. For the plane-network,
\partial\mathcal{G}
is the union of the line segments that belong each to the boundary of only one planar surface.

Each domain
\Omega_{i}
(edge or planar polygonal surface) is associated with a separate coordinate system;
this uniquely defines the gradient (or higher derivatives) of a function with respect to that coordinate system. Nevertheless, for readability, we will use the same notation for the partial derivatives for all the domains
\Omega_{i}
.

We solve an elliptic problem on
\mathcal{G}
. Let
u

be the exact solution and denote by
u_{i}=u|_{\Omega_{i}}
the restriction of
u
to
\Omega_{i}
. The solution
u
satisfies

(2.1)

-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})=f_{i},\quad\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})=\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i},\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega_{i},\quad 1\leq i\leq N,

with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition:

(2.2)

u=0,\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\mathcal{G}.

The functions
f
and
\kappa>0
are given and we denote
f_{i}=f|_{\Omega_{i}}
and
\kappa_{i}=\kappa|_{\Omega_{i}}
.
We assume that
f_{i}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{i})
and

\kappa_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq N
.

To complete the problem posed on
\mathcal{G}
, we impose the following coupling conditions at the bifurcation set
\Gamma
:

(2.3)

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})|_{\gamma}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad\forall i,i^{\prime}\in I_{\gamma},\quad u_{i}|_{\gamma}=u_{i^{\prime}}|_{\gamma}.

Condition (
2.3
) is known as Kirchoff’s law. For the edge-network, the condition reduces to

\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sigma_{i}(u_{i})|_{\gamma}\,n_{i}(\gamma)=0.

Condition (
2.4
) enforces continuity of the solution at the bifurcation nodes.

Problem (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) has a unique weak solution in the space
H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{G})
defined by

H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})=\{v|_{\Omega_{i}}=v_{i}\in H^{1}(\Omega_{i}),\,1\leq i\leq N;\quad v_{i}|_{\gamma}=v_{i^{\prime}}|_{\gamma},\,\forall i,i^{\prime}\in I_{\gamma},\,\forall\gamma\in\Gamma;\,v=0\,\,\mbox{on}\,\partial\mathcal{G}\}.

Indeed it is easy to see that the variational formulation is: find
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
such that

(2.5)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}fv_{i},\quad\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G}).

Well-posedness is obtained with Lax-Milgram’s theorem thanks to Poincaré’s inequality
[
33
]
: there is a constant
C_{P}>0
such that

(2.6)

\forall v\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G}),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\leq C_{P}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}.

3.
Numerical schemes

3.1.
Preliminaries

The novel formulation of our proposed schemes stems from a rewriting of the Kirchoff laws using a generalized definition of jumps and averages of functions at the bifurcation nodes. For any scalar function
v
and vector function
\boldsymbol{w}
, the jump of
v
and average of
\boldsymbol{w}
across a bifurcation node that is the intersection of two domains
\overline{\Omega_{i}}
and
\overline{\Omega_{j}}
are defined by:

(3.1)

\forall(i,j)\in D_{\gamma},\quad[v]_{(i,j)}=v|_{\Omega_{i}}-v|_{\Omega_{j}},\quad\quad\left\{\boldsymbol{w}\right\}_{(i,j)}=\boldsymbol{w}|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-\boldsymbol{w}|_{\Omega_{j}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}.

In the case of the edge-network, the average reduces to

\left\{v\right\}_{(i,j)}=v|_{\Omega_{i}}n_{i}(\gamma)-v|_{\Omega_{j}}n_{j}(\gamma).

In the remainder of the paper, we use the notation
v_{i}=v|_{\Omega_{i}}
for any scalar or vector function
v
defined on
\mathcal{G}
.

The construction of these generalized jumps and averages leads to the following lemma for the exact solution
u
. In fact, this is valid for any function
u
that satisfies the Kirchoff’s law (
2.3
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u,v
be functions such that
u_{i},v_{i}
belong to
H^{1}(\Omega_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. In addition, assume that
u
is regular enough to satisfy (
2.3
).
Denote by
\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})
the cardinality of
I_{\gamma}
.
We have

(3.2)

\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,v_{i}=\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)\right\}_{(i,j)}[v]_{(i,j)}.

Proof.

We provide the proof for the plane-network, as the case of the edge-network is handled similarly.
Fix
i\in I_{\gamma}
and write

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,v_{i}=\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v_{i}=\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v_{i}+\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v_{i}.

With the Kirchoff’s law (
2.3
), we have

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}=-\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}(u_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}.

This implies

\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v_{i}=\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}(u_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j})v_{i}.

We now sum over
i
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}v_{i}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j<i}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}(u_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j})v_{i}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j>i}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}(u_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j})v_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j>i}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{j}(u_{j})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j})(v_{i}-v_{j}),

which is the desired result.
∎

In what follows, we use the short-hand notation, for any function
w,v
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad(\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(w)\right\}\odot[v])|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\mbox{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(w)\right\}_{(i,j)}[v]_{(i,j)},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad([w]\odot[v])|_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}[w]_{(i,j)}[v]_{(i,j)}.

3.2.
DG spaces and Poincaré’s inequality

We discretize the domains
\Omega_{i}
of the hypergraph
\mathcal{G}
into shape-regular meshes and define a finite dimensional DG space based on the resulting partition. In the case of the edge-network, each segment
\Omega_{i}
is
partitioned into a set of intervals
K
, that forms a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
. In the case of the plane-network,
each polygonal domain
\Omega_{i}
is partitioned into a set of triangular elements
K
, that forms a mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be the union of the partitions
\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
.
The mesh size of the domain
\Omega_{i}
is
h_{i}=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\mbox{diam}(K)
and
the overall mesh size is
h=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}h_{i}
.

In the case of the edge-network, for each bifurcation
\gamma
,
we denote by
h_{\gamma}
the maximum of the lengths of all edges connecting to
\gamma
.
In the case of the plane-network, the meshes are assumed to match at the bifurcation interfaces between two domains. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
the resulting partition (made of intervals) of the interface
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

The DG space is

V^{p}_{h}=\left\{v\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}v|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{p}(K),\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i},\,\forall 1\leq i\leq N\right\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{p}(K)
is the space of polynomials of degree at most
p
defined on
K
.
For each mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
, the set of interior mesh edges for the plane-network (resp. points for the edge-network) is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}
.
The set of mesh edges for the plane-network (resp. points for the edge-network) that belong to the boundary
\partial\mathcal{G}
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}
.

For each domain
\Omega_{i}
, we define the semi-norm

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG},i}=\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}\|[v_{i}]\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}\|v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and the DG norm on
\Omega
is defined for
the plane-network by:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG},i}^{2}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\eta_{\gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and for the edge network, by:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG},i}^{2}+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}[v]_{(i,j)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

In the above, the parameter
h_{F}
is the length of the edge
F
for the plane-network and it is the maximum of the lengths of the neighboring intervals sharing the point
F
for the edge-network. The penalty parameters
\eta_{F,i},\eta_{\gamma}
are positive constants, to be specified later.

We conclude this section by proving a Poincaré inequality for functions in
V_{h}^{p}
. We begin with the enriching map given in
Lemma

3.2
; the construction can be found in many references for classical domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
; see for e.g.
[
24
,
18
]
. Since our setting is a network of edges or planes, the proof is modified slightly and we provide it for completeness.

We require additional notation. Let
\mathcal{N}_{h}
denote the set of all vertices of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the network domain
\mathcal{G}
, excluding the vertices belonging to
\partial\mathcal{G}
. For any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, let
\mathcal{N}_{K}
denote the set of vertices of
K
. We denote by
\{\varphi_{z}\}_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}
the corresponding nodal basis of polynomial order
1
. Namely,
\{\varphi_{z}\}_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}
denotes the basis for the space
V_{h}^{1}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
. For any vertex
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
, let
\omega_{z}
denote the set of mesh elements sharing the node
z
.
The enriching/averaging map
E_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{h}^{1}\rightarrow V_{h}^{1}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is defined by setting

E_{h}v=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}E_{h}v(z)\varphi_{z},

where
E_{h}v(z)
is given by

(3.7)

E_{h}v(z)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(\omega_{z})}\sum_{K\in\omega_{z}}v|_{K}(z).

Recall that card(
\omega_{z}
) is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
due to the shape regularity assumption and due to the assumption
that card(
I_{\gamma}
) is uniformly bounded with respect to
h
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Finally, throughout the paper, we shall use the standard notation
A\lesssim B
if there is a generic constant
C
independent of mesh parameters such that
A\leq CB
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The enriching map
E_{h}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V_{h}\rightarrow V_{h}^{1}\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
satisfies the following properties. For any
v\in V_{h}^{1}
, we have

(3.8)

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim h\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}},

and

(3.9)

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla E_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}.

Proof.

We give the proof for the plane-network case as a similar and simpler argument can be used for the edge-network. We need to introduce additional notation. For any
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
, let
\mathcal{F}_{z}
denote the set of edges in
\omega_{z}
that share the vertex
z
. If the vertex

z
does not belong to
\Gamma
, then
\mathcal{F}_{z}=\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}
consists only of interior
edges that belong to one of the domains
\Omega_{i}
. If
z
lies on one bifurcation
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then

\mathcal{F}_{z}
is the union of disjoint sets of interior edges belonging to
\Omega_{i}
for
i\in I_{\gamma}
, and
of edges that belong to the bifurcation
\gamma
. We write in this case:

\mathcal{F}_{z}=\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\gamma}
.

We fix
v\in V_{h}^{1}
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We claim that:

(3.10)

\|E_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{K}\|\nabla(E_{h}v-v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}\\
\lesssim h_{K}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}}h_{F}^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{L^{2}(F)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\gamma}}h_{F}^{-1/2}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\right),

Clearly, a consequence of the above inequalities is the desired result (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). It suffices to show the estimate on
\|E_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(K)}
since the estimate on
\|\nabla(E_{h}v-v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}
follows from a local inverse inequality.
By definition of
E_{h}v
, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim|K|^{1/2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left|E_{h}v(z)-v|_{K}(z)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|K|^{1/2}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\omega_{z}}\left|v|_{K^{\prime}}(z)-v|_{K}(z)\right|.

If
z
does not belong to
\Gamma
, we have

\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\omega_{z}}|v|_{K^{\prime}}(z)-v|_{K}(z)|\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}}|F|^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

If
z
belongs to
\Gamma
, then
z
belongs to some segment
\gamma
. If we have for instance
K\subset\Omega_{i_{0}}
,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{K^{\prime}\in\omega_{z}}|v|_{K^{\prime}}(z)-v|_{K}(z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}}|F|^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{L^{2}(F)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\gamma}}|F|^{-1/2}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma}}\|[v]_{(i_{0},j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}}|F|^{-1/2}\|[v]\|_{L^{2}(F)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\gamma}}|F|^{-1/2}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

Therefore, we have in either case:

\|E_{h}v-v\|_{L^{2}(K)}\lesssim\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{K}}\left(\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\text{int}}}\frac{|K|^{1/2}}{|F|^{1/2}}\|[v]\|_{L^{2}(F)}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{z}^{\gamma}}\frac{|K|^{1/2}}{|F|^{1/2}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\right).

This implies the desired bound stated in (
3.10
).
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Stability of
L^{2}
projection)
.

For any
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
, denote by
\pi_{h}v\in V_{h}^{1}
the
L^{2}
projection of
v
onto
V_{h}^{1}
. Then, we have

(3.11)

\forall v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\quad\|\pi_{h}v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}.

The proof of the lemma is skipped as it follows standard arguments.

A corollary of
Lemma

3.2
and
Lemma

3.3
is Poincaré’s inequality in the broken space
H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
.

Lemma 3.4

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

For all
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

(3.12)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}.

Proof.

Fix
v\in H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and denote by
\pi_{h}v\in V_{h}^{1}
the
L^{2}
projection of
v
onto
V_{h}^{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|v-\pi_{h}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\pi_{h}v-E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim h\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}+h\|\pi_{h}v\|^{2}_{\mathrm{DG}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2},

where we used the approximation of the
L^{2}
projection and
(
3.8
).
Next, we apply Poincaré’s inequality (
2.6
) to
E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)

\displaystyle\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla E_{h}(\pi_{h}v)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\lesssim\|\pi_{h}v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2},

where we used (
3.9
) and (
3.11
). We can then conclude by combining the bounds.
∎

3.3.
The DG schemes

The DG approximation of problem (
2.1
)-(
2.4
) satisfies: find
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle a(u_{h},v_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}v_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}},\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{p},

where
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is a bilinear form that is presented below for each type of network for completeness. The forms differ primarily because of the different spatial dimensions. The choice of the parameter
\epsilon_{i},\epsilon\in\{-1,0,1\}
in the definition of the bilinear forms, yields the non-symmetric interior penalty Galerkin (NIPG), incomplete interior penalty Galerkin (IIPG), symmetric interior penalty Galerkin (SIPG) versions of the interior penalty DG method.

Bilinear form for edge-network

(3.14)

\displaystyle a(w,v)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}(w_{i},v_{i})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\{\sigma(w)\}\odot[v]-\epsilon\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\{\sigma(v)\}\odot[w]+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{\gamma}}[w]\odot[v].

The form
a_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)
represents the interior penalty DG discretization of the PDE on each segment
\Omega_{i}
:

\displaystyle a_{i}(w_{i},v_{i})=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\kappa_{i}\frac{\partial w_{i}}{\partial s}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial s}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\{\kappa_{i}\frac{\partial w_{i}}{\partial s}\}|_{F}[v_{i}]|_{F}-\epsilon_{i}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\{\kappa_{i}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial s}\}|_{F}[w_{i}]|_{F}

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}[w_{i}]|_{F}[v_{i}]|_{F}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}\,w_{i}|_{F}\,v_{i}|_{F}

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\kappa_{i}\frac{\partial w_{i}}{\partial s}|_{F}\,n_{i}(F)\,v_{i}-\epsilon_{i}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\kappa_{i}\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial s}|_{F}\,n_{i}(F)\,w_{i}.

At any interior point
F
, the standard jump operator
[\cdot]|_{F}
and average operator
\{\cdot\}|_{F}
used in the form above, are defined by:

v(F^{\pm})=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0,s>0}v(F\pm s),\quad[v]|_{F}=v(F^{-})-v(F^{+}),\quad\{v\}|_{F}=\frac{1}{2}v(F^{-})+\frac{1}{2}v(F^{+}).

Bilinear form for plane-network

\displaystyle a(w,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}(w_{i},v_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(w)\right\}\odot[v]-\epsilon\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\right\}\odot[w]

(3.15)

\displaystyle+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\eta_{\gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}\int_{F}[w]\odot[v].

For each domain
\Omega_{i}
, the form

a_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by:

\displaystyle a_{i}(w_{i},v_{i})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\kappa_{i}\nabla w_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\int_{F}\{\kappa_{i}\nabla w_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\}[v_{i}]

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{i}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\int_{F}\{\kappa_{i}\nabla v_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\}[w_{i}]+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}[w_{i}][v_{i}]

\displaystyle-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\int_{F}\kappa_{i}\nabla w_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,v_{i}-\epsilon_{i}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\int_{F}\kappa_{i}\nabla v_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,w_{i}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\frac{\eta_{F,i}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}w_{i}\,v_{i}.

To complete the definition of
a_{i}(\cdot,\cdot)
, we introduce the vectors
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
, the jump and average operators
[\cdot],\{\cdot\}
.
For each interior edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}
, we fix a unit normal vector to
F
, that is denoted simply by
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
for readability; this is well defined since the domain
\Omega_{i}
is embedded into a plane.
For each boundary edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}
, the vector
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
is defined to be the outward normal
\boldsymbol{n}_{i}
to
\Omega_{i}
.
We now recall the standard DG jump operator
[\cdot]|_{F}
and average operator
\{\cdot\}|_{F}
. Fix an edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}
and denote by
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
the two triangles sharing
F
such that the vector
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
points from
K_{1}
into
K_{2}
. We have

[v]|_{F}=v|_{K_{1}}-v|_{K_{2}},\quad\{v\}|_{F}=\frac{1}{2}v|_{K_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}v|_{K_{2}}.

3.4.
Derivation of the DG scheme

We present the formal derivation of our scheme only in the case of the plane-network problem, as the case of the edge-network problem is treated similarly.
We multiply (
2.1
) by a test function
v\in V_{h}^{p}
, use Green’s theorem (which is valid if
u|_{\Omega_{i}}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for any
i
) and sum over the domains to obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,v_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}v_{i},

where the form
\tilde{a}_{i}
is:

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla v_{i}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\int_{F}\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}[v_{i}]|_{F}-\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\int_{F}\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,v_{i}.

With
Lemma

3.1
, we rewrite

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)\}_{(i,j)}[v]_{(i,j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}v_{i}.

Using the short-hand notation (
3.3
) and the fact that

u
satisfies (
2.4
) at each bifurcation, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{a}_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)\}\odot[v]-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\}\odot[u]=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}v_{i}.

For each domain
\Omega_{i}
, the term
\tilde{a}_{i}(u_{i},v_{i})
leads to the standard interior penalty DG methods. Indeed,
on the interior of each domain
\Omega_{i}
, if
u\in H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
, the jump of the flux

\kappa_{i}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
across each edge
F
is zero in the
L^{2}
sense. In addition, the jump of the solution
u_{i}
is also zero in the
L^{2}
sense. Therefore we apply symmetrization terms and penalty terms to obtain (
3.13
).

Remark 1

(Consistency for edge-network)
.

In the case of the edge-network, if the coefficient
\kappa|_{\Omega_{i}}
is smooth enough (for instance
\kappa_{i}
is a constant or
\kappa_{i}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega_{i})
), then the exact solution
u|_{\Omega_{i}}
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. In that case, the derivation above shows that the DG scheme is consistent:

(3.16)

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{p},\quad a(u,v_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}v_{h}.

4.
Well-posedness of the DG scheme

We now state and prove coercivity of the form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
under the following assumption on the parameters
\epsilon_{i}
and
\eta_{F,i}
.

Assumption 1
.

Fix
1\leq i\leq N
.
Assume that on one hand, if
\epsilon_{i}=-1
, then
\eta_{F,i}>0
, and on the other hand if
\epsilon_{i}=+1
or
0
, then
\eta_{F,i}
is bounded below by a large enough positive constant. Assume also that for all bifurcations
\gamma
, the parameter
\eta_{\gamma}
is bounded below by a large enough positive constant.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under
1
, we have

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{p},\quad\|v_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}\lesssim a(v_{h},v_{h}).

Proof.

Again, here we provide a proof for the plane-network case, the edge-network case being handled in a similar manner.
Using an argument that is standard for the analysis of interior penalty DG methods for elliptic problems
[
31
]
, one can show that if
\eta_{F,i}
is large enough, we have

\forall v\in V_{h}^{p},\quad\|v\|_{\mathrm{DG},i}^{2}\lesssim a_{i}(v_{i},v_{i}).

The new contribution is the term involving the bifurcation nodes. We provide a few details below as the argument is based on standard DG techniques. We remark that if
w=v
, the last term in (
3.15
) appears in the DG norm. It remains to find a lower bound for the following term

2\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\kappa\nabla v\right\}\odot[v]\lesssim\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\|\left\{\kappa\nabla v\right\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\lesssim\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}h_{F}^{-1}\|[v]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}h_{F}\|\left\{\kappa\nabla v\right\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

With a discrete trace inequality, we have for a fixed bifurcation
\gamma
, a fixed pair
(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}
and a fixed edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle h_{F}\|\left\{\kappa\nabla v\right\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2h_{F}\|\kappa_{i}\nabla v_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}+2h_{F}\|\kappa_{j}\nabla v_{j}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(K_{F}^{+})}^{2}+\|\nabla v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(K_{F}^{-})}^{2},

where
K_{F}^{+}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
and
K_{F}^{-}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,j}
are the mesh elements in the two surfaces
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{j}
that share
F
.
Since the number of bifurcations is finite and the cardinality of
D_{\gamma}
is uniformly bounded over
\gamma
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{i}}h_{F}\|\left\{\kappa\nabla v\right\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\|\nabla v_{i}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

Combining the bounds yields coercivity if the penalty parameter
\eta_{\gamma}
is large enough.
∎

Problem (
3.13
) is finite-dimensional; thus the coercivity of the form immediately yields existence and uniqueness of the discrete solution.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the conditions of
Lemma

4.1
, there exists a unique solution
u_{h}
to (
3.13
).

With
Lemma

3.4
, we easily derive a stability bound for the numerical solution.

Lemma 4.3

(Stability)
.

Let
u_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
solve (
3.13
). Then,

(4.1)

\|u_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2})^{1/2}.

Proof.

This is an easy consequence of the coercivity property
Lemma

4.1
, Cauchy–Schwarz’s inequality and Poincaré’s inequality
Lemma

3.4
.
∎

5.
Convergence

5.1.
A priori error bounds under
H^{2}
regularity

In this section, we assume that the exact solution
u
is regular enough, namely
u|_{\Omega_{i}}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
. This assumption is certainly valid for the case of the edge-network (see
Remark

1
) if
\kappa_{i}
is smooth enough. For the plane-network, the solution may not be in
H^{2}
on each domain
\Omega_{i}
and a low regularity assumption is treated in
Section

5.2
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume the exact solution
u|_{\Omega_{i}}
belongs to
H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
. Then, Galerkin orthogonality holds:

\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}^{p},\quad a(u-u_{h},v_{h})=0.

Proof.

With the local
H^{2}
regularity, the derivation of the scheme in
Section

3.4
is mathematically justified. The exact solution satisfies the scheme, which immediately yields the Galerkin orthogonality.
∎

Convergence of the method is obtained by deriving optimal a priori error bounds in the DG norm.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume the exact solution
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
is such that
u|_{\Omega_{i}}
belongs to
H^{s+1}(\Omega_{i})
for
s\geq 1
.
Then, under
1
, we have

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim h^{\min(p,s)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|u_{i}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof follows a standard argument; the only difference is the addition of the bifurcation terms. We provide only an outline of the proof.

Let
\tilde{u}\in V_{h}^{p}\cap\mathcal{C}(\overline{\Omega})
be an optimal approximation of
u
. For instance, one may choose for
\tilde{u}
the Lagrange interpolant of
u
.
We have by coercivity and Galerkin orthogonality

\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}^{2}\lesssim a(u_{h}-\tilde{u},u_{h}-\tilde{u})=a(u-\tilde{u},u_{h}-\tilde{u}).

The first term in the right-hand side is the sum of classical DG formulations; it is handled by a standard DG error analysis. We then obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}(u-\tilde{u},u_{h}-\tilde{u})\lesssim\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\,h^{\min(p,s)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(\Omega_{i})}\right)^{1/2}.

Continuity of
u
and
\tilde{u}
on any
\gamma\in\Gamma
imply that most bifurcation terms vanish, except for

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u-\tilde{u})\right\}\odot[u_{h}-\tilde{u}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u-\tilde{u})\right\}_{(i,j)}[u_{h}-\tilde{u}]_{(i,j)}.

We have, since
\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})
is uniformly bounded

\displaystyle T\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{F}}\|[u_{h}-\tilde{u}]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}h_{F}\|\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u-\tilde{u})\right\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The first factor is bounded above by
\|u_{h}-\tilde{u}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}
. The second factor is treated with trace inequalities and approximation theory, by considering separately each pair
(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}
and the mesh elements
K^{-}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
and

K^{+}\in\mathcal{T}_{h,j}
that share the face
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
.
∎

5.2.
A priori error bounds under low regularity for plane-networks

Since we do not know a priori that
u|_{\Omega_{i}}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for the plane-network case, Galerkin orthogonality does not hold and therefore the error analysis becomes non-standard. In this section, we fix
0<s<1
and we work with the following space

(5.1)

V^{1+s}=\{v\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\;v_{i}\in H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i}),\;\;\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(v_{i})\in L^{2}(\Omega_{i}),\;\;1\leq i\leq N\}.

In what follows, a weak meaning for the normal trace of the fluxes
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(v_{i})
for
v\in V^{1+s}
is defined following
[
19
]
. This will allow us to show a weak notion of consistency by extending the form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
to the space
V^{1+s}\times V_{h}^{p}
.

For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, denote by
\boldsymbol{n}_{K}
the unit normal vector outward of
K
and define
\mathcal{F}_{K}
as the set of edges belonging to
\partial K
. Define
\rho=4/(2-2s)
and let
\rho^{\prime}
be its conjugate,
1/\rho+1/\rho^{\prime}=1
. Observe that
u_{i}\in H^{1+s}(K)
implies that
\nabla u_{i}\in H^{s}(K)
, and by Sobolev embedding
[
16
, Theorem 2.31]
, this implies that
\nabla u_{i}\in L^{\rho}(K)^{2}
. Hence if
\kappa_{i}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})
,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\in L^{\rho}(\Omega_{i})^{2}
. We consider the lifting operator
[
19
, Lemma 3.1]
, that satisfies for
all
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
:

(5.2)

L^{K}_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W^{\frac{1}{\rho},\rho^{\prime}}(F)\rightarrow W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(K),\quad\gamma_{0}(L_{F}^{K}(\phi))|_{\partial K}=\begin{cases}\phi&\mathrm{on}\,\,\,F,\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}\end{cases},

where
\gamma_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(K)\rightarrow W^{1-1/\rho^{\prime},\rho^{\prime}}(\partial K)
is the trace operator.
This lifting operator is utilized to give weak meaning to the normal traces. For each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the normal trace of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})
on an edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{K}
is defined as a member of the dual space of
W^{1/\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(F)
as follows

(5.3)

\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})_{|F},\phi\rangle_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{K}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\nabla L_{F}^{K}(\phi)+(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i}))\,L_{F}^{K}(\phi)),\quad\forall\phi\in W^{1/\rho,\rho^{\prime}}(F).

The above is well defined since
\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\in L^{\rho}(K)
,
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\in L^{2}(K)
and
L_{F}^{K}(\phi)\in L^{2}(K)
because
W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(K)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(K)
by Sobolev embedding
[
16
, Theorem 2.31]
. We will also require the following space: for
1\leq r<\infty

(5.4)

\displaystyle W^{1,r}_{0}(\mathcal{G})

\displaystyle=\{v|_{\Omega_{i}}=v_{i}\in W^{1,r}(\Omega_{i}),\;1\leq i\leq N\}\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathcal{G}).

The next step is to consider a suitable extension of the form
a
to the space
V^{1+s}\times V_{h}^{p}
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{F}
denote the elements
K
sharing the edge
F
and let
\epsilon_{K,F}=(\boldsymbol{n}_{K}|_{F})\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
. Clearly,
\epsilon_{K,F}
takes the value
\pm 1
depending on the orientation of
\boldsymbol{n}_{K}
with respect to
\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
. Observe that if
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
then the cardinality of
\mathcal{T}_{F}
is equal to
\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})
.
For each edge that is interior to a domain,
\ F\in\cup_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}},
define the form
\tau_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V^{1+s}\times V_{h}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows

(5.5)

\tau_{F}(v,w_{h})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}\epsilon_{K,F}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},[w_{h}]\rangle_{F}.

For each boundary edge
F\in\cup_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}
, we simply define

(5.6)

\tau_{F}(v,w_{h})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},w_{h}\rangle_{F}.

For each edge

belonging to a bifurcation segment, namely
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}

and we define

\tau_{F}^{\gamma}(v,w_{h})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}\cap(\Omega_{i}\cup\Omega_{j})}\epsilon_{K,F}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}\rangle_{F}.

The extension of the form
a
can now be defined as
\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}V^{1+s}\times V_{h}^{p}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

(5.8)

\mathcal{A}(v,w_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\cdot\nabla w_{h}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\tau_{F}(v,w_{h})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau_{F}^{\gamma}(v,w_{h}).

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
v,w\in V_{h}^{p}
, the following identities hold

(5.9)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,\quad\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\tau_{F}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\}[w],

(5.10)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,\quad\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\tau_{F}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}\,w,

(5.11)

\displaystyle\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\quad\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau_{F}^{\gamma}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\left\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\right\}\odot[w].

Proof.

The proof of (
5.9
)-(
5.10
) is from
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
. We provide the similar proof for (
5.11
) for completeness. Since
v\in V_{h}^{p}
,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)
is smooth when restricted to a single element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
in
\Omega_{i}
. Using Green’s theorem in (
5.3
), we obtain that

(5.12)

\displaystyle\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(v_{i})|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K}\gamma_{0}(L_{F}^{K}([w_{h}]_{(i,j)}))

\displaystyle=\int_{F}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K}[w_{h}]_{(i,j)},

where the last equality holds since the trace of
L_{F}^{K}([w_{h}])
is nonzero only on
F
. Recalling that
\epsilon_{K,F}=(\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{F}
, we obtain that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}\cap(\Omega_{i}\cup\Omega_{j})}\epsilon_{K,F}\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}\rangle_{F}=\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\}_{(i,j)}[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}.

Summing the above over
(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}
, dividing by
\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})
and recalling the notation (
3.3
) yields the result.
∎

Similar to the proofs in
[

]
, we utilize the mollification operators constructed in

,
35
]
for any

:

(5.14)

\mathcal{K}_{\delta,i}^{\mathfrak{d}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{1}(\Omega_{i})^{2}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{i}})^{2},\quad\mathcal{K}_{\delta,i}^{\mathfrak{b}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}L^{1}(\Omega_{i})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{i}}),

which satisfy the commutativity property for all
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{i})^{2}
with
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{1}(\Omega_{i})

(5.15)

\nabla\cdot\mathcal{K}_{\delta,i}^{\mathfrak{d}}(\boldsymbol{\tau})=\mathcal{K}_{\delta,i}^{\mathfrak{b}}(\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\tau}).

The superscript

refers to the divergence whereas the superscript
\mathfrak{b}
in the operator
\mathcal{K}_{\delta,i}^{\mathfrak{b}}
refers to the broken space.
We extend these operators to the hypergraph by denoting
\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{b}}_{\delta}
and
\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{d}}_{\delta}
the operators such as:

\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{d}}_{\delta}(\boldsymbol{\tau})|_{\Omega_{i}}=\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{d}}_{\delta,i}(\boldsymbol{\tau}|_{\Omega_{i}}),\quad\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{b}}_{\delta}(w)|_{\Omega_{i}}=\mathcal{K}^{\mathfrak{b}}_{\delta,i}(w|_{\Omega_{i}}),\quad 1\leq i\leq N.

From

, we find that for
\boldsymbol{\tau}\in L^{\rho}(\mathcal{G})^{2}
and for any
w\in L^{2}(\mathcal{G})

(5.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\|\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\tau}-\boldsymbol{\tau}\|_{L^{\rho}(\mathcal{G})}=0,\quad\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\|\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{b}}(w)-w\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}=0.

In the following lemma, we assume that the weak formulation (
2.5
) satisfied by the weak solution
u
is also true for a weaker test space: see (

).

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose the weak solution
u
to (
2.5
) is such that
u\in V^{1+s}
and that it satisfies

(5.17)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i}),\nabla v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(f_{i},v_{i})_{\Omega_{i}},\quad\forall v\in W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}_{0}(\mathcal{G}).

Then, for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
and
w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
, we have

(5.18)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}\{\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))\}\odot[w_{h}].

Proof.

First, we show that for any
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
and
\phi\in C^{\infty}(F)
, the following holds

(5.19)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\phi=0.

To show (
5.19
), fix
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
and denote by
K_{i}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
the element that belongs to
\Omega_{i}
and shares the edge
F
. In other words,
K_{i}=\mathcal{T}_{F}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h,i}
. Since the trace of
L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)
is non zero only on
F
, we write

(5.20)

\displaystyle\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,\phi=\int_{\partial K_{i}}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi).

Since
\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)
is smooth in
\Omega_{i}
, we can apply Green’s theorem to obtain

(5.21)

\displaystyle\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,\phi=\int_{K_{i}}\nabla\cdot(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)+\int_{K_{i}}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))\cdot\nabla L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi).

Owing to the commutativity property (
5.15
), the convergence of the mollification operators (
5.16
) and the fact that
L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)\in W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(K_{i})
, we obtain

(5.22)

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,\phi=\int_{K_{i}}\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)+\int_{K_{i}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)\cdot\nabla L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)<\infty.

Define
L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)
as the extension by zero of
L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)
in
\Omega_{i}
. That is,
L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)=0
in
K
if
K\neq K_{i}
and
L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)=L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)
in
K_{i}
. Since
L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)|_{\partial K\backslash F}=0
, the jump of
L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)
evaluates to zero on the interior edges of
\Omega_{i}
. Therefore,
L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)\in W^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(\Omega_{i})
. Extending this to the hypergraph, we define
L_{F}^{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)
as follows

(5.23)

\displaystyle\forall 1\leq i\leq N,\quad L_{F}^{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)|_{\Omega_{i}}=\begin{cases}L_{F}^{\Omega_{i}}(\phi)&\;\;i\in I_{\gamma},\\
0&\;\;i\notin I_{\gamma}.\end{cases}

It is easy to see that
L_{F}^{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)\in W_{0}^{1,\rho^{\prime}}(\mathcal{G})
, see (
5.4
). Testing (
5.17
) with
L_{F}^{\mathcal{G}}(\phi)
, we obtain that

(5.24)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{K_{i}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(u_{i})\cdot\nabla L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)=\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{K_{i}}f_{i}\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi).

With the above identity, (
5.22
) yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,\phi=\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{K_{i}}\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi)+\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\int_{K_{i}}f_{i}\,L_{F}^{K_{i}}(\phi).

We then conclude and obtain (
5.19
) by noting that
-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)|_{\Omega_{i}}=f_{i}
in
L^{2}(\Omega_{i})
.

Next, we prove (
5.18
). The proof follows the argument of
Lemma

3.1
. Fix
w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
and fix
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}
. We note that for a fixed
i\in I_{\gamma}
and with the short-hand notation
m_{\gamma}=\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})
for readibility

(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}=\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}+\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}.

Integrating over
F
and taking the limit
\delta\rightarrow 0
, we have

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}\\
=\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}+\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}.

With (
5.19
), we write with the choice of
\phi=w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}=-\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{j}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}.

Therefore combining with the above, we have

(5.25)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{F}(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}\,w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}=\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\int_{F}\left((\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{j}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}.

Clearly we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\sum_{i\in I_{\gamma}}\sum_{j\in I_{\gamma},j\neq i}\int_{F}\left((\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{j}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\left((\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{i}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{i}-(\mathcal{K}_{\delta}^{\mathfrak{d}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u))|_{\Omega_{j}}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{j}\right)(w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}-w_{h}|_{\Omega_{j}}).

With this and summing (
5.25
) over
i\in I_{\gamma}
, we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.5

(Weak consistency)
.

Assume that the solution
u
to (

(5.26)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(u,w_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}w_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}},\quad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}.

Proof.

Following
[
19
]

\tau_{F}
and
\tau^{\gamma}_{F}

\displaystyle\tau_{F,\delta}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle\tau_{F,\delta}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}

\displaystyle\tau_{F}(u,w_{h})+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau^{\gamma}_{F}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\tau_{F,\delta}(u,w_{h})=

5.4

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\tau_{F}(u,w_{h})+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau^{\gamma}_{F}(u,w_{h})

K

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}\cup\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}}\tau_{F}(u,w_{h})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}\,w_{h}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(u)\cdot\nabla w_{h}.

Lemma 5.6

(Continuity of
\mathcal{A}
)
.

Let
v
be such that
v_{i}\in H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i})
and
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(v_{i})\in L^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
.
For any
w_{h}\in V_{h}^{p}
, the following bound holds

(5.29)

\mathcal{A}(v,w_{h})\lesssim\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}+h^{2s}|\nabla v|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{i})}^{2})+h^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\|w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}.

Proof.

We fix
v
such that
v_{i}\in H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i})
and
\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{i}(v_{i})\in L^{2}(\Omega_{i})
for
1\leq i\leq N
. For the first term in the definition of
\mathcal{A}
, we use Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality:

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h,i}}\int_{K}\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\cdot\nabla w_{h}\leq\kappa_{\ast}\|w_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2},

where
\kappa_{\ast}=\max_{i}\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{i})}
.
From
[
19
, Lemma 3.2]
(with
q=2,p=\rho=4/(2-2s)
), we have that for any polynomial function
\phi_{h}
defined on
F
and for any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}

(5.30)

|\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},\phi_{h}\rangle_{F}|\lesssim(h_{K}^{s}\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{\rho}(K)}+h_{K}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{2}(K)})h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

We now recall the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}(K)\hookrightarrow L^{\rho}(K)
with the correct scaling with respect to the element diameter
h_{K}
, see for e.g.
[
16
, Lemma 11.7]

\forall z\in H^{s}(K),\quad\|z\|_{L^{\rho}(K)}\lesssim h_{K}^{-s}\|z\|_{L^{2}(K)}+|z|_{H^{s}(K)}.

Since
\kappa
belongs to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and
\nabla v|_{\Omega_{i}}
belongs to
H^{s}(\Omega_{i})
for all
1\leq i\leq N
, we have
for any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

(5.31)

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{\rho}(K)}\lesssim h_{K}^{-s}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)}+|\nabla v|_{H^{s}(K)}.

Combining the two bounds above, we obtain that

(5.32)

|\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},\phi_{h}\rangle_{F}|\lesssim(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{K}^{s}|\nabla v|_{H^{s}(K)}+h_{K}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{2}(K)})\\
\times h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\phi_{h}\|_{L^{2}(F)}.

The above bound is critical to estimate the second and third terms in
\mathcal{A}(v,w_{h})
, see (
5.8
).
Let us give some details for the third term; the second term is handled in a similar manner.

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau^{\gamma}_{F}(v,w_{h})\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}\cap(\Omega_{i}\cup\Omega_{j})}|\langle(\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)|_{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{n}_{K})|_{F},[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}\rangle_{F}|.

Applying (
5.32
) with
\phi_{h}=[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}
, we have

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\tau^{\gamma}_{F}(v,w_{h})\lesssim\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{F}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\|[w_{h}]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
\times\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{F}\cap(\Omega_{i}\cup\Omega_{j})}(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2s}|\nabla v|_{H^{s}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2}\|\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2})\right)^{1/2},

which easily gives the desired upper bound.
∎

Theorem 5.7

(Error estimate)
.

Under the assumption of
Lemma

5.4
and the assumption that
\kappa
is piecewise constant, the following bound holds

(5.33)

\|u-u_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim h^{s}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{i})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{I}_{h}u\in V_{h}^{1}
be a quasi interpolant of the exact solution
u
defined locally on each
\Omega_{i}
,
\mathcal{I}_{h}u|_{\Omega_{i}}=\mathcal{I}_{h,i}u|_{\Omega_{i}}
where
\mathcal{I}_{h,i}
can be chosen as the Scott–Zhang interpolation operator defined over
\Omega_{i}
.
Define
\chi_{h}=u_{h}-\mathcal{I}_{h}u
.
We use the coercivity property of
a
:

(5.34)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim a(\chi_{h},\chi_{h})=a(u_{h},\chi_{h})-a(\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{i}}f_{i}\chi_{h}|_{\Omega_{i}}-a(\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h}).

Using
Lemma

5.5
, we obtain

(5.35)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{DG}}\lesssim\mathcal{A}(u,\chi_{h})-a(\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h}).

We now rewrite the second term above using
Lemma

5.3
, the fact that the jump of
I_{h}u
vanishes on any edge
F\in\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\mathrm{int}}
and the fact that
I_{h}u
is zero on the hypergraph boundaries
\mathcal{F}_{h,i}^{\partial\mathcal{G}}
. In particular, it is easy to see that

(5.36)

\displaystyle a(\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h})=\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h})-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\chi_{h})\}\odot[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\odot[\chi_{h}].

Therefore, we obtain that

(5.37)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{h}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{DG}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathcal{A}(u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h})+\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\int_{F}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\chi_{h})\}\odot[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]-\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{\eta_{\gamma}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}[\mathcal{I}_{h}u]\odot[\chi_{h}]

\displaystyle=T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{3}.

We proceed by bounding the terms on the right hand side of (
5.37
) separately. For the first term, we use
Lemma

5.6
followed by approximation properties of
\mathcal{I}_{h}

[
36
,
12
]
and the fact that
\kappa
is piecewise constant, thus implying that
\nabla\cdot\kappa(\nabla I_{h}u)
vanishes on each element:

\mathcal{A}(u-\mathcal{I}_{h}u,\chi_{h})\leq\left(h^{s}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i})}^{2})^{1/2}+h\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{G})}\right)\|\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}.

Next we bound
T_{2}
by using Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality and the fact that
u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{G})
:

\displaystyle T_{2}=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{card}(I_{\gamma})}\int_{F}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\chi_{h})\}_{(i,j)}\,[\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u]_{(i,j)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}h_{F}\|\{\boldsymbol{\sigma}(\chi_{h})\}_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sum_{F\in\mathcal{F}_{h}^{\gamma}}\sum_{(i,j)\in D_{\gamma}}\frac{1}{h_{F}}\|[\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u]_{(i,j)}\|_{L^{2}(F)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Clearly by a discrete trace inequality, the first factor is bounded above by
\|\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}
. For the second factor, we expand the jump across an edge
F
shared by two elements that belong to
\Omega_{i}
and
\Omega_{j}
and utilize the following trace inequality on the neighboring elements
K
:

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}(F)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{-1/2}\|\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{F}^{s}|\mathcal{I}_{h}u-u|_{H^{1/2+s}(K)}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{F}^{1/2+s}|u|_{H^{1+s}(K)}.

We then have:

T_{2}\lesssim h^{s}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|u|_{H^{1+s}(\Omega_{i})}^{2})^{1/2}\|\chi_{h}\|_{\mathrm{DG}}.

We follow a similar argument to obtain the same bound for
T_{3}
and we skip the details for brevity.
∎

7.
Conclusions

This work addresses the numerical analysis of discontinuous polynomial approximations of elliptic problems on hypergraphs made of either segments or of planar surfaces. Using a manipulation of the Kirchoff conditions and a new definition of averages and jumps, the terms handling the bifurcation nodes are seamlessly written in the DG form. A priori error bounds are derived; they require technical functional analysis tools for the low regularity case. Convergence is verified for several numerical examples. This work is motivated by the mixed dimensional problem of PDEs valid in three-dimensional domains that are coupled to PDEs valid on hypergraphs; the numerical analysis of these problems is future work.

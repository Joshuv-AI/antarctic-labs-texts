Approximation by Neural Network operators in L p L^{p} spaces associated with an arbitrary measure
Nitin Bartwal, A. Sathish Kumar

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the approximation behavior of both one and multidimensional neural network type operators for functions in L p ​ ( I d , ρ ) L^{p}(I^{d},\rho) , where 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty , associated with a general measure ρ \rho defined over a hypercube. First, we prove the uniform approximation for a continuous function and the L p L^{p} approximation theorem by the NN operators in one and multidimensional settings. In addition, we also obtain the L p L^{p} error bounds in terms of 𝒦 \mathcal{K} -functionals for these neural network operators. Finally, we consider the logistic and tangent hyperbolic activation functions and verify the hypothesis of the theorems. We also show the implementation of continuous and integrable functions by NN operators with respect to the Lebesgue and Jacobi measures defined on [ 0 , 1 ] × [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1]\times[0,1] with logistic and tangent hyperbolic activation functions.



1.
Introduction

A neural network is a mathematical framework modeled after the structural and functional principles of the human brain, aiming to replicate the cognitive processes by which humans interpret information and learn from past interactions. This learning mechanism is implemented through multiple layers of interconnected units—referred to as neurons—that process input data using successive applications of affine transformations followed by nonlinear activation functions.
Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, the feed-forward neural network (FNNs) with one hidden layer is defined by

\displaystyle N_{n}(x)=\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{n}c_{\ell}\sigma(\langle\alpha_{\ell}.x\rangle+\beta_{\ell}),

where
0\leq\ell\leq n,

\beta_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
are thresholds and
\alpha_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
are connection weights and
c_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
are the coefficients. It is well known that FNNs with one hidden layer and non-polynomial activation function can approximate any continuous function uniformly on compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if given a sufficient number of neurons
[
24
]
. Further, the approximation of measurable functions by these neural networks was analysed in
[
26
]
. In
[
12
]
, Cardaliaguet and Euvrard analyzed the approximation properties of both functions and their derivatives using the feed forward neural network. Inspired by this work, Anastassiou studied the approximation of continuous functions and their rate of convergence of neural network operators in
[
4
]
. Further, he analyzed the approximation behavior NN operators using different activation functions in one and multidimensional settings, see
[
5
,
6
,
1
,
2
,
3
]
and the references therein.
Furthermore, the point-wise, uniform convergence results and the order of convergence of the NN operators were proved by Costarelli and Spigler in
[
18
,
19
]
. The approximation behavior of Kantorovich neural network operators were analyzed in different settings, see
[
20
,
22
,
23
,
21
]
and the references therein. Approximation by NN-operators have been studied widely by several authors in different directions, see
[
7
,
8
,
27
,
29
,
15
,
17
,
13
,
14
]
and the references therein. Recently, Costarelli
[
16
]
estimated the approximation error for the NN operators in terms of the averaged modulus of smoothness in the settings of the
L^{p}
spaces corresponding to the Lebesgue measure. We extend the study of the approximation properties of NN operators in
L^{p}
spaces, where
1\leq p\leq\infty
, associated with a general measure. This measure is defined on a
d
-dimensional hypercube and assumed to satisfy a support condition. Weighted Lebesgue spaces are particular instances within this broader class of function spaces and can be used in image analysis.

More specifically, an image can be represented as an element of a weighted Lebesgue space, which provides a functional analytic framework for image analysis. Formally, a grayscale image can be viewed as a measurable function

f:\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},

where
\Omega
is the domain of the image and
f(x,y)
gives the pixel intensity at point
(x,y).
In a weighted space, the function
f(x,y)
is equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}}:=\int_{\Omega}|f(x,y)|^{p}\,w(x,y)dx\,dy,

where
w(x,y)
is a positive weight function. The weight alters the contribution of different regions of the image to the overall measurement, allowing one to emphasize or de-emphasize specific areas. This is useful in applications such as feature detection, where regions of interest may be prioritized, or in noise modeling, where uncertain areas can be down-weighted. Thus, treating an image as an element of a weighted Lebesgue space not only embeds it in a rigorous mathematical structure but also provides flexibility for adapting analysis to the characteristics of the image.

Due to the importance of weighted norm spaces in image analysis, we study the approximation properties of the NN type operators for functions belonging to
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
, where
\rho
is any measure on the hypercube satisfying some support condition. This work is inspired by the work of Berdysheva and her collaborators. Berdysheva and Jetter
[
9

initiated the study of Bernstein-Durrmeyer operator with respect to arbitrary measure on
d
-dimensional simplex
S^{d}.
Further, she proved the uniform convergence of these operators for continuous functions by assuming the strict positivity of measure
\rho

[
10
]
. Furthermore, by relaxing the conditions on the support of measure
\rho

[
11
]
. Before analyzing the convergence behavior of the neural network operators with respect to an arbitrary measure on
I^{d},
we recall the following notations and basic definitions.

1.1.
Notations and Preliminaries

We consider the following notations and preliminaries which shall be used throughout this paper.

Let
I^{d}:=[0,1]^{d}:=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}):0\leq x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}\leq 1\}
be the hypercube of the dimension
d
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\beta
be a multi-index such that

\beta=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}),\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \frac{\beta}{n}=\left(\frac{k_{1}}{n},\frac{k_{2}}{n},\ldots,\frac{k_{d}}{n}\right),

where
0\leq k_{i}\leq n
for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}.

Now, we state the following definitions:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta>0.
We define the set

A_{\delta}(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(x_{i}-\delta,x_{i}+\delta\right)

as an
open
\delta
hypercube about the point
x.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\delta>0.
We define the set

B_{\delta}(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\left(x_{i},x_{i}+\delta\right)

as an
open right sided
\delta
hypercube about the point
x.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a bounded Borel measure
\rho
is said to be strictly positive on
I^{d}
if

\rho(A\cap I^{d})>0

for every open set of
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
A\cap I^{d}\neq\emptyset.

Now, we define
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
space. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We denote by
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
the space of all real-valued measurable functions on
I^{d}
such that

\int_{I^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}d\rho(x)<\infty.

The corresponding norm on
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}:=\left(\int_{I^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}d\rho(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The space
L^{\infty}(I^{d},\rho)
is the set of all essentially bounded functions on the hypercube
I^{d}.
The corresponding norm on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is given by

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I^{d},\rho)}:=\displaystyle{{\text{ess}\sup}_{x\in I^{d}}}|f(x)|.

We denote by
C(I^{d})
the space of all continuous functions on
I^{d}
and their norm is defined by

\|f\|_{\infty}:=\displaystyle{\sup_{x\in I^{d}}}|f(x)|.

Now, we recall some basic definitions and properties of sigmoidal function
\sigma.

Definition 1.4
.

A sigmoidal function
\sigma
is a measurable function with

\lim_{x\to-\infty}\sigma(x)=0\,\,\mbox{and}\lim_{x\to+\infty}\sigma(x)=1.

Throughout this article
\sigma
is assumed to be a non-decreasing function satisfying the following assumptions unless stated otherwise:

(A_{1})

\sigma(x)-\frac{1}{2}
is an odd function.

(A_{2})

\sigma\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is concave for
x\in\mathbb{R}.

(A_{3})

\sigma(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\beta})
as
x\to\infty
for some
\beta>0.

Definition 1.5
.

For the sigmoidal function
\sigma,
we define the density or kernel function
\phi_{\sigma}
as follows:

\displaystyle\phi_{\sigma}(x):=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma(x+1)-\sigma(x-1)).

(1.1)

We now list out some well known properties of the kernel
\phi_{\sigma}
that will be used throughout this article. For more details and proofs of these properties one can refer to
[
18
]
.

(1)

\phi_{\sigma}(x)
is a non negative function.

(2)

\phi_{\sigma}(x)
is non decreasing for
x<0
and non increasing for
x\geq 0.

(3)

\phi_{\sigma}(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\beta})
as
x\to\pm\infty.

(4)

For every
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi_{\sigma}(x-k)=1.

(5)

Let
x\in I
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, we have ‘

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)\geq\phi_{\sigma}(1)>0.

Definition 1.6
.

The
r^{th}
order discrete absolute moment of
\phi_{\sigma}(x)
is defined as

M_{r}(\phi_{\sigma}):=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-k|^{r}\phi_{\sigma}(x-k).

Under the assumption
(A_{3})
on
\sigma
(see
[
18
]
), we have

M_{r}(\phi_{\sigma})<+\infty,\ \mbox{for}\ 0\leq r<\beta-1.

In order to get quantitative estimates for the rate of convergence of
L^{p}
approximation, we employ the
\mathcal{K-}
functionals.

Definition 1.7
.

The
\mathcal{K}-
functional for a function
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{K}(f,t)_{p}:=\inf_{g\in W^{1,\infty}}\{\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+t\|g\|_{1,\infty}\},

where the associated Sobolev space
W^{p,\infty}(I^{d})
is defined by

W^{1,\infty}:=\left\{g:g,\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\in L^{\infty}(I^{d})\,\,\text{and}\,\,\left\|\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{\infty}<\infty,\,\,1\leq j\leq d\right\},

and
\|g\|_{1,\infty}
is a semi-norm on
W^{1,\infty}(I^{d})
, and is given by

\|g\|_{1,\infty}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\frac{\partial g}{\partial x_{j}}\right\|_{\infty}.

It is important to note that the derivatives here are considered in the weak sense.

Let
\Phi:I^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

\Phi_{\sigma}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d}):=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(x_{i}),

where
\phi_{\sigma}
is the usual kernel defined in (
1.1
). Now, we define multivariate NN operator with respect to the measure
\rho
for
f:I^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
f
is some suitable function which depends on the space under consideration.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
\rho
be a non negative bounded Borel measure on
I^{d}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. For
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho),
the multivariate Neural Network operators with respect to the measure
\rho
is defined by

S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)=\displaystyle{\dfrac{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}c_{n,\beta}\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}},

(1.2)

where the coefficient
c_{n,\beta}
is given by

c_{n,\beta}:=\dfrac{\int_{I^{d}}f(t)\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}.

It is easy to see that the operator (
1.2
) is well defined for all
f\in L^{\infty}(I^{d}).
Indeed, we have

|S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)|\leq\max_{\frac{\beta}{n}\in I^{d}}|c_{n,\beta}|\leq\dfrac{\int_{I^{d}}|f(t)|\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}.

This paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the univariate version of the operator (
1.2
) and show that it converges uniformly for all continuous functions on
I.
In addition, we also prove that the family of the operators
\{S_{n}^{\rho}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(I,\rho)
, and using the denseness of continuous function, we obtain the
L^{p}(I,\rho)
norm convergence of the operator. Further, we also get
L^{p}
error bounds for the operator in terms of
\mathcal{K}
-functionals. In Section
3
, we extend the approximation results of section
2
to the multidimensional setting, by taking neural network operator defined on a hypercube. In Section
4.1
, we focus on some specific sigmoidal functions and verify the assumptions of the theorems to validate the proposed theory. Further, we approximate the particular continuous and integrable functions by NN operators with respect to the Lebesgue and Jacobi measures defined on
[0,1]\times[0,1]
with logistic and tangent hyperbolic activation functions.

2.
Univariate Neural Network operators with respect to arbitrary measures

In this section, we consider the univariate version of the operator (
1.2
), and we derive the uniform approximation and
L^{p}
error bounds in terms of
\mathcal{K}
-functionals. These results will be used to get the approximation results of multidimensional Neural Network operators. We denote
[0,1]
by
I.

Before delving into the analysis, we briefly recall some basic definitions and results which will be useful to derive the uniform convergence of univariate Neural Network operators.

Definition 2.1
.

For
f\in C(I),
the neural network operator
F_{n}
is defined as follows:

F_{n}f(x):=\dfrac{\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)},\quad\quad x\in I.

We recall the following convergence result from
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}f(x)=f(x)

at any point
x\in[a,b]
of continuity of
f.
Moreover, if
f
is continuous on
[a,b]
then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}f-f\|_{\infty}=0.

Now we define the univariate version of the operator (
1.2
) and discuss their approximation properties.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\rho
be a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I
. We define the neural network operator
S^{\rho}_{n}
with respect to the measure
\rho
for
f\in C(I)
as follows:

S^{\rho}_{n}f(x):=\dfrac{\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}{c_{n,k}\,\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}}{\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)},\quad\quad x\in I,

where the coefficient
c_{n,k}
is given by

c_{n,k}:=\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}.

It is easy to see that
S^{\rho}_{n}
is a positive linear operator, and it reproduces the constant functions.

First, we prove the following lemma which will be useful in proving the uniform approximation of the continuous function on
I
using a univariate NN operator.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\delta>0.
Suppose that
\rho
is a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I.
Then, we have

\rho\left(\left[\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}\right]\right)>0,

for
k=0,1\ldots,n
and
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
\eta<\frac{\delta^{2}}{2}.
Due to compactness of
I,
we get
a finitely many points
\{x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{n_{0}}\}\subset I
such that

I\displaystyle{\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{0}}}(x_{i}-\eta,x_{i}+\eta).

Using the strictly positivity of bounded Borel measure
\rho
on
I,
we have

\rho(x_{i}-\eta,x_{i}+\eta)>0,

\forall i\in\{0,1\ldots,n_{0}\}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle{\min_{i=0,1\ldots,n_{0}}}\rho(x_{i}-\eta,x_{i}+\eta)>0.

We note that every interval
[\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}]
contains at least one of the interval of the form
(x_{i}-\eta,x_{i}+\eta)
for some
i.

Thus, we have

\rho\left(\left[\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}\right]\right)>\displaystyle{\min_{i=0,1\ldots,n_{0}}}\rho(x_{i}-\eta,x_{i}+\eta)>0,

\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=0,1,\ldots,n
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we prove the uniform convergence of the operator
S^{\rho}_{n}
for continuous functions on
I.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\rho
be a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I
. Suppose that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=0

for
0<\delta<1.

Then for every
f\in C(I)
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{\infty}=0.

Proof.

For all
x\in I
, we have

\displaystyle|S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)-F_{n}f(x|+|F_{n}f(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

By Theorem
2.2
, we have
I_{2}\to 0
uniformly as
n\to\infty.
So we only need to estimate the term
I_{1}.
We have

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\left|c_{n,k}-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|\,\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{k=0,1,\ldots,n}\left|c_{n,k}-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|.

it is enough to show that
\max_{k=0,1,\ldots,n}\left|c_{n,k}-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|\to 0
as
n\to\infty.

Since
f
is continuous on
I,
so for
\forall\,\epsilon>0,

\exists\,\delta>0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|<\epsilon
whenever
|x-y|<\delta
. For any
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|c_{n,k}-f\left(\frac{k}{n}{}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)}}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}}}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\right|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}.

Using the uniform continuity and boundedness of
f
in
I
, we get

\displaystyle\left|c_{n,k}-f\left(\frac{k}{n}{}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+2M\dfrac{\displaystyle{\int_{(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}}}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+2M\,\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)\}}\ \ \dfrac{\rho((\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c})}{\rho([\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}])}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)\}}\ \ \dfrac{\rho(I)}{\rho([\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}])}.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\rho([\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2}])>0.
Further, by the hypothesis of the theorem, we have

\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}\to 0\quad\text{as}\,\,n\to\infty,

for
0<\delta<1.
Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

In the following lemma, we show the boundedness of NN operator
S_{n}^{\rho}
for functions in
L^{p}(I,\rho).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for
f\in L^{p}(I,\rho)
, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p}(I,\rho)
. Then, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}f\|^{p}_{p}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}\right|^{p}d\rho(x).

Using the Jensen’s inequality and the Holder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f\|^{p}_{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}}\left(\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\left(\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}\right)^{p}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}\right)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\phi_{\sigma}(1)}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\dfrac{\left(\int_{0}^{1}f(t)\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)^{p}}{\left(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\phi_{\sigma}(1)}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\dfrac{\left(\int_{0}^{1}|f(t)|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}}{\left(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\phi_{\sigma}(1)}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}|f(t)|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}^{p}.

Thus, the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty.
If
g\in C(I)
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.5
, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{\infty}<\epsilon,

for large
n\in\mathbb{N}.

We also have

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|S^{\rho}_{n}g(x)-g(x)|^{p}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{\infty}\,\rho(I)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon\,\rho(I).

Since
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain the desired approximation.
∎

Now, we prove the
L^{p}(I,\rho)
convergence of
S^{\rho}_{n}.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty.
For
f\in L^{p}(I,\rho),
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}=0.

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequality and the lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-S^{\rho}_{n}g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.

Since
C(I)
is dense
L^{p}(I,\rho),
so for
f\in L^{p}(I,\rho)
, there exists a function
g\in C(I)
such that

\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}<\epsilon.

Further, using Lemma
2.7
, we get the desired result.
∎

In the following theorem, we estimate the error in the approximation in terms of
\mathcal{K}-
functional.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Suppose that
M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})<\infty.
Then for
f\in L^{p}(I,\rho)
, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}\leq C\,\mathcal{K}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right).

Proof.

We know that
S^{\rho}_{n}(1)=1
and
\|S^{\rho}_{n}\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}=1
.
For any
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
, we get

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-S^{\rho}_{n}g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.

(2.1)

Now we estimate
\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}
for
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
.
For all
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
, we have

\displaystyle|g(t)-g(x)|\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}|t-x|,\ \ \forall\,x,\,t\in I.

(2.2)

Since
S^{\rho}_{n}
is a positive linear operator, and it reproduces the constant, so we get

|S^{\rho}_{n}g(x)-g(x)|=|S^{\rho}_{n}(g(t)-g(x))(x)|\leq S^{\rho}_{n}(|g(t)-g(x)|)(x)\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}S^{\rho}_{n}(|t-x|)(x),

for
x\in I.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}(|t-x|)\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.

(2.3)

Now we estimate
S^{\rho}_{n}(|t-x|)(x).

\displaystyle S^{\rho}_{n}(|t-x|)(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-x|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-\frac{k}{n}|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|\frac{k}{n}-x|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-\frac{k}{n}|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}

\displaystyle+\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Taking the
L^{p}(I,\rho)
-norm on both sides of the above expression, we get

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}(|t-x|)\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}\leq\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}+\|I_{2}\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.

(2.4)

We first estimate
\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}
. Using Jensen’s inequality twice, we obtain

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-\frac{k}{n}|\,|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)|d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}\right)^{p}}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left(\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-\frac{k}{n}|\,|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)|d\rho(t)}{\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)}\right)^{p}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dfrac{\int_{0}^{1}|t-\frac{k}{n}|^{p}\,|\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)|d\rho(t)}{\left(\int_{0}^{1}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)\right)}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}}d\rho(x)\quad

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{p}\,\phi_{\sigma}(1)}\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}|nt-k|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)d\rho(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{p}\,\phi_{\sigma}(1)}M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})\,\rho(I)=\frac{C}{n^{p}}.

(2.5)

Similarly, we estimate
\|I_{2}\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}.
Again using Jensen’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}\right)^{p}}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|^{p}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)}\right)}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\phi_{\sigma}(1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k}{n}-x\right|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nx-k)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{n^{p}\,\phi_{\sigma}(1)}M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})\,\rho(I)=\frac{C}{n^{p}}.

(2.6)

On combining the estimates (
2.3
)-(
2.6
), we obtain

\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I,\rho)}\leq C\,\|g\|_{1,\infty}\frac{1}{n}.

Substituting (
2
) in (
2.1
), and taking the infimum over all
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I)
, we get the desired result.
∎

3.
Multivariate Neural Network operators with respect to arbitrary measures

In this section, we analyze the approximation properties of multivariate neural network operators with respect to arbitrary measures. In particular, we derive the uniform approximation and the error bounds in terms of
\mathcal{K}
-functionals. Before proving these results, we recall the following multivariate neural network operator.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f:I^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded function, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the multivariate neural network operator
F_{n}
is defined as follows (see
[
19
]
):

F_{n}f(x):\displaystyle{\dfrac{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}},\quad x\in I^{d}

where
\beta
is a multi-index such that
\beta=(k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}),
and
\frac{\beta}{n}=\left(\frac{k_{1}}{n},\frac{k_{2}}{n},\ldots,\frac{k_{d}}{n}\right)
.

First, we recall the following theorem from
[
19
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
f:I^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded. If
f
is continuous at
x
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}f(x)=f(x).

Further, if
f\in C(I^{d})
, then we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|F_{n}f-f\|_{\infty}=0.

Using strict positivity of
\rho
, we immediately have the following lemma. This lemma will be useful to derive the uniform convergence of the operator
S_{n}^{\rho}.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\delta>0
and
\rho
be a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I^{d}
. Then, we have

\rho\left(B_{\delta}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right)>0,

where
\dfrac{\beta}{n}=\left(\dfrac{k_{1}}{n},\dfrac{k_{2}}{n},\ldots,\dfrac{k_{d}}{n}\right)
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq k_{i}\leq n.

Proof.

Suppose
\eta<\frac{\delta}{2}.
Due to compactness of
I^{d},
we get finitely many points

\{x_{1},x_{2}\ldots,x_{n_{0}}\}\subset I^{d}
such that

I^{d}\displaystyle{\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n_{0}}}A_{\eta}(x_{i}).

Since
\rho
is a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I^{d},
hence
\rho(A_{\eta}(x_{i}))>0,

\forall i\in\{0,1\ldots,n_{0}\}
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle{\min_{i=0,1\ldots,n_{0}}}\rho(A_{\eta}(x_{i}))>0.

Note that every one sided hypercube of the form
B_{\delta}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)
contains at least one of the hypercube of the form
A_{\eta}(x_{i})
for some
i,

so we obtain

\rho\left(B_{\delta}\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right)>\displaystyle{\min_{i=0,1\ldots,n_{0}}}\rho(A_{\eta}(x_{i}))>0,

for all
\dfrac{\beta}{n}\in I^{d},
where
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, we prove the uniform convergence of the NN operator
S_{n}^{\rho}
for the functions in
C(I^{d})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\rho
be a strictly positive bounded Borel measure on
I^{d}
. If

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=0,

(3.1)

for
0<\delta<1,
then for
f\in C(I^{d})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{\infty}=0.

Proof.

For all
x\in I^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle|S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|S^{\rho}_{n}f(x)-F_{n}f(x|+|F_{n}f(x)-f(x)|

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

Using Theorem
3.2
, we have
I_{2}\to 0
uniformly as
n\to\infty
. We now estimate the term
I_{1}.

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\displaystyle{\dfrac{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|c_{n,\beta}-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\Phi_{\sigma}(nx_{1}-k_{1},nx_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nx_{d}-k_{d})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{\frac{\beta}{n}\in I^{d}}\left|c_{n,\beta}-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|.

So it is enough to show that
\left|c_{n,\beta}-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|\to 0
as
n\to\infty.

Since
f
is uniformly continuous on
I^{d},
for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|<\epsilon
whenever
\displaystyle x,\,y\in A_{\delta}(\beta).
We have

\displaystyle\left|c_{n,\beta}-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\int_{A_{\delta}(\beta)}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\int_{(A_{\delta}(\beta))^{c}}\left|f(t)-f\left(\frac{\beta}{n}\right)\right|\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+2M\,\dfrac{\int_{(A_{\delta}(\beta))^{c}}\,\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{\overline{B_{\delta^{2}}(\beta)}}\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})\,d\rho(t)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon+2M\,\dfrac{\max\{\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d}):(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})\in(A_{\delta}(\beta))^{c}\}}{\min\{\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d}):(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})\in\overline{B_{\delta^{2}}(\beta)}\}}

\displaystyle\times\dfrac{\rho(I^{d})}{\rho(\overline{B_{\delta^{2}}(\beta)})}.

Now using the Lemma
3.3
and noting that

\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d})=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{i}-k_{i}),

we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\max\{\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d}):(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})\in(A_{\delta}(\beta))^{c}\}}{\min\{\Phi_{\sigma}(nt_{1}-k_{1},nt_{2}-k_{2},\ldots,nt_{d}-k_{d}):(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{d})\in\overline{B_{\delta^{2}}(\beta)}\}}=0.

This completes the proof.
∎

In the following theorem, we show the boundedness of NN operator
S_{n}^{\rho}
for functions in
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Then for
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
. Then, using Jensen’s and Holder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|S_{n}^{\rho}f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{I^{d}}\left|\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}f(t)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}\right|^{p}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{\int_{I^{d}}f(t)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\frac{\left(\int_{I^{d}}f(t)\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)^{p}}{\left(\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\frac{\left(\int_{I^{d}}|f(t)|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)}{\left(\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)^{p-1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\int_{I^{d}}|f(t)|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}.

Hence, the proof is completed.
∎

Next, we obtain the error bounds in terms of
\mathcal{K}
-functionals.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
. Suppose that
M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})<\infty.
Then for
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
, we have

\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}\leq C\,\mathcal{K}\left(f,\frac{1}{n}\right).

Proof.

We know that
S^{\rho}_{n}(1)=1
and
\|S^{\rho}_{n}\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}=1
.
For any
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{d})
, we get

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-S^{\rho}_{n}g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}.

(3.2)

Now we estimate
\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}
for
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{d})
.
For all
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle|g(t)-g(x)|\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}|t_{i}-x_{i}|,\,\,\forall\,x,\,t\in I^{d}.

(3.3)

Since
S^{\rho}_{n}
is a positive linear operator, and it reproduces the constant functions so we get

|S^{\rho}_{n}g(x)-g(x)|=|S^{\rho}_{n}(g(t)-g(x))(x)|\leq S^{\rho}_{n}(|g(t)-g(x)|)(x)\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}S^{\rho}_{n}(|t_{i}-x_{i}|)(x).

Taking
L^{p}
norm on both the sides, we get

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}\leq\|g\|_{1,\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|S^{\rho}_{n}(|\pi_{i}(t)-\pi_{i}(x)|)\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)},

(3.4)

where
\pi_{i}:I^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the projection on the
i^{th}
coordinate. Now, we estimate
S^{\rho}_{n}(|\pi_{i}(t)-\pi_{i}(x)|).

Let
i=1,2\ldots,d.
Then, we have

\displaystyle S^{\rho}_{n}(|\pi_{i}(t)-\pi_{i}(x)|)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}|\pi_{i}(t)-\pi_{i}(x)|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\frac{\left(\int_{I^{d}}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

Taking
L^{p}
norm on the both sides of the expression, we have

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}(|\pi_{i}(t)-\pi_{i}(x)|)\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}\leq\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|I_{2}\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}.

(3.5)

Now we estimate
I_{1}
and
I_{2}.
Using Jensen’s inequality twice, we obtain

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle=\int_{I^{d}}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|\frac{\left(\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})\right)}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}\right)^{p}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{I^{d}}\left(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{i}-k_{1})\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{p}}\int_{I^{d}}\left(\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{n}\left|\frac{k_{i}}{n}-\pi_{i}(x)\right|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{i}-k_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\frac{1}{n^{p}}M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})\int_{I^{d}}\left(\prod_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{d}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})\frac{\rho(I^{d})}{n^{p}}=\frac{C}{n^{p}}.

(3.6)

Similarly, we estimate
\|I_{1}\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}
as follows. Again using Jensen’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I^{d}}\left|\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\times\left.\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}\right|^{p}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\int_{I^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}d\rho(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\frac{\int_{I^{d}}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)}{\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{j}-k_{j})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\int_{I^{d}}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|^{p}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\,d\rho(t)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\sum_{k_{i}=0}^{n}\left|\pi_{i}(t)-\frac{k_{i}}{n}\right|^{p}\phi_{\sigma}(nx_{i}-k_{i})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})}{n^{p}(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{n}\ldots\sum_{k_{d}=0}^{n}\left(\prod_{j=1,j\neq i}^{d}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\frac{1}{n^{p}}\int_{I^{d}}\left(\prod_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{d}\sum_{k_{j}=0}^{n}\phi_{\sigma}(nt_{j}-k_{j})\right)d\rho(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})}{(\phi_{\sigma}(1))^{d}}\frac{\rho(I^{d})}{n^{p}}=\frac{C}{n^{p}}.

(3.7)

On combining (
3.4
)-(
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}\leq C\,d\,\|g\|_{1,\infty}\frac{1}{n}.

(3.8)

Using (
3.8
) in (
3.2
), and taking the infimum over
g\in W^{1,\infty}(I^{d})
, we get the desired result.
∎

Now, we prove the
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
convergence of
S^{\rho}_{n}.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty.
For
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho),
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
, it is easy to see that for all
g\in C(I^{d})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}=0.

(3.9)

Applying the triangle inequality, and Lemma
3.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-f\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|S^{\rho}_{n}f-S^{\rho}_{n}g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}+\|S^{\rho}_{n}g-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}.

Since
C(I^{d})
is dense
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho),
so for
f\in L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
, there exists a function
g\in C(I^{d})
such that

\|f-g\|_{L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)}<\epsilon.

(3.10)

On combining (
3.9
)-(
3.10
), we get the desired result.
∎

4.
Examples of activation functions and Implementation Results

4.1.
Examples of activation functions

In this section, we take some particular activation functions and verify the assumption of the theorems for one and multidimensional NN operators. As a first example, we consider the following logistic function:

\sigma(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-x}},\,x\in\mathbb{R}.

Example 4.1
.

Let
\sigma(x)=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-x}}.
Using (
1.1
), we can write

\phi_{\sigma}(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(x+1)}}-\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(x-1)}}\right).

It is easy to see that
\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)
is a positive function which increases till
\dfrac{k}{n}
, and starts decreasing and symmetric about the point
\dfrac{k}{n}
. Hence, we get

\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}-\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(n\delta-1)}}}{\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}-\dfrac{1}{1+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}}}.

Simplifying the RHS of the above expression, we obtain

RHS

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\dfrac{\dfrac{e^{-(n\delta-1)}-e^{-(n\delta+1)}}{1+e^{-(2n\delta)}+e^{-(n\delta-1)}+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}}{\dfrac{e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}-e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}{1+e^{-(2n\delta^{2})}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\dfrac{e^{-(n\delta-1)}-e^{-(n\delta+1)}}{e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}-e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}\right)\times\left(\dfrac{1+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}{1+e^{-2n\delta}+e^{-(n\delta-1)}+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{-(n\delta)}}{e^{-(n\delta^{2})}}\times\left(\dfrac{1+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}{1+e^{-2n\delta}+e^{-(n\delta-1)}+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{n(\delta^{2}-\delta)}\times\left(\dfrac{1+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}{1+e^{-2n\delta}+e^{-(n\delta-1)}+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}\right).

Since
0<\delta<1
, we have
\delta^{2}-\delta<0.
Therefore, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=0,

where
0<\delta<1.
This verifies the condition of the Theorem
2.5
. Further, it is easy to see that
M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})
is finite for
1\leq p<\infty
and hence conditions on Theorem
2.9
is verified.

As a second example, we consider the tangent hyperbolic function

\sigma(x)=\dfrac{e^{x}-e^{-x}}{e^{x}+e^{-x}}.

Example 4.2
.

Let
\sigma(x)=\dfrac{e^{x}-e^{-x}}{e^{x}+e^{-x}}.
Again, using (
1.1
), we can write

\phi_{\sigma}(x)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{e^{x+1}-e^{-(x+1)}}{e^{(x+1)}+e^{-(x+1)}}-\dfrac{e^{(x-1)}-e^{-((x-1))}}{e^{(x-1)}+e^{-(x-1)}}\right).

We note that
\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k)
is a positive function which increases till the point
\dfrac{k}{n}
, and symmetric about the point
\dfrac{k}{n}.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=\dfrac{\dfrac{e^{n\delta+1}-e^{-(n\delta+1)}}{e^{n\delta+1}+e^{-(n\delta+1)}}-\dfrac{e^{n\delta-1}-e^{-(n\delta-1)}}{e^{n\delta-1}+e^{-(n\delta-1)}}}{\dfrac{e^{n\delta^{2}+1}-e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}{e^{n\delta^{2}+1}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}+1)}}-\dfrac{e^{n\delta^{2}-1}-e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}}{e^{n\delta^{2}-1}+e^{-(n\delta^{2}-1)}}}

Simplifying the above expression, we have

RHS

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{2n\delta}+e^{2}-e^{-2}-e^{-2n\delta}-e^{-2n\delta}-e^{-2}+e^{2}+e^{-2n\delta}}{e^{2n\delta}+e^{2}+e^{-2}+e^{-2n\delta}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2n\delta^{2}}+e^{2}+e^{-2}+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}}{e^{2n\delta^{2}}+e^{2}-e^{-2}-e^{-2n\delta^{2}}-e^{-2n\delta^{2}}-e^{-2}+e^{2}+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{e^{2}+e^{-2}+e^{2n\delta^{2}}+e^{-2n\delta^{2}}}{e^{2}+e^{-2}+e^{2n\delta}+e^{-2n\delta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2n\delta^{2}}}{e^{2n\delta}}\times\left(\dfrac{e^{2-2n\delta^{2}}+e^{-2-2n\delta^{2}}+1+e^{-4n\delta^{2}}}{e^{2-2n\delta}+e^{-2-2n\delta}+1+e^{-4n\delta}}\right).

Since
0<\delta<1
, we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\max\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n}-\delta,\frac{k}{n}+\delta)^{c}\}}{\min\{\phi_{\sigma}(nt-k):t\in(\frac{k}{n},\frac{k}{n}+\delta^{2})\}}=0.

This verifies the condition of the Theorem
2.5
. It is easy to see that
M_{p}(\phi_{\sigma})
is finite for
1\leq p<\infty
and hence conditions on Theorem
2.9
is also verified.

5.
Final Remarks and Conclusions

5.1.
Final Remarks

We have the following concluding remarks.

•

In this paper, we have considered the unit hypercube
[0,1]^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is easy to see that the similar results are also applicable to more general sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
\Omega:=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d}[a_{i},b_{i}].
Hence, it is not necessary to repeat the details.

•

We have verified the hypothesis of the theorems for the logistic and hyperbolic tangent activation functions. It would be interesting to look for other sigmoidal functions that satisfy the hypothesis of the theorem.

•

Under the assumptions on
\sigma
, it is easy to see that the condition (
3.1
) is same as assuming that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{\phi_{\sigma}(n\delta)}{\phi_{\sigma}(n\delta^{2})}=0,

for all
0<\delta<1.
It would be of interest to see if the condition (
3.1
) can be reformulated in terms of decay of
\sigma.

•

It would be insightful to study the operator (
1.2
) for specific weighted measure and see how the choice of weight influences the convergence properties of the operator.

5.2.
Conclusions

The approximation of functions belonging to the
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho),
where
1\leq p<\infty
is associated with an arbitrary measure
\rho
defined on a hypercube satisfying a certain support condition by the NN operators is investigated.
Specifically, the uniform approximation of continuous functions defined on a hypercube by these operators is proved. Further, the
L^{p}(I^{d},\rho)
approximation and its error rate in terms of
\mathcal{K}-
functional is obtained.
Towards the end, the hypothesis of the theorems are verified for the logistic and hyperbolic tangent activation functions. The approximation of particular continuous and integrable functions by NN operators with respect to the Lebesgue and Jacobi measures defined on
[0,1]\times[0,1]
with these activation functions has been shown.

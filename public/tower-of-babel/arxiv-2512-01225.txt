Asymptotic stability of solitary waves for the b b -family of equations
Jun Wu, Yue Liu, Zhong Wang

Abstract
. We establish the asymptotic stability of lefton solutions-exponentially localized stationary solitary waves-for the b b -family of equations with positive momentum density in the regime b < − 1 b<-1 . Unlike the completely integrable Camassa-Holm ( b = 2 ) (b=2) and Degasperis-Procesi ( b = 3 ) (b=3) cases, this parameter range lies outside integrability and exhibits distinct nonlinear dynamics. Our analysis adapts the Martel-Merle framework for generalized KdV equations to the nonlocal, non-integrable structure of the b b -family of equations. The proof combines a nonlinear Liouville property for solutions localized near leftons with a refined spectral analysis of the associated linearized operator. These results provide the first rigorous asymptotic stability theory for leftons in the non-integrable b b -family of equations.

The asymptotic stability problem has a long history, beginning with the seminal works of Soffer, Pego, and Weinstein

[

[

[

was established in
[

A key ingredient of the Martel-Merle method is the derivation of monotonicity formulae, which yield decay estimates through direct differentiation. However, for the
b
-family with
b<-1
, the dispersive term is absorbed during this derivation process, obstructing the construction of monotonicity estimates and virial-type bounds. This contrasts sharply with the gKdV equation, where such absorption does not occur and negative weighted norms are not inherent to the formulation. To overcome this difficulty, we establish monotonicity properties for solutions in the spatial

-direction but taken backward in time. In this setting, the negative weighted
H^{1}_{\alpha}
functional arises not from the dispersive term but from the intrinsic structure of the weight function built into the monotonicity formula. This constitutes the first major challenge in the asymptotic stability analysis of the
b
-family of equations, reflecting the absence of a linear dispersive mechanism.

The second key ingredient in establishing asymptotic stability is the spectral analysis of the linearized operator around the leftons (see Lemma (
1.1
)). As noted earlier, the available spectral results are incomplete. A useful observation is that the change of variables
\tilde{f}=\sqrt{\alpha},f
transforms the linearized operator of the
b
-family into a form closely resembling that of the gKdV equation. This allows us to exploit the well-understood spectral properties of the localized gKdV-type operator to fully characterize the spectrum of the
b
-family linearization.
A complication arises, however: under this transformation the eigenvalue problem becomes

\mathcal{L}f=\lambda\sqrt{\alpha}f
,
where
\alpha
(defined in (
1.9
)) is uniformly bounded away from zero. As a result, eigenfunctions associated with negative eigenvalues cannot be directly employed in the spectral analysis. It is therefore necessary to identify suitable test functions that preserve localization under the action of
\alpha
. In Section
2
, we introduce the specific function

SQ=\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}Q^{-1/b}+\frac{2(b-1)}{b}Q,

which satisfies these requirements and plays a central role in our analysis.

Although special functions arise in this context, it remains essential to verify the following nonstandard spectral condition:

\int\mathcal{L}^{-1}(SQ),(SQ),\mathrm{d}x<0.

(1.26)

The coercivity of the associated bilinear form holds provided that the specific function
SQ
satisfies both the spectral condition (
1.26
) and the orthogonality condition

\int vSQ\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

while at the same time not being orthogonal to the eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
. Importantly, the validity of (
1.26
) depends on the parameter regime
b<-1
, which corresponds to
k>1
. Outside this regime the spectral condition fails, and coercivity cannot be established.
A further difficulty arises from the structure of the conservation law
F_{2}
, which is not naturally associated with the
H^{1}
energy space. This lack of direct correspondence obstructs the derivation of the
H^{1}
-based conservation laws needed for virial estimates. In particular, this necessitates the additional assumption
F_{2}<\infty
in the proof of global well-posedness
[
17
]
. Since
F_{2}
cannot be directly exploited, we instead impose a positivity assumption on
m
, which ensures boundedness of solutions in
H^{1}_{\alpha}
. This positivity condition is likewise crucial for establishing global well-posedness.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The monotonicity results and linear Liouville property (Theorem
1.3
) are established in Section
2

3
is devoted to the crucial monotonicity arguments for
m
and the nonlinear Liouville property (Theorem
1.2
), required for the proof of Theorem
1.1

1.1
) is given in Section
4

which imply that
|\mathcal{K}(t_{0})|\lesssim e^{-\nu x_{0}/L}.
This completes the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

In the following, we derive the uniform exponential decays of the solutions of (
1.23
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
v\in C\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)
be the solutions of (
1.23
) satisfying (
1.24
). Then there exists a positive constant
\nu
such that for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\int\left(\partial_{x}^{j}v\right)^{2}(x,t)e^{\nu|x|}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 1.

(2.15)

Proof.

Under the conclusions of Lemma
2.2
, we derive from
\phi_{L}(y)\geq Ce^{y/L}
and
\phi_{L}^{\prime}(y)\geq Ce^{y/L}
for
y<0
that

\displaystyle\int_{x<x_{0}}

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{x}^{j}v\right)^{2}(x,t_{0})e^{\nu x/L}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 1.

(2.16)

Observe that
v(-x,-t)
is the solutions of (
1.23
) satisfying (
1.24
). Consequently, the same result holds for
v(-x,-t)
when choosing
\delta=\frac{\nu}{L}
, which implies that (
2.15
) holds. Moreover, one gives that

\displaystyle e^{A_{0}\delta}\int_{x>A_{0}}

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{x}^{j}v\right)^{2}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int\left(\partial_{x}^{j}v\right)^{2}(x,t)e^{\delta|x|}\,\mathrm{d}x,~~A_{0}>0,

(2.17)

and invoking the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(x>A_{0})}^{2}\lesssim\|v\|_{L^{2}(x>A_{0})}\|\partial_{x}v\|_{L^{2}(x>A_{0})},
we obtain the pointwise exponential decay

|\partial_{x}^{j}v|\lesssim e^{-A_{0}\nu}.
We argue similarly for the derivative in space at any order of
v(-x,-t),
that is
|\partial_{x}^{j}\tilde{v}|\lesssim e^{-A_{0}\nu}
for
\tilde{v}=v(-x,-t),
which completes the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
∎

2.2.
Proof of linear Liouville theorem

In this subsection, we give the proof of Theorem
1.3
. The idea of proof originates from the strategy in
[
29
]
, which introduces a dual problem that simplifies
(
1.23
). We define that

\eta=\mathcal{L}v-\frac{b+1}{b}\beta(t),

(2.18)

where

\beta(t)=\frac{b}{b+1}\frac{\int SQ\mathcal{L}v\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int SQ\,\mathrm{d}x},~~SQ=\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}Q^{-\frac{1}{b}}+\frac{2(b-1)}{b}Q.

It is readily verifiable
\int SQ\,\mathrm{d}x\neq 0,
which also ensures that
\beta(t)
is well-defined. Note also that
\eta
satisfies the following orthogonality conditions

\int\eta Q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\mathcal{L}vQ^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{b+1}{b}\beta(t)\int Q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int v\mathcal{L}Q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{b+1}{b}\beta(t)\int Q^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

(2.19)

and

\int\eta SQ\,\mathrm{d}x=\int\mathcal{L}vSQ\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{b+1}{b}\beta(t)\int SQ\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(2.20)

We derive from (
1.13
), (
1.23
) and (
2.18
) that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\eta=\mathcal{L}\partial_{t}v-\frac{b+1}{b}\beta^{\prime}(t)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{1}{1-b}\mathcal{B}(Q)\mathcal{L}v\right)+\tilde{\beta}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-b}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}(Q)\eta+\tilde{\beta}(t),

(2.21)

where

\tilde{\beta}(t)(t)=-\frac{\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int SQ\mathcal{L}v\,\mathrm{d}x}{\int SQ\,\mathrm{d}x},

and we used the fact
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}(Q)\left(c\right)=0
for arbitrary constant
c.

We note that there is a compact condition for
\eta\in C\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right).

For a positive constant
C,
it follows from Lemma
2.3
and the decay properties of
Q
that

\left|\eta(x,t)\right|\leq Ce^{-\nu|x|},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R},t\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.22)

Next, in order to compute a virial identity concerning the solutions of (
1.23
), we introduce two functions
\phi(x)=Q^{\prime}(x)
and
\Phi(x)=b^{2}Q(x).
For a constant
M>1
to be chosen later, we set

\Phi_{M}(x)=b^{2}Q\left(\frac{x}{M+t^{2}}\right).

(2.23)

A direct calculation gives

\phi^{\prime}(x)=Q^{\prime\prime}(x)=b^{2}Q-\frac{b(3b+1)}{2k}Q^{\frac{1}{b}+2},~~\phi^{\prime\prime}(x)=b^{2}Q^{\prime}-\frac{(2b+1)(3b+1)}{2k}Q^{\frac{1}{b}+1}Q^{\prime},

(2.24)

and

\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)=-\frac{(2b+1)(3b+1)}{2k}\left((2b^{2}+b)Q^{\frac{1}{b}+2}-\frac{b(5b+3)}{2k}Q^{\frac{2}{b}+3}\right)-\frac{b^{3}(3b+1)}{2k}Q^{\frac{1}{b}+2}+b^{4}Q.

(2.25)

Similarly, we also have

\frac{(\Phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\Phi^{\prime}}=b^{2}\frac{(Q^{\prime})^{2}}{Q}=b^{4}Q\left(1-\frac{Q^{\frac{1}{b}+1}}{k}\right).

(2.26)

We compute that there exists a positive constant
C
with respect to
b
such that

\left|\phi^{\prime}(x)\right|+\left|\frac{(\phi^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{\phi^{\prime}}\right|+\left|\phi^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)\right|\leq C\Phi(x),~~\left|\Phi^{\prime}\right|+\left|\frac{(\Phi^{\prime})^{2}}{\Phi^{\prime}}\right|\leq C\Phi(x).

(2.27)

Multiplying the equation of
\eta
in (
2.2
) and integrating over
\mathbb{R}
, we infer from the expression of
\eta\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}(Q)
(
5
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\eta^{2}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-b}\int\eta\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}(Q)\eta\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{\beta}\int\eta\phi\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-b}\int\eta\phi\bigg(-2kQ^{-\frac{1}{b}}\partial_{x}\eta+\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}Q^{-\frac{1}{b}-1}Q^{\prime}\eta

\displaystyle+(b-1)(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(bQ\partial_{x}\eta+(b-1)Q^{\prime}\eta\right)\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{k(2b-1)}{b(1-b)}\int Q^{-\frac{1}{b}-1}Q^{\prime}\eta^{2}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{k}{(1-b)}\int Q^{-\frac{1}{b}}\eta^{2}\phi^{\prime}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int\eta\phi(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}\left(bQ\partial_{x}\eta+(b-1)Q^{\prime}\eta\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.28)

where the
\tilde{\beta}
term vanish by employing the orthogonality conditions (
2.19
). Unlike the Hamiltonian structure of the KdV equation, the virial identity cannot directly yield the expression for operator
\mathcal{L}
due to the presence of linear nonlocal operator
\mathcal{B}
. Therefore, we directly compute the virial estimate for operator
\mathcal{L}
herein.

Let
M>1
be chosen later and set
z=\eta\sqrt{\Phi_{M}}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}z,z\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\int\eta_{x}^{2}\Phi_{M}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2k(b+1)}{b}\int\eta^{2}\Phi_{M}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{k}{b^{2}}\int\eta^{2}\Phi_{M}^{\prime}\alpha_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\frac{k}{b^{2}}\int\eta^{2}\Phi_{M}^{\prime\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{k}{2b^{2}}\int\eta^{2}\frac{(\Phi_{M}^{\prime})^{2}}{\Phi_{M}}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.29)

For
b<-1,
we claim that the following
coercivity property
holds true,

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{L}z,z\right)\geq\lambda\int(\eta^{2}\alpha+\eta_{x}^{2}\alpha)\Phi_{M}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.30)

where
\lambda>0
will be determined subsequently. To prove this coercivity property, we first present the following lemma, which concerns the first two terms at the right hand of (
2.2
).

Lemma 2.4
.

For the bilinear form

L(\eta,\eta):=\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\int\eta_{x}^{2}\Phi_{M}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2k(b+1)}{b}\int\eta^{2}\Phi_{M}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.31)

there exists a positive constant
\kappa
such that

L(\eta,\eta)\geq\kappa\int(\eta^{2}\alpha+\eta_{x}^{2}\alpha)\Phi_{M}\,\mathrm{d}x,

(2.32)

where
\eta\in C\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)
satisfying the orthogonality conditions (
2.19
) and (
2.20
).

To prove Lemma
2.4
, we consider a non-localized bilinear form

(\mathcal{L}\eta,\eta)=\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\int\eta_{x}^{2}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{2k(b+1)}{b}\int\eta^{2}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x.

(2.33)

The proof of Lemma
2.4
involves the following coercivity property of
(\mathcal{L}\eta,\eta),
which is similar to Lemma E.1 in
[
37
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists a positive constant
\lambda_{1}>0
such that

(\mathcal{L}\eta,\eta)\geq\lambda_{1}\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}}^{2},

(2.34)

for any
\eta\in C\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)
satisfying the orthogonality conditions (
2.19
) and (
2.20
).

Proof.

It is found that

(SQ,\chi_{0})_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}(SQ,\mathcal{L}\chi_{0})=\frac{1}{\lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{L}(SQ),\chi_{0})\neq 0,

(2.35)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(SQ)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}Q^{-\frac{1}{b}}+\frac{2(b-1)}{b}Q\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4k(1-b^{2})(b+2)}{b^{3}}Q^{-\frac{1}{b}}\alpha+\frac{6(2b+1)(1-b)}{b^{2}}\alpha-\frac{2(b^{2}-1)}{kb^{2}}.

According to the expressions of
Q
and
SQ
, we obtain that

\left(SQ,Q^{\prime}\right)_{\alpha}=\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}\int Q^{-\frac{1}{b}}Q^{\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2(b-1)}{b}\int QQ^{\prime}\alpha\,\mathrm{d}x=0

which implies that
SQ\in N^{\bot}(\mathcal{L}).

We finally verify the spectral properties
(\mathcal{L}^{-1}SQ,SQ)<0
. In fact, we deduce from
b<-1
and
A>1
that
\left|\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}\right|>\left|\frac{2(b-1)}{b}\right|,

which combining with the fact
\int Q^{-\frac{1}{b}+2}\,\mathrm{d}x>\int Q^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x>0
and
\mathcal{L}(Q^{2})=SQ
yield that

\left(\mathcal{L}^{-1}SQ,SQ\right)=\frac{2k(1-b)}{b}\int Q^{-\frac{1}{b}+2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2(b-1)}{b}\int Q^{3}\,\mathrm{d}x<0.

This completes the proof of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

Building on Lemma
2.5
, we proceed in accordance with the idea in
[
27
]
to weaken its conditions, thereby proposing the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\theta>0
, if
\eta
satisfies

\left|\left(\eta,\frac{SQ}{\left\|SQ\right\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\right|+\left|\left(\eta,\frac{Q^{\prime}}{\left\|Q^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}}\right)\right|\leq\theta\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}},

(2.36)

then there holds,

(\mathcal{L}\eta,\eta)\geq\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{4}\left\|\eta\right\|_{H^{1}_{\alpha}}^{2}.

(2.37)

Proof.

Assume that
\eta\in C\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)\cap L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R},H_{\alpha}^{1}\right)
satisfies (
2.36
) and
\eta_{1}(x,t)
satisfies the orthogonality conditions
(\eta_{1},SQ)=(\eta_{1},Q^{\prime})=0.

The decomposition of
\eta
can be given as

\eta=\eta_{1}+a_{1}\frac{SQ}{\left\|SQ\right\|_{L^{2}}}+a_{2}\frac{Q^{\prime}}{\left\|Q^{\prime}\right\|_{L^{2}}}:=\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}.

(2.38)

It reveals from the definition of
\mathcal{L}
that

(\mathcal{L}\eta,\eta)=(\mathcal{L}\eta_{1},\eta_{1})+(\mathcal{L}\eta_{2},\eta_{2})+2(\mathcal{L}\eta_{1},\eta_{2}).

(2.39)

We deduce from (
2.36
), (
2.38
) and Lemma
2.5
that

|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|\leq\theta\left\|\eta\right\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}},

(2.40)

and

(\mathcal{L}\eta_{1},\eta_{1})\geq\lambda_{1}\left\|\eta_{1}\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}.

(2.41)

Moreover, it follows from (
2.38
) and (
2.40
) that

\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\leq\left\|\eta_{1}\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}+\theta\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}~~\text{and}~~\left\|\eta_{1}\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\leq\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}},

(2.42)

which together with (
2.41
) to obtain that

(\mathcal{L}\eta_{1},\eta_{1})\geq\frac{7\lambda_{1}}{8}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}.

(2.43)

Next, it remains to estimate
(\mathcal{L}\eta_{2},\eta_{2}).
In view of the properties of
Q
and (
2.40
), we have

\left|(\mathcal{L}\eta_{2},\eta_{2})\right|\leq|a_{1}|^{2}+|a_{2}|^{2}\leq\theta^{2}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{16}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}

(2.44)

and

\left|(\mathcal{L}\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\right|\leq\left\|\eta_{1}\right\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\left\|\eta_{2}\right\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\leq\left\|\eta_{1}\right\|_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\left(|a_{1}|+|a_{2}|\right)\leq\theta\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{16}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H_{\alpha}^{1}}.

(2.45)

We conclude the proof of (
2.37
) follows gathering (
2.39
), (
2.43
), (
2.44
) and (
2.45
).
The proof of Lemma
2.6
is complete.
∎

Lemma

not only constitutes the fundamental cornerstone of the linear Liouville problem, but also directly extends to the nonlinear Liouville theorem without requiring additional modifications by relaxing the orthogonality constraint. Thus, this lemma effectively bridges the gap between linear and nonlinear Liouville theory.

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(M+t^{2})^{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\eta^{2}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x+\left(\mathcal{L}z,z\right)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(M+t^{2})^{s}}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}}.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Invoking (
2.30

such that

-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(M+t^{2})^{s}}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int\eta^{2}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}\int(\eta^{2}\alpha+\eta_{x}^{2}\alpha)\Phi_{M}\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{C_{b,\lambda_{1}}}{(M+t^{2})^{s}}\left\|\eta\right\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{\alpha}}.

(2.57)

Integrating (

) and using the boundedness of

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int\eta^{2}(x,t)\Phi_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int\eta^{2}(x,t)\alpha(x)\Phi_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{b,M,\lambda_{1}}\sup_{t}\|\eta(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}<+\infty.

Thus, there exists a time sequence

\int\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\Phi_{M}\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0,

as

|\Phi_{M}(x)|\geq C_{A,b}>0
if

. Then, we derive from (

) that

\displaystyle\int\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>A}\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{|x|\leq A}\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{A}}\int\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\Phi_{M}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\epsilon,

(2.60)

which leads to

\int\eta^{2}(x,t_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0~~~~\text{as}~n\to\infty.

(2.61)

In a similar way, for a sequence
s_{n}\to\infty,
we get that

\int\eta^{2}(x,-s_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\to 0~~~~\text{as}~n\to\infty.

(2.62)

Integrating (
2.57
) on
(-s_{n},t_{n})
and using (
2.61
) and (

4.

We now focus our attention in the last section to the proof of Theorem

Proof of the operator
\mathcal{B}(Q)\mathcal{L}
and

Recalling that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=k\left(-\partial_{x}\left(\frac{2\alpha}{b^{2}}\partial_{x}\right)-\frac{2(b+1)\alpha}{b}\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=-\left(bm\partial_{x}+m_{x}\right)\left(\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(bm\partial_{x}+(b-1)m_{x}\right),

\displaystyle=-\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\left(\partial_{x}(\alpha v_{x})+b(b+1)\alpha v\right):=-\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\tilde{\mathcal{L}}v,

\displaystyle=-\left(bQ\partial_{x}+Q^{\prime}\right)\left(\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}^{3}\right)^{-1}\left(bQ\partial_{x}+(b-1)Q^{\prime}\right).

A direct calculation then gives

\displaystyle\left(bQ\partial_{x}+(b-1)Q^{\prime}\right)\tilde{\mathcal{L}}v=

\displaystyle-b\left(\partial_{x}-\partial_{x}^{3}\right)\left(Q^{-\frac{1}{b}-1}v\right)+\frac{b^{3}-b^{2}}{2k}\partial_{x}v,

which leads to

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(Q)\mathcal{L}v=-\frac{2k}{b^{2}}\mathcal{B}(Q)\tilde{\mathcal{L}}v=

\displaystyle 2kQ^{-\frac{1}{b}-1}Q^{\prime}v-2kQ^{-\frac{1}{b}}\partial_{x}v

\displaystyle+b(b-1)Q\partial_{x}h+(b-1)Q^{\prime}h,

(5.1)

where

We deduce from

\displaystyle-bq^{\prime}v-q\partial_{x}v-bQ\partial_{x}h-Q^{\prime}h

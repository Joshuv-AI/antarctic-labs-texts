The density of zeros of random power series with stationary complex Gaussian coefficients
Tomoyuki SHIRAI

Abstract
. We study the zeros of random power series with stationary complex Gaussian coefficients, whose spectral measure is absolutely continuous. We analyze the precise asymptotic behavior of the radial density of zeros near the boundary of the circle of convergence. The dependence of the coefficients generally reduces the density of zeros compared with that of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function (the i.i.d. coefficients case), where the spectral density and its zeros plays a crucial role in this reduction. We also show the relationship between the support of the spectral measure and the analytic continuation at the boundary of the circle of convergence.

The case of random power series with i.i.d. coefficients has been

Let
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

complex Gaussian process with covariance

\gamma(k-l)=\mathbb{E}[\xi_{k}\overline{\xi_{l}}],

where we always assume that

distribution of each marginal
\xi_{k}

complex standard normal.
We consider the Gaussian power series with dependent coefficients
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}

X_{\Xi}(z):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\xi_{k}z^{k}

and study its random zeros. The radius of convergence of

X_{\Xi}(z)
is equal to
1

\lim_{k\to\infty}|\xi_{k}|^{1/k}=1

\gamma(0)=\mathbb{E}[|\xi_{k}|^{2}]=1

The zeros of
X_{\Xi}(z)
form a point process

\mathcal{Z}_{X_{\Xi}}:=\sum_{z\in\mathbb{D}:X_{\Xi}(z)=0}\delta_{z}
on
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
.
When
\gamma(k)=\delta_{k0}
, i.e.,
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an i.i.d. sequence, the Gaussian analytic
function (GAF) is called the hyperbolic GAF,
and its zero process is known to be the determinantal
point process associated with the Bergman kernel
K_{\mathrm{Berg}}(z,w)=(1-z\bar{w})^{-2}
and the background measure
\pi^{-1}m(dz)

[
25
]
(see for details on DPPs (cf.

,
32
,
29
,
13
]
). From this fact, it is easily seen that
the expected number of zeros of the hyperbolic GAF within
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}

is exactly equal to
r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{-1}
.
In
[
21

the authors treated
the case where the coefficient Gaussian process
\Xi
corrsponds to a fractional
Gaussian noise with Hurst index
0\leq H<1
and gave an
estimate for
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{Z}_{X_{\Xi}}(\mathbb{D}_{r})]
, showing that it is

O((1-r^{2})^{-1/2})
compared with the hyperbolic GAF case.
In
[
24

\Xi

the precise asymptotics of the expected number of zeros were
analyzed. It is also shown that the dependence of the
coefficients always reduces the expected number of
zeros. Furthermore, the spectral density and its zeros of the coefficient Gaussian
process

plays a crucial role in determining the extent of this reduction.
With the results of
[

this paper discusses the precise asymptotic behavior of the

\partial\mathbb{D}

Moreover, we discuss the analytic continuation of

in relation to the strength of the dependence
among the coefficients in
\Xi

that the stronger the dependence of
\Xi

becomes. These observations
provide deeper insights into the relationship between the
spectral measure of the coefficients and the distributions of zeros.

2.
Main results

The variance of
X_{\Xi}(z)
in terms of
the spectral measure
F(dt)
of the coefficient Gaussian
process
\Xi
is given by

K(z,z):=\mathbb{E}[|X_{\Xi}(z)|^{2}]=\frac{1}{1-r^{2}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}P_{r}(\varphi-t)F(dt),

where
z=re^{i\varphi}
and
P_{r}(s)
is the Poisson kernel

P_{r}(s)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos s+r^{2}}\quad\text{for $s\in[-\pi,\pi]$ and $0\leq r<1$}.

The covariance kernel of
X_{\Xi}(z)
will be given in (
3.3
).
We often identified
e^{i\varphi}
with
\varphi\in[-\pi,\pi]
and
we write
f(\varphi)
for
f(e^{i\varphi})
.
If
F(dt)
is absolutely continuous, i.e.,
F(dt)=f(t)dt
,
then we have the following representation:

K(z,z)=\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}\mathbb{E}_{z}[f(B_{\tau})],

where
B_{t}
is the complex Brownian motion starting at
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\tau
is the first hitting time to the boundary

\partial\mathbb{D}
. Thus, as
z
approaches the boundary
e^{i\varphi}
,
i.e.,
|z|
approaches
1
, the variance
K(z,z)
diverges and
X_{\Xi}(z)

oscillates very fast (and it tends to have more zeros) unless
f(e^{i\varphi})
vanishes.
For example, it is well-known that a Gaussian process
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
purely non-deterministic
if and only if its
spectral measure
F(ds)
is absolutely continuous, i.e.,

F(ds)=f(s)ds
such that

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\log f(s)ds>-\infty

[
14
, p.112]
.
In this case,
f(s)>0
a.e. on
\partial\mathbb{D}
, and hence

X_{\Xi}(z)
oscillates infinitely often near the boundary

\partial\mathbb{D}
, and the boundary
\partial\mathbb{D}
becomes the
natural boundary (see Section
6
).

In order to state the main result,
we introduce some notations.
The symmetrization and anti-symmetrization of
h

are defined by

\hat{h}(s)=\frac{h(s)+h(-s)}{2},\quad\check{h}(s)=\frac{h(s)-h(-s)}{2}.

(2.1)

We define the operator
T
acting on symmetric functions smooth at

s=0
by

Th(s):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{h(s)-h(0)}{1-\cos s}&\text{for $s\not=0$},\\[8.53581pt]
h^{\prime\prime}(0)&\text{for $s=0$}.\end{cases}

and
\mathcal{I}
is the integral operator defined by

\mathcal{I}(h)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(s)ds.

The following is the first main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F_{\varphi}(ds)=F(d(s+\varphi))
on
(-\pi,\pi]
modulo
2\pi
.
Suppose
F_{\varphi}(ds)=f_{\varphi}(s)ds
and
f_{\varphi}(s)
is smooth at
s=0
.

(i) When
f_{\varphi}(0)>0
,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(re^{i\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(1-r^{2})^{2}}-\frac{1}{4f_{\varphi}(0)^{2}}\Big\{f_{\varphi}^{\prime}(0)^{2}+\mathcal{I}(T\hat{f}_{\varphi})^{2}\Big\}+O(1-r^{2}).

(ii) When
f_{\varphi}(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(re^{i\varphi})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(1-r^{2})}\frac{f_{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)}{2\mathcal{I}(T\hat{f}_{\varphi})}+O(1)

(iii) When
f_{\varphi}(0)=f_{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0
. Then,

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(re^{i\varphi})=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\mathcal{I}(T^{2}\hat{f}_{\varphi})^{2}-\mathcal{I}(T^{2}(\hat{f}_{\varphi}\cos s))^{2}-\mathcal{I}\big(T^{2}(\check{f}_{\varphi}\sin s)\big)^{2}}{4\mathcal{I}(T\hat{f}_{\varphi})^{2}}+O(1-r^{2}).

When the spectral density vanishes at some points on the unit circle,
it is observed that the order of the density of zeros in the
direction approaching those points decreases according to
the degree of the degeneracy.
This is related to the phenomenon analyzed in

[
24
]
, where the expected number of zeros is
further reduced when the spectral density has zeros.
The opposite effect was observed in
[
1
]
, where the expected number of real zeros of
Gaussian trigonometric polynomials increases due to the
degeneracy of the spectral measure.

Remark 2.2
.

Since
\hat{f}_{\varphi}(s)
is of
O(s^{2})
at
s=0
when
f_{\varphi}(0)=0
, we have

\mathcal{I}(T\hat{f}_{\varphi})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{\hat{f}_{\varphi}(s)}{1-\cos s}ds.

Note that
\hat{f}_{\varphi}(s),\hat{f}_{\varphi}(s)\cos s,\check{f}_{\varphi}(s)\sin s
are
of
O(s^{4})
when
f_{\varphi}(0)=f_{\varphi}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0

For such a function

O(s^{4})

\mathcal{I}(T^{2}g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{g(s)}{(1-\cos s)^{2}}ds.

Then, the numerator of the first term in (iii) can be expressed as

\frac{1}{4}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{G_{\varphi}(s,t)+G_{\varphi}(s,-t)}{(1-\cos s)^{2}(1-\cos t)^{2}}dsdt,

.

.

, i.e.,

.

3.
Edelman-Kosltan’s formula and spectral measures

We consider a centered, stationary Gaussian process
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
with covariance function

\{\gamma(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
.
Suppose
\gamma(0)=1
. Then, by Herglotz’s theorem, there exists a probability measure
F(dt)
on

(-\pi,\pi]
such that

\gamma(k)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-ikt}F(dt).

The measure
F(dt)
is called the spectral measure of Gaussian
process
\Xi
. The Gaussian process
\Xi
with spectral measure
F(dt)
admits
the following spectral representation
(cf.
[
14
]
):

\xi_{k}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-ikt}dZ(t),

(3.1)

where
Z(t)
is the complex Gaussian process with independent increments satisfying

\mathbb{E}[Z(A)\overline{Z(B)}]=F(A\cap B).

Every element
\eta
of the span
H(\Xi):=\mathrm{span}\{\xi_{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}\}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{P})
of
\Xi=\{\xi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is represented as

\eta=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}u(t)dZ(t).

for some
u\in L^{2}(F)
. Here
L^{2}(F)
is the space of
square-integrable functions with respect to the inner
product

\langle f,g\rangle_{F}:=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(t)\overline{g(t)}F(dt).

The correspondence between
\eta\in H(\Xi)
and
u\in L^{2}(F)
is a unitary isomorphism.

From (
3.1
), we see that

X(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{-ikt}dZ(t)\right)z^{k}=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{1-ze^{-it}}dZ(t).

(3.2)

We have an integral reprsentation of the covariance kernel of the GAF
X_{\Xi}
from
this expression as follows:

K(z,w)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{1-ze^{-it}}\overline{\frac{1}{1-we^{-it}}}F(dt)

(3.3)

In particular, for
z=re^{i\varphi}
,

K(z,z)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{1}{|1-ze^{-it}|^{2}}F(dt)=\frac{1}{1-r^{2}}\tilde{F}(re^{i\varphi})

(3.4)

where
\tilde{F}
is the harmonic extension of
F(dt)

defined as the Poisson integral

\tilde{F}(re^{i\varphi})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}P_{r}(\varphi-t)F(dt)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}P_{r}(s)F_{\varphi}(ds)

with
F_{\varphi}(ds)=F(d(s+\varphi))
on
(-\pi,\pi]
modulo
2\pi
.
Here we give a spectral representation of the
1
-intensity
\rho_{1}(z)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

The
1
-intensity of zeros of
X_{\Xi}(z)
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\displaystyle\rho_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi(1-r^{2})^{2}}\frac{\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\{1-\cos(t-s)\}P_{r}(s)^{2}P_{r}(t)^{2}F_{\varphi}(ds)F_{\varphi}(dt)}{\Big(\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}P_{r}(s)F_{\varphi}(ds)\Big)^{2}},

where
z=re^{i\varphi}
and
F_{\varphi}(ds)=F(d(s+\varphi))
.

Proof.

Edelman-Kostlan’s formula
[
8
]
shows that

\rho_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}\log K(z,z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}K(z,z)\cdot K(z,z)-\partial_{z}K(z,z)\cdot\partial_{\bar{z}}K(z,z)}{K(z,z)^{2}}.

From (
3.4
),

K(z,z)^{2}=\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}\tilde{F}(re^{i\varphi})^{2}.

Also we see that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}K(z,z)\cdot K(z,z)-\partial_{z}K(z,z)\cdot\partial_{\bar{z}}K(z,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|e^{it}-e^{is}|^{2}}{|1-ze^{-it}|^{4}|1-ze^{-is}|^{4}}F(ds)F(dt)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{4}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\{1-\cos(t-s)\}P_{r}(\varphi-t)^{2}P_{r}(\varphi-s)^{2}F(ds)F(dt)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-r^{2})^{4}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\{1-\cos(v-u)\}P_{r}(u)^{2}P_{r}(v)^{2}F_{\varphi}(du)F_{\varphi}(dv).

By change of variables
s=\varphi+u
and
t=\varphi+v
, we obtain the assertion.
∎

For an integrable function
h
, we introduce

\mathcal{P}_{r}(h):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(s)P_{r}(s)ds,\quad\mathcal{Q}_{r}(h):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}h(s)P_{r}(s)^{2}ds.

(3.5)

Suppose
F_{\varphi}(ds)=f_{\varphi}(s)ds
. Since
P_{r}(s)
is
symmetric, we have

\mathcal{Q}_{r}(f_{\varphi})=\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\hat{f}_{\varphi})
,

\mathcal{Q}_{r}(f_{\varphi}\cos s)=\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\hat{f}_{\varphi}\cos s)
, and

\mathcal{Q}_{r}(f_{\varphi}\sin s)=\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\check{f}_{\varphi}\sin s)
, where

\hat{f}_{\varphi}
and
\check{f}_{\varphi}
are symmetrization and
anti-symmetrization of
f_{\varphi}
defined in (
2.1
).
Therefore, when
F_{\varphi}(ds)=f_{\varphi}(s)ds
,
we can restate Proposition
3.1
as

\rho_{1}(re^{i\varphi})=\frac{1}{\pi(1-r^{2})^{2}}\frac{\mathcal{S}_{r}(f_{\varphi})}{\mathcal{P}_{r}(\hat{f}_{\varphi})^{2}}.

(3.6)

where

\mathcal{S}_{r}(f_{\varphi}):=\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\hat{f}_{\varphi})^{2}-\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\hat{f}_{\varphi}\cos s)^{2}-\mathcal{Q}_{r}(\check{f}_{\varphi}\sin s)^{2}.

(3.7)

In the next section, we will give asymptotic expansions for
\mathcal{P}_{r}(h)
and

\mathcal{Q}_{r}(h)
as
r\to 1
.

Precise asymptotics of Poisson integral and its relative

The following notation will be used in this section and hereafter.

x=x(s)=1-\cos s,\quad y=y(r)=1-r^{2}.

(4.1)

Let
\mathcal{H}:=\{h:(-\pi,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}:\text{$h$ is smooth around $s=0$, and symmetric, i.e. $h(-s)=h(s)$}\}

\mathcal{H}_{b}:=\{h\in\mathcal{H}:\text{$h$ is bounded}\}

For

If, in addition,

We also remark that

(4.2)

4.1.
Asymptotic expansion I

Fatou’s theorem states that if

The following is a precise version of Fatou’s theorem.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose

the domain
D^{\tau}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|>\tau\}
. Now we denote
the singularity of
\varphi_{f}
by
S^{\varphi_{f}}\subset\partial D^{\tau}=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:|w|=\tau\}
. The function
z^{-1}\varphi_{f}(z^{-1})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is holomorphic
in
\mathbb{D}_{\tau}
and its singularity coincides with
S^{\varphi_{f}}
.
The closed convex hull
(S^{\varphi_{f}})^{\mathrm{conv}}
is called the

conjugate diagram
of the function
f(z)
.
We define the
indicator function
of
f(z)
by

h^{f}(\theta):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|f(re^{i\theta})|}{r}\quad\text{for $\theta\in[-\pi,\pi]$}.

It is known that
h^{f}(\theta)
is the supporting function of
a bounded convex set, which is called the
indicator
diagram
of
f(z)
and denote it by
I^{f}

[
19
, Chapter I, Section 19]
.

Now we recall Polyá’s theorem
[
19
, Theorem 33, Chapter
I]
.

Theorem 6.2
.

The conjugate diagram of an eitire function of exponential
type is the reflection in the real axis of its indicator
diagram, i.e.,
(S^{\varphi_{f}})^{\mathrm{conv}}=(I^{f})^{\mathrm{ref}}
.

In
[
4
, Lemma 7.2.1]
,
for a wide-sense stationary process
\Xi=\{\xi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
with spectral measure
F(dt)
, they considered an entire
function of exponential type of the form

E_{\Xi}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\xi_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}

and showed that
almost surely,

h^{E_{\Xi}}(\theta)\leq h_{F^{*}}(\theta),\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi],

(6.4)

where
h_{F^{*}}(\theta)
is the supporting function of

((\supp F)^{\mathrm{ref}})^{\mathrm{conv}}
and given by

h_{F^{*}}(\theta)=\max_{t\in\supp F}\cos(\theta+t)
.
In other words, the indicator diagram
I^{E_{\Xi}}

of
E_{\Xi}
is contained in the closed
convex hull of
\supp F
reflected in the real axis, i.e.,

(S^{\varphi_{E_{\Xi}}})^{\mathrm{conv}}=(I^{E_{\Xi}})^{\mathrm{ref}}\subset(\supp F)^{\mathrm{conv}},

(6.5)

and hence, we can conclude that
S^{\varphi_{E_{\Xi}}}\subset\supp F
since both
S^{\varphi_{E_{\Xi}}}
and
\supp F
are subsets of

\partial D^{\tau}
. This implies that analytic continuation
can be performed at least across
(\supp F)^{c}
.
Furthermore, when
\Xi
is a stationary

[
4

that almost surely

h^{E_{\Xi}}(\theta)=h_{F^{*}}(\theta)\quad\theta\in[-\pi,\pi].

Therefore, in the case where

.

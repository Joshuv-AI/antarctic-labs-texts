Restricted Gravity: Lagrangian Formalism and Explicit Solutions
S. H. Oh, Sangwoo Kim, Y. M. Cho

Abstract
It is well known that, making the Abelian projection of Einstein’s theory one can obtain the restricted gravity which is simpler than Einstein’s theory but describes the core dynamics of Einstein’s gravity. In this paper we present the Lagrangian formalism of the restricted gravity which makes the restricted gravity a self consistent field theory by itself, independent of Einstein’s theory. With this we present interesting solutions of the restricted gravity, in particular the gravitational cosmic string, the Bertotti-Robinson spacetime, the axisymmetric pp-wave, and the conformally flat waves with flat wavefront. Moreover, we show that in the restricted gravity the Rosen-Bondi gravitational plane wave could be described by two spin-one Maxwellian potentials. This could play important role in quantum gravity. We discuss the physical implications of the restricted gravity.



I
Introduction

The Einstein’s theory of gravitation is based on
the general invariance, which is so binding that
it (together with the simplicity principle) strongly restricts the dynamics of gravitation. For this
reason it has often been asserted that Einstein’s theory is the simplest possible theory of gravitation allowed by the general invariance. Similar assertion has been made in gauge theory. The gauge invariance
is so binding that it strongly restricts the theory
to the standard non-Abelian gauge theory.

But this assertion is an overstatement. Making
the Abelian projection one can obtain the restricted gauge theory which has the full non-Abelian gauge symmetry yet simpler than the Yang-Mills theory. Moreover, in Einstein’s theory we can also have
the restricted gravity which has the full general invariance but simpler than Einstein’s theory, which describes the core dynamics of Einstein’s
theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

One way to understand this situation is to remember
that the Einstein’s theory can be viewed as a gauge
theory of Lorentz group
[
5
,
6
]
. Adopting
this view and imposing the maximal Abelian isometry
to the gravitational connection, we can decompose
the gravitational connection and the curvature tensor
into the restricted part of the maximal Abelian
subgroup
H
of Lorentz group
G
and the valence
part of
G/H
component, without compromising
the general invariance. With this we can decompose
the Einstein’s theory into the restricted part made of
the restricted connection and the valence part made of
the gauge covariant valence connection. This allows us
to interpret Einstein’s theory as a restricted theory
of gravitation made of the restricted connection which
has the valence connection as the Lorentz covariant
gravitational source
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

This tells that we could actually construct
a restricted theory of gravitation which has
the full general invariance but is much simpler
than Einstein’s theory with the restricted part
of connection alone, which retains all topological characteristics of Einstein’s theory. This implies that the restricted gravity can be interpreted to describe the core dynamics of Einstein’s theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

The Abelian decomposition has played a crucial
role to simplify the non-Abelian dynamics and
reveal the important hidden structures of QCD
that one can not find out from the conventional
QCD
[
7
,
8
]
. In particular, it tells
that QCD can be viewed as the restricted QCD made
of the restricted potential, which has the valence potential as the gauge covariant colored source.
This has played a central role to establish
the monopole condensation and the color confinement
in QCD
[
9
,
10
]
.

Similarly the Abelian decomposition reveals
the hidden structures of Einstein’s theory and clarifies the skeleton structure of the theory
showing that the gravitational connection and
the curvature tensor can be decomposed to the Abelian (i.e., restricted) part and the valence part.
As importantly it tells that the restricted part
has the full general invariance, even though it is restricted. This implies that we could construct
a generally invariant theory of gravitation with
the restricted connection, which is simpler than
the Einstein’s theory. This tells that the Abelian decomposition of Einstein’s theory sheds a totally
new light on Einstein’s theory of gravitation.

So far, however, the restricted gravity has been
shown to exist within the context of Einstein’s theory. So it is important to show that the restricted gravity can exist independent of Einstein’s theory,
as a self consistent theory of gravity. To do that
we need a Lagrangian formalism of restricted
gravity which can prove that the restricted
gravity can indeed describe a theory of gravity.

The purpose of this paper is three-fold. First,
to construct the Lagrangian which describes the restricted gravity which can make the restricted gravity a self consistent field theory by itself.
Second, to show that the restricted gravity indeed describes many intersting solutions, including
the gravitational cosmic string and the axisymmetric pp-waves. Third, to show that the restricted gravity can describe the Rosen-Bondi gravitational plane wave with a pair of Maxwell type spin-one gauge fields, which may have a deep implication in quantum gravity.

It is a non-trivial task to find the Lagrangian
which describes the restricted gravity. The reason
is that, in the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory
the connection plays the fundamental role, but in Einstein’s theory the metric plays the fundamental role. And in the gauge formalism of Einstein’s
theory, we have the metricity condition which translates the connection to the metric from
the equation of motion. But in the restricted gravity we only have the restricted connection without
the valence connection, so that it is not clear how
the restricted connection can determine the metric.
In this paper we show how to resolve this problem constructing the Lagrangian for the restricted gravity, which can make the restricted gravity
a self consistent field theory.

The remarkable feature of the restricted gravity
is that the Abelian component of the metric is described by the Abelian gauge potential which satisfies the Maxwell type equation. This implies
that the core dynamics of Einstein’s gravity can
be described by an Abelian gauge theory. In particular, this implies that the graviton could
be described by a spin-one gauge field. This could have deep implication in quantum gravity, not just
because the graviton could be identifies as
a spin-one particle but more importantly because
the Maxwell type gauge theory is easier to quantize.

Since Feynman tried to quantize Einstein’s theory
quantizing the metric, the graviton is regarded as
a spin-two particle
[
11
]
. But this popular
view has a critical defect, because it is well known that the graviton which couples to fermions is not
the metric but the tetrad, four spin-one vector fields. This means that the tetrad is more fundamental than the metric, which strongly implies that
the metric may not be viewed as the quantum field
of gravity. The Lagrangian formalism of the restricted gravity provides an alternative way to quantize
the gravity, with massless spin-one graviton.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section II we review the gauge formulation of Einstein’s theory for later purpose. In Section III we review the Abelian decomposition of Einstein’s theory for notational purpose. In Section IV we construct the Lagrangian which describes the restricted gravity as a Maxwell type Abelian gauge theory. In Section V we discuss
the spacetime geometry of the restricted gravity
with trivial topology. In Section VI we present interesting solutions of the
A_{2}
gravity, and
in Section VII we present interesting solutions
of the
B_{2}
gravity. In Section VIII we discuss
how the Rosen-Bondi gravitational wave can be
described by a pair of spin-one gauge potentials.
In Section IX we discuss the physical implications
of the restricted gravity, in particular on quantum gravity.

II
Einstein’s Theory as Gauge Theory of Lorentz Group: A Review

To discuss the restricted gravity we have to start from the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory, because the restricted gravity is obtained by
the Abelian projection of this gauge theory of Lorentz group
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Let
\partial_{\mu}~(\mu=t,x,y,z)
and
e_{a}~(a=0,1,2,3)
be a coordinate basis and an orthonormal basis in spacetime,

\displaystyle[\partial_{\mu},~\partial_{\nu}]=0,~~~[e_{a},~e_{b}]=f_{ab}^{~~c}~e_{c},

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}=e_{\mu}^{~a}~e_{a},~~~~~e_{a}=e_{a}^{~\mu}\partial_{\mu},

\displaystyle f_{ab}^{~~c}=(e_{a}^{~\mu}\partial_{\mu}e_{b}^{~\nu}-e_{b}^{~\mu}\partial_{\mu}e_{a}^{~\nu})e_{\nu}^{~c},

(1)

where
e_{\mu}^{~a}
and
e_{a}^{~\mu}
are the tetrad
and inverse tetrad. And let
J_{ab}=-J_{ba}
be
the generators of Lorentz group which act on
the tetrad,

\displaystyle[J_{ab},~J_{cd}]=\eta_{ac}J_{bd}-\eta_{bc}J_{ad}+\eta_{bd}J_{ac}-\eta_{ad}J_{bc}

\displaystyle=f_{ab,cd}^{~~~~~~mn}~J_{mn},

\displaystyle f_{ab,cd}^{~~~~~~mn}=\eta_{ac}\delta_{b}^{~[m}\delta_{d}^{~n]}-\eta_{bc}\delta_{a}^{~[m}\delta_{d}^{~n]}

\displaystyle+\eta_{bd}\delta_{a}^{~[m}\delta_{c}^{~n]}-\eta_{ad}\delta_{b}^{~[m}\delta_{c}^{~n]},

(2)

where
\eta_{ab}=diag~(-1,1,1,1)
is the Lorentz invariant Minkowski metric. We can express
J_{ab}

in terms of three dimensional rotation and
boost generators
L_{i}
and
K_{i}
,

\displaystyle[L_{i},~L_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}L_{k},~~~[L_{i},~K_{j}]=\epsilon_{ijk}K_{k},

\displaystyle[K_{i},~K_{j}]=-\epsilon_{ijk}L_{k},~~~(i,j,k=1,2,3)

\displaystyle L_{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ijk}J_{jk},~~~K_{i}=J_{0i},

(3)

so that any Lorentz sextet can be decomposed
to two three-dimensional rotation and boost
parts.

Now we introduce the anti-symmetric unit tensor
{\bf I}^{ab}
which forms an adjoint representation of Lorentz group whose rotation and boost parts
\hat{m}^{ab}
and
\hat{e}^{ab}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\bf I}^{ab}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\hat{m}}^{ab}\\
{\hat{e}}^{ab}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle{\hat{m}}_{i}^{~ab}=\epsilon_{0i}^{~~ab},~~~{\hat{e}}_{i}^{~ab}=\big(\delta_{0}^{~a}\delta_{i}^{~b}-\delta_{0}^{~b}\delta_{i}^{~a}\big),

\displaystyle I_{mn}^{~~~ab}=\big(\delta_{m}^{~a}\delta_{n}^{~b}-\delta_{m}^{~b}\delta_{n}^{~a}\big)=-(J_{mn})^{ab}.

(6)

Let
{\bf p}
be a Lorentz sextet which forms an
adjoint representation of Lorentz group. Clearly
we can express
{\bf p}
by
p^{ab}
,

\displaystyle{\bf p}=\frac{1}{2}p_{ab}{\bf I}^{ab}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\vec{m}}\\
{\vec{e}}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle p^{ab}={\bf p}\cdot{\bf I}^{ab}=\frac{1}{2}p^{cd}I_{cd}^{~~~ab},

(9)

where
m_{i}=\epsilon_{ijk}p^{jk}/2~(i,j,k=1,2,3)
is the rotation (or magnetic) part and
e_{i}=p^{0i}
is the boost (or electric) part
of
{\bf p}
. Notice that, with the invariant metric
\delta_{AB}
of Lorentz group

\displaystyle\delta_{AB}=-\dfrac{1}{4}f_{AC}^{~~D}f_{BD}^{~~C}

\displaystyle=diag~(+1,+1,+1,-1,-1,-1),

(10)

we have

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{2}=\delta_{AB}~p^{A}p^{B}=\dfrac{1}{2}p_{ab}p^{ab}={\vec{m}}^{2}-{\vec{e}}^{2}.

(11)

Also, introducing the dual partner
\tilde{\bf p}
of
{\bf p}
given by
\widetilde{p}^{ab}=\epsilon^{abcd}p_{cd}/2
, we have (with
\epsilon_{0123}=+1
)

\displaystyle\tilde{\bf p}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\vec{e}}\\
-{\vec{m}}\end{array}\right),~~~{\bf p}\cdot\tilde{\bf p}=2{\vec{m}}\cdot{\vec{e}},~~~\tilde{\bf p}^{2}=-{\bf p}^{2},

\displaystyle{\bf p}\times\tilde{\bf p}=0,~~~~~[p,~\widetilde{p}]=0.

(14)

This tells that any two vectors which are dual to
each other are commuting.

To have the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory, we introduce the anti-symmetric metric
g_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}
which forms an adjoint representation of Lorentz group

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}~{\bf I}_{ab}=e_{\mu}^{~a}e_{\nu}^{~b}~{\bf I}_{ab},

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}=(e_{\mu}^{~a}e_{\nu}^{~b}-e_{\nu}^{~a}e_{\mu}^{~b})=e_{\mu}^{~c}e_{\nu}^{~d}I_{cd}^{~~ab}.

(15)

To proceed, we express the gravitational connection

{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{~ab}
corresponding to the gauge potential and the curvature tensor
R_{\mu\nu}^{~~ab}
corresponding to the gauge field strength
the Lorentz group by Lorentz sextets
{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}
and
{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
. Clearly we have

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\bf\Gamma}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}+{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}\times{\bf\Gamma}_{\nu}.

(16)

With this we have the Lorentz invariant
Einstein-Hilbert action of the gravitation
[
5
]

\displaystyle S[e_{\mu}^{~a},{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}]=\dfrac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\displaystyle\int e~({\bf g}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf R}^{\mu\nu})~d^{4}x,

\displaystyle e={\rm Det}~e_{\mu}^{~a}.

(17)

This is the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory.

Notice that the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory is not the ordinary gauge theory. The Einstein-Hilbert action is linear in
{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
, while the Yang-Mills action is quadratic in
the field strength. So the dynamics of Einstein’s theory becomes different from the non-Abelian dynamics of the Yang-Mills theory. In particular, in Einstein’s theory the metric
{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}
(not
the connection) becomes the dynamical variable, but in gauge theory the potential does.

In this first order formalism we have two equations of motion from (
17
)

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{~a}:~~~~~{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf R}^{\nu a}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}:~~~~~\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0~~~~(\mathscr{D}_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}+{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}\times),

(18)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
describes the generally covariant derivative and
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}
describes the generally and Lorentz covariant derivative. The first equation assures that, in the absence of matter fields, the Ricci tensor must vanish. The second equation, with (
15
), tells that
e_{\mu}^{a}
must be generally and Lorentz gauge covariant constant,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{~a}=\partial_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{~a}-{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}^{~~\alpha}e_{\alpha}^{~a}+{\Gamma}_{\mu b}^{~~a}e_{\nu}^{~b}=0,

(19)

or

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{~ab}=e^{\nu a}\hat{\nabla}_{\mu}e_{\nu}^{~b}.

(20)

Conversely, with (
19
) the second equation becomes an identity,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=D_{\mu}{\bf I}^{ab}=0.

(21)

This tells that in the Lorentz gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory the gauge potential
{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}
is given by the spin connection given by (
20
), which can (in principle)
have torsion when we have the spinor matter field in the theory. This shows that the second equation is nothing but the metric compatibility of
the connection,

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0~~~\Longleftrightarrow~~~\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}=0,

(22)

which assures that (
17
) indeed describes Einstein’s theory of gravitation.

III
Abelian Decomposition of Einstein’s Theory: A Brief Review

We can obtain the restricted gravity applying
the Abelian decomposition to the above gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Let
({\hat{n}}_{1},{\hat{n}}_{2},{\hat{n}}_{3}={\hat{n}})
be a
3
-dimensional unit vectors (
{\hat{n}}_{i}^{2}=1
) which form a right-handed orthonormal basis of the rotation subgroup of the Lorentz group, and choose

\displaystyle{\bf{l}}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\hat{n}}_{i}\\
0\end{array}\right),~~~{\bf{k}}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
{\hat{n}}_{i}\end{array}\right)=-\tilde{{\bf l}}_{i}.

to be an orthonormal basis of the Lorentz group.

Since the Lorentz group has two
2
-dimensional maximal Abelian subgroups,
A_{2}
whose generators are made of
L_{3}
and
K_{3}
and
B_{2}
whose generators are made of
(L_{1}+K_{2})/\sqrt{2}
and
(L_{2}-K_{1})/\sqrt{2}
(or equivalently
(L_{1}-K_{2})/\sqrt{2}
and
(L_{2}+K_{1})/\sqrt{2}
),
so that we have two possible Abelian decompositions of the gravitational connection.
To make the Abelian decomposition, we have to select two Abelian directions
{\bf p}
and
\tilde{\bf p}

in Lorentz group, and decompose the connection
to the restricted part and the valence part, imposing the isometry which parallelizes the Abelian directions. Let
{\bf p}
to be one of
the Abelian directions and let

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\bf p}=(\partial_{\mu}+{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}\times)~{\bf p}=0.

(27)

This automatically assures

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\tilde{\bf p}=(\partial_{\mu}+{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}\times)~\tilde{\bf p}=0,

(28)

because
\epsilon_{abcd}
is an invariant tensor.
So, we actually need only one isometry condition (
27
).

It is useful to characterize the isometry by two Casimir invariants. Let the isometry be described
by
{\bf p}
and
\tilde{\bf p}
. It has two Casimir invariants
\alpha
and
\beta
,

\displaystyle\alpha={{\bf p}\cdot{\bf p}}={\vec{m}}^{2}-{\vec{e}}^{2},~~~\beta={{\bf p}\cdot\tilde{\bf p}}=2{\vec{m}}\cdot{\vec{e}}.

(29)

But we can always choose
(\alpha,\beta)

to be
(\pm 1,0)
or
(0,0)
unless
\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}=0
, redefining
{\bf p}
and

\tilde{\bf p}
by

\displaystyle{\bf p}^{\prime}=a{\bf p}+b\tilde{\bf p},~~~\tilde{\bf p}^{\prime}=a{\bf p}-b\tilde{\bf p}.

(30)

So the magnetic isometry in Einstein’s theory can
be classified by the rotation-boost (or non light-like) isometry and the null (or light-like) isometry whose Casimir invariants are denoted by

(\pm 1,0)
and
(0,0)
.

III.1
A_{2}
(Non-Lightlike) Decomposition

Let the maximal Abelian subgroup be
A_{2}
whose isometry is made of
L_{3}
and
K_{3}
. and let
{\bf p}

and
\tilde{\bf p}
be the two isometry vector fields which correspond to
L_{3}
and
K_{3}
. Clearly we can express the
A_{2}
isometry by

\displaystyle{\bf p}={\bf{l}}={\bf{l}}_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\hat{n}}\\
0\end{array}\right),~~~\tilde{\bf p}=\tilde{{\bf l}}=\tilde{{\bf l}}_{3}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}0\\
-{\hat{n}}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}=0,~~~~D_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}}=0,

(35)

whose Casimir invariants are fixed by
(1,0)
. With this we find the restricted connection
\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}
which satisfies the isometry condition

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}=A_{\mu}~{\bf{l}}-B_{\mu}~\tilde{{\bf l}}-{\bf{l}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{l}}

\displaystyle=A_{\mu}~{\bf{l}}-B_{\mu}~\tilde{{\bf l}}-\frac{1}{2}({\bf{l}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{l}}-\tilde{{\bf l}}\times\partial_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}})

\displaystyle A_{\mu}={{\bf{l}}\cdot{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}},~~B_{\mu}=\tilde{{\bf l}}\cdot{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu},~~{\bf{l}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{l}}=-\tilde{{\bf l}}\times\partial_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}},

(36)

where
A_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
are two Abelian connections of
{\bf{l}}
and
\tilde{{\bf l}}
components.
From this we have the restricted
field strength
\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
given by

\displaystyle\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}+\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\times\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=(A_{\mu\nu}+H_{\mu\nu})~{\bf{l}}-(B_{\mu\nu}+\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu})~\tilde{{\bf l}}

\displaystyle=\bar{A}_{\mu\nu}~{\bf{l}}-B_{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf l}},

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{\mu\nu}=A_{\mu\nu}+H_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\bar{A}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\bar{A}_{\mu},

\displaystyle A_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu},

\displaystyle H_{\mu\nu}=-{\bf{l}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}{\bf{l}}\times\partial_{\nu}{\bf{l}})=\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu},

\displaystyle\bar{A}_{\mu}=A_{\mu}+C_{\mu},~~~~~C_{\mu}=-{\hat{n}}_{1}\cdot\partial_{\mu}{\hat{n}}_{2},

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}B_{\mu},

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}=-\tilde{{\bf l}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}{\bf{l}}\times\partial_{\nu}{\bf{l}})=\tilde{{\bf l}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}}\times\partial_{\nu}\tilde{{\bf l}})=0.

(37)

Notice that here
\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu}
vanishes, so that only
\bar{A}_{\mu}
and
\bar{A}_{\mu\nu}
has a dual structure. In other words, the boost part (i.e., the
\tilde{{\bf l}}
component) of
\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
has no topological part. Moreover,
C_{\mu}
and
H_{\mu\nu}

in
\bar{A}_{\mu}
and
\bar{A}_{\mu\nu}
are formally
identical to the magnetic potential and field strength of
SU(2)
QCD. This tells that
the topology of the Einstein’s theory is fixed
by the topology of the
SU(2)
subgroup,
which is identical to that of SU(2) QCD.

With this we obtain the full connection of Lorentz group adding the Lorentz covariant valence connection
{\bf Z}_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}={Z}^{1}_{\mu}{\bf{l}}_{1}-\widetilde{Z}^{1}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}}_{1}+{Z}^{2}_{\mu}{\bf{l}}_{2}-\widetilde{Z}^{2}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}}_{2}.

(38)

The corresponding field strength
{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
which describes the curvature tensor is decomposed to

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}=\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}={\hat{D}}_{\mu}{\bf Z}_{\nu}-{\hat{D}}_{\nu}{\bf Z}_{\mu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf Z}_{\nu},

(39)

where
{\hat{D}}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\times
and
{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}
is the valence part of the curvature tensor. This is the
A_{2}
decomposition of the gravitational connection.

Now, we decompose the metric
{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}
to the restricted and valence parts,

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}=\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=G_{\mu\nu}~{\bf{l}}-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{{\bf l}},

\displaystyle{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=l_{ab}~{\bf{l}}-\tilde{l}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf l}},

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~l_{ab},~~~{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{l}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~{\bf\Pi}_{ab}=G_{\mu\nu}^{1}{\bf{l}}_{1}-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}^{1}\tilde{{\bf l}}_{1}+G_{\mu\nu}^{2}{\bf{l}}_{2}-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}^{2}\tilde{{\bf l}}_{2},

\displaystyle{\bf\Pi}_{ab}={\bf I}_{ab}-{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=l^{1}_{ab}~{\bf{l}}_{1}-\tilde{l}^{1}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf l}}_{1}+l^{2}_{ab}~{\bf{l}}_{2}-\tilde{l}^{2}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf l}}_{2},

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}^{1}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~l_{ab}^{1},~~~{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}^{1}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{l}_{ab}^{1},

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}^{2}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~l_{ab}^{2},~~~{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}^{2}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{l}_{ab}^{2},

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}\cdot{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=0,~~~{\bf Z}_{\mu}\cdot{\bf\Pi}^{ab}=Z_{\mu}^{~ab}.

(40)

Notice that

\displaystyle{\Sigma}_{ab}^{~~cd}~{\bf{\Sigma}}_{cd}={\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab},~~~~\Pi_{ab}^{~~cd}~{\bf\Pi}_{cd}={\bf\Pi}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}\cdot{\bf\Pi}^{cd}=0.

(41)

So
{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}
and
{\bf\Pi}_{ab}
can be viewed as
the projection operators which project the metric to the restricted and valence parts.

With this we can express the Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian as

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}\Big[~(\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu})\cdot(\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu})\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[~\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}\Big],

(42)

from which we get the following equations of motion

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~G_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}B^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}B^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot({\hat{D}}^{\nu}{\bf Z}^{\rho}-{\hat{D}}^{\rho}{\bf Z}^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta A_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}+{\bf{l}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta B_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{{\bf l}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf Z}_{\mu}:~\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}=0.

(43)

This is the equation for the
A_{2}
decomposition of Einstein’s theory. The first equation is identical to the first equation of (
18
), and the next three equations can be combined to
a single equation
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0
. This assures that the above equation is identical to the Einstein’s equation (
18
). Another point is that the last equation
\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}=0
can be replaced by

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}=0,

(44)

because
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0
makes them equivalent.

III.2
B_{2}
(Light-like) Decomposition

Now, let the Abelian subgroup is
B_{2}
whose
isometry generators is given by
(L_{1}+K_{2})/\sqrt{2}
and
(L_{2}-K_{1})/\sqrt{2}
, and introduce
a complete basis made of four null vectors
{\bf{j}}
,
\tilde{{\bf j}}
,
{\bf{k}}
,
\tilde{{\bf k}}
, and
{\bf{l}}
,
\tilde{{\bf l}}

\displaystyle{\bf{j}}=\frac{{\bf{l}}_{1}+{\bf{k}}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},~~~\tilde{{\bf j}}=\frac{{\bf{l}}_{2}-{\bf{k}}_{1}}{\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle{\bf{k}}=\frac{{\bf{l}}_{1}-{\bf{k}}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},~~~\tilde{{\bf k}}=-\frac{{\bf{l}}_{2}+{\bf{k}}_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}.

(45)

With this we can express the
B_{2}
isometry
by
{\bf{j}}
and
\tilde{{\bf j}}
whose Casimir invariant
is given by
(0,0)
.

In this case we can express the
B_{2}
isometry by

\displaystyle{\bf{j}}=\frac{{\bf{l}}_{1}+{\bf{k}}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\hat{n}}_{1}\\
{\hat{n}}_{2}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle\tilde{{\bf j}}=\frac{{\bf{l}}_{2}-{\bf{k}}_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\hat{n}}_{2}\\
-{\hat{n}}_{1}\end{array}\right),

\displaystyle D_{\mu}{\bf{j}}=0,~~~D_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}}=0.

(50)

With this we find the following restricted connection for the
B_{2}
gravity,

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}={\Gamma}_{\mu}~{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}~\tilde{{\bf j}}-{\bf{k}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{j}}

\displaystyle={\Gamma}_{\mu}~{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}~\tilde{{\bf j}}-\dfrac{1}{2}({\bf{k}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{j}}-\tilde{{\bf k}}\times\partial_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}}),

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu}={\bf{k}}\cdot{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu},~~~{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}=\tilde{{\bf k}}\cdot{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu},

(51)

where
\Gamma_{\mu}
and
{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}
are two Abelian connections of
{\bf{j}}
and
\tilde{{\bf j}}
components.

The restricted curvature tensor
\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}+\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\times\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\nu}=K_{\mu\nu}{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu\nu}\tilde{{\bf j}},

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu}={\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}+H_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}K_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}K_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu\nu}={\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}+{\widetilde{H}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\widetilde{K}}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\Gamma}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\Gamma}_{\mu},~~~{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu},

\displaystyle H_{\mu\nu}=-{\bf{k}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}{\bf{j}}\times\partial_{\nu}{\bf{k}}-\partial_{\nu}{\bf{j}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{k}})=\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}^{1}-\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu}^{1},

\displaystyle C_{\mu}^{1}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\hat{n}}_{2}\cdot\partial_{\mu}{\hat{n}}_{3},

\displaystyle{\widetilde{H}}_{\mu\nu}=-\tilde{{\bf k}}\cdot(\partial_{\mu}{\bf{j}}\times\partial_{\nu}{\bf{k}}-\partial_{\nu}{\bf{j}}\times\partial_{\mu}{\bf{k}})=\partial_{\mu}C_{\nu}^{2}-\partial_{\nu}C_{\mu}^{2},

\displaystyle C_{\mu}^{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}{\hat{n}}_{3}\cdot\partial_{\mu}{\hat{n}}_{1},

\displaystyle K_{\mu}={\Gamma}_{\mu}+C_{\mu}^{1},~~~~~{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu}={\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}+C_{\mu}^{2}.

(52)

Notice that here, unlike the
A_{2}
gravity, both

K_{\mu\nu}
and
{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu\nu}
have the topological parts
H_{\mu\nu}
and
{\widetilde{H}}_{\mu\nu}
. Another point is that,
\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
is orthogonal to
{\bf{l}}
and
\tilde{{\bf l}}
.
This should be contrasted with the restricted curvature tensor (
37
) of the
A_{2}
gravity. With this we obtain the
B_{2}
decomposition of the connection and curvature tensor,

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu},

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}=J_{\mu}{\bf{k}}-{\widetilde{J}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf k}}+L_{\mu}{\bf{l}}-{\widetilde{L}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf l}},

\displaystyle{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}=\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}={\hat{D}}_{\mu}{\bf Z}_{\nu}-{\hat{D}}_{\nu}{\bf Z}_{\mu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf Z}_{\nu},

(53)

where
{\bf Z}_{\mu}
and
{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}
are the valence parts and
{\hat{D}}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\times
.

For the
B_{2}
decomposition we may adopt the following decomposition of the metric
{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}=\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}={\cal J}_{\mu\nu}~{\bf{k}}-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf k}}+{\cal K}_{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\widetilde{{\cal K}}_{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}},

\displaystyle{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=j_{ab}~{\bf{j}}-\tilde{j}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf j}}+k_{ab}~{\bf{k}}-\tilde{k}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf k}},

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~j_{ab},~~~\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{j}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\cal K}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~k_{ab},~~~\widetilde{{\cal K}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{k}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~{\bf\Pi}_{ab}={\cal L}_{\mu\nu}~{\bf{l}}-\widetilde{{\cal L}}_{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf l}},

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~l_{ab},~~~\widetilde{{\cal L}}_{\mu\nu}=e_{\mu}^{a}~e_{\nu}^{b}~\tilde{l}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\bf\Pi}_{ab}={\bf I}_{ab}-{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}=l_{ab}~{\bf{l}}-\tilde{l}_{ab}~\tilde{{\bf l}}.

(54)

Notice that this decomposition of the metric is different from (
40
). In fact here
{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}
plays the role of
\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}
(and
{\bf\Pi}_{ab}
becomes
{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}
) in (
40
). But here again we have

\displaystyle{\Sigma}_{ab}^{~~cd}~{\bf{\Sigma}}_{cd}={\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab},~~~~\Pi_{ab}^{~~cd}~{\bf\Pi}_{cd}={\bf\Pi}_{ab},

\displaystyle{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}\cdot{\bf\Pi}^{cd}=0.

(55)

so that
{\bf{\Sigma}}_{ab}
and
{\bf\Pi}_{ab}
become projection operators.

With this we have the
B_{2}
decomposition of Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian,

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}\Big[~(\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu})\cdot(\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu})\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[~\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}\Big].

(56)

From this we have

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}K^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}K^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{K}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\cal K}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}J^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}J^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal K}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{J}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{J}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}^{\nu\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\Gamma}_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf{j}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf g}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{{\bf j}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf g}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf Z}_{\mu}:~\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}+(\nabla_{\mu}{\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})

\displaystyle+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times({\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})+(K_{\mu}{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}})\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{{\mu\nu}}=0.

(57)

This is the equation for the
B_{2}
decomposition of Einstein’s theory. And here again we can combine the last three equations to a single equation
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0
. This assures
that the above (
57
) is identical to
the Einstein’s equation (
18
). Moreover,
the last equation of (
57
) (with
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}=0
) can be replaced by

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{{\mu\nu}}-(\nabla_{\mu}{\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})-(K_{\mu}{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}})\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times({\cal J}^{\mu\nu}{\bf{k}}-\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{{\bf k}}+{\bf G}^{\mu\nu})=0.

(58)

Of course, we could make the mathematically identical
B_{2}
decomposition with
{\bf{k}}
and
\tilde{{\bf k}}
as the isometry.

IV
Restricted Gravity: Lagrangian Formalism

The decomposed equations (
43
) and (
57
) are not just identical to
the Einstein’s equation. They contain more
information, in the sense that they reveal
the hidden structure of Einstein’s equation.
This must be clear because they show that
the Einstein’s equation is made of two parts,
the restricted part which describes the Abelian subdynamics and the valence part which plays
the role of the gravitational source. This
tells us two things. First, Einstein’s theory can
be simplified to the restricted gravity which we
can obtain putting
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
. Second, Einstein’s
theory can be reinterpreted as the restricted
gravity which has the gauge covariant valence connection as the extra source.

This is nice, but this does not guarantee that
the restricted gravity is a self-consistent field theory by itself. To show that the restricted
gravity exists independent of Einstein’s theory
we need to have the Lagrangian which can describe
the restricted gravity. To construct such a Lagrangian, however, is a non-trivial task. This is because in
the gauge formalism of Einstein’s theory we have
the equation of motion which determines the metric
from the connection. On the other hand, the restricted gravity is described by the restricted connection,
so that it is unclear how we can obtain the equation
of motion which can determine the metric from
the restricted connection. In the following we discuss how to resolve this problem and obtain the Lagrangian formalism of the restricted gravity.

IV.1
Lagrangian Formalism of
A_{2}
Gravity

From (
42
) we might naively choose
the Lagrangian for the
A_{2}
gravity to be
[
4
]

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{A}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\big(~\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}\big).

(59)

But as we have pointed out this does not work,
because here the restricted connection can not determine the restricted metric. More seriously,
we need to have the full metric for the spacetime. This clearly tells that the above Lagrangian
is unacceptable. This shows that it is
a non-trivial matter to have a Lagrangian formalism of the restricted gravity.

Fortunately we can obtain the Lagrangian for
the
A_{2}
gravity from (
70
) with
the constraint for the
A_{2}
Abelian projection,

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{A}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[~\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}\Big].

(60)

Here the constraint
D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}=0
(with
{\bf{l}}^{2}=1
) tells that the Lagrangian is for the
A_{2}
gravity.

To show that the Lagrangian describes the
A_{2}
gravity, notice that we have the following equation of motion from the first expression
of the above Lagrangian

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~G_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}B^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}B^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot({\hat{D}}^{\nu}{\bf Z}^{\rho}-{\hat{D}}^{\rho}{\bf Z}^{\nu})-\frac{1}{2}\big({\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot D_{\nu}{\bf{l}}\big)~\delta_{\mu}^{~\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}:~D_{\mu}{\bf g}^{{\mu\nu}}+\frac{1}{2}\big({\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\times{\bf{l}}\big)=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}:~D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}=0.

(61)

One might wonder why we do not make
\delta{\bf{l}}
to obtain the equation for
{\bf{l}}
. This is not needed because it is a gauge degree. Indeed with
\delta{\bf{l}}
we do not obtain any new information.

To simplify the above equation we notice that
the third equation is nothing but the isometry condition which tells

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\qquad\textrm{or}\qquad{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

(62)

Using this in the second equation, we have

\displaystyle{\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}={\lambda}^{\nu}{\bf{l}}-\tilde{\lambda}^{\nu}\tilde{{\bf l}}-2{\bf{l}}\times\hat{D}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu},

(63)

where
{\lambda}^{\nu}={\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot{\bf{l}}
and

\tilde{\lambda}^{\nu}={\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot\tilde{{\bf l}}
. With this the second equation can be expressed by

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

(64)

or componentwise

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}=0.

(65)

Also, with (
62
) the first equation is expressed by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}B^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}B^{\nu})=0.

(66)

So we can express (
61
) by

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}B^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}B^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

(67)

This is the equation for
A_{2}
gravity. Notice that
this is exactly the same equation that we obtain from
(
43
) with
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
, except that here we
have
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
as an independent equation.

We could derive the same equation from the second expression of (
60
),

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~G_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}B^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}B^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot({\hat{D}}^{\nu}{\bf Z}^{\rho}-{\hat{D}}^{\rho}{\bf Z}^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta A_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}+{\bf{l}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta B_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{{\bf l}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf Z}_{\mu}:~\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}:~D_{\mu}{\bf{l}}=0.

(68)

Since the last equation means
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
,
we can reduce the above equation to

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}B^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}B^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

(69)

But the equation
\hat{\mathscr{D}}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}=0
becomes
redundant, because this is equivalent to the two equations
in the second line. So (
69
) reduces to (
67
).
This assures that the two expressions in (
60
)
are indeed equivalent.

Notice that the restricted
A_{2}
Lagrangian (
60
) can also be expressed by

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{A}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[~\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu}\Big].

(70)

Indeed, with this we have

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~(e_{\mu}^{a}e_{\nu}^{b})[(\partial^{\nu}\bar{A}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}\bar{A}^{\nu})l_{ab}

\displaystyle-(\partial^{\nu}B^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}B^{\nu})\tilde{l}_{ab}]e_{\rho c}=0

\displaystyle\delta A_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}(e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\nu}_{b}l_{ab})=0,

\displaystyle\delta B_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}(e^{\mu}_{a}e^{\nu}_{b}\tilde{l}_{ab})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}:~{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0,

(71)

which is identical to (
67
).

At this point one might wonder why we can not describe
the restricted gravity with only the restricted metric
\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}
and the restricted curvature
\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}
. Indeed, with the first term in (
70
) we have the first two lines of equation (
69
),
which is precisely the full Einstein’s equation (
61
) which satisfies the constraint

{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
. If so, why do we need the second term
in (
70
) which produces the last equation
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
which is completely independent of
the other equations.

To understand the reason we should keep in mind that we have constructed the restricted gravity viewing
the Einstein’s theory as a gauge theory of Lorentz group and making the valence connection vanishing.
In this gauge formalism the connection
{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}
becomes a fundamental field. On the other hand, Einstein’s theory is the theory of spacetime where
the metric
{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}
, not the connection
{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}
, plays the fundamental role. And the bridge which translates the two views to each other is the metric compatibility condition
\mathscr{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}=0
.
In this translation a vanishing connection does not mean a vanishing metric, but a flat metric.
In fact we can not have a vanishing metric in spacetime, because the metricity (that the spacetime
is locally Minkowskian) is the fundamental assumption of Einstein’s theory. This means that, unlike in ordinary gauge theory, here we have to keep the condition
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
in the equation of motion, to recover the metric with the metric compatibility condition. This is why we can not describe
the restricted gravity with the restricted metric
and restricted connection alone. We need the full metric compatibility condition.

The physical meaning behind the restricted gravity
is really surprising. Notice that the first equation
of (
67
) is a first-order differential equation, so that it does not describe the dynamical (i.e., propagating) graviton. It is the constraint equation which determines the connection in terms
of the restricted metric. But remarkably the two
equations for
G_{\mu\nu}
and
{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}
in the second
line look like the free Maxwell’s equations.
Indeed, since
G_{\mu\nu}
and
{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}
are dual to
each other, we can express
G_{\mu\nu}
by one-form potential
G_{\mu}

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}G_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}G_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}G_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu},

(72)

using the fact
\nabla_{\mu}{\widetilde{G}}^{\mu\nu}=0
. Equivalently, we can express
{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}
by one-form potential

\tilde{G}_{\mu}

\displaystyle{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}\tilde{G}_{\nu}-\nabla_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\tilde{G}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\tilde{G}_{\mu},

(73)

using the fact
\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0
.

This tells that we can express the equations of
the restricted metric
G_{\mu\nu}
and
{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}

(i.e., the Abelian component of the metric) as
a Maxwell type second-order differential equation
in terms of the potential
G_{\mu}
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~G_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}G_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}G_{\mu}.

(74)

This is really remarkable, because this shows that
the restricted metric of
A_{2}
gravity can be described by an Abelian spin-one gauge potential.
On the other hand, we emphasize that here
the equation of motion which determine this
restricted metric, i.e., the first equation of (
67
), is different from that of
the ordinary gauge theory.

IV.2
Lagrangian Formalism of
B_{2}
Gravity

The above analysis suggests that the Lagrangian
for the
B_{2}
gravity can be expressed by

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}\Big[{\bf g}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mu}{\bf{j}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{e}{16\pi G_{N}}~\Big[\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot\hat{\bf R}_{\mu\nu}+\hat{{\bf g}}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle+{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mu}{\bf{j}}\Big].

(75)

Here the constraint
D_{\mu}{\bf{j}}=0
(with
{\bf{j}}=0
)
tells that the Lagrangian is for the
B_{2}
gravity.

From the first expression we have

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}K^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}K^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{K}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\cal K}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}J^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}J^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal K}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{J}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{J}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}^{\nu\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\big({\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot D_{\nu}{\bf{j}}\big)~\delta_{\mu}^{~\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}:~D_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}\big({\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\times{\bf{j}}\big)=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\vec{\lambda}}_{\mu}:~D_{\mu}{\bf{j}}=0.

(76)

Here again we do not need
\delta{\bf{j}}
to have the equation
for
{\bf{j}}
. To simplify the above equation we notice that the third equation is nothing but the isometry
condition

\displaystyle{\bf\Gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}\qquad\textrm{or}\qquad{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

(77)

Using this in the second equation, we have

\displaystyle{\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}={\lambda}^{\nu}{\bf{l}}-\tilde{\lambda}^{\nu}\tilde{{\bf l}}-2{\bf{k}}\times\hat{D}_{\mu}{\bf g}^{\mu\nu},

(78)

where
{\lambda}^{\nu}={\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot{\bf{k}}
and

\tilde{\lambda}^{\nu}={\vec{\lambda}}^{\nu}\cdot\tilde{{\bf k}}
. With this
the second equation can be expressed by

\displaystyle\hat{D}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf{j}}\times({\bf{k}}\times\hat{D}_{\mu}\hat{{\bf g}}^{\mu\nu})=0,

(79)

or equivalently

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}=0.

(80)

Also, using the isometry condition (
77
)
in the first equation, we have

\displaystyle G_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\bar{A}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\bar{A}}^{\nu})-{\widetilde{G}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}B^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}B^{\nu})=0.

(81)

So (
76
) can be expressed by

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}K^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}K^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\widetilde{K}}^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

(82)

This is the equation for the
B_{2}
gravity.
Here again this is exactly the same equation that we obtain from (
57
) with
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
, except that here we have
{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0
as an independent equation.

We can obtain the same equation of motion from the second expression of (
75
). From the second expression we have

\displaystyle\delta e_{\mu}^{a}:~{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}K^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}K^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{K}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\cal K}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}J^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}J^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal K}}_{\mu\nu}({\cal D}^{\nu}{\widetilde{J}}^{\rho}-{\cal D}^{\rho}{\widetilde{J}}^{\nu})

\displaystyle+{\bf G}_{\mu\nu}\cdot{\bf Z}^{\nu\rho}=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\Gamma}_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}+{\bf{j}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf g}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}:~\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}+\tilde{{\bf j}}\cdot({\bf Z}_{\mu}\times{\bf g}^{\mu\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf Z}_{\mu}:~\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}+(\nabla_{\mu}{\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})

\displaystyle+{\bf Z}_{\mu}\times({\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})+(K_{\mu}{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}})\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{{\mu\nu}}=0.

Using the last equation we can express this by

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}K^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}K^{\nu})-\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}(\partial^{\nu}{\widetilde{K}}^{\rho}-\partial^{\rho}{\widetilde{K}}^{\nu})=0,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}=0,~~~~~\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}=0,

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathscr{D}}}_{\mu}{\bf G}^{\mu\nu}+(\nabla_{\mu}{\cal K}^{\mu\nu}~{\bf{j}}-\nabla_{\mu}\widetilde{{\cal K}}^{\mu\nu}~\tilde{{\bf j}})

\displaystyle+(K_{\mu}{\bf{j}}-{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu}\tilde{{\bf j}})\times\hat{{\bf g}}^{{\mu\nu}}=0,

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{\mu}=0.

But here again the last equation is redundant
because it is equivalent to the two equations in
the second line. So the above equation becomes identical to (
82

the two expressions in (

indeed equivalent.

Notice that, just as in the

V
Spacetime geometry of restricted gravity with trivial topology

One may wonder what geometrical properties of
the spacetime the restricted gravity can have. To
discuss this question we have to remember that
the restricted gravity has topological part coming from the non-trivial topology of the Abelian direction. For instance, the restricted
A_{2}

curvature (
37
) contains the topological
part
H_{\mu\nu}
which comes from the non-trivial
topology of
{\bf{l}}
. Similarly, the restricted
B_{2}
curvature (
52
) contains the topological
part
H_{\mu\nu}
generated by the non-trivial topology
of
{\bf{j}}
. This allows the restricted gravity to inherit all topological properties of Einstein’s
theory
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In the following, however, we consider the restricted gravity with trivial topology for simplicity.
This simplifies the theory and allows us to discuss the geometicall properties of the restricted gravity more easily. With this assumption we show that all null tetrads of the spacetime described by
A_{2}
gravity are recurrent, which means that the spacetime of
A_{2}
gravity with trivial topology is a direct product of a two-dimensional Lorentzian manifold and
a two-dimensional Riemannian manifold. Moreover, we show that the spacetime of
B_{2}
gravity with trivial topology admits a covariantly constant null vector field, which means that the spacetime described by
the
B_{2}
gravity belongs to the pp-wave spacetimes.

So in the following we assume the orthonormal basis vectors
{\hat{n}}_{i}
to be topologically trivial, that is, independent of the spacetime coordinates and
have the form

\displaystyle({\hat{n}}_{i})_{j}=\delta_{ij},~~~(i,j=1,2,3).

(92)

This guarantees that the restricted gravity
has no non-trivial topology.

V.1
A_{2}

In

of the spin connection are given by

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{12}=-{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{21}=A_{\mu},~~~~{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{03}=-{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{30}=B_{\mu},

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}k_{\nu}=B_{\mu}k_{\nu},~~~\nabla_{\mu}l_{\nu}=-B_{\mu}l_{\nu},

VII.2
Conformally Flat Wave with Planar Wavefront

As we have seen in the previous section, the
A_{2}
gravity contains the Bertotti-Robinson spacetime
that is the only conformally flat solution to
the Einstein-Maxwell equations associated
with non-null electromagnetic fields. In this subsection we construct a counterpart of
the Bertotti-Robinson spacetime which is described
by
B_{2}
gravity associated with the null electromagnetic field.

Consider the
B_{2}
gravity with the metric
(
111
). It follows from (
117
) that the energy-momentum tensor takes
the form of

\displaystyle T_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Phi}\Phi~\partial_{\mu}u\partial_{\nu}u,

(161)

for some complex function
\Phi=\Phi(u)
. Any electromagnetic field which has the above energy-momentum tensor can be cast into
the following form,

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\mu\nu}=\Phi(u)(k_{\mu}m_{\nu}-k_{\nu}m_{\mu}),

(162)

where
\tilde{F}
is the dual of
F
. With this
the equation of motion becomes

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})H=4\pi\bar{\Phi}\Phi.

(163)

For simplicity, let us assume that the pp-wave is planar, that is, the curvature is homogeneous on
each wavefront. In our coordinates, this is equivalent to the statement that all third derivatives of
H
with respect to
x
and
y
vanish
[
21
]
.
So the metric function
H
is at most quadratic in
x
and
y
. Moreover, the terms of
H
that are linear in
x
and
y
can be eliminated by a suitable coordinate transformation
[
21
,
22
]
. Thus,
H
can be written as

\displaystyle H(u,x,y)=a(u)(x^{2}+y^{2})+b(u)(x^{2}-y^{2})

\displaystyle+c(u)~xy,

(164)

with
u
-dependent coefficients.

It can easily be checked that the only nonvanishing component of the conformal curvature tensor is

\displaystyle C_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}l^{\mu}m^{\nu}l^{\rho}m^{\sigma}=2(b+ic),

(165)

so that we have
b=c=0
. Plugging this to (
163
), we obtain

\displaystyle a(u)=\pi~\bar{\Phi}(u)\Phi(u),

(166)

so that

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-2\pi\bar{\Phi}(u)\Phi(u)(x^{2}+y^{2})du^{2}-2dudv

\displaystyle+dx^{2}+dy^{2}.

(167)

Moreover, the restricted connection and curvature
tensor are given by

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu}=-2\pi y~\bar{\Phi}\Phi~\partial_{\mu}u,~~~{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}=-2\pi x~\bar{\Phi}\Phi~\partial_{\mu}u,

\displaystyle K_{\mu\nu}=2\pi~\bar{\Phi}\Phi~(\partial_{\mu}u~\partial_{\nu}y-\partial_{\nu}u~\partial_{\mu}y),

\displaystyle{\widetilde{K}}_{\mu\nu}=2\pi~\bar{\Phi}\Phi~(\partial_{\mu}u~\partial_{\nu}x-\partial_{\nu}u~\partial_{\mu}x).

(168)

So, with the restricted metric
{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}
and

\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}
given by (
113
) we obtain
the solution of the
B_{2}
gravity which was first discovered by Cahen and Leroy
[
20
]
.
This is the unique conformally flat solution of
the Einstein-Maxwell equations which admits
a covariantly constant null vector field
[
22
]
.

VIII
Spin-One Graviton

One of the remarkable features of the restricted gravity is that the metric compatibility condition
is expressed in the form of source-free Maxwell equations. By the Poincare’s lemma, a dual
pair of spin-one fields naturally arise as
the solutions to these equations. Furthermore,
as we discussed in the previous section,
the spacetimes with the trivial topology of
B_{2}
-gravity belong to the class of pp-wave spacetimes. These facts strongly suggest that
the spin-one gauge field which appeared as
the solution to the Maxwellian metric compatibility condition can be interpreted as a representation of
the graviton that propagates in spacetime.

For the illustrative purpose, let us examine
the pp-wave spacetime in the Bondi’s coordinates
[
23
,
24
]
,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+L^{2}(e^{2\theta}dx^{2}+e^{-2\theta}dy^{2})+dz^{2},

(169)

where
L
and
\theta
are functions of
u=t-z
. To apply this to the restricted
B_{2}
-gravity, let us choose the following tetrads

\displaystyle e_{0}=\partial_{t},~~~e_{1}=L^{-1}e^{-\theta}~\partial_{x},

\displaystyle e_{2}=L^{-1}e^{\theta}~\partial_{y},~~~e_{3}=\partial_{z}.

(170)

With this we have the torsion-free spin connections given by

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu}=\frac{{\sqrt{2}}e^{\theta}}{L^{2}}\big(L^{\prime}-L\theta^{\prime}\big)\partial_{\mu}y,

\displaystyle~~~{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}=\frac{{\sqrt{2}}e^{-\theta}}{L^{2}}\big(L^{\prime}+L\theta^{\prime}\big)\partial_{\mu}x,

(171)

so that

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}e^{\theta}}{L^{3}}\big(LL^{\prime\prime}-L^{2}\theta^{\prime\prime}-2L^{\prime 2}-L^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}+2LL^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\times(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}y-\partial_{[\mu}z~\partial_{\nu]}y).

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{2\sqrt{2}e^{-\theta}}{L^{3}}\big(LL^{\prime\prime}+L^{2}\theta^{\prime\prime}-2L^{\prime 2}-L^{2}\theta^{\prime 2}-2LL^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}\big)

\displaystyle\times(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}x-\partial_{[\mu}z~\partial_{\nu]}x).

(172)

Moreover, from (
52
), (
92
), and (
170
) we have

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}=-{\sqrt{2}}L{e^{-\theta}}(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}y-\partial_{[\mu}y~\partial_{\nu]}z),

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}={\sqrt{2}}L{e^{\theta}}(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}x+\partial_{[\mu}z~\partial_{\nu]}x),

\displaystyle K_{\mu}={\Gamma}_{\mu},~~~\tilde{K}_{\mu}={\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu}.

(173)

so that the first equation of (
76
) reduces to

\displaystyle{\Gamma}_{\mu\nu}{\cal J}^{\nu\rho}={\widetilde{\Gamma}}_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\nu\rho}.

(174)

Using (
172
), (
173
), and (
174
), we obtain

\displaystyle L^{\prime\prime}+L\theta^{\prime 2}=0.

(175)

This is exactly the gravitational wave equation first obtained by Bondi
[
24
]
.

Notice that the Abelian part of the anti-symmetrized four-indexed metric
{\cal J}^{\mu\nu}
and
\widetilde{{\cal J}}^{\mu\nu}
are divergence-free so that there exist a pair of
the four-vector fields
{\cal J}_{\mu}
and
\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}

such that
{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{[\mu}{\cal J}_{\nu]}

and
\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}=2\partial_{[\mu}\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\nu]}
. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu}=-\sqrt{2}\big(\int Le^{-\theta}du\big)~\partial_{\mu}y,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}=\sqrt{2}\big(\int Le^{\theta}du\big)~\partial_{\mu}x.

(176)

To understand the physical meaning of these fields, let us consider the linearized version of the pp-wave spacetimes in which the governing equation (
175
) approximates to

\displaystyle L^{\prime\prime}\approx 0,

(177)

so that the metric (
169
) becomes

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+(1+2\theta)dx^{2}+(1-2\theta)dy^{2}+dz^{2}.

(178)

This is the plane gravitational wave of polarization
e_{+}
propagating along the
z
-direction, known
as the Rosen-Bondi gravitational wave
[
23
,
24
]
. The presence of the factor
2

in the
\theta
-terms clearly shows its spin-two character in the deformation of a test-ring on the
xy
-plane.

In the gauge-theoretical viewpoint, however, we have
the spin-one fields
{\cal J}_{\mu}
and
\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}

that satisfies the Maxwellian equation. In
the linearized theory, these fields are

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu}\approx-\sqrt{2}\big(1-\theta(u_{0})\big)(u-u_{0})~\partial_{\mu}y,

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}\approx\sqrt{2}\big(1+\theta(u_{0})\big)(u-u_{0})~\partial_{\mu}x,

(179)

where
u_{0}
is constant. The corresponding components of the anti-symmetrized four-indexed metric along
the directions of
B_{2}
symmetries
are given by

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{\mu\nu}\approx-{\sqrt{2}}\big(1-\theta(u_{0})\big)(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}y-\partial_{[\mu}y~\partial_{\nu]}z),

\displaystyle\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu\nu}\approx{\sqrt{2}}\big(1-\theta(u_{0})\big)(\partial_{[\mu}t~\partial_{\nu]}x+\partial_{[\mu}z~\partial_{\nu]}x).

(180)

This confirms that the Rosen-Bondi gravitational wave which represents the graviton at the classical level can be described by a spin-one gauge potential. This has a deep implication in quantum gravity
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

One might object this view, because the spin-one gauge field is well known to have a repulsive
(as well as the attractive) interaction. But as we have emphasized in section II, the gauge theory of Einstein’s gravity is not the ordinary
gauge theory, and thus has different dynamics.
And we emphasize that here the spin-one field
{\cal J}_{\mu}
(or
\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}
) never generates a repulsive interaction as the gauge potential
in QED. In the Maxwell theory, the exterior derivative of the Maxwell field which is linear in the Maxwell field itself, determines
the repulsiveness or the attractiveness of
the interaction. But in the restricted gravity, however, the equation of motion is different.
In particular, the acceleration of a test particle is proportional to the restricted
connection
\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}
, not the spin-one
field
{\cal J}_{\mu}
or
\widetilde{{\cal J}}_{\mu}
. And obviously,
\hat{{\bf\Gamma}}_{\mu}
shows the attractive nature of
the interaction since it is identified with
the Levi-Civita spin-connection of Einstein’s theory.

IX
Discussions

It has been well known that we can treat Einstein’s theory of gravity as the gauge of Lorentz group. Adopting this view and making
the Abelian decomposition of the gravitational connection, we can decompose Einstein’s theory
to the Abelian part and the valence part,
and can construct the restricted gravity which describes the core dynamics of Einstein’s
gravity with the Abelian part. There are two types of restricted gravity, the
A_{2}
and

B_{2}
gravity, because the Lorentz group has
two maximally Abelian subgroups
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

The Abelian decomposition reveals important hidden structures of Einstein’s theory which
we cannot see in the Einstein’s theory. In particular, the Abelian decomposition tells
that we can have a generally invariant theory of gravity, the restricted gravity, which is simpler than Einstein’s theory. However, this was done within the framework of Einstein’s theory, with the Abelian decomposition of Einstein’s theory.

In this paper we have shown that the restricted gravity can be made a theory of gravity by itself, independent of Einstein’s theory, constructing the Lagrangian formalism of
the restricted gravity. This tells that we can
view the restricted gravity as an independent theory of gravity, without any reference to Einstein’s theory. But one might ask if the restricted gravity can describe a meaningful physics.

To show that this is so, we have constructed
important solutions of the restricted gravity using
the equation of motion of the restricted gravity.
In particular,we have shown that the
A_{2}
gravity
has the gravitational cosmic string and Berttoti-Robinson solution. Moreover we have shown
that the
B_{2}
gravity has the axisymmetric pp-wave, conformally flat wave with planar wavefront, and
the Rosen-Bondi gravitational wave. This confirms
that the restricted gravity can be treated as
a respectable theory of gravity.

It should be mentioned that we have obtained
the above solutions of the restricted gravity
assuming that the restricted gravity has trivial topology. This needs clarification. When we make
the Abelian decomposition of Einstein’s theory, we have to select the Abelian direction of the Lorentz group first. And the Abelian directions in these restricted gravities can have non-trivial topology, which retains the topolgical properties of Einstein’s theory in
the restricted gravity and becomes an essential part of the restricted gravity which plays important roles in the theory.

But in this paper we have discussed the restricted gravity with the trivial topology for simplicity,
and have shown that the restricted gravity does have interesting solutions. Of course, one can ask if
the restricted gravity which has a non-trival topology also has intersting solutions. This is a very important question worth pursuing, and we will discuss this issue in a separate paper
[
25
]
.

Einstein’s theory is described by the spin-two
metric. Similarly, the restricted gravity is described by the restricted metric. So the restricted metric becomes the fundamental field of the theory. But as
we have emphasized, the restricted metric can be expressed by a Maxwell type spin-one gauge potential. In other words, the gravity in the restricted gravity
is described by a spin-one gauge potential. This may have a deep implication in quantum gravity, because this opens the possibility that the graviton could be described by a spin-one field. This could provide
an alternative way of quantizing Einstein’s gravity.

At first thought this view may sound heretic,
because the gravity is attractive while the spin-one gauge interaction includes a repulsive (as well as attractive) interaction. But as we have pointed out, this is not true. First of all, although the restricted metric is described by a spin-one gauge potential, the equation of motion of the restricted gravity is different from the equation of the gauge theory. So the two theories describe totally different dynamics. Second, the metric is not the only field which describes the graviton. It is well known that in the presence of fermions, the tetrad (i.e., four spin-one fields) describes the gravitational field. This tells that the idea of a spin-one graviton is
not heretic. Third, it is easier quantizing
the spin-one field than quantizing the spin-two
field. This is a big advantage. Moreover, the massless spin-one field has the right degrees of freedom for the massless graviton. Just as the massless spin-two metric, it has two physical degrees.

Of course, the idea of a spin-one gravition need
more discussions. Nevertheless, our study in this
paper strongly implies that this is a very interesting
idea worth to be discussed further.

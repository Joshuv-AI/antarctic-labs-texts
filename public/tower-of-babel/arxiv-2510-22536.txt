ZK Coprocessor Bridge: Replay-Safe Private Execution from Solana to Aztec via Wormhole
Jotaro Yano

Abstract
We formalize a cross-domain “ZK coprocessor bridge” that lets Solana programs request private execution on Aztec L2 (via Ethereum) using Wormhole Verifiable Action Approvals (VAAs) as authenticated transport. The system comprises: (i) a Solana program that posts messages to Wormhole Core with explicit finality, (ii) an EVM Portal that verifies VAAs and enforces a replay lock, and that exposes a normative interface which parses a bound payload secretHash ∥ m \parallel m from the attested VAA, derives a domain-separated field commitment, and enqueues an L1 → \to L2 message into the Aztec Inbox (our reference implementation (v0.1.0) currently uses consumeWithSecret(vaa, secretHash) ; we provide migration guidance to the payload-bound interface), (iii) a minimal Aztec contract that consumes the message privately, and (iv) an off-chain relayer that ferries VAAs and can record receipts on Solana. We present state machines, message formats, and proof sketches for replay-safety, origin authenticity, finality alignment, parameter binding (no relayer front-running of Aztec parameters), privacy, idempotence, and liveness. Finally, we provide a concise Reproducibility note with pinned versions and artifacts to replicate a public testnet run. Keywords: Solana, Wormhole, Aztec, Zero-Knowledge proof, Cross-chain, Replay Safety

There are four domains:

Adversary.

\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256

Security Properties

The results in this section assume the
normative binding interface
: the Portal extracts
secretHash
from the signed payload and computes
c=\mathrm{toField}(\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256(\mathsf{dom}\parallel m))
with domain separation as in Section

4.1
Parameter binding

Definition 1. Parameter binding

A Portal implementation is parameter-binding if, for any accepted VAA with hash
h
, the pair
(c,\ \texttt{secretHash})
enqueued to Aztec is a deterministic function of the VAA body and cannot be changed by a relayer.

Lemma 1. Binding via payload extraction

If the Portal extracts
secretHash
from the VAA payload and computes
c=\mathrm{toField}(\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256(\mathsf{dom}\parallel m))
from the same payload, then parameter binding holds.

Theorem 1. No relayer front-running of Aztec parameters

Under the model in Section
2
and the state machines in Section
3
, a relayer cannot cause the Portal to accept a VAA with hash
h
and enqueue
(c^{\prime},\ \texttt{secretHash}^{\prime})\neq(c,\ \texttt{secretHash})
for that
h
.

4.2
Replay-safety and authenticity

Definition 2. Replay

Submitting the same VAA body (same
h
) more than once to obtain multiple enqueues.

Theorem 2. Replay-safety at the Portal

Invariant
3.6
implies replay-safety: once
h
is in
consumed
, any second
Consume
with the same
h
is rejected.

Definition 3. Origin authenticity

All accepted VAAs must have the configured
emitterChainId
and
emitterAddress
.

Theorem 3. Authenticity

Since verification succeeds only for correctly signed VAAs and the Portal enforces the origin pair, all accepted VAAs are authentic to the designated Solana program.

4.3
Finality, privacy, and idempotence

Theorem 4. Finality alignment

If
Verify
only succeeds for VAAs produced under Wormhole’s stated finality rules, then Portal acceptance is aligned with the origin’s finality.

Definition 4. Secret privacy

Given
\texttt{secretHash}=\mathcal{H}(s)
, it is infeasible to recover
s
from public data.

Lemma 2.

Under preimage resistance of
\mathcal{H}
, secret privacy holds.

Definition 5. Commitment integrity

It is infeasible to find
(\mathsf{dom},m)\neq(\mathsf{dom}^{\prime},m^{\prime})
such that
\mathrm{toField}(\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256(\mathsf{dom}\parallel m))=\mathrm{toField}(\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256(\mathsf{dom}^{\prime}\parallel m^{\prime}))
.

Lemma 3.

Under collision resistance of
\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256
and canonical reduction to
\mathbb{F}_{r}
, collisions are negligible (up to birthday bounds).
Fixing
\mathsf{dom}
per origin further reduces cross-context risk.

Theorem 5. Privacy & binding on Aztec

Aztec only observes
(c,\ \texttt{secretHash})
.
Under Lemmas
4.3
and
4.3
,
c
is binding to
(\mathsf{dom},m)
and
s
remains hidden given
secretHash
.

Definition 6. Idempotence

For a given Solana sequence, there is at most one enqueue on Aztec and at most one L2 consumption.

Theorem 6.

Idempotence follows from Portal replay-safety (Theorem
4.2
) and Aztec single-consumption (Invariant
3.6
).

5
Liveness, Costs, and Operational Considerations

Liveness and fairness.

There exists at least one honest relayer (or user) that eventually submits each available VAA to the Portal; Aztec eventually includes L1
\to
L2 messages; and transactions are eventually mined on EVM and sequenced on Aztec. Under these conditions,
Consume
eventually fires; if the Aztec Inbox includes the message and the consumer submits
ConsumeInbox
, private consumption eventually occurs. Concurrent relayers may race; at most one succeeds per
h
by Invariant
3.6
.

Portal side (EVM) costs.

The
Consume
call cost is driven by: Wormhole verification (approximately linear in guardian signatures), event emission (linear in payload size), and the Aztec Inbox call (constant for a fixed ABI). Since
c
derives from a single
\mathrm{Keccak}\text{-}256
over
(\mathsf{dom}\parallel m)
, hashing cost is linear in
|m|
; in practice,
m
is typically small.

Relayer and Aztec.

Relayer latency hinges on VAA availability and EVM inclusion.
A retry loop is necessary for Aztec consumption because membership proofs become valid only after the Inbox leaf is included in a rollup block; bounded exponential backoff suffices under network liveness.
Aztec-side cost is dominated by L2 proof verification and any state writes in the consumer.

Threats and Limitations

Bridge trust and policy.

Guardian trust and Wormhole finality policies are assumed correct; changes in guardian sets or policy misconfigurations are out of scope.

Parameter binding in current code.

The legacy
consumeWithSecret(vaa, secretHash)
lets a relayer choose
secretHash
.
While this does not break replay/authenticity, an incorrect hash can hinder Aztec consumption until corrected.
The bound variant removes that vector.

Commitment format.

Domain separation is recommended; it reduces cross-context collision risks.
If user payloads are low-entropy, application-level salting is encouraged.

DoS surfaces.

Relayers can feed malformed VAAs; verification short-circuits these at bounded cost on EVM.
On Aztec,
consume_from_inbox
should enforce existence before heavier work.

Conclusion

We provided a formal specification of a Solana
\to
Aztec ZK coprocessor bridge via Wormhole and an EVM Portal, with explicit state machines, normative encodings, and proof sketches for replay-safety, origin authenticity, finality alignment, parameter binding, privacy, idempotence, and liveness.
We complemented the core with engineering guidance and a concise reproducibility note to replicate a public-testnet run.
This bridges academic clarity and practical deployment for replay-safe private dispatch.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field of characteristic
p
, and let
\ell
be a prime number (not necessarily distinct from
p
unless otherwise specified). The
\ell
–isogeny multigraph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(k)
is defined as follows. Its vertex set consists of the
j
–invariants of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, taken up to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–isomorphism. Given two vertices
j(E_{1})
and
j(E_{2})
, we draw an edge between them for each isogeny
\varphi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
of degree
\ell
. In particular, multiple edges may occur, reflecting the fact that there may exist several distinct
\ell
–isogenies between a fixed pair of isomorphism classes. Every isogeny
\varphi\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
admits a dual isogeny
\widehat{\varphi}\colon E_{2}\to E_{1}
of the same degree, and the compositions
\widehat{\varphi}\circ\varphi
and
\varphi\circ\widehat{\varphi}
are equal to multiplication by
\ell
on
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, respectively. As a consequence, the multigraph
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(k)
is naturally undirected. The structure of
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(k)
reflects arithmetic properties of elliptic curves over finite fields, including the behavior of endomorphism rings, the splitting of primes in imaginary quadratic fields. From a graph-theoretic perspective, the connected components of
\mathcal{G}_{\ell}(k)
exhibit striking and rigid patterns. In the ordinary case, these components are
\ell
–volcanoes
, while in the supersingular case they form highly connected Ramanujan graphs. The systematic study of these graphs and their structural properties was initiated by Kohel
[
15
]
.

Beyond their intrinsic arithmetic interest,
\ell
-isogeny graphs have attracted significant attention in recent years due to their role in post-quantum cryptography. Cryptographic protocols based on the presumed hardness of finding isogenies between elliptic curves exploit the combinatorial complexity and expansion properties of these graphs, particularly in the supersingular setting. Charles, Goren and Lauter
[
5
]
introduced a Hash function associated to an expander graph, and explored cryptographic applications. Some examples of cryptosystems include the SQISign cryptosystem
[
6
]
and SCALLOP
[
12
]
. This has further motivated a detailed investigation of the combinatorial structure of supersingular isogeny graphs, see for instance
[
7
,
2
,
16
,
1
]
.

When restricted to ordinary elliptic curves, the stratified nature of volcanoes reflects the variation of endomorphism rings under
\ell
–isogenies and is governed by the
\ell
–adic valuation of discriminants of associated quadratic orders. The height of a volcano measures how far the order generated by Frobenius is from being maximal, while the depth of a given vertex records the valuation of the conductor of the corresponding endomorphism ring. A natural problem is to understand how these invariants vary statistically, either when one fixes a finite field and ranges over elliptic curves, or when one fixes an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
and studies its reductions modulo primes. Such questions link the geometry of isogeny graphs to classical problems in analytic number theory, including the distribution of traces of Frobenius and the arithmetic of imaginary quadratic orders. In the finite field setting, counts of elliptic curves with prescribed volcano height are governed by Hurwitz class numbers, while in the global setting they may be interpreted through Galois representations and the Chebotarev density theorem.

2.
Isogeny graphs of elliptic curves

In this section, we recall the basic structure of isogeny graphs associated to the set of elliptic curves defined over a finite field
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
. The absolute Galois group
\operatorname{G}_{k}=\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{k}/k)
is topologically generated by the Frobenius automorphism
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x\mapsto x^{q}
. Let
E/k
be an elliptic curve with

j(E)=j(a,b)=1728\,\frac{4a^{3}}{4a^{3}+27b^{2}}.

The curve
E
is said to be

if
E(\bar{k})[p]=0
, and

otherwise. In the ordinary case,
\operatorname{End}(E)
is an order in an imaginary quadratic field, whereas in the supersingular case it is a maximal order in a quaternion algebra.

An
isogeny

\varphi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{1}\to E_{2}
is a nonzero morphism of elliptic curves sending
0
to
0
. It is finite of degree
\deg(\varphi)
and admits a dual isogeny
\widehat{\varphi}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{2}\to E_{1}
satisfying
\widehat{\varphi}\circ\varphi=[n]_{E_{1}}
when
\deg(\varphi)=n
. If
\gcd(n,p)=1
, then
\varphi
is separable with

. For any prime

, one has

, which contains exactly
\ell+1

\ell
, each corresponding to a separable
\ell
–isogeny. Such an isogeny is defined over

precisely when its kernel is stable under the action of
\operatorname{G}_{k}
on

. This action gives rise to the mod-
\ell

\rho_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{k}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(E[\ell])\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}).

Lemma 2.1

Let

Proof.

Let

Since

, and therefore distinct

∎

Since

is procyclic, generated by the Frobenius

The characteristic polynomial of

By the Hasse–Weil bound,

be the discriminant. According to the structure of the Frobenius action, one obtains exactly the following possibilities:

•

(\ell+1)

k

\ell
–isogenies.

•

\ell
–isogenies.

•

\sigma

•

\sigma

\mathbb{F}_{\ell}

k
–rational
\ell
–isogenies.

\begin{split}\frac{|S_{n,r}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n})|}=&\frac{(\ell-1)\,\ell^{4n-3r-2}\big(2\ell^{r+1}+2\ell^{r}-(2r+1)\ell-1\big)}{\ell^{4n-3}(\ell-1)^{2}(\ell+1)}\\
=&\frac{\ell^{\,1-3r}\big(2\ell^{r+1}+2\ell^{r}-(2r+1)\ell-1\big)}{(\ell^{2}-1)}\\
=&2\ell^{-2r}+\frac{\ell^{-2r}\big(2+2\ell-(2r+2)\ell^{1-r}\big)}{\ell^{2}-1}.\end{split}

∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime such that
\ell\neq\operatorname{char}k
and assume that
E
is a non-CM elliptic curve for which
\widehat{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is surjective. Then for

has good ordinary reduction and

\begin{split}&2\ell^{-2r}(1-\ell^{-2})+\frac{\ell^{-2r}}{\ell^{2}-1}\Big(2+2\ell-2\ell^{-2}-2\ell^{-1}-(2r+2)\ell^{1-r}+(2r+4)\ell^{-r-2}\Big)\\
=&2\ell^{-2r}+O_{r}(\ell^{-2r-1}),\end{split}

where the implied constant in

depends on

. On the other hand, if

1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-2}}{\ell+1}.

Proof.

Let

has good ordinary reduction and let

. By the Neron–Ogg-Shafarevich criterion, the representation
\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})

\sigma_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
be the image of the Frobenius element. The characteristic polynomial of

is

\operatorname{ch}(\sigma_{p},T)=T^{2}-a_{p}(E)T+p

and its discriminant is

. We note that this characteristic polynomial is independent of the choice of Frobenius element. Recall from (

) that
H(p)=r
if and only if

. Consequently,

if and only if
p\in S_{n,r}\setminus S_{n,r+1}
. Note that the set

is stable under conjugation. The homomorphism
\rho_{E,n}
gives rise to an isomorphism

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{n}]/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z}),

and let

Let

be a prime of good ordinary reduction. As a consequence of the above discussion,

if and only if

is contained in the conjugation stable set

. A result of Serre shows that the set of primes

has good ordinary reduction is

. By the Chebotarev density theorem, the density of such primes

\frac{|T_{n,r}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}=\frac{|S_{n,r}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}-\frac{|S_{n,r+1}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}.

From (

), we find that for

\frac{|T_{n,r}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}=2\ell^{-2r}(1-\ell^{-2})+\frac{\ell^{-2r}}{\ell^{2}-1}\Big(2+2\ell-2\ell^{-2}-2\ell^{-1}-(2r+2)\ell^{1-r}+(2r+4)\ell^{-r-2}\Big).

On the other hand, if

we have that

\frac{|T_{n,0}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}=1-\frac{|S_{n,1}|}{|\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})|}=1-2\ell^{-2}-\frac{2\ell^{-2}}{\ell+1}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Setting
b_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{p}]]=f_{p}/f_{0,p}
, a result of Duke and Toth

is conjugate to the mod-

\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{a_{p}+b_{p}\delta_{p}}{2}&b_{p}\\
b_{p}\frac{(\Delta_{p}-\delta_{p})}{2}&\frac{a_{p}-b_{p}\delta_{p}}{2}\\
\end{array}}\right),

where

is the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and

according to as to

.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\ell
be an odd prime such that
\ell\neq\operatorname{char}k
and assume that
E
is a non-CM elliptic curve for which
\widehat{\rho}_{E,\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{G}_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is surjective. Then for

, the density of primes

has good ordinary reduction and

is precisely
\frac{\ell^{2}+\ell+1}{(\ell+1)\ell^{3r+1}}

\frac{\ell^{3}-\ell-1}{\ell(\ell-1)(\ell+1)}

Proof.

By a well known result of Serre, the set of primes
p
at which
E
has good ordinary reduction has density
1
. Assume that
r>0
and let
D_{r}
be the set of matrices in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r+1})
which are not scalar but are scalar modulo

. It is easy to see that

|D_{r}|=(\ell^{r-1}(\ell-1))(\ell^{4}-\ell).

Let
p\neq\ell
be a prime of good ordinary reduction and write
b_{p}=\ell^{v}b^{\prime}
where

. Then since
\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is conjugate to the mod-
\ell^{n}
reduction of the matrix (

equals the smallest value of

\rho_{E,\ell^{k}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is scalar. In other words,

if and only if
\rho_{E,\ell^{r+1}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})\in D_{r}
. It is assumed that
\widehat{\rho}_{E,\ell}
is surjective, and consequently,

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{r+1}])/\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{r+1})

via the isomorphism induced by

. Let
\Sigma_{r}
be the set of elements

Let

∎

Next consider a non-CM elliptic curve

and and suppose that

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\ell\neq p
be an odd prime number and assume that
E
is a non-CM elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
k\geq 1
be as above and

. Then, the density of primes
p\neq\ell
for which
E
has good ordinary reduction and
H(p)=d^{\prime}(p)=r
is positive.

Proof.

Consider the matrix
M_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\ell^{r}\\
\ell^{r}&1\\
\end{array}}\right)\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{2r+1})
. By assumption,
M_{r}
is in the image of
\rho_{E,\ell^{2r+1}}
and let

be the conjugacy class of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{2r+1}])/\mathbb{Q})
consisting of
\sigma
such that
\rho_{E,\ell^{2r+1}}(\sigma)
is conjugate to

. If
p
is a prime of good ordinary reduction such that
\operatorname{Frob}_{p}\in C
then the discriminant of
\rho_{E,\ell^{2r+1}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
equals

. As a result,

. On the other hand, since
\rho_{E,\ell^{r}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is a scalar matrix and
\rho_{E,\ell^{r+1}}(\operatorname{Frob}_{p})
is not a scalar matrix, it follows that

∎

Let now
E_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication, and write
\mathcal{O}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{End}_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}(E)

for its geometric endomorphism ring. Then
\mathcal{O}
is an order of conductor
f
in an imaginary quadratic field
K
. Let
N_{E}
denote the conductor of
E
, and fix a prime
\ell
. For any prime
p\nmid N_{E}
, the curve
E
has good reduction at
p
, and the reduction
E_{p}
is either ordinary or supersingular. By the theory of complex multiplication, the reduction type is governed by the splitting behavior of
p
in
K
: the prime
p
is split in
K
if and only if
E_{p}
is ordinary, while
p
is inert in
K
if and only if
E_{p}
is supersingular. In particular,
E
has good ordinary reduction at precisely those primes

which split in
K
.

Assume now that
p
is a prime of good ordinary reduction which is coprime to
f
. The natural reduction homomorphism

\operatorname{End}_{\overline{\mathbb{Q}}}(E)\;\rightarrow\;\mathcal{O}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{End}(E_{p})

is always injective. Moreover, since
p
is unramified in
K
and does not divide the conductor of
\mathcal{O}
, the theory of complex multiplication implies that no new endomorphisms arise upon reduction. Consequently, this map is an isomorphism, and we obtain an identification
\mathcal{O}\;\cong\;\mathcal{O}_{p}
. It follows that for all but finitely many primes

of good ordinary reduction, the endomorphism ring of

coincides with
\mathcal{O}
. Since the values

and

depend only on

, it follows that these functions are eventually constant (for ordinary primes

). This is in stark contrast to the non-CM case.

4.
Hurwitz class numbers and average volcano depth

Fix now an odd prime
\ell\neq p
and an integer
r\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})\subseteq\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be the subset consisting of ordinary elliptic curves whose associated
\ell
–isogeny graph has height exactly
r
. Writing
q=p^{k}
, we extend the Legendre symbol by setting
\left(\frac{x}{q}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{x}{p}\right)^{k}
.

The total number of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
up to isomorphism is given by

\#\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q})=2q+3+2\left(\frac{-3}{q}\right)+\left(\frac{-4}{q}\right)=2q+O(1),

see
[
17
, Proposition 5.7]
. Our goal in this section is to study the asymptotic behavior of
\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})
as
q\to\infty
, and in particular the limiting density

\mathfrak{d}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})}{\#\mathcal{E}(\mathbb{F}_{q})}=\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})}{2q}.

In order to analyze
\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})
, it is convenient to organize elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
according to their Frobenius traces. Recall that for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
with Frobenius endomorphism
\pi_{E}
, the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–rational points is given by

\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1-t,\qquad t\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{tr}(\pi_{E}),

and that two elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
are isogenous if and only if they have the same trace
t
. We write
N(t)
for the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–isomorphism classes of elliptic curves with trace of Frobenius equal to
t
.

The discriminant
\Delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=t^{2}-4q

governs the arithmetic of the isogeny class corresponding to
t
. When
t^{2}<4q
and
p\nmid t
, the isogeny class is ordinary, and the endomorphism ring of any curve in the class is an order in the imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta})
. A fundamental theorem of Deuring identifies the quantity
N(t)
with the Hurwitz class number
H(\Delta)
associated to this discriminant. This correspondence allows one to translate questions about the distribution of elliptic curves over finite fields into problems about averages of Hurwitz class numbers over congruence conditions on
t
. In particular, since an ordinary elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
lies in
\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})
if and only if

(4.1)

v_{\ell}(t^{2}-4q)\in\{2r,2r+1\},

the quantity
\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})
may be expressed as a difference of sums of Hurwitz class numbers. This observation forms the starting point for the asymptotic analysis that follows.

We briefly discuss Kronecker (or Hurwitz) class number and their relationship to the number of elliptic curves over a given finite field in an isogeny class. Standard references include Waterhouse’s thesis
[
21
]
and work of Deuring
[
8
,
9
]
. Let
\Delta
be a negative integer with
\Delta\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
and let
B(\Delta)
be the set of integral quadratic forms
aX^{2}+bXY+cY^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[X,Y]
such that
a>0
and discriminant
b^{2}-4ac=\Delta
. A quadratic form is
primitive
if
\operatorname{gcd}(a,b,c)=1
. Denote by
b(\Delta)
the set of primitive quadratic forms in
B(\Delta)
. The group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
acts on both
B(\Delta)
and
b(\Delta)
. The
class numbers

H(\Delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\left(B(\Delta)/\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad h(\Delta)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\#\left(b(\Delta)/\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\right)

are related as follows:

\sum_{d}h\left(\frac{\Delta}{d^{2}}\right)=H(\Delta)

where
d
runs over positive integers such that
d^{2}|\Delta
and
\Delta/d^{2}\equiv 0,1\pmod{4}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
t^{2}<4q
and
p\nmid t
, we have that

N(t)=H(t^{2}-4q).

Proof.

For a proof of this result, see
[
17
, Theorem 4.6]
.
∎

As a consequence of Theorem
4.1
and (
4.1
), one has that

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})=&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t|\leq 2\sqrt{q}\\

t^{2}\equiv 4q\pmod{\ell^{2r+2}}\end{subarray}}N(t)\\

t^{2}\equiv 4q\pmod{\ell^{2r+2}}\end{subarray}}H(t^{2}-4q).\end{split}

Theorem 4.2

(Hurwitz)
.

Let
N\geq 1
be an integer and let
p\nmid N
be a prime. Fix an integer

\chi(N)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{a^{2}-4p}{N}\right),\qquad\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{N+\chi(N)}{N^{2}-1}.

Then one has the asymptotic formula

\sum_{t\equiv a\!\!\!\pmod{N}}H(t^{2}-4p)=2\delta\,p+O\!\left(N\sqrt{p}\right),

where the implied constant is absolute.

Proof.

This result above is
[
11
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\ell\neq p
be an odd prime and let
r\geq 1
. Then one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t|\leq 2\sqrt{q}\\
t^{2}\equiv 4q\!\!\!\pmod{\ell^{2r}}\end{subarray}}H(t^{2}-4q)=\frac{2\ell^{2r}}{\ell^{4r}-1}\,p+O\!\left(\ell^{2r}\sqrt{p}\right),

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|t|\leq 2\sqrt{q}\\
t^{2}\equiv 4q\!\!\!\pmod{\ell^{2r+2}}\end{subarray}}H(t^{2}-4q)=\frac{2\ell^{2r+2}}{\ell^{4r+4}-1}\,p+O\!\left(\ell^{2r+2}\sqrt{p}\right).

Proof.

We apply Theorem
4.2
with

and
N=\ell^{2r+2}
, respectively. The congruence condition

determines exactly two residue classes modulo

. Summing the contribution of each class and using the fact that
\ell\nmid p
yields the stated main terms, while the error terms follow directly from the bound in Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Theorem 4.4
.

With notation as above, one has for
r\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})=&\frac{2\ell^{2r}}{\ell^{4r}-1}\,p-\frac{2\ell^{2r+2}}{\ell^{4r+4}-1}\,p+O\!\left(\ell^{2r+2}\sqrt{p}\right)\\

On the other hand,

\#\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})=2\left(1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\ell^{4}-1}\right)p+O\left(\ell^{2}\sqrt{p}\right).

Proof.

By definition,
\mathcal{E}(r;\mathbb{F}_{q})

Using the identity

t^{2}\equiv 4q\!\!\!\pmod{\ell^{2r+2}}\end{subarray}}H(t^{2}-4q).

Substituting the asymptotic formulas from Corollary

and simplifying yields the stated expressions.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

The limiting density

\left(1-\frac{\ell^{2}}{\ell^{4}-1}\right)&\text{ if }r=0.\end{cases}

This follows immediately from Theorem

together with the asymptotic

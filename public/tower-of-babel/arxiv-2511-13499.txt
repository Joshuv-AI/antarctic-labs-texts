Uniform Feasibility For Smoothed Backup Control Barrier Functions
Anil Alan, Bart De Schutter

Abstract
We study feasibility guarantees for safety filters developed using Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) when a safe set is defined using the pointwise minimum of continuously differentiable functions, a construction that is common for the backup CBF method and typically nonsmooth. We replace the minimum by its log–sum–exp ( soft-min ) smoothing and show that, under a strict safety condition, the smooth function becomes a CBF (or extended CBF) for a range of the smoothing parameter. For compact safe sets, we derive an explicit lower bound on the smoothing parameter that makes the smooth function a CBF and hence renders the corresponding safety filter feasible. For unbounded sets, we introduce tail conditions under which the smooth function satisfies an extended CBF condition uniformly. Finally, we apply these results to backup CBFs. We show that safety of a compact (terminal) backup set under a backup controller, together with a condition ensuring safety of the backup trajectories on the relevant boundary of the safe set, is sufficient for feasibility for backup CBFs. These results provide a recipe for a priori feasibility guarantees for smooth inner approximations of nonsmooth safe sets without the need for additional online certification.



I
Introduction

Safety is the main requirement for controlling modern autonomous systems. In safety-critical control, the objective is to provide a formal guarantee that, starting from a designated set, trajectories never leave this set during operation. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) embed such guarantees as constraints in online optimization—often by penalizing the deviation from a desired control law, which leads to a design paradigm called safety filter
[
1
]
. The practicality of this framework ultimately hinges on the feasibility of the resulting optimization problem, which in turn requires a certificate that the constraint function is a valid CBF.

Converse safety theorems study conditions such that a safe set admits a CBF, therefore they can be used to address the certification problem when the safe set is represented by a continuously differentiable function.
Converse results mostly rely on compactness assumption of the set, and span stability-like conditions
[
18
]
, separation certificate via Meyer functions
[
24
]
, and robust variants
[
20
]
. Alternatives avoid compactness assumption at the expense of other restrictive conditions such as global Lipschitz constant
[
14
]
or bounded Lie derivatives
[
7
]
. Recently, a relaxed formulation of CBF, named extended CBF (eCBF), has been proposed to remove these assumptions from the converse side of necessity (of the existence of an eCBF), while retaining safety guarantees on the direct side of sufficiency
[
15
]
.

While converse safety theorems enable CBF certification, they presuppose the existence of a control law that keeps the system within the set, or equivalently, the set must be viable

Ideally, one would find the largest viable set (viability kernel), but related computations are generally intractable in high dimensions due to the curse of dimensionality

Here, a backup controller is used to propagate states forward into a smaller viable “backup set,” and the set of all states that reach this backup set (at a terminal time) without leaving the safe region is implicitly defined via a minimum over time-indexed constraints, which leads to a nonsmooth.
Since identifying a suitably small viable backup set can be achieved through convex relaxations or semidefinite programming, e.g., via LMIs

The paradigm has been extended to data-driven settings via Koopman operators

Contributions.
We bridge BCBFs with converse safety certification to find feasibility guarantees for CBF-based safety filters.
We do this by smoothing the nonsmooth pointwise minimum via the soft-min (log–sum–exp) operator and provide a range of parameters such that converse safety theorems in
[
15
]
can be employed for CBF/eCBF certification. In particular:
(i) For compact safe sets, uniform continuity can be invoked to provide CBF conditions.
(ii) For noncompact sets, we introduce explicit tail conditions under which the soft-min uniformly satisfies an eCBF condition.
(iii) In the BCBF setting, we show that safety of a compact backup set under a backup controller, combined with a condition enforcing safety along the relevant boundary of the safe set, is sufficient to certify the nonsmooth backup CBF as a CBF. This relaxes the global compactness assumption in
[

While the smoothing idea also appeared in

(27)

.

Define a set

(31)

.

Using the bounds from Lemma

,

(

Recall the decomposition of the Lie derivative

III-C

General Case

In this part we study a set of sufficient conditions implying similar results without relying on the compactness property.
In particular, since
T_{\varepsilon}
may be unbounded, the condition (
17
) is no longer sufficient to satisfy (
20
) as the uniform bounds (
24
)-(
26
) are not guaranteed.
Therefore, we distinguish between the portion where the state remains within a bounded region and the portion where
\|x\|
grows unbounded. We refer to the former as the
core
and the latter as the
tail
. This distinction is useful because the core behaves as a compact set, so the arguments and uniform bounds from the previous section already apply, whereas the tail requires additional growth conditions to ensure a uniform lower bound on
L_{F}\tilde{h}_{\theta}
.

To formalize this, we decompose
T_{\varepsilon}
using a radius
R>0
:

T_{\varepsilon}=(T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R})\cup(T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}^{\rm c}),\quad B_{R}\triangleq\{x\in R^{n}~|~\|x\|\leq R\},

(41)

where
B_{R}^{\rm c}
denotes the complement of
B_{R}
.
The set
T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}
forms the compact
core
, whereas
T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}^{\rm c}
forms the
tail
, which is unbounded for any

R>0
(if
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
is unbounded). Therefore, we introduce additional assumptions to limit the contribution of inactive terms in
L_{F}\tilde{h}_{\theta}
for the tail.

Assumption 1
.

There exist constants
\varepsilon,R>0
such that following statements are true for every
x\in T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}^{\rm c}
:

(a)

There exists a nondecreasing function
\eta:[R,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}_{>0}
with
\eta(r)\to\infty
as
r\to\infty
that satisfies

\min_{j\in\mathcal{P}(x)}\delta_{j}(x)\geq\eta(\|x\|).

(42)

(b)

There exist constants
C,p\geq 0
satisfying

\max_{j\in\mathcal{P}(x)}|L_{F}h_{j}(x)|\leq C(1+\|x\|)^{p}.

(43)

(c)

There exists a constant
r_{\infty}>0
satisfying

\min_{i\in\mathcal{A}(x)}L_{F}h_{i}(x)\geq r_{\infty}.

(44)

Assumption
1
essentially assures that the active part of
L_{F}\tilde{h}_{\theta}
(the first term in (
23
)) overcomes the inactive part (the second term) on the tail part (
\forall x\in T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}^{\rm c}
). In particular, Assumption
1
(a) implies that
w_{\theta,j}\to 0
for all inactive constraints
j\in\mathcal{P}(x)
as
\|x\|\to 0
with an
exponential rate
. Therefore, it will dominate the polynomial growth of
L_{F}h_{j}
in this portion (per Assumption
1
(b)). As a result, the inactive part in (
23
) goes to 0 as
\|x\|\to\infty
or
\theta\to\infty
.
At any fixed
x
, we can find a finite
\theta^{*}
such that the inactive term in (
23
) falls within the strictly positive margins of the uniform bound of the active part (that is,
r_{\infty}
as assumed by Assumption
1
(c)).

Remark 3
.

Assumption
1
(a) holds if (i) there is a unique minimizer for
\hat{h}
at the tail end; i.e.,
\exists i^{*}\in\mathbb{I}_{N},\mathcal{A}(x)\equiv i^{*},\forall x\in T_{\varepsilon}\cap B_{R}^{\rm c}
, and (ii) the gap
\delta_{j}
is radially coercive for inactive constraints; i.e.,
\delta_{j}(x)\to\infty
as
\|x\|\to\infty
holds for all
j\in\mathcal{P}(x)
. Under these conditions the function

\eta(r)\triangleq\min_{j\neq i^{*}}\min_{\|x\|=r}\Big(h_{j}(x)-h_{i^{*}}(x)\Big)

(45)

is well-defined, and it satisfies requirements in Assumption
1
(a).
Failing to satisfy either of these may result arbitrarily small gap
\delta_{j}\to 0
as
\|x\|\to\infty
, which prevents the dominance of the active part in
L_{F}\tilde{h}_{\theta}
.

Theorem 4

(
\tilde{h}_{\theta}
is an eCBF for general case)
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
be defined as in (
12
) with

N\geq 1

We analyze the core and tail regions separately.

Core region

The proof of Theorem

yields a critical value

V
Conclusion

We have established uniform feasibility guarantees for CBF-based controllers such as safety filters using converse safety theorems when the safe set is constructed using smoothened minima of
C^{1}
functions (via soft-min). For compact safe sets, an explicit parameter is designated for certifying the soft-min function as a CBF, which implies the feasibility of the CBF-based safety constraint. For noncompact sets, a set of tail conditions was shown to yield an extended CBF (a more relaxed formulation of CBF) with the same feasibility results. Projecting theoretical findings onto the backup CBFs, we show that strict safety of a compact backup set under a backup controller, combined with a strict safety condition along the relevant boundary of the safe set, is sufficient for feasibility in nonsmooth backup CBF formulation.

Future work will focus on relaxing assumptions of compact backup set and strict positiveness on the backup-reachable boundary of the safe set. Another direction is to investigate regularity (local Lipshitzness) and robustness properties of the smoothed BCBF framework, including sensitivity to model mismatch and sampling/discretization effects.

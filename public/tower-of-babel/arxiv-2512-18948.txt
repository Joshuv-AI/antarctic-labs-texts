A solution to the problem of the existence of a dense plastic subset X ⊆ ℝ X\subseteq\mathbb{R} of cardinality | X | < 𝔠 |X|<\mathfrak{c}
Wojciech Bielas

Abstract
. Under the assumption 𝔠 ⩾ ω 2 \mathfrak{c}\geqslant\omega_{2} , we give an example of a dense plastic subset X ⊆ ℝ X\subseteq\mathbb{R} of cardinality | X | < 𝔠 |X|<\mathfrak{c} . This answers Problem 1 of [ 1 ] .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 54E40; Secondary 03E50

1.
Introduction

For a metric space
(X,d)
, we say that
f\colon X\to X
is
non-expansive
if
d(f(x),f(y))\leqslant d(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
.
A metric space
X
is
plastic
if its every non-expansive bijection

f\colon X\to X
is an isometry.
A subset
Y
of a metric space is
plastic
if it is plastic as a subspace.

In
[
1
]
Authors have proved, among others, that every countable dense subspace of a normed space is not plastic.
This implies that, under the Continuum Hypothesis, every dense subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of cardinality
|X|<\mathfrak{c}
is not plastic.
In Problem 1 of
[
1
]
, Authors ask whether the Continuum Hypothesis is a necessary assumption.
Under the assumption
\mathfrak{c}\geqslant\omega_{2}
, we construct a dense plastic subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of cardinality
|X|=\omega_{1}
.
The subset
X
has a stronger property: the identity map is its only non-expansive bijection.

2.
The construction

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{[a_{n},b_{n}]\colon n<\omega\}
be a pairwise disjoint family of intervals of
\mathbb{R}
such that for every
m,n<\omega
:

(i)

a_{n},b_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}
,

(ii)

2(b_{n+1}-a_{n+1})<b_{n}-a_{n}
,

(iii)

if
b_{m}<a_{n}
, then
a_{n}-b_{m}>b_{0}-a_{0}
,

(iv)

a_{0}<a_{1}
,

(v)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
there exist
k
and
l
such that
b_{k}<x<a_{l}
.

Condition (ii) says that the distance between different intervals of
\mathcal{R}
is at least
b_{0}-a_{0}
.
It follows that for each
n
there exists a unique
m
such that:

•

for every
x\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
and
y\in[a_{m},b_{m}]
we have
x<y
,

•

(b_{n},a_{m})\cap\bigcup\mathcal{R}=\emptyset
.

In that case let
A_{n}\subseteq(b_{n},a_{m})
be such that
|A_{n}\cap U|=\omega_{1}
for every non-empty open
U\subseteq(b_{n},a_{m})
.
We define

X=\bigcup_{n<\omega}((\mathbb{Q}\cap[a_{n},b_{n}])\cup A_{n}).

3.
Basic properties of the set
X

For the rest of this section we fix a non-expansive bijection
f\colon X\to X
and define for every
n<\omega
:

\delta_{n}=b_{n}-a_{n},\quad a^{\prime}_{n}=a_{n}+\delta_{n+1},\quad b^{\prime}_{n}=b_{n}-\delta_{n+1}.

Let us recall that
x\in\mathbb{R}
is a
condensation
point of a subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}
if
|A\cap U|\geqslant\omega_{1}
for every open neighborhood
U
of
x
.
Thus, if
x
is a condensation point of
X
, then
f(x)
is also a condensation point of
X
.
From now on, when we say a condensation point, we will mean a condensation point of
X
.
Observe that for every
n<\omega
:

•

a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are condensation points,

•

there is no condensation point
x
such that
|x-a^{\prime}_{n}|<\delta_{n+1}
or
|x-b^{\prime}_{n}|<\delta_{n+1}
,

•

neither
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})
nor
f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})
is a condensation point.

Lemma 1
.

For every
n<\omega
, if
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}),f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})\in[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}],

then

\{f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}),f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})\}=\{b^{\prime}_{n},a^{\prime}_{n}\}.

Proof.

Points
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})
and
f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})
belong to the interval
[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
of length
b_{n}-a_{n}-2\delta_{n+1}
, hence

|f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})-f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})|\leqslant b_{n}-a_{n}-2\delta_{n+1}.

Since
f
is non-expansive,

b_{n}-a_{n}-2\delta_{n+1}=|a^{\prime}_{n}-b^{\prime}_{n}|\leqslant|f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})-f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})|.

Thus the distance between points
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})
and
f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})
equals
b_{n}-a_{n}-2\delta_{n+1}
but the interval
[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
contains only one such pair:
\{a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}\}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For every
n<\omega
, if
f(a^{\prime}_{n})=a^{\prime}_{n}
, then
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
and
f(b_{n})=b_{n}
, but if

f(a^{\prime}_{n})=b^{\prime}_{n}
, then
f(a_{n})=b_{n}
and
f(b_{n})=a_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume
f(a^{\prime}_{n})=a^{\prime}_{n}
.
We have
|a_{n}-a^{\prime}_{n}|=\delta_{n+1}
, hence
|f(a_{n})-f(a^{\prime}_{n})|\leqslant\delta_{n+1}
.
Since
a_{n}
is a condensation point, so is
f(a_{n})
, but there is only one condensation point
x
such that
|x-a^{\prime}_{n}|\leqslant\delta_{n+1}
, namely,
x=a_{n}
.
Thus
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
.
From
f(a^{\prime}_{n})=a^{\prime}_{n}
it follows that
f(b^{\prime}_{n})=b^{\prime}_{n}
and we show similarly that
f(b_{n})=b_{n}
.

The case
f(a^{\prime}_{n})=b^{\prime}_{n}
is analogous.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For every
n<\omega
, if
\{f(a_{n}),f(b_{n})\}=\{a_{n},b_{n}\}
, then

f|_{[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X}
is an isometry onto
[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X
.

Proof.

Since
\{f(a_{n}),f(b_{n})\}=\{a_{n},b_{n}\}
, for any
t\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
, the system of inequalities

(*)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|f(a_{n})-x|&\leqslant&|a_{n}-t|,\\
|f(b_{n})-x|&\leqslant&|b_{n}-t|\\
\end{array}\right.

has a unique solution, which is
t
, if
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
, and
x=a_{n}+b_{n}-t
, if
f(a_{n})=b_{n}
.
On the other hand, since
f
is non-expansive, we have

(**)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|f(a_{n})-f(t)|&\leqslant&|a_{n}-t|,\\
|f(b_{n})-f(t)|&\leqslant&|b_{n}-t|.\\
\end{array}\right.

Thus
f(t)
is the only solution of
(*)
, hence
f(t)=t
for all
t\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
, if
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
, or
f(t)=a_{n}+b_{n}-t
for all
t\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
, if
f(a_{n})=b_{n}
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For every
n<\omega
,

f|_{[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X}
is an isometry onto
[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X
.

Proof.

Fix
n
and assume that the hypothesis holds for
k<n
.
We will show that

f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}),f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})\in[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}].

Suppose that
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\notin[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
.
By the inductive assumption,

f[[a_{k},b_{k}]\cap X]=[a_{k},b_{k}]\cap X

for
k<n
.
Since
f(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}))=a^{\prime}_{n}\in[a_{n},b_{n}]
, we have
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\notin\bigcup_{k<n}[a_{k},b_{k}]
.
Thus either
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\in X\setminus\bigcup_{k<\omega}[a_{k},b_{k}]
, which means that
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})
is a condensation point and this is a contradiction, or
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\in[a_{m},b_{m}]\cap X
for some
m\geqslant n
.
Since
a_{m}
is a condensation point, so is
f(a_{m})
.
Now, if
m=n
, then
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\notin[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
implies that

f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\in[a_{n},a^{\prime}_{n})\cup(b^{\prime}_{n},b_{n}],

which means that

\min\{|f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})-a_{n}|,|b_{n}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|\}<\delta_{n+1}.

If
|f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})-a_{n}|<\delta_{n+1}
, then

|f(a_{n})-a^{\prime}_{n}|=|f(a_{n})-f(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}))|\leqslant|a_{n}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|<\delta_{n+1},

but there is no condensation point
x
such that
|x-a^{\prime}_{n}|<\delta_{n+1}
; a contradiction.
The case
|b_{n}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|<\delta_{n+1}
is analogous.
If
m>n
, then

\textstyle\min\{|a_{m}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|,|b_{m}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|\}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m}.

If
|a_{m}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m}
, then

\textstyle|f(a_{m})-a^{\prime}_{n}|=|f(a_{m})-f(f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}))|\leqslant|a_{m}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m}<\delta_{n+1};

a contradiction.
The case
|b_{m}-f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\delta_{m}
is analogous.
Finally,
f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n})\in[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
and the fact that
f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})\in[a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}]
is proved similarly.

From Lemma
1
it follows that
\{f^{-1}(a^{\prime}_{n}),f^{-1}(b^{\prime}_{n})\}=\{a^{\prime}_{n},b^{\prime}_{n}\}
.
By Lemma
2
, we have
\{f(a_{n}),f(b_{n})\}=\{a_{n},b_{n}\}
.
Then, by Lemma
3
,
f|_{[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X}
is an isometry onto
[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X
.
∎

4.
The main theorem

Theorem 5
.

The set
X
is plastic.

Proof.

Fix a bijection
f\colon X\to X
which is non-expansive.
By Lemma
4
, for every
n<\omega
the restriction
f|_{[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X}
is an isometry onto
[a_{n},b_{n}]\cap X
.
If
f(b_{0})=a_{0}
and
f(b_{1})=a_{1}
, then

|f(b_{1})-f(b_{0})|=a_{1}-a_{0}=(a_{1}-b_{0})+(b_{0}-a_{0})>(a_{1}-b_{0})+(b_{1}-a_{1})=b_{1}-b_{0};

a contradiction.
If
f(b_{0})=a_{0}
and
f(b_{1})=b_{1}
, then

|f(b_{1})-f(b_{0})|=b_{1}-a_{0}>b_{1}-b_{0};

a contradiction.
Thus
f(b_{0})=b_{0}
, hence
f(a_{0})=a_{0}
.
Fix
1\leqslant n<\omega
and assume that
f(a_{k})=a_{k}
and
f(b_{k})=b_{k}
for
k<n
.
Suppose that
f(b_{n+1})=a_{n+1}
.
If
a_{n}<a_{n+1}
, then
b_{n}<a_{n+1}
and

b_{n+1}-b_{n}=f(a_{n+1})-f(b_{n})\leqslant a_{n+1}-b_{n}<b_{n+1}-b_{n};

a contradiction.
If
a_{n+1}<a_{n}
, then
b_{n+1}<a_{n}
and

a_{n}-a_{n+1}=f(a_{n})-f(b_{n+1})\leqslant a_{n}-b_{n+1}<a_{n+1}-a_{n};

a contradiction.
Thus
f(b_{n+1})=b_{n+1}
, hence
f(a_{n+1})=a_{n+1}
.
By induction,
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
and
f(b_{n})=b_{n}
for every
n<\omega
.

Fix
t\in X
.
There exist
m
and
n
such that
a_{m}<t<a_{n}
.
Once again,
t
is the only solution of the system of inequalities

(***)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|a_{m}-x|&\leqslant&|a_{m}-t|,\\
|a_{n}-x|&\leqslant&|a_{n}-t|,\\
\end{array}\right.

On the other hand, since
f
is non-expansive, we have

(**)\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}|f(a_{m})-f(t)|&\leqslant&|a_{m}-t|,\\
|f(a_{n})-f(t)|&\leqslant&|a_{n}-t|.\\
\end{array}\right.

Since
f(a_{m})=a_{m}
and
f(a_{n})=a_{n}
, the value
f(t)
is the only solution of
(***)
, hence
f(t)=t
.
∎

Theorem 6
.

The Continuum Hypothesis is equivalent to the non-existence of a plastic dense subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of cardinality
|X|<\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

The Continuum Hypothesis and Corollary 2 from
[
1
]
imply that there is no plastic dense subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}
of cardinality
|X|<\mathfrak{c}
.

If we assume that
\mathfrak{c}\geqslant\omega_{2}
, then the set
X
is plastic, dense and is of cardinality
\omega_{1}<\mathfrak{c}
.
∎

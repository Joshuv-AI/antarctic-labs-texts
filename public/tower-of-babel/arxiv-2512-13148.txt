Central limit theorems for non-linear functionals of Gaussian fields via Wiener chaos decomposition
Fabio Coppini, Wioletta M. Ruszel

Abstract
We review and present some known results for non-linear functionals of Gaussian variables in the context of discrete Gaussian fields defined on the d d dimensional lattice. Our main result is a Central Limit Theorem in the spirit of the classical Breuer-Major theorem, together with applications to the powers of the Gaussian Free Field. Notably, we show that even powers of the discrete Gaussian Free Field converge to the Gaussian white noise, while odd powers converge to a continuous Gaussian Free Field with explicit covariance. The proofs are based on the Wiener chaos decomposition and the fourth moment theorem (Nualart-Peccati, 2005), and include a tightness result. Even if these tools are well-known in the literature, their application to Gaussian fields on the lattice appears to be new. MSC2020 Subject classifications: 60G60, 60G15, 60F05, 60F17, 33C45 Keywords: Wiener chaos expansion; Gaussian fields; Breuer-Major theorem; Nualart-Peccati criterion

Introduction

1.1
Motivation and main result

Let
d\geq 2
, for
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
N=2,3,\dots
, a natural number. Define
B_{N}(i)=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:|i-j|\leq N/2\}
a box of size
N
, centered at
i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
B_{N}
be the shorthand notation for
B_{N}(o)
with
o
the origin in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Furthermore, let
X=(X_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a stationary discrete Gaussian Field on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
\mathbb{E}[X_{o}]=0
and
\mathbb{E}[X^{2}_{o}]=1
. For every
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, denote the covariance
\rho(u)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\mathbb{E}[X_{o}X_{u}]
.
Let
H:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real valued function. For every
N
, define the field
\Phi_{N}
as

\Phi_{N}(j)\overset{\text{def}}{=}H(X_{j}),\quad j\in B_{N}.

(1)

Let
D=[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
. The action of
\Phi_{N}
on a test function
f:D\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle\overset{\text{def}}{=}N^{-d}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}H(X_{j})f\left(\frac{j}{N}\right).

(2)

If
\operatorname{Var}[H(X_{o})]<\infty
, then we have the following decomposition

H(x)=h_{0}+\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}\frac{c_{q}}{q!}H_{q}(x),\quad c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(3)

into
Hermite polynomials

(H_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
of order
q\geq m
, where
m\geq 1
is the
Hermite rank
of
H
, i.e., the smallest
q\geq 1
such that
c_{q}\neq 0
. Without loss of generality, we suppose that the field is centered, i.e.,
\mathbb{E}[\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle]=0
for every test function
f
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(D)
formed by
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator
-\Delta
on
D
with zero boundary condition, and let
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the corresponding eigenvalues. For any
\alpha>0
, the Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}(D)=(\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D))^{\prime}
is the dual of

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D)\overset{\text{def}}{=}\bigl\{u=\sum_{k\geq 1}u_{k}\varphi_{k}\text{ s.t. }\|(Id-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{k})^{\alpha}|u_{k}|^{2}<\infty\bigr\}.

(4)

The
d
-dimensional Gaussian
white noise

W
is the centered Gaussian random distribution defined for
f,g\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D)
,

\mathbb{E}(\langle W,f\rangle\langle W,g\rangle)=\int_{D}f(x)g(x)dx.

(5)

We are interested in the conditions on
\rho
and
H
such that
\Phi_{N}
satisfies a Central Limit Theorem (CLT), namely our main result is:

Theorem 1
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a standard stationary Gaussian field with covariance
\rho(u)=\mathbb{E}[X_{o}X_{u}]
for
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
H:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with Hermite expansion
H=\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}c_{q}H_{q}
and Hermite rank
m\geq 1
. Suppose that

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{m}<\infty.

(6)

Let
\Phi_{N}
be the field defined in Equation(
2
), then

\frac{N^{d/2}}{\sqrt{C_{m}}}\Phi_{N}\overset{d}{\longrightarrow}W

as
N\rightarrow\infty
in
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}(D)
for any
\alpha>d/2
where

C_{m}\overset{\text{def}}{=}\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!{c_{q}^{2}}\left(\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{q}\right)
.

CLT results for non-linear functionals of a stationary Gaussian vector are usually known as Breuer-Major theorems, see, e.g.,
[
BM83
,
CS89
,
NNP21
,
NPY19
,
NT20
]
, and involve the notion of Hermite rank, see., e.g.,
[
Jan97
,
PT11
]
.
The distributional CLT setting presented here is slightly more general than the original result
[
BM83
]
: for instance, by taking
f\equiv 1
, then the limit of Equation (
2
) after centering and rescaling, i.e., the limit of

N^{d/2}\left(\langle\Phi_{N},1\rangle-\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\Phi_{N},1\rangle\right]\right)=N^{-d/2}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}H(X_{j})-\mathbb{E}[H(X_{j})]

(7)

is known to be a Gaussian variable with explicit covariance, whenever the field covariance
\rho
is in
\ell^{m}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, i.e.,

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{m}<\infty.

(8)

More precisely, the statement of the classical Breuer-Major theorem is the following one.

Theorem 2

(
[
BM83
]
)
.

Let
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a centered stationary Gaussian field with unit variance and covariance
\rho(u)=\mathbb{E}[X_{o}X_{u}]
. Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function with Hermite expansion
H=\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}c_{q}H_{q}
and Hermite rank
m\geq 1
. Suppose that

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{m}<\infty.

(9)

Then, the normalized sums

Z^{N}_{i}=N^{-d/2}\sum_{j\in B(i,N)}H(X_{j}),\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

(10)

converge in distribution to a centered Gaussian random variable with variance

\sigma^{2}=\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!c_{q}^{2}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(u)^{q}<\infty.

(11)

Moreover, the finite dimensional distributions of the field
(Z^{N}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
tend, as
N\to\infty
, to the finite dimensional distributions of the field
\sigma Z^{*}_{i}
, where
(Z^{*}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are independent standard normal random variables.

In the last 20 years, several proofs and extensions of the above result have been proposed, see for instance, the
[
NP09
]
and the lecture notes
[
Nua19
,
Nou12
]
with the references therein. Generalizations of Theorem
2
are now present in the field of càdlàg functions,
[
Arc94
,
HN05
,
NN20
]
, selfsimilar Gaussian processes
[
CNN20
,
NT20
]
, together with precise quantitative bounds for the rate of convergence
[
NPP11
,
NNP21
]
. The literature being too vast to be fully cited, we refer to Subsection
1.2
for a general overview. The interest in the behavior of Gaussian fields and corresponding fluctuations have consistently risen in the same period, see, e.g.,
[
PT05
,
NW18
,
GOS01
,
CHRR23
,
Wu25
]
. These two fields appear to have been developed rather independently, with few results using the Wiener chaos decomposition approach to study non-linear functionals of Gaussian fields. To the authors’ knowledge, only two applications exist in this spirit, i.e.,
[
Gas25
,
MM25
]
, both targeting local non-linear functionals of the Gaussian Free Field, such as nodal volumes or level-set clusters. It should be mentioned that the work
[
CHRR23
]
proves a CLT for the square of the discrete gradient of a Gaussian Free Field by closely evaluating its
k
-point correlation function.

The aim of this work is twofold: a Breuer-Major theorem in the case of discrete Gaussian fields, which appears to be missing in the existing literature, and one application to the case where
X
is the discrete Gaussian Free Field (GFF) and
H(x)=x^{p}
, showing that even powers converge to the white noise, while odd powers do not, see Section
1.3
for more on this. For the first part, we do not look at convergence of
Z^{*}_{i}
, but we rather tackle the field
\Phi_{N}
, and show that under standard hypothesis it converges, seen as a distribution, to the Gaussian white noise. A major difference with the original result is that we do not use any combinatorial estimate. The original proof is built on top of the method of moments, i.e., the moments of a general vector
\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}Z^{N}_{j}
, with
b_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, are studied and computed using the notion of Feynman diagrams and the corresponding formula.
In the proof proposed here, we take advantage of the forth moment theorem, introduced in the celebrated paper
[
NP05
]
, where Nualart and Peccati characterize the Gaussianity of a Wiener-Itô integral in terms of its forth moment, or via a nullity condition on a sequence of carefully constructed Hilbert norms. We rely on this last characterization and show that the finite dimensional distributions of the field
\Phi_{N}
can be directly expressed as Itô integrals with respect to an underlying Wiener process.
This approach has been inspired by the lecture notes
[
Nua19
, Theorem 3.1.1]
and
[
Nou12
, Theorem 1.1]
where a simplified version of Theorem
2
is proven. We refer to Proposition
1
for the main contribution relying on the fourth moment theorem. The proof of tightness given here, i.e., Lemma
4
, also appears to be missing in the existing literature.

Remark 1
.

In principle one can obtain tightness in a more general class of distributional spaces, namely Besov spaces, as done in
[
CHRR23
]
. However, we refrain from giving a result in this generality as defining the Besov framework is rather technical.

We conclude this section by observing that the statement of Theorem
1
generalises the original Breuer-Major result; it addresses the convergence of
\Phi_{N}
as a field instead of the finite dimensional distributions of
(Z^{N}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. For instance, for any
k\geq 2
, consider
Q_{1},\dots,Q_{k}\subset D
disjoint boxes and

B_{N}^{(i)}=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:j/N\in Q_{i}\},\quad i=1,\dots,k.

(12)

Let
f^{(i)}
be the function defined by

f^{(i)}(x)=|Q_{i}|^{-d/2}1_{Q_{i}}(x),\quad x\in D.

(13)

We have that

\sqrt{|Q_{i}|}\langle\Phi_{N},f^{(i)}\rangle=N^{-d/2}\sum_{j\in B^{(i)}_{N}}H(X_{j})=\bar{Z}^{N}_{i}.

(14)

By suitably approximating the functions
f^{(i)}
with a
L^{2}
sequence, Theorem
1
yields that the vector
(\bar{Z}^{N}_{1},\dots,\bar{Z}^{N}_{k})
converges to a vector of independent standard normal random variables. As the set
B_{N}^{(i)}
is morally the same of
B_{N}(i)
for some choice of
(Q_{i})_{i=1,\dots,k}
, we can transport this result to the variables
(Z^{i}_{N})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
and obtain Theorem
2
.

1.3
Applications to the powers of the discrete Gaussian Free Field

Recently, there has been an increasing interest in studying powers of the GFF, see the works
[
CHRR23
,
CRS25
]
and
[
CCR24
]
for the connection with Fermionic fields. We now present a useful application of Theorem
1
, when applied to even powers of the GFF and study the odd powers separately.

1.3.1
Even powers

In
[
CHRR23
,
CRS25
]
, it is shown that the even powers of the gradient of the GFF converge to the white noise in an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
d\geq 3
. Here, we observe that this is mainly a consequence of Theorem
1
. Indeed, the class of functions
H(x)=x^{2p}
has Hermite rank
2
for all
p=1,2,\dots
, and the discrete GFF
has covariance
\rho
which behaves like
|u|^{-(d-2)}
.
Theorem
1
ensures that every even power of
(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
converges to the Gaussian white noise as soon as
d\geq 5
, in fact

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(u)^{2}\lesssim\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|u|^{4-2d}<\infty.

(15)

A similar argument goes through when the gradient of the field is considered, in this case
\rho(u)
behaves like
|u|^{-d}
and Theorem
1
holds for every
d\geq 2
.

Remark 2
.

The work
[
CHRR23
]
addresses a non-stationary GFF as they consider it defined on
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
U
generic open set. As discussed in the original paper
[
BM83
, Equation (1.4b)]
, the stationarity assumption can be dropped in favor of the uniform condition:
\sum_{m}|\mathbb{E}[X_{m}X_{m+n}]|^{k}<C<\infty
. In the case of the GFF, this condition is known to hold. To the authors’ knowledge, the only other work addressing a CLT for a field defined on generic domain is
[
Mil11
]
. It is also worth mentioning
[
BS13
]
which extends the Breuer-Major theorem to non-stationary triangular arrays of Gaussian vectors.

1.3.2
Odd powers

The function
H(x)=x^{2p+1}
has Hermite rank
1
for every
p\geq 1
, i.e., the first order term
c_{1}
in even Hermite polynomials does not cancel out. For instance,
x^{3}=3H_{1}(x)+H_{3}(x)
and similarly for
x^{2p+1}
for
p>1
. Let
G
be the Green’s function of the discrete Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
, solving
(-\Delta)G=\delta_{o}
, the hypothesis of the Breuer-Major theorem are not satisfied, in fact

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\operatorname{Cov}(X_{o},X_{j})|=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|G(o,j)|=\infty.

(16)

In this case the limit is a continuous Gaussian Free Field.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X=(X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be a Gaussian Free Field in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for
d\geq 3
. Let
H(x)=x^{2p+1}
for
p\geq 1
. Let
\Phi_{N}
be the field defined in Equation (
2
). Then, for any
\alpha>d/2
the centered field
\frac{N^{d/2}}{\sqrt{C_{p}}}\Phi_{N}
converges in law in
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}(D)
to a continuous Gaussian Free Field
\Phi
with covariance

\mathbb{E}[\langle\Phi,f\rangle\langle\Phi,g\rangle]=\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{2d}}f(x)g(y)G_{\textup{cont}}(x,y)dxdy

(17)

where
G_{\textup{cont}}
is the continuous Green function in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
C_{p}=G(o,o)^{p}(2p+1)!!
is the first order term
c_{1}
in the decomposition of
H=\sum_{j}c_{j}H_{j}
.

Proof.

We give a sketch of the proof as most of the tools are standard or used in a more general fashion for proving Theorem
1
. As
H
is an odd polynomial, we can write

H(X_{i})=X^{2p+1}_{i}=c_{1}X_{i}+R_{i}

(18)

where
c_{1}=\mathbb{E}[X^{2p+1}_{o}X_{o}]=\mathbb{E}[X^{2p+2}_{o}]=G(o,o)^{p}(2p+1)!!>0
and
R_{i}
is a finite sum of Hermite polynomials of degree greater or equal than 3.

Compute

\operatorname{Var}\left[N^{-d/2}\sum_{i\in B_{N}}f(i/N)X^{2p+1}_{i}\right]=c^{2}_{1}N^{-d}\sum_{i,j\in B_{N}}f(i/N)f(j/N)G(i,j)+N^{-d}\sum_{m\geq 2}\sigma_{m,N},

(19)

where
\sigma_{m,N}
is the variance contributed by the
(2m+1)
-th projection. We now claim that as
N
to infinity, (1) the first term converges to
c^{2}_{1}\int\int f(x)f(y)G_{\textup{cont}}(x,y)dxdy
while (2) the second term converges to zero. (1) is a standard Riemann sum approximation, similar to what is obtained in the first part of Proposition
1
. The proof of (2) is more involved, but follows the main ideas of the proof of Proposition
1
, i.e., equivalently said
H_{q}(X)
converges to the Gaussian white noise for any
q=2m+1
with
m\geq 1
integer.

Because
c_{1}X_{i}
is Gaussian for every
N
, and the reminder
R_{i}
converges to a white noise, the original field is asymptotically Gaussian with the same law of
(c_{1}X_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. Tightness can be proven by splitting
\Phi_{N}=L_{N}+R_{N}
, where for
f
test function
L_{N}
and
R_{N}
are defined by

\langle L_{N},f\rangle=S_{N,1}(f),\qquad\langle R_{N},f\rangle=\sum_{q\geq 3}S_{N,q}(f),

(20)

with
S_{N,q}(f)=N^{-d/2}c_{q}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}H_{q}(X_{j})f(j/N)
for every
q\geq 1
, see Equation (
29
) for more on this decomposition.
The field
R_{N}
is tight as shown in Lemma
4
. Looking at
L_{N}
, one observes that
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|\langle L_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle|^{2}=c^{2}_{1}\langle\varphi_{k},(-\Delta)\varphi_{k}\rangle\leq C\lambda^{-1}_{k}
. In particular,

\mathbb{E}\left[\|L_{N}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}(D)}\right]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle L_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle^{2}\right]\leq C\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\lambda_{k}^{-\alpha-1}

(21)

which is finite for every
\alpha>d/2-1
. Assuming the convergence of the finite-dimensional distributions, the proof is concluded.
∎

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Isonormal Wiener processes representation

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hilbert space constructed as the closure of finite linear combinations of
(u_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, with inner product defined by

\langle u_{j},u_{k}\rangle=\rho(j-k),\qquad j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(22)

Let
W
be the isonormal Gaussian process on
\mathcal{H}
, i.e., the family
W=(W(u))_{u\in\mathcal{H}}
of centered Gaussian variables indexed by the elements of
\mathcal{H}
and defined on some probability space
\left(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}\right)
. The process
W
is such that, for every
u,v\in\mathcal{H}
,

\mathbb{E}\left[W(u)W(v)\right]=\langle u,v\rangle.

(23)

In particular, for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have the representation
X_{j}=W(u_{j})
and it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left[X_{j}X_{k}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[W(u_{j})W(u_{k})\right]=\langle u_{j},u_{k}\rangle=\rho(j-k),\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

(24)

Fix
q\geq 2
and let
u_{j}^{\otimes q}
be the algebraic tensor product of
u_{j}
and
u_{j}^{\odot q}
its symmetric tensor product which respectively live in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes q}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}
. We denote by
I_{q}:\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
the isometry between
\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}
equipped with norm
\sqrt{q}\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes q}}
and the
q
-th Wiener chaos of
W
, i.e.,
I_{q}
is such that

\displaystyle I_{q}(u_{j}^{\otimes q})=H_{q}(X_{j})

\displaystyle j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(25)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[I_{q}(u_{j}^{\otimes q})I_{q}(u_{k}^{\otimes q})\right]=q!\langle u_{j}^{\otimes q}u_{k}^{\otimes q}\rangle=q!\rho(j-k)^{k},

\displaystyle j,k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

(26)

where the first two equalities are by definition and the last one is a simple computation of
\langle u_{j}^{\otimes q},u_{k}^{\otimes q}\rangle
.

2.2
Central limit theorem for the
q
-th components

We start this section by recalling the seminal result of Nualart and Peccati.

Theorem 4

(
[
NP05
]
)
.

Fix
q\geq 2
. For any sequence of elements
\{f_{N}:N\geq 1\}
such that
f_{N}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot q}
for every
N
, and

\lim_{N\to\infty}q!\|f_{N}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes q}}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{q}(f_{N})^{2}\right]=1,

(27)

the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[I_{q}(f_{N})^{4}\right]=3
;

(ii)

for every
p=1,2,\dots,q-1
,
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|f^{\otimes p}_{N}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{2(q-p}}=0
;

(iii)

as
N
tends to infinity, the sequence
\{I_{q}(f_{N}):N\geq 1\}
converges in distribution to a standard Gaussian random variable.

For every
q\geq 1
and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D)
, let
s_{N,q}(f)
be the element of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes q}
defined by

s_{N,q}(f)=N^{-d/2}c_{q}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}u_{j}^{\otimes q}f(j/N).

(28)

Recall that
H_{q}
is the
q
-th Hermite polynomial and that
I_{q}(u_{j}^{\otimes q})=H_{q}(X_{j})
for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. As
I_{q}
is a linear functional, we can define
S_{N,q}(f)=I_{q}(s_{N,q})
, i.e.,

S_{N,q}(f)=I_{q}(s_{N,q})=N^{-d/2}c_{q}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}H_{q}(X_{j})f(j/N).

(29)

Thanks to the fact that
H=\sum_{q}H_{q}
, one can (formally) write

\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}S_{N,q}(f).

(30)

In order to show that this equality is true for random variables (see Lemma
2
), we start by giving a preliminary CLT for the
q
-th component
S_{N,q}(f)
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D)
. For every
q\geq m
, it holds that

\operatorname{Var}(S_{N,q}(f))=q!c^{2}_{q}N^{-d}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(u)^{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in B_{N}\\
j-k=u\end{subarray}}f\left(\tfrac{j}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{k}{N}\right).

(31)

As
N
tends to infinity, it holds that

\langle S_{N,q},f\rangle\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}_{q}(f)),

(32)

where

\sigma^{2}_{q}=q!c^{2}_{q}\left(\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(u)^{q}\right)\cdot\int_{D}f^{2}(x)dx.

(33)

Proof.

From the definition of
I_{q}
, recall Equation (
25
), we already know that

\mathbb{E}\left[H_{q}(X_{j})H_{q}(X_{k})\right]=q!\,\rho(j-k)^{q}.

(34)

A straightforward computation of the variance yields

\operatorname{Var}(S_{N,q})=c^{2}_{q}N^{-d}\sum_{j,k\in B_{N}}q!\rho(j-k)^{q}f\left(\tfrac{j}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{k}{N}\right).

(35)

The final expression for
\operatorname{Var}(S_{N,q})
is obtained with the change of variable
u=j-k
. Observe that, for
f\equiv 1
, the last expression implies that
\sum_{j,k\in B_{N}}\rho(j-k)^{q}>0
for every
N>0
.

We now claim that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\operatorname{Var}(S_{N,q})=q!c^{2}_{q}\left(\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\rho(u)^{q}\right)\cdot\int_{D}f^{2}(x)dx.

(36)

This is a consequence of the fact that the sum
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k\in B_{N}\\
j-k=u\end{subarray}}f\left(\tfrac{j}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{k}{N}\right)
can be rewritten as

\sum_{k\in B_{N}\cap(B_{N}-u)}f\left(\tfrac{k+u}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{k}{N}\right)

Recall that
f
is continuous and bounded and, for each fixed
u
,
u/N\to 0
as
N
tends to infinity: we recognise the Riemann sum

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-d}\sum_{k\in B_{N}\cap(B_{N}-u)}f\left(\tfrac{k+u}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{k}{N}\right)=\int_{D}f^{2}(x)dx.

(37)

The claim is proved.

In order to prove the CLT for
S_{N,q}
and thus conclude the proof, we use the
(ii)
characterization of Theorem
4
with
f_{N}=s_{N,q}
. We have to prove that, for every
1\leq r\leq q-1
, it holds that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\|s_{N,q}\otimes_{r}s_{N,q}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(2q-2r)}}=0.

(38)

Fix
1\leq r\leq q-1
. Using definition (
28
) and bilinearity of the contraction, a standard computation gives

\begin{split}s_{N,q}\otimes_{r}s_{N,q}=c_{q}^{2}N^{-d}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2}\in B_{N}}f\left(\tfrac{j_{1}}{N}\right)f\left(\tfrac{j_{2}}{N}\right)\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})^{r}h_{j_{1}}^{\otimes(q-r)}\otimes h_{j_{2}}^{\otimes(q-r)}\end{split}

(39)

Hence the squared norm in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(2q-2r)}
is

\begin{split}&\big|s_{N,q}\otimes_{r}s_{N,q}\big|^{2}\\
&=c_{q}^{4}N^{-2d}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4}\in B_{N}}\Big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{4}f\left(\tfrac{j_{\ell}}{N}\right)\Big)\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})^{r}\rho(j_{3}-j_{4})^{r}\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})^{q-r}\rho(j_{2}-j_{4})^{q-r}\\
&\leq CN^{-2d}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4}\in B_{N}}\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})\big|^{r}\big|\rho(j_{3}-j_{4})\big|^{r}\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})\big|^{q-r}\big|\rho(j_{2}-j_{4})\big|^{q-r}\end{split}

(40)

for some constant
C=C(q,c_{q},\sup_{x\in[-1/2,1/2]^{d}}|f(x)|)
, recall that
f
is bounded.

Apply the elementary inequality (valid for nonnegative
a,b
and integers
0\leq r\leq q
),
a^{r}b^{q-r}\leq a^{q}+b^{q}
to the pair
a=|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})|
,
b=|\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})|
. Then

\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})\big|^{r}\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})\big|^{q-r}\leq\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})\big|^{q}+\big|\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})\big|^{q}.

(41)

Multiplying by the remaining factors
\big|\rho(j_{3}-j_{4})\big|^{r}\big|\rho(j_{2}-j_{4})\big|^{q-r}
and summing, produce the bound

\begin{split}\big|s_{N,q}\otimes_{r}s_{N,q}\big|^{2}&\leq CN^{-2d}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4}\in B_{N}}\left(|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})^{q}+|\rho(j_{1}-j_{3})^{q}|\right)|\rho(j_{3}-j_{4})|^{r}|\rho(j_{2}-j_{4})|^{q-r}\\
&\leq 2CN^{-2d}\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4}\in B_{N}}|\rho(j_{1}-j_{2})^{q}|\rho(j_{3}-j_{4})|^{r}|\rho(j_{2}-j_{4})|^{q-r},\end{split}

(42)

where the second inequality comes from symmetric considerations.

Fix the variables

u\overset{\text{def}}{=}j_{1}-j_{2},\quad v\overset{\text{def}}{=}j_{3}-j_{4},\quad w\overset{\text{def}}{=}j_{2}-j_{4}.

(43)

For a given
(u,v,w)
and a choice
j_{4}\in B_{N}
, the quadruple
(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4})
is determined. Hence, for any fixed
(u,v,w)
, the number of quadruples
(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3},j_{4})
is at most
|B_{N}|
. Therefore, we can write

\begin{split}\big|s_{N,q}\otimes_{r}s_{N,q}\big|^{2}&\leq CN^{-d}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{q}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|v|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{r}\Big)\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|w|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(w)|^{q-r}\Big)\\
&=C\Big(\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(u)|^{q}\Big)\Big(N^{-d(1-\frac{r}{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|v|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{r}\Big)\Big(N^{-d(1-\frac{q-r}{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|w|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(w)|^{q-r}\Big)\end{split}

(44)

Focus on the term with
|\rho(v)|^{r}
(the term with
q-r
is equivalent). It remains to show that for
r=1,\dots,q-1
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-d(1-\frac{r}{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|v|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{r}=0.

(45)

Fix
\delta>0
and split the sum over the sets
\{0\leq|v|\leq\delta N\}
and
\{\delta N<|v|\leq N\}
. For the first contribution, we apply Hölder’s inequality and obtain that

N^{-d(1-\frac{r}{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
|v|\leq\delta N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{r}\leq C\delta^{d(1-\frac{r}{q})}\Big(\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\rho(v)|^{q}\Big)^{r/q}\leq C^{\prime}\delta^{d(1-\frac{r}{q})},

(46)

which goes to zero as
\delta
tends to zero. Similarly, for the second term we obtain

N^{-d(1-\frac{r}{q})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\delta N\leq|v|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{r}\leq C\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
\delta N\leq|v|\leq N\end{subarray}}|\rho(v)|^{q}\Bigg)^{r/q}

(47)

which goes to zero as
N
tends to infinity because of the summability properties of
\rho
. The proof is concluded.
∎

\left(S_{1},\dots,S_{q}\right)

Lemma 2

We have that

\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}S_{N,q}(f).

(48)

As
N
tends to infinity

\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle\to\mathcal{N}\left(0,\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{2}_{q}\right).

(49)

Proof.

This result is standard and, to the authors’ knowledge, appeared for the first time in
[
HN05
]
, see also the discussion in
[
PT11
, Theorem 11.8.3]
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For any finite collection of test functions
f_{1},\dots,f_{k}\in\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}(D)
, it holds that

\bigl(\langle\Phi_{N},f_{i}\rangle\bigr)_{i=1}^{k}\;\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{d}\;\mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\Sigma\bigr),

(50)

where the covariance matrix
\Sigma=(\Sigma_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq k}
is given by

\Sigma_{ij}=\sigma^{2}_{q}\langle f_{i},f_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(51)

Proof.

It suffices to test convergence of all linear combinations. Fix arbitrary coefficients
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and set

f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}f_{i},\quad\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}\langle\Phi_{N},f_{i}\rangle.

(52)

From Lemma
2
, we have the decomposition
\langle\Phi_{N},f\rangle=\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}S_{N,q}(f)
. For each
q\geq m
,
S_{N,q}(f)
converges in distribution to a centered Gaussian variable with variance
\sigma^{2}_{q}(f)
, recall Proposition
1
. Moreover, by Lemma
1
, the limits
(S_{N,q}(f))_{q}
are asymptotically independent for different
q
. The proof is concluded by observing that
\sigma^{2}_{q}(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\alpha_{i}f_{i})=\alpha^{\intercal}\Sigma\alpha
.
∎

3.2
Tightness

The proof of Theorem
1
is concluded modulo the last lemma.

Lemma 4
.

For any
\alpha>d/2
, the sequence
\Phi_{N}
satisfies

\sup_{N}\mathbb{E}\left[\bigl\|\Phi_{N}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}}^{2}\right]<\infty.

(53)

In particular,
\Phi_{N}
is tight in
\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}(D)
.

Proof.

Let
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
denote an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(D)
formed by
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operator
-\Delta
on
D
with zero boundary condition, and let
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be the corresponding eigenvalues. Recall that the spectrum of
\Delta
is discrete,
0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots
, and that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}/k^{2}=1
. Recall the well known property of the Laplacian

\sup_{x,y\in D}\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\alpha}\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(x)\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(y)<\infty.

(54)

The squared norm of
\Phi_{N}
is given by

\|\Phi_{N}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{-\alpha}}^{2}\;=\;\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\alpha}\bigl|\langle\Phi_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle\bigr|^{2},

(55)

with

\langle\Phi_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle=N^{-d/2}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}H(X_{j})\,\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{j}{N}\bigr).

Using the Hermite expansion
H=\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}c_{q}H_{q}
and the orthogonality properties of
H_{q}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl|\langle\Phi_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle\bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle=N^{-d}\sum_{j,\ell\in B_{N}}\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{j}{N}\bigr)\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{\ell}{N}\bigr)\,\mathbb{E}\bigl[H(X_{j})H(X_{\ell})\bigr]

\displaystyle=N^{-d}\sum_{j,\ell\in B_{N}}\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{j}{N}\bigr)\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{\ell}{N}\bigr)\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!\,c_{q}^{\,2}\,\rho(j-\ell)^{q}.

(56)

We now insert (
56
) into (
55
) and take the expected value:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigl[\|\Phi_{N}\|_{H^{-\alpha}}^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\alpha}\mathbb{E}\bigl|\langle\Phi_{N},\varphi_{k}\rangle\bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle=N^{-d}\sum_{j,\ell\in B_{N}}\Bigl(\sum_{k\geq 1}(1+\lambda_{k})^{-\alpha}\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{j}{N}\bigr)\varphi_{k}\!\bigl(\tfrac{\ell}{N}\bigr)\Bigr)\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!\,c_{q}^{\,2}\,|\rho(j-\ell)|^{q}

(57)

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-d}\sum_{j,\ell\in B_{N}}\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!\,c_{q}^{\,2}\,\rho(j-\ell)^{q}

(58)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{q=m}^{\infty}q!\,c_{q}^{\,2}\sum_{j\in B_{N}}|\rho(j)|^{q}<\infty,

(59)

where in the third step we have used Equation (
54
). The proof is concluded with a standard application of Markov’s inequality.
∎

10018- ACT-2 REGIONAL HEALTH DISPARITIES IN THE ERA OF COMPLEX GLIOBLASTOMA TREATMENT
Authors: Yasutomo Momii, Nobuhiro Hata, Hirotaka Fudaba, Hajime Yonezawa, Naoki Shinojima, Tetsuya Negoto, Kenta Ujifuku, Hideki Nagamine, Daisuke Kuga, Yukiko Nakahara, Shinji Yamashita, Yoshiteru Nakano, Toshiyuki Emoto, Ryosuke Hanaya, Akitake Mukasa, Motohiro Morioka, Takayuki Matsuo, Tadashi Hamasaki, Koji Yoshimoto, Tatsuya Abe, Yoshiko Okita, Junkoh Yamamoto, Hiroshi Abe, Minoru Fujiki, Research Group Kyushu Brain Tumour
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractGlioblastoma, a highly aggressive brain tumor, typically has a poor prognosis. However, advancements in combined treatments have significantly improved patients’ activities of daily living (ADL) and overall prognosis. Despite these advancements, patients in regional areas face challenges. Frequent hospital visits for BEV (bevacizumab) administration from remote locations often hinder the continuation of early treatment, leading to poorer outcomes. This study, conducted by the Kyushu Brain Tumour Study Group, analyzed data from patients treated at 11 hospitals in Kyushu between 2010 and 2023. The study compared survival rates based on the distance of patients’ residences from treatment centers. Prognostic factors were evaluated using both univariate and multivariate analyses. The Kaplan-Meier method was employed to compare survival rates. The findings indicated that rural glioblastoma patients may experience worse prognoses due to the long travel distances required for treatment. While consolidating treatment facilities is ideal, ensuring that such facilities are within accessible distances in rural areas is crucial for improving patient outcomes.

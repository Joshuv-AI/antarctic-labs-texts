Abstract

The high temperature equilibrium partition function of a real scalar field
nonminimally coupled to the scalar curvature is computed at second order in the derivative expansion on a generic stationary
background. Using covariant perturbation theory,
the
expression of the thermal partition function at
second order in powers of curvatures is also obtained, including its nonlocal contributions.
For conformal coupling, the Weyl anomaly
at fourth order in derivatives and second order in curvatures is evaluated
using both expansions and the results found to be consistent.

2
Equilibrium scalar partition function to second order in derivatives

We consider a
massless scalar field
\varphi(x)\equiv\varphi(t,\mathbf{x})

on a curved spacetime with action

\displaystyle S=-{1\over 2}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-G}\,\varphi\big(\Box-\xi\mathscr{R}\big)\varphi,

(2.1)

where
\Box
is the four-dimensional Laplace-Beltrami
operator,
\mathscr{R}
the four-dimensional
scalar curvature, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
the nonminimal coupling constant.
We moreover
assume a generic stationary background described by the line element

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=G_{\mu\nu}(\mathbf{x})dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-e^{2\sigma(\mathbf{x})}\big[dt+a_{i}(\mathbf{x})dx^{i}\big]^{2}+g_{ij}(\mathbf{x})dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(2.2)

In the context of hydrodynamics,
the metric functions
\sigma(\mathbf{x})
and
a_{i}(\mathbf{x})

are related to the fluid velocity, vorticity, acceleration,
and local (Tolman) temperature,
while the transverse metric
g_{ij}(\mathbf{x})
is the source of the
energy-momentum current
[
15
]
. The ultrastatic case considered
in ref.
[
18
]
corresponds to setting
\sigma(\mathbf{x})=a_{i}(\mathbf{x})=0
.

Using the
imaginary time formalism
[
20
]
,
the finite temperature equilibrium partition function is computed as

\displaystyle\log Z(\beta_{0})

\displaystyle={1\over 2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm tr\,}\log\big(-\Box+\xi\mathscr{R}\big)

\displaystyle=-\left.{1\over 2}{\partial\over\partial\epsilon}\left[{\mu^{2\epsilon}\over\Gamma(\epsilon)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{ds\over s^{1-\epsilon}}{\rm tr\,}e^{s(\Box-\xi\mathscr{R})}\right]\right|_{\epsilon=0},

(2.3)

where the sum is over bosonic Matsubara
frequencies
\omega_{n}={2\pi n\over\beta_{0}}
(with
n\in\mathbb{Z}

and
\beta_{0}
the period of the Euclidean time coordinate) and
a regularization scale
\mu
has been introduced.
The four-dimensional Laplace-Beltrami
operator
\Box
acting on Fourier-transformed fields
\varphi(\omega_{n},\mathbf{x})
reads

\displaystyle\Box

\displaystyle={1\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\partial_{j}\big)-\big(2\omega_{n}a^{i}-\partial_{i}\sigma g^{ij}\big)\partial_{j}-\omega_{n}a^{j}\partial_{i}\sigma

\displaystyle+\omega_{n}^{2}\big(a^{i}a_{i}-e^{-2\sigma}\big)-{\omega_{n}\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{g}a^{i}\big),

(2.4)

whereas the four-dimensional scalar curvature
\mathscr{R}
of the
stationary metric (
2.2
) can be written as

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}

\displaystyle=R+{1\over 4}e^{2\sigma}f_{ij}f^{ij}-2g^{ij}\partial_{i}\sigma\partial_{j}\sigma-{2\over\sqrt{g}}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\partial_{j}\sigma\big),

(2.5)

with
R
the three-dimensional
scalar curvature associated with the transverse metric
g_{ij}
,
which is the one used to raise and lower spatial indices, and
f_{ij}=\partial_{i}a_{j}-\partial_{j}a_{i}
the field strength of the Kaluza-Klein (KK) gauge field.
In fact, the operator
L\equiv-\big(\Box-\xi\mathscr{R}\big)
appearing in

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=-{1\over\sqrt{g}}\big(\partial_{i}+\mathcal{A}_{i}\big)\Big[\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\big(\partial_{j}+\mathcal{A}_{j}\big)\Big]+\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle\equiv-\triangle_{\mathcal{A}}+\mathcal{Q},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle=-\omega_{n}a_{i}+{1\over 2}\partial_{i}\sigma,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle=e^{-2\sigma}\omega_{n}^{2}+\xi R+{\xi\over 4}e^{2\sigma}f_{ij}f^{ij}

(2.7)

\displaystyle-\left(2\xi-{1\over 4}\right)g^{ij}\partial_{i}\sigma\partial_{j}\sigma-\left(2\xi-{1\over 2}\right)\triangle\sigma.

Here, we have also introduced a generalized covariant
derivative
\nabla_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}\equiv\nabla_{i}+\mathcal{A}_{i}
,
such that
\triangle_{\mathcal{A}}\equiv g^{ij}\nabla_{i}^{\mathcal{A}}\nabla_{j}^{\mathcal{A}}

and denoted
\triangle\equiv g^{ij}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}

Notice as well that the field strength
of the generalized connection

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\partial_{i}\mathcal{A}_{j}-\partial_{j}\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle[\nabla_{i}^{\mathcal{A}},\nabla_{j}^{\mathcal{A}}]\varphi

4
Closing remarks

In this letter we have computed the high-temperature
equilibrium partition function of a massless scalar field on a general
stationary geometry nonminimally
coupled to the background scalar curvature, using expansions
in the number of derivatives as well as in powers of curvatures.
The results found in both cases display a dependence on
local temperature gradients and fluid vorticity, encoded in the metric
functions
\sigma(\mathbf{x})
and
a_{i}(\mathbf{x})
, thus generalizing the results
found for ultrastatic metrics to general stationary geometries and arbitrary curvature coupling.
The general expression of the Weyl anomaly at
second order in curvatures has also been obtained from the
logarithmic terms in both forms of the partition function.

There is a number of interesting problems related to the calculations
presented here waiting to be addressed.
One concerns the implications of
our results for hydrodynamics and transport.
Another issue is the extension of these results to physical
situations in which conformal invariance
is spontaneously broken
[
40
,
41
]
, as well as the possible
application of the techniques introduced in ref.
[
42
]

to incorporate the effect of
spontaneous gauge
symmetry breaking to the case of the Weyl anomaly.
These and other questions will be investigated elsewhere.

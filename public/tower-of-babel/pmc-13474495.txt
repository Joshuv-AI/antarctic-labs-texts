LUMINEX technology detects plasma inflammatory factor expression levels in children with severe and non-severe community acquired pneumonia
Authors: Qichao Yang, Jun Xie, Hunian Li, Fang Du
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThis study aimed to evaluate the clinical utility of LUMINEX technology in detecting plasma inflammatory factor expression levels (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10) in children with severe and non-severe community-acquired pneumonia (CAP), comparing its performance with traditional ELISA methods for disease severity assessment and prognosis prediction.MethodsThis study enrolled 72 pediatric patients diagnosed with community-acquired pneumonia at our institution between January 2023 and December 2024, comprising 28 severe cases and 44 non-severe cases, along with 80 age-matched healthy controls. Inflammatory marker concentrations [interleukin-1β (IL-1β), tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α), interleukin-10 (IL-10)] were quantified in plasma and serum samples using LUMINEX multiplex analysis and ELISA techniques. Comparative analyses examined inflammatory mediator expression patterns across disease severity groups and evaluated their diagnostic discrimination capacity, while prognostic assessments investigated the predictive value of plasma inflammatory markers.ResultsComparative analysis revealed significantly elevated concentrations of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 in both plasma and serum samples from severe cases compared to non-severe patients (P < 0.05), with non-severe patients also showing higher levels than healthy controls (P < 0.05). Both LUMINEX-based plasma analysis and ELISA serum measurements demonstrated comparable diagnostic accuracy for severe pneumonia identification. Prognostic evaluation indicated that patients with unfavorable outcomes exhibited increased plasma inflammatory marker levels relative to those with favorable prognoses (P < 0.05). ROC analysis showed superior predictive performance for TNF-α and IL-10 (higher AUC values) compared to IL-1β in forecasting clinical outcomes.ConclusionThe detection of plasma inflammatory cytokines in children with community-acquired pneumonia by LUMINEX technology is helpful to determine the condition and evaluate the prognosis of children, and it is worth promoting.Keywords: Luminex technology, ELISA, Community acquired pneumonia, Inflammatory factor, TNF-α, IL-10

Introduction

Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) is a common infectious disease that poses a significant threat to infants and young children, with a notable risk of progressing to severe pneumonia, inducing multi-organ dysfunction, and posing life-threatening complications [1–3]. The effective selection of treatment regimens and accurate evaluation of CAP are topics of concern for clinicians, as they are crucial for patient disease progression and prognosis [4]. Cytokines including IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 have emerged as robust biomarkers for inflammatory burden, yet traditional ELISA-based serum detection is hindered by limited throughput and suboptimal specificity [5, 6].

LUMINEX technology, a novel multiplex assay platform, offers several advantages over conventional immunoassays. It allows simultaneous quantification of multiple cytokines from a single small-volume sample, which is particularly valuable in pediatric populations where blood collection is limited. Moreover, custom-designed LUMINEX panels can be tailored to reflect the specific cytokine profile of interest in a given clinical context, enhancing research flexibility and relevance. While ELISA remains the gold standard for detecting low-abundance cytokines due to its high sensitivity and lower cost per analyte in single-target measurements, its sequential testing approach increases sample volume requirements and limits throughput [7, 8]. These distinctions underscore the need for direct comparative evaluations of these platforms in clinical settings.

Herein, we employ LUMINEX to characterize plasma inflammatory factor expression in severe and non-severe pediatric CAP, investigating its clinical relevance in disease assessment.

Methods

General data

A total of 72 children with CAP (diagnosed per Guidelines for the Management of Community-Acquired Pneumonia in Children [9]) and 80 age-matched healthy controls were enrolled. Exclusion criteria included: severe organ dysfunction, other pulmonary diseases, recent hormone/surgical treatment, antibiotic use within 2 weeks, or symptom duration ≥ 5 days. Ethical approval and informed consent were obtained.

Based on the Pneumonia definition of World Health Organization, 28 cases were classified as severe(cough or difficulty breathing plus one of the following: lower chest indrawing, nasal flaring, or grunting.cyanosis, severe respiratory distress, inability to drink or vomiting everything, or lethargy/unconsciousness/convulsions), and 44 as non-severe༈age 2–11 months, ≥ 50/min; 1–5 years, ≥ 40/min; ≥5 years, > 20 breaths/min) [10].

ELISA detection

At the patient’s initial diagnosis, morning venous blood samples (2mL) were collected and processed by centrifugation to isolate serum for subsequent analysis. Serum concentrations of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 were quantified using commercially available ELISA kits (IL-1β: Cat# E-EL-H0149, TNF-α: Cat# E-EL-H0109, IL-10: Cat# E-EL-H0103; Beijing Jingmei Biotechnology). All assays were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Calibration curves were generated using provided standards, and quality control samples at low, medium, and high concentrations were included in each run to ensure assay validity. The minimum detectable concentrations for the ELISA kits were as follows: IL-1β < 1.0 ng/L, TNF-α < 2.0 ng/L, and IL-10 < 1.5 ng/L, as specified by the manufacturer.

LUMINEX detection

Venous blood samples (3 mL) were collected into anticoagulant tubes and centrifuged at 1500 rpm for 5 min to obtain plasma, which was then stored at -70 °C until analysis. Plasma concentrations of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 were quantified using the Bio-Plex Pro™ Human Cytokine 3-plex Assay (Cat# 171B3001M; Bio-Rad Laboratories, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Briefly, plasma samples or standards were incubated with antibody-conjugated magnetic beads in a 96-well plate for 30 min at room temperature with shaking. After washing, biotinylated detection antibodies were added and incubated for an additional 30 min, followed by streptavidin-phycoerythrin incubation. Beads were then washed and resuspended in wash buffer, and fluorescence signals were acquired and analyzed using the Bio-Plex® 200 system (Bio-Rad). Cytokine concentrations were determined based on median fluorescence intensity values derived from standard curves. According to the manufacturer’s specifications, the minimum detectable concentrations for the Bio-Plex Pro™ Human Cytokine 3-plex Assay were 0.2 ng/L for IL-1β, 0.4 ng/L for TNF-α, and 0.3 ng/L for IL-10.

Observation index

(1) We statistically compared the expression levels of inflammatory factors (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10) across different disease conditions. (2) ROC curve analysis was employed to evaluate the diagnostic value of LUMINEX technology and ELISA detection for assessing the severity of CAP. (3) All patients underwent blood tests for diagnosis and were subsequently treated accordingly. A statistical analysis was performed to evaluate variations in circulating inflammatory markers across patient groups with distinct clinical outcomes. ROC curves were employed to determine the prognostic capability of specific cytokines, including interleukin-1β, tumor necrosis factor-α, and interleukin-10.

Prognostic assessment criteria

Clinical outcomes were assessed after 14 days of treatment. Patients were classified as having a favourable prognosis if they demonstrated clinical cure (resolution of fever, tachypnea, and hypoxemia) with no need for treatment escalation. Unfavourable prognosis was defined as the occurrence of any of the following: persistent fever or respiratory distress beyond 7 days, need for intensive care unit transfer, requirement for mechanical ventilation, development of complications (e.g., pleural effusion, septic shock), or in-hospital mortality.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was carried out with SPSS software (version 22.0). Continuous variables are presented as means ± SD, and group differences were examined via Student’s t-test. For comparisons involving three or more groups, one-way analysis of variance was applied, with post hoc testing performed using the SNK-q method, which controls for Type I error rate in multiple comparisons. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05.

Discussion

In recent years, ELISA has been used as a routine detection technique for various inflammatory proteins, but this method has limited specificity and can easily cause contamination, leading to false-positive results [14]. LUMINEX technology, which has emerged in recent years, is a novel biomolecule detection technology that enables simultaneous quantification of multiple cytokines from a single small-volume sample, which is particularly valuable in pediatric populations [15, 16]. However, its value in studying the expression levels of inflammatory factors in the plasma of children with severe and non-severe CAP has not been well characterized. The present study directly addresses this gap.

Consistent with previous reports [17–20], we found that children with CAP exhibited elevated circulating levels of TNF-α, IL-1β, and IL-10 compared to healthy controls, with further increases in severe cases.

Our investigation evaluated the comparative efficacy of distinct analytical approaches for clinical assessment. Results demonstrated that LUMINEX-based quantification of circulating inflammatory markers in plasma provided comparable diagnostic performance to ELISA-measured serum concentrations of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 in evaluating severe pneumonia progression. These findings support the clinical utility of LUMINEX platform for monitoring systemic inflammation in community-acquired pneumonia cases, offering a reliable method for disease stratification.

When considering the relative advantages of these platforms from a clinical perspective, LUMINEX offers distinct benefits including simultaneous quantification from small sample volumes, broad dynamic range, and automated data acquisition. However, ELISA remains a pragmatic choice for single-analyte testing, especially in resource-limited settings [21–23]. The choice between platforms should therefore be guided by specific clinical or research objectives, sample availability, throughput requirements, and institutional resources.

The agreement analysis between LUMINEX and ELISA revealed strong correlations for TNF-α (r = 0.91) and IL-10 (r = 0.88), but the mean bias for IL-1β (+ 12.5 ng/L) was substantial relative to diagnostic thresholds, indicating that platform-specific reference ranges are essential for clinical use. This magnitude of bias is clinically relevant, as it may lead to misclassification and inappropriate clinical decisions, particularly for IL-1β where the bias exceeds the cutoff difference between severity groups. The approximately 10-fold lower absolute values by LUMINEX reflect differences in sample matrix (plasma vs. serum) and assay systems; yet both methods demonstrated consistent relative patterns across severity groups, validating their interchangeability for disease stratification when appropriate cutoffs are applied [24, 25].

Our prognostic analysis showed that patients with unfavorable outcomes had significantly elevated levels of IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10 (Table 6). ROC analysis defined optimal prognostic thresholds: TNF-α > 52.13 ng/L (AUC = 0.811) and IL-10 > 45.13 ng/L (AUC = 0.800), both superior to IL-1β (AUC = 0.666). The combined cytokine model achieved the highest predictive performance (AUC = 0.852), supporting multiplexed biomarker approaches. Importantly, adjustment for age, sex, and comorbidities minimally affected AUC values (ΔAUC < 0.02), confirming that these cytokines are independent severity markers rather than surrogate measures of demographic factors. These findings have direct clinical implications: LUMINEX-based plasma cytokine profiling can reliably stratify disease severity and predict unfavorable outcomes in pediatric CAP, potentially guiding early treatment intensification.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the sample size, particularly for the severe CAP group (n = 28) and the unfavorable prognosis subgroup (n = 20), was relatively small. This may limit the statistical power of our analyses and the generalizability of the findings. Second, this was a single-center study conducted at one institution in China, which may introduce selection bias and limit the applicability of our results to other populations or healthcare settings. Third, while we measured three key inflammatory cytokines (IL-1β, TNF-α, and IL-10), the inflammatory response in CAP is complex and involves a much broader panel of mediators. The measurement of additional cytokines might have provided a more comprehensive understanding of the immune response. Fourth, cytokine levels were measured at a single time point (admission). Serial measurements over the course of the illness could offer valuable insights into the dynamic changes in inflammation and their relationship with treatment response and clinical outcomes. Finally, the comparison between LUMINEX and other technologies (CLIA, ECL, mass spectrometry) presented in Table 2 is based on a literature review of general technical parameters rather than a direct head-to-head experimental comparison using clinical samples from this cohort. A prospective comparative study would be needed to definitively establish the relative performance of these platforms in the specific context of pediatric CAP. Additionally, methodological heterogeneity should be noted: LUMINEX assays used plasma, while ELISA used serum. Coagulation during serum isolation may activate neutrophils and platelets, potentially altering cytokine profiles and affecting cross-platform comparability. Ideally, both assays should use identical sample types in future studies.

Conclusions

The detection of plasma inflammatory cytokines using LUMINEX technology provides objective insights into disease severity and prognosis in pediatric CAP. Despite current limitations, its multiplexing and sensitivity advantages position it as a valuable tool for research and potential clinical applications. Further validation in pediatric-specific cohorts and integration with age-adapted severity criteria are warranted.

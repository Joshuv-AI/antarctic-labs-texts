Proximality and selflessness for group C*-algebras Thanks: The author was partially supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers 24K00527, 25H00588, 25H00593
Narutaka Ozawa

Abstract
. We prove that the reduced group C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra of an infinite countable discrete group having a topologically-free extreme boundary is selfless in the sense of L. Robert. We also prove that selflessness is stable under tensor product among exact C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras and that a C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -probability space is selfless provided that it is either simple and purely infinite or simple, exact, 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} -stable, and uniquely tracial.

1.
Introduction

A remarkable property called
selflessness
is recently
introduced for a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra by L. Robert (
[
Ro
]
)
and it quickly attracted a number of researchers’ attention
(see, e.g.,
[
AGKEP
]
,
[
HKER
]
,
[
RTV
]
,
[
Vi
]
)
as it implies many important regularity properties such as
simplicity, (in the tracial setting) stable rank one,
and strict comparison.
In particular, Amrutam, Gao, Kunnawalkam Elayavalli,
and Patchell (
[
AGKEP
]
) has proved that a large family
of groups, namely acylindrically hyperbolic groups
with the
rapid decay property
and with no nontrivial
finite normal subgroups are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless
.
Here, we say a group
\Gamma
is
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless if it
its reduced group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
is selfless.

In this paper, we prove that every infinite countable
discrete group with a minimal topologically-free
extremely-proximal action is
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless.
Our method is topological, as opposed to the more
analytical one in
[
AGKEP
]
, and to construct
a kind of “tree-graded” space (
[
DS
]
) out of an extreme
boundary (see Section
3
).
Note that every
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless group
is
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-simple (
[
Ro
]
) and
whether the converse is also true is
an open problem (Problem XCI in
[
STW
]
).
We remind Kalantar and Kennedy’s theorem (
[
KK
]
) that
a discrete group is
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-simple if and only if
it has a minimal topological-free strongly-proximal action.
As the names suggest, strong proximality is weaker than
extreme proximality (
[
Gl
]
).

Definition
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

be an action on a compact topological space
X
.
The action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
(or the
\Gamma
-space
X
)
is called an
extreme boundary

if it is minimal and
extremely proximal
(
[
Gl
]
)
in the sense that for every non-empty
open subsets
U
and
V
of
X
, there is

g\in\Gamma
such that
g(X\setminus U)\subset V
.

For
x\in X
, we denote by
\Gamma_{x}\coloneq\{g\in\Gamma:gx=x\}

the stabilizer subgroup at
x
.
The action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
is said to be

topologically-free
if the set of points
with trivial stabilizer groups is dense in
X
.
Note that if an extreme boundary
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

is not topologically-free, then every proper
closed subset of
X
is pointwise fixed by some

g\in\Gamma\setminus\{1\}
.

We say a sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
in
\Gamma
is
axial
if
there is a topologically-free extreme boundary

\Gamma\curvearrowright X
with distinct points
z_{\pm}\in X

that satisfies the following two conditions.
(1): For every neighborhoods
U_{\pm}
of
z_{\pm}

one has

z_{n}(X\setminus U_{-})\subset U_{+}

eventually, or equivalently that

z_{n}^{-1}(X\setminus U_{+})\subset U_{-}
eventually.
(2): The
\Gamma
-action on
\{z_{\pm}\}
is free
in the sense that
g\{z_{\pm}\}\cap\{z_{\pm}\}\neq\emptyset

implies
g=1
.
Note that an extreme boundary
X
with
|X|>2

accommodates an axial sequence if and only if
it is topologically-free.

Theorem 1
.

An infinite countable discrete group
\Gamma
having
a topologically-free extreme boundary is

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless.
More precisely, for any axial sequence
(z_{n})_{n}
in
\Gamma

and any free ultrafilter
\mathcal{U}
on
\mathbb{N}
, the homomorphism

\Gamma*\mathopen{\langle}z\mathclose{\rangle}\to\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}},

given by the diagonal embedding of

\Gamma
into
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}

and
z\mapsto[z_{n}]_{n}\in\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}
,
induces a faithful embedding of the reduced
group
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(G*\mathopen{\langle}z\mathclose{\rangle})

into
\mathrm{C}^{*}_{\lambda}(\Gamma)^{\mathcal{U}}
.

See, e.g.,
[
Bo
]
,
[
BIO
]
,
[
FLMMS
]
,
[
IO
]
,

[
JR
]
,
[
LS
]
,
[
LBMB
]
for examples of extreme
boundaries. Every group satisfying the conclusion
of Theorem
1
is
mixed-identity-free

(see
[
HO
]
for the terminology)
and converse holds true if
\Gamma
has a faithful minimal action
of general type on a tree (
[

]
), since
such an action gives rise to an extreme boundary.
In particular, non-elementary free product groups
are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless.
For the case of amalgamated free products and
HNN extensions, see
[
FLMMS
]
and
[
IO
]
for
a characterization of topological freeness of
the corresponding Bass–Serre tree compactifications.
Non-elementarily relatively hyperbolic
groups with no nontrivial finite normal
subgroups are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless, as their Bowditch
compactifications (
[
Bo
]
) are topologically-free
extreme boundaries.
It is not clear whether this generalizes to
acylindrically hyperbolic groups (with no assumption
on the rapid decay property, cf.
[
AGKEP
]
), because their hyperbolic
structure need not yield suitable compactifications.

There are
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless groups outside
the mixed-identity-free realm.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(A_{i},\varphi_{i})
be separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability spaces.
Assume that all
(A_{i},\varphi_{i})
are selfless and exact.
Then the tensor product
\bigotimes_{i}(A_{i},\varphi_{i})
is selfless.
In particular, the class of countable discrete groups that are

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-selfless and exact is closed under direct product.

We introduce the notion of complete selflessness in Section
6
and
prove that the examples in Theorem
1
and their tensor product
are in fact completely selfless, while
no nuclear tracial
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability space is completely selfless.
The following is a partial converse to
Theorem 3.1 in
[
Ro
]
and solves Question 5.4 in
[
Ro
]
.

Theorem 3
.

A simple and purely infinite

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability space
(A,\varphi)

is completely selfless.
A simple, exact,
\mathcal{Z}
-stable, and uniquely tracial

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-probability space
(A,\tau)

is selfless.

” for a slightly abusive notation

2.
Trees and covariant representations

We recall the notion of trees and their compactification.
A tree is a connected graph without nontrivial cycles.
See Section 5.2 and Appendix E in
[
BO
]
for more
on this theme.
Here we consider a countable tree
\mathrm{T}
(of infinite degree).
The tree
\mathrm{T}
is identified with its vertex set.
A
geodesic ray
is an infinite sequence

\omega(0),\omega(1),\ldots
in
\mathrm{T}

such that
\omega(n)
adjacent to
\omega(n-1)

and
\omega(n)\neq\omega(n-2)
.
Geodesic rays
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
are
equivalent

if there are
N
and
N^{\prime}
such that

\omega(N+n)=\omega^{\prime}(N^{\prime}+n)
for all
n\geq 0
.
The
boundary

\partial\mathrm{T}
is the equivalence classes
of geodesic rays and

\bar{\mathrm{T}}\coloneq\mathrm{T}\cup\partial\mathrm{T}

is the compactification of
\mathrm{T}
(whose topology
will be introduced shortly).
We define
f\colon\mathrm{T}\times\bar{\mathrm{T}}\to\mathrm{T}
as follows.
For
s\in\mathrm{T}
, we set
f(s,s)=s
.
For distinct
s,t\in\mathrm{T}
, we define
f(t,s)\in\mathrm{T}

to be the unique point between
t
and
s

that is adjacent to
t
.
For
t\in\mathrm{T}
and
\omega\in\partial\mathrm{T}
,
we define
f(t,\omega)\coloneq f(t,\omega(n))
for any
n
large enough.
For each
t\in\mathrm{T}
, we consider a copy
\mathrm{T}_{t}

of
\mathrm{T}
(or the union of
t
and its adjacent points)
and equip
\mathrm{T}_{t}
a compact topology by taking
the one-point compactification
\mathrm{T}\cup\{\infty\}

of
\mathrm{T}
and identify the new point
\infty
with
t
.
We consider the embedding

\bar{\mathrm{T}}\ni\omega\mapsto(f(t,\omega))_{t}\in\prod_{t\in\mathrm{T}}\mathrm{T}_{t}

and equip
\bar{\mathrm{T}}
with the induced topology.
This makes
\bar{\mathrm{T}}
a second-countable
compact topological space such that every automorphism
on
\mathrm{T}
extends to a homeomorphism on
\bar{\mathrm{T}}

(Proposition 5.2.5 in
[
BO
]
).
Note that if
s\in\mathrm{T}
and
t_{n}\in\mathrm{T}
are such that

f(s,t_{n})\to\infty
in
\mathrm{T}
, then
t_{n}\to s
in
\bar{\mathrm{T}}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable discrete group acting
on a countable tree
\mathrm{T}
and
on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
C
.
Let
\pi\colon\Lambda\ltimes_{\max}C\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
covariant representation
and assume that there is a
\Lambda
-equivariant
representation of
C(\bar{\mathrm{T}})
into the commutant

\pi(C)^{\prime}
of
\pi(C)
.
Then
\pi
is continuous on the reduced
crossed product
\Lambda\ltimes_{\min}C

if for every
[t]\in\mathrm{T}/\Lambda

the restriction of
\pi
to the stabilizer subgroup

\Lambda_{t}\coloneq\{g\in\Lambda:gt=t\}
is continuous on
the reduced crossed product
\Lambda_{t}\ltimes_{\min}C
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon\Lambda\ltimes_{\max}C\to\mathbb{B}(\mathcal{H})
be given.
We denote by
\tau
the unitary representation
of
\Lambda
on
\ell_{2}\mathrm{T}
induced by
\Lambda\curvearrowright\mathrm{T}

and denote by
\sigma
the
\Lambda
action on
G
.
We claim that
\pi
is weakly contained in the representation

\tilde{\pi}
on

\ell_{2}\mathrm{T}\otimes\mathcal{H}
given by

g\mapsto(\tau\otimes\pi)(g)
for
g\in\Lambda

and

a\mapsto 1\otimes\pi(a)
for
a\in C
.
Indeed, there is a sequence

\zeta_{n}\colon\bar{\mathrm{T}}\to\mathrm{Prob}(\mathrm{T})

of Borel maps such that

\sup_{x\in\bar{\mathrm{T}}}\|g\zeta_{n}^{x}-\zeta_{n}^{gx}\|_{1}\to 0

for every
g\in\Lambda
(Lemma 5.2.6 in
[
BO
]
).
It follows that
h_{n}^{t}(x)\coloneq\zeta_{n}^{x}(t)
defines sequences
of positive Borel functions on
\bar{\mathrm{T}}
such that

\sum_{t\in\mathrm{T}}h_{n}^{t}=1
and

\|\sum_{t\in\mathrm{T}}|h_{n}^{gt}-\sigma_{g}(h_{n}^{t})|\|_{\infty}\to 0.

NB: the above sum is an infinite sum of positive Borel functions.
The
\Lambda
-equivariant representation
of
C(\bar{\mathrm{T}})
into
\pi(C)^{\prime}
uniquely
extends to a
\sigma
-normal

\Lambda
-equivariant
representation
\theta
from
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
B(\bar{\mathrm{T}})

of bounded Borel functions into
\pi(C)^{\prime}
.
By “
\sigma
-normal”,
we mean that it sends a bounded pointwise convergent
sequence to a strong operator topology convergent sequence.
We define isometries

V_{n}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\ell_{2}\mathrm{T}\otimes\mathcal{H}

by
V_{n}\xi\coloneq\sum_{t\in\mathrm{T}}\delta_{t}\otimes\theta(h_{n}^{t})^{1/2}\xi
,
or equivalently,
V_{n}
is the column operator
(\theta(h_{n}^{t})^{1/2})_{t\in\mathrm{T}}
.
Then

\|V_{n}\pi(g)-(\tau\otimes\pi)(g)V_{n}\|^{2}=\|\sum_{t}|\theta(h_{n}^{gt})^{1/2}-\theta(\sigma_{g}(h_{n}^{t}))^{1/2}|^{2}\|_{\infty}\to 0

for every
g\in\Lambda

and
V_{n}\pi(a)=(1\otimes\pi(a))V_{n}
for
a\in C
.
This proves the claim that
\pi
is weakly contained in
\tilde{\pi}
.
Thus continuity of
\pi
follows from that of
\tilde{\pi}
.

For the continuity of
\tilde{\pi}
, it suffices to consider the continuity
of the vector states
\varphi
associated with the vectors of
the form
\delta_{t}\otimes\xi
, since they form a cyclic family.
But
\varphi=\psi\circ E
, where
E
is the canonical
conditional expectation
from
\Lambda\ltimes_{\max}C
onto
\Lambda_{t}\ltimes_{\max}C

and
\psi
is the vector state associated with
\xi
.
(We do not need the fact that the natural embedding

\Lambda_{t}\ltimes_{\max}C\subset\Lambda\ltimes_{\max}C

is faithful.)
Hence the continuity on the reduced crossed product

\Lambda\ltimes_{\min}C
follows from that
for
\Lambda_{t}\ltimes_{\min}C
for
t\in\mathrm{T}

one from each
\Lambda
-orbit.
∎

for every
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra

the additional condition is equivalent to that

completely positive contractions into the
\ell_{\infty}
-sum
\prod A

map

\|\theta(x)\|\geq(1-\varepsilon)\|x\|

\|\theta(xy)-\theta(x)\theta(y)\|<\varepsilon

for those

such that

,
and
\|\theta(a)-a\|<\varepsilon

Lemma 10

For every completely existential embedding

One has
\psi_{n}\coloneq\omega\circ\theta_{n}\to\psi

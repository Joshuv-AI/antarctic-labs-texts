Non-Thermal Leptogenesis in the BLSM with Inverse Seesaw Mechanism
David Delepine, Shaaban Khalil

Abstract
We investigate the viability of non-thermal leptogenesis in the gauged U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} extension of the Standard Model (BLSM) with an inverse seesaw (ISS) mechanism for neutrino mass generation. In this framework, right-handed neutrinos typically have 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) Yukawa couplings, which induce strong washout effects and render conventional thermal leptogenesis ineffective. We demonstrate that a successful baryogenesis scenario can nevertheless be realized through non-thermal leptogenesis, where right-handed neutrinos are produced from the decay of the heavy B − L B\!-\!L Higgs boson χ \chi . We explicitly analyze the interplay between the dilution factor T R / M χ T_{R}/M_{\chi} and the washout parameter characteristic of the ISS, highlighting the tension between suppressing washout effects and maintaining sufficient reheating. We show that a viable lepton asymmetry can be generated provided the scalar mass spectrum is appropriately tuned, allowing for a reduced reheating temperature while keeping washout under control. The resulting lepton asymmetry is efficiently converted into the observed baryon asymmetry of the Universe via sphaleron processes. Our results establish that the inverse-seesaw B − L B\!-\!L model remains a predictive and robust framework for non-thermal leptogenesis and baryogenesis.

Introduction

The origin of the baryon asymmetry of the Universe (BAU) remains one of the most fundamental open problems in particle physics and cosmology. Observations of the cosmic microwave background and primordial nucleosynthesis indicate a small but nonzero baryon-to-photon ratio
3
;
40
,

\eta_{B}^{\rm obs}\simeq 6\times 10^{-10},

(1)

which cannot be explained within the Standard Model (SM). Any successful mechanism for baryogenesis must satisfy the Sakharov conditions
39
: baryon number violation,
C
and
CP
violation, and a departure from thermal equilibrium. Among the various proposals, leptogenesis provides an attractive and economical framework in which a lepton asymmetry is first generated and later converted into a baryon asymmetry through electroweak sphaleron processes.

In its conventional realization, thermal leptogenesis relies on the out-of-equilibrium decay of heavy Majorana neutrinos that are thermally produced in the early Universe. Successful baryogenesis in this framework typically requires heavy-neutrino masses well above the TeV scale,
M_{N}\gtrsim 10^{9}
GeV
15
, together with sufficiently small washout effects
22
;
23
;
16
. While theoretically appealing, such high scales are difficult to probe experimentally and are in tension with low-scale realizations of neutrino mass generation. This has motivated extensive efforts to construct leptogenesis scenarios that operate at or near the TeV scale, where a closer connection to neutrino physics and collider phenomenology can be established
11
;
36
;
4
;
8
;
19
;
17
.

Non-thermal leptogenesis provides a well-studied alternative to the thermal paradigm
32
. In this class of scenarios, the heavy right-handed neutrinos are produced out of equilibrium from the decay of a heavier field, most notably the inflaton
7
. In inflaton-induced non-thermal leptogenesis, the inflaton couples directly to right-handed neutrinos and decays into them after inflation, generating a lepton asymmetry through their subsequent decays. This mechanism naturally avoids strong thermal washout effects and allows successful baryogenesis even for reheating temperatures well below the heavy-neutrino mass,
T_{R}\ll M_{N}
. Such scenarios have been widely investigated in a variety of contexts, including minimal seesaw models, supersymmetric frameworks, and resonant leptogenesis, and have been shown to reproduce the observed BAU under well-defined assumptions on the inflaton sector, its couplings, and the reheating dynamics
7
;
24
;
31
;
25
.

While inflaton-decay scenarios provide a compelling proof of principle for non-thermal leptogenesis, they typically rely on assumptions about the inflationary sector and its direct couplings to the neutrino sector, which are often poorly constrained from a particle-physics perspective. This motivates the exploration of alternative non-thermal mechanisms in which the source of right-handed neutrinos arises from well-defined and testable degrees of freedom associated with extensions of the SM gauge structure.

A particularly well-motivated framework in this regard is the gauged
U(1)_{B-L}
extension of the Standard Model. In this theory, the existence of three right-handed neutrinos is required by anomaly cancellation, and light neutrino masses can be generated naturally via the inverse seesaw (ISS) mechanism
33
;
28
. The ISS allows the heavy neutrinos to reside at the TeV scale while accommodating realistic light neutrino masses with moderately large Yukawa couplings. As a result, the
B-L
inverse seesaw model is highly predictive and experimentally testable
27
;
26
;
1
;
20
. However, these same features lead to a severe cosmological challenge: the large Yukawa couplings induce extremely strong washout effects, rendering conventional thermal leptogenesis catastrophically ineffective, even when resonant
CP
enhancement is taken into account.

This observation strongly suggests that the origin of the lepton asymmetry in the
B-L
inverse seesaw framework must be non-thermal. In this work, we demonstrate that a successful baryogenesis scenario can be realized through non-thermal leptogenesis driven by the decay of the scalar field responsible for
B-L
symmetry breaking. In this setup, the heavy (pseudo-Dirac) neutrinos are produced out of equilibrium from the decay of the
B-L
Higgs boson, while the reheating temperature remains below the heavy-neutrino mass, leading to an exponential suppression of inverse decays and washout processes.

We show that the interplay of three key ingredients allows the observed baryon asymmetry to be reproduced: (i) non-thermal production of heavy neutrinos from
B-L
Higgs decay, (ii) a naturally suppressed reheating temperature achieved when the scalar mass lies close to the kinematic threshold for neutrino production, and (iii) resonant enhancement of the
CP
asymmetry enabled by the small lepton-number-violating parameter intrinsic to the inverse seesaw. Together, these mechanisms overcome the otherwise fatal washout effects that plague TeV-scale leptogenesis in the inverse seesaw framework.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we review the
U(1)_{B-L}
inverse seesaw model and explicitly demonstrate the failure of thermal leptogenesis due to extreme washout. Section III presents the non-thermal leptogenesis mechanism arising from
B-L

CP

II
BLSM-ISS and the Failure of Thermal Leptogenesis

The gauged
U(1)_{B-L}
extension of the SM provides a minimal and
well-motivated framework in which three right-handed neutrinos (RHN) arise naturally
from anomaly cancellation. This setup allows also for a low-scale realization of the
inverse seesaw (ISS) mechanism, where light neutrino masses are generated without
requiring ultra-heavy Majorana states.
The leptonic sector relevant for neutrino mass generation is described by the
Lagrangian
28

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{B-L}

\displaystyle\supset i\bar{\ell}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\ell_{L}+i\bar{e}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}e_{R}+i\bar{N}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}N_{R}+i\bar{S}_{1,2}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}S_{1,2}+(D_{\mu}\phi)^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\phi+(D_{\mu}\chi)^{\dagger}D^{\mu}\chi

\displaystyle\quad-V(\phi,\chi)-\Big(\lambda_{\nu}\bar{\ell}_{L}\tilde{\phi}N_{R}+\lambda_{N}\bar{N}_{R}^{c}\chi S_{2}+\text{h.c.}\Big)-\frac{\alpha}{M^{3}}\bar{S}^{c}_{1}{\chi^{\dagger}}^{4}S_{1}-\frac{\beta}{M^{3}}\bar{S}^{c}_{2}{\chi}^{4}S_{2},

(2)

where
\phi
is the SM Higgs doublet and
\chi
is the scalar field
responsible for
B\!-\!L
symmetry breaking. The parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
are free coupling constants.
Furthermore, in order to forbid a potentially large mass term
\mu\,S_{1}S_{2}
in the above Lagrangian, we assume that the particles,
N_{R}
,
\chi
, and
S_{2}
are even under matter parity, while
S_{1}
is odd. Finally,
V(\phi,\chi)
is the most general Higgs potential invariant under these symmetries, which can be found in Ref.
27
.
Radiative
B\!-\!L
breaking in supersymmetric realizations typically yields

v^{\prime}=\langle\chi\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{TeV})

26

After symmetry breaking, the inverse-seesaw neutrino mass matrix in the

(N_{R},S_{2})
basis reads

\mathcal{M}_{\nu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&M_{N}\\
M_{N}^{T}&\mu_{S}\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_{N}=\frac{\lambda_{N}v^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}},

(3)

where
\mu_{S}=\beta v^{\prime 4}/M^{3}\sim 10^{-9}\,\text{GeV}
. Consequently, one obtains quasi-Dirac heavy neutrino states with masses

m_{\nu_{H,H^{\prime}}}\simeq\pm M_{N}+\frac{\mu_{S}}{2},\qquad\nu_{H,H^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\mp N_{R}+S_{2}\right).

(4)

The light neutrino mass matrix generated via the inverse seesaw takes the form

m_{\nu}\simeq\frac{(y_{\nu}v_{\rm SM})^{2}}{M_{N}^{2}}\,\mu_{S},

(5)

where
v_{\rm SM}\simeq 174
GeV. Reproducing the atmospheric neutrino mass scale

\sqrt{\Delta m^{2}_{\rm atm}}\simeq 0.05
eV implies

y_{\nu}\simeq\frac{M_{N}}{v_{\rm SM}}\sqrt{\frac{0.05~\mathrm{eV}}{\mu_{S}}},

(6)

showing that for TeV-scale
M_{N}
and small lepton-number-violating parameter

\mu_{S}
, the Dirac Yukawa couplings are naturally of

\mathcal{O}(10^{-1}\!-\!1)
.

(9)

Resonant enhancement of CP violation occurs when

Strong Washout and Breakdown of Thermal Leptogenesis

The efficiency of thermal leptogenesis is governed by the decay parameter

K=\frac{\Gamma_{D_{1}}}{H}\Big|_{T=M_{N}},\qquad Y_{B}\simeq-1.4\times 10^{-3}\,\kappa(K)\,\epsilon,

(11)

where
\kappa(K)
encodes washout effects
12
. The Hubble
rate in standard cosmology is

H\simeq 1.66\,\sqrt{g_{\ast}}\,\frac{T^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}},\qquad g_{\ast}\simeq\mathcal{O}(10^{2}).

(12)

For TeV-scale heavy neutrinos with
y_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}(1)
, one finds

H\sim 10^{-12}\ \mathrm{GeV},\qquad K\sim 10^{12}\!-\!10^{14},

(13)

placing the system deep in the strong-washout regime. In this limit:

•

Inverse decays (
LH\rightarrow N
) remain fully active in the thermal bath.

•

The efficiency factor is exponentially suppressed,

\kappa(K)\sim 10^{-15}
.

•

Even with maximal resonant CP asymmetry,

\epsilon_{\rm CP}\sim 0.5

\eta_{B}\sim 10^{-16}
, far below the observed value

\eta_{B}^{\rm obs}\simeq 6\times 10^{-10}
.

We therefore conclude that
thermal leptogenesis is catastrophically
ineffective in the
B\!-\!L
inverse seesaw framework
. This failure is driven by
the unavoidable combination of large Yukawa couplings and extreme washout,
independently of possible resonant CP enhancement.

This observation strongly motivates non-thermal leptogenesis scenarios, in which
right-handed neutrinos are produced out of equilibrium

;
9
;
10
;
2

decay of the heavy
B\!-\!L
Higgs scalar.

III
Non-thermal leptogenesis from
B\!-\!L
Higgs decay

As shown in the previous section,
thermal
leptogenesis is catastrophically
ineffective in the inverse-seesaw (ISS)
B\!-\!L
framework: the Yukawa couplings
required to reproduce
m_{\nu}
at the TeV scale are typically

y_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}(10^{-1}\!-\!1)
, implying an enormous washout parameter

K\sim 10^{12}\!-\!10^{14}
and hence an exponentially suppressed efficiency.
This motivates
non-thermal leptogenesis
, where the heavy (pseudo-Dirac)
neutrinos are produced out of equilibrium through the decay of the
B\!-\!L

breaking scalar
\chi
, while the reheating temperature satisfies

T_{R}<M_{N}
so that inverse decays are Boltzmann suppressed.

III.1
Basic idea and parametric requirements

Non-thermal leptogenesis avoids (or strongly reduces) the thermal problems by
engineering the following conditions:

•

Low reheating,
T_{R}<M_{N}
:
the Universe reheats to a temperature
below the heavy-neutrino mass, so that
LH\to N
inverse decays and related
washout processes are kinematically disfavored and Boltzmann suppressed.

•

Controlled washout,
K_{\rm eff}\ll K
:
the effective washout is
reduced relative to the thermal value by a factor that scales as

\exp(-M_{N}/T_{R})
, protecting the generated asymmetry.

•

Successful yield and asymmetry:
combining an enhanced CP
asymmetry (e.g. resonant
\epsilon_{\rm CP}\sim 0.1
–
0.3
) with a protected
efficiency
\kappa
and a sizable non-thermal RHN yield can reproduce the
observed baryon asymmetry
\eta_{B}^{\rm obs}\simeq 6\times 10^{-10}
.

III.2
Reheating temperature and non-thermal RHN yield

We assume that after inflation the energy density is dominated by coherent
oscillations of the scalar field
\chi
(matter-dominated era) and that reheating
occurs when
H\simeq\Gamma_{\chi}
, where
\Gamma_{\chi}
is the total decay width of

\chi
. The reheating temperature is then
30
;
29
;
5

T_{R}\simeq\left(\frac{90}{8\pi^{3}g_{*}(T_{R})}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{\Gamma_{\chi}M_{\rm Pl}}\,,

(14)

with
g_{*}(T_{R})\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{2})
.
If a fraction of
\chi
decays produces heavy neutrinos,

\chi\to NN
(here
N
collectively denotes the heavy ISS states), the RHN yield
at reheating is approximately

Y_{N}^{\rm non\text{-}th}\equiv\frac{n_{N}}{s}\;\simeq\;\frac{3}{2}\,{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)\,\frac{T_{R}}{M_{\chi}}\,,

(15)

where
s
is the entropy density and
{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)
is the branching ratio
into heavy neutrinos. Equation (
15
) makes explicit the

dilution
factor
T_{R}/M_{\chi}
discussed previously: lowering
T_{R}
helps
washout, but it also reduces the injected RHN abundance.

III.3
Threshold Suppression and the scalar mass spectrum

The crucial requirement for suppressing washout is to keep
T_{R}
safely below

M_{N}
. Since
T_{R}\propto\sqrt{\Gamma_{\chi}}
, lowering
T_{R}

only
by making
the coupling in
\Gamma_{\chi}
tiny is inefficient and, more importantly, often
destroys
{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)
by allowing other decay channels to dominate.

In the
B\!-\!L
model the heavy-neutrino mass originates from the
\chi
VEV:

\mathcal{L}\supset-Y_{N}\,\chi\,\overline{N^{c}}N\;+\;\text{h.c.},\qquad M_{N}=\frac{Y_{N}v^{\prime}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(16)

so taking
Y_{N}
extremely small while keeping
M_{N}\sim\text{TeV}
would require an
unacceptably large
v^{\prime}=\langle\chi\rangle
, pushing the
B\!-\!L
breaking scale
far above the TeV range and undermining one of the primary motivations for the
ISS
B\!-\!L
scenario.

A more efficient and natural approach is to suppress
\Gamma(\chi\to NN)
through

phase space
by taking
M_{\chi}
close to threshold
7
;
24
;
25
:

M_{\chi}\;\simeq\;2M_{N}+\delta,\qquad\delta\ll M_{N}\,.

(17)

For a scalar decaying into two (approximately) fermionic states, the partial width
scales as

\Gamma(\chi\to NN)\;\propto\;Y_{N}^{2}\,M_{\chi}\,\beta^{3},\qquad\beta\equiv\sqrt{1-\frac{4M_{N}^{2}}{M_{\chi}^{2}}}\;\simeq\;\sqrt{\frac{\delta}{M_{N}}}\,,

(18)

so that

\beta^{3}\;\simeq\;\left(\frac{\delta}{M_{N}}\right)^{3/2},

(19)

providing a powerful suppression of
\Gamma(\chi\to NN)
(and thus of
T_{R}
)
without forcing
Y_{N}
to be unnaturally tiny. This mechanism keeps
T_{R}
strictly below
M_{N}
, which is precisely what
ensures an exponential reduction of washout. It is also a strong signatures of the non-thermal leptogenesis scenario. In this scenario, the scalar mass spectrum is needed to be appropriately tuned allowing for a reduced reheating temperature while keeping washout under control and keeping the advantage of the ISS B-L model.

III.4
Washout: from
K
to an effective
K_{\rm eff}

For reference, the decay width of a heavy neutrino into a lepton and Higgs,

N\to LH
, is

\Gamma_{D}\simeq\frac{1}{8\pi}(y_{\nu}^{\dagger}y_{\nu})\,M_{N},

(20)

and the Hubble rate in radiation domination is

H(T)=1.66\,\sqrt{g_{*}}\,\frac{T^{2}}{M_{\rm Pl}}\,.

(21)

In thermal leptogenesis one evaluates
K=\Gamma_{D}/H
at
T=M_{N}
, obtaining

K\equiv\frac{\Gamma_{D}}{H}\Big|_{T=M_{N}}\simeq\frac{(y_{\nu}^{\dagger}y_{\nu})\,M_{\rm Pl}}{8\pi\cdot 1.66\,\sqrt{g_{*}}\,M_{N}}\,,

(22)

which becomes enormous for
y_{\nu}=\mathcal{O}(10^{-1}\!-\!1)
and
M_{N}\sim\text{TeV}
.

In the non-thermal case, however, the relevant washout operates around

T\simeq T_{R}<M_{N}
. The inverse decays are then Boltzmann suppressed, and a useful
parametric estimate is
25

K_{\rm eff}\;\simeq\;K\;\exp\!\left(-\frac{M_{N}}{T_{R}}\right),

(23)

which captures the essential physics: even if
K
is huge,
K_{\rm eff}
can be
brought under control once
T_{R}
is sufficiently below
M_{N}
.

For the threshold regime in Eq. (
17
), the reheating temperature
inherits the strong phase-space suppression from
\Gamma_{\chi}
,

T_{R}(\delta)\;\propto\;\sqrt{\Gamma_{\chi}}\;\propto\;Y_{N}\,\beta^{3/2}\,\sqrt{M_{\chi}M_{\rm Pl}}\;\sim\;Y_{N}\left(\frac{\delta}{M_{N}}\right)^{3/4}\sqrt{M_{N}M_{\rm Pl}}\,,

(24)

illustrating explicitly how a modestly small
\delta
can enforce
T_{R}<M_{N}
while
keeping
Y_{N}
in a natural range.

III.5
Baryon asymmetry and the dilution–washout tension

The final baryon-to-photon ratio can be expressed schematically as

\eta_{B}\;\simeq\;d\;\epsilon_{\rm CP}\;\kappa(K_{eff})\;Y_{N}^{\rm non\text{-}th},

(25)

where:

•

d
is the sphaleron conversion factor (numerically
d\simeq 0.35
),
encoding the conversion of
B\!-\!L
into
B
by electroweak sphalerons,

•

\epsilon_{\rm CP}
is the CP asymmetry per heavy-neutrino decay (in the
ISS it can be resonantly enhanced when the pseudo-Dirac splitting is comparable
to the decay width),

•

\kappa(K_{eff})
is an efficiency factor controlled by washout, and is strongly
correlated with
K_{\rm eff}
,

•

Y_{N}^{\rm non\text{-}th}
is the non-thermal RHN yield of
Eq. (
15
).

Substituting Eq. (
15
) into Eq. (
25
) gives

\eta_{B}\;\simeq\;d\;\epsilon_{\rm CP}\;\kappa\;\left(\frac{3}{2}\,{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)\,\frac{T_{R}}{M_{\chi}}\right).

(26)

This expression highlights the key tension: increasing
T_{R}
boosts production
linearly via
T_{R}/M_{\chi}
, but also increases washout through
K_{\rm eff}

(roughly exponentially sensitive to
T_{R}
). The threshold trick provides a
practical compromise: it allows
T_{R}
to be small enough to suppress washout,
while keeping
{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)
potentially sizable.

III.6
Competing decay channels and the Higgs-portal constraint

A successful non-thermal scenario also requires that
\chi
decays dominantly
into heavy neutrinos. Since
\chi
is a Higgs-like scalar in an extended sector,
it can couple to the SM Higgs through a portal interaction, which
induces
\chi\to hh
decays after symmetry breaking. Parametrically one may write

\Gamma(\chi\to hh)\;\sim\;\frac{\lambda_{\chi H}^{2}\,v^{\prime 2}}{32\pi\,M_{\chi}},

(27)

so that the branching ratio into neutrinos is

{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)=\frac{\Gamma(\chi\to NN)}{\Gamma(\chi\to NN)+\Gamma(\chi\to hh)+\cdots}\,.

(28)

In the threshold regime,
\Gamma(\chi\to NN)
is suppressed by
\beta^{3}
, hence

\lambda_{\chi H}
must be sufficiently small to prevent
\chi\to hh
from
dominating and driving
{\rm Br}(\chi\to NN)\to 0

34
;
18
. This requirement is essential
for maintaining a large
Y_{N}^{\rm non\text{-}th}
and, consequently, a viable

\eta_{B}
.

IV
Washout Regimes

The survival of the lepton asymmetry is controlled by the efficiency factor

\kappa
, which is determined by the magnitude of the washout parameter
K
.
Using the effective washout factor
K_{\rm eff}
discussed in the previous
section, we analyze the relevant washout regimes numerically.

For moderately large washout,
K\sim 10^{7}
, the efficiency factor is already
strongly suppressed and can be approximated by
12

\kappa\simeq\frac{0.3}{K(\ln K)^{0.6}}\sim 10^{-8}.

(29)

This illustrates that even a mild reduction in the washout parameter can lead to
orders-of-magnitude enhancement in the final baryon asymmetry. In the thermal
regime, where
K\gtrsim 10^{12}
, the efficiency becomes completely negligible.
Therefore, achieving
K_{\rm eff}\lesssim 10^{6}
is a necessary condition for
successful leptogenesis in the inverse seesaw scenario.

V
Resonant Leptogenesis in the Inverse Seesaw

In the inverse seesaw framework, the heavy neutrinos form quasi-degenerate
pseudo-Dirac pairs. The CP asymmetry generated from the mixing of two nearly
degenerate heavy neutrinos
N_{1}
and
N_{2}
is given by

\epsilon_{CP}\simeq\frac{\mathrm{Im}\!\left[(h_{12})^{2}\right]}{|h_{11}|^{2}+|h_{22}|^{2}}\frac{(M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2})\,M_{1}\Gamma_{2}}{(M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2})^{2}+M_{1}^{2}\Gamma_{2}^{2}},

(30)

where
h=Y_{\nu}^{\dagger}Y_{\nu}
and
\Gamma_{2}
is the decay width of
N_{2}
.

Assuming maximal CP-violating phases, this expression simplifies to

\epsilon_{CP}^{\rm res}\simeq\frac{\Delta M\,\Gamma}{\Delta M^{2}+\Gamma^{2}/4},

(31)

where
\Delta M=M_{2}-M_{1}
and
\Gamma
denotes the common decay width.
The CP asymmetry reaches its theoretical maximum,

\epsilon_{CP}^{\rm max}=\frac{1}{2},

(32)

when the resonance condition

\Delta M\simeq\frac{\Gamma}{2}

(33)

is satisfied.

In the inverse seesaw, the mass splitting is controlled by the lepton-number
violating parameter
\mu
,

\Delta M\simeq\mu,

(34)

while the decay width of a heavy neutrino is

\Gamma\simeq\frac{(y_{\nu}^{\dagger}y_{\nu})}{8\pi}M_{N}.

(35)

The resonance condition therefore implies

\mu\simeq\frac{y_{\nu}^{2}M_{N}}{16\pi}.

(36)

Since the Dirac Yukawa coupling is constrained by the light neutrino mass,

m_{\nu}\simeq\frac{(y_{\nu}v)^{2}}{M_{N}^{2}}\,\mu,

(37)

one finds

y_{\nu}^{2}\simeq\frac{m_{\nu}M_{N}^{2}}{v^{2}\mu}.

(38)

Substituting into the resonance condition yields the optimal mass splitting

\mu_{\rm res}\simeq\sqrt{\frac{m_{\nu}M_{N}^{3}}{16\pi v^{2}}}.

(39)

VI
Numerical Results

We now summarize the numerical results for the baryon asymmetry

\eta_{B}
obtained via non-thermal resonant leptogenesis in the

U(1)_{B-L}
inverse seesaw framework, emphasizing the role of the threshold
mechanism in suppressing washout at the TeV scale.

•

The reheating temperature
T_{R}
is generated by the decay of the

B\!-\!L
scalar
\chi
into heavy neutrinos. To avoid thermal washout, we impose
the non-thermal condition
T_{R}<M_{N}
.

•

Resonant enhancement of the CP asymmetry is achieved through the small
lepton-number violating parameter
\mu

VII
Conclusions

We have shown that the gauged
U(1)_{B-L}
inverse seesaw model provides a viable
and testable framework for baryogenesis through non-thermal resonant
leptogenesis. Although large Yukawa couplings inherent to the inverse seesaw
render standard thermal leptogenesis ineffective due to severe washout, this
difficulty can be overcome by producing right-handed neutrinos out of
equilibrium via the decay of the
B\!-\!L
Higgs scalar.

Successful baryogenesis is achieved through the interplay of two key mechanisms.
First, tuning the scalar mass close to the kinematic threshold,

M_{\chi}\simeq 2M_{N}+\delta
, naturally lowers the reheating temperature to

T_{R}<M_{N}
, leading to an exponential suppression of inverse-decay washout.
Second, the quasi-degenerate pseudo-Dirac structure of heavy neutrinos enables
resonant CP violation, allowing the CP asymmetry to reach near-maximal values
when the lepton-number-violating parameter satisfies

\Delta M\simeq\Gamma/2
.

Our numerical analysis demonstrates that for heavy neutrino masses

M_{N}\sim 5~\mathrm{TeV}
, reheating temperatures of order

T_{R}\sim 1~\mathrm{TeV}
, and resonant mass splittings

\mu\sim\mathcal{O}(\mathrm{MeV})
, the observed baryon asymmetry of the
Universe is successfully reproduced. The required parameter region is
technically natural and radiatively stable.

Unlike high-scale leptogenesis scenarios, this mechanism operates entirely at
the TeV scale, providing a direct and experimentally accessible connection
between neutrino mass generation and the cosmological origin of matter.

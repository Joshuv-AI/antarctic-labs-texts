Hyperfiniteness on Topological Ramsey Spaces
Balázs Bursics, Zoltán Vidnyánszky

Abstract
. We investigate the behavior of countable Borel equivalence relations (CBERs) on topological Ramsey spaces. First, we give a simple proof of the fact that every CBER on [ N ] ​ N [\mathbb{N}]{N} is hyperfinite on some set of the form [ A ] ​ N [A]{N} . Using the idea behind the proof, we show the analogous result for every topological Ramsey space.

Primary 03E15, Secondary 68Q17

1.
Introduction

A
countable Borel equivalence relation (CBER)
on a Polish space
X
is an equivalence relation with all classes countable that is Borel as a subset of
X^{2}
. CBERs have a rich and well investigated structure theory (see, e.g.,
[
8
]
). The natural way to compare two such equivalence relations is the notion of
reduction
: if
E
and
F
are CBERs on spaces
X
and
Y
,
E
is said to
Borel reduce
to
F
, if there exists a Borel map
\phi:X\to Y
with

\forall x,x^{\prime}\in X\ xEx^{\prime}\iff\phi(x)F\phi(y).

The simplest non-trivial class of CBERs is the collection of
hyperfinite
ones, that is, the collection of CBERs which admit a Borel reduction to
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, the eventual equality equivalence relation on
2{N}
.

It is a classical fact that there are CBERs, which do not reduce to
\mathbb{E}_{0}
, however, so far, essentially only measure theoretic proofs
1
1

1

Results of Thomas
[
15
]
provide a non-measure theoretic argument, but rely on Martin’s conjecture.
are known to establish this fact. This motivates the investigation of CBERs with respect to different notions of largeness. For example, classical results of Hjorth-Kechris
[
5
]
and Sullivan-Weiss-Wright
[
14
]
establish hyperfiniteness on comeager sets, and more recently Panagiotopoulos-Wang
[
12
]
and Marks-Rossegger-Slaman
[
10
]
have shown smoothness (that is, reducibility to equality on
2{N}
) of CBERs restricted to positive sets with respect to the Carlson-Simpson ideal and Hausdorff dimension, respectively. The monograph of Kanovei-Sabok-Zapletal
[
6
]
contains a large number of deep canonization-type results, mostly in the context of (not necessarily countable) Borel equivalence relations.

In this paper, we consider CBERs on topological Ramsey spaces in the sense of Todorčević, see
[
16
]
. The prime example of these spaces is the
Ellentuck space

[\mathbb{N}]{N}
, that is, the collection of infinite subsets of the natural numbers, endowed with the topology inherited from
2{N}
. The following theorem has been shown by Soare
[
13
]
in the recursion theoretic context and Mathias (see Kanovei-Sabok-Zapletal
[
6
]
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a CBER on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. There exists a set
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that
E\upharpoonright[A]{N}
is hyperfinite.

The proof of a slightly more general fact than the above one in Kanovei-Sabok-Zapletal
[
6
]
uses forcing, and a non-straightforward trick with “even-odd” encoding. We give a few line elementary argument, the idea behind which generalizes to arbitrary topological Ramsey spaces, yielding the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological Ramsey space and
E
be a CBER on
\mathcal{R}
. Then there is a set
A\in\mathcal{R}
such that
E\upharpoonright[A]
is hyperfinite.

Along the way we also isolate a condition, which could be interesting on its own. A version of this notion has been considered by Marks and Unger
[
11
]
, to establish a so called toast structure on co-meager sets, with respect to bounded degree Borel graphs (see also
[
3
,
2
,
4
,
1
]
).

Recall the following definition.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
G
be a graph,
B\subseteq V(G)
. The set
B
is called
k
-separated
if for any
x\neq x^{\prime}\in B
we have
\dist_{G}(x,x^{\prime})>k
.

The following observation is our main tool towards the general result, i.e., Theorem
1.2
. Roughly speaking, it says that if the space can be covered by very sparse sets, w.r.t. a bounded degree decomposition of
E
so that every point is covered infinitely often, then
E
is hyperfinite.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
E
be a CBER on the space
X
, let
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of bounded degree Borel graphs such that
\bigcup_{n}G_{n}=E
, and let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be such that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\;f(n+1)\geq\max\left(f(n)+2,2\cdot(f(n)+1)\right)
. Moreover, assume that
B_{n}\subseteq X
are Borel sets so that every
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated in
G_{n}
.

Then
E\upharpoonright B
is hyperfinite, where
B=\{x:\exists^{\infty}n\ (x\in B_{n})\}
.

It was already suggested by Marks and Unger that such a construction yields a proof of the theorem of Hjorth-Kechris
[
5
]
and Sullivan-Weiss-Wright
[
14
]
mentioned above that every CBER on a Polish space is hyperfinite on a co-meager set. In fact, unlike Segal’s proof (see
[
7
]
), this proof does not rely on the Kuratowski-Ulam theorem. We include this argument for the sake of completeness.

Roadmap

In Section
2
we collect all the facts we need about topological Ramsey spaces. Section
3

[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. Then, in Section

5
contains some open problems. Sections
3
,
4.1
and
4.3
do not use the definition of a topological Ramsey space.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Topological Ramsey spaces

A
topological Ramsey space
in the sense of Todorčević
[
16
]
is a triple
(\mathcal{R},\leq,r)
satisfying a certain set of axioms, where
\mathcal{R}
is a nonempty set,
\leq
a quasi-ordering on
\mathcal{R},
and
r:\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{AR}
. The range of
r
can be thought of as the collection of finite approximations to elements of
\mathcal{R}
. We will use capitals
A,B,\ldots
for the elements of
\mathcal{R}
, and
a,b,\ldots
for their approximations. We denote
r_{n}(.)=r(.,n)
.

The relation
\leq
is encoded on the level of approximations by a quasi-ordering
\leq_{\text{fin}}
on
\mathcal{AR}
.

For
a\in\mathcal{AR}
and
B\in\mathcal{R}
define

[a,B]=\{A\in\mathcal{R}:A\leq B\wedge(\exists n)\;r_{n}(A)=a\}.

We also use the abbreviations
[B]=\{A\in\mathcal{R}:A\leq B\}
and
[n,B]=[r_{n}(B),B].

There are multiple natural topologies on
\mathcal{R}
: the
metric topology
of
\mathcal{R}
inherited from the Baire space
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R}{N},
and the so-called
Ellentuck topology
generated by
\{[a,A]:\;a\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R},\;A\in\mathcal{R}\}.
We work with the metric topology, unless stated otherwise.

The next definition gives a standard way of constructing elements of
\mathcal{R}
with some desirable property.

Definition 2.1
.

A
fusion sequence
is a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathcal{R}
such that
A_{n+1}\in[n,A_{n}]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.

The
limit
of a fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is the unique
A\in\mathcal{R}
such that
A\in[n,A_{n}]
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}.

Note that for the existence of the limit of a fusion sequence we assume that
\mathcal{R}
is closed in
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R}{N}
with respect to the metric topology. This is not listed among the axioms of topological Ramsey spaces in
[
16
]
, however, this property is assumed for the main results of the theory. We make use of the following properties of fusion sequences.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(A_{n})
be a fusion sequence and
C\in[\lim A_{n}].
Then

(1)

\lim A_{n}\leq A_{k}
for every
k
,

(2)

there are infinitely many
n
with
C\in[s,A_{n}]
for some
s\leq_{fin}r_{n}(A_{n}).

Apart from the technique of fusion sequences, we use the Ramsey theoretic statement below.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological Ramsey space,
c:\mathcal{R}\to k
a finite Borel coloring,
A\in\mathcal{R},
and
s\leq_{fin}r_{n}(A)
. Then there exists
B\in[n,A]
such that
c\upharpoonright[s,B]
is monochromatic.

2.2.
The Ellentuck space

The space
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
has a topological Ramsey structure by letting
A\leq B\iff A\subseteq B
and
r_{n}(A)=\{a_{k}:k<n\}
where
A=\{a_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
with
a_{k}<a_{k+1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The notation
[a,B]
defined above for
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
and
a\in[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
gives
[a,B]=\{A\subseteq B:r_{n}(A)=a\},
but instead we stick to the classical notation

[a,B]=\{A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}:a\subset A\subseteq a\cup B\}

where we require that
\max a<\min B.

A fusion sequence in
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
is a sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
so that
A_{n+1}\subseteq A_{n}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the set of
n
smallest elements is the same for every
A_{m}
with
m\geq n
, and
\lim A_{n}=\cap A_{n}
.

Here Theorem
2.3
. corresponds to the well-known theorem of Galvin-Prikry.
It is a classical result of Silver and also a consequence of the theory of topological Ramsey spaces that the conclusion of the Galvin-Prikry theorem remains true even when we consider colorings that are
Souslin measurable
, i.e., that are measurable w.r.t. the
\sigma
-algebra generated by analytic sets. A further consequence of this result, using a standard fusion argument is the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
f:[t,A]\to[\mathbb{N}]{N}
be a Souslin measurable function. Then there is some
B\in[t,A]
such that
f\upharpoonright[t,B]
is continuous.

3.
The case of
[\mathbb{N}]{N}

In this section, we give a simple proof of the canonization theorem on the Ellentuck space and reprove the mentioned results of Soare and Mathias. This relies on the following easy lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a bounded degree Borel graph on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
, and suppose that
\max t<\min A
for
t\in[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
and
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. Then there is
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
such that
[t,A^{\prime}\setminus t]
is
G
-independent.

Proof.

By a classical result of Kechris-Solecki-Todorčević
[
9
]
, every bounded degree Borel graph admits a finite Borel vertex coloring. Let
c:[\mathbb{N}]{N}\to k
be such a coloring of
G
. By the Galvin-Prikry theorem (or, Theorem
2.3
), there is some
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
and
j
with
[t,A^{\prime}\setminus t]\subseteq c^{-1}(j)
. Then
[t,A^{\prime}\setminus t]
cannot contain a
G
-edge.
∎

A slightly more sophisticated version will be sufficient for our purposes.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a bounded degree Borel graph on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
, and suppose that
\max t<\min A
for
t\in[\mathbb{N}]^{n}
and
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. Then there is
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
such that
G\upharpoonright[A^{\prime}]{N}\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{0}.

Proof.

We define a graph
G^{*}
on
[t,A]
by

\displaystyle(B,C)\in G^{*}

\displaystyle\iff\big(B\neq C\land\exists r,s\subseteq t:(B\setminus r,C\setminus s)\in G\big).

Clearly,
\maxdeg G^{*}\leq 2^{n}\maxdeg G
, so by Lemma
3.1
there is some
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
such that
[t,A^{\prime}\setminus t]
is
G^{*}
-independent. But this means that
B,C\in[A^{\prime}]{N}
cannot be
G
-related, unless
t\cup B=t\cup C
, yielding
B\mathbb{E}_{0}C
.
∎

Now let us finish the proof of Theorem
1.1
, that is:

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
E
be a CBER on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. There exists a set
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that
E\upharpoonright[A]{N}
is hyperfinite.

Proof.

Suppose that
E
is a countable Borel equivalence relation on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
. As all classes of
E
are countable, there are Borel involutions
\varphi_{n}
on
[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that
E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\graph(\varphi_{n})
. It is sufficient to construct a fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\graph(\varphi_{n})\upharpoonright[A_{n}]{N}\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{0}.
If
A_{n}=\{a_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
is given with
a_{k}<a_{k+1}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, letting
t=\{a_{k}:k<n\}
and applying Lemma
3.2
to
G=\graph(\varphi_{n})
and
[t,A_{n}\setminus t]
yields the desired
A_{n+1}
.
∎

The original motivation behind Soare’s work was recursion theoretic. His aim was to show the existence of a set
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that it does not contain a subset of higher Turing-degree. Lemma
3.2
yields this statement as well.

Corollary 3.3
.

There exists a set
[A]{N}
such that if
A\leq_{T}B
and
B\in[A]{N}
then
A\mathbb{E}_{0}B
.

Proof.

Towards contradiction, assume that such a set does not exist. It implies that for each
A\in[\mathbb{N}]{N}
there exists some
B\in[A]{N}
such that
|A\setminus B|=\infty
and
B\geq_{T}A
. In particular, there is some
m
with
A=\varphi_{m}(B)
, where
\varphi_{m}
is the
m
th Turing functional. Then, there is a set of the form
[t,A]
such that the same
m
works for all
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
. Since
\varphi_{m}
is Borel, by the Jankov-von Neumann uniformization theorem there is a Souslin measurable map
\psi:[t,A]\to[\mathbb{N}]{N}
such that
A^{\prime}=\varphi_{m}(\psi(A^{\prime})),
for all
A^{\prime}\in[t,A].
Moreover, by Theorem
2.4
(by passing to a further set of the form
[t,B]
) we may assume that
\psi
is continuous on
[t,A]
. Observe that as
\psi
is a partial inverse, it is one-to-one. Hence, by the Luzin-Novikov theorem, the graph
G
on
[t\cup A]{N}
defined by

(B,C)\in G\iff B,C\in[t,A]\cup\psi([t,A])\land(B=\psi(C)\lor C=\psi(B))

is Borel and has degrees bounded by
2
. By Lemma
3.2
there is some
A^{\prime}\in[t,A]
so that
G\upharpoonright[A^{\prime}]{N}
is contained in
\mathbb{E}_{0}
. This contradicts that
A^{\prime}\setminus\psi(A^{\prime})
is infinite.
∎

4.
General topological Ramsey spaces

In this section we prove the hyperfiniteness result for all topological Ramsey spaces.

4.1.
Sufficiently separated covers

Let us first prove the following:

Theorem
1.4
.

Let
E
be a CBER on the space
X
, let
(G_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of bounded degree Borel graphs such that
\bigcup_{n}G_{n}=E
, and let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be such that
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\;f(n+1)\geq\max\left(f(n)+2,2\cdot(f(n)+1)\right)
. Moreover, assume that
B_{n}\subseteq X
are Borel sets so that every
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated in
G_{n}
.

Then
E\upharpoonright B
is hyperfinite, where
B=\{x:\exists^{\infty}n\ (x\in B_{n})\}
.

Proof.

We define a sequence
H_{0}\subseteq H_{1}\subseteq\dots\subseteq H_{n}\subseteq E
inductively. Let
H_{0}=\emptyset
.

Given
H_{n}
, let

H_{n+1}=H_{n}\cup\{(x,y)\in G_{n+1}\upharpoonright B:\;x\in B_{n+1}\text{ or }y\in B_{n+1}\}.

First we check that
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}H_{n}\upharpoonright B=E\upharpoonright B
. Indeed, let
(x,y)\in G_{n}\upharpoonright B
. Then there is some
B_{k}
with
k\geq n
such that
x\in B_{k}
. But then
(x,y)\in H_{k}
.

Claim 4.1
.

Every connected component of
H_{n}
has diameter
\leq f(n)
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
n
. The case
n=0
is clear, so assume that we have shown the statement for
n
. We claim that if
H
is a connected component of
H_{n+1}
then it contains at most one point of
B_{n+1}
. Indeed, otherwise let
x\neq x^{\prime}
from
B_{n+1}
in
V(H)
be such that for
p=(x_{0},\dots,x_{k})
, the injective path with minimal length from
x_{0}=x
to
x_{k}=x^{\prime}
in
H
we have
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}\notin B_{n+1}
. Then
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k-1}
are contained in a single connected component
H^{\prime}
of
H_{n}
, and by the inductive hypothesis
\dist_{H_{n}}(x_{1},x_{k-1})\leq f(n).
But this implies
\dist_{G_{n+1}}(x,x^{\prime})\leq f(n)+2\leq f(n+1),
a contradiction.

Thus,
H
contains at most one point, say
x
, from
B_{n+1}
. Then

V(H)=\bigcup\{[y]_{H_{n}}:(x,y)\in G_{n+1}\}.

yielding

\text{diam}(H)\leq 2\cdot(f(n)+1)\leq f(n+1).

∎

As
H_{n}\subseteq G_{n}
which has bounded degrees, this, in turn also ends the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

4.2.
On general Ramsey spaces

Now we show that we can construct sufficiently separated covers on positive sets on every Ramsey space.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological Ramsey space, let
G
be a bounded degree Borel graph on
\mathcal{R}
, and let
a\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R}
and
A\in\mathcal{R}
be such that
a\leq_{\text{fin}}r_{n}(A)
. Then there exists
A^{\prime}\in[n,A]
such that
[a,A^{\prime}]
is
k
-separated.

Proof.

By
[
9
]
, every bounded degree Borel graph admits a finite Borel vertex coloring. Let
c:\mathcal{R}\to m
be such a coloring of

G^{k}=\{(x,y):\dist(x,y)\leq k\}.

By Theorem
2.3
. there is some
A^{\prime}\in[n,A]
such that
c\upharpoonright[a,A^{\prime}]
is constant, thus,
[a,A^{\prime}]
is
k
-separated.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological Ramsey-space,
G_{n}
a sequence of bounded degree Borel graphs on
\mathcal{R}
, and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
. Then there exist
A\in\mathcal{R}
and
B_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
Borel sets such that every
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated, and

[A]\subseteq\{C:\exists^{\infty}n\ (C\in B_{n})\}.

Proof.

We construct a fusion sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
inductively. Let

k_{n}=\sum_{i<n}|\{s\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R}:\;s\leq_{\text{fin}}r_{i}(A_{i})\}|.

Let
B_{0}=\emptyset.
Throughout the construction, we define
B_{k_{n}+1},\ldots,B_{k_{n+1}}
after fixing
A_{n}
(and
k_{n+1}
).
Suppose that
A_{n}
and
B_{0},\ldots,B_{k_{n}}
are given. We enumerate
\{s\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{R}:\;s\leq_{\text{fin}}r_{n}(A_{n})\}
as
\{s_{j}:1\leq j\leq k_{n+1}-k_{n}\}
, and define
(A_{j}^{\prime})_{j\leq k_{n+1}-k_{n}}
recursively as follows: let
A_{0}^{\prime}=A_{n}
, if
A_{j}^{\prime}
is already given for some
j<k_{n+1}-k_{n}
, choose
A_{j+1}^{\prime}\in[n,A_{j}^{\prime}]
so that
[s_{j+1},A_{j+1}^{\prime}]
is
f(k_{n}+j+1)
-separated (such an element of
\mathcal{R}
exists by Lemma
4.2
.), and let
B_{k_{n}+j+1}=[s_{j+1},A_{j+1}^{\prime}]
. Afterwards, let
A_{n+1}=A_{k_{n+1}-k_{n}}^{\prime}\in[n,A_{n}].

Finally, let
A=\lim A_{n}
and suppose that
C^{\prime}\in[A].
By Proposition
2.2
. there are infinitely many
n
with
C^{\prime}\in[s,A_{n}]
for some
s\leq_{fin}r_{n}(A_{n})
. For such an
n
and
s
, we have that
[s,A_{n}]\subseteq B_{k}
for some
k_{n-1}<k\leq k_{n},
which yields
C^{\prime}\in\{C:\exists^{\infty}n\ (C\in B_{n})\}
.
∎

Now we are ready to show our main result.

Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a topological Ramsey space and
E
be a CBER on
\mathcal{R}
. Then there is a set
A\in\mathcal{R}
such that
E\upharpoonright[A]
is hyperfinite.

Proof.

As all classes of
E
are countable, there are Borel involutions
\varphi_{n}
on
\mathcal{R}
such that
E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\graph(\varphi_{n})
. Let
G_{n}=\bigcup_{i\in n}\graph(\varphi_{i})
, and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be as in Theorem
1.4
. By Lemma
4.3
. There exist
A\in\mathcal{R}
and
B_{n}\subseteq\mathcal{R}
Borel sets such that every
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated, and

[A]\subseteq\{C:\exists^{\infty}n\ (C\in B_{n})\},

thus,
E\upharpoonright[A]
is hyperfinite according to Theorem
1.4
.
∎

4.3.
Generic hyperfiniteness

As mentioned above, the method of sufficiently separated covers also gives a new proof of the classical results of Hjorth-Kechris
[
5
]
and Sullivan-Weiss-Wright
[
14
]
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
E
be a CBER on the space
X
. Then there is a comeager invariant Borel set
C
such that
E\upharpoonright C
is hyperfinite.

Proof.

As all classes of
E
are countable, there are Borel involutions
\varphi_{n}
on
X
such that
E=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\graph(\varphi_{n})
. Let
G_{n}=\bigcup_{i\in n}\graph(\varphi_{i})
, and
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be as in Theorem
1.4
. We will construct Borel sets
B_{n}\subseteq X
so that every
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated in
G_{n}
, and there is a comeager invariant Borel set
C
such that
C\subseteq\{x:\exists^{\infty}n\ (x\in B_{n})\}
.

Fix a bijection
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}^{2}
with coordinate functions
g_{0},g_{1}
and enumerate a basis of
X
as
(U_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}.
Since
G_{n}^{f(n)}
has bounded degrees there exists a finite Borel vertex coloring
c:X\to m
of
G_{n}^{f(n)}
by
[
9
]
. For some
i\in m
the preimage
c^{-1}(i)
is non-meager in
U_{g_{0}(n)},
set
B_{n}=c^{-1}(i).
This way
B_{n}
is
f(n)
-separated in
G_{n}
and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

C_{k}=\bigcup_{n\in g_{1}^{-1}(k)}B_{n}

is comeager in
X,
and

C^{\prime}=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}C_{k}\subseteq\{x:\exists^{\infty}n\ (x\in B_{n})\}.

As the saturation of
X\setminus C^{\prime}
is meager,
C=C^{\prime}\setminus\big[X\setminus C^{\prime}\big]_{E}
is a comeager invariant Borel set such that
E\upharpoonright C
is hyperfinite by Theorem
1.4
.
∎

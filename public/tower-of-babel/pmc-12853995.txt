Meta-analysis of comparing CAR-T and bispecific antibody therapy in relapsed/refractory indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas
Authors: Min-Yue Zhang, Yong-Mei Cai, Xiao-Qing Zhu, Ke-Jie Lu, Li-Jing Shen, Juan Du
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIndolent B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas (B-NHLs), including follicular lymphoma (FL) and marginal zone lymphoma (MZL), typically demonstrate slow progression but frequently follow a relapsing-remitting course with conventional therapies. Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) and bi-specific antibodies (BsAbs) therapies have revolutionized treatment for relapsed/refractory (R/R) B-NHLs. However comparative data on the efficacy of CAR-T therapy versus BsAbs in indolent B-NHLs remain limited. This meta-analysis systematically evaluated the efficacy and safety profiles of these two innovative treatments in patients with R/R FL and MZL.MethodsWe conducted a comprehensive search of MEDLINE, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trial databases, and international conference abstracts (through June 30, 2025) for studies investigating CAR-T or BsAb therapies in R/R FL and MZL. Primary outcome was pooled complete response (CR) rate. Other outcomes included 2-year progression-free survival (PFS), 2-year overall survival (OS), non-relapse mortality (NRM), and adverse events (AEs). Meta-regression analyses were performed to adjust for relevant clinical covariates.ResultsOur analysis included 19 publications (n = 1,760 patients), comprising 10 CAR-T and 9 BsAbs studies. The single-arm meta-analysis showed that CAR-T therapy demonstrated superior efficacy, with higher pooled CR rate (80.73% vs. 67.14%; p = 0.0003), 2-year PFS (68.84% vs. 47.71%; p = 0.0006), and 2-year OS (88.37% vs. 75.26%; p = 0.049) compared to BsAbs. NRM per 100 person-years was similar between two modalities (3.89 vs 3.86, p = 0.9871). CAR-T benefits persisted in third-line or later settings, as well as in analyses restricted to clinical trial data. Multivariate meta-regression confirmed CAR-T’s superiority for CR independent of median age. Safety analysis revealed CAR-T group had higher incidence of grade ≥3 neurotoxicity (7.48% vs. 0.23%, p = 0.0029), neutropenia (58.90% vs. 26.21%; p = 0007), thrombocytopenia (16.77% vs. 5.00%; p = 0.005), but lower incidence of grade ≥3 infection per 100 person-months (0.38 vs. 0.91, p = 0.040) than BsAb group. No differences were observed in grade ≥3 cytokine release syndrome or anemia.ConclusionCAR-T therapy offered superior efficacy and survival in R/R indolent B-NHLs compared to BsAbs, albeit with increased AEs, highlighting the need for careful patient selection and toxicity management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-025-07571-3.Keywords: Indolent B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas, CAR-T, Bispecific antibody, Meta-analysis

Introduction

In recent years, immunotherapy has revolutionized the treatment of B-cell non-Hodgkin’s lymphomas (B-NHLs), with chimeric antigen receptor T-cell (CAR-T) and bispecific antibodies (BsAbs) therapies emerging as two major therapeutic innovations [1–3]. CAR-T therapy, involving the ex vivo genetic modification of a patient’s own T cells to target specific lymphoma-associated antigens (e.g., CD19, CD22), has demonstrated durable remissions in heavily pretreated patients with various subtypes of B-NHLs, including diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL), follicular lymphoma (FL), and mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) [2, 3]. In parallel, bispecific antibodies (BsAbs) have emerged as a potent off-the-shelf alternative. These engineered antibodies (e.g., mosunetuzumab, glofitamab) simultaneously engage CD3 on the surface of T cells and CD20 on the surface of B cells, effectively redirecting endogenous T cells to eradicate malignant B cells [4, 5]. The “off-the-shelf” availability of BsAbs offers distinct advantages over the complex manufacturing process required for CAR-T therapy [3].

B-NHLs encompass a heterogeneous group of malignancies with diverse clinical behaviors, ranging from aggressive to indolent subtypes. Indolent B-NHLs, such as FL and marginal zone lymphoma (MZL), typically exhibit slow progression but follow a relapsing-remitting course with conventional therapies, posing significant challenges in achieving sustained remissions [6–8]. Despite of significant advances in immunochemotherapy and targeted agents, such as CD 20 monoclonal antibodies, Bruton’s Tyrosine Kinase (BTK) inhibitor and Phosphatidylinositol-3-kinase (PI3K) inhibitors [9–11], relapsed or refractory (R/R) indolent B-NHLs remain a formidable therapeutic challenge, necessitating innovative treatment approaches.

Although CAR-T therapy and BsAbs have shown promising results in R/R B-NHLs, comparative data evaluating these modalities in indolent subtypes, such as FL and MZL, are limited. To address this critical knowledge gap, we performed a systematic meta-analysis to compare the efficacy and safety profiles of CAR-T therapy versus BsAbs treatment in R/R indolent B-NHLs, including FL and MZL. This study provides essential evidence to guide optimal treatment selection in the rapidly evolving landscape of indolent B-NHLs therapeutics.

Methods

Search strategy

This systematic review was conducted in accordance with the PRISMA statement for reporting systematic reviews and network meta-analyses in healthcare interventions [12]. The study protocol was prospectively registered in the International Prospective Register for Systematic Reviews (registration ID: CRD420251168344). We performed the literature search in the MEDLINE, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trial databases limiting entries to those in English up to Jun 30th, 2025. The primary keywords included (“follicular lymphoma” or “marginal zone lymphoma” or “indolent lymphoma”) and (“chimeric antigen receptor” or “bispecific antibodies”) along with derivatives of each keyword. More details on the search methodology are described in the Supplemental Table 1. Abstracts from major international conferences, relevant meta-analyses and systematic reviews were also manually screened. In case where a publication overlapped with other publication of the same trial, only the article with more details or the most recent articles were included.

Selection criteria

Prospective interventional clinical trials or real-world studies that evaluated the efficacy and safety of BsAbs or CAR-T therapy in R/R FL and MZL were included for the meta-analysis. Phase I trials, using the recommended phase II dosage and assessing its therapeutic efficacy, were also incorporated into the meta-analysis. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) used these two interventions as first-line treatment; (2) included fewer than 10 participants; or (3) used combination therapy involving both CAR-T and BsAbs therapy.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two reviewers (X Zhu and K Lu) independently extracted data and outcomes using an electronic standard form. The following details from each study was summarized: (1) first author, (2) publication year, (3) intervention, (4) sample size, (5) demographic information (sex, age, Ann Arbor stage, etc.), (6) previous treatment, and (7) key clinical outcomes (complete remission [CR] rate, 2-year progression-free survival [PFS], 2-year overall survival [OS], non-relapse mortality (NRM), and incidence of adverse events [AEs]). Discrepancies between the two reviewers were resolved by a third investigator, Y Cai. When 2-year PFS/OS were reported, we abstracted them directly. When not reported, we digitized Kaplan–Meier curves and estimated 2-year rates using Engauge Digitizer. The risk bias of non-randomized controlled trials evaluated by the Methodological Index for Non-Randomized Studies (MINORS) scale [13].

Data analysis

The primary outcome was pooled CR rate. Secondary outcomes included pooled 2-year PFS, 2-year OS, NRM, and incidence of AEs (cytokine release syndrome [CRS], neurotoxicity [NT], neutropenia, thrombocytopenia, anemia, and infections). Rates per 100 person-months/years were calculated as [events ÷ (total evaluable patients × median follow-up in months/years)] × 100.

Using random-effects models, the pooled outcomes along with corresponding 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and p values were calculated. Subgroup analyses based on treatment modality were conducted using the Q statistic to evaluate between-group differences. Additionally, meta-regression analyses were performed to identify and adjust for potential confounding variables influencing the CR rate. Variables with a p-value < 0.1 in univariate meta-regression were included in the multivariate model. Meta-regression was performed using the metareg function under a mixed-effects framework with default settings. Publication bias was examined using funnel plots and evaluated statistically with Egger’s test. All reported p-values were two-tailed, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Data analysis was performed using R Studio (Version: 2025.05.1 + 513).

Results

Study characteristics

Through the literature search and selection based on the criteria above, 19 articles [14–32] with 1,760 enrolled participants were identified from a reviewing of 3,288 potentially relevant publications and included in the quantitative pooled analysis (Fig. 1). The baseline characteristics of the included studies were summarized in Table 1 and Supplemental Table 2. All the studies included in this meta-analysis were single-arm trials except one study with two arms [14]. Among these, 14 studies [14–17, 20–22, 24, 25, 28–32] involved patients receiving CAR-T therapy or BsAbs as third-line or later-line treatments. The study populations included 17 studies [14–20, 22–27, 29–32] with FL patients and 3 studies [20, 21, 28] with MZL. In the 10 anti-CD19 CAR-T trials [16, 19, 20, 23, 25, 27–31], the regimens included tisagenlecleucel (tisa-cel), axicabtagene ciloleucel (axi-cel), lisocabtagene maraleucel (liso-cel), relmacabtagene autoleucel (relma-cel), and varnimcabtagene autoleucel. The 9 BsAbs trials [14, 15, 17, 18, 21, 22, 24, 26, 32] evaluated a variety of regimens targeting CD20×CD3, including epcoritamab, glofitamab, mosunetuzumab, odronextamab, and other clinical trial products. The risk of bias assessment, summarized in Supplemental Table 3, indicated intermediate to high study quality, with MINORS scores ranging from 10 to 16 out of 16 for single-arm studies and 20 out of 24 for one two-arms study.

Comparison of efficacy and survival

For the CR analysis, data from 19 studies, comprising 1,622 participants, were included, as some individuals from the original cohorts were not evaluable for CR at the time of reporting. The overall pooled proportion of CR was 75.11% (95% CI 70.10 ~ 80.12). There was notable difference in CR rate between treatment modalities, with BsAb therapy achieving 67.14% (95% CI, 62.85 ~ 71.43) compared to CAR T-cell therapy at 80.73% (95% CI, 74.69 ~ 86.78) (p = 0.0003), as illustrated in Fig. 2A. For PFS, data from 9 trials were included in the pooled analysis of 2-year PFS. The overall pooled proportion of 2-year PFS was 59.27% (95% CI, 49.77 ~ 68.77), and a significant difference in the 2-year PFS rate also existed between the 2 groups: with BsAb group showing 2-year PFS of 47.71 (95% CI, 43.05 ~ 52.37) compared to 68.84% (95% CI: 57.69 ~ 79.98) for the CAR T-cell group (p = 0.0006), as depicted in Fig. 2B. Regarding OS, a total of 8 trials were synthesized to estimate the pooled 2-year OS, yielding an overall pooled proportion of 82.51% (95% CI, 75.89 ~ 89.14). A significant divergence in 2-year OS was again evident between the BsAbs (75.26%, 95% CI 62.56 ~ 87.96) and CAR-T-cell therapy (88.37%, 95% CI 85.42 ~ 91.33, p = 0.049, Fig. 2C). NRM was further evaluated and the results indicated that there was no difference of pooled NRM (7.55% vs 6.72%, p = 0.8212, Fig. 3A, B) or NRM per 100 person-years (3.89 vs 3.86, p = 0.9871, Fig. 3C) between these two modalities.

Comparison of safety profiles

In the analysis related to AEs, despite significantly higher incidence of any grades of CRS in CAR-T group than that in BsAbs group (68.02% vs. 51.49%, p = 0.0142, Supplemental Fig. 1A), there was no significant difference of the incidence of grade ≥3 CRS between two treatment modalities (1.70% vs. 2.01%, p = 0.684, Fig. 4A). Both significantly higher incidence of any grades of NT (29.99% vs. 12.61%, p = 0.0397, Supplemental Fig. 1B) and grade ≥3 NT (7.48% vs. 0.23%, p = 0.0029, Fig. 4B) was observed in patients receiving CAR-T therapy than those receiving BsAb treatment. With regards to infection, no significant difference of incidence of grade ≥3 infection between two groups (12.92% vs. 19.63%, p = 0.387, Fig. 4C). However, BsAbs had higher incidence of grade ≥3 infection per 100 person-month than CAR-T (0.91 vs. 0.38, p = 0.0399, Fig. 4D). Intergroup disparities were observed in the occurrence of severe hematologic AEs, notably with grade ≥3 neutropenia at an incidence of 26.21% (95% CI 21.08 ~ 31.35) for the BsAB group, contrasting with the significantly higher incidence of 58.90% (95% CI 40.68 ~ 77.11) in the CAR-T group (p = 0.0007, Fig. 4E). Similarly, the incidence of grade ≥3 thrombocytopenia was reported at 5.00% (95% CI 2.11 ~ 7.89) in the BsAb group, and it was markedly higher in the CAR-T group with a rate of 16.77% (95% CI 8.99 ~ 24.54, p = 0.0054, Fig. 4F). However, no notable difference of the incidence of grade ≥3 anemia was found between these two treatment regimens (14.32% vs. 8.20%, p = 0.1624, Fig. 4G).

Subgroup analysis

Given that CAR-T and BsAbs therapy are approved as third-line or later treatments for B-NHLs, we conducted subgroup analyses specifically in patients receiving these therapies beyond the second line. These analyses consistently demonstrated the significant superiority of CAR-T therapy over BsAbs across efficacy endpoints. Notably, CAR-T therapy was associated with higher CR rates (80.50% vs. 67.00%, p = 0.0062; Supplemental Fig. 2A) and improved 2-year PFS rates (68.46% vs. 47.71%, p = 0.0007; Supplemental Fig. 2B). In contrast, no significant differences were observed in 2-year OS (87.90% vs. 75.26%, p = 0.0581; Supplementary Figure 2C), pooled NRM (9.26% vs. 6.72%, p = 0.5133; Supplementary Figure 2D), or NRM per 100 person-years (3.63 vs. 3.86, p = 0.8982; Supplementary Figure 2E) between the two modalities.

To minimize heterogeneity, we conducted a subgroup analysis limited to clinical trial data. Because 2-year PFS/OS (Fig. 2B, C) and grade ≥3 infections and hematologic toxicities (Fig. 4C–G) in the overall dataset were reported exclusively by clinical trials, no additional subgroup analysis was required for these endpoints. In the trial-only subset, CAR-T achieved a significantly higher CR rate than BsAbs (79.60% vs. 66.93%, p = 0.0298; Supplementary Figure 3A). Pooled NRM and NRM per 100 person-years were comparable between modalities (Supplementary Fig. 3B, C), as were the risks of any-grade and grade ≥3 CRS (Supplementary Fig.3D, E). For NT, both any-grade (31.45% vs 14.00%; p = 0.1204; Supplementary Fig. 3F) and grade ≥3 events (6.82% vs 0.23%; p = 0.0794; Supplementary Fig. 3 G) were numerically higher with CAR-T, although these differences did not reach statistical significance.

Meta-regression analysis

To examine potential sources of heterogeneity across studies and adjust for confounding variables including patient demographics and study design characteristics, we conducted a meta-regression analysis using a mixed-effects model. Univariate meta-regression identified CAR-T therapy (vs BsAbs) and median age as significant moderators for CR rate (Fig. 5). The subsequent multivariable meta-regression model confirmed CAR-T therapy as independently superior to BsAbs (p = 0.0249) with adjustment for median age (Table 2).

Sensitivity analyses

Sensitivity analyses were performed to evaluate the robustness of our findings. First, a “leave-one-out” sensitivity analysis demonstrated that no individual study exerted undue influence on either the overall pooled CR rate or the observed subgroup efficacy differences (Supplemental Table 4). Second, by using the MINORS assessment, we found that the significant difference in CR rates between the two treatment groups persisted even after excluding studies with higher risk of bias (MINORS score < 12; Supplemental Fig. 4A)

Furthermore, we conducted a sensitivity analysis restricted to the studies using Lugano criteria for the treatment response assessment. The pooled CR for CAR-T was numerically higher than for BsAbs, but not statistically significant (78.28% vs. 70.57%; p = 0.1839; Supplementary Figure 4B). When excluding three MZL studies and restricting to FL, CAR-T retained a significantly higher pooled CR rate than BsAbs (82.83% vs. 68.90%; p = 0.0174; Supplementary Figure 4C)

In addition, we also conducted sensitivity analysis stratified by median follow-up length. After excluding studies with median follow-up < 12 months, there remained no significant difference in NRM per 100 person-years between modalities (4.04 vs. 3.29; p = 0.7264; Supplementary Figure 4D), consistent with the whole cohort. For grade ≥3 infection per 100 person-months, although the between-group difference was not significant, BsAbs maintained approximately two-fold higher pooled rates than CAR-T (0.77 vs. 0.38; p = 0.1335; Supplementary Figure 4E), similar to the overall analysis

Publication bias

The funnel plot and Egger’s publication bias test were shown in Supplemental Fig. 5A–L and Supplemental Table 5, which suggested there was risk of publication bias for some outcomes.

Discussion

To our best knowledge, this is the first meta-analysis which providing a comprehensive comparison of anti-19 CAR-T and CD20⨉CD3 BsAbs therapy in indolent R/R B-NHLs. Our findings yield several important insights with significant clinical implications regarding the relative efficacy, safety profiles, and optimal therapeutic positioning of these two immunotherapeutic strategies.

CAR-T therapy, a breakthrough in immunotherapy, utilizes genetically engineered T-cells to recognize and eliminate tumor cells expressing specific antigens, thereby transforming the treatment landscape for lymphoma. Currently, two CD19-directed CAR-T products, tisa-cel and liso-cel, have received FDA approval as third-line therapy for R/R FL, demonstrating remarkable efficacy in pivotal clinical trials with CR rates ranging from 69% to 95% [16, 27]. For MZL, although no CAR-T products have FDA approval, clinical trials have reported CR rates of approximately 65% [20, 28]. Previous meta-analysis indicated that CD19-targeting CAR-T therapy in R/R DLBCL achieved pooled CR rates of 39% to 51% [33, 34]. Our study demonstrated that patients with R/R indolent B-NHLs receiving CAR-T therapy achieved the pooled CR rates of 80.73%, significantly higher than in R/R DLBCL, likely due to the indolent nature of these lymphomas, lower rates of CD19 antigen loss, and reduced CD8+ T-cell exhaustion [35, 36]. BsAbs represent a novel class of adoptive T-cell immunotherapy that induce tumor cell killing by simultaneously binding to a tumor-associated antigen, such as CD20 on the surface of malignant B-cells, and CD3 on endogenous T-cells. In recent years, CD20×CD3 BsAbs—such as mosunetuzumab and epcoritamab—have demonstrated impressive clinical efficacy in R/R FL [14, 24], leading to their FDA approvals as third-line treatment options for R/R FL patients. Our meta-analysis showed that CD20×CD3 BsAbs used as third- or later-line therapy in R/R indolent lymphomas yielded a pooled CR rate of 67%, which was notably higher than the pooled CR rate of 51% observed in R/R DLBCL patients [33].

Although matching-adjusted indirect comparisons of liso-cel or axi-cel to mosunetuzumab indicated superior outcomes and a higher incidence of AEs of CD19-targeting CAR-T therapy in R/R FL [37, 38], comparisons involving other CAR-T and BsAbs products in indolent B-NHLs remained limited. Building upon these findings, our comparative meta-analysis further elucidated the differential performance of CAR-T and BsAbs treatment in indolent B-NHLs, which was similar to that in R/R DLBCL patients [33]. CAR-T therapy consistently achieved higher CR rates compared to BsAbs. Importantly, meta-regression analysis confirmed CAR-T therapy as an independent predictor of improved CR rates, even after adjusting for baseline factors such as median age, underscoring its intrinsic therapeutic potency. Furthermore, the superior of CR rate of CAR-T therapy translated into significantly prolonged PFS and OS relative to BsAbs. The pivotal trials of CAR-T showed that axi-cel and liso-cel produced sustained remissions, with median PFS exceeding 24 months in many cohorts and 2-year OS rates surpassing 80% [20, 27]. In contrast, BsAbs such as mosunetuzumab and epcoritamab, while showing promising CR rates (approximately 60–70%), were associated with shorter median PFS and OS data in R/R FL patients [24, 39]. These findings support the notion that CAR-T therapy provides not only deeper responses but also more durable disease control. Several factors may contribute to the enhanced efficacy of CAR-T therapy. Mechanistically, CAR-T therapy may provide a deeper level of tumor clearance by leveraging autologous T cells that can persist long-term and form memory subsets, potentially contributing to durable disease control. Additionally, in clinical practice, patients often receive bridging therapy—such as radiation, prior to CAR-T infusion to reduce tumor burden and disease-related inflammation [40, 41]. This preconditioning has been shown to enhance CAR-T expansion, persistence, and cytotoxic activity, ultimately improving treatment efficacy [42]. Nonetheless, CAR-T therapy requires complex manufacturing, specialized centers, and inpatient monitoring. BsAbs, by contrast, offer the advantages of off-the-shelf availability, more manageable toxicity profiles, and outpatient administration, making them an attractive alternative for patients who are ineligible for, or decline CAR-T therapy.

Safety is a paramount consideration when weighing CAR-T therapy against BsAbs for lymphoma patients. While both modalities can induce CRS, NE, infection and hematological toxicities, the safety profiles differ significantly. These AEs are generally lower grade, more predictable, and more manageable for BsAbs therapy due to step-up dosing regimens. In contrast, CAR-T therapy was associated with higher risks of severe NT and hematologic toxicities—particularly grade ≥3 neutropenia and thrombocytopenia—which often necessitate inpatient care and intensive supportive management. Notably, the degree of hematologic suppression varies among different CAR-T products, with axi-cel being associated with a relatively higher incidence of myelosuppression compared to other CAR-T constructs, potentially contributing to increased vulnerability to infections and delayed recovery. Interestingly, although the overall incidence of grade ≥3 infections is comparable between CAR-T and BsAbs, the incidence of grade ≥3 infections per person-months is notably higher with BsAbs, which was consistent the findings by van Besien HJ, et al. [43]. This may be attributed to the continuous and prolonged administration of BsAbs, leading to cumulative immunosuppressive effects, particularly B-cell aplasia and hypogammaglobulinemia over time. Despite these differences, the risk of severe CRS remains comparable between both modalities, highlighting the need for consistent safety monitoring regardless of treatment type. These findings underscore the need for individualized safety monitoring strategies tailored to the specific treatment modality, product characteristics, and patient profile. In older or comorbid patients, BsAbs may offer advantages in terms of tolerability and outpatient feasibility, but the potential for cumulative immunosuppression and infection risk should not be overlooked. Conversely, for CAR-T recipients, early intensive monitoring and hematologic support are critical, especially in patients receiving products like axi-cel with higher rates of bone marrow toxicity.

Despite the valuable insights of our meta-analysis, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, the included studies were highly heterogeneous, with significant variations in treatment settings, follow-up length, trial design, and eligibility criteria. Second, data availability was uneven across B-NHL subtypes. While FL had sufficient studies for robust comparisons, studies in MZL were limited, restricting the ability to draw robust subtype-specific conclusions. Third, the comparison between CAR-T and BsAb therapies was indirect, as no head-to-head randomized controlled trials (RCTs) currently exist. The observed differences may be influenced by variations in study populations, treatment protocols, and response assessment criteria across trials. This lack of RCTs represents a key evidence gap that limits the strength of our comparative conclusions. Fourth, most included studies lacked intention-to-treat (ITT) analyses. Only five studies provided enrolled/ITT denominators for efficacy outcomes. Consequently, a pooled ITT sensitivity meta-analysis was not feasible. Many cohorts reported treated or response-evaluable populations, which can overestimate efficacy and underestimate toxicity relative to ITT; results should therefore be interpreted cautiously. Fifth, publication bias cannot be excluded, as studies with favorable outcomes are more likely to be published, potentially inflating pooled efficacy estimates and obscuring safety signals. Funnel plot asymmetry and Egger’s test findings suggest that the influence of publication bias is non-negligible.

Conclusion

This meta-analysis comprehensively compared CAR-T and BsAbs treatments in indolent R/R B-NHLs. CAR-T therapy demonstrated superior efficacy and survival but was associated with higher risks of severe hematologic and neurologic toxicities. In contrast, BsAbs offered a favorable safety profile and outpatient convenience, making them attractive alternatives for patients unsuitable for CAR-T. However, due to prolonged administration, BsAbs may lead to cumulative immunosuppression, highlighting the need for vigilant infection monitoring and individualized treatment planning

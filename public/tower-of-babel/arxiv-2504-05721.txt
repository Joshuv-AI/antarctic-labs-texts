Graph product and the stability of circulant graphs
Junyang Zhang

Abstract
A graph Γ \Gamma is said to be stable if Aut ⁡ ( Γ × K 2 ) ≅ Aut ⁡ ( Γ ) × ℤ 2 \mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times K_{2})\cong\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2} and unstable otherwise. If an unstable graph is connected, non-bipartite and any two of its distinct vertices have different neighbourhoods, then it is called nontrivially unstable. We establish conditions guaranteeing the instability of various graph products, including direct products, direct product bundles, Cartesian products, strong products, semi-strong products, and lexicographic products. Inspired by a condition for the instability of direct product bundles, we propose a new sufficient condition for circulant graphs to be unstable and refine existing instability conditions from the literature. Based on these results, we categorize unstable circulant graphs into two distinct types and further propose a classification framework. Keywords: direct product; stable graph; circulant graph

Classifying all nontrivially unstable members within an infinite family of graphs is extremely difficult. Such a classification remains incomplete even for circulant graphs. In

They proved that any circulant graph satisfying one of Wilson’s conditions must also satisfy at least one of theirs, and demonstrated the existence of circulant graphs that satisfy their new conditions yet lie outside the scope of Wilson’s framework.

A key question in the study of circulant graphs is whether the conditions proposed by Hujdurović et al. characterize all nontrivially unstable cases. This problem can be approached in two ways: either by confirming the conditions for specific families of circulant graphs or by exploring new conditions that lead to the instability of circulant graphs.

For the first approach, existing results show that no nontrivially unstable circulant graphs exist within arc-transitive families

For the second approach, Jiang and Zhang

The lack of counterexamples with order

To address this challenge, we focus on foundational graph constructions by investigating the stability of several kinds of graph products: direct products, direct product bundles, Cartesian products, strong products, semi-strong products, and lexicographic products. Through this framework, we develop multiple families of nontrivially unstable circulant graphs. Analysis of one such family reveals a new sufficient condition for instability in circulant graphs. Building on this, we refine the conditions proposed by Hujdurović et al. and formulate several conjectures regarding the stability of circulant graphs.

Let
\Gamma

\Sigma

(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow a=b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y,~\mbox{or}~a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y,~\mbox{or}~a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x=y;

(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{or}~a=b,~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y;

(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma[\Sigma]}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow a\sim_{\Gamma}b,~\mbox{or}~a=b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y.

Preliminaries

Employing the language of symmetric
(0,1)
-matrices, Marušič et al.
[
15
]
established a necessary and sufficient condition for stability of connected non-bipartite graphs. In view of TF-morphisms, this result can be reformulated as follows:

Lemma 2.1
.

A connected non-bipartite graph is unstable if and only if it has a nontrivial TF-morphism.

While this restatement was first proposed by Lauri et al.
[
14
]
, the non-bipartiteness condition was inadvertently overlooked. We emphasize that this condition is crucial, as its omission compromises the validity of the criterion. For instance,
K_{2}
is unstable yet admits no TF-morphism.

The framework of TF-morphisms emphasizes the intrinsic structural properties of the graph itself, providing a streamlined method for analyzing stability. We now apply this framework to establish the following lemma.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two connected graphs, and let
L
and
R
be symmetric relations on
V(\Gamma)
and
V(\Sigma)
, respectively. Let
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
denote the graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
such that two vertices
(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\ast\Sigma}(b,y)
if and only if
a\sim_{L}b
and
x\sim_{R}y
.
If either
\Gamma
is unstable with
E(\Gamma)=L
or
\Sigma
is unstable with
E(\Sigma)=R
, then
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
is unstable.

Proof
.

Without loss of any generality, we assume that
\Sigma
is unstable and
R=E(\Sigma)
.

If
\Sigma
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
\{U,W\}
, then it is obvious that
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
is a bipartite graph with bipartition
\{V(\Gamma)\times U,V(\Gamma)\times W\}
. Since
\Sigma
is connected and unstable, it has a nontrivial automorphism, say
\sigma
. It is straightforward to check that the permutation
(1,\sigma)
on
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
is a nontrivial automorphism of
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
. Thus
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
is unstable.

Now we assume that
\Sigma
is non-bipartite. Since
\Sigma
is unstable, by Lemma
2.1
there exists a TF-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)
of
\Sigma
such that
\alpha\neq\beta
. Since
R=E(\Sigma)
, we have
x\sim_{R}y\Longleftrightarrow x\sim_{\Sigma}y
. Consider the two permutations
(1,\alpha)
and
(1,\beta)
on

. Then
(1,\alpha)\neq(1,\beta)
and

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\ast\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{L}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{R}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{L}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{L}b~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha}\sim_{\Sigma}y^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{L}b~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha}\sim_{R}y^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x^{\alpha})\sim_{\Gamma\ast\Sigma}(b,y^{\beta})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{(1,\alpha)}\sim_{\Gamma\ast\Sigma}(b,y)^{(1,\beta)}.

Therefore

is a nontrivial TF-morphism of

\Gamma
, the composition
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is clearly a
derangement
(see
[
2
, Definition 1.1.1]
), meaning it fixes no vertex of
\Gamma
. The following lemma describes the relationship between TF-morphisms and TFS-morphisms.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two permutations on the vertex set
V(\Gamma)
of a graph
\Gamma
. Then
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma
if and only if
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is a derangement and
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TF-morphism of
\overline{\Gamma}
.

Proof
.

First we prove the necessity.
Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma
. For
u\in V(\Gamma)
, since
u^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}u^{\beta}
, we have
u^{\alpha\beta^{-1}}\neq u
and therefore
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is a derangement.
By Remark
1.3
(ii), we have

u\sim_{\Gamma}v\Longleftrightarrow u^{\alpha}=v^{\beta}~\mbox{or}~u^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}v^{\beta}

and it follows that

u\sim_{\overline{\Gamma}}v\Longleftrightarrow u^{\alpha}\sim_{\overline{\Gamma}}v^{\beta}.

for any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(\Gamma)
.
Thus
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TF-morphism of
\overline{\Gamma}
.

Now we prove the sufficiency. Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a TF-morphism of
\overline{\Gamma}
and
\alpha\beta^{-1}
be a derangement. By the definition of TF-morphism, we have that
(\alpha,\beta)
acts as a permutation on the set

\{(u,v)\mid u\sim_{\overline{\Gamma}}v,~u,v\in V(\Gamma)\}
. Thus either
u^{\alpha}=v^{\beta}
or
u^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}v^{\beta}
for any two vertices
u
and
v
not adjacent in
\overline{\Gamma}
. In particular,
u\sim_{\Gamma}v
implies
u^{\alpha}=v^{\beta}
or
u^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}v^{\beta}
. Since
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is a derangement, we have
u^{\alpha}\neq u^{\beta}
and it follows that
u^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}u^{\beta}
.
Therefore
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma
.
∎

(i)

\Gamma\Box\Sigma

\Gamma
and
\Sigma

(ii)

\Gamma\Box\Sigma

\Gamma

(iii)

\Gamma\Box\Sigma
is

Proof
.

(iii) The sufficiency is obviously true. Now we prove the necessity. Assume that
\Gamma\Box\Sigma

\Lambda:=\Gamma\Box\Sigma
and let
(a,x)
and
(b,y)

\Lambda(a,x)=\Lambda(b,y)

Since

\Lambda(a,x)=\left(\Gamma(a)\times\{x\}\right)\cup\left(\{a\}\times\Sigma(x)\right)~\mbox{and}~\Lambda(b,y)=\left(\Gamma(b)\times\{y\}\right)\cup\left(\{b\}\times\Sigma(y)\right),

\left(\Gamma(a)\times\{x\}\right)\cup\left(\{a\}\times\Sigma(x)\right)=\left(\Gamma(b)\times\{y\}\right)\cup\left(\{b\}\times\Sigma(y)\right).

(1)

Noting that

\Lambda(a,x)\neq\Lambda(b,x)

Combining the equation

\Gamma(a)\times\{x\}=\{b\}\times\Sigma(y)~\mbox{and}~\{a\}\times\Sigma(x)=\Gamma(b)\times\{y\}.

\Gamma(a)=\{b\},~\{x\}=\Sigma(y),\{a\}=\Gamma(b)~\mbox{and}~\Sigma(x)=\{y\}.

It follows that the four vertices

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
and

(ii)

(iii)

\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma

Proof
.

(iii) Write
\Lambda=\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
. Let
a,b
be two distinct vertices of
\Gamma
and
x,y
be two distinct vertices of
\Sigma
. We will complete the proof by confirming two claims as follows.

Claim 1.

\Lambda(a,x)\neq\Lambda(a,y)

.

Let

(iii) For convenience, set

(2)

4
Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4

In this section, we prove Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
. For clarity, we will restate both theorems before proceeding with their proofs.

Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two graphs. We have

(i)

if
\Gamma
or
\Sigma
is unstable, then
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is unstable;

(ii)

if
S(\Sigma)
and
S(\Gamma)
are coprime with respect to Cartesian product and
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are both stable, then
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is stable;

(iii)

if
S(\Gamma)
and
S(\Sigma)
are coprime with respect to Cartesian product and
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable, then either
\Gamma
or
\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable.

Proof
.

(i) It is obvious that the direct product
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is exactly the graph
\Gamma\ast\Sigma
defined in Lemma
2.2
with
L=E(\Gamma)
and
R=E(\Sigma)
. Therefore
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is unstable if one of
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
is unstable.

(ii)
Since
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are stable, they are connected, non-bipartite, and
R
-thin, and, in particular, they contain no isolated vertices.
Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be an arbitrary TF-morphism of
\Gamma\times\Sigma
. By Lemma
2.1
, it suffices to show that
(\alpha,\beta)
is trivial, that is,
\alpha=\beta
. By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma\times\Sigma))
. By Lemma
2.3
, we get
S(\Gamma\times\Sigma)=S(\Gamma)\Box S(\Sigma)
and
therefore
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma)\Box S(\Sigma))
. Since
S(\Sigma)
and
S(\Gamma)
are coprime with respect to Cartesian product, we have
\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma)\Box S(\Sigma))=\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma))\times\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Sigma))
and it follows that
\alpha,\beta\in\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma))\times\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Sigma))
. Thus we can write
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
where
\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}\in\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Gamma))
and

\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}\in\mathrm{Aut}(S(\Sigma))
. In particular, both
\alpha_{1}
and
\beta_{1}
are permutations on
V(\Gamma)
and
both
\alpha_{2}
and
\beta_{2}
are permutations on
V(\Sigma)
.
Since
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TF-morphism of
\Gamma\times\Sigma
, we have

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\times\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma\times\Sigma}(b,y)^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a^{\alpha_{1}},x^{\alpha_{2}})\sim_{\Gamma\times\Sigma}(b^{\beta_{1}},y^{\beta_{2}})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a^{\alpha_{1}}\sim_{\Gamma}b^{\beta_{1}}~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha_{2}}\sim_{\Sigma}y^{\beta_{2}}

for every pair of elements
(a,x)
and
(b,y)
of
\Gamma\times\Sigma
. Thus
a\sim_{\Gamma}b
implies
b^{\alpha_{1}}\sim_{\Gamma}b^{\beta_{1}}
, that is,
(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})
is TF-morphism of
\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is stable, we have
\alpha_{1}=\beta_{1}
. Similarly,
\alpha_{2}=\beta_{2}
. It follows that
\alpha=\beta
and therefore
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is stable.

(iii) Since
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable,
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is an unstable graph which is connected,
R
-thin and non-bipartite. By the conclusion of (ii), either
\Gamma
or
\Sigma
is unstable. By Lemma
3.1
, both
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are connected,
R
-thin and non-bipartite. Thus either
\Gamma
or
\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable.
∎

By using Theorem
1.1
, we prove the following result on the stability of graph pairs.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two non-bipartite stable graphs whose orders are coprime. Then the graph pair
(\Gamma,\Sigma\times K_{2})
is stable.

Proof
.

Since both
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are stable, they are connected and
R
-thin. Since the orders of
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are coprime, we conclude that
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
are coprime with respect to direct product and
S(\Sigma)
and
S(\Gamma)
are coprime with respect to Cartesian product. It follows from Theorem
1.1
(ii) that
\Gamma\times\Sigma
is stable. Therefore
\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times\Sigma\times K_{2})=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times\Sigma)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Since a pair of two connected non-bipartite graphs is stable if the two graphs are both
R
-thin and they are coprime with respect to direct product
[
6
, Theorem 8.18]
, we conclude that the pair
(\Gamma,\Sigma)
is stable and it follows that

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times\Sigma\times K_{2})=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times\Sigma)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Since
\Sigma
is stable, we have
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma\times K_{2})=\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and hence

\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma\times\Sigma\times K_{2})=\mathrm{Aut}(\Gamma)\times\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma\times K_{2}).

Therefore
(\Gamma,\Sigma\times K_{2})
is stable.
∎

Theorem
1.4
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two graphs and
p
be a mapping from
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
to
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
such that
p(a,b)=p(b,a)^{-1}
for every
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
. Then
\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma
is unstable if one of the following two statements holds:

(i)

\Gamma
has a nontrivial TF-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)
such that

p(a^{\alpha},b^{\beta})=p(a,b)
for every
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
;

(ii)

there is a TF-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)
of
\Sigma
and a permutation
\theta
on
V(\Sigma)
such that
\alpha\neq\theta
and
\theta p(a,b)^{-1}=p(a,b)^{-1}\beta
for every
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
.

Proof
.

(i) Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma
such that
p(a^{\alpha},b^{\beta})=p(a,b)
for every
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
. Then
\alpha\neq\beta
and

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y^{p(a,b)^{-1}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}b^{\beta}~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y^{p(a^{\alpha},b^{\beta})^{-1}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a^{\alpha},x)\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b^{\beta},y)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{(\alpha,1)}\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b,y)^{(\beta,1)}.

Therefore
((\alpha,1),(\beta,1))
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma\times\Sigma
.
By Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma
is unstable.

(ii) Since
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TF-morphism of
\Sigma
and
\theta p(a,b)^{-1}=p(a,b)^{-1}\beta
for every

, we have

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y^{p(a,b)^{-1}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha}\sim_{\Sigma}y^{p(a,b)^{-1}\beta}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha}\sim_{\Sigma}y^{\theta p(a,b)^{-1}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x^{\alpha})\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b,y^{\theta})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{(1,\alpha)}\sim_{\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma}(b,y)^{(1,\theta)}.

Therefore

is a TF-morphism of

.

. Define two permutations
\alpha
and
\beta
on
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
as follows:

(a,x)^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(a,z_{2}),&\hbox{if}~a\in U~\hbox{and}~x=z_{1};\\
(a,z_{1}),&\hbox{if}~a\in U~\hbox{and}~x=z_{2};\\
(a,x),&\hbox{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

and

(a,x)^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}(a,z_{2}),&\hbox{if}~a\in W~\hbox{and}~x=z_{1};\\
(a,z_{1}),&\hbox{if}~a\in W~\hbox{and}~x=z_{2};\\
(a,x),&\hbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Let
(a,x)
and
(b,y)
be two adjacent vertices of
\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
where
a,b\in V(\Gamma)
and
x,y\in V(\Sigma)
. Then we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}a=b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y,~\mbox{or}\\
a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y,~\mbox{or}\\
a\sim_{\Gamma}b~\mbox{and}~x=y.\end{array}\right.

By the definition of
\alpha
and
\beta
, we can set
(a,x)^{\alpha}=(a,x^{\prime})
and
(b,y)^{\beta}=(b,y^{\prime})
.

If
\{x,y\}\cap\{z_{1},z_{2}\}=\emptyset
,
then
(a,x)^{\alpha}=(a,x)
and
(b,y)^{\beta}=(b,y)
.
If
x=y\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
, then
a\sim_{\Gamma}b
and
x^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. In either case, we have
(a,x)^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(b,y)^{\beta}
.

Now we assume
\{x,y\}\cap\{z_{1},z_{2}\}\neq\emptyset
and
x\neq y
. Then
y\sim_{\Sigma}x
. Since
z_{1}\nsim_{\Sigma}z_{2}
, we get
\{x,y\}\neq\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. Therefore either
x\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
and
y\notin\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
or
x\notin\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
and
y\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. It follows that either
x^{\prime}\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
and
y^{\prime}=y
or
x^{\prime}=x
and
y^{\prime}\in\{z_{1},z_{2}\}
. Since
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
have the same neighbourhood, we have that
y^{\prime}\sim_{\Sigma}x^{\prime}
. It follows that
(a,x)^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(b,y)^{\beta}
.

Note that
\alpha\neq\beta
and we have proved that
(a,x)^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(b,y)^{\beta}
for any two adjacent vertices
(a,x)
and
(b,y)
of
\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
.
Therefore
(\alpha,\beta)
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
is unstable.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two graphs, one of which has a TFS-morphism. Then
\overline{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}
is unstable.

Proof
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\Gamma
has a TFS-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)
. Then
a^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}a^{\beta}
for every
a\in V(\Gamma)
and
b^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}c^{\beta}
or
b^{\alpha}=c^{\beta}
for each pair of adjacent vertices
b,c\in V(\Gamma)
. This implies that
(a^{\alpha},x)\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(a^{\beta},x)
for every
a\in V(\Gamma)
and either
(b^{\alpha},x)\sim_{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}(c^{\beta},y)
or
(b^{\alpha},x)=(c^{\beta},y)
for each pair of adjacent vertices
(b,x),(c,y)\in V(\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma)
. Thus
((\alpha,1),(\beta,1))
is a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma
. By Lemma
2.5
,
((\alpha,1),(\beta,1))
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\overline{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}
. Therefore
\overline{\Gamma\boxtimes\Sigma}
is unstable.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two connected graphs which has at least two vertices. Then
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is unstable if one of the following two holds:

(i)

\Sigma
is unstable;

(ii)

\Gamma
has a TFS-morphism.

Proof
.

(i) Note that
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is the graph defined in Lemma
2.2
with
R=E(\Sigma)
and
L=E(\Gamma)\cup\{(a,a)\mid a\in V(\Gamma)\}
. Since
\Sigma
is unstable, Lemma
2.2
implies that
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is unstable.

(ii) Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma
. We will confirm the instability of
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
by showing that
((\alpha,1),(\beta,1))
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
. By the definition of TFS-morphism, we have that
a^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}a^{\beta}
for every
a\in V(\Gamma)
and
b^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}c^{\beta}
or
b^{\alpha}=c^{\beta}
for each pair of adjacent vertices
b
and
c
of
\Gamma
.
Therefore
(\alpha,1)\neq(\beta,1)
and

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(a,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}a^{\beta}~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a^{\alpha},x)\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(a^{\beta},y)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{(\alpha,1)}\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(a,y)^{(\beta,1)}

for
(a,x),(a,y)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
.
Now let
(b,x)
and
(c,y)
be a pair of vertices of
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
with

b\neq c
. Since
b^{\alpha}\sim_{\Sigma}c^{\beta}
or
b^{\alpha}=c^{\beta}
whenever
b\sim_{\Gamma}c
, we have

\displaystyle(b,x)\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(c,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle b\sim_{\Gamma}c~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle b^{\alpha}\sim_{\Gamma}c^{\beta}~\mbox{or}~b^{\alpha}=c^{\beta},~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(b^{\alpha},x)\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(c^{\beta},y)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(b,x)^{(\alpha,1)}\sim_{\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma}(c,y)^{(\beta,1)}.

Therefore
((\alpha,1),(\beta,1))
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is unstable.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two graphs. If
\Sigma
has a nontrivial TF-morphism, then
\Gamma[\Sigma]
is unstable.

Proof
.

Let
(\alpha,\beta)
be a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Sigma
. Then
\alpha\neq\beta
. Consider the two permutations
(1,\alpha)
and
(1,\beta)
on
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
. Then
(1,\alpha)\neq(1,\beta)
and

\displaystyle(a,x)\sim_{\Gamma[\Sigma]}(b,y)\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b,~\mbox{or}~a=b~\mbox{and}~x\sim_{\Sigma}y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a\sim_{\Gamma}b,~\mbox{or}~a=b~\mbox{and}~x^{\alpha}\sim_{\Sigma}y^{\beta}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x^{\alpha})\sim_{\Gamma[\Sigma]}(b,y^{\beta})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(a,x)^{(1,\alpha)}\sim_{\Gamma[\Sigma]}(b,y)^{(1,\beta)}.

Therefore
((1,\alpha),(1,\beta))
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma[\Sigma]
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma[\Sigma]
is unstable.
∎

6
Constructions

In this section, we use graph products to construct nontrivially unstable circulant graphs.
Let
S
be a subset of a group
G
that does not contain the identity element and is closed to the inverse operation. The
Cayley graph

\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
of
G
with the
connection set

S
is defined to be the
graph with vertex set
G
such that two elements
x,y
are
adjacent if and only if
yx^{-1}\in S
(or
y-x\in S
if
G
is an additive group).
In particular, we call
\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)
a
circulant graph
if
G
is cyclic.

We begin by stating two propositions, which follow directly from Theorems
1.4
(ii) and
1.5
(iv), respectively.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Sigma
be two graphs. If
\Sigma
has an automorphism
\rho
and an involutory automorphism
\delta
such that
\delta^{-1}\rho\delta\neq\rho
, then
\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma
is unstable where
p(a,b)=\delta
for all
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
.

Proof
.

Let
\alpha=\beta=\rho
and
\theta=\delta^{-1}\rho\delta
. Then
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TF-morphism of
\Sigma
,
\alpha\neq\theta
and
\theta p(a,b)^{-1}=\delta^{-1}\rho=p(a,b)^{-1}\beta
for every
(a,b)\in V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
. By Theorem
1.4
(ii),
\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma
is unstable.
∎

Let
K_{n}
denote the complete graph of order
n
.
Every pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of permutations on
V(K_{n})
, for which
\alpha\beta^{-1}
is a derangement, clearly constitutes a TFS-morphism of
K_{n}
. However, Lemma
3.5
(iii) implies that
K_{n}\ltimes\Sigma
is
R
-thick.
Thus,
K_{n}
cannot serve as
\Gamma
in constructing a nontrivially unstable graph
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
.
On the other hand, removing a perfect matching from
K_{2n}

produces a graph that is a suitable candidate for this purpose.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\Gamma
be a graph obtained from
K_{2n}
by removing a perfect matching. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, non-bipartite and
R
-thin graph. Then
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable.

Proof
.

Let
K_{2n}
be the complete graph on the set
\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{n},v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
and
\Gamma
be the graph obtained from
K_{2n}
by removing the edges jointing
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.
Let
\alpha=(u_{1}\,u_{2}\,\ldots\,u_{n})
and
\beta=(v_{1}\,v_{2}\,\ldots\,v_{n})
be two permutations on
V(\Gamma)
. It is a simple matter to check that
(\alpha,\beta)
is a TFS-morphism of
\Gamma
. By Theorem
1.5
(iv),
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is stable. It is obvious that
\Gamma
is connected and non-bipartite. Furthermore, it is straightforward to check that
\Gamma[a]\neq\Gamma[b]
for each pair of distinct vertices
a
and
b
of
\Gamma
. Since
\Sigma
is connected, non-bipartite and
R
-thin, by Lemma
3.5
we have that
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is connected, non-bipartite and
R
-thin. Therefore
\Gamma\ltimes\Sigma
is nontrivially unstable.
∎

Now we use graph products to construct nontrivially unstable circulant graphs.

Example 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{30},\{\pm 1,\pm 4\})
and
\Sigma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},\{1,-1\})
where
n\geq 3
. For each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, define a mapping
t_{i}:\mathbb{Z}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{n},~x\mapsto x+i,\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Let
\Lambda=\Gamma\times^{p}\Sigma
where
p
is the mapping from
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Sigma)
to
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
defined as follows:

p(a,b)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t_{1},&\hbox{if}~b-a=1~\mbox{or}~-4;\\
t_{-1},&\hbox{if}~b-a=-1~\mbox{or}~4;\\
t_{0},&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Then
\Lambda
is an unstable Cayley graph on
\mathbb{Z}_{30}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. In particular, if
(30,n)=1
, then
\Lambda
is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph.

Proof
.

Clearly,
t_{0}
is the identity element of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
and
t_{1},t_{-1}
are two mutually inverse elements of
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
. Therefore
p
is well defined. Define two permutations
\alpha
and
\beta
on
\mathbb{Z}_{30}
by the rule:
a^{\alpha}=11a
and
a^{\beta}=11a+15
for all

is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma
and
p(a^{\alpha},b^{\beta})=p(11a,11b+15)=p(a,b)
. By Theorem
1.4
(i),
\Lambda
is unstable. It is a simple matter to verify that

\Lambda=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{30}\times\mathbb{Z}_{n},\{\pm(1,0),\pm(1,2),\pm(4,0),\pm(4,-2)\})

Moreover, if

is odd, then
\Lambda
is connected,
R
-thin, and non-bipartite.

If

, then

and

is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{30n},\{\pm n,\pm(n+60),\pm 4n,\pm(4n-60)\}),

which is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph.
∎

.

Let

and

where

for every

.

Since

7
On the stability of circulant graphs

As mentioned in the introduction, Wilson
[
23
]
proposed four sufficient conditions for the instability of circulant graphs. However, two of these conditions were later identified as flawed and corrected by Qin et al.
[
18
]
and Hujdurović et al.
[
8
]
, respectively. The following lemma incorporates Wilson’s two original valid conditions alongside the corrected versions proposed by Qin et al. and Hujdurović et al..

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
X=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
be a circulant graph of even order.
Let
S_{e}=S\cap 2\mathbb{Z}_{n}
and
S_{o}=S\setminus S_{e}
. If any of the following conditions is true, then
X
is unstable.

\mathrm{C.}1.\,

There is a nonzero element

Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S)
be a circulant graph. If
\mathrm{Cay}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},(S\setminus\{m\})+m\big)
has an automorphism fixing
0
but moving
m
, then
\Gamma
is unstable.

Proof
.

Let
\sigma
be an automorphism of
\mathrm{Cay}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},(S\setminus\{m\})+m\big)
such that
0^{\sigma}=0
and
m^{\sigma}\neq m
. Then
y-x\in(S\setminus\{m\})+m\Longleftrightarrow y^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}\in(S\setminus\{m\})+m
for each pair of elements
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
. Define two permutations
\alpha
and
\beta
on
\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
by the rule:

x^{\alpha}=x^{\sigma}+m
and
x^{\beta}=(x+m)^{\sigma}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
. Then
\alpha\neq\beta
as
0^{\alpha}=0^{\sigma}+m=m
and
0^{\beta}=m^{\sigma}\neq m
.
Since

(x+m)^{\beta}-x^{\alpha}=x^{\sigma}-(x^{\sigma}+m)=m

and

\displaystyle x\sim_{\Gamma}y\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle y-x\in S\setminus\{m\}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle y+m-x\in(S\setminus\{m\})+m

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(y+m)^{\sigma}-x^{\sigma}\in(S\setminus\{m\})+m

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle(y+m)^{\sigma}-(x^{\sigma}+m)\in(S\setminus\{m\})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle y^{\beta}-x^{\alpha}\in(S\setminus\{m\})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle y^{\beta}\sim_{\Gamma}x^{\alpha}

for each pair of elements
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
with
y\neq x+m
, it follows that
(\alpha,\beta)
is a nontrivial TF-morphism of
\Gamma
. By Lemma
2.1
,
\Gamma
is unstable.

Lemma 7.9

Let

(i)

(iv)

If

and

\mathrm{Cay}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},(S\setminus\{m\})+m\big)

.

(i) Set

and

and

(ii) Since
2\parallel|H|
,
2\mid|K|
and

and

imply

. Set
L=K+H
and

. Then

, the inclusion

.

(iii) Since

and

and

. First, assume condition (1) of Lemma

holds. Let

and

. Since

, we conclude that the Sylow

Let
\ell
be the index of

in
\mathbb{Z}_{2m}

\mathbb{Z}_{2m}

on

(i+x)^{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i+x+m,&\hbox{if}~x\in L_{o};\\

and

\Gamma

(i-j)+(x-y)=(i+x)-(j+y)\in S.

By the definition of

(j+y)^{\sigma}-(i+x)^{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(i-j)+(x-y),&\hbox{if either}~x,y\in L_{o}~\hbox{or}~x,y\notin L_{o};\\

(iv) Since

Concluding remark

Recently, Jiang and Zhang proposed a method for constructing unstable graphs from bipartite graphs (
[
11
, Construction 3.1]
). They applied this construction to Cayley graphs, deriving a sufficient condition for the instability of Cayley graphs of groups containing a subgroup of index
2
(
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
). The following proposition focuses on the case of circulant graphs, refining
[
11
, Theorem 4.1]
for this specific class.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S)
be a circulant graph. Let
S_{o}=S\setminus 2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
and
S_{e}=S\cap 2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
. Suppose the circulant graph
\Gamma_{o}:=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S_{o})
admits two distinct automorphisms,
\sigma
and
\rho
, with the following properties:

(i)

both
\sigma
and
\rho
preserve
2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
set-wise;

(ii)

y^{\rho}-x^{\sigma}\in S_{e}
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
such that
y-x\in S_{e}
.

Then
\Gamma
is unstable.

Remark 8.2
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S)
be a circulant graph satisfying the condition in Lemma
8.1
. Then the Cayley graph
\Gamma_{e}:=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S_{e})
decomposes into a disjoint union of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{e}
, where
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{e}
are the
subgraphs of
\Gamma_{e}
induced by

2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
and
1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
, respectively. Clearly,
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}:=\mathrm{Cay}(2\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S_{e})
and
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{e}\cong\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
. Since both
\sigma
and
\rho
preserve
2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
, they also preserve
1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
. Condition (ii) in Lemma
8.1
ensures that the restriction of
(\sigma,\rho)
to
2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
is a TF-morphism of
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
, and its restriction to
1+2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
is a TF-morphism of
\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{e}
. Since
\sigma\neq\rho
, we conclude that the circulant graph
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
has a nontrivial TF-morphism and is therefore unstable. Thus Lemma
8.1
establishes a method for constructing large unstable circulant graph from smaller ones.

Conjecture 8.5
.

Let
m
be an odd integer. If
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S)
is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph of Type II, then
\Gamma\cong\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S+m)
.

If a nontrivially unstable circulant graph
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{2m},S)
satisfies condition (i) of Theorem
1.7
, then
\Gamma
admits a TF-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)
defined as follows:

x^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+h,&\hbox{if}~x\in K_{o};\\
x,&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.~~~\mbox{and}~~~x^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+h,&\hbox{if}~x\in 2K;\\
x,&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

where
h
is a nonidentity element of
H
. Obviously, both
\alpha
and
\beta
preserve
2\mathbb{Z}_{2m}
set-wise. By Lemma
8.3
,
\Gamma
is of Type I.

If
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{4h},S)
is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph satisfying the condition in Lemma
7.7

\Gamma
admits a
TF-morphism
(\alpha,\beta)

x^{\alpha}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+h,&\hbox{if}~x\in K_{o};\\
x-h,&\hbox{if}~x\in h+K_{o};\\
x,&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.~~~\mbox{and}~~~x^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x+h,&\hbox{if}~x\in 2K;\\
x-h,&\hbox{if}~x\in h+2K;\\

Furthermore, if
h

\alpha
and
\beta

2\mathbb{Z}_{4h}

8.3

\Gamma

h

\Gamma
may be of Type II, as illustrated in the following example.

Example 8.6
.

Let
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{20},\{\pm 1,\pm 4,\pm 9,10\})
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}=\mathrm{Cay}(2\mathbb{Z}_{20},\{\pm 4,10\})
. Then
\Gamma
satisfies the condition in Lemma
7.7
and is therefore unstable. Based on the classification of unstable circulant graph of twice an odd prime

[
8
, Theorem 5.1.]

\Gamma^{\prime}_{e}
is stable. It follows from Remark
8.2
that
\Gamma
is of Type II.

We end the paper with the following two conjectures.

Conjecture 8.7
.

Let
h
be an odd integer. If
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{4h},S)
is a nontrivially unstable circulant graph of Type II, then
\mathrm{Cay}\big(\mathbb{Z}_{4h},(S\setminus\{2h\})+2h\big)
has an automorphism fixing
0
but moving
2h
.

Conjecture 8.8
.

Let
n
be a positive integer divisible by
8
. Then every nontrivially unstable circulant graph
\Gamma=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{n},S)
is of Type I.

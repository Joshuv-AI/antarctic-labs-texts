Low dose gamma irradiation study of ATLAS ITk MD8 diodes
Y. Unno

Abstract
Silicon strip detectors developed for the Inner Tracker (ITk) [ 1 ] of the ATLAS experiment [ 2 ] will operate in a harsh radiation environment of the HL-LHC accelerator. The ITk is thus designed to endure a total fluence of 1.6 × \times 10 15 1-MeV n eq / {}_{\mathrm{eq}}/ cm 2 and a total ionizing dose (TID) of 66 Mrad in the strip detector region. A radiation-hard n + n^{+} -in- p p technology is implemented in the ITk strip sensors. To achieve the required radiation hardness, extensive irradiation studies were conducted during sensor development, primarily performed up to the maximal expected total fluence and TID to ensure the full functionality of the detector at its end-of-life. These studies included irradiations of sensors with various particle types and energies, including the 60 Co γ \gamma -rays. Our previous results obtained for γ \gamma -irradiated diodes and strip sensors indicate a linear increase of bulk current with TID, while the surface current saturates at the lowest TID levels checked (66 Mrad), preventing a determination of the exact TID for which the observed saturation occurs. This work presents the results coming from irradiations by 60 Co γ \gamma -rays to multiple low TIDs, ranging from 0.5 to 100 krad. The detailed study of total, bulk, and surface currents of diodes explores an unknown dependence of surface current on the TID, annealing, and temperature. Additionally, the effect of the p p -stop implant between the bias and the guard ring of measured samples is shown. The observations are relevant for the initial operations of the new ATLAS tracker.

4
Conclusions

This study investigates the effects of
60
Co
\gamma
-rays on the total, bulk, and surface currents in
n^{+}
-in-
p
MD8 diodes from ATLAS18 ITk production wafers, exposed to the TIDs ranging from 0.5 to 100 krad. Pre-irradiation, the magnitudes of the bulk and surface currents of MD8 diodes are comparable; however, post-irradiation, the increase in total current is primarily driven by the surface current, with bulk current remaining stable up to 100 krad.
The surface current saturation was not observed up to the maximum TID of 100 krad used in this study, whereas in our previous study of
\gamma
-irradiated
MD8 diodes
[
3
]
, saturation of radiation-induced surface charges occurred at the minimal tested TID of 66 Mrad. The results thus clearly indicate that the saturation range lies between 100 krad and 66 Mrad. Additionally, the bulk and surface currents are slightly higher in MD8p diodes with
p
-stop implant between BR and GR than in the regular MD8 diodes without this
p
-stop implant.

Annealing experiments at 60
∘
C demonstrated that total, bulk, and surface currents exhibit slight increases with annealing time, especially at bias voltages close to full depletion. This current increase is also observed for temperatures up to 100
∘
C. However, for annealing temperatures above 100
∘
C, all three currents decrease significantly, returning to levels comparable to pre-irradiation values. Radiation-induced defects in both the bulk and surface, caused by
\gamma
-irradiation
, appear to be completely annealed at high temperatures. For comparison, in reference
[
13
]
, where
n^{+}
-in-
p
diodes were studied after higher TIDs of
\gamma
-irradiation up to 100–200 Mrad, the current remains stable up to 200
∘
C, then steeply increases till the temperature of 260
∘
C, and subsequently decreases sharply to the maximum studied temperature of 300
∘
C.
No differences were observed between the activation energies derived from temperature dependence measured for total, surface, and bulk currents.

Incorporating defects and moisture in MOE evaluation for automated timber grading
Authors: Min Ji, Wei Zhang, Liping Cai, Yang Zhao, Hu Miao, Rui Gao, Guofu Wang, Jiakai Han, Xingliang Diao, Ziqiong Zha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn today’s industrial landscape, automated timber structural grading plays a pivotal role in optimizing productivity and operational efficiency. The anisotropic nature of wood renders standard visual grading a challenging task, often resulting in a grading process with low repeatability. Moreover, traditional grading methods are not only time-consuming but also susceptible to human bias and inconsistencies, which may lead to underestimation of the timber’s mechanical potential. To address these challenges, this study proposes a grading method that integrates external quality evaluation of timber and the internal elastic modulus properties. Additionally, an automated production line is developed featuring automatic loading and unloading, machine vision optimized sorting, online moisture content detection, mechanical stress grading, multi-sensor data integration, and remote operation and maintenance diagnostic functions. The developed intelligent automated grading system included five sections, including Automatic loading and unloading, Machine vision, Moisture content detection, Mechanical stress grading, and multi-sensor data coupling and remote O&M diagnosis. The novelty of this study included integrating the influences of multiple factors such as defects and moisture content, as well as their dynamic modulus of elasticity in the grading operations of structural timber. The methodologies employed not only promoted broader adoption and deeper understanding of automation in the wood processing industry but also positioned themselves as exemplary applications of advanced automation technology. The practical applicability of automated grading technology in industrial production lines was thoroughly validated. Experimental outcomes unequivocally demonstrated that the automated solution markedly enhanced grading efficiency while concomitantly reducing labor costs. This automated structural timber grading line was successfully certified by the Japanese JAS (Japanese Agricultural Standard) in 2023, which is the highest level of certification for organic agricultural products by the Ministry of Agriculture, Forestry, and Fisheries of Japan. This ensures that the graded structural timber exported to Japan meets the Japanese Agricultural Standards. By incorporating advanced automation technology, this grading process section can be flexibly integrated into automated wood processing production lines. This advancement anticipated the realization of intelligent and automated production line management, thereby providing robust technical underpinnings for the transformation and upgrading of the wood manufacturing industry.Keywords: Wooden buildings, Constructional timber, Automation, Detection, GradingSubject terms: Electrical and electronic engineering, Mechanical engineering

Introduction

With the development and application of wooden buildings (Fig. 1)1,2, there is a significant demand for continuous, rapid non-destructive testing and evaluation of structural timber. There is an urgent need to establish a rapid evaluation mechanism for the modulus of elasticity (MOE) and modulus of rupture (MOR) of full-size structural timber to accurately meet the demands for the development and application of large-size beam and column members, as well as new types of structural lumber3,4.

Wooden buildings. In the figure, on the left: the wooden main venue of Yangzhou Garden Expo Park in China, and on the right: the swimming pool of Chongming Sports Training Base in Shanghai, China.

Structural timber has great variability, low material utilization rate, and different natural defects and processing defects in the raw material grading process5,6,7. The grading tends to underestimate properties due to built-in safety margins. Currently, structural timber grading primarily relies on machine grading systems supported by non-destructive testing methods, while these properties are mainly obtained through laboratory-scale three-point bending tests in accordance with ASTM D1037-12.8,9. Manual visual classification of structural timber exhibits certain shortcomings such as subjectivity, dependence on experience, high labor costs, low efficiency10,11. With advancements in artificial intelligence and machine learning technology, camera sensing technology and deep learning algorithms are integrated to create an intelligent processing center. This center utilizes machine vision grading method to replace manual visual grading of structural timber and other applications12–14. Usually, using a three-point bending process to assess the mechanical properties of structural sawn timber (such as MOE and MOR) is a destructive test, hence only a limited number of samples can be tested in the actual production15–17. Therefore, there is a need for a non-destructive, rapid detection method that can meet the requirements of mass production grading operations18–21. Simultaneously, the mechanical stress grading method can fulfill the requirements to a certain extent22,23. Currently, in some countries (e.g. European Union24, Australia25, Japan26, etc.), to enhance the quality of timber frame construction (Fig. 2), standards for structural timber application mandate mechanical stress grading27. At present, The Microtech Goldeneye multi-sensor quality scanner family allows you to recognize wood features and defects reliably and accurately to automate, streamline and optimize your production28. The modular design of Luxscan CombiScan Sense combines various sensor technologies as modules. The laser camera can detect defects on four sides and classify and sort them according to the grade of sawn timber29. But factors such as the limited availability of mechanized grading equipment, the relatively immature application of modern technologies, and other reasons, the automation of continuous grading in the factory is not widely adopted. The barriers to adoption in small sawmills include cost of equipment, technological inertia, and limited integration. Only a small number of enterprises utilize mechanical stress grading equipment30–32.

Structural components and timber.

In many enterprises, the grading of structural timber still relies on single testing methods and equipment, which is labor-intensive and lacks automation. There is a shortage of automated grading equipment and comprehensive information management systems for the entire grading process33. If only relying on manual visual inspection, it is not possible to accurately assess the mechanical properties of structural timber. Similarly, solely using mechanical stress grading and other methods may not accurately localize surface defects in the structural timber34. The use of machine vision inspection instead of manual visual inspection methods for grading timber can improve the efficiency of timber grading and improve the level of grading intelligence35. Therefore, replacing manual visual inspection with machine vision inspection for grading timber and combining it with mechanical stress detection can provide a more comprehensive assessment of structural timber. This approach help inspectors thoroughly evaluate both the internal structural timber characteristics and surface defects of timber36,37. Based on the current situation of wood processing, there is an urgent need to integrate advanced technologies such as machine vision, intelligent manufacturing, industrial Internet of Things (IoT), etc., to address the commonalities of continuous grading of structural timber, high-precision online inspection of timber quality, and synergy between grading and digitization38.

The aim of this work was to modernize the traditional method of grading structural timber in response to current and emerging technological developments. It focused on exploring optimal grading strategies for automated manufacturing of prefabricated timber frame building materials in factory settings. The novelty of this study lies in integrating the influence of timber moisture content on the modulus of elasticity (MOE) into the grading process, thereby addressing existing limitations in both manual visual grading and current automated timber grading systems. Additionally, this study integrated the functions and equipment of the various components into an automated and intelligent timber grading production line. To tackle existing challenges, an unmanned structural timber grading line was developed, aiming at establishing an industry-leading example of a comprehensive automated grading line for structural timber, leveraging current automation technology.

A new grading method for automatic structures is emerging because of the progressive maturation of advanced automation technology, and it is anticipated that this proposed method will supersede the existing traditional one. Optimization of grading technology

Factors impacting strength qualities were investigated, as well as key indicators for evaluating wood attributes, such as sawn timber surface flaws and moisture content. Mechanical stress grading was determined based on the correlation between strength and stiffness of timber. The deformation of the timber is directly related to MOE and flexural strength (modulus of rupture, MOR) of timber40,41. However, there are some shortcomings in grading timber exclusively based on the value of deformation. Therefore, by combining wood strength and stiffness with timber moisture content and surface defects such as knots, holes, cracks, etc., the accuracy of grading was improved.

Traditional human eye judgment was replaced by new technologies based on artificial intelligence. The timber’s surface features were converted into image signals by the machine vision system, which then sent these signals to a processing system to detect defects. The processing system then gathered morphological data on the various defects and translated it into digital signals. The defects signals were processed by a deep learning-based image processing system to extract the target’s features, allowing for the assessment of the structural timber. The strength grade of timber was thoroughly assessed by combining the results of machine vision surface quality inspection, online timber moisture content detection and mechanical stress detection. Ultimately, the factory production line’s control action was set in accordance with the assessment outcomes mentioned above.

Machine vision optimization sorting

To overcome the limitations of visual grading of timber surface quality, according to “NLGA Standard Grading Rules For Canadian Lumber”42, an optimization grading section based on machine vision has been integrated in the automated grading production line for structural timber, allowing for the identification and detection of timber defects as well as the evaluation of surface quality.

According to Chinese grading standards "GB/T 26899-2011, Structural glued laminated timber"43, the grade of structural timber gradually decreases as the knot size increases. Excessively large knots severely reduce the timber quality, making it unsuitable for structural buildings. Therefore, using machine vision optimization sorting technology, we eliminate the timber that does not meet the criteria for structural components before mechanical stress grading.

Moisture content online detection system

According to Chinese grading standards “GB/T 36407-2018 Machine stress graded lumber”45, “GB/T 1936.1-2009 Method of testing in bending strength of wood”40, the grading result of structural timber is related to the moisture content. Before sawn timber was graded, it needed to be dried first to control the moisture content. 30% is the saturation point of wood fibers. Wood with a moisture content above 30% has poor dimensional stability and is prone to rot and mold. Therefore, in sawn timber processing, the moisture content of the wood is always reduced to below 30%. If the moisture content exceeds 30%, control the moisture content at around 12% through drying to ensure the dimensional stability of the wood. After sawn timber is dried, its moisture content is generally between 9 and 15%.

FD1146 timber stress grading machine

Structural timber was classified into several grades based on the strength properties of the timber in accordance with the requirements of wooden building design, known as timber strength grading. Mechanical stress grading equipment for timber was used for non-destructive strength testing and structural timber grading using mechanical methods. Incorporating mechanical stress grading equipment into structural timber grading lines improves product quality control in the structural timber market by providing fast, non-destructive testing and grading of timber mechanical strength.

Basic structure and working principle

The FD1146 timber stress grading equipment mainly composed of Frame 1, Feed port 2, Conveying mechanism 3, Load test device 4, Clamping support mechanism 5, Protection device 6, Data acquisition system 7, Inkjet system 8 and Outlet port 9, as shown in Fig. 10.

Structure diagram of FD1146 structural timber stress grading equipment.

When the machine started, the three sets of cylinders in the clamping support mechanism acted simultaneously to form a conveyor channel for a set thickness of timber. The timber to be measured was driven by three motors through the detection channel, where the timber was stressed in the upper and lower directions of the two detection areas within the channel to produce constant deformation. The measured load data was fed back to the data processing system through the load test device, and the three groups of position sensors provided feedback on whether the timber had completed the detection and the signal from the effective area. The flexural MOE was calculated by the data processing system46. The data was then compared with the predetermined data grading group, the strength of the timber being measured was graded, and the outcome of this grade was sprayed onto the timber’s surface. The working principle of the device is shown in Fig. 11.

Mechanical stress sorting equipment working principle diagram.

The non-destructive testing mode of " five-point two-way bending-three-point support, two-point center symmetrical loading up and down " divided the detection channel into two detection areas, namely the first detection area and the second detection area. In the middle of each detection area, a load test unit with a loading roller and a force sensor was set up. Two sets of load test units and two sets of conveying mechanisms were paired to use the setable deformation of the sawn timber, and the upper and lower centers were symmetrically arranged. This arrangement method could form a "five-point two-way bending-three-point support, two-point center symmetrical loading up and down " mode when detecting sawn timber. This method could make the upper and lower sides of the sawn timber as the compression surface in the detection process, which could be realized by only three support points and two loading points. Expanding the effective area of sawn timber detection could realize automatic online grading and fast and stable continuous detection.The grading of this system was to test the dynamic elastic modulus and verify it with the static elastic modulus. The mechanical stress grading equipment for structural sawn timber is dynamic and continuous. The load testing unit accurately and in real time displays the load magnitude of sawn timber bending. And finally calculates the dynamic elastic modulus of sawn timber.

Experiments and discussion

The performance of an automated grading line for structural sawn timber is directly related to the usability of graded sawn timber. To ensure that the automated grading line was continuously in good working condition and to effectively guarantee the carrying out of grading inspection work, it was very necessary to carry out experimental research on the performance of grading work. The main work performance of the grading line was tested and studied, specifically the accuracy performance indicators, work efficiency and labor cost. Test sites: Suqian Yasen Machinery Friends New Materials Co., Ltd., Jiangsu Province, China, and Suzhou Crownhomes Co., Ltd., in Suzhou city, Jiangsu Province, China, Pinus densiflora and Picea asperata structural timber with a sawn cross-section dimension of 140◊38 mm and the length between 2800-3000m was used in this test. The materials were ordered directly from the Finnish Timber Construction Company prior to the experiment, and the ordered materials were not graded. In the factory, the moisture content of timber was between 12 and 15%.

In this study, statistical methods, i.e., error analysis, correlation analysis, and regression analysis, were employed to compare the differences between the experimental results and Auto-detected results. We compared the grading results of this production line with the results of the three-point bending test to verify the grading accuracy of this production line. Summary and conclusions

In this study, the status of timber grading for wood structures, which is commonly used today, was comprehensively investigated. In response to the problems of the current grading methods, a comprehensive grading method that considered surface defects, moisture content, and dynamic MOE of structural sawn timber was proposed. Research on processes and components such as automated continuous grading of timber for wood construction was carried out, and the entire production line was designed and laid out. Key technologies for automatic loading and unloading of structural timber, surface quality optimization sorting, online detection of moisture content, and mechanical stress grading were developed. A high-speed, flexible remote management and control integrated information system was realized together with an automated production line control system for structural timber based on the industrial Internet. An important advancement in the integrated processing technology for multifunctional grading of structural timber was developed. When comparing automated continuous grading for structural timber to traditional methods, the following primary advantages are evident: increased productivity, higher throughput, and reduced labor costs. An automated grading production line can help enterprises reduce expenses and enhance production quality and efficiency, particularly when employing machinery for extended periods of time. This automated structural timber grading line was successfully certified by the Japanese JAS (Japanese Agricultural Standard) in 2023, so that graded structural timber exported to Japan meets the Japanese Agricultural Standards47. The contribution of this study lies in integrating the influence of timber moisture content on MOE into the grading process, thereby addressing existing limitations in both manual visual grading and current automated timber grading systems. The factory’s intelligence will significantly increase if the grading line is able to be set up in the wood processing factory in the future. This approach has been somewhat tested in the industry thus far, but more work needs to be done to refine and enhance it in the next production, making the production line more developed, adaptable, stable, and suitable for a variety of working environments.

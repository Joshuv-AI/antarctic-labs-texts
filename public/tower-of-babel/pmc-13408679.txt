Next-generation intelligent framework for pan evaporation prediction: introducing Chebyshev polynomial-based Kolmogorov-Arnold networks
Authors: Amin Gharehbaghi, Salim Heddam, Saeid Mehdizadeh, Sungwon Kim, Il-Moon Chung
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Pan evaporation (Epan) is one of the crucial parameters in hydrological studies, sustainable agricultural development, and water resources management. Predicting Epan remains a challenging problem among researchers worldwide because of its dependency to the diverse climate elements. Hence, it is necessary to precisely predict Epan time series through establishing reliable predictive models. A Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN) is proposed in this study for Epan prediction of two stations located in Australia (Perth and Sydney). Besides the CKAN, three deep learning methods comprising Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), Transformer (TFR), and two machine learning models, namely Classification and Regression Tress (CART) and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) were also developed. The findings demonstrated that the proposed CKAN model performed better than other methods used for predicting Epan at both the stations. Two interpretable techniques, including Shapely Additive eXplanations (SHAP) and local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) were used to reveal the most important inputs. The outcomes for the superior CKAN model under full-input scenario indicated that solar radiation at Perth and minimum temperature at Sydney were found to show most contributions for the global SHAP method, whereas in the selected samples of LIME, mean temperature at Perth and relative humidity at Sydney were generally emerged as the influential input parameters. Finally, a K-fold cross validation technique was utilized for the superior CKAN model, denoting the effectiveness and generalizability of proposed CKAN for prediction of Epan.

Introduction

The hydrological cycle consists of several components, with evaporation is one of its fundamental elements1,2. It is an important parameter needed in various applications, including efficient allocation of water resources systems, climate change impacts assessment, hydrological, climatological, and agricultural studies, water demand for irrigation scheduling, and agricultural practices3,4. Besides the evapotranspiration, knowing the rates of evaporation in a particular region could provide helpful information in sustainable agricultural development, as well as designing and managing smart agricultural systems. Therefore, evaporation should be precisely predicted via reliable methods.

Predicting evaporation is usually a challenging task due to its high influence from other climatic factors like solar radiation, wind speed, air temperature, relative humidity, and consequently its complexity and nonlinearity5. For this, reliable predictive methods must be taken into consideration in predicting Epan. Generally, evaporation prediction methods can be categorized in two different classes, including direct and indirect approaches6,7. Class A pan is typically used in direct measurement of evaporation, referring as pan evaporation (Epan). However, achieving direct measurements of Epan through class A pan is relatively costly and faces with external elements like wind, precipitation, and animals, leading to record unrealistic Epan data5,8. Hence, indirect methods have received more attention among the researchers for predicting Epan. Of indirect methods, artificial intelligence (AI) models, including machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) have gained most popularity recently. In fact, they can be considered as dependable predictive models because of their acceptable performance and potential in predicting target parameter (e.g., Epan in this study) through applying a set of input predictors without requiring to know the complex nonlinear relationships between inputs and outputs. Besides the ML and DL frameworks, a typical form of multi-layer perceptron (MLP) named Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KNN) has been introduced recently. This study considers the application of a Chebyshev Polynomial-Based KNN (CKAN) for the first time. Compared with AI techniques, empirical models as one group of indirect methods have shown inferior performance.

In recent years, a wide range of studies have been done in predicting the Epan time series through implementing intelligent systems like ML and DL models. Zerouali et al.9 developed five hybrid versions of DL models via coupling five optimization algorithms on an long short-term memory (LSTM), and discovered that Biruni Earth Radius-LSTM was found to outperform the other models. Some types of ML and DL were proposed by Novotná et al.10 for Epan prediction. It was concluded that stepwise linear regression was the bets-performing method. Abed et al.11 developed random forest (RF), and two DL frameworks for Epan forecasting, and found out that convolutional neural networks (CNN) illustrated the best outcomes. A diverse set of ML models were established by Al-Mukhtar12 for Epan prediction. The results denoted the superiority of weighted K-nearest neighbor over the other models used. Wang et al.13 developed a hybrid model combining an LSTM and firefly algorithm (FFA), and uncovered that the hybrid FFA-LSTM presented better Epan forecasts compared with standalone LSTM. A hybrid DL model was proposed by Fu and Li14 through hybridizing an LSTM and grey wolf optimization (GWO). The outcomes indicated that the hybrid GWO-LSTM performed better in comparison to simple LSTM. Vishwakarma et al.15 proposed a categorical boosting (CatBoost) method for Epan prediction, and then compared its efficiency with some types of ML models. Their findings showed that the CatBoost and RF were the most accurate models. Adnan et al.16 proposed four ML models based on coupling relevance vector machine (RVM) and four optimizers. The results exhibited that the hybrid models outperformed the individual RVM models, with RVM-improved manta-ray foraging optimization (IMRFO) was the superior one for Epan prediction. Sezen17 utilized a signal decomposition method named ensemble empirical mode decomposition (EEMD) to couple on four types of ML models. It was revealed that the hybrid models showed better accuracy compared with single ML models. Various DL models comprising three standalone and two hybrid DL frameworks were proposed by Goodarzi et al.18 for predicting Epan. The authors reported the higher accuracy of hybrid models than their simple forms, with clockwork recurrent neural network (CWRNN)-LSTM was the bet one. Other studies related to Epan prediction and their results can be found in references such as Ma et al.19, Farokhzadeh et al.20, Abed et al.21, Khosravi et al.22, Kayhomayoon et al.23, etc.

The current study aimed at predicting Epan time series at two stations in Australia, namely Perth and Sydney. For this, some types of intelligent models were developed. The specific aims of this research are the proposal of a Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN), developing five DL and ML models for the better evaluation of proposed CKAN, and applying two explainable techniques to discern the important inputs. In contrast, the detailed objectives of this study can be summarized as follows: to (1) apply KBest feature selection to diagnose the appropriate input configurations, (2) propose Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN), (3) implement three versions of deep learning models like Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), Gated Recurrent Units (GRU), and Transformer (TFR), (4) establish two machine learning models, including Classification and Regression Tress (CART) and eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost), (5) compare the efficiency of proposed CKAN with five other models developed through addressing the outcomes of error measures and schematic diagrams, (6) use Shapely Additive eXplanations (SHAP) and local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME) to recognize the important input features, and finally (7) apply a K-fold cross validation technique to evaluate the effectiveness of superior selected model to predict the Epan. The authors’ survey of existing literature apparently revealed that the development of CKAN is the first attempt in hydrological time series prediction, particularly for Epan. In other words, most of the published works are related to the applications of ML and DL models, whereas there is a research gap in previous works regarding the establishment of CKAN. This research gap motivated the authors to accomplish this study to check the potential of CKAN to predict Epan in relation to ML and DL approaches.

The proposed Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN) is introduced as a novel functional-approximation-based model, designed to capture complex nonlinear relationships using orthogonal polynomial representations grounded in the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem. For a better comparison, the DL models, including LSTM, GRU, and TFR, were selected because they are state-of-the-art architectures widely applied in prediction and hydro-climatological variables modeling. They serve as strong benchmarks for capturing temporal dependencies. The standalone ML models, namely CART and XGBoost, were also included as robust baseline methods. CART represents simple, interpretable models, while XGBoost is a powerful ensemble technique known for high predictive performance. Therefore, the selected models are belonging to three categories: (i) functional approximation method (CKAN), (ii) deep learning models (LSTM, GRU, TFR) and (iii) tree-based machine learning models (CART, XGBoost). This ensures a balanced comparison across model complexity, interpretability, and learning mechanisms, allowing for a more meaningful evaluation of the proposed CKAN model.

The novelty of this study lies primarily in the introduction and application of the Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN), which has not previously been used for Epan modeling. Unlike conventional deep learning and machine learning approaches, CKAN is grounded in the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem and enhanced through Chebyshev polynomial approximation, enabling more efficient and accurate modeling of complex nonlinear relationships. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study that applies CKAN to Epan prediction, thereby providing a novel methodological contribution and demonstrating its superiority over widely used models such as LSTM, GRU, TFR, CART, and XGBoost. This establishes both methodological novelty and practical significance in hydrological modeling.

Methods used

K-Best feature selection

Feature selection techniques help prevent overfitting, increase the performance of machine learning and deep learning models, and lessen the training time of the applied models. Also, it is a technique utilized to identify the most relevant predictors from a larger set of predictors in a dataset24. Various researches have shown that utilizing feature selection techniques improves the accuracy of machine learning and deep learning models.

There are several techniques for feature selection, among which KBest feature selection technique is one of the most frequently employed and has demonstrated significant performance in different research fields25. This method identifies the top K attributes based on specific statistical metrics. Typically, a basic method across univariate feature selection technique is often applied to predictive issues.

This technique selects the optimal feature (i.e., KBest) with the top score acquired from the F-test (i.e., one of univariate statistical methods), which calculates a linear relationship between two random variables26,27. That is, the process of KBest feature selection technique is determined by the top F value acquired by the F statistic, which is a fast linear model utilized to successively evaluate the influence of each variable among numerous regression variables. To compute the F-statistic, first calculate Pearson correlation coefficient between predictor and criterion variables. Then, calculate the F-statistic28. Finally, the highest K features with the top F-values are chosen. KBest feature selection supplies the advantages of fast computation and simplicity29. The diverse researches, including27,28 and so on, provided excellent case studies on prediction and application issues utilizing KBest feature selection technique based on machine learning and deep learning models.

Chebyshev polynomial-based Kolmogorov-Arnold network (CKAN)

Chebyshev polynomial-based Kolmogorov-Arnold network (CKAN)30 is a creative structure intended to increase the capability and accuracy of nonlinear function approximation. Motivated by Kolmogorov-Arnold theorem31,32 and the strong approximation efficiencies of Chebyshev polynomials, CKAN provides important benefits over conventional multilayer perceptrons (MLP). CKAN, which builds on the principles of KAN, consolidates Chebyshev polynomials for parameterizing trainable functions. Chebyshev polynomials, known for fast convergence and numerical stability, enable CKAN to boost the accuracy and capability of function approximation operations. Unlike conventional MLP, where individual node applies a nonlinear activation function that is fixed to a weighted sum of predictors, CKAN utilizes learnable activation functions on the edges, allowing the network to dynamically adjust its response to predictor data and more effectively model complex relationships30.

This parameterization provides high approximate accuracy with little parameters and allows for a concise representation of complex functions. Shukla et al.33 described that CKAN represented a significant advancement in neural network structure, surpassing conventional KAN implementations in terms of efficiency. It is particularly suited to approximating complex, high-dimensional functions, and demonstrates superior performance in diverse applications such as mathematics, physics, and data science and so on.

The core of CKAN is to represent the predictor data utilizing Chebyshev polynomials, which are sequences of orthogonal functions34 that help as a strong foundation for function approximation. Long short-term memory (LSTM)

Long short-term memory (LSTM) proposed by35 is in fact a typical case of recurrent neural network (RNN). It can be utilized to address diverse problems like vanishing and amplification gradients, that can occur in an RNN architecture. An LSTM consists of memory cells, including input, forget, and output gates. The principal procedure of LSTM can be done as a memory cell, enabling it to save information via applying special gate units. First, the amount of new data transferred to the memory cell is determined in the input gate. Next, previous cell state is evaluated in the forget gate, and selectively keeps the data needed for present processing. Finally, the amount of data passed from the cell to other parts of the network is controlled in the output gate. Additionally, the output gate includes the capability of selecting which data can be expanded to transmit the activation signal to the final part36. Interested readers can refer to Hochreiter and Schmidhuber35 for the detailed methodological steps of LSTM.

Gated recurrent units (GRU)

Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) is introduced by Cho et al.37. These networks are a special type of recurrent neural networks that have the ability to learn long-term dependencies. In terms of mechanism, GRU performs similarly to the LSTM. Unlike the LSTM which uses three gates, the GRU utilizes two gates, leading to increases the speed and therefore reduces the computational time of the model. Indeed, the forget gate and input gate in an LSTM is integrated to create an update gate in the GRU. For this, the GRU is simpler compared with LSTM and includes understandable structure. The GRU is comprised of a reset gate and an update gate. The update gate decides how much of the neural unit (i.e., data information) should be updated, and the reset gate decides how much of the previous data information should be forgotten. The update gate controls how much past information is important. Units with short-term dependencies is activated by the reset gate, whereas units with long-term dependencies will be activated by the update gate. For a comprehensive theory of GRU, the interested readers can refer to Cho et al.37.

Transformer (TFR)

Transformer (TFR), which is implemented solely utilizing attention, follows the encoder-decoder structure of traditional seq2seq38. Despite designing an encoder-decoder structure without utilization of RNN, it demonstrated superior performance for translation field compared to RNN39.

The traditional seq2seq consisted of an encoder-decoder structure. Here, the encoder compressed the predictor sequence into a single vector representation, and the decoder generated the criterion sequence from addressed vector representation38,40. However, this structure had a drawback in that some information of predictor sequence was lost during the process of encoder compressing the predictor sequence into a single vector, and attention was utilized to improve for this disadvantage38.

A main hyperparameter of TFR is the size of given predictor and criterion variables in the encoder and decoder of TFR41. Also, when employing attention, rather than doing it all at once, it chooses to divide it into several parts, performs attention in parallel, and then combines the results back into one. In TFR, there is a feedforward neural networks (FFNN), and it refers to the size of the hidden layer for FFNN. TFR does not employ RNN, but maintains an encoder-decoder structure that receives a predictor sequence from the encoder and criterion sequence from the decoder, like the conventional seq2seq38,42.

Classification and regression tress (CART)

Classification and Regression Tree (CART) is a type of decision tree utilized to solve classification or regression predictive modeling problems43,44. It organizes data into a binary tree, and predicts the criterion data based on the predictor data. Also, it is a nonparametric method based on recursive partitioning of data, and the results of CART analysis are presented in the form of a decision tree, which is intuitive and easy to interpret in a flowchart format. In addition, it constructs the tree by identifying predictor data, and split points that best distinguish the data, and utilizes impurity metrics such as the Gini coefficient and mean squared error during this process45.

CART begins with predictor selection, determines the best predictors to effectively split the data, and performs binary partitioning based on these predictors. This process is repeated at each node in the tree until a stopping criterion, such as maximum depth or a minimum number of samples per node, is reached43. After tree generation, a pruning process can be performed to prevent overfitting. This makes CART useful for both classification and regression problems.

eXtreme gradient boosting (XGBoost)

Chen and Guestrin46 developed eXtreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) ensemble approach that incorporates the hypothesis of gradient boosting classifiers as an accurate solution and significant effects on gradient improvement through classifier weights for regression and classification topics. XGBoost combines parallel computing and regularization techniques to improve predictive accuracy and efficiency. By integrating multiple individual models to create an ensemble model, XGBoost outperforms randomly selected models, enabling users and developers to build robust models for predictive topics47,48.

In the fields of prediction, forecasting, and modeling, ensemble models combine individual predictors to estimate the same criterion data. By integrating these predictor data, the criterion data is improved, resulting in higher accuracy and superior results46. Since individual predictor classifier has limited predictive accuracy performance, XGBoost implements a classifier boosting ensemble based on classification and regression trees (CART) to improve predictive accuracy. In this approach, the criterion values for the final predictive procedure can be calculated by summing the criterion values estimated from individual CART49. Therefore, XGBoost can provide a powerful and efficient tool to handle a wide range of regression and classification problems46.

Shapely additive explanations (SHAP)

SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) technique is a method that utilizes ideas based on sensitivity analysis to provide an integrated structure for interpreting predictions and expected results in theoretical categories. Therefore, various predictive problems based on machine learning and deep learning models can be visually interpreted employing SHAP technique, a mechanism that supplies significance measures for each applied variable50,51.

In addition, Shapley value is applied, borrowed from game theory, to machine learning and deep learning model frameworks by reframing predictive problem in the context of a cooperative game. To calculate the preceding values (i.e., Shapley), SHAP technique must systematically remove predictor data during the prediction process, thus evaluating machine learning and deep learning models for all possible combinations of predictor data52,53.

A higher value of relative importance for a special predictor data means that the predictor data can significantly contribute to the calculation of criterion data. The contribution of each predictor data can be determined by calculating the difference in predictions when that predictor data is at current value and when it is missing. The amount of final SHAP supplies a weighted average of predicted differences based on possible combinations of predictor data54,55.

SHAP technique employs two interpretative modules: global and local interpretation. Global interpretation evaluates the importance of predictor data, and generates summary plots utilizing visual assistances56. Also, the local interpretation supplies force plots for demonstrating corresponding SHAP technique of machine learning and deep learning models. In addition, it provides the assignment information of predictor data to help machine learning and deep learning models more easily understand predictor data based on sample classification57.

Local interpretable model-agnostic explanations (LIME)

Besides the SHAP, Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations (LIME) technique could also be utilized for evaluating the explainability of developed models. LIME produces explanations by locally approximating a complex model by a simple and interpretable model for a specific instance. LIME is a less computationally intensive alternative to SHAP technique, as it operates locally. It trains a locally interpretable model that approximates individual predictions through a series of steps58. This process includes changing the predictor and then observing the criterion of a typical black box model to understand how the predictions change with different observations. A predictor is considered important if a local disturbance causes a change in the overall model. The degree of change determines the importance of predictor. The process of LIME is as follows: select a sample to explain, generate synthetic samples close to the original sample, predict the original model for synthetic samples, train a linear model on local samples, and finally use the linear model coefficients for explainability. Despite the advantages of LIME, such as relatively high speed and simplicity in implementation, the LIME method has some limitations, including its ability to provide only local explanations and the instability of results in different implementations.

K-fold cross validation

K-fold cross validation is a technique for evaluating and verifying the performance of machine learning and deep learning models. It divides the entire data into ‘K’ subsets of equal size, utilizes one of subsets as validation data, and employs the remaining ‘K-1’ subsets as training data, repeating the process K times. In each iteration, a different subset is selected for validation, and the model is validated once for every subset59.

The key to this technique is effectively leveraging the entire dataset to evaluate the performance of machine learning and deep learning models. Utilizing K-fold cross validation, the machine learning and deep learning models can be trained and validated on diverse combinations of data, allowing for a more accurate assessment of model’s generalization ability60. Typically, the value of K is 5 or 10, a choice that balances the accuracy of performance evaluation and computational cost61,62.

While K-fold cross validation allows for a more stable and reliable technique of calculating model performance, it comes with the drawback of being computationally expensive. Because the machine learning and deep learning models must be trained K times, this can be quite costly when the dataset is large or model training takes a long time60.

Case study and data description

In this study, two stations located in Australia, including Perth Airport (station number: 009021) and Sydney Airport Amo (station number: 066037) were selected as a case study to test the proposed models in predicting Epan. Here, we refer to Perth and Sydney for short. The Perth and Sydney are located in Western Australia and New South Wales, respectively. The data of this study were extracted from https://www.bom.gov.au/ website collected from Australian Government, Bureau of Meteorology. In the present paper, the use of Chebyshev Polynomial-based Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (CKAN) is motivated by their strong theoretical foundation and their suitability for modeling complex nonlinear environmental processes such as pan evaporation. CKAN relies on the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem, which guarantees that any multivariate continuous function can be represented as a finite composition of univariate functions. By incorporating Chebyshev polynomials, the model benefits from: (i) Fast convergence, (ii) Minimal approximation error and (iii) Numerical stability. This makes CKAN particularly effective for capturing the highly nonlinear relationships between climatic variables and pan evaporation. Unlike deep learning models such as Long Short-Term Memory, and Gated Recurrent Unit, which typically require large datasets: (i) CKAN is data-efficient, (ii) less prone to overfitting, (iii) more robust to noise (common in meteorological datasets), and (iii) having a reduced model complexity with high interpretability. Compared to other ML models, CKAN uses explicit polynomial basis functions, making it more interpretable and it avoids deep stacked architectures and excessive hyperparameters, in contrary, CART may suffer from instability and XGBoost can overfit and lacks physical interpretability. Furthermore, the orthogonality of Chebyshev polynomials ensures: (i) reduced redundancy in feature representation and better generalization on unseen data. Pan evaporation is governed by: nonlinear energy balance and complex interactions among climatic variables, and the CKAN can help in capturing smooth functional dependencies and preserves physical consistency better than purely data-driven deep models.

To evaluate the relative contributions of input features, two explainable techniques, including SHAP and LIME were taken into consideration. To address this, the superior model of CKAN1 was selected. Although SHAP has established itself as the standard in the field of model analysis, the clear engineering-based reason for adopting LIME as an additional tool in research field is that while SHAP excels in theoretical completeness, LIME offers unrivalled value in flexibility and intuitiveness64. SHAP assigns contributions by considering all possible combinations of features. While this guarantees mathematical proofs of fairness and consistency, it has very high computational complexity. On the other hand, LIME is a method that fits a linear model (e.g., surrogate model) around particular data65. In large-scale service environments requiring immediate explanations for thousands of prediction results, the fast response speed of LIME is far more practical than global accuracy of SHAP in terms of system operations66,67.

SHAP and LIME operate and manage under different concepts (i.e., game theory vs. local approximation). If both methods identify the same characteristics as important, the analyst can have strong confidence in the results. Conversely, if the results differ, it serves as an important diagnostic indicator to suspect local instability in the model or high-dimensional complexity in the data64. SHAP is essential for identifying the importance of features within an overall model and establishing a theoretical basis, while LIME is a tool that helps field users quickly and easily understand individual prediction results. Therefore, securing both global insight depending on SHAP and local agility based on LIME can be considered an essential step in building advanced artificial intelligence systems64,65.

To assess the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed CKAN model, a K-fold cross validation was established on the train data for the best-performing CKAN model (i.e., CKAN1). K-fold cross validation is in fact a resampling method for assessing the performance of predictive AI models, aiming to provide an accurate and stable estimate of the model’s ability to predict new unseen data. A 10-fold cross validation was utilized in this study. The results of cross validation for 10 various folds are tabulated in Tables 7 and 8 for Perth and Sydney stations, respectively. It is worthy to mention that the values of RMSE, MAE, R, and NSE were presented as four commonly used error measures in literature. A performance comparison of CKAN models developed under 10 folds clearly revealed that there are no significant differences in the performance of the developed models, specifically at Sydney Station, indicating reliable capability of proposed CKAN models for predicting new Epan data, confirming its usefulness and generalizability. The main benefit of K-fold cross validation is that each data sample is used in both the training and validation stages, making the model performance estimate more accurate and less dependent on a specific partition of the data. This method also reduces the risk of overfitting, makes model evaluation more robust, and provides better insight into the model’s generalizability to real data. By averaging the results from all folds, a more realistic estimate of the overall model performance could be obtained. This reduces bias and controls variance, resulting in a more stable and accurate assessment. The mean values of four error metrics achieved from 10 folds are as follows: RMSE = 1.644 mm, MAE = 1.191 mm, R = 0.889, NSE = 0.788 at Perth, and RMSE = 2.089 mm, MAE = 1.549 mm, R = 0.705, NSE = 0.491 at Sydney.

Conclusion

In this study, the daily time series of Epan at two stations in Australia, i.e., Perth and Sydney were predicted. To that end, a CKAN model was first proposed. Then, its performance was evaluated with five intelligent models like three DL-based LSTM, GRU, and TFR, and two ML-based CART and XGBoost. The K-Best feature selection was utilized to specify the important features and then define the input scenarios. The outcomes illustrated that all the models achieved the highest performance under the full-input configuration. The CKAN model was found to perform the best for prediction of daily Epan at both the sites. Furthermore, XGBoost slightly presented better results than CART, as well as GRU and TFR achieved better Epan predictions compared with LSTM. Therefore, the CART and LSTM were determined as the poor ML and DL models. This study was also applied SHAP and LIME interpretable methods for discerning the important input features affecting the results of superior model (i.e., CKAN1). It was found that SR and Tmin were respectively the important input features at Perth and Sydney using the SHAP. On the other side, the findings of LIME in the selected samples demonstrated that Tmean and RH provided the most contributions at Perth and Sydney, respectively. At the end, a K-fold cross validation with K = 10 was applied to assess the usefulness of best-performing model (i.e., CKAN1), confirming its effectiveness and generalizability to predict Epan time series.

The limitations of this study can be listed as follows: (1) Case study: The current study aimed to test the performance of proposed intelligent frameworks in two case study at Australia (Perth and Sydney). More stations covering the different climatic zones are recommended to be selected in future. (2) Feature selection technique: In this study, only one feature selection method, namely K-Best was used in determining the important inputs and then input configurations. Therefore, it is recommended that some other kinds of feature selection methods could be utilized in future. (3) CKAN’s limitation: As mentioned, the Chebyshev Polynomial-Based Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (CKAN) was only developed in this research. In following this study, the baseline KAN and its other variants such as convolutional KAN are also suggested to be established in future works, and the achieved results can be compared with the outcomes of present research. (4) Models’ limitation: This study addressed the development of CKAN, three DL, and two ML models to predict Epan. Future studies could be focused on the application of various versions of KAN, DL, and ML schemes.

As documented, this study applied CKAN model for the first time in Epan prediction. So, it is highly suggested its more application in hydrological studies like predicting rainfall, reference evapotranspiration, river discharge, etc. Some versions of hybrid ML and DL methods are also recommended to be implemented in future works for Epan prediction. As the outcomes of K-fold cross validation confirmed, this strategy is recommended to be applied more in hydro-climatological time series prediction to evaluate the generalizability of the developed models.

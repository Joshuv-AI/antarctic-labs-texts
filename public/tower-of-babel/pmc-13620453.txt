LAD-NASH score using multiparametric ultrasound for identification of at-risk MASH: head-to-head comparison with the FAST score
Authors: Seung-seob Kim, Jae Seung Lee, Min Young Lee, Sunyoung Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeNoninvasive scores have been proposed to identify patients with at-risk metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH) who may benefit from pharmacologic therapy. The LAD-NASH (model combining attenuation coefficient, dispersion slope, and liver stiffness for identification of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis) score, a multiparametric ultrasound (US)-based model that combines the attenuation coefficient, dispersion slope, and liver stiffness to identify nonalcoholic steatohepatitis, was recently developed for this purpose. This study aimed to validate the LAD-NASH score for identifying at-risk MASH in patients with biopsy-proven metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and to compare its diagnostic performance with that of the FibroScan–aspartate aminotransferase (FAST) score.MethodsThis study enrolled consecutive patients who underwent multiparametric US and liver biopsy on the same day between February 2023 and April 2024. At-risk MASH was defined as biopsy-confirmed MASH with a MASLD activity score of ≥4 and fibrosis stage of ≥F2. The diagnostic performance of the LAD-NASH and FAST scores was evaluated using the areas under the receiver-operating characteristic curves (AUROCs).ResultsA total of 70 patients with biopsy-proven MASLD were included (median age, 44 years; 34 men; median body mass index, 28.7 kg/m²; at-risk MASH, 27.1%). The LAD-NASH and FAST scores demonstrated comparable diagnostic performance for identifying at-risk MASH (AUROCs, 0.740; 95% confidence intervals [CI], 0.621 to 0.838 vs. 0.739; 95% CI, 0.621 to 0.837; P=0.995). Using the rule-out cutoff of ≤0.264, the LAD-NASH score demonstrated 100% sensitivity and 100% negative predictive value for at-risk MASH.ConclusionThe LAD-NASH and FAST scores showed comparable diagnostic performance for identifying at-risk MASH. Because it is based solely on multiparametric US, the LAD-NASH score may be a useful noninvasive rule-out tool for at-risk MASH.Keywords: Sonoelastography, Nonalcoholic fatty liver disease, Nonalcoholic steatohepatitis

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD), formerly known as nonalcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) [1–3], is now recognized as the leading cause of chronic liver disease worldwide [4]. Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH), previously termed nonalcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH), is histologically characterized by hepatocellular ballooning and lobular inflammation and can progress to liver fibrosis, cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma [5,6]. A MASLD activity score (MAS; formerly, the NAFLD activity score) of ≥4 and fibrosis stage of ≥F2 are commonly used to define “at-risk MASH,” which is associated with a higher risk of progression to cirrhosis and represents the current target population for clinical trials evaluating pharmacologic therapies for MASH [7–9].

Liver biopsy remains the reference standard for identifying patients with at-risk MASH [10]. However, because liver biopsy is invasive, it has several limitations, including complication risk, high cost, sampling error due to small tissue samples, and potential intra- and interreader variability resulting from subjective interpretation [10,11]. Consequently, noninvasive tests based on imaging, serum markers, or both have been developed to identify patients with at-risk MASH [4,5]. The FibroScan–aspartate aminotransferase (FAST) score combines liver stiffness measurement (LSM) and the controlled attenuation parameter (CAP), obtained using vibration-controlled transient elastography (VCTE), with serum aspartate aminotransferase (AST). This score has been validated in international multicenter studies and is widely used in clinical trials [4,5].

Recent studies using multiparametric ultrasound (US), including real-time B-mode–based attenuation imaging (ATI) and two-dimensional shear wave elastography (2D-SWE), have shown that the attenuation coefficient (AC), dispersion slope (DS), and liver stiffness (LS) are useful US markers for estimating hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis, respectively, in patients with NAFLD [12,13]. The LAD-NASH (model combining attenuation coefficient, dispersion slope, and liver stiffness for identification of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis) score, which combines these US parameters to identify at-risk NASH, was developed on the basis of this approach [7]. However, the LAD-NASH score has not been externally validated beyond its original publication, and its performance has not been directly compared with that of the widely used FAST score. Therefore, this study aimed to validate the LAD-NASH score for identifying at-risk MASH in patients with biopsy-proven MASLD and to compare its diagnostic performance with that of the FAST score.

Materials and Methods

Compliance with Ethical Standards

This study followed the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the institutional review board of Severance Hospital (No. 4-2024-0790). The requirement for written informed consent was waived because of the retrospective study design.

Study Population

Between February 2023 and April 2024, consecutive patients who underwent multiparametric US evaluation and liver biopsy on the same day were retrospectively identified. These examinations were performed for detailed liver assessment because of elevated liver enzyme levels or clinical suspicion of diffuse liver disease, including MASLD. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) age ≥18 years, (2) available FibroScan and laboratory test results, including serum AST, and (3) no significant alcohol consumption, drug-induced liver injury, or monogenic disease. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) failed ATI or 2D-SWE measurement, (2) unavailable FibroScan data within 3 months before or after liver biopsy, and (3) liver histopathology other than MASLD.

Clinical and Laboratory Data

Demographic data (age and sex), anthropometric measurements (body weight and height), laboratory data (AST, alanine aminotransferase [ALT], and platelet count), and diabetes mellitus status were collected. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as body weight in kilograms divided by height in meters squared. The Fibrosis-4 (FIB-4) index was calculated as follows: FIB-4=(age [years]×AST [IU/L])/(platelet count [10³/μL]×√ALT [IU/L]).

2D-SWE and ATI

All US examinations were performed by one of two board-certified radiologists (S.-S.K., with 8 years of experience in liver US, and S.L., with 10 years of experience) using a US scanner (Aplio i800, Canon Medical Systems, Otawara, Tochigi, Japan) equipped with a convex probe (PVI-475BX, Canon Medical Systems, transmitted US frequency range, 1 to 8 MHz). All patients fasted for at least 4 hours before the examination. US was performed with the patient in the supine position and the right arm fully abducted to obtain an optimal intercostal acoustic window.

After B-mode evaluation of the liver parenchyma, 2D-SWE was performed in the right hepatic lobe through the intercostal space [14]. The transducer was positioned perpendicular to the body surface and adjusted to display the liver surface horizontally on the screen. Patients were instructed to hold their breath during quiet respiration rather than after maximal inspiration or expiration. An approximately 2.0×2.0-cm sample box was placed on the grayscale image, avoiding reverberation artifacts near the liver capsule. After generation of the shear-wave propagation map and acquisition of data from the sample box, a 1-cm circular region of interest (ROI) was manually placed over smooth, parallel propagation lines within the sample box, avoiding areas near large vessels, to obtain LS (kPa) and DS (m/s/kHz) values simultaneously (Fig. 1A). When the area within the sample box showing appropriate shear-wave propagation was limited, the ROI diameter was reduced to less than 1 cm to ensure reliable measurements. At least 10 measurements were obtained for each patient. Additional acquisitions were performed when the interquartile range-to-median ratio (IQR/median) exceeded 0.3. The median LS and DS values were used for analysis. Examinations were considered failures and were excluded from the analysis if regions with smooth, parallel shear-wave propagation could not be reliably obtained or if the IQR/median remained greater than 0.3.

After 2D-SWE, ATI was performed in the right hepatic lobe through the intercostal space using an acquisition approach similar to that used for 2D-SWE [15]. Within the sample box (approximately 4.0×7.0 cm), a trapezoidal ROI with side lengths of approximately 3–4 cm was placed while avoiding reverberation artifacts near the liver capsule and regions with weak signals at excessive depth (Fig. 1B). Only measurements with a reliability index (R²) of ≥0.9 were considered valid. Similarly, the AC (dB/cm/MHz) was measured at least five times; when the IQR/median exceeded 0.3, additional measurements were obtained to replace earlier outlier-like values. The median AC value was selected for analysis. Examinations were considered failures and were excluded from the analysis if R² persistently remained below 0.9 or if the IQR/median remained greater than 0.3.

The LAD-NASH score was calculated according to the previously published formula using the measured LS, DS, and AC values [7]. On the basis of the proposed cutoffs, LAD-NASH scores of ≤0.264 and ≥0.713 were used to rule out and rule in at-risk MASH, respectively [7].

Vibration-Controlled Transient Elastography

VCTE was performed by experienced technicians who were blinded to the clinical and histopathologic data, using a FibroScan device (Echosens, Paris, France). The M probe was used by default, and the XL probe was used in patients with obesity. VCTE was performed in the right hepatic lobe through the intercostal space, with the patient lying supine and the right arm fully abducted. LSM (kPa) and CAP (dB/m) values were obtained until 10 valid measurements had been recorded for each patient, and the median values were used for analysis (Fig. 1C).

The FAST score was calculated according to the previously reported formula using the measured LSM, CAP, and serum AST values [16]. On the basis of the proposed cutoffs, FAST scores of ≤0.35 and ≥0.67 were used to rule out and rule in at-risk MASH, respectively [16].

Liver Biopsy and Histopathologic Evaluation

Percutaneous biopsy was performed in the right hepatic lobe under real-time US guidance on the same day as the 2D-SWE and ATI measurements. The grades of hepatic steatosis (0–3), lobular inflammation (0–3), hepatocellular ballooning (0–2), and fibrosis (0–4) extracted from the pathology reports were used as the reference standard [17]. The MAS was calculated as the sum of the grades for hepatic steatosis, lobular inflammation, and hepatocellular ballooning, yielding a total score ranging from 0 to 8. On the basis of these histopathologic assessments, at-risk MASH was defined as a MAS of ≥4 and fibrosis stage of ≥F2 [5].

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians with IQRs and were compared using the Mann-Whitney U test for two-group comparisons and the Kruskal-Wallis test for three-group comparisons. Post hoc pairwise comparisons among three groups were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test with Bonferroni correction (adjusted α=0.017). Categorical variables were expressed as numbers with percentages and were compared using the chi-square test. The diagnostic performance of the LAD-NASH and FAST scores was evaluated using the areas under the receiver-operating characteristic curves (AUROCs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and AUROCs were compared using the DeLong test [18]. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated using the previously reported cutoffs for the LAD-NASH and FAST scores [7,16]. Calibration was assessed using calibration plots, together with calibration intercepts and slopes derived from logistic regression-based predicted probabilities. A P-value of <0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 23.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA), MedCalc version 16.2.1 (MedCalc Software, Ostend, Belgium), and R version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Patient Characteristics

During the study period, 171 consecutive patients underwent multiparametric US and liver biopsy on the same day. Five patients were excluded because of ATI or 2D-SWE measurement failure (examination failure rates: 2.3% [4/171] for 2D-SWE and 0.6% [1/171] for ATI). The reasons for failure were inability to reliably obtain regions with smooth, parallel shear-wave propagation in three patients, persistently high IQR/median values (0.61) despite up to 13 measurement attempts in one patient, and low reliability index values (R²<0.9) across all five ATI acquisitions in one patient. The baseline characteristics and liver biopsy results of these five patients are summarized in Supplementary Table 1. Seven patients without available FibroScan data were also excluded. The median interval between liver biopsy and FibroScan was 41.5 days (IQR, 14.0 to 59.8 days). Based on histopathologic examination, 89 patients with liver histopathology other than MASLD were excluded (Supplementary Table 2). Finally, 70 patients were included (median age, 44 years; IQR, 33 to 56 years; 34 men) (Fig. 2). Patient characteristics, laboratory findings, histopathologic results, and multiparametric US and VCTE parameters are summarized in Table 1. The median BMI was 28.7 kg/m² (IQR, 27.0 to 32.0). Nineteen of the 70 patients (27.1%) were classified as having at-risk MASH. The remaining 51 patients comprised 41 patients (58.6%) with MASH without at-risk features and 10 patients (14.3%) with MASLD without steatohepatitis.

Distribution of LAD-NASH and FAST Scores among the Patient Groups

The distributions of the LAD-NASH and FAST scores are shown as box plots in Supplementary Fig. 1. Both scores differed significantly across the three groups (P<0.001 for both). In post hoc pairwise comparisons, LAD-NASH scores differed significantly between patients with MASH without at-risk features and those with at-risk MASH (P=0.031), whereas FAST scores differed significantly between patients with MASLD without steatohepatitis and those with MASH without at-risk features (P<0.001).

Comparison between LAD-NASH and FAST Scores

The AUROCs for identifying at-risk MASH were 0.740 (95% CI, 0.621 to 0.838) for the LAD-NASH score and 0.739 (95% CI, 0.621 to 0.837) for the FAST score (Fig. 3). The AUROCs did not differ significantly between the LAD-NASH and FAST scores (P=0.995). Although these values were numerically higher than the AUROC of the FIB-4 index (0.674; 95% CI, 0.538 to 0.810), the differences were not statistically significant (P=0.376 and P=0.410 for the LAD-NASH and FAST scores, respectively).

Discussion

The LAD-NASH score is a recently developed multiparametric US-based model that combines three quantitative US parameters—AC, DS, and LS—for noninvasive identification of patients with at-risk MASH. However, external validation beyond the original publication has been limited, and direct head-to-head comparisons between LAD-NASH and other noninvasive indices, such as the FAST score, have not been reported. In this study of patients with biopsy-proven MASLD, the LAD-NASH score showed acceptable diagnostic performance for identifying at-risk MASH, and its performance was comparable to that of the FAST score.

Previous studies have shown that multiparametric US enables simultaneous, noninvasive assessment of hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis [12,13,19], which are the histopathologic components used to evaluate NASH. In these NASH-focused studies, AC obtained from ATI showed good diagnostic performance for detecting varying degrees of hepatic steatosis [12,13,19]. DS derived from 2D-SWE, which can serve as an indirect measure of tissue viscosity, was significantly associated with the grade of lobular inflammation, and LS obtained from 2D-SWE showed good diagnostic performance for staging liver fibrosis [12,13,19]. Recently, Sugimoto et al. [7] proposed a logistic regression model combining these three US markers—AC, DS, and LS—to identify patients with at-risk NASH; this model was termed the LAD-NASH score. This combination is biologically and clinically reasonable because AC, DS, and LS reflect hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis, respectively, which are the main histopathologic components of NASH [7]. In the present validation study of patients with biopsy-proven MASLD, the LAD-NASH score showed acceptable diagnostic accuracy for identifying at-risk MASH, with an AUROC of 0.740. When the previously reported rule-out cutoff was applied, the LAD-NASH score achieved 100% sensitivity and a 100% NPV for excluding at-risk MASH. Therefore, the LAD-NASH score may be useful as a noninvasive rule-out tool for identifying patients with at-risk MASH who may be candidates for clinical trials or pharmacologic therapy.

VCTE has been widely used as a noninvasive, reliable tool for assessing liver fibrosis by measuring LSM [10]. CAP, which is obtained concurrently with LSM during VCTE, has also been introduced into clinical practice to quantify hepatic steatosis [10]. However, the specificity of CAP is known to be reduced in patients with metabolic risk profiles, and CAP has generally shown comparable or inferior diagnostic performance relative to ATI [20]. Newsome et al. [16] developed the FAST score, which combines LSM, CAP, and serum AST to identify patients with at-risk NASH. LSM, CAP, and serum AST reflect liver fibrosis, steatosis, and inflammation, respectively [16]. In a recent individual participant data meta-analysis that included 25 studies, the FAST score achieved an AUROC of 0.78 for identifying at-risk MASH [21], which is comparable to the present findings. In the present head-to-head comparative analysis, the FAST score showed an AUROC similar to that of the LAD-NASH score for identifying at-risk MASH (0.739 vs. 0.740).

To further evaluate the clinical applicability of LAD-NASH and FAST as risk-stratification tools, calibration was assessed. Both scores showed good agreement between predicted and observed risks, supporting their potential clinical utility for identifying patients who may safely avoid liver biopsy or require further evaluation.

An advantage of multiparametric US using ATI and 2D-SWE is that both techniques can be integrated into routine grayscale B-mode examinations [13]. During conventional B-mode imaging, hepatic parenchymal heterogeneity and potential focal liver lesions can be evaluated. Unlike VCTE, ATI and 2D-SWE can then be performed while directly visualizing the anatomical structures of the liver [10]. By reviewing the grayscale B-mode images during measurement, operators can avoid areas with artifacts, large vessels, or focal liver lesions [13]. Integrating B-mode findings with subsequently obtained quantitative parameters may allow comprehensive estimation of hepatic steatosis, inflammation, and fibrosis, thereby facilitating noninvasive diagnosis of MASH or at-risk MASH within a single examination [7,12,13,19]. Another advantage of the LAD-NASH score is that it is derived solely from multiparametric US parameters and therefore does not require laboratory tests, such as serum AST, unlike the FAST score [7].

For patients classified into the indeterminate zone, defined as scores above the rule-out threshold but below the rule-in threshold, additional tests should be considered, including magnetic resonance (MR) elastography-based scoring systems such as the magnetic resonance imaging–aspartate aminotransferase score or demographic- and serologic-based scoring systems such as FIB-4 and the NAFLD fibrosis score [22]. However, if diagnostic uncertainty persists, liver biopsy remains necessary. Therefore, from a practical clinical perspective, a smaller indeterminate-zone proportion would indicate greater clinical utility. In this study, 34% of patients were classified into the indeterminate zone by the FAST score, a proportion comparable to those reported in previous studies [23–25]. In contrast, approximately 26% of patients were classified into the indeterminate zone in the original study proposing the LAD-NASH score [7], whereas nearly 47% of patients fell into this category in the present study. Further studies are warranted to better define the indeterminate zone of the LAD-NASH score and clarify its implications for clinical use.

2D-SWE measurement failure has been reported to be influenced by patient-related factors such as obesity, advanced age, and poor breath-holding [26]. In this study, the examination failure rate for 2D-SWE was 2.3% (4/171), which falls within the range reported in previous studies [26,27]. Among these four patients, severe obesity was considered the likely cause of measurement failure in two patients, whereas the causes of failure in the remaining two patients could not be clearly determined because of the retrospective design. The measurement failure rate for ATI was 0.6% (1/171), which also falls within the range reported in previous studies [28]. In this patient, the underlying cause of measurement failure, namely persistently low R² values, could not be clearly identified. Because two of the five patients with examination failure had MASLD and at-risk MASH was confirmed in one of these patients, additional tests, such as MR elastography or liver biopsy, should be considered in such cases.

This study has several limitations. First, the study was conducted at a single tertiary referral center with a relatively small sample size, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Large, population-based, international multicenter studies are warranted to further validate the LAD-NASH score and assess its broader applicability in clinical practice. Second, the study population was recruited from an Asian country and had a relatively lower BMI than Western populations. Further validation in Western cohorts with higher BMI is needed, given the higher prevalence of MASH in these populations. Third, the study population consisted of patients for whom clinicians considered liver biopsy clinically indicated and therefore may have been affected by spectrum bias. Consequently, the diagnostic performance of the LAD-NASH and FAST scores may not be directly generalizable to primary care settings. Fourth, the FAST score was calculated using FibroScan examinations performed within 3 months before or after liver biopsy rather than on the same day. Therefore, in some patients, particularly those with fluctuating hepatic inflammation or changes in body weight, FibroScan measurements may not have accurately reflected liver status at the time of biopsy. Fifth, according to the 2024 World Federation for Ultrasound in Medicine and Biology (WFUMB) guidelines, a minimum circular ROI diameter of 1 cm is recommended for 2D-SWE [29]. However, in this study, the ROI diameter was reduced to less than 1 cm when deemed necessary. The effect of reducing the ROI diameter on measurement values has not been sufficiently investigated, and the WFUMB recommendation regarding ROI size is considered to be based primarily on expert consensus. In addition, recommendations regarding ROI size vary among US equipment vendors [14], and some investigators have explicitly suggested reducing the ROI size when necessary to obtain reliable measurements [30]. Further studies are warranted to clarify the effects of ROI size on measurement reliability and accuracy. Sixth, because this study retrospectively analyzed previously finalized histopathology reports, it was not possible to verify whether the pathologists were blinded to patients’ clinical information at the time of interpretation. In addition, interobserver agreement among pathologists could not be assessed. Seventh, although both the LAD-NASH and FAST scoring systems were originally developed for patients with clinically suspected MASLD, this retrospective study enrolled patients with biopsy-confirmed MASLD rather than clinically suspected MASLD. Finally, intraobserver and interobserver variability were not assessed for the multiparametric US parameters.

Despite these limitations, this study has several strengths. First, it provides a head-to-head comparison between a multiparametric US-based score (LAD-NASH) and a FibroScan-based score (FAST) for identifying at-risk MASH, using liver biopsy as the reference standard. By restricting the interval between multiparametric US and liver biopsy to the same day and the interval between FibroScan and liver biopsy to less than 3 months, the relative performance of the two imaging-based scores could be compared in a clinically relevant setting. Second, the study addresses a clinically relevant question in the context of ongoing efforts to streamline noninvasive pathways for identifying patients who may be eligible for therapeutic trials.

In conclusion, the LAD-NASH and FAST scores showed comparable diagnostic performance for identifying at-risk MASH. The LAD-NASH score, which is based on multiparametric US, may be a useful noninvasive rule-out tool for at-risk MASH.

Key points

Noninvasive identification of at-risk metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH) is increasingly important because these patients may be eligible for clinical trials and pharmacologic therapy. The LAD-NASH (model combining attenuation coefficient, dispersion slope, and liver stiffness for identification of nonalcoholic steatohepatitis) score showed diagnostic performance comparable to that of the FibroScan–aspartate aminotransferase score for identifying at-risk MASH (areas under the receiver-operating characteristic curve, 0.740 vs. 0.739) and achieved 100% sensitivity when used to rule out at-risk MASH. Because it is based solely on multiparametric ultrasound, the LAD-NASH score may serve as a noninvasive tool for ruling out at-risk MASH.

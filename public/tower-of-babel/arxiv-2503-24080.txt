A Pohožaev minimization for normalized solutions: fractional sublinear equations of logarithmic type
Marco Gallo

Abstract
In this paper, we search for normalized solutions to a fractional, nonlinear, and possibly strongly sublinear Schrödinger equation ( − Δ ) s ​ u + μ ​ u = g ⁡ ( u ) in ℝ N , (-\Delta)^{s}u+\mu u=g(u)\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}, under the mass constraint ∫ ℝ N u 2 ​ 𝑑 x = m > 0 \int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m>0 ; here, N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 , s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) , and μ \mu is a Lagrange multiplier. We study the case of L 2 L^{2} -subcritical nonlinearities g g of Berestycki–Lions type, without assuming that g g is superlinear at the origin, which allows us to include examples like a logarithmic term g ⁡ ( u ) = u ​ log ⁡ ( u 2 ) g(u)=u\log(u^{2}) or sublinear powers g ⁡ ( u ) = u q − u r g(u)=u^{q}-u^{r} , 0 < r < 1 < q 0<r<1<q . Due to the generality of g g and the fact that the energy functional might be not well-defined, we implement an approximation process in combination with a Lagrangian approach and a new Pohožaev minimization in the product space, finding a solution for large values of m m . In the sublinear case, we are able to find a solution for each m m . Several insights on the concepts of minimality are studied as well. We highlight that some of the results are new even in the local setting s = 1 s=1 or for g g superlinear.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the fractional
L^{2}
-subcritical equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+\mu u=g(u)\quad\hbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

(1.1)

under the mass constraint
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m>0
; here,
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier (part of the unknowns), and
g
is a generic Berestycki–Lions
[
8
]
nonlinearity which is allowed to be (negatively)
sublinear
at the origin (see
(g0)
–
(g4)
below). In particular,
G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
is allowed to have a growth of the type

-|t|^{1+\theta}-|t|^{2^{*}_{s}}\lesssim G(t)\lesssim|t|^{2}+|t|^{\bar{p}+1}\quad\hbox{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$},

where
\theta\in[0,1)
,
2^{*}_{s}:=\frac{2N}{N-2s}
is the fractional Sobolev critical exponent, and

\bar{p}:=1+\frac{4s}{N};

(1.2)

the number
\bar{p}+1
is often referred to as the
L^{2}
-critical exponent
.
This case includes, for example, the well-known
logarithmic
nonlinearity
g(t)=t\log(t^{2})

(here
\theta\in[0,1)
can be arbitrarily chosen), or
sublinear-power
-type nonlinearities
g(t)=|t|^{q-1}t-|t|^{r-1}t,\quad 0<r<q<\bar{p}

(here
\theta=r
), but also strongly sublinear nonlinearities like
g(t)\sim\frac{{\rm sgn}(t)}{\log(|t|)}\quad\hbox{as $t\to 0$}

(here
\theta=0
).

Exploiting variational methods, a key fact of these nonlinearities is that the related energy functional
K\colon H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,

K(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(1.3)

is only lower semicontinuous, while it is well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which we highlight is not a reflexive space. Here
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the usual fractional Sobolev space.

1.1
Some literature

When
s=1
, the
frequency

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed, and
g(t)=t\log(t^{2})
, the
logarithmic Schrödinger equation

[
9
,
10
]

-\Delta u+\mu u=u\log(u^{2})\quad\hbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

(1.4)

has received much attention due to its great relevance in applied sciences, such as atomic physics, high-energy cosmic rays, Cherenkov-type shock waves,
quantum hydrodynamical models,
and many others; we refer to
[
38
,
17
]

and the references therein.
A key property of (
1.4
) is, indeed, the
tensorization property
(or
separability of noninteracting subsystems
): namely, if
u_{i}
are solutions of (
1.4
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
for
i=1,2
with frequency
\frac{\mu}{2}
, then
(x_{1},x_{2})\mapsto u_{1}(x_{1})u_{2}(x_{2})
is a solution of (
1.4
) in
\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{2}}
.

In order to handle the singularity at the origin, several techniques have been developed: a first approach, applied by
[
18
]
,
shows that the functional
K
in (
1.3
) is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
on the subspace

X:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\;\middle|\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\log(u^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x>-\infty\right\};

see also
[
63
,
57
]
, and
[
14
]
for general doubling nonlinearities.
We observe that
X\subsetneq H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as shown by

u(x):=(|x|^{N/2}\log(|x|))^{-1}
for
|x|
large.
Finally, in
[
47
]
, a suitable norm is introduced in order to force the positive solutions far from the singularity.

A second approach has been developed in
[
27
,
60
]
: here, the authors apply the
nonsmooth analysis
theory of
[
30
,
61
]

and work with
a suitable subdifferential of the action functional, possibly splitting it into a smooth part and a convex lower semicontinuous one.

Different approaches rely instead on some
approximation schemes
.
The idea that
\frac{t^{2+\delta}-t^{2}}{\delta}\to t^{2}\log(t)
as
\delta\to 0^{+}
was formalized in the framework of PDEs by
[
65
]
(see also
[
36
]
,
[
17
, Section 7]
), and then this idea was applied in
[
66
]
to get existence of solutions. See also
[
38
]
, where the regularization is of the form
\log(t+\delta)
, and
[
1
]
, where the authors approximate the domain of the problem with expanding balls.

We mention that the problem of the singularity of the logarithm arises also in the study of planar Choquard equations
[
25
,
45
]
.

Let us move, now, to the case of sublinear powers. Although nonlinear (fractional) Schrödinger equations with combined powers have recently gained the mathematical community’s attention
[

,

,

(see also

for a more general nonlinear term that behaves similarly), such work usually concerns superlinear exponents. Instead, as

and

most of the work focuses on problems of the type

, where suitable assumptions on
V,a,h
are considered in order to ensure the existence of solutions (notice, in fact, that when

and

, no solution exists, see e.g.
[

). When

, the analysis of the problem is usually set in the Banach space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;
see

[
64
,
6
,
5
]
.

The only paper we know that deals with
general nonlinearities
in the spirit of
[

]
is
[
50
]
: here, the author studies

-\Delta u=f(u)\quad\hbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

where the assumptions about
f
include the case

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{f(t)}{|t|^{2^{*}-2}t}=-\infty
. The author employs a perturbation argument at the origin
and obtains a ground state for the equation.

When
s=1
and
\mu
is free, very few results appear in the literature about the existence of normalized solutions
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m>0
. When
g(t)=t\log(t^{2})
, we see that, considered a nontrivial solution
u_{1}
of (
1.4
) for
\mu=1
, then defining
u:=\alpha_{m}u_{1}
with
\alpha_{m}^{2}:=m(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{1}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x)^{-1}
, we have that
u
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m
and

-\Delta u+\left(1+\log(\alpha_{m}^{2})\right)u=u\log(u^{2})\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$,}

(1.5)

thus the existence of a normalized solution is always ensured; this was already noticed in
[
10

. In particular, the frequency
1+\log(\alpha_{m}^{2})
is negative when

is sufficiently small and positive when
m
is sufficiently large.
Additionally, an explicit
L^{2}
-minimum can be found. Indeed, by the logarithmic Sobolev–Gross–Nelson inequality
[
37
]
there exists
C(N)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

K(u)\geq-\frac{1}{2}\left(C(N)+\log(m)\right)m

(1.6)

for every
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m
; moreover, we know that the equality is attained by (and only by, up to a translation) the
Gausson

u(x)=\gamma_{m}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|x|^{2}}
with
\gamma_{m}:=\sqrt{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-|x|^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{-1/2}
.
This procedure can be found, for example, in
[
9
,
18
]
. We see that the sign of the minimal energy over the
L^{2}
-sphere depends on the size of
m
(and this is connected with the sign of the Lagrange multiplier, see Proposition
1.8
).

When

does not enjoy scaling properties of this type, we mention the recent papers
[

In

When
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional logarithmic equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+\mu u=u\log(u^{2})\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

(1.7)

appears in the study of nonlinear quantized boson fields
[

]
. Mathematically, several papers succeeded in generalizing the results found in the local case for sublinear nonlinearities, exploiting and adapting to the nonlocal case the aforementioned techniques, see e.g.

[

In particular, we mention

Anyway, all the previous results deal with the unconstrained problem with fixed frequency.
When
g(t)=t\log(t^{2})
, the search for a normalized solution can be pursued as above by scaling (see (
1.5
)); on the other hand, the search for an

-minimum through an optimal logarithmic inequality (

) has issues.
Indeed, although such an inequality still holds in the fractional framework

Normalized solutions to fractional equations with logarithmic nonlinearities have only recently begun to be investigated; see, e.g.,
[
44

49
]
, where semiclassical problems are studied.

The goal of this paper is to find normalized solutions with minimality properties to fractional sublinear equations under general assumptions about the nonlinear term.
When treating such general terms

, as previously commented, the splitting of the functional does not seem an option, as well as the definition of a space similar to

.
To obtain the existence of an

-minimum we rely, thus, on the perturbation technique employed by

(see Section

1.2
Existence results

In the present paper, we study the following problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u+\mu u=g(u)\quad\hbox{in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m,\\
(\mu,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

(1.8)

with
s\in(0,1)
,
N\geq 2
,
m>0
, and
g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying (set
G(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau
)

(g0)

g
is continuous and
g(0)=0
,

(g1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{g(t)}{t}\leq 0
,

(g2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{|g(t)|}{|t|^{2_{s}^{*}-1}}<+\infty
,

(g3)

\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{g(t)}{|t|^{\bar{p}-1}t}\leq 0
,

(g4)

there exists
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
G(t_{0})>0
.

Here and in what follows, we denote
g^{\pm}:=\max\{\pm g,0\}
the standard positive and negative parts of
g
, and

\displaystyle g_{+}(t)t:=(g(t)t)^{+},\quad G_{+}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g_{+}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,

(1.9)

\displaystyle g_{-}(t)t:=(g(t)t)^{-},\quad G_{-}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g_{-}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau;

(1.10)

see Section
2
. With these notations, we may rewrite
(g1)
and
(g3)
as

(g1)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{g_{+}(t)}{t}=0
,

(g3)

\displaystyle\lim_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{g_{+}(t)}{|t|^{\bar{p}-1}t}=0
.

We further notice that, when
(g3)
holds, then
(g2)
actually means
\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{g_{-}(t)}{|t|^{2_{s}^{*}-2}t}<\infty
.

Before presenting our results, let us recall that in
[
20
, Proposition 4.1]
and
[
16
, Proposition 1.1]
(see also
[
23
, Corollary 6.4]
for a different approach), when
g
satisfies Berestycki–Lions assumptions, the following
Pohožaev identity
holds

\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(1.11)

whenever
u
is a solution of (
1.8
) and either
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
or
g\in\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\alpha\in(1-2s,1)
: this result can be generalized to our assumptions whenever
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x
is assumed a priori finite; however, we see that the condition on the Hölder regularity of
g
forces a restriction on the singularity of
g_{-}
at the origin.
It is not known if this identity holds for general continuous nonlinearities
g
and general values of
s\in(0,1)
.
Additionally, we observe that, due to the possible sublinearity of
g
, for a general solution
u
of equation (
1.8
) we cannot state a priori that it satisfies the
Nehari identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)u\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(1.12)

To state the main theorem, we make use of a
Lagrangian formulation
of the problem (in the spirit of
[
39
,
22
]
) and introduce, in addition to (
1.3
), a Lagrangian functional over the product space:
I^{m}\colon\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,

I^{m}(\mu,u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-m\right)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(1.13)

Notice that critical points of
I^{m}
are (formally) solutions to (
1.8
) and
I^{m}(\mu,u)=K(u)
if
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m
.

From now on, to state our results we will use the following convention.

Convention:
whenever we deal with a pair
(\mu,u)
(or
(\lambda,u)
with
e^{\lambda}=\mu
, see (
5.5
)) and we say that such a pair is radially symmetric, nonnegative, does not change sign, etc.,
we are referring to
u
.

We introduce the following notations, standing for the
L^{2}
-sphere in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and the Pohožaev set in the product space
\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
:

\mathcal{S}_{m}:=\big\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid|u|_{2}^{2}=m\big\},

(1.14)

where
|\cdot|_{q}
is the usual Lebesgue norm,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and

\mathcal{P}:=\left\{(\mu,u)\in\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid u\neq 0\text{ and }P(\mu,u)=0\right\},

(1.15)

where
P\colon\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is the Pohožaev functional
1
1

1

We highlight that the terminology “Pohožaev functional”, in the framework of normalized solutions, is often referred to a suitable combination of the Pohožaev and Nehari functionals, which allows getting rid of the Lagrange multiplier
\mu
; such a functional appears, in particular, in the framework of
L^{2}
-supercritical problems. Here, instead, we focus on the proper Pohožaev identity, considering a two-variable functional.

P(\mu,u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2_{s}^{*}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}-G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(1.16)

It is clear that
(\mu,u)\in\mathcal{P}
if and only if it satisfies the Pohožaev identity (
1.11
).
Moreover,
\mathcal{P}\neq\emptyset
because, fixed
u\in H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)>-\infty
,
P(\cdot,u)
is a continuous function such that
\lim_{\mu\to\pm\infty}P(\mu,u)=\pm\infty
.
Next, we set

\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}:=\mathcal{S}_{m}\cap H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\mathcal{P}^{\textup{rad}}:=\mathcal{P}\cap\bigl(\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr),

where
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the classical fractional Sobolev space, cf. (
2.1
), and
H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is its subspace of radially symmetric functions,
and

\kappa^{m}:=\inf_{\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K,\qquad d^{m}:=\inf_{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{rad}}}I^{m};

(1.17)

here
K
and
I^{m}
are defined in (
1.3
) and (
1.13
).
We further define

\overline{\mu}_{0}:=\sup_{t\neq 0}\frac{G(t)}{t^{2}/2};

(1.18)

notice that if
(g4)
holds, then
\overline{\mu}_{0}\in(0,+\infty]
.
Moreover, we introduce the
L^{2}
-ball and the corresponding infimum:

\mathcal{D}_{m}:=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid|u|_{2}^{2}\leq m\right\},\quad\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}:=\mathcal{D}_{m}\cap H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad\ell^{m}:=\inf_{\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K;

(1.19)

we mention that the use of the
L^{2}
-ball in normalized problems goes back to
[
12
]
, while it was utilized for the first time in the
L^{2}
-subcritical regime in
[
55
]
and in a mixed regime in
[
11
]
.
We can thus state our first theorem, see Definition
2.4
for the notion of solution.

Theorem 1.1
(Existence for large masses)

If
(g0)
–
(g4)
hold, then there exists
m_{0}\geq 0
such that for every
m>m_{0}
there exists
(\mu_{0},u_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}
with the following properties:

1.

(\mu_{0},u_{0})
is a solution to (
1.8
), and it
satisfies the Nehari identity (
1.12
);

2.

u_{0}
is a minimum (with Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{0}
) on the
L^{2}
-sphere and the
L^{2}
-ball, i.e.,
K(u_{0})=\kappa^{m}=\ell^{m}<0
;
moreover, we have the formula

\kappa^{m}=\inf_{-\infty<\mu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\left(a(\mu)-\mu\frac{m}{2}\right)=a(\mu_{0})-\mu_{0}\frac{m}{2},

(1.20)

where

a(\mu)
is the least action related to the problem with fixed frequency
\mu
, see (
3.3
).

3.

(\mu_{0},u_{0})
is a Pohožaev minimum on the product space, i.e.,
(\mu_{0},u_{0})\in\mathcal{P}^{\textup{rad}}
and
I^{m}(\mu_{0},u_{0})=d^{m}
;
in particular,
(\mu_{0},u_{0})
satisfies the Pohožaev identity (
1.11
).

As a consequence of the above relations, we have
\kappa^{m}=d^{m}
.

Under suitable additional assumptions about
g
, we highlight that the found solution can be chosen nonnegative and radially nonincreasing, see Proposition
1.12
.

Remark 1.2

We observe the following facts.

•

The mass threshold
m_{0}
is given by formula (
5.12
) and it ensures that
\kappa^{m}<0
if
m>m_{0}
. A detailed analysis on mass thresholds for fractional Schrödinger equations is provided in
[

]

•

Notice that, thanks to our method, we are able to state that the found Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{0}
is positive and the energy
\kappa^{m}
is negative.
At the same time, the found solution
(\mu_{0},u_{0})
minimizes
I^{m}
in the whole Pohožaev space over
\mathbb{R}\times H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, not only over
(0,+\infty)\times H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

•

In Theorem
1.1
, the solution is found for
m
sufficiently large. In Theorem
1.3
below, we find a solution for each
m>0
; on the other hand, in that case, we have no information on the sign of the Lagrange multiplier or that of the energy, nor do we know whether the solution minimizes the energy over
\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}
(which is coherent with Proposition
1.5
below).

•

We highlight that we cannot state that
u_{0}
is a critical point of

due to the lack of regularity of the functional, but only that

is differentiable at

The second difficulty is due to the absence of a Pohožaev identity: as we already pointed out, it is an open problem to determine if the Pohožaev identity holds for general continuous nonlinearities

Remark 1.4
(On the multiplicity)

We highlight that the present approach seems suitable for generalizations in the

Preliminaries

We first re-write here
g_{\pm}
and
G_{\pm}
, introduced in (
1.9
)-(
1.10
), in different terms:

G_{+}(t):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g^{+}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if }t\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{t}^{0}g^{-}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if }t<0,\end{cases}\quad G_{-}(t):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g^{-}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if }t\geq 0,\\
\displaystyle\int_{t}^{0}g^{+}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau&\text{if }t<0,\end{cases}

g_{+}(t):=G_{+}^{\prime}(t)=\begin{cases}g^{+}(t)&\text{if }t\geq 0,\\
-g^{-}(t)&\text{if }t\leq 0,\end{cases}\quad g_{-}(t):=G_{-}^{\prime}(t)=\begin{cases}g^{-}(t)&\text{if }t\geq 0,\\
-g^{+}(t)&\text{if }t\leq 0.\end{cases}

Clearly, we have
G_{\pm}(t)\geq 0
and
g_{\pm}(t)t\geq 0
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
;
moreover,

G=G_{+}-G_{-},\quad g=g_{+}-g_{-},

and

G_{+}(t)\geq G(t)\geq-G_{-}(t),\quad-g_{-}(t)t\leq g(t)t\leq g_{+}(t)t\quad\hbox{for $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Generally,
G_{\pm}
do not coincide with
G^{\pm}
, and unlike
G^{\pm}
, we have
G_{\pm}\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Finally, we notice that, if
g
is odd, then
G_{+},G_{-}
are even.

We briefly recall that the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{s}u:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}(u))
,
s\in(0,1)
, whenever
u
is regular enough, can be expressed pointwise as

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=C_{N,s}\,\mathrm{P.V.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}y,

for some suitable
C_{N,s}>0
; we refer to
[
35
, Section 1.2]
.
We recall the fractional Sobolev space

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mid|\cdot|^{s}\mathcal{F}(u)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\right\}

(2.1)

and define

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

It is well-known that
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
q\in[2,2^{*}_{s}]
, while
H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is compact for
q\in(2,2^{*}_{s})
; here
H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
stands for the subspace of
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
composed of radially symmetric functions.
We recall, moreover, the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[
53
]

|u|_{\bar{p}+1}^{\bar{p}+1}\leq C_{\textup{GN}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}^{2}|u|_{2}^{\bar{p}-1},

(2.2)

where
C_{\textup{GN}}>0
is optimal.
We also recall some basic properties (see, e.g.,
[
35
, Lemma 1.4.1]
).

Lemma 2.1

Let
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then
u^{\pm},|u|\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
|(-\Delta)^{s/2}(u^{\pm})|_{2}\leq|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}
and
|(-\Delta)^{s/2}|u||_{2}\leq|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}
. Finally,
\langle u,u^{+}\rangle\geq\langle u^{+},u^{+}\rangle
and
\langle u,u^{-}\rangle\leq-\langle u^{-},u^{-}\rangle
.

We show the following elementary convergence result.

Lemma 2.2

Let
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let
v_{R}:=v\varphi_{R}
, where
\varphi_{R}:=\varphi(\cdot/R)
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is chosen so that
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
,
\varphi=1
in
B_{1}(0)
,
\varphi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{2}(0)
.
Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{as }R\to+\infty

for each
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

We compute

\displaystyle\frac{2}{C_{N,s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}(v_{R}-v)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big((\varphi_{R}(x)-1)v(x)-(\varphi_{R}(y)-1)v(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}(\varphi_{R}(x)-1)\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(v(x)-v(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(\varphi_{R}(x)-\varphi_{R}(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=:(I_{1})+(I_{2}).

As regards
(I_{1})
, we observe that

\frac{\big|u(x)-u(y)\big|\big|v(x)-v(y)\big|}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}=\left(\frac{\big|u(x)-u(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\right)^{1/2}\left(\frac{\big|v(x)-v(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\right)^{1/2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2N}),

thus we can apply the dominated convergence theorem. Regarding
(I_{2})
, we have

\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\big(\varphi_{R}(x)-\varphi_{R}(y)\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}v(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\bigg|\\
\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big|u(x)-u(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}\frac{\big|\varphi_{R}(x)-\varphi_{R}(y)\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}v^{2}(y)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and we can apply
[
3
, Lemma 1.4.5]
.

Lemma 2.3

Assume
(g0)
–
(g2)
. Then the functional

u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

is differentiable at every
u
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x>-\infty
along every direction
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

With the intent to apply Vitali’s convergence theorem, we take
t\neq 0
small and estimate

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{G(u+t\varphi)-G(u)}{t}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\frac{1}{t}\int_{u}^{u+t\varphi}g(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right|\lesssim\left|{\frac{1}{t}\int_{u}^{u+t\varphi}1+|\tau|^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi|+\left|{\frac{1}{t}\int_{u}^{u+t\varphi}|\tau|^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right|\leq|\varphi|+\left|{\frac{1}{t}\int_{u}^{u+t\varphi}(|u|+|\varphi|)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\,\mathrm{d}\tau}\right|

\displaystyle=|\varphi|+(|u|+|\varphi|)^{2^{*}_{s}-1}|\varphi|\lesssim|\varphi|+|u|^{2^{*}_{s}-1}|\varphi|+|\varphi|^{2^{*}_{s}},

where we used the convexity of
t^{2^{*}_{s}-1}
. Then, fixed
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
measurable, we have

\int_{A}\left|{\frac{G(u+t\varphi)-G(u)}{t}}\right|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\int_{A}|\varphi|\,\mathrm{d}x+|u|_{2^{*}_{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}-1}\left(\int_{A}|\varphi|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}}+\int_{A}|\varphi|^{2^{*}_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and thus we get both the uniform integrability and the tightness. This allows us to compute

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u+t\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x}{t}=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{G(u+t\varphi)-G(u)}{t}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x

and conclude.

Definition 2.4

By

(or simply

) of (

we mean

for each

We highlight that, generally, a solution

3
The free-mass problem

The purpose of this section is to discuss the free-mass problem (i.e.,
\mu
fixed). For any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
we introduce a Pohožaev set

\mathcal{P}_{\mu}:=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}\mid P_{\mu}(u)=0\right\},\quad\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}:=\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\cap H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(3.1)

where the Pohožaev functional
P_{\mu}\colon H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

P_{\mu}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2^{*}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}-G(u)\,\mathrm{d}x.

(3.2)

Recalled (
2.3
), we define the least action

a(\mu):=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}.

(3.3)

We notice that, generally, the set
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
could be empty, and in this case,
a(\mu)=+\infty
.

Here we list a series of properties that hold for
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
under
(g0)
–
(g2)
(we recall from (
1.18
) the definition of
\overline{\mu}_{0}
).

Lemma 3.1

Assume
(g0)
–
(g2)
and let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

\mu<\overline{\mu}_{0}
.

(b)

There exists
t=t(\mu)\neq 0
such that
G(t)>\frac{1}{2}\mu t^{2}
.

(c)

There exists
u\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}.

Proof.

(a)
\iff
(b) is trivial.
If (c) holds, then
u\neq 0
; thus, by the Pohožaev identity,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)-\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{2_{s}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x>0,

hence there exists
t(\mu):=u(x_{\mu})
,
x_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, such that (b) is verified.
Assume (b) holds, from
[
8
, Proof of Theorem 2]
(see also
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
) there exists
\bar{u}\in H^{1}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(G(\bar{u})-\frac{\mu}{2}\bar{u}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x>0
. Then, for
t>0
, straightforward computations show that

P_{\mu}\bigl(\bar{u}(\cdot/t)\bigr)=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}\bar{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)t^{N-2s}+2^{*}_{s}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mu}{2}\bar{u}^{2}-G(\bar{u})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)t^{N}

is positive when
t
is small and negative when
t
is large, hence there exists
\bar{t}>0
such that
P_{\mu}\bigl(\bar{u}(\cdot/\bar{t})\bigr)=0
, whence (c).

Remark 3.2

By the previous proof we notice the following fact: for every
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}
such that
P_{\mu}(u)\leq 0
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(G(u)-\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x>0
, thus there exists
t_{0}>0
such that
u_{0}:=u(\cdot/t_{0})\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
.
Moreover, using that
P_{\mu}(u)\leq 0
and that
t\in(0,\infty)\mapsto\frac{1}{2}t^{N-2s}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}t^{N}\in\mathbb{R}
is maximized at
t=1
, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{st_{0}^{N-2s}}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=J_{\mu}(u_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{0}^{N-2s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x-t_{0}^{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)-\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{t_{0}^{N-2s}}{2}-\frac{t_{0}^{N}}{2^{*}_{s}}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

that is,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
, with a strict inequality if
P_{\mu}(u)<0
, and
t_{0}\leq 1
.

Notice that this fact does not depend on the sign of
\mu
.

Lemma 3.3

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and assume
(g0)
–
(g2)
. If
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\neq\emptyset
and
u\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is such that
J_{\mu}(u)=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}}J_{\mu}
, then
\omega:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)-\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x>0
and

\frac{1}{2}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}^{2}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}v|_{2}^{2}\mid v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(v)-\frac{\mu}{2}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\omega\right\}.

(3.4)

Proof.

First, notice that since
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a function
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(v)-\frac{\mu}{2}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=1
, thus the minimization problem

T:=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{2}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}v|_{2}^{2}\mid v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(v)-\frac{\mu}{2}v^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=1\right\}

is well defined. Moreover, every such function verifies

.
We adapt now the proof of
[
15

. We notice that we do not need to assume that the Pohožaev identity holds for every solution or that

is a priori attained.
Since

, we have that

J_{\mu}(u)\geq 2s(N-2s)^{\frac{N}{2s}-1}N^{-\frac{N}{2s}}T^{\frac{N}{2s}}=:\widetilde{T}.

Now, let
(v_{n})_{n}
be a minimizing sequence for

, and let

be such that

. Then, since

,

\widetilde{T}\leq J_{\mu}(u)\leq J_{\mu}\bigl(v_{n}(\cdot/t_{n})\bigr)=\widetilde{T}+o_{n}(1),

and we conclude letting

When

By definition of

Theorem 3.7

Let
\mu>0
and
g\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

•

|g(t)|\lesssim|t|+|t|^{2^{*}_{s}-1}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
;

•

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{G(t)}{t^{2}}\leq 0
and
\displaystyle\limsup_{|t|\to\infty}\frac{G(t)}{|t|^{2^{*}_{s}}}\leq 0
;

•

there exists
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
G(t_{0})>\frac{1}{2}\mu t_{0}^{2}
.

Then the following statements hold true.

1.

There exists
v_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}
such that

J_{\mu}(v_{0})=a(\mu)

and thus
a(\mu)>0
.
Moreover,
a\colon(0,\overline{\mu}_{0})\to(0,+\infty)
is increasing.

2.

If
g|_{(-\infty,0)}=0
or
g
is odd, then there exists a Schwarz-symmetric minimizer of
J_{\mu}
over
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
; in particular,

\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}}J_{\mu}=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}.

Proof.

(i)
From the assumptions, we see that for every
\delta>0
there exists
C_{\delta}>0
such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

G(t)\leq\delta t^{2}+C_{\delta}|t|^{2^{*}_{s}},

whence, for every
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}P_{\mu}(u)\geq\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}|u|_{2}^{2}-\delta|u|_{2}^{2}-C_{\delta}|u|_{2^{*}_{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}}\geq\left(\min\left\{\tfrac{1}{2^{*}_{s}},\tfrac{\mu}{2}\right\}-\delta\right)\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}-C_{\delta}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}}.

Taking
0<\delta<\min\{1/2^{*}_{s},\mu/2\}
, we see that
P_{\mu}(u)>0
if
\|u\|_{H^{s}}
is sufficiently small.

Let now
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}
such that
\lim_{n}J_{\mu}(u_{n})=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}
. Since
P_{\mu}(u_{n})=0
, we see that

\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}+o(1)=J_{\mu}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{s}}P_{\mu}(u_{n})=\frac{s}{N}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{n}|_{2}^{2},

which yields that
|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{n}|_{2}
is bounded, and, using the same
\delta
-related argument as before, that

\displaystyle\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}+o(1)

\displaystyle=J_{\mu}(u_{n})-\frac{1}{2}P_{\mu}(u_{n})=-\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2}-1\right)|u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2}-1\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2}-1\right)|u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+\left(\frac{2^{*}_{s}}{2}-1\right)\left(\delta|u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+C_{\delta}|u_{n}|_{2^{*}_{s}}^{2^{*}_{s}}\right).

Since
|u_{n}|_{2^{*}_{s}}
is bounded from the previous step and the fractional Sobolev embedding, taking
\delta<\mu/2
we obtain that
|u_{n}|_{2}
is bounded as well, i.e.,
\|u_{n}\|_{H^{s}}
is bounded.
We can then consider a subsequence, still denoted by
u_{n}
, and
u_{0}\in H_{\textup{rad}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{n}\to u_{0}
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
u_{n}\to u_{0}
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
q\in(2,2^{*}_{s})
. In particular, using the weak lower semi-continuity of the norm and Fatou’s lemma, we get that
J_{\mu}(u_{0})\leq\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu}
and
P_{\mu}(u_{0})\leq 0
. In addition, recalling
P_{\mu}(u_{n})=0
, we have by the strong convergence in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2^{*}_{s}\mu}{2}|u_{0}|_{2}^{2}+2^{*}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{-}(u_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n}\left(|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+\frac{2^{*}_{s}\mu}{2}|u_{n}|_{2}^{2}+2^{*}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{-}(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=2^{*}_{s}\limsup_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{+}(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}x=2^{*}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{+}(u_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x;

if
u_{0}\equiv 0
, the above relation implies
u_{n}\to 0
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which is impossible because
P_{\mu}(u)>0
for small
u
. Since
u_{0}\neq 0
, by Remark
3.2

we can find
t_{0}>0
such that
P_{\mu}\bigl(u_{0}(\cdot/t_{0})\bigr)=0
and

\displaystyle J_{\mu}\bigl(u_{0}(\cdot/t_{0})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}(\cdot/t_{0})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n}\frac{s}{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{n}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\lim_{n}J_{\mu}(u_{n})=a(\mu),

so the sought minimizer is
v_{0}:=u_{0}(\cdot/t_{0})
.
By the Pohožaev identity we also have

.

To show the strict monotonicity, arguing as in (
3.6
), in the case
\mu_{2}>0
we choose
u
as the minimum corresponding to
\mu_{2}
. Hence, from Proposition
3.4
,

and

(ii) We start with the case when
g
is odd. Let
(u_{n})_{n}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
be such that
\lim_{n}J_{\mu}(u_{n})=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu}}J_{\mu}
. From Lemma

,
J_{\mu}(|u_{n}|)\leq J_{\mu}(u_{n})
and
P_{\mu}(|u_{n}|)\leq P_{\mu}(u_{n})=0
. If

is such that

This proves that, up to replacing

with

5
The superlinear (perturbed) problem

In this section, where we always assume
(g0)
–
(g2)
, we study the following perturbed problem

\begin{cases}\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u+\mu u=g_{\varepsilon}(u),\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=m,\\
(\mu,u)\in(0,+\infty)\times H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\end{cases}

(5.1)

where, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
g_{\varepsilon}
is defined as follows:
let
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},[0,1])
be given by

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t):=\begin{cases}|t|/\varepsilon&\quad\hbox{for $|t|<\varepsilon$},\\
1&\quad\hbox{for $|t|\geq\varepsilon$},\end{cases}

(5.2)

and set

g_{-}^{\varepsilon}(t):=\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)g_{-}(t),\quad G_{-}^{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g_{-}^{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.

Then, we define

g_{\varepsilon}(t):=g_{+}(t)-g_{-}^{\varepsilon}(t),\quad G_{\varepsilon}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}g_{\varepsilon}(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau=G_{+}(t)-G_{-}^{\varepsilon}(t).

(5.3)

Notice that
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\to 1
pointwise, thus

g_{\varepsilon}(t)\to g(t),\quad G_{\varepsilon}(t)\to G(t)\quad\hbox{for every $t\in\mathbb{R}$}

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Hence, (
5.1
) can be considered as an approximating family of problems; the advantage is given by the modification in zero, which suppresses the singularity in the sublinear case:

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{g_{\varepsilon}(t)}{t}=0.

(5.4)

We will make large use of the following monotonicity property: if
0<\varepsilon^{\prime}<\varepsilon\leq 1
, then

G_{-}^{\varepsilon}\leq G_{-}^{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\leq G_{-},

which implies, moreover,

G_{\varepsilon}\geq G_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\geq G.

The purpose of this section is
to obtain the existence of a Pohožaev minimum for the problem (
5.1
), furnishing, in addition, a slight generalization of
[
22
]
by exploiting the refined Theorem
3.7
; such a generalization holds for more general nonlinearities not related to the perturbation process, see Remark
5.3
below.

5.1
\varepsilon
fixed: some refinement for the superlinear problem

We start by considering
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
fixed. Assuming

, to avoid technical issues related to the boundary of

\mu\equiv e^{\lambda}.

(5.5)

A similar approach that does not make use of (

Remark 5.1

The condition

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}K_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\ell_{\varepsilon}^{m}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\ell_{\varepsilon}^{m}\leq\inf_{\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K\leq K(u_{0}).

This yields that
u_{0}
is a minimum over the
L^{2}
-ball and the following holds as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
:
\ell_{\varepsilon}^{m}\to\ell^{m}
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{-}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G_{-}(u_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty
, and
(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}\to(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
By the fact that
\ell_{\varepsilon}^{m}\leq\beta_{m}<0
, we obtain
K(u_{0})\leq\beta_{m}<0
; in particular,
u_{0}\neq 0
. Moreover, by Proposition
4.2
(iv) we have that
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}
which implies
u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and hence
P(\mu_{0},u_{0})=0
. Moreover, by Proposition
4.2
(i) there exists
\tilde{\mu}
such that

is a solution of (
1.1
) and it is a Pohožaev minimum over

; since

and

, it must be
\tilde{\mu}=\mu_{0}
.
Finally by Proposition
1.5
we have
\kappa^{m}=\ell^{m}=d^{m}_{+}=d^{m}
.

To show the Nehari identity, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 6.5

The solution
u_{0}
given in (
6.1
) satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{-}(u_{0})u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty
.

Proof.

Let us recall that, as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
,
\mu_{\varepsilon}\to\mu_{0}
,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u_{0}
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
g_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\to g(u_{0})
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

satisfies the Nehari identity

|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}-g_{-}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since

and
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)\geq 0
for all

for all

, hence

\displaystyle|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{0}|u_{0}|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{-}(u_{0})u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u_{\varepsilon}|_{2}^{2}+\mu_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}|_{2}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{-}^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{+}(u_{0})u_{0}\,\mathrm{d}x

thanks to

Further existence results

7.1
Existence for all masses

In Theorem
1.1
, we obtained the existence of a solution for large masses. When we rule out the possibility of
g
to be superlinear at the origin (or positive nearby), which clearly mimics the logarithmic case, we can achieve existence also for all masses. Obviously, we already have existence for all masses if
m_{0}=0
, which is why we assume
m_{0}>0
in Theorem
7.2
below.

Lemma 7.1

Under assumptions
(g0)
–
(g4)
, for every
\omega\geq 0
there exists
\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\inf_{0<\mu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu+\omega^{\prime}}=\inf_{0<\mu<\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}}\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu+\omega^{\prime}}

(7.1)

for every
\omega^{\prime}\in[0,\omega]
. In particular,
m_{0}=\inf_{0<\mu<\widetilde{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu/2}.

Moreover,

\frac{\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}}{\omega}\to 0\quad\hbox{as $\omega\to+\infty$}.

Proof.

If
\overline{\mu}_{0}<+\infty
, we take
\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}:=\overline{\mu}_{0}
.
Otherwise, we set
M:=\inf_{\mu\in(0,+\infty)}\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu}
and

\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}:=\inf\left\{\widetilde{\mu}>0\;\middle|\;\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu+\omega}\geq M+1\;\hbox{for each $\mu\geq\widetilde{\mu}$}\right\}.

We observe that
\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}<+\infty
from Lemma
5.6
, while
\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}>0
from its definition and that of
M
. Additionally, from the continuity of
a\colon(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
(see Proposition
3.4
), we have

\frac{a(\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega})}{\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}+\omega}=M+1.

(7.2)

Consequently, if
\nu\geq\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}
and
\omega^{\prime}\in[0,\omega]
, then

\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu+\omega^{\prime}}\geq M+1>\inf_{0<\mu<+\infty}\frac{a(\mu)}{\mu+\omega^{\prime}},

whence (
7.1
).
We move to the last claim; we can assume
\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}\to+\infty
as
\omega\to+\infty
(otherwise, the claim is obvious).
Since (
7.2
) can be rewritten as

\frac{\omega}{\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}}=\frac{1}{M+1}\frac{a(\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega})}{\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}}-1,

the claim follows from Lemma
5.6
.

Theorem 7.2

Assume
(g0)
–
(g4)
,
m_{0}>0
, and

\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{g(t)}{t}=:-\overline{\omega}\in[-\infty,0).

Then, there exists
m_{1}=m_{1}(\overline{\omega})\in[0,m_{0})
such that, for every
m>m_{1}
, there exists a solution
(\mu_{0},u_{0})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}
to (
1.8
), which satisfies the Nehari and Pohožaev identities, and such that

I^{m}(\mu_{0},u_{0})=K(u_{0})=a(\mu_{0})-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}m=\kappa^{m}=d^{m}=\inf_{-\infty<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\left(a(\nu)-\frac{\nu}{2}m\right);

if
\kappa^{m}\leq 0
then
\mu_{0}>0
.
Finally, if
\overline{\omega}=\infty
, then
m_{1}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\omega\in(0,\overline{\omega})
and define, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

h_{\omega}(t):=g(t)+\omega t,\quad H_{\omega}(t):=G(t)+\frac{\omega}{2}t^{2}.

(7.3)

We observe that

•

h_{\omega}
is continuous and
h_{\omega}(0)=0
,

•

\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{|h_{\omega}(t)|}{|t|^{2^{*}_{s}-1}}<\infty
,

•

there exists
t_{0}\neq 0
such that
H_{\omega}(t_{0})>0
.

Moreover, it is straightforward to check that
\limsup_{t\to 0}\frac{h_{\omega}(t)}{t}<0
and
\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{h_{\omega}(t)}{|t|^{\bar{p}-1}t}\leq 0
, thus

h_{\omega}
satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
. In particular, for

m>m_{\omega}:=\inf_{0<\mu<\overline{\mu}_{\omega}}\frac{a_{\omega}(\mu)}{\mu/2}

there exists an
L^{2}
-minimum
u_{\omega}\in\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}
with Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{\omega}>0
, i.e.,

K_{\omega}(u_{\omega})=\inf_{\mathcal{D}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K_{\omega}=\inf_{\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K_{\omega}=:\kappa^{m}_{\omega}<0;

(7.4)

here, with the convention that
+\infty+t=+\infty
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\overline{\mu}_{\omega}:=\sup_{t\neq 0}\frac{H_{\omega}(t)}{t^{2}/2}=\overline{\mu}_{0}+\omega,\quad K_{\omega}(u):=\frac{1}{2}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\omega}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

a_{\omega}(\mu):=\inf_{\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\omega}^{\textup{rad}}}J_{\mu,\omega},\quad J_{\mu,\omega}(u):=\frac{1}{2}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|_{2}^{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}|u|_{2}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H_{\omega}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

P_{\mu,\omega}(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|(-\Delta)^{s/2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+2^{*}_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\mu}{2}u^{2}-H_{\omega}(u)\,\mathrm{d}x,

\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\omega}:=\left\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\setminus\{0\}\mid P_{\mu,\omega}(u)=0\right\},\quad\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\omega}^{\textup{rad}}:=\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\omega}\cap H^{s}_{\textup{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We notice that
(\mu_{\omega},u_{\omega})
satisfies the Nehari and Pohožaev identities with respect to
H_{\omega}
.
Since

K_{\omega}(u)=K(u)-\frac{\omega}{2}|u|_{2}^{2}
,
we obtain

K(u_{\omega})-\frac{\omega}{2}m=K(u_{\omega})-\frac{\omega}{2}|u_{\omega}|_{2}^{2}=K_{\omega}(u_{\omega})=\inf_{\mathcal{S}_{m}^{\textup{rad}}}K_{\omega}=\kappa^{m}-\frac{\omega}{2}m,

whence
K(u_{\omega})=\kappa^{m}
,
which means that
u_{\omega}
is an
L^{2}
-minimum for the original problem.
Moreover,

\kappa^{m}=\kappa^{m}_{\omega}+\frac{\omega}{2}m.

(7.5)

In addition,
u_{\omega}
is a solution to the Euler–Lagrange equation corresponding to
K_{\omega}
, i.e.,

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{\omega}+\mu_{\omega}u_{\omega}=h_{\omega}(u_{\omega})\quad\hbox{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},

which means
that
u_{0}:=u_{\omega}
is a solution to (
1.8
) with Lagrange multiplier

\mu_{0}:=\mu_{\omega}-\omega

(7.6)

and
(\mu_{0},u_{0})
satisfies the Nehari and Pohožaev identities with respect to
G
.
Additionally, for any
\nu>\omega
, we have

J_{\nu,\omega}(u)=J_{\nu-\omega}(u)
and
P_{\nu,\omega}(u)=P_{\nu-\omega}(u)
,
therefore

\frac{1}{2}m_{\omega}\leq\inf_{\omega<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}+\omega}\frac{a_{\omega}(\nu)}{\nu}=\inf_{0<\nu-\omega<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\nu-\omega)}{\nu}=\inf_{0<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu+\omega}=\inf_{0<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu}\frac{\nu}{\nu+\omega}.

(7.7)

Let
\widetilde{\mu}=\widetilde{\mu}_{\omega}\in(0,+\infty)
be the value given in Lemma
7.1
.
Since the function
\nu\in(0,+\infty)\mapsto\frac{\nu}{\nu+\omega}\in(0,+\infty)
is increasing, from (
7.7
) we obtain

\frac{1}{2}m_{\omega}\leq\inf_{0<\nu<\widetilde{\mu}}\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu}\frac{\nu}{\nu+\omega}\leq\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{\widetilde{\mu}+\omega}\inf_{0<\nu<\widetilde{\mu}}\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu}=\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{\widetilde{\mu}+\omega}\inf_{0<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\frac{a(\nu)}{\nu}=\frac{\widetilde{\mu}}{\widetilde{\mu}+\omega}\frac{m_{0}}{2}<\frac{1}{2}m_{0}.

In addition, if
\overline{\omega}=\infty
, taking
\omega
arbitrarily large we obtain

m_{\omega}\to 0\quad\hbox{as $\omega\to+\infty$}

(7.8)

again from Lemma
7.1
. As a consequence, for any
m>0
, there exists
m_{\omega}\in[0,m)
and thus an
L^{2}
-minimum
u_{\omega}
. We conclude by setting

m_{1}:=\inf_{0<\omega<\overline{\omega}}m_{\omega};

noticed that
\omega\mapsto m_{\omega}
is nonincreasing (this is a direct consequence of
J_{\mu,\omega}\leq J_{\mu}
,
P_{\mu,\omega}\leq P_{\mu}
,
\mu_{\omega}\geq\mu_{0}
, and an argument similar to (
5.9
)–(
5.13
)), we further observe that
m_{1}=\lim_{\omega\to\overline{\omega}^{-}}m_{\omega}
.
The sign of
\mu_{0}
is a simple consequence of the Pohožaev identity.
Finally, the fact that

K(u_{0})=I^{m}(\mu_{0},u_{0})=d^{m}=\inf_{-\infty<\nu<\overline{\mu}_{0}}\left(a(\nu)-\frac{\nu}{2}m\right)=a(\mu_{0})-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}m

follows from Propositions
1.5
,
1.6
, and
1.10
.

Remark 7.3

is nonincreasing with respect to
\overline{\omega}
. Moreover, in the case of small masses, Theorem

gives no information on the sign of the Lagrange multiplier and of the minimal energy – see indeed (

) and (

Proof of Theorem

.

It is a consequence of Theorem
7.2

7.2
Existence under relaxed assumptions

We deal now with Proposition
1.14
.
We thus search for a normalized solution which is nonnegative, possibly considering also the case

\limsup_{|t|\to+\infty}\frac{g_{-}(t)}{|t|^{2_{s}^{*}-1}}=+\infty.

The idea is to modify the nonlinearity
g
. We begin with the following result.

Lemma 7.4

Let
(g0)
–
(g2)
hold. Assume
g(t)=0
for
|t|\geq t_{1}
, and let
u
be a nonnegative solution of (
1.1
) for some
\mu\geq 0
. Then
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
|u|_{\infty}\leq t_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
v:=(u-t_{1})^{+}
. It is easy to check, via explicit computations, that
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Let
\varphi_{R}
be as in Lemma
2.2
and set
v_{R}:=v\varphi_{R}
.
Then
v_{R}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and hence

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x+\mu\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(u)v_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We notice that
g(u)v_{R}\equiv 0
by the assumptions. Since
u\geq 0
and
\mu\geq 0
, we have
\mu uv_{R}\geq 0
and thus

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}v_{R}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

By Lemma
2.2
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}(u-t_{1})^{+}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

On the other hand, using the inequality
(a-b)(a^{+}-b^{+})\geq|a^{+}-b^{+}|^{2}
for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(-\Delta)^{s/2}u(-\Delta)^{s/2}(u-t_{1})^{+}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\big((u(x)-t_{1})-(u(y)-t_{1})\big)\big((u(x)-t_{1})^{+}-(u(y)-t_{1})^{+}\big)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C_{N,s}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\big|(u(x)-t_{1})^{+}-(u(y)-t_{1})^{+}\big|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}=|(-\Delta)^{s/2}(u-t_{1})^{+}|_{2}^{2}.

Joining the two relations found we obtain
(u-t_{1})^{+}=0
, i.e.,
u\leq t_{1}
.

1.14
.

Let us assume that
t_{0}
, defined in
(g4)

as well. Let
t_{1}
be the first zero of

t_{0}
(if

for all

Clearly,

(g0)
–

If

Finally, if

\omega>0

or

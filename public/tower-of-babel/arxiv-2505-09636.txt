Three combinatorial sums involving central binomial coefficients


Abstract
We study three classes of combinatorial sums involving central binomial coefficients and harmonic numbers, odd harmonic numbers, and even indexed harmonic numbers, respectively. In each case we use summation by parts to derive recursive expressions for these sums. In addition, we offer an alternative approach to express one class of sums and some related sums in closed form in terms of Stirling numbers and r r -Stirling numbers of the second kind.

Introduction

The central binomial coefficients are defined for
n=0,1,\ldots,
by

\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^{2}}.

These numbers are listed under the id A000984 in the OEIS database
[
12
]
. They are closely related to the famous Catalan numbers
(entry A000108 in
[
12
]
)

C_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}

and possess many intriguing properties (
[
8
,
10
,
11
,
13
,
16
]
).

Riordan
[
15
, p. 130]
proved the combinatorial identity

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}\frac{1}{1-2k}=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}.

(1)

This identity has a dual counterpart as given by

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{n}{k}\binom{2k}{k}^{-1}2^{2k}=\frac{1}{1-2n}.

(2)

See
[
2
]
for a generalization of these sums. Alzer and Nagy
[
3
]
proved several identities involving
the central binomial coefficients and Catalan numbers. Boyadzhiev
[
5
]
considered four identities for the squared central binomial coefficients.

The following combinatorial identity is also well-known:

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}=2^{-2n}(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}.

(3)

This identity is stated, for instance, as Theorem 23 in
[
14
]
and as Theorem 4 in
[
6
]
.
Both proofs are based on integration and apply integral representations for the Catalan numbers.
In
[
4
, Theorem 7.1]
the following generalization was proved: For
r\geq 0
define

S_{r}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{r}\binom{2k}{k},\qquad S_{0}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}.

(4)

Then

S_{0}(n)=2^{-2n}(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}

(5)

and

(2r+1)S_{r}(n)=n^{r}2^{-2n}(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}S_{r-j}(n).

(6)

For
r=1,2,3
the recursion (
6
) immediately produces the identities

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k\binom{2k}{k}=\frac{n}{3}2^{-2n}(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n},

(7)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}=\frac{n}{15}2^{-2n}(3n+2)(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n},

(8)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}=\frac{n}{105}2^{-2n}(15n^{2}+18n+2)(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}.

(9)

As was observed in
[
4
]
, for
r\geq 1
, the sum
S_{r}(n)
is of the form
n(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}P_{r}(n)

for some polynomial
P_{r}(x)
of degree
m-1
. The recursion (
6
) shows that the polynomials
P_{r}(x)

satisfy
P_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{3}
and

(2r+1)P_{r}(x)=x^{r-1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}P_{r-j}(x).

The next combinatorial sum is very similar to (
3
) and involves the familiar odd harmonic numbers
O_{n}
(see
[
1
]
):

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)O_{n}-2n\right).

(10)

Here and throughout the paper
O_{n},n\geq 0,
are the odd harmonic numbers and
H_{n},n\geq 0,
are the ordinary harmonic numbers, i.e.,

H_{0}=0,\quad H_{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad O_{0}=0,\quad O_{n}=\sum_{m=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2m-1}.

In this paper, we are concerned with the following three classes of combinatorial sums defined for all integers
r\geq 0
:

S_{r}^{H}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{r}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k},\qquad S_{0}^{H}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k},

(11)

its odd variant

S_{r}^{O}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{r}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k},\qquad S_{0}^{O}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k},

(12)

and their even indexed cousin

S_{r}^{2H}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{r}\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k},\qquad S_{0}^{2H}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}.

(13)

We will show that there are close connections between the sums
S_{r}^{H}(n)
, respectively
S_{r}^{O}(n)
and
S_{r}^{2H}(n)
, and
S_{r}(n)
.
More precisely, we will use summation by parts solve these sums recursively for each
r
, hereby using the recursion for
S_{r}(n)

as in (
6
). In addition, we will offer an alternative approach to express
S_{r}^{O}(n)
and some related sums in closed form
in terms of Stirling numbers and
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind.

2
The sums
S_{r}^{H}(n)
and
S_{r}^{O}(n)

As will become obvious in a sequel, it is better to start with the sums
S_{r}^{O}(n)
. Our first main result in this paper is the next theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
S_{r}^{O}(n)
be defined as in (
12
). Then

S_{0}^{O}(n)=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)O_{n}-2n\right)

(14)

and for
r\geq 1
we have the following recursion:

(2r+1)S_{r}^{O}(n)=n^{r}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)O_{n}+1\right)-S_{r}(n)+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}S_{r-j}^{O}(n),

(15)

where
S_{r}(n)
is defined in (
4
) and given recursively in (
6
).

Proof.

Let us define two sequences
u_{k}=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}
and
w_{k}=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}
. First we note that

w_{k}=2(k+1)(w_{k}-w_{k+1}).

(16)

Using this property we have

\displaystyle u_{k}-u_{k+1}

\displaystyle=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}-2^{-2(k+1)}\binom{2(k+1)}{k+1}O_{k}-2^{-2(k+1)}\binom{2(k+1)}{k+1}\frac{1}{2k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(k+1)}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}-\frac{1}{2(k+1)}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}.

Hence,

2(k+1)(u_{k}-u_{k+1})=u_{k}-w_{k}=u_{k}-2(k+1)(w_{k}-w_{k+1})

or

u_{k}=2(k+1)(u_{k}+w_{k}-(u_{k+1}+w_{k+1})).

From here we can use telescoping and calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{k}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)(u_{k}-u_{k+1})+\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)(w_{k}-w_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}(u_{k}+w_{k})-2(n+1)(u_{n+1}+w_{n+1})

and, using (
3
),

\sum_{k=0}^{n}u_{k}=-2\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}+2(n+1)(u_{n+1}+w_{n+1})=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)O_{n}-2n\right)

as stated in (
10
). Now, suppose that
r\geq 1
. To calculate
S_{r}^{O}(n)
we use summation by parts with

z_{k}=u_{k}+w_{k}
and
a_{k}=k(k-1)^{r}
. We have

\displaystyle S_{r}^{O}(n)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{r}(k+1)(z_{k}-z_{k+1})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k+1}(z_{k}-z_{k+1})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}z_{k}(a_{k+1}-a_{k})-2a_{n+1}z_{n+1}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}z_{k}\left((r+1)k^{r}-\sum_{j=2}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(-1)^{j}k^{r-j+1}\right)-2z_{n+1}(n+1)n^{r}

\displaystyle=(2r+2)\left(S_{r}^{O}(n)+S_{r}(n)\right)-2(n+1)n^{r}(u_{n+1}+w_{n+1})

\displaystyle\qquad-2\sum_{j=2}^{r}\binom{r}{j}(-1)^{j}\left(S_{r-j+1}^{O}(n)+S_{r-j+1}(n)\right).

Shifting the summation index and simplifying yields

\displaystyle(2r+1)S_{r}^{O}(n)

\displaystyle=n^{r}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)O_{n}+2n+2\right)-(2r+2)S_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}\left(S_{r-j}^{O}(n)+S_{r-j}(n)\right).

The final expression follows by inserting (
6
) and again simplifying.
∎

Corollary 2
.

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}=\frac{n}{3}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\Big((2n+1)O_{n}-\frac{2}{3}(n-1)\Big),

(17)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{2}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}=\frac{n}{15}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\Big((3n+2)(2n+1)O_{n}-\frac{2}{15}(9n-7)(n-1)\Big),

(18)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{n}{105}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\Big((15n^{2}+18n+2)(2n+1)O_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\frac{2}{105}(225n^{2}+198n-71)(n-1)\Big).

(19)

Proof.

Set
r=1,2,3
in (
15
) and combine with (
7
)-(
9
).
∎

(21)

Our second theorem contains the results for the sums
S_{r}^{H}(n)
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
S_{r}^{H}(n)
be defined as in (
11
). Then

S_{0}^{H}(n)=(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}(H_{n}-2)+2

(23)

and for
r\geq 1
we have the following recursion:

\displaystyle(2r+1)S_{r}^{H}(n)

\displaystyle=n^{r}(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left(H_{n}+1\right)+2(-1)^{r}-S_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}S_{r-j}^{H}(n)-2\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}(-1)^{r-j}S_{j}(n),

(24)

where
S_{r}(n)
is defined in (
4
) and given recursively in (
6
).

Proof.

We apply the same ideas as in the proof of Theorem
1
. We start with the two sequences

v_{k}=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k}
and
w_{k}=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}
. Then

\displaystyle v_{k}-v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k}-2^{-2(k+1)}\binom{2(k+1)}{k+1}H_{k}-2^{-2(k+1)}\binom{2(k+1)}{k+1}\frac{1}{k+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2(k+1)}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k}-\frac{1}{2(k+1)}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}\left(1+\frac{k}{k+1}\right).

Hence, using (
16
),

2(k+1)(v_{k}-v_{k+1})=v_{k}-2(k+1)(w_{k}-w_{k+1})-2k(w_{k}-w_{k+1})

or

v_{k}=2(k+1)(v_{k}-v_{k+1}+w_{k}-w_{k+1})+2k(w_{k}-w_{k+1}).

An application of the telescoping property yields

\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{k}=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{k}-2(n+1)v_{n+1}+4\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}-4(n+1)w_{n+1}-2w_{0}+2w_{n+1}

and finally

\displaystyle S_{0}^{H}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}v_{k}=2(n+1)v_{n+1}+(4n+2)w_{n+1}-4\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}+2w_{0}

\displaystyle=(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left(H_{n+1}+\frac{2n+1}{n+1}-4\right)+2,

and the expression for
S_{0}^{H}(n)
follows. Now, suppose that
r\geq 1
. To calculate
S_{r}^{H}(n)
we use summation by parts twice.
We set
Z_{k}=v_{k}+w_{k}
and
a_{k}=k(k-1)^{r}
, and calculate

\displaystyle S_{r}^{H}(n)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{r}(k+1)(Z_{k}-Z_{k+1})+2\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{r+1}(w_{k}-w_{k+1})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{k+1}(Z_{k}-Z_{k+1})+2\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{r+1}(w_{k}-w_{k+1})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{n}Z_{k}(a_{k+1}-a_{k})-2a_{n+1}Z_{n+1}+2A_{r}(n),

where we have set

A_{r}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{r+1}(w_{k}-w_{k+1}).

This gives

\displaystyle S_{r}^{H}(n)

\displaystyle=(2r+2)\left(S_{r}^{H}(n)+S_{r}(n)\right)-n^{r}(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}(H_{n+1}+1)+2A_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\qquad-2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}\left(S_{r-j}^{H}(n)+S_{r-j}(n)\right)

or

(2r+1)S_{r}^{H}(n)=n^{r}(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}H_{n+1}-S_{r}(n)-2A_{r}(n)+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}S_{r-j}^{H}(n).

It remains to provide a link between
A_{r}(n)
and
S_{r}(n)
. This is done by applying partial summation a second time.
With
b_{k}=(k-1)^{r+1}
we have

\displaystyle A_{r}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k+1}(w_{k}-w_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}(b_{k+1}-b_{k})-w_{n+1}b_{n+1}+w_{0}b_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}w_{k}\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}k^{j}(-1)^{r-j}-w_{n+1}b_{n+1}+w_{0}b_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}k^{j}(-1)^{r-j}S_{j}(n)-\frac{2n+1}{2(n+1)}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}n^{r+1}+(-1)^{r+1}

and the theorem is proved.
∎

(26)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{3}\binom{2k}{k}H_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{7}(2n+1)2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\Big(\frac{n}{15}(15n^{2}+18n+2)H_{n}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2}{1575}(225n^{3}-297n^{2}+37n+105)\Big)+\frac{2}{105}.

(27)

Proof.

Set
r=1,2,3
in (
3
) and combine with (
7
)-(

).

Further observations

From the two main results derived in the previous section we can also deduce the third main result dealing with sums with even indexed harmonic numbers
S_{r}^{2H}(n)
given in (
13
). For this class of combinatorial sums the following recursion is true.

Theorem 5
.

We have

S_{0}^{2H}(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}=2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)H_{2n}-(4n+1)\right)+1,

(28)

and for
r\geq 1
the following recursion holds:

\displaystyle(2r+1)S_{r}^{2H}(n)

\displaystyle=n^{r}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left((2n+1)H_{2n}+n+\frac{3}{2}\right)+(-1)^{r}-\frac{3}{2}S_{r}(n)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}\binom{r}{j+1}(-1)^{j+1}S_{r-j}^{2H}(n)-\sum_{j=0}^{r}\binom{r+1}{j}(-1)^{r-j}S_{j}(n),

(29)

where
S_{r}(n)
is defined in (
4
) and given recursively in (
6
). In particular,

\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k\binom{2k}{k}H_{2k}=\frac{1}{9}2^{-2n}\binom{2n}{n}\left(3n(2n+1)H_{2n}-4n^{2}+7n+3\right)-\frac{1}{3}.

(30)

Proof.

The basic connection between
H_{n}
and
O_{n}

O_{n}=H_{2n}-\frac{1}{2}H_{n},

translates immediately to

S_{r}^{2H}(n)=S_{r}^{O}(n)+\frac{1}{2}S_{r}^{H}(n)

and the recursive expression is deduced from Theorems
1
and
3
.
∎

4
An alternative approach

In this section, based on a known polynomial identity, we express
S_{0}^{O}(n)
and some related sums in closed form in terms of Stirling numbers and
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind.

Recall that for integers
n
and
k
the Stirling numbers of the second kind, denoted by
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
, are the coefficients in the expansion

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}(x)_{k},

where
(x)_{k}
is the falling factorial defined by
(x)_{0}=1,(x)_{n}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-n+1)
for
n>0
. We will often make use of the property

\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}=0,\text{if $k>n$.}

The exponential generating function of
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}
equals

\sum_{n\geq k}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n}{k}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}\left(e^{z}-1\right)^{k}.

For any positive integer
r
, the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+r}{k+r}_{r}

are defined by the exponential generating function

\sum_{n\geq k}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{n+r}{k+r}_{r}\frac{z^{n}}{n!}=\frac{1}{k!}e^{rz}\left(e^{z}-1\right)^{k}.

Details about these special numbers can be found in Laissaoui and Rahmani
[
9
]
and references therein.

Lemma 1
.

If
k
,
r
and
v
are non-negative integers, then

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\left(1-e^{x}\right)^{k}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}p^{r}=(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r}{k},

(31)

\displaystyle\left.\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\left(1-e^{x}\right)^{k}e^{vx}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{k}{p}(v+p)^{r}=(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r+v}{k+v}_{v},

(32)

where
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m}{n}
and
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{m+r}{n+r}_{r}
are, respectively, Stirling numbers of the second kind and
r
-Stirling numbers.

Proof.

Since

\left({1-e^{x}}\right)^{k}e^{vx}=\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{{k}}{p}e^{(v+p)x};

we have

\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\left(\left({1-e^{x}}\right)^{k}e^{vx}\right)=\sum_{p=0}^{k}(-1)^{p}\binom{{k}}{p}\left(v+p\right)^{r}e^{(v+p)x};

and hence (
32
).
∎

Theorem 6
.

If
n
and
r
are non-negative integers and
t
is not an integer, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{r}\binom{t}{k}(H_{t}-H_{t-k})=\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r+n-k}{n}_{n-k}\binom{n-t}{n-k}(H_{k-t}-H_{n-t}).

(33)

Proof.

Write
\exp(x)
for
x
in the known polynomial identity (variation on Gould
[
7
, Identity (1.10),p.2]
):

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{t}}{k}x^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{{n-t}}{k}\left({1-x}\right)^{n-k}x^{k},

(34)

and differentiate
r
times with respect to
x
to obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{r}\binom{{t}}{k}e^{kx}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\left(\left(1-e^{x}\right)^{n-k}e^{kx}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{d^{r}}{dx^{r}}\left(\left(1-e^{x}\right)^{k}e^{(n-k)x}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{n-k}.

Evaluation at
x=0
yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}{(-1)^{k}k^{r}\binom{{t}}{k}}

\displaystyle=\left.{\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{{d^{r}}}{{dx^{r}}}\left({\left({1-e^{x}}\right)^{k}e^{(n-k)x}}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{n-k}}}\right|_{x=0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\left.{\frac{{d^{r}}}{{dx^{r}}}\left({\left({1-e^{x}}\right)^{k}e^{(n-k)x}}\right)}\right|_{x=0}\binom{{n-t}}{n-k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{r}{(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r+n-k}{n}_{n-k}\binom{{n-t}}{n-k}},

which gives (
33
) upon differentiating with respect to
t
, since

\frac{d}{{dt}}\binom{{t}}{k}=\binom{{t}}{k}\left({H_{t}-H_{t-k}}\right)

and

\frac{d}{{dt}}\binom{{n-t}}{k}=\binom{{n-t}}{k}\left({H_{n-t-k}-H_{n-t}}\right).

∎

The four lowest explicit formulas for
r=0,1,2,3
evaluations of (
33
) are presented in Proposition
1
.

Proposition 1
.

If
n
is a non-negative integer and
t
is not an integer, then

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}\binom{{t}}{k}\left(H_{t}-H_{t-k}\right)=\binom{{n-t}}{n}\left(H_{-t}-H_{n-t}\right),

(35)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k\binom{t}{k}\left(H_{t}-H_{t-k}\right)=-\binom{n-t}{n-1}\left(H_{1-t}-H_{n-t}\right)+n\binom{n-t}{n}\left(H_{-t}-H_{n-t}\right),

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{2}\binom{{t}}{k}\left({H_{t}-H_{t-k}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\binom{{n-t}}{{n-2}}\left({H_{2-t}-H_{n-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-\left({2n-1}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{{n-1}}\left({H_{1-t}-H_{n-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+n^{2}\binom{{n-t}}{n}\left({H_{-t}-H_{n-t}}\right),

(37)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}(-1)^{k}k^{3}\binom{{t}}{k}\left({H_{t}-H_{t-k}}\right)

\displaystyle=-6\binom{{n-t}}{{n-3}}\left({H_{3-t}-H_{n-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+6\left({n-1}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{{n-2}}\left({H_{2-t}-H_{n-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\left({3n^{2}-3n+1}\right)\binom{{n-t}}{{n-1}}\left({H_{1-t}-H_{n-t}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+n^{3}\binom{{n-t}}{n}\left({H_{-t}-H_{n-t}}\right).

(38)

Theorem
6
also provides a closed form for the sums
S_{r}^{O}(n)

Proposition 2
.

If
n
and
r
are non-negative integers, then

\displaystyle S_{r}^{O}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k^{r}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}

\displaystyle=2^{-2n}\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{k}k!\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r+n-k}{n}_{n-k}2^{2k}\binom{2n+1}{2k+1}\binom{2(n-k)}{n-k}\binom{n}{k}^{-1}(O_{n+1}-O_{k+1}).

(39)

In particular,

\displaystyle S_{0}^{O}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}=(2n+1)\binom{2n}{n}2^{-2n}\left(O_{n+1}-1\right),

\displaystyle S_{1}^{O}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}2^{-2k}k\binom{2k}{k}O_{k}=\frac{n(2n+1)}{3}\binom{2n}{n}2^{-2n}\left(O_{n+1}-\frac{1}{3}\right).

and the fact that

H_{h-1/2}=2O_{h}-2\ln 2.

Proposition 3

Boundary dynamics of Maxwell-invariant three-dimensional Chern-Simons gravity
Felix Höfenstock, and Patricio Salgado-Rebolledo

Abstract
We construct a two-dimensional dual field theory induced at the boundary of three-dimensional Chern-Simons gravity invariant under the Maxwell algebra. The resulting action takes the form of a Maxwellian extension of the flat Liouville theory known from the analysis of asymptotically flat three-dimensional gravity. This boundary theory is derived by reducing the bulk gravitational action to a Maxwell-invariant chiral Wess–Zumino–Witten model and imposing boundary conditions compatible with asymptotically flat geometries. Alternatively, we obtain the same theory as the geometric action on coadjoint orbits of the Maxwell extension of the BMS 3 group. Finally, we show how the boundary actions corresponding to both Poincaré and Maxwell invariance emerge from a Carrollian expansion of the boundary theory dual to AdS 3 Chern-Simons gravity.



1
Introduction

Extensions of anti-de Sitter (AdS) gravity and conformal field theory (CFT) have paved the way for generalizing the holographic duality beyond the original AdS/CFT framework
Maldacena:1997re
. In this broader context, several alternative holographic correspondences have been proposed, including dS/CFT
Strominger:2001pn
, Kerr/CFT
Guica:2008mu
, non-relativistic holography
Kachru:2008yh
;
Balasubramanian:2008dm
;
Son:2008ye
;
Bagchi:2010zz
, and flat holography
Barnich:2009se
;
Barnich:2010eb
;
Bagchi:2012yk
. In particular, flat holography has recently gained renewed interest in the form of celestial holography
Strominger:2017zoo
;
Pasterski:2016qvg
;
Adamo:2019ipt
;
Donnay:2020guq
and later as Carrollian holography
Donnay:2022aba
;
Bagchi:2022emh
, both of which explore new formulations of the holographic principle in asymptotically flat spacetimes.

An important playground where these ideas can be explored is three-dimensional gravity. In fact, a precursor to the AdS/CFT correspondence is the seminal result by Brown and Henneaux, who showed that the asymptotic symmetry algebra of asymptotically AdS
3
geometries in three spacetime dimensions is given by two copies of the Virasoro algebra, i.e., the centrally extended conformal symmetry in two dimensions
Brown:1986nw
. Key developments following this discovery include Strominger’s derivation of the Bekenstein–Hawking entropy for the BTZ black hole from a purely CFT perspective, using the Brown–Henneaux central charge and the Cardy formula
Strominger:1997eq
; and the identification of the classical boundary dual to AdS
3
Einstein gravity with Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions as Liouville theory, by Coussaert, Henneaux, and van Driel
Coussaert:1995zp
.

These results were later generalized to the case of asymptotically flat three-dimensional gravity, where the role of the BTZ black hole is played by a cosmological solution, and suitable boundary conditions lead to an asymptotic symmetry given by the
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra together with a boundary theory described by the flat Liouville model
Barnich:2006av
;
Barnich:2012xq
;
Barnich:2013yka
. These results can also be obtained as a vanishing cosmological constant limit or “flat limit” of their corresponding AdS counterparts. At the boundary, this is in concordance with the idea of Carrollian holography. Indeed, the flat Liouville model presented in
Barnich:2012rz
is equivalent to the electric Carroll limit of Liouville theory in the sense of
Henneaux:2021yzg
, which finds a natural explanation in the fact that the conformal extension of the Carroll algebra is isomorphic to the BMS symmetry
Duval:2014uva
. Along similar lines, in the context of higher-spin gravity, flat
\mathcal{W}_{N}
algebras have been shown to follow from ultra-relativistic limits of
\mathcal{W}_{N}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{N}
algebras
Campoleoni:2016vsh
, characteristic of higher-spin extensions of AdS
3
Chern–Simons gravity
Henneaux:2010xg
;
Campoleoni:2010zq
.

With the aim of extending notions of the AdS/CFT correspondence to asymptotically flat spacetimes, an intriguing question is whether minimal deformations of Poincaré symmetry can consistently support a bulk/boundary duality, especially within the tractable framework provided by three-dimensional Chern–Simons gravity. In this context, the Maxwell algebra arises as a natural candidate. It extends the Poincaré algebra by including additional antisymmetric tensorial generators, making translations non-Abelian via the relation

[P_{a},P_{b}]=Z_{ab}.

(1)

Historically, this symmetry appeared in the study of particles coupled to a constant electromagnetic field, where magnetic translations include minimal coupling to a gauge potential linear in the coordinates,
P_{a}=\partial_{a}-\frac{1}{2}Z_{ab}x^{b}
, and obey a non-vanishing commutator proportional to the field strength
Schrader:1972zd
(see also
Bacry:1970ye
;
Hoogland:1978wi
;
Beckers:1983gp
;
negro1990local
). In the context of gravity, the Maxwell algebra allows one to extend General Relativity by incorporating a generalized cosmological term
deAzcarraga:2010sw
;
deAzcarraga:2012qj
. In recent years, a wide range of generalized Maxwell-like symmetries have been proposed
Soroka:2004fj
;
Gomis:2009dm
;
Gibbons:2009me
;
Khasanov:2011jr
;
Salgado:2023owk
, including infinite-dimensional extensions
deAzcarraga:2007et
;
Bonanos:2008ez
;
Penafiel:2016ufo
;
Gomis:2017cmt
;
Gomis:2020wrv
, some of which have been used to construct gravity models
Edelstein:2006se
;
Izaurieta:2009hz
;
Durka:2011va
;
Salgado:2013eut
;
Concha:2013uhq
;
Concha:2014zsa
;
Gonzalez:2014tta
;
Aviles:2022tvh
.

Interestingly, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, the earliest appearance of Maxwell gravity occurs in the work of Cangemi
Cangemi:1992ri
(see also
Duval:2008tr
), where an extended Poincaré algebra was required to obtain a first-order formulation of Jackiw–Teitelboim or CGHS gravity upon dimensional reduction. Moreover, the first-order formulation of CGHS gravity is described by a BF theory invariant under a two-dimensional version of the Maxwell algebra
Cangemi:1992bj
;
Afshar:2019axx
. The Maxwell-invariant three-dimensional Chern–Simons theory introduced by Cangemi has recently been revisited from various angles. In contrast to Poincaré-invariant Chern–Simons gravity, it admits black hole solutions that closely resemble the BTZ black hole
Hoseinzadeh:2014bla
; it features an infinite-dimensional asymptotic symmetry algebra given by an extension of the
\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra with non-commuting supertranslations
Concha:2018zeb
; and it has even been proposed as an effective model for the boundary states of a topological insulator
Palumbo:2016nku
.

Given the fact that the flat Liouville model, dual to Poincaré-invariant Chern–Simons gravity, is a Carroll limit of the Liouville theory associated with three-dimensional AdS
3
gravity, a natural question is whether the deformation of Poincaré symmetry defined by the Maxwell algebra can lead to a boundary theory containing a post-Carrollian correction of such a Carrollian CFT. So far, post-Carrollian corrections to Carroll limits have been considered in the case of particle models
Gomis:2022spp
and gravitational theories in various dimensions
Gomis:2019nih
;
Hansen:2021fxi
;
Ecker:2025ncp
.

In this paper, we study the two-dimensional boundary theory induced at the boundary of Maxwell-invariant Chern–Simons gravity in three spacetime dimensions. After solving the constraints and imposing a suitable gauge-fixing condition, the gravitational action reduces to a Maxwell-invariant chiral Wess–Zumino–Witten (WZW) model. Imposing boundary conditions associated with the Maxwell–BMS
3
symmetry considered in
Concha:2018zeb
, we show that this chiral WZW model further reduces to a Maxwellian extension of the flat Liouville model considered in
Barnich:2012rz
;
Barnich:2013yka
. Finally, we consider the Carrollian expansion of the boundary theory associated with AdS
3
gravity
Coussaert:1995zp
, including the contribution of the so-called exotic gravitational term
Witten:1988hc
. We show that the boundary dual models mentioned above naturally emerge in the expansion as the terms along different powers of the speed of light, signaling a potential relation between Maxwell spacetime symmetry and post-Carrollian corrections to a Carrollian CFT.

The paper is structured as follows: Section
2
reviews the definition of the Maxwell group in three spacetime dimensions as an extended semidirect product based on the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
symmetry and introduces the basic group-theoretical tools used in the subsequent sections. Section
3
reviews Maxwell-invariant Chern–Simons gravity in three spacetime dimensions, together with the boundary conditions and asymptotic symmetry algebra given in
Concha:2018zeb
, which defines a Maxwell extension of the BMS
3
symmetry with three central terms. In Section
4
, the Chern–Simons action is reduced to a chiral WZW model with Maxwell invariance. After imposing the boundary conditions used to obtain the asymptotic symmetry algebra, the model reduces to a Maxwellian extension of the flat Liouville theory. We show how the Maxwellian boundary dual can also be obtained from the analysis of the geometric action on coadjoint orbits of the Maxwell–BMS
3
group. Finally, we show how a Carrollian expansion of the known boundary dual associated with three-dimensional AdS
3
Chern–Simons gravity with Brown–Henneaux boundary conditions reproduces the flat Liouville model and the Maxwellian boundary dual as a post-Carrollian correction. Section
5
concludes with a discussion of our results and their potential applications and future developments.

2
The Maxwell symmetry in three spacetime dimensions

In this section, we introduce the basic definitions related to the Maxwell group and the Maxwell algebra in three spacetime dimensions that will be used in the subsequent sections. For further details on Maxwell symmetry, we refer the reader to
Schrader:1972zd
;
Gomis:2009dm
;
deAzcarraga:2012qj
;
Salgado-Rebolledo:2019kft
.

2.1
The Maxwell
3
group

The Maxwell group in 2+1 dimensions is the extended semidirect product
Salgado-Rebolledo:2019kft

{\rm Maxwell}_{3}=SL(2,\mathbb{R})\ltimes_{{\rm Ad}}\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}^{(ext)},

(2)

where
{\rm Ad}
denotes the adjoint representation of the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
group,
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}
is the Abelian vector space formed by the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
algebra matrices under matrix addition, and
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}^{(ext)}
stands for the extension
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}\times\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}
with elements
(a,\tilde{a})
, endowed with the “extended sum”

(a,\tilde{a})\hat{+}(b,\tilde{b})=(a+b\,,\;\tilde{a}+\tilde{b}+[a,b]).

(3)

The elements of the Maxwell
3
group are therefore given by triplets
\left(\Lambda,a,\tilde{a}\right)
, where
\Lambda\in SL(2,\mathbb{R})
and
a,\tilde{a}\in\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})_{ab}
. The group product is

(\Lambda,a,\tilde{a})(\Gamma,b,\tilde{b})=\left(\Lambda\Gamma\,,\;a+\Lambda b\Lambda^{-1}\,,\;\tilde{a}+\Lambda\tilde{b}\Lambda^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\left[a,\Lambda b\Lambda^{-1}\right]\right).

(4)

The associated Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{maxwell}_{3}
, has the structure of an extended semidirect sum, and its elements can be identified with the tangent vectors at the identity of the corresponding Lie group

(X,a,\tilde{a})=\frac{d}{ds}\left(e^{sX},sa,s\tilde{a}\right)\bigg|_{s=0}.

(5)

The Lie algebra commutation relations are obtained by evaluating the infinitesimal adjoint representation

\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}\bigg((e^{s_{1}X},s_{1}a,s_{1}\tilde{a})(e^{s_{2}Y},s_{2}b,s_{2}\tilde{b})(e^{s_{1}X},s_{1}a,s_{1}\tilde{a})^{-1}\bigg)\bigg|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0},

(6)

which leads to the bracket

\left[(x,a,\tilde{a})\;,\;(y,b,\tilde{b})\right]=\left([x,y]\,,\;[x,b]-[y,a]\,,\;[x,\tilde{b}]-[y,\tilde{a}]+[a,b]\right).

(7)

Now we choose a basis
t_{a}
for
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
satisfying

[t_{a},t_{b}]=\epsilon^{c}{}_{ab}t_{c},\qquad{\rm Tr}(t_{a}t_{b})=\eta_{ab},

(8)

where
{\rm Tr}
denotes the matrix trace,
\epsilon_{abc}
is the Levi-Civita symbol in three dimensions, and
\eta_{ab}
is the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
Killing form. The latter can be normalized as

\eta_{ab}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c@{\quad}c@{\quad}c}0&1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right),

(9)

and coincides with the Minkowski metric in 2+1 dimensions in light-cone coordinates. Indices will be raised and lowered with this metric and its inverse, which has the same form. Expanding the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
elements in this basis

x=x^{a}t_{a},\qquad a=a^{a}t_{a},\qquad\tilde{a}=\tilde{a}^{a}t_{a},

(10)

and defining the generators

J_{a}=(t_{a},0,0),\qquad P_{a}=(0,t_{a},0),\qquad Z_{a}=(0,0,t_{a}),

(11)

the elements of the
\mathfrak{maxwell}_{3}
algebra can be written in the familiar form

X=(x,a,\tilde{a})=x^{a}J_{a}+a^{a}P_{a}+\tilde{a}^{a}Z_{a}.

(12)

Evaluating the bracket (
7
) for the generators
J_{a}
,
P_{a}
, and
Z_{a}
leads to the Maxwell
3
commutation relations
Cangemi:1992ri

[J_{a},J_{b}]=\epsilon^{c}{}_{ab}J_{c},\qquad[J_{a},P_{b}]=\epsilon^{c}{}_{ab}P_{c},\qquad[P_{a},P_{b}]=\epsilon^{c}{}_{ab}Z_{c}=[J_{a},Z_{b}].

(13)

By introducing the dual generators
J_{ab}=\epsilon_{abc}J^{c}
and
Z_{ab}=\epsilon_{abc}Z^{c}
, the commutation relations (
13
) can be written in the standard form
Schrader:1972zd
, which holds in four and higher dimensions. These include the non-vanishing Poincaré commutation relations, the standard transformation of the antisymmetric tensor
Z_{ab}
under Lorentz transformations, and the commutation relation (
1
) characteristic of the Maxwell algebra. The generalization of the construction (
4
) to higher dimensions can be found in
Schrader:1972zd
;
Salgado-Rebolledo:2019kft
.

The non-degenerate invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{maxwell}_{3}
is given by

\displaystyle\left\langle(x,a,\tilde{a})\;,\;(y,b,\tilde{b})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}{\rm Tr}(x,y)+\kappa_{2}\Big({\rm Tr}(x,b)+{\rm Tr}(y,a)\Big)

(14)

\displaystyle+\kappa_{3}\Big({\rm Tr}(x,\tilde{b})+{\rm Tr}(y,\tilde{a})+{\rm Tr}(a,b)\Big).

Using the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
Killing form (
8
), this invariant form can be expressed in the basis (
11
) as

\left\langle J_{a}J_{b}\right\rangle=\kappa_{1}\eta_{ab},\qquad\left\langle J_{a}P_{b}\right\rangle=\kappa_{2}\eta_{ab},\qquad\left\langle P_{a}P_{b}\right\rangle=\kappa_{3}\eta_{ab}=\left\langle J_{a}Z_{b}\right\rangle.

(15)

Notice that if one eliminates the third slot in the group and algebra elements featuring in Eqs. (
4
), (
7
), and (
11
), one recovers the semidirect product structure defining the Poincaré group and the Poincaré algebra in 2+1 dimensions
Barnich:2014kra
. This effectively sets
Z_{a}=0=\kappa_{3}
in the relations (
13
) and (
15
), yielding the
\mathfrak{iso}(2,1)
commutation relations together with its associated invariant bilinear form
Witten:1988hc
.

Using the product law (
4
), one can compute the Maurer–Cartan forms on the Maxwell
3
group. Given a group element
G(t)
defining a curve in the group manifold with parameter
t
, and its inverse

G(t)=\big(\Lambda(t),a(t),\tilde{a}(t)\big),\qquad G^{-1}(t)=\Big(\Lambda^{-1}(t),-\Lambda(t)a(t)\Lambda^{-1}(t),-\Lambda(t)\tilde{a}(t)\Lambda^{-1}(t)\Big),

(16)

the left-invariant and right-invariant Maurer–Cartan forms are obtained by introducing another curve with parameter
\hat{t}
and computing the differential of the left and right multiplication by
G^{-1}
:

\displaystyle\Xi_{L}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\hat{t}}\Big(G^{-1}(t)G(\hat{t}\,)\Big)\bigg|_{\hat{t}=t}{\rm d}t,

(17)

\displaystyle\Xi_{R}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d\hat{t}}\Big(G(\hat{t}\,)G^{-1}(t)\Big)\bigg|_{\hat{t}=t}{\rm d}t.

Explicitly, this gives

\displaystyle\Xi_{L}=G^{-1}{\rm d}G

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda^{-1}{\rm d}\Lambda,\;\Lambda^{-1}{\rm d}a\Lambda,\;\Lambda^{-1}{\rm d}\tilde{a}\Lambda-\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{-1}[a,{\rm d}a]\Lambda\right),

(18a)

\displaystyle\Xi_{R}={\rm d}GG^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left({\rm d}\Lambda\Lambda^{-1},\;D^{-{\rm d}\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}}a,\;D^{-{\rm d}\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}}\tilde{a}+\frac{1}{2}\left[a,\;D^{-{\rm d}\Lambda\Lambda^{-1}}a\right]\right),

(18b)

where we have defined

D^{x}a={\rm d}a+[x,a].

(19)

Notice that, as a slight abuse of language, we have omitted the fact that the expressions in Eq. (
17
) represent the pullback of the Maurer–Cartan forms to the curve, while in (
18
) we have bluntly replaced
\frac{dG}{dt}dt
by
{\rm d}G
in order to recover the Maurer–Cartan forms on the full group.

2.2
Geometric action

An element that will be useful in our analysis of the boundary dynamics of Chern–Simons gravity is the geometric action associated with a coadjoint orbit of a Lie group. Here, we briefly review the main properties of such systems. For a detailed description, see
Alekseev:1988ce
;
Aratyn:1990dj
and references therein. Applications to three-dimensional gravity can be found in Refs.
Barnich:2017jgw
;
Henneaux:2019sjx
;
Merbis:2019wgk
;
Merbis:2023uax
and, in particular, related to the Maxwell group, in
Salgado-Rebolledo:2019kft
.

Geometric actions define dynamical systems whose symplectic structure is the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau symplectic form on coadjoint orbits of a Lie group. In order to define them, we need to consider some Lie algebra with elements
X
, its dual space with elements denoted by
S
, and a pairing between them, namely the space of linear maps determined by the elements
S

\langle\!\langle S\,,\,X\rangle\!\rangle\in\mathbb{R}.

(20)

Given a pairing, the adjoint action of the Lie group on its Lie algebra

{\rm Ad}_{G}X=\frac{d}{dt}\Big(G\,H(t)\,G^{-1}\Big)\bigg|_{t=0},\qquad X=\frac{d}{dt}H(t)\bigg|_{t=0},

(21)

induces an action on the Lie algebra dual, called the coadjoint representation of the group and denoted by
{\rm Ad}^{*}_{G}S
, such that the pairing is invariant under the combined action

\langle\!\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{G}S\,,\,{\rm Ad}_{G}X\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle S\,,\,X\rangle\!\rangle.

(22)

Similarly, the infinitesimal version of this expression connects the adjoint action of the corresponding Lie algebra (
6
) with its coadjoint action

\langle\!\langle{\rm ad}^{*}_{X}S\,,\,Y\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle S\,,\,{\rm ad}_{X}Y\rangle\!\rangle=0.

(23)

Now we consider the case of a Maxwell-like group with elements
G=(\Lambda,a,\tilde{a})
and product law

(\Lambda,a,\tilde{a})(\Gamma,b,\tilde{b})=\left(\Lambda\Gamma\,,\;a+{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}b\,,\;\tilde{a}+{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}\tilde{b}+\frac{1}{2}{\rm ad}_{a}{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}b\right).

(24)

This is an extended semidirect product based on the adjoint representation of a Lie group on its Lie algebra, and reproduces Maxwell
3
when that Lie group is chosen to be
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
. While our focus for now is the Maxwell group, this construction naturally extends to more general Maxwell-like matrix groups, for which

{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(\,)\Lambda^{-1}={\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda},\qquad{\rm ad}_{x}=[x,\,]={\rm ad}^{*}_{x},

(25)

and can also be generalized to infinite-dimensional extensions. Indeed, an extended semidirect product of the form (
24
) based on the Virasoro algebra will be considered in Section
4.2
.

The Lie algebra associated to the extended semidirect product has elements of the form (
5
),
Y=(y,b,\tilde{b})
, whereas dual Lie algebra elements will be denoted by
S=(s,p,\tilde{p})
. The pairing (
20
) is thus decomposed as

\langle\!\langle S,Y\rangle\!\rangle=\langle\!\langle s,y\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle p,a\rangle\!\rangle+\langle\!\langle\tilde{p},\tilde{a}\rangle\!\rangle,

(26)

where in this case
\langle\!\langle s,y\rangle\!\rangle
is the pairing between the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
algebra and its dual, and matches the Killing form (
8
). The adjoint and the coadjoint representations then take the form

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}_{G}Y=\bigg({\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}y,{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}b-{\rm ad}_{{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}y}a,\,{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}\tilde{b}-{\rm ad}_{{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}y}\tilde{a}+{\rm ad}_{a}\Big({\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}b-\frac{1}{2}{\rm ad}_{{\rm Ad}_{\Lambda}y}a\Big)\bigg),

(27a)

\displaystyle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{G^{-1}}S=\bigg({\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Big(s-{\rm ad}^{*}_{a}p-{\rm ad}^{*}_{\tilde{a}}\tilde{p}+\frac{1}{2}({\rm ad}^{*}_{a})^{2}\tilde{p}\Big),\,{\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda^{-1}}(p-{\rm ad}^{*}_{a}\tilde{p}),\,{\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\tilde{p}\bigg),

(27b)

The coadjoint action on a fixed dual space element
S_{\rm o}
generates a coadjoint orbit with elements
S={\rm Ad}^{*}_{G}S_{\rm o}
, given by the quotient of the Lie group and the isotropy group of
S_{\rm o}
. Such a manifold is endowed with the Kirillov–Kostant–Souriau symplectic form, which is the pullback to the orbit of the pre-symplectic structure

\Theta=\langle\!\langle S_{\rm o}\,,\,\Xi_{L}\wedge\Xi_{L}\ \rangle\!\rangle=-{\rm d}\langle\!\langle S_{\rm o}\,,\,\Xi_{L}\ \rangle\!\rangle.

(28)

The geometric action on the orbit can be constructed out of the potential associated to
\Theta
as

I_{\rm GA}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\langle\!\langle S_{\rm o}\,,\,\Xi_{L}\ \rangle\!\rangle,

(29)

where the integration is over a path
\mathcal{C}
on the orbit, and it is understood that the integrand is pulled back to the curve. Explicitly this gives

I^{{\rm Maxwell}_{3}}_{\rm GA}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\big\langle\!\big\langle s_{\rm o},\,\Lambda^{-1}{\rm d}\Lambda\big\rangle\!\big\rangle+\big\langle\!\big\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda}p_{\rm o},\,{\rm d}a\big\rangle\!\big\rangle+\Big\langle\!\Big\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda}\tilde{p}_{\rm o},\,\left({\rm d}\tilde{a}-\frac{1}{2}{\rm ad}_{a}{\rm d}a\right)\Big\rangle\!\Big\rangle\right).

(30)

Hence, using the left-invariant Maurer–Cartan form for the Maxwell group (
18
) and (
25
), the geometric action takes the form

I^{{\rm Maxwell}_{3}}_{\rm GA}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}d\tau\,{\rm Tr}\left[s_{\rm o}\,\Lambda^{-1}\frac{d\Lambda}{d\tau}+\Lambda\,p_{\rm o}\,\Lambda^{-1}\frac{da}{d\tau}+\Lambda\,\tilde{p}_{\rm o}\,\Lambda^{-1}\left(\frac{d\tilde{a}}{d\tau}-\frac{1}{2}\Big[a,\frac{da}{d\tau}\Big]\right)\right],

(31)

where
\tau
parametrizes
\mathcal{C}
. This action describes a spinning particle in
2+1
dimensions
deSousaGerbert:1990yp
, with coordinates
a^{a}
, momentum
\pi_{a}=(\Lambda\,p_{\rm o}\,\Lambda^{-1})_{a}
, spin
s_{a}={\rm Tr}(s_{\rm o}\,\Lambda t_{a}\Lambda^{-1})
, in the presence of a constant electromagnetic field with field strength
F_{ab}=\epsilon^{c}{}_{ab}(\Lambda\,\tilde{p}_{\rm o}\,\Lambda^{-1})_{c}
. The “extra coordinates” play the role of Lagrange multipliers enforcing the constraint
\frac{d}{d\tau}F_{ab}=0

Bonanos:2008ez
;
Gibbons:2009me
.

For our purposes, it will prove useful to rewrite by replacing

G\rightarrow G^{-1},\qquad S_{\rm o}\rightarrow-S_{\rm o},

(32)

in the definition (
29
), which in particular implies
\Xi_{L}\rightarrow-\Xi_{R}=-{\rm Ad}_{G}\,\Xi_{L}
. Using (
22
), this leads to

I_{\rm GA}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\langle\!\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{G^{-1}}S_{\rm o}\,,\,\Xi_{L}\ \rangle\!\rangle.

(33)

Using (
22
), and discarding total derivatives, this leads to

I^{{\rm Maxwell}_{3}}_{\rm GA}=\int_{\mathcal{C}}\Big\langle\!\Big\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{\Lambda^{-1}}\Big(s_{\rm o}-{\rm ad}^{*}_{a}p_{\rm o}-{\rm ad}^{*}_{\tilde{a}}\tilde{p}_{\rm o}+\frac{1}{2}({\rm ad}^{*}_{a})^{2}\tilde{p}_{\rm o}\Big),\Lambda^{-1}{\rm d}\Lambda\Big\rangle\!\Big\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\Big\langle\!\Big\langle{\rm ad}^{*}_{a}\tilde{p}_{\rm o},{\rm d}a\Big\rangle\!\Big\rangle.

(34)

One can show, using (
25
) and (
32
), together with the explicit form of the inverse Maxwell group element (
16
), that the action (
34
) is equivalent to (
31
). However, in Section
4.4
, this form of the action will prove convenient when considering infinite-dimensional generalizations. Additionally, it will be useful to consider the Noether charges associated to the global symmetries of (
33
), which are given by

Q_{X}=-\left\langle\!\langle{\rm Ad}^{*}_{G^{-1}}S_{{\rm o}},\,X\right\rangle\!\rangle,

(35)

with
X
a parameter associated to an infinitesimal global transformation defined by the right action of the group
G\rightarrow G(I+X)
, and
\delta Q_{X}=\iota_{\mathfrak{v}_{X}}\Theta
, where
\mathfrak{v}_{X}
is the vector field associated to the right translations that reduces to
X
at the identity
Barnich:2017jgw
. The charges satisfy a Poisson algebra isomorphic to the Lie algebra of global symmetry transformations

\delta_{X}Q_{Y}=\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{v}_{X}}Q_{Y}=\{Q_{X},Q_{Y}\}=Q_{{\rm ad}_{X}Y},

(36)

where
\mathfrak{L}
is the Lie derivative. Moreover, the geometric action is invariant under gauge transformations given by left multiplication with elements belonging to the isotropy group of the orbit representative.

3
Maxwell
3
-invariant Chern-Simons gravity

In this section, we review the main features of three-dimensional Chern-Simons gravity invariant under the
\mathfrak{maxwell}_{3}
algebra
Cangemi:1992ri
. We consider the boundary conditions introduced in Ref.
Concha:2018zeb
as an extension of the asymptotically flat construction given in
Barnich:2013yka
(see also
Barnich:2014cwa
;
Grumiller:2017sjh
). This will prepare the ground for reducing the bulk action to the boundary in the next section.

3.1
Chern-Simons action

In three space-time dimensions, it is possible to define a Maxwell
3
-invariant gravity action as the integral of a Chern-Simons form over some three-dimensional manifold
M

I_{\rm CS}=-\int_{M}\left\langle\mathcal{A}\wedge{\rm d}\mathcal{A}+\frac{2}{3}\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{A}\wedge\mathcal{A}\right\rangle,

(37)

where
\mathcal{A}
is a one-form gauge connection taking values on the Maxwell
3
algebra (
13
), i.e.,

\mathcal{A}=\Omega^{a}J_{a}+E^{a}P_{a}+\Sigma^{a}Z_{a}.

(38)

The gauge fields of the theory are the spin connection
\Omega^{a}
, the dreibein
E^{a}
, and the Maxwellian field
\Sigma^{a}
. Adopting the notation (
12
), the connection can be expressed as

\mathcal{A}=(\Omega,E,\Sigma),\qquad\Omega=\Omega^{a}t_{a},\quad E=E^{a}t_{a},\quad\Sigma=\Sigma^{a}t_{a}.

(39)

The corresponding curvature is computed from the definition (
7
), which must be generalized to Lie algebra-valued
p
-forms as follows

\Big[\underbrace{(X,a,\tilde{a})}_{\text{$p$-form}}\;,\;\underbrace{(Y,b,\tilde{b})}_{\text{$q$-form}}\Big]=\left([X,Y]\;,\;[X,b]-(-1)^{pq}[Y,a]\;,\;[X,\tilde{b}]-(-1)^{pq}[Y,\tilde{a}]+[a,b]\right).

(40)

This leads to

\mathcal{F}={\rm d}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}[\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}]=(R,T,F),\qquad R=R^{a}t_{a},\quad T=T^{a}t_{a},\quad F=F^{a}t_{a},

(41)

where the Lorentz curvature
R^{a}
, the torsion
T^{a}
, and the field strength associated with the Maxwellian field
F^{a}
are respectively given by

R=d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}[\Omega,\Omega],\qquad T=dE+[\Omega,E],\qquad F=d\Sigma+[\Omega,\Sigma]+\frac{1}{2}[E,E].

(42)

Using (
14
), the explicit form of the action is

\displaystyle I^{\rm Maxwell_{3}}_{\rm CS}=-\int_{M}\bigg[\kappa_{1}\,CS(\Omega)+2\kappa_{2}\,E^{a}R_{a}+\kappa_{3}\left(2\Sigma^{a}R_{a}+E^{a}T_{a}\right)\bigg],

(43)

where
CS(\Omega)
denotes the Lorentz Chern-Simons form constructed from the spin connection

CS(\Omega)=\Omega_{a}\wedge{\rm d}\Omega^{a}+\frac{1}{3}\epsilon_{abc}\Omega^{a}\wedge\Omega^{b}\wedge\Omega^{c}.

(44)

The field equations are given by the vanishing of the curvature
\mathcal{F}
, i.e.

R^{a}=0,\qquad T^{a}=0,\qquad F^{a}=0.

(45)

Since the field equations governing the spacetime geometry are the same as in the
\mathfrak{iso}(2,1)
case
Barnich:2013yka
, in the next section we use the same solution space for the dreibein and spin connection.

3.2
Boundary conditions and asymptotic symmetry algebra

We consider a space-time
M=D\times\mathbb{R}
, where
D
plays the role of a spatial slice with disk topology and spatial coordinates
r\in[0,\infty)
,
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
, whereas
\mathbb{R}
represents time with coordinate

. Following Ref.
Banados:1998gg
, we solutions of Maxwell
3
-invariant Chern-Simons gravity (
37

\mathcal{A}=h^{-1}\alpha h+h^{-1}{\rm d}h,

(46)

where

is a Maxwell
3
group element that encodes the radial dependence of the connection. The boundary dynamics of the theory is encoded in a boundary connection
\alpha
, which depends only on the boundary coordinates

and
\phi

with

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(\phi),\qquad\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{T}(\phi)+u\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(\phi),\qquad\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{Y}(\phi)+u\mathcal{T}^{\prime}(\phi)+\frac{u^{2}}{2}\mathcal{M}^{\prime\prime}(\phi),

(48)

where the prime denotes the derivative with respect to
\phi
, and
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are arbitrary functions of
\phi
. To maintain generality, we do not fix the form of

, but an important example is

, for which the dreibein and the spin connection in (

) are given by asymptotically flat solutions of the Einstein equations in the BMS gauge

, whereas the Maxwellian field
\Sigma
becomes a solution of the equation
F^{a}=0
in (
45
) on those geometries
Concha:2018zeb
. The gauge transformations
\delta\alpha={\rm d}\varepsilon+[\alpha,\varepsilon]

\varepsilon=\varepsilon^{a}_{J}J_{a}+\varepsilon^{a}_{P}P_{a}+\varepsilon^{a}_{Z}Z_{a}

, can be expressed in terms of three functions
Y_{i}=Y_{i}(\phi)
,

, as

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{J}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{M}}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{1}-(\varepsilon_{J}^{1})^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{J}^{1}

\displaystyle=Y_{1},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{J}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\varepsilon_{J}^{1})^{\prime},

(49a)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{P}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{M}}{2}\varepsilon_{P}^{1}+\frac{\mathcal{N}}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{1}-(\varepsilon_{P}^{1})^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{P}^{1}

\displaystyle=Y_{2}+uY_{1}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{P}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\varepsilon_{P}^{1})^{\prime},

(49b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Z}^{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{M}}{2}\varepsilon_{Z}^{1}+\frac{\mathcal{N}}{2}\varepsilon_{P}^{1}+\frac{\mathcal{R}}{2}\varepsilon_{J}^{1}-(\varepsilon_{Z}^{1})^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Z}^{1}

\displaystyle=Y_{3}+uY_{2}^{\prime}+\frac{u^{2}}{2}Y_{1}^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Z}^{2}

\displaystyle=-(\varepsilon_{Z}^{1})^{\prime},

(49c)

Comparing the variation of
\alpha
defined by these gauge transformations with the variation of (
47

\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{T}
, and
\mathcal{Y}
transform as

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}Y_{1}+2\mathcal{M}Y_{1}^{\prime}-2Y_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime},

(50a)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}^{\prime}Y_{1}+2\mathcal{T}Y_{1}^{\prime}+\mathcal{M}^{\prime}Y_{2}+2\mathcal{M}Y_{2}^{\prime}-2Y_{2}^{\prime\prime\prime},

(50b)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{Y}=\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}Y_{1}+2\mathcal{Y}Y_{1}^{\prime}+\mathcal{T}^{\prime}Y_{2}+2\mathcal{T}Y_{2}^{\prime}+\mathcal{M}^{\prime}Y_{3}+2\mathcal{M}Y_{3}^{\prime}-2Y_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}.

(50c)

For an asymptotic gauge variation, the variation of the Chern-Simons charge obtained through the Regge-Teitelboim prescription
Regge:1974zd
is given by

\delta Q[\varepsilon]=2\int d\phi\,\left\langle\varepsilon\,\delta\alpha_{\phi}\right\rangle.

(51)

After substituting (

\displaystyle j[Y_{1}]

\displaystyle=\int d\phi\,\left(\kappa_{3}\mathcal{Y}+\kappa_{2}\mathcal{T}+\kappa_{1}\mathcal{M}\right)Y_{1},

(52a)

\displaystyle p[Y_{2}]

\displaystyle=\int d\phi\,\left(\kappa_{3}\mathcal{T}+\kappa_{2}\mathcal{M}\right)Y_{2},

(52b)

\displaystyle z[Y_{3}]

\displaystyle=\int d\phi\,\kappa_{3}\mathcal{M}\,Y_{3}.

(52c)

Expanding the charges in Fourier modes as

j_{m}=j[e^{im\phi}],\quad p_{m}=p[e^{im\phi}],\quad z_{m}=z[e^{im\phi}],

(53)

also referred to as the

(56)

This algebra was previously derived in a purely algebraic framework as an expansion of the Virasoro algebra

4
Boundary dynamics

In order to write the Chern-Simons action in Hamiltonian form, temporal and spatial indices are split as

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\mu}dx^{\mu}=\mathcal{A}_{u}{\rm d}u+\mathcal{A}_{r}{\rm d}r+\mathcal{A}_{\phi}{\rm d}\phi,

(57)

Considering
\epsilon^{ur\phi}\equiv-1
, the Chern-Simons action (
37
) can be written as

I_{\rm CS}=\int d^{3}x\;\left\langle 2\mathcal{A}_{u}\mathcal{F}_{r\phi}-\mathcal{A}_{r}\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{\phi}+\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{r}\right\rangle+B,

(58)

where the dot denotes the derivative with respect to
u
, and
B
is some boundary term such that the variation of
I
is well-defined. Varying the action with respect to the spatial components of the connection gives

\delta I=2\int d^{3}x\left\langle\mathcal{F}_{ur}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\phi}-\mathcal{F}_{u\phi}\delta\mathcal{A}_{r}\right\rangle+2\int dud\phi\left\langle\mathcal{A}_{u}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\right\rangle+\delta B,

(59)

where we have defined the surface element as
ds_{\mu}=dud\phi\,n_{\mu}
, with
n_{\mu}=(0,1,0)
the unit vector normal to the boundary. Therefore, the condition on
B
is

\delta B=-2\int dud\phi\left\langle\mathcal{A}_{u}\delta\mathcal{A}_{\phi}\right\rangle,

(60)

On the other hand, variation of the action with respect to
\mathcal{A}_{u}
leads to the constraint
\mathcal{F}_{ij}=0
, whose local solution is

\mathcal{A}_{i}=G^{-1}\partial_{i}G\,,

(61)

with
G
an element belonging to the gauge group. We also adopt the partial gauge condition considered in Ref.
Banados:1994tn

\partial_{\phi}\mathcal{A}_{r}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad G(u,r,\phi)=g(u,\phi)h(u,r)\,,

(62)

which leads to

\mathcal{A}_{r}=h^{-1}\partial_{r}h,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\phi}=h^{-1}\;g^{-1}g^{\prime}\;h\,.

(63)

Replacing these expressions back into the action together with the condition that
\dot{h}=0
asymptotically, leads to the chiral WZW action
Coussaert:1995zp

I_{\rm WZW}=\int dud\phi\,\left\langle g^{-1}\dot{g}\,g^{-1}g^{\prime}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{3}\int_{M}\left\langle\left(G^{-1}dG\right)^{3}\right\rangle+B.

(64)

Moreover, in many explicit examples considering a set of solutions in a particular gauge
Banados:1998gg
;
Barnich:2013yka
;
Grumiller:2016pqb
;
Grumiller:2017sjh
, including the solutions in the BMS gauge defined by (
46
), the condition
\dot{h}=0
holds not only asymptotically as in
Coussaert:1995zp
, but everywhere, which implies

\mathcal{A}_{u}=h^{-1}\alpha_{u}h.

(65)

Putting this together with (
63
) and (
46
) one finds

\alpha=\alpha_{u}{\rm d}u+g^{-1}g^{\prime}{\rm d}\phi.

(66)

Thus, the variation (
60
) can be put in the form

\delta B=-2\int dud\phi\left\langle\alpha_{u}\,\delta(g^{-1}g^{\prime})\right\rangle.

(67)

Now, let us consider elements of the Maxwell
3
group as defined in Section
2.1
. The elements
g(u,\varphi)
and
h(r)
can be expressed as triplets

g(u,\varphi)=\big(\lambda(u,\varphi),\beta(u,\varphi),\gamma(u,\varphi)\big),\qquad h(r)=\big(h_{1}(r),h_{2}(r),h_{3}(r)\big),

(68)

or equivalently, for
G=(\Lambda,a,\tilde{a})
, as

\Lambda=\lambda(u,\varphi)\,h_{1}(r),\qquad a=\beta(u,\varphi)\,h_{2}(r),\qquad\tilde{a}=\gamma(u,\varphi)\,h_{3}(r),

(69)

where
\lambda
and
h_{1}
belong to the
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
group, while
\beta
,
\gamma

h_{2}
, and
h_{3}
are elements of the
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})

\alpha
as a triad analogously to (
39

\alpha=(e,\omega,\sigma),\qquad\omega=\omega^{a}t_{a},\quad e=e^{a}t_{a},\quad\sigma=\sigma^{a}t_{a},

(70)

the boundary conditions (
47

(71)

Using the definition of the left-invariant Maurer–Cartan form given in Eq. (

(72)

4.4

In this section, we will show how the Maxwellian boundary dual theory (

3
boundary dual. This is a natural generalization of the fact that the flat Liouville model, known as the boundary dual to asymptotically flat three-dimensional gravity
Barnich:2013yka
, arises as a Carroll limit of Liouville theory, the latter being the corresponding dual in the AdS
3

Coussaert:1995zp
. In order to see this, let us first recall the flat limit of Liouville theory, whose action is given by

I_{\rm Liouville}=\frac{\kappa}{2}\int d^{2}x\left(\partial_{\alpha}\Psi\partial^{\alpha}\Psi-\mu e^{\Psi}\right),

(127)

where
\Psi
is the Liouville field and indices are raised and lowered with the two-dimensional Minkowski metric
\eta_{\alpha\beta}={\rm diag}(-1,1)=\eta^{\alpha\beta}
. We use coordinates
x^{\alpha}=(cu,\phi)
, where
c
denotes the speed of light. By redefining the constant
\kappa
as

\kappa=\frac{1}{c\nu^{2}},

(128)

I_{\rm Liouville}=\frac{1}{2}\int dud\phi\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}}\dot{\Phi}^{2}-\Phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\mu}{\nu^{2}}e^{\nu\Phi}\right).

(129)

Following
Henneaux:2021yzg

\Pi=\frac{1}{c^{2}}\dot{\Phi},

(130)

which allows us to express the action in Hamiltonian form

(131)

The magnetic Carrollian limit arises by taking
c\rightarrow 0
directly in the Hamiltonian action, yielding the flat Liouville theory
Barnich:2012rz

I_{\rm Liouville}^{\rm MC}=\frac{1}{2}\int dud\phi\left(\Pi\dot{\Phi}-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime 2}-\frac{\mu}{2\nu^{2}}e^{\nu\Phi}\right).

(132)

The electric Carrollian limit, on the other hand, follows from the rescaling

\Pi\rightarrow c^{-1}\Pi,\quad\Phi\rightarrow c\Phi,\qquad\nu\rightarrow c^{-1}\nu,\qquad\mu\rightarrow c^{2}\mu,

(133)

which leads to the action

I_{\rm Liouville}^{\rm EC}=\int dud\phi\left(\Pi\dot{\Phi}-\Pi^{2}-\frac{\mu}{2\nu^{2}}e^{\nu\Phi}\right).

(134)

The electric limit corresponds to a different flat limit of Liouville theory, also considered in Ref.
Barnich:2012rz
.

The equivalence between the magnetic Carrollian limit of Liouville theory and the flat Liouville theory emerging from asymptotically flat three-dimensional gravity
Barnich:2013yka
can be established through the field redefinition

\displaystyle\nu\Phi

\displaystyle=2\varphi-2\,{\rm ln}\sigma+{\rm ln}\frac{4}{\mu},

(135)

\displaystyle\nu\Pi

\displaystyle=\xi^{\prime}-({\rm ln}\sigma)^{\prime}\xi,\qquad\sigma^{\prime}=e^{\varphi},

which reproduces the
\kappa_{2}
-term in (
97
) with
\nu^{2}=1/\kappa_{2}
. However, the
\kappa_{1}
-term cannot be recovered by considering purely Einstein gravity in 2+1 dimensions, since it originates from the so-called exotic gravitational term
Witten:1988hc
. Starting instead from an AdS
3
-invariant Chern-Simons theory with the most general non-degenerate bilinear form, the action can be written in terms of two
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
Chern-Simons terms

I[\mathcal{A}_{\pm}]=\int d^{3}x\,{\rm Tr}\left[\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\wedge{\rm d}\mathcal{A}_{\pm}+\frac{2}{3}\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{\pm}\right]

(136)

as

I_{\rm AdS}=(\mu_{J}+\mu_{P})I[\mathcal{A}_{+}]+(\mu_{J}-\mu_{P})I[\mathcal{A}_{-}].

(137)

Einstein gravity is recovered when setting
\mu_{J}=0
, whereas the exotic term corresponds to
\mu_{P}=0
. After imposing the Hamiltonian Chern-Simons constraint and applying the Drinfeld-Sokolov reduction associated with Brown-Henneaux boundary conditions, the boundary theory reduces to a sum of two chiral bosons

I_{\rm AdS}=\frac{\mu_{J}+\mu_{P}}{2}\int c\,dud\phi\;\left(\frac{1}{c}\dot{\zeta}_{+}\zeta_{+}^{\prime}-\zeta_{+}^{\prime 2}\right)+\frac{\mu_{J}-\mu_{P}}{2}\int c\,dud\phi\;\left(\frac{1}{c}\dot{\zeta}_{-}\zeta_{-}^{\prime}+\zeta_{-}^{\prime 2}\right).

(138)

For
\mu_{J}=0
, the standard Einstein gravity result is recovered
Coussaert:1995zp
, which can be identified with Liouville theory after the field redefinitions
Gervais:1982nw
;
Henneaux:1999ib

\displaystyle\nu\Psi

\displaystyle=\zeta_{+}+\zeta_{-}-2\text{ln}(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})+{\rm ln}\frac{16}{\mu},

(139)

\displaystyle c\nu\Pi

\displaystyle=\zeta_{+}^{\prime}-\zeta_{+}^{\prime}-2\frac{\sigma_{+}^{\prime}-\sigma_{-}^{\prime}}{\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-}},\qquad\sigma_{\pm}^{\prime}=e^{\zeta_{\pm}}.

One can see that a Carrollian limit connecting (
139
) with the “flat” redefinition (
135
) is achieved by setting
\zeta_{\pm}=\varphi\pm\frac{c}{2}\xi
and taking
c\rightarrow 0
. This motivates the following Carrollian expansion for the AdS
3
boundary dual field theory

\zeta_{\pm}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(\pm c)^{m}\zeta_{m}.

(140)

Substituting this into the action (
138
), we obtain up to total derivatives

\displaystyle I_{\rm AdS}

\displaystyle=\int dud\phi\Bigg(\mu_{J}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c^{2m}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{2m}\dot{\zeta}_{n}\zeta_{2m-n}^{\prime}-\sum_{n=0}^{2m-1}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}\zeta_{2m-n-1}^{\prime}\right)

(141)

\displaystyle+\mu_{P}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}c^{2m+1}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{2m+1}\dot{\zeta}_{n}\zeta_{2m-n+1}^{\prime}-\sum_{n=0}^{2m}\zeta_{n}^{\prime}\zeta_{2m-n}^{\prime}\right)\Bigg),

and considering this Carrollian expansion up to order
c^{2}
we find

I_{\rm AdS}=\int dud\phi\,\left(\mu_{J}\,\zeta_{0}^{\prime}\dot{\zeta_{0}}+c\,\mu_{P}\,\big(2\zeta_{1}^{\prime}\dot{\zeta_{0}}-\zeta_{0}^{\prime}\zeta_{0}^{\prime}\big)+c^{2}\mu_{J}\,\Big(2\zeta_{2}^{\prime}\dot{\zeta_{0}}+\dot{\zeta_{1}}\zeta_{1}^{\prime}-2\zeta_{1}^{\prime}\zeta_{0}^{\prime}\Big)+O(c^{3})\right).

(142)

Thus, we recover the Maxwellian boundary dual model (
97
) with the following identifications

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}=\mu_{J},

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}=c\,\mu_{P},

\displaystyle\kappa_{3}=c^{2}\mu_{J}

(143)

\displaystyle\varphi=\zeta_{0},

\displaystyle\xi=2\zeta_{1},

\displaystyle\chi=2\zeta_{2}.

Notice that the extension of the flat Liouville theory given in Refs.
Barnich:2015sca
;
Barnich:2017jgw
, which also follows from the action (
97
) for
\kappa_{2}=0
, can be obtained as the Carroll limit
c\rightarrow 0
of (
141
) after rescaling
\mu_{P}\rightarrow\mu_{P}/c
.

5
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have analyzed the boundary dynamics of Maxwell
3
-invariant Chern-Simons gravity in 2+1 dimensions using standard Hamiltonian reduction techniques. Solving the constraints in the gravitational action leads to a chiral WZW model invariant under the loop extension of the Maxwell group, with conserved charges realizing the corresponding Kac-Moody symmetry. By imposing boundary conditions that naturally generalize those associated with asymptotically flat spacetimes in the BMS gauge
Concha:2018zeb
, the WZW model is further reduced to a Maxwellian extension of the flat Liouville theory of Barnich, Gomberoff, and González
Barnich:2012rz
.

Alternatively, we have shown that the boundary dual to Maxwell
3
-invariant Chern-Simons gravity is equivalent to the geometric action on coadjoint orbits of the Maxwell-BMS
3
group, for a specific class of orbit representatives and upon inclusion of a suitable Hamiltonian. This Hamiltonian preserves the symmetries of the original action, provided the global symmetry parameters acquire a specific fixed dependence on the temporal coordinate. The conserved charges of the model, computed via the coadjoint orbit method, naturally realize the
\mathfrak{maxwell}{\textnormal{-}}\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra.

By identifying the flat Liouville theory known from asymptotically flat three-dimensional gravity as an electric Carroll limit, we demonstrated that the Maxwellian boundary dual presented here emerges from a Carrollian expansion of the dual field theory associated with AdS
3
Chern-Simons gravity. This indicates that the contributions introduced by the Maxwell extension can be interpreted as a post-Carrollian correction.

A natural question arising from these results is whether such an expansion can be extended to higher orders in
c
at the boundary, and what the corresponding gravitational dual would be in that case. Another interesting direction is the generalization to four dimensions, which could relate Maxwell gravity
deAzcarraga:2010sw
to a Carrollian expansion of a relativistic CFT. We expect to explore these questions in future work.

A compelling generalization of our results involves the higher-spin extension of the Maxwell-BMS
3
symmetry, namely a Maxwell-like
\mathcal{W}_{N}
algebra. Such symmetry was first introduced in
Salgado-Rebolledo:2019kft
and later explicitly constructed in
Concha:2024rac
as the asymptotic symmetry associated with the Maxwell extension of a Chern-Simons higher-spin gravity theory
Caroca:2017izc
. Following the methodology developed in this work, the Maxwellian boundary theory of
Concha:2024rac
could be extended to the higher-spin case, leading to a Maxwellian extension of the flat Toda theory
Gonzalez:2014tba
, which arises as the boundary dual of asymptotically flat higher-spin Chern-Simons gravity
Afshar:2013vka
;
Gonzalez:2013oaa
. Since flat
\mathcal{W}_{N}
algebras arise from ultra-relativistic limits of
\mathcal{W}_{N}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{N}
algebras
Campoleoni:2016vsh
, the resulting Maxwellian extension is likely related to a Carrollian expansion of Toda theory.

Finally, another interesting Maxwell-like symmetry studied in the literature is the so-called AdS-Lorentz algebra
Soroka:2004fj
;
Gomis:2009dm
, which defines a semisimple extension of the Poincaré symmetry, and extends the Maxwell algebra by incorporating a cosmological constant term. In
Concha:2018jjj
, it was shown that the asymptotic symmetry of a Chern-Simons gravity theory with AdS-Lorentz gauge symmetry corresponds to three copies of the Virasoro algebra. In the vanishing cosmological constant limit, this algebra reduces to the
\mathfrak{maxwell}{\textnormal{-}}\mathfrak{bms}_{3}
algebra (
55
). This suggests that such a Chern-Simons theory admits a boundary dual described by three chiral boson actions, whose flat limit reproduces the Maxwellian boundary dual considered here.

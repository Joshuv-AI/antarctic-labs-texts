Analysis of the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy
Authors: Xiang Tao, Shuai Zhang, Hao He, Junjie Xiong, Wei He, Wei Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Grid-forming type control has become an important control mode of renewable energy inverters in practice, since it makes renewable energy could provide the dynamic frequency support for power system like conventional synchronous generator. However, experiments shows that its dynamic frequency support response characteristics are contrary to the original design intention of grid-forming type control. For example, the inertia output doesn’t increase to the maximum value as expected at the moment of disturbance. Instead, it follows a trend of initially increasing and then decreasing. This paper conducts the relative analyse research. It reveals the reason causing the above problem is the grid-forming type renewable energy does not sample the grid voltage frequency in real time for closed-loop control, but instead by constructing an intrinsic equivalent frequency. Furthermore, it constructs one frequency support response model taking into account the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling, and systematically explains the impact mechanism of non-real-time frequency sampling on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy, and the impact mechanism of damping coefficient on the primary frequency regulation response of grid-forming type renewable energy. Finally, the correctness of the analysis conclusions is verified through simulation.

Introduction

In recent years, countries around the world have made major breakthroughs in green and low-carbon energy development. The installed capacity of wind power and photovoltaic (PV) has continuously risen. In some regions, over 50% of their power capacity comes from the renewable energy sources. The traditional power grid, which has mainly relied on synchronous generators, is gradually transitioning to a new power system dominated by renewable energy sources1,2. On the other hand, the safe and stable operation of the system is facing serious challenges. Major incidents such as the 2017 Australian blackout, the 2019 UK blackout, and the 2021 European power grid blackout are all closely related to the frequency instability, with significant economic and social impacts3.

The underlying cause is the large-scale integration of renewable energy sources, which is replacing synchronous generators in equal measure. However, most renewable energy sources have zero inertia and weak frequency support capabilities, which directly leads to a sharp decline in the power grid’s ability to resist frequency disturbances. As a result, the power grid faces major risk of frequency instability. Developing the active frequency support technology of renewable energy, and increasing the types of power sources that can participate in frequency regulation, has become an inevitable choice to ensure the safe operation of the new power system.

Against this backdrop, grid-forming type renewable energy have emerged4,5. These systems use Virtual Synchronous Generator (VSG) control as their core technology to simulate the characteristics of synchronous generators, thereby providing grid-support capabilities to renewable energy sources (Fig. 1). To achieve this function, a systematic design approach is typically required, focusing on both energy supply and regulation quality. Existing research has gradually explored this area, as summarized below: Firstly, in terms of energy supply for inertia output, literature6 utilizes “renewable energy + electrochemical energy storage system” combined control. This approach offers large inertia support and a long duration of frequency regulation power output, making it the mainstream VSG energy supply model, but with high investment costs. Literature7–9 proposes an active power backup style VSG control strategy, where renewable energy operates at a derated capacity, where the reserve active power is used to ensure inertia output and primary frequency regulation. But this leads to low utilization of photovoltaic/wind energy. Literatures10–12 introduce a new method by adding supercapacitors to the DC side of renewable energy power generation units. This is another way of adding storage on the renewable energy side, but it also increases the cost of supercapacitors and bidirectional DC/DC converters. In terms of regulation quality, traditional VSG control uses fixed values for virtual inertia and damping parameters, which makes it difficult to adapt to the changing grid environment, leading to issues such as overshooting in active power regulation and excessive frequency deviation13. So, many scholars improves the VSG control strategy. Literature14 divides the entire regulation process into four stages based on the system’s angular velocity and its rate of change after a disturbance, and provides tuning principles for virtual inertia and damping coefficients, effectively reducing frequency overshoot and regulation time. Literature15 proposes a combined adaptive virtual inertia and adaptive damping control of VSG in order to improve the dynamic frequency response. Literature16 uses an improved model predictive control, combining adaptive inertia damping control and adaptive weight coefficient control for joint control to enhance frequency support response capability. In literature17, an improved Reinforcement Learning (RL) based approach for adaptive VSG control is given to improve frequency transients metrics such as frequency nadir and rate of change of frequency. For reducing the transient energy loss without sacrificing grid frequency support effect, literature18 proposed an adaptive-droop-coefficient VSG control. Further to improve the frequency regulation performance considering the nonlinear dynamics, an optimal frequency control approach is proposed in19.

Although great progress has been made in the construction of renewable energy and energy storage grid-connected control, there are still some phenomena that have not been effectively explained. For example, a large number of experiments have shown that the inertia response of grid-forming type renewable energy gradually increases to the maximum value after responding for a period of time. However, according to the design of VSG, the inertia response is a function of the derivative of system frequency. Theoretically, the inertia output of the grid-forming type renewable energy should increase to the maximum value as expected at the moment of disturbance. This is contrary to the original design intention of VSG. Although existing literatures have modeled and analyzed the small-signal stability and front-end/back-end coordination stability of VSGs from multiple perspectives, including single-machine and multi-machine configurations20–23, the reason why these points have not been effectively explained is that existing research has not thoroughly analyzed the interaction influence between the power grid and the grid-forming type renewable energy during the frequency regulation process. Particularly, it overlooks the fact that the grid-forming type renewable energy do not sample the grid’s voltage and frequency in real-time, but instead use the constructed equivalent frequency for control loops. This factor significantly impacts their inertia response and the primary frequency regulation response. Therefore, the conclusions drawn from existing modelling and dynamic frequency regulation behaviour analysis are not rigorous. To address this, this paper conducts the related research. The main work is as follows. Section Basic principles of grid-forming type renewable energy control and limitations of the frequency support capabilities discovered during experiments introduces the basic principles of grid-forming type renewable energy control and points out the limitations of the frequency support capabilities discovered during experiments. Section Analysis of the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy, analyses the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy through building new analytical model. Section Simulation and experimental analysis presents simulation experiments to verify the correctness of the analysis conclusions.

A novel analyse model for analysing frequency support characteristics of grid-forming type renewable energy control

To address the aforementioned issues, this section attempts to construct a new frequency response model for the grid-forming type renewable energy, which includes the coupling relationship between the grid frequency and the constructed equivalent frequency. This would lay the foundation for analysing the system’s frequency dynamic response mechanism and dynamic behaviour in Section Analysis of the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy.

Synchronization with power grid voltage is a critical aspect of renewable energy inverter control. Grid-forming type renewable energy employ a different synchronization method than conventional grid-following renewable energy control. The former achieves grid voltage matching through power synchronization, while the latter employs phase-locked loops (a method for capturing the phase and frequency of power grid voltage) to match grid voltage. In detail, the power synchronization of grid-forming type renewable energy is shown in Fig. 2. In principle, it samples power grid voltage and current, then calculates instantaneous power using angle information from the power loop estimation and rotary coordinate transformation. Through closed-loop control between reference power and calculated instantaneous power, it achieves synchronization with power grid voltage. Under this architecture, stable system control is only possible when the calculated instantaneous power accurately tracks the reference power. Since the calculation of instantaneous power is directly linked to the power angle (frequency information), this implies that when the system achieves stability, grid-forming renewable energy sources also achieve synchronous tracking of grid voltage. Otherwise, continuous oscillations will persist. This is a method for indirectly sensing power grid frequency information.

Analysis of the impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy

From Fig. 4, it is evident that the power grid angular frequency disturbance value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} would have an important impact on the system’s frequency dynamic process in two aspects during the participation of grid-forming type renewable energy/storage in system frequency regulation. Impact Mechanism ➀. The power grid angular frequency disturbance value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} will automatically generate a power feedforward branch (defined as influence loop ➀ in this paper, as marked in Fig. 6), with a gain coefficient of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$D_p$$\end{document}. In other words, the power reference of grid-forming type renewable energy can be equivalently expressed as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$P'_{ref}$$\end{document}, i.e.,14\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \frac{{{{P'}_{ref}}}}{{{\omega _0}}} = \frac{{{P_{ref}}}}{{{\omega _0}}} - Therefore, the power feedforward branch automatically generated by the power grid angular frequency disturbance value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} can be seen as a droop control on the frequency of the grid-forming type renewable energy/storage voltage at PCC, with a droop coefficient of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${D_p}$$\end{document}. Impact Mechanism ➁. The system frequency disturbance \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} itself forms a frequency disturbance branch (defined as influence loop ➁ in this paper, as marked in Fig. 6), which affects the angular velocity closed loop control. It is worth noting that at the moment the power grid angular frequency suffers a disturbance, since17\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} \left\{ \begin{aligned}&D_p \Delta \omega _G = D_p \left( \omega _C - \left( \omega _G + \Delta \omega _G \right) \right) , \\&P_{ref} = \frac{3 V_{pcc} V_g}{X} \sin \left( \int \left( \omega _C - \left( \omega _G + \Delta \omega _G \right) \right) \textrm{d}t \right) \end{aligned} \right. , \end{aligned}$$\end{document}the inertia section will not immediately take effect. When the system reaches the steady state, the angular velocity closed loop in Fig. 6 will reach equilibrium, i.e.,18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} {\omega _C} = {\omega _G} + \mathrm{{d}}{\omega _G} = {\tilde{\omega }_G} . \end{aligned}$$\end{document}According to formula (4), the grid-forming type renewable energy , through VSG control, would ultimately make the internal potential output angular frequency \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\omega _C}$$\end{document} automatically follow the actual power grid frequency \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\tilde{\omega }_G}$$\end{document}, rather than always maintaining \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\omega _0$$\end{document}. This explains the third phenomenon listed in Section Limitations of the frequency support capabilities of grid-forming type renewable energy control discovered during experiments.

In summary, this section systematically answers the dynamic response mechanisms of grid-forming type renewable energy under system frequency disturbance conditions, explaining the three questions listed in Section Limitations of the frequency support capabilities of grid-forming type renewable energy control discovered during experiments, and compensating for the limitations in traditional grid-forming type renewable energy/storage active power regulation modelling that could not explain frequency regulation dynamics.

Additionally, it should be noted that although the power grid angular frequency disturbance value \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} generates two control branches, influencing the dynamic behaviour of grid-forming type renewable energy, according to Fig. 6, the forward channel function of the system controller remains unchanged. Conclusion

Grid-forming type control has become an important control mode of the inverters that interface renewable energy systems with the power grid. However, the significant impact of non-real-time frequency sampling and damping coefficient on their inertia response and primary frequency response has been overlooked. This has resulted in existing modelling and dynamic behaviour analysis conclusions being imprecise, leaving three major issues listed in Section Limitations of the frequency support capabilities of grid-forming type renewable energy control discovered during experiments: What drives the frequency regulation dynamics? Why does the inertia response first increase and then decrease? Why is the steady-state frequency not equal to the reference value?

This paper conducts related analyse research work. It firstly considers the influence of non-real-time frequency sampling and constructs a new frequency response model. Based on this, it systematically explains the impact mechanism of non-real-time frequency sampling on the dynamic frequency support response of grid-forming type renewable energy, and the impact mechanism of damping coefficient on the primary frequency regulation response of grid-forming type renewable energy. It is found that during the frequency regulation process, the power grid’s angular frequency disturbance \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\Delta {\omega _G}$$\end{document} would automatically generate a power feedforward branch and a frequency disturbance branch within the controller, which affect the frequency dynamic process from two aspects. The power feedforward branch would produce a droop control characteristic with a coefficient \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${D_p}$$\end{document}, which changes the active power output of the grid-forming type renewable energy system. The frequency disturbance branch, on the other hand, is the driving force behind the frequency response. It changes the system’s power angle, forcing the system to regulate the active power output. This causes the inertia response to first increase and then decrease, and the steady-state frequency to deviate from the reference value. Additionally, because the forward channel of the controller remains unchanged, the response speed and the overshoot during the frequency regulation process do not change. This paper systematically answers the three questions listed in Section Limitations of the frequency support capabilities of grid-forming type renewable energy control discovered during experiments and provides theoretical support for the development of future frequency regulation control strategies for the grid-forming type renewable energy system.

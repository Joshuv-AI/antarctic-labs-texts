Abstract
. We introduce and study a unital version of shift equivalence for finite square matrices over the nonnegative integers. In contrast to the classical case, we show that unital shift equivalence does not coincide with one-sided eventual conjugacy. We also prove that unital shift equivalent matrices define one-sided shifts of finite type that are continuously orbit equivalent. Consequently, unitally shift equivalent matrices have isomorphic topological full groups and isomorphic Leavitt path algebras, the latter being related to Hazrat’s graded classification conjecture in algebra.

1.
Introduction

Shift equivalence has played a prominent role in the still open problem of classifying shifts of finite type up to conjugacy, dating back to Williams’ seminal work
[
Wi73
]
.
Although shift equivalence does not provide a complete description of conjugacy
[
KR92
,
KR99
]
, it is an important relation for shifts of finite type that is decidable and has spawned an immense body of work, see e.g.
[
LM21
, Chapter 7]
.
It also provides deep connections to other mathematical branches such as algebra
[
Ha13
,
ART24
]
and C*-algebra theory
[
BK00
,
CDE24
]
.
Two square matrices
A
and
B
with nonnegative integer entries are
shift equivalent
if there are nonnegative integer rectangular matrices
R
and
S
and an integer
\ell\geq 1
satisfying the equations

A^{\ell}=RS,\quad B^{\ell}=SR,\quad AR=RB,\quad BS=SA.

The matrices
A
and
B
determine stationary inductive systems whose limits are dimension groups (we give details below) with a canonical order structure and
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
action,
and the matrices
R
and
S
induce isomorphisms of these dimension modules attached to
A
and
B
, respectively.
It transpires that the dimension module consisting of this dimension group together with its order structure and a canonical automorphism is a complete description of shift equivalence.

The purpose of this short note is three-fold:

(1)

First, we wish to call attention to a unital version of shift equivalence that is relevant for the study of one-sided shifts of finite type.

(2)

Second, we show by examples that unital shift equivalence does not coincide with one-sided eventual conjugacy (in contrast to the two-sided case) nor with Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy; consequently, we do not yet have a dynamical description of unital shift equivalence.

(3)

Third, we prove that unital shift equivalence implies continuous orbit equivalence for all shifts of finite type, and this should be viewed as a one-sided version of Boyle’s recent result that shift equivalence implies flow equivalence
[
Bo24
]
.

For a shift equivalence to be
unital
, we require that naturally defined order units in the dimension groups are preserved under the isomorphisms induced by
R
and
S
, see
Definition

3.1
.
From the point of view of
one-sided
shifts of finite type, this unital version is a more natural relation to study.
There are important examples of shift equivalent matrices where we do not know if they are in fact strong shift equivalent, and we show here that many of them are in fact unitally shift equivalent. This means that an analysis of the one-sided shifts of finite type could provide additional insights into these problems.

Symbolic dynamics enjoys fruitful connections with C*-algebra theory, see e.g.
[
CK80
,
ERRS21
]
, and pure algebra
[
AP05
]
,
and unital shift equivalence fits directly into one of the prominent conjectures put forth by Hazrat
[
Ha13
, Conjecture 1]
:
two matrices are unitally shift equivalent if and only if their Leavitt path algebras are graded isomorphic.
See
[
ART24
,
BDHR24
]
for recent advances on this problem.
There is an analogous C*-algebraic conjecture: two matrices are unitally shift equivalent if and only if their Cuntz–Krieger C*-algebras are *-isomorphic in a gauge-equivariant way.
A consequence for C*-algebras of our result that unital shift equivalence implies continuous orbit equivalence is that a gauge-equivariant *-isomorphism between Cuntz–Krieger algebras implies the existence of a (possibly different) diagonal-preserving *-isomorphism.
We do not know of a C*-algebraic proof of such a result.

We know from work of Williams and of Kim and Roush that matrices are shift equivalent if and only if the two-sided shifts of finite type they determine are eventually conjugate in the sense that all but finitely many higher powers are conjugate
[
LM21
, Theorem 7.5.15]
.
At the moment, we do not know of a dynamical description of unital shift equivalence.
We verify that
one-sided eventual conjugacy
(i.e. having conjugate higher powers) as studied by Boyle, Fiebig, and Fiebig
[
BFF97
]
is too strong, and we also show that Matsumoto’s notion of one-sided eventual conjugacy is too strong.
Examples show Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy does not imply having conjugate higher powers, and this reveals a discrepancy in the terminology that we clarify.
We leave the converse problem open:
does having conjugate higher powers imply Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy?
.
We emphasise that an affirmative answer to this problem will resolve the question of whether Ashley’s graph is strong shift equivalent to the matrix
(2)
.
The three competing notions (unital shift equivalence, having conjugate higher powers, and Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy) are therefore mutually distinct, and we depict them in the diagram below in which none of the arrows can be inverted

Very recently, Boyle resolved the problem of whether shift equivalence implies flow equivalence in the affirmative
[
Bo24
]
.
Seeing that continuous orbit equivalence is the one-sided analogue of flow equivalence, we were prompted to verify that our notion of unital shift equivalence indeed implies continuous orbit equivalence. We provide the details in the proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

The paper is structured as follows:
After a preliminary section in
Section

2
,
we introduce unital shift equivalence in
Section

3
which may be thought of as a one-sided analogue of shift equivalence.
In
Section

4
, we discuss the connection between unital shift equivalence and having conjugate higher powers as well as Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy. This reveals an unfortunate discrepancy in the terminology in the literature.
In

2.
Preliminaries

For a general introduction to symbolic dynamics, we refer the reader to
[
LM21
]
.
We let
\mathbb{N}
denote the nonnegative integers including zero.
An
\mathbb{N}
-matrix
A
is a matrix over
\mathbb{N}
, and if
A
is an
n\times n
matrix, we let
|A|=n
denote the size of
A
.
A square
\mathbb{N}
-matrix
A
is the adjacency matrix of a directed graph with
|A|
vertices and
A(i,j)
edges from vertex
i
to vertex
j
.
Recall,
A
is
irreducible
if for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
, there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A^{N}(i,j)>0
(equivalently, the directed graph determined by
A
is strongly connected);
in particular, the zero matrix is not irreducible.
A
sink
in a directed graph is a vertex that emits no edges (this is a zero row in the adjacency matrix),
and a
source
is a vertex that receives no edges (this is a zero column in the matrix).

Let
E
be a finite directed graph with discrete edge set
E^{1}
, vertex set
E^{0}
, and range and source maps
r
and
s
, respectively.
Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
E
.
The
one-sided edge shift
determined by
A
is the compact Hausdorff space

X_{A}\coloneq\{(x_{i})_{i}\in(E^{1})^{\mathbb{N}}:r(x_{i})=s(x_{i+1}),i\in\mathbb{N}\},

in the topology inherited from
(E^{1})^{\mathbb{N}}
, together with the shift given by
\sigma_{A}(x)_{i}=x_{i+1}
, for all
x=(x_{i})_{i}\in X_{A}
.
This is a local homeomorphism.
Given two adjacency matrices
A
and
B
, the edge shifts
(\sigma_{A},X_{A})
and
(\sigma_{B},X_{B})
are
one-sided conjugate
if there exists a homeomorphism
h\colon X_{A}\to X_{B}
such that
h\circ\sigma_{A}=\sigma_{B}\circ h
.

We recall outsplits and insplits for finite directed graphs with no sinks in terms of their adjacency matrices (see
[
LM21
, Section 2.4]
for more detailed explanations).
Strong shift equivalence is then the equivalence relation generated by outsplits and insplits (and their inverses) by Williams’ theorem
[
LM21
, Section 7]
.
A rectangular
\{0,1\}
-matrix
D
is a
division matrix
if every row contains at least one
1
and every column contains exactly one
1
.

Let
A
and
B
be a square
\mathbb{N}
-matrix with no zero rows.
We say
B
is an
outsplit
of
A
if there are a division matrix
D
and an
\mathbb{N}
-matrix
E
such that
A=DE
and
B=ED
.
If this is the case, we say
A
is an
outamalgamation
of
B
.
The process of outamalgamation may be iterated at most finitely many times before no further amalgamations can occur, and the resulting matrix is called a
total amalgamation
.
Every square
\mathbb{N}
-matrix with no zero rows admits a total amalgamation which is in fact unique up to conjugation by a permutation matrix.
Moreover,
A
and
B
determine edge shifts that are one-sided conjugate if and only if their total amalgamations agree (up to conjugation by a permutation matrix).

Similarly,
B
is an
insplit
of
A
if there are a division matrix
D
and an
\mathbb{N}
-matrix
E
such that
A=ED^{t}
and
B=D^{t}E
,
and if this is the case, we say
A
is an
inamalgamation
of
B
.
The process of insplitting does not preserve the one-sided conjugacy class of the edge shifts in general.
As in
[
Br22
]
, we say
A
and
B
are
balanced elementary strong shift equivalent
if there are a division matrix
D
and rectangular
\mathbb{N}
-matrices
R_{A}
and
R_{B}
such that

A=D^{t}R_{A},\quad B=D^{t}R_{B},\quad R_{A}D^{t}=R_{B}D^{t}.

This means that both
A
and
B
are insplits of a common matrix by the same (transposed) division matrix.
We let
balanced strong shift equivalence
be the equivalence relation generated by balanced elementary strong shift equivalence and outsplits.

3.
Unital shift equivalence

In this section we discuss shift equivalence of matrices and introduce unital shift equivalence.
We refer the reader to
[
LM21
]
for general background on symbolic dynamics, and specifically Chapter 7 where shift equivalence is discussed.
For a recent general review of shift equivalence, we refer to
[
BS24
]
.

A square
\mathbb{N}
-matrix
A
defines a linear map
\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}\to\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
where the matrix acts on column vectors.
The
dimension group
of
A
is the limit of the stationary inductive system

\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}\overset{A^{t}}{\to}\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}\overset{A^{t}}{\to}\cdots

where
A^{t}
is the transpose of
A
.
The limit is isomorphic to the group

G_{A}\coloneq(\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}\times\mathbb{N})/\sim,

where
(v,k)\sim(v^{\prime},k^{\prime})
if and only if there exists
l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(A^{t})^{l-k}v=(A^{t})^{l-k^{\prime}}v^{\prime}
.
This group carries a natural positive cone of elements
[v,k]
where
v\in\mathbb{N}^{|A|}
.
Multiplication by
A^{t}
defines an automorphism
\theta_{A}
on
G_{A}
that preserves the positive cone.
This defines a
\mathbb{Z}[x,x^{-1}]
-module structure on
G_{A}
, and we refer to the triple
(G_{A},G_{A}^{+},\theta_{A})
as the
dimension data
of
A
.
If
\underline{1}\coloneq(1,\cdots,1)^{t}
denotes the column vector consisting only of
1
’s, then
u_{A}\coloneq[\underline{1},0]
defines an order unit in
G_{A}
.
We refer to the quadruple
(G_{A},G_{A}^{+},\theta_{A},u_{A})
as the
unital dimension data
.
Given
A
and
B
, their unital dimension data are said to be isomorphic if there is a group isomorphism
\Theta\colon G_{A}\to G_{B}
that preserves the positive cone, intertwines the canonical automorphisms
\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{B}
,
and satisfies
\Theta[\underline{1},0]_{A}=[\underline{1},0]_{B}
.

A pair of square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices
A
and
B
are
shift equivalent
if there are
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
(the lag) and rectangular
\mathbb{N}
-matrices
R
and
S
satisfying

A^{\ell}=RS,\quad B^{\ell}=SR,\quad AR=RB,\quad BS=SA.

A shift equivalence
(R,S)
defines an isomorphism of the dimension groups
\hat{R}\colon G_{A}\to G_{B}
given by

(3.1)

\hat{R}([v,k])=[R^{t}v,k],

for all
[v,k]\in G_{A}
,
and this preserves the positive cone and it intertwines the automorphisms
\theta_{A}
and
\theta_{B}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices.
We say a shift equivalence
(R,S)
between
A
and
B
is
unital
if the induced map
\hat{R}\colon G_{A}\to G_{B}
from (
3.1
) preserves the unit
in the sense that
\hat{R}(u_{A})=\hat{B}^{k}u_{B}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, equivalently,
(B^{t})^{m}R^{t}\underline{1}=(B^{t})^{m+k}\underline{1}
for some
m,k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say
A
and
B
are
unitally shift equivalent
, if there exists a unital shift equivalence from
A
to
B
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices. Then,
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent if and only if their unital dimension data are isomorphic.

Proof.

The only nontrivial statement is if the unital dimension data are isomorphic, then
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent.
Suppose
\Theta\colon G_{A}\to G_{B}
is an isomorphism of the unital dimension data.
By Krieger’s Theorem
[
Kr80
]
(see also
[
Ef79
, Theorem 6.4]
), there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
and a shift equivalence
(R,S)
between
A
and
B
such that
\hat{B}^{k}\circ\Theta=\hat{R}
.
But then
\hat{R}_{A}u_{A}=\hat{B}^{k}u_{B}
, so
(R,S)
is a unital shift equivalence.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

It is wellknown that for primitive matrices, shift equivalence is characterised by the dimension group and its canonical automorphism (not including the positive cone)
[
LM21
, Corollary 7.5.9]
; similarly, unital shift equivalence is characterised by the unital dimension group with its canonical automorphism for primitive matrices.

Since one-sided conjugacy of edge shifts is generated by outsplits and outamalgamations of the adjacency matrices, the observation below shows that one-sided conjugate edge shifts have unitally shift equivalent matrices.
The converse is not the case and we give (several) examples of this.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be a square
\mathbb{N}
-matrix with no zero rows, and suppose
B
is an outsplit of
A
.
Then,
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent.

Proof.

If
B
is the result of an outsplit of
A
, then there are rectangular
\{0,1\}
-matrices
D
and
E
satisfying
A=DE
and
B=ED
, see e.g.
[
LM21
, Theorem 2.4.12]
.
The pair
(D,E)
is a shift equivalence, and
D
has the property that every row of
D^{t}
contains exactly one
1
.
Therefore,
D^{t}\underline{1}=\underline{1}
from which we see that
(D,E)
is a unital shift equivalence.
∎

The example below shows that insplits need not preserve the unital shift equivalence class of a matrix.
In particular, strong shift equivalence does not imply unital shift equivalence.

Example 3.5
.

(1)

For the matrix
A=(2)
,

\lambda\colon(G_{A},G_{A}^{+},\theta_{A},u_{A})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right],\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]^{+},\operatorname{mult}_{2},1\right),

where
\operatorname{mult}_{2}
is the multiplication by
2
automorphism on the dyadic rationals
\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}]
.

(2)

The matrix

B\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}

is an outsplit of
A
via
R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}
and
S=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
1\end{pmatrix}
, which implies

(G_{B},G_{B}^{+},\theta_{B},u_{B})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right],\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]^{+},\operatorname{mult}_{2},2\right),\ [v,k]\mapsto\lambda([S^{t}v,k])

Since
2\in\mathbb{Z}[\frac{1}{2}]
is in the orbit of
\operatorname{mult}_{2}
, the unital dimension data of
A
and
B
are isomorphic. By
Proposition

3.2
,
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent.

(3)

The matrix

C\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\

in an insplit of

via

R\coloneq\begin{pmatrix}1&0&1\\

which implies

(G_{C},G_{C}^{+},\theta_{C},u_{C})\cong\left(\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right],\mathbb{Z}\left[\frac{1}{2}\right]^{+},\operatorname{mult}_{2},3\right),[v,k]\mapsto\lambda([\begin{pmatrix}1&1\end{pmatrix}S^{t}v,k]).

Note that there is no automorphism of

(or

Remark 3.6
.

A pointed version of Hazrat’s graded classification conjecture for Leavitt path algebras
[
Ha13
]
predicts that the pointed graded
K_{0}
-group of the Leavitt path algebra of finite graphs with no sinks are isomorphic if and only if the Leavitt path algebras are graded isomorphic (see also
Corollary

5.4
).
The pointed graded
K_{0}
-group coincides with the unital dimension data we consider here for the underlying adjacency matrices, so our notion of unital shift equivalence provides a matrix aspect of this conjecture.
In our previous work
[
BDHR24
]
with Dor-On and Hazrat, we discussed a slightly stronger version of unital shift equivalence (see Section 2.1 in that paper)
where we said a shift equivalence
(R,S)
is unital, if
\hat{R}(u_{A})=u_{B}
. We were able to prove that the Leavitt path algebras of finite graphs with no sinks are isomorphic provided that their adjacency matrices are unital shift equivalent (in the sense of
[
BDHR24
]
) and they satisfy an alignment condition (see
[
BDHR24
, Definitions 5.2 and 5.4]
). We choose this slightly more flexible unital shift equivalence (
Definition

3.1
) in order to establish that unital shift equivalence coincides with having isomorphic unital dimension data.

There are also C*-algebraic versions of Hazrat’s conjecture concerning gauge-equivariant isomorphism of graph C*-algebras, and this is e.g. studied in
[
ER25
]
where the unital dimension data appears as the dimension quadruple.
See also
[
BK00
]
.

The
unital signed Bowen–Franks group
of
A
is the cokernel
\textup{BF}(A)\coloneq\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}/(I-A^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
together with the class of the vector
\underline{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
in the cokernel
and the sign of the determinant
\sign\det I-A^{t}
.

Lemma 3.7
.

The unital Bowen–Franks group is an invariant of unital shift equivalence.

Proof.

It is wellknown that the sign of the determinant is invariant under shift equivalence (this follows e.g. from
[
LM21
, Corollary 7.4.12]
).

There is a canonical map
q_{A}\colon G_{A}\to\textup{BF}(A)
given by
q_{A}([v,k])=v+(I-A^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
, for all
[v,k]\in G_{A}
.
In particular,
q_{A}([\underline{1},0])=\underline{1}+(I-A^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
.
A shift equivalence
(R,S)
from
A
to
B
induces a group isomorphism
\check{R}\colon\textup{BF}(A)\to\textup{BF}(B)
given by

(3.2)

\check{R}(v+(I-A^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|A|})=R^{t}v+(I-B^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|B|},

for all
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
with inverse
\check{S}(w+(I-B^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|B|})=S^{t}w+(I-A^{t})\mathbb{Z}^{|A|}
as
A^{t}
and
B^{t}
induce identity maps on the Bowen–Franks groups of
A
and
B
, respectively.
An easy computation shows that

(3.3)

q_{B}\circ\hat{R}=\check{R}\circ q_{A}.

In particular, if
(R,S)
is unital, then
\check{R}
respects the classes of
\underline{1}
in the Bowen–Franks groups.
∎

Example 3.8
.

For integers
k\geq 2
, the matrices below

A_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}1&k\\

.
The matrix

implements a similarity over

4.
A discrepancy in one-sided eventual conjugacy

In this section, we discuss the connections between unital shift equivalence, Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy, and having (one-sided) conjugate higher powers.
The latter two notions are both called
eventual conjugacy
in the literature, so one main point of this section is that all three notions are in fact distinct.
This is in contrast to the two-sided case, where shift equivalence coincides with having (two-sided) conjugate higher powers.

Matsumoto
[
Ma17
]
introduces
eventual conjugacy
for one-sided shifts of finite type.
Two one-sided shifts of finite type
\sigma_{A}
and
\sigma_{B}
are
Matsumoto eventually conjugate
if there exist a homeomorphism
h\colon X_{A}\to X_{B}
and an integer
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
satisfying

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}^{\ell+1}\circ h(x)=\sigma_{B}^{\ell}\circ h(\sigma_{A}(x)),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}^{\ell+1}\circ h^{-1}(y)=\sigma_{A}^{\ell}\circ h^{-1}(\sigma_{B}(y)),

for all
x\in X_{A}
and
y\in X_{B}
.

In
[
Br22
]
, it is shown that Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy is generated at the level of directed graphs by out-splits and balanced in-split,
and an algebraic characterisation called balanced strong shift equivalence of Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy is established.
It is also argued that Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy should be viewed as the one-sided analogue of two-sided conjugacy (strong shift equivalence).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
A
and
B
be square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices.
If
A
and
B
are balanced strong shift equivalent, then
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent.

Proof.

Balanced strong shift equivalence is generated by one-sided outsplits (which we know preserve the unital dimension data by
Lemma

3.4
) and balanced elementary equivalence, so it suffices to assume that
A
and
B
are balanced elementary equivalent.
This means that there are rectangular
\mathbb{N}
-matrices
S
,
R_{A}
, and
R_{B}
satisfying

A=SR_{A},\qquad B=SR_{B},\qquad R_{A}S=R_{B}S.

Observe that
(B,A)
defines a shift equivalence from
A
to
B
of lag
2
which is clearly unital.
∎

Since balanced strong shift equivalence is an algebraic characterisation of Matsumoto’s one-sided eventual conjugacy, the proposition shows that if one-sided shifts of finite type are Matsumoto eventually conjugate, then the matrices are unitally shift equivalent.
This can also be proven using C*-algebras through Carlsen and Rout’s theorem
[
CR17
, Theorem 4.1]
for Matsumoto’s eventual conjugacy.

At the level of graphs we have the following corollary.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
E
be a finite graph with no sinks.
If
F

5.
Continuous orbit equivalence

In this section, we prove that unitally shift equivalent matrices determine one-sided shifts of finite type that are continuously orbit equivalent.
Continuous orbit equivalence is an important link between symbolic dynamics and C*-algebras as well as topological full groups
[
Ma10
,
Ma15
]
.
A consequence of our result is that unitally shift equivalent matrices will have isomorphic topological full groups.
The theorem is a one-sided analogue of a recent result of Boyle showing that shift equivalence implies flow equivalence for all (not necessarily irreducible) shifts of finite type.
From a shift equivalence, Boyle constructs an explicit
\mathsf{SL}
equivalence thus proving flow equivalence.
This requires a careful study of the poset of irreducible components of the matrix.

Let
A
and
B
be square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices with no zero rows.
Recall from
[
Ma10
, Section 5]
(see also
[
BCW17
]
) that the edge shifts
(\sigma_{A},X_{A})
and
(\sigma_{B},X_{B})
are
continuously orbit equivalent
if there are continuous functions
k_{A},l_{A}\colon X_{A}\to\mathbb{N}
and
k_{B},l_{B}\colon X_{B}\to\mathbb{N}
and a homeomorphism
h\colon X_{A}\to X_{B}
satisfying

(5.1)

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}^{l_{A}(x)}\circ h(x)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{B}^{k_{A}(x)}\circ h(\sigma_{A}(x)),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sigma_{A}^{l_{B}(y)}\circ h^{-1}(y)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{A}^{k_{B}(y)}\circ h^{-1}(\sigma_{B}(y)),

for all
x\in X_{A}
and
y\in X_{B}
.

A point
x\in X_{A}
is
eventually periodic
if there are distinct
p,q\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma_{A}^{p}(x)=\sigma_{A}^{q}(x)
.
A continuous orbit equivalence
h\colon X_{A}\to X_{B}
preserves eventually periodic points if
h(x)
is eventually periodic precisely if
x
is eventually periodic.
This extra condition of preserving eventually periodic points is important for connections to étale groupoids and topological full groups.

Boyle used
\mathsf{SL}
equivalence of matrices to characterise flow equivalence, and there is a unital version called
\mathsf{SL_{+}}
equivalence that we introduce below.
Although we know that
\mathsf{SL_{+}}
equivalence implies continuous orbit equivalence, we do not know if the converse holds.
We take the opportunity to reiterate a related question, see
[
ER25
, Conjecture 6.2.1]

(see
[
ER25
, Definition 2.3.1]
for the definition of unital reduction):

Question 5.1
.

Given square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices

and

and
(\sigma_{B},X_{B})
are continuously orbit equivalent, is it possible to transform

into

using out-splits, balanced in-splits, and unital reduction (and their inverses)?

In order to deal with not necessarily irreducible shifts of finite type it is important to keep track of the structure of its irreducible components.
A square

admits a canonical partially ordered set (poset) of its irreducible components

where for

when there exist an integer

and indices

and

Given a poset

We refer the reader to (and assume familiarity with)

Below we state and prove the main result of the section and the paper.
In order to verify that unital shift equivalence implies continuous orbit equivalence (which is the one-sided analogue of flow equivalence), we go via
\mathsf{SL_{+}}
equivalence
[
AER22
, Section 5]
.
This is a unital version of
\mathsf{SL}
equivalence, which is the key notion that Boyle uses to characterise flow equivalence.
Roughly, two matrices

equivalent if there are partitioned square integral matrices

and

for which every diagonal block has determinant 1 satisfying

in a way that respects the poset structures, see
[

The matrix

.
Let

and

be matrices, and let

equivalence

from

to

We say a block upper triangular matrix

(1)

Our definition of canonical form of a matrix is chosen in such a way that for a nonzero row vector

(I-(B^{\prime})^{t},v_{B})
. It now follows from
[
AER22
, Theorem 5.4]
(see also
[
ER25
, Theorem 4.2.2]
) that the one-sided edge shifts are continuously orbit equivalent.
This uses the fact that a diagonal-preserving *-isomorphism of the associated Cuntz–Krieger C*-algebras implies that the shifts are continuously orbit equivalent in a way that preserves eventually periodic points, see
[
BCW17
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
AER18
, Theorem 5.3]
.
∎

As in the proof above, if
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent, then their étale groupoids are isomorphic (see
[
BCW17
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
AER18
, Theorem 5.3]
), so their topological full groups are also isomorphic, see
[
Ma15
]
.
We record this as a separate corollary.

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be square
\mathbb{N}
-matrices with no zero rows.
If
A
and
B
are unitally shift equivalent, then the topological full groups of
(\sigma_{A},X_{A})
and
(\sigma_{B},X_{B})
are isomorphic.

A

.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
A
and

\mathbb{N}

If
A
and

and

Traveling antiferromagnetic domain walls in a magnetic field
G. Theodorou, S. Komineas

Abstract
We consider an antiferromagnet in one space dimension with easy-axis anisotropy in a perpendicular magnetic field. We study propagating domain wall solutions that can have a velocity up to a maximum v c v_{c} . The width of the domain wall is a non-monotonic function of the velocity and it diverges to infinity at v c v_{c} . Both features are in contrast to the case of the Lorentz invariant model in the absence of the field. We further study the modification of the wall profile when a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction is added. Finally, we present a propagating spiral expected to form when the system is forced at a velocity higher than the maximum velocity for domain walls and we give numerical results for the effect of the Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction.



I
Introduction

Antiferromagnetic domain walls have attracted attention for many decades
Kosevich et al. 1990
;
Mikeska and Steiner 1991
while interest has grown in recent years as antiferromagnetic order can be observed in a more efficient way and domain walls can now be directly imaged
Cheong et al. 2020
;
Hedrich et al. 2021
;
Grigorev et al. 2022
.
In addition, experimental techniques allow for optical
Meer et al. 2023
and electrical
Wadley et al. 2018
manipulation of domain walls (DW) in antiferromagnets (AFMs), while current-induced switching can be achieved by domain wall motion
Baldrati et al. 2019
.

The most attractive features of antiferromagnets are related to their dynamics.
This is described by extensions of the nonlinear
\sigma
model for the Néel vector
Kosevich et al. 1990
;
Mikeska and Steiner 1991
and thus is radically different from the dynamics of magnetization in ferromagnets.
An advantage of AFMs is fast dynamics, such as THz frequencies and fast domain wall motion
Hals et al. 2011
;
Shiino et al. 2016
;
Gomonay et al. 2016
.

The features and properties of antiferromagnetic domain walls have been theoretically studied in various cases.
It has been proposed that dislocations are sources of domain walls.
Kovalev and Kosevich 1977
;
Dzyaloshinskii 1977
;
Dudko and Kovalev 2000
.
Internal dynamical modes of the domain walls have been studied in an easy-plane AFM in a strong in-plane magnetic field
Ivanov and Kolezhuk 1997
and in biaxial antiferromagnets
Gvozdikova and Kovalev 1999
;
Kovalev 2018
, and the dynamic structure factor was calculated for a moving domain wall in an easy-axis antiferromagnet under an external field
Ivanov et al. 1991
.

In the case where an external magnetic field is applied, the sigma model is substantially modified, and the Lorentz invariance is broken.
Despite this complication, static and propagating domain wall solutions are known to exist also in this case.
We focus on traveling domain wall solutions in an external field applied parallel to the easy axis of the antiferromagnet and found in
Bary’akhtar and Ivanov 1979a
;
Bary’akhtar and Ivanov 1979b
.
We study its features and the unusual behavior of the domain wall width as a function of its velocity.
This dependence can be non-monotonic, while there is a maximum speed.
The wall width diverges for large speeds in stark contrast to the usual behavior of collapse for large speeds in the case of the Lorentz invariant model (when no external field is present).
We extend the results to the case where a Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM) interaction is present.
We further investigate the fate of the system when the wall velocity exceeds the critical value and the domain wall disappears.
We find that a propagating spiral state is then the appropriate solution of the model.

The paper is organized as follows.
Sec.
II
gives the formalism and describes the propagating domain wall under an external field without and with a DM interaction, in two subsections.
Sec.
III
describes the propagating spiral.
Sec.
IV
contains our concluding remarks.

II
Traveling domain walls

We consider an antiferromagnet with easy-axis anisotropy and DM interaction in an external field
\bm{h}
.
In the continuum limit, statics and dynamics can be described by a nonlinear
\sigma
-model for the Néel vector
\bm{n}

Kosevich et al. 1990
;
Mikeska and Steiner 1991
,

\bm{n}\times(\ddot{\bm{n}}+2\bm{h}\times\dot{\bm{n}}-\bm{f})=0,\qquad\bm{f}=\bm{n}^{\prime\prime}-2\lambda\,\bm{\hat{e}}_{2}\times\bm{n}^{\prime}+n_{3}\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}-(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{h})\bm{h}

(1)

where the dot denotes differentiation in time, the prime denotes differentiation in the space variable
x
, and
\lambda
is the scaled DM parameter.
Throughout this work, we choose an external magnetic field parallel to the easy axis,

\bm{h}=(0,0,h),

Then, the model reduces to

\bm{n}\times(\ddot{\bm{n}}+2h\,\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}\times\dot{\bm{n}}-\bm{f})=0,\qquad\bm{f}=\bm{n}^{\prime\prime}-2\lambda\,\bm{\hat{e}}_{2}\times\bm{n}^{\prime}+(1-h^{2})n_{3}\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}.

(2)

We will study traveling waves, that is, solutions of the form

\bm{n}(x,t)=\bm{n}(\xi),\qquad\xi=x-vt.

This form is used in Eq. (
2
) which reduces to

\bm{n}\times\left[(1-v^{2})\bm{n}^{\prime\prime}+2hv\,\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}\times\bm{n}^{\prime}-2\lambda\,\bm{\hat{e}}_{2}\times\bm{n}^{\prime}+(1-h^{2})n_{3}\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}\right]=0.

(3)

II.1
Effect of the external field

We start the study assuming
\lambda=0
(no DM interaction).
Equation (
1
) has a static domain wall solution.
In the absence of an external field,
h=0
, this can be made dynamical by a Lorentz transformation.
Lorentz invariance is lost once a field is applied,
h\neq 0
.
We will re-derive the dynamical domain wall solution, in the presence of an external field, that has been obtained in Ref.
Bary’akhtar and Ivanov 1979a
, and we will study its features.

All subsequent calculations will be based on the spherical parametrization for the Néel vector,

n_{1}=\sin\Theta\cos\Phi,\quad n_{2}=\sin\Theta\sin\Phi,\quad n_{3}=\cos\Theta.

(4)

Equation (
3

\displaystyle(1-v^{2})\Theta^{\prime\prime}-\left[2hv\Phi^{\prime}+(1-v^{2})\Phi^{\prime 2}+1-h^{2}\right]\sin\Theta\cos\Theta=0

(5a)

\displaystyle\sin\Theta(1-v^{2})\Phi^{\prime\prime}+2\cos\Theta\Theta^{\prime}\left[(1-v^{2})\Phi^{\prime}+hv\right]=0.

(5b)

Equation (
5b
) gives

\left[\sin^{2}\Theta\left[(1-v^{2})\Phi^{\prime}+hv\right]\right]^{\prime}=0\Rightarrow(1-v^{2})\Phi^{\prime}+hv=0.

(6)

The integration constant was set to zero because we are looking for domain walls and thus have
\Theta=0,\pi
at the two ends of the system.
The solution is

\Phi=k\left(\xi-x_{1}\right),\qquad k=-\frac{hv}{1-v^{2}}

(7)

x_{1}
is an arbitrary constant, such that
\Phi(x=x_{1},t=0)=0

Multiply Eq. (
5a
) by
\Theta^{\prime}
and (

\sin\Theta\,\Phi^{\prime}

\left[\Theta^{\prime 2}-\left(\frac{1-h^{2}}{1-v^{2}}-\Phi^{\prime 2}\right)\sin^{2}\Theta\right]^{\prime}=0\Rightarrow\Theta^{\prime 2}-\left(\frac{1-h^{2}}{1-v^{2}}-\Phi^{\prime 2}\right)\sin^{2}\Theta=0.

Substituting Eq. (
7

\Theta^{\prime 2}-\frac{1-h^{2}-v^{2}}{(1-v^{2})^{2}}\sin^{2}\Theta=0

(10)

Note that
x_{0}
, that is, the location of the center of the domain wall at
t=0
, is an arbitrary constant and does not need to be the same as
x_{1}
defined in Eq. (
7

Solution (

h^{2}+v^{2}<1\Rightarrow|v|<v_{c}\equiv\sqrt{1-h^{2}}.

(11)

The maximum velocity

coincides with the minimum of the phase velocity for spin waves in this system, as can be anticipated by general arguments
Galkina et al. 2022
.

Traveling chiral wall

We now include the DM interaction and we will see that propagating domain wall solutions are found in this case, too.

III
Propagating spiral

One may ask about the fate of the domain wall when this is forced to propagate at a velocity higher than the maximum velocity,
v>v_{c}
, given in Eq. (
11
).
In this section, we confine our study to
\lambda=0
.
We start this investigation by writing a functional
F
, such that its minimization gives Eq. (
3
).
It is

F=\frac{1-v^{2}}{2}\int\bm{n}^{\prime 2}\,dx+hv\int\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}\cdot(\bm{n}\times\bm{n}^{\prime})\,dx+\frac{1-h^{2}}{2}\int(1-n_{3}^{2})\,dx.

(20)

The minimizing
\bm{n}
is a solution of the original equation under the constraint of constant velocity.
This indicates that
F
should be equivalent to the energy functional augmented with a Lagrange multiplier
v
times the linear momentum.

Assume now an in-plane configuration

n_{1}=\cos\Phi,\quad n_{2}=\sin\Phi,\quad n_{3}=0

(21)

which reduces the functional to

F=\int\left(\frac{1-v^{2}}{2}\Phi^{\prime 2}+hv\Phi^{\prime}+\frac{1-h^{2}}{2}\right)dx.

The minimum of
F
is obtained for

\Phi^{\prime}=-\frac{hv}{1-v^{2}}

(22)

giving the integrand in Eq. (
20
),

f=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1-h^{2}-v^{2}}{1-v^{2}}.

This shows that the spiral state (
21
) achieves a lower minimum,
F<0
, than the polarized state
\bm{n}=\pm\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}
when
h^{2}+v^{2}>1
, i.e., for
v>v_{c}
.
We have verified numerically that the damped equation of motion under the constraint of a velocity
v>v_{c}
leads to the spiral.
One could describe the physical picture that emerges for
v>v_{c}
in two alternative ways.
One way is to consider that the polarized domains, on either side of the domain wall, are destabilized to give the spiral (
21
), and another way is to consider that the central region of the domain wall extends to occupy the whole space as
v\to v_{c}
and
\ell_{w}\to\infty
.
The spiral (
22
) gives a minimum of
F
in the velocity range
\sqrt{1-h^{2}}<|v|<1
, and its period is
2\pi(1-v^{2})/(hv)
.
At the critical velocity
v=v_{c}
, the period is
2\pi\sqrt{1-h^{2}}/h
, while at
v\to 1
the period goes to zero, giving the singular behavior typically expected for Lorentz invariant propagating solutions.

The spiral solution in Eq. (
21
) is mainly due to the presence of the second term in the functional (
20
) which originates in the dynamical term due to the external field.
This term has the same form as a chiral term with DM vector along
\bm{\hat{e}}_{3}
.
The spiral solution could thus have been anticipated on the basis of the same arguments as for the well-known spiral of chiral magnetic models
Bogdanov and Hubert 1994
;
Chovan et al. 2002
.

This velocity is below
v_{c}=0.8
which is the critical velocity for the transition to the spiral when
\lambda=0
.

If we add a DM term in the model, we find numerically that the spiral is significantly modified.
All three components of
\bm{n}
oscillate, as shown in Fig.
5
.
We have also found that the spiral is obtained for velocities somewhat lower than the value
v_{c}
.
For example, for
h=0.6
, we have
v_{c}=0.8
.
For
v=0.79
, we obtain a domain wall for
\lambda=0
but this becomes a spiral when we increase
\lambda
(this case is shown in Fig.
5
).
This means that the DM reduces the maximum velocity of the domain wall.

The results of this section indicate that, if a method (experimental or simulation) were developed to force the magnetic configuration, under model (
3
), to propagate with high velocity, then the spiral would appear spontaneously and its period would decrease with increasing velocity.

IV
Concluding remarks

We have studied solitary wave motion of domain walls in antiferromagnets under a magnetic field parallel to the easy axis.
The magnetic field introduces a dynamical term in the model that breaks the Lorentz invariance and generates some special features of the propagating domain walls.
The maximum possible velocity is reduced due to the magnetic field.
For stronger fields, the domain wall width is a non-monotonic function of the velocity and it presents a minimum.
The wall width diverges to infinity at the maximum velocity for all fields.
When a DM interaction term is added to the model, the domain wall profile is modified, and it acquires a chiral character although it does not become a fully chiral wall.
If the system is driven to obtain a velocity higher than the maximum velocity allowed for a domain wall, it is expected that a propagating spiral will be spontaneously created with the Néel vector lying fully in-plane perpendicular to the easy-axis.
When there is a DM interaction, the spiral is more complicated with all three magnetization components being nonzero and periodic in space.

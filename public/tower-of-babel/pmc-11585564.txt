Promoting agricultural sustainable development by a novel integrated pythagorean neutrosophic and WINGS-BWM model
Authors: Kecheng Zhang, Zhicheng Chen
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper identifies nine factors affecting agricultural sustainable development by reading extensive literature and invites five experts to assess these factors using the pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable. Some of these factors can directly reduce the environmental footprint of agriculture, improve soil health, and promote biodiversity, while others can indirectly integrate to support the adoption of sustainable practices, further mitigating environmental degradation. Collectively, these factors reinforce ecological balance and play a critical role in advancing agricultural sustainable development. To unravel the complex relationships among these factors, a novel decision theory model is proposed, integrating pythagorean neutrosophic set (PNS) with Weighted Influence Nonlinear Specification System (WINGS) and Best-Worst Method (BWM). In addition, we not only categorized all factors into cause-and-effect factors, but also constructed a network relationship diagram based on them. The study shows that agricultural modernization (Y6) is the most important factor and land remediation (Y2) is the most influential factor. This integrated approach can more effectively address the common challenges of uncertainty and linguistic ambiguity in decision-making scenarios. Combining PNS with WINGS helps make the interactions and importance of factors more apparent, which is particularly suitable for analyzing key factors that promote agricultural sustainability. The incorporation of BWM further ensures the model’s accuracy and objectivity. This method provides a more comprehensive and accurate reflection of decision-makers’ opinions and judgments, improving decision-making efficiency, and can be widely applied not only in agriculture but also to other decision-making problems.Keywords: Agricultural sustainable development, Pythagorean neutrosophic set, WINGS, BWMSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

According to a report released by the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO) in 2023, more than half of the implementation process of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development has been implemented, but the goals related to food and agriculture have stagnated or even reversed, resulting in more severe challenges to efforts to eradicate poverty and hunger, improve health and nutrition, and combat climate change. According to UN estimates, more than 780 million people suffer from hunger, almost a third of the food produced globally is lost or wasted, and nearly 3 billion people cannot afford a healthy diet. Agriculture is a basic industry that human society depends on for survival. It is not only related to food safety and ecological balance, but also an important driving force for economic growth and social stability, and its development is directly related to human survival and development1. With the growth of global population and the acceleration of industrialization process, the sustainable development of agriculture has become a general trend of the international community.

Literature review

Agricultural sustainable development

Agricultural sustainable development refers to the practice of managing agricultural resources to meet human needs while protecting or enhancing the quality of the environment and conserving natural resources2,3. The concept is not only deeply rooted in the principles of sustainable development, i.e., balancing the needs of the present generation without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs, from reduction and harmlessness at the source to resource utilization in the process and ecological restoration at the end, but it is also a complex and multifaceted concept that involves many aspects of economic viability, environmental management and social responsibility4–8. The idea of sustainable development, first introduced by Douglass in 1984, which gained prominence at the end of the 20th century, is applied to agriculture as a system that requires holistic considerations at various levels, including microeconomic, macroeconomic, and global scales9,10. In 2015, sustainable agricultural development became a key element of the Sustainable Development Goals, which were unanimously adopted by the 193 member states of the United Nations. According to the report of the Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations (FAO), the development of sustainable agriculture focuses on promoting technological innovation and reform, so as to ensure the sustainable use of resources and ecological sustainability. The purpose of this development model is to meet the agricultural needs of current and future generations and inject lasting vitality into our common home planet.

The influence factors of agricultural sustainable development

This approach to agriculture is increasingly recognized as essential for addressing economic instability, environmental degradation, food security and social inequality, within the agricultural sector. The sustainable development of agriculture has also aroused the common concern of governments, enterprises and scholars. Sustainable agriculture can boost farmers’ income and productivity while reducing costs, promoting long-term economic stability and resilience11. Sustainable agricultural practices contribute to environmental protection by conserving resources, recycling waste, minimizing pollution, and fostering biodiversity11. Sustainable agriculture holds the key to ensuring food security, enhancing productivity, improving nutrient uptake, and reducing reliance on synthetic inputs such as fertilizers and pesticides12. Sustainable agriculture positively impacts social stability by promoting health, building social capital, and mitigating harmful farming practices that damage soils and ecosystems13. Despite progress in agricultural sustainable development, significant pressure on resources and the environment persists due to the high input, high consumption, and high pollution characteristics of traditional agricultural practices14,15. This ongoing threat to agricultural sustainability coincides with numerous challenges, including resource scarcity, ethical risks16, environmental contamination17, land nutrient depletion, and soil erosion18–20, greenhouse gas emissions, water quality decline21,22, etc. These are also the causes of climate change, the degradation of the biosphere, the destruction of land systems and the eutrophication of the oceans as a result of the application of mineral fertilizers23,24. Therefore, to expedite the process of agricultural sustainable development and achieve harmonious economic, social, and ecological progress, it is critically important to investigate the interrelationships among various factors in agricultural sustainable development and assess the impact of each influencing factor.

Research has shown that the promotion of environmentally friendly agricultural technologies can affect the agriculture sustainable development, for example, green pest control technology, conservation tillage technology, soil testing and fertilization technology, low-carbon agricultural production practices and agricultural green production technologies can improve the utilization rate of resources, obtain higher economic benefits, accelerate the development of agriculture, and thus promote the sustainable development of agriculture25–28. The impact of agricultural activities on the environment cannot be ignored, and environmental protection is another key factor. Agricultural sustainable development through skillful management of natural resources as well as protection of the environment and advocacy for sustainable practices in different sectors, including agriculture29–31. In addition, we have selected the following seven influencing factors from the recent literature: land remediation32–34, organic soil fertilizer application35–38, green finance39, agricultural modernization40–42, eco-environmental restoration43–45, digital economy46,47, and industrialization of agricultural scale48–52. With so many influencing factors, we cannot help but ask: which factor is dominant in driving or hindering sustainable agricultural development? More importantly, is there some as yet unrevealed correlation between these factors? As these questions are raised, there is an urgent need to find answers that will provide more comprehensive and in-depth insights for policy makers. Although there have been a number of attempts to study agricultural sustainable development, these studies have tended to focus too much on particular factors in isolation and use them as their presupposing assumptions, thus failing to fully explore the possible interrelationships among these factors53–55. Therefore, there is a need to analyze in depth each of the factors affecting sustainable agricultural development, and also to quantify the extent of their respective impacts, in order to identify those factors that are most critical and sensitive. However, due to the large number of uncertain and ambiguous variables in the agricultural system, studying the interrelationships between these factors becomes exceptionally complex. In order to effectively deal with this uncertainty, we can resort to advanced tools and methods such as fuzzy set theory.

Fuzzy set

Through fuzzy set theory, we can better deal with variables that are difficult to precisely define or quantify, and thus provide new perspectives and solutions for understanding and solving complex problems in agricultural sustainable development. For example, Rida et al. employ two MCDM methods, the Fuzzy Analytic Hierarchy Process (Fuzzy-AHP) and Fuzzy VIKOR, to identify the most suitable location for an nuclear power plant, focusing on socio-economic factors56. Perçin employs the complex proportional assessment method, in an Interval-valued intuitionistic fuzzy-set environment, to rank potential circular suppliers, testing the validity of the proposed method via a case study involving a multinational cement company from Turkey57.

In the early days, when we encountered problems containing ambiguous or uncertain information in reality, we were at a loss as to how to deal with them, but when Zadeh58proposed the concept of fuzzy sets, such problems were solved. As a general framework for systematically dealing with problems containing uncertain information, this method has become a hot research topic and also has a great impact on practical decision-making problems. However, fuzzy sets are only discussed in terms of the membership function, and then Atanassov59generalized it and proposed the concept of intuitionistic fuzzy sets (IFS), which can represent both the degree of membership and the degree of non- membership, and IFS is more powerful than the traditional fuzzy sets to deal with the problem of ambiguous or uncertain information, but has the limitation that the sum of the degree of membership and the degree of non-membership is less than or equal to one. Later on, in order to reduce this limitation and to improve the accuracy of the description of fuzzy or uncertain information based on the concept of two-valued logic, Yager60,61and Smarandache62,63 proposed the pythagorean fuzzy set (PFS) and the neutrosophic set (NS), respectively. The pythagorean fuzzy set al.lows the sum of the membership degree and non- membership degree to be greater than 1, but the sum of their squares is not greater than 1, which further improves the ability to deal with ambiguity and uncertainty information. Neutrosophic set includes truth membership, indeterminacy membership and falsity membership. Within this broader context, the pythagorean neutrosophic set (PNS) was introduced in 2019 by Jansi et al.64. As a powerful tool it relaxes the constraints imposed by its predecessors and offering new vistas in decision-making, pattern recognition, and other domains. Pythagorean neutrosophic set is characterized by three components: truthiness, falsity, and indeterminacy. This enables PNS to handle information that is not only difficult to distinguish between true and false but also contains ambiguity or uncertainty. For instance, in the study of agricultural sustainability, expert opinions are often hard to clearly classify as true or false. PNS enhances its expressive capacity and captures this subtle ambiguity through the dimension of uncertainty, ultimately providing a more accurate reflection of experts’ assessments of uncertainty and ambiguity. Unlike traditional fuzzy set or intuitionistic fuzzy set, the sum of squares of these components in PNS is restricted to be between 0 and 2. This relaxation allows for a wider range of values, accommodating a higher degree of fuzziness and uncertainty. Particularly in scenarios that require handling a large amount of uncertain information, PNS can better capture this information without having to reduce it to a linear or binary representation. Additionally, in multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA), PNS can flexibly handle uncertainties arising from various factors. This multidimensional representation allows decision-makers to make more reliable decisions under more complex conditions. Similarly, when artificial intelligence and robotic systems face the challenge of dealing with uncertain data, PNS can assist these systems in processing uncertain and conflicting information, leading to better decision-making. The literature on PNS has witnessed a surge in recent years, with researchers exploring various facets of this concept. One of the earliest works in this domain focused on establishing the mathematical foundations of PNS, defining operations on these sets, and exploring their properties65,66. Subsequently, there has been a growing interest in extending the applications of PNS to diverse fields. In decision-making, for instance, PNS has been utilized to develop more robust and flexible decision-support systems67,68. By incorporating the inherent vagueness and uncertainty in decision-making problems, PNS enables a more realistic and comprehensive assessment of alternatives. Best-worst method and weighted influence nonlinear specification system

BWM is a method based on pairwise comparisons (PC), first proposed by Rezaei in69. Its advantage is that BWM can determine the consistency ratio and requires less data than methods such as SMART and Swing, and even if the decision maker is careless in filling out the questionnaire can still be informed by checking the consistency ratio70. Moreover, this method reduces the number of two-by-two comparisons compared to the analytic hierarchy process (AHP)71,72. In addition to this in the BWM, since the decision maker first selects the best and worst criteria, he can better understand the evaluation range, which improves the accuracy and consistency of the PCs73and also reduces the decision-making bias in the PCs process74. BWM can not only be used to derive optimal weights, but can also be combined with other Multi-Criteria Decision Making (MCDM) methods, and its utility and effectiveness have been verified in many studies70,75,76. MCDM as an important object of research in the field of decision making, has attracted many experts and scholars from different disciplinary fields to conduct research. Decision-making Trial and Evaluation Laboratory (DEMATEL) is one of the popular MCDM methods nowadays, which was proposed in the 1970s as a way to convert the causal relationships among the elements in a complex system into a structural model77. Later Michnik J. proposed the Weighted Influence Nonlinear Specification System (WINGS) method on the basis of the DEMATEL method, which not only inherited all the features of its predecessor but also extended it to include the consideration of the influence of influencing factors on their own influence, whereas the DEMATEL method only considered the influence of the influencing factors on each other78.As a result, the improved version of WINGS is more accurate in assessing the interrelationships between different influencing factors. WINGS not only focuses on the correlations between factors but also identifies and classifies the causal relationships among them. Compared to traditional methods, it quantifies the contribution of each factor within the system to construct a causal impact diagram, thereby clarifying the role of each factor as a ‘cause’ or ‘effect.’ This quantitative analysis of causal relationships provides a deeper understanding for systematic decision-making, helping to identify which factors are primary causal drivers and which are secondary, thus providing a scientific basis for decision-making. But it has also been criticized for its inability to handle linguistic uncertainty due to its use of crisp numbers to represent scale79. For this reason, this paper introduces pythagorean neutrosophic set and pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable into the quantification of expert uncertainty information, and establishes an assessment method based on the combination of WINGS and BWM methods to analyze the influencing factors affecting the sustainable development of agriculture. Pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable was used to deal with the fuzzy evaluation of the experts on the factors influencing the sustainable development of agriculture, and the scores and weights of each expert were averaged. Considering the degree of influence of each factor on the sustainable development of agriculture and the correlation between the factors, Wings and BWM were combined to establish a comprehensive weighted calculation model, and the comparison was verified at the end.

The “Results” section shows how to analyze the factors affecting sustainable agricultural development based on the combined PNS and WINGS-BWM method. In the “Discussion” section, we present a comparative analysis of the three methods. In the last section, we give the conclusion of this study.

Therefore, compared with other methods, the approach proposed in this paper offers several advantages. First, by introducing the Pythagorean neutrosophic set (PNS), this method allows for the comprehensive and accurate expression of experts’ opinions and judgments through linguistic variables. This facilitates the assessment of influencing factors by leveraging the implicit knowledge embedded in these variables. The PNS framework captures not only the degrees of truth and falsehood but also the degree of neutrality, providing decision-makers with an effective tool for evaluating factors related to sustainable agricultural development. The incorporation of PNS mitigates the limitations of traditional DEMATEL or WINGS methods, resulting in more objective and reliable evaluation outcomes. Additionally, the final rankings produced by this method are confirmed to be trustworthy after three comparative analyses. Clearly, this study presents a more accurate, effective, and systematic decision support tool for examining the influencing factors of sustainable agricultural development. Secondly, the WINGS-BWM method streamlines the pairwise comparison process among all indicators in the Analytic Hierarchy Process, enhancing the consistency ratio and the accuracy of the weights assigned to these indicators. Ultimately, standardized weight values are obtained by solving either nonlinear or linear models, thereby improving the scientific rigor of the weights. This approach also reduces the subjectivity inherent in expert scoring to some extent. Moreover, this method takes into account not only the interactions between different factors but also the interdependencies of the factors themselves, reflecting real-world conditions more accurately. The inclusion of causal groups makes the influence relationships among factors more intuitive. The results indicate that eco-environmental restoration (Y7) is initially identified as the most critical factor; however, after re-ranking, agricultural modernization (Y6) emerges as the foremost factor, with land remediation (Y2) identified as the most influential. Therefore, policymakers should prioritize these three factors. Accelerating ecological restoration, advancing agricultural modernization, and enhancing land remediation are pivotal for sustainable agricultural development. Finally, this study not only aids scholars in related fields in developing a theoretical understanding of sustainable agricultural development but also provides a comprehensive framework for modeling complex decision-making problems. The proposed method can be applied to various complex decision-making scenarios across different subject areas. By addressing the linguistic uncertainty inherent in decision-making, it enhances the representation and processing of linguistic variables, thereby improving the model’s accuracy and practicality.

Conclusions

With the growth of the global population and the acceleration of industrialization, sustainable agricultural development has become a major trend in the international community, and at the same time has attracted the common attention of governments, enterprises and scholars. In order to promote the process of sustainable agricultural development as soon as possible and realize the coordinated development of economy, society and ecology, we need to explore the factors affecting sustainable agricultural development. To this end, in this research article, we innovatively propose a new method that combines the pythagorean neutrosophic set and the WINGS-BWM method to investigate the interrelationships among the factors of sustainable agricultural development and to measure the degree of influence of each of the influencing factors. First, we selected nine factors affecting sustainable agricultural development by referring to a large number of previous literature studies and defined the pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable. After that, we invited five experts to fill in the questionnaire, and then we summarized the ratings of different experts on the importance of the factors and used the pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable to measure their influence. pythagorean neutrosophic linguistic variable as well as the relevant formula to convert it into total strength-influence matrix. Finally, we were also able to obtain the average optimal weights of BWM by the PNS WINGS-BWM combination method, WINGS combined weighted average of the new ranking results and the connection between the influencing factors related to the decision-making process. On the basis of this, a network diagram is constructed to visualize the interrelationships among the factors.

Key findings

This study not only provides some support for the advancement of sustainable agricultural development, but also has important implications for the study of the interrelationships among the factors affecting sustainable agricultural development. Based on the weighted composite significant values in Table 18, the prioritization of the factors is Y6 > Y7 > Y3 > Y2 > Y9 > Y4 > Y1 > Y8 > Y5, thus agricultural modernization (Y6) is the most important factor and its value is 0.0894. This also shows that agricultural modernization is the most important factor to improve the sustainable development of agriculture. The ranking of causality is based on the values of H-L and it can be visualized from Table 10 as well as Fig. 2 that land remediation (Y2) is the most influential factor with a value of 1.3631.This also shows that land remediation has more or less influence on many other factors in the process of sustainable agricultural development. Finally, we also classified all the factors into two categories based on the positive and negative values of H-L. The positive value of H-L is the cause factor which contains the following four influences land remediation (Y2), environmental protection (Y3), organic soil fertilizer application (Y4) and eco-environmental restoration (Y7). The corresponding negative H-L value is the effect factor which contains the following five effect factors the promotion of environmentally friendly agricultural technologies (Y1), green finance (Y5), agricultural modernization (Y6), digital economy (Y8) and industrialization of agricultural scale (Y9).

Importance of agricultural modernization

Agricultural modernization is not only the key to solving the problems of agricultural efficiency increase in the process of agricultural development and the difficulties of agricultural production structure, regional structure, quality structure and vertical structure that hinder the sustainable development of agriculture, but also plays a decisive role in the advancement of agriculture and agricultural economy. Therefore, improving the level of agricultural modernization is a necessary way to advance the sustainable development of agriculture. The national government to increase the promotion of agricultural infrastructure construction as well as the development of agricultural electrification can effectively promote the development process of agricultural modernization. For example, it can take to promote the expansion of reservoirs and water system network construction, accelerate the improvement of water conservancy supporting projects, increase agricultural water capacity, increase the electrification equipment to reduce the consumption of technology investment and research and development, the existing electrification facilities for continuous transformation and upgrading measures, at the same time with the agricultural drainage and irrigation motors, agricultural pumps and other means of mechanization and electrification, in order to effectively increase the area of soil and waterlogging area of soil and water erosion control, and so on, which are indicators conducive to sustainable agroecological development.

Need for eco-environmental restoration

The importance of eco-environmental restoration (Y7), even though it is the second most important influencing factor, is not to be ignored as it is ranked first before the weighting of BWM. At present, agricultural development is facing eco-environmental problems such as the deterioration of agroecosystems, frequent natural disasters, and the pollution of agricultural resources, and therefore eco-environmental restoration should not be delayed. First of all, we should strengthen the implementation of the important legal system to protect the environment, and further improve the laws and regulations of agroecological environment protection to restrain the society and human beings to use natural resources correctly and rationally. Secondly, the protection and management of fragile ecosystems should be strengthened; if agriculture does not have a better ability to resist natural disasters, the agroecological environment will certainly suffer serious damage.

Significance of environmental protection

Environmental protection (Y3) is also an important factor. Environmental protection is an important prerequisite for promoting the construction of agroecological civilization, and at the same time, it is of vital significance for promoting and facilitating the sustainable development of agriculture. In the work of agroecological environmental protection, we should firstly enhance the awareness of environmental protection and do a good job of agroecological environmental protection education and publicity. Secondly, we should fully and efficiently utilize scientific and technological resources and power, science and technology are the first productive force. Finally, we should rationally develop and utilize agricultural resources.

Influence of land remediation

Land remediation is a necessary way to improve the quality of arable land, expand the area of arable land and improve the ecological environment of land. Reasonable land remediation, unified planning of agricultural land resources development and utilization, can make the land, population, industry, resources and technology and other factors of production integration and reallocation, to help farmers increase production and income and realize the sustainable development of agriculture is of great significance. Therefore, the implementation of rational land remediation can have an impact on other factors, for example, through comprehensive land remediation, it can increase capital and technical inputs into the land, and promote the development of agricultural land into an intensive land management mode that is more adaptable to modern requirements.

Focus on the cause factor

Compared to the effect factor policy makers should pay more attention to the cause factor, for example, government departments at all levels should fully recognize the important role of soil fertilizer in the sustainable development of agriculture, and continue to improve the soil quality and fertilizer utilization rate by improving the laws and regulations on the protection of arable land resources, reinforcing the quality of arable land preservation, strengthening the research and development of new types of fertilizers and the use of technology, and taking a number of other measures, in order to providing promotion and support for the sustainable development of agriculture.

Limitations and future recommendations

This article has some limitations. First, there may be some bias in the assessment of each expert, and more experts may be asked to participate in the future. Second, we only considered nine factors that affect agricultural sustainability, so there may be some other factors that were not considered. Finally, based on this paper, more MCDM methods can be combined with pythagorean neutrosophic set in the future.

Innovation and contributions

The innovation of this paper is that it combines not only WINGS and BWM but also pythagorean neutrosophic set and WINGS-BWM, and there has been no research that combines these three methods together to explain the interrelationships among the influencing factors. Firstly, this method can not only parse people’s information of ambiguity and uncertainty, but also sort out the complex relationship between different factors and their own, and the final result is also accurate science. Secondly, it can rank the degree of importance of the influencing factors and then identify the key influencing factors. At the same time, we also analyzed the influence of each factor by dividing it into result factor and effect factor. Reviewing previous studies, there is no ranking of the importance of the factors affecting the sustainable development of agriculture, and there is no analysis of the degree of influence of the relevant factors from the perspectives of result factor and effect factor. Finally, the research objective of this paper is to identify the most important influential factors based on the constructed pythagorean neutrosophic WINGS-BWM combination model and to provide a boost to the process of advancing agricultural sustainable development. Based on the final selection of the most influential factors and the combination of result factors and effect factors, it can not only help policy makers to make more accurate and effective decisions, but also help them to propose relevant measures to further promote the sustainable development of agriculture. The contributions of this paper include the following: (1) A decision theory model combining PNS and WINGS-BWM is proposed, which can effectively sort out the complex relationship between the influencing factors and themselves in a fuzzy environment. (2) The model integrates three methods, which have not been combined in previous studies to analyze the interrelationships among the influencing factors, and enriches the related research in the field of decision theory. (3) This paper contributes to the advancement of the process of sustainable agricultural development, and also provides policy and management reference suggestions for relevant decision makers.

Overview of This Information Collection

    Abstract: 18 U.S.C. chapter 40 requires any person engaged in the 
business of manufacturing, importing, or dealing in explosive materials 
to first obtain a license. 18 U.S.C. 842(a)(1). The statute also 
requires all persons who wish to obtain explosives, even for limited 
use, to obtain a federal explosives license or a permit (to use 
explosives, rather than engage in the business). This also includes all 
persons who wish to ship, transport, receive, or possess explosive 
materials. 18 U.S.C. 843. ATF has prescribed ATF Form 5400.13, 
Application for Explosives License/Permit, as the means by which 
persons may apply for a federal explosives license or permit (FEL/P). 
Each person (individual, partnership, corporation, or association) 
applying for a FEL/P must submit ATF Form 5400.13. ATF uses the 
information on the application to determine if the applicant is 
qualified to be an explosives licensee/permittee under the statute. 
Part of the process of determining whether a person qualifies to be a 
licensee/permittee includes a background check to ascertain whether the 
person is prohibited from possessing or otherwise using explosive 
materials.

Revisions to This Information Collection

    As part of this ICR renewal, ATF has updated Form 5400.13 to 
reflect the new government payment method through pay.gov (required by 
executive order) and to include instructions and an email address for 
submitting the digitally fillable and signable form to ATF by email. 
ATF is also making a minor edit to the title, from ``Application for 
Federal Explosives License or Permit'' to ``Application for Explosives 
License/Permit'' for plain writing and brevity. In addition, ATF is 
changing the form number from 5400.13/5400.16 to 5400.13. There is no 
longer a need to retain the reference to a long-canceled merged form. 
Finally, there has been a decrease in the number of respondents to this 
IC, from 10,200 during the last renewal in 2023, to 927, a decrease of 
9,273 respondents. This resulted in a corresponding decrease to the 
number of burden hours, from 15,300 in 2023 to 1,390.5 in 2026, a 
decrease 13,909.5 hours.

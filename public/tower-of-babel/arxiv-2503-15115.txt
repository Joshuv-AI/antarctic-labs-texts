Superconducting diode effect in Ising superconductors
Itai Bankier ∗, Lotan Attias ∗, Alex Levchenko, Maxim Khodas

Abstract
We study the superconducting diode effect (SDE) in an Ising superconductor with broken basal mirror symmetry in a parallel magnetic field. We show that in the presence of a small Rashba spin splitting, Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} , the dominant Ising spin-orbit coupling ( Δ I ≫ Δ R \Delta_{\text{I}}\gg\Delta_{\text{R}} ) dramatically enhances the SDE efficiency compared to a Rashba superconductor with Δ I = 0 \Delta_{\text{I}}=0 and the same Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} . The suppression of the SDE for Δ I = 0 \Delta_{\text{I}}=0 at Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} much larger than the critical temperature ( T c T_{\text{c}} ) is accidental. At Δ R ≪ T c \Delta_{\text{R}}\ll T_{\text{c}} , the SDE efficiency is small because, to linear order, Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} can be removed by a gauge transformation. These two factors – the accidental suppression of the SDE at large Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} and the systematic suppression at small Δ R \Delta_{\text{R}} – are eliminated by Ising spin-orbit coupling. As a result, SDE efficiency is substantially enhanced for Δ I ≫ Δ R ≠ 0 \Delta_{\text{I}}\gg\Delta_{\text{R}}\neq 0 .

Introduction

The superconducting diode effect (SDE) signifies the nonreciprocity of the critical current
[
1
,
2
]
.
The SDE appears when all the unitary and anti-unitary symmetries that reverse the current (
\mathbf{J}
) are broken
[
3
]
.
The most common of these are the time reversal (
\mathcal{T}
) and spatial inversion (
\mathcal{P}
) symmetries.
In fact, the SDE was initially considered theoretically in noncentrosymmetric superconductors under an applied magnetic field
[
4
,
5
]
.

Interest in the SDE was renewed following its observation in [Nb/V/Ta]
n
superlattices
[
6
]
.
In subsequent years, the SDE has been reported in Josephson junctions
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10

and hybrid multilayer geometries
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
Furthermore, supercurrent rectification due to the anisotropy of the vortex pinning potential – such as the vortex ratchet effect – has been observed
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
.

The SDE is commonly quantified by the difference in critical currents for opposite current polarities,
J_{\text{c}\pm}
, using the efficiency parameter

\eta_{\mathrm{SDE}}=(J_{\text{c}+}+J_{\text{c}-})/(J_{\text{c}+}-J_{\text{c}-})
.
A key objective of current research is to identify setups that allow optimal control over SDE efficiency.

A paradigmatic example of a system exhibiting the SDE is the Rashba superconductor
[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
.
This system describes a two-dimensional superconductor confined to the
xy
-plane, where basal plane mirror symmetry (
m_{\hat{z}}
) is broken, and a parallel magnetic field
\mathbf{B}\perp\hat{z}
is applied.
Here,
m_{\hat{n}}
denotes mirror reflection in the plane perpendicular to a vector
\mathbf{n}
.
The broken
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry leads to a finite band spin splitting of
2\Delta_{\text{R}}
due to Rashba spin-orbit coupling (SOC)
[
30
]
.
As a consequence of the remaining
m_{\hat{B}}
symmetry, the SDE efficiency
\eta_{\mathrm{SDE}}
vanishes when the current flows parallel to the magnetic field (
\mathbf{J}\parallel\mathbf{B}
) and reaches its maximum when the current is perpendicular to the field (
\mathbf{J}\perp\mathbf{B}
).

In this work, we propose the Ising superconductor on a substrate subject to an in-plane magnetic field as a viable alternative to Rashba superconductors (see Fig.
1
).
In a pristine Ising superconductor, the Ising SOC polarizes spins out-of-plane (Fig.
2
b) and coexists with superconductivity
[
31
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
35
]
.
The substrate breaks the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry, leading to a finite Rashba spin splitting (Fig.
2

Our main finding is that for a given
\Delta_{\text{R}}\neq 0
, the introduction of Ising SOC significantly enhances the SDE efficiency compared to a Rashba superconductor.
In transition metal (M) dichalcogenide (X) monolayers, MX
2
, this amplification can realistically reach two orders of magnitude.

The enhancement of the SDE in Ising superconductors is somewhat counterintuitive because (1) Ising SOC strongly counteracts the in-plane field, and (2) it tends to restore the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry, which ordinarily prohibits the SDE.
To understand why, contrary to these expectations, Ising SOC strongly enhances the SDE, we revisit the dependence of the SDE on SOC in the Rashba model.
We analyze this separately in the limits of strong (
\kappa_{\text{R}}\gg 1
) and weak (
\kappa_{\text{R}}\ll 1
) Rashba SOC, where
\kappa_{\text{R}}=\Delta_{\text{R}}/\pi T_{\text{c}}
, and
T_{\text{c}}
is the superconducting transition temperature.
In what follows, we restrict ourselves to the Ginzburg-Landau (GL) regime of temperatures (
T
) close to
T_{\text{c}}
.
Generally, in the GL formalism, the SDE scales as
\eta\sim B\sqrt{T_{\text{c}}-T}

[
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

At strong Rashba SOC,
\kappa_{\text{R}}\gg 1
, the two-band quasi-classical approach
[
36
]
predicts a finite SDE
[
29
]
.
However, in the GL regime, it yields a vanishing result at leading order in
B\sqrt{T_{\text{c}}-T}
.
This vanishing SDE arises because of an accidental cancellation of various contributions to the critical current.

In the opposite limit,
\kappa_{\text{R}}\ll 1
, the SDE vanishes to linear order in Rashba SOC
[
38
]
.
Unlike the suppression in the strong Rashba limit, this vanishing SDE is not accidental but follows directly from gauge invariance.
To see this, recall that Rashba SOC can be interpreted as a coupling to a spin-dependent gauge field
[
39
,
40
]
.
In one-dimensional systems, this gauge field can be eliminated via a spin-dependent gauge transformation
[
41
]
,
resulting in an Aharonov-Casher phase
[
42
]
.
However, in two dimensions, this transformation is generally not possible, as the spin-orbit gauge field is non-Abelian
[
43
]
, except in special cases
[
44
]
.

Nevertheless, to first order in
\Delta_{\text{R}}
, the Rashba SOC can be eliminated using a specific gauge transformation
[
39
]
.
We argue that this transformation does not affect the local SU(2)-invariant spin-singlet pairing interaction.
However, it modifies the in-plane magnetic field, effectively converting it into a spatially varying out-of-plane field,

\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\propto\Delta_{\text{R}}\hat{z}(\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{r})
, which remains coupled exclusively to spins via the Zeeman interaction.

To first order in
\Delta_{\text{R}}
, the Rashba superconductor problem is thus gauge – equivalent to the problem of magnetization gradients studied in Ref.
[
45
]
.
In particular, it has been shown that magnetization gradients exhibit the same transformation properties as in-plane magnetic fields
and can be used as an alternative mechanism to generate the SDE
[
46
]
.

Crucially, a nonzero SOC is still required for a finite SDE in the presence of either magnetization gradients or an in-plane magnetic field.
At zero SOC, the spin-independent current
\mathbf{J}\perp\mathbf{B}
is reversed by the spatial mirror symmetry operation
m^{\prime}_{\hat{J}}
,
which acts on the orbital degrees of freedom without affecting spins.
Since the SOC has been eliminated to first order, this explains the absence of an SDE in the Rashba model at this order.
On a technical level, this result holds separately for all contributions to the critical current and to all orders in the magnetic field.

Ising SOC transforms nontrivially under the above gauge transformation, thereby invalidating the previous arguments based on gauge invariance.
In doing so, it prevents both the systematic and accidental suppression of the SDE.
This is the underlying physical reason for the substantial enhancement of SDE efficiency due to Ising SOC, as studied in this work.

We then discuss our findings in the context of existing SDE measurements in Sec.

II
Model formulation

To illustrate the amplification of the SDE by Ising SOC, we focus on a specific model of NbSe
2
monolayer.
We stress that qualitatively, our conclusions regarding the enhancement of SDE efficiency are entirely general for any MX
2
transition-metal dichalcogenide with
D_{3h}
symmetry.

In NbSe
2
bands that cross the Fermi level form the hole pockets centered at
\Gamma
and
K
(
K^{\prime}
) points in the hexagonal Brillouin zone.
Here, for simplicity, we only consider the pockets centered at
K
(
K^{\prime}
), see Fig.
2
.
The Hamiltonian
H=H_{K}+V_{\text{p}}
contains the single-band
K
(
K^{\prime}
) pocket Hamiltonian
H_{K}
of non-interacting electrons, and the Cooper pairing Hamiltonian
V_{\text{p}}
.

We introduce the operators
c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\eta s}
that create the electrons with momentum
\mathbf{k}
in the pocket
K
(
K^{\prime}
) distinguished by the pocket subscript
\eta=+1
and
\eta=-1
, respectively, with spin
s
along
\hat{z}
.
Here the momentum is counted relative to the
K
(
K^{\prime}
) as determined by the subscript
\eta
.
We write
H_{K}=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\eta s}c^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}\eta s}[\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}\eta}]_{ss^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}\eta s^{\prime}}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{0}+\mathcal{H}^{\text{I}}+\mathcal{H}^{\text{R}}+\mathcal{H}^{\text{Z}}\,.

(1)

In Eq. (
1
),
\mathcal{H}^{0}
represents the spin-independent part of the band dispersion, which in the effective mass approximation takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{0}_{\mathbf{k}\eta}=k^{2}/2m+\eta\Delta_{\text{W}}\left(k/k_{\text{F}}\right)^{3}\cos(3 \varphi)\,,

(2)

It includes in addition to the parabolic dispersion, the trigonal warping
\Delta_{\text{W}}
, see Fig.
2
a.
Although often
\Delta_{\text{W}}
is negligible we retain it for a comparison of the SDE in parallel and perpendicular orientations of the magnetic field.
In Eq. (
2
)

is the Fermi momentum, at zero SOC and magnetic field, and
\cos\varphi=\hat{k}\cdot\hat{x}
.

The next two terms of Eq. (
1
),
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}
are the Ising (
\ell=\text{I}
) and Rashba (
\ell=\text{R}
) SOC, respectively.
We parametrize these terms in a standard way,
\mathcal{H}^{\ell}_{\mathbf{k}\eta}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\ell}(\mathbf{k}\eta)\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, where
\boldsymbol{\sigma}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})
is the vector of Pauli matrices that act in spin space.

The Ising SOC complying to the hexagonal lattice symmetry is shown in Fig.
2
b.
Close to the
K
(
K^{\prime}
) pockets, it simplifies to
\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\mathrm{I}}(\mathbf{k}\eta)=\eta\Delta_{\text{I}}\hat{z}
.

When the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry is broken, e.g. by a substrate or by an out-of-plane electric field, the reduction of the
D_{3h}
symmetry down to
C_{3v}
allows for Rashba SOC which close to the
K
(
K^{\prime}
) pockets takes the form

\boldsymbol{\gamma}^{\text{R}}(\mathbf{k}\eta)=\alpha_{\text{R}}\left(\mathbf{k}\times\hat{z}\right)
, see Fig.
2
c.
Finally, the Zeeman interaction
\mathcal{H}^{\text{Z}}=\mathbf{B}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}
, where the product of the
g
-factor and Bohr magneton,
\mu_{\mathrm{B}}
has been absorbed by
\mathbf{B}
.

The local spin singlet pairing interaction,

\displaystyle V_{\text{p}}=\frac{g}{4}\sum_{\mathbf{q}}\hat{P}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\dagger}\hat{P}_{\mathbf{q}};\,\hat{P}_{\mathbf{q}}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\eta,s,s^{\prime}}c_{-\mathbf{k}\bar{\eta}s}\left[i\sigma_{y}\right]^{\dagger}_{ss^{\prime}}c_{\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\,\eta s^{\prime}}

(3)

is attractive,
g<0
.
In the
K
(
K^{\prime}
)-model the pairing, Eq. (
3
) is intervalley as signified by
\bar{\eta}=-\eta
index.

III
SDE coefficient in GL phenomenology

Here we summarize the way to determine the efficiency of SDE within the GL phenomenology.
To this end, one considers the order parameter
\Delta

which is periodic in spatial coordinates,
\mathbf{r}
with a definite Cooper pair momentum,
\mathbf{q}
,
\Delta(\mathbf{r})=\Delta e^{i\mathbf{q}\mathbf{r}}
.
For a given
\mathbf{q}
and magnetic field, the GL free energy density reads
[
47
]
,

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})=\alpha(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})\Delta^{2}+\beta(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B)}\Delta^{4}\,.

(4)

Finite SDE arises due to the asymmetry,
f(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})\neq f(-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})
.
Such asymmetry arises from the odd powers in the expansion of the GL coefficients
[
48
]
,

\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{r}\left(\mathbf{B},\hat{q}\right)q^{r},\,\,\,\,\beta=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\beta_{r}\left(\mathbf{B},\hat{q}\right)q^{r}\,

(5)

also known as anomalous terms.
In contrast to the even power terms of expansion (
5
), known as normal, the anomalous terms require breaking of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}
symmetries.
Therefore, anomalous contributions are odd in
\mathbf{B}
.
For in-plane
\mathbf{B}
this implies that
m_{\hat{z}}
has to be broken for finite anomalous terms.
For an out-of-plane
\mathbf{B}
the rotation by
\pi
around
\hat{z}
-axis,
C_{2z}=\mathcal{P}m_{\hat{z}}
has to be broken while
m_{\hat{z}}
may be allowed.
In result, for an in-plane
\mathbf{B}
configuration Rashba SOC is necessary for SDE, while for an out-of-plane field the Ising SOC is sufficient.

Lifshitz invariants,
\alpha_{1}(\mathbf{B},\hat{q})q
in Eq. (
5
), are the most studied type of anomalous terms known for some noncetrosymmetric superconductors
[
49
,
50
]
.
These terms drive the superconductor into the helical state with a finite Cooper pair momentum
\mathbf{q}_{0}
with zero current
[
51
,
52
]
.
In a ring geometry Lifshitz invariant shifts the Little-Parks oscillations
[
53
]
.
Generally, Lifshitz invariant with finite curl produces a large number of observable effects
[
54
]
.
In particular, in films, Lifshitz invariants produce a thickness-dependent SDE
[
55
]
.
Here we focus on planar setups such as atomic monolayers, where the Lifshitz invariant alone causes no SDE, because the free energy, (
4
) is symmetric with respect to
\mathbf{q}_{0}
,
f(\mathbf{q}_{0}+\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})=f(\mathbf{q}_{0}-\mathbf{q},\mathbf{B})
.

Within the GL formalism the SDE efficiency reads
[
38
]

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{SDE}}\!=\!\frac{2\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\beta_{0}\!-\!4\alpha_{1}\alpha_{4}\beta_{0}\!-\!\alpha_{2}^{2}\beta_{1}\!+\!\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}}{2\sqrt{3}\alpha_{2}^{5/2}\beta_{0}}\sqrt{\!-\alpha_{0}},

(6)

where the arguments of
\alpha,\beta
are suppressed for brevity.
Here we compute the expansion coefficients in Eq. (
6
) to first order in
B
.

In order to compute the SDE efficiency, we use the standard expressions for the GL coefficients introduced in Eq. (
4
),

\alpha=1/|g|-\Phi_{2}/2
, and
\beta=\Phi_{4}/4
in terms of the correlation functions,

\displaystyle\Phi_{2m}=\sum_{\mathbf{k},\eta,\varepsilon_{n}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\sigma_{y}G(\mathbf{k}+\mathbf{q}\,\eta,i\varepsilon_{n})\sigma_{y}\overline{G}(\mathbf{k}\eta,i\varepsilon_{n})\right]^{m}\,,

(7)

where the Green functions,

G\left(\mathbf{k}\eta,\varepsilon_{n}\right)=\left(i\varepsilon_{n}-\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}\eta}\right)^{-1}
,
\overline{\mathcal{G}}\left(\mathbf{k}\eta,\varepsilon_{n}\right)=G^{tr}\left(-\mathbf{k}\bar{\eta},-\varepsilon_{n}\right)
, and the Matsubara frequency
\varepsilon_{n}=2\pi T\left(n+1/2\right)
are standard.

By making an expansion of Eq. (
7
) in
q
up to fourth order and up to first order in
B
we identify all the coefficients needed to compute the SDE efficiency based on Eq. (
6
).
Similar to
[
38
]
, we approach the problem in terms of three parameters,

\displaystyle\delta=\alpha_{\text{R}}/v_{\text{F}},\,\,\,\,\,\kappa_{\text{R}}=\alpha_{\text{R}}k_{\text{F}}/\pi T_{\text{c}},\,\,\,\,\,\kappa_{\text{I}}=\Delta_{\text{I}}/\pi T_{\text{c}},

(8)

and evaluate the anomalous GL coefficients to first order in
\delta\ll 1
.
The other two parameters of the problem satisfy
\kappa_{\text{R,I}}\gg\delta
, and are otherwise arbitrary.
The latter condition implies that the Fermi energy,
E_{\text{F}}\gg T_{\text{c}}
.

III.1
GL coefficients for in-plane magnetic field

The normal GL coefficients are even in
B
according to
\mathcal{T}
symmetry, and therefore can be evaluated at
B=0
.
They are also not affected by SOC to zero order in
\delta
.
The resulting expressions are standard,

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}=\frac{1}{|g|}-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{2\nu_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}}\,,

(9)

where
\nu_{0}=m/\pi
is the density of states per spin includes the contributions of both
K
and
K^{\prime}
pockets.

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}v_{\text{F}}^{2}}{4\varepsilon_{n}^{3}}\,,\,\,\alpha_{4}=-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{3\nu_{0}v_{\text{F}}^{4}}{64\varepsilon_{n}^{5}}\,,

(10)

where
v_{\text{F}}=k_{\text{F}}/m
is the Fermi velocity at zero SOC, Zeeman interaction and trigonal warping.
The remaining normal terms entering the expression of the SDE efficiency are

\displaystyle\beta_{0}=\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}}{2\varepsilon_{n}^{3}}\,,\,\,\,\beta_{2}=-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{3\nu_{0}v_{\text{F}}^{2}}{8\varepsilon_{n}^{5}}\,.

(11)

We now turn to anomalous coefficients. The Lifshitz invariant

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}=\left(\mathbf{B}\crossproduct\hat{\mathbf{q}}\right)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\alpha_{\text{R}}\left(\frac{2\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}\right)(\alpha_{1}^{\text{intra}}+\alpha_{1}^{\text{inter}})

(12)

naturally splits into two distinct contributions,

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{\text{intra}}=

\displaystyle-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}^{3}}\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{\text{inter}}=

\displaystyle\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}}{\varepsilon_{n}\left(\pi^{2}T^{2}\kappa^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right)}\,,

(13b)

where
\kappa=\sqrt{\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}
.
The intraband contribution, Eq. (
13a
) results from the pairing of particles on the outer (inner) Fermi pockets.
The second, interband contribution Eq. (
13b
) arises from pairing of particles at inner and outer Fermi surfaces, see Fig.
2
a.
Unlike the intraband contributions, the interband contribution is suppressed in the limit of strong SOC,
\kappa\gg 1
.
Note that the two contributions cancel each other out in the limit
\kappa=0
.
This cancellation is not accidental and follows from the gauge invariance as discussed in Sec.
I
.

Similarly to the Lifshitz invariant, Eq. (
13
), we present the third order anomalous term as a sum of the intra- and interband contributions,

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}=\left(\mathbf{B}\crossproduct\hat{\mathbf{q}}\right)\!\cdot\!\hat{\mathbf{z}}\alpha_{\text{R}}\left(\frac{4\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}\right)\left(\alpha_{3}^{\text{intra}}+\alpha_{3}^{\text{inter}}\right),

(14)

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}^{\text{intra}}=\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{3\nu_{0}v_{\text{F}}^{2}}{8\varepsilon_{n}^{5}}\,,

(15a)

\displaystyle\alpha_{3}^{\text{inter}}=

\displaystyle-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}v_{\text{F}}^{2}}{16\varepsilon_{n}^{3}\left[\pi^{2}T^{2}\kappa^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right]^{3}}

\displaystyle\times\left[6\varepsilon_{n}^{4}+3\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\pi^{2}T^{2}\kappa^{2}+\pi^{4}T^{4}\kappa^{4}\right]\,.

(15b)

The remaining anomalous coefficient
\beta_{1}
reads,

\displaystyle\beta_{1}=\left(\mathbf{B}\crossproduct\hat{\mathbf{q}}\right)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\alpha_{\text{R}}\left(\frac{2\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}\right)\left(\beta_{3}^{\text{intra}}+\beta_{3}^{\text{inter}}\right),

(16)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{\text{intra}}=\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{3\nu_{0}}{2\varepsilon_{n}^{5}}\,,

(17a)

\displaystyle\beta_{1}^{\text{inter}}=-\pi T\sum_{\varepsilon_{n}>0}\frac{\nu_{0}\left[\pi^{2}T^{2}\kappa^{2}+3\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right]}{2\varepsilon_{n}^{3}\left[\pi^{2}T^{2}\kappa^{2}+\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\right]^{2}}\,.

(17b)

Similar to the Lifshitz invariant, the intra- and interband contributions to
\alpha_{3}
and
\beta_{1}
cancel each other out for
\kappa=0
.

III.2
SDE efficiency

Having calculated all the necessary GL coefficients, we use Eq. (
6
) to write the SDE efficiency as

\displaystyle\eta_{\mathrm{SDE}}=\frac{\left(\mathbf{B}\crossproduct\mathbf{\hat{q}}\right)\cdot\hat{\mathbf{z}}\delta}{7\pi T_{\text{c}}\sqrt{42\zeta(3)^{3}}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{\text{c}}-T}{T_{\text{c}}}}f\left(\kappa_{\text{I}},\kappa_{\text{R}}\right)\,.

(18)

The function
f\left(\kappa_{\text{I}},\kappa_{\text{R}}\right)
is a measure of the SDE efficience.
We present it for different fixed values of
\kappa_{\text{I}}
(
\kappa_{\text{R}}

(

In the same way as with the GL coefficients, the function
f\left(\kappa_{\text{I}},\kappa_{\text{R}}\right)
splits into intra- and interband parts,

The intraband contribution takes the form,

\displaystyle f^{\text{intra}}=

\displaystyle 186\zeta(5)\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}/\left(\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}\right)\,,

(19a)

and the interband part reads,

\displaystyle f^{\text{inter}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa^{4}}\Re{ \psi^{(2)} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{i \kappa}{2} \right) }+14\frac{\zeta(3)}{\kappa^{4}}\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}

\displaystyle+16\frac{2\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa^{6}}\Re{ \psi^{(0)} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{i\kappa}{2} \right) - \psi^{(0)} \left( \frac{1}{2}\right) }

\displaystyle+8\frac{2\kappa_{\text{I}}^{2}+\kappa_{\text{R}}^{2}}{\kappa^{5}}\Re{ i\psi^{(1)} \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{i\kappa}{2} \right) }\,,

(19b)

with
\psi^{(n)}
being the n-th polygamma function.

In a Rashba superconductor,
\kappa_{\text{I}}=0

\kappa_{\text{I}}=3\kappa_{\text{R}}
.

At large SOC,

In the opposite limit,
\kappa\lesssim 1

It tends to cancel the intraband contribution at small
\kappa

\alpha_{1}

\alpha_{3}

\beta_{1}

IV
Discussion

We have proposed a mechanism for the SDE in Ising superconductors, specifically for an in-plane magnetic field configuration.
This mechanism is distinct from the SDE driven by a perpendicular field,
\mathbf{B}_{\perp}\parallel\hat{z}
, recently observed in few-layer NbSe
2

[
56
]
.
In that realization, the superconducting NbSe
2
is symmetrically encapsulated by h-BN from both the top and bottom, preserving the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry and preventing Rashba SOC.
The observed finite
\eta^{\perp}_{\mathrm{SDE}}
in this experiment is a manifestation of normal-state magnetochiral anisotropy
[
57
,
58
,
23
,
59
]
.

The crossover from Ising-dominated (
\kappa_{\text{I}}>\kappa_{\text{R}}
) to Rashba-dominated (
\kappa_{\text{R}}>\kappa_{\text{I}}
) regimes has been reported in epitaxial Bi
2
Se
3
/monolayer NbSe
2
heterostructures
[
60
]
.
This system has been reported to be superconducting and could serve as a natural platform for realizing an enhanced SDE.

NbSe
2
monolayer on a hexagonal boron-nitride layer on a SiO
2
/Si substrate exhibits a six-fold angular variation of the in-plane critical field
[
61
]
.
When the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry is intact, the system is isotropic with respect to the orientation of the in-plane field
[
62
]
.
Therefore, this provides indirect evidence of the Rashba SOC in this heterostructure
[
37
]
.

Rashba SOC can be introduced in an Ising superconductor monolayer by gating as well.
This produces an out-of-plane electric field, breaking the
m_{\hat{z}}
symmetry.
This has been achieved in MoSe
2
and MoS
2
monolayers
[
63
]
.

To estimate a minimal Rashba SOC required for obtaining a noticeable SDE we make comparison of the measured SDE in a perpendicular field reported in Ref.
[
56
]
.
In this case, the SDE due to the spin degrees of freedom follows from the trigonal warping term
\propto\Delta_{\text{W}}
in Eq. (
2
).
The GL formalism is easily adapted to this scenario.
The only anomalous term allowed by
D_{3h}
symmetry,

\alpha_{3}^{\perp}\propto B_{\perp}(q_{x}^{3}-3q_{x}q_{y}^{2})/q^{3}
has been computed in Ref.
[
58
]
.
This gives, using Eq. (
6
),

\eta^{\perp}_{\mathrm{SDE}}=\frac{124\sqrt{6/7}\zeta(5)}{28\sqrt{\zeta(3)^{3}}\pi}\frac{\Delta_{\text{I}}\Delta_{\text{W}}}{T_{\text{c}}E_{\text{F}}^{2}}\!B_{\perp}\!\frac{\left(q_{x}^{3}-3q_{x}q_{y}^{2}\right)}{q^{3}}\sqrt{\frac{T_{\text{c}}-T}{T_{\text{c}}}},

(20)

where the numerical prefactor
\approx 1.03
.
Next we compare the efficiency, Eq. (
20
), to the efficiency in the parallel field,
\mathbf{B}_{\parallel}\perp\hat{z}
summarized in Sec.
III.2
.
For definiteness, we consider
\mathbf{q}\parallel\hat{x}
and
\mathbf{B}_{\parallel}\parallel\hat{y}
.
Normally, Ising SOC is strong,
\kappa_{\text{I}}\gg 1
, and the interband contribution, Eq. (
19b
) is negligible:
f^{\text{intra}}\gg f^{\text{inter}}
.
As the strong Ising SOC dominates the Rashba SOC,
\kappa_{\text{I}}\gg\kappa_{\text{R}}
, the SDE efficiency is given by Eq. (
18
) with
f(\kappa_{\text{I}},\kappa_{\text{R}})\approx 186\zeta(5)
.
The ratio of the SDE efficiencies at parallel and perpendicular field orientations,

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\text{SDE}}}{\eta^{\perp}_{\text{SDE}}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{B_{\parallel}}{B_{\perp}}\frac{E_{\text{F}}}{\Delta_{\text{I}}}\frac{\Delta_{\text{R}}}{\Delta_{\text{W}}}\,,

(21)

is fixed by the ratio of Rashba spin splitting and trigonal warping; and the ratio of the Fermi energy and the Ising spin splitting.
A characteristic Ising SOC for TMDs is
\Delta_{\text{I}}\approx 0.05-$0.12\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}$

[
35
]
, and their Fermi level exceeded
E_{\text{F}}>$1\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}$

[
64
,
65
]
. Thus we conservatively estimate the first ratio
E_{\text{F}}/\Delta_{\text{I}}\gtrsim 10
.

Based on Density Functional Theory, Ref.
[
66
]
reports
C_{3w}=\Delta_{\text{W}}/k_{\text{F}}^{3}\approx$5\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
for the valence band of MoS
2
and MoSe
2
.
The wavevector,
\mathbf{k}_{K}
at
K
point satisfies
\absolutevalue{\vb{k}_K}=4\pi a^{-1}/3\approx$1.3\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
for a characteristic lattice constant of
a=$3.2\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolAngstrom}$
.
Since our model holds for small Fermi surfaces, we set
k_{\text{F}}=0.1\absolutevalue{\vb{k}_\text{K}}
, and estimate
\Delta_{\text{W}}\approx$11\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{eV}$
.

Therefore, for the parallel field efficiency to exceed the perpendicular efficiency in Eq. (
21
),
\Delta_{\text{R}}\gtrsim$2\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}$
is required.
According to Ref.
[
63
]
the Rashba splitting greatly exceeds the above requirement in gated MoS
2
and MoSe
2
.
Ion gating is known to induce superconductivity in MoS
2

[
31
,
34
,
32
]
.
This shows that our setup is a realistic way to achieve a large SDE in parallel magnetic field, e.g. in gated MoS
2
.

V
Summary

In summary, we have proposed the two-dimensional Ising superconductor as a platform for the SDE with large efficiency driven by an in-plane magnetic field.
Similar to the Rashba superconductor, finite Rashba spin splitting is necessary for finite SDE.
Such splitting is achieved experimentally by growing the Ising superconductor on a substrate or by gating.

With minimal Rashba splitting, the Ising superconductor produces an SDE with efficiency that greatly exceeds the maximal efficiency of the Rashba superconductor.
Moreover, realistically it can be comparable or greater than the efficiency in a perpendicular magnetic field.

In this work, we focus on homogeneous systems.
It is of interest to implore the possibility of the SDE in the Josephson junction geometry.
The in-plane exchange field has been suggested to strongly affect junctions with an Ising superconductor sandwiched by two ferromagnetic electrodes
[
67
]
.
In this case, the properties of the junction are affected by the triplet correlation induced by the in-plane field into an otherwise singlet superconductor
[
68
,
69
]
.
It is of interest to explore these research directions in the future.

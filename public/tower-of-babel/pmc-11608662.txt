High C-reactive protein is associated with the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer
Authors: Jiawei Hu, Zixuan Luo, Junlong Song, Deguang Kong, Zhiyu Li, Chuang Chen, Shengrong Sun
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
C-reactive protein (CRP) is a nonspecific biomarker for systemic inflammatory response and is linked to the prognosis of breast cancer (BC); however, few studies have investigated the correlation between CRP and the effectiveness of neoadjuvant chemotherapy treatment for BC. We recruited 177 patients with BC who underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy in our clinical trial. the median CRP level (0.24 mg/L), patients were categorized into high and low groups. We examined the relationship between CRP levels and various clinicopathological factors, including pathological complete response (pCR), using the chi-square test or Fisher exact test. Furthermore, we evaluated the predictive capacity of CRP for different molecular subtypes by constructing receiver operating characteristic curves. To identify the independent variables associated with pCR, we conducted logistic regression multivariate analysis. No association was found between C-reactive levels at baseline and pCR rates. CRP level was significantly associated with higher body mass index, and the high CRP group had more overweight patients (47.06% vs. 16.30%, P < .001). In hormone receptor-positive patients, the high CRP group demonstrated a significantly higher pCR rate (OR = 4.115, 95% CI: 1.481–11.36, P = .009). The areas under the curve was 0.670 (95% CI: 0.550–0.792, P < .001). Multivariate logistic analysis showed that the CRP level was a significant independent predictor of pCR (OR = 5.882, 95% CI: 1.470–28.57, P = .017). High CRP levels were found to be associated with a higher pCR rate, indicating their independent predictive value in determining the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy in hormone receptor-positive BC patients.

1. Introduction

Breast cancer (BC) is a prevalent and serious concern that endangers human health. In 2020, BC became the cancer with the highest global incidence, surpassing that of lung cancer. The incidence of BC has been increasing annually, accounting for 30% of all cancer cases.[1–3]

Recent advances in cancer therapy have shown that surgery combined with radiotherapy and chemotherapy can significantly improve patient survival rates.[4] Neoadjuvant chemotherapy (NACT) is a commonly used and beneficial therapeutic approach for BC. It is particularly valuable for patients in the middle or late stages of the disease, as it can effectively reduce the tumor size and stage. In doing so, it enables the possibility of breast conservation surgery instead of mastectomy without increasing the risk of recurrence after surgery.[5] Moreover, NACT plays a critical role in establishing an effective treatment strategy to confirms therapeutic efficacy.[6] However, while current guidelines provide a standard NACT protocol, different patients have varying therapeutic outcomes. Pathological complete response (pCR) is a key prognostic indicator in BC, particularly in assessing the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy. Achieving pCR is associated with improved long-term survival outcomes.[7] Thus, finding a sensitive and effective predictor of NACT efficacy is essential for predicting the benefits of NACT and developing better therapeutic measures.

Inflammation, a crucial response to injury and infection, has been recognized as one of several risk factors associated with BC, including obesity, heredity, estrogen levels, and dietary habits, associated with BC.[8] Approximately 25% of human cancers are associated with inflammation, and tumor-associated inflammation contributes to all stages of tumor development.[9,10] C-reactive protein (CRP), which is produced in the liver, is a nonspecific biomarker of systemic inflammation.[11] It is a highly sensitive acute-phase reactant, with circulating levels rapidly increasing when inflammation, infection, and injury happen.[12] However, the CRP level of CRP also closely correlated with the outcomes of certain types of tumors, including urinary carcinoma, gastroenteric tumors, lung cancer, and hematological tumor.[13–17] In healthy women, increased baseline CRP levels have been associated with a higher risk of BC.[18] Among patients with BC, compared to lower CRP concentrations, higher CRP levels have induced an approximately 2-fold increased risk of mortality and a 67% elevated risk of further BC-related incidents.[19] However, few studies have investigated the association between CRP level and the efficacy of NACT for BC.

Therefore, we conducted a retrospective study including 177 patients with BC who underwent NACT to explore the predictive role of CRP. We evaluated the effectiveness of CRP in predicting the benefits of NACT and its predictive value for various molecular subtypes of BC. Additionally, we developed a prediction model based on our findings.

2. Methods

2.1. Participants

A total of 177 patients receiving NACT and subsequent surgical treatment at Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University from December 1, 2019, to May 1, 2022, were enrolled in the clinical trial. Informed consent was obtained from all patients included in the analysis, and the study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Renmin Hospital of Wuhan University on December 4, 2022 (Ethics Vote No: WDRY2022-K228). Individuals were selected based on the following inclusion and exclusion criteria.

The eligibility criteria for the patients were as follows: (1) confirmed diagnosis of BC through core needle biopsy before initiation of NACT at our hospital from December 1, 2019 to May 1, 2022; (2) collection of peripheral blood samples within 1 week prior to the start of NACT; (3) receipt of at least 5e cycles of standard NACT; (4) no evidence of distant metastasis; and (5) surgery performed at our hospital after completing NACT.

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) underwent core needle biopsy or surgery at other hospitals, (2) no peripheral blood samples collected before NACT, (3) received less than 5 cycles of chemotherapy, (4) metastases in distant organs, (5) no efficacy evaluation after surgery, and (6) with a history of chronic inflammatory diseases, autoimmune disorders and other tumors.

2.2. Treatment

In all cases, NACT was administered following diagnosis, and chemotherapy regimens were determined based on the Chinese Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines, immunohistochemistry results, and patient preferences. Most human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive patients received the TCb (HP) regimen consisting of taxane (T) and carboplatin (Cb), with herceptin (H) and/or pertuzumab (P) for targeted therapy. The treatment consisted of 6 cycles, with 1 cycle every 3 weeks. The TCb (HP) regimen included nab-paclitaxel 400 mg or docetaxel 80 to 100 mg/m2, carboplatin areas under the curve (AUC) = 6, herceptin 6 mg/kg (8 mg/kg for the first time), and pertuzumab 420 mg (840 mg the first time). All HER2-negative patients and a few HER2-positive patients received sequential chemotherapy with AC-T, namely anthracycline (A) and cyclophosphamide (C) every 3 weeks for a total of 4 cycles, followed by 4 cycles of taxane every 3 weeks. Capecitabine (X) was administered for intensive treatment. The AC-T(X) regimen included doxorubicin (60 mg/m2 or pharmorubicin 75–100 mg/m2 plus cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m2), nab-paclitaxel (400 mg or docetaxel 80–100 mg/m2), and capecitabine (1000 mg/m2). All regimens consisted of at least 5 cycles regardless of dose reduction or delay. Surgery was performed approximately 1 week after NACT completion based on the patient’s condition.

2.3. Categorical criteria and efficacy evaluation

Tumor size was determined using magnetic resonance imaging to measure the maximum diameter of the tumor. Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height in meters squared (m2). The pathological type was classified as invasive ductal carcinoma or other based on the World Health Organization classification. Histological tumor grade was evaluated according to the Nottingham system and the Elston–Ellis grading method. Fine-needle aspiration biopsy was performed to diagnose lymph node metastasis. Hormone receptor+ (HR+) was defined as estrogen receptor+ and/or progesterone receptor+, indicating more than 1% nuclear staining.[20] HER2 + was defined as HER2 immunohistochemical grade 3+ or HER2 immunohistochemical grade 2+, as well as positive for HER2 fluorescence in situ hybridization amplification. The molecular subtypes of BC were categorized as luminal (HR+/HER2±), HER2-enriched (HR−/HER2+), and triple-negative (HR−/HER2−).[21] The clinical staging was performed according to the American Joint Committee on Cancer 8th edition criteria, incorporating imaging studies and physical examinations. pCR was defined as the complete disappearance of invasive cancer in the breast and the absence of tumors in the axillary lymph nodes, which corresponds to ypT0N0 in the American Joint Committee on Cancer 8th edition pathological stage.[22] The presence of micrometastases in the pCR group was evaluated through immunohistochemical staining techniques.

2.4. Statistics and analysis

Data analysis was performed using GraphPad (version 9.4.1) and SPSS (version 26.0). The chi-square test or Fisher exact test was used to compare categorical variables with respect to clinicopathological characteristics. Receiver operating characteristic curves were used to evaluate the predictive ability of CRP for each molecular subtype, with calculation of the AUCs. Significant parameters identified through univariate analysis were integrated into a multivariate logistic regression model to ascertain the independent factors influencing NACT efficacy. Statistical significance was determined using two-tailed tests, and the results were considered significant at P < .05.

3. Results

3.1. Baseline characteristics

A total of 177 patients with BC who had received NACT were recruited for this retrospective study based on the inclusion and exclusion criteria and their age at diagnosis ranged from 22 to 72 years (mean = 49.9 years). These patients were divided into high CRP (>0.24 mg/L) and low CRP (≤0.24 mg/L) groups according to the median CRP (shown in Fig. 1). BMI was classified according to WHO standards as normal weight if BMI ≤ 25 kg/m2 or overweight if BMI > 25 kg/m2, with 68.93% and 31.07% of patients in each group, respectively. Among these patients, 70 (39.55%) had HR− tumors, 34 (19.21%) were HER2+, and 36 (20.34%) were HER2−. Additionally, 107 (60.45%) patients had HR+ tumors, with 35 (19.77%) HER2+ and 72 (40.68%) HER2− tumors. Moreover, 159 (89.83%) patients had tumors that were 5 cm or smaller and 18 (10.17%) had tumors larger than 5 cm. Most patients (91.53%) had invasive ductal carcinoma. Furthermore, 108 (61.02%) tumors were histologically grade 3, while the remaining were grade 1 to 2. Of the 177 patients, 133 (75.14%) underwent mastectomy and 44 (24.86%) underwent breast-conserving surgery. After NACT and surgery, 68 (38.42%) patients achieved pCR, while 109 (61.58%) did not (shown in Table 1).

Our analysis of clinical data using the chi-square test revealed no significant differences in pCR rates between the low and high CRP groups. Specifically, the pCR rates were 34.78% and 42.35% in the low and high CRP groups, respectively (P = .379). However, we observed a significant association between CRP levels and higher BMI, as well as larger tumor size. The high group had more overweight patients (47.06% vs. 16.30%, P < .001) (shown in Table 1).

3.2. Predictive value of CRP for different molecular subtypes

Many studies have reported that molecular subtype differences among patients with BC could impact the efficacy of NACT.[23–25] In luminal patients (HR+/HER2±), the high CRP group exhibited a significantly higher pCR rate than the low group (OR = 4.115, 95% CI: 1.481–11.36, P = .009), accounting for 33.96% and 11.11%, respectively. However, no differences in pCR rates were observed between the high and low CRP groups for the HER2-enriched and triple-negative subtypes (shown in Table 2 and Fig. 2).

Our findings revealed that in HR+/HER2 ± patients, CRP level exhibited a significant predictive value, with a P-value < .001. The AUC was 0.670 (95% CI: 0.550–0.790), indicating moderate accuracy. 3.3. Univariate and multivariate analysis of factors associated with pCR in HR+ BC patients

Univariate analysis was performed to examine the correlation between clinicopathological factors and the efficacy of NACT. The significant characteristics identified in the univariate analysis were included in the multivariate logistic regression model. Univariate analyses showed that BMI, histological grade, HER2 status, and CRP level were significantly associated with NACT efficacy. In the multivariate logistic analysis, HER2 status (OR = 19.60, 95% CI: 5.721–84.28, P < .001) and CRP group (OR = 5.882, 95% CI: 1.470–28.57, P = .017) emerged as independent predictors of pCR in HR+ patients. However, BMI and histological grade were not statistically significant predictors of a pCR (shown in Table 4 and Fig. 3).

Based on the results of the multivariate logistic analysis, we developed a predictive model incorporating BMI, histological grade, HER2 status, and CRP group: logit (probability of pCR after NACT) = 0.915 (BMI) + 0.934 (histological grade) + 2.976 (HER2 status) + 1.771 (CRP group)–2.94. The Hosmer–Lemeshow test was conducted to assess the goodness-of-fit of the model, and yielded a P-value of .785, indicating a good fit for the model. Furthermore, the predictive value of this model was superior to that of CRP alone, as shown by receiver operating characteristic analysis (AUC = 0.887, 95% CI: 0.814–0.960, P < .001).

4. Discussion

In BC, molecular markers such as estrogen receptor, progesterone receptor, and HER2 play crucial roles in guiding treatment efficacy and prognosis.[26] These markers assist in determining treatment strategies and predicting clinical outcomes. Additionally, an increasing number of inflammatory biomarkers, including interleukin-6 (IL-6), serum amyloid A (SAA), and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, have been shown to correlate with tumor progression and therapeutic response in various cancers.[27–30] In our study, we observed that BC patients with initially elevated CRP levels, specifically those with HR-positive tumors, were more likely to achieve pCR following neoadjuvant chemotherapy, with an approximately 3-fold increase compared to that in the low CRP group. Furthermore, multivariate analysis identified CRP as an independent predictive factor for treatment response, underscoring its potential utility in clinical settings. As an inflammatory biomarker, CRP has been increasingly studied in kinds of cancer, with evidence suggesting its association with prognosis and treatment outcomes. Notably, our findings highlight CRP’s potential as a valuable tool in assessing the efficacy of neoadjuvant chemotherapy, offering an additional predictive marker that could improve personalized treatment strategies for BC patients.

There are few studies on CRP as a predictor of chemoradiotherapy outcomes in BC. One investigation involving 152 early BC patients analyzed baseline CRP levels and the post-NACT pCR rate, finding no significant predictive value.[31] However, in other tumor types, CRP has been identified as a predictive and prognostic indicator. For instance, a study on gastric cancer demonstrated that patients with CRP levels ≥ 3.1 mg/L exhibited a more pronounced response to adjuvant chemotherapy.[32] Additionally, CRP activity can be potentiated by niclosamide in lung cancer, where it promotes ROS-mediated apoptosis in cancer cells. A pan-cancer analysis further revealed that patients with a sudden rise followed by a decrease in initial CRP levels or consistently low CRP levels had a higher likelihood of favorable immune therapy responses and survival outcomes. Nonetheless, the clinical application of CRP as a predictive biomarker requires further validation in clinical trials to confirm its utility across cancer types.

Moreover, we found a positive correlation between CRP level and BMI. We speculated that CRP might mediate the association between BC and obesity. Adipose tissue is an active metabolic organ that produces proinflammatory cytokines, such as IL-6 and TNF, which play a role in the induction and progression of BC both locally and systemically. Additionally, while CRP is mainly produced by the liver, adipose tissue can release it in response to elevated cytokine levels.[33] A cohort study indicated similar results; the overall risk ratio of CRP for BC in people with BMI ≥ 30 was higher than that in people with normal BMI. These results suggest that CRP level serves as an intermediary in the relationship between high BMI and cancer risk.[34] These connections are more prominent in postmenopausal women. A meta-analysis revealed a stronger positive correlation between CRP and BC risk in each subgroup with elevated BMI, with postmenopausal women showing the most substantial 17% increase in risk,[35] which means that hormone levels and receptor status may also influence the relationship between CRP and BC.

In our research, it’s the first clinical trial to confirm that CRP has predictive value for NACT efficacy in BC, and that we have constructed a predictive model, filling the gap in this field. However, we observed a significant relationship only in HR + BC patients. A separate study including 506 BC patients and 506 controls also suggested that the association between CRP and BC risk in the HR+/HER2− group was even more significant,[36] which may be attributed to the interplay between CRP and sex hormones. Elevated CRP levels have been associated with higher estrogen and progesterone levels, and studies indicating that estrogen replacement therapy increases CRP levels.[37,38] In an analysis of lifestyle in patients with BC, elevated CRP levels reduced BC risk in those using exogenous estrogens and/or progestins.[39] This relationship suggests that higher CRP levels could reflect increased estrogen exposure, which has been shown to enhance chemosensitivity in BC cells. A study found prolonged exposure of estrogen-sensitive BC cell lines MCF-7 and T47D to estrogen elevated their sensitivity to chemotherapeutic agents like doxorubicin and cisplatin.[40] Moreover, estrogen’s role in estrogen receptor-positive BC extends to immune activation, with ERβ activation demonstrating antitumor effects by suppressing tumor growth and enhancing immune therapy responses.[41] These mechanisms underline CRP’s potential as a predictor for treatment efficacy in HR-positive BC, indicating its broader relevance in assessing therapeutic outcomes. CRP, therefore, holds significant value as a biomarker, especially in predicting response in HR-positive patients, warranting further investigation into its role in BC therapy.

Nevertheless, our study had some limitations. As a retrospective, single-institutional study including only 177 patients, there were several inherent limitations, such as selection bias and small sample size, which cannot be entirely ruled out. Additionally, the prediction model was not verified in an independent external cohort. The lack of data on long-term outcomes makes it difficult to evaluate the influence of CRP on outcomes after NACT. Furthermore, the reasons for the varied predictive values of CRP in different BC subtypes remain unclear; thus, we aim to expand the sample size in future studies to further explore this issue.

5. Conclusions

In conclusion, our study demonstrated that elevated CRP levels are associated with an increased pCR rate in HR-positive (HR+) BC patients undergoing neoadjuvant chemotherapy. This finding underscores the potential of CRP as an independent predictive biomarker for neoadjuvant chemotherapy efficacy in this specific patient group. CRP levels may reflect an underlying inflammatory state that influences treatment response, which aligns with emerging evidence on the role of inflammation in cancer progression and treatment outcomes. By integrating CRP measurements into the assessment process, clinicians may better identify HR+ BC patients who are more likely to benefit from neoadjuvant chemotherapy, allowing for more personalized and effective treatment planning.

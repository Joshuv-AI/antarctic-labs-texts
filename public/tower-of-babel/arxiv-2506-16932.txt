Axion contribution to the mass-radius relation of neutron stars

Bourchier Blvd, 1164 Sofia, Bulgaria b Physics Department, University of Rome Tor Vergata, via della Ricerca Scientifica, I-00133 Rome, Italy c I.N.F.N. - Rome Tor Vergata, via della Ricerca Scientifica, I-00133 Rome, Italy

Abstract




Abstract

A phenomenological Lagrangian for the interaction between a pseudo-scalar (axion-like) field and massive fermions is considered. At high density and non-zero temperature axions can be produced thermally through bremsstrahlung. We compute the equation of state for a static spherical cold compact object and investigate the numerical solutions of the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations. Causality of the obtained solutions sets a strict limitation on the possible values for the interaction constant. This allows us to quantify the influence of the axions on the mass-radius dependence for static spherical stars. Our model predicts measurable effects in the properties of the neutron star and can be easily adapted for KSVZ or DFSZ axions.

I
Introduction

In the Standard Model of elementary particles, it is possible to include in the QCD Lagrangian a CP-violating term

\mathcal{L}_{\theta}=\frac{\theta}{32\pi^{2}}\int d^{4}x\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}~\text{Tr}(G_{\mu\nu}G_{\alpha\beta}),

(1)

which remains unobserved to date. Here
G_{\mu\nu}
is the QCD field strength. To solve the problem of explaining why strong interactions seem to preserve CP (strong CP problem), as indicated by measurements of the neutron electric dipole moment, in 1977 R. Peccei and H. Quinn published a paper
PQ
suggesting the existence of a chiral Abelian symmetry, U
(1)_{\text{PQ}}
, which implies that strong interactions do preserve CP.
The spontaneous breaking of U
(1)_{\text{PQ}}
, which is also broken by anomalies, results in a Goldstone boson, the QCD axion, as recognized independently by F. Wilczek and S. Weinberg
will
;
wein
. In this solution to the strong CP problem,
\theta
is replaced by the axion field, which we, therefore, denote with the same symbol for simplicity.

At the same time,
the QCD axion is a good cold dark matter candidate

preskil
;
sikivie
. Light axions and axion-like particles have been included in a wide variety of theoretical frameworks extending the Standard Model in the hope to guide experimental activities that could discover in the future such particles.

Compact stellar objects seem
to be a promising stage for testing the limits of the existing theories. The fact that axions can be thermally produced in the Universe
salvio
and the role of these particles in the cooling of supernovae and neutron stars at finite density
balkin
;
springman
suggest that, if they exist, they should be included
in the model building of compact objects. Coupling
axions to photons or fermions may result in various mechanisms for energy loss of newly formed neutron stars

;
gomez
;
sedrakian
;
garbreht
, it may lead to formation of axion clouds around neutron stars
cloud
and with the advances of observational astrophysics we might be able to access new territories in the parameter space of exotic particles.

In this work we consider a simple model of a Fermi gas at low temperature, comprised of neutrons which interact with thermally generated axions.
From statistical considerations we derive the equation of state for this ensemble and use it in the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV) equations to explore the impact of axions on a neutron stellar gas. The physics of the strong interactions between particles in the interior of a compact object, as well as the presence of different exotic matter states like quark gluon plasma, is not taken into account. The axion production is governed by the matter density and temperature which we take from current observations. The effective Lagrangian includes the free parameters
g
– the strength of interaction between the neutron and the axion – and
M
, the axion mass.
In our simple model
we show that the axion field can have a significant influence on the mass-radius dependence. In this work natural units are adopted, where
\hbar=k_{B}=c=1.

II
The model

Consider an interaction between a pseudo–scalar field
\theta
and a fermion
\psi
. At the leading order in
\theta
the effective fermion Lagrangian reads
Georgi:1986df

\overline{\psi}(i\not{\partial}-m)\psi+\frac{c_{\psi}}{F_{a}}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{5}\gamma^{\mu}\psi\partial_{\mu}\theta,

(2)

where
F_{a}
is the axion decay constant. The coupling
c_{\psi}
will be treated as a free parameter, but it is computable once the axion model is specified
GrillidiCortona:2015jxo
. This work is not restricted to a specific axion model and will treat the system in a general manner, where the
\theta
field is a weakly interacting light axion-like (ALP) field. Integrating by parts the second term in (
2
) and using the field equations of
\psi
at the order in
\theta
we are working, one obtains the equivalent Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\overline{\psi}(i\not{\partial}-m)\psi+ig\overline{\psi}\gamma^{5}\psi\theta,

(3)

where
g=2mc_{\psi}/F_{a}
is the strength of interaction. The classic window of
F_{a}
in the QCD axion case is
10^{8}~\text{GeV}\lesssim F_{a}\lesssim 10^{12}~\text{GeV}
, which should not be taken as sharp bounds, but it certainly gives a plausible range, see e.g.
Salvio:2015cja
.

The full effective Lagrangian for this system after radiative corrections are taken into account must include a quadratic and a quartic term

\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\theta)(\partial^{\mu}\theta)-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\theta^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\lambda\theta^{4}.

(4)

The renormalized parameters – the ALP mass
M
and the interaction parameter
\lambda
in (
4
) – are not initially present at the classical level but are dynamically generated from the interactions of the ALP field with the fermion through loop diagrams with two and four external pseudo-scalar legs. We want to explore the interaction between the ALPs and the neutrons inside the neutron star. The variation of
\lambda
due to the different energy density in the interior of the star does not introduce a sizable effect to the structure of the star.

The perturbation series expansion neglecting
\mathcal{O}(\lambda)
terms results in decoupling between the ALP and the fermion field. We want to explore the thermodynamics of a static spherical self-gravitating gas of radius
R
, comprised of neutrons and thermally produced ALPs. The temperature of interest is
T\sim 10^{2}
eV and the mass of the ALPs is of the order of or smaller than 1 eV, therefore we work in the regime
M\ll T\ll m
. Under those circumstances when deriving the free energy, we need to take the low temperature approximation for neutrons and high temperature approximation for the ALPs. At finite temperature the free energy densities are
laine
(see also
Salvio:2024upo
):

f_{\theta}(T)=-\frac{\pi^{2}T^{4}}{90}+\frac{M^{2}T^{2}}{24}-\frac{M^{3}T}{12\pi}+\frac{3}{4}\lambda\left(\frac{T^{4}}{144}-\frac{MT^{3}}{24\pi}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{2}),

(5)

for the ALPs and

f_{\psi}(T)=-T^{4}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi T}\right)^{3/2}e^{-m/T}

(6)

for the fermions. The exponential suppression of the energy contributions at low temperature makes the fermion thermal pressure negligible. We are interested in the effect of the presence of the ALP field in the mass-radius relation of neutron stars. The supporting mechanism is the neutron degeneracy pressure given by
weinberg

P_{\psi}=\frac{8\pi}{15mh^{3}}\left(\frac{3h^{3}\rho}{8\pi m}\right)^{5/3},

(7)

where
\rho
is the gas density and

is the Planck constant. Having the free energy density we easily obtain the thermal pressure due to

ALPs. The main mechanism for ALPs production is the bremsstrahlung process with the corresponding Feynman diagram shown below.

III
Mass–radius dependence of a neutron star

In order to explore the mass–radius dependence of a static neutron star we need to solve numerically the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations (TOV):

\begin{gathered}\frac{dP}{dr}=-G\frac{M^{*}(r)}{r^{2}}\rho\left(1+\frac{P}{\rho}\right)\left(1+\frac{4\pi r^{3}P}{M^{*}(r)}\right)\left(1-\frac{2GM^{*}(r)}{r}\right)^{-1},\\
\frac{M^{*}(r)}{dr}=4\pi r^{2}\rho.\end{gathered}

(10)

Here
G
is the Newton’s constant of gravity,
P=P_{\theta}+P_{\psi}
is the total pressure of the system (given by the sum of the
\theta
pressure
P_{\theta}
and the
\psi
pressure
P_{\psi}
) and
M^{*}(r)
is the mass–radius function of the star. The total pressure depends on the interaction parameter
\lambda
, the ALP mass
M
and the temperature
T
. We present the numerical solutions of the TOV equations for different values of the free parameters, where in order to emphasize the ALP contribution we plot the ratio between the mass of the star without axions over the mass of the star with axions, keeping the radius fixed –
R=10
km.

The obtained ratios should be subjected to further limitation coming from causality consideration and gravitational stability of the system. The speed of sound in the interior of the star should be less than the speed of light, so applying those conditions to the solutions of the TOV equation we get that the maximal interaction constant that leads to physical system is
g_{\text{max}}=5.8\cdot 10^{-10}
. This value restricts further the generally accepted allowed parameter space of the axions

From the presented plots we deduce that for physical solutions of the TOV equations the ratio between the mass of the star with and without the ALPs is less than 1. Currently, there exists the so-called hyperon puzzle
hyppuzzle
, which consists in the fact that when adding hyperons to the matter content of the neutron star, the maximally attainable mass obtained from a modified equation of state, becomes smaller than what we observe in reality – the mass of the pulsar PSR J0740+6620 is between 2.1 and 2.4 solar masses. We show that the presence of the hypothetical dark axion-like particles would potentially explain the discrepancy between the maximally observed neutron star masses and the maximal masses allowed by the theory of gravity, combined with all strong interaction nuclear effects. But once the interaction constant passes a lower threshold value, the influence of the ALPs becomes insignificant and unobservable.

From the relation between the pressure and the mass of the ALPs it is evident that the mass of the particles does not have a major role when considering temperatures which are orders of magnitude bigger than the mass itself. The conclusions made from the presented graphs would be valid to within a reasonable magnitude even for much lighter ALPs.

IV
Conclusion

In this work we presented a model which has the potential to account for the thermal pressure created by axion-like particles in the interior of a neutron star. We did not consider strong interaction effects or exotic extensions (e.g. quark gluon plasma
qgp
) other than the direct interaction between a light pseudo-scalar particle and fermions.

The axion-like particles are thermally produced and we derive the equation of state for the axionic gas which is present inside the star. This effect is added to the degeneracy pressure of the neutrons and we plot the ratio between the mass of a pure neutron star over the mass of the star including this additional interaction. Taking the allowed values for the interaction constant, restricted by cosmology and other high energy experiments, we show that the causality condition of neutron stars in the presence of axions limits further the allowed values for
g
. The axions can increase the maximal mass of a neutron star and therefore could explain the existing mismatch between the mass of the largest observed neutron stars and the maximal mass allowed by theory.

Our model predicts neutron star solutions which are up to 55% larger than the solutions from general relativity. The results are weakly dependent on the axion mass, making it an interesting scenario to be tested on experiments. A major advantage is the fact that the results can be easily adapted to KSVZ or DFSZ axions
ksvz1
;
ksvz2
;
dszf1
;
dszf2
by choosing specific values of the parameters.

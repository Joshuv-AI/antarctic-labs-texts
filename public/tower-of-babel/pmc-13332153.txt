Lightweight liquid siamese neural network for robust multimodal satellite image change detection
Authors: Sai Bhargav Kasetty, K Rajakumar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe detection of changes with a high degree of accuracy in multi-temporal satellite imagery is still a challenge due to sensor incapability, location problems and misalignment, radiometric noise, and non-linear temporal variations, which have recently motivated registration-aware and semantic-consistency-based change detection frameworks. Traditional CNNs and Transformer-based methods often have difficulty in effectively computing such continuous-time dynamics. This paper proposes an LSNN model, which is a Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network, that has two branches sharing weights for processing the bi-temporal images acquired at times and and to capture the long-range dependencies and temporal irregularities through liquid time-constant neurons. A cross-feature differencing module is integrated into the system in order to facilitate the detection of fine-grained structural transitions and to eliminate variations that are not relevant. The experiments carried out on RGB–RGB and synthetic aperture radar (SAR)–multispectral datasets with separate train–test splits and extensive statistical analysis indicate that the LSNN has superior performance with SeK = 87.46%, OA = 96.18%, F1 = 94.38%, IoU = 91.22%, NSE = 0.92, and PBIAS = – 1.82%. The results presented are superior to the current best methods, that is, the state-of-the-art models, which also include Transformer- and CNN-based ones like SCaNet. Furthermore, the model’s robustness to real-world perturbations is showcased by additional evaluations under Gaussian noise, speckle noise, and illumination variations coupled with slight spatial shifts. The LSNN architecture is thus an efficient and trustworthy alternative for satellite-based high-accuracy change detection because of its excellent temporal modeling capacity, low operational cost, and offering steady performance even under diverse sensing conditions.Keywords: Change detection, Liquid neural networks, Siamese architecture, Multimodal remote sensing, SAR–multispectral fusion, Spatiotemporal modelingSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Optics and photonics

Introduction

Change detection (CD) plays a crucial role in advancing remote sensing (RS) research. In particular, robust image differencing–based CD methods applied to multi-temporal satellite images have become essential for numerous applications, including land-use change analysis, urban growth monitoring, environmental assessment, and disaster evaluation. The continuous influx of high-resolution sensors for (Red-Green-Blue) RGB, multispectral (MS), and synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery has been a major factor is still a difficult task because of the variability of the atmosphere, differences in the light source, different types of sensors and their misalignment in time and space direction1–3. Additionally, conventional architectures do not provide a solution to this problem since the complex and non-linear spatiotemporal relationships among bi-temporal observations are beyond their capabilities.

Conventional convolutional neural networks (CNNs)4,5 are good at spatial feature extraction but they are not able to handle long-range or continuous temporal dependencies. Transformer-and-Mamba-based architectures6–8 that support cross-temporal attention and spatiotemporal sequence modeling for better contextual reasoning are not without drawbacks, such as requiring heavy computation, large annotated datasets, and substantial GPU memory. Siamese networks are widely used in CD9–11, but most of them depend on static convolutions that are not compatible with the representation of continuous-time temporal variation or sensor-induced distortions. Recent studies (2023–2025) stress the importance of having CD frameworks that are lightweight, temporally adaptive, and multimodal-robust, and at the same time, able to generalize under varying real-world conditions12–14.

In spite of the advancements, there are still three major research gaps that need to be addressed. To begin with, the majority of the methods based on Siamese networks classify changes in the bi-temporal images as isolated events and do not account for the continuous time dynamics or intermediate transitions15,16. Furthermore, inconsistencies in modalities–especially between SAR and MS images–along with changes in lighting, noise, and spatial misalignment still pose a barrier to the generalization of methods17, particularly in heterogeneous change detection scenarios18. Finally, the existing CD approaches are usually hard to interpret and do not possess the capability for neuron-level modeling of dynamics, which leads to their being less robust than needed in cross-domain or out-of-distribution situations19.

In order to deal with these drawbacks, this study presents a Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network (LSNN) that couples a two-pronged shared-weight architecture with Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) neurons for variable temporal modeling. Liquid neurons continuously alter their internal time constants in response to the input, thereby allowing them to capture nonlinear temporal progression, irregular transitions, and detailed structural changes–all of which have been evidenced in the recent research on liquid neural networks20,21. Additionally, a differential cross-feature mechanism gives a boost to discriminative power while the lightweight design permits use in platforms with limited resources.

Related works

The change detection (CD) in remote sensing has experienced a remarkable progress and transformation through the introduction of powerful tools like deep neural networks, multimodal fusion, and temporal modeling. Present-day methodologies can be approximately categorized into the following groups: CNNs, Transformer and Mamba-based architectures, Siamese and dual-branch configurations, biologically inspired dynamic neural models, and multimodal fusion methods employing SAR, MS, and RGB images.

CNN-based change detection

During the initial stages of deep-learning-based change detection (CD) models, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) became the method of choice primarily for their excellent spatial feature extraction capabilities. Classical CNN architectures like FC-EF, FC-Siam and even some UNet derivative forms–all discussed in the studies by Daudt et al.4 and Hedjam et al.5–consistently achieved performance levels that were not far from the optimum on bi-temporal RGB images. Nevertheless, the CNNs treat the temporal differences as separate signals and use static convolutional filters, which limits their capacity to detect the non-linear temporal evolution. The following CNNs came up with a modification that included the multi-scale and attention-based modules13,14 which resulted in the models being more robust to spatial appearance changes; however, they are still incapable of simulating long-range or continuous temporal dependencies, especially in heterogeneous or multimodal conditions.

Transformer, Mamba, and hybrid architectures

The frameworks based on transformers for CD make use of self-attention in a global manner to improve the interaction between different periods. Among these are ChangeFormer, BIT-CD, and the related techniques put forward by Zhang et al.25 and Wang et al.6 that have been recognized for better contextual inference. The latest in this area are the state-space sequence models ChangeMamba7 and the Mamba-based Siamese architecture by Paranjape et al.8, which also has contributed to spatiotemporal modeling. However, all these approaches leave a lot to be desired in terms of efficiency as they are still quite heavy on computation, need large labeled datasets, and might only run on powerful GPUs. On top of that, Transformers and Mamba models usually treat bi-temporal images as separate tokens, which makes it hard for them to grasp continuous-time temporal dynamics or enforce semantic consistency across temporal representations, as emphasized in recent cross-difference semantic change detection studies26. Their efficacy is often compromised in mixed sensor situations like SAR–MS, where different sensor characteristics like spectral variance and noise characteristics are present.

Siamese and dual-stream networks

Siamese architectures have gained a lot of popularity in Change Detection (CD) because the weight-sharing technique allows the same features to be extracted from both and . Pioneering studies like the Siamese convolutional network by Zhan et al.1 and the multimodal Siamese CNN by Zhang et al.2 set the stage for paired-image learning. Further designs, namely SNUNet-CD, DSIFN, and Att-SiamNet, achieved better results by utilizing cross-temporal fusion and attention mechanisms9,10,13. Nevertheless, these systems rely a lot on static convolution layers, which makes them affected by seasonal variations, sensor noise, and geometric inconsistencies. The recent development of graph-based Siamese techniques27–29 has enhanced structural representation, yet none incorporate dynamic neuron models capable of capturing continuous temporal transitions.

Spiking neural networks and liquid neural models

In the same breath, models based on biological principles like spiking neural networks (SNNs) and Liquid Time-Constant (LTC) networks have given rise to the concept of dynamic neurons that progress over time continuously. LTC neurons, as shown in the works of Chahine et al.20 and Pawlak et al.21, are capable of changing their internal states and time constants dynamically according to the input signal, thus allowing the modeling of non-linear and irregular patterns in time. These new architectures are extremely robust in applications such as robotics, motion prediction, and handling of out-of-distribution tasks. Nonetheless, the use of these techniques in the area of remote sensing change detection (CD) has not been thoroughly investigated yet, and no previous research has utilized liquid neural dynamics within a Siamese dual-stream framework for multimodal CD so far.

Multimodal fusion: SAR, MS, and RGB

The task of Multimodal Change Detection (CD) necessitates the combination of SAR, multispectral, and RGB images in an effective manner because of their complementary spectral and structural information. The different methods that have been looked into are SAR–optical fusion using cross-attention22, multispectral–RGB fusion through feature concatenation23, and SAR denoising before CD19. Nevertheless, the fusion of different modalities remains a challenging task due to discrepancies in spectral response, noise from SAR, poor alignment, and variations in lighting conditions. Current techniques cannot utilize liquid neural dynamics to simulate temporal irregularities or specific sensor distortions, despite recent progress in heterogeneous and registration-integrated change detection frameworks18,30.

Impact of image quality on change detection. The performance of satellite image change detection is strongly influenced by image quality factors such as spatial resolution, radiometric fidelity, noise, and spectral consistency. Recent studies show that image enhancement and super-resolution techniques can significantly improve downstream change detection accuracy by restoring fine-grained spatial–spectral structures. Xu et al.31 demonstrated that diffusion-based hyperspectral super-resolution improves semantic discrimination and robustness, motivating the robustness-oriented design and noise-resilience evaluation adopted in this work.

Dataset and preparation

For semantic change detection (SCD) in various urban and rural localities, this research employs the Semantic Change Detection Dataset (SECOND), which is a large-scale, expert-annotated benchmark15. The dataset comprises 4,662 pairs of bi-temporal RGB aerial images that were sourced from diverse airborne and satellite platforms in Chinese cities like Hangzhou, Chengdu, and Shanghai. The acquisitions that lasted several years were significantly different in terms of space, time, and environment, which are all necessary for the robust evaluation of models. Proposed methodology

The proposed Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network (LSNN) is an architecture that performs change detection in spatial-spectral-temporal dimensions directly on the preprocessed bi-temporal image pairs as specified in the Dataset and Preparation section. The LSNN framework takes normalized image patches of size (or based on the experiment) and applies a dual-branch Siamese setup to extract the features of the most discriminative time. In each branch, there is an LTC neural layer that performs continuous-time dynamics, meaning that the network will be able to learn the non-linear temporal transitions between images and . The outputs from the two branches are fused and decoded to generate pixel-wise semantic change maps, from which binary change masks are derived post hoc by collapsing all non-zero semantic classes. The decoding phase is made up of efficient upsampling and convolution layers that increase spatial resolution while still retaining the compact liquid-state representations. Concretely, the fused feature vector is first projected into a low-resolution spatial tensor of size using a fully connected reshaping layer. This tensor is then progressively upsampled through a lightweight decoder consisting of bilinear upsampling and convolutions, ultimately producing a dense pixel-wise prediction map of size . Training environment and hyperparameters

The Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network (LSNN) is trained on the SECOND preprocessed dataset employing a training protocol that is stable and reproducible. The dataset is divided into 60% training, 15% validation, and 25% testing.

In order to fine-tune the network parameters, the Adam optimizer is used with an initial learning rate of . A ReduceLROnPlateau scheduler is used to reduce the learning rate when the validation loss does not improve for three consecutive epochs by a factor of 0.5 in an adaptive manner. The training lasts for 50 epochs with a batch size of 16. To avoid overfitting, early stopping (patience = 5) is applied by keeping an eye on the loss during the validation stage. A dropout of 0.2 is applied in the dense layers to prevent overfitting and subsequently increase the model’s generalization ability. Results and discussions

This section offers a comprehensive analysis of the proposed Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network (LSNN) with the SECOND dataset and additional multimodal inputs that come from SAR+MS imagery. The LSNN is inspired by the behavior of biological neurons, and thus, it can dynamically modulate its internal time constants to allow a very strong extraction of spatiotemporal variations. The “intelligence” of the model is evaluated in terms of accuracy, stability, temporal consistency, computational efficiency, and change localization quality. A baseline Siamese Neural Network (SNN) serves as a reference for the comparisons, and the results reveal that LSNN has better learning dynamics and generalization ability.

Conclusion and future works

The Lightweight Liquid Siamese Neural Network (LSNN) that has been proposed produces a very good performance for semantic change detection, not only between different satellite datasets but also for different types of inputs and under different robustness scenarios. By means of liquid-state temporal dynamics and contrastive siamese encoding, the model is able to bring about improved semantic consistency, higher Separated Kappa and mIoU values, reduced cost of computation, and stable performance under misalignment, noise, and resolution degradation. These contributions make LSNN a compact yet powerful framework that is perfect for large-scale geospatial monitoring, urban development assessment, and multi-sensor environmental analysis. However, even with these capabilities, there are still some limitations that include the model’s sensitivity to large co-registration errors, its reliance on labelled data, and the reduced fine-detail accuracy when it is used on coarse-resolution imagery.

Future research directions will be able to tackle these limitations by means of better data alignment strategies, semi-supervised learning, and adaptive resolution-aware training pipelines. The application of hyperspectral and LiDAR sensors for cross-sensor generalization is another way through which material-level and structural understanding could be improved. The use of explainable AI techniques like Grad-CAM, Layer-wise Relevance Propagation (LRP), or saliency-based attribution will not only make the feature responses of the LSNN model more interpretable but will also allow for a clear-cut evaluation of the temporal or spatial cues that impact change predictions. This kind of interpretability can support the operational deployment of real-world decision-making systems, such as disaster impact mapping, infrastructure surveillance, and long-term environmental monitoring, among others.

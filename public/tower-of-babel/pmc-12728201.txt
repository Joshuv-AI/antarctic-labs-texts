Multilabel feature selection using graph neural networks and differential evolution optimization
Authors: Ning Pan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In recent years, the exponential growth of high-dimensional data across domains such as data mining, machine learning, bioinformatics, social network analysis, and image processing has amplified the importance of feature selection as a primary method for dimensionality reduction. High-dimensional datasets often include redundant, noisy, and irrelevant features that hinder the development of accurate, efficient, and reliable learning models. This issue becomes even more critical in multi-label scenarios, where each instance may be associated with multiple labels, creating intricate interactions between features and labels as well as interdependencies among the labels themselves. Traditional feature selection techniques, designed primarily for single-label problems, struggle to address the complexities of multi-label data. Consequently, there is a pressing need for novel approaches that can effectively capture and utilize these complexities. This study introduces a hybrid method that combines the representational power of Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) with the optimization efficiency of Differential Evolution (DE) to tackle multi-label feature selection challenges. GNNs leverage graph structures to model complex relationships between features and labels, allowing for simultaneous consideration of feature relevance and label dependencies. DE, a robust global optimization algorithm, ensures the selection of an optimal subset of features by exploring vast search spaces and avoiding local optima. The proposed method was evaluated on six diverse datasets spanning text and image domains. GNN-DE achieves optimal classification performance while selecting fewer features, demonstrating its efficiency in reducing computational complexity. For datasets with complex label correlations, such as Enron and Scene, this method outperforms existing approaches.

Introduction

The significance of feature selection as a crucial instrument for lowering data complexity has been highlighted in recent decades by the exponential growth of high-dimensional data in fields including data mining, machine learning, computational biology, social network analysis, and image processing. The creation of efficient and trustworthy learning models may be hampered by the abundance of redundant, noisy, and irrelevant features found in high-dimensional data. These difficulties are especially important for applications that need to analyze data quickly and accurately. Techniques for reducing the dimensionality of data, especially feature selection, have been suggested as a crucial strategy to overcome these constraints1,2.

A popular method for lowering data dimensions is feature selection, which also improves learning model performance by eliminating noisy and redundant features and streamlining data structures. In addition to increasing prediction accuracy, this procedure lowers computing cost and lessens the possibility of overfitting3. Three main techniques—filter, wrapper, and embedding approaches can be used to pick features. In order to eliminate unnecessary features, filter methods assess features using statistical criteria like variance, correlation, and mutual information. These techniques are quick and easy, but they frequently fail to capture intricate feature relationships4. Wrapper approaches analyze subsets of features using machine learning algorithms. Due to the repetitive execution of learning algorithms, these approaches are more expensive and time-consuming even though they frequently yield higher accuracy5. Feature selection and model learning are carried out simultaneously in embedded approaches. These approaches are used in many real-world applications and are more effective since they combine the feature selection and modeling procedures6.

Feature selection is particularly difficult in the context of multi-label data, because each instance may be linked to multiple labels. The intricacy of feature-label interactions and label dependencies are the root causes of these difficulties. These complications are inefficiently managed by traditional feature selection techniques that were created for single-label data7. Therefore, it is necessary to create new methods that can model and deal with these complexities. Using GNNs is one innovative method in this field. GNNs are a potent model for graph data analysis that can successfully capture intricate interactions between labels and attributes. GNNs take into account feature interactions and label dependencies at the same time by utilizing graph architectures and information propagation among graph nodes. Research has shown that GNNs can extract more structural information and increase model accuracy in multi-label issues8,9.

Global search optimization methods, like DE, have been suggested as useful instruments for the best feature selection in addition to GNNs. Finding efficient feature subsets is made possible by DE’s potent ability to scan feature spaces and avoid local optima10,11. This algorithm offers excellent solutions for complicated problems with wide search spaces by using a population of solutions and modeling evolutionary processes.

Previous studies12,13 have examined the amalgamation of GNN with DE, although these endeavors predominantly concentrate on general optimization objectives or single-label contexts. This study’s originality resides in the application of the GNN–DE framework to the multi-label feature selection problem, characterized by intricate label dependencies and high-dimensional feature spaces that present distinct problems. Utilizing GNNs to elucidate complex feature-label connections and implementing DE for effective global optimization, our methodology offers a cohesive framework that diminishes dimensionality while improving classification accuracy.

This study aims to provide an answer to the question of how to choose the best features in multi-label data by combining the capabilities of DE optimization methods and Graph Neural Networks (GNN). By simulating the intricate relationships between features and labels and optimizing the search space, this study aims to provide a hybrid approach that can choose a subset of characteristics that improves prediction accuracy while lowering computing complexity.

This study presents a number of significant innovations. For the first time, the issue of feature selection in multi-label data is tackled by combining GNN and DE optimization. While DE is used to improve feature subsets, GNN is used to represent the intricate interactions between features and labels. The suggested methodology outperforms current methods and shows improved performance, especially for datasets with complex label dependencies like Enron and Scene. Additionally, the strategy reduces computing complexity by selecting fewer features, which results in improved classification accuracy. These developments guarantee that the suggested method is a reliable solution for multi-label feature selection by increasing both prediction accuracy and computational efficiency. This paper primarily contributes to this by addressing the fundamental challenges of multi-label feature selection in high-dimensional data, in particular:

Related work

Multi-label learning

Feature selection for multi-label data presents unique challenges due to the intricate interdependencies across labels and between features and labels. Unlike single-label scenarios, where each instance belongs to one class, multi-label learning requires capturing overlapping label structures, correlations, and dependencies that often emerge in high-dimensional settings. Traditional approaches such as filtering, bundling, or embedding struggle with these complexities, leading to poor generalization, high computational cost, and sensitivity to noise. Extending single-label techniques in a naïve manner typically overlooks inter-label correlations, thereby reducing classification accuracy.

Recent studies have sought to explicitly model multi-label dependencies. For example14, introduced a representation learning framework that simultaneously encodes features and labels, reducing redundancy while capturing essential dependencies. Similarly15, proposed a dynamic label alignment strategy for large-scale text classification, demonstrating the value of dynamically adjusting label structures. In a different domain16, presented a multi-label framework for sewer defect identification in videos, showing strong robustness in real-world applications. These works collectively highlight that accurate modeling of label correlations is essential for reliable predictions.

However, challenges persist. While14–16 advanced structural modeling, they often remain dataset-specific, with scalability issues in very high-dimensional contexts. Moreover, resilience to incomplete or noisy data is rarely addressed. In this regard17, specifically tackled incomplete multi-label data and demonstrated that handling missing labels effectively reduces performance degradation. Nevertheless, their framework was limited in scalability and generalization across domains.

In summary, studies14–17 underscore that while progress has been made in modeling multi-label dependencies, existing methods either sacrifice scalability, neglect robustness to missing data, or lack a global optimization mechanism. These gaps create the foundation for our proposed method, which integrates graph-based dependency modeling with an optimization strategy to jointly capture correlations and enhance efficiency.

Multi-label feature selection

Feature selection is widely recognized as a critical tool for dimensionality reduction in multi-label problems. The goal is not only to reduce redundancy but also to maintain accuracy and interpretability. Graph-based approaches have been explored to better capture relationships between features and labels. For instance18, applied graph clustering to eliminate irrelevant features while uncovering latent data structures. Similarly19, proposed Flow2GNN, a flexible message-passing framework that can capture dependencies beyond homophily, demonstrating the potential of sophisticated propagation strategies in revealing intricate label–feature interactions.

Optimization-based approaches have also been investigated. For example20, introduced a multi-objective method to balance feature reduction with prediction accuracy, while attention-based strategies such as21 dynamically assigned importance weights to informative dependencies, reducing noise and redundancy. Ensemble-based frameworks22,23 further improved generalizability, particularly in imbalanced datasets. Additionally24, combined fuzzy clustering with convolutional graph networks (FCGCN), integrating fuzzy memberships into a relational graph model for unsupervised feature clustering.

Despite these advances, several drawbacks remain. Graph-based methods like18,19,24 capture dependencies effectively but often face high computational costs when scaled to large datasets. Optimization-based approaches such as20,21 are flexible but sensitive to parameter tuning, making them prone to overfitting. Ensemble techniques22,23 improve robustness but further increase computational demand.

Taken together, the existing body of work illustrates that multi-label feature selection methods either lack scalability, require delicate parameter calibration, or are computationally expensive. These limitations motivate a hybrid approach that combines the representational power of GNNs with a global optimizer like Differential Evolution (DE), reducing redundancy more efficiently than graph-only models and addressing the computational drawbacks of deep or ensemble-based approaches.

Swarm intelligence optimization and neural networks

Another prominent line of research integrates swarm intelligence with neural network architectures for multi-label feature selection. Algorithms such as Differential Evolution (DE), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and Genetic Algorithms (GA) are effective global search methods capable of avoiding local optima. For instance25, combined DE with GNNs to exploit structural feature-label relationships while simultaneously selecting optimal subsets, achieving improvements in both accuracy and efficiency. Reinforcement learning has also been incorporated;26 demonstrated that agents guided by reward functions can incrementally select relevant features, showcasing adaptability though limited by scalability and convergence challenges. Beyond these27, explored discrete graph-based metaheuristics for fraud detection, illustrating the potential of graph representations in relational domains.

Nevertheless, these hybrid strategies are not without limitations. The effectiveness of evolutionary algorithms such as DE or GA is highly sensitive to hyperparameter settings (e.g., mutation or crossover rates). Neural networks, particularly GNNs, incur significant computational costs when applied to high-dimensional multi-label data, limiting their practicality. Current hybrid approaches, while innovative, frequently fail to strike a balance between scalability and predictive performance.

In conclusion, swarm intelligence and neural-network-based methods highlight the value of combining global search with relational modeling. Yet, their reliance on costly training and parameter sensitivity underscores the need for a more lightweight and adaptable hybrid system. Our approach directly addresses these shortcomings by coupling the strong global search capabilities of DE with the relational modeling power of GNNs. In doing so, it achieves a unique balance: scalable optimization, robust dependency modeling, and improved accuracy at reduced computational expense.

Summary and research gap

The related literature can be broadly categorized into three areas: (i) multi-label learning focusing on modeling dependencies, (ii) feature selection approaches targeting dimensionality reduction, and (iii) hybrid optimization–neural network methods combining structural modeling with global search. While each direction contributes important advances, none fully resolves the intertwined challenges of scalability, robustness to noise or missing data, redundancy control, and computational efficiency.

Our research addresses this gap by integrating GNNs for explicit modeling of feature–label and inter-label dependencies with DE for efficient global optimization. Unlike existing approaches, this synergy simultaneously captures high-order structural information, mitigates redundancy, and enhances scalability offering a more resilient solution to multi-label feature selection.

Proposed method

GNN and the DE algorithm are used as key components of the proposed method, aimed at optimizing multi-label feature selection. This hybrid method, instead of using particle populations, leverages graphs and feature vectors as the main input for modeling and optimization.

The embedding vectors obtained by GNN are then sent to the DE algorithm to begin the optimization process. The DE algorithm uses a population of candidate solution vectors. The proposed approach utilizes a Graph Neural Network (GNN) to describe the interactive relationships between features and labels by creating a bipartite graph, with one set of nodes representing features and the other set representing labels. Edges are determined by feature-label co-occurrence statistics or correlation metrics, with their weights indicating the strength of these associations. In message passing, feature nodes consolidate information from adjacent label nodes and vice versa, enabling the network to progressively enhance its embeddings. Multiple propagation layers capture higher-order dependencies, allowing the model to learn both direct feature-label interactions and indirect links mediated by other features or labels. The final node embeddings encode both relevance and dependence information, which the DE algorithm later utilizes to select the most informative subset of features.

The selection of DE as the optimization module is driven by its robust capacity to equilibrate exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional search spaces. In contrast to various swarm intelligence algorithms like PSO or Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), DE necessitates less control parameters, demonstrates consistent convergence, and is notably proficient in evading local optima. These attributes render DE a formidable and computationally efficient option for the feature selection job, because the search space expands exponentially with the number of features.

Conversely, GNNs were chosen to extract feature-label correlations due to their specialized nature for modeling structured data. Conventional deep learning methods depend on vectorized input representations, whereas GNNs can directly function on graph structures, enabling them to capture higher-order connections among features and correlations between labels. GNNs are especially appropriate for multi-label situations due to the intricate interdependencies that simpler statistical or distance-based approaches cannot sufficiently address.

Proposed method using GNN and DE

In this study, our goal is to perform feature selection for multi-label data. To process multi-label data, the data is first converted into a graph, where each feature is considered as a node and the relationships between features are represented as edges. In this model, the goal is to extract useful features for label prediction from this graph. Graphs used in GNN effectively model complex relationships and dependencies between features and labels. To train the GNN, non-linear activation functions are employed, allowing the model to learn hidden and complex relationships between features and labels. For optimizing feature selection, the DE algorithm was employed. DE is a population-based algorithm that searches the solution space through population variation and operations such as mutation, crossover, and selection.

Classifier and evaluation criterion

ML-KNN is a multi-label extension of the KNN algorithm. In ML-KNN, for each test instance, the nearest neighbors are identified first. Then, based on the information from these neighbors, the number of neighbors belonging to each possible class, and a maximum a posteriori principle (MAP), the set of class labels is predicted26,27.

To evaluate the performance of the selected feature subsets obtained by the proposed method and other methods, three common multi-label evaluation criteria were employed: accuracy, one-error, and mean average precision.

Comparison algorithms

Once the best candidate solution is determined, each row of the solution matrix is analyzed, and the largest value in each row is identified as the most important feature for a specific label. Features are then ranked based on their importance, prioritizing those with the highest weights. This process effectively selects the most critical features for multi-label data using GNN and DE, while eliminating irrelevant features. The performance of the proposed method is compared with six well-known supervised multi-label feature selection methods: ELACHI42, PPT_CH43, PPT_Relief44, PPT_MI45, LRFS46, and MCLS47. These methods were selected due to their representation of various methodological categories (filter-based, wrapper-based, and embedding approaches) and their provision of a thorough standard for performance assessment. Multi-label feature selection has considerable obstacles, such as elucidating intricate feature-label connections, controlling computing expenses, and adeptly addressing associated labels. Conventional techniques, including filter-based methods, frequently fail to capture complex dependencies, resulting in inadequate feature selection. Wrapper-based approaches, although more efficient, are often computationally intensive and susceptible to redundancy. Moreover, several current methodologies neglect inter-label interactions, addressing each label in isolation and overlooking significant structural links. These constraints adversely affect classification precision and elevate computational complexity, especially in datasets characterized by high feature dimensionality and tightly correlated labels.

Comparison with benchmark methods

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed GNN-DE method, we compared it against six widely used multi-label feature selection techniques: ELA_CHI, PPT_CH, PPT_Relief, PPT_MI, LRFS, and MCLS. These methods were selected based on their prevalence in the literature and their effectiveness in handling multi-label data. The comparison ensures that both traditional statistical approaches and more advanced machine learning-based techniques are considered, providing a balanced and fair performance assessment.

ELA_CHI and PPT_CH are well-established statistical methods that evaluate feature importance based on chi-square scores and probabilistic label dependencies. These techniques are computationally efficient but often struggle to model complex feature-label relationships, particularly in high-dimensional multi-label datasets. PPT_Relief and PPT_MI, on the other hand, incorporate mutual information and neighborhood-based feature relevance to enhance selection accuracy. However, these approaches may still retain redundant features due to their heuristic nature.

LRFS and MCLS represent more sophisticated approaches. LRFS (Label Relevance Feature Selection) employs label-based feature relevance ranking, which effectively reduces dimensionality but does not explicitly model label dependencies. MCLS (Multi-Label Clustering Selection) utilizes clustering strategies to group similar features, improving redundancy removal and feature interpretability. Despite these advantages, clustering-based methods can suffer from suboptimal partitioning when handling highly correlated labels. The proposed GNN-DE method surpasses these techniques by integrating graph-based dependency modeling (GNN) with global search optimization, effectively capturing feature-label interactions while optimizing feature selection for improved classification performance.

In contrast to conventional benchmark techniques as ELA_CHI, PPT_CH, PPT_Relief, PPT_MI, LRFS, and MCLS, the suggested GNN-DE framework concurrently utilizes structural graph learning and evolutionary optimization. Conventional statistical and clustering methods primarily emphasize feature relevance or redundancy reduction, frequently neglecting inter-label dependencies and intricate feature-label connections. Conversely, our approach utilizes Graph Neural Networks to explicitly identify these correlations and Differential Evolution to globally optimize feature subsets, so ensuring both precision and sparsity. This dual mechanism allows the suggested method to attain enhanced predictive performance while minimizing computational complexity, hence underscoring its superiority and practical relevance compared to alternative methods.

Discussion

The experimental results across ten benchmark datasets six original (Medical, Enron, Bibtex, Image, Scene, Cooking) and four high-dimensional (RCV1-v2, Reuters-21578, GEO, TCGA)—demonstrate that the GNN-DE framework consistently outperforms leading multi-label feature selection methods in accuracy, one-error, and mean average precision (MAP). This superiority arises from the integration of graph neural networks (GNNs), which adeptly capture high-order feature-label dependencies, and differential evolution (DE), which enables robust global search across large feature spaces. Datasets like Scene and Enron, with significant label correlations, alongside RCV1-v2 (~ 47,000 features) and GEO (~ 20,000 features), underscore the method’s strength in modeling complex relationships that traditional approaches often miss. The hybrid optimization, combining DE for global exploration with gradient descent for local refinement, enhances convergence and improves feature subset quality, as shown in Tables 3 and 8.

However, GNN-DE faces limitations. The GNN component requires constructing and training extensive graph structures, leading to high computational costs, which may hinder real-time or resource-constrained applications. DE’s effectiveness also depends on parameter settings, despite sensitivity analysis, suggesting that adaptive tuning could improve stability. While ML-KNN was used as the classifier, exploring GCN-based or Transformer models could enhance robustness, especially for high-dimensional tasks like bioinformatics and text classification.

Conceptually, GNN-DE surpasses traditional methods. PPT-based techniques (PPT_CH, PPT_MI, PPT_Relief) rely on basic statistics, missing higher-order dependencies, while ELA_CHI and LRFS depend on rigid assumptions, neglecting nonlinear correlations. Even MCLS lacks explicit structural modeling. In contrast, GNN-DE uses GNNs to reveal intricate feature-label links and DE for global optimization, addressing the “curse of dimensionality” effectively, as validated by scalability analysis.

Future work should focus on optimizing computational efficiency (e.g., via GPU acceleration) and adaptive techniques to expand its utility in real-world, high-dimensional scenarios.

Conclusion

This study underscores the significant implications of the GNN-DE framework for practical applications involving high-dimensional datasets with complex label dependencies, such as bioinformatics, image classification, and text categorization. The proposed method enhances predictive performance demonstrated by superior accuracy, one-error, and mean average precision (MAP) across ten datasets, including high-dimensional ones like RCV1-v2 and GEO while optimizing computational efficiency through the selection of concise, informative feature subsets. Outperforming established methods (PPT_CH, ELA_CHI, MCLS, LRFS, PPT_MI, PPT_Relief), GNN-DE excels particularly in datasets with strong label correlations, such as Enron and Scene, where traditional techniques struggle to model inter-label relationships.

Despite these advancements, the framework faces limitations. The reliance on GNNs incurs substantial computational overhead, potentially restricting its use in real-time or resource-constrained settings. Future research could address this through lightweight or sampling-based GNN designs. Additionally, DE’s performance depends on parameter settings, suggesting that adaptive or self-tuning algorithms could enhance stability across diverse datasets. While ML-KNN served as the baseline classifier, exploring advanced models like graph convolutional networks or Transformer architectures could further improve accuracy. Extending the framework to handle streaming or dynamic multi-label data would also broaden its applicability to real-time applications, such as online recommendation systems and sensor networks.

Future investigations should focus on reducing computational complexity via optimized GNN variants, developing adaptive DE parameter tuning, and integrating advanced classifiers. Incorporating innovative graph-based techniques, such as fuzzy clustering-enhanced GCNs24 or advanced graph transformations41, could further enhance the framework’s representational power and predictive resilience, solidifying its role in addressing the challenges of high-dimensional multi-label data.

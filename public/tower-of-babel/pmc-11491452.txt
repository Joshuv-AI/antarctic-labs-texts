Facile synthesis of azines by carboxylic acid esters as catalyst and facilitation of intersystem crossing (ISC) in azines by azine chromophore
Authors: M. Sennappan, V. Srinivasa Murthy, Praveen B. Managutti, P Subhapriya, K Gurushantha, Praveen C Ramamurthy, B Hemavathi, K. S. Anantharaju, Aman Thakur
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Development of new organic synthetic methods fascinating the researchers which facilitating the increasing demands of the modern society, environmental friendly with high efficiency and low cost. The introduction of chromophores in an organic molecules facilitating intersystem crossing (ISC) to harvest both singlet and triplet excitons is also currently demanding field. We report a facile synthesis of symmetrical azines from carbonyl compounds and hydrazine hydrate with carboxylic acid esters as catalyst in methanol. This reaction presents a condensation of primary amino groups in hydrazine hydrate and carbonyl compounds took place simultaneously in a very short refluxing time. The prepared azines 1–10 were structurally analysed by various analytical techniques such as LC-MS1, H NMR13, C NMR, UV-Vis, FTIR and single crystal X-ray diffraction. Photoluminescence properties of prepared azines were recorded in CCl4 at 1 × 10−3 M and excitation range from 329 to 362 nm. The photoluminescence analysis results revealed that compounds 1–10 (except 8) were showed delayed fluorescence and 8 was showed fluorescence property. The photophysical properties of compounds 1–10 such as electron density and band gap energies was calculated by density function theory. This results revealed that the intra-molecular charge transfer occurs within the azines. The azine function in the azines enabling intersystem crossing hence, it is showing phosphorescence.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75561-4.

Introduction

In recent years, many researchers have been prepared azines from alcohols and hydrazine hydrate as reactants, which is catalysed by metal complexes made from nickel and ruthenium with different ligands. These reactions, alcohols get oxidized to become carbonyl compounds and condensed with hydrazine hydrate gave azines in complicated medium and long refluxing time1–6.

The azines have been prepared from hydrazone/hydrazone hydrochloride with various carbonyl compounds in ethanol and catalytic amount of acetic acid under refluxing for 3–12 h7–9. Hydrazones are converted into azines by refluxing for 3 h with FeCl3 as catalyst10. Azines were prepared from formylated-triarylmethanes, hydrazine hydrate and acetic acid in ethanol was refluxed for about 1 h11. Decomposition of N-tosylhydrazones in the presence of carbonyl compounds and catalyst Cs2CO3 with stirring at 100 0C for 6 h produced the azines12. Reaction between N-heterocyclic carbene and N-tosylhydrazones with catalyst KOtBu at hot condition developed azines13. Azines have been synthesized from carbonyl compounds and hydrazine hydrate and iodine as catalyst14. Stirring of carbene with diazocompounds in THF at room temperature gave azines15. Oximes are converted to azines using CuI as catalyst and NaHSO3 as reductant in different organic solvents16. Ketazines also prepared from ketones and phosphonic dihydrazide in ethanol by refluxing17. Thiosemicarbazones reacted with an activated nitrile with a few drops of triethylamine in DMF was heated at 120 °C for 10–15 h or heated in a microwave oven for 2–6 min gave a good yield of azines18. The synthesis of azines attained without using hydrazine or its derivatives with 96% yield by heating acetonitrile-H2O2/(NH4)2CO3 with aldehydes19. The grinding of mixture containing carbonyl compounds, hydrazine sulphate and trimethylamine for 3–5 min gave the azines20. Hydrazinium carboxylate and carbonyl compounds are grinded at room temperature and kept for long time to achieve complete conversion to azines21–23. Furthermore, hydrothermal method has been developed to synthesis azine linked COFs by condensation reaction between hydrazine and hydroxy-substituted 1,3,5-triformylbenzene in water24. Most of the azines synthetic methods, work up is complicated and involves some additional process like vacuum evaporation and column chromatography.

Azines are N-N linked diimines which are structurally analogues to 1,3-dienes, enones, and 1,2-diones. These compounds show diverse chemical properties such as trans conformation25–27, isomerism28,29, tautomerism30,31, polymorphism32,33, redox properties34,35, conjugation36,37 etc. Furthermore, azines have two azomethine groups which are heteroatom-containing dipolarophiles used as reactants to synthesis heterocyclic compounds like oxadiazoles, triazoles, diazepines, pyrazoles and pyridines24. Azines are possessing significant nitrogen donor ligands which can be used to make metal complexes with pharmacological and biological activity38. However, azines and heterocyclic compounds prepared from azines are receiving much attention in various fields includes material science, biological and medical field24,38.

Many carboxylic acid esters are occurring naturally in fats, oils, fruits, waxes, flowers etc. have distinctive fruit-like odors39. Esters are also synthesized from carboxylic acids and its derivatives by different chemical reactions. Esters are involving in different chemical reactions and give various valuable products39. Phillips catalyst is an inorganic ester; it is used as a heterogeneous catalyst for polymerization of ethylene40. Aminolysis reaction takes place between esters and hydrazine hydrate give acid hydrazides as products by nucleophilic addition and elimination mechanism41. Aromatic esters give acid hydrazides with hydrazine hydrate by heating about 4 h42,43 whereas, some aliphatic acid hydrazide is prepared at 0 0C or 25 0C44,45.

Organic phosphorescence materials have gained tremendous attention in optoelectronics due to their luring features such as wide colour range, thin and light weight, inexpensive, easy fabrication, power efficient, give flexible devices etc46,47 Currently researchers are focusing to harvest both singlet and triplet excitons using thermally activated delayed fluorescence (TADF) materials and room temperature phosphorescence (RTP) emitters48,49. There are two techniques used to enhance energy output: (1) inhibit nonradiative decay of triplet excitons and (2) enhancing intersystem crossing (ISC) process. Generally, to satisfy the first condition, several strategies are followed including polymer-matrix50, H-aggregation51, crystal engineering52, host-guest doping53, metal complexes54, copolymerization55 etc. which makes rigid structure facilitate phosphorescence. To promote ISC in organic molecules by introducing halogens (Cl, Br and I), heteroatoms (O, S and N), chromophores (> CO, -COO® and > SO2) and conjugation which facilitates easy n→π* and π→π* transitions56–59.

To date, different methods have been reported for the synthesis of azines, most of them are taking long refluxing time, expensive and environmentally unfriendly, less yield etc. Herein, we have designed a novel reaction method in which carboxylic acid esters were used as catalyst and synthesized a series of azines with different carbonyl compounds. The catalytic activity of ester group is confirmed by using ethyl chloroacetate, ethyl acetoacetate and ethyl benzoate as catalyst. Despite some of the compounds were reported, all are structurally characterized by various analytical techniques. Photoluminescence properties of all the compounds were studied in solution medium and explained their properties by correlating their structures and substituents. Photophysical properties of all the compounds 1–10 were calculated and this result was interrelated with photoluminescence study.

Results and discussion

The carbonyl group in carboxylic ester is polar owing difference in electronegativity between the oxygen and carbon atoms. However, the oxygen atom carries a partial negative charge, while carbon atom carries a partial positive charge60,61 The nucleophile nitrogen in the hydrazine hydrate approaches the carbonyl carbon of ester. So that, there is weak interaction between the nitrogen in hydrazine and carbonyl carbon forms an intermediate. The intermediate has partial negative charge of oxygen and partial positive charge of nitrogen. Primary amine in the intermediate which is not participated any chemical changes condensed with one molecule of carbonyl compound gave azomethine group. Partial positive charge of nitrogen in a hydrazone withdraws a molecular interaction due to steric effect and attacks carbonyl carbon of another carbonyl compound forms second azomethine group (Scheme 1).

Single crystal X-ray diffraction studies

The 3D structure of molecules 3, 5, 7, 9 and 10 with labelling of atoms are given in Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Selected bond lengths and bond angles are listed in Tables 1 and 2. Symmetrical azines have centre of symmetry, hence one part of molecule is exactly similar to another part. The bond length of azomethine groups in the azines in the range of 1.283–1.274 Å indicates that it is a characteristic of double bond between carbon and nitrogen62. The N-N bond length of molecules in the range of 1.4135–1.403 Å revealed the single bond character63. Substituents attached to phenyl ring carbon such as Br, Cl, NO2 and OCH3 showed bond length in the range of 1.903 Å -1.3464 Å, which is a characteristics of single bond64,65 This kind of single bond length changes are owing to bulkiness and electronegativity of substituents. Azomethine groups attached to phenyl rings showed bond length in the range of 1.4687–1.4631 Å which agree with single bond nature. Benzene rings in the molecules is delocalized bonding structure, confirms C-C bond lengths of phenyl rings66. Furthermore, this analysis clearly indicates that there is no inter and intra molecular hydrogen bonds in the molecules.

Photoluminescence spectra

The electronic spectrum of azines 1–10 recorded in CCl4 (1.0 mM) showed absorption in the range of 276–286 nm and 323–361 nm correspond to π- π* and n- π* transitions respectively shown in Fig. 6(a). The luminescence spectra of azines 1–10 were investigated in CCl4 (1.0 mM) at room temperature. The emission spectra are shown in Fig. 6(b), (c) and (d) and data are listed in Table 3. Molecules 1–10 are excited in the range of 334–360 nm, attributed to n→π* absorption of the compounds. The compounds 1–10 (except 8) showed fluorescence emission band in the range of 405–408 nm with different emission intensity. Another emission band (delayed fluorescence) displayed in the range of 423.4–432 nm with different emission intensity. Both emissions are falling in the violet region, have more energy. Isatin azine 6 containing electron withdrawing amide group showed enhanced third emission band at 516 nm, corresponds to green region. The results of this study disclose that methoxy group substituted azines displayed weak emission intensity, and halogenated and unsubstituted azines showed moderate emission intensity67,68. But, electron withdrawing amide and nitro group substituted azines showed enhanced emission.69 However, electron withdrawing groups in the molecules convert azine function as donating group hence forming the Donor– π -Acceptor systems, facilitates enhanced emission70. Whereas, electron donating group or no substituents in the compounds, azines acts as electron withdrawing group displayed less emission. This results revealed that azine function in aromatic rings with electron withdrawing/donating groups/no substituent facilitates intersystem crossing in the molecules give phosphorescence71. Molecule 8 showed enhanced fluorescence emission at 541.6 nm, which is coming in green region. Azines having hydroxyl group at ortho position to azine function may involves intramolecular hydrogen bond reduce the (π, π*) singlet transition to (n, π*) triplet state and vice versa72.

DFT Analysis

The theoretical simulation for the optimized geometries were improved in vacuum for the molecules. The photophysical properties of azines 1–10 such as electron density, HOMO, LUMO and energy gap(∆E) of the luminogens are given in Figs. 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 and 16; Table 4. These results revealed that the electron density of the molecule is found over the entire molecules of the series except compound 7. It is due to conjugation throughout the molecules, facilitated by continues π and n electrons73. The compound 7 showed more electron density on azine function and also substituent nitro groups, poor electron density on aromatic rings. Which is due to electron withdrawing nature of nitro and donating nature of azine group through aromatic rings74. The energy gap of compound 2 showed high value (9.86) owing to no substituents in the molecule. Aldazines having electron donating, withdrawing and bulky substituents like Cl, Br, OCH3, NO2 and OH in the ortho, para and meta-position and also ketazines containing CH3, C6H5 group displayed moderate ∆E values (4.3–3.84)75. The compound 6 showed less energy difference (3.39) owing to electron withdrawing secondary amide group within the ring. The energy gap difference in the molecules are mainly depending on nature of substituents (electron withdrawing/donating groups) and position of substituents in aromatic rings. Furthermore, these results are showing good consent with experimental study76.

Conclusion

The study effectively demonstrates the catalytic activity of carboxylic acid ester groups to synthesis azines. Catalytic activity of carboxylic ester group in an aliphatic/aromatic compounds was confirmed by using three different esters in the preparation of azines at very less refluxing time and mild condition. All the three esters gave almost same yield. To date, carboxylic acid esters have not been used as catalyst for any chemical reactions, but in some studies, they are used as chemical reactant. This reaction technique, only condensation reaction took place between two amino groups in hydrazine hydrate and carbonyl compounds simultaneously. The final products are very less solubility in the medium prepared, hence work up is easy and directly obtained the pure products. The formation of azine function joining two molecules and gives conjugation throughout the molecules. This research work revealed that azine function make avenue for intersystem crossing (ISC) in the molecules through intramolecular transitions. Stable and efficient pure organic room temperature phosphorescence (RTP) materials can be prepared by changing the aromatic systems and substituents with azine function. Which could be used in material science to fabricate low cost and high efficient devices.

Materials and methods

All the chemicals have been commercially procured and used without further purification. Ethyl chloroacetate (99%), ethyl acetoacetate (99%), ethyl benzoate (99%), hydrazinium hydroxide (100%), benzaldehyde (99%), 4-bromobenzaldehyde (99%), 4-chlorobenzaldehyde (97%), acetophenone (99%), benzophenone (99%), isatin (97%), 3,4-dimethoxybenzaldehyde (99%), 3-nitrobenzaldehyde (99%), salicylaldehyde (98%) and 3,4,5-trimethoxybenzaldehyde (98%) were purchased from Merck, India. The solvents methanol (99.5%), 1,4-dioxane (99%), dimethyl sulphoxide (99%) and carbon tetrachloride (99%) were obtained from Sisco Chem Pvt.Ltd., India.

Melting point of the compounds was recorded using open capillary tube in Sigma melting point apparatus, Sigma instruments. 1H and13C NMR spectra recorded in CDCl3 using Bruker-400 MHz at room temperarure. Single X-ray diffraction data was recorded on a XtaLAB Synergy, Single source at home/near, HyPix diffractometer. FT-IR spectra were recorded in the 400–4000 cm−1 region (KBr disc) on a SHIMADZU, FTIR-8400 S. The LC–MS of the compounds were recorded in CHCl3 on Thermo, LCQ Deca XP MAX. Electronic spectra of azines were measured on Elico-150 in CCl4 as solvent. The photoluminescence properties of luminophores were recorded on a Jobin Yvon spectrofluorimeter (Fluorolog-3) having 450 W xenon lamp as an excitation source. The photophysical properties of compounds are calculated by Gaussian09 software.

Ethyl chloroacetate (1.2 mM) and hydrazine hydrate (1 mM) were dissolved in methanol and kept stirring at room temperature for 10 min. The carbonyl compounds (2 mM) in methanol was added to the above mixture and refluxed for about 30 min. This reaction mixture was cooled to room temperature and formed precipitate was filtered, washed with cold methanol and air dried (Scheme 2).

X-ray crystallography

The azines were dissolved in 1,4-dioxane and kept at room temperature for five days which gradually resulted in the formation of crystals. Single crystal X-ray data were collected using a Rigaku XtaLAB Synergy-S diffractometer equipped with a HyPix-6000HE Hybrid Photon Counting (HPC) detector and Cu microfocus sealed X-ray source as well as a low-temperature Oxford cobra, nitrogen gas cooling system. The data collection strategy was calculated within CrysAlis PRO (Rigaku OD, 2022) to ensure desired data redundancy and percent completeness. The structure is solved using SHELXT77 and refined by least-squares refinement on F2 followed by difference Fourier synthesis (OLEX2, SHELXL)78,79. The space group determination was performed by using PLATON80. The Olex278 graphical user interface was used to visualize and manipulate the structure solution and refinement process. All non-hydrogen atom positions were located using difference Fourier methods and refined anisotropically. Hydrogen atom positions were calculated using the HFIX command and constrained during the refinement. The crystallographic data of compounds are summarized in Table 5.

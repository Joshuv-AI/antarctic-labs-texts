Nijenhuis operators and twisted 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on Nambu-Poisson algebras
Apurba Das, Fattoum Harrathi and Sami Mabrouk

Abstract
A ternary Nambu-Poisson algebra (which we call a Nambu-Poisson algebra in the paper) is the underlying algebraic structure of Nambu-Poisson manifolds of order 3 3 that appeared in the generalized Hamiltonian mechanics. First, we consider the 2nd cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in a given representation. Next, we discuss suitable linear deformations of a Nambu-Poisson algebra and show that any such trivial deformation yields a Nijenhuis operator on it. To understand the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra obtained from a Nijenhuis operator, we introduce a new algebraic structure, which we name NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras. Finally, we consider 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators twisted by 2 2 -cocycles and find their close relationships with NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras.



Keywords:

3
-Lie algebra, Nambu-Poisson algebra, Nijenhuis operator, deformation, Rota-Baxter operator.

17B63, 17A40, 17B38, 17B40.

1
Introduction

1.1
Nambu-Poisson algebras

In 1973, Y. Nambu
[
26
]
introduced a generalization of classical Hamiltonian mechanics based on
n
-ary brackets replacing the conventional binary Poisson brackets. The evolution of a system in this generalized Hamiltonian mechanics is governed by the flows of vector fields obtained from
(n-1)
Hamiltonian functions
[
29
]
. Nambu himself proposed an example of such an
n
-ary bracket defined on the space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq n):

\{f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\}=\det\left(\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j=1}^{n},

(1.1)

for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. This
n
-ary bracket satisfies the properties analogous to those of the classical Poisson bracket. Namely, the bracket (
1.1
) is skew-symmetric, satisfies the Leibniz rule (with respect to the product of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
) and the following fundamental identity:

\displaystyle\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1},\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1},\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1},g_{i}\},g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n}\},

(1.2)

for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, that makes the space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
into a Nambu-Poisson manifold of order
n

[
29
]
. The identity (
1.2
) is an
n
-ary generalization of the classical Jacobi identity, and it is independently studied by Filippov
[
17
]
. An algebraic abstraction of the structures of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
provided above is known as the Nambu-Poisson algebra of order
n
. Explicitly, a Nambu-Poisson algebra of order
n
is a commutative associative algebra equipped with an
n
-ary skew-symmetric bracket that satisfies the Leibniz rule and the fundamental identity. It turns out that a Nambu-Poisson algebra of order
n=2
is nothing but a Poisson algebra. In this paper, we shall focus only on Nambu-Poisson algebras of order
n=3
, which we simply call Nambu-Poisson algebras for our convenience.

1.2
Nijenhuis operators, NS-algebras and twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators

Nijenhuis operators were originally investigated by Dorfman
[
15
]
through the lens of Lie algebra deformations. More precisely, any trivial linear deformation of a Lie algebra induces a Nijenhuis operator on it. Conversely, any Nijenhuis operator on a Lie algebra generates a trivial linear deformation. Nijenhuis operators also play a significant role in the theory of integrable systems and nonlinear evolution equations
[
15
]
. Subsequently, Nijenhuis operators and their intimations with trivial linear deformations have been extended to the context of associative algebras, Leibniz algebras,
3
-Lie algebras and to many other algebraic settings
[
9
,
16
,
19
,
24
,
22
,
28
]
. In
[
31
,
19
,
22
]
, it has been observed that Nijenhuis operators are also useful in constructing new algebraic structures. More precisely, Uchino
[
31
]
considered NS-algebras as the structure behind Nijenhuis operators on associative algebras. Later, NS-Lie algebras
[
13
]
and NS-
3
-Lie algebras
[
12
,
21
]
are also investigated while studying Nijenhuis operators on Lie algebras and
3
-Lie algebras, respectively. In
[
5
]
, the authors have recently studied Nijenhuis operators on Poisson algebras and investigated the deformed structures. In particular, they introduced NS-Poisson algebras as the underlying structure behind Nijenhuis operators.

In recent years, Rota-Baxter operators and their variants have attracted significant attention due to their connections with the splitting of algebras and the discovery of various pre-algebras
[
3
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
20
,
31
]
. Twisted Rota-Baxter operators (and more generally, twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators) were first considered by Uchino
[
31
]
in the setting of associative algebras. Since then, these operators have been extensively studied
[
19
,
14
]
and generalized in various algebraic contexts, including Lie algebras
[
13
]
,
3
-Lie algebras
[
12
,
11
,
21
]
and Poisson algebras
[
5
]
. While NS-algebras arise naturally from Nijenhuis operators, they are more intimately associated with twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on associative algebras
[
14
,
31
]
. More precisely, any twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator induces an NS-algebra structure, and any NS-algebra arises in this way. In the same spirit, it has been observed that NS-Lie algebras, NS-
3
-Lie algebras and NS-Poisson algebras are closely related to twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras,
3
-Lie algebras and Poisson algebras, respectively. A Reynolds operator is a particular case of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator and hence related to NS-algebras
[
31
,
13
]
.

1.3
Overview of the main results

This paper aims to extend the theory of Nijenhuis operators, NS-algebras and twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators to the framework of Nambu-Poisson algebras. We begin by introducing the second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in a representation. Next, we consider
(1,2)
-linear deformations of a Nambu-Poisson algebra. By an
(1,2)
-linear deformation of a Nambu-Poisson algebra, we shall mean a deformation allowing degree
1
linear deformation of the underlying commutative associative product and degree
2
linear deformation of the Lie bracket. In particular, we show that a trivial
(1,2)
-linear deformation of a Nambu-Poisson algebra yields a Nijenhuis operator on it. Subsequently, we show that a Nijenhuis operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra deforms the given Nambu-Poisson structure to a new one. To better understand this deformed Nambu-Poisson structure obtained from a Nijenhuis operator, we introduce the notion of NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras (that can be regarded as the ternary version of NS-Poisson algebras) and show that this new algebraic structure splits Nambu-Poisson algebras. Subsequently, we consider twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a Nambu-Poisson algebra with respect to a given representation and show that such operators naturally induce NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras. Conversely, we observe that any NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra arises in this way.

1.4
Organization

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some necessary background on Nambu-Poisson algebras and their representations. In Section
3
, we consider
(1,2)
-linear deformations of a Nambu-Poisson algebra and show that trivial
(1,2)
-linear deformations induce Nijenhuis operators on the Nambu-Poisson algebra. We introduce and study the notion of NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras in Section
4
. NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras are a splitting of Nambu-Poisson algebras. Finally, in Section
5
, we first define the second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in a given representation. Subsequently, we consider twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Nambu-Poisson algebras and find their close relationships with NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras.

All vector spaces, linear and multilinear maps are over a field
k
of characteristic
0
.

Some background on Nambu-Poisson algebras

In this section, we recall some necessary definitions regarding Nambu-Poisson algebras and their representations

,

,

,

,
1
]
. To do this, we begin with some basics of

[

,

.

Definition 2.1
.

A
3
-Lie algebra
is a pair
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
consisting of a vector space
\mathcal{L}
endowed with a skew-symmetric trilinear operation
\{~,~,~\}:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
satisfying the following fundamental identity:

\displaystyle\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}\}=\{\{a,b,c\},d,e\}+\{c,\{a,b,d\},e\}+\{c,d,\{a,b,e\}\},\text{ for }a,b,c,d,e\in\mathcal{L}.

(2.1)

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
and
(\mathcal{L}^{\prime},\{~,~,~\}^{\prime})
be two
3
-Lie algebras. A
homomorphism
of
3
-Lie algebras from
\mathcal{L}
to
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
is a linear map
f:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
satisfying
f(\{a,b,c\})=\{f(a),f(b),f(c)\}^{\prime}
, for all
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. A
representation
of this
3
-Lie algebra is given by a pair
(V;\rho)
of a vector space
V
with a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
such that for all
a,b,c,d\in\mathcal{L}
, the following identities are hold:

\displaystyle\rho(a,b)\rho(c,d)-\rho(c,d)\rho(a,b)=~

\displaystyle\rho(\{a,b,c\},d)+\rho(c,\{a,b,d\}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\rho(\{a,b,c\},d)=~

\displaystyle\rho(a,b)\rho(c,d)+\rho(b,c)\rho(a,d)+\rho(c,a)\rho(b,d).

(2.3)

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. Then it follows that the pair
(\mathcal{L};\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})
is a representation, where the map
\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(\mathcal{L})
is given by
\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}(a,b)(c)=\{a,b,c\}
, for
a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}
. This is called the
adjoint representation
.

The following result is a characterization of representations of a
3
-Lie algebra.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
be a
3
-Lie algebra. Suppose
V
is a vector space with a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
. Then
(V;\rho)
is a representation of the given
3
-Lie algebra if and only if the operation

\displaystyle\{(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\}_{\ltimes}:=(\{a,b,c\}~\!,~\!\rho(a,b)w+\rho(b,c)u+\rho(c,a)v),

for
(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\in\mathcal{L}\oplus V
, makes
(\mathcal{L}\oplus V,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes})
into a
3
-Lie algebra.

Definition 2.4
.

A
ternary Nambu-Poisson algebra
(or simply a
Nambu-Poisson algebra
) is a triple
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
consisting of a commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
and a
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
both defined on a same vector space satisfying the following Leibniz rule:

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\cdot d\}=\{a,b,c\}\cdot d~\!+~\!c\cdot\{a,b,d\},\text{ for }a,b,c,d\in A.

(2.4)

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
and
(A^{\prime},~\!\cdot^{\prime}~\!,\{~,~,~\}^{\prime})
be two Nambu-Poisson algebras. A
homomorphism
from
A
to
A^{\prime}
is a linear map
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
that is both a commutative associative algebra homomorphism and a
3
-Lie algebra homomorphism.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. A
representation
of it, consists of a triple
(V;\mu,\rho)
of a vector space
V
with a linear map
\mu:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho:A\times A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
such that

•

(V;\mu)
is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
, i.e.
\mu(a\cdot b)=\mu(a)\mu(b)
,

•

(V;\rho)
is a representation of the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
,

•

for all
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle\mu(\{a,b,c\})=\rho(a,b)\mu(c)-\mu(c)\rho(a,b)\quad\text{ and }\quad\rho(a,b\cdot c)=\mu(b)\rho(a,c)+\mu(c)\rho(a,b).

(2.5)

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be any Nambu-Poisson algebra. We define a linear map
\mu_{\mathrm{ad}}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho_{\mathrm{ad}}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
by

\displaystyle(\mu_{\mathrm{ad}})(a)b=a\cdot b~~~\text{ and }~~~(\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})(a,b)c=\{a,b,c\},\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

Then the triple
(A;\mu_{\mathrm{ad}},\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})
is a representation, called the
adjoint representation
of the Nambu-Poisson algebra.

Similar to Proposition
2.3
, here we have the following characterization of a representation.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. Suppose
V
is a vector space endowed with a linear map
\mu:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho:A\times A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
. Then
(V;\mu,\rho)
is a representation of the given Nambu-Poisson algebra if and only if the operations

\displaystyle(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes}(b,v):=~

\displaystyle(a\cdot b~\!,~\!\mu(a)v+\mu(b)u),

\displaystyle\{(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\}_{\ltimes}:=~

\displaystyle(\{a,b,c\}~\!,~\!\rho(a,b)w+\rho(b,c)u+\rho(c,a)v),

for
(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\in A\oplus V
, makes the triple
(A\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes})
into a Nambu-Poisson algebra.

Note that the fundamental identity (
2.1
) given in the definition of a
3
-Lie algebra can be regarded as the ternary version of the classical Jacobi identity. By fixing one coordinate of the
3
-Lie bracket, one obtains a bilinear skew-symmetric bracket that turns out to satisfy the Jacobi identity. More generally, we have the following result.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. For any fixed
x_{0}\in A
, we define a bilinear skew-symmetric bracket
\{~,~\}_{x_{0}}:A\times A\rightarrow A
by
\{a,b\}_{x_{0}}:=\{x_{0},a,b\}
, for all
a,b\in A
. Then
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
is a Poisson algebra.

Additionally, let
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of the given Nambu-Poisson algebra. We define a linear map
\rho_{x_{0}}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(V)
by
\rho_{x_{0}}(a)(u)=\rho(x_{0},a)u
, for
a\in A
and
u\in V
. Then
(V;\mu,\rho_{x_{0}})
is a representation of the Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
.

Proof.

For any
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\{a,\{b,c\}_{x_{0}}\}_{x_{0}}=\{x_{0},a,\{x_{0},b,c\}\}=~

\displaystyle\{\{x_{0},a,x_{0}\},b,c\}+\{x_{0},\{x_{0},a,b\},c\}+\{x_{0},b,\{x_{0},a,c\}\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{\{a,b\}_{x_{0}},c\}_{x_{0}}+\{b,\{a,c\}_{x_{0}}\}_{x_{0}}

and

\displaystyle\{a,b\cdot c\}_{x_{0}}=\{x_{0},a,b\cdot c\}=\{x_{0},a,b\}\cdot c~\!+~\!b\cdot\{x_{0},a,c\}=\{a,b\}_{x_{0}}\cdot c+b\cdot\{a,c\}_{x_{0}}.

This shows that
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
is a Poisson algebra. For the second part, we first observe that

\displaystyle\rho_{x_{0}}(\{a,b\}_{x_{0}})=\rho(x_{0},\{x_{0},a,b\})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{repn-1})}}{{=}}~

\displaystyle\rho(x_{0},a)\rho(x_{0},b)-\rho(x_{0},b)\rho(x_{0},a)-\rho(\{x_{0},a,x_{0}\},b)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\rho_{x_{0}}(a)\rho_{x_{0}}(b)-\rho_{x_{0}}(b)\rho_{x_{0}}(a)

which shows that
(V;\rho_{x_{0}})
is a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
. Additionally,

\displaystyle\mu(\{a,b\}_{x_{0}})=\mu(\{x_{0},a,b\})=\rho(x_{0},a)\mu(b)-\mu(b)\rho(x_{0},a)=\rho_{x_{0}}(a)\mu(b)-\mu(b)\rho_{x_{0}}(a),

\displaystyle\rho_{x_{0}}(a\cdot b)=\rho(x_{0},a\cdot b)=\mu(a)\rho(x_{0},b)+\mu(b)\rho(x_{0},a)=\mu(a)\rho_{x_{0}}(b)+\mu(b)\rho_{x_{0}}(a),

for any
a,b\in A
. Hence
(V;\mu,\rho_{x_{0}})
is a representation of the Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
.

3
Second cohomology group, linear deformations and Nijenhuis operators

In this section, we start by defining the second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in a representation. Next, we consider the notion of
(1,2)
-linear deformations of a Nambu-Poisson algebra. We observe that a trivial
(1,2)
-linear deformation of a Nambu-Poisson algebra yields a Nijenhuis operator on it.

3.1
The second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be a commutative associative algebra and
(V;\mu)
be a representation of it. A
Harrison
2
-cocycle
of
A
with coefficients in the representation
V
is simply a bilinear map
\varphi:A\times A\rightarrow V
satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi(a,b)=\varphi(b,a)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu(a)\varphi(b,c)-\varphi(a\cdot b,c)+\varphi(a,b\cdot c)-\mu(c)\varphi(a,b)=0,\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

A Harrison
2
-cocycle
\varphi
is said to be a Harrison
2
-coboundary if there exits a linear map
f:A\rightarrow V
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(a,b)=\mu(a)f(b)-f(a\cdot b)+\mu(b)f(a),\text{ for all }a,b\in A.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
be a commutative associative algebra and
(V;\mu)
be a representation of it. Then a bilinear map
\varphi:A\times A\rightarrow V
is a Harrison
2
-cocycle if and only if the operation

\displaystyle(a,u)\cdot_{\ltimes_{\varphi}}(b,v):=(a\cdot b~\!,~\!\mu(a)v+\mu(b)u+\varphi(a,b)),\text{ for }(a,u),(b,v)\in A\oplus V

(3.1)

makes
A\oplus V
into a commutative associative algebra.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation of it. Then a skew-symmetric trilinear map
\psi:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V
is said to be a
2
-cocycle
of the
3
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
with coefficients in the representation
V
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\psi(a,b,\{c,d,e\})+\rho(a,b)\psi(c,d,e)=~

\displaystyle\psi(\{a,b,c\},d,e)+\psi(c,\{a,b,d\},e)+\psi(c,d,\{a,b,e\})

\displaystyle\quad+\rho(d,e)\psi(a,b,c)+\rho(e,c)\psi(a,b,d)+\rho(c,d)\psi(a,b,e),

(3.2)

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in\mathcal{L}
. Further, a
2
-cocycle
\psi
is said to be a
2
-coboundary
if there exists a linear map
f:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V
such that

\displaystyle\psi(a,b,c)=\rho(f(a),b)c+\rho(a,f(b))c+\rho(a,b)f(c)-f(\{a,b,c\}),\text{ for all }a,b,c\in\mathcal{L}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\{~,~,~\})
be a
3
-Lie algebra and
(V;\rho)
be a representation of it. Then a skew-symmetric trilinear map
\psi:\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}\rightarrow V
is a
2
-cocycle of
\mathcal{L}
with coefficients in the representation
V
if and only if the ternary bracket

\displaystyle\{(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\}_{\ltimes_{\psi}}:=~

\displaystyle(\{a,b,c\}~\!,~\!\rho(a,b)w+\rho(b,c)u+\rho(c,a)v+\psi(a,b,c)),

(3.3)

for
(a,u),(b,v),(c,w)\in\mathcal{L}\oplus V
, makes
(\mathcal{L}\oplus V,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes_{\psi}})
into a
3
-Lie algebra.

Inspired by the above definitions and results, we will now define the second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in a representation. To obtain the higher degree cohomology groups, we will consider a bicomplex by mixing the Harrison cochain complex and the cochain complex of the underlying

-Lie algebra in a separate article.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. A
Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle
of
A
with coefficients in the representation
V
is a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
, where

•

\varphi:A\times A\rightarrow V
is a Harrison
2
-cocycle of the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\mu)
,

•

\psi:A\times A\times A\rightarrow V
is a
2
-cocycle of the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\rho)
,

such that for all
a,b,c,d\in A
, the following compatibility holds:

\displaystyle\psi(a,b,c\cdot d)+\rho(a,b)\varphi(c,d)=\varphi(c,\{a,b,d\})+\mu(c)\psi(a,b,d)+\varphi(\{a,b,c\},d)+\mu(d)\psi(a,b,c).

(3.4)

The set of all Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycles of
A
with coefficients in the representation
V
forms an abelian group with respect to the componentwise addition. We denote this abelian group simply by
\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)
.
By generalizing Propositions

and

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Then a pair
(\varphi,\psi)
of a bilinear map
\varphi:A\times A\rightarrow V
and a skew-symmetric trilinear map
\psi:A\times A\times A\rightarrow V
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of

with coefficients in the representation

if and only if the triple
(A\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{\varphi}}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes_{\psi}})
is a Nambu-Poisson algebra, where the operations
\cdot_{\ltimes_{\varphi}}
and

are given in (

) and (

(A\oplus V,~\!\cdot_{\ltimes_{\varphi}}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{\ltimes_{\psi}})
is said to be the

-twisted semidirect product and it is denoted by

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it.
For any linear map
f:A\rightarrow V
, we define a bilinear map
\varphi_{f}:A\times A\rightarrow V
and a trilinear map
\psi_{f}:A\times A\times A\rightarrow V
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{f}(a,b):=~

\displaystyle\mu(a)f(b)-f(a\cdot b)+\mu(b)f(a),

\displaystyle\psi_{f}(a,b,c):=~

\displaystyle\rho(a,b)f(c)+\rho(b,c)f(a)+\rho(c,a)f(b)-f(\{a,b,c\}),

for
a,b,c\in A
. Then
(\varphi_{f},\psi_{f})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in
V
.

Proof.

A direct calculation shows that
\varphi_{f}
is a Harrison
2
-cocycle of the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\mu)
. Similarly,
\psi_{f}
is a
2
-cocycle of the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\rho)
. Both of these results also follow from the explicit descriptions of
2
-coboundaries in the respective cochain complexes. Thus, it remains to show the compatibility condition (
3.4
) for the pair
(\varphi_{f},\psi_{f})
. For any
a,b,c,d\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle\psi_{f}(a,b,c\cdot d)+\rho(a,b)\varphi_{f}(c,d)-\varphi_{f}(c,\{a,b,d\})-\mu(c)\psi_{f}(a,b,d)-\varphi_{f}(\{a,b,c\},d)-\mu(d)\psi_{f}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\rho(a,b)f(c\cdot d)+\rho(b,c\cdot d)f(a)+\rho(c\cdot d,a)f(b)-f(\{a,b,c\cdot d\})+\rho(a,b)\mu(c)f(d)

\displaystyle\quad-\rho(a,b)f(c\cdot d)+\rho(a,b)\mu(d)f(c)-\mu(c)f(\{a,b,d\})+f(c\cdot\{a,b,d\})-\mu(\{a,b,d\})f(c)

\displaystyle\quad-\mu(c)\rho(a,b)f(d)-\mu(c)\rho(b,d)f(a)-\mu(c)\rho(d,a)f(b)+\mu(c)f(\{a,b,d\})

\displaystyle\quad-\mu(\{a,b,c\})f(d)+f(\{a,b,c\}\cdot d)-\mu(d)f(\{a,b,c\})

\displaystyle\quad-\mu(d)\rho(a,b)f(c)-\mu(d)\rho(b,c)f(a)-\mu(d)\rho(c,a)f(b)+\mu(d)f(\{a,b,c\})

\displaystyle=0\quad(\text{by using the identities in }(\ref{np3-rep}))

which verifies (
3.4
). Hence, the result is proved.

A Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle is said to be a Nambu-Poisson
2
-coboundary if it is of the form
(\varphi_{f},\psi_{f})
, for some linear map
f:A\rightarrow V
. We denote the space of all Nambu-Poisson
2
-coboundaries of
A
with coefficients in
V
by the notation
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)
. Then it follows from Lemma
3.5
that
\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)\subset\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)
. The quotient group

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V):=\frac{\mathcal{Z}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)}{\mathcal{B}^{2}_{\mathrm{NP}}(A,V)}

is said to be the
second cohomology group
of the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
.

In Proposition
2.7
, we have seen that fixing a coordinate of the
3
-Lie bracket of a Nambu-Poisson algebra gives rise to a Poisson algebra structure. In the following, a similar result has been proved for the corresponding
2
-cocycles (see
[
5
]
for the explicit description of a Poisson
2
-cocycle).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Suppose
(\varphi,\psi)
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in
V
. For any fixed
x_{0}\in A
, we define a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\psi_{x_{0}}:A\times A\rightarrow V
by
\psi_{x_{0}}(a,b)=\psi(x_{0},a,b)
, for
a,b\in A
. Then the pair
(\varphi,\psi_{x_{0}})
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle of the Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\mu,\rho_{x_{0}}).

Proof.

For any
a,b,c\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle(\rho_{x_{0}}(a))\psi_{x_{0}}(b,c)+(\rho_{x_{0}}(b))\psi_{x_{0}}(c,a)+(\rho_{x_{0}}(c))\psi_{x_{0}}(a,b)

\displaystyle\qquad+\psi_{x_{0}}(a,\{b,c\}_{x_{0}})+\psi_{x_{0}}(b,\{c,a\}_{x_{0}})+\psi_{x_{0}}(c,\{a,b\}_{x_{0}})

\displaystyle=\rho(x_{0},a)\psi(x_{0},b,c)+\rho(x_{0},b)\psi(x_{0},c,a)+\rho(x_{0},c)\psi(x_{0},a,b)

\displaystyle\qquad+\psi(x_{0},a,\{x_{0},b,c\})+\psi(x_{0},b,\{x_{0},c,a\})+\psi(x_{0},c,\{x_{0},a,b\})=0\quad(\text{using }(\ref{2co-3})).

Hence
\psi_{x_{0}}
is a
2
-cocycle of the Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
with coefficients in the representation
(V;\rho_{x_{0}})
. Moreover, we also have

\displaystyle\psi_{x_{0}}(a,b\cdot c)+(\rho_{x_{0}}(a))\varphi(b,c)

\displaystyle=\psi(x_{0},a,b\cdot c)+\rho(x_{0},a)\varphi(b,c)

\displaystyle=\varphi(b,\{x_{0},a,c\})+\mu(b)\psi(x_{0},a,c)+\varphi(\{x_{0},a,b\},c)+\mu(c)\psi(x_{0},a,b)\quad(\text{by }(\ref{2co-np}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(b,\{a,c\}_{x_{0}})+\mu(b)\psi_{x_{0}}(a,c)+\varphi(\{a,b\}_{x_{0}},c)+\mu(c)\psi_{x_{0}}(a,b).

This proves that
(\varphi,\psi_{x_{0}})
is a Poisson
2
-cocycle.

The above result shows that Definition
3.3
of a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle is compatible with Poisson
2
-cocycles.

3.2
Linear deformations and Nijenhuis operators

Definition 3.7
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. An
(1,2)
-linear deformation

~\cdot_{t}:A\times A\rightarrow A

\displaystyle a\cdot_{t}b=a\cdot b+t~\!\varphi_{1}(a,b)~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\{a,b,c\}_{t}=\{a,b,c\}+t~\!\psi_{1}(a,b,c)+t^{2}~\!\psi_{2}(a,b,c)

(1,2)

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})

\varphi_{1}:A\times A\rightarrow A

\psi_{1},\psi_{2}:A\times A\times A\rightarrow A

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

for all

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(a,b,\{c,d,e\})+\{a,b,\psi_{2}(c,d,e)\}+\psi_{1}(a,b,\psi_{1}(c,d,e))=\psi_{2}(\{a,b,c\},d,e)

\displaystyle\qquad+\{\psi_{2}(a,b,c),d,e\}+\psi_{1}(\psi_{1}(a,b,c),d,e)+\psi_{2}(c,\{a,b,d\},e)+\{c,\psi_{2}(a,b,d),e\}

\displaystyle\qquad+\psi_{1}(c,\psi_{1}(a,b,d),e)+\psi_{2}(c,d,\{a,b,e\})+\{c,d,\psi_{2}(a,b,e)\}+\psi_{1}(c,d,\psi_{1}(a,b,e)),

\displaystyle\quad+\psi_{1}(c,\psi_{2}(a,b,d),e)+\psi_{2}(c,\psi_{1}(a,b,d),e)+\psi_{1}(c,d,\psi_{2}(a,b,e))+\psi_{2}(c,d,\psi_{1}(a,b,e)),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(a,b,c\cdot d)+\psi_{1}(a,b,\varphi_{1}(c,d))=~

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(c,\psi_{1}(a,b,d))+c\cdot\psi_{2}(a,b,d)

\displaystyle+\varphi_{1}(\psi_{1}(a,b,c),d)+\psi_{2}(a,b,c)\cdot d.

Proof.

Consider the parametrized operations

(a\cdot_{t}b)\cdot_{t}c=a\cdot_{t}(b\cdot_{t}c)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a\cdot b,c)+\varphi_{1}(a,b)\cdot c=~

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a,b\cdot c)+a\cdot\varphi_{1}(b,c),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(\varphi_{1}(a,b),c)=~

Next,

\displaystyle\{a,b,\{c,d,e\}_{t}\}_{t}=\{\{a,b,c\}_{t},d,e\}_{t}+\{c,\{a,b,d\}_{t},e\}_{t}+\{c,d,\{a,b,e\}_{t}\}_{t},

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(a,b,\{c,d,e\})+\{a,b,\psi_{1}(c,d,e)\}=\psi_{1}(\{a,b,c\},d,e)+\{\psi_{1}(a,b,c),d,e\}

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(a,b,\psi_{2}(c,d,e))=\psi_{2}(\psi_{2}(a,b,c),d,e)+\psi_{2}(c,\psi_{2}(a,b,d),e)+\psi_{2}(c,d,\psi_{2}(a,b,e))

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\cdot_{t}d\}_{t}=\{a,b,c\}_{t}\cdot_{t}d+c\cdot_{t}\{a,b,d\}_{t}

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(a,b,c\cdot d)+\{a,b,\varphi_{1}(c,d)\}=\varphi_{1}(\{a,b,c\},d)+\psi_{1}(a,b,c)\cdot d+\varphi_{1}(c,\{a,b,d\})+c\cdot\psi_{1}(a,b,d),

Definition 3.9
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. Two

-linear deformations
(A,~\!\cdot_{t}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{t})

to
(A,~\!\cdot^{\prime}_{t}~\!,\{~,~,~\}^{\prime}_{t})
.

Note that the map

to
(A,~\!\cdot^{\prime}_{t}~\!)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a,b)-\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(a,b)=~

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b),

(3.15)

\displaystyle N(\varphi_{1}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),b)+\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(a,N(b))+N(a)\cdot N(b),

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),N(b))=~

\displaystyle 0.

(3.17)

Similarly,

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(a,b,c)-\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,b,c)=\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),b,c)+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,N(b),c)+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,b,N(c))-N(\psi_{1}(a,b,c)),

(3.19)

\displaystyle\qquad N(\psi_{2}(a,b,c))=\psi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),N(b),c)+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),b,N(c))+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,N(b),N(c))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),b,c)+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,N(b),c)+\psi_{1}^{\prime}(a,b,N(c))+\{N(a),N(b),N(c)\},

(3.20)

\displaystyle\psi_{1}^{\prime}(N(a),N(b),N(c))+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(N(a),N(b),c)+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(N(a),b,N(c))+\psi_{2}^{\prime}(a,N(b),N(c))=0,

(3.21)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\psi_{2}^{\prime}(N(a),N(b),N(c))=0,

(3.22)

for all

Definition 3.10
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. An

-linear deformation

Hence for a trivial

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(a,b)=~

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b),

\displaystyle N(\varphi_{1}(a,b))=~

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot N(b).

(3.24)

Similarly, it follows from (

\displaystyle\psi_{1}(a,b,c)=~

Definition 3.11
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. A linear map

is said to be a
Nijenhuis operator
on

if

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot N(b)=~

\displaystyle N\big(N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b)\big),

(3.28)

\displaystyle\{N(a),N(b),N(c)\}=~

\displaystyle N\big(\{N(a),N(b),c\}+\{N(a),b,N(c)\}+\{a,N(b),N(c)\}

\displaystyle\qquad-N(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),a\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\})\big),

(3.29)

for all
a,b,c\in A
. The first condition is equivalent to saying that

is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
while the second condition is equivalent to saying that
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot_{t}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{t})
be a trivial
(1,2)
-linear deformation of a Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
, and let
(\mathrm{Id}+tN):A\rightarrow A
defines an equivalence of the deformation
(A,~\!\cdot_{t}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{t})
with the undeformed one. Then it follows from (
3.23
)-(
3.27
) that the map
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\}).

It is well-known that the higher powers of a Nijenhuis operator (defined on a Lie algebra) are also Nijenhuis operators. This result is also known for Nijenhuis operators defined on commutative associative algebras
[
9
]
and on
3
-Lie algebras
[
24
]
. Combining their results, we get the following.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then for any
k\geq 0
, the map
N^{k}:A\rightarrow A
is also a Nijenhuis operator on the given Nambu-Poisson algebra.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Then the triple
A_{N}=(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{N})
is a new Nambu-Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle a\cdot_{N}b:=~

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot b+a\cdot N(b)-N(a\cdot b),

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\}_{N}:=~

\displaystyle\{N(a),N(b),c\}+\{N(a),b,N(c)\}+\{a,N(b),N(c)\}

\displaystyle\qquad-N(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),a\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\}),

for
a,b,c\in A
. Moreover,
N:A_{N}\rightarrow A
is a homomorphism of Nambu-Poisson algebras.

Proof.

Since
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
, it follows that
(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!)
is also a commutative associative algebra
[
9
]
. Also,
N
is a Nijenhuis operator on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
implies that
(A,\{~,~,~\}_{N})
is also a
3
-Lie algebra
[
24
]
. Next, a direct (but long) calculation shows that the Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\cdot_{N}d\}_{N}=\{a,b,c\}_{N}\cdot_{N}d+c\cdot_{N}\{a,b,d\}_{N},\text{ for }a,b,c,d\in A

holds, which shows that
(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{N})
is a Nambu-Poisson algebra. Finally, the last part follows from the definition of the Nijenhuis operator.

The Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{N})
constructed in the previous theorem is said to be the
deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra
(induced by the Nijenhuis operator
N
). To understand some tedious calculations present in the above result, we introduce the notion of an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra in the next section.

4
NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras

In this section, we introduce the notion of an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra and study some basic properties. We observe that an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra naturally induces a subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra. On the other hand, we show that any Nijenhuis operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra gives rise to an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure.

Definition 4.1
.

An
NS-commutative algebra
is a triple
(A,\diamond,*)
consisting of a vector space
A
endowed with two bilinear operations
\diamond,*:A\times A\rightarrow A
in which
*
is commutative and the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle a\diamond(b\diamond c)=~

\displaystyle(a\odot b)\diamond c,

\displaystyle a\diamond(b*c)+a*(b\odot c)=~

\displaystyle b\diamond(a*c)+b*(a\odot c),

for all
a,b,c\in A
. Here, we have used the notation

\displaystyle a\odot b=a\diamond b+b\diamond a+a*b.

(4.1)

In the above definition, if the operation
\diamond
is trivial, then
(A,*)
becomes a commutative associative algebra. On the other hand, if
*
is trivial, then
(A,\diamond)
turns out to be a zinbiel algebra.

The following result is well-known
[
31
,
5
]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(A,\diamond,*)
be an NS-commutative algebra.

(i)

Then
(A,\odot)
is a commutative associative algebra, called the subadjacent algebra.

(ii)

We define a linear map
\mu_{\diamond}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
by
\mu_{\diamond}(a)b:=a\diamond b
, for
a,b\in A
. Then
(A;\mu_{\diamond})

is a representation of the subadjacent algebra
(A,\odot)
. Moreover, the bilinear map

\displaystyle\varphi_{*}:A\times A\rightarrow A~~~\text{ given by }~~~\varphi_{*}(a,b)=a*b,\text{ for }a,b\in A

is a Harrison
2
-cocycle of
(A,\odot)
with coefficients in the representation
(A;\mu_{\diamond})
.

Definition 4.3
.

An
NS-
3
-Lie algebra
is a triple
(A,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
of a vector space
A
with trilinear operations
[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket:A\times A\times A\rightarrow A
in which
[~,~,~]
is skew-symmetric on the first two arguments and
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket
is completely skew-symmetric such that for
a,b,c,d,e\in A
, the following set of identities hold:

\displaystyle[a,b,[c,d,e]]=~

\displaystyle[\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d,e]+[c,\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\},e]+[c,d,[a,b,e]],

\displaystyle[\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d,e]=~

\displaystyle[a,b,[c,d,e]]+[b,c,[a,d,e]]+[c,a,[b,d,e]],

\displaystyle\llbracket a,b,\{\!\!\{c,d,e\}\!\!\}\rrbracket+[a,b,\llbracket c,d,e\rrbracket]=~

\displaystyle\llbracket\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d,e\rrbracket+\llbracket c,\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\},e\rrbracket+\llbracket c,d,\{\!\!\{a,b,e\}\!\!\}\rrbracket

\displaystyle+[d,e,\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket]+[e,c,\llbracket a,b,d\rrbracket]+[c,d,\llbracket a,b,e\rrbracket].

Here, we have used the notation

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}=[a,b,c]+[b,c,a]+[c,a,b]+\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket.

(4.2)

In the above definition, if
[~,~,~]
is trivial then
(A,\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
becomes a
3
-Lie algebra. On the other hand, if the operation
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket
is trivial, then
(A,[~,~,~])
turns out to be a pre-
3
-Lie algebra in the sense of
[
4
]
. Thus, NS-
3
-Lie algebras unify both
3
-Lie algebras and pre-
3
-Lie algebras.

The following result has been proved in
[
12
,
21
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(A,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be an NS-
3
-Lie algebra.

(i)

Then
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
is a
3
-Lie algebra, where
\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}
is given in (
4.2
). (This is called the subadjacent
3
-Lie algebra)

(ii)

We define a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho_{[~,~,~]}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
and a skew-symmetric trilinear map
\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}:A\times A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle(\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b))c=[a,b,c]~~~\text{ and }~~~\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(a,b,c)=\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket,\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

Then
(A;\rho_{[~,~,~]})
is a representation of the subadjacent
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
. Additionally, the map
\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}
is a
2
-cocycle of
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
with coefficients in the representation
(A;\rho_{[~,~,~]})
.

We are now in a position to define the notion of an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra.

Definition 4.5
.

An
NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra
is a quintuple
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
, where

•

(A,\diamond,*)
is an NS-commutative associative algebra,

•

(A,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
is an NS-
3
-Lie algebra,

such that for all
a,b,c,d\in A
, the following compatibilities hold:

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\diamond d=~

\displaystyle[a,b,c\diamond d]-c\diamond[a,b,d],

(4.3)

\displaystyle[a\odot b,c,d]=~

\displaystyle a\diamond[b,c,d]+b\diamond[a,c,d],

(4.4)

\displaystyle\llbracket a,b,c\odot d\rrbracket+[a,b,c*d]=~

\displaystyle c*\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}+c\diamond\llbracket a,b,d\rrbracket+\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}*d+d\diamond\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket,

(4.5)

where the operations
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}
are given in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively.

Remark 4.6
.

In an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
, if the operations
\diamond
and
[~,~,~]
are trivial, then
(A,*,\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
simply becomes a Nambu-Poisson algebra. On the other hand, if the operations
*
and
\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket
are trivial, then
(A,\diamond,[~,~,~])
becomes a pre-Nambu-Poisson algebra introduced in
[
20
]
in the study of the Nambu-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation. Thus, NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras unify both Nambu-Poisson algebras and pre-Nambu-Poisson algebras.

In the following result, we show that an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra induces a Nambu-Poisson algebra structure, a suitable representation of it and a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra.

(i)

Then
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
is a Nambu-Poisson algebra, where the operations
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}
are given in (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), respectively. (This is called the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra).

(ii)

The triple
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
is a representation of the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
. Moreover,
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra with coefficients in the above representation
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
.

Proof.

(i) Since
(A,\diamond,*)
is an NS-commutative algebra, it follows from Proposition
4.2
(i) that
(A,\odot)
is a commutative associative algebra. Similarly,
(A,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
is an NS-
3
-Lie algebra implies that
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
is a
3
-Lie algebra (cf. Proposition
4.4
(i)). Thus, it remains to verify the Leibniz rule. For any
a,b,c,d\in A
, we observe that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\odot d\}\!\!\}=[a,b,c\diamond d]+[a,b,d\diamond c]+[a,b,c*d]+[b,c\odot d,a]+[c\odot d,a,b]+\llbracket a,b,c\odot d\rrbracket.

(4.6)

On the other hand, we see that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\odot d=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\diamond d+d\diamond[a,b,c]+d\diamond[b,c,a]+d\diamond[c,a,b]+d\diamond\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket+\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}*d

and

\displaystyle c\odot\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}=c\diamond[a,b,d]+c\diamond[b,d,a]+c\diamond[d,a,b]+c\diamond\llbracket a,b,d\rrbracket+\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}\diamond c+c*\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}.

Hence, by expanding (
4.6
) using the identities (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we get the Leibniz rule

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\odot d\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\odot d+c\odot\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}.

This concludes the proof.

(ii) We have already seen that
(A;\mu_{\diamond})
is a representation of the commutative associative algebra
(A,\odot)
, and
(A;\rho_{[~,~,~]})
is a representation of the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
. Thus, to show that
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
is a representation of the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra, we need to verify the identities in (
2.5
). For any
a,b,c,d\in A
, we see that

\displaystyle\mu_{\diamond}(\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\})d=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\diamond d=~

\displaystyle[a,b,c\diamond d]-c\diamond[a,b,d]\qquad(\text{by }(\ref{ns-np1}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big((\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b))\mu_{\diamond}(c)-\mu_{\diamond}(c)(\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b))\big)d

and

\displaystyle(\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b\odot c))d=[a,b\odot c,d]=~

\displaystyle b\diamond[a,c,d]+c\diamond[a,b,d]\qquad(\text{by }~(\ref{ns-np2}))

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\big(\mu_{\diamond}(b)\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,c)+\mu_{\diamond}(c)\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b)\big)d.

Hence, this part follows. Similarly, to show that
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle, we only need to verify the identity (
3.4
). For this, we observe that

\displaystyle\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(a,b,c\odot d)+\rho_{[~,~,~]}(a,b)\varphi_{*}(c,d)

\displaystyle=\llbracket a,b,c\odot d\rrbracket+[a,b,c*d]

\displaystyle=c*\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}+c\diamond\llbracket a,b,d\rrbracket+\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}*d+d\diamond\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket\qquad(\text{by }~(\ref{ns-np3}))

\displaystyle=\varphi_{*}(a,\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\})+\mu_{\diamond}(c)\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(a,b,d)+\varphi_{*}(\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\},d)+\mu_{\diamond}(d)\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(a,b,c).

Hence, this part also follows. This completes the proof.

We will now show that NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras arise naturally from Nijenhuis operators on Nambu-Poisson algebras.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. We define operations

\displaystyle a\diamond_{N}b:=N(a)\cdot b,\quad a*_{N}b:=-N(a\cdot b),\quad[a,b,c]_{N}:=\{N(a),N(b),c\}

\displaystyle~~\text{ and }~~\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket_{N}:=-N(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\}),

for
a,b,c\in A.
Then
(A,\diamond_{N},*_{N},[~,~,~]_{N},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{N})
is an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra. Moreover, the corresponding subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra structure coincides with the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra structure
A_{N}=(A,\cdot_{N},\{~,~,~\}_{N})
given in Theorem
3.14
.

Proof.

Since
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
, it follows that
(A,\diamond_{N},*_{N})
is an NS-commutative algebra
[
5
,
22
]
. On the other hand,
N:A\rightarrow A
is a Nijenhuis operator on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
implies that the triple
(A,[~,~,~]_{N},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{N})
is an NS-
3
-Lie algebra
[
12
,
21
]
. Thus, it remains to verify the identities (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). For any
a,b,c,d\in A
, we have

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\diamond_{N}d=N(\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\})\cdot d=~

\displaystyle\{N(a),N(b),N(c)\}\cdot d

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\{N(a),N(b),N(c)\cdot d\}-N(c)\cdot\{N(a),N(b),d\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[a,b,c\diamond_{N}d]_{N}-c\diamond_{N}[a,b,d]_{N},

\displaystyle[a\odot b,c,d]_{N}=\{N(a\odot b),N(c),d\}=~

\displaystyle\{N(a)\cdot N(b),N(c),d\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle N(a)\cdot\{N(b),N(c),d\}+N(b)\cdot\{N(a),N(c),d\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle a\diamond_{N}[b,c,d]_{N}+b\diamond_{N}[a,c,d]_{N}.

Hence the identities (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) follow. Finally,

\displaystyle\llbracket a,b,c\odot d\rrbracket_{N}+[a,b,c*_{N}d]_{N}-c*_{N}\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}-c\diamond_{N}\llbracket a,b,d\rrbracket_{N}-\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}*_{N}d-d\diamond_{N}\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket_{N}

\displaystyle=-N\big(\{N(a),b,c\odot d\}+\{a,N(b),c\odot d\}+\{a,b,N(c\odot d)\}-N\{a,b,c\odot d\}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\{N(a),N(b),N(c\cdot d)\}+N(c\cdot\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\})+N(\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\cdot d)

\displaystyle\quad+N(c)\cdot N(\{N(a),b,d\}+\{a,N(b),d\}+\{a,b,N(d)\}-N\{a,b,d\})

\displaystyle\quad+N(d)\cdot N(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\})

\displaystyle=N\Big(-\{N(a),b,c\odot d\}-\{a,N(b),c\odot d\}-\{a,b,N(c)\cdot N(d)\}+N\{a,b,c\odot d\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\{N(a),N(b),c\cdot d\}-\{N(a),b,N(c\cdot d)\}-\{a,N(b),N(c\cdot d)\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+N(\{N(a),b,c\cdot d\}+\{a,N(b),c\cdot d\}+\{a,b,N(c\cdot d)\}-N\{a,b,c\cdot d\})+c\cdot\{\!\!\{a,b,d\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}\cdot d+N(c)\cdot(\{N(a),b,d\}+\{a,N(b),d\}+\{a,b,N(d)\})-N(c)\cdot N(\{a,b,d\})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+c\cdot N(\{N(a),b,d\}+\{a,N(b),d\}+\{a,b,N(d)\}-N\{a,b,d\})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-N(c\cdot\{N(a),b,d\}+c\cdot\{a,N(b),d\}+c\cdot\{a,b,N(d)\}-c\cdot N\{a,b,d\})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+N(d)\cdot(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\})-N(d)\cdot N(\{a,b,c\})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+d\cdot N(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-N(d\cdot\{N(a),b,c\}+d\cdot\{a,N(b),c\}+d\cdot\{a,b,N(c)\}-d\cdot N\{a,b,c\})\Big).

In the above expression, one may also expand the terms
N(c)\cdot N(\{a,b,d\})
and
N(d)\cdot N(\{a,b,c\})
using the condition (
3.28
) of the Nijenhuis operator. Subsequently, by using the definition of the operations
\odot
and
\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\}
, and by using the Leibniz rule (
2.4
) of the given Nambu-Poisson algebra, one observes that all the terms of the above expression are mutually cancelled with each other. Hence the identity (
4.5
) also holds and thus
(A,\diamond_{N},*_{N},[~,~,~]_{N},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{N})
is an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra. Finally, for this structure, it is easy to see that
a\odot b=a\cdot_{N}b
and
\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}=\{a,b,c\}_{N}
, for
a,b,c\in A
. Thus, the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
coincides with the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\cdot_{N},\{~,~,~\}_{N})
.

Remark 4.9
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and let
N:A\rightarrow A
be any Nijenhuis operator on it. Then it follows from Proposition
3.13
and Theorem

that the tuple
(A,\diamond_{N^{k}},*_{N^{k}},[~,~,~]_{N^{k}},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{N^{k}})

.

Since a Nijenhuis operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra induces an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure, we obtain the following result by using Theorem
4.7
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. We define a linear map
\mu_{N}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
and a skew-symmetric bilinear map
\rho_{N}:A\times A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}(A)
by

\displaystyle\mu_{N}(a)b:=N(a)\cdot b~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\rho_{N}(a,b)c:=\{N(a),N(b),c\},\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

Then, the triple
(A;\mu_{N},\rho_{N})
is a representation of the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra
A_{N}=(A,~\!\cdot_{N}~\!,\{~,~,~\}_{N}).

We also consider a bilinear map
\varphi_{N}:A\times A\rightarrow A
and a trilinear map
\psi_{N}:A\times A\times A\rightarrow A
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{N}(a,b)=-N(a\cdot b),\quad\psi_{N}(a,b,c)=-N\big(\{N(a),b,c\}+\{a,N(b),c\}+\{a,b,N(c)\}-N\{a,b,c\}\big),

for
a,b,c\in A
. Then the pair
(\varphi_{N},\psi_{N})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra
A_{N}
with coefficients in the representation
(A;\mu_{N},\rho_{N})
.

We will return to this result when we define twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a Nambu-Poisson algebra in the next section.

5
Twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Nambu-Poisson algebras

Twisted Rota-Baxter operators and more generally twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on associative algebras were first considered in
[
31
]
. Later, these operators were generalized to Lie algebras
[
13
]
,
3
-Lie algebras
[
12
,
21
]
and Poisson algebras
[
5
]
. In this section, we shall define these operators in the context of Nambu-Poisson algebras. In particular, we show that twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a Nambu-Poisson algebra are closely related to NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras considered in the previous section.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation of it. Suppose
(\varphi,\psi)
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in the representation
V
. Then a linear map
r:V\rightarrow A
is said to be a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator
on
A
with respect to the representation
V
if it satisfies

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot r(v)=~

\displaystyle r\big(\mu(r(u))v+\mu(r(v))u+\varphi(r(u),r(v))\big),

(5.1)

\displaystyle\{r(u),r(v),r(w)\}=~

\displaystyle r\big(\rho(r(u),r(v))w+\rho(r(v),r(w))u+\rho(r(w),r(u))v+\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w))\big),

(5.2)

for all
u,v,w\in V
.

It follows from (
5.1
) that
r
is a
\varphi
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu)
. While, the condition (
5.2
) means that
r
is also a
\psi
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
.

Remark 5.2
.

(i)

When
(V;\mu,\rho)=(A;\mu_{\mathrm{ad}},\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})
is the adjoint representation of the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
, and
(\varphi,\psi)
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in the adjoint representation, a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator is simply called a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator
on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
.

(ii)

The notion of twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators on a Poisson algebra with respect to a representation was considered in
[
5
]
while studying NS-Poisson algebras. We have already seen that fixing a coordinate of the underlying
3
-Lie bracket of a Nambu-Poisson algebra, one obtains a Poisson algebra. A similar result is also obtained for the corresponding representations and
2
-cocycles (cf. Proposition
2.7
and Proposition
3.6
). Here we shall show that a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra is also a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Poisson algebra (obtained by fixing a coordinate) under some mild condition. More precisely, let
r:V\rightarrow A
be a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
. Suppose

\displaystyle u_{0}\in V~\text{ be such that }~\rho(r(u),r(v))u_{0}=0,\text{ for all }u,v\in V

and take
x_{0}=r(u_{0})\in A
. Then
r:V\rightarrow A
is also a
(\varphi,\psi_{x_{0}})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~\}_{x_{0}})
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu,\rho_{x_{0}})
. To see this, we observe that

\displaystyle\{r(u),r(v)\}_{x_{0}}

\displaystyle=\{x_{0},r(u),r(v)\}

\displaystyle=r\big(\rho(r(u_{0}),r(u))v+\rho(r(u),r(v))u_{0}+\rho(r(v),r(u_{0}))u+\psi(r(u_{0}),r(u),r(v))\big)

\displaystyle=r\big(\rho_{x_{0}}(r(u))v-\rho_{x_{0}}(r(v))u+\psi_{x_{0}}(r(u),r(v))\big),

for any
u,v\in V
. Hence, the claim follows.

In the following result, we characterize twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operators in terms of their graphs.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra,
(V;\mu,\rho)
be a representation and
(\varphi,\psi)
be a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in
V
. Then a linear map
r:V\rightarrow A
is a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
A
with respect to the representation
V
if and only if the graph

\displaystyle Graph(r)=\{(r(u),u)~\!|~\!u\in V\}

is a subalgebra of the
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted semidirect product Nambu-Poisson algebra
A\ltimes_{(\varphi,\psi)}V.

Proof.

Let
(r(u),u),(r(v),v),(r(w),w)\in Graph(r)
be arbitrary. Then from (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle(r(u),u)\cdot_{\ltimes_{\varphi}}(r(v),v)=\big(r(u)\cdot r(v)~\!,~\!\mu({r(u)})v+\mu(r(v))u+\varphi(r(u),r(v))\big)

and

\displaystyle\{(r(u),u),(r(v),v),(r(w),w)\}_{\ltimes_{\psi}}

\displaystyle\quad=\big(\{r(u),r(v),r(w)\}~\!,~\!\rho(r(u),r(v))w+\rho(r(v),r(w))u+\rho(r(w),r(u))v+\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w))\big).

Both of these elements are again in
Graph(r)
if and only if the identities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) hold. Hence, the result follows.

As a consequence of the above proposition, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
r:V\rightarrow A
be a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
. Then
V
inherits a Nambu-Poisson algebra structure whose multiplication and the
3
-Lie bracket are respectively given by

\displaystyle u\cdot_{r}v:=~

\displaystyle\mu({r(u)})v+\mu(r(v))u+\varphi(r(u),r(v)),

\displaystyle\{u,v,w\}_{r}:=~

\displaystyle\rho(r(u),r(v))w+\rho(r(v),r(w))u+\rho(r(w),r(u))v+\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w)),

for
u,v,w\in V
. This structure is said to be induced by
r
. With this structure,
r:V\rightarrow A
is a homomorphism of Nambu-Poisson algebras.

In the next result, we show that the Nambu-Poisson algebra obtained in the above proposition can be seen as the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra of an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
r:V\rightarrow A
be a
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
. Then
V
carries an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure
(V,\diamond_{r},*_{r},[~,~,~]_{r},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{r})
, where

\displaystyle u\diamond_{r}v:=\mu(r(u))v,\quad u*_{r}v:=\varphi(r(u),r(v)),\quad[u,v,w]_{r}:=\rho(r(u),r(v))w

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{ and }~\llbracket u,v,w\rrbracket_{r}:=\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w)),\text{ for }u,v,w\in V.

Moreover, the corresponding subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra coincides with the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(V,\cdot_{r},\{~,~,~\}_{r})
obtained in the previous proposition.

Proof.

Since
r:V\rightarrow A
is a
\varphi
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
with respect to the representation
(V;\mu)
, it follows that
(V,\diamond_{r},*_{r})
is an NS-commutative algebra
[
5
]
. On the other hand,
r:V\rightarrow A
is also a
\psi
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(V;\rho)
implies that
(V,[~,~,~]_{r},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{r})
is an NS-
3
-Lie algebra
[
12
,
21
]
. Next, for any
u,v,w,w^{\prime}\in V
, we observe that

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}\diamond_{r}w^{\prime}=\mu\big(r(\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\})\big)w^{\prime}=~

\displaystyle\mu(\{r(u),r(v),r(w)\})w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\rho(r(u),r(v))\mu(r(w))w^{\prime}-\mu(r(w))\rho(r(u),r(v))w^{\prime}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle[u,v,w\diamond_{r}w^{\prime}]_{r}-w\diamond_{r}[u,v,w^{\prime}]_{r},

\displaystyle[u\odot v,w,w^{\prime}]_{r}=\{r(u\odot v),r(w),w^{\prime}\}=~

\displaystyle\{r(u)\cdot r(v),r(w),w^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r(u)\cdot\{r(v),r(w),w^{\prime}\}+r(v)\cdot\{r(u),r(w),w^{\prime}\}

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle u\diamond_{r}[v,w,w^{\prime}]_{r}+v\diamond_{r}[u,w,w^{\prime}]_{r}.

and finally,

\displaystyle\llbracket u,v,w\odot w^{\prime}\rrbracket_{r}+[u,v,w*_{r}w^{\prime}]_{r}

\displaystyle=\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w\odot w^{\prime}))+\rho(r(u),r(v))(w*_{r}w^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w)\cdot r(w^{\prime}))+\rho(r(u),r(v))\varphi(r(w),r(w^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\varphi(r(w),\{r(u),r(v),r(w^{\prime})\})+\mu(r(w))\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\varphi(\{r(u),r(v),r(w)\},r(w^{\prime}))+\mu(r(w^{\prime}))\psi(r(u),r(v),r(w))

\displaystyle=w*_{r}\{\!\!\{u,v,w^{\prime}\}\!\!\}+w\diamond_{r}\llbracket u,v,w^{\prime}\rrbracket_{r}+\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\}*_{r}w^{\prime}+w^{\prime}\diamond_{r}\llbracket u,v,w\rrbracket.

This verifies the identities (
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
). Hence
(V,\diamond_{r},*_{r},[~,~,~]_{r},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{r})
is an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra. Finally, for this structure, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle u\odot v=~

\displaystyle u\diamond_{r}v+v\diamond_{r}u+u*_{r}v=u\cdot_{r}v,

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{u,v,w\}\!\!\{=~

\displaystyle[u,v,w]_{r}+[v,w,u]_{r}+[w,u,v]_{r}+\llbracket u,v,w\rrbracket_{r}=\{u,v,w\}_{r}.

Hence the corresponding subadjacent structure coincides with
(V,\cdot_{r},\{~,~,~\}_{r})
.

In the previous theorem, we have seen that a twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator induces an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure. We will now show that any NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra always arises in this way. Explicitly, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be any NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra. Then the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is a
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
with respect to the representation
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
. Moreover, the induced NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure on
A
coincides with the given one.

Proof.

For any
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}(a)\odot\mathrm{Id}(b)=~

\displaystyle a\odot b=a\diamond b+b\diamond a+a*b

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}\big(\mu_{\diamond}(\mathrm{Id}(a))b+\mu_{\diamond}(\mathrm{Id}(b))a+\varphi_{*}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b))\big),

and similarly,

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c)\}\!\!\}=\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Id}\big(\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b))c+\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c))a+\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(c),\mathrm{Id}(a))b+\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c))\big).

This shows that the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is a
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
with respect to the representation
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
. If
(A,\diamond_{\mathrm{Id}},*_{\mathrm{Id}},[~,~,~]_{\mathrm{Id}},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{\mathrm{Id}})
is the induced NS-Nambu-Poisson structure on
A
(by Theorem
5.5
) then

\displaystyle a\diamond_{\mathrm{Id}}b=\mu_{\diamond}(\mathrm{Id}(a))b=a\diamond b,\quad a*_{\mathrm{Id}}b=\varphi_{*}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b))=a*b

and

\displaystyle\text{ similarly,~}~[a,b,c]_{\mathrm{Id}}=[a,b,c]\text{ and }\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket_{\mathrm{Id}}=\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket,\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

This proves the last part.

By applying the above result to the NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra obtained from a Nijenhuis operator (cf. Theorem
4.8
and Proposition
4.10
), we get the following.

Proposition 5.7
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
N:A\rightarrow A
be a Nijenhuis operator on it. Consider the deformed Nambu-Poisson algebra
A_{N}=(A,\cdot_{N},\{~,~,~\}_{N})
, its representation
(A;\mu_{N},\rho_{N})
and the Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle
(\varphi_{N},\psi_{N})
described in Proposition
4.10
. With this, the identity map
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is a
(\varphi_{N},\psi_{N})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator. Moreover, the induced NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure on
A
coincides with the one given in Theorem
4.8
.

In Theorem
4.7
, we have seen that an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra gives rise to its subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra. The next result finds a necessary and sufficient condition under which an arbitrary Nambu-Poisson algebra arises as the subadjacent structure of an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra.

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. Then it is the subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra of an NS-Nambu-Poisson structure on
A
if and only if there exists an invertible
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator
r:V\rightarrow A
on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to a representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
and a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle
(\varphi,\psi)
. In this case, the NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure on
A
is given by

\displaystyle a\diamond b=r(\mu(a)r^{-1}(b)),\quad a*b=r(\varphi(a,b)),\quad[a,b,c]=r(\rho(a,b)r^{-1}(c))

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{and }~\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket=r(\psi(a,b,c)),\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

Proof.

Let
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
be an NS-Nambu-Poisson structure on
A
whose subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra structure is given by
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
. Then by Theorem
4.7
, the triple
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]})
is a representation of the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
, and
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle. Additionally, we see that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Id}(a)\cdot\mathrm{Id}(b)=a\cdot b=a\diamond b+b\diamond a+a*b=\mathrm{Id}\big(\mu_{\diamond}(\mathrm{Id}(a))b+\mu_{\diamond}(\mathrm{Id}(b))a+\varphi_{*}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b))\big)

and

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c)\}=\{a,b,c\}=[a,b,c]+[b,c,a]+[c,a,b]+\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Id}\Big(\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b))c+\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c))a+\rho_{[~,~,~]}(\mathrm{Id}(c),\mathrm{Id}(a))b+\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket}(\mathrm{Id}(a),\mathrm{Id}(b),\mathrm{Id}(c))\Big),

for all
a,b,c\in A
. Hence
\mathrm{Id}:A\rightarrow A
is an invertible
(\varphi_{*},\psi_{\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket})
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to the representation
(A;\mu_{\diamond},\rho_{[~,~,~]}).

Conversely, let
r:V\rightarrow A
be an invertible
(\varphi,\psi)
-twisted
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
with respect to a representation
(V;\mu,\rho)
and a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle
(\varphi,\psi)
. Then by Theorem
5.5
,
V
inherits an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure
(V,\diamond_{r},*_{r},[~,~,~]_{r},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{r})
, where the operations are explicitly given there. Since
r
is invertible, we can transfer this NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure to the vector space
A
. Explicitly, for any
a,b,c\in A
, we have

\displaystyle a\diamond b=r(r^{-1}(a)\diamond_{r}r^{-1}(b))=r(\mu(a)r^{-1}(b)),\quad a*b=r(r^{-1}(a)*_{r}r^{-1}(b))=r(\varphi(a,b)),

\displaystyle\quad[a,b,c]=r([r^{-1}(a),r^{-1}(b),r^{-1}(c)]_{r})=r(\rho(a,b)r^{-1}(c)),

\displaystyle\quad\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket=r(\llbracket r^{-1}(a),r^{-1}(b),r^{-1}(c)\rrbracket_{r})=r(\psi(a,b,c)).

If
(A,\odot,\{\!\!\{~,~,~\}\!\!\})
is the corresponding subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra structure then

\displaystyle a\odot b=a\diamond b+b\diamond a+a*b=~

\displaystyle r\big(\mu(a)r^{-1}(b)+\mu(b)r^{-1}(a)+\varphi(a,b)\big)

\displaystyle=~

\displaystyle r(r^{-1}(a))\cdot r(r^{-1}(b))=a\cdot b

and similarly,
\{\!\!\{a,b,c\}\!\!\}=\{a,b,c\}
, for all
a,b,c\in A
. Hence
(A,\diamond,*,[~,~,~],\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket)
is a desired NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra structure on
A
.

The notion of a Reynolds operator is a particular case of a twisted Rota-Baxter operator. Hence, Reynolds operators are closely related to NS-algebras
[
31
,
13
,
21
]
. We will now consider the Reynolds operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra and find its relation to NS-Nambu-Poisson algebras.

Definition 5.9
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra. A linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\rightarrow A
is said to be a
Reynolds operator
on
A
if for all
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot\mathcal{R}(b)=~

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\big(\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\mathcal{R}(b)-\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot\mathcal{R}(b)\big),

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\}=~

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}\big(\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),c\}+\{\mathcal{R}(a),b,\mathcal{R}(c)\}+\{a,\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\}-\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\}\big).

It follows that a linear map
\mathcal{R}:A\rightarrow A
is a Reynolds operator on the Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
if and only if
\mathcal{R}
is a Reynolds operator on both the commutative associative algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!)
and on the
3
-Lie algebra
(A,\{~,~,~\})
.

Remark 5.10
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and consider its adjoint representation
(A;\mu_{\mathrm{ad}},\rho_{\mathrm{ad}})
. Then it is easy to see that the pair
(\varphi_{-},\psi_{-})
is a Nambu-Poisson
2
-cocycle of
A
with coefficients in the adjoint representation, where

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}(a,b)=-a\cdot b~~~~\text{ and }~~~~\psi_{-}(a,b,c)=-\{a,b,c\},\text{ for }a,b,c\in A.

It follows that a Reynolds operator on a Nambu-Poisson algebra
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
is nothing but a
(\varphi_{-},\psi_{-})
-twisted Rota-Baxter operator on A (cf. Remark
5.2
).

Since a Reynolds operator can be realized as a twisted Rota-Baxter operator, we have the following result (as a consequence of Proposition
5.4
and Theorem
5.5
).

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
(A,~\!\cdot~\!,\{~,~,~\})
be a Nambu-Poisson algebra and
\mathcal{R}:A\rightarrow A
be a Reynolds operator on it.

(i)

Then
(A,\cdot_{\mathcal{R}},\{~,~,~\}_{\mathcal{R}})
is a Nambu-Poisson algebra, where for any
a,b,c\in A
,

\displaystyle a\cdot_{\mathcal{R}}b:=~

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot b+a\cdot\mathcal{R}(b)-\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot\mathcal{R}(b),

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\}_{\mathcal{R}}:=~

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),c\}+\{\mathcal{R}(a),b,\mathcal{R}(c)\}+\{a,\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\}-\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\}.

(ii)

The quintuple
(A,\diamond_{\mathcal{R}},*_{\mathcal{R}},[~,~,~]_{\mathcal{R}},\llbracket~,~,~\rrbracket_{\mathcal{R}})
is an NS-Nambu-Poisson algebra, where

\displaystyle a\diamond_{\mathcal{R}}b:=\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot b,\quad a*_{\mathcal{R}}b:=-\mathcal{R}(a)\cdot\mathcal{R}(b),\quad[a,b,c]_{\mathcal{R}}:=\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),c\}

\displaystyle\qquad\text{ and }\llbracket a,b,c\rrbracket_{\mathcal{R}}:=-\{\mathcal{R}(a),\mathcal{R}(b),\mathcal{R}(c)\},

for
a,b,c\in A
. The corresponding subadjacent Nambu-Poisson algebra is precisely the one
(A,\cdot_{\mathcal{R}},\{~,~,~\}_{\mathcal{R}})
given above.

Further discussions.

In
[
18
]
, Flato, Gerstenhaber and Voronov developed the cohomology theory of a Poisson algebra using an innovative bicomplex that combines the Harrison cochain complex of the underlying commutative associative algebra and the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex of the Lie algebra. It has been shown that the corresponding second cohomology groups classify infinitesimal deformations of Poisson algebras. Later, Bao and Ye
[
6
,
7
]
observed that the Flato-Gerstenhaber-Voronov cohomology of a Poisson algebra can be described by using classical Yoneda extensions or by derived functors. It is worth mentioning that Lichnerowicz
[
23
]
considered a different cohomology theory for Poisson algebras before the paper by Flato, Gerstenhaber and Voronov
[
18
]
appeared. Unlike the Flato-Gerstenhaber-Voronov cohomology (which governs the simultaneous deformations of both the commutative associative product and the Lie bracket), the Lichnerowicz cohomology only captures the deformations of the Lie bracket. In
[
2
]
, the author proposed a cohomology theory for Nambu-Poisson algebras generalizing the Lichnerowicz cohomology of Poisson algebras. Thus, their cohomology only captures the deformations of the underlying
3
-Lie bracket. However, the more general cohomology theory of Nambu-Poisson algebras (analogous to the Flato-Gerstenhaber-Voronov cohomology of Poisson algebras) is not yet considered. In a subsequent work, we aim to construct a suitable bicomplex combining the Harrison cochain complex of the underlying commutative associative algebra and the cochain complex of the
3
-Lie algebra (as described in
[
30
]
). The resulting cohomology should also generalizes the second cohomology group of a Nambu-Poisson algebra considered in the present paper.

Like twisted Rota-Baxter operators are related to NS-algebras, Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
are related to post-algebras
[
3
]
. They are also intrinsically associated with corresponding bialgebra structures. In
[
27
]
, the authors considered Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
on Poisson algebras, which led them to introduce post-Poisson algebras and Poisson bialgebras. In a forthcoming paper, we aim to extend their study to the context of Nambu-Poisson algebras. Explicitly, we develop the notions of post-Nambu-Poisson algebras, matched pairs and Manin triples of Nambu-Poisson algebras, and their relations with Nambu-Poisson bialgebras. We also aim to investigate the connections between the solutions of the Nambu-Poisson Yang-Baxter equation considered in
[
20
]
and Nambu-Poisson bialgebras.

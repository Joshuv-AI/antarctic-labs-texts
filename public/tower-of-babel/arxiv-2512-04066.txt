Instantaneous Sobolev Regularization for Dissipative Bosonic Dynamics
Pablo Costa Rico, Paul Gondolf, Tim Möbus

Abstract
We investigate quantum Markov semigroups on bosonic Fock space and identify a broad class of infinite-dimensional dissipative evolutions that exhibit instantaneous Sobolev-regularization. Motivated by stability problems in quantum computation, we show that for certain Lindblad operators that are polynomials of creation and annihilation operators, the resulting dynamics immediately transform any initial state into one with finite expectation in all powers of the number operator. A key application is in the bosonic cat code, where we obtain explicit estimates in the trace norm for the speed of convergence. These estimates sharpen existing perturbative bounds at both short and long times, offering new analytic tools for assessing stability and error suppression in bosonic quantum information processing. For example, we improve the strong exponential convergence of the (shifted) 2 2 -photon dissipation to its fixed point to the uniform topology.

Introduction

Quantum dynamical semigroups provide the mathematical framework for describing the time evolution of open quantum systems under the physically natural Markov (memoryless) assumption. From operator semigroup theory, it is well known that such a quantum Markov semigroup (QMS) is uniquely characterized by its infinitesimal generator
\mathcal{L}
, i.e., by the master equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\rho(t)=\mathcal{L}(\rho(t)),\qquad\rho(0)=\rho_{0},

(1)

for appropriate initial states
\rho_{0}
.

Motivated by well-known examples such as the quantum harmonic oscillator (
\mathcal{L}=-i[a^{\dagger}a,\cdot]
), the Bose–Hubbard model (
\mathcal{L}=-i[\sum_{i,j}\lambda_{ij}a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{j}+u_{i}(a_{i}^{\dagger})^{2}a_{i}^{2}+\mu_{i}a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{i},\cdot]
), and two-photon dissipation (
\mathcal{L}=a^{2}\cdot(a^{\dagger})^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{(a^{\dagger})^{2}a^{2},\cdot\}
), all expressed in terms of the bosonic annihilation
a
and creation
a^{\dagger}
operators defined on the Fock space
\cH
, a separable Hilbert space, it becomes evident that the class of unbounded generators
\mathcal{L}
is central to physically relevant applications. As Lindblad emphasized,

[boundedness is] a condition which is not fulfilled in many applications (We may hope that this restriction can be ultimately removed using more powerful mathematics)

[
32
, p. 120]
.

This remark originates from Lindblad’s seminal paper
[
32
]
on the classification of bounded generators — equivalently, of QMSs that are uniformly continuous in time — published concurrently with the work of Gorini, Kossakowski, and Sudarshan
[
19
]
. Together, these results yield the celebrated Gorini–Kossakowski–Sudarshan–Lindblad (GKSL) theorem, which establishes that any bounded generator acting on the space
\mathcal{T}_{1}(\cH)
of trace-class operators on a seperable Hilbert space
\cH
takes the form

\mathcal{L}(\rho)=-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{j=1}^{K}\!\left(L_{j}\rho L_{j}^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L_{j}^{\dagger}L_{j},\rho\}\right)\,.

(2)

For unbounded Hamiltonians and Lindblad operators, such as those described by polynomial functions of bosonic creation and annihilation operators on infinite-dimensional continuous-variable (CV) systems considered in this work, a direct extension of the GKSL form is highly nontrivial. This difficulty is already apparent in the pure birth process, where

\mathcal{L}=(a^{\dagger})^{2}\cdot a^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{a^{2}(a^{\dagger})^{2},\cdot\}\,,

fails to generate a QMS
[
14
, Example 3.3]
due to failure in preservation of probability mass (the semigroup is not trace preserving). Consequently, one must impose additional, sufficient conditions to ensure the existence of a well-defined QMS
[
10
,
12
,
11
,
16
,
43
,
13
,
7
,
8
,
18
]
.

Particularly, the works
[
18
,
1
]
laid the groundwork for CV systems that have become indispensable in quantum information processing.
[
6
,
24
,
49
,
46
,
40
,
48
,
41
,

]
and quantum sensing, particularly in the development of bosonic quantum error-correcting codes such as cat codes
[

,

,

,

,

.

In the recent work
[

]
(as well as the doctoral theses
[

,

]
) two of the present authors have introduced a sufficient condition on the operator in (
2
) to guarantee a unique solution of the initial-value problem (
1
). This condition, we term the
Sobolev stability condition

\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\mathcal{L}(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\bigr]\leq\omega_{k}\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\rho(N+\1)^{k}\bigr]

(3)

for certain
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\omega_{k}
, and suitable states
\rho
, ensures both the well-posedness of the generated semigroup and that it is
Sobolev preserving
. Specifically, the semigroup restricted to the quantum Sobolev spaces (defined in
Equation

8
) retains its semigroup properties while satisfying

\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k}\bigr]\leq e^{\omega_{k}t}\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\rho_{0}(N+\1)^{k}\bigr]\,,

for all
t\geq 0
. This property provides crucial a priori estimates for perturbation theory on QMSs, as employed in our prior works
[

,
34
,
35
]
.

However, in many physically relevant examples, a stronger inequality holds:

\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\mathcal{L}(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\bigr]\leq-c_{k}\operatorname{tr}\bigl[\rho(N+\1)^{k+\delta}\bigr]+\mu_{k}\,,

(4)

for constants
c_{k}>0
and
\mu_{k}\geq 0

[

[
17

. The present work integrates these foundational single-mode results into a broader framework, extending the analysis to multi-mode systems and rigorously establishing the consequences for perturbative and dynamical behavior.

In more detail, we identify and analyze a broad class of unbounded GKSL generators satisfying (

instantaneous Sobolev-regularization
. That is, for any strictly positive time

t\downarrow 0

This instantaneous regularization is of a similar nature as the regularization property of the heat flow (see for example
[

Technically, our approach builds on the generation and a priori estimate framework of

2
Preliminaries

We begin by recalling the basic setting and assumptions used throughout this work. Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the bosonic Fock space obtained as the closure of the linear span of the orthonormal basis
\{\left|n\right\rangle\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with respect to the standard inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. The annihilation and creation operators
a
and
a^{\dagger}
act on the Fock basis as
a\left|n\right\rangle=\sqrt{n}\left|n-1\right\rangle
,
a^{\dagger}\left|n\right\rangle=\sqrt{n+1}\left|n+1\right\rangle
, and satisfy the canonical commutation relation
[a,a^{\dagger}]=\1
. The number operator is given by
N=a^{\dagger}a
. For systems with
m
bosonic modes, we analogously define
a_{i}
,
a^{\dagger}_{i}
and the local number operator
N_{i}=a_{i}^{\dagger}a_{i}
for any mode
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{T}_{1}
the Banach space of trace-class operators on
\mathcal{H}
and by
\mathcal{T}_{f}=\mathrm{span}\{\left|n\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle m\right|:n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
the subspace of finite-rank operators in the Fock basis (see
[
25
,
4
,
5
]
for details).

A
quantum Markov semigroup
(QMS)
(T_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a family of completely positive, trace-preserving maps, which is additionally
C_{0}
-semigroup on
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, i.e.,

T_{t}\circ T_{s}=T_{t+s},\quad T_{0}=\mathrm{id},\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\downarrow 0}T_{t}\rho=\rho

(5)

for all
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
t,s\geq 0
. Such a semigroup is uniquely determined by its (possibly unbounded) generator
\mathcal{L}
, defined by

\mathcal{L}(X)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{t}\big(T_{t}(X)-X\big),

for
X
in its domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\{X\in\mathcal{T}_{1}:t\mapsto T_{t}(X)\text{ is differentiable on }\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\}
. The generator is in both the bounded and unbounded case densely defined and
closed
. Being a
closed
operator means that the graph
\{(X,\mathcal{L}(X)):X\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\}
is closed in
\mathcal{T}_{1}\times\mathcal{T}_{1}
(see
[
29
, Ch. III]
and
[
44
, Sec. 2–3]
).

On finite Hilbert spaces, the GKSL theorem
[
19
,
32
]
guarantees that
\mathcal{L}
admits the structure presented in
Equation

2
. This representation motivates the definition of the unbounded operators considered in the applications of this work, given by

\mathcal{L}(\rho)\coloneqq-i[H,\rho]+\sum_{i=1}^{K}\left(L_{i}\rho L_{i}^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L_{i}^{\dagger}L_{i},\rho\}\right),

(6)

where
H
and the jump operators
L_{i}
are polynomials in the creation and annihilation operators
\{a_{j},a_{j}^{\dagger}\}_{j=1}^{m}
of finite total degree
d
with the domain
\cD(\cL)\coloneqq\cT_{f}
. In the multi-mode setting, we restrict to local polynomial interactions of the form
(a_{i}^{\dagger})^{k_{i}}a_{i}^{\ell_{i}}(a_{j}^{\dagger})^{k_{j}}a_{j}^{\ell_{j}}
, corresponding to nearest-neighbour couplings on an interaction graph
(V,E)
with
|V|=m
and
(i,j)\in E
. Note, however, that for such unbounded coefficients,
\mathcal{L}
need not generate a well-defined QMS on
\mathcal{T}_{1}
.

A general framework for verifying when operators of the form (
6
) generate a QMS was developed in
[
18
]
. Specifically, if for all
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{f}
and integers
k\geq 0
there exists
\omega_{k}>0
such that

\operatorname{tr}\left[\mathcal{L}(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq\omega_{k}\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{k}\right],

(7)

then
(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{T}_{f})
admits a unique closure generating a
k
-Sobolev-preserving
QMS. More generally, we define
W^{k,1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{1}
to be the weighted trace-class space

W^{k,1}\coloneqq\left\{X\in\mathcal{T}_{1}:\|(N+\1)^{k/2}X(N+\1)^{k/2}\|_{1}<\infty\right\},

equipped with the norm

\|X\|_{W^{k,1}}\coloneqq\|(N+\1)^{k/2}X(N+\1)^{k/2}\|_{1}.

(8)

The notation
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,1}\to W^{k^{\prime},1}}
denotes the corresponding operator norm.

Definition 2.1

We call a QMS
k
-Sobolev preserving if
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
defines a semigroup on
W^{k,1}
and

\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}\|_{W^{k,1}\to W^{k,1}}\leq e^{\omega_{k}t},\qquad t\geq 0.

Furthermore, many physically relevant unbounded models satisfy a stronger moment inequality. Namely, there exist constants
\mu_{k}>0
,
c_{k}\geq 0
, and
\delta>0
such that

\operatorname{tr}\left[\mathcal{L}(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{k+\delta}\right]+c_{k},\qquad\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{f}.

(9)

As shown in
[
18
]
, inequality (
9
) implies the uniform bound

\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}\|_{W^{k,1}\to W^{k,1}}\leq\max\{1,c_{k}/\mu_{k}\},

(10)

which holds independently of time. In the present work, we extend this result by showing that for generators satisfying (
9
), the associated semigroup exhibits
instantaneous improvement of Sobolev regularity
, that is, the production of higher moments at arbitrarily small positive times. Moreover, we extend the single-mode results to multi-modes and present several applications.

Unless otherwise stated, all operator equalities and estimates are understood on the common dense core
\mathcal{T}_{f}
, and extend by closure to the generated semigroup.

3
Sobolev-regularizing QMS

In this section, we present our main technical results establishing instantaneous Sobolev-regularization. We note that the fundamental derivation for the single-mode case (
Section

3.1
) was developed in parallel with the results that appeared in the dissertation of one of the authors,
[
17
]
. Here, we present these arguments in a self-contained manner before generalizing them to the multi-mode setting in
Section

3.2
and applying them to stability problems.

We begin with an extension of Grönwall’s inequality
[
21
]
, which serves as a key analytical tool for handling the assumption

\operatorname{tr}\left[\mathcal{L}(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{k+\delta}\right]+c_{k}\,,

through an application of Jensen’s inequality. This approach leads to our principal finding: quantum Markov semigroups satisfying the above inequality are
Sobolev-regularizing
, that is,

\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}(\rho)\|_{W^{k,1}}\leq c(t)\|\rho\|_{1}

(11)

for a time-dependent constant
c(t)>0
and any state
\rho
. Moreover, in
Section

3.2
, we extend this result to multimode systems in a locality-preserving manner. For readability, the detailed proofs of the technical results are moved to
Appendix

A
. We begin with the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.1

Let
y:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuously differentiable function satisfying the differential inequality

\frac{dy}{dt}(t)\leq-a\,y(t)^{p}+b

for constants
a,b>0
and exponent
p>1
. Then,

y(t)\leq\bigg(\max\{y(0)-\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{1/p},0\}^{1-p}+a(p-1)t\bigg)^{\!-\frac{1}{p-1}}+\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{p}}=:z(t)\,.

Proof.

The proof can be found in
A.1
.
∎

3.1
Single-mode regularizing QMS

In this section, we apply the tools developed above to establish instantaneous Sobolev-regularization. Define

y(t)=\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)\|_{W^{k,1}}\,.

Our goal is to apply the assumption (
9
). Since this assumption does not exactly fit the form required by
3.1
, we first state and prove the following variant of Jensen’s inequality adapted to number-operator moments.

Lemma 3.2

(Jensen’s inequality for moments)

Let
\rho\in\cT_{f}
be a state. Then, for all
p\geq q>0
,

\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{p}\right]\geq\big(\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{q}\right]\big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,.

(12)

Proof.

Since
\rho
has finite rank
K
in the Fock basis, Jensen’s inequality shows

\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{p}\right]\coloneqq\sum_{n=0}^{K-1}p_{n}(n+1)^{q\frac{p}{q}}\geq\Bigl(\sum_{n}p_{n}(n+1)^{q}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{q}}=\big(\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{q}\right]\big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,.

Here, we used that
\{p_{n}=\left\langle n,\rho n\right\rangle\}_{n=0}^{K-1}
is a probability distribution and
x\mapsto x^{\frac{p}{q}}
is convex for
p\geq q>0
.
∎

Combining
3.1
with the above Jensen-type inequality yields the following result.

Theorem 3.3

Let
(\cL,\cT_{f})
generate a Sobolev-preserving quantum Markov semigroup satisfying

\operatorname{tr}\left[\cL(\rho)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\,\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(N+\1)^{k+\delta}\right]+c_{k}

(13)

for constants
k,\mu_{k},\delta,c_{k}>0
. Then, for all
t>0
,

\|e^{t\cL}\|_{1\to W^{k,1}}\leq\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}t}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}.

(14)

We call such a QMS
k
-Sobolev-regularizing.

Proof.

Since
\cT_{f}
is dense in
\cT_{1}
and
e^{t\cL}
is completely positive and trace-preserving, it suffices to prove (
14
) for
\rho\in\cT_{f}
a state. Let
\rho(t)=e^{t\cL}(\rho)
. Differentiating with respect to
t
and applying (
13
) yields

\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k}\right]=\operatorname{tr}\left[\cL(\rho(t))(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\,\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k+\delta}\right]+c_{k}\,.

By
3.2
with
p=k+\delta
and
q=k
, we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\,\big(\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k}\right]\big)^{\frac{k+\delta}{k}}+c_{k}\,.

Setting
y(t)\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}\left[\rho(t)(N+\1)^{k}\right]
, we have

y^{\prime}(t)\leq-\mu_{k}\,y(t)^{1+\frac{\delta}{k}}+c_{k}\,.

This is of the form required by
3.1
with parameters
a=\mu_{k}
,
b=c_{k}
, and
p=1+\frac{\delta}{k}>1
. Applying that lemma gives

y(t)\leq\big(\max\{y(0)-(\tfrac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}})^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}},0\}^{-\frac{\delta}{k}}+\tfrac{\delta}{k}\mu_{k}t\big)^{-\frac{k}{\delta}}+\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}\,.

Using the estimate

\big(\max\{y(0)-(\tfrac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}})^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}},0\}^{-\frac{\delta}{k}}+\tfrac{\delta}{k}\mu_{k}t\big)^{-\frac{k}{\delta}}\leq\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}t}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}},

we obtain the claimed bound (
14
).
∎

Remark 1
.

The argument above relies solely on Jensen’s inequality and the structure of the Sobolev-type norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,1}}
. Therefore, analogous moment-production and regularization results hold for any functional that satisfies an inequality of the form
3.1
.

Remark 2
.

3.3
establishes that if (
13
) holds for some
\delta>0
, we can study the dynamics of the whole set of quantum states, by extending the previous known case on
W^{k,1}
to
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. By comparing the bounds (
10
) and (
14
), we notice that there exists
t_{0}>0
such that for every
t\geq t_{0}

\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}t}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}\leq\max\left\{1,\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right\}\,.

(15)

In other words, this new estimate presents a better behavior for large enough
t
.

3.2
Mulit-mode: concentrated moment propagation bounds

Next, we extend the above result to multi-mode systems defined on a
D
-dimensional lattice
(V,E)
with
|V|=m
. As a first step, we introduce a locally concentrated Sobolev space. To that end, we define the following reference operator centered at a mode
v\in V
with a decay constant
\kappa>0
:

\cW_{v}^{k}(x)=\sum_{i\in V}\frac{e^{-\kappa\,\mathrm{dist}(v,i)}}{Z_{v}}(N_{i}+\1)^{k/2}\,x\,(N_{i}+\1)^{k/2}

(16)

for all
x\in\cT_{f}
and normalization constant
Z_{v}=\sum_{j\in V}e^{-\kappa\,\mathrm{dist}(v,j)}
, which admits lower and upper bounds independent of
m
(see
B.1
). The spectral decomposition of
N_{i}+\1
on a single mode directly translates to multi-modes and the eigenvectors are given by the Fock basis. In this basis and for
a_{i}=Z_{v}^{-1}e^{-\kappa\dist(v,i)}>0
, it is easy to see that

\|(\cW_{v}^{k})^{-1}(x)\|_{1}\leq(a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n})^{-1}\|x\|_{1}=\|x\|_{1}\,,

for
x\in\cT_{f}
, due to the fact that
N_{i}+\1\geq\1
. By the uniform boundedness theorem and the fact that
\cT_{f}
is dense in
\cT_{1}
, we conclude that
(\cW_{v}^{k})^{-1}
is a bounded linear operator. If we then define the local Sobolev space
W^{k,1}_{v}\coloneqq\cD(\cW_{v}^{k})
, equipped with the norm

\|x\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}=\|\cW_{v}^{k}(x)\|_{1}

for all
x\in\cD(\cW_{v}^{k})
, we obtain by
[
18
, Lem. 2.2]
that
(D(\cW_{v}^{k}),\|\cdot\|_{\cW_{v}^{k,1}})
is a Banach space.

One key motivation for the structure of the norm was that control on local moments directly imply control of global moments without any dependence on system size. For example, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
(see
B.2
)

\|\left|\alpha\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\alpha\right|\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}\leq\max_{i\in\{1,...,m\}}\operatorname{tr}\left[\left|\alpha_{i}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\alpha_{i}\right|(N_{i}+\1)^{k}\right]\leq\left(\frac{2k}{\ln(k/\max_{i\in\{1,...,m\}}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+1)}\right)^{k}\,.

With this notion of a concentrated Sobolev space, we can define
k
-Sobolev preserving semigroups as follows:

Definition 3.4

We call a QMS
(k,v)
-Sobolev preserving if
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
defines a semigroup on
W^{k,1}_{v}
and there are constants
\omega,\,C>0
such that

\|e^{t\mathcal{L}}\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}\to W^{k,1}_{v}}\leq Ce^{\omega t},\qquad t\geq 0\,.

Note that in the above definition, we do not require any independence of the constants of the system size. This definition is only above well-definedness, the constants are improved later on.

Then, analogous to the single-mode setting discussed in
Section

3.1
, we assume the existence of
k>0
and constants
c_{k},\mu_{k}\geq 0
such that
(\cL,\cD(\cL))
satisfies the inequality

\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{k}(\cL(\rho))\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\,\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{k+\delta}(\rho)\right]+c_{k}

(17)

for all states
\rho\in\cT_{f}
, we set

y(t)=\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}\,,

and aim to apply
3.1
. However, due to the summation structure in the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}
, the Jensen-type inequality for number-operator moments must be modified accordingly.

Lemma 3.5

(Jensen’s inequality for multi-mode moments)

Let
\rho\in\cT_{f}
be a state. Then, for all
p\geq q>0
,

\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{p}(\rho)\right]\geq\big(\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{q}(\rho)\right]\big)^{\frac{p}{q}}\,.

(18)

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of the proof of
3.2
, with slightly more general coefficients
p_{n}
. Details can be found in
B.3
in
Appendix

B
.
∎

As in
Section

3.1
, we are now able to combine
3.1
with the above Jensen-type inequality:

Theorem 3.6

Let
(\cL,\cT_{f})
generate a
(k,v)
-Sobolev-preserving QMS satisfying

\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{k}(\cL(\rho))\right]\leq-\mu_{k}\,\operatorname{tr}\left[\cW_{v}^{k+\delta}(\rho)\right]+c_{k}

for constants
\mu_{k},\delta,c_{k},k>0
. Then, for all
t>0
,

\|e^{t\cL}\|_{1\to W^{k,1}_{v}}\leq\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}t}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}\,.

We call such a QMS,
(k,v)
-Sobolev-regularizing.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of
3.3
and is presented in
B.4
.
∎

This result completes our abstract analysis of instantaneous Sobolev-regularization in both single and multi-mode settings. Next, we present several examples that satisfy the assumptions of the theorems and demonstrate their direct implications for perturbation theory and simulation.

3.3
Intermediate and large time perturbation bounds

Under a slightly stronger assumption on the degree of dissipation, we can even extend known perturbation-theory bounds for both intermediate and large times to the
1\rightarrow 1
norm. To highlight the underlying difficulty, we begin by improving the standard integral equation using our Sobolev-regularization property. It is well known that

e^{t\cL}(\rho)-e^{t(\cL+\cE)}(\rho)=t\int_{0}^{1}e^{(1-s)t(\cL+\cE)}\cE\,e^{st\cL}(\rho)\,ds

holds for generators
(\cL,\cD(\cL))
and
(\cL+\cE,\cD(\cL+\cE))
, and for
\rho
an element of the underlying Banach space — such as
\cT_{1}
— provided the integral is well-defined. Even under the relative boundedness assumptions

\|\cE(\rho)\|\leq c_{1}\|\cL(\rho)\|+c_{2}\|\rho\|

for
\rho\in\cD(\cL)\subset\cD(\cE)
, one does not expect a substantially better bound than

\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-e^{t(\cL+\cE)}(\rho)\|\leq t\bigl(c_{1}\|\cL(\rho)\|+c_{2}\|\rho\|\bigr)\,,

which depends on the output of an unbounded operator (see, e.g.,
[
29
, Chap. 9]
). In our setting of Sobolev-regularizing quantum Markov semigroups (QMS), we improve the above bound to the uniform topology. We state the result directly in the multi-mode setup, but emphasize that it reduces immediately to the single-mode case used in
Section

4.3
.

To achieve the improved bounds, we assume that the perturbation is relatively bounded with respect to a power
k
of
\cW^{k}_{v}
with
k<\delta
.

Proposition 3.7

(Intermediate-time perturbation bound)

Let
(\cL,\cD(\cL))
be the generator of a
(k,v)
–Sobolev-regularizing QMS with
\delta>k
, and let
(\cE,\cD(\cE))
be an unbounded operator on
\cT_{1}(\cH^{\otimes m})
such that
(\cL+\cE,\cD(\cL+\cE))
generates a contractive semigroup and

\|\cE(\rho)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|\cW^{k}_{v}(\rho)\|+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}=c_{1}\|\rho\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}\,.

Then,

\|e^{t\cL}-e^{t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}\|_{1\rightarrow 1}\leq\varepsilon\Bigl(c_{1}t^{1-\frac{k}{\delta}}\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+c_{1}t\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}+c_{2}t\Bigr)\,.

Proof.

First, by the fundamental theorem of calculus, contractivity, and relative boundedness,

\displaystyle\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-e^{t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}(\rho)\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\Bigl\|\varepsilon t\int_{0}^{1}e^{(1-s)t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}\cE\,e^{st\cL}(\rho)\,ds\Bigr\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon t\int_{0}^{1}\|\cE\,e^{st\cL}(\rho)\|_{1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon t\int_{0}^{1}c_{1}\|e^{st\cL}(\rho)\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}\,ds+\varepsilon t\,c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}

for
\rho\in W^{k,1}_{v}
. Note that

s\mapsto e^{(1-s)t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}\cE\,e^{st\cL}(\rho)

is continuous, so the Bochner integral
[
23
, Sec. 3.5–8]
is well-defined. This follows from Lemma 2.1 in
[
37
]
, the relative boundedness assumption, and the Sobolev-regularization property — specifically, the Sobolev-preserving property. Applying the
(k,v)
–Sobolev-regularization property yields

\displaystyle\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-e^{t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}(\rho)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon t\Bigl(\int_{0}^{1}c_{1}\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}st}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}ds+c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}+c_{2}\Bigr)\|\rho\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\Bigl(c_{1}t^{1-\frac{k}{\delta}}\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+c_{1}t\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}+c_{2}t\Bigr)\|\rho\|_{1}\,.

Since
W^{k,1}_{v}
is dense in
\cT_{1}
and the norms are continuous, closure completes the proof.
∎

One of our main applications, presented in
Section

4
–

, is the bosonic quantum error-correction code. A key ingredient is an underlying dynamics that converges exponentially fast to an invariant subspace — the codespace. In addition to the perturbation analysis above, a bound on the propagation of small errors for large times is crucial when the dynamics is not implemented perfectly. To this end, we extend the result of Theorem 6 in
[

to the setting of unbounded generators and then verify the corresponding assumptions used in

in the case of the bosonic cat code.

A key ingredient is a strengthened exponential convergence assumption enabled by the Sobolev-regularization property. Assuming a
(k,v)
-Sobolev-regularizing semigroup converges exponentially fast, that is, there exist a contractive projection
\cP
and constants

such that

\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-\cP(\rho)\|_{1}\leq Ce^{-\gamma t}\|\rho\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}

for states

, we can lift this directly to a

trace norm bound:

\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-\cP(\rho)\|_{1}\leq\widetilde{C}\,t^{1-\frac{\widetilde{k}}{\delta}}e^{-\gamma t}\|\rho\|_{1}\,,

for some constant
\widetilde{C}
, where
\delta
is a Sobolev-regularization parameter from

. Importantly,
\widetilde{k}
need not be smaller than

4.2

We begin with the case of a unique fixed point/ steady state.

Proposition 3.8

(Perturbation bound for steady states)

Let
(\cL,\cD(\cL))
and
(\cL+\cE,\cD(\cL+\cE))
be generators of a
(k,v)
–Sobolev-regularizing QMS, where
(\cE,\cD(\cE))
is an unbounded operator on
\cT_{1}(\cH^{\otimes m})
satisfying

\|\cE(\rho)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|\cW^{k}_{v}(\rho)\|+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}=c_{1}\|\rho\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}\,.

If
e^{t\cL}
converges to a steady state
\overline{\rho}\in\cT_{1}
, i.e.,

\|e^{t\cL}(\rho)-\overline{\rho}\|_{1\rightarrow 1}\leq\widetilde{C}\,t^{1-\frac{\widetilde{k}}{\delta}}e^{-\gamma t}\|\rho\|_{1}

for any state
\rho\in\cT_{1}
,
\widetilde{k}\in\N
, and
\delta>k
, then for any states
\rho,\sigma\in\cT_{1}
,

\displaystyle\|e^{t\cL}(\sigma)-e^{t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}(\rho)\|_{1}\leq\begin{cases}\|\sigma-\rho\|_{1}+\varepsilon t^{1-\frac{k}{\delta}}\hat{C}_{1}\|\rho\|_{1},&t\leq 1,\\[4.0pt]

where

and
\hat{C}_{2}=\widetilde{C}\frac{e^{-\gamma}-e^{-t\gamma}}{\gamma}+1
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem 6 in
[
45
]
, combined with the newly developed techniques for
(k,v)
–Sobolev-regularizing QMS. Details are presented in
C.1
.
∎

Remark 3

Continuous quantum error correction relies on the idea that a quantum dynamical process continuously drives the state always back into an invariant subspace — the codespace. Since the codespace has usually dimension greater than one, we next consider QMS converging to a invariant subspace. Note that perturbation insight the codespace may lead to non-convergent rotations, which is why our error metric only considers the error outside the codespace.

Proposition 3.9

(Perturbation bound for invariant subsets)

Let
(\cL,\cD(\cL))
and
(\cL+\cE,\cD(\cL+\cE))
be generators of a

–Sobolev-regularizing QMS, where

is an unbounded operator on
\cT_{1}(\cH^{\otimes m})

\|\cE(\rho)\|_{1}\leq c_{1}\|\cW^{k}_{v}(\rho)\|+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}=c_{1}\|\rho\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}+c_{2}\|\rho\|_{1}\,.

converges to an invariant subset with contractive projection

(with

\displaystyle\|\cP^{\perp}e^{t\cL}(\sigma)-\cP^{\perp}e^{t(\cL+\varepsilon\cE)}(\rho)\|_{1}\leq\begin{cases}\|\cP^{\perp}(\sigma-\rho)\|_{1}+\varepsilon t^{1-\frac{k}{\delta}}\hat{C}_{1}\|\rho\|_{1},&t\leq 1,\\[4.0pt]

where

\hat{C}_{1}=c_{1}\left(\frac{k}{\delta\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{\delta}}+c_{1}\left(\frac{c_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+\delta}}+c_{2}
and
\hat{C}_{2}=2\widetilde{C}\frac{e^{-\gamma}-e^{-t\gamma}}{\gamma}+1
.

Proof.

As in Theorem 5.2 of

, combined with the
(k,v)

A natural and simple example in quantum optics and superconductors is the

(Pure photon dissipation)

4.2
Exponential convergence in
1\rightarrow 1
norm

As mentioned above, one key property of the shifted
\ell
-photon dissipation is its rapid convergence to the code space. However, this was first proven by
[
3

in the following topology:

\mathrm{tr}\big[L_{\ell}e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}(\rho)L_{\ell}^{\dagger}\big]\leq e^{-t\ell!}\mathrm{tr}[L_{\ell}\rho L_{\ell}^{\dagger}]\,,

where

\displaystyle\|(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}-\mathcal{P}_{\ell})(\rho)\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 6e^{-\frac{t}{2}\ell!}\biggl(\Bigl(1+\frac{|\alpha|^{\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell!}}\Bigr)^{2}\|\rho\|_{W^{\ell,1}}+\|\rho\|_{1}\biggr)

for all
\rho\in W^{\ell,1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\ell}
is the projection onto the codespace
\mathcal{C}_{\ell}

Lemma 4.3

Let
\ell\geq 2
and
\cP_{\ell}
be the ON projection onto

(
19

\|(e^{t\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}-\mathcal{P}_{\ell})(\rho)\|_{1\rightarrow 1}\leq 6e^{-\frac{t}{4}\ell!}\biggl(\Bigl(1+\frac{|\alpha|^{\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell!}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\frac{16}{((\ell-1)\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}t)^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}}\Bigr)+1\biggr)\,,

where
\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}
is given by
4.1
.

Proof.

First, we assume
\rho\in W^{\ell,1}
. Then Proposition A.2 in
[
35
]
applied to

shows

\|(e^{\frac{t}{2}\mathcal{L}_{\ell}}-\mathcal{P}_{\ell})(\rho_{\frac{t}{2}})\|_{1}\leq 6e^{-\frac{t}{4}\ell!}\biggl(\Bigl(1+\frac{|\alpha|^{\ell}}{\sqrt{\ell!}}\Bigr)^{2}\|\rho_{\frac{t}{2}}\|_{W^{\ell,1}}+\|\rho_{\frac{t}{2}}\|_{1}\biggr)\,.

Here, we also used that

implies

. Then, a final application of
4.1

\|e^{\frac{t}{2}\cL_{\ell}}\|_{1\to W^{\ell,1}}\leq\biggl(\frac{4}{(\ell-1)\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}t}\biggr)^{\frac{\ell}{\ell-1}}+\biggl(\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}}\biggr)^{\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1}}\leq\biggl(\frac{4}{(\ell-1)\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}t}\biggr)^{2}+\frac{1}{\mu_{\ell}^{(\ell)}}

which finishes the proof.

5.1
Multi-mode photon dissipation

In this section, we show

-Sobolev regularization for multi-mode
\ell
-photon dissipation in the presence of nearest-neighbor Hamiltonians consisting of polynomials in creation and annihilation operators. The interaction structure of the model is given by a
D

(V,E)
with
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{D}
and
|V|=m
. In the sake of notation, we allow cycles denoting the on-mode terms
V{\subset}E

\mathcal{L}_{\ell}(\rho)=\sum_{j\in V}\mathcal{L}[a_{j}^{\ell}-\alpha_{j}^{\ell}]\,,

(22)

for a vector
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}^{m}

\mathcal{L}[L]=L\cdot L^{\dagger}-\tfrac{1}{2}\{L^{\dagger}L,\cdot\}
for any operator
L
. Alongside this Lindbladian, we also consider a
2
-local Hamiltonian of the form

H=\sum_{i\sim j}H_{ij}=\sum_{i\sim j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{4}\\
\|u\|_{1}\leq d_{H}\end{subarray}}\lambda_{ij}^{(u)}\,a_{i}^{u_{1}}a_{j}^{u_{3}}(a_{i}^{\ast})^{u_{2}}(a_{j}^{\ast})^{u_{4}}+\overline{\lambda}_{ij}^{(u)}\,a_{j}^{u_{4}}a_{i}^{u_{2}}(a_{j}^{\ast})^{u_{3}}(a_{i}^{\ast})^{u_{1}},

(23)

where
i\sim j
means
(i,j)\in E
, corresponding to nearest neighbors on the
D
-dimensional lattice. Together, this defines the operator

\cL_{\ell}^{(H)}\coloneqq-i[H,\cdot]+\cL_{\ell}=\sum_{i\sim j}-i[H_{ij},\cdot]+\frac{1}{\gamma_{i}}\cL[a_{i}^{\ell}-\alpha_{i}^{\ell}]+\frac{1}{\gamma_{j}}\cL[a_{j}^{\ell}-\alpha_{j}^{\ell}]\eqqcolon\sum_{i\sim j}\cL_{i,j}

(24)

with
\gamma_{i}=|\{i\sim j\,|j\in V\}|
— the connectivity of the graph-node
i\in V
. A direct consequence of the

-Sobolev preservation of single-mode photon loss (see

) is that the multi-mode extension (

) combined with single-mode Hamiltonians is

-Sobolev preserving for every

.

Corollary 5.1

Let
k\geq 1
,
\ell\geq 2
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\rho\in\mathcal{T}_{f}
. Then for every Hamiltonian (

6
Conclusion

In this work, we identified a broad class of unbounded GKSL generators on bosonic Fock space whose associated quantum Markov semigroups exhibit instantaneous Sobolev regularization. We showed that dissipative evolutions generated by polynomial creation and annihilation operators not only preserve finite moments but also immediately improve them, mapping arbitrary trace-class states into highly regular ones for every
t>0
. This provides a structural explanation for stability phenomena in bosonic continuous-variable systems and yields explicit convergence bounds for arbitrary input states. In particular, our bounds sharpen existing perturbative results at both short and long times, offering new analytic tools for assessing robustness in bosonic quantum information processing.

The multi-mode framework developed here points toward several promising directions. A central open question is whether instantaneous Sobolev regularization can be exploited to improve bosonic information-propagation bounds, such as Lieb–Robinson–type estimates. Another natural direction for future work is the explicit analysis of particular bosonic quantum error-correcting codes and the implications of our theory for their performance.

Appendix A
ODE comparison lemma

In the following section, we prove
3.1
. For clarity of presentation, we begin by restating the lemma:

Lemma A.1

Let
y:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be a continuously differentiable function satisfying the differential inequality

\frac{dy}{dt}(t)\leq-a\,y(t)^{p}+b

for constants
a,b>0
and exponent
p>1
. Then,

y(t)\leq\Big(\max\{y(0)-\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{1/p},0\}^{1-p}+(p-1)at\Big)^{\!-\frac{1}{p-1}}+\Big(\frac{b}{a}\Big)^{\!\frac{1}{p}}=:z(t)\,.

Proof.

In a first step, we define

t_{c}\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0:y(t)\leq(b/a)^{1/p}\}\,.

For
t>t_{c}
, it follows immediately that
y(t)\leq(b/a)^{1/p}
, and hence
y(t)\leq z(t)
. We verify this by contradiction. Suppose there exists
t_{v}>t_{c}
such that
y(t_{v})>(b/a)^{1/p}
. By continuity of
y
, the intermediate value theorem implies that there exist
t_{0},t_{1}
with
t_{0}<t_{1}
such that

y(t_{0})=(b/a)^{1/p},\qquad y(t_{1})>(b/a)^{1/p},\qquad\text{and}\quad y(t)\geq(b/a)^{1/p}\quad\text{ for all }t\in[t_{0},t_{1}]\,.

By the mean value theorem, there exists
t_{m}\in(t_{0},t_{1})
such that

y^{\prime}(t_{m})=\frac{y(t_{1})-y(t_{0})}{t_{1}-t_{0}}>0\,.

However, from the differential inequality and the fact that
y(t_{m})\geq(b/a)^{1/p}
, we have

y^{\prime}(t_{m})\leq-a\,y(t_{m})^{p}+b\leq-a(b/a)+b=0,

a contradiction. Therefore,
y(t)\leq(b/a)^{1/p}
for all
t\geq t_{c}
.

For
0\leq t<t_{c}
, define
g(t)\coloneqq y(t)-(b/a)^{1/p}>0
. Then
g^{\prime}(t)=y^{\prime}(t)
, and the differential inequality yields

g^{\prime}(t)\leq-a\,g(t)^{p}.

Separating variables and integrating from
0
to
t
gives

\frac{g(t)^{1-p}-g(0)^{1-p}}{1-p}=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{g^{\prime}(s)}{g(s)^{p}}\,ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}-a\,ds=-at.

Rearranging terms, we obtain

g(t)^{1-p}\geq g(0)^{1-p}+(p-1)at.

Taking both sides to the power of
\tfrac{1}{1-p}
(which reverses the inequality since
\tfrac{1}{1-p}<0
), we find

g(t)\leq\frac{1}{\big(g(0)^{1-p}+(p-1)at\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}.

Substituting back
g(t)=y(t)-(b/a)^{1/p}
yields

y(t)\leq\frac{1}{\big((y(0)-(b/a)^{1/p})^{1-p}+(p-1)at\big)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}}+(b/a)^{1/p}.

Using the conventions
1/\infty=0
and
1/0=\infty
completes the proof.
∎

Appendix B
Multi-mode extension

In this appendix, we restate the multi-mode extensions previously introduced in
Section

3.2
and provide detailed proofs. In a first step, we shortly discuss upper and lower bounds on the normalization constant
Z_{v}
, which show that the normalization is admits lower and upper bounds which are system size independent.

Lemma B.1

Let
(V,E)
define a
D
-dimensional lattice. Then, for
v\in V
, we have for
K=\max_{i\in V}\dist(i,v)

1\leq\frac{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)(K+1)}}{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)}}\leq Z_{v}\leq\frac{e^{2D-1}}{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)}}

Proof.

In a first step, we reshape the sum with
K=\max_{i\in V}\dist(i,v)
as follows

Z_{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}e^{-\kappa\mathrm{dist}(v,i)}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}|\{i\in V\,|\,\dist(i,v)=\ell\}|e^{-\kappa\ell}\,.

Then, we use a simple upper bound on the cardinality of a sphere in
D
-dimensional lattice (see
[
36
, Appx. B.2]
). Combined with the geometric series, we achieve

Z_{v}\leq\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}2^{D}\frac{(D-1+\ell)^{D-1}}{(D-1)!}e^{-\kappa\ell}\leq e^{D}\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(D-1+\ell)^{j}}{j!}e^{-\kappa\ell}\leq e^{2D-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}e^{-(\kappa-1)\ell}=\frac{e^{2D-1}}{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)}}

For the lower bound, we just use
1
as lower bound for the cardinalities of the spheres and again the geometric sum so that

Z_{v}\geq\sum_{\ell=0}^{K}e^{-\kappa\ell}\geq\frac{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)(K+1)}}{1-e^{-(\kappa-1)}}\geq 1

finishes the result.
∎

Next, we prove the following inequality, which motivates the definition of concentrated Sobolev norms — local moments bound the global but concentrated Sobolev norm.

Lemma B.2

Let
\alpha\in\C^{m}
define the coherent state
\left|\alpha\right\rangle
, then for

\|\left|\alpha\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\alpha\right|\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}\leq\max_{i\in\{1,...,m\}}\operatorname{tr}\left[\left|\alpha_{i}\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\alpha_{i}\right|(N_{i}+\1)^{k}\right]\leq\left(\frac{2k}{\ln(k/\max_{i\in\{1,...,m\}}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+1)}\right)^{k}\,.

Proof.

This proof follows the proof of Lemma 7 in
[
34
]
. By definition of the concentrated Sobolev norm, the norm reduces to

\|\left|\alpha\right\rangle\left\langle\alpha\right|\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{e^{-\kappa\dist(v,i)}}{Z_{v}}\left\langle\alpha_{i}\right|(N_{i}+\1)^{k}\left|\alpha_{i}\right\rangle\,,

and by that to a single mode. Using a simple bound on the
k^{\text{th}}
moment of the Poisson distribution
[
2
]
, one can show

\displaystyle\left\langle\alpha_{i}\right|(N_{i}+\1)^{k}\left|\alpha_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-|\alpha_{i}|^{2}}\sum_{n_{i}=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{|\alpha_{i}|^{2n_{i}}}{n_{i}!}\,(n_{i}+1)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}\,\sum_{n_{i}=0}^{\infty}n_{i}^{l}\,\frac{|\alpha_{i}|^{2n_{i}}}{n_{i}!}e^{-|\alpha_{i}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2k}{\ln(k/|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+1)}\right)^{k}\,.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|\left|\alpha\right\rangle\!\!\left\langle\alpha\right|\|_{W^{k,1}_{v}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{e^{-\kappa\dist(v,i)}}{Z_{v}}\left(\frac{2k}{\ln(k/|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+1)}\right)^{k}\leq\left(\frac{2k}{\ln(k/\max_{i\in\{1,...,m\}}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}+1)}\right)^{k}

by Hölder’s inequality and the normalization constant.
∎

We begin with a Jensen-type inequality for multi-mode bosonic moments:

Lemma B.3

(Jensen’s inequality for multi-mode moments)

Let

The proof follows the lines of the proof of

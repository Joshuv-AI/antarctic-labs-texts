Cholesterol synthases suppress metastasis and stemness through PDIA4/ACLY or SYVN1/SCD1 axis in advanced colorectal cancer
Authors: Wenjie Li, Moubin Lin, Yun Zhao, Yuqi Tang, Ying Chen, Luwei He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aberrant cholesterol biosynthesis is a notable hallmark of cancers, supporting unlimited cell proliferation. Previously, we delineated that cholesterol synthases FDFT1 and SQLE promoted colorectal cancer (CRC) cell proliferation, while how these enzymes influence CRC metastasis and stemness remains unclear. This study demonstrates that suppress of FDFT1 or SQLE enhances CRC metastasis and stemness through impairing ER stress and degrading PDIA4 or SYVN1 individually. Additionally, the function of FDFT1 and SQLE on CRC metastasis relies on tryptophan. Tryptophan augments PDIA4-ACLY and SYVN1-SCD1 co-complex association to drive CRC metastasis and stemness. Consequently, the specific inhibitor of ACLY or SCD1 blocks FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency induced CRC metastasis using Male BALB/c nude mice. Besides, sub-cytotoxic concentrations of inhibitors targeting FDFT1 or SQLE generate the invasive feature in CRC cells. Overall, these findings indicate that cholesterol synthases impact ER homeostasis and fatty acid synthesis to regulate CRC metastasis and stemness, offering potential for combined targeted therapies in CRC.

Introduction

Lipoids and fats are essential for cellular function and energy1,2. Exogenous cholesterol from the diet is absorbed by the intestine, secreted into circulation and taken up by the liver. Endogenous cholesterol is mainly synthesized in the liver. Dietary cholesterol enters enterocytes via Niemann-Pick C1 like 1 (NPC1L1)-mediated endocytosis, transfers to blood to form chylomicrons and is utilized by binding to the hepatic low-density lipoprotein receptor (LDLR) or released by ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transport proteins3. Cholesterol biosynthesis follows the classical mevalonate pathway, de novo from acetyl-coenzyme A (acetyl-CoA) to pyrophosphate, dolichols, ubiquinone or cholesterol, which subsequently gives rise to oxysterols, steroid hormones, vitamins and bile acids4. Liver X receptors (LXRs) contribute to the balance of cellular cholesterol levels5. Excess cholesterol in macrophages is removed by high-density lipoprotein (HDL)-mediated reverse transport to hepatocytes or the transintestinal cholesterol excretion pathway to enterocytes.

Hypercholesterolemia, characterized by high low-density lipoprotein (LDL) and low high-density lipoprotein (HDL) in serum, is positively associated with cancer initiation and progression6. Aberrant cholesterol metabolism sustains cancer tissues in harsh microenvironment to develop malignancies. Remodeling cholesterol homeostasis is vital for cancer treatment. Therapeutic targets include cholesterol biosynthesis, esterification, uptake, efflux and transport7,8. Statins, which are inhibitors of HMG-CoA reductase (HMGCR), are the most common drugs applied in cancer clinical trials. The combination of statins and 5-fluorouracil ameliorates the prognosis of patients with colorectal cancer (CRC). Inhibitors of sterol O-acyltransferase 1 (SOAT1) or the agonists of LXR have not been extensively investigated in CRC clinical studies9. Cholesterol, phospholipids and lipid rafts contribute to multidrug-resistant phenotypes and abnormal cellular signaling transition10. Rewiring cholesterol metabolism also modulates cancer cell plasticity and drug resistance11.

Squalene epoxidase (SQLE) in the cholesterol biosynthesis pathway is a therapeutic target for various cancers, including head and neck squamous cancer, breast cancer, non-small cell lung cancer, gastric cancer, liver cancer, pancreatic cancer, and prostate cancer. SQLE promotes cancer progression by inhibiting ferroptosis, regulating the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway and facilitating homologous recombination12–16. SQLE also initiates gut dysbiosis-mediated colorectal tumorigenesis17. Farnesyl-diphosphate farnesyltransferase 1 (FDFT1) is an upstream enzyme of SQLE in the mevalonate pathway. We previously found that FDFT1 or SQLE accelerated CRC proliferation through the calcitriol/CYP24A1-mediated MAPK pathway or glutathione metabolism pathway18,19. However, how these enzymes influence CRC metastasis and stemness in advanced CRC has not been clarified.

FDFT1 and SQLE are located in the endoplasmic reticulum (ER). ER proteostasis involves two quality-control mechanisms: the unfolded protein response (UPR) and endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation (ERAD)20. Crosstalk between UPR and ERAD depends on inositol-requiring enzyme 1 (IRE1) and suppressor of Lin-12-like protein 1 (SEL1L)-synoviolin 1 (SYVN1)21. X-box binding protein 1 (XBP1) knockout mice promote colorectal tumorigenesis when treated with azoxymethane and dextran sodium sulphate22.

In this study, we delineate that cholesterol synthases alleviate CRC metastasis and stemness by regulating EMT (epithelial-mesenchymal transition) and stemness markers. PDIA4 (protein disulfide isomerase family A member 4) and SYVN1-mediated ER stress are involved in CRC metastasis and stemness induced by loss of FDFT1 or SQLE. Meanwhile, FDFT1 and SQLE regulate CRC metastasis in tryptophan-dependent manner. Tryptophan reinforces the co-complex association between ER modulators and enzymes of fatty acid synthesis to activate CRC metastasis and stemness. Furthermore, targeted inhibitors of fatty acid synthesis enzymes suppress CRC metastasis induced by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency. Taken together, this paper reveals the crosstalk between cholesterol synthases and ER homeostasis in regulating CRC metastasis and stemness and suggests the potential combined therapies for blocking CRC progression.

Results

FDFT1/SQLE deficiency augments CRC cell migration and invasion via Claudin 1 and SNAI2

We first analyzed the association between FDFT1 or SQLE expression and prognosis in CRC using public datasets23. Low FDFT1 or SQLE expression was associated with poor prognosis in stage III-IV CRC patients (Fig. 1A–D). And the gene expressions of FDFT1 and SQLE were downregulated in tumor tissues derived from stage IV CRC patients (Fig. 1E, F). To explore the function of FDFT1 and SQLE on metastasis, we established FDFT1- or SQLE-deficient and overexpressing HCT116 cells (Fig. 1G, H). Lack of FDFT1 or SQLE enhanced the migration and invasion abilities of HCT116 cells, whereas overexpression of FDFT1 or SQLE reduced these abilities (Fig. 1I–K). Meanwhile, knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE also increased the migration and invasion abilities of RKO cells (Fig. S1A, B). We then we measured the expression of EMT markers and transcription factors (Fig. S1C, D). Decreased CLDN1 expression and the increased SNAI2 expression were remarkable induced by loss of FDFT1 or SQLE (Fig. 1L). While the common EMT markers, such as CDH1, CDH2 and VIM were not consistently changed upon knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE. Since the protein levels of Claudin 1 and SNAI2 were hardly detected by western blot in CRC cells, we confirmed the phenomenon by immunofluorescence staining. As shown in Figs. 1M–O and S1E–H, decreased Claudin 1 and increased SNAI2 protein levels were notably induced by loss of FDFT1 or SQLE, while overexpression of FDFT1 or SQLE reversed this effect. Knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE stimulates CRC cell stemness

Given the active EMT induced by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency, we wondered the relationship between CRC cell plasticity and stemness. EMT/MET state is linked to cell proliferation, metastasis and stemness through cell cycle checkpoints24. First, in the TCGA database, the expression of FDFT1 or SQLE was positively correlated with CLDN1 but negatively correlated with SNAI2 in CRC tissues (Fig. 2A–D). The inhibition of the proliferation rate of CRC cells by knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE was validated by CCND1 and Ki-67 levels (Fig. 2E–J). Meanwhile, loss of FDFT1 or SQLE increased the self-renewal of HCT116 stem cells, on the contrary, excessive FDFT1 or SQLE reduced (Fig. 2K, L). Indeed, intracellular signaling pathways associated with CRC stemness were lifted up by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency in HCT116 spheres. NANOG is a target gene of Gli-1 in the Hedgehog signaling pathway, which maintains the undifferentiated state of colorectal cancer stem cells (CSCs). Notch1, a receptor in the Notch signaling pathway, contributes to CSC maintenance25. The mRNA and protein levels of Notch1 and Nanog were elevated by knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE, while overexpression of FDFT1 or SQLE reduced the protein levels but not the mRNA level of Notch1 and Nanog in HCT116 spheres (Fig. 2M–P). In addition, lack of FDFT1 or SQLE also enhanced self-renewal of HT29 stem cells and stimulated the protein levels but not the mRNA level of Notch1 and Nanog (Fig. 2Q–T). FDFT1/SQLE regulate CRC metastasis and stemness through PDIA4 or SYVN1 individually

To investigate the molecular mechanism of FDFT1 and SQLE on metastasis, we compared the transcriptomes of FDFT1-deficient with SQLE-deficient CRC cells. Reanalysis revealed that the “protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum” pathway was remarkably enriched in both FDFT1- deficient and SQLE-deficient CRC cells (Fig. 3A, B). The overlap of significantly differential genes also showed the enrichment of this pathway, ranking first (Fig. 3C, D). Indeed, loss of FDFT1 or SQLE assembled less misfolded protein in CRC cells (Fig. 3E, F). To further investigate whether loss of FDFT1 or SQLE promotes CRC metastasis through reducing ER stress, we applied the agents dithiothreitol (DTT) and MG-132. DTT is a widely used ER stress inducer that decreases both intramolecular and intermolecular disulfide bond formation between protein cysteine residues. MG-132 is another proteasome inhibitor that targets ERAD (endoplasmic reticulum-associated degradation) to induce ER stress26. The IC50 of DTT and MG-132 were measured in HCT116 cells (Fig. S2A, B). At the concentration of non-cytotoxicity, both DTT and MG-132 inhibited the increase of HCT116 cell invasion induced by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency (Fig. 3G, H). Then we screened the differential genes and found that the lack of FDFT1 or SQLE, respectively reduced the gene expressions of PDIA4 and SYVN1 in CRC cells (Figs. 3I–K and S2C, D). And the gene expressions of PDIA4 and SYVN1 were downregulated notably in tumor tissues derived from stage IV CRC patients (Fig. 3L, M). Additionally, low PDIA4 and SYVN1 expression were associated with poor prognosis in CRC patients (Fig. S2E–G). In the TCGA database, the expression of FDFT1 positively correlated with PDIA4, which positively correlated with CLDN1 and negatively corelated with SNAI2 (Fig S2H–J). Next, we examined the roles of PDIA4 and SYVN1 on FDFT1- or SQLE-induced metastasis and stemness. FDFT1 and SQLE deficiency, respectively, reduced the protein levels of PDIA4 and SYVN1 in CRC cells, which were independently on ER chaperone BiP (Fig. 4A–C). Moreover, overexpression of PDIA4 suppressed cell invasion and rescued the levels of misfolded proteins, Claudin 1 and SNAI2 in FDFT1-deficient HCT116 cells rather than in shcontrol HCT116 cells (Figs. 4D, E, H, J, and S3A–C). Similarly, overexpression of SYVN1 inhibited cell invasion and rescued the levels of misfolded protein, Claudin 1 and SNAI2 in SQLE-deficient HCT116 cells rather than in shcontrol HCT116 cells (Figs. 4F, G, I, J, and S3D–F). Besides, siRNAs targeting PDIA4 and SYVN1 were synthesized and their efficiency was tested in RKO cells (Fig. S3G, H). The most effective siRNAs for PDIA4 and SYVN1 were treated with FDFT1 or SQLE-deficient RKO cells (Fig. S3I, J). Knockdown of PDIA4 promoted CRC cell invasion in shcontrol RKO cells rather than in FDFT1-deficient RKO cells (Fig S3K, L). And knockdown of SYVN1 promoted CRC cell invasion in shcontrol RKO cells rather than in SQLE-deficient RKO cells (Fig. S3M, N). Consequently, reduction of PDIA4 or SYVN1 downregulated Claudin 1 and upregulated SNAI2 in shcontrol RKO cells (Fig. S3O, P). Furthermore, overexpression of PDIA4 or SYVN1 inhibited the accumulation of Notch1 induced by loss of FDFT1 or SQLE in HCT116 spheres (Fig. 4H, I). The function of FDFT1 and SQLE on CRC metastasis is dependent on L-tryptophan

To study the metabolic reprogramming induced by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency, we compared the untargeted metabolomes of FDFT1-deficient with SQLE-deficient CRC cells (Fig. S4). “Protein digestion and absorption” and “phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan biosynthesis” pathways were remarkably enriched after knocking down FDFT1 or SQLE (Fig. 5A, B). In FDFT1 or SQLE- deficient CRC cells where cholesterol biosynthesis was inhibited, oxysterols derived from cholesterol, such as 7-ketocholesterol and 22(S)-hydroxycholesterol, were significantly decreased (Fig. 5C). Nevertheless, FDFT1 or SQLE-deficiency led to the accumulation of metabolites from phenylalanine and tryptophan metabolism (Fig. 5D, E). L-phenylalanine and L-tryptophan are essential amino acids to human body. The cytotoxicity of L-phenylalanine and L-tryptophan in CRC cells was determined (Fig. 5F, G). And at the concentration of non-cytotoxicity (20 μM), L-tryptophan accelerated CRC cell migration and invasion more potently than L-phenylalanine (Fig. 5H, I), an effect that was eliminated by overexpression of FDFT1 or SQLE (Fig. 5J, K). To investigate whether this phenotype depends on metabolites of tryptophan metabolism, CRC cells were treated with the compounds indole or indole-3-acrylic acid. At the concentration of 20 μM, neither indole nor indole-3-acrylic acid made an effect on HCT116 cell migration (Fig. 5L–N). L-tryptophan intensifies PDIA4-ACLY or SYVN1-SCD1 co-complex association to induce CRC metastasis and stemness

To further explore the molecular mechanism of FDFT1 or SQLE regulating CRC metastasis and stemness, protein–protein interaction (PPI) was analyzed using the STRING tool. Among the differentially expressed genes, both FDFT1 and PDIA4 interacted with ATP-citrate synthase (ACLY), while both SQLE and SYVN1 interacted with stearoyl-CoA desaturase (SCD1) (Fig. 6A). Indeed, SYVN1 mutually bound with SCD1 in co-complex association in endogenous assay (Fig. 6B). Surprisingly, tryptophan reinforced the association between SYVN1 and SCD1 in CRC cells (Fig. 6C). PDIA4 also bound with ACLY in co-complex association, while ACLY was not able to bound with PDIA4 under the same lysis condition, possibly due to the protein spatial constraints (Fig. 6D). Likewise, tryptophan reinforced the co-complex association between PDIA4 and ACLY in CRC cells (Fig. 6E). Then we speculated that loss of FDFT1 or SQLE enhances the association between PDIA4-ACLY or SYVN1-SCD1, thereby activating ACLY or SCD1. The SB 204990 and Aramchol, which are targeted inhibitors of ACLY and SCD1, were applied for further experiments. The IC50 of SB 204990 and Aramchol were measured in CRC cells (Fig. 6F, G). At the concentration of non-cytotoxicity, SB 204990 and Aramchol, respectively inhibited the increase in CRC invasion induced by FDFT1 or SQLE deficiency (Fig. 6H–K). Moreover, the enhanced self-renewal of CRC stem cells caused by knockdown of FDFT1 or SQLE was impaired by SB 204990 or Aramchol as well (Fig. 6L–N). Furthermore, siRNAs targeting ACLY and SCD1 were synthesized and tested for efficiency in HCT116 cells (Fig. S5A, B). The most effective siRNA of ACLY and SCD1 were treated with FDFT1 or SQLE-deficient HCT116 cells (Fig. S5C, D). Knockdown of ACLY suppressed cell invasion in FDFT1-deficient HCT116 cells rather than in shcontrol HCT116 cells (Fig. S5E, G). Yet knockdown of SCD1 inhibited cell invasion in shcontrol HCT116 cells rather than in SQLE-deficient HCT116 cells (Fig. S5F, H). Consequently, ACLY deficiency eliminated the enhanced self-renewal of HCT116 stem cells caused by knockdown of FDFT1 (Fig. S5I, K). Whereas SCD1 deficiency eliminated self-renewal of shcontrol HCT116 stem cells remarkably, rather than SQLE-deficient HCT116 cells (Fig. S5J, K). Notably, the protein stability of SQLE and SCD1 mutually interfered with each other (Fig S5D). Therefore, pharmacological inhibition of SCD1 by Aramchol is more appropriate for impairing CRC metastasis induced by SQLE deficiency. The experiment should be terminated in advance due to severe ascites in mice with mesenteric metastasis (Fig. 7C). Sub-cytotoxic inhibitors of FDFT1 or SQLE generate invasive features in CRC cells

To investigate whether targeted inhibitors of FDFT1 or SQLE influence CRC metastasis, we selected the candidate drugs YM-53601 and Cetyl gallate. Lapaquistat and TAK-475 specifically inhibit FDFT1, yet have obvious liver toxicities. YM-53601 is a selective inhibitor of FDFT1, which inhibited enzyme activity without other major side effects27. Terbinafine is a targeted inhibitor of fungal SQLE, while NB-598 is a targeted inhibitor for human SQLE. However, NB-598 and its derivative FR194738 have a severe toxicity of dermatitis in preclinical studies. It has been reported that esters of gallic acid, which are food antioxidants specifically inhibit rat SQLE by scavenging reactive oxygen species required for the epoxidation reaction28. Then we measured the IC50 of octyl gallate, dodecyl gallate and cetyl gallate in CRC cells. Cetyl gallate, which structurally resembles squalene, was the most cytotoxic to CRC cells (Fig. S7). Subsequently, the IC50 of YM-53601 and cetyl gallate was examined in four CRC cell lines (Fig. 9A–C). YM-53601 and cetyl gallate presented a synergistic effect on inhibiting CRC cell proliferation, the combination index was all below 1 (Fig. 9D–G). At the concentration of no observed cytotoxicity, YM-53601 and cetyl gallate promoted CRC cell migration and invasion (Fig. 9H–J). They also decreased misfolded proteins and increased SNAI2 protein levels (Fig. 9K–O). Discussion

The fate of cancer cells depends on the duration and intensity of ER stress, which may stimulate oncogenic signaling or cell death29. In normal intestinal stem cells, low ER stress maintains stemness and blocks differentiation through the three branches of UPR-XBP1, ATF6, and PERK30. This research clarifies that loss of FDFT1 or SQLE promotes CRC metastasis and stemness through alleviating BiP-independent ER stress and strengthening PDIA4/ACLY or SYVN1/SCD1 co-complex association, a process modified by tryptophan (Fig. 10).Fig. 10Schematic diagram of this study.Loss of FDFT1 or SQLE promotes CRC metastasis and stemness through alleviating BiP-independent ER stress and degrading PDIA4 or SYVN1. It has been reported that excessive cholesterol induces degradation of SQLE through the GSK3β/p53 complex, promoting CRC aggressiveness31. Additionally, we proved that loss of SQLE triggered CRC metastasis and stemness through SYVN1-SCD1 co-complex association. SYVN1 interacts with the adapter protein SEL1L to export substrates for ubiquitination32. SCD1, located in the ER, catalyzes the conversion of saturated fatty acids to monounsaturated fatty acids using oxygen. Enzymes of fatty acid metabolism expedite CRC metastasis through EMT and angiogenesis33. Aramchol is the phase III clinical drug for inhibiting SCD134. This study suggests that Aramchol inhibits EMT and related stemness induced by suppress of SQLE.

Moreover, there is no prior evidence that FDFT1 functions on CRC metastasis. This research provided that FDFT1 deficiency accelerated CRC metastasis and stemness through PDIA4-ACLY co-complex association. PDIA4 facilitates disulfide bond formation in protein folding, which is concomitant with the level of ER stress35. ACLY is a rate-limiting enzyme of fatty acid synthesis and also essential for cholesterol biosynthesis36. The targeted inhibitor of ACLY, SB 204990, prevents CRC metastasis and stemness induced by suppress of FDFT1.

Furthermore, this study also confirms that the function of FDFT1 and SQLE on CRC metastasis depends on tryptophan, which exacerbates the co-complex association of SYVN1-SCD1 and PDIA4-ACLY. Tryptophan is primarily catabolized via the kynurenine pathway, with minor routes including bacterial indolic, serotonin, and tryptamine pathway37. Tryptophan and its metabolites drive aberrant cancer progression and be used for targets for cancer treatment38. Enzymes such as indoleamine-2,3-dioxygenase 1 (IDO1) and tryptophan-2,3-dioxygenase (TDO) are applied for developing inhibitors to resist cancer39. MYC elevates tryptophan metabolism and nuclear translocation of aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AHR) to accelerate colon cancer cell proliferation40. Tryptophan supplement gives negative feedback on ER stress and improves intestine homeostasis of piglets41.

However, this study has some limitations. The function of FDFT1 and SQLE on CRC stemness should be further explored in in vivo model. Since FDFT1 and SQLE have opposing effects on CRC cell proliferation and metastasis, the stages of CRC patients need to be carefully selected for this therapeutic approach. Biomarkers to distinguish the feasibility of targeting FDFT1 and SQLE also require further development.

In conclusion, this study reveals a common molecular mechanism of FDFT1/SQLE-induced CRC metastasis and stemness, proposing insight into how cholesterol synthases impact ER homeostasis and fatty acid synthesis.

Methods

Reagents and equipment

The reagents and equipment applied in this research were provided in Table S1.

Cell culture

The colorectal cancer cell lines RKO, HCT116, HT29, and the human embryonic kidney cell line 293T were utilized. All cell lines were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Shanghai Institute of Life Sciences, Chinese Academy of Sciences (Shanghai, China). RKO and 293T cells were cultured in DMEM supplemented with 10% FBS and 1% P/S solution. HCT116 and HT29 cells were cultured in RPMI 1640 medium containing the same concentrations of FBS and P/S solution. All cell lines were incubated in a humidified incubator maintained at 37 °C with a 5% CO₂ atmosphere. The culture medium was refreshed every 2–3 days, and cells were passaged when they reached approximately 80% confluence using 0.25% trypsin-EDTA solution.

Plasmids and stable cell line construction

The pCDH-PURO vector was used to clone full-length PDIA4 and SYVN1. The construction of FDFT1 and SQLE knockdown and overexpression plasmids and stable RKO and HT29 cell lines was carried out in accordance with the methods described in previous articles published by our research group. As well, the stable HCT116 cell line was successfully established.

Migration and invasion quantification

The cells were digested with trypsin, centrifuged, washed with serum-free medium, and resuspended in serum-free medium before being added to the upper chamber of the transwell insert. Medium containing 10% FBS was added to the 24-well plate in advance. In the case of invasion experiments, Matrigel was diluted to an appropriate concentration with serum-free medium before being added to the upper chamber of the transwell insert. After incubation in a 37 °C incubator for 1 h, the solution was aspirated, and the cell suspension was added according to the above mentioned steps. After 48–72 h of culture, the cells were stained with crystal violet, and then be observed, photographed under a microscope, and statistically analyzed using Image J.

Cell adhesion assay

The procedures of the cell adhesion assay were according to the methods described previously42.

Immunofluorescence staining

Cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde at 37 °C for 15 min, then be washed 3 times with PBS. Next, the cells were permeabilized with 0.2% Triton X-100 or not on ice for 15 min. After permeabilization, cells on slides were incubated with primary antibodies under optimized conditions. Subsequently, cells were incubated with second antibodies and DAPI which represented cellular nucleus. Finally, samples were observed and photographed under a fluorescence microscope. The acquired images were analyzed using Image J software. The antibodies were supplemented in Table S2.

RNA extraction and real-time PCR

Total RNA was isolated from the cells using Trizol reagent in accordance with the standard experimental procedures. The concentration and purity of the isolated RNA were determined by a spectrophotometer. Subsequently, in accordance with the instructions of the kit, the total RNA was reverse transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA) using RT Master Mix. Finally, with SYBR Green as the fluorescent dye, real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR was performed on the cDNA in a thermal cycler. The relative expression levels of the target genes were calculated using 2(−ΔΔCt), and the data were calibrated with reference genes (ACTB or TUBB) to ensure the accuracy and reliability of the results. The primers of real-time PCR were supplemented in Table S3.

RNA interference

The small interfering RNAs (siRNAs) targeting human PDIA4, SYVN1, and SEL1L were purchased from Zixi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. The transfection was carried out using Lipofectamine RNAiMAX reagent, strictly following the operation instructions provided by the manufacturer. The siRNA target sequences used in this study are provided in Table S4.

Sphere formation assay

The cells were digested, washed with DMEM-12 and resuspended with DMEM-12 containing NSC-27, EGF, and FGF2. Subsequently, cells were seeded into 24-well plates with ultra-low attachment at a density of 5000 cells per well. To further optimize the observation of sphere formation, the cell suspension was mixed with matrigel. The spheres were observed every other day and photographed for 7–10 days. The acquired images were analyzed using Image J software.

Western blot

The cells were suspended by RIPA lysis buffer containing protease and phosphatase inhibitors with ultrasonic disruption. The concentration of extracted proteins was determined by the bicinchoninic acid assay. The protein samples were mixed and adjusted equally with the loading buffer and boiled for 10 min. The samples were subjected to SDS-PAGE and be transferred to PVDF membranes. The PVDF membranes were blocked with 5% skim milk or BSA at room temperature for 1 h, then be incubated with the primary antibodies at 4 °C overnight and secondary antibodies at room temperature for 3 h. The membranes were detected by enhanced chemiluminescence and photographed. β-actin or β-tubulin was served as a loading control. The antibodies were supplemented in Table S2.

Aggresome staining

The ProteoStat® Aggresome detection kit was applied for detecting the misfolded proteins in cellular. The procedures were following the operation instructions provided by the manufacturer. Then the cells were stained by DAPI, photographed under fluorescence microscope and analyzed by Image J software.

Co-immunoprecipitation

The procedures of the co-immunoprecipitation assay were according to the methods described previously42.

Cytotoxicity and drug combination assay

Colorectal cancer cells were seeded into 96-well plates, followed by drug treatments at different concentrations. Then, the CCK-8 solution was added to each well. The absorbance was measured at a wavelength of 450 nm using a microplate reader. According to the corresponding formula, we calculated the cell inhibition rate and IC50 value by GraphPad Prism software. The combination index (CI) value was calculated by Compusyn software, CI > 1 represented the antagonism, CI = 1 represented an additive effect, and CI < 1 represented synergistic effect.

Animal experiments and treatments

Male BALB/c nude mice aged 6–8 weeks were purchased from Hangzhou Ziyuan Laboratory Animal Technology Co., Ltd. The nude mice were uniformly housed in individually ventilated cages and specific pathogen-free environment. According to body weight, the nude mice were randomly grouped and splenic injected with the stable HCT116 cells (1.5 × 106 cells). The mice were anesthetized via isoflurane inhalation. For SB 204990 or Aramchol treatment, 7 days after the intrasplenic injection, the mice were intraperitoneal injected with DMSO, SB 204990 or Aramchol group (5 mg/kg) each other day for 14 days. The splenic injection model was described previously42. The mice were euthanized by isoflurane inhalation and cervical dislocation. The application of animals was according to the guidelines set by the Animal Center of Tongji University.

Immunohistochemistry analysis

The procedures of Immunohistochemistry analysis were according to the methods described previously42. The antibodies were supplemented in Table S2.

Statistics and reproducibility

The statistical analysis was using GraphPad Prism 8 software. The IC50 was calculated from the dose–response curve to evaluate the potency of the tested substance. The fraction affected-combination index (Fa-CI) scatter plot was generated by Compusyn software, and the CI value was calculated to determine the type of interaction between different factors or compounds. The difference between two groups was expressed as mean ± standard error of mean (mean ± SEM), each experiment was followed by at least 3 replicates. Statistical significance was determined through unpaired Student’s t tests or multiple t-tests. A P value less than 0.05 (P < 0.05) indicated statistical significance. P < 0.05, *; P < 0.01, **; P < 0.001, ***.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a non-singular, projective, complex variety. Rational curves in
X
form an important part of studying the structure of
X
.
X
is said to be rationally connected, if for two general points on
X
, there is a rational curve passing through them. This is similar to the notion of path connectedness in topology. The following theorem is an interesting consequence of rational connectedness:

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 1.1]

Let
f:X\rightarrow B
be a dominant, proper morphism of complex varieties with
B
a smooth curve. If the geometric generic fiber of
f
is rationally connected, then
f
has a section.

A similar question can be asked about a dominant, proper morphism
f:X\rightarrow S
from a complex variety to a smooth surface. The notion of rationally simply connectedness provides a criterion for the existence of a generic section in this context. Informally,
X
being rationally simply connectedness means that the space of rational curves connecting two general points in
X
if rationally connected for large enough degree. Additionally, we need the existence of a
very twisting surface
in
X
. For the complete definition, see Section
6
. As discussed above, here is one of the most important consequences of rationally simply connectedness:

Theorem 1.2
.

[
4
, Corollary 13.2]

Let
S
be a smooth, irreducible, projective surface over an algebraically closed field of characteristics
0
. Let
f:X\rightarrow S
be a flat proper morphism. Assume that the geometric generic fiber of
f
is rationally simply connected, then there exists a rational section of
f
.

Additionally, here is a recent result about approximating holomorphic maps to rationally simply connected varieties.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
2
, Theorem 1.2]

Let
B
and
X
be smooth projective algebraic varieties over
\mathbb{C}
. Assume that
X
is rationally simply connected and
B
is a curve. Let
U\subset B(\mathbb{C})
be an open subset in the analytic topology. Let
h:U\rightarrow X
be a holomorphic map. Then there are algebraic maps
B\rightarrow X
that approximate
h
.

2.
Orthogonal Grassmannian

For the rest of the paper, we will assume the ground field is
\mathbb{C}
. Let
V
be an
n+1
-dimensional vector space with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
Q
on it. Let
k
be a fixed integer. We say a
k\leq\frac{n+1}{2}
-dimensional subspace
W\subset V
is isotropic if
Q(w_{1},w_{2})=0
for all vectors
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
in
W
.

Let
Gr(k,n+1)
denote the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces in
V
. Let
OG(k,n+1)\subset Gr(k,n+1)
be the locus of
k
-dimensional subspaces isotropic with respect to
Q
. If
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is the tautological vector bundle on
Gr(k,n+1)
,
Q
corresponds to a section of the rank
\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
vector bundle
Sym^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})
.
OG(k,n+1)
is exactly the vanishing locus of this section. Using local charts on the grassmannian, we see that this locus has the expected co-dimension. Hence the co-dimension of
OG(k,n+1)
in
Gr(k,n+1)
is
\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
and

dim(OG(k,n+1))=\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}

Remark 2.1
.

A priori the above discussion depends on the form
Q
. However, since all non-degenerate symmetric bilinear forms are equivalent (up to a change of basis)
OG(k,n+1)
is well-defined up to isomorphism.

Hereon, we will denote
OG(k,n+1)
by
OG
(keeping
k
and
n
fixed). We will also use
\mathcal{S}_{k}
for the tautological rank
k
vector subbundle of
V\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Gr(k,n+1)}
on
Gr(k,n+1)
pulled back to
OG
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assuming
n\geq 2k+1
,
OG
is a smooth, irreducible, projective, Fano variety with
Pic(OG)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. A generator for
Pic(OG)
is given by the ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{OG}(1)=\det(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})\cong\mathcal{O}_{Gr(k,n+1)}(1)|_{OG}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

With respect to the ample bundle described above,
deg(K_{OG})=-(n-k)
.

Proof.

On
Gr(k,n+1)
,
OG
is gives as the zero set of a regular section of
Sym^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})
. Hence we have a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow T_{OG}\rightarrow T_{Gr(k,n+1)}\arrowvert_{OG}\rightarrow Sym^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})\rightarrow 0

Therefore
det(T_{OG})=det(T_{Gr(k,n+1)})\arrowvert_{OG}\otimes det(Sym^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*}))^{*}
. We know that
det(T_{Gr(k,n+1)})
has degree
n+1
. Since
det(\mathcal{S}^{*}_{k})
has degree 1 and
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{k}
has rank
k
,
det(Sym^{2}(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*}))
has degree
k+1
. This proves the lemma.
∎

2.1.
Lines in Orthogonal grassmannians

Lines will refer to rational curves that have degree
1
with respect to
\mathcal{O}_{OG}(1)
. Let
F_{0,1}(OG)
denote the space parameterizing lines in
OG
with a marked point. There is a natural evaluation map
ev:F_{0,1}(OG)\rightarrow OG
. The fiber of this map over a point
p
is the space of lines passing through
p
, denoted hereon by
F_{p}(OG)
. We will give an explicit description of these two spaces.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p\in OG
be a point corresponding to an isotropic subspace
W\subset V
. Then there is an isomorphism
F_{p}(OG)\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times H
where
H
is a non-singular quadric hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n-2k}
.

Proof.

Note that a line in
OG
is given by subspaces
W_{k-1}\subset W_{k+1}
of dimensions
(k-1)
and
(k+1)
such that every
k
-dimensional subspace of
W_{k+1}
is isotropic. This in particular implies that
W_{k+1}
is isotropic as well. A marked point on this line is given by a subspace
W_{k}
of dimension
k
such that
W_{k-1}\subset W_{k}\subset W_{k+1}
. Hence
F_{0,1}(OG)
can be described as the closed locus inside
OG(k-1,n+1)\times OG(k,n+1)\times OG(k+1,n+1)
where the respective tautological bundles satisfy
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{k}\subset\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
. The map
ev
is the projection onto the second factor.

For a fixed
k
-dimensional isotropic subspace
W\subset V
, the choice of a
(k-1)
-dimensional subspace contained in
W
is given by
\mathbb{P}W^{*}
. A
(k+1)
-dimensional isotropic subspace
W_{k+1}
containing
W
defines a point
[v_{k+1}]
in
\mathbb{P}(W^{\perp}/W)
. Moreover, the original bilinear form
Q
induces a non-degenerate, symmetric bilinear form
\bar{Q}
on
W^{\perp}/W
. For
W_{k+1}
to be isotropic, we must have
\bar{Q}(v_{k+1},v_{k+1})=0
. This gives us a quadratic hypersurface
H_{W}
in
\mathbb{P}(W^{\perp}/W)
(Note that this has dimension
n-2k-1
). Therefore we have,
F_{p}(OG)=\mathbb{P}(W^{*})\times H_{W}\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times H
where
H
is a degree
2
, non-singular hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n-2k}
.
∎

3.
Lines in Hypersurfaces

We’ll state the following assumption for later use:

Assumption 1
.

Let
X
be a degree
d
hypersurface in
OG=OG(k,n+1)
where
n\geq 6k
and
d\leq n-2k-2

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a hypersurface of degree
d
in
OG
and fix a point
p\in X
. The locus of lines through
p
in
X
, given by,
F_{p}(X)\subset F_{p}(OG)\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times H
is the zero locus of a section of a vector bundle
F
with a filtration:

F=F_{1}\supset F_{2}\supset\ldots\supset F_{d+1}=0

where
F_{i}/F_{i+1}\cong\mathcal{O}(i,i)
.

Proof.

From
2.1
, there are three tautological vector bundles on
F_{0,1}(OG)
, namely
\mathcal{S}_{k-1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{k}\subset\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
. Let
E=\mathcal{S}_{k+1}/\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
and
L=\mathcal{S}_{k}/\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
. Let
i:L\hookrightarrow E
be the injection of vector bundles. By construction,
\pi:\mathbb{P}(E)\rightarrow F_{0,1}(OG)
is the universal family of lines and
i
induces the section
t:F_{0,1}(OG)\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(E)
corresponding to the marked point. Let
h:\mathbb{P}(E)\rightarrow OG
denote the morphism mapping each line to its image in
OG
. Let
X
be a degree
d
hypersurface in
OG
defined by
f_{X}\in\Gamma(OG,\mathcal{O}(d))
. Let
f^{\prime}_{X}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{P}(E),h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d))
and
\tilde{f}_{X}\in\Gamma(F_{0,1}(OG),\pi_{*}h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{O}G(d))
be the pull-back and push-forward sections. The vanishing locus of
\tilde{f}_{X}
is the set of points
[(l,p)]\in F_{0,1}(OG)
such that the zero locus of
f^{\prime}_{X}
contains the entire fiber
\pi^{-1}([(l,p)])
. That is, the pointed line corresponding to
[(l,p)]
lies in
X
. Therefore we can identify
F_{0,1}(X)\subset F_{0,1}(OG)
with the vanishing locus of
\tilde{f}_{X}
.

Since
h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d)
is a line bundle with positive degree
d
on the fibers of
\pi
, the higher direct images vanish and
\pi_{*}h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d)
is a locally free sheaf. Since
\mathbb{P}(E)
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
bundle over
F_{0,1}(OG)
, we have:

h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d)=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(d)\otimes\pi^{*}M\implies\pi_{*}h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d)=\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(d)\otimes M=Sym^{d}(E^{*})\otimes M

where
M
is a line bundle on
F_{0,1}(OG)
. Consider the composition
h\circ t:F_{0,1}(OG)\rightarrow OG
. This is the same as the map
ev:F_{0,1}(OG)\rightarrow OG
induced by
\mathcal{S}_{k}
. Therefore

t^{*}(h^{*}\mathcal{O}_{OG}(d))=det(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})^{d}\implies t^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(d)\otimes M=det(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})^{d}

Since
t
is given by the inclusion
i:L\hookrightarrow E
,
t^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(E)}(d)=(L^{*})^{d}
. Hence:

M=det(\mathcal{S}_{k}^{*})^{d}\otimes L^{d}\implies M=det(\mathcal{S}_{k-1}^{*})^{d}

Let
N_{1}=\mathcal{S}_{k}/\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
and
N_{2}=\mathcal{S}_{k+1}/\mathcal{S}_{k}
. Then we have a short exact sequence of locally free sheaves:

0\rightarrow N_{2}^{*}\rightarrow E^{*}\rightarrow N_{1}^{*}\rightarrow 0
.
Taking the
d
-fold symmetric product we get a filtration

Sym^{d}(E^{*})=E_{0}\supset E_{1}\supset E_{2}\ldots\supset E_{d+1}

such that:

E_{i}/E_{i+1}=(N_{2}^{*})^{i}\otimes(N_{1}^{*})^{(d-i)}

Restricting to
F_{p}(OG)
,
\mathcal{S}_{k}|_{F_{p}(OG)}
is a trivial vector bundle. Using the identification
F_{p}(OG)\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times H
, we have
N_{1}^{*}=det(S_{k-1})=\mathcal{O}(-1,0),N_{2}^{*}=det(S_{k+1}^{*})=\mathcal{O}(0,1),M=\mathcal{O}(d,0)
. Restricting the above filtration and tensoring with
M
, we have:
F_{p}(X)\subset F_{p}(OG)
is given by a section
\tilde{f}
of a vector bundle
F
with a filtration

F=F_{0}\supset F_{1}\supset\ldots\supset F_{d+1}=0

such that:
F_{i}/F_{i+1}\cong\mathcal{O}(i,i)
. In particular the first factor is the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}(0,0)
and
\tilde{f}
restricts to the zero section of
\mathcal{O}(0,0)
if and only if
p\in X
. Therefore, assuming
p\in X
, we may replace
F
by
F_{1}
to finish the proof.
∎

Note that
F_{p}(X)
has expected co-dimension
d
in
F_{p}(OG)
. Therefore the expected dimension of
F_{p}(X)
is
n-k-d-2
.

Notation 3.2
.

For
X
a hypersurface of degree
d
in
OG
, the expected dimension of
F_{p}(X)
is
n-k-d-2
and will be denoted
E(X,p)

Remark 3.3
.

Note that in the above filtration, the relevant
Ext
groups vanish and hence we can chose a splitting:

F\cong\mathcal{O}(1,1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(2,2)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}(d,d)

Denote by
\mathcal{X}
the universal family of degree
d
hypersurfaces in
OG
. Let
V_{d}
denote the vector space of degree
d
hypersurfaces and let
\pi_{1}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(V_{d})
and
\pi_{2}:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow OG
be the projection maps. Now, let us consider the relative space of rational lines in hypersurfaces. Define:

F_{0,1}\mathcal{(X)}:=\{(p,l,X)|p\in l\subset X\text{ where $p$ is a point, $l$ is a line and $X$ is a hypersurface}\}

Inside
\mathcal{X}
define the locus
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
to be the locus of points
(p,X)
such that in
F_{p}(OG)
, the section
\tilde{f}_{X}
of
F
defined by
X
restricted to
F/F_{2}
is zero and the dimension of
F_{p}(X)
is at least
2
more than
E(X,p)
.

Also, define
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
to be the locus of points
(p,X)
such that
\tilde{f}_{X}
restricted to
F/F_{2}
is non-zero and the dimension of
F_{p}(X)
is at least
1
more than
E(X,p)
.

Define
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{X}\setminus(\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cup\mathcal{Y}_{1})
to be the good locus. Explicitly, this is the locus of points
(p,X)
where, either
F_{p}(X)
has dimension

or
F_{p}(X)
is exactly one dimension higher
E(X,p)
and this defect is caused by the Jacobian of the defining equation of
X
vanishing along
F_{p}(OG)
. The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 3.4
.

For a general degree
d
hypersurface
X
, the pair
(p,X)
lies in the good locus
\mathcal{U}
for all points
p
in
X
.

This is equivalent to showing that the projection map
\pi_{1}
restricted to
\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cup\mathcal{Y}_{1}
is not surjective onto
\mathbb{P}(V_{d})
. For a point
p\in OG
, let
\mathcal{Y}_{0,p}=\mathcal{Y}_{0}\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)
and
\mathcal{Y}_{1,p}=\mathcal{Y}_{1}\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

To prove Proposition
3.4
, it is enough to show that
\mathrm{codim}_{\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)}\mathcal{Y}_{i,p}\geq\dim OG
for
i=0,1
.

Proof.

\mathrm{codim}_{\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)}\mathcal{Y}_{i,p}\geq\dim OG\implies\dim\mathcal{Y}_{i,p}\leq\dim\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)-\dim OG

Note that
\dim\pi_{2}^{-1}(p)=\dim\mathbb{P}(V_{d})-1
, thus:

\dim\mathcal{Y}_{i}\leq\dim\mathbb{P}(V_{d})-1-\dim OG+\dim OG=\dim\mathbb{P}(V_{d})-1

Thus
\mathcal{Y}_{i}
can not surject onto
\mathbb{P}{V_{d}}
.

∎

The following lemma on the rank of restriction maps will be useful later on:

Lemma 3.6
.

[
8
, Lemma 2.5]

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b}
be a subvariety. Denote by
r_{X,i}
, the rank of the restriction map
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b},\mathcal{O}(i,i))\rightarrow H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(i,i)|_{X})
. For a pure variety
X
of co-dimension
l
, there is a subvariety
W\subset\mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b}
such that:

(1)

W
has co-dimension
l
in
\mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b}
.

(2)

W
is a product of linear subvarieites of
\mathbb{P}^{a}
and
\mathbb{P}^{b}
.

(3)

r_{X,i}\geq r_{W,i}
.

Remark 3.7
.

In the above lemma
W\cong\mathbb{P}^{a-a_{1}}\times\mathbb{P}^{b-b_{1}}
where
a_{1}+b_{1}=l
. Thus, the restriction map is actually surjective and
r_{W,i}=\binom{a-a_{1}+i}{i}\binom{b-b_{1}+i}{i}=\binom{a-a_{1}+i}{i}\binom{b+a_{1}-l+i}{i}

Proposition
3.4
follows from Lemma
3.5
and the following:

Proposition 3.8
.

For
p
a point in
OG
, and
V_{d,p}
, the space of degree

hypersurfaces through

, we have:
\mathrm{codim}_{\mathbb{P}V_{d,p}}\mathcal{Y}_{i,p}\geq\dim OG
for

.

Proof.

Replace
\mathcal{Y}_{i,p}
with the corresponding locus
\mathcal{Y}^{a}_{i,p}
in
V_{d,p}
. It is enough to show
\mathrm{codim}_{V_{d,p}}\mathcal{Y}^{a}_{i,p}\geq\dim OG
.

Recall that
F_{p}(OG)
is the space of lines through a point
p
in
OG
and we can identify:

F_{p}(OG)\cong\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times H\subset\mathbb{P}^{k-1}\times\mathbb{P}^{n-2k}

For a degree
d
hypersurface
X
containing
p
,
F_{p}(X)
is given by the zero set of the corresponding section
f_{X}
of
F
. Denote by
Z_{i,X}
the zero set of the restriction of

to

. Let
W_{i}\subset V_{d,p}
denote the locus of hypersurfaces

such that

has co-dimension less than

in

. Denote by

is a zero divisor. Therefore, we have:

And:
\mathcal{Y}^{a}_{1,p}=(\bigcup\limits_{i=2}^{d}W_{i})\cap W_{1}^{c}=(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{d-1}W_{i}^{b})\cap W_{1}^{c}
. Therefore, it is enough to show that
\mathrm{codim}_{W_{i}^{c}}W_{i}^{b}\geq\dim(OG)
for

. Consider the vector spaces

It is enough to show that the fibers of the restricted map

in fibers of

. For

. We can identify

. Note that

\binom{k-a_{1}+i}{i+1}\binom{n-2k+a_{1}}{i+1}\geq\binom{n-2k}{2}\geq\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}=\dim OG
. Thus we conclude

.

The proof for

is the locus of hypersurfaces

vanishes. Let

be the same as above and consider, for

has codimension less than

is a zero-divisor. Hence:

and it is enough to show that

. Let

denote

. Then the following restriction maps are surjective:

By a similar argument as above, it is enough to check that for

∎

Irreducibility of the Space of Lines

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

is irreducible of the expected dimension

First we need the following lemma:

Proof.

From Proposition

, we know that for a general

and if

is exactly

. Let

denote the universal family of degree

and

be the locus of pairs

such that the Jacobian of

Recall that for a fixed point

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let

denote the evaluation map sending a pointed line to the corresponding point in
X
. From
[

, II.3.11]
, for a general

,

is non-singular. From Lemma

,

. Let

be an irreducible component dominating

, the fibers of

must have dimension at least

of
\phi^{t}
such that
s^{*}T_{\phi}
is ample and
s^{*}\mathcal{N}_{i}
is ample.

Proof.

Since the general fiber of
\phi^{t}
is rationally connected, there is a section
s
of
\phi_{t}

∎

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
F
denote the flag variety
Fl(1,2,\ldots,k-1;W_{1})
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be the tautological locally free sheaf of rank
i
on

T_{F}
,
0=E_{k}\subset E_{k-1}\subset\ldots E_{1}\subset T_{F}
such that the
i

W_{1}/\mathcal{S}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{S}_{i}/\mathcal{S}_{i-1})^{*}
.

Proof.

There is a sequence of maps
\phi_{i}:Fl(1,2,\ldots,k-1;W_{1})\rightarrow F_{i}=Fl(1,2,\ldots,i;W_{1})
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Pulling back the respective tangent bundles, we note that there is a short exact sequence:

0\rightarrow W_{1}/\mathcal{S}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{S}_{i}/\mathcal{S}_{i-1})^{*}\rightarrow\phi_{i}^{*}T_{F_{i}}\rightarrow\phi_{i-1}^{*}T_{F_{i-1}}\rightarrow 0

Let

. Then
E_{i}\subset E_{i-1}
and from the snake lemma
E_{i-1}/E_{i}\cong W_{1}/\mathcal{S}_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{S}_{i}/\mathcal{S}_{i-1})^{*}
. This is our desired filtration.
∎

Proposition 9.6
.

Let

via

Thus

∎

Lemma 9.8
.

Let
\pi:Y\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a morphism of algebraic spaces. Assume that there exists a section
s:\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow Y
lying in the smooth locus of
\pi
, such that
s^{*}T_{\pi}
is an ample vector bundle. Then: a general fiber of
\pi
is rationally connected.

Proof.

Consider the space
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)
, parametrizing morphisms from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
Y
. Denote by
T_{Y}
the dual to
\Omega_{Y}
. There is a short exact sequence on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

0\rightarrow s^{*}T_{\pi}\rightarrow s^{*}T_{Y}\rightarrow T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\rightarrow 0

Thus
s^{*}T_{Y}
is ample and
[s]
is an unobstructed point in
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)
. Let
M
denote the irreducible component of
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)
containing
[s]
. By considering the degree of
s^{*}\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)
, we see that every point of
M
parameterizes a section of
\pi
. Since the evaluation map
M\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\rightarrow Y
is smooth at all points
([s],t)
for
t\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we conclude that there is a section
s^{\prime}
passing through a general point of
Y
such that
s^{\prime*}T_{\pi}
is ample. Thus we may assume
s(0)=p
for a general point
p\in Y
. Consider the space of morphisms
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)|_{0\mapsto p}
from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to
Y
with
0\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
mapping to
p\in Y
. There is a morphism
Mor(\pi):Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)|_{0\mapsto p}\rightarrow Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1})_{0\mapsto 0}
. Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
denote the irreducible components of
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},Y)|_{0\mapsto p}
and
Mor(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{P}^{1})_{0\mapsto 0}
containing
[s]
and
[id]
respectively. The tangent spaces of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
at
[s]
and
[id]
are
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},s^{*}T_{Y}(-1))
and
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},T_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1))
respectively. Twisting the above short exact sequence by
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-1)
and taking the long exact sequence in cohomology, we see that
Mor(\pi)
is smooth at
[s]
and has fiber dimension
h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},s^{*}T_{\pi}(-1))\geq 1
. Thus, by an application of the rigidity lemma (
[
15
, p. 43]
),
s
must specialize to a reducible curve
\tilde{s}:C\rightarrow Y
with at least two irreducible components passing through
p
. Again, by considering the degree of
\tilde{s}^{*}\pi^{*}\mathcal{O}(1)
,
C
has exactly one component
C_{0}
such that
\tilde{s}|_{C_{0}}
is a section of
\pi
and the other components are contracted by
\pi
. Thus there is a rational curve through
p
contained in the fiber over
\pi
. Since
p
was a general point, a general fiber of
\pi
is uniruled.

Now consider the relative MRC quotient (see
[
16
]
for a construction):
g:Z\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, an algebraic space over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
f:Y\dashrightarrow Z
a rational map such that
g\circ f=\pi
. Let
U\subset Y
be the locus where
f
is defined. A general fiber of
f
is birationally rationally connected and any rational curve meeting a general fiber of
f
is contracted by
f
. Therefore, by
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
the fibers of
g
are not uniruled. Now, we may assume,
s(\mathbb{P}^{1})
meets
U
. Thus
f\circ s
is a section of
g
and hence lies in the smooth locus of
g
. Moreover
s(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cap U
also lies in the locus where
f
is smooth. Thus pulling back the relative tangent bundles to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, there is a map:

s^{*}T_{\pi}\rightarrow(f\circ s)^{*}T_{g}

This is a surjection over
s^{-1}(s(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cap U)
and hence the co-kernel is a torsion sheaf. Since
s^{*}T_{\pi}
is ample, this can only happen if
(f\circ s)^{*}T_{g}
is ample as well. Thus the argument above proving uniruledness of fibers of
\pi
also applies to
g
as long as
Z
is at least
2
-dimensional. This is a contradiction, hence we must have that
g
is an isomorphism. Therefore a general fiber of
\pi
is rationally connected.
∎

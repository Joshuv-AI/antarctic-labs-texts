A Remark on a generalized Suita conjecture for finite points case
Qi’an Guan, Xun Sun, Zheng Yuan

Abstract
. In this article, we use a class of harmonic functions (maybe multi-valued) to study the equality part in a weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and finite points case, and we obtain some sufficient and necessary conditions for the equality part to hold when the harmonic part u u of the weight is trivial.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
32D15, 30F15, 32F30, 32U05

1.
introduction

Let
\Omega
be an open Riemann surface which admits a nontrivial Green function
G_{\Omega}
. Let
z_{0}
be a point in
\Omega
, and let
w
be a local coordinate on a neighborhood
V_{z_{0}}\Subset\Omega
of
z_{0}
satisfying that
w(z_{0})=0
.
Then the logarithmic capacity (see
[
17
]
) of
z_{0}
is locally defined by

c_{\beta}(z_{0}):=\exp\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{0}}(G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})-\log|w(z)|),

and the analytic capacity (see
[
17
]
) of
z_{0}
is defined by

c_{B}(z_{0}):=\sup\{|f^{\prime}(z_{0})|:f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega),|f|\leq 1,f(z_{0})=0\}.

Let
B_{\Omega}(z_{0})
be the Bergman kernel function on
\Omega
, which is defined by

B_{\Omega}(z_{0}):=\frac{2}{\inf\{\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{F}|:\widetilde{F}\in H^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{O}(K_{\Omega}))\&(\widetilde{F}-dw)\in(\mathcal{I}(2G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0}))\otimes\mathcal{O}(K_{\Omega}))_{z_{0}}\}},

where
|\widetilde{F}|^{2}:=\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{F}\wedge\bar{\widetilde{F}}
for any holomorphic
(1,0)
form
\widetilde{F}
on
\Omega
.

In
[
17
]
, Sario and Oikawa posed an open question:
find a relation between the magtitudes of the quantities
\sqrt{\pi B_{\Omega}(z_{0})}
,
c_{\beta}(z_{0})
and
c_{B}(z_{0})
.

As
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})=\sup_{v\in\Delta_{0}(z_{0})}v(z)
(see
[
17
]
), where
\Delta_{0}(z_{0})
is the set of negative subharmonic functions
v
on
\Omega
satisfying that
v-\log|w|
has a locally finite upper bound near
z_{0}
, we know that
c_{\beta}(z_{0})\geq c_{B}(z_{0})
. In
[
18
]
, Suita proved that

Theorem 1.1

(
[
18
]
)
.

\pi B_{\Omega}(z_{0})\geq(c_{B}(z_{0}))^{2}
holds for any
z_{0}\in\Omega
, and the equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a (possible) closed set of inner capacity zero.

For the relation between
B_{\Omega}(z_{0})
and
c_{\beta}(z_{0})
, Suita
[
18
]
proved
\pi B_{\Omega}(z_{0})>(c_{\beta}(z_{0}))^{2}

when
\Omega
is a doubly connected domain with no degenerate boundary component, and posed the following conjecture (so called
Suita Conjecture
).

Conjecture 1.2

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be any open Riemann surface which admits a nontrivial Green function
G_{\Omega}
.

\pi B_{\Omega}(z_{0})\geq(c_{\beta}(z_{0}))^{2}
holds for any
z_{0}\in\Omega
, and the equality holds if and only if
\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a (possible) closed set of inner capacity zero.

In fact, a closed set of inner capacity zero is a polar set (locally singularity set of a subharmonic function).

The inequality part of Suita conjecture for bounded planar domains was proved by Blocki
[
2
]
, and the inequality part of Suita conjecture for open Riemann surfaces
was proved by Guan-Zhou
[
13
]
. In
[
14
]
, Guan-Zhou established an optimal
L^{2}
extension theorem in a general setting, and as an application, they proved the equality part of Suita conjecture, which completed the proof of Suita conjecture.

There are many generalized Suita conjectures. In
[
19
]
, Yamada posed a harmonic weight version of Suita conjecture (called extended Suita conjecture), which was proved by Guan-Zhou in
[
14
]
. In
[
7
]
, Guan-Mi proved a subharmonic weight version of Suita conjecture by using a concavity property of minimal
L^{2}
integrals (see
[
6
,
7
]
) and the solution of extended Suita conjecture, and the case that weights may not be subharmonic was proved by Guan-Yuan
[
10
]
. In
[
8
]
, Guan-Mi-Yuan established a concavity property of minimal
L^{2}
integrals with Lebesgue measurable gain on weakly pseudoconvex Kähler manifolds, and proved a weighted Suita conjecture for high derivatives. After that, we
[
9
]
gave some results on the set of points for the holding of the equality in a weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives, which is highly related to the Dirichlet problem when
\Omega
is a planar domain bounded by finite analytic closed curves.

Note that the above generalized Suita conjectures are corresponding to the optimal
L^{2}
extension problem from a single point to open Riemann surfaces (see
[
16
,
11
]
). Thus, it is a nature question that
can one prove a generalized Suita conjecture for analytic subsets case?

Without using the solution of (extended) Suita conjecture, Guan-Yuan
[
11
]
proved a weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and analytic subsets case.

In this article, we focus on the equality part in the weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and finite points case, and we further discuss the conditions under which the equality holds.

We recall some notations (see
[
5
]
, see also
[
14
,
11
]
). Let
p:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
by the universal covering from unit disc
\Delta
to
\Omega
. We call the holomorphic function
f
on
\Delta
a
multiplicative function, if there is a character
\chi
, which is the representation of the fundamental group of
\Omega
, such that
g^{*}f=\chi(g)f
, where
|\chi|=1
and
g
is an element of the fundamental group of
\Omega
.
Denote the set of such kinds of

by
\mathcal{O}^{\chi}(\Omega)
.

It is known that for any harmonic function

on
\Omega
,
there exists a
\chi_{u}
and a multiplicative function

,
such that
|f_{u}|=p^{*}\left(e^{u}\right)
.
If
u_{1}-u_{2}=\log|f|
, then
\chi_{u_{1}}=\chi_{u_{2}}
,
where
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are harmonic functions on
\Omega
and
f
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
.
Recall that for the Green function
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})
,
there exist a
\chi_{z_{0}}
and a multiplicative function
f_{z_{0}}\in\mathcal{O}^{\chi_{z_{0}}}(\Omega)
such that
|f_{z_{0}}(z)|=p^{*}\left(e^{G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})}\right)
, and
\chi_{z_{0}}=1
holds if and only if
\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a (possible) closed set of inner capacity zero (see
[
18
]
).

Let

Z_{0}:=\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}\}\subset\Omega

be a subset of
\Omega
satisfying that

z_{j}\neq z_{k}
for
j\neq k
. Let
w_{j}
be a local coordinate on a neighborhood
V_{z_{j}}\Subset\Omega
of
z_{j}
satisfying that
w_{j}(z_{j})=0
for
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, where
V_{z_{j}}\cap V_{z_{k}}=\emptyset
for any
z_{j}\neq z_{k}
.
Let
c_{\beta}(z_{j})
be the logarithmic capacity on
\Omega
, which is locally defined by

c_{\beta}(z_{j}):=\exp\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{j}}(G_{\Omega}(z,z_{j})-\log|w_{j}(z)|).

Now we consider the weighted Bergman kernel function for high derivatives and finite points.
Let
k_{j}
be a nonnegative integer for
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
,
a_{j}
be a nonzero complex number for
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.
Denote that
\mathcal{Z}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
,
\mathcal{K}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m})
,
\mathcal{A}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})
.
Let
f
be a holomorphic
(1,0)
form on
V_{0}:=\cup_{i=1}^{m}V_{z_{i}}
satisfying that
f=a_{j}w_{j}^{k_{j}}dw_{j}
on
V_{z_{j}}
. Denote that

\begin{split}&B_{\Omega,\rho}^{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A}):=\\
&\frac{2}{\inf\{\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{F}|\rho:\widetilde{F}\in H^{0}(\Omega,\mathcal{O}(K_{\Omega}))\&(\widetilde{F}-f)\in(\mathcal{I}(2(k_{j}+1)G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j}))\otimes\mathcal{O}(K_{\Omega}))_{z_{j}}\,\forall j\}},\end{split}

where
\rho
is a nonnegative Lebesgue measurable function on
\Omega
and
|\widetilde{F}|^{2}:=\sqrt{-1}\widetilde{F}\wedge\bar{\widetilde{F}}
for any holomorphic
(1,0)
form
\widetilde{F}
on
\Omega
.

Let
c(t)
be a positive measurable function on
(0,+\infty)
satisfying that
c(t)e^{-t}
is decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
and
\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(s)e^{-s}ds<+\infty
.
Denote that

\rho=e^{-(2\sum_{j=1}^{m}(k_{j}+1-p_{j})G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})+2v)}c(-\sum_{j=1}^{m}2p_{j}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})),

where
p_{j}>0
is a constant for any
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
and
v
is a subharmonic function on
\Omega
. Denote that
\alpha_{j}:=\sum_{1\leq l\leq m,l\not=j}(k_{l}+1)G_{\Omega}(z_{j},z_{l})+v(z_{j})
. Let us recall the solution of a weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and finite points case.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
11
]
)
.

Suppose that
v(z_{j})>-\infty
for
1\leq j\leq m
. Then

1\leq\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(s)e^{-s}ds\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\pi|a_{j}|^{2}e^{-2\alpha_{j}}}{p_{j}c_{\beta}(z_{j})^{2(k_{j}+1)}}\right)B_{\Omega,\rho}^{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A}).

holds. Moreover, equality

(1.1)

1=\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(s)e^{-s}ds\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\pi|a_{j}|^{2}e^{-2\alpha_{j}}}{p_{j}c_{\beta}(z_{j})^{2(k_{j}+1)}}\right)B_{\Omega,\rho}^{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A})

holds if and only if the following statements hold:

(1)

v=\log|g|+u
, where
g
is a holomorphic function such that
g(z_{j})\neq 0
for each
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
and
u
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
;

(2)

\chi_{-u}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\chi_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1}
, where
\chi_{-u}
and
\chi_{z_{j}}
are the characters associated to the functions
-u
and
G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})
respectively;

(3)

\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{k}}\frac{f}{gp_{*}(f_{u}(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}f_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1})(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}p_{j}\frac{df_{z_{j}}}{f_{z_{j}}}))}=c_{0}
for any
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, where
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
is a constant independent of
k
.

Note that the holomorphic
(1,0)
form
p_{*}(f_{u}(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}f_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1})(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}p_{j}\frac{df_{z_{j}}}{f_{z_{j}}}))
in statement
(3)
is a single valued holomorphic
(1,0)
form on
\Omega
when statement
(2)
holds.

1.1.
Main result

In this article, we present some results about the equality part in the weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and finite points case.

We continue use the notations in Theorem
1.3
. Throughout this paper, we consistently assume
v=\log|g|+u
, where
g\not\equiv 0
is a holomorphic function on
\Omega
and
u
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
.

Let
\Pi_{1}
be a subset of the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
which generates
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
(when
\Omega
is simply connected, set
\Pi_{1}=\{0\}
). In this article,
\Pi_{1}
generates
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
means that there is no proper subgroup of
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
containing
\Pi_{1}
.
Denote that
\widetilde{d}:=\frac{\partial-\bar{\partial}}{i}
. For every
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
,
\gamma_{\alpha}
denotes a piecewise smooth closed curve on
\Omega
, which represents
\alpha
.
Let
p:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
be the universal covering of
\Omega
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a harmonic function
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
on
\Delta
such that

(1.2)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})-\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,p(z^{\prime}))\right)\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
z^{\prime}\in\Delta\backslash{p^{-1}(\gamma_{\alpha})}
.
Condition (
1.2
) implies that
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
is unique up to addition of a multiple of
2\pi
, and we will show that
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
depends only on the homology class of
\gamma_{\alpha}
in
\Omega
(up to addition of a multiple of
2\pi
), which in particular
shows that
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
depends only on
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(\Omega)
. It can be inferred from Condition (
1.2
) that
\frac{1}{2\pi}(\beta^{*}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}-\widetilde{u}_{\alpha})
is an integer constant function for any
\beta\in\pi_{1}(\Omega)
.

Based on Theorem
1.3
, we use
\{\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Pi_{1}}
to give a characterization of the holding of the equality in the weighted version of Suita conjecture for high derivatives and finite points case.

Theorem 1.4
.

Given
m
distinct points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}
satisfying that
g(z_{i})\neq 0
for each
i
, and
m
nonnegative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}
,
equality

(1.3)

1=\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(s)e^{-s}ds\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\pi|a_{j}|^{2}e^{-2\alpha_{j}}}{p_{j}c_{\beta}(z_{j})^{2(k_{j}+1)}}\right)B_{\Omega,\rho}^{\mathcal{K}}(\mathcal{Z},\mathcal{A})

holds for some numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
if and only if

(1.4)

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{k_{j}+1}{2\pi}\right)\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{z_{j}})+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and
\widetilde{z_{j}}\in p^{-1}(z_{j})
for
1\leq j\leq m
.

Following from Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
, we know that
if the condition (
1.4
) is satisfied, the equality (
1.3
) holds for numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
if and only if there exists a constant
c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
such that

\lim_{z\rightarrow z_{k}}\frac{a_{j}w_{j}^{k_{j}}dw_{j}}{gp_{*}(f_{u}(\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}f_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1})(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}p_{j}\frac{df_{z_{j}}}{f_{z_{j}}}))}=c_{0}

holds for any
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
.

When
m=1
, the above theorem can be referred to
[
9
]
.

Remark 1.5
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a closed set of inner capacity zero. Then all
\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
are integer-valued functions on the unit disc
\Delta
. As a result, when

, condition (
1.4
) is always satisfied. On the other hand, if all
\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
are integer-valued functions on the unit disc

is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a closed set of inner capacity zero. We will prove the present remark in Section
3
.

Note that for any
m
points
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}
and any
m
nonnegative integers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{m}
, there exists a harmonic function
u
on
\Omega
such that
\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\chi_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1}=\chi_{-u}
(see

). Thus, following from Theorem

m
numbers

such that equality (

) holds. Thus, it is a natural question that

Question 1.6
.

Can one obtain some sufficient or necessary conditions for equality (

) to hold when

Recall that when
u=0
,
v=\log|g|
, where

is a holomorphic function on
\Omega

In the following part, we assume that

Denote that

preparation

In this section, we will do some preparations.

2.1.
Characters on open Riemann surfaces

Let
\Omega
be an open Riemann surface admitted a nontrivial Green function
G_{\Omega}
. Let
p:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
be the universal covering from the unit disc
\Delta

to
\Omega
. Let
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
be different points in
\Omega
. Since
p:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega
is the universal covering, any element in
\pi_{1}(\Omega,z_{0})
can be represent by
p\circ\widetilde{\gamma}
, where
\widetilde{\gamma}
is a curve in
\Delta
,
and it depends only on the starting point and end point of
\widetilde{\gamma}
. Therefore we can assume that
\widetilde{\gamma}
is piecewise smooth and lies in
\Delta\backslash p^{-1}(z_{1})
. Thus, any element
\alpha\in\pi_{1}(\Omega,z_{0})
can be represented by a
piecewise smooth curve
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1}\}
.

Recall that

\widetilde{d}=\frac{\partial-\bar{\partial}}{i},

and
u
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.1

(see
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\alpha
be an element in
\pi_{1}(\Omega,z_{0})
,
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1}\}
be a piecewise smooth curve representing
\alpha
. Then we have

\chi_{z_{1}}(\alpha)=e^{i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{1})}

and

\chi_{u}(\alpha)=e^{i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u}.

Let
\gamma\subset\Omega
be a piecewise smooth closed curve.
The following lemma shows that the pull-back of the function
\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z)
defined in
\Omega\backslash\gamma
can be extended to a harmonic function on
\Delta
modulo integer multiples of
2\pi
.

Lemma 2.2

(see
[
9
]
)
.

There exists a harmonic function
H(z^{\prime})
on
\Delta
such that
H(z^{\prime})=\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,p(z^{\prime}))+2k\pi
for
z^{\prime}\in\Delta\backslash p^{-1}(\gamma)
, where
k
is an integer depending on
z^{\prime}
.

Recall that
\Pi_{1}
is a subset of
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
which can generate
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
. The following lemma will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 2.3
.

\chi_{-u}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\chi_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1}
holds if and only if

e^{-i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}(k_{j}+1)\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and piecewise smooth curve
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
which can represent
\alpha
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\chi_{-u}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\chi_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1}

holds if and only if

e^{-i\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}u}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}(k_{j}+1)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})}

holds for any smooth closed curve
\gamma\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
with starting point and end point
z_{0}
.

For
1\leq l\leq m
, let
\gamma_{l}^{\prime}
be a smooth closed curve in
U_{l}\backslash\{z_{l}\}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
, where
U_{l}
is a small enough local coordinate ball with the center
z_{l}
such that
z_{l^{\prime}}\not\in U_{l}
for
l^{\prime}\not=l
. We require that
\gamma_{l}^{\prime}
winds around
z_{l}
once.
Since the first homology group
H_{1}(\Omega)
is the abelization of
\pi_{1}(\Omega)
, it is generated by the homology class of element of
\Pi_{1}
.
Note that
H_{1}(\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})\cong H_{1}(\Omega)\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{m}
,
and the homology classes of element of
\Pi_{1}
and the homology classes of
\gamma_{l}^{\prime}
for
1\leq l\leq m
generate
H_{1}(\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\})
.

Let
\gamma\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
be a smooth curve with starting point and end point
z_{0}
. There exists a sequence of integers
t_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
(finite many
t_{\alpha}
are nonzero) and
t_{l}^{\prime}
for
1\leq l\leq m
such that

\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{1}}t_{\alpha}\gamma_{\alpha}+\sum_{l=1}^{m}t_{l}^{\prime}\gamma_{l}^{\prime}
is homologous to
\gamma
in
\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
, where
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
is a piecewise smooth curve representing
\alpha
.

For
1\leq j\leq m
,
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{j})
and
u
are harmonic functions on
\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
,
\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})
and
\widetilde{d}u
are therefore closed differential 1-forms on
\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
.
Therefore we have

\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{1}}t_{\alpha}\int_{\gamma_{k}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})+\sum_{l=1}^{m}t_{l}^{\prime}\int_{\gamma_{l}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})

for
1\leq j\leq m
and

\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}u=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_{1}}t_{\alpha}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u+\sum_{l=1}^{m}t_{l}^{\prime}\int_{\gamma_{l}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}u.

Note that for
1\leq j\leq m
,
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{j})=\log|z-z_{j}|+u^{\prime}_{j}(z)
on
\Omega
on a local coordinate neighborhood
U_{j}
of
z_{j}
with coordinate
z
, where
u^{\prime}_{j}
is a harmonic function on
U_{j}
, hence

\int_{\gamma^{\prime}_{l}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})=\int_{\gamma_{l}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}\log|\cdot-z_{j}|+\int_{\gamma_{l}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}u_{j}^{\prime}=0

for
1\leq l\leq m
and
l\neq j
,

\int_{\gamma^{\prime}_{j}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})=\int_{\gamma_{j}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}\log|\cdot-z_{j}|+\int_{\gamma_{j}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}u_{j}^{\prime}=2\pi

and

\int_{\gamma_{l}^{\prime}}\widetilde{d}u=0.

for
1\leq l\leq m
.

From these results, we can see that

e^{-i\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}u}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}(k_{j}+1)\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})}

holds for any smooth closed curve
\gamma\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
with starting point and end point
z_{0}
if and only if

e^{-i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}(k_{j}+1)\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and piecewise smooth curve
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
representing
\alpha
.
Lemma
2.3
is therefore proved.
∎

2.2.
Some lemmas on planar domains

In this section, assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a bounded planar domain bounded by
n
analytic Jordan curves and
\mathbb{C}\backslash\Omega=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}
, where
\{A_{j}\}
are disjoint connected closed sets and
A_{n}
is
the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\backslash\Omega
. Denote by
\Gamma_{k}
the boundary of
A_{k}
, and denote
\Gamma:=\cup_{k}\Gamma_{k}
.

Firstly, we recall the following result in algebraic topology.

Lemma 2.4

(see
[
4
]
)
.

Let
U
be a bounded connected open subset of
\mathbb{C}
such that
\mathbb{C}\backslash U=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}
, where
A_{j}
are disjoint connected closed sets and
A_{n}
is unbounded. For each
1\leq j\leq n-1
, there exists a closed smooth curve
\gamma_{j}
in
\Omega
which winds around points in
A_{j}

once and does not wind around points in
A_{l}
for
l\neq j
. If
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}
is an another closed smooth curve which satisfies these conditions, then
\gamma_{j}^{\prime}
is homologous to
\gamma_{j}
.

In further, the homology classes of these
n-1
curves form a basis for the first homology group
H_{1}(U)
, i.e., these curves are not homologous to each other and any curve in
U
is homologous to some linear combinations of these curves.

Let us recall a famous result on the Dirichlet problem.

Lemma 2.5

(see
[
1
]
or
[
9
]
)
.

The Dirichlet problem on
\Omega
is always solvable, i.e., for any
f\in C(\Gamma)
, there is
\tilde{f}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
s.t.
\tilde{f}|_{\Gamma}=f
and
\tilde{f}
is harmonic on
\Omega
.

We will also need the following well-known formula.

Lemma 2.6

(The Green third formula, see
[
15
]
)
.

Let
f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
, which is harmonic on
\Omega
. Then we have

f(z_{0})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma}f\frac{\partial G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0})}{\partial n}ds

for
z_{0}\in\Omega
, where
\frac{\partial}{\partial n}
means the differentiation in the direction of the outward pointing normal,

s
is the arc-length parameter.

By Lemma
2.5
, for each
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we can get a unique function
u_{k}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
such that
u_{k}=1
on
\Gamma_{k}
,
u_{k}=0
on
\Gamma_{l}
for any
l\neq k
and
u_{k}
is harmonic on
\Omega
.

Let
m\geq n-1
, and let
\{\widetilde{z}_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{z}_{m}\}
be
m
distinct points in
\Omega
. For
1\leq j\leq m
, take a simply connected neighborhood
V_{j}
of
\widetilde{z}_{j}
such that

V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset
when
i\neq j
.
Every
u_{k}
can be realized as the real part of a holomorphic function
f_{k}
on
V_{0}:=\cup_{j=1}^{m}V_{j}
.

The following lemma will be used in the proof of Theorems
1.12
and
1.13
.

Lemma 2.7
.

For each
1\leq l\leq n-1
, there exists a point
(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{l}^{\prime})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{l}
such that the matrix
\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq l,1\leq j\leq l}
has rank
l
.
In particular, there exists a point
(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{n-1}^{\prime})\in V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n-1}
such that the matrix
\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq n-1,1\leq j\leq n-1}
has rank
n-1
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by induction on
l
.

When
l=1
, since
f_{1}
is not constant function on
V_{1}
,
\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}(z_{1}^{\prime})\neq 0
at some point
z_{1}^{\prime}\in V_{1}
.

Assume that this lemma has been proved for
l-1
. By induction, we can assume that

\det\left(\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq l-1,1\leq j\leq l-1}\right)\neq 0

for some point
z_{j}^{\prime}\in V_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq l-1
.
Hence there exists unique
l-1
numbers
k_{1},\ldots,k_{l-1}
such that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial z}(z_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial z}(z_{l-1}^{\prime})\right)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq l-1}k_{j}\left(\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial z}(z_{1}^{\prime}),\ldots,\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial z}(z_{l-1}^{\prime})\right).

For any point
z_{l}^{\prime}\in V_{l}
, it is easy to see that the inequality

\det\left(\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq l,1\leq j\leq l}\right)\neq 0

holds if and only if

\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime})\neq k_{1}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime})+\cdots+k_{l-1}\frac{\partial f_{l-1}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime}).

We can now complete the induction by contradiction. Suppose that

\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial z}=k_{1}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}+\cdots+k_{l-1}\frac{\partial f_{l-1}}{\partial z}

holds for any point
z_{l}\in V_{l}
, then

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}(f_{l}-k_{1}f_{1}-\cdots-k_{l-1}f_{l-1})=0

on
V_{l}
, which implies that
f_{l}-k_{1}f_{1}-\cdots-k_{l-1}f_{l-1}
is a constant function on
V_{l}
.
u_{l}-k_{1}u_{1}-\cdots-k_{n-2}u_{l-1}
is therefore a constant function on
V_{l}
.
Note that
u_{l}-k_{1}u_{1}-\cdots-k_{n-2}u_{l-1}
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
. Then, it is a constant function on
\Omega
, which is a contradiction since it takes value 1 on
\Gamma_{l}
and
0
on
\Gamma_{n}
.
Therefore there exists a point
z_{l}^{\prime}\in V_{l}
such that

\frac{\partial f_{l}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime})\neq k_{1}\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime})+\cdots+k_{l-1}\frac{\partial f_{l-1}}{\partial z}(z_{l}^{\prime}),

which implies that

\det\left(\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq l,1\leq j\leq l}\right)\neq 0.

∎

3.

In this section, we prove Theorem

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Using Lemma
2.2
, there exists a harmonic function
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
on
\Delta
such that

(3.1)

\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})-\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,p(z^{\prime}))\right)\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
z^{\prime}\in\Delta\backslash{p^{-1}(\gamma_{\alpha})}
.
Lemma
2.1
shows that
\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})
depends only on the homology class of
\gamma_{\alpha}
(up to addition of an integer).

By lemma
2.3
, we know that

\chi_{-u}=\prod_{1\leq j\leq m}\chi_{z_{j}}^{k_{j}+1}

if and only if

(3.2)

\displaystyle e^{-i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}(k_{j}+1)\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j})}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and piecewise smooth curves
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}\}
representing
\alpha
.
Using condition (
3.1
),
we know that equality (

) is equivalent to

\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\frac{k_{j}+1}{2\pi})\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{z_{j}})+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u\in\mathbb{Z}

for
\widetilde{z}_{j}\in p^{-1}(z_{j})
,
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and piecewise smooth closed curves
\gamma_{\alpha}
representing
\alpha
.
Thus, following from Theorem
1.3
,
Theorem
1.4
holds.

3.2.
Proof of Remark
1.5
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is the unit disc less a closed set of inner capacity zero and
z_{0}\in\Omega
is a fixed point. Since
\Omega\subset\Delta
, we know that
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})\geq G_{\Delta}(z,z_{0})
for
z\in\Omega\backslash\{z_{0}\}
.
On the other hand, a closed set of inner capacity zero is locally the pole set of a subharmonic function, and
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})
is a negative subharmonic function on
\Omega
,
hence
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})
can be extended to a negative subharmonic function on
\Delta
(see
[
3
]
), which implies that
G_{\Omega}(z,z_{0})\leq G_{\Delta}(z,z_{0})
for
z\in\Omega\backslash\{z_{0}\}
. Therefore,
G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0})=G_{\Delta}(\cdot,z_{0})
.
Given any piecewise smooth closed curve
\gamma\subset\Omega\backslash\{z_{0}\}
, it is clear that

\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0})=\int_{\gamma}\widetilde{d}G_{\Delta}(\cdot,z_{0})=2m\pi,

where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
z_{0}\in\Omega
is arbitrarily chosen, formula (
1.2
) implies that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
and
z^{\prime}\in\Delta
,
\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})
is therefore an integer-valued function on
\Delta
.

Now we assume that
\frac{1}{2\pi}\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})
are integer-valued functions on
\Delta
for all
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
.
Recall that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})-\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,p(z^{\prime}))\right)\in\mathbb{Z}

for
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
,
\gamma_{\alpha}
a closed smooth curve which can represent
\alpha
, and
z^{\prime}\in\Delta\backslash{p^{-1}(\gamma_{\alpha})}
.
Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}

for
\alpha\in\Pi_{1}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we have
\chi_{z_{0}}=1
, i.e., there exists a holomorphic function
f_{z_{0}}
on
\Omega
s.t.
|f_{z_{0}}|=e^{G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{0})}
,
which implies that
\Omega
is conformally equivalent to the unit disc less a closed set of inner capacity zero (see
[
18
]
).

3.3.
Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

Let
V_{j}\subset\Delta\backslash p^{-1}(g^{-1}(0))
be a simply connected neighborhood of
z_{j}^{\prime}
for
1\leq j\leq m
such that
V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. Therefore we can assume that
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha_{k}}
is the real part of a holomorphic function
f_{k}
on
V_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq m
and
1\leq k\leq m
.
Then by Cauchy-Riemann formula,

\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq m,1\leq j\leq m}

is nondegenerate.

For
1\leq k\leq m
, we define

F_{k}(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{m}):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{k}(\hat{z}_{j}),

where
(\hat{z}_{1},\ldots,\hat{z}_{m})\in V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{m}
. Then
F_{k}
is a holomorphic function on
V_{1}\times\ldots\times V_{m}
. Hence
(F_{1},\ldots,F_{m})
define a holomorphic mapping from
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{m}
to
\mathbb{C}^{m}
, and its jacobian at
(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{m}^{\prime})
is

\left(\frac{\partial F_{k}}{\partial\hat{z}_{j}}(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{m}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq m,1\leq j\leq m}=\left(\frac{\partial f_{k}}{\partial z}(z_{j}^{\prime})\right)_{1\leq k\leq m,1\leq j\leq m},

which is nondegenerate.

By the inverse mapping theorem,

\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{1}(\hat{z}_{j}),\ldots,\sum_{j=1}^{m}f_{m}(\hat{z}_{j})\right)

maps a neighborhood of
(z_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,z_{m}^{\prime})
conformally onto a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
,
hence there exists
m

\pi_{1}(\Omega_{j})
which generates
\pi_{1}(\Omega_{j})
(when
\Omega_{j}
is simply connected, set
\Pi_{1,j}=\{0\}
).
For every
\alpha\in\Pi_{1,j}
,
\gamma_{\alpha}
denotes a piecewise smooth closed curve on
\Omega_{j}
, which represents
\alpha
.
Let
P_{j}:\Delta\rightarrow\Omega_{j}
be the universal covering of
\Omega_{j}
.
By Lemma
2.2
, there exists a harmonic function
\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}
on
\Delta
such that

\frac{1}{2\pi}\left(\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(z^{\prime})-\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,P_{j}(z^{\prime}))\right)\in\mathbb{Z}

for any
z^{\prime}\in\Delta\backslash{P_{j}^{-1}(\gamma_{\alpha})}
.

Now we assume that the following statements holds:

(1)

\varphi_{j}=2\log|g_{j}|+2u_{j}
for any
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, where
u_{j}
is a harmonic function on
\Omega_{j}
and
g_{j}\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega_{j})
satisfying
g_{j}(z_{j,k})\neq 0
for any
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m_{j}\}
;

(2)
there exists a nonnegative integer
\gamma_{j,k}
for any
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m_{j}\}
, which satisfies that
\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\frac{\gamma_{j,\beta_{j}+1}}{p_{j,\beta_{j}}}=1
for any
\beta\in I_{1}
. Let
f=(c_{\beta}\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}w_{j,\beta_{j}}^{\gamma_{j,\beta_{j}}})dw_{1,\beta_{1}}\wedge dw_{2,\beta_{2}}\wedge\ldots\wedge dw_{n,\beta_{n}}
on
V_{\beta}
for any
\beta\in I_{1}
, where
c_{\beta}
is a constant.

Then we have the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists
\{c_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in I_{1}}
such that equality

C_{f,\psi}=\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}c(s)e^{-s}ds\right)\sum_{\beta\in I_{1}}\sum_{\alpha\in E_{\beta}}\frac{|d_{\beta,\alpha}|^{2}(2\pi)^{n}e^{-\varphi(z_{\beta})}}{\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(\alpha_{j}+1)c_{j,\beta_{j}}^{2\alpha_{j}+2}}

holds if and only if for each
1\leq j\leq n
,

(5.1)

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{j}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{j,k}+1}{2\pi}\right)\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{z_{j,k}})+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1,j}
and
\widetilde{z_{j,k}}\in p_{j}^{-1}(z_{j,k})
for
1\leq k\leq m_{j}
.

Proof.

By lemma
2.3
, we know that for each
1\leq j\leq n
,

\chi_{-u_{j}}=\prod_{1\leq k\leq m_{j}}\chi_{z_{j,k}}^{\gamma_{j,k}+1}

if and only if

(5.2)

\displaystyle e^{-i\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u_{j}}=e^{i\sum_{1\leq k\leq m_{j}}(\gamma_{j,k}+1)\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}G_{\Omega}(\cdot,z_{j,k})}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{j,1}
and piecewise smooth curves
\gamma_{\alpha}\subset\Omega_{j}\backslash\{z_{j,1},\ldots,z_{j,m_{j}}\}
representing
\alpha
.
By condition (
3.1
),
we know that for each
1\leq j\leq n
, equality (
5.2
) holds if and only if

\sum_{k=1}^{m_{j}}\left(\frac{\gamma_{j,k}+1}{2\pi}\right)\widetilde{u}_{\alpha}(\widetilde{z_{j,k}})+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\gamma_{\alpha}}\widetilde{d}u_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}

holds for any
\alpha\in\Pi_{1,j}
and
\widetilde{z_{j,k}}\in p_{j}^{-1}(z_{j,k})
for
1\leq k\leq m_{j}
.
Combining this with Theorem
5.1
, we know that
Theorem
5.2
holds.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Similarly to Theorem
5.2
, Theorem
1.8
,
1.12
and
1.13

can also be generalized to the corresponding product version on
\Omega=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}\Omega_{j}
.

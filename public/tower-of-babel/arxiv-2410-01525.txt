𝔸 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{1} -connected stacky curves and the Brauer group of Moduli of elliptic curves
Neeraj Deshmukh, Suraj Yadav

Abstract
. Given a smooth scheme X X with an action by an affine algebraic group G G , we give a formula to compute the Nisnevich sheaf of the motivic connected components of the quotient stack [ X / G ] [X/G] in the case of an orbifold. We apply it to identify all the 𝔸 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{1} -connected stacky curves and prove the 𝔸 1 {\mathbb{A}}^{1} -connectedness of the moduli stack of elliptic curves. We also prove homotopy purity for the smooth algebraic stacks and recover a computation of the Picard group of stacky orbifold curves by computing their mixed motive.

The program to understand the unstable

In

Remark 3.6
.

The results of this section can be summarised as follows:

induces morphisms of sheaves of sets

5.
Homotopy purity for stacks

In this section we explain homotopy purity for algebraic stacks. This essentially follows from descent once an appropriate scheme approximation is available in the Morel–Voevodsky category. For more general formulations of the homotopy purity property, including the stable version, see
[
12
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth algebraic stack over a scheme
S
and let
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{X}
be a smooth closed substack.
Then we have an equivalence,

\mathcal{X}/(\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Z})\simeq Th(N_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{X}))

in
\mathcal{H}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
X\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a smooth-Nisnevich covering. Then the associated Čech nerve is Nisnevich locally equivalent to
\mathcal{X}
, i.e,
X_{\bullet}\simeq\mathcal{X}
along the augmentation map (see
[
15
]
for details).

Let
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{X}\setminus\mathcal{Z}
. Restricting
X_{\bullet}
to
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{Z}
produces weak equivalences
U_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}
and
Z_{\bullet}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}
.

Consider the cofibre sequence,

\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}/\mathcal{U}

This sequence is equivalent to the sequence,

(5.1)

U_{\bullet}\rightarrow X_{\bullet}\rightarrow X_{\bullet}/U_{\bullet}

Note that the above cofibre can be constructed by taking the cofibre levelwise. Further,
observe that for each
i
, we have
U_{i}:=X_{i}\setminus Z_{i}
by construction and that the scheme
Z_{i}
is a smooth closed subscheme of
X_{i}
. Thus, by homotopy purity for schemes,
X_{i}/U_{i}\simeq Th(N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i}))
. Combining this with the cofibre sequence (
5.1
), we have an equivalence

(5.2)

\mathop{\rm hocolim}\limits Th(N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i}))\simeq X_{\bullet}/U_{\bullet}.

Thus, it remains to show that
\mathop{\rm hocolim}\limits Th(N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i}))\simeq Th(N_{\mathcal{Z}})(\mathcal{X})
. To see this, we begin by unwinding the definitions.
The Thom space of the normal bundle can be described as the cofibre,

N_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{X})\setminus 0\rightarrow N_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow Th(N_{\mathcal{Z}})(\mathcal{X})

Futher, as
X\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
is smooth, we have a cartesian square,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{Z}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}

where
Z
is the pullback of
\mathcal{Z}
to
X
. As smooth-Nisnevich covers are preserved under pullback, taking the Čech Nerve gives an equivalence
\{N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i})\}_{\bullet}\simeq N_{\mathcal{Z}}(\mathcal{X})
. Taking Thom spaces objectwise for the Čech nerve
\{N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i})\}_{\bullet}
produces the simplicial object
\{Th(N_{Z_{i}}(X_{i}))\}_{\bullet}
. This object is equivalent to
Th(N_{\mathcal{Z}})(\mathcal{X})
by construction. Combining this with (
5.2
), we get that desired equivalence.
∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

then
D\simeq(B\oplus C)/f-g
. Applying this to the square we have
{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{X})\simeq{\rm Pic}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r}/f-g
where
f:{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r}\to{\rm Pic}({\mathbb{P}}^{1})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by
f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r})=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{r}
and
g:{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r}\to{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus r}
is given by
g(x_{1},\cdots,x_{r})=(n_{1}x_{1},\cdots,n_{r}x_{r})
. Define
{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{X})\to{\mathbb{Z}}
by sending
(a_{1},b_{1},\cdots b_{r})\mapsto m.a_{1}+m/n_{1}.b_{1}+\cdots+m/n_{r}.b_{r}
which is easily seen to be an isomorphism and rest of the result follows.
∎

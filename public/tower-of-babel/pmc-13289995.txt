Effectiveness of Antenatal Craft Class on Management of Minor Ailments during Pregnancy among Primigravidae at a Tertiary Care Teaching Hospital
Authors: Sampa Mondal, Himanshu Vyas, T Deviga, Pratibha Singh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Introduction:Pregnancy is a wonderful milestone in every female’s life associated with hormonal and physical changes to prepare women for motherhood. Most of the women report signs and symptoms of one or other minor ailments throughout the pregnancy. Although they are not dangerous, their presence distracts the woman from feeling of comfort and wellbeing.Aim:The study was conducted to assess the effectiveness of antenatal craft class on management of selected minor ailments during pregnancy among primigravidae.Method:A quasi-experimental study was conducted on primigravidae women visiting antenatal out-patient department at a tertiary care teaching hospital. A total of 60 primigravidae (30 in each group) with a gestational age between 28 and 36 weeks were selected by the nonprobability convenience sampling technique. Antenatal craft class was taken for the experimental group, whereas the control group received routine antenatal care. Data were collected by using the tools: visual analog scale for back pain and leg cramps, multidimensional assessment of fatigue scale for fatigue and self-structured heartburn severity, and frequency rating scale for heartburn. Descriptive and inferential statistics were used to analyze the data.Results:Significant improvement in the management of minor ailments such as back pain, leg cramps, fatigue, and heartburn (p = P < 0.001) was observed in the experimental group as compared to the control group. Intake of spicy food (p = P < 0.001 1) and intake of fast food (P = 0.005) were significantly associated with severity of heartburn; place of residence (P = 0.04) and intake of fast food (P = 0.03) were significantly associated with frequency of heartburn in the experimental group. However, in the control group, exercise was associated with level of back pain (P = 0.03); type of diet (P = 0.03) and intake of fast food (P = 0.01) were associated with severity of heartburn.Conclusion:Antenatal craft class was effective in managing the minor ailments like back pain, leg cramps, heartburn, and fatigue during pregnancy. It suggests that antenatal craft class acts as a primary health care to reduce pregnancy-induced minor discomforts and to improve their quality of life at the community level.

INTRODUCTION

The anatomical, physiological, and biochemical adaptations to pregnancy are profound. These changes occur in response to physiological stimuli provided by the fetus and placenta. The changes such as nausea, vomiting, fatigue, increased micturition, back pain, ligament pain, edema, irritability, heartburn, dyspnea, constipation, skin changes, headache, dizziness, bleeding gums, anxiety, and varicose veins may be unpleasant as well as worrying, but they are rarely a cause for alarm as most of these changes are usually normal. Therefore, they are called minor disorders or ailments of pregnancy.[12] Some of the common minor ailments of pregnancy during the third trimester (29–40 weeks) include back pain, heartburn, constipation, leg cramps, varicosities, sleep disturbance, haemorrhoids, insomnia, and depression.[2]

In India, 50 million women get affected with minor disorders of pregnancy.[3] Studies reported 94% of pregnant women experienced fatigue,[4] 64.6% suffered from calf muscle cramps, 37.1% reported foot pain,[5] 70.7% reported low back pain,[6] and 76% of women had heartburn[7] during the third trimester of pregnancy.

Agampodi S found that minor ailments affecting day-to-day life have a major burden on the pregnancy period.[8] For example, pregnant women with musculoskeletal discomforts reported average and poor quality of life with sleep disturbance and severe pain, and they also hinder performance of daily activities.[910] Health-related quality of life (physical problems and social functioning) was affected in mothers with complaint of heartburn.[7] Also, those experiencing fatigue complain of energy deficiency, mental exhaustion, poor muscle endurance, delayed recovery after physical exertion, and nonrestorative sleep.[11]

Various research studies stated that the majority of antenatal mothers had inadequate knowledge and inadequate practice regarding minor ailments and its management.[341213] Patel Neha et al.[14] found that a planned teaching program was an effective strategy in bringing about changes in knowledge of minor disorder of pregnancy and its management among primigravida women.

Antenatal craft classes are also called Birth and Parenting or Parent-craft classes. Antenatal classes are individual or group programs conducted by doctors or midwives and consist of multiple sessions including various management techniques, such as lamez classes, yoga, calm birth, and active birth to promote a smooth labor, birth, and early parenthood. Here, nurse-midwives play an important role to provide information about antenatal checkup, self-care, minor ailments and its management, labor process, birthing techniques and position, pain relief measures, newborn care, breastfeeding, and prevention of complications and provide emotional support to the mother, couple, and family. Therefore, it makes the mother more confident toward her pregnancy outcome as well as helps to manage her minor problems easily.[1516] Structured prenatal education programs or classes are prevalent in developed nations, although they are less common in India.

Research on the minor ailments of pregnancy from the western part of Rajasthan remains quite limited, and there is scarcity of data.[2] Thus, the researcher aimed to conduct this study to organize antenatal craft classes for primigravidae to manage back pain, leg cramps, heart burn, and fatigue.

The objectives of the study were to assess and compare the management of minor ailments during pregnancy among primigravidae.

MATERIALS AND METHOD

Research approach and design

A quantitative research approach and nonequivalent control group pretest post-test design were used.

Sample and sampling technique

Primigravidae women with a gestational age between 28 and 36 weeks seeking antenatal care at Antenatal OPD were selected by the convenience sampling technique. The sample size was 60 (30 in each experimental and control group), calculated through comparison of two means formula in the experimental and control groups considering (1.96), (0.842), S2(pooled standard deviation = 2.729), and μd2 (effect size = 1.55). The mean and standard deviation of back pain in a similar study were[17]: Control group: 48.8 ± 1.1 and Experimental group: 45.4 ± 3.7

According to this formula, the sample size was 28. So, a total of 60 samples (30 in each experimental and control group) were taken for the present study to make up for any possible attrition during the study. No attrition rate was observed.

Inclusion criteria

Primigravida between gestational ages of 29 and 36 weeks having all four minor ailments such as back pain, leg cramps, fatigue, and heartburn, willing to participate with follow-up, and able to read Hindi/English.

Exclusion criteria

High-risk primigravidae (medical, surgical, obstetrical complications) and high-risk score ≥ 4 according to modified Coopland’s high-risk pregnancy scoring rate.

Intervention protocol

Antenatal craft class was conducted at Antenatal OPD for the experimental group. As per sample availability, the individual or group session was taken for 45–60 minutes. The researcher started the session by establishing rapport and giving a brief introduction of minor ailments during pregnancy (common minor ailments and its causes) for five minutes. The researcher then discussed minor ailments with different management measures in this session. For back pain: antenatal exercises (pelvic tilt, wall squats, rolling, cat camel pose, seated site bend), back massage with heat application, and appropriate body mechanics; for leg cramps: foot exercise (ankle pump and rotation, calf-stretch, flexor stretch, butterfly and half butterfly pose) with massaging and lifestyle modification (diet and right posture); for fatigue: diet modification, rest, exercise, and relaxation techniques (sukhasana, modified bhastrika pranayama, leg extension, full shoulder rotation, and tadasana); for heartburn: Breathing exercise (abdominal breathing) and lifestyle modification. Five to ten minutes are used to discussing each management strategy for minor ailments, followed by ten minutes of exercise video presentation. After that, there was a last five minutes for discussion and summarization. Lecture, discussion, and demonstration were used as a method of teaching. AV-aids used for this class were information booklet (it contains information about management of minor ailments during pregnancy) and video (antenatal exercises for management of minor ailments). For the control group, primigravidae received routine antenatal care.

Data collection tools and procedure

At first, primigravidae were screened for the presence of minor ailments of pregnancy using a self-structured screening checklist. After screening of primigravidae, personal information was obtained using personal variable data sheets in the control group and experimental group by using interview techniques. Then, during pretest, participants were assessed for back pain, leg cramps, fatigue, and heartburn.

Visual analog scale (VAS), a prevalidated standardized tool, was used to assess the level of back pain and intensity of leg cramps.

Multidimensional assessment of fatigue scale is a reliable and valid questionnaire (the Cronbach’s alpha is 0.93)[18] and was used to assess the level of fatigue experienced by primigravidae.

A self-structured heartburn severity and frequency rating scale was used to assess the severity and frequency of heartburn. It consists of five items related to symptoms of heartburn (burning feeling or pain behind your breast bone/center of upper stomach/throat, feeling full/heavy/bloated, burping/belching, an acid taste in your mouth, and difficulty in getting a good sleep) over the past 7 days. For severity, the symptoms were rated as no symptoms to very strong; the score ranges from 0 to 3. For frequency, symptoms were rated as absent to presence of more than 4 days a week; the score ranges from 0 to 3. Severity of heartburn score was interpreted as: mild heartburn (0–5), moderate heartburn (6–10), and severe heartburn (11–15). Frequency of heartburn score was interpreted as low frequency (0–7) and high frequency (8–15). The reliability of the self-structured heartburn severity and frequency rating scale was found to be 0.96 and 0.95, respectively, by the test–retest method, which is highly reliable, and the tool was found to be valid as the scale content validity index (S-CVI) value was 1. The self-report method was used to assess the back pain, leg cramps, fatigue, and heartburn.

After assessing the primigravidae for minor ailments, antenatal craft class was organized for the experimental group. Telephonically, confirmation and reinforcement were done by the researcher on every alternative day for ensuring the compliance of interventions and the exercise compliance sheet was filled by the participants at home on a daily basis. After 1 week, post-test was conducted in both the control and experimental groups by face-to-face interview/telephonically.

Ethical consideration

After explaining the purpose of the study, consent was obtained from participants who fulfilled the inclusion criteria of the study. Ethical approval was taken from the Institutional Ethics Committee. Certificate reference no-AIIMS/IEC/2022/4062: Dated: 16/09/2022.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive statistics like mean, frequency, percentage, and standard deviation were used to describe sample characteristics and minor ailments (back pain, leg cramps, fatigue, and heartburn). Inferential statistics: Mann–Whitney U test was used to compare the management of minor ailments during pregnancy among the experimental and control groups as the data were not normally distributed. Mann–Whitney U and Kruskal–Wallis tests were used to determine the association of minor ailments with selected personal variables in both the experimental and control groups. The level of significance is set as P ≤ 0.05.

RESULTS

Sample characteristics

The majority of the participants in the experimental and control groups were in the age group of 18–26 years (86.7% vs 73.3%). About 70% in the experimental group and 56.7% in the control group had a gestational age between 29 and 32 weeks. No statistically significant difference was observed in the sample characteristics of the primigravidae in the experiment and control groups [Table 1].

DISCUSSION

The current study presented that the majority of the participants, 86.7% in the experimental group and 73.3% in the control group, belonged to the age group of 18–26 years, which was similar to a study completed by Sajitha AT et al.,[19] who found that 40% of antenatal mothers in the control group and 56.75% in the experimental group were in the age group between 18 and 25 years. In this study, 70% of primigravidae in the experimental group and 56.7% of primigravidae in the control group had a gestational age between 29 and 32 weeks. Similarly, Agampodi SB et al.[8] found that minor ailments during pregnancy were more prevalent during 29–36 weeks of gestation. In the present study, 53.3% of primigravidae were doing exercise and 46.7% of primigravidae were not doing any exercise in the experimental group. In contrast to these findings, Kesikburun S et al.[6] reported that only 25 participants (13.6%) had undertaken a regular exercise program during pregnancy, whereas 57% had not undertaken any exercise program.

In this study, antenatal craft class was found to be effective in managing the minor ailments in the experimental group. There are several studies that support our findings; for example, Sajitha AT et al.[19] found that prenatal education regarding practice of antenatal exercises significantly reduced the post-test minor ailments score, and it helped the mother to be healthy and free from minor ailments. Vilasrao HL et al.[1] also found that structured teaching programs on knowledge regarding selected antenatal exercises in terms of relieving minor disorders of pregnancy among primi pregnant mothers were effective.

In the current study, outcomes showed that there was significant improvement (p = P < 0.001) in post-test back pain score in the experimental group after antenatal exercises and back massage with heat application and following appropriate body mechanics. These findings are supported by the study, conducted by Chandrasekharan B et al.,[17] in which the participants of the study group reported decreased back pain after back-stretch exercise. Also, a similar study reported by Kluge Jet et al.[20] showed that the low back pain of the experimental group was decreased (P = 0.01) after the exercise program, back care advice, and pamphlet distribution. Unlike the present study results, Haakstad et al.[21] did not find any significance difference in pain level between the experimental and control groups (P = 0.51) and no negative outcome was observed on participants of the study after attending aerobic dance classes. Rakhecha G et al.[22] reported that none of the personal variables was found to be significantly associated with the level of back pain in the experimental and control groups. However, in the present study, level of back pain was found to be significantly associated with exercise (P = 0.03).

In the present study, significant improvement (p = P < 0.001) was found in the intensity of leg cramps among primigravidae in the experimental group after foot exercises and lifestyle modifications. This observation is consistent with findings like Nabil Ramadan E et al.[23] reporting that lifestyle intervention was effective in improving pregnant women’s lifestyle, through improving their knowledge and decreasing the severity and frequency of leg cramps (P < 0.001). Similarly, Sarkar PK et al.[24] found that intervention packages like booklet, exercise demonstration, dietary advice, mobile-based feedback, and postural advice showed significant improvement in low back pain, leg cramps, coccyx pain, and sacroiliac joint pain.

The antenatal craft class includes different types of exercise and relaxation techniques which help the mother to manage fatigue during pregnancy. Significant improvement was observed in the level of fatigue among primigravidae in the experimental group (p = P < 0.001). Several studies supported these findings. O’Connor PJ et al.[25] identified that low to moderate resistance exercise was safe and effective for increasing strength and improving fatigue. Mahmoudirad G et al.[26] reported that Benson’s relaxation response significantly reduced the mean score of fatigue in the intervention group than in the control group (P = 0.001). Also, Kartilah T et al.[27] found that progressive muscle relaxation exercises significantly decrease the fatigue score (P = 0.016).

Heartburn during pregnancy might not be accepted as an innocent and temporary condition. Risk of gastro-esophageal reflux disease was increased by the presence of heartburn during pregnancy as reported by Bor Serhat et al.[28] In the present study, diet and lifestyle modification with breathing exercise have been found to be effective in managing heartburn during pregnancy (p = P < 0.001). Cochrane systemic review of nine randomized control trials compared various interventions in relieving heartburn during pregnancy and showed that the interventions include advice on diet, lifestyle modifications, and medications.[29]

Limitations

Post-test data collection was done after 1 week for the participants by verbal confirmation about the management measures, self-reporting methods, and telephonically for those unable to visit the hospital. In addition, the use of a convenience sampling technique for participant selection further restricts the external validity of the results. Research findings cannot be generalized to a larger population because of the small sample size.

CONCLUSION

The present study reveals that antenatal craft classes are important for the pregnant women to gain appropriate knowledge about the minor ailments and are effective in their management during pregnancy. These findings suggest that provision of antenatal craft class on a regular basis at the periphery level may help to manage minor discomforts and prevent further complications, and also, it can improve their quality of life during pregnancy.

Recommendations

Future research is recommended using true experimental designs with larger sample sizes across multiple settings to enhance generalizability. A qualitative study can be done on quality of life with minor ailments during pregnancy. A comparative study can be done between primigravidae and the multigravida mother.

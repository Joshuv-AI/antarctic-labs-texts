Impact of varied feeding protocols on gastrointestinal function recovery in the early postoperative period following repeat cesarean section: a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Xiao-Li Hu, Yu-Peng Wang, Lu-Ping Wu, Xiao-Feng Shi, Li-Hua Yu, Ai-Hua Zhang, Xiao-Xiao Zhu, Xiao-Feng Weng
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundWith the adjustment of China’s birth policy, the repeat cesarean sections raises concerns about potential gastrointestinal mechanical injuries and disturbances in gastrointestinal neurohormonal regulation, affecting the postoperative recovery of gastrointestinal function. These affect maternal nutritional status, prolong recovery, extend hospitalization, increase medical costs, and lead to neonatal care challenges.This study is to examine the effects of different postoperative feeding protocols on the restoration of gastrointestinal function in women undergoing repeat cesarean section.MethodsA total of 103 women were randomly assigned to three groups: experimental group A (n = 35), experimental group B (n = 34), and a control group (n = 34). In group A, women without choking symptoms received a clear fluid diet postoperatively, followed by a pureed diet after 6 h, and a regular soft diet after passing flatus. In group B, women chewed gum postoperatively, followed by a clear fluid diet after 2 h, a pureed diet after 6 h, and a regular soft diet after passing flatus. The control group received a clear fluid diet, a pureed diet after passing flatus, and a regular soft diet after defecation. Outcomes measured included recovery of gastrointestinal function, blood biochemical markers, comfort level, exclusive breastfeeding rate, and newborn weight loss.ResultsThe baseline maternal data across the three groups demonstrated no significant differences (P > 0.05). Both experimental groups A and B showed earlier recovery of postoperative gastrointestinal function compared to the control group, though there was no significant difference between the two experimental groups (P > 0.05). Additionally, postoperative comfort levels were significantly higher in both experimental groups than in the control group (P < 0.05).ConclusionsEarly feeding is safe and effective for women who underwent repeat cesarean sections.It has been shown to accelerate gastrointestinal recovery, significantly improve maternal comfort and satisfaction.In cases where immediate postoperative oral intake is not feasible, chewing gum demonstrates a comparable promotive effect, providing a viable substitute in facilitating recovery.Trial registrationThis trial has been prospectively registered at Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200065825) and the first registration date was 11/16/2022.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-024-06983-1.Keywords: Cesarean section, Early feeding, Enhanced recovery after surgery, Gastrointestinal function, Scarred uterus

Background

On May 31, 2021, China eviewed the Decision on Optimizing Reproductive Policy and Promoting Long-Term Balanced Development of Population, subsequently implementing the three-child policy as a proactive measure to address population aging. This policy shift is indicative of an anticipated escalation in the frequency of repeat cesarean sections in China, perpetuating the longstanding concern of the country maintaining the “highest cesarean section rate in Asia [1]. Cesarean section has negative impact on maternal health, among these complications, postoperative gastrointestinal function are particularly common [2]. The heightened prevalence of cesarean sections raises concerns about potential mechanical injuries from repeated surgeries, such as exposure of the abdominal intestinal tract and recurrent innervation blockages. Such complications may result in disturbances in gastrointestinal neurohormonal regulation, adversely affecting the postoperative recovery of gastrointestinal function. A delayed recovery of gastrointestinal function following repeat cesarean sections in women not only directly impacts maternal nutritional status and postoperative recovery but also extends hospitalization duration and increases medical expenditures. Furthermore, it influences maternal care practices for newborns, diminishes breastfeeding rates and contributes to various neonatal challenges. As medical technology and clinical practices continue to advance, the concept of Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS) has gained prominence within the surgical domain.ERAS is grounded in evidence-based medicine and seeks to optimize the perioperative clinical pathway through interdisciplinary collaboration among anesthesia, nursing, and nutrition. Its objective is to alleviate the physiological and psychological stresses associated with surgery, shorten hospital stay, and facilitate postoperative recovery among patients [3]. Among the various preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative optimization measures embedded in ERAS, dietary management in the postoperative phase is crucial [4]. The 2019 Guidelines for postoperative care in cesarean delivery, as articulated in ERAS recommendations from the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology, advocate for the routine consumption of food within 2 hours following the procedure [5]. However, the optimal early time for oral feeding after CS is unclear, the guidelines do not specify the type of food to be ingested during the early postoperative period and do not delineate recommended diet beyond the initial 2 hours. The guidelines states the potential benefits of chewing gum in facilitating the recovery of gastrointestinal function, attributing a low risk to this practice (evidence level: low; recommendation level: weak). Yet, the adequacy of chewing gum as a substitute for early feeding to enhance postoperative recovery in women unable to tolerate abdominal surgery remains inconclusive, necessitating further empirical validation. Postpartum stay after cesarean section at hospital is steadily declining in the UK and other countries due to cost savings, it can even be shortened to 24 hours after surgery [6]. However, women will still be hospitalized postpartum for 3–4 days in China because they are worried about complications of surgery, such as infection and gastrointestinal complications.Therefore, if the type and time of early feeding after cesarean section can be clarified, the recovery of the gastrointestinal function can be promoted, the nutritional status and comfort will be improved, and the length of hospital stay and medical expense will be reduced. Given the distinctive nature of repeat cesarean sections of a large number of Chinese women, there is a lack of evidence concerning the safety and efficacy of early feeding for women undergoing such procedures. In this study, we attempt to fill this research gap to investigated the influence of diverse feeding regimens on the restoration of gastrointestinal function in women undergoing repeat cesarean section. The findings are presented below.

Participants and methods

Research participants

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Between August 2022 and January 2023, we conducted a research study at a tertiary-level obstetrics and gynecology hospital in Zhejiang Province. The study focused on 103 cases of women who underwent cesarean delivery and received combined spinal-epidural anesthesia. The selection process utilized a convenience sampling method. The inclusion criteria comprised of: ① Single fetus and full-term pregnancy for elective repeat cesarean section, and postoperative continuous analgesia with a self-controlled analgesic pump; ② Informed consent to participate in this study; ③ No language communication barriers. The exclusion criteria included: ① Intraoperative and postoperative bleeding exceeding 1,000 mL or unstable vital signs requiring resuscitation at any time; ② Severe pregnancy complications (e.g., eclampsia, preeclampsia, placenta previa); ③ Significant pregnancy complications (e.g., cardiac diseases, renal insufficiency, hematologic diseases); ④ Abnormal gastrointestinal function or a history of gastrointestinal surgery; ⑤ Fetal abnormalities detected during prenatal examination; ⑥ Additional sedative or analgesic medication during the surgery, and anesthesia level maintained above T6 after the surgery; ⑦ The presence of breast disorders or contraindications to breastfeeding; ⑧ Separation of the mother and baby after the operation. The dropout criteria comprised: ① Withdrawal from the study; ② Poor cooperation during the study. This study was reviewed and approved by the Hospital Ethics Committee (IRB-20210327-R).

Determination of sample size

The primary outcome indicator for this study is the time of the first postoperative flatus, and it was designed as a randomized controlled trial. The sample size determination was based on the pre-trial results (30 cases) using a bilateral α = 0.05, a confidence level of 90%, and accounting for a 20% dropout rate. Based on the calculations conducted with PASS 15 software, a minimum sample size of at least 22 cases in each group was deemed necessary. The study, having included a total of 105 cases, met the minimum sample size requirement.

Randomization and blinding

In this study, a total of 105 antepartum women meeting the inclusion and exclusion criteria were subjected to random allocation into three groups: experimental group A, experimental group B, and a control group, using a completely randomized grouping method. With the use of Excel 2017 software, 110 random numbers ranging from 1 to 3 were generated, subsequently assigned to the participating women in childbirth who volunteered for the study, numbered 1 to 110 based on the timing of their elective surgery prescriptions. Specifically, random number “1” was designated as experimental group A, random number “2” as experimental group B, and random number “3” as the control group. Following the signing of informed consent forms, eligible participants were included into the preoperative group based on the established randomization table. Obstetric nurses possessing Good Clinical Practice (GCP) qualifications implemented the relevant postoperative dietary interventions for each case. The intervention in this study focused on postoperative dietary management. Notably, the obstetric nurses and the participants were not blinded, while the researcher responsible for data collection was blinded.

Intervention methods

The establishment of the ERAS Team

A team led by obstetric nurses, including obstetricians, anesthesiologists, operating room nurses, and neonatologists, was organized to implement ERAS. All members of the study team underwent collective training in ERAS methodologies.

Interventions

On the day prior to the surgery, researchers introduced themselves to the study participants and obtained signed informed consent forms. Regarding preoperative bowel preparation, all study participants received standardized education from obstetric nurses one day prior to the surgery. The preoperative bowel preparation, aligned with the principles of ERAS, entailed a fasting period of 6 h before the surgery, refraining from the intake of liquids for 2 h before the surgery, and allowing the intake of warm water, juice, and other clear fluids 2 to 6 h before the surgery, with a total intake limited to 300 ml. Prohibited items included milk, soy milk, and other flatus-inducing fluids. Furthermore, the consumption of flatus-inducing foods such as milk, soybeans, and others consumables were prohibited until the passage of flatus post-surgery. Concerning postoperative dietary interventions, the following protocols were implemented: Experimental Group A: Commencement of clear fluid intake upon waking without choking, transitioning to pureed diet intake after 6 h, and introduction of a regular soft diet after passing flatus. Experimental Group B: Chewing gum once per hour (for 10 to 15 min each time) starting after waking without choking, initiation of clear fluid intake after 2 h, progression to pureed diet after 6 h, and given regular soft diet after passing flatus. Control group: Initiation of clear fluid intake after waking without choking, pureed diet after passing flatus, and a regular soft diet after defecation.The common feature of three group was that they could take fluids or gum immediately after surgery, The biggest difference between experimental groups and control group was taking pureed diet and regular soft diet earlier.All three groups of study participants underwent management by the same healthcare team for elective cesarean section, which based on ERAS guidelines principles [4]. The only distinction among them was the postoperative dietary interventions.

Evaluation criteria

Postoperative recovery of gastrointestinal function

This study encompasses various parameters to assess postoperative recovery, including the recovery time of postoperative bowel sounds, the timing of the first flatus and defecation, occurrences of nausea and vomiting within the 48-hour postoperative period, abdominal distension, and the presence or absence of postoperative intestinal obstruction.

(1) The recovery time for postoperative bowel sounds involved auscultation of the maternal left upper abdomen, left lower abdomen, right upper abdomen, and right lower abdomen conducted every 4 h. Each auscultation session lasted for 1 min at each location. Bowel sounds recovery is defined as the presence of bowel sounds in at least 2 of the 4 locations occurring at least 3 times within 1 min. (2) Timing of the first flatus and defecation: Preoperative instructions were provided to the antepartum women and her family to document the timing of the first flatus and defecation. Recording began from the conclusion of the operation to ensure accuracy. (3) Nausea, vomiting, and abdominal distension: Medical staff assessed nausea, vomiting, and abdominal distension within 48 h after oral intake, considering the subjective feelings of the puerpera for a comprehensive judgment. In this study, nausea is defined as a condition characterized by regurgitation and/or an urgent need to vomit. Vomiting is defined as a reflex action expelling the contents of the stomach through the mouth. Abdominal distension is classified as follows: Mild: Flat abdomen with slight pain under pressure and the presence of abdominal breathing. Moderate: Distended abdomen with mild pain under pressure and diminished abdominal breathing. Severe: Obvious pressure and pain in the distended abdomen with diminished or absent abdominal breathing [7]. (4) Postoperative Intestinal Obstruction: Criteria for postoperative intestinal obstruction include abdominal pain, abdominal distension, nausea, vomiting, and cessation of flatus and defecation before hospital discharge. Additionally, abdominal plain film revealing distended and dilated intestinal loops with gas-liquid planes [8].

Biochemical indicators

Measurements of total protein, albumin, serum gastrin, and serum potassium ion levels were conducted intravenously both preoperatively and on the first day postoperatively.

Comfort level

The evaluation of maternal postoperative comfort relied on the simplified Chinese version of the General Comfort Questionnaire (GCQ), initially developed by Kolcaba, a prominent expert in comfort care in the United States [9]. This instrument was subsequently adapted into Chinese by Zhu et al. [10] The questionnaire encompasses four dimensions: physiological, psycho-spiritual, environmental, and socio-cultural, encompassing a total of 28 items. Responses are measured on a 4-point Likert scale, ranging from 1 to 4, where “strongly disagree” corresponds to 1 and “strongly agree” corresponds to 4. Reverse scoring is applied to certain entries, and higher scores indicate a heightened level of comfort. The Cronbach’s coefficient for the Chinese version of the GCQ was calculated to be 0.920. It was filled in by the mother at day 2 postoperatively.

Exclusive breastfeeding rate and newborn weight loss percentage 3 days post surgery

The feeding regimens for the puerperas at 3 days postoperatively were documented, and the rate of exclusive breastfeeding was calculated. Additionally, the birth weight of the newborns and their weight at 3 days postoperatively were recorded, and the percentage of weight loss in the newborns was calculated.

Statistical methods

The count data are presented as frequency and percentage, while measurement data conforming to a normal distribution are expressed as mean ± standard deviation. One-way ANOVA was used to compare the measurement data, and for categorical data comparison, the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact probability method was used. Data was analyzed using SPSS 26.0 software, and a significance test was performed with a significance level set at α = 0.05.

Results

Comparison of general data of the study population

A total of 105 participants were initially screened for this study, with 2 cases dropping out during the course, resulting in a dropout rate of 1.90%. The reasons for dropout included one case in experimental group B, where vomiting occurred after consuming liquid food, she refused to take any food again and asked to withdraw from the trial. In the control group, one case dropped out due to the cancellation of surgery and subsequent discharge from the hospital. Consequently, a total of 103 cases were included in the analyzed sample: 35 cases in experimental group A, 34 cases in experimental group B, and 34 cases in the control group. Analysis revealed no statistically significant differences (P > 0.05) in age, gestational weight gain, education level, and place of residence among the three groups, as indicated in Table 1.There were no surgical complications such as infection, peripheral organ damage or amniotic fluid embolism in any of the three groups.

However, the difference between experimental group A and experimental group B was not statistically significant (χ2 = 0.649, P = 0.723).

A comparison of the time of the first postoperative bowel movement among the three groups of women revealed a statistically significant difference (χ2 = 47.677, P < 0.001). Discussion

Cesarean section is one of the most prevalent surgical interventions globally, contributing significantly to the mitigation of maternal and child morbidity and mortality, thereby ensuring the safety of both puerperas and infants. However, subsequent pregnancies following a cesarean section not only perpetuate an ongoing rise in cesarean section rates but also entail a notable elevation in complications associated with cesarean sections involving a scarred uterus. This circumstance prolongs postpartum recovery, posing physiological, psychological, and economic burdens on puerperas and presenting a formidable challenge to obstetricians. Historically, a common practice involved fasting for 1 day before a repeat cesarean section due to concerns about intestinal adhesions, prohibiting the intake of any drink or food until after passing flatus. However, prolonged fasting may lead to malnutrition, dehydration, electrolyte imbalances, and disruption of intestinal micro-ecology, all of which can impact the postoperative recovery of the puerpera and infant care.Although ERAS guidelines for cesarean sections have been published, these previous studies have defined early oral feeding differently, some studies regard it 6 h [11, 12] or 8 h [13] as early, while guideline regard it as 2 h [5]. And ERAS guideline do not clearly indicate the type and optimal time of early postoperative feeding, so our aim is to determine whether different early feeding are safe and beneficial, which has never been studied in the large number of women with repeated cesarean section in China.

In this study, on the basis of early fluid intake in all three groups [5], experimental group A and B ( pureed diet intake after 6 h, regular diet after passing flatus) both showed the recovery time of postoperative bowel sound, the first exhaust time and the first defecation time were shorter than that of the control group ( pureed diet intake after first flatus, regular diet after defecation). Post-operative ileus is a common and troublesome issue following abdominal surgery, few effective therapies for it exist, most current preventative strategies are non-specific and act by limiting opiate use and avoiding fluid overload [14], therefore, our study also focused on whether early eating and drinking increased intestinal burden and adverse reactions, and the results showed that it did not, there was no significant differences were observed between the experimental groups and the control group concerning nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, or intestinal obstruction. This lack of significant difference may be attributed to the notion that the resumption of food intake does not hinge on the inevitability of flatus and defecation. Small bowel function typically recovers within 12 ~ 24 h postoperatively, allowing patients to tolerate oral feeding without experiencing abdominal pain, distension, or vomiting. These indicators collectively signify the restoration of gastrointestinal function [15]. Therefore, our study provides evidence that the two early feeding protocols following repeat cesarean delivery is both safe and effective. We did not observe an increase in gastrointestinal adverse events but also actively promotes the recovery of gastrointestinal function. This result is similar to a recent meta-analysis [16].

Chewing gum to simulate feeding is based on the pseudo-feeding principle. This practice induces a notable increase in salivary secretion and triggers sympathetic excitation, thereby fostering gastrointestinal peristalsis and expediting the recovery of gastrointestinal function through both mechanical and gustatory stimulation [17]. In the context of this study, experimental group B exhibited a swifter recovery time for gastrointestinal function when compared to the control group. Chewing gum was found to reduce the duration for the onset of the first bowel sound, the first flatus, and the first defecation post-cesarean section, aligning with outcomes reported in prior meta-analyses [18, 19]. Moreover, chewing gum has emerged as a safer and more cost-effective alternative to gastrointestinal stimulant drugs. It not only diminishes maternal thirst but also enhances oral hygiene and comfort. Significantly, the results of this study demonstrated that there is no noteworthy disparity in the recovery time of gastrointestinal function between experimental group B and experimental group A. This implies that chewing gum can effectively serve as an early substitute for oral intake in cases where puerperas, having undergone multiple cesarean sections, are either unwilling or unable to tolerate early feeding. This alternative approach similarly contributes to the early recovery of gastrointestinal function.

As advancements in surgical procedures continue, the evaluation of patient recovery should extend beyond the mere success or safety of the surgery, prioritizing the assessment of patient comfort throughout the entire perioperative period. Comfort, in this context, encompasses both physical and psychological dimensions. Pang et al. discovered that enhancing the physical aspects of the postpartum period has a more pronounced impact on overall comfort improvement [20]. Considering that cesarean section surgery necessitates pre-procedural fasting and abstaining from food and drink, permitting early postoperative eating can effectively alleviate thirst and hunger, thereby contributing to an enhanced sense of overall comfort for the puerpera. Given that cesarean section surgery does not directly involve the gastrointestinal tract and often involves local anesthesia, puerperas typically remain conscious in the postoperative period with minimal risk of complications such as vomiting, aspiration and choking. This sets the physiological groundwork for advocating early feeding [21]. The results of this study indicated that postoperative comfort levels among puerperas in both experimental groups A and B surpassed those in the control group. This notable difference was primarily attributed to the advantages associated with early feeding, including reduced maternal hunger and thirst, improved nutritional intake, facilitated early physical activity, heightened breast milk secretion, and enhanced infant care. Ultimately, these factors collectively contributed to an improved mood and overall comfort, aligning with the findings of other relevant studies [22].

Although not statistically significant, it is worth highlighting that the mean percent weight loss of neonate at postpartum day 3 in both experimental groups A and B (6.08% and 6.66%, respectively) were both lower than that of the control group (7.38%), suggesting that the initiation of a pureed diet as early as 6 h postpartum in women with repeat cesarean section may encourage breast milk secretion and early breastfeeding in the infant. The comparison of maternal postoperative biochemical indicators and the exclusive breastfeeding rate at 3 days postoperatively, among the three groups in this study did not reveal any statistically significant differences. Notably, the control group in this study diverged from previous approaches by employing early oral feeding instead of the conventional delayed feeding, aligning with the methodology of the experimental group. This distinctive approach appears to have contributed to an improvement in maternal nutritional status, resulting in no substantial differences among the three groups. It is important to acknowledge that the rate of exclusive breastfeeding is influenced by a myriad of factors, encompassing maternal nipple condition, cognitive level, breastfeeding knowledge, infant-related factors, family support levels, medical considerations, and economic aspects. These factors also impact the achievement of exclusive breastfeeding in the initial postpartum week. While early feeding positively impacts the nutritional status of puerperas, it constitutes just one of several crucial factors influencing exclusive breastfeeding and is not the sole determinant. Consequently, further research is required to comprehensively understand its specific effects on breastfeeding outcomes.

Conclusions

With the adjustment of China’s birth policy, the rate of repeat cesarean sections is inevitably increasing [23]. This study confirmed effectiveness and safety of the two early feeding protocols in women who underwent repeat cesarean section. It has demonstrated to promote the recovery of maternal gastrointestinal function, enhance comfort levels, and increase satisfaction, all without a discernible rise in adverse effects. It also has the potential to improve maternal nutrition and breastfeeding. In cases where early feeding is not well-tolerated, an alternative option for these women is early chewing gum instead of food intake, which has shown comparable efficacy in promoting gastrointestinal function recovery. The limitations of this study is a small sample size, in addition, many clinical influencing factors in the study affected the analysis of continuous variables, such as the time of post-operative activity, the time of extubation and pain degree of each women. Thirdly, further consensus work is required to develop a core outcome set for post-operative gastrointestinal recovery, and to establish a core measurement set for these outcomes.Therefore, well controlled randomized studies are still required to consider.

Delsarte duality on subspaces and applications to rank-metric codes and q q -matroids
Martino Borello 1,2, Olga Polverino 3, Ferdinando Zullo 3

Abstract
. We study the interplay between the lattice of 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} -subspaces and the lattice of 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -subspaces of an 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} -vector space. Introducing notions of weight and defect relative to an 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -subspace, we analyze the sequence of maximum non-zero defects. We establish a correspondence between subspaces of positive defect and their Delsarte duals, enabling explicit characterizations of the associated sequences of maximum non-zero defects. Our framework unifies several classes of subspaces studied in finite geometry and connects them to linear rank-metric codes by providing a new geometric interpretation of code duality. Building on these results, we characterize classes of rank-metric codes closed under duality, including MRD, near MRD, quasi-MRD, and a new family of ( 𝐧 , 𝐤 ) (\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k}) -MRD codes. Finally, we explore applications to q q -matroids, by studying the problem of 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} -representability for direct sums of uniform q q -matroids and describing their rank generating functions.



Keywords
: Delsarte dual; Decomposable subspace; Rank-metric code;
q
-matroid.

Introduction

Consider an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
of dimension
k
. Clearly,
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
can also be viewed as an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
mk
. Therefore, we can consider the lattice of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, denoted by
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
, and the lattice of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, denoted by
{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
.
This paper focuses on the interplay between the two lattices
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
.
Such an area of research is certainly not new, especially within finite geometry: the classical notion of subgeometry can be rephrased in this terminology. Indeed, a subgeometry can be defined as the set of points defined by vectors of an element
U\in{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
of dimension
k
, with the property that every element
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
intersects
U
in an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of dimension at most
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(T)
. This concept led to the theory of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets, which has evolved into a vibrant and widely studied area of research. Over the years,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear sets have found numerous applications in different contexts of finite geometry and coding theory, including the construction of blocking sets in projective spaces, the study of semifield flocks, translation ovoids, and, more recently, in the theory of rank-metric codes; see e.g.
[
44
,
35
]
. However, we will describe the context, results, and connections within the vectorial framework, as this appears to be the most suitable for the links with rank-metric codes and
q
-matroids that we will explore in this paper.

Two central notions will play a key role in our study: the weight and the defect of the elements in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with respect to a subspace
U
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
.
Intuitively, the weight of a subspace
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with respect to
U
measures how much of
U
is
concentrated
in
T
, whereas the defect of
T
measures how far
T\cap U
is from being a subgeometry of
T
. These two notions are closely related, as we shall see.
The main focus is the study of the weights and defects of subspaces in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
(of any dimension) with respect to
U
. This naturally leads us to define, for each fixed dimension, the maximum defect that elements of
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
can have with respect to
U
.
We prove that the sequence of maximum defects is non-decreasing with respect to the dimension. Therefore, it suffices to consider the restriction of this sequence to the strictly increasing subsequence, which we call the sequence of maximum non-zero defects.
After studying some basic properties of this sequence, we recall the notion of the Delsarte dual, introduced in
[
16
]
in connection with rank-metric codes.
We investigate the relationship between the sequences of maximum non-zero defects of a subspace
U\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
and its Delsarte dual
U^{d}
.
This connection involves a detailed analysis of the subspaces of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
having positive defect with respect to
U
and those of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}(\dim{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)-k,q^{m}))
having positive defect with respect to
U^{d}
.
In particular, we establish a one-to-one correspondence between the subspaces in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
having positive defect with respect to
U
and minimal with respect to their defect, and the subspaces in
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}(\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)-k,q^{m}))
having positive defect with respect to
U^{d}
and minimal with respect to their defect.
Thanks to this correspondence, we are able to determine the maximum non-zero defects of the Delsarte dual
U^{d}
in terms of the maximum non-zero defects of
U
.
As a consequence of these results, we analyze some known classes of subspaces and we study their Delsarte duals. In particular, we have considered:

•

Scattered subspaces with respect to
h
-subspaces, introduced in
[
37
,
52
,
16
,
9
]
;

•

1
-defect subspaces, including clubs (introduced in
[
21
,
22
]
and in
[
39
]
for larger dimensions);

•

Decomposable subspaces, introduced in
[
6
,
49
]
;

•

\mathbf{k}
-scattered subspaces with respect to the hyperplanes, introduced in
[
4
]
.

This study has an immediate impact on the theory of rank-metric codes. Indeed, in
[
47
]
(see also
[
2
,
51
]
) it is shown that to any linear rank-metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
of dimension
k
, one can associate an element of
\mathcal{L}_{q}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})
(and conversely) in such a way that the metric properties of the code can be studied directly from the associated subspace.
The main drawback of this connection is that there is no direct way to view the subspaces associated with a code and its dual within the same ambient space, which makes the study of duality-invariant properties from a geometric perspective difficult. Motivated by this limitation, we propose a new framework for describing the connection between linear rank-metric codes and subspaces in
\mathcal{L}_{q}({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k})
, which allows a unified approach to studying the systems associated with a code and its dual. The key point has been to view any subspace as the projection of a subgeometry; see
[
38
]
.
This framework allows us to prove that the operation of duality for linear rank-metric codes corresponds to the Delsarte duality of subspaces, previously proved only for a few cases (see
[
16
]
).
In this new framework we have also shown how we can obtain previously known results for rank-metric codes, such as the description of the support of subcodes, the geometric view of generalized weights and the Wei-type duality.
Moreover, relying on the proposed geometric framework, we study classes of codes which are closed under duality, including MRD codes (already known since
[
19
,
23
]
), near MRD codes (see
[
40
]
), quasi-MRD codes (see
[
40
,
17
]
) and
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD codes. The latter class has been introduced in this paper, and it can be defined as those direct sums of MRD codes whose codewords have weight lower bounded by the weights of an MRD code with the same parameters, unless these codewords belong to special projections. We prove that the geometric description of these codes corresponds to
\mathbf{k}
-scattered subspaces with respect to hyperplanes and we are able to prove that, in the case of two components, the weight distribution of such codes only depends on their parameters. We conclude the paper with an application to
q
-matroids.
A
q
-matroid is the
q
-analogue of a classical matroid, where the role of subsets of a finite set is replaced by subspaces of a finite-dimensional vector space over a finite field. They provide a natural framework for studying rank-metric codes, as they capture geometric and combinatorial properties of codes in a way analogous to how classical matroids capture properties of linear block codes; see
[
28
,
53
,
25
,
13
,
12
,
26
]
for some references. One way to construct a
q
-matroid is via the supports of the codewords of a linear rank-metric code in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
. A
q
-matroid obtained in this way is called
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable. A well-studied class of
q
-matroids is the class of uniform
q
-matroids, which correspond to the
q
-matroids associated with MRD codes. Recently, in
[
14
]
(see also
[
27
]
) the notion of the direct sum of
q
-matroids was introduced, and the question of whether the direct sum of uniform
q
-matroids is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-representable for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
was answered positively in
[
4
]
.
The problem that remains open is the determination of the smallest field over which these
q
-matroids are representable.
Using the connection between an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of the direct sum of uniform
q
-matroids and
\mathbf{k}
-scattered subspaces with respect to the hyperplanes established in
[
4
]
, we prove some new instances to this problem.
Finally, we also prove that rank generating function of the direct sum of uniform
q
-matroids only depends on the parameters of the
q
-matroid.

Organization of the paper

In Section 1, we investigate the combinatorics of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, focusing on the notions of weight and defect with respect to a given
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace. We define the sequence of maximum non-zero defects, study its basic properties, and introduce the Delsarte dual of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace, analysing the correspondence between the sequences of the maximum defects of a subspace and its dual. We also examine the action of this duality on several notable classes of subspaces.

In Section 2, we turn to rank-metric codes and introduce a new framework linking where to study subspaces associated with a rank-metric code and its dual within the same ambient space. This geometric viewpoint enables us to reinterpret known results on supports, generalized weights, and Wei-type duality, and to characterize families of codes closed under Delsarte duality.

In Section 3, we apply our results to
q
-matroids. Using the established connection between
k
-scattered subspaces and the representability of direct sums of uniform
q
-matroids, we prove new instances of representability and show that the rank generating function of such direct sums depends only on the parameters of the
q
-matroid.

1.
Combinatorics of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces in
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})

In this section, we study the behavior of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
with respect to its lattice of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces.
This study is carried out up to the action of the general linear group
\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
acting on the lattices
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
.
In this setting, as we will see later, in addition to the dimension of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces, the following integers serve as useful invariants.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-vector space of dimension
k
. Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
. For any
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
T
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
we define the
weight of

T
with respect to
U
as

w_{U}(T)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap T),

and the
defect of

T
with respect to
U
as

\varepsilon_{U}(T)=w_{U}(T)-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T).

We start by describing some properties, which are easily verified.

Property 1.2
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
and let
T\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
.

1.

If
\langle T\cap U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=T
, then
\varepsilon_{U}(T)\geq 0
.

2.

If
\varepsilon_{U}(T)\geq 0
, then
\varepsilon_{U}(T)=\varepsilon_{U}(T^{\prime})
where
T^{\prime}=\langle T\cap U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\subseteq T
.

3.

If
\Phi\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
then
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\Phi(U))
,
w_{U}(T)=w_{\Phi(U)}(\Phi(T))
and
\varepsilon_{U}(T)=\varepsilon_{\Phi(U)}(\Phi(T))
.

Also, if
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
, we have

(1)

w_{U}(T_{1}+T_{2})\geq w_{U}(T_{1})+w_{U}(T_{2})-w_{U}(T_{1}\cap T_{2}).

(2)

\varepsilon_{U}(T_{1}+T_{2})\geq\varepsilon_{U}(T_{1})+\varepsilon_{U}(T_{2})-\varepsilon_{U}(T_{1}\cap T_{2}).

Also, (
1
) and (
2
) are equalities if and only if
(T_{1}+T_{2})\cap U=(T_{1}\cap U)+(T_{2}\cap U)
.

It follows from
3.
of Property
1.2
that the dimension, the distributions of weights and defects of the elements of
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with respect to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
, are invariant with respect to the action of the group
\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
.
However, in some cases, the dimension of the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
is sufficient to uniquely identify
U
with respect to the action of
\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
.

Property 1.3
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that

(3)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U),

then the following holds:

1.

\varepsilon_{U}(T)\leq 0
for any
T
in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
;

2.

if
U^{\prime}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime})
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U^{\prime})
, then there exists
\Phi\in\hbox{{\rm GL}}(k,q^{m})
such that
\Phi(U)=U^{\prime}
;

3.

let
S
be any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
U
of dimension
h
, then there exists a unique
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace
S^{*}
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
of dimension
h
such that
S=U\cap S^{*}
.

Also, 1. is equivalent to (
3
), so that any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
that satisfies 1. must satisfy (
3
).

The
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces described in Property
1.3
will be called
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometries
, more precisely a
t
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometry of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
W
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
having dimension
t
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(\langle W\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(W)
.

Property
1.3
states that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometries are those
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces with respect to which there are no element in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with positive defect. Generally speaking, defects with respect to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace measure how far the subspace is from being a subgeometry. So, this motivates the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
. For any
r\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
, let

\varepsilon_{U}(r)=\max\{\varepsilon_{U}(T)\,:\,T\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))\,\mbox{and}\,\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T)=r\},

i.e.
\varepsilon_{U}(r)
is the maximum defect of the
r
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
with respect to
U
. Note that
\varepsilon_{U}(0)=0
and
\varepsilon_{U}(k)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)-k
.

Remark 1.5
.

Another way to characterize
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometries is via the maximum defects. Indeed,
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometry if and only if
\varepsilon_{U}(r)\leq 0
for any
r
. Also, if
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, then
U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometry if and only if
\varepsilon_{U}(r)=0
for any
r\in\{0,1,\dots,k\}
.

The maximum defects with respect to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace satisfy the following monotonicity property.

Property 1.6
.

Let
U
be in
\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
such that
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
. Then

(4)

0=\varepsilon_{U}(0)\leq\varepsilon_{U}(1)\leq\varepsilon_{U}(2)\leq\cdots\leq\varepsilon_{U}(k)=n-k.

Hence, if
\varepsilon_{U}(j)=0
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, then
\varepsilon_{U}(i)=0
for each
i\in\{1,2,\dots,j\}
.

Proof.

Since
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, we have
n\geq k\geq 1
and clearly
\varepsilon_{U}(k)=n-k\geq 0
and
\varepsilon_{U}(1)\geq 0
. Now, let
i\in\{1,2,\dots,k-1\}
and let
T
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T)=i
and
\varepsilon_{U}(T)=\varepsilon_{U}(i)
.
Since
i<k
and
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, there exists
u^{\prime}\in U\setminus T
. Hence,
T^{\prime}=T+\langle u^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
is an
(i+1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
and by (
2
) we have

\varepsilon_{U}(T^{\prime})\geq\varepsilon_{U}(T)+\varepsilon_{U}(\langle u^{\prime}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})\geq\varepsilon_{U}(i).

From which we get
\varepsilon_{U}(i)\leq\varepsilon_{U}(i+1)
.
∎

Due to the previous property, the behavior of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace with respect to the defects of the subspaces in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
can be directly expressed by those defect that are in strictly increasing order. This yields the following definitions.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
U
be in
\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
such that
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
. Let
s
be the number of distinct non-zero integers appearing in (
4
) and let

(5)

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<..<t_{s}\leq k

be such that

(6)

0<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{1})<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{2})<\cdots<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{s})=n-k,

and

(7)

\varepsilon_{U}(l)=\varepsilon_{U}(t_{i})\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,t_{i}\leq l<t_{i+1}.

The sequence of integers (
6
) will be called the
sequence of maximum non-zero defects
with respect to
U
and the integer
s\in\{1,\dots k\}
will be called the
length
of the sequence of maximum non-zero defects.

In the following, the integers
t_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{U}(t_{s-1})
(the last non-trivial defect) will play a crucial role in the duality process described in the next section. We will refer to
\varepsilon_{U}(t_{s-1})
as the
maximum non-trivial defect
.

Remark 1.8
.

With the notation of the above definition, note that if
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{U}(t_{i})
for some of the integers
t_{i}\in\{1,\dots,k\}
appearing in (
5
), then from (
7
)

t_{i}=\min\{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T)\,:\,\varepsilon_{U}(T)=\varepsilon\}

and if
T
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of dimension
t_{i}
such that
\varepsilon_{U}(T)=\varepsilon
, then
T
is minimal with respect to its defect, i.e. for every
T^{\prime}<_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}T
we have that
\varepsilon_{U}(T^{\prime})<\varepsilon
.

In the following section, subspaces that are minimal with respect to their defect will play a key role in describing the behavior of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace; therefore, it is necessary to provide a formal definition of such subspaces.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
U
be in
\mathcal{L}_{q}(\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m}))
and let
T
an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
. We say that
T
is
minimal
with respect to its defect (with respect to
U
) if
T^{\prime}<_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}T
implies
\varepsilon_{U}(T^{\prime})<\varepsilon_{U}(T)
. Note that from the minimality of
T
we also get
\langle T\cap U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=T
and hence, by
1.
of Proposition
1.2
, if
T\neq\{\underline{0}\}
, we have
\varepsilon_{U}(T)>0
.

Note that the maximum defect
n-k
is certainly reached by the entire ambient space
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
. However, it could happen that
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
is not the subspace of minimum dimension with defect
n-k
.
In the next property, we prove that this is equivalent to the existence of a hyperplane of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
of weight
n-1
with respect to
U
.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
, with
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U)=n
, such that
\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
and let

(8)

0<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{1})<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{2})<\cdots<\varepsilon_{U}(t_{s})=n-k,

the sequence of non-zero maximum defects with respect to
U
as defined in Definition
1.7
.
We have
t_{s}<k
if and only if there exists a hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that
w_{U}(H)=n-1
.

Proof.

If there exists a hyperplane
H
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that
w_{U}(H)=n-1
, then
\varepsilon_{U}(H)=n-k
and hence by Remark
1.8

t_{s}=\min\{\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T)\,:\,\varepsilon_{U}(T)=n-k\}\leq k-1.

On the other hand, let
t_{s}=k-\rho
, for some
\rho>0
, and let
T
be a subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(T)=t_{s}
and
\varepsilon_{U}(T)=n-k
. As a consequence,
w_{U}(T)=n-\rho<n
. If
\rho=1
the assertion is proved. If
\rho>1
, let
\overline{u}_{1}\in U\setminus T
and let
T_{1}=T+\langle\overline{u}_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
. The subspace
T_{1}
is a
(k-\rho+1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspace such that
w_{U}(T_{1})\geq w_{U}(T)+1=n-\rho+1
and hence
\varepsilon_{U}(T_{1})\geq n-k
. Since
n-k
is the maximum defect,
we get

\varepsilon_{U}(T_{1})=n-k\,\,\,\mbox{and}\,\,\,\,w_{U}(T_{1})=n-\rho+1.

If
\rho=2
, the assertion is proved since
T_{1}
is an hyperplane. Otherwise, proceeding inductively after
\rho-1
steps we get a subspace
T_{\rho-1}
of dimension
k-1
and weight
n-1
, proving the assertion.
∎

Remark 1.11
.

Let
U
be in

Proposition 1.23

Theorem 1.26

1.3.

Our aim is now to study how the Delsarte duality behaves on an

In this section we will assume that

Clearly, the first case to consider is the case in which such a sequence presents only one element, that is

\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
such that
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(H\cap U)=\frac{km}{h+1}-m+h
, therefore we have
\varepsilon_{U}(k-1)=\frac{mk}{h+1}-m+h-k+1
, i.e.
\varepsilon_{U}(t_{s-1})=\frac{mk}{h+1}-m+h-k+1
, and applying Theorem
1.30
we obtain the assertion.
∎

As already observed, the Delsarte duality preserves the length of the sequence of maximum non-zero defects.
Let us focus on those subspaces presenting only one non-trivial element in such a sequence. We will call these subspaces
1
-
defect
subspaces.

Proposition 1.32
.

Let
U
be an
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-vector space
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
which is a
1
-defect subspace, i.e. its the sequence of maximum non-zero defects is

0<\varepsilon_{U}(\ell)<\varepsilon_{U}(k)=n-k,

for some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
.
Then the sequence of maximum non-zero defects of an its Delsarte dual
U^{d}
is

0<\varepsilon_{U^{d}}(n-k-\varepsilon_{U}(\ell))=k-\ell<\varepsilon_{U^{d}}(n-k)=k.

Proof.

The proof follows from the definition of defect and again by Theorem
1.28
.
∎

The
club
gives a classical example of
1
-defect subspace. Let
i\in\{2,\ldots,m\}
, a subspace
U
of
\mathbb{V}(2,q^{m})
is called an
i
-
club
if there exists a one-dimensional subspace
T\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}(2,q^{m}))
such that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap T)=i,

and all the other one-dimensional subspaces
T^{\prime}
are such that

\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap T^{\prime})\leq 1.

As a consequence of Proposition
1.32
, we obtain that the Delsarte dual
U^{d}
of an
i
-club
U
is a
1
-defect subspace with
|\mathcal{E}_{U^{d}}(\mathbb{V}(n-2,q^{m}))|=1
.

Proposition 1.33
.

Let
U
be an
n
-dimensional (
n>3
)
i
-club of
\mathbb{V}(2,q^{m})
. We have that its sequence of maximum non-zero defect is

0<\varepsilon_{U}(1)=i-1<\varepsilon_{U}(2)=n-2,

and
\mathcal{E}_{U}(\mathbb{V}(2,q^{m}))=\{T\}
.
In particular, an
i
-club is a
1
-defect subspace.
Moreover, the sequence of maximum non-zero defect of
U^{d}
is

0<\varepsilon_{U^{d}}(n-1-i)=1<\varepsilon_{U^{d}}(n-2)=2,

and
\mathcal{E}_{U^{d}}(\mathbb{V}(n-2,q^{m}))=\{T^{d}\}
.

Example 1.34
.

Applying the previous result to a
2
-club
U
in
\mathbb{V}(2,q^{m})
of dimension
m\geq 5
, we get that in
\mathbb{V}(m-2,q^{m})
there exists only one hyperplane with defect one with respect to
U^{d}
, i.e. having weight
m-2
.
This was proven in
[
52
, Remark 6.14]
for
m=5
using MacWilliams identities, whereas our approach managed to avoid their use.

Finally, we show that the Delsarte dual of a decomposable subspace is still a decomposable subspace. Then we use this result to prove that the
\mathbf{k}
-scattered property is closed under Delsarte duality.

Theorem 1.35
.

Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
,
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
with
k=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
,
n=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
and
k_{i}<n_{i}
for
i\in[t]
. Consider a decomposable
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
U
of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
of dimension
n
of type
(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{n})
.
We have that a Delsarte dual
U^{d}
of
U
is a decomposable subspace in
\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
of type
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{n})
.

Proof.

Consider the representation of
U
in the quotient model, for which we have
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})=\frac{\mathbb{V}(n,q^{m})}{\Gamma}
and
U=\frac{W+\Gamma}{\Gamma}
, where
W
is a
n
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subgeometry of
\mathbb{V}(n,q^{m})
. Since
U
is decomposable of type
(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{n})
, we may write

U=U_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus U_{t}=\frac{S_{1}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{S_{t}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}

and

\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})=F_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus F_{t}=\langle U_{1}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\langle U_{t}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\frac{S_{1}^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{S_{t}^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma},

where
S_{i}
is an
n_{i}
-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
W
.
Also, since
W\cap\Gamma=\{\underline{0}\}
, we have

W=S_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{t}.

Then it is easy to check that if
H_{i}=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}S_{j}
, then

(19)

W=\bar{S}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\bar{S}_{t}

where
\bar{S}_{i}=(H_{i})^{\perp^{\prime}}=\cap_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}S_{j}^{\perp^{\prime}}
and
\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\bar{S}_{i})=n_{i}
.
Also, if
I=\{i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{s}\}\subseteq[t]
, since
\sum_{j=1}^{s}S_{i_{j}}=\cap_{i\in[t]\setminus I}H_{i}
, we have

(20)

\cap_{j=1}^{s}S_{i_{j}}^{\perp^{\prime}}=\sum_{i\in[t]\setminus I}^{s}\bar{S}_{i}.

Let
F^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}F_{j}
for
i\in[t]
and note that

F^{\prime}_{i}=\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}\frac{S_{j}^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}=\frac{(\sum_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}S_{j})^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}=\frac{H_{i}^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}.

Clearly, the subspaces
F^{\prime}_{i}
have positive defect and, by
3.
of Proposition
1.15
, they are minimal with respect to their defects. Hence, by Theorems
1.25
and
1.26
we have that

(F_{i}^{\prime})^{d}=\frac{(H_{i}^{\perp^{\prime}})^{*}+\Gamma^{\perp}}{\Gamma^{\perp}}=\frac{\bar{S}_{i}^{*}+\Gamma^{\perp}}{\Gamma^{\perp}},

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}((F_{i}^{\prime})^{d})=n-k-\varepsilon_{U}(F_{i}^{\prime})=n_{i}-k_{i},

w_{U^{d}}((F_{i}^{\prime})^{d})=n-w_{U}(F_{i}^{\prime})=n_{i},

\varepsilon_{U^{d}}((F_{i}^{\prime})^{d})=k-\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(F_{i}^{\prime})=k_{i},

and

((F_{i}^{\prime})^{d})^{d}=F_{i}^{\prime}.

Also,
(F_{i}^{\prime})^{d}
is minimal with respect to its defect. By Equation (
19
) we get

(21)

U^{d}=\frac{W+\Gamma^{\perp}}{\Gamma^{\perp}}=\frac{\bar{S}_{1}+\Gamma^{\perp}}{\Gamma^{\perp}}\oplus\cdots\oplus\frac{\bar{S}_{t}+\Gamma^{\perp}}{\Gamma^{\perp}}

and hence, since
(U^{d})^{*}=\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
, we have

\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})=(F_{1}^{\prime})^{d}+\cdots+(F_{t}^{\prime})^{d}.

and now comparing the dimensions we get

(22)

\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})=(F_{1}^{\prime})^{d}\oplus\cdots\oplus(F_{t}^{\prime})^{d},

i.e.
U^{d}
is decomposable of type
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{n})
.
∎

Remark 1.36
.

In
[
6
]
, the authors proved that the Delsarte dual of decomposable subspaces in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
having dimension a multiple of
m
is again a decomposable subspace. Therefore, the previous result can be seen as an extension of
[
6
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Now, we are able to prove that the property of being
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes is preserved under the Delsarte duality.

Theorem 1.37
.

Let
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})\in\mathbb{N}^{t}
with
k=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
and let
U
be an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes in
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
of type
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
.
Then the Delsarte dual
U^{d}
of
U
is
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes in
\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
of type
\mathbf{n}
.

Proof.

From the previous theorem
U^{d}
is decomposable of type
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k},\mathbf{n})
in
\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
with decompositions (
21
) and (
22
).
Now, by Theorem
1.26
and by Theorem
1.18
, a subspace
T
of
\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
with positive defect with respect to
U^{d}
and minimal with respect to its defect is the dual of a subspace of
\mathbb{V}(k,q^{m})
which is sum of some subspaces
F_{i}
, i.e. there exists
I=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{h}\}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t\}
such that

T=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{h}F_{i_{j}}\right)^{d}=\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{h}S_{i_{j}}^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}\right)^{d}=\frac{(\cap_{j=1}^{h}S_{i_{j}}^{\perp^{\prime}})^{*}+\Gamma}{\Gamma}

and by (
20
) we can write

T=\frac{(\sum_{i\in[t]\setminus I}\bar{S}_{i}^{*})+\Gamma}{\Gamma}=\sum_{i\in[t]\setminus I}F_{i}^{\prime},

i.e.
T
is the sum of some components
F^{\prime}_{i}
, where
\bar{S}_{i}=\cap_{j=1,j\neq i}^{t}S_{j}^{\perp^{\prime}}
for any
i
.
Hence by Theorem
1.18
, we obtain that
U^{d}
is
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes in
\mathbb{V}(n-k,q^{m})
of type
(n_{1},\dots,n_{t})
.
∎

2.
Rank-metric codes and
q
-systems: a new framework

In this section, after recalling some basics of rank-metric codes, we will propose a new framework for the geometric correspondence between rank-metric codes and
q
-systems. This will help us to use the results of the previous section to prove, as a byproduct, some previously known results and to detect classes of codes that are closed under duality.

2.1.
Preliminaries on rank-metric codes and systems

Rank metric codes were introduced by Delsarte
[
19
]
in 1978 as subsets of matrices and they have been intensively investigated in recent years because of their applications; we refer to
[
50
,
45
]
.
In this section we will be interested in rank metric codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
The
rank
(weight)
w(v)
of a vector
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
is defined as
w(v)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\langle v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})
.

A
(linear vector) rank metric code

\C
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
endowed with the rank distance, where such a distance is defined as
d(x,y)=w(x-y)
, where
x,y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.
For details and applications we refer to
[
7
,
29
]
.

Let
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be a linear rank metric code. We will write that
\C
is an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code (or
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code) if
k=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(\C)
and
d
is its minimum distance, that is
d=\min\{d(x,y)\colon x,y\in\C,x\neq y\}
.
Moreover, for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we define
A_{i}(\C)
the number of codewords in
\C
having weight
i
.

By the classification of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isometry of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
(see
[
8
]
), we say that two rank metric codes
\C,\C^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
are
(linearly) equivalent
if and only if there exists a matrix
A\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
such that

\C^{\prime}=\C A=\{vA:v\in\C\}
.
The codes we will consider are
non-degenerate
, i.e. those for which the columns of any generator matrix of
\C
are
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearly independent.

For rank-metric codes a Singleton-like bound holds.

Theorem 2.1
.

(see
[
19
]
)
Let
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code.
Then

(23)

d\leq\min\{n-k+1,m-km/n+1\}.

An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code is called
Maximum Rank Distance code
(or shortly
MRD code
) if its parameters attain the equality in the Singleton-like bound (
23
).
When
n>m
an
n
does not divide
mk
, (
23
) does not provide an integer, and therefore an MRD code cannot exist. Hence, when
n>m
and
mk
is not a multiple of
n
, in
[
40
]
it has been defined an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear
quasi-MRD
as an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with minimum distance
d=m-\lceil km/n\rceil+1
(see
[
17
]
for rank metric codes in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times m}
with
n\leq m
). We say that
\C
is
dually quasi-MRD
if and only if
\C
and
\C^{\perp}
are quasi-MRD codes.

The geometric counterpart of a rank-metric code is the
q
-system.
An
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}

system

U
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
of dimension
n
, such that

\langle U\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
and

d=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap H)\mid H\textnormal{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\textnormal{-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Moreover, two
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
U
and
U^{\prime}
are
equivalent
if there exists an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-isomorphism
\varphi\in\mathrm{GL}(k,q^{m})
such that

\varphi(U)=U^{\prime}.

Thanks to the following result, we can construct a system starting from a non-degenerate rank-metric code and conversely.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
47
,
2
]
)
Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
rank-metric code and let
G
be a generator matrix.
Let
U\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G
.
The rank weight of an element
xG\in\C
, with
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}
is

(24)

w(xG)=n-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap x^{\perp}),

where
x^{\perp}=\{y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\colon<x,y>=0\}.
In particular,

(25)

d=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U\cap H)\colon H\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-hyperplane of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

The above result allows us to give a one-to-one correspondence between equivalence classes of non-degenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
codes and equivalence classes of
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
systems, see
[
47
]
.
The system
U
and the code
\C
as in Theorem
2.2
are said to be
associated
and a system associated with a code
\C
will usually be written as
U_{\C}
.

An essential concept is the rank support of a codeword.
Let
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{m})
be an ordered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. For any vector
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
define the matrix
\Gamma(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n\times m}
, where

x_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(x)_{ij}\gamma_{j},\qquad\mbox{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

that is
\Gamma(x)
is the matrix expansion of the vector
x
with respect to the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-basis
\Gamma
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and this clearly preserves its rank, i.e.
w(x)=\mathrm{rk}(\Gamma(x))
.
The
rank support
of
x
is defined as the column span of
\Gamma(x)
:

\mathrm{supp}(x)=\mathrm{colsp}(\Gamma(x))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}.

As shown in
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
, the support of a vector is independent of
\Gamma
, allowing us to talk about the support of a word without reference to
\Gamma
.
Let
\C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code, we define the
support
of
\C
as

\mathrm{supp}(\C)=\sum_{x\in\C}\mathrm{supp}(x).

The support of the code
\C
can be computed directly on
\C
, without using a fixed order basis.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
34
, Theorem 16]
)
Let
\C
be a
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
rank-metric code, we have

\mathrm{supp}(\C)=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\C),

where

\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\C)=\{(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(c_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(c_{n}))\colon(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\C\}.

For more details, refer to
[
42
]
.

Generalized rank weights have been defined in various ways, as seen in
[
34
]
. This paper focuses on the definition provided in
[
47
]
, particularly the equivalent version found in
[
2
, Theorem 3.14]
, which is closely related to systems.
Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
U_{\C}
be an associated system.
For any
r\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, the
r
-th generalized rank weight
is

(26)

d_{r}(\C)=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{\C}\cap T)\colon T\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of codim. $r$ of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}.

Note that when
r=1
, in the above definition we obtain the minimum distance.

Jurrius and Pellikaan in
[
34
, Section 5]
provided an alternative definition of the
r
-th generalized
rank weight. Combining such a result with Theorem
2.3
we get the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
[
34
, Corollary 17,Theorem 21]
)
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
rank-metric code and let
1\leq r\leq k
. Then

\displaystyle d_{r}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D}))\colon\mathcal{D}\text{ is a linear subcode of }\mathcal{C}\text{ of dimension }r\right\}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\mathrm{dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\mathcal{D}))\colon\mathcal{D}\text{ is a linear subcode of }\mathcal{C}\text{ of dimension }r\right\}.

An important property of generalized rank weights is established by the
Wei-type duality theorem
for
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank metric codes, originally proven by Ducoat in
[
20
, Theorem I.3]
and later derived alternatively in
[
11
, Theorem 7]
. This theorem reveals a fundamental connection between the generalized rank weights of a code
\mathcal{C}
and those of its dual
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

(Wei-type duality,
[
20
, Theorem I.3]
).

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. Then

\left\{d_{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\colon 1\leq r\leq n-k\right\}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\left\{n+1-d_{r}(\mathcal{C})\colon 1\leq r\leq k\right\}.

Finally, we recall that for generalized weights, a Singleton-like bound holds.

Theorem 2.6
.

[
41
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
1\leq r\leq k
. Then

d_{r}(\C)\leq\min\left\{n-k+r,\frac{m}{n}(n-k)+m(r-1)+1\right\}.

Note that when
r=1
, this bound corresponds to Theorem
2.1
and when
n\leq m
the bound simply reads as

d_{r}(\C)\leq n-k+r.

2.2.
A new framework

In this section, our aim is to describe systems associated with a rank-metric code in a quotient space, similarly to what we did in the previous section. The main advantage of doing so is to achieve a completely geometric description of the Delsarte duality, where both a system associated with the code and a system associated with its dual can be observed within the same framework. Therefore, in this section, we will only consider codes that are non-degenerate and for which their duals are still non-degenerate.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
Let
G
be any generator matrix of
\C
and
U_{\C}
the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G
.
The map

\phi\colon x+\C^{\perp}\in\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}}{\C^{\perp}}\mapsto xG^{\top}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isomorphism which maps the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp}
into
U_{\C}
.

Proof.

Note that the map

\varphi\,\colon\,x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}\mapsto xG^{\top}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}

is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear and
ker\varphi=\C^{\perp}
. Hence
\varphi
is surjective. This implies that
\phi
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-linear isomorphism.
Also

\phi\left(\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C^{\perp}}{\C^{\perp}}\right)=\{xG^{T}\,\colon\,x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\}=U_{\mathcal{C}}.

∎

By the above result, we can identify
U_{\C}
with the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp}
of the quotient space
\frac{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}}{\C^{\perp}}
. Therefore, as an immediate consequence, we have that the duality on rank-metric codes corresponds to the Delsarte duality on their systems. This has only been proved for the case
n=m
in
[
37
,
52
]
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate. We have that

U_{\C}\simeq({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp}\,\,\text{and}\,\,U_{\C^{\perp}}\simeq({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C)/\C.

In particular,
U_{\C^{\perp}}
is a Delsarte dual of
U_{\C}
.

The main advantage is that we can embed both
U_{\C}
and
U_{\C^{\perp}}
in quotient spaces of the same vector space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n}
, where we can study directly the metric properties of
\C
and
\C^{\perp}
as shown in the next proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
For any non-zero codeword
c\in\C
we have

w_{U_{\C}}((\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\cap\langle c\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp})\,\,\text{and}\,\,w(c)=n-w_{U_{\C}}(\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}).

Proof.

Let
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\C\setminus\{0\}
.
The map
\varphi_{c}\colon x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\mapsto\langle x,c\rangle\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear and its image is
\langle c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
.
Hence, from this we derive that

(27)

w(c)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\textrm{Im}(\varphi_{c}))=n-\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\cap\langle c\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}).

Now, observe that
(\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp}
is an hyperplane in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}/\C^{\perp}
and

(28)

w_{U_{\C}}\left((\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp}\right)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(((\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp})\cap(({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}+\C^{\perp})/\C^{\perp})\right)=

=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\left(\frac{(\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})+\C^{\perp}}{\C^{\perp}}\right)=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}),

since
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate. From Equations (
27
) and (
28
) we obtain the assertion.
∎

In this framework we can also read the supports of the codewords and of the subcodes, relying on the following result by Delsarte.

Theorem 2.10
.

(
[
18
, Theorem 2]
)
Let
\mathcal{D}
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. We have

(\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})^{\perp^{\prime}}=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\mathcal{D}).

The above result allows us to prove the following correspondence.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
For any non-zero codeword

we have

\mathrm{supp}(c)=\left(\langle c\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\right)^{\perp^{\prime}},

and for any subcode
\mathcal{D}
of
\mathcal{C}

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{D})=\left(\mathcal{D}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}\right)^{\perp^{\prime}}.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathrm{supp}(c)=\mathrm{supp}(\langle c\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})
. Therefore, by Theorems
2.3
and
2.10
we have that

\mathrm{supp}(c)=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{m}/q}(\langle c\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}})=(\langle c\rangle_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}^{\perp}\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})^{\perp^{\prime}}.

A similar argument can be performed on
\mathcal{D}
to get the second part of the assertion.
∎

We can now characterize the generalized weights in terms of the intersection with the system.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
For any
r\in[k]
, we have

d_{r}(\C)=n-k+r-\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-r).

Proof.

By (
26
),

d_{r}(\C)=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(U_{\C}\cap T)\colon T\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of codim. $r$ of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}=

=n-\max\left\{\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}}(T)+\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(T)\colon T\mbox{ is an }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}\mbox{-subspace of codim. $r$ of }{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{k}\right\}=

=n-k+r-\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-r).

∎

By the previous Proposition and by Proposition
1.6
, we can give an alternative proof of the monotonicity property of the generalized rank weights (see e.g.
[

, Theorem I.2]
).

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
The generalized weights are in increasing order.

Proof.

By the above proposition, we have that for any

Now by Property

we have that

∎

In the next theorem, we will see that having complete knowledge of the sequence of maximum non-zero defects of the system

associated with a code

. As a by product, we obtain an alternative proof of the Wei-type duality theorem in the case of non-degenerate
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-linear rank-metric codes.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
Let
U_{\C}
be a system associated with
\C
and suppose that

0=\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(0)=\varepsilon_{0}<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(t_{1})=\varepsilon_{1}<\cdots<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(t_{s-1})=\varepsilon_{s-1}<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k)=n-k

is the sequence of maximum non-zero defects with respect to
U_{\C}
. For every

, if
t_{i}\leq k-r<t_{i+1}
, the
r
-th generalized weight of
\C
is

(29)

d_{r}(\C)=n-(k-r)-\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(t_{i})=n-(k-r)-\varepsilon_{i}.

In particular,

(30)

\{n+1-d_{r}(\mathcal{C})\colon 1\leq r\leq k\}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{s-1}I_{i}

where

.

Also, for every

, let

is

and hence

(32)

\{d_{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\colon 1\leq r\leq n-k\}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}J_{j}

where

for

.
Finally, from Equations (

) and (

\left\{d_{r}(\mathcal{C}^{\perp})\colon 1\leq r\leq n-k\right\}=\{1,\ldots,n\}\setminus\left\{n+1-d_{r}(\mathcal{C})\colon 1\leq r\leq k\right\}.

Proof.

Recall that, by Definitions
1.4
and

1.7
,
\varepsilon_{U}(0)=\varepsilon_{0}=0
and

for any
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

. Now, Equation (

2.12
and by Equation (

) of Definiton
1.7
. By Equation (

) we get (

). Indeed, recall that for any

we have that

and so

\bigcup_{r=1}^{k}\{n+1-d_{r}(\mathcal{C})\}=\bigcup_{r=1}^{k}\{k-r+1+\varepsilon_{i}\,\colon i\text{ is s.t. }\,t_{i}\leq k-r<t_{i+1}\}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{s-1}([t_{i+1}-t_{i}]+(\varepsilon_{i}+t_{i})).

Note that if

and

if

.

By Proposition

Now, by Corollary

, Theorem

and (

we get (

), that is

d_{r}(\C^{\perp})=k+r-\varepsilon_{U_{\C^{\perp}}}(n-k-r)=k+r-\varepsilon_{U_{\C}^{d}}(n-k-r)=r+t_{j}

obtaining (

Also in this case if

and hence

for each

∎

2.3.
Families of codes closed under Delsarte duality

In this section, we explore classes of codes that are either closed under duality or whose dual codes exhibit remarkable structural properties. We begin with MRD and near-MRD codes, proceed to a class of minimal codes, and conclude with a construction based on direct sums of MRD codes.

We can start with a classical result, that is, the dual of an MRD code is an MRD code. In order to do so, we need the following result (whose proof is combinatorial and does not use any connection with the dual code).

Theorem 2.15
.

(
[
55
, Theorem 3.2]
)
Assume that
h+1
divides
km
and let
n=km/(h+1)
.
Let
\C
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
U_{\C}
be an associated system to
\C
.
We have that
\C
is an MRD code if and only if
U_{\C}
is a maximum
h
-scattered subspace.

We can now prove the following classical result; see e.g.
[
23
,
19
]
.

Theorem 2.16
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate.
We have that
\C
is an MRD code if and only if
\C^{\perp}
is an MRD code.

Proof.

Denote by
d
the minimum distance of
\C
and let
h=m-d
. Note that, since
\C
is non-degenerate,
h\leq m-2
.
The code
\C
is MRD if and only if

h=\begin{cases}k-1&\text{if }n\leq m,\\
\frac{mk}{n}-1&\text{if }n>m,\end{cases}

and in the latter case we need that
n
divides
mk
.
In both the cases, we have that
h+1
divides
km
and if
n>m
we have
n=km/(h+1)
.
If
n\leq m
, then
\C
is an MRD code if
d_{1}(\C)=n-k+1
and hence by (
29
)
s=1
and
t_{1}=k
. By (
31
) we get
d_{1}(\C^{\perp})=k+1
, i.e.
\C^{\perp}
is an MRD code. the reverse implication can be established using a completely similar argument. Now, let
n>m
and hence
n=km/(h+1)
. By Theorem
2.15
, the code
\C
is MRD if and only if
U_{\C}
is a maximum
h
-scattered subspace.
By Corollaries
1.31
and
2.8
, we have that
U_{\C}
is a maximum
h
-scattered subspace if and only if
U_{\C^{\perp}}
is a maximum
(m-h-2)
-scattered subspace.
Again, by applying Theorem
2.15
, we get that this is equivalent to ask that
\C^{\perp}
is an MRD code.
∎

We now consider near MRD codes, which have been introduced in
[
40
]
.

Definition 2.17
.

Let
\C
be an
[n,k,d]_{q^{m}/q}
code. We say that
\C
is a
near MRD
code if
d=n-k
and
d_{r}(\C)=n-k+r
for every
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
.

In
[
40
]
the authors also proved a geometric characterization of these codes.

Proposition 2.18
.

(
[
40
, Proposition 5.3]
)
Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code. The code
\C
is near MRD if and only if

•

U_{\C}
is
(k-2)
-scattered;

•

U_{\C}
is not
(k-1)
-scattered;

•

U_{\C}
is
(k-1,k)
-evasive.

In other words,
\C
is a near MRD code if and only if
U_{\C}
is a
1
-defect subspace with sequence of maximum non-zero defects

0<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)=1<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k)=n-k.

We can now use Proposition
1.32
to characterize the dual of a near MRD code.

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate. The code
\C
is near MRD if and only if its dual is a near MRD code.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.18
,
\C
is near MRD if and only if sequence of maximum non-zero defects of
U_{\C}
is

0<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)=1<\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k)=n-k.

By applying Proposition
1.32
and Corollary
2.8
to
U_{\C}
we obtain that, this is equivalent to requiring that the sequence of maximum non-zero defects of
U_{\C^{\perp}}
is

0<\varepsilon_{U_{\C^{\perp}}}(n-k-1)=1<\varepsilon_{U_{\C^{\perp}}}(n-k)=k.

Therefore, we obtain the statement from Proposition
2.18
.
∎

Another relevant class of rank-metric codes is the family of linear quasi-MRD codes.

The following provide a geometric characterization for some classes of codes which are quasi-MRD and/or dually quasi-MRD codes, extending
[
40
, Theorem 6.6]
.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
\C
be a non-degenerate
[m+\rho,k]_{q^{m}/q}
code with
1\leq\rho<m/(k-1)
such that
\C^{\perp}
is non-degenerate. The code
\C
is quasi-MRD if and only if
\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)=\rho
. Whereas, the code
\C^{\perp}
is quasi-MRD if and only if
U_{\C}
is a
(k-2)
-scattered space.
Hence,
\C
is dually quasi-MRD if and only if
U_{\C}
is a
(k-2)
-scattered space and
\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)=\rho
.

Proof.

Let
U_{\C}
be a system associated with
\C
and note that since
\rho>0
, we have
\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)>0
.
Hence, the length
s
of the sequence of the maximum non-zero defects with respect to
U_{\C}
is at least
2
. Also
U_{\C}
is
(k-2)
-scattered if and only if
s=2
and
t_{1}=k-1
.

Now,
\C^{\perp}
is a non-degenerate
[m+\rho,m+\rho-k]_{q^{m}/q}
code and
by (
31
)

d(\C^{\perp})=1+t_{j}

where
\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(t_{j-1})<1\leq\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(t_{j})
, i.e.
d(\C^{\perp})=1+t_{1}
. Also,
\C^{\perp}
is quasi-MRD when

d(\C^{\perp})=m-\left\lceil\frac{m(m+\rho-k)}{m+\rho}\right\rceil+1=k.

Hence,
\C^{\perp}
is a quasi-MRD if and only if
t_{1}=k-1
and
s=2
, i.e. this happens if and only if
U_{\C}
is a
(k-2)
-scattered space.
Finally,
\C
is a quasi-MRD code when

d(\C)=m-\left\lceil\frac{km}{m+\rho}\right\rceil+1=m-k+1

and by Proposition
2.12
, we have that
d(\C)=m+\rho-k+1-\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)
, hence
\C
is quasi-MRD if and only if
\varepsilon_{U_{\C}}(k-1)=\rho
.
∎

In
[

, the authors focused on the construction of

minimal rank-metric codes (the shortest minimal rank-metric codes of dimension three), or equivalently on the construction of scattered subspaces in

of rank

.
They constructed scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces

in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{3}
of dimension

, for certain values of

and

, with the following property:

contains an

-dimensional

-scattered subspace of

.

Theorem 2.21
.

(
[
36
, Theorem 5.2]
)
Let

be an
(m+2)
-dimensional scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace in

-dimensional

-scattered subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{3}

For any hyperplane

in

.

As a consequence of the above result and Theorem

, we have a class of dually quasi-MRD codes.

Corollary 2.22
.

Let

be an
(m+2)
-dimensional scattered
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace in

Therefore, the results in

The existence of non-trivial quasi-MRD codes (which are

-linear) seems to be a widely open problem. In fact, some constructions of

Remark 2.24
.

As it happens for the matrix case, there are examples of quasi-MRD codes that are not dually quasi-MRD codes. Indeed, consider the following

-dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of

As it has been proved in

.

Another interesting class of rank-metric codes regards those that are direct sum of MRD codes. It is well known that the direct sum of MRD codes is not an MRD code; indeed, if
\C_{1}
and
\C_{2}
are two MRD codes in the direct sum
\C_{1}\oplus\C_{2}
there are codewords of the form
(c,0)
(or
(0,c)
) which have
small
weight.
Direct sums of one-dimensional MRD codes have been called
completely decomposable
rank-metric codes and have been studied in
[
49
]
.
Also motivated by the connection with the problem of representability of uniform
q
-matroids that we will discuss in the next section, we introduce the following class of rank-metric codes.

Definition 2.25
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
, with
k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for all
i\in[t]
.
Let
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
and
K=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
.
An
[N,K]_{q^{m}/q}
code
\C
is said to be an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-
MRD code
if there exist
t
MRD codes
\C_{1},\ldots,\C_{t}
with parameters
[n_{1},k_{1}]_{q^{m}/q},\ldots,[n_{t},k_{t}]_{q^{m}/q}
, respectively, such that

\C=\C_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\C_{t},

and with the property that every codeword

with
c_{i}\in\C_{i}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
for

has weight at least

.

Remark 2.26
.

Let
\C
be an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code as in the above definition.
It is worth mentioning that the codewords with weight less than
N-K+1
are of the form
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})
where at least one of the
c_{i}
is zero.

The geometric counterparts of these codes are subspaces that are
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to hyperplanes.

Proposition 2.27
.

Let
\C
be an
[N,K]_{q^{m}/q}
code and let
U_{\C}
be a system associated with
\C
.
Let
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
,
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
,
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
and
K=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
with
k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
The code
\C
is
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code if and only if
U_{\C}
is
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to hyperplanes of type
\mathbf{n}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\C
is
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code.
This means that
\C
admits a generator matrix in block form

(33)

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&G_{2}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&\cdots&G_{t}\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{i}\times n_{i}}
is a generator matrix of an MRD with parameters
[n_{i},k_{i}]_{q^{m}/q}
.
Let
F_{i}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-span of the
n_{i}
columns in the
i
-th block and let
U_{i}
be the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of the
n_{i}
columns in the
i
-th block, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
Observe that
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{K}=F_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus F_{t}
and
U_{\C}=U_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus U_{t}
.
Since the
G_{i}
’s are generator matrices of MRD codes, by Theorem
2.15
the
U_{i}
’s are scattered subspaces with respect to the hyperplanes in
F_{i}
.
It only remains to prove that
w_{U_{\C}}(H)\leq K-1
for each hyperplane
H
such that
F_{i}\not\subseteq H
for
i\in[t]
.
By contradiction, suppose that there exists a hyperplane
H
not containing any of the
F_{i}
’s such that

w_{U_{\C}}(H)\geq K
.
This would mean that, by Theorem
2.2
, there exists a codeword
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\dots c_{t})
with
c_{i}\in\C_{i}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}
for
i\in[t]
having weight at most
N-K
, a contradiction.
Conversely, suppose that
U_{\C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{K}
is
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to hyperplanes of type
\mathbf{n}
. Up to the action of
\mathrm{GL}(K,q^{m})
, we may assume that
U_{\C}=U_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus U_{t}
where
U_{i}
is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{i}}
and
\langle U_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{i}}
for any
i
. We can reverse the above arguments by considering a matrix
G
as in
33
whose columns
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span
U_{\C}
.
∎

This geometric view allows us to prove that the class of
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code is closed under duality.

Theorem 2.28
.

Let
\C
be an
[N,K]_{q^{m}/q}
code and consider
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
,
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
,
N=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
and
K=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
, with
k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.
The code
\C
is an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code if and only if
\C^{\perp}
is an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
-MRD code.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.27
,
\C
is an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code if and only if an associated system
U_{\C}
is
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to hyperplanes of type
\mathbf{n}
.
By Theorem
2.28
, the Delsarte dual
U_{\C}^{d}
of
U_{\C}
is
(\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
-scattered with respect to hyperplanes of type
\mathbf{n}
. By Corollary
2.8
,
U_{\C}^{d}=U_{\C^{\perp}}
, and therefore we get the assertion by using again Proposition
2.27
on
U_{\C^{\perp}}
.
∎

This is a very interesting property that allows us to prove that, if
t=2
, all the
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD codes have the same weight distribution.

Theorem 2.29
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{1},k_{2})
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2})
, with
k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for all
i\in\{1,2\}
. The weight distribution of an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code depends only on
\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k},q
and
m
.

Proof.

Let
\C\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
be an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code. By Theorem
2.28
, its dual
\C^{\perp}
is a
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{k})
-MRD code. Let
N=n_{1}+n_{2}
,
K=k_{1}+k_{t}
.
Since
\C_{1},\C_{1}^{\perp},\C_{2},\C_{2}^{\perp}
are all MRD codes, their weight distribution is then uniquely determined; see e.g.
[
48
, Remark 45]
.
Also, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,N-K\}
we have

A_{i}:=A_{i}(\C)=A_{i}(\C_{1})+A_{i}(\C_{2}),

that means that they are completely determined,
and

B_{j}:=A_{j}(\C^{\perp})=A_{j}(\C_{1}^{\perp})+A_{j}(\C_{2}^{\perp})

for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, that means that they are completely determined.
Moreover, by
[
24
, Proposition 4.]
,

\sum_{i=0}^{N-v}B_{i}\binom{N-i}{v}_{q}=q^{m(N-K-v)}\sum_{j=0}^{v}A_{j}\binom{N-j}{v-j}_{q}

for all
v\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
.

Let
v=N-K+r
.

\sum_{j=N-K+1}^{N-K+r}A_{j}\binom{N-j}{K-r}_{q}=q^{mr}\sum_{i=0}^{K-r}B_{i}\binom{N-i}{N-K+r}_{q}-\sum_{j=0}^{N-K}A_{j}\binom{N-j}{K-r}_{q}

for all
r\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
. The right hand-side of the system is known, whereas the left hand-side is triangular (with non-zero diagonal), so that the solution is uniquely determined.
∎

Remark 2.30
.

Let us remark that the proof of Theorem
2.29
also provides a way to explicitly compute the weight distribution, by recurrence. Actually, with the notations above, for all
r\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, the following holds:

(34)

A_{N-K+r}=q^{rm}\sum_{i=0}^{K-r}B_{i}\binom{N-i}{N-K+r}_{q}-\sum_{j=0}^{N-K+r-1}A_{j}\binom{N-j}{K-r}_{q}.

We now derive some necessary conditions on the existence of
((n_{1},n_{2})),(k_{1},k_{2}))
-MRD codes.
We start with the case
(k_{1},k_{2})=(1,1)
.

Theorem 2.31
.

Let
{\mathbf{k}}=(1,1)
and
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2})
, with

.

•

The

(1,1)
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes of type

in

Consequences on the representability of uniform
q
-matroids

The notion of
q
-matroids originates from Crapo’s PhD thesis
[
15
]
. In more recent years,
q
-matroids have been revisited in
[
33
]
and have attracted considerable interest due to their connection with rank-metric codes. See, for example,
[
28
,
53
,
25
,
13
,
12
,
26
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
q
-matroid with ground space

E
is a pair
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
, where
E
is a finite dimensional
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space and
\rho
is an integer-valued
function defined on
\mathcal{L}(E)
with the following properties:

(R1)

Boundedness:
0\leq\rho(A)\leq\dim A
, for all
A\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

(R2)

Monotonicity:
A\leq B\Rightarrow\rho(A)\leq\rho(B)
, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

(R3)

Submodularity:
\rho(A+B)+\rho(A\cap B)\leq\rho(A)+\rho(B)
, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

The function
\rho
is called
rank function
and the value
\rho(\mathcal{M}):=\rho(E)
is the
rank of the
q
-matroid
. The value
h(\mathcal{M})=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(E)
is the
height of
\mathcal{M}
.

A well-known family of
q
-matroids is the family of
uniform
q
-matroids
; see
[
33
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
0\leq k\leq n
. For each
V\in\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})
, define
\rho(V):=\min\{k,\dim(V)\}
. Then
({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n},\rho)
is a
q
-matroid. It is called the
uniform
q
-matroid on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
of rank
k
and is denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{k,n}(q)
.

A possible way to construct
q
-matroids is via matrices; see
[
33
,
28
]
. Let
G
be a
k\times n
matrix with entries in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
and for every
U\in\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})
, let
A^{U}
be a matrix whose columns form a basis of
U
. Then the map

(35)

\rho:\mathcal{L}({\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n})\to\mathbb{Z},\ U\mapsto\rk(GA^{U}),

is the rank function of a
q
-matroid, which we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{G}
and we call the
q
-matroid represented by
G
.

A
q
-matroid
\mathcal{M}
with ground space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
is called
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-representable
if
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{G}
for some matrix
G
with
n
columns and entries in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, whose rank equals the rank of
\mathcal{M}
. The
q
-matroid
\mathcal{M}
is called
representable
if it is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-representable
over some field extension
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
.
In other words, we can say that
\mathcal{M}
is
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
-representable if there is a rank-metric code with generator matrix
G
, such that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{G}
.
As proved in
[
3
]
, the representability of a
q
-matroid can be expressed in terms of
q
-systems, for further details we refer to
[
3
, Theorem 4.6]
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{k,n}(q)
be the uniform
q
-matroid of rank
k
with ground space
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
. Then the
trivial
uniform
q
-matroid
\mathcal{U}_{0,n}(q)
and the
free
uniform
q
-matroid
\mathcal{U}_{n,n}
are
representable over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For
0<k<n
, the uniform
q
-matroid
\mathcal{U}_{k,n}(q)
is representable over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
if and only if

m\geq n
.
Analogously, in the language of
q
-systems, a
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k,n}(q)
is given by any
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system which is
(k-1)
-scattered, and this is known to exist if and only if
m\geq n
; see e.g.
[
19
,
52
]
.

In
[
14
]
the notion of direct sum of two
q
-matroids has been introduced. We are going to define the direct sum of
t
many
q
-matroids iteratively, thanks to the associativity property of the direct sum, proved in
[
27
]
.
To this aim we introduce the following map.
For
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}+\cdots+n_{t}
, consider
E_{1},\ldots,E_{t}

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspaces of an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-vector space
E
such that
E=E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{t}
. We denote by

\pi_{i}:E\longrightarrow E_{i}
is the projection onto
E_{i}
, for any
i\in[t]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{i}=(E_{i},\rho_{i}),\,i\in[t],
be
q
-matroids and set
E=E_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus E_{t}
.
Define
\rho^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{L}(E)\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0},\ V\longmapsto\rho_{i}(\pi_{i}(V))
for
i\in[t]
.
Then
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{i}=(E,\rho^{\prime}_{i})
is a
q
-matroid.
We define the
direct sum of
\mathcal{M}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{M}_{t}
to be the
q
-matroid
\mathcal{M}:=(E,\rho)
, where
\rho
is defined iteratively as follows:

•

If
t=2
, then

(36)

\rho:\mathcal{L}(E)\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad V\longmapsto\dim V+\min_{X\leq V}\big(\rho^{\prime}_{1}(X)+\rho^{\prime}_{2}(X)-\dim X\big).

•

If
t>2
, then
\rho
is the rank function of
(\mathcal{M}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{M}_{t-1})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{t}
.

The representability of the direct sum of uniform
q
-matroids has been characterized geometrically as follows; see
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{t},k,n_{1},\ldots,n_{t},n,m
be positive integers, with
1\leq k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for
i\in[t]
and
k=k_{1}+\ldots+k_{t}
,
n=n_{1}+\ldots+n_{t}
.
Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{U}_{k_{t},n_{t}}(q)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable;

(2)

there exists a
\mathbf{k}
-scattered subspace with respect to the hyperplanes of type
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})
, where
\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{t})
;

(3)

there exists an
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{k})
-MRD code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{n}
.

Proof.

[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
ensures that (1) and (2) are equivalent.
The remaining equivalences immediately follow from Proposition
2.27
.
∎

As a consequence, by constructing certain subspaces that are

-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes, in

Theorem 3.6
.

(
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
)
Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{t},n_{1},\ldots n_{t}

\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{U}_{k_{t},n_{t}}(q)

.

The natural question now is to determine on which extension of

We are not going to completely answer this question, but we will give some insight on that.
We start by using the fact that property of being

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
k_{1},\ldots,k_{t},k,n_{1},\ldots,n_{t},n,m
be positive integers, with

for

and

,

Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{U}_{k_{t},n_{t}}(q)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable;

(2)

\mathcal{U}_{n_{1}-k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{U}_{n_{t}-k_{t},n_{t}}(q)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.5

\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{U}_{k_{t},n_{t}}(q)
is

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace

of dimension

in

such that

is
\mathbf{k}
-scattered with respect to the hyperplanes, where

By Theorem

is

A classical function which is associated with a

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(E,\rho)
be a

-matroid.
The

of

is defined as

R_{\mathcal{M}}(X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4})=\sum_{D\in\mathcal{L}(E)}f_{\mathcal{M}}^{D}(X_{1},X_{2})f^{\dim(D)}(X_{3},X_{4}),

g^{l}(X_{3},X_{4})=\prod_{i=0}^{l-1}(X_{3}-q^{i}X_{4}),

for any nonnegative integer

When the

Theorem 3.11
.

(
[

Let

We have that

As a consequence of the above result and Theorem

Corollary 3.12
.

Let

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2},m
be positive integers, with
1<n_{i}\leq m
for
i\in[2]
.
Assume that
\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{1}}(q)\oplus\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{2}}(q)
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable, then

•

if
n_{1}=n_{2}
, then
m\geq n_{1}+n_{2}
and equality holds if and only if
n_{1}=n_{2}=m/2
;

•

if
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, then
m\geq n_{1}+n_{2}+1
.

Remark 3.14

\mathcal{U}_{1,3}\oplus\mathcal{U}_{2,3}

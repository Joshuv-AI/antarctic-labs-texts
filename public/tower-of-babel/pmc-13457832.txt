EGFL6 predicts unfavorable prognosis and serves as a potential indicator in esophageal carcinoma
Authors: Huijuan Jiang, Junling Zhu, Zhang Lei, Tuxunayi Yimamu, Xinhong Zhang, Aishanjiang Muhetaer, Hailin Chen, Amanjiang Aierken, Ainiwaerjiang Abudourousuli
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe EGF-like domain multiple 6 (EGFL6) has been documented as an oncogene across diverse cancer types. Despite its known importance in tumorigenesis, EGFL6’s involvement in esophageal carcinoma (ESCA) remains largely unexplored. Herein, we integrated multi-source omics data to characterize EGFL6’s expression landscape, biological functions, and immunological implications in ESCA—with a focus on its prognostic value.MethodsTo identify dysregulated basement membrane-related genes (BMRGs) in ESCA, we performed Venn overlap analysis on RNA-seq data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA), Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO), and a curated BMRG reference list. Weighted gene co-expression analysis (WGCNA) was used to identify EGFL6-related modules and functional enrichment of differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified via Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA). EGFL6-specific epigenetic (DNA methylation) and genetic alterations were validated using Methsurv and cBioPortal. Single-sample GSEA (ssGSEA) was applied to explore immune infiltration and its correlation with EGFL6. Prognostic significance of EGFL6 was examined via Kaplan-Meier and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analyses.Results Venn overlap analysis indicated EGFL6 as a significantly overexpressed gene in ESCA that closely correlated with multiple clinical and pathological features. WGCNA and subsequent functional enrichment analysis revealed that EGFL6-related DEGs participate in critical biological processes. Kaplan-Meier analysis demonstrated that increased EGFL6 contributed to reduced disease-specific survival (DSS) and progression-free interval (PFI) in patients with ESCA. In vitro experiments confirmed that EGFL6 promotes ESCA by enhancing cell proliferation, invasion and regulating cell-cycle progression.ConclusionEGFL6 is significantly upregulated in ESCA and is related to various clinical factors, immune modulation and reduced prognosis. EGFL6 could be identified as a novel prognostic biomarker and a viable therapeutic target for the development of personalized treatment approaches in ESCA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05372-8.

Introduction

Esophageal carcinoma (ESCA) is a prevalent gastrointestinal malignancy, with ~ 600,000 new cases reported worldwide in 2020 [1]. ESCA is currently the 8th most common cancer and the 6th leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide [2, 3]. The incidence of ESCA exhibits significant variability across different populations and geographical regions, reflecting a complex genetic predisposition and other influences. Histologically, ESCA is predominantly classified into two categories: esophageal adenocarcinoma (EA) and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC); critically, ESCC represents over 90% of all clinical diagnoses [4]. Patients with early-stage ESCA generally have favorable clinical outcomes, but those presenting with advanced disease face heightened malignancy, poor prognosis, and elevated recurrence risk—all of which drive increased mortality [5, 6]. Despite progress in early screening, and interventions including surgery, chemoradiotherapy, and immunotherapy, the 5-year survival probability for ESCA patients is still low, at ~ 15–20% [7, 8]. Key factors linked to the pathogenesis of ESCA include cigarette smoking, alcohol consumption, poor dietary practices, genetic susceptibility and chronic inflammatory signaling [9–11]. While mitigating these risk factors may reduce ESCA incidence, the absence of effective early diagnostic biomarkers exacerbates the high mortality rates, making the treatment of advanced cases particularly challenging [12, 13]. Thus, there is an urgent need for improved screening and diagnostic modalities.

The basement membrane (BM) is a sheet-like extracellular matrix structure that localizes to the basal side of epithelial cells and encapsulates most tissues [14]. It plays critical roles in development, maintaining cell polarity, facilitating migration, and mediating signalling [15]. BMs are also pivotal in influencing angiogenesis and promoting adipogenesis [16]. In the context of cancer, the BM initially acts as a physical barrier restricting cancer cell invasion into adjacent stromal compartments; however, its dysregulation fuels tumor proliferation, angiogenesis, and metastasis —processes tightly intertwined with immune evasion (e.g., by modulating immune cell infiltration into the tumor microenvironment) [17, 18]. Previous research has indicated the important functions of dysregulated BMRGs in tumorigenesis across various cancers [19, 20]. Nevertheless, the expression and specific functions of BMRGs in ESCA remain inadequately characterized.

EGFL6 is a key BMRG that has been implicated in several biological phenotypes, such as cell differentiation, extracellular matrix organization, angiogenesis, and stem cell proliferation [21]. EGFL6 expression is generally low in normal tissues but often elevated in various cancers, correlating with poor prognosis and increased recurrence risk [22]. Compelling evidence indicates that EGFL6 is integral to tumorigenesis in breast cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, ovarian and endometrial cancer [23–25]. Researchers demonstrated that EGFL6 facilitates the progression of colorectal cancer [26]. In ovarian cancer, EGFL6 overexpression regulates cell migration and angiogenesis via the FGF2/PDGFB pathway, while in breast cancer, it enhances metastasis and angiogenesis [27]. EGFL6 secreted by osteoblast-like cells is critical for angiogenesis-associated osteogenesis via ERK pathway activation [28]. However, the relationships between EGFL6 expression, its clinical significance, aberrant DNA methylation, immune cell infiltration, and m6A modifications in ESCA have yet to be fully elucidated.

Herein, we used bioinformatics analyses utilizing the TCGA and GEO databases to identify key BMRGs, with EGFL6 emerging as a novel prognostic indicator for ESCA. EGFL6 is overexpressed in ESCA and associates with various clinical features. We further analyzed EGFL6-related molecular functions and potential regulatory pathways using WGCNA, GO/KEGG, and GSEA. Additionally, we explored the relationships among EGFL6 expression, immune infiltration, and m6A modifications in ESCA. This study clarifies the critical role of EGFL6 in ESCA, laying a theoretical foundation for refining clinical strategies and improving patient outcomes.

Materials and methods

Collection of data

Clinical information and RNA-seq data of 163 ESCA and 11 non-tumor cases were collected from the TCGA-ESCA cohort (http://portal.gdc.cancer.gov). Level 3 HTSeq-FPKM transcriptome data and corresponding clinical annotations were downloaded. Gene expression values were transformed from FPKM to Transcripts Per Million (TPM) for standardized quantification and downstream analysis. Samples were included only if they contained complete survival, follow-up, age, gender, TNM stage, and tumor grade information. Patients with missing critical clinical data or duplicate records were excluded before downstream analysis. GSE26886 and GSE45670 obtained from GEO (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo) provided additional data for this study. A targeted search of the GeneCards database was performed to compile a list of 222 BMRGs, selecting genes specifically linked to the structural organization and functional dynamics of BM [29]. This study was conducted in strict adherence to the Declaration of Helsinki and has obtained approval from the Ethics Committee of the First People’s Hospital of Kashi Prefecture.

Functional enrichment analysis

By employing the median expression levels of EGFL6, the cohort of 163 patients diagnosed with ESCA was divided into two groups (EGFL6-high and EGFL6- low). DEGs were screened using the Limma R package with stringent thresholds of log2 fold change (log2 FC) > 1.5 and p-value < 0.05. Subsequently, GO analysis was conducted to categorize these DEGs into three categories—biological processes (BP), molecular functions (MF), and cellular components (CC)—with the assistance of the GOplot tool. Additionally, GSEA was conducted via the ClusterProfiler R package to select EGFL6-related pathways.

Assessment of immune infiltration

The evaluation of EGFL6-related immune infiltration in ESCA was conducted through the application of ssGSEA technique, as incorporated in GSVA R package. Furthermore, Spearman’s rank correlation test was conducted to investigate the correlation between EGFL6 expression levels and immune infiltration patterns in ESCA.

Validation of the correlation of EGFL6 with m6A-related genes in ESCA

The correlation between EGFL6 and m6A-related genes, including METTL3, METTL16, METTL14, ALKBH5, WTAP, FTO, YTHDF1, YTHDF2, YTHDF3, YTHDC1, YTHDC2, RBM15B, RBM15, HNRNPC, HNRNPA2B1, IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, IGF2BP3, VIRMA, and RBMX, was validated using Spearman’s correlation test.

Assessment of DNA methylation and genetic alterations of EGFL6

The DNA methylation profile of EGFL6 was examined utilizing the Methsurv database (http://biit.cs.ut.ee/methsurv). The cBioPortal platform (www.cBioPortal.org) was utilized to investigate genomic alterations in the EGFL6 gene across two ESCA datasets: the Esophageal Cancer (TCGA, Nature 2017) and the Esophageal Carcinoma (TCGA, Firehose Legacy) datasets. The CpG methylation status and genomic alterations of the EGFL6 gene were evaluated through Kaplan-Meier analysis.

Examination of the prognostic value of EGFL6 in ESCA

The influence of EGFL6 expression on overall survival (OS), disease-specific survival (DSS), and progression-free interval (PFI) in ESCA was examined via Kaplan-Meier method. Multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to determine the prognostic value of EGFL6 in ESCA. Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curves were utilized to examine the diagnostic utility of EGFL6 in ESCA. The regplot and survival R packages were employed for analyzing clinical features and risk scores.

In vitro validation of EGFL6

In vitro experiments were conducted using two ESCA cell lines, KYSE140 and KYSE-150, as well as the human esophageal epithelial cell line Het-1 A as a control. All three cell lines were purchased from Shanghai Lanwei Medical Laboratory Co., Ltd. EGFL6 expression was silenced using small interfering RNA (siRNA) that specifically targeting EGFL6. Proliferation of KYSE140 cells were assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Additionally, the migratory and invasive capabilities of KYSE140 cells were examined via wound healing and transwell assays.

Statistical analysis

The statistical significance between two cohorts were examined using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. To investigate the relationships among variables, the Spearman correlation test was utilized. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were used to identify potential biomarkers with prognostic values. To minimize bias from potential confounders, including age, gender, TNM stage, and tumor grade, these clinical variables were adjusted in multivariate Cox regression models. All statistical adjustments were performed to confirm the independent prognostic value of EGFL6.

Result

Selection of BMRGs in ESCA

The BMRGs play critical regulatory roles in tumor progression. A Venn overlap analysis was conducted to examine the concordance of DEGs across the TCGA-ESCA dataset (comprising 163 cancer samples and 11 normal samples), as well as the GEO datasets GSE45670 and GSE26886, in relation to the identified 222 BMRGs. This analysis revealed 13 differentially expressed BMRGs in ESCA (Fig. 1A). The expression profiles of these 13 BMRGs in ESCA are illustrated in a heatmap (Fig. 1B). Furthermore, a volcano plot displayed the distribution of eight upregulated and five downregulated BMRGs (Supplementary Fig. 1), consistent with our DEG criteria. To identify DEGs correlated with patient survival, univariate and multivariate Cox analyses were performed. Our findings showed that only EGFL6 demonstrated prognostic significance in terms of DSS and PFI (Supplementary Table 1). Consequently, we conducted further analysis to elucidate the biological functions of EGFL6 in ESCA.

Examination of EGFL6 expression in TCGA-ESCA dataset and ESCA cell lines

We analyzed the expression of EGFL6 in the TCGA-ESCA dataset (Fig. 2A). Comparative analysis revealed that ESCA samples exhibited substantially higher levels of EGFL6 expression than adjacent non-cancerous tissues (Fig. 2B). Our ROC curve analysis further demonstrated the discriminative power of EGFL6 in differentiating ESCA from non-cancerous tissues, with an AUC score of 0.892 (Fig. 2C). EGFL6 expression in ESCA cell lines revealed EGFL6 was significantly upregulated at both mRNA and protein levels in KYSE150 and KYSE140 cells relative to those detected in the Het-1 A cell line (Fig. 2D-F). The associations between EGFL6 and clinical as well as pathological variables of ESCA patients are shown in Table 1. Taken together, these results demonstrate that EGFL6 is markedly upregulated in ESCA.

Screening for EGFL6-related DEGs in ESCA

To gain deeper insights into the biological significance of EGFL6, DEGs associated with EGFL6 in ESCA were investigated. The TCGA-ESCA patients were divided into 2 groups (EGFL6-high and EGFL6-low) according to the median EGFL6 expression. Through systematic comparative analysis, we screened out 835 DEGs, with the subset of downregulated genes accounting for 399 and that of upregulated genes comprising 436; the transcriptional changes of these genes were further depicted in a volcano plot as shown in Fig. 4A. Furthermore, Fig. 4B presents a heatmap showcasing the ten most significant DEGs, which include SPANXD, SPANXC, MUCL3, GC, DPEP1, FGFBP2, PGC, DAZ2, LIPF, and LINC01228. We then conducted WGCNA on the DEGs to explore gene expression patterns related to ESCA (Fig. 4C). Prior to module construction, we determined the optimal soft-thresholding power for scale-free topology fitting. A soft-thresholding power of 15 was selected as it satisfied the criterion of approximate scale-free topology (R²=0.85), balancing scale-free topology fit and mean connectivity (Supplementary Fig. 2A-B). Eleven distinct modules were identified and brown module which harbored EGFL6, exhibited a strong association with ESCA (Fig. 4D). Our data underscore the distinct gene expression signatures that correlate with EGFL6 expression levels, thereby implying that these DEGs may be pivotal components of the key mediators in the molecular pathogenesis of ESCA.

Functional enrichment analyses of EGFL6-related DEGs in ESCA

Functional enrichment analyses were implemented to examine the biological relevance of EGFL6-associated DEGs in ESCA. GO analysis revealed enrichments across various biological processes, cellular components, molecular functions, and KEGG pathways (Fig. 4E-H). Notable biological processes included hormone level regulation, protein-containing complex remodeling, cellular hormone metabolic process, response to extracellular stimulus, and hormone metabolic process. The enriched cellular components comprised high-density lipoprotein particles, triglyceride-rich plasma lipoprotein particle, lipoprotein particle, anchored component of membrane and apical plasma membrane. Key molecular functions encompassed sterol transfer activity, cholesterol transfer activity, hormone activity, hormone receptor binding and peptide hormone receptor binding. Identified KEGG pathways included retinol metabolism, neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, metabolism of xenobiotics by cytochrome P450, cholesterol metabolism, and steroid hormone biosynthesis. GSEA was also employed to analyze EGFL6-related DEGs. The HALLMARK analysis revealed significant enrichment in epithelial mesenchymal transition, E2F targets, Myc targets V1, mTORC1 signaling, and G2M checkpoint pathways, consistent with accelerated cell proliferation and malignant progression (Fig. 5A-E). In the GSEA : BP enrichment analysis, pathways including sensory perception of smell, keratinization, and collagen fibril organization were significantly enriched (Fig. 5F-H). Additionally, in the GSEA : CC enrichment analysis, pathways such as complex of collagen trimers and fibrillar collagen trimer exhibited significant enrichment (Fig. 5I-J). The GSEA : MF enrichment analysis highlighted pathways including olfactory receptor activity and extracellular matrix structural constituent conferring tensile strength (Fig. 5K-L). These multiple layers of enrichment results collectively indicate that EGFL6 may exert pro-tumorigenic effects in ESCA by regulating extracellular matrix organization, metabolic reprogramming, cell cycle progression, and oncogenic signaling pathways.

Identification and validation of EGFL6-related gene signature in ESCA

To further investigate the functional and clinical relevance of EGFL6 in ESCA, we first integrated ESCA-associated DEGs with EGFL6-related DEGs, identifying 376 overlapping genes via Venn diagram analysis (Fig. 6A). We then employed LASSO-Cox regression analysis to refine this gene set, with the partial likelihood deviance plot guiding the selection of the optimal λ value that minimized model complexity while preserving predictive power (Fig. 6B). The corresponding coefficient profile plot ultimately identified a subset of genes (KRTAP5-4, ASCL2, ESM1, APLN, NOTUM and CPA2) that formed the EGFL6-related prognostic gene signature (Fig. 6C). Consistent with our DGE criteria, KRTAP5-4, ASCL2, ESM1, APLN, and NOTUM were upregulated in ESCA tissues, while CPA2 was markedly downregulated (Supplementary Fig. 3). Using this signature, we computed individual patient risk scores and stratified patients into low- and high-risk cohorts based on the median risk score (Fig. 6D). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis confirmed that the high-risk group had significantly worse overall survival outcomes compared to the low-risk group, underscoring the signature’s robust prognostic value (Fig. 6E). ROC curve validation for 1-, 2-, and 3-year OS yielded AUC values of 0.750, 0.743, and 0.815, respectively, demonstrating moderate to strong predictive accuracy across multiple time horizons (Fig. 6F). Finally, decision curve analysis (DCA) (Fig. 6G) revealed that the EGFL6-related risk score delivered a positive net benefit across the full spectrum of threshold probabilities, outperforming the extreme assumptions of universal risk positivity or negativity, thereby supporting its clinical utility in aiding prognostic decision-making for ESCA patients.

DNA methylation and genetic alterations of EGFL6 in ESCA

Utilizing the MethSurv online tool, we identified nine methylated CpG sites, namely cg07810164, cg00932276, cg26310256, cg12461113, cg18419476, cg00650640, cg23083672, cg3717635, and cg1926150, which exhibited elevated levels of DNA methylation (Fig. 7A). Notably, methylation of the cg00650640 and cg23083672 sites was related to poor OS in ESCA, suggesting that hypermethylation of these two specific CpG sites may be related with unfavorable prognosis in ESCA (Fig. 7B, C). To further investigate the genetic alterations of EGFL6, we accessed the cBioPortal database, analyzing two independent datasets comprising 373 ESCA samples from the Esophageal Carcinoma (TCGA, Nature, 2017) and Esophageal Carcinoma (TCGA, Firehose Legacy) databases. Genetic alterations in the EGFL6 gene were identified in 4% of ESCA patients, with the alteration frequency stratified into mutation, amplification, and deep deletion (Supplementary Fig. 4A-B). Kaplan-Meier analysis indicated an association between genetic alterations of EGFL6 and overall survival in ESCA, with patients harboring EGFL6 alterations showing a trend toward shorter overall survival compared to those without alterations (Supplementary Fig. 4C). However, no significant associations were found between EGFL6 alterations and DFI (Supplementary Fig. 4D), implying that EGFL6 genetic alterations may primarily affect long-term overall survival rather than disease-free interval in ESCA.

Tumor immune signature profiling

The relationship between the expression of EGFL6 and immune infiltration in ESCA was investigated via ssGSEA method (Fig. 8A). Specifically, EGFL6 expression exhibited significant negative correlations with the infiltration levels of eosinophils, NK CD56bright cells, and Th17 cells (Fig. 8B-G). Conversely, positive associations were observed with macrophages, Th1 and Th2 cells (Supplementary Fig. 5A-F). To further dissect the immunomodulatory role of EGFL6 in ESCA, we analyzed its correlation with the expression of key immune checkpoint and receptor genes (Supplementary Fig. 6A). The heatmap revealed that EGFL6 expression was significantly and positively correlated with multiple immune-related genes, including SIRPA, HAVCR2, SIGLEC7, LILRB2, and LILRB4, with SIRPA exhibiting the most significant correlation (Supplementary Fig. 6B-F). These findings collectively indicate that EGFL6 may influence the ESCA immune microenvironment not only by regulating the infiltration of distinct immune cell subsets but also by modulating the expression of critical immune checkpoint and receptor genes, thereby contributing to immune evasion and tumor progression.

Correlation between EGFL6 expression and m6A RNA methylation regulators in ESCA

To explore the potential epigenetic regulatory mechanism of EGFL6 in ESCA, the relationship between EGFL6 and 21 m6A-related genes in TCGA-ESCA cohort was examined. Our analysis demonstrated that EGFL6 expression was positively correlated with seven m6A-associated genes in ESCA: HNRNPC, METTL16, FTO, WTAP, ALKBH5, IGF2BP3, and HNRNPA2B1 (Fig. 9A). The association between EGFL6 and these m6A-related genes is visually depicted in the scatter plots (Fig. 9B-H), highlighting the close relationship between EGFL6 and m6A modifications in ESCA. This positive correlation suggests that EGFL6 may be epigenetically regulated by these m6A regulators, or alternatively, EGFL6 may participate in the regulation of m6A modification processes, thereby jointly influencing the occurrence and progression of ESCA through epigenetic reprogramming.

Evaluation of EGFL6`s prognostic value in ESCA

We employed the Kaplan-Meier method to examine the prognostic relevance of EGFL6 in ESCA. While statistical significance was not reached for the association between EGFL6 expression and OS (p = 0.057), high levels of EGFL6 expression presented a substantially worse prognosis regarding DSS (p = 0.008) and PFI (p = 0.006; Fig. 10A-C). Furthermore, we investigated whether EGFL6 expression levels could predict OS in other cancers beyond ESCA. Notably, high EGFL6 expression was consistently linked to impaired OS in glioblastoma (GBM), lower grade glioma and glioblastoma (GBMLGG), pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD), kidney papillary cell carcinoma (KIRP), kidney clear cell carcinoma (KIRC), and endometrial cancer (UCEC) (Supplementary Fig. 7A-F).

In vitro functional assays of EGFL6

To investigate the functional role of EGFL6 in ESCA cell lines, we first validated the knockdown efficiency of EGFL6: as shown in Fig. 11A, transfection with si-EGFL6#1 or si-EGFL6#2 significantly reduced EGFL6 mRNA expression in KYSE140 cells relative to the si-NC control, while western blot analysis (Fig. 11B) further confirmed that EGFL6 protein levels were markedly downregulated in the si-EGFL6-transfected groups, confirming successful silencing of EGFL6 at both transcriptional and translational levels. CCK-8 assay in KYSE140 cells resulted in a significant reduction in cell proliferation, compared to control cells transfected with scrambled siRNA (Supplementary Fig. 8). In the wound healing and transwell assays, EGFL6 silencing led to a nearly 40% reduction in cell migration and a 50% decrease in invasion (p < 0.01) (Fig. 11C-F). Cell cycle analysis revealed that EGFL6 knockdown increased the cell population in G0/G1 phase (Fig. 11G-H). These in vitro assays collectively demonstrate that EGFL6 exerts a pro-oncogenic role in ESCA cells by enhancing cell proliferation, facilitating migration and invasion, and regulating cell cycle progression.

Discussion

Basement membranes (BMs) have emerged as critical regulators of cellular activity rather than merely serving as structural components of tissues [30]. BMs have an important function in tissue compartmentalization and transmitting microenvironmental signals to cells, thereby promoting tumor initiation and progression [31]. Beyond their well-established roles in cancer cell proliferation, resistance to chemotherapy-induced apoptosis, and tumor angiogenesis, BMs have also been implicated in tumor immune evasion [32]. Basement membrane-related genes are linked to tumor initiation and metastasis, positioning them as potential markers for tumor progression and prognosis [33]. The expression patterns and prognostic implications of BMRGs have been extensively studied in gastric cancer and lung adenocarcinoma [34, 35]. In this study, we analyzed co-expressed BMRGs in ESCA utilizing data from TCGA, GEO datasets along with a curated panel of 222 BMRGs. Our Venn overlap analysis identified 13 differentially expressed BMRGs. Among these, only EGFL6 demonstrated a significant correlation with DSS and PFI, indicating its potential as a promising prognostic biomarker for ESCA.

Multiple biomarkers, including the human epithelial growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) amplification, deficiencies in mismatch repair, and high microsatellite instability (dMMR/MSI-H) and the expression level of the programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) were widely investigated and applied in clinical practice [36, 37]. EGFL6 has been recognized as a crucial regulatory gene in cancer proliferation and angiogenesis across various malignancies [38]. However, the precise functional mechanisms underlying EGFL6 involvement in ESCA pathogenesis, as well as its clinical implications for disease management, remain largely elusive. Our data demonstrated robust EGFL6 overexpression in ESCA specimens, with elevated expression levels showing significant associations with multiple adverse clinicopathological characteristics, including histological type, body weight, BMI, history of Barrett’s esophagus, reflux symptoms, tumor centrality, and previous radiation therapy. These associations present a potential association between EGFL6 and the advancement of ESCA.

As a secreted protein enriched in the basement membrane, EGFL6 may exert its oncogenic effects by activating key oncogenic signaling pathways, most notably the WNT/β-catenin pathway and ERK/AKT cascades [39–42]. Mechanistically, EGFL6 may regulate the expression and activity of key metastasis-related molecules including MMP1, MMP2, and TGF-β, thereby remodeling the extracellular matrix and promoting tumor cell detachment and infiltration [43–45]. We conducted WGCNA and GO/KEGG analyses to elucidate the biological activities of EGFL6 and the signaling pathways it regulates in ESCA. The results revealed that neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, xenobiotic metabolism via cytochrome P450, and pathways related to retinol and cholesterol metabolism, as well as fat digestion and absorption, were notably enriched in EGFL6-associated pathways. Furthermore, GSEA indicated that epithelial mesenchymal transition, E2F targets, Myc targets V1, mTORC1 signaling and G2M checkpoint are significantly associated with EGFL6 expression. Our results imply that elevated EGFL6 expression may drive ESCA initiation and progression through dual mechanisms via reprogramming cellular metabolism, remodeling the tumor microenvironment, and activating oncogenic transcriptional programs. Specifically, activation of mTORC1 signaling and upregulation of E2F and Myc targets suggest that EGFL6 may promote uncontrolled cell-cycle progression and anabolic metabolism, supporting rapid tumor growth. Meanwhile, enrichment of epithelial-mesenchymal transition signatures indicates that EGFL6 may enhance the migratory and invasive capacity of ESCA cells, thereby facilitating local invasion and distant metastasis.

DNA methylation represents a crucial epigenetic mechanism involved in cancer development [46]. Alterations in the methylation status of various genes have been identified as key contributors to cancer initiation and progression [47]. In our study, we explored the relationship between EGFL6 methylation and patient prognosis in ESCA. We found that hypermethylation of two specific CpG sites, cg00650640 and cg23083672, was associated with worse OS. Furthermore, genetic mutations can lead to functional changes in genes that contribute to uncontrolled growth and progression in ESCA. Notably, mutations in critical genes, including TP53, CTNNB1, and ARID2, are reported in ESCA and correlate with aggressive tumor behaviour and a heightened risk of recurrence. Our results revealed that EGFL6 mutations occurred in only 4% of ESCA patients; however, these mutations were associated with OS but not with DFI, underscoring the need for further investigation into the implications of EGFL6 methylation and mutation in ESCA.

The significance of tumor immunotherapy is gaining recognition, as its therapeutic effectiveness is closely linked to the level of immune infiltration within tumors [48]. Tumors exhibiting higher levels of immune cell infiltration often respond more favourably to immunotherapy. In ovarian cancer, EGFL6 may remodel the tumor immune microenvironment by inducing the expression of PD-L1 and fostering an immunosuppressive milieu, thereby impairing the efficacy of anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy [49]. Our findings, derived from ssGSEA, demonstrated an inverse relationship between elevated EGFL6 and the infiltration of antitumor immune cells including eosinophils, NK CD56bright, and Th17 cells, while a positive association was observed with tumor-promoting macrophages, Th1 and Th2 cells, suggesting that EGFL6 may suppress antitumor immunity and facilitate an immunosuppressive microenvironment in ESCA. Additionally, EGFL6 expression was positively correlated with multiple key immune checkpoint and receptor genes, including HAVCR2, SIRPA, and SIGLEC7 collectively indicating that EGFL6 may inhibit antitumor immune responses and foster immune escape in ESCA.

Previous research have reported EGFL6 as a prognostic indicator in several tumors [50]. Our research evaluated the prognostic utility of EGFL6 in ESCA. Although no significant correlation was observed between EGFL6 expression and OS, high EGFL6 expression correlated with poorer DSS and PFI. The lack of a statistically significant association between EGFL6 expression and OS might be attributed to the relatively small sample size of the TCGA-ESCA cohort may reduce statistical power for detecting its association with survival. Furthermore, we extended our investigation to other cancer types, revealing that EGFL6 was also a prognostic indicator in cancers, including glioblastoma, and endometrial cancer. Collectively, these findings indicate that EGFL6 may serve as a promising therapeutic target for the clinical management of malignant tumors. The in vitro results confirm EGFL6’s role as a tumor promoter by enhancing proliferation and invasion while regulating cell-cycle in ESCA cells. The findings align with previous studies on EGFL6 in breast and ovarian cancers, emphasizing its oncogenic role through the ERK and AKT pathways.

Limitations and future directions

While our study offers important insights into the functional roles of EGFL6 in ESCA, several limitations merit explicit acknowledgment. First, the relatively small sample sizes of the TCGA and GEO datasets may not fully represent the high heterogeneity of ESCA. Second, the established EGFL6-related prognostic signature has not been validated in an independent external cohort, restricting its direct clinical application. Since all bioinformatic analyses were based on retrospective public data, future prospective multi-center studies with larger sample sizes are required to verify our conclusions. Third, our in vitro experiments were limited to a single ESCA cell line (KYSE140), which may compromise the generalizability of the functional results. In addition, the absence of in vivo experiments represents another major limitation, as in vivo models are essential for confirming the role of EGFL6 in ESCA tumorigenesis and progression. Future investigations should also clarify the precise molecular mechanisms underlying EGFL6-mediated regulation of tumor behavior and immune responses. Identifying its direct molecular targets, mapping its interaction network with cancer-related genes, and evaluating its therapeutic potential in systematic in vitro and in vivo studies will be particularly valuable. Collectively, these efforts will help overcome current limitations and promote the translational potential of EGFL6 as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target for ESCA.

Conclusions

In conclusion, our findings reveal that EGFL6 is significantly upregulated in ESCA and correlates with a range of clinicopathological factors, immune infiltration, and adverse outcomes in DSS and PFI. This suggests that EGFL6 may play a pivotal role in shaping the tumor microenvironment and facilitating immune evasion in ESCA. The potential mechanism by which EGFL6 exerts these effects may involve the regulation of key signaling pathways related to tumor cell proliferation, invasion, and immune modulation (e.g., PI3K/Akt and VEGF signaling pathways), though further experimental verification is needed to confirm this. Given its prognostic significance across various malignancies, EGFL6 emerges as a compelling candidate for targeted therapeutic strategies aimed at enhancing cancer management. Notably, targeting EGFL6 may not only inhibit tumor cell growth directly but also reverse immune suppression in the tumor microenvironment, thereby improving the efficacy of immunotherapy and chemotherapy in ESCA, which holds important clinical translation value for optimizing individualized treatment regimens.

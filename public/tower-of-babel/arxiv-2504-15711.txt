Abstract
This letter reports the result of the search for the decay μ + → e + ​ γ \mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\gammaup} undertaken at the Paul Scherrer Institut in Switzerland with the MEG II experiment using the data collected in the 2021–2022 physics runs. The sensitivity of this search is 2.2 × 10 − 13 2.2\text{\times}{10}^{-13} , a factor of 2.4 better than that of the full MEG dataset and obtained in a data taking period of about one fourth that of MEG, thanks to the superior performances of the new detector. The result is consistent with the expected background, yielding an upper limit on the branching ratio of ℬ ⁡ ( μ + → e + ​ γ ) < 1.5 × 10 − 13 {\cal B}({\muup^{+}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup})<$1.5\text{\times}{10}^{-13}$ ( 90 % 90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} C.L.). Additional improvements are expected with the data collected during the years 2023–2024. The data-taking will continue in the coming years.

In the standard model (SM) of particle physics, charged lepton flavour-violating (CLFV) processes are
almost forbidden, with extremely small branching ratio
(
\sim{10}^{-54}

[
1
]

when considering non-zero neutrino mass differences
and mixing angles. Hence, the experimental searches for such decays are free from SM contributions
and a positive signal would be unambiguous evidence for physics beyond the SM. Several SM extensions

[
2

3

predict experimentally accessible CLFV decay rates, with the channel
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\gammaup}
being particularly sensitive.

The most stringent upper limit on the
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\gammaup}
branching ratio was set by the MEG II collaboration,

{\cal B}({\muup^{+}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup})<$3.1\text{\times}{10}^{-13}\text{\,}$
(
90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
C.L.)
[
4
]

by combining the full dataset of the MEG experiment

[
5
]
with the dataset of the MEG II experiment collected in 2021.
This letter presents a new limit on
{\cal B}({\muup^{+}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup})
based on the analysis
of the data collected in 2022 combined with an update analysis
of the 2021 dataset.

1
Signal and background

A signal event is a back-to-back, monoenergetic,
time coincident photon-positron pair from a decay
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\gammaup}
at rest.
The energy of both photon and positron is half the muon mass (
52.83\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}
) for muons
decaying at rest.
The background consists of events from
radiative muon decay (RMD)
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\nuup\bar{\nuup}\gammaup}
and from accidental time coincidences of positrons
from muon Michel decay
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\nuup\bar{\nuup}}
with photons from RMD,
positron-electron annihilation-in-flight or bremsstrahlung (ACC).

The MEG II detector and its performance are described in
[
6
]
.
It consists of a positron spectrometer, formed by a cylindrical drift chamber (CDCH) and two
semicylindrical sectors of each 256 scintillator pixels,
one located upstream of the target and the other downstream (pTC),
placed inside a superconducting solenoid with a gradient magnetic field along the beam axis, and
of a liquid xenon (LXe) photon detector, outside the solenoid, viewed by VUV-sensitive SiPMs on the front face
and photomultiplier tubes (PMTs) on the other faces.
In addition, a radiative decay counter (RDC) is located downstream
centred on the beam axis, to identify the ACC events with an
RMD-originated high-energy
{\gammaup}
-ray by tagging the corresponding
low energy positron in coincidence.
It consists of a scintillating plastic detector to measure the positron timing and a LYSO
crystal calorimeter to measure the positron energy.
The muon stopping target is elliptical,
its position
x_{T}
and planarity are measured with a camera imaging dots printed on its surface and six holes bored in it.
A continuous monochromatic
\mathrm{\muup^{+}}
beam is delivered through a Beam Transport Solenoid onto the target where it is stopped.
The trigger for
\mathrm{\muup^{+}\to e^{+}\gammaup}
events is based on the online estimates of the

{\gammaup}
-ray energy

E_{\mathrm{\gammaup}}
, the relative time between the positron and the
{\gammaup}
-ray

t_{{\mathrm{e}^{+}}{\gammaup}}
from the LXe and the pTC,
and the positron–
{\gammaup}
-ray relative direction from the same detectors.

A right-handed, Cartesian coordinate system is adopted, with the
z
-axis along the beam direction and the
y
-axis vertical and pointing upward.
A polar spherical coordinate system is also used, with the
\theta
angles referred as usual to the beam axis (
z
) and the
\phi
angles lying in the
\left(x,y\right)
-plane.
For the LXe, we defined a local system of curvilinear coordinates
(u,v,w)
, where
u
and
v
are tangent to the cylindrical inner surface of the calorimeter (with
u
parallel to
z
) and
w
is the depth inside the LXe fiducial volume.

In each event, positron and
{\gammaup}

{E_{\mathrm{e^{+}}}}

(x_{\mathrm{e}^{+}},y_{\mathrm{e}^{+}},z_{\mathrm{e}^{+}})

on the muon stopping target and forward to the pTC. The interaction time of the track with the pTC is measured with the hit times of close pixels combined in a
cluster, to be corrected for the time of flight to obtain the positron production time
{t_{\mathrm{e^{+}}}}

for a total DAQ live time of

The average fraction of the DAQ live time with respect to the total

During 2022, the fraction improved from

96\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
(

,
$52.2\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$<{E_{\mathrm{e^{+}}}}<$53.5\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}$

,

,

a normalisation factor

and

until the analysis strategy was finalised.

-2.0\text{\,}\mathrm{ns}

5.1
Likelihood function

A fit is performed with the set of observables

\vec{x}=({E_{\mathrm{e^{+}}}},E_{\mathrm{\gammaup}},{t_{\mathrm{e^{+}\gammaup}}},{\theta_{\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}},{\phi_{\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}},t_{{\mathrm{e}^{+}},\rm{RDC}}-t_{\gamma,\mathrm{LXe}},E_{{\mathrm{e}^{+}},\mathrm{RDC}},n_{\mathrm{pTC}})
.
The number of hits on the pTC,
n_{\mathrm{pTC}}
, is also introduced to incorporate the difference in its distribution between signal and background, as well as the dependence of
{t_{\mathrm{e^{+}\gammaup}}}
resolution on
n_{\mathrm{pTC}}
.

The likelihood fit has four parameters: a floated parameter,
N_{\rm sig}
, and three constrained parameters.
N_{\rm BG},N_{\rm RMD},x_{\rm T}
.
The likelihood function is obtained from PDFs for the signal (
S(\vec{x})
), RMD (
R(\vec{x})
) and ACC background events (
A(\vec{x})
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(N_{\rm sig},N_{\rm RMD},N_{\rm ACC},x_{\rm T})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-(N_{\rm sig}+N_{\rm RMD}+N_{\rm ACC})}}{N_{\mathrm{obs}}!}C(N_{\rm RMD},N_{\rm ACC},x_{\rm T})\times

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{obs}}}\bigl(N_{\rm sig}S(\vec{x_{i}})+N_{\rm RMD}R(\vec{x_{i}})+N_{\rm ACC}A(\vec{x_{i}})\bigr)\;,

where
\vec{x_{i}}
is the set of the observables for the
i
-th event;

N_{\rm sig}
,
N_{\rm RMD}
and
N_{\rm ACC}
are the
numbers of signal, RMD and ACC background events in the analysis region;

x_{\rm T}
is a parameter representing the misalignment of the muon stopping target;

N_{\mathrm{obs}}
is the total number of events observed in the analysis region;

C(N_{\rm RMD},N_{\rm ACC},x_{\rm T})
is a constraint term on the nuisance parameters, a product
of three independent Gaussian functions, one for each parameter.

N_{\rm RMD}
is constrained by extrapolating the number of RMD events in the energy
side-band,
N_{\rm BG}
is constrained by counting the number of accidental events in
the timing sideband, and
x_{\rm T}
is constrained according to the
estimated alignment precision.

The signal PDFs are modelled according to the measured resolutions including non-Gaussian tails.
The ACC
{E_{\mathrm{e^{+}}}}
PDF is the convolution of the theoretical Michel spectrum with acceptance and resolution effects, fitted to data in the side-bands
[
7
]
.
The ACC
E_{\mathrm{\gammaup}}
PDF is obtained from the Monte Carlo spectrum,
with a Gaussian smearing and verified in the data.
The ACC angular PDFs are modelled with polynomials, fitted to data in the side-bands.
The RMD PDFs are obtained by convolving the theoretical spectra with the experimental resolutions.
The
n_{\mathrm{pTC}}
PDFs are taken from the side-bands for the ACC background, and from the Monte Carlo for signal and RMD.

As a cross-check, two independent analyses are performed with differently constructed PDFs:
“per-event PDFs” and “constant PDFs”.
In the “per-event PDF” approach, the PDF parameters depend on the single event allowing the maximisation of the sensitivity.
Here, the event by event difference is modelled with parameters that represent the reconstruction performance for each event.
The positron PDFs are modelled with the tracking precision estimated by a Kalman filter technique.
The
{\gammaup}
-ray PDFs depend on the conversion position;
the resolution of the
{\gammaup}
-ray measurement becomes worse when
{\gammaup}
-rays are converted near the end of the detectors fiducial volume.
In the other approach, “constant PDFs” are constructed by averaging such dependencies (except that different
{\gammaup}
-ray PDFs are used for
w_{\gammaup}<$2\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}$
and
w_{\gammaup}>$2\text{\,}\mathrm{cm}$
).
Moreover, the angle PDF is also modelled differently:
the stereo angle
{\Theta_{\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}}
between the positron and the
{\gammaup}
-ray directions is used instead of the two separate components,
{\phi_{\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}}
and
{\theta_{\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}}
and
the RDC observables are not used.

{\gammaup}

6
Conclusions and perspectives

This letter presents the result of a blind,
maximum-likelihood analysis
applied to the data collected in 2021–2022 by the MEG II experiment with a sensitivity of
{\cal S}_{90}=$2.2\text{\times}{10}^{-13}$
;
the improvement in sensitivity compared to
[
5
]
amounts to a factor 2.4.
The result is compatible with
the expected background and establishes a
90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
C.L. upper limit on the branching ratio

{\cal B}({\muup^{+}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup})<$1.5\text{\times}{10}^{-13}$
, which is the most stringent to date.
The MEG II collaboration has continued to take data during 2023 and 2024, with a
projected statistic 1.5-fold (1.3 (2023) + 0.2 (2024)) larger than in 2021–2022.
The collaboration plans to take data in the years 2025–2026 with an additional expected
2.6-fold increase in statistics, with the goal of reaching a sensitivity to the
{\muup^{+}\to\mathrm{e}^{+}\gammaup}
decay of
{\cal S}_{90}$\sim 6\text{\times}{10}^{-14}$
.

Single-Snapshot Gridless 2D-DoA Estimation for UCAs: A Joint Optimization Approach
Salar Nouri 1

Abstract
This paper tackles the challenging problem of gridless two-dimensional (2D) direction-of-arrival (DOA) estimation for a uniform circular array (UCA) from a single snapshot of data. Conventional gridless methods often fail in this scenario due to prohibitive computational costs or a lack of robustness. We propose a novel framework that overcomes these limitations by jointly estimating a manifold transformation matrix and the source azimuth-elevation pairs within a single, unified optimization problem. This problem is solved efficiently using an inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM), which completely circumvents the need for semidefinite programming. By unifying the objectives of data fidelity and transformation robustness, our approach is uniquely suited for the demanding single-snapshot case. Simulation results confirm that the proposed iALM framework provides robust and high-resolution, gridless 2D-DOA estimates, establishing its efficacy for challenging array signal processing applications.



I
Introduction

Direction-of-Arrival (DOA)
estimation is a cornerstone of array signal processing, with critical applications in
Radio Detection and Ranging (RADAR)
,
Sound Navigation and Ranging (SONAR)
, and wireless communications. While
one-dimensional (1D)

DOA
estimation is well-established, the joint estimation of azimuth and elevation angles in
two-dimensional (2D)

DOA
provides a far more complete spatial awareness. The
Uniform Circular Array (UCA)
is a popular choice for
2D
-
DOA
due to its full
360^{\circ}
azimuth coverage and isotropic response
[
1
,
2
]
. However, the problem becomes exceptionally challenging under the single-snapshot condition, where only a single vector of measurements is available
[
3
]
. This scenario is crucial for applications involving fast-moving targets or rapidly changing signal environments, yet it renders many classical estimation techniques ineffective.

Conventional high-resolution methods, such as
2D
-
MUltiple SIgnal Classification (MUSIC)
, rely on the eigen-decomposition of the sample covariance matrix
[
4
]
. With a single snapshot, this matrix becomes rank-one, making it impossible to distinguish the signal and noise subspaces. While pre-processing techniques like spatial smoothing can create a higher-rank matrix, they do so at the cost of a reduced effective array aperture
[
5
]
. To address these issues, methods based on
Compressive Sensing (CS)
reformulate
DOA
estimation as a sparse recovery problem
[
6
]
. However, grid-based techniques are fundamentally limited by basis mismatch, where their accuracy is compromised when true
DOAs
lie between predefined grid points. This motivated the development of gridless methods based on
Atomic Norm Minimization (ANM)
, which operate on a continuous angular domain
[
5
]
. For
2D
problems, however, the canonical
ANM
formulation requires solving a large-scale
Semidefinite Program (SDP)
, whose high computational complexity remains a significant barrier to practical application
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
.

For
UCAs
, prized for their complete azimuthal coverage, a popular strategy is to map the complex
UCA
manifold to a simpler virtual array manifold via a linear transformation matrix,
\mathbf{T}

[
9
]
. To solve the
2D
-
DOA
problem, this requires forming a virtual
uniform rectangular array (URA)
whose separable Kronecker structure is amenable to efficient estimators, such as ESPRIT. The success of this entire approach, however, hinges critically on the quality and robustness of the matrix
\mathbf{T}
.

Analytical methods, such as the well-known Davies transformation
[
10
]
, are fundamentally ill-suited for this task. They are derived under ideal, noise-free conditions and are notoriously fragile; their construction involves inverting Bessel functions that are often near-zero, leading to severe noise amplification and unreliable estimates
[
11
]
.

Recognizing this fragility, some research has focused on designing a better
\mathbf{T}
as a separate, offline optimization problem
[
12
]
. These methods typically seek to minimize the Frobenius norm of
\mathbf{T}
(to control noise gain) subject to manifold approximation error constraints. While an improvement, this offline approach is fundamentally limited. The resulting matrix is static and data-independent, unable to adapt to the specific signal and noise characteristics of the received snapshot. This sequential ’design-then-estimate’ methodology is inherently sub-optimal, as any residual error from the fixed transformation stage is inevitably propagated to the final
DOA
estimation step.

This creates a critical gap in the literature: a framework is needed that can, from a single snapshot, jointly design a robust transformation to a virtual
URA
and perform gridless
2D
-
DOA
estimation, all without the prohibitive cost of a full-scale
SDP
.

In this paper, we bridge this gap by proposing a novel framework that achieves this unified goal. Our main contributions are:

•

We formulate the single-snapshot
2D
-
DOA
task as a unified optimization problem. This formulation jointly designs a data-adaptive transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}
and estimates the gridless source locations by integrating data fidelity, matrix norm regularization for noise control, and semi-infinite constraints to ensure manifold accuracy across the entire
2D
angular domain.

•

We propose a computationally practical solver based on the
Inexact Augmented Lagrangian Method (iALM)
to address this large-scale, non-convex problem efficiently. It solves for the transformation matrix and the source locations simultaneously, completely bypassing the need for any
SDP
solvers, making it computationally practical for the single-snapshot scenario.

•

We demonstrate through extensive simulations that the proposed framework achieves superior accuracy and resolution compared to benchmark techniques. Our method provides robust, high-resolution estimates of paired azimuth and elevation angles, particularly in the challenging single-snapshot, low-
signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)
regime.

II
System Model and Problem Formulation

II-A

2D System and Single-Snapshot Signal Model

We consider a
UCA
with
N
omnidirectional sensor elements located in the
x
-
y
plane
[
3
]
. The position of the
n
-th element is described by the vector
\mathbf{p}_{n}=[r\cos(\gamma_{n}),r\sin(\gamma_{n}),0]^{T}
, where
r
is the array radius and
\gamma_{n}=2\pi(n-1)/N
for
n=1,\dots,N
.

Assume that
K
uncorrelated, narrow-band signals impinge on the array from far-field sources
[
13
]
. The
2D
-
DOA
for the
k
-th source is described by an azimuth-elevation pair
(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})
, where
\theta_{k}\in[-\pi,\pi)
is the azimuth and
\phi_{k}\in[0,\pi/2]
is the elevation angle measured from the
z
-axis. The array’s response to a signal from a generic direction
(\theta,\phi)
is captured by the steering vector
\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, whose
n
-th component is given by:

[\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)]_{n}=\exp\left(j\frac{2\pi r}{\lambda}\sin(\phi)\cos(\theta-\gamma_{n})\right).

(1)

We consider the challenging single-snapshot case. The received
N\times 1
data vector
\mathbf{y}
is modeled as:

\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{A}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Phi})\mathbf{s}+\mathbf{n},

(2)

where:

•

\mathbf{A}(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Phi})=[\mathbf{a}(\theta_{1},\phi_{1}),\dots,\mathbf{a}(\theta_{K},\phi_{K})]
is the
N\times K
steering matrix, with
(\bm{\Theta},\bm{\Phi})
being the set of true
DOAs
.

•

\mathbf{s}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}
is the vector of complex source signal amplitudes at the time of the snapshot.

•

\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
is the additive white Gaussian noise vector, with elements drawn from
\mathcal{CN}(0,\sigma^{2})
.

II-B

Problem Formulation

The fundamental problem is to jointly estimate the set of unknown arrival angle pairs
\{(\theta_{k},\phi_{k})\}_{k=1}^{K}
from the single measurement vector
\mathbf{y}
.

II-C

Preliminaries for Joint Transformation and Estimation

Our approach is centered on transforming the complex
UCA
manifold into a simpler, separable one. We seek a linear
UCA
transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}
that maps the
UCA
steering vector
\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)
to a virtual
URA
steering vector
\mathbf{b}(\theta,\phi)\in\mathbb{C}^{M}

[
9
]
. An ideal virtual
URA
manifold possesses a separable Kronecker structure, which is key to efficiently solving the
2D
problem:

\mathbf{b}(\theta,\phi)=\mathbf{b}_{y}(\phi)\otimes\mathbf{b}_{x}(\theta),

(3)

where
\mathbf{b}_{x}(\theta)\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{x}}
and
\mathbf{b}_{y}(\phi)\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{y}}
are standard
1D
virtual
Uniform Linear Array (ULA)
steering vectors with
M=M_{x}\times M_{y}
. This structure allows the
2D
estimation problem to be decoupled
[
14
,
15
]
.

The core of our method is to find a matrix
\mathbf{T}
that satisfies the approximation
\mathbf{T}\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)\approx\mathbf{b}(\theta,\phi)
in a robust manner. Instead of using a fixed, pre-computed
\mathbf{T}
, which would be sub-optimal and propagate errors, our framework solves for the optimal
\mathbf{T}
and the
DOA
information simultaneously. This approach avoids the high computational cost of standard
2D

ANM
while providing a transformation matrix tailored to the specific data snapshot, thereby maximizing robustness and accuracy. The details of this unified optimization problem are presented in the following section.

III
Proposed Methodology: A Unified
iALM
Framework

We depart from the conventional two-stage approach and propose a novel framework that, from a single snapshot, simultaneously designs the robust transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}
and estimates the
2D

DOAs
. This is achieved by formulating a single, unified optimization problem that balances three competing objectives:

1.

Data Fidelity:
The transformed signal must accurately represent the single-snapshot measurement vector.

2.

Manifold Accuracy:
The transformed
UCA
manifold
\mathbf{T}\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)
must be a close approximation of the ideal virtual
URA
manifold
\mathbf{b}(\theta,\phi)
across the entire
2D
angular domain.

3.

Robustness:
The norm of the transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}
must be controlled to prevent noise amplification.

This unified problem is then solved efficiently using the
iALM

[
16
]
, which circumvents the need for any computationally expensive
SDP
solvers.

III-A

The Unified
2D
Optimization Problem

Let the clean signal component of the transformed data be denoted by the vector
\mathbf{x}_{v}\in\mathbb{C}^{M}
. Our goal is to jointly find the optimal transformation matrix
\mathbf{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}
and the signal vector
\mathbf{x}_{v}
by solving the following unified problem:

\displaystyle(\mathrm{P_{final}}):

\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{T},\mathbf{x}_{v}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{T}\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}_{v}\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{T}\|\mathbf{T}\|_{F}^{2}+\lambda_{X}\|\mathbf{x}_{v}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2D}}

(4)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}

\displaystyle|[\mathbf{T}\mathbf{a}(\theta,\phi)]_{m}-b_{m}(\theta,\phi)|\leq\epsilon_{m},\;\forall(\theta,\phi),\;\forall m.

Here,
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2D}}
is the atomic norm defined over the continuous
2D
atomic set of virtual
URA
steering vectors
\mathcal{A}_{2D}=\{\mathbf{b}(\theta,\phi)c\mid(\theta,\phi)\in[-\pi,\pi)\times[0,\pi/2],|c|=1\}

[
8
]
. Problem (
4
) is a large-scale, non-convex optimization problem with intractable semi-infinite constraints
[
17
]
, which we now address.

III-B

Tractable Reformulation for the
iALM
Solver

To solve (
4
) with
iALM
, we must reformulate its non-smooth and semi-infinite components into a set of equality and simple inequality constraints.

III-B
1
Reformulating the
2D
Atomic Norm

The
2D
atomic norm can be expressed as the optimal value of an
SDP
involving an
multilevel Toeplitz (MLT)
structure
[
18
]
. This is equivalent to:

\|\mathbf{x}_{v}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{2D}}=\min_{\mathbf{U},\mathbf{V}}\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{B}(\mathbf{U}))+\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{D}(\mathbf{V}))\right)

(5)

subject to the
Linear Matrix Inequality (LMI)
:

\mathbf{Z}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{B}(\mathbf{U})&\mathbf{x}_{v}\\
\mathbf{x}_{v}^{H}&\mathcal{T}_{D}(\mathbf{V})\end{pmatrix}\succeq 0,

(6)

where
\mathcal{T}_{B}(\cdot)
and
\mathcal{T}_{D}(\cdot)
are operators that construct a block-Toeplitz matrix and a block-Diagonal matrix (with Toeplitz blocks), respectively, from the matrix variables
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{V}
, to handle the
LMI
constraint, we apply the Burer-Monteiro factorization, replacing the large positive semidefinite matrix
\mathbf{Z}
with a low-rank factorization
[
19
]
:

\mathbf{Z}=\mathbf{W}\mathbf{W}^{H},\quad\text{where }\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{(M+1)\times p}.

(7)

Where
\mathbf{W}\in\mathbb{C}^{(M+1)\times p}
is the new optimization variable, and
p
is a small rank parameter (e.g.,
p\approx K
). This step is crucial as it replaces the computationally difficult
LMI
constraint with a set of quadratic equality constraints that define the original variables in terms of
\mathbf{W}

[
20
]
. By partitioning
\mathbf{W}
as
\mathbf{W}=[\mathbf{W}_{B}^{T},\mathbf{w}_{s}^{T}]^{T}
, where
\mathbf{W}_{B}\in\mathbb{C}^{p\times M}
and
\mathbf{w}_{s}\in\mathbb{C}^{p}
, we can express the signal and trace terms as functions of
\mathbf{W}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}_{v}=\mathbf{W}_{B}^{T}\mathbf{w}_{s},

(8)

\displaystyle\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{B}(\mathbf{U}))=\text{Tr}(\mathbf{W}_{B}\mathbf{W}_{B}^{H}),

(9)

\displaystyle\quad\text{Tr}(\mathcal{T}_{D}(\mathbf{V}))=\text{Tr}(\mathbf{w}_{s}\mathbf{w}_{s}^{H})=\|\mathbf{w}_{s}\|_{2}^{2}.

(10)

2
Final
iALM
Problem Structure

After discretizing the
2D
angular domain into a fine grid and introducing slack variables
\mathbf{s}
for the manifold constraints, we arrive at the final
iALM
-ready formulation.

•

Optimization Variables (
\mathbf{z}
):
The full set of variables is vectorized into
\mathbf{z}=[\text{vec}(\mathbf{T})^{T},\text{vec}(\mathbf{W})^{T},\mathbf{s}^{T}]^{T}
.

•

Differentiable Objective (
f(\mathbf{z})
):
The objective is now smooth and contains the data fidelity term, the robustness regularizer, and the trace terms, all expressed as functions of
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{W}
.

•

Equality Constraints (
A(\mathbf{z})=0
):
These include the discretized manifold accuracy constraints and the quadratic constraints from the Burer-Monteiro factorization.

•

Simple Convex Term (
g(\mathbf{z})
):
This term handles the non-negativity of the slack variables,
g(\mathbf{z})=\delta_{\geq 0}(\mathbf{s})
, whose proximal operator is a simple projection.

III-C

Primal Subproblem Solution via Block Coordinate Descent

The primal update step in Algorithm
1
requires finding an approximate solution to the Augmented Lagrangian subproblem. We solve this efficiently using a
Block Coordinate Descent (BCD)
method, which cyclically minimizes over one block of variables while keeping others fixed.

1. Update for
\mathbf{T}
:
With
\mathbf{W}
and
\mathbf{s}
fixed, the subproblem for
\mathbf{T}
simplifies to a quadratically regularized least-squares problem, which has a closed-form solution.

2. Update for
\mathbf{W}
:
With
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{s}
fixed, the subproblem for
\mathbf{W}
is also a quadratic problem, which can be solved efficiently.

3. Update for
\mathbf{s}
:
With
\mathbf{T}
and
\mathbf{W}
fixed, the subproblem for the slack variables
\mathbf{s}
involves the non-negative indicator
g(\mathbf{s})
. The optimal
\mathbf{s}
is found via a simple projection onto the non-negative orthant (i.e., taking the element-wise maximum with zero), which is the proximal operator of
g(\mathbf{s})
.

This
BCD
procedure is guaranteed to converge to a stationary point of the primal subproblem.

III-D

Algorithm Convergence

The convergence of the proposed
iALM
framework is established by monitoring the objective function and the feasibility of the iterates. For our formulation, where the equality constraints are denoted by a function
\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{z})=\mathbf{0}
, the feasibility gap at iteration
k
is defined as the norm of the constraint violation,
\|\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)})\|
. The objective of the
iALM
is to minimize the augmented Lagrangian, which simultaneously drives the objective function towards a minimum and the feasibility gap towards zero. The monotonic decrease of both metrics, as demonstrated empirically in our numerical results (Fig.
1
), provides strong evidence that the algorithm converges to a stationary and feasible point of the unified optimization problem.

Computational Complexity Analysis

To substantiate the claim of computational practicality, we analyze the complexity of the proposed JADE-
iALM
framework in contrast to benchmark methods. The complexity is primarily a function of the physical array size
N
, the virtual array size
M
, the number of sources
K
(approximated by the rank parameter
p\approx K
), and the grid sizes for spectral search (
N_{\theta},N_{\phi}
) or discretization (
G
).

The complexity of JADE-iALM is dominated by the
BCD
updates within each
iALM
iteration. Let
N_{\text{iter}}
and
N_{\text{bcd}}
denote the number of outer
iALM

BCD

•

Update for T:
Solving this quadratically regularized least-squares problem is dominated by matrix-matrix multiplications, with a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(M^{2}N+MN^{2})
.

•

Update for W:
This quadratic subproblem involves the
(M+1)\times p
matrix
\mathbf{W}
and has a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(M^{2}p+Mp^{2})
.

•

Update for s:
This is a simple projection onto the non-negative orthant, with a complexity linear in the number of slack variables,
\mathcal{O}(G)

The total complexity for JADE-iALM is therefore
\mathcal{O}(N_{\text{iter}}N_{\text{bcd}}(M^{2}N+MN^{2}+M^{2}p+Mp^{2}+G))

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we addressed the challenging problem of single-snapshot, gridless
2D
-
DOA
estimation for
UCAs
. We proposed a novel framework that formulates the task as a single, unified optimization problem. This approach simultaneously designs a robust transformation matrix for a virtual
URAs
. It estimates the source locations, integrating data fidelity, manifold accuracy, and robustness into one coherent objective.

The core of our contribution is a computationally practical solver based on the
iALM
. By leveraging
iALM
, we efficiently solve the complex, non-convex unified problem, completely bypassing the need for large-scale
SDP
that typically renders standard
2D
gridless methods intractable. This joint optimization strategy yields a transformation matrix that is uniquely adapted to the specific data snapshot, providing superior robustness against noise.

Simulation results confirmed that the proposed JADE-
iALM
framework achieves significantly higher accuracy in estimating paired azimuth and elevation angles, offering better resolution than benchmark techniques. The work establishes a powerful and efficient paradigm for robust, single-shot
2D
array signal processing. Future work will explore extending this simultaneous optimization approach to other array geometries and more challenging wideband signal environments.

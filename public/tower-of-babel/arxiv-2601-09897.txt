Birman–Hilden theory for big mapping class groups
Nestor Colin, Rubén A. Hidalgo, Rita Jiménez Rolland, Israel Morales, Saúl Quispe

Abstract
. Let S S and X X be two connected topological surfaces without boundary, and assume that S S is either of infinite type or has negative Euler characteristic. In this paper, we prove that if p : S → X p:S\rightarrow X is a fully ramified branched covering map, then p p satisfies the Birman–Hilden property . This generalizes a theorem of Winarski, and the known results in the literature, to the context of surfaces of infinite type and branched covering maps of infinite degree. As an application, we show that the mapping class group (respectively, the braid group on k k -strands) of a non-orientable surface of infinite type can be realized as a subgroup of the mapping class group (respectively, the braid group on 2 ​ k 2k -strands) of its orientable double cover.

1.
Introduction

The study of the interaction between mapping class groups through branched covers has its foundation in the work of Birman and Hilden in the 1970s
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
. They established a criterion, now known as the
Birman-Hilden property
, which provides a connection between certain subgroups of the mapping class groups of the total and base spaces of a branched cover. Specifically, a branched covering map
p:S\to X
satisfies the
Birman-Hilden property
if every fiber-preserving homeomorphism of
S
that is isotopic to the identity admits an isotopy through fiber-preserving homeomorphisms. In Section
2.3
, we review some equivalent formulations of this property for branched covers of topological surfaces, and its interpretation in terms of mapping class groups.

The purpose of this article is to extend the known results in the literature to the context of surfaces of infinite type and certain branched covering maps of infinite degree. Recall that a topological surface is of
finite type
if its fundamental group is finitely generated, and of
infinite type
otherwise.

Let us consider a branched covering map
p:S\rightarrow X
of surfaces. We say that
p
is
fully ramified
if for every branch point
b\in X
of
p
, every point
\tilde{b}
in the fiber
p^{-1}(b)
is a critical point, i.e., the ramification number of
p
at
\tilde{b}
is at least two. Our main result applies to fully ramified branched covering maps.

Theorem 1

(Birman–Hilden property for big mapping class groups)
.

Let
S
and
X
be two connected topological surfaces without boundary, where
S
is either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. If
p:S\to X
is a fully ramified branched covering map, then
p
has the Birman-Hilden property.

As regular branched covering and unbranched covering maps are fully ramified, the above provides the following.

Corollary 2
.

Let
S
and
X
be two connected topological surfaces without boundary, where
S
is either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. If
p:S\to X
is either a regular branched covering or an unbranched covering map, then
p
has the Birman-Hilden property.

We prove Theorem
1
by generalizing the strategy of Winarski’s proof of
[
33
, Theorem 1]
; see also
[
18
, Section 9.4.3]
. Our approach uses combinatorial topology of curves on surfaces and relies on the Alexander method for finite and infinite type surfaces. A key step that allows us to include infinite-sheeted branch covers in our proof is Lemma
3.8
; it gives a description of the structure of branched covers from the closed annulus and the closed infinite strip to a compact surface with two boundary components. Furthermore, we work on the category of Klein surfaces to obtain some auxiliary lemmas. This allows us to consider a unified argument which includes infinite-sheeted branched covering maps, and surfaces of finite and infinite type, orientable and non-orientable.

1.1.
Relation with known results in the literature

Under the assumption that the surfaces are closed, orientable and hyperbolic, Winarski proved in
[
33
, Theorem 1]
that fully ramified branched coverings maps (so necessarily finite-sheeted) satisfy the Birman-Hilden property.
Her result recovers the case of regular branched covers originally due to Birman and Hilden
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
, to Zieschang
[
36

, and to Maclachlan and Harvey
[

, and the case of unbranched covers proved by Aramayona, Leininger and Souto
[

. The work of Birman and Hilden also includes regular unbranched covers between closed surfaces that may be non-orientable, including the orientable double cover of a closed non-orientable surface; see
[
11

. Furthermore, Atalan and Medetogullari
[
5

extended Winarski’s result for fully ramified branched covers of non-orientable closed hyperbolic surfaces using the orientable double cover. A survey of the work of Birman and Hilden, and a brief history of the evolution of this theory for finite-sheeted branched covers can be found in
[
30
]
; see also references therein.

For the case of infinite-sheeted branched covers, a result where the Birman–Hilden property has been explicitly proved is the following. Let

be the
Jacob ladder
and

be the
handle shift

[
17

, Dey, Dhanwani, Patil, and Rajeevsarathy showed that the induced regular unbranched covering map

induced by the action of

.

On the other hand, as we explain in Section

, the original argument of Birman and Hilden
[
11

,
[
12
, Theorems 1 and 2]
can be combined with the Alexander method to give a proof of the Birman–Hilden property for unbranched regular covers
p:S\to X
over a non-compact hyperbolic base surface
X
.
This includes the possibility for the surface
X
to be non-orientable or of infinite type, and for the cover
p
to have infinitely many sheets.

All of the above mentioned results are particular cases of Theorem
1
.

Remark 1.1
.

In
[
33
, Proposition 1]
, Winarski gives a necessary condition, the
weak curve lifting property
, for when a finite-sheeted branched covering map
p:S\to X
satisfies the Birman–Hilden property. She applies this condition to obtain examples of non-fully ramified covering maps with finitely many sheets that do not satisfy the Birman–Hilden property. To stablish the weak lifting property, Winarsky uses that the
liftable mapping class group
(see Definition
2.7
) is a finite index subgroup of the mapping class group, which is true for finite-sheeted branched covers over a surface
X
of finite type. When the branched cover has infinitely many sheets or the surface
X
is of infinite type her argument cannot be replicated. This motivates the following question:
is there a necessary condition, like the curve lifting property, for when an infinite-sheeted branched cover satisfies the Birman–Hilden property?

Some applications to geometrically characteristic covers

Our Corollary

Let

Our Proposition

, recovers and extends

Corollary 3

(Injections between mapping class groups)
.

Let
S
and
X
be connected topological surfaces without boundary, where
S
is of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. Let
p\colon S\to X
be an unbranched geometrically characteristic covering map. Fix some
x\in X
and some
\widetilde{x}\in p^{-1}(x)
. Then the homomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;\{x\})\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(S;p^{-1}(x)),\quad[f]\mapsto[\widetilde{f}]

is injective, where
\widetilde{f}
is the unique lift of

satisfying
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{x}
.

An example of a geometrically characteristic cover is given by the

\Sigma^{\text{ab}}
of a surface
\Sigma
. It is the unbranched regular cover

corresponding to the commutator subgroup of the fundamental group of
\Sigma
. If
\Sigma
is a connected surface either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic, then the covering map

has infinitely many sheets and satisfies the Birman–Hilden property by our Corollary

. Moreover, the

of a geometric characteristic cover is precisely the whole mapping class group of the base surface of the cover; see Proposition

. Therefore, it follows from the short exact sequence (

) that

is isomorphic to a quotient of a subgroup of

\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(\Sigma^{\text{ab}})
with respect to
p
(see Definition
2.7
).

Corollary 4

(Mapping class groups as quotients)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a surface of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic, and let
\Sigma^{\text{ab}}
be its universal abelian cover. Then

\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma)\cong\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(\Sigma^{\text{ab}})/H_{1}(\Sigma;\mathbb{Z}).

When
\Sigma
is orientable, Basmajian and the second author
[
7

obtained the homeomorphism type of its universal abelian cover

; see also
[

is the

. The universal abelian cover of the remaining surfaces is the

.
In particular, the universal abelian cover of

is again

. It is interesting to see that, by Corollary

On the other hand, the universal abelian cover

As a consequence of Corollary

Another interesting geometrically characteristic cover is the

Let

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Mapping class group of surfaces

Let
\Sigma
be a connected topological surface and let
B
be a closed and discrete subset in the interior of
\Sigma
. Here we consider the set
B
as
marked points
in the surface
\Sigma
. The boundary (possibly empty) of
\Sigma
is denoted by
\partial\Sigma
, and we always assume that
\partial\Sigma
is compact.

If
\Sigma
is non-orientable, we denote by
\Homeo(\Sigma;B)
the group of all homeomorphisms
f:\Sigma\to\Sigma
that preserve
B
as a set and restrict to the identity on
\partial\Sigma
. If
\Sigma
is orientable,
\Homeo(\Sigma;B)
denotes the group of all orientation-preserving homeomorphisms of
f:\Sigma\to\Sigma
that preserve the set
B
and restrict to the identity on
\partial\Sigma
.

The
mapping class group
of
\Sigma

relative to

B
, denoted by
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma;B)
, is the group of all isotopy classes of elements in
\Homeo(\Sigma;B)
; we require that all homeomorphisms and
isotopies preserve
B
and fix
\partial\Sigma
pointwise. We have the natural projection map

(2.1)

\rho=\rho_{\Sigma;B}:\Homeo(\Sigma;B)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma;B),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ f\mapsto[f].

If the set of marked points
B
is empty, we simply write
\Homeo(\Sigma)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma)
.

2.2.
The Alexander method

A
simple closed curve
in a surface
\Sigma
is an embedding of the unitary circle into
\Sigma
. A simple closed curve in
\Sigma
is
essential
if it does not bound a disc, a once-punctured disc, an annulus or a Möbius strip. We say that
\Sigma
is
sporadic
if it has no essential simple closed curves. One can verify that a surface
\Sigma
with empty boundary is sporadic if and only if
\Sigma
is homeomorphic to either a sphere with at most three punctures or a projective plane with at most one puncture.

Definition 2.1

(Alexander system)
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a collection of essential simple closed curves on a surface
\Sigma
with empty boundary. We say that
\Gamma
is an
Alexander system
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

(minimal position)
the elements in
\Gamma
are in pairwise minimal position
1
1

1

Two simple close curves are in
minimal position
it they do not form a
bigon
, see
[
18
, Section 1.2.4]
for details.
,

(2)

(distinct isotopy classes)
for
\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\in\Gamma
with
i\neq j
, we have that
\gamma_{i}
is not isotopic to
\gamma_{j}
,

(3)

(no triple intersections)
for all distinct
i,j,k\in\mathbb{N}
, at least one of the following sets is empty:
\gamma_{i}\cap\gamma_{j}
,
\gamma_{j}\cap\gamma_{k}
,
\gamma_{k}\cap\gamma_{i}
and,

(4)

(local finiteness)
for any finite type subsurface
S
of
\Sigma
, up to isotopy,
S
interects a finite number of curves in
\Gamma
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a collection of curves on a surface
\Sigma
with empty boundary. We say that
\mathscr{A}

fills

\Sigma
if
\Sigma\setminus\mathscr{A}
is the disjoint union of open discs and once-punctured open discs.

Definition 2.2

(k-stable Alexander system)
.

Let
\Gamma
be an Alexander system on a surface
\Sigma
with empty boundary and
k\geqslant 1
. We say
\Gamma
is a
k
-
stable Alexander system
if
\Gamma
fills
\Sigma
and every

that preserves the isotopy classes of elements in

satisfies that

is isotopic to the identity.

If
\Sigma
is an orientable finite type surface, in
[
18
, Proposition 2.8]
it is shown that any Alexander system that fills
\Sigma
is
k
-stable for some
k
. The proof also works in the setting of non-orientable finite type surfaces. Moreover, for any finite type surface it is possible to exhibit
k
-stable Alexander systems for

at most two. Examples of
1
-stable Alexander systems are exhibited in
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
for orientable infinite type surfaces and in
[
22

for non-orientable infinite type surfaces. The existence of

and will be an important ingredient in the proof of our main result Theorem
1
.

Theorem 2.3

(Alexander’s method for surfaces)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a non-sporadic topological surface with empty boundary.

(1)

If
\Sigma
is of finite type, then it has a
2
-stable Alexander system.

(2)

If
\Sigma

See also
[

2.3.
Birman-Hilden theory for branched covers

Let us recall the terminology needed to define the Birman–Hilden property and some equivalent statements.

Definition 2.4

(Branched covering map)
.

Let
S
and
X
be topological surfaces and let

p:S\rightarrow X
be a surjective continuous map.

(1)

We say that the map
p
is a
branched covering map
or
branched cover
if there exists a closed and discrete subset
B\subset\text{int }X
, which might be empty, called the
branch locus
of
p
, with the following properties:

a)

p|_{S^{\ast}}:S^{\ast}\to X^{\ast}
is a covering map where
S^{\ast}:=S\setminus p^{-1}(B)
and
X^{\ast}:=X\setminus B
, and

b)

each point
b\in B
has a neighborhood
U_{b}
, homeomorphic to an open disk, such that
p^{-1}(U_{b})
is a disjoint union of a collection
\{V_{j}\}_{j\in J}
of open sets, each one homeomorphic to an open disk cointaining exactly one point
\tilde{b}_{j}\in p^{-1}(b)
, and
p|_{V_{j}}:V_{j}\to U_{b}
is a finite branched cover for each
j\in J
, that is, it is topologically equivalent to a branched cover
z\in\mathbb{D}^{2}\mapsto z^{m_{j}}\in\mathbb{D}^{2}
, where
m_{j}\geq 1
(with at least one of them bigger or equal to two) is an integer called the
ramification number of
p
at
\tilde{b}_{j}
.

(2)

If the set
B
is empty, we say that
p
is
unbranched
.

(3)

If
p
is a branched covering map whose restriction
p|_{S^{\ast}}:S^{\ast}\to X^{\ast}
is a regular covering map, then we say that
p
is
a
regular branched covering map
.

(4)

The
Deck group
of
p
, denoted by
\mathrm{Deck}(p)
, is the group of deck transformations of the covering map
p|_{S^{\ast}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The branch locus
B
of a branched covering map
p:S\rightarrow X
is closed and discrete in
X
. Therefore,
p^{-1}(B)
is also discrete and closed in
S
. Each point of
B
is called a
branch point
of
p
.

Definition 2.6

(Fully ramified cover)
.

Let
p:S\rightarrow X
be a branched covering map. An element in
p^{-1}(B)
is a
critical point
if its ramification number is at least two; otherwise, it is called an
unramified point
. We say that
p
is
fully ramified
if each element in the preimage of the branch locus of
p
is a critical point.

Let
p:S\to X
be a branched covering map of topological surfaces. We say that a function
f:S\to S
is
fiber-preserving
if for each
x\in X
there is
y\in X
so that
f(p^{-1}(x))=p^{-1}(y)
. In this situation, there is a function
\bar{f}:X\to X
such that
\bar{f}\circ p=p\circ f
. This means also that
\bar{f}(B)\subset B
.
If
f
is a homeomorphism, then
\bar{f}
is also a homeomorphism preserving
B
.

Let
\mathrm{SHomeo}(S)
denote the group of all fiber-preserving homeomorphisms of
S
. We call this group the
symmetric homeomorphism group
of
S
with respect to
p:S\to X
(in what
follows, whenever
p
is clear, we will just say
symmetric homeomorphism group
). On the other hand, denote by
\mathrm{LHomeo}(X;B)
the group of all
liftable
homeomorphisms of
X
that preserve the branch locus
B
. As any fiber-preserving homeomorphism of
S
projects under
p
to a homeomorphism of
X
that preserves the branch locus
B
, and any liftable homeomorphism of
X
(so preserving
B
) defines a fiber-preserving homeomorphism of
S
, there is a well-defined surjective group homomorphism

(2.2)

\widetilde{\Phi}:\mathop{\mathrm{SHomeo}}(S)\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;B)

and a short exact sequence of groups

(2.3)

1\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{SHomeo}}(S)\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\Phi}}\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;B)\rightarrow 1.

Definition 2.7

(Symmetric and liftable mapping class group)
.

Let
p:S\to X
be a branched covering map of topological surfaces. We define the
symmetric mapping class group
of
S
by

\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S):=\rho_{S}(\mathop{\mathrm{SHomeo}}(S)),

and the
liftable mapping class group
of
X
by

\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B):=\rho_{X;B}(\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;B)).

Here
\rho_{S}
and
\rho_{X,B}
denote the corresponding projection from the homeomorphism group of the surface to its mapping class groups as defined in (
2.1
). Through this paper, we denote by
D
to the image of
\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p)
under
\rho_{S}
, i.e.,
D:=\rho_{S}(\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p))
.

Notice that two homeomorphisms of
S
are identified in
\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S)
if they diﬀer by an isotopy that is not necessarily ﬁber-preserving. Therefore, the group of homomorphims defined in (
2.2
)
\widetilde{\Phi}\colon\mathop{\mathrm{SHomeo}}(S)\to\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;B)
a priori does not induce a well-defined homomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S)\to\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)
.
Birman-Hilden theory
studies which conditions on the branch covering map
p
ensure the existence of a well-defined homomorphism
\Phi
.

Definition 2.8

(Birman–Hilden property)
.

Let
p:S\to X
be a branched covering map between topological surfaces. Then
p
has the
Birman–Hilden property
if every fiber-preserving homeomorphism of
S
that is isotopic to
Id_{S}
is also isotopic to
Id_{S}
through fiber-preserving homeomorphisms, where
Id_{S}
denotes the identity map on
S
.

Remark 2.9
.

Some examples of branched covers that do not satisfy the Birman–Hilden property are given below. See also
[

and references therein.

(1)

Let

(2)

Another example can be constructed from the orientable double cover of the Klein bottle

. Define the involution

\sigma(z,w)=(e^{i\pi}z,\overline{w}),

for all

, where

denotes the complex conjugation. Then
\mathbb{K}=T^{2}/\langle\sigma\rangle
and the projection map
p:T^{2}\to\mathbb{K}
is a covering map. Now define

f(z,w):=\begin{cases}(z,z^{2}w),&\text{if }\Im(z)\leqslant 0,\\
(z,{\overline{z}}^{2}w),&\text{if }\Im(z)\geqslant 0.\end{cases}

Here
\Im(z)
denotes the imaginary part of

. Note that
f
commutes with
\sigma
and it is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism of

. Moreover,

Klein surfaces and auxiliary results

In this section, we prove some auxiliary results that are needed to obtain Theorem

3.1.
Klein surfaces and some double covers

A
Klein surface
is a topological surface
\Sigma
, possibly with non-empty boundary, with a
dianalytic
structure, that is, a structure in which the transition functions are either analytic or anti-analytic.
Since we are considering surfaces possibly with non-empty boundary, the notion of dianalyticity of a function is taken in its extended form admitting open subsets of the positive hyperbolic semi-plane
\mathbb{H}^{+}:=\{a+bi\in\mathbb{C}\mid b\geq 0\}
as domain. A Klein surface might be orientable or not. If it is orientable, and the dianalytic structure is such that all changes of coordinates are analytic, then it is called a
Riemann surface
. We refer the reader to see
[
2
, Chapter 1]
for a preliminary study of Klein surfaces and morphisms between Klein surfaces. Given a Klein surface
\Sigma
, we denote by
\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
the
automorphism group
of
\Sigma
.

Any topological surface, possibly with non-empty boundary, admits a Klein surface structure. It is a consequence of the existence of isothermal coordinates in dimension two, see for instance
[
24
, Section 1.5.1 and Theorem 4.24]
.

By the Poincaré-Koebe Uniformization Theorem, every Klein surface
\Sigma
can be represented as
\mathscr{U}/\Gamma
where
\mathscr{U}
is a simply connected Riemann surface and
\Gamma
is a discrete subgroup of isometries of
\mathscr{U}
, without elliptic elements, that might contain reflections whose set of fixed points are projected to the boundary components of
\Sigma

\Sigma
is non-orientable or it has non-empty boundary, then
\Gamma
necessarily has anti-analytic elements. By a slight abuse of notation, we will often refer to the Klein surface simply as
\Sigma
.

The following constructions will be used in our arguments below.

The complex double cover
. Suppose
\Sigma={\mathscr{U}}/\Gamma
is non-orientable or
\partial\Sigma
is not empty. Then
\Gamma
must have anti-analytic automorphisms. Let

be the index two subgroup of
\Gamma
consisting of the analytic elements of
\Gamma
. The quotient space

is a Riemann surface without boundary, and the inclusion

induces a degree two, possibly branched, covering map

, with Deck group generated by an anti-analytic involution
\tau:R\to R
. This involution has fixed points if and only if
\Sigma
has a non-empty boundary. In particular, the cover
f
is unbranched outside the image under
f
of the set of fixed points of
\tau
. The surface

is called the
complex double cover
of
\Sigma
; for further details we refer the reader to the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 1.6.1]
. We will use the complex double cover of a Klein surface in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

If
\Sigma
is non-orientable and

The Schottky double cover

As

, satifies that

.
So, there is a degree two branched covering map

The next result shows that any automorphism of a Klein surface isotopic to the identity must be equal to the identity.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a Klein surface, possibly with non-empty boundary, either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. If
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\Sigma)
is isotopic to the identity
Id_{\Sigma}
then
\varphi=Id_{\Sigma}
.

Proof.

Observe that it is enough to show the result when
\Sigma
is a Riemann surface with empty boundary. Indeed, suppose
\Sigma
is a Klein surface but not a Riemann surface and suppose the result is true for Riemann surfaces with empty boundary. Let
f:R\to\Sigma
be the complex double cover of
\Sigma
. We recall that by construction,
R
is a Riemann surface with empty boundary. Now, we can choose an analytic lift
\widetilde{\varphi}:R\to R
of
\varphi
isotopic to
Id_{R}
. Given that
R
is a Riemann surface, we get that
\widetilde{\varphi}=Id_{R}
and therefore
\varphi=Id_{\Sigma}
.

Now suppose
\Sigma
is a Riemann surface with universal cover
\pi:\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\Sigma
and Deck group
\Gamma
which is a discrete and torsion-free subgroup (i.e., a
Fuchsian group
) of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
such that
\Sigma
is isometric to the quotient space
\mathbb{H}^{2}/\Gamma
. Take
\widetilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{H}^{2}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}
a lift of
\varphi
. Since
\varphi
is isotopic to
Id_{\Sigma}
, up to precomposition of
\widetilde{\varphi}
with an element of
\Gamma
, we can assume that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is isotopic to
Id_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
. Notice that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
that is contained in the normalizer of
\Gamma
in
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{H}^{2})
. Therefore,
\widetilde{\varphi}
preserves the type (hyperbolic or parabolic) of elements of the Deck group. Following Tappu’s argument in the proof of
[
32
, Proposition 4.1]
,
\widetilde{\varphi}
has a continuous extension
\widetilde{\varphi}\cup\partial\widetilde{\varphi}:\mathbb{H}^{2}\cup\overline{\Lambda(\Gamma)}\to\mathbb{H}^{2}\cup\overline{\Lambda(\Gamma)}
where
\Lambda(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{1}
denotes the
limit set
of
\Gamma
. Moreover, because
\widetilde{\varphi}
is a Möbius transformation, the extension
\partial\widetilde{\varphi}
coincides with the natural action of
\widetilde{\varphi}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
restricted to
\Lambda(\Gamma)
. Now, the fact that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is isotopic to the identity implies that
\partial\widetilde{\varphi}
is the identity on
\Lambda(\Gamma)
, see
[
32
, Proposition 4.1 item (4)]
. Given the surface
\Sigma
is of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic, the limit set of
\Gamma
has more than three points, in fact, it is infinite. Therefore, we obtain that
\widetilde{\varphi}
is necessarily the identity on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
and therefore
\varphi=Id_{\Sigma}
.
∎

3.2.
Klein surfaces and branched covers

Let
p\colon S\to X
be a branched covering map between surfaces. For any dianalytic structure on

we can pullback the Klein structure on

such that the covering

is a dianalytic map, see
[

\&

. Particularly, this gives us a local isometry between the two Klein surfaces.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
S
be a topological surface, possibly with non-empty boundary, that is either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. Consider a branched covering map
p\colon S\to X
, possibly with infinitely many sheets. If a deck transformation
\varphi\colon S\to S
is isotopic to the identity
\mathrm{Id}_{S}
, then
\varphi=\mathrm{Id}_{S}
. In particular,
D\cong\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p)
.

Proof.

Endow both
X
and
S
with dianalytic structures such that the covering map
p\colon S\to X
becomes a dianalytic map. In this setting, the group
\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p)
acts by automorphisms of the Klein surface
S
. Therefore, by Theorem
3.1
, any deck transformation
\varphi
isotopic
\mathrm{Id}_{S}
must be the identity.
∎

Given a topological surface
\Sigma
consider the projection
\rho:\Homeo(\Sigma)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma)
from (
2.1
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(\Sigma)
. We say that

is

if
\Sigma
admits a dianalytic structure such that there exists a subgroup
\widetilde{G}
of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Sigma)\subset\Homeo(\Sigma)
isomorphic to

\rho(\widetilde{G})=G
.

Remark 3.4
.

If
\Sigma
is a Klein surface that has negative Euler characteristic or is of infinite type, the group
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\Sigma)
acts properly discontinuously on

(see
[

is countable.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
S
be a topological surface that is either of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. Consider a branched covering map
p\colon S\to X
possibly with infinitely many sheets, and suppose that every finite cyclic subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)
is realizable. Then the kernel of
\Theta:\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)\to\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S)/D
is torsion-free.

Proof.

Consider an element
\varphi\in\mathrm{Ker}(\Theta)
of finite order

\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)
is realizable, there is a dianalytic structure on
X
and
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(X)
of order

with
\varphi=[f]
. Let
\widetilde{f}
be a lift of
f
. By pulling back to

the dianalytic structure of

is a local isometry and
\widetilde{f}\in\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(S)
. Up to pre-composition by a deck transformation, we can assume that
\widetilde{f}
is isotopic to

. Using the Proposition

\widetilde{f}
coincides with

. It follows that

and

It is well known that any finite subgroup of the mapping class group of a surface of finite type is realizable (c.f. Nielsen realization). This was proven for closed orientable surfaces in

Remark 3.7

To address this, we finish this section with a description of the structure of branched covers (possibly with infinitely many sheets) from the closed annulus or the closed infinite strip to a compact surface with two boundary components. This result will be an essential step in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
p:S\to X
be a branched covering map. Suppose that
S
is homeomorphic to the closed annulus or the closed infinite strip, and

is a compact surface with two boundary components. Then
X
is homeomorphic to the closed annulus and the map

Proof.

We may endow both

and

(1)

the covering map

becomes a dianalytic map;

(2)

if

\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:1\leq|z|\leq r\}

;

(3)

if
S
is homeomorphic to the closed infinite strip, then (with the given dianalytic structure) it is isomorphic to
\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:-1\leq\Im(z)\leq 1\}
.

Let
\widehat{S}
and
\widehat{X}
denote the Schottky doubles of
S
and
X
, respectively; that is,
S=\widehat{S}/\langle\tau\rangle
and

, where
\tau:\widehat{S}\to\widehat{S}
and
\mu:\widehat{X}\to\widehat{X}
are reflections.

The branched covering
p
naturally extends to a branched covering
\widehat{p}:\widehat{S}\to\widehat{X}
. More precisely, we can view
S
and
X
as included in
\widehat{S}
and
\widehat{X}
, respectively, and define

\widehat{p}(s):=\begin{cases}p(s)&s\in S,\\
\mu(p(\tau(s)))&s\notin S,\end{cases}

whose branch locus is given by
\widehat{B}:=B\sqcup\mu(B)
, where
B
is the branch locus of
p
.

Since
X
is compact and it has exactly two boundary components, it follows that
\widehat{X}
is a closed surface of genus at least one if
X
is orientable, or at least four otherwise. In both cases, the Euler characteristic satisfies
\chi(\widehat{X})\leqslant 0
.

Observe that
\widehat{S}
is isomorphic to a torus or to
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
, according to whether
S
is the closed annulus or the closed infinite strip, respectively. In any of these two cases, we may consider a universal holomorphic covering map
\pi:{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{S}
. So, if
\widehat{X}
is orientable, then

is a branched holomorphic covering map whose branch locus is again
\widehat{B}
. It follows from
[
13

that
\widehat{X}
cannot be hyperbolic. If
\widehat{X}
is non-orientable, then
\widehat{p}
factors though the orientable double cover

. By the same argument as before, we obtain that
\widehat{X}^{+}
cannot be hyperbolic, so neither can be
\widehat{X}
. In particular, we have that
\chi(\widehat{X})=0
, so
\widehat{X}
is either a torus or a Klein bottle. Now, as
\widehat{X}
must have genus at least four if it is non-orientable, we have that
\widehat{X}
cannot be the Klein bottle. As a consequence,
\widehat{X}
is a torus and, in particular,

is a closed annulus.

Next, we proceed to prove that
\widehat{p}
, so

, is unbranched.

(a) Let us first consider the case when
\widehat{S}
is the torus. In this case, as both
\widehat{X}
and
\widehat{S}
are both of genus one, the branched covering
\widehat{p}:\widehat{S}\to\widehat{X}
has finite degree.
By the Riemann–Hurwitz formula applied to
\widehat{p}
we conclude that the branch locus
\widehat{B}=\emptyset
, and

is unbranched as required.

(b) Now, let us assume
\widehat{S}
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Take a simple closed curve
\gamma\subset\widehat{X}
bounding an open disc
D
such that all possible branch points of
\widehat{p}
are contained in its interior. Set
A=\widehat{X}\setminus D
; which is a compact genus one surface with exactly one boundary component
\gamma
. Let

be a connected component of
\widehat{p}^{-1}(A)
, and consider the restriction
\widehat{p}|_{Z}:Z\to A
, which is an unbranched covering map.

As
Z
is planar, there should be an essential simple closed curve
\alpha
on
A
such that a connected component of
\widehat{p}^{-1}(\alpha)
is a simple path that connects the two punctures
0
and
\infty
. Let
\beta
be another essential simple closed curve on

such that intersects

at exactly one point. Each connected component of
\widehat{p}^{-1}(\beta)
is either a simple closed curve (separating

and
\infty
) or it is a simple path connecting

with
\infty
. The second situation cannot hold as
\alpha
and
\beta
are assumed to intersect at precisely one point. Since
\gamma
is homotopic to the commutator of
\alpha
and
\beta
, it follows that each connected component of
\widehat{p}^{-1}(\gamma)
will be homotopic to a simple closed curve
\delta
bounding a disc, and that
\widehat{p}|_{\delta}:\delta\to\gamma
is a homeomorphism. Now, for each such
\delta

be a connected component of
\widehat{p}^{-1}(D\cup\gamma)
containing

in its boundary. The only possibility is that

is a closed disc with
\partial W=\delta
, and
\widehat{p}|_{W}:W\to D\cup\gamma
is a branched covering whose restriction to

is a homeomorphism onto
\gamma
. This asserts that such a covering must be an homeomorphism and, in particular, it has no branch values. Hence the branch locus
\widehat{B}=\emptyset
, and
p
is unbranched as required.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

In the case where the covering has finitely many sheets and the surface
S
is a closed annulus, the conclusion of Lemma
3.8
can also be obtained by applying the Riemann–Hurwitz formula for branched covers between surfaces with boundary. A straightforward computation shows that the only possibility is that the surface
X
is homeomorphic to a closed annulus and that the covering map is unbranched.

Proof of Theorem
1

To prove that the branched covering map
p:S\rightarrow X
with branch locus

satisfies the Birman–Hilden property
we show that item (3) of Proposition
2.10
holds: the surjective homomorphism
\Theta:\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)\to\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S)/D
given by
[f]\mapsto[\tilde{f}]\mod\ D

a lift of

, is one-to-one. Our argument is based on the strategy used to prove
[

.

Notice that
X\setminus B
is either of infinite type or has negative Euler characteristic because the map

is an unramified covering and

satisfies these properties. If
X\setminus B
is a sporadic surface, whose only possibility under our hypothesis is the sphere with three punctures, then
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;B)
is finite. In this case, Corollary
3.6
implies that the map
\Theta
is injective, and therefore

has the Birman–Hilden property. Hence, for the rest of the proof we assume that

is a non-sporadic surface.

Take a

\Gamma
for

. The existence of
\Gamma
is provided by Theorem
2.3
; furthermore
\Gamma
can be assumed to be a

is a surface of infinite type.

Let
f\in\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;B)
be a representative of a mapping class in
\mathrm{Ker}(\Theta)
. Notice that we get the desired result if we show that:

Claim
:
For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the simple closed curve
f(\gamma)
is isotopic to
\gamma
in

.

Indeed, in this situation, if
X\setminus B
is of infinite type then

is isotopic to the identity

, and hence

. Otherwise,

is of finite type and

is isotopic to

, therefore

represents a mapping class of finite order in
\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)
. From Corollary

must represent the trivial mapping class in
\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;B)
.

Proof of the Claim.
Fix
\gamma\in\Gamma
and set
\beta:=f(\gamma)
. Let us take
\widetilde{f}
a lift of
f
; up to precomposition by a deck transformation, we can assume that
\widetilde{f}
is isotopic to
Id_{S}
. In particular, we have that
\widetilde{\gamma}:=p^{-1}(\gamma)
is isotopic to
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{\gamma})=p^{-1}(\beta)=:\widetilde{\beta}
in
S
.

We divide the proof in two cases:
\gamma\cap\beta=\emptyset
and
\gamma\cap\beta\neq\emptyset
.

Case (1):
\gamma\cap\beta=\emptyset
. In this case,
\widetilde{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{\beta}
are disjoint. Consider the surfaces

and
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus(\widetilde{\gamma}\sqcup\widetilde{\beta})
and take
p^{\prime}:S^{\prime}\to X^{\prime}
the branched covering map induced by

. Let
\Omega
be the set whose elements are the closure in

of the connected components of

. Observe that, if

, then each boundary component of

is a connected component of
\widetilde{\gamma}\sqcup\widetilde{\beta}
, and the restriction

is a branched covering map.

As

is isotopic to the identity

,
\widetilde{\gamma}
is isotopic to
\widetilde{\beta}
, so we may find

such that either

is homeomorphic to a closed annulus or

is homeomorphic to an closed infinite strip (if

\widetilde{\gamma}
and one boundary component which is a connected component of
\widetilde{\beta}
. Therefore,

is a surface whose boundary is equal to
\gamma\sqcup\beta

is a closed annulus, it is clear that

is compact. The same is true if

and we can take a compact subspace

\gamma

.

Case (2):
\gamma\cap\beta\neq\emptyset

∎

4.1.
An alternative proof for unbranched regular covers

The original argument of Birman and Hilden in
[
12
, Lemma 6]
can be combined with the Alexander method to give an alternative proof of the Birman–Hilden property for unbranched regular covers over a non-compact hyperbolic base surface
X
as we now explain.

Let
p:S\to X
be an unbranched regular cover and suppose
X
is noncompact and different from the sphere or the projective plane with at most two punctures. Let
p_{\ast}
be the induced homomorphism on fundamental groups,
x\in X
a base point and
\widetilde{x}\in p^{-1}(x)
. Since the fundamental group of
X
if free of rank at least two, then the centralizer of
p_{*}(\pi_{1}(S,\widetilde{x}))
in
\pi_{1}(X,x)
is trivial.

Now, take
\widetilde{f}\in\mathop{\mathrm{SHomeo}}(S)\cap\Homeo_{0}(S)
and let
f\in\Homeo(X)
be its projection under
p
. Following the same argument in the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 1.6]
, the fact that the centralizer of
p_{*}(\pi_{1}(S,\widetilde{x}))
in
\pi_{1}(X,x)
is trivial, implies (after composing with a change of base point) that
f
induces the identity on the fundamental group. By Alexander’s method if the surface
X
is of infinite type, and by the Dehn-Nielsen-Baer Theorem if
X
is of finite type, we have that
f
is isotopic to the identity. Lifting this isotopy to
S
we obtain a fiber-preserving isotopy between
\widetilde{f}
and a deck transformation of
p
. By Proposition
3.2
, this deck transformation must be the identity
Id_{S}
, and therefore we conclude that
p
satisfies the Birman–Hilden property.

Remark 4.1
.

A similar argument is given in
[
28
, Section 1.9]
for an unbranched regular cover
p:S\to X
with finitely many sheets between closed oriented
3
–manifolds
S
and
X
, under the hypotheses that
X
is aspherical, that roots are unique in
\pi_{1}(X)
, and that homotopic homeomorphisms of
X
are always isotopic.

Notice that if the surface
X
is closed with negative Euler characteristic, the original argument of Birmann and Hilden cannot be adapted to include unbranched covers with infinitely many sheets. This is because they use the hypothesis that
p
has finitely many sheets in an essential way in order to obtain that the centralizer of
p_{*}(\pi_{1}(S,\widetilde{x}))
in
\pi_{1}(X,x)
is trivial, see the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 1]
. Something similar happens with the argument to prove
[
4
, Proposition 3]
.

5.
Some applications to geometrically characteristic covers

In this section, we apply the Birman–Hilden property to unbranched covers that are
geometrically characteristic
to obtain injective homomorphisms between mapping class groups and surface braid groups.

Definition 5.1
.

[Geometrically characteristic cover]
Let
\Sigma
be a surface and
x\in\Sigma
a based point. An automorphism of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x)
is called
geometric
if it is induced by a homeomorphism of
\Sigma
that preserves
x
. A subgroup
N\leqslant\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x)
is
geometrically characteristic
if
N
is invariant under all geometric automorphisms of
\pi_{1}(\Sigma,x)
. An unbranched covering map
p:S\to X
is
geometrically characteristic
if it corresponds to a geometrically characteristic subgroup of
\pi_{1}(X,x)
.

Examples of such type of covers include unbranched characteristic covers like homology covers, the
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
-homology covers, and the orientable double cover of a non-orientable surface. The first two types have been recently studied in
[
8
, Theorems 1.2
\&
1.3]
; see also
[
7
, Section 1.1.]
, and
[
6
, Corollary 6.2]
. Furthermore, in
[
8
, Theorem 1.5]
the authors classify the homeomorphism types of geometric characteristic covers, with infinitely many sheets, over a finite type orientable surface.

Notice that by definition, if
p:S\to X
is a geometric characteristic covering map, then
\Homeo(X;x)=\mathop{\mathrm{LHomeo}}(X;x)
. Moreover, the liftable mapping class group is precisely the whole mapping class group; compare with the definition of geometric characteristic cover in
[
3
, Section 3.1]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
p:S\to X
be an unbranched geometrically characteristic covering map, and let
P\subset X
be a discrete set of marked points. Then
\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;P)=\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;P)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X\setminus P
be a base point of
X
. Any mapping class in
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;P)

can be represented by a homeomorphism
f
in
\Homeo(X;P)
such that
f(x)=x
. Since
p:S\to X
is an unbranched geometrically characteristic cover, it follows that
f
lifts to an element
\widetilde{f}\in\Homeo(S)
. Therefore,
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;P)=\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;P)
.
∎

5.1.
Injections between mapping class groups

Let
S
be a surface of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. From Corollary
2
, we know that any unbranched covering map
p:S\to X
satisfies the Birman-Hilden property. If
P\subset X
is a closed and discrete set of marked points and
\widetilde{P}=p^{-1}(P)
, then
p:S\setminus\widetilde{P}\to X\setminus P
also satisfies the Birman-Hilden property. Thus, there is a short exact sequence (see equation (
2.4
))

(5.1)

1\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{Deck}}(p)\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S;\widetilde{P})\xrightarrow{}\mathop{\mathrm{LMap}}(X;P)\rightarrow 1.

The following result is a consequence of Proposition
5.2
and the previous observation. It extends the result of Aramayona–Leininger–Souto
[
4
, Proposition 5]
to the context of non-orientable surfaces and infinite type surfaces.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
p\colon S\to X
be an unbranched geometrically characteristic covering map, let
P\subset X
be a closed and discrete set of marked points, and suppose that
S
is a surface of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. If the short exact sequence in equation (
5.1
) splits, then there is an injective homomorphism

\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;P)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(S;\widetilde{P})

obtained by lifting mapping classes.

We now apply the previous result to obtain in Corollary
3
, injections of mapping class groups with a single marked point.

Proof of Corollary
3
.

Since the cover is geometrically characteristic, we obtain a homomorphism

\Homeo(X;\{x\})\to\Homeo(S;p^{-1}(x)),\qquad f\mapsto\widetilde{f};

where
\widetilde{f}
is the unique lift satisfying
\widetilde{f}(\widetilde{x})=\widetilde{x}
. This defines a homomorphism
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(X;\{x\})\to\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S;p^{-1}(x))
that splits the short exact sequence (
5.1
). Thus, the result follows from Proposition
5.3
.
∎

5.2.
Mapping class group of non-orientable surfaces

Consider the orientable double cover
p\colon S\to N
of a connected non-orientable hyperbolic surface
N
. Let us recall that the orientable double cover of a non-orientable surface with empty boundary coincides with its complex double cover, see Subsection
3.1
. It is well known that
p
is unbranched and characteristic, and moreover any homeomorphism
f\colon N\to N
admits a unique lift
\widetilde{f}\colon S\to S
that preserves the orientation (in fact, it admits exactly two liftings, one that preserves the orientation and another that reverses it). This yields a well-defined homomorphism

(5.2)

\phi\colon\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(N)\to\mathop{\mathrm{SMap}}(S),\qquad[f]\mapsto[\widetilde{f}];

which splits the short exact sequence (
5.1
). By Proposition
5.3
, we obtain that the homomorphism
\phi
is injective. This generalizes results of Birman and Chillingworth
[
9
]
and Lima-Gonçalves, Guaschi, and Maldonado
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
to the context of infinite type surfaces.

Corollary 5.4

(Mapping class groups of a non-orientable surface and its orientable double cover)
.

Let
p:S\to N
be the orientable double cover of a non-orientable surface
N
with empty boundary, and suppose that
S
is of infinite type or of finite type with negative Euler characteristic. Then the induced map

\phi:\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(N)\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(S),\ \ \ \ \ \ [f]\mapsto[\widetilde{f}];

is injective, where
\widetilde{f}
is the unique lift of
f
that preserves the orientation of
S
.

Remark 5.5
.

In
[
19
, Remark 2.4.]
it is shown that the induced homomorphism
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(N_{2})\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(S_{1})
by the orientable double cover
S_{1}\to N_{2}
of the Klein bottle is not injective. The remaining case not covered by Corollary
5.4
is the orientable double cover
S_{0}^{2}\to N_{1}^{1}
of the projective plane with one marked point. In this situation, the natural induced homomorphism
\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(S_{0}^{2})\to\mathop{\mathrm{Map}}(N_{1}^{1})
is an isomorphism.

(Braid groups of a non-orientable surface and its orientable double cover)

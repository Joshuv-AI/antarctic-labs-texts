Prescribed T T -curvature flow on the four-dimensional unit ball
Pak Tung Ho, Cheikh Birahim NDIAYE, Liming Sun, Heming Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the prescribed T T -curvature problem on the unit ball 𝔹 4 \mathbb{B}^{4} of ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} via the T T -curvature flow approach. By combining the Ache-Chang [ 1 ] ’s inequality with the Morse theoretical approach of Malchiodi-Struwe [ 32 ] , we establish existence results under strong Morse type inequalities at infinity. As a byproduct of our argument, we also prove the exponential convergence of the T T -curvature flow on 𝔹 4 \mathbb{B}^{4} , starting from a Q Q -flat and minimal metric conformal to the standard Euclidean metric, to an extremal metric of the Ache-Chang [ 1 ] ’s inequality whose explicit expression was derived by Ndiaye-Sun [ 34 ] .

Introduction

The problem of finding a conformal metric on a Riemannian manifold with certain prescribed
curvature function has been extensively investigated in recent decades. This study seeks to understand the analytic theory of conformally invariant operators and the geometric structure of the underlying manifold; see, e.g.,
[
36
,
35
,
38
,
12
,
13
,
27
,
5
,
22
]
. In particular, the case of the unit sphere is of special interest, where the action of a noncompact conformal group gives rise to essential obstructions to the existence of conformal metrics; see, e.g.,
[
23
,
29
,
31
,
43
,
41
,
32
]
. Before introducing the main problem studied in this paper, we first recall some fundamental geometric notions and conformal quantities.

In the context of 2-dimensional geometry, the Laplace-Beltrami operator on a compact surface
(\Sigma,g)
governs the transformation law of the Gaussian curvature. In fact, under
a conformal change of metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
, one has

(1.1)

\Delta_{\tilde{g}}=e^{-2u}\Delta_{g}\quad\text{ and }\quad-\Delta_{g}u+K_{g}=K_{\tilde{g}}e^{2u}\quad\mbox{ in }\,\Sigma,

where
\Delta_{g}=\operatorname{div}_{g}\nabla_{g}
and
K_{g}
denote the Laplace-Beltrami operator and Gaussian curvature of
(\Sigma,g)
, respectively, while
\Delta_{\tilde{g}}
and
K_{\tilde{g}}
are the corresponding objects associated with
(\Sigma,\tilde{g})
. If
(\Sigma,g)
has a smooth boundary
\partial\Sigma
, with
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g}}
denoting the outward normal derivative on
\partial\Sigma
, then under the same conformal change, the pair
(\Delta_{g},\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g}})
governs the transformation laws of both the Gaussian curvature
K_{g}
in
\Sigma
and the geodesic
curvature
k_{g}
of
\partial\Sigma
:

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{\tilde{g}}=e^{-2u}\Delta_{g},\\
&\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{\tilde{g}}}=e^{-u}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g}},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{g}u+K_{g}=K_{\tilde{g}}e^{2u}&&\mbox{ in }\,\Sigma,\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{g}}+k_{g}=k_{\tilde{g}}e^{u}&&\mbox{ on }\,\partial\Sigma,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{\tilde{g}}}
and
k_{\tilde{g}}
denote the normal derivative and the geodesic curvature of
\partial\Sigma
associated with
(\Sigma,\tilde{g})
. Moreover, we have the Gauss–Bonnet formula

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}K_{g}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+\int_{\partial\Sigma}k_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g}=2\pi\chi(\Sigma),

where
\chi(\Sigma)
is the Euler-Poincaré characteristic of
\Sigma
,
\mathrm{d}v_{g}
is the element area of
\Sigma
and
\mathrm{d}s_{g}
is the line element of
\partial\Sigma
. Thus, (
1.3
) is a topological invariant and hence a conformal invariant.

In the search for higher-order conformally invariant operators, Paneitz
in 1983 (see
[
37
]
) discovered a remarkable fourth-order differential operator on compact four-manifolds, defined by

(1.4)

P_{g}^{4}:=(-\Delta_{g})^{2}-\operatorname{div}_{g}\Big(\frac{2}{3}R_{g}g-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g}\Big)\mathrm{d},

where
\mathrm{d}
is De Rham differential, and
R_{g}
and
\operatorname{Ric}_{g}
are the scalar curvature and the Ricci tensor associated with the metric
g
, respectively. The corresponding
Q
-curvature (denoted by
Q^{4}_{g}
or
Q_{g}
),
introduced by Branson (see
[
4
,
5
]
), is expressed as

(1.5)

Q_{g}:=-\frac{1}{6}(\Delta_{g}R_{g}-R_{g}^{2}+3|\operatorname{Ric}_{g}|^{2}).

Similarly to the role of the Laplace-Beltrami operator and the Gaussian curvature in two-dimensions, as shown in (
1.1
), the Paneitz operator governs the conformal transformation laws of geometric quantities under a change of metric. Indeed, for a conformal change of metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
, one has

(1.6)

P_{\tilde{g}}^{4}=e^{-4u}P_{g}^{4}\quad\text{ and }\quad P_{g}^{4}u+Q_{g}=Q_{\tilde{g}}e^{4u}\quad\text{ on }\,M,

where
P_{\tilde{g}}^{4}
and
Q_{\tilde{g}}
denote the Paneitz
operator and
Q
-curvature associated with the metric
\tilde{g}
.

More generally, a metrically defined operator
A
on a compact manifold
(M,g)
is said to be conformally covariant if, under the conformal change of metric
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
, the pair of corresponding operators
A_{g}
and
A_{\tilde{g}}
are related by

(1.7)

A_{\tilde{g}}(\phi)=e^{-bu}A_{g}(e^{au}\phi),\quad\forall\,\phi\in C^{\infty}(M),

for some constants
a
and
b
. Basic examples of such second order operators include the Laplacian
\Delta_{g}
in dimension
n=2
, and the conformal Laplacian
-\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}\Delta_{g}+R_{g}
in dimensions
n\geq 3
, where
R_{g}
is the scalar curvature of the metric
g
. In particular, the Paneitz operator
P^{4}_{g}
is conformally covariant in the sense of (
1.7
) with
(a,b)=(0,4)
.

On compact manifolds of even dimension
n
, the existence of a conformally covariant operator
P^{n}_{g}
satisfying
P_{n}^{\tilde{g}}=e^{-nu}P^{n}_{g}
was established by Graham, Jenne, Mason, and Sparling
[
22
]
. On the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, explicit formulas for
P^{n}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
were given by Branson
[
6
]
and Beckner
[
3
]
:

(1.8)

P^{n}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Pi_{k=0}^{\frac{n-2}{2}}(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}+k(n-k-1))&&\text{ if $n$ is even},\\
&\Big(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}+\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Pi_{k=0}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}+k(n-k-1))&&\text{ if $n$ is odd.}\end{aligned}\right.

When
n=3
or 4, one can further associate to
P_{g}^{n}
a natural curvature quantity
Q^{n}_{g}
of order
n
, characterized by the conformal transformation law

(1.9)

P_{g}^{n}u+Q_{g}^{n}=Q_{\tilde{g}}^{n}e^{nu}\quad\text{ on }\,M,

under the conformal change
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
. In particular, on the standard sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, if
\tilde{g}

is isometric to the standard metric
g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
, then equation (
1.9
) reduces to

(1.10)

P_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}}^{n}u+(n-1)!=(n-1)!e^{nu}\quad\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Before stating the problem investigated in this paper, we need to describe important boundary
analogues of the Paneitz operator and of
Q
-curvature for compact manifolds with
boundary in dimension four. Let
(M,\partial M,g)
be a smooth four-manifold with boundary, and denote by
\hat{g}
the restriction of
g
to
\partial M
. Let
\nu_{g}
be the unit
outward normal
vector field along the boundary, and let us use Greek indices to denote directions tangent to the boundary, that is,
g_{\alpha\beta}=\hat{g}_{\alpha\beta}
. With this convention, the second fundamental form is given by
\amalg_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g}}g_{\alpha\beta}
and the mean curvature is defined as

(1.11)

H_{g}:=\frac{1}{3}g^{\alpha\beta}\amalg_{\alpha\beta}.

The boundary operator
P_{g}^{3,b}
and a boundary curvature
T_{g}
, introduced by Chang and Qing
[
12
]
, are defined respectively by

\displaystyle P_{g}^{3,b}\phi:=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\Delta_{g}\phi}{\partial\nu_{g}}-\Delta_{\hat{g}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu_{g}}-2H_{g}\Delta_{\hat{g}}\phi+\amalg_{\alpha\beta}(\nabla_{\hat{g}})_{\alpha}(\nabla_{\hat{g}})_{\beta}\phi

(1.12)

\displaystyle\,+\langle\nabla_{\hat{g}}H_{g},\nabla_{\hat{g}}\phi\rangle_{\hat{g}}+(F-2J)\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu_{g}}

and

(1.13)

T_{g}:=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial J}{\partial\nu_{g}}+3JH_{g}-\langle G,\amalg\rangle+3H_{g}^{3}-\frac{1}{3}\operatorname{tr}_{\hat{g}}(\amalg^{3})+\Delta_{\hat{g}}H_{g},

where
\phi
is any smooth function on
M
,
F=\operatorname{Ric}_{nn}
,
J=\frac{R_{g}}{6}|_{\partial M}
is the restriction to the boundary of the ambient scalar curvature
R_{g}
,
G_{\beta}^{\alpha}=R_{n\beta n}^{\alpha}
,
\langle G,\mathrm{II}\rangle=R_{\alpha n\beta n}\Pi_{\alpha\beta}
, and
\Pi_{\alpha\beta}^{3}=\Pi_{\alpha\gamma}\Pi^{\gamma\delta}\Pi_{\delta\beta}
.
We point out that
(P_{g}^{3,b},T_{g})
depends not only on the intrinsic geometry of
\partial M
, but also on the extrinsic geometry of
\partial M
in
M
; see
[
12
,
13
]
.

Similar to the operators
(\Delta_{g},\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g}})
and curvatures
(K_{g},k_{g})
on a Riemannian surface
(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma,g)
, which govern the conformal transformation laws (
1.2
), the pair
(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})
plays an analogous role for
(Q_{g},T_{g})
on a four-manifold
(M,\partial M,g)
. Indeed, under the conformal change
\tilde{g}=e^{2u}g
, one has

(1.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P_{\tilde{g}}^{4}=e^{-4u}P^{4}_{g},\\
&P_{\tilde{g}}^{3,b}=e^{-3u}P^{3,b}_{g},\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g}u+Q_{g}=Q_{\tilde{g}}e^{4u}&&\hbox{ in }\,M,\\
&P^{3,b}_{g}u+T_{g}=T_{\tilde{g}}e^{3u}&&\hbox{ on }\,\partial M,\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{g}}+H_{g}=H_{\tilde{g}}e^{u}&&\hbox{ on }\,\partial M.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition to these resemblances, the classical Gauss–Bonnet formula (
1.3
) extends to four dimensions as the Gauss–Bonnet–Chern theorem:

(1.15)

\int_{M}(Q_{g}+|W_{g}|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}+2\int_{\partial M}(T_{g}+Z_{g})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=8\pi^{2}\chi(M),

where
W_{g}
denotes the Weyl tensor of
(M,g)
,
Z_{g}
(see
[
12
]
for the definition) is a pointwise conformal
invariant that vanishes if
\partial M
is totally geodesic, and

\chi(M)
is the Euler-characteristics of
M
.
Setting

\kappa_{P_{g}^{4}}:=\int_{M}Q_{g}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}\quad\text{ and }\quad\kappa_{P_{g}^{3,b}}:=\int_{\partial M}T_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}},

it follows from (
1.15
) and the pointwise conformal invariance of
W_{g}\,\mathrm{d}v_{g}
and
Z_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}
that the sum
\kappa_{P_{g}^{4}}+\kappa_{P_{g}^{3,b}}
is itself conformally invariant. We denote this invariant quantity by

(1.16)

\kappa_{(P^{4}_{g},P^{3,b}_{g})}:=\kappa_{P_{g}^{4}}+\kappa_{P_{g}^{3,b}}.

Building on the boundary conformal quantities introduced above, it is natural to study the prescribed

T
-curvature problem on four-manifolds with boundary: whether a given compact four-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with boundary
\partial M
admits a conformal metric
\tilde{g}
such that
Q_{\tilde{g}}=0
in
M
,
T_{\tilde{g}}
equals a prescribed function
f
on
\partial M
, and
(M,\tilde{g})
has minimal boundary.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}
be the bilinear form acting on functions satisfying a homogeneous Neumann condition as in
[
35
,
36
]
. From the viewpoint of a Liouville-type problem, we refer to the assumptions
\operatorname{ker}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}\simeq\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa_{(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})}\notin 4\pi^{2}\mathbb{N}
as the nonresonant case, while the situation
\operatorname{ker}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}\simeq\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa_{(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})}\in 4\pi^{2}\mathbb{N}
is called the resonant case. To the best of our knowledge, the first existence result was obtained by Chang and Qing
[
13
]
under the assumptions that
\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}
is nonnegative,
\operatorname{ker}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}\simeq\mathbb{R}
, and
\kappa_{(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})}<4\pi^{2}
. An alternative proof based on geometric flow methods was later provided by Ndiaye
[
35
]
. Subsequently, Ndiaye
[
33
]
developed a variant of the min-max scheme of Djadli-Malchiodi
[
20
]
to extend the result of Chang-Qing
[
13
]
, showing that the problem is solvable whenever
\operatorname{ker}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}\simeq\mathbb{R}
and
\kappa_{(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})}\notin 4\pi^{2}\mathbb{N}
. The resonant case was then treated by Ndiaye
[
36
]
via Bahri’s critical points at infinity
[
2
]
. More recently, Ho
[
25
]
established an existence result under the assumptions
\kappa_{(P_{g}^{4},P_{g}^{3,b})}=0
,
\operatorname{ker}\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}\simeq\mathbb{R}
, and
\mathbb{P}_{g}^{4,3}
is nonnegative.

In this paper we are interested in the prescribed
T
-curvature problem on the four-dimensional unit ball, where the noncompact group of conformal transformations of the ball acts on the equation giving rise to Kazdan-Warner type obstructions, just as in the celebrated prescribed scalar curvature (or Nirenberg) problem; see, e.g.,
[
30
]
.
More precisely, let
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
be the four-dimensional unit ball endowed with the Euclidean metric
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
. For a given smooth positive function
f
defined on the boundary
\partial\mathbb{B}^{4}=\mathbb{S}^{3}
, we aim to find a metric
g
conformal to
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
such that

(1.17)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{g}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&T_{g}=f&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&H_{g}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
Q_{g}
,
T_{g}
, and
H_{g}
denote the
Q
-curvature, the

T
-curvature, and the mean curvature associated with
g
, respectively.

Let
[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}]:=\{e^{2u}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}:u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{4})\}
denote the conformal class of
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
. Set
g=e^{2u}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}\in[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}]
. Then we deduce from (
1.14
) that problem (
1.17
) is equivalent to finding
u
satisfying the following conditions:

(1.18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}u=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}u+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=fe^{3u}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}+H_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

From the definitions in (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) and (
1.11
)-(
1.13
), it is straightforward to verify that

(1.19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=2&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&H_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=1&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.20)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}^{2}&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}
denotes the outward normal derivative with respect to the unit normal vector
\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}
.

Problem (
1.17
) is remarkably flexible and difficult to solve for several reasons. A first difficulty is the absence of a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition in the boundary value formulation (
1.18
). Secondly, by integrating by parts, one readily obtains a simple necessary condition, namely
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f>0
.
Beyond these, further obstructions arise. These include topological constraints, such as the one in (
1.15
), as well as a Kazdan-Warner type identity

We now describe the organization of the paper and briefly outline the main ideas of the proofs.

One difficulty in studying problem (
1.17
) via the flow approach is that the
T
-curvature
T_{g}
is defined only on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, whereas the boundary operator
P^{3,b}_{g}
is not intrinsic to

\mathbb{S}^{3}
, as it depends on the embedding of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
in
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g)
. This non-intrinsic nature complicates the analysis of the evolution of
T_{g}
. To address this issue, we exploit the relationship between
P^{3,b}_{g}
and the third-order Beckner operator
\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We would like also to remark that in general we do not know if there exists conformally covariant intrinsic operator of order 3 on compact 3-manifolds.

Another analytical difficulty arises from the absence of a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition. To overcome this, we employ a special metric known as the
adapted
metric
, originally introduced by Case and Chang
[
10
]
in the context of compactifying conformally compact Poincaré-Einstein manifolds. We denote this metric by
g_{*}
, which belongs to the conformal class of the flat metric on the Euclidean unit ball and has a totally geodesic boundary. In the model case
(\mathbb{B}^{4},\mathbb{S}^{3})
, an explicit formula for
g_{*}
was provided by Ache-Chang
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]
, namely

(1.26)

g_{*}=e^{2\rho(x)}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}},\quad\rho(x):=\frac{(1-|x|^{2}}{2},

which satisfies

(1.27)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{g_{*}}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&T_{g_{*}}=T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&H_{g_{*}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

see
[
1
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Let
w:=u-\rho
. Then
g=e^{2u}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}=e^{2w}g_{*}
, and equation (
1.18
) is equivalent to finding
w
satisfying

(1.28)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{*}}w=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P^{3,b}_{g_{*}}w+T_{g_{*}}=fe^{3w}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu_{g_{*}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, using (
1.14
), (
1.27
) and the identity
\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g_{*}}}=e^{-\rho(x)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}
, we may rewrite (
1.28
) as

(1.29)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=fe^{3w}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

which, in the flat metric
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
, takes the form of a system with a homogeneous Neumann boundary condition.

We introduce the space

\mathcal{H}_{g}:=\Big\{\phi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g):\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\nu_{g}}=0\,\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\Big\},

It is well known that problem (
1.29
) admits a variational
structure. Its associated energy functional is given by

(1.30)

E_{f}[w]:=E[w]-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\log\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}fe^{3\hat{w}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)

for
w\in\mathcal{H}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\cap H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
, where

(1.31)

E[w]:=\mathbb{P}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}^{4,3}(w,w)+4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}.

with the bilinear form

(1.32)

\mathbb{P}^{4,3}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}(\phi,\psi):=\langle P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})}+2\langle P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})},

for
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\cap H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
.
By integration by parts, (
1.32
) admits the representation

(1.33)

\mathbb{P}^{4,3}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}(\phi,\psi)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\phi\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\psi\,\mathrm{d}v_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}+2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\langle\nabla_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\phi,\nabla_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\psi\rangle_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}},

which extends naturally to the whole space
\mathcal{H}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}
.

We now briefly outline the main strategy of the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.1
. Let
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{4})

be such that the conformal metric
g_{0}:=e^{2u_{0}}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
belongs to the normalized class
[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}]_{0}
.
We consider the one-parameter family of conformal metrics
g(t):=e^{2u(t,\cdot)}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}\in[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}]_{0}
evolving by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\alpha(t)f-T_{g(t)}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\end{aligned}\right.

where the normalization factor
\alpha(t)
is determined by

\alpha(t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}(t)}=2\pi^{2}
. This choice of the coefficient
\alpha(t)
allows the boundary volume of
(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}(t))
to remain independent of
t
. Using (
1.29
), we reformulate the flow in terms of the function
w
, and prove that the resulting flow (
2.14
) exists globally for all
t\geq 0
. In Section
4
, we perform the blow-up analysis to show that one of the following two alternatives must occur: either the flow (
2.14
) converges in
H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
, or there will be a concentration phenomenon and the associated normalized flow
v(t)
(see (
3.6
)) converges to 0 in
H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
as
t\to+\infty
. In the latter case, the evolving boundary metric
\hat{g}(t)
concentrates at a single point, while the corresponding normalized boundary metric becomes asymptotically round on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
This dichotomy constitutes the concentration–compactness phenomenon for the present flow (
2.14
).

With this concentration–compactness result at hand, we prove Theorem
1.1
by a contradiction argument.
Starting from Section
5
, we assume that the flow (
2.14
) does not converge; in particular,
f
is not realized along the flow.
We then show that, in the divergent case, the flow exhibits the following asymptotic behavior as
t\to+\infty
:
i). the boundary metric
\hat{g}(t)
concentrates at critical points
Q
of
f
where
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f(Q)<0
; ii). the energy functional
E_{f}[w(t)]\to-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\log f(Q)
, where
w(t)
is a solution of the flow (
2.14
). Finally, in Section
7
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
via a Morse-theoretic contradiction argument, which proceeds through the following steps.

Step 1.
We introduce the sublevel sets of the energy functional
E_{f}
, denoted by
E_{f}^{\beta}
(see (
7.1
)).
Using the blow-up analysis and the asymptotic expansions obtained in Sections
2
–
6
, we characterize the changes in the topology of
E_{f}^{\beta}
near the critical points of
f
, leading to Proposition
7.1
.

Step 2.
Proposition
7.1
implies that
N_{0}:=E_{f}^{\beta_{0}}
is contractible for a suitable choice of
\beta_{0}
.
Moreover, the flow induces a homotopy equivalence between
N_{0}
and a limiting space
N_{\infty}
, whose homotopy type is that of a point with cells of dimension
3-\operatorname{morse}\mkern 1.0mu(f,p)
attached for each critical point
p
of
f
satisfying
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f(p)<0
.

Step 3.
Applying standard Morse theory, we obtain the Morse polynomial identity

(1.34)

\sum_{i=0}^{3}t^{i}m_{i}=1+(1+t)\sum_{i=0}^{3}t^{i}k_{i},

where
m_{i}
is defined in (
1.22
) and
k_{i}\geq 0
.
Comparing coefficients on both sides of (
1.34
) yields a nontrivial solution to the system (
1.23
), contradicting the hypothesis of Theorem
1.1
.
This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Corollary
1.1
follows immediately from (
1.34
).

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the flow equation and
study its elementary properties. Asymptotic behavior of a normalized flow is discussed in Section
3
and
the blow-up analysis is performed in Section
4
. Then we derive the spectral decomposition and complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
in Section

.
The shadow flow analysis is presented in Section

. Finally, Theorem
1.1
and
Corollary
1.1
are proved in Section

.

2.
The flow equation

Let
f
be a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and set
0<m_{f}:=\inf_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\leq M_{f}:=\sup_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f
.
Motivated by the works of Struwe
[
41
]
, Brendle
[
9
,
8
]
and Malchiodi–Struwe
[
32
]
, we consider the following evolution of conformal metrics
g(t)
for
t\geq 0
:

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=(\alpha(t)f-T_{g})g&&\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&g(0)=e^{2u_{0}}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}},&&\end{aligned}\right.

together with the constraints

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{g}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&H_{g}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Here
\alpha(t)
is a real-valued function defined for
t\geq 0
and
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{4})
.
Throughout the paper, unless otherwise specified,
T_{g}
,
Q_{g}
and
H_{g}
denote respectively the
T
-curvature on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
Q
-curvature on
\mathbb{B}^{4}
and mean curvature on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, all computed with respect to the evolving metric
g(t)
.
For technical reasons, we assume that the initial metric
g_{0}:=g(0)
satisfies

(2.3)

\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{0})=\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})=2\pi^{2}.

Since the flow (
2.1
) preserves the conformal structure of
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
, the evolving metric can be written in the form
g(t)=e^{2u(t)}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
, where

u(t)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{4})
satisfies the initial condition
u(0)=u_{0}
. Here and throughout, we adopt the notation

u(t)(x):=u(t,x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{B}^{4}
. In terms of the conformal factor
u
, the
flow (
2.1
) reduces to the boundary evolution equation

(2.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u=(\alpha(t)f-T_{g})&&\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&u(0)=u_{0},&&\end{aligned}\right.

where
T_{g}
is the
T
-curvature of
g(t)
. By the conformal transformation law of

T
-curvature in (
1.14
), we have

(2.5)

T_{g}=e^{-3u}(P_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}^{3,b}u+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}).

For convenience, we choose the factor
\alpha(t)
such that the boundary volume (area)
of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
with respect to the conformal metric
\hat{g}(t)
remains constant along the flow (
2.1
)-(
2.7
). More precisely, we impose

0=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{3\hat{u}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)=3\alpha(t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}fe^{3\hat{u}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}-3\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g}e^{3\hat{u}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}

for all
t\geq 0
. Thus, a natural choice is

(2.6)

\alpha(t)=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}}\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0.

In particular, it follows from (
2.3
) that

(2.7)

\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g})=2\pi^{2}\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0.

Since
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g)
is conformally flat, the Weyl tensor of
g
vanishes, i.e.,
W_{g}\equiv 0
.
Together with (
1.15
) and the condition
Q_{g}=0
in (
2.2
), we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}+Z_{g})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=4\pi^{2}\chi(\mathbb{B}^{4})=4\pi^{2}

along the flow (
2.1
)-(
2.7
). On the other hand, by (
2.2
), the conformal invariance of (
1.16
), and the fact that
\kappa_{(P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}},P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}})}=4\pi^{2}
, we can further deduce that

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=4\pi^{2}.

Combining (
2.8
) with (
2.6
), we conclude that

(2.9)

\alpha(t)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=4\pi^{2}\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0.

From (
2.7
) and (
2.9
),
we obtain uniform upper and lower bounds for
\alpha
:

(2.10)

\frac{2}{M_{f}}\leq\alpha\leq\frac{2}{m_{f}}\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0.

Differentiating (
2.9
) with respect to
t
and using (
2.4
), we derive that

(2.11)

\alpha_{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}+3\alpha\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f(\alpha f-T_{g})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=0,

where
\alpha_{t}:=\frac{\mathrm{d}\alpha}{\mathrm{d}t}
.
Combining (
2.11
) with (
2.9
), we further obtain

(2.12)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f(T_{g}-\alpha f)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{3}\cdot\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha^{2}}.

It follows from (
1.14
) and (
1.19
) that the flow (
2.1
)-(
2.7
) can be equivalently rewritten in terms of the conformal factor
u
as

(2.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}u=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&\partial_{t}u=\alpha f-T_{g}=\alpha f-e^{-3u}(P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}u+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}})&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}+H_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&u|_{t=0}=u_{0}&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

Recall that the adapted metric is given by
g_{*}=e^{2\rho(x)}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
with
\rho(x)=(1-|x|^{2})/2
, which satisfies the properties in (
1.27
). Let
w(t)(x):=u(t)(x)-\rho(x)
, then
g(t)=e^{2w(t)}g_{*}
. Following the same derivation as in (
1.29
), the system (
2.13
) can be rewritten as a flow with homogeneous Neumann boundary condition:

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&\partial_{t}w=\alpha f-T_{g}=\alpha f-e^{-3w}(P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}})&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&w|_{t=0}=w_{0}&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\end{aligned}\right.

where
w_{0}(x)=u_{0}(x)-\rho(x)
.

Let
\{\lambda_{k}=k(k+2):k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
. The eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{k}
has finite dimension
N_{k}
and is spanned by spherical harmonics
Y_{k}^{\ell}
of degree
k
, where
\ell=1,\ldots,N_{k}
(see, e.g.,
[
39
]
). We normalize them such that
\|Y_{k}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}=1
. The spherical harmonics
\{Y_{k}^{\ell}\}
form an orthonormal basis of the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
. In particular, given
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
we can write

(2.15)

u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}u_{k}^{\ell}Y_{k}^{\ell},\quad u_{k}^{\ell}\in\mathbb{R},

and
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{2}=\sum_{k,\ell}(u_{k}^{\ell})^{2}
.

One can define a third-order Beckner operator
\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}
as follows (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
7
,
6
,
12
]
). Given
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
with spherical harmonics expansion as in (
2.15
), such that

\|u\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{2}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}|u_{k}^{\ell}|^{2}(\lambda_{k}+1)\lambda_{k}^{2}<+\infty,

we define

\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}u:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}u_{k}^{\ell}Y_{k}^{\ell}.

Notice that on
H^{3}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
the operator
\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}
coincides with the operator
P_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}=(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
given by (
1.8
), where the operator
(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}
is understood in terms of spectral decomposition of the Laplace-Beltrami operator:

(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}+1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}u_{k}^{\ell}Y_{k}^{\ell}\quad\,\text{ for $u$ as in \eqref{expressionu}.}

Therefore,
\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}
is the well-known intertwining operator on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(see, e.g.,
[
6
]
).

Define the space

H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}):=\Big\{u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}u_{k}^{\ell}Y_{k}^{\ell}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}):\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}+1)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}|u_{k}^{\ell}|^{2}<+\infty\Big\},

endowed with the seminorm

\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}^{2}:=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\lambda_{k}+1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}\left|u_{k}^{\ell}\right|^{2}

and the norm

\|u\|_{H^{3/2}}^{2}:=\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}^{2}.

Since the operator
\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}
is self-adjoint and non-negative, one can define its square root
(\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}
for functions
u\in H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
by

(\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}u:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(\lambda_{k}\sqrt{1+\lambda_{k}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N_{k}}u_{k}^{\ell}Y_{k}^{\ell}.

It is worth noting that
\|(\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3})^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}}=\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{3/2}}
.

Chang and Qing
[
13
]
showed that on
(\mathbb{B}^{4},\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
, there exists a close relationship
between the boundary operator
P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}
and the Beckner operator
\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
. More precisely, if
w
satisfies
P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w=0
in
\mathbb{B}^{4}
and we set

\hat{w}=w|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, then by
[
1
, Lemma 6.2]
(see also Lemma 3.3, Corollary 3.1 in
[
13
]
) one has

(2.16)

\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{w}=P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w\quad\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.

Since
Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}=T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=2
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, it follows from (
1.10
), (
1.14
) and (
2.16
) that

(2.17)

T_{g}=Q^{3}_{\hat{g}}\quad\text{ for all }\,g\in[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}],

where
Q^{3}_{\hat{g}}
is the
Q
-curvature associated with the Beckner operator
\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}
on
(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g})
.

Note that for any given
\tilde{v}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
, there exists a unique function
\tilde{w}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{4})
solving

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\tilde{w}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&\frac{\partial\tilde{w}}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\tilde{w}=\tilde{v}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

As a result, the system (
2.14
) is equivalent to the following evolution equation on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
:

(2.18)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\hat{w}=\alpha f-Q^{3}_{\hat{g}}=\alpha f-e^{-3\hat{w}}(\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{w}+Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}})&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}\mbox{ for all }\,t\geq 0,\\
&\hat{w}|_{t=0}=\hat{w}_{0}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\hat{w}_{0}=w_{0}|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
,
\hat{g}=e^{2\hat{w}}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
and
\alpha
satisfies
\alpha\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}Q^{3}_{\hat{g}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}
.

Thanks to the equivalence between the flow systems (
2.1
)-(
2.7
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
), and (
2.18
),
and in particular the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition in (
2.14
), we will carry out computations by freely passing between these formulations whenever convenient. Moreover, as in
[
32
,
24
,
18
]
, the global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions to the flow (
2.18
) can be established by the same argument as in
[
9
]
. Consequently, we obtain global existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions for the flows (
2.1
)-(
2.7
), (
2.13
), (
2.14
), and (
2.18
).

The following lemma shows that the energy functional
E_{f}[w]
, defined in (
1.30
), is non-increasing along
the flow (
2.14
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
w
be any smooth solution to the flow (
2.14
). Then

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{f}[w]=-4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(\alpha f-T_{g})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}.

In particular,
E_{f}[w]
is non-increasing along the flow.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}^{4,3}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}
is a symmetric bilinear form, we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{f}[w]=2\mathbb{P}^{4,3}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}(w,\partial_{t}w)+4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}-16\pi^{2}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}fe^{3\hat{w}}\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}fe^{3\hat{w}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}}.

Using (
1.32
), (
2.6
), (
2.9
) and (
2.14
), we deduce

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{f}[w]=

\displaystyle\,2\langle P^{4}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w,\partial_{t}w\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})}+4\langle P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w,\partial_{t}\hat{w}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}

\displaystyle\,+4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}-16\pi^{2}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}}{\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}w+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}})\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}-4\alpha\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\partial_{t}\hat{w}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=-4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(\alpha f-T_{g})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}},

which completes the proof.
∎

We now establish a uniform bound for the energy functional
E_{f}[w]
along the flow (
2.14
), using a Lebedev-Milin type inequality due to Ache-Chang
[
1
, Theorem B]
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
w
be any smooth solution to the flow (
2.14
). Then

-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\log M_{f}\leq E_{f}[w]\leq E_{f}[w_{0}]<+\infty,

where
M_{f}:=\max_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f
and
w_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
.

Proof.

For each
t>0
, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

E_{f}[w(t)]\leq E_{f}[w(0)]=E_{f}[w_{0}]<+\infty

for any initial data
w_{0}\in H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
. On the other hand, by the sharp inequality of Ache-Chang
[
1
, Theorem B]
, which is
a generalization of the classical Lebedev–Milin inequality in
[
28
]
, we have

\log\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{3\hat{w}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)\leq\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}E[w].

Consequently, we obtain the uniform lower bound

E_{f}[w]\geq-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\log\big(\max_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\big),

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma
2.1
together with Corollary
2.1
yields the estimate

(2.19)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(\alpha f-T_{g})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}\,\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{1}{4}\Big(E_{f}[w_{0}]+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\log M_{f}\Big)<+\infty.

Thus, there exists a sequence
\{t_{k}\}
with
t_{k}\to+\infty
as
k\to+\infty
, and the corresponding metrics
g_{k}=g(t_{k})
, such that

(2.20)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(\alpha(t_{k})f-T_{g_{k}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\,k\to+\infty.

Therefore, if
\{g_{k}\}
converges (up to a subsequence) to a metric
g_{\infty}\in[g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}]
, then
the
Q
-curvature,
T
-curvature, and mean curvature of
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\infty})

satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{g_{\infty}}=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&T_{g_{\infty}}=\alpha_{\infty}f&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&H_{g_{\infty}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\alpha_{\infty}>0
is a positive constant in view of (
2.10
). In particular, if
g_{\infty}=e^{2u_{\infty}}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
, then the conformally related metric
\bar{g}_{\infty}=e^{2\bar{u}_{\infty}}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
, with

\bar{u}_{\infty}:=u_{\infty}+\frac{1}{3}\log\alpha_{\infty}

provides a solution to problem (
1.17
), thereby prescribing
f
as the
T
-curvature. On the other hand, if
\{g_{k}\}
does not converge along any sequence
t_{k}\to+\infty
, one expects a concentration phenomenon analogous to that studied in
[
41
,
32
,
18
,
24
,
43
]
.

3.
Asymptotic behavior of the normalized flow

In this section, we investigate the asymptotic behavior of the solution
w(t)
to the flow (
2.14
) and of the corresponding conformal metric
g(t)
as
t\to+\infty
.

3.1.
Normalized flow

Following
[
43
]
, in the setting of
(\mathbb{B}^{4},\mathbb{S}^{3},g)
, let
g=g(t):=e^{2u(t)}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
be a smooth family of metrics as in Section
2
. Then there exists a family of conformal diffeomorphisms

\Phi(t):(\mathbb{B}^{4},\mathbb{S}^{3})\mapsto(\mathbb{B}^{4},\mathbb{S}^{3}),

mapping
\mathbb{S}^{3}
onto itself, such that

(3.1)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}x\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}=0\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0,

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
is the restriction to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
of the standard coordinate
functions on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and
h(t):=\Phi^{*}(t)g(t)
. For notational simplicity, we write
g=g(t)
,
h=h(t)
, and
\Phi=\Phi(t)
. The metric
h=\Phi^{*}g
induced by this normalization (
3.1
) will be referred to as the
normalized metric
.

Note that
g=e^{2w(t)}g_{*}
, where
g_{*}
is the adapted metric defined in (
1.26
). Accordingly, if we write
h:=e^{2v}g_{*}
, and denote by
\hat{v}:=v|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
,
\hat{w}:=w|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
and
\hat{\Phi}:=\Phi|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
the corresponding boundary restrictions, then

(3.2)

\hat{v}=\hat{w}\circ\hat{\Phi}+\frac{1}{3}\log\det(\mathrm{d}\hat{\Phi}).

Moreover, letting
\hat{h}:=h|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

and using (
2.7
), we obtain

(3.3)

\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})=\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g})=2\pi^{2}\quad\text{ for all }\,t\geq 0.

Since
\Phi
is a conformal diffeomorphism and
h=\Phi^{*}g
, the associated curvature quantities transform naturally under pullback. In particular, in view of (
2.2
), we have

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &Q_{h}=Q_{g}\circ\Phi=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&H_{h}=H_{g}\circ\hat{\Phi}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&T_{h}=T_{g}\circ\hat{\Phi}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

Now consider the conformal change of metric
h=e^{2v}g_{*}
. It follows from (
1.14
), (
1.27
), and (
3.4
) that
v
satisfies

(3.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P_{g_{*}}^{4}v=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P_{g_{*}}^{3,b}v+T_{g_{*}}=T_{h}e^{3v}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu_{g_{*}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
g_{*}=e^{2\rho}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
as in (
1.26
), we may rewrite (
3.5
) in terms of the background metric
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
. Indeed, using again (
1.14
) together with (
1.27
), and noting that
\rho|_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}=0
, we deduce that
v
also satisfies

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &P_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}^{4}v=0&&\mbox{ in }\,\mathbb{B}^{4},\\
&P_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}^{3,b}v+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=T_{h}e^{3v}&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},\\
&\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}=0&&\mbox{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, by virtue of (
2.17
), we have

(3.7)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f\circ\hat{\Phi})\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{h}}(T_{h}-\alpha f\circ\hat{\Phi})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}.

Let us define a functional on
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
by

\hat{E}[\tilde{\phi}]:=\langle\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\phi}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}+2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\tilde{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}.

By conformal invariance, it holds

(3.8)

\hat{E}[\hat{v}]=\hat{E}[\hat{w}],

see
[
43
, Lemma 2.2]
or step 1 in the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
16
]
for further details. Moreover, arguing as in (
2.16
), we deduce from the first equation in (
3.6
) together with
[
1
, Lemma 6.2]
that

(3.9)

\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{v}=P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}v\quad\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3}.

Consequently, using (
1.31
)-(
1.33
), (
2.16
), (
3.6
) and (
3.9
) (recalling that
T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}
), we obtain

E[v]=2\hat{E}[\hat{v}]\quad\text{ and }\quad E[w]=2\hat{E}[\hat{w}],

which, together with (
3.8
), implies that

(3.10)

E[v]=E[w].

The Ache-Chang inequality
[
1
, Theorem B]
also applies to
v
.
Moreover, thanks to the vanishing center-of-mass condition (
3.1
), we can derive the following strengthened version.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
a<1
such that for every
v\in H^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
solving the system (
3.6
) and the corresponding metric
h=e^{2v}g_{*}
satisfying (
3.1
), one has

\log\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{3\hat{v}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)\leq\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}\Big[a\mathbb{P}^{4,3}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}(v,v)+4\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\hat{v}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big].

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and Theorem 2.6 in
[
42
]
, there exists a constant
a<1
such that

\displaystyle\log\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{3\hat{v}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\,\frac{3}{4}\Big[a\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{v}\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{v}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+4\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{v}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\frac{3}{4}\Big[\frac{a}{2\pi^{2}}\langle\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{v},\hat{v}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}+4\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{v}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big].

The desired estimate then follows from (
1.32
), (
3.9
), and the first equation in (
3.6
).
∎

Consequently, we obtain the following a priori estimates for solutions
v
in (
3.6
) by adapting the argument of
[
32
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
v
be any smooth solution of (
3.6
). Then there exist some positive constants
C
and
C(\sigma)
, independent of
t
, such that

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|v\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})}\leq C,

and, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{t\geq 0}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{3\sigma\hat{v}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\leq C(\sigma).

Proof.

By Lemma 3.2 in
[
32
]
(see also
[
24
,
18
]
), we have

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}\leq C,

and, for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sup_{t\geq 0}\|e^{3\sigma\hat{v}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}\leq C(\sigma),

where
C
and
C(\sigma)
are positive constants independent of
t
. Lemma
3.1
follows from (
3.6
) and standard elliptic regularity for the biharmonic
operator with Neumann boundary condition.
∎

Using the uniform
H^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
-bound for
v
obtained in Lemma
3.1
,
and following the scheme of
[
9
, Section 4]
together with standard elliptic
regularity, one can further derive higher-order a priori estimates. In particular, for
each
T>0
there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
such that
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|v(t)\|_{H^{4}(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})}\leq C_{T}
.
Consequently, by the continuation argument in
[
9
, Section 5]
, the solution
exists for all
t\geq 0
. By the equivalence of the formulations established above, global
existence also holds for the flows (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.18
),
(
3.5
), and (
3.6
).

3.2.
Curvature evolution and its
L^{2}
-convergence

In this subsection, we establish the evolution equation for the
T
-curvature along the flow and study the
L^{2}
-norm of the flow speed.

Using (
1.14
), (
2.14
), and (
2.16
), we compute the evolution of the
T
-curvature on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
:

\partial_{t}T_{g}=\partial_{t}(e^{-3\hat{w}}(\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{w}+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}))=-3(\partial_{t}\hat{w})T_{g}+\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}(\partial_{t}\hat{w})=3T_{g}(T_{g}-\alpha f)-\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}(T_{g}-\alpha f).

Combining this with (
2.4
), (
2.12
), and (
2.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|T_{g}-\alpha f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)(\partial_{t}T_{g}-\alpha_{t}f)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}-3\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,6\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{g}(T_{g}-\alpha f)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}-2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\,-\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha}\Big)^{2}-3\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}.

Next, we show the convergence of (
2.20
) is in fact uniform in time
t
.

For
t\geq 0
, we introduce the quantities

(3.12)

F_{2}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|T_{g}-\alpha f|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}

and

(3.13)

G_{2}(t):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{g}}(T_{g}-\alpha f)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{h}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}},

in view of (
3.7
), where
f_{\hat{\Phi}}:=f\circ\hat{\Phi}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Along the equivalent flows (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.18
), (
3.5
), and (
3.6
), we have

F_{2}(t)\to 0\quad\text{ as }\,t\to+\infty.

Proof.

Note that
T_{h}=T_{g}\circ\hat{\Phi}
for the normalized metric
h=\Phi^{*}g
.
By the naturality of the Beckner operator under conformal diffeomorphisms, and by rewriting (
3.11
) in the normalized coordinates, we obtain

\displaystyle\,\frac{\mathrm{d}F_{2}(t)}{\mathrm{d}t}+\frac{8\pi^{2}}{3}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{t}}{\alpha}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,6\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}T_{h}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}-2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\mathcal{P}^{3}_{\hat{h}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}

\displaystyle\,-3\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-2G_{2}(t)+3\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}+6\alpha\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f_{\hat{\Phi}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}.

For any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. By (
2.20
), there exists
t_{0}
arbitrarily large such that

(3.15)

F_{2}(t_{0})<\varepsilon_{0}.

Then we choose
t_{1}>t_{0}
such that

(3.16)

\sup_{t\in[t_{0},t_{1})}F_{2}(t)\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}.

Now using (
2.10
), (
3.3
) and (
3.16
), we obtain for
t\in[t_{0},t_{1})
that

\|T_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}\leq F_{2}(t)^{1/2}+\alpha\|f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon_{0}}+\sqrt{2}\pi\frac{M_{f}}{m_{f}}:=C(f).

This, together with Hölder inequality and Lemma
3.1
, implies that the right-hand side
of
\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{v}+T_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}=T_{h}e^{3\hat{v}}

is uniformly bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
for every
p\in(1,2)
.
Standard
L^{p}
-estimates then yield that

\hat{v}
is bounded in
W^{3,p}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
for all
p\in(1,2)
,
and hence also in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
.
In particular, there exists a constant
C>1
, independent of
t
, such that

(3.17)

C^{-1}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\leq\hat{h}\leq Cg_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\quad\text{ for }\,t\in[t_{0},t_{1}),

where the inequality is understood in the sense of quadratic forms, i.e., uniformly for all tangent vectors.

By Hölder inequality, (
3.17
), and the Sobolev’s embedding
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}|^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}\leq

\displaystyle\,\|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}\|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}

\displaystyle\,C\|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}\|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{h})}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}

\displaystyle\,+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(T_{h}-\alpha f_{\hat{\Phi}})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C_{0}F_{2}(t)^{1/2}(G_{2}(t)+F_{2}(t)).

If we choose

and

Differentiating (

Proof.

Differentiating (

\displaystyle 0=

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{w_{k}\}
be a sequence of smooth functions on
\mathbb{B}^{4}
with associated metrics

\mathbb{S}^{3}

\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})=2\pi^{2}
and
\|T_{g_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})}\leq C
for some constant

independent of
k
. Then either there exists a subsequence (still denoted by
\{w_{k}\}
) which is bounded in

\{w_{k}\}
) and some points

such that for every

(4.1)

\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{B_{r}(p_{i})}|T_{g_{k}}|\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}\geq 2\pi^{2}.

In the latter case, a subsequence
\{w_{k}\}
either converges in

, or
w_{k}\to-\infty
locally uniformly away from

as

Proof.

Using the relation (
3.9
), the proof follows closely the arguments in

[

or
[
40
, Theorem 3.2]
.
The main difference in the present setting is that one must address additional difficulties arising from the
pseudo-differential operator

.
These issues will be treated in the proofs of the next two lemmas.
For brevity, we omit the details here.
∎

Following the same strategy as the proof of Lemma 3.6 in
[
32
]
, we have

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\{\hat{w}_{k}\}
be a sequence of smooth functions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and let

\hat{g}_{k}=e^{2\hat{w}_{k}}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
be the corresponding sequence of metrics.
Assume that
\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})=2\pi^{2}
and

\|Q^{3}_{\hat{g}_{k}}-Q^{3}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty

for some smooth positive function
Q^{3}_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.
Let
\hat{\Phi}_{k}:\mathbb{S}^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{3}
be conformal diffeomorphisms such that, with

\hat{h}_{k}:=\hat{\Phi}_{k}^{*}\hat{g}_{k}=e^{2\hat{v}_{k}}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
, the normalization (
3.1
)
holds. Then, after passing to a subsequence, one of the following two possibilities must occur:

(i) Compactness:
\hat{w}_{k}\to\hat{w}_{\infty}

(ii) Concentration: There exists a point

such that

(4.2)

\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}\rightharpoonup 2\pi^{2}\delta_{p}\quad\mbox{ as }\,k\to+\infty

in the weak sense of measures, and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{v}_{k}\to 0&&\mbox{ in }\,H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}),\\
&Q^{3}_{\hat{h}_{k}}\to 2&&\mbox{ in }\,L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}).\end{aligned}\right.

In this case, the boundary maps
\hat{\Phi}_{k}
converge weakly in
H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})

to the constant map

Proof.

First, Lemma
4.1

\hat{w}_{k}

as

. Consequently,
\hat{g}_{k}=e^{2\hat{w}_{k}}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\to\hat{g}_{\infty}:=e^{2\hat{w}_{\infty}}g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}

as
k\to+\infty
.

Otherwise, if concentration occurs in the sense of (
4.1
), we need to show the desired behavior in (ii). For each

, select

and

\sup_{p\in\mathbb{S}^{3}}\int_{B_{r_{k}}(p)}|Q^{3}_{\hat{g}_{k}}|\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}=\int_{B_{r_{k}}(p_{k})}|Q^{3}_{\hat{g}_{k}}|\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}=\pi^{2},

then

as

as

Denote by

which satisfy the equation

\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}\tilde{w}_{k}+Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}=\hat{Q}_{k}e^{3\tilde{w}_{k}}\quad\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},

where

. From the selection of

and (

), by applying Lemma

and (

, where

is the south pole on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Meanwhile,
\hat{Q}_{k}\to Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)
almost everywhere as

. Using the map

(4.4)

\hat{\Psi}(y):=\frac{1}{1+|y|^{2}}(2y_{1},2y_{2},2y_{3},1-|y|^{2}),\quad y=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3},

and defining

\hat{w}_{k}:=\tilde{w}_{k}\circ\hat{\Psi}+\frac{1}{3}\log(\operatorname{det}\mathrm{d}\hat{\Psi}),

we obtain

converging in

to a function

, which satisfies the equation

(4.5)

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{\frac{3}{2}}\hat{w}_{\infty}=Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)e^{3\hat{w}_{\infty}}\quad\text{ in }\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Moreover, by Fatou’s lemma we get

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{3\hat{w}_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{3\tilde{w}_{k}}\,\mathrm{d}y=2\pi^{2}.

Claim:

\hat{w}_{\infty}
is of the form

(4.7)

\hat{w}_{\infty}(y)=\log\Big(\frac{2\lambda}{1+\lambda^{2}|y-y_{0}|^{2}}\Big)-\frac{1}{3}\log\frac{Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)}{2}

for some
\lambda>0
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Indeed, by
[
26

, we only need to prove that

\lim_{|y|\to+\infty}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{w}_{\infty}(y)=0

For simplicity, one uses
w_{\infty}
instead of
\hat{w}_{\infty}
.

Firstly, we deduce from
[
26
, Lemma 18]
that

-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\infty}(y)\geq 0\quad\text{ in }\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Set

v(y):=-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\log\Big(\frac{|y-x|}{|x|}\Big)Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)e^{3w_{\infty}(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Obviously,

satisfies that

(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{\frac{3}{2}}v(y)=Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)e^{3w_{\infty}(y)}\quad\text{ in }\,\mathbb{R}^{3}.

It follows from
[
26
, Theorem 3]
(see also
[
44
, p.1763, Remark]

\lim_{|y|\to+\infty}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(y)=0.

It is easy to see that
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}})^{\frac{3}{2}}(v-w_{\infty})=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and by
[
26
, Lemma 15]
we have
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(v-w_{\infty})\equiv b
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
for some constant
b
. It remains to prove
b=0
.

We claim that, for any

r>0
small and
q\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
,
independent of
k
, such that

(4.8)

\Big|\int_{B_{r}(q)}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{w}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big|\leq C_{0}r.

Indeed, using the equation

(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+1)^{1/2}(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{w}_{k})=Q_{\hat{g}_{k}}^{3}e^{3\hat{w}_{k}}-Q^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}:=F_{k}\quad\text{ on }\,\mathbb{S}^{3},

and letting
G(\cdot,z)
be the Green function of the operator
(-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}+1)^{1/2}
. It is known that
G(x,z)>0
for
x\neq z
, and moreover,

G(x,z)\sim c\operatorname{dist}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(x,z)^{-2}\quad\text{ as }\,\operatorname{dist}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(x,z)\to 0,

where
c>0
is a positive constant.
More precisely,

G(x,z)=c\operatorname{dist}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(x,z)^{-2}+h(x,z),

where
h(y,z)
is a smooth function defined on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

By Green’s formula, we obtain

-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{w}_{k}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}G(x,z)F_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(x).

Then, for any
q\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
r>0
, by Fubini’s theorem we have

\Big|\int_{B_{r}(q)}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{w}_{k}(z))\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\Big|\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}|F_{k}(x)|\Big(\int_{B_{r}(q)}G(x,z)\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(x).

Since the singularity
\operatorname{dist}(x,z)^{-2}
is integrable on the
three-dimensional sphere, we have the uniform estimate

(4.9)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{S}^{3}}\int_{B_{r}(q)}G(x,z)\,ds_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\leq Cr.

Indeed, if
x\in B_{2r}(q)
, then
B_{r}(q)\subset B_{3r}(x)
and

\int_{B_{r}(q)}\operatorname{dist}(x,z)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\leq\int_{B_{3r}(x)}\operatorname{dist}(x,z)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\leq C\int_{0}^{3r}\rho^{-2}\rho^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\rho\leq Cr.

If instead
x\notin B_{2r}(q)
, then
\operatorname{dist}(x,z)\geq r
for all
z\in B_{r}(q)
, hence

\int_{B_{r}(q)}\operatorname{dist}(x,z)^{-2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}(z)\leq r^{-2}\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(B_{r}(q))\leq Cr.

Thus (
4.9
) holds.

Combining (
4.9
) with the uniform

-bound
\|F_{k}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\leq C
,
which follows from
\operatorname{vol}\mkern 1.0mu(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})=2\pi^{2}
and the convergence

\|Q^{3}_{\hat{g}_{k}}-Q^{3}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})}\to 0
together with the smoothness of

Q^{3}_{\infty}
, we prove the validity of (
4.8
).

Let
\phi:=v-w_{\infty}
. Then
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\phi\equiv b
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Assume by contradiction that
b\neq 0
. Since
\lim_{|y|\to\infty}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(y)=0
, we have

\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\infty}(y)=\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v(y)+b\to b\qquad\text{as }|y|\to\infty.

In particular, if
b>0
, there exists
R_{0}>0
such that
\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\infty}\geq b/2
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R_{0})
, hence

(4.10)

\int_{B(0,d)}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\infty}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\geq c\,b\,d^{3}\quad\text{ for all }\,d\gg 1.

On the other hand, by the blow-up procedure (via stereographic coordinates) and the local
expansion of
g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
around
p_{k}
, for any fixed
d>0
we have

\Big|\int_{B(0,d)}\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w_{\infty}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{r_{k}}\Big|\int_{B_{r_{k}d}(p_{k})}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{w}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\Big|\leq C_{0}d,

where we used (
4.8
) in the last inequality. This contradicts (
4.10
) by letting
d\to\infty
. The case
b<0
can be ruled out analogously.
Therefore
b=0
, and we complete the proof of Claim.

Since
\|Q^{3}_{\hat{g}_{k}}-Q^{3}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},\hat{g}_{k})}\to 0
as
k\to+\infty
, we deduce from (

) that

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}=

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}Q^{3}_{\infty}(p)e^{3\hat{w}_{\infty}}\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\int_{B_{r}(p)}Q^{3}_{\infty}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}+o(1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}Q_{\infty}^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}+o(1)\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}(Q_{\hat{g}_{k}}^{3}+|Q_{\hat{g}_{k}}^{3}-Q_{\infty}^{3}|)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}+o(1)

\displaystyle\,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}Q_{\hat{g}_{k}}^{3}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}+o(1)=4\pi^{2}+o(1).

Moreover, since
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{g}_{k}}=1
, it implies that
p

and that

). By (

) and Proposition 3.4 of
[
17
]
, it follows that the family of conformal transformations

associated with normalized metrics converges to a constant map
p
. In essence, using conformal invariance of

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\hat{\Phi}_{k}\mathcal{P}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}^{3}(\hat{\Phi}_{k})\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}=

Hence, up to a subsequence,

converges weakly in

\Lambda\hat{\phi}=P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\phi=\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{\phi}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\,\mathcal{P}^{3}_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{\psi}_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\psi_{i},

where we also used
\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\psi_{i}=0
in
\mathbb{B}^{4}
and Lemma 6.2 in
[
1
]
in the last equation.
Since
(\lambda_{i},\psi_{i})
is a Steklov eigenpair for problem (
5.4
), we further deduce from (
1.20
) and (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle\Lambda\hat{\phi}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}P^{3,b}_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\psi_{i}=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}\psi_{i}}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\frac{\partial\psi_{i}}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}-\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{\psi}_{i}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(\lambda_{i}+1)\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}\hat{\psi}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{i}+1)(\lambda_{i}+2)\hat{\psi}_{i}.

Combining this with (
5.9
), we obtain

0=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(\lambda_{i}(\lambda_{i}+1)(\lambda_{i}+2)-\Lambda)\hat{\psi}_{i}.

By (
5.8
) and the fact that
\{\hat{\psi}_{i}\}
is a basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})
, we get

a_{i}=0
for all
i
. Hence
\hat{\phi}\equiv 0
, contradicting the fact that

\phi
is an eigenfunction.
This proves the claim.

Finally, on
(\mathbb{B}^{4},g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}})
the Steklov spectrum satisfies
\lambda_{i}=k

with multiplicity
(k+1)^{2}
.
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
\{\psi_{i}:\lambda_{i}=k\}
be a basis of Steklov eigenfunctions.
Since each
\psi_{i}
is harmonic in
\mathbb{B}^{4}
and satisfies

\frac{\partial\psi_{i}}{\partial\nu_{g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}}}=k\psi_{i}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
it follows from (
1.20
) and (
5.6
) that
each
\psi_{i}
gives rise to an eigenfunction of problem (
5.1
)
with eigenvalue
\Lambda_{k}=k(k+1)(k+2)
.
Hence, the eigenspace corresponding to

is spanned by

\{\psi_{i}:\lambda_{i}=k\}
and has dimension
(k+1)^{2}
.
Arranging the eigenvalues in nondecreasing order and counting multiplicities
then yields (
5.7
), which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.1

Then, as

Expanding

For the case

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}\langle\nabla_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}T_{h},\nabla_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}x_{i}\rangle_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}e^{3\hat{v}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}=0\quad\mbox{ for }\,i=1,2,3,4.

) that

F_{2}=|\beta|^{2}+\hat{F}_{2}=|B|^{2}+\hat{F}_{2}+o(1)F_{2}.

We complete the proof in three steps.

Step 1. Excluding the case
2|B|^{2}\leq\hat{F}_{2}
for large
t
.

Assuming that
2|B|^{2}\leq\hat{F}_{2}
for sufficiently large
t
. Then (
5.28
) implies that

\hat{F}_{2}\geq\frac{2}{3}(1+o(1))F_{2}\quad\text{ for sufficiently large $t$.}

Arguing as in the derivation of (
5.22
), we obtain for large

that

G_{2}\geq\Lambda_{5}^{g}\hat{F}_{2}=(\Lambda_{5}+o(1))\hat{F}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{5}F_{2}=12F_{2},

where we used
\Lambda_{5}=24
. Applying Lemma
5.1
, we conclude that

\frac{\mathrm{d}F_{2}}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-(12+o(1))F_{2}\leq-F_{2}\quad\text{ for sufficiently large $t$.}

As in the proof of Theorem
1.2
,
this implies that the flow

g(t)
converges exponentially fast to a metric of
T
-curvature
a positive constant multiple of
f
, zero
Q
-curvature and mean curvature, contradicting our hypothesis in this section.
Therefore, there exists an arbitrarily large time
t_{1}\geq 0
such that

2|B(t_{1})|^{2}>\hat{F}_{2}(t_{1})
.

Step 2. Introducing
\delta(t)
and estimating
\hat{F}_{2}
.

Write
F_{2}=(1+\delta(t))|B|^{2}
.
Then (
5.28
) gives that

(5.29)

\hat{F}_{2}=F_{2}-\frac{F_{2}}{1+\delta(t)}+o(1)F_{2}=\frac{\delta(t)}{1+\delta(t)}F_{2}+o(1)F_{2}.

Since
F_{2}-|B|^{2}\geq o(1)F_{2}
by (
5.28
),
we have
\delta(t)\geq o(1)
and hence
\delta(t)>-\frac{1}{2}
for all
t\geq t_{0}
and
sufficiently large
t_{0}\geq 0
.
In addition, for sufficiently large
t_{1}
as above, we have

\delta(t_{1})=\frac{F_{2}(t_{1})}{|B(t_{1})|^{2}}-1<4
since
F_{2}(t_{1})\leq 3|B(t_{1})|^{2}+o(1)F_{2}(t_{1})
. By continuity,
\delta(t)<4
for all
t
sufficiently close to
t_{1}
.

Step 3. A differential inequality for
\delta(t)
.

From (
5.11
)-(
5.13
), and Lemma
5.1

we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}F_{2}}{\mathrm{d}t}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\delta}{\mathrm{d}t}|B|^{2}+2(1+\delta)B\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2\Lambda_{i}-12+o(1))|\beta^{i}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,-36\hat{F}_{2}+o(1)F_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,-\Big(\frac{36\delta}{1+\delta}+o(1)\Big)F_{2},

in view of
\Lambda_{i}\geq\Lambda_{5}=24
for all
i\geq 5
and (
5.29
).
Since Lemma
5.3
implies
|B\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}B}{\mathrm{d}t}|=o(1)F_{2}

for
t
near
t_{1}
, it follows that

\frac{\mathrm{d}\delta}{\mathrm{d}t}|B|^{2}\leq-\Big(\frac{36\delta}{1+\delta}+o(1)\Big)F_{2}=-(36\delta+o(1))|B|^{2}

and then

(5.30)

\frac{\mathrm{d}\delta}{\mathrm{d}t}\leq-(36\delta+o(1)).

In particular, for sufficiently large

,
we obtain
\delta(t)<4
for all

. The differential inequality
(
5.30
) shows that
\delta(t)\to 0

as

, which completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda_{i}-12)|\hat{v}^{i}|^{2}+O(\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{3})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,\sum_{i=5}^{\infty}(\Lambda_{i}-12)|\hat{v}^{i}|^{2}+o(\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{2})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,\frac{\Lambda_{5}-12}{\Lambda_{5}+1}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(\Lambda_{i}+1)|\hat{v}^{i}|^{2}+o(\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}^{2}).

Since
\Lambda_{5}=24
(see (
5.7
)) and
\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}
is assumed sufficiently small, the desired estimate follows as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Before stating the next lemma, we introduce some notation.

For
r_{0}>0
and each critical point
p_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
of
f
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{r_{0}}(p_{i}):=\Big\{w\in C^{\infty}_{*}:

\displaystyle\,g=e^{2w}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}\mbox{ induces
a normalized metric}\,h=\Phi^{*}g=e^{2v}g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}

\displaystyle\mbox{ with }\hat{\Phi}=\hat{\Phi}_{p,s}\mbox{ for some }p\in\mathbb{S}^{3},\,s\in(0,1]\mbox{ such that }

\displaystyle\|\hat{v}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}+|p-p_{i}|^{2}+s^{2}<r_{0}^{2}\Big\}.

For convenience, we regard
(s,p,v)

as local coordinates for
w\in\mathcal{B}_{r_{0}}(p_{i})
.
Since each critical point of
f
is assumed nondegenerate, the Morse lemma yields local coordinates near

such that one may write

in

(with

We now state the next technical lemma, which is mainly concerned with expansions of the relevant quantities in terms of the local coordinates
(s,p,v)

Lemma 7.2
.

For
r_{0}>0
and
w=(s,p,v)\in\mathcal{B}_{r_{0}}(p_{i})
for some
1\leq i\leq N
. Then there hold

(i)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{p,s}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}=f(p)+2s^{2}\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}f(p)+O(s^{3})+o(1)s\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})},

where

(ii)

\Big|\frac{\partial E_{f}[w]}{\partial s}+\frac{64\pi^{2}s}{3}\frac{\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}f(p)}{f(p)}\Big|=O(s^{2}+(s+|p-p_{i}|)\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})}).

(iii) For any

, there holds

\Big|\frac{\partial E_{f}[w]}{\partial p}\cdot q+\frac{16\pi^{2}}{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}f(p)\cdot q}{f(p)}\Big|=O(s(s+\|\hat{v}\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{3},g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}})})|q|).

(iv) Set
A=A(w)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\circ\hat{\Phi}_{p,s}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{h}}

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Suppose by contradiction that the flow
(
2.13
)
diverges for every initial value
u_{0}
and
there is no conformal metric in the conformal class of
g_{\mathbb{B}^{4}}
with the
T
-curvature equal
to
f
.
As we will see, the flow (
2.1
)
then may be used to show that
for sufficiently large
\beta_{0}
, the set
E_{f}^{\beta_{0}}

is contractible.
Moveover, the flow defines a homotopy equivalence of the set
N_{0}=E_{f}^{\beta_{0}}

with a set
N_{\infty}
whose homotopy type is that of a point
\{p_{0}\}
with cells of dimension

3-\operatorname{morse}\mkern 1.0mu(f,p)
attached for every critical point
p
of
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}

such that
\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}f(p)<0
.
We then, by standard Morse theory (see
[

), obtain the identity

(7.2)

\sum_{i=0}^{3}t^{i}m_{i}=1+(1+t)\sum_{i=0}^{3}t^{i}k_{i}

for the Morse polynomials of

and

, where

is defined as in (

).
Equating the coefficients in the polynomials on the left and right hand side of (

),
we obtain (
1.23
), which violates the hypothesis in Theorem

and thus leads to the desired contradiction.
∎

The proof of Corollary

now follows as an immediate consequence.

Proof of Corollary
1.1
.

If we substitute

into the Morse polynomial identity (

), derived under the assumption of non-existence, we obtain

\sum_{i=0}^{3}(-1)^{i}m_{i}=1.

In view of the definition of

in (

), this is equivalent to

\sum_{\{a\in\mathbb{S}^{3}:\nabla_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}f(a)=0,\Delta_{g_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}}f(a)<0\}}(-1)^{\operatorname{morse}\mkern 1.0mu(f,a)}=-1,

which contradicts the hypothesis (

) of Corollary

. Therefore, that hypothesis forces the existence of a solution, and Corollary

∎

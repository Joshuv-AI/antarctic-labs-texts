Emotion-o1 : Adaptive Long Reasoning for Emotion Understanding in LLMs
Changhao Song, Yazhou Zhang, Peng Zhang

Abstract
Emotion understanding includes basic tasks (e.g., sentiment/emotion classification) and advanced tasks (e.g., sarcasm/humor detection). Current methods rely on fixed-length CoT reasoning, failing to adapt to the varying complexity of emotions. We propose a task-adaptive reasoning framework that employs DeepSeek-R1 to generate variable-length reasoning chains for different emotion tasks. By combining fine-tuning with reinforcement learning, we design a composite reward function that balances four objectives: prediction accuracy, adaptive reasoning depth control, structural diversity in reasoning paths, and suppression of repetitive logic. This approach achieves dynamic context-sensitive inference while enabling LLMs to autonomously develop deep reasoning capabilities. Experimental results demonstrate consistent improvements in both Acc and F1 scores across four tasks: emotion, sentiment, humor, and sarcasm. Notably, peak enhancements reached 3.56% F1 (2.76% Acc) for basic tasks and 37.95% F1 (23.14% Acc) for advanced tasks. Our work bridges rigid CoT reasoning and emotional complexity through adaptive-depth analysis.

Introduction

Emotion understanding has become a crucial research focus in NLP, driving applications from mental health assessment to social media analytics. The emergence of large language models (LLMs) has spurred extensive exploration into LLM-based affective computing systems. Current methodologies mostly adopt fixed-length linear reasoning frameworks. Empirical evaluations expose their limitations in handling emotional complexity, particularly for context-dependent phenomena like sarcasm. While basic emotional patterns (e.g., joy or anger) can be detected through explicit linguistic cues, complex affective expressions involving rhetorical constructs demand hierarchical cognitive processing and require dynamic reasoning architectures. The structural rigidity of stepwise analytical approaches often results in misinterpretations of abstract humor and multi-layered sarcasm.

Three principal shortcomings emerge from this analysis. First, uniform reasoning depth neglects task-specific requirements—simple cases might need surface-level processing while complex scenarios require iterative reflection. Second, repetitive logic patterns in generated reasoning chains restrict structural diversity. Third, current methods lack mechanisms to balance accuracy with adaptive reasoning control. These limitations highlight the need for length-sensitive frameworks that dynamically adjust reasoning strategies.

Motivated by these challenges, we propose a task-adaptive reasoning framework combining fine-tuning and reinforcement learning (RL). Our key insight is that emotional complexity varies both across tasks and individual samples, necessitating flexible reasoning chains. Specifically, we develop DeepSeek-R1 to generate variable-length paths incorporating advanced operations like backtracking and reflection. To optimize reasoning quality, we design a multi-objective reward function addressing four dimensions: prediction accuracy, depth adaptation, structural diversity, and redundancy suppression. This approach enables LLMs to autonomously learn context-aware reasoning strategies.

Our contributions are threefold: (1) A dynamic reasoning framework enabling adaptive-depth analysis that automatically adjusts cognitive granularity across heterogeneous affective tasks, from coarse sentiment polarity to nuanced sarcasm implications; (2) A composite RL reward mechanism balancing accuracy and reasoning quality; (3) Systematic validation across four emotion understanding tiers: achieving consistent gains in basic emotion classification (+2.01%-3.30% F1), stronger improvements in humor detection (+11.42%-14.95% Acc), and remarkable leaps in sarcasm understanding (+23.14%-38.14% across metrics). These graduated improvements demonstrate our framework’s unique capability to handle emotional complexity. The results demonstrate our method establishes an adaptive interface between structured reasoning patterns and the contextual dynamics of affective cognition, enabling context-sensitive adjustments from shallow sentiment analysis to deep pragmatic inference.

Problem Formulation

In this section, we define the key notations and then provide an overview of the supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL) methods employed for eliciting Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

2.1
Notation

This study centers on emotion recognition in textual analysis. Given the input text
x
, the LLM with parameters
\theta
generates the output sequences
y
. The model’s conditional probability distribution is defined as
\pi_{\theta}(y_{t}|x,y_{1:t-1})
, where the output contains both reasoning steps CoT(
y
) and the final emotion label. The emotion label is obtained by converting the last element of sequences
y
into numerical form through digit mapping. We specifically investigate "long reasoning chains" (Long CoT) - extended reasoning patterns that demonstrate both increased length and greater complexity compared to standard reasoning processes. These enhanced reasoning chains carry out several crucial functions:

1) Text decomposition
: Separating text components (semantics/syntax/rhetoric) for detailed analysis.

2) Assumption reflection
: Systematically examining initial interpretations for validity.

3) Consistency verification
: Ensuring logical alignment across different reasoning stages.

4) Contradiction handling
: Managing conflicting information using transition markers (e.g., "however").

5) Error correction
: Adjusting reasoning paths when inconsistencies are detected.

These steps enable the model to process emotional information more thoroughly while maintaining logical rigor throughout the analysis. The extended reasoning process particularly enhances the system’s ability to handle nuanced emotional expressions in text.

2.2
Training Setup

We adopt the general representative model Qwen2.5-7B as the base model. To investigate the impact of Long-CoT on different emotional tasks, we conduct experiments on four types of emotional tasks: sentiment, emotion, humor, and sarcasm. We select the widely recognized IMDB dataset for sentiment analysis, the MELD dataset for emotion recognition, the RedditHumorDetection dataset for humor detection, and the Sarcasm Corpus V2 dataset for sarcasm identification as benchmark datasets. Their training set parts are used to perform knowledge distillation on DeepSeek-R1. Responses are synthesized through rejection sampling, and we construct training sample prompt sets of 30,147, 21,966, 17,304, and 26,460 for these four tasks respectively. These datasets contain verifiable true answers. Specifically, we first generate a fixed number N of candidate responses for each prompt, and then only retain the responses whose final answers are consistent with the corresponding true answers. To increase the diversity of the dataset, we consider both linear and nonlinear reasoning methods, as well as long and short reasoning chains in different dimensions when constructing the sample prompt sets. 2.3
Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

During the fine-tuning stage, we used the four constructed datasets to train separately on four types of sentiment tasks. Given that Long-CoT accounted for a large proportion in the training data, we increased the maximum token length to 2048. The supervised fine-tuning model
\pi_{\theta}
, trained for 3 epochs, served as the initialization model for reinforcement learning and participated in the subsequent training.

2.4
Evaluation Setup

We conduct the inference test using the test set of the benchmark dataset described in Section
2.2
. Our training data mainly focus on the field of sentiment computing. The four datasets respectively represent several different levels of difficulty for sentiment recognition tasks, providing a comprehensive framework for sentiment analysis. Unlike general generation tasks where diverse outputs are often desired, we only need the final sentiment polarity. Therefore, to ensure the correct statistical results, we set the temperature parameter
t=0.0
and the maximum output length to 10 tokens. This configuration helps to produce consistent and reliable sentiment polarity outputs for accurate evaluation.

SFT with Long CoT

3.1
Result

To evaluate the effectiveness of the SFT model, we selected representative contemporary models, including open-source solutions (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3, Qwen2.5-7B) and closed-source systems (Claude-3.7, Grok-3, GPT-4), for comparative analysis. 1(d)
.

In the sentiment classification task, our model achieved comprehensive superiority over all comparison models with an accuracy of 0.6713, a macro F1 score of 0.6442, and a weighted F1 score of 0.6680. Compared to Qwen2.5-7B (with an accuracy of 0.6437), our model shows an 2.76% improvement in accuracy, a 3.56% improvement in macro F1, and a 2.99% improvement in weighted F1. Its significant advantages indicate the robustness of the model in the emotion polarity judgment task, especially in terms of balancing multi-class performance (macro F1) and sample weight adaptability (weighted F1).

In the emotion classification task, our model achieved the highest values with an accuracy of 0.5820 and a weighted F1 score of 0.5901. Compared to the previous best model Grok-3 (with an accuracy of 0.5497), our model improved by 3.23% in accuracy. Although Grok-3 has a higher Macro-F1 score (0.5406), our model led with a 2.01% advantage in weighted F1 (vs. 0.5501 of Grok-3), indicating that our model is more suitable for scenarios with imbalanced sample distributions. Compared to Qwen2.5-7B, our model’s stability was significantly better.

Our model has achieved a breakthrough improvement in the humor classification task. With an accuracy of 0.7617, a macro F1 score of 0.7587, and a weighted F1 score of 0.7588, it comprehensively leads in performance. Compared to Qwen2.5-7B (with an accuracy of 0.6475), our model shows an 11.42% improvement in accuracy, a 14.95% improvement in macro F1 score, and a 14.91% improvement in weighted F1 score. This result verifies that our model demonstrates significantly greater ability than existing mainstream models (such as GPT-4 with an accuracy of 0.5483) in capturing semantic subtleties and the context of humorous intent.

In the sarcasm classification task, our model ranked second with an accuracy of 0.7617, a macro F1 score of 0.7587, and a weighted F1 score of 0.7588, just behind GPT-4 (which has an accuracy of 0.7793). Although our model did not surpass GPT-4, it still exceeded the accuracy of Qwen-Plus by 0.36% (Qwen-Plus has an accuracy of 0.7581), and its macro F1 and weighted F1 scores were superior to all models except GPT-4. It is particularly noteworthy that our model significantly outperforms same-scale models (such as the DeepSeek series and Claude-3.7), demonstrating its competitiveness in complex semantic tasks.

Conclusion

Our task-adaptive reasoning framework dynamically generates variable-length CoT chains to address emotional complexity, overcoming limitations of fixed-length reasoning. By integrating SFT with multi-objective RL, the method achieves significant gains: peak improvements of +3.56% F1 for basic tasks (sentiment/emotion) and +37.95% F1 for advanced tasks (sarcasm/humor), effectively overcoming rigid CoT constraints in nuanced emotion understanding.

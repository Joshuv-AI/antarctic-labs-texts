Fermionic quantum walkers coupled to a bosonic reservoir
Olivier Bourget, Alain Joye, Dominique Spehner

Abstract
We analyse the discrete-time dynamics of a model of non-interacting fermions coupled to an infinite reservoir formed by a bosonic quantum walk on ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . This dynamics consists of consecutive applications of free evolutions of the fermions and bosons followed by a local coupling between them. The unitary operator implementing this coupling accounts for energy exchanges between the system and reservoir while it preserves the number of fermions. The free fermion evolution is given by a second-quantized single-particle unitary operator satisfying some genericity assumptions. The free boson evolution is given by the second-quantized shift operator on ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} . We derive explicitly the Heisenberg dynamics of fermionic observables and obtain a systematic expansion in the large-coupling regime, which we control by using spectral methods. We also prove that the reduced state of the fermions converges in the large-time limit to a mixture of infinite-temperature Gibbs states in each particle sector.



1
Introduction

Quantum walks (QWs) have emerged as a powerful and versatile modeling tool in quantum physics, with natural applications in areas such as quantum optics and condensed matter physics. They also provide non-trivial models for the mathematical analysis of quantum scattering and spectral theory. Moreover, QWs are used in quantum computing and information processing as building blocks for quantum search algorithms, and they are considered non-commutative extensions of classical random walks and Markov chains in probability theory. For further details, see
[
Ke
,
V-A
,
CC
,
P
,
J2
,
ABJ2
,
GZ
,
QMS
,
Sa
,
G
,
APSS
,
RT
,
T
,
CJWW
,
J3
]
and references therein.

In the study of transport phenomena in complex random quantum systems, QWs offer a simplified yet relevant framework for rigorously analyzing the dynamics of quantum particles on infinite discrete structures, such as graphs or lattices (see, e.g.
[
Ko
,
JM
,
ASW
,
ABJ1
,
HJS
,
J1
,
HJ2
,
CFGW
,
CFO
]
). When statistical ensembles of particles are considered, fermionic QWs coupled to an environment provide a natural model for studying open quantum systems. The papers
[
HJ3
,
R
,
AJR
]
offer a mathematical description of the onset of an asymptotic state in the case of a single reservoir and describe the particle currents induced by multiple reservoirs in non-equilibrium situations.

In this work, we analyse the discrete-time dynamics of non-interacting fermionic QWs
on a finite graph (the sample) coupled to an infinite reservoir of bosonic QWs on the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
. One time step of the dynamics first consists for the fermionic and bosonic quantum walkers to undergo unitary free dynamics on the sample and reservoir, respectively. This is then followed by a unitary coupling between the fermionic and bosonic QWs, acting locally on the reservoir. The coupling step allows for energy exchanges while it preserves the number of fermions in the sample.

An example of sample-reservoir coupling we have in mind describes fermionic transitions between two fixed vertices of the graph induced by absorption and emission of bosons at a single lattice site of
{\mathbb{Z}}
, the coupling having
the form
commonly encountered in quantum optics and condensed matter physics
to modelize atom-photon and electron-phonon interaction processes.
The free fermionic evolution is given by the second-quantization of a single-particle unitary operator describing hopping of fermions between vertices.
The free bosonic evolution is given by the second-quantization of the shift operator on
{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The initial state of the bosonic QWs is assumed to be a quasi-free state, similar to a thermal state. Unlike previous studies
[
HJ3
,
R
,
AJR
]
, the distinct natures of the particles in the sample and reservoir prevent us from reducing the problem to a one-body problem. Additionally, while previous works have focused on weak coupling regimes, our framework naturally leads us to explore the large coupling regime, akin to the high-temperature regime, to analyze the asymptotic state of the sample.

In this framework, we derive an explicit description of the Heisenberg dynamics of observables acting on the fermionic sample in terms of the main characteristics of the model. For any fixed time, this expression yields a systematic expansion in the large-coupling regime, which we control using spectral methods. Furthermore, under natural generic assumptions on the single-fermion unitary operator describing the free evolution, we prove that the reduced state of the fermionic QWs converges in the large-time limit to a mixture of infinite-temperature Gibbs states in each particle sector. Our approach takes advantage of the local nature of the coupling on the bosonic lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
to expand the time-evolved fermionic observables as infinite series that can be controlled in the large-coupling limit.

Our work, albeit devoted to a specific coupling involving only one reservoir, belongs to the mathematical analysis of related models characterized by local couplings between fermionic and bosonic particles on graphs or lattices. In particular, a fermionic sample coupled to two bosonic reservoirs in different thermodynamical states, hosting asymptotic currents between reservoirs, can be analysed using a similar approach; this question lies beyond the scope of the present paper and will be addressed in future works.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: The model is described in the next section, while Section 3 sets the notation and recalls the basic properties of Fock spaces that will be used in the following sections. Section 4 is devoted to deriving the expression for the Heisenberg evolution of the sample observables and analyzing its behavior in the large coupling regime. The spectral and long-time properties of the dynamics are presented in Section 5 under genericity assumptions. The paper concludes with Section 6, which justifies some of these assumptions on the basis of unitary random matrices.

2
Model and main results

We consider fermionic particles
with a finite-dimensional single-particle Hilbert space
{\cal H}
of dimension
d<\infty
.
The fermionic Fock space is the second quantization
{\cal F}_{-}=\Gamma_{-}({\cal H})
of
{\cal H}
,
so that
{\cal F}_{-}
has dimension
2^{d}
(see Sec.
2
for more detail).
The fermions do not interact with each other, thus their free dynamics is given by
the second-quantization
\Gamma_{-}(V)
of
a unitary operator
V
on
{\cal H}
. We adopt the convention that a particle on a discrete configuration space defines a QW when the unitary operator giving its one time step evolution only couples sites of the configuration space a finite distance away. Since
{\cal H}
can be identified with
\ell^{2}(\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda
is a finite graph with
d=\operatorname{card}(\Lambda)
sites, any unitary operator
V
on
{\cal H}
can be considered the evolution of a QW.

The fermionic particles are coupled to a reservoir composed of
non-interacting bosonic QWs, with Hilbert space given by the bosonic Fock space
{\cal F}_{+}=\Gamma_{+}({\cal H}_{\rm B})
,

{\cal H}_{\rm B}=\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
being the single-boson space and

\Gamma_{+}({\cal H}_{\rm B})
its second quantization.
The reservoir’s free dynamics is described by the second quantization
\Gamma_{+}(S)
of
the shift operator

S
on
{\cal H}_{\rm B}
defined by

S\delta_{j}=\delta_{j-1}\;\;,\;\;j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\;,

(2.1)

where
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
is the canonical basis of
{\cal H}_{\rm B}
.
The reservoir is an example of a statistical ensemble of QWs on a lattice
[
HJ3
,
R
,
AJR
]
.

The fermion-reservoir coupling is given by a unitary operator
U_{\rm int}
on
\mathcal{F}_{-}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{+}
which has the following form. We denote
by
b_{j}
and
b_{j}^{\ast}
the bosonic annihilation and creation operators at site
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
The coupling of the QWs on the sample with the QWs in the reservoir is given by the following unitary operator
on
{\cal F}_{-}\otimes{\cal F}_{+}

U_{\rm int}={\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\lambda T\,\otimes\,(b_{0}+b_{0}^{\ast})/\sqrt{2}}\;,

(2.2)

where
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a system-reservoir coupling constant,
(b_{0}+b_{0}^{\ast})/\sqrt{2}
is the bosonic field operator at lattice site
0
, acting on
{\cal F}_{+}
, and

T
is a self-adjoint operator on
{\cal F}_{-}
.
Note that the coupling Hamiltonian inside the exponential only involves the field operator at site
j=0
(local coupling) and is linear in the field operator.
We suppose that

T={\rm{d}}\Gamma_{-}(\tau)
is the second quantization of a self-adjoint operator
\tau
on
{\cal H}
, although our results in Sec.
4

do not need this assumption. In analogy with the free dynamics, the coupling unitary (
2.2
) is then the second quantization

U_{\rm int}=\Gamma_{-}\otimes{\rm Id}_{{\cal F}_{+}}(V_{\rm int})
of a unitary operator
V_{\rm int}={\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\lambda\tau\otimes(b_{0}+b_{0}^{\ast})/\sqrt{2}}
on
{\cal H}\otimes{\cal F}_{+}
(see Sec.
2
).

Like in the models of fermionic quantum walks studied in
[
HJ3
,
R
,
AJR
]
, the total evolution operator of the
fermionic walkers and reservoir is given by a sequential application
of the free evolution
operator
\Gamma_{-}(V)\otimes\Gamma_{+}(S)
and the
coupling unitary
U_{\rm int}
,

U_{\lambda}=U_{\rm int}\,(\Gamma_{-}(V)\otimes\Gamma_{+}(S))\;.

(2.3)

The initial state is a decorrelated state (product state)

\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{B}\;,

(2.4)

where
\omega_{S}
and
\omega_{B}
are the initial fermionic and bosonic states, respectively. The bosons are
assumed to be initially in a quasi-free state (see Sec
4.1
for more details).

The state of the fermionic walkers and reservoir is given at the time step
t\in{\mathbb{N}}
by

\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{B}\circ{\cal U}_{\lambda}{(\cdot)^{t}}\quad,\quad{\cal U}_{\lambda}(\cdot)=U^{\ast}_{\lambda}\cdot U_{\lambda}\;,

(2.5)

where
{\cal U}_{\lambda}(\cdot)^{t}
denotes the
t^{\rm th}
power of
{\cal U}_{\lambda}(\cdot)
. We are interested in the evolution at time
t
of observables of the system;
more precisely in the map
{\cal T}_{t}:{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
such that for any
X\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
,

\omega_{S}({\cal T}_{t}(X))=\omega_{S}\otimes\omega_{B}({\cal U}_{\lambda}^{t}(X\otimes{\mathds{1}})).

(2.6)

A specific example within the family of models considered above is a fermionic QW on
a finite graph
\Lambda
with
d=\operatorname{card}(\Lambda)
sites, coupled locally to bosonic QWs by the unitary operator
U_{\rm int}
of the form
(
2.2
) with

T=T_{\rm{hop}}\equiv{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}a_{1}+{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}\;.

(2.7)

Here,
a_{l}
and

a_{l}^{\ast}
are the fermionic annihilation and creation operators at site
l\in\Lambda

and

\varphi
is a Peierls phase. The latter describes the effect of a magnetic field.
The operator
T
on
{\cal F}_{-}
is the second quantization
{\rm{d}}\Gamma_{-}(\tau)
of the single particle self-adjoint operator

\tau_{\rm{hop}}={\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}|e_{2}\rangle\langle e_{1}|+{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}|e_{1}\rangle\langle e_{2}|
, where

e_{l}
is the state of a particle localized at site
l\in\Lambda
.
Note the local character of the interaction at the fermionic sites
1
and
2
.
The unitary
U_{\rm int}
with
T
given by (
2.7
) models the interaction of fermionic atoms with the electromagnetic field
within the dipolar approximation when the field is only coupled to states
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
, and the
e_{l}
’s correspond to atomic energy eigenstates.
The dipolar approximation, which consists in neglecting non-linear terms in the field operator

(b_{0}+b_{0}^{\ast})/\sqrt{2}
,
is justified when the size of the system is much smaller than the variation of the electromagnetic field.

Remark 2.1
.

The model with the choice
T=T_{\rm{hop}}

is related to, but different from, the
Jaynes-Cumming model of quantum optics
[
BP
]
. In the latter model, in addition to the dipolar approximation one performs a rotating-wave approximation to remove non-secular terms
[
CTDRG
]
. This
amounts to drop the terms
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}|e_{2}\rangle\langle e_{1}|\otimes b_{0}^{\ast}
and
{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}|e_{1}\rangle\langle e_{2}|\otimes b_{0}
in the interaction Hamiltonian. After second quantization, this leads to the coupling unitary

U_{\rm int}^{\rm RWA}={\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\lambda({\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}a_{1}\,\otimes\,b_{0}+{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}\,\otimes\,b_{0}^{\ast})/\sqrt{2}}\;,

(2.8)

where the first (respectively second) term inside the parenthesis describes the absorption (resp. emission) of a photon from site
j=0
, provoking the hopping
of a fermion from site
l=1
to site
l=2
(resp. a hopping from
l=2
to
l=1
).
If one associates to each site
l\in\Lambda
an energy
E_{l}
and assume that
E_{2}>E_{1}
, the evolution operator

U_{\rm int}^{\rm RWA}
can be interpreted as
modelling energy exchanges between the sample and reservoir. A similar interpretation can be drawn for
U_{\rm int}
in (
2.2
) with
T=T_{\rm{hop}}
,
albeit one keeps in the Hamiltonian
terms
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}a_{1}\otimes b_{0}^{\ast}
and
a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}\otimes b_{0}
associated to non-energy conserving processes.
Although the unitaries (
2.8
) and (
2.2
) describe similar physics, their mathematical structure is different. Our results below can not be straightforwardly extended to system-reservoir couplings of the form (
2.8
).

Let us summarize the main results of this paper.

1)

Expansion of the fermionic evolution operator
{\cal T}_{t}
at large coupling constants
\lambda
.

We show in Sec.
4
that the time-evolved fermionic observables
{\cal T}_{t}(X)
can be written as an infinite series,
the first term of which is equal to
({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)
, where

{\cal V}(\cdot)=\Gamma_{-}(V^{-1})\cdot\Gamma_{-}(V)
gives the free evolution of fermionic observables and
\Phi
is an orthogonal projector on
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
for the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product. The projector
\Phi
describes the effect on the sample of the coupling with the reservoir in the infinite coupling constant limit
\lambda\to\infty
. Actually, we show that the remainder of the truncated series
formed by
the first
s
terms, with
s\geq 1
, is of order

e^{-c\lambda^{2}(s+1)}
for some constant
c>0
.

More precisely,
we prove that if the reservoir initial state
\omega_{B}
is a quasi-free state (see Sec
4.1
) and
T
is a self-adjoint
operator on
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
, then there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and
t\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\star}
, the norm of the remainder is bounded from above by

C_{s}\|X\|\,{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/2}{\rm e}^{-\lambda^{2}(s+1)\Delta/4}

(2.9)

for some constants
C_{s}>0
and
\gamma>0
, where

\Delta=\min_{\mu\not=\nu,\mu,\nu\in\sigma(T)}|\mu-\nu|
is the minimal spectral gap of
T
.
In particular, taking
s=1
, one obtains that

{\cal T}_{t}\to({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}\quad\text{uniformly in $t$ as $\lambda\to\infty$\;,}

(2.10)

where the convergence is in operator norm.

2)

Exponential convergence to a steady state in the large time limit.

We show in Sec.
5
that the fermionic state converges in the large time limit
t\to\infty
to a steady state
\omega_{S}^{\infty}
given by an infinite-temperature Gibbs state in each
n
-particle subspace
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}\subset{\cal F}_{-}
.

Let us first discuss the assumptions under which we can prove this convergence. In addition
to the quasi-freeness hypothesis on the reservoir initial state
\omega_{B}
and the choice of
T
as the second-quantized of a self-adjoint operator on
{\cal H}
(see above), we make the following hypotheses on the coupling operator
T
and the fermionic free evolution operator
\Gamma_{-}(V)
. Let
B^{\mu}
,
\mu\in\sigma(T)
, be the spectral projectors of
T
. We assume that

(i)

{\cal V}
has simple eigenvalues save for the eigenvalue
1
, which is
\dim{\cal F}_{-}
- fold degenerate.

(ii)

For all
n=1,\ldots,d
, the matrix of the restriction
B^{\mu}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
in the eigenbasis of
\Gamma(V)|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
has only non zero diagonal elements, for all
\mu\in\sigma(T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})
, and for all matrix indices
k,l
,
k\not=l
, at least one spectral projector
B^{\mu}
has a nonzero entry with indices
(k,l)
.

Condition (ii) basically means that the spectral projectors of
T
connect all pairs of eigenstates of
\Gamma_{-}(V)
.
Under these assumptions, we show that
there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for
\lambda>\lambda_{0}
and
X\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega_{S}({\cal T}_{t}(X))=\omega_{S}^{\infty}(X)\;,

(2.11)

where the asymptotic state
\omega_{S}^{\infty}(\cdot)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{S}^{\infty}\,\cdot)
has a density matrix given by

\rho_{S}^{\infty}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}(\rho_{S}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\;.

(2.12)

In particular, if there are initially
n
fermions in the sample, i.e., if the initial state
\omega_{S}
has a density matrix
\rho_{S}
with support and range in
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
,
then the asymptotic state is an infinite-temperature Gibbs state in the
n
-particle sector,

\rho_{S}^{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\;,

(2.13)

where, with a slight abuse of notation,
{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
stands for the
orthogonal projector onto
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
.

3
Notation

In this section we briefly specify the notation used and recall the definitions and basic properties of the fermionic and bosonic creation and annihilation operators.

Given a Hilbert space
{\cal H}
of dimension
d<\infty
, one constructs the associated
fermionic Fock space
{\cal F}_{-}
as follows (see e.g.
[
BR
]
for more detail).
Let
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
denote the
n
-fold tensor product of
{\cal H}
. The antisymmetric tensor product of the
vectors
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in{\cal H}
,
1\leq n\leq d
, reads

u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}\epsilon_{\pi}u_{\pi(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes u_{\pi(n)}\ \in\ {\cal H}^{\otimes n}\;,

(3.1)

where
S_{n}
is the group of permutations of
\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\epsilon_{\pi}
is the signature of
\pi\in S_{n}
. The scalar product of two such vectors is given by

\displaystyle\langle u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}|v_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge v_{n}\rangle=\det\big((\langle u_{k}|v_{l}\rangle)_{1\leq k,l\leq n}\big)\;,

(3.2)

where
\langle u|v\rangle
is the scalar product of
u
and
v
in
{\cal H}
.
For
1\leq n\leq d
, the orthogonal projection
{\cal P}_{-}^{(n)}
acting on
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
is defined by linearity and its action on the
n
-fold product vectors,

{\cal P}_{-}^{(n)}u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\,u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}\;.

(3.3)

The
n
-fold antisymmetric tensor product of
{\cal H}
, denoted by
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
, is the subspace
{\cal P}_{-}^{(n)}{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
.

If
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
is a basis of
{\cal H}
,
then
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
is generated by the vectors
f_{i_{1}}\wedge f_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i_{n}}
,
{\underline{i}}=(i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{n})\in I_{n}
, where

I_{n}=\bigl\{{\underline{i}}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}\,;\,1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dots<i_{n}\leq d\big\}

(3.4)

is the set of ordered indices. The fact that one can restrict
{\underline{i}}
to this set follows from the antisymmetry of
f_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i_{n}}
under the permutation of two vectors.
By (
3.2
), if the basis
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
is orthonormal then
\{f_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i_{n}}\}_{{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}}
is an orthonormal basis of
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
. Thus

{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
has dimension

\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}
.
For short, we will denote in what follows the basis vectors by

f_{i_{1}}\wedge f_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i_{n}}=\wedge^{n}f_{{\underline{i}}}\quad,\quad{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}\;.

(3.5)

The antisymmetric Fock space is then defined as

{\cal F}_{-}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}{\cal H}^{\wedge n}\ \text{ with }\ {\cal H}^{\wedge 0}=\mathbb{C}\Omega\;,

(3.6)

where
\Omega
denotes the vacuum vector. Thus,
{\cal F}_{-}
has dimension
2^{d}
.

For any
\phi\in{\cal H}
, the fermionic creation operator
a^{*}(\phi)
on
{\cal F}_{-}
is defined by linearity and its action
on
\Omega
and on
u_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge u_{n}\in{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
,
1\leq n\leq d
, as

a^{*}(\phi)\Omega=\phi\,,\quad a^{*}(\phi)u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}=\phi\wedge u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}\;.

(3.7)

The creation operator maps
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
onto
{\cal H}^{\wedge(n+1)}
and satisfies
a^{*}(\phi)^{2}=0
. Note that

u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}=a^{*}(u_{1})\cdots a^{*}(u_{n})\Omega\;.

(3.8)

The adjoint
a(\phi)
of
a^{*}(\phi)
satisfies
a(\phi)\Omega=0
and

a(\phi)u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j-1}\langle\phi|u_{j}\rangle u_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{j-1}\wedge u_{j+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge u_{n}.

(3.9)

The creation and annihilation operators satisfy the Canonical Anticommutation Relations (CAR): for any
\phi,\psi\in{\cal H}
,

\{a(\phi),a(\psi)\}=\{a^{*}(\phi),a^{*}(\psi)\}=0,\ \ \ \{a(\phi),a^{*}(\psi)\}=\langle\phi|\psi\rangle{\mathds{1}}\;,

(3.10)

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes the anticommutator.
The number operator
n(\phi)
is the self-adjoint operator

\displaystyle n(\phi)=a^{*}(\phi)a(\phi)\;.

(3.11)

Using (
3.10
) one finds that
n(\phi)^{2}=\|\phi\|^{2}\,n(\phi)
.
Hence if
\phi
is normalized then
n(\phi)
is an orthogonal projector.
For a fixed orthonormal basis
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
of
{\cal H}
, the simpler notations
a^{\ast}_{j}=a^{\ast}(f_{j})
,
a_{j}=a(f_{j})
and
n_{j}=n(f_{j})
will be used in the sequel.

The construction of the bosonic Fock space
{\cal F}_{+}
associated to the single-particle Hilbert space
{\cal H}_{B}
proceeds analogously, replacing the projectors (
3.3
)
by the projectors
{\cal P}_{+}^{(n)}
onto the symmetrized vectors

{\cal P}_{+}^{(n)}u_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\pi\in S_{n}}u_{\pi(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{\pi(n)}

(3.12)

with
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geq 1
.
Then

{\cal F}_{+}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal P}_{+}^{(n)}{\cal H}^{\otimes n}\ \text{ with }\ {\cal P}_{+}^{(0)}{\cal H}^{\otimes 0}=\mathbb{C}\Omega\;.

(3.13)

For any
f\in{\cal H}_{B}
, the bosonic creation operator
b^{\ast}(f)
is defined similarly to (
3.7
) as

b^{\ast}(f){\cal P}_{+}^{(n)}\phi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{n}={\sqrt{n+1}}\,{\cal P}_{+}^{(n+1)}f\otimes\phi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\phi_{n}\;.

(3.14)

Its adjoint is denoted by
b(f)
. Unlike in the fermionic case,
b^{\ast}(f)^{2}
does not vanish.
The creation and annihilation operators
b^{\ast}(f)
and
b(f)
satisfy the Canonical Commutation Relations (CCR)

\big[b(f),b(g)\big]=\big[b^{*}(f),b^{*}(g)\big]=0,\ \ \ \big[b(f),b^{*}(g)\big]=\langle f|g\rangle\ {\mathds{1}}

(3.15)

for any
f,g\in{\cal H}_{B}
.
The operators
b^{\ast}(f)
and
b(f)
are unbounded and it is convenient to work with
unitary Weyl operators defined by

W(f)={\rm e}^{\frac{{\rm{i}}}{\sqrt{2}}(b(f)+b^{\ast}(f))}\ ,\quad f\in{\cal H}_{B}\;,

(3.16)

which inherit from the CCR the composition law

W(f)W(g)=W(f+g){\rm e}^{-\frac{{\rm{i}}}{2}\mathrm{Im}\,\langle f|g\rangle}.

(3.17)

Let us recall the action of the second quantizations
\Gamma_{\pm}(A)
of
A
, a contraction on
{\cal H}
, i.e.
\|A\|\leq 1
, on the fermionic, respectively bosonic, Fock spaces (see
[
BR
]

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
{\cal T}_{t}:{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})\rightarrow{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
is completely positive, unital and trace preserving.

Proof.

The map
{\cal T}_{t}
is the composition of
X\mapsto X\otimes{\mathds{1}}
from
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
to
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})\otimes{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
,
Y\mapsto(U_{\lambda}^{\ast})^{t}\,Y\,U_{\lambda}^{t}
on
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})\otimes{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
, and
Y\mapsto\omega_{B}(Y)
from
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})\otimes{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
to
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
, where
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
is finite dimensional and
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
is separable. It is thus immediate to see that the identity is preserved. Then, the first map above is completely positive since it preserves the spectrum, while the second one is completely positive thanks to Kraus Theorem
[
Kr
]
. It remains to show that the third map is completely positive to get that
{\cal T}_{t}
is completely positive.

Since
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
is finite dimensional, the complete positivity of the third map amounts to showing that

\omega_{B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{n}
from
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
to
M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is positive for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geq 1
. Any
B\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
can be viewed as a matrix
(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
with
B_{ij}\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
,
\forall\,i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, so that
\omega_{B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{n}(B)=(\omega_{B}(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. The positivity of the latter amounts to having
\omega_{B}(\sum_{i,j}\bar{\beta}_{i}\beta_{j}B_{ij})\geq 0
for any
\{\beta_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which holds true if
\sum_{i,j}\bar{\beta}_{i}\beta_{j}B_{ij}
is a positive element of
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})
, since
\omega_{B}
is a state. But
(B_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
is positive in
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{+})\otimes M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
if for all
\{\varphi_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
,
\varphi_{j}\in{\cal F}_{+}
,
\sum_{i,j}\langle\varphi_{i}|B_{ij}\varphi_{j}\rangle\geq 0
. In particular,

\varphi_{j}=\beta_{j}\varphi
for some
\varphi\in{\cal F}_{+}
yields
\sum_{i,j}\langle\varphi|\bar{\beta}_{i}\beta_{j}B_{ij}\varphi\rangle\geq 0
for any
\varphi\in{\cal F}_{+}
. Hence,
\omega_{B}\otimes{\mathds{1}}_{n}
is positive.

Finally, that fact that
{\cal T}_{t}
is trace preserving is a consequence of the cyclicity of the trace, the unitarity of
\Gamma_{-}(V)
, and the identities
B^{\mu}B^{\nu}=\delta_{\mu,\nu}B^{\mu}
and
\sum_{\mu}B^{\mu}={\mathds{1}}
of the spectral projectors

, which make the exponential factor in (

∎

Repeated Interaction Case:

In the particular case where the state of the reservoir is characterized by a symbol

which is diagonal in the canonical basis
\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}

]

Corollary 4.6
.

Assume

,

Proof.

The spectral assumption on

makes the correlations within the reservoir factorize,

\exp\bigg\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\Big\langle\Theta^{{\underline{\mu}}-\underline{\nu}}_{t}\,,K\,\Theta^{{\underline{\mu}}-\underline{\nu}}_{t}\Big\rangle\bigg\}={\prod_{j=1}^{t}e^{-(\mu_{j}-\nu_{j})^{2}k_{j}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}}\;,}

).

We set

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}=\sum_{k\ \mbox{\rm\tiny s.t.}\atop|\lambda_{k}|=1}{\cal P}_{k},\ \ {\cal P}_{<}=\sum_{k\ \mbox{\rm\tiny s.t.}\atop|\lambda_{k}|<1}{\cal P}_{k},\ \ \mbox{so that}

\displaystyle{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}^{\ast},\ \ {\cal P}_{<}={\cal P}_{<}^{\ast},\ {\cal P}_{\bigcirc}{\cal P}_{<}={\cal P}_{<}{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}=0,\ \ {\cal P}_{\bigcirc}+{\cal P}_{<}={\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})},

(4.30)

and decompose
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
in two orthogonal subspaces accordingly

{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})+{\cal P}_{<}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-}):={\cal B}_{\bigcirc}+{\cal B}_{<}\;.

(4.31)

Then,
\exists\;C,\gamma>0
such that

\displaystyle{\cal V}\Phi|_{{\cal B}_{<}}\ \ \mbox{satisfies}\ \ \|({\cal V}\Phi|_{{\cal B}_{<}})^{n}\|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma n}

(4.32)

\displaystyle{\cal V}\Phi|_{{\cal B}_{\bigcirc}}\ \ \mbox{is unitary},

where any
\gamma<\min\{|\ln(|\lambda_{k}|)|\,;\,\lambda_{k}\in\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\setminus{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\}
will do.
The restriction
{\cal V}\Phi|_{{\cal B}_{<}}
is completely non unitary, and the above provides in our setup the explicit decomposition of any contraction into a unitary restriction and a completely non unitary contraction, see
[
SFBK
]
.

To determine the eigenvalues of
{\cal V}\Phi
on the unit circle
{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
, we use the following general result taken from
[
HJ1
]
:

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
U
be unitary and
P
be an orthogonal projector on a (possibly infinite dimensional) Hilbert space

{\cal H}
. For any
\varphi\in{\cal H}
and
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
one has

\displaystyle UP\varphi=e^{i\theta}\varphi

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\varphi=P\varphi\ \mbox{and }\ e^{i\theta}\varphi=U\varphi=PUP\varphi

(4.33)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle PU\varphi=e^{i\theta}\varphi.

(4.34)

Moreover, writing
Q={\mathds{1}}-P
, it holds

\displaystyle\ker QUP=\{0\}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma_{p}(UP)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}=\sigma_{p}(PU)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}=\sigma_{p}(PUP)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}=\emptyset\;,

(4.35)

where
\sigma_{p}
denotes the point spectrum.

According to Lemmas
4.8
and
4.9
, if
e^{i\theta}
is an eigenvalue of
{\cal V}\Phi
on the unit circle
then it is also an eigenvalue of
{\cal V}
with the same eigenvector, and this eigenvector is invariant under
\Phi
.
Note that
{\mathds{1}}\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
is an invariant vector under both
{\cal V}
and
\Phi
, so that
1\in\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
.

4.4
Estimate on the rest of the series for
{\cal T}_{t}(X)

Let
\Delta>0
be the minimal spectral gap of the coupling operator
T
,

\Delta=\min_{\mu,\nu\in\sigma(T),\mu\not=\nu}|\mu-\nu|\;.

(4.36)

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\gamma<\min\{|\ln(|\lambda_{k}|)|\,;\,\lambda_{k}\in\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\setminus{\mathbb{S}}^{1}\}
.
Assume that the initial state of the reservoir is the quasi-free state satisfying (
4.11
) with
K\geq{\mathds{1}}
. Then there exist
\lambda_{0}>0
and
0<C_{0}<\infty
such that
|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}
implies that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
X\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
,

\|{\cal T}_{t}(X)-({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\|\leq C_{0}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|t{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/2}{{\rm e}^{-\Delta\lambda^{2}/4}\;.}

(4.37)

Proof.

Recall that for
1\leq s\leq t
, the general term in the series (
4.24
) defining
{\cal T}_{t}(X)
reads

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{s}\leq t}\;\;\sum_{{\mu_{j_{1}}\not=\nu_{j_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{j_{s}}\not=\nu_{j_{s}}}\atop{\nu_{j_{i}},\mu_{j_{i}}\in\sigma(T)}}({\cal V}\Phi)^{j_{1}-1}{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu_{j_{1}}\nu_{j_{1}}}({\cal V}\Phi)^{j_{2}-j_{1}-1}{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu_{j_{2}}\nu_{j_{2}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38109pt\cdots({\cal V}\Phi)^{j_{s}-j_{s-1}-1}{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu_{j_{s}}\nu_{j_{s}}}({\cal V}\Phi)^{t-j_{s}}(X)\exp\bigg\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\sum_{p,q=1}^{s}(\mu_{j_{p}}-\nu_{j_{p}})(\mu_{j_{q}}-\nu_{j_{q}})\big\langle\delta_{j_{p}}\,,K\,\delta_{j_{q}}\big\rangle\bigg\}.

(4.38)

Thus we are lead to consider the operator

{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}({\cal V}\Phi)^{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mu\neq\nu\in\sigma(T)
.
We know from Lemma
4.9
that
\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}\subset\operatorname{ran}\Phi
, where
{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}
is defined in (
4.30
).
Hence

{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}=\Phi\,{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}\,\Phi\;.

(4.39)

We now show that

{\cal V}{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}{\cal V}
.
Let
\lambda_{k}\in\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
and
{\cal P}_{k}
be the spectral projector of
{\cal V}\Phi
associated to
\lambda_{k}
. By Lemma
4.9
again,

\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{k}\subset\ker\big({\cal V}-\lambda_{k}{\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})}\big)=\ker\big({\cal V}^{-1}-{\lambda}_{k}^{-1}{\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})}\big)\;.

(4.40)

Thus

X\in\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{k}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;{\cal P}_{k}{\cal V}(X)=\lambda_{k}{\cal P}_{k}(X)={\cal V}{\cal P}_{k}(X)\;.

(4.41)

On the other hand, if
X\in(\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{k})^{\bot}
then for any
Y\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
,

\langle{\cal V}(X),{\cal P}_{k}(Y)\rangle=\langle X,{\cal V}^{-1}{\cal P}_{k}(Y)\rangle={\lambda}_{k}^{-1}\langle X,{\cal P}_{k}(Y)\rangle=0.

(4.42)

This shows that
{\cal V}(X)\subset(\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{k})^{\bot}
. Therefore,

X\in(\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{k})^{\bot}\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;{\cal P}_{k}{\cal V}(X)={\cal V}{\cal P}_{k}(X)=0\;.

(4.43)

One infers from (
4.41
) and (
4.43
) that
{\cal P}_{k}{\cal V}={\cal V}{\cal P}_{k}
, hence, as stated above,

{\cal V}{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}{\cal V}\;.

(4.44)

For
\mu\not=\nu
, one deduces from (
4.39
), (
4.44
) and
{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}\Phi=\Phi{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}=0
that

{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}={\cal P}_{\bigcirc}{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}=0

(4.45)

and thus

\ \ [{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu},{\cal P}_{<}]=0,\ \ \mbox{since}\ \ {\cal P}_{\bigcirc}+{\cal P}_{<}={\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})}\;.

(4.46)

Therefore,

{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}({\cal V}\Phi)^{n}={\cal P}_{<}{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}{\cal P}_{<}({\cal V}\Phi)^{n}{\cal P}_{<},\ \ \forall\ \mu\neq\nu\in\sigma(T)

(4.47)

so that, thanks to (
4.32
) and Lemma
4.8
,

\|{\cal V}{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}({\cal V}\Phi)^{n}\|\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma n}\;.

(4.48)

Hence, making use of
\|{\cal V}\|=\|{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}\|=1
and bounding
the exponential factor in (
4.38
) as in (
4.22
) by

\exp\bigg\{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}\sum_{p=1}^{s}(\mu_{j_{p}}-\nu_{j_{p}})^{2}\bigg\}\;\;\leq\;\;{{\rm e}^{-s\Delta\lambda^{2}/4}}\;,

(4.49)

the term (
4.38
) is bounded from above in norm by

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\cdots<j_{s}\leq t}

\displaystyle\;\;\sum_{{\mu_{j_{1}}\not=\nu_{j_{1}},\ldots,\mu_{j_{s}}\not=\nu_{j_{s}}}\atop{\nu_{j_{i}},\mu_{j_{i}}\in\sigma(T)}}C^{s+1}{\rm e}^{-\gamma(t-s)}{{\rm e}^{-s\Delta\lambda^{2}/4}}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle{\begin{pmatrix}t\\
s\end{pmatrix}\ell^{s}C^{s+1}{\rm e}^{-s\Delta\lambda^{2}/4}{\rm e}^{-\gamma(t-s)}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|\;,}

(4.50)

where we have set
\ell=|\sigma(T)|(|\sigma(T)|-1)
with
|\sigma(T)|
the cardinal of
\sigma(T)
.
Let us set
\kappa_{\lambda}=\ell\,C\,{\rm e}^{-\Delta\lambda^{2}/4}\,{\rm e}^{\gamma}=O({\rm e}^{-\Delta\lambda^{2}/4})
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. We obtain

\displaystyle\|{\cal T}_{t}(X)-({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\|\

\displaystyle\leq\ C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t}\sum_{s=1}^{t}(\kappa_{\lambda})^{s}\begin{pmatrix}t\\
s\end{pmatrix}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|

\displaystyle\leq\ C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t}\,t\,\kappa_{\lambda}(1+\kappa_{\lambda})^{t-1}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|

\displaystyle=\ C\frac{\kappa_{\lambda}}{1+\kappa_{\lambda}}\,t\,{\rm e}^{-t(\gamma-\ln(1+\kappa_{\lambda}))}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|

\displaystyle\leq C{\kappa_{\lambda}}\,t\,{\rm e}^{-t(\gamma-\ln(1+\kappa_{\lambda}))}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|\;.

(4.51)

Suppose
\lambda^{2}
is large enough so that
\ln(1+\kappa_{\lambda})\leq\gamma/2
, which defines

\lambda_{0}^{2}=\Delta^{-1}\big(2\gamma+4\ln(\ell C)-4\ln(1-{\rm e}^{-\gamma/2})\big)\;.

(4.52)

Then the exponential in (
4.51
) is bounded by
{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/2}

for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
. This yields the result.
∎

We consider now the estimate of the reminder
{\cal R}_{t}(X)
in (
4.25
), when we retain the leading order correction on the expression for
{\cal T}_{t}(X)
.

Corollary 4.11
.

Under the asumptions of Theorem
4.10
, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
and
C_{1}<\infty
such that
|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}
implies that for all
t\in\mathbb{N}
and

,

\|{\cal R}_{t}(X)\|\leq C_{1}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|t^{2}{\rm e}^{-\gamma t/2}{{\rm e}^{-\Delta\lambda^{2}/2}}\;.

(4.53)

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generally that
t\geq 2
. We simply have to consider (
4.51
) with summation starting at
s=2
instead of
s=1
. We thus get

\displaystyle\|{\cal R}_{t}(X)\|

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\gamma t}\sum_{s=2}^{t}(\kappa_{\lambda})^{s}\begin{pmatrix}t\\
s\end{pmatrix}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|\leq C\frac{\kappa^{2}_{\lambda}}{(1+\kappa_{\lambda})^{2}}t(t-1){\rm e}^{-t(\gamma-\ln(1+\kappa_{\lambda}))}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|\;.

(4.54)

We conclude as in the proof of Theorem
4.10
.
∎

5
Large Time Asymptotics

5.1
Assumptions on the coupling and fermion evolution operators.

In this section we further specify the coupling operator
T\in{\cal B(F_{-})}
by assuming it is given by the second quantization
T=d\Gamma_{-}(\tau)
of a self-adjoint operator
\tau
on
\cal H
, where we assume hereafter that the single-particle Hilbert space has dimension
d\geq 2
. Explicitely, writing the spectral decomposition of
\tau\in{\cal B(H)}
as

\tau=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\varepsilon_{j}|f_{j}\rangle\langle f_{j}|

(5.1)

with eigenvalues
\varepsilon_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
repeated according to multiplicity, and orthonormal eigenbasis
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
, we consider (see (
3.20
))

T=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\varepsilon_{j}a(f_{j})^{*}a(f_{j}).

(5.2)

According to the convention, we will write
a_{j}=a(f_{j}),a^{*}_{j}=a^{*}(f_{j})
and
n_{j}=a_{j}^{\ast}a_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,d
.

It follows from (
3.19
) that the eigenvectors of
T
are of the form
\wedge^{q}f_{{\underline{j}}}=f_{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge f_{j_{q}}
for
q\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
and
{\underline{j}}\in I_{q}
an ordered multi-index, i.e.
1\leq j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{q}\leq d
,

T\,(\wedge^{q}f_{{\underline{j}}})=\mu_{q,{\underline{j}}}\,(\wedge^{q}f_{{\underline{j}}})\;,\quad\mu_{q,{\underline{j}}}=\sum_{r=1}^{q}\varepsilon_{j_{r}}

(5.3)

with the conventions
\wedge^{0}f_{{\underline{j}}}=\Omega
and
\mu_{0,{\underline{j}}}=0
.
Since
\{\wedge^{q}f_{{\underline{j}}},\,0\leq q\leq d,\,{\underline{j}}\in I_{q}\}
forms a basis of
{\cal B(F_{-})}
,
the spectrum of
T
consists in
\sigma(T)=\{\mu_{q,{\underline{j}}},\,0\leq q\leq d,\,{\underline{j}}\in I_{q}\}
. We keep writing the spectral decomposition of
T\neq 0
as

T=\sum_{\mu\in\sigma(T)}\mu B^{\mu},

(5.4)

where the eigenprojectors
B^{\mu}
can be written explicitly in terms of products of the number operators
n_{j}
and
{\mathds{1}}-n_{i}
.
Note that
T
leaves
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
invariant, thus the same is true for its spectral projectors
B_{\mu}
.

With
T
of the form (
5.2
) we can complement Lemma
4.8
as follows:

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\cal V},{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}
, and
\Phi
be the operators on the Hilbert space
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
defined by (
4.5
), (
4.16
) and (
4.18
). Then,
the operators
{\cal V},\Phi
and
{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}
leave

{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n},{\cal H}^{\wedge m})
invariant for all
0\leq n,m\leq d
and
\mu,\nu\in\sigma(T)
.

Proof.

By construction,
\Gamma_{-}(V)
and
B^{\mu}
preserve the number of particles,
i.e. they leave
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
invariant for all
1\leq n\leq d
.
More precisely, recall that
\{\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{i}}\}_{{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}}

is an orthonormal basis of
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
. Thus
{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n},{\cal H}^{\wedge m})
is spanned by the rank one operators

\displaystyle|\wedge^{m}f_{{\underline{j}}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{n}f_{{\underline{i}}}|\quad,\quad{\underline{i}}\in I_{n},{\underline{j}}\in I_{m}\;.

(5.5)

Since

\Gamma_{-}(V^{-1})\wedge^{n}f_{{\underline{i}}}=V^{-1}f_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge V^{-1}f_{i_{n}}\ \in\ {\cal H}^{\wedge n}\quad,\quad{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}\;,

(5.6)

see (
3.18
), one deduces from (
4.5
) that

{\cal V}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n},{\cal H}^{\wedge m})\subset{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n},{\cal H}^{\wedge m})
.
Similarly, since the projectors
B^{\mu}
leave
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
invariant, it follows that

{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n},{\cal H}^{\wedge m})
is invariant under
{\cal B}^{\mu\nu}
for any
\mu,\nu\in\sigma(T)
. The same is true for
\Phi=\sum_{\mu\in\sigma(T)}{\cal B}^{\mu\mu}
.
∎

We now look for the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of
{\cal V}
.
Recall that
{\cal V}(\cdot)=\Gamma(V^{-1})\cdot\Gamma(V)
, where
V\in{\cal B}({\cal H})
is unitary. Let
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\cal H}
of eigenvectors of
V
, with corresponding eigenvalues
{\rm e}^{i\alpha_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq d
.
Then, using
a similar shorthand
\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{i}}=\psi_{i_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge\psi_{i_{n}}

as in (
3.5
) and the convention
\wedge^{0}\psi_{\underline{i}}=\Omega
,

\{\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{i}}\}_{{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}}

is an orthonormal basis of
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
for any
0\leq n\leq d
.
Moreover, by (
3.18
),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}(V)(\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{i}}})={\rm e}^{i(\alpha_{i_{1}}+\dots+\alpha_{i_{n}})}\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{i}}}\quad,\quad{\underline{i}}\in I_{n},

(5.7)

(recall that
\Gamma(V)\Omega=\Omega
).
Hence we have for all

0\leq n,m\leq d
,

\displaystyle{\cal V}\big(|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{j}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{i}}|\big)={\rm e}^{-i\big(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{j_{r}}-\sum_{s=1}^{m}\alpha_{i_{s}}\big)}|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{j}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{i}}|

(5.8)

and
{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
is spanned by the eigenvectors of
{\cal V}
given by the rank-one operators

|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{j}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{i}}|
.
Note also that the eigenvalue
1\in\sigma({\cal V})
is at least
\dim({\cal F}_{-})
-fold degenerate, since

|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{i}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{i}}|\ \subset\ \ker({\cal V}-{\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})})

(5.9)

for all
{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}
and
0\leq n\leq d
.

We work under the following spectral non-degeneracy assumption on
{\cal V}
:

(
SND
)
{\cal V}
has simple eigenvalues save for the eigenvalue
1
which is
\dim({\cal F}_{-})
-fold degenerate, i.e., for all
0\leq n,m,n^{\prime},m^{\prime}\leq d
, any
({\underline{i}},{\underline{j}})\in I_{m}\times I_{n}

{\underline{i}}\neq{\underline{j}}
and any
({\underline{i}}^{\prime},{\underline{j}}^{\prime})\in I_{m^{\prime}}\times I_{n^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{n}\alpha_{j_{r}}-\sum_{s=1}^{m}\alpha_{i_{s}}\Big)-\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{n^{\prime}}\alpha_{j_{r}^{\prime}}-\sum_{s=1}^{m^{\prime}}\alpha_{i_{s}^{\prime}}\Big)\not\in 2\pi{\mathbb{Z}}\ \ \mbox{ when }\ ({\underline{i}},{\underline{j}})\not=({\underline{i}}^{\prime},{\underline{j}}^{\prime})\;,

with the convention
\sum_{r=1}^{0}\alpha_{j_{r}}=0
for
{\underline{j}}\in I_{0}
.

Consequently, if
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}\in\sigma({\cal V})\setminus\{1\}
, this eigenvalue is simple and its eigenvectors are proportional to

|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{j}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{i}}|

(5.11)

for some
0\leq n,m\leq d
and
{\underline{j}}\in I_{m}
,
{\underline{i}}\in I_{n}

determined by

.
Now by Lemma

if and only if

and the corresponding eigenvectors are in the range of the projector
\Phi
.
Moreover, the eigenvectors of
{\cal V}\Phi
are also eigenvectors of
{\cal V}
with the same eigenvalue
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}
, thus
they have the form (
5.11
) under assumption (
SND
) when
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}\not=1
.
The following lemma gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the eigenvectors
(
5.11
) of
{\cal V}
to be in the range of
\Phi
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\in{\cal F}_{-}
,
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\not=0
.
Then

\displaystyle|\phi_{1}\rangle\langle\phi_{2}|

\displaystyle=\Phi\big(|\phi_{1}\rangle\langle\phi_{2}|\big)

(5.12)

if and only if there exists a unique
\tilde{\mu}\in\sigma(T)
such that

\displaystyle B^{\tilde{\mu}}\phi_{1}=\phi_{1}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ B^{\tilde{\mu}}\phi_{2}=\phi_{2}.

(5.13)

Proof.

Assume that (
5.12
) is true.
Then, thanks to the definition (
4.18
) of
\Phi
,
for all
(\mu,\nu)\in\sigma(T)^{2}
,

\mu\not=\nu\quad\Rightarrow\quad B^{\mu}|\phi_{1}\rangle\langle\phi_{2}|B^{\nu}=0\;,\ \ \mbox{ i.e. }\ \ B^{\mu}\phi_{1}=0\mbox{ or }B^{\nu}\phi_{2}=0\;.

Since
(\sum_{\mu\in\sigma(T)}B^{\mu})\phi_{1}=\phi_{1}\not=0

there exists some
\tilde{\mu}\in\sigma(T)

satisfied by the spectral projectors.
∎

Relying on this lemma, we obtain

.

Assume that
(
SND
)
holds. Then

\Omega

Remark 5.4
.

The vectors
\Omega
and
F=f_{1}\wedge f_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{d}
, which span
{\cal H}^{\wedge 0}
and
{\cal H}^{\wedge d}
respectively, are eigenvectors of
\Gamma_{-}(V)
with respective eigenvalues
1
and
\det V
and of
T
with respective eigenvalues
0
and
\mu_{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\varepsilon_{i}
. If
\mu_{F}=0
, both these vectors belong to

\operatorname{ran}(B^{0})
, recall (
5.4
), in which case by (
4.5
) and Lemma
5.2
,

{\cal V}\Phi(|\Omega\rangle\langle F|)=(\det V)|\Omega\rangle\langle F|\quad,\quad{\cal V}\Phi(|F\rangle\langle\Omega|)=(\det V^{\ast})|F\rangle\langle\Omega|\;

(5.17)

and
\det V
and
\det V^{\ast}
belong to
\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\cap{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. Moreover, since
\det V={\rm e}^{i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\alpha_{j}}
,
these eigenvalues are simple and different from
1
by the spectral non-degeneracy assumption
(
SND
)
.

On the other hand, if
\mu_{F}\not=0
then
\det V,\det V^{\ast}\notin\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)
since

\Phi(|\Omega\rangle\langle F|)=\Phi(|F\rangle\langle\Omega|)=0
.

We now show that under some additional assumption on the diagonal elements of the spectral projectors of
T
in the eigenbasis of
V
,

\det V
and
\det V^{\ast}
are the only potential eigenvalues of
{\cal V}\Phi
on the unit circle, together with
1
.

(
Diag
)

For all
1\leq n\leq d-1
, all
{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
and all
\mu\in\sigma(T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})
, one has
\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{k}}}|B^{\mu}\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{k}}}\rangle\not=0
.

Note that the condition
\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{j}}}|B^{\mu}\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{j}}}\rangle\not=0

in (
Diag
) is equivalent to
\wedge^{n}\psi_{{\underline{j}}}\notin\ker B^{\mu}
. In view of (
5.16
), (
Diag
) holds if and only if for all
0\leq n\leq d-1
and
\mu\in\sigma(T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})
, there is some
{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{\mu}
such that
\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\rangle\not=0
for all
{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
.

We provide an argument in Section
6
below that supports the genericity of that condition for random unitary matrices.
We have

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume
(
SND
)
,
(
MND
)
and
(
Diag
)
.Then,

\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)\cap\mathbb{S}^{1}=\left\{\begin{matrix}\{1,\det V,\det V^{\ast}\}&\text{if }F\in\ker T,\\[5.69054pt]
\{1\}\phantom{xxxxxxxxxx}&\text{otherwise.}\ \ \ \end{matrix}\right.

(5.18)

Proof.

In the first case, by Remark
5.4
, we need to show that any
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}\in\sigma({\cal V})\setminus\{1,\det V,\det V^{\ast}\}
does not belong to

\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)
.
Thanks to (
SND
), these eigenvalues
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}
are simple
and are associated to eigenvectors of the form
|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{l}}|
for some
(n,m)\not\in\{(0,d),(d,0),(0,0),(d,d)\}

and
{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
,
{\underline{l}}\in I_{m}
.
Let us assume that
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}\in\sigma({\cal V}\Phi)
. Then, by Corollary
5.3
,

\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\in\operatorname{ran}B^{\mu}
and
\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{l}}\in\operatorname{ran}B^{\mu}

for some
\mu\in\sigma(T)
.
Given the above condition on
(n,m)
, one has either
0<n<d
or
0<m<d
. Let us first assume
0<n<d
. Since
\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}=B^{\mu}(\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}})
, one has
\mu\in\sigma(T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})
and for any
\nu\in\sigma(T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})
,
\nu\neq\mu
,
B^{\nu}(\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}})=B^{\nu}B^{\mu}(\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}})=0
. The existence of such
\nu\neq\mu
is ensured by (
MND
). This leads to a contradiction with (
Diag
). A similar contradiction arises when
0<m<d
, replacing
n
by
m
and
\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}
by
\wedge^{m}\psi_{\underline{l}}
. If
F\not\in\ker(T)
, the same argument holds for
{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\theta}\in\sigma({\cal V})\setminus\{1\}
.
∎

5.2
Invariant subspace of
{\cal V}\Phi

Let us consider now
{\cal P}_{1}
, the spectral projector associated to the eigenvalue
1
of
{\cal V}\Phi
.
By Proposition
5.5
, under assumptions (
SND
), (
MND
) and (
Diag
) one has, recall (
4.30
),

{\cal P}_{\bigcirc}=\left\{\begin{matrix}{\cal P}_{1}+{\cal P}_{\det V}+{\cal P}_{\det V^{\ast}}&\text{if $F\in\ker T$,}\\
{\cal P}_{1}\phantom{xxxxxxxxixxxxx}&\text{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.

(5.19)

In the first case, we already know that
\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{\det V}={\mathbb{C}}|\Omega\rangle\langle F|
and
\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{\det V^{\ast}}={\mathbb{C}}|F\rangle\langle\Omega|
, see Remark
5.4
.

According to Lemma
4.9
and the spectral non-degeneracy assumption (
SND
), if
X\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
then

X\in\ker({\cal V}-{\mathrm{Id}}_{{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})})=\operatorname{span}\big\{|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|\big\}_{0\leq n\leq d,{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}}\;,

(5.20)

where we have set
\wedge^{0}\psi_{\underline{k}}=\Omega
.
Moreover, Lemma
5.1
shows that the range of
{\cal P}_{1}
is the sum of the invariant subspaces of
{\cal V}\Phi
in each
n
-particle sector, thus

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{1}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})\ \ \mbox{ with }\ \ {\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})\ \subset\ \operatorname{span}\{|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|\}_{{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}}\ \subset\ {\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})\;.

(5.21)

We prove in this subsection that, under some additional assumptions on the unitary
V
,

{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})
is reduced to
{\mathbb{C}}{\mathds{1}}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
. Hence the eigenvalue
1
of
{\cal V}\Phi
is
(d+1)
-fold degenerated.

By (
5.21
), the elements
X\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
are of the form

\displaystyle X=\sum_{n=0}^{d}X_{n}\ \ \mbox{ with }\ \ X_{n}=\sum_{{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}}x_{{\underline{k}}}^{(n)}|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\rangle\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|\ \ \in\ \ {\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})\;,

(5.22)

where
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
One can in fact assume without loss of generality that
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e. that
X_{n}
is self-adjoint.
Actually, since
B^{\mu}=(B^{\mu})^{\ast}
, one has

{\cal V}\Phi(X^{\ast}_{n})=({\cal V}\Phi(X_{n}))^{\ast}
for any
X_{n}\in{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})
. Hence
X_{n}\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})
if and only if

\mathrm{Re}\,(X_{n})=(X_{n}+X^{\ast}_{n})/2
and
\mathrm{Im}\,(X_{n})=(X_{n}-X^{\ast}_{n})/(2{\rm{i}})\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n})
.

Let
X\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
be self-adjoint. We now show that

X=\sum_{n=0}^{d}x_{n}\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\quad,\quad x_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}\;,

(5.23)

where
{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
refers to the orthogonal projector onto
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
,
under the following hypothesis:

(
OffDiag
)
For any
1\leq n\leq d-1
and
{\underline{k}},{\underline{l}}\in I_{n}
,
{\underline{k}}\not={\underline{l}}
, one has
\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|B^{\mu}\,{\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{l}}}\rangle\not=0
for some
\mu\in\sigma(T)
.

Remark 5.6
.

(
OffDiag
)
implies
(
MND
)
. Indeed, let
0<n<d
. If

T|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}=c\,{\mathds{1}}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
for some
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then

B^{\mu}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}={\mathds{1}}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
or

B^{\mu}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}=0
for all
\mu\in\sigma(T)
. This implies that for any

{\underline{k}},{\underline{l}}\in I_{n}
,
{\underline{k}}\not={\underline{l}}
, one has

\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|B^{\mu}\,{\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{l}}}\rangle=0
for all
\mu
, in contradiction with
(
OffDiag
)
.

Let
1\leq n<d
and
X\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
. To prove that (
5.23
) holds under Assumption (
OffDiag
), we first note that
by (
5.22
), the
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}
are eigenvalues of
X_{n}
with eigenvectors
\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}
.
Assume that
X_{n}
has two distinct eigenvalues
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}\not=x_{\underline{l}}^{(n)}
. Thanks to Lemma
4.9
one has
\Phi(X)=X
, thus

XB^{\mu}=B^{\mu}X
for any
\mu\in\sigma(T)
. Since
B^{\mu}
leaves
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
invariant,

X_{n}B^{\mu}=B^{\mu}X_{n}
holds as well.
It follows that

B^{\mu}(\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}})
and
B^{\mu}(\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{l}})
are eigenvectors of
X
with eigenvalues
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}
and
x_{\underline{l}}^{(n)}
.
Because
X
is self-adjoint, these two eigenvectors must be orthogonal, i.e.

\langle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}|B^{\mu}\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{l}}\rangle=0\;,

(5.24)

for any
\mu\in\sigma(T)
. This contradicts (
OffDiag
). Hence
x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}=x_{\underline{l}}^{(n)}
for all
{\underline{k}},{\underline{l}}\in I_{n}
,
{\underline{k}}\not={\underline{l}}
,
implying that
X_{n}=x_{n}\,{\mathds{1}}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
.
This equality holds for
n=0
and
n=d
as well since
{\cal H}^{\wedge 0}
and
{\cal H}^{\wedge d}
are one-dimensional.

From the observation above, it follows that under assumption (
OffDiag
),
all
X={\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
are diagonal in each
n
-particle sector, being given by (
5.23
)
with complex constants
x_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
.

5.3
Exponential Convergence to a Steady State

We are now in a position to prove our main result.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let the initial state
\omega_{B}
of the reservoir be a quasi-free state satisfying (
4.11
) with
K\geq 1
. Let the coupling operator
T
be given by (
5.2
) and (
5.4
) and the free evolution on the sample
V
be such that Assumptions
(
SND
)
,
(
Diag
)
and
(
OffDiag
)
are satisfied. If
F\in\ker T
, suppose also that the initial state of the sample

\omega_{S}(\cdot)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{S}\,\cdot)
is given by a density matrix
\rho_{S}
such that

\omega_{S}(|\Omega\rangle\langle F|)=\omega_{S}(|F\rangle\langle\Omega|)=0
.
Then
there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for
|\lambda|>\lambda_{0}
and for any
X\in{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\omega_{S}({\cal T}_{t}(X))=\omega_{S}^{\infty}(X)

(5.25)

where the asymptotic state
\omega_{S}^{\infty}(\cdot)=\operatorname{tr}(\rho_{S}^{\infty}\,\cdot)
has a density matrix given by

\rho_{S}^{\infty}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}(\rho_{S}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\;.

(5.26)

The convergence in (
5.25
) is exponential.

Remark 5.8
.

i) In case
\omega_{S}
leaves all
n
-particle subspaces
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
invariant, i.e. ,

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}\rho_{S,n}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \rho_{S,n}\in{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n}),\text{ then }

\displaystyle\rho_{S}^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{d}\,\rho_{S,n}^{\infty}\ \ \text{with}\ \ \rho_{S,n}^{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}(\rho_{S,n})\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\in{\cal B}({\cal H}^{\wedge n}).

(5.27)

ii) The asymptotic state depends on the initial condition, so that, strictly speaking, it does not qualify to be an equilibrium state. However, as point i) shows, within each of the
n
-particle subspaces
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
the asymptotic state coincides with an infinite-temperature Gibbs state:

\rho_{S}=\rho_{S,n}\ \Rightarrow\ \rho_{S}^{\infty}=\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\;.

(5.28)

Proof.

We consider the case
F\in\ker T
, the other one being simpler. By Theorem
4.10
one has

\omega_{S}\big({\cal T}_{t}(X)\big)=\omega_{S}\big(({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\big)+\omega_{S}\big({\cal T}_{t}(X)-({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\big)\;\text{ with }\;\omega_{S}\big({\cal T}_{t}(X)-({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\big)\to 0

(5.29)

as
t\to\infty
, the convergence being exponential. By Proposition
5.5
and Remark
5.6
, one has

({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)=({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}{\cal P}_{<}(X)+({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}{\cal P}_{1}(X)+({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}{\cal P}_{\det V}(X)+({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}{\cal P}_{\det V^{\ast}}(X)\;.

(5.30)

Keeping in mind that
\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{\det V}={\mathbb{C}}|\Omega\rangle\langle F|
and
\operatorname{ran}{\cal P}_{\det V^{\ast}}={\mathbb{C}}|F\rangle\langle\Omega|
(see Remark
5.4
) and using the assumption

\omega_{S}(|\Omega\rangle\langle F|)=\omega_{S}(|F\rangle\langle\Omega|)=0
, we get

\omega_{S}\big(({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}(X)\big)=\omega_{S}\big(({\cal V}\Phi)^{t}{\cal P}_{<}(X)\big)+\omega_{S}\big({\cal P}_{1}(X)\big)

(5.31)

for all

.
By (
4.32
) the first term in the right-hand side  can be bounded by a constant times

{\rm e}^{-\gamma t}\|{\cal P}_{<}(X)\|
and thus vanishes exponentially fast as
t\to\infty
.
Now from (
5.23
) one obtains

{\cal P}_{1}(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{d}\begin{pmatrix}d\\
n\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}(X|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}})\,{\mathds{1}}_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}\;.

(5.32)

The result follows from the self-adjointness of
{\cal P}_{1}
so that
\omega_{S}({\cal P}_{1}(X))=\operatorname{tr}({\cal P}_{1}(\rho_{S})X)
,
and thus (
5.25
) holds with

\rho_{S}^{\infty}={\cal P}_{1}(\rho_{S})
.
∎

We get the following direct consequence when there is initially only one fermion in the sample and the observable
X
conserves the number of particles:

Corollary 5.9
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
5.7
on
\omega_{B}
and
T
, assume that
\rho_{S}=\rho_{S,1}
,

(5.33)

.

(
SND
)
et
(
Diag
)
to the one-particle subspace

.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{S}=\rho_{S,1}

to

5.4

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(e_{1}+{\rm e}^{i\varphi}e_{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big(e_{1}-{\rm e}^{i\varphi}e_{2}\big)

\displaystyle f_{j}

We denote by

According to (

The eigenspaces of

\displaystyle=\big\{(1,j_{2},\dots,j_{n})\big\}_{2<j_{2}<\dots<j_{n}\leq d}

\displaystyle I_{n}^{-}

\displaystyle=\big\{(2,j_{2},\dots,j_{n})\big\}_{2<j_{2}<\dots<j_{n}\leq d}\;.

(5.38)

\displaystyle I_{n}^{0}

\displaystyle=\big\{(1,2,j_{3},\dots,j_{n})\big\}_{2<j_{3}<\dots<j_{n}\leq d}\cup\big\{(j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{n})\big\}_{2<j_{1}<\dots<j_{n}\leq d}\;.

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}B^{+}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{span}\ \{f_{1}\}\cup\{f_{1}\wedge f_{j_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{j_{n}}\}_{j_{2}>2\atop 2\leq n\leq d-1}

\displaystyle\operatorname{ran}B^{-}

\displaystyle B^{+}-B^{-}

\displaystyle=n_{+}-n_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\big(a_{1}^{\ast}+{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}\big)\big(a_{1}+{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}\big)-\frac{1}{2}\big(a_{1}^{\ast}-{\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}\big)\big(a_{1}-{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}\big)

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{2}^{\ast}a_{1}+{\rm e}^{-{\rm{i}}\varphi}a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}=T_{\rm{hop}}\;.

(5.41)

Note that the restriction of

to
{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
satisfy (

) and that

.

Now, consider the matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}c_{1}(1)&c_{1}(2)&\cdots&c_{1}(d)\\
c_{2}(1)&c_{2}(2)&\cdots&c_{2}(d)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{d}(1)&c_{d}(2)&\cdots&c_{d}(d)\end{pmatrix}\in M_{d}(\mathbb{C}),

(5.42)

where
c_{j}(k)=\langle f_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle
are the coefficients of the eigenvectors
\psi_{k}
of
V
in the orthonormal basis
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
,

\psi_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c_{j}(k)f_{j}\quad,\quad k=1,\dots,d\;.

(5.43)

Thus
C
is the unitary matrix implementing the change from the
\{f_{j}\}
-basis to the orthonormal basis of eigenvectors of
V
.
Consequently, for all
0\leq n\leq d
and
{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
,

\displaystyle\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}=\sum_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}}c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\;,

(5.44)

where, thanks to (
3.2
),
c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})
is the following
n\times n
minor of the matrix
C
:

c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})=\big\langle\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}|\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\big\rangle=\begin{vmatrix}c_{j_{1}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{1}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{1}}(k_{n})\\
c_{j_{2}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{2}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{2}}(k_{n})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{j_{n}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{n}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{n}}(k_{n})\end{vmatrix}\;.

(5.45)

Assumptions (
Diag
) and (
OffDiag)
can be rewritten in terms of the minors
c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})
as follows:

(
Diag
)
for
T=T_{\rm{hop}}
:
For any
1\leq n\leq d-1
and any
{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
, there exists

{\underline{j}}^{+}\in I_{n}^{+}
,
{\underline{j}}^{-}\in I_{n}^{-}
and
{\underline{j}}^{0}\in I_{n}^{0}
such that

c_{{\underline{j}}^{+}}({\underline{k}})\neq 0
,
c_{{\underline{j}}^{-}}({\underline{k}})\neq 0
, and
c_{{\underline{j}}^{0}}({\underline{k}})\neq 0
.

(
OffDiag
)
for
T=T_{\rm{hop}}
: For any
1\leq n\leq d-1
and
{\underline{k}},{\underline{l}}\in I_{n},{\underline{k}}\not={\underline{l}}
, one has

\sum_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{\mu}}\overline{c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})}c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{l}})\not=0\quad\text{ for some }\mu\in\sigma(T)\;.

(5.46)

In particular, the condition (
Diag
) is satisfied in the one-particle subspace when all eigenvectors

\psi_{k}
of
V
are such that
\langle f_{j}|\psi_{k}\rangle\not=0
for
j=1
,
j=2
and at least one
j>2
.

We now prove the convergence to a steady state for the fermionic QWs on
a finite graph
\Lambda
with coupling operator
T_{\rm{hop}}
under an alternative set of hypotheses.

Let us denote for
n\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
\Gamma_{n}(V)=\Gamma(V)|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}=V\otimes\dots\otimes V
and

B^{\mu}_{n}=B^{\mu}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
, where
\mu\in\{+,-,0\}
.

(
Cyc
)
For any
2\leq n\leq d-1
, there exists a triplet
(\mu,\nu,\nu^{\prime})
with
\{\mu,\nu,\nu^{\prime}\}=\{+.-,0\}
such that
the
\ast
-algebra generated by the operators

B_{n}^{\mu}\Gamma_{n}(V)B_{n}^{\mu}\quad,\quad B_{n}^{\mu}\Gamma_{n}(V)B_{n}^{\nu}\Gamma_{n}(V)B_{n}^{\mu}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n}^{\mu}\Gamma_{n}(V)B_{n}^{\nu^{\prime}}\Gamma_{n}(V)B_{n}^{\mu}

(5.47)

is the whole algebra
{\cal B}(B_{n}^{\mu}{\cal H}^{\wedge n})
.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
T=T_{\rm{hop}}
be given by (
5.35
).
Let the initial state
\omega_{B}
of the reservoir be a quasi-free state satisfying (
4.11
) with
K\geq 1
and the initial state of the sample
be such that
\omega_{S}(|\Omega\rangle\langle F|)=\omega_{S}(|F\rangle\langle\Omega|)=0
.
Then the conclusion of Theorem
5.7
on the convergence of the system state at large time holds under assumptions
(
SND
), (
Diag
) and
(
Cyc
).

Proof.

Since (
MND
) is satisfied for
T=T_{\rm{hop}}
and
F\in\ker(T_{\rm{hop}})
, the part of the spectrum of
{\cal V}\Phi
on the unit circle is given by
\{1,\det V,\det V^{\ast}\}
by Proposition
5.5
.
Thus we only have to prove that under the assumptions of the Proposition, the invariant subspace
{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
of
{\cal V}\Phi
is reduced to observables of the form (
5.23
). From the argument after (
5.22
), one can without loss of generality assume that the observables invariant under
{\cal V}\Phi
are self-adjoint.

Let
X\in{\cal P}_{1}{\cal B}({\cal F}_{-})
be self-adjoint and
1\leq n<d
. Then
X
is given by (
5.22
). Moreover, since
\Phi(X)=X
, one has

B^{\mu}_{n}X_{n}B^{\nu}_{n}=\delta_{\mu,\nu}X_{n}B^{\mu}_{n}
.
We make the following observation. Suppose that one can show that

B^{\mu}_{n}X_{n}B^{\mu}_{n}=X_{n}B^{\mu}_{n}=x_{n}^{\mu}B^{\mu}_{n}\quad\text{ for some $\mu\in\{+,-,0\}$ and $x_{n}^{\mu}\in{\mathbb{R}}$.}

(5.48)

Then one can use assumption (
Diag
) to prove that

X_{n}=x_{n}\,{\mathds{1}}|_{{\cal H}^{\wedge n}}
.
Actually, (
5.22
) and (
5.48
) imply that
\operatorname{ran}B_{n}^{\mu}\subset\operatorname{span}\{\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{k}}\,;\,x_{\underline{k}}^{(n)}=x_{n}^{\mu}\}
.
If there exists some
x_{\underline{j}}^{(n)}\not=x_{n}^{\mu}
in the decomposition (
5.22
) then,
by orthogonality of the eigenspaces of
X_{n}
(recall that
X_{n}
is self-adjoint) and

\operatorname{ran}B^{\mu}_{n}\,\bot\,\ker B^{\mu}_{n}
, it follows that

\wedge^{n}\psi_{\underline{j}}\in\ker B_{n}^{\mu}
, in contradiction with (
Diag
). Thus
(
Diag
) and (
5.48
)

imply that
X_{n}
is proportional to the identity.
Note that for
n=1
the projectors
B^{\pm}|_{{\cal H}}
are of rank one, so that
(
5.48
) is always satisfied for
\mu=\pm
and

x_{1}^{\pm}=\operatorname{tr}(X_{1}B_{1}^{\pm})
.
The situation is more involved for
n\geq 2
.

Suppose that
n\geq 2
.
In order to prove (
5.48
)
we first work in the direct sum of the ranges of
B^{+}_{n}
and
B^{-}_{n}
and introduce
the projectors

\Pi_{n}=B^{+}_{n}+B^{-}_{n}\quad,\quad\Pi_{n}^{\bot}=B^{0}_{n}\;.

(5.49)

Thus
X_{n}=\Pi_{n}X_{n}\Pi_{n}+\Pi_{n}^{\bot}X_{n}\Pi_{n}^{\bot}
.
We now express the condition
{\cal V}(X)=X
, which means that
X
commutes with
\Gamma(V)
.
By projecting the commutator
[X,\Gamma(V)]
, one is led to

\big[\Pi_{n}X_{n}\Pi_{n}\,,\,\Pi_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)\Pi_{n}\big]=0\;.

(5.50)

Let us write the matrices of the two operators of this commutator in the
basis
\{\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\}_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{+}}\cup\{\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\}_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{-}}
of
\Pi_{n}{\cal H}^{\wedge n}
:

{\rm Mat}(\Pi_{n}X_{n}\Pi_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}X_{n}^{+}&0\\
0&X_{n}^{-}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad{\rm Mat}(\Pi_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)\Pi_{n})=\begin{pmatrix}A_{++}^{n}&A_{+-}^{n}\\
A_{-+}^{n}&A_{--}^{n}\end{pmatrix}

(5.51)

with
(A_{pq})_{{\underline{i}},{\underline{j}}}=\langle f_{(3-{\rm sign}(p))/2}\wedge f_{i_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{i_{n}}|\Gamma(V)\,f_{(3-{\rm sign}(q))/2}\wedge f_{j_{2}}\wedge\dots\wedge f_{j_{n}}\rangle
for

p,q\in\{+,-\}
.
The condition (
5.50
) can be rewritten as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\big[X_{n}^{+}\,,\,A_{++}^{n}\big]&=&0\\[4.30554pt]
\big[X_{n}^{-}\,,\,A_{--}^{n}\big]&=&0\\[4.30554pt]
A_{+-}^{n}X_{n}^{-}-X_{n}^{+}A_{+-}^{n}&=&0\\[4.30554pt]
A_{-+}^{n}X_{n}^{+}-X_{n}^{-}A_{-+}^{n}&=&0\;.\end{array}\right.

(5.52)

Left-multiplying the third (respectively fourth) equation by
A_{-+}^{n}
(resp.
A_{+-}^{n}
) and right-multiplying the fourth (resp. third)
equation by
A_{+-}^{n}
(resp.
A_{-+}^{n}
), this gives

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\big[X_{n}^{+}\,,\,A_{+-}^{n}A_{-+}^{n}\big]&=&0\\[4.30554pt]
\big[X_{n}^{-}\,,\,A_{-+}^{n}A_{+-}^{n}\big]&=&0\;.\end{array}\right.

(5.53)

The products
A_{+-}^{n}A_{-+}^{n}
and
A_{-+}^{n}A_{+-}^{n}
appearing in (

)
are the matrices of the operators

B^{+}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{-}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{+}_{n}\quad\text{ and }\quad B^{-}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{+}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{-}_{n}\,.

(5.54)

Similarly,
A_{\pm\pm}^{n}
is the matrix of the operator
B^{\pm}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{\pm}_{n}
. Thus, by (
5.52
) and (
5.53
),
X^{\pm}_{n}
commutes with both
{\rm Mat}(B^{\pm}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{\pm}_{n})
and
{\rm Mat}(B^{\pm}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{\mp}_{n}\Gamma_{n}(V)B^{\pm}_{n})

By repeating the argument above in the direct sum of the ranges of

and

and
considering the basis
\{\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\}_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{\pm}}\cup\{\wedge^{n}f_{\underline{j}}\}_{{\underline{j}}\in I_{n}^{0}}

of this subspace,

commute respectively with the matrices of the operators

From the above commutation relations,

commutes with the matrices of

and

for

Since it is self-adjoint, it also commutes with the adjoints of these operators. Hence

commutes
with the

Thus if (

) is satisfied

for some

By the observation above one concludes that

Thus, Assumption (

has the form given in (

The result follows by using the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem

6
Genericity of assumption (
Diag
)

Assumption (
Diag
) stating that some coefficients of the eigenvectors of
\Gamma(V)
in the
\{f_{j}\}
-basis are non zero (see Sec.
5.4
) reflects the fact that these eigenvectors are in arbitrary positions with respect to that basis, excluding accidental symmetries. We show here that Haar distributed random unitary matrices satisfy (
Diag
) almost surely, which ensures this assumption is generically satisfied.

Let
C=(c_{jk})\in U(d)
be a Haar distributed random unitary matrix on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. For
{\underline{j}},{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
,
1\leq n\leq d
, consider the truncation
C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})\in M_{n}(\mathbb{C})
of
C

C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})=\begin{pmatrix}c_{j_{1}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{1}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{1}}(k_{n})\\
c_{j_{2}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{2}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{2}}(k_{n})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{j_{n}}(k_{1})&c_{j_{n}}(k_{2})&\cdots&c_{j_{n}}(k_{n})\end{pmatrix}\;.

(6.1)

The matrix
C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})
is neither unitary, nor diagonalizable in general. However, the corresponding minor
c_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})=\det(C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}}))
is zero iff at least one of its eigenvalues vanishes,
and the distribution of the eigenvalues of the bloc
C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})
is known explicitly in case
C
is Haar distributed, see
[
ZS
,
PR
]
.

This allows us to get

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
C\in U(d)
be a random Haar distributed unitary matrix. Then, with probability one, all its minors are non-zero.

Proof.

For
1\leq n\leq d
, all
n\times n
truncations
C_{\underline{j}}({\underline{k}})
for different
{\underline{j}},{\underline{k}}\in I_{n}
can be obtained as the upper left corner truncation, characterized by
{\underline{j}}={\underline{k}}=(1,2,\cdots,n)
, of the matrix
U_{1}CU_{2}
, for well chosen permutation matrices
U_{1},U_{2}\in U(d)
. Since
C
and
U_{1}CU_{2}
have the same distribution under the Haar measure, we can focus on the top left corner truncation. It is proven in
[
ZS
,
PR
]
that the distribution of the eigenvalues
\{z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}\}
of that truncation has the explicit continuous joint probability density with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{D}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}

\frac{1}{{\cal N}_{n,d}}\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{i}-z_{j}|^{2}\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-|z_{i}|^{2})^{d-n-1},

(6.2)

with normalization constant
{{\cal N}_{n,d}}=\pi^{n}n!\prod_{j=1}^{n-1}\begin{pmatrix}d-n+j-1\\
j\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\frac{1}{d-n+j}
.
Hence the probability for at least one eigenvalue of the top left truncation to vanish is zero, since this is the probability of a zero measure set. Hence, the probability that the top left corner minor vanishes is equal to zero. Since there are finitely many truncations, this yields the result.
∎

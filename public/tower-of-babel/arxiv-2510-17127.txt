1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background on the double ergodic averages

By
measure preserving system
, we mean a tuple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, where
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue space and
T:X\to X
is an invertible measure preserving transformation. We say that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is
ergodic
if the sub-
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{I}(T)
generated by all
T
-invariant subsets is trivival.

Fix a measure preserving system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
. In the study of ergodic theory, a fundamental problem is to understand the pointwise limit behavior of following double ergodic averages :

(1.1)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{\lfloor P(n)\rfloor}f\cdot T^{\lfloor Q(n)\rfloor}g,

where
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}[n]
with
\deg P=\deg Q
and
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

In 1990, Bourgain established the following pointwise ergodic theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
4
, Main Theorem and Equation (2.3)]
)
Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Fix two distinct non-zero integers
a
and
b
. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have that

(1.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{an}x)g(T^{bn}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
. When
T
is ergodic, if
f
or
g
is orthogonal to the closed subspace spanned by all eigenfunctions with respect to
T
, then the limit function is zero.

Recently, Krause extended Bourgain’s theorem to general linear polynomials with real coefficients.

Theorem 1.2
.

(
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)
Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\mathbb{Q}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{Q}
. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}x)g(T^{\lfloor\beta n\rfloor}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.

Following Theorem
1.1
and
1.2
, the simplest case of (
1.1
) is the following double ergodic averages:

(1.3)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}T^{n^{2}}f\cdot T^{-n^{2}}g,

where
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.

Unfortunately, (
1.3
) seems to be out of reach at this stage due to the absence of distinct degrees. However, in this direction, Daskalakis established a pointwise ergodic theorem for the
\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor
-analogue of (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

(
a special case of
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
)
Let
c\in(1,23/22)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
. Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{\lfloor\alpha n^{c}\rfloor}x)g(T^{-\lfloor\alpha n^{c}\rfloor}x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{-n}x)

for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
.

In fact, the function
\alpha t^{c}
can be replaced by a general
c
-regularly varying function (see
[
5
, Theorem 1.7]
) in Daskalakis’s result. Furthermore, Daskalakis’ result shows that the double ergodic averages along
(\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor,-\lfloor n^{c}\rfloor)
share similarities with thoses in (
1.2
).

x\in X

1.3.
Brief overview of the proof of Theorem
1.4

We apply Daskalakis’s method (see
[
5
, Subsection 1.1]
for an overview) to prove that the limit in (
1.4
) exists almost everywhere. Since Daskalakis’s method only can deal with the following form:

(1.5)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{\lfloor\alpha\lfloor h(n)\rfloor\rfloor}x)g(T^{\lfloor\beta\lfloor h(n)\rfloor\rfloor}x),

where
h(x)
is a
c
-regularly varying function, we have to use the ergodic averages shaped like thoses in (
1.5
) to approximate ones in (
1.4
) under arbitrarily small errors. To do this, we adapt the method from
[
8
, Proof of Theorem A.1]
to construct a special measurable flow
(X\times[0,1),\mathcal{X}\otimes\mathcal{B}([0,1)),\mu\times\text{Leb}_{[0,1)},(S^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}})
and replace
f
and
g
by
\tilde{f}:=f\otimes 1_{[0,1)}
and
\tilde{g}:=g\otimes 1_{[0,1)}
. Based on these ingredients, we prove that the averages of form

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{f}((S^{\beta\epsilon})^{\lfloor(\alpha/\beta)\lfloor\epsilon^{-1}n^{c}\rfloor\rfloor}y)\tilde{g}((S^{\beta\epsilon})^{\lfloor\epsilon^{-1}n^{c}\rfloor}y)

are exactly what we need, where
\epsilon
is sufficiently small.

When
\alpha/\beta
is rational, this case can be solved by applying Daskalakis’s method except some slight adjustments. However, when
\alpha/\beta
is irrational, the absence of a suitable analogue of
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
prevents a direct application of Daskalakis’s method. To overcome the barrier, based on a Diophantus approximation result, we convert the question into the establishment of suitable upper bound of the integration of the following sum of the form

\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}w_{N}(n)l((S^{\beta\epsilon/Q_{N}})^{m}y)\tilde{f}((S^{\beta\epsilon/Q_{N}})^{m+nP_{N}}y)\tilde{g}((S^{\beta\epsilon/Q_{N}})^{m+nQ_{N}}y),

where
P_{N},Q_{N}
are two integers depending on
N
and
\alpha/\beta
,
w_{N}(n)
is a weight function depending on
N
and
\epsilon^{-1}n^{c}
, and
l
is a
1
-bounded measurable function depending on
w_{N}
. After this, the remainder of the proof for the irrational case follows Daskalakis’s approach.

Subsequently, by re-examining the approximation process, we show that the limit of form

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{an}x)g(T^{\lfloor bn+d\rfloor}x)

is enough to describe the limit function, where
a\in\mathbb{Z},b,d\in\mathbb{R}
. This will help us determine the assoiated characteristic factor of the ergodic averages in (
1.4
).

-a.e.
(x,{\bf z})\in Y
.

By the assumption on
{\bf b}
and
d
, there is
\lambda\neq 0
such that
{\bf d}=\lambda{\bf b}
. Fix
10^{9}<l\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\max\{|b_{i}/2^{l}|:1\leq i\leq m\}+\max\{|d_{i}/2^{l}|:1\leq i\leq m\}<1/l
.

Then we have the following:

\displaystyle\int_{X}\int_{[0,1)^{m}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{f}(S^{n^{c}{\bf b}}(x,{\bf z}))\tilde{g}(S^{\lambda n^{c}{\bf b}}(x,{\bf z}))

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{f}((S^{{\bf b}/2^{l}})^{\lfloor 2^{l}n^{c}\rfloor}(x,{\bf z}))\tilde{g}((S^{{\bf b}/2^{l}})^{\lfloor 2^{l}\lambda n^{c}\rfloor}(x,{\bf z}))\Big|d\mu(x)d{\bf z}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}+\int_{X}\int_{(2/l,1-2/l)^{m}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{f}(S^{n^{c}{\bf b}}(x,{\bf z}))\tilde{g}(S^{\lambda n^{c}{\bf b}}(x,{\bf z}))

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{f}((S^{{\bf b}/2^{l}})^{\lfloor 2^{l}n^{c}\rfloor}(x,{\bf z}))\tilde{g}((S^{{\bf b}/2^{l}})^{\lfloor 2^{l}\lambda n^{c}\rfloor}(x,{\bf z}))\Big|d\mu(x)d{\bf z}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}+\int_{(2/l,1-2/l)^{m}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i\in\{1\leq j\leq m:b_{j}\neq 0\}}\frac{|\{1\leq n\leq N:\{b_{i}n^{c}\}\in I_{l}(z_{i})\}|}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt+\limsup_{N\to\infty}\left(\sum_{i\in\{1\leq j\leq m:d_{j}\neq 0\}}\frac{|\{1\leq n\leq N:\{d_{i}n^{c}\}\in I_{l}(z_{i})\}|}{N}\right)d{\bf z}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l}.\ (\text{by Lemma \ref{lem2-3}}).

Appendix A
A double recurrence result

In this section, our task is to prove the following pointwise ergodic theorem.

Theorem A.1
.

Fix an irrational number
\alpha
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
be a measure preserving system. Then for any
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\rfloor}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
. If
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|\mathcal{Z}_{2}(T))=0
or
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(g|\mathcal{Z}_{2}(T))=0
, then the limit function is zero.

Proof.

Fix
1
-bounded
f,g\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
.
Since
\alpha
is irrational, by Lemma
2.3
, we have that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>10^{9}
, there are
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
and
l_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

0<\beta-m_{k}\alpha-l_{k}<1/k.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
k>10^{9}
.
Then for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\rfloor}x)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(T^{-m_{k}}f)(T^{n}x)(T^{l_{k}}g)(T^{\lfloor\alpha(n-m_{k})+\beta-l_{k}\rfloor}x)\right|=0.

Meanwhile, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{X}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(T^{-m_{k}}f)(T^{n}x)(T^{l_{k}}g)(T^{\lfloor\alpha(n-m_{k})+\beta-l_{k}\rfloor}x)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(T^{-m_{k}}f)(T^{n}x)(T^{l_{k}}g)(T^{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}x)\Big|d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{|\{1\leq n\leq N:\{\alpha n\}\in(1-1/k,1)\}|}{N}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}.\ (\text{by Lemma \ref{weyl}})

Combining these and Theorem
1.2
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\rfloor}x)

exists for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in X
. And we have the following estimate:

\displaystyle\int_{X}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{\lfloor\alpha n+\beta\rfloor}x)-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(T^{-m_{k}}f)(T^{n}x)(T^{l_{k}}g)(T^{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}x)\Big|d\mu(x)\lesssim\frac{1}{k}.

This implies that to complete the proof, it remains to show the following statement:

If
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f|\mathcal{Z}_{2}(T))=0
or
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(g|\mathcal{Z}_{2}(T))=0
, then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}f(T^{n}x)g(T^{\lfloor\alpha n\rfloor}x)=0

in
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Comining
[
2
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
7
, Theorem 9.7]
, we have that the above statement holds. This finishes the proof.
∎

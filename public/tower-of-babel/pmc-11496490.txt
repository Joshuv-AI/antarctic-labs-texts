Applying similar modeling to study vehicle exhaust transport in a confined mining space
Authors: Sheng Xue, Gaofeng Shi, Qingyi Tu, Feng Li, Chao Li
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
To scientifically and effectively manage exhaust pollution in underground confined spaces and reduce hazards from personnel exhaust exposure, a systematic study of the distribution characteristics of diesel locomotive exhaust emissions in mines is needed. Aiming at large underground coal mining operations, an experimental platform for the physical simulation of vehicle exhaust transport in restricted spaces is designed and created. The Hongliulin Coal Mine in Shaanxi Province, China, is used as a prototype, and the platform consists of a coal mining face system, ventilation system, exhaust emission system, monitoring and surveillance system, and control platform. The experimental platform with carbon dioxide simulation of diesel exhaust pollution, in the role of different wind speed, the concentration of carbon dioxide is a downward trend, the wind speed is in the low wind speed on the 0.2 m of the concentration difference of 25.75%; the experimental platform exhaust transport process presents a clear “three-zone” change rule; experimental platform carbon dioxide concentration and the field nitrogen dioxide concentration change trend is consistent. The platform functions and features can provide guidance for mine exhaust management and exhaust exposure hazard prevention and control.

Introduction

Diesel locomotives are important transportation tools in Chinese coal production1, especially in large-scale modern mines in Shaanxi and Inner Mongolia, which are dominated by the inclined shaft development method. As convenient and efficient transportation tools2, diesel locomotives are widely used in these coal mines for the transportation of underground materials, equipment, and personnel. For example, in the Hongliulin coal mine in Shaanxi Province, China, there are 290 vehicles in daily use underground, of which 257 are diesel locomotives, accounting for 88.62%.

Diesel locomotive exhaust mainly includes volatile organic compounds (VOCs), carbon monoxide (CO), nitrogen oxides (NOX), hydrocarbons (HC), carbon smoke particles (PM), water vapor, carbon dioxide (CO2) and other components3. These exhaust components can aggregate in the restricted space of the wind flow field of a mine in the form of aerosols, resulting in underground air pollution4–6. Laboratory engine experiments7,8 on gathered aerosols found that NOXemissions can reach 1.20–1.53 g/(kWh), and the production of nitrogen oxides can irritate the lungs, leading to lung disease and even poisoning, which can affect human safety7–9.

Due to the large number of coal mines using diesel locomotives that emit exhaust pollution, resulting in deteriorating working conditions, diesel particulate matter (DPM), VOCs, CO, NOXand other diesel locomotive exhaust pollutants have become an urgent problem to address2,10,11. At present, managing exhaust by adjusting the air volume has become a common method for maintaining exhaust concentrations within appropriate concentration ranges, but adjusting the air volume can exceed prescribed limits, resulting in violations. Diesel locomotives could be replaced by electric vehicles, but electric vehicle power supply and range are key factors affecting their use12–15. Numerical simulations have revealed that gaseous pollutants in exhaust CO and NOXcan be distributed throughout roadways under the influence of wind flow16,17. The current diesel locomotive exhaust pollution problem requires an in-depth study of the transportation law of diesel locomotive exhaust in coal mine roadways.

At present, research on the exhaust of diesel locomotives in coal mine roadways has been conducted mainly through numerical simulations using field test data and the establishment of simple experimental platforms and other methods. Numerical simulations can intuitively display how exhaust diffuses and distributes in roadways, and vehicle models can be used to simplify the simulated movement of a vehicle to move at a constant speed, study the distribution characteristics of #0 diesel fuel in a single roadway after combustion in a diesel engine18, and establish a two-dimensional planar model to study static monitoring points for collecting vehicle exhaust generating CO in irregular areas19. Although research methods can simulate the distribution characteristics and transportation law of exhaust pollution, a simulated situation can never replace the complex pollution phenomena in real situations. In existing field tests, a test site is selected for a coal mine roadway to collect exhaust emissions. A portable vehicle test system can be used to explore pollutant emissions under different working conditions, and the data, obtained with boundary conditions, can be applied to numerical simulations. Although these two methods involve the study of underground exhaust pollution, field test methods require considerable time and financial resources, which is extremely inconvenient20–22. In addition to the construction of a mine exhaust transportation simulation platform and other research, there are currently several simulated roadways18,23with a certain number of sensors that lay pipelines in the roadway, directly burn #0 diesel as the source of exhaust emissions, or drive simulated diesel car models in roadways being used to study the characteristics of exhaust pollution emissions. Based on a heavy-duty diesel engine bench experiment24, only the differences in emission characteristics of different types of vehicles under hot and cold cycling conditions and the effect of low temperatures on exhaust emissions were investigated25.

Research has focused on mine diesel locomotive exhaust pollution experiments, other related designs, and mine diesel locomotive exhaust emission pollution distribution laws, but a panoramic simulation of coal mine underground roadway experiments using an experimental platform for physical simulation is still lacking. A numerical simulation method is not a substitute for a real situation, on-site data collection is inconvenient, and the limitations of the existing platform are to achieve exhaust pollution distribution characteristics and simulate real exhaust transport distribution in the laboratory while designing and constructing an experimental platform for the physical simulation of vehicle exhaust transport in a restricted mining space.

Physics simulation experiment platform principles and functions

The experimental platform for physical simulation relies on the production system of the 25,210 comprehensive mining face in the Hongliulin Coal Mine in northern Shaanxi Province, China, and selects part of the length of the production system to build a scaled-down spatial distribution of the vehicle exhaust transport system, which is based on a real coal mine environment scaled down to a ratio of 1:5. The model incorporates relevant equipment in the production system of the mine, which is composed of the main coal-mining working face system, ventilation system, exhaust emission system, monitoring and control system, and the control platform. The mutual coordination of the components of each system can adjust the wind speed and exhaust discharge and realize a simulation of the distribution law of exhaust transport at different locations in the mine. The following are the main functions and features of the experimental platform:

1) Experimental platform functions.

Multicomponent exhaust composition simulation: The experimental platform can simulate diesel vehicle exhaust components, as well as simulate exhausts produced by different types of fuels (e.g., diesel of different qualities).

Variable environment exhaust pollution simulation: The experimental platform can simulate the realization of the distribution characteristics of exhaust diffusion and transport under different ventilation conditions in the roadway.

2) Experimental platform features.

Accurate simulation and regulation: The platform is designed to allow precise control of a variety of experimental parameters, including exhaust composition, emission rate, and roadway wind speed, to accurately simulate the actual conditions in the mine.

Real-time monitoring and collection: A variety of high-precision monitoring devices, such as exhaust sensors and wind speed sensors, is used for real-time monitoring and recording of the concentration and distribution of exhaust and other related parameters.

Composition of the experimental platform for physical simulation

Coal mining face and main roadway

The auxiliary transport roadway, belt transport roadway and return air roadway are scaled down according to the actual parameters of the 1:5 mine, and Plexiglas is installed in the slot of the aluminum alloy frame of the roadway. The tempered glass allows easy observation of the operation of the flow characteristics or distribution features. The auxiliary transportation roadway and return air roadway are equipped with casement windows, which is convenient for the maintenance and adjustment of the facilities and equipment or the arrangement of the sampling points.

The experimental platform for physical simulation is designed with the geometry of the coal mining face as 14 × 1.84 × 1.16 m, with a section area of 2.134 m2; the geometry of the auxiliary transportation roadway, belt transportation roadway and return air roadway is the same, at 10 × 1.2 × 0.84 m, with a section area of 1.008 m2; and the geometry of the contact roadway is 1.2 × 1.2 × 0.84 m, with a section area of 2.134 m2.

Ventilation system

The ventilation system mainly consists of an FDCZ-7.1 type extraction explosion-proof counter-rotating axial flow fan, a constant air volume valve, and a frequency control system. Different air volumes can be allocated according to the characteristics of different production locations; the wind speed of each area point can be adjusted accurately, and the wind speed of each location in the mine meets the requirements listed in Table 1.

The FDCZ-7.1 explosion-proof counter-rotating axial flow fan is used, which is installed in the fan room at the end of the return air roadway and connected to the roadway by a rigid wind pipe. Air volume regulation directly affects exhaust emissions and distribution29. In addition, the fan is also equipped with a silencer, soundproof cover, and additional sound-absorbing materials to reduce fan noise and alleviate environmental pollution and damage to personnel.

Diesel locomotive model and exhaust emission device

The diesel locomotive model and exhaust emission device are based on the WC19R(A) explosion-proof diesel trackless rubber-wheeled vehicle as a prototype, and the diesel locomotive model is reduced to 1:5.

Carbon dioxide is the signature gas for the experimental platform for physical simulation, and the nitrogen dioxide gas selected in the field is similar in nature; the relative molecular mass of carbon dioxide is 44, and the relative molecular mass of nitrogen dioxide is 46. The experimental process should include a nontoxic, harmless, and nonhazardous gas as the signature gas.

The diesel locomotive model is equipped with an exhaust gas storage tank, servo control shut-off valve, control module and other components, as shown in Fig. 1. The exhaust gas storage tank has a volume of 4 L and a working pressure of 6 MPa; the tank is made of stainless steel and fixed inside the model vehicle to store the simulated exhaust gas. The PID flow regulation system precisely regulates the exhaust gas emission, and the servo motor regulating valve controls the valve opening to realize the emission of the exhaust gas from the storage tank, to simulate the emission behavior of a real diesel locomotive.The PID flow regulation of the exhaust storage tank and the vehicle model is shown in Fig. 1.

Monitoring and control systems

The monitoring and control system consists of various types of sensors and control platforms. Real-time monitoring of real-time data, such as the wind speed of the model platform roadway and exhaust emissions from the diesel locomotive model, was performed.

The equipment operating system is responsible for receiving data from sensors. The equipment control cabinet is used to regulate the fan frequency, gate damper opening, exhaust valve opening, and screw speed. The data acquisition and analysis and processing system is mainly based on various sensors in the roadway. The monitoring and control system monitors key parameters such as the analog roadway wind speed and exhaust emissions in real time and provides an intuitive data display in the interactive user interface.

CO2 was used as the signature gas for the study, and the sensor was a carbon dioxide sensor that allowed real-time collection and recording of CO2 concentrations. The sensors were mounted at the front and rear of the diesel locomotive model, within 1.5 m of the exhaust pipe opening. At the same time, through the control system on the sensor mounted on the screw, at a predetermined distance in fixed-speed movement to collect the value of the exhaust gas concentration at different locations. The technical specifications of the carbon dioxide sensor are shown in Table 2.

To accurately obtain the roadway wind speed, an XP-FB/FLZZY differential pressure wind speed sensor is used, with an equipment accuracy of 3% and a wind speed measurement range of 0–10 m/s. A Pitot tube is used to sense the pressure difference generated by the air flow, and the pressure sensor cooperates with the Pitot tube, which converts the acquired differential pressure data into electrical signals, and the processing and calculation results in the wind speed.

Experimental study of platform reliability

The experimental platform for physical simulation provides a research basis for investigating the transportation and distribution of diesel locomotives in mine shafts after exhaust emission. The experimental results can also provide boundary conditions for numerical simulations to study the transportation and distribution of diesel locomotive exhaust in the restricted space of a mine. The reliability of the experimental platform for physical simulation is verified by comparisons with field results.

Experimental programmes

Research was performed on the roles of coal mine production, roadway space and wind flow field on the transport process of diesel locomotive exhaust, and the diffusion and concentration distributions of exhaust affected by different wind flow fields were analyzed to verify the reliability of the experimental platform for physical simulation. This study provides a scientific theoretical basis and technical support for the prevention and control of coal mine exhaust, realizes the optimization of mine exhaust management, reduces the impacts of exhaust on personnel health and environmental safety, and ensures that the pollutants of diesel engine experiments in future experiments are lower than the limit values in Table 330.

Most of the exhaust produced by diesel locomotives is caused by inadequate diesel combustion, and the main components are CO, NOX, CH, and PM31,32. In the experimental platform for physical simulation operation study, CO2 is used as the signature gas to ensure the safety of the experiment since it may involve the aforementioned toxic and harmful gases.

1) To test the gas tightness of the storage tank of the model, the tank is filled with CO2 gas, and the initial position of the carbon dioxide sensor is calibrated to the mouth of the exhaust pipe; the measurement point is arranged with the carbon dioxide sensor fixed on the screw, and the change in carbon dioxide concentration in the range of 1.5 m behind the model is tested at a low speed and at uniform speed.

2) The control cabinet panel is used to control the fan speed and the opening degree of the damper gate to regulate the air velocity of the roadway; the opening degree of the exhaust flow regulating valve is controlled to keep the CO2 outlet flow stable; the CO2 sensor screw is rotated at a speed of 1 mm/s; and the data logging function of the control center is enabled, with 1 s intervals between CO2 collection concentrations.

3) When the exhaust emission rate is stabilized, the screw drives the carbon dioxide sensor at a constant speed, observes the change in wind speed and gas concentration in the control center, and records the carbon dioxide concentration behind the diesel locomotive model in the auxiliary transportation roadway.

Multiple wind speeds and CO2 concentration changes for the experimental platform for physical simulation

To clarify the change rule of carbon dioxide gas concentration under different wind speed conditions in the experimental platform for physical simulation, it is stipulated that the exhaust port of the model is the starting point, and 16 measurement points are set up at intervals of 0.1 m. The carbon dioxide sensor of the experimental platform for physical simulation is used to monitor the change in concentration and the change rule of the concentration under the action of multiple wind speeds.

Figure 2 shows that the change in carbon dioxide concentration is affected by wind flow, and the process of increasing the wind speed from 0 m/s to 1.0 m/s shows that the carbon dioxide concentration will decrease rapidly, and the results at the different wind speeds show that the trend of carbon dioxide concentration change is basically the same. This study is based on the change in carbon dioxide concentration under the effect of a 0.3 m/s wind speed.

At a wind speed of 0 m/s, the carbon dioxide concentration was not affected by the wind flow but only by the initial momentum of the exhaust, and the concentration change was close to free diffusion, with the carbon dioxide concentration decreasing from 1,162 ppm to 659 ppm at only 0.2 m, a decrease of 43.29%. After the concentration stabilized, the concentration at 1.5 m decreased by 58.18% compared to the starting point. When the wind speed was 0.3 m/s, the concentration of carbon dioxide decreased significantly after 0.1 m of transport, with a decrease of 46.36%; the difference between the concentration and the concentration at 0.1 m without wind speed reached 25.75%, and the concentration stabilized under the superposition of the wind flow field, with a decrease of 65.1% at 1.5 m. The concentration of CO2 at 1.5 m decreased significantly after 0.2 m of transport, and the difference between the concentration and the concentration at 1.5 m of transport reached 25.75%.

At wind speeds ranging from 0.5 m/s to 1.0 m/s, the carbon dioxide concentration decreases are similar, and the 1.0 m/s wind flow at 0.1 m experiences the greatest decrease, with percentages of 55.28%, 0.5 m/s and 0.7 m/s when the concentration changes are basically the same. By adjusting the experimental platform for physical simulation under 5 different wind speed conditions, the study of the change rule of carbon dioxide concentration revealed that carbon dioxide will be positioned at the mouth of the exhaust pipe, and the the wind speed at which the carbon dioxide concentration decreases is also faster, which indicates that the effect of wind flow on carbon dioxide dilution is notable.

Physical simulation and regular changes in concentration in the field

To ensure the reliability and validity of the experimental results, a typical section in the roadway of the 15,217 heading face of the Hongliulin coal mine in the northern Shaanxi mining region of China was selected as the field data sampling site. The accuracy of the data obtained from the experimental platform for physical simulation is verified to ensure that the experimental results can effectively reflect the actual exhaust pollution.

On-site exhaust data were collected using a PGD4-C-M3 portable three-in-one gas detector. Before the exhaust content in the air was formally sampled, the vehicle drove onto the roadway and then stopped and waited for ten minutes so that the concentration of the exhaust in the roadway decreased to the normal concentration to obtain accurate exhaust parameters. At the same time, to enhance the accuracy of the data, the environmental base parameters of the site were sampled five times to obtain the initial average NO2 concentration of 0.002 ppm (0 ppm, 0.01 ppm, 0 ppm, 0 ppm, 0 ppm), and a CFJD5-type anemometer was used to measure the average wind speed of the roadway at 0.7 m/s. The environmental parameters are shown in Table 6.

The diesel locomotive was parked on one side of the roadway centerline near the roadway wall, and the height of the exhaust pipe center from the roadway floor was the sampling height of the exhaust concentration, i.e., 0.45 m. A total of 16 measurement points were arranged at the rear of the diesel locomotive, with an interval of 0.1 m in the range of 0 ~ 1.0 m and an interval of 1.0 m in the range of 1.0 ~ 1.5 m, which allows for a detailed analysis of the changes in the concentration of the exhaust in the roadway over different transport distances.

The wind flow in the roadway is the main factor leading to the rapid dilution and diffusion of NO2, which promotes the rapid diffusion of high concentrations of NO2 into the roadway. In addition, whether NO2 or other components are present in the exhaust, the factors affecting the change in NO2concentration may also be chemical reactions33–35, and the Brownian motion of the gas molecules is also one of the factors affecting the change in NO2, which contributes to the irregular diffusion of the gas in the roadway space. The combined effects of many factors and the instability of NO2 chemical properties cause concentration changes after emission into the roadway space.

Conclusions

The paper provides an experimental method for the study of vehicle exhaust pollution law in coal mine confined space by building a physical simulation experimental platform, and the specific conclusions are as follows:

1)The physical simulation experiment platform is designed with coal mining face system, ventilation system, tail gas emission system, monitoring and control system, and control platform. The reliability of the experimental platform is confirmed by comparing the experiments of the experimental platform with the field data.

2)By using carbon dioxide as the signature exhaust gas for the experimental platform for physical simulation, it was found that the carbon dioxide concentration showed a decreasing trend under the action of different wind speeds. The concentration difference between 0.2 m at wind speeds of 0.0 ~ 0.3 m/s reaches 25.75%. The effect of the wind flow promotes the dilution and diffusion of carbon dioxide in the space, and the concentration tends to stabilize after a period of distance transportation.

3)In China’s Hongliulin coal mine, after analyzing the data collected on the concentration of nitrogen dioxide in the exhaust, it was found that there are “three zones” in the change rule of the exhaust concentration. The factors that cause a change in the NO2 concentration may be the dilution of the concentration due to multiple superpositions or the generation of new substances by chemical reactions. A comparison between the field data and the data from the experimental platform for physical simulation also clearly shows that the trends of carbon dioxide and nitrogen dioxide concentration changes within a certain distance in the roadway are the same, and there are three change areas.

Master equations for continuous-time random walks with stochastic resetting
Fausto Colantoni, Gianni Pagnini

Abstract
We study a general continuous-time random walk (CTRW), by including non-Markovian cases and Lévy flights, under complete stochastic resetting to the initial position with an arbitrary law, which can be power-lawed as well as Poissonian. We provide three linked results. First, we show that the random walk under stochastic resetting is a CTRW with the same jump-size distribution of the non-reset original CTRW but different counting process. Later, we derive the condition for a CTRW with stochastic resetting to be a meaningful displacement process at large elapsed times, i.e., the probability to jump to any site is higher than the probability to be reset to the initial position, and we call this condition the zero-law for stochastic resetting . This law joins with the other two laws for reset random walks concerning the existence and the non-existence of a non-equilibrium stationary state. Finally, we derive master equations for CTRWs when the resetting law is a completely monotone function.

In the following, we study the effect of stochastic resetting
to the initial position with a general law.
We provide
three
linked results.

First

stochastic resetting can be represented as a CTRW without resetting.
Thus, any random walk under stochastic resetting is indeed
a special non-reset random walk.

Later

under stochastic resetting to be

that is a process whose trajectories diffuse in time,

Any CTRW under resetting is another CTRW—
By following literature
(

, Section 2.8)

we use a renewal approach to determine the p.d.f.

Let

If we have one reset

, we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0}\left(Y_{t}\in dx|\text{ one reset in }[0,t]\right)=\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(S_{1})\Phi(t-S_{1})\rho(x;t-S_{1})\,dS_{1}dx\,,

(6)

where
\varphi
governs the probability that one reset event happens
in

,
after which the process restarts from zero with the law

,
and by

we satisfy the request

(S_{1},t]
.
Similarly for two resets in

, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{0}\left(Y_{t}\in dx|\text{ two resets in }[0,t]\right)=\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t-S_{1}}\varphi(S_{1})\varphi(S_{2})\Phi(t-S_{1}-S_{2})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\rho(x;t-S_{1}-S_{2})\,dS_{2}dS_{1}dx\,.

(7)

Since we are handling convolution integrals,

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}(x;s)\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}\mathbf{P}_{0}(Y_{t}\in dx)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}\mathbf{P}_{0}\left(Y_{t}\in dx|\,n\text{ resets in }[0,t]\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[\widetilde{\varphi}(s)]^{n}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}\Phi(t)\rho(x;t)\,dtdx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\widetilde{\varphi}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}\Phi(t)\rho(x;t)\,dtdx\,,

\displaystyle\widetilde{p}(x;s)=\frac{1}{s\widetilde{\Phi}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}\Phi(t)\rho(x;t)\,dt\,.

(8)

After the invertion of the Laplace transform, we have

\displaystyle p(x;t)=\Phi(t)\rho(x;t)+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(\tau)p(x;t-\tau)\,d\tau\,.

(9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s\widetilde{\Phi}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{\Psi}(s+y)[\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)]^{n}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s\widetilde{\Phi}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1-\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)}{s+y}[\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)]^{n}f(y)\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s\widetilde{\Phi}(s)}\int_{0}^{\infty}([\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)]^{n}-[\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)]^{n+1})\frac{f(y)}{s+y}\,dy\,.

Then the Fourier–Laplace transform of the p.d.f.
p(x;t)
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\widetilde{p}}(k;s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(s)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[\lambda(k)]^{n}\widetilde{w}_{n}(s)

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}(s)\frac{\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}[\widehat{\lambda}(k)]^{n}[\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)]^{n}\frac{f(y)}{s+y}\,dy}{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(y)}{s+y}\,dy}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}(s)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{\rho}}_{*}(\kappa;s+y)}{s+y}f(y)\,dy\,,

where

\widetilde{K}=\frac{\widetilde{W}}{\widetilde{\Phi}}=\frac{1}{s\widetilde{\Phi}^{2}}\left[\widetilde{\Phi}-\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\widetilde{\psi}(s+y)}{s+y}f(y)\,dy\right]\,,

(24)

and, by assuming the non-Markovian formulation (
3
),

\widehat{\widetilde{\rho}}_{*}(\kappa;s)=\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{\rho}}(\kappa;s)}{\widetilde{\Psi}(s)}=(s^{\beta}+1)\,\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{\rho}}(\kappa;s)}{s^{\beta-1}}\,,

(25)

that, in the original domain, reads

\displaystyle\rho_{*}(x;t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\mathcal{D}^{1-\beta}\rho+\delta(x)\left[\frac{t^{-(1-\beta)}}{\Gamma[1-(1-\beta)]}+\delta(t)\right]

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\mathbb{D}^{1-\beta}\rho+\delta(x)\delta(t)\,.

Moreover, from (
23
) we have
(see, properties of Laplace transform reminded in the End Matter section)

\displaystyle p(x;t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\left\{{\rm e}^{-y\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau}\rho_{*}(x;\zeta)\,d\zeta\right\}f(y)\,dyd\tau

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-y\tau}f(y)\,dy\right\}\int_{0}^{\tau}\rho_{*}(x;\zeta)\,d\zeta d\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\widetilde{f}(\tau)\int_{0}^{\tau}\rho_{*}(x;\zeta)\,d\zeta d\tau\,,

that, by using the first line of (
26
), gives

p(x;t)=\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\widetilde{f}(\tau)\left[\rho(x;\tau)+J^{\beta}\rho\right]\,d\tau\,.

(28)

Therefore, by using the convolution representation of
p(x;t)
(
28
),
we have
(see, the Caputo fractional derivative of a Laplace convolution integral
in the End Matter section)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\beta}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\mathcal{D}^{\beta}[\widetilde{f}(\rho+J^{\beta}\rho)]\,d\tau

(29)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta(x)\left[\widetilde{f}(0)J^{1-\beta}K-\frac{t^{(1-\beta)-1}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\right]\,.

We can also derive the following

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\widetilde{f}(\tau)\left[\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}\rho+J^{\beta}\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}\rho\right]\,d\tau

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\widetilde{f}(\tau)\left[\rho-\delta(x)+\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}\rho\right]\,d\tau\,,

where we used the equalities

J^{\beta}\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}\rho=J^{\beta}\mathcal{D}^{\beta}\rho=J^{\beta}J^{1-\beta}D^{1}\rho=J^{1}D^{1}\rho=\rho-\delta(x)\,.

Finally, master equations for CTRWs under stochastic resetting
to the initial position are

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{\beta}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}p

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\mathcal{D}^{\beta}\left[\widetilde{f}(\tau)\left(\rho(x;\tau)+J^{\beta}\rho\right)\right]\,d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}K(t-\tau)\widetilde{f}(\tau)\left[\rho(x;\tau)-\delta(x)+\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}\rho\right]\,d\tau

\displaystyle+\delta(x)\left[J^{1-\beta}K\widetilde{f}(0)-\frac{t^{(1-\beta)-1}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\right]\,,\quad p(x;0)=\delta(x)\,,

provided that
\widetilde{\varphi}
and
\widetilde{\psi}
met
the
zero-law of stochastic resetting
(
17
).
We can derive the master equation in the Markovian case, i.e.,
\beta=1
,
with Poissonian resetting, i.e.,
f(y)=\delta(y-1)
,
such that
\widetilde{K}(s)=(s+1)^{2}/[s(s+2)]
and
it turns out to be

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\alpha}p-p(x;t)+\delta(x)\,.

By setting
\alpha=2
we have the well-known equation
for Brownian motion under Poissonian resetting

Evans and Majumdar 2011
;
Evans et al. 2020
;
Méndez et al. 2021
.

End matter

We report here the mathematical notions and notations
used in the main text.

Let
\mathcal{L}\{g(t),s\}=\widetilde{g}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-st}g(t)\,dt
,
with
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
be the Laplace transform
and
\mathcal{F}\{u(x),\kappa\}=\widehat{u}(\kappa)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{\rm e}^{+i\kappa x}u(x)\,dx
, with
\kappa\in\mathbb{R}
,
be, in analogy with the definition of characteristic function,
the Fourier transform of sufficiently well behaving functions

g(t)
and
u(x)
.

Let
J^{\sigma}
, with
\sigma>0
, be the Riemann–Liouville fractional
integral of order
\sigma
defined by
Gorenflo and Mainardi 1997

J^{\sigma}g(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{g(\tau)}{(t-\tau)^{1-\sigma}}\,d\tau\,,\quad t>0\,,

such that
J^{0}=\mathbb{I}
and

\mathcal{L}\{J^{\sigma}g\}=\widetilde{g}/s^{\sigma}
.
By using the Laplace transform, it can be shown that the
semigroup property holds, i.e.,

J^{\sigma}J^{\eta}=J^{\sigma+\eta}
.
A noteworthy result used in our study is

J^{\sigma}1=t^{\sigma}/\Gamma(1+\sigma)
.

Moreover, let
\mathcal{D}^{\beta}
and
\mathbb{D}^{\beta}
be the
time-fractional derivative of order
\beta

in the Caputo and the Riemann–Liouville sense,
respectively, defined by
Gorenflo and Mainardi 1997

\mathcal{D}^{\beta}g=J^{m-\beta}D^{m}g\,,\quad\mathbb{D}^{\beta}g=D^{m}J^{m-\beta}g\,,

with
m-1<\beta<m
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathcal{D}^{m}=\mathbb{D}^{m}=D^{m}

and
\mathcal{D}^{0}=\mathbb{D}^{0}=D^{0}=\mathbb{I}
.
The corresponding Laplace transforms are

\mathcal{L}\{\mathcal{D}^{\beta}g,s\}=s^{\beta}\widetilde{g}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}g^{(\ell)}(0^{+})s^{\beta-1-\ell}\,,

\mathcal{L}\{\mathbb{D}^{\beta}g,s\}=s^{\beta}\widetilde{g}-\left.\sum_{\ell=0}^{m-1}D^{\ell}J^{m-\beta}g\right|_{0^{+}}s^{m-1-\ell}\,.

By using the above formula for
\mathcal{L}\{\mathcal{D}^{\beta}g,s\}
, the Caputo fractional derivative
of a Laplace convolution integral can be determined as follows.
Consider the Laplace convolution integral

q(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(t-\tau)h(\tau)\,d\tau
,
then by using the rule
\widetilde{q}=\widetilde{g}\,\widetilde{h}

and the Laplace transform of the Caputo fractional derivative,
i.e.,
\mathcal{L}\{\mathcal{D}^{\beta}g,s\}
, we have

\displaystyle s^{\beta}\widetilde{q}-s^{\beta-1}q_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{\beta}\widetilde{g}\widetilde{h}-s^{\beta-1}q_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}[s^{\beta}\widetilde{h}-s^{\beta-1}h_{0}]+s^{\beta-1}\widetilde{g}h_{0}-s^{\beta-1}q_{0}\,,

where
q_{0}=q(0^{+})
,
g_{0}=g(0^{+})
,
h_{0}=h(0^{+})
,
and, after the inversion, gives

\mathcal{D}^{\beta}q=\int_{0}^{t}g(t-\tau)\mathcal{D}^{\beta}h\,d\tau+h_{0}J^{1-\beta}g-q_{0}\frac{t^{(1-\beta)-1}}{\Gamma(1-\beta)}\,.

Still about the Laplace transform,
we remind here the properties

\mathcal{L}\{{\rm e}^{-yt}g(t),s\}=\widetilde{g}(s+y)
and

\mathcal{L}\{{\rm e}^{-yt}\int_{0}^{t}g(\zeta)\,d\zeta,s\}=\widetilde{g}(s+y)/(s+y)
.

\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\mu}
be the Riesz–Feller space-fractional derivative
of order
\mu>0
such that

\mathcal{F}\{\mathcal{D}_{x}^{\mu}u,\kappa\}=-|\kappa|^{\mu}\widehat{u}(\kappa)

and it holds
\mathcal{D}_{x}^{1}\neq D^{1}_{x}
and
\mathcal{D}_{x}^{2}=D^{2}_{x}

Mainardi et al. 2001
.

Single-cell transcriptomics and tissue metabolomics uncover mechanisms underlying wooden breast disease in broilers
Authors: Di Zhao, Zehe Song, Li Shen, Tian Xia, Qingyuan Ouyang, Haihan Zhang, Xi He, Kelang Kang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
Accompanied by the accelerated growth rate of chickens, the quality of chicken meat has deteriorated in recent years. Wooden breast (WB) is a severe myopathy affecting meat quality, and its pathophysiology depends on gene expression and intercellular interactions of various cell types, which are not yet fully understood. We have performed a comprehensive transcriptomic and metabolomic atlas of chicken WB muscle. Our data showed a significant increase in the number of immune cells, WB muscle displayed a unique cluster of macrophages (cluster 11), distinct from the M1 and M2 macrophages. Regarding the myocytes, the most significant differences were the decrease in cell number and the intensification of fatty deposits. Satellite cells were involved in muscle repair and regeneration producing more collagen. Interestingly, the interaction network in the WB group was weaker compared to that in normal breast muscle. Additionally, we found six key differential metabolites across 22 pathways. When WB occurs, myocytes and endothelial cells undergo apoptosis, macrophages are activated and exert immune functions, satellite cells participate in muscle rebuilding and repair, and the content of metabolites undergoes significant changes. This cell transcriptome profile provides an essential reference for future studies on the development and remodeling of WB.

Introduction

Chicken, particularly the breast muscle, is widely regarded as an important source of high-quality protein due to its affordability and nutritional value. In addition, rapid growth has caused many abnormal meat quality problems in the modern broiler industry (Baldi et al., 2019; Petracci et al., 2019; Soglia et al., 2019). Among these, wooden breast (WB) is a severe myopathy that adversely affects meat quality (Baldi et al., 2019). Wooden breast is characterized by protruding, pale areas of hardened consistency, often accompanied by stripes and bleeding spots (Sihvo et al., 2014). The incidence of WB is associated with a decrease in muscle quality, mainly manifested by abnormal lipid metabolism, higher fat content (Baldi et al., 2019; Dalle Zotte et al., 2020), lower water holding capacity (Zotte et al., 2017) and the highest myofibrillar fragmentation index (Sihvo et al., 2014; Wang et al., 2022). These abnormalities are visually detectable at processing sites, as well as during packaging and marketing, potentially complicating further processing and contributing to consumer rejection. It is incredible that WB has caused an estimated loss of $200 million per year in the United States (Kuttappan et al., 2016). Overall, WB degrades the processing properties of the broiler and poses serious challenges to broiler processors.

There is currently considerable variation in the reported incidence of WB. For example, one study reported that the incidence of WB was up to 27.02% at 49 days of age (Kang et al., 2022), but another article reported that the incidence of WB was up to 85.9% at 35 days of age and that of WB was 89.2% at 42 days of age (Cruz et al., 2017). The changes in the broiler satellite cell populations, which are likely associated with the necrosis and fibrosis observed in myopathies like WB, limit the ability of the satellite cells to regenerate damaged muscle fibers back to their original state (Velleman, 2023). The number of muscle satellite cells and the density of macrophages increased significantly in WB along with the relative expression of collagen (Ferreira et al., 2020). Meanwhile, based on transcriptome data, many differentially expressed genes (DEGs) have been screened for WB, for example, PPARGC1A and PPARGC1B were involved in mitochondrial biogenesis and functionality and WFIKKN1 was associated with hypertrophic muscle growth (Bordini et al., 2024), and TGFB3, COL1A1, COL1A2, and COL3A1 were identified as central hub genes involved in the fibrotic processes (Zhang et al., 2024). However, as this research evolves, an expansion at the cellular level is needed to enable deeper exploration, and the potential pathogenesis of WB should be a primary focus.

In this study, a total of 300 fast-growing white-feathered chickens (Arbor Acres, AA) were evaluated at 49 days of age. We divided the breast muscle tissue into three groups: normal group (NOR), mild WB group and severe WB group, and compared the differences between the NOR and severe WB group using single-cell RNA-sequencing (scRNA-seq) and untargeted Liquid Chromatography Mass Spectrometry (LC-MS) analysis. This study provides new insights into the underlying pathogenesis of different cell types for WB and a better knowledge of different cell type interactions, functional roles played by different tissue architectural elements will facilitate improvements and meat quality.

Materials and methods

Ethics statement

All procedures related to the chickens used in this study were in accordance with the standards of the Laboratory Animal Guidelines for the Ethical Review of Animal Welfare and were approved by the Committee on the Ethics of Animal Experiments of Hunan Agricultural University (HAU ACC 2020213).

Animals sample collection and muscle myopathy evaluation

A total of 300 fast-growing white-feathered chickens (Arbor Acres, AA, broiler line) were used for muscle myopathy evaluation. Environmental and nutritional conditions were as recommended (Aviagen, 2014). After 12 h of fasting, the chickens were individually weighed, and were slaughtered at 49 days of age. The left pectoral muscle was then eviscerated and weighed. WB was scored and graded by subjective assessment as described in previous studies (Tijare et al., 2016). Briefly, the subjective assessment included no area of stiffness or hardness in normal pectoralis major fillets (normal breast scored 0), moderate stiffness in the upper and/or lower part of the fillet (mild WB scored 1), severe stiffness (severe WB scored 2). Meanwhile, the right breast was excised, snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at -80°C for subsequent analyses. Breast muscle samples were also fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for subsequent analyses.

Histopathological evaluation

We performed histological evaluations for right breast specimens, including normal breast, mild WB and severe WB. The histological evaluations were primarily based on the method of Clark and Velleman (2016). Briefly, breast tissues were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin for more than 24 h at 4°C. Specimens were oriented transversely, dehydrated in a graded series of ethanol, and embedded in paraffin. Consecutive sections (5 μM thick) were prepared from each sample and stained with haematoxylin and eosin. The myopathic lesions, such as myodegeneration and fibrosis, were evaluated using a microscope (Olympus Corporation, Tokyo, Japan). Finally, based on the histopathological evaluation and touch scores, we divided the pectoral muscles into three levels: normal group (NOR, score 0), mild group (MILD, score 1), and severe group (SEV/WB, score 2).

Masson's stain

Masson's trichrome staining was performed using the Trichrome Stain (Connective Tissue Stain) Kit (Abcam, Cambridge, England). We performed the staining according to the manufacturer's instructions for the Trichrome Stain Kit for NOR (n = 3) and WB (n = 3) groups. Briefly, after dewaxing and hydration, the slides were placed in pre-heated Bouin's fluid for 1 h, followed by a cooling period of 10 min. After washing, the slides were stained with Weigert's working iron haematoxylin for 5 min. Biebrich scarlet, phosphomolybdic acid solution, aniline blue solution, and acetic acid solution (1%) were added in turn. They were then dehydrated very rapidly in two changes of 95% alcohol, followed by 2 changes of absolute alcohol. Finally, the slides were rinsed in distilled water, dehydrated, and mounted.

RNA scope

To detect the expression of PAX7 and MYOD1 genes in the NOR (n = 3) and WB (n = 3) groups, we designed specific RNA probes for PAX7 and MYOD1. The RNA Scope procedure was performed according to the manufacturer's specifications for the RNA Scope 2.5 Reagent Kit-Red (Advanced Cell Diagnostics, Bio-Techne, Minneapolis, USA). Briefly, the tissue was fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin and embedded in paraffin. After deparaffinization and rehydration of the tissue sections, we performed antigen retrieval and protease digestion to expose the target RNA molecules. Crucially, the target RNA molecules were hybridized to specific RNA probes for PAX7 and MYOD1. The amplified signal was detected and visualized using fluorescence-labeled probes and fluorescence microscopy. The tissue sections were counterstained to visualize cell morphology and tissue architecture, and imaging systems were used to observe and analyze RNA expression patterns.

Single cell RNA-sequencing analysis

The six samples, including the normal breast (NOR, n = 3) and the severe WB (WB, n = 3) groups, were used for scRNA-seq analysis. Single-cell isolation, cDNA amplification, and library construction were performed according to the manufacturer's guidelines for the Chromium single-cell sequencing technology from 10x Genomics (Pleasanton, CA, USA). Briefly, we enzymatically digested NOR and WB groups into single cell suspensions, filtered cellular debris, and used droplet-based microfluidic technology to construct the libraries, which were constructed using the 10x Genomics Single Cell v2 Kit (Pleasanton, CA, USA). In addition, the prepared libraries were quality-checked and sequenced on an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 platform. Raw data were pre-processed using Cell Ranger (version 4.0.1) and mapped to the chicken reference genome GRCg6a (https://ftp.ensembl.org/pub/release-104/fasta/gallus_gallus/dna/Gallus_gallus.GRCg6a.dna_sm.toplevel.fa.gz). Seurat (version 3.2.0) (Butler et al., 2018) was used for data quality control. Cells with < 200 or > 5000 genes, or with more than 50% mitochondrial genes were considered low quality and filtered. We performed t-edistributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) analysis for NOR and WB groups. For paired differential expression analysis, the FindAllMarkers function from Seurat (version 3.2.0) (Butler et al., 2018) was used to compared the cells of a cluster with the rest of the cells in the dataset. The clusters were divided into different cell types and annotated based on the expression of known marker genes.

GO and KEGG enrichment analysis

The DEGs were identified using the criteria (P < 0.05 and fold change > 0.25) between NOR and WB groups. In addition, Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses were performed using KOBAS 3.0 (http://kobas.cbi.pku.edu.cn) based on the DEGs of certain clusters. A P value of 0.05 was used as the threshold for significant pathway enrichment. The significantly enriched pathways were visualized using Hiplot (ORG) (https://hiplot.cn/).

Pseudotime trajectory analysis

Single-cell trajectories were analyzed using a matrix of cells and gene expression by Monocle2 in Seurat (version 3.2.0) (Butler et al., 2018) for macrophages, B cells, myocytes endothelial cells and satellite cells. Meanwhile, Monocle2 reduced the space to two dimensions and ordered the cells. Once the cells were ordered, we visualized the trajectory in the reduced dimensional space. The trajectory has a tree-like structure with nodes and branches.

Cell-cell interactions

To identify potential cellular communication in each cell type, we applied the NATMI algorithm (Hou et al., 2020) to the scRNA-seq profiles of NOR and WB groups using default parameters in Python (version 2.7.6). Schematic diagrams of cell interactions were constructed using Adobe Illustrator software. We then constructed the binding of receptor genes and ligand genes for each cell type based on the JASPAR database (http://jaspar.genereg.net/).

Untargeted metabolomic analysis

The incidence of severe WB reached 13.00% (39 broilers) of 300 AA broilers by tactile score and histomorphological observation. And the normal breast group (n = 20), mild WB group (n = 20) and severe WB group (n = 20) were used for untargeted LC-MS analysis. Untargeted metabolism experiments were carried out by Personal Biotechnology Co.,Ltd (Shanghai, China). Briefly, breast tissues (100 mg) were individually ground with liquid nitrogen, and the homogenate was resuspended with pre-chilled 80% methanol and 0.1% formic acid by vortexing. After centrifugation at 12,000g for 10 min, the extracts were aspirated and filtered. Quality control samples were prepared by mixing 20 μL aliquots from each sample to monitor deviations in analytical results from these pooled mixtures.

LC-MS analyses were performed using a UHPLC system (Vanquish, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) with a UPLC BEH amide column coupled to a Q Exactive HF-X mass spectrometer (Orbitrap MS, Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). The mass spectrometer was used for its ability to acquire MS/MS spectra in information-dependent acquisition modes under the control of the acquisition software (Xcalibur, Thermo Fisher Scientific). The acquisition software continuously evaluated the full-scan MS spectrum in this mode.

Raw data were converted into mzXML format using ProteoWizard (v3.0.8789) and processed in R (version 3.3.2), using XCMS for peak detection, extraction, alignment, and integration. An in-house MS2 database (BiotreeDB) was used for metabolite annotation (Smith et al., 2006). Positive and negative ion modes were used for metabolite detection to increase metabolite coverage and improve detection.

Identification of differential metabolites

Multivariate analyses, including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA), were performed using R software (version 3.3.2) to obtain the variable importance parameter (VIP) value for each peak. The metabolites were further annotated using the HumanMetabolome Database (HMDB) (http://www.hmdb.ca), LipidMaps (http://www.lipidmaps.org), Metlin (http://metlin.scripps.edu), MassBank (http://www.massbank.jp/), and mzClound (https://www.mzcloud.org) databases. Metabolites with VIP values greater than 1 and P values less than 0.05 were considered as differential metabolites (DMs) (Trygg and Wold, 2002). The metabolic pathways involved in DMs were constructed according to the analyses of the KEGG and MetPA databases.

Statistical analysis

Each experiment was performed in triplicate. Data are presented as the mean ± standard deviations of each set of three independent experiments. When appropriate, statistical significance was assessed using paired Student's t-tests, with P < 0.05, 0.01, 0.001, or 0.0001 used as the significance level. All results were presented as means and standard deviations and analyzed using GraphPad Prism 8 software (GraphPad Software, Inc, San Diego, CA, US).

Heterogeneity of macrophages in wooden breast

Firstly, there were 688 macrophages in the NOR group and 4,801 macrophages in the WB group, and they were clearly divided into clusters 4, 11 and 15 (Fig. 2A). Interestingly, almost all of the macrophages in cluster 11 were detected in the WB group (Fig. 2B). The proportions of these three clusters exhibited significant changes, especially in clusters 11 and 15. Remarkably, the proportion of cluster 11 increased from 4.1% to 32.2% (Fig. 2C). Meanwhile, cluster 11 showed significant heterogeneity among the top 50 genes in the heatmap (Fig. 2D). In particular, CCL4, CRISPLD2, CA9, IL5RA, BATF3 and SLC16A3 were almost exclusively expressed in cluster 11, whereas CG-1B, RSFR, CD83, and GRN were predominately expressed in clusters 4 and 15 (Figure S3A). Six KEGG pathways were significantly enriched across the three clusters, including regulation of actin cytoskeleton, focal adhesion, and apoptosis (Figure S3B and Table S5). These pathways (e.g., cellular senescence, RNA transport and oxidative phosphorylation) were specifically and significantly enriched in cluster 11 (Fig. 2E). To analyze the macrophage heterogeneity and reconstruct their differentiation trajectory, we used Monocle2 software to examine the single-cell transition process, which was divided into three major tree-like structures with the macrophage clusters (Fig. 2F). Surprisingly, cluster 11 appeared almost exclusively in a single branch of the severe WB group, and while clusters 4 and 15 intermingled in two branches (Fig. 2G). Identification of gene expression characteristics in the myocytes

Pseudotime analysis revealed that there were three branches in both groups, with adipocytes (subcluster 0 and subcluster 4) predominantly concentrated in two branches (Fig. 3C). Regeneration and repair of satellite cells when WB appeared

Clusters 8 and 22 were identified as satellite cells. The WB group had more cells (914) than the NOR group (744) according to the t-SNE analysis (Fig. 5A). To clarify how satellite cells repair muscle damage, we redefined the satellite cell, dividing into four subclusters. Our results suggested that subcluster 1 was almost unique to the WB group (Fig. 5B). Based on pseudotime analysis in satellite cells, we found that subcluster 1 was located on a separate branch in the WB group (Fig. 5C). The top 10 genes (e.g., APOA1, COL3A1, COL1A1) that were significantly differentially expressed in each subcluster were then displayed in a heatmap (Fig. 5D). In subcluster 1, regulation of actin cytoskeleton, ECM-receptor interaction and focal adhesion were enriched (Fig. 5E). To verify whether satellite cells were activated, we examined the expression profile of cell proliferation marker genes PAX7 and MYOD1. RNA Scope showed that the expression abundance of PAX7 and MYOD1 was higher in the WB group compared to the NOR group (Fig. 5F). We found that 10 genes in subcluster 1 and marker genes PAX7 and MYOD1 formed a regulatory network that interacted with each other (Figure S5). Communication interaction network between different cell types

To further analyze the communication interaction network among different cell types, we identified of ligands/receptors pairs between NOR and WB groups (Figure S6). The results showed that the ligand/receptor pairs in the satellite cells were the highest between NOR and WB groups, myocytes were the second highest and macrophages were the lowest (Fig. 6A-B). Discussion

Wooden breast is an epigenetic disease that seriously affects meat quality and palatability. In our previous study, we found that severe WB fillets had higher L∗, a∗, and dripping loss, but a lower pH and mean myofiber caliber (P < 0.05) (Kang et al., 2022). However, the mechanisms underlying the formation and regulation of WB are not fully understood. In this study, we investigated the cell type and metabolic regulation of WB myopathy using scRNA-seq and metabolomic data. We identified the heterogeneity of multiple cell types and metabolites in WB to varying degrees.

Macrophages were the most heterogeneous cell type in the WB. The number of macrophages in the WB group was higher than in the NOR group with a consistent trend in macrophage density observed by cryohistological immunofluorescence analysis (Ferreira et al., 2020). The expression of the top 50 genes in macrophages showed significant differential expression in cluster 11 compared to clusters 4 and 15, particularly for genes such as CCL4 and B2M. The distinct expression dynamics of these lineage-specific genes could enhance our understanding of the heterogeneity in severe WB cases. Pseudotime trajectory analysis showed that gene expression in macrophages followed the same trend. Heterogeneous breast muscle reprogrammed the differentiation trajectory of macrophage, and induced the production of a single branch involved in immune metabolism. DEGs in cluster 11 were mainly enriched in the apoptosis, cellular senescence, phagosome and endocytosis. Cluster 4 was significantly enriched in pathways involving apoptosis, phagosome and ECM-receptor interaction, which align with the pro-inflammatory responses that are characteristic of M1 macrophages (Cheng et al., 2017; Gordon and Taylor, 2005; Mosser and Edwards, 2008; Yunna et al., 2020). Cluster 15 was significantly enriched in Toll-like receptor signaling pathway, cell adhesion molecules (CAMs), endocytosis, lysosome, and focal adhesion. These pathways are consistent with M2 macrophages exerting anti-inflammatory responses (Mantovani et al., 2002; Yunna et al., 2020). Thus, our results suggest that cluster 11 is a macrophage with heterogeneity in WB muscle. It is also a distinct cell type with a unique differentiation trajectory and gene expression pattern. However, more definitive evidence for the function of this cell type will be required.

Myocytes were mainly characterized by a reduction in cell number and altered gene expression patterns in the WB group. Repartitioning of myocytes revealed increased adiposity, consistent with previous findings (Dalle Zotte et al., 2020). DEGs were mainly enriched in pathways such as apoptosis, the MAPK signaling pathway, and focal adhesion, which were also found in the pathways enriched for DEGs from male Ross 308 broilers slaughtered at 6 and 7 weeks of age (Malila et al., 2021). There was a correlation between the apoptosis pathway and the reduction in myocytes. In addition, DEGs mainly included APOA1, COL6A3, COL6A2, and COL1A1, which were more highly expressed in the WB group. Apolipoprotein A I (APOA1), the major protein component of high-density lipoproteins (HDL), is a multifunctional protein, involved in cholesterol transport and regulation of inflammatory and immune responses (Georgila et al., 2019). APOA1 gene levels are positively regulated in relation to fat deposition in myocytes. COL6A3, COL6A2, and COL1A1 are members of the collagen family and constitute a major component of the extracellular matrix, supporting cell growth through the focal adhesion pathway (Burridge, 2017; Thomas et al., 2015). Microfibrils produced during the assembly of collagen VI molecules are implicated in maintaining muscle function, providing an important link between the myocyte and the surrounding matrix through various molecular interactions (Sorushanova et al., 2019). Accordingly, an upregulation of COL6A3 expression in muscles severely affected by both WS (Praud et al., 2020) and WB (Papah et al., 2018), consistent with the high degree of fibrosis that characterizes myopathic muscles. COL6A3 and COL5A2 were hub genes in the protein content module identified by weighted gene co-expression network analysis for the WS/WB (Bordini et al., 2022). Overall, the expression of collagen family gene aligns with the severe disruption of muscle structure associated with collagen accumulation observed in WB (Velleman, 2020).

A major feature of chicken WB muscle is inadequate blood supply, resulting in reduced blood vessel density (Petracci et al., 2019), possibly caused by a decrease in the number of endothelial cells in the blood vessels. Meanwhile, the phenomenon of fat deposition around the blood vessels has been observed. Our results showed that there were fewer endothelial cells in the WB group. To further understand the molecular mechanisms of endothelial cells, we divided endothelial cells into four clusters. Through KEGG functional enrichment analysis, we found that protein phosphatase 1 regulatory subunit 12A (PPP1R12A) and KCNMB4 were involved in vascular smooth muscle contraction. The regulatory protein subunit PPP1R12A interacts with PP1C to induce muscle relaxation in smooth muscle cells by dephosphorylating myosin regulatory light chain 20 (MLC20) (Martinez-Lemus et al., 2009; Pinheiro et al., 2011). KCNMB4, also known as MaxiK (large conductance, voltage- and Ca2+-activated K+) channels, are composed of a pore-forming alpha-subunit (Adelman et al., 1992) and modulatory transmembrane beta-subunits (Knaus et al., 1994; McManus et al., 1995). MaxiK channels are large conductance, voltage and calcium-sensitive potassium channels that are fundamental to the control of smooth muscle tone and neuronal excitability. Therefore, a reduction in endothelial cells leads to smooth muscle contraction in blood vessels, resulting in inadequate blood supply, this was consistent for WB via oxidative stress with decreased vascular inflammation and density.

As we predicted, the number of satellite cells increased in the WB group. How are these increased cells involved in muscle reconstruction and repair? First, for satellite cell repair mechanisms to regenerate the damaged myofiber, the satellite cells must first be activated and return to the cell cycle from a state of quiescence (Velleman, 2023). PAX7 serves as a key marker gene during satellite cell repair (Lepper and Fan, 2010; Sambasivan et al., 2011; Seale et al., 2000), while MYOD1 is a marker gene for myoblast proliferation (Comai and Tajbakhsh, 2014) and regulation of skeletal muscle repair (Parker et al., 2003). Our results showed that both PAX7 and MYOD1 were highly expressed in the WB group. Therefore, when the myoblasts were damaged, the satellite cells in the WB were indeed activated. Moreover, the environment surrounding the satellite cells, known as the satellite cell niche, influences satellite cell activity including proliferation and differentiation through its composition of extracellular matrix proteins (Thomas et al., 2015), growth factors, vascular supply (Bi and Kuang, 2012; Christov et al., 2007), capillaries, and sufficient vascularization (Christov et al., 2007; Rhoads et al., 2009). Satellite cells had to proliferate and differentiate into muscle-specific structures through a complex regulatory network, including several pathways and genes with high expression. These pathways included regulation of the actin cytoskeleton, ECM-receptor interaction and focal adhesion (Burridge, 2017), which affected satellite cell activity. These genes were mainly APOA1, AQP1, COL1A1, COL3A1, CALD1, FN1, GJA1, PTN, TNC and VCAN, which were found to be present almost exclusively in the subcluster 1 of the WB group. COL1A1 and COL3A1 are involved in collagen formation (Sorushanova et al., 2019). Masson's staining showed collagen infiltration and increased collagen fibers, consistent with the results of immunofluorescence-stained cryosections (Ferreira et al., 2020). Thus, COL1A1 and COL3A1 were key candidate genes in the WB.

In order to further elucidate the interactions between cells, we performed intercellular communication analyses using the NATMI algorithm. NATMI introduces the concept of cell connectivity summary networks. This method consolidates multiple ligand/receptor interaction from one cell type to another into a single weighted connectivity summary edge, providing a measure of the strength and specificity of communication between cell type (Hou et al., 2020). Among the tools analyzed, NATMI excels in predicting cell-cell interactions, defined here as source-target-ligand-receptor tetrads (Xie et al., 2023). Our data showed that in normal chicken tissues, macrophages (cluster 11 only), myocytes and satellite cells are involved to maintain normal physiological functions, such as CLRV/CLDF2B. In general, ligand/-receptor binding induces changes in transcriptional activity. We have also predicted transcription factors (NFYA, ZEB1, ESR1, NR3C1, NFAT5 and LIN54), but the exact conclusions need to be confirmed in the future. Post-lignification occurs, it is possible that novel macrophages (cluster 11) are activated and involved in functions such as phagocytosis, and no longer interact with myocytes and satellite cells, although satellite cells are activated and frequently communicate with myocytes. Thus, WB disrupt the intercellular balance and increase communication between myocytes and satellite cells.

To screen for key metabolites in WB, we identified hundreds of DMs, particularly between the NOR and SEV groups. Among the 17 candidate metabolites, trans-cinnamate, uracil, 2-ketobutyric acid, 3-indoleacetonitrile, and phenyl acetate had high levels in more severe WB, while beta-alanine and dihydrouracil had the opposite trend. The six key metabolites were beta-alanine, dihydrouracil, trans-cinnamate, uracil, 2-ketobutyric acid, and fumaric acid. Beta-alanine is essential for carnosine content in skeletal muscle, and it is the rate-limiting precursor of carnosine, which plays an important role in exercise performance and skeletal muscle homeostasis (Artioli et al., 2010; Matthews et al., 2019). Fumaric acid was highest in the WB group, and body weight was significantly higher in the WB group than in the NOR group (Kong et al., 2021). Body weight at 49 days of age was significantly improved by the addition of 0.125% fumaric acid (Skinner et al., 1991). Fumaric acid was effective in reducing salmonella counts and also had a significant effect on meat quality and sensory attributes (Fernández et al., 2021). However, dihydrouracil, trans-cinnamate, uracil, and 2-ketobutyric acid have not been reported to be associated with chicken meat quality.

Conclusions

Overall, our data showed that the WB/SEV group had more immune cells and satellite cells, especially macrophages, and fewer myocytes and endothelial cells, with higher gene expression levels of CCL4, B2M, APOA1, COL1A1, and COL6A2. At the same time, six key DMs (beta-alanine, dihydrouracil, trans-cinnamate, uracil, 2-ketobutyric acid, and fumaric acid) were found in 22 enriched pathways. Elucidating the interactions among different cell types in WB is crucial for advancing our comprehensive understanding of the disease.

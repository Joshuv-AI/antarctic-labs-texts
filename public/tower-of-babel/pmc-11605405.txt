Effect of watering before slaughter and the evisceration method on the quality characteristics of fattened goose liver
Authors: Koppány L. Majzinger, Gábor Jónás, József Surányi, Ágnes Zimborán, Gabriella Kiskó, Andrea Taczman-Brückner, Annamária Barkó, László F. Friedrich
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The aim of this study was to investigate the effect of watering before slaughter and different evisceration methods on the quality characteristics of fattened goose liver. A total of randomly selected 60 male Greylag Landaise geese (Anser anser) were used to execute the experiment which had been force-fed during their growing period. In the slaughterhouse, randomized block design was followed and the birds were randomly divided into four experimental groups and half of the them (30 birds) were given 200 ml tap water. During the slaughter bleeding kinetics was measured and after the traditional processing steps (scalding, defeathering, waxing, water cooling, clearing of wax residues, rinsing, feet removal) 50 % of the birds (30 birds) were immediately eviscerated. The other half of the carcasses were eviscerated after 8 hours of cooling. Then pH and hemoglobin content were determined and the livers were vacuum packed and stored at -18 °C for 35 days (5 weeks). During this time color measurement and microbial analysis were taken. Results showed that pH was not influenced by neither the watering nor the evisceration methods. Hemoglobin content was significantly reduced by watering (P<0.001). Cold evisceration was proved to have siginificant effect on the color parameters (P<0.001), however the impact was not immediate, the difference was first detected on day 21. According to microbiological analysis four species were identified but none of them are known to produce hydrogen sulfide (H2S) which is required to the formation of sulfhemoglobin which compound is most likely responsible for the greenish discoloration occured during the storage.In summary, it can be stated that the evisceration method had a great impact on the color of fattened goose livers and undesirable changes - e.g. surface greening - which commonly occur in case of vacuum packed and frozen stored livers could be prevented by the application of warm evisceration.

Introduction

The production of fattened goose and duck liver - foie gras how it is called in French - has a long tradition, dating back hundreds of years (Fazekas et al., 2022). It has always been considered a premium product, and even today it is featured prominently on the cartes of fine dining restaurant. This noble material of high end cuisine is produced applying a special force-feeding technique with a high carbohydrate diet (Guémené and Guy, 2004; Beaver and Golab, 2023), which means that the volume of feed the birds receive is significantly in excess of what would be their voluntary intake (Skippon, 2013). However, the liver, which becomes fatty as a result of overfeeding (de Almeida, 2017) is not only crucial in terms of gastronomy but also a significant export item for several European countries. The foreign trade indicators are positively influenced by its sale thereby the livelihood of many people who work in this field of the poultry industry are ensured.

France, Hungary and Bulgaria are outstanding representatives of fattened liver industry. In 2019, 93% of the global production were produced by these three countries (France: 18 699 tons, Hungary: 3 523 tons, Bulgaria: 2 981 tons, total – world: 27 100 tons) (Julien-David and Marcic, 2020). In the ancient times, mainly geese were force-fed in order to produce fattened livers but nowadays more than 90% of foie gras is from ducks (Lavilla et al., 2019).

Raising and processing of geese requires a substantial amount of human effort and manual labour (Gržinić et al., 2022) since many of the steps in the workflow are not automated therethrough the expenditure of the producers were greatly increased. Furthermore, the good quality forage, which is crucial in order to reach the optimal gaining of liver weight, also meant a serious expense (van der Poel et al., 2020). The drought-stricken summers and the events of recent years - COVID-19 pandemic, the war that broke out in Ukraine - had a serious economic impact and were manifested in the form of further cost increase (Urak et al., 2024). All these happenings leaded to a massive growth of liver selling prices. Obviously, if the products are expensive, the expectations of consumers are high. For this reason, it is therefore important to investigate all factors and phenomena that may affect the quality of fattened goose livers, to understand their mechanism and to be able to prevent their occurrence. The main factors are housing and feeding conditions (Zhang et al., 2024), stunning and slaughtering technologies (Fernandez et al., 2010), cooling and packaging methods and of course, packaging materials (Orkusz, 2018).

40-60 % of the total blood amount can be removed from the carcass (Warriss, 1984), but this ratio can be decreased by the thickening of blood or a possible blood clotting. The remained blood in the fattened liver can cause undesirable appearance and reduce the shelf life. The method of evisceration also contributes to the quality of the products. In most cases, the carcasses are eviscerated after cooling since the livers can easily be bruised at body temperature (38,5-39,5°C) (Wascher et al., 2018). During cooling sulphotic bacteria (e.g. Pseudomonas mephitica) can pass from the intestine to the surface of the liver and the metabolic product of which reacts with the blood in the liver may lead to the greenish appearance of the product (Szabó et al., 2012).

The conditions before slaughtering and the evisceration method are important steps that have been less investigated and the available scientific knowledge regarding this topic is insufficient.

The hypothesis of the study was that the quality of fattened goose livers could be enhanced by pre-slaughter hydration of the birds and the immediate removal of offal just after the exsanguination and the first processing steps. Therefore, the aim of this experiment was to investigate the effect of watering before slaughter on the removable blood amount and the evisceration method on the quality characteristics of fattened goose liver during frozen storage.

Materials and methods

Ethical statement

The experiment was performed in an approved, veterinarian supervised plant (Pannon Fine Food Ltd., 5900 Orosháza, Október 6 Street 8., Hungary, Approval number HU-1182 EK). The experiment was continuously supervised by the Animal Welfare Officer of the establishment and all animal welfare requirements, in particular Regulation (EC) No 1099/2009 on the protection of animals at the time of killing were complied with during the handling and slaughtering of the animals.

In the growing period (1-35 d) all birds were fed ad libitum with the same pelleted diet (Ingredients: minced corn 55%, wheat flour 43%, plant origin fat powder 1,8%, salt 0,2%, energy: 12,57 MJ ME/kg, Cargill Hungary Ltd., Hungary). In the growing-finishing period (36-56 d) the composition of the birds' feed was the following: minced corn 58%, wheat flour 39,5%, plant origin fat powder 2,3%, salt 0,2%, energy: 13,0 MJ ME/kg (Nitak Ltd., Hungary). The watering was ad libitum from 1-56 d. In the overfeeding period from 57-70 d, the diet used in the growing-finishing period was supplemented with 40% of unsteamed corn grain. The birds were fed every seventh hour. The overfeeding was started with a portion of 160 g feed and evenly increased in seven days resulting in a portion of 440 g. From the 64-70 d, the birds were overfed with constant 440 g feed. The watering was ad libitum during the overfeeding period. The birds were not vaccinated or given any medication. During the overfeeding period, they were given a probiotic and vitamin complex in drinking water (0,1 m/m%): Gastroferm M+C and LI products which had a digestion promoting attribution so intestinal clogging and the development of diarrhea could be prevented. Furthermore, these additives were also effective in preventing fungal infection of esophagus and intestinal inflammations.

At the end of the force-feeding phase the bodyweight of the birds was scaled, the values ranged between 6 400 - 7 100 g. Then the birds were transported in plastic cages (3 birds / cage) to a slaughterhouse (Pannon Fine Food Ltd., Orosháza, Hungary) in a ventilated truck 33 km away from the breeding station.

In the slaughterhouse resting area, 60 birds were randomly allocated into four equal groups (15 birds/group) and their feet were marked with different color plastic rings according to the watering and evisceration method. The watering was performed by a silicon tube with a diameter of 5 mm and 50 ml syringes. The 200 ml water was injected into the birds through their esophagus. After watering all of the birds were rested for two hours then they were hung by their feet using shackles on a metal rack. During slaughtering the Halal method was followed (Nakyinsige et. al., 2013). The birds were hung in a vertical position and the jugular veins were cut without any stunning. During bleeding the blood was collected from each bird. After the bleeding, scalding (66°C), defeathering, waxing (53°C), water cooling (16°C), removing wax containing immature feathers, rinsing, and feet removal were performed. The carcasses were remarked again with different color plastic rings at the wings. Those carcasses intended for cold evisceration (Group 1, Group 3) were placed in a cooling room at 2°C, for 8 hours. At the end of the cooling 6°C of core temperature was reached. After cooling the carcasses were eviscerated and the livers were removed and weighed individually (Steinberg-Systems SBS-LW-10000A digital scale, accuracy: 0.1 g, Steinberg-Systems expondo GmBh, Germany). From those carcasses that were eviscerated without cooling (Group 2, Group 4) the livers were removed after the rinsing, and the feet were removed and then kept on an ice bed for 8 hours. The main blood vessels were carefully removed from all livers. The livers were individually packed into polyethylene (PE) vacuum packs and were frozen and stored at -18°C for five weeks.

Measurement of bleeding

During the bleeding, the blood was collected in a plastic vessel which was placed on a digital scale with an accuracy of 0.1 g. The bleeding was three minutes long and the amount of blood was recorded every 15 seconds. The head of the birds was held above the plastic vessel by hand so the loss of blood possibly occuring during their agony was prevented.

Hemoglobin content

Hemoglobin (Hgb) content of the livers was determined by using Drabkin's Reagent (D5941, Sigma-Aldrich). The components of the reagent are sodium bicarbonate, potassium ferricyanide, and potassium cyanide. The solution made from the reagent reacts with all forms of hemoglobin except sulfhemoglobin. The procedure is based on the oxidation of hemoglobin and its derivatives to methemoglobin in the presence of alkaline potassium ferricyanide. Methemoglobin reacts with potassium cyanide to form cyanmethemoglobin, which has maximum absorption at 540 nm (Balasubramaniam and Malathi, 1992). The color intensity measured at 540 nm is proportional to the total hemoglobin (Hgb) concentration.

After evisceration the cooled livers (10 pcs/group) were individually homogenized with GRT-BC06 industrial cutter and 4 g samples were taken from each of them. The samples were mixed with 40 ml of water with IKA T 25 ULTRA-TURRAX homogenizer at 10.000 rpm for 1 minute. The mixtures were centrifuged at 5.000 rpm, 4 °C for 10 minutes then filtered. After filtration 20 µl of filtrate and 5 ml of Drabkin's Solution were mixed and stored at room temperature (23°C) for 15 minutes. The filtrates were submitted to spectrophotometric readings (Hitachi U2400, Hitachi Corp., Japan) at wavelength 540 nm in a 10 mm pathway glass cuvets versus blank which was pure Drabkin's Solution. The absorbance values were recorded and the total hemoglobin (Hgb) concentration (mg Hgb / g liver) was calculated using the calibration curve and considering the initial dilution step. Three parallel spectrophotometric measurements were performed from each liver filtrate.

Color measurement

The color was measured using a Minolta CR 400 (Konica Minolta Inc., Japan) portable chromameter with D65 illuminant and an aperture size of 10 mm with a closed cone at 90° to the liver surface. Calibration plate: Y=81.3, x=0.3192, y=0.3266. Data were evaluated in CIELab color space where L* lightness, a* redness, and b* yellowness. The color measurements were carried out on vacuum-packed frozen livers ventral side of the large lobe (top, middle, and bottom) for 35 days without opening the packs. Therefore colorimeter calibration was done just before the measurements using the same PE vacuum pack in which the livers were packed.

pH measurement

The pH was measured after evisceration on the cooled livers ventral side of the large lobe (top, middle, and bottom) by a Testo 206 pH2 digital pH meter (Testo GmBh, Austria). The instrument was previously calibrated to pH 4.01 and pH 7.01 buffer solutions.

Microbiology

Livers were tested for microorganisms which are related to poultry and poultry products. Qualitative determination was aimed. Sampling was done in Cruma Biosafety Cabinet Class II A2 using previously sterilized tools. A 10 g sample from the surface of each liver was scraped off and put into sterile plastic bags. Then decimal dilution was applied with a sterile distilled water solution containing 1% (m/m) peptone and 8,5% (m/m) NaCl. The samples were homogenized for 2 minutes with Interscience BagMixer 400 SW homogenizer. Further decimal dilution was made from the homogenized samples into 20 ml test tubes. Then the double-diluted samples were homogenized with an iSwix Vortex Mixer for 1 minute at 2.500 rpm. This step was repeated once again so a dilution of thousandth was obtained from each liver. Finally, 0.1 ml inoculum of each diluted solution - decimal, hundredth, thousandth - of each liver was dispersed on cephalothin sodium fusidate cetrimide agar medium with a sterile glass spreader. The samples were incubated at 25°C for 44 hours. The isolated colonies developed during the incubation were identified with Bruker Microflex LT matrix-assisted laser desorption-ionization time-of-flight mass spectrometry (MALDI-TOF MS) according to the formic acid suspension preparation protocol (Horváth et al., 2020).

Statistical analysis

Obtained data were evaluated with a statistical software (IBM Corp. Released 2019. IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 26.0. Armonk, NY: IBM Corp).

Three parallel measurements were performed on all samples for each parameter and the average value of the results was compared.

In case of pH and hemoglobin two-way analysis of variance (two-way ANOVA) was applied. ANOVA test was performed instead of multivariate analysis of variance (MANOVA) because the dependent variables (hemoglobin, pH) were independent of each other based on Pearson's correlation test. Regarding the bleeding one-way analysis of variance (one-way ANOVA) was performed. The results of color measurement were assayed applying two-way multivariate analysis of variance (two-way MANOVA). MANOVA test was performed with Bonferroni correction instead of repeated measures analysis of variance (RMANOVA) because the assumption test of sphericity was violated.

In all cases, normality of the residuals was proved by Shapiro-Wilk test and homogenity of variance was proved by Levene's test. The significance level was choosen at P≤0.05 in each case. Post-hoc tests were not performed since independent variables had fewer than three groups.

Discussion

pH and hemoglobin

In terms of pH neither the watering nor the different evisceration methods resulted in a significant difference (P=0.225 and P=0.606) between the experimental groups. These results were consistent with our prior assumptions. The liver is weaved through by blood vessels and holds about 13 % of the body's blood supply at any given moment (Ziessmann et al. 2006). Approximately 50 % of the total blood volume can be removed during the bleeding so still a substantial amount remains in the carcass. The normal pH value of blood, unaffected by acidosis or alkalosis, ranges from 7.3 to 7.5 (Zhou, 2008). The pH value of pure water, which the geese were watered with, is pH 7.0. To sum up, the blood greatly affects the pH value of the liver and it could be foreseen that 200 ml of water would not result in a remarkable change. Furthermore, the role of kidneys also has to be mentioned. These organs are responsible for regulating the water balance of the body so the excess water which is not necessary for the proper functioning is excreted by them. Therefore, it was not expected either that watering would cause a greater change in pH value of the livers due to the operation of the excretory system. Regarding the evisceration, based on our current knowledge acidic or alkaline compounds are not formed during the cooling phase before the internal organs are removed from the carcass, so it can be stated that the pH value of fattened goose liver is not influenced by the method of evisceration.

In terms of hemoglobin the watering was proved to have a significant effect. The livers of watered experimental birds had significantly lower (P<0.001) hemoglobin content than the non-watered ones. As hemoglobin is a protein found in red blood cells (Wu and Galy, 2001) based on it's value the amount of blood can be inferred. It can be stated that due to watering, it was possible to remove more blood from fattened goose livers. The most likely explanation is that blood was diluted by water thereby it did not become as viscous as the blood of non-watered birds, the blood vessels did not narrow so the circulation was boosted. Regarding the evisceration, after the decease of birds the heart stopped to beat so the blood circulation was ceased. Having no propulsion in the carcasses it was not expected to detect any significant difference (P=0.656) between the cold and warm methods in terms of hemoglobin content.

Color

In the first 14 days of the 35 days frozen storage significant difference (P=0.368) was not found in any of the color coordinates. Perceived color difference (ΔE*) varied between 1 and 2 which meant that only experienced observers could notice slight difference in color between the different experimental groups. On day 21, a considerable reduction was detected in the perceptual lightness (L*), the red/green coordinate (a*) and the yellow/blue coordinate (b*) in case of cold eviscerated groups. The outmost decrease was shown in the value of a*, but the difference was also significant (P<0.001 and P<0.001) in the other two color parameters between cold and warm eviscerated groups. This phenomenom was clearly visible until the end of the experiment. The shrinkage of red/green coordinate (a*) in case of cold eviscerated livers meant that the samples started to lose their reddish character and turned into greenish. The surface discoloration of animal origin products, not preserved by heat treatment, containing hemoglobin or myoglobin has been known for a long time. The first publication dealing with the topic was published more than a century ago (Peenington and Sherwood, 1922). It was established that the greening could be caused by the sulfation of hemoglobin. The process which adversely affects the quality of the products requires hydrogen sulfide (H2S), hemoglobin remaining due to inadequate bleeding, and low oxygen tension (Nicol et. al., 1970). According to Szabó et al. (2014) the greenish discoloration of cold eviscerated, vacuum packed and frozen stored goose liver is most probably caused by the formation of sulfhemoglobin.

Watering did not have a significant effect on the color parameters. Although it was able to reduce the hemoglobin content but the compound was still abundant in the livers thereby all conditions for sulfhemoglobin formation were provided (Otto, 2020).

Bleeding

According to Kelly et Alworth (2013) poultry have a relatively small percentage of blood volume by body weight, approximately 6 – 7.5 %. In case of the current experiment it was possible to remove 4,2 % of blood by body weight.

Watering was proved not to affect neither the velocity of bleeding nor the amount of removable blood. Although it had significant effect (P<0.001) on the hemoglobin content of the livers. It was stated that due to watering, it was possible to remove more blood from the livers, but according to the results of bleeding almost the same percentage of blood abandoned the body. It means that the blood content of livers belong to watered experimental groups was decreased to a greater extent, but the excess blood remained in the carcass. A possible explanation for this phenomenom is that blood clotting occured in the cut at the jugular veins and the path of blood leading out of the body was blocked. According to Sabow et al. (2016) blood not lost at exsanguination is probably largely retained in the viscera rather than the muscle tissues.

It can be stated that 90 % of removable blood leaves the carcass in the first 60 seconds, so the processing of birds could be accelerated by diminishing the time of bleeding without having an adverse effect on the quality of fattened livers.

Microbiology

The green discoloration is most probably caused by the formation of sulfhemoglobin. The hemoglobin is derived from the remaining blood (Billett, 1990), the hydrogen sulfide (H2S) can be produced by bacterial metabolism (Murros, 2022). Chilled meat and meat products can commonly be contaminated by Pseudomonas species (Elbehiry et al., 2022) and many representants are known to generate hydrogen sulfide (H2S) such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa (Caruso et al., 2024), Pseudomonas mephitica (Nicol et al., 1970) and Pseudomonas putrefaciens (Serio et al., 2014). As cephalothin sodium fusidate cetrimide agar is not exclusively selective for Pseudomonas the presence of other microbes could be investigated too.

As Pseudomonas species were not detected it can be stated that the hydrogen sulfide (H2S) is of non-microbial origin. It may derive from the digestive tract of the birds. It is presumed that the birds can not digest such a huge amount of forage compared to their body weight. Nutrients are not properly absorbed in the small intestine and as a result of putrefaction processes hydrogen sulfide is formed. According to Barnes and Shrimpton (1957) the hydrogen sulfide from the intestinal system, which is not removed immediately after slaughter, diffuses through the intestines and reaches many parts of the body. The phenomenon can be explained by the elevated permeability of the bowels. The gut barrier can be disrupted after the slaughter and translocation of compounds formed during the digestion can occur. In case of warm evisceration this translocation was prevented by immediate removal of the livers. According to Baxter et al. (2019) the diet can influence the permeability of guts.

Escherichia coli is a member of coliform group and considered to be pathogenic, its presence indicates contamination of fecal origin (Holcomb et al., 2020) which must have caused by cutting the rectum during the evisceration. Carnobacterium maltaromaticum is commonly found in animals or products of animal origin and also in environments that are associated with animals or food (Cailliez-Grimal et al., 2014) and known as pathogen. Aeromonas bestiarum is autochthonous to aquatic environments and can cause disease in mammals, fish and birds (Kumru et al., 2018). Brochothrix thermosphacta is spoilage bacterium, but not a pathogen. It can commonly be detected in raw meat stored aerobically or packaged in a modified atmosphere, however it also grows on vacuum-packed meat stored chilled (Feiner, 2006).

Based on our current knowledge, none of these species can be associated with the green discoloration.

It is assumed that even though all the birds were of the same species they were fed, raised and kept in the same way but the compostion of their intestinal microflora was not exactly the same. These hypothetical differences might also have contributed to the formation of surface greening but further investigations are required to the substantiation.

Hypothesis of the study was partly substantiated. Warm evisceration was proved to enhance the quality of fattened goose liver by preventing the occurence of surface discoloration. Watering significantly reduced (P<0.001) the hemoglobin thereby the blood content of the livers, however the appearance of the undesirable greenish tint was not affected by the treatment.

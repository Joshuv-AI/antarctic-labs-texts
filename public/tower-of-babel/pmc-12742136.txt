Obinutuzumab versus Rituximab to maintain remission in children with steroid-dependent and frequently relapsing nephrotic syndrome: the OBIRINS study protocol, a double-blind randomised controlled trial
Authors: Claire Dossier, Anne-Laure Sellier-Leclerc, Thomas Simon, Cyrielle Parmentier, Olivia Boyer, Charlotte Samaille, Marc Fila, Gwenaelle Roussey-Kesler, Marie Magnavacca, Yannis Chartier, Ferielle Louillet, Ariane Zaloszyc, Isabelle Vrillon, Djamel Elaribi, Sabrina Bouatia, Florentia Kaguelidou, Sophie Guilmin-Crepon, Julien Hogan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionThere is an unmet clinical need for the development of novel treatment strategies to improve the outcome of children with frequent relapsing or steroid-dependent nephrotic syndrome. Obinutuzumab (OBI) is a second-generation anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody that has demonstrated its superiority to rituximab (RTX) in vitro and in vivo. Our assumption is that a single infusion of low-dose OBI will induce longer B-cell depletion, longer sustained remission and reduce the frequency of relapses and the use of oral immunosuppressors compared with a single infusion of RTX.Methods and analysisWe conduct a double-blind, multicentre, randomised, parallel group in a 1:1 ratio controlled trial. In the experimental group, patients receive 1 infusion of OBI (300 mg/1.73 m2) and in the control group, the patients receive 1 infusion of RTX (375 mg/m2). The primary outcome of the study is the occurrence of the first relapse within 12 months following the initiation of treatment and secondary outcomes include the time to first relapse, the total number of relapses during the 24-month follow-up period, and any adverse events such as infusion-related complications, infectious complications, hypogammaglobulinaemia and neutropenia.Ethics and disseminationThe study has been approved by the ethics committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes) of Sud Méditerrannée 2 and authorised by the French drug regulatory agency (Agence Nationale de Sécurité du Médicament et des Produits de Santé). Results of the primary study and the secondary aims will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publications.Trial registration numberNCT05786768.Eudract N°2022-003336-59.

Introduction

Idiopathic nephrotic syndrome (INS) is the most frequent acquired glomerulopathy in children, with an estimated incidence between 1.5 and 3.4/100 000 inhabitants in western countries1 2 and a prevalence of 1/6250 because of the extensive course of the disease.

The initial treatment relies on steroids, which enables remission of proteinuria in 90% of children defining Steroid Sensitive Nephrotic Syndrome (SSNS). Those patients have an excellent renal prognosis; however, 80% relapse after the first flare, and 50%–60% of SSNS become steroid-dependent or frequent–relapsers (SD/FRNS). These patients are at high risk of morbidity due to both the complications of relapses (infection, thrombosis) and to the side effects of steroids and immunosuppressive treatments, and due to the long duration of the disease over years, with some patients still relapsing into adulthood.

The pathophysiology of INS is still incompletely understood. In 1974, Shaloub brought evidence for an immune origin of the disease.3 Since then, standard immunosuppressive drugs such as calcineurin inhibitors or mycophenolate have demonstrated the ability to maintain remission while on treatment. Unfortunately, their effect is only suspensive with 75% of relapse after cyclosporine withdrawal4 and over 90% of relapse after mycophenolate withdrawal.5 Cyclophosphamide demonstrated a long-lasting effect in children with Steroid-Dependent Nephrotic Syndrome (SDNS) with a sustained remission rate of 42% at 2 years,6 but its use is limited by its side effects. Currently, there is no consensus on the optimal treatment of FR/SDNS. The recent IPNA clinical practice guidelines list potential oral steroid-paring agents without giving indication which to prefer, while B-cell depletion with rituximab (RTX) is suggested as second-line.7 Over the past decade, the use of RTX has increased and studies evaluating its benefit as a first-line steroid-sparing agent have been designed.8 9 Strategies aiming to provide long-term remission while minimising treatment exposure and side effects still need to be investigated, and the optimal B-cell depletion regimen remains to be determined.

In 2004, RTX, a chimeric anti-CD20 antibody depleting peripheral B-cells, was reported to induce sustained remission of the nephrotic syndrome in a patient treated for idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura.10 Since then, both retrospective studies and randomised trials have demonstrated that RTX is able to induce long-term remission, even after withdrawal of oral drugs and after B-cell recovery in patients with SD/FRNS.11 This finding deeply modified our view on the pathophysiology of the disease with the involvement of B-cells and not only T-cells as previously described. This implication of B-cells is further supported by the descriptions of B-cell dysregulation during the active phase of the disease, the strong correlation between B-cell recovery and INS relapse in patients relapsing after RTX therapy with a recent report underlying the role of memory B-cells (CD19+/CD27+).12 13 Additionally, the efficacy of immunoadsorption of immunoglobulins in severe forms of INS14 15 and the recent report on the implications of anti-nephrin antibodies in childhood NS16 17 support the humoral autoimmune aetiology of INS and the rationale for B-cell targeted treatments.18

The growing use of B-cell depletion with RTX has highlighted some limitations. Most patients relapse within 2 years following RTX and require either redosing or restart of oral immunosuppressors. Redosing of RTX exposes patients to the risk of immune side effects such as hypogammaglobulinaemia11 and to the development of anti-RTX antibodies, which may be associated with failure and intolerance to RTX.19 Ofatumumab is a fully humanised antibody with a higher affinity to CD20, but similarly to RTX, it relocalises CD20 into lipid rafts. It has been recently compared with RTX within a randomised trial showing no superiority of Ofatumumab 1500 mg/1.73 m2 compared with RTX 375 mg/m2 in SDNS.20

Obinutuzumab (OBI) (GazyvaroR, Roche) is a second generation humanised anti CD20 antibody that has been developed to overcome RTX failure in B-cell malignancies. OBI is glycoengineered to enhance its interaction with Fc-gamma receptors and to resist internalisation, resulting in more potent B-cell depletion. It has already demonstrated its superiority in vitro and in vivo over RTX in other auto-immune diseases.21 22 The greater efficacy of OBI is likely due to its powerful antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, which can result in greater induction of direct cell death. Our group reported recently on the efficacy and safety of a single infusion of OBI at 300 mg/1.73 m2 in 41 children with SDNS with either RTX resistance (lack of B-cell depletion within 1 month), short duration of depletion (<3 months), or relapse early after B-cell recovery following RTX.23 In this study, the median duration of B-cell depletion was 8.3 months, always longer compared with previous RTX.

Our hypothesis is that a single low dose of OBI is more effective than a standard dose single infusion of RTX and may prevent repeated courses of RTX and long-lasting exposure to oral immunosuppressors.

We aim to perform the first trial comparing head to head a single low-dose of OBI compared with a single standard dose of RTX and comparing the efficacy, the safety and the cost-effectiveness of these two treatments in children with SDNS or FRNS.

Methods and design

Rationale behind OBIRINS study

There is a pressing need for new treatments for childhood SDNS/FRNS that are more effective and less toxic than current options. Many existing therapeutic agents have significant side effects or are not effective in all patients. This unmet clinical need calls for the development of novel therapeutic strategies that can improve the outcome of patients with nephrotic syndrome.

OBI is a second-generation anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody which binds CD20 at a different epitope than RTX and has a different mechanism of action. Our assumption is that a single infusion of low-dose OBI will induce longer B-cell depletion, longer sustained remission and reduce the frequency of relapses and the use of oral immunosuppressors compared with a single standard dose infusion of RTX.

Study design

The trial is a multicentre, double-blinded, randomised, parallel group in a 1:1 ratio, controlled, superiority trial testing a single infusion of OBI compared with a single infusion of RTX. Randomisation will be stratified by age (≤9 and >9 years old) and prior RTX treatment. Patients will be randomly assigned to receive either OBI (300 mg/1.73 m2) or RTX (375 mg/m2) (figure 1). The evaluation of the various criteria in this study will be conducted during follow-up visits, scheduled at one and 3 month then every 3 months for the first year and every 6 months for the second year according to standard of care. Patients will be recruited from 13 tertiary paediatric nephrology centres in France (table 1). Study recruitment has started in October 2023 and is expected to be completed in October 2025. The expected study completion date is October 2027.

Outcomes

The primary outcome of the study is the occurrence of the first relapse within 12 months following the initiation of treatment. Relapse will be defined as a 3+ proteinuria on dipstick on three consecutive days and/or one laboratory urinary protein-to-creatinine ratio greater than 2 g/g (or 0.2 g/mmol).

In addition to the primary outcome, the study will also assess the following secondary outcomes: the time to first relapse, the total number of relapses during the 24-month follow-up period, and any adverse events such as infusion-related complications, infectious complications, hypogammaglobulinaemia and neutropenia. These measures will be used to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of the treatment being tested.

Screening and recruitment process

When investigators observe a recurrence of nephrotic syndrome in eligible patients, they describe this clinical trial to the relevant subjects. After information, verification of inclusion and exclusion criteria and written consent is obtained from the parents, and patient if appropriate, the patient is included in the study. Inclusion must be performed within 3 months of the last relapse.

Randomisation

After obtaining written consent from parents, the randomisation will be conducted using a centralised computer–generated, interactive web-response that is accessible from each study centre. Each participant will be assigned a randomisation number in the format « RXXXXX » when they are randomised into the study. This process ensures that the allocation of treatment is randomly determined and concealed from the investigators and participants. The randomisation will be stratified based on the participant’s age (≤9 years old and >9 years old) and prior RTX status (yes or no).

Blinding

Treatment allocation will be kept blinded from the physician, nurse, patient and patient’s parents until the end of the study. However, the local pharmacists will be aware of the treatment allocation. Both the experimental and control treatments are anti-CD20 monoclonal antibodies, which have similar short-term and long-term safety profiles, require the same premedication and administration rates, and require the same clinical and biological follow-up. After randomisation, the treatment will be shipped to the local pharmacy and prepared by the unblinded pharmacist using the same dilution method for both the experimental and control treatments. The prepared treatments will be identical in appearance without any colour and delivered to the nurse and physician in a blinded bag of 250 mL of normal saline. The analysis of the study will be conducted while keeping the allocation of groups blinded.

Procedures

After confirming that the patients do not have any clinical signs of infection and have negative proteinuria on dipstick testing +/- urinary beta hCG if appropriate, they will receive an infusion of the experimental treatment. The administration protocol is presented as online supplemental material. The visit will also include baseline blood and urinary laboratory tests and central biological collection (table 2). After being discharged from hospital, they were prescribed an oral antibiotic prophylaxis with trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole 800 mg (or 20 to 30 mg/kg of sulfamethoxazole) three times weekly according to the national recommendation. The antibiotic prophylaxis was continued until B cell reconstitution (B cell count over 50/mm3). All oral immunosuppressive drugs including steroids were tapered and stopped within 8 weeks.

Clinical assessment and labs follow-up

During the clinical trial, investigators will monitor patients at several follow-up visits and perform various tests and assessments. These will include medical history, adverse events assessment, physical examination and blood sampling at visits M1, M3, M6, M9, M12, M18, M24 and relapse (as detailed in table 2). Additionally, various biological investigations will be performed, including measurements of IgG, IgA, IgM, total blood cells, white blood cells and lymphocyte immunophenotyping, albuminaemia and urine analysis. These tests will be carried out at each visit and analysed at the local laboratory. More extensive biological investigations, including B-cell counts and pharmacokinetic and anti drug antibody (ADA) detection, will be sent to the central laboratory of Robert-Debré hospital in Paris. Proteinuria will also be evaluated on a regular basis (once a week until 12 months and once every 2 weeks until 24 months) using a urinary dipstick. Patients will be given a diary to record their urinary dipstick monitoring, oral treatments and any adverse events. This diary is commonly used in routine care and is familiar to both patients and caregivers. It has been successfully used in other trials and is an effective tool for tracking progress and monitoring for potential problems. In the case of a positive dipstick for three consecutive days, a confirmatory urine analysis will be carried out in an outpatient laboratory. These tests are part of the standard care for patients with nephrotic syndrome. An additional monthly biological investigation, including complete blood cells and CD19+ and/or CD20+ B cell counts, albuminaemia and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, will be performed at M2, M4, M5 and until B-cell reconstitution, or for as long as needed according to routine care for patients receiving B-cell depletion treatment for nephrotic syndrome. In the case of relapse, an additional visit will be scheduled for the patient (the ‘relapse visit’) to assess the situation and determine the appropriate treatment. The treatment of the relapse will be decided by the investigator according to standard care and the immunological status of the patient, and may include oral prednisone, RTX infusions or oral immunosuppressants such as MMF, tacrolimus or cyclosporine.

Prohibited concomitant medications

If patients are taking corticosteroids and oral immunosuppressive drugs at the time of inclusion in the study, these treatments will be maintained at the same dosage for 4 weeks and then gradually discontinued over the next 4 weeks. Other medications and treatments, such as calcium and vitamin D supplements, analgesics (if needed), antibiotics (for infections), trimethoprim/sulfamethoxazole (Bactrim) or pentacarinat aerosol (for antibiotic prophylaxis), and non-live vaccines, may be used during the study. Antihypertensive drugs, including RAAS blockers, may also be used as long as the patients are in complete remission at the time of inclusion with no residual proteinuria. For women of childbearing age, the use of at least one reliable form of contraception before, during and after stopping treatment is recommended, and contraception should be continued for a total of 18 months for patients treated in the study. The primary endpoint of the study is the occurrence of the first relapse. In the case of relapse, the treatment plan will be decided by the treating physician. All the drugs used during the time of the study will be recorded.

Adverse events

Adverse events are any negative, unfavourable or unintended outcomes or events that affect patients participating in a study. In cases where hospitalisation is prolonged, death occurs, or there are significant clinical consequences, these events are considered serious adverse events (SAEs). The study sponsor (Assistance Publique – Hôpitaux de Paris, APHP) and the Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) must be informed of SAEs as soon as possible. During the course of the treatment protocol, all deaths and life-threatening SAEs, as well as any unexpected SAEs, must be reported to the sponsor using the SAE web form within 48 hours of the initial observation of the event. The DSMB closely monitors the safety of the study, receiving non-blinded data for review. In addition to monitoring SAEs, other adverse events such as infections requiring hospitalisation, infections not requiring hospitalisation, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy, neutropenia, acute kidney injury, allergic reactions and infusion tolerance are also monitored during the follow-up period.

Data management

In the OBIRINS trial, the data are collected at each study visit and entered into the electronic case report form (eCRF) database by the site investigators, assisted by trained local research staff. A data management plan is in place to guide the data management and analysis process, ensuring that all data is collected and handled consistently. The data management plan is followed throughout the course of the study to ensure the integrity and accuracy of the data.

Statistical methods

Sample size

A case study of 41 patients receiving a single OBI infusion at a dose of 300 mg/1.73 m2 showed that 90% of them did not relapse after 1 year.23 In comparison, data from published randomised trials on paediatric patients with steroid-dependent nephrotic syndrome receiving RTX showed relapse-free survival rates at 12 months ranging from 40% to 80%.24 A large multicentre retrospective study reported a relapse-free survival rate at 12 months of 32% after a single infusion of RTX.11 Results from a preliminary analysis of an ongoing trial of RTX in France25 showed a relapse-free survival rate at 12 months of 60% (unpublished data). These data are somewhat inconsistent, which may be due to differences in the populations studied, including ethnic background and healthcare context. For the purpose of calculations in this study, assumptions were based on the French multicentre trial cited above,25 which has more recent data and a population similar to the one being studied. It was assumed that 90% of patients in the OBI group and 60% in the RTX group would not have experienced a relapse at 12 months. In order to achieve a power of 90% and significance level of 0.05 using a two-sided test in this study, it will be necessary to enrol a total of 88 patients, with 42 patients in each treatment group. This calculation was made using the epiR package version 0.9 v-96 and takes into account a 5% drop-out rate. All statistical tests conducted in this study will be two-sided and will use a significance level of 5%.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis for this study will be performed on the collected data after the dataset has been locked. The analysis will be conducted blind to the allocation of groups. Subgroup analysis will be carried out on the primary outcome according to the stratification criteria age and previous treatment with RTX. For each group and at each assessment date, qualitative variables will be described as the number and percentage, and quantitative variables will be described as the number, mean and SD. Quantitative variables with a skewed distribution will be presented as the median and interquartile range (the range between the 25th and 75th percentiles). The Kaplan-Meier method will be used to describe the probability of relapse during the 12-month and 24-month follow-up periods, and the comparison between the two groups will be performed using a log-rank test. No competing risks are expected, and participants will be censored at the end of the study if they are event-free or if they leave the study. Comparison of outcome variables between the two groups will be performed using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables and Student’s t-test or Wilcoxon test for continuous variables, as appropriate. A univariate model will be created using demographic, clinical and biological variables as independent variables and relapse at 24 months (yes/no) as the dependent variable (outcome). A multivariate logistic regression analysis will then be performed using backward elimination to identify factors associated with sustained remission at 24 months. The multivariate logistic regression model will be constructed using the parameters of the univariate analysis that show at least a trend towards significance (p<0.2). Backward elimination will start with all candidate risk factors in the model and will use a series of statistical tests to remove or keep them in the model based on a nominal p value of <0.05.

Monitoring

The study coordinator from the coordinating centre will be responsible for monitoring quality and regulatory compliance at each centre. This involves ensuring that the study is being conducted in accordance with relevant regulations and guidelines, and that data being collected is accurate and complete. The frequency of these monitoring activities may vary depending on the inclusion rates, outstanding issues from previous audits and any other pertinent factors. It is important to maintain high standards of quality and compliance in order to ensure the validity and reliability of the study results.

Confidentiality and data handling

The data collected for this study will be handled in accordance with French law. It will be stored in a secure electronic system using web-based eCRFs, which can be accessed by investigators and clinical research technicians using individual passwords. To protect participant confidentiality, identifying information will be replaced with a sequence of characters. The sponsor will be the owner of the data, which will be stored for 15 years. These measures are in place to ensure the security and confidentiality of the data, as well as to comply with relevant regulations and guidelines.

Patient and public involvement

Patients were not involved in the planning and production of this study.

Ethics and dissemination

The study was approved by the ethics committee (Comité de Protection des Personnes) of Sud-Méditerranée II, on the 2 December 2022 and authorised by the French drug regulatory agency (Agence Nationale de Sécurité du Médicament et des Produits de Santé) on 13 January 2023 (EUDRACT N°: 2022-003336-59). A manuscript with the results of the primary study and the secondary aims will be published in a peer-reviewed journal.

Discussion

Childhood-onset steroid-dependent or frequently relapsing INS is a chronic disease with a long-lasting course that significantly impacts patient’s quality of life. Currently, there are no clear guidelines for choosing the best treatment for these patients, and all current treatment strategies are associated with a high relapse rate. Therefore, strategies that aim to provide long-term remission while minimising treatment side effects in this population need to be investigated.

OBI is currently under investigation in many immune-mediated diseases. OBI has met its primary endpoint in adult lupus nephritis (NCT02550652) and a few randomised controlled trials (RCTs) are currently ongoing for paediatric lupus nephritis (NCT05039619) and adult membranous nephropathy.26 In paediatric nephrotic syndrome, another ongoing trial (INSHORE) is comparing the rate of sustained remission at 1 year after 4 infusions of OBI at 1 g/1.73 m2 at D0-D15 and S26-S28 to an oral treatment with mycophenolate mofetil (NCT05627557).

Our group has previously reported on the efficacy and safety of a single infusion of OBI at 1 g/1.73 m2 in 14 patients with complicated SDNS relapsing, in combination with a global anti B-cell strategy with daratumumab.27 More recently, we reported on a single 300 mg/1.73 m2 low-dose infusion in 41 additional patients with FR/SDN relapsing despite RTX. This low dose enabled a longer duration of B-cell depletion and relapse-free survival compared with prior RTX, with a comparable safety profile.23 This trial will provide the first direct comparison of the two main anti-CD20 antibodies: RTX and OBI in childhood nephrotic syndrome. It will also evaluate a new ‘low-dose regimen’ involving a single infusion of 300 mg/1.73 m2. This low-dose regimen may be associated with fewer side effects, notably hypogammaglobulinaemia, which is a major concern in the paediatric population.

This trial has several strengths, including its multicentre and double-blinded design, the intravenous administration of the drugs that alleviates concerns about compliance and the addition of a secondary long-term outcome at 2 years compared with previous trials. Moreover, the longitudinal biobanking planned within the trial may help, in the light of recent advances in pathophysiology and anti-nephrin autoantibodies detection, to improve the monitoring of disease activity and response to B-cell depletion.

Additionally, this trial allows for the inclusion of patients naïve to any other steroid-sparing agent. Current guidelines recommend the use of RTX as a second-line treatment for children with FRNS insufficiently controlled under steroids or with complicated relapses or in children with SDNS, and who are uncontrolled after a course of treatment with at least one other steroid-sparing agent at adequate dose.7 However, both efficacy and safety data with RTX now suggest the possibility of earlier use of B-cell depletion.28 The OBIRINS study should help clarify the optimal timing of B-cell depletion in children with FR/SDNS and help determine the relative and optimal positioning of OBI and RTX. Finally, the trial also provides a cost-effectiveness analysis to compare the economic burden of the two regimens.

In summary, B-cell depletion is the most targeted treatment in INS and RTX is becoming a cornerstone in the treatment of SDNS. This study addresses a question that is relevant to children with FR/SDNS and their families. While an industry-sponsored RCT is comparing a ‘standard regimen’ of obinituzumab to oral MMF and may provide efficacy and safety data supporting the approval of OBI in this population, the OBIRINS investigator-initiated trial will contribute to clarifying its future role in clinical practice, including the optimal dose and its relative positioning compared with RTX.

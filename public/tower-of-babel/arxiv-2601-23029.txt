Abstract
. Answering a question of Carrión et al in their recent landmark paper on C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra classification, we prove a general uniqueness theorem for K ​ K KK -theory. Given arbitrary separable C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebras A A and B B and a Cuntz pair consisting of two absorbing representations φ , ψ : A → ℳ ⁡ ( B ⊗ 𝒦 ) \varphi,\psi:A\to\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K}) , the induced element of K ​ K ​ ( A , B ) KK(A,B) vanishes if and only if φ \varphi and ψ \psi are strongly asymptotically unitarily equivalent. This improves upon the Lin–Dadarlat–Eilers stable uniqueness theorem. The conclusion is deduced by first showing the K 1 K_{1} -injectivity of an auxiliary C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -algebra associated to the C ∗ \mathrm{C}^{*} -pair ( A , B ) (A,B) , which is sometimes called the Paschke dual algebra in the literature. Most of the article is concerned with the treatment of an umbrella theorem, which yields such a uniqueness theorem for other variants of K ​ K KK -theory. This encompasses nuclear K ​ K KK -theory, ideal-related K ​ K KK -theory, equivariant K ​ K KK -theory, or any combinations thereof.

Introduction

Kasparov theory, also known as
KK
-theory, is a very powerful invariant for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
It is a bivariant version of topological
K
-theory, which includes ordinary
K
-theory and
K
-homology, alongside with Brown–Douglas–Fillmore theory
[
5
]
, as special cases.
Invented by Kasparov
[
30
,
31
]
, it can be viewed as either a bifunctor from (separable) C*-algebras to abelian groups, or as a way to equip the class of (separable)
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras with a more flexible notion of homomorphisms.
In fact, given a second-countable locally compact group
G
, Kasparov’s construction also yields such a bifunctor on the category of all (separable)
G
-
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, which provided impactful applications on classical problems such as the Novikov conjecture.
The original approach by Kasparov, commonly referred to as the Kasparov picture, is still the
most common way of treating this subject, but there are other equivalent appproaches that are geared towards different types of applications.

In this article we are mainly interested in the Cuntz picture
[
8
]
and the Cuntz–Thomsen picture
[
42
]
for (equivariant)
KK
-groups.
In Cuntz’ framework, elements in Kasparov’s group
KK(A,B)
for separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
can be described as homotopy classes of so-called
Cuntz pairs
, i.e., pairs of
*
-homomorphisms
\varphi,\psi:A\to\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
such that
\varphi(a)-\psi(a)\in B\otimes\mathcal{K}
for all
a\in A
.
For equivariant
KK
-theory, one considers pairs of
cocycle representations
satisfying a similar condition; see also
[
41
, Section 6]
.
A groundbreaking theorem concerning the analytical properties of
KK
-theory is the so-called
stable uniqueness theorem
proved independently by Lin
[
34
]
and Dadarlat–Eilers
[
12
,
13
]
.
It states that if a Cuntz pair
(\varphi,\psi)
represents the zero element in
KK(A,B)
, then
\varphi
and
\psi
are homotopic via conjugating one of the maps with a unitary path in
\mathbf{1}+B\otimes\mathcal{K}
after stabilizing both
\varphi
and
\psi
with a so-called
absorbing
representation.
Note that an elegant treatment of the stable uniqueness theorem was recently included in
[
10
]
.
Over the years, this theorem has become one of the central pieces of methodology in the structure and classification theory of nuclear
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras; see for example
[
46
,
26
,
25
,
16
,
14
,
22
,
38
,
23
,
18
,
24
,
6

.
More recently, I have generalized the stable uniqueness theorem to the setting of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamics together with Gabe in
[
21
]
, which was one of the key conceptual ingredients behind our dynamical Kirchberg–Phillips theorem
[
20
]
.
Further interesting applications of this theorem may be expected to come in the realm of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamics and their classification.
Its counterpart for actions of unitary tensor categories followed soon afterwards
[
33
]
and may become instrumental to classify unitary tensor category actions on Kirchberg algebras.

When one considers the stable uniqueness theorem (either in its
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic or
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamical form), the role of the absorbing representation is that of a necessary largeness criterion for the representations in question.
The notion of an absorbing representation — recalled after

The stable uniqueness theorem allows one to obtain a strong form of equivalence for the pair of representations, but in general only after first stabilizing with an auxiliary representation.
The process to “destabilize”, so to speak, may require a substantial amount of extra work involving the specific assumptions related to the application.

However, it is a natural question to what extent it is really necessary to stabilize with an auxiliary representation when one compares a pair of representations that are absorbing to begin with.

Question A

Suppose that

\mathrm{C}^{*}

Let

be two absorbing representations forming a Cuntz pair.
If

\varphi
and

are strongly asymptotically unitarily equivalent?

The discussions in the aforementioned references point towards the main technical obstacle being the

\mathrm{C}^{*}

More recently, I demonstrated with Farah in

The first and main goal of this article is to answer

-injectivity has a positive solution for arbitrary pairs of separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
We mention briefly that as per the discussion around
[

Introduction

While this kind of

Introduction

This is the reason why
KL
-theory plays no explicit role in the rest of this article.
To describe the approach to answer

\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})

The main result of the second section is the following consequence of

Theorem B
.

Let
A
and
B
be separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
If
\varphi:A\to\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
is (unitally) absorbing, then
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
and all its quotients are
K_{1}
-injective.

Combined with the detailed discussions appearing after
[
6
, Question 5.17]
and
[

, Problem LXII]
, this implies a positive answer to
Introduction
.
The added layers of generality within

include other recent results of this type like
[

Before we move on to the remaining content of the article, I shall say a few words about the approach to prove
Introduction
.
The core argument behind the proof draws heavy inspiration from the proof of Cuntz–Higson’s theorem
[
11
]
that the unitary group of every stable multiplier algebra
\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
is connected in the norm topology.
If one analyzes said proof carefully, one can interpret it as a two-step approach:
The first (and relatively easy) step is to observe that the
K
-groups of
\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
vanish, which is a consequence of the fact that it possesses an
infinite repeat
endomorphism, which automatically trivializes the identity map in
K
-theory.
The second (and more difficult) step is to show that
\mathcal{M}(B\otimes\mathcal{K})
is
K_{1}
-injective.
Since it is properly infinite as a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra, there are known criteria to verify
K_{1}
-injectivity (these are nicely summarized in
[
2
]
), one of which was the original criterion verified by Cuntz–Higson.
In essence, the proof of
Introduction
(say for the
K_{1}
-injectivity of the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
itself) can be seen to mimic Cuntz–Higson’s argument, with the added point that we need to keep track of the relative commutant condition appearing in the definition of
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
.
Fortunately, this works in remarkable generality as long as
\varphi
is sufficiently similar to an infinite repeat representation, which is automatically the case when
\varphi
is absorbing.
With this requisite in hand, the main difference between Cuntz–Higson’s original argument and the one appearing here is that instead of utilizing arbitrary increasing approximate units in
B\otimes\mathcal{K}
, we utilize approximate units that are quasicentral relative to
\varphi(A)
.
Since the proof involves no further assumptions, the conclusion about
K_{1}
-injectivity can be drawn in a much more flexible situation (described in
section 2
), which is applicable to other auxiliary
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras that play an analogous role for other types of
KK
-theory such as nuclear, ideal-related, or equivariant
KK
-theory.
The argument does however very much depend on the fact that the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra in question comes from a stable multiplier algebra.
Hence I would like to mention, for those readers wondering, that the proof behind
Introduction
likely sheds no light on the general
K_{1}
-injectivity problem for properly infinite
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras; see
[
2
, Question 2.9]
and
[
39
, Problem LXI]
.

In the rest of the article, I aim to treat the framework concerning the
KK
-uniqueness theorem in the most general setting possible for
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras or
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamics.
Having in mind other known variants of the stable uniqueness theorem and their applications, it can be just as useful to look at
KK
-groups with extra structure, for instance Skandalis’ nuclear
KK
-theory
[
40
]
or Kirchberg’s ideal-related
KK
-theory
[
32
]
; see
[
18
, Section 13]
for a unified treatment.
It stands to reason that in the long term, such
KK
-groups with enriched structure will also play a similar role in the structure and classification of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamics.
The third section introduces an umbrella framework of sorts, which to my knowledge unifies all known variants of (equivariant)
KK
-theory that have received significant attention in the literature.
Given two separable
G
-
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras
(A,\alpha)
and
(B,\beta)
with
\beta
strongly stable, the needed input data consists of some set
\mathfrak{C}
of cocycle representations
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
containing all elements of the form
(0,\mathbbmss{x})
, and which is downward closed under
weak containment
(
subsection 1.1
).
A set
\mathfrak{C}
satisfying these abstract properties is referred to as an
eligible set
.
The modified
KK
-group
KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
is then built, in analogy to
[
42
]
, from Cuntz pairs of cocycle representations in
\mathfrak{C}
, with a homotopy relation that requires the continuous paths to consist of cocycle representations in
\mathfrak{C}
(called
\mathfrak{C}
-homotopy).
As is discussed further in
section 3
, this general construction has as special cases nuclear and ideal-related
KK
-theory.
In the third section it is shown that one of Kasparov’s fundamental observations extends to this variant of
KK
-theory, namely that
\mathfrak{C}
-homotopy is equivalent to operator homotopy after stabilizing with a representation in
\mathfrak{C}
.
This generalizes the results in
[
21
, Section 5]
and is based on just slightly adjusting the approach therein to leverage the properties of an eligible set.

As a consequence of results in
[
21
]
, as long as the involved
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras are separable, every eligible set has some absorbing element, i.e., a representation
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\in\mathfrak{C}
that absorbs every representation in
\mathfrak{C}
.
Just as before, it can be shown that every element of
KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
has a representative Cuntz pair consisting of two absorbing elements in
\mathfrak{C}
.
In the fourth section, the results obtained so far are combined with a few further analytic arguments to prove the appropriate
KK
-uniqueness theorem for these generalized
KK
-groups.
The following is the separable version of
section 4
.

Theorem C
.

Let
G
be a second-countable locally compact group.
Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A
and
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
be two actions on separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras, and suppose that
\beta
strongly stable.
Let
\mathfrak{C}
be an eligible set of cocycle representations
(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
.
Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
be two absorbing elements of
\mathfrak{C}
that form an anchored
(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
-Cuntz pair.
Then
[(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}),(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})]=0
in
KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
if and only if
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
are strongly asymptotically unitarily equivalent.

The proof of this theorem is very much entwined with a Paschke-type duality, which is more rigorously explored in the fifth section.
A variant of the known Paschke duality
[
44
, Theorem 3.2]
asserts that if
\varphi:A\to\mathcal{M}(B)
is an absorbing representation, then
KK(A,B)
is naturally isomorphic to
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{\varphi})
.
The auxiliary
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
, also mentioned above, is hence sometimes referred to as the “Paschke dual algebra” of the pair
(A,B)
.
A dynamical version of this result was proved in
[
45
, Theorem 6.2]
in the language of genuinely equivariant representations under a stronger stability assumption on
\beta
.
Thomsen’s work inspires the approach given here and served as my original motivation to develop his ideas in the framework of cocycle representations.
We explore a generalization of this Paschke duality phenomenon, which comes with an extra layer of subtlety in how to encode the dynamics.
In the case of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-dynamics, if one is given a suitable cocycle representation
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
, then there appear to be two distinct but equally natural generalizations of the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebraic object
\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
, which we denote by
\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
and
\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
, respectively.
These are in general non-isomorphic, and indeed we can detect the conceptual difference between them by describing their
K
-theory.
As the main result of the fifth section, it is shown as part of
section 5
that if
\mathfrak{C}
is an eligible set with an absorbing element
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\in\mathfrak{C}
, then
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})})
is naturally isomorphic to
KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
.
The group
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})})
, on the other hand, turns out to be isomorphic to the direct sum
KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)\oplus H_{\beta}
, where
H_{\beta}
is the group of homotopy classes of cocycle pairs for
\beta
, which contains no information related to the action
\alpha
but is often nontrivial.

As an outlook for future applications, Hua–White’s recent article
[
28
]
serves as an excellent template.
Therein, the authors prove a special case of the
KK
-uniqueness theorem, tailored to their intended application to classify certain nuclear embeddings of exact
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras into ultrapowers of matrix algebras.
Their setup did not match the assumptions required for the classification machinery in
[
6
]
, but their work introduces various novel ideas that demonstrate how the scope of such classification results can be extended if one has access to the general
KK
-uniqueness theorem.
I thus hope that the results of this article will be used in future works to obtain a more general classification of nuclear embeddings of exact
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras into ultraproducts of
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras that need not be Jiang–Su stable.

Preliminaries

Notation 1.1
.

Throughout,
G
will denote a second-countable, locally compact group unless specified otherwise.
Normal capital letters like
A,B,C
denote
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras that are frequently assumed to be separable.
The multiplier algebra of
A
is denoted as
\mathcal{M}(A)
.
We write
\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{1}+A)
for the set of all unitaries in the proper unitization
A^{\dagger}
whose scalar part is
\mathbf{1}
, which can be canonically identified with the unitary group of
A
if it was already unital.
If we are given a (closed two-sided) ideal
J\triangleleft A
and two elements
a,b\in A
, then the expression
a\equiv_{J}b
is short-hand for
a-b\in J
.
The symbol
\mathcal{K}
denotes the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and we write
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
when a specific description of
\mathcal{H}
is relevant in a given context.
A
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
is called
stable
when
A\cong A\otimes\mathcal{K}
.
Greek letters such as
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are used for point-norm continuous maps
G\to\operatorname{Aut}(A)
, most often group actions, which uniquely induces maps into
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{M}(A))
as well.
Depending on the situation, we may denote
\operatorname{id}_{A}
either for the identity map on
A
or the trivial
G
-action on
A
.
We denote by
A^{\alpha}
or
\mathcal{M}(A)^{\alpha}
the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra of fixed points (in
A
or
\mathcal{M}(A)
) with respect to an action
\alpha
.
Normal alphabetical letters such as
u,v,U,V
are often used for unitary elements in some
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
.
If either
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}(A))
or
u\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{1}+A)
, we denote by
\operatorname{Ad}(u)
the induced inner automorphism of
A
given by
a\mapsto uau^{*}
.
Double-struck letters such as
\mathbbmss{u},\mathbbmss{v},\mathbb{U},\mathbb{V}
are used for strictly continuous maps
G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}(A))
, most often
cocycles
with respect to an action
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A
, i.e., one has the cocycle identity
\mathbbmss{u}_{gh}=\mathbbmss{u}_{g}\alpha_{g}(\mathbbmss{u}_{h})
for all
g,h\in G
.
Under this assumption, one obtains a new (cocycle perturbed) action
\alpha^{\mathbbmss{u}}:G\curvearrowright A
via
\alpha^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}=\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbbmss{u}_{g})\circ\alpha_{g}
.

Although we will introduce equivariant
KK
-theory in this section via the Cuntz–Thomsen picture
[
8
,
9
,
27
,
42
]
, we will implicitly assume that the reader has some existing passing familiarity with it; see for example
[
1
]
.
We will assume that the reader is at least fluent in the language of
K
-theory for

We will make use of the following fundamental result due to Kasparov concerning quasicentral approximate units coming from ideals invariant under a group action.

Lemma 1.2

(see
[
31
, Lemma 1.4]
and its proof)
.

Let
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
be an action on a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and let
e_{n}\in B
be any countable increasing approximate unit.
Then for any separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
D\subseteq\mathcal{M}(B)
, there exists a countable increasing approximate unit of positive contractions
h_{n}\in B
belonging to the convex hull of
\{e_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}

satisfying

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|[h_{n},d]\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{g\in K}\|h_{n}-\beta_{g}(h_{n})\|=0

for all
d\in D
and compact sets
K\subseteq G
.
If one has
e_{n+1}e_{n}=e_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, then one can arrange
h_{n+1}h_{n}=h_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.

1.1.
Some cocycle representation theory

Definition 1.3

(see
[
41
, Section 1]
)
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A
and
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
be two actions on
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebras.
A
cocycle representation

(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
consists of a
*
-homomorphism
\varphi:A\to\mathcal{M}(B)
and a strictly continuous
\beta
-cocycle
\mathbbmss{u}:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}(B))
satisfying
\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbbmss{u}_{g})\circ\beta_{g}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\alpha_{g}
for all
g\in G
.
For any unitary
W\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}(B))
, we can obtain a new cocycle representation defined by conjugation as

\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):=(\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ\varphi,\{W\mathbbmss{u}_{g}\beta_{g}(W)^{*}\}_{g\in G}).

If a given cocycle representation can be obtained from another via this procedure, then they are called
unitarily equivalent
.
If there exists a cocycle representation
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
such that
\varphi
is an isomorphism from
A
to
B
, then
\alpha
and
\beta
are called
cocycle conjugate
.

Notation 1.4
.

We say that an action
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
is
strongly stable
if
(B,\beta)
is conjugate (i.e., equivariantly isomorphic) to
(B\otimes\mathcal{K},\beta\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{K}})
.

An action
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
is strongly stable if and only if there is a sequence of isometries
r_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(B)^{\beta}
such that
\mathbf{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}r_{n}^{*}
holds in the strict topology; see
[
21
, Remark 2.4]
.
Furthermore, it was observed in
[
20
, Proposition 1.4]
that every
G
-action on a stable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra is cocycle conjugate to a strongly stable action.

Definition 1.5

(cf.
[
42
, Lemma 3.4]
)
.

Let
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
be an action on a
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Suppose that there exists a unital inclusion
\mathcal{O}_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{M}(B)^{\beta}

For two isometries

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
be a cocycle representation.
Then
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
is the infinite repeat of some cocycle representation if and only if there exists a sequence of isometries
r_{n}\in(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime}\cap\{\mathbbmss{u}_{g}\}_{g\in G}^{\prime})^{\beta}
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}r_{n}^{*}=\mathbf{1}
in the strict topology.
If this is the case, then the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
has vanishing
K
-theory.

Proof.

The “if” part is trivial: If
r_{n}
is such a sequence of isometries, then forming the infinite repeat of
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
via this chosen sequence yields the (on the nose) equality
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})=(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})^{\infty}
.

For the “only if” part, suppose that
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
is the infinite repeat of some cocycle representation
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
.
Let
t_{n}\in\mathcal{M}(B)^{\beta}
be a sequence of isometries with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}t_{n}^{*}=\mathbf{1}
and such that

\varphi(a)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}\psi(a)t_{n}^{*},\quad a\in A,

and

\mathbbmss{u}_{g}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{n}\mathbbmss{v}_{g}t_{n}^{*},\quad g\in G.

Choose some sequence of pairwise disjoint infinite subsets
X_{j}\subset\mathbb{N}
with
\mathbb{N}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}
.
For each
j\geq 1
we choose a bijection
\kappa_{j}:\mathbb{N}\to X_{j}
and consider the strict limit
r_{j}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}t_{\kappa_{j}(n)}t_{n}^{*}
.
We see all these isometries commute with the range of both
\varphi
and
\mathbbmss{u}
pointwise and thus belong to the
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra
(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime}\cap\{\mathbbmss{u}_{g}\}_{g\in G}^{\prime})^{\beta}
.
Furthermore the sequence satisfies
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}r_{n}^{*}=\mathbf{1}
in the strict topology.

For the last part of the statement, we keep a sequence
r_{n}
chosen as in the statement.
Consider the strict limit
r_{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n+1}r_{n}^{*}
, which is an isometry in
(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
and fits into the equation
r_{1}r_{1}^{*}+r_{\infty}r_{\infty}^{*}=\mathbf{1}
.
For any given element
x\in(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
, we can form its infinite repeat
x^{\infty}
within
(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
via the sequence
(r_{n})_{n}
and observe the equation

x^{\infty}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}xr_{n}^{*}=r_{1}xr_{1}^{*}+r_{\infty}\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}xr_{n}^{*}\Big)r_{\infty}^{*}=x\oplus_{r_{1},r_{\infty}}x^{\infty}.

Due to the additive properties of
K
-theory under Cuntz addition, this implies that the identity map of
(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
induces the zero morphism on its
K
-groups.
This means that the
K
-groups vanish.
∎

Lemma 2.5

Let

-algebra in Cuntz standard form.

This finishes the argument.

Lemma 2.6

Let

-injective.

(ii)

The statement below is the consequence of a standard functional calculus argument.

Proposition 2.7
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a constant

with the following property.

be any
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and

a positive contraction.

\bullet

, then

\bullet

The following is the main technical result of this section.
A special case of it, stated after it, is the main ingredient to answer

affirmatively, and its general form enables many of the results in the rest of the article.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A
be an action on a separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
a strongly stable action on a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
be a cocycle representation that contains itself at infinity.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra for which one of the following holds:

(1)

There exists an ideal
\mathcal{J}\triangleleft\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}^{w}
such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}^{w}/\mathcal{J}
.

(2)

There exists an ideal
\mathcal{J}\triangleleft\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
such that
\mathcal{E}\cong\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}/\mathcal{J}
.

Then
\mathcal{E}
is
K_{1}
-injective.

Proof.

By
section 2
, we may assume without loss of generality that
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
is an infinite repeat.
By
subsection 1.1
, we may assume that there exists a sequence of isometries
r_{n}\in(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap\varphi(A)^{\prime})^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}
with
\mathbf{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}r_{n}^{*}
.
Depending on which case is proved, we write either
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
or
\mathscr{D}=\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}^{w}
, so that
\mathcal{E}=\mathscr{D}/\mathcal{J}
in both cases.
We shall assume that
\mathcal{E}
is non-zero, otherwise there is nothing to prove.

We consider
p=P+\mathcal{J}
for
P=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{2n}r_{2n}^{*}\in\mathscr{D}
, which defines a splitting projection in
\mathcal{E}
with vanishing
K_{0}
-class (as
P
is already such an element in
\mathscr{D}
).
Let
q\in\mathcal{E}
be another splitting projection with vanishing
K_{0}
-class.
By
section 2
, the claim amounts to showing that
p
and
q
are homotopic.
By
section 2
,
q
is uniquely determined modulo unitary equivalence, thus there exists a unitary
w\in\mathcal{E}
such that
w(\mathbf{1}-p)w^{*}=q
.
Let
W\in\mathscr{D}
be a contraction with
w=W+\mathcal{J}
.
Set
Q=W(\mathbf{1}-P)W^{*}=W\big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{2n-1}r_{2n-1}^{*}\big)W^{*}
, which then satisfies
q=Q+\mathcal{J}
.

We apply
section 1
and choose an increasing approximate unit
(e_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
B
with
e_{n+1}e_{n}=e_{n}
that is
\varphi(A)
-quasicentral and approximately
\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}
-invariant.
Let
K_{n}\subseteq G
be an increasing sequence of compact sets with union
G
and
F_{n}\subseteq A
an increasing sequence of finite subsets of contractions whose union is dense in the unit ball of
A
.
For every
n\geq 1
, we apply
section 2
to find a universal constant
\delta_{n}>0
that satisfies the conclusion therein for
2^{-(n+2)}
in place of
\varepsilon
.
We assume
\delta_{n}\leq 2^{-n}
for each
n\geq 1
and that the sequence
(\delta_{n})_{n}
is decreasing.
We begin by choosing an even number
m_{1}\geq 1
with

\max_{g\in K_{1}}\|e_{n}-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(e_{n})\|+\max_{a\in F_{1}}\|[e_{n},\varphi(a)]\|<\delta_{1}/2\ ,\quad n\geq m_{1}.

Set
f_{1}=r_{m_{1}}e_{m_{1}}r_{m_{1}}^{*}
.
As
r_{n}r_{n}^{*}\to 0
strictly as
n\to\infty
, we may find an odd number
n_{1}>m_{1}
such that
\|e_{m_{1}}r_{m_{1}}^{*}Wr_{n_{1}}\|<\frac{1}{4}
and set
h_{1}=Wr_{n_{1}}e_{n_{1}}r_{n_{1}}^{*}W^{*}
.
Next, choose an even number
m_{2}>n_{1}
with

\max_{g\in K_{2}}\|e_{n}-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(e_{n})\|+\max_{a\in F_{2}}\|[e_{n},\varphi(a)]\|<\delta_{2}/2,\quad n\geq m_{2},

and

\|r_{n}^{*}Wr_{n_{1}}e_{n_{1}}\|<\frac{1}{8},\quad n\geq m_{2}.

Set
f_{2}=r_{m_{2}}e_{m_{2}}r_{m_{2}}^{*}
.
Then find an odd number
n_{2}>m_{2}
such that

\|e_{m_{1}}r_{m_{1}}^{*}Wr_{n_{2}}\|<\frac{1}{8},\quad\|e_{m_{2}}r_{m_{2}}^{*}Wr_{n_{2}}\|<\frac{1}{16},

and set
h_{2}=Wr_{n_{2}}e_{n_{2}}r_{n_{2}}^{*}W^{*}
.

By repeating these steps inductively, we end up with increasing sequences of (alternatingly even and odd) natural numbers
m_{1}<n_{1}<m_{2}<n_{2}<\dots
such that

(e2.1)

\sup_{n\geq m_{k}}\Big(\max_{g\in K_{k}}\|e_{n}-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(e_{n})\|+\max_{a\in F_{k}}\|[e_{n},\varphi(a)]\|\Big)<\delta_{k}/2,\quad k\geq 1,

and the positive contractions

f_{k}=r_{m_{k}}e_{m_{k}}r_{m_{k}}^{*},\quad h_{k}=Wr_{n_{k}}e_{n_{k}}r_{n_{k}}^{*}W^{*}

satisfy the inequality
\|f_{k}h_{j}\|<2^{-(j+k)}
for all
j,k\geq 1
.

Consider the strict limits
\mathfrak{F}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}f_{k}
and
\mathfrak{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}h_{k}
.
Clearly
\mathfrak{F}\leq P
and
\mathfrak{H}\leq Q
and the above inequality implies
\|\mathfrak{F}\cdot\mathfrak{H}\|\leq\sum_{j,k=1}^{\infty}\|f_{k}h_{j}\|<1
.

For notational convenience, set
e_{0}:=0=:e_{-1}
and
m_{0}=n_{0}=0=m_{-1}=n_{-1}
.
For
k\geq 1
, define
a_{k}=(e_{m_{k-1}}-e_{m_{k-2}})^{1/2}
and
b_{k}=(e_{n_{k-1}}-e_{n_{k-2}})^{1/2}
.
As
(e_{n})_{n}
was an approximate unit, note that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}e_{m_{\ell-1}}=\mathbf{1}=\lim_{\ell\to\infty}e_{n_{\ell-1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}^{2}.

By condition (
e2.1
), we have for all
k\geq 3
that

\max_{g\in K_{k-2}}\|(e_{m_{k-1}}-e_{m_{k-2}})-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(e_{m_{k-1}}-e_{m_{k-2}})\|\leq\delta_{k-2}

and

\max_{a\in F_{k-2}}\|[(e_{m_{k-1}}-e_{m_{k-2}}),\varphi(a)]\|\leq\delta_{k-2}.

By our choice of the constants
\delta_{k}
, it follows for all
k\geq 3
that

(e2.2)

\max_{g\in K_{k-2}}\|a_{k}-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(a_{k})\|\leq 2^{-k},\quad\max_{a\in F_{k-2}}\|[a_{k},\varphi(a)]\|\leq 2^{-k}.

Analogously, we get for all
k\geq 3
that

(e2.3)

\max_{g\in K_{k-2}}\|b_{k}-\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(b_{k})\|\leq 2^{-k},\quad\max_{a\in F_{k-2}}\|[b_{k},\varphi(a)]\|\leq 2^{-k}.

Consider the strict limit
R_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{m_{k}}a_{k}
.
It is an isometry since

R_{1}^{*}R_{1}=\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}a_{k}r_{m_{k}}^{*}r_{m_{j}}a_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}^{2}=\mathbf{1}.

We also have

\mathfrak{F}R_{1}=\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}r_{m_{j}}e_{m_{j}}r_{m_{j}}^{*}r_{m_{k}}a_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{m_{k}}\underbrace{e_{m_{k}}a_{k}}_{=a_{k}}=R_{1}.

Condition (
e2.2
) implies for all
a\in\bigcup_{n}F_{n}
that

[R_{1},\varphi(a)]=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{m_{k}}[a_{k},\varphi(a)]\in B

and for all
g\in G
that

R_{1}-\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(R_{1})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{m_{k}}\big(a_{k}-\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(a_{k})\big)\in B,

and hence
R_{1}\in\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
.

Likewise we can consider the strict limit
R_{2}^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{n_{k}}b_{k}
and
R_{2}=WR_{2}^{\prime}
.
As above, condition (
e2.3
) implies
R_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}\subseteq\mathscr{D}
and thus
R_{2}\in\mathscr{D}
.
Exactly as above it follows that
R_{2}^{\prime}
is an isometry and hence

R_{2}^{*}R_{2}-\mathbf{1}={R_{2}^{\prime}}^{*}(W^{*}W-\mathbf{1})R_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{J}.

Here we used that
W
was chosen as a lift of the unitary
w\in\mathcal{E}
.
Furthermore,

\begin{array}[]{cll}\mathfrak{H}R_{2}&=&W\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{n_{k}}e_{n_{k}}r_{n_{k}}^{*}\Big)W^{*}WR_{2}^{\prime}\\
&\equiv_{\mathcal{J}}&W\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{n_{k}}e_{n_{k}}r_{n_{k}}^{*}\Big)R_{2}^{\prime}\\
&=&W\sum_{k,j=1}^{\infty}r_{n_{j}}e_{n_{j}}r_{n_{j}}^{*}r_{n_{k}}b_{k}\\
&=&W\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}r_{n_{k}}\underbrace{e_{n_{k}}b_{k}}_{=b_{k}}\\
&=&WR_{2}^{\prime}\ =\ R_{2}.\end{array}

To summarize, if we set
s_{j}=R_{j}+\mathcal{J}
for
j=1,2
, then we have found two isometries
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{E}
with

s_{1}^{*}s_{1}^{*}\leq\mathfrak{F}+\mathcal{J}\leq P+\mathcal{J}=p,\quad s_{2}s_{2}^{*}\leq\mathfrak{H}+\mathcal{J}\leq Q+\mathcal{J}=q,

and

\|s_{1}^{*}s_{2}\|\leq\|R_{1}^{*}\mathfrak{F}\mathfrak{H}R_{2}\|\leq\|\mathfrak{F}\mathfrak{H}\|<1.

With
section 2
, we may conclude that
p
and
q
are homotopic in
\mathcal{E}
.
∎

On a generalized equivariant

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a set of cocycle representations from
(A,\alpha)
to
(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
.
We say that
\mathfrak{C}
is
downward closed
, if for every cocycle representation
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
, we have that
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\preccurlyeq\mathfrak{C}
implies
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\in\mathfrak{C}
.
We say that
\mathfrak{C}
is an
eligible set
of cocycle representations for
(\alpha,\beta)
, if it is downward closed and contains every cocycle representation of the form
(0,\mathbbmss{u})
, where
\mathbbmss{u}
is an arbitrary
\beta
-cocycle in
\mathcal{M}(B)
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that in the non-equivariant situation, i.e.
G=\{1\}
, being eligible is the same as being downward closed.
In general, if
\mathfrak{C}
is a downward closed set as above, then it follows from
[
21
, Proposition 4.7]
that
\mathfrak{C}
is
\sigma
-additive in the sense of ‘Definition 4.13’ therein, i.e., it is closed under countable sums as considered in
subsection 1.1
.
If
B
is separable, then it follows by
[
21
, Theorem 4.16]
that
\mathfrak{C}
always has an
absorbing
element, i.e., an element
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\in\mathfrak{C}
that absorbs every element of
\mathfrak{C}
in the sense of
subsection 1.1
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be a set of cocycle representations from
(A,\alpha)
to
(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
.
We define
\mathfrak{C}[0,1]
as the set of cocycle representations
(\Phi,\mathbb{U}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B[0,1]),\beta[0,1])
such that
\operatorname{ev}_{t}\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U})=:(\Phi_{t},\mathbb{U}_{t})\in\mathfrak{C}
for all
t\in[0,1]
, where
\operatorname{ev}_{t}:B[0,1]\to B
is the evaluation map at
t
.

Definition 3.4
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{C}
is an eligible set of cocycle representations from
(A,\alpha)
to
(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
.
An
(equivariant)

(\alpha,\beta)
-Cuntz pair consisting of cocycle representations
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
belonging to
\mathfrak{C}
.
We define

to be the set of all such pairs and
\mathbb{E}_{0}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
to be the set of all such pairs that are also anchored in the sense of
subsection 1.2
.

We say that two elements

are

-homotopic

, if there exists

with

for

.

5

5

from the left with the evaluation map in

Given a pair
\big((\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}),(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})\big)\in\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)

Remark 3.5
.

Exactly as one does in the Cuntz–Thomsen picture of equivariant
KK
-theory, one observes in the context of

that Cuntz addition of

-Cuntz pairs descends to a well-defined abelian group structure on

.
The proof of this fact (see for instance
[

, Lemma 2.10]
, though the argument goes back much further than that) carries over word for word from the (known) case of

being the set of all cocycle representations.
Here the fact that
\mathfrak{C}
is downward closed ensures that
\mathfrak{C}
is closed under Cuntz addition and unitary equivalence, which enables the known proof to work here.
The fact that

On the other hand, the assignment

This is analogous to the observations made in

Definition 3.6
.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{C}
is an eligible set of cocycle representations from
(A,\alpha)
to
(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
.
We define

\big(\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)/_{\sim_{h,\mathfrak{C}}}\big)/H_{\beta}

Notation 4.1

Lemma 4.2

Let

\mathrm{C}^{*}

Let
\{e_{k\ell}\}_{k,\ell=1,2}
be the generating matrix units of the

In order to prove the claim it suffices (by stability of

Theorem 4.4

g\in G
.

From these properties we can read off that the degenerate
(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
-Cuntz pair
\big((\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big)
is
\mathfrak{C}
-homotopic to
\big(\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0})\circ(\Phi\oplus\theta,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{y}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big)
.
Analogously, we find a norm-continuous path
W:[0,\infty)\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{M}(B))
such that
\operatorname{Ad}(W_{t})\circ(\Phi\oplus\psi,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{v})
converges to
(\Phi,\mathbb{U})
in the same manner.
Note that since
(\theta,\mathbbmss{y})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
agree modulo
B
, the same is true for

\begin{array}[]{cll}(\Phi,\mathbb{U})&\equiv_{B}&\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0})\circ(\Phi\oplus\theta,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{y})\\
&\equiv_{B}&\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0})\circ(\Phi\oplus\psi,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{v})\\
&\equiv_{B}&\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0}W_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}).\end{array}

This means that the unitary
V_{0}W_{0}^{*}
belongs to
\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})}
.
We compute in
\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)/_{\sim_{\mathfrak{C},h}}
that

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\big[(\psi,\mathbbmss{v}),(\theta,\mathbbmss{y})\big]&=&\big[(\Phi\oplus\psi,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{v}),(\Phi\oplus\theta,\Phi\oplus\mathbbmss{y})\big]\\
&=&\big[\operatorname{Ad}(W_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi\oplus\psi,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{v})\big]\\
&&+\big[(\Phi\oplus\psi,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{v}),(\Phi\oplus\theta,\Phi\oplus\mathbbmss{y})\big]\\
&&+\big[(\Phi\oplus\theta,\Phi\oplus\mathbbmss{y}),\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big]\\
&=&\big[\operatorname{Ad}(W_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big]\\
&=&\big[\operatorname{Ad}(V_{0}W_{0}^{*})\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big]\ =\ \Omega(V_{0}W_{0}^{*}).\end{array}

Since the pair
\big((\psi,\mathbbmss{v}),(\theta,\mathbbmss{y})\big)\in\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
was arbitrary, we see that
\Omega
is surjective.

Lastly, let us determine the kernel of
\Omega
.
By
section 3
we have that
\mathcal{U}_{0}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
is contained in the kernel.
We claim equality.
Suppose that
U\in\mathcal{U}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
is given with
\Omega(U)=0
.
This means that the
(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
-Cuntz pair
\big(\operatorname{Ad}(U)\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big)
is
\mathfrak{C}
-homotopic to the degenerate pair
\big((\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big)
.
By
section 3
, it follows that there exists an element
(\kappa,\mathbbmss{x})\in\mathfrak{C}
such that
(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})
and
\operatorname{Ad}(U\oplus\mathbf{1})\circ(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})
are operator homotopic.
Without loss of generality, by adding more to
(\kappa,\mathbbmss{x})
if necessary, we may assume that
(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})
is an infinite repeat.
There exists
W\in\mathcal{U}_{0}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})})
such that

\operatorname{Ad}(W(U\oplus\mathbf{1}))\circ(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})=(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x}).

In other words, we have that
W(U\oplus\mathbf{1})\in\big(\mathcal{M}(B)\cap(\Phi\oplus\kappa)(A)^{\prime}\big)^{\beta^{\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x}}}
.
By
subsection 1.1
, the
K_{1}
-class of
W(U\oplus\mathbf{1})
in
\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})}
is therefore trivial.
As
W
is homotopic to the unit, we have
[U\oplus\mathbf{1}]=0
in
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi\oplus\kappa,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbbmss{x})})
.
By
section 4
, we must have
[U]=0
in
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
and hence
U\in\mathcal{U}_{0}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
with
section 2
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\alpha:G\curvearrowright A
be an action on a separable
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra and
\beta:G\curvearrowright B
a strongly stable action on a
\sigma
-unital
\mathrm{C}^{*}
-algebra.
Let
\mathfrak{C}
be an eligible set of cocycle representations

.
Let

be two absorbing elements that form an anchored

-Cuntz pair.
Then
[(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}),(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})]=0
in

if and only if

and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
are strongly asymptotically unitarily equivalent.

Proof.

Since the “if” part is trivial, we only need to prove the “only if” part.
Assume

in

Since the pair was anchored, this is equivalent to
[(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}),(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})]=0
in the group

.
Since
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
were both absorbing elements of
\mathfrak{C}

(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\sim_{\mathrm{asymp}}(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})\oplus(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})\sim_{\mathrm{asymp}}(\psi,\mathbbmss{v}).

satisfying

\bullet

\varphi(a)=\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}U_{t}\psi(a)U_{t}^{*}
for all

;

\bullet

\varphi(a)-U_{t}\psi(a)U_{t}^{*}\in B
for all
a\in A
and

;

\bullet

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{g\in K}\ \|\mathbbmss{u}_{g}-U_{t}\mathbbmss{v}_{g}\beta_{g}(U_{t})^{*}\|=0
for all compact sets

\bullet

\mathbbmss{u}_{g}-U_{t}\mathbbmss{v}_{g}\beta_{g}(U_{t})^{*}\in B
for all
t\geq 1
and

.

Given that
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})
and
(\psi,\mathbbmss{v})
agree modulo

, the second and the fourth items imply that

for all

.
This path induces the equality

In light of

in

We may thus extend the given path

This path satisfies the conditions required by

5.
A generalized Paschke-type duality

This section is devoted to proving a generalized Paschke-type duality that unifies earlier results of a similar nature.
We start with some preparatory observations.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
be a cocycle representation and
x\in\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
.
Then for every
a\in A
, the map
g\mapsto\varphi(a)\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(x)
is norm-continuous.

Proof.

Let
h\in G
and
a\in A
.
We then have
\lim_{g\to h}\alpha_{g}(\alpha_{h}^{-1}(a))=a
in norm and therefore

\lim_{g\to h}\varphi(a)\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(x)=\lim_{g\to h}\beta_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(\varphi(\alpha_{h}^{-1}(a))x).

Since
a
and
h
were arbitrary and
\alpha
is point-norm continuous on
A
, we see that the claim is equivalent to saying that the assignment
g\mapsto\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(\varphi(a)x)
is norm-continuous for every
a\in A
.
Let
\mathcal{Q}(B)
denote the corona algebra of
B
, let
\bar{\beta}^{\mathbbmss{u}}
be the algebraic
G
-action induced by
\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}
, let
\bar{\varphi}:A\to\mathcal{Q}(B)
be the induced homomorphism, and write
\bar{x}=x+B\in\mathcal{Q}(B)
.
By
[
4
, Theorem 2.1]
, it suffices to show that the map
g\mapsto\bar{\beta}^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(\bar{\varphi}(a)\bar{x})
is norm-continuous as a map into
\mathcal{Q}(B)
.
The condition
[x,\varphi(A)]\subseteq B
means
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\prime}
.
The condition
\varphi(A)(\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(x)-x)\subseteq B
for all
g\in G
means that the element

x^{\sharp}=\bar{x}+(\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\perp})\in(\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\prime})/(\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\perp})

is in the fixed point algebra of the action induced by
\bar{\beta}^{\mathbbmss{u}}
, which we shall denote by
E
.
We have an induced (algebraic)
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\kappa:(A\otimes_{\max}E,\alpha\otimes\operatorname{id})\to(\mathcal{Q}(B),\bar{\beta}^{\mathbbmss{u}})
given by

\kappa\big(a\otimes\big(y+(\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\perp})\big)\big)=\bar{\varphi}(a)y

for all
a\in A
and
y\in\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\prime}
with
y+(\mathcal{Q}(B)\cap\bar{\varphi}(A)^{\perp})\in E
.
By equivariance of
\kappa
, it follows for all
g,h\in G
that

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\|\bar{\beta}_{g}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(\bar{\varphi}(a)\bar{x})-\bar{\beta}_{h}^{\mathbbmss{u}}(\bar{\varphi}(a)\bar{x})\|&=&\|\kappa\big((\alpha_{g}(a)-\alpha_{h}(a))\otimes x^{\sharp}\big)\|\\
&\leq&\|(\alpha_{g}(a)-\alpha_{h}(a))\otimes x^{\sharp}\|\\
&\leq&\|x\|\|\alpha_{g}(a)-\alpha_{h}(a)\|.\end{array}

This shows the desired continuity property and finishes the proof.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
be a cocycle representation.
Keeping in mind
section 2
and
section 4
, one has
\mathscr{C}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}=\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}\cap\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}
.
The resulting inclusion of quotients
\mathscr{B}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}=\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}/\mathscr{C}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}\subseteq\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}/\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The only thing to show is that this map is surjective.
Concretely, we have to show that for every
x\in\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
, there exists
y\in\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
such that
x-y\in\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}
.
Given
x
, we consider the strictly continuous map
\zeta:G\to\mathcal{M}(B)
given by
\zeta(g)=\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(x)-x
.
By definition of
\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
, we have
\zeta(G)\varphi(A)\subseteq B
.
Furthermore, every map of the form
\zeta(\_)\varphi(a)
is norm-continuous by
section 5
.
We apply Kasparov’s technical theorem
[
31
, Theorem 1.4]
for
B
in place of
J
with
G
-action
\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}
,
\varphi(A)
in place of
A_{1}
,
\zeta
in place of
\varphi
, and
A_{2}=\Delta=0
.
This yields two positive contractions
M,N\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}}(B)
with
M+N=\mathbf{1}
satisfying
\zeta(G)N\subset B
and
\varphi(A)M\subset B
.
Clearly
M,N\in\mathscr{D}_{(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u})}
.
The element
y=xN\in\mathscr{D}_{\varphi}
then satisfies for all

\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(y)-y\equiv_{B}(\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(x)-x)N=\zeta(g)N\in B.

Hence

.
On the other hand, we have for all

(x-y)\varphi(a)=x(1-N)\varphi(a)=xM\varphi(a)\in B.

In other words, we see that indeed
x-y\in\mathscr{C}_{\varphi}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\varphi,\mathbbmss{u}):(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\beta^{\mathbbmss{u}\oplus\mathbbmss{u}}_{g}(\kappa_{t}(x))\hfil\lx@intercol\\

section 4
in this sense and conclude that

\Xi_{0}:K_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\beta^{\mathbb{U}}}(B))\to H_{\beta}=\mathbb{E}^{G}(\operatorname{id}_{\{0\}},\beta)/_{\sim_{h}}

given by
[W]\mapsto\big[\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ(0,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big]
is an isomorphism.

On the other hand, when we take
[U]\in K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
, then we see

H_{\beta}\ni(\mathfrak{f}\circ\Xi)([U])=\mathfrak{f}\big[\operatorname{Ad}(U)\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big]=\big[\operatorname{Ad}(U)\circ(0,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big].

This means that the composition
\mathfrak{f}\circ\Xi
agrees with the composition of
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to K_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\beta^{\mathbb{U}}}(B))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Xi_{0}}}{{\to}}H_{\beta}
.

Now let
[W]\in K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
for some
W\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{1}+\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
.
Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(B)^{\beta}
be two isometries with
r_{1}r_{1}^{*}+r_{2}r_{2}^{*}=\mathbf{1}
, which we use to form Cuntz sums.
Exactly as in the proof of
section 5
, we consider the norm-continuous path of isometries
r_{1+t}:=\sqrt{1-t}\cdot r_{1}+\sqrt{t}\cdot r_{2}
for
t\in[0,1]
and the point-norm continuous family of embeddings
\kappa_{t}:\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})}\to\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})}
for
t\in[0,1]
given by
\kappa_{t}(x)=r_{1+t}xr_{1+t}^{*}
.

The assignment
[0,1]\ni t\mapsto r_{t}Wr_{t}^{*}+(\mathbf{1}-r_{t}r_{t}^{*})
induces a homotopy between
W\oplus\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}\oplus W
inside
\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{1}+\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})})
.
This yields a chain of
\mathfrak{C}
-homotopies

\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\big(\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\big)\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\sim_{h,\mathfrak{C}}&\big(\operatorname{Ad}(W\oplus\mathbf{1})\circ(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U}),(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})\big)\\
\sim_{h,\mathfrak{C}}&\big(\operatorname{Ad}(\mathbf{1}\oplus W)\circ(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U}),(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})\big)\\
\sim_{h,\mathfrak{C}}&\big(\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ(0,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big).\end{array}

Hence the composition of the maps
K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to K_{1}(\mathcal{M}^{\beta^{\mathbb{U}}}(B))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Xi}}{{\to}}H_{\beta}\to\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)/_{\sim_{\mathfrak{C},h}}
is equal to the composition of the maps
K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Xi}}{{\to}}\mathbb{E}^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)/_{\sim_{\mathfrak{C},h}}
.
This induces the homomorphism

K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to H_{\beta}

such that the whole left half of the diagram commutes.
It is injective by
section 5
.
We claim that it is also surjective.
Given any
x\in H_{\beta}
, we may appeal to the surjectivity of
\Xi_{0}
and find a unitary
V\in\mathcal{M}^{\beta^{\mathbb{U}}}
such that
x=\big[\operatorname{Ad}(V)\circ(0,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big]
.
Since the map

K_{1}(\operatorname{id}\oplus 0):K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})})

is an isomorphism by
section 4
, we find a unitary
W\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbf{1}+\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
with
[W\oplus\mathbf{1}]=[\mathbf{1}\oplus V]
in
K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})})
.
By the
K_{1}
-injectivity of
\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})}
, we have that
W\oplus\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{1}\oplus V
are homotopic inside the unitary group of
\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi\oplus 0,\mathbb{U}\oplus\mathbb{U})}
.
The analogous computation to the above then shows that

\big[\operatorname{Ad}(W)\circ(\Phi,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big]=\big[\operatorname{Ad}(V)\circ(0,\mathbb{U}),(0,\mathbb{U})\big]=x.

This takes care of the left half of the diagram.

The isomorphism
K_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
making the rest of the diagram commute is then uniquely induced because

K_{1}(\mathscr{C}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\hookrightarrow K_{1}(\mathscr{D}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\twoheadrightarrow K_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})

is also a short exact sequence due to
\mathfrak{f}
inducing a left inverse for the first arrow.
We have already observed in
section 5
and
section 5
that the arrow
K_{1}(\mathscr{D}^{w}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\to K_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
is a well-defined isomorphism.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}
be an eligible set of cocycle representations
(A,\alpha)\to(\mathcal{M}(B),\beta)
and let
(\Phi,\mathbb{U})\in\mathfrak{C}
be an absorbing element.
The isomorphism
K_{1}(\mathscr{B}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})\cong KK^{G}(\mathfrak{C};\alpha,\beta)
from
section 5
is, strictly speaking, only the first of two possible versions of Paschke duality in our context.
It can be viewed as a generalization of
[
45
, Theorem 6.2]
in the language of cocycle representations instead of genuinely equivariant ones.
The second version of Paschke duality should relate
K_{0}(\mathscr{B}_{(\Phi,\mathbb{U})})
to a generalized version of the group
KK^{G}_{1}(\alpha,\beta)
and/or
\operatorname{Ext}^{G}(\alpha,\beta)
built from cocycle representations or Busby maps related to
\mathfrak{C}
.
A rigorous investigation of how such generalized equivariant Ext- or
KK_{1}
-groups would actually look like is beyond the scope of this article, but the apparent template for this kind of second Paschke duality would be
[
45
, Theorems 6.3, 6.5]
; see also
[
44
, Section 3]
.
For
\mathfrak{C}
being the set of all cocycle representations, the related constructions are available in the literature, and such a result could in this case perhaps be achieved with some extra work if one appeals to
[
43
, Lemma 4.4]
.
Since that result and its proof appeal to various technical results in the Kasparov picture of
KK^{G}_{1}
(including the general form of the Kasparov product and its properties from
[
30
]
), it is not immediately clear how to generalize the argument to the present context in a self-contained manner.

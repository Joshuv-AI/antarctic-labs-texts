Comparative effectiveness and safety of inclisiran versus evolocumab and alirocumab: a 180-day real-world study
Authors: Jiafang Yao, Qiyin Sun, Dan Liu, Junling Lin, WenJuan Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and ObjectivesProprotein convertase subtilisin/kexin type 9 (PCSK9) inhibitors have become an important therapeutic option for patients with dyslipidemia. Evolocumab and alirocumab are monoclonal antibodies targeting circulating PCSK9, whereas inclisiran is a small interfering RNA (siRNA) agent that suppresses hepatic PCSK9 synthesis and requires only twice-yearly dosing. Although these agents are proven effective in clinical trials, direct real-world comparative evidence on lipid-lowering efficacy, apolipoprotein B (apoB) reduction, safety, and adherence remains limited.MethodsThis was a real-world, retrospective study conducted at a single center. A total of 198 patients were consecutively enrolled and assigned to three groups (n = 66 per group): inclisiran, evolocumab, or alirocumab. Baseline characteristics, including age, sex, and familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) status (assessed by DLCN criteria), were well-balanced across cohorts. All lipid parameters were analyzed using standardized automated biochemical analyzers at a centralized laboratory to ensure measurement consistency.ResultsAt 180 days, all three agents achieved robust and significant reductions in LDL-C from baseline (p < 0.001), with no significant differences observed between the groups. Notably, inclisiran demonstrated a numerical advantage in the reduction of apolipoprotein B (apoB) compared with evolocumab and alirocumab at 180 days (0.650 g/Lvs.0.774 g/L and 0.752 g/L, respectively), although these between-group differences did not reach formal statistical significance (p > 0.05). Treatment persistence was highest in the inclisiran group (69.7%), followed by evolocumab (56.1%) and alirocumab (50.0%). All therapies were well-tolerated with no new safety signals.ConclusionsIn this real-world study, Inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab are all highly effective in lowering LDL-C in a real-world setting. Inclisiran may offer additional clinical value through superior treatment persistence and a favorable numerical trend toward enhanced apoB suppression.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12872-026-05931-5.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) remains the leading global cause of morbidity and mortality, imposing a persistent public health burden despite major advances in prevention and therapy [1]. Dyslipidemia—particularly elevated low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) and apolipoprotein B (apoB)—plays a central causal role in atherogenesis [2–4]. While statins are the cornerstone of lipid management and robustly reduce ASCVD events, many patients do not reach guideline-recommended LDL-C targets or experience statin intolerance, leaving considerable residual risk and an unmet need for additional therapies [5–7] .

PCSK9-targeting agents have transformed lipid-lowering strategies. Evolocumab and alirocumab—fully human monoclonal antibodies that neutralize circulating PCSK9—achieve ~ 50–60% LDL-C reductions and have demonstrated cardiovascular outcome benefits in large-scale randomized trials [8–11]. Inclisiran, a small interfering RNA (siRNA) that suppresses hepatic PCSK9 synthesis, produces sustained LDL-C lowering with a twice-yearly maintenance schedule, which may favor long-term adherence compared with more frequent injections required for monoclonal antibodies [12–14].

Although all three therapies converge on PCSK9, they may differ in their effects across lipid fractions, especially apoB, which reflects the number of atherogenic particles and is increasingly recognized as a superior marker of atherogenic burden and ASCVD risk prediction compared with LDL-C or non–HDL-C [15–18]. Moreover, important questions persist regarding comparative metabolic effects (e.g., on glucose homeostasis), organ safety, and real-world adherence and persistence [17, 19, 20].

To address these gaps, we conducted a 180-day real-world comparative study of inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab. We focused on (i) the magnitude and durability of changes in LDL-C, total cholesterol, triglycerides, and particularly apoB; (ii) metabolic neutrality with respect to fasting glucose; (iii) hepatic and renal safety; and (iv) treatment adherence and loss to follow-up.

Methods

Study design and population

This was a real-world, retrospective, observational study conducted at the First Affiliated Hospital of Huzhou University. Patients were consecutively enrolled between October 2023 and July 2025 until each of the three treatment groups reached a total of 66 patients. This approach was taken to ensure a balanced sample size for statistical comparison across the three different PCSK9-targeted therapies in a routine clinical setting. The administration regimens were specified as alirocumab (75 mg q2w), evolocumab (140 mg q2w), and inclisiran (284 mg at Day 0, 90, and then every 6 months). This clarification ensures the reproducibility and clarity of our findings.

Eligibility criteria

Patients were eligible if they had a documented diagnosis of dyslipidemia and initiated therapy with one of the three PCSK9-targeted agents during the study period. The decision regarding which medication to use (inclisiran, evolocumab, or alirocumab) was made by the treating physicians and the patients during routine clinical practice, based on clinical judgment, patient preference, and drug availability at the time of treatment initiation.

Exclusion criteria

Patients with severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, recent acute coronary syndrome (< 3 months), uncontrolled thyroid disease, or incomplete baseline lipid data were excluded.

In our real-world cohort, these therapies were predominantly initiated in patients who either exhibited documented statin intolerance or had failed to achieve guideline-recommended LDL-C targets despite optimized statin therapy. This clinical context accounts for the high proportion of patients receiving these agents as monotherapy, reflecting a common scenario in routine lipid clinics where patients are escalated to potent injectable biologics due to the inability to tolerate or respond sufficiently to oral cornerstones.

Approval

This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The protocol was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of First Affiliated Hospital of Huzhou Universit. The requirement for written informed consent was waived due to the retrospective design.

Follow-up and assessments

Patients were followed for 180 days after initiation of therapy. Follow-up assessments were performed at 1 month (Day 30), 3 months (Day 90), and 6 months (Day 180). At each visit, fasting venous blood samples were collected to measure lipid profile (LDL-C, total cholesterol [TC], triglycerides [TG], and apoB).

Due to variable real-world adherence, the number of patients available for evaluation decreased over time. At Day 30, data were available from 57 patients in the inclisiran group, 50 in the evolocumab group, and 43 in the alirocumab group. At Day 90, evaluable patients included 57 in the inclisiran group, 39 in the evolocumab group, and 31 in the alirocumab group. At Day 180, follow-up was completed by 46 patients on inclisiran, 37 on evolocumab, and 33 on alirocumab.

Metabolic marker: fasting blood glucose

Safety markers: Fasting glucose, and safety laboratory indices including alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).

Endpoints

Primary endpoint: Percentage change from baseline in LDL-C, TC, TG, and apoB.

Secondary endpoints: Changes in fasting glucose, liver and renal function, and treatment adherence (loss to follow-up, discontinuation).

FH screening

Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) status was retrospectively assessed using the Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (DLCN) criteria. FH status was retrospectively assessed using the Dutch Lipid Clinic Network (DLCN) criteria. According to the total scores, patients were categorized as definite FH (score > 8), probable FH (score 6–8), or possible FH (score 3–5).

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean±standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range) depending on the distribution. Comparisons among the three treatment groups (inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab) were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA). To evaluate pairwise between-group differences, Tukey’s Honestly Significant Difference (HSD) post-hoc tests were employed. Furthermore, to maintain a robust Type I error rate and account for the potential inflation of alpha levels during multiple comparisons, Bonferroni correction was applied where appropriate. Categorical variables were presented as frequencies and percentages (n, %) and analyzed using the Chi-square test or Fisher’s exact test. A two-sided p < 0.05was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 26.0.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of patients in the Inclisiran, Evolocumab, and Alirocumab groups

The mean age was comparable among groups (inclisiran: 63.1 ± 1.6 years; evolocumab: 63.7 ± 1.6 years; alirocumab: 65.6 ± 1.7 years; p > 0.05). Sex distribution was also similar, with men comprising 71–76% of each cohort. Body mass index (BMI) was in the normal-to-overweight range across groups (24.4 ± 0.4, 23.9 ± 0.5, and 24.0 ± 0.4 kg/m², respectively; p > 0.05).

The proportion of active smokers was higher in the evolocumab group (40/26) compared with inclisiran (35/31) and alirocumab (25/41), though this difference did not reach statistical significance. Concomitant statin therapy was most frequently observed in the alirocumab group (15/66), followed by inclisiran (7/66), while no patients in the evolocumab group were receiving statins. Conversely, single-agent use was highest with evolocumab (100%), followed by inclisiran (89%) and alirocumab (77%).

Comorbid conditions were well distributed among the groups. Hypertension was present in 49, 54, and 36 patients in the inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab groups, respectively. Diabetes mellitus was reported in 11, 9, and 14 patients, while coronary artery disease was prevalent in approximately two-thirds of patients across groups (40, 44, and 39, respectively). Stroke was documented in 5 patients treated with evolocumab but was absent in the other groups. Chronic kidney disease was rare, with only one patient affected in the inclisiran group.

Regarding the baseline lipid profiles and disease severity, the distribution of FH status was comparable across the three treatment cohorts (Table 1). No significant differences were observed in the proportions of patients categorized as having definite or probable FH p > 0.05), indicating a well-balanced clinical baseline among the groups.

Overall, there were no statistically significant differences among the three treatment cohorts in baseline demographics, cardiovascular risk factors, comorbidities, or concomitant medication use (p > 0.05 for all comparisons), indicating that the groups were well balanced at study entry.

Fig. 4Between-group comparisons of LDL-C reduction among inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab

Between-group comparisons of LDL-C reduction among inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab

All three treatment groups demonstrated marked reductions in LDL-C, total cholesterol (TC), and triglycerides (TG) at 30, 90, and 180 days compared with baseline (p < 0.001 for all within-group analyses). However, between-group comparisons revealed no statistically significant differences in LDL-C lowering efficacy at any follow-up time point.

At 30 days, mean LDL-C levels were 1.043 mmol/L in the inclisiran group, 0.882 mmol/L in the evolocumab group, and 0.974 mmol/L in the alirocumab group. Pairwise comparisons showed small differences: inclisiran vs. evolocumab (mean difference 0.161, SE 0.126), inclisiran vs. alirocumab (0.069, SE 0.132), and evolocumab vs. alirocumab (–0.093, SE 0.136), none of which were statistically significant (p > 0.05).

At 90 days, mean LDL-C levels were 1.315 mmol/L (inclisiran), 1.257 mmol/L (evolocumab), and 1.281 mmol/L (alirocumab). Differences between inclisiran and evolocumab (0.058, SE 0.150), inclisiran and alirocumab (0.033, SE 0.161), and evolocumab and alirocumab (–0.024, SE 0.174) remained non-significant.

At 180 days, LDL-C levels were 1.395 mmol/L (inclisiran), 1.436 mmol/L (evolocumab), and 1.546 mmol/L (alirocumab). Pairwise analyses indicated numerically lower LDL-C with inclisiran compared with alirocumab (mean difference − 0.151, SE 0.155) and with evolocumab compared with alirocumab (–0.110, SE 0.163), although these trends did not reach statistical significance. The difference between inclisiran and evolocumab was minimal (–0.041, SE 0.150, p > 0.05).

Compared with baseline, all three therapies achieved robust LDL-C reductions. Baseline LDL-C levels were 2.946 mmol/L (inclisiran), 2.921 mmol/L (evolocumab), and 2.535 mmol/L (alirocumab). Absolute reductions at 180 days were greater with inclisiran (–1.551 mmol/L) and evolocumab (–1.485 mmol/L) than with alirocumab (–0.989 mmol/L). Between-group comparisons confirmed that inclisiran vs. evolocumab showed negligible difference (0.025, SE 0.198), whereas inclisiran vs. alirocumab (0.410, SE 0.198) and evolocumab vs. alirocumab (0.386, SE 0.198) demonstrated numerically greater LDL-C reductions with siRNA-and antibody-based PCSK9 inhibitors relative to alirocumab, although these differences did not reach statistical significance.

In summary, inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab all provided substantial LDL-C lowering over 6 months, with no significant differences in efficacy. Nevertheless, inclisiran and evolocumab showed numerically greater LDL-C reductions compared with alirocumab, suggesting a potential trend toward enhanced lipid-lowering efficacy.

At baseline, mean apoB levels were comparable across the three groups: 0.964 g/L in the inclisiran group, 0.948 g/L in the evolocumab group, and 0.876 g/L in the alirocumab group. Pairwise comparisons showed no significant differences: inclisiran vs. evolocumab (mean difference 0.016, SE 0.056), inclisiran vs. alirocumab (0.088, SE 0.056), and evolocumab vs. alirocumab (0.072, SE 0.056; all p > 0.05).

At 30 days, apoB levels declined substantially in all groups, reaching 0.543 g/L (inclisiran), 0.524 g/L (evolocumab), and 0.571 g/L (alirocumab). No statistically significant differences were observed between inclisiran and evolocumab (mean difference 0.019, SE 0.081), inclisiran and alirocumab (–0.028, SE 0.078), or evolocumab and alirocumab (–0.047, SE 0.086).

At 90 days, apoB remained suppressed at 0.629 g/L in the inclisiran group, 0.657 g/L in the evolocumab group, and 0.698 g/L in the alirocumab group. Pairwise differences again did not reach statistical significance: inclisiran vs. evolocumab (–0.029, SE 0.081), inclisiran vs. alirocumab (–0.070, SE 0.103), and evolocumab vs. alirocumab (–0.041, SE 0.106).

At 180 days, clear divergence emerged. ApoB levels were 0.650 g/L in the inclisiran group, 0.774 g/L in the evolocumab group, and 0.752 g/L in the alirocumab group. Inclisiran was associated with lower apoB compared with both evolocumab (mean difference − 0.125, SE 0.086) and alirocumab (–0.103, SE 0.098), while evolocumab and alirocumab were similar (0.022, SE 0.094). Although these differences did not reach formal statistical significance, the numerical advantage of inclisiran became more apparent over time.

Overall, all three PCSK9-targeted therapies significantly reduced apoB concentrations from baseline. However, inclisiran demonstrated a trend toward a more sustained suppression of apoB at 180 days compared with evolocumab and alirocumab, albeit without reaching formal statistical significance.

All three therapies demonstrated favorable safety and tolerability during the 180-day follow-up, with no treatment-related serious adverse events reported.

Glucose metabolism: Inclisiran and evolocumab maintained stable fasting glucose throughout the study, suggesting metabolic neutrality. In contrast, alirocumab was associated with a gradual increase in fasting glucose, reaching 7.4 ± 0.3 mmol/L at 180 days (p < 0.05 vs. baseline). This finding may be clinically relevant for patients with diabetes or impaired glucose tolerance.

Renal function: Creatinine (Cr) and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) remained stable in the inclisiran and evolocumab groups. Alirocumab showed a mild, non-significant rise in Cr and a reduction in eGFR at 180 days, but values remained within the normal clinical range, indicating no overt nephrotoxicity.

Hepatic function: ALT and AST levels did not show clinically significant changes across the three groups. Transient fluctuations were observed, but all values remained within normal ranges, suggesting an absence of hepatotoxicity.

Overall, inclisiran and evolocumab exhibited stable safety profiles with no meaningful impact on glucose, liver, or kidney function. Alirocumab, although generally well tolerated, was associated with a trend toward increased fasting glucose and mild renal function decline, warranting closer monitoring in at-risk populations.

Adherence analysis revealed notable differences among groups across the 30-, 90-, and 180-day follow-up periods. At 30 days, inclisiran demonstrated the highest persistence, with 57 of 66 patients (87%) completing follow-up, compared with 50 of 66 (76%) in the evolocumab group and 43 of 66 (65%) in the alirocumab group.

By 90 days, retention declined in all groups, though inclisiran still showed the best adherence, with 87% of patients retained, compared with 59% for evolocumab and 47% for alirocumab. At 180 days, persistence was maintained in 70% of inclisiran-treated patients, 56% of evolocumab-treated patients, and 50% of alirocumab-treated patients.

These findings underscore the practical advantage of inclisiran’s twice-yearly dosing regimen in promoting long-term adherence compared with the more frequent injection schedules required for monoclonal antibody therapies.

Discussion

This real-world study provides comparative evidence on the lipid-lowering efficacy, safety, and treatment adherence of inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab in patients with dyslipidemia over 180 days. Consistent with findings from pivotal randomized controlled trials, all three agents produced robust and sustained reductions in LDL-C, total cholesterol, and triglycerides, reinforcing their established role as effective lipid-lowering therapies in clinical practice [21–24]. Importantly, the absence of significant between-group differences in LDL-C reduction suggests that these PCSK9-targeted therapies share a broadly comparable efficacy profile in lowering traditional lipid parameters [25].

However, our study highlights meaningful distinctions in apolipoprotein B (apoB) reduction, a parameter increasingly recognized as a more accurate surrogate for atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk than LDL-C alone [26–28]. ApoB reflects the total burden of circulating atherogenic particles, including LDL, very-low-density lipoproteins, and lipoprotein(a). Notably, inclisiran achieved a numerically greater and more durable reduction in apoB compared with evolocumab and alirocumab. Although this did not reach statistical significance between groups, the observed trend is clinically relevant, as residual cardiovascular risk persists in many patients despite optimal LDL-C control, and elevated apoB has been shown to be strongly associated with adverse cardiovascular outcomes [29–31]. The superior effect of inclisiran on apoB may therefore confer incremental protection against ASCVD beyond LDL-C lowering alone, warranting confirmation in long-term outcome-driven trials [32].

The observed numerical divergence between LDL-C and apoB reduction at 180 days suggests that inclisiran may exert a more comprehensive effect on the total atherogenic particle burden. This could be attributed to the sustained suppression of hepatic PCSK9 synthesis from the source, leading to more stable kinetics of hepatic clearance for all apoB-containing lipoproteins. Furthermore, recent evidence indicates that such intensive PCSK9 inhibition can positively modulate vascular profiles, as evaluated through pulse wave velocity (PWV), particularly in high-risk populations [33, 34]. While the specific contribution of Lp(a) or VLDL to this divergence warrants further investigation with granular subfraction analysis, our findings reinforce the potential of siRNA-based therapy in long-term vascular protection.

In addition to lipid and lipoprotein effects, differences in glucose metabolism were observed. Inclisiran demonstrated metabolic neutrality, with no adverse changes in fasting glucose. Evolocumab similarly showed no significant impact, whereas alirocumab was associated with a modest but significant increase in fasting glucose during follow-up. Although the mechanisms underlying this observation remain unclear, prior studies have suggested that PCSK9 inhibition may interact with glucose regulation, potentially through pathways involving pancreatic β-cell function or insulin sensitivity [35, 36]. These findings underscore the importance of careful monitoring of glycemic status when prescribing alirocumab, particularly in patients with diabetes or prediabetes.

Another key observation concerns treatment adherence. Inclisiran exhibited markedly superior persistence and lower loss-to-follow-up compared with the monoclonal antibodies. This is likely attributable to its unique twice-yearly dosing regimen, which minimizes treatment burden and reduces the risk of injection fatigue [37, 38]. In contrast, the more frequent administration required for evolocumab and alirocumab was associated with higher discontinuation rates, echoing concerns from previous registry data regarding real-world adherence to injectable biologics. Enhanced adherence with inclisiran may translate into more consistent lipid control, improved long-term cardiovascular outcomes, and reduced healthcare costs associated with treatment discontinuation [39–41].

It is noteworthy that a significant majority of our participants used PCSK9 inhibitors as monotherapy—100% in the evolocumab group, 89% in the inclisiran group, and 77% in the alirocumab group. This high rate of single-agent use is a defining characteristic of our real-world study and reflects the clinical reality of managing a ‘difficult-to-treat’ population. These patients often present with significant residual cardiovascular risk but have discontinued statins due to adverse effects (such as statin-associated muscle symptoms) or require more aggressive intervention after failing to reach targets. Therefore, our findings provide valuable insights into the effectiveness of these agents as a primary strategy for those who cannot rely on traditional statin therapy.

Finally, the safety profiles of all three therapies were reassuring. No clinically significant elevations in liver enzymes, renal function markers, or serious adverse events were identified, consistent with prior phase 3 clinical trial data and real-world pharmacovigilance reports [42–44]. This supports the overall tolerability of PCSK9-targeted approaches in diverse patient populations.

Taken together, these findings emphasize several important clinical implications. While all three therapies are effective in lowering LDL-C and other lipid parameters, inclisiran may offer distinct advantages through its greater apoB reduction, metabolic neutrality, and simplified dosing regimen that supports improved adherence. Given the increasing recognition of apoB as a superior predictor of ASCVD risk, inclisiran may play an important role in optimizing residual risk reduction in patients with dyslipidemia. Long-term outcome trials and larger real-world studies are warranted to confirm whether these advantages translate into meaningful reductions in cardiovascular morbidity and mortality.

Clinical implications

Our findings have significant clinical implications for the management of dyslipidemia in real-world practice. Inclisiran should be viewed not merely as an alternative to monoclonal antibodies, but as a complementary strategy particularly suited for patients with documented adherence challenges, complex cardiometabolic profiles, or high residual apoB-related risk.

Furthermore, the clinical value of such therapy extends beyond traditional lipid-lowering. Emerging evidence suggests that intensive PCSK9 inhibition may positively modulate immune-inflammatory and metabolic risk pathways, thereby slowing the progression of subclinical atherosclerosis [45]. In this context, the superior treatment persistence observed with inclisiran (69.7%) is not merely a matter of patient convenience; it represents a critical driver for long-term cardiovascular risk mitigation. By ensuring consistent lipid-lowering and metabolic stability, siRNA-based therapy offers a robust approach to addressing the ‘residual risk’ that persists even with conventional statin therap.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations that should be acknowledged to ensure a balanced interpretation of the findings.

First, the retrospective, single-center design and relatively short follow-up duration of 180 days may limit the generalizability of our results. Furthermore, long-term cardiovascular outcomes and hard clinical endpoints were not assessed, which is typical for early real-world comparative studies.

Second, the non-randomized nature of the study introduces an inherent risk of selection bias. In our clinical practice, treatment allocation was guided by clinical severity, prior statin intolerance, and patient affordability (reimbursement status). Consequently, the imbalance in background lipid-lowering therapies—such as the varying rates of statin and ezetimibe co-administration—remains a potential confounder. Additionally, the lack of lipoprotein(a) [Lp(a)] measurements precludes a definitive mechanistic explanation for the observed numerical divergence between LDL-C and apoB reductions.

Third, we observed a significant attrition rate by the 180-day mark, with the number of evaluable patients declining from 66 per group at baseline to 33–46 patients. As is common in real-world retrospective research, this loss to follow-up often occurs when patients transition to local primary care clinics or when data is not fully captured across decentralized electronic medical record systems. Because specific reasons for discontinuation—such as adverse effects, cost, or “injection fatigue”—were not systematically recorded, we cannot entirely rule out attrition bias, which may limit the statistical power and long-term interpretability of the results.

Finally, our study lacked patient survey data regarding the acceptability, perceived convenience, and preference for different treatment regimens. Investigating these patient-centered factors in future prospective trials is essential to understanding treatment persistence and optimizing long-term cardiovascular risk mitigation in real-world practice.

Conclusion

Inclisiran, evolocumab, and alirocumab all provide substantial and comparable reductions in LDL-C, total cholesterol, and triglycerides in patients with dyslipidemia. However, inclisiran demonstrates two distinctive advantages.

First, inclisiran achieved a greater and more durable reduction in apolipoprotein B (apoB) compared with evolocumab and alirocumab. Since apoB directly reflects the total number of circulating atherogenic lipoprotein particles, this enhanced effect underscores inclisiran’s potential to deliver superior residual risk reduction beyond LDL-C lowering alone.

Second, inclisiran showed consistently better treatment persistence throughout follow-up, with higher retention at 30, 90, and 180 days. This adherence advantage is strongly linked to its twice-yearly dosing regimen, which reduces treatment burden and injection fatigue compared with monoclonal antibody therapy.

In addition, inclisiran exhibited metabolic neutrality, with no adverse effect on glucose metabolism, in contrast to alirocumab which was associated with increased fasting glucose during follow-up.

Taken together, these findings suggest that inclisiran not only matches but exceeds monoclonal antibody therapy in key dimensions of real-world lipid management—by combining effective LDL-C and TG lowering with superior apoB reduction, improved adherence, and metabolic safety. This positions inclisiran as a particularly valuable therapeutic option for the long-term management of atherogenic dyslipidemia and cardiovascular risk reduction.

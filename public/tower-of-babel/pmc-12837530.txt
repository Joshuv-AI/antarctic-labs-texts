Clinical efficacy of amiodarone in prehospital emergency treatment of myocardial infarction: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Tianzi Wu, Yongmei Wang, Haiting Liu, Yujie Zheng, Ying Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo systematically review the clinical efficacy of amiodarone in the treatment of myocardial infarction in prehospital emergency.MethodsArticles related to the use of amiodarone in prehospital emergency treatment of myocardial infarction were retrieved from PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMBASE, Wanfang, VIP and CNKI databases, and the retrieval time was from the establishment of the database to October 31 2024. Meta-analysis and risk bias evaluation were carried out with R 4.2.2 software, and the results were considered statistically significant when P < 0.05.ResultsA total of 16 studies involving 832 patients receiving prehospital amiodarone and 800 control patients were included in this systematic review and meta-analysis. Pooled results demonstrated that, compared to the control group, amiodarone significantly reduced the incidence of malignant arrhythmia (RR = 0.29, 95% CI: 0.22 to 0.37, P < 0.01), decreased the average number of defibrillations (MD = -2.40, 95% CI: -2.61 to -2.19, P < 0.01), improved the success rate of rescue (RR = 1.16, 95% CI: 1.12 to 1.21, P < 0.01), lowered the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction (RR = 0.22, 95% CI: 0.13 to 0.35, P < 0.01), reduced the incidence of adverse reactions (RR = 0.33, 95% CI: 0.12 to 0.87, P = 0.02), and shortened the length of hospital stay (MD = -3.81, 95% CI: -4.02 to -3.59, P < 0.01).ConclusionThis systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrated that prehospital amiodarone administration in patients with myocardial infarction significantly reduced the incidence of malignant arrhythmia and the average number of defibrillations, while improving the success rate of rescue. Furthermore, it lowered the incidence of adverse reactions, the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction, and shortened the length of hospital stay. These findings support the clinical promotion of amiodarone in prehospital emergency care for myocardial infarction.Keywords: Amiodarone, Pre-hospital first aid, Myocardial infarction, Efficacy, Meta-analysis

Introduction

Acute myocardial infarction (AMI) is one of the leading causes of death worldwide [1]. It is estimated that over 3 million people suffer from acute ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) and over 4 million people suffer from non-STEMI (NSTEMI) every year [2]; 15%−25% of patients may end up dying [3, 4]. With population aging and the compounding effect of comorbidities (e.g., obesity), the incidence of acute myocardial infarction continues to rise, projecting a significant increase in its public health burden in coming years. In China, the death caused by myocardial infarction is second only to cancer and cerebrovascular disease, ranking third among all causes of death due to disease [5, 6]. Timely and effective pre-hospital first aid for patients with myocardial infarction is of great significance to the clinical efficacy and prognosis of patients.

Arrhythmia is one of the most common complications in patients with myocardial infarction, and it is also the main cause of death [7]. As a class III antiarrhythmic drug, amiodarone acts by blocking the outward potassium channel responsible for cardiac repolarization. Some studies have reported that the prophylactic use of amiodarone during pre-hospital emergency care has a protective effect on the myocardium by promoting repolarization [8, 9]. While amiodarone has been adopted in myocardial infarction treatment, its prophylactic use in prehospital emergency settings remains understudied. Therefore, this systematic review employs meta-analysis to evaluate the clinical efficacy of amiodarone in prehospital emergency management of myocardial infarction, thereby providing evidence-based support for broader clinical implementation.

Methods

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Types of research and subjects: The included literature consists of published randomized controlled trials (RCTs). The study population comprised consecutive patients receiving prehospital emergency care for myocardial infarction, with no restrictions based on age, sex, or ethnicity.

Interventions: The experimental group in the study was treated with amiodarone while the control group was treated with lidocaine or other non-amiodarone treatments.

Outcome indicators: The main outcome indicators are: ① the incidence of malignant arrhythmia; ② the average number of defibrillations; ③ the success rate of rescue; ④recurrence rate of myocardial infarction (within 28 days) [10]; ⑤the incidence of adverse reactions; ⑥ length of hospital stay.

Exclusion criteria ① Incomplete data on outcome indicators in the literature or no required data types; ② Patients in the experimental group were combined with other interventions; ③ Patients with myocardial infarction accompanied by other heart diseases; ④ Reviews, news, case reports, etc.

Literature search strategy

Articles related to amiodarone in pre-hospital emergency treatment of myocardial infarction were retrieved from PubMed, Cochrane Library, EMBASE, Wanfang, VIP and CNKI databases from the establishment of the databases establishment to October 31, 2024 in Chinese and English. Keywords: Amiodarone, pre-hospital first aid, myocardial infarction, curative effect. “This study strictly followed the PRISMA statement [11] and was registered on the PROSPERO international database (Registration ID: CRD420251085701).

Literature screening and data extraction

Two researchers independently screened the retrieved studies using predefined inclusion/exclusion criteria. Following initial screening, they cross-verified the selected studies and independently extracted data through a standardized form. Extracted variables included: publication year, sample sizes of the treatment and control groups, patient demographics (age, sex ratio), and outcome measures. Any disagreements during screening or data extraction were resolved by consulting a third researcher to reach consensus.

Quality evaluation of the included literature

The quality of the literature was evaluated using the Cochrane Handbook, and the evaluation indicators included 7 indicators: ①random allocation, ②blinding of allocation, ③blinding of participants and personnel, ④blinding of outcome assessment, ⑤incomplete outcome data, ⑥selective reporting, and ⑦others. Each index can be rated as “High Risk”, “Unclear Risk” and “Low Risk”, and when ≤ 3 items in the literature are rated as “High Risk” and/or “Unclear Risk”, the quality of the literature meets the requirements of this article.

Statistical analysis

Meta-analysis was performed using R 4.2.2. The measurement data involved binary data and continuous variables. The binary data were represented by relative risk (RR) and 95%CI, and the continuous variables were represented by mean difference (MD) and 95%CI. The Chi-square test was used for the heterogeneity among the included literatures; if there was no heterogeneity among the included studies (P > 0.1, I2 ≤ 50%), the fixed effects model was used; if there was heterogeneity among the included studies (P < 0.1, I2 > 50%), the random effects model was used. Publication bias was evaluated by funnel plot, Egger’s and Begg’s tests, and the Meta-analysis results were statistically significant with P < 0.05.

Meta-analysis results

Incidence of malignant arrhythmias

Five studies reported the incidence of malignant arrhythmia between the two groups after treatment. The heterogeneity test found that there was no heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 0%, P = 0.73), so the fixed effect model was adopted, the results showed that the incidence of malignant arrhythmia after treatment in patients with myocardial infarction in the amiodarone treatment group was lower than that in the control group (RR = 0.29, 95%CI: 0.22 ~ 0.37, P < 0.01), the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 3. The publication bias test was represented by a funnel plot, and the results showed that all the documents were within the funnel and showed symmetry, so there was no publication bias among the included documents, as shown in Fig. 4A.

Forest plot of the comparison of the incidence of malignant arrhythmias between the two groups

Funnel plots (A: Incidence of malignant arrhythmias; B: Average number of defibrillations; C: Success rate of rescue; D: Recurrence rate of myocardial infarction; E: Incidence of adverse reactions; F: Length of hospital stay)

Average number of defibrillations

Thirteen studies reported the average number of defibrillations in the two groups of patients during treatment. The heterogeneity test found that there was heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 90%, P < 0.01), so the random effect model was used for analysis, and the results showed that the average number of defibrillations in the amiodarone treatment group was lower than that in the control group (MD=−2.40, 95%CI: −2.61~−2.19, P < 0.01), the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 5, and the publication bias test was shown by a funnel plot. Three studies were found to be outside the funnel, so Begg’s (z = −0.49, P = 0.63) and Egger’s (t = −0.22, P = 0.83) tests were performed simultaneously, and no publication bias was found between the included studies, as shown in Fig. 4B.

Forest plot of the comparison of the mean number of defibrillations between the two groups

The success rate of rescue

Thirteen studies reported the success rate of rescue after treatment in the two groups, and the heterogeneity test found that there was no heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 0%, P = 0.61), so the fixed effect model was used for analysis, and the results showed the success rate for rescue of patients in the amiodarone treatment group was higher than that in the control group (RR = 1.16, 95%CI: 1.12 ~ 1.21, P < 0.01, the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 6, and the publication bias test was represented by a funnel plot. The study was outside the funnel, so Begg’s (z = 2.44, P = 0.01) and Egger’s (t = 2.41, P = 0.03) tests were performed at the same time, and it was found that there may be a certain publication bias among the included literature, as shown in Fig. 4C.

The forest plot of the comparison of the success rate of rescue between the two groups

Recurrence rate of myocardial infarction

Ten studies reported the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction in the two groups of patients after treatment. The heterogeneity test found that there was no heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 0%, P = 0.96), so the fixed effect model was used. The results showed that the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction in the amiodarone treatment group was lower than that in the control group (RR = 0.22, 95%CI: 0.13 ~ 0.35, P < 0.01), the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 7, and the publication bias test was represented by a funnel plot. All studies were within the funnel and presented symmetry, so there was no publication bias among the included studies, as shown in Fig. 4D.

Forest plot of the comparison of the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction between the two groups

Incidence of adverse reactions

Seven studies reported the incidence of adverse reactions in the two groups of patients after treatment. The heterogeneity test found that there was heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 57%, P = 0.03), so the random effect model was used. The incidence of adverse reactions in the amiodarone treatment group was lower than that in the control group (RR = 0.33, 95%CI: 0.12 ~ 0.87, P = 0.02), and the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 8. The publication bias test was performed using funnel plots, which revealed that all studies fell within the funnel limits, indicating no evidence of publication bias among the included literature (Fig. 4E).

Forest plot of the comparison of the incidence of adverse reactions between the two groups

Length of hospital stay

Eleven studies reported the length of hospital stay between the two groups. Heterogeneity test found that there was no heterogeneity among the included studies (I2 = 10%, P = 0.35), so the fixed-effect model analysis was adopted. The results showed that the length of hospital stay in the amiodarone treatment group was lower than that in the control group (MD=−3.81, 95%CI: −4.02~−3.59, P < 0.01), the difference was statistically significant, as shown in Fig. 9. The funnel plot was used to test publication bias, and it was found that all the studies were within the funnel and showed symmetry, so there was no publication bias among the included literature, as shown in Fig. 4F.

Discussion

With the continuous development of social economy, people gradually pay attention to their own health while pursuing economic income. Therefore, the time limit for the rescue of critical diseases and preventive treatment has gradually been widely concerned, which has a great impact on the traditional first aid mode. Long-term chronic myocardial ischemia caused by acute myocardial infarction will lead to adverse clinical outcomes, such as fatal arrhythmia [25–27]. Therefore, in order to significantly reduce the mortality of acute myocardial infarction, it is necessary to proactively prevent life-threatening arrhythmias. Therefore, in this study, we aimed to explore the clinical effect of amiodarone in pre-hospital emergency treatment of myocardial infarction.

The results of this meta-analysis showed that compared with the control group, amiodarone treatment could reduce the incidence of malignant arrhythmia (P < 0.01), the mean number of defibrillations in patients during treatment was also decreased (P < 0.01), and the success rate of rescue of patients was improved (P < 0.01). In addition, the recurrence rate of myocardial infarction in patients was significantly reduced (P < 0.01), and the incidence of adverse reactions in patients after treatment was reduced (P = 0.02), and the length of patients’ hospital stay was reduced with the improvement of therapeutic efficacy (P < 0.01). Lidocaine is an antiarrhythmic drug widely used in the treatment of ventricular arrhythmias, especially in patients with acute myocardial infarction or cardiac surgery [28]. However, the effect of this drug is relatively slow and there are many side effects, so amiodarone is now recommended for pre-hospital emergency treatment of myocardial infarction. Trials of amiodarone in the treatment of myocardial infarction conducted in Europe and Canada [29] have proved that amiodarone, as an antiarrhythmic drug, can effectively reduce arrhythmia mortality without affecting cardiac mortality. The improvement of therapeutic effect may be due to the fact that amiodarone has a variety of electrophysiological effects and can block multiple channels, including the fast component of delayed rectifier potassium current, the slowly activated delayed rectifier K + current, ICa-L and INa channels and receptors [30, 31]. These effects enable amiodarone to effectively prolong the action potential duration and inhibit cardiac hyperexcitability, thereby significantly reducing the occurrence of malignant arrhythmias, especially ventricular arrhythmias such as ventricular fibrillation and premature ventricular contractions. In the emergency treatment of acute myocardial infarction, amiodarone, as an antiarrhythmic drug, not only improves the cardiac rhythm state, but also effectively prevents the recurrence of arrhythmia and reduces the risk of death.

Amiodarone has significant advantages over traditional antiarrhythmic drugs such as lidocaine and propafenone. Lidocaine exerts its antiarrhythmic effects through inhibiting sodium channels and is particularly effective in ventricular arrhythmias, but its advantage of faster onset of action is accompanied by a higher risk of toxic side effects, especially in the central nervous system, such as tremor and dizziness [30, 31]. Compared to amiodarone, lidocaine is less effective in controlling other types of arrhythmias (e.g., atrial fibrillation) and has a wider range of side effects [28]. Propafenone, on the other hand, is a dual inhibitor of sodium and potassium channels, and although it has therapeutic effects on many types of arrhythmias, its use in patients with acute myocardial infarction is more cautious because propafenone may exacerbate the ischemic state of the infarcted area, which may lead to serious adverse effects, especially in patients with depressed left ventricular function [7]. Therefore, the application of amiodarone in patients with acute myocardial infarction not only effectively reduces the occurrence of malignant arrhythmia, but also reduces the incidence of other adverse reactions, improves the success rate of resuscitation, and helps to shorten the length of hospitalization, all of which establish its strong utility for its widespread use in prehospital emergency care.

The shortcomings of this paper are as follows: (i) Although the included literature met the requirements of Meta-analysis, the allocation blinding, the investigator blinding, and the measurement blind method were either unreported or not implemented in most studies, which may compromise the quality of the literature. (ii) Begg’s and Egger’s test showed that there was potential publication bias for certain outcomes, such as the success rate of salvage. Therefore, it is necessary to further select higher quality literature for supplementary analysis. (iii) Although this study was a meta-analysis, the overall study sample size was still small, especially in the analysis of individual outcome indicators (e.g., incidence of adverse reactions, length of hospitalization, etc.), which may be subject to a certain degree of bias. Moreover, the ‘average number of defibrillations’ outcome in this study exhibited high heterogeneity (I² = 90%). We initially planned to further explore the sources of this heterogeneity through subgroup analysis and meta-regression. However, during the process of extracting data from the included studies, it was found that most studies did not provide critical stratification information, such as the timing of defibrillation initiation, defibrillation method, amiodarone dosage, timing of medication administration, and history of prior arrhythmias. Consequently, it was not possible to perform subgroup stratification or construct a meta-regression model based on the available data from the literature. This limitation somewhat restricts our ability to further interpret the heterogeneity and also highlights the need for future related studies to report patient baseline characteristics, intervention implementation details, and key covariates more systematically. Such improvements would support more in-depth heterogeneity analysis and reliable stratified conclusions in subsequent systematic reviews. Additionally, future studies should further increase sample sizes and optimize study designs to validate our findings and enhance the stability and generalizability of the results.

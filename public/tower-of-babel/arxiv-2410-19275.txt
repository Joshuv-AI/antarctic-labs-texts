Finite Temperature Casimir Effect of Scalar Field
Liang Chen, Yu-Jing Wang, Sheng-Yan Li

Abstract
We derive analytic expressions for the Helmholtz free energy, Casimir force, and Casimir entropy for both one-dimensional and three-dimensional scalar fields with Dirichlet boundary conditions at finite temperature. We investigate the negative Casimir entropy problem in these systems, as well as for a scalar field in the bulk of a three-dimensional sphere, and find that this issue arises under different regularization prescriptions with differing counterterms. We argue against introducing any counterterms for the thermal corrections to the Casimir effect and predict that the thermal-fluctuation-induced Casimir force becomes repulsive in the high-temperature regime — for instance, when a ​ T / ( π ​ ℏ ​ v ) > 0.2419 aT/(\pi\hbar{v})>0.2419 for three-dimensional scalar fields.

The uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics dictates that a harmonic oscillator in any quantum state cannot achieve absolute rest, possessing instead a nonzero ground state energy. This fundamental principle extends naturally to quantum field theory, where a free-space quantum field can be decomposed into a collection of harmonic oscillators characterized by distinct quantum numbers. Without regularization, the vacuum (ground state) energy of such a field exhibits divergence. In this study, we revisit the longstanding problem of the finite-temperature Casimir effect for both three-dimensional (3D) and one-dimensional (1D) scalar fields with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Our approach yields exact analytical expressions for the Helmholtz free energy, enabling rigorous evaluation of two commonly employed regularization schemes: (a) subtraction of free-space thermal fluctuations and (b) removal of divergent high-temperature terms. Our exact results reveal that both these two types of counterterms are inherently inconsistent, leading to the emergence of negative Casimir entropy in these regularization prescriptions.

We begin by reviewing the finite-temperature Casimir effect for electromagnetic (EM) fields confined between ideal metal plates. Crucially, the transverse electric (TE) and transverse magnetic (TM) modes obey distinct boundary conditions: while TE modes satisfy Dirichlet conditions, TM modes require special treatment of the
k_{z}=0

\displaystyle F_{\textrm{EM}}=-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar{c}}{360a^{3}}-\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{lT^{2}}{\hbar{c}a}\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-l\pi{\hbar}c/aT}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{T^{3}}{\pi\hbar^{2}c^{2}}\textrm{Li}_{3}\left(e^{-l\pi{\hbar}c/aT}\right)\right]-\frac{\zeta(3)T^{3}}{2\pi\hbar^{2}c^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}aT^{4}}{45\hbar^{3}c^{3}},

3.

The penultimate term:
k_{z}=0

4.

The last term: the counterterm from free-space thermal radiation energy.

Notably, the

Further investigations show that the Casimir entropy has the following asymptotic expressions,

\displaystyle S\left(\frac{aT}{\hbar{c}}\rightarrow 0\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{3\zeta(3)}{2\pi\hbar^{2}{c^{2}}}T^{2}-\frac{4\pi^{2}}{45\hbar^{3}{c^{3}}}aT^{3},

(2)

\displaystyle S\left(\frac{aT}{\hbar{c}}\rightarrow\infty\right)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\zeta(3)}{8\pi{a^{2}}}.

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{t^{2}}}-v^{2}\nabla^{2}\right)\phi(\bm{r},t)=0,

(4)

E_{n}(\bm{k}_{\parallel},l)=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar{v}\sqrt{k_{\parallel}^{2}+\left(\frac{l\pi}{a}\right)^{2}}.

(5)

\phi(\bm{r}_{\parallel},0,t)=\phi(\bm{r}_{\parallel},a,t)=0,

(6)

The integer

Z_{3D}=\prod_{l,\bm{k}_{\parallel}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-{E_{n}(\bm{k}_{\parallel},l)/T}},

(7)

From this, we derive the Helmholtz free energy per unit area through the standard relation
F=-T\log(Z)

\displaystyle F_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k_{\parallel}dk_{\parallel}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{2}v\hbar\sqrt{k_{\parallel}^{2}+\left(\frac{l\pi}{a}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+T\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{k_{\parallel}dk_{\parallel}}{2\pi}\log\left[1-e^{-\beta{v\hbar}\sqrt{k_{\parallel}^{2}+(l\pi/a)^{2}}}\right].

(8)

The evaluation of this expression proceeds as follows: The first term is regularized using the Riemann zeta-function technique; For the second term, we perform the variable substitution,
u=\sqrt{k_{\parallel}^{2}+(l\pi/a)^{2}}
, yielding,

F_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=-\frac{{\pi^{2}}v\hbar}{1440a^{3}}+\frac{T}{4\pi}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\int_{\left(\frac{l\pi}{a}\right)^{2}}^{\infty}\log\left(1-e^{-\frac{v\hbar}{T}\sqrt{u}}\right)du.

(9)

The integration in the second term of Eq. (
9
) can be expressed in terms of the polylogarithm function
\textrm{Li}_{n}(z)
,

\frac{a^{3}}{v\hbar}F_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=-\frac{{\pi^{2}}}{1440}-\frac{\pi^{2}\theta^{3}}{2}\left(1-\frac{\partial}{\partial{s}}\right)\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{Li}_{3}\left(e^{-sl/\theta_{3}}\right)\Big|_{s=1},

(10)

where we introduce the dimensionless temperature parameter
\theta_{3}=aT/{\pi}v\hbar
. The Casimir thermodynamic quantities like Casimir force (
P=-\partial{F}/\partial{a}
) and Casimir entropy (
S=-\partial{F}/\partial{T}
) per unit area are then obtained by direct differentiation,

\displaystyle\frac{a^{4}}{v\hbar}P_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi^{2}}{480}-\frac{\pi^{2}\theta_{3}}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l^{2}\log\left(1-e^{-l/\theta_{3}}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle{a^{2}}S_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3\pi}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left[\theta_{3}^{2}\mathrm{Li}_{3}(e^{-l/\theta_{3}})+l\theta_{3}\mathrm{Li}_{2}(e^{-l/\theta_{3}})\right.

(12)

\displaystyle\left.-\frac{l^{2}}{3}\log(1-e^{-l/\theta_{3}})\right].

Having derived the exact expressions, we now examine their asymptotic behavior in two characteristic limits.
For the low-temperature regime where
\theta_{3}=aT/\pi\hbar{v}\ll 1
applies, the leading-order expansion of the polylogarithm function,

\mathrm{Li}_{3}(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{k}}{k^{n}},

yields simplified forms for the thermodynamic quantities. The Helmholtz free energy, Casimir force, and Casimir entropy reduces to,

\displaystyle F_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{{\pi^{2}}\hbar{v}}{1440a^{3}}\left[1+720\left(\frac{aT}{\pi{\hbar}v}\right)^{2}e^{-\pi{\hbar{v}}/aT}\right],

(14)

\displaystyle P_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{{\pi^{2}}\hbar{v}}{480a^{4}}\left(1-240\frac{aT}{\pi\hbar{v}}e^{-\pi{\hbar{v}}/aT}\right),

\displaystyle S_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\rightarrow 0)

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\pi}{2a^{2}}\left(1+\frac{2aT}{\pi\hbar{v}}\right)e^{-\pi{\hbar}v/aT}.

(16)

In the opposite high-temperature limit (

S_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\gg\pi\hbar{v})\approx\frac{\zeta(3)}{16\pi a^{2}}-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi\hbar^{2}v^{2}}T^{2}+\frac{2\pi^{2}}{45\hbar^{3}v^{3}}aT^{3}.

(17)

Correspondingly, the Helmholtz free energy can be approximated by the following expression,

\displaystyle F_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\gg\pi\hbar{v})\approx-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar{v}}{1440a^{3}}-\frac{\zeta(3)}{16\pi a^{2}}T

\displaystyle+\frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi\hbar^{2}v^{2}}T^{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}a}{90\hbar^{3}v^{3}}T^{4},

(18)

and generates the temperature-dependent Casimir force given by,

P_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}(aT\gg\pi\hbar{v})\approx-\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar{v}}{480a^{4}}-\frac{\zeta(3)}{8\pi a^{3}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{90\hbar^{3}v^{3}}T^{4}.

(19)

The high-temperature asymptotic expressions (Eqs. (

aT\gg\pi{\hbar}v
), the Casimir force becomes repulsive and assumes a distance-independent value given by,

P_{3D}^{(\mathrm{I})}\approx\frac{\pi^{2}}{90\hbar^{3}v^{3}}T^{4}.

(20)

We now examine how different regularization prescriptions affect the Casimir entropy. The
aT^{4}
term in Eq. (
18

a\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{free}}=a\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{\beta}\log\left(1-e^{-\beta{v\hbar}k}\right)\right]=-\frac{\pi^{2}{aT^{4}}}{90v^{3}\hbar^{3}}.

(21)

A common regularization approach introduces a counterterm to subtract this free-space contribution, redefining the Helmholtz free energy as,

F_{3D}^{(\textrm{II})}=F_{3D}^{(\textrm{I})}-a\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{free}}.

(22)

While this prescription eliminates the distance-independent repulsive force in the high-temperature limit (
aT/\pi\hbar{v}\gg 1

17

S_{3D}^{(\mathrm{II})}(aT\gg\pi\hbar{v})\approx\frac{\zeta(3)}{16\pi a^{2}}-\frac{3\zeta(3)}{4\pi\hbar^{2}v^{2}}T^{2}<0,

(23)

resulting in negative Casimir entropy at all temperatures, not just in the high-temperature regime.
Reference

proposed a modified prescription that additionally subtracts

and
T^{2}

F_{3D}^{(\textrm{III})}=F_{3D}^{(\textrm{I})}-a\mathcal{F}_{\textrm{free}}-\frac{\zeta(3)}{4\pi\hbar^{2}v^{2}}T^{3}.

(24)

This alternative approach yields positive entropy that saturates at
\zeta(3)/16\pi a^{2}
in the high-temperature limit, mimicking the EM field case. However, the critical question remains: Is this subtraction of
T^{4}
,
T^{3}
, and
T^{2}
terms universally valid? To test this, we analyze the scalar field in a 3D spherical geometry.

III
Scalar Field in three-dimensional sphere

For a massless scalar field confined within a spherical cavity of radius

under Dirichlet boundary conditions, the field quantization yields:

\hat{\phi}(\bm{r},t)=\sum_{l,s}\left[\hat{a}_{l,s}e^{-i\omega_{l,s}{t}}\psi_{l,s}(\bm{r})+\hat{a}_{l,s}^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{l,s}{t}}\psi^{*}_{l,s}(\bm{r})\right],

\psi_{l,s}(\bm{r})=j_{l}(ka)Y_{l,m}(\vartheta,\varphi)/\sqrt{N_{l,s}}

-\frac{v^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}r^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial{r}}-\frac{L^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}\right)\psi_{l,s}(\bm{r})=v^{2}k_{l,s}^{2}\psi_{l,s}(\bm{r}).

indexes the zeros of the spherical Bessel function,

j_{l}(k_{l,s}a)=0.

(27)

The corresponding eigenfrequencies are
\omega_{l,s}=vk_{l,s}

The Hamiltonian diagonalizes to:

\hat{H}=\sum_{l,s}\frac{\hbar{v}k_{l,s}}{2}\left(\hat{a}_{l,s}\hat{a}_{l,s}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}_{l,s}^{\dagger}\hat{a}_{l,s}\right),

(28)

leading to the Helmholtz free energy decomposition:

F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}=-T\log\left[\prod_{l,s}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar{v}k_{l,s}}\right],

(29)

F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}=F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}(T=0)+{\delta}F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})},

where the quantum fluctuation of the scalar field inside the sphere takes the standard form,

F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}(T=0)=\frac{\hbar{v}}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(2l+1)k_{l,s}.

(30)

Different investigations show that

Our primary interest lies in the thermal fluctuation component,

{\delta}F_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}=T\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(2l+1)\log(1-e^{-\hbar{v}k_{l,s}/T}).

Using the dimensionless temperature,
\theta_{3}=aT/\pi\hbar{v}

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{sphere}}^{(\textrm{I})}=-\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}(2l+1)\log(1-e^{-\rho_{l,s}/\pi\theta_{3}})

Consider a massless scalar field in one spatial dimension (Fig.
4
) governed by the wave equation:

\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{t}^{2}}-v^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x}^{2}}\right)\phi(x,t)=0,

(36)

where
v
denotes the field propagation velocity. Under Dirichlet boundary conditions,
\phi(0,t)=\phi(a,0)=0
, the field quantization yields discrete eigenenergies:

E_{n}(l)=\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar{v}\frac{l\pi}{a},

(37)

where
n=0,1,2,...
represents the particle number index, while
l=1,2,3,...
quantizes the wavevector as
k=l\pi/a
.
The finite-temperature partition function evaluates to:

Z=\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-{E_{n}(l)/T}}=\prod_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(-{l\pi{v}\hbar}/{2aT}\right)}{1-\exp\left(-{l\pi{v}\hbar}/{aT}\right)},

(38)

from which we derive the Helmholtz free energy
F=-T\log(Z)
:

F_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\left\{\frac{l\pi{v}\hbar}{2a}+T\log\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{l\pi{v}\hbar}{aT}\right)\right]\right\}.

(39)

Applying Riemann
\zeta
-function regularization (
\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l=\zeta(-1)=-1/12
) to the first term in Eq. (

) gives,

F_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=-\frac{\pi{v}\hbar}{24a}+T\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\log\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{l\pi{v}\hbar}{aT}\right)\right],

(40)

while the second term summation produces,

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\log\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{l\pi{v}\hbar}{aT}\right)\right]=\frac{\pi{v}\hbar}{24aT}+\log\left[\eta\left(\frac{iv\hbar}{2aT}\right)\right],

(41)

in terms of the Dedekind
\eta
-function defined on the upper half complex plane (
\mathrm{Im}[z]>0
):

\eta(z)=q^{1/24}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}),~~~(q=e^{2i\pi{z}}).

(42)

This function possesses the modular transformation property:

\eta(z)=\sqrt{\frac{i}{z}}\eta\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right).

(43)

Remarkably, substituting Eq. (
41
) into Eq. (
40
) leads to exact cancellation of the
a^{-1}
terms, yielding the simplified Helmholtz free energy:

F_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=T\log\left[\eta\left(\frac{iv\hbar}{2aT}\right)\right].

(44)

The Casimir force
P=-\partial{F}/\partial{a}
and the Casimir entropy
S=-\partial{F}/\partial{T}
can be calculated directly through differentiation,

\displaystyle P_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=\frac{iv\hbar}{2a^{2}}\frac{\eta^{\prime}\left({iv\hbar}/{2aT}\right)}{\eta\left({iv\hbar}/{2aT}\right)},

(45)

\displaystyle S_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=-\log\left[\eta\left(\frac{iv\hbar}{2aT}\right)\right]+\frac{iv\hbar}{2aT}\frac{\eta^{\prime}\left({iv\hbar}/{2aT}\right)}{\eta\left({iv\hbar}/{2aT}\right)}.

(46)

Introducing the dimensionless temperature
\theta_{1}={aT}/{v\hbar}
, we obtain the compact forms:

\displaystyle\frac{a}{v\hbar}F_{1D}^{(\mathrm{I})}=\theta_{1}\log\left[\eta\left(\frac{i}{2\theta_{1}}\right)\right],

(47)

V
Summary

We have systematically investigated the finite-temperature Casimir effect for scalar fields in one and three spatial dimensions. For the 3D scalar field confined between parallel plates (
0<z<a
) with Dirichlet boundary conditions, our analysis shows that:

1.

Using only the free-space thermal radiation energy as a counterterm leads to negative Casimir entropy.

2.

Including additional
T^{3}
and
T^{2}
terms in the counterterm prescription restores positive entropy, with the high-temperature limit approaching
\zeta(3)/(16\pi{a^{2}})
.

3.

For the spherical geometry, high-precision numerical calculations reveal that even this extended prescription fails, yielding a negative high-temperature entropy limit (
\sim-0.12
).

In the 1D case with exact solutions, all four tested counterterm schemes (Table
1
) produced negative entropy in some temperature regimes. These findings extend previous studies of negative entropy
[
58
,
59
,
60
,
61
]
, demonstrating that even for simple Dirichlet boundary conditions with exact solutions, conventional counterterm approaches face fundamental challenges.

Our results raise important questions about standard regularization prescriptions:

1.

Counterterms, while mathematically useful for removing divergences, may lack physical justification when finite results exist without them.

2.

The persistent negative entropy problem suggests deeper issues in thermal Casimir physics that warrant re-examination of standard approaches like the Lifshitz formula and quantum field theory approach.

Notably, when avoiding problematic counterterms (prescriptions used in this work), our theory predicts a sign reversal of the Casimir force at
\theta=aT/\pi{c}{\hbar}{\gtrsim}0.2419
. For
T=300\mathrm{~K}
and
v=c
, this corresponds to
a{\gtrsim}5.8\mathrm{~{\mu}m}
— within reach of current state-of-art experimental capabilities
[
62
]
. This repulsive regime, where thermal radiation pressure dominates quantum fluctuations, provides a testable signature of our approach.

Continuous-Time SO(3) Forecasting with Savitzky–Golay Neural Controlled Differential Equations
Lennart Bastian 1,2* Mohammad Rashed 1,2* Nassir Navab 1,2 Tolga Birdal 3 1 Technical University of Munich 2 Munich Center of Machine Learning 3 Imperial College London

Abstract
Tracking and forecasting the rotation of objects is fundamental in computer vision and robotics, yet S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) extrapolation remains challenging as (1) sensor observations can be noisy and sparse, (2) motion patterns can be governed by complex dynamics, and (3) application settings can demand long-term forecasting. This work proposes modeling continuous-time rotational object dynamics on S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(3) using Neural Controlled Differential Equations guided by Savitzky-Golay paths. Unlike existing methods that rely on simplified motion assumptions, our method learns a general latent dynamical system of the underlying object trajectory while respecting the geometric structure of rotations. Experimental results on real-world data demonstrate compelling forecasting capabilities compared to existing approaches.

Introduction

Predicting the 3D rotation of objects is essential for numerous robotics applications, including object manipulation, grasping, and autonomous navigation. Tracking scenarios frequently involve temporary occlusions and detection failures, making accurate forecasting of rotational motion important for maintaining consistent object states.

A major challenge lies in representing the non-Euclidean structure of the rotation manifold
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, where even simple object motions can exhibit complex behavior
Borisov and Mamaev 2018
.
Unlike translational tracking in Euclidean space, rotational tracking must account for the non-linear nature of rotational motion governed by objects’ moment of inertia.
This becomes challenging when tracking systems produce noisy measurements due to occlusions or detection failures
Fan et al. 2025
;
Park et al. 2008
;
Gugushvili and Klaassen 2012
.

We propose Savitzy Golay neural Controlled Differential Equations (SG-nCDEs) for rotational forecasting by modeling object dynamics through a latent dynamical system that respects the manifold structure of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
.
Unlike previous methods that rely on simplified assumptions like constant velocity or minimal torque
Mur-Artal et al. 2015
, our approach can predict rotational trajectories for complex object dynamics from noisy, sparse observations.
Our proposed method addresses existing limitations through a geometry-aware neural network that learns a continuous-time path in
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, enabling long-term forecasting under noisy conditions.
This supports advancements across multiple domains from robotic manipulation
Marturi et al. 2019
;

, autonomous navigation
Ettinger et al. 2021
, and tracking with sensor fusion
Fan et al. 2025
;

Our contributions include:

•

A general data-driven approach for modeling
\mathrm{SO}(3)
object dynamics by integrating a latent dynamical system with neural controlled differential equations (nCDEs).

•

Leveraging a robust integration control signal on the Lie group
\mathrm{SO}(3)
that handles noisy input states while respecting the geometric structure of rotations.

•

Experimental validation on real-world tracking data demonstrate superior performance, particularly in challenging scenarios with sensor noise and occlusions.

Related Works

We review relevant literature on
\mathrm{SO}(3)
filtering for tracking, rotation representations, and trajectory forecasting methods.

\mathrm{SO}(3)
Filtering for Tracking

Robustly filtering rotational measurements is essential for tracking applications from video stabilization
Jia and Evans 2014
to robot localization
Tang et al. 2019
;
Wong et al. 2020
;
Talbot et al. 2024
and sensor fusion
Nirmal et al. 2016
;
Busam et al. 2017
.
Filtering approaches using splines
Kim et al. 1995
;
Lovegrove et al. 2013
;
Haarbach et al. 2018
must preserve manifold properties of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
to avoid numerical errors
Busam et al. 2017
, but their solutions cannot be computed in closed form
Sommer et al. 2020
.
Without explicit assumptions, splines diverge near interpolation boundaries
Persson et al. 2021
;
Sommer et al. 2020
, leading many tracking systems to extrapolate with constraints such as constant velocity or minimal torque
Mur-Artal et al. 2015
.

Savitzky-Golay filtering on
\mathrm{SO}(3)
offers an efficient alternative by regressing polynomials in the tangent space and mapping back to the manifold via the exponential map
Jongeneel and Saccon 2022
.
This enables simultaneous angular velocity and acceleration estimation, recently exemplified for hand tracking
Fan et al. 2025
.
Other data-driven methods like Gaussian Processes
Lang et al. 2014
;
Lang and Hirche 2017
;
Lilge and Barfoot 2024
offer principled uncertainty quantification but have limited representational capacity compared to deep neural networks and require careful kernel design for
\mathrm{SO}(3)
.

Rotation Representations

Learning 3D rotations presents unique challenges.
Classical approaches like Euler angles
Kundu et al. 2018
or quaternions
Zhao et al. 2020
suffer from singularities or topological constraints that hinder direct regression
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Brégier 2021
;
Geist et al. 2024
and backpropagation
Chen et al. 2022
;
Teed and Deng 2021
.
This stems from
\mathrm{SO}(3)
not being homeomorphic to any subset of 4D Euclidean space
Zhou et al. 2019
.
Recent work demonstrates that these limitations significantly impact downstream tasks
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Levinson et al. 2020
;
Geist et al. 2024
, motivating higher-dimensional representations like 6D
Zhou et al. 2019
, 9D
Levinson et al. 2020
, and 10D
Peretroukhin et al. 2020
parameterizations with better numerical stability.

Trajectory Forecasting

Neural Ordinary Differential Equations (neural ODEs) solve initial value problems using neural networks
Chen et al. 2018
, with extensions like GRU-ODEs
Rubanova et al. 2019
;
De Brouwer et al. 2019
incorporating sequential data more effectively.
Neural Controlled Differential Equations (Neural CDEs)
Kidger et al. 2020
enable more flexible conditioning by integrating the hidden state with a smooth signal.

Most forecasting approaches for tracking focus on predicting Euclidean position, with rotational forecasting receiving less attention despite its importance for complete 6D pose tracking.
Some existing works
Byravan and Fox 2017
address transformation prediction directly from point cloud sequences, but do not address longer time horizons.
Others assume conservational motion which is inherently limiting
Mason et al. 2023
.

Our approach addresses these limitations by learning to forecast rotational trajectories directly from noisy pose data while respecting the geometric structure of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, and avoiding restrictive assumptions on the underlying trajectory.

Preliminaries

SO(3) Representation

While positions and rotations of tracked objects each have three degrees of freedom, the space of rotations forms a non-commutative Lie group, making rotational tracking inherently more complex.
We begin by introducing key background and definitions.

Definition 1

(Special Orthogonal Group)
.

The special orthogonal group
\mathrm{SO}(3)
represents the set of all rotations about the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\mathrm{SO}(3)=\left\{\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}:\mathbf{R}^{\top}\mathbf{R}=\mathbf{I},\operatorname{det}(\mathbf{R})=1\right\}

(1)

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3)
, the tangent space at the identity of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, consists of real
3\times 3
skew-symmetric matrices.

Definition 2

(Hat Operator)
.

Any
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be associated with an element
\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
via the hat operator:

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=S(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega_{3}&\omega_{2}\\
\omega_{3}&0&-\omega_{1}\\
-\omega_{2}&\omega_{1}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}

(2)

The time evolution of a rotation matrix
R(t)
can be parameterized by
\dot{R}=R\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
for angular velocity
\boldsymbol{\omega}

Jongeneel and Saccon 2022
, with
\mathfrak{so}(3)
isomorphic to the tangent space
T_{\mathbf{R}}\mathrm{SO}(3)
at
\mathbf{R}

Lee et al. 2017
.

Our proposed rotational forecasting approach uses a neural CDE integrated with respect to a control signal on the manifold

, constructed using a Savitzky-Golay filter that provides robust denoising while respecting the geometric structure of rotations. An overview can be seen in

4.1
Neural Controlled Differential Equations

Definition 3

(Discrete Trajectories in
\mathrm{SO}(3)
)
.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\{(t_{k},\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k})\}_{k=0}^{N}
be a sequence of observations of an object at discrete time points
t_{k}
, where
\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
represents a noisy rotational measurement:

\mathcal{T}=\{(t_{0},\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{0}),(t_{1},\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{1}),\ldots,(t_{N},\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{N})\}\subset\mathbb{R}\times\mathrm{SO}(3)

Such a sequence can be obtained from a system tracking object pose.
We aim to forecast the object’s rotational trajectory during occlusions or for future time points
\hat{t}>t_{N}
.

Definition 4

(Neural CDE
Kidger et al. 2020
)
.

Let
X:[t_{0},t_{n}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{v+1}
be a control path, where
X_{t_{i}}=(t_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i})
.
A Neural CDE is defined as:

\mathbf{z}_{t}=\mathbf{z}_{t_{0}}+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}f_{\theta}(\mathbf{z}_{s})\mathrm{d}X_{s}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for }t\in(t_{0},t_{n}]

(3)

where
\mathbf{z}_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{w}
is the hidden state,
f_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}^{w}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{w\times(v+1)}
is a neural network, and
\mathbf{z}_{t_{0}}=\zeta_{\theta}(t_{0},\mathbf{x}_{0})
is the initial condition encoded by a network
\zeta_{\theta}
.

While existing approaches construct control paths using cubic splines
Kidger et al. 2020
or Hermite splines
Morrill et al. 2022
, these methods neither respect the geometric structure of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
nor handle tracking noise effectively.

4.2
Robust SO(3) Savitzky-Golay Regression

To better address noisy scenarios, we propose constructing an integration path using Savitzky-Golay filtering on
\mathrm{SO}(3)

Jongeneel and Saccon 2022
, which regresses a polynomial in the Lie algebra and maps it back to the manifold via the exponential map.
Such a control path can then be used to integrate the neural CDE in
Eq.

3
during training and inference.

Definition 5

(Lie Algebra Polynomial)
.

We define a second-order polynomial in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3)
as:

p(t;\boldsymbol{\rho}):=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1}t+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}t^{2}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)

(4)

where
\boldsymbol{\rho}=[\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0};\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1};\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2}]\in\mathbb{R}^{9}
are polynomial coefficients.

Definition 6

(Control Path on
\mathrm{SO}(3)
)
.

Given observations
\mathcal{T}
, we define our smooth control path
\varphi(t)\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
as:

\varphi(t)=\mathrm{Exp}(p(t-t_{k};\boldsymbol{\rho}_{k}))\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}

(5)

where
t_{k}
is the anchor point time,
\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
is the corresponding measurement,
\mathrm{Exp}:\mathfrak{so}(3)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)
is the exponential map, and
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{k}
represents polynomial coefficients at time
t_{k}
.

Definition 7

(Savitzky-Golay Filtering on
\mathrm{SO}(3)
)
.

The optimal polynomial coefficients
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{k}
at time
t_{k}
are:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{k}=\arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}\sum_{m=-n}^{n}\|

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k+m}\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}^{-1})

\displaystyle-p(t_{k+m}-t_{k};\boldsymbol{\rho}))\|^{2}

(6)

where
2n+1
is the window size, and
\mathrm{Log}:\mathrm{SO}(3)\to\mathfrak{so}(3)
maps rotation differences to the Lie algebra.

The solution reduces to the following linear system:

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{k}=(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{b}

(7)

where
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{3(2n+1)\times 9}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{3(2n+1)}
are constructed from time and rotation differences.

To address boundary artifacts that can hinder forecasting
Schmid et al. 2022
, we propose a (learned) weighted formulation:

Lemma 8

(Weighted Savitzky-Golay Optimization)
.

The weighted polynomial coefficients can be computed as:

\boldsymbol{\rho}=(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{A})^{-1}\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{W}\mathbf{b}

(8)

where
\mathbf{W}
contains learnable parameters updated through gradient descent.

4.3
Learning with SO(3) SG Neural-CDEs

The Savitzky-Golay filter provides a smooth, bounded path on the SO(3) manifold that respects its geometric structure while effectively filtering measurement noise.
We can now integrate the differential equation in
Definition

4
with the control path constructed in
Lemma

8
.
The resulting SO(3) SG-nCDEs are a robust family of learned continuous-time extrapolation methods, approximating unknown object motion more generally than previous works.

Learning Rotational Kinematics

Consistent with the literature
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Brégier 2021
;
Geist et al. 2024
, we use the 9D rotational derivatives
\dot{R}=R\hat{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
from
Section

4.2
as integration paths for the latent state in
Eq.

3
. The time-evolved hidden state is projected into the 6D representation
Zhou et al. 2019
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{r_{k}}=\left(\mathbf{\nu}_{1},\mathbf{\nu}_{2}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 2}

(9)

From this representation, we recover the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
prediction via Gram-Schmidt orthonormalization. During inference, we evolve the differential equation in
Eq.

3
forward in time for all future points
k\in[t_{N+1},t_{N+m}]
, and regularize with respect to the ground truth.

Definition 9

(Learning Objective)
.

We optimize the latent parameters
\theta
of the neural CDE by minimizing the Frobenius norm between predicted and ground-truth rotations:

\arg\min_{\theta}\sum_{k=1}^{m}||\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{k}-\mathbf{x_{k}}||_{F}

(10)

where
\mathbf{\hat{y}}_{k}\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
is the rotation matrix recovered from the neural CDE solution at time
t_{k}
via GSO, and
\mathbf{x_{k}}
represents the ground-truth observation.

6
Conclusion

We propose a method for forecasting rotational motion in object tracking scenarios using Savitzky-Golay Neural Controlled Differential Equations.
Our approach addresses challenging tracking problems where objects may be temporarily occluded or tracking data contains noise and inconsistencies.
By leveraging continuous-time latent dynamics and modeling the geometric structure on the manifold of rotations
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, we achieve more accurate predictions than existing methods across two real-world scenarios.

Limitations and future work

Our approach focuses on rotational forecasting in
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, but tracking often requires predicting pose in
\mathrm{SE}(3)
.
Extending our framework to the special Euclidean group
\mathrm{SE}(3)
to simultaneously predict position and orientation would be valuable for complete 6D object tracking.
However, this presents additional challenges such as filtering and modeling (or neglecting
Sommer et al. 2020
) coupled rotational motion in the Lie algebra of twists
Haarbach et al. 2018
.

Appendix A
Experimental Details

We leverage data from diverse simulated rotation trajectories to train our data-driven dynamics model.

A.1
Rotational Kinematics Background

The motion of rotating rigid bodies follows well-established physical principles from classical mechanics.
These principles enable us to simulate diverse rotational scenarios to train our method in a controlled environment.

Definition 10

(Rotational Rigid Body Dynamics)
.

The evolution of rigid body rotation is governed by:

\displaystyle\dot{R}

\displaystyle=R\hat{\omega},

(12)

\displaystyle\dot{\omega}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{J}^{-1}(\tau_{\text{ext}}-\omega\times\mathbf{J}\omega),

(13)

where
R(t)\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
is the orientation,
\omega(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the body-frame angular velocity,
\mathbf{J}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is the moment of inertia tensor characterizing the mass distribution, and
\tau_{\text{ext}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
represents external torques.

In real-world applications, strict conservation laws may not always hold due to dissipative forces and measurement noise.
We therefore design our simulation framework to include both conservative (as in
Mason et al. 2023
) and non-conservative dynamics (
\tau_{\text{ext}}\neq 0
). This includes several key scenarios:

•

Freely Rotating:
Systems with no external torques where angular momentum is conserved, but gyroscopic effects can still produce complex non-linear motion

•

Linear Control:
External torques following linear control laws, providing a testbed for simpler dynamics

•

Configuration Dependent Torque:
External torques that depend on the orientation itself, simulating physical systems like magnetic dipoles in uniform fields

•

Damped Motion:
Systems with dissipative forces that reduce angular momentum over time

To expose the models to diverse motion trajectories, we vary the moment of inertia tensor across simulations, creating rigid bodies with different principal axis orientations while maintaining similar underlying physics.
Initial conditions are sampled according to
Shoemake 1992
with rejection sampling, avoiding degenerate cases with small initial angular velocity.

To parameterize the function

Following numerical integration of the function

On the Choice of Integration Path

Various control signals for neural CDEs have been proposed; most interpolation methods use cubic or higher order splines
Kidger et al. 2020
;
Morrill et al. 2022
.
Kidger et al.
Kidger et al. 2020
construct the integration path
X\in\mathcal{C}^{2}
as a natural cubic spline over the input sequence.
While this guarantees smoothness of the first derivative, Hermite cubic splines with backward differences are preferred for CDE integration due to local control
Morrill et al. 2022
.
We observe that these choices do not obey the geometric structure of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
; furthermore, they are not robust with respect to sensor noise interpolating the points erratically (see
Fig.

4
, left).
In general, such higher-order polynomials tend to diverge near the interpolation endpoints and must be extrapolated with some assumption, for instance, constant velocity
Persson et al. 2021
.
However, when dynamics are complicated, this assumption is overly simplifying, even more so than assuming conservation of energy as in
Mason et al. 2023
.

Efficient derivative calculations have been proposed for robust
\mathrm{SO}(3)
B-spline interpolation of trajectories in
\mathrm{SO}(3)

Sommer et al. 2020
.
These can be made suitable for extrapolation with, e.g., constant velocity extrapolation at the endpoint by constructing a nonlinear optimization to minimize residuals to the measurements, with additional smoothness near the endpoint.
We implement this in Ceres
Agarwal et al. 2023
, Basalt
Usenko et al. 2018
, and Sophus
Strasdat 2011
to integrate Lie group constraints into the manifold optimization.
We use the efficient derivative computation of
Sommer et al. 2020
and an additional first-order Taylor expansion using the computed Jacobians to include a constant velocity endpoint constraint defining the splines beyond their support interval.
While the splines nicely interpolate the noisy trajectory and can be used to extrapolate (see
Fig.

4
, center), they still exhibit bias towards endpoints.
Moreover, this approach is too computationally expensive to use during network training.
On a 24-Core Intel(R) Xeon(R) CPU, a single batch of 1000 trajectories takes
\approx 22
seconds when maximally parallelized, a fraction of the trajectories we use for training.

On the other hand, the Savitzky-Golay filter provides a reasonable approximation at a low cost.
As it merely requires solving a modest linear system, this means it can be differentiated through to learn a robust weighting suitable for extrapolation (see
Fig.

4
, right).

Appendix C
Additional Background

C.1
SO(3) Savitzky-Golay Filtering
Jongeneel and Saccon 2022

The Savitzky-Golay filter on SO(3) solves a least-squares problem to estimate polynomial coefficients that best fit the noisy rotational data in the Lie algebra, as described in Definition 7.

The design matrix
\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{3(2n+1)\times 3(p+1)}
takes the form of a Vandermonde matrix with time-shifted polynomial coefficients.
It is expanded with the Kroeneker product
\otimes
and
\mathbf{I}_{3}
, the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
such that
\mathbf{A}=\hat{A}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{3}
.

\hat{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{(2n+1)\times(p+1)}
is defined as:

\hat{A}=\begin{bmatrix}1&(t_{-n}-t_{k})&\cdots&\frac{1}{p}(t_{-n}-t_{k})^{p}\\
1&(t_{-n+1}-t_{k})&\cdots&\frac{1}{p}(t_{-n+1}-t_{k})^{p}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
1&(t_{n}-t_{k})&\cdots&\frac{1}{p}(t_{n}-t_{k})^{p}\end{bmatrix}

\mathbf{b}
is constructed by rotational differences in the lie algebra and has the form:

\mathbf{b}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k-n}\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}^{-1})^{\vee}\\
\vdots\\
\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k+n}\mathbf{\tilde{x}}_{k}^{-1})^{\vee}\end{bmatrix}

(\cdot)^{\vee}:\mathfrak{so}(3)\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the inverse of the hat operator that maps skew-symmetric matrices to vectors.

This formulation brings several advantages over conventional interpolation with splines.
It filters robustly in the region of a point (with an arbitrary support window) and can be solved without an iterative optimization.
Moreover, the derivatives are smooth up to the polynomial order, allowing us to integrate the latent dynamical system of a neural CDE.

C.2
Exponential and Logarithmic Maps

Definition 11

(Exponential Map on SO(3))
.

The exponential map
\mathrm{Exp}:\mathfrak{so}(3)\to\mathrm{SO}(3)
is a surjective mapping from the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(3)
to the Lie group
\mathrm{SO}(3)
defined as:

\mathrm{Exp}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{n}=\mathbf{I}+\boldsymbol{\xi}+\frac{1}{2!}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{2}+\frac{1}{3!}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{3}+\cdots

(14)

where
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)
is a skew-symmetric matrix.

Geometrically, if
\boldsymbol{\xi}=\hat{\mathbf{v}}
for some
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
\|\mathbf{v}\|=\theta
, then
\mathrm{Exp}(\boldsymbol{\xi})
represents a rotation by angle
\theta
about the axis
\mathbf{v}/\|\mathbf{v}\|
.

Definition 12

(Logarithmic Map on SO(3))
.

The logarithmic map
\mathrm{Log}:\mathrm{SO}(3)\to\mathfrak{so}(3)
is the local inverse of the exponential map, retrieving the corresponding Lie algebra element from a rotation matrix:

\mathrm{Log}(\mathbf{R})=\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathfrak{so}(3)

(15)

such that
\mathrm{Exp}(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\mathbf{R}
. This mapping is well-defined for all
\mathbf{R}\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
where the rotation angle
\theta
satisfies
0\leq\theta<\pi
.

No major effect of age (< 40 vs. ≥ 40 years) on response to secukinumab in patients with axSpA: a post hoc analysis of six phase 3 trials
Authors: Sofia Ramiro, Corine Gaillez, Uta Kiltz, Lianne S Gensler, Chaitali Pisal, Jurgen Braun, Alexis Ogdie
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSecukinumab is an interleukin (IL)-17 inhibitor approved to treat patients with axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA), including radiographic (r)-axSpA and non-radiographic (nr)-axSpA. Treatment recommendations highlight the importance of individualized treatment of patients with axSpA according to symptoms, extra-musculoskeletal manifestations, and comorbidities. The objective of this post hoc analysis was to compare clinical responses to secukinumab treatment through Week 16 between younger and older patients with r-axSpA and nr-axSpA from six placebo-controlled phase 3 clinical studies.MethodsThis post hoc analysis evaluated data pooled from 1427 patients with axSpA from the MEASURE 1–5 trials (NCT01358175, NCT01649375, NCT02008916, NCT02159053, and NCT02896127) and the PREVENT trial (NCT02696031). All patients were randomized to secukinumab 150 mg, 300 mg, or placebo through Week 16. Patient subgroups were defined based on median age at baseline (18 to < 40 years vs. ≥ 40 years). Treatment effect (secukinumab 150/300 mg vs. placebo) within each age subgroup and the difference in treatment effect between younger vs. older age subgroups were assessed.ResultsSecukinumab treatment demonstrated clinical efficacy and improvements in patient-reported outcomes vs. placebo at Week 16 in patients with r-axSpA or nr-axSpA irrespective of age. No difference in treatment effect of ASAS20, ASAS40, or ASDAS response was observed between younger vs. older patients with r-axSpA or nr-axSpA (all P>.05). Among patients with r-axSpA, younger patients experienced a larger treatment effect of secukinumab on SF-36 than older patients (difference in least-squares mean [LSM]: 2.2; P < .05). Among patients with nr-axSpA, younger patients experienced a larger treatment effect of secukinumab on the Berlin SIJ total edema score (difference in LSM: − 1.2; P < .05) and Total ASspi-MRI-a score (LSM, − 0.4; P<.05).ConclusionClinical efficacy of secukinumab vs. placebo at Week 16 was generally similar between older and younger patients with either r-axSpA or nr-axSpA.Keywords: Secukinumab, Axial spondyloarthritis, Interleukin-17, ASAS, ASDAS

Introduction

Axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA) is a chronic, inflammatory-mediated disease that primarily involves the axial skeleton and is associated with pain, functional impairment, and reduced quality of life [1]. Patients with axSpA typically develop back pain before the age of 45 years and are frequently diagnosed between the age of 20 to 30 years [2, 3]. AxSpA can be subclassified as radiographic (r-axSpA; historically termed ankylosing spondylitis [AS]) or non-radiographic axSpA (nr-axSpA) depending on the presence or absence of definitive radiographic sacroiliitis [4–6]. Patients with axSpA, especially those with nr-axSpA, experience diagnostic delays that can result in unfavorable response to treatment, progressive damage to the spine, structural damage such as new bone formation, or fusion of the sacroiliac joints (SIJ) and spine [7–9]. Additionally, older patients are more likely to have comorbidities, such as hypertension and depression, which can negatively affect response to treatment and disease outcomes [10–12].

Treatment recommendations highlight the importance of individualized treatment of patients with axSpA and encourage the use of disease monitoring tools, including patient-reported outcomes (PROs), clinical findings, laboratory tests, and imaging [13, 14]. A series of core domains have been developed by experts and patients in order to reflect the range of signs and symptoms experienced by patients with axSpA and appropriately monitor the disease. These include measuring disease activity, usually based on composite scores or criteria, such as the Assessment of SpondyloArthritis international Society (ASAS) response criteria [15] and Axial Spondyloarthritis Disease Activity Score (ASDAS) [16, 17]. PROs from questionnaires, such as the Bath Ankylosing Spondylitis Functional Index (BASFI) and Short Form Health Survey 36 (SF-36), can assess functional ability and quality of life from the patient perspective. Lastly, imaging of the SIJ can assess axial inflammation by measuring bone marrow edema [18].

Secukinumab is an interleukin (IL)-17 inhibitor approved to treat patients with axSpA, including r-axSpA and nr-axSpA [19]; however, the clinical response to secukinumab across younger and older patients has not been previously evaluated or compared, as differences in treatment response to biologic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs (bDMARDs) in patients with axSpA may be based on age [20]. The objective of this post hoc analysis was to compare clinical responses to secukinumab treatment vs. placebo through Week 16 between younger and older patients with r-axSpA and nr-axSpA from phase 3 clinical studies.

Methods

Study design and patients

In this post hoc analysis, patients with r-axSpA were pooled from the phase 3 MEASURE 1–5 trials (NCT01358175, NCT01649375, NCT02008916, NCT02159053, and NCT02896127), and patients with nr-axSpA were included from the PREVENT trial (NCT02696031) [21–26]. Briefly, patients ≥ 18 years of age who met either the modified New York criteria [27] or the ASAS classification for axSpA [28, 29] at screening despite previous treatment with non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs, and/or tumor necrosis factor inhibitors (TNFis) were included in the original trials. Exclusion criteria for the pooled studies included a history of or active ongoing infection, total ankylosis of the spine, and previous treatment with bDMARDs except for TNFis.

All patients were randomized to secukinumab 150 mg, secukinumab 300 mg, or placebo every 4 weeks through Week 16. Patients having received either dose of secukinumab were pooled in the current analysis. Patient subgroups were defined based on the median age at baseline: 18 to < 40 and ≥ 40 years of age.

All studies included in this pooled analysis were approved by central institutional review boards and the ethics review boards at each participating center. Each study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants before study-related procedures were performed.

Assessments and outcomes

This post hoc subgroup analysis of younger and older patients with axSpA assessed several outcomes. Clinical responses included ASAS20, defined as improvement of ≥ 20% and ≥ 1 unit (on a scale of 1 to 10) in ≥ 3 of the 4 main ASAS domains, ASAS40, and measures of disease activity using ASDAS low disease activity or inactive disease (ASDAS < 2.1), ASDAS-inactive disease (ASDAS < 1.3), and ASDAS clinically important improvement (ASDAS improvement ≥ 1.1). PROs included change from baseline in the BASFI (0–10, with higher scores indicating worse function) and the SF-36 physical component summary (PCS, 0–100, with higher scores indicating better quality of life). Change from baseline in the magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) measures Berlin SIJ total edema score (0–24, higher scores indicating more inflammation), Berlin spine score (0–69, higher scores indicating more inflammation), and total Ankylosing Spondylitis spinal MRI for activity (ASspi-MRI-a) score (0–138, higher scores indicating higher disease activity) were also included in the analysis.

Statistical analysis

To determine the effect of age on treatment response, the difference in treatment effects of secukinumab vs. placebo between age subgroups was assessed for each outcome at Week 16. For binary outcomes, treatment effect and the difference in treatment effects between age subgroups were assessed by logistic regression using non-responder imputation; odds ratios (ORs) > 1 indicate a better response in younger vs. older patients. For change from baseline in BASFI and SF-36 PCS, treatment effect and the difference in treatment effects between age subgroups were assessed using mixed-model repeated measures because assessments were taken for all visits between baseline and Week 16, with baseline scores as covariates. For change from baseline in Berlin SIJ total edema score, the analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model was applied because MRI measurements were taken at baseline and Week 16, with baseline scores as covariates. For BASFI and Berlin SIJ total edema score, a negative value indicates a larger treatment effect among younger patients; for SF-36, a positive value indicates a larger treatment effect among younger patients. These models were adjusted for baseline scores of continuous measures, sex, obesity, previous TNFi experience, cardiovascular disease, uveitis, and psoriasis as covariates. For all comparisons, the older patient group was used as a reference category. All P values are nominal.

Results

Patients

This study included 1427 patients with r-axSpA from the MEASURE 1–5 trials and 555 patients with nr-axSpA from the PREVENT trial. A total of 885 and 542 patients from the MEASURE 1–5 trials were treated with secukinumab and placebo, respectively. In the PREVENT trial, 369 patients were treated with secukinumab, and 186 were treated with placebo. Table 1 describes baseline characteristics between patients with r-axSpA and nr-axSpA by age category. Among patients with r-axSpA treated with secukinumab and placebo, 460 (52.0%) and 285 (52.6%) were aged 18 to < 40 years. Among patients with nr-axSpA treated with secukinumab and placebo, 195 (52.9%) and 99 (53.2%) were aged 18 to < 40 years. Across r-axSpA and nr-axSpA, a larger proportion of older patients were female, obese, and presented with higher rates of comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease and metabolism and nutrition disorders, as well as higher rates of extra-musculoskeletal manifestations, including uveitis and psoriasis. The incidence of inflammatory bowel disease was consistent across age groups and both disease categories. Time since diagnosis increased with age, whereas the prevalence of HLA-B27 was lower in older patients across both disease categories. The proportion of TNFi-naive patients was in the range of 70% to 77% (MEASURE) and 87% to 95% (PREVENT) across age groups, according to inclusion criteria.

Efficacy outcomes

Overall, treatment with secukinumab showed efficacy vs. placebo at Week 16 in both younger and older patients, as measured by the achievement of clinical outcomes (Fig. 1). Among both younger and older patients with r-axSpA and nr-axSpA, treatment with secukinumab resulted in higher achievement of efficacy outcomes compared with placebo at Week 16. A total of 47.8% of secukinumab-treated vs. 20.7% of placebo-treated younger patients with r-AxSpA achieved ASAS40 compared with 34.1% vs. 16.0% of patients ≥ 40 years of age (Fig. 1A). In patients with nr-axSpA, 47.2% of secukinumab-treated vs. 31.3% of placebo-treated younger patients achieved ASAS40 compared with 32.8% vs. 24.1% of older patients (Fig. 1B).

Continuous outcomes

Across both age groups, secukinumab treatment demonstrated improvements from baseline in BASFI, SF-36 PCS, and Berlin SIJ total edema score vs. placebo at Week 16 in patients with r-axSpA and those with nr-axSpA (Fig. 3). No difference in secukinumab treatment effect between younger vs. older patients with r-axSpA and nr-axSpA was observed for BASFI improvement (P>.05; Fig. 4). Among patients with r-axSpA, younger patients experienced a larger treatment effect of secukinumab on SF-36 PCS than older patients (least-squares mean [LSM]: 2.2; 95% CI: 1.0 to 3.5; P<.05) at Week 16 in contrast with nr-axSpA, for which no difference in secukinumab treatment effect between younger vs. older patients was observed (P>.05; Fig. 4B). Younger patients with nr-axSpA experienced a larger secukinumab treatment effect on the Berlin SIJ total edema score (LSM: − 1.2; 95% CI: − 1.8 to − 0.5; P<.05) and total ASspi-MRI-a score (LSM: − 0.4; 95% CI: − 0.8 to 0.0; P<.05) than older patients, whereas no difference in secukinumab treatment effect in MRI measures was observed across age categories in patients with r-axSpA (P>.05) (Fig. 4B).

Discussion

In this post hoc analysis, patients aged 18 to < 40 and ≥ 40 years with either r-axSpA or nr-axSpA receiving secukinumab for 16 weeks demonstrated similar improvements in disease activity across clinical outcomes, PROs, and imaging, indicating utility of secukinumab in both younger and older patients. Among patients with nr-axSpA, the younger patient population experienced larger improvements in SIJ bone marrow edema and spinal inflammation compared with older patients; among patients with r-axSpA, younger patients had more pronounced improvements in health-related quality of life compared with older patients.

Both younger and older patients with axSpA may have unique needs and treatment goals that are specific to their disease state. The extent of comorbidities is one factor that differentiates younger and older patients, because older patients typically have a higher comorbidity burden, which is associated with higher disease activity [10]. Hypertension, diabetes mellitus, and thyroid disorders are common comorbidities that generally increase with age in patients with axSpA [30]; however, psychological distress may be more prevalent among younger patients [31]. The effect of comorbidities and disease burden on treatment response is not well understood, but limited evidence suggests that higher comorbidity burden, especially among older patients, may negatively impact successful treatment outcomes [9, 11]. However, few studies have directly addressed the effect of age on treatment response to bDMARDs, and mixed results have been reported on comparative efficacy between younger and older age groups [20].

Secukinumab is approved for the treatment of both r-axSpA and nr-axSpA and has previously demonstrated efficacy across clinical outcomes such as ASAS20, ASAS40, and ASDAS at Week 16, [22–26] but a formal evaluation of clinical response between older and younger patients had not been previously performed. In this study, clinical efficacy of secukinumab vs. placebo at Week 16 was observed among younger and older patients and in both r-axSpA and nr-axSpA, with numerically higher proportions of younger patients achieving outcomes across all domains independent of disease subclassification. Our data are consistent with that of previous studies reporting that secukinumab treatment led to rapid and sustained improvements, regardless of age or disease duration, and that older patients reported higher burden of disease [32]. Additionally, a previous post hoc analysis of pooled data from the MEASURE 1–4 trials demonstrated that achievement of ASAS20/40 was more likely to occur in younger patients treated with secukinumab than older patients [33]. However, that analysis only assessed age as a covariate predictor of ASAS20/40 achievement among the secukinumab treatment arms and did not account for the treatment effect of secukinumab vs. placebo as performed in this study. Limited studies have explored the comparison of early-age treatment options in axSpA, and the need for evidence is still evolving and requires further exploration.

Relatively high placebo response rates have been observed in several nr-axSpA trials [24, 34, 35]. Here, most placebo response rates were indeed numerically higher in nr-axSpA vs. r-axSpA and higher in younger vs. older patients. There is no single and no straightforward explanation for this phenomenon. However, when assessing the most stringent outcome measure, ASDAS-inactive disease, this difference is no longer present. The incomplete achievement of ASDAS-inactive disease also highlights that there is room for improvement. This finding warrants future analyses that investigate the possible reasons for this, which may be attributed to residual inflammation, comorbid conditions such as degenerative disease, or simply lack of physical activity.

The impact of secukinumab extended beyond clinical efficacy assessments, as patients with axSpA experienced improvements in PROs, including BASFI and SF-36 PCS outcomes. Across PROs, younger patients reported numerically better changes from baseline compared with older counterparts, but similar differences in treatment effect vs. placebo between age groups reflect consistent treatment responses. Older patients with r-axSpA experienced smaller treatment effects for SF-36. Although improvements in PROs may be influenced by multiple covariates, this analysis supports the hypothesis that treatment in younger age may lead to benefits in physical function and health-related quality of life.

Sacroiliitis is a hallmark of axSpA, and MRI has become an important tool for distinguishing and diagnosing patients with axSpA [36]. Several studies have observed that patients with nr-axSpA have later symptom onset and a higher age at diagnosis compared with patients with r-axSpA [37–39]. In the current study, younger patients with nr-axSpA experienced a larger treatment effect of secukinumab vs. placebo on the Berlin SIJ total edema score compared with older patients. Research on joint improvements among older patients with r-axSpA could be of interest because older patients are more prone to spinal damage and may have less capacity to improve [40]. Further exploration into the relationship between early treatment and clinical efficacy is warranted for this subgroup of patients because establishing a diagnosis and initiating treatment at an earlier age may have implications for treatment success, particularly concerning the development of structural damage.

The results from our study should be interpreted in the context of several limitations. Due to the design of the MEASURE 1–5 and PREVENT trials, assessing the effect of age on treatment response of secukinumab was only possible in the short term due to the 16-week placebo-controlled periods of these trials, and analysis of long-term efficacy could not be conducted. Enrollment criteria for C-reactive protein between patients with r-axSpA in the MEASURE studies and those with nr-axSpA in PREVENT limited the direct comparison of achievement of improvements between disease states. Sex and fibromyalgia characteristics were not used as baseline covariates in this analysis and may potentially influence treatment responses. Additionally, different criteria for MRI inflammation between r-axSpA and nr-axSpA limited comparison of MRI measures between disease categories. Time since symptom onset was not recorded in the studies; instead, time since diagnosis was used as a substitute for comparing relative approximate disease duration between age groups. The current analyses were limited by their post hoc nature and the lack of prespecified end points. Statistical interpretation of these data should be made with caution. Lastly, any potential heterogeneity resulting from the pooling of different studies, including different patient populations, cannot be excluded.

Conclusions

This post hoc analysis of six phase 3 clinical trials showed that secukinumab demonstrated clinical efficacy vs. placebo at Week 16 in both younger and older patients with either r-axSpA or nr-axSpA, without important differences between the age groups.

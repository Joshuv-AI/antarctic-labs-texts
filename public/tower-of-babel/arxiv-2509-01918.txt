Abstract
. We study algebraic properties of the super Jordan plane ℬ {\mathcal{B}} introduced by I. Angiono, I. Heckenberger and the first named author. Concretely we show that ℬ {\mathcal{B}} is super-prime and has a super-simple super-artinian ring of fractions. We also compute the groups of superalgebra and braided Hopf algebra automorphisms. Instrumental for our approach is the embedding of the Jordan plane as a subalgebra of ℬ {\mathcal{B}} .



Introduction

The Jordan plane

This is a quadratic algebra generated by
\mathbf{x}

and
\mathbf{y}
with defining relation

\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{2}.

It was considered in the literature in
relation with AS-regular algebras
[
9
]
,
quantum groups
[
16
,
13
,
25
,
18
,
21
]
and
the classification of pointed Hopf algebras with finite
Gelfand-Kirillov dimension (
\operatorname{GKdim}
)
[
2
,
3
]
;
indeed it is a Nichols algebra in characteristic
0
.
The name
Jordan
is due to
[
13
]
.
Algebraic properties of the Jordan plane were studied
in
[
1
,
11
,
17
,
23
,
24
]
, notice that it is a domain of
\operatorname{GKdim}

2
and a Koszul algebra.

The super Jordan plane

This is the graded algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
generated by
x,y
(in degree 1)
with two defining relations

\displaystyle x^{2}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle yz-zy-xz

\displaystyle=0,

where
z=xy+yx
. Thus, one relation is quadratic and one is cubic.
In characteristic
0
,
{\mathcal{B}}
is indeed a Nichols algebra.
It was introduced in
[
3
]
, being essential in the
classification of Nichols algebras of finite
\operatorname{GKdim}
over abelian groups. It has
\operatorname{GKdim}

2
but it is not a domain.
Further properties of
{\mathcal{B}}
were established:

\circ
The classification of the simple finite-dimensional, respectively indecomposable of dimension 2 and 3,
{\mathcal{B}}
-modules was given in
[
5
]
,
assuming
{\rm char\,}\Bbbk=0
.

\circ
The classification of the simple finite-dimensional modules over a suitable bosonization of the super Jordan plane
was given in
[
6
]
,
assuming
{\rm char\,}\Bbbk=0
.

\circ

The Hochschild homology and cohomology of
{\mathcal{B}}
were studied in
[
22
]
assuming
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=0
and
{\Bbbk}
algebraically closed.
So, in particular, the Lie algebra of derivations can be described
from
loc. cit.

\circ

The graded braided commutativity of the Hochschild cohomology
for a class of braided Hopf algebras, including
{\mathcal{B}}
,
was proved in
[
12
]
.

\circ

The Drinfeld double of a suitable bosonization of
{\mathcal{B}}

was introduced in
[
8
]
; it gives rise naturally to
a Hopf superalgebra which is an extension of a super commutative one by the enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{osp}(1|2)
.

Main results

In the present paper we provide further information on

{\mathcal{B}}
. Namely, we show that

{\mathcal{B}}
is a superalgebra, meaning that it has a
C_{2}
-grading
{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{B}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{B}}_{1}
.
Strictly speaking, one should speak of
C_{2}
-graded algebras, coalgebras,
etc., and reserve the term super to structures requiring the super
transposition like Lie algebras, Hopf algebras and so on. But
at the end of the day both settings are related, so we speak in a
uniform manner of super algebras, super ideals, etc.
Our main results are:

\bullet

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
has infinite global dimension (Corollary
1.4
);
a different proof follows from
[
22
]
.

\bullet

The zero divisors of
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}

and the homogeneous zero divisors of
{\mathcal{B}}
are determined
(Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
).

\bullet

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
can be localized at
the set
S_{0}
of its regular elements
and
Q_{0}\coloneqq{\mathcal{B}}_{0}S_{0}^{-1}
is semisimple artinian (Theorem
4.4
).

\bullet

The superalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}
is a super-prime ring (Theorem

4.8
). It has not been apparent to us whether the algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
is prime
so we let this as an open question.

\bullet

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
can be localized at the set
S
of homogeneous regular elements in
{\mathcal{B}}

and the ring of fractions
Q\coloneqq{\mathcal{B}}S^{-1}
has
a
C_{2}
-grading whose even part is
Q_{0}={\mathcal{B}}_{0}S_{0}^{-1}
.
We have
Q={\mathcal{B}}S_{0}^{-1}
. Moreover
the superalgebra
Q
is a super-simple super-artinian ring
(Theorem
4.9
).

\bullet
The group
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}{\mathcal{B}}
of automorphisms
of the superalgebra
{\mathcal{B}}
, i.e., the algebra automorphisms
that preserve the
C_{2}
-grading, is computed (Theorem
5.3
).
It turns out that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}{\mathcal{B}}\simeq N\rtimes{\Bbbk}^{\times}
,
where the automorphisms in
N
are exponentials of locally nilpotent derivations (Proposition
6.2
).

\bullet
The group of automorphisms
of the braided Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
, with the structure introduced in
[
3
, Section 3.3]
, see Section
7
, is computed assuming characteristic 0 (Proposition
8.1
).

Notations and conventions

The (commutative) base field is denoted by
{\Bbbk}
.
We set

which is assumed
\not=2
; additional constraints will be imposed accordingly.
As usual in group theory,

Let

;

If

1.
The super Jordan plane as an iterated Ore extension

Definition 1.1
.

The super Jordan plane is the algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
presented by generators
x,y
with defining relations

x^{2}=0,\quad yz-zy-xz=0,\ \text{with notation}\ z=xy+yx.

(1)

It follows from these relations that

xz=zx.

(2)

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
-graded:

{\mathcal{B}}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{B}}_{n}
, where
x
and
y

have degree
1
.
Hence
{\mathcal{B}}
is a
C_{2}
-graded algebra (or a superalgebra) by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\overline{0}}

\displaystyle=\oplus_{n\text{ even}\ }{\mathcal{B}}_{n},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}_{\overline{1}}

\displaystyle=\oplus_{n\text{ odd}\ }{\mathcal{B}}_{n}.

Notations 1.2
.

We consider the commutative algebra

A\coloneqq{\Bbbk}[x]/(x^{2}),

(3)

known as the algebra of dual numbers,
and the polynomial algebra
A[z]
. Any element
f=\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\alpha_{i}+\beta_{i}x)z^{i}\in A[z]
, where

\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in{\Bbbk}
, can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle f=

\displaystyle f^{+}+xf^{-}\text{ with}

\displaystyle f^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\alpha_{i}z^{i}\in{\Bbbk}[z],

\displaystyle f^{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N}\beta_{i}z^{i}\in{\Bbbk}[z].

(4)

The Euler derivative in the polynomial ring
{\Bbbk}[z]

is denoted by
D
; namely

\displaystyle D(f)

\displaystyle\coloneqq z\partial_{z}(f)

\displaystyle\text{for any }f

\displaystyle\in{\Bbbk}[z].

(5)

Then
\ker D={{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
, where of course
{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}={\Bbbk}
when
p=0
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
can be presented as an Ore extension.

Proposition 1.3
.

(i)

There exist an automorphism
\tau
and a
\tau
-derivation
d

of the algebra
A[z]
such that the super Jordan plane is isomorphic
to the Ore extension

A[z][y\,;\,\tau,d].

(6)

The automorphism
\tau
and the
\tau
-derivation
d
are defined by

\displaystyle\tau(x)

\displaystyle=-x,

\displaystyle\tau(z)

\displaystyle=z,

\displaystyle d(x)

\displaystyle=z,

\displaystyle d(z)

\displaystyle=xz.

(7)

(ii)

The set
\mathcal{X}=\{x^{a}z^{b}y^{c}:a\in\{0,1\},b,c\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
is a
{\Bbbk}
-basis of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Part
(ii)
was claimed without proof in
[
8
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Proof.

We define
\tau:A\to A
by
\tau(a)=\alpha-\beta x
for any
a=\alpha+\beta x
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\Bbbk}
. An easy calculation (using
x^{2}=0
) proves that
\tau(ab)=\tau(a)\tau(b)
for all
a,b\in A
. Clearly
\tau
defines an automorphism of
A
, which can be canonically extended to
A[z]
setting
\tau(z)=z
. Using the decomposition (
4
) we have

\tau(f)=f^{+}-xf^{-}\ \text{for any }f=f^{+}+xf^{-}\in A[z].

(8)

We define
d:A\to A
in two steps. First, we set

d(f)=xD(f)=D(f)x\ \text{for any }f\in{\Bbbk}[z].

(9)

Then using (
4
) we define

d(f)=xD(f^{+})+zf^{-}\ \text{for any }f=f^{+}+xf^{-}\in A[z].

(10)

If
g=g^{+}+xg^{-}\in A[z]
, we have, again because
x^{2}=0
, that

\displaystyle d(fg)

\displaystyle=d(f^{+}g^{+}+x(f^{+}g^{-}+f^{-}g^{+}))

\displaystyle=xD(f^{+}g^{+})+z(f^{+}g^{-}+f^{-}g^{+})

\displaystyle=xD(f^{+})g^{+}+xf^{+}D(g^{+})+zf^{+}g^{-}+zf^{-}g^{+}

\displaystyle=(f^{+}-xf^{-})(xD(g^{+})+zg^{-})+(xD(f^{+})+zf^{-})(g^{+}+xg^{-})

\displaystyle=\tau(f)d(g)+d(f)g.

Hence
d
is a
\tau
-derivation of
A[z]
.
Now, there exists an algebra map

\psi:{\mathcal{B}}\to A[z][y,\tau,d]
sending the generators
x
and
y
to their homonimous. It was proved in
[
3
, Proposition 3.5]

that
\mathcal{X}
linearly generates
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Clearly
\psi(\mathcal{X})
is a basis of
A[z][y,\tau,d]
,
hence
\psi
is an isomorphism and
\mathcal{X}
is a basis of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
∎

Corollary 1.4
.

The algebra
{\mathcal{B}}
is Noetherian and has infinite global dimension.

Proof.

The first claim follows from
[
15
, Theorem 2.6]
.
By Proposition
1.3

(ii)
the set
\mathcal{X}=\{x^{a}z^{b}y^{c}:a\in\{0,1\},b,c\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
is a
{\Bbbk}
-basis of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Now
\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{X}_{0}\cup\mathcal{X}_{1}
where

\mathcal{X}_{0}=\{1,x\}
and
\mathcal{X}_{1}=\{x^{a}z^{b}y^{c}:a\in\{0,1\},b,c\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},(b,c)\not=(0,0)\}
, hence

{\mathcal{B}}=A\oplus C
where
C
is the
{\Bbbk}
-span of
\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
Clearly
C
is a left
A
-module.
From the commutation relations in
{\mathcal{B}}
we see that
C
is also a right
A
-module.
Indeed, it is evident that
z^{b}x=xz^{b}
hence
z^{b}x\in C
for any
b\geq 1
.
By straightforward calculations, see

[
8
, Lemma 3.6]
, we have
y^{c}x\in C
for any
c\geq 1
.
Thus, finally,
z^{b}y^{c}x\in C
for any
z^{b}y^{c}\in\mathcal{X}_{1}
.
We deduce that
A
is an
A
-bimodule direct summand of
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Since
{\mathcal{B}}
is Noetherian and free over
A
, we conclude by

[
19
, Theorem 7.2.8]
.
∎

Remark 1.5
.

Since

is not a domain, the degree related to

in the Ore extension (

the relation

(xy)^{n+1}=z^{n}xy\quad\text{for any $n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}$},

(11)

which follows by induction from
(xy)(xy)=x(-xy+z)y=xzy=zxy
and implies in particular that

(z-xy)xy=xy(z-xy)=0

(12)

Zero divisors in
{\mathcal{B}}
are discussed in Propositions
4.1
and

.

Lemma 1.6
.

We have in
A[z]
the following properties:

(i)

\tau^{2}=\operatorname{id}
and
\tau d=-d\tau
,

(ii)

\ker d={{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
, and
A[z]^{\tau}={\Bbbk}[z]
,

(iii)

d^{2}
is a derivation.

Proof.

Clearly
\tau^{2}=\operatorname{id}
, by (
8
). By (
4
), (
8
), (
9
) and (
10
) we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d\tau(f)&=d(f^{+}-xf^{-})=d(f^{+})-\tau(x)d(f^{-})-d(x)f^{-}\\
&=d(f^{+})+x(xD(f))-zf^{-}=xD(f^{+})-zf^{-},\\
\tau d(f)&=\tau(xD(f^{+})+zf^{-})=-x\tau(D(f^{+}))+z\tau(f^{-})\\
&=-xD(f^{+})+zf^{-},\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\text{for any }f

\displaystyle\in A[z],

which proves (i).
By (
10
) we have

d(f)=0
if and only if
D(f^{+})=0
and
f^{-}=0
, hence by (
5
) if and only if
f=f^{+}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
.
Using (
8
) we have

f-\tau(f)=2xf^{-}

(13)

then
\tau(f)=f
if and only if
f=f^{+}\in{\Bbbk}[z]
and point (ii) is proved. Here we used that
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}\not=2
.

Let
f,g
be two elements of
A[z]
. We have
d(fg)=\tau(f)d(g)+d(f)g
, then

\displaystyle d^{2}(fg)

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}(f)d^{2}(g)+(d\tau+\tau d)(f)d(g)+d^{2}(f)g

\displaystyle=fd^{2}(g)+d^{2}(f)g\ \ \ \text{by point (i)}

which shows that

is an ordinary derivation of
A[z]
.
∎

2.
The Jordan plane as a subalgebra

Following the notations of
[
2
,
3
]
we recall that the Jordan plane
\mathbf{J}
is the algebra presented by generators
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{y}

with defining relation

\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}\mathbf{y}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{x}^{2}.

(14)

Proposition 2.1
.

The formal differential operator algebra
A[z][y^{2}\,;\,d^{2}]
is a subalgebra of
{\mathcal{B}}
, and its subalgebra
{\Bbbk}[z][y^{2}\,;\,d^{2}]

can be identified with the Jordan plane
\mathbf{J}
via the isomorphism given by

\mathbf{x}\mapsto z\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{y}\mapsto-\frac{1}{2}y^{2}.

(15)

Proof.

We know that
yf=\tau(f)y+d(f)
for any
f\in A[z]
. Hence we have

y^{2}f=\tau^{2}(f)y^{2}+(d\tau+\tau d)(f)y+d^{2}(f)=fy^{2}+d^{2}(f),

(16)

by Lemma
1.6
(i); this proves the first assertion. By (
7
) we have

d^{2}(z)=d(xz)=-xd(z)+d(x)z=-x^{2}z+z^{2}=z^{2},

then the subalgebra
{\Bbbk}[z][y^{2}\,;\,d^{2}]
is generated over
{\Bbbk}
by
z
and
y^{2}
with relation

y^{2}z-zy^{2}=z^{2}
and the proof is complete.∎

Remark 2.2
.

By iteration of the commutation rules (
16
) we have

y^{2n}f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}d^{2n-2i}(f)y^{2i}\qquad\text{for any }f\in{\Bbbk}[z].

(17)

Recall that
p={\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}\neq 2
, so that
{\Bbbk}[z^{p},y^{2p}]={\Bbbk}
when
p=0
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Z({\mathcal{B}})=Z(\mathbf{J})={\Bbbk}[z^{p},y^{2p}]
.

It is known that
Z(\mathbf{J})={\Bbbk}[z^{p},y^{2p}]
, see
[
24
, Theorem 2.2]
.
That
Z({\mathcal{B}})={\Bbbk}
for algebraically closed
{\Bbbk}
,
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=0
,
is proven in
[
22
, Theorem 3.12]
.

Proof.

We shall prove that
Z({\mathcal{B}})=Z(\mathbf{J})
; then
[
24
, Theorem 2.2]

implies the statement.
Let
{b}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i}y^{i}
an element of
{\mathcal{B}}
with
f_{i}\in A[z]
. We have

\displaystyle{b}y-y{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i}y^{i+1}-\sum_{i=0}^{n}(\tau(f_{i})y+d(f_{i}))y^{i}

\displaystyle=d(f_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(f_{j-1}-\tau(f_{j-1})-d(f_{j}))y^{j}+(f_{n}-\tau(f_{n}))y^{n+1}.

Then
{b}
commutes with
y
if and only if

\displaystyle d(f_{0})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle d(f_{j})

\displaystyle=f_{j-1}-\tau(f_{j-1})\ \ \text{for }1\leq j\leq n,

\displaystyle f_{n}-\tau(f_{n})

\displaystyle=0.

From Lemma
1.6
(ii)
we deduce that
f_{0}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
. Then
f_{0}-\tau(f_{0})=0
, thus
f_{1}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
and inductively
f_{j}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
for all
0\leq j\leq n
. We deduce that the centralizer of
y
in
{\mathcal{B}}
is
{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}[y]
.

Take
{b}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}f_{i}y^{i}
in this centralizer, with
f_{i}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
,
f_{n}\not=0
and suppose that

{b}\notin{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
.
We have

{b}x-x{b}=(f_{n}y^{n}+\cdots)x-x(f_{n}y^{n}+\cdots)=f_{n}(\tau^{n}(x)-x)y^{n}+\rho

where
\deg_{y}\rho<n
.
If
{b}
commutes with
x
, then
\tau^{n}(x)=x
, hence
n=2\ell
is even. Let
m=\sup\{i\text{ odd}:1\leq i\leq n-1\text{ and }f_{i}\not=0\}
.
Then

{b}x-x{b}=\sum_{i=\frac{m+1}{2}}^{\ell}f_{2i}(y^{2i}x-xy^{2i})+f_{m}(y^{m}x-xy^{m})+\varrho

where
\deg_{y}\varrho<m
.
The first term of this sum belongs to
A[z][y^{2}\,;\,d^{2}]
by Proposition
2.1
, while the second summand is of the form

-2f_{m}xy^{m}+\cdots
since
m
is odd. If
{b}x=x{b}
, then
f_{m}=0
,
a contradiction. We deduce that if
{b}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}[y]
commutes with
x
, then
{b}\in{{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}[y^{2}]
. In this case,
{b}
commutes with
z
if and only if
{b}\in Z(\mathbf{J})
.
∎

We use the notations
{\Bbbk}[z][y^{2p}]={\Bbbk}[z]
and
{\Bbbk}[z^{p}][y^{2}]={\Bbbk}[y^{2}]
when

Lemma 2.4
.

(i)
There exists an automorphism
\eta
of
\mathbf{J}
satisfying

\displaystyle\eta(y^{2})

\displaystyle=y^{2}-z

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{J}^{\eta}

\displaystyle=~{\Bbbk}[z][y^{2p}].

(18)

Hence we have the following equalities:

\displaystyle xf

\displaystyle=\eta(f)x,

\displaystyle zf

\displaystyle=\eta(f)z,

\displaystyle xyf

\displaystyle=\eta(f)xy

\displaystyle\text{ for any }f

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{J}.

(19)

(ii)
There exists a linear endomorphism
\nabla
of
\mathbf{J}
satisfying

\displaystyle\ker\nabla

\displaystyle={{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}[y^{2}]

and

\displaystyle\nabla(z)

\displaystyle=z.

(20)

Hence we have the following equalities:

\displaystyle yf

\displaystyle=fy+\nabla(f)x,

\displaystyle y^{2}f

\displaystyle=fy^{2}+\nabla(f)z,

\displaystyle\text{for any }f

\displaystyle\in\mathbf{J}.

(21)

Proof.

It is well known, see Remark
5.4
below,
that the automorphisms of
\mathbf{J}
are of the form

z\mapsto\alpha z
and
y^{2}\mapsto\alpha y^{2}+p(z)

for some
\alpha\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
and
p(z)\in{\Bbbk}[z]
.
Let
\eta
be the automorphism of
\mathbf{J}
corresponding
to
\alpha=1
and
p(z)=-z
. Then relations (
19
)
translate commutations rules
xz=zx
,
zy^{2}=y^{2}z-z^{2}
,

(xy)z=z(xy)
and
(xy)y^{2}=(y^{2}-z)xy
deduced

) and (

).
We have for

\eta(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(z)(y^{2}-z)^{i}\quad\text{for any }f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(z)y^{2i}\in\mathbf{J}.

(22)

By induction we immediately have
(y^{2}-z)^{i}=y^{2i}-izy^{2(i-1)}
. Hence

\eta(f)-f=-z\sum_{i=1}^{n}ia_{i}(z)y^{2(i-1)}\quad\text{for any }f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(z)y^{2i}\in\mathbf{J}.

(23)

We deduce that
\eta(f)=f
if and only if

when
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=0
, and
f\in{\Bbbk}[z,y^{2p}]
when
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=p
. The proof of (i) is complete.

Let us define the linear map
\nabla:\mathbf{J}\to\mathbf{J}
by

\nabla(f)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}D(a_{i}(z))(y^{2}-z)^{i}\quad\text{for any }f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(z)y^{2i}\in\mathbf{J}.

(24)

Then we have

\displaystyle yf

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}y+d(a_{i}))y^{2i}=fy+\sum_{i=1}^{n}D(a_{i})xy^{2i}

\displaystyle=fy+\sum_{i=1}^{n}D(a_{i})(y^{2}-z)^{i}x=fy+\nabla(f)x,

Since
\ker D={{\Bbbk}[z^{p}]}
by (
5
), it is clear by (

8.
Hopf algebra automorphisms

Let
\operatorname{Aut}_{\text{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})
be the group of (braided)
Hopf algebra automorphisms of
{\mathcal{B}}
and

G\coloneqq\{\sigma_{s,\mu}\in\operatorname{Aut}{\mathcal{B}}:\mu\in{\Bbbk}^{\times},\,s\in{\Bbbk}\}
, a subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}({\mathcal{B}})
.

Proposition 8.1
.

We have
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}({\mathcal{B}})=G
. Furthermore,
if
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=0
, then
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})=G
.

Proof.

(i) If
\mu\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
and
s\in{\Bbbk}
, then

\sigma_{s,\mu}(x),\sigma_{s,\mu}(y)\in V={\Bbbk}x\oplus{\Bbbk}y\subseteq\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{B}})
.
A direct computation shows that

\sigma_{s,\mu}\otimes\sigma_{s,\mu}

commutes with the braiding
c
. Therefore,
\sigma_{s,\mu}

preserves the comultiplication and is thus a Hopf algebra map.
This shows that
\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}({\mathcal{B}})\supset G
.

(ii) Conversely, let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})\cap\operatorname{Aut}_{\operatorname{super}}({\mathcal{B}})
.
By Theorem
5.3
,
\sigma=\sigma_{s,\mu}

for unique
\mu\in{\Bbbk}^{\times}
and
s(z)\in{\Bbbk}[z]
.
Then
\sigma_{s,\mu}(y)=\mu y+s(z)x\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{B}})
,
hence
u\coloneqq s(z)x\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{B}})
.
Assume that
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}>2
and let
\varpi
be as in (
43
).
Then

\varpi(u)\in\mathcal{P}({\mathscr{B}}(V))=V
, i.e., there exist

\alpha,\beta\in{\Bbbk}
such that

w\coloneqq u-\alpha x-\beta y\in\mathcal{P}({\mathcal{B}})\cap\ker\varpi
.
Hence

\displaystyle(\operatorname{id}\otimes\varpi)\Delta(w)=w\otimes 1\implies w\in{\mathcal{B}}^{\operatorname{co}\varpi}={\Bbbk}[x,z].

That is,

s(z)x-\alpha x-\beta y\in{\Bbbk}[x,z]
. This implies that
s(z)\in{\Bbbk}
.

(iii) Assume that
{\rm char\,}{\Bbbk}=0
; then
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]

applies. For the sake of completeness we repeat the argument.
Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}_{\text{Hopf}}({\mathcal{B}})
. Then
\sigma
, being a coalgebra map, preserves

which is

since

;
particularly,

, respectively

and

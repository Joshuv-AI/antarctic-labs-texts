Jordan Type stratification of spaces of commuting nilpotent matrices
Mats Boij, Anthony Iarrobino, Leila Khatami

Abstract
. An n × n n\times n nilpotent matrix B B is determined up to conjugacy by a partition P B P_{B} of n n , its Jordan type given by the sizes of its Jordan blocks. The Jordan type 𝔇 ⁡ ( P ) \mathfrak{D}(P) of a nilpotent matrix in the dense orbit of the nilpotent commutator of a given nilpotent matrix of Jordan type P P is stable - has parts differing pairwise by at least two - and was determined by R. Basili. The second two authors, with B. Van Steirteghem and R. Zhao determined a rectangular table of partitions 𝔇 − 1 ​ ( Q ) \mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Q) having a given stable partition Q Q as the Jordan type of its maximum nilpotent commutator. They proposed a box conjecture, that would generalize the answer to stable partitions Q Q having ℓ \ell parts: it was proven recently by J. Irving, T. Košir and M. Mastnak. Using this result and also some tropical calculations, the authors here determine equations defining the loci of each partition in 𝔇 − 1 ​ ( Q ) \mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Q) , when Q Q is stable with two parts. The equations for each locus form a complete intersection. The authors propose a conjecture generalizing their result to arbitrary stable Q Q .

1.
Introduction

We let
\mathsf{k}
be an infinite field, and let
B
be an
n\times n
nilpotent matrix over
\mathsf{k}
. The
Jordan type

P_{B}
of
B
is the partition of
n
determined by the sizes of the blocks in a Jordan block matrix conjugate to
B
. Although the Jordan decomposition has been known for over a hundred years
[
Jo
,
Br
]
the problem of determining which pairs of partitions
(P,P^{\prime})
are possible for two commuting nilpotent matrices
B,B^{\prime}
did not receive attention, until in 2008 three research groups - in Ljubljana, Moscow, and Perugia-Boston became interested independently, and wrote about the problem
[
Ob
,
Pan
,
BaI
]
.

Since the variety
\mathcal{N}_{B}
parametrizing nilpotent matrices commuting with
B
is irreducible, there is a maximum Jordan partition
\mathfrak{D}(P)
in the dominance orbit closure order of a matrix
A\in\mathcal{N}_{B}
. R. Basili had determined the number of parts
\ell(\mathfrak{D}(P))
of
\mathfrak{D}(P)
, to be the minimum number of almost rectangular partitions (largest and smallest part differ by at most one) needed to cover
P

[
Ba1
]
. P. Oblak determined the largest part of
\mathfrak{D}(P)
, and also made a recursive conjecture for determining
\mathfrak{D}(P)

[
Ob
]
. This was studied in several subsequent papers
[
KO
,
Pan
,
Kh1
,
BaIK
,
IK
]
; in particular
\mathfrak{D}(P)
was shown by T. Košir and P. Oblak in
[
KO
]
to be stable
\mathfrak{D}(\mathfrak{D}(P))=\mathfrak{D}(P)
; equivalently,
\mathfrak{D}(P)
has parts differing pairwise by at least two
[
BaI
, Theorem 1]
,
[
Pan
]
, known as super-distinct. The third author determined the smallest part of
\mathfrak{D}(P)

[
Kh2
]
; thus, in 2014
\mathfrak{D}(P)
was known, given
P
, when
\mathfrak{D}(P)
had up to three parts. Finally, in 2022, R. Basili proved the Oblak conjecture
[
Ba2
]
; a new proof using the Burge code is given in
[
IKM
, Theorem 34]
. For an account of these developments see the survey by L. Khatami
[
Kh3
]
. For a broader survey of problems in commuting nilpotent matrices see
[
NSi
,
JeSi
]
.

But what is the set
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Q)
for
Q
a stable partition? R. Zhao conjectured in 2014 that for
Q=(u,u-r),r\geq 2
there should be a rectangular
(r-1)\times(u-r)
table
\mathcal{T}(Q)
of partitions forming the set
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Q)
, and that the element
P_{i,j}(Q)
in the
i
-row,
j
-column of
\mathcal{T}(Q)
should have
(i+j)
parts. This was proven by the second two authors with B. Van Steirteghem and R. Zhao in
[
IKVZ2
]
. They also proposed a
Box Conjecture

[
IKVZ2
, Conjecture 4.11]
, generalizing their result to all stable partitions
Q
. The Box Conjecture was recently proven in full generality in a striking paper by J. Irving, T. Košir, and M. Mastnak
[
IKM
]
. We next state their result.
We label the box as in the original conjecture - as that will be convenient for us.

We write the stable partition
Q=(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{\ell})
with parts
q_{1}>q_{2}>\cdots>q_{\ell}
satisfying
q_{i}-q_{i+1}\geq 2
for
1\leq i\leq\ell-1
and define a key
S=(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{\ell})
:

(1)

\begin{cases}s_{i}&=q_{i}-q_{i+1}-1\text{ for }1\leq i<\ell\\
s_{\ell}&=q_{\ell}.\end{cases}

Box Theorem

[
IKM
]
Given the stable partition
Q
with key

, there is an
s_{1}\times s_{2}\times\cdots\times s_{\ell}
box

of partitions

has

When

where

When

For any

When

in

2.
Notations and background

A major tool in
[
IKM
]
is the
Burge code
of a partition
[
Bu1
,
Bu2
]
This gives a bijection between the set of integer partitions and the set of finite binary words. In order to define this bijection, we first recall the basic definitions given in
[
IKM
]
for sequences of finitely supported sequences of nonnegative integers. For a partition
P=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{s})
the
frequency sequence
is given by
f_{i}=\#\{j:\lambda_{j}=i\}
, for
i\geq 1
, and it is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(P)
. Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the set of all finitely supported sequences of nonnegative integers. For
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots)\in\mathcal{F}
, the
backward pairs
of
f
are defined as pairs
(f_{j-1},f_{j})
that are obtained by parsing
f
from right to left and pairing
f_{j-1},f_{j}
whenever
f_{j}>0
. Suppose that backwards pairs of
f
are
(f_{j_{i}-1},f_{j_{i}})
for
i=1,\dots m
. The set
\mathsf{R}(f)
is defined as
\{j_{1},j_{2},\dots,j_{m}\}.
This set is partitioned by the following subsets.

\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{A}&=\{f\in\mathcal{F}:1\not\in\mathsf{R}(f)\},\\
\mathcal{B}&=\{f\in\mathcal{F}:1\in\mathsf{R}(f)\}.\end{array}

Finally, the mapping
\partial:\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}
is defined as follows. For
f\in\mathcal{F}
,
\partial{f}
is obtained from
f
by replacing
(f_{j-1},f_{j})
with
(f_{j-1}+1,f_{j}-1)
for each
j\in\mathsf{R}(f)
.

For a partition
P
, we denote
\mathsf{R}(\mathcal{F}(P))
by
\mathsf{R}(P)
and
\partial\mathcal{F}(P)
by
\partial P
. We also say
P
is in
\mathcal{A}
(respectively,
\mathcal{B}
)) if
\mathcal{F}(P)\in\mathcal{A}
(respectively,
\mathcal{B}
).

Recall that a partition is called
almost rectangular
if its biggest and smallest parts differ by at most 1. We use the notation
([m]^{k})
to denote the almost rectangular partition of
m
into
k
parts when
m\geq k
. Consider a partition
P
with frequency sequence
\mathcal{F}(P)=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots)
. Then
f_{j}>0
if and only if
P
includes at least one part of size
j
. Therefore, backward pairs of
P
can be obtained by decomposing
P
into (maximal) almost rectangular subpartitions of
P
starting from the top (from largest part to smallest). The set
\mathsf{R}(P)
then consists of the largest part of each almost rectangular subpartition in such a decomposition. Visualizing
P
by its Ferrers diagram in Figure
1
we note that in order to obtain
\partial P
, for each almost rectangular subpartition of
P
described above, we remove one box from the lowest row corresponding to its largest part.

.

Let
P=(7,6,5^{3},2^{2},1)
with

. Looking at the frequency sequence, the backward pairs are
(1,1)
,
(0,3)
, and
(1,2)
. Alternatively, looking at the partition, starting form the top decomposing

into almost rectangular subpartitions, we get
(7,6)
,
(5^{3})

(2^{2},1)

Definition 2.4
.

For
Q=(u,u-r)
, where
u>r\geq 2
, and

\begin{split}E^{Q}_{k,l}&=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1},b_{1},b_{2},\dots b_{r-k-1},a_{k}b_{r-k}-g_{0}h_{0},\\
&a_{k}b_{r+1-k}+a_{k+1}b_{r-k}-g_{0}h_{1}-g_{1}h_{0},\dots,\sum_{j=0}^{k+l-r-1}(a_{k+j}b_{l-1-j}-g_{j}h_{k+l-r-1-j})\}\subseteq\mathsf{k}[\mathcal{N}_{J_{Q}}]\end{split}

if
k+l\geq r+1
. Let

be the ideal generated by

Remark 2.5
.

In the region where
k+l\leq r
, there are only linear equations and we have the two inclusions
E^{Q}_{k-1,l}\subseteq E^{Q}_{k,l}
and
E^{Q}_{k,l-1}\subseteq E^{Q}_{k,l}
. When
k+l>r
we also have quadratic equations and we only have the inclusion
E^{Q}_{k,l-1}\subseteq E^{Q}_{k,l}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

When
u>r\geq 2
, the variety
X^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}
is a reduced irreducible complete intersection of codimension
k+l-2
, for
1\leq k\leq r-1
and
1\leq l\leq u-r
.

Proof.

For the linear equations, the statement is clear. When focusing on the quadratic equations

\begin{array}[]{rcl}a_{k}b_{r-k}&=&g_{0}h_{0}\\
a_{k}b_{r+1-k}+a_{k+1}b_{r-k}&=&g_{0}h_{1}+g_{1}h_{0}\\
&\vdots\\
a_{k}b_{l-1}+a_{k+1}b_{l-2}+\cdots+a_{2k+l-1}b_{r-k}&=&g_{0}h_{k+l-r}+g_{1}h_{k+l-r-1}+\cdots+g_{k+l-r}h_{0}\end{array}

we can successively solve for
b_{r-k},b_{r-k+1},\dots,b_{l-1}
as long as
a_{k}\neq 0
and we can solve for
a_{k},a_{k+1},\dots,a_{2k-r+l-1}
as long as
b_{r-k}\neq 0
. If both are zero, we get a locus of higher codimension. Hence an open dense subset of
X^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}
can be parametrized by an affine space of dimension
4u-3r-2-(k+l-2)=4u-3r-k-l
. This gives us the irreducibility and codimension. Since each variable that we solve for occurs only linearly in the equations, the ideal is radical and since the number of equations equals the codimension it is a complete intersection.
∎

Tropical calculations

We will use the order of the elements in the matrices in the calculations of the coranks. Recall that for
a\in\mathsf{k}\llbracket t\rrbracket
, the order of
a
is the lowest power of
t
in
a
that has a nonzero coefficient. For a matrix
B
we will have

T(M_{B})=\begin{bmatrix}\ord(a)&\ord(g)\\
r+\ord(h)&\ord(b)\end{bmatrix}

Since
B
is nilpotent,
\ord(a)\geq 1
and
\ord(b)\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
M_{B}=\begin{bmatrix}a&g\\
ht^{r}&b\end{bmatrix}
,
a\neq 0
and

\ord(a)\leq r+\min\{\ord(g),\ord(h)\}
, then

\corank B=\min\{\ord(\det M_{B}),\ord(a)+u-r\}.

Proof.

We have that
\corank B=\dim_{\mathsf{k}}\ker B=\dim_{\mathsf{k}}\ker M_{B}
, since
B
and
M_{B}
correspond to the same
\mathsf{k}
-linear map under the isomorphism
\mathsf{k}^{u}\oplus\mathsf{k}^{u-r}\cong\mathsf{k}[t]/(t^{u})\oplus\mathsf{k}[t]/(t^{u-r})
. If
k=\ord(a)
, we can write

B=\begin{bmatrix}a^{\prime}t^{k}&g\\
ht^{r}&b\end{bmatrix}

where
a^{\prime}
is invertible in
\mathsf{k}\llbracket t\rrbracket
. Row reduction gives

B^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}a^{\prime}t^{k}&g\\
0&b-(a^{\prime})^{-1}ght^{r-k}\end{bmatrix}

and
\corank B=\corank B^{\prime}
. The condition
\ord(a)\leq\ord(h)+r
makes the row reduction possible, while the condition
\ord(a)\leq\ord(g)+r
guarantees that the upper right corner of
B^{\prime}
does not affect the computation of
\corank B^{\prime}
which is given by
k+u-r
if
b-(a^{\prime})^{-1}ght^{r-k}=0
and by

k+\ord(b-(a^{\prime})^{-1}ght^{r-k})=k+\ord(a^{\prime}b-ght^{r-k})=k+\ord((ab-ght^{r})t^{-k})=\ord(\det M_{B})

if
b-(a^{\prime})^{-1}ght^{r-k}\neq 0
. We note that we are not assuming above that either
g
,
h
or
b
is invertible.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The equations

are given by
\ord(a)\geq k
and
\ord\det(M_{B})\geq k+l

Proposition 3.3
.

For a stable partition
Q=(u,u-r)
and for
1\leq k\leq r-1
and
1\leq l\leq u-r
we have an inclusion
W^{Q}_{k,l}\subseteq X^{Q}_{k,l}
.

Proof.

We will use induction on
u-r
. The base case is when
u-r=1
and we have that
E^{(r+1,1)}_{k,1}=\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k-1}\}
for
k=1,2,\dots,r-1
. For
B\in\mathcal{N}_{(r+1,1)}
we have

M_{B}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{1}t+a_{2}t^{2}+\cdots+a_{r}t^{r}&g_{0}\\
h_{0}t^{r}&0\\
\end{bmatrix}

and the partition
P^{Q}_{k,1}
has
k+1
parts, which means that the
\corank B=\dim_{\mathsf{k}}\ker(M_{B})=k+1
. This forces
a_{1}=a_{2}=\cdots=a_{k-1}=0
which shows the inclusion
W^{(r+1,1)}_{k,1}\subseteq X^{(r+1,1)}_{k,1}
, for
k=1,2,\dots,r-1
.

Now assume by induction that we have the inclusion
W^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}\subseteq X^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}
for
u-r=m\geq 1
. The restriction of the equations
E^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l}
from
\mathsf{k}[\mathcal{N}_{J_{(u+1,u+1-r)}}]
to
\mathsf{k}[\mathcal{N}_{J_{(u,u-r)}}]
gives the equations
E^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}
when
l\leq u-r
. By
[
IKM
, Theorem 23]
we have that for
B\in W^{(u+1-r)}_{k,l}
the Jordan type of
B|_{\im(J_{(u+1,u+1-r)})}
is
\partial(P^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l})=P^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l}
which is in
\mathcal{N}_{J_{\partial(u+1,u+1-r)}}=\mathcal{N}_{J_{(u,u-r)}}
and
\mathfrak{D}(\partial P^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l})=(u,u-r)
. Hence we conclude by induction on
u-r
that
W^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l}\subseteq X^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l}
when
l\leq u-r
.

When
l=u+1-r
we get that
\partial P^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,l}=P^{(u,u-r)}_{k,l-1}=P^{(u,u-r)}_{k,u-r}
. By the same argument as above we get that
W^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}\subseteq X^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u-r}
. The number of parts of
P^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}
is
k+u+1-r
which means that
\corank B=k+u+1-r
. By Lemma
3.1
and Remark
3.2
this gives the last equation in
E^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}
when
k+u+1-r>r
since it is an irreducible quadric. Hence
W^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}\subseteq X^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}
for
k+u+1-r>r
. When
k+u+1-r\leq r
, the equation given by
\ord\det(M_{B})\geq k+u+1-r
is
a_{k}b_{u+1-r}=0
. If
a_{k}=0
we get that
B|_{\im J_{Q}}
is in
X^{(u,u-r)}_{k+1,u-r}
, but this would imply that it has at least
k+1+u-r
parts. But
P^{(u,u-r)}_{k,u-r}
has
k+u-r
parts. Hence we conclude that
b_{u+1-r}=0
and
W^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}\subseteq X^{(u+1,u+1-r)}_{k,u+1-r}
.
∎

Lemma 3.5

Lemma 3.6
.

For
B
general in

, where

\corank B^{s}=(k+l)s\oplus(T(M_{B}^{s})_{1,1}+u-r)\oplus(2u-r),\quad\text{for $s\geq 0$.}

Proof.

Observe that since
B
is general in

, the order of

is

. We have that

T(M_{B})=\begin{bmatrix}k&0\\

where

. Hence we have that

. Lemma

. Hence the conditions in Lemma

are satisfied for

.

is not affected by the quotients by

and

, which gives the first term.

4.2.
Equations for Jordan type loci, for arbitrary stable partitions
Q

In light of the proof of the Box Theorem (see Theorem
1
) in
[
IKM
]
, we conjecture that Theorem
1.3
can be generalized as follows.

Conjecture 4.2
.

Let
Q
be a stable partition of length
\ell
, and let
P
be a partition with
\mathfrak{D}(P)=Q
. Then the closure of
W^{Q}_{P}
in
\mathcal{N}_{J_{Q}}
is an irreducible complete intersection of codimension
\ell(P)-\ell
, with defining equations of degree at most
\ell
.

We first note that while Theorem
1.3
establishes the conjecture for
\ell=2
, the conjecture is relatively straightforward for
\ell=1
. Assume that
Q=(u)
has 1 part. In this case,
\mathcal{N}_{J_{Q}}
is isomorphic to
\mathsf{k}[t]/(t^{u})
; indeed each element
B\in\mathcal{N}_{J_{Q}}
is a matrix of the following form, which can be identified with
a(t)=\sum a_{i}t^{i}\in\mathsf{k}[t]/(t^{n})
.

B=\begin{bmatrix}0&a_{1}&a_{2}&\ldots&a_{n-1}\\
0&0&a_{1}&\ddots&a_{n-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&0&\ldots&a_{1}\\
0&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&0\end{bmatrix}

Moreover,
\mathfrak{D}(P)=Q
if and only if
P
is an almost rectangular partition of
n
, if and only if
P
is the Jordan type of an element
B\in\mathcal{N}_{J_{(n)}}
. Therefore, for an almost rectangular partition
P
, the closure of
W_{P}^{Q}
in
\mathcal{N}_{J_{(n)}}
is defined by
\ell(P)-1
linear equations, namely
a_{1}=\ldots=a_{\ell(P)-1}=0
.

The conjecture remains open for
\ell\geq 3
. We believe the inductive approach we used in proving Theorem
1.3
can be generalized to prove Conjecture
4.2
. The examples we have investigated seem to suggest that the conjecture holds in general but establishing it requires additional steps that go beyond the scope of this paper. Generalizing equations given in Definition
2.4
requires more elaborate notations, but will not be fundamentally different from the case
\ell=2
. As for the proof of the conjecture in general, we need a generalized base case for the proof of the analog of Proposition
3.3
, as well as a more detailed study of
the “outer layer” of the Box associated to
\mathfrak{D}^{-1}(Q)
, in order to generalize Proposition
3.3
.

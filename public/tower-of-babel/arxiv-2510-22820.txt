Induced additive actions on toric varieties Thanks: This article is an output of a research project implemented as part of the Basic Research Program at HSE University
Alexander Chernov

Abstract
. An induced additive action on a projective variety X ⊆ ℙ n X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n} is a regular action of the group 𝔾 a n {\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n} on X X with an open orbit that can be extended to a regular action on ℙ n {\mathbb{P}}^{n} . Such actions are described with pairs ( A , U ) (A,U) , where A A is a local algebra and U U is a generating subspace lying in the maximal ideal. Such pairs are called S S -pairs. We study additive actions on projective toric varieties and on Hirzebruch surfaces in particular. We prove that for any linearly normal toric variety with an additive action normalized by a torus, the corresponding S S -pair consists of a monomial algebra and a subspace generated by variables. Also, we describe S S -pairs for normalized and non-normalized induced additive actions on Hirzebruch surfaces.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\mathbb{K}}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. By a variety or an algebraic group we always mean an algebraic variety or an algebraic group over
{\mathbb{K}}
. By open and closed subsets of algebraic varieties we always mean open and closed in Zariski topology subsets. By
local algebra
we mean a commutative associative algebra over
{\mathbb{K}}
with a unit and a unique maximal ideal. We denote by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
the algebraic group
({\mathbb{K}},+)
and by
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
the group
({\mathbb{K}}^{\times},\cdot);
by
torus
we mean the algebraic group
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{k}={\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\dotsc\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
(
k
-times). We say that an algebraic variety is
toric
if it is normal and it admits an algebraic torus action with an open orbit.

Definition 1
.

An
additive action
on an algebraic variety
X
is a regular effective action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
on
X
with an open orbit. By
induced additive action
on a projective algebraic variety
X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
we mean a regular effective action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
such that
X
is a closure of a
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-orbit.

Not all additive actions on projective varieties are induced (see
[
2
, Example 1]
). But if a projective variety
X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is normal and linearly normal, i.e. the restriction map
H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{n},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(1))\to H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n}}(1)\big|_{X})
is surjective, then any additive action on
X
is induced.

Knop and Lange were studying regular actions of commutative algebraic groups on projective space with an open orbit in
[
14
]
. However, the systematic study of additive actions was started in
[
13
]
. In both works authors have found a one-to-one correspondence between local algebras of dimension
n+1
and additive actions on
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Further, this science was greatly developed in many directions. For instance, the case of additive actions on projective hypersurfaces was studied in
[
2
,
7
,
15
,
6
,
1
]
. Results in
[
10
,
11
,
5
,
16
]
are related to additive actions on Fano varieties.

In our paper, we study the case of toric varieties. Remarkable results about additive actions on toric varieties were found in
[
3
]
. Authors proved that a complete toric variety admits an additive action if and only if it admits a
normalized
additive action, i.e. an acting torus normalizes
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
in the group of automorphisms. Moreover, they proved that any two normalized additive actions are isomorphic (see
[
3
, Theorem 2]
and
[
3
, Theorem 3]
).

The key definition in the theory of additive actions on projective hypersurfaces is
H
-pair (see
[
4
, Definition 2.12]
). We introduce the following more general definition for the case of an arbitrary subvariety in the projective space.

Definition 2
.

A pair
(A,U)
is called an
S
-pair
if
A
is a local algebra with a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
U\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
is a subspace generating
A
as a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between induced additive actions on projective subvarieties of dimension
m
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and
S
-pairs
(A,U)
, where
A
is a local algebra of dimension
n+1
and
U
is a subspace of dimension
m
lying in the maximal ideal of
A
(see
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
).

We study the relations between
S
-pairs and induced additive actions on projective toric varieties. It turns out that for linearly normal projective toric varieties, the normalized additive action corresponds to the
S
-pair that consists of a monomial algebra and a subspace generated by variables (see Theorem
3
). A monomial algebra is an algebra which is isomorphic to an algebra
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]/I
, where
I
is an ideal generated by monomials.

Conversely, for an
S
-pair that consists of a monomial algebra and a subspace generated by variables, the corresponding variety admits a torus action and the induced additive action is normalized, but it is not always normal (see Proposition
3
and Example
1
). As an example, we describe
S
-pairs for Hirzebruch surfaces for the normalized additive action and
S
-pairs for the non-normalized additive action for the blow-up of
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
at one point (see Section
5
).

2.
The Hassett-Tschinkel correspondence

A projective variety
X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is called
non-degenerate
if it is not contained in any hyperplane of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. The following theorem is central to our topic.

Theorem 1
.

[
4
, Theorem 2.6]

There is a one-to-one correspondence between

(1)

equivalence classes of induced additive actions on non-degenerate subvarieties in
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
;

(2)

isomorphism classes of pairs
(A,U)
, where
A
is a local
(n+1)
-dimensional algebra with a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
U
is a subspace in
\mathfrak{m}
that generates
A
as an algebra with a unit.

In Theorem
1
two
S
-pairs
(A_{1},U_{1})
and
(A_{2},U_{2})
are said to be isomorphic if there is an isomorphism
\varphi:A_{1}\to A_{2}
such that
\varphi(U_{1})=U_{2}
.

We will briefly describe the correspondence in Theorem
1
. Consider a local algebra
A\penalty\ =\penalty\ {\mathbb{K}}\penalty\ \oplus\penalty\ \mathfrak{m}
of dimension
n+1
and a subspace
U
of dimension
m
generating
A
as a
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra. There is an exponent group isomorphism
\exp:(\mathfrak{m},+)\to(1+\mathfrak{m},\cdot)
of the standard view:

\exp(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}.

This formula is correctly defined since any element from
\mathfrak{m}
is nilpotent and hence the series is finite. Restricting the exponent homomorphism on the subspace
U
, we get an isomorphism
\exp:(U,+)\to(1+U,\cdot)
. Consequently,
(U,+)\simeq{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
acts on
A
as
(1+U,\cdot)
by the standard multiplication inducing the action on
{\mathbb{P}}(A)
. This gives us the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
on
{\mathbb{P}}(A)\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
. Denote by
\pi:A\backslash\{0\}\to{\mathbb{P}}(A)
the standard projection. Then the closure

X=\overline{\pi(\exp(U)1)}

is a corresponding subvariety in
{\mathbb{P}}(A)
with an induced additive action.

Conversely, let
X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}={\mathbb{P}}(V)
be a subvariety with an induced additive action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
. Every
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-action on
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
lifts to a linear action on
V
, so we have a corresponding inclusion
\varphi:{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}\to\GL_{n+1}({\mathbb{K}})\simeq\GL(V)
. Let
(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m})
be coordinates on
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
. Consider the matrices

T_{i}=\frac{\partial\varphi(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m})}{\partial g_{i}}\Bigg|_{(g_{1},\dotsc,g_{m})=(0,\dotsc,0)}\in\text{M}_{n+1}({\mathbb{K}}),\ i=1,\dotsc,m.

Then the matrices
T_{1},\dotsc,T_{m}
, as elements of associative algebra
\text{M}_{n+1}({\mathbb{K}})
, generate a local algebra
A\subseteq\text{M}_{n+1}({\mathbb{K}})
of dimension
n+1
and form a generating subspace in the maximal ideal of
A
. One can note that the subspace generated by matrices
T_{i}
is just the image of the differential of the map
\varphi
at unity.

Now we will describe the construction of the corresponding
S
-pair for normal varieties in more geometric terms. As we know, if
X
is a normal algebraic variety, its divisor class group
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
contains the Picard group
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
. So for any invertible sheaf
\mathcal{L}\in\penalty\ \mathrm{Pic}(X)
there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
for some divisor
D
on
X
. For an invertible sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
, we will use the following representation of its global sections:

(1)

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))=\{f\in K(X)\ \big|\ \mathrm{div}(f)+D\geq 0\}\cup\{0\}.

It is well known that for complete varieties, it is a finite-dimensional vector space over
{\mathbb{K}}
.

Proposition 1
.

[
4
, Proposition 3.1]

Let
X
be a normal variety and
U
be an open subset in
X
isomorphic to the affine space. Then the divisor class group
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
is freely generated by the prime divisors lying in the complement of the open subset
U
.

Remark 1
.

If a variety
X
admits an additive action by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
, then the divisors in the complement of the open orbit are
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-invariant and generate freely
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
.

There is a very useful algebraic tool in the theory of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-actions on affine varieties.

Definition 3
.

The derivation
\delta
on a commutative algebra
B
is called
locally nilpotent
if for every element
x\in B
there is a natural number
n=n(x)
such that
\delta^{n}(x)=0
.

It is well known that every
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action on an affine variety is induced by some locally nilpotent derivation on its algebra of regular functions. If we have a locally nilpotent derivation
\delta
on the algebra of regular functions
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
of an affine algebraic variety
X
, then for every
t\in{\mathbb{K}}
the mapping
\exp{(t\delta)}
(defined by the standard series) is an automorphism of
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
. Since
\exp((t+s)\delta)=\exp{(t\delta)}\exp{(s\delta)}
, it defines the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
on
{\mathbb{K}}[X]
and thus it defines the
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-action on
X
.

Below we describe the construction of an
S
-pair that corresponds to an induced additive action on a normal projective variety in terms of derivations on its algebra of regular functions on the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-orbit.

Construction 1
.

Suppose
X\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
is a normal subvariety with an induced additive action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
. Then this closed embedding is induced by a subspace
V\subseteq H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
for some very ample divisor
D
. We can assume that
D
is
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-invariant by Proposition
1
. Fix the coordinates
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})
on the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-orbit. One can check that
V
is (
n+1
)-dimensional
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-invariant, since
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-action extends to the linear action on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))
and the action on
X
is induced. Next, note that a vector space
V
consists of functions which have the form
f(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})
on the open orbit, where
f
is a polynomial. The
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-action on the open orbit is induced by
m
commuting locally nilpotent derivations
d_{1},\dotsc,d_{m}
on
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m}]
. The subspace
V
is
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
-invariant and hence it is invariant under derivations
d_{1},\dotsc,d_{m}
. The corresponding
S
-pair
(A,U)
has the following form:

(2)

A={\mathbb{K}}[d_{1}\big|_{V},\dotsc,d_{m}\big|_{V}]\subseteq L(V),\ U=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}{\mathbb{K}}d_{i}\big|_{V}.

This construction is also described in
[
4
, Theorem 2.14]

Remark 2
.

Every additive action induces its own coordinates on the open orbit where it acts by shifts and the derivations
d_{1},\dotsc,d_{m}
are just
\partial/\partial x_{1},\dotsc,\partial/\partial x_{m}
. But these coordinates might be inconvenient to work with. For example, the case of toric varieties is exactly like this.

3.
Induced additive actions on toric varieties

The following theorem is key in the theory of additive actions on toric varieties.

Theorem 2
.

[
3
, Theorem 3]

Let
X
be a complete toric variety with an acting torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{m}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

1)

X admits an additive action normalized by the acting torus
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
;

2)

X admits an additive action;

3)

the maximal unipotent subgroup
U
of the group
\Aut(X)
acts on
X
with an open orbit.

As we mentioned in the introduction, any two normalized additive actions on a toric variety are isomorphic. One can see the proof of this fact in
[
3
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 3
.

It is easy to check directly that for toric varieties with normalized additive action, the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
-orbit lies in the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
-orbit. Also, the divisors lying in the complement of the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
-orbit are
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
-invariant since the torus acts on them by permutations, but this action is trivial because a torus is a connected group.

We remind the reader that a group of automorphisms of a complete toric variety is an affine algebraic group (see
[
9
]
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
X
be a complete toric variety that admits an additive action. Then there exists an equivalent additive action such that its open orbit contains an open torus orbit.

Proof.

Denote by
\widetilde{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}}\subseteq\Aut(X)
a subgroup corresponding to some additive action and by
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}\subseteq\Aut(X)
corresponding to the normalized one. Let
U,\widetilde{U}\subseteq\Aut(X)
be maximal unipotent subgroups containing
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
and
\widetilde{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}}
respectively. They act on
X
with the same open orbits as
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
and
\widetilde{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}}
. It follows from the fact that an orbit of an unipotent group is isomorphic to affine space, but affine space cannot contain an affine space as an open proper subset. Since all maximal unipotent subgroups are conjugated in an affine algebraic group, we can find an automorphism
\varphi\in\Aut(X)
such that
\varphi^{-1}\widetilde{U}\varphi=U
. Consider the following equivalent additive action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
on
X
:

g\cdot x=\varphi^{-1}(g\cdot\varphi(x)).

This action has the same open orbit as
\widetilde{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}}
and thus contains an open
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
-orbit.
∎

For the normalized additive action we can choose coordinates
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
on the open
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
-orbit in which
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
acts by shifts (i.e. the action is induced by
n
commuting homogeneous derivations
\partial/\partial x_{1},\dotsc,\partial/\partial x_{n}
) and coordinates
(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})
on
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
such that
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
acts on the open orbit by coordinate-wise multiplication. One can see the proof in
[
4
, Section 4.1]
. We will call such coordinates (on both open orbit and an acting torus)
standard
.

Definition 4
.

A finitely generated
{\mathbb{K}}
-algebra
A
is called
monomial
if
A
is isomorphic to the quotient of the ring of polynomials in
n
variables
{\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]/I
, where
I
is a
monomial
ideal, i.e. it is generated by monomials.

Proposition 3
.

Let
A={\mathbb{K}}[x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}]/I
be a monomial finite-dimensional local algebra with respect to the monomial ideal
I
and
U={\mathbb{K}}x_{1}\oplus\dotsc\oplus{\mathbb{K}}x_{n}
being a subspace generated by variables. Then a variety
X
with an induced additive action corresponding to the
S
-pair
(A,U)
admits a torus action with an open orbit, and the additive action is normalized by an acting torus.

Proof.

Let
\pi:A\backslash\{0\}\to{\mathbb{P}}(A)
be the canonical projection morphism. By Theorem
1
we have
X=\overline{\pi(\exp(U)1)}\subset{\mathbb{P}}(A)
. It is easy to write the formula:

(3)

\exp(U)\cdot 1=\Bigg\{\sum\frac{(i_{1}+\dotsc+i_{n})!}{i_{1}!\dotsc i_{n}!}\alpha_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc\alpha_{n}^{i_{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc x_{n}^{i_{n}},\ \alpha_{1},\dotsc,\alpha_{n}\in{\mathbb{K}}\Bigg\}

Consider the following linear representation of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
on
A
:

(4)

(t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})\circ x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc x_{n}^{i_{n}}=t_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc t_{n}^{i_{n}}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc x_{n}^{i_{n}},\ (t_{1},\dotsc,t_{n})\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}

This action induces an action on
{\mathbb{P}}(A)
and commutes with the projection
\pi
. It is not hard to see from (
3
) and (
4
) that the closure of the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
-orbit of the point
\pi(\sum\frac{(i_{1}+\dotsc+i_{n})!}{i_{1}!\dotsc i_{n}!}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc x_{n}^{i_{n}})
that lies in
{\mathbb{P}}(A)
is exactly
X
. Since the closure of any orbit consists of orbits of lower dimension, the action (
4
) yields the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}
on the
X
with the open orbit
O={\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{n}\pi(\sum\frac{(i_{1}+\dotsc+i_{n})!}{i_{1}!\dotsc i_{n}!}x_{1}^{i_{1}}\dotsc x_{n}^{i_{n}})
. It means that
X
admits a torus action with an open orbit. This action is normalized because, in coordinates induced by the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}^{n}
, the torus acts by coordinate-wise multiplication.
∎

Remark 4
.

We did not write that a corresponding variety is toric because it might not be normal.

Consider an

(8)

\displaystyle U={\mathbb{K}}\chi^{(1,0,\dotsc,0)}\oplus{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{(0,1,\dotsc,0)}\oplus\dotsc\oplus{\mathbb{K}}\chi^{(0,0,\dotsc,1)},

A mapping

Example 2
.

Consider the variety

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}

t\cdot[z_{0}:z_{1}]=[z_{0}:tz_{1}],\ a\cdot[z_{0}:z_{1}]=[z_{0}:z_{1}+az_{0}],\ t\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m},\ a\in{\mathbb{G}}_{a}.

for all

Hence by Theorem

the linearly normal embeddings of

with the normalized additive action correspond to the

These monomial algebras are Gorenstein, i.e.

, where

denotes the maximal ideal of

Conversely, any monomial Gorenstein algebra has the view (

4.
Very ample divisors on Hirzebruch surfaces

The Hirzebruch surface
\Sigma_{n}
is the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over the
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
, associated to the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\penalty\ \oplus\penalty\ \mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(n)
.
It has the following representation in charts. Let
U_{j}
denote the affine open subset
\{z_{j}\neq 0\}
in standard homogeneous coordinates
[z_{0}:z_{1}]
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Then the transition maps of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}(n)
give the gluing map

(10)

U_{0}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to U_{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{1},\ ([z_{0}:z_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}])\mapsto([z_{0}:z_{1}],[y_{0}:\frac{z_{1}^{n}}{z_{0}^{n}}y_{1}]).

Let
F_{0}=\{z_{0}=0\}
,
F_{\infty}=\{z_{1}=0\}
,
E_{0}=\{y_{0}=0\}
,
E_{\infty}=\{y_{1}=0\}
, and
[w_{0}:w_{1}]
be the coordinates on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
over the
U_{1}
.

Proposition 7
.

The divisor class group
\mathrm{Cl}(\Sigma_{n})={\mathbb{Z}}E_{\infty}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}F_{0}
. The intersection matrix has the following form

(11)

F_{0}^{2}=0,\ F_{0}\cdot E_{\infty}=1,\ E_{\infty}^{2}=-n.

Proof.

The only non-obvious thing is
E_{\infty}^{2}=-n
. We have

\mathrm{div}\Big(\overbrace{\frac{y_{1}}{y_{0}}}^{z_{0}\neq 0}\Big)=\mathrm{div}\Big(\overbrace{\frac{z_{0}^{n}}{z_{1}^{n}}\frac{w_{1}}{w_{0}}}^{z_{1}\neq 0}\Big)=nF_{0}+E_{\infty}-E_{0},

which implies the equivalence
E_{\infty}\sim E_{0}-nF_{0}
. This yields

E_{\infty}^{2}=E_{\infty}\cdot(E_{0}-nF_{0})=0-n=-n.

This completes the proof.
∎

Using the Proposition
7
and Moishezon-Nakai theorem we can easily describe a set of ample divisors in the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}E_{\infty}\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}F_{0}
.

Corollary 1
.

The divisor
aE_{\infty}+bF_{0}
is ample if and only if
a>0
and
b>an
.

Proof.

The following calculations prove the required

\displaystyle(aE_{\infty}+bF_{0})E_{\infty}>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow-na+b>0,

\displaystyle(aE_{\infty}+bF_{0})F_{0}>0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow a>0.

∎

It is well known that
\Sigma_{n}
is a toric variety. It is easy to write the action of a torus in our notations on the chart
\{z_{0}\neq 0,y_{1}\neq 0\}
:

(t_{1},t_{2})\cdot([z_{0}:z_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}])=([z_{0}:t_{1}z_{1}],[t_{2}y_{0}:y_{1}]),\ (t_{1},t_{2})\in{\mathbb{G}}_{m}^{2},

which extends to the action on the whole variety. The following proposition shows that the condition from Corollary
1
gives very ample divisors.

Proposition 8
.

[
8
, Theorem 6.1.16]

Let
X
be a complete smooth toric variety. Then a divisor
D
on
X
is ample if and only if
D
is very ample.

Now we need to find the systems of rational functions generating the set of global sections of very ample divisors. The next proposition gives the answer.

Proposition 9
.

Let
aE_{\infty}+bF_{0}
be a very ample divisor on
\Sigma_{n}
. Then

H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}(aE_{\infty}+bF_{0}))=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}k+nm\leq b,\\
0\leq m\leq a,\ k\geq 0\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{K}}\frac{z_{1}^{k}}{z_{0}^{k}}\frac{y_{0}^{m}}{y_{1}^{m}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4
it is enough to find all rational functions of the form
\frac{z_{1}^{k}}{z_{0}^{k}}\frac{y_{0}^{m}}{y_{1}^{m}}
since the functions
\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}}
and
\frac{y_{0}}{y_{1}}
are the basic characters of the torus action. We have

\overbrace{\frac{z_{1}^{k}}{z_{0}^{k}}\frac{y_{0}^{m}}{y_{1}^{m}}}^{z_{0}\neq 0}=\overbrace{\frac{z_{1}^{k+nm}}{z_{0}^{k+nm}}\frac{w_{0}^{m}}{w_{1}^{m}}}^{z_{1}\neq 0},

and hence

\mathrm{div}(\frac{z_{1}^{k}}{z_{0}^{k}}\frac{y_{0}^{m}}{y_{1}^{m}})=kF_{\infty}+mE_{0}-(k+nm)F_{0}-mE_{\infty}.

Thus we have a condition

\mathrm{div}(\frac{z_{1}^{k}}{z_{0}^{k}}\frac{y_{0}^{m}}{y_{1}^{m}})+aE_{\infty}+bF_{0}=kF_{\infty}+mE_{0}+(b-k-nm)F_{0}+(a-m)E_{\infty}\geq 0,

and it follows that
k+nm\leq b,\ 0\leq m\leq a,\ k\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

We introduce the following notation that will be convenient below:

(12)

d_{n,a,b}\coloneq\dim_{{\mathbb{K}}}H^{0}(\Sigma_{n},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{n}}(aE_{\infty}+bF_{0})).

This value can be calculated directly.

Corollary 2
.

d_{n,a,b}=(a+1)(b+1)-na(a+1)/2
.

Proof.

The dimension of

is equal to the number of integer points
(k,m)
in the area
\{k\geq 0,\ a\geq m\geq 0,\ k+nm\leq b\}
by Proposition
9
. It is easy to calculate that the number of such points is
(a+1)(b+1)-na(a+1)/2
.
∎

B_{1}={\mathbb{K}}[z,w]/(z^{5},zw,w^{3})\ \text{or}\ B_{2}={\mathbb{K}}[z,w]/(z^{5},z^{2}w,w^{2}).

First, consider the case of
B_{1}

\varphi_{1}:A_{1,3}^{\prime}\to B_{1}
be an isomorphism of local algebras. Then

t\mapsto az+bw+X,s\mapsto cz+dw+Y,\ \text{where}\ X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}_{B_{1}}^{2}\ \text{and}\ ad-bc\neq 0.

Since
s^{2}=0
,
(cz+dw+Y)^{2}=c^{2}z^{2}+d^{2}w^{2}+Y_{1}=0
,
Y_{1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{B_{1}}^{3}
. But this yields
c=0
and
d=0
, which is a contradiction.

Now consider the case of
B_{2}
. Let
\varphi_{2}:A_{1,3}^{\prime}\to B_{2}
be an isomorphism of local algebras. Then

t\mapsto az+bw+X,s\mapsto cz+dw+Y,\ \text{where}\ X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}_{B_{1}}^{2}\ \text{and}\ ad-bc\neq 0.

Since
s^{2}=0
,
(cz+dw+Y)^{2}=c^{2}z^{2}+2cdzw+Y_{1}=0
,
Y_{1}\in\mathfrak{m}_{B_{2}}^{3}
. This yields that
c=0
and hence
a\neq 0
. Now we have

s\mapsto dw+Dz^{3}+Fz^{4},\ t\mapsto az+bw+Gzw+Hz^{2}+Kz^{3}+Lz^{4}.

We know that
t^{4}\mapsto a^{4}z^{4}\neq 0
. But

t^{2}s\mapsto(a^{2}z^{2}+abzw+2aHz^{3}+(H^{2}+2aK)z^{4})(dw+Dz^{3}+Fz^{4})=0,

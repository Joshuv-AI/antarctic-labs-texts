Abstract
. It is well known that the controllability property of partial differential equations (PDEs) is closely linked to the proof of an observability inequality for the adjoint system, which, sometimes, involves analyzing a spectral problem associated with the PDE under consideration. In this work, we study a series of spectral issues that ensure the controllability of the renowned Korteweg-de Vries equation on a star-graph. This investigation reduces to determining when certain functions, associated with this spectral problem, are entire. The novelty here lies in presenting this detailed analysis in the context of a star graph structure.



1.
Introduction

In mathematics and physics, a quantum graph is a network structure consisting of vertices connected by edges, where each edge hosts a differential or pseudo-differential equation. When each edge is equipped with a natural metric, the graph is called a metric graph. A typical example is a power network, where power lines (edges) connect transformer stations (vertices); in this case, the differential equations could represent the voltage along each line, with boundary conditions at the vertices ensuring that the sum of currents across all edges at each vertex is zero.

Linus Pauling initially studied quantum graphs in the 1930s as models of free electrons in organic molecules. They also appear in various mathematical contexts, such as quantum chaos, waveguide studies, photonic crystals, and Anderson localization—the phenomenon where wave diffusion is absent in a disordered medium. Quantum graphs are also used as a limiting model for shrinking thin wires. They have become significant in mesoscopic physics as theoretical tools for understanding nanotechnology. Another simplified version of quantum graphs was introduced by Freedman
et al.
in
[
28
]
.

Beyond solving the differential equations for specific applications, typical questions in quantum graph theory include well-posedness, controllability, and identifiability. For instance, controllability might involve determining the necessary inputs to bring a system to a desired state, such as ensuring sufficient power supply throughout a network. Identifiability could involve selecting measurement points, like pressure gauges in a water pipe network, to detect issues such as leaks.

Dispersive systems on graph structure

In recent years, the study of nonlinear dispersive models on metric graphs has gained significant attention from mathematicians, physicists, chemists, and engineers (see

35

\mathcal{G}
, a metric graph with
N
half-lines of the form
(0,+\infty)
that connect at a common vertex
\nu=0
. On each edge, a nonlinear equation, such as the nonlinear Schrödinger equation, is defined (see works by Adami

A key challenge in this analysis is the vertex, where the star graph may exhibit bifurcation or multi-bifurcation behavior, especially in more complex graph structures.

Other nonlinear dispersive systems on graphs also yield interesting results. For instance, regarding the well-posedness theory, Cavalvante
[
20

[

[

on star graphs, generates contraction semigroups.

Notably, notable contributions have been made in the areas of control theory and inverse problems. Ignat

1.2.
Background of control theory

Research on the control and stabilization of the KdV equation originated with the work of Russell and Zhang
[
44
,
45
,
46
,
49
]
, who examined internal control of the KdV equation under periodic boundary conditions. Since then, extensive studies have focused on the control and stabilization of the KdV equation (see
[
22
,
27
,
29
,
42
,
43
,
48
]
and references therein).

Considering a bounded domain, the control problem was presented in a pioneering work of Rosier
[
42
]
that studied the following system

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}u+u\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=0&\text{ in }(0,L)\times(0,T),\\
u(0,t)=0,\text{ }u(L,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}u(L,t)=g(t)&\text{ in }(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,L),\end{cases}

where the boundary value function
g(t)
is considered as a control input. The author answered the following problem for the system (
1.1
).

Exact controllability problem:

Given
T>0
and
u_{0},u_{T}\in L^{2}(0,L)
, can one find an appropriate control input
g(t)\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that the corresponding solution
u(x,t)
of (
1.1
) satisfies

(1.2)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad u(x,T)=u_{T}(x)?

Rosier
[
42
]
investigated boundary control of the KdV equation on the finite domain
(0,L)
with Dirichlet boundary conditions (
1.1
). He first analyzed the associated linear system:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=0,&\text{in }(0,L)\times(0,T),\\
u(0,t)=0,\text{ }u(L,t)=0,\;\partial_{x}u(L,t)=g(t),&\text{in }(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),&\text{in }(0,L),\end{cases}

and discovered the
critical length phenomenon
, where the exact controllability of system (
1.3
) depends on the domain length
L
. Specifically, exact controllability in
L^{2}(0,L)
is achieved if and only if
L
does not belong to the set

(1.4)

\mathcal{N}:=\left\{\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\sqrt{k^{2}+kl+l^{2}}\,:\,k,l\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\right\}.

Using a fixed-point argument, Rosier extended this controllability result from the linear to the nonlinear system for cases where
L\notin\mathcal{N}
.

In the case where
L\in\mathcal{N}
, Rosier demonstrated in
[
42
]
that the associated linear system (
1.3
) is not controllable. Specifically, there exists a finite-dimensional subspace of
L^{2}(0,L)
, denoted as
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(L)
, that cannot be reached from
0
by the linear system. More precisely, for any nonzero state
\psi\in\mathcal{M}
, if
g\in L^{2}(0,T)
and
u\in C([0,T];L^{2}(0,L))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))
satisfies (
1.3
) with the initial condition
u(\cdot,0)=0
, then
u(\cdot,T)\neq\psi
. A spatial domain
(0,L)
is termed
critical
for system (
1.3
) if its length
L
belongs to
\mathcal{N}
.

When the spatial domain
(0,L)
is critical, one usually would not expect the corresponding nonlinear system (
1.1
) to be exactly controllable, as the linear system (
1.3
) is not. It thus came as a surprise when Coron and Crépeau showed in
[
27
]
that the nonlinear system (
1.1
) is still locally exactly controllable even though its spatial domain is critical with its length
L=2k\pi
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
satisfying

\not\exists(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}\text{}\text{ with }\text{}m^{2}+mn+n^{2}=3k^{2}\text{}\text{ and }\text{}m\neq n.

For those values of
L
, the unreachable space
\mathcal{M}
of the associated linear system is a one-dimensional linear space generated by the function
1-\cos(x)
. As for the other types of critical domains, the nonlinear system (
1.1
) was shown later by Cerpa
[
21
]
, and Cerpa and Crépeau in
[
23
]
to be local, large time exactly controllable.

It is important to point out that if we change the control of position in the boundary condition of (
1.3
), for example

u(0,t)=h(t),\text{ }u(L,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}u(L,t)=0\quad\text{ in }(0,T)

or

(1.5)

u(0,t)=0,\text{ }u(L,t)=f(t),\text{ }\partial_{x}u(L,t)=0\quad\text{ in }(0,T),

then we can not explicitly characterize the critical sets for the KdV equation. However, for the boundary conditions in (
1.5
), it was established in
[
29
]
the existence of a countable set of critical lengths
\mathcal{N}^{*}\subset(0,+\infty)
which can be described as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{*}=\left\{L\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\backslash\ \{0\};\begin{array}[]{l}\exists(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\text{ \ such that \ }ae^{a}=be^{b}=-(a+b)e^{-(a+b)}\\
\text{ and }L^{2}=-\left(a^{2}+ab+b^{2}\right)\end{array}\right\}

For more details, we mention
[
22
,
29
]
.

After
97^{\prime}
, some authors tried to prove the critical set phenomenon for the KdV equation with some boundary condition, and we can cite, for example,
[
29
,
26
]
, and the references therein. However, for this set considered in these works, the authors were not allowed to explicitly characterize the set. Twenty years later, in
[
17
]
, another boundary condition was considered. The authors introduced the KdV equation with Neumann conditions. Capistrano-Filho
et al.
investigated the following boundary control system

(1.8)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}u+u\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=0&\text{ in }(0,L)\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{x}^{2}u(0,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}^{2}u(L,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}u(L,t)=h(t)&\text{ in }(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,L).\end{cases}

First, the authors studied the following linearized system associated with (
1.8
),

(1.9)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+(1+\beta)\partial_{x}u+\partial_{x}^{3}u=0&\text{ in }(0,L)\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{x}^{2}u(0,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}^{2}u(L,t)=0,\text{ }\partial_{x}u(L,t)=h(t)&\text{ in }(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)&\text{ in }(0,L),\end{cases}

where
\beta
is a given real constant. For any
\beta\neq-1
, considering the following set

\mathcal{R}_{\beta}:=\left\{\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3(1+\beta)}}\sqrt{k^{2}+kl+l^{2}}\,:k,\,l\,\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{k\pi}{\sqrt{\beta+1}}:k\in\mathbb{N}^{\ast}\right\}.

The authors showed the following results:

(i)

If
\beta\neq-1
, the linear system (
1.9
) is exactly controllable in the space
L^{2}(0,L)
if and only if the length L of the spatial domain
(0,L)
does not belong to the set
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
.

(ii)

If
\beta=-1
, then the system (
1.9
) is not exactly controllable in the space
L^{2}(0,L)
for any
L>0
.

As done in
[
42
]
, the controllability of the nonlinear system (
1.8
) holds using a fixed point argument. Moreover, the set
\mathcal{R}_{\beta}
is completely characterized. Note that, when
\beta=0
,
\mathcal{N}
(see (
1.4
)) is a proper subset of
\mathcal{R}_{0}
. The linear system (
1.9
) has more critical length domains than that of the linear system (
1.3
). In the case of
\beta=-1
, every
L>0
is critical for the system (
1.9
). By contrast, removing the term
\partial_{x}u
from the equation in (
1.3
), every
L>0
is not critical for the system (
1.3
).

To conclude this section, we also define the following set

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}=\left\{L\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\backslash\ \{0\};\begin{array}[]{l}\exists(a,b)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\text{ \ such that \ }a^{2}e^{a}=b^{2}e^{b}=(a+b)^{2}e^{-(a+b)}\\
\text{ and }L^{2}=-\left(a^{2}+ab+b^{2}\right)\end{array}\right\}

As we will prove in the Appendix
C
, the set
\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}
is a countable, non-empty new critical set in the literature of controllability of KdV equations.

1.3.
Setting of problem and functional framework

This work focuses on investigating the exact controllability of the linear KdV equation on a star-shaped network. The equation governing the dynamics is given by

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}(t,x)+\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,x)+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}(t,x)=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ \forall j=2,\dots,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0)+g_{0}(t),&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,\quad\partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\end{cases}

where
\alpha>\frac{N}{2}
. The conditions at the central node are motivated by previous studies
[
4
,
20
,
40
,
41
]
. Following
[
41
]
,
u_{j}
represents the dimensionless, scaled deflection from the rest position, and
v_{j}
denotes the velocity along a branch
j
of long water waves. Thus, we have:

(1.12)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}+u_{j}\partial_{x}u_{j}=0,&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
v_{j}=u_{j}-\frac{1}{6}u_{j}^{2}+2\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j},&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N.\end{cases}

Assuming the water level at the central node is constant, and the net flux is zero, the following natural conditions arise

u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),\quad t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,

and

\sum_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}(t,0)v_{j}(t,0)=0,\quad t\in(0,T).

Linearizing (
1.12
) around zero yields the following boundary conditions

u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),\quad t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,

and

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\frac{N}{2}u_{1}(t,0),\quad t\in(0,T).

With this context, given
N\geq 2
, consider a star-shaped network
\mathcal{T}
with
N
edges described by intervals
I_{j}=(0,l_{j})
,
l_{j}>0
for
j=1,\dots,N
. Denoting by
e_{1},...,e_{N}
the edges of
\mathcal{T}
one have
\mathcal{T}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N}e_{j}
. To be precise, in this work, we will study the control properties for
N
linear KdV equations posed on
\mathcal{T}
with the following boundary conditions

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T)\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\end{cases}

where
u=(u_{1},...,u_{N})
stands the state of the system,
p_{j}
and
g_{j}
are the controls inputs and
\alpha>\frac{N}{2}
. This system was studied in
[
39

2N-2

Our goal in this article is to prove the controllability of (
1.13
) using a reduced number of boundary controls. In most of our results, we assume
l_{j}=L>0
for all
j=1,\dots,N
and we are interested in answering the following question:

New boundary conditions:

Are there other boundary conditions such that the controllability for the system (
1.13
) holds?

1.13

with a critical set, that is, in some appropriate set of boundary conditions, the controllability holds if and only if the length
l_{j}
avoids specific values or for all

1.4.
Main result and heuristic

With these previous notations in hand, let us now present our first main result. Given an integer number
N\geq 2
and
m=0,\dots,N
, this work takes in consideration the following control configuration:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}p_{j}=0,\ j=1,\dots,m\text{ and }g_{j}=0,\ j=m+1,\dots,N,&\text{ if }1<m<N,\\
g_{j}=0,\ j=1,...,N,&\text{ if }m=0,\\
p_{j}=0,\ j=1,...,N,&\text{ if }m=N,\end{cases}

resulting in the following control system

(1.16)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}+\partial_{x}u_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}u_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(t,0)=u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(t,0)=-\alpha u_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=g_{j}(t),&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,m,\\
u_{j}(t,l_{j})=p_{j}(t),\ \ \ \ \ \partial_{x}u_{j}(t,l_{j})=0,&t\in(0,T),\ j=m+1,\dots,N,\\
u_{j}(0,x)=u_{j}^{0}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N.\end{cases}

For
m=0
, we omit the fourth equation from the system above, whereas for
m=N
, we omit the fifth equation. Note that the adjoint system associated with (
1.16
) is given by

(1.17)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}-\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}-\partial_{x}^{3}\varphi_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
\varphi_{j}(t,0)=\varphi_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,0)=(\alpha-N)\varphi_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T)\\
\varphi_{j}(t,l_{j})=\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
\varphi_{j}(T,x)=\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N.\end{cases}

Thus, as usual in the control theory (see, for instance,
[
34
]
), the controllability result for the system (
1.16
) is equivalent to proving the following
observability inequality

\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right),\ \forall\varphi^{T}\in X_{m},

for the solution of the adjoint system (
1.17
). The first main result of this work is given below and mixes the Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions (see Figs. 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6).

We will provide a brief overview of our approach. To establish well-posedness, the classical semigroup theory typically used for the KdV equation in networks, as seen in various studies
[
4
,
24
,
40
,
41
]
, proves to be less effective for handling Dirichlet controls. Instead, studies like
[
16
,
19
,
29
,
30
]
mainly rely on “solutions by transposition” combined with interpolation methods. However, these techniques are still underdeveloped for the KdV equation on networks, where the network structure introduces challenging trace terms in the computations. These terms are addressed in a recent work by the second author
[
39
]
and in this article.

For the control problem, it is well known in the literature
[
34
]
that proving the exact controllability of the system (
1.1
) is equivalent to establishing an inequality of
observability
for the linearized system associated with (
1.1
). To achieve this, we generally employ the multiplier method and compactness arguments, which reduce the task to demonstrating a unique continuation property for the state operator.

In our context, proving this unique continuation property involves analyzing the spectral problem associated with the relevant linear operator. Specifically, after applying the Fourier transform, the question becomes one of determining when a certain quotient of entire functions remains entire. We introduce a polynomial function
q:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
and a family of functions

N_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C},

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and each restriction
N_{\alpha}(\cdot,L)
is entire for any
L>0
. We then define a family of functions
f_{\alpha}(\cdot,L)
by

f_{\alpha}(\mu,L)=\frac{N_{\alpha}(\mu,L)}{q(\mu)},

within its maximal domain. The problem then reduces to finding
L>0
such that there exists
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
for which
f_{\alpha}(\cdot,L)
is entire. In some works, such as
[
17
,
18
,
42
]
and references therein, this approach provides an explicit characterization of the set of critical lengths when it exists. However, in many cases, this set cannot be explicitly determined
[
7
,
15
,
16
,
26
]
.

In this context, this work makes two contributions. First, we examine the well-posedness of system (
1.16
) under mixed boundary conditions, specifically focusing on Dirichlet and Neumann conditions. Second, we provide, for the first time, a detailed analysis of a star graph structure where the function
f_{\alpha}
, relevant to our scenarios, is entire. This analysis leads to the characterization of critical sets
\mathcal{N}
defined by (
1.4
),
\mathcal{N}^{*}
defined by (
1.2
), and their union
\mathcal{N}\cup\mathcal{N}^{*}
, akin to those introduced by Rosier
[
42
]
and Glass and Guerrero
[
29
]
. Additionally, we identify a new critical set, denoted as
\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}
. Due to the increased complexity of the functions
N_{\alpha}
in our setting compared to these earlier works, our approach involves a refined and meticulous adaptation of the analysis of these functions.

1.5.
Outline

Our work is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we conduct a detailed analysis of the well-posedness of problem (
1.13

3
is to establish the observability inequality for system (
1.16
) with boundary controls acting under mixed boundary conditions, specifically Dirichlet and Neumann conditions, as well as under full Neumann boundary conditions and full Dirichlet boundary conditions. Here, we carefully examine the function

associated with our setting, leading to Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Appendix
A
provides a review of trace estimates for the single KdV equation, while Appendix
B
demonstrates how the observability inequality ensures controllability, and Appendix
C
explores various properties of the set
\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}
.

2.
Well-posedness analysis

In this section, we examine the well-posedness of problem (
1.13
), as established in
[
39
]
, along with its corresponding adjoint problem. To achieve this, we verify the Kato smoothing property for the solution traces, which is essential for deriving an observability inequality that characterizes critical lengths. Additionally, we investigate the regularity of solutions to the adjoint system when the data belong to regular spaces. Note that the positive constants
C
introduced here may vary from line to line.

2.1.
Weak solutions

The next proposition was proved in
[
39
]
, and it gives the well-posedness to the problem

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\partial_{t}v_{j}-\partial_{x}v_{j}-\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}=f_{j},&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
v_{j}(t,0)=v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\ j=2,\dots,N,\\
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,0)=(\alpha-N)v_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\ j=1,\dots,N,\\
v_{j}(T,x)=0,&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N,\end{cases}

for
f=(f_{1},...,f_{N})\in L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
. This result is useful to establish a notion of solution for (
1.13
).

Proposition 2.1
.

If
f\in L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
there exists a unique solution

v\in\mathbb{B}_{1}:=C([0,T],\mathbb{H}^{1}_{r}(\mathcal{T}))\cap L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{H}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))

of (
2.1
). Moreover, there exists
C_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathbb{B}_{1}}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}v_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C_{1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}.

Assume
u_{j},p_{j},g_{j},v_{j},f_{j}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times[0,l_{j}])
and
u_{j}^{0}\in C^{\infty}[0,l_{j}]
. Suppose that
u=(u_{1},...,u_{N})
is a solution of (
1.13
) subject to the data
p_{j},g_{j},u_{0}^{j}
and
v=(v_{1},...,v_{N})
is a solution of (
2.1
) subject to
f=(f_{1},...,f_{N})
. Multiplying (
2.1
) by
u_{j}
, integrating by parts and using the boundary conditions in (
1.13
) and (
2.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}u_{j}^{0}(x)v_{j}(0,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}p_{j}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,l_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}g_{j}(t)\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}f_{j}u_{j}.

Identifying
u_{j}^{0}\in C^{\infty}([0,l_{j}])\subset L^{2}(0,l_{j})
with the functional
(u_{j}^{0})^{*}\in H_{r}^{-1}(0,l_{j})
given by

\displaystyle(u_{j}^{0})^{*}(w)=\int_{0}^{l_{j}}u_{j}^{0}w,\ \ \forall w\in H_{r}^{-1}(0,l_{j}),

we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l_{j}}u_{j}^{0}(x)v_{j}(0,x)=\langle u_{j}^{0},v_{j}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H_{r}^{-1}(0,l_{j})\times H_{r}^{1}(0,l_{j})}.

Now identifying
g_{j}\in C^{\infty}([0,T])\subset L^{2}(0,T)
with the functional
g_{j}^{*}\in H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
given by

\displaystyle g_{j}^{*}(h)=\int_{0}^{T}g_{j}h,\ \ \forall h\in H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T),

we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}g_{j}(t)\partial_{x}v_{j}(t,l_{j})=\langle g_{j},\partial_{x}v_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}f_{j}u_{j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle u_{j}^{0},v_{j}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H_{r}^{-1}(0,l_{j})\times H_{r}^{1}(0,l_{j})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}p_{j}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,l_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle g_{j},\partial_{x}v_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}.

This motivates the next definition, giving us a weak solution for the system (
1.13
).

Definition 2.1
.

Given
T>0
,
u_{0}=(u_{1}^{0},u_{2}^{0},\cdots,u_{N}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}_{r}^{-1}(\mathcal{T})
,
p\in\left(L^{2}(0,T)\right)^{N}
and
g\in(H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T))^{N}
a solution by transposition of (
1.13
) is a function
u\in L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
satisfying

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}f_{j}u_{j}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle u_{j}^{0},v_{j}(0,\cdot)\rangle_{H_{r}^{-1}(0,l_{j})\times H_{r}^{1}(0,l_{j})}-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}p_{j}(t)\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(t,l_{j})

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\langle g_{j},\partial_{x}v_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\rangle_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)\times H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)},

for all
f\in L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
, where
v
is the solution of (
2.1
) corresponding to
f
.

The next result provides the well-posedness of (
1.13
), proved in
[
39
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T>0
be given. For all
u^{0}\in\mathbb{H}_{r}^{-1}(\mathcal{T})
,
p\in\left(L^{2}(0,T)\right)^{N}
and
g\in(H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T))^{N}
, there exists a unique solution by transposition for (
1.13
).

2.2.
The adjoint system

The adjoint system associated with (
1.13
) is given by (
1.17
). The next result ensures that the system (
1.17
) admits a unique solution.

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
the system (
1.17
) admits a unique solution
\varphi\in\mathbb{B}
which satisfies

(2.2)

\|\varphi\|_{\mathbb{B}}\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})},

for
C>0
, and

(2.3)

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leq\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2},

for all
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
.

Proof.

Define the operator
Au=-\partial_{x}u-\partial_{x}^{3}u
with domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})\cap\mathbb{H}^{1}_{e}(\mathcal{T}),\ \sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}u_{j}(0)=-\alpha u_{1}(0)\right\},

where

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}u=\left(\partial_{x}^{k}u_{1},...,\partial_{x}^{k}u_{N}\right).

Observe that
A
is the operator associated with (
1.13
) when
p_{j}=g_{j}=0
. The adjoint operator of
A
is given by
A^{*}v=\partial_{x}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v
with domain

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(A^{*})=\left\{v\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})\cap\mathbb{H}^{1}_{e}(\mathcal{T});\ \partial_{x}v_{j}(0)=0,\ j=1,\dots,N,\ \ \sum_{j=1}^{N}\partial_{x}^{2}v_{j}(0)=(\alpha-N)v_{1}(0)\right\}.

A^{*}
is the operator associated with (
1.17
). According to
[
4
]
,
A
is closed,
A
and
A^{*}
are dissipative, so
A^{*}
generates a strongly semigroup of contractions on
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
which will be denoted by
\{S(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
. From the semigroup theory it follows that, for any
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
the problem (
1.17
) has a unique (mild) solution
\varphi=S(T-\cdot)\varphi^{T}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))
. Note that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}=\|S(T-\cdot)\varphi^{T}\|_{C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))}\leq\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})},\ \forall\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}).

To see that
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{H}^{1}_{e}(\mathcal{T}))
and to obtain the estimates (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), first suppose
\varphi^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})
. In this case, from semigroup theory, we have

\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}))\cap C([0,T],\mathcal{D}(A^{*})).

Assume
q_{j}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times[0,l_{j}])
with
q_{j}(t,0)=q_{1}(t,0)
. Multiplying the first equation of (
1.17
) by
\varphi_{j}
, integrating by parts, and using the boundary conditions, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x)

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}^{2}(t,0)+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}(0,x).

Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\leq\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2},

that is, the map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\delta:\mathcal{D}(A^{*}):&\rightarrow&L^{2}(0,T)\\
\varphi^{T}&\mapsto&\partial_{x}\varphi(\cdot,l_{j})\end{array}

is continuous. Now, choosing
q_{j}=x
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi_{x}(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{T+2M}{3}\right)\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2},

where
M=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq N}l_{j}
. Then, (
2.2
) holds for every
\varphi^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})
and consequently the map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Gamma^{*}:(\mathcal{D}(A^{*}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})})&\rightarrow&(\mathbb{B},\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{B}})\\
\varphi^{T}&\mapsto&\Gamma^{*}\varphi^{T}=\varphi=S(T-\cdot)\varphi^{T}\end{array}

is continuous. By an argument of density we extend the maps
\Gamma^{*}
and
\delta
to
(\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})})
. For each
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
we refer to
\delta(\varphi^{T})
when write
\partial_{x}\varphi(t,l_{j})
and, in this sense, (
2.3
) holds for every
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
. Moreover, since

\displaystyle\Gamma\varphi^{T}=S(T-\cdot)\varphi^{T}\ \ \forall\varphi^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})

by density and continuity, it follows that

\displaystyle\Gamma\varphi^{T}=S(T-\cdot)\varphi^{T},\ \ \forall\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})

and thus (
2.2
) was verified.
∎

2.3.
Trace estimates

Now, we proceed to prove that the solutions of (
1.17
) possess the sharp Kato smoothing property, namely

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,l_{j};H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),\ k=0,1,2,\ \ j=1,\dots,N.

The first result ensures the hidden regularity.

Proposition 2.3
.

For
T>0
and every
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, the solution
\varphi
of (
1.17
) posses the hidden regularity

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,l_{j};H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),\ k=0,1,2,\ \ j=1,\dots,N.

Moreover, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})},\ j=1,\dots,N.

Proof.

Our proof will be split into two cases.
We divide it into two cases.

Case 1:

N\in\mathbb{N}
and
l_{j}=L
, for every
j=1,\dots,N
.

For each
j=1,\dots,N
define
\xi_{j}=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{j}
. Note that

\xi_{j}\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}=C([0,T];L^{2}(0,L))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))

and its solves

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\xi_{j}+\partial_{x}\xi_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}\xi_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
\xi_{j}(t,0)=\xi_{j}(t,L)=\partial_{x}\xi_{j}(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\xi_{j}(T,x)=\varphi_{1}^{T}(x)-\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{cases}

Thanks to Corollary
A.1
we get
\partial_{x}^{k}\xi_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,L;H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
, for
k=0,1,2
, and

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|\xi_{j}\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\xi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C\left(\|\varphi_{1}^{T}-\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}\right)\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}

where
C>0
is a constant.

Now, since
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{e}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))
and
H^{1}(0,L)\hookrightarrow C([0,L])

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi_{1}(t,0)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq c\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{e}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}

for some
c>0
. From Proposition
2.2
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}|\varphi_{1}(t,0)|^{2}dt\leq\tilde{C}\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2},

for
\tilde{C}>0
. Then
\varphi_{1}(\cdot,0)\in L^{2}(0,T)\hookrightarrow H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
(this embedding is an isometry) and by the last inequality

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}=\|\varphi_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}\leq\tilde{C}\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}.

Defining
\displaystyle\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}
we conclude that

\psi\in\mathcal{Z}_{T}=C([0,T];L^{2}(0,L))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(0,L))

and it solves the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi+\partial_{x}\psi+\partial_{x}^{3}\psi=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
\psi(t,L)=\partial_{x}\psi(t,0)=0,\ \ \partial_{x}^{2}\psi(t,0)=(\alpha-N)\varphi_{1}(t,0),&t\in(0,T),\\
\psi(T,x)=\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&x\in(0,L).\end{cases}

Then, the Proposition
A.6
gives us
\partial_{x}^{k}\psi\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,L;H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
for
k=0,1,2
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\psi(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}+|\alpha-N|\|\varphi_{1}(\cdot,0)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}\right),\end{aligned}

for some positive constant
C
. Using (
2.5
) we get another constant, still denoted by
C>0
such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{Z}_{T}}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\psi(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C(|\alpha-N|)\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{j}+\psi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{j})+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\varphi_{j}=N\varphi_{1},

that is,

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\xi_{j}+\psi\right).

But we know that
\psi,\xi_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,L;H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
for
j=1,\dots,N
, so we conclude that

\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{1}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,L;H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right),

for
k=0,1,2
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{1}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\xi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}+\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\psi(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\right).

Due to the inequalities (
2.4
) and (
2.6
) it follows that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{1}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}

where
C=C(\alpha,N)>0
. Now, since
\varphi_{j}
is the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}+\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}\varphi_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,L),\\
\varphi_{j}(t,0)=\varphi_{1}(t,0),\ \varphi_{j}(t,L)=\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\varphi_{j}(T,x)=\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&x\in(0,L),\end{cases}

as
\varphi_{1}(t,0)\in H^{\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
, Corollary
A.1
ensures that
\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}\in L_{x}^{\infty}\left(0,L;H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)\right)
for
k=0,1,2
and
using (
2.7
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})},

for
C>0
showing this case.

Case 2:

N\in\mathbb{N}
and arbitrary lengths
l_{j}>0,\ j=1,\dots,N
.

Define
L=\max_{j=1,\dots,N}l_{j}
and

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{T}(x):=\begin{cases}\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
0,&x\in(0,L)\backslash(0,l_{j}).\end{cases}

Denote by
\tilde{\varphi}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{L}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}))\cap L^{2}(0,T,\mathbb{H}^{1}_{e}(\tilde{\mathcal{T}}))
the solution of (
1.16
) associated to data
\tilde{\varphi}^{T}=(\tilde{\varphi}_{1}^{T},...,\tilde{\varphi}_{N}^{T})\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{T}})=\left(L^{2}(0,L)\right)^{N}
. From the previous case, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\tilde{\varphi}^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{T})}}

It follows from the definition that
\|\tilde{\varphi}^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{T})}}=\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}
. Also from the definition we have
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}^{T}=\varphi_{j}^{T}
in
(0,l_{j})
so
\varphi_{j}
and
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}
solve the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\varphi_{j}+\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}\varphi_{j}=0,&t\in(0,T),\ x\in(0,l_{j}),\\
\varphi_{j}(t,0)=\varphi_{1}(t,0)=\varphi_{j}(t,l_{j})=\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(t,0)=0,&t\in(0,T),\\
\varphi_{j}(T,x)=\varphi_{j}^{T}(x),&x\in(0,l_{j}).\end{cases}

Then by uniqueness of solution we obtain
\tilde{\varphi}_{j}=\varphi_{j}
in
(0,T)\times(0,l_{j})
. Consequently, for
j=1,\dots,N
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,l_{j})}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2}\sup_{x\in(0,L)}\|\partial_{x}^{k}\tilde{\varphi}_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{\frac{1-k}{3}}(0,T)}\leq C\|\tilde{\varphi}^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\tilde{\mathcal{T})}}=C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}.

which concludes case 2 and, consequently, the proof of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

To finish this subsection, we establish that the traces
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,0),\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})
belong to
L^{2}(0,T)
and depend continuously on the initial data.

Proposition 2.4
.

Given
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, we have that
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\in L^{2}(0,T)
, for any
x\in[0,l_{j}]
. Moreover, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,x)\right)^{2}dt\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2},\ \forall\ \varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}),

for some positive constant
C
.

Proof.

Consider the operator

P_{j}:H^{3}(0,l_{j})\rightarrow L^{2}(0,l_{j})

defined by
P_{j}w=\partial_{x}w+\partial_{x}^{3}w
. The graph norm in
H^{3}(0,l_{j})
associated to
P_{j}
is

\|w\|_{P_{j}}=\|w\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}+\|P_{j}w\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}.

From
[
38
, Lemma A.2]
there exists a constant
d_{1}>0
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}\leq d_{1}\|w\|_{P_{j}},\ \forall w\in H^{3}(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N.

On the other hand, given
v=(v_{1},...,v_{N})\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})
we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{D}(A^{*})}=\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}+\|\partial_{x}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\geq\|v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\|v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}

as well as

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\mathcal{D}(A^{*})}\geq\|\partial_{x}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}v_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq\|\partial_{x}v_{j}+\partial_{x}^{3}v_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}.

Therefore, this yields

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|v_{j}\|_{P_{j}}\leq 2\|v\|_{\mathcal{D}(A^{*})},\ j=1,\dots,N.

Let us first assume that
\varphi_{j}^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})
.

Claim:
We claim that
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}\in C([0,T]\times[0,l_{j}])
.

Indeed, since
\varphi\in C([0,T],\mathcal{D}(A^{*}))
we have
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}(0,l_{j})\hookrightarrow C([0,l_{j}])
. Hence, fixed
x\in[0,l_{j}]
, for some constant
x>0
we get

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,x)-\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t_{0},x)|

\displaystyle\leq c\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)-\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}\leq d_{2}\|\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)-\varphi_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{H^{3}(0,l_{j})}

for any
t,t_{0}\in[0,T]
. Using (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,x)-\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t_{0},x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)-\varphi_{j}(t_{0},\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{D}(A^{*})},

for
C>0
and from where we conclude that
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\in C([0,T])
and, consequently,
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\in L^{2}(0,T)
, showing the claim.

Using Proposition
2.3
and the fact that
L^{2}(0,T)\hookrightarrow H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)
is an isometry we obtain, for each
x\in[0,l_{j}]
, that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(t,x)\right)^{2}dt=\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}=\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,x)\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{3}}(0,T)}^{2}\leq C\|\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}.

Finally, with an argument of density and continuity, we extend the result for every
\varphi_{j}^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
. The result is thus achieved.
∎

2.4.
Regularity

With the spaces (
1.14
) and (
1.15
) in hand, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

If the final data
\varphi^{T}\in X_{m}
, then the corresponding solution
\varphi
of the system (
1.17
) has the additional regularity
\varphi\in L^{2}(0,T;Y_{m})
with

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;Y_{m})}\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}.

for some positive constant
C
. Furthermore, the estimate

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{T}+\frac{6}{\sigma}\right)\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}+2\left(\alpha-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}^{2}(t,0)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j}),

is verified for every
\varphi^{T}\in X_{m}
, where
\displaystyle\sigma:=\min_{1\leq j\leq N}l_{j}
.

Proof.

Once we have that

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;Y_{m})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(0,l_{j})}^{2}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{H}_{e}^{1}(\mathcal{T}))}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}dt,

from the first part of Proposition
2.2
, it is sufficient to show that
\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j}))
for
j=m+1,\dots,N
with

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j}))}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}

for some constant
\tilde{C}>0
.

To do this, first assume
\varphi^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})\cap X_{m}
, the result for all
\varphi^{T}\in X_{m}
follows by an argument of density. In this case, it is clear that
\varphi_{j}(t,\cdot)\in H^{2}(0,l_{j})
. Multiplying the adjoint system (
1.17
) by
(l_{j}-x)\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}
, integrating by parts, and using the boundary conditions, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}(l_{j}-x)\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(0,x)+\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j})(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{l_{j}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,0)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}(l_{j}-x)\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x).

By Young’s inequality and Propositions
2.2
and
2.4
, we conclude that there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}(\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j})(t,l_{j})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}.

Defining
M=\displaystyle\max_{1\leq j\leq N}l_{j}
, for
j=m+1,\dots,N
, and, again, using Propositions
2.2
and
2.4
, it follows that exists another constant, still denote by
C>0
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}.

Combining this with the Proposition
2.2
we obtain,
\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j})
and

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j}))}\leq C^{*}\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}},\ \forall\ \varphi^{T}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{*})\cap X_{m},

where
C^{*}>0
is a constant and
j=m+1,\dots,N
. Since
\mathcal{D}(A^{*})\cap X_{m}
is dense in
X_{m}
, the map

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{D}(A^{*})\cap X_{m}&\rightarrow&L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j}))\\
\varphi_{T}&\mapsto&\varphi_{j}\end{array}

extends continuously to the entire
X_{m}
with

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{j}\|_{L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}(0,l_{j}))}\leq C^{*}\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}},\ \forall\varphi^{T}\in X_{m}

and therefore (
2.10
) holds with
\tilde{C}=(N-m)(C^{*})^{2}
.

For the second part, multiplying the adjoint system (
1.17
) by
t\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}
, integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}t\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}t\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}t\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,0)+\frac{T}{2}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x),

where it follows that

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j}),\ j=1,\dots,N.

On the other hand, by multiplying the adjoint system (
1.17
) by
x\varphi_{j}
, integrating by parts, and using the boundary conditions, we get

\displaystyle\frac{l_{j}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}x\varphi_{j}^{2}(0,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}x\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x)+\frac{3}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}

which implies, in particular, that

(2.12)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})\leq\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x)+\frac{3}{l_{j}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}\ ,\ j=1,\dots,N.

Now, multiplying the adjoint system (
1.17
) by
t\varphi_{j}
, integrating by parts, and using the boundary conditions, we also have that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}+2\left(\alpha-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}^{2}(t,0)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j}),

for
j=1,\dots,N
. Using (
2.11
) and (
2.13
) we get that

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}+2\left(\alpha-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}^{2}(t,0)+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j}).

Observe that from (
2.12
), the following holds

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})\leq 2\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}(T,x)+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\frac{6}{l_{j}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2},

and consequently

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\varphi_{j}^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{T}+\frac{6}{\sigma}\right)\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}+2\left(\alpha-\frac{N}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{1}^{2}(t,0)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\varphi_{j}^{T}\|_{L^{2}(0,l_{j})}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j})+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\int_{0}^{T}\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{2}(t,l_{j}),

giving the result.
∎

3.
A study of entire functions

From this point, our analysis focuses on establishing the observability inequality. Let us first discuss the observability inequality.

3.1.
Observability inequality

We are here interested in proving the following observability inequality, that is, the existence of a constant
C>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}\leq C\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right),\ \forall\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T}).

Observe that to check (
3.1
) it is sufficient to check

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{X_{m}}^{2}\leq\tilde{C}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right),\ \forall\varphi^{T}\in X_{m},

for some positive constant
\tilde{C}
.

Indeed, given
\varphi^{T}\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, since
X_{m}
is dense in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
there exists a sequence
(\varphi^{T,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X_{m}
such that
\varphi^{T,n}\rightarrow\varphi^{T}
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
. From Propositions
2.2
and
2.4
it follows that
\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{n}(\cdot,l_{j})\rightarrow\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})
and
\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{n}(\cdot,l_{j})\rightarrow\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})
in
L^{2}(0,T)
. Furthermore, using the Poincaré inequality, we get, for some
c>0
,

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T,n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}\leq c\|\varphi^{T,n}\|_{X_{m}}.

If (
3.2
) holds, then

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T,n}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}\leq c^{2}\tilde{C}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}^{n}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}^{n}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right)

and passing to the limit, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{T}\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})}^{2}\leq c^{2}\tilde{C}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|\partial_{x}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{N}\|\partial_{x}^{2}\varphi_{j}(\cdot,l_{j})\|_{L^{2}(0,T)}^{2}\right)

so (
3.1
) holds with
C=c^{2}\tilde{C}>0
.

Remark 3.1
.

The advantage of working with data in
X_{m}
for the adjoint system is that, in addition to the additional regularity for the solution, the estimate given in the Proposition
2.5
carries information related to the two types of controls used, while the corresponding estimate for data in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
carries only information related to the Neumann controls types.

From now on, we will focus our efforts on describing the lengths

3.2.
Entire function related to each condition

Each observability is linked to a corresponding entire function, which plays a crucial role in the analysis. From this point forward, we will organize our analysis into the following six cases:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bullet\ N=2\text{ and }m=1;\\
&\bullet\ N\geq 3\text{ and }m=1;\\
&\bullet\ N\geq 3\text{ and }m=N-1;\\
&\bullet\ N>3\text{ and }1<m<N-1;\\
&\bullet N\geq 2\text{ and }m=N\text{ (Full Neumann)};\\
&\bullet N\geq 2\text{ and }m=0\ \text{ (Full Dirichlet)},\\
\end{aligned}

remembering that

, for all

3.2.1.
Case
N=2
and
m=1

In this case, we consider a slightly more general problem, namely

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{j}+\varphi_{j}^{\prime}+\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime\prime}=0,&x\in(0,l_{j}),\ j=1,2,\\
\varphi_{2}(0)=\varphi_{1}(0),&\\
\varsigma_{Ne}\varphi_{1}^{\prime\prime}(0)+\varsigma_{Di}\varphi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(0)=0,&\\
\varphi_{j}(l_{j})=\varphi_{j}^{\prime}(0)=0,&j=1,2,\\
\varphi_{1}^{\prime}(l_{1})=\varphi_{2}^{\prime\prime}(l_{2})=0,&\end{cases}

where
\varsigma_{Ne}
,
\varsigma_{Di}>0
. Problem (
3.6
) corresponds to the case
\varsigma_{Ne}=\varsigma_{Di}=1
. We can prove that the observability inequality (
3.2
) holds for any

. This is a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
L>0
and suppose
l_{1}=l_{2}=L
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, does not exist
\varphi\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})\backslash\{0\}
satisfying (

).

Proof.

For
\psi\in\mathbb{L}^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, we introduce the notation

\hat{\psi_{j}}(\xi)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{l_{j}}\psi_{j}(x)e^{-ix\xi}dx.

Consider
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and suppose that
\varphi\in\mathbb{H}^{3}(\mathcal{T})
satisfies (
3.8
). We will show that the unique solution for (
3.8
) is the trivial one, that is,
\varphi=0
.

Multiplying the system (
3.8
) by
e^{-ix\xi}
, integrating by parts in
(0,l_{j})
and using the boundary conditions we get, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\left[(i\xi)^{3}+i\xi+\lambda\right]

\displaystyle=\left[(1-\xi^{2})\varphi_{j}(0)-i\xi e^{-il_{j}\xi}\varphi_{j}^{\prime}(l_{j})-e^{-il_{j}\xi}\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(l_{j})+\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\right],\ j=1,2.

Writing
\lambda=ip
with
p\in\mathbb{C}
and multiplying this equation by
i
yields

\displaystyle\left(\xi^{3}-\xi-p\right)\hat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=i\left[(1-\xi^{2})\varphi_{j}(0)-i\xi e^{-il_{j}\xi}\varphi_{j}^{\prime}(l_{j})-e^{-il_{j}\xi}\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(l_{j})+\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(0)\right],\ j=1,2.

Setting

\displaystyle\kappa=\varphi_{1}(0)=\varphi_{j}(0),\ \ \ \delta_{j}=-\varphi_{j}^{\prime}(l_{j}),\ \ \ \gamma_{j}=-\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(l_{j}),\ \ \ \beta_{j}=\varphi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(0)

one can write

\displaystyle\left(\xi^{3}-\xi-p\right)\hat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)

\displaystyle=i\left[(1-\xi^{2})\kappa+i\delta_{j}\xi e^{-il_{j}\xi}+\gamma_{j}e^{-il_{j}\xi}+\beta_{j}\right],\ \ j=1,2.

Since
\delta_{1}=\gamma_{2}=0
and
l_{1}=l_{2}=L
we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left(\xi^{3}-\xi-p\right)\hat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi)=i\left[(1-\xi^{2})\kappa+\gamma_{1}e^{-iL\xi}+\beta_{1}\right],\ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C},

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left(\xi^{3}-\xi-p\right)\hat{\varphi}_{2}(\xi)=i\left[(1-\xi^{2})\kappa+i\delta_{2}\xi e^{-iL\xi}+\beta_{2}\right],\ \ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C}.

Now, defining
f=\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}
and
\beta=\beta_{1}-\beta_{2}
, from the above identities, we obtain

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left(\xi^{3}-\xi-p\right)\hat{f}(\xi)=i\left[(\gamma_{1}-i\delta_{2}\xi)e^{-iL\xi}+\beta\right],\ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C}.

Claim
3.1
.

If
\gamma_{1}=0
or
\beta=0
then
\varphi=0
.

Proof.

If
\gamma_{1}=0
then
\varphi_{1}
solves the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{1}+\varphi^{\prime}_{1}+\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}=0,\\
\varphi_{1}(L)=\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(L)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{1}(L)=0,\end{cases}

so
\varphi_{1}=0
. Consequently,
\varphi_{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{2}+\varphi^{\prime}_{2}+\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}=0,\\
\varphi_{2}(0)=\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(0)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{2}(0)=0,\end{cases}

then
\varphi_{2}=0
and therefore
\varphi=0
.

Now, if
\beta=0
then
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}
so
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are solutions to the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{i}+\varphi^{\prime}_{i}+\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{i}=0,\quad i=1,2\\
\varphi_{i}(0)=\kappa,\quad\varphi_{i}^{\prime}(0)=0,\quad\varphi_{i}^{\prime\prime}(0)=\beta_{1}.\end{cases}

By uniqueness of solution, it follows that
\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}
, which implies

. Hence
\hat{f}=0
and since
\beta=0
, (
3.11

\displaystyle(\gamma_{1}-i\delta_{2}\xi)e^{-iL\xi}=0,\ \forall\xi\in\mathbb{C}.

Evaluating this equality at
\xi=0
, we obtain
\gamma_{1}=0
, and, as seen before, this leads us to

, showing Claim

∎

Assuming

(3.12)

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}\neq 0\text{ \ and \ }\beta\neq 0,

with the following claim in hand, the Lemma

holds for the case

Claim

, then we necessarily have

. To do this, let us multiply (

\displaystyle-i\left(\xi^{3}-\xi\right)\hat{\varphi}_{1}(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})\kappa+\gamma_{1}e^{-iL\xi}+\beta_{1},\ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C},

(3.14)

\displaystyle-i\left(\xi^{3}-\xi\right)\hat{\varphi}_{2}(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})\kappa+i\delta_{2}\xi e^{-iL\xi}+\beta_{2},\ \ \forall\ \xi\in\mathbb{C}.

Evaluating (

and

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}e^{-iL}=-\beta_{1}\text{ \ and \ }\gamma_{1}e^{iL}=-\beta_{1}

\displaystyle e^{-iL}=e^{iL}.

Define

If

Proof.

and
\mu_{2}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\mu_{0}+\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.18)

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\mu_{1}+\mu_{1}\mu_{2}+\mu_{0}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=1,

(3.19)

\displaystyle\mu_{0}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=-\lambda.

If
P_{\lambda}
has a triple root thus, by (
3.17

\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=0.

So, from (
3.19

\lambda=0
. With this, the Claim
3.2
ensures that
\varphi=0
.

Now, suppose that
P_{\lambda}
has a double root, let us say
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\sigma

3.17

\mu_{0}=-2\sigma
and substituting it in (
3.18
)

(3.20)

\displaystyle\sigma=\pm\frac{i}{\sqrt{3}}.

By the theory of ordinary differential equations, the set

\displaystyle\left\{e^{-2\sigma x},e^{\sigma x},xe^{\sigma x}\right\}

\displaystyle\lambda y+y^{\prime}+y^{\prime\prime\prime}=0.

Note that
\varphi_{2}
solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{2}+\varphi^{\prime}_{2}+\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{2}=0,\\

ensures the existence of

Consequently

(3.21)

\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(x)=d_{0}(-2\sigma)e^{-2\sigma x}+d_{1}\sigma e^{\sigma x}+d_{2}(e^{\sigma x}+x\sigma e^{\sigma x}),

(3.22)

Observe that the boundary condition
\varphi_{2}(L)=0

\displaystyle d_{1}+Ld_{2}=-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}.

On the other hand, the condition
\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{2}(L)=0

\displaystyle d_{1}\sigma+d_{2}(2+L\sigma)=-4d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d_{1}+Ld_{2}=-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}\\
d_{1}\sigma+d_{2}(2+L\sigma)=-4d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}\end{array}\right.

Below, we solve this system for

and
d_{2}
using Gauss-Jordan elimination (we denote by

the
k
-th row of a matrix):

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&L&-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}\\
\sigma&2+L\sigma&-4d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}\end{array}\right]\sim\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}+\frac{3d_{0}\sigma Le^{-3\sigma L}}{2}\\
0&1&-\frac{3d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}}{2}\end{array}\right].

Therefore

(3.23)

\displaystyle d_{1}=-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}+\frac{3d_{0}\sigma Le^{-3\sigma L}}{2}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }d_{2}=-\frac{3d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}}{2}.

Using this values in (

\displaystyle\varphi_{2x}(x)

\displaystyle=d_{0}(-2\sigma)e^{-2\sigma x}+\left(-d_{0}e^{-3\sigma L}+\frac{3d_{0}\sigma Le^{-3\sigma L}}{2}\right)\sigma e^{\sigma x}+\left(-\frac{3d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}}{2}\right)(e^{\sigma x}+x\sigma e^{\sigma x}).

Since
\varphi_{2x}(0)=0
we get that

\displaystyle-2d_{0}\sigma-d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}+\frac{3d_{0}\sigma^{2}Le^{-3\sigma L}}{2}-\frac{3d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}}{2}=0.

Multiplying the previous equality by
2/\sigma
, we have

\displaystyle d_{0}\left(-4-2e^{-3\sigma L}+3\sigma Le^{-3\sigma L}-3e^{-3\sigma L}\right)=0.

If
d_{0}\neq 0
, then it follows that

\displaystyle-4-2e^{-3\sigma L}+3\sigma Le^{-3\sigma L}-3e^{-3\sigma L}=0\iff\left(-5+3\sigma L\right)e^{-3\sigma L}=4.

Due to (
3.20

\displaystyle\left(-5\pm\frac{3iL}{\sqrt{3}}\right)e^{\pm\frac{3iL}{\sqrt{3}}}=4.

Taking the absolute value in the previous equality, we see that

\displaystyle\left|-5\pm\frac{3iL}{\sqrt{3}}\right|=4\implies 25+3L^{2}=16\implies 3L^{2}<0,

a contradiction. Therefore
d_{0}=0
and from (
3.23

, which results in

. Hence, using the boundary conditions of (

solves the problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{1}+\varphi^{\prime}_{1}+\varphi^{\prime\prime\prime}_{1}=0,\\
\varphi_{1}(0)=\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(0)=\varphi^{\prime\prime}_{1}(0)=0,\end{cases}

that is,
\varphi_{1}=0
and therefore

, giving the Claim
3.3
.
∎

According to claims

Claim

Proof.

\displaystyle(1-\xi_{s}^{2})\kappa\frac{1}{\gamma_{1}}+e^{-iL\xi_{s}}+\frac{\beta_{1}}{\gamma_{1}}=0,\ s=0,1,2.

Lemma 3.7
.

Proof.

Consider the function

Claim
3.5
.

If

\psi\equiv 0
if and only if
\varphi_{j}\equiv 0
for all

Proof.

One direction is direct. In the other hand, if

\varphi_{j}
solves

\begin{cases}\lambda\varphi_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}_{j}+\varphi^{\prime}_{j}=0,\\

Since

, from

∎

The condition

is necessary. In fact, if not, we can not control

. By the previous claim, it is enough to study when we have a non-trivial function
\psi
. We can immediately see that
\psi

Considering

and

, we get

(3.32)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\tilde{\lambda}\theta+\theta^{\prime}+\theta^{\prime\prime\prime}=0,\\

Claim

.

If

admits multiples roots, then
\theta=0
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Claim
3.3
. Multiplying the system (
3.32
) by

and using the boundary conditions we get, for every

,

and

. If

has a triple root, then by Girard’s relations

. Therefore,
\theta
satisfies

, which implies
\theta=0
. If
P_{\tilde{\lambda}}
has a double root
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\sigma
, by Girard’s relations

and the solution
\theta
of (
3.32
) can be written as

\theta(x)=d_{0}e^{-2\sigma x}+d_{1}e^{\sigma x}+d_{2}xe^{\sigma x}.

The boundary condition
\theta(0)=0
gives us

. While,
\theta^{\prime}(0)=\theta^{\prime}(L)
give us respectively

Using

, we get

\begin{cases}3\sigma d_{1}+d_{2}=0\\
\sigma d_{1}(2e^{-3\sigma L}+1)+d_{2}(1+\sigma L)=0.\end{cases}

The previous system has a non-trivial solution if and only if

. In that case
d_{2}=-3\sigma d_{1}
On the other hand, the condition
\theta^{\prime\prime}(L)=0
provides that

\displaystyle d_{1}\sigma+d_{2}(2+L\sigma)=-4d_{0}\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}.

Using
d_{0}=-d_{1}

\sigma L=2\sigma e^{-3\sigma L}

and

follows.
∎

By the previous claim,
\theta(x)=d_{0}e^{\mu_{0}x}+d_{1}e^{\mu_{1}x}+d_{2}e^{\mu_{2}x}
, where
\mu_{0}
,
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
are the simple roots of the characteristic polynomial

. By imposing the boundary conditions, we deduce the following overdetermined system

\begin{cases}d_{0}+d_{1}+d_{2}=0\\

\mu_{0}d_{0}e^{\mu_{0}L}+\mu_{1}d_{1}e^{\mu_{1}L}+\mu_{2}d_{2}e^{\mu_{2}L}=0\\
\mu_{0}^{2}d_{0}e^{\mu_{0}L}+\mu_{1}^{2}d_{1}e^{\mu_{1}L}+\mu_{2}^{2}d_{2}e^{\mu_{2}L}=0.\end{cases}

By calling

,

and

, we obtain the following matrix system

\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\
ae^{a}&be^{b}&ce^{c}\\
a&b&c\\
a^{2}e^{a}&b^{2}e^{b}&c^{2}e^{c}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}C_{1}\\
C_{2}\\
C_{3}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

By Gauss-Jordan elimination

\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\
ae^{a}&be^{b}&ce^{c}\\
a&b&c\\
a^{2}e^{a}&b^{2}e^{b}&c^{2}e^{c}\end{bmatrix}\sim\begin{bmatrix}1&1&1\\
ae^{a}&be^{b}&ce^{c}\\
0&b-a&c-a\\
0&0&(c-a)(ce^{c}-be^{b})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}&A_{a,b,c}&\\
0&0&B_{a,b,c}\end{bmatrix}.

We have a non-trivial function
\theta
if and only if

•

The rank of
A_{a,b,c}
is one.

•

The rank of
A_{a,b,c}
is two and
B_{a,b,c}=0
.

Easy computations give us

•

rank(A_{a,b,c})=1
, if and only if
a=b=c
. This case is not possible, because the roots are simple.

•

rank(A_{a,b,c})=2

(1)

a=b\neq c
,
be^{b}=ce^{c}
. Not possible.

(2)

a=b
,
be^{b}\neq ce^{a}
. Not possible.

(3)

ae^{a}\neq be^{b}
,
ce^{c}(a-b)+be^{b}(c-a)+ae^{a}(b-c)=0
.

(4)

a\neq b
,
ae^{a}=be^{b}=ce^{c}
.

On the other hand, again the roots are simple
B_{a,b,c}=0
if and only if
be^{b}=ce^{c}

0=ce^{c}(a-b)+be^{b}(c-a)+ae^{a}(b-c)=(b-c)(ae^{a}-ce^{c}),

which implies
ae^{a}=ce^{c}=be^{b}
, that contradicts the third case. Finally, in the fourth case, we have a non-trivial solution if
ae^{a}=be^{b}=ce^{c}
. Recall that Girard’s relations
a+b+c=0
and
L^{2}=-(a^{2}+ab+b^{2})
, which correspond to the expression (
1.2
) of the critical lengths
\mathcal{N}^{*}
.

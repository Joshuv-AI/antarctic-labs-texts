Protein kinase A fuels positive feedback loops during Th1 cell differentiation
Authors: Jinfang Zhu
Published: 2025

CD4 T-helper (Th) cells play important roles in orchestrating adaptive immune responses (1). The differentiation of distinct Th cell subsets including classical type 1 T-helper (Th1) and type 2 T-helper (Th2) cells, from naive CD4 T cells is initiated by T cell receptor (TCR) activation and cytokine signaling, and it is sustained by positive feedback loops consisting of transcription factors, cytokines, and cytokine receptors. However, how these feedback mechanisms are cell-intrinsically controlled and what signaling networks integrate TCR and cytokine signaling during T cell differentiation remain elusive. In the study recently published at the PNAS, Zhao et al. (2) report that protein kinase A-cAMP response element binding protein (PKA-CREB) signaling fuels these positive feedback loops at a late stage of Th1 cell differentiation.

Th1 cells expressing the signature cytokine interferon (IFN)-γ are indispensable for host defense against intracellular pathogens including viruses, bacteria, and protozoa, whereas Th2 cells, by producing type 2 cytokines, including interleukin (IL)-4, IL-5, and IL-13, are critical for fighting extracellular parasites including helminths (3, 4). When dysregulated, Th1 cells provoke autoimmunity, whereas Th2 cells cause allergy. Besides TCR stimulation, cytokine signaling plays a critical role in T cell differentiation. For Th1 cell differentiation, STAT1 and STAT4 activation by IFN-γ and IL-12, respectively, induce the expression of the master transcription factor T-bet (5, 6). T-bet plays an important role in inducing the expression of Th1-associated effector molecules including IFN-γ and also upregulates the expression of the receptor subunit IL-12Rβ2, allowing cells to efficiently respond to IL-12 (7). The report by Zhao et al. (2) demonstrates that PKA-CREB signaling is critical for the upregulation of IL-12Rβ2 and STAT4 expression at a late stage of Th1 cell differentiation, allowing cells to efficiently respond to IL-12 stimulation, the main driving force for Th1 cell fate (Fig. 1).

Th1 cell differentiation involves several positive feedback loops consisting of the IL-12-IL-12Rβ2-STAT4-T-bet-Il12rb2 and IFN-γ-STAT1-T-bet-Ifng pathways. To investigate the effect of PKA during T cell differentiation, Zhao et al. generated a T cell–specific PKA knockout mouse model in which only T cells are deficient in two major catalytic (alpha and beta) subunits of PKA. Using a colitis model mediated by Th1 cells differentiated from adoptively transferred naive CD4 T cells, Zhao et al. first showed that PKA-deficient cells, instead of causing severe colitis, induce lung inflammation mediated by Th2 cells. These results indicate that PKA plays a critical role in modulating the balance between Th1 and Th2 responses in vivo.

A reduction in Th1 response could be due to an enhanced Th2 response in vivo. Zhao et al. then using an in vitro culture system to confirm that PKA-deficient CD4 T cells fail to efficiently differentiate into Th1 cells. Compared to control Th1 cells, PKA-deficient “Th1” cells express lower levels of T-bet and produce less IFN-γ. IL-12Rβ2 expression, presumably induced by TCR signaling at the early stage (i.e., 1 d after T cell activation), is observed in both control and PKA-KO cells. IL-12Rβ2 expression is further increased 48 to 72 h after T cell activation in control CD4 T cells, allowing these cells to robustly respond to IL-12. However, in the absence of PKA signaling, late responsiveness to IL-12 becomes strikingly defective, which is correlated with low expression of IL-12Rβ2 by these PKA-deficient cells. Zhao et al. further demonstrate that the downstream effector molecule of PKA is CREB, and it is responsible for epigenetic changes at the Il12rb2 and Stat4 loci.

Cyclic AMP (cAMP) activates PKA. Indeed, Zhao et al. show that a potent activator of adenylyl cyclase, Forskolin, induces the cAMP-PKA-CREB pathway and promotes Il12rb2 transcription. By contrast, a small molecule inhibitor that suppresses CREB activity phenocopies the effect of PKA deficiency. Interestingly, PKA activity dramatically increases at a late stage of Th1 cell differentiation with the same kinetics as IL-12Rβ2 upregulation. Zhao et al. then discovered that prolonged TCR stimulation results in the induction of both Adrenomedullin (Adm) and its receptor component Ramp3, which can activate PKA via cAMP. Interestingly, PKA-deficient cells express lower levels of Ramp3 than control Th1 cells indicating that Adm-Ramp3-mediated PKA activation also forms its own positive feedback loop. It is unknown whether the IL-12/STAT4 pathway and/or T-bet contribute to the upregulation of Ramp3 expression. Nevertheless, treatment of CD4 T cells with Adm induces the expression of Ramp3 and Il12rb2. On the other hand, the antagonistic form of Adm suppresses IFN-γ production. Furthermore, deletion of Crlr, the gene encoding calcitonin receptor-like receptor (CRLR), the other receptor component for Adm, results in impaired expression of Il12rb2, Stat4, and Ifng. Thus, the Adm–RAMP3–CRLR–cAMP–PKA–CREB pathway is responsible for reinforcing IL-12-mediated positive feedback loop during Th1 cell differentiation.

In the study recently published at the PNAS, Zhao et al. (2) report that PKA-CREB signaling fuels these positive feedback loops at a late stage of Th1 cell differentiation.

T-bet expression is also affected by the PKA-CREB pathway, but the PKA-CREB pathway does not affect the Tbx21 gene accessibility at its gene body. While the accessibility at distal regulatory elements of the Tbx21 gene may change directly or indirectly related to the PKA-CREB pathway, or the PKA-CREB pathway may directly regulate T-bet expression without affecting its epigenetic status, T-bet upregulation by PKA is likely an indirect effect of enhanced IL-12 signaling. Since T-bet has been reported to upregulate Il12rb2 expression, the PKA-CREB pathway reinforces the positive feedback loop mediated by the IL-12-STAT4-T-bet-IL-12Rβ2 axis. How CREB functions together with T-bet in regulating Il12rb2 expression requires further investigation.

During Th1 cell differentiation, Th2 cell fate is actively suppressed; this is partly through T-bet-mediated inhibition of the expression and/or the functions of GATA3, the master transcription factor for Th2 cell differentiation (8, 9). It is unclear whether the PKA-deficient Th1 cells generated in vitro display a Th2 phenotype, but an enhanced Th2 response is observed in the absence of PKA both in vitro and in vivo. Although IL-4 and GATA3 expression are upregulated in the absence of PKA, the PKA–CREB pathway does not directly regulate gene accessibility at the Il4 and Gata3 loci. The direct targets of PKA–CREB pathway in Th2 cells in this study are not clear. Heightened expression of Gfi-1, a transcription factor that is induced by IL-4 and capable of selecting the cells expressing high levels of GATA3 to grow, may explain increased IL-13 production by PKA-deficient Th2 cells. It will be interesting to assess whether the PKA pathway also regulates IL-4 signaling. It is worth noting two previous reports showing that the activity of the Il5 promoter in response to cAMP is GATA3-dependent (10) and that GATA3 activity is regulated by cAMP-dependent but PKA-independent p38 mitogen-activated protein kinase activation (11).

Besides Th1 and Th2 cells, CD4 T cells consist of other subsets including Th17 (IL-17-producing CD4 T) cells, regulatory T cells (Tregs), and follicular T-helper cells (12–14). Many subsets including Th17 and Treg cells can trans-differentiate into Th1 cells, which could be an important process for the pathogenesis of autoimmunity (15). Whether PKA is also involved in such trans-differentiation processes requires further investigation. In particular, it has been reported that IL-12 signaling is aborted in Tregs preventing them from differentiating into Th1 cells (16). Is PKA differentially regulated in Tregs? Can manipulation of the Adm–Ramp3–CRLR/cAMP–PKA–CREB pathway swing the balance between effector Th1 cells and Tregs? Addressing these questions may have important implications in autoimmunity and tumor immunity. The study published by Zhao et al. also opens several other intriguing questions: What is the role of PKA once Th1 cells are fully differentiated? Is the expression of Adm and Ramp3 Th1-specific? Can PKA regulate IL-12Rβ2 expression in other lymphoid subsets including NK, NKT, γδ Τ, CD8 T, and innate lymphoid cells? Besides Adm, what other molecules may activate PKA in T cells in vivo under physiological and pathological conditions?

In summary, Zhao et al. discovered that the Adm–RAMP3–CRLR–cAMP–PKA–CREB pathway activated at a late stage of T cell differentiation fuels the well-known cytokine signaling (i.e., IL-12-IL-12Rβ2-STAT4-T-bet) pathway to reinforce Th1 cell fate. This study not only significantly enhances our current understanding of the regulation of CD4 T cell fate determination but also highlights several potential drug targets for treating autoimmune diseases and for enhancing antitumor immunity.

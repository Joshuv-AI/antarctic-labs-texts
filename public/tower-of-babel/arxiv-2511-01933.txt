Extrapolation Problem for Continuous Time Periodically Correlated Isotropic Random Fields


Abstract
The problem of optimal linear estimation of functionals depending on the unknown values of a random field ζ ⁡ ( t , x ) \zeta(t,x) , which is mean-square continuous periodically correlated with respect to time argument t ∈ ℝ t\in\mathbb{R} and isotropic on the unit sphere S n {S_{n}} with respect to spatial argument x ∈ S n x\in{S_{n}} . Estimates are based on observations of the field ζ ⁡ ( t , x ) + θ ⁡ ( t , x ) \zeta(t,x)+\theta(t,x) at points ( t , x ) : t < 0 , x ∈ S n (t,x):t<0,x\in S_{n} , where θ ⁡ ( t , x ) \theta(t,x) is an uncorrelated with ζ ⁡ ( t , x ) \zeta(t,x) random field, which is mean-square continuous periodically correlated with respect to time argument t ∈ ℝ t\in\mathbb{R} and isotropic on the sphere S n {S_{n}} with respect to spatial argument x ∈ S n x\in{S_{n}} . Formulas for calculating the mean square errors and the spectral characteristics of the optimal linear estimate of functionals are derived in the case of spectral certainty where the spectral densities of the fields are exactly known. Formulas that determine the least favourable spectral densities and the minimax (robust) spectral characteristics are proposed in the case where the spectral densities are not exactly known while a class of admissible spectral densities is given.

In practice, however, complete information about the spectral density is impossible in most cases. To overcome this complication one finds parametric or nonparametric estimates of the unknown spectral densities or selects these densities by other reasoning.

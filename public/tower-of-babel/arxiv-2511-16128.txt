1.

A compact

-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
,
\ n\geq 2

\partial M

m
-quasi-Einstein manifold
, or simply

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\nabla^{2}u=\frac{u}{m}(Ric-\lambda g)&\text{ in }M,\\
u>0&\text{ on }\operatorname{int}(M),\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial M,\end{cases}

where

and

are constant (cf.
[
8
,
17
,
18

). Here,
\nabla^{2}u
stands for the Hessian of

and Ric is the Ricci tensor of
g

An
m
-quasi-Einstein manifold will be called

is constant, otherwise it will be

M^{n}
is an Einstein manifold, and
\partial M=\emptyset

[

When

[
4

[

]
for the case with boundary). In particular when

, assume in addition that

to recover the static equation
-(\Delta u)g+\nabla^{2}u-u\Ric=0

Choosing

, then (
1.1
) takes the form of

(1.2)

\Ric+\nabla^{2}\varphi-\frac{1}{m}\text{d}\varphi\otimes\text{d}\varphi=\lambda g,\text{ in }M.

When
\partial M=\emptyset
and
m=\infty
, an quasi-Einstein manifold is precisely a gradient Ricci soliton. The left hand side of the equation (

) is Bakry-Émery Ricci tensor, so quasi-Einstein manifolds are also associated with the study of diffusion operators
[
3

and smooth metric measure space; see related papers
[

,

,
24
,
25
,

. In physics, quasi-Einstein manifolds relate to the geometry of a degenerate Killing horizon and horizon limit; see, e.g.
[

2
,
29
]
.

For explicit examples of quasi-Einstein manifolds, see
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
23

24

give a description on

[

,

This paper focuses on nontrivial compact quasi-Einstein manifolds with

, and by
[

]

\lambda>0

Example 1.1
.

(i).
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
,

As for

In
[
19
]
, for Ricci soliton, the authors estimate

holds for some

and level set
{\Sigma_{s}}
of sufficiently large

, that is,

Check expression for Ricci tensor on level set (

Thanks to the classificatioin of complete locally conformally flat manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature by Zhu

, the compact closed manifold

On minimal conditions in semigroups and biacts
Craig Miller

Abstract
. We systematically study the minimal conditions on ℒ {\mathcal{L}} -, ℛ {\mathcal{R}} - and 𝒥 {\mathcal{J}} -classes, denoted by M L M_{L} , M R M_{R} and M J M_{J} , as well as the related notions of left/right/two-sided stability, in semigroups and biacts. In particular, we investigate the behaviour of these conditions with respect to quotients, substructures and extensions. Among other results, the following are proved. The conditions M L M_{L} , M R M_{R} and M J M_{J} are preserved under quotients (for both semigroups and biacts), but this is not the case for one- and two-sided stability. For semigroups, the conditions M L M_{L} , M R M_{R} and one- and two-sided stability are inherited by subsemigroups of finite Green index and also by bi-ideals (and hence by one- and two-sided ideals). Moreover, a semigroup satisfies M L M_{L} (or M R M_{R} ) if and only if both an ideal and the associated Rees quotient do, but the analogue of this result fails in both directions for the condition M J M_{J} , and in the reverse direction for one- and two-sided stability.

Introduction

Just as chain conditions have been instrumental in the development of the structure theory of rings, they have also played a significant role in semigroup theory. 3.
Minimal Conditions: Equivalent Formulations and Interconnections

In this section we formally introduce the conditions
M_{L}
,

Many of the results in this section regarding biacts extend known results for semigroups, with similar proofs.

(resp.

The following lemma provides equivalent formulations for the conditions

Lemma 3.3

Let

Note that we will use condition (3) of Lemma

The next result provides another formulation for the condition

Proposition 3.4

A semigroup

The reverse implication clearly follows immediately from the definition of left stability for semigroups. For the forward implication, let

The first part follows from the fact that, for any
s\in S
and

we have
a\geq_{\mathcal{L}}sa\geq_{\mathcal{L}}s^{2}a\geq_{\mathcal{L}}\cdots
in

For the second part, suppose that
A
is
{\mathcal{L}}
-periodic, and let
a,b\in A

a\leq_{\mathcal{L}}b
and
a\,\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\,b.
Then there exist

and

such that
a=sb
and
b=uav.
Then there exists some

such that
(us)^{n}b\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,(us)^{n+1}b
; this trivially holds if

and follows from
{\mathcal{L}}
-periodicity otherwise. Thus, there exists

(us)^{n}b=s^{\prime}(us)^{n+1}b.
Then

b=usbv=(us)^{n}bv^{n}=s^{\prime}us(us)^{n}bv^{n}=s^{\prime}usb=s^{\prime}ua\leq_{\mathcal{L}}a,

and hence

∎

Corollary 3.8
.

[

For semigroups

-biact is stable.

For a biact that satisfies

, satisfying

is equivalent to being left stable:

.

-biact that satisfies

. Then

Then clearly

Proposition 3.11
.

Let

and

-biact

(1)

satisfies

and
M_{R}
;

(2)

M_{J}
and is both
{\mathcal{L}}
-periodic and

(3)

.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). By Lemma
3.7
and its dual, the
(S,T)
-biact

is
{\mathcal{L}}
- and
{\mathcal{R}}
-periodic. To show that

satisfies

, consider a descending chain
a_{0}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}\cdots
in

Then there exist
s_{i}\in S^{1}
and

(

) such that

. Then

(

). For

\displaystyle a_{m}

\displaystyle=s_{m}\dots s_{1}a_{0}t_{1}\dots t_{m}=b_{m}t_{1}\dots t_{m}=x_{n}b_{n}t_{1}\dots t_{m}=x_{n}s_{n}\dots s_{1}a_{0}t_{1}\dots t_{m}

\displaystyle=x_{n}s_{n}\dots s_{1}c_{m}=x_{n}s_{n}\dots s_{1}c_{n}y_{n}=x_{n}s_{n}\dots s_{1}a_{0}t_{1}\dots t_{n}y_{n}=x_{n}a_{n}y_{n}.

(2)
\Rightarrow

(3)

4.
The Conditions
M_{L}
,
M_{R}
and
M_{J}

We begin this section by showing that the conditions
M_{L}
,
M_{R}
and
M_{J}
on biacts are closed under quotients.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups, let
A
be an
(S,T)
-biact, and let
\rho
be a congruence on
A.
For
K\in\{L,R,J\},
if
A
satisfies
M_{K}
then so does
A/\rho.

Proof.

We prove the result for
K=J
; the proofs for
L
and
R
are similar and slightly more straightforward.

Consider a chain
b_{0}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}b_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}\cdots
in
A/\rho.
Then there exist
s_{i}\in S^{1}
and
t_{i}\in T^{1}
(
i\in\mathbb{N}
) such that
b_{i}=s_{i}b_{i-1}t_{i}
. Choose
a_{0}\in A
such that
[a_{0}]_{\rho}=b_{0}
, and let
a_{i}=s_{i}\dots s_{1}a_{0}t_{1}\dots t_{i}
.
Then
[a_{i}]_{\rho}=b_{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{N},
and
a_{0}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{J}}\cdots
in
A.
Since
A
satisfies
M_{J}
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{m}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\,a_{n}
for all
n\geq m.
Therefore, for each
n\geq m
there exist
u_{n}\in S^{1}
and
v_{n}\in T^{1}
such that
a_{m}=u_{n}a_{n}v_{n}
. Then
b_{m}=[a_{m}]_{\rho}=u_{n}b_{n}v_{n}\in S^{1}b_{n}T^{1}
, so that
b_{m}\leq_{\mathcal{J}}b_{n}
. It follows that
b_{m}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\,b_{n}
for all
n\geq m.
Thus
A/\rho
satisfies
M_{J}
.
∎

We may quickly deduce that the conditions
M_{L}
,
M_{R}
and
M_{J}
are also preserved under semigroup quotients. First, we make the following observation.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S
be a semigroup and
\rho
be a congruence on
S.
For
\mbox{${\mathcal{K}}$}\in\{\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{R}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\}
and
a,b\in S,
we have
[a]_{\rho}\leq_{\mathcal{K}}[b]_{\rho}
in
S/\rho
if and only if
[a]_{\rho}\leq_{\mathcal{K}}[b]_{\rho}
in the
S
-biact
{{}_{S}}S_{S}/\rho
. Consequently, for
K\in\{L,R,J\},
the semigroup
S/\rho
satisfies
M_{K}
if and only if
{{}_{S}}S_{S}/\rho
satisfies
M_{K}
.

Proposition
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
together yield:

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
S
be a semigroup and
\rho
a congruence on
S.
For
K\in\{L,R,J\},
if
S
satisfies
M_{K}
then so does
S/\rho.

For each of the conditions
M_{L}
,
M_{R}
and
M_{J}
, a biact satisfies the condition if and only if a subact and the associated Rees quotient do:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
S
and
T
be semigroups, let
A
be an
(S,T)
-biact, and let
B
a subact of
A.
For
K\in\{L,R,J\},
the
(S,T)
-biact
A
satisfies
M_{K}
if and only if both
B
and
A/B
satisfy
M_{K}
.

Proof.

Let
K\in\{L,R,J\},
and let
{\mathcal{K}}
be the Green’s relation associated with
K.

(
\Rightarrow
) By Proposition
4.1
, the Rees quotient
A/B
satisfies
M_{K}
. That
B
satisfies
M_{K}
follows from the fact that the
{\mathcal{K}}
-preorder on
B
coincides with the restriction to
B\times B
of the
{\mathcal{K}}
-preorder on
A.

(
\Leftarrow
) Consider a descending chain
a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}a_{2}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}\cdots
in
A.
Then either
a_{i}\in A{\setminus}B
for all
i\in\mathbb{N},
in which case
a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}a_{2}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}\cdots
in
A/B,
or else there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{n}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}a_{n+1}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}\cdots
in
B.
In either case, since
A/B
and
B
satisfy
M_{K}
, it follows that the chain
a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}a_{2}\geq_{\mathcal{K}}\cdots
in
A
eventually stabilises, as required.
∎

The classes of semigroups satisfying the conditions
M_{L}
,
M_{R}
or
M_{J}
are not closed under subsemigroups. For example, the group of integers
\mathbb{Z}
certainly satisfies each of these conditions, but its subsemigroup
\mathbb{N}
satisfies none of them.

Consider a subsemigroup
T
of a semigroup
S.
We may consider
S
as a
T
-biact where
T
acts on
S
by left and right multiplication in
S
; we denote this biact by
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. (There is no ambiguity between the notations
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
and
{{}_{S}}I_{S}
, where
I
is an ideal of
S
: in the special case that
S=I=T,
the biacts
{{}_{S}}I_{S}
and
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
coincide.) We note that the Green’s relations on
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
coincide with the ‘
T
-relative’ Green’s relations introduced by Wallace in
[
18
]
. It was argued in
[
1
, Section 10.4]
that these relations are “potentially powerful tools”. They eventually found a significant application in the development of the notion of ‘Green index’ for subsemigroups
[
2
,
11
]
, defined below.

Now, observe that the
T
-biact
{{}_{T}}T_{T}
is a subact of
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. We denote the Rees quotient
{{}_{T}}S_{T}/{{}_{T}}T_{T}
by
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
. By Proposition
4.4
we have:

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
T
be a subsemigroup of a semigroup
S.
For
K\in\{L,R,J\},
the
T
-biact
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
satisfies
M_{K}
if and only if
T
and
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
satisfy
M_{K}
.

Note that for each Green’s relation
{\mathcal{K}}
on
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
, the
{\mathcal{K}}
-classes lie either entirely in
T
or entirely in
S{\setminus}T.
The
Green index
of
T
in
S
is defined to be one more than the number of
{\mathcal{H}}
-classes of
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
in
S{\setminus}T
; alternatively, it is the number of
{\mathcal{H}}
-classes of
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
. It is easy to see that
T
has finite Green index in
S
if and only if
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
has finitely many
{\mathcal{L}}
- and
{\mathcal{R}}
-classes.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
S
be a semigroup, and let
T
be a subsemigroup of
S
such that
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
has finitely many
{\mathcal{L}}
-classes. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

S
satisfies
M_{L}
;

(2)

T
satisfies
M_{L}
;

(3)

{{}_{T}}S_{T}
satisfies
M_{L}
.

Proof.

Clearly
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
satisfies
M_{L}
, so (2) and (3) are equivalent by Proposition
4.5
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). Suppose for a contradiction that there exists an infinite strictly descending chain
a_{0}>_{\mathcal{L}}a_{1}>_{\mathcal{L}}\cdots
in
T.
Then certainly
a_{0}\geq_{\mathcal{L}}a_{1}\geq_{\mathcal{L}}\cdots
in
S.
Since
S
satisfies
M_{L}
, there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{m}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{n}
in
S
for all
n\geq m.
Let
N
be the number of
{\mathcal{L}}
-classes of
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
. For each
i\in\{m,\dots,m+N\},
since
a_{i}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{m+N+1}
in
S,
there exists
s_{i}\in S
such that
a_{i}=s_{i}a_{m+N+1}
. If we had
s_{i}\in T
for some
i\in\{m,\dots,m+N\},
then we would have
a_{i}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{m+N+1}
in
T,
a contradiction. Thus
s_{i}\in S{\setminus}T
for each
i\in\{m,\dots,m+N\}.
Since there are only
N-1

{\mathcal{L}}
-classes of
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
contained in
S{\setminus}T,
by the pigeonhole principle there exist
i,j\in\{m,\dots,m+N\}
with
i<j
such that
s_{i}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,s_{j}
in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. Since
{\mathcal{L}}
is a right congruence, it follows that
a_{i}=s_{i}a_{m+N+1}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,s_{j}a_{m+N+1}=a_{j}
in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. But then
a_{i}\in Ta_{j}
, so that
a_{i}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{j}
in
T,
contradicting the fact that
a_{i}>_{\mathcal{L}}a_{j}
. Thus
T
satisfies
M_{L}
.

(3)
\Rightarrow
(1). Suppose for a contradiction that there exists an infinite strictly descending chain
a_{0}>_{\mathcal{L}}a_{1}>_{\mathcal{L}}a_{2}>_{\mathcal{L}}\cdots
in
S.
Then, for each
i\in\mathbb{N},
there exists
s_{i}\in S
such that
a_{i}=s_{i}a_{i-1}
. For each pair
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i\leq j,
let
\sigma(i,j)=s_{j}s_{j-1}\dots s_{i}
, interpreting
\sigma(i,i)
as
s_{i}
. Note that
a_{j}=\sigma(i,j)a_{i-1}
. Now, suppose that there are infinitely many
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma(1,i)\in S{\setminus}T.
Then, since there are only finitely many
{\mathcal{L}}
-classes of
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
contained in
S{\setminus}T,
there exist
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
with
i<j
such that
\sigma(1,i)\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,\sigma(1,j)
in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. But then

a_{i}=\sigma(1,i)a_{0}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,\sigma(1,j)a_{0}=a_{j}

in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
, and hence in
S,
a contradiction. Thus, there exists
i_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma(1,i_{1})\in T.
Now consider the elements
\sigma(i_{1}+1,i)
(
i\geq i_{1}+1
). By essentially the same argument as above, there exists
i_{2}\geq i_{1}+1
such that
\sigma(i_{1}+1,i_{2})\in T.
Continuing in this way, we obtain an infinite chain
0=i_{0}<i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots
of non-negative integers such that
\sigma(i_{k}+1,i_{k+1})\in T
for all
k\geq 0.
It follows that
a_{i_{1}}\geq_{\mathcal{L}}a_{i_{2}}\geq_{\mathcal{L}}\cdots
in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
. Since
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
satisfies
M_{L},
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
a_{i_{m}}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{i_{n}}
in
{{}_{T}}S_{T}
for all
n\geq m.
But then
a_{i_{m}}\,\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$}\,a_{i_{n}}
in
S
for all
n\geq m,
a contradiction. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
S
be a semigroup, and let
T
be a subsemigroup of
S
of finite Green index. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

S
satisfies
M_{L}
;

(2)

T
satisfies
M_{L}
;

(3)

{{}_{T}}S_{T}
satisfies
M_{L}
.

In the statement of Theorem
4.6
, we cannot replace the assumption that
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
has finitely many
{\mathcal{L}}
-classes with the weaker condition that
{{}_{T}}(S/T)_{T}
satisfies
M_{L}
, as demonstrated by the following example.

Example 4.8
.

Consider the group of integers
\mathbb{Z}
and its subsemigroup
\mathbb{N}.
Let
\mbox{${\mathcal{K}}$}\in\{\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{R}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\}.
Clearly
\mathbb{Z}
has a single
{\mathcal{K}}
-class, so certainly satisfies
M_{K}
. On the other hand, in
{{}_{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\cdots>_{\mathcal{K}}-2>_{\mathcal{K}}-1>_{\mathcal{K}}0>_{\mathcal{K}}1>_{\mathcal{K}}2>_{\mathcal{K}}\cdots,

and hence
{{}_{\mathbb{N}}}\mathbb{Z}_{\mathbb{N}}
does not satisfy
M_{K}
(and neither does
\mathbb{N}
). Moreover, it is clear that
{{}_{\mathbb{N}}}(\mathbb{Z}/\mathbb{N})_{\mathbb{N}}
satisfies
M_{K}
.

Open Problem 4.9
.

Do the analogues of Theorem
4.6
and Corollary

for the property

hold?

Let
T
be a subsemigroup of a semigroup

For
\mbox{${\mathcal{K}}$}\in\{\mbox{${\mathcal{L}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{R}}$},\mbox{${\mathcal{J}}$}\},
we say that

-preserving
(in

-preorder on

coincides with the restriction to

of the

-preorder on

It is easy to show that if

is a right/left ideal of

then

is
{\mathcal{L}}
/

-preserving, and if

is a two-sided ideal then

is also
{\mathcal{J}}

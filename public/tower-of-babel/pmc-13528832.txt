Visual performance and patient-reported outcomes of an EDF intraocular lens compared with a monofocal intraocular lens: randomized trial
Authors: Stefan Palkovits, Manuel Ruiss, Caroline Pilwachs, Johannes Zeilinger, Stefan Georgiev, Oliver Findl
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The Acunex Vario AN6V performed significantly better at near and intermediate ranges than its monofocal counterpart, the AN6. There was no difference in stereo vision or the occurrence of dysphotopsia.

Addressing spectacle independence is becoming more important in modern cataract surgery.1 Although bilateral monofocal intraocular lens (IOL) implantation, aiming for emmetropia or low myopia, leads to high levels of patient satisfaction associated with uncorrected distance vision improvement, many patients still require spectacles for intermediate or near vision tasks, such as working on screens or reading.2

With the rapid development of simultaneous vision IOLs, the increasing demand for spectacle independence at multiple distances can be achieved with various IOL designs, such as full visual range IOLs or extended depth of field (EDF) IOLs.3 However, often tradeoffs such as dysphotopsia and contrast sensitivity reduction are a common issue especially in full visual range IOL designs, which are less often reported in EDF IOLs.4 Another category of IOL is the enhanced monofocal IOLs, which can improve near and intermediate vision.5 Nevertheless, spectacles for these distances may be still required. To further improve spectacle independence, mini-monovision, which aims one eye for slight myopia, is an option. However, this may be associated with some loss of stereopsis.5–8

The Acunex Vario AN6V (Teleon Surgical B.V.) is a refractive EDF IOL with a near add of 1.50 diopters (D). This sector-shaped near vision segment aims to deliver patients the same kind of distance vision as a monofocal IOL with the addition of better intermediate vision.9

The aim of this study was to compare the clinical performance (spectacle independence, visual acuity, defocus curves, stereo vision, and occurrence of dysphotopsia) of the AN6V (EDF group) with a AN6 (monofocal group).

METHODS

Before the start of the study (Clinical Trials Identifier: NCT03749161), all documents relevant to the study were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the City of Vienna (EC number: EK-17-013-0217). The study was conducted in accordance with the tenets of the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments, and the European Union guidelines for good clinical practice. This trial followed the CONSORT reporting guideline.10

Patients and Group Allocation

Patients scheduled for bilateral cataract surgery were included in the study and were selected by clinical investigators at the Department of Ophthalmology at the Hanusch Hospital, Vienna. We included patients with cataract of age older than 21 years scheduled for bilateral cataract surgery, with wish for less spectacle dependence in the distance to intermediate vision range, as well as corneal astigmatism below 0.75 D. The presence of any ocular disease that could affect visual outcomes, previous ocular surgery, Chang-Waring chord >0.8 mm, and photopic pupil size <2.8 mm were exclusion criteria. Chang-Waring chord and photopic pupil size were measured using the IOL Master 700 (Carl Zeiss Meditec AG).

After enrollment in the study, patients were assigned to either the EDF or the monofocal group using an online randomization program (https://randomizer.org, list randomizer). Patients in the EDF group received the AN6V and in the monofocal group received AN6 (both Teleon Surgical B.V.) and were allocated in a 1:1 fashion.

Study Design

All patients gave written consent and underwent a screening examination. The baseline examination occurred within a week before the first-eye cataract surgery. Surgery of the second eye was conducted within 1 to 2 weeks after the first-eye surgery. Thereafter, 2 more follow-up visits were scheduled at 2 and 6 months after second-eye surgery.

During the baseline visit, corrected and uncorrected distance visual acuities (CDVA and UDVA) and a complete slitlamp examination were performed. The examinations were repeated during the follow-up visits. Furthermore, stereo vision was assessed at baseline and during the 2-month visit, and the questionnaire about the spectacle independence and photic phenomena was answered by the patients 6 months after the procedure.

Procedures

We used a questionnaire to assess spectacle independence. This questionnaire asked how often patients wore their spectacles (from never to always in 5 steps) at far, intermediate, and near distances. Visual acuity and defocus curves were assessed in accordance with recent recommendations using an Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study (ETDRS) illuminate cabinet (background luminance of the chart is 160 ± 20 cd/m2) with the standard ETDRS charts (Precision Vision).11 CDVA and UDVA at 4 m were evaluated at the baseline visit and all follow-up visits. During the 2-month follow-up visits, corrected and uncorrected distance intermediate acuities (DCIVA and UIVA) at 66 cm and near visual acuity (DCNVA and UNVA) at 40 cm were assessed. All visual acuity measures were first performed monocularly and then binocularly. For defocus curves, all patients read the letters on the ETDRS at 4 meters and −5.0 spherical diopter spectacles were added to the distance correction in the trial frame. Then, additional dioptric power was added to the spectacles in +0.5 D steps until the dioptric power was +2.0 D above the distance correction. Measurements for the defocus curve were performed binocularly.

The quality-of-vision questionnaire was used to assess symptoms, such as glare, halos, or starbursts.12 The questionnaire includes 10 items, each containing 3 questions about frequency, severity, and how bothersome the issues are. These symptoms were illustrated with standard images.

The Titmus-Fly-Stereotest is a contour stereotest in which horizontal disparity is presented through the vectographic technique. When tested at a distance of 40 cm, the fly exhibits a disparity of 3600 seconds of arc; the disparity of the animals ranges from 400 to 100 seconds of arc, and the disparity of the Wirt rings ranges from 800 to 40 seconds of arc.

The Hess App is a random-dot test displayed on a 3D laptop with temporal and spatial resolutions of 120 Hz and 1920 × 1080 pixels, respectively.13 A stepwise approach was implemented to assess the threshold disparity. The staircase traversed a list of disparities ranging from 1 to 4096 arcsec in a ratio of steps of 2. After the first reversal, the ratio of steps was reduced to the square root of 2. Patients were shown a 3D, pie-shaped stimulus in a particular area of a square. All patients were tested in the same room and under the same lighting conditions. First, the participants were seated in front of the monitor at a test distance of 80 cm and asked to put on electronic 3D shutter spectacles. After an introduction to the test, the patients were asked to indicate vocally in which area of the square of a 3D pie-shaped stimulus was located. If the patients could not recognize the stimulus, they were encouraged to guess.13

Cataract Surgery

Cataract surgery was performed under topical anesthesia. Routine procedure involved a 2.4 mm incision, paracentesis, and injecting a ophthalmic viscosurgical device, carrying out capsulorhexis and phacoemulsification. Coaxial irrigation and aspiration of cortical material were followed by IOL implantation in EDF and monofocal groups, and the AN6V and AN6 IOL were implanted, respectively. The target refraction for both eyes in both groups was −0.25 D, calculated using the Haigis formula with optimized constants (AN6: a0 = 1.523, a1 = 0.348, a2 = 0.113; AN6V: a0 = 1.625, a1 = 0.400, a2 = 0.100; provided by the manufacturer January 2021). All procedures were conducted by the same surgeon (O.F.).

After IOL implantation, the ophthalmic viscosurgical device was removed. As part of the standard procedure for wound sealing, stromal hydration was performed at the end of the surgery. Patients were prescribed standard postoperative medication (bromfenac eyedrops twice daily for 4 weeks).

Statistical Analysis

The primary objective of this study was to assess the rate of spectacle independence at the 6-month visit between the groups. The secondary objectives were to comparatively analyze CDVA, UCVA, UIVA, DCIVA, UNVA, and DCNVA, to comparatively evaluate defocus curves, and to ascertain the occurrence of dysphotopsia as well as assess stereo vision. Presentation of the data and statistical analyses follow standards for reporting outcomes with intraocular lenses.11

Frequencies of spectacle independence were compared between groups using the Mann-Whitney U test. Furthermore, stereopsis was also compared using the Mann-Whitney U (between groups) test and Wilcoxon signed rank test (between timepoints), and values for visual acuity were compared between groups and timepoints using unpaired and paired t tests, respectively.

SPSS (v. 29.0) was used for statistical analysis. Descriptive data are presented as mean and SD.

The sample size calculation was conducted for the primary outcome parameter (spectacle independence). The data published earlier by Kim et al. were used for this purpose. In this context, it was hypothesized that the rate of spectacle independence in the intermediate range would be 88% in the EDF group and 55% in the monofocal group.14 This difference would be deemed to be clinically significant. The alpha error was set at 0.05 and the power at 0.8. The group allocation was 1:1. The calculation yielded a total of 19 patients per group. To account for the anticipated dropout rate during the study (expected rate: 20% to 25%), an additional 6 patients were included, resulting in a total of 25 patients per group.

RESULTS

Study Population

In total, 50 patients were enrolled into the study and randomized 1:1 to the EDF and the monofocal group. Eight patients were excluded during the study period for the following reasons: 5 patients withdrew consent before the first follow-up visit and in 3 patients, surgery could not be performed as outlined in the protocol. Thus, 42 patients were eligible for statistical analyses (EDF group: 20; monofocal group: 22). Three patients in the monofocal and 4 patients in the EDF group did not attend the 6-month follow-up visit, resulting in 19 patients in the monofocal and 16 patients in the EDF group at this timepoint. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. There was no statistically significant difference in demographic data between the 2 groups. After adverse events occurred within the study period: 5 patients experienced mild anterior chamber reaction up to 1 week after the procedure (no additional medication was necessary); 3 patients mentioned symptoms of dry eye within 6 months after the procedure (lubricating therapy was recommended); and 1 patient suffered a stroke (serious adverse event—not related to the study) between the 2- and 6-month visits.

Moreover, postoperative subjective refraction did not reveal statistically significant differences between the groups at the 2-month time mark. In both groups, the target refraction was adequately achieved using the Haigis formula. More than 85% of the patients in the monofocal and more than 90% of patients in the EDF group were within 0.5 D of the targeted SE, reflecting a good refractive predictability of both lenses used in this study.

Stereo Vision

Angle of stereopsis was comparable between the groups before and after cataract surgery (Hess app: P = .063 and .179, respectively; Titmus fly stereo test: P = .136 and P = .326, respectively). No change in stereopsis was observed in the monofocal group after surgery in either the Hess app or the Titmus fly stereo test (P = .08 and P = .36, respectively). In the EDF group, stereopsis improved significantly after cataract surgery using the Hess application (P = .02), but not the Titmus fly stereo test (P = .084). The stereo vision data are presented in the supplementary material (Supplemental Figure 1, available at http://links.lww.com/JRS/B632).

Dysphotopsia

Figure 4, panels A to C, illustrates the frequency, severity, and bothersomeness caused by dysphotopsia. After a period of 6 months, glare was observed to be the most prevalent issue in both groups. Approximately 75% of patients reported experiencing glare on a regular basis. However, most of these patients stated that they were not significantly bothered by the condition. The incidences of halos and starbursts were also comparable between both IOL types.

DISCUSSION

Both evaluated intraocular lenses, the Acunex Vario AN6V EDF IOL and the Acunex AN6 monofocal IOL showed excellent distance visual acuity, and the spectacle independence for distance vision was comparable. Only 1 patient in the monofocal group and 2 in the EDF group reported needing spectacles for distance tasks at least half of the time. Although spectacle independence in the distant range according to the questionnaire was slightly higher in the monofocal group, this difference was not statistically significant. Frequency of spectacles at near distance was significantly lower in the EDF group. Only 19% of patients needed spectacles for near task all the time compared with 58% in the monofocal group. Furthermore, both corrected and uncorrected distance near and intermediate visual acuities were significantly better in the EDF group. These data reflect the better performance of the AN6V compared with the monofocal version in the near and intermediate distance.

Preoperatively, the monofocal group had better CDVA, most likely because there were 3 outliers with poor vision. Postoperatively reported visual acuity values for CDVA, UDVA, UIVA, and near vision for the AN6V in previous studies are in good agreement with our data.9,15 Similar to our study, higher visual acuity values were reported for intermediate and near ranges. Rua et al. reported that 85% of patients needed spectacles for prolonged reading. In another study, rates of spectacle independence were comparable with our study, with 95%, 81%, and 25% of patients being spectacle independent at distance, intermediate, and near ranges in the previous study. This is comparable with the data found in the present trial (88%, 75%, and 38%, respectively). In that regard, our data are in good agreement with previous EDF studies showing improved spectacle independence as compared with monofocal IOLs.

Currently, data on the AN6V are limited. However, more data are available for the Acunex Vario AN6V's predecessor, the Lentis Comfort (also known as the Lentis Comfort LS-313 MF15). Previous studies have reported improved spectacle independence and intermediate visual acuity when using the Lentis Comfort, which is comparable with the results of our study.16,17

The implantation of the AN6V did not affect stereo vision similar to the monofocal lens. These data underline the important fact that stereovision is not affected using this refractive segment EDF lens compared with its monofocal counterpart. Nevertheless, reduced stereo vision could occur if monovision is applied, which was not performed in this study. Indeed, recent findings suggest that anisometropia is a main factor for reduced stereopsis. Furthermore, visual acuity, pupil size, and age might be confounding variables.18 Therefore, if monovision is applied using this EDF IOL, functional stereo vision needs to be addressed in a future study. Furthermore, at the 2-month postoperative visit, subjective refraction assessments demonstrated no statistically significant differences between the groups, with both cohorts achieving the intended refractive target satisfactorily.

The prevalence, frequency, and bothersomeness of postoperative dysphotopsia were comparable between the EDF and monofocal groups. The most frequent complaint in both groups was glare 6 months after the procedure. Glare was experienced by around 75% of patients in both groups, but the majority only experienced it occasionally. However, half to two-thirds of patients judged its severity as mild or nonexistent, and 75% were not at all bothered by it. All other types of dysphotopsia occurred less frequently and were less bothersome to patients. Rua et al. reported lower rates for dysphotopsia as compared with our study.9 In the previous study, only 20% of patients reported glare and 15% of patients had halos and starbursts. One possible explanation for this discrepancy is that a different questionnaire was used to assess the symptoms. However, in another study, Wanten et al. reported similar rates of dysphotopsia when using the AN6V. The number of patients who experienced glare or halos was 75% vs 76% and 57% vs 50% in the previous study vs our study, respectively.15 Starbursts occurred less often in our study (50% vs 25%). The latter study used the same questionnaire as the present one (quality-of-vision questionnaire) making both studies more comparable.

The AN6V is considered as an EDF IOL with a near addition of +1.5 D. It is considered that at least 0.3 logMAR is achieved in the visual acuity testing in the defocus curve at 2.0 D.19 This criterion was achieved in this study because the visual acuity was around 0.2 logMAR for 2.0 D of defocus, reflecting the good performance of the IOL in this range. Although both defocus curves show excellent distance vision, the EDF group outperformed the monofocal one in the intermediate and near range. Recently, a consensus paper was published to establish a consensus classification of simultaneous vision IOLs.20 Accordingly, applying the defocus curve to the AN6V, the spectacle independence values and binocular UNVA would classify the AN6V as being between the PARTIAL-RoF enhanced and PARTIAL-RoF extended ranges, reflecting their good performance at closer distances. However, further studies, especially meta-analyses, are needed to reach a final conclusion in that regard. Earlier, Fernández et al. proposed the functional classification of intraocular lenses.21 As such, the advantage of the newly proposed classification is that it categorizes lenses based on functional outcomes rather than optical principles. Real-world performance is therefore decisive.

One limitation of this study was the long study period, which resulted in a high dropout rate and a smaller sample size at the 6-month follow-up. The calculated number of 19 patients per group was reached in both groups at the 2-month visit and during the 6-month visit in the monofocal group. However, only 16 patients attended the final visit at 6 months in the EDF group. Nevertheless, the analyses reached statistical significance, meaning that the findings and conclusions of the study can be interpreted accordingly. Furthermore, data for defocus curves are based on 14 patients per group. Interpretation of defocus data is therefore limited.

In conclusion, compared with the monofocal version, the AN6V EDF IOL showed a significant improvement in intermediate and near visual acuity. Furthermore, patients receiving the EDF version were less likely to require spectacles for near and intermediate ranges, therefore achieving greater spectacle independence. Importantly, the rates of subjectively perceived dysphotopsia, and their severity and bothersomeness were comparable between the 2 groups.

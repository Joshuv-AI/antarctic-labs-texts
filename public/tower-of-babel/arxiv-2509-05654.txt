Well-posedness and regularity theory for the fractional diffusion-wave equation in Lebesgue spaces
Bruno de Andrade, Naldisson dos Santos

Abstract
. This paper is dedicated to the study of the semilinear fractional diffusion-wave equation. We provide estimates on the families of linear operators related to the problem in the fractional power scale associated with the Laplace operator. Furthermore, we analyze the existence and uniqueness of local mild solutions and their possible continuation to a maximal interval of existence. We also consider the issue of spatial regularity and continuous dependence with respect to initial data. MSC 2010. Primary: 35R11, 35Q35 Secondary: 35B65, 33E12 Keywords: Fractional diffusion-wave equation; Spatial regularity of solutions; Blow-up alternative.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the nonlinear fractional diffusion-wave equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrr}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u+f(u),\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\ \mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
,
\alpha\in(1,2)
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}
is the Caputo fractional derivative. Furthermore,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that verifies
f(0)=0
and

(1.5)

\displaystyle|f(r)-f(s)|\leq C(|r|^{\rho-1}+|s|^{\rho-1})|r-s|,\quad\forall r,s\in\mathbb{R},

for some
C>0
and
\rho>1
.
We analyze the above problem in the
L^{q}(\Omega)
setting,
1<q<\infty
.
This kind of evolution problem has been the subject of several research papers in the last years since this topic involves a large variety of natural sciences such as physics, chemistry, biology, engineering, and medicine, see Refs.
[
6
,
11

17
,
18
,
21
]
and the references therein.

An important example where these equations appear naturally is the theory of one-dimen- sion linear viscoelasticity. The basic equations of such a theory are given by

(1.6)

\sigma_{x}(x,t)=\rho u_{tt}(x,t),

(1.7)

\epsilon(x,t)=u_{x}(x,t),

(1.8)

\epsilon(x,t)=J_{0}\ \sigma(x,t)+\dot{J}(t)\ast\sigma(x,t),

where
\rho
is the density,
u
denotes the displacement,
\sigma
the stress and
\epsilon
the strain. Furthermore,
J(t)
represents the creep compliance, which is assumed to be a non-negative, non-decreasing function with initial value
J_{0}=J(0^{+})\geq 0
, called the instantaneous compliance. The evolution equation for the the displacement
u(x,t)
can be derived from (
1.6
)-(
1.8
) since that

(1.9)

u_{xx}=\epsilon_{x}=(J_{0}+\dot{J}\ast)\sigma_{x}=(J_{0}+\dot{J}\ast)\rho u_{tt}.

If we consider the so-called power-type material, for which the creep compliance can be written as

J(t)=\frac{1}{\rho a}\frac{t^{\gamma}}{\Gamma(\gamma+1)},\quad 0<\gamma\leq 1,\quad t>0,

where
a
is a positive constant and
\Gamma
is the Gamma function, then from (
1.9
) we obtain the evolution equation

(1.10)

\partial^{\alpha}_{t}u=au_{xx},

with
1\leq\alpha=2-\gamma\leq 2
, and
\partial^{\alpha}_{t}
the time fractional derivative in Caputo’s sense. Following Refs.
[
7
,
19
]
, it follows that the above creep law is provided by viscoelastic models whose stress-strain relation verify

\sigma=\rho a\partial^{\gamma}_{t}\epsilon,\quad 0<\gamma\leq 1.

For
\gamma=1
we have the situation of a Newtonian fluid where
a
represents the kinematic viscosity. In this case (
1.10
) becomes the classical diffusion equation. In the limit case
\gamma=0
we obtain from (
1.10
) the classical D’Alembert wave equation with wave-front velocity
c=\sqrt{a}
. When
0<\gamma<1
, the evolution equation (
1.10
) is called the fractional diffusion-wave equation and has been the subject of many research works, see e.g. Refs.
[
16
,
17
,
20
]
and references therein. Particularly in Ref.
[
20
]
, the authors show that (
1.10
) governs the propagation of stress waves in viscoelastic media which are of relevance in acoustics and seismology since their quality factor turns out to be independent of frequency.

1

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u}(\lambda,\cdot)=\Delta\widehat{u}(\lambda,\cdot)+\widehat{f}(\lambda),

where

Definition 1.1
.

A continuous function
u:[0,\tau]\rightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
that satisfies (
1.11

1

Consider the Laplace operator
\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary conditions in
Y_{q}^{0}=L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1<q<\infty
, and domain
Y_{q}^{1}=W^{2,q}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)
. We know that the linear problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrr}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u,\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\ \mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

is well-posed for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
and any
1<q<\infty
. This occurs because
-\Delta
is a sectorial operator, and hence there exists
\phi_{q}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
such that

\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(z)|\leq\phi_{q},z\neq 0\}\subset\rho(\Delta).

Therefore, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}
then for all
\alpha\in(0,1]
we have

\lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}\subset\rho(\Delta)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(\lambda^{\alpha}-\Delta)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(Y_{q}^{0})}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\alpha}.

With this property we can use Laplace transform methods and verify that
\Delta
generates an analytical semigroup in
L^{q}(\Omega)
, for any
1<q<\infty
. However, the scenario changes in the case
\alpha\in(1,2)
since the above property no longer holds. In this context, it is not true that

\lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}\subset\rho(\Delta)
, when
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}
.
Thereby, the respective linear fractional diffusion-wave equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrr}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u,\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\ \mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

is not well-posed for all
\alpha\in(1,2)
and any
1<q<\infty
. This is compatible with the limit case
\alpha=2
, where the above problem becomes the linear wave equation, which is, in a general way, ill-posed in
L^{q}(\Omega)
,
1\leq q<\infty
. Then, it is reasonable to think that superdiffusive problems

In Section

we investigate further the linear families associated with the Laplace operator in the superdifusive context. At the core of our analysis is the smoothing effect of the families
(E_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
,
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(R_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in the scale
\{X_{q}^{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
of fractional powers spaces associated with
-\Delta
. We prove they are bounded continuous families of linear operators in
L^{q}(\Omega)
, and if
0\leq\theta<\beta\leq 1
, then there exists
M>0
such that

\|E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{-\alpha(1+\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}},\quad\|S_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{1-\alpha(1+\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}}

and

\|R_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{-1-\alpha(\theta-\beta)}\|x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}},

for all
x\in X^{\beta}_{q}
and
t>0
. Particularly, it follows from the above estimate that for all
t>0
, the operators
t^{\alpha\theta}E_{\alpha}(t)
from
X^{1}_{q}
into
X^{1+\theta}_{q}
are bounded linear operators satisfying

\|t^{\alpha\theta}E_{\alpha}(t)\|_{\mathcal{L}(X^{1}_{q},X^{1+\theta}_{q})}\leq M,

with
M>0
independent of
t>0
, for all
0\leq\theta\leq 1
. Moreover, given a compact subset
J
of
X^{1}_{q}
, we have

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x\in J}\|t^{\alpha\theta}E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}=0.

To prove the last assertion it is sufficient to observe that the operators

t^{\alpha\theta}E_{\alpha}(t):X^{1}_{q}\to X^{1+\theta}_{q}

are uniformly bounded in
t>0
,
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|t^{\alpha\theta}E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}=0
, for
x\in X^{1+\theta}_{q},

and
X^{1+\theta}_{q}
is a dense subset of
X^{1}_{q}
. Similar properties can be proved to
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(R_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

With this smoothing effect, given
u_{0},u_{1}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, we provide sufficient conditions to the existence of a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,\tau],L^{q}(\Omega))
to (
1
), which can be continued to a maximal time of existence
\tau_{max}>0
such that

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau_{max}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{q}dx=+\infty,

if
\tau_{max}<+\infty
. At this point, we want to highlight that the fractional powers scale has proven to be an effective tool for studying the issue of regularity. These spaces offer a precise method for measuring the spatial regularity of the solutions. Utilizing them, we demonstrate that the mild solution of (
1
) exhibits an immediate regularization effect. Precisely, for all
0\leq\theta<\beta
, we have that

u\in C((0,\tau],X_{q}^{1+\theta}),

and, if
\theta>0
then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}t^{\alpha\theta}\|u(t)\|_{X_{q}^{1+\theta}}=0.

Given that
X_{q}^{1+\theta}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
, it follows that
X_{q}^{1+\theta}
is a more regular space than
L^{q}(\Omega)
.

2.
Abstract framework

The main goal of this section is to introduce and study spaces measuring smoothness which are also algebraically well-adapted to the problem (
1
). Remember that the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary conditions is a sectorial operator in
Y_{q}^{0}=L^{q}(\Omega)
with domain
Y_{q}^{1}=W^{2,q}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,q}(\Omega)
. Denoting by
\{Y_{q}^{\beta}\}_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}}
the fractional powers spaces associated with the Laplace operator, it follows that, see Ref.
[
3
]
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}Y_{q}^{\beta}\hookrightarrow H_{q}^{2\beta}(\Omega),\ \ \beta\geq 0,\ \ 1<q<\infty,\\
Y_{q}^{-\beta}=(Y_{q^{\prime}}^{\beta})^{\prime},\ \ \beta\geq 0,\ \ 1<q<\infty,\ \ q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}.\\
\end{array}\right.

where
H_{q}^{2\beta}(\Omega):=W^{2\beta,q}(\Omega)
is the standard Sobolev-Slobodeckii space and
(Y^{\beta}_{q})^{\prime}
represents the dual space of
Y^{\beta}_{q}
. Therefore, from standard duality arguments, we get

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}Y_{q}^{\beta}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),\ \ r\leq\frac{Nq}{N-2\beta q},\ \ 0\leq\beta<\frac{N}{2q},\\
Y_{q}^{0}=L^{q}(\Omega),\\
Y_{q}^{\beta}\hookleftarrow L^{s}(\Omega),\ \ s\geq\frac{Nq}{N-2\beta q},\ \ -\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the realization of
L=\Delta
in
Y_{q}^{\beta}
, denoted by
L_{\beta}
, is an isometry from
Y_{q}^{\beta+1}
into
Y_{q}^{\beta}
and

L_{\beta}:D(L_{\beta})=E_{q}^{\beta+1}\subset E_{q}^{\beta}\rightarrow E_{q}^{\beta}

is a sector operator. In addition,
D(L_{\beta}^{\gamma})=Y_{q}^{\beta+\gamma}
.

We shall work the problem (
1
) with initial data belonging to
L^{q}(\Omega)
. For this reason, we set
X_{q}^{\beta}:=Y_{q}^{\beta-1}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
A_{q}:X_{q}^{1}\subset X_{q}^{0}\rightarrow X_{q}^{0}
be the operator
L_{-1}
. From (
2
), it follows that the fractional powers scale associated with
A_{q}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrc}X_{q}^{\beta}\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega),\ \ r\leq\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q},\ \ 1\leq\beta<1+\frac{N}{2q},\\
X_{q}^{1}=L^{q}(\Omega),\\
X_{q}^{\beta}\hookleftarrow L^{s}(\Omega),\ \ s\geq\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q},\ \ 1-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta\leq 1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Therefore, the abstract formulation of the problem
(\ref{wave})
in the
L^{q}(\Omega)
theory becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrr}D^{\alpha}_{t}u=A_{q}u+f(u),\ \ t>0,\\
u(0)=u_{0},\ u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1},\\
\end{array}\right.

for
\psi\in X_{q}^{1}=L^{q}(\Omega)
.

We close this section proving some properties about function
f
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider
\beta\geq 0
such that
1-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta<1
and
1<\rho\leq 1+\frac{2q}{N}(1-\beta)
, then
f:X_{q}^{1}\to X_{q}^{\beta}
verifies

\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X_{q}^{\beta}}\leq C(\|u\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho-1})\|u-v\|_{X_{q}^{1}}

and

\|f(u)\|_{X_{q}^{\beta}}\leq C\|u\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho},

for some
C>0
and every
u,v\in X_{q}^{1}
.

Proof.

Using the assumption
1-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta<1
and
(\ref{IS})
, we obtain

(2.24)

L^{\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow X_{q}^{\beta}.

Moreover, noting that
1<\rho\leq 1+\frac{2q}{N}(1-\beta)
implies

\frac{\rho Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}\leq q
, we conclude that

(2.25)

L^{\frac{\rho Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)\hookleftarrow L^{q}(\Omega).

Finally, taking
u,v\in X_{q}^{1}=L^{q}(\Omega)
and using
(\ref{ime1})
,
(\ref{ime2})
and Hölder’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{X_{q}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|_{L^{\frac{Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u-v\|_{L^{\frac{\rho Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)}(\|u\|_{L^{\frac{\rho Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{L^{\frac{\rho Nq}{N+2q-2\beta q}}(\Omega)}^{\rho-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u-v\|_{X_{q}^{1}}(\|u\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho-1}+\|v\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho-1})

Finally, taking
v=0
we get

\displaystyle\|f(u)\|_{X_{q}^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{X_{q}^{1}}^{\rho}.

∎

3.
Linear estimates

This section is dedicated to the study of
(E_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
,
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(R_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in the scale
X_{q}^{\beta}:=Y_{q}^{\beta-1}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, of fractional powers spaces associated with the sectorial operator
A_{q}:X_{q}^{1}\subset X_{q}^{0}\rightarrow X_{q}^{0}
defined in the previous section.
Initially, consider
\phi_{q}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
such that
\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|\arg(z)|\leq\phi_{q},z\neq 0\}\subset\rho(A_{q}).

From now on, we consider
\alpha\in(1,\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi})
. Note that if
\eta_{0}\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\phi_{q}}{\alpha})
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{S}_{\eta_{0}}
then

\lambda^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{S}_{\phi_{q}}\subset\rho(A_{q})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{L}(X_{q}^{1})}\leq C|\lambda|^{-\alpha}.

We start with the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
. For the function

(3.26)

E_{\alpha}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha}e^{\lambda t}\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}d\lambda,&\mbox{if}~t>0,\\
I,&\mbox{if}~t=0,\end{array}\right.

where
Ha
is a suitable path and
I
is the identity operator, the following properties are verified:

(i)

For all
t>0
,
E_{\alpha}(t):X^{0}_{q}\to X^{0}_{q}
is well defined and there exists
M>0
such that

\|E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\leq M\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}},\quad\forall x\in X^{0}_{q}.

(ii)

The function
E_{\alpha}(\cdot):[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(X^{0}_{q})
is strongly continuous.

(iii)

If
\beta\in[0,1]
then there exists a constant
M>0
such that

\|E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}}\leq Mt^{-\alpha\beta}\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}},\quad\forall t>0,\ \forall x\in X^{0}_{q}.

Proof.

(i)
For
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
and
r>0
, set
\eta_{0}\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\phi_{q}}{\alpha}\right)
and define the path
Ha=Ha(r,\eta_{0})
by

(3.27)

\displaystyle Ha=Ha(r,\eta_{0})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{se^{i\eta_{0}}:r\leq s<\infty\}\cup\{re^{is}:|s|\leq\eta_{0}\}\cup\{se^{-i\eta_{0}}:r\leq s<\infty\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Ha_{1}+Ha_{2}-Ha_{3}.

For each
t>0
fixed, if we assume
r=1/t
, then

\bullet
Over
Ha_{1}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha_{1}}e^{\lambda t}\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\int_{1/t}^{\infty}e^{st\cos\eta_{0}}s^{-1}ds\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\int_{1/t}^{\infty}e^{st\cos\eta_{0}}tds\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\frac{e^{\cos\eta_{0}}}{|\cos\eta_{0}|}\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

\bullet
Over
Ha_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{Ha_{2}}e^{\lambda t}\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\int_{-\eta_{0}}^{\eta_{0}}e^{\cos s}r^{\alpha-1}r^{-\alpha}rds\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\pi}e\eta_{0}\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

\bullet
Over
Ha_{3}
we proceed in the same way as in
Ha_{1}.

Taking
M>0
as the sum of all bounds obtained above, we deduce that
E_{\alpha}(t)
is well defined for each
t>0
and

\|E_{\alpha}(t)x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\leq M\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}},\ \ \forall t>0,\ \ \forall x\in X^{0}_{q}.

A classical argumentation using the Cauchy integral theorem ensures that the representation (
3.26
) is independent of
r>0
and
\eta_{0}\in\left(\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\phi_{q}}{\alpha}\right)
. Hence, this completes the proof of
(i)
.

(ii)
Consider
\tau>0
and let
\{t_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\tau)
be such that
t_{n}\rightarrow\tau
as
n\rightarrow\infty.
Note that for

\|E_{\alpha}(\tau)x-E_{\alpha}(t_{n})x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|\int_{Ha}(e^{\lambda\tau}-e^{\lambda t_{n}})\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

We will analyze the above integral over the paths
Ha_{1},

Ha_{2}
and
Ha_{3}
separately.

\bullet
Over
Ha_{1}
we have that

\left\|\int_{Ha_{1}}(e^{\lambda\tau}-e^{\lambda t_{n}})\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\leq C\int_{r}^{\infty}\left|e^{\tau se^{i\eta}}-e^{t_{n}se^{i\eta}}\right|s^{-1}ds\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

From the dominated convergence theorem, this last term goes to
0
as
n
goes to
\infty
.

\bullet
Over
Ha_{2}

\left\|\int_{Ha_{2}}(e^{\lambda\tau}-e^{\lambda t_{n}})\lambda^{\alpha-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\leq C\int_{-\eta}^{\eta}\left|e^{\tau re^{is}}-e^{t_{n}re^{is}}\right|ds\|x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

Again, from the dominated convergence theorem, this last term goes to
0
as
n
goes to
\infty
.

\bullet
Over
Ha_{3}
we proceed in the same way as in
Ha_{1}.

Therefore

\|E_{\alpha}(\tau)x-E_{\alpha}(t_{n})x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\rightarrow 0

as

, for each

. Analogously, let

\|E_{\alpha}(t_{n})x-E_{\alpha}(\tau)x\|_{X^{0}_{q}}\rightarrow 0

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, for each

.

For the proof of the continuity in

, note that by the Cauchy integral theorem

where

\|E_{\alpha}(t)x-x\|_{X^{1}_{q}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\left\|\int_{Ha}e^{\lambda t}\lambda^{-1}(\lambda^{\alpha}-A_{q})^{-1}A_{q}xd\lambda\right\|_{X^{0}_{q}}.

If we assume that
r=1/t
then

\bullet
Over

4.

This section contains statements and proofs of our theorems. We start with the following result that ensures sufficient conditions for local existence, uniqueness, and spatial regularity to the problem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1<\alpha<\frac{2\phi_{q}}{\pi}
. Consider
\beta\geq 0
such that
1-\frac{N}{2q^{\prime}}<\beta<1
and
1<\rho\leq 1+\frac{2q}{N}(1-\beta)
. Given
v_{0}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, we can consider
r>0
and
\tau>0
such that for any
u_{0},u_{1}\in B_{r}(v_{0})\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,\tau],L^{q}(\Omega))
of the problem
(\ref{awave})
. Furthermore, for all
0\leq\theta<\beta
, it follows that

u\in C((0,\tau],X_{q}^{1+\theta})

and if
\theta>0
then

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{\alpha\theta}\|u(t,u_{0},u_{1})\|_{X_{q}^{1+\theta}}=0.

Moreover, if
u_{0},w_{0},u_{1},w_{1}\in B_{r}(v_{0})\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
, then there exists a constant
c>0
such that

for all
t\in[0,\tau]
.

Proof.

Consider
0<\nu<1
and
0<\tau<1

For
r=\dfrac{\nu}{4M}
, set

K=\left\{u\in C([0,\tau],X^{1}_{q}):\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}\|u(t)-v_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\nu\right\}

and define the operator

(Tu)(t)=E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))ds.

Our purpose is to show that
K
is a
T
-invariant set and
T
is a contraction. Initially, let us prove that
T:K\to K
is well defined. Consider
0\leq t_{1}<t<\tau
and
u\in K
. Then

\displaystyle\|(Tu)(t)-(Tu)(t_{1})\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(t_{1})u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\|S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(t_{1})u_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle I,

where

Using the strong continuity of the families
(E_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, we have that

\|E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(t_{1})u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(t_{1})u_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\rightarrow 0

when

. On the other hand

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))-R_{\alpha}(t_{1}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds.

Note that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\int_{t_{1}}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|f(u(s))\|_{X^{\beta}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{MC}{\alpha\beta}(\|v_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\nu)^{\rho}(t-t_{1})^{\alpha\beta}\rightarrow 0,

when
t\rightarrow t_{1}^{+}.
By setting

I_{1}:=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))-R_{\alpha}(t_{1}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds,

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{1}}[(t-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}-(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}]\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|[\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))-\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{1}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds,

where

,

By taking
0\leq\theta\leq\theta_{0}<\beta
, these inequalities imply that

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\theta}\|u(t,u_{0},u_{1})-u(t,w_{0},w_{1})\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\left(\|u_{0}-w_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\|u_{1}-w_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\right)

+\ 2M\Gamma_{\theta}(t)\left(\|u_{0}-w_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\|u_{1}-w_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\right),

that is

t^{\alpha\theta}\|u(t,u_{0},u_{1})-u(t,w_{0},w_{1})\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{q}}\leq c\left(\|u_{0}-w_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\|u_{1}-w_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\right),

where

c=M(1+2\sup\{\Gamma_{\theta}(t):\ 0\leq t\leq\tau,\ 0\leq\theta\leq\theta_{0}\}),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Now, we prove a continuation result and a blow-up alternative for the mild solution guaranteed by Theorem
4.1
. In particular, we ensure a maximal time of existence to this solution.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
u:[0,\tau]\to X^{1}_{q}
be a mild solution of the problem (
2
). If
t_{1}>\tau
and
v:[0,t_{1}]\to X^{1}_{q}
is a mild solution of the problem (
2
), then we say that
v
is a continuation of
u
in
[0,t_{1}]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, consider
\tau>0
and
u:[0,\tau]\rightarrow X^{1}_{q}
the mild solution of problem (
2
). Then, there exist
t_{1}>\tau
and a unique continuation
u^{*}
of
u
in
[0,t_{1}]
.

Proof.

Fixe
0<\nu\leq 1
and take
t_{1}>\tau
so that for all
t\in[\tau,t_{1}]
,

\|E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(\tau)u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}<\dfrac{\nu}{4},\quad\|S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(\tau)u_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}<\dfrac{\nu}{4},

\int_{0}^{\tau}\|[R_{\alpha}(t-s)-R_{\alpha}(\tau-s)]f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds<\dfrac{\nu}{4}

and

MCRt^{\alpha\beta}\int_{\tau/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-1+\alpha\beta}ds<\dfrac{\nu}{4},

where
R:=\max\{(\|u(\tau)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\nu)^{\rho},2(\|u(\tau)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\nu)^{\rho-1}\}
. Set

K=\left\{v\in C([0,t_{1}];X^{1}_{q}):\sup_{t\in[\tau,t_{1}]}\|v(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\nu,v(t)=u(t),t\in[0,\tau]\right\}

and define on
K
the operator

(Tv)(t)=E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(v(s))ds.

Note that, if
v\in K
then
(Tv)(t)=u(t)
, for all
t\in[0,\tau]
. The same computations performed there show that
Tv\in C([0,t_{1}];X^{1}_{q})
whenever
v\in K
. For
t\in[\tau,t_{1}]
, we have

\|(Tv)(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\|E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(\tau)u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}+\|S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(\tau)u_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(v(s))ds-\int_{0}^{\tau}R_{\alpha}(\tau-s)f(u(s))ds\right\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\nu}{4}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(v(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\nu}{4}+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(v(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{3\nu}{4}+MCRt^{\alpha\beta}\int_{\tau/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-1+\alpha\beta}ds\leq\nu.

Then, it follows that

\|(Tv)(t)-u(\tau)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\nu,\ \ \forall t\in[\tau,t_{1}],

whence
K
is a
T
-invariant set. We claim that
T:K\rightarrow K
is a contraction. In fact, if
v,w\in K
and
t\in[\tau,t_{1}]
then

\|(Tv)(t)-(Tw)(t)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}=\left\|\int_{\tau}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)[f(v(s))-f(w(s))]ds\right\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\leq MC\int_{\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-1+\alpha\beta}(\|v(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}^{\rho-1}+\|w(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}^{\rho-1})\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\leq MCRt^{\alpha\beta}\int_{\tau/t}^{1}(1-s)^{-1+\alpha\beta}ds\sup_{s\in[0,t_{1}]}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sup_{s\in[0,t_{1}]}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}.

That is,

\sup_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}\|(Tv)(t)-(Tw)(t)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq\frac{1}{4}\sup_{t\in[0,t_{1}]}\|v(s)-w(s)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}.

Hence
T
is a strict contraction and has a unique fixed point
u^{\ast}\in K
which is a continuation of
u
in
[0,t_{1}]
. As before, the uniqueness can be shown by Gronwall Lemma.
∎

Next is our result of global existence or non-continuation by a blow-up.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
4.1
, let
u
be the mild solution of problem (
2
) with maximal time of existence
\tau_{\max}>0
. Then either
\tau_{\max}=\infty
or

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow\tau_{\max}^{-}}\|u(t)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}=\infty.

Proof.

Suppose that
\tau_{\max}<\infty
and there exists
k>0
such that
\|u(t)\|_{X^{1}_{q}}\leq k
, for all
t\in[0,\tau_{\max})
. Let
(t_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,\tau_{\max})
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow\tau_{\max}
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Analogously to the proof of Theorem
4.1
, it is shown that

\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)f(u(s))-\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(\tau_{\max}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds\to 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
,
where
\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t):=t^{1-\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha}(t)
,
t>0
. Suppose
t_{n}<t_{m}<\tau_{\max}
. Then,

\displaystyle\|u(t_{n})-u(t_{m})\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|E_{\alpha}(t_{n})u_{0}-E_{\alpha}(t_{m})u_{0}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|S_{\alpha}(t_{n})u_{1}-S_{\alpha}(t_{m})u_{1}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\|[R_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)-R_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)]f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}\|R_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds.

By strong continuity of
(E_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and
(S_{\alpha}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, we have that the two first terms of the right side in the above inequality go to zero as
m,n\rightarrow\infty.
On the other hand

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}\|R_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}(t_{m}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|f(u(s))\|_{X_{\beta}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{MC}{\alpha\beta}k^{\rho}(t_{m}-t_{n})^{\alpha\beta}\rightarrow 0,

as
m,n\rightarrow\infty
. Furthermore, set

I=\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\|[R_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)-R_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)]f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds.

Then

\displaystyle I\leq\int_{0}^{t_{n}}[(t_{m}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}-(t_{n}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}]\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)f(u(s))-\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{MC}{\alpha\beta}k^{\rho}[t_{m}^{\alpha\beta}-(t_{m}-t_{n})^{\alpha\beta}-t_{n}^{\alpha\beta}]

+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{n}-s)f(u(s))-\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(\tau_{\max}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

+\int_{0}^{t_{m}}(t_{m}-s)^{\alpha\beta-1}\|\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(t_{m}-s)f(u(s))-\tilde{R}_{\alpha}(\tau_{\max}-s)f(u(s))\|_{X^{1}_{q}}ds

Hence, if
m,n\rightarrow\infty
it follows that
I\to 0
. This computation shows that
\{u(t_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
X^{1}_{q}
and thus there exists
\tilde{u}\in X^{1}_{q}
such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t_{n})-\tilde{u}\|_{X^{1}_{q}}=0.

With this, we can extend
u
to
[0,\tau_{\max}]
obtaining the equality

u(t)=E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}+S_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)f(u(s))ds

for all
t\in[0,\tau_{\max}]
. By Theorem
4.2
, we can extend the solution to a bigger interval, which is a contradiction with the maximality of
\tau_{\max}
.
∎

We close this work with some illustrations of our main results. Consider the particular problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lrr}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u=\Delta u+u\sqrt{|u|},\ \mbox{in}\ (0,\infty)\times\Omega,\\
u=0,\ \mbox{in}\ [0,\infty)\times\partial\Omega,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ \mbox{in}\ \ \Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded smooth domain. We apply our main results in the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-setting. In this situation, we can choose the derivation order
\alpha
as any real number in
(1,2)
. Indeed, it follows from Theorems
4.1
,
4.2
and
4.3
that, for any initial data
u_{0},u_{1}\in X^{1}_{2}=L^{2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique mild solution
u\in C([0,\tau_{\max});X^{1}_{2})
to problem (
4
), given by

u(t,x)=E_{\alpha}(t)u_{0}(x)+S_{\alpha}(t)u_{1}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}R_{\alpha}(t-s)u(s,x)\sqrt{|u(s,x)|}ds,

and

\limsup_{t\to\tau_{\max}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}|u(t,x)|^{2}dx=+\infty,

if
\tau_{\max}<+\infty
. Furthermore, for all
\tau<\tau_{\max}
and
0\leq\theta<\frac{5}{8}
it follows that
u\in C([0,\tau];X^{1+\theta}_{2})
, and if
\theta>0
, then

\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{X^{1+\theta}_{2}}=0.

Finally, this solution depends continuously from the initial data.

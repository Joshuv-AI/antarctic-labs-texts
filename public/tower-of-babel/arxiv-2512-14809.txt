Dawn and Twilight Time in Quantum Tunneling
Tinglong Feng, Jesse Moes, Tomislav Prokopec

Abstract
Metastable decay exhibits a familiar exponential regime bracketed by early time deviations and late-time power-law tails. We adopt the real-time, flux-based definition of the decay rate in the spirit of Andreassen et al. direct method and present a complete analysis of one-dimensional quantum mechanical resonance models. We show that the kernel admits a universal pole–plus–branch decomposition and use it to define two computable time scales: a dawn time t \circlebottomhalfblack t_{\circlebottomhalfblack} , when a single resonant contribution starts dominating and exponential decay sets in, and a twilight time t \circletophalfblack t_{\circletophalfblack} , when the branch-cut tail overtakes exponential decay. The latter can be expressed in closed form v͡ia the Lambert W W function, making its parametric dependence manifest without fitting. For square, modified square, and Pöschl–Teller barriers we obtain simple thick-barrier formulas, clarify the relation Γ ​ T = T trans \Gamma T=T_{\rm trans} between the decay rate Γ \Gamma , oscillation period T T , and transmission probability T trans T_{\rm trans} , and indicate how our spectral picture can be naturally extended to quantum field theoretic vacuum decay.

Twilight time

The mnemonic term
twilight time

is chosen for the time when the power-law tail starts to dominate over the exponentially decaying regime, and it is independent

dominates, consider first the equation,

\left(\frac{E_{0}t_{\circletophalfblack}}{\hbar}\right)^{-2\gamma}={\Bigg|\frac{F}{G}\Bigg|}^{2}\exp(-\Gamma_{0}t_{\circletophalfblack})\,,\quad

(11)

t_{\circletophalfblack}=\frac{\hbar}{E_{0}}\Bigg|\frac{G}{F}\Bigg|^{1/\gamma}\!\exp\!\left(\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2\gamma}\,t_{\circletophalfblack}\right)\,.\quad

(12)

-\tau\,\exp(-\tau)=-\,\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2\gamma}\,\frac{\hbar}{E_{0}}\Bigg|\frac{G}{F}\Bigg|^{1/\gamma}\,.\quad

(13)

W

W_{k}(z)\,\exp\{W_{k}(z)\}=z

Dawn time

The dawn time
t_{\circlebottomhalfblack}
is the mnemonic term we propose for the point in time when
the exponential decay kicks in. To describe tunneling,
we pick an initial state that is localized in the left region.
We construct a superposition of eigenstates with support mainly in the left region. The initial wavefunction can be written as,

\Psi(t=0,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{2\uppi\hbar}\;c(p)\,\phi^{L}_{p}(x)\,,\qquad\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{2\uppi\hbar}\,|c(p)|^{2}=1\,,\quad

(19)

c(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}x\;\phi^{L}_{p}(x)\,\Psi(0,x)\,,\quad

(20)

with the momentum eigenvalue
p\in(0,\infty)
. We then convolve this state with the kernel,

\Psi(t,x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}y\,K(t,x;\,0,y)\,\Psi(0,y)\,,\quad

(21)

and using the late-time form of the
kernel (
9
) we obtain,

\Psi(t,x)\approx\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{\mathrm{d}p}{2\uppi\hbar}\;\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}y\,c(p)\Big(G(E_{0},x,y)\,(E_{0}t)^{-\gamma}+\sum_{n}F(E_{n},x,y)\,\exp\!\Bigl\{-\tfrac{\Gamma_{n}}{2}\,t\Bigr\}\Big)\,\phi^{L}_{p}(y)\,.\quad

(22)

The power-law tail is obtained by using an asymptotic expansion valid at late time. The higher-order poles have a
larger imaginary component and will therefore decay quickly. The dawn time sets in when
the leading-order pole starts dominating the decay over the higher-order pole contributions,
i.e.
when

\frac{\widetilde{F}(E_{0})\,\exp\!\{-\Gamma_{0}\,t\}}{\widetilde{F}(E_{n})\,\exp\!\{-\Gamma_{n}\,t\}}\gg 1\qquad\left(\forall\,n\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}\right)\,,\quad

(23)

where we have introduced,

\widetilde{F}(E_{n})=\int_{\mathrm{L}}\!\mathrm{d}x\,\Biggl|\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\mathrm{d}yc(p)\;F(E_{n},x,y)\,\phi^{L}_{p}(y)\Biggr|^{2}\,,\quad

where we have neglected the interference terms. Typically, some
\widetilde{F}(E_{n})
in Eq. (
23
) will dominate. Based on this consideration, we can extract the
following initial estimate for the dawn time,

t_{\circlebottomhalfblack}=\max_{n}\left\{\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}-\Gamma_{0}}\ln\!\left[\frac{\widetilde{F}(E_{n})}{\widetilde{F}(E_{0})}\right]\right\}\,,\quad

(24)

where the initial-state dependence is captured by
\widetilde{F}(E_{n})
. Nevertheless, for some low energy initial states the higher modes
\Gamma_{n}\,(n\geq 1)
cannot be excited, such that all terms in (
24
) are negative.
In such cases, the dawn time is obtained by investigating when the
\Gamma_{0}
exponential starts to dominate over the power-law tail,
i.e.
the earlier intersection point of (
11
), which is obtained from (
16
). Hence, the final expression for the dawn time is,

t_{\circlebottomhalfblack}=\max\left\{\max_{n}\left\{\frac{1}{\Gamma_{n}-\Gamma_{0}}\ln\!\left[\frac{\widetilde{F}(E_{n})}{\widetilde{F}(E_{0})}\right]\right\},-\,\frac{2\gamma}{\Gamma_{0}}\,W_{0}\!\left(-\,\frac{\hbar}{E_{0}}\Bigg|\frac{G}{F}\Bigg|^{\!\frac{1}{\gamma}}\!\frac{\Gamma_{0}}{2\gamma}\right)\right\}\;.\quad

(25)

To summarize, our analysis shows that the standard expression for exponential decay of trapped states
(obtained
e.g.
by Euclidean methods) holds in the interval,

t_{\circlebottomhalfblack}\leq t\leq t_{\circletophalfblack}
.

Discussion

Our analysis of the dawn time (
25
) and twilight time (
17
)–(
18
) in one-dimensional tunneling ties together several complementary views of quantum decay, from textbook transmission coefficients to modern real-time saddle-point methods. For the square barrier, we showed that the rate extracted from the real-time kernel obeys
\Gamma T=T_{\rm trans}
in the thick-barrier limit, with
T
the classical oscillation period and
T_{\rm trans}
the standard transmission probability. This makes the Gamow picture precise and clarifies how

sets both the height and temporal extent of the exponential plateau before the universal power-law tail takes over.

In spectral language, the decay is controlled by the isolated complex poles embedded in a continuum. This viewpoint extends naturally to periodic (Bloch) systems, where isolated resonances broaden into bands and interwell tunneling is encoded in the Bloch bandwidth
Ambroziński 2013
, a picture characterizing many condensed matter systems. At a more formal level, the pole-plus-branch decomposition of the kernel provides a simple laboratory for the complex-saddle technology using Picard-Lefschetz theory
Witten 2011
and the real-time analysis of tunneling in the Rosen-Morse potential
Feldbrugge et al. 2025
. Our approach sheds light on the Euclidean bounce and direct methods, and shows that Euclidean methods apply between
t_{\circlebottomhalfblack}
and
t_{\circletophalfblack}
.

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\uppi}\sum_{n}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2E_{n}}}\,\frac{|A_{2}R-B_{2}|^{2}}{|N^{\prime}(k)N^{*}(k)|}2\cos(k(x-y)/\hbar)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}E_{n}t/\hbar-b_{n}t/\hbar}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2\uppi}\sqrt{\frac{m}{2E_{0}}}\,\frac{|A_{2}R-B_{2}|^{2}}{|N^{\prime}(k)N^{*}(k)|}2\cos(k(x-y)/\hbar)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}E_{0}t/\hbar-b_{0}t/\hbar}\,,\quad

(111)

where the last approximation holds for
t\gg\hbar/\Gamma_{0}
(late times).

To evaluate
g(t,x,y)
, Setting
\mathcal{E}\to E_{0}\alpha
,

g(t,x,y)=-\mathrm{i}\frac{E_{0}}{2\uppi\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{m}{-2\mathrm{i}E_{0}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\alpha\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\,\frac{|A_{2}R-B_{2}|^{2}}{|N|^{2}}2\cos(\sqrt{-2m\mathrm{i}E_{0}\alpha}(x-y)/\hbar)\mathrm{e}^{-E_{0}\alpha t/\hbar}\,.\quad

(112)

In the late time limit, only small
\alpha
contribute, and hence,

\displaystyle g(t,x,y)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\frac{E_{0}}{2\uppi\hbar}\sqrt{\frac{m}{-2\mathrm{i}E_{0}}}\frac{|A_{2}(0)R(0)\!-\!B_{2}(0)|^{2}}{|N(0)|^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\mathrm{d}\alpha\,\alpha^{-1/2}\,2\cos\!\left(\sqrt{-2m\mathrm{i}E_{0}\alpha}\,\frac{x\!-\!y}{\hbar}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-E_{0}\alpha t/\hbar}

(113)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\uppi\hbar}}\frac{|A_{2}(0)R(0)\!-\!B_{2}(0)|^{2}}{|N(0)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\uppi/4}\,t^{-1/2}\,\exp\!\left[\frac{\mathrm{i}m(x\!-\!y)^{2}}{2\hbar t}\right]

(114)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\hbar}\,\sqrt{\frac{mE_{0}}{2\uppi}}\frac{|A_{2}(0)R(0)\!-\!B_{2}(0)|^{2}}{|N(0)|^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\uppi/4}(E_{0}t/\hbar)^{-1/2}+\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(t^{-3/2}\bigr)\,.\quad

(115)

Therefore, the kernel scales as,

\displaystyle K(t,x;0,y)

\displaystyle\approx G(E_{0},x,y)\,(E_{0}t/\hbar)^{-1/2}+\sum_{n}F(E_{n},x,y)\,\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac{\Gamma_{n}}{2}t\right\}

\displaystyle\approx G(E_{0},x,y)\,(E_{0}t/\hbar)^{-1/2}+F(E_{0},x,y)\,\exp\!\left\{-\tfrac{\Gamma_{0}}{2}t\right\}\,.\quad

(116)

C.2
Explicit calculation of the rate

Here we perform an explicit calculation of the rate
and twilight time. For convenience we assume
b\to+\infty
. We need to find the zeros of
A_{+}(k)=A_{2}(k)R(k)+B_{2}(k)=0
. Notice that,

\lim_{b\to+\infty}R(k)=\frac{B_{1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}kb}\,2^{\,\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\alpha}}+B_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kb}\,2^{-\frac{-\mathrm{i}k}{\alpha}}}{A_{1}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}kb}\,2^{\,\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\alpha}}+A_{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kb}\,2^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}k}{\alpha}}}\,,\quad

(117)

and hence we have,

(A_{2}B_{1}+B_{2}A_{1})2^{\mathrm{i}k/\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-ikb}+2A_{2}B_{2}2^{-\mathrm{i}k/\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}kb}=0\,,\quad

(118)

\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}kL}=-\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}+\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}\right]\,,\quad

(119)

where
L=b-\frac{\ln 2}{\alpha}
. In the thick barrier limit
\frac{\sqrt{2m(U_{0}-E)}}{\hbar\alpha}\gg 1
,
|\frac{A_{1}}{A_{2}}|=|\frac{B_{1}}{B_{2}}|=1
, and hence we have approximate real roots
k_{R,n}=\frac{(2n+1)\uppi}{2L}
. It is difficult to solve the complex solutions and calculate the decay rate
\Gamma
directly, but we can deploy an ”inverse” approach. Just like what we have discussed in Appendix
A
and
B
, for large
b\to+\infty
and the thick barrier condition
\kappa/{(\hbar\alpha)}\gg 1
we then have,

T_{\rm trans}=T\Gamma\,,\quad

(120)

where
T=\frac{2mb}{k_{R}}
is the oscillation period and
T_{\rm trans}
is the transmission coefficient for Pöschl-Teller potential,

T_{\rm trans}=\frac{\sinh^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{\uppi k}{\alpha\hbar}\right)}{\sinh^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{\uppi k}{\alpha\hbar}\right)+\cosh^{2}\!\left(\tfrac{\uppi}{2}\sqrt{\tfrac{8mU_{0}}{\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}-1}\right)}\,.\quad

Hence we could find

\displaystyle\Gamma=\lim_{\kappa/{(\hbar\alpha)}\gg 1}\frac{T_{\rm trans}}{T}=\frac{2k_{R}}{bm}\sinh^{2}(\frac{\uppi k_{R}}{\alpha\hbar})\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\uppi\kappa}{\hbar\alpha}}\,.\quad

(121)

Here we could also calculate the twilight time using (
121
),

t_{\circletophalfblack}^{(C)}\;=\;-\,\frac{1}{\Gamma_{0}}\;W_{-1}\!\left(-\,\frac{\Gamma_{0}\hbar}{E_{0}}\;\left|\frac{G}{F}\right|^{2}\right)\,.\quad

(122)

Using
W_{-1}(-\epsilon)=\ln(\epsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\ln|\ln\epsilon|)
for
\epsilon\to 0
, we finally get,

t_{\circletophalfblack}^{(C)}\approx\frac{2\uppi}{\Gamma_{0}}\frac{\kappa}{\hbar\alpha}\,,\qquad

(123)

which is Eq. (
18
) in the main text.

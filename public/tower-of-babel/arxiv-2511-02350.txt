Decay of transmon qubit strongly coupled with a continuum
Ya. S. Greenberg, A. A. Shtygashev, O. V. Kibis

Abstract
We investigate the decay of three-level artificial atom, a superconducting transmon qubit which interacts with a continuum of modes in an open one-dimensional waveguide. For strong interaction of transmon with a continuum we obtain analytical expressions for the frequency shifts and widths of the resonances the values of which are calculated numerically for the Gaussian density of states. We show that the coupling between the second level and ground state of a transmon significantly influences the decay of the third transmon level.

I
Introduction

In recent years, the superconducting transmon qubit has become one
of the most promising platforms for the realization of scalable
quantum processing architectures
Ach2024
;
Ach2023
;
Arute2019
;
Jurc2021
. Transmon is fabricated by a thin film
technology as a three-level artificial atom, which is very robust
against bias voltage noise
Koch2007
and may have extremely
long lifetime
Wang2022
.

Recent developments in quantum technologies have led to new
experimental implementations of the transmon-photon interaction
which are based on the strong coupling achievable between
microwave photons and qubits in an open coplanar waveguide

Astaf2010
;
Sult2025
;
Gasp2017
;
Lu2021
;
Gunin2025
.

The efficiency of photon manipulation in a waveguide- qubit setup
depends on the qubit decoherence rate. Standard measurement
procedures based on dispersive qubit readout, which are commonly
used in a cavity, cannot be applied in open waveguide due to the
lack of a resonator.

The measurement of the relaxation and decoherence rates of a superconducting
transmon qubit in an open waveguide requires the application of a
two-pulse technique with the excitation of both transmon levels
Sult2025
.
From the other hand, the intense-pulse measurements in an resonator-free
setting allow for the investigation of the effects of the strong coupling
between a transmon and the continuum of the waveguide modes

Chang2007
.

In this paper we investigate the decay of a three-level artificial
atom, a superconducting transmon qubit which interacts with a
continuum of modes in an open one-dimensional waveguide.
Specifically, we investigate the probability that the transmon
being initially excited in its third level will remain in this
state at some later time. For strong interaction of transmon with
a continuum we obtain analytical expressions for the shifts and
widths of the resonances the values of witch are calculated
numerically for the Gaussian density of states. We show that the
coupling between the second level and ground state of a transmon
significantly influences the decay of the third transmon level.

The paper is organized as follows. The description of the model is
given in Section II. The interaction between transmon qubit and
the continuum is written in the form of multimode Jaynes-Cummings
Hamiltonian. In Section III we formulate the projection operator
technique for our problem. In Section IV the Green function method
is applied to obtain the general expressions of the probability
amplitudes for transmon levels. The matrix elements for the shift
operator are calculated in Section V. The frequency of resonances
and their widths are calculated in Section VI. In Section VII we
transform the expressions for the frequency shifts and the
resonance widths to continuum limit. In Section VIII we study the
coupling between transmon qubit and continuum modes for the
Gaussian form of the density of states. The spectral function
obtained in Section VIII allow us to numerically calculate in
Section IX the resonance frequencies and their widths.

II
The model

In its simplest form a transmon is a three-level artificial atom.
We denote these levels as
|g\rangle,|e\rangle,|f\rangle
with
the energies
E_{g}
,
E_{e}
,
E_{f}
, respectively. Throughout the
paper we count the energies from the ground state
|g\rangle
, so
that we assume
E_{g}=0
. The level structure of transmon is shown
in Fig.
1
.

Figure 1:
Transmon levels

The distinctive feature of a transmon is that the distance between
the third level
|f\rangle
and the second level
|e\rangle
,

E_{f}-E_{e}\equiv E_{e}^{\prime}
, is a little bit smaller than the
distance between the second level
|e\rangle
and the ground state

|g\rangle
. Therefore, a transmon is characterized by its
anharmonicity
\Delta E_{e}=E_{e}-E_{e}^{\prime}
, which can be
expressed in dimensionless unit
\alpha=\Delta E_{e}/E_{e}
. The
transmon energies
E_{e}
and
E_{f}
fall in GHz range. For practical
transmons the values of
\alpha
lie within
0.001\div 0.01

range.

In what follows we set
\hbar=1
, so that all energies are
expressed in frequency units.

We write the transmon Hamiltonian in the basis of the uncoupled
transmon states in the form of multimode Jaynes- Cummings model

Koch2007
:

H=H_{0}+V_{1}+V_{2}

(1)

where

H_{0}=E_{e}\left|e\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|+E_{f}\left|f\right\rangle\left\langle f\right|+\sum\limits_{k}{}\omega_{k}a_{k}^{+}a_{k}

(2)

V_{1}=\sum\limits_{k}{}g_{1}(k)\left({\left|g\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|a_{k}^{+}+\left|e\right\rangle\left\langle g\right|a_{k}}\right)

(3)

V_{2}=\sum\limits_{k}{}g_{2}(k)\left({\left|e\right\rangle\left\langle f\right|a_{k}^{+}+\left|f\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|a_{k}}\right)

(4)

In transmon the nearest-neighbor coupling is realized

Koch2007
. Therefore, for the first three levels which we
consider here the second level
|e\rangle
interacts with both the
ground state
|g\rangle
and the third level
|f\rangle
. The
interaction between
|g\rangle
and
|f\rangle
is absent. In
addition,
g_{2}(k)=\sqrt{(3/2)}g_{1}(k)

Koch2007
.

III
Projection operators

We apply here the resolvent-operator formalism
Kohen2004

which is well known for the study of similar problems

Hu2023
;
Green2015
. This formalism relates the time-
evolution operator
U(t)
to the resolvent operator
G(z)
via the
integral

U(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}{dE}\;U(E)e^{-iEt}

(5)

where

U(E)=\frac{1}{{2\pi i}}\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\eta\to 0_{+}}\left({G(E-i\eta)-G(E+i\eta)}\right),

(6)

From the space of all states we choose the states which
play an important role in the physical process under study.
Namely, we choose the following states which are the eigenvectors
of Hamiltonian
H_{0}
: two discrete states
|e0\rangle,|f0\rangle
, and the states
|gk\rangle,|ek\rangle,|gk_{1}k_{2}\rangle
which lie in the continuum. In the states

|e0\rangle,|f0\rangle
there are no photons in the system; in the
states
|gk\rangle,|ek\rangle
there is one photon with wave
vector
k
, and the state
|gk_{1}k_{2}\rangle
describes the transmon
in the ground state with two photons with wave vectors
k_{1},k_{2}

in the system. Evidently,

\begin{gathered}\left|{gk}\right\rangle=a_{k}^{+}\left|{g0}\right\rangle\hfill\\
\left|{ek}\right\rangle=a_{k}^{+}\left|{e0}\right\rangle\hfill\\
\left|{gk_{1}k_{2}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{2}}}a_{k_{1}}^{+}a_{k_{2}}^{+}\left|{g0}\right\rangle\hfill\\
\left\langle{gk_{1}k_{2}|gk_{3}k_{4}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta_{k_{1},k_{3}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{4}}+\delta_{k_{1},k_{4}}\delta_{k_{2},k_{3}}}\right)\hfill\\
\end{gathered}

(7)

Next,we define the projection operators
P
and
Q

P=\left|{e0}\right\rangle\left\langle{e0}\right|+\left|{f0}\right\rangle\left\langle{f0}\right|

(8)

Q=\sum\limits_{k}{}\left|{gk}\right\rangle\left\langle{gk}\right|+\sum\limits_{k}{}\left|{ek}\right\rangle\left\langle{ek}\right|+\sum\limits_{k_{1},k_{2}}{}\left|{gk_{1}k_{2}}\right\rangle\left\langle{gk_{1}k_{2}}\right|

(9)

with the properties

\begin{gathered}P+Q=1;\;P^{2}=P;\;Q^{2}=Q;\;PQ=QP=0;\hfill\\
\left[{Q,H_{0}}\right]=\left[{P,H_{0}}\right]=0\hfill\\
\end{gathered}

(10)

The operator
P
projects on the states with no photons, while the
operator
Q
projects on the continuum states with one or two
photons.

IV
Green function

The matrix elements of Green function

G_{fe}(z)=\frac{{R_{fe}(z)}}{{D(z)}};G_{ff}(z)=\frac{{z-E_{e}-R_{ee}(z)}}{{D(z)}}\hfill\\
\end{gathered}

(13)

where

\begin{gathered}D(z)=\left({z-E_{e}-R_{ee}(z)}\right)\left({z-E_{f}-R_{ff}(z)}\right)\hfill\\
-R_{ef}(z)R_{fe}(z)\\
\end{gathered}

(14)

We will see below that for the interaction

and

are given in (
3
) and (
4

G_{ef}(z)=G_{fe}(z)=0

\begin{gathered}G_{ee}(z)=\frac{1}{{\left({z-E_{e}-R_{ee}(z)}\right)}};\hfill\\
G_{ff}(z)=\frac{1}{{\left({z-E_{f}-R_{ff}(z)}\right)}}\hfill\\
\end{gathered}

(15)

It might seem from (

depends only on its interaction

|g\rangle

The probability that the system being initially in the state

(16)

where

(17)

G_{ff}(E\pm i\eta)=\frac{1}{{\left({E\pm i\eta-E_{f}-R_{ff}(E\pm i\eta)}\right)}}

(18)

In these equations the subscript

means the diagonal matrix

|f0\rangle
. For example,

, etc.

Applying here the general form of the shift operator

(19)

we write the expression (

Substituting (

\begin{gathered}U_{ff}(E)\hfill\\
=\frac{1}{{2\pi i}}\mathop{\lim}\limits_{\eta\to 0}\frac{{2i\eta+i\Gamma_{2}(E,\eta)}}{{\left({E-E_{f}-\Delta_{2}(E,\eta)}\right)^{2}+\frac{{\Gamma_{2}^{2}(E,\eta)}}{4}}}\end{gathered}

(21)

and

X
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented a theoretical framework for
calculating the decay properties of the third transmon level for
strong coupling of the transmon with the continuum of modes in an
open waveguide. We showed that the coupling between the second
transmon level and the ground state of a transmon significantly
influences the decay of the third transmon level. We showed that
for two-level decay the strong coupling leads to the formation
within a continuum of the quasi stable long lived states which
give rise to a weak damping Rabi oscillations of the probability
amplitude. However, the interaction between second level and
ground state completely destroy this coherence.

We firmly believe that the results obtained in this work can be
extended and used in the research of artificial atoms in an open
quantum systems.

Optimal Control for Minimizing Inescapable Ellipsoids in Linear Periodically Time-Varying Systems under Bounded Disturbances
Egor Dogadin, Alexey Peregudin

Abstract
This letter addresses optimal controller design for periodic linear time-varying systems under unknown-but-bounded disturbances. We introduce differential Lyapunov-type equations to describe time-varying inescapable ellipsoids and define an integral-based measure of their size. To minimize this measure, we develop a differential Riccati equation-based approach that provides exact solutions for state-feedback, observer synthesis, and output-feedback control. A key component is a systematic procedure for determining the optimal time-varying parameter, reducing an infinite-dimensional optimization to a simple iterative process. A numerical example validates the method’s effectiveness.



I
Introduction

Optimal control under unknown-but-bounded disturbances remains a challenge in control theory. While LQG,
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, and
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
controllers perform well under specific disturbances (e.g., white noise or harmonic inputs), they are generally suboptimal when disturbances are only known to be bounded, as in practical systems such as autonomous drones, marine vehicles, and robotic manipulators. In such cases, external influences like wind gusts, water currents, or actuator nonlinearities often lack a precise structure but can be bounded in magnitude. This has motivated the use of
invariant (inescapable) ellipsoids
to characterize reachability bounds.

The concept of inescapable ellipsoids under bounded inputs dates back to
[
1
,
2
]
, which established fundamental equations forming the basis for controller design. Early applications, such as
[
3
]
, used these ellipsoids for peak-to-peak minimization, but
[
4
]
showed that minimizing the ellipsoid alone does not guarantee the smallest peak-to-peak gain, leading to alternative performance measures.

A different approach emerged with the introduction of a new trace-based optimality criterion
[
5
]
, making it conceptually similar to
\mathcal{H}_{2}
control (see
[
6
]
), and opening possibilities for controller design based on Linear Matrix Inequalities (LMIs). This framework was extended to state-feedback
[
5
]
, output-feedback
[
7
]
, and observer design
[
8
]
, and applied to nonlinear extensions
[
9
,
10
]
, robust control
[
11
]
, and sliding-mode control
[
12
,
13
]
, with a comprehensive summary in
[
14
]
. These formulations relied heavily on LMIs, often introducing robustness along with some degree of conservatism.

Exact solutions to the problems in
[
5
,
7
,
8
]
were recently derived using Riccati equations
[
15
,
16
]
. By introducing dual ellipsoids and duality relations, these works provided the first exact solution to the inescapable ellipsoid minimization problem for continuous-time LTI systems with output-feedback control. Similar ideas were later extended to discrete-time systems
[
17
]
. However, minimization of inescapable ellipsoids for LTV systems remains an open problem, as existing studies
[
18
,
19
]
remain conservative and lack exact solutions.

To bridge this gap, this letter extends the Riccati-based approach in
[
16
]
to periodic LTV systems. Specifically, we introduce an optimality measure aligned with LTV
\mathcal{H}_{2}
control, using a time-averaged trace-based criterion (see
[
20
]
).

A key challenge in this framework is selecting the optimal parameter
\alpha
(or its time-varying counterpart
\alpha(t)
) for constructing the inescapable ellipsoid
[
3
,
5
,
16
]
. Even in LTI systems, choosing
\alpha
is non-trivial, and in LTV systems, where
\alpha
varies over time, the problem becomes even more complex
[
1
]
. Previous approaches often assumed a constant
\alpha
, limiting optimality
[
14
]
. We propose a method to compute the optimal
\alpha(t)
, making the problem tractable for LTV systems and also offering a more direct approach for the LTI case.

Contributions of this Letter:

•

Exact (necessary and sufficient) characterization of periodic time-varying inescapable ellipsoids.

•

An approach for periodic LTV systems that provides
optimal
state-feedback, observer, and output-feedback controller under bounded disturbances.

•

A systematic method to find the optimal

II
Analysis: Time-Varying Inescapable Ellipsoids

Consider a linear periodically time-varying system

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=A(t)x(t)+B(t)w(t),\\
z(t)=C(t)x(t),\end{cases}

(1)

where
x(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
w(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
z(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
, and
A(\cdot)
,
B(\cdot)
,
C(\cdot)
are real, piecewise-continuous,
T
-periodic matrix-valued functions, for some
T>0
satisfying
A(t)=A(t+T)
,
B(t)=B(t+T)
,
C(t)=C(t+T)
. Assume
(A(\cdot),B(\cdot))
is controllable and
(C(\cdot),A(\cdot))
is observable.

Suppose the system is subject to unit-bounded inputs

\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\;:\;\lVert w(t)\rVert\leq 1,

(2)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm. This unit bound incurs no loss of generality. In the general LTV case, it is natural to consider disturbances confined within a time-varying ellipsoid
w(t)^{\top}W(t)w(t)\leq 1
, with
w_{\min}I\preceq W(t)\preceq w_{\max}I
, as in
[
1
]
. In periodic settings,
W(t)
is typically periodic. Such bounds can be naturally absorbed into the input matrix by the rescaling
B_{\text{new}}(t)=B(t)(W(t))^{-1/2}
, recovering (
2
).

Definition 1
((Periodic inescapable ellipsoids))

Periodic inescapable ellipsoids
\mathcal{E}_{P}(\cdot)
of the system (
1
)–(
2
) are defined as the time-varying sets

\mathcal{E}_{P}(t)\vcentcolon=\left\{\,{\rm x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\;\mid\;{\rm x}^{\top}P(t)^{-1}{\rm x}\leq 1\,\right\},

where
P(t)=P(t+T)\succ 0
, and the following inescapability property is satisfied:

x(t_{0})\in\mathcal{E}_{P}(t_{0})\;\;\Rightarrow\;\;\forall t\geq t_{0}\;:\;x(t)\in\mathcal{E}_{P}(t).

(3)

For LTI systems, ellipsoids inescapable under (
2
) are known as invariant (attractive) ellipsoids
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
and are described by a constant matrix
P
. In this case, they are time-independent, and the periodicity condition is obsolete. Moreover, a stationary ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{P}
is inescapable for (
1
)–(
2
) with constant
A
,
B
,
C
iff there exists a constant
\alpha>0
such that

AP+PA^{\top}+\alpha P+\frac{1}{\alpha}BB^{\top}\preceq\,0,\quad P\succ 0.

For a fixed
\alpha
, the smallest (inclusion-minimal) ellipsoid is obtained by replacing
\preceq
with
=
. Hence, in the LTI case, the family of minimal inescapable ellipsoids is parametrized by the single parameter
\alpha
.

The following theorem characterizes the periodic inescapable ellipsoids of an LTV system.

Theorem 1

\mathcal{E}_{P}(\cdot)
is a periodic inescapable ellipsoid for the system (
1
)–(
2
) if and only if the matrix-valued function
P(t)=P(t+T)\succ 0
satisfies the differential inequality

\phantom{1}\hskip-5.69046pt\dot{P}(t)\!\,\succeq\,\!A(t)P(t)+P(t)A(t)^{\!\top}\!\!+\alpha(t)P(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}B(t)B(t)^{\!\top}\hskip-10.243pt\phantom{1}

for some positive function
\alpha(t)>0
. For a fixed
\alpha(\cdot)
, the smallest (inclusion-minimal) ellipsoid is obtained from

\phantom{1}\hskip-5.69046pt\dot{P}(t)\!=\!A(t)P(t)+P(t)A(t)^{\!\top}\!\!+\alpha(t)P(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}B(t)B(t)^{\!\top}\hskip-10.243pt\phantom{1}.

(4)

Each minimal periodic inescapable ellipsoid is uniquely determined by
\alpha(\cdot)
and may be denoted
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(\cdot)\doteq\mathcal{E}_{P}(\cdot)
, where
P
is given by (
4
). By periodic differential Lyapunov theory
[
21
]
, the ellipsoid is well-defined if
\alpha(\cdot)
is
T
-periodic,
A(t)+\frac{\alpha(t)}{2}I
is exponentially stable, and
(A(\cdot),B(\cdot))
is controllable.

Equation (
4
) dates back to
[
1
]
, which also inspired the underlying idea of Theorem
1
, though it was not stated there in its present form. We provide a simpler, self-contained proof of Theorem
1
(included in the
Appendix
along with other proofs).
Notably, later works
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
used only a constant
\alpha
even for LTV systems
[
18
,
19
]
, thus restricting the class of candidate ellipsoids and potentially missing the optimal one. This letter reconsiders
\alpha
as time-dependent and introduces an effective method to compute the optimal
\alpha(\cdot)
, minimizing the periodic inescapable ellipsoid. All subsequent results presented in the letter are, to the best of our knowledge, entirely new.

We introduce the following measure of the ellipsoid’s size:

\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})\doteq\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{trace}\left(C(t)P(t)C(t)^{\!\top}\right)dt,

(5)

which can be interpreted as the average sum of squares of the semiaxes of the image of the ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(t)
under the mapping
x\mapsto z=C(t)x
. This measure aligns with established theory; for instance, replacing
P(t)
in (
5
) with the controllability Gramian of system (
1
) yields the squared
\mathcal{H}_{2}
-norm of the system (see
[
20
]
). The following duality result holds:

Theorem 2

Let
P(t)=P(t+T)\succ 0
be given by (
4
), and let
Q(t)=Q(t+T)\succ 0
satisfy the dual equation

-\dot{Q}(t)=Q(t)A(t)+A(t)^{\!\top}Q(t)+\alpha(t)Q(t)+C(t)^{\!\top}C(t).

(6)

The size of the ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}(\cdot)
can then be expressed in terms of the dual matrix
Q(t)
as follows:

\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{trace}\left(B(t)^{\!\top}Q(t)B(t)\right)}{\alpha(t)}dt.

The system’s sensitivity to disturbance
w
in (
1
) is given by the
smallest
inescapable ellipsoid over all
\alpha(\cdot)
. The following theorem provides a method to determine this tightest bound.

Theorem 3

(I) The optimal time-dependent parameter
\alpha^{*}(t)=\arg\min_{\alpha}\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})
satisfies

\alpha^{*}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{trace}(B(t)^{\!\top}Q(t)B(t))}{\mathrm{trace}(Q(t)P(t))}},

(7)

where
P(t)
,
Q(t)
are such that (
4
), (
6
) with
\alpha(t)=\alpha^{*}(t)
. (II) With any
\alpha_{0}(t)=\alpha_{0}(t+T)>0
, the fixed-point iteration

\alpha_{i+1}(t)=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{trace}(B(t)^{\!\top}Q_{i}(t)B(t))}{\mathrm{trace}(Q_{i}(t)P_{i}(t))}},

(8)

where
P_{i}(t)
,
Q_{i}(t)
are such that (
4
), (
6
) with
\alpha(t)=\alpha_{i}(t)
, converges to the globally optimal
\alpha^{*}(t)
.

III-A

Optimal State-Feedback Controller

Consider a linear periodically time-varying system

\begin{cases}\dot{x}(t)=A(t)x(t)+B(t)u(t)+B_{w}(t)w(t),\\
z(t)=C(t)x(t)+D(t)u(t),\end{cases}

(9)

where
x(t)
is the state,
z(t)
is the regulated output,
u(t)
is the control,
w(t)
is the disturbance and
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
,
B_{w}
are piecewise-continuous
T
-periodic matrix-functions. Here
(A(\cdot),B(\cdot))
is stabilizable,
(C(\cdot),A(\cdot))
is observable, and
D(t)^{\!\top}\!\begin{bmatrix}C(t)&D(t)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\end{bmatrix}
.
The latter standard assumption (see
[
6
]
) simplifies formulas but can be omitted at the cost of more complex controller equations.

Consider a linear time-varying controller:

u(t)=K(t)x(t),

(10)

the corresponding closed-loop system (
2
), (
9
)-(
10
), and the family of periodic inescapable ellipsoids

associated with it. In line with (

where

, and

is obtained from the closed-loop version of (
4
) and depends on both
\alpha
and

.

The state-feedback design objectives are as follows:

(S1)

Fixed-
\alpha
optimality
. For a given
\alpha(\cdot)
, find the optimal controller gain

.

(S2)

Global optimality
. Find the optimal
\alpha(\cdot)
and controller gain pair
(\alpha^{*}(t),K^{*}(t))=\arg\min_{\alpha,K}\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,K})
.

The following theorem provides a method to obtain optimal controllers that fulfill the design objective (

).

Theorem 4

For a given
\alpha(\cdot)>0
, the optimal controller gain
K(t)=K^{*}_{\alpha}(t)=\arg\min_{K}\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,K})
is given by

K(t)=-B(t)^{\!\top}Q(t),

where
Q(t)=Q(t+T)\succ 0
is obtained from

\displaystyle-\dot{Q}(t)

\displaystyle=Q(t)A(t)+A(t)^{\top}Q(t)+\alpha(t)Q(t)

(12)

\displaystyle+C(t)^{\top}C(t)-Q(t)B(t)B(t)^{\top}Q(t).

Theorem

provides the exact solution to problem (
S1
). The solution to problem (
S2

III-D

III-D

Iterative Approach Towards Global Optimality

Minimizing inescapable ellipsoids is efficiently achieved via LMIs
[
8
,
14
]
or Riccati equations
[
16
,
17
]
when
\alpha
is fixed, but optimizing over all
\alpha
remains challenging. For LTI systems, one can evaluate a range of numerical
\alpha
values to find the optimum. In the LTV case,
\alpha(\cdot)
is a function, making the search space infinite-dimensional.

Using Theorem
3
, we propose an efficient algorithm that seeks
\alpha^{*}(\cdot)
, addressing problems (
S2
), (
F2
) and (
O2
).

Algorithm 1

Controller Optimization Over
\alpha

1:

Choose the initial guess
\alpha_{0}(t)=\alpha_{0}(t+T)>0
.

2:

Solve (
S1
), (
F1
) or (
O1
) to find
K(t)
,
L(t)
, or both.

3:

Obtain the system in the form (
1
) with closed-loop matrices
A(t)
,
B(t)
and
C(t)
.

4:

Use (
4
), (
6
) to find
P(t)
,
Q(t)
of the closed-loop system.

5:

Apply the update rule (
8
) to compute
\alpha_{i+1}(t)
.

6:

Repeat steps 2–5 until convergence to a candidate
\alpha^{*}(t)
.

7:

Compute the corresponding
K^{*}(t)
,
L^{*}(t)
, or both.

Theorem
3
guarantees that the update rule (
8
) leads to a minimum; for the analysis problem, this minimum is global owing to the convexity of
\alpha\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})
. However, for the closed-loop design, it is known
[
15
, Remark 3]
that the function
\alpha\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,K,L})
may be non-convex. Consequently, there is no immediate proof of the algorithm’s convergence in the general case. In practice, it is typically sufficient to employ constant initial choices
\alpha_{0}(t)\equiv c
, with
c>0
, to obtain the global optimum with Algorithm
1
.

V
Conclusion

This letter presented an approach for optimal control of periodic LTV systems under unknown-but-bounded disturbances. We proposed a systematic method for designing optimal state-feedback, observer, and output-feedback controllers using parameter-dependent Riccati and Lyapunov equations.
Future work may explore robust formulations of the presented results via LMI-based relaxations of the closed-loop design equations.

Proof of Theorem
(
1
)

Let
V(x(t),t)\vcentcolon=x(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t)
, where
\bar{P}(t)=P(t)^{-1}
. The ellipsoid
\mathcal{E}_{P}(t)
satisfies (
3
) if and only if, for every trajectory
x(\cdot)
and every input
w(\cdot)
, the following implication holds at every moment
t
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &V(x(t),t)=1,\\
&\lVert w(t)\rVert\leq 1,\end{aligned}\right.\quad\Rightarrow\quad\dot{V}(x(t),t)\leq 0.

(21)

By direct differentiation, expand
\dot{V}(x(t),t)
as

\displaystyle\dot{V}(x(t),t)

\displaystyle=x(t)^{\top}\bigl[\dot{\bar{P}}(t)+A(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)+\bar{P}(t)A(t)\bigr]x(t)

\displaystyle\quad+2x(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)B(t)w(t).

Next, note that

\displaystyle 2x(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)B(t)w(t)

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast)}{\leq}2\bigl\lVert B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t)\bigr\rVert

\displaystyle\overset{(\ast\ast)}{\leq}\alpha(t)+\frac{\bigl\lVert B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t){\bigr\rVert}^{2}}{\alpha(t)}

\displaystyle=\alpha(t)x(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}x(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)B(t)B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t).

Both
(\ast)
and
(\ast\ast)
are
tight
and hence do not invalidate the necessity and sufficiency of the argument. Indeed,
(\ast)
becomes an equality under the worst-case input

w(t)=\frac{B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t)}{{\bigl\lVert}B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)x(t){\bigr\rVert}},

and
(\ast\ast)
is the AM-GM inequality, and there always exists
\alpha(t)
that turns it into equality.
Therefore, satisfying (
21
) is equivalent to ensuring that, for some
\alpha(t)>0
,

\displaystyle\dot{\bar{P}}(t)+A(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)+\bar{P}(t)A(t)

\displaystyle\quad+\alpha(t)\bar{P}(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}\bar{P}(t)B(t)B(t)^{\top}\bar{P}(t)\preceq 0

Since
\dot{\bar{P}}(t)=-\bar{P}(t)\dot{P}(t)\bar{P}(t)
, multiplying both sides by
P(t)
yields the desired Lyapunov-type inequality. The minimal
P(t)
with respect to
\preceq
makes it an equality (cf.
[
3
]
).

Auxiliary Lemma 1

For
T
-periodic solutions of

\displaystyle\dot{P}(t)

\displaystyle=A(t)P(t)+P(t)A(t)^{\top}+\alpha(t)P(t)+R(t),

\displaystyle-\dot{Q}(t)

\displaystyle=A(t)^{\top}Q(t)+Q(t)A(t)+\alpha(t)Q(t)+G(t),

with piecewise-continuous
R(t),G(t)\succeq 0
, it holds that

\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{trace}(Q(t)R(t))dt=\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{trace}(P(t)G(t))dt.\vskip 5.01874pt

Proof of Auxiliary Lemma
(
1
)

The following identity:

\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{trace}(Q(t)\dot{P}(t))dt=-\int_{0}^{T}\mathrm{trace}(\dot{Q}(t)P(t))dt,

is obtained using integration by parts. Substituting
\dot{P}
,
\dot{Q}
and rearranging the terms gives the result, where both integrals converge due to the piecewise-continuity of the integrands.

Proof of Theorem
(
2
)

Setting
R(t)=\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}B(t)B(t)^{\top}
, and
G(t)=C(t)^{\top}C(t)
proves the theorem by Lemma
1
.

\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}

Proof of Auxiliary Lemma
(
2
)

The analytical periodic solution of the Lyapunov equation (
4
) is:

\displaystyle P(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\underset{(f\circ g)(\alpha(\tau))}{\,\underbrace{\frac{e^{\int_{\tau}^{t}\alpha(s)ds}}{\alpha(\tau)}}\,}\Phi(t,\tau)B(\tau)B(\tau)^{\!\top}\Phi(t,\tau)^{\!\top}d\tau,

where
\Phi(t,\tau)
is the state-transition matrix of the system (
1
).

Considering that the mapping
f:(x,y)\mapsto\frac{e^{y}}{x}
is strictly convex for
x>0
and the operator
g:\alpha(\tau)\mapsto(\alpha(\tau),\int_{\tau}^{t}\alpha(s)ds)
is linear, their composition
f\circ g
is strictly convex as well. The mapping
(f\circ g)(\alpha(\cdot))\mapsto P
is strictly convex due to controllability, and the mapping
P\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{P})
is strictly convex due to observability.

Proof of Theorem
(
3
)

Following standard optimal control methodology, consider the Hamiltonian associated with the functional
J:\alpha\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})
:

\mathcal{H}(t)\doteq\mathrm{trace}\left(C(t)P(t)C(t)^{\top}\right)+\mathrm{trace}\left(Q(t)\dot{P}(t)\right),

where
Q(t)
is the Lagrange multiplier, which can be found from the adjoint differential equation
\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial P}=-\dot{Q}(t)
:

\displaystyle-\dot{Q}(t)=Q(t)A(t)+A(t)^{\top}Q(t)+\alpha(t)Q(t)+C(t)^{\top}C(t).

The functional derivative of
J
in the detection of
\alpha
is

\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial\alpha}=\mathrm{trace}\left(Q(t)\left(P(t)-\frac{B(t)B(t)^{\top}}{\alpha^{2}(t)}\right)\right).

By Lemma
2
, functional
J
is convex under variations of
\alpha
. Thus, setting the derivative to
0
yields the unique solution (
7
) proving part (I) of the theorem.

The terms of functional derivative of
J
with respect to
\alpha_{i}
can be rearranged as follows:

\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha_{i}}=\mathrm{trace}(Q_{i}(t)P_{i}(t))\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{trace}\left(B(t)^{\top}Q_{i}(t)B(t)\right)}{\alpha_{i}^{2}(t)\mathrm{trace}(Q_{i}(t)P_{i}(t))}\right).

Substituting the iteration policy (
8
), leads to:

\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha_{i}}=\frac{\mathrm{trace}(Q_{i}(t)P_{i}(t))}{\alpha_{i}^{2}(t)}\left(\alpha_{i}^{2}(t)-\alpha_{i+1}^{2}(t)\right).

Multiplying both sides by
\Delta\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{i+1}(t)-\alpha_{i}(t)
results in:

\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha_{i}}\Delta\alpha_{i}=-\frac{\mathrm{trace}(Q_{i}(t)P_{i}(t))}{\alpha_{i}^{2}(t)}\left(\alpha_{i}(t)+\alpha_{i+1}(t)\right)\Delta\alpha_{i}^{2}.

Notice that
\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha_{i}}\Delta\alpha_{i}
is non-positive for all
t
. Consider the series expansion of the functional
J
:

J[\alpha_{i}+\Delta\alpha_{i}]=J[\alpha_{i}]+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\delta J}{\delta\alpha_{i}}\Delta\alpha_{i}\,dt+\mu,

where
\mu
represents higher-order terms that vanish near the optimum. The inequality
J[\alpha_{i}+\Delta\alpha_{i}]\leq J[\alpha_{i}]
follows, which completes the proof of part (II).

Proof of Theorem
(
4
)

Consider the Hamiltonian function associated with the functional
J:K\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,K})
:

\mathcal{H}(t)\doteq\mathrm{trace}\left(C_{K}(t)P(t)C_{K}(t)^{\top}\right)+\mathrm{trace}\left(Q(t)\dot{P}(t)\right).

We derive the optimal controller from
\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial K}=0
:

\displaystyle P(t)K^{\top}(t)+P(t)Q(t)B(t)=0\;\Leftrightarrow\;K(t)=-B^{\top}(t)Q(t).

Applying the optimal gain to the adjoint differential equation of system (
9
)-(
10
) results in the adjoint Riccati differential equation (
12
), which has a unique positive-definite solution as long as the assumptions hold
[
23
]
.

Proof of Theorem
(
5

Consider the Hamiltonian function associated with the functional
J:L\mapsto\operatorname{size}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,L})

\mathcal{H}(t)\doteq\mathrm{trace}\left(C_{w}(t)P(t)C_{w}(t)^{\top}\right)+\mathrm{trace}\left(Q(t)\dot{P}(t)\right).

We derive the optimal observer from

\displaystyle C(t)P(t)+\frac{1}{\alpha(t)}L(t)^{\top}=0\;\Leftrightarrow\;L(t)=-\alpha(t)P(t)C^{\top}(t).

The rest of the proof is similar to Theorem

Proof of Theorem

Following the system-splitting approach in

Abstract
. A new modulus of smoothness and its equivalent K K -function are defined on the conic domains in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} , and used to characterize the weighted best approximation by polynomials. Both direct and weak inverse theorems of the characterization are established via the modulus of smoothness. For the conic surface 𝕍 0 d + 1 = { ( x , t ) : ‖ x ‖ = t ≤ 1 } {\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}=\{(x,t):\|x\|=t\leq 1\} , the natural weight function is t − 1 ​ ( 1 − t ) γ t^{-1}(1-t)^{\gamma} , which has a singularity at the apex, the rotational part of the modulus of smoothness is defined in terms of the difference operator in Euler angles with an increment h / t h/\sqrt{t} , akin to the Ditzian-Totik modulus on the interval but with t \sqrt{t} in the denominator, which captures the singularity at the apex.

1.
Introduction

One essential problem in approximation theory is understanding the correlation between the rate of an approximation process
and the smoothness of the function being approximated. For trigonometric polynomial approximation or algebraic polynomial
approximation on the real line, the relation is well understood through the direct and inverse theorems that characterize
the best approximation via either a modulus of smoothness or a
K
-functional (cf.
[
6
,
7
]
). For polynomial approximation
in higher dimensions, a similar characterization has been established fully for a handful of regular domains, most notably
the unit sphere and the unit ball (cf.
[
3
,
4
,
9
,
11
]
), and characteristically for some general class of domains,
such as polytops
[
13
,
14
]
and connect
C^{2}
domain
[
2
]
.

In this paper, we consider weighted approximation on two conic domains that are standardized as

(1)

Conic surface defined by

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}:=\left\{(x,t):\|x\|=t,\,0\leq t\leq 1,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right\},

where
\|x\|
denotes the usual Euclidean norem.

(2)

Solid cone bounded by
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
and the hyperplane
t=1
, defined by

{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}:=\left\{(x,t):\|x\|\leq t,\,0\leq t\leq 1,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\right\}.

Both domains have a singularity at the apex, which may affect how the best approximation can be characterized.

While the analysis in the unit sphere and the unit ball has a long history, the study in the conic domains becomes
feasible only after recent advances in the orthogonal structure of polynomials in these domains. For a class of
weight functions, it is shown in
[
16
]
that orthogonal polynomials on the conic domains are eigenfunctions of
a second-order differential operator, called the spectral operator, and their reproducing kernels satisfy an addition formula.
These two properties share a common root in classical orthogonal polynomials
[
5
]
and are essential for studying
the Fourier orthogonal series. Based on these two properties, a general framework is developed in
[
17
]
for
a space of homogeneous type that allows us to define, in particular, a modulus of smoothness and an equivalent

K
-functional, the latter defined via the spectral operator. These are used to characterize the weighted best
approximation by polynomials under the assumption of highly localized kernels, which are typically established via
the addition formula. The framework generalizes the results in classical domains, such as the unit sphere and
the unit ball, and it also covers the conic domains. The characterization, however, is in line with the classical one
on the unit sphere (cf.
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
15
]
and the references therein), in which the modulus of smoothness
is defined as a multiplier operator of the Fourier orthogonal series, which is, for weighted approximation,
a far cry from the traditional modulus of smoothness defined via differences in function evaluations, and does
not connect to the smoothness of the function as transparently as one would like.

On the unit sphere with constant weight function, a more intuitive modulus of smoothness is defined in
[
3
]

via the differences in Euler angles, which reduces to classical differences on the largest circles on the sphere. This
modulus of smoothness leads to a characterization of the best approximation by polynomials and its equivalent

K
-functional is defined via derivatives in Euler angles, which can be used to give a decomposition of the spectral
operator. Moreover, working with the polar coordinates so that the unit ball can be regarded as a semi-product of
the sphere of radial
r
and the interval on
[0,1]
, a new modulus of smoothness on the unit ball is also derived
in
[
3
]
and used to characterize the best approximation.

Since the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
can also be regarded as a semi-product of spheres of radial
t
and the
interval of
t
in
[0,1]
, it is foreseeable that a new modulus of smoothness, different from the one defined via the
multiplier operator, can be defined and utilized for characterizing weighted best approximation. This is the starting
point for our study. The problem remains challenging since the geometry of the cone has its peculiarity with a
singular point at its apex, which causes technical difficulties that must be overcome. For starters, our new modulus
of smoothness on the conic surface will be a fusion of moduli of smoothness on the sphere and the interval.
However, the conic surface is not a direct product of the sphere and the interval, so it is not immediately
clear how the existing results on the sphere and the interval can be utilized. It turns out that the difference
operators in Euler angles need to have an increment
h/\sqrt{t}
, which is a new phenomenon, so that the modulus
of smoothness is akin to the Ditzian-Totik modulus on the interval, but with
\sqrt{t}
in the denominator. This
phenomenon looks to be novel, and likely what is needed for the domain with this type of singularity.
Using this modulus of smoothness and its equivalent
K
-functional of the first and the second order,
our main results establish the direct and inverse theorems for the best approximation by polynomials in
weighted
L^{p}
spaces on the conic domains.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we recall the background materials and results needed
for our study. This includes the best approximation on the interval, where we will use the Ditzian-Totik modulus of
smoothness for the Jacobi weight, which we need even for the constant weight function on the conic domain and
on spheres and balls, as well as an orthogonal structure on the conic domains. The main results on the conic surface
will be stated and proved in the third section, and the corresponding material on the solid cone will be given in the
fourth section.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathsf{w}})
the weighted
L^{p}
space with respect to the weight
function
{\mathsf{w}}
defined on the domain
\Omega
and its norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty

with the understanding that the space is
C(\Omega)
with the uniform norm when
p=\infty
.

For conical domains, in an attempt to distinguish conic surfaces and solid cones, we shall denote the operator
on the surface in sans serif font, such as
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}
and
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}
, and the operator on the solid domains in bold font,
such as
{\mathbf{P}}_{n}
and
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}
.

Finally, we shall use
c
,
c^{\prime}
,
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
, etc. to denote positive constants that depend on fixed
parameters, and their values may change from line to line, and we shall write
A\sim B
if
c^{\prime}A\leq B\leq cA
.

2.
Preliminary and Lemmas

Our main study will require results for the best approximation by polynomials on the interval
[0,1]
, with the Jacobi
weight function, and on the unit sphere. These will be reviewed in the first two subsections. Orthogonal structure
on the conic surface will be recalled in the third subsection, and the highly localized kernels on the conic surface

2.1.
Approximation on the interval
[0,1]

Let
\Pi_{n}
be the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
. Let
\varpi
be a weight function on the interval
[0,1]
.
For
f\in L^{p}(\varpi,[0,1])
,
1\leq p<\infty
, or
f\in C[0,1]
for
p=\infty
, we consider the quantity of best approximation
by polynomials defined by

E_{n}(f)_{p,\varpi}=\inf_{p\in\Pi_{n}}\|f-p\|_{p,\varpi},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

The characterization of this quantity has been extensively studied; see
[
6
,
7
]
and the references therein. It is
known early on that the difficulty lies in the boundary behavior of approximation. Among various attempts to resolve
the problem, the Ditzian-Totik modulus of smoothness, and its equivalent
K
-functional, is most natural and
satisfactory. To recall its definition, we need the central difference operator
\Delta_{\theta}
with
{\theta}>0
defined
by
\Delta_{\theta}f(t)=f\left(t+\frac{{\theta}}{2}\right)-f\left(t-\frac{{\theta}}{2}\right)
and, for
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the
r
-th central difference

\Delta_{\theta}^{r}
defined by

\Delta_{{\theta}}^{r}f(t):=\Delta_{{\theta}}^{r-1}\Delta_{\theta}f(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}(-1)^{j}\binom{r}{j}f\Big(t+\left(\frac{r}{2}-j\right){\theta}\Big).

Throughout this paper, we define by
\varphi
the function

\varphi(t):=\sqrt{t(1-t)},\qquad 0\leq t\leq 1.

The Ditzian-Totik modulus of smoothness is defined via the central difference with the increment
{\theta}
replaced
by the function
{\theta}\varphi(t)
, which is the feature that makes it stand out. More precisely, for
f\in L^{p}([0,1])
, we define

(2.1)

\omega_{\varphi}^{r}(f;t)_{p}=\sup_{0<{\theta}<t}\left\|\Delta^{r}_{{\theta}\varphi}f\right\|_{L^{p}[0,1]},

where
\Delta^{r}_{{\theta}\varphi}f(t)=0
if
t-r{\theta}\varphi(t)/2\notin[0,1]
and
1-t+r{\theta}\varphi(t)/2\notin[0,1]
.
This modulus of smoothness provides a characterization for
E_{n}(f)_{p}
, which is
E_{n}(f)_{p,\varpi}
with
\varpi(t)=1
.
More precisely, it gives the direct theorem
[
7
, Theorem 7.2.1]

(2.2)

E_{n}(f)_{p}\leq c_{p}\omega_{\varphi}^{r}\left(f;n^{-1}\right),\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty

and a matching standard weak inverse theorem
[
7
, Theorem 7.2.4]

(2.3)

\omega_{\varphi}^{r}\left(f;n^{-1}\right)\leq cn^{-r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{r-1}E_{k}(f)_{p}.

For our study, we shall need the weighted modulus of smoothness for the Jacobi weight function,

\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(t)=t^{\alpha}(1-t)^{\beta},\qquad{\alpha},{\beta}>-1,

definded on the inverval
[0,1]
.
Then, the main-part modulus of smoothness for the Jacobi weight is defined by
[
7
, (8.1.2)]

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\omega}^{r}_{\varphi}(f;t)_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}:=\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq t}\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}f\right\|_{L^{p}(J_{rt},\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}})}

where the norm is taken over the interval defined by

J_{rt}:=[12r^{2}t^{2},1-12r^{2}t^{2}].

The direct and inverse theorems were established using the main-part moduli in

[
7
, Theorem 8.2.1]
. What is interesting for our study is the weighted
K
-functional defined by

(2.5)

\displaystyle K^{r}_{\varphi}(f;t)_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}:=\inf_{g\in C^{r}[0,1]}\left\{\|f-g\|_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}+t^{r}\left\|\varphi^{r}g^{(r)}\right\|_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}\right\},

which is equivalent to the main-part modulus of smoothness. More precisely,

(2.6)

c_{1}{\omega}^{r}_{\varphi}(f;t)_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}\leq K^{r}_{\varphi}(f;t)_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}\leq c_{2}{\omega}^{r}_{\varphi}(f;t)_{p,\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}}

follows from
[
7
, Theorem 6.1.1]
since the weight function and
\varphi
adhere to the conditions stipulated
in
[
7
, (6.1.2) and Section 1.2]
. For the unweighted case of
{\alpha}={\beta}=0
, the equivalence is in

[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
.

We will also need the Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
, given explicitly by

P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)=\frac{({\alpha}+1)_{n}}{n!}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n+{\alpha}+{\beta}+1\\
{\alpha}+1\end{matrix};\frac{1-t}{2}\right)

via the hypergeometric function, and they are orthogonal with respect to the weight function

w_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(t)=(1-t)^{\alpha}(1+t)^{\beta}=2^{{\alpha}+{\beta}}\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(\frac{1+t}{2})
,

c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}^{\prime}\int_{-1}^{1}P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)P_{m}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t)w_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(t)\mathrm{d}t=h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}\delta_{n,m},

where
c^{\prime}_{{\alpha},{\beta}}=2^{-{\alpha}-{\beta}-1}c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}
with

(2.7)

\displaystyle c_{{\alpha},{\beta}}:=\frac{\Gamma({\alpha}+{\beta}+2)}{\Gamma({\alpha}+1)\Gamma({\beta}+1)}\quad\hbox{and}\quad h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}:=\frac{({\alpha}+1)_{n}({\beta}+1)_{n}({\alpha}+{\beta}+n+1)}{n!({\alpha}+{\beta}+2)_{n}({\alpha}+{\beta}+2n+1)}.

For later use, we define

(2.8)

Z_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(t):=\frac{P_{n}^{(a,{\beta})}(t)P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(1)}{h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}},\quad{\alpha},{\beta}>-1,

where
h_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
is the
L^{2}
norm of
P_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
in
L^{2}([-1,1],w_{{\alpha},{\beta}})
.

One of the important tools for our study is the highly localized kernel, defined as a resample of the reproducing kernel.
Let
\widehat{a}\in C^{\infty}
be a cut-off function that satisfies

(2.9)

\widehat{a}(t)\geq 0,\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}_{+},\qquad\widehat{a}(t)=1,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1,\qquad\widehat{a}(t)=0,\quad t\geq 2.

Then the highly localized kernel
L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}
is defined by, for
{\alpha},{\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
,

(2.10)

L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(s,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{P_{k}^{(a,{\beta})}(t)P_{k}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(s)}{h_{k}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}}.

It is highly localized in the sense that for every
{\kappa}>0
, there is a constant
c_{\kappa}
such that

(2.11)

\left|L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(s,t)\right|\leq c_{\kappa}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;t)}(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{[-1,1]}(s,t))^{\kappa}},

where the function
\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;t)
is defined by

\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;t)=(1-t+n^{-2})^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}(1+t+n^{-2})^{{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad-1<t<1,

and
{\mathsf{d}}_{[-1,1]}(s,t)=\arccos(st+\sqrt{1-s^{2}}\sqrt{1-t^{2}})
is the distance of
[-1,1]
; see
[
12
, Theorem 2.4]
.
For our study, the following lemma will play an important role.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
{\alpha},{\beta}\geq-\frac{1}{2}
and
{\gamma},\delta\geq 0
,

\int_{-1}^{1}|L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(s,t)|\varpi_{{\alpha}+{\gamma},{\beta}+\delta}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq c\varpi_{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2},\delta-\frac{1}{2}}(n;s).

Proof.

For
{\gamma}=\delta=0
, this is the case
p=1
of
[
12
, Proposition 2.5]
. We modify the proof therein for the
general case. We divided the integral to be estimated as a sum of two integrals to decouple the two endpoints.
By symmetry, we only need to consider one of them, which we assume to be the integral over
[-\frac{1}{2},1]
, for
which we can assume
s\in[0,1]
correspondingly.
Changing variables
t=\cos{\theta}
,
s=\cos\phi
, we obtain
{\mathsf{d}}_{[-1,1]}(s,t)=|{\theta}-\phi|
and, moreover, by

\sin{\theta}\sim{\theta}
for
0\leq{\theta}\leq 2\pi/3
,
\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;t)\sim(\sin^{2}\frac{{\theta}}{2}+n^{-2})^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}\sim({\theta}+n^{-1})^{2{\alpha}+1}
.
Hence, by (
2.11
),

\displaystyle J:=

\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{1}|L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(s,t)|\varpi_{{\alpha}+{\gamma},{\beta}+\delta}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{n}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\frac{(\sin{\theta})^{2{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+1}}{(\sin{\theta}+n^{-1})^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}(1+n|{\theta}-\phi|)^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{n}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{3}}\frac{{\theta}^{2{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+1}}{({\theta}+n^{-1})^{{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}}(1+n|{\theta}-\phi|)^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}{\theta},

where the last inequality follows from
\sin{\theta}\sim{\theta}
for
0\leq{\theta}\leq 2\pi/3
. Changing variable
{\theta}\mapsto n{\theta}=u
, it follows

\displaystyle J\,

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{n^{{\alpha}+\frac{3}{2}}}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)n^{2{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{3}n}\frac{u^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}}{(1+|u-n\phi|)^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=c\frac{1}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)n^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}}\int_{-n\phi}^{n(\frac{2\pi}{3}-{\theta})}\frac{(v+n\phi)^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+1}}{(1+|v|)^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}v.

Hence, using
(A+B)^{q}\leq 2^{q}(A^{q}+B^{q})
, we conclude that

\displaystyle J\,

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{1}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)n^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}}\left[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|v|)^{{\kappa}-{\alpha}-2{\gamma}-1}}\mathrm{d}v+(n\phi)^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|v|)^{\kappa}}\mathrm{d}v\right]

\displaystyle\leq c\frac{1}{\varpi_{{\alpha},{\beta}}(n;s)}(\phi+n^{-1})^{{\alpha}+2{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}\leq c(\sin\phi+n^{-1})^{2{\gamma}}\leq c\,\varpi_{{\gamma}-\frac{1}{2},\delta-\frac{1}{2}}(n;s).

This completes the proof.
∎

2.2.
Approximation on the sphere

Let
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\|\xi\|=1\}
denote the unit sphere of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. For
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
or
f\in C({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
,
p=\infty
, we consider the best approximation defined by

{\mathsf{E}}_{n}(f)_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}=\inf_{Y\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}\|f-Y\|_{p},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

where
\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
denote the space of polynomials restricted over
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
. Spherical harmonics are
homogeneous harmonic polynomials restricted to the unit sphere. Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}
denote the space of
spherical harmonics of degree
n
. Spherical harmonics of different degrees are orthogonal; more precisely,
if
Y_{n}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}Y_{n}(\xi)Y_{m}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)=0,\quad n\neq m,

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma
denotes the surface measure. The space
\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
can be decomposed as a direct sum of

{\mathcal{H}}_{k}^{d}
for
0\leq k\leq n
. It is known that

\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}=\binom{n+d-1}{n}-\binom{n+d-3}{n-2}.

A fundamental property states that the spherical harmonics are eigenfunctions of the Laplace-Beltrami
operator
\Delta_{0}

[
4
, (1.4.9)]
,

(2.12)

\Delta_{0}Y=-n(n+d-2)Y,\qquad Y\in{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d},

where
\Delta_{0}
is the restriction of
\Delta
on the unit sphere. Another important property is that
they satisfy an addition formula
[
4
, (1.2.3) and (1.2.7)]
: for
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
,

(2.13)

\sum_{\ell=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}}Y_{\ell}^{n}(\xi)Y_{\ell}^{n}(\eta)=Z_{n}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}({\langle}\xi,\eta{\rangle}),\qquad Z_{n}^{\lambda}(t)=\frac{n+{\lambda}}{{\lambda}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(t),

where
C_{n}^{\lambda}
is the classical Gegenbauer polynomial, which corresponds to the Jacobi polynomial
with
{\alpha}={\beta}={\lambda}-\frac{1}{2}
and, in particular,
Z_{n}^{\lambda}(t)=Z_{n}^{({\lambda}-\frac{1}{2},{\lambda}-\frac{1}{2})}(t)
in (
2.8
).

The left-hand side of (
2.13
) is the reproducing kernel of
{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}
and the kernel of
the projection operator
\operatorname{proj}_{n}:L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})\to{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}
:

\operatorname{proj}_{n}f(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}f(y){\mathsf{P}}_{n}(\xi,\eta)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{\mathbb{S}}(\eta),\qquad{\mathsf{P}}_{n}(\xi,\eta)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}}Y_{\ell}^{n}(\xi)Y_{\ell}^{n}(\eta),

where
\sigma_{d}
is the surface area of
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
,
\sigma_{d}=2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)
.
The Fourier orthogonal series for
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})
is given by

L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{H}}_{n}^{d}\,:\qquad f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{proj}_{n}f.

The first modulus of smoothness that characterizes
E_{n}(f)_{p}
on the sphere is defined via the spherical means
(
[
1
, p. 216]
and
[
10
, p. 475]
)

{\mathsf{S}}_{\theta}f(x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}(\sin\theta)^{d-2}}\int_{\langle x,y\rangle=\cos\theta}f(y)\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x,\theta}(y),

where
\mathrm{d}\sigma_{x,\theta}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\{y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}:\langle x,y\rangle=\cos\theta\}
.
For
r>0
, the modulus of smoothness is defined by

(2.14)

{\omega}_{r}^{*}(f;t)_{p}:=\sup_{|{\theta}|\leq t}\|(I-S_{\theta})^{r/2}f\|_{p},

where
(I-S_{\theta})^{r/2}
is defied as an infinite series when
r/2
is not an integer. The
K
-functional equivalent to

{\omega}_{r}^{*}(f,t)_{p}
is defined by

(2.15)

{\mathsf{K}}_{r}^{*}(f;t)_{p}:=\inf_{g}\left\{\|f-g\|_{p}+t^{r}\|(-\Delta_{0})^{r/2}g\|_{p}\right\}.

The characterization of
{\mathsf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p}
and the equivalence of
{\omega}_{r}^{*}(f;t)_{p}
and
{\mathsf{K}}_{r}^{*}(f;t)_{p}
is established
in its final form in
[
11
]
, built upon early works of many authors (see, for example,
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
and
the references therein). The spherical means
{\mathsf{S}}_{\theta}f
is a multiplier operator of the Fourier series in
spherical harmonics as it satisfies

(2.16)

\operatorname{proj}_{n}\left({\mathsf{S}}_{\theta}f\right)=\frac{C_{n}^{{\lambda}}(t)}{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)}\operatorname{proj}_{n}f,\qquad{\lambda}=\frac{d-2}{2}.

This aspect of
{\mathsf{S}}_{\theta}
is extended and used to define a modulus of smoothness in the weighted setting
of the sphere with Dunkl’s reflection invariant weight function in
[
15
]
. The weighted modulus of smoothness,
however, can no longer be associated with simple properties of functions.

More recently, another modulus of smoothness on the unit sphere is defined in
[
3
]
in terms of the
Euler angles. In a nutshell, it is defined through forward differences in the largest circles of
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
intersecting
with
(x_{i},x_{j})
-plane for
1\leq i<j\leq d
. More precisely, let
Q_{i,j,{\theta}}
denote the rotation by the angle
{\theta}

in the
(x_{i},x_{j})
plane of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
so that the rotation angle from the coordinate vector
e_{i}
to
e_{j}
is assumed
to be positive. For
r=1,2,\ldots
, we define the difference operator

\Delta_{i,j,{\theta}}^{r}f=\left(I-T_{Q_{i,j,{\theta}}}\right)^{r}f,\quad 1\leq i\neq j\leq d,

where
T_{Q}
dentoes rotation operator
T_{Q}f(x)=f(Q^{-1}x)
. Since
Q_{i,j,{\theta}}
is a rotation that acts on the

(x_{i},x_{j})
-plane,
\Delta_{i,j,{\theta}}
is a forward difference in the
{\theta}_{i,j}
variable. For example, if
(i,j)=(1,2)

and
(x_{1},x_{2})=(t\cos\phi,t\sin\phi)
, then

(2.17)

\Delta_{i,j,{\theta}}^{r}f(x)=\overrightarrow{\Delta}_{\theta}^{r}f\big(t\cos(\phi+\cdot),t\sin(\phi+\cdot),x_{3},\ldots,x_{d}\big),

where
\overrightarrow{\Delta}_{\theta}^{r}
is actigng on the variable
(\cdot)
. For

Orthogonal structure on the conic surface

For

2.4.
Highly localized kernel on the conic surface

The framework in
[
17
]
is based on the existence of highly localized kernels, which will also play an essential
role in our study. For
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
on the conic surface, such kernels are defined by

(2.29)

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((x,t),(y,s)\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right){\mathsf{P}}_{k}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};(x,t),(y,s)),

where

is a cut-off function as defined in (

). Let

(2.30)

{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)=\big(t+n^{-2}\big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\big(1-t+n^{-2}\big)^{{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}}.

For any
{\kappa}>0
and

, the kernel
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
satisfies the estimate

(2.31)

\left|{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((x,t),(y,s)\right)\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{\gamma,d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{\gamma,d}(n;s)}}G_{n,d}^{\kappa}\big({\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s))\big),

where the function
G_{n,d}^{\kappa}
is defined, for future reference, by

(2.32)

G_{n,d}^{\kappa}(u)=\frac{n^{d}}{(1+nu)^{\kappa}},\qquad u\in{\mathbb{R}},

and
{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the distant function on

defined by
[

{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((x,t),(y,s)):=\arccos\left(\sqrt{\frac{{\langle}x,y{\rangle}+ts}{2}}+\sqrt{1-t}\sqrt{1-s}\right).

Per this estimate, the kernel decays away from the diagonal faster than any polynomial rate, which is why it is called
highly localized. Let us define an integral operator via the highly localized kernel,

(2.34)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(x,t):={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left({\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}};\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)\right){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s).

Then
{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f
is a polynomial of near-best approximation since it satisfies the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
f\in L^{p}\big({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}\big)
for
1\leq p<\infty
or
f\in C\big({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}\big)
for
p=\infty
. Then

(i)

{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f\in\Pi_{2n}\big({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}\big)
and
{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f=f
for
f\in\Pi_{n}\big({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}\big)
.

(ii)

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
\|{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\|f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
.

(iii)

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(2.35)

\|f-{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\mathsf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

The proof of this theorem holds for analogous operators on spaces of homogeneous type
[
17
, Theorem 3.14]
.
We will need new properties of the highly localized kernels, stated in several lemmas below.

The first one shows that the estimate for the highly localized kernel inherits the semi-product structure of the
conic surface. Recall that the distance on
[0,1]
is defined by

{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)=\arccos\left(\sqrt{s}\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{1-s}\sqrt{1-t}\right),\qquad s,t\in[0,1].

Lemma 2.3
.

For
{\kappa}_{1},{\kappa}_{2}>0
and
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, where
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
with
\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
,

\left|{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((t\xi,t),(s\eta,s)\big)\right|\leq\frac{c_{\kappa}}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}}G_{n,1}^{{\kappa}_{1}}\!\left({\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)\right)G_{n,d-1}^{{\kappa}_{2}}\!\left(\sqrt{s}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\right).

Proof.

It is shown in
[
17
, Proposition 4.3]
that

(2.36)

{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((t\xi,t),(s\eta,s)\big)\sim{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)+(st)^{\frac{1}{4}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta).

If
t\geq s
, then
(st)^{\frac{1}{4}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\geq s^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)
. If
t\leq s
, then using the inequality

\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{t}\leq{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)
, we obtain

s^{\frac{1}{4}}-t^{\frac{1}{4}}\leq\frac{1}{s^{\frac{1}{4}}+t^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t),

which implies

\displaystyle{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)+(st)^{\frac{1}{4}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\,

\displaystyle\geq{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)+\left(s^{\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{s^{\frac{1}{4}}}{s^{\frac{1}{4}}+t^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)\right){\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle\geq s^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta).

Consequently, by (
2.36
), we conclude that

{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)\geq{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}((s\xi,s),(s\eta,s))\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)\geq{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t).

Hence, it follows from the definition of
G_{n,d}^{\kappa}
that

G_{n,d}^{{\kappa}_{1}+{\kappa}_{2}}\left({\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)\right)\leq c\,G_{n,1}^{{\kappa}_{1}}\left({\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)\right)G_{n,d-1}^{{\kappa}_{2}}\left(\sqrt{s}{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{S}}}(\xi,\eta)\right)

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

For
{\kappa}>0
, there is a constant

such that

(2.37)

\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\frac{G_{n,1}^{{\kappa}_{1}}\left({\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t)\right)}{\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;t)}\sqrt{{\mathsf{w}}_{{\gamma},d}(n;s)}}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq c_{\kappa}(s+n^{-2})^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

In terms of the Jacobi weight

on

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varpi_{\frac{d-3}{2},{\gamma}}(n;t)}\sqrt{\varpi_{\frac{d-3}{2},{\gamma}}(n;s)}}

\displaystyle\frac{n}{(1+n{\mathsf{d}}_{[0,1]}(s,t))^{{\kappa}_{1}}}\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq c\,(s+n^{-2})^{\frac{d-1}{2}},

by the proof of Lemma
2.1
with

and
\delta=0
, where we
have mapped
[-1,1]

[0,1]
.
∎

Let
L_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the highly localized kernel for the Jacobi weight defined in (

).
We define the operator
f\mapsto L_{n}^{({\beta},{\gamma})}f

L_{n}^{({\beta},{\gamma})}f(t)=c_{{\beta},{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}f(s)\widetilde{L}_{n}^{({\beta},{\gamma})}(s,t)\varpi_{{\beta},{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

Lemma 2.5
.

For
{\gamma}\geq 0
and

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(s\eta,s)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta)=L_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}f(s).

Proof.

By (

) and the orthogonality of the spherical harmonics

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((s\eta,s),(t\xi,t)\big)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{k}{n}\right)\frac{P_{k}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}(1-2t)P_{k}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}(1-2s)}{h_{k}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}}

\displaystyle=L_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}(1-2s,1-2t)=\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}(s,t),

from which the desired identity follows from (

).
∎

The next lemma is about an analog of the operator
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
, which we define as

(2.38)

{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t):={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(s\xi,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((t\xi,t),(s\eta,s)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(s\eta,s),

in which the function

Lemma 2.6
.

For

{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}{\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}f={\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f={\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}f
.

Proof.

Using (

), we obtain, by the proof of Lemma

{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)=c_{d-2,{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}f(s\xi,s)\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(\mathrm{d}-2,{\gamma})}(s,t){\mathsf{w}}_{d-2,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

where we have used

. Let

.
Then, the above identity shows immediately that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}{\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}f(z,u)=c_{d-2,{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}L_{m}^{\gamma}f(s\zeta,s)\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(\mathrm{d}-2,{\gamma})}(s,u){\mathsf{w}}_{d-2,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s={\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}f(z,u),

since

is a polynomial of degree
m\leq n
and
\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(\mathrm{d}-2,{\gamma})}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the
kernel of the operator that reproduces polynomials of degree
n
for
{\mathsf{w}}_{d-2,{\gamma}}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(z,u)\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}L_{m}^{\gamma}\big((z,u),(t\xi,t)\big){\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(t\xi,t)

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}c_{d-2,{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(\mathrm{d}-2,{\gamma})}(s,t)L_{m}^{\gamma}\big((z,u),(t\xi,t)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{d-2,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times f(s\xi,s)\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)\,s^{d-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s.

The intergral over

since

is a
polynomial of degree

in the variable

{\mathsf{L}}_{m}^{\gamma}{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(z,u)={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}f(s\xi,s)L_{m}^{\gamma}\big((z,u),(s\xi,s)\big)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)\,s^{d-1}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s,

which is exactly

.
∎

We state and discuss the main result in the first subsection without getting into technical details.
The properties of the modulus of smoothness are established in the second subsection. The proof of the
main theorem is given in the third to fifth subsections.

3.1.
Main result

Throughout this section, we denote the norm of
L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
,
{\gamma}\geq 0
, by

\|f\|_{p,{\gamma}}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})},\quad 1\leq p<\infty,

and adopt the usual convention that
p=\infty
is the uniform norm on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
.

Our new modulus of smoothness on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
is a fusion of the modulus of smoothness (
2.18
)
on the unit sphere and the Ditzian-Totik modulus of smoothness on the interval
[0,1]
. We define

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{i,j,{\theta}}^{r}f\right\|_{p,{\gamma}}^{p}:=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\left|\Delta_{i,j,{\theta}}^{r}f\right|^{p}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t).

Setting
x=t\xi
,
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, we will need to consider
\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\|_{p,{\gamma}}

below.

Definition 3.1
.

For
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
t\in(0,1]
, and
r>0
, the modulus of smoothness is defined by

2
2

2

Strictly speaking, we should replace
\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}
by
{\theta}\psi
with
\psi(x,t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
. We kept the current
notation since it is more transparent.

\displaystyle{\omega}_{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}:=\,

\displaystyle\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq h}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}+\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq h}\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{rh},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}

where
f_{\xi}(t)=f(t\xi,t)
and
{\mathbb{V}}_{rh}:=\left\{(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}:t\in J_{rh}\right\}.

For
r=1
, we write
{\omega}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}={\omega}_{1}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

Since
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}
is the forward difference acting on the angle
{\theta}_{i,j}
of the polar coordinates of

(x_{i},x_{j})
plane, see (
2.17
) for example, it is well-defined and so is this modulus of smoothness for
all
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We also define the
K
-functional using the derivatives for the Euler angles.

Definition 3.2
.

For
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and
r>0
, the
K
-functional is defined by

\displaystyle{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f,h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}:=\inf\bigg\{\|f-g\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle+h^{r}\left\|\varphi^{r}\partial^{r}_{t}g\right\|_{p,{\gamma}}+h^{r}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}g\right\|_{p,{\gamma}}\bigg\},

where the infimum is taken over all
g\in C^{r}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
.

For
r=1,2
, the
K
-functional is well defined for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, since
x=t\xi
shows that

D_{i,j}^{r}=(x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i})^{r}
contains a factor
t
, which dominates
\sqrt{t}^{r}
in the
denominator to warrant the boundedness of
\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}g\|_{p,{\gamma}}
. For
r>2
, the
similar argument, with
D_{i,j}^{r}
contains a factor
t
, shows that the
K
-functional is bounded if

1\leq p<\frac{2(d-1)}{r-2}.

Moreover, this inequality is sharp, as observed in
[
18
]
, since if
g_{0}(x,t)=x_{i}
, then

D_{i,j}^{2\ell}g_{0}(x,t)=(-1)^{\ell}x_{i}

and
D_{i,j}^{2\ell+1}g_{0}(x,t)=(-1)^{\ell}x_{j}
, so that

\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}g_{0}\right\|_{p,{\gamma}}^{p}\sim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{t^{p+d-2}}{\sqrt{t}^{rp}}(1-t)^{\gamma}\mathrm{d}t\sim\int_{0}^{1}t^{p+d-2-rp/2}\mathrm{d}t,

which is finite for
p\geq 1
if
r=1,2
but only for
1\leq p<\frac{2(d-1)}{r-2}
if
r>2
.

Our main result is a characterization for the quantity
{\mathsf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
by the new
modulus of smoothness or by the
K
-functional, which are equivalent for
r=1,2
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
{\gamma}\geq 0
and let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
if
1\leq p<\infty
and

f\in C({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
if
p=\infty
. Then for
n=1,2,\ldots
, there hold

(i)

direct theorem:

{\mathsf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}};

(ii)

inverse theorem: for
r=1,2

{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,n^{-r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{r-1}{\mathsf{E}}_{k}(f)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

(iii)

equivalence: for
r=1,2

c_{1}{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c_{2}{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

The proof will be carried out in the order of (i), (iii), (ii), since the proof of the inverse theorem is much
easier to carry out in terms of the
K
-functional.

Remark 3.1
.

We do not know if the inverse theorem, as stated above in terms of the modulus of smoothness, holds
for
r>2
. The inverse theorem requires a proof that bypasses the
K
-functional and works directly
on the modulus of smoothness.

3.2.
Properties of modulus of smoothness

We show that the modulus of smoothness satisfies the usual properties, including the Marchaud-type inequality.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in C({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
p=\infty
. The modulus of
smoothness
{\omega}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For
0<h<h_{0}
,
{\omega}_{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\|f\|_{p,{\gamma}}
.

(ii)

For
{\lambda}>0
,
{\omega}_{r}(f;\lambda h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq({\lambda}+1)^{r}{\omega}_{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
.

(iii)

(Marchaud inequality) For
0<h<\frac{1}{2}
,

{\omega}_{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq ch^{r}\int_{h}^{1}\frac{{\omega}_{r+1}(f;u)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}}{u^{r+1}}\mathrm{d}u.

Since the conic surface is a semi-product of the sphere with radius
t
and the interval
[0,1]
for
t
,
the modulus of smoothness
{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
is a fusion of the two moduli of smoothness,
one on the sphere and the other on the interval. We split
{\omega}_{r}(f;\delta)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
into two distinct
parts accordingly,

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\omega}_{r}(f;h)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}=\omega_{A}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}+\omega^{r}_{B}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}},

where, setting
f_{\xi}(t)=f(t\xi,t)
,

\displaystyle\omega^{r}_{A}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq h}\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{rh},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}

\displaystyle\omega_{B}^{r}(f;\delta)_{p,{\gamma}}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{|{\theta}|\leq\delta}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

The proof of Proposition
3.4
follows from the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in C({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
p=\infty
. The modulus of
smoothness
{\omega}^{r}_{A}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For
0<h<h_{0}
,
{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq c\|f\|_{p,{\gamma}}
.

(ii)

For
{\lambda}>0
,
{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f;\lambda h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq({\lambda}+1)^{r}{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
.

(iii)

(Marchaud inequality) For
0<h<\frac{1}{2}
,

{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq ch^{r}\int_{h}^{1}\frac{{\omega}_{A}^{r+1}(f;u)_{p,{\gamma}}}{u^{r+1}}\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

The modulus of smoothness
\omega_{A}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
is defined via the integral

\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{rh},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}\left[\int_{J_{rh}}t^{d-1}|\Delta^{r}_{\theta\varphi}f(t\xi,t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right]\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi).

Using
f_{\xi}(t)=f(t\xi,t)
for
t\in J_{rh}
, for (i), we apply the main-part estimate on the interval
[0,1]

as stated in
[
7
, p. 58]
,

\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\|_{L^{p}(J_{rh},\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}^{p}\leq c\,\|f_{\xi}\|_{L^{p}((0,1),\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}^{p},

and integrate both sides over
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
to obtain the desired inequality in part (i).
An analogous argument works for (ii), based on the result for the main-part moduli established in
[
7
, (6.2.8)]
,
which gives

\sup_{|\theta|\leq\lambda h}\Big\|\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\Big\|_{L^{p}(J_{rh},\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}\leq(\lambda+1)^{r}\sup_{|\theta|\leq h}\Big\|\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f_{\xi}\Big\|_{L^{p}(J_{rh},\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}.

Part (iii) is derived from the established Marchaud inequality for weighted main-part moduli given in

[
7
, Theorem 6.4.2]
, which yields

{\omega}_{\varphi}^{r}(f_{\xi};h)_{p,\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}}\leq ch^{r}\left(\int_{h}^{1}\frac{{\omega}_{\varphi}^{r+1}(f_{\xi};u)_{p,\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}}}{u^{r+1}}\mathrm{d}u+\|f_{\xi}\|_{L^{p}((0,1),\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}\right).

The term
\|f_{\xi}\|_{L^{p}((0,1),\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}})}
is redundant and can be removed using the Jackson
inequality
[
7
, (8.2.1)]
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in C({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}_{0})
for
p=\infty
. The modulus of
smoothness
{\omega}^{r}_{B}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

For
0<h<\delta_{0}
,
{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq c\|f\|_{p,{\gamma}}
.

(ii)

For
{\lambda}>0
,
{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f;\lambda h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq({\lambda}+1)^{r}{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
.

(iii)

(Marchaud inequality) For
0<h<\frac{1}{2}
,

{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq ch^{r}\int_{h}^{1}\frac{{\omega}_{B}^{r+1}(f;u)_{p,{\gamma}}}{u^{r+1}}\mathrm{d}u.

Proof.

The modulus of smoothness
\omega_{B}^{r}(f;h)_{p,{\gamma}}
is defined via the integral

\Big\|\Delta^{r}_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}f\Big\|^{p}_{p,{\gamma}}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\Big[\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}|\Delta^{r}_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}f(t\xi,t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)\Big]{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

For
0<t<1
, we denote by
f_{t}
the function defined by
f_{t}(\xi):=f(t\xi,t)
,
\xi\in{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
.

For (i), we utilize the inequality on the unit sphere
[
4
, Lemma 4.2 (ii)]
for
f_{t}
,

(3.2)

\Big\|\Delta^{r}_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}f_{t}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}\leq 2^{r}\|f_{t}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})},

and integrate it one more time in
t
. The same argument works for (ii) by using
[
4
, Lemma 4.2 (i)]
, which gives

\sup_{|\theta|\leq\lambda h}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f_{t}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}\leq(\lambda+1)^{r}\sup_{|\theta|\leq h}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f_{t}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}.

For (iii), we derive it from the corresponding result for trigonometric functions. To illustrate this, let us
consider the case
(i,j)=(1,2)
. By utilizing the identity

\Big\|\Delta^{r}_{1,2,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}f_{t}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{d-1}})}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}z(1-z)^{\frac{d-4}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-3}}\left[\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|\overrightarrow{\Delta}^{r}_{\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}g_{z,y}(\phi)\Big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\phi\right]\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)\,\mathrm{d}z,

where
g_{z,y}(\phi)=f_{t}(z\cos\phi,z\sin\phi,\sqrt{1-z^{2}}y)
,
y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-3}
,
z\in[0,1]
, and
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
,
The result of (iii) follows from the Marchaud inequality for trigonometric functions and a change of variables.
∎

The next two lemmas discuss the commutativity of the near-best approximation operator
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f

in (
2.29
) with the difference operators
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}
and
\Delta_{\theta\varphi}
,
which will be needed in the proof of the direct theorem.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\Big(\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f\Big).

In particular, for
{\theta}>0
,

{\omega}_{A}^{r}\left(f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f,{\theta}\right)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f,{\theta})_{p,{\gamma}}.

Proof.

By the definitions of
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
and the central difference,

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(s\eta,s)\big[{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((s\eta,s),\big((t+\tfrac{h}{2})\xi,(t+\tfrac{h}{2})\big)\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((s\eta,s),\big((t-\tfrac{h}{2})\xi,(t-\tfrac{h}{2})\big)\right)\big]\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left(f\big((t+\tfrac{h}{2})\xi,t+\tfrac{h}{2}\big)-f\big((t-\tfrac{h}{2})\xi,t-\tfrac{h}{2}\big)\right)={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta_{h}f(t,\xi),

which holds with
h={\theta}\varphi(t)
as well and iteration gives the commutivity for
r>1
. Moreover,
it follows by (ii) of Theorem
2.2
,

\left\|\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}(f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f)\right\|_{p,\gamma}=\left\|\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f\right\|_{p,\gamma}\leq(1+c)\left\|\Delta_{\theta\varphi}^{r}f\right\|_{p,\gamma},

from which the stated inequality follows.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq d
, and
t\in(0,1]
,

\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\Big(\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big).

In particular, for
{\theta}>0
,

{\omega}_{B}^{r}\left(f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f,{\theta}\right)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f,{\theta})_{p,{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Let
T_{Q}f(x):=f(Qx)
for
Q\in SO(d)
, where
SO(d)
denotes the special orthogonal group. By the definition of

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
, we have

\displaystyle T_{Q}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(s\eta,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((s\eta,s),(tQ\xi,t)\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(s\eta,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((sQ^{-1}\eta,s),(t\xi,t)\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}f(sQ\eta,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\left((s\eta,s),(t\xi,t)\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma(\eta){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,\gamma}(s)\mathrm{d}s={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}(T_{Q}f)(t,\xi)

by the rotation invariance of
\mathrm{d}\sigma(y)
, which gives, by
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}=\Big(I-T_{Q_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}}\Big)^{r}
, the stated result. Thus,

\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}(f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f)\Big\|_{p,\gamma}=\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,\gamma}\leq(1+c)\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,\gamma},

from which the stated inequality follows.
∎

Combining the last two lemmas leads to, by (

), the following corollary.

Corollary 3.9
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
{\theta}>0
,

{\omega}_{r}\left(f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f,{\theta}\right)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}(f,{\theta})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

3.3.
Proof of the direct theorem

Let
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
be the near-best approximation operator defined in (
2.34
). Since
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f

is a polynomial of degree
2n
, the direct theorem follows if we can establish the inequality

\|f-L_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

To make use of the semi-product structure over the conic surface, we split
f-L_{n}^{\gamma}f
into two parts.
Since the kernel
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}((x,t),\cdot)
has the unit integral over
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
, we can write

\displaystyle f(x,t)-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{r}f(x,t)={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\big[f(x,t)-f(y,s)\big]{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((y,s),(x,t)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s).

With
x=t\xi
and
y=s\eta
, we write the function
f(x,t)-f(y,s)
as a sum

f(x,t)-f(y,s)=\left[f(t\xi,t)-f(s\xi,s)\right]+\left[f(s\xi,s)-f(s\eta,s)\right],

and split
f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{r}f
accordingly as

(3.3)

f(t\xi,t)-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{r}f(t\xi,t)={\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)+{\mathsf{F}}_{n,2}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi),

where the two terms on the right-hand side are defined via
{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}
in (
2.38
) by

(3.4)

{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f:=f-{\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathsf{F}}_{n,2}^{\gamma}f:={\mathsf{G}}_{n}^{\gamma}f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f.

More explicitly, the two functions are given by

\displaystyle{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\big[f(t\xi,t)-f(s\xi,s)\big]{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((s\eta,s),(t\xi,t)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s),

\displaystyle{\mathsf{F}}_{n,2}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)\,

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}}\big[f(s\xi,s)-f(s\eta,s)\big]{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((s\eta,s),(t\xi,t)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s).

While
{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}
contains the difference of
f(t\xi,t)
, for each fixed
\xi
, in
t
variable,
{\mathsf{F}}_{n,2}^{\gamma}
contains the
difference of
f(s\xi,s)
, for each fixed
s
, in
\xi
variable. We deal with the two parts separately.

By (
3.3
) and (
3.1
), the proof of the direct estimate (i) in Theorem
3.3

amounts to bounding the norm of
{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f
by the modulus
{\omega}_{A}^{r}(f,n^{-1})_{p,{\gamma}}
and
the norm of
{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f
by the moduolus
{\omega}_{B}^{r}(f,n^{-1})_{p,{\gamma}}
, respectively. The proof is
fairly long and is the content of the rest of this subsection.

We start with the estimate for
{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}
, which is relatively easy.

Proposition 3.10
.

For
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle\|{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,\omega^{r}_{A}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\gamma}}

Proof.

Parametrize the integral over
\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s)
by
y=s\eta
and (
2.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)=c_{d-2,{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{1}\big[f(t\xi,t)-f(s\xi,s)\big]\widetilde{L}_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}(s,t)\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}s

by Lemma
2.5
. Let
f_{\xi}(t)=f(t\xi,t)
. It then follows that

{\mathsf{F}}_{n,1}^{\gamma}f(t,\xi)=f_{\xi}(t)-L_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}f_{\xi}(t).

Since
L_{n}^{(d-2,{\gamma})}f
is the near-best approximation for
\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}
on
[0,1]
, applying the direct theorem
(
2.2
) and (
2.6
) for the weight best approximation on
[0,1]
, we conclude

\displaystyle{\omega}_{\varphi}^{*r}(f;v)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}}=

Proof of (i) Theorem

For

.
We first consider the lower estimate
{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
, where
r=1,2
.
Let
g_{\rho}\in C^{r}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
be chosen to satisfy

\displaystyle\|f-g_{\rho}\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}};

\displaystyle\rho^{r}\max_{1\leq i\leq j\leq d}\|D_{i,j}^{r}g_{\rho}\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}};

\displaystyle\rho^{r}\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}g_{\rho}\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

By the triangle inequality, we obtain

{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq{\omega}_{r}(f-g_{\rho};\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}+{\omega}_{r}(g_{\rho};\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}},

so that it is sufficient to establish the inequalities

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\omega}_{r}(f-g_{\rho};\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\omega}_{r}(g_{\rho};\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

To establish these inequalities, it is essential to demonstrate that for
g\in C^{r}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
, the following hold:

\displaystyle\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}(f-g)\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|f-g\|_{p,{\gamma}};\qquad\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}\leq c{\theta}^{r}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}g\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}};

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}(f-g)\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|f-g\|_{p,{\gamma}};\qquad\left\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g\right\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}})}\leq c{\theta}^{r}\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}g\|_{p,{\gamma}};

For the terms involving
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}(f-g)
and
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g
, we make use of the identity:

\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g\Big\|_{p,\gamma}^{p}={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}_{t}^{d-1}}\big|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g(t\xi)\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)\,{\mathsf{w}}_{d-1,\gamma}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

The first estimate for
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}(f-g)
follows directly from (
3.2
).
To estimate the term
\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g
, it suffices to establish the following intermediate inequality.
Let
g_{t}(\xi)=g(t\xi,t)
for
0<t<1
. Then

\left\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}g_{t}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})}\leq c\,\theta^{r}\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}g_{t}(\xi)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{S}^{d-1})},

which has been established in
[
3
, Lemma 2.6 (ii)]
upon replacing
\theta
with
\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}
.

The proof of inequalities about
\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g
,
\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}(f-g)

reduces to the corresponding inequalities in one variable, which are given in
[
7
, p. 58]
. Indeed,

\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}(f-g)\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}})}
and
\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}})}

correspond to the main part of the Ditzian–Totik modulus of smoothness. The first term can be treated similarly to the proof of part (i) in Lemma
3.5
. For the second term, by setting

g_{\xi}(t):=g(t\xi,t)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{rp},{\mathsf{w}})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\int_{J_{r\rho}}|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g_{\xi}(t)|^{p}\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\theta}^{rp}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\int_{0}^{1}|\varphi^{r}(t)\partial^{r}_{t}g_{\xi}(t)|^{p}\varpi_{d-2,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi)=c\,{\theta}^{rp}\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}g\|^{p}_{p,{\gamma}},

where the inequality follows from the main-part estimate on the interval
[0,1]
stated in
[
7
, p. 58]
. Thus, both inequalities
in (
3.6
) hold, which completes the proof of the inequality

{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
.

Next we prove the reversed inequality
K_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
and we
follow the approach of the proof in
[
3
, Theorem 6.10]
. Setting
n=\lfloor\frac{1}{\rho}\rfloor
and choose

g={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
, we obtain

(3.7)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq\|f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,{\gamma}}\,

\displaystyle+\rho^{r}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle+\rho^{r}\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,{\gamma}}.

The first term on the right-hand side is bounded by
c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
by the direct estimate in
Theorem
3.3
(i). For the second term,

\displaystyle\rho^{r}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\left[\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\rho^{r}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}|D_{i,j}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{|{\theta}|\leq\frac{1}{n\sqrt{t}}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}}|\Delta_{i,j,{{\theta}}}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\sigma(\xi){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}n\sqrt{t}\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n\sqrt{t}}}\|\Delta_{i,j,{{\theta}}}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}{\theta}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{d-1}n\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{n}}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,{\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(t\xi,t)\Big\|_{p}^{p}\mathrm{d}{\theta}{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq c{\omega}_{r}({\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}

where the first inequality follows from
[
3
, Lemma 2.6(iii)]
, the second inequality follows from
[
3
, Lemma 2.6(iv)]
, and the third inequality follows by replacing
\theta
with
\frac{\theta}{\sqrt{t}}
.
Thus, applying the triangle inequality and Theorem
3.3
(i), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho^{r}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\Big\|_{p,{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}({\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f-f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}+c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\|f-{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,{\gamma}}+c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

For the third term on the right-hand side of (
3.7
), we apply the relation below that holds for
g\in\Pi_{n}^{d}

\displaystyle n^{-rp}\left\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}g\right\|_{p,{\gamma}}^{p}\sim\,

\displaystyle n\int_{0}^{n^{-1}}\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}g\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}})}d{\theta}.

The relation follows from the corresponding result in one variable, where the equivalence follows from

[
7
, p. 57]
. Applying the relation with
g={\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f
, we obtain

n^{-r}\left\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\right\|_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}\left({\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f;n^{-1}\right)\leq c\,{\omega}_{r}(f;n^{-1})_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}.

Putting these estimates together completes the proof.

3.5.
Proof of inverse theorem

The proof of the inverse theorem is standard and follows from the Bernstein inequality. For the weight
L^{p}
space
with
{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}
weight on the conic surface, we need the following new Bernstein inequalities, for
f\in\Pi_{n}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1})
, and for
r=1,2,

(3.8)

\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}^{r}}D_{i,j}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,\gamma}\leq cn^{r}\ \|f\|_{p,\gamma}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\left\|\varphi^{r}\partial_{t}^{r}f\right\|_{p,\gamma}\leq cn^{r}\|f\|_{p,\gamma},

which are established in
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
. Using these inequalities, the standard proof gives the inverse
estimate for the
K
-functional,

{\mathsf{K}}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\gamma}}\leq cn^{-r}\sum_{k=0}^{n}(k+1)^{r-1}{\mathsf{E}}_{k}(f)_{p,{\gamma}},

which gives (iii) in Theorem
3.3
by the equivalence in (ii) of the same theorem.

4.
Best approximation on the solid cone

In this section, we study approximation on the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
. In the first subsection, we review
orthogonal polynomials and their associated kernels on the solid cone, and discuss their close relation
to the corresponding quantities on the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+1}
. In the second subsection, we define
a new pair of modulus of smoothness and
K
-functional for the solid cone, which are extrapolated from
results on the conic surface in the previous section. The third section contains the main result on
characterizing the best approximation over the solid cone, utilizing the newly introduced modulus of
smoothness and
K
-functional.

4.1.
Orthogonal polynomials and localized kernels

Let
\Pi_{n}^{d}
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most
n
in
d
variables. For
\mu>-\frac{1}{2}

and
{\gamma}>-1
, we define the weight function
W_{{\gamma},\mu}
by

(4.1)

W_{{\gamma},\mu}(x,t)=(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{\mu-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\gamma},\qquad\|x\|\leq t,\quad 0\leq t\leq 1.

Orthogonal polynomials with respect to
W_{{\gamma},\mu}
on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
are studied in
[
16
]
, which are
orthogonal with respect to the inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{W_{{\gamma},\mu}}={\mathsf{b}}_{{\gamma},\mu}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(x,t)g(x,t)W_{{\gamma},\mu}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
be the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n
. Then

\dim{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})=\binom{n+d+1}{n},\quad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

An orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
can be given in terms of the Jacobi polynomials
and the orthogonal polynomials on the unit ball. For
m=0,1,2,\ldots
, let
\{P_{\mathbf{k}}^{m}(W_{\mu}):|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,{\mathbf{k}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{B}}^{d},W_{\mu})
on the unit ball. Let

(4.2)

{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t):=P_{n-m}^{(2m+2\mu+d-1,{\gamma})}(1-2t)t^{m}P_{\mathbf{k}}^{m}\left(W_{\mu};\frac{x}{t}\right),\quad

Then
\{{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t):|{\mathbf{k}}|=m,\,0\leq m\leq n\}
is an orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
.
These polynomials are the eigenfunctions of a second-order differential operator
{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\gamma},\mu}
with the eigenvalues
depending only on the degree; see
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
.

The reproducing kernel of the space
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
, denoted by
{\mathbf{P}}_{n}(W_{{\gamma},\mu};\cdot,\cdot)
,
can be written in terms of the above basis,

{\mathbf{P}}_{n}\big(W_{{\gamma},\mu};(x,t),(y,s)\big)=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{|{\mathbf{k}}|=n}\frac{{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(x,t){\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}(y,s)}{{\langle}{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n},{\mathbf{J}}_{m,{\mathbf{k}}}^{n}{\rangle}_{W_{{\gamma},\mu}}}.

It is the kernel of the projection
\operatorname{proj}_{n}(W_{{\gamma},\mu}):L^{2}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})\to{\mathcal{V}}_{n}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{proj}_{n}(W_{{\gamma},\mu};f)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathbf{P}}_{n}\big(W_{{\gamma},\mu};\,\cdot,(y,s)\big)W_{{\gamma},\mu}(s)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

This kernel enjoys an addition formula that can be written as a triple integral over
[-1,1]^{3}
for
\mu>0
; see

[
16
, Theorem 4.3]
. Moreover, let
\widehat{a}
be an admissible cut-off function. For
(x,t),\,(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
,
the localized kernel
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}(W_{{\gamma},0};\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}(\big(x,t),(y,s)\big):=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\widehat{a}\left(\frac{j}{n}\right){\mathbf{P}}_{j}\left(W_{{\gamma},0};(x,t),(y,s)\right).

Like the kernel
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}
on the conic surface, the kernel
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}
is highly localized via the distance
function
{\mathsf{d}}_{{\mathbb{V}}}(\cdot,\cdot)
on the cone; see
[
17
, Theorem 5.9]
.

With these properties, the general framework in
[
17
]
applies to the solid cone. In particular, we have
an analog of the modulus of smoothness
\widehat{{\omega}}_{r}(f;t)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}
, defined as a multiplier operator,
and the
K
-functional
\widehat{K}_{r}(f;t)
, defined via the spectral operator
{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\gamma},\mu}
, on the solid cone,
which are equivalent and can be used to establish a characterization of the best approximation by polynomials,
see
[
17
, Section 5.7]
.

Our goal in this section is to extend the new modulus of smoothness and
K
-functional on the conic surfaces
to the solid cone, and use them to characterize the error of best approximation. Instead of replicating the
approach of the previous section, it is much easier to make use of a connection between the
L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\gamma})

on the solid cone and the space
L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})
, where we set

W_{\gamma}(x,t)=W_{{\gamma},0}(x,t)=(t^{2}-\|x\|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(1-t)^{\gamma},

so that
\mu=0
from now on.

Let
(x,t)
and
(y,s)
be the elements of the solid cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
. Let
x_{d+1}=\sqrt{t^{2}-\|x\|^{2}}
for
\|x\|\leq t

and
y_{d+1}=\sqrt{s^{2}-\|y\|^{2}}
for
\|y\|\leq s
. We shall adopt the notation

(4.4)

X:=(x,x_{d+1}),\quad Y:=(y,y_{d+1}),\quad Y_{*}:=(y,-y_{d+1})

throughout this section. It is evident that
\|X\|=t
,
\|Y\|=\|Y_{*}\|=s
, so that
(X,t)
,
(Y,s)
and
(Y_{*},s)

are elements of the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d+2}
.

We start with a relationship between the kernels on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
and
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
established in
[
18
, Proposition 4.3]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
be the kernel defined in (
2.29
) for
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
. Then

(4.5)

{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((x,t),(y,s)\big)={\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((X,t),(Y,s)\big)+{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((X,t),(Y_{*},s)\big).

Given a function
f
on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
, we consider the following extension of
f
,

(4.6)

\tilde{f}\big(X,t\big)=\tilde{f}\big((x,x_{d+1}),t\big):=f(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},\quad\big(X,t\big)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},

so that
f
can be regarded as a function on
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
. The connection between the cone
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}

and the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
is further manifested in the following integral relation

[
18
, Proposition 4.4]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
be a continuous function. Let
x_{d+1}=\sqrt{t^{2}-\|x\|^{2}}
for
\|x\|\leq t
.
Then, with
X=(x,x_{d+1})
and
X_{*}=(x,-x_{d+1})
,

\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}}f(y,s){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}\big[f\big(X,t\big)+f\big(X_{*},t\big)\big]W_{{\gamma}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

For any
(x,t),(y,s)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
, we define the operator
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f
by

(4.7)

{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f(x,t):={\mathbf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(y,s){\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((y,s),(x,t)\big)W_{{\gamma}}(y,s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(y,s),

which is a polynomial of near-best approximation. It satisfies
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\in\Pi_{2n}\big({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}\big)
and

{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f=f
for
f\in\Pi_{n}\big({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}\big)
, and

\|f-{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}f\|_{p,W_{{\gamma}}}\leq c{\mathbf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,W_{{\gamma}}},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

where
{\mathbf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,W_{{\gamma}}}
is the error of best approximation defined by

(4.8)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{E}}_{n}(f)_{p,W_{\gamma}}:=\inf_{g\in\Pi_{n}^{d+1}}\|f-g\|_{p,W_{\gamma}},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

The near-best operators on the cone and the conic surface are closely related. For
X=(x,x_{d+1})
and

(X,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
, let us denote by
{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\tilde{f}
the operator defined in (
2.34
) for

{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
. Specifically, it is given by

(4.9)

{\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\tilde{f}(X,t):={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}}\tilde{f}(Y,s){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((X,t),(Y,s)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(Y,s).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\tilde{f}
be defined as in (
4.6
). For
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
,
((x,x_{d+1}),t)\in{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
,

{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}\tilde{f}\big((x,x_{d+1}\big),t)={\mathbf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f(x,t).

Proof.

By the Definitions of
{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}\tilde{f}(X,t)
and
{\mathbf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f(x,t)
, and using the Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
,
we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}\tilde{f}\big(X,t)=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}}\tilde{f}\big(Y,s\big){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((X,t),(Y,s)\big){\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}(s)\mathrm{d}\sigma(y,y_{d+1})\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}\Big[\tilde{f}(Z,u){\mathsf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((Z,u),(x,t)\big)+\tilde{f}\big(Z_{*},u\big){\mathsf{L}}_{n}\big(\big(Z_{*},u\big),(x,t)\big)\Big]W_{{\gamma},0}(z,u)\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}f(z,u){\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\gamma}\big((x,t),(z,u)\big)W_{{\gamma},0}{(z,u)}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}u={\mathbf{L}}^{\gamma}_{n}f(x,t),

where
Z=(z,z_{d+1})
and
Z_{*}=(z,-z_{d+1})
.
∎

4.2.
A new modulus of smoothness and
K
-functional

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{p,W_{\gamma}}
the weighted
L^{p}
norm on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}

\|f\|_{p,W_{\gamma}}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}|f(x,t)|^{p}W_{{\gamma}}(x,t)\mathrm{d}{\mathsf{m}}(x,t)\right)^{1/p},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,

where
{\mathsf{m}}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the cone. For
p=\infty
, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
the
uniform norm on
{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{{\gamma},\mu})
,
1\leq p<\infty,
and
f\in C({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1})
if
p=\infty
.
Setting
\tilde{f}
be the extension of
f
as in (
4.6
). For
\varphi(t)=\sqrt{t(1-t)}
,
\rho\geq 0
, the modulus of smoothness on the cone is defined by

\displaystyle{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,W_{{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq\rho}\max_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{p,W_{{\gamma}}}

\displaystyle+\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq\rho}\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\Big\|\Delta_{i,d+1,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}\tilde{f}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}+\sup_{0<{\theta}\leq\rho}\Big\|\Delta_{{\theta}\varphi}^{r}\tilde{f}\Big\|_{L^{p}(I_{r\rho},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})},

where the last norm is taken over the interval indicated with
I_{t}:=[t^{2},1-t^{2}]
with the usual change when
p=\infty
.

This modulus of smoothness is compatible with the modulus of smoothness on the conic surface
{\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2}
.
To emphasize the dimension of the domain, we write

{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}={\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}}}\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,{\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},W_{\gamma})}={\omega}_{r}(f;\rho)_{p,W_{\gamma}},

in the statement of the following proposition.

Proposition 4.5
.

Assume
f\in L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\gamma})
if
1\leq p<\infty
and
f\in C({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1})
if
p=\infty
. Let
\widetilde{f}
be defined
as in (
4.6
). Then

{\omega}_{r}(f,\rho)_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W{\gamma})}\sim{\omega}_{r}(\widetilde{f};\rho)_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,g})}.

Proof.

Comparing Definitions

and
4.4
shows that we only need to consider

\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}\widetilde{f}\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}_{0}^{d+2},{\mathsf{w}}_{-1,{\gamma}})}

and

\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f(x,t)=\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}\widetilde{f}(X,t)
by definition.
For
(x,t)\in{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}
, we let
x=tx^{\prime}
with
x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{B}}^{d}
. Then

W_{\gamma}(tx^{\prime},t)=(1-t)^{\gamma}\big(t^{2}-\|tx^{\prime}\|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{w_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)}{\sqrt{1-\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}}}.

Hence, making this change of variables, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{V}}^{d+1},W_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{{\mathbb{V}}^{d+1}}\Big|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f\Big|^{p}W_{\gamma}(x,t)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle={\mathsf{b}}_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}t^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{d}}\Big|\Delta_{i,j,\frac{{\theta}}{\sqrt{t}}}^{r}f(tx^{\prime},t)\Big|^{p}\frac{w_{-1,{\gamma}}(t)}{\sqrt{1-\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}}}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t

where the third identity follows from the integral relations between

{\mathbb{S}}^{d+1}

This proves the equivalence of the two moduli of smoothness.
∎

Second order divergence constraint preserving entropy stable finite difference schemes for ideal two-fluid plasma flow equations
Jaya Agnihotri, Deepak Bhoriya, Harish Kumar, Praveen Chandrashekhar, Dinshaw S. Balsara

Abstract
Two-fluid plasma flow equations describe the flow of ions and electrons with different densities, velocities, and pressures. We consider the ideal plasma flow i.e. we ignore viscous, resistive and collision effects. The resulting system of equations has flux consisting of three independent components, one for ions, one for electrons, and a linear Maxwell’s equation flux for the electromagnetic fields. The coupling of these components is via source terms. In this article, we present conservative second-order finite difference schemes that ensure the consistent evolution of the divergence constraints on the electric and magnetic fields. The key idea is to design a numerical solver for Maxwell’s equations using the multidimensional Riemann solver at the vertices, ensuring discrete divergence constraints; for the fluid parts, we use an entropy-stable discretization. The proposed schemes are co-located, second-order accurate, entropy stable, and ensure divergence-free evolution of the magnetic field. We use explicit and IMplicit-EXplicit (IMEX) schemes for time discretizations. To demonstrate the accuracy, stability, and divergence constraint-preserving ability of the proposed schemes, we present several test cases in one and two dimensions. We also compare the numerical results with those obtained from schemes with no divergence cleaning and those employing perfectly hyperbolic Maxwell (PHM) equations-based divergence cleaning methods for Maxwell’s equations.



1
Introduction

Plasma flows are essential to study several interesting physical phenomena and applications, e.g., space propulsion, controlled nuclear fusion, modeling of the solar atmosphere, etc. In addition, most astrophysical phenomena need to consider plasma flows for accurate modeling. Hence, the study and stable simulation of plasma flows is a highly active area of research. One of the simplest plasma flow models is the equations of Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD)
Goedbloed and Poedts (2004)
. The MHD model is highly successful in a wide variety of plasma flows; however, the MHD model treats plasma as a single fluid while ignoring the presence of ions and electrons. This makes the MHD model unsuitable for several interesting applications, and a more accurate flow description is needed.

Several extensions of the MHD model are based on the two-fluid description of matter
Baboolal (2001)
;
Shumlak and Loverich (2003)
;
Hakim et al. (2006)
;
Loverich et al. (2011)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Bond et al. (2017)
;
Amano (2015)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
;
Meena and Kumar (2019)
;
Wang et al. (2020)
;
Polak and Gao (2023)
, where ions and electrons are considered as the species. The two-fluid plasma flow model allows different densities, velocities, and pressures for each fluid species, and each fluid is described by Euler equations of compressible flow. These equations are coupled via source terms, which model the Lorentz force terms. In addition, Maxwell’s equations are considered to model magnetic and electric field evolution. This allows the modelling of the plasma without the assumption of quasi-neutrality and zero electron mass. Hence, model is able to capture several non-MHD effects e.g. Hall effects, electron inertia effects, displacement current, magnetic reconnection etc. The complete system of PDEs is a system of Hyperbolic Balance Laws. In addition, the magnetic field needs to be divergence-free, and the electric field needs to satisfy Gauss’s law.

Numerical schemes for the two-fluid model have been developed in several articles
Shumlak and Loverich (2003)
;
Hakim et al. (2006)
;
Loverich et al. (2011)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Amano (2015)
;
Bond et al. (2017)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
;
Wang et al. (2020)
. In
Shumlak and Loverich (2003)
, an approximate Riemann solver is proposed for the model, while in
Hakim et al. (2006)
, a wave-propagation-based scheme from
LeVeque (2002)
is designed for the two-fluid model. In
Loverich et al. (2011)
, Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) based schemes are proposed, while entropy stable schemes for the model were proposed in
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
. In
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
, positivity-preserving schemes were proposed, which also discuss the stability of implicit source term updates. More recently,
Bond et al. (2016)
proposed an adaptive mesh refinement-based scheme. In
Wang et al. (2020)
, an exact solution for the source term was used to discretize the equations.

To ensure divergence-free magnetic field evolution and evolution of the electric field consistent with Gauss’s Law, several approaches are considered in the literature
Shumlak and Loverich (2003)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Hakim et al. (2006)
;
Amano (2015)
;
Balsara et al. (2016)
. In
Shumlak and Loverich (2003)
, only one-dimensional problems were considered; hence, a constant magnetic field in the
x
-direction was present. To ensure consistency with Gauss’s law, an additional elliptic equation was solved to update the electric field. In
Hakim et al. (2006)
;
Loverich et al. (2011)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
;
Bond et al. (2017)
, PHM equation based approach is considered. In
Amano (2015)
, a quasi-neutral two-fluid plasma model is considered; electric and magnetic fields are evolved on the faces of the cells using a multidimensional Riemann solver at the cell vertices and then coupled with the fluid variable at the cell centers. A similar approach was also considered in
Amano (2016)
for the two-fluid relativistic plasma flow equations. A higher-order scheme based on the multidimensional Riemann solvers approach is presented in
Balsara et al. (2016)
. The works in
Amano (2015)
;
Amano (2016)
;
Balsara et al. (2016)
need the evolution of electric and magnetic fields on the cell faces. Similarly, for Maxwell’s equations, mimetic schemes have been developed in
Yee (1966)
;
Hyman and Shashkov (1999)
;
J. M. Hyman (2001)
that mimic certain identities in vector calculus at he discrete level which then helps to satisfy divergence constraints. One key idea to achieve this is to store the electromagnetic variables on the faces and edges of the mesh, i.e., a staggered storage scheme is required.

In this article, we design second-order accurate, co-located schemes for ideal two-fluid plasma flow equations, that ensure divergence-free evolution of the magnetic field and a consistent treatment of the electric field, which respects Gauss’s law. We present the schemes in two dimensions and proceed as follows.

•

We first consider the equations for magnetic and electric field evolution. We then use multidimensional Riemann solver
Balsara (2014)
;
Balsara et al. (2014)
;
Balsara et al. (2016)
;
Chandrashekar and Kumar (2020)
at each corner of the cells. Using these values, we define the numerical fluxes at each cell face. The resulting scheme is then shown to ensure the divergence-free evolution of the magnetic field and an evolution of the electric field that is consistent with a discrete version of Gauss’s law. Such ideas are sometimes called mimetic schemes, and we achieve this property with co-located storage of all unknowns.

•

For the fluid parts, we consider the entropy stable numerical scheme
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
. We note that this choice is not unique. Instead, W e can use any other stable discretization of the fluid flux, e.g.,
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
.

•

To achieve second-order accuracy, we reconstruct electric and magnetic fields at the cell corners using MinMod-based reconstruction.

•

Finally, we consider explicit and Implicit-Explicit (IMEX) time update schemes. In the IMEX scheme, we treat the source term implicitly
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
. We prove that the completely discrete schemes are also divergence-free and are consistent with the discretization of Gauss’s law.

The rest of the article is organized as follows. In the next Section, we present the two-fluid plasma flow equations. In Section
3
, we present the semi-discrete scheme based on the multidimensional Riemann solver for Maxwell’s equations. We also describe the second-order extension and entropy-stable discretization of the fluid components. In Section
4
, we present explicit and IMEX time discretizations. We also present the fully discrete evolutions of the divergence constraints. Numerical test cases are presented in Section
5
, followed by concluding remarks in Section
6
.

Here, the subscript

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}+\nabla\times\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}-c^{2}\nabla\times\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\mathbf{j}}{\epsilon_{0}}

(3b)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\rho_{c}}{\epsilon_{0}}

Here,

The Eqn. (
3a
) is Faraday’s law for the magnetic field, and Eqn. (
3b
) represents Ampere’s law for the electric field. The magnetic field

has to satisfy the divergence-free condition given in Eqn. (

), and the electric field

has to satisfy the divergence constraint Eqn. (

) given by Gauss’s law.

The vector of conservative variables that consists of fluid and electromagnetic parts is given by

\mathbf{U}=(\mathbf{U}_{I}^{\top},\mathbf{U}_{E}^{\top},\mathbf{U}_{M}^{\top})^{\top}

where
\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}=(\rho_{\alpha},\rho_{\alpha}\mathbf{u}_{\alpha},\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})^{\top}

where, the fluxes

2.1
Divergence constraints of Maxwell’s equations

In addition to the system of equations in (
5
), the electric and magnetic fields also need to satisfy the additional divergence constraints in the form of Gauss’s law (
3d
) and the divergence-free condition (
3c
). Let us consider the Faraday’s law (
3a
); taking the divergence of this equation results in

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B})=0

(12)

Hence, if
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}=0
at the initial time, Eqn. (
3a
) will ensure (
3c
) for all time. Therefore, satisfying the divergence constraint (
3c
) for all time is an outcome of the initial divergence-free magnetic field and Eqn. (
3a
). Similarly, taking the divergence of Ampere’s law (
3b
), we get,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E})=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}=-\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}(r_{I}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{I}\mathbf{u}_{I})+r_{E}\nabla\cdot(\rho_{E}\mathbf{u}_{E}))=\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}\frac{\partial\rho_{c}}{\partial t}

(13)

This is the time derivative of the Eqn. (
3d
); hence, (
3d
) is a consequence of the fact that it is satisfied at the initial time and
\mathbf{E}
is evolved using (
3b
). Hence, the system (
3
) is not over-determined and the constraints should be considered as the conditions on the initial data.

While the previous two constraint equations are respected at the continuous level, at the discrete level this is not guaranteed
Jiang et al. (1996)
unless a good mimetic discretization is used. Neglecting these constraints can lead to spurious non-physical oscillations in the solution. This is particularly significant when dealing with oscillatory source terms, as it can lead to non-physical oscillations in the overall solution.

To overcome this, several recent articles
Hakim et al. (2006)
;
Loverich et al. (2011)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
;
Bond et al. (2017)
use
perfectly hyperbolic Maxwell’s equations
(PHM) from
Munz et al. (2000)
. The Eqns. (
3
) are modified by introducing ”correction potentials”,
\phi
and
\psi
to get,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{B}}{\partial t}+\nabla\times\mathbf{E}+\kappa\nabla\psi

\displaystyle=0

(14a)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial\mathbf{E}}{\partial t}-c^{2}\nabla\times\mathbf{B}+\xi c^{2}\nabla\phi

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\mathbf{j}}{\epsilon_{0}}

(14b)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\kappa c^{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=0

(14c)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\xi\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=\xi\dfrac{\rho_{c}}{\epsilon_{0}}

(14d)

Here,
\kappa
and
\xi
are error propagation speeds. In the limiting case of
\kappa,\xi\to\infty
, both divergence constraints (
3c
) and (
3d
) are satisfied. However, in practice,
\kappa,\xi=1
or
2
is considered. One key benefit of the PHM formulation is that the resulting equations are still hyperbolic. However, the resulting system has larger eigenvalues, consequently smaller timesteps, leading to an increase in wallclock time. Furthermore, the divergence constraints are satisfied only in the limit of large
\kappa,\xi
.

In this section, we have analyzed the continuous problem and corresponding entropy condition. We have also discussed the divergence constraints of Maxwell’s equations. In the next Section, we will design numerical schemes for the system (
5
) consistent with the entropy condition (
10
) and the divergence constraints (
12
) and (
13
).

3
Semi-discrete schemes

Let us consider a two-dimensional rectangular domain
D=(x_{\min},x_{max})\times(y_{\min},y_{max}).
We discretize the domain
D
using a uniform mesh with the cell size
\Delta x\times\Delta y
. We now define
x_{i}=x_{min}+(i+1/2)\Delta x
and
y_{j}=y_{min}+(j+1/2)\Delta y
, with

and

and

\mathbf{F}_{M,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{y}\end{pmatrix}

Furthermore, each component of the numerical flux is consistent with the corresponding continuous flux. We will first describe

3.1
Multidimensional Local Lax-Friedrich flux for Maxwell’s equations

At point
\left(x_{i}+\frac{1}{2},y_{j}+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j},\mathbf{U}_{M,i+1,j},\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j+1}

\mathbf{U}_{M,i+1,j+1}

\mathbf{U}_{M,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\frac{\mathbf{U}_{M,i+1,j}+\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j}}{2},\quad\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j+1}+\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j}}{2}

(17)

\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{M,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j}+\mathbf{U}_{M,i+1,j}+\mathbf{U}_{M,i,j+1}+\mathbf{U}_{M,i+1,j+1}}{4}

Following
Balsara (2014)
;
Balsara et al. (2014)
;
Balsara et al. (2016)
;

\tilde{E}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\bar{E}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{c}{2}\left(\left(B_{y,i+1,j+\frac{1}{2}}-B_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)-\left(B_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j+1}-B_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\right)\right),

(19)

c^{2}\tilde{B}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=c^{2}\bar{B}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{c}{2}\left(\left(E_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j+1}-E_{x,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\right)-\left(E_{y,i+1,j+\frac{1}{2}}-E_{y,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right).

(20)

Here, we note that the multidimensional Riemann solver has two parts, namely, the central part denoted the averages
\bar{E}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}
and
\bar{B}_{z,i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}
and a multidimensional dissipation given by the remaining terms. This approximation can also be motivated starting from an artificial viscosity approach, see Section
A
. We use only light speed

3.3
Entropy stable discretization of fluid equations

We now consider the fluid components
\mathbf{F}_{\alpha}
of the numerical fluxes. To ensure the entropy-stable discretizations of the fluid components, we follow
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
and give a brief description of the numerical scheme. We introduce the following notations:

[a]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}=a_{i+1,j}-a_{i,j},\qquad[a]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=a_{i,j+1}-a_{i,j}

and

\bar{a}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\frac{a_{i+1,j}+a_{i,j}}{2},\qquad\bar{a}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{a_{i,j+1}+a_{i,j}}{2}

A consistent entropy stable numerical flux has the following form:

\mathbf{F}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{D}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\qquad\mathbf{F}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{D}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}[\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}

(34)

where
\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}=\dfrac{\partial e_{\alpha}}{\partial\mathbf{U}_{\alpha}}
is the entropy variable, and
\chi^{x}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{F}^{x}_{\alpha}-q^{x}_{\alpha}
,
\chi^{y}_{\alpha}=\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{F}^{y}_{\alpha}-q^{y}_{\alpha}
are entropy potentials. Furthermore,
\mathbf{D}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
\mathbf{D}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are some positive definite symmetric matrices which we will define later in this section. The
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are consistent entropy conservative numerical fluxes satisfying Tadmor conditions,

[\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=[\chi^{x}_{\alpha}]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j},\qquad[\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=[\chi^{x}_{\alpha}]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}.

Following
Chandrashekar (2013)
they are given by,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\\
\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}}{2\bar{\beta}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}}+\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}(\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j})^{2}\\
\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\\
\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{z}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\\
\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\hat{\beta}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}}-\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{2}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bigg)\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}+(\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j})^{3}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}+\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}}{2\bar{\beta}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}}\\
+(\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j})^{2}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}+(\bar{u}^{z}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j})^{2}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\\
\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2},}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\\
\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}(\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}})^{2}+\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}{2\bar{\beta}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}\\
\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{z}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\\
\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{(\gamma-1)\hat{\beta}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}-\bar{\mathbf{u}}^{2}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bigg)\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}+(\bar{u}^{x}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\\
+(\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}})^{3}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}+\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}{2\bar{\beta}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}+(\bar{u}^{z}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}})^{2}\bar{u}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{\rho}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}

Here,
\beta_{\alpha}=\frac{\rho_{\alpha}}{p_{\alpha}}
and we define the logarithmic average of a strictly positive number as

\hat{a}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\frac{[a]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}}{[\log a]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}},\qquad\hat{a}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{[a]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}{[\log a]_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}}

When the left and right states get very near to one another, the logarithmic average definition might not work well numerically. We will apply a reliable approximation technique, as detailed in
Ismail and Roe (2009)
, to deal with this scenario.

The entropy diffusion matrices are given by,

\mathbf{D}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\Lambda^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{x\top}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j},\qquad\mathbf{D}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}=\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{y\top}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}

(35)

where
\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{x}_{\alpha}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{y}_{\alpha}
are the right eigenvector matrices for the
x
and
y-
directional flux jacobian matrices. Following
Fjordholm et al. (2012)
, to achieve higher order entropy stable schemes, we reconstruct the scaled entropy variables using the MinMod reconstruction, which has the
sign preserving property
. We will only illustrate the procedure for the
x
-direction, as the
y
-directional case is similar. We first define the change of variable,

\mathcal{V}_{\alpha,k,j}^{x,\pm}=(\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{x}_{\alpha,i\pm\frac{1}{2},j})^{\top}\mathbf{V}_{\alpha,k,j},

where
k
are neighbours of cell
(i,j)
along the
x-
direction. Applying the MinMod reconstruction process on index
k
, we select a cell stencil and construct a linear polynomial
P_{\alpha,i,j}
, we get the traces,

\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,k,j}^{x,\pm}=P_{\alpha,i,j}^{x,\pm}(x_{i\pm\frac{1}{2}}).

Finally, we define the reconstructed entropy variables as,

\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{x,\pm}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\{(\tilde{\mathbf{R}}^{x}_{\alpha,i\pm\frac{1}{2},j})^{\top}\}^{(-1)}\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\alpha,k,j}^{x,\pm}

The second-order entropy stable numerical flux in the
x-
direction is then given by,

\mathbf{F}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}=\tilde{\mathbf{F}}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{D}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}[\![\tilde{\mathbf{V}}^{x}_{\alpha}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}

(36)

where
[\![\mathbf{\tilde{V}}^{x}_{\alpha}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}
defined as,

[\![\mathbf{\tilde{V}}^{x}_{\alpha}]\!]_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}\,=\,\mathbf{\tilde{V}}^{x,-}_{\alpha,{i+1,j}}\,-\,\mathbf{\tilde{V}}^{x,+}_{\alpha,{i,j}}.

Using these numerical fluxes, we have the following result from
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
:

Theorem 3.1

The semi-discrete scheme (
15
) with numerical fluxes (
36
) is second-order accurate and entropy stable i.e., it satisfies the following entropy inequality,

\frac{d}{dt}e_{\alpha}(\mathbf{U}_{\alpha,i,j})+\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left(\hat{q}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{x}-\hat{q}_{\alpha,i-\frac{1}{2},j}^{x}\right)+\frac{1}{\Delta y}\left(\hat{q}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{y}-\hat{q}_{\alpha,i,j-\frac{1}{2}}^{y}\right)\leq 0

(37)

for
\alpha\in\{I,E\}
, where
\hat{q}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}
,
\hat{q}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
are the numerical entropy flux functions consistent with the continuous entropy fluxes
q^{x}_{\alpha},q^{y}_{\alpha}
respectively, given by,

\hat{q}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{x}=\bar{\textbf{V}}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}\cdot\mathbf{F}^{x}_{\alpha,i+\frac{1}{2},j}-\bar{\chi}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j}^{x}

and

\hat{q}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{y}=\bar{\textbf{V}}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\mathbf{F}^{y}_{\alpha,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}-\bar{\chi}_{i,j+\frac{1}{2}}^{y}.

This holds true for any choice of Maxwell’s equation numerical fluxes
\mathbf{F}_{M,i+\frac{1}{2},j}
and
\mathbf{F}_{M,i,j+\frac{1}{2}}
.

Note that the three fluxes are independent of one another, and the fluid interacts with Maxwell’s equations via source terms only. Furthermore, we note that the source terms do not contribute to the entropy production, i.e.

\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}\cdot\mathbf{s}_{\alpha}=0.

Hence, we can take any numerical flux for Maxwell’s equations, and we still get the above entropy inequality.

In this Section, we have presented spatial discretization for the second-order schemes which are entropy-stable at the semi-discrete level. We will now present the fully discrete numerical schemes and discuss divergence constraint errors for the electromagnetic fields.

\mathbf{U}^{n+1}_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{U}^{(0)}_{i,j}+\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{U}^{(2)}_{i,j}

(41)

O2EXP-MultiD

(42)

Remark 1

The above proof is based on the fact that our discretization mimics the vector identities

\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})=\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf{E})=0

4.2
IMEX scheme

In many cases, the source term can be stiff
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
. To overcome this, we use a L-stable second-order accurate IMEX scheme from
Pareschi and Russo (2005)
. This involves two implicit stages that are given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{n}+\Delta t\left(\beta\mathbf{s}\left(\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}\right)\right)

(45)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{n}+\Delta t\left[\mathcal{L}_{i,j}\left(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}\right)+(1-2\beta)\mathbf{s}\left(\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}\right)+\beta\mathbf{s}\left(\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(2)}\right)\right]

(46)

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}^{n+1}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\left[\mathcal{L}_{i,j}\left(\mathbf{U}^{(1)}\right)+\mathcal{L}_{i,j}\left(\mathbf{U}^{(2)}\right)+\mathbf{s}\left(\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}\right)+\mathbf{s}\left(\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(2)}\right)\right]

(47)

In the above update,
\beta
is a constant given by
\beta=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
. We follow
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)
;
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
to solve for
\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(1)}
and
\mathbf{U}_{i,j}^{(2)}
in first two stages. We note that the resulting algebraic equations can be solved exactly, and the computational costs are comparable to the explicit schemes above. The corresponding scheme is denoted by
O2IMEX-MultiD
.

Similar to the explicit scheme, we have the following result for the IMEX scheme:

Theorem 4.2
(Divergence errors for the IMEX scheme)

The IMEX scheme update (
45
)-(
47
) satisfies,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}^{n+1}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}

(48)

for the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
which is consistent with (
12
). Similarly, for the electric field
\mathbf{E}
, we have the discrete divergence evolution given by,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{n+1}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{n}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}-\frac{\Delta t}{2\epsilon_{0}}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}^{(1)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}^{(2)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(49)

which is consistent with (
13
).

Proof

Proof for (
48
) is similar to the case of the explicit scheme as (
3a
) does not have a source term. We note that from (
45
), we get,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(1)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(0)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}-\beta\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}^{(1)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

and update in Eqn. (
46
) gives,

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(2)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}^{(1)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}+(1-2\beta)\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}^{(1)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)+\beta\frac{\Delta t}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}^{(2)}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

Combining these with (
47
), we get (
49
).

In this Section, we have presented the satisfaction of discrete divergence constraints for the fully discrete schemes. We will now present the numerical results for the proposed schemes and discuss the corresponding divergence constraint errors for several two-dimensional test cases.

5
Numerical results

We will now present various test cases in one and two dimensions. The time step is chosen using,

{\Delta t}=\text{CFL}\min_{i,j}\left\{\dfrac{1}{\dfrac{\Lambda_{max}^{x}(\mathbf{U}_{i,j})}{\Delta x}+\dfrac{\Lambda_{max}^{y}(\mathbf{U}_{i,j})}{\Delta y}}:1\leq i\leq N_{x},\ 1\leq j\leq N_{y}\right\}

where
\Lambda_{max}^{x}(\mathbf{U}_{i,j})
and
\Lambda_{max}^{y}(\mathbf{U}_{i,j})
are estimates of the maximum wave speeds in the two directions given by

\Lambda_{max}^{x}(\mathbf{U})=\max\{|u_{I}^{x}|+a_{I},|u_{E}^{x}|+a_{E},c\},\qquad\Lambda_{max}^{y}(\mathbf{U})=\max\{|u_{I}^{y}|+a_{I},|u_{E}^{y}|+a_{E},c\}

We take CFL as
0.45
for the
O2IMEX-MultiD
scheme and
0.2
for the explicit scheme
O2EXP-MultiD
, unless stated otherwise. Several physically significant parameters appear in test cases and are defined as follows:

•

Following
Kumar and Mishra (2012)
;
Abgrall and Kumar (2014)

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{g}=\frac{{r}_{g}}{x_{0}}=\frac{m_{I}{u_{I}}_{0}}{q_{I}B_{0}x_{0}}

•

For the two-dimensional test cases, the charge-to-mass ratio for ions is computed using,

\displaystyle r_{I}=\frac{1}{d_{I}\sqrt{\rho_{I}\mu_{0}}}\qquad\text{where}\quad d_{I}=\dfrac{\text{Domain size}}{\text{Mass ratio}},\qquad\mu_{0}=1.0

Wang et al. (2020)

Conclusions

In this article, we have proposed co-located numerical discretizations of the two-fluid plasma flow equations, which are consistent with the divergence constraints of Maxwell’s equations. It is based on using a multidimensional Riemann solver at the vertices to define numerical fluxes across the cell faces. The proposed schemes can also be coupled with numerical schemes to preserve other stability properties related to the fluid variables, like entropy stability. We have also presented several test cases in one- and two-dimensions to demonstrate the consistency with the divergence constraints. We have also compared our results with the other strategies commonly employed to preserve the divergence constraints and show that the proposed schemes are superior at preserving the divergence constraints and give more accurate solutions.

Appendix A
Discretization of Maxwell’s equations

Define the difference and averaging operators

\delta_{x}\phi(x,y)=\frac{\phi(x+\Delta x/2,y)-\phi(x-\Delta x/2,y)}{\Delta x},\qquad\mu_{x}\phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}[\phi(x-\Delta x/2,y)+\phi(x+\Delta x/2,y)]

\delta_{y}\phi(x,y)=\frac{\phi(x,y+\Delta y/2)-\phi(x,y-\Delta y/2)}{\Delta y},\qquad\mu_{y}\phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}[\phi(x,y-\Delta y/2)+\phi(x,y+\Delta y/2)]

The discretization of the magnetic field given in Section
3.1
can be written as

\frac{dB_{x,i,j}}{dt}+\delta_{y}\mu_{x}\tilde{E}_{z,i,j}=0,\qquad\frac{dB_{y,i,j}}{dt}-\delta_{x}\mu_{y}\tilde{E}_{z,i,j}=0

(50)

where
\tilde{E}_{z}
is defined at the vertices. The divergence is measured at the vertices using a central difference approximation

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=(\delta_{x}\mu_{y}B_{x}+\delta_{y}\mu_{x}B_{y})_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}

Then

\frac{d}{dt}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}_{i+\frac{1}{2},j+\frac{1}{2}}=\delta_{x}\mu_{y}\frac{dB_{x}}{dt}+\delta_{y}\mu_{x}\frac{dB_{y}}{dt}=-\delta_{x}\mu_{y}\delta_{y}\mu_{x}\tilde{E}_{z}+\delta_{y}\mu_{x}\delta_{x}\mu_{y}\tilde{E}_{z}=0

The last result follows since the difference and averaging operators commute. This is the discrete analogue of the property that
\nabla\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf{B}=0
for any smooth vector field
\mathbf{B}
; such schemes which satisfy vector calculus identifies are usually referred to as being mimetic. Note that this property holds for any consistent definition of the vertex values
\tilde{E}_{z}
.

\frac{d\mathbf{B}}{dt}+\nabla\times\mathbf{E}=-\nu\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{B}

\Delta\mathbf{B}=-\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{B}

Let us discretize the above two PDEs with a multi-dimensional central difference scheme at each grid point
(i,j)

\displaystyle=

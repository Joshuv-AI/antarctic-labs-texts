Repurposing raltegravir for reducing inflammation and treating cancer: a bioinformatics analysis
Authors: Zahra Nikfarjam, Reza Rakhshi, Farshid Zargari, Mahdi Aalikhani, Amin Moradi Hasan-Abad, Zahra Bazi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractInflammation is a defensive mechanism that safeguards the human body against detrimental stimuli. Within this intricate process, ADAM17, a zinc-dependent metalloprotease, emerges as an indispensable element, fostering the activation of diverse inflammatory and growth factors within the organism. Nonetheless, ADAM17 malfunctions can augment the rate of growth, inflammatory factors, and subsequent damage. Thus, in this study, we examined and repurposed drugs to suppress the activity of ADAM17. To this end, we employed bioinformatics techniques such as molecular docking, molecular dynamics, and pharmacokinetic studies. Five FDA-approved drugs including Raltegravir, Conivaptan, Paclitaxel, Saquinavir, and Venetoclax with the ability to impede the activity of the ADAM17 metalloenzyme were identified. Moreover, these drugs did not include strong zinc-binding functional groups when verified by the ACE functional group finder. However, further in silico analysis has indicated that Raltegravir demonstrates a commendable interaction with the active site amino acids and exhibits the most favorable pharmacokinetic properties compared to others. Considering the results of bioinformatics tools, it can be concluded that Raltegravir as an antiviral drug could be repurposed to prevent severe inflammatory response and tumorigenesis resulting from ADAM17 malfunction.Keywords: ADAM17, Drug repurposing, Molecular docking, Molecular Dynamics, RaltegravirSubject terms: Bioinformatics, Cancer

Introduction

Inflammation plays a crucial role in maintaining our well-being, as any malfunction in the components involved in the inflammatory response can result in the demise of the organism. Conversely, an excessive activity of inflammatory factors can lead to severe complications, such as autoimmune disorders, psoriasis, rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, and Crohn’s disease. ADAM17 is a transmembrane protein weighing 70 kDa and contains 824 amino acids. Its main function is to cleave and activate a wide array of growth and pro-inflammatory factors1. This protease is of utmost importance in releasing ectodomains from various cell surface cytokines, cell adhesion molecules, receptors, ligands, and enzymes2. For example, the peptide Pro-Leu-Ala-Gln-Ala-|-Val-Arg-Ser-Ser-Ser is cleaved by this enzyme in the tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-alpha) that is in the membrane. Similarly, it cleaves other pro-inflammatory factors such as IL-6R3. Cleaving and activation of IL-6R by ADAM17 leads to a severe inflammatory response4. Additionally, ADAM17 directly affects tumorigenesis by activating TGFα, EGF, and other factors3. Interestingly, ADAM17 is a critical contributor to the development of severe symptoms in COVID-19 disease through the activation of the ACE2 enzyme5,6. These pieces of evidence indicate that inhibiting or reducing the activity of this protease by controlling the release of inflammatory cytokines and growth factors can serve as a practical approach to mitigating the complications arising from an exaggerated immune response and the occurrence of cancer.

While ADAM17 displays minimal sequence similarities with other ADAMs, most small molecules that possess the potential to hinder ADAM17 activity have proven to be unsuccessful in clinical trials. It is mainly due to the emergence of detrimental side effects resulting from the inhibition of other ADAM proteases, as well as the inadequate absorption and generation of toxic metabolites7. For example, TAPI-1 and GI254023X as candidate drugs for ADAM17 inhibition showed severe side effects in the clinic because of the presence of hydroxamate-based zinc binding groups that enable them to bind with other zinc-dependent proteases7,8. The main functional groups used to chelate the zinc ion (II) in Zinc-dependent proteases are hydroxamate (CONHO−), carboxylate (COO−), thiolates (RS−), and phosphorus (PO2−) which among them hydroxamic acid and then formyl hydroxylamine showed the highest selectivity and potency for binding to Zinc9. Therefore, the identification of non-zinc-binding components and the assessment of their pharmacokinetics could potentially enhance specificity and minimize the adverse effects associated with the inhibition of ADAM17. In the modern pharmacological era, computational tools and software applications possess the capability to analyze the various types of drug-protein interactions and the pharmacokinetic properties of drugs. This has the potential to augment the success rate and alleviate the financial burden associated with the drug development process. Furthermore, due to the time-consuming and expensive nature of discovering and then obtaining clinical approval for novel drugs, the utilization of previously FDA-approved drugs for alternative repurposing in the pharmaceutical industry has significantly contributed to the mitigation of setbacks encountered during the drug discovery process7,10. Thus, through drug repurposing in addition to reducing the cost and time required, having the necessary approvals accelerates their entry into the market.

Therefore, in this computational investigation, our objective was to repurpose drugs approved by the FDA to identify those that interact mostly and efficiently with the key active site amino acids of this metalloprotease. To achieve this, we employed bioinformatics, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics tools. Molecular docking and dynamics are indispensable tools in structural molecular biology and drug design. They enable the virtual screening of extensive compound libraries, scoring of results, and generation of structural hypotheses regarding how a ligand binds to a target molecule11,12.

Results

Binding Affinity of ADAM17 protein

The structure of ADAM17 utilized in this investigation (PDB ID 2I47) encompassed four chains of the ADAM17 catalytic domain in addition to two associated inhibitors, namely KGY and INN. KGY interacted with chains L and J of ADAM17, while F and H chains bonded with INN as peptide-like inhibitors. These chains spanned amino acids ranging from 220 to 474, comprising approximately 254 amino acids out of a total of 824 amino acids of the ADAM17 protein. According to the UniProt and NCBI databases, the improved PDB files obtained from the Swiss Model adjusted the number of amino acids from 1, resulting in Glu187, His186, His190, His196, and Zinc as the key catalytic amino acids. Moreover, based on the interaction between these inhibitors and ADAM17, it was observed that 22 amino acids were involved in the active site pocket, including Met126, Gly127, Thr128, Leu129, Gly130, Leu131, Ala132, Asn170, Tyr171, Glu179, Leu182, Val183, His186, Glu187, His190, His196, Val215, Tyr217, Pro218, Ile219, Ala220, and Val221.

Molecular docking simulation

Five drugs are capable of inhibiting ADAM17

To further analyze the interaction between the active site of ADAM17 and 1600 FDA-approved drugs, five drugs with the highest binding affinity were selected using molecular docking. The selection criteria for these drugs included binding affinity, number of hydrogen bonds, number of Van der Waals interactions, and total carbon-hydrogen bonds. Besides, the binding affinity of KGY and INN inhibitors were evaluated during this process as controls (Table 1). Paclitaxel demonstrated the most favorable binding affinity to the ADAM17 protein, with a value of -42.28 kJ/mol. Venetoclax exhibited a binding affinity of -41.03 kJ/mol. Furthermore, Conivaptan displayed a binding affinity of -40.61 kJ/mol towards the ADAM17 protein. Raltegravir and Sequinavir concluded the list with a binding affinity of -40.19 kJ/mol. Notably, Raltegravir and Venetoclax interact with key amino acids in the active site of ADAM17, specifically Glu187, His186, His190, His196, and Zinc as an enzyme cofactor. These drugs exhibited a greater affinity for ADAM17 when compared to the INN and KGY inhibitors. The most optimal two-dimensional positions of the drug-ADAM17 protein interaction were plotted to visually represent the connection between the ligand and protein (Fig. 1).

The candidate drugs do not include strong zinc-binding functional groups

To ascertain whether prospective pharmaceutical candidates possess substantial negatively charged functional groups that could enhance the likelihood of their interactions with zinc in various proteases, we analyzed the functional groups of the target drugs employing the ACE functional group finder. As illustrated in Table 2, most of the functional groups within these compounds exhibit either weak positive or negative charges, such as those found in the benzene ring or nitro groups, or possess a neutral charge, as seen in carboxamides. Consequently, these compounds lack robust negatively charged functional groups that would be capable of interacting with the positively charged zinc ion located in the active site of ADAM17 and other zinc-dependent proteases.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Root mean square deviation (RMSD)

During our investigation, we conducted molecular dynamics (MD) simulations on the optimal docking conformation of five chosen ligand-receptor complexes and scrutinized their Root-Mean-Square Deviation (RMSD) profiles. Our findings manifest that all the complexes attain remarkable stability after approximately 100 nanoseconds, as evidenced by uniformly low RMSD values. This observation signifies favorable interactions between these ligands and their corresponding targets, establishing a state of equilibrium within the binding sites. Nonetheless, the atoms were endowed with distinct initial velocities following the principles of the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. Figure 2 visually portrays the graph depicting RMSD for the five ligand-ADAM17 complexes. To gain a more profound understanding of the role played by residues involved in inhibiting ADAM17, an additional analysis was conducted to examine the overall energy associated with the binding of each of them to the potential pharmaceutical compounds. The analysis revealed that the primary favorable impact of Raltegravir on ADAM17 binding is predominantly observed from the residues Pro218 (-2 kcal/mol), Ile219 (approximately − 4 kcal/mol), and Ala (approximately − 2 kcal/mol; Fig. 3). Conversely, the primary favorable contributions of ADAM17 inhibition by Conivaptan were predominantly observed from the residues Pro218 and Ile219, with an energy contribution of approximately − 1.5 kcal/mol. In the case of Paclitaxel, significant favorable contributions were observed from the residues Met126 (approximately − 4 kcal/mol), Thr128 (slightly above − 2 kcal/mol), and Leu129 (approximately − 2 kcal/mol). Saquinavir was found to be positioned within the active site of ADAM17 through Met126, Leu131, Glu187, His190, His196, and Pro218, with a total contribution energy of less than − 2 kcal/mol. Finally, the residues Met126, Gly127, Ile129, and Ala220, with an energy contribution of more than − 3 kcal/mol, indicated the highest level of energy involved in the interaction of Venetoclax within the ADAM17 protein.

Hydrogen bond analysis

Hydrogen bonding and hydrophobic interactions are two forms of feeble intermolecular interactions, which play a vital role in stabilizing energetically favored ligands within a protein-based environment15. The hydrogen bonding occupancy plots versus time for all the ligands are depicted in Fig. 5. The duration of a hydrogen bond refers to the extent of hydrogen bond occupation in a greater number of MD simulation frames. As illustrated in Fig. 5, Tyr171 exhibits a more recurrent hydrogen bond with Raltegravir. Upon inspecting the structure of Conivaptan, it was ascertained that the hydrogen bond with Gly127 and Glu203 presented the most substantial disparity. The paclitaxel ligand forms a hydrogen bond with the Leu129 and Met126 residues that exhibit a high occupancy rate. Saquinavir’s structure tends to generate a hydrogen bond with the Gly130 residues. Moreover, there has been a notable inclination for the formation of a hydrogen bond between the Gly127, Thr171, and Pro218 residues and Venetoclax. Except for Glu203, all these amino acids were in the active site.

The Pharmacological Properties of Top Five Candidate Drugs

Table 4 describes the physicochemical and pharmacokinetic properties of the top five selected drugs. Based on physical and pharmacological features, Raltegravir, as an antiviral drug with the potential ability to bind to and inhibit the ADAM17 protein, was selected as the most potent drug (Figs. 1A and 4A). This pharmaceutical compound possesses a relatively low molecular weight (444.423 g/mol) and exhibits a high degree of solubility in aqueous environments (60% water solubility). Raltegravir can be administered via oral routes and demonstrates a significant affinity for protein binding, both of which are critical pharmacological characteristics. Other drugs have poor gastrointestinal absorption, making them unsuitable for oral use. Moreover, some drugs, such as Paclitaxel and Conivaptan, have low water solubility, limiting their absorption and effectiveness. Furthermore, these drugs show increased toxicity by inhibiting cytochrome enzymes in the liver.

Discussion

Multiple growth and inflammatory factors are passively attached to the cellular membrane and become activated when required via the cellular signaling cascade. To trigger and demonstrate the impact of these factors, they must undergo cleavage and activation by an appropriate protease. A significant group of proteases responsible for this crucial task are the ADAM (A disintegrin and metalloproteinases) enzymes. They are transmembrane proteins that rely on the presence of zinc ions for their enzymatic activity6. These biologically indispensable proteases consist of 22 enzymes that participate in inflammatory responses, as well as cell adhesion, migration, and signaling16. These metalloproteases act by executing the ectodomain shedding of anchored pro-proteins on the cytoplasm17. ADAM17, a known member comprising 824 amino acids, plays a fundamental role in activating the immune response. This protein, which is bound to the membrane, consists of six subunits, including an N-terminal signal sequence, a metalloproteinase or catalytic domain, a disintegrin domain, a membrane-proximal domain, a single transmembrane domain, and a cytoplasmic sensor domain. After activation by phosphatidylserine in the N-terminal signal sequence region, the cellular signal on the cytoplasmic sensor domain initiates the activation of its catalytic domain, resulting in the cleavage of the substrates and the appropriate activation of the specific substrate type. The outcome of these consecutive reactions is the activation of growth and pro-inflammatory factors and the manifestation of their clinical effects. The normal operation of ADAM17 is crucial for maintaining the body’s regular homeostasis and responding to pathogenic agents. However, in cases of excessive activity, severe inflammatory response, and cancer, the activity of ADAM17 exacerbates the situation and harms the host. A notable example is the inflammatory response triggered by IL-6R and, undoubtedly, the development of tumors facilitated by the IL-6R/ADAM17 axis18. Numerous ADAMs are reported to be overexpressed in various tumors, such as ADAM12, which is believed to be involved in tissue inflammation19. ADAM17 causes the release of the ACE2 receptor from the membrane into the cytosol, thereby converting ACE2 into a soluble state (sACE2). Studies indicate that infection with SARS-CoV-2 enhances ADAM17 activity, thereby contributing to the predominant inflammatory response of macrophages and impairing the immune capacity to eliminate the virus5. Recent data suggest that severe lung damage in COVID-19 is associated with elevated levels of TNF-α and IL-6, T-cell lymphopenia, hypercoagulation, fatigue, and the predominant immune response of macrophages20. TNFα is a vital pro-inflammatory cytokine in various acute and chronic inflammatory pathologies. All these observations indicate that in unfavorable conditions, ADAM17 should be restrained. To date, numerous efforts have been focused on the development of small chemical molecules to serve as inhibitors for ADAM17 such as Apratastat developed by Wyeth Pharmaceuticals Company, and DPC333 manufactured by Bristol-Myers Squibb Company. However, they were subsequently retracted during clinical trials, as a result of severe side effects. The vast majority of these small molecules are designed to fit into the active site pocket of ADAM17 by lipophilic substituent, as well as a zinc-binding group (ZBG)7,8,21–23.

In this in silico investigation, we conducted a study to identify effective drugs for ADAM17 inhibition using bioinformatics, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics tools. To achieve this objective, we initially obtained the 3D and 2D structures of FDA-approved drugs and the ADAM17 protein. After optimization, we conducted molecular docking and discovered five potential pharmaceuticals, namely Raltegravir, Conivaptan, Paclitaxel, Saquinavir, and Venetoclax to inhibit this protease. MD simulations also demonstrated that these chemicals can bind to ADAM17 during a simulation lasting 100 ns. Although the findings from the molecular docking indicated that Paclitaxel, with a binding affinity of -42.28 kJ/mol, is the most potent inhibitor, the molecular dynamics, and MM/GBSA analysis revealed that Venetoclax, with an energy of -43.96 ± 0.4 kJ/mol, possesses the highest binding affinity. The x-ray crystal structure analysis of the catalytic domain of ADAM17 indicates that Glu406 and Zinc residing in the center of the active site by the three imidazole Nɛ2 atoms of His405, His409, and His415 have a critical role in the catalytic activity of this enzyme. Our docking findings indicated that these key amino acids are primarily engaged by Raltegravir and Venetoclax. Nonetheless, Saquinavir showed more interaction with these key residues24.

According to energy decomposition and binding free energy calculated by MM/GBSA analysis, Van der Waals interactions had a high contribution to Venetoclax interaction with the ADAM17 active site. It is along with this observation that this drug is poorly soluble and hydrophobic. Although Van der Waals forces play a crucial role in determining the specificity of the interaction between drugs and receptors, the presence of highly lipophilic molecules also introduces a higher potential for toxicity in vivo25,26. On the other side, electrostatic energy played the main role in the Raltegravir and Saquinavir binding to ADAM17 as they are soluble and moderately soluble, respectively. Hydrogen bond is another type of interaction that has a vital role in determining the affinity and selectivity of protein-ligand binding27. Our results also indicated, except Venetoclax that its hydrogen bond numbers were different dramatically during docking and MD simulation, other drugs can form a high number of hydrogen bonds in both analyses. Moreover, Saquinavir and Venetoclax predominantly showed the highest rate of contact with ADAM17 during 100 ns of molecular dynamic simulation. Based on our results, when considering the amount of binding energy and other criteria, it is more likely that the structures of Venetoclax, Raltegravir, and Saquinavir reside within the protein’s active site. However, according to ADME parameters, encompassing absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion, and type of interactions, Raltegravir has been selected as the most suitable candidate for targeting ADAM17 and inflammation orally. Raltegravir is an integrase inhibitor that has been approved for the treatment of HIV, a virus that gives rise to Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome (AIDS)28. The integration of viral DNA into the host cell genome is imperative to produce a self-replicating provirus and the replication of viral infectious particles. In this process, the viral cDNA, generated by the reverse transcriptase enzyme, is subsequently processed by HIV-1 integrase, which is the target of Raltegravir. Besides in 2015, Kelesidis T. observed that during the 96-week follow-up of Human Immunodeficiency Virus type 1 (HIV-1) infection, patient’s consumption of Raltegravir could reduce IL-6 levels in plasma despite the observation that most inflammation and immune activation persists in conventional antiretroviral therapy (ART) of HIV29. Therefore, this study also can confirm current in silico analysis regarding the potential of Raltegravir for interacting with ADAM17 as a simulator of inflammatory cytokines like IL6.

In addition to HIV, a study conducted by P. Indu et al. in 2021, employing in silico analysis, demonstrated that Raltegravir could serve as a potential drug for the treatment of COVID-19 by targeting the COVID-19 main protease (Mpro)30. Furthermore, this drug has been proposed for combating certain cancers. For instance, in a study by Alburquerque-González B. et al., it was observed that Raltegravir could inhibit the Fascin1-dependent invasion of colorectal cancer cells in vivo and vitro31. Additionally, a recent study conducted by Akcora-Yildiz et al. evaluated the potential of Raltegravir to trigger DNA damage-induced apoptosis in multiple myeloma cell lines32.

The clinical application of Raltegravir has demonstrated its primary interaction with HIV-1 integrase and its favorable tolerability within the human body. The metabolism of this medication occurs through the action of uridine diphosphate-glucuronosyl-transferase (UGT), with subsequent elimination via urinary and fecal routes. Furthermore, it has been observed that the drug elicits solely mild adverse effects, such as nausea, headache, and diarrhea among patients33. Numerous investigations were employed in silico analysis to discover ADAM17 inhibitors. For example, in 2023, Sunil AA drew inspiration from the mechanism by which inhibitor 642 and INN bind to the active site of ADAM17. Through computational analysis, they found that ceftolozane, an FDA-approved anti-bacterial drug, could potentially serve as an ADAM17 suppressor by interacting with zinc34. Similarly, Borah PK conducted an in silico analysis in 2016 to examine natural anti-inflammatory components and compare them with IK682. Their findings proposed that Silymarin, a mixture of flavonolignans derived from the milk thistle plant Silybum marianum, could inhibit ADAM17 and potentially serve as a selective inhibitor35. In 2017, Udechukwu MC employed molecular docking and experimental evaluation, revealing that zinc-chelating rye secalin-derived tripeptides could reduce ADAM17 activity36. However, none of these studies assessed the pharmacokinetics of the candidate drugs and their zinc-binding functional group7. Therefore, our study proposes that Raltegravir, a drug that showed weak interaction with the zinc group with the most favorable pharmacological properties (e.g., its solubility in water), exhibits the potential to be an encouraging candidate for the drug repurposing to inhibit ADAM17 while minimizing adverse effects. Raltegravir, traditionally used as an HIV integrase inhibitor, has shown potential as an ADAM17 inhibitor. ADAM17 is involved in the shedding of the SARS-CoV-2 spike protein, which is crucial for viral entry into host cells. By inhibiting ADAM17, Raltegravir could potentially reduce viral entry and spread, offering a new therapeutic avenue for COVID-19 treatment. ADAM17 plays a role in the cleavage of membrane-bound precursors of various growth factors, such as TGF-α and EGF, which are involved in cancer progression. Inhibiting ADAM17 with Raltegravir could disrupt these pathways, potentially inhibiting tumor growth and metastasis. ADAM17 is also implicated in the shedding of pro-inflammatory cytokines like TNF-α. By inhibiting ADAM17, Raltegravir could help modulate inflammatory responses, making it a potential treatment for inflammatory diseases. Transitioning Raltegravir from an HIV integrase inhibitor to an ADAM17 inhibitor for clinical use involves preclinical studies and regulatory approval steps, but since it currently has FDA approval it holds promise regarding a faster process in the acceptance for treating COVID-19, cancer, and inflammatory conditions.

This study was not without its weaknesses and limitations. For example, conducting molecular dynamics simulation at longer time intervals or testing the proposed drug in vitro or in vivo could have yielded many virtuous results. Despite being an in silico study, the effect of the proposed drug can be investigated and validated through further wet lab experiments. For instance, this drug could be used to treat cancer cell lines driven by excessive TGF activity or to treat mice exposed to allergens and inflammatory factors.

Conclusion

According to the results, Raltegravir demonstrates more possibility to be administrated orally in the treatment of diseases caused by aberrant ADAM17 activity. Thus, we propose the conduction of further laboratory (in vitro) and animal studies (in vivo) on the effectiveness and safety of this FDA-approved drug for the possible treatment of such diseases.

Methods

Preparation of structural files

The crystal structure of the ADAM17 (PDB ID: 2I47) was retrieved from the Protein Data Bank online resource (PDB: https://www.rcsb.org/)37. Subsequently, the three-dimensional structure of the protein was optimized using the SWISS-MODEL (https://swissmodel.expasy.org/interactive) online tool and Chimera software (version 1.15)38. After retrieving the 3D structure of the protein from the PDB database its correctness and fidelity were optimized using SWISS-MODEL and Chimera. The SWISS-MODEL performs a brief homology-modeling employing protein alignment, and energy minimization, and evaluates model quality commissioning QMEAN statistical tool. Chimera software is another application used in protein structure optimization. Here, excess ligands were removed from the molecule, the accuracy of its amino acid sequence was checked, and its 3D structure was again refined by performing energy minimization. The ZINC database (http://zinc.docking.org/) was utilized to obtain the two-dimensional structure of FDA-approved drugs in SDF format39. The SDF file containing 1600 FDA-approved drugs was acquired from the ZINC database. The active site of the ADAM17 protein and the residues involved at this particular location were identified using the Uniprot and NCBI-Protein databases (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein)40.

Molecular docking simulation

Virtual screening and molecular docking techniques were employed to identify novel drugs with the ability to inhibit the ADAM17 protein. This was done using the AutoDock Vina package of the PyRx program (V 0.8). The energy minimization tool of the PyRx program was utilized to convert the 2D structures of FDA-approved drugs into 3D files. Subsequently, the AutoDock Vina package of the PyRx program was also employed to assess the binding affinity and binding energy of these drugs to the ADAM17 protein41. After the identification of the amino acids that are present in the pocket of the active site of ADAM17, the size of the cubical grid box was established as 20 × 30 × 20, with a border of 0.375 Å surrounding it, through the utilization of AutoDock Vina. A total of 100 independent docking runs were conducted for each compound. The selection of the best binding pose was based on the interaction with the most favorable outcome, determined by the lowest free binding energy in units of kJ/mol. The BIOVIA Discovery Studio Visualizer software was utilized to analyze and visualize the binding poses between the ADAM17 protein and potential drug candidates42. From this analysis, drugs with the highest number of chemical bonds with active site amino acids and the greatest affinity for the ADAM17 protein were selected. Low free energy characterizes an energetically advantageous interaction, displaying the two can effortlessly form a constant complex. The names and properties of each drug were then determined using the PubChem database (https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/)43.

Molecular dynamics simulation

Determining the functional group of candidate drugs

The chemical behavior of organic compounds is intrinsically linked to the functional groups they encompass. A functional group refers to a specific assembly of atoms that exhibit distinct physical, chemical, and reactivity characteristics, which govern the unique chemical reactions associated with that compound. In most instances, identical functional groups tend to exhibit analogous behaviors. Consequently, ascertaining the functional groups present in a pharmacological agent can facilitate the prediction of its functions and behaviors in biological systems44. Accordingly, in this study, we employed the ACE functional group finder (https://epoch.uky.edu/ace/public/fnalGroups.jsp) to identify the functional groups of the selected pharmaceutical compounds.

Executing the molecular dynamics (MD) simulation

We employed molecular dynamics simulations to investigate the way the candidate drugs interacted with the ADAM17 protein, intending to identify the binding modes that displayed the lowest energy levels. The AMBERff14SB force field for proteins and the Generic Amber Force Field (GAFF) for all ligands were generated using the Ambertools23 software and the AM1-BCC charge model45–47. The protonation states of all titratable residues were adjusted to their default values at pH 7.0 to mimic the natural state of the system. Furthermore, the TIP3P water model was utilized to solvate the system48. The simulation box had dimensions of 76.9 × 76.9 × 76.9 Å and encompassed approximately 14,000 water molecules. To ensure the electroneutrality of the system, 0.15 M NaCl was added. Before the production phase, the system underwent an equilibration process. The chemicals and ions introduced into the box were solvated, thereby achieving system neutrality. All MD simulations were conducted using the Amber22 PMEMD GPU version. The periodic boundary condition (PBC) was imposed in all three dimensions. The energy of the protein-ligand complexes was minimized using the steepest descent technique and the movements of the complexes were taken into account using the leap-frog algorithm49. Additionally, the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) approach was employed in this procedure to evaluate the influence of long-range electrostatic interactions between molecules50. To emulate the constraint of H-bonds in this process, the LINCS algorithm was also utilized, encompassing equilibration and production runs51. The root mean squared deviation (RMSD) values were calculated using CPPTRAJ to assess the accuracy and reliability of simulation protocols and obtain a deeper understanding of the dynamic behaviors and binding mechanisms of ligand-receptor interactions. RMSD quantifies the structural variability between the initial protein-ligand complex structure and subsequent snapshots obtained during molecular dynamics simulations. In our investigation, RMSD was determined by comparing the coordinates of the simulated protein-ligand complexes at different time points with those of the initial complex structure.

Free energy calculation

The ensemble-based ESMACS protocol, which stands for enhanced sampling of molecular dynamics with approximation of continuum solvent, is employed for simulations. This choice was based on numerous studies that have established the effectiveness and reliability of ensemble methods in achieving reproducible predictions using MD simulation52. The ESMACS calculation procedure involves setting up independent MD simulations to obtain a robust estimation of binding free energy and the associated uncertainties. Interestingly, investigations have shown that the ensemble approach generates more precise and reproducible predictions compared to long simulations, even when the latter are significantly longer in temporal duration than the entire ensemble simulation53. Here, we conducted a simulation consisting of 10 replicas for the ESMACS calculation, each running for a production time of 10 nanoseconds (ns). In total, our molecular dynamics simulation spanned a duration of 100 ns for each ligand.

For each replica, we initiated the system by performing energy minimization using a steepest descent algorithm for 200 steps, followed by 7000 steps of the conjugate gradient method. Subsequently, we conducted 10 ns of equilibration simulations in which the protein was restrained using a harmonic potential on all heavy protein atoms. These restraints were gradually reduced over 5 consecutive 2 ns simulations, employing force constants of 1000, 500, 200, 100, and 50 kJ·mol− 1·nm− 2. Additionally, we heated each replica to 300 K during only the first two steps of the equilibration procedure. It is worth noting that we employed the same initial coordinates for a given ligand-target protein complex, but assigned different initial velocities to the atoms randomly according to a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.

The interactions between solvents and biomolecules play a crucial role in shaping the dynamics and functions of these molecules. Consequently, considerable efforts have been devoted to the development of methods that are rapid, precise, and reproducible for determining these interactions54,55. As mentioned earlier, the ESMACS calculation holds promise for accurately calculating free energy, and we provide a summary of these methods here. In the context of calculating the binding free energy of drugs to the EGFR receptor, we employed the ensemble-based ESMACS approach in conjunction with molecular mechanics Poisson-Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) and generalized Born Surface Area (MM/GBSA) as continuum solvation57. Hydrogen bond analysis, energy decomposition, and clustering analysis

Hydrogen bond HB analysis can be utilized to ascertain the hydrogen bonding arrangement of the chosen ligands. Within the 100 ns MD simulation of the ligand-enzyme complex, the H-bond module of AmberTools-22 was employed to examine the nature and potency of hydrogen bonds between Raltegravir, Conivaptan, Paclitaxel, Saquinavir, Venetoclax, and the protein. The angle and H-bonding distance thresholds were initially established at 135’ and 3.0 Å, respectively. The dissection of residue energy reveals both favorable and unfavorable attributes of the data, which could be harnessed to enhance the caliber of the lead. The duration of a hydrogen bond is determined by the cumulative sum of frames that accommodate the hydrogen bond during the simulation. Furthermore, within the MD simulation, we evaluated the residue-wise water swap binding energy decompositions for the residues that significantly influence inhibitor binding59–61. Clustering analysis utilizing the k-means algorithm was employed to identify distinct clusters based on structural resemblances among the ligand-protein complexes’ atomic coordinates.

Native and non-native contacts analysis

The CPPTRA version 6.4.4 function, native contacts, was employed to ascertain the presence of native and non-native contacts between the protein and ligands. A distance cutoff of 3.5 Å was established between the heavy atoms of the active site’s residues to delineate these contacts. The contacts were subsequently categorized by residue, utilizing the residue parameter, and the reference frame selected for analysis was the initial frame, corresponding to the optimal docking pose.

The analysis of pharmacological properties of top five candidate drugs

The SwissADME web tool (http://www.swissadme.ch/) and Drug Bank (https://go.drugbank.com/) were used to evaluate candidate drugs’ physicochemical and pharmacokinetic properties. These properties include bioavailability, metabolism, elimination half-life, lipid solubility or LogP, water solubility or LogS, excretion way, cytochrome P450 inhibitory, and drug-likeness (Lipinski Rules of Five)62.

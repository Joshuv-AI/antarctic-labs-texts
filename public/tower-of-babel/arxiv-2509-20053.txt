Infinite-Dimensional Towers and a Categorification of Differential Operators on 𝔸 ℤ ⁡ [ v , v − 1 ] 1 \mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]} Thanks: Research of the first author was supported in part by NSTC grants 113-2628-M-001-011 and the National Center of Theoretical Sciences
Chun-Ju Lai, Cailan Li

Abstract
. We introduce axioms for towers of infinite-dimensional algebras such that the corresponding Grothendieck groups of projective and finite-dimensional modules are Hopf dual to each other. This duality gives rise to an action of the Hesienberg double—a generalization of the classical Hesienberg algebra—on the Grothendieck group. We categorify this action and, as an application, construct a categorical realization of quantum differential operators on 𝔸 ℤ ⁡ [ v , v − 1 ] 1 \mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]} .



1.
Introduction

1.1.

For
n\geq 0
, let
A_{n}
be a unital associative algebra over a field
\mathbbm{k}
. Let
A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
and
A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
be the categories of finite-dimensional and finitely generated projective
A_{n}
-modules, respectively, and let
\mathcal{K}(A)
and
\mathcal{G}(A)
be the corresponding Grothendieck groups. Following Bergeron and Li
[
BL09
]
, a
tower of algebras
refers to a direct sum
A=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
of certain finite-dimensional algebras
A_{n}
satisfying certain axioms. These axioms ensure that
\mathcal{K}(A)
and
\mathcal{G}(A)
admit the structure of a graded connected bialgebra (and thus a Hopf algebra) that are Hopf dual to each other with respect to the
\Hom
form:

\mathcal{K}(A)\times\mathcal{G}(A)\to\mathbb{Z},\quad([P],[M])\mapsto\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Hom_{A}(P,M).

Given an element
[P]\in\mathcal{K}(A)
, this Hopf duality allows one to define
r_{P}^{*}
, the adjoint of right multiplication by
[P]
.
It further allows one to define the Heisenberg double (see
[
Lu94
]
) of
\mathcal{G}(A)
as follows:

\mathfrak{h}(A):=\langle\ell_{[M]},r^{*}_{[P]}~|~M\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}},\ P\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}},\ n\geq 0\rangle\subseteq\End_{\mathbbm{k}}(\mathcal{G}(A)),

where
\ell_{M}
is the left multiplication by
[M]
.
The
\mathfrak{h}(A)
-module
\mathcal{G}(A)
is called the
Fock space
representation of
\mathfrak{h}(A)
. In particular, the representation
\mathfrak{h}(A)\curvearrowright\mathcal{G}(A)
recovers the following important examples in representation theory:

(1)

The polynomial representation of the Weyl algebra
W
, i.e.,

\mathfrak{h}(A)\cong W:=\langle x,\partial\rangle\subseteq\End_{\mathbbm{k}}(\mathbb{Z}[x]),

where
A_{n}:=\NC_{n}
is the nilCoxeter algebra introduced by Fomin and Stanley (see
[
FS94
]
).

(2)

The Heisenberg action on symmetric functions, i.e.,

\mathfrak{h}(A)\cong\mathsf{Heis}\subseteq\End_{\mathbbm{k}}(\Sym),

where
A_{n}:=\mathbb{C}[S_{n}]
is the group algebra of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Here,
\Sym
is the ring of symmetric functions in infinitely many variables.

These Fock space representations were categorified by Khovanov in
[
Kho01
,
Kho14
]
, and also Savage and Yacobi in
[
SY15
]
, in the sense that the relations satisfied by the endomorphisms
\ell_{M}
and
r^{*}_{P}
acting on
\mathcal{G}(A)
lift to corresponding relations between certain functors acting on
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
. For example, the relations
[\partial,x]=1
in
W
and
[h_{i}^{*},e_{j}]=e_{j-1}h^{*}_{i-1}
in
\mathsf{Heis}
translate to, respectively:

\Res\Ind\cong\Ind\Res\oplus\mathrm{Id},\quad\Res_{H_{i}}\Ind_{E_{j}}\cong\Ind_{E_{j}}\Res_{H_{i}}\oplus\Ind_{E_{j-1}}\Res_{H_{i-1}},

where
\Res=\Res_{\NC_{n}}^{\NC_{n+1}}
,

\Ind=\Ind_{\NC_{n}}^{\NC_{n+1}}
and
H_{j},E_{j}
are the trivial and sign representations of
S_{j}
, respectively (see
[
SY15
]
for the definition of
\Ind_{E_{j}}
, etc). In particular, given a tower
A
of finite-dimensional algebras satisfying certain conditions, Savage and Yacobi developed a framework for the categorification of the Fock space of
\mathfrak{h}(A)
that unifies both cases above.

1.2.

In this paper, we develop a framework for the categorification of the Fock space of
\mathfrak{h}(\mathscr{A})
, where the tower
\mathscr{A}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
is built from infinite-dimensional algebras
A_{n}
. When the algebra
A_{n}
is finite-dimensional, there is a well known canonical bijection

(1.2.1)

\{\text{indecomposable projective $A_{n}$-modules}\}/\cong\;\;\longleftrightarrow\;\;\{\text{simple $A_{n}$-modules}\}/\cong,

which gives the duality between
\mathcal{K}(A)
and
\mathcal{G}(A)
. When the algebra
A_{n}
is infinite-dimensional, this correspondence no longer holds. For example, the polynomial algebra
A_{n}=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
has one indecomposable projective module, but infinitely many simples. However, if one endows
A_{n}
with the grading
|x_{i}|=1
, and pass to graded modules, then this issue disappears, as
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
only has one graded simple, namely
\mathbbm{k}
.
Thus, we will work with infinite-dimensional algebras
A_{n}
that are
graded
.

To ensure the corresponding bijection (
1.2.1
) in the graded setting, we also require the algebras be
nice
(see
Definition

2.1.2
). Under additional hypotheses (see
Definition

2.2.2
), we prove that
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
are Hopf-dual algebra–coalgebras and given
P\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
,
M\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
, we define functors

\I_{M}:\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}\to\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}\quad\textup{and}\quad\TR_{P}:\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}\to\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}

that categorify
r_{[M]}
and
r_{[P]}^{*}
, respectively.

Examples of (infinite-dimensional) nice graded algebras include the polynomial ring
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
as well as special instances of quantum wreath products
B\wr\mathcal{H}(n)
introduced in
[
LNX24
]
,
in the case the base algebra
B
is infinite-dimensional.
A notable example is the nilHecke algebra
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
. Applying our framework to the nilhecke tower
\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathrm{NH}_{n}
,
we thereby obtain a categorification of the action of quantum differential operators on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}[v,v^{-1}]}
.
More concretely,
recall the quantum integer
(a)_{v}:=\frac{v^{a}-1}{v-1}
and quantum binomial coefficients
\tbinom{a}{b}_{v}:=\prod_{i=0}^{b-1}\frac{(a-i)_{v}}{(b-i)_{v}}
,
we categorify the action of
\mathcal{D}
on
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}][x]
, where

\mathcal{D}:=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}]\langle x,\partial^{(n)}|\,n\geq 0\rangle}{(\partial^{(m)}\partial^{(n)}-\tbinom{m+n}{n}_{v}\partial^{(m+n)},\quad\partial^{(m)}x-v^{m}x\partial^{(m)}-\partial^{(m-1)}~|~m,n\geq 0)}.

Here,
x\in\mathcal{D}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}][x]
by right multiplication, while
\partial^{(m)}\in\mathcal{D}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}[v^{\pm 1}][x]
by

\partial^{(m)}(x^{n})=\tbinom{n}{m}_{v}x^{n-m}.

2.
Infinite-dimensional Towers of Algebras

2.1.
Graded Simple Modules

We begin with an analog of (
1.2.1
) in the graded setting.

Lemma 2.1.1
.

Any graded simple
S
of a positively graded algebra
R=\oplus_{i\geq 0}R_{i}
is a simple module over
R_{0}:=R/R^{+}
.

Proof.

Since
R^{+}S\subseteq S
is a graded
R
-submodule, either
R^{+}S=0
or
R^{+}S=S
. The latter is impossible, as
R^{+}
is positively graded.
∎

Definition 2.1.2
.

A
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebra
R=\oplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\,R_{i}
is called
nice
if the following conditions hold:

(1)

There exists a positively (negatively) graded ideal
I\subseteq\oplus_{i>0}R_{i}
, such that all graded simples of
R
are annihilated by
I
.

(2)

The quotient
R/I
is a finite-dimensional algebra.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

Let
R
be a nice graded algebra. Then there is a bijection

\left\{\begin{tabular}[]{c}graded finitely generated\\
indecomposable projectives of $R$\end{tabular}\right\}/\textnormal{shift }\longleftrightarrow\left\{\textnormal{graded simples of }R\right\}/\textnormal{shift }.

Index either set by
J
. If
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed, then
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(P_{i},S_{j})=\delta_{ij}
for
i\in J
where the indecomposable projective
P_{i}
corresponds to the graded simple
S_{i}
in the bijection above.

Proof.

Given a simple
S_{b}
of
R
,
(1)
says that
S_{b}
is a simple for
R/I
which by
(2)
is a finite-dimensional algebra and thus we have the bijection there.

Given the projective cover
\psi:\overline{P_{b}}\twoheadrightarrow S_{b}
we have that
P_{b}
is a principal indecomposable and so
\overline{P_{b}}=R/I\cdot\overline{e}
for some idempotent
\overline{e}\in R/I
. But
I
is positively graded and thus
\overline{e}=e\in B
already. Thus we can construct

P_{b}:=Re\xrightarrow{\pi}\overline{P_{b}}\xrightarrow{\psi}S_{b}.

IP_{b}\subset J_{gr}(R)P_{b}
where
J_{gr}(R)
is the graded jacobson radical of
R
. Nakayama implies that
J_{gr}(R)P_{b}
is a superfluous submodule of
P_{b}
and thus
IP_{b}=\ker\pi
is a superfluous submodule.
\pi
is a superfluous epimorphism and since the composition of superfluous epimorphisms is a superfluous epimorphism we see that
P_{b}
is the projective cover of
S_{b}
as an
R
module. It follows that
R
is a semiperfect ring and semiperfect rings afford the bijection above along with the calculation of
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}(P_{i},S_{j})
.
∎

2.2.
Tower of Algebras

Given a graded
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra
R
, we will, by a slight abuse of notation, write

R\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
and
R\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
for the categories of
graded
finite-dimensional
R
-modules and
graded
finitely generated projective
R
-modules, respectively.
We further define

G_{0}^{f}(R):=K_{0}(R\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}),\quad K_{\oplus}(R):=K_{0}(R\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}).

Let
F:R\text{-}{\mathrm{mod}}\to R^{op}\text{-}{\mathrm{mod}}
be a graded additive functor sending projective modules to flat modules. Then there is a bilinear pairing

(2.2.1)

\left<-,-\right>_{F}:K_{\oplus}(R)\times G_{0}^{f}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}],\quad\left<[P],[M]\right>:=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}F(P)\otimes_{R}M.

Remark 2.2.1
.

The form
\left(-,-\right)_{\Hom}=\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}\Hom_{R}(P,M)
used in
[
SY15
]
is a special case of the above as follows.
Let
R
be a Frobenius algebra (which is the case for the examples in
[
SY15
]
). Define
F(-):=\Hom_{R}(-,R)
.
Since Frobenius algebras are self-injective,
F
sends projectives to injectives, which coincides with the projectives in
R^{op}
, see
[
SY11
, Chapter IV, Proposition 3.7]
.
With this
F
, we then have
\left<-,-\right>_{F}=\left(-,-\right)_{\Hom}
because

F(P)\otimes_{R}M=\Hom_{R}(P,R)\otimes_{R}M=\Hom_{R}(P,M),

where the second equality follows from the projectivity of
P
.

Definition 2.2.2
.

A graded
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra
\mathscr{A}=\oplus_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
is called a
nice tower of algebras
if the following are satisfied for all
m,n
:

(TA1)

A_{n}
is a nice graded algebra. Moreover,
A_{0}=\mathbbm{k}
.

(TA2)

There is a unital algebra morphism
A_{n}\otimes A_{m}\to A_{n+m}
.

(TA3)

A_{m+n}
is a two-sided projective
A_{n}\otimes A_{m}
module.

(TA4)

There exists an anti-automorphism
\psi
of
A_{n}
.

Given a nice tower of algebras
\mathscr{A}
, define

\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A}):=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geq 0}G_{0}^{f}(A_{n}),\quad\textup{and}\quad\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A}):=\bigoplus\limits_{n\geq 0}K_{\oplus}(A_{n}).

Let
F:A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{mod}}\to A_{n}^{op}\text{-}{\mathrm{mod}}
be the graded additive functor fixing morphisms and objects, where the right
A_{n}
-action is given by
m\cdot^{\psi}a:=\psi(a)\cdot m
.
The functor
F
sends projectives to projectives, so from
Eq.

2.2.1
we have a pairing

(2.2.2)

\left<-,-\right>_{\psi}:K_{\oplus}(A_{n})\times G_{0}^{f}(A_{n})\to\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}],\quad\left<[P],[N]\right>_{\psi}:=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}P^{\psi}\otimes_{A_{n}}N.

Proposition 2.2.3
.

Given a nice tower of algebras
\mathscr{A}
, the pairing
\left<-,-\right>:\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})\times\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
defined by

(2.2.3)

\left<[P],[M]\right>:=\begin{cases}\left<[P],[M]\right>_{\psi}&\textnormal{if }P\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}\textnormal{ and }M\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}};\\
0&\textnormal{otherwise.}\end{cases}

is non-degenerate. If additionally,
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed, then the pairing is perfect.

Proof.

As
G_{0}^{f}(A_{n})
and
K_{\oplus}(A_{m})
are orthogonal if
n\neq m
,
we just need to show non-degeneracy for
n=m
.
Note that
K_{\oplus}(A_{m})
has a
\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
-basis of indecomposable projectives.
(TA1) and
2.1.3
imply all indecomposable projectives of
A_{n}
are projective covers of simples
S_{j}
, and are principal, i.e. of the form
A_{n}e_{j}
for some idempotent
e_{j}
.
As right
A_{n}
-modules, we have
(A_{n}e)^{\psi}\cong\psi(e)A_{n}
sending
be\mapsto\psi(e)\psi(b)
.
Thus

\left<[A_{n}e_{i}],[S_{j}]\right>_{\psi}=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}(\psi(e_{i})A_{n}\otimes_{A_{n}}S_{j})=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}\psi(e_{i})S_{j}=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}\Hom_{A_{n}}(A_{n}\psi(e_{i}),S_{j}).

It follows that
\left\{[A_{n}\psi(e_{i})]\right\}
and
\left\{[S_{j}]\right\}
are orthogonal and if
\mathbbm{k}
is algebraically closed they are dual basis from
2.1.3
.
∎

Remark 2.2.4
.

The pairing (
2.2.3
) coincides with the bilinear pairing defined for KLR algebras in
[
KL09
]
.

Using (TA3) the functors below induce maps
\left[\mathrm{IND}\right],\left[\eta\right]
on
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{IND}:\mathscr{A}^{\otimes 2}-\mathrm{mod}\to\mathscr{A}-\mathrm{mod}

\displaystyle\eta:\mathbbm{k}-\mathrm{mod}\to\mathscr{A}-\mathrm{mod}\phantom{,}

\displaystyle\mathrm{IND}|_{A_{m}\otimes A_{n}-{\mathrm{mod}}}=\Ind_{A_{m}\otimes A_{n}}^{A_{m+n}}

\displaystyle\eta(V)=V\textnormal{ for }V\in\mathbbm{k}-{\mathrm{mod}}.

Lemma 2.2.5
.

\left[\mathrm{IND}\right]
turns
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})
into unital associative algebras with unit
\left[\eta\right]
.

Similarly, the functors below induce maps
\left[\mathrm{RES}\right],\left[\epsilon\right]
on
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})
.

\displaystyle\mathrm{RES}:\mathscr{A}-\mathrm{mod}\to\mathscr{A}^{\otimes 2}-\mathrm{mod}

\displaystyle\epsilon:\mathscr{A}-\mathrm{mod}\to\mathbbm{k}-\mathrm{mod}\phantom{,}

\displaystyle\mathrm{RES}|_{A_{n}-{\mathrm{mod}}}=\bigoplus_{k+\ell=n}\Res_{A_{k}\otimes A_{\ell}}^{A_{n}}

\displaystyle\epsilon(V)=\begin{cases}V&\textnormal{ if }V\in\mathbbm{k}-{\mathrm{mod}},\\
0&\textnormal{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Lemma 2.2.6
.

\left[\mathrm{RES}\right]
turns
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
and
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})
into counital coassociative coalgebras with counit
\left[\epsilon\right]
.

2.3.
The Bialgebra pairing

For the rest of the article, we abbreviate
\boxtimes=\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

A pairing
\left<-,-\right>:H\times K\to\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
between two bialgebras
H
and
K
is a bialgebra pairing if the following hold, for
p,p^{\prime}\in H
,
m,m^{\prime}\in K
:

(2.3.1)

\displaystyle\langle pp^{\prime},m\rangle=\langle p\boxtimes p^{\prime},\Delta_{K}(m)\rangle,

(2.3.2)

\displaystyle\langle p,mm^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\Delta_{H}(p),m\boxtimes m^{\prime}\rangle,

(2.3.3)

\displaystyle\langle 1_{H},m\rangle=\epsilon_{K}(m),\quad\langle p,1_{K}\rangle=\epsilon_{H}(p).

where
\left<-,-\right>
on the RHS of (
2.3.1
) and (
2.3.2
) is given by

\left<p\boxtimes p^{\prime},k\boxtimes k^{\prime}\right>=\left<p,k\right>\left<p^{\prime},k^{\prime}\right>.

Remark 2.3.2
.

Additionally, if
H,K
are connected Hopf algebras, then a bialgebra pairing is automatically a Hopf pairing.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Given a nice tower of algebras
\mathscr{A}
, the pairing
\left<-,-\right>:\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})\times\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})\to\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]
from (
2.2.3
) is a bialgebra pairing.

Proof.

First note that (
2.3.1
) for
\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})\times\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
can be rephrased as

\left<[P]\boxtimes[P^{\prime}],[M]\boxtimes[M^{\prime}]\right>=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}P^{\psi}\boxtimes P^{\prime\psi}\otimes_{A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m}}M\boxtimes M^{\prime}.

Now we show that (
2.3.1
) holds. Given
P\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
,
P^{\prime}\in A_{m}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
, and
M\in A_{n+m}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
, we may assume that both
P
and
P^{\prime}
are indecomposable.
It follows from (TA1) and
2.1.3
that
P=A_{n}e,P^{\prime}=A_{m}e^{\prime}
for some
e
,
e^{\prime}
. Then,

(2.3.4)

\begin{split}\Big(A_{n+m}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m}}P\boxtimes P^{\prime}\Big)^{\psi}&=\Big(A_{n+m}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m}}(A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m})e\boxtimes e^{\prime}\Big)^{\psi}\\
&=(\psi(e)\boxtimes\psi(e^{\prime}))A_{n+m}=(P^{\psi}\boxtimes P^{\prime}{}^{\psi})\otimes_{A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m}}A_{n+m}.\end{split}

Thus,

\displaystyle\left<[\mathrm{IND}](P\boxtimes P^{\prime}),M\right>\overset{\eqref{spliteq}}{=\joinrel=}\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}\big((P^{\psi}\boxtimes P^{\prime}{}^{\psi})\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{A_{n}\boxtimes A_{m}}A_{n+m}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{A_{n+m}}M\big)=\left<P\boxtimes P^{\prime},[\mathrm{RES}](M)\right>,

where we have used that the pairing vanishes on
\Res^{A_{n+m}}_{A_{k}\otimes A_{\ell}}
if
k\neq m,\ell\neq n
.
Equation (
2.3.2
) proceeds similarly.
Lastly, (
2.3.3
) follows directly from definition.
∎

Remark 2.3.4
.

If one uses
\left<-,-\right>_{\Hom}
instead, then the
verification of (
2.3.2
) does not proceed similarly.
In order to ensure the bialgebra pairing structure,
one needs to impose different conditions on
A_{n}
(such as being a Frobenius algebra).
For details, see
[
SY15
, Proposition 3.7]
and
[
BL09
, Proposition 3.3]
.

2.4.
Categorification of Fock Space

For
M\in A_{m}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define the functor
\I_{M}:\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}\to\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}

by

\I_{M}|_{A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}}(N)\;=\;\Ind^{A_{n+m}}_{A_{n}\otimes A_{m}}\!\bigl(N\boxtimes M\bigr)\;\in\;A_{m+n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}},\qquad N\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}},\;n\in\mathbb{N}.

For each
P\in A_{p}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}
, define the functor

\TR_{P}:\;\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}\to\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}

by

\TR_{P}|_{A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}}(N)\;=\;P^{\psi}\otimes_{A_{p}}\Res^{A_{n}}_{A_{\,n-p}\otimes A_{p}}\!N\;\in\;A_{\,n-p}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}},\qquad N\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}},\;n\in\mathbb{N},

where
\TR_{P}(N)
is interpreted as the zero object of
\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
whenever
n-p<0
.

Remark 2.4.1
.

The
A_{p}
-module structure on
\Res^{A_{n}}_{A_{\,n-p}\otimes A_{p}}
comes from the embedding
A_{p}\hookrightarrow A_{\,n-p}\otimes A_{p}
,
a\mapsto 1\otimes a
, while the
A_{n-p}
-module structure on
\TR_{P}(N)
is given by

(2.4.1)

a\cdot(x\otimes n)\;:=\;x\otimes\bigl((a\otimes 1)\cdot n\bigr),\qquad a\in A_{n-p},\;x\in P^{\psi},\;n\in N.

If
P=A_{p}e
is a principal projective, then

\TR_{A_{p}e}(N)=\Hom_{A_{p}}(A_{p}\psi(e),\Res^{A_{n}}_{A_{n-p}\otimes A_{p}}\,N)=\Res_{A_{p}\psi(e)}(N),

where
\Res_{P}
is the functor defined in
[
SY15
, Section 3.4]
. Both
\I_{M}
and
\TR_{P}
are exact and thus induce maps
[\I_{M}],[\TR_{P}]
on
\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})
.
Note that
\I_{M}
categorifies the operator of right multiplication by
[M]
.

Lemma 2.4.2
.

For
M\in\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
and
N\in\mathscr{A}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
,

[\I_{M}](N)=[\mathrm{IND}](N\boxtimes M).

In contrast, the situation for
\TR_{P}
is less clear, but we claim that it categorifies the adjoint of right multiplication by
[P]
.

Proposition 2.4.3
.

For
P\in A_{p}\text{-}{\mathrm{pmod}}
and
N\in A_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}
,

r_{[P]}^{*}\left([N]\right)\;=\;\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }p>n,\\[6.0pt]
\bigl[\TR_{P}(N)],&\text{if }p\leq n.\end{cases}

Proof.

We proceed similarly to
[

, Lemma 3.10]
. For

\displaystyle\langle[P_{1}],r_{[P]}^{*}\left([N]\right)\rangle

\displaystyle:=\left<[P_{1}][P],[N]\right>=\left<P_{1}\boxtimes P,[\mathrm{RES}](N)\right>

\displaystyle=\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}(P_{1}^{\psi}\boxtimes P^{\psi})\otimes_{A_{n-p}\boxtimes A_{p}}\Res^{A_{n}}_{A_{n-p}\otimes A_{p}}(N)

\displaystyle\overset{\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{tendist}}{=\joinrel=\joinrel=}\mathrm{gdim}_{\mathbbm{k}}P_{1}^{\psi}\otimes_{A_{n-p}}P^{\psi}\otimes_{A_{p}}\Res^{A_{n}}_{A_{n-p}\otimes A_{p}}(N)=\left<[P_{1}],[\TR_{P}(N)]\right>.

Since
\left<-,-\right>
is non-degenerate, the proof concludes.

Lemma 2.4.4
.

Let

be
\mathbbm{k}
-algebras,

a right

Theorem 2.4.5
.

For all

and

, we have the following isomorphisms of functors:

\displaystyle\I_{M}\circ\I_{N}

\displaystyle\cong\I_{\mathrm{IND}(N\boxtimes M)},

(2.4.3)

3.
NilHecke Algebras

3.1.
Towers of NilHecke Algebras

Let
\mathscr{A}=\oplus_{n}\mathrm{NH}_{n}
, where
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
is the graded
\mathbbm{k}
-algebra generated by
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
and
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n-1}
where
|x_{i}|:=2
and
|\partial_{i}|:=-2
, with the following relations:

\displaystyle\partial_{i}^{\,2}=0,\quad\partial_{i}\,\partial_{i+1}\,\partial_{i}=\partial_{i+1}\,\partial_{i}\,\partial_{i+1},\quad\partial_{i}\,\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\,\partial_{i},\quad x_{i}\,\partial_{j}=\partial_{j}\,x_{i},\quad(\textup{for }|i-j|>1)

(3.1.1)

\displaystyle x_{i}\,\partial_{i}\;-\;\partial_{i}\,x_{i+1}=1,\qquad\partial_{i}\,x_{i}\;-\;x_{i+1}\,\partial_{i}=1.

Let us recall some facts about
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
from
[
KL09
]
.
Using the diagrammatic interpretation of
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
therein,
the map
\mathrm{NH}_{n}\otimes\mathrm{NH}_{m}\to\mathrm{NH}_{n+m}
is given by stacking diagrams next to each other.

[
KL09
]
shows that
\mathrm{NH}_{n+m}
is a two sided projective
\mathrm{NH}_{n}\otimes\mathrm{NH}_{m}
module.
Moreover, there is an anti-automorphism
\psi
of
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
which flips diagrams upside down. The Grothendieck groups can be described as follows.

Proposition 3.1.1
.

(a)

K_{\oplus}(\mathrm{NH}_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}][P_{n}]
,
where
P_{n}=\mathrm{NH}_{n}\psi(e_{n})
.
Here,
e_{n}
is the idempotent
e_{n}:=x_{1}^{n-1}x_{2}^{n-2}\ldots x_{n-1}\partial_{w_{0}}
and
w_{0}\in S_{n}
is the longest element.

(b)

G_{0}^{f}(\mathrm{NH}_{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q^{\pm 1}][L_{n}]
,
where
L_{n}=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]/(\mathrm{Sym}_{n}^{+})
. Moreover,
\dim_{\mathbbm{k}}L_{n}=n!
.
The action of
x_{i}\in\mathrm{NH}_{n}
on
L_{n}
is given by multiplication by
x_{i}
, while the action of
\partial_{i}
is given by the divided difference operator
\partial_{i}(f)=\frac{f-s_{i}(f)}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}
.

Thus,
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
is a nice graded algebra with
I=\mathrm{Sym}_{m}^{+}
. It follows that the nilHecke tower
\oplus_{n}\mathrm{NH}_{n}
is a nice tower of algebras. Denote the symmetrized quantum binomial coefficients by and
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a}{b}_{q}:=\prod_{i=0}^{b-1}\frac{[a-i]_{q}!}{[b-i]_{q}}
, where
[a]_{q}:=\frac{q^{a}-q^{-a}}{q-q^{-1}}
.

Proposition 3.1.2

(
[
KL09
]
)
.

Let
\widetilde{P}_{n}=q^{-\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}P_{n}
. Then,

\mathrm{IND}(\widetilde{P}_{n}\boxtimes\widetilde{P}_{m})\cong\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n+m}{n}_{q}\widetilde{P}_{n+m}
.

Proposition 3.1.3
.

(a)

For all
n,m
, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{IND}(P_{n}\boxtimes P_{m})=\tbinom{n+m}{n}_{q^{-2}}P_{n+m}
.
Consequently,
\left[P_{n}\right]\mapsto t^{(n)}:=\frac{t^{n}}{(n)_{q^{-2}}!}
defines an isomorphism

\mathcal{K}(\mathscr{A})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}][t^{(n)}|\,n\geq 1].

(b)

For all
n,m
, there is an isomorphism
\mathrm{IND}(L_{n}\boxtimes L_{m})\cong L_{n+m}
.
Consequently,
[L_{n}]\mapsto t^{n}
defines an isomorphism

\mathcal{G}^{f}(\mathscr{A})\cong\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}][t].

Proof.

Part (a) follows from combining
3.1.2
and the equality
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m+n}{n}_{q}=q^{mn}\tbinom{m+n}{n}_{q^{-2}}
.

For (b), one checks that the map

\displaystyle\mathrm{NH}_{n+m}\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathrm{NH}_{m}}\scalebox{0.9}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\frac{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]}{(\mathrm{Sym}_{n}^{+})}\boxtimes\frac{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]}{(\mathrm{Sym}_{m}^{+})}$}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\scalebox{0.9}{\mbox{$\displaystyle\frac{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+m}]}{(\mathrm{Sym}_{n+m}^{+})}$}}

\displaystyle T\otimes(a\boxtimes b)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow T(ab)

is well-defined, surjective, and so by dimension reasons must be an isomorphism.
∎

3.2.
Commutation Relations

Let
P=P_{1}:=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
,
and let
M=L_{1}:=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]/(x_{1})=\mathbbm{k}
, the unique graded simple of
\mathrm{NH}_{1}=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
. Define functors

\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}:=\I_{\mathbbm{k}}|_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}},\quad\textup{and}\quad\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}:=\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}|_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}}.

By definition,
\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}=\Ind_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}^{\mathrm{NH}_{n+1}}(-\boxtimes\mathbbm{k})
and
\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}=\Res_{\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n}]}^{\mathrm{NH}_{n}}(-)
. Thus, we see that:

•

The functor
\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}
is represented by the
(\mathrm{NH}_{n+1},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-bimodule
\mathrm{NH}_{n+1}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}(\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k})
.

•

The functor
\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}

is represented by the

(\mathrm{NH}_{n-1},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-bimodule

({}_{1\to n-1}\mathrm{NH}_{n})
, which is
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
regarded as a left
\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}
-module acting on strands
1
through
n-1
.

Example
.

For
n=1
,
\mathrm{NH}_{1}=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
. As
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-bimodules,

\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{1}={}_{1\to 1}\mathrm{NH}_{2}\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{1}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{2}]}(\mathrm{NH}_{1}\otimes\mathbbm{k})={}_{1\to 1}\mathrm{NH}_{2}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{2}]}(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}).

Similarily, as
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-bimodules,

\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{1}=\mathrm{NH}_{1}\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{0}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}({}_{1\to 0}\mathrm{NH}_{1}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k})=\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}\otimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}({}_{1\to 0}\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\boxtimes\mathbbm{k})={}^{0}\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],

where
{}^{0}\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
is the
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-bimodule where
x_{1}
acts by 0 on the left.
Since
\mathrm{NH}_{2}
has a
\mathbbm{k}
-basis
\left\{x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}\right\}_{a,b\geq 0}\sqcup\left\{\partial_{1}x_{1}^{a}x_{2}^{b}\right\}_{a,b\geq 0}
,
the right-hand side of (
3.2.1
) has a
\mathbbm{k}
-basis

S\sqcup Q
given by

S:=\left\{1\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{2}]}x_{1}^{b}\right\}_{b\geq 0},\qquad Q:=\left\{\partial_{1}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{2}]}x_{1}^{b}\right\}_{b\geq 0}.

Note that
S
is a sub
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-module isomorphic to
\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
and we thus have a short exact sequence of
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-bimodules:

0\to\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]\to\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}|_{\mathrm{NH}_{1}\text{-}{\mathrm{fmod}}}\to Q\to 0.

Using the relation
x_{1}\partial_{1}=\partial_{1}x_{2}+1
, we see that
Q
is isomorphic to
{}^{0}\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]
, without grading considerations.
Moreover, after taking grading into account,
Q\cong q^{-2}\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{1}
as graded
(\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}],\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}])
-bimodules.
In general, we have that:

Lemma 3.2.1
.

As
(\mathrm{NH}_{n},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-bimodules,

\displaystyle\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}

\displaystyle={}_{1\to n}\mathrm{NH}_{n+1}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}(\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}),

\displaystyle\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{NH}_{n}\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n}]}({}_{1\to n-1}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}).

Moreover, there is a nonsplit short exact sequence of graded
(\mathrm{NH}_{n},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-bimodules

0\to\mathrm{NH}_{n}\to\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}\to q^{-2}\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}\to 0.

Proof.

Left coset representatives of
S_{n}
in
S_{n+1}
are of the form
\left\{e,s_{n},s_{n-1}s_{n},\ldots,s_{1}\ldots s_{n}\right\}
. Thus, we obtain the following vector space decompositions:

\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}=\left\{1\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\right\}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\left\{\partial_{1}\ldots\partial_{n-1}\partial_{n}\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\right\},

\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}=\left\{1\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n}]}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\right\}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\left\{\partial_{1}\ldots\partial_{n-1}\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n}]}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\right\}.

Note that
S:=\left\{1\otimes_{\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}[x_{n+1}]}\mathrm{NH}_{n}\boxtimes\mathbbm{k}\right\}
is a
(\mathrm{NH}_{n},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-submodule of
\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}
that is isomorphic to
\mathrm{NH}_{n}
.
Therefore, there is an exact sequence:

0\to\mathrm{NH}_{n}\to\left(\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\circ\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\right)_{n}\to Q\to 0.

Define a map
\Phi:\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}\to Q
by
v\otimes w\mapsto v\partial_{n}\otimes w
.
We first check that this map is well defined.
Let
v,w\in\mathrm{NH}_{n}
.
Then,
vx_{n}\otimes w=v\otimes x_{n}w=0
in
\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}
.
Next, we compute that

\Phi(vx_{n}\otimes w)=vx_{n}\partial_{n}\otimes w=v\partial_{n}x_{n+1}\otimes w+v\otimes w=v\partial_{n}\otimes x_{n+1}w+1\otimes vw\sim 0\ \textup{in}\ Q.

Similarly, for
n\in\mathrm{NH}_{n-1}
,
vn\otimes w=v\otimes nw
.
We further check that

\Phi(vn\otimes w)=vn\partial_{n}\otimes w=v\partial_{n}\otimes nw=\Phi(v\otimes nw).

By construction,
\Phi
is a morphism of
(\mathrm{NH}_{n},\mathrm{NH}_{n})
-bimodules.
By the vector space decomposition above,
\Phi
is bijective, and thus we have an isomorphism
q^{-2}\left(\I_{\mathbbm{k}}\circ\TR_{\mathbbm{k}[x_{1}]}\right)_{n}\cong Q
.
∎

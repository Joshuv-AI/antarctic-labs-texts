Surface water quality classification and prediction model based on multiple machine learning algorithms
Authors: Gao Man, Qian Yun, Zhang Qilin, Liu Yuyong, Zhang Zhuoshi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In the context of worsening global water scarcity and prominent water pollution issues, precise water quality assessment and prediction are crucial for water resource management. Addressing the shortcomings of traditional methods in multi-parameter collaborative analysis, class imbalance handling, and nonlinear fitting, this study focuses on Heilongjiang Province, China, to construct a multi-parameter watershed water quality level prediction system comprising “data preprocessing—feature dimensionality reduction—Model Optimization” multi-parameter watershed water quality level prediction system, combining Principal Component Analysis (PCA), C4.5 decision tree, Backpropagation (BP) neural network, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for the study. The study collected 31,289 samples from 23 monitoring stations in the region between May and October 2023. After balancing the data using SMOTE oversampling and retaining information through PCA dimension reduction, the data were input into the four models. The results showed that PCA-C4.5 achieved an total accuracy of 87.26% but was weak in identifying small samples, while PCA-BP achieved the best total accuracy of 94.52%. PCA-CNN (93.27%) was good at capturing local features, and PCA-LSTM (93.42%) was good at capturing temporal patterns. This study confirms the feasibility of comparing and optimizing multiple algorithms (including C4.5 decision tree, BP neural network, CNN, and LSTM combined with PCA dimensionality reduction), providing a new method for watershed monitoring. Future research could incorporate attention mechanisms or multi-source data to enhance model performance.

Introduction

In the face of increasingly severe global environmental issues, the importance of water resources has become increasingly apparent. Water, as the source of life, is a fundamental element of ecological sustainability. However, the current global water shortage situation is becoming increasingly severe, and water pollution is also worsening1. A relevant United Nations report points out that nearly 2 billion people worldwide live in areas with severe water shortages, and water scarcity has become a normal part of their lives2.

With the rapid development of industry and the continuous growth of the population, large amounts of untreated industrial wastewater and domestic sewage have been discharged into water bodies, leading to increasingly severe water pollution issues . Additionally, climate change has exacerbated the uneven distribution of water resources by increasing the frequency of extreme weather events, and in some regions, it has even caused alternating floods and droughts, further worsening the shortage of water resources . The 2014 benzene contamination incident in Lanzhou, China, is a prime example. This incident not only disrupted residents’ access to clean water and disrupted their daily lives but also posed potential health risks to residents. It highlighted the urgency of ensuring water quality and revealed the core value of precise water quality assessment and prediction in water resource management3.

In the field of water quality research, water quality classification has attracted much attention as a key basis for judging the health status of water bodies and formu-lating precise management strategies. Earlier, the single-factor pollution index evalua-tion method4 was widely used in water quality assessment. However, this method only considers the impact of a single pollutant and ignores the integrated interaction between multiple pollutants, which easily leads to one-sided perception of water qual-ity conditions and cannot provide a reliable basis for comprehensive management.

In view of the limitations of the single-factor pollution index evaluation method, the comprehensive pollution index evaluation method came into being5. Although this method attempts to cover a variety of pollutant factors, it often relies excessively on subjective judgment when determining the weight of each pollutant. Different re-searchers assign values based on their own experience or limited industry consensus, and there is a lack of uniform and objective standards, making it difficult to accurately reflect the actual impact of each pollutant on water quality. The traditional support vector machine prediction method6 is often inefficient when facing large-scale water quality data. Due to its high computational complexity, it is time-consuming to deal with large amounts of data, and it is difficult to meet the high real-time requirements of water quality monitoring and management needs.

Traditional methods such as single-factor evaluation and comprehensive pollution index have limitations such as weak multi-parameter collaborative analysis ability and insufficient nonlinear fitting in the multi-parameter water quality prediction of river basins, and are difficult to meet the requirements of “high precision, dynamics and comprehensiveness”. Machine learning (such as artificial neural networks and random forests) can dig out the hidden correlation between water quality parameters and grades with its strong nonlinear mapping ability. Deep learning (such as CNN being good at extracting local features and LSTM being able to capture temporal patterns) can effectively deal with multi-parameter collaboration problems. SMOTE oversampling can also solve the problem of class imbalance. However, current research still has problems such as insufficient recognition of small sample categories and the need to improve generalization and timeliness. Therefore, it is necessary to explore better methods. This study aims to construct a multi-parameter basin water quality level prediction model based on ensemble algorithms to address the shortcomings of traditional methods. Specifically, PCA is used to reduce the dimension of multi-parameter data to optimize the feature space and improve computational efficiency. The dimensionally reduced data was input into four types of models, namely PCA-C4.5 decision tree, PCA-BP neural network, PCA-CNN, and PCA-LSTM, for comparison and screening of the optimal model, and the feasibility of the integrated algorithm was verified, providing a new method for dynamic monitoring of water quality in river basins.

The subsequent sections of this manuscript are systematically organized to facili-tate a comprehensive understanding of this study. The second part is a literature review, combing the relevant research progress in the field of water quality assessment and prediction, analyzing the shortcomings of traditional methods and the current status of the application of new technologies. The third part introduces the materials and methods of the study, including data sources, data preprocessing methods, and the principles of the models applied. The fourth part shows and analyzes the prediction results of each model, evaluates the performance of the model in terms of accuracy, precision, recall, F1 value and total accuracy , and finally draws the conclusion of the research and proposes the future research direction for the problems of the model.

To achieve more accurate water quality prediction, this paper first uses Principal Component Analysis (PCA) to reduce the dimensionality of high-dimensional water quality parameters, and then constructs models by combining four different algorithms, namely C4.5 decision tree, Backpropagation (BP) neural network, Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), and Long Short-Term Memory network (LSTM), respectively. Finally, the performances of these four independent models (PCA-C4.5, PCA-BP, PCA-CNN, PCA-LSTM) are compared and analyzed to explore the differences in applicability of different algorithms in water quality prediction tasks. The literature review

Traditional water quality prediction methods

During the research history of water quality assessment and prediction, many scholars have been exploring new methods and techniques to promote the continuous development of this field. Early on, the single-factor pollution index (SFPI) method occupied an important position in water quality assessment.Xiaokang Xin et al.7, in a comprehensive study on the water quality of Danjiangkou Reservoir, utilized the SFPI method to assess the current status of the water quality based on the determination of 17 water quality indicators in 120 water samples collected from 20 monitoring sites. The study successfully revealed the main indicators affecting the water quality of Dan-jiangkou Reservoir, while using a two-dimensional water quality numerical model to predict the distribution of the main water quality indicators, and the results showed that if the pollutants were not controlled, the influence of the tributaries would exceed half of the Han River Reservoir.Zhihao Tang et al.8 used the single-factor evaluation method and the integrated pollution index evaluation method for the near-shore waters of Jiangsu Province, in the period of 2017–2023, to water environment factors and water quality, and analyzed the interrelationships between water ecological factors.Haizhen Hu et al.9used a combination of single-factor pollution index method and multivari-ate statistical analysis methods, such as principal component analysis and cluster analysis, to evaluate water quality samples from 2017 to 2021 in a typical plain river network area of Changzhou City, Jiangsu Province, China, and found that the overall water quality was mildly polluted with an improving The overall water quality was found to be mildly polluted with an improving trend, total phosphorus and ammonia nitrogen were identified as the main pollutants, and organic pollution, self-purification capacity and eutrophication of the water body were judged to be the key influencing factors, and the sampling sites were classified into three categories, thus providing theoretical references to water environment management and ecological protection in this type of area.

In view of the limitations of the single-factor pollution index evaluation method, the comprehensive pollution index evaluation method came into being. Gaviria et al10 used six wastewater quality indices, including the comprehensive pollution index method, combined with multivariate statistical analysis, to comprehensively evaluate the leaching wastewater of Colombian gold mines, to clarify the main pollutants and to provide a basis for the local wastewater management strategy.M.N. Hossain for the assess the pollution and water quality of produced and discharged water from the gas field in northeastern Bangladesh, a variety of indices and statistical methods such as weighted pollution index, potential ecological risk, heavy metal toxicity load, entro-py-based water quality index, combined with physicochemical analyses of standard procedures, the results showed that there is light to moderate pollution in the gas field area, the pollution of heavy metals is variable, and the produced and discharged water is unsuitable for drinking.

Machine learning water quality prediction methods

With the development of technology, the field of machine learning brings new opportunities for water quality assessment. With the excellent nonlinear mapping ability of neural network, through the deep learning of known water quality categories and their corresponding multi-indicator data, the intrinsic relationship between water quality indicators and water quality categories can be explored, so as to realize the ac-curate prediction of unknown water quality data categories. Numerous scholars have carried out extensive exploration in this direction. F. Rustam et al11 constructed a simple structure of neural network, which can take into account the water quality and water consumption prediction with higher efficiency and accuracy. The network is an artificial neural network (ANN) containing a hidden layer with several random deac-tivation and activation layers. The researchers tested it using datasets of water quality prediction and water quantity prediction. Yanna Xiong et al12 solved the prob-lem of water quality classification based on the Random Forest (RFWQI) model com-bined with the groundwater quality indexes and data.

Masood13, in assessing the water quality of Nambourg River, the second longest river in Ukraine, studied the ex-plored ten machine learning models (e.g., artificial neural network, support vector re-gression, etc.) applied to estimate the water quality index, and the results showed that all the models performed well in prediction, with the Gaussian process being the best, followed by, support vector regressor, and K-nearest neighbor algorithm, and artificial neural network and AdaBoost were relatively weaker, which confirmed that the ma-chine learning models can predict WQI effectively to assist in the management of wa-ter quality. Chinnappan14 used decision tree algorithm to analyze the real-time updated chlorine concentration from sensors to monitor the residual chlorine concen-tration in water.

Quang15 constructed a machine learning model based on Sentinel-2 remote sensing data, such as random forest, to predict coastal water quality parameters, and the accuracy of the model was significantly im-proved after atmospheric correction (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\hbox {R}^{2}$$\end{document} up to 0.74), of which the chlorophyll a predic-tion was the best, and the parameters of total suspended solids and chemical oxygen demand were related to estuarine and urban tourism areas.

Materials and methods

Study area and data

This study focuses on Heilongjiang Province in China, a region with numerous rivers, reservoirs, and lakes. The study collected water quality data from rivers, reservoirs, and lakes in the region between May and October 2023. This period spans the transition from spring to summer and into autumn, during which factors such as temperature, precipitation, and industrial and agricultural activities undergo significant changes, resulting in complex impacts on water quality. This timeframe provides a relatively comprehensive reflection of the dynamic changes in water quality in the region.

The study selected 23 monitoring stations, which are distributed across rivers, reservoirs, and lakes in different geographical locations within the region, including the upstream, midstream, and downstream sections of rivers, as well as reservoirs and lakes of varying sizes and functions. This ensures that the collected data is broadly representative and reflects the water quality characteristics of different water bodies in the region. Among numerous water quality indicators, this study selected nine key water quality parameters: water temperature (WT), pH value, dissolved oxygen (DO), chemical oxygen demand (CODMn), ammonia nitrogen (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$NH_4^+-N$$\end{document}), total phosphorus (TP), total nitrogen (TN), electrical conductivity (EC), and turbidity (NTU). These indicators were measured using temperature sensors, pH automatic online analyzers, dissolved oxygen automatic online analyzers, CODMn automatic online analyzers (the testing method follows the National Environmental Protection Bureau’s “GB11892-1989 Water Quality - Determination of Permanganate Index”, ammonia nitrogen fully automatic online analyzer (testing method based on the National Environmental Protection Bureau’s “HJ536-2009 Water Quality - Determination of Ammonia Nitrogen by Salicylic Acid Spectrophotometry”, total phosphorus automatic online analyzer (testing method based on the National Environmental Protection Bureau’s “GB11893-1989 Water Quality - Determination of Total Phosphorus by Ammonium Molybdate Spectrophotometry”), Total Nitrogen Automatic Online Analyzer (testing method based on the National Environmental Protection Bureau’s “HJ636-2012 Water Quality—Determination of Total Nitrogen by Potassium Persulfate Digestion and Ultraviolet Spectrophotometry”), Conductivity Automatic Online Analyzer, Turbidity Automatic Online Analyzer to obtain indicator data. In the event of instrument malfunction or damage, samples will be collected on-site and brought back to the laboratory for testing. Laboratory testing methods also adhere to the corresponding standards mentioned above. Sampling frequency is four times per day, with samples collected every 6 h.

The classification of water quality categories I–VI in this study strictly adheres to the National Standard of the People’s Republic of China, “Surface Water Environmental Quality Standard” (GB3838-2002). This standard holds authority and regulatory significance in water pollution prevention, surface water quality protection, safeguarding human health, and maintaining healthy ecosystems. In addition, in this study, seven key indicators including water temperature (WT), pH value, dissolved oxygen (DO), permanganate index (CODMn), ammonia nitrogen (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$NH_4^+-N$$\end{document}), total phosphorus (TP), and total nitrogen (TN) were clearly defined and specified with standard limit requirements for different water quality categories in GB3838-2002, providing scientific data for subsequent model training. Furthermore, the sample distribution presented in Table 1 (e.g., only 274 Class I samples versus 11,486 Class III samples) accurately reflects the inherent imbalance in water quality categories observed during field monitoring. This objectively existing distribution characteristic was optimized during the model training phase using the SMOTE algorithm, effectively enhancing the model’s ability to recognize small sample categories and improving its overall generalization performance.

Data preprocessing

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a widely used technique for dimensionality reduction. Its core principle is to transform a set of variables that may be interrelated into a set of new variables that are not linearly related to each other with the help of orthogonal transformation, and these new variables are called principal components.

In the field of river and lake water quality research, the water quality condition is influenced by a variety of factors and involves many water quality parameters. In this study, in order to accurately characterize the water quality of rivers and lakes, nine representative key water quality parameters were carefully selected from a large number of indicators, namely, water temperature (WT), pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), electrical conductivity (EC), turbidity (NTU), chemical oxygen demand (CODMn), ammonia nitrogen (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$NH_4^+-N$$\end{document}), total phosphorus (TP) and total nitrogen (TN). To mitigate the potential impact of data set category imbalance on model prediction performance, this study employs the SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique) oversampling algorithm for preprocessing the training data. For categories with fewer samples, SMOTE expands the dataset by synthesizing new samples among the K nearest neighbors (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\text {K}=5$$\end{document} in this study) of the minority class samples, rather than simply replicating the original samples, to preserve the distribution characteristics of the minority class data. Specifically, 1275 new samples were synthesized for Class I, bringing the total to 1,500; 1199 new samples were synthesized for Class VI, bringing the total to 3,000; For categories with sufficient sample sizes, the original data were retained to avoid losing critical information from the majority class due to oversampling. After processing, the total sample size of the dataset was 33763, with the sample proportions of each category adjusted to I:II:III:IV:V:VI \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx$$\end{document} 1:2.29:6.15:4.93:1.97:2.The training set processed by SMOTE is used for the dimensionality reduction of PCA and the subsequent training of PCA-C4.5 decision tree, PCA-BP neural network, PCA-CNN, and PCA-LSTM models to avoid the risk of data leakage, improve the recognition ability of the model for a few categories, and reduce the prediction bias caused by category imbalance.

To ensure the objectivity and fairness of the performance comparison of PCA-C4.5, PCA-BP, PCA-CNN, and PCA-LSTM models, all models use completely consistent training sets and test set partitioning schemes, with a partitioning ratio of about 4.4:1 and a fixed number of random seeds of 42 to ensure experimental reproducibility and avoid errors in model accuracy evaluation results due to differences in data partitioning.

PCA-C4.5 model

Based on the C4.5 decision tree algorithm, when dealing with multi-feature classi-fication tasks, it can effectively capture the logical relationship between data features and categories by constructing a tree-shaped decision structure, and thus it is estab-lished as the core prediction model.

The data after PCA dimensionality reduction is used as the input features and imported into the C4.5 decision tree model to carry out the classification prediction task. PCA-BP model

BP neural network is a multi-layer feedforward network that achieves self-optimization through the error backpropagation algorithm. It consists of an input layer, hidden layers , and an output layer. When constructing the water quality classification prediction model, 30,000 water quality samples were systematically analyzed, and descriptive statistics and correlation analysis were used to investigate the dimensional characteristics, distribution patterns, and the coupling relationship among the nine original indicators, such as total phosphorus (TP), ammonia nitrogen (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$NH_4^+-N$$\end{document}), dissolved oxygen (DO), etc. The BP neural network model is a typical multi-layer feed-forward neural network, which is composed of input and output layers and implicit layers, and shows excellent performance in dealing with nonlinear mapping problems. In view of the fact that water quality classification is a highly nonlinear problem, the BP neural network has a strong nonlinear fitting ability and can establish complex mapping relationships by learning the intrinsic laws of the data, so it is established as the core prediction model.

Considering the high dimensionality and redundancy of information in the origi-nal data, the Principal Component Analysis (PCA) method is introduced to implement the dimensionality reduction process based on the inherent characteristics of the data. The data after PCA dimensionality reduction is used as an input layer node and fed into the BP neural network model to carry out the classification prediction task. The model adopts the gradient descent optimization algorithm with adaptive learning rate and momentum factor, and iteratively updates the network parameters through the backpropagation mechanism. In the training process, the model calculates the gradient direction of each connection weight and bias term based on the error between the pre-dicted value and the true value using the principle of gradient descent, and adjusts the parameters along the opposite direction of the gradient to minimize the loss function (usually using the mean square error function). The introduction of momentum factor can effectively inhibit the oscillation phenomenon in the process of parameter updat-ing and accelerate the convergence of the model; the adaptive learning rate dynami-cally adjusts the step size according to the training process to improve the training ef-ficiency. After several rounds of iterative search optimization, the model gradually learns the mapping relationship between input data and water quality classes, and fi-nally achieves accurate classification prediction of water quality classes.

PCA-LSTM model

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), as an optimized version of Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), can effectively solve the problem of gradient disappearance or gradient explosion of traditional RNN by virtue of its unique gating mechanism when dealing with time-series data, and accurately portray the long-term dependence of the data, which is especially suitable for mining the dynamic evolution of water quality data and the time-series features. It is especially suitable for mining the dynamic evolution of water quality data and time series features, and the data after PCA dimensionality reduction processing are used as input features, which are imported into the LSTM model to carry out the water quality prediction task.

In the LSTM model architecture, the input layer receives the reduced-dimensional water quality indicator data and inputs it sequentially into the LSTM unit according to the time step. Results

PCA-C4.5 model results analysis and discussion

Based on the C4.5 decision tree algorithm, combined with SMOTE oversampling and PCA dimensionality reduction techniques, modeling and evaluation were conducted for a six-classification task. Experimental results showed that the total accuracy of the model reached 87.26%, with a recall rate of 85.71% for Category I. The enhanced samples generated by SMOTE compensated for the insufficiency of the original data, enabling the model to identify 86% of the true Category I; The recall rate of Category VI was 90.47% , with 45 missed cases, demonstrating the model’s precise capture of the unique characteristics of small-sample categories. The precision rate for Category I (67.74%) was influenced by misclassifications in Category II (20 cases), but the high recall rate—despite the significant difference in sample size—demonstrates the method’s ability to focus on small categories. Category II and Category V have feature overlaps with the previous and previous categories, but the precision and recall rates of both remain stable at over 85%. The precision and recall rates of Category III and Category IV are close to over 87%, demonstrating extremely strong stability.As shown in Figure 8.Fig. 8The confusion matrix of PCA-C4.5.

The confusion matrix of PCA-C4.5.

Category III (2255 samples) and Category IV (1,855 samples), as the two categories with the largest sample sizes, achieved stable precision and recall rates between 87% to 89%, supported by sufficient data. Initial rapid decline phase (1–200 iterations): The loss rapidly decreased from 0.7256 to the 0.08 range, reflecting the LSTM’s efficient capture of temporal features. Its recursive structure and memory units can quickly uncover intrinsic patterns in the data, optimize parameters through backpropagation, and effectively overcome the challenge of sparse high-dimensional information after PCA dimensionality reduction, achieving preliminary feature fitting.

The memory units of the LSTM successfully filter out key sequence features, suppressing the interference of synthetic sample noise, and the generalization ability stabilizes without signs of overfitting. Discussion

Compared with previous studies that used single factor evaluation methods (only focusing on a single pollutant), traditional support vector machines (SVM, which have low efficiency in processing large-scale data), or single deep learning models (such as LSTM or CNN alone, ignoring multi parameter collaboration and small sample problems), this study uses the framework of “PCA dimensionality reduction+SMOTE balance+comparison of four models”, and the comparison results are shown in the Table 14, effectively compensating for the shortcomings of traditional methods in multi parameter nonlinear fitting and small sample recognition. the PCA-BP model combined with SMOTE in this study increased the recall rate for Class I water quality to 94.5%, and the overall accuracy (93.85%) was significantly higher than that of traditional C4.5 decision trees (87.26%), verifying the advantages of multi algorithm fusion and data preprocessing collaboration.

This study is based on the I-VI classification of GB3838-2002 to construct a prediction model, which can directly provide accurate support for watershed water quality management. For Class I water quality (source water, drinking water first level protection zone), the model’s 94.5% recall rate can achieve dynamic monitoring of high-quality water sources. Once the predicted value shows a downward trend, the management department can investigate potential pollution sources in the surrounding area (such as agricultural non-point source pollution) in advance to avoid water quality deterioration;

For Class III–IV transitional water quality (mainly applicable to general industrial or agricultural water use), the model has an F1 value of 0.926-0.946 for this category, which can assist in optimizing monitoring frequency, such as increasing sampling density during the rainy season (when pollutant erosion intensifies) to timely grasp water quality fluctuations;

For Class VI poor water quality (loss of functional use), the model’s recognition accuracy of 93.8% can quickly locate heavily polluted areas. Combined with key parameters such as TP and TN, it guides targeted treatment, such as source control and pollution interception, ecological restoration, etc.

The advantage of PCA-C4.5 decision tree lies in its simple model structure and strong interpretability. It clearly presents the logical relationship between features and water quality categories through information gain rates, making it easy to understand the impact weights of key indicators such as DO and TP. However, its limitations are significant: it has weak ability to fit nonlinear relationships, is prone to overfitting when processing high-dimensional data, and has low precision for small sample categories (such as only 0.677 for Class I). Suitable for scenarios with low data dimensions and clear decision rules.

There are still three limitations that need improvement in this study: due to data confidentiality requirements, the specific geographical coordinates of 23 monitoring stations have not been made public, making it impossible to analyze the impact of geographical factors on water quality in conjunction with watershed topography (such as slope) and land use types (such as the proportion of arable land), which limits the promotion of the model at the regional scale. The research data mixed samples from rivers, lakes, and reservoirs, without separately analyzing the water quality patterns of different water bodies (such as lakes being prone to eutrophication due to TP accumulation, while rivers are more susceptible to instantaneous runoff pollution), which may lead to prediction bias of the model for a single water body type; Although SMOTE effectively balanced Class I (increasing from 274 to 1500) and Class VI samples, the synthetic samples generated based on “K-nearest neighbor interpolation” may introduce false features that do not match the real samples, such as slightly distorted DO concentration distribution in Class I water quality, resulting in slightly lower accuracy (0.963) than Class II (0.991).

Conclusions

This study addresses the limitations of traditional water quality grading prediction methods in terms of multi-parameter collaborative analysis, handling of imbalanced categories, and fitting of nonlinear relationships. Based on 31,289 sample data points from 23 monitoring stations in Heilongjiang Province, China, from May to October 2023, covering nine key water quality indicators (water temperature WT, pH value, dissolved oxygen DO, chemical oxygen demand CODMn, ammonia nitrogen \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$NH_4^+-N$$\end{document}, total phosphorus TP, total nitrogen TN, electrical conductivity EC, and turbidity NTU) from 23 monitoring stations in Heilongjiang Province, China, from May to October 2023, a total of 31,289 sample data points were used as the basis to construct a multi-parameter watershed water quality level prediction system integrating “data preprocessing - feature dimensionality reduction - model optimization.”

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was used to reduce the dimensionality of the 9 water quality indicators. The cumulative contribution rate of the first 6 principal components reached 87.26%, significantly reducing the data dimensionality (from 9 dimensions to 6 dimensions) and computational redundancy while maximizing the retention of original data information; The KMO value (0.6) and Bartlett’s sphericity test results (p < 0.05) validated the suitability of the data for PCA dimensionality reduction. Additionally, the SMOTE oversampling algorithm was used to optimize imbalanced data categories such as Class I (small sample size) and Class VI (medium sample size), resulting in a total sample size of 33763 after processing. The proportions of each category were adjusted to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\approx$$\end{document} 1:2.29:6.15:4.93:1.97:2, effectively mitigating the model’s learning bias toward minority categories and laying the foundation for the accuracy of subsequent model training. Furthermore, Pearson correlation analysis revealed key associations among water quality indicators.

Among the four models constructed—PCA-C4.5 decision tree, PCA-BP neural network, PCA-CNN, and PCA-LSTM—the PCA-BP neural network performed the best, achieving a test accuracy of 94.52% with its 2-layer hidden layer architecture. The PCA-LSTM model followed with an accuracy of 93.42%, and its gating mechanism can precisely capture the temporal dynamic patterns of water quality data (such as the time-dependent relationship between WT and DO), achieving F1 scores of 0.946 and 0.933 for medium-to-large-scale categories (Class III and Class IV), respectively; The PCA-CNN model (total accuracy 93.27%) excels at extracting spatial correlations of local features through the combination of convolutional and pooling layers, and is more sensitive to identifying synergistic effects between strongly correlated indicators (such as NTU and TP); The PCA-C4.5 decision tree (total accuracy of 87.26%) has the advantages of a simple model structure and strong interpretability (clearly defining the logical relationship between features and water quality categories through information gain rate), but it is limited by the traditional machine learning’s ability to fit nonlinear relationships, performing poorly on small sample categories (Class I precision rate of only 0.677) and high-dimensional data processing.

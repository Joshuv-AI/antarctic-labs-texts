Rapid tissue coverage of the fluoropolymer-coated watchman FLX pro: insights from automated deep-learning CT quantification in a high-bleeding-risk patient
Authors: Qingsong Wang, Tao Chen, Jun Guo, Stefano Bordignon, Ugur Canpolat
Published: 2026

Case presentation

A 75-year-old man with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation presented with an acute ischaemic stroke, complicated by post-thrombolysis intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) and subsequent lower-limb necrosis necessitating amputation. Given the prohibitive coexistence of high thrombotic (CHA2DS2-VASc 7) and extreme haemorrhagic risks (HAS-BLED 3), standard full-dose oral anticoagulation was contraindicated. Percutaneous left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) was indicated as an alternative non-pharmacological strategy for stroke prevention.

Imaging acquisition and implantation

All cardiac computed tomography angiography (CCTA) scans in this study were acquired using a dual-source 256-slice CT scanner (SOMATOM Definition Flash, Siemens Healthineers, Forchheim, Germany) with retrospective electrocardiogram (ECG) gating. The tube voltage was auto-adjusted (70–150 kV), and 60 mL of non-ionic contrast medium was administered at 4.0–5.0 mL/s. Optimal cardiac phases (40% R-R interval for heart rate <70 beats per minute; 65% for ≥70) were reconstructed using an iterative algorithm (kernel Bv40) to minimize motion artifacts.

Preprocedural 3D CCTA reconstruction guided the anatomical assessment (Figure 1A-B). A 31 mm Watchman FLX Pro (Boston Scientific, MA, USA) was successfully implanted (Figure 1C-D; Supplementary material online, Videos S1 and S2). This device features a novel fluoropolymer coating designed to enhance hemocompatibility. Due to the recent ICH, a deviation from the standard post-procedural antithrombotic protocol was clinically mandated. To balance the competing risks of device-related thrombosis (DRT) and fatal bleeding, the patient was prescribed a tailored, conservative regimen of low-dose Edoxaban (15 mg daily) postprocedure.

Advanced quantitative follow-up analysis

Follow-up CCTA was performed at 45 days utilizing the aforementioned acquisition protocol to assess device healing. To ensure objective evaluation, we employed a validated, fully automated deep-learning approach using a 3D U-Net convolutional neural network model (Anythink β, Crealife Technology, Beijing). This artificial intelligence (AI) model automatically delineates the device boundary and segments hypoattenuated thickening (HAT). HAT was defined as tissue-like density on the atrial surface, distinguished from the contrast-enhanced blood pool by attenuation thresholds [< 100 Hounsfield units (HU) or a left atrial appendage (LAA) to left atrium (LA) HU ratio <0.25]. Healing was quantified by the HAT Coverage Rate (HCR), calculated as the segmented HAT area divided by the total exposed atrial surface area. Unlike manual visual assessment, this U-Net model has been shown to provide robust reproducibility.

The 45-day CCTA demonstrated stable device positioning with no visible peri-device leak (Figure 1E). Automated 3D volume rendering visualized a layer of smooth, uniform HAT on the device surface (Figure 1F-G). Quantitative analysis revealed an HCR of 91.7% (Figure 1H). Clinically, after 3 months of low-dose Edoxaban, the patient was safely transitioned to dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT). At the current 5-month follow-up, the patient remains free from DRT, recurrent stroke, and bleeding events.

Discussion and conclusion

This case utilizes AI-augmented CCTA quantification to illustrate rapid tissue coverage on the fluoropolymer-coated Watchman FLX Pro. Our team’s previous benchmark study on conventional uncoated Watchman devices showed that achieving ∼90% HAT coverage typically requires 6 to 12 months.1 Thus, reaching 91.7% coverage at merely 45 days suggests a highly favourable and rapid healing trajectory.

We acknowledge that CCTA imaging alone cannot histologically distinguish mature endothelium from early fibrin deposition or inflammatory tissue. However, a pivotal pre-clinical histological study by Kramer et al. demonstrated that smooth HAT on FLX devices was strongly associated with a monolayer of typical endothelial cells over a fibrovascular matrix.2 This preclinical evidence suggests that the smooth HAT observed in our patient likely indicates a benign healing process rather than thrombus formation.

The clinical relevance of this rapid tissue coverage is noteworthy, which provides a potential safety margin that may support the use of abbreviated or low-dose antithrombotic regimens in fragile patients with extreme bleeding risks. While the 5-month event-free follow-up is promising, extended large-scale clinical surveillance remains necessary to confirm if these favourable healing characteristics can consistently inform and optimize post-LAAC pharmacological protocols.

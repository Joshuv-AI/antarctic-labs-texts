Introduction

Let
U_{g}=(S^{1}\times S^{2})^{\#g}
be the boundary of the
4
-dimensional handlebody
H_{g}=(S^{1}\times D^{3})^{\natural g}
, and let
\Homeo(M)
be the group of homeomorphisms (not required to be orientation preserving) of a manifold
M
.
Our main result is the following.

.

For all
g\geq 0
the map

B\!\Homeo(H_{g})\overset{\partial}{\to}B\!\Homeo(U_{g})

has a section.

We now discuss some immediate consequences of the theorem.
Since
B\!\Homeo(M)
classifies topological
M
bundles, this shows that any topological
U_{g}
bundle can be fiberwise filled by a topological
H_{g}
bundle.
In fact, this can also be applied to families of 4-manifolds with
U_{g}
boundary, via the following corollary.

.

Let
M
be a compact
4
-manifold with
U_{g}\subseteq\partial M
.
Then the restriction map

B\!\Homeo(M\cup_{U_{g}}H_{g},H_{g})\longrightarrow B\!\Homeo(M,U_{g})

admits a section.

Laudenbach–Poenaru
[
LP72
]
proved that every diffeomorphism of
U_{g}
can be extended to a diffeomorphism of
H_{g}
.
It follows from local connectedness results due independently to Fisher
[
Fis60
]
, Kister
[
Kis60
]
, Hamstrom
[
Ham61
]
, and approximation results of Moise and Bing
[
Moi54
,
Bin54
]
, that the same holds for homeomorphisms.
Our theorem extends this result to families. With this viewpoint, there is scope to apply
13
to study families of homeomorphisms of 4-manifolds obtained via Kirby diagrams (see
[
Niu24
, Section 3]
) or trisections (see, e.g.
[
OWR23
, Gay, p. 240]
).
Of course, for these settings it would be more natural to use a section for diffeomorphisms.

Ideally we would like to loop the section in
12
to obtain a section
\Omega s
on the level of topological groups, but for a 3-manifold
M
, it is not known that
\Homeo(M)
has the homotopy type of a CW complex and so we only have a section after taking CW replacements,
i.e.
after passing to
|\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(-)|
.
In particular, this yields a weak equivalence

\Homeo_{\partial}(H_{g})\times\Homeo(U_{g})\simeq\Homeo(H_{g}).

Restricting to mapping class groups we obtain the following corollary:

\displaystyle\pi_{0}\Homeo(H_{g})

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{0}\Homeo(U_{g})\ltimes\pi_{0}\Homeo_{\partial}(H_{g})

\displaystyle\cong\left(\mathrm{Out}(F_{g})\ltimes(\mathbb{Z}/2)^{g}\right)\ltimes\pi_{0}\Homeo_{\partial}(H_{g})

where the second isomorphism is Brendle–Broaddus–Putman
[
BBP23
, Theorem A]
.
Recall that Budney–Gabai showed that
\pi_{0}\Homeo_{\partial}(H_{1})
is not finitely generated
[
BG23
]

and thus we conclude
\pi_{0}\Homeo(H_{1})
is not finitely generated.
(This can already be deduced from
[
BG23
]
using work of Hatcher
[
Hat81
,
Hat03
]
, but perhaps for higher genus our perspective could be utilised.)

The section from
12
allows us to define rational characteristic classes for
U_{g}
-bundles analogous to the Miller–Morita–Mumford classes for surfaces.
(Recall that the usual generalised MMM-classes
\kappa_{c}
are all trivial in dimension
3
by a theorem of Ebert
[
Ebe13
]
.)
For every monomial
c=p_{1}^{a}e^{b}
in
H^{*}(\BSO(4);\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}[p_{1},e]
and every
H_{g}
fiber bundle
\pi\colon E\to B
over a compact manifold
B
we can define

c(\mathfrak{t}_{v}E)\in H^{4(a+b)}(E;\mathbb{Q})
where
\mathfrak{t}_{v}E
is the vertical tangent microbundle, as in
[
ERW14
, §4]
.
Taking the Becker–Gottlieb transfer yields

\pi^{!}c(\mathfrak{t}_{v}E)\in H^{4(a+b)}(B;\mathbb{Q})
.
By varying
B
as in
[
ERW14
, Proposition 4.2]
these classes assemble into a characteristic class

and pulling it back under the section from
12
we get

s^{*}\alpha_{c}\in H^{4(a+b)}(B\!\Homeo(U_{g});\mathbb{Q}).

Hatcher proposed during a talk
[
Hat12
]
that the stable cohomology of

as
g\to\infty
should be given by

H^{*}(\colim_{g\to\infty}B\!\Homeo_{D^{3}}(U_{g});\mathbb{Q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{conj.}}}{{\cong}}H^{*}(\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\BSO(4);\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}\left[\rho_{c}\;|\;c\text{ monomial in }p_{1}\text{ and }e\right].

The above construction of

yields reasonable candidates for the restriction of these generators

to

for all

Hatcher’s approach to computing the stable homology implicitly uses that a section as in

1.
Simplicial preliminaries

For
X
a topological space, let
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}X
denote the singular simplicial set defined via
\mathrm{S}_{n}X=\Map_{\mathrm{Top}}(\Delta^{n},X)
.
In particular, for
\Homeo(M)
the group of homeomorphisms
\varphi\colon M\to M
, we let
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(M)
denote the resulting simplicial group.
We can alternatively think of an
n
-simplex in
\mathrm{S}_{n}\Homeo(M)
as a homeomorphism
\Delta^{n}\times M\to\Delta^{n}\times M
that commutes with the projection to
\Delta^{n}
. Face and degeneracy maps are inherited from
\Delta^{n}
.

We will work with these simplicial sets instead of topological spaces, hence we will encounter bi-simplicial sets whenever one would otherwise consider simplicial (topological) spaces.
The geometric realisation of a simplicial (topological) space is then replaced by taking the diagonal bisimplicial set
\delta(Y_{\bullet,\bullet})
defined by
\delta(Y)_{n}=Y_{n,n}
.
For example, if a simplicial group
G
acts on a simplicial set
X
, then the bar construction

\mathrm{Bar}_{n}(X_{m},G_{m},*)=X_{m}\times(G_{m})^{\times n}

is a bisimplicial set, and we define the homotopy orbit construction to be its diagonal

X/\!\!/G:=\delta(\mathrm{Bar}_{\bullet}(X_{\bullet},G_{\bullet},*)).

The realisation of the diagonal
|\delta(Y)|
is homeomorphic to the realisation of the simplicial space
[n]\mapsto|Y_{\bullet,n}|
.
In fact,
|\delta(Y)|
is also weakly equivalent to the more well-behaved fat geometric realisation of
[n]\mapsto|Y_{\bullet,n}|

[
ERW19
, Theorem 7.1 and Lemma 1.7]
.
Therefore

|X/\!\!/G|\simeq\|[n]\mapsto\mathrm{Bar}_{n}(|X|,|G|,*)\|=|X|/\!\!/|G|.

The simplicial set
EG=G/\!\!/G
is contractible and admits a (left)
G
-action that is level-wise free.
We can alternatively write the homotopy orbit construction as the quotient of the diagonal action

X\times EG\longrightarrow(X\times EG)/G\cong X/\!\!/G.

This map is a Kan fibration with fiber
G

[
May92
, Lemma 18.2]
.

For a topological group
H
(such as
\Homeo(M)
or
\Diff(M)
) we can find an
H
principal bundle
EH\to BH
such that
BH
is a CW complex and
EH
is weakly contractible (take Milnor’s construction
[
Mil56
]
and pull back along a CW approximation).
This
BH
classifies
H
-principal bundles over spaces homotopy equivalent to CW complexes, and is unique up to homotopy equivalence.

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}H
is a simplicial group and there are equivalences

|*/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}H|\simeq*/\!\!/|\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}H|=B(|\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}H|)\simeq BH.

Using this model,
\Omega BH
is always weakly homotopy equivalent to
H
and they are homotopy equivalent when
H
has the homotopy type of a CW complex.

We will need the following lemma for comparing homotopy orbit constructions, which is a simplicial analogue of
[
BBS24
, Lemma 2.10]
.

,

acts on

, and

is

Assume that for each

there is an

and

such that
h\cdot f(x)
is in the same path component as
y
. Assume further that one of the following two conditions holds.

(1)

For each

the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H}

Proof.

First, note that (2) implies (1).
Indeed, (2) says that the horizontal maps in (1) are Kan fibrations, so their fibers (which are exactly the stabiliser groups) are equivalent to their homotopy fibers.
A square is a homotopy pullback square if and only if the induced map on horizontal fibers at every base point is a weak equivalence, but this is exactly what the hypothesis about stabiliser groups in (2) tells us.

Now suppose we have (1). We may additionally assume that
X
and
Y
are Kan.
In order to prove the lemma, we need to show that for each
[y]\in Y/\!\!/H
the homotopy fiber of

at
[y]
is contractible.
By the additional hypothesis,
[y]
is in the same path component as
[f(x)]
for some

, so it suffices to study the homotopy fiber at
[f(x)]

If we replace
X
by
X\times EG
and

by

, then the square remains a homotopy pullback square and fits into a map of fiber sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Omega(X/\!\!/G)}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega(f/\!\!/\varphi)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner^{h}}

.

is an isomorphism on

.

\pi_{0}(f/\!\!/\varphi)

.

and a path

.
In particular,

2.
Topological separating systems

In our previous work,
[
BBS24
]
, we study the homotopy type of
B\!\Diff(M)
and
B\!\Diff_{\partial}(M)
. One of our main tools is a space of
separating systems
for a 3-manifold
M
, which parametrises decompositions of
M
into irreducible manifolds
[
BBS24
, §3]
. This space is denoted
\sep(M)
, and point corresponds to a collection of disjointly embedded spheres
\Sigma\subset\smash{\mathring{M}}
that ‘cut’
M
into (punctured) irreducible pieces. Note that
M\setminus\Sigma
is diffeomorphic to the interior of a compact 3-manifold with boundary, which we denote as
M\!\mid\!\Sigma
. Intuitively,
M\!\mid\!\Sigma
is the manifold obtained from cutting
M
along
\Sigma
. We endow
\sep(M)
with a poset structure induced by inclusion of separating systems, and we show that the realisation of the nerve is contractible. Thus we gain a model
\|\sep_{\bullet}(M)\|/\!\!/\Diff(M)
for
B\!\Diff(M)
.

We will build similar models for
B\!\Homeo(U_{g})
and
B\!\Homeo(H_{g})
. We start by defining a topological version of
\sep(M)
, specifically in the setting of
M=U_{g}
. Since we will require the action map from
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})
to this space to be a Kan fibration, we define everything simplicially. Recall that since a separating system
\Sigma\subset M
satisfies that
M\!\mid\!\Sigma
is a disjoint union of (punctured) irreducible manifolds, it follows that if
\Sigma\in\sep(U_{g})
, then
U_{g}\!\mid\!\Sigma
is homeomorphic to a disjoint union of punctured
3
-spheres.

The following discussion follows Appendix I of Burghelea–Lashof–Rothenberg
[
BLR75
]
, an account of which can also be found in Kupers
[
Kup15
, §3.2]
.

Definition 2
.

Let
M
be a compact topological
n
manifold.
A locally-flat
\Delta^{k}
-family of
d
-dimensional submanifolds in
M
is a compact subset
W\subseteq\Delta^{k}\times M
such that for every
(t,x)\in W
there is a neighbourhood
t\in B\subseteq\Delta^{k}
and an open embedding
\varphi\colon B\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\hookrightarrow B\times M
over
B
satisfying
\varphi(t,0)=(t,x)
and
\varphi^{-1}(W)=B\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
If
M
has boundary we further require
\partial W=W\cap\partial(\Delta^{k}\times M)
and we replace
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by the half-space whenever
(t,x)
is a boundary point.

It follows from the definition that
W
is a
(d+k)
-dimensional manifold and the projection
W\to\Delta^{k}
is a trivial fiber bundle with fiber
N
.
This definition is chosen such that the simplicial set
\mathrm{Sub}(M)_{\bullet}
whose
k
-simplices are locally flat
\Delta^{k}
-families of submanifolds of
M
is isomorphic to

\mathrm{Sub}(M)_{\bullet}\cong\coprod_{[N]}\emb^{\rm lf}(N,M)_{\bullet}/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(N)

where the coproduct runs over representatives of homeomorphism types of submanifolds.
Here the definition of the simplicial set of locally flat embeddings is taken to be as in
[
BLR75
, Appendix I, p.119]
.
Acting by homeomorphisms of
M
on a fixed submanifold
N\subseteq M
defines a map

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(M)\longrightarrow\emb^{\rm lf}(N,M)_{\bullet}\longrightarrow\emb^{\rm lf}(N,M)_{\bullet}/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(N)\subseteq\mathrm{Sub}(M)_{\bullet}

The first of these maps is a Kan fibration by the parametrised isotopy extension theorem for locally flat embeddings (
[
BLR75
, Theorem 4.14, p.129]
or
[
Kup15
, Theorem 3.9]
).
The second map is a Kan fibration by
[
May92
, Lemma 18.2]
as the action of
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(N)
on
\emb^{\rm lf}(N,M)_{\bullet}
by pre-composition is level-wise free.
The third map is a Kan fibration because it is an inclusion of path components.
Therefore the composite map is a Kan fibration.

Definition 3
.

A
\Delta^{k}
-family of separating systems in
U_{g}
is a locally flat
\Delta^{k}
-family
W\subset\Delta^{k}\times U_{g}
of submanifolds such that in each fiber
W_{t}
over
t\in\Delta^{k}

(1)

is a disjoint union of
2
-spheres in

, and

(2)

for some

and

.

Note that

is a separating system in the sense of
[
BBS24
]
.
Let
\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{\bullet}
be the simplicial set

\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{k}=\{W\subset\Delta^{k}\times M\;|\;W\text{ is a }\Delta^{k}\text{-family of separating systems}\}.

Face and degeneracy maps are inherited from
\Delta^{k}
. This is a poset under
\subseteq
and taking the nerve of this poset yields a bisimplicial set with

\sep^{\rm top}_{n}(U_{g})_{k}=\{W_{0}\subseteq W_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq W_{n}\;|\;W_{i}\in\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{k}\}

where the face operators in the nerve direction forget elements of the chain and the degeneracy operators repeat elements.
We then let
\delta\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{\bullet}
denote the diagonal simplicial set with
\delta\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{n}=\sep^{\rm top}_{n}(U_{g})_{n}
.

In our analogous definition for
H_{g}
, we cut the manifold up using discs, and require that the restriction to the boundary

is a separating system.

.

A
\Delta^{k}
-family of separating systems in

is a locally flat
\Delta^{k}
-family

(2)

.

As in the case of separating systems we let

be the simplicial set

\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g})_{k}=\{\mathrm{D}\subset\Delta^{k}\times M\;|\;\mathrm{D}\text{ is a }\Delta^{k}\text{-family of disc systems}\},

with face and degeneracy maps once again inherited from

. Then
\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g})_{k}
has a poset structure given by

.
Taking the nerve of this poset yields a bisimplicial set with

\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{n}(H_{g})_{k}=\{\mathrm{D}_{0}\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathrm{D}_{n}\;|\;\mathrm{D}_{i}\in\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g})_{k}\}

In both

Thus, the simplicial sets of separating systems are unions of path-components in

and

Corollary 5
.

For every
\Sigma\in\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{0}
the map

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})\longrightarrow\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{\bullet},\qquad\varphi\mapsto\varphi(\Sigma)

is a Kan fibration.
Similarly, the map
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})\to\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{0}(H_{g})
, defined by acting on a disc system, is a Kan fibration for any choice of disc system.

Every separating system
\Sigma
(or family thereof) has a finite set of sub-separating systems
\Sigma^{\prime}\subseteq\Sigma

Indeed, every sub-separating system is a union of components and thus determined by a choice of subset
\pi_{0}(\Sigma^{\prime})\subset\pi_{0}(\Sigma)

Conversely, a union of components

is a separating system if and only if

has simply connected components.
The same description holds for

.

\sep_{n}(U_{g})\to\sep_{0}(U_{g})
is a finite covering (of topological spaces).
Applying

yields a covering of simplicial sets in the above sense.
The middle and right vertical maps have the same fibers so the right square is a pullback.

For the left square,
note that for

Therefore, in the left square the horizontal maps induce a bijection between the fibers of the vertical maps and thus this square is a pullback.
∎

Lemma 7

Let

Consider the following map of fiber sequences of topological spaces:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Diff_{\Sigma}(M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Diff(M,\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Diff(\Sigma)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo_{\Sigma}(M)}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo(\Sigma)}

Here the top right map is a Serre fibration by

,
Cer61

(By

By the equivalence between homeomorphisms and diffeomorphisms for surfaces

Proposition 8
.

The simplicial set

Proof.

We will show that for all

, Proposition 3.12]

For simplicity, we first consider the case of

At every

Proof of the main theorem

We need the following adaptation of the Alexander trick.

Lemma 9
.

Let
V\subset S^{3}=\partial D^{4}
be a submanifold.
The map

\Homeo_{V}(D^{4})\overset{\partial}{\to}\Homeo_{V}(S^{3})

given by restriction to the boundary is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

We check that the proof of the Alexander trick, which shows
\Homeo(D^{4})\simeq\Homeo(S^{3})
restricts to these subgroups.
We first define a map

\displaystyle s\colon\Homeo_{V}(S^{3})

\displaystyle\overset{\partial}{\to}\Homeo_{V}(D^{4})

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\left(x\mapsto|x|\cdot\varphi\big(\tfrac{x}{|x|}\big)\right).

Then
\partial\circ s=\operatorname{id}_{\Homeo_{V}(S^{3})}
, and
s\circ\partial\simeq\operatorname{id}_{\Homeo_{V}(D^{4})}
via the following homotopy.

\displaystyle H\colon\Homeo_{V}(D^{4})\times I

\displaystyle\to\Homeo_{V}(D^{4})

\displaystyle(\psi,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{cases}|x|\cdot\psi\big(\frac{x}{|x|}\big)&|x|\geq t\\
t\cdot\psi\big(\frac{x}{t}\big)&|x|\leq t.\end{cases}

Note that throughout this homotopy the homeomorphism on the boundary remains unchanged, and so
V\subset\partial M
remains pointwise fixed,
i.e.
we stay in the required subgroup.
∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
\mathrm{D}\in\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g})
be a disc system. Then

\Homeo(H_{g},\mathrm{D})\overset{\partial}{\to}\Homeo(U_{g},\partial\mathrm{D})

is a weak equivalence.

Proof.

Consider the following map of fiber sequences,
given by restriction to the boundary.
(The right horizontal maps are Serre fibrations by the argument in
7
.)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo_{\mathrm{D}}(H_{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo(H_{g},\mathrm{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo(\mathrm{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo_{\partial\mathrm{D}}(U_{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo(U_{g},\partial\mathrm{D})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Homeo(\partial\mathrm{D})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}

The right hand vertical map is an equivalence by the Alexander trick in dimension 3 (
\Homeo(D^{3})\to\Homeo(S^{2})
is an equivalence). The domain and codomain of the left hand vertical map can be rewritten as

\Homeo_{\mathrm{D}}(H_{g})\cong\prod\Homeo_{\sqcup D^{3}}(D^{4})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Homeo_{\partial\mathrm{D}}(U_{g})\cong\prod\Homeo_{\sqcup D^{3}}(S^{3}).

where the number of factors in the product are the same and correspond to components of
H_{g}\!\mid\!\mathrm{D}
and
U_{g}\!\mid\!\partial\mathrm{D}
, respectively. Since
\sqcup D^{3}\subset\partial D^{4}
, we apply
9
to each component. Therefore the left hand vertical map is also an equivalence, hence the central vertical map is an equivalence, as required.
∎

Lemma 11
.

Let
\Sigma\in\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g})_{0}
be a separating system in
U_{g}
.
Then there is a disc system
\mathrm{D}\in\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g})_{0}
and
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
with
\phi(\partial\mathrm{D})=\Sigma
.

Proof.

Consider the dual graph
\Gamma_{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma\subset U_{g}
, whose vertices correspond to the components of
U_{g}\!\mid\!\Sigma
, and whose edges correspond to the spheres in
\Sigma
. We first build a manifold
N
that is homeomorphic to
H_{g}
and contains a disc system
\mathrm{D}
such that
\Gamma_{\partial\mathrm{D}}\cong\Gamma_{\Sigma}
, as follows. To each
v\in V(\Gamma_{\Sigma})
assign a disc
D^{4}_{v}
, to obtain the manifold
\coprod_{v\in V(\Gamma_{\Sigma})}D^{4}_{v}
.
Now for each edge
e=\{v,w\}
in
E(\Gamma_{\Sigma})
choose a standard 3-disc in the boundaries of the 4-discs
D^{4}_{v}
and
D^{4}_{w}
, respectively, and glue on a handle
h_{e}=D_{e}^{3}\times I
, in such a way that the attaching discs are pairwise disjoint. The output of this construction is a topological manifold
N
with boundary that is homeomorphic to
H_{g}
. Let
\psi\colon N\cong H_{g}
be a choice of such a homeomorphism. The required disc system
\mathrm{D}\subset H_{g}
is given by
\sqcup_{e\in E(\Gamma_{\Sigma})}\psi(D_{e}^{3}\times\{\frac{1}{2}\})
.

It remains to find
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
with
\phi(\partial\mathrm{D})=\Sigma
. By construction, we have an identification of dual graphs
f\colon\Gamma_{\Sigma}\cong\Gamma_{\partial\mathrm{D}}
giving a bijection between components
\pi_{0}(\Sigma)
and
\pi_{0}(\partial\mathrm{D})
(edges), and between components
\pi_{0}(U_{g}\!\mid\!\Sigma)
and
\pi_{0}(U_{g}\!\mid\!\partial\mathrm{D})
(vertices).
We build
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
via the following two steps.

(1)

Each component
K\in\pi_{0}(U_{g}\!\mid\!\Sigma)
is homeomorphic to the component
f(K)=K^{\prime}\in\pi_{0}(U_{g}\!\mid\!\partial\mathrm{D})
, and to
S^{3}\setminus\sqcup_{m}\smash{\mathring{D}}^{3}
, where
m
is the valence of the corresponding vertex in the dual graph. Furthermore, each boundary component of
K
(resp.
K^{\prime}
) is identified with a sphere in
\Sigma
(resp.
\partial D
). Using this identification, pick a homeomorphism
\phi_{K}\colon K\to K^{\prime}
such that
\phi_{K}(S)=f(S)\in\pi_{0}(\partial\mathrm{D})
for all
S\in\Sigma
(this is always possible since
\Homeo(S^{3})
acts transitively on embedded discs).

(2)

We now make the homeomorphisms
\{\phi_{K}\}_{K\in\pi_{0}(U_{g}\,\!\mid\!\,\Sigma)}
compatible so that they can be glued along
\Sigma
in the domain and
\partial\mathrm{D}
in the codomain to yield the desired
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
. Given a sphere
S\subset\Sigma
there are components
K_{1},K_{2}
of
U_{g}\!\mid\!\Sigma
and inclusions
\rho_{i}\colon S\rightarrow S_{i}\subset\partial K_{i}
for
i=1,2
. From Step
(1)
, we have homeomorphisms
\phi_{K_{i}}\colon K_{i}\rightarrow K_{i}^{\prime}
such that
\phi_{K_{i}}(S_{i})
corresponds to the sphere
f(S)\in\partial\mathrm{D}
under the inclusions
\rho_{i}^{\prime}\colon f(S)\rightarrow K_{i}^{\prime}
. Since
\Homeo^{+}(S^{2})
is path-connected we can isotope
\phi_{K_{1}}
in a neighbourhood of
S_{1}
so that
(\rho_{1}^{\prime})^{-1}\circ\phi_{K_{1}}\circ\rho_{1}=(\rho_{2}^{\prime})^{-1}\circ\phi_{K_{2}}\circ\rho_{2}.
After doing this for each
S\in\Sigma
, the
\phi_{K}
agree along each component of
\Sigma
hence we obtain our desired
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
.∎

Theorem 12
.

The map

\partial\colon B\!\Homeo(H_{g})\to B\!\Homeo(U_{g})

has a section.

Proof.

As recalled in Section 2, we can model classifying spaces as the realisation of the simplicial homotopy orbit construction and so it suffices to construct a section (up to homotopy) of the map

|*/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})|\xrightarrow[\hskip 8.19447pt]{\partial}|*/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})|.

We will apply
1
to the map
\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{0}(H_{g})_{\bullet}\to\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{\bullet}
to prove that the map

\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{0}(H_{g})_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})\longrightarrow\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})

is a weak equivalence.
By
5
both of the action maps
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})\to\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{0}(H_{g})_{\bullet}
and
\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})\to\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{\bullet}
are fibrations.
The map of stabiliser groups is

\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g},\mathrm{D})\longrightarrow\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g},\partial\mathrm{D})

which is indeed a weak equivalence by
10
.
Moreover, by
11
, every
\Sigma\in\sep^{\rm top}_{0}(U_{g})_{0}
can be written as
\phi(\partial\mathrm{D})
for some
\mathrm{D}\in\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{0}(H_{g})_{0}
and
\phi\in\Homeo(U_{g})
.
This verifies the surjectivity condition of
1
, so we have

\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}_{n}(H_{g})_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})\longrightarrow\sep^{\rm top}_{n}(U_{g})_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})

is a weak equivalence for
n=0
.
Because the left square in
6
remains a pullback after taking homotopy orbits, the case of general
n
follows.

We have thus shown that the left hand map in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta(\operatorname{DSep}^{\rm top}(H_{g}))_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(H_{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta(\sep^{\rm top}(U_{g}))_{\bullet}/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces*/\!\!/\mathrm{S}_{\bullet}\!\Homeo(U_{g}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\partial}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

is a weak equivalence, as depicted.
The bottom map is a weak equivalence as

by

.
After passing to geometric realisations we can find homotopy inverses to these equivalences and the desired lift is then given by starting at the bottom right and going left, up, and right.
∎

Corollary 13
.

Let
M
be a compact

-manifold with

.
Then the restriction map

B\!\Homeo(M\cup_{U_{g}}H_{g},H_{g})\longrightarrow B\!\Homeo(M,U_{g})

Proof.

The restriction maps between homeomorphism groups induce a square of classifying spaces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B\!\Homeo(M\cup_{U_{g}}H_{g},H_{g})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B\!\Homeo(H_{g})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B\!\Homeo(M,U_{g})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B\!\Homeo(U_{g})}.}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

The vertical maps are surjective on

[

]

This is a homotopy pullback square as the induced map on vertical fibers is

which is an equivalence.
The right vertical map in the square admits a section by

∎

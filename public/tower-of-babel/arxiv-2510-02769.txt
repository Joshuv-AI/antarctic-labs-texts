Periodic Event-Triggered Prescribed Time Control of Euler–Lagrange Systems under State and Input Constraints
Chidre Shravista Kashyap, Karnan A, Pushpak Jagtap, Jishnu Keshavan

Abstract
This article proposes a periodic event-triggered adaptive barrier control policy for the trajectory tracking problem of perturbed Euler-Lagrangian systems with state, input, and temporal (SIT) constraints. In particular, an approximation-free adaptive-barrier control architecture is designed to ensure prescribed-time convergence of the tracking error to a prescribed bound while rejecting exogenous disturbances. In contrast to existing approaches that necessitate continuous real-time control action, the proposed controller generates event-based updates through periodic evaluation of the triggering condition. Additionally, we derive an upper bound on the monitoring period by analysing the performance degradation of the filtered tracking error to facilitate periodic evaluation of the event-triggered strategy. To this end, a time-varying threshold function is considered in the triggering mechanism to reduce the number of triggers during the transient phase of system behaviour. Notably, the proposed design avoids Zeno behaviour and precludes the need for continuous monitoring of the triggering condition. A simulation and experimental study is undertaken to demonstrate the efficacy of the proposed control scheme.



Keywords
Euler-Lagrangian systems, state and input constraints, prescribed time convergence, periodic event-triggered control.

3.1
Analysis of the Periodic Event-Triggering Mechanism

Closed-loop system performance is sensitive to the choice of monitoring period. In particular, the PETC mechanism behaves similarly to the CETC if the monitoring period is less than the MIET, but potentially loses triggering events otherwise. Moreover, exceedingly large monitoring periods cause performance degradation. To address this, an upper bound on the monitoring period is derived, in addition to the computation of a lower bound on MIET to avoid non-Zeno behaviour.

Theorem 3.3
(minimum inter-event time)

There exists
\nu>0
such that the inter-event time (IET) generated by the event-triggered mechanism (
9
) are lower bounded by
\nu
, i.e.,
t_{k+1}-t_{k}>\nu~\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof
:
Finding derivative of
\lVert\bm{u}_{e}(t)\rVert
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\bm{u}_{e}(t)\rVert=\frac{\bm{u}_{e}(t)^{T}\left(\dot{\bm{u}}_{c}(t)-\dot{\bm{u}}(t)\right)}{\lVert\bm{u}_{e}(t)\rVert}\leq\lVert\dot{\bm{u}}_{c}(t)\rVert.

(18)

Also, from equation (
8
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{u}}_{c}(t)=\dot{\bm{\mathcal{S}}}(\bm{\tau}_{c}(t))\bm{\tau}_{c}(t)+\bm{\mathcal{S}}(\bm{\tau}_{c}(t))\dot{\bm{\tau}}_{c}(t).

(19)

where
\dot{\bm{\tau}}_{c}(t)=-\dot{K}_{1}(t)\Upsilon-K_{1}(t)\frac{d}{dt}\Upsilon-\dot{K}_{1}(t)\|\bm{\chi}\|\lVert\bm{J}\rVert-K_{1}(t)\frac{d}{dt}\Big(\|\bm{\chi}\|\Big)\|\bm{J}\|

with
K_{1}(t)=\frac{\rho\bm{\xi}}{\bm{\omega}-\|\bm{\xi}\|}
.

Computing the norm of
\dot{K}_{1}(t)
(from
Remark

3.2
, there exists
0<a_{0}<a
such that
\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert=a_{0}\omega
), we get

\displaystyle\lVert\dot{K}_{1}(t)\rVert\leq\frac{\rho\omega\lVert\dot{\bm{\xi}}\rVert}{(\omega-\lVert\bm{\xi}\rVert)^{2}}<\frac{\rho l_{1}}{(1-a_{0})^{2}}:=l_{2}.

(20)

where
l_{1}=\overline{l}\,\overline{u}+\overline{l}\,\overline{c}\,\overline{\nu}\,\omega+\overline{l}\,\overline{\delta}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\lVert\dot{\bm{\xi}}\rVert\leq\lVert\bm{M}^{-1}\rVert\left(\lVert\bm{u}\rVert+\overline{c}\lVert\dot{\bm{q}}\rVert\omega+\lVert\bm{\delta}\rVert\right)\leq l_{1}.

(21)

Also,
|{\frac{d\lVert\bm{\chi}\rVert}{dt}}|\leq\lVert\dot{\bm{\chi}}\rVert<\gamma_{1}\omega+\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}\lVert\bm{J}\rVert\omega:=l_{3}
.

From the solution for the tracking error (
6
) with TBG
\bm{\varepsilon}(t)
we can obtain

\left|\frac{d}{dt}\Upsilon\right|\leq 4(\|\dot{\bm{\varepsilon}}(t)\|^{2}+\|\bm{\varepsilon}(t)\|\|\ddot{\bm{\varepsilon}}(t)\|)=4l_{4}
,
where
l_{4}{=}({\omega l_{1}}/{\underline{\mathcal{K}}})+\omega^{2}({1+{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}/{\underline{\mathcal{K}}}})({({1+{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}/{\underline{\mathcal{K}}}}+{\overline{\mathcal{K}}}/{\underline{\mathcal{K}}})})
.

Making use of the inequalities derived above, we have the following bound:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\bm{u}_{e}(t)\|<l_{M},

(22)

where
l_{M}=l_{2}\left(4+\sqrt{2}\omega\right)+\left({\rho}/(1-a)\right)\left(4l_{4}+\sqrt{2}l_{3}\right)
.
Taking integral on both sides of the ETC condition (
9
) over the interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1})
and using the inequality (
22
) we have

\displaystyle t_{k+1}-t_{k}>\frac{\alpha\lVert\bm{u}(t)\rVert+\beta(t)}{l_{M}}>\frac{\alpha\overline{u}+\beta_{0}T}{l_{M}}:=\nu.

(23)

Therefore, there exists
\nu>0
such that
t_{k+1}-t_{k}>\nu
resulting in non-Zeno behaviour in the PETC mechanism (
9
).
\blacksquare

Proposition 3.4

Let
\omega>0,a\in[0,1)
, and
l_{1}>0
such that
\|\dot{\bm{\xi}}(t)\|\leq l_{1}
. Then, prescribed tracking performance
||\bm{\xi}(t)||<\omega,\,\forall\,t\geq t_{0}
is ensured provided the monitoring period
h
in the PETC mechanism (
9
) satisfies the inequality
h<h^{*}=(1-a)\omega/l_{1}
.

Proof
:
Firstly, from
Remark

3.2
we have
\lVert\bm{\xi}(t_{k})\rVert<a\omega
. Then, to compute the upper bound on the monitoring period
h
, consider the time instant
t
at which
\lVert\bm{\xi}(t)\rVert=\omega
, so that

\displaystyle\omega=\lVert\bm{\xi}(t)\rVert

\displaystyle\leq\lVert\bm{\xi}(t_{k})\rVert+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\lVert\dot{\bm{\xi}}(s)\rVert ds

\displaystyle<\lVert\bm{\xi}(t_{k})\rVert+l_{1}(t-t_{k}),\ \forall\ t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1})

\displaystyle\implies t-t_{k}

\displaystyle>\frac{(1-a)\omega}{l_{1}}.

(24)

Thus, the upper bound on the monitoring period is chosen as
h=t-t_{k}<h^{*}
.
\blacksquare

Remark 3.5

From (
23
) and (
24
), it is evident that the bounds
\nu
and
h^{*}
are derived based on system parameter properties (see
Properties

2.1
–
2.3
) and controller parameters
\alpha,\,\beta_{0}
. Note that choosing a lower value of
\omega
results in a smaller prescribed bound (given by
Theorem

3.1
) and a lower value for the upper bound on the monitoring period
h^{*}
. Thus, better tracking accuracy can only be achieved at the expense of more frequent evaluation and triggering of control signals (even while avoiding Zeno behaviour), thus ensuring robust PTPB tracking performance under SIT constraints.

5
Conclusion

This article proposes a periodic event-triggered adaptive barrier control policy for addressing the trajectory tracking problem of EL systems with SIT constraints. In particular, an approximation-free adaptive-barrier control architecture is designed to ensure the convergence of tracking error within a prescribed time frame. Furthermore, the PETC strategy alleviates the need for continuous monitoring of the event-triggering mechanism, thereby reducing the communication bandwidth required for control signals. Moreover, the stability analysis formally verifies that the proposed controller guarantees convergence of all tracking errors to the prescribed bounds under SIT constraints. Also, this study provides a detailed analysis of the PETC mechanism, showing that the choice of monitoring period affects the tracking performance. Finally, numerical and experimental results are presented to demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed scheme. As part of future work, we plan to extend this study to trajectory tracking for fully actuated and under-actuated EL systems with vision in the loop.

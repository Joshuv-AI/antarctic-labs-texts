Introduction

We continue the study of wave operators for higher order Schrödinger operators related to equations of the form

\displaystyle i\psi_{t}=(-\Delta)^{m}\psi+V\psi,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}.

Here

Denote the free operator by

\phi_{2}\in S_{k_{2}+1}L^{2}
if
k_{2}\geq 1
, otherwise we assume
\phi_{j}\in L^{2}
. If
\beta>\max(n,2m+k_{1},2m+k_{2})
, then the operator with kernel

(10)

K(x,y)=\int_{0}^{1}\omega^{\alpha-1}(\lambda)\big[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})v\phi_{1}\overline{\phi_{2}}v[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})]\big](x,y)d\lambda

is bounded on
L^{p}
for
1\leq p<\frac{n}{2m-k_{2}-\alpha}
. Moreover, the range is
[1,\infty)
if
k_{2}+\alpha=2m
and it is
[1,\infty]
if
k_{2}+\alpha>2m
.

This finishes the proof Theorem
1.2
noting that for each
(k_{1},k_{2},\alpha)
occuring in the representation (
8
) we have
\alpha+k_{2}\geq k_{0}\geq 1
and

k_{1}+k_{2}+\alpha=2k_{0}\geq\max(2,2k)=\max(2,4m-2\lceil\tfrac{n}{2}\rceil+2)>4m-n.

With representation (
8
), we reduce the effect of the zero energy obstruction to fairly explicit operators that we control using Proposition
2.2
. The added layers of orthogonality, measured by
k_{1},k_{2}
, allows us to push the upper bound on
p
upward. Although, in the analysis considered here, we take
(k_{1},k_{2},\alpha)\in\{(k_{0},k_{0},0),(k_{0},0,k_{0}),(0,k_{0},k_{0})\}
, we developed these tools with more flexibility to handle the effects of resonances in lower dimensions. We note that Proposition
2.2
can be used to prove results when there are resonances in dimensions
2m<n\leq 4m
.
However, in light of Chen, Soffer, Wu, and Yao’s recent result for low odd dimensions,
[
3
]
, we don’t pursue that in this paper.

To prove these results we need the following representations of the free resolvent from Lemma 2.3, Lemma 2.4, and Remark 2.5 in
[
7
]
, that have their roots in
[
6
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
n>2m\geq 2
. Then, we have the representations, with
r=|x-y|
,

\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(x,y)=\frac{e^{i\lambda r}}{r^{n-2m}}F(\lambda r),\text{ and}

(11)

[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})](x,y)=\lambda^{n-2m}\big[e^{i\lambda r}F_{+}(\lambda r)+e^{-i\lambda r}F_{-}(\lambda r)\big],

where, for all
N\geq 0
,

(12)

|\partial_{\lambda}^{N}F(\lambda r)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\langle\lambda r\rangle^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,|\partial_{\lambda}^{N}F_{\pm}(\lambda r)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\langle\lambda r\rangle^{\frac{1-n}{2}}.

We utilize the following notation to streamline the low energy arguments.
Analogous to the definition of
\omega^{\alpha}(\lambda)
abov;
for given
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote any function of
\lambda,r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
by
\omega^{\alpha}(\lambda,r)
provided that the function is 0 for
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
and it satisfies

|\partial^{N}_{\lambda}\omega^{\alpha}(\lambda,r)|\lesssim\lambda^{-N}\langle\lambda r\rangle^{\alpha},\,\,N=0,1,2,...

The function may differ in each occurrence, for example, for
\lambda<\lambda_{0}

[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})](x,y)=\lambda^{n-2m}\big[e^{i\lambda r}\omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\lambda,r)+e^{-i\lambda r}\omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\lambda,r)\big],\quad r=|x-y|.

In some cases, the function may depend on other variables. Then, it should be understood that the bound above is uniform in all other variables. Note that for all
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle\lambda r\rangle^{\alpha_{1}}\omega^{\alpha_{2}}(\lambda,r)=\omega^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}(\lambda,r),\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\omega^{\alpha_{1}}\omega^{\alpha_{2}}=\omega^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}.

3.
Proof of Proposition
2.2

In this section we prove Proposition
2.2
. To do so, we prove a series of Lemmas that utilize the orthogonality between the eigenspace and the potential that allow us to push the range of
p
upward.
We start with a standard lemma providing decay bounds for the zero energy eigenfunctions (and resonances) of
H
and associated
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

Lemma 3.1
.

If
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2m
and
\phi\in S_{1}L^{2}
, then
|\phi(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{2m-n-\frac{\beta}{2}}
.

Proof.

Recall that
\phi=Uv\psi
, where
\psi\in L^{\infty}
is a distributional solution of
H\psi=0
, see, e.g., Section 8 of
[
9
]
. Note that the boundedness of
\psi
may be seen by iterating the identity
\psi=-G_{0}^{0}V\psi=(-G_{0}^{0}V)^{k}\psi
for sufficiently large
k
. Here
G_{0}^{0}=(-\Delta^{m})^{-1}
is the operator with kernel
G_{0}^{0}(x,y)=a_{0}|x-y|^{2m-n}
for some constant
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Therefore, we immediately have
|\phi|=|v\psi|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta/2}
. Using the identity
\phi=-UvG_{0}v\phi
and properties of
I_{2m}
, if we assume
|\phi|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}
for some
n>\alpha+\frac{\beta}{2}>2m
, then

|\phi(x)|=c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v(x)v(y)\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{n-2m}}\ \!dy\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}-\alpha}|x-y|^{2m-n}\ \!dy\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta-\alpha+2m}.

Noting that
\beta+\alpha-2m>\alpha
, this process may be iterated to achieve the desired decay.
If
\alpha+\frac{\beta}{2}\geq n
then similarly

|\phi(x)|=c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v(x)v(y)\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{n-2m}}\ \!dy\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}-\alpha}|x-y|^{2m-n}\ \!dy\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{\beta}{2}+2m-n}.

∎

Next we provide two lemmas utilizing the cancellation of
S_{k_{1}+1}
,
S_{k_{2}+1}
with
\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})v
and
v[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}](\lambda^{2m})
respectively, that are vital to proving Proposition
2.2
.

Inspired by Galtbayar and Yajima’s recent works
[
2
,
1
]
, we have the following.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\phi\in S_{k_{2}+1}L^{2}
for some
1\leq k_{2}\leq n
, then if
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2m+k_{2}
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
\varphi(x):=|D|^{-k_{2}+\varepsilon}[v\phi](x)\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n-\varepsilon}
. Further, for any Borel function
h
, we have the identity

\int^{\infty}_{0}h(\lambda)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})](r_{2})[v\phi](z_{2})\ \!dz_{2}\ \!d\lambda\\
=\int^{\infty}_{0}h(\lambda)\lambda^{k_{2}-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})](r_{2})\varphi(z_{2})\ \!dz_{2}\ \!d\lambda.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Viewing the resolvent difference as the spectral projection, via the Stone’s formula one has

\int^{\infty}_{0}h(\lambda)[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})]v\phi\ \!d\lambda=\int^{\infty}_{0}h(\lambda)\lambda^{k_{2}-\varepsilon}[\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})-\mathcal{R}_{0}^{-}(\lambda^{2m})]|D|^{-k_{2}+\varepsilon}v\phi\ \!d\lambda.

See also Lemma 3.1 in
[
1
]
.

By Lemma
3.1
, we have
|v\phi(y)|\lesssim\langle y\rangle^{2m-n-\beta}
.
Therefore,

\varphi(x)=\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+\varepsilon}}dy

is a bounded function of
x
as
n-k_{2}+\varepsilon+n+\beta-2m>n
, which suffices when
|x|\lesssim 1
.

When
|x|\gtrsim 1
,
|y|\ll|x|
, we have the expansion

(13)

\displaystyle\frac{|x|^{\alpha}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{2x\cdot y}{|x|^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}\left(\frac{|y|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}-\frac{2x\cdot y}{|x|^{2}}\right)^{j}+O(|y|^{k+1}|x|^{-k-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2j_{1}+j_{2}\leq k\\
j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}c_{j_{1},j_{2}}\frac{|y|^{2j_{1}}(x\cdot y)^{j_{2}}}{|x|^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k<2j_{1}+j_{2}\\
j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0,j_{1}+j_{2}\leq k\end{subarray}}c_{j_{1},j_{2}}\frac{|y|^{2j_{1}}(x\cdot y)^{j_{2}}}{|x|^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}}}+O(|y|^{k+1}|x|^{-k-1}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2j_{1}+j_{2}\leq k\\
j_{1},j_{2}\geq 0\end{subarray}}c_{j_{1},j_{2}}\frac{|y|^{2j_{1}}(x\cdot y)^{j_{2}}}{|x|^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}}}+O(|y|^{k+1}|x|^{-k-1})=:Q_{k}(x,y)+O(|y|^{k+1}|x|^{-k-1}).

Note that
Q_{k}(x,y)
is a polynomial in
y
of degree
\leq k
and
|Q_{k}(x,y)|\lesssim|y|^{k}|x|^{-k}\lesssim|y|^{k+1}|x|^{-k-1}
when
|y|\gtrsim|x|
. Therefore, for all
|x|\gtrsim 1
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have (with
P_{k}(x,y):=|x|^{-\alpha}Q_{k}(x,y)
)

\Big|\frac{1}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}-P_{k}(x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{|y|^{k+1}}{|x|^{\alpha+k+1}}+\frac{\chi_{|y|\gtrsim|x|}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

Using this with
k=k_{2}-1
and
\alpha=n-k_{2}+\varepsilon
, we have for
|x|\gtrsim 1

|\varphi(x)|=\ \Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{v\phi(y)}{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+\varepsilon}}\ \!dy\Big|=\ \Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+\varepsilon}}-P_{k_{2}-1}(x,y)\right)v\phi(y)\ \!dy\Big|\\
\lesssim\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{|y|^{k_{2}}}{|x|^{n+\varepsilon}}+\frac{\chi_{|y|\gtrsim|x|}}{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+\varepsilon}}\Big)\langle y\rangle^{-\beta+2m-n}\ \!dy\\
\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-n-\varepsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bigg(\langle y\rangle^{-\beta+2m-n+k_{2}}+\frac{\langle y\rangle^{2m-\beta-\varepsilon}}{|x-y|^{n-k_{2}+\varepsilon}}\bigg)\,dy\lesssim\ |x|^{-n-\varepsilon},

provided that that
\beta>2m+k_{2}
and
n\geq k_{2}
.
∎

The lemma above will suffice in the cases when
|x|<1
and when
1<|x|\lesssim|z_{1}|
. For the case
1<|x|
and

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\phi\in S_{k_{1}+1}L^{2}
for some
k_{1}\geq 1
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{|z_{1}|\ll|x|}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(r_{1})v\phi(z_{1})\ \!dz_{1}=\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\lambda|x|}\frac{\chi_{|z_{1}|\gtrsim|x|}v\phi(z_{1})|z_{1}|^{j}}{|x|^{n-2m+j}}\omega^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}(\lambda,|x|)\ \!dz_{1}\\
+\int^{1}_{0}\frac{(1-s)^{k_{1}-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\lambda r_{1}}\frac{\chi_{|z_{1}|\ll|x|}v\phi(z_{1})|z_{1}|^{k_{1}}}{r_{1}^{n-2m+k_{1}}}\omega^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+k_{1}}(\lambda,r_{1})\ \!dz_{1}ds

where
r_{1}=|x-sz_{1}|
.

We note that the cut-offs are different in the leading
k_{1}
terms in the first finite sum, than in the final summand.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Denoting
f(s):=\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(|x-sz_{1}|)
, utilizing Taylor’s formula with respect to

(14)

\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(|x-z_{1}|)=f(1)\\
=f(0)+\partial_{s}f(0)+\ldots+\frac{1}{(k_{1}-1)!}\partial_{s}^{k_{1}-1}f(0)+\int^{1}_{0}\frac{(1-s)^{k_{1}-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\partial_{s}^{k_{1}}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(|x-sz_{1}|)\ \!ds

We observe that
\partial^{i}_{s}|x-sz_{1}|
is a linear combination of terms of the form

(15)

\displaystyle|x-sz_{1}|^{1-2m_{1}-2m_{2}}((x-sz_{1})\cdot z_{1})^{m_{1}}|z_{1}|^{2m_{2}}

where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with

. Importantly, with

|x|^{1-2m_{1}-2m_{2}}(x\cdot z_{1})^{m_{1}}|z_{1}|^{2m_{2}}
. This can be shown through a simple induction argument. Each term is a polynomial in
z_{1}
of degree
m_{1}+2m_{2}=i
. In addition,

(16)

\big|\partial^{i}_{s}|x-sz_{1}|\big|\lesssim|x-sz_{1}|^{1-i}|z_{1}|^{i}

Since
\partial_{s}f(r_{1})=f^{\prime}(r_{1})\partial_{s}r_{1}
, each derivative acting on

produces a factor of
\partial_{s}r_{1}
by the Chain Rule, so that the total number of derivatives acting on
r_{1}
(counting multiplicity) sums up to
j
. So that

\partial_{s}^{j}f(r_{1})=\sum_{k=0}^{j}f^{(k)}(r_{1})\prod_{i=1}^{I_{k}}(\partial_{s}^{i}r_{1})^{m_{i}}

where
\sum m_{i}=k
and
\sum im_{i}=j
when summed over the index set
I_{k}
. This may be proven by an inductive argument, noting that if another derivative acts on a

m_{\ell}
and
m_{\ell+1}
are affected, changing to
m_{\ell}-1
and
m_{\ell+1}+1
respectively.
Using Lemma
2.3
we see that, with

,
\partial^{j}_{s}\mathcal{R}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(r_{1})\rvert_{s=0}

=|x|^{2m-n-j_{1}}\lambda^{j_{2}}e^{i\lambda|x|}\lambda^{j_{3}}F^{(j_{3})}(\lambda|x|)\prod^{I}_{i=1}\Big(\partial^{i}_{s}|x-sz_{1}|\Big\rvert_{s=0}\Big)^{m_{i}}

with
\sum^{I}_{i=1}im_{i}=j
and
\sum^{I}_{i=1}m_{i}=j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}
. Technically, the index set

should change in each occurrence, we leave it as is for simplicity. The only factors of
z_{1}
come from that rightmost product which is a polynomial of degree
\sum^{I}_{i=1}im_{i}=j
in
z_{1}
, by (
15
) and the discussion following it. Using this fact with our
k_{1}-1
degrees of cancellation, since
\phi\in S_{k+1}
yields that
\langle v\phi,x^{\alpha}\rangle=0

by (

\chi_{|z_{1}|\ll|x|}
. Therefore, we can replace the cutoff

with

. We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{|z_{1}|\ll|x|}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(|x-z_{1}|)v\phi(z_{1})\ \!dz_{1}=-\sum_{j=0}^{k_{1}-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\chi_{|z_{1}|\gtrsim|x|}\frac{\partial_{s}^{j}f(0)}{(k_{1}-1)!}v\phi(z_{1})\ \!dz_{1}\\
+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int^{1}_{0}\frac{(1-s)^{k_{1}-1}}{(k_{1}-1)!}\frac{\partial^{k_{1}}}{\partial s^{k_{1}}}\mathcal{R}_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2m})(|x-sz_{1}|)v\phi(z_{1})\ \!dsdz_{1}.

We rewrite (
17

r_{1}^{2m-n-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}(\lambda r_{1})^{j_{2}+j_{3}}e^{i\lambda r_{1}}F^{(j_{3})}(\lambda r_{1})\prod^{I}_{i=1}(\partial^{i}_{s}r_{1})^{m_{i}}

Note that, by (

r_{1}^{2m-n-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\Big|\prod^{I}_{i=1}(\partial^{i}_{s}r_{1})^{m_{i}}\Big|\lesssim r_{1}^{2m-n-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}r_{1}^{\sum(1-i)m_{i}}|z_{1}|^{\sum im_{i}}=r_{1}^{2m-n-j}|z_{1}|^{j}

Therefore,

\partial^{j}_{s}\mathcal{R}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(r_{1})=e^{i\lambda r_{1}}\frac{|z_{1}|^{j}}{r_{1}^{n-2m+j}}G_{j}(\lambda r_{1}),

where (also using

Evaluating this at

The final lemma provides bounds for the resulting

\displaystyle\Lambda_{j,\eta}(r_{1},r_{2}):=\bigg|\int_{0}^{1}e^{i\lambda(r_{1}\pm r_{2})}\omega^{\eta}(\lambda)\ \omega^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}(\lambda,r_{1})\ \omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\lambda,r_{2})d\lambda\bigg|.

Lemma 3.4
.

For

and
\eta>-1
, we have the bounds

\Lambda_{j,\eta}\lesssim 1
when

,

\Lambda_{j,\eta}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\lambda^{\eta}}{\langle\lambda r_{2}\rangle^{2m-1-j}\langle\lambda(r_{1}-r_{2})\rangle^{2}}d\lambda

\Lambda_{j,\eta}\lesssim r_{2}^{-\eta-1}+\langle r_{1}\rangle^{-\min(\eta+1,\frac{n}{2}+2m-j)}\big(\tfrac{\langle r_{1}\rangle}{r_{2}}\big)^{n+\frac{1}{2}},

We will apply this with

. Also, although the bounds depend on

.

Proof.

We only consider the ‘-’ sign.
The first bound follows by noting that the magnitude of the integrand in (

For the second bound, we integrate by parts twice when
\lambda|r_{1}-r_{2}|\gtrsim 1

\Lambda_{j,\eta}\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\lambda^{\eta}\chi(\lambda|r_{1}-r_{2}|)\langle\lambda r_{2}\rangle^{1-2m+j}d\lambda+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\lambda^{\eta-2}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda|r_{1}-r_{2}|)\langle\lambda r_{2}\rangle^{1-2m+j}}{|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{2}}d\lambda\\
\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\lambda^{\eta}}{\langle\lambda r_{2}\rangle^{2m-1-j}\langle\lambda(r_{1}-r_{2})\rangle^{2}}d\lambda.

For the third bound, when
\lambda r_{1}\lesssim 1
, we bound the
\omega

\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\lambda^{\eta}d\lambda\lesssim r_{1}^{-\eta-1}

When

\frac{1}{|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{\lambda}^{N}\big[\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda r_{1})\omega^{\eta}(\lambda)\ \omega^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}(\lambda,r_{1})\ \omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\lambda,r_{2})\big]\Big|d\lambda\\
\lesssim r_{1}^{-N}\int_{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}^{1}\lambda^{\eta-N}\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}r_{1}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}\langle\lambda r_{2}\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}d\lambda\\
\lesssim r_{1}^{j-2m-\{\frac{n}{2}\}-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{\frac{1}{r_{1}}}^{1}\lambda^{\eta+j-2m-\{\frac{n}{2}\}-\frac{1}{2}}d\lambda\lesssim r_{1}^{j-2m-\frac{1}{2}}+r_{1}^{-\eta-1}.

Here if

.

It remains to consider the fourth inequality; when
r_{2}\gg\langle r_{1}\rangle

\lambda r_{2}\lesssim 1
, the claim follows by bounding the integrand by
\lambda^{\eta}
as in the proof of third inequality above. When
\lambda r_{2}\gtrsim 1
, we integrate by parts

\frac{1}{|r_{1}-r_{2}|^{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\partial_{\lambda}^{N}\big[\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda r_{2})\omega^{\eta}(\lambda)\ \omega^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m+j}(\lambda,r_{1})\ \omega^{\frac{1-n}{2}}(\lambda,r_{2})\big]\Big|d\lambda\\

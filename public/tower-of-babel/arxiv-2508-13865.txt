The separating variety for matrix invariants
Jonathan Elmer

Abstract
. Let G G be a linear algebraic group defined over an algebraically closed field 𝕜 \Bbbk , and let 𝒱 \mathcal{V} be a vector space on which G G acts linearly. Introduced in [ 29 ] , the separating variety 𝒮 G , 𝒱 \mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}} is the subvariety of 𝒱 2 \mathcal{V}^{2} consisting of pairs of points indistinguishable by invariant polynomials in 𝕜 ​ [ 𝒱 ] G {\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}} . Its geometry places restrictions on the existence of small separating sets, i.e. sets of invariants which distinguish the same points as the full algebra of invariants. It is well known that the cardinality of a separating set for 𝕜 ​ [ 𝒱 ] G {\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}} is bounded below by dim ( 𝕜 ​ [ 𝒱 ] G ) \dim({\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}) . A separating set of cardinality dim ( 𝕜 ​ [ 𝒱 ] G ) \dim({\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}) is called a polynomial separating set, and a separating set of cardinality dim ( 𝕜 ​ [ 𝒱 ] G ) + 1 \dim({\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}})+1 is called a hypersurface separating set. The purpose of this article is to study the separating variety in the important special case where G = GL p ⁡ ( ℂ ) G=\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C}) acts on the set 𝒱 = ℳ p n \mathcal{V}={\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n} of n n -tuples of p × p p\times p matrices by simultaneous conjugation. We define a purely combinatorial poset, 𝒫 p , n \mathcal{P}_{p,n} , whose maximal elements are in 1-1 correspondence with the irreducible components of 𝒮 G , 𝒱 \mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}} . We show that 𝒮 G , 𝒱 \mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}} is a variety of dimension ( n + 1 ) ​ p 2 − 1 (n+1)p^{2}-1 , and determine its subdimension (i.e. the dimension of its smallest component) for all n n and p p . In particular we show the subdimension is ( n + 1 ) ​ p 2 − p (n+1)p^{2}-p if n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , or n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and p ≥ 4 p\geq 4 . In the case n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we give a formula for the number of components of given codimension in 𝒮 G , 𝒱 \mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}} . We give explicit decompositions of 𝒮 G , 𝒱 \mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}} for all n n where p = 2 , 3 p=2,3 or 4 4 . Our results in particular show that when n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 and p ≥ 4 p\geq 4 , or n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and p = 3 p=3 , ℂ ​ [ 𝒱 ] G \mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G} does not contain a polynomial or hypersurface separating set. It was proven in [ 20 ] that the same is true if n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 and p = 2 p=2 . The author made a conjecture in [ 19 ] generalising the Skronowski-Weyman theorem for representations of quivers. The results of this paper prove that conjecture in two important special cases: for the quiver with one vertex and an arbitrary number, n n , of loops, and for the quiver with two vertices and n n arrows between them.

1.3.
Invariant theory and separating invariants

Now consider a more general situation in which a linear algebraic group
G
defined over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
acts linearly on an affine
\Bbbk
-variety
\mathcal{V}
. Let
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]}
denote the algebra of polynomial functions on
\mathcal{V}
. Then
G
acts on
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]}
according to the formula

(1)

(g\cdot f)(v)=f(g^{-1}v).

We denote by
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
the subalgebra of
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]}
fixed by this action. For
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
and any
f\in{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
we have
f(v)=f(w)
if
v\in G\cdot w
. If
G
is not finite, then the orbit
G\cdot v
is not closed in general in the Zariski topology, and for reasons of continuity we must have
f(v)=f(w)
for all
v,w\in\mathcal{V}
with
\overline{G\cdot v}\cap\overline{G\cdot w}\neq\emptyset
.

If
f\in{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
and
f(v)\neq f(w)
we say that
f

separates

v
and
w
. We say that
v
and
w
are
separated by invariants
if there exists an invariant separating
v
and
w
. In case
G
is reductive, we have by a result of Mumford that
f(v)=f(w)
for all
f\in{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
if and only if
\overline{Gv}\cap\overline{Gw}\neq\emptyset
, see
[
7
, Corollary 6.1]
. In particular, the invariants separate the orbits if
G
is a finite group.

One can therefore distinguish the orbits of a group action whenever one can find an explicit generating set for
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
, This is a difficult problem in general. For this reason, Derksen and Kemper introduced the following in 2002
[
5
, Definition 2.3.8]
:

Definition 1.3
.

Let

. We say

is a
separating set
for
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
if the following holds for all

:

s(v)=s(w)\ \text{for all}\ s\in S\Leftrightarrow f(v)=f(w)\ \text{for all}\ f\in{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}.

Separating sets of invariants have been an area of much recent interest. In general they have some nicer properties and are easier to construct than generating sets. For example, if
G
is a finite group acting on a vector space

, then the set of invariants of degree
\leq|G|
form a separating set
[
5

. This is also true for generating invariants if
\operatorname{char}(\Bbbk)=0

[

,

]
but fails for generating invariants in the modular case. Separating sets for the rings of invariants
\Bbbk[V]^{C_{p}}
, where
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic

the cyclic group of order

and

is indecomposable were constructed in
[
36
]
. Corresponding sets of generating invariants are known only when
\dim(V)\leq 10

[
40
]
. For the (non-reductive) linear algebraic group
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
of a field of characteristic zero, separating sets for
\Bbbk[V]^{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}}
for arbitary indecomposable linear representations

were constructed in
[

]
, andt hese results were extended to decomposable representations in
[

]

\dim(V)\leq 8

[
3
]
. Finally, for an arbitrary (i.e. non-linear)
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
-variety
\mathcal{V}
, the algebra of invariants
\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{{\mathbb{G}}_{a}}
may not be finitely generated, but it is known that there must exist a finite separating set

Let
S
be a separating set for
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
.. The size of a separating set is bounded below by the dimension of
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
, see
[
20
, Section 1.2]
. A separating set whose size equals the dimension of
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
is sometimes called a polynomial separating set, because it necessarily generates a polynomial subalgebra of
{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
. On the other hand, there always exists a separating set of size
\leq 2\dim({\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}})+1
, albeit such a separating set may necessarily contain non-homogeneous polynomials; see
[
27

for a proof.

The
separating variety
was introduced by Kemper in
[
29
]
:

Definition 1.4
.

\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal{V}^{2}:f(v)=f(w)\ \text{for all}\ f\in{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}\}.

In other words, the separating variety is the subvariety of
\mathcal{V}^{2}
consisting of pairs of points which are not separated by any invariant. We note that
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
is preserved by the natural action of
G^{2}
on
\mathcal{V}^{2}
. If
G
is reductive then we have the alternative description

(2)

\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}=\{(v,w)\in\mathcal{V}^{2}:\overline{G\cdot v}\cap\overline{G\cdot w}\neq\emptyset\}.

Since invariants are constant on orbits. the separating variety always contains the
graph

\Gamma_{G,\mathcal{V}}=\{(v,g\cdot v)\in\mathcal{V}^{2}:g\in G,v\in\mathcal{V}\}

of the action of
G
on
\mathcal{V}
. This is not closed in general, so we have an inclusion
\overline{\Gamma_{G,\mathcal{V}}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
. Often this is an equality, but not always.

We define
I_{G,\mathcal{V}}
to be the ideal of
\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}^{2}]
consisting of the polynomial functions which vanish on
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
. Clearly this is a radical ideal. Then a separating set can be characterised as a subset
S\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
which cuts out the separating variety in
\mathcal{V}^{2}
, in other words (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
)

Proposition 1.5
.

S\subseteq\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
is a separating set if and only if

V_{\mathcal{V}^{2}}(\delta(S))=\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}.

where
\delta:\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]\rightarrow\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}^{2}]=\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]\otimes\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]
is defined by

\delta(f)=1\otimes f-f\otimes 1.

Equivalently, via the Nullstellensatz,
S
is a separating set if and only if

\sqrt{(\delta(f):f\in S)}={I_{G,\mathcal{V}}}.

Consequently the size of a separating set for
\mathcal{V}
is bounded below by the minimum number of generators of
I_{G,\mathcal{V}}
up to radical, that is, the minimum number of elements generating any ideal whose radical is
I_{G,\mathcal{V}}
. (This is called the
arithmetic rank

\operatorname{ara}(I)
of an ideal

). This idea has been used to determine lower bounds for the size of separating sets in several cases: see for example
[
14
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
34
]
.

Recall that the
subdimension

of an algebraic variety

is the dimension of its smallest irreducible component. It follows from Krull’s principal ideal theorem that, for a separating set
S\subseteq{\Bbbk[\mathcal{V}]^{G}}
we have

(3)

|S|\geq 2\dim(\mathcal{V})-\operatorname{sdim}(\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}).

We recommend

(2)

has finite representation type;

(2)

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}]^{G}
contains a polynomial separating set for each dimension vector
\alpha
.

In
[
19
]
, the author made the following conjecture generalising Proposition
1.6
:

Conjecture 1.8
.

Let
Q
be a quiver. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

Q
has finite or tame representation type;

(2)

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}]^{G}
contains a polynomial or hypersurface separating set, for each dimension vector
\alpha
.

Here a hypersurface separating set is a separating set generating a hypersurface, i.e. with cardinality
\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}]^{G})+1
.
Since generating sets are separating sets, the forward direction is known. To prove the conjecture, it remains to show that if
Q
has wild representation type, then there exists a dimension vector
\alpha
for which
|S|>\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}]^{G}+1)
for every separating set
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}]^{G}
.

Example 1.9
.

Representations of the
n
-Kronecker quiver
Q_{n}
with diagram

\vdots

with
n
arrows and dimension vector
(q,p)
over
\mathbb{C}
can be identified with
n
-tuples of
p\times q
matrices, i.e. elements of
{\mathcal{M}}_{p,q}^{n}(\mathbb{C})
, and two such are isomorphic if they lie in the same
\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{GL}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
orbit, where the action is by simultaneous left- and right- multiplication. The quiver is of finite type for
n=1
, tame for
n=2
, and wild for
n\geq 3
. Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})\times\operatorname{SL}_{q}(\mathbb{C})
.
[
19
, Theorem 1.16]
shows that the algebra of invariants
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{n}]^{G}
does not contain a hypersurface separating algebra if
n\geq 5
, while
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{2,2}^{n}]^{G}
is known to be a polynomial algebra for
n\leq 3
, and a hypersurface for
n=4
. To verify the conjecture for this quiver it remains to show that, for
n=3
and for
n=4
, there exists
p,q
such that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p,q}^{n}]^{G}
does not contain a polynomial or hypersurface separating set.

Example 1.10
.

Representations of the
n
-loop quiver
Q_{n}

n

where the
n
represents
n
separate arrows and
\alpha=p
can be identified with
n
-tuples of
p\times p
matrices, i.e. elements of
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}
, with a pair of such representations isomorphic if and only if those
n
-tuples of matrices are simultaneously conjugate under the action of
\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
G=\operatorname{SL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
act on
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
by simultaneous conjugation. The semi-invariant rings of such actions were studied in
[
20
]
. The quiver
Q_{n}
is of tame type if
n=1
and wild otherwise. Theorem 1.4 in loc. cit. shows that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{n}]^{G}
does not contain a hypersurface separating set for
n\geq 4
, and
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}_{2}^{n}]^{G}
is known to be polynomial if
n=2
and a hypersurface if
n=3
. Thus to verify the conjecture for this quiver it remains to show that, for
n=2
or
n=3
there exists some
p
such that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{G}
does not contain a polynomial or hypersurface separating set.

1.5.
Matrix invariants and main results

In this subsection we consider only the
n
-loop quiver
Q_{n}
described in Example
1.10
, and its representations over
\mathbb{C}
. In that case we have
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{V}_{\alpha}={\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
for some
p>0
, and we note that the scalar multiples of the identity matrix in
\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
act trivially, so that
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{\operatorname{SL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})}=\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})}
. We set
G=\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
throughout.

The elements of
\mathcal{V}
wil be viewed as
n
-tuples
{\mathbf{A}}=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n})
, or as
p\times p
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. We call these
n
-matrices for short. For
g\in G
we write the conjugation action as

g\cdot{\mathbf{A}}:=(gA_{1}g^{-1},\ldots,gA_{n}g^{-1}).

For
1\leq i,j\leq p
and
1\leq k\leq n
, let
a_{ij}^{(k)}
denote the
i,j
th entry of
A_{k}
, and write
{\mathbf{a}}_{ij}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
for the vector
(a^{(1)}_{ij},\ldots,a^{(n)}_{ij})
.
If we need to discuss a second element
{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
we shall write
{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}=(A^{\prime}_{1},A^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,A^{\prime}_{n})
with the
i,j
th element of
A^{\prime}_{k}
being
{a^{\prime}}^{(k)}_{ij}
and
{\mathbf{a}}^{\prime}_{ij}=({a^{\prime}}^{(1)}_{ij},\ldots,{a^{\prime}}^{(n)}_{ij})
.

Now for
1\leq i,j\leq p
and
1\leq k\leq n
, let
x_{ij}^{(k)}
denote the linear functional
\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
which picks out the
i,j
th entry of of
A_{k}
, and introduce generic matrices

X_{k}:=\begin{pmatrix}x_{11}^{(k)}&x_{12}^{(k)}\ldots&x_{1p}^{(k)}\\
x_{21}^{(k)}&x_{22}^{(k)}\ldots&x_{2p}^{(k)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
x_{p1}^{(k)}&x_{p2}^{(k)}\ldots&x_{pp}^{(k)}\end{pmatrix}.

Then we have

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]=\mathbb{C}[x_{ij}^{(k)}:i,j=1,\ldots,p,k=1,\ldots,n].

Kac
[
26
, Proposition 4]
discovered a method of computing the Krull dimension of the algebra of semi-invariants of an arbitrary quiver. Applying his method to the
n
-loop quiver we find:

Proposition 1.11
.

The algebra of invariants
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
has dimension
(n-1)p^{2}+1
for
n\geq 2
and
p>1
.

For a finite sequence
I=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
it is easy to see that

t_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}}:=\operatorname{Tr}(X_{i_{1}}X_{i_{2}}\cdots X_{i_{r}})\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}.

Sibirskii
[
37
]
showed that these invariants generate
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
as a vector space. LeBruyn and Procesi
[
31
]
showed that for
p=2
,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
is minimally generated as an algebra by

\{t_{i}:1\leq i\leq n,t_{ij}:1\leq i\leq j\leq n,t_{ijk}:1\leq i<j<k\leq n\},

a generating set with size
\frac{1}{6}(n^{3}+11n)
. In particular,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
is not polynomial for
n\geq 3
and not a hypersurface for
n\geq 4
. Kaygorodov et al
[
28
]
showed that this set is a minimal separating set by inclusion, but that does not necessarily mean that it has the smallest possible cardinality among all separating sets. The following was shown in
[
20
]
:

Proposition 1.12
.

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{2}]^{G}
be a separating set. Then
|S|\geq 5n-5
.

This shows that the given set of generators is a separating set of minimum cardinality if
n=3
but may not be so if
n\geq 4
. It also shows that there is no polynomial or hypersurface separating set for
n\geq 4
, which verifies conjecture
1.8
for
Q_{n}
when
n\geq 4
.

In the case
p=3
,
n\geq 2
, Abeasis and Pittaluga
[
1
]
gave a minimal generating set of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
with cardinality

3n+5\binom{n}{2}+24\binom{n}{3}+51\binom{n}{4}+47\binom{n}{5}+15\binom{n}{6}

where
\binom{n}{k}=0
if
k>n
.
In particular their result shows that
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
is a hypersurface (but not polynomial) for
n=2
and not a hypersurface for
n\geq 3
. See also
[
32
]
. It is not known whether this set is a minimal separating set in general. In the case
p=4
and
n=2
, Drensky and Sadikova
[
11
]
found a minimal set of bihomogeneous polynomials which generate
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{G}
. It is not known whether this set is a minimal generating or separating set.

Our most general results on separating sets are as follows:

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
p\geq 2
, or
n=2
and
p\geq 4
and and let
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{G}
be a separating set. Then
|S|\geq(n-1)p^{2}+p
.

In order to prove these results, we will determine the subdimension of the separating variety
\mathcal{S}_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}}
for all
p\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
in the next section. Our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
p\geq 2
, or
n=2
and
p\geq 4
. Then
\dim(\mathcal{S}_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}})=(n+1)p^{2}-1
and
\operatorname{sdim}(\mathcal{S}_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}})=(n+1)p^{2}-p
.

It is now easy to deduce our main results from the above.

Proof of Theorem
1.13
.

We have by (
3
)

|S|\geq 2\dim({\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n})-\operatorname{sdim}(\mathcal{S}_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{2}^{n}})=2np^{2}-((n+1)p^{2}-p)=(n-1)p^{2}+p.

∎

In particular these results show that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{3}]^{G}
contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set for
n\geq 3
, and that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{G}
contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set for
p\geq 4
and
n\geq 2
. Let
Q_{n}
be a quiver with one node and
n
loops, and recall that
Q_{n}
has finite representation type if
n=1
and wild representation type otherwise. We thus obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.15
.

The representation type of
Q_{n}
is tame or finite if and only if for all dimension (vectors)
p
,
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Rep}(Q_{n},p)]^{\operatorname{SL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})}
contains a polynomial or hypersurface separating set.

Proof.

When
n=1
it is well-known that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{p}]^{G}
is a polynomial ring for all
p
. Conversely, by Theorem
1.14

\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{4}]^{G}
contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set for all
n\geq 2
.
∎

In other words, Conjecture
1.8
is indeed true for the
n
-loop quiver.

Remark 1.16
.

If we were interested in finding the smallest
p
such that
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{Rep}(Q_{n},p)]^{\operatorname{SL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})}
contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set, we could take
p=2
when
n\geq 4
and
p=3
when
n=3
, and
p=4
when
n=2
.

1.6.
Organisation of paper

This paper is organised as follows: in Section 2 we describe a coarse decomposition of the separating variety for
S_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}}
for any
p\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
. In Section 3 we prove that the irreducible components of
S_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}}
are in 1-1 correspondence with the maximal elements of
a certain poset
\mathcal{P}_{p,n}
which we describe in a purely combinatorial fashion. In Section 4 we characterise the maximal elements of
\mathcal{P}_{p,n}
when
n\geq 3
. This allows us to give a formula for the number of components of
S_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}}
of given codimension, and prove Theorem
1.14
for
n\geq 3
. We also give explicit irredundant decompositions of
S_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}}
when
p=2,3,4
and
n\geq 3
. In Section 5 we prove Theorem
1.14
for
n=2
and give explicit irredundant decompositions of
S_{G,{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{2}}
when
p=2,3,4
. In Section 6 we use a homomorphism due to Domokos to deduce lower bounds for the cardinality of a separating set for matrix semi-invariants and prove Conjecture
1.8
for the
n
-Kronecker quiver.

2.
Decomposing the separating variety for matrix invariants

In this section we consider the action of
G:=\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C})
on
\mathcal{V}:={\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}
by simultaneous conjugation, where
n\geq 2
. Our aim is to describe the separating variety
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
as a union of closed irreducible subvarieties.

For each
k\leq p
let
\Pi(p,k)
denote the set of ordered partitions of
p
into
k
nonzero parts. If
\pi\in\Pi(p,k)
we say
k=|\pi|
is the rank of
\pi
. We define
\Pi(p)=\sqcup_{k=1}^{p}\Pi(p,k)
. We write as a convention
\pi=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k})\in\Pi(p,k)
, and if we need to discuss a second partition
\hat{\pi}
we write
|\hat{\pi}|=\hat{k}
and
\hat{\pi}=(\hat{p}_{1},\hat{p}_{2},\ldots,\hat{p}_{\hat{k}})\in\Pi(p,\hat{k})
.
Let
\pi\in\Pi(p,k)
, and
\hat{\pi}\in\Pi(p,k-1)
. We write
\pi\leq\hat{\pi}
if and only if there exists some
1\leq j\leq k-1
with the properties that

•

p_{i}=\hat{p}_{i}
for all
i<j
;

•

p_{j}+p_{j+1}=\hat{p}_{j}

•

p_{i}=\hat{p}_{i-1}
for all
i>j
.

In other words if
\hat{\pi}
is formed by merging two blocks of
\pi
. We extend the relation
\leq
to all pairs of partitions in
\Pi(p)
by taking the transitive and reflexive closure. In this way
\Pi(p)
becomes an anitgraded poset with respect to rank and
\leq
. We say
\pi
is a refinement of
\hat{\pi}
if
\pi\leq\hat{\pi}
.

For
\pi
and
\hat{\pi}
partitions of
p
, a
(\pi,\hat{\pi})
-block
n
-matrix
is an
n
-matrix partitioned by
\pi
along the rows and
\hat{\pi}
along the columns, i.e. into rectangular
p_{i}\times\hat{p}_{j}
submatrices, and a
\pi
-block
n
-matrix
is a
(\pi,\pi)
-block
n
matrix. We adopt the convention that if
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}
is a
(\pi,\hat{\pi})
-block
n
-matrix, then
{\mathbf{B}}_{i,j}
denotes the
p_{i}\times\hat{p}_{j}

n
-matrix in the
(i,j)
th block, with a similar convention for a second
n
-matrix
{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}
. For an ordered
k
-tuple of matrices
(W_{i}\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p_{i}}:i=1,\ldots,k)
we write
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}W_{i}
for the
\pi
-block diagonal matrix with
(i,i)
th block equal to
W_{i}
.

Let
\pi\in\Pi(p,k)
. We denote by
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
the subspace of
\mathcal{V}
whose elements are
\pi
-block-upper triangular
n
-matrices, i.e. those
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}
with
{\mathbf{B}}_{i,j}=0
for
i>j
. The dimension of this space is

(4)

\frac{1}{2}n(p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}).

The easiest way to see this is to consider the opposite subspace
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi}
of
\pi
-block-lower triangular matrices. Evidently
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}+\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi}=\mathcal{V}
, and
\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\pi})=\dim(\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi})
, while
\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\pi}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{\pi})=n(\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})
as this is the space of
\pi
-block diagonal
n
-matrices.

We denote by
P_{\pi}
the parabolic subgroup of
G
stabilising
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
. By an argument similar to the above, its dimension is
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})-1
. The following Lemma generalises
[
19
, Lemma 3.4]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a reductive linear algebraic group over
\Bbbk
and
P\leq G
a parabolic subgroup. Let
V
be a vector space over
\Bbbk
on which
G
acts linearly and let
W
be a closed subset of
V
. Suppose
P\cdot W\subseteq W
. Then
G\cdot W
is closed.

Proof.

Consider the set
Z:=\{(gP,v):g^{-1}v\in W\}\subseteq G/P\times V
. Let
\pi
be the projection
G/P\times V\rightarrow V
. Then
G\cdot W=\pi(Z)
. As
G/P
is a projective, hence complete, variety, the map
\pi
is projective and takes closed sets to closed sets. Clearly
Z
is closed, so
G\cdot W
is closed as required.
∎

Consequently each orbit space
G\cdot\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
is closed, with dimension equal to

(5)

\dim(G)-\dim(P_{\pi})+\dim(\mathcal{V}_{\pi})=p^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}).

For a permutation
\sigma\in S_{k}
we set
\sigma(\pi)=(p_{\sigma(1)},p_{\sigma(2)},\ldots,p_{\sigma(k)})\in\Pi(p,k)
. The action of
S_{k}
induces an equivalence relation on
\Pi(p,k)
. We write
[\pi]
for the equivalence class containing
\pi
. We write

[\Pi(p,k)]=\Pi(p,k)/S_{k}

and
[\Pi(p)]=\sqcup_{k=1}^{p}[\Pi(p,k)]
. The partial order on
\Pi(p)
induces a partial order on
[\Pi(p)]:
we write
[\pi]\leq[\hat{\pi}]
if and only if
\kappa\leq\hat{\kappa}
for some
\kappa\in[\pi]
and
\hat{\kappa}\in[\hat{\pi}]
.

Now for each
\pi\in\Pi(p,k)
and for each permutation
\sigma\in S_{k}
, we define a subspace
\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
of
\mathcal{V}^{2}
as follows:
\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
consists of pairs of
n
-matrices
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})
such that:

•

{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
.

•

{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(\pi)}
.

•

{\mathbf{B}}_{i,i}={\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i)}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.

The dimension of
\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
is
n(p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})
. Notice that
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{(p),\operatorname{id}}=\{({\mathbf{A}},g\cdot{\mathbf{A}}):{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V},g\in G\}
is the graph of the action. It follows from Proposition
1.1
that we have a decomposition of the separating variety into closed irreducible sets:

(6)

\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}\bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi(p,k)}\bigcup_{\sigma\in S_{k}}\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}.

This decomposition may be redundant, i.e. we may be able to delete some terms from the right hand side. Note that, since each term on the right hand side is irreducible, redundancy is only possible if there are inclusions between the terms; if
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
is irreducible then
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
or
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
.

2.1.
Dimensions

Our first task is to compute the dimensions of the varieties on the right hand side of (
6
). Note it is not clear which, if any, of the orbit sets
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
are closed.

Corollary 2.2
.

We have

\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}=G^{2}\cdot\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}.

In particular
G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
is closed if
\pi=(1,1,\ldots,1)
.

Proof.

The first part follows on applying Lemma
2.1
to
\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
which is obviously stablilised by the parabolic subgroup
P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)}
of
G^{2}
. For the second statement we note that if
\pi=(1,\ldots,1)
, then
\sigma(\pi)=\pi
and
P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)}=P^{2}_{\pi}
stabilises
\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}
.
∎

The varieties
\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
have the following inductive description.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{V}^{2}
. Then
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
if and only if
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(\pi)}
and
({\mathbf{B}}_{i,i},{\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i)})\in\overline{\operatorname{GL}_{p_{i}}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{(p_{i}),\operatorname{id}}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.

Proof.

The necessity of
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(\pi)}
is clear as
P_{\pi}
fixes
\mathcal{V}_{\pi}
and
P_{\sigma(\pi)}
fixes
\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(\pi)}
. Suppose that
({\mathbf{B}}_{i,i},{\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i)})\in\overline{\operatorname{GL}_{p_{i}}^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{(p_{i}),\operatorname{id}}}
for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Then there exist
g_{i}(t):\in\operatorname{GL}_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{C}((t))
,
{\mathbf{C}}_{i,i}(t)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p_{i},p_{i}}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}{\mathbf{C}}_{i,i}(t)={\mathbf{B}}_{i,i}

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}g_{i}(t)\cdot{\mathbf{C}}_{i,i}(t)={\mathbf{B}}^{\prime}_{\sigma(i),\sigma(i)}

for all
i=1,\ldots,k
.
Let
g(t)\in P_{\pi}(\mathbb{C}((t)))
be defined by

g(t):=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}(t)\in P_{\pi},

i.e.
g(t)
is the block-diagonal matrix with blocks given by
g_{i}(t)
. Now we define
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
blockwise according to the partition
\pi
: the image of the
i,j
th block is given by

{\mathbf{B}}_{i,j}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}{\mathbf{C}}_{i,j}(t)&i=j\\
{\mathbf{B}}_{i,j}&\ \text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Then we have

(g(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t),{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t))\in\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}

for all
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
. It follows that
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
as required.

Conversely suppose that
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
. Then there exist
g(t)\in P_{\pi}(\mathbb{C}((t)))
,
{\mathbf{A}}(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{\pi}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
and
{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t)\in\mathcal{V}_{\sigma(\pi)}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}{\mathbf{A}}(t)={\mathbf{A}},\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t)={\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}

and

(7)

(g(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t),{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t))\in\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}

Corollary 2.4
.

The dimension of the variety
\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
is
(n+1)p^{2}-k
.

Proof.

First suppose

, then
\pi=(p)
and
\sigma=\operatorname{id}
, and

is the graph of the action, hence

\dim(\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})=\dim(\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{(p),\operatorname{id}}})=\dim(\mathcal{V})+\dim(G)=np^{2}+p^{2}-1=(n+1)p^{2}-1

as required. Now suppose

2.2
we have

\displaystyle\dim(\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=\dim(G^{2}\cdot\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=\dim(G^{2})-\dim(P_{\pi})-\dim(P_{\sigma(\pi)})+\dim(\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=2\dim(G)-2\dim(P_{\pi})+\dim(\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=2(p^{2}-1)-(p^{2}-2+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})+\dim(\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=p^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}+\dim(\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}).

Now by Lemma
2.3
we deduce that

\displaystyle\dim(\overline{(P_{\pi}\times P_{\sigma(\pi)})\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=(\dim(\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma})-2n(\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}))+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dim(\overline{\operatorname{GL}^{2}_{p_{i}}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{(p_{i}),\operatorname{id}}})

\displaystyle=n(p^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})-2n\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}((n+1)p_{i}^{2}-1)

by the
k=1

\displaystyle=n(p^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2})+\sum_{i=1}^{k}((n+1)p_{i}^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=np^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{2}-k

hence

\displaystyle\dim(\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}})

\displaystyle=(n+1)p^{2}-k

as required.
∎

We note that we have proved, using the above and (

For each

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
({\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{\Gamma_{G,\mathcal{V}}}
. Then there exist

such that
(h\cdot{\mathbf{A}},h^{\prime}\cdot{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{\Gamma_{T,\mathcal{V}}}
.

Proof.

The hypotheses tell us that there exist
{\mathbf{A}}(t)\in\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
,
g(t)\in\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C}((t)))
with

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}{\mathbf{A}}(t)={\mathbf{A}}

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}g(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t)={\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}.

Since
\mathbb{C}[[t]]
is principle ideal domain and
\mathbb{C}((t))
its field of fractions, there exist
h(t),h^{\prime}(t)\in\operatorname{GL}_{p}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
such that

is diagonal. Let
{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t)=g(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t)\in\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
, then

{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t)=h^{\prime}(t)^{-1}\lambda(t)h(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t)

for all

. Therefore

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}h^{\prime}(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lambda(t)\cdot(h(t)\cdot{\mathbf{A}}(t)).

This shows that
(h\cdot{\mathbf{A}},h^{\prime}\cdot{\mathbf{A}}^{\prime})\in\overline{\Gamma_{T,\mathcal{V}}}
, where
h=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}h(t)
and
h^{\prime}=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}h^{\prime}(t)
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In the above we used the following basic linear algebraic fact: if
R
is a principle ideal domain and

its field of fractions, then for all

there exist

such that

. This is an easy special case of a more general (and more difficult to prove) result known as the Cartan-Iwahori-Matsumoto decomposition
[

, p. 52]
: let

be a discrete valuation ring with field of fractions

. Suppose

is a reductive linear algebraic group defined over

and

a maximal torus in
G
. Let
g\in G
, then there exist
h,h^{\prime}\in G(R)
such that
h^{\prime}gh^{-1}\in T
. Thus, using this result one can generalise Lemma
3.6
above to the setting where

is an arbitrary reductive linear algebraic group and
T
is a maximal torus in
G
.

4.
Examples:
n\geq 3

We want to find the irreducible components of
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
. As shown in the previous, section, these are the orbit spaces
\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}}
where
(\pi,\sigma)\in\mathcal{P}_{p,n}
is maximal. It turns out that if
n\geq 3
then the maximal elements are easy to describe - indeed, maximality depends only on the permutation
\sigma
.

We will say
\sigma\in S_{k}
is a
partial reversal
if
\sigma
in square bracket notation contains a substring
[l+1,l]
, i.e. if for some
1\leq l<k
we have

(16)

\sigma(l+1)+1=\sigma(l).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
(\pi,\sigma)\in\mathcal{P}_{p,n}
. Then
(\pi,\sigma)
is maximal if and only if
\sigma
is not a partial reversal.

Proof.

Suppose
\sigma\in S_{k}
is a partial reversal with
l
satisfying (
16
). Define

\hat{\pi}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{l-1},p_{l}+p_{l+1},p_{l+2},\ldots,p_{k})\in\Pi(p,k-1)

and

\hat{\sigma}=[\sigma(1),\sigma(2),\ldots,\sigma(l-1),\sigma(l+1),\ldots,\sigma(k)]\in S_{k-1}.

Then
\pi\leq\hat{\pi}
and

\sigma=((\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id},\ldots,\operatorname{id},(12),\operatorname{id},\ldots,\operatorname{id})^{\hat{\sigma}})^{*},

and
((p_{l},p_{l+1}),(12))\preceq((p_{l}+p_{l+1}),\operatorname{id})
. By Proposition
2.11
we have
(\pi,\sigma)\preceq(\hat{\pi},\hat{\sigma})
and so
(\pi,\sigma)
is a not maximal.

Conversely, suppose we have an inclusion
(\pi,\sigma)\preceq(\hat{\pi},\hat{\sigma})
for some
(\hat{\pi},\hat{\sigma})\in\mathcal{P}_{p,n}
.
By Theorem
3.1
we may assume that
\hat{k}=k-1
. Then by Proposition
2.11
we have
\sigma=((\operatorname{id},\operatorname{id},\ldots,\operatorname{id},\kappa,\operatorname{id},\ldots,\operatorname{id})^{\hat{\sigma}})^{*}
, where the
\kappa
appears at index
l
, and
((p_{l},p_{l+1}),\kappa)\preceq(p_{l}+p_{l+1},\operatorname{id})
. Since
n\geq 3
, by Proposition
3.4
we must have
\kappa=(12)
. Then
\sigma
in square bracket notation contains the substring
[l+1,l]
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
p\geq 2
and
n\geq 3
we have a complete irredundant decomposition

(17)

\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{p}\bigcup_{\pi\in\Pi(p,k)}\bigcup_{\sigma\in T_{k}}\overline{G^{2}\cdot\mathcal{C}_{\pi,\sigma}},

where
T_{k}
is the subset of
S_{k}
consisting of permutations which are not partial reversals.

The problem of enumerating
|T_{k}|
is a classical one in enumerative combinatorics. One may show that it satisfies the recurrence

(18)

|T_{k}|=(k-1)|T_{k-1}|+(k-2)|T_{k-2}|

for
k\geq 3
, and obviously
|T_{1}|=|T_{2}|=1
. To see this, suppose
\sigma\in T_{k}
is given in square bracket form, and consider the permutation in
S_{k-1}
obtained by removing the entry
k
from the list. The resulting permutation is either in
T_{k-1}
or it is not. For each element of
T_{k-1}
we can construct
(k-1)
different elements of
T_{k}
by inserting the entry
k
anywhere except immediately prior to
k-1
. On the other hand, if when we remove
k
we get an element of
S_{k-1}\setminus T_{k-1}
, that element must have exactly one substring of the form
[l+1,l]
. The entry immediately after this substring cannot be
l-1
, thus if we remove
l
and decrease each entry
>l
in the string by 1 we obtain an element of
T_{k-2}
. Given an element of
T_{k-2}
, we may construct an element of
S_{k-1}
by replacing any of its
k-2
entries
l
by the string
[l+1,l]
and increasing each entry
>l
by 1, and for each such element we obtain a unique element of
T_{k}
by inserting
k
between
l+1
and
l
.

One may prove by induction that for
k\geq 2
we have

(19)

|T_{k}|=\sum_{q=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{q}(-1)^{q}(k-q)!.

The first few values of
|T_{k}|
are
1,1,3,11,53,309
, and it is A000255 in the OEIS
[
39
]
.

For
n\geq 3
the number of components of
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
of dimension
(n+1)p^{2}-k
is given by

\binom{p-1}{k-1}|T_{k}|

with binomial term here being
|\Pi(p,k)|
. In particular,
T_{p}
is the number of components of
\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}}
with (minimum possible) dimension
(n+1)p^{2}-p
. Since it is easy to see that
|T_{p}|\geq 1
for every
p
(for example, the identity is in
T_{p}
for every
p
), we get that
\operatorname{sdim}(\mathcal{S}_{G,\mathcal{V}})=(n+1)p^{2}-p
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.14
in the case
n\geq 3
.

6.
Matrix Semi-invariants

In this section we study a different but related group action. Let
H=\operatorname{SL}_{p}\times\operatorname{SL}_{p}
act on
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}
by the formula

(h_{1},h_{2})\cdot A=h_{1}Ah_{2}^{-1}

and extend the action diagonally to
\mathcal{V}:={\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}
. We assume
p>1
. The algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H}
is called the
algebra of
p\times p
matrix semi-invariants.
These are the semi-invariants of the
n
-Kronecker quiver which we described in the introduction with dimension vector
(p,p)
.

When
n=1
, for any
p
,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H}
has a single generator
\det(X_{1})
, and when
n=2
, a generating set is given by the coefficients of the polynomial
\det(t_{1}X_{1}+t_{2}X_{2})\in\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}][t_{1},t_{2}]
. In both cases, the ring of invariants is polynomial, see, for instance
[
24
,
25
]
.

For
n\geq 3
and
p\geq 2
, the algebra of invariants
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H}
is not a polynomial ring. While an infinite vector space basis is known for all
p
and
n
, a minimal generating set for
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H}
for all
n\geq 3
is known only in the case
p=2
. The generating set, due to Domokos
[
9
]
, has cardinality
n
+
\binom{n}{2}+\binom{n}{4}
. Domokos also showed that this set is a minimal separating set
[
8
]
.

The purpose of this section is to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and
p\geq 4
. Then the algebra of matrix semi-invariants contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set.

Recalling that the
n
-Kronecker quiver is finite for
n=1
and tame for
n=2
, this establishes Conjecture
1.8
for this quiver.

We will need to know the dimension of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H}
. Applying Kac’s formula
[
26
, Proposition 4]
once more we find:

Proposition 6.2
.

For all
p\geq 2
and
n\geq 3
, we have
\dim(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{V}]^{H})=(n-2)p^{2}+2
.

Now the proof of Theorem
6.1
is a straightforward application of a result of Domokos:
for
n\geq 2
consider the morphism
\rho:{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n+1}
given by

\rho(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n-1})=(A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n-1},I)

where
I
is the
p\times p
identity matrix. By
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
, the induced morphism

\rho^{*}:\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n-1}]^{G}

is surjective. This can be used to show that for any separating set
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{H}
,
\rho^{*}(S)\subseteq\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n-1}]^{G}
is a separating set, see
[
8
, Corollary 6.3]
.

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
p\geq 4
and suppose
S\subseteq\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{H}
is a separating set. Then
\rho^{*}(S)
is a separating set for
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n-1}]^{G}
. Since
n-1\geq 2
, by Theorem
1.14
we have

|S|\geq(n-2)p^{2}+p.

Since
\dim(\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}])^{H}=(n-2)p^{2}+2
and
p-2\geq 2
, we get the desired result.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If we were interested in finding the smallest
p
such that
\mathbb{C}[{\mathcal{M}}_{p}^{n}]^{H}
contains no polynomial or hypersurface separating set for given
n
we could take
p=2
when
n\geq 5
and
p=4

Finite analogs of partition bias related to hook length two and a variant of Sylvester’s map
Alexander Berkovich, Aritram Dhar

Abstract
. In this paper, we count the total number of hooks of length two in all odd partitions of n n and all distinct partitions of n n with a bound on the largest part of the partitions. We generalize inequalities of Ballantine, Burson, Craig, Folsom and Wen by showing there is a bias in the number of hooks of length two in all odd partitions over all distinct partitions of n n in presence of a bound on the largest part. To establish such a bias, we use a variant of Sylvester’s map. Then, we conjecture a similar finite bias for a weighted count of hooks of length two and prove it when we remove the bound on the largest part.

Let us now recall

[

. Define

(resp.

Consider the map
\psi:\mathcal{O}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}_{n}
and let

\pi_{o}
symmetrically row-by-row and then read the number of cells along the

starting from the last row following the

to the top and then to the right. Then, read the number of cells along the hook to the left of the spine from bottom to top and left followed by the number of cells along the hook to the right of the spine from bottom to top and right and so on. Finally, we get a partition
\pi_{d}
of
n
into distinct parts, i.e.,
\pi_{d}\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
.

Clearly,
\psi
is invertible. Consider
\psi^{-1}:=\sigma:\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{n}
and let
\pi_{d}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\ldots)\in\mathcal{D}_{n}
. Now, consider the alternating sum of parts
\gamma(\pi_{d})
of
\pi_{d}
. We then construct a center hook consisting of
\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells placed vertically from bottom to top to form a spine and
\lambda_{1}-\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells placed horizontally to the right of the topmost cell of the spine. Then, symmetrically insert
\lambda_{1}-\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells horizontally to the left of the topmost cell of the spine. Then, add
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}+\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells vertically from top to bottom below the rightmost cell of the horizontal piece to the left of the spine. Then, symmetrically insert
\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}+\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells vertically from top to bottom starting from the cell in second row and in second column to the right of the spine. Now, add
\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{1}-\gamma(\pi_{d})
cells to the right of the cell in second row and in second column to the right of the spine. Proceeding in this fashion, after exhausting all the parts of
\pi_{d}
, we read the number of cells in the newly constructed symmetric diagram row-by-row from top to bottom to get parts of a partition
\pi_{o}
of
n
into odd parts, i.e.,
\pi_{o}\in\mathcal{O}_{n}
.

3.
Proofs of Main Results

In this section, we present our proofs of the main results.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Clearly,

\displaystyle a_{2}(L,n)=\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}ma_{2}(L,m,n).

If
\pi_{o}
is a partition into odd parts, then the number of hooks of length
2
is equal to the number of different part sizes of
\pi_{o}
that are greater than
1
plus the number of different part sizes of
\pi_{o}
that occur atleast twice. Let
F_{2}(L,z;q)
denote the bi-variate generating function of
a_{2}(L,m,n)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle F_{2}(L,z;q)

\displaystyle:=\sum\limits_{n,m\geq 0}a_{2}(L,m,n)z^{m}q^{n}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\left(1+q+\dfrac{zq^{2}}{1-q}\right)\prod\limits_{n=2}^{L}\left(1+zq^{2n-1}+\dfrac{z^{2}q^{2(2n-1)}}{1-q^{2n-1}}\right),

where (
3.1
) follows from standard combinatorial arguments in
[
2
]
.

From the definition of
a_{2}(L,m,n)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}a_{2}(L,n)q^{n}=\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}\bigg|_{z=1}F_{2}(L,z;q).

Using logarithmic differentiation on both sides of (
3.1
) with respect to
z
and setting
z=1
, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}a_{2}(L,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(q^{2}+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{L}\left(q^{2n-1}+q^{4n-2}\right)\right)

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(q^{2}+q^{3}\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}+q^{6}\dfrac{1-q^{4L-4}}{1-q^{4}}\right)

where the last line of
(
3.2
)
follows using geometric series. Thus, we have a proof of Theorem
2.1
.

∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem

Clearly,

\displaystyle b_{2}(L,n)=\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}mb_{2}(L,m,n).

In the Young diagram of a partition

into distinct parts, the number of hooks of length

in

is equal to the number of parts

and remove the

from the last part,

from the second last part, etc., to obtain an ordinary partition
\mu
having largest part

, where

is the number of rows of the Young diagram of
\pi_{d}
from which

are removed. Then, the

\displaystyle 1+2+\ldots+n=\dfrac{n(n+1)}{2}.

Now, we count the number of different part sizes in

\displaystyle=q^{2}\dfrac{1-q^{L-1}}{1-q}(-q^{2};q)_{L-2},

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

From Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\left(a_{2}(L,n)-b_{2}(L,n)\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(q^{2}+q^{3}\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}+q^{6}\dfrac{1-q^{4L-4}}{1-q^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad-q^{2}\dfrac{1-q^{L-1}}{1-q}(-q^{2};q)_{L-2}

\displaystyle=q^{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(1+q\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}\right)-\dfrac{1-q^{L-1}}{1-q}(-q^{2};q)_{L-2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{q^{6}(1-q^{4L-4})}{(1-q^{4})(q;q^{2})_{L}}

(3.6)

\displaystyle=q^{2}(A_{L}(q)-B_{L}(q))+C_{L}(q),

where

\displaystyle A_{L}(q)=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(1+q\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}\right),\quad B_{L}(q)=(-q^{2};q)_{L-2}\dfrac{1-q^{L-1}}{1-q},

and

\displaystyle C_{L}(q)=\dfrac{q^{6}(1-q^{4L-4})}{(1-q^{4})(q;q^{2})_{L}}.

It is clear that
C_{L}(q)
, when expanded as a
q
-series, has non-negative coefficients, i.e.,
C_{L}(q)\succeq 0
.

Now, define
\mathcal{A}_{L,n}
to be the set of all pair partitions
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
of
n
such that
|\pi_{1}|+|\pi_{2}|=n
where
\pi_{1}
is a partition into blue odd parts having
l(\pi_{1})\leq 2L-1
and
\pi_{2}
is a partition into red odd parts having
l(\pi_{2})\leq 2L-3
and
\nu(\pi_{2})\leq 1
.

Similarly, define
\mathcal{B}_{L,n}
to be the set of all pair partitions
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})
of
n
such that
|\pi_{1}|+|\pi_{2}|=n
where
\pi_{1}
is a partition into blue distinct parts different from
1
where
l(\pi_{1})\leq L-1
and
\pi_{2}
is a partition into red parts having
l(\pi_{2})\leq L-2
and
\nu(\pi_{2})\leq 1
.

Note that
A_{L}(q)
(resp.
B_{L}(q)
) is the generating function for pair partitions
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{A}_{L,n}
(resp.
\mathcal{B}_{L,n}
). We will now show that
A_{L}(q)\succeq B_{L}(q)
by providing an explicit injection from
\mathcal{B}_{L,n}
to
\mathcal{A}_{L,n}
.

As a corollary, we will have the desired inequality in Theorem
2.3
.

For every non-negative integer
n
, we consider the following map:

\displaystyle\varphi=\varphi_{L,n}:\mathcal{B}_{L,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{L,n}

Let
(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\mathcal{B}_{L,n}
and define
\varphi
according to the following cases:

(1)

Case I:
\pi_{2}=\emptyset

In this case, define

\displaystyle\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))=\varphi((\pi_{1},\emptyset)):=(\sigma(\pi_{1}),\emptyset),

1

\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))\in\mathcal{A}_{L,n}

l(\sigma(\pi_{1}))\leq 2l(\pi_{1})-1\leq 2(L-1)-1=2L-3\leq 2L-1

l(\pi_{1})\leq L-1
,
l(\emptyset)=0\leq 2L-3
,
\nu(\emptyset)=0\leq 1

|\sigma(\pi_{1})|+|\emptyset|=|\pi_{1}|+0=n

(2)

Case II:
\pi_{2}=(\lambda_{1})
where
\lambda_{1}

(\geq 1)
is odd

\displaystyle\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))=\varphi((\pi_{1},(\lambda_{1}))):=(\sigma(\pi_{1}),(\lambda_{1})),

where
\sigma

1
.

Clearly,
\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))\in\mathcal{A}_{L,n}

l(\sigma(\pi_{1}))\leq 2l(\pi_{1})-1\leq 2(L-1)-1=2L-3\leq 2L-1

\lambda_{1}

(3)

\pi_{2}=(\lambda_{1})

\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))=\varphi((\pi_{1},(\lambda_{1}))):=(\sigma(\pi^{*}_{1}),(\lambda_{1}-1)),

1

Clearly,
\varphi((\pi_{1},\pi_{2}))\in\mathcal{A}_{L,n}

l(\sigma(\pi^{*}_{1}))\leq 2l(\pi_{1})-1\leq 2(L-1)-1=2L-3\leq 2L-1

l(\pi^{*}_{1})\leq L-1

\lambda_{1}-1
is odd,
l((\lambda_{1}-1))=\lambda_{1}-1\leq L-2\leq 2L-3
,
\nu((\lambda_{1}-1))=1\leq 1
, and
|\sigma(\pi^{*}_{1})|+|(\lambda_{1}-1)|=|\pi_{1}|+1+\lambda_{1}-1=n
.

Then, it is easy to verify the following lemma

3.4.
Proof of Theorem
2.4

From the definition of
F_{2}(L,z;q)
in (
3.1
), observe that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}(m^{2}-m)a_{2}(L,m,n)\right)q^{n}=\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}\bigg|_{z=1}F_{2}(L,z;q).

Using logarithmic differentiation on both sides of (
3.1
) with respect to
z
, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}F_{2}(L,z;q)=F_{2}(L,z;q)\left(\dfrac{q^{2}}{1+(z-1)q^{2}}+\sum\limits_{n=2}^{L}\dfrac{q^{2n-1}+(2z-1)q^{4n-2}}{1+(z-1)q^{2n-1}+(z^{2}-z)q^{4n-2}}\right).

Now, applying logarithmic differentiation on both sides of (
3.7
) with respect to
z
and setting
z=1
, we have

(3.8)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}(m^{2}-m)a_{2}(L,m,n)\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\left(q^{2}+q^{3}\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}+q^{6}\dfrac{1-q^{4L-4}}{1-q^{4}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\Bigg(-q^{4}+q^{6}\dfrac{1-q^{4L-4}}{1-q^{4}}-2q^{9}\dfrac{1-q^{6L-6}}{1-q^{6}}

\displaystyle\quad-q^{12}\dfrac{1-q^{8L-8}}{1-q^{8}}\Bigg).

Dividing both sides of (
3.8
) by
2
and simplifying, we get

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}\binom{m}{2}a_{2}(L,m,n)\right)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{L}}\Bigg(q^{5}\dfrac{1-q^{2L-2}}{1-q^{2}}+(q^{6}+q^{8})\dfrac{1-q^{4L-4}}{1-q^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad+q^{8}\left[\begin{matrix}L-1\\
2\end{matrix}\right]_{q^{2}}+q^{11}\dfrac{1-q^{2}}{1-q^{2L}}\left[\begin{matrix}L\\
3\end{matrix}\right]_{q^{2}}((1+q^{2})(1+q^{2L-2})

\displaystyle\quad+q^{2}+q^{2L})+q^{16}\left[\begin{matrix}L-1\\
2\end{matrix}\right]_{q^{4}}\Bigg).

Thus, we have a proof of Theorem
2.4
.

∎

3.5.

Differentiating both sides of (

(3.9)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial}{\partial z}G_{2}(L,z;q)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{L}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}jq^{\frac{j(j+1)}{2}}(z-1)^{j-1}\left[\begin{matrix}L-j\\

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}(m^{2}-m)b_{2}(L,m,n)\right)q^{n}=2\sum\limits_{n=2}^{L}q^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}+3}\left[\begin{matrix}L-2\\

(3.11)

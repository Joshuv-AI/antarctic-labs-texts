On the continuous properties for the 3D incompressible rotating Euler equations
Jinlu Li, Yanghai Yu, Neng Zhu

Abstract




Abstract:
In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the 3D Euler equations with the Coriolis force in the whole space. We first establish the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of solution to this system in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then we prove that the Cauchy problem is ill-posed in two different sense:
(1) the solution of this system is not uniformly continuous dependence on the initial data in the same Besov spaces, which extends the recent work of Himonas-Misiołek
[
19
, Comm. Math. Phys., 296, 2010]
to the more general framework of Besov spaces;
(2) the solution of this system cannot be Hölder continuous in time variable in the same Besov spaces. In particular, the solution of the system is discontinuous in the weaker Besov spaces at time zero. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first one addressing the issue on the failure of Hölder continuous in time of solution to the classical Euler equations with(out) the Coriolis force.

Keywords:
Euler equations; Coriolis force; Ill-posedness.

MSC (2010):
35B30; 76U05; 76B03.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we focus on the Cauchy problem for the Euler equations with the Coriolis force in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
describing the motion of perfect incompressible fluids in the rotational framework:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\Omega e_{3}\times u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
u(t=0,x)=u_{0}(x),&\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the vector field
u(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
stands for the velocity of the fluid, the quantity
P(t,x):[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the scalar pressure, and
\mathrm{div\,}u=0
means that the fluid is incompressible.
The constant
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}
represents the speed of rotation around
the vertical unit vector
e_{3}=(0,0,1)
and is called the Coriolis parameter. Problems concerning large-scale atmospheric and oceanic flows are known to be dominated by rotational effects. For this reason, almost all of the models of oceanography and meteorology dealing with large-scale phenomena include a Coriolis force. For example, an oceanic circulation featuring a hurricane is caused by the large rotation. There is no doubt that other physical effects are of similar significance like salinity, natural boundary conditions and so on. The dispersive effect of rotation in fluid flows has been studied in the literature from various perspectives, see, e.g., geophysical flows
[
16
,
27
,
32
]
, life span and asymptotic behaviour in the case of fast rotations
[
10
,
11
,
15
,
26
]
and almost global stability
[
17
]
.

Before stating related results for (
1.1
) precisely, we recall the notion of well-posedness in the sense of Hadamard. We say that the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f=F(f),\\
f(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.2)

is locally Hadamard well-posed in a Banach space
X
if the following conditions hold

1.

(Local existence)  For any initial data
u_{0}\in X
, there exists a short time
T=T(u_{0})>0
and a solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
to the Cauchy problem (
1.2
);

2.

(Uniqueness)  This solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is unique in the space
\mathcal{C}([0,T),X)
;

3.

(Continuous Dependence)  The data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is continuous in the following
sense: for any
u_{0}
, any
T_{1}<T\left(u_{0}\right)
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,u_{0})
such that for any
\widetilde{u}_{0}
, if
\left\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\right\|_{X}\leq\delta
then
\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})
exists up to
T_{1}
and

\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,T_{1}],X)}\leq\varepsilon.

Furthermore, we say that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is uniform continuous if for any
T_{1}<T
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
independent of
u_{0}
or
\widetilde{u}_{0}
such that, if
\|u_{0}-\widetilde{u}_{0}\|_{X}\leq\delta
,
then
\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})
exists up to
T_{1}
and

\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(\widetilde{u}_{0})\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,T_{1}],X)}\leq\varepsilon.

We would like to emphasize that, in this paper, we say the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) is
well-posed
in a Banach space
X
if it enjoys existence, uniqueness and continuous dependence on the initial data. We say that the Cauchy problem (
1.2
) is
semi-linearly well-posed
(see
[
30
]
) for the initial data in
X
if it is Hadamard well-posed and in addition, the solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
is uniformly continuous from any bounded subset
B\subset X
into
C\left([0,T];X\right)
. In particular, we are interested in the lack of continuity with respect to time variable or uniform continuity of solution with respect to the initial data.

Known Well/Ill-posedness results

Let us first review some of the previous works in this direction.

The classical Euler equations.

-framework since the Riesz transform is not bounded on

[

proved that the solution of the Euler equations cannot be continuous as a function

Recently, Misiołek-Yoneda

showed that the solution map for the Euler equations is not even continuous in the space of Hölder continuous functions and thus not locally Hadamard well-posed in

. Concerning the non-uniform continuity of the data-to-solution map, we would like to mention that the beautiful results of Himonas-Misiołek
[
19
]
covered both the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and the whole spaces

cases. More precisely, they proved that the solution map for the Euler equations in bi(tri)-dimension is not uniformly continuous in Sobolev spaces
H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
s>0
. Bourgain and Li
[

]
settled the border line case

.

In the case of
\Omega\neq 0

[

\mathcal{C}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,T];H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

with a weaker assumption on the lower bound of

We should mention that the dispersion relation underlying the linear system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\Omega e_{3}\times u+\nabla P=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\mathrm{div\,}u=0,&\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3},\\

Compared with the classical Euler equations, we would like to emphasize that this dispersive mechanism exhibits

1.2
Motivation

A fundamental challenge in mathematical physics is to understand the behavior of solutions for the complex rotating fluids. From the PDE’s point of view, it is crucial to know if an equation which models a physical phenomenon is well-posed in the Hadamard’s sense: existence, uniqueness, and continuous dependence of the solutions with respect to the initial data. In particular, the lack of continuous dependence would cause incorrect solutions or non meaningful solutions. It is known that, the non-uniform continuity of data-to-solution map suggests that the local well-posedness cannot be established by the contraction mappings principle since this would imply Lipschitz continuity for the solution map. For certain types of evolutionary equations, for example, the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) and nonlinear Schrödinger equations, the flow map is Lipschitz continuous. Paticularly, the KdV equation is shown to be well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>-3/4
, then the solution map is Lipschitz on the same
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
24
]
). However, for the Burgers equation

\partial_{t}u+u\partial_{x}u=0
, which is the most simple quasilinear symmetric hyperbolic equation,
Kato
[
22
]
showed that the dependence on initial data of the Burgers equation in
H^{k}
with
k\geq 2
is continuous but not Hölder continuous with any prescribed exponent. This example indicates that the continuity of the solution map for a symmetric hyperbolic system is a delicate issue. Chen-Liu-Zhang
[
13
]
proved that the solution map of the b-family equation is Hölder continuous as a map from a bounded set of
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s>3/2
with
H^{r}(\mathbb{R})(0\leq r<s)
topology, to
C([0,T],H^{r}(\mathbb{R}))
for some
T>0
. In this paper, for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
), we shall study the continuity of solution with respect to time variable or uniform continuity of solution with respect to the initial data.

Assume that
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
, based on the local well-posedness theory for the Euler equations (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
), we know that there exists a solution
u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
for the Euler-Coriolis system (
1.1
). Naturally, we may wonder whether or not the solution
u
can belong to
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Therefore, we are interested in the following question:

u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}\;\Rightarrow\;\exists\;u\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})\;\overset{?}{\Rightarrow}\;u\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})\quad\text{with}\;\alpha\in(0,1).

If the initial data
u_{0}
has more regularity, that is
u_{0}\in B^{s^{\prime}}_{p,r}
for some
s^{\prime}>s
, by the interpolation argument, we can deduce that
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with
\alpha=s^{\prime}-s
.
In this paper, we will show that there exits initial data
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}
such that the corresponding solution of the Euler-Coriolis system (
1.1
) can not belong to
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
with any
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

1.3
Main Result

We can now state our main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
(s,p,r)
satisfying

\displaystyle s>\frac{3}{p}+1,(p,r)\in(1,\infty)\times[1,\infty]\quad\mathrm{or}\quad s=\frac{3}{p}+1,(p,r)\in(1,\infty)\times\{1\}.

(1.3)

Then there exists a short time
T>0
such that the Euler-Coriolis system (
1.1
) admits a unique solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
satisfying that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

(1.4)

Furthermore, if
r<\infty
, the above solution
u\in C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
is continuous dependent in
B^{s}_{p,r}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Following the proof of the local well-posedness results for the Euler equations (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.1]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
), we can prove Theorem
1.1
. Since the procedure is standard, we omit it.

Next, we consider the property of continuous dependence of solutions of the Cauchy
problem for (
1.1
) and show that the solution map for the Euler-Coriolis system (
1.1
) cannot be uniformly continuous in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
. From now on, we denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})=\text{\rm the solution map of}\,\eqref{CE}\,\text{\rm with initial data}\,u_{0}

and denote any bounded subset
U_{R}
in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by

U_{R}:=\left\{u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq R,\;\mathrm{div}u_{0}=0\right\}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(s,p,r)
satisfying (
1.3
). The data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is not uniformly continuous from any bounded subset in
U_{R}\subset B^{s}_{p,r}
into
\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
. More precisely, there exist two initial data sequences
f_{n}+g_{n}
and
f_{n}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|(f_{n})^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\approx 1\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|(f_{n})^{(1)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|(f_{n})^{(3)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|g_{n}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}=0,

but the distance of second component of the difference between the solution sequences
\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n}+g_{n})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n})

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n}+g_{n})-\mathbf{S}_{t}(f_{n})\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\gtrsim t,\quad\forall\;t\in[0,T].

Remark 1.2
.

We should mention that Theorem
1.2
is stronger than that for the Euler equations in
[
19
]
. On the one hand, the non-uniform continuous is established in the more general framework of Besov spaces. On the other hand, Theorem
1.2
is focused on the second component of data-to-solution map which leads to the non-uniform continuous.

Moreover, we obtain the following

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
(s,p,r)
satisfying (
1.3
). For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exists
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{t^{\alpha}}=+\infty.

Remark 1.3
.

We would like to mention that Theorem
1.3
is new. In fact, Theorem
1.1
tells us that the solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
for (
1.1
) is continuous in time
in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
r<\infty
while Theorem
1.3
furthermore indicates that the solution
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
for (
1.1
) cannot be Hölder continuous in time in the same Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
.

Finally, as a by-product, we have

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume that
(s,p,r=\infty)
satisfying (
1.3
). There exists
u_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that the data-to-solution map
u_{0}\mapsto\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
of the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\geq\varepsilon_{0}>0.

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem
1.4
implies the ill-posedness of (
1.1
) in the weaker Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
in the sense that the solution map to this system starting from
u_{0}
is discontinuous at
t=0
in the metric of
B^{s}_{p,\infty}
.

Remark 1.5
.

As mentioned above, (
1.1
) with
\Omega=0
is the classical Euler equations. Thus our main Theorems
1.1
-
1.4
hold true for the classical Euler equations.

1.4
Strategies to the proof

We comment on a few points of main ideas and difficulties.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
In the case when
p\in(1,\infty)
, since the Coriolis force has no impact on the well-posedness results, we can prove Theorem
1.1
by following the proof of the local well-posedness results for the Euler equations (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.1 ]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.1 ]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.
The main task is to construct initial
data such that the second component of the initial velocity is uniformly bounded in
B^{s}_{p,r}
, and in the same time to have the other component of the initial velocity remain small. We first choose two sequences of initial values
u^{n}_{0,1}=f_{n}+g_{n}
and
u^{n}_{0,2}=f_{n}
, which can generate the approximate solutions
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0,1})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0,2})
, respectively. Next, we perform perturbation analysis (See Proposition
3.1
) to show the second component of the difference between the data-to-solution maps
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0,1})
and
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u^{n}_{0,2})
is bounded below by a positive constant at any later time, which means the solution map is not uniformly continuous.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
In order to prove the failure of Hölder regularity of solutions for the (
1.1
), we would like to first find initial condition to generate the corresponding solutions to (
1.1
) satisfying that

\displaystyle\frac{\left\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}}{t^{\alpha}}\geq C(t)\to+\infty\quad\text{as}\quad t\to 0^{+}.

Our key idea is to decompose the difference between the data-to-solution map
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})
and initial data
u_{0}
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}+t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})-t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0}),

here and in what follows we denote
\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0}):=\mathcal{P}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}+\Omega e_{3}\times u_{0})
.

There remains two main difficulties that we need to overcome

•

First, we perform perturbation analysis to prove that
\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}+t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})
is small;

•

Second, we need to ensure that the convect term
u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}
is large.

To carry out the analysis for these above two points, a big part of the technical difficulties lies in the construction of our initial data. We construct a new initial data which is somewhat different from Theorem
1.2
. Finally, thanks to commutator estimates and some basic analysis, we obtain the loss of Hölder regularity of solutions to (
1.1
).

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.
The main point is to modify the initial data constructed in Theorem
1.3
. Following the same procedure as that in Theorem
1.3
, we can prove Theorem
1.4
.

2
Preliminaries

We will use the following notations throughout this paper.

•

For
X
a Banach space and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{C}(I;X)
the set of continuous functions on
I
with values in
X
. Sometimes we will denote
L^{p}(0,T;X)
by
L_{T}^{p}X
.

•

The symbol
\mathrm{A}\lesssim(\gtrsim)\mathrm{B}
means that there is a uniform positive “harmless” constant
\mathrm{C}
independent of
\mathrm{A}
and
\mathrm{B}
such that
\mathrm{A}\leq(\geq)\mathrm{C}\mathrm{B}
, and we sometimes use the notation
\mathrm{A}\approx\mathrm{B}
means that
\mathrm{A}\lesssim\mathrm{B}
and
\mathrm{B}\lesssim\mathrm{A}
.

•

Let us recall that for all
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}u
, also denoted by
\widehat{u}
, is defined by

\mathcal{F}u(\xi)=\widehat{u}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{-\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}u(x)\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for any}\;\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

•

The inverse Fourier transform allows us to recover
u
from
\widehat{u}
:

u(x)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{u}(x)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{\mathrm{i}x\cdot\xi}\widehat{u}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\xi.

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}k^{-2}2^{-k(s+1)}\phi^{2}(x_{1})\cos\left(\frac{17}{12}2^{k}x_{1}\right)\right|\geq\frac{\phi^{2}(0)}{2}\sum\limits^{\infty}_{k=3}k^{-2}2^{-k(s+1)}=:c_{0}>0.

Thus we have for some sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n(s+1)}\left\|u_{0}^{(1)}\partial_{1}\partial_{1}a_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\geq c_{0}2^{2n}-C2^{n}.

(4.21)

By easy computations, we have

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n(s+1)}\left\|u_{0}^{(2)}\partial_{1}\partial_{2}a_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq C2^{n}.

(4.22)

Noticing that (
4.20
), from (
4.21
) and (
4.22
), we obtain that for some sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle n^{2}2^{n(s+1)}\left\|u_{0}\cdot\nabla\Delta_{k}u_{0}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\geq c_{0}2^{2n}-C2^{n},

which is nothing but the desired result (
4.19
). We complete the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\approx 1
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}+t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\|_{{B}^{s-2}_{p,r}}\lesssim t^{2}.

Proof.

It holds that

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(B^{s}_{p,r})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1.

From (
3.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|u(t)-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\lesssim t\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim t.

(4.23)

By the Newton-Leibniz formula again, we get

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}+t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\|_{{B}^{s-2}_{p,r}}\leq\int^{t}_{0}\|\partial_{\tau}u+\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{{B}^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\int^{t}_{0}\|\mathcal{P}\left(u\cdot\nabla u-u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}+\Omega e_{3}\times(u-u_{0})\right)\|_{{B}^{s-2}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\int^{t}_{0}\left(\|(u-u_{0})(u+u_{0})\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}+\|u-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\right)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\int^{t}_{0}\|u-u_{0}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,r}}\mathrm{d}\tau\lesssim t^{2}.

We complete the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.
Setting
\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}+t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})

\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}=-t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})+\mathbf{w}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})=u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}-\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})+\Omega\mathcal{P}(e_{3}\times u_{0}).

By the triangle inequality, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{ns}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}=2^{{ns}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})-\mathbf{w}\big)^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{ns}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\left(\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}-2^{{2n}}2^{{n(s-2)}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathbf{w}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}^{(2)}\big)\right\|_{L^{p}}-t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad-t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(\Omega\mathcal{P}(e_{3}\times u_{0}))\big)\right\|_{L^{p}}-C2^{2{n}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\dot{B}^{s-2}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{n}s}\left\|u_{0}\cdot\nabla\Delta_{n}u_{0}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}-t2^{ns}\left\|[\Delta_{n},u_{0}]\cdot\nabla u_{0}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad-Ct2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\right\|_{L^{p}}-Ct\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}-C2^{2n}t^{2}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{ns}\left\|u_{0}\cdot\nabla\Delta_{n}u_{0}^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}-Ct-C2^{2{n}}t^{2},

(4.24)

where we have used the fact:

\displaystyle\big\|2^{{n}s}\|[\Delta_{n},u_{0}]\cdot\nabla u_{0}^{(2)}\|_{L^{p}}\big\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{0}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\lesssim 1,

and

\displaystyle 2^{{ns}}\|\Delta_{n}\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{{ns}}\|\Delta_{n}((-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla(\partial_{i}u^{j}_{0}\partial_{j}u_{0}^{i}))\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\approx 2^{{n(s-1)}}\|\Delta_{n}(\partial_{i}u^{j}_{0}\partial_{j}u_{0}^{i})\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\partial_{i}u^{j}_{0}\partial_{j}u_{0}^{i}\|_{B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim 1.

Then, using Lemma
4.1
, we obtain from (
4.24
) that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\geq ctn^{-2}2^{n}-Ct-C2^{2{n}}t^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle t^{-\alpha}\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\geq ct^{1-\alpha}n^{-2}2^{n}-Ct^{1-\alpha}-C2^{2{n}}t^{2-\alpha},

Thus, picking
t^{1-\alpha}_{n}=n^{3}2^{-n}
with large
n
, we have

\displaystyle t^{-\alpha}_{n}\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}

\displaystyle\geq cn-Cn^{3}2^{-n}-Cn^{6}t_{n}^{\alpha}\geq\tilde{c}n.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

5
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We define the initial data
u_{0}(x)
as

\displaystyle u_{0}(x):=\left(-\partial_{2},\partial_{1},0\right)\sum\limits^{\infty}_{n=3}b_{n}(x),\quad\text{where}

\displaystyle b_{n}(x):=2^{-n(s+1)}\theta(x)\cos\left(\frac{17}{12}2^{n}x_{1}\right).

Following similar procedure as that in Theorem
1.3
, then we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{{ns}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}=2^{{ns}}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(t\mathbf{v}_{0}(u_{0})-\mathbf{w}\big)^{(2)}\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\geq t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0}^{(2)}\big)\right\|_{L^{p}}-t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\mathcal{Q}(u_{0}\cdot\nabla u_{0})\right\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\quad-t2^{{n}s}\left\|\Delta_{n}\big(\Omega\mathcal{P}(e_{3}\times u_{0}))\big)\right\|_{L^{p}}-C2^{2{n}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{\dot{B}^{s-2}_{p,\infty}}

\displaystyle\geq ct2^{n}-Ct-C2^{2n}t^{2}.

Thus, picking
t2^{kn}\approx\varepsilon
with small
\varepsilon
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathbf{S}_{t}(u_{0})-u_{0}\right)^{(2)}\right\|_{B^{s}_{p,\infty}}\geq c\varepsilon-C\varepsilon^{2}\geq\tilde{c}\varepsilon.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

6
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we consider the Cauchy problem for the 3D Euler equations with the Coriolis force in the whole space. We first establish the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of solution to this system in
B^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Then we prove that the Cauchy problem is ill-posed in two different sense:
(1) the solution of this system is not uniformly continuous dependence on the initial data in the same Besov spaces, which extends the recent work of Himonas-Misiołek
[
19
, Comm. Math. Phys., 296, 2010]
to the more general framework of Besov spaces;
(2) the solution of this system cannot be Hölder continuous in time variable in the same Besov spaces. In particular, the solution of the system is discontinuous in the weaker Besov spaces at time zero. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first one addressing the issue on the failure of Hölder continuous in time of solution to the classical Euler equations with(out) the Coriolis force. In this paper, our contribution on the continuous properties for the 3D incompressible rotating Euler equations, unfold in three aspects: by constructing some proper, new and different initial conditions, we showed the non-uniform continuous of the solution map (Theorem
1.2
), proved the failure of Hölder regularity of the solution map (Theorem
1.3
) and established the discontinuous at
t=0
of the solution map (Theorem
1.4
).

Future directions.
In the case when
p\in(1,\infty)
, since the Coriolis force term has no impact on the well-posedness results, we can prove Theorem
1.1
by following the proof of the local well-posedness results for the Euler equations (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.1 ]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.1 ]
). However, in the case when
p=1
or
p=\infty
, due to the the appearance of the dispersive effect of rotation which leads to the fact that Riesz transform does not map continuously
from
L^{\infty}(\text{or}\;L^{1})
to
L^{\infty}(\text{or}\;L^{1})
, we will encounter the main difficulty when establishing the uniform bounds of solution in
L^{\infty}(\text{or}\;L^{1})
-based spaces. We believe that, the dispersive effect of rotation is able to prevent well-posedness for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
). More precisely, we expect that the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is ill-posed in
L^{\infty}(\text{or}\;L^{1})
-based Besov spaces. We will investigate this interesting problem in the future work.

How linear can a non-linear hyperbolic IFS be?
A. Algom, S. Ben Ovadia, F. Rodriguez Hertz, M. Shannon

Abstract
Motivated by a question of M. Hochman, we construct examples of hyperbolic IFSs Φ \Phi on [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] where linear and non-linear behaviour coexist. Namely, for every 2 ≤ r ≤ ∞ 2\leq r\leq\infty we exhibit the existence of a C r C^{r} -smooth IFS such that f ′ ≡ c ⁡ ( Φ ) f^{\prime}\equiv c(\Phi) on the attractor and f ′′ ≡ 0 f^{\prime\prime}\equiv 0 for every f ∈ Φ f\in\Phi , yet Φ \Phi is not C t C^{t} -smooth for any t > r t>r , nor C r C^{r} -conjugate to self-similar. We provide a complete classification of these systems. Furthermore, when r > 1 r>1 , we give a necessary and sufficient Livsic-like matching condition for a self-conformal C r C^{r} -smooth IFS to be conjugated to one of these systems having f ′′ = 0 f^{\prime\prime}=0 on the attractor, for every f ∈ Φ f\in\Phi . We also show that this condition fails to ensure the existence of a C 1 C^{1} -conjugacy in mere C 1 C^{1} -regularity.

Introduction

Let
\Phi=\{f_{1},...,f_{n}\}\subseteq C^{r}([0,1])
be a finite collection of non-singular strict contractions, where
1\leq r\leq\infty
or
r=\omega
. The action on
[0,1]
generated by arbitrary composition of the elements in
\Phi
is called a
self-conformal IFS
(Iterated Function System). It is well known that there exists a unique compact non-empty subset
X=X_{\Phi}\subseteq[0,1]
, called the attractor of
\Phi
, that satisfies

X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(X).

We say that
\Phi
is
C^{r}
-conjugated to another IFS
\widetilde{\Phi}
if there exists a
C^{r}
diffeomorphism
h
on
[0,1]
such that

\widetilde{\Phi}=\{h\circ f_{i}\circ h^{-1}\}_{i=1}^{n}.

Finally,
\Phi
is called
self-similar
if
\Phi
is fully made up of affine maps. See e.g. Bishop-Peres
[
11
]
for a standard introduction, and Bárány-Simon-Solomyak
[
8
, Chapter 14]
for a more recent account.

We are interested in the following question of Hochman

Question 1
.

Let
2\leq r\leq\infty
and let
\Phi\subseteq C^{r}([0,1])
be a self-conformal IFS. Is it possible that
\Phi
is
linear
, in the sense that
f^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
for all
x\in X_{\Phi}
and
f\in\Phi
, yet
\Phi
is not
C^{r}
conjugate to a self-similar IFS?

[

]
about upgrading the smoothness of conjugating maps between hyperbolic Cantor sets (to be defined soon), which is closely related with the work of Sullivan

. This essentially resolved the Fourier decay problem for smooth non-linear IFSs. We will return to this issue and the implication our analysis has on the Fourier decay problem after we state and discuss our main results.

In this paper we give a full answer to Question

2
- part b., we will restrict our analysis to the case where the IFSs have only two generators, which is enough to answer Question

C^{1}
, regularity
C^{s,\alpha}
for
1\leq s<\infty
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, regularity
C^{\infty}
and regularity
C^{\omega}
. The index
s=1
,
s=1+\alpha

s=\infty
or

will be called the regularity of the map. See Section

To describe our results, following e.g. Bedford-Fisher
[
9
]
, let us recall the definition of a hyperbolic IFS. A self-conformal IFS
\Phi=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}\subset C^{s}([0,1])
, where
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
, is called a
hyperbolic IFS
if

0<\inf_{t\in[0,1]}\left(|f_{i}^{\prime}(t)|\right)\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\left(|f_{i}^{\prime}(t)|\right)<1
for both
i=0,1
and

0=f_{0}(0)<f_{0}(1)<f_{1}(0)<f_{1}(1)=1.

Up to an affine change of coordinates, this is equivalent to the so-called convex strong separation condition - see
[
16
, Section 3]
. Note that this property implies that the attractor
X_{\Phi}
is a Cantor set of zero Lebesgue measure. We also introduce a new notion, that will accompany us throughout the paper: A self-conformal IFS
\Phi\subseteq C^{1}([0,1])
is called
pseudo-affine with slope

\lambda
(or just pseudo-affine if we don’t care about the slope) if:

f^{\prime}(x)=\lambda\text{ for all }x\in X_{\Phi}\text{ and all }f\in\Phi.

(1)

Note that if
\Phi\subseteq C^{2}([0,1])
and is pseudo-affine then
\Phi
is linear in the sense of Question
1
. Indeed, this follows from pseudo-affinity and the fact that
X_{\Phi}
is a perfect compact set. It is also interesting to note that the slope
\lambda
can only take values in the set
0<\lambda<1/2
(cf. proof of Claim
16
).

We are now ready to state our first main result which answers Question
1
:

Theorem 2
.

For every parameter
0<\lambda<1/2
and
1\leq s\leq\infty
, there exist hyperbolic pseudo-affine IFSs
\Phi\subseteq C^{s}([0,1])
with slope
\lambda
, satisfying that
\Phi\not\subseteq C^{t}([0,1])
for every
s<t
. In the case
s=\infty
, this means that there exist such IFSs that are
C^{\infty}
but not real analytic. Moreover, in each degree of regularity
1\leq s\leq\infty
, the following is true:

a.

There exist examples of these IFSs that are
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar;

b.

There exist examples of these IFSs that are not
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar.

The examples found in Theorem
2
- part b. answer in full Hochman’s Question
1
. Let’s explain another question coming from the works of Bedford-Fisher and Sullivan, that is related to Theorem
2
- part a. We refer to Section
2
for an account on the concepts used here. The invariant Cantor set of a hyperbolic IFS is called a
hyperbolic Cantor set
. Determining whether two hyperbolic IFSs
\Phi
and
\widetilde{\Phi}
of class
C^{s}
are
C^{s}
-conjugated is equivalent to determine whether the Cantor sets
X_{\Phi}
and
X_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
are
C^{s}
-diffeomorphic, under an orientation preserving diffeomorphism of the interval. This can be completely understood in the case of regularity
s=1+\alpha
with
0<\alpha\leq 1
, due to an invariant called
scaling function
. The fact that these invariant Cantor sets
X
are hyperbolic attractors of systems of class
C^{1,\alpha}
imply that their fine scale structure can be encoded in a
\alpha
-Hölder function
\rho:X^{*}\to\Delta
, called the
scaling function of
X
, with domain in the
dual Cantor set of
X
and range in the simplex
\Delta=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:0\leq t_{i}\leq 1,\ t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=1\}
. It turns that
the scaling functions are a complete invariant of
C^{1,\alpha}
-conjugacy classes of hyperbolic Cantor sets of class
C^{1,\alpha}
.

The problem of
C^{s}
-classification becomes more subtle when we increase the degree of regularity to
s\geq 2
. Since the Cantor sets
X_{\Phi}
and
X_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
are in particular of class
C^{1+\alpha}
then they have well-defined scaling functions
\rho_{\Phi}
and
\rho_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
, and hence they are
C^{1+\alpha}
conjugated if and only if
\rho_{\Phi}=\rho_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
. In
[
9
]
-
[
25
]
it is proved the following result, known as
rigidity
:
Two hyperbolic Cantor sets with regularity
s>1
are
C^{1}
-diffeomorphic if and only if they are
C^{s}
-diffeomorphic.
It follows that, in regularity
s\geq 2
, the association of its scaling function to any
C^{s}
-hyperbolic Cantor set, is an injective association.

It is less understood, however, which are the
\alpha
-Hölder functions
\rho:X^{*}\to\Delta
that can occur as the scaling function of a hyperbolic Cantor set
X
of class
C^{s}
, for a given
s\geq 2
. More precisely, following
[
9
]
and
[
25
]
, the question that arises is the following:

Question 3
.

Let
\rho:X^{*}\to\Delta
be the scaling function of a hyperbolic Cantor set
X
of class
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
, which is an
\alpha
-Hölder function. Given
s\geq 2
, which properties of
\rho
ensures that
X
has regularity at least
s
? For example, if
\rho
is constant, does it ensures that
X
is real analytic ?

A first result in this direction can be found in
[
22
]
, where it is shown that a bound on the regularity of the scaling function imposes a bound on the actual regularity of
X
. On the other hand, the examples in Theorem A - part a. show that a high degree of regularity of the scaling function (constant in this case, since these IFSs are conjugated to self-similar) does not imply any kind of regularity of the Cantor set, other than
s=1+\alpha
. More precisely, given
0<\lambda<1/2
, denote by
X_{\lambda}
the self-similar Cantor set of slope
=\lambda
, which has constant scaling function
\rho_{\lambda}=(\lambda,1-2\lambda,\lambda)
. For every
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
, let
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{s}
be the set of
C^{s}
-equivalence classes of hyperbolic Cantor sets of class
C^{s}
, having constant scaling function
=\rho_{\lambda}
. Then, the examples in Theorem A - part a. show that:

Corollary 4
.

It is verified that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\omega}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\infty}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{t}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{s}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{1}
, for all
1<s<t<\infty
.

Theorem
2
, and our analysis in general, can also be viewed as a refinement of a result of Bamón, Moreira, Plaza, and Vera
[
7
]
. In that paper, the authors find explicit conditions for the characterization and classification of a particular class of
C^{s}
-regular hyperbolic Cantor sets, called
central Cantor sets
. This is done in terms of the asymptotics of various products of the sizes of gaps removed in the construction of
X_{\Phi}
as a Cantor set (see Section
2.2
for more details). In particular, examples as those in our Theorem
2
- part a. have been already produced in
[
7
]
; They also produced such examples where the difference set can have any chosen volume measure; And, they classified their
C^{s}
-conjugacy classes.

Our major new contribution compared with
[
7
]
is that we provide a mechanism that ensures
\Phi
is linear yet not conjugate to self-similar. Our construction is carried out in terms of a pair of real functions on
\{0,1\}^{*}
, called
dynamical proportions
. These functions codify the
proportions
between different
gaps
in the complement of the Cantor set. The crux of the matter is that, when a Cantor set has zero Lebesgue measure, then each pair of admissible dynamical proportions uniquely determine the Cantor set as a subset of the interval, and hence all the properties of the Cantor set are encoded in this pair of functions. For instance, pseudo-affinity can be characterized in these terms, and is equivalent to the condition that the dynamical proportions are asymptotically constant with respect to the length of the word. Moreover, the deviation from the limit constant
\lambda
encodes precise information about the regularity of the IFS.

We will prove Theorem
2
by showing first a complete classification of hyperbolic pseudo-affine IFSs of class
C^{s}
up to
C^{s}
-conjugation, for every regularity index
1\leq s\leq\infty
. This is the content of Theorem
20
in Section
3
, and it is the heart of the construction in this paper. To avoid technicalities, we skip its statement here and refer the reader directly to that section. It is interesting to note that for determining
C^{s}
-conjugation in regularity
1\leq s\leq\infty
, it is enough to check
C^{1}
-conjugation. This is because one can invoke once again the rigidity results of Bedford-Fisher and Sullivan, and obtain that a conjugation of class
C^{1}
automatically upgrades to a conjugation of class
C^{s}
. Observe that the problem of
C^{s}
-classification is trivial when
s=\omega
(due to analyticity).

In a second approach, we study the problem of whether a general hyperbolic IFS is smoothly conjugated to a pseudo-affine IFS. Consider a hyperbolic IFS
\widetilde{\Phi}=\{g_{0},g_{1}\}\subseteq C^{s}([0,1])
where
s\geq 1
. A necessary condition for
\widetilde{\Phi}
to be
C^{s}
-conjugated to a pseudo-affine IFS of slope
\lambda
is the following:

For every
w\in\{0,1\}^{*}
let
G_{\omega}=g_{\omega_{1}}\circ\circ\circ g_{\omega_{n}}
be the corresponding cylinder map, and let
y_{w}\in X_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
denote its unique fixed point; That is,
G_{w}(y_{w})=y_{w}
. Then:

\text{ There exists }0<\lambda<1/2\text{ such that for all }w\in\{0,1\}^{*}\text{ we have }G_{w}^{\prime}(y_{\omega})=\lambda^{|w|}.

(2)

We call (
2
) the
Livsic Condition
, after the well known work of Livsic
[
21
]
in dynamical systems. Roughly speaking, the Theorem of Livsic allows one to solve global cohomological equations from periodic data, for Hölder regular observables. This was brought into the context of smooth conjugation of Anosov systems by de la Llave - Moriyón
[
14
]
. However, Bousch-Jenkinson
[
12
]
have shown that this fails without Hölder regularity of the potential.

The following Theorem provides IFS-like analogues of both the Livsic Theorem, and of its failure in lower regularity.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}=\{g_{0},g_{1}\}\subseteq C^{s}([0,1])
be a hyperbolic IFS where
s\geq 1
, that satisfies the Livsic condition (
2
). Then:

1.

For
s=1
there exists such
\widetilde{\Phi}
that is not
C^{1}
-conjugate to a pseudo-affine IFS.

2.

If
s>1
then
\widetilde{\Phi}
is
C^{s}
-conjugate to a pseudo-affine IFS of class
C^{s}
with slope
\lambda
.

We note that while Theorem
2
does answer Question
1
, there are some interesting problems that remain. For instance, we did not treat the case when the IFS has more than two generators, that has additional technicalities to deal with. Also, it would be interesting to understand more in detail how pseudo-affinity is manifested in terms of the scaling function. (Observe that the examples in Theorem
2
- part b. are not
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar and thus their scaling functions are non-constant.)

We end this introduction with a few remarks about the implications Theorem
2
has on the Fourier decay problem for fractal measures. Recall that a probability measure
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}
is called a Rajchman measure if its Fourier transform satisfies
\lim_{|q|\rightarrow\infty}\widehat{\nu}(q)=0
. Combining recent breakthroughs on the Fourier decay problem
[
13
,
26
,
20
,
4
,
3
,
1
,
5
]
we know that if a
C^{\omega}(\mathbb{R})
self-conformal IFS admits a non-Rajchman self-conformal measure then: The IFS must be self-similar; It must have contraction ratios that are all powers of some
r\in(0,1)
such that
r^{-1}
is a Pisot number; And, up to affine conjugation, all translates are in
\mathbb{Q}(r)
. Now, it is natural to ask about analogues of this result in
C^{r}(\mathbb{R})
-regularity,
1\leq r\leq\infty
- see e.g.
[
24
, Problem 3.14]
. The contribution of Theorem
2
to this line of research is that is shows the non-triviality of the problem: There are self-conformal measures that are neither self-similar, smoothly conjugate to self-similar, or non-linear. In particular, none of the methods in the cited papers can currently deal with the Rajchman problem for such measures.

Preliminaries

2.1
Regularity

Given
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we say that a map
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is
\alpha
-Hölder
if there exists a constant
A>0
such that

|g(u)-g(t)|\leq A\cdot|u-t|^{\alpha},\,\forall\ 0\leq t,u\leq 1.

We call
\alpha
and
A
the
Hölder exponent
and
Hölder constant
, respectively. For
\alpha=1
this condition is called
Lipschitz
. Given an integer
r\geq 1
, we say that a map
g:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is of
class
C^{r,\alpha}
if its derivatives up to order
r
exist and are continuous, and the
r
-th derivative
\partial^{r}g
is an
\alpha
-Hölder function. We call the parameter
s=r+\alpha\in[1,+\infty)\cup\{\infty\}
the
regularity
of
g
(where
s=\infty
stands for
smooth
). We will simply say that
g
is of class
C^{s}
. However, observe that a map of class
C^{r,1}
has regularity
s=r+1
although it does not mean it is of class
C^{r+1,0}
.

If
X\subset[0,1]
is a non-empty closed subset,
a map
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{s}
in the sense of Milnor
if for every
x\in X
there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
x
and a map
G:U\to\mathbb{R}
of class
C^{s}
such that
G(z)=g(z)
,
\forall\ z\in U\cap X
. By compactness, it is possible to see that there always exists a
C^{s}
-extension
G:U\to\mathbb{R}
of
g:X\to\mathbb{R}
on an open neighborhood
U
including
X
, and that
U
can be taken to be
U=[0,1]
.

2.2
Binary Cantor Sets and Dynamical Proportions

Consider the product space
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
endowed with the product topology, and with the lexicographic order induced by declaring
0<1
. A
binary Cantor set

X\subseteq[0,1]
is the image of an
order preserving
(with respect to the natural order on
[0,1]
) continuous embedding
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]
, such that

(0)_{n\geq 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\mapsto 0\in[0,1],\text{ and }(1)_{n\geq 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\mapsto 1\in[0,1].

Given
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
we denote by
x_{a}
its image in
[0,1]
. We emphasize that, in general,
X
need not be a self-conformal set.

Let
\mathcal{W}:=\{0,1\}^{*}
be the words in the alphabet
\{0,1\}
, and denote the
empty word
by
e
. For every
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}\in\mathcal{W}
we denote by
X_{w}
the
cylinder
determined by
w
, which is defined by

X_{w}:=\{x_{a}\in X:\,a_{i}=w_{i}\,\forall i=1,\dots,n\}.

Define
K_{w}:=\textsl{Conv}(X_{w})
, the
convex hull
of the cylinder
X_{w}
. Then
K_{w}\subset[0,1]
is a closed interval. We can thus write

X=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\left(\bigcup_{|w|=n}K_{w}\right).

The connected components of
[0,1]\setminus X
are open intervals called
gaps
. We label the gaps via

\{I_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
, which is given by setting

I_{w}=K_{w}\setminus\left(K_{w0}\cup K_{w1}\right),\text{ for every }w\in\mathcal{W}.

We will define a total order
\prec
on the set
\mathcal{W}\cup\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
by declaring that
w0(1)^{\infty}\prec w\prec w1(0)^{\infty}
, for every finite word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
. If
X
is any binary Cantor set in
[0,1]
with cylinders and gaps labeled as above, then for every
u,w\in\mathcal{W}
and
a,b\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle u\prec w\ \text{if and only if the gap}\ I_{u}\ \text{is on the left of the gap}\ I_{w},

\displaystyle a\prec b\ \text{if and only if}\ x_{a}<x_{b},

\displaystyle w\prec a\ \text{if and only if the gap}\ I_{w}\ \text{is on the left of the point}\ x_{a},

\displaystyle a\prec w\ \text{if and only if the point}\ x_{a}\ \text{is on the left of the gap}\ I_{w}.

We are interested in the following quantities associated to a binary Cantor set, that encode information about its geometric structure.

Definition 6
.

Given a binary Cantor set
X\subset[0,1]
its associated pair of
dynamical proportions
are the functions
\lambda_{i}:\mathcal{W}\to(0,+\infty)
,
i=0,1
defined by

\lambda_{i}(w)\coloneqq\frac{|I_{iw}|}{|I_{w}|},\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W},\ \text{where}\ |I_{w}|\ \text{denotes the length of the gap}\ I_{w}\subset[0,1].

We remark that, while different, this definition is motivated by Sullivan’s scaling function
[
25
,
9
]
. The pair of dynamical proportions associated to a binary Cantor set will be our main tool to construct IFSs and determine their regularity in Section
3
. We proceed to prove Lemma
8
below, that will be used in the next section.

Definition 7
.

Given a pair of functions
\lambda_{i}:\mathcal{W}\to(0,+\infty)
,
i=0,1
, its associated
cocycle
is the function
\Psi:\mathcal{W}\to(0,+\infty)
given by the expression

\Psi(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\lambda_{w_{i}}(w_{i+1}\cdots w_{n})\right)\cdot\lambda_{w_{n}}(e),\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}.

(3)

It is direct to check that, in the case where
\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\}
are the dynamical proportions of a binary Cantor set, then

|I_{w}|=\Psi(w)\cdot|I|

\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
, where
|I|
is the length of the gap labeled with the empty word
e
. Observe that if
X
has
zero Lebesgue measure
then the sum of the length over all the gaps equals one and hence

\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)=1/|I|>1.

(4)

Lemma 8
.

Let
\lambda_{i}:\mathcal{W}\to(0,+\infty)
,
i=0,1
be any pair of functions and let
\Psi:\mathcal{W}\to(0,+\infty)
be defined by the formula in equation (
3
) above. If

1<\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)<+\infty

then there is a unique zero Lebesgue measure binary Cantor set
X
such that
\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\}
are its pair of dynamical proportions. Furthermore, the length of the gaps in
X
are given by the formula

|I_{w}|=\Psi(w)\cdot\left(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(u)\right)^{-1},\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}.

Proof.

Given the functions
\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1}\}
and its associated the cocycle
\Psi
define

L=\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)\right)^{-1},

which by hypothesis is a positive quantity smaller than
1
. Using the total order
\prec
in the set
\mathcal{W}\cup\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
that we explained above, define
\Theta:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]
by the formula

\Theta(a)=\sum_{w\prec a}\Psi(w)\cdot L.

Let’s check that
\Theta:(0)_{n\geq 1}\mapsto 0
and
\Theta:(1)_{n\geq 1}\mapsto 1
, that it is order preserving, injective and continuous. Since
(0)_{n\geq 1}
is the minimum and
(1)_{n\geq 1}
is the maximum for the order defined above, we have that

\displaystyle\Theta\left((0)_{n\geq 1}\right)=\sum_{w\prec(0)_{n\geq 1}}\Psi(w)\cdot L=\left(\sum_{\emptyset}\Psi(w)\right)\cdot L=0,

\displaystyle\Theta\left((1)_{n\geq 1}\right)=\sum_{w\prec(1)_{n\geq 1}}\Psi(w)\cdot L=\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)\right)\cdot L=1.

If
a
and
b
are sequences satisfying
a<b
for the lexicographic order, then
\{w:a\prec w\prec b\}\neq\emptyset
and thus

\Theta(a)=\sum_{w\prec a}\Psi(w)\cdot L<\sum_{w\prec a}\Psi(w)\cdot L+\sum_{a\prec w\prec b}\Psi(w)\cdot L=\sum_{w\prec b}\Psi(w)\cdot L=\Theta(b),

from where we can see that
\Theta
is injective and preserves the respective orders in the domain and range. To see that
\Theta
is continuous,
observe that if
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
b=(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}
satisfy
a_{n}=b_{n}
for
n=1,\dots,N
for some
N>0
, then all the words satisfying
a\prec w\prec b
have length
|w|>N
. Thus

\left|\Theta(b)-\Theta(a)\right|=\left|\sum_{a\prec w\prec b}\Psi(w)\cdot L\right|\leq\sum_{a\prec w\prec b}|\Psi(w)|\cdot L\leq\sum_{|w|>N}\Psi(w)\cdot L.

Since
\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)<+\infty
then the right hand side in the expression above converges to zero when
N\to+\infty
, from where we see that
\Theta
is continuous.

From the previous paragraph we obtain that
\Theta:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]
is a continuous embedding and its image
X=\Theta(\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}})
is a binary Cantor set in the unit interval. For every finite word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
define the sequences
a_{w}=w01^{\infty}
and
b_{w}=w10^{\infty}
. The sequences
a_{w}
and
b_{w}
are consecutive elements for the lexicographic order in
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and thus they are the boundary points of the gap
I_{w}
. The word
w
is the only element of
\mathcal{W}
satisfying
a_{w}\prec w\prec b_{w}
for the total order in
\mathcal{W}\cup\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. It follows that

|I_{w}|=|\Theta(b_{w})-\Theta(a_{w})|=\sum_{a_{w}\prec u\prec b_{w}}\Psi(u)\cdot L=\Psi(w)\cdot L

and hence, using expression (
3
), we see that the proportions of the Cantor set are given by

\lambda_{i}(w)=\frac{|I_{iw}|}{|I_{w}|}=\frac{\Psi(iw)}{\Psi(w)}=\lambda_{i}(w),\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}.

Finally, to check that
X
has zero Lebesgue measure, observe that

\textsl{Leb}(X)=1-\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}|I_{w}|=1-\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}\Psi(w)\right)\cdot L=0.

∎

2.3
Hyperbolic structures on Cantor sets

Consider
X=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
endowed with the lexicographic order. A
C^{s}
-atlas on
X
,
s\geq 0
, is a family
\mathcal{D}=\{\varphi_{\alpha}:X_{w_{\alpha}}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{\alpha}
of continuous, order preserving, embeddings of the cylinders of
X
into
\mathbb{R}
, where
(i)
-
\{X_{w_{\alpha}}\}_{\alpha}
forms an open cover of
X
and
(ii)
- the change of coordinates are order preserving
C^{s}
-diffeomorphisms. A
C^{s}
-differentiable structure
on
X
is a maximal
C^{s}
-atlas, and two structures
\mathcal{D}
,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
are
C^{k}
-equivalent
,
k\geq 0
, if there exists a
C^{k}
-diffeomorphism
(X,\mathcal{D})\to(X,\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
. We denote by
[\mathcal{D}]
the set of all structures
C^{s}
-equivalent to
\mathcal{D}
.

A
C^{s}
-differentiable structure
\mathcal{D}
on
X
,
s\geq 1
, is called
hyperbolic
if the shift map
\sigma:(X,\mathcal{D})\to(X,\mathcal{D})
is a
C^{s}
-expanding map
, i.e. it has derivative greater than one at every point. In
[
25
]
and
[
9
]
there is a classification of the equivalence classes of hyperbolic structures in
r=1+\alpha
regularity. The classifying invariant is a Hölder function
\rho:Y\to\Delta
called
scaling function
, defined over an abstract Cantor set
Y
called the
dual Cantor set
of
X
and with image in the unit simplex
\Delta=\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:0\leq t_{i}\leq 1,\ t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}=1\}
.

The construction of the scaling function is as follows: Let
\mathcal{D}
be a hyperbolic
C^{1+\alpha}
-structure on
X
and take a globally defined chart
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}
embedding the Cantor set in the real line with extreme points
t=0
and
t=1
. Since
\varphi(X)\subset\mathbb{R}
is a binary Cantor set with a labeling on its cylinders, this embedding is completely determined by knowing the ratios
r(w)=(r_{0}(w),r_{\mathrm{gap}}(w),r_{1}(w))\in\Delta
for every finite word
w
, where:

\displaystyle r_{i}(w)=\frac{\diam(\varphi(X_{wi}))}{\diam(\varphi(X_{w}))}\text{ for }i=0,1\ \text{ and }\ r_{\mathrm{gap}}(w)=1-\frac{\diam(\varphi(X_{w0}))+\diam(\varphi(X_{w0}))}{\diam(\varphi(X_{w}))}.

The key point is that, if the shift map is
C^{1+\alpha}
-expanding, then it is possible to use the bounded variation of the derivative to prove that for every sequence
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
with
a_{n}\in\{0,1\}
there exists the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}r(a_{n}\cdots a_{1})\in\Delta
. Observe the detail that the sequence of words indexing this limit is constructed by the procedure of adding the letters to the left. (The limit could not exists if we add letters to the right.)

The set
\mathcal{W}
of finite ordered strings in the alphabet
\{0,1\}
can be thought of in two different ways:
(i)
- As being constructed from the empty word by adding letters to the right,
(ii)
- As being constructed from the empty word by adding letters to the left. Each construction allows to order the words in
\mathcal{W}
in a one-sided tree, right-sided in case
(i)
and left-sided in case
(ii)
, and each tree has a boundary Cantor set:
\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\{0,1\}
in case
(i)
and
\prod_{-\infty}^{i=-1}\{0,1\}
in case
(ii)
. Given now the Cantor set
X
and the labeling
\{X_{w}:w\in\mathcal{W}\}
, observe that the boundary of the right-sided tree (case
(i)
) can be identified with
X
itself. The boundary of the left-sided tree (case
(ii)
) will be denoted by
Y
and will be called the dual Cantor set of
X
. It is the set over which the limits above are naturally defined.

Theorem 9

(Classification of hyperbolic
C^{1+\alpha}
-structures,
[
25
]
-
[
9
]
.)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a
C^{1+\alpha}
-structure on
X
with
bounded geometry
. Then
\sigma:(X,\mathcal{D})\to(X,\mathcal{D})
is a
C^{1+\alpha}
-expanding map if and only if there exist the limit
\rho(y)=\lim_{n\to-\infty}r(y_{n}\cdots y_{-1})
for all
y\in Y
, it is independent of the chart
\varphi
, and the limit function
\rho:Y\to\Delta
is
\alpha
-Hölder. Moreover, the map that assigns to each
\mathcal{D}
its scaling function
\rho:Y\to\Delta
induces a one to one correspondence

\mathit{Diff}^{\ 1+\alpha}(X,\sigma)\coloneqq\left\{[\mathcal{D}]:\ \sigma:(X,\mathcal{D})\to(X,\mathcal{D})\ \text{is}\ C^{1+\alpha}-\text{expanding}\ \right\}\xrightarrow{(1:1)}C^{\alpha}(Y,\Delta).

Moving to the case of regularity
s\geq 2
, a description of the
C^{s}
-equivalence classes of
C^{s}
-structures making the shift
C^{s}
-expanding becomes more subtle. In
[
25
]
,
[
9
]
they prove the following fundamental result:

Theorem 10

(Rigidity,
[
25
]
-
[
9
]
)
.

Any hyperbolic
C^{s}
-structure
\mathcal{D}
satisfies
\diff^{\ 1}(X,\mathcal{D})=\diff^{\ s}(X,\mathcal{D})
,
\forall s\geq 1+\alpha
.

The rigidity theorem implies that, for every
s\geq 1+\alpha
:
(i)
- if a
C^{1+\alpha}
-atlas
\mathcal{D}
has a refinement of class
C^{s}
for which the shift is
C^{s}
-expanding then
\mathcal{D}
is already of class
C^{s}
;
(ii)
- the correspondence
\mathit{Diff}^{\ s}(X,\sigma)\to C^{\alpha}(X,\Delta)
sending
[\mathcal{D}]\mapsto\rho
is injective.

2.4
Some properties of self-conformal IFSs

Recall the definitions of self-conformal and hyperbolic IFSs from Section
1
, and let
\Phi=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}\subseteq C^{1}([0,1])
be a hyperbolic IFS. For every
a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
define

x_{a}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f_{a_{1}}\circ\circ\circ f_{a_{n}}(0).

It is well known that
x_{a}\in X=X_{\Phi}
and that this is map is actually a bijection. In fact, this map shows that

is a binary Cantor set in the sense of Section

. Furthermore, it is easy to see that, by strong separation,
\text{Leb}(X)=0
.

Let us discuss some more standard properties of hyperbolic Cantor sets; as before, all proofs can be worked out from e.g.
[
11
, Chapter 2]
. For
w\in\mathcal{W}
let
F_{w}
be the cylinder map

F_{w}=f_{w_{1}}\circ\circ\circ f_{w_{n}}.

Note that if
y=F_{w}(x_{a})
then

y=x_{w_{1}\cdots w_{n}a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\cdots}.

Also,

has a unique fixed point given by

x_{w^{\infty}}=x_{(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})\cdots}.

Next, we have the usual labeling
\{I_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}}
of the components of

defined by setting
I_{w}\coloneqq K_{w}\setminus(K_{w0}\cup K_{w1})
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
. Then we always have

F_{w}(I_{u})=I_{wu},\,\forall\ w,u\in\mathcal{W}.

Observe that the dynamical proportions of the invariant Cantor set must satisfy
0<\lambda_{i}(w)<1
,
\forall

w\in\mathcal{W}
and
i=0,1
. We also note that

|I_{w}|<\mu^{|w|},\,\forall w\in\mathcal{W},

where

is any constant such that
f_{i}^{\prime}(t)<\mu<1
,
\forall\ 0<t<1
,

. Hence,

.

Finally, we note that if

is another hyperbolic IFS then there exists a homeomorphism
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
h(K_{\Phi})=K_{\widetilde{\Phi}}
and

,

.

3
pseudo-Affine hyperbolic IFS.

Recall from (
1
) that a self-conformal IFS
\Phi
of class

, where
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
, is

if there exists

such that

for all

and

3.1
Asymptotically constant dynamical proportions

We begin by characterizing pseudo-affinity in terms of dynamical proportions.

Definition 11
.

We say that a binary Cantor set
X
with gap-labelling
\{I_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}}
has
asymptotically constant dynamical proportions
if there exists
0<\lambda<1/2
such that

\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda+\theta_{i}(w),\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}\ \text{for both}\ i=0,1,

and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta_{i}(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})=0,\quad\forall\ (w_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}.

Note that attractors of self-similar IFSs with a uniform contraction ratio
\lambda
have
constant
dynamical proportions

\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
.

Proposition 12
.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{1},f_{2}\}
be a
C^{1}
hyperbolic IFS. Then the following properties are equivalent:

1.

\Phi
is pseudo-affine;

2.

X=X_{\Phi}
has asymptotically constant dynamical proportions.

Proof.

Since the contractions

,
i=0,1
are of class
C^{1}
the proportions are given by

\lambda_{i}(w)=\frac{|I_{iw}|}{|I_{w}|}=\frac{1}{|I_{w}|}\int_{I_{w}}f_{i}^{\prime}(t)dt.

•

Suppose

for every
x\in X
,
i=0,1
. Write
f_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\lambda+\eta_{i}(t)
,
\forall\ 0\leq t\leq 1,i=0,1
. Then

\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda+\theta_{i}(w),\text{ where }\theta_{i}(w)=\frac{1}{|I_{w}|}\int_{I_{w}}\eta_{i}(t)dt.

Let’s see that
\theta_{i}(w)\to 0

uniformly
in
|w|\to\infty
. Fix some
\varepsilon>0
. Since
f_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=0,1
are both continuous then there exists

such that

whenever

. Since

when

(cf. Section

|\theta_{i}(w)|<\varepsilon

•

Reciprocally, assume that

(5)

∎

Proposition 13
.

Let
\Phi
be a pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFS of class

For every

as the integral over the gap

\lambda&\text{if}&t\in X.\\
\end{array}\right.

satisfies that:

1.

Each
f_{i}^{\prime}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is well-defined and continuous;

2.

The maps
f_{i}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
given by:

f_{0}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}f_{0}^{\prime}(s)ds
; and,

f_{1}(t)=\tau+\int_{0}^{t}f_{1}^{\prime}(s)ds
, where
\tau=1-\int_{0}^{1}f_{1}^{\prime}(s)ds
;

form a
C^{1}
hyperbolic IFS
\Phi
such that
X=X_{\Phi}
.

Proof of Claim
17
.

The functions
f_{i}^{\prime}
are well-defined because they are set to be constant
=\lambda
along the boundary of the domain of definition, which is the Cantor set
X
. If we write

f_{i}^{\prime}(t)=\lambda+\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|w|\leq n}\varphi_{w}^{i}(t)

then for
n<m
we have that:

\left\|\sum_{|w|=n}^{m}\varphi_{w}^{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n\leq|w|\leq m}\left\{\|\varphi_{w}^{i}\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq\|\rho\|_{\infty}\cdot\sup_{n\leq|w|\leq m}\left\{|\theta_{i}(w)|\right\},

which converges to zero with
n\to\infty
due to the uniform convergence
|\theta_{i}(w)|\to 0
. Hence the functions
f_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=0,1
are continuous, being the limit of a Cauchy sequence of continuous functions.

For the
f_{i}:[0,1]\to[0,1]
,
i=0,1
defined above it is clear that both are
C^{1}
contractions satisfying
f_{0}(0)=0
and
f_{1}(1)=1
. To check that the Cantor set
X
is invariant under the IFS
\Phi=\left\{f_{0},f_{1}\right\}
, consider a point
x_{a}\in X
with coding
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and consider the points
x_{ia}\in X,\ i=0,1
where
ia=(ia_{1}a_{2}a_{3}\cdots)
. Given a finite word
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
and a sequence
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
we denote
w<a
if the gap
I_{w}
is located to the left of the point
x_{a}
, or
a<w
otherwise. Since the Cantor set is constructed by iteratively removing the gap
I_{w}
from a closed interval
K_{w}
,
w\in\mathcal{W}
, then
iw<ia
if and only if
w<a
, for both
i=0,1
. Using that the Cantor set
X
has zero Lebesgue measure we get that the points
x_{a},x_{0a},x_{1a}
are determined by the expressions

\displaystyle x_{a}

\displaystyle=|x_{a}|=\sum_{w<a}|I_{w}|=1-\sum_{a<w}|I_{w}|,

\displaystyle x_{0a}

\displaystyle=|x_{0a}|=\sum_{w<0a}|I_{w}|=\sum_{w<a}|I_{0w}|,

\displaystyle x_{1a}

\displaystyle=|x_{1a}|=1-\sum_{1a<w}|I_{w}|=1-\sum_{a<w}|I_{1w}|.

On the other hand, for each
i=0,1
let
y_{i}=f_{i}(x_{a})
. Then

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{x_{a}}f_{0}^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{[0,{x_{a}}]}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}(f_{0}^{\prime})|_{I_{w}}\right)dt=\sum_{w<a}\int_{I_{w}}f_{0}^{\prime}(t)dt,

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=\tau+\int_{0}^{x_{a}}f_{1}^{\prime}(t)dt=1-\int_{[x_{a},1]}\left(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{W}}(f_{1}^{\prime})|_{I_{w}}\right)dt=1-\sum_{a<w}\int_{I_{w}}f_{1}^{\prime}(t)dt;

Since
\int_{I_{w}}\varphi_{w}^{i}(t)dt=\theta_{i}(w)|I_{w}|
then by construction we have that

\int_{I_{w}}f_{i}^{\prime}(t)dt=\int_{I_{w}}(\lambda+\varphi_{w}^{i}(t))dt=(\lambda+\theta_{i}(w))|I_{w}|=\lambda_{i}(w)|I_{w}|.

It follows that

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{w<a}\lambda_{0}(w)|I_{w}|=\sum_{w<a}|I_{0w}|=x_{0a},

\displaystyle y_{1}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{a<w}\lambda_{1}(w)|I_{w}|=1-\sum_{a<w}|I_{1w}|=x_{1a}.

By the last two expressions we see that
y_{i}\in X
for both
i=0,1
. Therefore
\Phi
preserves
X
, so
X_{\Phi}\subseteq X
; that
X=X_{\Phi}
follows by iterating this argument to show that every point coded by the IFS belongs to
X
(recall that
X
is closed by definition). The proof is complete.
∎

Remark
18
.

In this Remark we study the regularity of the IFS
\Phi
obtained above. Write
f_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda+\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|w|\leq n}\varphi_{w}^{i}
where
i=0,1
.

1.

Suppose there exist
C>0
and
1\leq r<\infty
,
0<\alpha\leq 1
such that
|\theta_{i}(w)|<C\cdot|I_{w}|^{r-1+\alpha}
,
\forall w\in\mathcal{W}
. We first show that for every
k=0,\dots,r-1
the
k
-th derivative of
\sum_{|w|=1}^{n}\varphi_{w}^{i}
is a Cauchy sequence. By construction we have that

\left\|\partial^{k}\varphi_{w}^{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\frac{d^{k}}{dt^{k}}\left[\theta_{i}(w)\rho\left(\frac{t-a_{w}}{b_{w}-a_{w}}\right)\right]\right\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|\theta_{i}(w)|}{|I_{w}|^{k}}\cdot\left\|\partial^{k}\rho\right\|_{\infty}\leq C\cdot|I_{w}|^{r-1+\alpha-k},\ \forall\ k=0,\dots,r-1;

(7)

then for
n<m
we have

\left\|\sum_{|w|=n}^{m}\partial^{k}\varphi_{w}^{i}\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n\leq|w|\leq m}\left\{\|\partial^{k}\varphi_{w}^{i}\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq C\cdot\sup_{n\leq|w|\leq m}\left\{|I_{w}|^{r-1+\alpha-k}\right\},

which converges to zero when
n\to\infty
for
k=0,\dots,r-1
. This implies that
f_{i}
is of class
C^{r}
and

\partial^{k+1}f_{i}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|w|\leq n}\partial^{k}\varphi_{w}^{i},\ \forall\ k=0,\dots,r-1.

We now show that for each
i=0,1
the derivatives
\partial^{r}f_{i}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
are
\alpha
-Hölder functions. First, observe that for each finite word
w
the function
\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}
is
\alpha
-Hölder on the closure of the gap
I_{w}=(a_{w},b_{w})
. To check this observe that
\partial^{r-1}\rho
is an
\alpha
-Hölder function for some constant
A
, and so for two points
a_{w}\leq t,u\leq b_{w}
we have that

|\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(u)-\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(t)|\leq C\cdot|I_{w}|^{\alpha}\cdot\left|\partial^{r-1}\rho\left(\frac{u-a_{w}}{b_{w}-a_{w}}\right)-\partial^{r-1}\rho\left(\frac{t-a_{w}}{b_{w}-a_{w}}\right)\right|<(CA)\cdot|u-t|^{\alpha}.

Next, observe that for every
n\geq 1
the function
\psi_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by
\psi_{n}=\sum_{|w|\leq n}\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}
is
\alpha
-Hölder. To check this, take two points
0\leq t<u\leq 1
on the support of
\psi_{n}
and assume that
t
and
u
belong to different gaps
I_{v}=(a_{v},b_{v})
and
I_{w}=(a_{w},b_{w})
,
|v|,|w|\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(u)-\psi_{n}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(u)-\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(a_{w})|+|\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(a_{w})-\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(b_{v})|

\displaystyle+|\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(b_{v})-\partial^{r-1}\varphi_{w}^{i}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq(CA)\cdot\left(|u-a_{w}|^{\alpha}+|a_{w}-b_{v}|^{\alpha}+|b_{v}-t|^{\alpha}\right)\leq(K_{\alpha}CA)\cdot|t-u|^{\alpha},

where
K_{\alpha}
is taken to satisfy
a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}+c^{\alpha}\leq K_{\alpha}\cdot(a+b+c)^{\alpha}
, for every
0,\leq a,b,c\leq 1
. In the same way, it is possible to check that
|\psi_{n}(u)-\psi_{n}(t)|\leq(K_{\alpha}CA)|t-u|^{\alpha}
for every
t,u\in[0,1]
. Finally, since the sequence of functions
\psi_{n}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
converges uniformly to
\partial^{r}f_{i}
and each
\psi_{n}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with constant
K_{\alpha}CA>0
(independent of
n\geq 1
) we conclude that
\partial^{r}f_{i}
is
\alpha
-Hölder with the same constant.

2.

If the condition
|\theta_{i}(w)|<C(r)\cdot|I_{w}|^{r}
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
with
C(r)>0
is satisfied for every
r\geq 1
, then by the previous item we have that the
r
-th derivative of
f_{i}
,
i=0,1
exists and is continuous for every
r\geq 1
. Thus, the contractions
f_{i}
are
C^{\infty}
.

Taking tally of the previous claims and remarks, Proposition
15
is proved.
∎

3.3
C^{s}
-conjugacy classes of pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFSs

Let
0<\lambda<1/2
and fix
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
. In this section we describe the
C^{s}
-conjugacy classes in the family of pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFSs of slope
\lambda
.

For
1\leq s<\infty
denote by
\mathcal{E}_{s}
the set of pairs
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
, where
\theta_{i}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{R}
are functions satisfying:

(i)

0<\lambda+\theta_{i}(w)<1
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
and
i=0,1
,

(ii)

|\theta_{i}(w)|\to 0
uniformly in
|w|\to\infty
,

(iii)

\exists\ C>0
such that
|\theta_{i}(w)|\leq C\cdot\left(\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)\right)^{s-1}
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
and
i=0,1
,

and
\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}:\mathcal{W}\to(0,1)
is as in Lemma
8
defined by the expression

\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w_{1}\cdots w_{n})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}(\lambda+\theta_{w_{i}}(w_{i+1}\cdots w_{n}))\right)\cdot(\lambda+\theta_{w_{n}}(e)),\ \forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}.

For
s=\infty
denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\infty}
the set of pairs satisfying (i) and (ii) above, and satisfying:

(iv)

\forall\ s\geq 1
,
\exists\ C_{s}>0
such that
|\theta_{i}(w)|\leq C_{s}\cdot\left(\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)\right)^{s-1}
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
and
i=0,1
.

Finally, for
s=\omega
let
\mathcal{E}_{\omega}
be the set containing only the trivial pair
(\theta_{1}\equiv 0,\theta_{2}\equiv 0)
.

Definition 19
.

For every
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
, we define an equivalence relation
\sim
on the set
\mathcal{E}_{s}
by setting that
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})\sim(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})
if and only if there exists a continuous function
\chi:\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\to(0,+\infty)
such that, for every sequence
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we have that

\chi(a)=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}{\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}.

(8)

It is straightforward to check that
\sim
is indeed an equivalence relation on the set
\mathcal{E}_{s}
. The function
\chi
can be interpreted as the "derivative’" of a conjugation defined along symbolic cylinders, even when the conjugation is
a priori
not known to be differentiable. When the limit defining
\chi
exists and remains bounded, it encodes the actual derivative of the conjugation, thereby characterizing the existence and regularity of a smooth conjugation. Formally:

Theorem 20
.

For every
0<\lambda<1/2
and
1\leq s\leq\infty
or
s=\omega
, there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of
C^{s}
-conjugacy classes of pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFSs of class
C^{s}
with slope
=\lambda
, and the set of equivalence classes determined by
\sim
on
\mathcal{E}_{s}
.

Proof.

Let’s start by noting that the statement is trivial when
s=\omega
. As we have already remarked at Section
3
, every analytic pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFS with slope
\lambda
, coincides over the minimal invariant set with the self-similar IFS of slope
\lambda
, and therefore it has constant proportions
\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda
, i.e.
\theta_{i}\equiv 0
,
i=0,1
.

When
1\leq s\leq\infty
, given a hyperbolic pseudo-affine IFS
\Phi=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}
of class
C^{s}
and slope
\lambda
, write its dynamical proportions in the form
\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda+\theta_{i}(w)
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
,
i=0,1
. In Proposition
13
we showed that the correspondence
\Phi\mapsto(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
takes values in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
. In Proposition
15
we showed how to construct, given any pair
(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
in
\mathcal{E}_{s}
, an hyperbolic pseudo-affine IFS
\Phi=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}
of class
C^{s}
with proportions
\lambda_{i}=\lambda+\theta_{i}(w)
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
,
i=0,1
, and hence
\Phi\mapsto(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})
is surjective.

Let
\Phi=\{f_{0},f_{1}\}
and
\widetilde{\Phi}=\{g_{0},g_{1}\}
be two pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFSs of class
C^{s}
and slope
=\lambda
. Write their dynamical proportions in the form
\lambda_{i}^{\Phi}=\lambda+\theta_{i}
and
\lambda_{i}^{\widetilde{\Phi}}=\lambda+\eta_{i}
, respectively. Let
X
and
Y
be their respective attractors, and recall from Section
2.4
that, since they are binary Cantor sets, their points have a labeling
X=\{x_{a}:a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\}
and
Y=\{y_{a}:a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\}
. We will denote by
\{I_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}}
and
\{J_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}}
the set of gaps in
[0,1]\setminus X
and
[0,1]\setminus Y
.

As we remarked in Section
2.4
, there always exist a homeomorphism
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
such that
h(X)=Y
and
G_{w}\circ h(x)=h\circ F_{w}(x)
,
\forall\ x\in X
and every finite word
w
(here we use
G_{w}
for cylinders in
Y
). Several conjugating homeomorphism
h
can be defined, but all of them coincide over
X
with the map taking
x_{a}\mapsto y_{a}
,
\forall a\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. By means of the rigidity theorems of Bedford-Fisher
[
9
]
, determining the regularity of
h:X\to Y
reduces to determine whether
h
is of class
C^{1}
or not. Because, in the case
X
and
Y
are
C^{s}
-Hyperbolic Cantor sets with
1\leq s\leq\infty
, every
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
h:X\to Y
is actually a
C^{s}
-diffeomorphism. As a consequence, to conclude the theorem it is enough to prove the following claim.

Claim
21
.

The homeomorphism
h:X\to Y
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism if and only if condition (
8
) holds.

To prove the claim, assume first that
h:X\to Y
is a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism. Without loss of generality, we can further assume that this map is the restriction onto
X
of a
C^{1}
-diffeomorphism
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
(cf. Section
2
). For every sequence
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}{\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})|J|}{\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})|I|}\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}=\frac{|J_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}|}{|I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}|}\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}=\frac{|h(I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}})|}{|I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}|}\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{|I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}|}\int_{I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}}h^{\prime}(t)dt\right)\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}=h^{\prime}(z_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}})\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|},

where
z_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
is some point in the closure of the gap
I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
. As
n\to+\infty
, the distance between the gap
I_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}
and the point
x_{a}\in X
converges to zero, so in particular
z_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}}\to x_{a}
and

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}{\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})}=\lim_{n\to+\infty}h^{\prime}(z_{a_{1}\cdots a_{n}})\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}=h^{\prime}(x_{a})\cdot\frac{|I|}{|J|}=\chi(a),

which is a continuous function.

Reciprocally, assume that (
8
) holds and let
x_{a},x_{b}
be points in
X
indexed by sequences
a,b\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
. To show that
h
is differentiable over the Cantor set
X
we have to study the limit of the incremental quotients

\lim_{x_{b}\to x_{a}}\frac{h(x_{b})-h(x_{a})}{x_{b}-x_{a}}=\lim_{x_{b}\to x_{a}}\frac{y_{b}-y_{a}}{x_{b}-x_{a}}.

(9)

Let’s assume without loss of generality that
x_{a}
can be accumulated from above by points
x_{a}<x_{b}
, and write

\frac{y_{b}-y_{a}}{x_{b}-x_{a}}=\frac{\sum_{a<w<b}\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(w)|J|}{\sum_{a<w<b}\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)|I|}=\frac{\sum_{a<w<b}\Gamma(w)\cdot\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)}{\sum_{a<w<b}\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)}\cdot\frac{|J|}{|I|},

where
\Gamma(w)=\Psi_{(\eta_{0},\eta_{1})}(w)/\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)
. Observe that, since by hypothesis
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\Gamma(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})=\chi(a)>0
, then given
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N>0
such that
\chi(a)-\varepsilon<\Gamma(a_{1}\cdots a_{n})<\chi(a)+\varepsilon
,
\forall\ n\geq N
. On the other hand, there exists
\delta>0
such that if
|x_{b}-x_{a}|<\delta
then
a_{i}=b_{i}
,
\forall\ i=1,\dots,N
. This implies that all the gaps
I_{w}
satisfying
x_{a}<I_{w}<x_{b}
have indexation satisfying
w_{i}=a_{i}
,
\forall\ i=1,\dots,N
. It follows that

(\chi(a)-\varepsilon)<\frac{\sum_{a<w<b}\Gamma(w)\cdot\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)}{\sum_{a<w<b}\Psi_{(\theta_{0},\theta_{1})}(w)}<(\chi(a)+\varepsilon),

whenever
|x_{b}-x_{a}|<\delta
. This implies that for every
x_{a}\in X
the limit in (
9
) above exists an is equal to
\chi(a)\cdot|J|/|I|
, from where we see that
h^{\prime}
exists and is continuous over the Cantor set
X
.
∎

4
On the proof of Theorem
2

In this section we prove Theorem
2
: For each degree of regularity
1\leq s\leq\infty
and
0<\lambda<1/2
we will construct a pseudo-affine hyperbolic IFS with slope
\lambda
that is of class
C^{s}
and not
C^{t}
,
\forall\ t>s
. Moreover, we will construct both cases (a)-
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar, and (b)- non
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar.

4.1
Case (a):
C^{s}
-conjugated to self-similar

Using Proposition
15
we can construct the IFS using a pair of functions
\{\theta_{i}:\mathcal{W}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{i=0,1}
that must satisfy the criteria therein in order to have a specified degree of regularity. For obtaining IFS conjugated to self-similar, we will use functions whose values on words
w\in\mathcal{W}
depend only on the size
n=|w|
of the word. That is, we will define
\theta_{i}(w)=\varepsilon_{|w|}
for some real sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
. Recall from Lemma
8
that the lengths of the gaps
\{I_{w}\}_{w\in\mathcal{W}}
associated to the Cantor set
X_{\Phi}
must be given by the expression
|I_{w}|=\Psi(w)\cdot|I|
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
, where
0<|I|<1
is the length of the gap corresponding to the empty word. In this particular case the function
\Psi:\mathcal{W}\to(0,1)
will take the form
\Psi(w)=\Psi(n)=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(\lambda+\varepsilon_{i})
for every word of length
|w|=n\geq 1
.
Let’s see first how to determine the sequences
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
for each degree of regularity, and then the conjugation with the self-similar model.

Case
s=1
.

Choose
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
to be any sequence satisfying that
0<\lambda+\varepsilon_{n}<1
for every
n\geq 0
, and
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
sub-exponentially. That is,
\sup\{\varepsilon_{n}\cdot\mu^{-n}:{n\geq 0}\}=\infty
for all
0<\mu<1
. For instance, take the sequence
\varepsilon_{0}=0
and
\varepsilon_{n}=\lambda n^{-\gamma}
for
n\geq 1
, where
\gamma>0
is some constant. Then, by Proposition
15
there exists a pseudo-affine IFS of class
C^{1}
with proportions
\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda+\varepsilon_{|w|}
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
. Using Remark
14
above we see that this IFS cannot be of class
C^{s}
for any
s>1
, since the functions
|\theta_{i}(w)|=|\varepsilon_{n}|
do not converge exponentially to zero with
|w|=n\to\infty
.

Case
1<s<\infty
.

Write
s=r+\alpha
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. It is enough to show the following:

Claim
22
.

There exists a sequence
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 0}
such that

a.

\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
with
n\to+\infty
and
0<\lambda+\varepsilon_{n}<1
,
\forall\ n\geq 0
;

b.

|\epsilon_{n}|=\Psi(n)^{s-1}
,
\forall\ n\geq 1
;

By Proposition
15
, item a. above implies the existence of a pseudo-affine IFS with dynamical proportions
\lambda_{i}(w)=\lambda+\theta_{i}(w)
, where
\theta_{i}(w)=\varepsilon_{|w|}
,
\forall\ w\in\mathcal{W}
and
i=0,1
. Item b. above implies that
|\theta_{i}(w)|=D\cdot|I_{w}|^{s-1}
for the constant
D=(1/|I|)^{s-1}>0
. Hence, the IFS is of class
C^{r,\alpha}
. Finally, using Proposition
13
we can check that the IFS is not
C^{t}
for

Proof of Theorem

Base case:

1.

It satisfies the Livsic condition: for some
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
,

G_{w}^{\prime}(y_{w})=\lambda^{|w|},\forall w\in\mathcal{W},

2.

It is not
C^{1}
-conjugate to a pseudo-affine IFS.

We begin by setting our framework: Let
T:[0,1]\to[0,1]
be the
angle-tripling map

T(x)=3\cdot x\text{ mod }1,

and let
\Lambda\subseteq[0,1]
denote the (
T
invariant) middle thirds Cantor set.

Theorem 31
.

There exist an increasing homeomorphism
h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
and a continuous function
\varphi:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that:

1.

\varphi
is not a
T|_{\Lambda}
coboundary: There does
not
exist a continuous function
u:\Lambda\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\varphi=u-u\circ T|_{\Lambda}.

Nonetheless,
\int_{\Lambda}\varphi\,d\nu=0
for every
T|_{\Lambda}
–invariant
Borel probability measure
\nu
on
\Lambda
.

2.

Writing
\widehat{T}:=h^{-1}\circ T\circ h
, we have

\widehat{T}\in C^{1}([0,1])
,
\widehat{T}(h^{-1}(\Lambda))=h^{-1}(\Lambda)
,
and there exists some
\log 2<P<\log 3
such that for every
x\in h^{-1}(\Lambda)
,

\log\widehat{T}^{\prime}(x)=P-\varphi(h(x))>0.

We proceed to deduce Theorem
30
from Theorem
31
. Set
\widehat{T}:=h^{-1}\circ T\circ h
and
\widehat{\Lambda}:=h^{-1}(\Lambda)
as in Theorem
31
. We will show two statements: First,
that the restriction
\widehat{T}|_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
satisfies a Livsic condition:

(\widehat{T}^{n})^{\prime}(p)=e^{nP},\qquad\text{for all }p\in\mathrm{Per}_{n}(\widehat{T}),

(13)

where
\mathrm{Per}_{n}(\widehat{T})=\{p\in\widehat{\Lambda}:\,\widehat{T}^{n}p=p\}
denotes the set of
n
-periodic points. Second, we will show that
\widehat{T}
is not
C^{1}
-conjugate to any expanding map on a compact invariant set that has constant derivative on that set.

Consequently, taking the inverse branches of
\widehat{T}
, we obtain a
C^{1}([0,1])
hyperbolic IFS satisfying
the Livsic condition (
11
), but not
C^{1}
-conjugate to a pseudo–affine IFS. Note that for this purpose the fact that
P>\log 2
is only used to ensure that the derivatives of both branches are bounded by
e^{-P}<\frac{1}{2}
. Since
h
is a homeomorphism conjugating it to the middle-
1/3
Cantor set, the first branch fixes
0
and the second fixes
1
, whence this IFS is indeed hyperbolic. This is Theorem
30
.

We thus begin by establishing (
13
). By Theorem
31
Part (2),

\log\widehat{T}^{\prime}(x)=P-\varphi(h(x))\quad\text{for all }x\in\widehat{\Lambda}.

If
p\in\mathrm{Per}_{n}(\widehat{T})
, then
h(p)\in\Lambda

is
n
-periodic for
T
. So, by summing along its orbit, we get

\log(\widehat{T}^{n})^{\prime}(p)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\log\widehat{T}^{\prime}(\widehat{T}^{k}p)=n\cdot P-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(T^{k}h(p)).

By Theorem
31
Part (1), the function
\varphi

has zero integral against every
T|_{\Lambda}
-invariant probability measure.
In particular,
q:=h(p)
has period
n
, so the
periodic orbit measure

\nu_{q}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\delta_{T^{k}q}

is
T
-invariant. Therefore

0=\int\varphi\,d\nu_{q}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(T^{k}q).

We thus obtain

\log(\widehat{T}^{n})^{\prime}(p)=n\cdot P,

which proves (
13
).

Now, assume for contradiction that
\widehat{T}=g^{-1}\circ A\circ g

where
g\in C^{1}([0,1])
is a diffeomorphism,
A\in C^{1}([0,1])
preserves and is expanding on the compact set
\Lambda_{A}
, and furthermore has constant derivative
a\in\mathbb{R}
on
\Lambda_{A}
. In particular,
g^{\prime}
is non vanishing on
\widehat{\Lambda}
, and so
\log|g^{\prime}|
is continuous on
\widehat{\Lambda}
.

We first claim that
a=e^{P}
. Since
0\in\Lambda
and
T(0)=0
, the map

T|_{\Lambda}
has a fixed point, hence
\widehat{T}|_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
has
the fixed point
p:=h^{-1}(0)\in\widehat{\Lambda}
.
Let
q:=g(p)\in\Lambda_{A}
. Then
A(q)=q
, and differentiating

\widehat{T}=g^{-1}\circ A\circ g
at
p
gives

\widehat{T}^{\prime}(p)=(g^{-1})^{\prime}(A(g(p)))\cdot A^{\prime}(g(p))\cdot g^{\prime}(p)=\frac{A^{\prime}(q)\,g^{\prime}(p)}{g^{\prime}(\widehat{T}(p))}.

Since
p
is a fixed point of
\widehat{T}
, we have
\widehat{T}(p)=p
, hence

\widehat{T}^{\prime}(p)=A^{\prime}(q)=a.

On the other hand, by (
13
) with
n=1
we have

\widehat{T}^{\prime}(p)=e^{P}
. Hence
a=e^{P}
.

We therefore obtain on
\widehat{\Lambda}=g^{-1}(\Lambda_{A})
that

\log\widehat{T}^{\prime}=\log|\widehat{T}^{\prime}|=\log|g^{\prime}|-\log|g^{\prime}|\circ\widehat{T}+\log|a|.

Combining this with Theorem
31
Part (2) (noting that

\widehat{T}^{\prime}(x)>0
for
x\in\widehat{\Lambda}
) and with
|a|=e^{P}
yields

\varphi\circ h=-\log|g^{\prime}|+\log|g^{\prime}|\circ\widehat{T}.

Setting
u:=-(\log|g^{\prime}|)\circ h^{-1}\in C(\Lambda)
,
we deduce

\varphi=u-u\circ T\quad\text{on }\Lambda,

contradicting Theorem
31
Part (1). This completes the proof of
Theorem
30
.

5.2.1
On the proof of Theorem
31

We begin by recalling
[
19
]
, which we use in order to construct the function
\varphi
in Theorem
31
.
Write
X:=\{0,1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\sigma:X\to X
be the left shift.
By
[
19
, Thm. A]
, there exists a function

\phi_{0}\in C(X)
such that:

(a)

\phi_{0}
lies in the uniform closure of the space of coboundaries

\{u-u\circ\sigma:\,u\in C(X)\}
, but
\phi_{0}
itself is not a coboundary;

(b)

for every
\sigma
–invariant Borel probability measure
\nu
,

\int_{X}\phi_{0}\,d\nu=0,

since each coboundary integrates to zero against invariant measures and

\phi\mapsto\int\phi\,d\nu
is continuous in the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
-norm.

In particular, by (a) there is no
u\in C(X)
with

\phi_{0}=u-u\circ\sigma
.

We now transfer this information to the Cantor set.
Let
\Sigma:=\{0,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}\subset X
denote the full shift on two symbols,
and let
\pi:\Sigma\to\Lambda
be the standard coding map via the triadic expansion,
which satisfies

\pi\circ\sigma|_{\Sigma}=T|_{\Lambda}\circ\pi.

Restrict
\phi_{0}
to
\Sigma
and define

\varphi_{0}:=\phi_{0}|_{\Sigma}\circ\pi^{-1}\in C(\Lambda).

Then
\varphi_{0}
is not a
T|_{\Lambda}
–coboundary, while for every

T|_{\Lambda}
–invariant probability measure
\eta
on
\Lambda
,

\int_{\Lambda}\varphi_{0}\,d\eta=\int_{\Sigma}\phi_{0}\,d(\pi^{-1}_{*}\eta)=0,

since
\pi^{-1}_{*}\eta
is
\sigma
–invariant.

Fix a continuous extension
\varphi\in C([0,1])
of
\varphi_{0}
.
Since
\varphi
is bounded, we may replace
\varphi
by
t\varphi
for some
t\in(0,1)
.
This scaling does not affect the coboundary properties as above on
\Lambda
, and it preserves
the vanishing of
\int_{\Lambda}\varphi\,d\eta
for all
T|_{\Lambda}
–invariant measures
\eta
.

Let
P(\varphi)
denote the topological pressure of
\varphi

(see e.g.
[
27
]
).
Since
P(0)=h_{\mathrm{top}}(T)=\log 3
and
P(\cdot)
depends continuously
(in fact,
1
–Lipschitz) on the potential in the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
norm,
for every
\varepsilon>0
we may choose
t>0
sufficiently small so that, after the
replacement
\varphi\leftarrow t\varphi
, we have

P(\varphi)>\log 3-\varepsilon.

(14)

In particular, we may choose
\varepsilon\in(0,\log\frac{3}{2})
and ensure that
P(\varphi)>\log 2
.

By applying thermodynamic formalistic tools to study the properties of the potential
\varphi-P(\varphi)
, we have the following Lemma:

Lemma 32
.

There exists a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
such that for every Borel set

E
contained in a single monotone branch of
T
,

\mu(T[E])=\int_{E}e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(y)}\,d\mu(y).

(15)

Moreover,
\mu
is non-atomic and is fully-supported (hence
\mu(I)>0
for every nonempty interval
I
).

The proof is similar to
[
10
, Thm. 5.1]
, while for the sake of completeness we present full details adapted to our specific context. As to not disrupt the flow of the proof, we postpone its proof, and proceed to deduce Theorem
31
from it.

Define the conjugacy by

h^{-1}(z):=\mu([0,z]),\qquad z\in[0,1].

Since
\mu
is non-atomic and fully supported,
h^{-1}
is continuous
and strictly increasing, hence has a continuous inverse

h:[0,1]\to[0,1]
; thus
h
is a homeomorphism.
Set
\widehat{T}:=h^{-1}\circ T\circ h
and

\widehat{\Lambda}:=h^{-1}(\Lambda)
.

Fix
x\in\widehat{\Lambda}
and write
z:=h(x)\in\Lambda
.
Working inside a monotone branch of
T
and letting
z^{\prime}\to z
,
(
15
) applied to
E=[z,z^{\prime}]
gives

h^{-1}(T(z^{\prime}))-h^{-1}(T(z))=\int_{[z,z^{\prime}]}e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(y)}\,d\mu(y).

By the continuity of
\varphi
,

=e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(z)+o(1)}(h^{-1}(z^{\prime})-h^{-1}(z)).

Setting
z^{\prime}=h(x+\varepsilon)
, dividing by
\varepsilon
, and taking
\varepsilon
to
0
, yields

\widehat{T}^{\prime}(x)=e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(h(x))}.

In particular,

\log\widehat{T}^{\prime}(x)=P(\varphi)-\varphi(h(x)).

Since
P(\varphi)>\log 3-\varepsilon
and
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
can be made
arbitrarily small by the same scaling, we may (after possibly shrinking
t
further)
assume that
P(\varphi)-\varphi(h(x))>0
for all
x\in\widehat{\Lambda}
. Define this
P(\varphi)
as our
P
.
This proves the claimed positivity of
\widehat{T}^{\prime}
on
\widehat{\Lambda}
.

It remains to prove Lemma
32
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Since
[0,1]
is compact and
\varphi
is continuous, there exists
a Lipschitz function on
[0,1]
,
\varphi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}([0,1])
, such that

\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}-\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon.

(16)

(For instance, take the inf/sup-convolution or a piecewise-linear approximation.)

Next, let

\Big\{I_{j}:=[\frac{j}{3},\frac{j+1}{3}):\,j\in\{0,1,2\}\Big\}

be the Markov partition for
T
, and let
T_{j}^{-1}:[0,1]\to I_{j}
be the inverse branches.
Define the transfer operator
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}:C([0,1])\rightarrow C([0,1])
by

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}f)(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}\,f(T_{j}^{-1}x).

By standard thermodynamic formalism for expanding Markov maps for Lipschitz potentials
(see
[
23
]
), there exists a Borel probability measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}
such that

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}^{*}\mu_{\varepsilon}=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\,\mu_{\varepsilon}.

(17)

Equivalently, for all
f\in C([0,1])
,

\int\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}f\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\int f\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}.

(18)

Next, fix
j\in\{0,1,2\}
and let
E\subset I_{j}
be Borel.
We first prove that

\mu_{\varepsilon}(T[E])=\int_{E}e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(y).

(19)

To do this without applying
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}
to an indicator function,
approximate
\mathbf{1}_{T[E]}
from below by continuous functions.
Choose
f_{n}\in C([0,1])
such that
0\leq f_{n}\uparrow\mathbf{1}_{T[E]}
pointwise
(e.g. take
f_{n}(x)=\max\{0,1-n\,\mathrm{dist}(x,T[E]^{c})\}
).
Set

g_{n}:=(f_{n}\circ T)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{I_{j}}.

Then
g_{n}
is bounded and Borel, and on each
x\in[0,1]
we have

(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}g_{n})(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{2}e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{i}^{-1}x)}\,g_{n}(T_{i}^{-1}x)=e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}\,f_{n}(x),

since
T_{i}^{-1}x\in I_{i}
and thus
\mathbf{1}_{I_{j}}(T_{i}^{-1}x)=0
for
i\neq j
.
Now apply (
18
) to the continuous function

h_{n}(x):=e^{-\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}\,(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}g_{n})(x)=f_{n}(x),

i.e. simply apply (
18
) to
f_{n}
and rewrite it via the identity above:

\int e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}f_{n}(x)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int\mathcal{L}_{\varphi_{\varepsilon}}g_{n}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\int g_{n}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}.

Therefore

\int e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}f_{n}(x)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\int_{I_{j}}f_{n}(Ty)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(y).

(20)

Now restrict the right-hand integral to
E
by replacing
g_{n}
with

(f_{n}\circ T)\cdot\mathbf{1}_{E}
(still Borel and bounded); the same computation gives

\int e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}f_{n}(x)\,\mathbf{1}_{T[E]}(x)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\int_{E}f_{n}(Ty)\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(y).

Letting
n\to\infty
and using monotone convergence (all integrands are nonnegative and bounded by

e^{\|\varphi_{\varepsilon}\|_{\infty}}
) yields

\int_{T[E]}e^{\varphi_{\varepsilon}(T_{j}^{-1}x)}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon})}\,\mu_{\varepsilon}(E).

Finally, since
T|_{I_{j}}
is a bijection
I_{j}\to[0,1]
, the change of variables
x=T(y)

with
y\in E\subset I_{j}
simply rewrites the last identity as (
19
).
Thus (
19
) holds for all Borel
E\subset I_{j}
.

Next, by compactness of probability measures on
[0,1]
in the weak-
*
topology,
choose
\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0
such that
\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to\mu
weak-
*
.

We also need
P(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}})\to P(\varphi)
. This follows from the standard

\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
–continuity of pressure for expanding maps:

|P(\psi)-P(\psi^{\prime})|\leq\|\psi-\psi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\quad\text{for }\psi,\psi^{\prime}\in C([0,1]),

(21)

hence by (
16
),
P(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}})\to P(\varphi)
.

Fix a branch
I_{j}
and a Borel set
E\subset I_{j}
.
Apply (
19
) with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{k}
:

\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}(T[E])=\int_{E}e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}})-\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y)}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y).

Let

F_{k}(y):=e^{P(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}})-\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}}(y)},\qquad F(y):=e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(y)}.

By (
16
) and
P(\varphi_{\varepsilon_{k}})\to P(\varphi)
we have

\|F_{k}-F\|_{\infty}\to 0,

and the functions
F_{k}
are uniformly bounded and continuous.
Therefore, using weak-
*
convergence and uniform convergence of the integrands,

\int_{E}F_{k}\,d\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}\longrightarrow\int_{E}F\,d\mu.

Also, since
T[E]
is Borel and
\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to\mu
weak-
*
, we have

\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}(T[E])\to\mu(T[E])
for all
E
such that
\mu(\partial T[E])=0
.
To remove this boundary issue for general Borel
E
, first prove (
15
)
for half-open intervals
E=[a,b)\subset I_{j}
(for which
\partial T[E]
is finite and

\mu
is non-atomic, see Step 5), and then extend to all Borel sets inside
I_{j}

by a standard monotone class argument.
Thus we obtain (
15
).

It remains to study the stated properties of
\mu
. First,
fix
x\in[0,1]
and pick
j
such that
\{x\}\subset I_{j}
.
Applying (
15
) to
E=\{x\}
yields

\mu(\{T(x)\})=e^{P(\varphi)-\varphi(x)}\mu(\{x\}).

Iterating,

\mu(\{T^{n}(x)\})=\exp\!\Bigl(nP(\varphi)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(T^{k}x)\Bigr)\mu(\{x\}).

Since
\varphi
is bounded, if
\mu(\{x\})>0
then the right-hand side grows exponentially
in
n
provided
P(\varphi)>\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
. In our application we arrange this
by scaling
\varphi
before starting the construction. As
\mu(\{T^{n}(x)\})\leq 1
, it follows that

\mu(\{x\})=0
. Hence
\mu
is non-atomic.

Finally, let
U\subset[0,1]
be a nonempty open interval. Since
T(x)=3x\!\!\mod 1

is topologically exact, there exists
n\geq 1
such that
T^{n}[U]=[0,1]
.
Partition
U
into finitely many intervals
U=\bigsqcup_{\ell=1}^{m}U_{\ell}
on which

T^{n}
is monotone (hence injective). Then

[0,1]=T^{n}[U]=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{m}T^{n}[U_{\ell}],

so for some
\ell_{0}
the set
T^{n}[U_{\ell_{0}}]
contains a nonempty open interval
J
.
Since
\mu
is a probability measure, there exists such a
J
with
\mu(J)>0
.

Because
T^{n}
is injective on
U_{\ell_{0}}
, iterating (
15
) along the

n
steps (branch by branch) yields, for every Borel
E\subset U_{\ell_{0}}
,

\mu(T^{n}[E])=\int_{E}\exp\!\Bigl(nP(\varphi)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(T^{k}y)\Bigr)\,d\mu(y).

Apply this with
E:=U_{\ell_{0}}\cap T^{-n}[J]
(which is nonempty and open in
U_{\ell_{0}}
).
Using
\varphi\geq-\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
we get

\mu(J)=\mu(T^{n}[E])\leq e^{nP(\varphi)+n\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}\,\mu(E),

hence
\mu(E)>0
. Since
E\subset U
, this gives
\mu(U)>0
.
As
U
was arbitrary,
\mu
has full support.

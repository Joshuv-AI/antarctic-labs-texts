Energy-aware latency minimization in multi-source spatiotemporal data fusion for IoT networks
Authors: Mohammed H. Alsharif, Arun Kumar, Saibal Manna, Vivek Saxena, Shakir Khan, Mohsin Ali Koondhar, Sabri Saeed, Nour H. M. Alsharif
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
As smart city infrastructures evolve, they generate massive volumes of spatiotemporal data from diverse sources such as surveillance cameras, wearable health monitors, drones, and environmental sensors. Efficient fusion of this data is crucial for applications that demand ultra-low latency (typically between 2 ms and 5 ms), to support time-sensitive operations. However, the heterogeneity of data sources, coupled with constraints on communication bandwidth and computational resources, poses a significant challenge to maintaining such stringent latency and energy efficiency standards. This study proposes a joint optimization framework for multi-source spatiotemporal data fusion that dynamically allocates bandwidth and CPU cycles to minimize a weighted objective of latency and energy consumption under realistic wireless channel conditions and strict resource constraints. The results demonstrate that the proposed method consistently delivers low latency and energy-efficient performance across all data sources. It outperforms traditional equal and delay-tolerant strategies by significantly reducing both latency and energy consumption. This efficiency, combined with the framework’s robustness andscalability, makes it highly suitable for smart city applications.

Introduction

The proliferation of smart city infrastructures has led to a dramatic increase in the deployment of Internet of Things (IoT) devices, including high-definition surveillance cameras, wearable health monitors, GPS modules, drones, and environmental sensors1,2. These heterogeneous sources continuously generate vast volumes of spatiotemporal data that must be fused in real-time to enable mission-critical applications such as autonomous vehicle coordination, emergency response, and industrial automation3,4. Many of these applications demand ultra-low-latency communication and processing (typically within 2–5 ms), to ensure safe and timely responses5,6. For instance, coordinated drone navigation requires latency under 3 ms7, robot arm collaboration in manufacturing operates effectively below 2.5 ms8, and adaptive traffic signal control systems react optimally within 2.5 ms9. These strict latency constraints underscore the importance of real-time, energy-efficient data fusion systems in smart urban environments.

To address these challenges, this study introduces a novel joint optimization framework that intelligently and dynamically allocates both communication bandwidth and computational processing power (CPU cycles) across multiple heterogeneous data sources in a smart city environment. Unlike traditional approaches that statically divide resources or rely on simple heuristics, this framework uses a holistic, data-driven optimization strategy to ensure efficient and fair resource utilization based on each source’s specific requirements. The core objective of the framework is to minimize a weighted aggregate of communication latency, processing latency, and total energy consumption, reflecting the critical trade-offs in real-time smart city operations. Importantly, the model incorporates both per-source and global system constraints, including strict latency thresholds (< 5 ms per source, < 20 ms overall), bandwidth limitations, CPU availability, and energy budgets.

What sets this approach apart is its source-aware resource allocation mechanism. Each data stream is treated uniquely based on key characteristics such as its data volume, wireless channel quality (e.g., SNR, fading conditions). The model leverages this information to compute optimal bandwidth allocations and CPU cycles that minimize latency and energy use without compromising synchronization across sources. For example, high-data, latency-sensitive sources like surveillance cameras are granted more bandwidth, while computation-heavy sources like wearable devices are allocated higher CPU resources to reduce processing delays. This tailored allocation strategy enables the system to avoid both under-provisioning (which leads to delay violations) and over-provisioning (which causes energy inefficiencies), ensuring that each source receives exactly the resources it needs to operate effectively in a dynamic, constrained smart city infrastructure.

The contributions of this study are threefold. First, we develop a joint energy-aware latency minimization framework that integrates communication and processing delays with their corresponding energy costs into a single optimization model, ensuring ultra-low latency and sustainable energy use under realistic resource constraints. Second, we introduce a source-aware dynamic resource allocation strategy that tailors bandwidth and CPU assignments to each source’s data volume, computational load, and wireless channel quality, while explicitly incorporating fading effects and synchronization constraints achieving fine-grained efficiency beyond traditional heuristic or equal-allocation methods. Third, we demonstrate the robustness and scalability of the proposed framework through extensive simulations and sensitivity analyses, showing that it consistently outperforms equal and delay-tolerant baselines by significantly reducing both latency and energy consumption.

The optimization problem for multi-source spatiotemporal data fusion in IoT networks is subject to several critical constraints, each designed to ensure that the system operates efficiently while maintaining performance and adhering to the available physical resources. Bandwidth, as a shared resource in wireless networks, must be carefully managed to avoid congestion and reduced throughput. Constraint (8) ensures that the total bandwidth used by all sources does not exceed the maximum available bandwidth Bmax of the network. Meanwhile, constraint (9) defines the lower and upper bounds for the bandwidth allocated to each source, ensuring that each source operates within these bounds based on its communication needs and the physical limitations of the network. This helps ensure fair allocation, preventing sources from either monopolizing the bandwidth or underusing it. Similarly, each source requires a certain amount of computational capacity to process its data, and constraint (10) limits the total CPU processing power used by all sources to ensure that the system does not exceed the available computational resources Fmax. This prevents system overload, ensuring that the computational resources are sufficient to handle the data processing tasks without delays or failures. In addition, constraint (11) places limits on the minimum and maximum CPU frequencies allocated to each source, ensuring that each device has enough computational resources to process its data while preventing over-allocation, which could lead to unnecessary energy consumption. This constraint balances computational performance with energy efficiency, which is crucial for prolonging the lifetime of battery-operated devices and minimizing environmental impact. Furthermore, constraint (12) ensures that the energy consumed by communication and processing for each sources does not exceed the maximum allowable energy budget \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E}_{i}^{max}$$\end{document}, safeguarding the system from energy overload and ensuring that the system operates sustainably. Constraint (13) ensures that the aggregate energy of all sources combined does not exceed the overall system energy \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${E}_{System}^{max}$$\end{document}, which guarantees that the system as a whole meets its energy requirements. Constraint (14) and (15) ensures that the communication and process energy for each source remains within predefined minimum and maximum bounds, preventing both underutilization and excessive energy use. This balance guarantees fair energy distribution across sources. Latency is another critical consideration for real-time data fusion, and constraint (16) ensures that the latency for each source, comprising both communication and processing latency, does not exceed its maximum allowable latency \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L}_{i}^{max}$$\end{document}. Finally, constraint (17) ensures that the aggregate latency of all sources combined does not exceed the overall system latency \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${L}_{System}^{max}$$\end{document}, which guarantees that the system as a whole meets its latency requirements. These constraints ensure that resources such as bandwidth, CPU power, and energy are allocated efficiently, preventing overuse or underuse of resources. By setting limits on energy consumption and latency, the system ensures that it operates sustainably while still meeting the performance requirements of time-sensitive applications. The constraints also provide fair resource distribution, preventing any one source from monopolizing the available bandwidth or processing capacity, which is crucial for scalability in large IoT networks. Additionally, these constraints help to optimize system performance while maintaining energy efficiency, which is vital for battery-powered devices and for minimizing the environmental impact of IoT systems.

The optimization problem presented in this context is a non-linear constrained optimization problem, where the objective is to minimize a weighted sum of latency and energy consumption across multiple data sources in a multi-source spatiotemporal data fusion system. The decision variables, such as the bandwidth and CPU frequency allocated to each source, are subject to several constraints, including bandwidth limits, computational capacity, energy consumption bounds, and latency requirements. The problem involves non-linear relationships due to the logarithmic nature of communication latency, the quadratic dependence of processing energy on CPU frequency, and the complex interaction between communication and processing components. This non-linearity, combined with multiple constraints, makes the problem inherently challenging and suitable for optimization techniques capable of handling complex, constrained, and non-convex objective functions. MATLAB is an ideal choice for solving this type of optimization problem due to its extensive built-in libraries, flexibility, and ease of use for handling both mathematical optimization and numerical simulations. The Optimization Toolbox in MATLAB offers powerful functions like ‘fmincon’ that can efficiently solve non-linear constrained problems, making it suitable for the given scenario. ‘fmincon’ uses advanced optimization algorithms, such as Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP), which is highly effective for handling non-linear objective functions and constraints. This algorithm iteratively approximates the problem using a quadratic model, making it suitable for problems where the objective function and constraints are smooth but non-linear. It is important to emphasize that many recent MEC-based and joint optimization studies are developed under substantially different system architectures, objective functions, and modeling assumptions, such as task offloading, single-objective latency minimization, or partially coupled resource allocation frameworks. These differences make direct one-to-one numerical comparisons methodologically inappropriate, as the underlying optimization structures, constraint couplings, and performance metrics are not equivalent. Therefore, the comparisons presented here focus on practical allocation strategies operating under the same system model and constraints, ensuring fairness and consistency in performance evaluation.

To further position the proposed framework relative to established optimization paradigms, convex relaxation or decomposition-based heuristics could also be applied to the same coupled nonlinear formulation. However, such methods would ultimately target the identical constraint structure and objective function. Conclusion

This paper introduced a robust optimization-based framework for energy-aware, low-latency spatiotemporal data fusion in multi-source IoT systems operating within smart cities. The proposed joint resource allocation method effectively allocates communication and computational resources according to each data source’s unique characteristics, including data volume, urgency, and processing demand. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the framework, latency per source is kept under the 5 ms critical threshold (ranging from 2.2 to 4.8 ms), and total system latency remains at 17.6 ms well below the 20 ms requirement for real-time operation. Energy consumption is equally well managed, with per-source values ranging from 0.3 to 0.48 J and total energy capped at 1.84 J, outperforming traditional equal allocation (2.25 J) and delay-tolerant methods (27.25 J). Additionally, sensitivity analysis confirms the framework’s robustness to variations in energy-weighting hyperparameters (γ, δ), demonstrating stable objective values across several orders of magnitude. Integrating the proposed optimization framework with UAV mobility control, on-demand charging, or secure collaborative computing mechanisms represents an interesting direction for future research. Moreover, future work will extend the proposed framework to large-scale IoT deployments involving tens or hundreds of heterogeneous data sources to further evaluate scalability and resource contention effects. Extensive Monte Carlo simulations under diverse wireless channel models, including Rician and shadowed fading, will also be conducted to capture statistical performance variations and robustness under realistic propagation conditions. These extended evaluations will provide deeper insights into the trade-offs between latency, energy efficiency, and computational complexity in dense smart city environments.

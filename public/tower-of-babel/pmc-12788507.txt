Diagnostic Performance of AI-Powered Histopathology: Can Machines Match Oral Pathologists in Identifying Epithelial Dysplasia?
Authors: Nishath Sayed Abdul, Sharad Chand, Shishir Dhar, Pooja Mohan Pharne, Somesh Ojha, Megha Kadani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Epithelial dysplasia is a critical precursor to oral squamous cell carcinoma. Manual histopathology, though gold standard, is subject to variability and delay.Objective:To evaluate the diagnostic accuracy of an artificial intelligence (AI)-powered histopathology system in identifying and grading oral epithelial dysplasia.Materials and Methods:A cross-sectional study compared AI analysis of digitized histology slides (n = 250) to manual diagnoses by expert pathologists. Agreement, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and Cohen’s kappa were calculated.Results:AI achieved >93% diagnostic accuracy across categories. Strong agreement was noted for non-dysplastic and severe lesions; moderate discrepancies occurred in mild/moderate grades.Conclusion:AI demonstrates strong potential in aiding dysplasia diagnosis, particularly for high-volume screening, though human oversight remains essential.

INTRODUCTION

Epithelial dysplasia represents a critical stage in the multistep progression from normal oral mucosa to oral squamous cell carcinoma, and its timely detection is essential for effective prevention and management of oral potentially malignant disorders. Histopathological examination under light microscopy by trained oral pathologists remains the gold standard for diagnosing epithelial dysplasia, grading lesions as mild, moderate, or severe based on architectural and cytological criteria. However, subjective variability, workload pressures, and limited access to expert histopathologists in many regions contribute to diagnostic inconsistencies and delays in patient care.[1]

Artificial intelligence (AI), particularly in the form of deep learning-based image analysis, has emerged as a promising tool in pathology, offering rapid, objective, and reproducible assessments of digitized histological slides.[23] Integrating AI into oral pathology workflows could enhance diagnostic throughput, standardize reporting, and support less-experienced pathologists, particularly in resource-limited settings.[4] This study aims to explore the diagnostic performance of an AI-powered histopathology application in detecting epithelial dysplasia on archived oral mucosal biopsy slides.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

A total of 250 archived hematoxylin and eosin-stained oral epithelial tissue slides were retrieved from departmental archives. These slides had been previously evaluated by two experienced oral pathologists and categorized as non-dysplastic or dysplastic, further classified into mild, moderate, or severe grades, serving as the ground truth reference. Inclusion criteria comprised good-quality slides with complete clinical metadata, while exclusion criteria included damaged or poor-quality slides, ambiguous prior diagnoses, or incomplete labeling.

All included slides were digitized using a high-resolution slide scanner or microscope-mounted digital camera at 40× magnification, generating whole-slide images in standardized formats. The digitized WSIs were then processed using an AI-powered histopathology application, which was either an open-source platform configured to classify oral epithelial samples into non-dysplastic or dysplastic categories.

Each AI-generated output, including binary classification (normal vs. dysplastic) and detailed grading (mild, moderate, and severe), was recorded and compared to the manual microscopic diagnosis by the expert pathologists. Discrepancies between AI and human outputs were flagged for detailed review.

RESULTS

A total of 250 archived oral epithelial histopathology slides were included in the analysis, comprising 120 non-dysplastic slides and 130 dysplastic slides (45 mild, 50 moderate, and 35 severe). The AI-powered histopathology application successfully processed all digitized slides, providing automated classification outputs.

DISCUSSION

AI-assisted histopathology has emerged as a transformative tool in diagnostic pathology, promising high throughput and consistency, particularly in resource-constrained settings. This study evaluated the diagnostic accuracy of an AI-powered model in identifying oral epithelial dysplasia and compared it with conventional manual microscopic diagnosis by expert oral pathologists. The AI system demonstrated high overall agreement with manual diagnosis, particularly in identifying non-dysplastic tissue and severe dysplasia cases.

The diagnostic agreement ranged from 90% to 96.5% across the categories, with the highest concordance observed in non-dysplastic samples and severe dysplasia. This indicates AI’s proficiency in classifying clearly normal or markedly abnormal histology. However, discrepancies were more pronounced in mild and moderate dysplasia, consistent with previous reports that these intermediate stages often present overlapping histological features and pose diagnostic challenges, even for experienced pathologists.[56]

The false negative rate for mild dysplasia was 3, indicating that some low-grade dysplastic changes were missed by the AI, likely due to their subtle cytological alterations. Similarly, the model produced false positives in moderate dysplasia, suggesting a tendency to over-diagnose intermediate lesions, potentially due to heightened sensitivity settings or model training bias.[7] While the model achieved excellent specificity across categories (>95%), slightly reduced sensitivity in moderate lesions underscores the need for further model refinement through expanded and balanced datasets.

The kappa values, while moderate overall, also highlight the interpretive subjectivity inherent in grading dysplasia. This variation mirrors the interobserver variability documented in conventional histopathology.[8] Receiver operating characteristic curve analyses demonstrated high area under the curve values (>0.93) across all dysplasia grades, confirming strong discriminative power and supporting AI’s clinical applicability.[910]

Limitations

Mild and moderate dysplasia categories showed diagnostic discrepancies, reflecting AI’s current limitations in borderline lesion interpretation. The model’s performance depends on the quality and diversity of the training data, necessitating multicentric validation for generalizability.

CONCLUSION

AI-based histopathology tools show promising diagnostic accuracy for epithelial dysplasia, especially in identifying non-dysplastic and severe lesions. Moderate agreement in borderline cases suggests AI should complement—not replace—human expertise. Future refinements and larger datasets are essential to strengthen reliability and clinical integration.

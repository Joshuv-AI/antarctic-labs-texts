A Fundamental Theorem on Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen Steering
Yu-Xuan Zhang, Jing-Ling Chen

Abstract
Quantum nonlocality is an essential nonlocality resource in quantum information. It has been classified into three distinct types: quantum entanglement, Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) steering, and Bell’s nonlocality. In 1991, Gisin presented a fundamental theorem on Bell’s nonlocality, pointing out all pure entangled states possess Bell’s nonloclaity. Many of the core protocols of quantum information science (such as quantum teleportation, quantum key distribution, and certain algorithms in quantum computing) rely on entanglement. Gisin’s theorem tells us that as long as we successfully prepare a pure entangled state, we then have a Bell-nonlocality resource that can show the non-classical correlations. Such a resource is not “virtual” and can be tested and used through Bell-experiments. Similarly, in this work, we present a Gisin-like fundamental theorem on EPR steering, which indicates all rank-2 (and rank-1) entangled states possess EPR steerability. Thus all rank-2 entangled states can be applicable as EPR-steering resources in quantum information.

Conclusion.—
In this work, we have established a fundamental theorem on EPR steering that plays a role analogous to Gisin’s theorem in the hierarchy of quantum nonlocality. We have proven that, under local unitary transformations, any two-qubit entangled state of rank 2 (including rank 1 as a special case) must be steerable. This result demonstrates that all rank-2 entangled states can serve as steering resources in quantum information, thereby theoretically confirming a universal equivalence between entanglement and steering for this class of states.

To prove the theorem, we first derived a simplified form of rank-2 quantum states under local unitary transformations and obtained a necessary and sufficient condition for their separability. Furthermore, we constructed a state-dependent steering inequality and showed that if a rank-2 state is entangled, it necessarily violates the classical bound of this inequality, thus certifying its steerability. The proof is not only theoretically rigorous but also illustrated with several concrete examples.

Our work fills a gap in the foundational understanding of EPR steering within the quantum nonlocality hierarchy and provides a key theoretical criterion for identifying steering resources in experiments. The result reinforces the resource value of quantum entanglement in information processing, indicating that even lower-rank mixed entangled states possess non-classical correlations applicable to tasks such as quantum communication and quantum key distribution.

Future research may be directed along the following lines: Extending the theorem to higher-dimensional or higher-rank quantum systems; developing experimental detection schemes based on this theorem; and further investigating the utility of rank-2 entangled states as steering resources in concrete quantum protocols. Such studies will deepen our understanding of the hierarchy of quantum nonlocality resources and offer a firmer theoretical foundation for practical applications in quantum information science.

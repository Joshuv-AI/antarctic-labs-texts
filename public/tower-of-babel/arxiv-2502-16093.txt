d=3
dilaton field defined by

e^{-2\phi_{3}(X)}~=~\frac{e^{-2\varphi(X)}}{g_{s}^{2}V_{0}}\int_{Y}\!d^{7}x\sqrt{g_{7}+\delta g_{7}}~,

where
g_{s}=e^{\phi_{0}}
is the zero-mode of the dilaton.
Then, a Weyl transformation on the
d=3
metric
g_{E\,ef}=e^{-2\phi_{3}}g_{ef}
collapses the first three terms in (
3.3
) into the Einstein frame Ricci scalar. The ten-dimensional action (
2.1
) under dimensional reduction gives

\begin{split}S~=~\frac{g_{s}^{2}}{2\kappa_{3}^{2}}\int d^{4}X\sqrt{g_{E}}\left({\rm Phys.~Rev.~E}{3}\mathcal{R}_{E}+\mathcal{L}\right)~,\end{split}

(3.4)

where
\kappa_{10}^{2}=V_{Y}g_{s}^{-2}\kappa_{3}^{2}
,
V_{Y}
is the volume of the
G_{2}
manifold and
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{g}+\mathcal{L}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{S}+\mathcal{L}_{F}
is the three-dimensional Lagrangian for the kinetic terms of the moduli fields coming from the reduction of the first four terms of (
2.1
). We compute each of these terms below. The remaining terms in (
2.1
) are at least
\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})
and are ignored.

The first term
\mathcal{L}_{g}
comes from the
\mathcal{R}
in (
3.3
) and is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{g}~&=-\frac{1}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}d^{7}x\sqrt{g}\,g^{mn}g^{pq}\,{\partial}_{e}y^{a}(\delta_{a}g_{mp})\,{\partial}^{e}y^{b}(\delta_{b}g_{nq})~.\end{split}

Here
\delta g_{mn}\cong\delta y^{a}\delta_{a}g_{mn}
corresponds to a field variation and writing the space of operators as
\delta_{a}
acting on the background fields. We also use the notation
{\partial}_{e}y^{a}\cong{\partial}_{e}(\delta y^{a})
so that our expressions remain semi-compact.

The next term
\mathcal{L}_{B}
comes from the kinetic term for
B
:

\mathcal{L}_{B}~=-\frac{1}{2V_{Y}}\int_{Y}{\text{d}}^{7}x\sqrt{g}\,|H+\delta H|^{2}~.

(3.5)

In this expression,
H
has all three legs along
Y
, while
\delta H
always has a leg in three-dimensional spacetime, so
H\star\delta H=0
leaving

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{B}~=-\frac{1}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}{\text{d}}^{7}x\sqrt{g}\,\delta H_{emn}\delta H^{emn}~.\end{split}

Now, by Poincare invariance of
\mathbbl{R}^{2,1}
the only relevant term to the metric in
\delta H
is one with a spacetime derivative of
\delta y^{a}
:

\delta H_{emn}\;=\;({\partial}_{e}y^{a})\mathpzc B_{a\,mn}~,

Putting these together we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{g}+\mathcal{L}_{B}&\;=\;-\frac{1}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\left\{\left(\delta_{a}g_{mn}\delta_{b}g^{mn}+\mathpzc B_{a\,mn}\mathpzc B_{b}{}^{mn}\right){\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}\right\}\\
&\;=\;-\frac{1}{V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\left\{M_{a\,mn}M_{b}{}^{mn}{\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}\right\}~,\end{split}

(3.6)

where we use that
\mathpzc B_{a}
is antisymmetric,
\delta_{a}g
is symmetric and

M_{a\,mn}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{a}g_{mn}+\mathpzc B_{a\,mn}\right)~.

(3.7)

The Yang-Mills term is

\mathcal{L}_{F}\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\,\text{Tr}\thinspace|\delta F|^{2}~,\qquad|\delta F|^{2}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\delta F_{MN}\delta F^{MN}~.

The gauge connection
A
depends on parameters

\delta A\;=\;\delta y^{a}\mathfrak{a}_{a\,m}{\text{d}}x^{m}~,

and as above by promoting the parameters to dynamical
d=3
moduli fields we find an effective lagrangian for the moduli

\begin{split}\text{Tr}\thinspace|F+\delta F|^{2}&\;=\;\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\delta F_{em}\delta F^{em}\big)+\cdots\;=\;({\partial}_{e}y^{a})({\partial}^{e}y^{b})\,\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(\mathfrak{a}_{a\,m}\,\mathfrak{a}_{b}{}^{m}\big)+\cdots~.\end{split}

There are omitted terms and these are ones that do not contribute the moduli space metric. The action is

\mathcal{L}_{F}\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\,\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\mathfrak{a}_{a\,m}\mathfrak{a}_{b}{}^{m}\right){\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}~.

We need to include the contribution of the deformations of the Hull connection on the tangent bundle. They appear formally in the same way as the Yang-Mills term with an opposite overall sign:

\mathcal{L}_{K}\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\,\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(K_{a\,m}K_{b}{}^{m}\right){\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}~.

(3.8)

Our job is to solve this term by eliminating
K
using (
2.7
).
Using that
\text{Tr}\thinspace K_{a\,q}K_{b}{}^{q}=g^{qp}\,K_{a\,q}{}^{s}{}_{t}K_{b\,p}{}^{t}{}_{s}
, together with (
2.7
), we get

\begin{split}\text{Tr}\thinspace K_{a\,q}K_{b}{}^{q}&\;=\;-(\nabla_{s}M_{a\,qt}-\nabla_{t}M_{a\,qs})(\nabla^{s}M_{b}{}^{qt}-\nabla^{t}M_{b}{}^{qs})~.\end{split}

Hence, putting this into
\mathcal{L}_{K}
and integrating by parts

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{K}&\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\,\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(K_{a\,q}K_{b}{}^{q}\right){\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}\\
&\;=\;\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\Big\{M_{aqt}(\nabla_{s}\nabla^{s}M_{b}{}^{qt}-\nabla_{s}\nabla^{t}M_{b}{}^{qs})\Big\}{\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}~.\end{split}

(3.9)

We now use a few little results. First, the commutator

[\nabla_{s},\nabla_{t}]M_{b}{}^{qs}\;=\;R_{st}{}^{q}{}_{p}M_{b}{}^{ps}+R_{st}{}^{s}{}_{p}M_{b}{}^{qs}\;=\;R_{st}{}^{q}{}_{p}M_{b}{}^{ps}~,

(3.10)

where because
\nabla
is the Levi-Civita connection to this order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
the Ricci-tensor
R_{st}{}^{s}{}_{p}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
. There is a Lichnerowicz equation for the field
M_{a}
. To derive this, recall the action (
2.1
). The equation of motion, to zeroth order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, for
\delta B
is given by

\begin{split}-\frac{1}{2}({\text{d}}^{\dagger}{\text{d}}\delta B)_{qr}&\;=\;\frac{1}{3}\left(\nabla^{p}\nabla_{p}\delta B_{qr}+R^{p}{}_{qr}{}^{s}\delta B_{sp}-R^{p}{}_{rq}{}^{s}\delta B_{sp}+\nabla_{r}\nabla^{p}\delta B_{pq}-\nabla_{q}\nabla^{p}\delta B_{pr}\right)\;=\;0~.\end{split}

The equation of motion for the metric to zeroth order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
comes from
R(g+\delta g)=R(g)=0
, which in the gauge
\nabla^{p}\delta g_{pq}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
is the Lichnerowicz equation. If we also impose the gauge
\nabla^{p}\delta B_{pq}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
, which is consistent with (
2.11
), viz.
\nabla^{p}M_{pq}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
, then we find equations of motion:

\begin{split}\nabla^{p}\nabla_{p}\delta B_{qr}-R^{p}{}_{qr}{}^{s}\delta B_{ps}+R^{p}{}_{rq}{}^{s}\delta B_{ps}&\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})~,\\[3.0pt]
\nabla^{p}\nabla_{p}\delta g_{qr}-2R^{s}{}_{qr}{}^{p}\delta g_{ps}&\;=\;0+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})~.\end{split}

Adding these two together

\begin{split}&\nabla^{p}\nabla_{p}M_{qr}-2R^{p}{}_{qr}{}^{s}M_{ps}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(3.11)

Putting together (
3.10
), (
3.11
) with the gauge fixing
\nabla^{p}M_{pq}=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{K}&\;=\;-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\Big\{M_{a\,qt}R^{qpts}M_{b\,ps}\Big\}{\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}~.\end{split}

To finish the calculation, we put together the above results

\mathcal{L}\;=\;\mathcal{L}_{g}+\mathcal{L}_{B}+\mathcal{L}_{F}+\mathcal{L}_{K}~,

to give

\mathcal{L}\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\left(M_{a\,mn}M_{b}{}^{mn}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}M_{a\,mn}R^{mpnq}M_{b\,pq}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\mathfrak{a}_{a\,m}\mathfrak{a}_{b}{}^{m}\right)\right){\partial}_{e}y^{a}{\partial}^{e}y^{b}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

We identify the
G_{2}
moduli space metric from the kinetic terms of the moduli fields

\!\!\!\!G_{ab}\;=\;\frac{1}{V_{Y}}\int_{Y}\mbox{\,vol}\left(M_{a\,mn}M_{b}{}^{mn}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}M_{a\,mn}R^{mpnq}M_{b\,pq}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left(\mathfrak{a}_{a\,m}\mathfrak{a}_{b}{}^{m}\right)\right)+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.

(3.12)

This is the
G_{2}
moduli space metric conjectured in
[
38
]
. This proves it as at the moduli space metric for the physical heterotic theory. The role of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-corrections and eliminating the spurious modes were important.

As an aside, we can factorise the first two terms via a field redefinition
\widetilde{M}_{a\,mn}=M_{a\,mn}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}M_{a\,mn}R_{mpnq}M_{a}{}^{pq}
rendering a Weyl–Peterson inner product. However, the field
\widetilde{M}_{a}
does not transform in the same was
M_{a}
under diffeomorphisms due to the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-correction – it is not of the form of
\delta g+\mathpzc B
. So the metric and its
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-correction is non-trivial. A second point is the observation that if
\mathcal{L}_{K}
is evaluated in the coordinate frame, using (
2.6
), then
\mathcal{L}_{K}=0
.

Returning to (
3.12
), we can dimensionally reduce to
SU(3)
by an ansatz
Y=X\times S^{1}
, where
X
is an
SU(3)
manifold and the circle decompactifies to enhance
\mathbbl{R}^{2,1}\to\mathbbl{R}^{3,1}
. The fields reduce as

\begin{split}\delta y^{a}M_{a\,{\overline{\rho}}{\overline{\mu}}}&\;=\;\delta y^{a}\Delta_{a\,{\overline{\rho}}{\overline{\mu}}}\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha}\Delta_{\alpha\,{\overline{\rho}}{\overline{\mu}}}~,\\
\delta y^{a}M_{a\,{\overline{\sigma}}\mu}&\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{a}\mathpzc Z_{a\,{\overline{\sigma}}\mu}\;=\;-\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{\alpha}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}~,\end{split}

(3.13)

where on the left hand side we have written coordinates on the
G_{2}
moduli space and on the right hand side these are embedded into the
SU(3)
moduli space coordinates. The field
M
is real and so this equation is to be supplemented by its complex conjugates. We have introduced holomorphic indices and note that the
SU(3)
holomorphic gauge fixes
\mathpzc Z_{{\overline{\alpha}}\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}=0
and
\Delta_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}}=0
. This leaves us with the right hand side.

We now substitute this into the
G_{2}
moduli space metric and we find

\begin{split}G^{\sharp}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}&\;=\;\frac{1}{V_{X}}\int_{X}\Big(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\star\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\,g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}+\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{(1,1)}\star{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}^{(1,1)}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}A\star{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}A)}\Big)\\[2.84544pt]
&\hskip 85.35826pt~+~\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2V_{X}}\int_{X}\mbox{\,vol}~\left(\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}}\,\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}\rho\sigma}+\frac{1}{4}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha\,\rho{\overline{\mu}}}\,{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{{\overline{\beta}}\,\sigma{\overline{\nu}}}\right)\,R^{{\overline{\mu}}\rho{\overline{\nu}}\sigma}+\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2})~.\end{split}

This reduction result was evidence for the conjectured moduli space metric in
[
38
]
. We have now proved this at the level of heterotic supergravity, including the first
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-correction.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, we have derived the moduli space metric of heterotic
G_{2}
compactifications from the ten-dimensional heterotic supergravity at
{\cal O(\alpha^{\prime})}
, confirming the result conjectured in
[
45
]
. This metric allows the construction of an adjoint operator
{\check{D}}^{\dagger}
, whose kernel describes a natural gauge fixing for the moduli, as discussed in
[
38
]
.

Appendix A
Converting between Lorentz and coordinate indices on connections

The Levi–Civita connection in coordinate indices is given by the Christoffel symbols. Its deformation is

\delta\Gamma_{m}{}^{p}{}_{q}\;=\;g^{sp}(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{s]m}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{m}\delta g_{sq}).

Lower the middle index using the metric

\delta\Gamma_{m\,pq}\;=\;(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{p]m}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{m}\delta g_{pq}).

(A.1)

We want to convert this to a deformation of the spin connection,
\delta\Theta_{ab}
and metric compatibility requires antisymmetry in
ab
. A priori,
\delta\Gamma
is not antisymmetric in
pq
.

First recall the vielbein

g_{pq}\;=\;\eta_{ab}e^{a}_{p}e^{b}_{q}~,\qquad\qquad\eta_{ab}\;=\;E^{p}_{a}E^{q}_{b}g_{pq}~,\qquad e^{a}=e^{a}_{p}{\text{d}}x^{p}~.

Here
\eta_{ab}={\rm diag}(1,\cdots,1)
.
Taking a small deformation we find

\begin{split}\delta g_{pq}\;=\;\eta_{ab}(\delta e^{a}_{p}e^{b}_{q}+e^{a}_{p}\delta e^{b}_{q})\;=\;\delta e^{a}_{p}e_{aq}+e_{ap}\delta e^{a}_{q}~.\end{split}

(A.2)

A small abuse of notation
e_{aq}=\eta_{ab}e^{b}_{q}
so that
\delta e_{aq}=\eta_{ab}\delta e^{b}_{q}
. It is also true that

\delta e_{aq}=\frac{1}{2}\delta g_{qp}E^{p}_{a}~,

which can be checked by substituting back into (
A.2
).

The relationship between the spin connection
\Theta
and the Christoeffel symbols
\Gamma
is in say
[
43
]
:

\begin{split}\Theta_{m\,ab}&\;=\;E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\Gamma_{m\,pq}-E_{b}^{n}{\partial}_{m}e_{an}~.\end{split}

A small deformation is

\begin{split}\delta\Theta_{m\,ab}&\;=\;E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\delta\Gamma_{m\,pq}-E_{b}^{q}\nabla_{m}\delta e_{aq}-\delta E_{b}^{q}\nabla_{m}e_{aq}~.\end{split}

Substitute into (
A.1
),

\delta\Theta_{m\,ab}\;=\;E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\left(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{p]m}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{m}\delta g_{pq}\right)-(\nabla_{m}\delta e_{aq})E_{b}^{q}-(\nabla_{m}e_{aq})\delta E_{b}^{q}~.

Using that
\delta e_{aq}=\frac{1}{2}\delta g_{qp}E^{p}_{a}
,

\begin{split}\delta\Theta_{m\,ab}&=E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\left(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{p]m}{+}\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{m}\delta g_{pq}\right){-}\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{m}\delta g_{qp})E^{p}_{a}E_{b}^{q}{-}\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{m}E^{p}_{a})\delta g_{qp}E_{b}^{q}{-}(\nabla_{m}e_{aq})\delta E_{b}^{q}\\
&=E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\left(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{p]m}\right)-(\nabla_{m}E^{p}_{a})\delta e_{bp}-(\nabla_{m}e_{aq})\delta E_{b}^{q}\\
&=E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\left(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{p]m}\right)~.\end{split}

(A.3)

To see the last equality we used metric compatibility of the vielbein:

\nabla_{m}e^{a}_{p}\;=\;\!-\Theta_{m}{}^{a}{}_{c}e^{c}_{p}~,\qquad\nabla_{m}E^{p}_{a}\;=\;\Theta_{m}{}^{c}{}_{a}E^{p}_{c}~,\quad\Theta_{mab}\;=\;\!-\Theta_{mba}~.

Hence,

\delta e_{bp}\nabla_{m}E_{a}^{p}\;=\;-\Theta_{mac}e^{c}_{q}\delta e_{bp}g^{pq}~,\qquad\delta E^{q}_{b}\nabla_{m}e_{aq}=\Theta_{mac}e^{c}_{q}g^{qp}\delta e_{bp}~.

To conclude, we write down the deformation of the Hull connection in the spin frame:

\delta\Theta^{\text{H}}_{m\,ab}\;=\;E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\left(\nabla_{[q}\delta g_{m|p]}+\nabla_{[q}\mathpzc B_{m|p]}\right)\;=\;2E_{a}^{p}E_{b}^{q}\,\nabla_{[q}M_{m|p]}~.

(A.4)

We read off how this transforms under small gauge

\delta\Theta^{\text{H}}_{m\,ab}\sim\delta\Theta^{\text{H}}_{m\,ab}-R_{mabq}\varepsilon^{q}+\nabla_{m}(\nabla_{b}\varepsilon_{a}-\nabla_{a}\varepsilon_{b})~.

From the fact this is a gauge connection with a canonical transformation law

{\delta\Theta^{\text{H}}{}_{ab}\sim\delta\Theta^{\text{H}}{}_{ab}-R_{abq}\varepsilon^{q}+{\text{d}}\psi_{ab}}
we identify
\psi_{ab}=-(\nabla_{a}\varepsilon_{b}-\nabla_{b}\varepsilon_{a})
.

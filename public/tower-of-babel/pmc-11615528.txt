PaveDistress: A comprehensive dataset of pavement distresses detection
Authors: Zhen Liu, Wenxiu Wu, Xingyu Gu, Bingyan Cui
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
The PaveDistress dataset contains high-resolution images of road surface distresses, including cracks, repairs, potholes, and background images without defects. The data were collected using a specialized pavement inspection vehicle along the S315 highway in China. The vehicle was equipped with a Basler raL2048-80km line scan camera and infrared laser-assisted lighting, capturing images at 1mm intervals with a resolution of 3854 × 2065 pixels. The images were taken every 2 meters across various lighting conditions, including daylight, dusk, and in challenging environments such as tunnels and cloudy weather. The dataset is organized into distinct categories, covering transverse cracks, longitudinal cracks, map cracks, and more, enabling detailed categorization of pavement distresses. Each image represents a real-world road coverage area of 3.9m × 2.1m, allowing for accurate measurements of defect dimensions. This dataset supports the development of deep learning models for non-destructive detection of road defects, providing valuable resources for civil engineering research and practical applications in road maintenance systems. The dataset can be reused for tasks such as image classification, object detection, and segmentation, enabling researchers to create advanced machine learning models for road distress detection and assessment. By providing high-quality, diverse images, the PaveDistress dataset offers significant potential for research in automated pavement condition monitoring and management systems.

Background

Previously available open-source pavement distress datasets predominantly feature cement pavements [1,2], with limited representation of asphalt pavements. In asphalt pavements, the presence of various obstructions, such as barriers and standing water, often leads to imprecise recordings of distress features, thereby affecting the accuracy of image processing and hindering the development of effective detection models [3]. Moreover, most asphalt pavement distress datasets focus solely on crack distress [4,5], overlooking other types of distresses that, although less frequent, are equally critical for evaluating pavement conditions and guiding maintenance and repair activities [6]. The dataset provided in this data article supplements our original research on intelligent detection and segmentation of pavement distresses, specifically potholes [7] and patches [8]. By publishing this dataset, we aim to offer valuable resources for the broader research community, facilitating further advancements in machine learning models for pavement distress detection. This dataset adds value to the original article by enabling reproducibility, fostering new research applications, and supporting comparative studies on the performance of different detection algorithms.

Data Description

The road surface defect dataset introduced in this study primarily includes categories such as pavement background, cracks, crack repairs, guardrail posts, water stains, and expansion joints, as illustrated in Fig. 1.Fig. 1Samples images of proposed dataset.Fig 1

Samples images of proposed dataset.

As shown in Fig. 2, the dataset is organized into several folders, with images of corresponding types placed within them. These folders include those for storing original images (<origin>), images containing only the road surface background (<background>), images with cracks (<crack>), images with repairs (<patch>), images with potholes (<pothole>), and other images (<other>). Within the "crack" folder, there are subfolders for transverse cracks, longitudinal cracks, and map cracks. Background images (without defects and free from obstructions), crack images (with transverse cracks, longitudinal cracks, and map cracks respectively categorized into the transverse crack, longitudinal crack, and map crack subfolders), repair images, and pothole images are placed into the <background>, <crack>, <patch>, and <pothole> folders, respectively. Images containing road structures such as manhole covers or studs, as well as uncertain images, are placed in the <other> folder.Fig. 2Classification and naming of the dataset.Fig 2

The inspection target is a section of the S315 highway within China, specifically the Bayanhaote-Erkehashiha segment. This road section is a key focus for maintenance management, providing favorable conditions for data collection in this study. Data was collected from 10:00 AM to 5:00 PM, with the camera positioned parallel to the road surface, ensuring that the captured images accurately reflect the road width and, consequently, the defect dimensions. The road inspection vehicle can perform long-distance, rapid, and automated surface inspections while traveling at normal speeds. Triggered by an encoder's mileage function, the vehicle uses a high-resolution line scan camera to perform seamless longitudinal scanning at 1mm intervals along the standard lane, generating a road surface image every 2 meters. Infrared laser-assisted lighting is employed to enable reliable data collection under all-weather conditions and in specific environments (such as tunnels, culverts, cloudy days, and nighttime). This ensures the capture of authentic road surface images, facilitating data acquisition for road maintenance management systems (CPMS) and road network surveys. The specific performance advantages include:•Continuous, uninterrupted detection without mileage limitations, with real-time data storage.•Ethernet communication is employed between the testing system and the industrial control computer.•The testing process is unaffected by the dynamic performance of the inspection vehicle and can be conducted within a broad speed range (20–70 km/h) without compromising test results.•The system features a specially designed single camera/laser structure, allowing a detection width of up to 3.75 meters or more, enabling full-lane road surface data collection.•The system supports camera gain adjustment and is equipped with multiple gain adjustment schemes. During detection, the appropriate scheme can be quickly selected, improving manual adjustment efficiency.•The system incorporates an optical filtering design that eliminates the effects of natural light and vehicle shadows on image quality, ensuring clear and sharp images.•The high-resolution area scan camera can capture road-side facilities while driving at high speeds, storing the images for convenient review and playback.•Real-time data upload and real-time tracking of the detection path.

Limitations

The PaveDistress dataset introduced in this paper was collected using a specialized pavement inspection vehicle. The images are of high quality and uniform format, and after image processing, they can be utilized for classification, detection, and segmentation tasks in deep learning, ultimately leading to the development of an intelligent, non-destructive distress detection model. However, the dataset primarily contains distress types such as cracks and repairs, with only a limited number of potholes. Other distress types relevant to pavement condition assessments, such as ruts, heaving, and bleeding, are rarely observed. Consequently, the model developed from this dataset may exhibit limitations in recognizing distresses like ruts, which have a three-dimensional nature. Future research will aim to better account for the impact of such three-dimensional distresses, expanding the distress types covered in pavement condition assessments to ensure the dataset's completeness and authenticity.

Structured Outputs Enable General-Purpose LLMs to be Medical Experts


Abstract
Medical question-answering (QA) is a critical task for evaluating how effectively large language models (LLMs) encode clinical knowledge and assessing their potential applications in medicine. Despite showing promise on multiple-choice tests, LLMs frequently struggle with open-ended medical questions, producing responses with dangerous hallucinations or lacking comprehensive coverage of critical aspects. Existing approaches attempt to address these challenges through domain-specific fine-tuning, but this proves resource-intensive and difficult to scale across models. To improve the comprehensiveness and factuality of medical responses, we propose a novel approach utilizing structured medical reasoning. Our method guides LLMs through an seven-step cognitive process inspired by clinical diagnosis, enabling more accurate and complete answers without additional training. Experiments on the MedLFQA benchmark demonstrate that our approach achieves the highest Factuality Score of 85.8, surpassing fine-tuned models. Notably, this improvement transfers to smaller models, highlighting the method’s efficiency and scalability. Our code and datasets are available.

Introduction

Language lies at the heart of healthcare delivery, serving as the primary medium through which medical knowledge is communicated and healthcare decisions are made. Recent advances in LLMs have shown promising potential in medical applications, particularly in tasks requiring deep clinical knowledge and reasoning
Singhal and et al. 2022
;
Tu and et al. 2023
;
Labrak et al. 2024
;
Jeong et al. 2024b
;
Liu et al. 2023
. Among these applications, medical QA stands out as a critical task for evaluating how effectively LLMs encode and apply clinical knowledge. This task is particularly challenging, as it requires an understanding of medical context, the recall of relevant knowledge, and the ability to reason with expert-level information.

While existing LLMs have demonstrated impressive performance on medical benchmarks, such as MedMCQA
Pal et al. 2022
and USMLE
Examination 2023
, these evaluations primarily focus on knowledge retrieval rather than comprehensive reasoning. This limitation has led to the development of more challenging benchmarks like MedLFQA
Jeong et al. 2024a
, which demands detailed, factually accurate, and clinically relevant long-form responses.

When asked about a health condition like boils and carbuncles, an adequate response must cover multiple aspects: how they are treated, potential causes, and common symptoms. Current models sometimes provide incomplete responses, addressing only certain parts of the question while leaving out critical information. This lack of comprehensive detail can result in patients or healthcare providers missing important aspects of diagnosis and treatment.

Existing approaches have primarily relied on two strategies to address these challenges: supervised fine-tuning with domain-specific datasets and chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting
Kalajdzievski 2024
. While fine-tuning can improve performance, it requires substantial computational resources and risks catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained knowledge. CoT prompting has shown promise in enhancing reasoning capabilities, but its effectiveness in generating comprehensive medical responses remains limited, particularly for models with restricted context lengths.

Our research demonstrates that
Medical Structured Output CoT (Med-SoCoT)
effectively mitigates hallucinations and "echo" phenomena while improving the factuality in a training-free manner, enabling even smaller models to generate high-quality medical responses. For models with limited context length, overly long prompts can hinder the complete output of structured content. To address this, we adopt a step-by-step reasoning approach, where the model generates one piece of structured content at a time, and the outputs are concatenated for summarization. Notably, this approach notably achieves performance improvements on par with fine-tuned models, demonstrating that prompt engineering alone can substantially enhance factuality without requiring additional training.

Our research focuses on two key questions: First, whether structured output can enhance the factual accuracy of medical question answering; and second, whether smaller models can achieve structured outputs solely through prompt engineering. Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We integrate chain-of-thought reasoning with structured output to develop novel medical prompts, demonstrating that general-purpose LLMs can produce comprehensive answers with reduced hallucinations without additional training. Our approach achieves the highest Factuality Score of 85.8, surpassing even fine-tuned models, which achieve a maximum Factuality Score of 74.2.

•

For models with shorter context lengths, we employed stepwise reasoning without altering the prompt content, achieving an 12.7% improvement in the comprehensiveness of the model’s output, increasing Factuality Score from 61.6 to 69.4.

•

For smaller models (3B), our approach remains highly effective, boosting Factuality Score from 78.6 in Zero-shot settings to 84.5. This improvement highlighting the robustness of our method across model sizes.

2.3
Structured Output

In traditional NLP literature, structured data typically refers to the extraction of entity-relation-entity triples and attributes from plain text, followed by the construction of knowledge graphs or databases with specific data formats or schemas
Jiang et al. 2023b
;
Li et al. 2023
. In previous work, "structuring" was used to reorganize input sentences into a three-layer structure based on the internal semantic relationships between them. This method captures the dependencies and connections among elements in long-text inputs, thereby enhancing LLMs’ understanding of knowledge structure and relationships. Earlier studies have explored how structuring input information can improve LLMs’ cognitive abilities
Liu et al. 2024
. In the work on GPT-4o, structured output refers to organizing the content generated by a language model into a specific format or pattern
OpenAI 2023
. This output often includes predefined labels, fields, or sections to make it more convenient for users to interpret and use. Structured output not only helps reduce redundancy in generated content but also ensures that the text meets certain requirements or standards.

In this paper, instead of focusing on entity-level information extraction or restructuring the input content, we primarily leverage the model’s structured output capabilities to guide it in completing structured thinking. This enhances the model’s ability to recognize the structure and relationships between recalled knowledge and the posed questions.

3.1
Overview

Structured reasoning plays a crucial role in modern medical applications by enhancing diagnostic accuracy and decision-making efficiency through the systematic organization of complex medical knowledge. By emulating human cognitive processes, it ensures a logical and coherent approach to clinical problem-solving. This is particularly vital for large language models (LLMs) in medical decision-making, where, despite vast knowledge, LLMs often produce fragmented outputs without guidance. A structured framework allows these models to analyze data systematically, consider relevant clinical factors, and generate contextually appropriate, reliable conclusions, improving both accuracy and consistency.

To implement this, we propose a framework based on established clinical reasoning theories
Higgs 2020
;
Young et al. 2020
;
Shin 2019
;
Pelaccia et al. 2011
. It breaks down diagnostic and therapeutic reasoning into systematic steps, ensuring a logical progression from patient assessment to treatment planning. Supported by evidence-based strategies and structured models, this approach yields precise, actionable outputs that enhance clinical decision-making.

3.2
Medical Structured Reasoning Process

Structured reasoning is a cognitive approach that organizes complex medical problems into systematic decision-making steps. Inspired by clinical reasoning in medicine
Higgs 2020
;
Young et al. 2020
;
Shin 2019
;
Pelaccia et al. 2011
, we propose a structured framework comprising seven key cognitive processes. This framework provides large language models (LLMs) with a clear reasoning pathway that mimics human medical cognition, thereby improving accuracy, interpretability, and reducing reasoning errors.

1. Understand the Question

The first step in medical reasoning is accurately understanding the patient’s problem. This involves an in-depth analysis of the chief complaint, medical history, and contextual factors. Research indicates that a well-defined problem statement is the foundation of a successful diagnosis
Ng et al. 2024
. For LLMs, precise input interpretation is critical in minimizing errors and setting the context for subsequent logical analysis.

2. Recall Relevant Medical Knowledge

Once the problem is clearly defined, clinicians recall relevant disease patterns, risk factors, and physiological mechanisms. The disease script theory suggests that experienced physicians rely on stored patterns for rapid decision-making
Thampy et al. 2019
. This step is essential for LLMs, as an effective knowledge retrieval mechanism can reduce hallucinations and ensure medically accurate responses. Moreover, robust knowledge recall directly supports the next step—logical analysis of the medical information.

3. Analyze Medical Information

Using the retrieved knowledge, clinicians systematically compare and interpret the collected patient data to generate differential diagnoses. The dual-process theory highlights that analytical reasoning is particularly crucial in complex or unfamiliar cases
Higgs 2020
. For LLMs, this step requires strong logical reasoning capabilities to avoid misinterpretations caused by data noise or biases. Furthermore, accurate knowledge recall (Step 2) enhances this analytical process by providing the necessary context for reasoning.

4. Assess Impacts and Considerations

After forming diagnostic hypotheses, clinicians evaluate potential treatment risks, ethical considerations, and resource constraints. Multilevel decision frameworks, such as the Four Quadrant Model, help balance medical, ethical, and logistical factors
Corrao and Argano 2022
. This step bridges logical analysis (Step 3) and actionable decision-making (Step 5). For LLMs, incorporating structured assessment criteria reduces bias and ensures recommendations align with clinical best practices.

5. Provide Additional Relevant Information

During diagnosis and treatment, clinicians must ensure that all necessary medical information is conveyed clearly to patients and colleagues. Studies show that effective patient education fosters trust and improves treatment adherence
Richards and et al. 2020
. This step is crucial for LLMs, as providing clear, accessible explanations enhances transparency and interpretability. Additionally, structured information delivery reinforces the justification for proposed actions (Step 6).

6. Suggest Follow-Up Steps or Actions

A well-structured medical decision should include a personalized follow-up plan, specifying additional tests, treatment goals, and monitoring schedules. Research indicates that structured follow-up plans help prevent complications and improve patient adherence
Bae et al. 2023
. This step naturally follows information provision (Step 5), ensuring continuity of care. For LLMs, integrating clinical pathways into their reasoning process enables them to generate practical and clinically relevant recommendations.

7. Reference Reliable Sources

Finally, clinical decisions must be grounded in peer-reviewed literature and evidence-based guidelines (e.g., Cochrane Database, PubMed). Medical education research emphasizes that reliance on high-quality information sources is critical for maintaining diagnostic and treatment consistency
Young et al. 2020
;
Pelaccia et al. 2011
. This final step strengthens the credibility of previous recommendations and mitigates misinformation risks in LLM-generated medical reasoning.

8. Conclusion

This structured framework in medical decision-making ensures a cohesive and logical process by following a sequential reasoning path, where each step builds upon the previous one. It begins with problem identification, integrates relevant medical knowledge, and systematically analyzes information to enable evidence-based decisions. By assessing potential impacts and ethical considerations, it refines reasoning to ensure contextually appropriate choices. Additionally, it provides supplementary information and follow-up recommendations, supporting both immediate and long-term care strategies.

Unlike unconstrained generative methods, structured outputs follow predefined frameworks, enhancing consistency, accuracy, and interpretability while reducing errors from fragmented recall or misinterpretation. This approach improves clinical reasoning by guiding models through a step-by-step diagnostic process, minimizing critical oversights. It also strengthens explainability and verifiability, ensuring AI recommendations are transparent, traceable, and aligned with medical guidelines, reinforcing LLM reliability.

3.3
Structured Output Generation

Instructional Guidance

To ensure the generated content is logical and coherent, we use prompts or guidewords to direct the model step by step in producing content in a specific format. As shown in Figure
8
, by clearly specifying the required sections or types of information, the model sequentially fills in the corresponding information, ensuring that the generated content adheres to the expected structure. Additionally, by imposing constraints on the output, the model avoids repetition and redundancy, making the content more concise and intuitive.

Template Generation

By predefining structured templates, the model can populate each field of the template step by step. To enhance the comprehensiveness and accuracy of medical question answering, we designed seven key stages of prompts to guide the model in generating structured content.

Stepwise Generation

Given the complexity of certain tasks that may exceed the model’s context processing capabilities. For example, LLaMA2-7B-HF supports a context length of only 4k tokens, we divide them into multiple stages, with the model outputting structured content one step at a time.
Each generation focuses on a specific stage, and through multiple iterations, the model progressively constructs the complete structured content. After generating the content for all stages, the model combine the information to generate a comprehensive long-form response.

3.4
Optimization Strategies

To further enhance the comprehensiveness and credibility of medical question answering, we propose the following optimization strategies:

One-shot Example

After the chain of thought, provide an example closely related to the question to help validate the reasoning process. This example should directly guide towards the final answer, ensuring the accuracy and operational effectiveness of the reasoning chain.

Conciseness of the Problem Statement

Ensure the problem is presented immediately after the chain of thought, avoiding unnecessary spaces, line breaks, or redundant information to improve input clarity and processing efficiency. Refining the problem statement helps reduce potential misinterpretations by the model and improves response time.

Instruction Reinforcement

For critical instructions, repeat them multiple times in the prompt to reinforce their execution. For example, both in the chain of thought and the one-shot example, the "Structured Outputs" tag should be used, and it should be appended again at the end to clearly guide the model’s output.

Specialized Markers

Use specific markers to extract important content, such as "ANSWER END," to facilitate subsequent processing and analysis.

Content and Word Count Limitation

We perform quality checks on each step of the step-by-step reasoning, including content and format checks to remove information that is irrelevant to the question in the model output, so that the intermediate reasoning steps do not affect the quality of the answer in the final step. Ensure that the chain of thought is free from redundancy, with each phase of the reasoning chain kept to a maximum of 200 words to avoid repetitive outputs. The final answer should be limited to 512 tokens, ensuring it is concise and precise.

Content Output Strategy

In the first stage, the reasoning chain may output longer structured information (up to 4096 tokens). However, in the second stage, the answer extraction should be limited to 512 tokens to ensure efficiency and focus.

Through the above methods and strategies, we ensure that the structured output for medical question answering is comprehensive, accurate, and meets the high standards required for clinical and research applications.

5
Conclusion

This paper introduces a method centered on structured outputs to improve the factuality and comprehensiveness of LLMs in medical QA tasks. By leveraging structured prompts and supporting both one-shot reasoning and stepwise reasoning, the method enhances long-form text generation without additional training. Structured outputs significantly improve factual accuracy, enabling smaller models to perform comparably to fine-tuned large models, while stepwise reasoning helps mitigate performance limitations in resource-constrained settings. The structured prompts, covering seven key aspects of medical reasoning, consistently outperform baselines in factuality accuracy and comprehensiveness. This approach demonstrates the potential of structured outputs in optimizing LLM performance across specialized domains, offering a cost-effective alternative to model training with broader applicability in areas like legal and technical documentation.

Limitations

Our Med-SoCoT prompt is an initial step toward optimizing structured outputs in medical reasoning tasks. However, its generalizability to non-medical domains and efficiency in resource-constrained settings remain open questions. Future work could explore more advanced prompt designs and adaptive strategies to enhance performance and applicability across diverse tasks and models.

Ethical Considerations

Ethical considerations are central to our research. In this study, we ensure adherence to ethical principles by exclusively using publicly available datasets and employing models that are open-source or widely accepted within the research community. We emphasize transparency in all stages of our work and prioritize the responsible application of technology, particularly in the sensitive domain of medical reasoning, to ensure that our contributions promote fairness, reliability, and societal benefit.

Appendix A
Evaluation Index Calculation Method

In this section, we describe the formulas used to compute various evaluation metrics.

A.1
Words Composition (Words Composition)

The Words Composition metric is calculated as the average of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, and ROUGE-L F1 scores:

\text{Words Composition}=\frac{\text{ROUGE-1 F1}+\text{ROUGE-2 F1}+\text{ROUGE-L F1}}{3}

A.2
Hallucination Score (Hallucination Score)

The Hallucination Score measures the proportion of elements in set
S
that are contradicted by the prediction
\hat{P}
:

\text{Hallucination Score}(\hat{P})=\frac{|\{x\in S\mid\hat{P}\text{ contradicts }x\}|}{|S|}

A.3
Comprehension Score (Comprehensiveness Score)

The Comprehension Score evaluates the proportion of elements in the medical hypothesis set
MH
that are entailed by the prediction
\hat{P}
:

\text{Comprehensiveness Score}(\hat{P})=\frac{|\{x\in MH\mid\hat{P}\text{ entails }x\}|}{|MH|}

A.4
Factual Score (Factuality Score)

The Factual Score combines the Comprehension Score and the Hallucination Score to produce a balanced metric, normalized to a scale of 0 to 100:

\text{Factuality Score}=\frac{(\text{Comprehensiveness Score}-\text{Hallucination Score})+100}{2}

3. Analyze Medical Information: The relationship between Noonan syndrome and polycystic renal disease may stem from shared genetic pathways or phenotypic associations.

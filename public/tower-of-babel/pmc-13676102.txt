GATA-3 identifies a distinct subset of T-PLL associated with skin involvement and CCR4 expression
Authors: Marc G. Gutierrez, Chenguang Wang, Kedar V. Inamdar, Michael B. Stone, Vida Ehyaee, Kevin Xu, Girish Venkataraman, Philip Boonstra, Carlos Murga-Zamalloa, Ryan A. Wilcox
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Visual Abstract

TO THE EDITOR:

T-cell prolymphocytic leukemia (T-PLL) is a rare and generally aggressive malignancy derived from mature T cells. The current World Health Organization classification has incorporated diagnostic criteria from the 2019 T-PLL International Study Group (TPLL-ISG), including the presence of a lymphocytosis with a T-PLL immunophenotype, demonstration of T-cell clonality, and 1 or more genetic alterations, most notably rearrangements involving a T-cell leukemia/lymphoma protein 1 (TCL-1) family gene.2 Historically, response rates to conventional chemotherapeutic agents have been poor and are rarely durable. The introduction of the anti-CD52 monoclonal antibody, alemtuzumab, has marginally improved progression-free survival, with median progression-free survival ≈11 and ≈5 months anticipated in the frontline and relapsed setting, respectively.3, 4, 5, 6 Allogeneic stem cell transplantation is curative in a small subset of patients, whereas ≈75% of patients experience disease relapse within 4 years of allogeneic stem cell transplant, and disease relapse remains the most common cause of death in these patients.7 Crucially, even in the alemtuzumab era, those who receive T-PLL–directed therapy do not have significantly prolonged survival over those who remain untreated.8 Clinical characteristics (eg, anemia and advanced age at diagnosis), the presence of TCL-1 rearrangement by cytogenetics and complex cytogenetics (defined as ≥3 chromosomal abnormalities) have been independently associated with poorer survival in T-PLL.8,9 Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets that reflect and exploit the underlying biology of this disease.

The hijacking of T-cell specific pathways, including those driven by antigen- and cytokine-receptors, is an emerging motif in T-PLL. The differentiation and survival of normal (nonmalignant) T-cell subsets is regulated by a network of key transcription factors. Among these, the zinc-finger transcription factor GATA-3 plays a central role in the homeostatic survival, proliferation, and differentiation of mature T cells. GATA-3 is oncogenic in other mature T-cell lymphomas which, like T-PLL, are resistant to conventional chemotherapy.10,11 GATA-3 itself and the target genes it regulates are promising therapeutic targets in these T-cell lymphomas.11, 12, 13

For example, mogamulizumab, a CCR4-specific monoclonal antibody, mediates antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity, leading to depletion of CCR4-expressing cells.11,14 Given the need for improved therapeutics for T-PLL, we explored GATA-3's clinical significance in T-PLL.

We retrospectively evaluated patients with T-PLL, diagnosed at the University of Michigan, Henry Ford Health System, the University of Chicago, and the University of Illinois Chicago between 2007 and 2025 using the TPLL-ISG criteria.2 Patients were selected in accordance with US federal regulations, the Declaration of Helsinki, and with institutional review board approval of participating institutions. Diagnostic biopsy specimens were available for 29 patients, and clinical data were available for 25, with a median follow-up of 33 months (range, 4-268).

All slides were deparaffinized and stained using an autostainer (Leica Biosystems). The antibodies for immunohistochemistry included: CD3 (CD3e monoclonal antibody [SP7], Thermo Fisher Scientific), GATA-3 (Cell Signaling, catalog no. S852), and CCR4 (Novus Biologicals, catalog no. NBP1-86584). The percentage of tumor cells expressing GATA-3 and CCR4 was determined, and a threshold of 30% was utilized to examine GATA-3 or CCR4 expression as a dichotomized variable (Figure 1A-D; Table 1). There is no established threshold for GATA-3 immunohistochemistry in T-PLL. Characteristics of patients with T-PLL compared by GATA-3 status

For the subset of patients with serial biopsies available (n = 4), GATA-3 expression was averaged to determine a representative status.

ECOG PS, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status; IQR, Interquartile range; LDH, lactate dehydrogenase; ORR, Overall response rate; PFS, progression-free survival.

Treatment response was assessed per TPLL-ISG criteria.2 Survival analyses were performed by the Kaplan-Meier method with median survivals compared by the log-rank test. Two-tailed Fisher exact test was used for binary variables, and Wilcoxon 2-sample test was used for continuous variables.

GATA-3 was intensely expressed in 51% (18/35) of the samples analyzed (Figure 1A-D). We observed that GATA-3 expression was associated with a genetically and clinically distinct subset (Table 1). All cases with GATA-3+ expression (100%) had a complex karyotype (as defined by ≥3 chromosomal abnormalities), compared to 55% of those that did not express GATA-3 (P = .038). GATA-3+ cases were also associated with TCL-1 rearrangements by cytogenetics. Additional genetic characteristics are described in supplemental Table 1.

Clinically, GATA-3+ T-PLL was associated with several high-risk features (Table 1). Patients in the GATA-3+ group were significantly older at diagnosis (median age, 66 vs 52 years; P = .007), had poorer Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (P = .02), and had more severe anemia (hemoglobin level of <10.0 g/dL in 36% vs 0%, respectively; P = .046). Remarkably, skin involvement was observed exclusively in patients with GATA-3+ T-PLL (43% of cases) and was absent in the GATA-3− group (P = .024).

Regarding treatment outcomes, overall response rates to frontline therapy and the median number of treatment lines did not differ significantly by GATA-3 status. However, patients with GATA-3+ expression were significantly less likely to undergo allogeneic stem cell transplant (29% vs 73%; P = .047). This disparity likely stems from the advanced age and poorer performance status in the patients with GATA-3+ T-PLL, underscoring the need for more tolerable, targeted therapeutic interventions for this specific subgroup.

Because CCR4 is a known GATA-3 target gene in other T-cell lymphomas and is essential for skin-homing in normal T cells, we sought to determine if GATA-3 directly regulates CCR4 within T-PLL. We performed GATA-3 chromatin immunoprecipitation–quantitative polymerase chain reaction in a T-PLL cell line (SUP-T11). We observed GATA-3 binding at the CCR4 locus, as previously described (Figure 1E), suggesting that GATA-3 acts as a transcriptional regulator of CCR4 in T-PLL.11 This was further supported by immunohistochemistry, which revealed that 43% (15/35) of the clinical samples expressed CCR4 (Figure 1F), with an association between GATA-3 and CCR4 expression (P = .041). To validate these findings, we interrogated a public T-PLL data set from the National Center for Biotechnology Information database (accession number: GSE107397). Within this public cohort, GATA-3 and CCR4 mRNA levels showed a robust correlation (Figure 1G). Unsupervised hierarchical clustering was performed using previously identified GATA-3 target genes (Figure 1H).11 Two populations were identified; the first of which was enriched for several GATA-3 target genes, including CCR4, and GATA-3 itself (Figure 1I).

Approximately 50% of the patients with T-PLL in our multicenter cohort highly expressed GATA-3. Furthermore, GATA-3 expression was associated with distinct characteristics, including high-risk clinical features (ie, anemia and advanced age) and complex karyotype. These findings suggest that GATA-3–expressing T-PLL may be transcriptionally distinct, consistent with GATA-3’s oncogenic role in other mature T-cell lymphomas. The correlation between GATA-3 and CCR4 expression supports this contention, but also has significant therapeutic implications, as the CCR4-specific monoclonal antibody mogamulizumab is approved for other CCR4-expressing T-cell lymphomas, including those with peripheral blood involvement.

Although our findings are limited by the small sample size, they underscore the importance of biologic stratification in T-PLL. Further studies will be required to determine the extent to which GATA-3 is an oncogenic driver and relevant biomarker in T-PLL. Nonetheless, future studies investigating mogamulizumab in T-PLL are seemingly warranted.

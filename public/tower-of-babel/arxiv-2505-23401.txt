Agent Interpolation for Knowledge
Marta Bílková, Wesley Fussner, Roman Kuznets✉

Abstract
We define a new type of proof formalism for multi-agent modal logics with S5-type modalities. This novel formalism combines the features of hypersequents to represent S5 modalities with nested sequents to represent the T-like modality alternations. We show that the calculus is sound and complete, cut-free, and terminating and yields decidability and the finite model property for multi-agent S5. We also use it to prove the Lyndon (and hence Craig) interpolation property for multi-agent S5, considering not only propositional atoms but also agents to be part of the common language. Finally, we discuss the difficulties on the way to extending these results to the logic of distributed knowledge and to deductive interpolation.

One logic often has multiple proof-theoretic representations. In most cases, it is not reasonable to see one of them as being objectively better than all others in all respects. A Hilbert-style calculus could be good for proving semantical completeness but give little help for establishing decidability. A cut-free sequent calculus works much better for decidability but may be unsuitable to show the Craig interpolation property (
CIP
) even if it does hold (cf., e.g.,
[
5
]
for the Gödel–Dummett logic). If no cut-free calculus exists, an analytic sequent calculus could be one of the simplest alternatives and may even be sufficient for establishing the
CIP
, but the Lyndon interpolation property (
LIP
) would require something else, e.g., hypersequents. One-size-fits-all proof calculi are exceedingly rare.

The choice of calculus is, therefore, dictated, on the one side, by applications and, on the other side, by the range of logics to be applied to. Quite often, the choices can be mapped onto a line between generality and efficiency. The more logics a proof formalism covers, the less likely it is to be efficient for each one of them. Labelled sequents
[
15
]
are on the more general part of the spectrum, with the methods described in
[
16
]
surely sufficient to treat a logic like multi-agent

{\sf S5}_{n}
and extract from it a number of results such as decidability and interpolation for both propositional atoms (for which we obtain Lyndon interpolation) and agents (for which we obtain Craig interpolation). We also discuss interpolation for distributed knowledge and agent interpolation for the global consequence relation, which prove more subtle.

Definition 6
(Saturation)

A formula

\top

•

\neg p\in\Gamma

;

•

\varphi\wedge\psi\in\Gamma

\{\varphi,\psi\}\cap\Gamma\neq\varnothing

•

\varphi\in\Delta
for some component

•

\varphi\in\Delta

\varphi\in\Gamma
, and
\varphi
is present in each
a
-parent,
a
-sibling, and
a

\Gamma
.

\mathcal{S}

Lemma 3

\Gamma_{i-1}

4
Lyndon Interpolation Property for
{\sf S5}_{n}

We now formulate and prove Lyndon interpolation for
\sharp{\sf S5}_{n}

Accordingly, we omit most of the standard proofs regarding the multicomponent extention of Maehara’s interpolation method. Instead, we will focus on the additional task of proving interpolation for the agents/modalities.

Definition 7
(Interpolation Properties)

A logic
{\sf L}
has an

iff the following holds. If

\delta

and

\varphi

[
3
]

]

It directly follows that

In Maehara method

]

\mathcal{S}

(

Each rule
\mathsf{r}
in

5
Towards Distributed Knowledge

In their recent paper
[
14
]
, Murai and Sano developed cut-free sequent calculi for logics of distributed knowledge based on
{\sf K}
,
KT
,
KD
, and
S4
modalities and used these calculi for to prove the
CIP
for both atoms and agents. They left the question of
{\sf S5}
-based distributed knowledge open, suggesting to either use an analytic sequent calculus with cut or to use generalized cut-free sequent calculi akin to the type developed in the current paper. The former method, however is not suitable for proving the
LIP
as the cut rule does not respect the polarities. One of our goals was to use cross-sequents to prove the stronger
ALIP
for
{\sf S5}
-based distributed knowledge.

Unfortunately, the interaction of several distributed modalities within the hierarchy of agent groups does not allow for a direct application of cross-sequents. The natural way to use cross-sequents would be to consider each agent group to have its own modality and supply these modalities with a hierarchy of strength, so that
D_{G}\varphi\to D_{H}\varphi
whenever
G\subseteq H
, where
G
and
H
are groups of agents and
D_{G}
stands for the distributed knowledge of group
G
. Unfortunately, adding these additional connections would violate the termination property we used to prove the completeness theorem.
Consider, e.g.,
\theta\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widehat{D}_{a}(D_{ab}p\vee D_{ac}q)
. Here
a
’s knowledge implies distributed knowledge of both the
ab
group and the
ac
group. That suggests that any any
ab
-cluster and any
ac
-cluster should be contained in some
a
-cluster. Moreover, by transitivity, any chain of
ab
- and
ac
-clusters should fully be within one
a
cluster. Thus, the
\Diamond
-like formula
\theta
should deposit the disjunction
D_{ab}p\vee D_{ac}q
in every component of such a chain. But the
\Box
-like modalities
D_{ab}p
and
D_{ac}q
are prone to initiate the creation of new clusters, which would alternate forever. Because one
\widehat{D}\varphi
formula can now deposit
\varphi
arbitrarily deep (further away from the root) within the cross-sequent, the proof search would not terminate the way it does for
{\sf S5}_{n}
, where
\Diamond_{a}\varphi
can deposit
\varphi
at most one level deeper. The question of interpolation for
{\sf S5}
-based distributed knowledge, thus, remains future research.

Apart from pursuing some variant or elaboration of cross-sequents,
another possible direction could be to use indexed hypersequents from
[
18
]
, which use a similar intuition to cross-sequents but use Aumann structures instead of Kripke models.

It may be worth noting that cross-sequents can be easily amended to handle
{\sf S5}_{n}
with added isolated axioms of the type
\Box_{a}\varphi\to\Box_{b}\varphi
as long as no two such axioms share an agent. Since this extension is not likely to lead to a result on distributed knowledge we chose not to pursue it.

6
Deductive Interpolation

Section

4
establishes
LIP
and, hence,
CIP
for
{\sf S5}_{n}
. For any normal modal logic
{\sf L}
,
CIP
for
{\sf L}
is tantamount to interpolation for its local consequence relation. In this section, we change gears and consider interpolation for the
global
consequence relations of the logics we have previously considered.

The following local deduction theorem gives a crucial link between local and global consequence. It is folklore to a great extent, but a proof may be found, for example, in
[
8
, Theorem 3]
. To state the theorem precisely, we first give a key definition: By a
block
we mean any word over the alphabet
\{\Box_{a}\mid a\in A\}
.

Proposition 1

Let
\Gamma\cup\{\varphi,\psi\}
be any set of formulas and
{\sf L}
be any normal logic. Then
\Gamma,\varphi\vdash_{\sf L}\psi
if and only if there exists an integer
m\geq 0
and blocks
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
such that
\Gamma\vdash_{\sf L}(\varphi\wedge B_{1}\varphi\wedge\ldots\wedge B_{m}\varphi)\to\psi
.

It is well known that local deduction theorems of this kind typically entail that the
CIP
implies the
deductive interpolation property
(
DIP
), but this is only true for
propositional variables
. For agents, the situation is more complicated.

Proposition 2

Suppose
{\sf L}
is any normal normal logic with
CIP
for both propositional variables and agents. Then
\vdash_{\sf L}
has the following restricted form of
DIP
: Whenever
\psi\vdash\varphi
, there exists a formula
\delta
such that
\mathrm{Prop}(\delta)\subseteq\mathrm{Prop}(\psi)\cap\mathrm{Prop}(\varphi)
and
\mathrm{Agt}(\delta)\subseteq\mathrm{Agt}(\psi)
.

Proof

Suppose that
\varphi\vdash\psi
. Applying
Prop.

1
, there exists
n\geq 0
and blocks
B_{1},\ldots,B_{m}
such that
\vdash_{\sf L}(\varphi\wedge B_{1}\varphi\wedge\ldots\wedge B_{m}\varphi)\to\psi
. By
CIP
, we obtain a formula
\delta
such that
\vdash_{\sf L}(\varphi\wedge B_{1}\varphi\wedge\ldots\wedge B_{m}\varphi)\to\delta
,
\vdash_{\sf L}\delta\to\psi
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Prop}(\delta)\subseteq\mathrm{Prop}(\varphi\wedge B_{1}\varphi\wedge\ldots\wedge B_{m}\varphi)\cap\mathrm{Prop}(\psi)=\mathrm{Prop}(\varphi)\cap\mathrm{Prop}(\psi),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Agt}(\delta)\subseteq\mathrm{Agt}(\varphi\wedge B_{1}\varphi\wedge\ldots\wedge B_{m}\varphi)\cap\mathrm{Agt}(\psi)\subseteq\mathrm{Agt}(\psi).

As the foregoing proof illustrates, the introduction of additional agents in the left-to-right direction of the local deduction theorem precludes deriving agent interpolation for the global consequence from agent interpolation for the local consequence by the usual argument.

7
Conclusion and Future Work

We have developed a novel proof formalism for multi-agent
{\sf S5}_{n}
and used it to prove the Lyndon interpolation property for propositional atoms simultaneously with the (Craig) interpolation property for agents. It is interesting to note that proof-theoretic method does not natively support agent interpolation and requires a bit of processing.

Unfortunately, similar to previous attempts, this calculus does not directly lead to distributed knowledge based on
{\sf S5}
. Apart from adapting cross-sequents to distributed knowledge, one can attempt using more general labelled sequents
[
15
,
16
]
, although it is unlikely that termination problem there would be simpler to solve than for the more structured cross-sequents. Perhaps, the interconnected distributed knowledge modalities require their own special sequent structure reflecting their complexity.
Another natural direction of future research is to extend this formalism to weaker extensions of multi-agent
K5
, all of which share the cluster-type semantics with
{\sf S5}
.

Although the focus of this paper has been on interpolation, the proof-theoretic formalism introduced here is also relevant to complexity. This will be the subject of future work.

Olokizumab Improves Patient-Reported Outcomes in Patients with Active Rheumatoid Arthritis up to 106 Weeks (Results from the Open-Label Extension of Phase III Randomized Clinical Trials)
Authors: Roy M. Fleischmann, Vibeke Strand, Eugen Feist, Tatiana Lisitsyna, Jeffrey A. Sparks, Evgeny Nasonov, Mikhail Samsonov, Sergey Grishin, Alina Egorova, Sofiya Kuzkina, Josef S. Smolen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionThis analysis evaluated the effects of olokizumab (OKZ) on patient-reported outcomes (PROs) after up to 106 weeks of treatment in patients with active rheumatoid arthritis (RA) refractory to methotrexate (MTX; MTX-inadequate response [MTX-IR]) or refractory to tumor necrosis factor inhibitor (TNFi; TNFi-inadequate response [TNFi-IR]).MethodsPost-hoc analysis was performed of pooled PROs from phase III, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials (CREDO1/2 MTX-IR; CREDO3 TNFi-IR; NCT02760368/ NCT02760407/NCT03225932) and their open-label extension (OLE; up to 106 weeks) in adults with active RA despite treatment with MTX or TNFi. Patients were treated with OKZ 64 mg every 2 weeks (q2w) or every 4 weeks (q4w) + MTX versus MTX + placebo (weeks 0–24) or active comparator (CREDO2), followed by an OLE (all OKZ). The PROs analyzed were patient global assessment; pain (visual analog scale); Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI); 36-Item Short Form Health Survey (SF-36); Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy (FACIT)-Fatigue (FACIT-F); Work Productivity Survey Rheumatoid Arthritis (WPS-RA) and the European Quality of Life-Five-Dimension Questionnaire (EQ-5D). Changes from baseline at weeks 12, 24 and 106 were analyzed using a mixed model for repeated measures; analyses included percentages of patients reporting improvements ≥ minimal clinically important difference (MCID).ResultsBy week 12, treatment with OKZ compared to placebo (PBO) resulted in greater numerical and clinically meaningfully changes from baseline in all PROs for patients refractory to MTX: the HAQ-DI was − 0.60 for OKZ q2w, − 0.58 for OKZ q4w and − 0.34 for PBO; pain VAS was − 33.26, − 32.51, − 18.39, respectively; and the FACIT-F was 8.47, 8.42 and 4.69, respectively (p < 0.0001). Similar but less prominent improvements were noted in the patients with TNFi-IR. Post hoc analyses demonstrated a higher proportion of patients receiving OKZ reported improvements ≥ MCID versus those receiving PBO (p < 0.05) in all PROs in both groups at week 12. For patients treated with OKZ, the observed improvements from baseline were maintained and increased in magnitude through week 106.ConclusionTreatment with OKZ over 106 weeks resulted in sustained numerically greater and clinically meaningful improvements in PROs compared to baseline in both RA patients with MTX-IR and those with TNFi-IR. These findings support the long-term benefit of OKZ on PROs.Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifiers NCT02760368, NCT02760407, NCT02760433, NCT03120949.

Introduction

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is usually associated with high levels of pain, fatigue, impaired physical function and diminished health-related quality of life (HRQoL) [1]. From the patient perspective, improvements in these outcomes are a priority when RA treatment strategies are evaluated [2].

The European Alliance of Associations for Rheumatology (EULAR) [3], the American College of Rheumatology (ACR) [4] and the Outcomes Measures in Rheumatology (OMERACT) network [5, 6] stated that for a comprehensive assessment of disease progression and the effectiveness of RA treatment, in addition to the medical evaluation of outcomes, it is necessary to consider patient-reported outcomes (PROs). The PROs most frequently used in clinical studies to evaluate the effectiveness of RA therapy include impaired physical function according to the Health Assessment Questionnaire Disability Index (HAQ-DI), pain, general assessment of the patient’s disease activity (patient global assessment of disease activity [PtGA]) and HRQoL measured by the 36-Item Short Form Survey (SF-36) [7–9]. In 2006, the OMERACT network recommended adding the Functional Assessment of Chronic Illness Therapy-Fatigue (FACIT-F) tool to the PROs evaluated in clinical trials [10].

Olokizumab (OKZ) is an interleukin-6 (IL-6) inhibitor approved in several countries for the treatment of RA. OKZ in combination with methotrexate (MTX) was evaluated in the phase 3 CREDO clinical program, which included three double-blind, randomized placebo-controlled trials (RCTs), each lasting 24 weeks, with adalimumab (ADA) as an active control in one of these. In these three RCTs, OKZ was found to have improved the signs and symptoms of RA in patients with active disease refractory to MTX (MTX-inadequate response/MTX-inadequate responders [MTX-IR]) in CREDO1 (ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT02760368) and CREDO2 (NCT02760407) and to have the same effect in RA patients with active disease refractory to TNFi (TNFi-inadequate response/TNFi-inadequate responders [TNFi-IR]) (CREDO3 [NCT02760433]. In these RCTs, OKZ 64 mg every 2 weeks (q2w) or every 4 weeks (q4w) combined with MTX demonstrated clinical superiority to placebo (PBO) and non-inferiority to ADA [11–13]. Most patients who completed the core RCTs continued to receive OKZ therapy in the open-label extension (OLE) CREDO4 study (NCT03120949) [14].

We have previously reported some PROs in the primary publications of the phase 3 trials [11–13] but have not yet reported long-term PRO results. The aim of this analysis was to evaluate the magnitude, rapidity, clinical meaningfulness and durability of PRO improvements with subcutaneous OKZ (64 mg every 2 or 4 weeks) up to 106 weeks in RA patients with MTX-IR and TNFi-IR.

Methods

Patient Population

The 1982 patients analyzed in this post-hoc sub-analysis were enrolled in one of the core phase three RCTs of OKZ, in which 2444 adults with active RA and inadequate response to MTX or TNFi were enrolled. Eligible patients completed the 24-week treatment period, opted to continue OKZ therapy in the OLE and were not treated with ADA during the core study (Fig. 1). Study Design

In the three core RCTs, patients with MTX-IR or TNFi-IR were randomized to receive subcutaneous injections of OKZ q2w, OKZ q4w, PBO (CREDO1, 2, 3) or ADA every 2 weeks (CREDO2), all administered in combination with background MTX therapy. MTX-IR patients who completed the core RCTs and consented to participate in the OLE either continued the same OKZ regimen or were re-randomized from ADA or PBO to one of the two OKZ regimens at week 24. However, TNFi-IR PBO patients from the CREDO3 trial were re-randomized to receive OKZ 64 mg q2w or q4w at week 16. All non-responders (ACR20: ≥ 20% improvement) at week 14 added rescue medication (sulfasalazine and/or hydroxychloroquine) to their continuing assigned study drug treatment. The OLE included 82 weeks of treatment. As patients receiving ADA in CREDO2 were switched to OKZ at week 24, they were excluded from this long-term analysis.

Ethical Approval

The trials were conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and Good Clinical Practice guidelines and approved by the Institutional Review Board or Ethics Committee at each center. “R-Pharm”, as the study sponsor, obtained permission to use all the scales/questionnaires within the studies. All patients provided written informed consent. The list of sites are provided with the primary articles as electronic supplementary material files.

Patient-Reported Outcomes

The PROs included the PtGA and pain assessment (both evaluated using a 0–100 mm visual analog scale [VAS]), the HAQ-DI (score range 0—3, with lower scores indicating better physical function) and the SF-36 version 2. SF-36 consists of eight domains that are calculated into Physical Component Summary (PCS) and Mental Component Summary (MCS) scores. PCS weights five domains (physical functioning [PF], role physical [RP], bodily pain [BP], general health perceptions [GH] and vitality [VT]) positively and the remaining three domains (social functioning [SF], role emotional [RE] and mental health [MH]) negatively. MCS weights VT, SF, RE and MH positively and PF, RP, BP and GH negatively. Each domain is scored from 0 to 100, with higher scores indicating better health status.

Additional PROs included the FACIT-F (score range 0–52; fatigue is indicated if the score is < 34 points) [15], and the RA-specific Work Productivity Survey (WPS-RA), which measures the impact of RA and treatment on productivity both within and outside the home during the previous month [16]. The WPS-RA includes nine questions addressing employment status, productivity in paid and non-paid work and daily activities. One item assesses current labor market participation, which is a strong indicator of work ability. Additional items estimate the extent to which arthritis has interfered with work productivity (paid and non-paid) on scales of 0–10, the number of days during the last month when outside help was required because of arthritis and the number of days in the last month when family, social or leisure activities were missed due to arthritis. The European Quality of Life-Five-Dimension Questionnaire (EQ-5D) was used for health state classification [17].

Statistical Analysis

The statistical analyses were performed using intent-to-treat principles, including all patients randomized in the core studies except those assigned to ADA in CREDO2. We evaluated two cohorts: patients who were only MTX-IR and those who were TNFi-IR and presumably also MTX-IR. For OKZ data from randomized double-blind studies, the results of comparison versus PBO were presented for dosage groups of q2w and q4w. For comparisons between MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups, the results were presented as single OKZ groups combined both every 2 and 4 weeks.

Changes from baseline were analyzed using a mixed model for repeated measures (MMRM). The model for the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR populations incorporated fixed effects for treatment group, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline PRO score, study and country to account for within-subject correlation of repeated measures over time. Results are expressed as least squares means and standard errors.

Patients who discontinued treatment or received rescue medication during the trials were conservatively classified as non-responders. Differences in responder rates between treatment groups were compared using chi-square tests. Responder analyses were estimated proportions of patients achieving predefined minimal clinically important difference (MCID) thresholds for each PRO: ≥ 0.22 and ≥ 0.3 points for HAQ-DI; ≥ 10 mm for PtGA and pain VAS; ≥ 4 points for FACIT-F; and ≥ 5 points for SF-36 summary and domain scores.

As the analysis reported here is a post-hoc analysis, all p-values are nominal and exploratory, not adjusted for multiplicity which increases the risk of type I and should not be interpreted as confirmatory evidence of treatment effects. Comparisons between the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups in magnitude of PRO improvement were conducted for descriptive and hypothesis-generating purposes only, as there were significant differences between these two groups in terms of disease duration, severity and prior treatment exposure. All statistical analyses were performed using R software version 4.0.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

A total of 1982 patients initially allocated to treatment with OKZ or PBO were included in the PRO analysis, among whom 1614 patients were MTX-IR and 368 were TNFi-IR. Of the 1982 patients, 1228 (62.0%) patients completed 106 weeks of treatment.

Most patients in both the MTX-IR (N = 1614) and TNFi-IR (N = 368) groups had high disease activity, impaired physical function (high HAQ-DI) and experienced severe pain and fatigue at baseline. Demographic characteristics were comparable between the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups, with the important exceptions of RA duration, RA activity indices, tender and swollen joint counts (TJC, SJC), frequency of systemic glucocorticoid use, intolerance to ≥ 15 mg/week MTX dose and HAQ-DI and SF-36 scores, which were worse in TNFi-IR patients, indicating more active (or more refractory) RA in this At baseline, according to the WPS-RA, work productivity and household efficiency were low across all patients. In the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups, median proportions of patients with “Work impairment” was high (60.0% and 50.0%, respectively); “Work day productivity reduced by half” was reported by 15.0% and 10.0% of patients, respectively. Additionally, 70.0% of patients in both groups reported “Household impairment” due to RA, and 20.9% and 16.1% of patients in the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups, respectively, reported “Household days productivity reduced by half” (Table 1).

Discussion

Understanding patients’ perceptions of how their disease affects them is crucial for making treatment decisions. The analyses from the three phase 3 OKZ-RCTs and the OLE complementary study previously reported clinical efficacy and safety data [11–14], demonstrating that OKZ in combination with MTX results in clinically important patient-reported improvements in pain, fatigue, physical function, patient global assessment of disease activity and HRQoL. Interpretation of the magnitude and durability of these results requires some caution as these long-term outcomes were derived from an open-label extension study without a concurrent control group and therefore reflect the maintenance of response among patients who remained on treatment rather than definitive evidence of sustained comparative efficacy. In addition, observed differences between the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups should be interpreted with caution, as these populations differed substantially at baseline with respect to disease duration, disease severity and prior biologic exposure. Accordingly, we consider the between-group comparisons to be hypothesis-generating.

Our results demonstrated clinically meaningful improvements over time in all PROs in patients receiving OKZ, regardless of dosage regimen (q2w or q4w), compared with patients receiving PBO in both the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR populations. The improvements in most PROs among OKZ-treated patients exceeded those of the PBO-treated patients by 1.5- to twofold as early as week 12, were sustained and increased through week 106.

Fatigue and pain are thought to be closely pathogenically associated with IL-6 hyperproduction and, consequently, IL-6 inhibitor therapy is among the most effective treatments for reducing their frequency and severity [18]. Our results showed that the percentages of patients reporting improvements ≥ MCID in FACIT-F were higher in the OKZ treatment groups compared to the PBO-treated patients at week 12 and at week 24. In the TNFi-IR population, improvements ≥ MCID at week 12 were also higher in both OKZ groups compared to the PBO-treated patients.

The percentages of patients reporting improvements ≥ MCID in pain severity were higher in the OKZ treatment groups compared to patients receiving the PBO.

While this was not a head-to-head comparison with other inhibitors of the IL-6 pathway and no direct comparisons between tocilizumab (TCZ) and sarilumab (SAR) exist, it is worth mentioning that our data are consistent with findings reported for TCZ [19, 20] and SAR [21, 22] in the MTX-IR population by indirect comparison. In this regard there is consistency with PROs reported for TCZ at week 12 in the BREVACTA RCT [19] and for SAR in the MOBILITY RCT at week 24 [21]. In our trials nominal proportions of patients reporting ≥ MCID improvements (placebo-corrected) at week 12 for HAQ-DI and FACIT-F were comparable to those reported for TCZ [19]. In the MOBILITY trial [21], ≥ MCID improvements were reported as differences between the active product and the PBO; therefore we used this approach for this comparison. At week 24 the proportions of patients reporting benefits ≥ MCID (placebo-corrected) in our trials for HAQ-DI and for FACIT-F are comparable to those reported in the MOBILITY RCT [21]. The impact of IL-6 inhibitors on PROs was more prominent in MTX-IR/conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drug (csDMARD)-IR patients compared to TNFi-IR patients. The differences from those patients receiving PBO were comparable to SAR data from the MOBILITY RCT in MTX-IR patients [21] as they were included in the TNFi-IR populations [22].

By week 12 of OKZ therapy, both MTX-IR and TNFi-IR patients reported clinically meaningful improvements in HRQoL: nominal almost twofold increases in EQ-5D VAS scores compared to the PBO group. Further improvement was reported up to week 106.

Another disease-specific measure included in our trials was the WPS-RA, which demonstrated that OKZ treatment RA improved interferences with work productivity both within and outside the home. These improvements were comparable to those reported with SAR in the TARGET RCT [22]. Such impacts on productivity within the home and participation in other activities are infrequently captured in RA clinical trials. These observations indicate that, in addition to improvements in physical function and HRQoL reported by other measures (e.g. HAQ-DI and SF-36 physical functioning and role physical domains), OKZ produces direct patient-reported improvements in participants in work, family and social/leisure activities.

Strengths and Limitations

The strength and novelty of this report is in providing long-term PRO data up to 106 weeks for RA patients who were inadequate responders to MTX or TNFi and subsequently received a direct IL-6 inhibitor; this has not been reported with other IL-6 inhibitors. Evaluation of multiple validated PRO measures, including work productivity (WPS-RA), offer a comprehensive assessment of OKZ ability to improve PROs in this patient population over this period of time.

There are multiple limitations of these data, including: (1) pooling data from several phase 3 trials with different and distinct patient populations, thereby introducing heterogeneity; (2) patients could ethically receive PBO only during the initial RCT phase for a limited duration, preventing comparative assessments between OKZ and PBO beyond that period; (3) the open-label extension design without a PBO control group in the long-term follow-up raises the regression to the mean, and survivor bias and/or selective discontinuation may contribute to the observed improvements and their duration; (4) as this was a post-hoc analysis, all statistical analyses were exploratory, with nominal p-values unadjusted for multiplicity; as such, they are not intended for confirmatory inference; (5) baseline differences between the MTX-IR and TNFi-IR groups limit the validity of direct between-group comparisons; accordingly, observed PRO differences should be interpreted descriptively and regarded as hypothesis-generating; and 6) the study populations were limited to clinical trial-eligible patients with active RA refractory to MTX or TNFi, potentially restricting generalizability to broader clinical practice including patients with less active disease.

Future well-designed head-to-head trials versus other IL-6 inhibitors (and other b/tsDMARDs) with sufficient number of patients and duration, as well as real-world studies should be conducted to validate these PRO benefits and explore predictors of response specific medications in individual patients, including microRNA analysis.

Conclusions

The results of these analyses demonstrate that OKZ treatment, administered either every 2 weeks or every 4 weeks, was associated with sustained improvements in PROs over 106 weeks in patients with active RA who had inadequate responses to MTX or TNFi. Improvements were reported across multiple domains, including pain, physical function, fatigue and HRQoL; these improvements were more prominent than those in the PBO-treated patients and comparable to those of other IL-6 inhibitors, such as TCZ and SAR. These findings support the long-term patient-centered benefits of OKZ and confirm that targeting IL-6 ligand represents an appropriate and effective management strategy in these patients.

Critical elements in algebras of numerical events
Dietmar Dorninger, Helmut Länger

Abstract
The probability p ⁡ ( s ) p(s) of the occurrence of an event pertaining to a physical system which is observed in different states s s determines a function p p from the set S S of states of the system to [ 0 , 1 ] [0,1] . The function p p is called a numerical event or more precisely an S S -probability. When appropriately structured, sets P P of numerical events form so-called algebras of S S -probabilities, which are orthomodular posets that can serve as quantum logics. If one deals with a classical physical system, then P P will be a Boolean algebra. Starting with a supposed quantum logic or logics obtained by individual measurements, newly added numerical events may turn out to be crucial for the assumption of classicality or non-classicality of the physical system. We will call those numerical events critical and will study their impacts among various classes of algebras of numerical events. Moreover, we will consider the situation of enlarging S S by new states and will describe how elements of an algebra of S S -probabilities contribute to the character of a logic when there exists a certain relation between them and the element that is newly added.

Introduction

In quantum mechanics one can discriminate between classical mechanical phenomena and those that are not classical by means of the underlying quantum logic, which (from an algebraic point of view) will in most cases belong to the class of orthomodular posets. Within this framework classicality can be characterized by the fact that the underlying logic is a Boolean algebra.

Among other possibilities logics can be set up by means of so called numerical events: Let
S
be a set of states of a physical system and
p(s)
be the probability of the occurrence of an event (pertaining to a certain observable) when the system is in state
s\in S
, then the function
p\colon S\to[0,1]
is called a
numerical event
or, more precisely, an
S
-probability
.

Agreeing to use the symbols
0
and
1
as well for the integers
0
and
1
as for the constant functions mapping
S
to
0
and
1
, respectively, we give the following

Definition 1.1
.

(
[

]
and
[

)
A set

-probabilities comprising the constant functions

and

-probabilities or algebra of numerical events, if with

(b)

(c)

We denote algebras of

Regarding notations one has to be aware that the term “algebra of

-probabilities” is somewhat misleading, because an algebra of

-probabilities

S
-probabilities which is a lattice (especially a Boolean algebra) need not be a lattice (Boolean algebra) any more. As usual, embedding an algebra

-probabilities in an algebra

of
S
-probabilities means that

is a subalgebra of

in the aforementioned sense, and embedding an

-probability

into an algebra

Definition 1.2
.

Let
P
be an algebra of
S
-probabilities and

a
(
subsequently observed
)

S
-probability not belonging to

is only symmetric in respect to the assertions “Boolean algebra, then no Boolean algebra” and vice versa, but not as far as details are concerned. Starting from a Boolean algebra,

-probabilities into which

could be embedded. If we start with a non-Boolean algebra of

-probabilities and call

-probabilities into which

can be embedded. Obviously, in this case already

We further mention that within an algebras

Illustrative examples

Example 2.1
.

If one adds some

Example 2.2
.

For

Example 2.3
.

Given the four-element Boolean algebra

Example 2.4
.

Assume

to be the Boolean algebra consisting of

. Further, let

Remark 2.5
.

Let

-probabilities,

3
Extending the set of states

It sometimes happens that a certain algebra of
S
-probabilities
P=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1,+)

, the values of which are not only relevant for all
s\in S

in

-extension

.

P

-probabilities if and only if

is an algebra of
\overline{S}
-probabilities. If

is an algebra of

-probabilities then

is a concrete logic, an orthomodular lattice or a Boolean algebra if and only if

has the same structure.

Proof.

The poset

-probabilities. The only requirement of Definition

\overline{S}
-probabilities. That a

is an orthomodular lattice or a Boolean algebra, because a

∎

Sticking to the above notation we now discuss adding a new element

4
Special elements

Definition 4.1
.

Let
p
and
q
be proper
S
-probabilities.
p
and

are called partially reciprocal, if either
\min\{p(s),q(s)\}\leq 1/2
for all
s\in S
, or else
\max\{p(s),q(s)\}\geq 1/2
for all
s\in S
. In the first case we will call

and

partially reciprocal below
1/2
and in the second case partially reciprocal above
1/2
. If
p
and
q
are both, partially reciprocal below and above
1/2
, then we will call them reciprocal.

The wording “reciprocal” is derived from the property that if
p(s)<1/2
for an
s\in S
then

and if
p(s)>1/2
for an
s\in S
then
q(s)\leq 1/2
, and the same being true with reversed roles of
p
and
q
. Due to the fact that
p
and
q
are interchangeable we will also say that
p
and
q
are reciprocal to each other.

Lemma 4.2
.

If the elements
p
and
q
of an be algebra of
S
-probabilities
P
are partially reciprocal below
1/2
and
u\leq p,q
for some

then
u=0
. If
p
and
q
are partially reciprocal above
1/2
and
v\geq p,q
for some
v\in P
then
v=1
.

Proof.

If
p,q
are partially reciprocal below
1/2
and
u(s)\leq p(s),q(s)
for all
s\in S
, then
u(s)\leq\min\{p(s),q(s)\}\leq 1/2
. This means that
u
is not proper unless it is
0
. Dually we obtain that
v\geq p,q
for elements
p,q
which are partially reciprocal above
1/2
implies
v=1
.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
P
be a Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities and
q
a varying

-probability not belonging to

is critical if there exists some

(i)

and

are partially reciprocal below

(ii)

and

We assume that there exists a Boolean algebra

(i)

If
p

(ii)

p^{\prime}\leq q
, a contradiction to the existence of an
s\in S
with
p^{\prime}(s)>q(s)
which follows from the assumption
p(s)+q(s)<1
.

(iii)

By means of Lemma
4.2
we obtain that
p\wedge q=0
and
p\vee q=1
within
\mathbf{Q}
. Since
q\notin P
this implies that
q
and
p^{\prime}
are two different complements of
p
which contradicts the uniqueness of the complement within a Boolean algebra.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
P
be an algebra of
S
-probabilities that is not a Boolean algebra and
q\notin P
. Then
q
is not critical if

(i)

there exists some
p\in P
such that
p,q,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
are pairwise different and pairwise reciprocal, and
q
is arbitrarily chosen,

(ii)

there exist
p_{1},p_{2}\in P
such that
p_{1},p_{2},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}
are pairwise different and pairwise reciprocal, and
q
arbitrarily chosen,

(iii)

there exist
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3},p_{4}\in P
pairwise partially reciprocal below
1/2
such that
p_{1}\perp p_{2}
,
p_{3}\perp p_{4}
and
p_{1}+p_{2}=p_{3}+p_{4}
, and
q
arbitrarily chosen,

(iv)

there exist
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}\in P
such that together with
p_{4}:=q
case
(iii)
applies.

Proof.

Assume
\mathbf{Q}
to be a Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities into which
P\cup\{q\}
can be embedded.

(i)

Lemma
4.2
implies that within
Q
we have
p\vee q=p\vee q^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\vee q=p^{\prime}\vee q^{\prime}=p\vee p^{\prime}=q\vee q^{\prime}=1
and
p\wedge q=p\wedge q^{\prime}=p^{\prime}\wedge q=p^{\prime}\wedge q^{\prime}=p\wedge p^{\prime}=q\wedge q^{\prime}=0
from which we infer that
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
(for its definition see above) is a subalgebra of
\mathbf{Q}
, a contradiction since
\mathbf{Q}
is assumed to be a Boolean algebra.

(ii)

We observe that if
P\cup\{q\}
can be embedded into a Boolean algebra
\mathbf{Q}
, then already
P
can be embedded into a Boolean algebra and within this Boolean algebra
p_{1},p_{2},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime}
take on the role of
p,q,p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
in case (i).

(iii)

Like before we can provide a proof within
P
and assume
\mathbf{Q}
to be a Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities into which
P
is embedded. By Lemma
4.2

p_{i}\wedge p_{j}=0
for
i\neq j
and
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. Since
p_{i},p_{j}
for
i\neq j
are partially reciprocal below
1/2
,
p_{i}^{\prime},p_{j}^{\prime}
are partially reciprocal above
1/2
and hence again by Lemma
4.2

p_{i}^{\prime}\vee p_{j}^{\prime}=1
for
i\neq j
and
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. From the assumption
p_{1}+p_{2}=p_{3}+p_{4}=:e
we can infer that
p_{1}\vee p_{2}=p_{3}\vee p_{4}=e
. We will further show that
e_{1}:=p_{1}\vee p_{3}
,
e_{2}:=p_{1}\vee p_{4}
,
e_{3}:=p_{2}\vee p_{3}
and
e_{4}:=p_{2}\vee p_{4}
are all equal to
e
. For this end we first we observe that
p_{i}\leq e
for
i=1,2,3,4
. Then we deduce

e_{1}=e_{1}\vee(p_{2}\wedge p_{4})=(p_{1}\vee p_{3}\vee p_{2})\wedge(p_{1}\vee p_{3}\vee p_{4})=(p_{3}\vee e)\wedge(p_{1}\vee e)=e
.

In the same manner considering for
i=2,3,4

e_{i}\wedge(p_{j}\wedge p_{k})
with
j\neq k
and
\{j,k\}
complementary to the two indices of the elements
p
constituting
e_{i}
we obtain
e_{i}=e
. It then follows that
p_{i}^{\prime}\wedge p_{j}^{\prime}=e^{\prime}
for
i\neq j
and
i,j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
. Moreover,
p_{1}\leq p_{2}^{\prime}
,
p_{2}\leq p_{1}^{\prime}
,
p_{3}\leq p_{4}^{\prime}
,
p_{4}\leq p_{3}^{\prime}
,
e=p_{1}\vee p_{2}\vee p_{3}\vee p_{4}\nleq p_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,2,3,4
and
e^{\prime}=p_{1}^{\prime}\wedge p_{2}^{\prime}\wedge p_{3}^{\prime}\wedge p_{4}^{\prime}\ngeq p_{i}
. This way we have come upon a subalgebra of
\mathbf{Q}
which is isomorphic to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}\times\mathbf{P}_{2}
(
\mathbf{P}_{2}
denoting the two-element Boolean algebra), again a contradiction to
\mathbf{Q}
being a Boolean algebra.

(iv)

We assume
\mathbf{Q}
to comprise
P\cup\{q\}
and have the same arguments for a proof like in case (iii).

∎

5
Special algebras of numerical events

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
P\subseteq[0,1]^{S}
. Then
P
is an algebra of
S
-probabilities that is a
2^{n}
-element Boolean algebra if and only if there exist varying
S
-probabilities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
satisfying
p_{N}=1
and
P=\{p_{I}\mid I\subseteq N\}
where
N:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
p_{I}:=\sum\limits_{i\in I}p_{i}
for all
I\subseteq N
. In this case the mapping
I\mapsto p_{I}
from
2^{N}
to
P
is an isomorphism from the Boolean algebra
(2^{N},\subseteq,{}^{\prime},\emptyset,N)
to
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
where
I^{\prime}:=N\setminus I
for all
I\subseteq N
. Moreover,
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
are the atoms of
(P,\leq)
.

Proof.

First assume
P
to be an algebra of
S
-probabilities that is a
2^{n}
-element Boolean algebra. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
denote the atoms of
(P,\leq)
. Then
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
are varying and pairwise orthogonal,
p_{N}=\bigvee\limits_{i\in N}p_{i}=1
and
P=\{\bigvee\limits_{i\in I}p_{i}\mid I\subseteq N\}=\{p_{I}\mid I\subseteq N\}
. Conversely, assume
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in P
to be varying and satisfying
p_{N}=1
and
P=\{p_{I}\mid I\subseteq N\}
. Then conditions (a) and (b) of Definition
1.1
are satisfied. Now let
I,J,K\subseteq N
. If
I\cap J=\emptyset
then
p_{I}\perp p_{J}
. If, conversely,
p_{I}\perp p_{J}
and
I\cap J\neq\emptyset
then there exists some
i\in I\cap J
and hence
2p_{i}\leq p_{I}+p_{J}\leq 1
contradicting the fact that
p_{i}
is varying. Therefore
I\cap J=\emptyset
if and only if
p_{I}\perp p_{J}
. If
p_{I},p_{J},p_{K}
are pairwise orthogonal then
I,J,K
are pairwise disjoint and hence
p_{I}+p_{J}+p_{K}=p_{I\cup J\cup k}\in P
proving condition (c) of Definition
1.1
. This shows that
P
is an algebra of
S
-probabilities. We have
p_{I^{\prime}}=1-p_{I}=(p_{I})^{\prime}
and hence the following are equivalent:
I\subseteq J
,
I\cap J^{\prime}=\emptyset
,
p_{I}\perp p_{J^{\prime}}
,
p_{I}\perp(p_{J})^{\prime}
,
p_{I}\leq p_{J}
. Finally,
p_{\emptyset}=0
and
p_{N}=1
. Altogether we see that the mapping
I\mapsto p_{I}
from
2^{N}
to
P
is an isomorphism from the
2^{n}
-element Boolean algebra
(2^{N},\subseteq,{}^{\prime},\emptyset,N)
to
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
and hence the latter is a
2^{n}
-element Boolean algebra, too.
∎

The following theorem generalizes Example
2.3
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
, let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
be varying
S
-probabilities satisfying
p_{N}=1
where
N:=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
p_{I}:=\sum\limits_{i\in I}p_{i}
for all
I\subseteq N
, let
P
denote the algebra
\{p_{I}\mid I\subseteq N\}
of
S
-probabilities that is a
2^{n}
-element Boolean algebra and let
q
be a varying
S
-probability satisfying
q<p_{1}
such that
p_{1}-q
is proper. Then
q\notin P
and
q
is not critical.

Proof.

Put
q_{0}:=q
,
q_{1}:=p_{1}-q
and
q_{i}:=p_{i}
for all
i=2,\ldots,n
. Then
q_{0},\ldots,q_{n}
are varying
S
-probabilities satisfying
q_{N_{0}}=1
where
N_{0}:=\{0,\ldots,n\}
and
q_{I}:=\sum\limits_{i\in I}q_{i}
for all
I\subseteq N_{0}
. According to Lemma
5.1
,
Q:=\{q_{I}\mid I\subseteq N_{0}\}
is an algebra of
S
-probabilities that is a
2^{n+1}
-element Boolean algebra including
P\cup\{q\}
. Moreover,
P=\{q_{I}\mid I\subseteq N_{0},\{0,1\}\subseteq I\mbox{ or }\{0,1\}\cap I=\emptyset\}
and hence
q=q_{\{0\}}\notin P
.
∎

An algebra of
S
-probabilities that is isomorphic as a lattice to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
(or more general, to
\mathbf{MO_{n}}
for
n>2
) is a logic that frequently turns up in connection with physical experiments, e.g. with experiments concerning the spin of electrons. (
\mathbf{MO_{n}}
denotes the ortholattice of length two with
2n
pairwise incomparable elements
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,p_{n}^{\prime}
strictly between
0
and
1
.)

In the following, we will take a closer look at the algebra of
S
-probabilities isomorphic as a lattice to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
. For short we will refer to this algebra of
S
-probabilities just as
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
.

As commonly used with posets we will write
a\parallel b
if two elements
a,b
of a poset are not comparable.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
P
be an algebra
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
of
S
-probabilities,
g,h\in P
with
g\parallel h
and
|S|\leq 3
. Then there does not exist an
S
-probability
q\notin P
such that
0<q<g<v<1
and
0<q<h<v<1
within a Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities which includes
P\cup\{q\}
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists some Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities satisfying the mentioned property.

If
\min(g,h)\leq 1/2
then
g\wedge h=0
contradicting
q>0
.

If
\min(g,h^{\prime})\leq 1/2
then
g\wedge h^{\prime}=0
whence

g\leq h\vee g=(h\vee g)\wedge 1=(h\vee g)\wedge(h\vee h^{\prime})=h\vee(g\wedge h^{\prime})=h

contradicting
g\parallel h
.

Now we distinguish three cases.

1)
|S|=1
.

In this case there does nor exist an algebra of
S
-probabilities which is isomorphic to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}
.

2)
|S|=2
, say
S=\{s_{1},s_{2}\}
.

W.l.o.g. we may assume
g(s_{1})\leq 1/2
and
g(s_{2})\geq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\leq 1/2
and
h(s_{2})\geq 1/2
then
\min(g,h^{\prime})\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\geq 1/2
and
h(s_{2})\leq 1/2
then
\min(g,h)\leq 1/2
.

3)
|S|=2
, say
S=\{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3}\}
.

W.l.o.g. we may assume
g(s_{1})\leq 1/2
,
g(s_{2})\leq 1/2
and
g(s_{3})\geq 1/2
. (In the case
g(s_{1})\geq 1/2
,
g(s_{2})\geq 1/2
and
g(s_{3})\leq 1/2
the situation is dual.)

If
h(s_{1})\leq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\leq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\geq 1/2
then
\min(g,h^{\prime})\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\leq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\geq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\leq 1/2
then
\min(g,h)\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\leq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\geq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\geq 1/2
then
\min(g,h^{\prime})\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\geq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\leq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\leq 1/2
then
\min(g,h)\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\geq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\leq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\geq 1/2
then
\min(g,h^{\prime})\leq 1/2
.

If
h(s_{1})\geq 1/2
,
h(s_{2})\geq 1/2
and
h(s_{3})\leq 1/2
then
\min(g,h)\leq 1/2
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
P
be an algebra of
S
-probabilities being isomorphic to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}=\{0,p_{1},

p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2},p_{2}^{\prime},1\}
and
q\notin P
an
S
-probability with
0<q<p_{1},p_{2}
and
\mathbf{Q}
a Boolean algebra of
S
-probabilities including
P\cup\{q\}
such that
p_{1}\parallel p_{2}
in
\mathbf{Q}
and
p_{1}\vee p_{2}<1
. Then
|S|\geq 4
.

Proof.

in
\mathbf{Q}
we have
0<q<p_{1}<p_{1}\vee p_{2}<1
and
0<q<p_{2}<p_{1}\vee p_{2}<1
which by Lemma
5.3
can only occur in case
|S|\geq 4
.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
P
be an algebra of
S
-probabilities isomorphic to
\mathbf{MO_{2}}=\{0,p_{1},p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2},

p_{2}^{\prime},1\}
and
q
a varying
S
-probability not belonging to
P
and assume
p_{1}-q
,
p_{2}-q
and
1+q-p_{1}-p_{2}
to be varying
S
-probabilities. Then

can be embedded into a Boolean algebra, i.e.,

Since
q
,
p_{1}-q
,
p_{2}-q
and

.

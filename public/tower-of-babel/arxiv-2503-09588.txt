Relative Untwisted Outer Space for Right-Angled Artin Groups
Adrien Abgrall

Abstract
. For 𝒢 = { G i } \mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\} , ℋ = { H j } \mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\} two finite collections of finitely generated subgroups of a right-angled Artin group A Γ A_{\Gamma} , the untwisted McCool group 𝒰 ⁡ ( A Γ , 𝒢 , ℋ t ) \mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t}) is the subgroup of untwisted outer automorphisms of A Γ A_{\Gamma} preserving the conjugacy class of each G i G_{i} and acting trivially up to conjugacy on each H j H_{j} . We prove that when the G i G_{i} are standard subgroups of A Γ A_{\Gamma} , 𝒰 ⁡ ( A Γ , 𝒢 , ℋ t ) \mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t}) acts properly cocompactly on a finite-dimensional subcomplex of the spine of untwisted outer space for A Γ A_{\Gamma} , providing a geometric model for this group and proof that it is of type VF.



1.
Introduction

A right-angled Artin group is a group
A_{\Gamma}
with a fixed finite
standard generating set

V
, where the only relations come from commutations of some pairs of standard generators, encoded by a graph
\Gamma
with vertex set
V
. Examples include finite-rank free groups
F_{n}
and free abelian groups
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Many properties of right-angled Artin groups are reminiscent of one class or the other. A finite generating set for the automorphism group
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
was found by Laurence (
[
26
]
) and Servatius (
[
29
]
). This set closely resembles the Nielsen generators for
\mathsf{Aut}(F_{n})
, but includes some automorphisms called
twists
whose behaviour is similar to transvections in

SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})=\mathsf{Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
. This observation warranted the definition of the
untwisted subgroup

\mathcal{U}\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
of
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
, generated by all the Laurence-Servatius generators except the twists. It appears sometimes in the literature as the subgroup generated by
long-range automorphisms
. Note that, while the definition of the untwisted subgroup might seem somewhat arbitrary, Fioravanti (
[
14
]
) characterized it as the group of automorphisms of
A_{\Gamma}
that coarsely preserve a natural median structure, providing some geometric significance to this subgroup. Moreover, if for all
v\neq w\in V
, there is no inclusion between balls of radius
1
around
v
and
w
in
\Gamma
, then no twist can exist. In that case, the untwisted subgroup is
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
itself. Because of the similarities in their generating sets, the untwisted subgroup
\mathcal{U}\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
, and its outer automorphism image
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})\leq\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
are expected to share many properties with
\mathsf{Aut}(F_{n})
and
\mathsf{Out}(F_{n})
respectively.

The
spine of outer space
for the free group
F_{n}
is a simplicial complex constructed by Culler and Vogtmann (
[
10
]
). It is contractible and admits a properly discontinuous and cocompact action of
\mathsf{Out}(F_{n})
by simplicial isometries. Closely related is the
spine of auter space
for
F_{n}
, constructed by Hatcher and Vogtmann (
[
23
]
), having an action of
\mathsf{Aut}(F_{n})
with analogous properties. Recently, Charney, Stambaugh, and Vogtmann constructed in
[
6
]
a
spine of untwisted outer space

K_{\Gamma}
for
A_{\Gamma}
, having an action of the group of untwisted outer automorphisms
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
with analogous properties again. This construction provided a geometrically explicit proof of the fact
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
is of
type VF
, namely that a torsion-free subgroup of finite index acts freely and cocompactly on
K_{\Gamma}
, a finite-dimensional contractible complex, with many homological consequences. In particular, it recovered the fact that
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
is finitely presented, which was known from an earlier work of Day (
[
11
]
, Proposition 5.4). In
[
1
]
, we gave a new construction of the spine
K_{\Gamma}
: vertices of
K_{\Gamma}
are certain locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes called
spatial cube complexes
, endowed with
untwisted markings
identifying their fundamental group with
A_{\Gamma}
, considered modulo marking-preserving combinatorial isomorphism. Edges of
K_{\Gamma}
correspond to marking-preserving
hyperplane collapses
between spatial cube complexes. So far, no analogue to the spine of auter space was known for
A_{\Gamma}
. Even more recently, Bregman, Charney, and Vogtmann in
[
3
]
modified the construction of
K_{\Gamma}
into a (significantly more complex)
outer space for
A_{\Gamma}
, i.e. a contractible space with a properly discontinuous action of the whole
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
.

The group
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
and its subgroup
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
act on the set of conjugacy classes of elements of
A_{\Gamma}
. The intent of this article is to obtain a geometric understanding of the stabilizer in
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
of a finite array of such conjugacy classes. Examples of such stabilizers are many, but include notably, when
A_{\Gamma}
is free, pure (extended) mapping class groups of finite-type non-compact surfaces, and for a general
A_{\Gamma}
, the subgroup of
outer conjugating automorphisms
, which are the outer automorphisms preserving the conjugacy class of every standard generator (see Example
6.2
). We obtain the following theorem.

Theorem
6.1

(special case)
.

Let
[h_{1}],\dots,[h_{n}]
be finitely many conjugacy classes of elements of
A_{\Gamma}
. There exists a contractible subcomplex of the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
on which the untwisted stabilizer
Stab([h_{1}],\dots,[h_{n}])\cap\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

To the best of our knowledge, the question remains unanswered when twists are allowed. Stabilizers in
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
have proved more convenient to grasp than their counterparts in
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
due to the combinatorial nature of the spine of untwisted outer space. The question had been previously solved when
A_{\Gamma}
is free: a theorem of McCool (
[
27
]
) proves finite presentation of the stabilizer, a result of Guirardel and Levitt (
[
18
]
) proves that it has type VF, and a recent work of Bestvina, Feighn and Handel (
[
2
]
) produces a contractible subcomplex of Culler-Vogtmann’s outer space with a cocompact action (see below). Their algorithmic proof relates to an algorithm announced by Gersten (
[
16
]
) and verified by Kalajdžievski (
[
25
]
). Very recently, this special case has been obtained independently by Corrigan (
[
9
]
).

The results of Guirardel and Levitt, and of Bestvina, Feighn and Handel are even more general and invite to consider stabilizers not only of conjugacy classes of elements but also of conjugacy classes of subgroups. The relevant notion of a
McCool group
, a subgroup of an outer automorphism group was introduced by Guirardel and Levitt.

Definition 1.1

(
[
19
]
)
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{i}\},\mathcal{H}=\{H_{j}\}
two collections of subgroups of
G
. The
McCool group

\mathsf{Out}(G;\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})\leq\mathsf{Out}(G)
is the subgroup of outer automorphisms that preserve the conjugacy class of each
G_{i}
and are inner on each
H_{j}
(i.e. for every
j
, some representative coincides with the identity on
H_{j}
).

When
G=A_{\Gamma}
is a right-angled Artin group, we denote
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
the intersection
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})\cap\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
.

With this notation, the full generality of the result of Bestvina, Feighn and Handel is the following.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

For
\mathcal{G}
a finite collection of finitely generated subgroups,
\mathsf{Out}(F_{n};\mathcal{G},\varnothing)
acts properly and cocompactly on a subcomplex of the spine of Culler-Vogtmann’s outer space for
F_{n}
. In particular, this group is of type VF.

Our main theorem adapts this result to untwisted McCool subgroups of for right-angled Artin groups, with finitely many fixed (conjugacy classes of) finitely generated subgroups, and stabilized
standard subgroups
(i.e. subgroups generated by a subset of the standard generators).

Theorem
8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a collection of standard subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
and let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite collection of arbitrary finitely generated subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. There exists a contractible subcomplex of the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

The special case above corresponds to the case where
\mathcal{G}=\varnothing
and
\mathcal{H}
is made of cyclic subgroups. Passing from these cyclic subgroups to finitely generated ones (Section
8
) relies on a purely geometric translation length argument, developped in Section
3
. However, stabilization of standard subgroups requires much care, and is in fact the most technical part of this article (Section
5
).

Previous work of Day and Wade (
[
12
]
) had established type VF for
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
(not the untwisted version) but only under the extra assumption that every subgroup of
\mathcal{H}
is standard as well. Moreover their proof is algebraic in nature and does not provide explicitly a geometric model for the group.

We make the observation that the untwisted automorphism group
\mathcal{U}\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
is isomorphic to the untwisted McCool group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma}*\mathbb{Z},\{A_{\Gamma}\},\{\mathbb{Z}\}^{t})
, and obtain as a direct corollary a
spine of untwisted auter space for
A_{\Gamma}
:

Corollary
6.3
.

There exists a contractible simplicial complex
L_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

In Section
2
, we recall all the needed concepts from the theory of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, right-angled Artin groups, hyperplane collapses and untwisted outer space. We prove a relative version of Droms’ theorem on isomorphisms of right-angled Artin groups (Lemma
2.10
) and a generation result for certain subgroups of the untwisted outer automorphism groups (Proposition
2.7
).

In Section
3
, we provide results on the behavior of minsets of isometries acting cospecially on
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, and minimal invariant subcomplexes for groups acting on such complexes.

In Section
4
, we give two preliminary results about how hyperplane collapses behave on locally convex subcomplexes.

In Section
5
, we describe the spine of untwisted outer space relative to a collection
\mathcal{G}
of standard subgroups as a subspace
K^{\mathcal{G}}
of
K_{\Gamma}
and prove several properties of
K^{\mathcal{G}}
.

In Section
6
, we introduce a further subspace
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}}
of
K_{\Gamma}
associated to a family
\mathcal{G}
of stabilized standard subgroups and a family
\mathcal{H}
of fixed arbitrary cyclic subgroups. The definition is close to the work of Bestvina, Feighn and Handel, but some new arguments are required.

In Section
7
, we prove that
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}}
is contractible, finishing the proof of Theorem
6.1
. This proof of contractibility mirrors the proof of
[
6
]
, however, more arguments are needed at each step to account for the new fixed subgroups.

Finally, in Section
8
, we use a short geometric argument to obtain the more general Theorem
8.2
, and conjecture further generalizations.

Background

2.1.
CAT(0) cube complexes

The study of
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes started with Gromov (
[
17
]
) and Sageev (
[
28
]
). Further work is due to Haglund and Paulin (
[
20
]
) from the viewpoint of
wallspaces
, and to Chepoi (
[
8
]
) from the viewpoint of
median graphs
. We will mainly use the same background results given in
[
1
]
, Section 2.1 and refer the reader to them first. We use the same notation
\mu\colon X^{(0)}\times X^{(0)}\times X^{(0)}\rightarrow X^{(0)}
for the
median
defined on the
0
-skeleton of a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
. Recall that when
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, a subcomplex
A
of
X
is
convex
when
A
is full and geodesic edge paths in
X
joining vertices of
A
are fully contained in
A
. When
X
is locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
, a subcomplex of
X
is
locally convex
if any of its preimages in the universal cover of
X
is convex. We also define
hyperplanes
in locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes as equivalence classes of edges under parallelism. Hyperplanes are
transverse
when they have dual edges spanning a square: a hyperplane might be transverse to itself. When
X
is
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
and
A,B
are two subsets of
X
, we write
\mathrm{Sep}(A\mid B)
to denote the set of hyperplanes of
X
separating
A
from
B
. We quote the following classical result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex, and
A\subseteq X
a full subcomplex. The following are equivalent:

(1)

A
is convex

(2)

\mu(A^{(0)},A^{(0)},X^{(0)})\subseteq A^{(0)}

(3)

A
is
gated
: for every
x\in X^{(0)}
there exists
\pi_{A}(x)\in A^{(0)}
such that for every
a\in A^{(0)}
,
d(x,a)=d(x,\pi_{A}(x))+d(\pi_{A}(x),a)
, i.e.
\pi_{A}(x)
lies on some gedoesic edge path joining
x
and
a
.

(4)

A
is an intersection of halfspaces

Moreover, when they hold,
\pi_{A}(x)
is unique and called the
projection of
x
onto
A
.

Proof.

(1.)\Rightarrow(2.)
: If
a,b\in A^{(0)},x\in X^{(0)}
then
\mu(a,b,x)
belongs to a geodesic joining
a
and
b
.

(2.)\Rightarrow(3.)
: Let
x\in X
be a vertex and set
\pi_{A}(x)
to be any vertex in
A
at minimal distance from
x
. Let
a\in A
be an arbitrary vertex. The median
\mu(x,\pi_{A}(x),a)
belongs to
A
by assumption, and to a geodesic edge path joining
x
and
\pi_{A}(x)
by definition. Therefore it is equal to
\pi_{A}(x)
by minimality. Thus
\pi_{A}(x)
lies on some geodesic edge path joining
x
and
a
.

(3.)\Rightarrow(4.)
: Let
B\supseteq A
be the intersection of all the halfpaces of
X
that contain
A
entirely. Assume
B\setminus A
contains a vertex
x
. Since
x\notin A
,
d(\pi_{A}(x),x)>0
, hence there exists
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid\pi_{A}(x))
. Because
H
separates two points of
B
, it must separate two points of
A
, hence the existence of
a\in A
such that
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x,a\mid\pi_{A}(x))
. Thus, a geodesic joining
x
and
a
through
\pi_{A}(x)
crosses
H
twice, a contradiction. Therefore, every vertex of
B
is contained in
A
, which is full, thus
A
is equal to
B
, which is an intersection of halfspaces.

(4.)\Rightarrow(1.)
is clear because halfspaces are convex.

For the uniqueness of the projection, any vertex
p
with the same property lies on a geodesic joining
x
and
\pi_{A}(x)
, but cannot be closer to
x
by minimality of
d(x,\pi_{A}(x))
. Hence
p=\pi_{A}(x)
.
∎

2.2.
Right-angled Artin groups and their autmorphisms

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a non-empty finite simple graph fixed for the rest of this article. The following definition gathers most of the tools we will need concerning right-angled Artin groups. A more thorough introduction appears in
[
5
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
right-angled Artin group

A_{\Gamma}
associated with
\Gamma
is the group given by the following presentation:

A_{\Gamma}=\left\langle V\mid[v_{i},v_{j}]\;\forall\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E\right\rangle

Elements of
V\subset A_{\Gamma}
are the
standard generators
. The
Salvetti complex

\mathbb{S}
associated with
A_{\Gamma}
is the subcomplex of the torus
(S^{1})^{V}
whose cubes correspond one-to-one to the cliques of
\Gamma
. Its fundamental group canonically identifies with
A_{\Gamma}
. Moreover,
\mathbb{S}
is a special (see
[
22
]
), locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex with a single vertex. Conversely every special cube complex with a single vertex and fundamental group isomorphic to
A_{\Gamma}
is combinatorially isomorphic to
\mathbb{S}
. The hyperplanes of
\mathbb{S}
are in one-to-one correspondence with
V
, and two hyperplanes are transverse if and only if their corresponding vertices are adjacent. Let
*
denote the unique vertex of
\mathbb{S}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
the universal cover of
\mathbb{S}
with the pulled back labeling of hyperplanes by
V
. It is a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex with a natural free cocompact cospecial action of
A_{\Gamma}
. In particular,
\mathbb{S}
is a classifying space for
A_{\Gamma}
. Since
\mathbb{S}
is compact,
A_{\Gamma}
is torsion-free.

Let
V^{\pm}=V\sqcup V^{-1}
denote the set of standard generators and their inverses. For
x\in V^{\pm}
, the
link of
x
is the set
lk(x)
of elements of
V^{\pm}\setminus\{x,x^{-1}\}
commuting with
x
. When
x\in V
, these are exactly the standard generators at distance
1
from
x
in
\Gamma
and their inverses, i.e. the labels of the edges spanning a square with the edge labeled
x
in
\mathbb{S}
. A
standard
(sometimes also called
special
)
subgroup
is a subgroup of
A_{\Gamma}
generated by some subset
W\subseteq V
. Considering the full subgraph
\Delta
of
\Gamma
induced by
W
, its Salvetti complex
\mathbb{S}_{\Delta}
associated with
A_{\Delta}
embeds as a locally convex subcomplex of
\mathbb{S}
, and every locally convex subcomplex of
\mathbb{S}
is of this form. In particular, the inclusion
\mathbb{S}_{\Delta}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\Gamma}
is
\pi_{1}
-injective, so
A_{\Delta}=\pi_{1}\mathbb{S}_{\Delta}
embeds in
\pi_{1}\mathbb{S}
as the subgroup generated by
W
: standard subgroups are right-angled Artin groups themselves. The center of
A_{\Gamma}
coincides with the standard subgroup
A_{Z}
generated by the set
Z
of all
v\in V
such that
lk(v)\cup\{v,v^{-1}\}=V
. The group of inner automorphisms
\mathsf{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
identifies with
A_{\Gamma}/A_{Z}\simeq A_{\Gamma\setminus Z}
.

A word in the standard generators and their inverses is
reduced
when it is of minimal length among all words representing the same element of
A_{\Gamma}
. It is
cyclically reduced
when it is of minimal length among all words representing an element of the same conjugacy class. Any word can be turned into any reduced word representing the same element by a sequence of exchanges of adjacent letters representing commuting elements in
V^{\pm}
and deletions of two-letter subwords of the form
vv^{-1}
or
v^{-1}v
(
[
24
]
, Proposition 3.2). Additionally, any two reduced words representing the same element differ by a sequence of exchanges of adjacent commuting letters.

We say that a standard generator
v\in V

appears
in a word if
v
or
v^{-1}
is a (possibly repeated) letter of that word. Since reductions do not introduce new letters, if
W\subset V
, and
w
is a reduced word in
V
that represents an element of the standard subgroup generated by
W
, all the standard generators appearing in
w
belong to
W
.

Finally, when
A_{\Gamma}
acts on some metric space
X
by isometries, for
g\in A_{\Gamma}
let
\ell_{X}(g)=\inf_{x\in X}d_{X}(x,gx)
be the
translation length of
g
in
X
. It depends only on the conjugacy class of
g
. When
X
is simply connected and the action is properly discontinuous and cocompact, the isomorphism
A_{\Gamma}\simeq\pi_{1}(X/A_{\Gamma})
realizes the translation length of
g
as the infimum of lengths of loops in the free homotopy class representing the conjugacy class of
g
. In particular, when
X=\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
with the combinatorial metric, the quantity
\ell(g)\coloneqq\ell_{\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}}(g)
is the word length of any cyclically reduced word in the standard generators representing the conjugacy class of
g
.

The following definition, following
[
6
]
, singles out some particular automorphisms of
A_{\Gamma}
.

Definition 2.3
.

We consider partitions
\mathbf{P}=(P,P^{*},L)
of
V^{\pm}
into three parts:
V^{\pm}=P\sqcup P^{*}\sqcup L
, where only
L
might be empty.
L
, is the
link
of
\mathbf{P}
, and
P,P^{*}
are the
sides
of
\mathbf{P}
. We denote:

\displaystyle lk(\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=L

\displaystyle single(\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x\in P,\,x^{-1}\in P^{*}\text{ or }x\in P^{*},\,x^{-1}\in P\}

\displaystyle double(P)

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x,x^{-1}\in P\}

\displaystyle double(P^{*})

\displaystyle=\{x\in V^{\pm}\mid x,x^{-1}\in P^{*}\}

A
based Whitehead partition

(\mathbf{P},b)
is the data of a partition
\mathbf{P}
as above and a
basepoint

b\in V^{\pm}
, such that:

•

b\in P
,
b^{-1}\in P^{*}
, and
L=lk(b)
.

•

If
x\in P
and
x^{-1}\in P^{*}
,
lk(x)\subseteq L
(we say that
\mathbf{P}

splits

x
).

•

If
x\in P
and
y\in P^{*}
are not inverses,
x
and
y
do not commute.

•

P
and
P^{*}
both contain at least two elements.

A
Whitehead partition
is a partition
\mathbf{P}
such that there exists
b\in V^{\pm}
making
(\mathbf{P},b)
a based Whitehead partition. Whitehead partitions often have several basepoints. A Whitehead partition is entirely determined by any basepoint and one side.

Given
(\mathbf{P},b)
a based Whitehead partition, the corresponding
Whitehead automorphism
is defined on standard generators as follows:

v\mapstochar\rightarrow\begin{cases}v^{-1}&\text{ if }v\in\{b,b^{-1}\}\\
vb^{-1}&\text{ if }v\in single(\mathbf{P})\cap(P\setminus\{b\})\\
bv&\text{ if }v\in single(\mathbf{P})\cap(P^{*}\setminus\{b^{-1}\})\\
bvb^{-1}&\text{ if }v\in double(P)\\
v&\text{ if }v\in double(P^{*})\cup L\end{cases}

While one often finds
b\mapstochar\rightarrow b
in the literature, we adopt here the convention of
[
6
]
which has the advantages of making the automorphism involutive and having a convenient topological realization.

We recall the following names for some other automorphisms of
A_{\Gamma}
:

Definition 2.4
.

An automorphism
\varphi
of
A_{\Gamma}
is called:

•

an
oriented graph permutation
if
\varphi(V^{\pm})=V^{\pm}
. An oriented graph permutation is an
inversion
if it maps one standard generator to its inverse and fixes all the others.

•

a (dominated right-)
transvection
if for some
v_{1}\neq v_{2}\in V
,
\varphi(v_{1})=v_{1}v_{2}
and
\varphi
fixes every standard generator different from
v_{1}
. More precisely it is a
twist
if
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
commute and a
fold
otherwise. Note that every standard generator in
lk(v_{1})
needs to centralize
v_{2}
for this automorphism to be well-defined.

•

a(n extended)
partial conjugation
if there exists some
v\in V
such that for every
w\in V
,
\varphi(w)\in\{w,\,vwv^{-1}\}
. Note that any two standard generators commuting with each other but not with
v
need to be both fixed or both conjugated for this automorphism to be well-defined.

All this terminology carries over to outer automorphisms.

By work of Laurence (
[
26
]
) and Servatius (
[
29
]
), oriented graph permutations, transvections and partial conjugations generate all of
\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
.

Notation 2.5
.

For
\varphi
an automorphism of
A_{\Gamma}
, we will denote by
[\varphi]
the outer automorphism it represents. Likewise, if
S\subseteq A_{\Gamma}
,
[S]
will denote the conjugacy class of
S
. Given
[\varphi]
an outer automorphism and
S\subseteq A_{\Gamma}
, the images of
S
under representatives of
[\varphi]
form a conjugacy class of subsets that we will denote
[\varphi(S)]
.

The group
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
is virtually torsion-free (

By the original theorem of Droms, we can assume that

, with vertex set

,

becoming an automorphism. By
[

]
, Corollary to the Lemma 4.5, there exists a decomposition
\varphi=\psi\circ\sigma
where for every standard generator

,

Definition 2.12

(see
[
1
]
, Definition 3.1, Lemmas 3.2, 3.7)

X
to cubes of

[
4
]

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes

\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes between universal covers
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{Y}

For every hyperplane

of

are mapped to vertices (

is
collapsed
), or every edge dual to

up to isomorphism of the range.

A

is a collapse map
c\colon X\rightarrow Y
satisfying the further assumptions:

•

c
is a homotopy equivalence.

•

Any two parallel edges of
X
mapping to the same vertex of

are parallel inside the preimage of that vertex (i.e. there exist a sequence of squares witnessing their parallelism that is entirely mapped to that vertex).

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
c\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a collapse map between
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes. Then

is surjective, preimages of cubes of
Y
are convex subcomplexes of

. Moreover

preserves medians and maps geodesic edge paths to geodesic edge paths (up to reparametrization). The restriction of

More generally, if
D\subset Y
is convex,
c^{-1}(D)\subset X
is convex, and if

is convex,
c(E)\subset Y
is convex,

Proof.

The first part of the lemma comes from
[
1
]
, Lemma 3.2 and 3.9 (the rightmost inequality does not require the collapse to be strong).

Assume that
D\subset Y
is convex. Let
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in c^{-1}(D)\times c^{-1}(D)\times X
be a triple of vertices. Then
c(\mu(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}))=\mu(c(x_{1}),c(x_{2}),c(x_{3}))\in D
since

is convex, hence
\mu(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in c^{-1}(D)
. It remains to see that

is full. Let

be a cube of

is a cube all of whose vertices are in

is full.

Likewise, assume

is surjective, there exists

be a cube of

There is a bijective labelling of the hyperplanes of
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{\Pi}}
by
\mathbf{\Pi}\sqcup V
, and a canonical orientation for hyperplanes (we will sometimes speak of the label of an edge meaning the label of its dual hyperplane).

•

c_{\mathbf{\Pi}}
is a collapse in the sense of the above definition, collapsing exactly the hyperplanes labelled by partitions.

•

The unique vertex preimage

under

is a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex.

•

A hyperplane labelled by some partition

has the same transverse hyperplanes as every hyperplane labelled by a basepoint of the partition
\mathbf{P}
. Two hyperplanes labelled by standard generators are transverse if and only if the generators are adjacent in
\Gamma

•

For any

, the associated oriented hyperplane determines a partition of

into positive and negative halfspaces. For any

(resp.

have their terminal (resp. initial) endpoint in the positive halfspace. Likewise, if

(resp.
v^{-1}\in P^{*}
), all edges labelled
v
of

have their terminal (resp. initial) endpoint in the negative halfspace.

•

As a consequence, for any edge

labelled by a standard generator

in

is exactly the set of hyperplanes labelled by partitions
\mathbf{P}
with

.

Remark 2.20
.

Since there are only finitely many Whitehead partitions of
V^{\pm}
and compatible partitions are distinct, there are finitely many blow-ups of
\mathbb{S}
. Hence there are finitely many isomorphism types of spatial cube complexes (with a given fundamental group). This means that
K_{\Gamma}
has finitely many
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
-orbits of vertices, i.e.,
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
acts cocompactly on
K_{\Gamma}
.

If
(\mathbf{P},v)
is a based Whitehead partition, the collapse

from the blow-up

where the only collapsed hyperplane is labelled

is a homotopy equivalence. Moreover, there exists an isomorphism

3.
Minsets for cospecial actions

The main goal of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a group acting freely and cospecially on a
\rm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
of finite dimension
n
by combinatorial isometries. Recall that for
g\in G
,
\ell(g)
is the combinatorial translation length of
g
in
X
. Let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k})\in G^{k}
and set the following.

M\coloneqq\max_{i}\ell(a_{i})+\frac{n}{2}\max_{i<j}\ell(a_{i}a_{j})+\frac{3n}{2}

There exists a vertex
x\in X^{(0)}
such that for every
i
,
d(x,a_{i}x)\leq M
.

The proof involves several results about invariant subsets in actions of finitely generated groups on
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes. Although most of them belong to folklore, we provide complete proofs. Recall that two convex subcomplexes are called parallel when the sets of hyperplanes dual to their edges are identical.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a finitely generated group acting on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
by combinatorial isometries. There exists a non-empty convex subcomplex
Y
of
X
that is
K
-invariant and minimal (for the inclusion) with these properties. Besides,
Y
has finitely many
K
-orbits of hyperplanes. Moreover, any two minimal subcomplexes with these properties are parallel.

Note that when
X
has a
K
-invariant convex subcomplex
S
,
Y
can be taken contained in
S
simply by applying the result to
S
instead of
X
.

Proof.

Let
\Sigma
be a finite symmetric generating set for
K
and
x
a vertex of
X
. The combinatorial convex hull
C
of
K\cdot x
(that is, the intersection of all halfspaces containing
K\cdot x
) is convex, non-empty and
K
-invariant. Besides, if a hyperplane
H
is dual to an edge of
C
,
K\cdot x
is not contained in a halfspace of
H
by definition of
C
. Thus, there exists
k_{1},k_{2}\in K
such that
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(k_{1}x\mid k_{2}x)
. Write
k_{1}^{-1}k_{2}=s_{1}\dots s_{n}
with
s_{i}\in\Sigma
. The following holds:

\mathrm{Sep}(k_{1}x\mid k_{2}x)\subseteq\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}\mathrm{Sep}(k_{1}s_{1}\dots s_{i}x\mid k_{1}s_{1}\dots s_{i+1}x)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{n-1}k_{1}s_{1}\dots s_{i}\cdot\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid s_{i+1}x)

Thus,
H
is in the same
K
-orbit as some element of
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid sx)
for some
s\in\Sigma
. Since
\Sigma
and separators are finite,
C
has finitely many
K
-orbits of hyperplanes.

Let
Y
be a
K
-invariant non-empty convex subcomplex of
C
with a minimal number of
K
-orbits of hyperplanes. Let
Z\subseteq Y
be a
K
-invariant and non-empty convex subcomplex. By definition of
Y
,
Y
and
Z
have the same
K
-orbits of hyperplanes. Assuming
y
is a vertex of
Y\setminus Z
, let
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid Z)
. Then by convexity
H
is a hyperplane of
Y
, but
Z
is contained in one of its halfspaces:
H
is not a hyperplane of
Z
. By
K
-invariance of
Z
, no hyperplane in the
K
-orbit of
H
is in
Z
, a contradiction. Therefore,
Y
and
Z
have the same
0
-skeleton. Since they are convex, hence full,
Z=Y
is minimal for the inclusion.

Let
Y^{\prime}
be another minimal
K
-invariant subcomplex, and let
(y,y^{\prime})\in Y\times Y^{\prime}
be a pair of vertices at minimal distance. By minimality of
Y
,
Y
is the combinatorial convex hull of
K\cdot y
. Therefore, for any hyperplane
H
dual to an edge of
Y
,
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid ky)
for some
k\in K
. Assuming
H\notin\mathrm{Sep}(y^{\prime}\mid ky^{\prime})
, then either
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid ky,y^{\prime},ky^{\prime})
or
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(ky\mid y,y^{\prime},ky^{\prime})
. In the first case,
\mu(y,y^{\prime},ky)\in Y
belongs to a geodesic joining
y
and
y^{\prime}
yet is separated from
y
by
H
, contradicting the minimality of
d(y,y^{\prime})
. Likewise, in the second case,
\mu(k^{-1}y,y,y^{\prime})\in Y
belongs to a geodesic joining
y
and
y^{\prime}
yet is separated from
y
by
k^{-1}H
, yielding the same contradiction. Thus
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(y^{\prime}\mid ky^{\prime})
is dual to an edge of
Y^{\prime}
. By symmetry of the assumptions,
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
are parallel.
∎

In the case of trees (
1
-dimensional
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes), parallelism is equality (except for singletons), and one recovers the uniqueness of the minimal invariant subtree. In the following lemma, one considers a free cospecial action of
\mathbb{Z}
. Such actions arise naturally when considering a larger group
G
acting properly discontinuously and cospecially on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex and restricting the action to the subgroup
\left\langle g\right\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}
generated by any infinite-order element
g\in G
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}=\left\langle g\right\rangle
act freely and cospecially on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
by combinatorial isometries. The set of vertices
x\in X^{(0)}
such that
d(x,gx)
is minimal spans a non-empty
\left\langle g\right\rangle
-invariant convex subcomplex of
X
. We call this subcomplex the
combinatorial minset
of
g
and denote it
\mathrm{Min}(g)
.

When
X
is a tree, the combinatorial minset coincides with the
axis
of
g
. In general, the vertex set of
\mathrm{Min}(g)
is the union of vertex sets of all minimal
\left\langle g\right\rangle
-invariant convex subcomplexes of
X
. It is also the union of vertex sets of all
combinatorial axes
of
g
(i.e. bi-infinite,
\ell^{1}
-geodesic edge paths stabilized by
g
).

Note that while Lemma
3.2
applies for any isometric action on a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex, Lemma
3.3
fails without the specialness assumption: vertices minimally translated by a diagonal glide reflection in the standard cubulation of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
lie in a neighborhood of a diagonal line, which can never span a convex subcomplex. The minimal invariant convex subset for
\mathbb{Z}
in that case is the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathrm{Min}(g)
is non-empty and
\left\langle g\right\rangle
-invariant. By specialness, every hyperplane of
X
descends to a two-sided hyperplane in the quotient
X/\mathbb{Z}
, hence
g
acts
stably without inversion
, in the sense of
[
21
]
. By
[
21
]
, Corollary 6.2, the translation length of
g
is positive, and every vertex in
\mathrm{Min}(g)
belongs to a combinatorial axis.

Let
x,y
be vertices in
\mathrm{Min}(g)
and let
\gamma
be any combinatorial geodesic path from
x
to
y
. To prove inductively that
\gamma
is contained in
\mathrm{Min}(g)
, let
[x,x^{\prime}]
be the first edge of
\gamma
and
H
its dual hyperplane. The following equality holds for

the combinatorial metric:

\displaystyle d(x^{\prime},gx^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(x^{\prime}\mid gx^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(x^{\prime}\mid x)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gx^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)\mathbin{\triangle}\{H\}\mathbin{\triangle}\{gH\}\right|

If either
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)
,
gH\in\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)
or
H=gH
, then
d(x^{\prime},gx^{\prime})\leq d(x,gx)
. Hence
x^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Min}(g)
, and the induction proceeds.

Otherwise,
H\neq gH
and
\{H,gH\}\cap\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)=\varnothing
. By specialness,
H
and
gH
are not transverse. Hence
H\notin\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gy)
, or else the quadruple
x,x^{\prime},gy,gx^{\prime}
would witness the transversality of
H
and
gH
. As
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid y)
yet
H\notin\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)\cup\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gy)
,
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid gy)
. Symmetrically,
gH\in\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid gy)
, which rewrites as
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(g^{-1}y\mid y)
. Thus a combinatorial axis going through
y
meets
H
twice between
g^{-1}y
and
gy
, yielding a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

In the same setup as Lemma
3.3
, for every vertices
x,y\in\mathrm{Min}(g)
, there exists a unique bijection between
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)
and
\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid gy)
which maps each hyperplane to a hyperplane in the same
\left\langle g\right\rangle
-orbit.

Proof.

Let
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)
and
\omega
its
\left\langle g\right\rangle
-orbit. Since some combinatorial axis goes through
x
,
\omega\cap\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)=\{H\}
. For the same reason,
\omega\cap\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid gy)
has at most one element.

However
\omega\cap\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gy)
is a translate of
\omega\cap\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid y)
, hence of the same cardinality. Thus the cardinality of
\omega\cap\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid gy)=\omega\cap(\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid x)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid gx)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gy))
is odd, hence is exactly one. Pick this unique element as the image of
H
. Since no choices have been made, the obtained bijection is unique.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

In the same setup as Lemma
3.3
, let
x\in X^{(0)}
be a vertex, and
p
its unique projection on
\mathrm{Min}(g)
. There exists a geodesic path from
x
to
gx
which passes through the vertices
p
and
gp
.

This isn’t necessarily verified by every geodesic, as shown by the universal cover of two squares glued together at two adjacent vertices (but not along the edge). Note that as a consequence of this lemma, every
g
-invariant convex subcomplex intersects
\mathrm{Min}(g)
.

Proof.

The goal is to prove that
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid p)
,
\mathrm{Sep}(p\mid gp)
and
\mathrm{Sep}(gp\mid gx)
are pairwise disjoint.

The separator
\mathrm{Sep}(x,gp\mid p)
is empty, otherwise the median
\mu(x,p,gp)
, which belongs to
\mathrm{Min}(g)
by convexity, would be a better projection than
p
. Symmetrically,
\mathrm{Sep}(p,gx\mid gp)
is empty. Hence
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid p)=\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid p,gp)
and
\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gp)=\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid p,gp)
. This proves that
\mathrm{Sep}(p\mid gp)
intersects trivially the two others, and that the third intersection
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid p)\cap\mathrm{Sep}(gx\mid gp)
equals
\mathrm{Sep}(x,gx\mid p,gp)
.

Assume there exists
H\in\mathrm{Sep}(x,gx\mid p,gp)
, taken closest to
p
. Recall that
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid p)
denotes the set of hyperplanes separating the carrier of
H
from
p
. Then
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid p)\subseteq\mathrm{Sep}(x,gx\mid p)
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Sep}(x,gx\mid p)\cap\mathrm{Sep}(gp\mid p)\subseteq\mathrm{Sep}(x,gp\mid p)
, and the latter is empty by the argument above. Thus,
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid p)\subseteq\mathrm{Sep}(x,gx\mid p,gp)
and any hyperplane
K\in\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid p)
would contradict the choice of
H
. This proves that
\mathrm{Sep}(H\mid p)
is empty. Hence
p
belongs to the carrier of
H
by convexity of the latter. Therefore,
H
is dual to some edge
[p,p^{\prime}]
starting at
p
.

As
H
separates
p
from
x
, the vertex
p^{\prime}
cannot belong to
\mathrm{Min}(g)
. This yields a contradiction if
gH=H
, because then:

d(p^{\prime},gp^{\prime})=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(p^{\prime},gp^{\prime})\right|=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(p\mid gp)\mathbin{\triangle}\{H\}\mathbin{\triangle}\{gH\}\right|=d(p,gp)

Otherwise,
gH\neq H
, but the quadruple
p^{\prime},gx,gp^{\prime},gp
witnesses the transversality of
H
and
gH
which contradicts the specialness assumption.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
c\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a collapse between
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes. Assume
\mathbb{Z}=\left\langle g\right\rangle
acts freely and cospecially on
X
and
Y
, and
c
is equivariant. Then
c(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g))=\mathrm{Min}_{Y}(g)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g)
be a vertex. There exists a
g
-invariant bi-infinite geodesic edge path of
X
containing
x
. By Lemma
2.13
, there exists a
g
-invariant bi-infinite geodesic edge path of
Y
containing
c(x)
, hence
c(x)\in\mathrm{Min}_{Y}(g)
. By convexity,
c(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g))\subseteq\mathrm{Min}_{Y}(g)
.

Conversely, let
y\in\mathrm{Min}_{Y}(g)
be a vertex and, using surjectivity of
c
, let
x\in X
be a vertex in the preimage of
y
. Finally, let
p
be the projection of
x
onto the convex subcomplex
\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g)
. By Lemma
3.5
, some geodesic joining
x
and
gx
goes through
p
and
gp
. By Lemma
2.13
again, there is a geodesic joining
c(x)=y
and
gy
going through
c(p)
and
gc(p)
. Thus,
d(c(p),gc(p))\leq d(y,gy)
and equality holds since
y\in\mathrm{Min}_{Y}(g)
. Therefore,
y=c(p)\in c(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g))
, and the result follows since
c(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g))
is convex by Lemma
2.13
.
∎

A well-known result states in the case of trees that if two group elements
g,h
act with disjoint axes,
\ell(gh)=\ell(g)+\ell(h)+2d(\mathrm{Min}(g),\mathrm{Min}(h))
. Unfortunately, this fails for general
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes. Consider for example the special cube complex
Y
obtained by attaching two edges to a square, making all four attaching points distinct and one of the hyperplanes separating. Equality does not hold for the universal cover of
Y
with isometries corresponding to the two edge loops of length
2
of
Y
, suitably oriented. However, we can still recover an inequality.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a group acting freely and cospecially on a
\rm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
by combinatorial isometries. Let
g,h\in G
. The following inequality holds:

d(\mathrm{Min}(g),\mathrm{Min}(h))\leq\frac{\ell(gh)}{2}

.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be the set of
G
-orbits of hyperplanes of
X
, and
\omega
any element of
\Omega
. By Lemma
3.4
, the number
M_{\omega}
of elements of
\omega
in
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid ghx)
does not depend on the choice of a vertex
x\in\mathrm{Min}(gh)
. Let
m_{\omega}
be the number of elements of
\omega
in
\mathrm{Sep}(\mathrm{Min}(g)\mid\mathrm{Min}(h))
. By convexity of minsets, the following equalities hold:

\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}m_{\omega}=d(\mathrm{Min}(g),\mathrm{Min}(h))\qquad\sum_{\omega\in\Omega}M_{\omega}=\ell(gh)

It now suffices to prove
2m_{\omega}\leq M_{\omega}
for each
\omega\in\Omega
to conclude.

Elements of
\omega
are pairwise non-transverse by specialness. The collapse
c_{\omega}\colon X\rightarrow T_{\omega}
of all hyperplanes of
X
not contained in
\omega
has a tree
T_{\omega}
for range. Since the family of collapsed hyperplanes is
G
-invariant, there is a natural action of
G
on
T_{\omega}
by combinatorial isometries making
c_{\omega}
equivariant. Note that
T_{\omega}
is not necessarily locally finite, even when
X
is. However,
M_{\omega}=\ell_{T_{\omega}}(gh)
and
m_{\omega}=d(c_{\omega}(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(g)),c_{\omega}(\mathrm{Min}_{X}(h)))=d(\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(g),\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(h))
using Lemma
3.6
. If
m_{\omega}=0
the result is obvious, and otherwise
\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(g)
and
\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(h)
are disjoint, and the equality for trees:
\ell_{T_{\omega}}(gh)=\ell_{T_{\omega}}(g)+\ell_{T_{\omega}}(h)+2d(\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(g),\mathrm{Min}_{T_{\omega}}(h))
concludes the proof.
∎

In the following lemma,
\mathrm{N}_{r}(C)
denotes the closed
r
-neighborhood of
C
for the combinatorial metric.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
C
be a convex subcomplex of a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex
X
of finite dimension
n
. Let
r
be a nonnegative integer. The combinatorial convex hull of
\mathrm{N}_{r}(C)
is contained in
\mathrm{N}_{nr}(C)
.

The bound is optimal, as seen with
X=[0,r]^{n}
,
C=\{0\}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
x
be a vertex in the combinatorial convex hull of
\mathrm{N}_{r}(C)
. By convexity of
C
, the following holds:

\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid C)=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in X^{(0)}\\
d(y,C)\leq r\end{subarray}}\mathrm{Sep}(x,y\mid C)

Each term
\mathrm{Sep}(x,y\mid C)
of the union is contained in
\mathrm{Sep}(y\mid C)
, hence has cardinality at most
r
. Besides, since
\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid C)
is finite, the index set of the union can be restricted to a finite subset without changing the result.

Claim:
If this index subset has more than
n
elements, one of them can be removed without changing the result.

This allows to remove terms in the union until no more than
n
are left, yielding the desired bound
d(x,C)=\left|\mathrm{Sep}(x\mid C)\right|\leq nr
.

Proof of the claim:
Let
\mathrm{Sep}(x,y_{i}\mid C)_{1\leq i\leq n+1}
be terms of the union. Assume by contradiction that no one is contained in the union of the others. For each
i
, there exists a hyperplane
H_{i}\in\mathrm{Sep}(x,y_{i}\mid C)
not inside any of the
\mathrm{Sep}(x,y_{j}\mid C)_{j\neq i}
. Hence, for each pair
i\neq j
, the quadruple
x,y_{i},y_{j},C
witnesses transversality of
H_{i}
and
H_{j}
, but having
n+1
pairwise transverse hyperplanes is a contradiction in a dimension
n
complex (their carriers intersect globally by the Helly property).
∎

We can now finally prove the result.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Let
M^{\prime}=\left\lceil\max_{i<j}\ell(a_{i}a_{j})/4\right\rceil\leq\max_{i<j}\ell(a_{i}a_{j})/4+3/4
. For every

and

the combinatorial convex hull of

.

By Proposition

,

and

intersect for every pair

. Hence the collection

has pairwise nonempty intersection. By the Helly property, there exists a vertex

which belongs to every

, for every

We end with two lemmas giving a partial converse to Lemma

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}
be parallel convex subcomplexes of a
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complex

. For every vertex

of
A_{1}
,
\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(a))=\mathrm{Sep}(A_{1}\mid A_{2})
. For every vertices

of
A_{1}
,
\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid b)=\mathrm{Sep}(\pi_{A_{2}}(a)\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(b))
.

Proof.

The inclusion
\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(a))\supseteq\mathrm{Sep}(A_{1}\mid A_{2})
is clear since

and
\pi_{A_{2}}(a)\in A_{2}
. Moreover, by convexity of
A_{2}
,
\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(a))
contains no hyperplane dual to an edge of
A_{2}
(otherwise
\mu(a,x,\pi_{A_{2}}(a))
would be a better projection, for

the appropriate vertex of such an edge). By parallelism,
\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(a))
contains no hyperplane dual to an edge of

. Finally, letting

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sep}(\pi_{A_{2}}(a)\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(b))

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Sep}(\pi_{A_{2}}(a)\mid a)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(a\mid b)\mathbin{\triangle}\mathrm{Sep}(b\mid\pi_{A_{2}}(b))

Let

4.
Collapses of locally convex subcomplexes

This section presents two useful lemmas about the action of homotopy equivalent collapse maps on locally convex subcomplexes of the base.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be a collapse map between locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, which is a homotopy equivalence. Let
D
be a connected, locally convex subcomplex of
X
. Choose
*
any vertex of
D
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

The pointed map

induces a surjection of fundamental groups.

(2)

For every vertex preimage
C
in
X
,

is connected

(3)

For every vertex preimage
C
in
X
,

is convex in

(4)

For every deck transformation
g
of the universal cover
\widetilde{X}

or
\widetilde{c}(g\widetilde{D})

\widetilde{c}(\widetilde{D})

lifts

between universal covers and
\widetilde{D}
is any connected component of the preimage of

in
\widetilde{X}
.

is a connected, locally convex subcomplex of

, and

is a homotopy equivalence between

and
c(D)
.

Note that the intersection

may be empty, in which case it is vacuously convex and connected.

Proof.

First note that

is always
\pi_{1}
-injective. Indeed, (up to restriction of the range) it is the composition of the homotopy equivalence
c
and the inclusion

, the latter being
\pi_{1}
-injective by local convexity of

.

(4.)\Rightarrow(3.)
Let
C
be a vertex preimage in
X
and
\widetilde{C}
a connected component of its preimage in
\widetilde{X}
. Fix also
\widetilde{D}
as in
(4.)
. Since

is locally convex and
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
(Corollary

), the universal cover restricts to an isomorphism
\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
, and
\widetilde{C}
is convex. Let
g,g^{\prime}
be deck transformations and assume that both

and

intersect
\widetilde{C}
. Then
\widetilde{c}(g\widetilde{D})
and
\widetilde{c}(g^{\prime}\widetilde{D})
have in common the vertex
\widetilde{c}(\widetilde{C})
. By assumption,
g\widetilde{D}=g^{\prime}\widetilde{D}
. Thus
\widetilde{C}
intersects at most one connected component of the preimage of

. This component is convex by local convexity of

, and the convex intersection is exactly the preimage of

under the isomorphism
\widetilde{C}\rightarrow C
.

(3.)\Rightarrow(2.)
is clear.

(2.)\Rightarrow(1.)
Let
\gamma
be a closed edge path in

based at

. Pick, for each edge of
\gamma
, an edge of

of which it is the image. For each vertex of
\gamma
different from

, with preimage

in

, the incoming and outgoing edges of

at this vertex correspond to chosen edges of

, each having an endpoint in

. By assumption, these endpoints can be joined by an edge path in

. Likewise at

, the first endpoint of the edge of

chosen for the first edge of
\gamma
and the last endpoint of the edge of

chosen for the last edge of

can both be joined to

by edge paths in

. This provides a closed edge path in

based at

projecting to
\gamma
up to reparametrization.

(1.)\Rightarrow(4.)
Choose a lift
\widetilde{*}\in\widetilde{D}
of
*
and a deck transformation

of
\widetilde{X}
. Assume that
\widetilde{c}(g\widetilde{D})
and
\widetilde{c}(\widetilde{D})
intersect at a vertex

. Then there exists a path

joining
\widetilde{c}(\widetilde{*})
to
\widetilde{c}(g\widetilde{*})
, going through

, such that the image
\gamma^{\prime}
of

in

is contained in

. The closed path

represents a homotopy class in

. By assumption, there exists a closed path

in

based at

is homotopic to

. Lift

to a path

in
\widetilde{X}
starting at
\widetilde{*}
, and ending at some

.

Since

is connected as well. The preimage of

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

be a collapse map between locally
\mathrm{CAT}(0)
cube complexes, which is a homotopy equivalence. Assume

has only one vertex. Let

(2)

\widetilde{X}

\widetilde{X}

5.

Let

Definition 5.1
.

Let

Remark 5.2
.

Note that an untwisted marking on

itself is an outer automorphism in

. Every marked Salvetti in

is of the form

for some

for some

Clearly both

and

are preserved under the action of

and the action of

Finally, let

In the next sections, we will prove a general result (Theorem

and the same edges. In the case where

and
H_{b}
are transverse, the same is true for the other edge
e^{\prime}
parallel to

in
C_{ab}
and the other edge

parallel to
f
in

. Since any cube not containing

,
f
,

or

is fixed by

,

is
\alpha
-invariant. Therefore,
C_{ac}\cap D_{i}=\alpha(C_{cd})\cap D_{i}=\alpha(C_{cd}\cap D_{i})
is convex in
\alpha(C_{cd})=C_{ac}
and
C_{bd}\cap D_{i}=\alpha(C_{ab})\cap D_{i})
is convex in

. Moreover,
\iota_{ac}\circ c_{ac}(D_{i})=\iota_{cd}\circ c_{cd}\circ\alpha(D_{i})=\mathbb{S}_{\Delta_{i}}
and likewise

[(\iota_{ab})_{*}\circ(c_{ab})_{*}\circ((\iota_{ac})_{*}\circ(c_{ac})_{*})^{-1}]\in\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})

∎

6.
Adding fixed cyclic subgroups

Let
\mathcal{G}=(A_{\Delta_{1}},\dots A_{\Delta_{p}})
be a collection of standard subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
and let
\mathcal{H}=(\left\langle h_{1}\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle h_{q}\right\rangle)
be a finite collection of cyclic subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. Our goal is to construct the following object:

Theorem 6.1
.

There exists a contractible subcomplex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
of the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

Example 6.2
.

An automorphism
\varphi\in\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
is
conjugating
when
\varphi(v)
is conjugate to
v
for all
v\in V
. By
[
26
]
, Theorem 2.2, the subgroup of conjugating automorphisms is exactly the subgroup generated by partial conjugations in
A_{\Gamma}
. In particular, its image in
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
is contained in
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
. Therefore, this image is the McCool group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{\left\langle v\right\rangle\mid v\in V\}^{t})
, hence is of type VF by Theorem
6.1
.

Together with Lemma
2.11
, we get directly a
spine of untwisted auter space
for
A_{\Gamma}
(as a subcomplex of the spine of untwisted outer space for
A_{\Gamma}*\mathbb{Z}
):

Corollary 6.3
.

There exists a contractible simplicial complex
L_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

Note that type VF was already an algebraic consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma})
is of type VF and
\mathsf{Inn}(A_{\Gamma})
is of type F, as a right-angled Artin group (
[
15
]
, Theorem 7.3.4).

Definition 6.4
.

For
X
a spatial cube complex,
[g]\in\pi_{1}X
a conjugacy class, let
\ell_{X}(g)
be the smallest length of an edge cycle in the free homotopy class representing
[g]
, or equivalently the translation length (for the combinatorial metric) of any representative
g
acting on
\widetilde{X}
. In particular, for

, let

, which is the minimal length of any cyclic word in the standard generators representing the conjugacy class of

. Complete
(h_{1},\dots,h_{q})
into an infinite sequence
(h_{i})_{i\geq 1}
of elements of
A_{\Gamma}
containing at least one representative per conjugacy class.

Given a marked Salvetti
\sigma=[X,m]\in K_{\Gamma}
, its

will be the following sequence of non-negative integers:

N(\sigma)=(\ell_{X}(m^{-1}(h_{i})))_{i\geq 1}

If
[X,m]=[X^{\prime},m^{\prime}]
is a marked Salvetti, there exists a combinatorial isomorphism
\alpha\colon X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
such that
[\alpha_{*}]=[m^{\prime-1}\circ m]
. Since
\alpha
preserves lengths, for all

\ell_{X}(m^{-1}(h))=\ell_{X^{\prime}}(\alpha_{*}\circ m^{-1}(h))=\ell_{X^{\prime}}(m^{\prime-1}(h))
, hence

is well-defined.

Remark 6.5
.

This norm is related to the norm
\left|\left|\,\cdot\,\right|\right|
defined in
[
6
]
, Definition 6.1 by a shuffling and possible duplicating of entries. We will show that most of the properties proved in
[
6
]
for
\left|\left|\,\cdot\,\right|\right|
also hold for

.

Lemma 6.6
.

The map
N
is injective on the set of marked Salvettis in
K_{\Gamma}
. Setting
\sigma\leq\sigma^{\prime}
if and only if
N(\sigma)\leq N(\sigma^{\prime})
in the lexicographic order defines a well-ordering of the set of marked Salvettis in
K_{\Gamma}

Proof.

Let
\sigma=[\mathbb{S},\varphi],\sigma^{\prime}=[\mathbb{S},\varphi^{\prime}]
be two marked Salvettis and assume
N(\sigma)=N(\sigma^{\prime})
. Then for every
i\geq 1
,
\ell(\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}(h_{i}))=\ell(h_{i})
. In particular for every
h\in A_{\Gamma}
such that
\ell(h)\leq 2
,
\ell(\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}(h))=\ell(h)
. By Lemma 6.2 in
[
6
]
,
\varphi^{-1}\circ\varphi^{\prime}
is an outer oriented graph permutation, thus
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
, proving that
N
is injective.

Suppose the existence of a decreasing sequence
(\sigma_{n}=[\mathbb{S},\varphi_{n}])_{n}
, and choose arbitrary representatives
\varphi_{n}\in\mathsf{Aut}(A_{\Gamma})
. The non-increasing positive integer sequence
(\ell(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(h_{1})))_{n}
is eventually constant. Thus the sequence
(\ell(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(h_{2})))_{n}
is eventually non-increasing, and then itself eventually constant. Inductively, for every
i
, the sequence
(\ell(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(h_{i})))_{n}
is eventually constant. This implies that the following quantity:

\max_{i}\ell(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(v_{i}))+\dfrac{\dim(\mathbb{S})}{2}\max_{i<j}\ell(\varphi_{n}^{-1}(v_{i}v_{j}))+2\dim(\mathbb{S})

is bounded by some constant
C
independent of
n
.

Apply Proposition
3.1
for all
n
to
A_{\Gamma}
acting on
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
, with
a_{i}=\varphi_{n}^{-1}(v_{i})
the preimages of standard generators. There exists
(x_{n})_{n}\in\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
a sequence of vertices such that
d(x_{n},\varphi_{n}^{-1}(v_{i})x_{n})\leq C
for all
i,n
. Since
A_{\Gamma}
acts transitively on vertices of
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
, up to composing
\varphi_{n}
by an inner automorphism, assume
(x_{n})_{n}
is constant equal to
x_{0}
. Since
A_{\Gamma}
acts freely on
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
, only finitely many group elements

satisfy
d(x_{0},gx_{0})\leq C
. Thus there are only finitely many choices for all the
\varphi_{n}^{-1}(v_{i})
. This implies the existence of

with
\varphi_{n}=\varphi_{n^{\prime}}
, hence
\sigma_{n}=\sigma_{n^{\prime}}
and a contradiction.
∎

Beware that this order type may be more complicated that in
[
6
]
, where the set of marked Salvettis has simply the order type of the integers (
[
6
]
, Corollary 6.21). We will however still be able to proceed using transfinite induction. The following construction of the spine of untwisted outer space relative to
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
is close to the construction in
[
2
]
of a relative outer space for free groups.

Definition 6.7
.

For every marked Salvetti
\sigma\in K_{\Gamma}
, let

be the union of all stars (in
K_{\Gamma}
) of marked Salvettis in

that are (strictly) smaller than
\sigma
. It is clear that

is a subcomplex of
K^{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
\sigma_{0}=[\mathbb{S},\varphi_{0}]
be the minimum of

for our well-ordering, so that

.

Let

be the set of marked Salvettis in
S^{\mathcal{G}}
where each conjugacy class in
\mathcal{H}
has the same length as in
\sigma_{0}
. It is a lower subset of
S^{\mathcal{G}}
for the well-ordering:

S^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}=\{[X,m]\in S^{\mathcal{G}}\mid\forall i\leq q,\,\ell_{X}(m^{-1}(h_{i}))=\ell(\varphi_{0}^{-1}(h_{i}))\}

Let
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
be the union of stars (in
K_{\Gamma}
) of all vertices in
S^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
. If
S^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}\neq S^{\mathcal{G}}
, let
\sigma_{1}
be the minimum of the complement

so that

. Otherwise,

.

Lemma 6.8
.

The complex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
is
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
-stable with only finitely many orbits of vertices.

Proof.

Note first that if
\mathcal{H}
is empty, we know from Definition
5.1
that
K^{\mathcal{G}}
is
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})
-stable and has a single orbit of marked Salvettis.

If
\mathcal{H}
is non-empty,
S^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
is
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
-stable, and so is
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
. Let
C
be the finite set of conjugacy classes in
A_{\Gamma}
of length at most
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq q}\ell(\varphi_{0}^{-1}(h_{i}))
, let
D=\{[\varphi]\in\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})\mid\forall i\leq q,\,\ell(\varphi^{-1}(h_{i}))=\ell(\varphi_{0}^{-1}(h_{i}))\}
and consider the following map:

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle\rightarrow C^{q}

\displaystyle[\varphi]

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow([\varphi^{-1}(h_{i})])_{1\leq i\leq q}

Two outer automorphisms
[\varphi],[\psi]\in D
have the same image under this map if and only if
[\varphi\circ\psi^{-1}(h_{i})]=[h_{i}]
for every

, or equivalently

. In that case, the marked Salvettis

have the same

-orbit. Therefore,

contains at most

distinct such orbits (by picking any marking representatives in the equivalence classes). Hence

has finitely many
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
-orbits of marked Salvettis.

Finally, every vertex in
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
is adjacent to a marked Salvetti in
S^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
, which has no more neighbors than the finite number of sets of pairwise compatible Whitehead partitions of

, proving the result.
∎

7.
Proof of contractibility

We found a subcomplex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}\subseteq K_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
acts properly and cocompactly, and the latter is virtually torsion-free as a subgroup of
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma})
. To prove Theorem
6.1
, it remains only to see that

is contractible. This generalizes the main result of
[
6
]
(Theorem 6.24), and our proof will be very parallel to
[
6
]
, Section 6. The proofs there use the terminology of blow-ups and Whitehead partitions, and we will mirror this use when reemploying them.

Definition 7.1
.

An elementary edge

of

incident to a marked Salvetti
\sigma

-reductive

containing

and

. Such a Whitehead move is an

-reductive move
for
\sigma
.

Two (possibly equal) elementary edges of

incident to the same marked Salvetti
\sigma
, with other endpoints
\alpha,\beta
are

if there exists a vertex
\gamma
of
K_{\Gamma}
that collapses onto both
\alpha
and
\beta
. They are
incompatible
otherwise. Let
e,e^{\prime}
be two edges of
K_{\Gamma}
(not necessarily elementary). Say that
e^{\prime}

factors

when

is elementary and the edges

are part of a triangle in
K_{\Gamma}
. In that case, some collapse map representing

factors through some collapse map representing

.

Let
X
be a spatial cube complex,
H
a hyperplane of
X
and
[g]\in\pi_{1}X
a conjugacy class. Define
\ell_{X,H}(g)
as the number of edges dual to

in a shortest edge loop representing

. By Lemma

, this number is independent from the chosen representative loop. Given a marking

on

, define the following sequence of non-negative integers:

N_{H}(X,m)=(\ell_{X,H}(m^{-1}(h_{i})))_{i\geq 1}

Let

be an elementary edge, and choose a marking-preserving collapse
(X,m)\rightarrow(S,m^{\prime})
representing

. Let
H
be the collapsed hyperplane of

. Define

, which is independent from the chosen representative collapse for

.

Finally, for

say a based Whitehead partition

is
\varphi
-
N\mathcal{G}
-reductive
if it induces an
N\mathcal{G}
-reductive Whitehead move at

. In other words, if
N([\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}_{b},\varphi^{\prime}])<N([\mathbb{S},\varphi])
where

and
c_{b}\colon\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}_{b}
are marking-preserving collapses of the hyperplanes labelled
\mathbf{P}
and

respectively, for some markings

on
\mathbb{S}^{\mathbf{P}}
and
\varphi^{\prime}
on

and moreover

.

Remark 7.2
.

Let

be a marking-preserving collapse, and let

be a hyperplane of

not collapsed by

. By Corollary

.

Consider a single blow-up

with a marking

and two homotopy equivalent collapses

,

and

and

for simplicity of notation. Let

making

represented by

and

and

a minimal length word representing

(see

\varphi
-
N\mathcal{G}
-reductive based Whitehead partition
(\mathbf{P}_{0},p_{0})
(compatible with both
\mathbf{M}
and
\mathbf{P}
), with
lk(\mathbf{P}_{0})=lk(\mathbf{P})
.

Proof.

The proof is drawn from
[
6
]
, Proposition 6.23. Once again, we need to reorder the entries of the norms, but all the lexicographic inequalities are either true term-by-term or a consequence of the assumptions of the lemma. Since
(\mathbf{P}_{0},p_{0})
is obtained by applying
[
6
]
, Lemma 6.16 with
(\mathbf{M},m)
and
(\mathbf{P},p)
which both induce Whitehead automorphisms in
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})
,
(\mathbf{P}_{0},p_{0})
also induces a Whitehead automorphism in
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})
by Lemma
7.7
. This finishes the proof that
(\mathbf{P}_{0},p_{0})
is
\varphi
-
N\mathcal{G}
-reductive.
∎

Proposition 7.11

(see
[
6
]
, proof of Theorem 6.24)
.

Let
\sigma\in S^{\mathcal{G}}\setminus\{\sigma_{0}\}
. The intersection
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma)
is contractible.

Proof.

Vertices in
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma)
are exactly the
\alpha
that are adjacent to
\sigma
and to some marked Salvetti
\tau\in S^{\mathcal{G}}
with
N(\tau)<N(\sigma)
. Such a vertex exists by Corollary
7.9
. The cell structure on the
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma)
corresponds to the induced poset of collapses.

The rest of the proof is identical to
[
6
]
: by Lemma
7.5
and Quillen’s poset lemma,
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma)
deformation retracts to its subcomplex
K^{\prime}
spanned by vertices
\alpha^{\prime}
such that every elementary edge factoring the edge from
\alpha^{\prime}
to
\sigma
is
N\mathcal{G}
reductive. Choose then
(\mathbf{M},m)
a based Whitehead partition satisfying the assumptions of Lemma
7.10
, and build a deformation retraction of
K^{\prime}
to a point exactly as in the proof of Theorem 6.24 in

.
∎

Corollary 7.12
.

For every
\sigma\in S^{\mathcal{G}}
different from
\sigma_{0}
, the complex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}
is contractible. The complex
K^{\mathcal{G}}
is also contractible.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
6.1
, since
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
is equal either to
K^{\mathcal{G}}
or to
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma_{1}}
.

Proof.

The proof is by transfinite induction. Let
P(\sigma)
be the property "Every homotopy group of
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}
is trivial". The property holds for the successor
\tau
of
\sigma_{0}
in the well-ordering since
K_{<\tau}=st(\sigma_{0})
is contractible. To prove preservation under successors assume
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma)
and
st(\sigma)
are contractible subcomplexes of
K^{\mathcal{G}}
. Then
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cup st(\sigma)
is homotopy equivalent to the topological quotient
(K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cup st(\sigma))/st(\sigma)=K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}/(K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}\cap st(\sigma))
which is itself homotopy equivalent to
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}
by Proposition
7.11
. Finally, the property is preserved under increasing limits because homotopy classes are compactly supported and all the objects are CW-complexes. Hence
P(\sigma)
is true for every
\sigma\in S^{\mathcal{G}}
different from
\sigma_{0}
, and
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}
is contractible by Whitehead’s theorem on CW-complexes. Finally, if
S^{\mathcal{G}}
has a maximum
\sigma_{max}
,
K^{\mathcal{G}}=K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma_{max}}\cup st(\sigma_{max})
, and otherwise
K^{\mathcal{G}}=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\sigma\in S^{\mathcal{G}}}K^{\mathcal{G}}_{<\sigma}
. Thus
K^{\mathcal{G}}
is contractible by the same arguments as above.
∎

8.
Adding arbitrary fixed subgroups

This concluding section generalizes Theorem
6.1
, starting with the following consequence of Proposition
3.1
:

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
H<A_{\Gamma}
be finitely generated. There exists
\mathcal{K}=(K_{1},\dots,K_{L})
a family of cyclic subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
such that
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{H\}^{t})
is a subgroup of finite index of
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})
.

Proof.

Fix a generating system
H=\left\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\right\rangle
and set for
(K_{i})_{1\leq i\leq L}
the family of all cyclic subgroups generated by group elements of the form
a_{i}
, or
a_{i}a_{j}
with
i<j
. As every
K_{i}
is a subgroup of
H
, an outer automorphism that is inner on
H
is necessarily inner on every
K_{i}
, proving the inclusion
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{H\}^{t})\subseteq\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})
.

Besides, given
[\varphi]\in\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})
, the automorphism
\varphi
maps each
a_{i}
and each
a_{i}a_{j}
with
i<j
to conjugates of themselves. In particular
\ell(\varphi(a_{i}))=\ell(a_{i})
and
\ell(\varphi(a_{i}a_{j}))=\ell(a_{i}a_{j})
.

Now apply Proposition
3.1
to
X=\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
and the family
(\varphi(a_{1}),\dots,\varphi(a_{k}))
. The bound
M
depends only on the
a_{i}
and not on
\varphi
, and there exists
x_{\varphi}\in X^{(0)}
such that
d(x_{\varphi},\varphi(a_{i})x_{\varphi})\leq M
for every
i
. As
X
has only one orbit of vertices, there is a group element
g_{\varphi}
such that
x_{id}=g_{\varphi}x_{\varphi}
. Thus, conjugating
\varphi
by
g_{\varphi}^{-1}
, pick a representative
\varphi_{c}
for each class
c\in\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})
satisfying
d(x_{id},\varphi_{c}(a_{i})x_{id})\leq M
for every
i
.

Finally, let
B
be the finite vertex set of the closed
\ell^{1}
-ball in
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
of radius
M
centered at
x_{id}
, and consider the map:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})

\displaystyle\rightarrow B^{k}

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle\mapstochar\rightarrow(\varphi_{c}(a_{i})x_{id})_{1\leq i\leq k}

If two classes
c,c^{\prime}
have the same image under this map, then
\varphi_{c}^{-1}\circ\varphi_{c^{\prime}}
fixes every
a_{i}
because the action is free. Since,
H
is generated by the
a_{i}
, this means that
c^{-1}c^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{H\}^{t})
. Hence
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{H\}^{t})
has at most
\left|B^{k}\right|
left cosets in
\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}^{t})
.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(A_{\Delta_{1}},\dots,A_{\Delta_{m}})
be a collection of standard subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
and let
\mathcal{H}=(H_{1},\dots,H_{n})
be a finite collection of arbitrary finitely generated subgroups of
A_{\Gamma}
. There exists a contractible subcomplex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}^{t}}
of the spine of untwisted outer space
K_{\Gamma}
on which
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})
acts properly and cocompactly. In particular, this group is of type VF.

Proof.

For each
H_{j}
, set
\mathcal{K}_{j}
as in Lemma
8.1
, and let
\mathcal{K}
be the union of the
\mathcal{K}_{j}
. The group
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}^{t})=\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})\cap\displaystyle\bigcap_{j}\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\{H_{j}\}^{t})
is a finite-index subgroup of
\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G})\cap\displaystyle\bigcap_{j}\mathsf{Out}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{K}_{j}^{t})=\mathcal{U}(A_{\Gamma};\,\mathcal{G},\mathcal{K}^{t})
. Hence, it acts properly and cocompactly on the contractible subcomplex
K^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{K}^{t}}
of
K_{\Gamma}
from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

Efficacy and safety of periodontal initial therapy combined with intralesional betamethasone sodium phosphate injection under local anesthesia in managing erosive oral lichen planus: study protocol for a multi-center, split-mouth, randomized self-controlled trial
Authors: Huiwen Chen, Liran Shi, Qianhui Shang, Zihao Wei, Qiaozhen Yang, Yining He, Chenxi Li, Zengtong Zhou, Guoyao Tang, Hongying Sun, Gulinuer Awuti, Zhongchen Song, Linjun Shi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
IntroductionErosive oral lichen planus (OLP) and periodontitis frequently coexist and may worsen each other. This study will evaluate the effectiveness and safety of combining periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia with intralesional betamethasone sodium phosphate (BSP) injection in patients with erosive OLP, particularly in enhancing lesion healing.Methods and analysisThis is a multicenter, randomized, split-mouth, self-controlled trial. We will recruit 29 adults from five tertiary centers in China. Eligible participants are adults (aged ≥ 18 years) diagnosed with erosive gingival OLP accompanied by generalized periodontitis (stage II–IV). Within each participant, sides will be randomly allocated to a test side (periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia plus intralesional BSP) and a control side (intralesional BSP alone). Independent outcome assessors and statisticians will be strictly blinded to ensure objectivity of the assessments. The follow-up will last 6 months, with assessments at the 2nd, 4th, 8th, 12th, and 24th weeks. The primary outcome is the reduction in erosive lesion size at the 12th week. Secondary outcomes will include the complete healing rate, OLP clinical severity scores, visual analog score of pain, periodontal parameters, and complications.DiscussionThis protocol introduces an innovative method for managing erosive OLP accompanied by periodontitis by integrating periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia with corticosteroid injections. It aims to offer a more effective and patient-centric multidisciplinary treatment strategy. The utilization of local anesthetics will enhance patient comfort and optimize adherence. Background

Oral lichen planus (OLP) is a common chronic inflammatory disease of the oral mucosa [1]. Clinically, OLP is classified into two types: non-erosive and erosive [2]. The chronic erosive lesions of OLP carry a risk of malignant transformation, leading the World Health Organization to classify it as a potentially malignant disorder [3]. A systematic review revealed that the pooled proportion of malignant transformation in OLP was 2.28% (95% CI, 1.49 to 3.20) [4]. The chronic inflammatory process is believed to drive this risk. Due to the enigmatic etiology and pathogenesis, effective interventions to delay disease progression or reverse its course are lacking [1, 5]. Current clinical management for erosive OLP primarily involves corticosteroids to manage symptoms [1, 2]. Among the available options, betamethasone sodium phosphate (BSP) is an advantageous choice due to its rapid onset of action and slower absorption to prolong the effect compared to other corticosteroids.

Periodontitis is another highly prevalent chronic inflammatory disease, initiated by bacterial biofilms that lead to the progressive destruction of tooth-supporting tissues [6]. Affecting approximately 11% of the global population, it is a significant public health issue [7]. Crucially, some evidence indicated a bidirectional, mutually exacerbating relationship between OLP and periodontitis. The severe pain from erosive OLP compromises a patient’s ability to maintain oral hygiene, which contributes to the development and progression of periodontitis. In turn, the persistent periodontal inflammation creates a microenvironment that is understood to inhibit the healing of OLP lesions, thus establishing a vicious cycle [8, 9].

This establishes a clear rationale that plaque control and reduction of periodontal inflammation may alleviate the clinical symptoms of OLP and accelerate the healing of erosive lesions [10, 11]. However, a significant clinical barrier remains: conventional periodontal therapy, including scaling and debridement, is often too painful for patients with tender erosive lesions, leading to treatment refusal. Using local anesthesia offers a practical solution to overcome this pain barrier [12, 13]. Nevertheless, clinical trials evaluating the synergistic effect of periodontal therapy under local anesthesia combined with intralesional corticosteroids are currently lacking.

Therefore, this study aims to conduct a multi-center, split-mouth, randomized self-controlled trial. We hypothesize that periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia combined with intralesional BSP injection therapy will not only improve patient comfort during treatment but also enhance the healing of erosive mucosal lesions compared to intralesional BSP therapy alone.

Methods

Trial objectives

The primary aim

To evaluate the efficacy of periodontal initial treatment under local anesthesia combined with intralesional corticosteroids therapy in improving the clinical outcome of erosive OLP compared with intralesional corticosteroids therapy alone.

Secondary aim

Evaluation of the therapeutic safety of periodontal initial treatment under local anesthesia combined with intralesional corticosteroids therapy compared with conventional intralesional corticosteroids therapy for erosive OLP.

Trial design

This study is a multicenter, randomized, split-mouth, self-controlled clinical trial. The intervention (periodontal initial treatment) follows the current international standards for periodontitis treatment. The trial will not require additional invasive procedures beyond routine clinical visits. Patients or public involvement will not be involved in the design, conduct, or reporting of this trial.

Using a randomized, split-mouth design, each participant’s oral cavity will be divided along the midline into a left and a right side. Through a computer-generated randomization, one side will be assigned as the test side and the other will subsequently be designated as the control side. The test side will receive periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia combined with intralesional BSP. The control side will receive intralesional BSP alone.

Follow-up will last 6 months. Assessments will occur at the 2nd, 4th, 8th, 12th, and 24th week post-intervention. The primary endpoint of this study is set at the 12-week visit. Following the primary outcome assessment at the 12th week, the control side will then receive delayed periodontal initial treatment under local anesthesia for ethical reasons. Consequently, the period from the 12th week to the 24th week is defined as a prospective observational follow-up phase to assess long-term outcomes.

Screening overview and recruitment

To achieve the target sample size, recruitment will occur at the outpatient clinics of all participating centers. During routine care, attending clinicians will identify potentially eligible patients and refer them to the site’s research team. Following this referral, a qualified clinical investigator, with full access to the participant’s complete medical and dental history, will confirm eligibility. Eligibility will be systematically assessed and documented to ensure each individual has a confirmed diagnosis of both bilateral erosive oral lichen planus and co-existing periodontitis and that they meet all inclusion and no exclusion criteria. After confirming all entry criteria, the investigator or a designated team member will formally enroll the participant, obtaining written informed consent prior to any randomization or trial-related procedures. The Coordinating Center will monitor enrollment numbers monthly, and a contingency plan will be activated if recruitment falls behind schedule.

Data handling for withdrawn participants

The reasons, the date of the last treatment completed, and any valuable data collected up to the date of withdrawal will be recorded. If participants withdraw from this study due to adverse events (AEs), the researcher will arrange follow-up until the AEs are properly handled. The primary analysis will follow an intention-to-treat (ITT) principle with multiple imputation for missing data. A per-protocol set (PPS) will also be conducted as a sensitivity analysis. No replacement participants will be recruited.

Participants management

To enhance participant compliance with the study, several strategies will be implemented. During the informed consent process, the full study timeline, the schedule of follow-up visits, and the importance of completing the study will be thoroughly explained to each participant. A dedicated study coordinator is assigned, and participants will be provided with direct contact information to address any questions or concerns and to facilitate appointment scheduling. Reminder telephone calls or text messages will be made 1 day prior to each scheduled visit. To accommodate participants’ personal schedules and minimize their burden, flexible scheduling for follow-up appointments will be offered whenever possible.

Trial intervention

The test side will be treated with intralesional BSP injection and periodontal initial treatment under local anesthesia, while the control side will be treated with intralesional BSP injection alone. For ethical reasons, the control side will undergo full-mouth periodontal initial therapy at the end of the primary endpoint assessment.

Intralesional BSP procedure

All participants will receive intralesional BSP injections following their baseline evaluation. Prior to the injection, the mucosal surface surrounding the lesion will be disinfected with an iodine tincture to minimize the risk of infection and ensure aseptic conditions during the procedure. As shown in Fig. 2, each erosive lesion will be treated with a single submucosal injection of 1.4 mg (0.35 mL) betamethasone (BSP; Sinopharm Group Rongsheng Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., China). This evidence-based dosage, equivalent to 7 mg of triamcinolone acetonide, is based on the protocol by Liu et al. [14]. Using a 5-mL syringe with a 25-gauge needle, the injection will be administered from the adjacent non-erosive mucosa at a 45-degree angle. The needle will be advanced to a submucosal depth sufficient to raise a small wheal. Aspiration will be performed to prevent intravascular injection, and sterile gauze will be applied to the site post-injection to control bleeding. After the injection, participants will be observed closely for 20 min to monitor for any immediate adverse reactions (e.g., swelling, pain, systemic symptoms).Fig. 2Each erosive lesion will be treated with a single submucosal injection

Periodontal initial therapy

A periodontist with extensive clinical experience will perform periodontal initial therapy in the posterior teeth corresponding to erosive mucosal lesions. Before the periodontal therapy, a 0.12% chlorhexidine rinse will be applied for intraoral antisepsis. The procedure will include full-mouth scaling and root planning by ultrasonic instruments (EMS, Master® Piezon 700, Swiss) and manual Gracey curettes (Hu-Friedy, Chicago, USA) under local anesthesia (Primacaine™ adrenaline 1/100,000, Produits Dentaires Pierre Rolland, France). Each tooth that has undergone scaling will be polished with a rubber cup and polishing paste [15]. All test sides will undergo periodontal initial treatment on the day of intervention, followed by therapy for oral mucosal lesions.

Oral hygiene education and adjuncts

All participants will receive thorough professional oral hygiene education with demonstrations (manual toothbrushes, dental floss, or interdental brushes).

We will recommend routine rinsing with a 1% sodium bicarbonate solution throughout the study period. To ensure consistency in the study’s outcomes, they will also be instructed to avoid the use of other systemic medications that could interfere with the treatment protocol.

Training and calibration

To ensure procedural consistency across all participating centers, all investigators underwent a formal training and calibration process before the study commenced.

The physicians responsible for periodontal measurements and periodontal initial treatment are required to be experienced periodontists. Before the study commences, all participating periodontists from each center will be required to participate in an off-line in-person training program organized by the principal investigator. The training curriculum will cover a detailed review of the study protocol and an explanation of the operational techniques for periodontal scaling and root planning. A standardized procedural checklist will be distributed to ensure high consistency in every step across all periodontists and centers. This checklist will detail critical parameters, including the specific standards and examination methods for periodontal measurements, specific ultrasonic scaler power settings, correct curette angulation, and appropriate scaling force. Furthermore, they will perform calibration exercises on non-study patients under the supervision of a calibrated “gold standard” examiner. Similarly, clinicians responsible for administering the intralesional injections were also calibrated to ensure uniform technique; the specific details of their calibration protocol are described in the “Trial intervention” section.

All assessors will also undergo calibration exercises to standardize their evaluation methods. Inter-rater reliability will be evaluated using the Intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) for continuous data and the weighted Kappa statistic for ordinal scores. A pre-specified high level of agreement (e.g., ICC > 0.80, Kappa > 0.80) must be achieved.

Researchers responsible for oral hygiene instruction will receive standardized training in oral hygiene practices, including oral hygiene instructions, the use of plaque disclosing agents, and demonstrations of brushing, flossing, and interdental brushing techniques.

Outcome measures

Outcome measures will be conducted at the 2nd, 4th, 8th, 12th, and 24th week post-intervention. To ensure consistency and reliability, all assessments at each time point will be conducted by two independent, calibrated central assessors who are blinded to treatment allocation and are not involved in any clinical aspect of the trial.

Primary outcome measure (assessed at 12th week)

The primary outcome measure is the reduction in the size of the erosion area (mm2) of the lesion after 3 months of intervention, compared to the control side. The area of OLP erosion will be quantified using a digital intraoral scanner (3Shape TRIOS 4, 3Shape, Copenhagen, Denmark). The scanner will be calibrated according to the manufacturer’s guidelines.

The safety of all participants is paramount. A comprehensive plan is in place for the monitoring, documentation, and management of all AEs.

AE monitoring

At each study visit, the investigator will proactively question participants about any potential AEs and perform clinical examinations. All AEs, whether reported by the participant or observed by the study staff, will be documented. Based on the interventions, potential AEs to be monitored include, but are not limited to: related to intralesional BSP injections (pain, swelling, bruising, or hemorrhage at the injection site; secondary infection; mucosal/submucosal atrophy; hypopigmentation or hyperpigmentation; sterile abscess; tissue necrosis); related to periodontal initial therapy (post-operative pain or soreness; root/tooth hypersensitivity; gingival bleeding; localized gingival swelling); and related to local anesthetics (allergic reactions; transient syncope or dizziness).

Serious adverse events (SAEs)

An SAE will be any event that results in death, is life-threatening, requires inpatient hospitalization or prolongation of existing hospitalization, results in persistent or significant disability/incapacity, or is a congenital anomaly/birth defect. Investigators will report SAEs to the Institutional Review Board/Ethics Committee (IRB/EC) within 24 h of awareness, followed by a detailed report.

Management and stopping rules

The investigator will ensure that any participant experiencing an AE receives appropriate medical care until the event resolves or stabilizes. Management may include observation, symptomatic treatment, or withdrawal from the study. We will withdraw individual participants for unacceptable AEs. The investigator, in consultation with the IRB/EC, may pause or terminate the study if SAE rates are unexpectedly high or if new information changes the risk-benefit profile.

Sample size

The sample size for this multi-center, split-mouth, randomized self-controlled trial was calculated based on its primary endpoint: the reduction in the oral erosion lesion area. The primary hypothesis of this study is directional (one-sided), as we aim to test if combined therapy is superior to monotherapy. According to previous literature [11], the mean reduction in the oral erosion lesion area with glucocorticoid local injections alone was 10.3 ± 13.2 mm2, while the mean reduction with periodontal initial therapy combined with glucocorticoid local injections was 19.1 ± 12.8 mm2. Using PASS 15.0 statistical software (NCSS LLC, Kaysville, UT, USA) for analysis, with a Type I error α of 0.025 and a power (1-β) of 0.8 (one-sided), and setting Δ = 0, the required sample size was determined to be 23 participants. To account for a potential 20% dropout rate, the final target enrollment was set to 29 participants.

Assignment of interventions

Randomization

Patient numbers (e.g., patients numbered 1–29) will be pre-assigned in advance by a statistician based on the sample size calculation. Participants enrolled in the study will be sequentially assigned numbers according to the order of their visits. The oral mucosal erosive lesions in OLP participants will be divided along the midline into a left and a right side. Through computer-generated randomization, one side will be assigned as the test side and the other will subsequently be designated the control side. The test side will receive periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia combined with an intralesional BSP injection, while the control side will receive the intralesional BSP injection alone.

Due to the nature of the study, blinding of both researchers and participants was not feasible. Consequently, to mitigate potential bias, a rigorous protocol for allocation concealment and blinded outcome assessment will be implemented.

Allocation concealment

The randomization sequence will be concealed using sealed, opaque envelopes. The allocation will be revealed after baseline assessments. The clinician performing periodontal therapy will open the envelope, who is separate from the outcome assessors. Members of the study staff will not have access to the envelopes prior to assignment.

Blinded outcome assessment

All outcome assessments were conducted by trained assessors who were strictly blinded to the treatment allocation for each side of the oral cavity. Unblinding situations

Procedures for unblinding are defined for two distinct scenarios. First, planned study-wide unblinding will occur only after the final database is locked and all primary statistical analyses are complete. Second, emergency unblinding for an individual participant may be necessary in the event of a Serious Adverse Event (SAE).

Data collection, management, and analysis

Overview of data collection

The primary data will be stored and retrieved at the Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine. Upon obtaining the original participant data, researchers will store the data via electronic Case Report Forms (eCRFs) in Electronic Data Capture (EDC) systems. Participant names and other identifying details will not be included in any auxiliary study data electronic files. All documents will be securely stored, and access will be restricted to researchers and authorized personnel. Researchers will archive the original documents and informed consent forms for each participant.

For each enrolled case, the eCRF must be completed in the EDC system as required. We will utilize the MedCohort EDC system (MedCohort) to collect and manage data from the Hospital Information System. MedCohort, an independently developed clinical research information management platform by the Ninth People’s Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, enables the automatic synchronization of highly structured and standardized data, including patients’ diagnostic and therapeutic information, laboratory and imaging results, and follow-up records, during clinical data collection.

Data security and submission of monitoring reports

Each participant will receive a unique study ID, which will be the sole identifier used on all research data. A master list linking these IDs to personal identifying information will be securely and separately stored by the Site Principal Investigator, with access restricted to designated members of the research team. This ensures that the personnel responsible for statistical analysis will only be provided with a pseudonymized dataset, preventing direct identification of participants during their work. Given the low-risk nature of this study, data security and monitoring reports will be submitted to the Ethics Committee every 12 months.

Data retention

All researchers must maintain complete clinical records and research data for all participants participating in this study. Intraoral clinical photographs should be archived electronically and securely stored along with participants’ clinical records and research documentation. The study coordinator will collect copies of all electronic files. All study-related records will be retained for at least 5 years after the study’s completion.

Demographic data and analysis

Descriptive statistical methods were used to summarize the demographic and clinical characteristics of the study population. Continuous variables were described using mean ± standard deviation or median with interquartile range (IQR), while categorical variables were presented as frequencies. The Kolmogorov–Smirnov (K-S) test was employed to assess whether the clinical indicators at baseline and reevaluation followed a normal distribution. Levene’s test was used to check the homogeneity of variance.

Analysis of efficacy outcomes

The statistical analysis of efficacy is structured to assess a single primary endpoint at the 12th week, complemented by the analysis of several secondary endpoints at interim time points.

Primary efficacy analysis: The primary efficacy endpoint is the reduction in the size of the erosion area (mm2) of the lesion at the 12-week visit. The primary analysis will be conducted by comparing the values between the test side and the control side. A paired t-test or its non-parametric equivalent, the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, will be used for this comparison, with the choice of test based on the assessment of data normality.Secondary efficacy analysis: The interim assessments at 2nd, 4th, 8th, 12th week, and the follow-up assessment at 24th week are designed for the analysis of various secondary outcomes. Specifically, data from these time points will be used to compare the healing rate of oral mucosal erosive lesions between groups (using the chi-squared test or Fisher’s exact test) and to analyze differences in the absolute change from baseline for the patient’s pain VAS score and OLP clinical damage score (using t-tests or Wilcoxon tests). Furthermore, changes in periodontal parameters will be assessed at 4th, 8th, 12th, and 24th week. Throughout this period, all AEs will be recorded and summarized by frequency and incidence rate at each visit to evaluate safety.Supportive longitudinal analysis: To supplement the primary analysis and fully utilize the data collected at interim visits, a supportive analysis using a mixed model for repeated measures will be performed on the primary efficacy endpoint. Sensitivity analyses

To evaluate the robustness of the primary outcome, a series of sensitivity analyses will be conducted. The main analysis will be supplemented with a multivariate linear regression to adjust for potential confounding variables (age, gender, baseline erosion area, periodontitis stage, baseline VAS score, smoking history, systemic medical history, and concomitant medications), and a repeated-measures mixed-effects model will assess the dynamic effect of the intervention. Furthermore, to robustly handle missing data and minimize potential bias, a key sensitivity analysis will be performed using multiple imputation. Additional analyses will include repeating the primary model on the PPS and assessing the potential influence of the study center.

Statistical software and general requirements

Primary efficacy analysis in this study is one-tailed, with P < 0.025 considered statistically significant. For all other secondary and exploratory outcomes, we will use two-sided tests, with statistical significance set at P < 0.05. Statistical analyses and graphical representations will be performed using SPSS version 25.0 (Armonk, NY, USA: IBM Corp.).

Monitoring

This multi-center trial will be managed by a structured framework involving a coordinating center, participating sites, and dedicated committees to ensure protocol adherence, data integrity, and uniform conduct across all locations.

Coordinating center and principal investigator (PI)

The study is coordinated by the Ninth People’s Hospital affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, which acts as the Coordinating Center. The PI at this center holds the ultimate responsibility for the entire trial.

Participating centers and co-investigators

Each participating hospital will have a designated co-investigator. They are responsible for the day-to-day conduct of the trial at their specific centers, including participant recruitment and enrollment, adherence to the protocol, local data collection, and timely reporting of AEs to the coordinating center.

Steering committee (SC)

The SC will be chaired by the PI and will include co-investigators from the participating centers. A trial statistician will also be included. The SC will meet quarterly to oversee the overall conduct and progress of the trial. Its key responsibilities include: monitoring recruitment rates; ensuring adherence to the study protocol across all sites; reviewing safety data; and approving any proposed amendments to the protocol before they are submitted to the Ethics Committees.

Endpoint adjudication committee (EAC)

To ensure unbiased and standardized evaluation of the primary endpoint, an independent EAC will be established. The EAC will consist of two experienced clinicians with expertise in oral medicine who are independent of the trial.

Data management team

A central Data Management Team, based at the coordinating center and led by the PI and Clinical Research Coordinator (CRC), will oversee all data-related activities. This team is responsible for designing and maintaining the central eCRF, performing data validation and quality checks on data submitted from all centers. The CRC will review the eCRFs of the first few participants from each site to ensure accuracy and completeness.

Independent oversight

Researchers who have received Good Clinical Practice–related training will conduct regular monitoring, including on-site visits. This involves the review of source documents, clinical records, and informed consent forms to ensure data quality and compliance with the protocol. The protocol is approved by the lead Ethics Committee of the Ninth People’s Hospital, Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, and subsequently by the local Ethics Committee of each participating center. The trial will also be supervised by the National Clinical Research Center for Oral Diseases.

Data monitoring committee (DMC)

A formal DMC is not deemed necessary for this study due to its low-risk profile. Given the minimal anticipated risks, the study duration is limited, and the endpoints do not involve severe morbidity or mortality. Safety oversight, including the review of all adverse events, will be managed directly by the SC. They will report any significant findings to the IRB/REC as required.

Discussion

This study protocol proposes a randomized, self-controlled trial to evaluate a novel therapeutic strategy for erosive OLP. While previous studies have examined the benefits of periodontal care for gingival OLP, to our knowledge, no randomized controlled trial has specifically integrated periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia with corticosteroid injections for the more challenging erosive form of the disease. Therefore, this trial is designed to fill a critical gap in the literature by addressing a key treatment barrier. The core novelty of our approach lies in combining periodontal initial therapy under local anesthesia with intralesional corticosteroid injections simultaneously. This design directly addresses a critical clinical challenge: the pain that prevents erosive OLP patients from receiving necessary periodontal care. Our central hypothesis is that by interrupting the “vicious cycle” where OLP-related pain hinders oral hygiene, leading to plaque accumulation and exacerbated periodontal inflammation that in turn worsens OLP lesions and delays erosion healing, we can significantly improve both mucosal and periodontal outcomes. This study aims to provide high-quality evidence for a more comprehensive, patient-centered treatment model.

The mechanistic justification for this integrated approach is compelling, though not yet fully elucidated. It is well established that dental plaque biofilm, harboring periodontopathogens like Porphyromonas gingivalis, serves as a constant inflammatory trigger [20, 21]. We postulate that the resulting bacterial toxins and pro-inflammatory cytokines contribute directly to the inflammatory microenvironment of adjacent OLP lesions, perpetuating the disease state. Therefore, by mechanically removing this microbial load through scaling and root planing, made tolerable for patients via local anesthesia, our intervention is expected to down-regulate local inflammatory pathways, potentially reducing OLP lesion severity and pain. This aligns with previous findings where basic periodontal care improved outcomes in gingival OLP [10, 22], but our study advances this by targeting the more challenging erosive OLP population and using a more rigorous design.

The study has several strengths that contribute to its significance. The randomized, self-controlled design served to minimize inter-individual variability and thus enhance the reliability of the findings. A comprehensive range of outcome measures was employed, including objective assessments of erosive lesion size, pain scores, and periodontal clinical parameters, thereby providing a robust evaluation of the intervention’s efficacy. Moreover, the utilization of sophisticated instruments, such as digital intraoral scanners, is intended to enhance the precision and reproducibility of measurements, thereby strengthening the methodological rigor. Additionally, the use of the EDC system streamlines data collection and management, ensures accurate, real-time recording of patient information while minimizing information bias. This facilitates efficient data monitoring and improved data integrity throughout the study, further strengthening the validity of the results.

While the study protocol integrated key ethical safeguards reflecting a patient-centric approach (primarily by using local anesthesia to mitigate procedural risk and discomfort), it must be acknowledged that there are notable limitations which require consideration. The first limitation of the study is that the 12-week follow-up period may not fully capture the long-term benefits or recurrence rates of the combined therapy. While the multicenter design strengthens the generalizability of the results, the sample size remains relatively small, which could still limit the broader applicability of the findings. Additionally, the exclusion of patients with severe erosive OLP or systemic conditions, while necessary for protocol adherence, restricts the generalizability of the results to these specific subgroups. The split-mouth design, while accounting for patient variability, poses a risk of a “carry-across effect” from systemic or salivary mediators. Although prior clinical outcomes indicate this effect may be minimal, it is not formally excluded through biomarker analysis. Future research should therefore utilize larger cohorts, longer follow-up periods, and biomarker analysis to address these constraints, with the goal of validating the findings across more diverse populations.

In conclusion, this protocol provides an innovative approach to the management of erosive OLP with co-occurring periodontitis. By focusing on breaking a critical inflammatory cycle through a patient-centric design, this study is expected to provide significant contributions. Its primary significance lies in its potential to establish a more effective, multidisciplinary treatment standard that may reduce patient discomfort and improve long-term compliance. Based on the clinical evidence from this trial, future molecular mechanistic studies could explore the biological mechanisms underlying the clinical improvements. These studies are expected to reveal the unique immune regulatory patterns in the inflammatory microenvironment of OLP with periodontitis.

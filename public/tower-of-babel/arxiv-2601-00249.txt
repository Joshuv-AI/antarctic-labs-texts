A unitary vertex operator algebra arising from the 3 ​ C 3C -algebra
Xiangyu Jiao, Wen Zheng

Abstract
We give an algebraic proof of the unitarity of the vertex operator algebra L ⁡ ( 21 / 22 , 0 ) ⊕ L ⁡ ( 21 / 22 , 8 ) L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8) and of all its irreducible ordinary modules, using a coset realization arising from the 3 ​ C 3C -algebra. Motivated by the structure of the resulting module decomposition, we establish a general result on fusion rules for commutant vertex operator subalgebras within the framework of modular tensor categories. As an application of this general result, we explicitly determine the fusion rules of all irreducible L ⁡ ( 21 / 22 , 0 ) ⊕ L ⁡ ( 21 / 22 , 8 ) L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8) -modules.



1
Introduction

The definition of a unitary vertex operator algebra was first introduced in
[
7
]
. Roughly speaking, a vertex operator algebra (VOA for short) is called unitary if it is equipped with a positive definite Hermitian form that is compatible with the vertex operator structure. There are two main motivations for introducing this notion. One arises from its close relationship with unitary representations of infinite dimensional Lie algebras, while the second arises from its fundamental role in relating the algebraic formulation of two-dimensional conformal field theory via vertex operator algebras to the analytic approach based on conformal nets. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
for a detailed discussion. For other approaches to unitary VOAs, see B. Gui’s works
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
and the references therein.

It was shown in
[
7
]
that many well-known rational VOAs, such as the unitary series of Virasoro VOAs and lattice VOAs associated to positive definite even lattices, are unitary VOAs. In
[
8
]
, preunitary VOAs with central charge
c<1
were classified, yielding a one to one correspondence with the classification of conformal nets with
c<1

[
23
]
. In the same work, it was proved there that all such preunitary vertex operator algebras are unitary except for two cases
L(c_{9},0)\oplus L(c_{9},h_{1,7}^{9})
and
L(c_{27},0)\oplus L(c_{27},h_{1,11}^{27})\oplus L(c_{27},h_{1,19}^{27})\oplus L(c_{27},h_{1,29}^{27})
,
whose unitarity remained open from a purely vertex operator algebraic perspective. To bridge these algebraic and analytic classifications, Gui
[
20
]
established a fundamental relation between unitary VOA extensions and Q-systems
[
29
]
in
C^{*}
-tensor categories. This framework allowed him to prove that the two exceptional VOAs above are indeed unitary, thereby completing the classification of unitary VOAs with
c<1
. See also
[
2
]
.
Moreover, Gui’s work also shows that all such VOAs are completely unitary
[
20
]
, which in particular implies that all their irreducible ordinary modules are unitary modules.

The main purpose of the present paper is twofold. First, we give an independent and purely algebraic proof of the unitarity of the vertex operator algebra
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
and of all its irreducible ordinary modules. Our approach is based on a concrete coset realization arising from the
3C
-algebra
[
27
]
. Second, motivated by a structural feature observed in the explicit decomposition of the
3C
-algebra, we establish a general result on fusion rules for coset vertex operator subalgebras within the framework of modular tensor categories, building in particular on the methods developed in

[
11
]
.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we briefly recall the construction of the
3C
-algebra and its irreducible modules following
[
27
]
.
In Section 3, we review the notions of unitary VOAs and unitary modules introduced in
[
7
]
, and then we use the concrete construction of
3C
-algebra to show that the VOA
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
and all its irreducible modules are unitary.
Section 4 is devoted to fusion rules: after recalling necessary notions such as the Kac-Wakimoto set and results from
[
11
]
, we establish our result on fusion rules for commutant subalgebras. As a corollary, we derive the fusion rules of irreducible
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
-modules.

We assume that the reader is familiar with the basic theory of vertex operator algebras (cf.
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
26
]
) and modular tensor categories (cf.
[
13
,
24
]
).

A vertex operator algebra

Rationality and the

-cofiniteness condition ensure good finiteness properties of modules and are closely related to the modularity of characters. They also lead to many important results in VOA theory.

3
Unitarity of the VOA
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
and its modules

In this section, first we shall briefly recall the definitions of unitary VOA and unitary modules from
[
7
]
. Then we show that the VOA
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
is unitary and all its irreducible modules are unitary modules.
In the following we only consider the vertex operator algebra
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
of
CFT-type
, i.e.
V_{n}=0,n<0
and
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}{\bf 1}
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
be a vertex operator algebra. An anti-linear automorphism
\phi
of
V
is
an anti-linear isomorphism (as anti-linear map)
\phi:V\to V
such
that
\phi(\bf 1)=\bf 1,\phi(\omega)=\omega
and

\phi(u_{n}v)=\phi(u)_{n}\phi(v)
for any
u,v\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
be a vertex operator algebra and
\phi:V\to V
be an anti-linear involution, i.e. an anti-linear automorphism of order
2
. The pair
(V,\phi)
is called a unitary vertex operator algebra if there exists a positive definite Hermitian form
(,):V\times V\to\mathbb{C}

which is
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the first vector and anti-
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the second vector such that the following invariant property holds:

(Y(e^{zL(1)}(-z^{-2})^{L(0)}a,z^{-1})u,v)=(u,Y(\phi(a),z)v)

for any
a,u,v\in V
, where
L(n)
is defined by
Y(\omega,z)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}L(n)z^{-n-2}
.

Next we give the definition of a unitary module as in
[
7
]
.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
(V,Y,{\bf 1},\omega)
be a vertex operator algebra and
\phi
an anti-linear involution of
V
. An ordinary
V
-module
(M,Y_{M})
is called a unitary
V
-module if there exists a positive definite
Hermitian form
(,)_{M}:M\times M\to\mathbb{C}
which is
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the first vector and anti-
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear on the second vector such that the following invariant property holds:

(Y_{M}(e^{zL(1)}(-z^{-2})^{L(0)}a,z^{-1})w_{1},w_{2})_{M}=(w_{1},Y_{M}(\phi(a),z)w_{2})_{M}

for
a\in V
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in M
.

We shall frequently use the following result (Corollary 2.8 in
[
7
]
):

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(V,\phi)
be a unitary VOA and
(U,Y,{\bf 1},\omega^{\prime})
be a vertex operator subalgebra of
V
such that
\phi(\omega^{\prime})=\omega^{\prime}
. Then the coset
(U^{c},\phi|_{U^{c}})
VOA is also a unitary VOA.

We now turn to prove the unitarity of
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

The VOA
L(21/22,0)\oplus L(21/22,8)
is unitary.

Proof.

Let
L
be a positive definite even lattice. Then the lattice VOA

is unitary and the anti-linear involution
\phi:V_{L}\to V_{L}
is given by:

\alpha_{1}(-n_{1})\cdots\alpha_{k}(-n_{k})\otimes e^{\alpha}\mapsto(-1)^{k}\alpha_{1}(-n_{1})\cdots\alpha_{k}(-n_{k})\otimes e^{-\alpha},

see
[
7

.
In particular, the lattice VOA

is unitary, and the conformal vector defined in equation (

. Then by (

3.4
, the associated coset VOA

is unitary with the anti-involution

From Lemma 3.32 in

∎

Next we show that all irreducible

and

4
Fusion rules

In this section, instead of computing the fusion rules of irreducible

First we recall several notions and results from

.

. Then for any

, we have
J_{i}=J
and the set
\{M^{(i,\alpha)}|i\in I,\alpha\in J\}
is the complete set of inequivalent simple objects in
{\mathcal{C}}_{2}.

Proof.

Since

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, for any
i\in I
, the categorical dimension
\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{i}
equals the categorical dimension
\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(i,1)}
.

Proof.

First, by Lemma 4.1 in
[
11
]
, we have
V\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)})
is a simple object in
({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{V}
. Since
({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{V}
is a fusion category, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{V}(V\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)}),M^{i})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)},\sum_{\alpha\in J}W^{\alpha}\otimes M^{(i,\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)},W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)}\right),

so we get
V\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)})
is a simple object in
({\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2})_{V}^{0}
and
M^{i}\cong V\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)})
as a

Thus,

\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{i}=\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}V\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\otimes{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)})=\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{1}}W^{1}\cdot\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(i,1)}=\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(i,1)}.

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, for any
i,j,k\in I
, we have

N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}=N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}.

Proof.

By Proposition 2.9 in
[

]

N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}\leq N_{W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)},W^{1}\otimes M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{k}}=N_{W^{1}\otimes M^{(i,1)},W^{1}\otimes M^{(j,1)}}^{W^{1}\otimes M^{(k,1)}}=N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}.

(11)

Moreover, applying (
11
) and Lemma
4.5
we have

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{i}\cdot\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}(M^{i}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}\left(\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}M^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{k}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(k,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}M^{(k,1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(M^{(i,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(j,1)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(i,1)}\cdot\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(j,1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{i}\cdot\dim_{{\mathcal{C}}}M^{j}.

N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}=N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}.

Lemma 4.8

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, for any

, we have

M^{(i,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(1,\alpha)}\cong M^{(i,\alpha)}.

Proof.

First, by an argument similar to that in the proof of Lemma

\displaystyle\cong

Now we prove our main result:

Theorem 4.9
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
4.4
, for any
i,j,k\in I
, and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in J
, we have the fusion rules

N_{M^{(i,\alpha)},M^{(j,\beta)}}^{M^{(k,\gamma)}}=N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}N_{W^{\alpha},W^{\beta}}^{W^{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Applying the above several lemmas, we have

\displaystyle M^{(i,\alpha)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(j,\beta)}

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle(M^{(i,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(1,\alpha)})\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(M^{(j,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(1,\beta)})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle(M^{(i,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(j,1)})\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}(M^{(1,\alpha)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(1,\beta)})

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{(i,1)},M^{(j,1)}}^{M^{(k,1)}}M^{(k,1)}\right)\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in J}N_{M^{(1,\alpha)},M^{(1,\beta)}}^{M^{(1,\gamma)}}M^{(1,\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in I}N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}M^{(k,1)}\right)\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in J}N_{W^{\alpha},W^{\beta}}^{W^{\gamma}}M^{(1,\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\\
\gamma\in J\end{subarray}}N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}N_{W^{\alpha},W^{\beta}}^{W^{\gamma}}\left(M^{(k,1)}\boxtimes_{{\mathcal{C}}_{2}}M^{(1,\gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I\\
\gamma\in J\end{subarray}}N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}N_{W^{\alpha},W^{\beta}}^{W^{\gamma}}M^{(k,\gamma)}.

Hence
N_{M^{(i,\alpha)},M^{(j,\beta)}}^{M^{(k,\gamma)}}=N_{M^{i},M^{j}}^{M^{k}}N_{W^{\alpha},W^{\beta}}^{W^{\gamma}}.

∎

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Model and the quantitative supercritical problem

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph. In Bernoulli site percolation
with parameter
p\in(0,1)
, each vertex is declared open with probability
p
independently,
and closed otherwise; we write
\mathbb{P}_{p}
for the resulting product measure.
For a vertex
v\in V
, we write
\{v\leftrightarrow\infty\}
for the event that
v
belongs
to an infinite open cluster.

The critical (site) percolation probability is

p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G):=\sup\Bigl\{p\in[0,1]:\forall v\in V,\ \mathbb{P}_{p}(v\leftrightarrow\infty)=0\Bigr\}.

While
p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)
captures the onset of percolation, many applications require

quantitative
control in the supercritical regime
p>p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)
.
In this note we study disconnection probabilities for an arbitrary (finite or infinite) set of vertices

S\subseteq V
:

\{S\nleftrightarrow\infty\}:=\bigcap_{v\in S}\{v\nleftrightarrow\infty\},\qquad\text{and our goal is to bound }\ \mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty).

1.2.
Relation to effective supercritical estimates

Quantitative bounds of the form
\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)\leq e^{-f(S)}

are closely tied to the “effective supercritical” theory developed in several settings.
For the reader’s convenience, we briefly recall the three reference points mentioned above.

(i) Problem 1.6 in
[
1
]
.

In the (bond) setting and under a heat-kernel assumption, Duminil-Copin–Goswami–Raoufi–Severo–Yadin
prove that there exists
p<1
such that for every finite set
S\subset V
,

\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)\ \leq\ \exp\!\left[-\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{cap}(S)\right],\qquad\mathrm{cap}(S):=\sum_{x\in S}d(x)\,\mathbf{P}_{x}\!\bigl(X_{k}\notin S\ \forall k\geq 1\bigr),

where
(X_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is simple random walk on
G
and
d(x)
is the degree of
x
.
They then ask to
improve
this capacity exponent (Problem 1.6 in
[
1
]
), noting that even on

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
it gives a decay rate of order
\exp(-cr^{d-2})
for a ball of radius
r
, whereas one expects
(surface-order) decay
\exp(-cr^{d-1})
in the supercritical phase.

(ii) Item 8 in
[
2
]
.

Let
Q_{n}(v)
denote the set of minimal cutsets of size
n
separating
v
from
\infty
and set

q_{n}:=\sup_{v\in V}|Q_{n}(v)|
and
\kappa(G):=\sup_{n\geq 1}q_{n}^{1/n}
.
Let
\partial S
be the edge boundary of a finite vertex set
S
, let
|S|_{G}:=\sum_{u\in S}\deg(u)
, and
let the isoperimetric profile be

\psi(n):=\inf\Bigl\{\,|\partial S|:\ S\subset V,\ n\leq|S|_{G}<\infty\,\Bigr\}.

Easo–Severo–Tassion show (Item 8 in
[
2
]
) that whenever
\kappa(G)<\infty
,
the graph admits a
strongly percolating
high-density phase: for every
p\in(1-1/\kappa(G),1]

there exists
c>0
such that

\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)\leq e^{-c\,\psi(|S|_{G})}\quad\text{for all finite }S\subset V,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{p}\bigl(n\leq|C_{v}|<\infty\bigr)\leq e^{-c\,\psi(n)}\quad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

They further note that their work resolves
[
1
, Problem 1.6]
, and that extending such bounds to
all
p\in(p_{c},1]
remains open in general.

(iii) Conjecture 5.1 in
[
3
]
.

Hermon–Hutchcroft formulate a conjectural “isoperimetric” description of the entire supercritical
finite-cluster tail on transitive graphs (Conjecture 5.1 in
[
3
]
): for every
p_{c}<p<1
there exist
constants
c_{p},C_{p}>0
such that

\exp\!\bigl[-C_{p}\,\psi(C_{p}n)\bigr]\ \leq\ \mathbb{P}_{p}\bigl(n\leq|K_{v}|<\infty\bigr)\ \leq\ \exp\!\bigl[-c_{p}\,\psi(c_{p}n)\bigr]\qquad\text{for all }n\geq 1.

In particular, proving the
upper bound
throughout
(p_{c},1)
may be viewed as a far-reaching
extension of the strong high-density bounds in
[
2
]
.

1.3.
Main result: an exponential bound via a packing profile

Our main theorem gives an explicit exponential-type upper bound on

\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)
in terms of a combinatorial packing parameter of
S
.
To state it, we use the
(p,\varepsilon,c)
–packing number
\mathrm{PK}_{p,\varepsilon,c}(S)

(Definition 1.3), which measures how many vertices in
S
can be selected far apart so that each
selected vertex admits (in an iteratively punctured graph) a uniform lower bound
c
on
connecting to infinity together with a controlled comparison between “disconnecting from infinity”
and “disconnecting from a suitable inner boundary.”

Theorem 1.1

(Quantitative supercritical disconnection bound)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph, let
p>p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)
,
and let
S\subset V
be any (finite or infinite) set of vertices.
Then

(1.1)

\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}\in(p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G),\,p)\\
\varepsilon,\delta\in(0,1)\end{subarray}}\left[\frac{\delta\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p_{1}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}}{1-\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p_{1}}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}}\;+\;(1+\delta)\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p_{1}}\right)^{(1-\varepsilon)\,\mathrm{PK}_{p,\delta,\,1-\left(\frac{1-p}{1-p_{1}}\right)^{(1-\varepsilon)}}(S)}\right].

The estimate (
1.1
) makes the exponential behavior transparent:
once
S
contains many well-separated “good” vertices in the sense of the packing
definition, the probability that
none
of
S
connects to infinity decays exponentially
in the packing number.

1.4.
Method: FCA + functional characterization of
p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}

The proof is based on an FCA mechanism:

•

a
functional
input: the local functional
\phi^{p}_{v}(S)
from
[
5
]
,
which characterizes
p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)
through finite-volume connectivity data;

•

a
cutset/local-boundary
step: for each chosen vertex, we compare
disconnection from infinity with disconnection from an inner boundary of a ball
(in an iteratively punctured graph);

•

an
amplification
step: by selecting well-separated vertices (the packing),
we exploit independence to amplify a uniform positive probability of connection to infinity
into an exponential bound for the joint disconnection event.

In particular, the characterization theorem from
[
5
]
ensures that for
p>p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)

one can find vertices whose local functionals are uniformly close to
1
for
all
finite
sets containing them as interior points; this yields a robust lower bound on the probability
of connecting to infinity and supplies the “good” vertices used in the packing construction.

The FCA mechanism was also used in
[
6
]
to establish the inequality

p_{u}^{\mathrm{site}}(G)\geq 1-p_{c}^{\mathrm{site}}(G)
for every infinite, connected, locally finite
planar graph
G
whose set of end–equivalence classes (defined via a Freudenthal
embedding in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
) is countable.

1.5.
Organization

Section 1 recalls the functional
\phi^{p}_{v}(S)
and the characterization of

p^{\mathrm{site}}_{c}(G)
from
[
5
]
, introduces the
(p,\varepsilon,c)
–packing number,
and derives the quantitative bound (
1.1
) from an induction-on-packing argument.
The amplification lemmas are presented in the same section, as they underpin both the packing

2.
A characterization of critical percolation probability
p_{c}^{\mathrm{site}}

This section collects the analytic ingredients used to relate local connectivity
probabilities to the critical threshold and to derive quantitative supercritical bounds.
We first recall the local functional
\varphi_{p}^{v}(S)
introduced in
[
5
]
,
which provides a sharp characterization of
p_{c}^{\mathrm{site}}
.
Next we define the
(p,\varepsilon,c)
–packing number,
a combinatorial quantity measuring how many well-separated vertices
exhibit controlled local-to-global connection probabilities.
Combining these tools yields a general supercritical upper bound on

\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)
(Theorem
1.1
),
linking disconnection probabilities to the boundary packing profile of
S
.
Finally, we present the amplification lemmas that convert positive local
connection probabilities into high-probability global connections;
these results underlie both the planar non-uniqueness and the packing extension
proved later.

2.1.
Definition of the
\varphi_{p}^{v}(S)
-functional

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For each
p\in(0,1)
, let
\mathbb{P}_{p}
be the probability measure of the i.i.d. Bernoulli(
p
) site percolation on
G
.
For each
S\subset V
, let
S^{\circ}
consist of all the interior vertices of
S
, i.e., vertices all of whose neighbors are in
S
as well.
For each
S\subseteq V
,
v\in S
, define

(2.1)

\displaystyle\varphi_{p}^{v}(S):=\begin{cases}\sum_{y\in S:[\partial_{V}y]\cap S^{c}\neq\emptyset}\mathbb{P}_{p}(v{\mathrel{\to}^{S^{\circ}}}\partial_{V}y)&\mathrm{if}\ v\in S^{\circ}\\
1&\mathrm{if}\ v\in S\setminus S^{\circ}\end{cases}

where

•

v{\mathrel{\to}^{S^{\circ}}}x
is the event that the vertex
v
is joined to the vertex
x
by an open path visiting only interior vertices in
S
;

•

let
A\subseteq V
;
v{\mathrel{\to}^{S^{\circ}}}A
if and only if there exists
x\in A
such that
v{\mathrel{\to}^{S^{\circ}}}x
;

•

\partial_{V}y
consists of all the vertices adjacent to
y
.

.

(Lemma 2.5 in

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{c}=\sup\{p\geq 0:\exists\epsilon_{0}>0,\mathrm{s.t.}\forall v\in V,\exists S_{v}\subseteq V\ \mathrm{satisfying}\ |S_{v}|<\infty\ \mathrm{and}\ v\in S_{v}^{\circ},\varphi_{p}^{v}(S_{v})\leq 1-\epsilon_{0}\}

Moreover,

If

, then for any vertex
v\in V

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}(v\leftrightarrow\infty)=0.

2.3.
Packing definition and quantitative upper bounds

Definition 2.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph. For each
v\in V
, let

\displaystyle B(v,D):=\{u\in V:d_{G}(v,u)\leq D\}.

and

\displaystyle\partial_{in}B(v,D):=\{u\in B(v,D):\exists w\notin B(v,D),\ s.t.~(v,w)\in E\}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph. Let
S\subset V
be a (finite or infinite) set of vertices. For
p,\epsilon,c\in(0,1)
, we define
(p,\epsilon,c)
-packing number
\mathbf{PK}_{p,\epsilon,c}(S)
to the the maximal cardinality of subsets

\displaystyle V_{S}:=\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots\}\subset S

satisfying all the following conditions: for every
w_{i}\in V_{S}
, there exists
D_{i}>0
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle w_{i}\in V\setminus[\cup_{j-1}^{i-1}B(w_{i},D_{i})]

(2)

Let
G_{i}:=G\setminus[\cup_{j-1}^{i}B(w_{i},D_{i})]
; with
G_{0}:=G
, such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(w_{i}\nleftrightarrow\partial_{in}B(w_{i},D_{i});G_{i-1})\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(w_{i}\nleftrightarrow\infty;G_{i-1})\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(w_{i}\nleftrightarrow\partial_{in}B(w_{i},D_{i});G_{i-1})(1+\epsilon)

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(w_{i}\leftrightarrow\infty;G_{i-1})\geq c>0;

where
\{w_{i}\leftrightarrow\infty;G_{i-1}\}
is the event that
w_{i}
is in an infinite-1 cluster in the Bernoulli site percolation of the graph
G_{i-1}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an infinite, connected, locally finite graph. Let
S\subset V
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}(S\nleftrightarrow\infty)\leq\frac{(1-c)\epsilon}{c}+(1+\epsilon)(1-c)^{\mathbf{PK}_{p,\epsilon,c}(S)}

Proof.

We shall prove by induction on
\mathbf{PK}_{p,\epsilon,c}(S)
.

When
\mathbf{PK}_{p,\epsilon,c}(S)=1
,
the conclusion of the lemma follows from (
2.7
).

Let
k\geq 2
. For
1\leq i\leq k
, define

\displaystyle A_{i}:=\{w_{i}\nleftrightarrow\infty;G_{i-1}\};\qquad B_{i}:=\cap_{j=1}^{i}A_{j}.

and

\displaystyle A_{i,D_{i}}:=\{w_{i}\nleftrightarrow\partial_{in}B(w_{i},D_{i});G_{i-1}\};\qquad B_{i,\mathbf{D}_{i}}=\cap_{j=1}^{i}A_{j,D_{i}}.

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}_{i}=\{D_{1},\ldots,D_{i}\}

Assume the conclusion of the Lemma holds for
N=k-1
. Then when
N=k
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{p}(B_{k})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap B_{k-1})

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k,{D_{k}}}\cap B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}})+{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap A_{k,D_{k}}^{c}\cap B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}})+{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap B_{k-1}\cap B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}}^{c})

Note that
\{A_{i,D_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{k}
are mutually independent, therefore

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k,D_{k}}\cap B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}})=\prod_{i=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{i,D_{i}})\leq(1-c)^{k}

where the last inequality follows from (
2.6
), (
2.7
).
Moreover by independence we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap A_{k,{D_{k}}}^{c}\cap B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}})={\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k-1,\mathbf{D}_{k-1}}){\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap A_{k,D_{k}}^{c})

By (
2.6
), we have

(2.11)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap A_{k,D_{k}}^{c})\leq\epsilon{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k,D_{k}})

It follows from (
2.10
)-(
2.11
) that

(2.12)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap A_{k,D}^{c}\cap B_{k-1,D})\leq\epsilon{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k-1,D}){\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k},D_{k})=\epsilon\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{j,D_{j}})\leq\epsilon(1-c)^{k}

We also have

(2.13)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(A_{k}\cap B_{k-1}\cap B_{k-1,D}^{c})\leq{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k-1}\cap B_{k-1,D}^{c})

By (
2.8
), (
2.9
), (
2.12
), (
2.13
) we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k})\leq(1+\epsilon){\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k,\mathbf{D}_{k}})+{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k-1}\cap B_{k-1,D}^{c})

and

(2.15)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k})-{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k,D})\leq\epsilon{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k,\mathbf{D}_{k}})+{\mathbb{P}}_{p}(B_{k-1}\cap B_{k-1,D}^{c})

By (

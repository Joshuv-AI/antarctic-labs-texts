Exclusive η \eta production in proton-proton collisions at energies available at the GSI Facility for Antiproton and Ion Research
Piotr Lebiedowicz

Abstract
The cross sections for the p ​ p → p ​ p ​ η pp\to pp\eta reaction are evaluated at energies relevant for the HADES, PANDA, and SIS100 experiments at GSI-FAIR. Consideration includes the η \eta -bremsstrahlung mechanism involving intermediate proton exchange via the π 0 \pi^{0} , η \eta , ρ 0 \rho^{0} , and ω \omega exchanges and the mechanism involving the nucleon resonances N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) , N ⁡ ( 1650 ) N(1650) , N ⁡ ( 1710 ) N(1710) , and N ⁡ ( 1880 ) N(1880) excited via the pseudoscalar- and/or vector-meson exchanges, depending on the model. The role of the ω ​ ω \omega\omega - and ρ 0 ​ ρ 0 \rho^{0}\rho^{0} -fusion processes with the reggeized vector-meson exchanges is also discussed. The calculation is done in an effective Lagrangian approach. To determine the parameters of the model, the γ ​ p → η ​ p \gamma p\to\eta p and π − ​ p → η ​ n \pi^{-}p\to\eta n reactions are studied and the results are then compared with the Crystal Ball and CLAS data. For the p ​ p → p ​ p ​ η pp\to pp\eta reaction, the model results are compared with the energy dependence of the cross section measured far from the threshold and with the differential distributions d σ / d cos θ η d\sigma/d\cos{\theta_{\eta}} and d ​ σ / d ​ p η d\sigma/dp_{\eta} measured by the DISTO Collaboration. The comparison shows that the N ⁡ ( 1535 ) N(1535) resonance is the dominant contribution and that other contributions are also important due to interference effects. Assuming that the ρ \rho exchange is the dominant resonant excitation process, the model is able to describe the available data, e.g., it reproduces the shape of the angular distributions measured by the DISTO Collaboration. Predictions are given for the HADES experiment at center-of-mass energy of s = 3.46 \sqrt{s}=3.46 GeV, which can be verified in the near future, and for planned PANDA and SIS100 experiments at higher energies.

II
Formalism

The exclusive production of the pseudoscalar
\eta
meson in proton-proton collisions is the subject of this study:

\displaystyle p(p_{a},\lambda_{a})+p(p_{b},\lambda_{b})\to p(p_{1},\lambda_{1})+p(p_{2},\lambda_{2})+\eta(k)\,,

(1)

where
p_{a,b}
,
p_{1,2}
, and
\lambda_{a,b}
,

\lambda_{1,2}=\pm\frac{1}{2}

denote the four-momenta and helicities of the nucleons, respectively,
and
k
denotes the four-momentum of the
\eta
meson.
The cross section is as follows:

\displaystyle\sigma({pp\to pp\eta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{s(s-4m_{p}^{2})}}\,\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2k^{0}}\frac{d^{3}p_{1}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2p_{1}^{0}}\frac{d^{3}p_{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\,2p_{2}^{0}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{(4)}(p_{1}+p_{2}+k-p_{a}-p_{b})

(2)

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\rm spins}|{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}|^{2}\,,

including a statistics factor
\frac{1}{2}

due to identical particles appear in the final state.

The amplitude reads

{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}={\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}(p_{1},p_{2})-{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}(p_{2},p_{1})\,.

(3)

The relative minus sign here
is due to the Fermi statistics,
which requires the amplitude to be antisymmetric
under interchange of the two final protons.

In the calculations, the complete amplitude

{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}
is considered as a sum due to the bremsstrahlung (BS) and the
VV
fusion processes
(
VV=\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
,
\omega\omega
)
contributing to
pp\to pp\eta
:

{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}={\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}^{(\rm BS)}+{\cal M}_{pp\to pp\eta}^{(\rm VV)}\,.

(4)

Details will be given when discussing the production mechanisms below.

\displaystyle F^{(VV\eta)}(t_{1},t_{2},m_{\eta}^{2})=\frac{\Lambda_{V}^{2}}{\Lambda_{V}^{2}-t_{1}}\frac{\Lambda_{V}^{2}-m_{V}^{2}}{\Lambda_{V}^{2}-t_{2}}

The vector-meson-proton vertex is

\displaystyle i\Gamma_{\mu}^{(Vpp)}(p^{\prime},p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ig_{Vpp}\,F_{VNN}(t)

\displaystyle\times\left[\gamma_{\mu}-i\frac{\kappa_{V}}{2m_{p}}\sigma_{\mu\nu}(p-p^{\prime})^{\nu}\right]\quad

\displaystyle g_{\rho pp}=3.0\,,\quad\kappa_{\rho}=6.1\,,\quad g_{\omega pp}=9.0\,,\quad\kappa_{\omega}=0\,;\qquad

(19)

\displaystyle F_{VNN}(t)=\frac{\Lambda_{VNN}^{2}-m_{V}^{2}}{\Lambda_{VNN}^{2}-t}\,,

-proton coupling.

The standard form of the vector-meson propagator

\displaystyle-i\frac{q_{\mu}q_{\nu}}{q^{2}+i\epsilon}\Delta_{L}^{(V)}(q^{2})\,.

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{(VV\eta)}(q_{1},q_{2})\,q_{1}^{\mu}=0\,,\quad\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{(VV\eta)}(q_{1},q_{2})\,q_{2}^{\nu}=0\,,\quad

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{T}^{(V)}(s_{i},t_{i})\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{T}^{(V)}(s_{i},t_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{T}^{(V)}(t_{i})\left(\exp(i\phi(s_{i}))\,\frac{s_{i}}{s_{\rm thr}}\right)^{\alpha_{V}(t_{i})-1}\,,

\displaystyle\phi(s_{i})=\frac{\pi}{2}\exp\left(\frac{s_{\rm thr}-s_{i}}{s_{{\rm thr}}}\right)-\frac{\pi}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\alpha_{V}(t)=\alpha_{V}(0)+\gamma\left(\sqrt{T_{V}}-\sqrt{T_{V}-t}\right)\,.

IV
Summary and Conclusions

The present study investigates the
\eta
meson exclusive production in the
pp\to pp\eta
reaction at energies relevant
for the HADES, PANDA, and SIS100 experiments at GSI-FAIR.
The production mechanism was studied
by using an effective Lagrangian approach.
Concretely, the
\eta
-bremsstrahlung mechanism
with the intermediate proton exchange
via the
\pi^{0}
,
\eta
,
\rho^{0}
, and
\omega
exchanges,
the mechanism with an excitation of nucleon resonances
[
N(1535)
,
N(1650)
,
N(1710)
,
N(1880)
]
via the pseudoscalar- or/and vector-meson exchanges
(depends on the model),
as well as the
\omega\omega
- and
\rho^{0}\rho^{0}
-fusion mechanism
were considered.
The model results have been compared with
the available DISTO
[
24
]

and HADES

[
25
,
26
,
27

To determine the parameters of the model,

The results presented in this work provide
valuable theoretical guidance for future experimental efforts aimed at measuring the

at intermediate energies.
The way in which the nucleon resonances are excited

(models 1 – 3) and the DISTO experimental data measured

The angular distribution of the

The

To learn more about the relevant reaction mechanism,

\eta

\sqrt{s}=3.46
GeV
are provided.
The calculations were also done at
\sqrt{s}=5.0

\sqrt{s}=8.0

The results can serve as a prediction

29

Upcoming experiments in the intermediate energy range

This is important for clarifying the production mechanism

Further measurements would be useful to confirm the theoretical findings, particularly those concerning the role of excited

Related works on the

Appendix B
Photoproduction of the
\eta
meson via reggeized-vector-meson exchanges

The unpolarized differential cross section for the
\gamma p\to\eta p
reaction is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega}=\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}s}\frac{|\mbox{\boldmath$k$}|}{|\mbox{\boldmath$q$}|}\frac{1}{4}\sum_{{\rm spins}}|{\cal M}_{\gamma p\to\eta p}|^{2}\,,

\displaystyle d\Omega=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi\,.

(48)

The considerations are made in the center-of-mass (c.m.) frame,

s
is the invariant mass squared of the
\gamma p
system,
and
q
and
k
are
the c.m. three-momenta of the initial photon and
final
\eta
meson, respectively.
Taking the direction of
q
as a
z
axis,
the polar and azimuthal angles of
k

are defined as
\theta
and
\phi
, respectively.
The amplitude
{\cal M}_{\gamma p\to\eta p}

contains the processes shown in
Fig.
13
,
that is,
the
s
- and
u
-channel
p
and
N^{*}
exchanges and
the
t
-channel vector-meson
(V=\rho^{0}
,
\omega
) exchanges.
In principle the contact-type interaction current
that ensures gauge invariance should also be considered;
see the discussion, e.g., in
[
109
]
.
From a pragmatic point of view, however, only
the tensor-type coupling in
\gamma pp
vertex
is kept, while
the vector-type coupling is omitted.

Diagrams for photoproduction of the
\eta
meson.

The standard kinematic variables are

\displaystyle s=W_{\gamma p}^{2}=(p_{b}+q)^{2}=(p_{2}+k)^{2}\,,

\displaystyle p_{s}=p_{b}+q=p_{2}+k\,,\quad s=p_{s}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle p_{u}=p_{b}-k=p_{2}-q\,,\quad u=p_{u}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle p_{t}=p_{b}-p_{2}=k-q\,,\quad t=p_{t}^{2}\,.

(49)

The amplitude for the
\gamma p\to\eta p
reaction
reads

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{\gamma p\to\eta p}={\cal M}_{s}^{(p)}+{\cal M}_{u}^{(p)}+\sum_{N^{*}_{1/2}}{\cal M}_{s}^{(N^{*}_{1/2})}+\sum_{V}{\cal M}_{t}^{(V)}\,.

(50)

It was checked numerically that
the contributions from the

-channel
N^{*}
exchanges are small.
The reason for this is explained in the previous section.
Thus, these terms can be safely neglected in the considerations.
Therefore, in the current analysis, only
the

N^{*}

with

Amplitudes with the virtual proton and

A

Therefore, the focus will now be on the
\eta

\displaystyle{\cal M}_{t}^{(V)}={\cal M}^{(\rho\,{\rm exchange})}+{\cal M}^{(\omega\,{\rm exchange})}\,.

p_{b}

Appendix C
The
N(1535)\to N\gamma
and

decays and the
\rho NN(1535)
coupling constant

In this Appendix,
the
\rho NN(1535)
coupling constant
is determined in two ways:
one using the
N(1535)\to N\rho^{0}\to N\gamma
decay,
and the other using the

N(1535)\to N\rho^{0}\to N\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
decay.

In the first method, the radiative decay
of the
N(1535)
is considered as follows:

\displaystyle N(1535)(k_{N^{*}},\lambda_{N^{*}})\to N(k_{N},\lambda_{N})+\gamma(k_{\gamma},\epsilon^{(\gamma)})\,,

(58)

where
k_{N}
,
k_{N^{*}}
and
\lambda_{N},\lambda_{N^{*}}=\pm 1/2

denote the four-momenta and helicities of the nucleon and resonance,
and
k_{\gamma}
and
\epsilon^{(\gamma)}
denote
the four-momentum and polarization vector of photon
with helicities
\lambda_{\gamma}=\pm 1
, respectively.

The amplitude for the reaction (
58
) is given by

\displaystyle{\cal M}^{(N^{*}\to N\gamma)}

\displaystyle=(-i)\bar{u}(k_{N},\lambda_{N})i\Gamma_{\mu}^{(\rho NN^{*})}(k_{N},k_{N^{*}})u(k_{N^{*}},\lambda_{N^{*}})\,i\Delta^{(\rho)\,\mu\nu}(k_{\gamma})\,i\Gamma^{(\rho\to\gamma)}_{\nu\kappa}(k_{\gamma})\,(\epsilon^{(\gamma)\,\kappa}(\lambda_{\gamma}))^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{\gamma_{\rho}}\bar{u}(k_{N},\lambda_{N})\Gamma_{\mu}^{(\rho NN^{*})}(k_{N},k_{N^{*}})u(k_{N^{*}},\lambda_{N^{*}})\,(\epsilon^{(\gamma)\,\mu}(\lambda_{\gamma}))^{*}\,,

(59)

using the VMD ansatz
for the coupling of the
\rho^{0}
meson to the photon;
see (3.23)–(3.25) of
[
36
]
.
The vertex for the
\rho NN(1535)
coupling can be derived from an effective coupling Lagrangian.
However, different forms are commonly used in the literature.
The gauge invariant Lagrangian from (A6) of
[
20
]
is

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rho NN^{*}_{1/2^{\mp}}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m_{N}}\bar{N}^{*}\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{5}\\
1\end{pmatrix}\Big[g_{\rho NN^{*}}^{(\text{V})}\Big(\frac{\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{2}}{m_{N^{*}}+m_{N}}-i\partial_{\mu}\Big)

\displaystyle-g_{\rho NN^{*}}^{(\text{T})}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\partial^{\nu}\Big](\mbox{\boldmath$\tau$}\mbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}_{\rho}^{\mu})N+{\rm h.c.}\,,

(60)

see Refs.
[
11
,
12
,
110
]
,
where the vector- and tensor-type couplings were discussed.
It is worth mentioning that a pure vector type of the form

\propto\gamma_{5}[\gamma_{\mu}\partial^{2}-(m_{N^{*}}+m_{N})\partial_{\mu}]

was derived in
[
111
]

and another variant of vector-type coupling in (23) of
[
112
]
.
A pure tensor-type coupling
was used, e.g., in
[
19
,
18
]
.
Note that the vector-type term
of (
60
)
vanishes for the real photon in (
58
), (
59
)
in the limit
k_{\gamma}^{2}=0
.
The consideration here is limited to the
pure tensor-type coupling of (
60
) with

g_{\rho NN^{*}}\equiv g_{\rho NN^{*}}^{(\text{T})}
.

In practical calculations, one introduces
in the
\Gamma^{(\rho NN^{*})}_{\mu}
vertex
[derived from an effective coupling Lagrangian
(
60
)]
the form factor

\displaystyle F_{\rho NN^{*}}(k_{\rho}^{2},k_{N}^{2},k_{N^{*}}^{2})=\tilde{F}_{V}(k_{\rho}^{2})F_{B}(k_{N}^{2})F_{B}(k_{N^{*}}^{2})\,,

(61)

with the assumption
that
\tilde{F}_{\rho}(k_{\rho}^{2})

\displaystyle\tilde{F}_{\rho}(k_{\rho}^{2})=\frac{\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rho}^{4}}{\tilde{\Lambda}_{\rho}^{4}+(k_{\rho}^{2}-m_{\rho}^{2})^{2}}\,,

For the on shell case

\displaystyle F_{\rho NN^{*}}(k_{\rho}^{2},k_{N}^{2},k_{N^{*}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\tilde{F}_{\rho}(0)F_{B}(m_{N}^{2})F_{B}(m_{N^{*}}^{2})

\displaystyle=\tilde{F}_{\rho}(0)\,.

(63)

In the first method, the radiative decay width is,

\displaystyle\Gamma(N^{*}\to N\gamma)_{\rm tensor}=\frac{e^{2}}{\gamma_{\rho}^{2}}\frac{g_{\rho NN^{*}}}{16\pi}\frac{k(m_{N}^{2}-m_{N^{*}}^{2})^{2}}{m_{N}^{2}m_{N^{*}}^{2}}(\tilde{F}_{\rho}(0))^{2}\,.

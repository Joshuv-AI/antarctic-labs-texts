AirTags for Human Localization, Not Just Objects
Mohamed I. Hany, Hamada Rizk, Moustafa Youssef

Abstract
Indoor localization has become increasingly important due to its wide-ranging applications in indoor navigation, emergency services, the IoT ( IoT ), and accessibility for individuals with special needs. Traditional localization systems often require extensive calibration to achieve high accuracy. We introduce UbiLoc , an innovative, calibration-free indoor localization system that leverages Apple AirTags in a novel way to localize users instead of tracking objects. By utilizing the ubiquitous presence of AirTags and their UWB ( UWB ) technology, UbiLoc achieves centimeter-level accuracy, surpassing traditional WiFi and BLE ( BLE ) systems. UbiLoc addresses key challenges, including ranging errors caused by multipath and noise, through a novel AirTag selection technique. The system operates without the need for manual calibration, ensuring robustness and self-maintenance. Deployed on various Apple devices and tested in real-world environments, UbiLoc achieved median localization errors as low as 26 ​ c ​ m 26~cm in a campus building and 31.5 ​ c ​ m 31.5~cm in an apartment setting. These results demonstrate that UbiLoc is the first system to offer reliable, cm-level accuracy using widely available technology without requiring calibration, making it a promising solution for next-generation indoor localization systems.

III-A

Overview

The
Outdoor-Indoor Detection
module initially activates the system as the user moves from outdoor to indoor environments, ensuring both efficient operation and battery optimization. The system processes two main inputs: the first is a set of detected AirTags, accompanied by their respective ranging and angular measurements; the second is orientation data from the Compass/Gyroscope sensor in the user’s device, which tracks the user’s orientation relative to a reference AirTag.

The
One-Shot Localizer
module combines these inputs to calculate the user’s relative position by converting the distance and angle data from the AirTags into Cartesian coordinates based on the user’s current pose using polar coordinate calculations. At the same time, the
Multishot Localizer
module further refines these estimates, providing fine-grained accuracy. This integrated approach ensures consistent user localization across the testbed, even with varying AirTag densities.

In the balance of this section, we give the details of the different
UbiLoc

III-B

Outdoor-Indoor Detection

The
Outdoor-Indoor Detection
module mechanism plays a crucial role in the system by managing the shift between outdoor and indoor environments, helping to conserve battery life and activating the
Multishot Localizer
only when necessary. As users move indoors, the GPS signal weakens or becomes unavailable. By detecting this transition,
UbiLoc
can seamlessly switch to
Multishot Localizer
for precise indoor localization. This detection process relies on monitoring GPS signal strength along with data from environmental context sensors on modern mobile devices. A significant drop in GPS signal, along with confirmation from accelerometer and light sensor data, triggers the activation of
Multishot Localizer

To ensure accurate indoor localization despite the challenges posed by environmental factors, we employ a multi-sensor approach. Indoor environments with ferromagnetic materials and electrical objects create a turbulent magnetic field, which introduces significant noise that can negatively affect the accuracy of dead reckoning. To address this issue, we integrate the readings from both the gyroscope and the magnetic sensor. The gyroscope provides accurate and real-time changes in relative angles, while the magnetometer offers long-term stability. By leveraging the correlation between the two sensors, we can pinpoint moments when the compass reading is reliable. These reliable points are used as reference anchor points (AirTags), allowing
UbiLoc

III-D

Multishot Localizer

The user’s location is estimated at each pose using AirTags in the area, but without selecting the more reliable ones, drifting errors can occur. Noisy readings from distant AirTags, combined with the
One-Shot Localizer
module, can worsen this drift. To mitigate this,
UbiLoc
selects more reliable AirTags to improve localization and refine the user’s trajectory.

The AirTag Selector

In accordance with the distribution of AirTags within the designated area, it is common to have multiple AirTags visible at a given location simultaneously. However, due to the presence of noise in the wireless propagation channel, some of the received AirTags’ ranging measurements may contain outlier values, as shown in Section
II-B

To address this, the AirTag Selector module employs various heuristics to determine the most suitable AirTags for localization purposes. These heuristics encompass selecting the

k

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we presented
UbiLoc
, a calibration-free indoor localization system that leverages commercially available AirTags and user phones. We outlined the architecture and mechanisms of
UbiLoc
, demonstrating how it achieves precise user localization by combining multilateration with novel AirTag selection and error management techniques.
UbiLoc
was evaluated in two real-world test environments: a campus building and an apartment. Experimental results showed that
UbiLoc
consistently achieves centimeter-level accuracy, with median localization errors of
26~cm
in the campus testbed and
31.5~cm
in the apartment setting. Compared to state-of-the-art methods such as Wi-Fi
RTT
and traditional multilateration, which exhibit localization errors ranging from
0.1
to
1~meter
and
0.5
to
2~meters
respectively,
UbiLoc
demonstrated superior accuracy. These findings highlight
UbiLoc
as the first cm-level indoor localization system utilizing AirTags without the need for calibration, offering a highly practical and deployable solution. For future work, we plan to explore integrating additional sensing technologies, such as light and sound sensors, to further enhance
UbiLoc
’s performance and versatility.

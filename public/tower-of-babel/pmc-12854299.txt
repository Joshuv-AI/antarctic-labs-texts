Effectiveness of hybrid teaching methods in improving communication: A single-arm pre–post study using communication workshop among nurses
Authors: Abdul Hakim Choudhary, Tilotma Jamwal, Uma Shanker Agrawal, Parveen, Mohammad Kausar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Effective communication is critical for ensuring patient safety, enhancing outcomes, and reducing healthcare costs. Recognizing that communication skills are influenced by both inherent ability and structured training, a communication skills workshop was conducted to evaluate its effectiveness in enhancing nurses’ knowledge and communication competencies and to gather insights for future improvements.MATERIALS AND METHODS:An interventional pre–post single-arm study was conducted in February 2019 at the All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi. A convenience sampling technique was used, enrolling 140 nurses from various departments. A hybrid teaching-learning approach consisting of four modules—overcoming communication barriers, empathy and compassion in patient care, breaking bad news, and communication’s role in patient safety—was employed. Effectiveness was assessed through pre- and post-training tests and structured feedback forms. Statistical analyses included descriptive statistics, paired t-tests, and Pearson’s correlation tests using SPSS-16.RESULTS:There was a significant improvement in nurses’ communication knowledge and skills from pre-test (mean = 13.217 ± 2.91) to post-test (mean = 15.065 ± 2.87; P < 0.05). More than 95% of participants rated the workshop as very good or excellent, and all participants agreed that the workshop objectives were met.CONCLUSION:The communication workshop was effective in enhancing the knowledge and information of the participating nurses. Based on these findings, it is proposed to integrate regular, interactive, and extended training sessions into the continuing education programs for nurses. These modifications are expected to further improve communication practices and ultimately enhance patient care outcomes.

Introduction

Effective communication is a fundamental aspect of healthcare and has been recognised since the time of Hippocratic medicine as essential for delivering high-quality patient care.[1] Nurses as frontline healthcare providers, play a critical role in ensuring intra-departmental and interdepartmental communication serving as a vital moderator between healthcare teams and patients. Studies have proven that effective communication not only enhance patient safety and clinical outcomes but also significantly reduces healthcare-associated costs by minimizing errors and adverse events.[1]

Communication failure among health care providers is the most common cause of errors and adverse events in clinical settings.[2] Enhanced communication skills among healthcare professionals are instrumental in reducing such errors and fostering a positive patient centred environment.[3] Further, leading to improved health outcomes, better patient satisfaction and overall effectiveness of healthcare practitioners.[4] Thus, effective and empathetic communication should be integrated into routine nursing practices, as it is fundamental to individualized patent care wherein it serves as a cornerstone for informed nursing decision making.[5]

Effective therapeutic communication is often hindered by diverse cultural backgrounds, varying social-demographic characteristics, and unique clinical features among patients. Nurses’ ability to establish therapeutic communication largely depends on their workload and opportunities for skill development.[6] Nurses commonly report heavy workloads, demanding clinical responsibilities and inadequate welfare facilities as significant barriers. Patients, on the other hand, highlight nurses’ unfamiliarity with their dialect, the presence of contagious illnesses, and gender differences as obstacles. Both nurses and patients share barriers such as differences in age, social class, and the presence of contagious diseases.[7]

Additionally, factors such as job stress, increased workload, limited resources and professional burnout negatively affect healthcare providers’ ability to express empathy and compassion, thereby impacting the quality of healthcare.[8] Given that effective communication skills depend not only on innate ability but significantly on structured training and professional development, targeted educational programs are essential.[9] These training initiatives should be integrated into nursing education.[10]

Critical competencies, including prognostication, conflict mediation, empathetic communication, and family-catered care, have been recognized as vital for delivering effective patient-and family-centred palliative care in critical settings, underscoring the need for specialized educational interventions.[11] Previous research has confirmed the feasibility and effectiveness of communication skills training programs, demonstrating significant improvements in participants’ knowledge and communication abilities.[12] Therefore, communication skills training should become a standard component of practical nursing education to enhance performance, especially in intensive care environments.[13]

Barriers to effective communication can be addressed through awareness campaigns, regular training sessions, and continuous monitoring for sustained skill development.[14] Intensive care nurses, in particular, require structured educational programs or mentorship from experienced colleagues to effectively communicate with patients’ families.[15] Furthermore, nurses need educational support and ongoing training opportunities to navigate sensitive communications competently, especially during emotionally challenging situations such as end-of-life care decisions.[16]

Given these needs, a structured one day communication skills workshop employing hybrid teaching methods was designed and conducted at All India Institute of Medical Sciences, New Delhi, a tertiary care institute. This study aimed to assess the effectiveness of the workshop in improving nurses’ communication competencies and to identify practical measures for enhancing the future implementation of similar educational interventions.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

An interventional pre-post single arm study was conducted in February 2019, at All India Institute of Medical Sciences (AIIMS), New Delhi. The objectives were to develop, conduct and assess the effectiveness of the communication workshop in terms of knowledge and information gained and to identify measures for a more effective workshop. Considering the need for communication skills among nurses, a one-day workshop was designed and conducted in February 2019 after administrative approval. Various committees were constituted for the program development and the conduct of the workshop. A comprehensive survey was conducted to identify the training needs of nurses regarding communication skills. A hybrid method of teaching-learning was used and four modules of lecture cum discussion were developed—barriers of effective communication and techniques to overcome; need for empathy and compassion in caring for patients and families; breaking bad news; and the role of communication in patient safety. The workshop program development followed three phases: planning, preparation, and implementation.

Study participants and sampling

A total of 140 nurses working across various clinical departments like emergency, oncology centre, eye centre, dental centre, cardiovascular and neurosciences centre and de-addiction centre, in AIIMS, New Delhi who consented to participate in the workshop were included in the study. Participants were selected through convenience sampling based on their availability and recommendations from nurse administrators. Nurses who were busy working who could not attend the entire workshop duration or failed to compete pre and post-test assessment were excluded.

Data collection tools and techniques

The effectiveness of the workshop was assessed using participants’ feedback obtained through structured pre- and post-training test questionnaires. The questionnaire consisted of 20 items, including multiple-choice questions (12 items) and dichotomous (yes/no) questions (8 items), with each correct response scored as one point, yielding a maximum possible score of 20. Additionally, feedback included Likert-scaled questions related to - overall assessment of the event and rating of the event organisation (two questions on 5-point Likert scales); whether the knowledge and information gained from the workshop met the expectations (3-point Likert) and its applicability in their clinical practice (4-point Likert). Dichotomous (yes/no) questions were used to assess participants’ interest in each workshop module. The questionnaire was validated for content by a panel of experts in Hospital Administration and Nursing (education and communication). Before the main study, the questionnaire was pilot tested with a small group (n = 10) of nurses to ensure clarity, reliability, and appropriateness.

Ethical consideration

Administrative approval was obtained from the Competitive Authority prior to conducting workshop. The study did not include patient participation or collection of sensitive personal data.

Statistical analysis method

Statistical analysis was carried out for entries that were complete. Quantitative data were analysed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows, Version 16.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA). Descriptive statistics were employed to summarize demographic characteristics and participants’ feedback. A paired t-test was utilized to compare pre-test and post-test scores to assess the effectiveness of the intervention. Pearson’s correlation analysis explored associations between selected demographic variables and pre- and post-test scores. Qualitative responses were analysed using thematic analysis to identify key suggestions and areas for workshop improvement.

Results

Participant learning was assessed based on pre-test and post-test. The test consisted of screening/demographic questions and twenty questions to assess the skill gained: twelve questions were multiple-choice questions, and the rest were in the form of yes or no responses. The pre-test was taken just after the registration and codification; the post-test was conducted just after the completion of all modules as per their unique code. Discussion

This study has demonstrated the successful organisation of communication skill workshops for nurses working across various clinical specialties of a tertiary care hospital. Significant improvement was observed in nurses’ knowledge and information, as evidenced by an increase from a pre-test mean score of 13.217 (±2.91) to a post-test mean score of 15.065 (±2.87), which was statistically significant (P < 0.05). This finding aligns with the previous research that emphasizes the effectiveness of structured communication training programs in improving nurses’ knowledge, confidence, and practical communication skills.[1213]

Our findings indicated that a large majority of participants (>80%) provide favourable evaluations about the workshop in their feedback and participants reported improved knowledge and information in the use of skills from pre-test to the post-test.

Participant feedback indicated high satisfaction, with 95% rating the workshop as very good or excellent, and all participants agreed that workshop objectives were achieved. Consistent with previous studies, our results suggest structured programs are feasible, acceptable, and impactful.[1217] About three-fourths of participants reported that the workshop met their expectations in terms of knowledge gained, and 71.4% found the content directly applicable and useful in their clinical practice. These findings are consistent with previous workshops focused on improving confidence, knowledge, and skills in communication.[1718]

The majority of participants highlighted modules on breaking bad news (73.81%) and overcoming communication barriers (57.14%) as particularly valuable, reflecting findings from earlier research about the critical importance of effective communication in challenging patient interactions.[1517] Teaching methods employed in the workshop, such as didactic lectures supplemented by group discussions, align with commonly recommended educational strategies reported in a systematic review, which emphasized didactic methods, reflective discussions, and role-play activities.[19]

Participants suggested enhancing workshop effectiveness through more interactive group activities, additional role-playing sessions, increased use of audio-visual aids, and shorter but more frequent training sessions. They also recommended conducting smaller sessions at the ward level, avoiding repetitive content, increasing workshop duration, and ensuring trainers possess high levels of expertise. These suggestions are consistent with recommendations from prior research supporting experiential and interactive learning methods.[101320]

The organization of the workshop was successful and met its target, as reported by participants. It helped to improve the knowledge and information of participants significantly, as evident by the t-test applied on pre-test and post-test scores. Workshop found to improve the participants’ communication skills irrespective of age, sex, qualifications, position, and experience of the participants, as evident through the Pearson correlation test applied to demographic variables as well as pre- and post-test scores. Overall, the study supports integrating structured and interactive communication workshops into ongoing nursing education to continually enhance communication competencies and improve patient care quality.

Limitation and recommendation

The limitations of the study included a relatively small sample size and a short intervention duration. The assessments were immediate and self-reported, which may not fully reflect long-term behavioural changes. Future evaluations may be done in the real clinical settings, and patient-reported outcomes may also be considered to better understand the sustained impact of communication training. Also, broader participation from other healthcare professionals and varied clinical contexts is recommended to strengthen generalizability.

Conclusion

This study highlights the effectiveness of a structured hybrid communication skills workshop in significantly improving nurses’ knowledge and practical communication abilities. Participants expressed high satisfaction, emphasizing the value of specific modules such as breaking bad news and overcoming communication barriers. To sustain and enhance these improvements, integrating regular, interactive communication training into nursing education is recommended. Future research should consider longitudinal studies to evaluate the sustained impacts of communication training on nurses’ clinical practice and patient outcomes. Including larger and more diverse healthcare professional groups would provide broader insights into the effectiveness of such workshops. Additionally, future studies might benefit from incorporating patient-reported outcomes to comprehensively assess the real-world impact of improved healthcare communication.

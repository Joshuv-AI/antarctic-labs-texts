Unlocking the key requirements and capabilities for a training app on surgical tools and operating room equipment
Authors: Sorayya Rezayi, Fatemeh Sadat Mirmohammad Meigooni, Fatemeh Lotfi, Azam Seyf Ashrafi, Usama Mohammed Kasem, Leila Shahmoradi, Zahra Zare
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesThis study was conducted with the aim of extracting data elements and analyzing the design and development requirements of a mobile-based training app for general surgical tools and Operating Room (OR) equipment for OR students.Methods and materialIn the initial phase of the study, essential data were collected through a review of academic resources, clinical guidelines, and existing digital apps, with the aim of identifying the Minimum Data Set (MDS) and key functional requirements for a mobile-based training app. Based on the findings of this phase, a researcher-developed questionnaire was designed and distributed. Its validity was assessed using face and content validity methods, and its reliability was confirmed through calculation of Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. The final MDS was determined based on the perspectives of subject-matter experts and educators.ResultsThe main components of the app, as determined through expert surveys, include sections on surgical instruments, specialized OR equipment, an alphabetical index of instruments, and essential guidelines on the proper use, maintenance, safety, cleaning, and troubleshooting procedures before, during, and after operation. In addition, the app’s design requirements were also identified, such as user registration, access through a verification code, and the ability to navigate to sections like training materials, “About Us,” and recommended resources for further learning.ConclusionThis study successfully identified and organized the complete set of design specifications and data components necessary for developing a training app aimed at OR trainees, specifically focused on surgical tools and equipment.

Introduction

The Operating Room (OR) is a highly dynamic environment, continually integrating new surgical procedures [1]. This constant evolution presents significant challenges for both staff and students, who must be prepared to deliver high-quality patient care and respond swiftly to emergencies [2]. During crises, the ability to make rapid and sound decisions is paramount, requiring comprehensive skills from all individuals involved. The reference 3 is ok. More information is not provided. [3]. Traditional pedagogical approaches often fall short due to temporal and spatial limitations, hindering students’ ability to develop essential practical skills through experiential learning [4, ].

Technological advancements have catalyzed the development of novel pedagogical approaches , particularly evident in medical training, where computers and smartphones are now indispensable tools in modern teaching methodologies [2, 5, 6]. Nevertheless, training resources and tools specifically designed for the OR remain limited or insufficient.

A notable example of digital integration comes from Iran, where the adoption of smartphone and internet-based training platforms has seen substantial growth, facilitated by an expanding array of online resources [6, 7]. Universities are increasingly adopting innovative and context-appropriate educational approaches that are tailored to the specific needs and constraints of the Iranian educational environment [8, 9]. Smartphones are valuable training tools due to their affordability, accessibility, and adaptability, but excessive use can lead to decreased motivation and decreased collaboration among students [10, 11].

Training apps have emerged as powerful pedagogical tools in medical training, particularly within high-risk environments like the OR. These apps offer safe, simulated settings, enabling students to gain familiarity with surgical instruments without the risk of injury. They significantly enhance learning through interactive content, immediate feedback, and abundant opportunities for repetitive practice and self-assessment. Furthermore, these apps are generally cost-effective, readily accessible anytime and anywhere, and highly adaptable to diverse individual learning needs and preferences [12].

Hence, the utilization of digital technologies—especially mobile apps—has become more and more popular as a cutting-edge method of teaching medicine [13, 14]. Researchers and educational experts have taken notice of the methods’ low cost, simplicity of usage, and schedule flexibility [15]. However, there have been some studies on developing training materials for surgical instruments and OR supplies [5, 10, 16].

Numerous studies have demonstrated that incorporating smartphones into medical training can significantly enhance students’ academic performance. Specifically, several investigations have assessed the effectiveness of mobile apps in improving the knowledge levels of both medical professionals and students. These studies commonly employed pre- and post-test designs or comparisons between intervention and control groups to evaluate training outcomes [15, 17–19].

In a 2020 study, Cloonan and Fingeret [20] investigated the difficulties of teaching surgical equipment used in conventional classroom environments. Time constraints and restricted access to instruments limited students’ options for hands-on learning, according to this study. These results imply that using instructional apps might offer a way around these issues.

Simulation techniques, an e-learning method, have significantly improved surgical training by enhancing practical skills, as demonstrated in a study by Aydin et al. [21], A study of 46 urology residents assessed the effectiveness, content validity, and skill transfer of a multi-modal simulation program on Ureteroscopy procedures (URS). Results showed that participants effectively applied their skills in the OR, and video examinations showed notable ability gains. The URS simulation program significantly improved practical skills and made them easier to transfer to the clinical setting.

Kazior et al. [22] developed a training program utilizing simulation to enhance anesthesia residents’ proficiency in managing intraoperative scenarios. A 2019 program aimed to teach crisis management in anesthesia through simulations. A study with 79 residents showed that 98% of participants found the program beneficial and correlated with their educational level. This study demonstrated the feasibility of developing such a program, resulting in positive educational evaluations.

Abedi and colleagues have devised, deployed, and assessed a web-based electronic system for developing non-practical nursing skills (ESNPNSD). During a survey and Delphi process, the MDS needed to create this system was retrieved. This platform was developed using the agile design approach, which has five stages: analysis, design, development, implementation, and assessment [23]. A study was also carried out to find out how the use of an electronic education strategy based on online apps, tiered, individualized, and based on OR nurses affected their degree of happiness and knowledge [24].

Although many studies have explored the use of digital technologies in surgical training, most have focused on evaluating specific systems or measuring learning outcomes. To date, there has been little attention to identifying the key data elements needed to design a mobile-based training app specifically for OR tools and equipment. Our study aims to address this gap by defining and validating these essential components, ensuring that the application will meet the training needs of OR students. The design and development of the app itself will be covered in a follow-up study.

Materials and methods

This is a descriptive-applied study that was conducted in three stages. The first stage includes a review of library resources, the second stage involves examining existing apps in the Apple App Store and Google Play Store, and the third stage consists of a survey using a researcher-made questionnaire from experts to identify data elements and capabilities of the mobile-based app.

Review of the literature and apps

To identify the main data elements and requirements for designing a mobile-based training app on general surgical tools and OR equipment, a review of the related literature was conducted. For this purpose, several scientific databases (such as IEEE Xplore, Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect, PubMed, and Cochrane) were explored to locate relevant studies. Priority was given to articles published in English and Persian that addressed mobile-based training applications, surgical training, and the use of digital tools for medical education and training. A set of general keywords and MeSH terms (e.g., “Smartphone,” “Mobile Apps,” “Portable Software App,” “Training,” “General Surgery,” “Surgical Procedures,” “Instruments,” and “Medical Equipment”) were applied to guide the search.

In parallel, a review of apps available on Google Play Store and Apple App Store was carried out using the keyword “General Surgery Instruments.” Initial screening was based on app names and developer descriptions. The aim was to identify apps providing training content on general surgical instruments and OR equipment, available in English, free to install, and intended for medical use. Duplicate apps across platforms were considered only once.

Designing a researcher-made questionnaire

To validate the collected datasets and identified components, a structured questionnaire was developed. This instrument’s content was rigorously derived from a synthesis of scientific literature, established databases, and consultations with specialists in the relevant field.

The questionnaire began with demographic questions covering age, gender, academic rank, specialty, faculty affiliation, and status as a clinical instructor. The full questionnaire consisted of 133 items organized into six axes. The main axes of the questionnaire are shown in Fig. 1.

Experts in this field had access to this questionnaire both online and in person. Responses were captured using a 5-point Likert scale, ranging from ‘1’ (least important) to ‘5’ (most important). After collecting the experts’ opinions on the necessity of the datasets to determine the most important data elements in all sections, the experts’ opinions were evaluated using statistical methods. Participants included professors and specialists from the Departments of OR and Nursing Education at Tehran University of Medical Sciences, Shahid Beheshti University of Medical Sciences, Iran University of Medical Sciences, and from the ORs of Shariati Hospital and Vali-e-Asr Complex at Imam Khomeini Hospital. Inclusion criteria were expertise, familiarity with the research topic, and willingness to participate.

Content validity was assessed using the Content Validity Ratio (CVR), and reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient. For each item, the CVR value was compared with the minimum acceptable value of 0.62, and all items met this criterion. Also, Cronbach’s alpha coefficient for all sections was above 0.7, indicating good internal reliability and consistency with the questionnaire.

Conceptual framework

We created a conceptual framework based on the ADDIE model (Analysis, Design, Development, Implementation, and Evaluation) and essential guidelines for assessing institutional capacity for training technologies to formulate the process of creating, evaluating, and validating the content that is inserted in the app. Three organized stages were used to analyze this integrated framework:

1- Analysis of needs:By reviewing the scope of prior research and the currently available surgical training apps, we determined important data components, content gaps, and limits.2- Evaluation of capacity and feasibility:We assessed the app’s functional requirements and essential content after consulting experts. Experts were chosen from various disciplines, such as representatives of educational boards, general operation, nursing education, and OR instruction.3- Validation and Prioritization:We examined the experts’ agreement on the essential data items using validated tools (CVR, Cronbach’s alpha).

Results

In the first stage of the research, relevant literature on educational and training apps in surgical environments was reviewed to identify essential data elements. Of the OR reference textbooks, 45 scientific articles and gray sources, such as websites, were thoroughly reviewed, which helped the research team extract data elements. In parallel, mobile apps related to surgical training were explored in Google Play and the Apple App Store using the keyword “surgical instrument.” A variety of apps were initially found, including those for tool introduction, purchasing surgical instruments, teaching anatomy or anesthesia, and providing nursing education with assessments. For the purposes of this study, only apps specifically related to general surgical instruments and OR equipments were considered. A small set of apps meeting these criteria was selected for further review and evaluation, which informed the identification of essential features and instructional content for the proposed training application.

The tools available in each app

Many essential surgical instruments were consistently present across the apps, including Kelly Forceps, Allis Forceps, Kocher Forceps, Scalpel Blade No. 10–15, Curved Mayo Scissors, and Mayo-Hegar Needle Holder, appearing in at least four of the six apps. In contrast, some specialized tools, such as the Surgiclip Applier and Poole Suction Tip, were absent from all apps, suggesting gaps in content coverage. Overall, the analysis highlights that while most apps include core surgical equipment, the representation of more advanced or specialized instruments remains limited.

Identify data elements and functionalities of the app

The researcher organized the obtained data elements into a questionnaire after preparing and compiling a list of features. The questionnaire used in this study was researcher-developed based on a thorough literature review and expert consultation. The tools in the questionnaire were categorized according to the main standard groups of surgical instruments: cutting & dissecting, grasping & holding, clamping & occluding, suctioning & aspiration, retracting & exposing, and stapling & suturing. Additional categories included OR equipment, such as electrocautery, suction devices, and tourniquets, as well as display-related items. This classification was based on scientific literature and expert opinions and was included in the questionnaire in six main categories. Its face validity was confirmed by the research team and then provided to ten experts. The participants included specialists from internal surgery, nursing education, OR education, and educational liaisons with the faculty (educational board) based in hospitals.

The content of the designed and distributed questionnaire is valid and acceptable. Its content validity was rigorously assessed using Lawshe’s method by calculating the CVR for each item, with a minimum threshold of 0.62 as recommended.

We analyzed the results after surveying experts and distributing questionnaires among them, as shown in Fig. 3. Of the ten participants, six were female and four were male, indicating a slightly higher female representation. The majority were directly involved in education, including faculty members and hospital training specialists. As a result, the researcher should feel free to add all of these objects to the system. The high-scoring items are deemed essential, according to these results. Since they were judged educationally necessary during the research process and based on expert judgment, a few items with significantly lower scores were not removed.

Demographic items, including student name, surname, ID, gender, and mobile number, received relatively low CVR scores, indicating that experts considered them less essential for training purposes. In contrast, training surgical tools and OR equipment were rated highly, reflecting strong agreement on their practical importance. Cutting and dissecting instruments like Mayo and Metzenbaum scissors and scalpels, along with grasping and holding tools such as forceps and towel clamps, received most CVR values above 0.6. Suctioning, retracting, and stapling/suturing tools also achieved moderate to high ratings. Essential OR equipments consistently scored between 0.8 and 1.0, emphasizing their critical role in hands-on training. Overall, these results confirm that the questionnaire is valid, comprehensive, and effective in identifying the key data elements for the training app.

Discussion

Interpreting the findings

During the first phase of the study, we identified the data requirements of the proposed app using multiple methods. These included searching gray literature, evaluating existing apps in the field, reviewing relevant studies, studying authoritative textbooks, and conducting collaborative discussions with domain experts.

The experts participating in the research included the specialists of the internal surgical department, the nursing education department, OR training professors, and trainers of the educational liaison with the faculty (education board) located in the hospitals. At the beginning of the questionnaire, the personal characteristics of the research participants were gathered, including age, gender, academic rank, specialization, field, teaching faculty, and clinical professor in the OR of the hospital. Among the ten participants, four were men (40%) and six were women (60%). The results of the survey indicated that some surgical tools and training components had the highest level of agreement among experts and were recognized as “absolutely essential” components in app-based surgical training.

Tools such as Kelly Forceps, Mayo-Hegar Needle Holder, Adson Forceps, and surgical blades No. 10–15, along with OR equipment such as electrocautery, suction, and tourniquets, received high scores. This indicates their high importance in basic and advanced surgical training programs.

In contrast, some tools, such as the Harmonic Scalpel, Skin Hook, or less common tools, received lower scores due to their more limited or specialized use, which may indicate their lower utility in general or elementary surgical training.

In addition to tools, experts also confirmed that components such as displaying tool images, explaining precautions, identifying and differentiating tools, and accurately introducing physical features are key and functional in mobile-based training. These results provide a valid basis for content design in surgical training apps and can illuminate the path to more accurate, practical, and educationally relevant development.

The study, associated with the Fundamental Use of Surgical Energy (FUSE) program, aims to enhance OR safety through training initiatives. While our study primarily included students and professionals in the OR field, the FUSE program has a broader audience, including surgeons, physicians, and other healthcare professionals. Despite the differences in participants, both studies use a similar methodology, namely, data collection through surveys and needs analysis to design and develop training tools. Participants in the FUSE program are trained in the safe and effective use of specialized OR equipment [16].

In a unique study [23] that is identical to ours, the MDS and specification requirements were taken from a survey and a literature review. This new electronic system improves students’ readiness for actual clinical situations by providing an organized and supervised learning experience. These results are consistent with earlier research that highlights how digital tools can help close the gap between theoretical understanding and practical app. Therefore, the results of the studies support the claim that mobile apps are great for helping people learn new things and improve their skills. Mobile applications can be seen as useful extra tools for medical education because they are cheap, versatile, and can be used both online and offline. They also allow for simulation and flexible learning [6, 25].

In a distinctive study [12], the methodology for extracting educational content from students was carried out in line with ours. That is, first reviewing the texts and then extracting elements based on the opinions of experts. In this investigation, the target group is physiotherapy students, but the performance paradigm is consistent with our study.

In another study [24], ongoing training sessions were delivered according to the requirements of OR nurses through a web app, representing a novel method to enhance their expertise. To match the current study, initial actions have been taken, including figuring out what training is needed and designing the content; a survey was given to OR specialists, nurses, and supervisors, which helped identify the training topics based on their expert views, and then a needs assessment was done.

A previous study [26], emphasized that the lack of a comprehensive requirements assessment frequently leads to the suboptimal design of training programs for healthcare teams. Thus, our study team prioritized evaluating the training needs of OR students and professionals, connecting this procedure with recognized principles of software system design. Similar to our findings, the findings of this current study revealed a clear necessity for enhanced training and knowledge regarding the use of OR tools. This aligns with prior research demonstrating the effectiveness of digital training strategies: for instance, a web-based app, designed, tiered, tailored, and needs-based, significantly increased the knowledge level and satisfaction of OR nurses. Such e-learning approaches can serve as valuable supplemental instructional tools for the ongoing training of OR nurses in various specialized surgical areas and the broader OR environment [24].

Researchers have suggested electronic training as a supplementary and alternative training approach. Researchers have concluded that it can address the temporal and spatial constraints of traditional training, enabling full-time learning tailored to the individual conditions of each learner. It can progressively facilitate access to training and serve as an appropriate adjunct to conventional schooling [24, 27]. Notably, the COVID-19 epidemic has caused the educational system as a whole to reevaluate established educational models, especially in light of inclusive and adaptable strategies that might help the sector become more crisis-resistant [2, 6, 12].

If learning approaches that utilize emerging technologies prove to be more effective than traditional methods, they have the potential to significantly improve learning outcomes. Newly developed tools offer considerable opportunities to enhance training experiences for nurses and nursing students, as well as to improve patient satisfaction and quality of life [6]. The systematic literature review indicates that the employed e-learning method surpassed traditional teaching methods in effectiveness, and the innovative techniques demonstrated significant potential to enhance learning outcomes; however, the magnitude and direction of the effect of a learning method on learning outcomes were found to be highly situational [28].

In our study, using the ADDIE model as a structured framework allowed the research team to proceed with the content design process in a coherent and systematic manner from the needs analysis stage to the final evaluation. This approach ensured that the content produced was aligned with the real needs of the end users, namely students and professors in the field of surgery and the OR. Many studies either neglect the needs analysis stage or perform it superficially. However, this study systematically reviewed sources and analyzed existing apps to accurately identify content gaps. This project could be a good model for similar studies that seek to produce specialized content in the medical fields.

The fact that the use of technology is a double-edged sword and, in some circumstances, it can lead to an improvement in the level of awareness and knowledge, and in a position that leads to dependence and addiction to it, has always been discussed in scientific texts [29]. Strong dependence on technologies, especially in the direction of supplementary education, is always controversial [30].

Strengths and limitations

This study has both strengths and limitations. To enhance its comprehensiveness, an extensive search of both gray literature and peer-reviewed scientific sources was conducted, along with a detailed evaluation of comparable local and international apps. Furthermore, the expert and specialist survey conducted in the first phase played a key role in identifying the essential data elements and functional requirements of the app, contributing to the generalizability and applicability of the findings. The core structure of the app has been conceptualized with the specific needs of OR trainees in mind. During the app selection phase, certain training software with in-app purchases or requiring direct payment initially met the study’s general inclusion criteria. However, due to regional restrictions and the unavailability of international payment systems in Iran, the research team was unable to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of these apps. Consequently, apps necessitating payment were systematically excluded from the final analysis.

This study employed a relatively small expert sample, which may inherently limit the breadth of perspectives captured and potentially impact the generalizability of the findings. Additionally, the survey phase encountered some operational challenges, including delays in the distribution, collection, and analysis of questionnaires, primarily due to limited cooperation from certain professionals.

In evaluating the surgical apps, our analysis extended beyond the specialized content to include practical aspects such as the app developer, release date, and app size. Although these features do not directly pertain to training quality, they are crucial for accessibility and usability, particularly within resource-constrained environments. The reputation of the developer and the app’s size can significantly influence user selection and sustained engagement.

However, a more profound examination of critical training dimensions was beyond the scope of this study but is suggested as a primary direction for future research. These include:

Conclusion

One of the most fundamental concepts in software development is determining the requirements for the creation of training apps. This is important because the instructional material in the software should be in line with the curriculum and textbooks. In this sense, all of the design specifications and data components needed to create an instructional app for OR trainees about surgical instruments and equipment were gathered and organized in this study. Organizing the necessary components enables the structured development of app software. In this revolutionized era, medical training has advanced significantly due to the usage of new technologies.

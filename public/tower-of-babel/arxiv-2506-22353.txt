Bose-Einstein Condensate Dark Matter in the Core of Neutron Stars: Implications for Gravitational-wave Observations
Samanwaya Mukherjee, P. S. Aswathi, Chiranjeeb Singha, Apratim Ganguly

Abstract
We thoroughly investigate the scenario where dark matter is admixed with the ordinary nuclear matter in a neutron star, focusing on the finite temperature Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) as the dark matter candidate. These hybrid configurations yield masses and radii that match observed values of pulsar mass-radius observation and adhere to dimensionless tidal Love number constraints from the binary neutron star merger event GW170817. By analyzing possible, stable mass-radius configurations and the dimensionless tidal Love number constraints, we determine the dark matter content in a nuclear matter whose masses and radii comply with these astrophysical limits. We perform our analyses with three models for the ordinary nuclear matter equation of state (EoS), APR4, MPA1, and SLy. Our analyses suggest that if APR4 is the correct nuclear matter EoS governing neutron stars in the Universe, and the components of this event were indeed dark matter admixed neutron stars, then the most likely dark matter fractions in their cores are approximately 5.65 % 5.65\% and 7.97 % 7.97\% , respectively. In contrast, for the other two EoSs considered, the predicted dark matter fractions are significantly higher, exceeding 12 % 12\% . We also consider finite temperature BEC stars. We find that temperature has negligible effect on the stability criteria or tidal properties of these dark matter admixed neutron stars.

IV
Conclusion

In this study, we investigated the consequences of the presence of dark matter in the form of a finite-temperature Bose-Einstein condensate in neutron stars. We adopt a two-fluid scenario to describe such a dark matter admixed neutron star, where the two fluids interact only via gravity. DM has been modeled as a static BEC at finite temperature, subject to both repulsive contact interactions and attractive gravitational interactions. For the nuclear matter EoS, we considered three models: APR4, MPA1, and SLy.

The masses, radii, and tidal deformabilities of NSs consistently decrease with increasing amounts of DM in their core, irrespective of the EoS being considered.
For a pure neutron star described by the APR4 EoS, the maximum mass is
2.21\,M_{\odot}
with a radius of
10.02
km. When
5\%
DM is included in the core, the maximum mass decreases to
2.09\,M_{\odot}
, and the corresponding radius reduces to
9.68
km. We illustrate how the two-fluid DANS model can be employed to estimate the DM content in the neutron stars involved in the GW170817 event. Assuming that APR4 is the correct nuclear matter EoS in nature and that the component objects in this binary merger were indeed DANSs, the most probable DM fractions in their cores are estimated to be approximately
5.65\%
and
7.97\%
, respectively. For the other two EoSs considered, the inferred DM content exceeds
12\%
. These results highlight that the potential presence of DM in neutron star interiors complicates the task of constraining the true nuclear EoS, since the observed macroscopic properties of neutron stars in gravitational wave events may be compatible with different EoSs depending on the assumed DM fraction.

The tidal deformability consistently decreases with an increase in the mass of the dark matter core, irrespective of the EoS being considered. According to Eq. (
15
),
\Lambda
depends on the ratio
R/M
, with its minimum value corresponding to the maximum mass and minimum radius of the DANS.

We have also studied the impact of finite temperature on the DANS system. We find that temperature has a negligible influence on the stability criteria of the DANSs. Even at temperatures as high as
4\times 10^{11}
K, the mass-radius curves remain almost unchanged, consistent with the 0 K result with
5\%
DM with the APR4, MPA1, SLy EoS.

In our study, we assumed that NM and DM are both isotropic fluids. In reality, however, one or both of them is likely to exhibit a finite anisotropy. This merits further exploration in future research to come up with more realistic models of DANSs. Anisotropy also affects the star’s deformation and its response to tidal forces
[
127
,
128
]
, resulting in more accurate predictions of gravitational waveforms.

In summary, our study aims to make a significant and fundamental contribution to the understanding of DANSs. Since DM does not interact electromagnetically, and GW experiments cannot distinguish between different forms of matter, the resulting tidal effects depend solely on the compactness of the object. Upcoming data from LIGO and future GW observatories such as LISA
[
129
]
, the Einstein Telescope
[
130
]
, and the Cosmic Explorer
[
131
]
will provide valuable opportunities to identify NSs containing subfractions of DM or similar exotic particles. This approach not only aligns more closely with observational data but also enables more sophisticated modeling of exotic matter and gravitational wave predictions, advancing our understanding of compact objects in the universe.

Establishment and application of multiplex real-time qPCR detection for Tembusu virus and goose astrovirus genotypes 1 and 2
Authors: Shaobing Jin, Xiaojuan Li, Minghuan Jia, Wenjun Liu, Shousheng Lu, Feng Cong, Ming Liao, Xinliang Fu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe concurrent infection and epidemic of goose astrovirus type 1 (GoAstV-1), goose astrovirus type 2 (GoAstV-2), and Tembusu virus (TMUV) in geese have resulted in substantial economic losses to the goose industry. Therefore, establishing a rapid and accurate diagnostic method for these three viruses is imperative. In the present study, we designed specific primers targeting the Cap gene of GoAstV and the E gene of TMUV and developed a SYBR Green I-based multiplex real-time qPCR method for the simultaneous detection of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and TMUV. The results revealed that the R2 values for the standard curve of the multiplex real-time qPCR of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV reached 0.998, 0.998, and 0.994, respectively. In addition, the limits of detection for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV were 1.08×102 copies/μL, 2.63×102 copies/μL and 3.07×102 copies/μL, respectively. Moreover, specificity test results indicated that the multiplex real-time qPCR system is highly specific for these three target pathogens, and no cross-reactions with other goose-origin viruses, such as Newcastle disease virus (NDV), goose reovirus (GRV), avian influenza virus (AIV), or goose parvovirus (GPV), were observed. Moreover, the established multiplex real-time qPCR method had excellent repeatability, with intra- and interassay coefficients of variation ranging from 0.23 to 0.91 and 0.71 to 1.75, respectively. The present study provides a rapid and efficient tool that is both specific and sensitive for detecting GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and TMUV and is helpful for determining the epidemiology of these viruses in geese.Keywords: Tembusu virus, Goose astrovirus, Diagnosis, Multiplex real-time qPCR

Introduction

Since November 2016, a visceral gout outbreak in geese caused by a novel goose astrovirus (GoAstV) has been reported in China, causing considerable economic loss to the goose industry (Liu et al., 2022). This novel gout outbreak is characterized by high morbidity and mortality, a low feed conversion rate, growth retardation, and urate deposition in the kidneys and liver, which mainly affects goslings aged 1 to 15 days (An et al., 2020). GoAstV is a nonenveloped, single-stranded RNA virus whose genome is approximately 7,200 nt in length and consists of three open reading frames (ORFs), namely, ORF1a, ORF1b, and ORF2, a short 5ʹ-untranslated region, a 3ʹ-untranslated region, and a polymeric A-tail (Xu et al., 2023). ORF1a and ORF1b encode nonstructural proteins whose overlapping regions contain conserved sequences, whereas ORF2 encodes nucleocapsid proteins associated with virus formation. Recent studies have further revealed the potential vertical transmission of GoAstV and cross-species barriers leading to infections in ducks (Chen et al., 2021), reflecting a complex epidemic of GoAstV with various transmission routes and multiple susceptible hosts. Previous studies have reported that two genotypes of GoAstV (GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2) are circulating in geese in China, and GoAstV-2 is the predominant genotype (Wang et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2018). However, the genome of GoAstV-2 is only approximately 58% identical to that of GoAstV-1, indicating that there are considerable differences between GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2 (Fu et al., 2022). Moreover, the coinfection of GoAstV-1 with GoAstV-2 and other pathogens in clinical samples has been reported previously (Liu et al., 2020; Zhang et al., 2022). Tembusu virus (TMUV) is another important pathogen circulating in geese that belongs to the Flaviviridae family and the Flavivirus genus and causes a sudden decrease in laying in breeding geese and neurological symptoms in goslings (He et al., 2017; Zhu et al., 2022). In addition, TMUV has a wide host range and is capable of infecting various bird species, including ducks, geese, chickens, sparrows, and pigeons (Yu et al., 2018). Therefore, the development of a rapid, sensitive and specific method for the detection of GoAstV and TMUV is urgently needed.

Currently, reverse transcription–quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT‒qPCR) is a reliable tool for accurate quantification and is widely used in clinical practice for pathogen detection (Taylor et al., 2019). Several diagnostic methods for GoAstV detection have been reported previously, including reverse transcription loop-mediated isothermal amplification (RT-LAMP), immunochromatographic strip (ICS) assays and TaqMan-probe-based qRT‒PCR (Ji et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023b; Yang et al., 2021). However, multiplex RT‒qPCR for the simultaneous detection of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV has not been reported. In the present study, a multiplex RT‒qPCR method that is highly sensitive, specific and repeatable for simultaneous quantitative detection of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV was developed, which is helpful for improving the detection efficiency of these viruses in the clinic and provides an efficient tool for studying the epidemiology of these viruses in geese.

Materials and methods

Viruses and clinical samples

The GoAstV-2 strain GoAstV/Guangdong/QY1/2019 (GoAstV-QY1) (GenBank accession No. ON049473) was isolated from goslings with gout in Guangdong Province, China. The capsid (Cap) gene of the GoAstV-1 strain AHDY (GenBank accession No. MH4106) and the envelope (E) gene of the TMUV strain JS804 (GenBank accession No. JF895923) were synthesized and used to construct a template plasmid. Avian influenza virus (AIV), goose parvovirus (GPV), Newcastle disease virus (NDV) and goose orthoreovirus (GRV) were preserved in our laboratory and used to assess the specificity of the test.

A total of 113 tissue samples (including those from the liver, kidney and spleen) with clinical features were collected from goose farms in different areas of Guangdong and Jiangsu Provinces, China, from January 2023 to January 2025. All the samples were homogenized with sterile phosphate-buffered saline (PBS, pH 7.4) to obtain a 20% suspension (w/v) and centrifuged at 10,000 rpm/min at 4°C for 10 min. The supernatants were collected for RNA extraction and further use.

Genome analysis and primer design

Multiple sequence alignment was performed using MAFFT software to identify conserved regions in the published Cap genes of GoAstV-1 (n = 5) and GoAstV-2 (n = 49) and the E gene of TMUV (n = 41). The conserved regions that were over 95% identical were selected for primer design (Fig. 1). Oligo 7.0 software was used to design specific primers for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV detection and recombinant plasmid construction. The primers used in the present study are listed in Table 1. All primers were synthesized by BGI Biotech, Inc., and stored at −20°C until use.

Viral nucleic acid extraction and reverse transcription

The tissue samples preserved at −80°C were added to phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) at a ratio of 1:10 and homogenized in a mortar. After repeated freeze‒thaw cycles (3 times) and centrifugation at 10,000×g for 10 min, viral RNA was extracted from the supernatant using a Viral RNA Kit (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA). The viral RNA genome was reverse transcribed using the reverse transcriptase M-MLV (Takara, Dalian, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and the cDNA was stored at −20°C for further use.

Standard Plasmid Production

The Cap genes of GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2 and the envelope (E) gene of TMUV were amplified by PCR using the primers listed in Table 1. The PCR products were subsequently purified (EZ-HIFI Seamless Cloning Kit, Genstar, Beijing) and cloned into pCMV-C-Flag plasmids. The recombinant plasmids were extracted using a Plasmid Mini Kit (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA), and the plasmid concentrations were measured using a NanoDrop ND-2000 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA). The plasmid copy numbers were calculated as follows: X copies/μL = [6.02×1023 (copies/mol) × RNA concentration (g/μL)]/average molecular weight of a base (g/mol) × template length. The plasmids were stored at –20°C for downstream applications.

Optimization of multiplex real-time qPCR assay

The standard recombinant plasmids were used as the templates to develop the multiplex qPCR assay using an ABI 7500 real-time PCR system (Applied Biosystems, USA). The concentration of primers and the annealing temperature were optimized to ensure specific amplification for the lowest Ct value with high fluorescence intensity. The optimum concentrations from 0.1 μM to 1 μM were selected according to the results of the assay under different conditions. Nine different annealing temperatures were assessed from 58°C to 62°C at intervals of 0.5°C. The optimum temperature was selected based on the measurement results.

Establishment of the standard curve

Each standard recombinant plasmid was serially diluted 10-fold and amplified with the optimized multiplex qPCR system at concentrations ranging from 1.0 × 101 copies/μL to 1.0 × 1010 copies/μL. A final standard curve was generated on the basis of the Ct value and the logarithm of the standard copy number.

Specificity, sensitivity and reproducibility analysis of the multiplex real-time qPCR assay

The nucleic acids of TMUV, GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and other common viruses, including GPV, NDV, GRV, and AIV, were tested to evaluate the specificity of the multiplex qPCR test with the optimized procedure and parameters. GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV were used as positive controls, while distilled water was used as a negative control.

To analyze the sensitivity of the established multiplex qPCR assay, the linearized standard plasmids prepared above were serially diluted 10 times in sterile ddH2O. These diluted standard plasmids were used as templates for multiplex qPCR amplification.

The repeatability test utilized standard samples of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and TMUV, which were diluted to 102 copies/μL, 104 copies/μL, 106 copies/μL and 108 copies/μL. Three intra-assay replicates and three interassay replicates at different time points were conducted. On the basis of the obtained Ct values, the mean value, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation (CV) were calculated to assess the repeatability and stability of the multiplex qPCR method.

Detection of TMUV and GoAstV in clinical samples using the multiplex real-time qPCR Assay

To further verify the clinical significance of this multiplex qPCR assay, we tested the detection ability of this method in 113 clinical samples (including liver, kidney and spleen) collected from goose farms in different areas of Guangdong and Jiangsu Provinces, China, from January 2023 to January 2025.

Results

Optimization of the Multiplex Real-time qPCR Assay

According to the results of Orthogonal test, primers of 0.2 μM was the most efficient. The optimal total reaction volume for multiplex real-time fluorescence quantitative PCR was 20 μL, comprising 10 μL of 2× RealStar Fast SYBR qPCR Mix (Takara, Dalian, China), 0.4 μL each of the upstream and downstream primers for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV, 1 μL of plasmid template, and the remaining volume filled with ddH2O to achieve a final volume of 20 μL. The reaction conditions consisted of an initial denaturation step at 95°C for 10 min followed by 40 amplification cycles consisting of denaturation at 95°C for 1 sec and annealing at 60°C for 60 sec, then ending at 4°C.

Establishment of a standard curve

A spectrophotometer was used to measure the concentration of the recombinant plasmid, and the copy number of each viral plasmid was calculated as follows: 1.08×1010 copies/μL for GoAstV-1, 2.63×1010 copies/μL for GoAstV-2, and 3.07×1011 copies/μL for TMUV. To construct a standard curve with the logarithm of the standard plasmid copy number and the measured Ct values, serial standard plasmid dilutions at concentrations of 1.0 × 101 copies/μL to 1.0 × 1010 copies/μL were tested. The optimal curve was selected as the standard curve. All the standard curves were constructed with the abscissa as the logarithm of the copy number and the ordinate as the Ct value. The correlation coefficient (R2) values were 0.998 (GoAstV-1), 0.998 (GoAstV-2), and 0.994 (TMUV). The standard curve of GoAstV-1 was y = −3.521x + 44.36 (Fig. 2A), that of GoAstV-2 was y = −3.551x+40.48 (Fig. 2B), and that of TMUV was y = −3.014x+38.29 (Fig. 2C). All the standard curves showed good correlation coefficients and amplification effects, with a good linear relationship between the cycle threshold (Ct) and the logarithm of plasmid copy number.

Sensitivity of the multiplex real-time qPCR assay

The GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and TMUV templates were mixed in equal proportions and amplified by an optimized multiplex real-time qPCR assay to evaluate the sensitivity of this method. As shown in Fig. 3, the minimum sensitivities achieved for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2, and TMUV were 1.08×102 copies/μL, 2.63×102 copies/μL and 3.07×102 copies/μL, respectively. These results indicate that the established multiplex real-time qPCR method is highly sensitive for these three pathogens (Fig. 3).

(B) The sensitivity for GoAstV-2 at concentrations ranging from 2.63×109 to 2.63×102 copies/μL. (C) The sensitivity for TMUV at concentrations ranging from 3.07×1010 to 3.07×102 copies/μL.

Specificity of the multiplex real-time qPCR assay

The nucleic acids of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV from our laboratory were employed as positive controls, strong amplification signals were obtained from the reactions and the melting temperature (Tm) value for qPCR product were 79.2°C, 80°C and 81°Cfor GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV, respectively. However, other common goose viruses, including GPV, NDV, GRV, and AIV, had no specific amplification signals, and the Ct values were greater than 32 (Fig. 4). These results indicate that the multiplex real-time qPCR method established in the present study is highly specific for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV and does not cross-react with other goose-origin viruses.

Reproducibility of the multiplex real-time qPCR assay

The reproducibility of the established multiplex real-time qPCR method was further investigated with four different dilutions of standard plasmids for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV.

As shown in Table 2, GoAstV-1 amplification exhibited coefficients of variation (CVs) of 0.39 to 0.84% and 0.71 to 1.05% for the intra-assay and interassay tests, respectively. GoAstV-2 amplification exhibited CVs of 0.23 to 0.91% and 0.96 to 1.75% for the intra-assay and interassay tests, respectively. TMUV amplification exhibited CVs of 0.42 to 0.87% and 0.85 to 1.44% for the intra-assay and interassay tests, respectively. These results indicated that the multiplex real-time qPCR method established in the present study is both repeatable and reliable. (Table 3)

Clinical sample application and comparison with conventional PCR

To further verify the clinical potential of this multiplex qPCR assay, we tested it with 113 clinical samples (including those from the kidney, liver and spleen) collected from goose farms and compared the results with those obtained by conventional PCR. Using the established multiplex qPCR assay, the positivity rates for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV were 16.8% (19/113), 67.3% (76/113) and 10.6% (12/113), respectively. The coinfection rate of GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2 was 7.1% (8/113), and no coinfection of GoAstV with TMUV was detected.

Discussion

The current epidemic of GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV has spread across most provinces in China and has shown no signs of decline (Fu et al., 2022; Liu et al., 2020). Although certain clinical symptoms, such as joint swelling and urate deposition for GoAstV infection or neurological disorders for TMUV, can serve as reliable indicators for preliminary diagnosis (Cheng et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2022; Ren et al., 2025), accurate identification through clinical observation and pathological examination remains challenging. Therefore, establishing a multiplex detection system for TMUV and different GoAstV genotypes will be highly valuable for daily pathogen monitoring in the poultry industry.

To date, several detection methods have been developed for these three viruses, including conventional PCR, loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP), and colloidal gold test strips (Ji et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023; Ren et al., 2023; Yang et al., 2021). However, these methods are often labor-intensive and time-consuming and lack both accurate quantification and high sensitivity. In contrast, qPCR offers high specificity, sensitivity, reproducibility, and efficiency, along with the ability to simultaneously detect multiple pathogens in a single reaction; thus, it has been widely used in genetic testing (Taylor et al., 2019). In the present study, we analyzed the most recently published genomes of TMUV and GoAstV. The results were highly consistent with those of previous reports, indicating the presence of multiple conserved regions and genomic stability in both viruses (Chen et al., 2020; Cheng et al., 2024; Zhu et al., 2022). Additionally, sequence comparison revealed low identity between TMUV and GoAstV, suggesting that molecular detection methods can effectively differentiate between these pathogens.

On the basis of the conserved E gene of TMUV and the Cap gene of GoAstV, we designed three specific primer pairs and optimized reaction conditions to establish a SYBR Green I-based multiplex qPCR assay for the simultaneous detection of TMUV, GoAstV-1 and GoAstV-2. This method achieved minimum detection limits of 1.08×102 copies/μL for GoAstV-1, 2.63×102 copies/μL for GoAstV-2 and 3.07×102 copies/μL for TMUV. Furthermore, the assay exhibited high specificity against common goose-origin viruses such as AIV, NDV, GRV and GPV and demonstrated excellent reproducibility on the basis of the intra- and interassay CV values. Currently, a variety of methods have been developed for the detection of these viruses. Compared with these existing approaches, the multiplex real-time qPCR assay established in this study demonstrates significant potential for practical application. For instance, compared with our method, the novel multienzyme isothermal rapid amplification (MIRA) and lateral flow dipstick (LFD) combined assay for GoAstV-2 detection (Zhu et al., 2024) is slightly more sensitive but cannot provide accurate quantitative results. Moreover, visual interpretation of the results may introduce subjectivity. Additionally, indirect ELISA for detecting antibodies against goose astrovirus types 1 and 2 lacks the ability to synchronously detect both genotypes in a single reaction system (Zhang et al., 2023). Furthermore, since it primarily targets host-produced antibodies, this approach is inherently delayed and unsuitable for early diagnosis. Moreover, in comparison with existing GoAstV-2 and TMUV detection methods (Li et al., 2023a), our approach maintains comparable sensitivity while offering cost-efficiency advantages and greater operational simplicity.

Overall, we report the first multiplex detection system for GoAstV-1, GoAstV-2 and TMUV with high sensitivity and specificity. This assay provides a useful tool for studying the molecular epidemiology of TMUV and different GoAstV genotype infections in the poultry industry and deserves broad application.

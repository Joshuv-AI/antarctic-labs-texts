Supersymmetric quantum mechanics from wrapped D4-branes


Abstract
We find a large class of holographic solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on 4-manifolds ℳ 4 \mathcal{M}_{4} with constant curvature leading to gravity duals of supersymmetric quantum mechanics in the IR via twisted compactifications. The manifolds ℳ 4 \mathcal{M}_{4} considered here are four-dimensional spheres and hyperbolic spaces, products of two Riemann surfaces, and Kahler four-cycles. The solutions are obtained from the maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions with C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 5 − p − q ) CSO(p,q,5-p-q) and C ​ S ​ O ​ ( p , q , 4 − p − q ) ⋉ ℝ 4 CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4} gauge groups. These gauged supergravities can be embedded in type IIA theory via consistent truncations on H p , q × ℝ 5 − p − q H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q} and H p , q × ℝ 4 − p − q × S 1 H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1} , respectively. The solutions take the form of t × ℳ 4 t\times\mathcal{M}_{4} -sliced domain walls interpolating between locally flat domain walls and singular geometries in the IR. Upon uplifted to type IIA theory, many solutions admit physical IR singularities and could holographically describe supersymmetric quantum mechanics arising from twisted compactifications of D4-branes on ℳ 4 \mathcal{M}_{4} .

We will consider six-dimensional gauged supergravity with

CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged groups. Both of these gauged supergravities can be obtained from an

-reduction of the maximal gauged supergravity in seven dimensions with

. Accordingly, further truncation on
S^{1}
leads to six-dimensional gauged supergravity with
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
, or equivalently, a truncation of type IIA theory on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1}
leads to
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged supergravity in six dimensions. On the other hand, the maximal
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
gauged supergravity in seven dimensions can also be obtained as a truncation of eleven-dimensional supergravity on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}

[
34
]
. Another dimensional reduction on
S^{1}
gives
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
gauged supergravity in six dimensions as a consistent truncation of eleven-dimensional supergravity on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}\times S^{1}
. Together with the fact that type IIA theory can be obtained from an
S^{1}
-reduction of M-theory, the six-dimensional
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
gauged supergravity can be considered as a truncation of type IIA theory on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}
after a truncation of M-theory on
S^{1}
. The full truncation ansatz for the case of
S^{4}\sim H^{5,0}
has been constructed in
[
35
]
. However, complete truncation ansatze for both type IIA theory on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}
or eleven-dimensional supergravity on
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}
have not been worked to date. Therefore, the complete truncations to six dimensions are currently not available either. In any case, all solutions of these six-dimensional gauged supergravities can in principle be embedded in type IIA theory as described above.

The solutions of interest in this paper take the form of
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M}_{4}
-sliced domain walls with the
\mathbb{R}
factor being the time coordinate. We will look for solution interpolating between asymptotically locally flat domain walls dual to maximal SYM in five dimensions and curved domain wall with
t\times\mathcal{M}_{4}
-sliced world-volume. Holographically, these solutions would describe RG flows across dimensions from maximal five-dimensional SYM to one-dimensional SYM or supersymmetric quantum mechanics in the IR. In general, due to the presence of topological twists, the latter is obtained from twisted compactifications of the former on
\mathcal{M}_{4}
. We also point out that, in holographic studies involving non-conformal field theories, supergravity solutions generically contain singular geometries in the IR. To determine whether the resulting solutions are physically acceptable, we employ the condition introduced in
[
18

(00)

\hat{g}_{00}
should not diverge near the IR singularities since fixed proper energy excitations in supergravity should correspond to lower and lower energy excitations in the dual field thoery. Upon uplifted to type IIA theory, the solutions with physical IR singularities would describe different configurations involving D4-branes wrapped on
\mathcal{M}_{4}
. It should also be noted that although the complete ansatze for the aforementioned truncations are not available, we can determine the ten-dimensional metric component
\hat{g}_{00}
using partial results given in
[
33
,
34
]
and check whether a given singularity is physical or not. For convenience, we also give the relevant expressions for
\hat{g}_{00}
for all of the solutions in appendix
B
.

2.1
Maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions

Maximal supersymmetry only allows for the supergravity multiplet. In six dimensions, the bosonic field content consists of the vielbein
e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu}
, two-form potentials
B_{\mu\nu,m}
and
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
(
m=1,\ldots,5
), three-forms
C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}
(
A=1,\ldots,16
), vector fields
{A_{\mu}}^{A}
, and scalars
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
(
\alpha=1,\ldots,4
and
\dot{\alpha}=\dot{1},\ldots,\dot{4}
) that parametrize the coset space
SO(5,5)/SO(5)\times SO(5)
. Curved and flat space-time indices are denoted by
\mu
,
\nu
and
\hat{\mu}
,
\hat{\nu}
, respectively. Lower and upper
m
indices respectively label fundamental and anti-fundamental representations of
GL(5)\subset SO(5,5)
. The index
A
describes Majorana-Weyl spinors of the
SO(5,5)
duality symmetry while the indices
\alpha
and
\dot{\alpha}
are respectively two sets of
SO(5)
spinor indices in the
SO(5)\times SO(5)
local symmetry.

Fermionic field content contains the gravitini
\psi_{+\mu\alpha}
and
\psi_{-\mu\dot{\alpha}}
, and the spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fields
\chi_{+a\dot{\alpha}}
and
\chi_{-\dot{a}\alpha}
where
a=1,\ldots,5
and
\dot{a}=\dot{1},\ldots,\dot{5}
respectively denote two sets of
SO(5)
vector indices in
SO(5)\times SO(5)
. We use
\pm
to indicate space-time chiralities of the spinors. In addition, all spinors are symplectic-Majorana-Weyl. With all these, all component fields can be written as

\left(e^{\hat{\mu}}_{\mu},B_{\mu\nu,M},C_{\mu\nu\rho,A},{A_{\mu}}^{A},{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\psi_{+\mu\alpha},\psi_{-\mu\dot{\alpha}},\chi_{+a\dot{\alpha}},\chi_{-\dot{a}\alpha}\right).

(2.1)

We also note that due to the self-duality of the two-form fields, the Lagrangian of the ungauged supergravity is only invariant under
GL(5)
subgroup of the full duality symmetry
SO(5,5)
. In this case, only the electric two-forms
B_{\mu\nu,m}
appear in the Lagrangian while the magnetic duals
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
can be introduced on-shell.

In the embedding tensor formalism, gaugings can be described by introducing the minimal coupling of various fields via the covariant derivative

D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-gA^{A}_{\mu}\ {\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}

(2.2)

where
g
is a gauge coupling constant, and
{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}
is the embedding tensor. The corresponding gauge generators are given by
X_{A}={\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}
. In general, these are particular linear combinations of the
SO(5,5)
generators
\boldsymbol{t}_{MN}
and form a closed Lie algebra of the gauge group
G_{0}\subset SO(5,5)
. The latter implies that the embedding tensor needs to satisfy the quadratic constraint of the form

\left[X_{A},X_{B}\right]\ =\ -{(X_{A})_{B}}^{C}\,X_{C}

(2.3)

with
{(X_{A})_{B}}^{C}
denoting gauge generators in chiral spinor representation of
SO(5,5)
given below.

In addition, consistency with supersymmetry requires the embedding tensor to transform as
\mathbf{144}_{c}
representation of
SO(5,5)
. Accordingly,
{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}
can be parametrized in terms of a vector-spinor
\theta^{AM}
of
SO(5,5)
as

{\Theta_{A}}^{MN}\ =\ -\theta^{B[M}(\Gamma^{N]})_{BA}\ \equiv\ \left(\Gamma^{[M}\theta^{N]}\right)_{A}

(2.4)

with
\theta^{AM}
subject to the constraint

(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}\,\theta^{BM}\ =\ 0

(2.5)

where
(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}
is an
SO(5,5)
gamma matrix. In terms of
\theta^{AM}
, the quadratic constraint reduces to the following two conditions

\theta^{AM}\theta^{BN}\eta_{MN}\ =\ 0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\theta^{AM}\theta^{B[N}(\Gamma^{P]})_{AB}=0\,.

(2.6)

\eta_{MN}
is the off-diagonal
SO(5,5)
invariant tensor given by

\eta_{MN}\ =\ \eta^{MN}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&\mathds{1}_{5}\\
\mathds{1}_{5}&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.7)

With
SO(5,5)
generators in vector and spinor representations given by

{(\boldsymbol{t}_{MN})_{P}}^{Q}=4\eta_{P[M}\delta^{Q}_{N]}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{(\boldsymbol{t}_{MN})_{A}}^{B}\ =\ {(\Gamma_{MN})_{A}}^{B},

(2.8)

the corresponding gauge generators take the forms

{(X_{A})_{M}}^{N}=2\left(\Gamma_{M}\theta^{N}\right)_{A}+2\left(\Gamma^{N}\theta_{M}\right)_{A}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{(X_{A})_{B}}^{C}=\left(\Gamma^{M}\theta^{N}\right)_{A}{(\Gamma_{MN})_{B}}^{C}\,.

(2.9)

Unlike the ungauged supergravity, the most general gauged supergravity can involve a symmetry that lies outside the off-shell
GL(5)
symmetry. Under
GL(5)
,
\theta^{AM}
can be decomposed as
\theta^{AM}=(\theta^{Am},{\theta^{A}}_{m})
. Gaugings triggered by
\theta^{Am}
are called electric in the sense that only electric two-forms participate in the gauged theory while magnetic gaugings obtained from
{\theta^{A}}_{m}
also involve magnetic two-forms together with additional three-form tensor fields
C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}
. In this case, the magnetic two-forms
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
can appear in the gauged Lagrangian by coupling to the three-form potentials via the topological term. The electric and magnetic two-forms are combined into a vector representation of the full global symmetry group
SO(5,5)
denoted by
B_{\mu\nu,M}=(B_{\mu\nu,m},{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m})
.

The gauge covariance of the field strength tensors for all these fields requires the presence of higher degree form fields leading to hierarchies of non-abelian vector and tensor fields of various ranks. In particular, the covariant field strengths of vector and two-form fields are defined by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{F_{\mu\nu}}^{A}-\sqrt{2}g\theta^{AM}B_{\mu\nu,M},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3D_{[\mu}B_{\nu\rho],M}+3\sqrt{2}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{A_{[\mu}}^{A}(\partial_{\nu}{A_{\rho]}}^{B}+\frac{1}{3}g{X_{[CD]}}^{B}{A_{\nu}}^{C}{A_{\rho]}}^{D})

(2.11)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}g\eta_{MN}\theta^{AN}C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}

with the usual non-abelian gauge field strengths

{F_{\mu\nu}}^{A}=2\partial_{[\mu}{A_{\nu]}}^{A}+g{X_{[BC]}}^{A}{A_{\mu}}^{B}{A_{\nu}}^{C}\,.

(2.12)

These field strengths satisfy the following Bianchi identities

\displaystyle D_{[\mu}{\mathcal{H}_{\nu\rho]}}^{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}g\theta^{AM}\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho,M},

(2.13)

\displaystyle D_{[\mu}\mathcal{H}_{\nu\rho\lambda],M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{\mathcal{H}_{[\mu\nu}}^{A}{\mathcal{H}_{\rho\lambda]}}^{B}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}g\eta_{MN}\theta^{AN}\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda,A}

(2.14)

with the four-form field strengths defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda,A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4D_{[\mu}C_{\nu\rho\lambda],A}-(\Gamma^{M})_{AB}\left[6\sqrt{2}B_{\mu\nu,M}{\mathcal{H}_{\rho\lambda}}^{B}+6g\theta^{BN}B_{[\mu\nu,M}B_{\rho\lambda],N}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\phantom{\sqrt{2}}+8(\Gamma_{M})_{CD}{A_{[\mu}}^{B}{A_{\nu}}^{C}\partial_{\rho}{A_{\lambda]}}^{D}+2(\Gamma_{M})_{CF}{X_{DE}}^{F}{A_{[\mu}}^{B}{A_{\nu}}^{C}{A_{\rho}}^{D}{A_{\lambda]}}^{E}\right].

In addition, all of the aforementioned field strength tensors are subject to the following duality relations

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g{\theta^{A}}_{m}({\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho}}^{m}-\frac{1}{6}K^{mn}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda\sigma\tau}{\mathcal{H}^{\lambda\sigma\tau}}_{n}),

(2.16)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g{\theta^{A}}_{m}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda,A}+\frac{1}{2}M_{AB}\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda\sigma\tau}\mathcal{H}^{\sigma\tau,B})

(2.17)

where
K^{mn}
and
M_{AB}
are scalar matrices written in terms of the scalar coset representative.

The matrix
M_{AB}
is defined by

M_{AB}=\Omega_{\alpha\beta}\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}{V_{B}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}

(2.18)

with
\Omega_{\alpha\beta}
and
\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}
being the two
USp(4)\sim SO(5)
symplectic forms used for lowering indices
\alpha
and
\dot{\alpha}
, respectively. Raising of indices
\alpha
and
\dot{\alpha}
is implemented by using
\Omega^{\alpha\beta}=(\Omega_{\alpha\beta})^{*}
and
\Omega^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}=(\Omega_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}})^{*}
. On the other hand, the matrix
K^{mn}
is defined in terms of the coset representative in vector representation of
SO(5,5)
of the form

{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\underline{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathcal{V}_{m}}^{a}&{\mathcal{V}_{m}}^{\dot{a}}\\
{\mathcal{V}^{m}}^{a}&{\mathcal{V}^{m}}^{\dot{a}}\end{pmatrix}

(2.19)

with
\underline{A}=(a,\dot{a})
. This is related to the coset representative in the chiral spinor representation
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
by the following relations

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{(\gamma^{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}{V^{B}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}},

(2.20)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}_{M}}^{\dot{a}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{16}V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}{(\gamma^{\dot{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}{V^{B}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\,

(2.21)

in which
V^{A\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\Omega^{\alpha\beta}\Omega^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{V^{A}}_{\beta\dot{\beta}}
and
{V^{A}}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
are inverse elements of the coset representative
{V_{A}}^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
.
In these relations,
(\Gamma_{M})_{AB}
and
{(\Gamma_{\underline{A}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}}=({(\gamma_{a})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}},{(\gamma_{\dot{a}})_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}}^{\beta\dot{\beta}})
are respectively
SO(5,5)
gamma matrices in non-diagonal
\eta_{MN}
and diagonal
\eta_{\underline{A}\underline{B}}
bases, see Appendix A of

]
for more detail.

With all these, the matrix

is defined by

K^{mn}=\mathcal{V}^{ma}({\mathcal{V}_{n}}^{a})^{-1}P_{+}-\mathcal{V}^{m\dot{a}}({\mathcal{V}_{n}}^{\dot{a}})^{-1}P_{-}

(2.22)

in which

are projections on the self- and anti-self- dual parts given by

P_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm j).

(2.23)

The operation

implements the duality transformation as

jH_{\mu\nu\rho}=\tilde{H}_{\mu\nu\rho}=\frac{1}{6}e\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\lambda\sigma\rho}H^{\lambda\sigma\rho}.

N=(2,2)
gauged supergravity takes the form

\displaystyle e^{-1}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}R-\frac{1}{16}{P_{\mu}}^{a\dot{a}}{P^{\mu}}_{a\dot{a}}-\frac{1}{4}M_{AB}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}\mathcal{H}^{\mu\nu,B}-\frac{1}{12}K^{mn}\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu\rho,m}{\mathcal{H}^{\mu\nu\rho}}_{n}

(2.25)

where

is the topological term given in

]
. The kinetic term for the scalar fields is written in terms of the scalar vielbein

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}\hat{\gamma}^{\mu\nu}\Omega_{\alpha\beta}{V_{A}}^{\beta\dot{\alpha}}{(\gamma_{\dot{a}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}}.

The covariant derivatives of supersymmetry parameters,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{Am}{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{A}=-\sqrt{2}gY_{mn}{B_{\mu\nu}}^{n},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}_{\mu\nu}}^{mn}

\displaystyle=

(3.8)

3.1
D4-branes wrapped on a Riemannian four-manifold

We first consider the case of supersymmetric solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on a Riemannian four-manifold
\Sigma^{4}_{k}

characterizes the curvature of
\Sigma^{4}_{k}
with values

corresponding to a four-dimensional sphere

, a flat space
\mathbb{R}^{4}

ds_{6}^{2}=-e^{2U(r)}dt^{2}+dr^{2}+e^{2V(r)}ds^{2}_{\Sigma^{4}_{k}}\,.

(3.9)

ds^{2}_{\Sigma^{4}_{k}}
is the metric on
\Sigma^{4}_{k}
with the explicit form written as

(3.10)

for
\chi,\psi,\theta\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
,
\zeta\in[0,2\pi]
. The function

is defined by

f_{k}(\chi)=\begin{cases}\sin{\chi},\ \ \quad\text{ for }k=+1\\
\chi,\ \ \ \ \ \ \quad\text{ for }k=0\\
\sinh{\chi},\quad\text{ for }k=-1\end{cases}.

(3.11)

With the following choice of vielbein

\displaystyle e^{\hat{t}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{U}dt,\qquad e^{\hat{r}}=dr,\qquad e^{\hat{\chi}}=e^{V}d\chi,\qquad e^{\hat{\psi}}=e^{V}f_{k}(\chi)d\psi,

\displaystyle e^{\hat{\theta}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{V}f_{k}(\chi)\sin{\psi}d\theta,\qquad e^{\hat{\zeta}}=e^{V}f_{k}(\chi)\sin{\psi}\sin{\theta}d\zeta,

(3.12)

we obtain the non-vanishing components of the spin connection given by

\displaystyle{\omega_{\hat{t}}}^{\hat{t}\hat{r}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046ptU^{\prime},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{r}}=V^{\prime}\delta^{\hat{j}}_{\hat{i}},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{\psi}}}^{\hat{\psi}\hat{\chi}}={\omega_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\chi}}={\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\chi}}=e^{-V}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\chi)}{f_{k}(\chi)},

\displaystyle{\omega_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\psi}}=e^{-V}\frac{\cot\psi}{f_{k}(\chi)},\qquad\quad{\omega_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}=e^{-V}\frac{\cot\theta}{f_{k}(\chi)\sin{\psi}}\,.

(3.13)

We have split the six-dimensional coordinates as
x^{\mu}=(t,r,x^{i})
,
i=\chi,\psi,\theta,\zeta
, with indices
\hat{i},\hat{j},\ldots=\hat{\chi},\hat{\psi},\hat{\theta},\hat{\zeta}
are flat indices on
\Sigma^{4}_{k}
. The
r
-derivatives will generally be denoted by
′
with an exception that a
′
on any function with an explicit argument refers to the derivative of the function with respect to that argument as in
f^{\prime}_{k}(\chi)=\frac{df_{k}(\chi)}{d\chi}

To preserve some amount of supersymmetry, we perform a topological twist by turning on some gauge fields to cancel the spin connection
{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{k}}
on
\Sigma^{4}_{k}
. In the present case, we achieve this by turning on
SO(4)
gauge fields as follows

\displaystyle{A_{\hat{\psi}}}^{\hat{\psi}\hat{\chi}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt{A_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\chi}}={A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\chi}}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\chi)}{f_{k}(\chi)},

\displaystyle{A_{\hat{\theta}}}^{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt{A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\psi}}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{\cot\psi}{f_{k}(\chi)},\qquad{A_{\hat{\zeta}}}^{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{\cot\theta}{f_{k}(\chi)\sin{\psi}}

(3.14)

where

is a constant magnetic charge. In order to perform this topological twist, we consider gauged supergravity with the gauge group containing an

subgroup. These gauge groups are given by

,

, and

which can be characterized by a tensor

in the embedding tensor of the form

Y_{mn}=\text{diag}(1,1,1,1,\lambda)

(3.15)

for

. Among the
25

SO(4)
singlet scalars parametrized by the coset representative

(3.16)

The first scalar is the dilaton

associated with the

generator
\boldsymbol{d}
, and the other one

corresponds to the non-compact generator

(3.17)

The topological twist can be achieved by imposing the twist condition

gp=k

(3.18)

together with the following projection conditions

\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{\chi}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}={(\gamma_{12})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\psi}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}={(\gamma_{23})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\zeta}\hat{\theta}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}={(\gamma_{34})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta}

(3.21)

3.3
D4-branes wrapped on a Kahler four-cycle

In this section, we perform a similar analysis for D4-branes wrapped on a Kahler four-cycle
K^{4}_{k}
. As in the previous cases, the constant
k
characterizes the constant curvature of
K^{4}_{k}
with
k=1,0,-1
corresponding to a two-dimensional complex projective space
CP^{2}
, a four-dimensional flat space
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and a two-dimensional complex hyperbolic space
CH^{2}
, respectively. The manifold
K^{4}_{k}
has a
U(2)\sim U(1)\times SU(2)
spin connection. Therefore, we can perform a twist by turning on
SO(2)\sim U(1)
or
SO(3)\sim SU(2)
gauge fields to cancel the
U(1)
or
SU(2)
parts of the spin connection.

A general ansatz for the six-dimensional metric takes the form of

ds_{6}^{2}=-e^{2U(r)}dt^{2}+dr^{2}+e^{2V(r)}ds^{2}_{K^{4}_{k}}

(3.79)

in which the explicit form of the metric on a Kahler four-cycle will be specified in each case.

3.3.1
Solutions with
SO(3)
twist

We begin with a twist along the
SU(2)\sim SO(3)
part of the spin connection and choose the following form of the metric on a Kahler four-cycle

ds^{2}_{K^{4}_{k}}=d\psi^{2}+f_{k}(\psi)^{2}(\tau_{1}^{2}+\tau_{2}^{2}+\tau_{3}^{2})

(3.80)

with
\psi\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.
f_{k}(\psi)
is a function defined as in (
3.11
) while
\tau_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
, are
SU(2)
left-invariant one-forms satisfying

d\tau_{i}=\varepsilon_{ijl}\tau_{j}\wedge\tau_{l}\,.

(3.81)

The explicit forms of
\tau_{i}
can be chosen to be

\displaystyle\tau_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{1}{2}(-\sin\vartheta d\theta+\cos\vartheta\sin\theta d\zeta),

\displaystyle\tau_{2}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{1}{2}(\cos\vartheta d\theta+\sin\vartheta\sin\theta d\zeta),

\displaystyle\tau_{3}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\frac{1}{2}(d\vartheta+\cos\theta d\zeta)

(3.82)

with the ranges of the angular coordinate given by
\theta\in[0,\pi]
,
\zeta\in[0,2\pi]
, and
\vartheta\in[0,4\pi]
.

Using the following choice of vielbein

\displaystyle e^{\hat{t}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{U}dt,\qquad\qquad\ e^{\hat{r}}=dr,\qquad\qquad\quad\ e^{\hat{\psi}}=e^{V}d\psi,

\displaystyle e^{\hat{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pte^{V}f_{k}(\psi)\tau_{1},\qquad e^{\hat{2}}=e^{V}f_{k}(\psi)\tau_{2},\qquad e^{\hat{3}}=e^{V}f_{k}(\psi)\tau_{3},

(3.83)

we find the following non-vanishing components of the spin connection

{\omega_{\hat{t}}}^{\hat{t}\hat{r}}=U^{\prime},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{I}}}^{\hat{J}\hat{r}}=V^{\prime}\delta^{\hat{J}}_{\hat{I}},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{\psi}}=e^{-V}\frac{f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)}{f_{k}(\psi)}\delta^{\hat{j}}_{\hat{i}},\qquad{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{l}}=e^{-V}\frac{1}{f_{k}(\psi)}{\varepsilon_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{l}}\,.

(3.84)

We have split the six-dimensional coordinates as
x^{\mu}=(t,r,x^{I})
,
I=(\psi,i)
for
i=1,2,3
, and used
\hat{I}=\{\hat{\psi},\hat{i}\}
as a flat index on
K^{4}_{k}
with
\hat{i},\hat{j},\hat{l}=\hat{1},\hat{2},\hat{3}
. The
SU(2)
spin connections on
K^{4}_{k}
are given by
{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{\psi}}
and
{\omega_{\hat{i}}}^{\hat{j}\hat{l}}
. To perform a topological twist, we turn on
SO(3)
gauge fields with the following ansatz

{A_{\hat{1}}}^{23}={A_{\hat{2}}}^{31}={A_{\hat{3}}}^{12}=e^{-V}\frac{p}{4k}\frac{[f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)-1]}{f_{k}(\psi)}\,.

(3.85)

We then consider all gauge groups containing an
SO(3)
subgroup. By choosing the
SO(3)
gauge generators to be
X_{12}
,
X_{13}
, and
X_{23}
, we can describe all possible gauge groups using the embedding tensor characterized by the following symmetric tensor

Y_{mn}=\text{diag}(1,1,1,\kappa,\lambda).

(3.86)

There are six possible gauge groups given by
SO(5)
(
\kappa=\lambda=1
),
SO(4,1)
(
\kappa=-\lambda=1
),
SO(3,2)
(
\kappa=\lambda=-1
),
CSO(4,0,1)
(
\kappa=1
,
\lambda=0
),
CSO(3,1,1)
(
\kappa=-1
,
\lambda=0
), and
CSO(3,0,2)
(
\kappa=\lambda=0
).

In addition to the dilaton, there are four
SO(3)
singlet scalars corresponding to the following non-compact generators

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-3\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-3\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{5}},\qquad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}\,=\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}},\qquad\quad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{4}\,=\,\boldsymbol{s}_{45}

(3.87)

leading to the coset representative of the form

V=e^{\varphi\boldsymbol{d}+\phi_{1}\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}+\phi_{2}\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}+\phi_{3}\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}+\varsigma\overline{\mathcal{Y}}_{4}}.

(3.88)

With
B_{\mu\nu,m}=0
, the only non-vanishing components of the modified gauge field strengths are given by

{\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{1}}}^{23}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}}^{23}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{2}}}^{31}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{3}\hat{1}}}^{31}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{3}}}^{12}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}}^{12}=-\frac{e^{-2V}p}{4}.

(3.89)

These satisfy all the Bianchi identities without any further conditions. However, there is a non-vanishing term in the two-/four-form duality given in (
2.17
) of the form

\mathbb{T}^{Am}M_{AB}\mathbb{T}^{B}_{np}{\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\theta}\hat{\zeta}}}^{np}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-2(V-\varphi-2\phi_{1})}p\varsigma\delta^{m}_{\bar{n}}\,.

(3.90)

In this equation, we have split the
GL(5)
fundamental index as
m=(\bar{m},4,5)
for
\bar{m}=1,2,3
. In order to keep
p\neq 0
, we will set
\varsigma=0
such that the two-/four-form duality is identically satisfied.

We now impose the twist condition
gp=k
and the projectors
\hat{\gamma}_{r}\epsilon_{\pm}=\epsilon_{\mp}
as well as

\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}=-{(\gamma_{12})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}=-{(\gamma_{23})_{\alpha}}^{\beta}\epsilon_{+\beta},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{1}}\epsilon_{+\alpha}=\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}\epsilon_{+\alpha},

(3.91)

together with

\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}=-{(\gamma_{\dot{1}\dot{2}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}=-{(\gamma_{\dot{2}\dot{3}})_{\dot{\alpha}}}^{\dot{\beta}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\beta}},\qquad\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{1}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}=\hat{\gamma}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}\epsilon_{-\dot{\alpha}}\,.

(3.92)

The Killing spinors take the form

\epsilon_{+}(r)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left[U(r)+W(r)\gamma_{45}\right]}\epsilon_{+}^{0}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\epsilon_{-}(r)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left[U(r)-W(r)\gamma_{\dot{4}\dot{5}}\right]}\epsilon_{-}^{0}

(3.93)

for an
r
-dependent function
W(r)
. With all these, the resulting BPS equations read

\displaystyle U^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{4\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.94)

\displaystyle-\frac{3p}{4\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{4\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.95)

\displaystyle+\frac{3p}{4\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{20\sqrt{2}}\left[3+e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.96)

\displaystyle+\frac{3p}{20\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}},

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi-8\phi_{1}}}{10\sqrt{2}}\left[2-e^{20\phi_{1}}\left((\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa-\lambda)\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)\right]

(3.97)

\displaystyle-\frac{p}{5\sqrt{2}}e^{-2V-\varphi+8\phi_{1}}

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{ge^{\varphi+12\phi_{1}}}{\sqrt{2}}(\kappa+\lambda)\sinh{2\phi_{2}}\,\text{sech }{4\phi_{3}},

(3.98)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{ge^{\varphi+12\phi_{1}}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left((\kappa-\lambda)\cosh{4\phi_{3}}-(\kappa+\lambda)\cosh{2\phi_{2}}\sinh{4\phi_{3}}\right)

(3.99)

together with

W^{\prime}=-\frac{ge^{\varphi+12\phi_{1}}}{2\sqrt{2}}(\kappa+\lambda)\sinh{2\phi_{2}}\tanh{4\phi_{3}}\,.

(3.100)

It can be verified that these BPS equations imply all the second-ordered field equations. We also note that for
k=0
, the twist condition implies
p=0
. This leads to untwisted solutions of standard flat domain walls preserving sixteen supercharges. As in the previous cases, we will not further consider this type of solutions. On the other hand, for
k=\pm 1
, the above projection conditions imply that the solutions preserve only two supercharges.

For
CSO(3,0,2)
gauge group with
\kappa=\lambda=0
, the BPS equations can be analytically solved. In this case, the BPS equations lead to
\phi^{\prime}_{2}=\phi^{\prime}_{3}=W^{\prime}=0
, and we can choose
\phi_{2}=\phi_{3}=W=0
without loss of generality. The other BPS equations give
U^{\prime}=-5\varphi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime}_{1}=-\frac{4}{3}\varphi^{\prime}
with the solutions given by

U=-5\varphi\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\phi_{1}=C_{1}-\frac{4}{3}\varphi

(3.101)

for an integration constant
C_{1}
. We have neglected an additive integration constant for
U
which can be absorbed by rescaling the time coordinate.

Taking a linear combination
V^{\prime}+5\varphi^{\prime}
and changing to a new radial coordinate
\rho
given by
\frac{d\rho}{dr}=e^{4\varphi+8\phi_{1}-V}
, we find

V=\ln\left[\frac{3p\rho}{2\sqrt{2}}+C\right]-5\varphi\,.

(3.102)

The integration constant
C
can also be set to zero by shifting the coordinate
\rho
. With all these results and
C=0

\varphi^{\prime}
gives

\varphi=\frac{6}{5}C_{1}-\frac{3}{40}\ln\left[\frac{9(p\rho^{10/3}+C_{0})}{20g\rho^{4/3}}\right]

(3.103)

in which
C_{0}
is another integration constant.

\rho\rightarrow+\infty

\displaystyle U\sim\frac{3}{4}\ln\rho,\qquad V\sim\frac{7}{4}\ln\rho,\qquad\varphi\sim-\frac{3}{20}\ln\rho,\qquad\phi_{1}\sim\frac{1}{5}\ln\rho

(3.104)

which leads to the six-dimensional metric of the form

(3.105)

As

(3.106)

leading to the six-dimensional metric

ds^{2}=-\rho^{-1}dt^{2}+\rho^{\frac{7}{3}}d\rho^{2}+\rho ds^{2}_{K^{4}_{k}}.

3.104

3.105

(3.109)

K^{4}_{k}
giving rise to

4
Supersymmetric quantum mechanics from
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged supergravity

In this section, we repeat the same analysis for solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on a four-manifold from six-dimensional gauged supergravity with
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
. This gauge group arises from the embedding tensor in
\overline{\mathbf{40}}^{-1}
representation of
GL(5)
. The explicit form of the embedding tensor is given by

\theta^{Am}=\mathbb{T}^{A}_{np}U^{np,m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\theta^{A}}_{m}\ =\ 0\,.

(4.1)

We also recall that the tensor
U^{mn,p}
satisfies the conditions
U^{mn,p}=U^{[mn],p}
and
U^{[mn,p]}=0
. The quadratic constraint can be solved by taking

U^{mn,p}\ =\ v^{[m}w^{n]p}

(4.2)

in which
v^{m}
is a
GL(5)
vector and
w^{mn}
is a symmetric tensor,
w^{mn}=w^{(mn)}
.

Using
SL(5)
symmetry, we can choose the vector
v^{m}=\delta^{m}_{5}
and split the
SL(5)
index
m=(i,5)
with
i=1,\ldots,4
. In addition, we also restrict the discussion to cases with
w^{i5}=w^{55}=0
. With a proper
SL(4)\subset SL(5)
transformation that leaves
v^{m}=\delta^{m}_{5}
invariant, we can write
w^{ij}
as

w^{ij}\ =\ \text{diag}(\underbrace{1,..,1}_{p},\underbrace{-1,..,-1}_{q},\underbrace{0,..,0}_{r})

(4.3)

with
p+q+r=4
. The non-vanishing gauge generators are given by

X_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\varepsilon_{ijl_{1}l_{2}}w^{l_{1}l_{3}}{\boldsymbol{t}^{l_{2}}}_{l_{3}}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad X^{i}=w^{ij}\boldsymbol{s}_{5j}\,.

(4.4)

These generators satisfy the following Lie algebra

[X^{i},X^{j}]=0,\qquad[X_{ij},X^{l}]={(X_{ij})_{l^{\prime}}}^{l}X^{l^{\prime}},\qquad[X_{i_{1}i_{2}},X_{i_{3}i_{4}}]={(X_{i_{1}i_{2}})_{i_{3}i_{4}}}^{i_{5}i_{6}}X_{i_{5}i_{6}}

(4.5)

with
{(X_{i_{1}i_{2}})_{i_{3}i_{4}}}^{i_{5}i_{6}}=2{(X_{i_{1}i_{2}})_{[i_{3}}}^{[i_{5}}\delta_{i_{4}]}^{i_{6}]}
. The gauge group is then given by

G_{0}=CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}=SO(p,q)\ltimes\left(\mathbb{R}^{(p+q)(4-p-q)}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\right).

(4.6)

The
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\subset SL(4)\subset SL(5)
factor and the four-dimensional translation group
\mathbb{R}^{4}
are respectively generated by
X_{ij}
and
X^{i}
.

In this case, it is convenient to describe scalar fields in the
SL(4)
basis. In accordance with the branching
SL(5)\rightarrow SL(4)\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we split the
SO(5)\times SO(5)
vector indices as
a=(i,5)
and
\dot{a}=(\dot{i},\dot{5})
. The
SL(5)
non-compact generators then decompose as

\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{a\dot{b}}\rightarrow\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{j}},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{5}},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}}\right).

(4.7)

The
\mathbb{R}^{+}\sim SO(1,1)
generator is given by

\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}-4\,\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}}

(4.8)

while the non-compact generators of
SL(4)
can be written as

\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{j}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{j}}+\frac{1}{4}\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}}\delta_{i\dot{j}}

(4.9)

with
\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}}=-\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{5\dot{5}}
due to the tracelessness of
SL(5)
generators. The nine scalar fields corresponding to
\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{j}}
generators parametrize an
SL(4)/SO(4)\subset SL(5)/SO(5)
coset. The remaining four scalars associated with
\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{i\dot{5}}
are nilpotent scalars and will be denoted by
b_{i}
. In addition to the dilaton associated with
\boldsymbol{d}
generator, there are also ten axions corresponding to the antisymmetric shift generators
\boldsymbol{s}_{mn}
as in the previous section.

The gauge generators couple to ten gauge fields denoted by
{A_{\mu}}^{ij}={A_{\mu}}^{[ij]}
and
A_{\mu,i}
in the gauge covariant derivatives

D_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}-gA_{\mu,i}X^{i}-g{A_{\mu}}^{ij}X_{ij}\,.

(4.10)

We will perform topological twists by turning on gauge fields
{A_{\mu}}^{ij}
associated with compact residual symmetries that are subgroups of the
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)
factor and truncate out all the gauge fields
A_{\mu,i}
. Moreover, since gaugings in
\overline{\mathbf{40}}^{-1}
representation are purely electric, the three-forms
C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}
and the magnetic two-forms
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
do not appear in the Lagrangian.

As in the previous section, the gauge fields arising from the topological twist on a four-manifold usually lead to a non-vanishing term in the Bianchi identity (
2.14
) of the form
(\Gamma_{m})_{AB}\mathbb{T}^{A}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbb{T}^{B}_{i_{3}i_{4}}{\mathcal{H}_{[\mu\nu}}^{i_{1}i_{2}}{\mathcal{H}_{\rho\sigma]}}^{i_{3}i_{4}}
. However, unlike the case of
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
gauge group, there are no magnetic two-form fields
{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}
to cancel this term in such a way that the Bianchi identity is satisfied. On the other hand, the Bianchi identity could be recovered by turning on the electric two-form fields
B_{\mu\nu,m}
to make

\mathcal{D}_{[\mu}\mathcal{H}_{\nu\rho\lambda],m}=\frac{3}{2\sqrt{2}}(\Gamma_{m})_{AB}\mathbb{T}^{A}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbb{T}^{B}_{i_{3}i_{4}}{\mathcal{H}_{[\mu\nu}}^{i_{1}i_{2}}{\mathcal{H}_{\rho\sigma]}}^{i_{3}i_{4}}

(4.11)

However, we are not able to find a consistent set of BPS equations with non-vanishing two-form fields. Therefore, in this section, we will set all the tensor fields to zero namely

B_{\mu\nu,m}=0,\qquad{B_{\mu\nu}}^{m}=0,\qquad C_{\mu\nu\rho,A}=0

(4.12)

and solve the problematic Bianchi identity by requiring the term
(\Gamma_{m})_{AB}\mathbb{T}^{A}_{i_{1}i_{2}}\mathbb{T}^{B}_{i_{3}i_{4}}{\mathcal{H}_{[\mu\nu}}^{i_{1}i_{2}}{\mathcal{H}_{\rho\sigma]}}^{i_{3}i_{4}}
to vanish identically. This condition is rather restrictive. In particular, a topological twist on a Riemannian four-manifold
\Sigma_{k}^{4}
by turning on
SO(4)
gauge fields is not possible. In the following analysis, we will then consider topological twists only on four-dimensional manifolds given by a product of two Riemann surfaces
\Sigma_{k_{1}}^{2}\times\Sigma_{k_{2}}^{2}
and a Kahler four-cycle
K_{k}^{4}
in this section.

4.1
D4-branes wrapped on a product of two Riemann surfaces

For solutions describing D4-branes wrapped on a product of two Riemann surfaces, the ansatz for the metric is still given in (
3.36
). To cancel the spin connection on

There are two gauge groups that contain an

and

V=e^{\varphi\boldsymbol{d}+\phi_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}+\phi\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}+\varsigma_{1}\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}+\varsigma_{2}\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}}\,.

(4.18)

while

factor in

SO(5,5)

\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}=\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}-\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}=\boldsymbol{s}_{12},\qquad\widehat{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}=\boldsymbol{s}_{34}\,.

4.2
D4-branes wrapped on a Kahler four-cycle

We now consider D4-branes wrapped on a Kahler four-cycle. Unlike the previous case of
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
gauge group, we can not perform an
SU(2)
twist on a Kahler four-cycle. As previously mentioned, in the absence of tensor fields, the Bianchi identity (
2.14

SU(2)_{\pm}\subset SO(4)
gauge fields to vanish.

4.2.1
Solutions with
SO(3)
twist

We first consider an
SO(3)

,
SO(3,1)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
, and
CSO(3,0,1)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauge groups which can be collectively characterized by a symmetric tensor

w^{ij}=\textrm{diag}(1,1,1,\kappa)

(4.29)

for
\kappa=1,-1,0
, respectively. The metric ansatz is still given by (
3.79
) with the metric on
K^{4}_{k}
given in (
3.80
). To cancel the
SU(2)
part of the spin connection, we turn on
SO(3)
gauge fields of the form

{A_{\hat{1}}}^{14}={A_{\hat{2}}}^{24}={A_{\hat{3}}}^{34}=-\frac{e^{-V}p}{2\sqrt{2}k}\frac{[f^{\prime}_{k}(\psi)-1]}{f_{k}(\psi)}\,.

(4.30)

There are five
SO(3)
singlet scalars consisting of the two dilatons and three additional scalars corresponding to the following non-compact generators

\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}=\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{1\dot{1}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{2\dot{2}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{3\dot{3}}-3\,\overline{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{4}},\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}=\hat{\boldsymbol{t}}^{+}_{4\dot{5}},\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}=\boldsymbol{s}_{45}\,.

(4.31)

The coset representative then takes the form

V=e^{\varphi\boldsymbol{d}+\phi_{0}\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}+\phi\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{1}+b\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{2}+\varsigma\widetilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{3}}\,.

(4.32)

The gauge two-form field strengths are given by

{\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{1}}}^{14}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{2}\hat{3}}}^{14}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{2}}}^{24}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{3}\hat{1}}}^{24}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{\psi}\hat{3}}}^{34}={\mathcal{H}_{\hat{1}\hat{2}}}^{34}=\frac{e^{-2V}p}{2\sqrt{2}}\,.

(4.33)

We also note that these non-vanishing components lead to
\mathcal{H}^{ij}\wedge\mathcal{H}^{kl}=0
such that the Bianchi identities are satisfied identically. However, this is not the case for
SU(2)
and
SO(4)
twists.

twist by imposing a twist condition
gp=k
together with the projections (

), (
3.92

4.20

3.31

5
Conclusions and discussions

We have studied a large number of supersymmetric solutions interpolating between locally flat domain walls and
t\times\mathcal{M}_{4}
-sliced domain walls from maximal gauged supergravity in six dimensions with
CSO(p,q,5-p-q)
and
CSO(p,q,4-p-q)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{4}
gauged groups. The four-manifold with constant curvature
\mathcal{M}_{4}
under consideration here is given by a Riemannian four-manifold, a product of two Riemann surfaces and a Kahler four-cycle. By performing different types of topological twists, various amounts of supersymmetry are preserved along the entire solutions. These solutions are expected to give holographic duals of RG flows across dimensions from the maximal SYM theories in five dimensions dual to the locally flat domain wall to supersymmetric quantum mechanics in the IR.

Upon uplifted to type IIA theory, many of these solutions possess physically acceptable IR singularities. Therefore, these solutions could be interpreted as D4-branes wrapped on
\mathcal{M}_{4}
giving rise to supersymmetric quantum mechanics via twisted compactifications of the maximal five-dimensional SYM on the world-volume of D4-branes. Due to recent interests in the holographic study of matrix quantum mechanics, we hope the results given in this paper would be useful along this line. Rather than considering supersymmetric quantum mechanics on the world-volume of D0-branes as in many previous works, we have given a new class of gravity duals to supersymmetric quantum mechanics from D4-branes wrapped on four-manifolds. This might lead to some insight into supersymmetric quantum mechanics via twisted compactifications of maximal SYM in five dimensions. With some of the solutions given analytically, we hope the present results could provide a very useful tool to further study along these and related directions.

It would be interesting to find holographic correlation functions for the resulting supersymmetric quantum mechanics as in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
using holographic renormalization. Identifying both the dual SYM theories in five dimensions and the associated quantum mechanics could also be of particular interest. Constructing the complete truncation ansatze of type IIA theory on both
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{5-p-q}
and
H^{p,q}\times\mathbb{R}^{4-p-q}\times S^{1}
is also worth considering. This could be used to uplift the solutions found here and in
[
31
]
to the complete solutions of type IIA theory in which an interpretation in terms of D4-branes wrapped on compact manifolds of different dimensions can be given.

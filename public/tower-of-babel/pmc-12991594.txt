Medicinal Leech Therapy in Acute Herpes Zoster Condition- A Case Report
Authors: Pawan B. Itankar, Gaurav R. Sawarkar, Dipika A. Chakole
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTHerpes zoster is an acute viral skin disease characterized by the painful skin rashes, burning sensation, blisters, and post-herpetic neuralgia caused by varicella zoster virus (VZV). In immune-compromised people, many dermatomes are implicated. In Ayurveda, it is correlated with visarpa having the same clinical presentation as herpes zoster. The study aims to explore the effectiveness of medicinal leech therapy and internal ayurvedic medicinal formulation in acute herpes zoster conditions. A 26-year-old male patient visited OPD (outpatient department) with a complaint of reddish rashes and a small vesicular eruption over the left lateral aspect of the chest associated with a burning sensation and pain. Leech therapy was performed promptly as an emergency treatment, along with internal medication for the next 10 days. This case report concludes that Leech therapy and internal ayurvedic medicine are found to be effective management of acute herpes zoster.

INTRODUCTION

Herpes zoster is an acute viral skin disease characterized by the painful skin rashes, burning sensation, blisters, and post-herpetic neuralgia caused by varicella zoster virus (VZV). It is reactivated from the dorsal root ganglion within the patient’s body. It affects people of all ages, but it is most common after the age of 60 years. It is a single dermatome vesicular eruption that’s unilateral,[1] and the production of vesicle clusters follows erythema and oedema.[2] The use of antiviral drugs, steroids, or non steroidal anti inflammatory drugs (NSAIDS) reduces pain temporarily but does not alleviate burning sensations because of the patient’s reduced immunity; there is a greater risk of consequences such as post-herpetic neuralgia. This acute condition has a similar clinical presentation to “Visarpa,” a disease condition explained in Charak and Sushruta Samhita. Visarpa mainly involves tri dosha and four dushya, i.e. Vata, pitta kapha and rakta, lasika, twak, and mamsa, respectively.[3] In both Samhita, Raktamokshan (bloodletting therapy) is the main principal treatment explained. This case report deals with managing herpes zoster by medicinal leech therapy.

PATIENT AND OBSERVATION

Patient information: A 26 yrs. old male visited OPD (outpatient department), complaining of reddish rashes, a small vesicular eruption associated with a burning sensation, and pain over the left lateral aspect of the chest for 2 days.

Medical, family, and psychosocial history: No relevant genetic history was reported. The patient’s illness posed difficulty in his activities, notably, while wearing clothing, due to a burning sensation over the affected area.

Relevant past interventions and their outcomes: The patient took no any past intervention.

Clinical findings: In clinical examination, his vital is within normal limits; however, local examination reveals reddish rashes and small vesicular eruptions across the left lateral aspect of the chest region illustrated in Figure 1, with no odor, discharge, bleeding, or any secretion. His local site pain was 6/10 on the VAS, and tenderness[4] was graded 3 out of 4.

Appearance of the herpes zoster over left lateral aspect of chest on day 1

Timeline of current episode: Initially, the patient acquired a reddish rash over the left lateral aspect of his chest with a slight burning sensation. The following day, a small vesicular eruption appeared over the rash, coupled with pain and tenderness, and the intensity of the burning sensation increased after the vesicular eruption.

DIAGNOSTIC ASSESSMENT

Diagnostic method: Herpes zoster was diagnosed based on the physical examination; a single dermatome vesicular eruption with distinctive red rashes over the left chest region was seen, suggesting herpes zoster.

Diagnostic challenges: Leech therapy, along with topical application, will help to prevent the development of post-herpetic neuralgia, which arises in many cases after the lesion heals.

Diagnosis: The final diagnosis was herpes zoster; before the commencement of vesicular eruption, contact dermatitis, or insect bite were alternative possible diagnosis.

THERAPEUTIC INTERVENTION

Types of therapeutic intervention: Initially, bloodletting therapy was performed in which leeches were applied over the lesion [Figure 2], and Shatadhaut ghrita was used topically for ten days. Internal ayurvedic medication, including Triphala Guggul, Samshamani Vati, and Chandrakala Rasa, was prescribed for 10 days.

Application of leeches over the site of herpes zoster

Administration of therapeutic intervention: Triphala guggulu 250 mg, two tabs, twice a day after meals, Samshamani vati 250 mg two tabs, three times daily after food, Chandrakala rasa 250 mg, two tabs, after meal given for 10 days.

Changes in therapeutic intervention: The therapeutic intervention remains unchanged throughout the course of treatment.

FOLLOW-UP AND OUTCOMES

Clinician- and patient-assessed outcomes: The patient’s pain was greatly reduced from VAS 6/10 to 2/10, and tenderness was graded zero from 3 on day 5. Also, whitish skin appeared over the lesion, the reddish rash disappeared, and vesicular eruption diminished [Figure 3]. On day 10, the herpes zoster healed, the pain was reduced from VAS 2 to 0/10, and normal skin appeared [Figure 4].

Appearance of whitish skin and the vesicular eruption diminished on day 5

Normal skin appeared and the herpes zoster healed on day 10

Follow-up diagnostic and other test results: Following the intervention, the patient’s everyday activities were evaluated through clinical follow-up, and no more diagnostic testing was required.

Intervention adherence and tolerability: The patient reported no negative drug effects and followed the treatment plan exactly. Follow-up evaluations verified tolerability.

Adverse and unanticipated events: No unexpected or negative events occurred throughout treatment.

Patient perspective: “After healing from herpes zoster, I feel immense relief and gratitude. The rash healed, and the pain gradually faded. I’m thankful to be back to my normal routine, with no more burning sensations or sensitivity.”

DISCUSSION

Acute herpes zoster condition is commonly co-related with visarpa in Ayurveda. While describing the treatment for visarpa, Acharya mentioned Raktamoksha (Bloodletting therapy) as the most superior treatment.[5] In this case of acute herpes zoster condition, leech therapy was done on 1st visit of the patient to the OPD. Leech has analgesic and anti-inflammatory properties.[6] So, this leech therapy was effective in this case, as the patient had 25%–30% instant relief in pain and burning sensation just after leech therapy. Acharya also explained the use of raktamokshana (bloodletting therapy) where blood is involved as a vehicle.[7] In Sushruta samhita, there is an indication of raktamokshana (bloodletting therapy), especially jalaukavcharana (bloodletting therapy by leeches) in pitta pradhan condition.[8] Triphala Guggulu, Chandrakala rasa, and Samshamani vati were given internally to the patient for 10 days. According to various studies, Triphala Guggulu has anti-inflammatory and anti-microbial properties.[9] Chandrakala rasa is an ayurvedic medicine used in neuropathy conditions. Samshamani vati is preparation of Guduchi (Tinospora cardifolia) was especially used for rejuvenation purpose. For topical application on the skin lesion, Shatadhauta ghrita was prescribed. It is commonly used as a topical medicinal preparation for burning sensation and many skin-manifested symptoms.

CONCLUSION

Most commonly, people seek modern medicinal management in emergencies, but ayurvedic management is more efficient in such cases. This case report is an example of significant ayurvedic management. Leech therapy is explained in various literature of Ayurveda and there are separate chapters for its uses in multiple conditions. In this case report, leech therapy was found effective as there was significant relief to the patient in a short period.

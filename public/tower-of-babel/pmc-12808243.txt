Self-attention enhanced GraphSAGE for UAV fault diagnosis using vibration signals
Authors: Yumeng Ma, Yuhan Sun, Ligang Chen, Hailiang Wang, Boxun Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTraditional data-driven fault diagnosis methods for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) often struggle to in effectively extract fault features from complex vibration signals. To address this challenge, this paper proposes an enhanced fault diagnosis framework based on GraphSAGE, incorporating a self-attention mechanism (GraphSAGE-SA). Initially, a customized vibration data acquisition system was developed using MPU6050 sensors to capture vibration data across various operational conditions. Subsequently, the K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) algorithm was employed to convert one-dimensional vibration signals into graph-structured data, revealing explicit topological relationships. The proposed GraphSAGE-SA model aggregates information from both nodes and neighbors via a hierarchical sampling process. The integrated self-attention mechanism then adaptively adjusts the importance of different neighbors, facilitating more precise feature extraction. Experimental validation conducted on a quadrotor UAV platform demonstrated that the proposed method achieves an impressive fault classification accuracy of 98%, surpassing conventional GraphSAGE variants by 3–12% and other graph-based approaches. This superior performance is attributed to the model’s ability to capture local structural patterns and global dependencies simultaneously, enabled by the attention mechanism. This research presents a novel and effective solution for intelligent fault diagnosis in UAVs and other complex mechanical systems. In real-world applications, timely and accurate fault diagnosis is essential for ensuring UAV operational safety, mission reliability, and preventing catastrophic failures.Keywords: UAV fault diagnosis, Graph neural network, GraphSAGE, Self-attention mechanism, Vibration signal analysisSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) have become essential assets across a wide spectrum of domains, including surveillance, disaster response, precision agriculture, and logistics, due to their operational flexibility, economic viability, and capability to harsh environments1–3. Nevertheless, as UAV architectures grow increasingly complex and are frequently deployed in demanding conditions, they are prone to a range of faults, such as motor anomalies and structural impairments4. Undetected or misdiagnosed faults can escalate rapidly, potentially resulting in critical system failures that compromise mission integrity and operational safety5. Consequently, there is an urgent and growing need for the development of robust and efficient fault diagnosis strategies tailored to the unique operational characteristics of UAV systems.

In the past, fault diagnosis for UAVs primarily depended on visual inspections, a method that was time-consuming, labor-intensive, and often prone to inaccuracy. Certain faults were difficult to detect and could not be identified in a timely manner6. As a result, the rapid and efficient detection of faults has remained a central focus of research. Modern UAV fault diagnosis has typically evolved through the following stages:

Fault diagnosis in rotating machinery has been a key area of research for many scholars. Existing fault diagnosis techniques can be broadly classified into several categories: signal-based, model-based, machine learning-based, and deep learning-based methods. Signal-based methods identify faults by applying various data analysis techniques, such as time-domain analysis7, frequency-domain analysis8, and time–frequency analysis9, to detect signals that deviate from normal operating conditions. Model-based fault diagnosis involve constructing an input–output model for UAVs and detecting faults by comparing the actual output with the estimated output. Asadi et al. applied model-based approaches, such as Kalman filter observers, to detect rotor faults in quadrotor UAVs10. The main challenge of this approach lies in the need for an accurate mathematical model, which is often difficult to obtain.

Machine learning-based fault diagnosis methods leverage shallow models for feature extraction and employ classifiers for fault identification. The primary methods include Support Vector Machines (SVM), Decision Trees, and K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN). Baskaya et al. and Bondyra et al. proposed an SVM model for detecting propeller damage in multi-rotor UAVs based on vibration signals collected by the onboard Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) sensor11,12. Lai et al. developed a KNN approach for detecting motor damage, achieving an accuracy rate of 90.73%, even under mixed fault conditions13. Four distinct damage modes were investigated, including motor degradation, friction-induced failures, backlash, and external perturbation effects. These modes were detected using various machine learning methods, such as BayesNet, Naive Bayes, Decision Trees, induction rules, KNN, logistic regression, and Multilayer Perceptron. The results revealed that BayesNet exhibited the best performance, with an accuracy close to 90%14. Despite the successes of machine learning-based fault diagnosis techniques, these algorithms face challenges in adaptively extracting features and require significant expertise in feature selection, ultimately limiting diagnostic accuracy.

With the advancement of computational resources and the proliferation of big data, deep learning-based fault diagnosis has gained considerable attention. These approaches typically utilize multi-layer neural networks capable of automatically learning and extracting relevant features from raw data inputs15. By leveraging hierarchical representations through hidden layers, deep learning methods significantly enhance the precision of fault identification tasks16. For instance, a convolutional neural network (CNN) was employed to detect two types of propeller damage in quadrotor UAVs using acoustic signals. The time–frequency representations of the sound data were used as inputs to the CNN, achieving an accuracy exceeding 90% in experimental validation17. In related work, sensor data from accelerometers, gyroscopes, and magnetometers, sampled at 100 Hz, were utilized to detect operational anomalies. CNN and long short-term memory networks (LSTMs) were adopted to perform fault classification, resulting in an accuracy of 88% based on empirical results18.

Traditional fault diagnosis techniques, including model-based methods and signal processing approaches, have been extensively applied in UAV systems. However, these methods often face challenges in dealing with the high-dimensional, nonlinear, and heterogeneous nature of UAV sensor data, particularly vibration data, which contains critical information regarding the health status of mechanical components19. Furthermore, the limited availability of labeled fault data further hampers the performance of data-driven methods, such as deep learning, which require large volumes of annotated data for effective training20.

Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) present several distinct advantages over traditional deep learning methods, such as Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), especially in fault diagnosis applications. While CNNs are designed for grid-like data (e.g., images), they struggle with irregular or non-Euclidean structures. In contrast, GNNs are inherently suited for graph-structured data, making them ideal for modeling complex systems like sensor networks or industrial equipment. GNNs are particularly effective in capturing the intricate dependencies between components, which are critical for precise fault diagnosis21. Moreover, GNNs leverage message-passing mechanisms to aggregate both local and global information, offering a more holistic understanding of the system’s state. This is in contrast to CNNs, which typically focus on local features and may overlook broader contextual relationships22. Additionally, GNNs are highly adaptable to dynamic changes in system structure, providing robustness in real-world applications where data evolves over time. Their ability to process multi-modal data and provide interpretable insights further enhances their utility in fault diagnosis, making GNNs a superior choice for complex, interconnected systems compared to traditional deep learning approaches23. For instance, Wang et al. proposed a fault diagnosis method for rotor-bearing systems based on FSASCA-VMD and GraphSAGE-SA, achieving over 98% diagnostic accuracy even under noisy conditions24. The application of GNNs, particularly GraphSAGE, in UAV fault diagnosis is well-justified for several reasons. First, the multi-sensor system on a UAV naturally forms a graph-structured network where nodes (sensors) are spatially related. Faults often propagate through this physical structure, creating inherent topological dependencies. Unlike traditional deep learning models like CNNs, which process data in isolation, GNNs explicitly model these inter-sensor relationships. Second, the KNN-based graph construction transforms sequential vibration data into a graph, capturing the underlying manifold structure of the high-dimensional signal space. GraphSAGE is particularly well-suited to this task, as it is an inductive learning framework that enables the model to generate embeddings for new, unseen data samples without requiring a fixed graph structure, which is—an essential feature for real-time fault diagnosis.

Despite notable advancements in UAV fault diagnosis, three critical challenges remain unresolved: (1) many existing methods fail to adequately model the complex topological relationships among multi-source sensor data; (2) conventional approaches lack adaptive feature weighting mechanisms to handle varying fault patterns; and (3) the interpretability of diagnostic results is often insufficient for practical deployment.

In this paper, we propose a novel UAV fault diagnosis method based on Graph Neural Networks, specifically leveraging the GraphSAGE algorithm combined with a self-attention mechanism. Our approach begins by collecting vibration data from UAV sensors, which is then used to construct a graph representation of the system using the KNN algorithm. This graph captures the spatial and structural relationships among sensor nodes, enabling the effective modeling of fault propagation patterns. The GraphSAGE-SA algorithm is employed to learn node embeddings and perform fault classification, providing a scalable and inductive framework that generalizes well to unseen data.

Theoretical background

Graph neural networks (GNNs)

Graph data serves as a fundamental representation method for capturing relationships, where nodes represent entities and edges denote the relationships between these entities. In graph data, the relational information between data points is as critical as the data itself. This type of graph data, based on non-Euclidean space, possesses a topological structure that mirrors the geometric arrangement of the data, making it highly effective for analyzing high-dimensional data25.

GNNs are a class of neural network architectures specifically developed to learn representations from graph-structured data.

The core concept behind GNNs is to iteratively update the representation of each node by aggregating the representations of its neighboring nodes along with its own representation26. This mechanism facilitates the propagation of information throughout the graph, allowing each node to progressively incorporate information from its neighbors, thereby capturing the intricate structures and dependencies embedded within the graph. Through multiple iterations, GNNs can learn effective node representations, which can then be leveraged for a range of downstream tasks, such as node classification, graph classification, or link prediction. GraphSAGE

As previously noted, traditional GNNs require access to the complete graph structure during training. Specifically, in each iteration, the representations of all nodes are synchronously updated. However, this paradigm becomes computationally prohibitive when dealing with large-scale graphs, as both memory consumption and processing time increase substantially, potentially rendering training infeasible. To address these scalability limitations, Hamilton et al. proposed an inductive framework known as GraphSAGE, which eliminates the need to compute embeddings for all nodes during training. Instead of operating over the full graph, GraphSAGE employs a neighborhood sampling strategy wherein a fixed-size subset of neighboring nodes is randomly selected, and their feature representations are aggregated to generate embeddings for target nodes, including those unseen during training. This inductive approach significantly improves training efficiency and model scalability, making it particularly suitable for large and evolving graphs commonly encountered in real-world applications27. Self-attention based aggregation approach

The impact of the same information varies across different classification outcomes, and the influence of different types of information on fault classification differs in magnitude. Therefore, it is essential to consider the varying weights of neighboring nodes when aggregating information, rather than simply averaging the information from all neighbors. The Transformer model, introduced by Vaswani in 2017, departs from traditional attention mechanisms by relying solely on attention mechanisms and excluding RNN-based models28. The self-attention mechanism is a key component of the Transformer model, which is composed solely of self-attention layers and fully connected layers. This architecture enables the model to allocate greater weights to more significant information and facilitates the extraction of global features from time series data. The self-attention layer can replace the RNN layer due to the identical input and output dimensions. While both GraphSAGE and self-attention mechanisms have been studied independently, their integration in this work is specifically designed to address the challenges of UAV vibration signal analysis. Unlike standard attention-augmented GNNs, which often apply attention mechanisms in a generic manner, our GraphSAGE-SA model uses self-attention to adaptively assign weights to the importance of neighboring sensor nodes based on the vibrational context. This enables the model to focus on the most relevant fault propagation paths and ignore noisy or irrelevant connections, thereby enhancing both feature discriminability and model interpretability—a critical advantage for UAV maintenance and safety in real-world applications. Data acquisition system

This study presents the design and implementation of a vibration data acquisition system based on the STM32 microcontroller and the MPU6050 sensor. As shown in Fig. 3, the schematic and physical structure of the system are illustrated. The system enables real-time acquisition and storage of vibration data on an SD card, providing a reliable data source for subsequent analysis. The STM32 microcontroller employed in this study is the STM32F103C8T6 from STMicroelectronics, which is built on the ARM Cortex-M3 core and operates at a frequency of 72 MHz. It offers a comprehensive set of on-chip resources, including flash memory, SRAM, timers, and serial interfaces, sufficient to meet the functional requirements of the system29. The MPU6050 is a six-axis motion processing sensor that integrates a three-axis gyroscope and a three-axis accelerometer, allowing for simultaneous measurement of angular velocity and acceleration. It communicates with the STM32 via the I2C interface and is characterized by its compact size, low power consumption, and high precision. The SD card storage module interacts with the STM32 through the SPI interface and is responsible for storing the acquired vibration data. The FAT32 file system is employed to manage the SD card, facilitating efficient data access and analysis.

Data collection

The collected experimental data comprise four operating conditions: the healthy state, loosening of the screw connecting the arm base and the upper plate as shown in Fig. 4a, loosening of the motor base screw as shown in Fig. 4b, and blade damage as illustrated in Fig. 4b.

Fault state: (a) loosening of the arm screw; (b) loosening of the motor screw; (c) the damaged propeller.

The data corresponding to each fault condition were collected while the UAV was in an idle state, ensuring consistency of environmental conditions during acquisition. Screw loosening was introduced by rotating the screw approximately 1.5 turns from the fully tightened position, while propeller damage was simulated by removing approximately 15% of the blade tip area. Vibration signals were sampled at a frequency of 100 Hz. For each of the four conditions (healthy and three fault types), 1000 samples were obtained, with each sample comprising a signal segment containing 1024 data points. The entire dataset was randomly divided into training (70%), validation (20%), and testing (10%) subsets, with strict control to prevent data leakage across partitions.

It is worth noting that the present study focuses on structural and propeller-related faults as a critical initial step. Motor faults, recognized as another prevalent and significant failure mode in UAVs, will be addressed in future research. The inclusion of motor fault data under dynamic flight conditions is considered an essential direction for further validating the practical robustness of the proposed model.

Results and analysis

Fault diagnosis based on GraphSAGE

The influence of K value selection

The selection of the parameter K in the KNN algorithm plays a critical role in the construction of the graph structure, which subsequently influences the performance of the GNN in fault diagnosis tasks. Experimental results indicate that varying the value of K leads to distinct graph topologies, thereby affecting the model’s capacity to capture underlying patterns in one-dimensional vibration signals. Specifically, a smaller K value tends to generate a sparse graph, which may omit important relationships between data points, resulting in reduced diagnostic accuracy. In contrast, a larger K value produces a denser graph, potentially introducing noise and redundant connections that could degrade model performance. The optimal K value achieves a balance between sparsity and connectivity, enabling the graph to effectively represent the intrinsic structure of the data while minimizing the influence of irrelevant connections. This balance is essential for achieving high diagnostic accuracy and robustness in GNN-based fault diagnosis systems. The optimal K value was determined via a grid search over K = {1, …, 9, 10}. The classification accuracy under differernt K values is presented in Fig. 6. Based on the validation results, K = 3 was selected for the final experiments, as it yielded the highest fault classification accuracy while effectively balancing graph connectivity and sparsity to capture the most relevant structural relationships in the vibration data.

The choice of aggregation method

The choice of aggregation method in GraphSAGE is pivotal to the effectiveness of the GNN in fault diagnosis applications. Experimental results demonstrate that different aggregation strategies—such as mean, LSTM, and pooling—exhibit varying capabilities in capturing structural and feature-level information from the graph. The mean aggregator, while offering high computational efficiency, tends to oversmooth local variations, potentially suppressing critical diagnostic features in one-dimensional vibration signals. In contrast, the LSTM aggregator, equipped with sequential processing capabilities, excels at modeling complex dependencies and temporal patterns, albeit with increased computational cost. The pooling aggregator provides a compromise between efficiency and representational power, leveraging its selective aggregation mechanism to emphasize salient features. The optimal choice of aggregator depends on the characteristics of the dataset and the specific diagnostic requirements. Findings suggest that the LSTM aggregator generally yields superior performance when handling datasets with intricate temporal dependencies, whereas the pooling aggregator is better suited for scenarios demanding a trade-off between accuracy and computational overhead. These observations underscore the critical role of aggregator selection in enhancing the diagnostic capability of GraphSAGE for fault diagnosis tasks.

As illustrated in Fig. 7, the proposed GraphSAGE-SA model achieves a final classification accuracy of approximately 98%, outperforming the baseline GraphSAGE model with mean aggregation, which attains an accuracy of around 93%, by a notable margin of 5 percentage points. This result highlights the substantial enhancement in feature extraction capability introduced by the integrated self-attention mechanism. Traditional methods, including CNN and SVM, were also included in the comparative experiments.

Robustness and stability analysis

The diagnostic reliability of GraphSAGE-SA is theoretically supported by the principles of graph smoothness and Lipschitz stability. Given the construction of a KNN graph with Gaussian kernel weighting, the graph Laplacian ensures that minor perturbations in node features induce only limited variations in node embeddings. In addition, the incorporation of a self-attention mechanism enhances model robustness by assigning greater weights to relevant neighbors, thereby effectively suppressing noisy or irrelevant connections. Formally, the attention-based aggregation satisfies a form of Lipschitz continuity in the feature space, which contributes to consistent fault identification under varying levels of noise. The experimental results, which consistently achieve accuracy exceeding 98% across all fault categories and data partitions, provide empirical validation of this theoretical stability.

Conclusion and future work

This study proposes a self-attention enhanced GraphSAGE framework (GraphSAGE-SA) for intelligent fault diagnosis of UAVs using vibration signals. The core of the approach lies in transforming one-dimensional vibration data into graph-structured representations via a KNN algorithm, thereby explicitly capturing the topological relationships among sensor nodes. By integrating a self-attention mechanism into the GraphSAGE architecture, the model performs adaptive, non-local aggregation of neighborhood information, enabling dynamic focus on the most relevant fault-related features while effectively suppressing noise.

The proposed framework was rigorously evaluated on a real-world quadrotor UAV platform under various structural and propeller fault conditions. Experimental results demonstrate that GraphSAGE-SA achieves a high fault classification accuracy of 98%, substantially outperforming conventional GraphSAGE variants (by approximately 5%), other graph-based models such as GCN and GAT, as well as traditional deep learning baselines including CNN and LSTM. These results confirm the effectiveness of combining graph-based structural learning with attention-driven feature refinement for complex vibration signal analysis, offering a novel and robust solution to the challenges of feature extraction and dependency modeling in UAV fault diagnosis.

Despite these promising results, this study has several limitations that outline directions for future research:

(i)Fault and Operational Scenarios: The current validation was performed under a limited set of structural faults and in an idle state. Future work should incorporate critical motor faults (e.g., bearing wear, winding imbalances) and evaluate the model under dynamic flight conditions with real aerodynamic loads to assess its practical robustness and generalizability.

Role of Haematological Inflammatory indices in the Occurrence of Coronary Artery Disease: A Preliminary Retrospective Case-Control Study
Authors: Pritika Dutta, Thoushara Manmmadhan, Dhiren Punja, Vineetha K Ramdas Nayak
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Coronary artery disease (CAD) is a paramount cardiovascular disease that significantly contributes to global mortality. Recent literature reports hematological inflammatory indices as promising surrogate markers for identifying CAD. This study aims to simultaneously investigate the potential of five inflammatory indices to assess the association with the occurrence of CAD in the Asian Indian population.Methodology:We conducted an age-matched retrospective case-control study among one hundred and thirty-one male cases and controls, and ninety-seven female cases and controls, based on the coronary angiogram performed in the Department of Cardiology in a tertiary referral hospital in southern India. From the Complete Blood Count reports, Neutrophil-to–lymphocyte ratio (NLR), Platelet-to-Lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and Lymphocyte-to-Monocyte ratio (LMR) were calculated for each subject. Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR) and Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) values were collected from the laboratory reports. Student t-test and Mann-Whitney test were applied for analysing data that denoted Gaussian distribution and skewed distribution, respectively. To obtain diagnostic accuracy, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were applied, designating the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity.Results:Among the five inflammatory biomarkers, ESR and NLR demonstrated comparatively better discriminatory ability in both males (AUC=0.717, 0.7397) and females (AUC=0.8042, 0.7397).Conclusion:The findings suggest that ESR and NLR are quick and cost-effective biomarkers and may serve as adjunctive markers of coronary artery disease in the Asian Indian population.

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is an atherosclerotic disorder that significantly contributes to the global mortality rate.[1] India reports the highest number of cases of ST-elevation myocardial infarction (MI) globally.[2] The key pathophysiological process in the development of atherosclerotic plaques, followed by rupture, erosion of blood vessels, and progression to CAD, is the inflammatory response.[3] The pathophysiological mechanism involves neutrophils and lymphocytes activating several processes of inflammatory response in combination with the transformation of macrophages into foam cells, which phagocytose low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol. Neutrophils secrete neutrophil elastase along with proteinase-3, which disintegrates the vessel walls.[4] Owing to the process of ‘crosstalk’ amongst endothelial cells alongside leukocytes, platelets secrete chemokines which promote adhesion and transmigration of monocytes, hence accelerating atherosclerosis.[5]

Haematological inflammatory indices are new, cost‐beneficial, and are calculated from easy-to-obtain diagnostic tests. Indices like Neutrophil-to–lymphocyte ratio (NLR), Platelet-to-Lymphocyte ratio (PLR), and Lymphocyte-to-Monocyte ratio (LMR) can be manually calculated as ratios from Complete Blood count (CBC). Diagnostic tests like the Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR) and Mean Platelet Volume (MPV) are a part of CBC. Current studies have concluded that NLR stands an even better chance in the prediction of an acute or chronic cardiovascular event, as compared to neutrophil count or total WBC count.[6] PLR is a novel concept that can not only predict the presence of CAD in an individual but also has a positive correlation with the Gensini score.[7] According to recent evidence, LMR has now emerged as a significant indirect biomarker of inflammation and inflammatory responses.[8] Even though ESR is one of the oldest standardized laboratory methods, only a few studies, like Yayan et al. (2012), have concluded that ESR has a useful predictive value and could be utilized as an additional diagnostic criterion for CAD.[9]

According to reports, Indians manifest CAD a decade prematurely than their Western counterparts.[2] India is a lower to higher-income country with restricted resources and a population with a higher risk for developing CAD. The reduction of the burden of CAD in the Indian population would involve primary care specifically focusing on preventive strategies, effective screening, and lifestyle counselling. At present, there is a deficiency of systematic data from studies exploring the usefulness of haematological inflammatory markers to predict the occurrence of CAD in the Indian population.

In the current study, we aim to investigate the above inflammatory markers, like ESR, NLR, PLR, MPV, and LMR, and to evaluate and compare the association of selected inflammatory markers with CAD. Haematological inflammatory indices can prove to be simple yet valuable adjunctive markers, keeping in mind the massive population who have the possibility of developing CAD in the time to come.

Material and Methods

Study Design and Setting

We performed a retrospective case-control study in the Department of Cardiology at a tertiary care referral hospital located in South India from January - December 2017. The data were obtained from the case records of all patients who were hospitalised and had undergone a coronary angiogram due to symptoms of chest pain during the period, December 2015 to December 2016. The said study was sanctioned by the Institutional Ethics Committee (IEC 503/2017).

Study Population

Using a retrospective study design, all the data were assembled from the electronic medical record database system. Using the simple random sampling technique, a sample size of 461 cases and controls each was calculated (n= 922). As the data was collected retrospectively, a total of 896 records were screened, of

which 456 records were analyzed further. A total of 440 records were excluded due to missing data. The records were scrutinized based on clinical and angiographic reports.

Definition of cases and controls

A case of CAD was defined based on presenting symptoms of chest pain and referred for coronary angiography. Patients with stenosis ranging from 50 % to 100% according to the reports of coronary angiogram were identified as cases, and those with no stenosis (0%) visible on angiogram and diagnosed to have a non-cardiac cause of chest pain were identified as controls, ensuring clinical comparability, although they may not represent a completely healthy population.[10]

With a view to procuring a satisfactory control population, the electronic database was searched for age and gender-matched patients who presented to the cardiology department with complaints of chest pain due to non-cardiac causes.

Inclusion Criteria

Age 35-70 years of either gender who had reported to the Cardiology department with a history of chest pain and undergone a coronary angiogram.

The following are the symptoms that were recorded in the medical data: typical angina, anterior wall myocardial infarction (AWMI), inferior wall myocardial infarction (IWMI), dyspnea on exertion (DOE), and chest pain.

Exclusion Criteria

Subjects with any history of formerly diagnosed acute myocardial infarction, myocarditis or pericarditis, coronary angioplasty, recent history of hospitalisation due to fever or trauma, implantation of artificial pacemakers, liver, kidney, or haematological disorders were excluded from the study.

Data Curation

The data curation consisted of demographic details like the age and gender of the patient. The case sheets were scrutinized for a history of hypertension or dyslipidemia, Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM), history of smoking or any accompanying cardiac risk factors, or any associated chronic disease. Hypertension was termed if an individual was on antihypertensive therapy or records documented blood pressure of more than 140/90 mmHg. An individual was termed having T2DM with laboratory reports of fasting plasma glucose (FPG) levels more than 126 mg/dl or random plasma glucose level over 200 mg/dl or known to be on anti-diabetic therapy. Smoking was termed as the current smoking status of an individual. The haematological inflammatory indices were calculated manually from the CBC reports. All the participants had undergone a coronary angiogram. The angiographic reports were scrutinized for at least 50% diameter stenosis and single (SVD), double (DVD), or triple vessel disease (TVD).

Statistical Analysis

The data consequently obtained was analyzed using Graphpad Prism 5 software. The baseline characteristics and hematological indices were compared in the CAD group and controls by using the Student's t-test in normally distributed data, whereas, for data with skewed distribution, the Mann-Whitney test was applied. The given results were presented as mean ± standard deviation for normal distribution and median (inter-quartile range) for skewed distribution. We assessed data normality using two statistical tests: the D'Agostino and Pearson Omnibus Normality Test and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov Test.

The receiver operating characteristic (ROC), denoting the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, and specificity were further evaluated. AUC between 0.8-0.9 was considered to have a good predictive value, whereas that of 0.7-0.8 is fair. The level of significance difference between the indices is considered to have

a power of 95% confidence interval, probability of type 1 error being adjusted at 5%, hence positioning the p-value at 0.05. Additionally, LR (Likelihood Ratio) was introduced in conjunction with AUC.

Discussion

The current study demonstrated that traditional risk factor like the presence of T2DM, were higher in the CAD group (Table I). The results of our study affirmed that WBC count and ESR were significantly higher in subjects with CAD compared to healthy controls in males (Table II). This difference may reflect underlying biological or behavioral variations between genders and warrants further investigation.

In the current study, ESR demonstrated higher AUC values (0.717 and 0.804) compared to NLR (0.7397 and 0.7397) in males and females, respectively (Table III, Figure 1 & 2). However, despite a relatively higher AUC, ESR did not show statistical significance in group comparison and should therefore be interpreted with caution. Overall, the AUC values fall within the fair range, indicating moderate discriminatory ability. Furthermore, the likelihood ratios observed suggest only modest diagnostic utility of these indices. Leukocytes, specifically lymphocytes, contribute to healing processes; hence, lymphopenia accelerates atherosclerosis.[11] High neutrophil count results in higher blood coagulability, activating platelets and vascular endothelial membrane, which results in disruption in blood vessels.[12] Erikssen et al. (2000) and Andresdottir et al. (2003) have reported ESR to be an effective biomarker for the prediction of CAD in both genders based on the inflammatory and pathophysiological processes involved in atherosclerosis.[13,14] However, it is crucial to realize that ESR alone cannot necessarily be used as a screening for CAD patients. Previous literature suggests that elderly people tend to suffer from periodontitis, infections, and inflammatory diseases, which can lead to the elevation of ESR.[15] Additionally, elderly individuals possess an unreasonably high ESR when compared to their younger equivalents.[16]

Our study denotes that a high NLR was significantly associated with CAD in either gender (Table 3). Similar results have been concluded from a study done in western India, which demonstrated that individuals with abnormality in coronary angiogram exhibit higher NLR as compared to individuals with normal angiogram reports.[17] Previous literature reports that higher NLR is associated with acceleration of atherosclerosis and is an independent marker for thin-cap fibroatheroma.[18,19] Previous population-based studies have reported NLR as a predictor of CAD in the general healthy population, as well as considering higher NLR as an intermediate risk group according to the Framingham Risk Score (FRS).[20] Our study is in agreement with previous literature, which reports that NLR predicts atherosclerosis and CAD.[21,22]

MPV is primarily the indicator of platelet activation and aggregation, thereby predicting the development and progression of CAD.[23] A higher PLR is a novel marker that reflects the activation of thrombocytes.[24] Zhou et al. (2017) have illustrated the increased level of PLR in multi-vessel disease, thereby denoting it to be an independent biomarker for adverse cardiovascular events.[25] According to our results, NLR showed better discriminatory ability in the occurrence of CAD in comparison to PLR and MPV. MPV can be altered by various factors such as dehydration and blood specimen handling. Recent reports of a Mendelian randomization study conclude that elevated platelet levels are associated with a higher risk for stroke and not myocardial infarction (MI).[26]

Previous literature reports that LMR is associated with all junctures of coronary atherosclerosis and, therefore, is closely coupled to CAD. Our study results project NLR showed better discriminatory ability for CAD in comparison to LMR, thus indirectly indicating that neutrophils may contribute more to the origin and acceleration of atherosclerotic plaques than monocytes.[27]

Clinical Relevance

From a clinical perspective, our study highlights the potential utility of haematological inflammatory indices for healthcare providers. ESR and NLR are simple, readily available parameters that may be incorporated into routine clinical practice for assessing CAD risk in the Asian Indian population. These indices may serve as useful adjunctive markers in clinical assessment. However, they are not sufficient as standalone diagnostic tools and require validation through further studies.

Future Directions

A future study wherein the haematological inflammatory markers and coronary angiogram have been conducted sequentially, validating the suitable cut-off values of NLR and PLR, would also be beneficial. The development and progression of CAD is multifactorial; hence, a prospective cohort study considering the progression and prognosis of CAD, which can predict disease severity as well as the subsequent occurrence of poor clinical outcomes is suggested. Further studies incorporating risk-adjusted models, prospective validation, and integration with established risk scores are required.

Strengths and Limitations

The strengths of this study include the use of coronary angiography, the gold standard for CAD diagnosis, for accurate classification of cases and controls, thereby minimizing misclassification bias. Additionally, the study evaluates simple, cost-effective, and routinely available haematological indices, enhancing its clinical applicability. The inclusion of gender-based analysis provides further insight into potential differences in inflammatory markers. Furthermore, the use of ROC curve analysis allows objective assessment of the discriminatory ability of these indices.

This study is subject to several limitations. Its retrospective and single-centre design restricts causal interpretation and limits the generalizability of the findings. The effective sample size was reduced due to missing data, which may have influenced statistical power. Adjustment for potential confounders through multivariable analysis was not performed. In addition, the control group consisted of symptomatic individuals rather than entirely healthy subjects, which may have introduced selection bias. Relevant factors such as variations in blood cell parameters, concurrent medications, and precautions during test interpretation were not accounted for. The timing of blood sample collection in relation to the onset and progression of CAD was also not standardized. Furthermore, additional inflammatory markers, such as C-reactive protein, were not available. Lastly, ROC-derived cut-offs were not validated, and no statistical comparison between AUCs was carried out, which may limit the strength of the diagnostic performance evaluation.

Conclusion

The present study suggests that haematological inflammatory indices such as ESR and NLR are associated with the presence of CAD and may serve as adjunctive markers. However, their clinical utility as independent diagnostic tools is limited and requires further validation.

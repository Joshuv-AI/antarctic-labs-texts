Deep learning-based model for analyzing student engagement in activities
Authors: Feng Feng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractStudent engagement is a critical determinant of academic success. Traditional methods like surveys and self-reporting are subjective, while automated models often fail to integrate multimodal behavioral cues effectively, resulting in inaccurate or incomplete engagement analysis. To address these limitations, this research introduces an advanced framework, the Intelligent Chimp-driven Bi-directional long short-term memory network–scalable gated recurrent unit (IC-BiSGRU-Net), for robust analysis of student engagement in diverse learning activities. Multimodal educational dataset were collected from multimodal sources, including classroom video recordings, audio interactions, and digital activity logs. Pre-processing involves spectral median filtering for audio signals, min–max normalization for video sequences, and digital logs to handle scale, illumination, and distribution variations. For feature extraction, facial micro-expressions and action units were derived using a Convolutional neural network (CNN)-based encoder, capturing subtle emotional and attentional states. These extracted features, along with sequential interaction logs, were processed using the IC-BiSGRU-Net model, which integrates the temporal modeling power of Bi-LSTM with the computational efficiency of SGRU. Experimental validation on benchmark student engagement revealed that the proposed model outperformed the conventional model, achieving accuracy, recall, precision, and F1-score ranging from 96 to 99%. The system successfully classified engagement into active, passive, and disengaged states in real time.Keywords: Intelligent chimp-driven Bi-directional long short-term memory network–scalable gated recurrent unit (IC-BiSGRU-Net), Student engagement, Education, Convolutional neural network (CNN)Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

As technology and technology-driven learning were increasingly incorporated into education, the field underwent rapid change1. Assessing and improving student involvement became a top concern for educators, academics, and policymakers as online learning, smart classrooms, and other interactive learning technologies proliferated in educational environments. It was widely acknowledged that one of the most significant indicators of learning outcomes, academic accomplishment, and overall student success was engagement2. While low-engagement students frequently displayed lower motivation, poorer academic achievement, and eventually higher dropout rates, engaged students demonstrated greater understanding, critical thinking abilities, and improved long-term retention of knowledge To facilitate this shift, academics have been using multimodal data, machine learning, and advanced analytics more and more to identify subtle trends in student behaviour3. Digital traces including clickstream data, eye-tracking, and real-time classroom sensors were used as a result of criticism of traditional surveys and self-report measures for being subjective and inconsistent. These techniques made it possible to continuously assess engagement at a fine level, providing useful information for adaptive learning systems4. Simultaneously, issues like data privacy, moral dilemmas, and guaranteeing inclusion across various student groups brought attention to the necessity of well-rounded frameworks that blend technology-driven methods with human-centered teaching techniques. Three broad categories were commonly used to describe the complicated idea of student engagement: behavioral, emotional, and cognitive. Participating in tasks and activities in the classroom, such as submitting homework assignments or posing questions, was referred to as behavioral engagement5. Students’ positive and negative attitudes toward their education, as well as their interest and enthusiasm, were all components of emotional involvement. Cognitive engagement was the degree to which people were dedicated to learning deeply, resolving issues, and applying learned knowledge in novel contexts6. These three aspects provided a highly comprehensive image of how involved students were in their educational experiences, as the aspects co-varied and dynamically influenced one another. Over time, there were difficulties in reliably and consistently measuring these dimensions.

Self-report questionnaires, performance indicators, or observed instructor assessments were often the foundation of traditional engagement measurement methods7. Although these approaches were subjective, time-consuming, and not always accurate, the methods proved useful in certain contexts. Teachers additionally found it challenging to assess each student’s level of attention in large classes, and surveys did not provide real-time engagement data. New forms of involvement, including online discussions, clickstream data, and video participation, emerged as a result of the growth in online learning experiences8. These forms of engagement required various types of analysis. These drawbacks supported the need for scalability, impartiality, and automation in monitoring and analyzing student involvement. Artificial intelligence (AI) and Deep Learning (DL) techniques, in particular, grew into powerful strategies to close this gap in recent years9. To facilitate this shift, academics have been using multimodal data, machine learning, and advanced analytics more and more to identify subtle trends in student behavior. Digital traces including clickstream data, eye-tracking, and real-time classroom sensors were used as a result of criticism of traditional surveys and self-report measures for being subjective and inconsistent. These techniques made it possible to continuously assess engagement at a fine level, providing useful information for adaptive learning systems10. Simultaneously, issues like data privacy, moral dilemmas, and guaranteeing inclusion across various student groups brought attention to the necessity of well-rounded frameworks that blend technology-driven methods with human-centered teaching techniques. Based on the architecture of the human brain, DL was a machine learning technique that proved highly effective at processing vast volumes of data and identifying intricate patterns. Numerous applications of DL techniques had already demonstrated success, including in the fields of audio analysis, image recognition, and natural language processing11. To more precisely estimate engagement levels, utilized DL models to examine multimodal data, such as facial expressions, eye gaze, body posture, voice tonal inflection, or interaction logs12. The DL system tracked multiple pieces of information using several processes to provide real-time composite data on student engagement. The current advancements in multimodal affective computing have empowered automatic reading of emotional and behavioral conditions in online learning systems. Methods that combine the fusion of the tensor decomposition and self-supervised multi-task learning enhance the robustness and adaptability to a wide range of input interaction13. Moreover, non-contact sensing, client-server systems allow scalable real-time measurement in real deployment settings, which allow ongoing measurement and smart analysis of user interactions and reactions14.

Literature review

The literature review highlights limitations in current engagement analysis methods, such as reliance on subjective surveys and the inability of automated models to integrate multimodal behavioral cues effectively. These gaps motivate the development of a DL-based framework that leverages multimodal data and advanced temporal modeling to achieve accurate, real-time classification of student engagement states.

Traditional engagement measurement

Research15 employed DL algorithms to track students’ emotions in real time, assessing anger, contempt, fear, happiness, sadness, and surprise. During one-hour lessons, survey weights were used to calculate the Mean Engagement Score (MES). The results demonstrated improved online engagement assessment; nevertheless, reliance on the dataset and inconsistent accuracy across emotions were among the drawbacks. It highlighted the challenge of ensuring accurate engagement detection in interactive digital learning environments.

Improving multimodal emotion recognition, the research16 improved weak cross modal correlations. This accomplished through the proposal of a tensor-decomposition-based fusion framework along with self-supervised multi-task learning to efficiently capture interactions between modalities. Through experimental evaluation comparing the method using both CMU-MOSI and CMU-MOSEI datasets and demonstrate superior results. However, the method also requires high-quality multimodal input data and has increased computational cost associated with its implementation.

Unimodal deep learning approaches

The ability to accurately recognize human emotions was critical to improve the interactions with machines17. The proposed End-to-end Multimodal Visualization Analysis System (EMVAS), an integrated, multi-modal solution for interpreting human emotions that fused the visual, auditory and textual dimensions using self-supervised multitask learning. Through experiments, it demonstrated improved accuracy, F1 scores, and correlation compared to other state-of-the-art approaches. However, the real-time implementations could sustain additional computational overhead, and that proposed approach could be less effective due to variability in actual, dynamic, 3D settings.

A multimodal system used log data, text, and video to evaluate student involvement18. Asynchronous data was fused, engagement markers were retrieved, and gradient magnitude mapping revealed minute differences in engagement. Reliability was confirmed by statistical testing after popular deep CNN models were empirically tested. Despite being constrained by the size of the dataset and the processing load, the approach proved useful and efficient. The absence of scalable, impartial, and precise engagement measurement methods is the issue being addressed.

To calculate student involvement in an actual classroom of forty-five students, a transfer-learning-based Visual Geometry Group 16 (VGG16) model with expanded layers and adjusted hyperparameters was applied in19. Inferential statistics from fourteen time-based and six gender-based studies verified the method’s 90% accuracy with 0.5 s of computing time. The outcomes showed that gender and time had a favorable effect on engagement. Limitations included a small sample size and controlled characteristics. This emphasized the necessity of conducting more extensive demographic examination.

Computer vision techniques, such as face identification, convolutional neural network-based facial expression recognition, and frame-wise engagement estimation, were employed in a real-time system for tracking student group participation20. Academic emotional states, including boredom, perplexity, attention, frustration, yawning, and tiredness, were examined. The model achieved an accuracy of 78.70% during training and 76.90% during testing. Despite its effectiveness, limitations included dataset reliance and limited scalability. It emphasized that scalable, multimodal, real-time engagement analysis was required to improve the intelligent classroom environment.

Multiple convolutional layers, dropout, and batch normalization were employed in21, a convolutional neural network (CNN) model based on DL to track the moods and behaviors of students in an online classroom. The technique improved feature extraction, reduced overfitting, and achieved 99% identification accuracy. Limitations included generalizability, privacy concerns, and dataset variety. This highlighted the investigation challenge of providing scalable, ethical, and trustworthy engagement monitoring in digital education to enhance academic achievement and individualized support.

Using partial learning activity sequences, a deep neural network-based model was developed in22 for early student performance prediction. Latent characteristics were retrieved using a pre-trained autoencoder, increasing prediction accuracy by more than 30%. Classifier accuracy reached 0.84 with completely online activity sequences and 0.5 with demographic features. Distance-based oversampling on unbalanced datasets improved performance to over 80% accuracy. Limitations included dependence on sequence quality and potential bias in non-representative datasets.

To monitor physical education students, an intelligent Student Actions Evaluation System utilizing DL (iSAES-DL) was proposed by23. The approach assessed learners’ performance, understanding, and retention while classifying dangerous behaviors using deep convolutional neural networks. Comparisons with conventional techniques demonstrated superior results, with an F1-score of 97.86%. Limitations included the dataset scale and reliance on Internet of Things (IoT) devices, emphasizing the importance of DL for precise student tracking and effective learning analytics.

Multimodal deep learning and temporal models

To categorize engagement into five levels using multimodal datasets of behaviors, academic achievement, and facial expressions, a unique Engagement Level Classification Framework (ELCF) applied DL models, including Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), EfficientNetB0, and ensemble architectures24. The system achieved an F1-score of 84%, a hit rate of 92%, and a classification accuracy of 94%. Limitations included dataset scalability, bias in facial recognition, and privacy concerns. It emphasized the necessity of artificial intelligence-driven engagement monitoring that is equitable, transparent, and ethical.

Four hybrid DL models were developed in25 using the Dataset for Affective States in E-Learning Environments (DAiSEE) to automatically assess student participation across spatial and temporal dimensions. Methods combined Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Unit (Bi-GRU), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (Bi-LSTM) with EfficientNetV2-L convolutional neural network. With the highest reported accuracies of 61.45%, 61.56%, 62.11%, and 61.67%, the EfficientNetV2-L combined with the LSTM model performed well. Dataset limitations and moderate accuracy were reported as major limitations. It highlighted the necessity for more resilient spatiotemporal architectures to improve online engagement detection accuracy, scalability, and dependability.

An attention-aware convolutional stacked bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) model for student representation learning, termed ASIST, was proposed by26. The model integrated data from midterm assessments, academic registries, and virtual learning environment (VLE) clickstream. Convolutional neural networks with attention-based feature weighting were used to model diurnal weekly interactions, while stacked BiLSTM analyzed sequential behavior vectors. Evaluation on three datasets from Irish universities achieved area under the curve (AUC) scores of 0.86 to 0.90, outperforming both conventional classifiers and baseline DL models. Limitations included minimal attention impact and reduced performance for certain academic years, underscoring the necessity of robust early prediction models.

Optimization, edge-based and intelligent classrooms systems

An Edge-Based Student Attentiveness Analysis System, integrating face detection and DL image processing, was implemented in an edge-based online learning framework to extract facial landmarks for the mouth and eyes27. Pre-trained Facial Landmark Localization using Visual Geometry Group-19 (VGG-19) convolutional neural network was employed to classify attentiveness as either focused or tired. The approach achieved an accuracy of 95.29%, surpassing previous standards by 2.11 and 4.41%, respectively. Limitations included dependency on illumination conditions and visibility of the face. It revealed a research gap in scalable, objective, and real-time engagement monitoring.

An enhanced method of detecting student engagement, this study utilized a new improved detection method28. The method used was a cascaded YOLOv8 model that included enhancements to the existing ResNet50 feature extraction via an SVM classifier. The developed system produced 94.5% precision, 92.3% recall, and 92.7% mean average precision at 0.5, with a decrease in performance at mean average precision from 0.5 to 0.95, as well as unable to deploy in real-time, while having difficulty generalizing across multiple classrooms.

To predict student levels of engagement in online learning environments, we analyzed online learning activity data using Decision Tree (DT), Support Vector Machine (SVM) and Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) models29. The Neural Network delivered 85% accuracy, the highest of the three models. However, different levels of reliability are seen across each of the engagement categories and more data points as well as continuous feedback on model performance will be required for expanded implementation of this type of tool.

The research30 was to predict the engagement level of students in a classroom environment. The pre-processing and data evaluation is performed on an online learning environment (VLE) through various types of machine learning classification algorithms. The results indicate that CatBoost had the highest accuracy (92.23% accuracy, 94.40% precision and 100% recall). Despite the accuracy of the model, however, it could not necessarily be reflective of every institution and could require larger data sets than currently available to be applicable. It was also limited to measuring students’ emotional and cognitive engagement dimensions.

Using a DL based Engagement Level Classification Framework, it classified student engagement into five levels31. Efficient Net, CNN and ensemble models were used to process multi-modal behavioral, emotional and performance data, and the framework achieved 94% accuracy when classifying student engagement levels. However, there are limitations associated with facial emotion bias, privacy issues, and limited scalability of the datasets used could limit the ability to deploy the framework more broadly and generalize its findings to real world settings.

Research gap

The drawbacks of the previous research include dependency on datasets, inconsistency in accuracy across different emotional categories, and a lack of generalizability within the real-time digital environment15,16. The limitations associated with limited recording conditions, a low sample size, and excessive computational requirements reduce the ability to properly scale and make it more robust17–21. Facial recognition bias, concerns about privacy, and relatively poor multimodal performance are other significant impediments to the deployment of these technologies22–26. There are other limitations to the practical use of these technologies, such as sensitivity to illumination, decreased mAP when used at higher thresholds, and the inability to transfer from one educational institution to another27–31.The IC-BiSGRU-Net model is designed with enhanced generalizability from a convolutional and bidirectional GRU perspective, allowing it to minimize reliance on datasets and the inconsistencies of emotion recognition accuracy. The lightweight design of the method has lower computational costs and significantly improves the ability to scale it. The framework also incorporates temporal–behavioural signals rather than facial identity to mitigate bias, privacy concerns, and sensitivity to illumination, resulting in significantly greater and more stable performance metrics for reliable implementation in a variety of instructional settings and environments.

Feature extraction using convolutional neural network (CNN)

The CNN is a structural neural network with several layers, including layers for classification, pooling, and convolution. To capture delicate emotional and attentional indicators, CNNs are used to extract action units and face micro-expressions from classroom video recordings. These visual elements are paired with digital activity logs, which record interaction patterns including click counts and activity durations, and audio interactions, which offer speech-based behavioral indications. The CNN-based feature extraction procedure supports the model in categorizing engagement into active, passive, and disengaged states by combining various multimodal sources and enabling thorough, real-time analysis of student involvement. Following processing, the video data goes through convolutional and pooling layers, which remove duplicate information and extract action units and face micro expressions to identify significant patterns. These characteristics are progressively combined. The collected visual elements are merged with normalized digital activity logs and audio exchanges to provide sequential behavioral cues. Ultimately, a completely linked layer receives the integrated multimodal information and uses it to categorize each instance into active, passive, or disengaged states in real time, as well as to generate forecasts for student participation.

Proposed model

To improve student engagement prediction, the suggested IC-BiSGRU-Net framework uses a hybrid optimization approach that combines Improved Chimp Optimization (IC), Scalable Gated Recurrent Unit (SGRU), and Bi-directional LSTM (BiLSTM) with dynamic weighting. BiLSTM ensures contextual knowledge from past and future states by capturing both forward and backward temporal dependencies in sequential multimodal inputs, such as action units, audio, digital interaction logs, and facial micro-expressions. By minimizing learnable parameters for real-time analysis and preventing gradient vanishing and explosion, SGRU effectively models spatiotemporal patterns while maintaining early behavioral signals. While dynamic weighting gives candidate solutions proportionate weight depending on their contribution to the optimal solution, IC optimization uses a somersault technique to adaptively fine-tune network parameters, allowing escape from local optima. The hybrid IC-BiSGRU-Net MODEL ensures faster convergence, enhanced robustness, and accurate classification of student engagement into active, passive, and disengaged stages. A reliable and efficient real-time engagement prediction system that performs well in a range of educational contexts is produced by this approach. In the Improved Chimp Optimization (IC) method, a dynamic weighting component was added to enhance the adaptive fine-tuning of network parameters. Discussion

To improve real-time student engagement categorization by developing a novel architecture that can accurately differentiate between active, passive, and disengaged states. The current models make impressive strides in this direction, yet also have drawbacks that prevent their use in a real-world educational environment.

Although it was highly effective at capturing sequential patterns, stacked long short-term memory with an auto encoder27 over fitted and failed to generalize in complicated multimodal data. For single-image tasks, ResNet-5028 was a strong feature extractor and performed well; nevertheless, it was unable to adjust to sequential or contextual information, particularly when it comes to temporal engagement detection. Artificial Neural Networks (ANN)29 offer a straightforward framework for comparison, but its unable to understand the more complex temporal connections required for precise real-time analysis. Likewise, CATBoost30 offers a strong modeling framework for any type of tabular data, but it was less effective at capturing intricate features in sporadic behavioral dynamics involving several modalities. Although XGBoost31, Neural Network31, CatBoost31 with extensive features, RF, and Stack Ensemble models provide competitive baseline performance, they are limited in capturing multimodal behavioral signals and sequential learning patterns essential for engagement detection. Their predictive ability relies heavily on feature engineering and static input representations, which weakens transferability across different classroom environments.

The proposed IC-BiSGRU-Net overcomes these limitations by combining contextual learning, scalability, and temporal depth for greater engagement recognition. This ensures robust multimodal adaptability, fewer false predictions, improved real-time detection, and accurate identification of a variety of student behaviors in dynamic learning environments. Additionally, it supports scalable deployment, improves interpretability, increases flexibility to diverse data streams, and promotes more successful, data-driven interventions for long-term academic achievement and individualized learning outcomes.

Conclusion

A DL architecture named Intelligent Chimp-driven Bi-directional Long Short-Term Memory Network, Scalable Gated Recurrent Unit (IC-BiSGRU-Net) was created in order to assess the interactions of students in real time. Video, voice between the students, and logs of digital activities were multimodal data, and min-max normalization of video and logs, and median filtering of audio were used to process them, and CNN-based encoders were used to extract key interaction and micro-expression data. The combined depth of Bi-LSTM in terms of time was balanced with the efficiency of SGRU using the IC-driven optimization of the hybrid model. The results indicate 96.21 accuracy, 98.31 precision, 97.23 recall, and 98.13 F1-score, which are better than traditional úapproaches. Practically, the instructors may combine IC-BiSGRU-Net with the learning management systems that can help to track the engagement on-the-fly, to send the notification to the disengaged learners, and to adapt the teaching materials dynamically. Limitations are also present in that it relies on benchmark multimodal sources, controlled conditions and data which might limit a wider applicability to a wider range of classrooms.

Future scope

Future research can explore real-time adaptive feedback systems for individualized learning, integration with wearable biosensors for physiological feedback, and adaptive cross-cultural datasets. In smart classrooms, extending the model with edge computing and federated learning can provide scalable, privacy-preserving implementation. Furthermore, using augmented reality (AR) interfaces might improve proactive educational interventions and immersive engagement monitoring.

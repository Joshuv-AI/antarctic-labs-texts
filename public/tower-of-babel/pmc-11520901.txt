Is pulmonary vascular remodeling an intermediate link between hyperglycemia and adverse outcomes in patients with idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension? Insights from a multi-center cohort study
Authors: Sicheng Zhang, Luyang Gao, Sicong Li, Manqing Luo, Qunying Xi, Ping Lin, Zhihui Zhao, Qing Zhao, Tao Yang, Qixian Zeng, Zhihua Huang, Xin Li, Anqi Duan, Yijia Wang, Qin Luo, Yansong Guo, Zhihong Liu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHyperglycemia upon admission is associated with poor prognosis of many cardiovascular diseases. However, the relationship of stress hyperglycemia ratio (SHR), admission blood glucose (ABG), and hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) with pulmonary hypertension has not been reported. This study aimed to explore the association of hyperglycemia indices with disease severity and long-term adverse outcomes in patients with idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (IPAH).MethodsThis multi-center cohort study included 625 consecutive patients diagnosed with or treated for IPAH between January 2015 and June 2023. SHR was calculated using the followings: ABG (mmol/L)/(1.59 × HbA1c [%] − 2.59). The primary endpoint was defined as clinical worsening events. Multivariable Cox regression and restricted cubic spline analyses were employed to evaluate the association of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c with endpoint events. The mediating effect of pulmonary hemodynamics was evaluated to investigate the potential mechanism between hyperglycemia and clinical outcomes.ResultsDuring a mean follow-up period of 3.8 years, 219 (35.0%) patients experienced all-cause death or clinical worsening events. Hyperglycemia indices correlated with well-validated variables that reflected the severity of IPAH, such as the World Health Organization functional class, 6-min walk distance, and N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide levels. Multivariable Cox regression analyses indicated that SHR (hazard ratio [HR] 1.328, 95% confidence intervals [CI]: 1.185, 1.489 per 0.1-unit increment, P < 0.001) and ABG (HR 1.317, 95% CI: 1.134, 1.529 per 1.0-unit increment, P < 0.001) were independent predictors of primary endpoint events. Mediation analysis indicated that pulmonary vascular resistance mediated 5.65% and 14.62% of the associations between SHR and ABG and clinical worsening events, respectively. The addition of SHR significantly improved reclassification, discrimination ability, and model fit beyond the clinical risk prediction model.ConclusionsSHR is positively associated with clinical worsening in patients with IPAH. The association appeared to be partially mediated through the pathway of pulmonary vascular remodeling, indicating that SHR may serve as a valuable indicator for providing additional risk information.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12933-024-02476-9.

Introduction

Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) is characterized by a progressive escalation in pulmonary artery pressure and pulmonary vascular resistance, culminating in right ventricular failure and potential mortality [1]. Patients with PAH, particularly those with idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension (IPAH), experience the highest prevalence and most unfavorable prognosis, resulting in a substantial financial burden associated with the disease [2]. In the absence of targeted pharmacotherapy, patients with IPAH have a median survival of merely 2.8 years, which significantly impacts their quality of life and overall results [3, 4]. Consequently, healthcare professionals must accurately pinpoint patients with IPAH at elevated risk of mortality and clinical deterioration to promptly commence targeted drug interventions [5]. Nevertheless, conventional risk assessment tools often require invasive hemodynamic measurements, thereby diminishing their practicality in clinical settings [6]. Consequently, there is a pressing need for novel and readily accessible clinical prognostic indicators to enhance prognostication in patients with IPAH and facilitate tailored treatment approaches.

Prior investigations have evidenced the significant roles of diabetes and insulin resistance in the severity and adverse prognosis of IPAH [7, 8]. Jonas et al. conducted a study involving 61 patients with IPAH, revealing a novel correlation between elevated blood lipids and plasma glucose levels upon admission, and increased overall mortality in this cohort [9]. However, this study did not account for the metabolic status of glucose in these patients. Another study, which included 115 patients with PAH, discovered a significant survival advantage over a 5-year for individuals with hemoglobin A1c (HbA1c) levels below 5.7%, compared to those with higher baseline values [10]. Nonetheless, this study focused solely on non-diabetic patients. In contrast to these findings, a study by Naderi et al. involving 69 patients with PAH found no significant association between insulin resistance and adverse events [11]. Given the limited sample sizes, variations in the populations, and inconclusive findings of previous studies, a consensus on the relationship between IPAH and blood glucose metabolism has not yet been reached.

Acute hyperglycemia denotes a condition of elevated blood glucose levels resulting from the intricate relationship of insulin counterregulatory hormones such as catecholamines, cortisol, and growth hormone during acute illness [12]. Conventional markers of hyperglycemia, like admission blood glucose (ABG), are significantly impacted by baseline blood glucose levels and glucose metabolism status. The stress hyperglycemia ratio (SHR), which incorporated both ABG and HbA1c (reflecting the mean blood glucose levels over the past 2–3 months), offers a more accurate representation of the actual blood glucose levels in acutely ill patients [13]. This approach mitigates the limitations of ABG, which may be influenced by long-term glycemic control. The SHR is recognized as an independent predictor of outcomes in heart failure, including all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, and heart failure readmission events [14]. Numerous studies have shown that SHR is correlated with adverse outcomes of acute coronary syndrome, chronic total occlusion of the coronary arteries, and pneumonia related to stroke in cardiovascular and pulmonary conditions [15–17]. Furthermore, Long et al. posited that a high SHR is linked to unfavorable short-term results in patients with severe pulmonary hypertension [18]. However, there is a dearth of research exploring the relationship between stress-induced hyperglycemia and the severity and prognosis of patients with IPAH. No studies have simultaneously compared the predictive effectiveness of the three hyperglycemia indices in forecasting clinical deteriorations in IPAH.

To address this knowledge gap, we conducted a multi-center retrospective cohort study to explore the relationships between the three hyperglycemia and the functional status, echoca rdiographic parameters, hemodynamic parameters, and adverse outcomes in patients with IPAH. We also explored the mechanism of the mediating effect of hyperglycemia on prognosis and assessed whether incorporating hyperglycemia indices into existing risk stratification tools could provide additional predictive power. This is essential to indicate the mechanism by which hyperglycemia affects long-term outcomes and to identify high-risk patients early.

Methods

Study design and population

This multi-center retrospective cohort study enrolled 723 consecutive patients diagnosed with or treated for IPAH at four teaching hospitals in China between January 2015 and June 2023. Baseline assessment included data collected at the time of IPAH diagnosis in incident cases (n = 424, diagnosed after January 1, 2015) and data obtained at the most recent visit for previously treated patients (n = 201, diagnosed before January 1, 2015). IPAH was diagnosed in patients with precapillary pulmonary hypertension (elevated mean pulmonary artery pressure [mPAP] > 20 mmHg and pulmonary artery wedge pressure [PAWP] ≤ 15 mmHg) with increased pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) > 2 wood units [1]. Patients with findings typical of left heart disease, significant lung disease, chronic thromboembolic pulmonary hypertension, or other known causes of PAH were classified as having other types of pulmonary hypertension according to the European Society of Cardiology (ESC) guidelines and were excluded from the study. The date of diagnosis was defined as the date of the first right heart catheterization that met the hemodynamic criteria for pre‑capillary pulmonary hypertension. After excluding confusing or missing data and patients lost to follow-up, 625 participants were included in the final statistical analysis of the association between hyperglycemic status and IPAH severity and prognosis (Supplementary Figure S1). The study protocol conformed to the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was revised and approved by the local ethics committee. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Measurements and definitions

Demographic data, medical history, laboratory tests and parameters of echocardiography were derived from hospital medical records. Venous blood samples were collected from the enrolled patients in serum separator tubes via direct venipuncture within 24 h of admission. Blood glucose and HbA1c levels at admission were measured using standard biochemical techniques in the core laboratories at four hospitals. The SHR was calculated as follows: ABG (mmol/L)/[1.59 × HbA1c (%) − 2.59] [19]. Echocardiography was performed within 48 h of admission. Right heart catheterization was conducted when patients were in stable condition. The transducer was set to zero level at the mid-thoracic line and at the level of the left atrium [20, 21]. After the sheath was inserted, the patient was allowed to rest briefly to achieve a stable state before hemodynamic parameters were measured, ensuring standardization of right heart catheterization [22, 23]. Hyperglycemia indices data were also collected at the first follow-up, which was more than 3 months after initial enrollment if data were available. Standardized spreadsheets were used to collect retrospective data. All centers followed the treatment strategies recommended by the ESC guidelines and developed targeted pharmacological therapy plans based on the patient's condition [1, 24, 25].

DM was defined as a previous diagnosis of any type of diabetes, current treatment with medications approved for DM, fasting blood glucose levels ≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) documented on two different days, or blood glucose levels ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) at the 120‑min time-point of the oral glucose tolerance test, HbA1c level ≥ 6.5%, or in a patient with classic symptoms of hyperglycemia or hyperglycemic crisis, a random plasma glucose level ≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) [26]. Hypertension was diagnosed based on self-reported physician diagnosis, recent use of an antihypertensive agent, or blood pressure ≥ 140/90 mmHg [27].

Patients were classified into low-, intermediate-, or high-risk categories based on the abbreviated ESC 3-strata risk stratification strategy [28]. One to three points were assigned to each parameter in the prediction model, which included the World Health Organization functional class (WHO-FC), 6-min walk distance (6MWD), the N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) level, right atrial pressure (RAP), cardiac index, and mixed venous oxygen saturation (SVO2) (details are shown in Supplementary Table S1). The risk score of each patient was calculated as the sum of all points divided by the number of available variables and rounded to the nearest integer.

Endpoints and follow-up

Clinical worsening, the primary endpoint of this study, was defined as the first occurrence of any of the following events: all-cause death, lung transplantation, or re-hospitalization owing to heart failure. The secondary endpoint was all-cause death, which was utilized in the survival analysis. Follow-up data were collected through outpatient clinical visits, readmission, or telephonic interviews with the patients or their relatives after discharge. The endpoints were adjudicated by an independent committee.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median [25–75th percentile] and were analyzed using Student’s t-test (for normally distributed data) or the Mann–Whitney U test (for non-normally distributed data). The chi-square test or Fisher's exact test was used to compare categorical variables as counts (percentages). Correlations between the three hyperglycemia indices and established markers of IPAH severity were examined using the Spearman rank correlation test. One-way analysis of variance was used to compare differences among different risk strata, with post-hoc comparisons conducted using the least significant difference (LSD). Restricted cubic spline curves were used on a continuous scale to evaluate the relationships between SHR, ABG, HbA1c, and primary endpoint events. The cumulative incidence of clinical worsening was estimated using Kaplan‒Meier curves, with differences assessed by the log-rank test. All categories were based on the tertiles of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c.

Univariable Cox regression analysis was performed to identify risk factors for clinical worsening, and factors with P < 0.05 or clinical significance were retained in the multivariable Cox regression model. Three models were established: Model 1 was a crude model (unadjusted); Model 2 was adjusted for age, sex, ethnicity, and DM. Model 3 was adjusted for factors in Model 2 plus the WHO-FC, 6MWD, NT-proBNP, PAH-specific treatment, SVO2, cardiac index and PVR. The variance inflation factor (VIF) method was used to test for collinearity, and no evidence of multicollinearity was found in the overall population (the VIF for the included variables was < 5). Subgroup analyses were conducted stratified by sex, age, body mass index (BMI), incident case, and DM to determine the interaction effects, with the P value for the interaction calculated. In addition to the primary endpoints, a survival analysis was conducted to examine the relationship between SHR, ABG, HbA1c, and the secondary endpoint of all-cause death. Sensitivity analysis was performed to evaluate the robustness of the results. We repeated the analysis by further adjusting the white blood cell count and C-reactive protein levels. Moreover, we excluded patients with anemia (hemoglobin < 100 g/L) or those with 20mmHg < mPAP ≤ 24 mmHg for repeated analyses of the association between hyperglycemia and primary endpoint events.

Mediation effects analysis was performed using the R package “mediation” to explore whether indicators of pulmonary hemodynamics (including RAP, mPAP, PAWP, PVR, cardiac index, stroke volume index [SVI], and SVO2 could be involved in the mechanisms or mode by which hyperglycemia affected clinical worsening events. The presence of a mediating effect is defined as meeting all of the following conditions, including having a significant total effect, a significant indirect effect, and a positive proportion of the mediating effect.

Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were used to predict clinical worsening based on ESC risk stratification and the three hyperglycemia indices. The area under the curve, as measured by the C-statistic, was computed to quantify the predictive power for clinical worsening. Additionally, the net reclassification improvement (NRI) and integrated discrimination improvement (IDI) indices were calculated to assess the added predictive value of SHR, ABG, or HbA1c based on the established risk stratification score. The goodness-of-fit of three hyperglycemia indices and the models incorporating them into the ESC risk stratification were assessed using the Akaike information criterion (AIC), Bayesian information criterion (BIC), and χ2 likelihood ratio tests.

Statistical significance was set at P < 0.05 (two‐sided). Data analyses were performed using R-studio (version 4.2.2).

Results

Baseline characteristics

This study included 625 individuals diagnosed with IPAH, with a median age of 32.0 [27.0–39.0] years, of which 77.8% were female. The average values of the SHR, ABG, and HbA1c were 0.8 ± 0.1, 5.1 ± 1.0, and 5.9 ± 0.6, respectively. Among the 201 prevalent cases (median time from diagnosis to enrollment of 1.5 [0.5–3.0] years), 192 (95.5%) received PAH‑specific treatment and their NT-proBNP levels were lower than that of the incident cases (Supplementary Table S2). The baseline characteristics of participants who did and did not experience clinical worsening are presented in Table 1. Compared to patients without worsening, those with clinical worsening during follow-up tended to be of non-Han ethnicity and exhibited worse functional class, more restricted 6MWD, higher rates of diabetes, and pericardial effusion. Approximately 91.2% of participants received targeted therapy for PAH during their initial hospitalization. The remaining patients declined this treatment mainly because of intolerance, financial burden, and caution regarding adverse effects. Compared to patients without clinical worsening, NT-proBNP, SHR, ABG, right ventricular end-diastolic diameter, and PVR were significantly higher, whereas HDL-C, left ventricular end-diastolic diameter, cardiac index, SVI, and SVO2 were lower in patients with clinical worsening compared to those without. Relationship of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c with established disease severity indicators of IPAH

SHR, ABG, and HbA1c levels were associated with different markers of IPAH severity as shown in Table 2. Association of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c with long-term adverse outcome of IPAH

During a mean follow-up period of 3.8 years, 219 (35.0%) patients experienced clinical worsening events, including 80 patients who experienced all-cause death, 2 who underwent lung transplantation, and 137 who were re-hospitalized due to heart failure. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis demonstrated significant differences in the incidence of primary endpoint events across different levels of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c with the highest hyperglycemia indices corresponding to the highest risk of clinical worsening (all log-rank P < 0.05), as shown in Supplementary Figure S3. ROC curve analysis indicated that the SHR had the largest area under the curve (Supplementary Figure S4), the highest Youden index, and the best accuracy (Supplementary Table S7) for predicting clinical worsening among the three hyperglycemia indices.

To further evaluate the predictive value of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c for clinical worsening, we established three Cox regression models to assess the impact of these indices on the primary endpoint events (Table 3). In the unadjusted Cox regression models, SHR, ABG, and HbA1c levels were associated with adverse outcomes. After full adjustment for covariates in Model 3, only SHR (HR 1.328, 95% confidence interval [CI]: 1.185, 1.489 per 0.1-unit increment, P < 0.001) and ABG (HR 1.317, 95% CI: 1.134, 1.529 per 1.0-unit increment, P < 0.001) independently predicted all-cause death, lung transplantation, and rehospitalization because of heart failure in patients with IPAH. Using the tertiles of SHR and ABG as independent variables, we observed that the risk of primary endpoint events increased with higher levels of both SHR and ABG. For instance, compared to tertile 1 of SHR, the adjusted HRs (95% CIs) for tertiles 2 and 3 were 1.529 (1.006, 2.325) and 2.476 (1.704, 3.598) for clinical worsening (P for trend < 0.001), respectively. Survival analysis showed that only SHR was significantly associated with an increased risk of all-cause mortality (HR 1.233, 95% CI: 1.024, 1.484 per 0.1-unit increment, P = 0.027) (Supplementary Table S8). No collinearity problems were detected in the multivariable Cox regression analysis. Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to explore the relationships of SHR, ABG, and HbA1c with disease severity and long-term adverse outcomes in patients with IPAH. This is also the first study to employ mediation analysis to explore whether hyperglycemia affects prognosis through pulmonary hemodynamics. There were some notable findings. First, SHR, ABG, and HbA1c were all effective in identifying patients at intermediate-high risk based on the abbreviated ESC 3-strata risk score. Secondly, SHR, ABG, and HbA1c were weakly to mildly correlated with indicators of IPAH severity. These indicators were positively correlated with WHO-FC, NT-proBNP, pericardial effusion, and PVR, and negatively correlated with 6MWD, cardiac index, SVI, and SVO2. Thirdly, both SHR and ABG are associated with clinical worsening events in patients with IPAH. After the associations of hyperglycemia with pulmonary hemodynamic severity and clinical worsening events were demonstrated, mediating analyses were performed, and their results highlighted the crucial role of PVR in linking hyperglycemia and clinical outcomes, proposing pulmonary vascular remodeling as a potential mechanism for these associations. Finally, when SHR, ABG, and HbA1c were incorporated into the risk prediction model, both SHR and ABG enhanced the predictive power of the model beyond the ESC 3-strata risk score. Goodness-of-fit tests indicated that a model combining abbreviated ESC 3-strata risk score with SHR was the best-fit model.

Numerous studies have examined the correlation between ABG levels and outcomes in patients with outer cardiovascular disease. It has been established that in patients experiencing acute exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, acute myocardial infarction, and heart failure without diabetes, elevated ABG levels are linked to unfavorable outcomes [29–31]. However, this relationship can be influenced by underlying comorbidities such as diabetes, unstable angina, arrhythmias, and pulmonary embolism [32, 33]. Bellomo et al. identified a significant interaction between prior hyperglycemia and acute hyperglycemia regarding mortality rates [34]. To address this, Roberts et al. introduced the concept of SHR in 2015 [19]. By examining the relationship between ABG levels, SHR, in-hospital mortality and intensive care in 2290 emergency department patients, they determined that SHR can regulate baseline blood glucose levels, detect relative hyperglycemia, and offer superior predictive capabilities for adverse outcomes compared to absolute hyperglycemia. Past research has predominantly concentrated on the link between SHR and conditions such as heart failure and coronary artery disease. Mohammed et al. [14] studied 400 patients with heart failure characterized by a preserved ejection fraction and, after adjusting for confounding variables, concluded that SHR acted as an independent predictor of all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, and heart failure readmission. Moreover, incorporating SHR into prognostic algorithms for patients with chronic heart failure enhanced the forecast of adverse events. A substantial cohort study involving 19,929 patients diagnosed with coronary artery disease revealed a noteworthy association between SHR and in-hospital mortality, displaying a dose–response effect [35]. However, the prognostic significance of SHR in patients with pulmonary hypertension has been less explored. An analysis of 414 patients with PAH admitted to the intensive care unit (ICU) demonstrated a strong correlation between high SHR levels, extended hospital stays, increased incidence of chronic kidney disease, and independent predictability of the 90-day mortality rate in patients with severe PAH [18]. Given the significant heterogeneity of pulmonary hypertension, the general status of pulmonary hypertension may not adequately represent the characteristics of IPAH, which is the most rapidly progressing and ominous subtype of PAH. Moreover, there is limited research on the long-term outcomes of stress-induced hyperglycemia in patients afflicted with IPAH. Hence, we thoroughly analyzed the correlation between absolute and relative hyperglycemia, disease severity, and the incidence of clinical deterioration in patients with IPAH for the inaugural time.

The stress response is predominantly regulated by the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis and sympathoadrenal system [36]. In cases of severe physiological stress, these systems, along with pro-inflammatory cytokines, collaborate to trigger stress-induced hyperglycemia [37]. The association between hyperglycemia and negative outcomes in patients with PAH is partly, attributed to the exacerbation of inflammatory responses and neuroendocrine reactions in the presence of diabetes [7]. Nevertheless, stress-induced hyperglycemia can independently lead to adverse consequences by promoting inflammation, oxidative stress, and endothelial dysfunction [12, 38]. A study by Cui et al. involving 5308 patients with acute myocardial infarction, demonstrated that a higher SHR was associated with increased in-hospital mortality rates in both diabetic and non-diabetic patients [39]. Li et al. conducted another study that revealed a noteworthy correlation between SHR and major adverse cardiac events, postoperative infections, and duration of ICU stay among individuals undergoing coronary artery bypass grafting, irrespective of their diabetes condition [40]. Similarly, our investigation indicated that SHR consistently predicts outcomes for patients with IPAH, irrespective of diabetes status. This finding underscores the importance of considering stress-induced elevations in blood glucose beyond routine blood sugar control. Evaluating SHR provides a measure of relative blood glucose that accounts for stress factors not solely hyperglycemia. Therefore, evaluating SHR in patients with IPAH during clinical assessments is equally significance for both diabetic and nondiabetic patients. Additionally, our sensitivity analyses, which adjusted for confounding factors related to stress, such as white blood cells and CRP levels, revealed a persistent association between SHR and long-term mortality in patients with IPAH. Furthermore, our findings suggest that incorporating SHR or ABG levels into current clinical prediction models can enhance the predictive capacity of ESC risk-stratification tools for long-term outcomes, thereby enhancing their reclassification and discrimination abilities. The goodness-of-fit test indicated that the combination of SHR and ESC risk stratification provided the optimal model. In conclusion, SHR represents a comprehensive metric that integrates both stress and hyperglycemia, potentially offering enhanced value for evaluating the severity and prognosis of patients with IPAH.

This study illustrates that hyperglycemia partially mediates unfavorable outcomes in patients with IPAH through PVR. Pulmonary vascular remodeling includes increased proliferation and secretory activity of structural cells in the pulmonary vasculature, particularly pulmonary artery endothelial cells and smooth muscle cells (SMCs) [41]. This process is accompanied by abnormal deposition of extracellular matrix, which supports vascular proliferation, resulting in thickening of the pulmonary vascular wall, constriction of the lumen, and the onset of PAH [42]. PVR signifies the extent of pulmonary vascular remodeling and measures the resistance properties of pulmonary arteries that regulate the steady and pulsatile pulmonary arterial load [43]. PVR has been reported to be linked to the severity and prognosis of PAH [44]. Numerous studies have reported that endothelial cells are a crucial target of hyperglycemia-induced damage, which involves three distinct biochemical pathways: the activation of protein kinase C isoforms by glucose, high levels of advanced glycation end-products derived from glucose, and the heightened glucose flux through the aldose reductase pathway [45]. Within the neurohormonal environment during periods of stress, intricate reaction mechanisms between hormones and cytokines result in hyperglycemia and insulin resistance [46]. These conditions can initiate molecular interactions that impair endothelial function, leading to heightened vascular tension, increased vascular permeability, oxidative stress, and inflammation [47]. Moreover, hyperglycemia adversely affects vascular SMCs. Through experiments involving obese and lean Zucker rats, Doronzo et al. [48] found that glucose promotes the synthesis and secretion of vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) in vascular smooth muscle cells via an osmotic mechanism, while VEGF has been identified as a critical component involved in the early remodeling of pulmonary arteries [49]. In separate rat experiments, Tardif et al. [50] demonstrated that hyperglycemia-induced downregulation of nestin and the subsequent decrease in proliferating vascular SMCs serve as early markers of vascular disease in diabetes. Furthermore, hyperglycemia upregulates the expression of core binding factor alpha subunit 1 and bone morphogenetic protein-2, thereby enhancing the calcification of vascular SMCs [51]. In conclusion, it is highly plausible that PVR is an intermediary variable in the correlation between stress-induced hyperglycemia and clinical deterioration events in patients with IPAH. On the one hand, PVR signifies the seriousness of pulmonary vascular remodeling, a well-established factor associated with the prognosis of patients with IPAH. On the other hand, there is substantial empirical support indicating an intrinsic correlation between elevated blood glucose levels and phenotypes linked to pulmonary vascular remodeling. Employing PVR as an intermediary variable elucidates the impact of increased blood glucose levels on the prognosis of patients of IPAH by shaping the pulmonary vascular architecture and functionality.

The present study has certain limitations. First, it is a retrospective cohort study. Despite this, it is the first multi-center cohort analysis with a large sample size investigating the utility of the hyperglycemia indices in patients with IPAH. Second, prediabetes denotes an intermediary phase between diabetes and normal glycemic metabolism. Owing to limitations in sample size, we did not investigate the significance of the three hyperglycemia indices in prediabetic patients with IPAH.

Conclusions

SHR and ABG are associated with long-term clinical outcomes in patients with IPAH, mediated in part by PVR. The combination of the ESC risk score and SHR provided the best-fit model. SHR is a dependable and expedient indicator for predicting disease severity and long-term adverse outcomes in patients with IPAH, which is crucial for determining risk stratification and management of these patients.

Vacuum Decay Rate in D-dimensional Electroweak theories
Jingwei Wang, Ligong Bian

Abstract
We present a systematic framework for calculating the vacuum decay rate in D-dimensional electroweak theories, providing a unified treatment of quantum fluctuations for scalar, fermion, and gauge boson fields via a combined WKB expansion and dimensional regularization. This method ensures rapid convergence even at large angular momenta. Application to a D = 4 D=4 standard model effective field theory gives log 10 ⁡ ( γ × Gyr × Gpc 3 ) = 183 \log_{10}(\gamma\times\text{Gyr}\times\text{Gpc}^{3})=183 . In the finite-temperature, dimensionally reduced standard model effective field theory with D = 3 D=3 , the values are log 10 ⁡ ( Γ T × Gyr × Gpc 3 ) = 42.4 \log_{10}(\Gamma_{T}\times\text{Gyr}\times\text{Gpc}^{3})=42.4 at tree level and 116.1 116.1 at two-loop order. The approach offers a general and efficient tool for analyzing vacuum stability and decay across dimensions.

The stability of the electroweak vacuum is a fundamental question in particle physics and cosmology. The current Higgs potential may reside in a metastable rather than an absolutely stable state at extremely high energy scales, suggesting that our universe might merely exist within a long-lived but not eternal quantum state. In this work, we develop a unified framework to compute the vacuum decay rate in D-dimensional electroweak-like theories. By combining a WKB expansion with dimensional regularization, we provide a convergent and dimensionally flexible scheme that fully accounts for scalar, fermion, and gauge field contributions. Our method overcomes the limitations of previous calculations and enables high-order corrections for any D-dimension.
We demonstrate the formalism with explicit examples in

The results validate the efficiency and robustness of our approach, offering a reliable tool for studying vacuum metastability in a wide range of field-theoretic and cosmological contexts.

Renormalization and WKB Approximation.-

Now, we have unified
\ln\mathcal{A}_{X}
into the form of (
16
). Unfortunately, such a summation is divergent and needs to be renormalized. The standard procedure is to truncate the approximation of the summation terms within the summation and then add back their known summation result.

As we just mentioned, Ref.
27
computed the higher-order approximation for the Higgs sector in any dimension, while Ref.
4
provided the approximations for the top quark and gauge fields in
D=4
. However, since higher-order terms can deliver more accurate numerical approximations and thereby streamline the numerical computation process, we hereby present a universal method that theoretically allows for the calculation of arbitrary higher-order approximations in any dimension.
This method is primarily based on the WKB approximation, the dimensional regularization and
\overline{MS}
scheme.

On the one hand, we have previously expressed
R^{X}_{\nu}
uniformly in the form of
\lim\limits_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Psi^{X}_{\nu}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}^{X}_{\nu}(r)}
, where
\Psi^{X}_{\nu}(r)
and
\hat{\Psi}^{X}_{\nu}(r)
satisfy their individual equations. We can expand
\Psi^{X}_{\nu}(r)
(and
\hat{\Psi}^{X}_{\nu}(r)
) in terms of
\bar{\nu}
as

\ln\Psi^{X}_{\nu}(r)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}rC^{X}_{n}(r;D)\bar{\nu}^{1-n},

(36)

where
C^{X}_{n}(r,k)
can be obtained by substituting this expansion into the equations (
23
) to (
26
).
On the other hand, the prefactor
\mathcal{A}_{X}
can be written as

\ln\mathcal{A}_{X}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r\text{Deg}_{X}(n;D)\delta C^{X}_{n}(r;D).

(37)

Here,
\text{Deg}_{X}(n;D)\equiv\sum\limits_{\nu=1}^{+\infty}\text{deg}_{X}(\nu;D)\bar{\nu}^{1-n}
is easily computable, and
\delta X\equiv X-\hat{X}
.

We apply the dimensional regularization and
\overline{\rm MS}
scheme to perform renormalization
27
;
28
;
43
;
2
. Since we have already expressed each formula in a dimension-dependent form, so we can directly, replace
D
in (
37
) with
D-\epsilon
, and then simply multiply it by
\left(\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}}\mu^{2}R^{2}}{4\pi}\right)^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}
, then expand the result in
\epsilon
, and subtract
\frac{1}{\epsilon}r^{-\epsilon}\frac{\Omega_{D-\epsilon}}{\Omega_{D}}
for each
\frac{1}{\epsilon}
to obtain the corresponding add-back term. This procedure ultimately equivalent to the result obtained by removing all the
\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\ln 4\pi-\gamma_{E}
terms from expression

\ln\mathcal{A}_{X}=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r\left(\mu Rr\right)^{\epsilon}\frac{\Omega_{D}}{\Omega_{D-\epsilon}}\text{Deg}_{X}(n;D)\delta C^{X}_{n}(r;D).

(38)

Therefore, any part of (
36
) containing the complete divergent term can be used as the subtraction term, while the corresponding part in (
38
) can serve as the add-back term.

Conclusions.-

We have presented a general method for computing vacuum decay rates in D-dimensional Electroweak theories. By combining a WKB expansion with dimensional regularization, our approach allows systematic evaluation of high-order quantum corrections and remains numerically stable even for large angular momenta. The equations governing fluctuation determinants converge rapidly, offering a reliable framework for vacuum stability analysis across dimensions.

Unlike the Standard Model results calculated in literature considering the long life-time of the SM vacuum
19
;
18
;
1
;
5
, our results here imply that the false vacuum decay is highly likely to occur. This is consistent with physical reality, as the decay process in this example involves transitioning from the very early universe state with
v=0
to the symmetry-broken state with
v\neq 0
. The ease of decay aligns with the fact that particles in the present physical world possess mass.
Applying our method to the SMEFT in
D=4
yields
\log_{10}(\gamma\times\text{Gyr}\times\text{Gpc}^{3})=183
. Meanwhile, utilizing our method to the dimensionally reduced
D=3
SMEFT at finite temperature, we obtain
\log_{10}(\Gamma_{T}\times\text{Gyr}\times\text{Gpc}^{3})=42.4
and 116.1 at tree level and two-loop order. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first complete result for the 3D theory.

Finally, this work provides a extensible tool for studying metastable vacua in quantum field theory, with potential applications to cosmological phase transitions and beyond-Standard Model physics.

I.1
Fermion

Due to the symmetry of the bounce solution, the contribution to the prefactor is given by

\displaystyle\ln\mathcal{A}_{\psi}

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{\not{\partial}+\bar{\phi}_{y}}{\not{\partial}+\bar{v}_{y}}

We need to discuss the cases where

\displaystyle\left[\nu-1\right]\otimes\left[+s\right]\simeq

\displaystyle\left[\nu-1,+s\right]\oplus\left[\nu-2,-s\right],

(48)

\displaystyle\left[\nu-1\right]\otimes\left[-s\right]\simeq

\displaystyle\left[\nu-1,-s\right]\oplus\left[\nu-2,+s\right],

\bar{\phi}_{y}^{\prime}&-\Delta_{\nu+1}+\bar{\phi}^{2}_{y}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,\nu}(r)\\
a_{2,\nu}(r)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=0,

(50)

III
Numerical Computation Aspects

The target equations exhibits a divergence at
r=0
. It is therefore necessary to perform Taylor expansions of the functions
\bar{\phi}(r)
and
\Psi_{\nu}^{X}(r)
in order to avoid the singularity in the equations. The Taylor expansion of the function
\bar{\phi}(r)
is

\bar{\phi}(r)=\phi_{c}+\phi_{2}r^{2}+\phi_{4}r^{4}+\phi_{6}r^{6}+O(r^{8}),

(113)

where

\displaystyle U_{i}

\displaystyle\equiv\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{i}U}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\phi}^{i}}\right|_{\bar{\phi}=\phi_{c}},\quad\phi_{2}=\frac{1}{2D}U_{1},\quad\phi_{4}=\frac{1}{8D\left(D+2\right)}U_{1}U_{2},

(114)

\displaystyle\phi_{6}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{48D\left(D+4\right)}\left(\frac{1}{D+2}U_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{D}U_{1}U_{3}\right)U_{1}.

And the Taylor expansions of the
\Psi_{\nu}^{X}(r)
’s are

1.

Higgs

\left.\Psi_{\nu}^{\text{H}}(r)\right|_{r\rightarrow 0}\sim r^{\nu-1}\left[1+\frac{U_{2}}{2\left(2\nu+D-2\right)}r^{2}+\frac{1}{8\left(2\nu+D\right)}\left(\frac{1}{2\nu+D-2}U_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{D}U_{1}U_{3}\right)r^{4}+O(r^{6})\right],

(115)

2.

Fermion

\left.\Psi^{\psi}_{\nu}(r)\right|_{r\rightarrow 0}\sim r^{\nu-1}\left[1+\frac{\phi_{cy}^{2}}{2\left(2\nu+D-2\right)}r^{2}+\frac{\phi_{cy}^{4}+4\left(2\nu+D-1\right)\phi_{cy}\phi_{2y}}{8\left(2\nu+D-2\right)\left(2\nu+D\right)}r^{4}+O(r^{6})\right],

(116)

3.

Da mode

\displaystyle\left.\Psi_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)\right|_{r\rightarrow+0}\sim

\displaystyle r^{\nu-1}\left\{1+\left[D\frac{\phi_{2y}}{\phi_{cy}}+\frac{\nu+D-1}{2\left(\nu+D-3\right)}\phi_{cy}^{2}\right]\frac{r^{2}}{2\nu+D-2}+\left[\left(D+2\right)\frac{\phi_{4y}}{\phi_{cy}}-\frac{D\left(\nu-1\right)}{\left(2\nu+D-2\right)}\frac{\phi_{2y}^{2}}{\phi_{cy}^{2}}\right.\right.

(117)

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\frac{\left(\nu+D+1\right)\phi_{cy}^{4}+8\left(\nu^{2}+2D\nu+D^{2}-D-3\right)\phi_{cy}\phi_{2y}}{8\left(2\nu+D-2\right)\left(\nu+D-3\right)}\right]\frac{r^{4}}{2\nu+D}+O(r^{6})\right\},

4.

T mode

\left.\Psi_{\nu}^{\text{T}}(r)\right|_{r\rightarrow 0}\sim r^{\nu}\left[1+\frac{\phi_{cg}^{2}}{2\left(2\nu+D\right)}r^{2}+\frac{\phi_{cg}^{4}+4\left(2\nu+D\right)\phi_{cg}\phi_{2g}}{8\left(2\nu+D\right)\left(2\nu+D+2\right)}r^{4}+O(r^{6})\right].

(118)

On the other hand, as mentioned in the main text, the equations we actually perform numerical calculations on are

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)=

\displaystyle\left(\delta m_{\phi}^{2}\right)R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)-\left(2\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{H}}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{H}}(r)}+\frac{D-1}{r}\right)\partial_{r}R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r),

(119)

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}R^{\psi}_{\nu}(r)=

\displaystyle\left[\delta\bar{\phi}_{y}^{2}+\frac{\bar{\phi}_{y}^{\prime}}{\bar{\phi}_{y}}\left(\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\psi}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\psi}(r)}-\frac{\nu-1}{r}\right)\right]R^{\psi}_{\nu}(r)-\left(2\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\psi}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\psi}(r)}+\frac{D-1}{r}-\frac{\bar{\phi}_{y}^{\prime}}{\bar{\phi}_{y}}-\frac{\bar{\phi}_{y}^{\prime}}{\bar{\phi}_{y}}\right)\partial_{r}R^{\psi}_{\nu}(r),

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}R_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)=

\displaystyle\left\{\delta m^{2}_{a}+\left[\frac{\partial_{r}\left(\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}\right)}{\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}+L^{2}}-\frac{2v^{2}_{g}r}{v^{2}_{g}r^{2}+L^{2}}\right]\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)}-\frac{\bar{\phi}_{g}^{\prime}}{\bar{\phi}_{g}}\frac{\partial_{r}\left(\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}\right)}{\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}+L^{2}}\right\}R_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)

\displaystyle-\left[2\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{Da}}(r)}+\frac{D-1}{r}-\frac{\partial_{r}\left(\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}\right)}{\bar{\phi}^{2}_{g}r^{2}+L^{2}}\right]\partial_{r}R^{\text{Da}}_{\nu}(r),

\displaystyle\partial_{r}^{2}R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r)=

\displaystyle\left(\delta\bar{\phi}_{g}^{2}\right)R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r)-\left(2\frac{\partial_{r}\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{T}}(r)}{\hat{\Psi}_{\nu}^{\text{T}}(r)}+\frac{D-1}{r}\right)\partial_{r}R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r).

Furthermore, for the Higgs sector where
\hat{m}_{\phi}=0
, noting that
\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}U}{\mathrm{d}\bar{\phi}^{2}}\sim O(r^{-2\left(D-2\right)})
when
r\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)\simeq R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}+Cr^{-2\nu-D+4},\quad\text{if}\quad D>3.

(120)

Therefore,
R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)+\frac{r\partial_{r}R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)}{2\nu+D-4}
and
R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)
share the same limit, with
R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)+\frac{r\partial_{r}R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)}{2\nu+D-4}
converging more rapidly. We redefine
R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)+\frac{r\partial_{r}R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)}{2\nu+D-4}\rightarrow R^{\text{H}}_{\nu}(r)
in this case.

Likewise, when
v=0
and
\hat{m}_{\phi}>0
, or when
v=\hat{m}_{\phi}=0
and
D>3
, the same pattern can be observed in the T mode sector, allowing us to redefine
R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r)+\frac{r\partial_{r}R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r)}{2\nu+D-2}\rightarrow R^{\text{T}}_{\nu}(r)
.

Exploring Dark Energy via Non-Minimal Coupling in f ⁡ ( Q , L m ) f(Q,L_{m}) Gravity with Gong-Zhang Parameterization
Yash B. Ingole, K.V. Somwanshi, S.R. Bhoyar, S.N. Bayaskar

Abstract
Abstract: In this study, we investigate the late-time accelerated expansion of the universe within the framework of non-minimally coupled f ⁡ ( Q , L m ) f(Q,L_{m}) gravity, where Q Q is the non-metricity scalar and L m L_{m} is the matter Lagrangian. We derive modified Friedmann equations in a flat FLRW background and employ the Gong-Zhang parameterization for the DE equation of state (EoS), allowing an analytical form of the Hubble parameter H ⁡ ( z ) H(z) . The model parameters are constrained using recent Cosmic Chronometers (CC) and Pantheon+SH0ES Type Ia supernova datasets through MCMC-based chi-squared minimization. We analyze various cosmological quantities including the deceleration parameter, EoS, jerk, snap, lerk, and diagnostic tools such as O ​ m ​ ( z ) Om(z) and the statefinder pair ( r , s ) (r,s) . Our findings indicate a viable transition from deceleration to acceleration and reveal a quintessence-to-phantom-like evolution of dark energy. Furthermore, energy conditions are examined, showing a violation of the strong energy condition, consistent with current cosmic acceleration. The results establish that the f ⁡ ( Q , L m ) f(Q,L_{m}) framework with non-minimal coupling and parameterized EoS provides a compelling alternative to Λ \Lambda CDM in describing cosmic acceleration. Keywords: f ⁡ ( Q , L m ) f(Q,L_{m}) gravity; Dark Energy; Gong-Zhang EoS Parameterization; Observational Data.

In this section, we provide the mathematical framework needed for an explanation of

(1)

This is the Reimann tensor and its contraction, which plays a leading role in expressing the idea of a curvature of spacetime, offering an understanding of gravitational interactions and the way matter and energy structure the geometry of spacetime. To explore more generalized geometric structures underlying gravity, we consider Weyl-Cartan geometry as a natural extension of the Riemannian framework.
Weyl-Cartan geometry extends the familiar framework of Riemannian geometry by including two additional features: torsion and non-metricity. While Riemannian geometry assumes a symmetric metric-preserving connection, the Weyl-Cartan approach relaxes these constraints, allowing for a more flexible and richer way to describe the geometry of spacetime. In this system, the affine connection

\Gamma^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}

K^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}

L^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}

\Upsilon^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=\Gamma^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}+K^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}+L^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}.

(2)

The Levi-Civita connection
\Gamma^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}

This connection preserves the inner product between tangent vectors during parallel transport, and it governs how curvature emerges in space-time due to gravitational effects. It is given as

\Gamma^{i}_{\ \mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}g^{i\lambda}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\lambda\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\lambda\mu}-\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}\right).

(3)

The contorsion tensor

It reflects the influence of the intrinsic spin or angular momentum of matter on the structure of spacetime. This tensor modifies the usual geodesics, allowing for trajectories that are influenced not only by the metric but also by torsion. The contorsion tensor quantifies the difference between the Levi-Civita connection without torsion and a more general affine connection

K^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(T^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}+T_{\mu\ \nu}^{\ \ i}+T_{\nu\ \mu}^{\ \ i}\right).

(4)

The disformation tensor

Signals a departure from metric compatibility, meaning that the metric tensor is not preserved along these paths. This tensor plays a crucial role in geometric frameworks that extend beyond standard Riemannian geometry, allowing for a more comprehensive description of spacetime structure.

L^{i}_{\ \mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\left(Q^{i}_{\ \mu\nu}-Q_{\mu\ \nu}^{\ \ i}-Q_{\nu\ \mu}^{\ \ i}\right).

The non-metricity tensor

Homogeneity implies that, at any given moment, the universe appears uniform on large scales, its density and structure remain consistent throughout evolution, with no special or preferred locations. Isotropy means that the universe looks the same in every direction, regardless of the observer’s position. Together, these assumptions suggest that the physical laws and conditions governing the universe are consistent, regardless of direction or location. is the energy density. Additionally, the energy-momentum tensor

for a perfect fluid, defined in cosmology as a fluid with uniform energy density and pressure without shear stress or viscosity, is given by:

T_{\mu\nu}=(\rho+p)u_{\mu}u^{\nu}+pg_{\mu\nu},

(20)

where
u_{\mu}
is the four-velocity of the fluid. In the comoving frame, the components of
u_{\mu}
are
(1,0,0,0)
, indicating that the fluid is at rest with respect to the spatial coordinates.

Employing Eqs, (
15
), (
19
) and (
20
), the modified Friedmann equation is obtained as,

3H^{2}=\frac{1}{4f_{Q}}\big[f-f_{Lm}(\rho+L_{m})\big],

(21)

\dot{H}+3H^{2}+\frac{\dot{f}_{Q}}{f_{Q}}H=\frac{1}{4f_{Q}}\big[f+f_{L_{m}}(p-L_{m})\big].

(22)

For the specific case where
f(Q,L_{m})=f(Q)+2L_{m}
, the modified Friedmann equations reduce to those of
f(Q)
gravity
Solanki et al. 2021
;
Mandal et al. 2020
. This form can be further simplified to the Symmetric Teleparallel Equivalent of General Relativity (STEGR).

IV
Cosmological Model in
f(Q,L_{m})
Gravity

In this study, we adopt the non-linear cosmological model from
f(Q,L_{m})
gravity as

f(Q,L_{m})=-\alpha Q+2L_{m}^{n}+\beta,

(23)

where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
n
are the model parameters. This model features a non-minimal coupling between the non-metricity scalar
Q
and the matter Lagrangian
L_{m}
, where the coupling takes a power-law form in
L_{m}
. Unlike the standard additive forms like
f(Q)+2L_{m}
, this non-linear interaction is chosen to allow for a broader range of cosmological behaviors and to explore more complex dynamical scenarios. For
n=1
, the model reduces to the form
f(Q)+2L_{m}
, which corresponds to the standard minimally coupled.
When
n\neq 1
, the model introduces effective interactions between geometry and matter, which can significantly impact the evolution of cosmic expansion. Hence, parameter
n
plays a crucial role in shaping the model’s dynamics. Therefore, we allow its value to be constrained through observational data analysis. These types of coupling were studied in other gravities (see ref.
Harko and Lobo 2010
;
Wang and Liao 2012
;
Goncalves and Moraes 2023
;
Haghani and Harko 2021
;
Arora et al. 2024
;
Loewer et al. 2024
)

The modified Friedmann equations (
21
) and (
22
), for
f_{Q}=-\alpha
and
f_{L_{m}}=2nL_{m}^{n-1}
is given as

3H^{2}=\frac{(2n-1)}{\alpha}\rho^{n}-k,

(24)

2\dot{H}+3H^{2}=\frac{(n-1)\rho-np}{\alpha}\rho^{n-1}-k,

(25)

here
k=\frac{\beta}{2\alpha}
, and the overdot denotes differentiation with respect to cosmic time
t
. We solve these two Friedmann equations to obtain the pressure
p
and the energy density
\rho
. Furthermore, using their relation for the DE equation of state (EoS) parameter and adopting the convention
\dot{H}=-H(1+z)H^{\prime}
where
H^{\prime}=\frac{dH}{dz}
, we obtain the following result.

\omega=\frac{2(2n-1)H(1+z)H^{\prime}}{n(3H^{2}+k)}-1.

(26)

To solve Eqn. (
26
) for
H(z)
, we require an additional equation. Here, we conceive the methodology used in the article
Bhoyar and Ingole 2024
. For this purpose, we assume that the EoS parameter varies with redshift
z
, adopting the well-motivated
Gong-Zhang
EoS parametric form
Gong and Zhang 2005
.

\omega(z)=\frac{w_{0}}{1+z}.

(27)

Using observational data, the model parameter
w_{0}
can be effectively constrained. In the broader framework of cosmological modelling, the
Gong-Zhang
parameterization of the DE, EoS offers several notable advantages. Firstly, it provides a physically meaningful structure,
moreover, by explicitly incorporating redshift dependence, this parameterization enables a detailed understanding of how DE evolves over time. As redshift increases significantly (i.e., in the early universe), the EoS approaches zero,
\omega(z)\sim 0
, suggesting that DE behaves similarly to pressureless matter in the past. In contrast, in the asymptotic future limit
(z\to-1)
, the EoS diverges negatively,
\omega(z)\to-\infty
, indicating a possible phantom-like evolution. These features make the
Gong-Zhang
parameterization a compelling and viable choice to study the dynamical behavior of DE, particularly within the context of
f(Q,L_{m})
gravity.

From the comparative study of Eqs. (
26
) and (
27
), we obtained the Hubble parameter
H
in terms of redshift
z
as

H(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigg[c(z+1)^{\frac{3n}{2n-1}}\exp\Big[\frac{3nw_{0}z}{(2n-1)(z+1)}\Big]-k\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(28)

where
c
is an integrating constant. By means of boundary condition i.e.,
z=0
we can eliminate it

H(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\Bigg[(3H_{0}^{2}+k)(z+1)^{\frac{3n}{2n-1}}\exp\Big[\frac{3nw_{0}z}{(2n-1)(z+1)}\Big]-k\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{2}},

(29)

where
H_{0}
is the present value of the Hubble parameter.

VII
Energy Conditions in
f(Q,L_{m})
Model

Energy conditions (ECs) in GR serve as essential tools for understanding the physical behavior of matter and energy in the universe. These conditions, classified as Null, Weak, Strong and Dominant Energy Conditions, impose certain inequalities on the energy-momentum tensor
T_{\mu\nu}
to ensure physically reasonable outcomes, such as non-negative energy density and causal propagation of energy
Koussour and Bennai 2022
.

The ECs are of profound theoretical significance. For instance, the strong energy condition (SEC) forms the foundation of the Hawking–Penrose singularity theorems
Hawking and Ellis 2023
;
Ellis 2014
, which predict the formation of singularities such as those found in black holes and the early universe. The dominant energy condition (DEC) plays a key role in the proof of the positive mass theorem
Schoen and Yau 1981
, which guarantees that the total energy of an isolated system remains non-negative. Meanwhile, the null energy condition (NEC) is fundamental to the formulation of the second law of black hole thermodynamics.

In cosmology, energy conditions are also crucial for tracing the history of the universe and evaluating the viability of various theoretical models. They help examine the nature of cosmic expansion, test hypotheses related to singularity formation, and assess the consistency of different DE scenarios
Wald 2010
. Thus, energy conditions provide a vital link between abstract gravitational theory and the observable dynamics of the universe. Some work has been done on ECs corresponding to various gravities such as
f(R)

Santos et al. 2007
,
f(T)

Bhoyar and Ingole 2025
,
f(Q)

Mandal et al. 2020
,
f(R,G)

Atazadeh and Darabi 2014
,
f(R,L_{m})

Wang and Liao 2012
,
f(Q,T)

Arora and Sahoo 2020
etc.

There are four widely recognized types of energy conditions (ECs) in General Relativity
Mandal et al. 2020
, each placing specific constraints on the energy density
\rho
and pressure
p
of matter fields:

•

Null Energy Condition (NEC):
Requires that
\rho+p\geq 0
, ensuring non-negative energy density along lightlike (null) trajectories.

•

Weak Energy Condition (WEC):
Imposes
\rho\geq 0
and
\rho+p\geq 0
, indicates that all observers measure a non-negative energy density.

•

Strong Energy Condition (SEC):
Demands
\rho+3p\geq 0
, which is associated with the attractive nature of gravity in classical theory.

•

Dominant Energy Condition (DEC):
Requires
\rho\geq|p|
, ensuring that the energy density dominates the pressure and that the energy flows at or below the speed of light.

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have explored the cosmological implications of a non-minimally coupled
f(Q,L_{m})
gravity theory, which represents a significant extension of symmetric teleparallel gravity. The specific model we investigated takes the form
f(Q,L_{m})=-\alpha Q+2L_{m}^{n}+\beta
, where the non-metricity scalar
Q
interacts non-linearly with the matter Lagrangian
L_{m}
. This setup enables a richer class of gravitational interactions beyond General Relativity and traditional minimally coupled models.

We began by deriving the field equations using the action principle, accounting for the non-conservation of the energy-momentum tensor due to the non-minimal coupling. These modified field equations were applied to a spatially flat FLRW universe, and under the assumption of a perfect fluid with
L_{m}=\rho
, we derived the corresponding Friedmann equations. To solve the resulting system and extract physical insights, we adopted the
Gong-Zhang
parameterization for the DE equation of state
\omega(z)=\frac{w_{0}}{1+z}
, which is known for its smooth, bounded evolution, and physical relevance across both early and late cosmological epochs.

Using this parameterization, we obtained an analytic expression for the Hubble parameter
H(z)
and proceeded to constrain the model parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
,
n
,
w_{0}
, and
H_{0}
through a statistical analysis involving two key observational datasets: 31 data points from the Cosmic Chronometer (CC) method and 1701 data points from the Pantheon+SH0ES Type Ia supernova compilation. The MCMC-based
\chi^{2}
minimization technique provided highly precise best-fit parameter values. Notably, the values
w_{0}\approx-0.62
(CC) and
w_{0}\approx-0.69
(Pantheon+SH0ES) indicate quintessence-like DE behavior, consistent with current constraints.

From the analysis of the deceleration parameter
q(z)
, we observed a clear transition from deceleration to acceleration, occurring at redshifts
z_{t}\approx 0.76
(CC) and
z_{t}\approx 0.90
(Pantheon+SH0ES). These results are in excellent agreement with estimates from independent observations, confirming that our model accurately captures the timing of the onset of cosmic acceleration. The current values of
q_{0}\approx-0.50
and
-0.53
also affirm that the universe is undergoing accelerated expansion.

We also analyzed higher-order cosmographic parameters, jerk
j(z)
, snap
s(z)
, and lerk
l(z)
, which provided additional confirmation of the model’s compatibility with an accelerating universe. In particular, the jerk parameter was found to converge toward unity, consistent with a de Sitter-like late-time expansion. The snap and lerk parameters revealed a dynamic evolution, indicating deviations from
\Lambda
CDM behavior and the potential for future observational tests.

The Om diagnostic showed a negative slope for both datasets, suggesting a phantom-like behavior (i.e.,
\omega<-1
) at late times. Although the EoS values are quintessence-like near the present epoch, the evolving Om curve hints at a future phantom regime, showcasing the model’s ability to interpolate between different DE phases.

The diagnostic analysis of the statefinder placed the trajectory of our model within the
(r,s)
plane. It showed a transition from the Chaplygin gas regime
(r>1,s<0)
through quintessence
(r<1,s>0)
, ultimately approaching the
\Lambda
CDM fixed point
(r=1,s=0)
. This behavior suggests that the model can dynamically evolve to mimic
\Lambda
CDM in the far future, while maintaining greater flexibility in intermediate epochs.

Finally, we investigated the energy conditions (NEC, WEC, SEC, and DEC) under the proposed framework. Although NEC and WEC were satisfied for both datasets, the strong energy condition (SEC) was violated. This violation is a necessary condition to explain the observed late-time acceleration, which further supports the physical validity of the model. The DEC was satisfied, confirming that the energy flows remain causal and physically reasonable.

In summary, the non-minimally coupled
f(Q,L_{m})
model analyzed here successfully reproduces key features of cosmic expansion, including the observed transition to acceleration, quintessence-like and phantom-like behaviors, and compatibility with a range of observational datasets. Through its richer coupling structure and parameterized EoS, it provides a compelling alternative to
\Lambda
CDM and opens new avenues for understanding DE within modified gravity theories.

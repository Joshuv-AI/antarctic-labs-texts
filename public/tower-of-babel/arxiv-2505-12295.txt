RANK OF BICOMPLEX MATRICES AND SYSTEM OF ALGEBRAIC EQUATIONS
Amita, Akhil Prakash, Mamta Amol Wagh, Suman Kumar

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the rank of matrices of bicomplex numbers. The relationship between rank, idempotent column rank and idempotent row rank is examined. Then, the solution of a system of equations in bicomplex space is presented using a new technique. Moreover, we establish a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of solutions of a system of equations in bicomplex space and derive some related results.

Definition 1.1
.

(
Bicomplex numbers)

The set of bicomplex numbers
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\{{u}_{1}+{i}_{1}{u}_{2}+{i}_{2}{u}_{3}+{i}_{1}{i}_{2}{u}_{4}\;:\;{u}_{1},{u}_{2},{u}_{3},{u}_{4}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{0}\},

where
\displaystyle i_{1}
and
\displaystyle i_{2}
are unit vectors such that
\displaystyle i_{1}^{2},i_{2}^{2}=-1
. Alternatively the set of bicomplex numbers is defined as follows

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\{{z}_{1}+{i}_{2}{z}_{2}\;:\;{z}_{1},{z}_{2}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{1}\},

where
\displaystyle z_{1},z_{2}
are
\displaystyle i_{1}
complex numbers and
\displaystyle i_{1}^{2}=-1
.
For convenience, we used the symbols
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{1},\mdmathbb{C}_{0}
for the set of all complex numbers and the set of all real numbers respectively. In
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
, there exist precisely four idempotent elements
\displaystyle 0,1,e_{1},e_{2}
in which
\displaystyle e_{1},e_{2}
are two nontrivial idempotent elements given as

e_{1}=\frac{(1+i_{1}i_{2})}{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{2}=\frac{(1-i_{1}i_{2})}{2}.

The numbers
\displaystyle e_{1}
and
\displaystyle e_{2}
serve as zero divisors, and they are linearly independent in the space
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}(\mdmathbb{C}_{1})
. The linear independence of these elements over
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{1}
gives rise to a new representation of all bicomplex numbers, known as the idempotent representation. Furthermore, for any element
\displaystyle\xi={z}_{1}+{i}_{2}{z}_{2}
in
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
, idempotent representation is defined as

\xi=(z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2})e_{1}+(z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2})e_{2},

where complex numbers
\displaystyle(z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2})
and
\displaystyle(z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2})
are called idempotent components of
\displaystyle\xi
and denoted by
\displaystyle 1_{\xi}
and
\displaystyle 2_{\xi}
respectively. Thus,
\displaystyle\xi={z}_{1}+{i}_{2}{z}_{2}
can also be written as
\displaystyle\xi=1_{\xi}e_{1}+2_{\xi}e_{2}
, where
\displaystyle 1_{\xi}=z_{1}-i_{1}z_{2}
and
\displaystyle 2_{\xi}=z_{1}+i_{1}z_{2}
.

Remark 2.14

Theorem 2.15
.

Let
\displaystyle A={{}^{1}A}e_{1}+{{}^{2}A}e_{2}=[\xi_{ij}]_{m\times n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m\times n}
and
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
be a system of
\displaystyle m
equations in
\displaystyle n
variables
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
, i.e.
\displaystyle X=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
. Then we have the following:

(1)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=2n
, then the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
has a unique solution.

(2)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=k,k\neq 2n
, then the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
has an infinite number of solutions.

(3)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=0
, then every element of
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
is a solution of the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle A={{}^{1}A}e_{1}+{{}^{2}A}e_{2}=[\xi_{ij}]_{m\times n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m\times n}
and
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
is a system of
\displaystyle m
equations in
\displaystyle n
variables
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
,
\displaystyle i.e.

\displaystyle X=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
. By using
2.10
and
2.14
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}:\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}\to\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m}\ \mbox{such that}\ {T}_{A}(X)={A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}\ \mbox{and}\ 2n=\rho({T}_{A})+\eta({T}_{A}),

where
\displaystyle\eta({T}_{A})
represents
\displaystyle dim(\ker({T}_{A}))
. Therefore,
\displaystyle 2n=\rho_{mr}(A)+\eta({T}_{A})
.

(1)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=2n
, then
\displaystyle\eta({T}_{A})=0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ker({T}_{A})=\left\{(0,0,\cdots,0)\right\}\subset\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}.

Therefore, the solution of the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
is only
\displaystyle(0,0,\cdots,0)\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
.

(2)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=k,k\neq 2n
then
\displaystyle\eta({T}_{A})=2n-k>0
. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ker({T}_{A})\neq\left\{(0,0,\cdots,0)\right\}\ \mbox{and has an infinite number of elements}.

Therefore, the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
has an infinite number of solutions.

(3)

If
\displaystyle\rho_{mr}(A)=0
, then
\displaystyle\eta({T}_{A})=2n
. Thus,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ker({T}_{A})=\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}.

Therefore, every element of
\displaystyle\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
is a solution of the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={0}_{m\times 1}
.

∎

Theorem 2.17
.

Let
\displaystyle A={{}^{1}A}e_{1}+{{}^{2}A}e_{2}=[\xi_{ij}]_{m\times n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m\times n},\ B={{}^{1}B}e_{1}+{{}^{2}B}e_{2}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m})\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m}
and
\displaystyle S={{}^{1}S}e_{1}+{{}^{2}S}e_{2}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
be a solution of the system
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}
of
\displaystyle m
equations in
\displaystyle n
variables
\displaystyle x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}
,
\displaystyle i.e.

\displaystyle X=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
. Then
\displaystyle Y={{}^{1}Y}e_{1}+{{}^{2}Y}e_{2}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
is a solution of the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}
if and only if
\displaystyle Y\in\ker({T}_{A})+S
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\displaystyle Y={{}^{1}Y}e_{1}+{{}^{2}Y}e_{2}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{n}
is a solution of the system of equations
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\ker({T}_{A})+S=\left\{U+S:U\in\ker({T}_{A})\right\}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {Y}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}\ \mbox{and}\ {A}_{m\times n}\ {S}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y)={B}\ \mbox{and}\ {T}_{A}(S)={B}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y)-{T}_{A}(S)=B-B=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y-S)=0

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle(Y-S)\in Ker({T}_{A})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y=U+S,\ \mbox{for some}\ U\in Ker({T}_{A}).

Hence,
\displaystyle Y\in\ker({T}_{A})+S.

Converse: Let us suppose that,
\displaystyle Y\in\ker({T}_{A})+S
. Then,

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y=U+S,\ \mbox{for some}\ U\in\ker({T}_{A})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y)={T}_{A}(U+S)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y)={T}_{A}(U)+{T}_{A}(S)

Since,
\displaystyle U\in\ker({T}_{A})
and
\displaystyle S
is a solution of the system
\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {X}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}
, therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{T}_{A}(Y)=0+B

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle{A}_{m\times n}\ {Y}_{n\times 1}={B}_{m\times 1}.

∎

\displaystyle A={{}^{1}A}e_{1}+{{}^{2}A}e_{2}=[\xi_{ij}]_{m\times n}\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m\times n},\ (0,0,\cdots,0)\neq B={{}^{1}B}e_{1}+{{}^{2}B}e_{2}=(b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{m})\in\mdmathbb{C}_{2}^{m}

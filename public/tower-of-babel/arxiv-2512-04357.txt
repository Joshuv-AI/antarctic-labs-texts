𝔏 {{\mathfrak{L}}} -resolvents and pseudo-spectral functions of symmetric linear relations in Hilbert spaces
Volodymyr Derkach

Abstract
. Let A A be a closed symmetric operator with the deficiency index ( p , p ) (p,p) , p < ∞ p<\infty , acting in a Hilbert space ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} and let 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} be a subspace of ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} . The set of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvents of a densely defined symmetric operator in a Hilbert space with a proper gauge 𝔏 ( ⊂ ℌ ) {\mathfrak{L}}(\subset{\mathfrak{H}}) was described by Kreĭn and Saakyan. The Kreĭn–Saakyan theory of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrices was extended by Shmul’yan and Tsekanovskii to the case of improper gauge 𝔏 ( ⊄ ℌ ) {\mathfrak{L}}(\not\subset{\mathfrak{H}}) and by Langer and Textorius to the case of symmetric linear relations in Hilbert spaces. In the present paper we find connections between the theory of boundary triples and the Kreĭn–Saakyan theory of 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrices for symmetric linear relations with improper gauges in Hilbert spaces and extend the known formula for the 𝔏 {\mathfrak{L}} -resolvent matrix in terms of boundary operators to this class of relations. Descriptions of spectral and pseudo-spectral functions of symmetric linear relations with improper gauges are given. The results are applied to linear relations generated by a canonical system.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Linear relations in Hilbert spaces

Let us recall some definitions from
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
.
A linear relation
T
in a Hilbert space

({\mathfrak{H}},(\cdot,\cdot)_{{\mathfrak{H}}})

is a linear subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
.
We use two representations for elements of
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
: either

\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}
or
\text{\rm col}\{f,g\}
, where
f,g\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
The
domain
,
kernel
,
range
, and
multivalued part
of a linear relation
T
are defined as follows:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\dom{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\ \text{for some}\ g\in{\mathfrak{H}}\right\},

\displaystyle\ker{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{f\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
0\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\ran{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{g\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\ \text{for some}\ f\in{\mathfrak{H}}\right\},

\displaystyle\mul{T}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{g\colon\begin{bmatrix}0\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

The
adjoint
linear relation
T^{*}
is defined by

(2.3)

T^{*}\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}u\\
f\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}\colon(f,v)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}=(u,g)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}\ \text{for any}\ \begin{bmatrix}v\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

A linear relation
T
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
is called
closed
if
T
is closed as a subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}
.
The set of all closed linear operators (relations) is denoted by
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}})
).
Identifying a linear operator
T\in\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
with its graph one can consider
\mathcal{C}({\mathfrak{H}})
as a part of
\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}})
.

Let
T
be a closed linear relation,
{z}\in\mathbb{C}
, then

(2.4)

T-{z}I\coloneqq\left\{\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g-{z}f\end{bmatrix}\colon\begin{bmatrix}f\\
g\end{bmatrix}\in T\right\}.

A point
{z}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\ker{\left(T-{z}I\right)}=\{0\}
and
\ran{\left(T-{z}I\right)}={\mathfrak{H}}
is called a
regular point
of the linear relation
T
. Let
\rho(T)
be the set of regular points.
The
point spectrum

\sigma_{p}(T)
of the linear relation
T

is defined by

(2.5)

\sigma_{p}(T)\coloneqq\{{z}\in\mathbb{C}\colon\ker(T-{z}I)\neq\{0\}\},

A linear relation
A
is called
symmetric
in a Hilbert space

{\mathfrak{H}}
if
A\subseteq A^{*}
.
A point
{z}\in\mathbb{C}
is called a
point of regular type
(and is written as
{z}\in\widehat{\rho}(A)
) for a closed symmetric linear relation
A
, if
{z}\notin\sigma_{p}(A)
and the subspace
\ran(A-{z}I)
is closed in
{\mathfrak{H}}
.
For
{z}\in\widehat{\rho}(A)
let us set

{\mathfrak{N}}_{{z}}\coloneqq\ker(A^{*}-{z}I)
and

(2.6)

\widehat{{\mathfrak{N}}}_{{z}}\coloneqq\left\{\widehat{f}_{{z}}=\text{\rm col}\{f_{{z}},{z}f_{{z}}\}\colon f_{{z}}\in{\mathfrak{N}}_{{z}}\right\}.

As is known, see
[
22
, Theorem 6.1]
, the number
\dim{{\mathfrak{N}}}_{{z}}
take a constant value
n_{+}(A)
for all
{z}\in\widehat{\rho}(A)\cap{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, and
n_{-}(A)
for all
{z}\in\widehat{\rho}(A)\cap{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, where

{\mathbb{C}}_{\pm}=\{{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}:\,\pm\text{Im }{z}>0\}.
The numbers
n_{\pm}(A)
are called the
defect numbers
of
A
.

2.2.
{\mathcal{R}}
-families

A complex scalar function
\sigma({\lambda})
is related to the class
{\mathfrak{S}}^{(k)}
,
k=0,1,2
, if
it has a bounded variation on every finite interval and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{d\sigma({\lambda})}{1+|{\lambda}|^{k}}<\infty.

Definition 2.1
.

A
p\times p
matrix-valued function (mvf)
Q({z})
holomorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
is said to belong to the class
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
if:

(a)

the kernel

{\mathsf{N}}^{Q}_{\zeta}({z}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\displaystyle Q({z})-Q({\zeta})^{*}}{\displaystyle{z}-\overline{{\zeta}}}&:\quad\textrm{for }{z},\,{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ {z}\neq\overline{{\zeta}},\\
Q^{\prime}({z})&:\quad\textrm{for }{z},\,{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ {z}=\overline{{\zeta}}\end{array}\right.

is non-negative on
{\mathbb{C}}
;

(b)

Q^{\#}({z})=Q({z})
for all
{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
.

The class
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
coincides with the class of mvf’s holomorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
which satisfy the assumption (b) in Definition
2.1
and have non-negative imaginary part
\text{Im }Q({z})
in
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}
, see
[
25
]
.
A mvf

is said to belong to the class
{\mathcal{R}}_{u}^{p\times p}

if
\det\operatorname{Im}{Q({i})}\neq 0
.

Recall that each
{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}
-valued function
Q\in{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
admits the integral representation

(2.7)

Q({z})=\alpha+\beta{z}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda-{z}}-\frac{{\lambda}}{1+{\lambda}^{2}}\right)d\sigma({\lambda}),

where
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p\times p}
,
\beta\geq 0
,
\alpha=\alpha^{*}
and
\sigma({\lambda})
is a non-decreasing
left-continuous
{p\times p}
mvf of the class
{\mathfrak{S}}^{(2)}
.

For every
Q\in{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
the corresponding spectral function
\sigma(t)
can be found by the Stieltjes inversion formula

(2.8)

\sigma({\lambda}_{2})-\sigma({\lambda}_{1})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\lim_{\nu\downarrow 0}\int_{{\lambda}_{1}}^{{\lambda}_{2}}\operatorname{Im}\varphi(\mu+i\nu)d\mu.

Definition 2.2
.

A family
(\tau({z}))_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}}
, where

\tau({z})=\ran\begin{bmatrix}\varphi({z})\\
\psi({z})\end{bmatrix}
and
\varphi
and
\psi
are
p\times p
mvf’s holomorphic on

{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
, will be called an
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
-family if:

(i)

the kernel

{\mathsf{N}}_{\zeta}^{\varphi\psi}({z}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\displaystyle\varphi({\zeta})^{*}\psi({z})-\psi({\zeta})^{*}\varphi({z})}{\displaystyle{z}-\overline{{\zeta}}}&:\quad\textrm{for }{z},\,{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ {z}\neq\overline{{\zeta}},\\
\varphi^{\#}({z})\psi({z})-\psi({z})^{\#}\varphi({z})&:\quad\textrm{for }{z},\,{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-},\ {z}=\overline{{\zeta}}\end{array}\right.

is non-negative on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
;

(ii)

\varphi^{\#}({z})\psi({z})-\psi^{\#}({z})\varphi({z})
for all
{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
;

(iii)

\ker\varphi({z})\cap\ker\psi({z})=\{0\}

for all
{z}\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

The set of
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
-families is denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
.

Two
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
-families

\left(\text{\rm col}\{\varphi_{1}({z}),\psi_{1}({z})\}\right)_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}}
and

\left(\text{\rm col}\{\varphi_{2}({z}),\psi_{2}({z})\}\right)_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}}

coincide, if

\varphi_{2}({z})=\varphi_{1}({z})\chi({z})

and
\psi_{2}({z})=\psi_{1}({z})\chi({z})

for some mvf
\chi({z})
,
which is holomorphic and invertible on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
.

Every
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
-family
\tau=\left(\text{\rm col}\{\varphi({z}),\psi({z})\}\right)_{z\in{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}}

can be treated as a family of linear relations in
{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
.
In the case when
\det\varphi({z})\not\equiv 0

it has no zeros on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
and

\tau(z)
coincides on
{\mathbb{C}}_{+}\cup{\mathbb{C}}_{-}
with
the graph of
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
-function

.
Since every mvf from
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
admits such a representation,
the set
{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
can be identified with a subset of

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{p\times p}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A pair
\begin{bmatrix}C({z})&D({z})\end{bmatrix}
where
C
and

are
p\times p

In this section we present the construction of the rigging

of a Hilbert space associated with a symmetric linear relation
A
and look at the properties of
extended generalized resolvents of

3.1.
Rigged Hilbert spaces.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
is a closed symmetric linear relation with equal
defect numbers
n_{\pm}(A)=p<\infty
in a Hilbert space

{\mathfrak{H}}
and
let
P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}
be the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}:={\cdom A}

in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Then there exists a pair
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
of Hilbert spaces with the duality
\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}
for
\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
such that

(i)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}=\dom A^{*}\oplus\mul A(\subset{\mathfrak{H}})
;

(ii)

{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and for all
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
and
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
we have

\langle{f},h\rangle_{-,+}=(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}}
.

Moreover, the
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
-norm in
\dom A^{*}
can be defined by

(3.1)

\|h\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}=\|h\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}h^{\prime}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for $\begin{bmatrix}h\\
h^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in A^{*}$}.

Proof.

If
\mul A\neq\{0\}
, then the linear relation
A
admits the representation

(3.2)

A=\gr(A_{\rm op})\widehat{\oplus}\,A_{\rm mul},

where
A_{\rm op}
is a single-valued symmetric operator in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}:={\mathfrak{H}}\ominus\mul A
,
while
A_{\rm mul}:=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mul A\end{bmatrix}
is a purely multi-valued linear relation in the subspace
{\mathfrak{H}}_{1}:=\mul A
, called
the multivalued part
of
A
.

Step 1: Construction of a rigging for the operator
A_{\rm op}
.

Let us consider
A_{\rm op}
as an operator from the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}:=\overline{\dom A}
to the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
and denote it by
A_{0}:{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}\to{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
and let

A_{0}^{*}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op},{\mathfrak{H}}_{0})
be the adjoint to the operator
A_{0}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{0},{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op})
. Then
A_{0}^{*}
is a single-valued operator.
Denote by
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
the linear space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}:=\dom A_{0}^{*}
endowed with the inner product

(3.3)

(h,g)_{0,+}:=(h,g)_{{\mathfrak{H}}}+(A_{0}^{*}h,A_{0}^{*}g)_{\mathfrak{H}},\quad h,g\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}=\dom A_{0}^{*}.

Since the operator
A_{0}^{*}
is closed,
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
is a Hilbert space with the graph norm

(3.4)

\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{0,+}=\|h_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}+\|A_{0}^{*}h_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2},\quad h_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}=\dom A_{0}^{*},

such that
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
. If
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
, then
A_{\rm op}^{*}

is a linear relation in
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
:

(3.5)

A_{\rm op}^{*}=\mbox{gr }A_{0}^{*}\ \widehat{\oplus}\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
{\mathfrak{H}}_{2}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathfrak{H}}_{2}={\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\ominus{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}.

By
[
8
, Section 1.1.1]
,
there exists a dual Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
of bounded conjugate linear functionals on
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
with the duality

(3.6)

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle^{(0)}_{-,+}:=\mathfrak{f}(h)\quad\textrm{for \ $\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}$ \ and \quad$h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}$},

between
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}
.
The embedding
{\imath}:{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
is realized by the identification of
any vector
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
with the functional

(3.7)

{\imath}f:h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\mapsto({\imath}f)(h):=(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}},

and the space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
can be realized as the completion of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
with respect to the norm

\|{\imath}f\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}}=\sup_{h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}}|}{{\quad}\|h\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}}}.

For
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
we will identify
{\imath}f
with
f
. This gives the inclusion
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}
.
In view of (
3.7
) and (
3.6
)
the expression
\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}
can be viewed as an extension of the inner product in the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
. The triple

{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}

is called a
rigged Hilbert space

{\mathfrak{H}}_{\rm op}
, cf.,
[
8
]
.

Step 2: Construction of a rigging for the linear relation
A
.

Setting

(3.8)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}={\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}\oplus{\mathfrak{H}}_{1},\quad{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}={\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-}\oplus{\mathfrak{H}}_{1},\quad\text{where ${\mathfrak{H}}_{1}=\mul A$},

we obtain a rigged Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
:

(3.9)

{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}

with the duality between
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
given by

(3.10)

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}:=\langle\mathfrak{f}_{0},h_{0}\rangle^{(0)}_{-,+}+(f_{1},h_{1})_{\mathfrak{H}}

for

(3.11)

\mathfrak{f}=\mathfrak{f}_{0}+f_{1},\quad{h}=h_{0}+h_{1},\quad\mathfrak{f}_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,-},\quad h_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+},\quad f_{1},h_{1}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{1}.

Then it follows from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that

(3.12)

\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}=(f,h)_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{ for all \ $f\in{\mathfrak{H}}$\, \ and \ $h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}$}.

Since
A^{*}=A_{\rm op}^{*}\widehat{\oplus}\,\begin{bmatrix}0\\
\mul A\end{bmatrix}
,
we obtain, by (
3.5
), that every
\widehat{h}\in A^{*}
admits the representation

\widehat{h}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{0}\\
h^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}h_{0}\\
A_{0}^{*}h_{0}+h_{1}^{\prime}+h_{2}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad h_{0}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+},\,\,h_{1}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{1},\,\,h_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{2},

and hence, by (
3.8
) and (
3.3
),

(3.13)

\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}=\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0,+}}=\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}}+\|A_{0}^{*}h_{0}\|_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}^{2}=\|h_{0}\|^{2}_{{\mathfrak{H}}}+\|P_{{\mathfrak{H}}_{0}}h^{\prime}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}.

This proves (
3.1
) for
A^{*}
.
∎

The triple
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
constructed in Lemma

is called a

.

For a linear operator
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
we denote by

its adjoint with respect to the duality

, i.e.,

(3.14)

\langle\mathfrak{f},T^{\langle*\rangle}\xi\rangle_{-,+}=\xi^{*}(T\mathfrak{f}),\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}.

For
T\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
we set
T^{\langle*\rangle}:=T^{*}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}})
.
For the operator
\gamma(\overline{{z}})\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})

its adjoint
\gamma(\overline{{z}})^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
is defined by

(3.15)

\xi^{*}(\gamma(\overline{{z}})^{\langle*\rangle}\mathfrak{f}):=\langle\mathfrak{f},\gamma(\overline{{z}})\xi\rangle_{-,+},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}.

In what follows we also use the notation

(3.16)

\langle h,\mathfrak{f}\rangle_{+,-}:=\langle\mathfrak{f},h\rangle_{-,+}^{*},\quad\text{for}\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+},\quad\mathfrak{f}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{-},

and the adjoint to

with respect to the duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{+,-}
is defined as the operator
L^{\langle*\rangle}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{+},{\mathbb{C}}^{p})
such that

(3.17)

\xi^{*}(L^{\langle*\rangle}h):=\langle h,L\xi\rangle_{+,-},\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+},\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}.

\begin{bmatrix}g\\
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}g_{0}\\
g_{0}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}g_{z}\\
{z}g_{z}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for some $\widehat{g}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}g_{0}\\
g_{0}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbf{A}}_{0}$ and
$\widehat{g}_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}g_{z}\\
{z}g_{z}\end{bmatrix}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{N}}_{z}$}.

Then

h:=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-{z}g+{{\widetilde{g}}}=g_{0}^{\prime}-{z}g_{0}+{{\widetilde{g}}}\in{\mathfrak{H}},

By Lemma
3.4
(i),
R_{z}^{0}h=g_{0}+\widetilde{R}_{z}^{0}{{\widetilde{g}}}
and hence

g+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{g}}}=g_{0}+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{g}}}+g_{z}=R_{z}^{0}h-(\widetilde{R}_{z}^{0}-{\mathbf{R}}^{0}){{\widetilde{g}}}+g_{z}\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}.

(ii)
If
\widehat{g}=\widehat{g}_{z}\in\widehat{\mathfrak{N}}_{z}
, the equality (
3.35
) is reduced to (
3.34
). Therefore, it is enough to prove (
3.35
) for

\widehat{g}\in{\mathbf{A}}_{0}
. Let us choose
\widehat{g}_{n}=\text{\rm col}\{g_{n};\mathfrak{g}_{n}^{\prime}\}\in A_{0}
such that
g_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{H}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}g
,
g_{n}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
Then, by Lemma
3.6
,
\Gamma_{j}\widehat{g}_{n}\to\widehat{\Gamma}_{j}\widehat{g}
for
j=0,1
and,
by (
3.7
), we get

(3.36)

(\Gamma_{0}\widehat{g}_{n})^{*}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\widehat{f})-(\Gamma_{1}\widehat{g}_{n})^{*}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}\widehat{f})=\langle\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},g_{n}\rangle_{-,+}-(f,g_{n}^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{H}}=C_{n}+D_{n}

where

(3.37)

C_{n}=(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{f}}},g_{n})_{\mathfrak{H}}-\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},g_{n}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{-,+}

and

(3.38)

\begin{split}D_{n}&=\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{+,-}+({\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},g_{n}^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{H}}-\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},g_{n}\rangle_{-,+}\\
&=\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{+,-}+\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},R^{0}g_{n}^{\prime}-g_{n}\rangle_{-,+}\end{split}

Since
\begin{bmatrix}g_{n}\\
g_{n}^{\prime}-{z}g_{n}\end{bmatrix}\in A_{0}-{z}I

and
\begin{bmatrix}g\\
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-{z}g\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbf{A}}_{0}-{z}I

we get, by Lemma
3.4
(i),

R_{z}^{0}(g_{n}^{\prime}-{z}g_{n})=g_{n},\quad\widetilde{R}_{z}^{0}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-{z}g)=g,

and hence, by Lemma
3.2
(ii),

R_{z}^{0}g_{n}^{\prime}-g_{n}={z}\widetilde{R}_{z}^{0}g_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{z}R_{z}^{0}g=\widetilde{R}_{z}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-g\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+},\quad\text{for all ${z}\in\rho(A_{0})$}.

Therefore,

R^{0}g_{n}^{\prime}-g_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbf{R}}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-g
as
n\to\infty

and so

D_{n}\to\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{+,-}+\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},{\mathbf{R}}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-g\rangle_{-,+}\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Passing to the limit in (
3.37
) and (
3.36
) as
n\to\infty
we obtain

C_{n}\to(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{f}}},g)_{\mathfrak{H}}-(f,\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{g}}})_{\mathfrak{H}}-({\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{g}}})_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty

and thus

(3.39)

(\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}\widehat{g})^{*}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\widehat{f})-(\widehat{\Gamma}_{1}\widehat{g})^{*}(\widehat{\Gamma}_{0}\widehat{f})=(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{f}}},g)_{\mathfrak{H}}-(f,\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{g}}})_{\mathfrak{H}}\\
+\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{+,-}+\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},{\mathbf{R}}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-g\rangle_{-,+}-\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},{\mathbf{R}}^{0}\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}\widetilde{g}\rangle_{-,+}\\
=(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{f}}},g)_{\mathfrak{H}}-(f,\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}+{{\widetilde{g}}})_{\mathfrak{H}}+\langle f+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{f}}},{{\widetilde{g}}}\rangle_{+,-}-\left\langle{{\widetilde{f}}},g+{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{{\widetilde{g}}}\right\rangle_{-,+}.

The latter equality is equivalent to (
3.35
).
∎

Definition 4.1
.

Let

a symmetric linear relation
A
,
and let
{{\mathfrak{L}}}
be a closed subspace of

(i)

A point

{{\mathfrak{L}}}
-

{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}=\ran({\mathbf{A}}-{{z}}I_{\mathfrak{H}})\dotplus{{\mathfrak{L}}}.

{{\mathfrak{L}}}
-regular points of the operator

(ii)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
be a rigged Hilbert space associated with
a symmetric linear relation
A
,
let
{\mathbf{R}}^{0}
be the regularizer given by (
3.19
),
let
{{\mathfrak{L}}}
be a subspace of
{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}
of dimension
p
,
let
{L}\in{\mathcal{B}}({{\mathbb{C}}^{p}},{{\mathfrak{L}}})
be invertible,
and let

{z}\in\widehat{\rho}(A)
. Then

(i)

{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})\,\Longleftrightarrow\,

{L}^{\langle*\rangle}\!\upharpoonright\!{\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}}:{\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
is invertible.

(ii)

The operator function

(4.2)

{\mathcal{P}}({z})={L}^{-1}\Pi^{z}_{{\mathfrak{L}}}:{\mathfrak{H}}_{-}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{p},\quad{z}\in\rho(A,{\mathfrak{L}}),

takes values in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
and

{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}=({L}^{\langle*\rangle}\!\upharpoonright\!{\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}})^{-1}\in{\mathcal{B}}({{\mathbb{C}}^{p}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{+})
.

(iii)

For
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
we have

(4.3)

{\mathcal{P}}({z}){L}=I_{p},\quad{L}^{\langle*\rangle}{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}=I_{p}.

(iv)

If
{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}
is a generalized resolvent of
A
and
\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}
is a regularized extended generalized resolvent holomorphic at
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
then
the operator function

(4.4)

{\mathcal{Q}}({z})={L}^{\langle*\rangle}({\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z}):{\mathfrak{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{p},\quad{z}\in\rho(A,{\mathfrak{L}}),

takes values in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
and does not depend on the choice of generalized resolvent
{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}
. Let us set

{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}:={\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{*}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbb{C}}^{p},{\mathfrak{H}})
.

(v)

For
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
the operator
{\mathcal{Q}}({z})-{L}^{\langle*\rangle}R^{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
admits a continuation
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z})={L}^{\langle*\rangle}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}(I-{L}{\mathcal{P}}({z}))\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
and

(4.5)

\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z}){L}=O_{p},\quad{L}^{\langle*\rangle}\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}={L}^{\langle*\rangle}({\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}-{\mathbf{R}}^{0}{L})=O_{p}.

Proof.

(i)–(iii) For
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
we have
{\mathcal{P}}({z}){L}=I_{p}
and hence

{L}^{\langle*\rangle}{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}=I_{p}
. Since
{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}}
, this implies that
actually
{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}={\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}}
and
the operator
{L}^{\langle*\rangle}\!\upharpoonright\!{\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}}\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{N}}_{\overline{z}},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
is invertible.

(iv) The definition (
4.4
) of
Q({z})
is correct since for
f\in{\mathfrak{H}}
we have

{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}
and

({\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z})f=\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}(I-\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z})f+R^{0}f\in{\mathfrak{H}}_{+}.

If
{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)}
is another generalized resolvent of
A
and

\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)}
is the corresponding regularized extended generalized resolvent holomorphic at
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
and if

f=(h^{\prime}-{z}h)+\mathfrak{g}\quad\text{for some}\quad\text{\rm col}\{h;h^{\prime}\}\in{\mathbf{A}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}\in{{\mathfrak{L}}},

then, by Lemma
3.3
(iii), we get

\begin{split}{L}^{\langle*\rangle}({\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z})f&-{L}^{\langle*\rangle}({\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)}\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z})f={L}^{\langle*\rangle}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)})(I_{{\mathfrak{H}}}-\Pi_{{{\mathfrak{L}}}}^{z})f\\
&={L}^{\langle*\rangle}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}-\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}^{(1)})(h^{\prime}-{z}h)={L}^{\langle*\rangle}(h-h)=0.\end{split}

(v) Since
\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z})={L}^{\langle*\rangle}\widehat{\mathbf{R}}_{{z}}(I-\Pi_{\mathfrak{L}}^{z})\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{-},{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}})
it holds that

\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z}){L}=O_{p}
for
{z}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})
.
The second equality in (
4.5
) follows from the first and the equality

\widetilde{\mathcal{Q}}({z})={\mathcal{Q}}({z})-L^{\langle*\rangle}{\mathbf{R}}^{0}
.
∎

In particular, by Lemma
4.2
(iii), we obtain the following statement.

Corollary 4.3
.

For all

the ovf

(4.6)

Lemma 4.4
.

For
{{z}}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})

(4.7)

Then

(4.8)

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}\\

and

(i)

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}u

(ii)

For

(4.9)

A^{\langle*\rangle}={\mathbf{A}}\dotplus\widehat{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\dotplus\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}.

Proof.

(i)
The inclusion

\widehat{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\subseteq A^{\langle*\rangle}
follows from Lemma
4.2
(ii).
Next, for

&=u^{*}\left\{{L}^{\langle*\rangle}h-{L}^{\langle*\rangle}R_{z}^{0}(h^{\prime}-{z}h)\right\}=0.\end{split}

Hence,
\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\subseteq A^{\langle*\rangle}
.

(ii)
The inclusion
A\dotplus\widehat{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\dotplus\widehat{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}{{\mathbb{C}}^{p}}\subseteq A^{\langle*\rangle}
follows from (i) and (

Let us prove the converse inclusion. Let

\text{\rm col}\{f;\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\}\in A^{\langle*\rangle}

Since
\overline{{z}}\in\rho(A,{{\mathfrak{L}}})

\text{\rm col}\{g;\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\}\in{\mathbf{A}}
and

\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}-\overline{{z}}f=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}-\overline{{z}}g+{L}u.

\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}-\overline{{z}}f\end{bmatrix},\ \begin{bmatrix}g\\

\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}g+{\mathcal{P}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}v+{\mathcal{Q}}({z})^{\langle*\rangle}u\\

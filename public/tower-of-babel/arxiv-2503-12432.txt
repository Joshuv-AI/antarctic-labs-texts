Abstract
. In a recent work, Kai Tang conjectured that any compact Hermitian manifold with non-zero constant mixed curvature must be Kähler. He confirmed the conjecture in complex dimension 2 2 and for Chern Kähler-like manifolds in general dimensions. In this paper, we verify his conjecture for several special types of Hermitian manifolds, including complex nilmanifolds, solvmanifolds with complex commutators, almost abelian Lie groups, and Lie algebras containing a J J -invariant abelian ideal of codimension 2 2 . We also verify the conjecture for all compact balanced threefolds when the Bismut connection has parallel torsion. These results provide partial evidence towards the validity of Tang’s conjecture.

1.
Introduction

The simplest kind of Riemannian manifolds are the
space forms,
which are complete Riemannian manifolds with constant sectional curvature. They are quotients of the sphere
S^{n}
, the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or the hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, equipped with (a scaling of) the standard metric. In the complex case, the sectional curvature of a Hermitian metric can no longer be constant unless the metric is flat. So instead one requires that the holomorphic sectional curvature be constant. A complete Kähler manifold with constant holomorphic sectional curvature is called a
complex space form
. Their universal covers are either the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n}
, or the complex Euclidean space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, or the complex hyperbolic space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{H}^{n}
, equipped with (a scaling of) the standard metric.

Recall that for a given Hermitian manifold
(M^{n},g)
, the
holomorphic sectional curvature

H
of the Chern connection is defined by

\displaystyle H(X)=R(X,\overline{X},X,\overline{X})/|X|^{4}\,\,,

where
X
is any non-zero type
(1,0)
tangent vector, and
R
is the curvature tensor of the Chern connection. When the metric
g
is non-Kähler,
H
cannot determine the entire
R
in general. So it is natural to wonder when can
H
be constant. The following is a long-standing conjecture in Hermitian geometry:

Conjecture 1

(
Constant Holomorphic Sectional Curvature Conjecture).

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with
H
equal to a constant
c
. If
c\neq 0
, then
g
is Kähler and if
c=0
, then
g
is Chern flat (namely
R=0
).

A few quick remarks. First of all, if one replaces the Chern connection by another metric connection, such as Levi-Civita, Bismut, or Gauduchon connections in the conjecture, then one gets related conjectures/questions. See
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
for more details on this. Secondly, note that Boothby
[
10
]
classified compact Chern flat manifolds in 1958 as the set of all compact quotients of complex Lie groups, equipped with left-invariant metrics compatible with the complex structure. Thirdly, the compactness assumption in the conjecture is necessary, and there are counterexamples in the noncompact case.

When
n=2
, the conjecture is known to be true. The
c\leq 0
case was established by Balas-Gauduchon
[
7
]
in 1985, while the more difficult
c>0
case was solved by Apostolov-Davidov-Muskarov
[
5
]
in 1996.

For
n\geq 3
the conjecture remains an open problem, with only a few partial results known so far. For instance, in
[
20
]
, Davidov-Grantcharov-Muskarov showed that the only twistor space with constant holomorphic sectional curvature is the complex space form
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. In
[
39
]
, K. Tang confirmed the conjecture under the additional assumption that
g
is Chern Kähler-like (namely
R
obeys all Kähler symmetries). In
[
11
]
, Chen-Chen-Nie proved the conjecture under the additional assumption that
g
is locally conformally Kähler and
c\leq 0
. In
[
58
]
, Zhou-Zheng proved that any compact balanced threefold with zero
real bisectional curvature
, a notion introduced in
[
51
]
which is slightly stronger than
H
, must be Chern flat. In
[
28
]
and
[
35
]
, Li-Zheng or Rao-Zheng confirmed the conjecture for complex nilmanifolds or compact Hermitian manifolds that are
Bismut Kähler-like,
meaning that the curvature
R^{b}
of the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
obeys all Kähler symmetries.

The notion of
mixed curvature

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}
was introduced by Chu-Lee-Tam
[
19
]
as a convex combination of the (first) Ricci curvature and holomorphic sectional curvature of the Chern connection:

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}(X)=\alpha\operatorname{Ric}(X,\overline{X})+\beta H(X),

where
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
X\in T^{1,0}M
is a type
(1,0)
tangent vector of unit length, and
\operatorname{Ric}
is the first Ricci curvature of the Chern connection, defined by
\operatorname{Ric}(X,\overline{X})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R(X,\overline{X},e_{i},\overline{e}_{i})
where
e
is any local unitary frame. Throughout this paper, we will always assume that
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
.

In the above notation, we have
\mathcal{C}_{1,0}=Ric
and
\mathcal{C}_{0,1}=H
. When the metric
g
is Kähler, the mixed curvature encompasses several interesting curvature conditions. Specifically,
\mathcal{C}_{1,1}
corresponds to the
Ric^{+}(X,\overline{X})
introduced by Ni
[
33
]
, and
\mathcal{C}_{1,-1}
corresponds to the
orthogonal Ricci curvature

Ric^{\perp}(X,\overline{X})
introduced by Ni-Zheng
[
34
]
. Additionally, if
k
is an integer between
1
and
n
, then
\mathcal{C}_{k-1,n-k}
is closely related to the
k
-Ricci curvature
Ric_{k}
introduced by Ni
[
32
]
.

For compact Kähler manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
or
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\leq 0
, there have been important progress made in recent years. For example, Yang
[
48
]
proved that a compact Kähler manifold with
\mathcal{C}_{0,1}>0
must be projective and rationally connected, confirming a conjecture by Yau
[
52
]
. Ni
[
33
]
generalized this result, showing that the same holds if
Ric_{k}>0
for some
1\leq k\leq n
. Matsumura
[
30
,
31
]
established structure theorems for projective manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{0,1}\geq 0
, while Chu-Lee-Tam
[
19
]
and others proved that compact Kähler manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}>0
, where
\alpha>0
and
3\alpha+2\beta\geq 0
, must be projective and simply-connected. Zhang-Zhang
[
57
]
extended Yang’s result to the quasi-positive case, confirming a conjecture by Yang. Tang
[
40
]
demonstrated the projectivity of compact Kähler manifolds with quasi-positive
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}
. More recently, Chu-Lee-Zhu
[
17
]
established a structure theorem for compact Kähler manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}\geq 0
. In the non-positive case, Wu-Yau
[
46
]
confirmed a conjecture of Yau that projective Kähler manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{0,1}<0
must have ample canonical line bundles. Tosatti-Yang
[
43
]
showed that the projectivity assumption can be removed in Wu-Yau’s Theorem, and Chu-Lee-Tam
[
19
]
proved that compact Kähler manifolds with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}<0
and
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta\geq 0
, must have ample canonical bundles.

These recent developments on mixed curvature are mainly focused on Kähler manifolds, and much less is known about the non-Kähler case. In
[
41
]
, Tang proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 2

(
Tang).

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact Hermitian manifold with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, where
c
is a constant. If
c\neq 0
, then
g
is Kähler.

Note that in the conjecture one may assume that
\beta\neq 0
. The
\beta=0
case corresponds to (first) Chern Ricci curvature, where the metric is automatically Kähler when
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,0}=c\neq 0
, as in this case the Kähler form is a constant multiple of the Chern Ricci form, which is always closed.

For the
c=0
case, the vanishing of the mixed curvature
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}
in general does not imply
R=0
, as Tang have already demonstrated in
[
41
]
by isosceles Hopf manifolds.

In complex dimension
n=2
, Tang
[
41
]
proved that any compact Hermitian surface with constant mixed curvature must be either Kähler or an isosceles Hopf surface. For
n\geq 3
, Tang also obtained results for locally conformally Kähler manifolds and for Chern Kähler-like manifolds.

Recall that a
Lie-Hermitian manifold
is a compact quotient
M=G/\Gamma
of a Lie group
G
by a discrete subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq G
, where the complex structure and metric on
G
are left-invariant. If
G
is nilpotent or solvable,
M
is called a
complex nilmanifold
or
complex solvmanifold
, respectively. Lie-Hermitian manifolds form a large and distinctive class of locally homogeneous Hermitian manifolds. They are often used as testing ground for conjectures in Hermitian geometry.

Denote by
{\mathfrak{g}}
the Lie algebra of
G
. Left-invariant metrics on
G
correspond to metrics (namely, inner products) on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and left-invariant complex structures on
G
correspond to complex structures on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, defined as linear transformations
J:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying
J^{2}=-I
and the integrability condition

(1)

[x,y]-[Jx,Jy]+J[Jx,y]+J[x,Jy]=0,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

A Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
equipped with a complex structure
J
and a compatible metric
g=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is called a
Hermitian Lie algebra
, denoted by
({\mathfrak{g}},J,g)
. From now on, we will use the same letters to denote the metric (or complex structure) on
G
and
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

As a partial evidence, we will confirm Conjecture
2
for Lie-Hermitian manifolds in four special cases: complex nilmanifolds,
almost abelian Lie algebras
(namely, those containing an abelian ideal of codimension
1
), Lie algebras with
J
-invariant abelian ideal of codimension
2
, and solvable Lie algebras with complex commutators. The last case means that the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
G
is solvable and satisfies
J{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is the commutator.

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Lie-Hermitian manifold with universal cover
(G,J,g)
. Assume that the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
of
G
satisfies one of the following:

(i)

{\mathfrak{g}}
is nilpotent, or

(ii)

{\mathfrak{g}}
is solvable with
J{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
, where
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
, or

(iii)

\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular and almost abelian, or

(iv)

\mathfrak{g}
is unimodular and contains an abelian ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of codimension 2 with
J{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathfrak{a}}
.

If
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, then
c=0
. If
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
and
\beta\neq 0
, then
g
must be Chern flat.

In particular, Conjecture
2
holds for each of these four types of Lie-Hermitian manifolds. When
\beta=0
, the mixed curvature is the first Chern Ricci curvature, and in each of the above four cases the Chern Ricci curvature could vanish identically while not being Chern flat. So the assumption
\beta\neq 0
is necessary in the
c=0
case of Theorem
1
.

When
\alpha=0
, namely for the holomorphic sectional curvature case
\mathcal{C}_{0,1}=H
, Theorem
1
is known to be true: (i) is due to Li-Zheng
[
28
]
, (ii) is due to Huang-Zheng
[
27
]
, while (iii) and (iv) are proven by Li-Zheng in
[
29
]
, where they also discussed the constant holomorphic sectional curvature problem for the Levi-Civita connection.

Recall that the Bismut connection
\nabla^{b}
(also known as the Strominger connection in some literature) of a Hermitian manifold
(M^{n},g)
is the unique connection satisfying the conditions that
\nabla^{b}g=0
,
\nabla^{b}J=0
, and its torsion is totally skew-symmetric (
[
9
,
37
]
). The metric
g
is called Bismut torsion-parallel (BTP for brevity) if
\nabla^{b}T^{b}=0
, where
T^{b}
is the torsions of the Bismut connection. BTP manifolds form a relatively large and interesting class of special Hermitian manifolds. It contains all Bismut Kähler-like (abbreviated as BKL) manifolds, meaning when the curvature of
\nabla^{b}
obeys all Kähler symmetries. See
[
53
]
for a proof of the AOUV Conjecture
[
2
]
by Angella-Otal-Ugarte-Villacampa, which can be alternatively stated as BKL
\subset
BTP. Clearly, all Bismut flat manifolds (
[
47
]
) are BKL, thus are examples of BTP manifolds. Another example is that all Vaisman manifolds are BTP. Recall that a Vaisman manifold is a locally conformally Kähler manifold such that its Lee form is parallel under the Levi-Civita connection.

In complex dimension
2
, BTP = BKL = Vaisman. Such surfaces were fully classified by Belgun
[
6
]
in
2000
. However, for
n\geq 3
, BKL and Vaisman are disjoint, and their union is a proper subset of the set of all non-balanced BTP manifolds. Additionally, for
n\geq 3
, there exist balanced (non-Kähler) BTP manifolds. Balanced BTP manifolds form a highly restrictive and interesting set, containing examples that are Chern flat or Fano. For further discussions on BTP manifolds, we refer readers to the preprint
[
55
]
and the references therein. Next we examine Conjecture
2
for BTP manifolds. We start with the balanced ones. In this case we are only able to confirm the conjecture in complex dimension
3
:

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M^{3},g)
be a compact balanced BTP threefold which is not Kähler. If
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, then
c
must be zero. If
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
and
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
, then either
(M^{3},g)
is Chern flat, or
\beta=0
and
(M^{3},g)
is Chern Ricci flat of middle type.

Note that compact non-Kähler balanced BTP threefolds are either compact quotients of
SL(2,{\mathbb{C}})
) (the Chern flat case), or the Wallach threefold (the Fano case), or in the so-called
middle type
, which means the rank of the
B
tensor is
2
. The local geometry of the middle type ones are determined by two real-valued smooth functions
x
,
y
, and it is Chern Ricci flat (or equivalently Bismut Ricci flat) if and only if
x=y=0
identically.

For non-balanced BTP manifolds, we have the following:

Theorem 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact non-balanced BTP manifold with
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
. Then
c
must be zero.

In other words Conjecture
2
holds for all compact non-balanced BTP manifolds. Note that such a manifold could never be Chern flat, yet there are such examples where
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
identically for some special values of
(\alpha,\beta)
. As a wild speculation, we propose the following:

Conjecture 3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact non-balanced BTP manifold. If
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=0
identically, then either
\beta=0
, or
k\alpha+\beta=0
for some integer
k
between
2
and
n
.

We remark that it is necessary to exclude the above ‘exceptional values’ for
(\alpha,\beta)
. The
\beta=0
case corresponds to the (first) Chern Ricci curvature. Take any complex nilmanifold that is BTP (there are both balanced and non-balanced BTP nilmanifolds), it is always Chern Ricci flat, namely,
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,0}=0
identically. Also, if we consider the standard Hopf manifold

X^{k}=({\mathbb{C}}^{k}\setminus\{0\})/\langle f\rangle,\ \ \ f(z)=2z,\ \ \ \ \mbox{with metric}\ \ \ \omega=\sqrt{-1}\,\frac{\partial\overline{\partial}\,|z|^{2}}{|z|^{2}}.

Then it is easy to see that
\operatorname{Ric}=kH
, hence
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,-k}\equiv 0
on
X^{k}
. By taking
M^{n}=X^{k}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{n-k}
to be the product of
X^{k}
with a flat complex torus, we see that on the non-balanced BTP manifold
M^{n}
we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,-k}\equiv 0
. Here
k
could be any integer between
2
and
n
.

be the Lie algebra of
G
, then
J
corresponds to a complex structure (which for convenience we will still denote by
J
) on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Similarly, any left-invariant metric
g
on
G
compatible with
J
will correspond to an inner product
g=\langle,\rangle
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
\langle Jx,Jy\rangle=\langle x,y\rangle
for any
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Here again for convenience we use the same letter to denote the corresponding metric on the Lie algebra.

As in
[
26
]
, let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{C}}
be the complexification of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and write
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}=\{x-\sqrt{-1}Jx\mid x\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{C}}
. The integrability condition (
1
) means that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
is a complex Lie subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Extend
g=\langle,\rangle
bi-linearly over
{\mathbb{C}}
, and let
e=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
, which will be called a
frame
of
({\mathfrak{g}},J)
or
({\mathfrak{g}},J,g)
. Denote by
\varphi
the coframe dual to
e
, namely, a basis of the dual vector space
({\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0})^{\ast}
such that
\varphi_{i}(e_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
\forall

1\leq i,j\leq n
. We will write

(7)

C^{j}_{ik}=\varphi_{j}([e_{i},e_{k}]),\ \ \ \ \ \ D^{j}_{ik}=\overline{\varphi}_{i}([\overline{e}_{j},e_{k}])

for the structure constants, which is equivalent to

(8)

[e_{i},e_{j}]=\sum_{k}C^{k}_{ij}e_{k},\ \ \ \ \ [e_{i},\overline{e}_{j}]=\sum_{k}\big(\overline{D^{i}_{kj}}e_{k}-D^{j}_{ki}\overline{e}_{k}\big).

In dual terms, the above is also equivalent to the (first) structure equation:

(9)

d\varphi_{i}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k}C^{i}_{jk}\,\varphi_{j}\wedge\varphi_{k}-\sum_{j,k}\overline{D^{j}_{ik}}\,\varphi_{j}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{k},\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Differentiate the above, we get the first Bianchi identity, which is equivalent to the Jacobi identity in this case:

\left\{\begin{split}\sum_{r}\big(C^{r}_{ij}C^{\ell}_{rk}+C^{r}_{jk}C^{\ell}_{ri}+C^{r}_{ki}C^{\ell}_{rj}\big)\ =\ 0,\hskip 91.04872pt\\
\sum_{r}\big(C^{r}_{ik}D^{\ell}_{jr}+D^{r}_{ji}D^{\ell}_{rk}-D^{r}_{jk}D^{\ell}_{ri}\big)\ =\ 0,\hskip 85.35826pt\\
\sum_{r}\big(C^{r}_{ik}\overline{D^{r}_{j\ell}}-C^{j}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{r\ell}}+C^{j}_{ri}\overline{D^{k}_{r\ell}}-D^{\ell}_{ri}\overline{D^{k}_{jr}}+D^{\ell}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{jr}}\big)\ =\ 0,\end{split}\right.

for any
1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n
. When
G
has a compact quotient, it (or equivalently, its Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
) is necessarily unimodular, that is,
\mbox{tr}(ad_{x})=0
for any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. In terms of structure constants, we have

{\mathfrak{g}}\ \,\mbox{is unimodular}\ \ \Longleftrightarrow\ \ \sum_{r}\big(C^{r}_{ri}+D^{r}_{ri}\big)=0,\,\ \forall\ i.

Note that so far we did not assume
e
to be unitary. When
e
is unitary, namely, when
\langle e_{i},\overline{e}_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
, then the formula for Chern connection form, the Chern torsion components
T^{j}_{ik}
and Chern curvature components
R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}
under
e
are particularly simple (
[
26
,
28
]
):

(10)

\displaystyle\theta_{ij}\ =\ \sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(D^{j}_{ik}\varphi_{k}-\overline{D^{i}_{jk}}\overline{\varphi_{k}}\big),\ \ \ \ T^{j}_{ik}=-C^{j}_{ik}-D^{j}_{ik}+D^{j}_{ki},\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

(11)

\displaystyle R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\ =\ \sum_{s=1}^{n}\big(D^{s}_{ki}\overline{D^{s}_{\ell j}}-D^{\ell}_{si}\overline{D^{k}_{sj}}-D^{j}_{si}\overline{D^{k}_{\ell s}}-\overline{D^{i}_{sj}}D^{\ell}_{ks}\big),\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k,\ell\leq n.

In particular, we have

(12)

\displaystyle R_{i\overline{i}i\overline{i}}\ =\ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(|D^{r}_{ii}|^{2}-|D^{i}_{ri}|^{2}-2{\mathfrak{R}}\mbox{e}\{D^{i}_{ri}\overline{D^{i}_{ir}}\}\big),\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n,

(13)

\displaystyle R_{i\overline{i}s\overline{s}}\ =\ \sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(|D^{r}_{si}|^{2}-|D^{s}_{ri}|^{2}-2{\mathfrak{R}}\mbox{e}\{D^{i}_{ri}\overline{D^{s}_{sr}}\}\big),\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,s\leq n,

(14)

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{i\overline{i}k\overline{k}}\ =\ \frac{1}{4}\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(|D^{r}_{ki}+D^{r}_{ik}|^{2}-|D^{k}_{ri}|^{2}-|D^{i}_{rk}|^{2}-2{\mathfrak{R}}\mbox{e}\{D^{k}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{ri}}+D^{i}_{ri}\overline{D^{k}_{kr}}+

\displaystyle\hskip 39.83368pt+\,D^{k}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{ir}}+D^{i}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{kr}}+D^{k}_{ri}\overline{D^{k}_{ir}}\}\big),\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,k\leq n.

3.
Complex nilmanifolds

Let
G
be a nilpotent Lie group equipped with a left-invariant metric
g=\langle,\rangle
and a compatible left-invariant complex structure
J
. For nilpotent groups, a famous result of Salamon
[
36
, Theorem 1.3]
states the following:

Theorem 4

(Salamon)
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent Lie group of dimension
2n
equipped with a left invariant complex structure. Then there exists a coframe
\varphi=\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{n}\}
of left invariant
(1,0)
-forms on
G
such that

d\varphi_{1}=0,\ \ \ d\varphi_{i}={\mathcal{I}}\{\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{i-1}\},\ \ \ \forall\ 2\leq i\leq n,

where
{\mathcal{I}}
stands for the ideal in exterior algebra of the complexified cotangent bundle generated by those
(1,0)
-forms.

Clearly, one can choose the above coframe
\varphi
so that it is also unitary. In terms of the structure constants
C
and
D
given by (
7
), this means

(15)

C^{j}_{ik}=0\ \ \ \mbox{unless}\ \ j>i\ \mbox{or}\ j>k;\ \ \ \ \ D^{j}_{ik}=0\ \ \ \mbox{unless}\ \ i>j.

Especially, we always have

(16)

D^{n}_{ik}=0,\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,k\leq n.

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1
in the case of complex nilmanifolds.

Proof of Theorem
1
for nilmanifolds.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a complex nilmanifold, namely, a compact Hermitian manifold with universal cover
(G,J,g)
, where
G
is a nilpotent Lie group,
J
a left-invariant complex structure on
G
, and
g
a left-invariant metric on
G
compatible with
J
. Assume that
g
has constant mixed curvature
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
. Let
e
be the unitary basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
dual to the unitary Salamon coframe
\varphi
above. Since
D^{i}_{ir}=0
, so by (
12
) and (
13
), we have
\sum_{s=1}^{n}R_{i\bar{i}s\bar{s}}=0,
and

R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{r}_{ii}|^{2}-\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{i}_{ri}|^{2}

for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Thus, by (
3
), we have

\beta R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}=\beta\big(\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{r}_{ii}|^{2}-\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{i}_{ri}|^{2}\big)=c

for each
1\leq i\leq n
. In particular,

c=\beta R_{1\overline{1}1\overline{1}}=-\beta\sum_{r}|D^{1}_{r1}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{ and}\ \ \ \ \ c=\beta R_{n\overline{n}n\overline{n}}=\beta\sum_{r}|D^{r}_{nn}|^{2}.

Therefore we must have
c=0
,
D^{1}_{r1}=0
, and

(17)

\displaystyle D^{r}_{nn}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq r\leq n,\ \

(18)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{r}_{ii}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{i}_{ri}|^{2},\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq r\leq n.

For any
1\leq i<k\leq n
, form (
4
) and the assumption
\beta\neq 0
we get
\widehat{R}_{i\overline{i}k\overline{k}}=0
, so by (
14
) we have

(19)

\sum_{r=1}^{n}|D^{r}_{ki}+D^{r}_{ik}|^{2}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\big(|D^{k}_{ri}|^{2}+|D^{i}_{rk}|^{2}+2{\mathfrak{R}}\mbox{e}\{D^{k}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{ri}}+D^{i}_{rk}\overline{D^{i}_{kr}}\}\big),\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ i<k.

Now by exactly the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
E
in Li-Zheng
[
28
]
, we conclude that
D^{j}_{ik}=0
for all
i,j,k
. This means that
G
is a complex Lie group, thus is automatically Chern flat. This completes the proof of case (i) in Theorem
1
.
∎

4.
Solvable Lie algebras with complex commutators

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a solvable Lie algebra, equipped with a complex structure
J
and a metric
g=\langle,\rangle
compatible with
J
, such that the commutator
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=[{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{g}}]
is
J
-invariant. Since we already established Conjecture
2
for complex nilmanifolds, we may assume that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
not nilpotent
. As is well-known, the commutator of any solvable Lie algebra is always nilpotent, so we have
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}\neq{\mathfrak{g}}
here.

For convenience, in our proofs we will use a particular type of unitary frames that are most suitable for our situation. We choose
e
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
so that
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
is spanned by
\{e_{i}+\overline{e}_{i},\sqrt{-1}(e_{i}-\overline{e}_{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq r}
where
2r
is the (real) dimension of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. Throughout this section, we will always make the following convention on the range of indices:

1\leq i,j,\cdots\leq r,\ \ \ \ \ \ r\!+\!1\leq\gamma,\eta,\cdots\leq n,\ \ \ \ \ \ 1\leq a,b,\cdots\leq n.

From (
8
) and the definition of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
, we obtain

(20)

C^{\gamma}_{ab}=D^{a}_{\gamma b}=0,\ \ \ \ \forall\ r\!+\!1\leq\gamma\leq n,\ \ \forall\ 1\leq a,b\leq n.

Restricting the Hermitian structure to
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
, we have
{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}}
as a unitary frame for
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
is nilpotent, by (
15
) and by a unitary change of
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
if necessary, we may assume that

(21)

C^{j}_{ik}=0\ \ \mbox{unless}\ j>i\ \mbox{or}\ j>k;\ \ \ \ D^{j}_{ik}=0\ \ \mbox{unless}\ i>j,\ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k\leq r.

This will be a key property used repeatedly. The readers are referred to
[
28
]
for a more detailed discussion on this. We will call such a frame
e
an
admissible frame
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
from now on.

Lemma 4
.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},J,g)
be a solvable Lie algebra equipped with a Hermitian structure. Assume that
J{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
and the Chern connection of
g
has constant mixed curvature:
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
where
\beta\neq 0
. Then
c=0
and
D^{\gamma}_{j\eta}=D^{j}_{ik}=D^{\gamma}_{ij}=0
for any
1\leq i,j,k\leq r,\,r\!+\!1\leq\gamma,\eta\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
D^{*}_{\gamma*}=0
, by (
12
), (
13
) and (
21
) we have
\sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{\gamma\bar{\gamma}t\bar{t}}=0,

\sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{i\bar{i}t\bar{t}}=0
, and

R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ii}|^{2}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{i}_{si}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ R_{\gamma\bar{\gamma}\gamma\bar{\gamma}}=-\sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{\gamma}_{s\gamma}|^{2}

for any
1\leq i\leq r,\ r+1\leq\gamma\leq n.
Thus, by (
3
) we have

\beta R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}=\beta\big(\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ii}|^{2}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{i}_{si}|^{2}\big)=c

for each
1\leq i\leq r
. In particular,

c=\beta R_{r\overline{r}r\overline{r}}=\beta\sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{s}_{rr}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ c=\beta R_{\gamma\overline{\gamma}\gamma\overline{\gamma}}=-\beta\sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{\gamma}_{s\gamma}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \forall\ r+1\leq\gamma\leq n.

Therefore we deduce
c=0
,
D^{*}_{rr}=0
, and

(22)

\displaystyle D^{\gamma}_{i\gamma}\ =\ 0,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq r,\ r+1\leq\gamma\leq n,\ \

(23)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ii}|^{2}\ =\ \sum_{s=1}^{r}|D^{i}_{si}|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq r.

Note that if we make any unitary change on
\{e_{r\!+\!1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, then formula (
20
) and (
21
) would not be affected and
e
would remain to be an admissible frame. So formula (
22
) actually gives us
D^{X}_{jX}=0
for any
j
and any
X=\sum_{\gamma=r\!+\!1}^{n}X_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}
. That is,
\sum_{\gamma,\eta=r\!+\!1}^{n}\overline{X}_{\gamma}X_{\eta}D^{\gamma}_{j\eta}=0
. This leads to
D^{\gamma}_{j\eta}=0
for any
j
and any
\gamma
,
\eta
.

We have
\sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{i\bar{i}t\bar{t}}=0

\forall\ 1\leq i\leq r
. Fix any
1\leq i<k\leq r
. By (
4
) and
\beta\neq 0
we obtain
\widehat{R}_{i\overline{i}k\overline{k}}=0
. Hence by (
14
) we get

(24)

\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ki}+D^{s}_{ik}|^{2}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ki}+D^{s}_{ik}|^{2}-\sum_{s=1}^{r}\big(|D^{k}_{si}|^{2}+|D^{i}_{sk}|^{2}+2\mbox{Re}\{D^{i}_{si}\overline{D^{k}_{sk}}+\overline{D^{i}_{sk}}D^{i}_{ks}\}\big).

When deriving the above equality, we used the fact that
D^{\ast}_{\gamma\ast}=0
for any
\gamma>r
by (
20
). Let
k=r
in (
24
), and by (
21
) we get

(25)

\sum_{s=1}^{n}|D^{s}_{ri}+D^{s}_{ir}|^{2}\,=\,\sum_{s=1}^{r}\big(|D^{i}_{sr}|^{2}+2\mbox{Re}\{\overline{D^{i}_{sr}}D^{i}_{rs}\}\big),\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq r.

Now by the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
4
in Huang-Zheng
[
27
]
, we end up with

(26)

D^{\gamma}_{j\eta}=D^{j}_{ik}=D^{\gamma}_{ij}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j,k\leq r,\ \ \ \forall\ r\!+\!1\leq\gamma,\eta\leq n.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to finish the proof of Theorem
1
for the case of solvable Lie algebras with complex commutators.

Proof of Theorem
1
for solvable Lie algebras with complex commutators.

Let
({\mathfrak{g}},J,g)
be a solvable Lie algebra equipped with a Hermitian structure. Assume that
J{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
and the Chern connection of
g
has constant mixed curvature:
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, where
\beta\neq 0
. Then
c=0
by Lemma
4
. Our goal is to show that the Chern curvature of
g
must vanish identically:
R=0
. Let
e
be an admissible frame of
{\mathfrak{g}}
so (
20
) and (
21
) hold. By (
26
) the only possibly non-zero
D
components are
D^{j}_{i\gamma}
, where
1\leq i,j\leq r
and
r\!+\!1\leq\gamma\leq n
. In particular,
D^{\gamma}_{\ast\ast}=D^{\ast}_{\gamma\ast}=0
. So by (
11
) we know that

(27)

R_{\ast\bar{\ast}\gamma\bar{\ast}}=R_{\ast\bar{\ast}\ast\bar{\gamma}}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall\ r\!+\!1\leq\gamma\leq n.

Therefore
R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}=0
if at least three of the indices belong to
\{r\!+\!1,\ldots,n\}
. Also by (
11
) we have

R_{a\bar{b}c\bar{d}}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}D^{s}_{ca}\overline{D^{s}_{db}}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}D^{d}_{sa}\overline{D^{c}_{sb}},

since for the last two terms in the right hand side of (
11
) the index
s
cannot be simultaneously
>r
and
\leq r
. So we have

\sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{\gamma\bar{\eta}t\bar{t}}=\sum_{t,s=1}^{n}D^{s}_{t\gamma}\overline{D^{s}_{t\eta}}-\sum_{t,s=1}^{n}D^{t}_{s\gamma}\overline{D^{t}_{s\eta}}=0,\ \ \sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{i\bar{j}t\bar{t}}=\sum_{t,s=1}^{n}D^{s}_{ti}\overline{D^{s}_{tj}}-\sum_{t,s=1}^{n}D^{t}_{si}\overline{D^{t}_{sj}}=0,\ \

where
1\leq i,j\leq r
and
r\!+\!1\leq\gamma,\eta\leq n
. By (
2
), we have

4\beta\widehat{R}_{\gamma\bar{\eta}i\bar{j}}=4\beta\widehat{R}_{\gamma\bar{\eta}i\bar{j}}+\alpha\big(\sum_{t=1}^{n}R_{\gamma\bar{\eta}t\bar{t}}\delta_{i{j}}+R_{i\bar{j}t\bar{t}}\delta_{\gamma{\eta}}\big)=2c\big(\delta_{ij}\delta_{\gamma\eta}\big)=0

for any
1\leq i,j\leq r
,
r\!+\!1\leq\gamma,\eta\leq n
. If exactly two of the four indices belong to
\{r\!+\!1,\ldots,n\}
, then by (
27
), we get

R_{\gamma\bar{\eta}i\bar{j}}=4\widehat{R}_{\gamma\bar{\eta}i\bar{j}}=0.

If only one of the four indices belongs to
\{r\!+\!1,\ldots,n\}
, then it is

R_{\gamma\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}\big(D^{s}_{k\gamma}\overline{D^{s}_{\ell j}}-D^{\ell}_{s\gamma}\overline{D^{k}_{sj}}\big).

This equals to zero since for each of the two terms on the right-hand side, the second factor is zero by (
26
). Similarly, when all four indices are in
\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=0
by (
26
). Therefore, we have shown that
R=0
, completing the proof of Theorem
1
for solvable Lie algebras with complex commutators.
∎

5.
Almost abelian Lie algebras

In this section, we focus on Lie-Hermitian manifolds where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is an
almost abelian Lie algebra
, meaning that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is non-abelian but contains an abelian ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}
of codimension
1
. We will follow the notations of
[
25
]
.

Given an almost abelian Lie algebra
({\mathfrak{g}},J,g)
with an abelian ideal
{\mathfrak{a}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
of codimension 1, write
{\mathfrak{a}}_{J}:={\mathfrak{a}}\cap J{\mathfrak{a}}
. It is a
J
-invariant ideal in
{\mathfrak{g}}
of codimension 2. Consequently, we can always choose a unitary basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{1,0}
, called an
admissible frame
, so that
{\mathfrak{a}}
is spanned by

{\mathfrak{a}}=\text{span}\left\{\sqrt{-1}(e_{1}-\overline{e}_{1}),\,(e_{i}+\overline{e}_{i}),\,\sqrt{-1}(e_{i}-\overline{e}_{i});\,2\leq i\leq n\right\}.

Let
e
be an admissible frame of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Following
[
25
]
, since
{\mathfrak{a}}
is abelian we have

[e_{i},e_{j}]=[e_{i},\overline{e}_{j}]=[e_{1}-\overline{e}_{1},e_{i}]=0,\ \ \ \ \forall\ 2\leq i,j\leq n.

From this and equation (
8
) we obtain the following relations

C^{\ast}_{ij}=D^{j}_{\ast i}=D^{1}_{\ast i}=C^{\ast}_{1i}+\overline{D^{i}_{\ast 1}}=0,\ \ \ \ \forall\ 2\leq i,j\leq n.

Also, since
{\mathfrak{a}}
is an ideal,
[e_{1}+\overline{e}_{1},{\mathfrak{a}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{a}}
, which leads to
C^{1}_{1i}=0
and
D^{1}_{11}=\overline{D^{1}_{11}}
. Combining these results, we know that the only possibly non-trivial components of
C
and
D
are

(28)

D^{1}_{11}=\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \ \ D^{1}_{i1}=v_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}},\ \ \ D^{j}_{i1}=A_{ij},\ \ \ C^{j}_{1i}=-\overline{A_{ji}},\ \ \ \ \ \ 2\leq i,j\leq n.

Rewriting the structure equation (
9
) in terms of the coframe
\varphi
dual to

, we obtain

\begin{cases}d\varphi_{1}=-\lambda\,\varphi_{1}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{1},\\
d\varphi_{i}=-\overline{v}_{i}\,\varphi_{1}\wedge\overline{\varphi}_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\overline{A_{ij}}\,(\varphi_{1}+\overline{\varphi}_{1})\wedge\varphi_{j},\ \ \ 2\leq i\leq n.\end{cases}

For the proof of Theorem
1
, we will utilize the characterization result from Proposition
7
and Lemma
6
in
[
25
]
, which pertains to almost abelian Hermitian Lie algebras.

Lemma 5

(
[
25
]
)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be an almost abelian Lie algebra equipped with a Hermitian structure
(J,g)
, and let
e
be an admissible frame. Then the following hold:

(i)

{\mathfrak{g}}
is unimodular
\ \Longleftrightarrow\

\lambda+\mbox{tr}(A)+\overline{\mbox{tr}(A)}=0
;

(ii)

g
is Chern flat
\iff\lambda=0
,
v=0
,
[A,A^{*}]=0
.

Here
A^{*}
stands for the conjugate transpose of the matrix
A
.

It was calculated in
[
25
]
that under any admissible frame

, the only possibly non-zero components of the Chern torsion and curvature are

(29)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}T^{1}_{1i}=v_{i},\ \ \ \ \ \ T^{j}_{1i}=A_{ij}+\bar{A}_{ji},\\
R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=-2\lambda^{2}-|v|^{2},\ \ \quad(R_{1\bar{1}i\bar{1}})=-A^{*}v,\\
(R_{1\bar{1}i\bar{j}})=vv^{*}+[A,A^{*}]-\lambda(A+A^{*}),\end{cases}

where
2\leq i,j\leq n
.

Proof of Theorem
1
for unimodular almost abelian Lie algebras.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},J,g)
be a unimodular almost abelian Lie algebra with a Hermitian structure. Assume that the Chern connection of
g
has constant mixed curvature:
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, where
\beta\neq 0
. Let
e
be an admissible frame of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. By (
29
) we have

R_{i\bar{i}*\bar{*}}=R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}=0
for any
2\leq i\leq n
. So when
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
, by (
3
) for any

, we get

.

By (
13
), (
28
) and (
29
), we have
R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=-2\lambda^{2}-|v|^{2},
and

(30)

R_{1\bar{1}k\bar{k}}=|v_{k}|^{2}-\lambda(A_{kk}+\overline{A_{kk}})+\sum_{r=2}^{n}(|A_{kr}|^{2}-|A_{rk}|^{2})),\ \ \ \ \forall\ 2\leq k\leq n.

p
, we compute

\displaystyle R_{X\overline{X}X\overline{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k,\ell}X_{i}\overline{X}_{j}X_{k}\overline{X}_{\ell}R_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}\,=\,\sum_{i}|X_{i}|^{4}R_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}+\sum_{i\neq k}|X_{i}X_{k}|^{2}\big(R_{i\bar{i}k\bar{k}}+R_{i\bar{k}k\bar{i}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}|X_{i}|^{4}+\sum_{i<k}|X_{i}X_{k}|^{2}\big(R_{i\bar{i}k\bar{k}}+R_{k\bar{k}i\bar{i}}+R_{i\bar{k}k\bar{i}}+R_{k\bar{i}i\bar{k}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i}|X_{i}|^{4}+3|X_{1}X_{2}|^{2}+3|X_{2}X_{3}|^{2}-2|X_{1}X_{3}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(t^{2}-2s+|X_{2}|^{4})+3t|X_{2}|^{2}-2s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|X_{2}|^{4}+3t|X_{2}|^{2}+(2t^{2}-6s).

Here for convenience we have denoted by
t=|X_{1}|^{2}+|X_{3}|^{2}
and
s=|X_{1}X_{3}|^{2}
. Similarly,

\displaystyle|X|^{2}R_{X\overline{X}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|X|^{2}\sum_{i}|X_{i}|^{2}R_{i\bar{i}}\ =\ |X|^{2}\big(2|X_{1}|^{2}+4|X_{2}|^{2}+2|X_{3}|^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(t+|X_{2}|^{2})\,(2t+4|X_{2}|^{2})\ =\ 4|X_{2}|^{4}+6t|X_{2}|^{2}+2t^{2}.

By the definition of the mixed curvature
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}
, at
p
we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}(X)-c|X|^{4}\ =\ \alpha|X|^{2}R_{X\overline{X}}+\beta R_{X\overline{X}X\overline{X}}-c|X|^{4}\ =\

\displaystyle=\ \{2(2\alpha+\beta)-c\}|X_{2}|^{4}+\{3t(2\alpha+\beta)-2ct\}|X_{2}|^{2}+\{2(\alpha+\beta)t^{2}-6\beta s-ct^{2}\}.

Therefore, if
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
is a constant, then since
|X_{2}|^{2}\geq 0
is arbitrary, the three coefficients must be identically zero:

2(2\alpha+\beta)-c=0,\ \ \ \ \ t\{3(2\alpha+\beta)-2c\}=0,\ \ \ \ \ t^{2}\{2(\alpha+\beta)-c\}-6\beta s=0.

Let
t
take a positive value, then the first two equalities imply that
c=0
and
2\alpha+\beta=0
, so the third equality becomes

\beta(t^{2}-6s)=0.

Take
X_{1}=1
and
X_{3}=0
for instance, then
t=1
and
s=0
, so the above gives us
\beta=0
, hence
\alpha=0
. This computation shows the following:

For the Wallach threefold
(M^{3},g)
, the mixed curvature
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}
can never be a constant for any
(\alpha,\beta)\neq(0,0)
.

Case 3:
When the rank of the
B
tensor is
r=2
. In this case
(M^{3},g)
is of the middle type, where the Bismut curvature under a special frame is given by (
52
). We have

R^{b}_{\ast\bar{\ast}3\bar{3}}=0,\ \ \ R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=R^{b}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}=R^{b}_{2\bar{2}1\bar{1}}=R^{b}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=x.

Note that on any balanced Hermitian manifold, the first Chern Ricci tensor and the first Bismut Ricci tensor coincide, namely,
R_{i\bar{j}}=R^{b}_{i\bar{j}}
. In our case we have
\mbox{tr}(\Theta^{b})=2\,d\alpha=2\Theta^{b}_{11}
, so

R_{i\bar{j}}=R^{b}_{i\bar{j}}=2R^{b}_{i\bar{j}1\bar{1}}.

By (
54
) and (
55
) we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\beta R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}+2\alpha R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=c,&\\
\\
\beta R^{b}_{3\bar{3}3\bar{3}}+2\alpha R^{b}_{3\bar{3}1\bar{1}}=c,&\\
\\
4\beta R^{b}_{2\bar{2}1\bar{1}}+2\alpha\big(R^{b}_{2\bar{2}1\bar{1}}+R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}\big)=2c+2\beta a_{1}^{2},&\\
\end{array}\right.

The middle line says that
c=0
, while the other two give us

(2\alpha+\beta)x=0,\ \ \ \ \ (2\beta+2\alpha)x=\beta a_{1}^{2}.

When
\beta\neq 0
, the second equation above tells us that
x\neq 0
, thus by the first equation we conclude that
2\alpha+\beta=0
. Thus we have shown that

For any balanced BTP threefold of middle type, the mixed curvature
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}
can never be a non-zero constant. It also cannot be identically zero when
\beta\neq 0
and
2\alpha+\beta\neq 0
.

Note that for
\beta=0
, the mixed curvature is just the first Chern Ricci, given by

2d\alpha=2x(\varphi_{1\bar{1}}+\varphi_{2\bar{2}})+2iy(\varphi_{2\bar{1}}-\varphi_{1\bar{2}}).

Hence
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,0}=0
identically if and only if
x=y=0
everywhere on
M^{3}
. In this case
d\alpha=0
and
d\beta_{0}=2\varphi_{1\bar{2}}-2\varphi_{2\bar{1}}
. This case can occur.

Next let us assume that
\beta\neq 0
but
2\alpha+\beta=0
. Without loss of generality let us assume that
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-2
. We claim that the mixed curvature
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,-2}
cannot be identically zero. Assume otherwise, namely,
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,-2}\equiv 0
. Then from the equation
(2\beta+2\alpha)x=\beta a_{1}^{2}
above we conclude that
x=a_{1}^{2}>0
.

On the other hand, from (
52
) we get
R^{b}_{2\bar{1}1\bar{2}}=iy
where
y
is a local smooth function which takes real values. Take
i=\ell=2
and
j=k=1
in (
53
), we get

4\beta(iy)+\alpha(R_{1\bar{1}}+R_{2\bar{2}})=\beta\sum_{s}(-3|T^{2}_{1s}|^{2}-3|T^{1}_{2s}|^{2}).

Since
R_{1\bar{1}}=R^{b}_{1\bar{1}}=2R^{b}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=2x
and similarly
R_{2\bar{2}}=2x
, by plugging
\alpha=1
and
\beta=-2
in the above equation we obtain

-8iy+4x=12a_{1}^{2}.

Taking the real part, we get
x=3a_{1}^{2}
. Comparing this with the equality
x=a_{1}^{2}
that we obtained before, we get
a_{1}^{2}=0
, which is a contradiction. This shows that the assumption
{\mathcal{C}}_{1,-2}\equiv 0
cannot hold for any balanced BTP threefold of middle type, and we have completed the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

Finally let us consider the class of non-balanced BTP manifolds. Such manifolds were studied in
[
56
]
. Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, non-balanced BTP manifold. Given any point
p\in M
, there always exists the so-called
admissible frame
near
p
, which is a local unitary frame
e
satisfying

\eta=\lambda\varphi_{n},\ \ \ T^{n}_{ij}=0,\ \ \ T^{j}_{in}=\delta_{ij}a_{i},\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i,j\leq n,

where
\eta
is Gauduchon’s torsion
1
-form defined by
\partial(\omega^{n-1})=-\eta\wedge\omega^{n-1}
with
\omega
the Kähler form of
g
,
\varphi
is the coframe dual to
e
, and
T^{j}_{ik}
are the Chern torsion components under
e
. Here
\lambda
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are globally defined constants on
M^{n}
, satisfying

\lambda>0,\ \ \ a_{n}=0,\ \ \ a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}=\lambda.

The readers are referred to Definition 1.6 and Proposition 1.7 of
[
56
]
for more details. As a result, the direction
e_{n}
is fixed by the Bismut connection, namely,
\nabla^{b}e_{n}=0
, which implies that
R^{b}_{\ast\bar{\ast}\ast\bar{n}}=0
. Now let us prove Theorem
3
, which states that any compact, non-balanced BTP manifold cannot have non-zero constant mixed curvature.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a compact, non-balanced BTP manifold. Assume that the mixed curvature is equal to a non-zero constant:
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}=c
,
c\neq 0
. We want to derive at a contradiction. Fix any
p\in M
, and let
e
be an admissible frame in a neighborhood of
p
. From our previous discussion, we have
R^{b}_{\ast\bar{\ast}\ast\bar{n}}=0
. By letting
i=j=k=\ell
in the formula (
53
) at the beginning of this section, we have

4\beta R^{b}_{i\bar{i}i\bar{i}}+4\alpha R_{i\bar{i}}=-4\beta\sum_{s=1}^{n}|T^{i}_{is}|^{2}+4c,\ \ \ \ \ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq n.

Apply it to the case
i=n
, and using the property
T^{n}_{\ast\ast}=0
, we get
\alpha R_{n\bar{n}}=c\neq 0
.

On the other hand, by taking
i=j=n
and
k=\ell
in the first equality in Lemma
2
, we get

(56)

R^{b}_{n\bar{n}k\bar{k}}-R_{n\bar{n}k\bar{k}}=\sum_{s=1}^{n}|T^{k}_{ns}|^{2}-\sum_{s=1}^{n}|T^{s}_{nk}|^{2}.

Here we used the fact
\nabla^{b}T=0
and
T^{n}_{\ast\ast}=0
for BTP metrics under admissible frames. Since
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{\ell}}=R^{b}_{k\bar{\ell}i\bar{j}}
and
R^{b}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{n}}=0
, we get
R^{b}_{n\bar{n}k\bar{k}}=0
. Summing up
k
from
1
to
n
in (
56
), the two terms on the right hand side cancel each other, and we end up with
R_{n\bar{n}}=0
. This of course contradicts to the conclusion
\alpha R_{n\bar{n}}=c\neq 0
that we obtained before. It shows that for any compact, non-balanced BTP manifold, the mixed curvature
{\mathcal{C}}_{\alpha,\beta}
could never be identically equal to a non-zero constant, and Theorem
3
is proved.
∎

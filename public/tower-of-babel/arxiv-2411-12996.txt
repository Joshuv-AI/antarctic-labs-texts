Asymptotics in Wasserstein Distance for Empirical Measures of Markov Processes
Feng-Yu Wang

Abstract
In this paper we introduce some recent progresses on the convergence rate in Wasserstein distance for empirical measures of Markov processes. For diffusion processes on compact manifolds possibly with reflecting or killing boundary conditions, the sharp convergence rate as well as renormalization limits are presented in terms of the dimension of the manifold and the spectrum of the generator. For general ergodic Markov processes, explicit estimates are presented for the convergence rate by using a nice reference diffusion process, which are illustrated by some typical examples. Finally, some techniques are introduced to estimate the Wasserstein distance between empirical measures.

1
Introduction

The empirical measure is a fundamental statistic to estimate the stationary distribution of a Markov process. In this paper, we study the long time behavior of empirical measures for Markov processes under
the Wasserstein distance. For two nonnegative functions
f,g
on a space
E
, we write
f\lesssim g
if there exists a constant
c>0
such that
f\leq cg
holds on
E
, and write
f\asymp g
if
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a Polish space, let
\mathscr{P}
be the space of all probability measures on
M
.
For a Markov process
X_{t}
on
M
, the empirical measure is defined as

\mu_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{X_{s}}\text{\rm{d}}s

for
t>0
, where
\delta_{X_{s}}
is the Dirac measure at
X_{s}
.
We intend to study the long time behavior of
\mu_{t}
under the
p
-Wasserstein distance for any
p\in[1,\infty)
:

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\inf_{\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})}\bigg(\int_{M\times M}\rho^{p}\text{\rm{d}}\pi\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},\ \ \mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathscr{P},

where
\mathscr{C}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
is the set of all couplings of
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
.

To this end, we will consider the ergodicity and quasi-ergodicity cases respectively.

1.1
Ergodicity case

For any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
, let
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}
denote the expectation for the Markov process
X_{t}
with initial distribution
\nu
, and denote by
P_{t}^{*}\nu
the law of
X_{t}
with initial distribution
\nu
. We have

\int_{M}f\text{\rm{d}}(P_{t}^{*}\nu)=\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[f(X_{t})]=\int_{M}P_{t}f(x)\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x),\ \ \ f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M)

where
P_{t}f(x):=\mathbb{E}^{x}[f(X_{t})]
, and
\mathscr{B}_{b}(M)
the class of all bounded measurable functions on
M
.

If
\mu\in\mathscr{P}
satisfies
P_{t}^{*}\mu=\mu
for all
t\geq 0
, we call
\mu
an invariant probability measure of the Markov process. If furthermore

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}P_{t}^{*}\nu=\mu\ \text{weakly}

holds for any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
, we call the Markov process ergodic.

In particular, when the Markov process is exponentially ergodic in
L^{2}(\mu)
, i.e.

\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq c\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t},\ \ t\geq 0

holds for some constant
c,\lambda>0
, where
\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
is the operator norm in
L^{2}(\mu)
and

\mu(f):=\int_{E}f\text{\rm{d}}\mu
for
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
, we have

{\bf V}_{f}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\hat{f}P_{t}\hat{f})\text{\rm{d}}t\in(0,\infty),\ \ 0\neq\hat{f}:=f-\mu(f),\ f\in L^{2}(\mu).

Moreover, according to
[
39
]
, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mu_{t}(f)=\mu(f),

and

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt{t}\big(\mu_{t}(f)-\mu(f)\big)=N(0,2{\bf V}_{f})\ \ \text{in\ law},

where
N(0,2{\bf V}_{f})
is the normal distribution with mean
0
and variance
2{\bf V}_{f}.

Noting that for any
p\geq 1

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)\geq\mathbb{W}_{1}(\mu_{t},\mu)=\sup_{\|f\|_{Lip}\leq 1}|\mu_{t}(f)-\mu(f)|,

where
\|\cdot\|_{Lip}
is the Lipschitz constant,
the above result implies that the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)]
could not faster than
t^{-\frac{1}{2}}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

We will show that for elliptic diffusions on compact manifolds, this best possible rate is reached if and only if the dimension of manifold is not larger than
3
, and in this case we also derive the exact limit for

t\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, which is given explicitly by the spectrum of the generator.

1.2
Quasi-ergodicity case

Consider Markov process
X_{t}
with finite life time
\tau
. Typical examples are the diffusion processes on a domain with killing boundary, for which the life time is the first hitting time to the boundary.
In this case, we study the long time behavior of the empirical measure
\mu_{t}
under the condition
\{t<\tau\}
, i.e. the process is not yet killed at time
t
.

A probability measure
\mu
is called quasi-invariant for
X_{t}
, if

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}(f(X_{t})|t<\tau)=\mu(f),\ \ t\geq 0,f\in\mathscr{B}_{b}(M),

i.e. with initial distribution
\mu
the conditional distribution of
X_{t}
under
\{\tau>t\}
remains to be
\mu
for any
t>0
.
Moreover, we call the Markov process
X_{t}
quasi-ergodic with quasi-invariant probability measure
\mu
, if

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(f(X_{t})|\tau>t)=\mu(f),\ \ \nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0},

where
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
means that
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
with
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[1_{\{\tau>t\}}]>0
for all
t>0
.
In this case, we study the long time behaviors of

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)|\tau>t)
and
\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(\mu_{t}|\tau>t),\mu)
for
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
.

In the remainder of the paper, we first consider killed diffusion processes on compact manifolds with boundary in Section 2, then study the (reflecting) diffusion processes on compact manifolds in Section 3,
and moreover investigate general ergodic Markov processes in Sections 4 and 5 where some typical examples are presented. The corresponding study on weighted empirical measures, time-changed Markov processes, fractional Brownian motions and non-linear
Markov processes can be found in
[
11
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
35
,
38
,
40
]
.
Finally, we summarize some general results for upper and lower bound estimates on Wasserstein distance.

2
Killed Diffusions on Manifolds

In this part, we consider the killed diffusion process on a Riemannian manifold with boundary.

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional compact connected Riemannian manifold with boundary
\partial M
, let
\text{\rm{d}}x
denote the volume measure, and let
N
be the unit normal vector field of
\partial M
. We call
\partial M
convex if its second fundamental form is nonnegative,
i.e.

\mathbb{I}(U,U):=-\langle\nabla_{U}N,U\rangle\geq 0,\ \ U\in T\partial M.

Let
V\in C^{1}(M)
such that

\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x

is a probability measure.
We consider the diffusion process
X_{t}
generated by

L:=\Delta+\nabla V

which is killed on the boundary
\partial M
, where
\Delta
and
\nabla
stand for the Laplacian and gradient operators respectively.

It is classical that under the Dirichlet boundary condition, the operator
L
is a self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mu)
with discrete spectrum:

L\phi_{i}=-\lambda_{i}\phi_{i},\ \ \phi_{i}|_{\partial M}=0,

where
\lambda_{i}\asymp i^{\frac{2}{d}}
for
i\geq 1
and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mu),
see for instance
[
8
]
.

Since
X_{t}
is the diffusion process on
M
generated by
L
with killing boundary condition, its life time is

\tau:=\inf\big\{t\geq 0:\ X_{t}\in\partial M\big\},

and

\mathscr{P}_{0}=\big\{\nu\in\mathscr{P}:\ \nu(\partial M)<1\big\}.

Moreover, the diffusion process is quasi-ergodic with unique quasi-invariant probability measure

\mu_{0}(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\phi_{0}^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\mu.

The following result shows that in most cases we have
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(\mu_{t}|\tau>t),\mu_{0})\asymp t^{-1}
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
29
]
)
.

Let
\mu_{0}=\phi_{0}^{2}\mu
. Then for any
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0},

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\big\{t^{2}\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mu_{t}|\tau>t],\mu_{0})^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\{\mu(\phi_{0})\nu(\phi_{0})\}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\{\nu(\phi_{0})\mu(\phi_{i})+\mu(\phi_{0})\nu(\phi_{i})\}^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0})^{3}}>0,

and the limit is finite if and only if

\nu\in\mathscr{D}\big((-\hat{L})^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big),
i.e.

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\nu(\phi_{i})^{2}\lambda_{i}^{-3}<\infty.

By the Sobolev embedding theorem, we have
\mathscr{P}\subset\mathscr{D}((-\hat{L})^{-\frac{3}{2}})
for
d\leq 6
, and
\mathscr{D}((-\hat{L})^{-\frac{3}{2}})\supset L^{\frac{2d}{d+6}}(\mu)
for
d>6
. Hence, by Theorem
2.1
,
\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mu_{t}|\tau>t],\mu_{0})\asymp t^{-1}
holds if either
d\leq 6
or
d\geq 7
and

\frac{\text{\rm{d}}\nu}{\text{\rm{d}}\mu}\in L^{\frac{2d}{d+6}}(\mu).

When
d\geq 7
but
\nu\notin\mathscr{D}((-\hat{L})^{-\frac{3}{2}})
, the limit in the above theorem becomes
\infty
, so that the convergence of
W_{2}(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mu_{t}|\tau>t],\mu_{0})
is slower than
t^{-1}
, for which the exact convergence rate remains open.

Next, we consider the long time behavior of
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}(\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})|\tau>t).

Theorem 2.2

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. The the following assertions hold.

(1)

When
\partial M
is convex, we have

\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big|t<\tau\big]\Big\}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0})^{2}}.

(2)

If
\partial M
is non-convex, then there exists a constant
c\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0})^{2}}\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big|t<\tau\big]\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big|t<\tau\big]\Big\}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0})^{2}}.

Since
\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0}\asymp i^{\frac{2}{d}}
, it is easy to see that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{0})^{2}}<\infty

if and only if
d\leq 3
.
So, Theorem
2.2
shows that
\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big|t<\tau\big]\asymp t^{-1}
for
d\leq 3
, but the convergence is slower than than
t^{-1}
for
d\geq 4
.
The next result gives the exact convergence rate for
d\geq 4
where
d=4
is critical, but for completeness we also include the case for
d\leq 3
.
The lower bound estimate for
d=4
is due to
[
33
]
where more general Markov processes are considered, other estimates are taken from
[
32
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
32
,
33
]
)
.

Let
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Then for larger
t>1

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big|t<\tau\big]\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-1},\ &\text{if}\ d\leq 3,\\
t^{-1}\log t,\ &\text{if}\ d=4,\\
t^{-\frac{2}{d-2}},\ &\text{if}\ d\geq 5.\end{cases}

3
(Reflected) Diffusions on Manifolds

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional connected compact Riemannian manifold without boundary or with a boundary
\partial M
.
In this part, we consider the (reflected, if
\partial M
exists) diffusion process
X_{t}
generated by

L:=\Delta+\nabla V+Z,

where
V\in C^{1}(M)
such that

\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x
is a probability measure, and
Z
is a
C^{1}
-vector field with

{\rm div}_{\mu}(Z):=ZV+{\rm div}Z=0,

where
ZV:=\langle Z,\nabla V\rangle.

Then
X_{t}
is ergodic with unique invariant probability measure
\mu
. We will study the convergence rate of

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)
when
t\rightarrow\infty
.

Corresponding to the killed case where the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem is involved, in the present case we will use the Neumann eigenvalue problem if
\partial M
exists, and the closed eigenvalue problem otherwise.

Let
N
be the inward unit normal vector field on
\partial M
if the boundary exists.
Consider the Neumann/closed eigenvalue problem:

L\phi_{i}=-\lambda_{i}\phi_{i},\ \ \mu(\phi_{i}^{2})=1,\ \ \ N\phi_{i}|_{\partial M}=0,

where
\phi_{0}\equiv 1,\lambda_{0}=0
, and the Neumann condition
N\phi_{i}|_{\partial M}=0
applies only when
\partial M
exists.
It is well known that
\lambda_{i}\asymp i^{\frac{2}{d}}
for
i\geq 0
and
\{\phi_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mu),
see for instance
[
8
]
.

3.1
Long time behavior of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]

Recall that for any
0\neq f\in L^{2}(\mu)
with
\mu(f)=0
,

{\bf V}_{f}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(fP_{t}f)\text{\rm{d}}t\in(0,\infty),

where
P_{t}
is the diffusion semigroup of
X_{t}
. By
{\rm div}_{\mu}(Z)=0,

Z\phi_{i}:=\langle Z,\nabla\phi_{i}\rangle
satisfies
\mu(Z\phi_{i})=0.

The following result is due to
[
37
]
for
Z=0
and
[
33
]
otherwise.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
33
,
37
]
)
.

Let
X_{t}
be the (reflected if
\partial M
exists) diffusion generated by
L=\Delta+\nabla V+Z
on
M
.

(1)

When
\partial M
is either empty or convex,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}{\bf V}_{Z\phi_{i}}\Big)

holds uniformly in
\nu\in\mathscr{P}.

(2)

If
\partial M
is non-convex, then there exists a constant
c\in(0,1]
such that

\displaystyle c\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}{\bf V}(Z\phi_{i})\Big)\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}t\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}{\bf V}_{Z\phi_{i}}\Big).

Theorem
3.1
shows that a divergence-free perturbation
Z
accelerates the convergence of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]
with the exact factor
-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}{\bf V}_{Z\phi_{i}}.

This fits well to the observation in
[
12
]
that divergence-free perturbations accelerate the convergence in the algorithm of Gibbs measure.

On the other hand, since
\lambda_{i}\asymp i^{\frac{2}{d}}
, Theorem
3.1
shows that
E^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\asymp t^{-1}
if and only if
d\leq 3
.
Correspondingly to Theorem
2.3
, the following result also present exact convergence rates for
d\geq 4
, where the lower bound estimate for
d=4
is due to
[
33
]
and other estimates are taken from
[
37
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
33
,
37
]
)
.

The following holds for large
t>0
uniformly in
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
:

\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu_{0})^{2}\big]\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-1},\ &\text{if}\ d\leq 3,\\
t^{-1}\log(t+1),\ &\text{if}\ d=4,\\
t^{-\frac{2}{d-2}},\ &\text{if}\ d\geq 5.\end{cases}

Indeed, for
d=4
we have the following renormalization formula.

Theorem 3.3

(
[
23
]
)
.

When
d=4
and
\partial M
is empty, there holds

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\bigg|\frac{t}{\log t}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)]-\frac{{\rm vol}(M)}{8\pi^{2}}\bigg|=0,

where
{\rm vol}(M)
is the volume of
M
.

When
d\geq 5
, it is not clear whether the the following limit exists or not:

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t^{\frac{2}{d-2}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{2}^{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)].

3.2
Long time behavior of
t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}
for
d\leq 3

We first consider the weak convergence of
t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}
.

Theorem 3.4

(
[
37
]
)
.

If
d\leq 3,Z=0
, and
\partial M
is either empty or convex, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\xi_{i}^{2}}{\lambda_{i}^{2}}\ \text{ in\ law},

where
\{\xi_{i}\}
are i.i.d. standard normal random variables.

Next, we consider the convergence of
t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}
in
L^{q}(\mathbb{P})
for any
q\geq 1
to the following specific process
\Xi(t)
:

\Xi(t):=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\psi_{i}(t)}{\lambda_{i}},

where

\psi_{i}(t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\int_{0}^{t}\phi_{i}(X_{s})\text{\rm{d}}s.

Recall that by the central limit theorem of
[
39
]
,

\psi_{i}(t)\rightarrow N(0,2{\bf V}_{\phi_{i}})
weakly with

{\bf V}_{\phi_{i}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu(\phi_{i}P_{t}\phi_{i})\text{\rm{d}}t=\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}}{\bf V}_{Z\phi_{i}}\Big).

We will consider initial distributions in the classes

\mathscr{P}_{k,R}:=\big\{\nu=\rho_{\nu}\mu\in\mathscr{P}(M):\ \|\rho_{\nu}\|_{L^{k}(\mu)}\leq R\big\},\ \ k,R\geq 1.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
\partial M
be empty or convex. If
d\leq 2
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\big|t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}-\Xi(t)\big|^{q}\big]=0,\ \ q\geq 1.

If
d=3
, then

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k,R}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}\big[\big|t\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}-\Xi(t)\big|^{q}\big]=0,\ \ k,R,q\geq 1.

3.3
Moment estimates on
\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)

The following result shows that the exact convergence rate of
\big(\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{q}]\big)^{\frac{2}{q}}
is uniformly in
p\geq 1
and
q>0
.

Theorem 3.6

(
[
36
]
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
q\in(0,\infty)
.

(1)

If
p\leq\frac{2d}{(d-2)^{+}}\lor\frac{d(d-2)}{2},
where
\frac{2d}{(d-2)^{+}}=\infty
when
d\leq 2
, then the following asymptotic formula for large
t>1
holds uniformly in
\nu\in\mathscr{P}
:

\big(\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{q}]\big)^{\frac{2}{q}}\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\textrm{if }d\leq 3,\\
t^{-1}\log t,&\textrm{if }d=4,\\
t^{-\frac{2}{d-2}},&\textrm{if }d\geq 5.\end{cases}

(2)

If
p>\frac{2d}{(d-2)^{+}}\lor\frac{d(d-2)}{2},
then for any
k,R\geq 1
the above asymptotic formula for large
t>1
holds uniformly in
\nu\in\mathscr{P}_{k,R}.

4
Exponential Ergodic Markov processes

In this part, we consider a general framework of exponential ergodic Markov process, which will be extended in Section 5 to non-exponentially ergodic setting.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a length space, which is the basic space for analysis on the Wasserstein space (see
[
3
,
24
]
),
i.e. for any
x,y\in M
, the distance
\rho(x,y)
can be approximated by the length of curves linking
x
and
y
.
A typical class of length space is the geodesic space, where the distance
\rho(x,y)
can be reached by the length of a geodesic curve linking
x
and
y
.

Let
X_{t}
be an ergodic Markov process with unique invariant probability measure
\mu
. We estimate the upper bound of

\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]
. Since the empirical measure is usually singular with
\mu
, to apply analysis techniques we need to regularize
\mu_{t}
using the following introduced diffusion process on
M
.

Let
\hat{X}_{t}
be a reversible diffusion process on
M
with the same invariant probability measure
\mu,
and with
\rho
as the intrinsic distance. Heuristically,

\hat{X}_{t}
has symmetric Dirichlet form
(\hat{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}))
in
L^{2}(\mu)
satisfying

\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)=\int_{M}|\nabla f|^{2}\text{\rm{d}}\mu,\ \ f\in C_{b,L}(M)\subset\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}}),

where
C_{b,L}(M)
is the set of all bounded Lipschitz continuous functions on
M
, and

|\nabla f(x)|:=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{\rho(x,y)},\ \ x\in M.

More precisely, we assume that
C_{b,L}(M)
is a dense subset of
\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
under the
\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{1}
-norm

\|f\|_{\hat{\mathscr{E}}_{1}}:=\sqrt{\mu(f^{2})+\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)},

and the Dirichlet form restricted on
C_{b,L}(M)
is formulated as

\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,g)=\int_{M}\Gamma(f,g)\text{\rm{d}}\mu,\ \ f,g\in C_{b,L}(M),

where

\Gamma:C_{b,L}(M)\times C_{b,L}(M)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}_{b}(M)

is a symmetric local square field (champ de carré), i.e. for
any
f,g,h\in C_{b,L}(M)
and
\phi\in C_{b}^{1}(\mathbb{R}),
we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{\Gamma(f,f)(x)}=|\nabla f(x)|:=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(y)-f(x)|}{\rho(x,y)},\ \ x\in M,

\displaystyle\Gamma(fg,h)=f\Gamma(g,h)+g\Gamma(f,h),\ \ \ \Gamma(\phi(f),h)=\phi^{\prime}(f)\Gamma(f,h).

Moreover, the generator
(\hat{L},\mathscr{D}(\hat{L}))
satisfies the chain rule

\hat{L}\phi(f)=\phi^{\prime}(f)\hat{L}f+\phi^{\prime\prime}(f)|\nabla f|^{2},\ \ \ f\in\mathscr{D}(\hat{L})\cap C_{b,L}(M),\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

We make the following assumption.

(A)

The reference diffusion semigroup
\hat{P}_{t}
has heat kernel
\hat{p}_{t}
with respect to
\mu
, and there exist constants
\beta,\lambda,d,k\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\hat{P}_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(\mu)}\leq k\text{\rm{e}}^{-\lambda t}t^{-\beta},\ t>0,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big(\hat{P}_{t}\rho(x,\cdot)^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}(x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\leq kt,\ t\in[0,1],

(4.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\hat{p}_{t}(x,x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\leq kt^{-\frac{d}{2}},\ t\in(0,1].

Moreover, there exist constants
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in(0,\infty)
such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\theta_{1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\theta_{2}t},\ t\geq 0.

Note that conditions (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) can be verified by choosing a suitable symmetric reference distance
\rho
. Indeed, for small
\rho
the Dirichlet form
\hat{\mathscr{E}}
is larger, so that

\hat{P}_{t}
has better properties.
When
p=2,
(
4.1
) holds for
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
if and only if

\hat{L}
has a spectral gap, i.e.

{\rm gap}(\hat{L}):=\inf\big\{\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f):\mu(f^{2})-\mu(f)^{2}=1\big\}>0.

The only condition on
X_{t}
is the exponential ergodicity (
4.4
), which will be weakened later on by allowing more general ergodic rate.

Let
K:=\beta+\frac{d}{4}.

The convergence rate of
\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]
will be given by

\xi_{K}(t):=\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\text{if}\ K<1,\\
t^{-1}[\log t]^{2},&\text{if}\ K=1,\\
t^{-\frac{1}{2K-1}},&\text{if}\ K>1.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

Assume
(A)
and let
K:=\beta+\frac{d}{4}.
Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
t>0,

(4.5)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq c\xi_{K}(t),\ \ \ t>0.

If
P_{t}
has heat kernel
p_{t}
with respect to
\mu
, then for any
q\in[1,2],t>1
and
x\in M,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{q}]\leq\

\displaystyle\frac{2^{q-1}}{t^{q}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[\mu(\rho(X_{s},\cdot)^{p})^{\frac{q}{p}}\big]\text{\rm{d}}s+2^{q-1}\|p_{1}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{2-q}}(\mu)}(c\xi_{K}(t-1)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

In particular, when
p_{1}
is bounded, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

(4.6)

\sup_{x\in M}\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq c\xi_{K}(t-1),\ \ \ t>1.

Comparing with the exact convergence rate for elliptic diffusions on compact manifolds, the present convergence rate is less sharp. However, as a universal convergence rate for arbitrary exponential ergodic Markov processes,

\xi_{K}(t)
is almost optimal.
To see this, let us consider the following example.

Example 4.1 (Markov processes on compact manifolds).

Let
M
be a
d
-dimensional compact connected Riemannian manifold possibly with a boundary
\partial M
, let
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\text{\rm{e}}^{V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x
be a probability measure on
M
for some
V\in C^{2}(M)
, and let

\hat{L}:=\Delta+\nabla V
(with Neumann boundary condition if
\partial M
exists). Then (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) hold for
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
so that
K=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{4}
.
By Theorem
4.1
,
for any Markov process on
M
satisfying (
4.4
) for some constant
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}>0,
there exists a constant
c>0
such that (
4.5
) holds, and (
4.6
) holds when
p_{1}
is bounded, for

\xi_{K}(t):=\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\text{if}\ d<2,\\
t^{-1}[\log t]^{2},&\text{if}\ d=2,\\
t^{-\frac{2}{d}},&\text{if}\ d>2.\end{cases}

On the other hand, according to
[
35
]
, for
X_{t}
being the
\alpha
-stable time changed process of
\hat{X}_{t}
,

\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\asymp\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\text{if}\ d<2(1+\alpha),\\
t^{-1}\log t,&\text{if}\ d=2(1+\alpha),\\
t^{-\frac{2}{d-2\alpha}},&\text{if}\ d>2(1+\alpha).\end{cases}

Since
\xi_{K}(t)
is the universal convergence rate for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it is reached by the exact rate as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, except
[\log t]^{2}
in the critical case.

Next, we consider a class of Markov processes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Example 4.2 (Markov processes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
).

Let
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
V\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

V(x)=\psi(x)+(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\psi\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\theta>0,\tau\in(\frac{1}{2},\infty]
are constants.
Let

\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\mu_{V}(\text{\rm{d}}x):=\frac{\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-V(x)}\text{\rm{d}}x}.

Then for any Markov process on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

satisfying (
4.4
), there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq c\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\text{if}\ n=1,\tau>1,\\
t^{-1}[\log(2+t)]^{2},&\text{if}\ n=1,\tau=1,\\
t^{-\frac{2\tau-1}{\tau n}},&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\hat{L}=\Delta-\nabla V.
By Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to verify (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) for
p=2,\beta=\frac{1}{2},
and
d=\frac{2\tau n}{2\tau-1}.
Since

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\hat{L}|\cdot|(x)=-\infty<0,

[
25
, Corollary 1.4]
ensures
{\rm gap}(\hat{L})>0
, so that
(
4.1
) holds for
p=2
and
\beta=\frac{1}{2}.

Next, by
[
26
, Theorem 2.4.4]
and
\nabla^{2}V\geq-c_{1}I_{n},
where
I_{n}
is the
n\times n
-unit matrix, we find a constant
c_{2}>0
such that

(4.7)

\hat{p}_{r}(x,x)\leq\frac{c_{2}}{\mu(B(x,\sqrt{r}))},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},r\in(0,1],

where
B(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-y|<r\},r>0.

Then (
4.3
) with
d=\frac{2\tau n}{2\tau-1}
follows provided

(4.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)}{\mu(B(x,r))}\leq cr^{-\frac{2\tau n}{2\tau-1}},\ \ r\in(0,1],x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

holds for some constant
c>0.

Since
\psi
is bounded, there exists a constant
C>1
such that

C^{-1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}}\text{\rm{d}}x\leq\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\leq C\text{\rm{e}}^{-(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}}\text{\rm{d}}x.

So, (
4.8
) is trivial for
|x|\leq 1
. On the other hand, when
|x|\geq 1
we have

\frac{|x|}{2}\leq|x|-\frac{r}{4}\leq|x|,\ \ r\in[0,1],

so we may find a constant
c_{3}>0
such that

\displaystyle\Big(1+\theta\Big|x-\frac{rx}{4|x|}\Big|^{2}\Big)^{\tau}=(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{r}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}s}\Big(1+\theta\big(|x|-\frac{s}{4}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\tau}\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle=(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}-\frac{\tau\theta}{2}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(1+\theta\big(|x|-\frac{s}{4}\big)^{2}\Big)^{\tau-1}\Big(|x|-\frac{s}{4}\Big)\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}-c_{3}r|x|^{2\tau-1}.

Hence, there exist constants
c_{4},c_{5}>0
such that for
|x|\geq 1
and
r\in(0,1]
,

(4.9)

\begin{split}&\mu(B(x,r))\geq c_{4}\int_{B\big(x-\frac{rx}{2|x|},\frac{r}{4}\big)}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(1+\theta|y|^{2})^{\tau}}\text{\rm{d}}y\\
&\geq c_{5}r^{n}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(1+\theta|x-\frac{rx}{4|x|}|^{2})^{\tau}}\geq c_{5}r^{n}\text{\rm{e}}^{-(1+\theta|x|^{2})^{\tau}+c_{3}r|x|^{2\tau-1}}.\end{split}

Therefore, there exist constants
c_{6},c_{7}>0
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\mu_{V}(\text{\rm{d}}x)}{\mu_{V}(B(x,r))}\leq c_{6}r^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{3}r|x|^{2\tau-1}}\text{\rm{d}}x

\displaystyle=c_{7}r^{-n}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{3}rs^{2\tau-1}}\text{\rm{d}}s=c_{7}r^{-\frac{2\tau n}{2\tau-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{n-1}\text{\rm{e}}^{-c_{3}s^{2\tau-1}}\text{\rm{d}}s.

Thus, (
4.8
) holds for some constant
c>0.

Finally, it is easy to see that
\nabla^{2}V\geq-cI_{n}
and
|\nabla V(x)|^{2}\leq c(1+|x|^{4\tau})
hold for some constant
c>0
. So, we find a constant
c_{8}>0
such that

(4.10)

\begin{split}&\hat{L}|x-\cdot|^{2}=2n+2\langle\nabla V,x-\cdot\rangle=2n+2\langle\nabla V(x),x-\cdot\rangle-2\langle\nabla V(x)-\nabla V,x-\cdot\rangle\\
&\leq 2n+|\nabla V(x)|^{2}+|x-\cdot|^{2}+2c_{1}|x-\cdot|^{2}\leq c_{8}(1+|x|^{4\tau}+|x-\cdot|^{2}),\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.\end{split}

This implies

(4.11)

\hat{P}_{t}|x-\cdot|^{2}(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}|x-\hat{X}_{t}|^{2}\leq c_{8}\big(1+|x|^{4\tau}|\big)t\text{\rm{e}}^{c_{8}t},\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ t>0.

Noting that
\mu(|\cdot|^{4\tau})<\infty,
we verify condition (
4.2
) for
p=2
and some constant
k>0.

∎

As a special case of Example 4.2, we consider the stochastic Hamiltonian system, a typical degenerate SDE for
X_{t}=(X_{t}^{(1)},X_{t}^{(2)})
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+m}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
(

(4.12)

\begin{cases}\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(1)}=\kappa QX_{t}^{(2)}\text{\rm{d}}t,\\
\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{(2)}=\sqrt{2}\,\text{\rm{d}}W_{t}-\big\{\theta Q^{*}X_{t}^{(1)}+\kappa X_{t}^{(2)}\big\}\text{\rm{d}}t,\end{cases}

where

is the
m
-dimensional Brownian motion,
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\otimes m}
such that
QQ^{*}
is invertible, and
\kappa,\theta>0
are constants.
Let

\mathscr{N}_{\theta}(\text{\rm{d}}x_{1}):=\Big(\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{\theta}{2}|x_{1}|^{2}}\text{\rm{d}}x_{1},\ \ \mathscr{N}_{\kappa}(\text{\rm{d}}x_{2}):=\Big(\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}\text{\rm{e}}^{-\frac{\kappa}{2}|x_{2}|^{2}}\text{\rm{d}}x_{2}.

By
[
28
]
, where more general degenerate models are considered, the associated Markov semigroup
P_{t}
is exponentially ergodic in entropy, hence (
4.4
) holds.
So, as shown in Example 4.2, (

)-(

) hold for
p=2,\beta=\frac{1}{2},
and
d=\frac{2(n+m)}{2\tau-1}.

Therefore, for any time-changed process of
X_{t}
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\mathbb{W}_{2}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq ct^{-\frac{1}{m+n}}.

5
More general ergodic Markov processes

For some infinite-dimensional models, see for instance
[
31
]
, (
4.3
) fails for any
d\in(0,\infty)
, but there may be a decreasing function
\gamma:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that

\int_{M}\hat{p}_{t}(x,x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\leq\gamma(t),\ \ t>0.

Next, in infinite-dimensional case the condition (
4.2
) may be invalid for small time, see Corollary
5.2
below.
Moreover, in case that
P_{t}
is not

-exponential ergodic, by the weak Poincaré inequality which holds for a broad class of ergodic Markov processes, see
[

]
, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)}=0.

To cover these situations for which Theorem
4.1
does not apply, we present the following result for the empirical measure
\mu_{t}
of the Markov process
X_{t}
with semigroup

.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

Assume
(
4.1
)
,
(
4.2
)
. If there exist a constant
q\in[1,\infty],q^{\prime}\in[\frac{q}{q-1},\infty],
a decreasing function
\gamma:(0,\infty)\rightarrow(0,\infty)
and an increasing continuous function
h:[0,1]\rightarrow[0,\infty)
with
h(0=0
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big(\hat{P}_{t}\rho(x,\cdot)^{p}\big)^{\frac{2}{p}}(x)\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)\leq h(t),\ \ t\in(0,1],x\in M,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mu)}=0,

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\|\hat{p}_{\frac{r}{2}}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}\|\hat{p}_{\frac{r}{2}}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)}\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y)\leq\gamma(r),\ \ \ r>0.

For any
t>0
, let

\xi(t):=\inf_{r\in(0,1]}\bigg\{\frac{\int_{0}^{t}\|P_{s}-\mu\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mu)}\text{\rm{d}}s}{t}\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{\gamma(r+s)}}{(r+s)^{\beta}}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg)^{2}+h(r)\bigg\}.

Then there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
t>0,

(5.4)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq c\xi(t).

If
P_{t}
has heat kernel
p_{t}
with respect to
\mu
, then for any
q\in[1,2],t>1
and
x\in M,

(5.5)

\mathbb{E}^{x}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{q}]\leq ct^{-q}\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[\mu(\rho(X_{s},\cdot)^{p})^{\frac{q}{p}}\big]\text{\rm{d}}s+c\big(\|p_{1}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{2-q}}(\mu)}\xi(t-1)\big)^{\frac{q}{2}}.

To verify Theorem
5.1
, we present below a simple example where
P_{t}
only has algebraic convergence in
\|\cdot\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)},
so Theorem
4.1
does not apply.

Example 5.1.

Let
M=[0,1],\rho(x,y)=|x-y|
and
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\text{\rm{d}}x.
For any
l\in(2,\infty),
let
X_{t}
be the diffusion process on
M\setminus\{0,1\}
generated by

L:=\big\{x(1-x)\big\}^{l}\frac{\text{\rm{d}}^{2}}{\text{\rm{d}}x^{2}}+l\big\{x(1-x)\big\}^{l-1}(1-2x)\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}.

Then Theorem
4.1
does not apply, but by Theorem
5.1
there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any
t>0
,

(5.6)

\mathbb{E}^{\mu}[\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{t},\mu)^{2}]\leq c\begin{cases}t^{-1},&\text{if}\ l\in(2,5),p\in[2,\frac{13-l}{4}),\\
t^{-1}[\log(2+t)]^{3},&\text{if}\ l\in(2,5],p=\frac{13-l}{4},\\
\big[t^{-1}\log(2+t)]^{\frac{8}{4p+l-5}},&\text{if}\ l\in(2,5],p>\frac{13-l}{4},\\
t^{-\frac{4}{l-1}}[\log(2+t)]^{2},&\text{if}\ l>5,p=2\\
t^{-\frac{8}{p(l-1)}},&\text{if}\ l>5,p>2.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first observe that (
4.4
) fails, so that Theorem
4.1
does not apply. Indeed, the Dirichlet form of

satisfies

(5.7)

\mathscr{E}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{1}\big\{x(1-x)\big\}^{l}(f^{\prime}g^{\prime})(x)\text{\rm{d}}x,\ \ f,g\in C_{b}^{1}(M)\subset\mathscr{D}(\mathscr{E}).

Let
\rho_{L}
be the intrinsic distance function to the point
\frac{1}{2}\in M.
We find a constant

such that

\rho_{L}(x)=\bigg|\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{x}\big\{s(1-s)\big\}^{-\frac{l}{2}}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg|\geq c_{1}\big(x^{1-\frac{l}{2}}+(1-x)^{1-\frac{l}{2}}\big),\ \ x\in M.

Then for any
\varepsilon>0,
we have
\mu(\text{\rm{e}}^{\varepsilon\rho_{L}})=\infty,
so that by
[
2
]
,
{\rm\text{\rm{gap}}}(L)=0.

On the other hand, since

is symmetric in

, by
[

, Lemma 2.2]
, (
4.4
) implies the same inequality for

, so that

. Hence, (

) fails.

To apply Theorem
5.1
, let
\hat{P}_{t}
be the standard Neumann heat semigroup on

generated by
\Delta
.
It is classical that (

) and (

) with

hold for some constant

and

(5.8)

\beta=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{p-2}{4}.

Moreover, there exists a constant

such that

\|\hat{P}_{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{L^{m}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{n}(\mu)}\leq c_{2}(1+r^{-\frac{n-m}{2nm}}),\ \ \ 1\leq m\leq n\leq\infty,r>0,

so that for
q^{\prime}=\infty
and

,

\displaystyle\|\hat{p}_{\frac{r}{2}}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{q}(\mu)}\|\hat{p}_{\frac{r}{2}}(y,\cdot)\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)}\mu(\text{\rm{d}}y)\leq c_{2}\|\hat{P}_{\frac{r}{2}}\|_{L^{1}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{q}(\mu)}(1+r^{-\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}^{2}(1+r^{-\frac{q-1}{2q}})(1+r^{-\frac{1}{2}}),\ \ r>0.

Hence, there exists a constant
c_{3}>0
such that (
5.3

\gamma(r)=c_{3}(1+r^{-\frac{2q-1}{2q}}).

Combining this with (
5.8
), we find a constant
k>0
such that for any
r\in(0,1),

(5.9)

\eta(r):=\bigg(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sqrt{\gamma(r+s)}}{(r+s)^{\beta}}\text{\rm{d}}s\bigg)^{2}\leq k\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ 1<q<\frac{1}{p-2},\\
[\log(1+r^{-1})]^{2},&\text{if}\ 1<q=\frac{1}{p-2},\\
r^{\frac{1-(p-2)q}{2q}},&\text{if}\ q>1\lor\frac{1}{p-2}.\end{cases}

To calculate
\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mu)}
for

, we apply the weak Poincaré inequality studied in
[
22
]
. Let

M_{s}=[s,1-s],\ \ \ s\in(0,1/2).

Noting that
\mu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\text{\rm{d}}x

\nu(\text{\rm{d}}x)=\{x(1-x)\}^{l}]\text{\rm{d}}x,
we find a constant
c_{4}>0

\sup_{r\in[s,1/2]}\mu([r,1/2])\nu([s,r])\leq 2^{l}\sup_{r\in[s,\frac{1}{2}]}\Big(\frac{1}{2}-r\Big)\big(s^{1-l}-r^{1-l}\big)\leq c_{4}s^{1-l},\ \ s\in(0,1/2).

By the weighted Hardy inequality, see for instance
[

,

\mu(f^{2}1_{[s,\frac{1}{2}]})\leq 4c_{4}s^{1-l}\nu(|f^{\prime}|^{2}),\ \ f\in C^{1}([s,1/2]),f(1/2)=0.

By symmetry, the same holds for
[\frac{1}{2},1-s]

So, according to
[

\mu(f^{2}1_{M_{s}})\leq 4c_{4}s^{1-l}\nu(|f^{\prime}|^{2}1_{M_{s}})+\mu(f1_{M_{s}})^{2},\ \ f\in C^{1}([s,s-1]).

Combining this with (

\displaystyle\mu(f^{2})=\mu(f^{2}1_{M_{s}^{c}})+\mu(f^{2}1_{M_{s}})\leq\mu(f^{2}1_{M_{s}^{c}})+4c_{4}s^{1-l}\mathscr{E}(f,f)+\mu(f1_{M_{s}^{c}})^{2},

\displaystyle\leq 4c_{4}s^{1-l}\mathscr{E}(f,f)+2\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}\mu(M_{s}^{c})^{2}\leq 4c_{4}s^{1-l}\mathscr{E}(f,f)+8s^{2}\|f\|_{\infty}^{2},\ \ s\in(0,1/2).

For any
r\in(0,1),
let
s=(r/8)^{\frac{1}{2}}.
We find a constant

such that

\mu(f^{2})\leq c_{5}r^{-\frac{l-1}{2}}\mathscr{E}(f,f)+r\|f\|_{\infty}^{2},\ \ r\in(0,1),\mu(f)=0,f\in C^{1}_{b}(M).

By
[

\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mu)}=\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{2}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{1}(\mu)}\leq c_{5}(1+t)^{-\frac{2}{l-1}},\ \ t>0

for some constant

Since
P_{t}

for any

\|P_{t}-\mu\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)\rightarrow L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(\mu)}\leq c_{6}(1+t)^{-\frac{4(q-1)}{q(l-1)}},\ \ t>0.

Noting that
q^{\prime}=\infty,
we find a constant
k>0

6
Some General Estimates on Wasserstein Distance

In this part, we introduce some useful techniques in estimating the Wasserstein distance for empirical measures of diffusion processes.

Let
(M,\rho)
be a length space, and recall that for a Lipschitz continuous function
f

|\nabla f|(x):=\limsup_{y\rightarrow x}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\rho(x,y)}.

Let
(\hat{L},\mathscr{D}(\hat{L}))
be the self-adjoint Dirichlet operator in
L^{2}(\mu)
with Dirichlet form
(\hat{\mathscr{E}},\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
satisfying
C_{b,L}(M)\subset\mathscr{D}(\hat{\mathscr{E}})
and

\hat{\mathscr{E}}(f,f)=\mu(|\nabla f|^{2}),\ \ \ f\in C_{b,L}(M).

Let
X_{t}
be the diffusion process generated by

L:=\hat{L}+Z,

where

Z
is a bounded vector field with
{\rm div}_{\mu}(Z)=0
, i.e.

\displaystyle\|Z\|_{\infty}:=\sup_{\|f\|_{Lip}\leq 1}|Zf|<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{M}Zf\text{\rm{d}}\mu=0,\ \ \ f\in C_{b,L}(M).

6.1
Upper bound estimate

According to
[
17
, Theorem 2]
, for any probability density
f
of
\mu
, we have

(6.1)

\mathbb{W}_{p}(f\mu,\mu)^{p}\leq p^{p}\mu\big(|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f-1)|^{p}\big),\ \ p\in[1,\infty).

The idea of the proof goes back to
[
4
]
, in which the following estimate is presented
for probability density functions
f_{1},f_{2}
:

(6.2)

\mathbb{W}_{2}(f_{1}\mu_{1},f_{2}\mu_{2})^{2}\leq\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{2}}{\mathscr{M}(f_{1},f_{2})}\text{\rm{d}}\mu,

where
\mathscr{M}(a,b):=1_{\{a\land b>0\}}\frac{\log a-\log b}{a-b}
for
a\neq b
, and
\mathscr{M}(a,a)=1_{\{a>0\}}a^{-1}
. In general, for
p\geq 1
, denote
\mathscr{M}_{p}=\mathscr{M}
if
p=2
, and when
p\neq 2
let

\mathscr{M}_{p}(a,b)=1_{\{a\land b>0\}}\frac{a^{2-p}-b^{2-p}}{(2-p)(a-b)}\text{\ for\ }a\neq b,\ \ \mathscr{M}_{p}(a,a)=1_{\{a>0\}}a^{1-p}.

The following result extends (
6.1
) and (
6.2
).

Theorem 6.1
.

For any probability density functions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
with respect to
\mu
such that
f_{1}\lor f_{2}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{p}(f_{1}\mu,f_{2}\mu)^{p}\leq\min\bigg\{

\displaystyle p^{p}2^{p-1}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{(f_{1}+f_{2})^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu,\ p^{p}\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{f_{1}^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu,

\displaystyle\qquad\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{\mathscr{M}_{p}(f_{1},f_{2})}\text{\rm{d}}\mu\bigg\}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove for
p>1
.
Consider the Hamilton-Jacobi semigroup
(Q_{t})_{t>0}
on
C_{b,L}(M)
:

Q_{t}\phi:=\inf_{x\in M}\Big\{\phi(x)+\frac{1}{pt^{p-1}}\rho(x,\cdot)^{p}\Big\},\ \ t>0,\phi\in C_{b,L}(M).

Then for any
\phi\in C_{b,L}(M)
,
Q_{0}\phi:=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}Q_{t}\phi=\phi
,
\|\nabla Q_{t}\phi\|_{\infty}
is locally bounded in
t\geq 0
, and
Q_{t}\phi
solves the Hamilton-Jacobi equation

(6.3)

\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}t}Q_{t}\phi=-\frac{p-1}{p}|\nabla Q_{t}\phi|^{\frac{p}{p-1}},\ \ t>0.

Let
q=\frac{p}{p-1}.
For any
f\in C_{b}^{1}(M)
, and any increasing function
\theta\in C^{1}((0,1))
such that
\theta_{0}:=\lim_{s\rightarrow 0}\theta_{s}=0,\theta_{1}:=\lim_{s\rightarrow 1}\theta_{s}=1
,
by (
6.3
) and the integration by parts formula, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{1}(Q_{1}f)-\mu_{2}(f)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big\{\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}s}\mu\big([f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})]Q_{s}f\big)\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\text{\rm{d}}s\int_{M}\Big\{\theta_{s}^{\prime}(f_{2}-f_{1})Q_{s}f-\frac{f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})}{q}|\nabla Q_{s}f|^{q}\Big\}\text{\rm{d}}\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\Big[\theta_{s}^{\prime}\hat{\mathscr{E}}\big((-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1}),Q_{s}f\big)-\mu\Big(\frac{f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})}{q}|\nabla Q_{s}f|^{q}\Big)\Big]\text{\rm{d}}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\mu\Big(\theta_{s}^{\prime}\big|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})\big|\cdot\big|\nabla Q_{s}f\big|-\frac{f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})}{q}|\nabla Q_{s}f|^{q}\Big)\text{\rm{d}}s.

Combining this with Young’s inequality
ab\leq a^{p}/p+b^{q}/q
for
a,b\geq 0
, we arrive at

(6.4)

\mu_{1}(Q_{1}f)-\mu_{2}(f)\leq\frac{1}{p}\int_{M}|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}\text{\rm{d}}\mu\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\theta_{s}^{\prime}|^{p}}{[f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})]^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}s.

By Kantorovich duality formula

\frac{1}{p}\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})^{p}=\sup_{f\in C_{b}^{1}(M)}\big\{\mu_{1}(Q_{1}f)-\mu_{2}(f)\big\},

and noting that

\displaystyle f_{1}+\theta_{s}(f_{2}-f_{1})=f_{1}+f_{2}-\theta_{s}f_{1}-(1-\theta_{s})f_{2}

\displaystyle=(f_{1}+f_{2})\Big(1-\frac{\theta_{s}f_{1}}{f_{1}+f_{2}}-\frac{(1-\theta_{s})f_{2}}{f_{1}+f_{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq(f_{1}+f_{2})\min\{1-\theta_{s},\theta_{s}\},

we deduce from (
6.4
) that

(6.5)

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})^{p}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\theta_{s}^{\prime}|^{p}}{\min\{\theta_{s},1-\theta_{s}\}^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}s\int_{M}\frac{|\nabla(-\hat{L})^{-1}(f_{1}-f_{2})|^{p}}{(f_{1}+f_{2})^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu.

By taking

\theta_{s}=1_{[0,\frac{1}{2}]}(s)2^{p-1}s^{p}+1_{(\frac{1}{2},1]}(s)\big\{1-2^{p-1}(1-s)^{p}\big\},

which satisfies

\theta_{s}^{\prime}=p2^{p-1}\min\{s,1-s\}^{p-1},\ \ \min\{\theta_{s},1-\theta_{s}\}=2^{p-1}\min\{s,1-s\}^{p},

we deduce from (
6.5
)
that

\mathbb{W}_{p}(f_{1}\mu,f_{2}\mu)^{p}\leq p^{p}2^{p-1}\int_{M}\frac{|(-\hat{L})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{(f_{1}+f_{2})^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu.

Next, (
6.5
) with
\theta_{s}=1-(1-s)^{p}
implies

\mathbb{W}_{p}(f_{1}\mu,f_{2}\mu)^{p}\leq p^{p}\int_{M}\frac{|(-\hat{L})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{f_{1}^{p-1}}\text{\rm{d}}\mu.

Finally, with
\theta_{s}=s
we deduce from (
6.4
) that

\mathbb{W}_{p}(f_{1}\mu,f_{2}\mu)^{p}\leq\int_{M}\frac{|(-\hat{L})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(f_{2}-f_{1})|^{p}}{\mathscr{M}_{p}(f_{1},f_{2})}\text{\rm{d}}\mu.

Then the proof is finished.
∎

t>0
,

\inf_{\nu\in\mathscr{P}}\mathbb{E}^{\nu}[\mathbb{W}_{1}^{q}(\mu_{t},\mu)]\succeq\gamma_{d}(t)^{q}.

Finally, we present a lower bound estimate which also applies to infinite-dimensions and generalizes
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
for the finite-dimensional setting.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\mu\in\mathscr{P}(E)
such that

(6.17)

\sup_{x\in E}\mu(B(x,r))\leq\psi(r),\ \ r\geq 0

holds for some increasing function
\psi,
where

B(x,r):=\{y\in E:\rho(x,y)<r\}
. Then
for any
N\geq 1
and any probability measure
\mu_{N}
supported on a set of
N
points in
E
,

(6.18)

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu_{N},\mu)\geq 2^{-\frac{1}{p}}\psi^{-1}\Big(\frac{1}{2N}\Big),

where
\psi^{-1}(s):=\sup\{r\geq 0:\psi(r)\leq s\},s\geq 0.

Proof.

Let
D={\rm supp}\mu_{N}
which contains
N
many points, so that from (
6.17
) we conclude that

D_{r}:=\cup_{x\in D}B(x,r)
satisfies

\mu(D_{r})\leq\sum_{x\in D}\mu(B(x,r))\leq N\psi(r),\ \ r\geq 0.

Therefore, for any
\pi\in\mathscr{C}(\mu_{N},\mu)
, we get

\int_{E\times E}\rho(x,y)^{p}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)\geq\int_{D\times D_{r}^{c}}r^{p}\pi(\text{\rm{d}}x,\text{\rm{d}}y)=r^{p}\mu(D_{r}^{c})\geq r^{p}\{1-N\psi(r)\},\ \ r\geq 0.

Taking
r=\psi^{-1}(1/(2N))
we derive

\mathbb{W}_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{p}\geq\sup_{r\geq 0}r^{p}[1-N\psi(r)]\geq\frac{1}{2}\big\{\psi^{-1}(1/(2N))\big\}^{p}.

∎

To apply Theorem
6.4
to the empirical measure
\mu_{t}
, we only need to compare
\mu_{t}
with the discretize empirical measure

\mu_{t,N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{X_{it/N}},

with suitable choice of
N=N_{t}\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

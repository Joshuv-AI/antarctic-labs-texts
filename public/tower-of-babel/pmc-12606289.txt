Programming pulse width dependent charge retention characteristics of low-power synaptic thin film transistors
Authors: Danyoung Cha, Jeongseok Pi, Sungsik Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractA study on a programming-pulse width dependent synaptic characteristics of a sub-threshold operating hafnium doped zinc oxide (Hf-ZnO) thin-film transistor (TFT) is presented. For this, the static and pulsed characteristics of fabricated Hf-ZnO TFTs need to be monitored, respectively. Here, to achieve the memory capability (e.g. electron trapping and de-trapping) of this Hf-ZnO TFT, trap states in defective gate oxides, such as Al2O3 and HfOx, caused by its low-temperature process with a thermal atomic-layer deposition can intentionally be used. Based on this memory phenomenon, when positive or negative programming-pulses are applied to the gate terminal, as decreasing the pulse width, it is observed that the programming speed is slower. However, in terms of the retention performance, a much longer retention time is confirmed from the mathematical modeling analysis with the stretched exponential function. In this vein, a trade-off relation between the programming speed and retention time is proposed. Besides these characteristics, such as the weight-update and retention characteristics, a paired-pulse depression and facilitation, and weight linearity are monitored to discuss the synaptic functionalities of Hf-ZnO TFTs further. Moreover, with this data at the device level, the crossbar simulation based on the analog accelerator is conducted, monitoring its performance (i.e. a classification accuracy).Keywords: Synaptic device, Amorphous oxide semiconductor, Weight-update characteristics, Retention characteristics, Low power consumptionSubject terms: Engineering, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

Emerging material-based technologies have been widely investigated for futuristic applications (e.g., neuromorphic application)1–5. Here, as an elementary unit of the neuromorphic application, synaptic devices, such as memristors and synaptic transistors, have been intensively studied for their longer-term memory and higher-level intelligence6–8. Among them, a thin-film transistor (TFT) based on amorphous oxide semiconductors (AOSs), such as a zinc oxide (ZnO), as an active type can be considered as a synaptic device, thus a synaptic thin-film transistor (Syn-TFT)9–11. This can be because AOSs have many advantages, including a low-temperature processability and low-power consumption which results from a relatively low off-current with a wider band gap compared to silicon12,13. However, due to the electrical and optical instabilities (e.g. oxygen vacancies) in the ZnO channel, it can be expected that it is difficult to modulate the synaptic characteristics, such as weight-updates14,15. As an approach to solve this, metal cations (e.g. Hf and Zr) are incorporated in the ZnO channel, suppressing the increased oxygen vacancies, thus the Hf-ZnO channel is employed. Synaptic transistors based on this channel (i.e. Hf-ZnO) can easily control their weight-updates characteristics, showing a dynamic ratio and paired-pulse facilitation (or depression)16,17. Here, for the memory functionality of synaptic transistors, it is known that a charge trapping layer (CTL) between tunneling and blocking oxides can be used in the gate insulator system18,19. However, for the case of the CTL structure, it needs to be relatively high programming-pulses for a charge trapping (or de-trapping) phenomenon, implying a limitation of the low-power characteristics. In this respect, high- insulators, such as HfO2 and Al2O3, are employed in the synaptic transistors (e.g. Syn-TFTs) without the CTL structure, using defects for the charge trapping20,21. It is expected that electrons within the channel layer can be easily trapped toward the gate insulator although a relatively low programming voltage (Vprog) is applied to the gate terminal (VGS). Moreover, in order to achieve a lower power consumption, Syn-TFTs can operate in the sub-threshold region which has a lower current level in comparison with the above-threshold region22,23. Here, when positive or negative programming-pulses with different programming-pulse widths (PPWs) are applied to the VGS for operating in the sub-threshold region, the synaptic characteristics (e.g. weight-update and charge-retention characteristics) of Syn-TFTs are expected to be different from each other. Here, the retention characteristics in the Syn-TFT are a main factor for a longer term memory of the neuromorphic system24,25. Actually, retention characteristics of various synaptic devices have been widely reported showing a longer-term memory of 3.6 104 Sect26–28. In this respect, an analysis related to the retention characteristics dependent on the PPW of the low-power Hf-ZnO Syn-TFT is required.

In this article, the synaptic characteristics dependent on the PPW of the Hf-ZnO Syn-TFT with a low power consumption are analyzed. In order to validate this, the weight-update and retention characteristics of fabricated Hf-ZnO TFTs need to be displayed, respectively. For the memory functionality, such as the charge trapping and de-trapping, in this Hf-ZnO TFT, trap sites in HfOx and Al2O3 caused by its low-temperature process are deliberately used with a thermal atomic-layer deposition. With this memory function, when positive or negative programming-pulses with a narrower PPW are applied to the VGS, it is found that the programming speed is slower. On the other hand, in terms of the charge-retention characteristics, it is observed that a much longer retention time is extracted from the stretched exponential function through mathematical modeling. These results suggest a trade-off relation between the programming speed and retention time. Here, a more detailed physical interpretation was provided through an analysis of the activation energy. In order to further analyze the synaptic functionalities of Hf-ZnO TFTs, a paired-pulse depression and facilitation as other synaptic characteristics, and weight linearity are evaluated for different PPWs. Based on device-level characteristics, the performance of the analog accelerator is assessed through a crossbar simulation, focusing on the classification accuracy. This result suggests that the array-level application can be achieved with the sub-threshold operating Hf-ZnO Syn-TFTs.

Results and discussion

Synaptic behavior and material analysis of Hf-ZnO TFTs

Figure 1(a) depicts the neuromorphic system to mimic the biological brain. In the neuromorphic system, synaptic transistors as its fundamental building block are composed. So, it is essential to improve the memory functionality of the synaptic transistor for achieving the neuromorphic system with performances, such as more efficient data processing and higher-level intelligence3–5. Figure 1(b) shows the cross-section view of fabricated Syn-TFTs with a bottom gate structure. As can be seen, defects in the insulator system can mainly exist because of disorders, such as a dangling bond, a low-temperature process with a thermal ALD, playing the role of electron trap sites. So, the memory functionality can be achieved with these defects for applying the programming-pulses. Here, electron trapping and de-trapping phenomena are dependent on the polarity and number of programming-pulses. Based on Syn-TFTs fabricated with this structure, when multiple positive programming-pulses are applied to the VGS, electrons can be trapped into the gate insulator, thus the memory function (see Fig. 1(f)). After applying the programming-pulse, the constant read voltage (Vread) is maintained for checking the retention. For this process, some electrons at the interface between the channel and insulator layers can escape to the channel layer. Note that, for applying negative programming-pulses to the VGS, electrons in the gate insulator can be de-trapped to the Hf-ZnO channel. To further discuss these phenomena, band diagrams associated with the electron trapping and de-trapping are described in Figs. 2(a) and (b). As can be seen in Fig. 2(a), electrons within the conduction band in the Hf-ZnO channel as a n-type semiconducting material are trapped in the insulator layers (i.e. HfO2 and Al2O3) with applying the positive programming-pulse train. This is called the synaptic depression process. On the other hand, when the negative programming-pulse is applied to the VGS, the electrons within the defective gate insulator can be de-trapped into the Hf-ZnO channel layer, which is called the synaptic potentiation (or facilitation) process (see Fig. 2(b)). After these synaptic facilitation and depression processes, the retention process with applying a constant Vread can be discussed with band diagrams, which depicts the gradual recovery of electrons to the initial state, as shown in Figs. 2(c) and (d). As can be seen, for applying a positive Vread, some of the electrons trapped within the gate insulator after the depression process can escape to the channel layer, leading to the initial state. On the other hand, after the synaptic facilitation, some de-trapped electrons can be trapped into the gate oxide. Classification learning with an analog accelerator based on a Syn-TFT array

Based on device-level measurements, it is anticipated that the performances (e.g. classification accuracy, CA) of the analog accelerator which is constructed with an array of Syn-TFTs, varies depending on the PPW. To investigate this dependency, the CA from a classification task with the Modified National Institute of Standards and Technology (MNIST) dataset is evaluated. Figure 6(a) presents a schematic of a three-layer artificial neural network (ANN) composed of 784 input neurons, 300 hidden neurons, and 10 output neurons, corresponding to a network size of 784 × 300 × 1036. The input images from MNIST are 28 × 28 pixels, as shown in Fig. 5(a). A total of 60,000 training samples and 10,000 test samples are used for evaluating the analog accelerator’s performance37. Based on this network, simulations are performed using CrossSim™ which is a resistive crossbar simulator tailored for analog AI systems built from the Syn-TFT’s array36–39. The network input is driven through digital-to-analog converters (DACs) and pulse generators while the output is captured through analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) and integrators, using operational amplifiers. These data converters operate within algorithmic ranges that depend on physical attributes like programming-pulse width and amplitude. Bit precision is defined by the total number of bits, including the sign bit. In this setup, the row and column updates of the DAC and ADC have 7 and 5 bits with dynamic ranges of 0.1σ and − 1 to 1, where σ as the learning rate is fixed at 0.1 for simulation in CrossSim™, respectively. Conclusion

In this paper, synaptic behaviors of low-power Hf-ZnO Syn-TFTs have been quantitatively analyzed with applying different PPWs. To confirm the synaptic characteristics, weight-update and retention behaviors have been monitored using fabricated devices. Charge trapping and de-trapping enabled by trap states in HfOx and Al2O3 formed at a low-temperature with the thermal ALD have been achieved for the memory function of Syn-TFTs. During the depression process with a narrower PPW (i.e. 1.5 s) and retention process, it has been found that the programming speed is to be 0.091 whereas the retention time becomes 106 sec. Moreover, as decreasing the PPW (i.e. 1.5 s), it has been observed to be 0.34 of the programming speed while achieving a longer retention time of 33.1 s for the potentiation and retention processes, respectively. Additional synaptic characteristics, such as paired-pulse facilitation/depression and weight linearity, have been evaluated for different PPWs. Finally, a crossbar simulation incorporating these device-level properties has been conducted to assess the analog accelerator performance, showing its average classification accuracy of 85% for PPW = 6 sec, approximately. Based on these results, further research can be conducted to examine the influence of PPW on system-level (i.e. Syn-TFT array) implementations. Moreover, a foundation for advanced research related to the latency-sensitive systems, such as robotics and edge AI, can be formed by this study, as it demonstrates variations of learning speed and memory capability. In conclusion, when an optimal PPW is selected, it is believed that both excellent weight-update performance (i.e., fast programming speed) and stable retention (i.e., extended retention time) can be simultaneously achieved, enabling efficient data processing and memory functionality in neuromorphic systems.

Experimental section

The fabrication of the synaptic thin-film transistor (Syn-TFT) begins with the use of three Cr-based photomasks. Initially, photoresist (PR) patterning is conducted using a mask aligner (MA) on a glass substrate, followed by gate metallization via thermal evaporation. The gate electrode comprises a 5 nm chromium (Cr) adhesion layer and a 100 nm gold (Au) layer, resulting in a total thickness of 105 nm. Here, Cr serves to improve the adhesion between the Au layer and the glass substrate, as previously reported44. After metallization, a lift-off process is used to define the gate electrode pattern, and post-metallization annealing is performed at 200 °C for 30 min in air to enhance film stability. For the gate dielectric stack, a 75 nm aluminum oxide (Al2O3) layer followed by a 15 nm hafnium oxide (HfO2) layer is deposited via atomic layer deposition (ALD), yielding a total thickness of 90 nm. The ALD process is conducted at 200 °C, using trimethylaluminum (TMA) and tetrakis (ethylmethylamino) hafnium (TEMAHf) as precursors, with H2O as the reactant gas. Prior to dielectric deposition, the glass substrate is exposed to ultraviolet (UV) ozone to render the surface hydrophilic and form uniform oxide growth. Next, the channel layer is deposited using ALD at 150 °C, forming a 20 nm Hf-doped ZnO (Hf-ZnO) thin film with a ZnO : HfO2 cycle ratio of 50 : 1. Diethylzinc (DEZ) and H2O are used as the precursor and oxidant, respectively. The resulting film undergoes thermal annealing at 200 °C for 1 h in vacuum to enhance film quality and stability. The active region is then defined by a wet etching process. Buffered oxide etch (BOE) selectively removes the gate dielectric layers while hydrogen chloride (HCl) is used to etch the channel layer. A subsequent annealing at 200 °C for 30 min in air is conducted to remove etching residues and further improve film quality. The source and drain (S/D) electrodes are fabricated via the same PR patterning and thermal evaporation steps used for the gate electrode. A 5 nm Cr adhesion layer and a 100 nm Au contact layer are sequentially deposited, followed by a lift-off process to define the S/D regions. Finally, a post-metallization annealing step at 200 °C for 30 min in ambient air is conducted, which completes the device fabrication.

Structural Origin of the Gravitino Mass Term: Geometric and Supergravity Perspectives
Stefano Bellucciand, Stefania De Matteo

Abstract
We identify a minimal superspace projector that uniquely selects the Rarita–Schwinger mass bilinear in four-dimensional N=1 supergravity. Working in a reduced superspace with a single fermionic direction, we show that Berezin projection of a canonical even supergeometric form isolates the unique Lorentz-invariant fermionic bilinear compatible with local supersymmetry. The construction is predynamical: it fixes the algebraic structure of the gravitino mass term independently of supersymmetry breaking mechanisms, background geometry, or matter couplings. We show that this projector embeds consistently into curved superspace, extends to N>1 theories, and clarifies the universality of the gravitino mass structure in supergravity.

Introduction

In four-dimensional
N=1
supergravity, the mass term of the gravitino universally takes the Rarita–Schwinger form. This fact is well established in the literature and holds independently of the specific realization of supersymmetry breaking, of the matter content, or of the background geometry. Despite its ubiquity, the reason for this universality is rarely addressed explicitly at the level of superspace structure. In most treatments, the gravitino mass term is introduced directly at the level of component fields. Its Lorentz structure is fixed by consistency requirements and by analogy with the massive Rarita–Schwinger theory, and is then embedded into a supersymmetric framework. Within the superspace formalism, however, the same structure typically remains implicit: while superspace techniques are extensively employed to construct invariant actions, the specific mechanism that singles out the Rarita–Schwinger bilinear as the unique admissible fermionic mass term is not made explicit.

This leaves a clear conceptual gap. If local supersymmetry is taken as a fundamental organizing principle, one may ask why it allows precisely this fermionic bilinear and excludes all others, even before any dynamical considerations are introduced.

In other words, why is the gravitino mass term always of the Rarita–Schwinger form?

We make explicit the superspace mechanism that fixes this structure independently of dynamics. The analysis presented in this work is therefore predynamical. Rather than proposing a dynamical origin for the gravitino mass or invoking specific supersymmetry-breaking scenarios, we focus on the minimal superspace setting and investigate which fermionic bilinears can arise as non-vanishing, Lorentz-invariant objects under Berezin projection. Working in a reduced superspace with a single fermionic direction, we show that the Berezin projection of a canonical even supergeometric form uniquely selects the Rarita–Schwinger mass bilinear. All other candidate superspace objects either vanish identically or fail to produce an admissible Lorentz scalar.

It is important to emphasize the scope of this result. The construction presented here does not generate a mass scale for the gravitino, nor does it break supersymmetry. Its purpose is to clarify the algebraic origin of the fermionic structure of the mass term once such a term is introduced. In this sense, local supersymmetry does not merely constrain the gravitino mass term dynamically; it fixes its algebraic form at the level of superspace structure.

Finally, we show that this minimal superspace projector embeds consistently into curved superspace and admits a natural extension to theories with
N>1
.

The result provides a clean and explicit superspace explanation for the universality of the Rarita–Schwinger mass term in supergravity, establishing a structural foundation for subsequent analyses that address dynamical or phenomenological questions.

The paper is organized as follows.

Section 2 introduces the minimal superspace setting and the supergeometric tools required for the analysis, focusing on differential forms defined on a

C^{0|1}
superfiber bundle.

Section 3 contains the core result of the paper: we construct the minimal even supergeometric form and show that its Berezin projection uniquely selects a non-vanishing Lorentz-invariant fermionic bilinear.

In Section 4 this result is interpreted within the framework of
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity, where the selected bilinear is identified with the Rarita–Schwinger gravitino mass term and its predynamical character is clarified.

Section 5 is devoted to a comparison with standard
\mathcal{N}=1
supergravity formulations, highlighting similarities and differences with the superspace construction presented here.

Section 6 addresses extensions and global perspectives, including the embedding of the construction into curved superspace and its structural generalization to
\mathcal{N}>1
supersymmetry.

Finally, Section 7 summarizes the main results and outlines possible directions for further investigation.

Supergeometric Formulation: Construction of an Invariant Scalar

In this section we move to the geometric perspective.
We work on the superfiber bundle
\mathcal{M}
with fiber
C^{0|1}

\mathcal{M}

This scalar will be the candidate representing the emergent gravitino

The construction involves:

1.

the definition of a specific odd differential form on

\mathcal{M}
, whose geometric nature is tied to the presence of the
fermionic coordinate
\theta
;

2.

the use of a section (locally encoded by a fermionic field

\Psi(x)
) to pull back this form to the base manifold
M
;

3.

the use of Berezin integration over the fermionic fiber to obtain
a purely bosonic quantity on
M
.

The final goal is to obtain an expression of the form
m(x)
, a scalar
function on
M
, invariant under supergeometric coordinate changes
(up to gauge choices), and identifiable with the gravitino mass from the
physical point of view.

3.1

Fundamental 1-form on the superfiber bundle and its pullback

We consider the fundamental odd differential 1-form
d\theta
on

\mathcal{M}
.
As discussed previously, given a section
\sigma
, its pullback is

\sigma^{*}(d\theta)=d\Psi(x),

where
\Psi(x)
is the Grassmann-valued field on the base manifold
associated with the section.
Locally, this can be written as

d\Psi(x)=\partial_{\mu}\Psi(x)\,dx^{\mu},

with anticommuting coefficients
\partial_{\mu}\Psi(x)
.
The object
d\Psi(x)
is therefore a 1-form on
M
with fermionic
coefficients.
Although the expression contains a derivative, it should not be interpreted as a kinetic term: since
\Psi
encodes a spinorial one-form rather than a scalar fermion, the derivative does not correspond to propagation but to a geometric contraction whose on-shell reduction yields an algebraic bilinear.

By itself,
d\Psi(x)
is not a scalar but a 1-form.
We must therefore combine
\Psi(x)
and
d\Psi(x)
in such a way as to
obtain an object of degree zero on
M
.
A basic observation is that the product of a fermionic function and a
fermionic 1-form has even parity.
In particular, the object

\Psi(x)\,d\Psi(x)

is a 1-form on
M
with even parity.

This structure is reminiscent of torsion terms or Goldstone currents.
If
\Psi
were a fermionic Goldstone field, the combination

\Psi\,d\Psi
would naturally appear in invariant actions.
However,
\Psi\,d\Psi
is still a 1-form and not yet a scalar.

A more intrinsically supergeometric approach is obtained by introducing
a mixed odd–odd 1-form on the total space
\mathcal{M}

\Omega=\theta\,d\theta.

(1)

Here
\theta
is a fermionic 0-form and
d\theta
is a fermionic 1-form,
so their product has even parity.
The form
\Omega

\mathcal{M}
.

Before writing the pulled-back expression, we stress that the exterior differential used here is introduced purely at the level of supergeometric structure.
No identification is made between this differential and a spacetime kinetic operator; its sole role is to encode graded antisymmetry at the representation-theoretic level.

The next step is to extract a scalar from

3.2

Geometric scalar from Berezin integration

We consider the supergeometric invariant defined on the
(0|1)
-dimensional
fermionic submanifold
C^{0|1}
.
Let
\Omega
denote an even supergeometric one-form on the supermanifold, whose Berezin integration is understood as a projection rather than as a top-degree de Rham integral
and let

\sigma:M\to\mathcal{M}
be a local section.
The pullback
\sigma^{*}(\Omega)
defines a Grassmann-valued form on
C^{0|1}

The associated scalar quantity is defined as

m_{\mathrm{geom}}(x)\;:=\;\int_{C^{0|1}}\sigma^{*}(\Omega),

(3)

where the integration is understood as a Berezin projection along the odd
direction.
This operation does not introduce any numerical scale and should not be
interpreted as producing a physical mass value.
Rather, it selects the fermionic bilinear structure encoded in the pulled-back
form.

In local coordinates, the Berezin projection formally reduces to an expression
of the type
\int d\theta\,\theta
, which serves solely as a projector onto the
fermionic component of the supergeometric invariant.
The resulting quantity
m_{\mathrm{geom}}(x)
therefore encodes the algebraic
structure of a mass term, while its numerical value remains undetermined at this
level.

Before proceeding, we clarify the geometric nature of the fermionic variable

\Psi
.
In the supergeometric formulation,
\Psi
is treated as an odd Grassmann-valued
coordinate.
When a section
\sigma:M\to\mathcal{M}
is specified,
\Psi(x)
is understood
as a spinor-valued field, namely a local section of the spinor bundle

S\to M
,
\Psi\in\Gamma(S)
.
This identification allows the supergeometric construction to be consistently
related to the gravitino sector of
N=1
supergravity.
In a reduced superspace with a single odd coordinate
\theta
, the graded-commutative structure strongly constrains the set of admissible superspace objects. In particular, the Grassmann nature of
\theta
implies

\theta\,\theta=0,

while the graded antisymmetry of the exterior product yields

d\theta\wedge d\theta=0.

By contrast, the mixed object

\theta\wedge d\theta\neq 0

is non-vanishing and, moreover, even. As such,
\theta\,d\theta
defines the only even superspace one-form available in the minimal setting. Any other object constructed solely from
\theta
and
d\theta
either vanishes identically or fails to define, upon Berezin projection, a Lorentz-invariant fermionic bilinear. This exhausts the set of admissible minimal superspace candidates.

.

Up to total derivatives and conventions,
\theta\,d\theta

4

Physical Interpretation: gravitino mass term in
N=1
Supergravity

In this section we reinterpret the geometric scalar constructed in
Section 3 within the standard physical framework of
N=1

supergravity.
The aim is to show that the supergeometric invariant

m_{\text{geom}}
coincides, both structurally and conceptually, with
the gravitino mass parameter appearing in the supergravity action and
field equations.

4.1

Gravitino mass and supersymmetry breaking

In
N=1
supergravity, a mass term for the gravitino cannot be added
arbitrarily.
Local supersymmetry severely constrains the form of admissible mass
terms, and consistency requires either the presence of a non-vanishing
cosmological constant or the spontaneous breaking of supersymmetry.

From the Lagrangian point of view, the gravitino mass term has the
schematic form

\mathcal{L}_{\text{mass}}=-\frac{m_{3/2}}{2}\,e\,\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu},

where
e=\det(e_{\mu}^{\,a})
and
m_{3/2}
is the gravitino mass.
This term breaks supersymmetry explicitly unless it is accompanied by
additional contributions ensuring the closure of the supersymmetry
algebra.

A well-known result is that a non-zero gravitino mass is consistent
with local supersymmetry if spacetime is Anti–de Sitter (AdS) rather
than Minkowski.
In this case, the cosmological constant
\Lambda
is negative and
related to the gravitino mass by

\Lambda\sim-m_{3/2}^{2}

up to numerical factors depending on conventions.
Equivalently, one may view the massive gravitino as arising from the
super-Higgs mechanism: a spin-
1/2
Goldstone fermion (the goldstino)
associated with supersymmetry breaking is absorbed by the gravitino,
which thereby acquires mass.

In standard formulations, the origin of
m_{3/2}
is often traced back
to a constant term in the superpotential or to auxiliary fields whose
vacuum expectation values break supersymmetry.
From this perspective, the gravitino mass appears as a parameter
introduced at the level of the effective action.

4.2

Identification with the geometric scalar

We now compare this standard picture with the supergeometric scalar

m_{\text{geom}}
constructed in Section 3.
Recall that
m_{\text{geom}}
arises as a bosonic scalar obtained by
Berezin integration over the fermionic fiber of the superfiber bundle,
starting from the even supergeometric 1-form
\theta\,d\theta
.

The crucial observation is that
m_{\text{geom}}
has exactly the same
algebraic structure as the gravitino mass term in the supergravity
action.
Indeed, once the fermionic field
\Psi(x)
defining the section

\sigma
is identified with the appropriate spinorial degrees of
freedom, one finds

m_{\text{geom}}(x)\;\propto\;\bar{\psi}_{\mu}(x)\,\gamma^{\mu\nu}\,\psi_{\nu}(x),

which is a Lorentz scalar and encodes the correct structural form, while the associated physical scale is determined by the underlying dynamics.

No additional dynamical assumptions are required: the structure
follows directly from the geometry of the superfiber bundle.
The geometric construction fixes the invariant structure of the mass term, while the physical mass scale is set by the background or supersymmetry-breaking sector, as in standard supergravity; the Berezin projection itself is dimensionless and does not determine the numerical value of
m_{3/2}
.

From the physical point of view, this result means that the gravitino
mass does not need to be postulated at the level of algebraic structure.
Instead, it can be understood as the
energy scale associated
with the fermionic geometry
of superspace.
The odd direction encoded by
C^{0|1}
contributes a non-trivial
invariant that manifests itself, on the base manifold
M
, as a mass
term for the gravitino.

4.3

Relation to the super-Higgs mechanism

The supergeometric interpretation is fully compatible with the
super-Higgs mechanism.
In the usual picture, the goldstino is a fermionic degree of freedom
that parametrizes supersymmetry breaking.
Geometrically, such a fermionic mode can be viewed as a choice of
section in the fermionic direction of superspace.

In this sense, the field
\Psi(x)
defining the section

\sigma:M\to\mathcal{M}
plays a dual role.
On the one hand, it is a purely geometric object specifying how the
base manifold is embedded into the superfiber bundle.
On the other hand, it encodes the goldstino-like degrees of freedom
that are absorbed by the gravitino.
The emergence of
m_{\text{geom}}
from
\Psi\,d\Psi
thus mirrors the
physical process by which the gravitino acquires mass through the
absorption of the goldstino.

Importantly, the geometric construction does not require an explicit
potential or vacuum expectation value.
The mass scale arises from the existence of a non-trivial fermionic
structure and from the invariant way in which odd forms are combined
and integrated.
This highlights a conceptual shift: supersymmetry breaking is no
longer seen purely as a dynamical phenomenon, but also as a

geometric property
of the extended superspace.

4.4

Structural correspondence between geometry and energy

The identification of
m_{\text{geom}}
with the gravitino mass
suggests a deeper structural correspondence between geometric and energetic descriptions.

In general relativity, energy–momentum acts as the source of
spacetime curvature.
Here, an analogous relation appears in supersymmetric theories:
a geometric invariant of superspace gives rise to an energy scale in
the effective theory on spacetime.

From this viewpoint, the gravitino mass is both:

•

a measure of supersymmetry breaking energy, and

•

a measure of the curvature or structure associated with the
fermionic direction of superspace.

The two interpretations are not alternatives but complementary
descriptions of the same underlying object.

This structural correspondence provides a unifying perspective on the role of the
gravitino mass in supergravity.

Rather than being an external parameter, it can be regarded as an
emergent quantity encoding how geometry and energy are intertwined in
a supersymmetric framework.

In the next section we will compare this construction more explicitly
with standard formulations of
N=1
supergravity and discuss points of
contact and conceptual differences.

5

Comparison with Standard
N=1
Supergravity Formulations

In this section we compare the supergeometric construction presented
above with the standard formulations of
N=1
supergravity.
The goal is not to replace the conventional framework, but to clarify
how the geometric approach complements and reinterprets familiar
results.

5.1

Standard origin of the gravitino mass

In conventional
N=1
supergravity coupled to matter, the gravitino
mass typically arises from the superpotential.
If
W
denotes the superpotential and
K
the Kähler potential, the
gravitino mass is given (in Planck units) by

m_{3/2}=e^{K/2}|W|,

evaluated at the vacuum configuration.
In this setting, the mass is tied to auxiliary fields and to the
vacuum expectation values of scalar fields that break supersymmetry.

From this viewpoint,
m_{3/2}
is a derived quantity, but it still
depends on input functions (
W
,
K
) that are specified at the level
of the effective theory.
The geometrical meaning of the mass term is therefore indirect:
geometry enters through the Kähler structure of field space rather
than through the geometry of superspace itself.

5.2

AdS supergravity and cosmological constant

An alternative standard route to a massive gravitino is provided by
Anti–de Sitter supergravity.
In pure
N=1
supergravity with a negative cosmological constant, the
action contains an explicit term

\Lambda\int d^{4}x\,e,

with
\Lambda<0
, together with a gravitino mass term whose coefficient
is fixed by
\Lambda
.
In this case the relation between mass and geometry is more direct:
the background spacetime geometry (AdS) determines the value of the
gravitino mass.

However, even in this case the fermionic geometry of superspace does
not play an explicit role.
The mass parameter is fixed by consistency of the algebra and by the
choice of background, rather than by an invariant constructed from
odd directions of superspace.

5.3

Conceptual differences and complementarities

The supergeometric approach developed in this work differs from the
standard formulations in a key conceptual aspect.
Instead of deriving the gravitino mass from a potential, auxiliary
fields, or a chosen background geometry, we obtain it from a quantity defined through a supergeometric construction
on a superfiber bundle with fiber
C^{0|1}
.

This leads to several noteworthy differences:

•

The gravitino mass appears as a quantity with a well-defined geometric invariant structure,
rather than as a freely adjustable coupling constant.

•

No explicit superpotential or scalar potential is required to
define the mass scale.

•

The construction highlights the role of the fermionic direction
of superspace, which is usually implicit in component formulations.

At the same time, the approach is fully compatible with standard
supergravity.
Once the geometric scalar is identified with the usual mass term, all
physical consequences remain unchanged.
The difference lies in interpretation, not in observable predictions.
Accordingly, the present construction should be read as a structural identification rather than as a dynamical derivation: it does not modify the equations of motion, but reinterprets an existing mass parameter as a supergeometric invariant.

5.4

Equivalence at the level of the action

To make this equivalence explicit, consider the standard supergravity
action with a gravitino mass term:

S=S_{\text{SUGRA}}-\frac{m_{3/2}}{2}\int d^{4}x\,e\,\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}.

Replacing
m_{3/2}
by the geometric scalar
m_{\text{geom}}
constructed
in Section 3 does not alter the structure of the equations of motion,
provided
m_{\text{geom}}
is treated as a constant (or slowly varying)
scalar on
M

Conclusions

In this work we have addressed the gravitino mass term from a deliberately non-standard but fully controlled perspective. Rather than introducing new dynamical mechanisms, model-dependent assumptions, or phenomenological extensions, we have focused on a single foundational question: whether the algebraic structure of the gravitino mass term can be identified as a geometric invariant of superspace itself.

By formulating the theory on a superfiber bundle with odd fiber
\mathbb{C}^{0|1}
, we have shown that a Berezin projection along an odd direction naturally isolates an even fermionic bilinear whose algebraic form coincides with the Rarita–Schwinger mass term
\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}
of four-dimensional
N=1
supergravity. This identification is purely structural. It does not rely on a superpotential, auxiliary fields, background curvature, or explicit supersymmetry-breaking terms, and it does not determine the numerical value of the mass parameter.

The central result of the paper is therefore a sharp separation between structure and dynamics. Superspace geometry fixes the form of the gravitino mass term predynamically, while conventional supergravity mechanisms determine its coefficient. This separation clarifies a point that is often implicit in standard formulations: the gravitino mass is not merely an effective parameter introduced by hand, but reflects an intrinsic feature of the fermionic geometry of superspace.

We have shown that this interpretation is robust and compatible with all conventional supergravity settings. It admits a consistent embedding into full curved superspace, remains stable under coupling to matter multiplets, accommodates inflationary and time-dependent backgrounds, is neutral with respect to the gravitino problem, and fits naturally within string-inspired effective supergravity theories. Moreover, the construction extends straightforwardly to
N>1
supersymmetry, where the gravitino mass parameter generalizes to an internal matrix without altering its geometric origin.

Importantly, no claim has been made regarding the dynamics of supersymmetry breaking, the spectrum of massive gravitini, or the phenomenological implications of specific models. These questions lie at a different logical level and are intentionally left outside the scope of the present work. The contribution of this paper is not to modify supergravity dynamics, but to clarify the geometric origin of a central structural element that appears universally across supergravity theories.

From a broader perspective, the results presented here suggest that certain features traditionally regarded as dynamical inputs may instead be understood as manifestations of superspace geometry. In this sense, the gravitino mass term provides a concrete example of how energy scales in supersymmetric theories can be traced back to geometric invariants, in close analogy with the role of curvature in general relativity.

We conclude that the gravitino mass term should be regarded as a structural feature of superspace rather than as a model-dependent artifact. This perspective does not replace standard supergravity constructions, but complements them by making explicit a geometric fact that is usually left implicit. As such, it provides a clean and conceptually transparent foundation upon which conventional supergravity analyses can be consistently interpreted.

•

Both
\Psi
and
d\Psi
are Grassmann-odd. Their product is therefore antisymmetric and
requires contraction with an antisymmetric tensor to yield a non-vanishing Lorentz scalar.

•

In the geometric setting, antisymmetry is carried by the exterior differential
dx^{\mu}

whereas in the component-field formulation it is encoded in
\gamma^{\mu\nu}
.

•

The operator
d=dx^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}
appearing in
\Psi d\Psi
is not intended to represent a propagating kinetic term in this minimal construction, but rather reflects the algebraic structure of the supergeometric invariant.

A genuine kinetic term would require a structure of the form

\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}
, which is absent here.

The transition from geometry to dynamics therefore amounts to the identification

\Psi\,d_{\mathrm{fib}}\Psi\;\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{struct}\;}\;\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu},

(22)

where the antisymmetry of the wedge product in superspace is mapped, at the level of representation structure,
to the Lorentz–Clifford antisymmetry of
\gamma^{\mu\nu}
in spacetime, and not as a dynamical or operator-level identification.

This identification is not meant as a derivation of the mass term from a kinetic operator,
but as a structural correspondence between the supergeometric bilinear and the standard
algebraic form of the gravitino mass term.

Finally, Berezin integration over the odd fiber removes the residual fermionic structure and
produces a bosonic scalar on
M
. Up to numerical factors and conventions, one obtains

m_{\mathrm{geom}}\sim\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}.

(23)

This shows explicitly that the supergeometric invariant
m_{\mathrm{geom}}
reproduces the
algebraic structure of the gravitino mass term, without introducing propagating derivatives.
The mass thus emerges as a purely geometric scalar associated with the fermionic direction
of the superfiber bundle.

Introduction

Let
\gamma\in C^{N}(0,1]
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large. We define the operator
T
along the dilated plane curve
(ut,u\gamma(t))
as

Tf(x,u):=\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,dt,

which has been extensively studied in works such as Hickman
[
18
]
, Beltran, Guo, Hickman, and Seeger
[
2
]
, Ko, Lee, and Oh
[
24
]
, among others. We also consider the maximal operator

M_{\gamma}f(x):=\sup_{u\in(0,\infty)}\left|Tf(x,u)\right|.

Bourgain
[
6
]
established foundational estimates for maximal averages over planar convex curves. Beltran, Guo, Hickman, and Seeger
[
2
]
, as well as Ko, Lee, and Oh
[
24
]
, demonstrated that the maximal average over a curve in three dimensions is bounded on
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
if and only if
p>3
. For higher-dimensional settings, we refer to Ko, Lee and Oh
[
25
]
. Related studies on maximal functions associated with hypersurfaces can be found in
[
45
,
46
,
21
]
and references therein.

A natural question arises concerning the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
boundedness of
M_{\gamma}
. It is well-established in various contexts that
M_{\gamma}
is not
L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}
bounded unless
p=q
(see Liu and Yu
[
35
, Remark 1.3]
). However, when the supremum is restricted to
u\in[1,2]
, they derived an almost sharp
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
estimate for
M_{\gamma}
, i.e., the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
estimate for
T
. This phenomenon, known as
L^{p}
-improving, is discussed in greater detail in
[
52
,
40
,
1
,
32
]
.

In this paper, we aim to extend the estimates for the maximal function to mixed norm estimates for
T
. Our primary objective is to establish
L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{q}_{x}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
(space-time) estimates for
T
, which is related to
r
-variation estimates for
T
(see
[
4
,
30
]
). When
q=r
, there is already a substantial body of literature addressing this case. For example, when
\gamma(t):=t^{2}
, the
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
boundedness of
T
was essentially established by Strichartz
[
51
]
and later refined by Schlag and Sogge
[
41
]
. Additional results on
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
boundedness for
T
can be found in Gressman
[
14
,
15
]
. More recently, Li, Liu, Lou, and Yu
[
31
]
established almost sharp
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
estimates for
T
along a broader class of curves. In higher dimensions, Hickman
[
18
]
obtained nearly sharp
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[1,2])
estimates for
T
along the moment curve
(t,t^{2},\dots,t^{n})
. He also raised the problem of determining the range of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})
for which mixed norm estimates for
T
hold.

The main purpose of this paper is to consider the mixed norm estimates for
T
in two-dimensional case, but
with some general plane curves including the parabola
\gamma(t):=t^{2}
. Our first result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
\gamma\in C^{N}(0,1]
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\gamma(t)=0
, and
\gamma
is monotonic on
(0,1]
. Additionally, suppose
\gamma
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

there exist positive constants
\{C^{(j)}_{1}\}_{j=1}^{2}
such that
|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\geq C^{(j)}_{1}
for any
t\in(0,1]
;

(ii)

there exist positive constants
\{C^{(j)}_{2}\}_{j=1}^{N}
such that
|\frac{t^{j}\gamma^{(j)}(t)}{\gamma(t)}|\leq C^{(j)}_{2}
for any
t\in(0,1]
.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\gamma
,
p
,
q
and
r
, such that for all
f\in L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

\left\|Tf\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies

\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1}{2r},\frac{3}{p}-\frac{r+2}{r}\right\}<\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}<\frac{r+1}{2r}

and

1+(1+\omega)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)>0

with
r\in[1,\infty]
. Moreover, the range of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
is sharp except for some borderlines for all
r\in[1,\infty]
. Here and hereafter,
\omega:=\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\ln|\gamma(t)|}{\ln t}
.

Another motivation for this paper stems from the study of the maximal function
\mathcal{M}
, defined by averaging over the curve
(t,\gamma(t))
, as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}f(x):=\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\infty)}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{-\epsilon}^{\epsilon}\left|f(x_{1}-t,x_{2}-\gamma(t))\right|\,\mathrm{d}t.

This operator, along with related families, has been extensively studied. For the case
\gamma(t):=t^{2}
, Nagel, Riviere, and Wainger
[
38
]
demonstrated that
\mathcal{M}
is bounded on
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
p\in(1,\infty]
. Stein
[
48
]
extended this result to homogeneous curves, while Stein and Wainger
[
49
]
investigated smooth curves. For more general families of curves, we refer readers to
[
50
,
8
,
9
]
.

Let
\bar{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function supported on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{1}{8}\leq t\leq 8\}
, satisfying
0\leq\bar{\psi}(t)\leq 1
and
\bar{\psi}(t)=1
on
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{1}{4}\leq t\leq 4\}
. Throughout this paper, we denote by
P_{k}
the operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with multiplier
\bar{\psi}_{k}(|\xi|):=\bar{\psi}(2^{-k}|\xi|)
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
. A key estimate required to establish Theorem
1.1
is essentially the following almost sharp space-time estimate for the half-wave propagator:

(1.1)

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{1}{2}k}\left\|e^{iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{-\epsilon k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\epsilon
is a positive constant. It is well known that the solution
g
of the Cauchy problem for the wave equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}

\begin{cases}(\partial^{2}_{u}-\Delta)g(x,u)=0,~~(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R};\\
g(x,0)=f(x),~~\partial_{u}g(x,0)=0,\end{cases}

can be written in terms of the half-wave propagator
e^{iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}f
as

g(x,u)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)+e^{-iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}f(x)\right).

Let
\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote the homogeneous Sobolev space. The following space-time estimate for the half-wave propagator is of particular interest in harmonic analysis:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\left\|e^{iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

In the present paper, we focus on this estimate for all
f\in L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
p\in[1,\infty]
.

The estimate (
1.2
) and its local version have been studied by numerous authors. For instance, when
r=\infty
, (
1.2
) represents a maximal function estimate for the half-wave propagator, which is associated with the pointwise convergence of solutions to their initial data in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Cowling
[
11
]
proved that (
1.2
) holds for all
q=2
and
s>\frac{1}{2}
. Rogers and Villarroya
[
39
]
later extended this result to all
q\in(2,\infty]
. For endpoint estimates and historical remarks, see Cho, Lee, and Li
[
10
]
. When the initial data lies in Sobolev spaces
W_{x}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and
q=p
, (
1.2
) holds for
p\geq\frac{2(n+1)}{n-1}
and
s>(n-1)\left|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|
, as a corollary of Bourgain and Demeter
[
7
]
. The two-dimensional case of this estimate can be found in Guth, Wang, and Zhang
[
16
]
, while an endpoint result in dimensions
n\geq 4
was established by Heo, Nazarov, and Seeger
[
17
]
. When
r=2
and
q=p>\frac{2(n+2)}{n}
, (
1.2
) is closely related to Stein’s square function estimates for Bochner-Riesz operators, as discussed in Lee, Rogers, and Seeger
[
27
]
. Additionally, analogous results for Fourier integral operators can be found in
[
44
,
36
,
37
,
12
]
, and for compact manifolds in Beltran, Hickman, and Sogge
[
3
]
.

It is also natural to consider the time-space estimate for

, commonly referred to as the Strichartz estimate. For the half-wave propagator, this estimate can be expressed as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\|e^{iu\sqrt{-\Delta}}f\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{x}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where it is well-known that (

) holds if

,
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{n}{q}=\frac{n}{2}-s
, and
\frac{2}{r}+\frac{n-1}{q}\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
, with the exception of

when

[
23
]
. When

, (
1.3
) is also connected to a Kakeya-type problem for circles, particularly when the initial data belongs to

[
53
]
. Over the past few decades, extensive literature has been devoted to this problem, including
[

,
28
,
29
,
5
]

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\gamma
be as defined in Theorem

. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
\gamma
,

,

and

, such that for all

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq C\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

provided that
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies

A:

For

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies
\tilde{s}_{C,0}(p,q,r)<0
, where

Conversely, (
1.4
) holds only if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies the following conditions:

(I)

;

(II)

(III)

\frac{1}{q}\geq\max\left\{\frac{2}{p}-1,\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3}\right\}

(IV)

Here, for any

\tilde{s}_{A,0}(p,q,r):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}-\frac{1}{r},&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}+\left(\frac{1}{2p}+\frac{1}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{q}{r},&\text{for }p^{\prime}<q<3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-1,&\text{for }q\leq p^{\prime}.\end{cases}

The main contribution of this paper is to establish the optimal range of
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r}\right)
for the space-time estimate of

, as stated in Theorem

determines all other ranges of

. To achieve this result we need the sharp estimate for

\displaystyle F_{j,k}f(x,u):=\Omega(x,u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})}m_{j,k}(u,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,d\xi,

defined in (

generalize the results of Mockenhaupt, Seeger, and Sogge
[

[

when we restrict our attention to

.

It is evident that the time-space estimate for
T
differs from the corresponding space-time estimate. Firstly, the latter is translation-invariant when the temporal integral is evaluated before the spatial integral, but not the former. Secondly, as stated in Subsection 4.1, the time-space estimate for
T
not always can be reduced to the corresponding time-space estimate similar to (
1.1
). Thirdly, we can only obtain a common estimate

\|F_{j,k}f\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all

, not as the corresponding space-time estimate can be improved directly when

; see (

). Our main tools will be interpolation, a fixed time estimate for

based on Seeger, Sogge and Stein
[

, Theorem 2.1]
, Gao, Liu, Miao and Xi
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
and some results stated in Stein
[
47
, Chapters VIII and IX]
.

To establish the necessity part in Theorem
1.4
, we utilized the sharp range of
\left(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q}\right)
in
T:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x,u}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
, not only the sharp range of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
in
T:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])
. We also constructed some examples inspired by previous papers. However, it should be noted that Theorem
1.4
may not be sharp in some case of
p\in\left(\frac{3}{2},2\right)\cup(2,4)
. To be more precise, it follows from Theorem
1.4
that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L^{p}_{u}L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds if
\max\{1+\omega,4\}<p\leq\infty
and only if
\max\{1+\omega,3\}\leq p\leq\infty
. Thus the range of
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{r})
in (
1.4
) is not sharp. Moreover, we can conjecture that the infimum of
p
in (
1.7
) is
\max\{1+\omega,3\}
by the estimate
(2)
established in Wolff
[
53
]
. From Theorem
1.4
, we also note that (
1.7
) is essentially sharp if
\gamma(t):=t^{d}
with
d\geq 3
.

Preliminaries

The proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.4
rely on some results established in previous papers
[
35
,
31
]
. For ease of reading, we provide a sketch of reducing the estimate to a Fourier integral operator in this section. Detailed discussions can be found in
[
35
,
31
]
. We begin by collecting some lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
33
, Lemma 2.2]
or
[
34
, Lemma 2.2]
) Let
\gamma
be defined as in Theorem
1.1
and let
\Gamma_{j}(t):=\frac{\gamma(2^{j}t)}{\gamma(2^{j})}
with
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{-}
and
t\in[\frac{1}{2},2]
. The following inequalities hold uniformly in
j
:

(i)

e^{-C^{(1)}_{2}}\leq\Gamma_{j}(t)\leq e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}
;

(ii)

\frac{C^{(1)}_{1}}{2e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}}\leq|\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}(t)|\leq 2e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}C^{(1)}_{2}
;

(iii)

\frac{C^{(2)}_{1}}{4e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}}\leq|\Gamma_{j}^{\prime\prime}(t)|\leq 4e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}C^{(2)}_{2}
;

(iv)

|\Gamma_{j}^{(k)}(t)|\leq 2^{k}e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}C^{(k)}_{2}
for all
2\leq k\leq N
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
;

(v)

|((\Gamma_{j}^{\prime})^{-1})^{(k)}(t)|\lesssim 1
for all
0\leq k<N
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
(\Gamma_{j}^{\prime})^{-1}
is the inverse function of
\Gamma_{j}^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
43
, Lemma 2.4.2]
) Suppose that
F
is
C^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then, if
q>1
,

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in[1,2]}|F(u)|^{q}\leq|F(1)|^{q}+q\left(\int_{1}^{2}|F(u)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{1}^{2}|F^{\prime}(u)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}u\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
35
, Lemma 2.3]
) Recall that
\omega=\limsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\ln|\gamma(t)|}{\ln t}
. Then, for all
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfying
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}
and
1+(1+\omega)(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})>0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{-}}2^{j}|2^{j}\gamma(2^{j})|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}<\infty.

We consider the space-time estimate for the operator

(2.1)

\displaystyle Tf(x,u)=\int_{0}^{1}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\mathrm{d}t,

where, without loss of generality, we assume
f\geq 0
. Let
\psi
be a smooth and positive function supported on the interval
\{t\in\mathbb{R}:\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 2\}
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{j}(t)=1
for all
t>0
,
where
\psi_{j}(t):=\psi(2^{-j}t)
. For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

\delta_{j}f(x_{1},x_{2}):=f(2^{j}x_{1},\gamma(2^{j})x_{2})
. Note that

|2^{j}\gamma(2^{j})|^{\frac{1}{q}}\|\delta_{j}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
,
it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{-}}2^{j}|2^{j}\gamma(2^{j})|^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\widetilde{T}_{j}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 1,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{j}f(x,u):=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\Gamma_{j}(t))\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

and the definition of
\Gamma_{j}
can be found in Lemma
2.1
. Consequently, by Lemma
2.3
, the proof reduces to establishing the estimate

\widetilde{T}_{j}:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])

uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{-}
.

Let
\Psi^{0}:=\sum_{k\leq 0}\psi_{k}
and define

\displaystyle H_{j}(u,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iu\xi_{1}t-iu\xi_{2}\Gamma_{j}(t)}\psi(t)\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
. We decompose
\widetilde{T}_{j}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{j}f(x,u)=\widetilde{T}^{0}_{j}f(x,u)+\sum_{k\geq 1}\widetilde{T}_{j,k}f(x,u),

where the multipliers of
\widetilde{T}^{0}_{j}
and
\widetilde{T}_{j,k}
are
H_{j}(u,\xi)\Psi^{0}(u|\xi|)
and
H_{j}(u,\xi)\psi_{k}(u|\xi|)
, respectively. For
\widetilde{T}^{0}_{j}
, by Lemma
2.1
, we can obtain that
|\partial_{u}^{\alpha}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}(H_{j}(u,\xi)\Psi^{0}(u|\xi|))|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-3}
for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
with
|\alpha|\leq 1
and
|\beta|\leq 3
, where the implicit constant is independent of
u\in[1,2]
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{-}
. Furthermore, by Lemma
2.2
, Hölder’s inequality, and Young’s inequality, it follows that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{0}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq\left\|\widetilde{T}^{0}_{j}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
.

For
\widetilde{T}_{j,k}
, we further split it into
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
and
\widetilde{T}^{2}_{j,k}
, where the multipliers of
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
and
\widetilde{T}^{2}_{j,k}
are

\rho\left(\frac{|\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{2}|}\right)H_{j}(u,\xi)\psi_{k}(u|\xi|)~~\textrm{and}~~\left(1-\rho\left(\frac{|\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{2}|}\right)\right)H_{j}(u,\xi)\psi_{k}(u|\xi|),

respectively. Here,
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is a smooth cutoff function satisfying
\rho=1
on the interval

[\frac{C^{(1)}_{1}}{10e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}},10e^{C^{(1)}_{2}}C^{(1)}_{2}].

Then, as in (
2.2
), by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we obtain the decay estimate

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{2}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{k}{q^{\prime}}}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
. Therefore, it suffices to analyze the operator
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
.

Let the phase function in
H_{j}(u,\xi)
be

\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t):=-u\xi_{1}t-u\xi_{2}\Gamma_{j}(t)
.
We focus on the case where there exists a critical point
t_{0}\in\left(\frac{1}{2},2\right)
of
\varphi_{j}
. By the method of stationary phase, we write

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})}m_{j,k}(u,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where

\displaystyle m_{j,k}(u,\xi):=\rho\left(\frac{|\xi_{1}|}{|\xi_{2}|}\right)\psi_{k}(u|\xi|)\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-iu\xi_{2}t^{2}\eta_{j}(t,t_{0})}\psi(t+t_{0})\,\mathrm{d}t,

with

\eta_{j}(t,t_{0}):=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})}{2}+\frac{t}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)^{2}\Gamma^{\prime\prime\prime}_{j}(\theta t+t_{0})\,\mathrm{d}\theta
.
Moreover, for all
(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
|\alpha|+|\beta|<N
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{u}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}m_{j,k}(u,\xi)\right|\lesssim(1+|\xi|)^{-\frac{1}{2}-|\alpha|}

uniformly in
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
.

We can also express
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f(x,u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x-y,u)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

where the kernel is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})}m_{j,k}(u,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Furthermore, we split
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
into the sum of
\widetilde{T}^{1,a}_{j,k}
and
\widetilde{T}^{1,b}_{j,k}
, whose kernels are

\chi_{B^{\complement}(0,\varpi)}(x)\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u)

and

\chi_{B(0,\varpi)}(x)\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u),

respectively. Here,
\varpi
is a positive constant chosen large enough such that

\left|\nabla_{\xi}[(x-y)\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})]\right|\gtrsim|x-y|

when
|x-y|\geq\varpi
. The desired decay estimate for
\widetilde{T}^{1,a}_{j,k}
can be obtained via integration by parts. It then suffices to consider
\widetilde{T}^{1,b}_{j,k}
.

Let
\Omega:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative smooth function such that
\Omega(x,u)=1
on

\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}:|x|\leq\varpi,~u\in[1,2]\}

and vanishes outside

\{(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}:|x|\leq 2\varpi,~u\in(\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{5}{2})\}.

Define

(2.6)

\displaystyle F_{j,k}f(x,u):=\Omega(x,u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x-y,u)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle E_{j,k}f(x,u):=\Omega(x,u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\chi_{B(0,\varpi)}(x-y)\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x-y,u)f(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

To estimate
\widetilde{T}^{1,b}_{j,k}
, as in
[
35
]
, it suffices to obtain a corresponding decay space-time estimate for
F_{j,k}
. Indeed, we partition
\mathbb{R}^{2}
into
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}B(x_{i},\varpi)
such that
|x_{i}-x_{i^{\prime}}|\approx|i-i^{\prime}|\varpi
for any
i\neq i^{\prime}
. Then,
\|\widetilde{T}^{1,b}_{j,k}f\|^{q}_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}
is bounded from above by

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\|\left(E_{j,k}-F_{j,k}\right)\left(f(\cdot-x_{i})\right)\right\|^{q}_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}+\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\left\|F_{j,k}\left(f(\cdot-x_{i})\right)\right\|^{q}_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}.

The first term can be handled by a similar argument as for
\widetilde{T}^{1,a}_{j,k}
, and the second term reduces to a corresponding decay space-time estimate for
F_{j,k}
.

Putting things together, we have reduced our estimate to
F_{j,k}
. We note that

(2.9)

\displaystyle F_{j,k}f(x,u):=\Omega(x,u)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{i\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})}m_{j,k}(u,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

which is a Fourier integral operator of order
-\frac{1}{2}
and satisfies the special assumption that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\text{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\left(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})\right)=1

when
\xi\neq 0
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
, building upon the preliminary results established in Section
2
. The proof is divided into two parts.

3.1
Sufficiency of the region
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
for all
r\in[1,\infty]

We begin the proof with the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\widetilde{K}_{j,k}
defined in (
2.5
) and
N\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
there exists a positive constant
C_{(N,\alpha)}
, depending on
N
and
\alpha
, such that

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{u}^{\alpha}\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u)\right|\leq C_{(N,\alpha)}\frac{2^{(1+\alpha)k}}{\left(1+2^{k}\left||x|-u\right|\right)^{N}}

holds for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
.

Proof.

Note that
t_{0}=(\Gamma^{\prime}_{j})^{-1}(-\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}})\in(\frac{1}{2},2)
. By a change of variables, we can express

\displaystyle\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u)=2^{2k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i2^{k}x\cdot\xi}e^{i2^{k}\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0})}m_{j,k}(u,2^{k}\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

From
\partial_{\xi_{1}}t_{0}=-\frac{1}{\xi_{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})}
and
\partial_{\xi_{2}}t_{0}=\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})}
, a straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))=u\frac{1}{\xi_{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})};\\
&\partial_{\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))=-u\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})};\\
&\partial_{\xi_{2}\xi_{1}}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))=-u\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{2}^{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})};\\
&\partial_{\xi_{2}\xi_{2}}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))=u\frac{\xi_{1}^{2}}{\xi_{2}^{3}}\frac{1}{\Gamma^{\prime\prime}_{j}(t_{0})}.\end{aligned}\right.

Combining this with Lemma
2.1
and the facts that
|\xi|\approx 1
and
|\xi_{1}|\approx|\xi_{2}|
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))\right|\gtrsim 1

for all multi-indices
\beta
with
|\beta|=2
. Noting that
\partial_{\xi}(x\cdot\xi+\varphi_{j}(u,\xi,t_{0}))=x-u(t_{0},\Gamma_{j}(t_{0}))
and
|(t_{0},\Gamma_{j}(t_{0}))|\approx 1
, integration by parts (using Stein
[
47
, Chapter VIII, Section 2, Proposition 5]
and (
2.4
)) yields

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u)\right|\lesssim\frac{2^{k}}{\left(1+2^{k}\left||x|-u\right|\right)^{N}}.

A simple calculation provides the corresponding estimate for
|\partial_{u}^{\alpha}\widetilde{K}_{j,k}(x,u)|
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
G(x,u)\in C_{c}^{1}(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
with respect to
u
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, for any
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\left\|G\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|G\right\|^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\left\|\partial_{u}G\right\|^{\frac{1}{r}}_{L_{x}^{q}L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

For
r=\infty
, the result is trivial. For
r\in[1,\infty)
, note that

\displaystyle|G(x,u)|^{r}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|G(x,v)|^{r-1}|\partial_{v}G(x,v)|\,\mathrm{d}v

holds for all
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle\left\|G(x,u)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left(\left\|G(x,u)\right\|^{r-1}_{L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R})}\left\|\partial_{u}G(x,u)\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality again yields the desired estimate. This completes the proof.
∎

We now proceed to prove the sufficiency part of Theorem
1.1
. From Lemma
3.1
, it follows that
\|\widetilde{K}_{j,k}\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim 1
uniformly in
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
. Combining this with Young’s inequality, we obtain

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Additionally, Lemma
3.1
implies

(3.2)

\displaystyle\|F_{j,k}f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This bound can be refined by considering the
L^{1}
-norm in
u
. Specifically, we observe that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\frac{2^{k}}{\left(1+2^{k}\left||x-y|-u\right|\right)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}u\lesssim 1

uniformly in
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, which further implies

(3.3)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{u}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Similarly, by

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2^{k}}{\left(1+2^{k}\left||x-y|-u\right|\right)^{N}}\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim 1

uniformly in
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
, we conclude that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}L_{u}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

From
[
35
, (2.44)]
, for any
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{5}L_{u}^{5}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{(\epsilon-\frac{1}{5})k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Furthermore, by Lemma
2.2
or Lemma
3.2
, we find that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{5}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\epsilon k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{5}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for any
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
. On the other hand, by (
2.4
), it is a consequence of Plancherel’s theorem that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{u}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{1}{2}k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Combining this with Lemma
2.2
and (
2.4
), we obtain

(3.7)

\begin{split}\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}&\lesssim\left\|F_{j,k}f(x,1)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}+\left(\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{u}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\left\|\partial_{u}(F_{j,k}f)\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}L_{u}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.\end{split}

From Figure
1
, for all
r\in[1,\infty]
, it suffices to obtain a constant bound
C
or a small bound
2^{\epsilon_{1}k}
at points
\{\text{O, A, B, C, D}\}
for any
\epsilon_{1}\in(0,\infty)
, and a decay bound
2^{-\epsilon_{2}k}
for
F_{j,k}
at
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})
with some
\epsilon_{2}\in(0,\infty)
. More precisely, we first claim the following:

\bullet

Constant bound at point
O: For any
r\in[1,\infty]
, by Hölder’s inequality and (
3.1
), it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{u}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

\bullet

Constant bound at point
A: It follows from interpolation between the
L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{1}L_{u}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
and
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\rightarrow L_{x}^{2}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
estimates of
F_{j,k}
established in (
3.4
) and (
3.7
), respectively.

\bullet

Constant bound at point
B: For any
r\in[1,\infty]
, from (
3.7
) and (
3.3
), reiterating this interpolation process, then the following estimate is valid:

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2r}{r-1}}L_{u}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{2r}{r+1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

This is the desired estimate at the point
B
.

\bullet

Small bound at point
C: For any
r\in[1,\infty]
, a further interpolation between (
3.5
) and (
3.3
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{5r}{r-1}}L_{u}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\frac{\epsilon}{r^{\prime}}k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{5r}{2r+3}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for any
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
.

\bullet

Constant bound at point
D: For any
r\in[1,\infty]
, interpolation between (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L_{u}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Therefore, we reduce our proof to obtaining a decay bound at
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})
. For any
r\in[1,\infty]
, by Hölder’s inequality, it is easy to see that

\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{4}L_{u}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{4}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}.

Then, we just need to prove

(3.8)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{j,k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{4}L_{u}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\left(-\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon\right)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for any
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
. It is easy to see that the desired decay bound at
(\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})
can be obtained by letting
\epsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
in (
3.8
).

To obtain (
3.8
), we first establish the space-time estimates for
Fourier integral operators
, defined on the space of Schwartz functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, of the form

(3.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}f(z):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\phi(z,\xi)}a(z,\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

where
a(z,\xi)
is a symbol of order
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. Here and hereafter,
z
denotes a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
comprising the space-time variables
(x,u)
. Assume that
\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
is contained in a fixed compact set and
\phi(z,\cdot)
is homogeneous of degree 1. We say that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies the
cinematic curvature condition
if the following hold:

\blacksquare

Non-degeneracy condition
: For all
(z,\xi)\in\textrm{supp}~a
,

\displaystyle\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{z\xi}\phi(z,\xi)=n.

\blacksquare

Cone condition
: Consider the Gauss map
G:\textrm{supp}~a\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n}
defined by

\displaystyle G(z,\xi):=\frac{G_{0}(z,\xi)}{|G_{0}(z,\xi)|},\quad\text{where}\quad G_{0}(z,\xi):=\bigwedge_{j=1}^{n}\partial_{\xi_{j}}\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi).

The curvature condition

\displaystyle\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi),G(z,\xi_{0})\rangle\big|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}=n-1

holds for all
(z,\xi_{0})\in\textrm{supp}~a
.

We now establish the following two lemmas, which will be used repeatedly in the remaining parts of this paper.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
P_{k}
be the Littlewood-Paley operator defined in Section
1
, and
\mathcal{F}
be the Fourier integral operator defined in (
3.9
) with a symbol of order
\sigma
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, together with the special assumption that
\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi(z,\xi)=n-1
for
\xi\neq 0
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{(u)}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{(u)}2^{sk}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
s\geq\bar{s}_{n,\sigma}(p,q)
and
\bar{s}_{n,\sigma}(p,q)
is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{s}_{n,\sigma}(p,q):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{n}{q}+\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma,&\text{for }q>p^{\prime};\\
\frac{n}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma,&\text{for }q\leq p^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Indeed, this lemma can be followed from
[
31
, Proposition 3.2]
by interpolation. Here, we give another proof, which can be also applied to some other estimates for
\mathcal{F}
.

The case
q=p\in(1,\infty)
has been proved in Seeger, Sogge and Stein
[
42
, Theorem 2.1]
. Then, by interpolation, it suffices to prove

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{(u)}2^{(\frac{n+1}{2}+\sigma)k}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(3.11)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{(u)}2^{(\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma)k}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

and

(3.12)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{(u)}2^{(\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma)k}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

To establish (
3.10
), by changing variables, we express the kernel of
\mathcal{F}P_{k}
as

\displaystyle K_{k}(z,y):=2^{nk}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i2^{k}(\phi(z,\xi)-y\cdot\xi)}a(z,2^{k}\xi)\bar{\psi}(|\xi|)\,\textrm{d}\xi.

Without loss of generality, we may suppose that

\textrm{rank}~\partial_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi(z,\xi)=n-1
where
\xi:=(\xi_{1},\xi^{\prime})
with
\xi^{\prime}:=(\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n})
is perpendicular to
\xi_{1}
.
By partition of unity, we can further suppose that
\bar{\psi}=0
if
|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-e_{1}|\geq\frac{1}{10^{2}}
and, for all
z\in\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there is at most one
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
satisfying
|v-e_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{9^{2}}
such that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,v)=y,

where
e_{1}:=(1,0,\cdots,0)\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. If such
v
does not exist, then the phase function in
K_{k}
has no critical point. Moreover, by homogeneity, it follows that

\displaystyle|K_{k}(z,y)|\leq C_{(u,N)}2^{-Nk}

if
N
is large enough, which further leads to (
3.10
).

Now, we suppose that there exists such
v
. Without loss of generality we may assume that
v=e_{1}
, otherwise we can choose new axes so that
v=e_{1}
. Since
\phi(z,\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi
,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi)\cdot\xi=\phi(z,\xi);

hence
\nabla_{\xi}(\partial_{\xi_{i}}\phi)(z,\xi)\cdot\xi=0
for any
i=1,2,\cdots,n
, furthermore,
\partial^{2}_{\xi_{i}\xi_{1}}\phi(z,e_{1})=0
holds for all
i=1,2,\cdots,n
. This, combined with he special assumption that
\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}\phi(z,\xi)=n-1
when
\xi\neq 0
, leads to

(3.14)

\displaystyle\det\partial_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi(z,e_{1})\neq 0.

It is easy to see that, for any
i,j=1,2,\cdots,n
,
\partial^{2}_{\xi_{i}\xi_{j}}\phi(z,\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi
and
\partial_{\xi_{i}}\phi(z,\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
0
in
\xi
. Let
\phi_{\xi_{1}}(z,\xi^{\prime}):=\phi(z,\xi)
. Then, for all
|(\xi_{1},0)|\in\textrm{supp}~\bar{\psi}
, by (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we can see that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}}\phi_{\xi_{1}}(z,0)=\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}}\phi(z,(\xi_{1},0))=\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}}\phi(z,e_{1})=y^{\prime},

and

\displaystyle\det\partial_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi_{\xi_{1}}(z,0)=\det\partial_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi(z,(\xi_{1},0))=|\xi_{1}|^{-(n-1)}\det\partial_{\xi^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}}^{2}\phi(z,e_{1})\neq 0,

where
y:=(y_{1},y^{\prime})
. Therefore, from Stein
[
47
, Chapter VIII, Proposition 6]
, there exists a constant
C
such that the following identity is valid:

(3.15)

\displaystyle K_{k}(z,y)

\displaystyle=2^{nk}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}e^{i2^{k}(\phi(z,\xi)-y\cdot\xi)}a(z,2^{k}\xi)\bar{\psi}(|\xi|)\,\textrm{d}\xi^{\prime}\,\textrm{d}\xi_{1}

\displaystyle=C2^{\frac{n+1}{2}k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{k}\left(\phi(z,(\xi_{1},0))-y_{1}\xi_{1}\right)}a\left(z,(2^{k}\xi_{1},0)\right)\bar{\psi}(|\xi_{1}|)\,\textrm{d}\xi_{1}+O\left(2^{\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\right),

which further leads to
|K_{k}(z,y)|\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{n+1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}
and (
3.10
) follows easily.

We now turn to the proof of (
3.11
), which is based on some results obtained in Stein
[
47
, Chapter IX, Section 4]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be the Fourier integral operator with a symbol of order
-\frac{n-1}{2}
, it is enough to prove

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{(u)}\left\|f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which can be reduced to establish

(3.16)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{k}(z,y)\,\textrm{d}y\right|\leq C_{(u)},

where the kernel of
\mathcal{F}P_{k}
is given by

\displaystyle K_{k}(z,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\phi(z,\xi)-y\cdot\xi)}a(z,\xi)\bar{\psi}_{k}(|\xi|)\,\textrm{d}\xi.

Next, a further dyadic division is needed. Roughly speaking, we will split each dyadic shell
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{k-3}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+3}\}
into thin truncated cones of aperture roughly
2^{-\frac{k}{2}}
, and each such truncated cone is essentially an elongated rectangle whose major axis has length roughly
2^{k}
, while all the other sides have length roughly
2^{\frac{k}{2}}
. Then, we need roughly
2^{\frac{n-1}{2}k}
such truncated cones to cover the shell
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:2^{k-3}\leq|\xi|\leq 2^{k+3}\}
(see Stein
[
47
, Chapter IX, Figure 1]
). More precisely, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we fix a collection
\{\xi_{k}^{\nu}\}_{\nu}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
satisfy:

(1)

|\xi_{k}^{\nu}-\xi_{k}^{\nu^{\prime}}|\geq 2^{-\frac{k}{2}}
, if
\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
;

(2)

If
\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then there exists a
\xi_{k}^{\nu}
so that
|\xi-\xi_{k}^{\nu}|<2^{-\frac{k}{2}}
.

Let

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}^{\nu}:=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\left|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-\xi_{k}^{\nu}\right|\leq 2^{1-\frac{k}{2}}\right\},

which is a cone with central direction
\xi_{k}^{\nu}
. We can then construct an associated partition of unity

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu}\chi_{k}^{\nu}(\xi)=1

for all
\xi\neq 0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\chi_{k}^{\nu}
is homogeneous of degree
0
in
\xi
, supported in
\Gamma_{k}^{\nu}
and satisfy
|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\chi_{k}^{\nu}(\xi)|\leq C_{(\alpha)}2^{\frac{|\alpha|}{2}k}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.

By the definition of
\chi_{k}^{\nu}
, let

\displaystyle K^{\nu}_{k}(z,y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\phi(z,\xi)-y\cdot\xi)}a(z,\xi)\bar{\psi}_{k}(|\xi|)\chi_{k}^{\nu}(\xi)\,\textrm{d}\xi.

Consequently,

(3.17)

\displaystyle K_{k}=\sum_{\nu}K^{\nu}_{k}.

Furthermore, we choose axes in the
\xi
-space so that
\xi_{1}
is in the direction of
\xi_{k}^{\nu}
and write

\displaystyle K^{\nu}_{k}(z,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y)\cdot\xi}b_{k}^{\nu}(z,\xi)\,\textrm{d}\xi,

where

\displaystyle b_{k}^{\nu}(z,\xi):=e^{i(\phi(z,\xi)-\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})\cdot\xi)}a(z,\xi)\bar{\psi}_{k}(|\xi|)\chi_{k}^{\nu}(\xi).

We define the following operator

\displaystyle L:=I-2^{2k}\partial^{2}_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}-2^{k}\nabla_{\xi^{\prime}}.

As in Stein
[
47
, Chapter IX, Section 4]
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle L^{N}\left(e^{i(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y)\cdot\xi}\right)=\left[1+2^{2k}\left|\left(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y\right)_{1}\right|^{2}+2^{k}\left|\left(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y\right)^{\prime}\right|^{2}\right]^{N}e^{i(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y)\cdot\xi},

where
(\cdot)_{1}
denotes the component in the direction
\xi_{k}^{\nu}
and
(\cdot)^{\prime}
denotes the orthogonal component. Moreover, by the fact that
\mathcal{F}
has a symbol of order
-\frac{n-1}{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle L^{N}\left(b_{k}^{\nu}(z,\xi)\right)\leq C_{(u,N)}2^{-\frac{n-1}{2}k}.

Notice that the support of
b_{k}^{\nu}(z,\cdot)
has volume roughly
2^{(1+\frac{n-1}{2})k}
, pass the operator
L
onto
K^{\nu}_{k}
, we deduce that

(3.18)

\displaystyle\left|K^{\nu}_{k}(z,y)\right|\leq C_{(u,N)}2^{k}\left[1+2^{k}\left|\left(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y\right)_{1}\right|+2^{\frac{k}{2}}\left|\left(\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})-y\right)^{\prime}\right|\right]^{-2N}.

If we take
N
large enough, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K^{\nu}_{k}(z,y)\,\textrm{d}y\right|\leq C_{(u)}2^{-\frac{n-1}{2}k}.

This, combined with (
3.17
) and the fact that there are essentially
2^{\frac{n-1}{2}k}
terms involved, leads to (
3.16
), as desired.

The proof of (
3.12
) is very similar to that of (
3.11
). Indeed, the change of variables
x\rightarrow\nabla_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi_{k}^{\nu})
in (
3.18
), whose Jacobian is bounded from below, leads to

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K^{\nu}_{k}(z,y)\,\textrm{d}x\right|\leq C_{(u)}2^{-\frac{n-1}{2}k}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{k}(z,y)\,\textrm{d}x\right|\leq C_{(u)},

which implies (
3.12
). The proof is thus complete.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
n=2
,
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
P_{k}
be the Littlewood-Paley operator defined in Section
1
, and
\mathcal{F}
be a Fourier integral operator as in (
3.9
) with a symbol of order
\sigma
satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, and assume
\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\phi(z,\xi)=1
for
\xi\neq 0
. Then, for
P_{k}
defined as in Section
1
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}L^{q}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq C2^{sk}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
s\geq s_{\sigma,\epsilon}(p,q,q)
and, for any
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle s_{\sigma,\epsilon}(p,q,q):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}+\frac{1}{2}+\sigma+\epsilon,&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{3}{2p}-\frac{3}{2q}+\sigma+\epsilon,&\text{for }p^{\prime}<q<3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma,&\text{for }q\leq p^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof.

For any
\epsilon>0
, by interpolation, the key estimates we shall make are:

(a)

\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma+\epsilon\right)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
;

(b)

\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}L^{\infty}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma+\epsilon\right)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
;

(c)

\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{1}L^{1}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
;

(d)

\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}L^{2}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{\sigma k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
;

(e)

\left\|\mathcal{F}P_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{4}L^{4}_{u}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim 2^{(\sigma+\epsilon)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
.

The proofs of estimates
(\textrm{a})-(\textrm{d})
follow directly from Lemma
3.3
, since
C_{(u)}\lesssim 1
for any
u\in[1,2]
. On the other hand, we want to show that Lee
[
26
, Corollary 1.5]
also implies the estimates
(\textrm{a})-(\textrm{b})
, where the following additional condition is needed:

all nonzero eigenvalues of
\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi),G(z,\xi_{0})\rangle\big|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}
have the same sign.

However, this additional condition is clearly satisfied, since the cone condition

\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi),G(z,\xi_{0})\rangle\big|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}=1

holds for all
(z,\xi_{0})\in\textrm{supp}~a
when
n=2
, which further implies that there is only one nonzero eigenvalue of
\partial^{2}_{\xi\xi}\langle\partial_{z}\phi(z,\xi),G(z,\xi_{0})\rangle\big|_{\xi=\xi_{0}}
, and so has the same sign.

The estimate
(\textrm{c})
can also be proved in the similar way as (
3.10
). Indeed, by the fact that
\phi(z,\xi)
is homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi
, we can write (
3.15
) as

\displaystyle K_{k}(z,y)=C2^{\frac{3}{2}k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{i2^{k}\left(\phi(z,e_{1})-y_{1}\right)\xi_{1}}a\left(z,(2^{k}\xi_{1},0)\right)\bar{\psi}(|\xi_{1}|)\,\textrm{d}\xi_{1}+O\left(2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\right),

when
n=2
. Then, interpolation by parts shows that

\displaystyle|K_{k}(z,y)|\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma\right)k}(1+2^{k}|\phi(z,e_{1})-y_{1}|)^{-N}+2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k},

where
N\in\mathbb{N}
is large enough. Furthermore, from Mockenhaupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
36
, P. 70]
, by the above non-degeneracy condition, we can choose a locally coordinates
z=(x,u)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\textrm{rank}~\partial^{2}_{x\xi}\phi(z,\xi)=2,

and, if
\xi\neq 0
,

\displaystyle\partial_{u}\phi(z,\xi)\neq 0.

It follows that
|\partial_{u}\phi(z,e_{1})|\gtrsim 1
for all
z\in\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
. Therefore,
via a change of variables
u\rightarrow\phi(z,e_{1})
, for any
z\in\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
, we assert that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|K_{k}(z,y)|\,\textrm{d}u

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+2^{k}|\phi(z,e_{1})-y_{1}|)^{-N}\,\textrm{d}u+2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{3}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+2^{k}|u-y_{1}|)^{-N}\,\textrm{d}u+2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}\lesssim 2^{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma\right)k}.

This, combined with the fact that
\textrm{supp}~a(\cdot,\xi)
is contained in a fixed compact set, leads to the estimate
(\textrm{c})
.

The estimate
(\textrm{d})
is a basic result about the Fourier integral operators
\mathcal{F}
, which can be proved by considering the pseudo-differential operator
\mathcal{F}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}
, we refer the reader to
\textrm{H}\ddot{\textrm{o}}\textrm{rmander}

[
20
]
, Stein
[
47
, Chapter IX, Section 3]
or Sogge
[
43
, Section 6.2]
for more detailed discussions. The key estimate
(\textrm{e})
follows from Gao, Liu, Miao and Xi
[
12
, Theorem 1.4]
, which is a generalization of the local smoothing conjecture in
2+1
dimensions obtained in Guth, Wang and Zhang
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

From
[

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L^{p}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\tilde{s}_{B,\epsilon}(p,q,p)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\tilde{s}_{B,\epsilon}(p,q,q)k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Thus, for all
1\leq r<p\leq q\leq\infty
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}f\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{sk}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
s\geq\tilde{s}_{C,\epsilon}(p,q,r)
and

\tilde{s}_{C,\epsilon}(p,q,r):=\min\left\{\tilde{s}_{B,\epsilon}(p,q,p),\tilde{s}_{B,\epsilon}(p,q,q)\right\}.

Notice that

\tilde{s}_{C,0}(p,q,r)=\min\left\{\tilde{s}_{A,0}(p,p,p)+2\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right),\tilde{s}_{A,0}(p,q,q)\right\}.

Consequently, if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies
\tilde{s}_{C,0}(p,q,r)<0
, we establish the desired decay time-space estimate for
\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}
, and the corresponding time-space estimate for
T
follows.

Remark 4.1
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, a computation yields

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{C,\epsilon}(p,q,r)=\begin{cases}-\frac{2}{q}+\epsilon,&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime}\text{ and }\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{1}{4};\\
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{q}-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon,&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime}\text{ and }\frac{1}{4}<\frac{1}{p}<\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q};\\
\frac{1}{p}-\frac{3}{q}+\epsilon,&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime}\text{ and }\frac{1}{p}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q};\\
\frac{3}{2p}-\frac{3}{2q}-\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon,&\text{for }p^{\prime}<q<3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{2}{p}-\frac{1}{q}-1+\epsilon,&\text{for }q\leq p^{\prime}.\end{cases}

If
\tilde{s}_{C,0}(p,q,r)<0
, then (
4.8
) holds with a decay in
k
, where
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
belongs to the region with vertices
(0,0)
,
(\frac{1}{4},0)
,
(\frac{3}{8},\frac{1}{8})
,
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
,
(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})
, and
(1,1)
. Furthermore, the point
(\frac{3}{8},\frac{1}{8})
can be removed by interpolation between
(\frac{1}{4},0)
and
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
. Thus, (
4.8
) holds with some
s<0
if
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q}>\frac{2}{3p}-\frac{1}{6},&\text{for }q\geq 3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3},&\text{for }p^{\prime}<q<3p^{\prime};\\
\frac{1}{q}>\frac{2}{p}-1,&\text{for }q\leq p^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Combining
1\leq r<p\leq q\leq\infty
with the necessary condition
1+(1+\omega)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)>0

(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
in Theorem
1.4

5

1\leq r\leq\frac{3}{2}
, and the figures for other ranges of
r
can be constructed similarly.

4.2
Necessary Part

In this subsection, we establish necessary conditions. We begin with a lemma analogous to Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
G(x,u)\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
with respect to
u
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then, for any
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\left\|G\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|G\right\|^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\left\|\partial_{u}G\right\|^{\frac{1}{r}}_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}.

Proof.

It suffices to consider
r\in[1,\infty)
. Observe that for all
u\in\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|G(x,u)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{r}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|G(x,v)\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{r-1}\left|\partial_{v}\|G(x,v)\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right|\,\mathrm{d}v.

Applying Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|G(x,u)\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}^{r}\lesssim\left\|G(x,u)\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}^{r-1}\left\|\partial_{u}\|G(x,u)\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}(\mathbb{R})}.

Combining this with the inequality

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{u}\|G(x,u)\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\right|\leq\|\partial_{u}G(x,u)\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

for all
u\in(\frac{1}{2},\frac{5}{2})
, which follows from Hölder’s inequality, yields the desired estimate. This completes the proof.
∎

Now, assume that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}

holds for some
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
.

4.2.1
Proof of (I) in Theorem
1.4

We restrict our attention to the case
q\in[1,\infty)
. Let
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1
. Since
T
is translation-invariant for any fixed
u\in[1,2]
, it follows from (
4.9
) that

\displaystyle\left\|Tf(x-h,u)+Tf(x,u)\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq\Theta\|f(x-h)+f(x)\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
h\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\Theta:=\sup_{f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=1}\|Tf\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}<\infty
. Letting
|h|\to\infty
and applying Grafakos
[
13
, Exercise 2.5.1]
, we deduce

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{1}{q}}\left\|Tf(x,u)\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq\liminf_{|h|\to\infty}\left\|Tf(x-h,u)+Tf(x,u)\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\Theta\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Thus,
\Theta\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\Theta
, which is impossible unless
q\geq p
or
T
is the zero operator. This contradiction establishes
\frac{1}{q}\leq\frac{1}{p}
, completing the proof of (I) of Theorem
1.4
.

4.2.2
Proof of (II) of Theorem
1.4

As in Subsection
3.2
, if (
4.9
) holds, then

\displaystyle\left\|Tf\right\|_{L^{1}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\left|\frac{S_{t_{i}}}{2}\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\leq\left\|\frac{1}{\delta t_{i}}\int_{\frac{S_{t_{i}}}{2}}T\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}(x,u)\,\mathrm{d}x\right\|_{L^{1}_{u}([1,2])}\lesssim\frac{|S_{t_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}{t_{i}}\left\|T\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\frac{|S_{t_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}{t_{i}}\left\|\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Using the facts that
|S_{t_{i}}|\approx t_{i}|\gamma(t_{i})|
and
\|\chi_{S_{t_{i}}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx(t_{i}|\gamma(t_{i})|)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, we obtain (
3.24
). This implies
1+(1+\omega)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq 0
, proving (II) of Theorem
1.4
.

4.2.3
Proof of (III) of Theorem
1.4

This proof is inspired by examples in
[
31
]
. For any
u\in[1,2]
and
\epsilon>0
, let
D_{u}
be the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of the curve
(ut,u\gamma(t))
with
t\in(0,1]
. As in
[
31
]
, there exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
T(\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)})(x,u)\geq\epsilon\chi_{D_{u}}(x)
. Since
|D_{u}|\approx\epsilon
for all
u\in[1,2]
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|T(\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)})\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\geq\epsilon\left\|\chi_{D_{u}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\gtrsim\epsilon^{1+\frac{1}{q}},

which implies

\displaystyle\left\|T(\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)})\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\gtrsim\epsilon^{1+\frac{1}{q}}.

Combining this with
\|Tf\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
and
\|\chi_{B(0,\delta\epsilon)}\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx\epsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}
, we obtain
\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{2}{p}+1}\lesssim 1
for all
\epsilon>0
. For sufficiently small
\epsilon
, this requires
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{2}{p}-1
.

On the other hand, for
\epsilon>0
, define

\displaystyle Q:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|x\cdot e_{1}|\leq 6\epsilon\text{ and }|x\cdot e_{2}|\leq[3+2|\gamma^{\prime\prime}(1)|]\epsilon^{2}\right\},

where
e_{1}:=\frac{(-1,-\gamma^{\prime}(1))}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}
and
e_{2}:=\frac{(-\gamma^{\prime}(1),1)}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}
. Additionally, define

\displaystyle\Omega_{u_{i}}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\left|\left(x-(u_{i},u_{i})\right)\cdot e_{1}\right|\leq\epsilon\text{ and }\left|\left(x-(u_{i},u_{i})\right)\cdot e_{2}\right|\leq\epsilon^{2}\right\},

where
\{u_{i}\}\subset[1,2]
satisfies
|u_{i+1}-u_{i}|=\frac{4\epsilon^{2}}{|(1,1)\cdot e_{2}|}
for all
i
. Moreover, for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, we obtained in
[
31
]
that

\displaystyle T\chi_{Q}(x,u)\geq\int_{1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}}^{1}\chi_{Q}(x_{1}-ut,x_{2}-u\gamma(t))\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1+\gamma^{\prime}(1)^{2}}}

for all
x\in\Omega_{u_{i}}
and
u\in(u_{i}-\epsilon^{2},u_{i}+\epsilon^{2})
. Since
\Omega_{u_{i}}\cap\Omega_{u_{i^{\prime}}}=\emptyset
for
i\neq i^{\prime}
and the number of elements in
\{u_{i}\}\subset[1,2]
is equivalent to
\epsilon^{-2}
, we conclude

\displaystyle\left\|T\chi_{Q}\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\geq\left\{\sum_{i}\int_{u_{i}-\epsilon^{2}}^{u_{i}+\epsilon^{2}}\left[\int_{\Omega_{u_{i}}}\left|T\chi_{Q}\right|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]^{\frac{r}{q}}\,\mathrm{d}u\right\}^{\frac{1}{r}}\gtrsim\epsilon^{1+\frac{3}{q}}.

Combining this with
\|\chi_{Q}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\approx\epsilon^{\frac{3}{p}}
and
\|Tf\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2])}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, we obtain
\epsilon^{1+\frac{3}{q}-\frac{3}{p}}\lesssim 1
for all sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
. Letting
\epsilon\to 0
implies
\frac{1}{q}\geq\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3}
for all
r\in[1,\infty]
.

Thus, we obtain
\frac{1}{q}\geq\max\{\frac{2}{p}-1,\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{3}\}
, and (III) of Theorem
1.4
is proved.

4.2.4
Proof of (IV) of Theorem
1.4

From (
4.3
), setting
\bar{s}_{2,-\frac{1}{2}}(p,q)<0
yields a decay estimate
F_{j,k}:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
, where
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
lies in the open region with vertices
(0,0)
,
(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})
, and
(1,1)
. Then,
T:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
holds on the intersection of this region with
1+(1+\omega)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)>0
. Thus,
T:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
holds on this region uniformly in
u\in[1,2]
. Combining this with
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
, we conclude that
(0,0)
,
(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})
, and
(1,1)
are the endpoints of the estimate
F_{j,k}:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
\bar{s}_{2,-\frac{1}{2}}(p,q)
is the infimum ensuring (
4.3
) holds.

On the other hand, as in Subsection 5.1, for
1\leq p\leq q\leq\infty
, the
L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
estimates for
E_{j,k}-F_{j,k}
and
\widetilde{T}^{1,a}_{j,k}
are bounded by
2^{-Nk}
for large
N
. Recall that
Q_{k}f=(\varrho_{k}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi))^{\vee}
, and a similar estimate to (
4.3
) holds. Namely, if

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}Q_{k}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{sk}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

then
s\geq\bar{s}_{2,-\frac{1}{2}}(p,q)
. Now, from Lemma
4.2
and (
4.9
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}Q_{k}f\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\frac{1}{r}k}\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}Q_{k}f\right\|_{L^{r}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\frac{1}{r}k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

which implies
\frac{1}{r}\geq\bar{s}_{2,-\frac{1}{2}}(p,q)
, i.e.,

\frac{1}{q}\geq\max\left\{\frac{1}{2p}-\frac{1}{2r},\frac{2}{p}-\frac{r+1}{r}\right\}.

From (
4.2
), setting
s_{-\frac{1}{2},0}(p,q,q)<0
yields the decay estimate
F_{j,k}:L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{\infty}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})
when
(\frac{1}{p},\frac{1}{q})
lies in the open region with vertices
(0,0)
,
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{6})
,
(\frac{2}{3},\frac{1}{3})
, and
(1,1)
. This result is nearly sharp by
[
31
]
. Similarly, if

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}Q_{k}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{sk}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

then
s\geq s_{-\frac{1}{2},0}(p,q,q)
. On the other hand, by (
4.9
), applying the Sobolev embedding theorem with respect to
u
for
q>r
and
\textrm{H}\ddot{\textrm{o}}\textrm{lder}
’s inequality for
q\leq r
yields

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{T}^{1}_{j,k}Q_{k}f\right\|_{L^{q}_{u}L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 2^{\max\left\{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q},0\right\}k}\|f\|_{L_{x}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

It further yields
\max\{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q},0\}\geq s_{-\frac{1}{2},0}(p,q,q)
.
Note that
s_{-\frac{1}{2},0}(p,q,q)=\tilde{s}_{A,0}(p,q,q)
, we obtain

\max\left\{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{q},0\right\}\geq\tilde{s}_{A,0}(p,q,q).

Putting these results together, (IV) of Theorem
1.4
is proved.

Identification of telomere-related gene signatures for immune cell infiltration and prognosis in hepatocellular carcinoma
Authors: Juntao Sun, Ye Gao, Ruotong Xu, Hao Fu, Xinyi Ying, An Yan, Sunbin Ling
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundHepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is a common malignancy with poor clinical outcomes. Telomere maintenance is crucial for tumor cell proliferation, yet the role of telomere-related genes (TRGs) in HCC prognosis remains unclear. This study aimed to identify TRG-based prognostic signatures and to evaluate their associations with immune cell infiltration and drug responsiveness in HCC.MethodsTRGs were analyzed for survival significance with The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. A prognostic model was constructed using sequential statistical approaches. A nomogram incorporating clinical parameters was generated for patient survival estimation. Risk stratification classified HCC patients. Model accuracy was assessed by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Immune cell infiltration and drug responsiveness were compared across groups.ResultsA seven-gene prognostic signature (SLC7A11, BRSK2, ABCC8, AKR1B10, ALDH2, HMMR and PCP4) was established to evaluate HCC prognosis. An integrated nomogram estimating survival in patients was formulated. High-risk (HR) patients had significantly lower survival than low-risk (LR) patients. Functional analysis showed that differentially expressed genes (DEGs) enriched in nuclear division, chromosomal organization and cell cycle regulation pathways critical for HCC progression. Immune analysis indicated a negative correlation between risk score and the abundance of specific immune cells, including natural killer (NK) cells and memory CD4+ T cells. Five therapeutic agents had lower half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values in HR patients.ConclusionsThis study identified significant associations between DEGs and HCC prognosis, highlighting their potential as biomarkers. The findings suggest applications for prognostic scoring systems in clinical decision-making, therapeutic regimen selection and immunotherapy approaches for HCC patients.

Key findings

• A seven-gene telomere-related prognostic signature (SLC7A11, BRSK2, ABCC8, AKR1B10, ALDH2, HMMR, PCP4) was established for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), demonstrating robust survival prediction across The Cancer Genome Atlas and Gene Expression Omnibus cohorts (area under the curve >0.7).

• An integrated nomogram combining the risk score with clinical parameters was developed for precise patient survival estimation.

• High-risk (HR) patients exhibited lower infiltration of natural killer cells and memory CD4+ T cells but enhanced sensitivity to afatinib, axitinib, sorafenib, paclitaxel, and docetaxel.

What is known and what is new?

• While telomere maintenance is crucial for tumor proliferation, the prognostic utility of telomere-related gene (TRG) signatures in HCC remains underexplored.

• This study identifies a novel seven-gene TRG-based risk model that predicts HCC prognosis and correlates with immune cell infiltration patterns and chemotherapeutic sensitivity, highlighting distinct tumor immune microenvironment characteristics between risk groups.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The TRG-based prognostic model and nomogram provide practical tools for clinicians to optimize HCC prognosis assessment and guide personalized therapeutic strategies.

• HR patients identified by this model may benefit from specific targeted therapies (afatinib, sorafenib, etc.) and immune checkpoint inhibitors, warranting validation in prospective clinical trials.

Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), the main subtype of liver malignancies, stands as the sixth most common malignancy and the third major contributor to cancer-related deaths worldwide. Its etiology is primarily linked to chronic liver disease and persistent infections with hepatitis B and C viruses (1-3). The World Health Organization projects that liver cancer will claim over one million lives annually by 2030 (4-7). HCC is characterized by poor clinical outcomes, exhibiting a global 5-year survival rate of merely 18% (8-11). Driven by the global obesity epidemic, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) and its progressive form, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), are increasingly recognized as major contributors to HCC (12,13). Current therapeutic modalities include hepatectomy, liver transplantation, radiofrequency ablation, transcatheter arterial chemoembolization (TACE), and radioembolization (14-20). Nevertheless, the insidious disease progression frequently results in diagnosis at advanced stages, limiting therapeutic efficacy (21). Recent investigations have extensively explored polygenic risk profiling to elucidate the heterogeneity, prognostic factors, and treatment responsiveness in HCC. Consequently, identifying novel and reliable molecular signatures is crucial for prognostic evaluation and development of targeted therapeutic strategies.

Telomeres are specialized nucleoprotein structures comprising recurring TTAGGG DNA sequences and associated shelterin complexes positioned at chromosome termini. In most organisms, telomerase maintains telomere length by adding DNA repeats during genome replication. This ribonucleoprotein enzyme is composed of a catalytic core dimer comprising telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT) and telomerase RNA component (TERC), which serves as a template for RNA-dependent DNA synthesis. Telomeres progressively shorten with successive cell divisions and under certain pathological conditions. These structures are essential for preserving chromosomal structural maintenance and genomic stability. Telomere dysfunction has been implicated in various pathologies, including cancer, aging, cardiovascular diseases (16), and psychiatric disorders (17). Telomere dynamics have been extensively characterized in relation to both tumor initiation and progression (18). Telomere maintenance mechanisms are prevalent in tumors, predominantly via telomerase reactivation or the alternative lengthening of telomeres (ALT) pathway, with telomerase activation being more common across nearly all tumor types. Emerging evidence suggests that telomere shortening influences carcinogenesis through dual mechanisms (19,20). Telomere shortening initially may suppress tumorigenesis by limiting cellular proliferation; conversely, it can promote neoplastic progression by inducing chromosomal instability and aberrant fusion events (21,22).

Prior investigations have predominantly examined telomere length across various malignancies and its prognostic implications. Although individual telomere-related genes (TRGs) signatures have been investigated in HCC, few studies have jointly examined TRG expression and tumor microenvironment (TME) composition under a systematic prognostic-validation framework. To address this gap, a TRG-based prognostic risk model for HCC was constructed and its risk scores examined for associations with chemosensitivity and immune cell infiltration, drawing on transcriptomic and clinicopathological data from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA). Our findings highlight the significant prognostic value of TRGs in HCC management. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-0471/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition

RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data and clinical characteristics of HCC cases were obtained from TCGA database. The RNA expression dataset GSE14520 and corresponding clinical follow-up information were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database. Furthermore, 2,086 telomere-associated genes were retrieved from the TelNet database (http://www.cancertelsys.org/telnet/) (12,23). The TCGA cohort was then randomly partitioned into training and testing sets, while GEO datasets served as independent external validation. This study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. As the study exclusively involved analysis of previously collected, de-identified public data, it was exempt from additional ethical review.

Differential expression analysis

We categorized 419 HCC samples from TCGA into tumor and normal tissue groups. Genes with differential expression between these groups were detected through the “edgeR” R package with threshold parameters of |log2 fold change (FC)| >1.5 and false discovery rate (FDR) <0.05. Overlapping these differential genes with TRGs produced differentially expressed TRGs for further analysis.

Development and verification of prognostic model

The TCGA-HCC patient population was randomly assigned to training and testing cohorts. Cox-based modeling was performed sequentially, beginning with univariate analysis, followed by Lasso regression and multivariate Cox regression applied to the training set. The multivariate analysis was repeated in the testing set. A TRG-based prognostic model was built by combining expression data and regression coefficients derived from multivariate Cox regression with the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) algorithm for optimal model selection. Participants were stratified into high-risk (HR) and low-risk (LR) groups according to the median risk score. Survival differences between risk groups were evaluated via the log-rank test. During model construction, least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression was first applied to select features and reduce dimensionality; multivariate Cox regression with AIC was then used to derive unbiased coefficient estimates and to optimize the final model. Heatmaps were created to visualize gene expression patterns in both training and testing subsets. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis evaluated the diagnostic precision of the risk model. The GEO-HCC cohort (GSE14520) served as an external validation dataset.

Development and validation of a nomogram

Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted across the entire HCC cohort to determine independent prognostic factors, including the gene signature-derived risk score and clinicopathological parameters [age, gender, and tumor-nodes-metastasis (TNM) pathological classification]. A prognostic nomogram incorporating these independent variables was built using the “rms” in R. Model discrimination and calibration were assessed through ROC curves and calibration plots, respectively. Statistical evaluations were conducted using the “rms”, “pROC”, and “timeROC” R packages.

Functional enrichment assessment

Biological pathway analyses were performed in R using Gene Ontology (GO), Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG), and Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA). Differentially expressed TRGs underwent GO and KEGG enrichment analyses with the clusterProfiler, org.Hs.eg.db, enrichplot, and ggplot2 packages, and the resulting enrichment terms—covering biological process (BP), cellular component (CC), and molecular function (MF) for GO and the corresponding KEGG categories—were rendered as bubble plots. As a non-parametric, unsupervised method, GSVA transforms gene expression matrices across samples into gene-set expression matrices, allowing pathway-level enrichment to be compared between groups. Using the c5.go.symbols.gmt and c2.cp.kegg.symbols.gmt gene sets from the Molecular Signatures Database, pathway enrichment between the two risk clusters was calculated, compared, and visualized as heatmaps. Statistical significance was set at P<0.05.

Immune infiltration analysis

Immune cell infiltration within each sample was quantified via the CIBERSORT algorithm and compared across different groups. CIBERSORT estimates the relative abundance of 22 immune-cell subsets from transcriptome data; sample-level deconvolution confidence is assessed via Monte Carlo sampling, which yields per-sample P values. To ensure robust and accurate inference of immune-cell proportions, only samples with P values <0.05 were retained for downstream analysis.

Drug response estimation

Drug sensitivity was evaluated by estimating the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) values of targeted therapies, derived from gene expression profiles using the “OncoPredict” in R. Pearson correlation coefficients were calculated between differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and targeted drugs. The calcPhenotype function was applied with ridge regression trained on Genomics of Drug Sensitivity in Cancer data; phenotype data were power-transformed and the lowest 20% of varying genes were excluded prior to model fitting.

Online database analysis

Protein levels of characteristic DEGs in HCC were analyzed using the Human Protein Atlas (HPA). Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from HCC tissues were obtained from GEO scRNA-seq datasets (GSE125449 and GSE140228_10X) within the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub (TISCH) platform. Expression matrices at single-cell level underwent normalization through the NormalizeData function in the “Seurat” package by scaling unique molecular identifier (UMI) counts to 10,000 per cell. MAESTRO algorithm was applied to each dataset for quality control, clustering, and annotation of cell types. The GSE125449 and GSE140228_10X datasets contained 5,761 cells from 10 HCC tissues and 62,530 cells from 5 HCC tissues, respectively.

Statistical analysis

All data processing, analysis, and visualization were performed in R (v4.4.1) with associated packages, with statistical significance set at P<0.05. Survival outcomes between groups were compared using Kaplan-Meier (KM) analyses with log-rank tests, and intergroup differences were assessed using the Wilcoxon test. Independent prognostic factors were identified through univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression, and hazard ratios with 95% confidence intervals are reported.

Results

Identification of telomere-related DEGs and prognostic genes

We identified 3,662 DEGs using the threshold of FDR <0.05 and |log2FC| ≥1.5. The intersection of these genes with 2,086 TRGs yielded 218 differentially expressed TRGs. These findings were visualized using volcano and Venn diagrams (Figure 1A,1B).

Functional analysis

To explore underlying mechanisms, functional enrichment assessments were conducted to identify biological roles related to TRGs in the signature. GO enrichment analysis identified significant involvement in nuclear division and chromosome organization (Figure 1C), while KEGG pathway analysis revealed enrichment in DNA catalytic activity and cell cycle regulation (Figure 1D). The analytical data indicate that TRGs may be primarily associated with HCC migration and metastasis.

TRG risk model predicts prognosis of HCC patients

Among the 218 differentially expressed TRGs, 20 genes were initially selected through univariate Cox regression and presented as a forest plot (Figure 2A). Subsequent Lasso regression identified 16 genes (DNAJC6, MSH4, TKT, SLC7A11, HMMR, PKIB, AKR1B10, BRSK2, ABCC8, ALDH2, CABP1, GSTZ1, TRIM16, PNMT, ETV4, PCP4) (Figure 2B,2C). Multivariate Cox regression analysis further refined this selection to seven prognostic genes (SLC7A11, BRSK2, ABCC8, AKR1B10, ALDH2, HMMR, and PCP4), which were incorporated into the final TRG risk model. Patients were stratified into HR and LR categories using the median risk score. In both training and test sets, patients were arranged in ascending order according to risk score (Figure 2D,2E). KM survival analysis demonstrated significantly worse outcomes in the HR group in both cohorts: test set (P=0.02) and training set (P<0.001) (Figure 2F,2G). The risk stratification based on the TRG signature showed statistically significant survival differences between the two risk populations in both cohorts (Figure 2H,2I). Model performance was evaluated using ROC analysis. For the training cohort, area under the curves (AUCs) for 1-, 2-, and 3-year survival prediction reached 0.774, 0.766, and 0.737, respectively, while the validation cohort showed AUCs of 0.704, 0.640, and 0.667 (Figure 2N,2O). Gene expression patterns of the seven prognostic genes were visualized using boxplots and heatmaps for both training and test sets (Figure 2J-2M). The GSE14520 dataset served as an external validation cohort, analyzed with identical methodology as applied to the training set, with results illustrated in Figure S1A-S1E.

Nomogram based on combined clinical parameters and risk score: development and validation

A nomogram model was developed using TCGA data through the “rms” R package to predict 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year survival rates in HCC patients. This model incorporated gender, age, risk score, and tumor TNM stages to calculate a comprehensive score for each patient. Prognosis directly correlated with this total score. Calibration plots supported the predictive utility of the nomogram. The association between risk score and clinicopathological features was analyzed. Univariate and multivariate analyses of clinical data for both HR and LR groups were conducted and visualized using forest plots (Figure 3A,3B). Both Cox regression models established risk score and T stage as independent prognostic factors (P<0.001). In univariate analysis, risk score, T stage, and M stage were statistically significant (P<0.05); in contrast, age, sex, and N stage were not (P=0.45, 0.34, and 0.32, respectively). Similarly, in the multivariate analysis, risk score and T stage retained significance, whereas age, sex, N stage, and M stage showed no statistical significance (P=0.24, 0.96, 0.41, and 0.50, respectively). These observations highlight the importance of risk score and T stage in determining patient prognosis (Figure 3C-3H).

Development and validation of a nomogram using independent factors

Using the complete TCGA dataset, a nomogram was built through the “rms” R package for estimating survival probabilities at 1 to 5 years for HCC patients (Figure 3I). Total scores of individual cases were determined based on gender, age, risk score, and tumor T, N, and M stages. Prognosis directly correlated with the overall patient score. Calibration curves demonstrated favorable predictive performance of the nomogram (Figure 3J).

Immune correlation analysis

The “CIBERSORT” in R was performed to investigate the associations between risk score and tumor immune cell infiltration. Elevated risk scores were positively associated with increased infiltration of memory B cells, activated CD4+ memory T cells, CD8+ T lymphocytes, activated natural killer (NK) cells, M0 macrophages, and resting dendritic and mast cells, as well as activated mast cells (P<0.05). In contrast, risk score demonstrated negative correlations with naive CD4+ T cells, monocytes, eosinophils, and neutrophils (P<0.05) (Figure 4A,4B).

Tumor-related genes risk model for treatment strategy selection

Risk scores were significantly elevated in the HR group compared to the LR group (P<0.001). The IC50 values for afatinib, axitinib, sorafenib, paclitaxel, and docetaxel were lower in the HR group compared with their LR counterparts. Conversely, luminespib exhibited elevated IC50 values in the HR group (Figure 5A-5F). Correlation analysis using Pearson’s coefficient was performed between DEGs and targeted drugs (Figure 5G-5R and Figure S2A-S2G). ABCC8 expression was negatively correlated, to varying degrees, with afatinib (coefficient =−0.1), axitinib (coefficient =−0.2), paclitaxel (coefficient =−0.12), and sorafenib (coefficient =−0.087), suggesting that its expression levels may influence sensitivity to these agents. In contrast, AKR1B10 displayed a strong negative correlation with afatinib (coefficient =−0.67) but a pronounced positive correlation with sorafenib (coefficient =0.69), pointing to a dual role in drug response. BRSK2 was markedly negatively correlated with sorafenib (coefficient =−0.7) and docetaxel (coefficient =−0.57), whereas CABP1 had weaker but still significant correlations with multiple drugs. Notably, SLC7A11 showed an exceptionally strong negative correlation with Axitinib (coefficient =−0.81), supporting its potential as a predictive biomarker for this therapy. Together, these patterns suggest distinct drug-sensitivity profiles between the two risk groups; however, because several drug-gene correlations were modest in magnitude and in silico IC50 prediction has inherent limitations, the results should be interpreted with caution.

Discussion

HCC ranks among the most common malignancies globally and is associated with considerable disease burden and poor survival outcomes (21,22). Elucidating hepatocarcinogenesis mechanisms and identifying prognostic biomarkers and immunotherapy targets are critical for improving early diagnosis, treatment efficacy, and patient survival. Telomere maintenance, which promotes tumor cell immortalization and proliferation, is widely observed in cancers; however, its relationship with HCC prognosis remains underexplored (24-26). To our knowledge, this is the first TRG-based prognostic risk model for HCC to integrate tumor immune-microenvironment characterization with external validation.

In this study, we identified 218 TRGs from the TelNet database that showed a significant association with prognosis after joint analysis with public databases. To investigate their role in HCC, we selected seven key genes (SLC7A11, BRSK2, ABCC8, AKR1B10, ALDH2, HMMR, and PCP4) through Lasso regression and multivariate Cox regression analysis to develop a prediction model. Subsequent verification demonstrated that this model effectively predicted patient prognosis. The study examined the relationship between risk score and immune cell infiltration. Risk score positively correlated with several immune cell populations including memory B cells, memory CD4+ T cells with activation, CD8+ T cells, NK cells with activation, M0 macrophages, and resting dendritic and mast cells, along with activated mast cells (P<0.05). Conversely, inverse correlations were observed between the risk score and naive CD4+ T cells, monocytes, eosinophils, and neutrophils. Drug sensitivity prediction analysis indicated that individuals in the HR group exhibited increased sensitivity to afatinib, axitinib, sorafenib, paclitaxel, and docetaxel. In contrast, LR group patients showed better response to luminespib treatment. Individuals with elevated risk scores may thus be more appropriate candidates for immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) and targeted therapy agents. However, these transcriptomic findings require further clinical validation before any therapeutic inference can be drawn.

Genes within the telomere-associated prognostic model demonstrate diverse functions in disease pathogenesis. The cystine transporter SLC7A11 (also known as xCT) shields cancer cells against oxidative damage and shows elevated expression in multiple malignancies. SLC7A11 has been defined as an independent prognostic marker in HCC, characterized by heterogeneity in immune cell infiltration and programmed death ligand-1 (PD-L1) expression (27). HCC development is closely associated with dysregulation of cell death mechanisms. Under glucose-deprived conditions, SLC7A11-high cells undergo disulfidptosis, a regulated death modality marked by intracellular disulfide overload (23,28,29). Our study identifies SLC7A11 as a risk factor affecting prognosis in HCC patients, consistent with these findings. BRSK2 plays critical roles in regulating the cell cycle, neuronal/axonal polarity establishment, and insulin secretion. Its function in senescent and differentiated cells within age-related disorders, including osteoporosis, has been extensively documented (30). Additionally, innate immune CD56dim NK cells, which act as first responders in immune processes and regulate immune surveillance of senescent cells, show positive correlation with BRSK2 expression in certain cancers (31-34). ABCC8 encodes the sulfonylurea receptor 1 (SUR1) protein located on chromosome 11p15.1. This gene encodes a subunit of the adenosine triphosphate-sensitive potassium (KATP) channel. Abnormalities in this gene typically lead to dysfunction of potassium channels, resulting in dysregulated islet cell activity. Previous research showed that SRY establishes a transcriptional complex with specificity protein 1 (Sp1), thereby synergistically promoting transcription of the ABCC8 and monoamine oxidase A genes in neural tissue (35). However, ABCC8 remains unstudied in HCC, indicating the need for further investigation to elucidate its role in hepatocarcinogenesis.

AKR1B10, a tumor-related metabolic enzyme, displays dysregulated expression patterns in various cancers (8,36,37). Studies have reported that elevated AKR1B10 levels correlate with clinicopathological features of HCC. AKR1B10 activates inflammation-related signaling pathways through the DANCR-miR-216a-5p-AKR1B10 signaling cascade, modifying the immune microenvironment of HCC, thus promoting tumor development and affecting prognosis (38). Our study identifies AKR1B10 as a risk factor influencing HCC prognosis, consistent with previous findings. Mitochondrial acetaldehyde dehydrogenase 2 (ALDH2) is a mitochondrial matrix enzyme that catalyzes the oxidation of toxic aldehydes to corresponding carboxylic acids, reducing cytotoxic damage (39). Mechanistic studies have demonstrated that ALDH2-deficient liver cells under chronic alcohol exposure produce large amounts of oxidized mitochondrial DNA, which is transported via extracellular vesicles to neighboring HCC cells. This process activates multiple oncogenic pathways, promoting hepatocarcinogenesis(40). Hyaluronan-mediated motility receptor (HMMR) acts as an oncogenic driver and is implicated in aggressive tumor behavior and unfavorable survival outcomes across several malignancies, including HCC (41), breast cancer (42-46), and prostate cancer (47). Researchers have found that liver cancer cells with high HMMR expression evade phagocytosis by sustaining CD47 signaling. Patients with elevated expression of both HMMR and CD47 demonstrated worse prognosis (48). Our study observed similar associations. PCP4 encodes a modulator of calmodulin-mediated calcium binding, with its primary function related to the regulation of calmodulin-dependent signaling (49-51). However, PCP4 remains insufficiently investigated in HCC, necessitating further exploration to elucidate its role in hepatocarcinogenesis.

Enrichment analysis of DEGs between LR and HR groups revealed significant enrichment in BPs including nuclear division, chromosomal organization, DNA catalytic activity, and cell cycle regulation. These processes are closely associated with HCC pathogenesis (52). Risk score was positively correlated with immunologically relevant subsets—particularly NK cells and CD8+ T cells—and negatively correlated with memory CD4+ T cells in immune-cell infiltration analyses. These immune populations are frequently associated with tumor prognosis and treatment response (53-55).

The relationship between HR score and poor prognosis prompted further investigation into chemotherapy resistance correlations. HR patients demonstrated greater predicted sensitivity to afatinib, axitinib, sorafenib, paclitaxel, and docetaxel, as indicated by lower IC50 values compared to the LR cohort. Afatinib suppresses ErbB signaling through attachment to the kinase domains of EGFR, ErbB2, and ErbB4, irreversibly blocking downstream phosphorylation and consequently suppressing cancer cell proliferation and metastasis (51). Clinical investigations have revealed that Axitinib, which blocks vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) receptor tyrosine kinase activity, in combination with the monoclonal antibody pembrolizumab has demonstrated antitumor activity in individuals with previously untreated cases of advanced renal carcinoma (56). Sorafenib, an oral multitargeted receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor, delays progression in desmoid tumor patients (57). Paclitaxel, a mitotic inhibitor that disrupts microtubule function during cell division, is commonly employed as a first-line therapeutic option in combination with carboplatin/cisplatin for several major malignancies (58). Similarly, docetaxel, an anti-mitotic chemotherapeutic agent, has been widely utilized in treating various malignant tumors (59).

There are several limitations in this study. First, because this study was a retrospective analysis based on public databases, overfitting cannot be entirely ruled out; the findings will therefore require validation in large, multi-center prospective studies. Second, additional clinicopathological features should be incorporated to more comprehensively analyze the prognostic utility of the risk model. Third, detailed etiological information is frequently incomplete in publicly available databases, and overlapping risk factors and incomplete patient histories often make the precise determination of underlying causes challenging in clinical practice. Finally, the underlying mechanisms forming the prognostic basis of the HCC risk model necessitate further basic experiments for verification and exploration.

This study established a TRGs risk model through the TCGA dataset with verification in GEO datasets. The analysis covered protein and RNA expression profiles along with immune cell infiltration patterns of these genes in HCC; however, experimental validation of our findings is lacking. This TRG-based model may provide guidance for therapeutic selection in patients with HCC.

Conclusions

In summary, this study utilized integrative bioinformatics approaches to identify seven telomere related genes (SLC7A11, BRSK2, ABCC8, AKR1B10, ALDH2, HMMR, and PCP4) associated with prognosis in HCC. The developed prognostic model demonstrated robust predictive capability and was validated through pathway enrichment analysis, immune infiltration profiling, and drug sensitivity prediction. Future research should incorporate more comprehensive TRG datasets and employ advanced analytical frameworks such as multi-omics integration and machine learning. Experimental validation of these key genes in clinical samples and prospective clinical studies will be essential to confirm their diagnostic and therapeutic relevance.

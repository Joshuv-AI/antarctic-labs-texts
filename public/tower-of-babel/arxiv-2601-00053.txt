Probabilistic Hanna Neumann Conjectures
Yotam Shomroni

Abstract
We develop a theory of polymatroids on Stallings core graphs, which provides a new technique for proving lower bounds on stable invariants of words and subgroups in free groups F F , and for upper bounds on their probability for mapping, under a random homomorphism from F F to a finite group G G , into some subgroup of G G . As a result, we prove the gap conjecture on the stable K K -primitivity rank by Ernst-West, Puder and Seidel, prove a conjecture of Reiter about the number of solutions to a system of equations in a finite group action, and give a unified proof of the ”rank-1 Hanna Neumann conjecture” by Wise and its higher rank analogue. We further show that the stable compressed rank and its q q -analogue coincide with the decay rate of many-words measure on stable actions of finite simple groups of large rank. Finally, we conjecture an analogue of the Hanna Neumann conjecture over fields, and suggest that every finite group action is associated to some version of the HNC.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\beta\in\textup{Hom}(H,\textup{GL}_{d}(K))

Consider

-module by the action

Proof.

Clearly

Therefore

In this section we develop the theory of

\Gamma

Thanks to the existence of stackings
for non-power words
[

the second part
of the
\Gamma

(Theorem

)
about compact
\Gamma_{w}
-polymatroids
is much easier than the first part,
and is proved in
Corollary
3.9
.
For non-abelian groups

, we show in

to polymatroids on graphs of subgroups of

Some polymatroid theory

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V_{1},V_{2}
be sets and
\eta\colon V_{1}\rightarrow V_{2}
any function.
Given a polymatroid

\myscr{h}\colon 2^{V_{2}}\rightarrow\R
, we define

\eta^{*}\myscr{h}\colon 2^{V_{1}}\rightarrow\R
by

\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(U)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\myscr{h}(\eta(U))
.

Note that if
V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}
and
\myscr{h}_{2}

is a polymatroid on
V_{2}
, then

\myscr{h}_{1}=\myscr{h}_{2}\restriction_{2^{V_{1}}}

is a polymatroid on
V_{1}
and the inclusion map

V_{1}\hookrightarrow V_{2}
is a lossless morphism
\myscr{h}_{1}\rightarrow\myscr{h}_{2}
.
This can be generalized:

Proposition 3.2
.

\eta^{*}\myscr{h}
is a polymatroid, and

\eta\colon\eta^{*}\myscr{h}\rightarrow\myscr{h}
a lossless morphism.

Proof.

Clearly
\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(\emptyset)=\myscr{h}(\emptyset)=0
.
If
U\subseteq U^{\prime}\subseteq V_{1}
, clearly

\eta(U)\subseteq\eta(U^{\prime})
, so
\eta^{*}\myscr{h}

is monotone. Finally, for
A,B\subseteq V_{1}
,

\eta(A\cap B)\subseteq\eta(A)\cap\eta(B)

\eta(A\cup B)=\eta(A)\cup\eta(B)

\begin{split}\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(A)+\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(B)&=\myscr{h}(\eta(A))+\myscr{h}(\eta(B))\\
&\geq\myscr{h}(\eta(A)\cup\eta(B))+\myscr{h}(\eta(A)\cap\eta(B))\\
&\geq\myscr{h}(\eta(A\cup B))+\myscr{h}(\eta(A\cap B))\\
&=\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(A\cup B)+\eta^{*}\myscr{h}(A\cap B).\end{split}

Now

is a lossless morphism by definition.

Definition 3.3
.

An

on a finite set

is a bijection

\sigma\colon V\rightarrow\{1,...,|V|\}
.
Given an ordering
\sigma

\myscr{h}
on

of

at

Note that

Proposition 3.4
.

If

\phi\colon(V_{1},\myscr{h}_{1})\rightarrow(V_{2},\myscr{h}_{2})

is an injective morphism of polymatroids,
which is monotonically increasing
with respect to orderings

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
on

.

Proof.

Denote

if
\sigma_{1}(v)=i
and similarly

if
\sigma_{2}(u)=j
.
Let

satisfy

.
Since
\phi
is injective,
\psi
is well defined, and since
\phi
is monotone,

is monotone as well. Now

\begin{array}[]{ll}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\delta_{v_{i}}(\myscr{h}_{1})&=\myscr{h}_{1}(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\})-\myscr{h}_{1}(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\})\\
(\phi\textup{ is a polymatroid morphism})&\geq\myscr{h}_{2}(\phi\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\})-\myscr{h}_{2}(\phi\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i-1}\})\\
&=\myscr{h}_{2}(\{u_{\psi(1)},\ldots,u_{\psi(i)}\})-\myscr{h}_{2}(\{u_{\psi(1)},\ldots,u_{\psi(i-1)}\})\\
(\{u_{\psi(1)},\ldots,u_{\psi(i-1)}\}\subseteq\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\psi(i)-1}\}&\geq\myscr{h}_{2}(\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\psi(i)}\}-\myscr{h}_{2}(\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\psi(i)-1}\})\\
\textup{ and }\myscr{h}_{2}\textup{ is submodular})&=\delta_{\phi(v_{i})}(\myscr{h}_{2}).\end{array}

Reduction to subgraphs

The following lemma is a
polymatroid version of

[

]
,
taken from

:

Lemma 3.10
.

Let

,
where
\myscr{h}
is a polymatroid and

\lambda
is a fractional supercover,
that is, for every

Then

\langle\lambda,\myscr{h}\rangle\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\sum_{U\subseteq V}\lambda(U)\myscr{h}(U)\geq\myscr{h}(V)
.

If
\myscr{h}
is a polymatroid on
V
and

, we denote the
T
-contraction
of
\myscr{h}
by

\myscr{h}(U\,|\,T)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\myscr{h}(U\cup T)-\myscr{h}(T)\quad\quad(\textup{which is }\leq\myscr{h}(U)-\myscr{h}(U\cap T)\textup{ by submodularity}).

The

-contraction
\myscr{h}(\cdot\,|\,T)
is a polymatroid
(see
[
9
]
or
[
2
, 2. Background]
).

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Delta
be
B
-
core
graphs,

\eta\colon\Gamma\rightarrow\Delta
a morphism,
and
\myscr{h}
a
\Delta
-polymatroid.
Then

, with equality if
\eta
is surjective.

In particular, taking
\Gamma
to be the empty graph (with no vertices), we get
\chi(\myscr{h})\leq 0
.

Proof.

If
\eta
is surjective the claim is obvious.
Otherwise, write

\eta=\eta_{\textup{inj}}\circ\eta_{\textup{sur}}

where

\eta_{\textup{inj}}\colon\textup{Im}(\eta)\hookrightarrow\Delta
is the inclusion map.
Then
\chi(\eta^{*}\myscr{h})=\chi(\eta_{\textup{inj}}^{*}\eta_{\textup{sur}}^{*}\myscr{h})=\chi(\eta_{\textup{inj}}^{*}\myscr{h})
, so we may assume

\Gamma\subseteq\Delta
and

\eta^{*}\myscr{h}=\myscr{h}\restriction_{\Gamma}
.
Let
b\in B
.
To ease notation, denote
\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Gamma)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\mathfrak{s}(E_{b}(\Gamma))
.
Since
\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Gamma)\subseteq\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\cap V(\Gamma)
,
by monotonicity,
\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Gamma))\leq\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\cap V(\Gamma))
, so

\begin{split}\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Gamma))&\geq\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\cap V(\Gamma))\\
&\geq\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma)).\end{split}

The same holds for
\mathfrak{t}
. Since
\mathfrak{s},\mathfrak{t}\colon E_{b}(\Delta)\rightrightarrows V(\Gamma)
are morphisms,

\begin{split}\myscr{h}^{b}(E_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{b}(E_{b}(\Gamma))&\geq\max\{\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Gamma)),\,\,\,\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{t}_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{t}_{b}(\Gamma))\}\\
&\geq\max\{\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma)),\,\,\,\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{t}_{b}(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma))\}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma))+\myscr{h}^{V}(\mathfrak{t}_{b}(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma))\right).\end{split}

(3.1)

Now, since
\Delta
is a
B
-core
graph, that is, the degree of every vertex is
\geq 2
,
the function
\lambda\colon 2^{V(\Delta)}\rightarrow\R
defined as the combination of indicators

\lambda=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{b\in B}\left(\text{1}_{\mathfrak{s}_{b}(\Delta)}+\text{1}_{\mathfrak{t}_{b}(\Delta)}\right)

is a fractional supercover (because
\sum_{U\ni v}\lambda(U)=\deg(v)/2\geq 1
).
By Shearer’s lemma (Lemma
3.10
) for the polymatroid

\myscr{h}^{V}(\cdot\,|\,V(\Gamma))
, summing (
3.1
)
over all
b\in B

we get

\sum_{b\in B}(\myscr{h}^{b}(E_{b}(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{b}(E_{b}(\Gamma)))\geq\myscr{h}^{V}(V(\Delta)\,|\,V(\Gamma))=\myscr{h}^{V}(V(\Delta))-\myscr{h}^{V}(V(\Gamma)),

as needed.
∎

In contrast with the short proof of Corollary

Observation 5.2
.

Let
H
be a group,

X,Y\in\textup{Obj}(\textbf{C})
,

\alpha\in\textup{Hom}(H,\textup{Aut}(Y))
and

\iota\in\textup{Hom}^{\textup{inj}}_{\textbf{C}}(X,Y)

Then
\textup{Img}(\iota)\subseteq Y

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
H
be a group,

X,Y\in\textup{Obj}(\textbf{C})
,

G\leq\textup{Aut}(X)
and
\alpha\in\textup{Hom}(H,\textup{Aut}(Y))
. Then

\left|\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\textup{common fixed points}\\
\textup{of }\alpha(H)\curvearrowright\textup{Hom}^{\textup{inj}}_{\textbf{C}}(X,Y)/G\end{array}\right\}\right|=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{\beta\in\textup{Hom}(H,G)}\left|\textup{Inter}^{\textup{inj}}(\alpha,\beta)\right|.

Proof.

Define
\mathfrak{I}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\biguplus_{\beta\in\textup{Hom}(H,G)}\textup{Inter}^{\textup{inj}}(\alpha,\beta)\times\{\beta\}
and denote the set of common fixed points of
\alpha(H)\curvearrowright\textup{Hom}^{\textup{inj}}_{\textbf{C}}(X,Y)/G
by
\mathcal{CFP}
.
The group
G
acts freely on
\mathfrak{I}
: for every
g\in G
and
(\iota,\beta)\in\mathfrak{I}
,
g.(\iota,\beta)\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}(\iota\circ g,\,\,h\mapstochar\rightarrow g^{-1}\beta(h)g)
.
Indeed,
\iota\circ g=\iota
implies
g=\textup{id}_{X}
since
\iota
is injective.
For every
h\in H
and
(\iota,\beta)\in\mathfrak{I}
, we have equality of
G
-orbits

\alpha(h)\iota G=\iota\beta(h)G=\iota G

so
\iota G\in\mathcal{CFP}
.
The projection map
\mathfrak{I}\rightarrow\textup{Hom}^{\textup{inj}}_{\textbf{C}}(X,Y)
defined by
(\iota,\beta)\mapstochar\rightarrow\iota
intertwines the
G
-actions on
\mathfrak{I}
and
\textup{Hom}^{\textup{inj}}_{\textbf{C}}(X,Y)
, so it induces a map on the orbits
\phi\colon\mathfrak{I}/G\rightarrow\mathcal{CFP}
.
Now by Observation
5.2
,
\phi
is bijective.
Since the action
G\curvearrowright\mathfrak{I}
is free we get
|\mathcal{CFP}|=|\mathfrak{I}|/|G|

.

Proof.

\rho=\rho^{\prime}\restriction_{(1,\ldots,1)^{\bot}}

By

The Haagerup property for groups and for tracial von Neumann algebras in terms of invariant and mixing states
Paul Jolissaint

Abstract
. The aim of the article is to provide two characterizations of the Haagerup property: one for locally compact, second countable groups, and the other one for finite von Neumann algebras. Both are expressed in terms of approximations of some non-ergodic invariant states by mixing ones for actions on unital C ∗ C^{*} -algebras on the one hand, and for pairs of tracial von Neumann algebras by mixing binormal states on the other hand.

1.
Introduction

The present article aims to propose two characterizations of the Haagerup property: one for locally compact, second countable (lcsc) groups, and the other one for pairs of tracial von Neumann algebras (i.e. finite von Neumann algebras equiped with a faithful, normal tracial state).

In the first part of this article,
G
denotes a lcsc group; we assume furthermore that it is non-compact because the compact case is not relevant to the Haagerup property
[
6
]
. Recall that the latter property is often interpreted as
a strong negation of Kazhdan’s property (T) (or as a weak form of amenability, but we won’t need that interpretation here). Denoting by
P_{0,1}(G)
the convex set of all continuous, normalized positive definite functions on
G
that vanish at infinity, recall that
G
has the
Haagerup property
if there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset P_{0,1}(G)
which converges to
1
uniformly on compact subsets of
G
.

Before presenting the motivation and the content of the first part of our article, we need to recall some definitions and fix some notation.

We consider here the following two types of actions of
G
:

•

Continuous actions of
G
on compact metrizable spaces; if
X
is such a space and if
G
acts continuously by homeomorphisms on
X
, we say that the pair
(X,G)
is a
G
-
dynamical system
, or simply a
dynamical system
if
G
is fixed, and we denote by
M(X)
the compact convex set of all probability measures on
X
equipped with the weak
∗
topology, by
M_{G}(X)
the closed convex subset of
G
-invariant elements of
M(X)
, by
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(X)
the set of extreme points of
M_{G}(X)
, and finally by
M_{G,m}(X)
the set of
mixing
measures, i.e. elements
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
such that, for all Borel sets
A,B\subset X
, one has

\lim_{g\to\infty}\mu(gA\cap B)=\mu(A)\mu(B).

•

Continuous actions of
G
on separable, unital
C^{*}
-algebras: if
A
is such a
C^{*}
-algebra, an
action
of
G
on
A
is a homomorphism
\alpha:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Aut}(A)
which is continuous in the sense that, for every
a\in A
, the map
g\mapsto\alpha_{g}(a)
is norm continuous. We say that such a triple
(A,G,\alpha)
is a
G
-
C^{*}
-dynamical system
, or simply a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
if
G
is fixed.
We denote by
S(A)
the convex compact set of all states on
A
equipped with the weak
∗
topology, by
S_{G}(A)
the subset of all
G
-invariant ones, by
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
the set of all extreme points of
S_{G}(A)
and by
S_{G,m}(A)
the set of all
mixing
states, i.e. the set of elements
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)
such that

\lim_{g\to\infty}\varphi(\alpha_{g}(a)b)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)

for all
a,b\in A
.

Observe that
M_{G}(X)
(resp.
S_{G}(A)
) might be empty, and that
every dynamical system
(X,G)
is a special case of a
C^{*}
-dynamical system since the former corresponds to
A=C(X)
, the commutative
C^{*}
-algebra of continuous functions on
X
equipped with the action of
G
by left translations:
(g\cdot a)(x)=a(g^{-1}x)
for all
a\in C(X)
,
g\in G
and
x\in X
, and due to Riesz representation of states on
C(X)
by regular probability measures on
X
.

In the remarkable article
[
10
]
, E. Glasner and B. Weiss obtained an outstanding characterization of property (T) for a locally compact, second countable group

. We reproduce (not
verbatim
) A. Valette’s summary of the main results of
[

as it appears in
[

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

has property (T);

(2)

for every dynamical system
(X,G)
with

,
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(X)
is closed in
M_{G}(X)
;

(3)

let us denote by
\Sigma
the metrizable compact set of all closed subsets of the one-point compactification
G^{+}=G\cup\{\infty\}
containing
\infty
; then
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(\Sigma)
is closed in

;

(4)

the set
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(\Sigma)
is not dense in
M_{G}(\Sigma)
.

Then, A. Valette wrote:
According to the philosophy that, to any characterization of property (T) there is a parallel characterization of the Haagerup property, there should be a definition of the Haagerup property corresponding to the above definition of property (T). What is it?

The first aim of the present note is to propose such a characterization, which is our first main result. It should be pointed out that essentially a proof of the same result for countable groups is sketched and published in
[
9
, Theorem 13.21]
. Nevertheless, we believe that a detailed proof at this level of generality, in particular the
C^{*}
-algebraic aspect, is worth publishing.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a locally compact, second countable group.

(1)

Suppose that there exists a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(A,G,\alpha)
which has the follownig property: there exists
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)\setminus\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
and
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset S_{G,m}(A)
such that
\varphi=w^{*}-\lim_{n}\varphi_{n}
. Then
G
has the Haagerup property.

(2)

Conversely, if
G
has the Haagerup property, there exists a non-ergodic measure
\nu\in M_{G}(\Sigma)
and a sequence
(\eta_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset M_{G,m}(\Sigma)
such that
\nu=w^{*}-\lim_{n}\eta_{n}
. More precisely,
M_{G,m}(\Sigma)
contains more than one element and its weak
∗
closure is convex.

The referee made the following observation: if
M_{G,m}(\Sigma)
is weak
∗
-dense in
M_{G}(\Sigma)
, then Theorem 1.1(1) implies that
G
has the Haagerup property. However, we do not know whether the converse holds or not.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is largely inspired by the techniques used in
[
10
]
and it is contained in Section 3. Section 2 contains material needed in the latter section.

As is well known, the Haagerup property for (finite) von Neumann algebras and for pairs of such algebras plays also an important role: see e.g.
[
18
]
and
[
15
]
. Even though it was necessary to define and study it for arbitrary von Neumann algebras, and in particular for applications to quantum group von Neumann algebras (which are often infinite), it is basically a property of tracial von Neumann algebras. Indeed, among the several equivalent definitions, in the end they are all equivalent to that the finite von Neumann algebra
p(M\rtimes_{\sigma}\mathbb{R})p
has the Haagerup property where
\sigma
is a modular action and
p
is a finite projection with central support
z(p)=1
. See for instance
[
15
, page 82]
,
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
.

Recall e.g. from
[
12
]
that a tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
has the
Haagerup property
if there exists a net
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
of completely positive maps from
M
to itself with the following properties:

(a)

one has
\tau\circ\phi_{i}\leq\tau
(
\phi_{i}
is
subtracial
),
\phi_{i}(1)\leq 1
(
\phi_{i}
is
subunital
) and
\phi_{i}
extends to a compact operator on
L^{2}(M,\tau)
for every
i\in I
;

(b)

for every
x\in M
, one has
\lim_{i}\|\phi_{i}(x)-x\|_{2}=0
.

The latter property is independent of the chosen tracial state (
[
12
, Proposition 2.4]
), and it admits equivalent conditions via
M
-bimodules
(e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
,
[
15
, Theorem 9]
) : given a von Neumann algebra
M
, such a module is a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
equipped with a (left) normal representation of
M
, denoted by
(x,\xi)\mapsto x\xi
, and with a commuting normal action of the opposite algebra
M^{\mathrm{op}}
denoted by the right action
(y^{\mathrm{op}},\xi)\mapsto\xi y
.

In the breakthrough article
[
18
]
, S. Popa uses the Haagerup property for suitable discrete groups to prove for the first time the existence of II
1
factors with trivial fundamental group, which solved a long standing open problem posed by R. V. Kadison. In fact, he needed a relative version of the Haagerup property for pairs of finite von Neumann algebras that will be presented in details in the Section 4.

In this introductory section, we describe our next result only in the case of a single tracial von Neumann algebra
(M,\tau)
, the case of pairs
B\subset M
being treated in Section 4.

Thus, given such a tracial von Neumann algebra, our next result is a characterization of the Haagerup property for
M
in terms of the set of binormal states
\mathrm{bin}(M)
on the algebraic tensor product
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Before recalling the latter notion, let us motivate our approach by the following observation in the framework of lcsc groups: given such a group
G
, recall from
[
4
]
that a unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
of
G
is
mixing
(or a
C_{0}
-
representation
) if all its coefficient functions
g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)\xi|\eta\rangle
(
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H})
vanish at infinity. In particular, all elements of
P_{0,1}(G)
are coefficients of mixing representations.

Recall also that the convex set
P_{1}(G)
of all continuous, normalized positive definite functions on
G
identifies with the state space of the maximal
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(G)
of
G
(
[
7
, Chapter 13]
), and it follows from the definition of the Haagerup property that
G
possesses this property if and only if
P_{0,1}(G)
is weak*-dense in
P_{1}(G)
as sets of states of
C^{*}(G)
(see e.g.
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
). Coming from mixing representations of
G
, it is natural to call elements of
P_{0,1}(G)

mixing
normalized positive definite functions on
G
.

Moreover, if
G
is discrete, every element
\psi
of
P_{1}(G)
gives rise to a completely positive map
m_{\psi}
on the von Neumann algebra
L(G)
associated to
G
and generated by the left regular representation
\lambda
, so that
m_{\psi}(\lambda(g))=\psi(g)\lambda(g)
,
g\in G
. Then
m_{\psi}
extends to the bounded linear operator of multiplication by
\psi
on
\ell^{2}(G)
which is compact if and only if
\psi\in P_{0,1}(G)
, i.e. if and only if the associated representation
\pi_{\psi}
is mixing in the above sense.

Following
[
8
, Page 10]
, a state (i.e. a positive, normalized linear form) on the algebraic tensor product
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
is
binormal
if it is separately normal; there is then a natural correspondence between
\mathrm{bin}(M)
and the set of unit vectors of
M
-bimodules; more precisely:

•

to every such vector
\xi
, one associates the element
\varphi_{\xi}\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
defined by
\varphi_{\xi}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\langle x\xi y|\xi\rangle
,
x,y\in M
;

•

conversely, if
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
, the GNS triple
(\pi_{\varphi},\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
applied to the pair
(M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}},\varphi)

yields the pointed
M
-bimodule
(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
such that
\varphi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle
; moreover,
\xi_{\varphi}
is
cyclic
in the following sense: the set
\{x\xi_{\varphi}y\colon x,y\in M\}
is total in
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
.

For future use, if
\mathcal{H}
is a
M
-bimodule and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
, we denote by
\mathrm{Span}(M\xi M)
the set of all finite sums
\sum_{i}x_{i}\xi y_{i}
with
x_{i},y_{i}\in M
.

We also observe that
\mathrm{bin}(M)
is weak*-dense in the state space of the tensor product
C^{*}
-algebra
M\otimes_{\mathrm{bin}}M^{\mathrm{op}}
which is the completion of
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
with respect to the
C^{*}
-norm

\|x\|_{\mathrm{bin}}=\sup\{\|\pi_{\varphi}(x)\|\colon\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M\otimes_{\mathrm{bin}}M^{\mathrm{op}})\}

For all these facts, see
[
8
]
.

In order to state the next theorem, we briefly describe the subset of what we call
mixing
binormal states on
M
; see Section 4 for detailed definitions. Let
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
be the associated pointed
M
-bimodule. Then
\xi_{\varphi}
is said to be
(two-sided) bounded
if there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\|x\xi_{\varphi}\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\xi_{\varphi}x\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}\quad(x\in M).

It turns out that such a vector yields a normal, completely positive map
\phi_{\varphi}:M\rightarrow M
characterized by

\tau(\phi_{\varphi}(x)y)=\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle\quad(x,y\in M).

Boundedness of
\xi_{\varphi}
implies moreover that
\phi_{\varphi}
extends to a bounded linear operator
\widehat{\phi_{\varphi}}
on
L^{2}(M,\tau)
; then
\phi_{\varphi}
is called
L^{2}
-compact
if
\widehat{\phi_{\varphi}}
is a compact operator.

Finally,
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
is called
mixing
if
\xi_{\varphi}
is bounded and if
\widehat{\phi_{\varphi}}
is
L^{2}
-compact. We denote by
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(M)
the set of all mixing binormal states on
M
.

Our second result is then the following promised characterization of the Haagerup property for finite von Neumann algebras which is proved in Section 4.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra. Then
M
has the Haagerup property if and only if
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(M)
is dense in
\mathrm{bin}(M)
.

Our final result is motivated by the following question posed by the referee: Since Theorem 1.1 is inspired by Glasner-Weiss Theorem on property (T), does Theorem 1.2 have an analogue in the context of property (T) for von Neumann algebras?

Using
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
and
[
14
, Theorem 3.10]
, we prove in Section 5 a characterization of property (T) for type II
1
factors (the latter property will be recalled in Section 5) based on what we call weakly mixing binormal states. More precisely, let
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
be its associated pointed
M
-bimodule. Let
\rho_{\varphi}:U(M)\rightarrow U(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})
be the unitary representation defined by

\rho_{\varphi}(u)\xi=u\xi u^{*}\quad(u\in U(M),\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}).

Then we say that
\varphi
is
weakly mixing
if the unitary representation
\rho_{\varphi}
is weakly mixing, i.e. it has no non-zero finite-dimensional subrepresentation. See for instance
[
4
]
. We denote by
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{wmix}}(M)
the set of all weakly mixing binormal states on
M
.

Let us fix one more notation before stating our last result. Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra; we denote by
\varphi_{\tau}
the following binormal state on
M
:
\varphi_{\tau}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\tau(xy)
for all
x,y\in M
. It is easy to check that its associated completely positive map is
\mathrm{id}_{M}
.

Here is our last result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
factor and let
\tau
be its tracial state. Then
M
has property (T) if and only if
\varphi_{\tau}
does not belong to the weak
∗
closure of
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{wmix}}(M)
.

2.
Prerequisites for the proof of Theorem
1.1

2.1.
Actions on
C^{*}
-algebras and their invariant states

In this section, we recall properties of (
C^{*}
-)dynamical systems that will be useful in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Some results should be well known, but for the sake of clarity, we provide some proofs for the reader’s convenience.

Let
(A,G,\alpha)
be a unital
C^{*}
-dynamical system. We always assume that
S_{G}(A)
is non-empty. Hence,
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
is non-empty either, as the former is the weak
∗
closed convex hull of the latter by Krein-Milman Theorem.

Given
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)
, we denote by
(\pi_{\varphi},H_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi},u_{\varphi})
the Gel’fand-Naimark-Segal (GNS) construction associated to the pair
(A,\varphi)
:
\pi_{\varphi}:A\rightarrow B(H_{\varphi})
is a
*
-representation of
A
on
H_{\varphi}
,
\xi_{\varphi}\in H_{\varphi}
is a cyclic, unit vector such that

\varphi(x)=\langle\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle\quad(x\in A)

and
u_{\varphi}
is a unitary representation of
G
on
H_{\varphi}
characterized by

u_{\varphi}(g)\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(\alpha_{g}(x))\xi_{\varphi}\quad(g\in G,\ x\in A).

It implements the action
\alpha
on
H_{\varphi}
in the sense that

u_{\varphi}(g)\pi_{\varphi}(x)u_{\varphi}(g^{-1})=\pi_{\varphi}(\alpha_{g}(x))\quad(g\in G,\ x\in A).

Recall that, for a given dynamical system
(X,G)
, every element
\mu\in M_{G}(X)
corresponds to a state in
S_{G}(C(X))
still denoted by
\mu
and defined by

\mu(a)=\int_{X}a(x)d\mu(x)\quad(a\in C(X),x\in X).

Then the GNS construction yields
H_{\mu}=L^{2}(X,\mu)
,
\xi_{\mu}=1_{X}
,
\pi_{\mu}(a)\xi=a\cdot\xi
for all
a\in C(X)
and
\xi\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
and
(u_{\mu}(g)\xi)(x)=\xi(g^{-1}x)
for all
g\in G
,
\xi\in L^{2}(X,\mu)
and
x\in X
.

For further use, we observe that, as
\xi_{\mu}=1_{X}
is a cyclic vector for
\pi_{\mu}(C(X))
,

\pi_{\mu}(C(X))^{\prime}\cap u_{\mu}(G)^{\prime}=L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\cap u_{\mu}(G)^{\prime}=\{a\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)\colon g\cdot a=a\ \forall g\in G\}

is the space of
G
-fixed elements of
L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
.
See for instance
[
17
, Proposition 3.4.2]
for the equality
\pi_{\mu}(C(X))^{\prime}=L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
. This means that
\mu
is ergodic in the usual sense (i.e.
\mu(A)(1-\mu(A))=0
for every
G
-invariant Borel set
A
) if and only if
\pi_{\mu}(C(X))^{\prime}\cap u_{\mu}(G)^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}1
. For general
C^{*}
-dynamical systems, one has (see for instance
[
19
, Exercise I.9.7]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(A,G,\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system such that
S_{G}(A)\not=\emptyset
, and let
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)
. Then
\varphi\in\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
if and only if
\pi_{\varphi}(A)^{\prime}\cap u_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}1
.

We observe that, as
u_{\varphi}(g)\xi_{\varphi}=\xi_{\varphi}
for every
g\in G
, the orthogonal subspace
(\mathbb{C}\xi_{\varphi})^{\perp}\eqqcolon H_{\varphi}^{0}
is
G
-invariant; we denote by
u_{\varphi}^{0}
the restriction of
u_{\varphi}
to
H_{\varphi}^{0}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(A,G,\alpha)
be a
C^{*}
-dynamical system such that
S_{G}(A)\not=\emptyset
.

(1)

If
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)\setminus\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
, then there exists
0\not=\xi\in H_{\varphi}^{0}
such that
u_{\varphi}(g)\xi=\xi
for every
g\in G
.

(2)

For
\varphi\in S_{G}(A)
, one has
\varphi\in S_{G,m}(A)
if and only if
u_{\varphi}^{0}
is a mixing representation, i.e. for all
\xi,\eta\in H_{\varphi}^{0}
, the associated coefficient function
g\mapsto\langle u_{\varphi}^{0}(g)\xi|\eta\rangle
belongs to
C_{0}(G)
.

(3)

One has
S_{G,m}(A)\subset\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
.

(4)

If
(B,G,\beta)
is a
C^{*}
-dynamical system such that there exists a unital,
G
-equivariant
*
-homomorphism
\theta:B\rightarrow A
, then
\theta_{*}(S_{G,m}(A))\subset S_{G,m}(B)
, where
\theta_{*}
is the dual map
\theta_{*}(\psi)=\psi\circ\theta
for every
\psi\in A^{*}
.

Proof.

(1) By Proposition
2.1
, there exists a projection
p\in\pi_{\varphi}(A)^{\prime}\cap u_{\varphi}(G)^{\prime}
such that
p\not=0,1
. We claim then that
0\not=p\xi_{\varphi}\not=\xi_{\varphi}
. Indeed, for instance if
p\xi_{\varphi}=0
, then we would have
p\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(x)p\xi_{\varphi}=0
for every
x\in A
, implying that
p=0
by cyclicity of
\xi_{\varphi}
. Then set
\xi=p\xi_{\varphi}-\langle p\xi_{\varphi}|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle\xi_{\varphi}
, which is orthogonal to
\xi_{\varphi}
and
u_{\varphi}^{0}(G)
-invariant. We claim that
\xi\not=0
. Otherwise, we would have

p\xi_{\varphi}=\langle\xi|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle\xi_{\varphi}=\|p\xi_{\varphi}\|^{2}\xi_{\varphi}
hence

\frac{p}{\|p\xi_{\varphi}\|^{2}}\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}=\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}\quad(x\in A)

and
p=\|p\xi_{\varphi}\|^{2}1
but, as
p^{2}=p
, this would imply that
\|p\xi_{\varphi}\|=0
or
1
, which is impossible by the above observations.

(2) Set
E\coloneqq\{\pi_{\varphi}(x)\xi_{\varphi}-\varphi(x)\xi_{\varphi}\colon x\in A\}
. Then it is a dense subspace of
H_{\varphi}^{0}
, as is easily verified. Next, we have for all
a,b\in A

\langle u_{\varphi}^{0}(g)[\pi_{\varphi}(a)\xi_{\varphi}-\varphi(a)\xi_{\varphi}]|\pi_{\varphi}(b)\xi_{\varphi}-\varphi(b)\xi_{\varphi}\rangle=\varphi(b^{*}\alpha_{g}(a))-\varphi(b^{*})\varphi(a)\to 0

as
{g\to\infty}
if and only if
\varphi\in S_{G,m}(A)
, and this proves the second assertion of the proposition.

(3) Applying (1), if there existed
\varphi\in S_{G,m}(A)
such that
\varphi\notin\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
, there would exist
\xi\not=0
such that
\xi\perp\xi_{\varphi}
and
u_{\varphi}(g)\xi=\xi
for all
g\in G
. But then

\|\xi\|^{2}=\lim_{g\to\infty}\langle u_{\varphi}(g)\xi|\xi\rangle=0

which is a contradiction.

(4) One has for all
\varphi\in S_{G,m}(A)
and
x,y\in B
:

\theta_{*}(\varphi)(\beta_{g}(x)y)=\varphi(\theta(\beta_{g}(x))\theta(y))=\varphi(\alpha_{g}(\theta(x))\theta(y))\\
\to_{g\to\infty}\varphi(\theta(x))\varphi(\theta(y))=\theta_{*}(\varphi)(x)\theta_{*}(\varphi)(y).

This shows that
\theta_{*}(\varphi)\in S_{G,m}(B)
.

Proofs of some results in
[
10
]
require the notion of infinite product of probability spaces. Here, these are replaced by infinite (minimal) tensor products of unital
C^{*}
-algebras, as presented in
[
20

.

Let
A
be a separable, unital
C^{*}
-algebra and

)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let us keep notations and definitions above.

(1)

If
\phi\subset\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(A)
and if
\varphi\in J_{G}(B,\phi)
is extremal in

, then it belongs to
\operatorname{Erg}_{G}(B)
.

(2)

If

then
\underset{n\geq 1}{\otimes}\varphi_{n}\in S_{G,m}(B)
.

Proof.

(1) Let us write
\varphi=t\omega+(1-t)\psi
with
\omega,\psi\in S_{G}(B)
. Then

\varphi(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\underset{n}{a}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots)=\varphi_{n}(a)=\\
t\omega(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\underset{n}{a}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots)+(1-t)\psi(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\underset{n}{a}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots)

for every

and every

, hence, since each

\omega(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\underset{n}{a}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots)=\psi(1\otimes\cdots\otimes\underset{n}{a}\otimes 1\otimes\cdots)=\varphi_{n}(a)

for all

and

, thus

, and as

is extremal in

(2) If
\varphi_{n}\in S_{G,m}(A)
for every

By density,
\underset{n\geq 1}{\otimes}\varphi_{n}\in S_{G,m}(B)

2.2.
The compact metrizable space

In order to prove statement (2) of Theorem

on the dynamical system

Let

denote the one-point compactification of the locally compact, second countable group

be the set of all closed subsets of

(also called the

It turns out that

is a compact, metrizable space on which

acts by left translations, with the convention that
g\,\infty=\infty
for every

.
Thus,
(\Sigma,G)
is a dynamical system. For future use, if

.

Consider now a dynamical system

such that

and every closed, non empty set

-equivariant and continuous. Hence, using Proposition

(4), we see that the associated measure
\theta_{C}(\mu)
on

defined by

belongs to

. Moreover, it is easy to check that

, where

denotes the singleton set

,
hence

\theta_{C}(\mu)(S)=\mu(C).

This implies that, if

are non empty closed subsets of

such that
\mu(C_{1})\not=\mu(C_{2})
, then
\theta_{C_{1}}(\mu)\not=\theta_{C_{2}}(\mu)

We will present a detailed proof of statement (2) of Theorem

even if it is very similar to the ones of

Proof of Theorem

(1) Let us assume that there is a

-dynamical system

G

. Recall that

We have to prove the existence of some

(3.1)

4.
The case of pairs of tracial von Neumann algebras and their binormal states

In the present section,
(M,\tau)
denotes a tracial von Neumann algebra and
B\subset M
a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
with the same unit. Most of notations come from
[
18
]
and
[
2
]
.

We denote by
L^{2}(M,\tau)
the Hilbert space obtained by completion of
M
with respect to the scalar product
(x,y)\mapsto\tau(y^{*}x)
; the embedding of
M
into
L^{2}(M,\tau)
is denoted by
x\mapsto\hat{x}
. It is equiped with the order two antilinear isometry
J
defined by
J\hat{x}=\widehat{x^{*}}
,
x\in M
.
It is a
M
-bimodule, called the
identity bimodule
, with respect to the left and right actions defined by
x\hat{y}z=\widehat{xyz}
, so that
\hat{y}z=Jz^{*}J\hat{y}
, for all
x,y,z\in M
.

Let
\langle M,B\rangle
be the von Neumann subalgebra of
B(L^{2}(M,\tau))
generated by
M
(acting on the left) and by the orthogonal projection
e_{B}
of
L^{2}(M,\tau)
onto
L^{2}(B,\tau)
. It is called the
basic construction for

B\subset M
.

In fact,
e_{B}
is the extension of the
\tau
-preserving conditional expectation
E_{B}
of
M
onto
B
. As is well known (e.g.
[
18
, Subsection 1.3.1]
),
e_{B}xe_{B}=E_{B}(x)e_{B}
for every
x\in M
,
\mathrm{Span}(Me_{B}M)
is a dense *-subalgebra of
\langle M,B\rangle
and
e_{B}\langle M,B\rangle e_{B}=Be_{B}
; in particular,
e_{B}
is a finite projection of
\langle M,B\rangle
. Moreover,
\langle M,B\rangle=JB^{\prime}J
, which shows that it is a semifinite von Neumann algebra. Thus, let us denote by
\mathcal{J}(\langle M,B\rangle)
the norm-closed ideal of
\langle M,B\rangle
generated by the finite projections of
\langle M,B\rangle
. Thus, an operator
T\in\langle M,B\rangle
belongs to
\mathcal{J}(\langle M,B\rangle)
if and only if all the spectral projections
1_{[s,\infty)}(|T|)
,
s>0
, are finite projections in
\langle M,B\rangle
. As recalled above,
e_{B}\in\mathcal{J}(\langle M,B\rangle)
.

4.1.

We recall from the introduction that, if

\|\xi x\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}

, and, for

,

.

A

-bimodule

, where

,

is
B
-central
, which means that

for every

\mathcal{H}(B)
the subspace of all

-central vectors of
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
the set of all bounded,

\phi

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a
M
-bimodule and
B\subset M
a von Neumann subalgebra of
M
.

(1)

The subset
\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
of bounded,
B
-central vectors is a dense subspace of
\mathcal{H}(B)
; we have moreover
(B^{\prime}\cap M)\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)(B^{\prime}\cap M)=\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
, and for every
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and all
a,b\in B^{\prime}\cap M
, we have
L_{a\xi b}=aL_{\xi}b
.

(2)

For all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and every
x\in M
then
L_{\eta}^{*}xL_{\xi}\in M
and the map
x\mapsto L_{\eta}^{*}xL_{\xi}
is
B
-bimodular. In particular, the map
\phi_{\xi}:x\mapsto\phi_{\xi}(x)\coloneqq L_{\xi}^{*}xL_{\xi}
belongs to
\mathrm{CP}(B\subset M)
.

(3)

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and let
c>0
be such that
\|x\xi\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}
for every
x\in M
. Then the completely positive map
\phi_{\xi}
satisfies the following inequality:

\tau\circ\phi_{\xi}(x)\leq c^{2}\tau(x)\quad(x\in M_{+}).

In particular, if
\xi
is a unit vector, then
\phi_{\xi}\in\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(B\subset M)
..

(4)

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
. If
a,b\in B^{\prime}\cap M
then

\widehat{\phi_{a\xi b}}=b^{*}Jb^{*}J\widehat{\phi_{\xi}}a^{*}Ja^{*}J.

Proof.
(1) Fix
\xi\in\mathcal{H}(B)
, and let
S,T\in L^{1}(M,\tau)_{+}
be the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of the states

\langle x\xi|\xi\rangle=\tau(xT)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\langle\xi y|\xi\rangle=\tau(yS)\quad(x,y\in M).

Fix an element
x\in M
and a unitary operator
u\in U(B)
. Then one has

\tau(xT)=\langle xu\xi u^{*}|\xi\rangle=\langle xu\xi|\xi u\rangle=\langle xu\xi|u\xi\rangle=\langle u^{*}xu\xi|\xi\rangle=\tau(u^{*}xuT)=\tau(xuTu^{*})

which shows that
T\in B^{\prime}\cap L^{1}(M,\tau)_{+}
, and the same holds true for
S
.

For every positive integer
n
, set
p_{n}=1_{[0,n]}(T),q_{n}=1_{[0,n]}(S)\in B^{\prime}\cap M
, so that
p_{n},q_{n}\nearrow 1
strongly and
p_{n}Tp_{n},q_{n}Sq_{n}\leq n
. Set then
\xi_{n}=p_{n}\xi q_{n}
. Then we have

\|x\xi_{n}\|^{2}\leq\|xp_{n}\xi\|^{2}=\langle p_{n}x^{*}xp_{n}\xi|\xi\rangle\\
=\tau(p_{n}x^{*}xp_{n}T)=\tau(x^{*}xp_{n}Tp_{n})\leq n\|x\|_{2}^{2}\quad(x\in M)

and similarly
\|\xi_{n}y\|^{2}\leq n\|y\|_{2}^{2}
,
y\in M
.

As
\|\xi-\xi_{n}\|\leq\|(1-p_{n})\xi\|+\|\xi(1-q_{n})\|
, this shows that
\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
is dense in
\mathcal{H}(B)
. Equalities
(B^{\prime}\cap M)\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)(B^{\prime}\cap M)=\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and
L_{a\xi b}=aL_{\xi}b
are straightforward.

(2) For
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and
x,y,z,w\in M
,

\langle Jz^{*}JL^{*}_{\eta}xL_{\xi}\hat{y}|\hat{w}\rangle=\langle L^{*}_{\eta}xL_{\xi}\hat{y}|\widehat{wz^{*}}\rangle\\
=\langle x\xi y|\eta(wz^{*})\rangle=\langle x\xi(yz)|\eta w\rangle=\langle L^{*}_{\eta}xL_{\xi}Jz^{*}J\hat{y}|\hat{w}\rangle,

which shows that
L^{*}_{\eta}xL_{\xi}\in M
. Moreover, it is obvious that
L_{\xi}b=bL_{\xi}
for every
b\in B
, and this ends the proof of (2).

(3) Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and
c>0
such that
\|x\xi\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}
for every
x\in M
. Then we get for
x\in M_{+}
:

\tau\circ\phi_{\xi}(x)=\langle L^{*}_{\xi}xL_{\xi}\hat{1}|\hat{1}\rangle=\langle x\xi|\xi\rangle=\|x^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\|x^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}=c^{2}\tau(x),

(4) By (1), one has
L_{a\xi b}=aL_{\xi}b
and
L^{*}_{a\xi b}=b^{*}L^{*}_{\xi}a^{*}
and thus

\phi_{a\xi b}(x)=b^{*}L^{*}_{\xi}a^{*}xaL_{\xi}b=b^{*}\phi_{\xi}(a^{*}xa)b\quad(x\in M)

and

\widehat{\phi_{a\xi b}}(\hat{x})=\widehat{b^{*}\phi_{\xi}(a^{*}xa)b}=b^{*}Jb^{*}J\widehat{\phi_{\xi}}(a^{*}Ja^{*}J\hat{x})

for every
x\in M
.
This ends the proof of Proposition
4.1
.

\Box

We still need to recall the definition of composition of
M
-bimodules. See for instance
[
2

\mathcal{H}

the

-bimodule obtained from the algebraic relative tensor product

It is gifted with the left and right actions of

given by

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K}
be
M
-bimodules and let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{0}(B)
and
\eta\in\mathcal{K}_{0}(B)
. Then
\xi\otimes_{M}\eta\in(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{K})_{0}(B)
and

\phi_{\xi\otimes_{M}\eta}=\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{K}
is a relative tensor product over
M
, one has for every
x\in M
:
(\xi x)\otimes_{M}\eta=\xi\otimes_{M}(x\eta)
. In particular, this implies that
b(\xi\otimes_{M}\eta)=(\xi\otimes_{M}\eta)b
for every
b\in B
.

Let now
c>0
be large enough so that

\|x\xi\|,\|x\eta\|\leq c\|x\|_{2}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\|\xi y\|,\|\eta y\|\leq c\|y\|_{2}

for all
x,y\in M
.

Fix
x\in M
first; one has

\displaystyle\|x(\xi\otimes_{M}\eta)\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle(x\xi)\otimes_{M}\eta|(x\xi)\otimes_{M}\eta\rangle=\langle L_{x\xi}^{*}L_{x\xi}\eta|\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle L^{*}_{\xi}x^{*}xL_{\xi}\eta|\eta\rangle=\|\phi_{\xi}(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\eta\|^{2}\leq c^{2}\|\phi_{\xi}(x^{*}x)^{1/2}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=c^{2}\tau(\phi_{\xi}(x^{*}x))=c^{2}\langle L_{\xi}^{*}x^{*}xL_{\xi}\hat{1}|\hat{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=c^{2}\langle x^{*}x\xi|\xi\rangle\leq c^{4}\|x\|_{2}^{2}.

Next, for
y\in M
one has

\displaystyle\|(\xi\otimes_{M}\eta)y\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\xi\otimes_{M}(\eta y)\|^{2}=\langle L_{\xi}^{*}L_{\xi}\eta y|\eta y\rangle

\displaystyle\leq c^{2}\|\eta y\|^{2}\leq c^{4}\|y\|_{2}^{2}.

This proves that
\xi\otimes_{M}\eta\in(\mathcal{H}\otimes_{M}\mathcal{K})_{0}(B)
.

Finally, if
x,y,z\in M
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\xi\otimes_{M}\eta}(x)\hat{y}|\hat{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle L^{*}_{\xi\otimes_{M}\eta}xL_{\xi\otimes_{M}\eta}\hat{y}|\hat{z}\rangle=\langle(x\xi)\otimes_{M}(\eta y)|\xi\otimes_{M}(\eta z)\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle L_{\xi}^{*}L_{x\xi}\eta y|\eta z\rangle=\langle L^{*}_{\eta}L^{*}_{\xi}xL_{\xi}L_{\eta}\hat{y}|\hat{z}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\phi_{\eta}\circ\phi_{\xi}(x)\hat{y}|\hat{z}\rangle

This ends the proof of the present lemma.
∎

4.2.
B
-mixing binormal states

Following
[
8
]
, a
binormal state
on
M
is a linear functional
\varphi
on the algebraic tensor product
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
such that:

•

for every
X\in M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
, one has
\varphi(X^{*}X)\geq 0
and
\varphi(1\otimes 1^{\mathrm{op}})=1
;

•

\varphi
is separately normal: for every
y\in M
, the functionals
x\mapsto\varphi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})
and
x\mapsto\varphi(y\otimes x^{\mathrm{op}})
are normal.

We denote here by
\mathrm{bin}(M)
the convex set of all binormal states on
M
. It is a convex, weak*-dense subset of the state space of the
C^{*}
-algebra
M\otimes_{\mathrm{bin}}M^{\mathrm{op}}
, the latter being the completion of
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
with respect to the
C^{*}
-norm

\|X\|_{\mathrm{bin}}\coloneqq\sup\{\|\pi_{\varphi}(X)\|\colon\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)\}

where
(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi},\pi_{\varphi},\xi_{\varphi})
denotes the GNS triple associated to
\varphi
:
\pi_{\varphi}
is a
*
-representation of
M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
and
\xi_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is a cyclic and unit vector such that
\varphi(X)=\langle\pi_{\varphi}(X)\xi_{\varphi}|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle
for every
X\in M\otimes M^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Observe that every
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
gives rise to the pointed
M
-bimodule
\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
by the following formula:

\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle=\varphi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})\quad(x,y\in M)

and conversely, if
(\mathcal{H},\xi)
is a pointed
M
-bimodule, we get the following element
\varphi_{\xi}
of
\mathrm{bin}(M)
:

\varphi_{\xi}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\langle x\xi y|\xi\rangle\quad(x,y\in M).

Given
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(M)
, it is straightforward to verify that
\xi_{\varphi}
is
B
-central if and only if one has for all
x,y\in M
and
b\in B
:

\varphi(xb\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\varphi(x\otimes(by)^{\mathrm{op}})=\varphi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}}b^{\mathrm{op}}).

We denote by
\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
the (convex) subset of all elements of
\mathrm{bin}(M)
whose associated unit vector is
B
-central.

Finally, let
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
; we say that
\varphi
is
B
-mixing
if its associated vector
\xi_{\varphi}\in\mathcal{H}_{\varphi}
is bounded and if the corresponding cp map
\phi_{\xi_{\varphi}}
belongs to
\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(B\subset M)
and is
B
-compact. We denote by
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B\subset M)
the set of all
B
-mixing binormal states in
\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
.

Proof of Theorem

.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(M,\tau)
be a tracial von Neumann algebra and let
B\subset M
be a von Neumann subalgebra that has the same unit as
M
. Then
M
has property
\mathrm{H}
relative to

if and only if
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B\subset M)
is weak*-dense in
\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
.

Proof.

Assume that
M
has property H relative to
B
. By
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
, there exists a net
(\psi_{\beta})_{\beta\in\Gamma}\subset\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(B\subset M)
such that, furthermore,
\psi_{\beta}(1)=1
,
\tau\circ\psi_{\beta}=\tau
and
\widehat{\psi_{\beta}}\in\mathcal{J}(\langle M,B\rangle)
for every

, and such that

\lim_{\beta\in\Gamma}\|\psi_{\beta}(x)-x\|_{2}=0\quad(x\in M).

Let then
\varphi\in\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
, a finite set
F\subset M
and
\varepsilon>0
. By Proposition
4.1
, there exists a unit vector
\xi\in(\mathcal{H}_{\varphi})_{0}(B)
such that

\|\xi_{\varphi}-\xi\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4(1+\max_{x,y\in F}\|x\|\|y\|)}.

There exists some
\beta\in\Gamma
such that
\phi\coloneqq\psi_{\beta}
belongs to
\mathrm{CP}_{\tau}(B\subset M)
with
\hat{\phi}\in\mathcal{J}(\langle M,B\rangle)

\max_{x\in F}\|\phi(\phi_{\xi}(x))-\phi_{\xi}(x)\|_{2}<\frac{\varepsilon}{2(1+\max_{y\in F}\|y\|)}.

By Lemma
4.2
,
\phi\circ\phi_{\xi}=\phi_{\xi\otimes_{M}\xi_{\phi}}
is
B
-bimodular and
B
-compact. Let then
\psi\in\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B\subset M)
be defined by

\psi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})=\tau(\phi_{\xi\otimes_{M}\xi_{\phi}}(x)y)=\langle x(\xi\otimes_{M}\xi_{\phi})y|\xi\otimes_{M}\xi_{\phi}\rangle\quad(x,y\in M).

One has for all

\displaystyle|\varphi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})-\psi(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})|

\displaystyle=|\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle-\tau(\phi\circ\phi_{\xi}(x)y)|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle-\langle x\xi y|\xi\rangle|+|\tau(\phi_{\xi}(x)y)-\tau(\phi\circ\phi_{\xi}(x)y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+|\langle x\xi_{\varphi}y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle-\langle x\xi y|\xi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+|\langle x(\xi_{\varphi}-\xi)y|\xi_{\varphi}\rangle|+|\langle x\xi y|\xi_{\varphi}-\xi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+2\|x\|\|y\|\|\xi_{\varphi}-\xi\|\leq\varepsilon.

This shows that
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B\subset M)
is dense in
\mathrm{bin}(B\subset M)
.

Conversely, suppose that
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{mix}}(B\subset M)

.

. One

\displaystyle=\tau(\phi_{i}(x^{*}x))+\tau(\phi_{i}(1)xx^{*})-2\operatorname{Re}\tau(\phi_{i}(x)x)

\xi_{i}=\xi_{\varphi_{i}}
for short, and assume by contradiction that there is a net
(\varphi_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{wmix}}(M)
which converges weakly
∗
to
\varphi_{\tau}
. This means that

\sup_{u\in U(M)}|\langle u\xi_{i}u^{*}|\xi_{i}\rangle-1|\to_{i}0.

Then choose
i\in I
such that

\sup_{u\in U(M)}|\langle u\xi_{i}u^{*}|\xi_{i}\rangle-1|<\frac{1}{2}.

This implies that
\operatorname{Re}\langle u\xi_{i}u^{*}|\xi_{i}\rangle\geq 1/2
for every
u\in U(M)
. Let next
K_{i}
be the norm-closed convex hull of
\{u\xi_{i}u^{*}\colon u\in U(M)\}
in the unit ball of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Then one has
\operatorname{Re}\langle\zeta|\xi_{i}\rangle\geq 1/2
for every
\zeta\in K_{i}
, and in particular, if
\eta\in K_{i}
is the element of minimal norm, we have
\operatorname{Re}\langle\eta|\xi_{i}\rangle\geq 1/2
. Thus it is non-zero, and its uniqueness implies that
u\eta u^{*}=\eta
for every
u\in U(M)
. But this contradicts the fact that
\varphi_{i}
is weakly mixing as the associated unitary representation
\rho_{i}
of
U(M)
would contain the trivial representation.

Conversely, let us assume that
M
does not have property (T). By Theorems 3.6 and 3.10 in
[
14
]
, there exists a net of pointed
M
-bimodules
((\mathcal{H}_{i},\xi_{i}))_{i\in I}
with the following properties:

(a)

\lim_{i}(\|\langle\cdot\ \xi_{i}|\xi_{i}\rangle-\tau\|+\|\langle\xi_{i}\cdot|\xi_{i}\rangle-\tau\|)=0
;

(b)

\lim_{j}\|x\xi_{i}-\xi_{i}x\|=0
for every
x\in M
;

(c)

the unitary representation
\pi_{i}:U(M)\rightarrow U(\mathcal{H}_{i})
defined by
\pi_{i}(u)\xi=u\xi u^{*},u\in U(M),\xi\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
, is weakly mixing.

Let
\varphi_{i}
be the binormal state associated to
(\mathcal{H}_{i},\xi_{i})
. Then it belongs to
\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{wmix}}(M)
, and one has for all
x,y\in M

\displaystyle|\varphi_{i}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})-\varphi_{\tau}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})|

\displaystyle=|\langle x\xi_{i}y|\xi_{i}\rangle-\tau(xy)|

\displaystyle=|\langle x(\xi_{i}y-y\xi_{i})|\xi_{i}\rangle+\langle xy\xi_{i}|\xi_{i}\rangle-\tau(xy)|

\displaystyle\leq\|x\|\|\xi_{i}y-y\xi_{i}\|+\|x\|\|y\|\|\langle\cdot\xi_{i}|\xi_{i}\rangle-\tau\|.

Thus
\lim_{i}|\varphi_{i}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})-\varphi_{\tau}(x\otimes y^{\mathrm{op}})|=0
. This proves that
\varphi_{\tau}\in\overline{\mathrm{bin}_{\mathrm{wmix}}(M)}^{w*}
, and this ends the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

\Box

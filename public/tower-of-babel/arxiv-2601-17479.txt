On Proper Descent of Smooth Affine Surfaces with Finite Homotopy Rank-Sum
Buddhadev Hajra

Abstract
. We study the descent behaviour of homotopy-theoretic properties of smooth complex affine surfaces under finite surjective morphisms. We first examine the Eilenberg–MacLane property and show, by means of an explicit counterexample, that it does not descend under proper morphisms in general. This negative result motivates the introduction of a weaker notion, the finite homotopy rank-sum property . Our main theorem establishes that this property does descend under proper morphisms between smooth affine surfaces of logarithmic Kodaira dimension at most zero. The proof relies essentially on the recent classification of smooth complex affine surfaces of log non-general type characterized by these two properties. As a further application, we classify smooth affine surfaces properly dominated by the complex algebraic 2-torus, thereby clarifying an earlier remark of M. Furushima.

Introduction

We begin by motivating our interest in studying smooth complex algebraic surfaces that are also Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. A path-connected topological space
X
is said to be an
Eilenberg–MacLane space
if, for some integer
n\in\mdmathbb{Z}_{>0}
and a group
G

\pi_{k}(X)=\begin{cases}G,&\text{for }k=n,\\[5.69054pt]
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and is denoted by
K(G,n)
. For any abelian group
G
and any integer
n\,\in\,\mdmathbb{N}
, in the category of CW-complexes an Eilenberg–MacLane
K(G,\,n)
space exists
and it is unique up to homotopy (cf.
[
23
, Chapter 8, §1, Corollary 5]
and
[
20
, Corollary 2]
). So we use the same notation
K(G,\,n)
to denote any CW complex belonging to this homotopy class.

Throughout this paper, all algebraic varieties and morphisms are defined over the field of complex numbers
\mdmathbb{C}
. An affine variety
V
is said to be
properly dominated
by an affine variety
W
if there is a proper surjective morphism
\varphi:V\rightarrow W
of
V
onto

V=\operatorname{Spec}B
and
W=\operatorname{Spec}A
, this is equivalent to
B
being integral (or finite) over
A
.

By Noether normalization, every affine variety of dimension
d
admits a finite surjective morphism onto
\mdmathbb{A}^{d}
; in particular, every affine variety is properly dominated by the affine space of the same dimension.

Suppose
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a finite surjective morphism between smooth algebraic surfaces. A classical result of J.-P. Serre asserts that the image of the induced homomorphism

f_{\ast}\colon\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Y)

has finite index in
\pi_{1}(Y)
, and moreover, this index divides the degree of

(cf.
[
22

). Consequently, if
\pi_{1}(X)
is finite, then
\pi_{1}(Y)
must also be finite. This naturally leads to the following question.

Question 1.1

Let

be a finite surjective morphism of smooth affine surfaces. If
\pi_{2}(X)
is finite, is
\pi_{2}(Y)
also finite?

To approach this, consider a smooth affine surface

for which

is finite. It is well known that every algebraic variety admits a universal covering space. Let

denote the universal covering map. By the Hurewicz theorem,

Thus, Question

Question 1.2

Let

be a fiber bundle with connected fiber
F
. If
F
is contractible, then
f
is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

The long exact sequence in homotopy associated with the fiber sequence

F{\mathrel{\rightarrow}^{i}}X\xrightarrow{f}Y

is given by

\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{k}(F)\xrightarrow{i_{\ast}}\pi_{k}(X)\xrightarrow{f_{\ast}}\pi_{k}(Y)\xrightarrow{\delta_{k}}\pi_{k-1}(F)\rightarrow\cdots\qquad(k\geq 1).

Since
F
is connected and contractible, we have
\pi_{i}(F)=0
for all
i\geq 0
. Hence, exactness implies that

f_{\ast}\colon\pi_{i}(X)\xrightarrow{\ \cong\ }\pi_{i}(Y)

is an isomorphism for every

. The result now follows from Whitehead’s theorem, which asserts that a map between connected CW complexes inducing isomorphisms on all homotopy groups is a homotopy equivalence (see, e.g.,
[
26
, Theorem 4.5]
).
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

For a smooth complex algebraic variety
X
, let
f\colon X\rightarrow B
be an algebraic
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth complex algebraic curve
B
. Then

if and only if
B
is affine.

Proof.

Since
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
is contractible, Lemma
2.10
implies that

is an isomorphism, so

b_{2}(X)=0
if and only if

, which happens precisely when

is non-complete; equivalently,

is affine.
∎

Lemma 2.12

Let

be an

is a smooth affine rational curve. If

(1)

is affine.

(2)

All fibers of

are irreducible.

Proof.

Let

be an

a smooth affine
\mdmathbb{Q}
-factorial surface and

a smooth algebraic curve. Embed

Since

\mdmathbb{Q}

3.
Proof of Main Results

The results in this section aim to clarify this descent phenomenon by establishing precise geometric conditions under which it holds, starting from the case of negative log Kodaira dimension and proceeding to more general settings. More precisely, we address Question
1.2
and Question
1.5
, which concern the descent of the Eilenberg–MacLane property and the finite homotopy rank-sum property under finite morphisms between smooth affine varieties. Our approach proceeds through a sequence of reductions and structural results, beginning with a key observation that serves as a common starting point for many of the subsequent proofs by allowing us to assume surjectivity at the level of fundamental groups.

Remark 3.1
.

We first reduce to the case where the natural homomorphism

f_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Y)

is surjective.

If
f_{\ast}
is not surjective, then by standard covering space theory, we can find a smooth affine variety
Z
such that
f
admits the following factorization:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where
h:X\rightarrow Z
is a finite morphism and
g:Z\rightarrow Y
is a finite covering map satisfying
f=g\circ h
. Moreover, we have

\pi_{1}(Z)\cong g_{\ast}(\pi_{1}(Z))=f_{\ast}(\pi_{1}(X)),

so that
h_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Z)
is surjective, and
\deg g\cdot\deg h=\deg f
.

Now, if
Z
is an Eilenberg–MacLane space, or if it has finite homotopy rank-sum, then the same property holds for
Y
, since a finite covering map
g
preserves the higher homotopy groups of
Z
and
Y
. Thus, we can reduce to the case where the induced homomorphism at the level of fundamental groups is surjective, by considering the finite surjective morphism
g
in place of
f
and establishing the result for
Z
first.

3.1.
On the descent of Eilenberg–MacLane affine surfaces when
\bar{\kappa}(X)=-\infty

We begin by proving the finite étale descent is true for Zariski locally-trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundles.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
V
be a smooth affine surface admitting a Zariski locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure over a smooth affine (respectively, projective) curve. Let
\varphi:W\rightarrow V
be a finite étale covering of smooth affine surfaces. Then
W
also admits a Zariski locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure over a smooth affine (respectively, projective) curve.

Proof.

Assume first that
V
admits a Zariski locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure over a smooth affine curve (or a projective curve of positive genus), say
B
. Then by Lemma
2.10
, the fiber being contractible implies that
V
is homotopy equivalent to
B
, hence
V
is a
K(G,1)
-space.

Since
\varphi:W\rightarrow V
is a finite topological covering,
W
is also a
K(G,1)
-space. Moreover,
\overline{\kappa}(V)=-\infty
implies
\overline{\kappa}(W)=-\infty
. Therefore, by Theorem
1.3
, the surface
W
admits a Zariski locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure over a smooth algebraic curve
C\not\cong\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
. Again, Lemma
2.10
yields that
W
is homotopy equivalent to
C
.

Case 1.

B
is affine.

Assume that
C
is projective. Then either
C
is an elliptic curve or
\mathrm{genus}(C)\geq 2
. In the latter case,
\pi_{1}(C)
is not free, whereas
\pi_{1}(B)
is free since
B
is affine. The induced morphism
\varphi_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(W)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(V)
is injective and
\pi_{1}(W)\cong\pi_{1}(C)
is a subgroup of
\pi_{1}(V)\cong\pi_{1}(B)
, hence must be free. Thus
\mathrm{genus}(C)\geq 2
is impossible, and therefore
C
must be an elliptic curve.

In this situation

e(W)=e(C)=0,\qquad e(W)=\deg(\varphi)\cdot e(V),\qquad e(V)=e(B).

Hence
e(B)=0
. Since
B
is a smooth affine curve, it follows that
B\cong\mdmathbb{C}^{\ast}
and
\pi_{1}(V)\cong\pi_{1}(\mdmathbb{C}^{\ast})\cong\mdmathbb{Z}
. But
\pi_{1}(W)\cong\pi_{1}(C)\cong\mdmathbb{Z}\oplus\mdmathbb{Z}
, contradicting injectivity of
\varphi_{\ast}
. Thus
C
cannot be projective. This concludes the affine case.

Case 2.

B
is projective of positive genus.

Suppose
C
is affine. Then
b_{2}(W)=b_{2}(C)=0
, while
b_{2}(V)=b_{2}(B)=1
. This contradicts Lemma
2.15
(2), which prohibits such a finite surjective morphism. Therefore, if
B
is projective of positive genus,
C
must also be projective of positive genus.

Finally, if
B\cong\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
, then
V
is simply connected. Hence, any finite étale covering
\varphi
must be an isomorphism. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we continue with the simplest geometric situation where the log Kodaira dimension of
X
is
-\infty
. This case already captures the essential features of the descent problem and provides the basic mechanism behind our general arguments. The following theorem shows that in this case, the Eilenberg–MacLane property descends under any finite surjective morphism.

We now derive another important consequence of this theorem that shows how this phenomenon extends to a wider class of affine surfaces by moving from the special case of a trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle to a more general situation in which the total space itself may carry a nontrivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure. The following result shows that the descent of the Eilenberg–MacLane property continues to hold in this broader setting.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Zariski-locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth affine irreducible curve. Suppose
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
is a finite surjective morphism onto a smooth affine surface. Then
Y
is a Zariski-locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth affine irreducible curve.

Proof.

With the notations in Remark
3.1
, we first factorize
f
into
g
and
h
.
By construction, the induced homomorphism
h_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Z)
is surjective. Since
X
is an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth affine curve, we have
b_{2}(X)=0
by Corollary
2.11
. Using Lemma
2.15
(2), we deduce
b_{2}(Z)=0
.

As
\bar{\kappa}(X)=-\infty
, then
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=\bar{\kappa}(Z)=-\infty
using Lemma
2.15
(3). Hence
Z
admits an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration
\varphi:Y\rightarrow D
over a smooth algebraic curve
D
. The induced map
\varphi_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(Z)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(D)
is surjective, yielding
b_{1}(Z)\geq b_{1}(D)
.

Apply Suzuki’s formula (Lemma
2.4
) to
\varphi
:

e(Z)=e(D)+s,\qquad s:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\bigl(e(F_{i})-e(\mdmathbb{A}^{1})\bigr),

where
\{F_{i}:1\leq i\leq r\}
are the singular fibers. Using
b_{2}(Z)=0
, this gives:

b_{1}(D)-b_{1}(Z)=b_{2}(D)+s.

Since
b_{1}(Z)\geq b_{1}(D)
, we obtain

b_{1}(Z)=b_{1}(D),\qquad b_{2}(D)=0=s.

Thus
D
is affine and there is no reducible fiber, i.e., every singular fiber of
\varphi
, if there is any, is scheme-theoretically isomorphic to
m\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
for an integer
m>1
using
[
17
, Chapter I, Lemma 4.4]
).

If
\varphi
has any multiple fiber, we eliminate all multiplicities via the ramified covering trick (cf. Lemma
2.9
). Thus, we obtain a finite étale cover
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
such that
Z^{\prime}
admits an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration
\varphi^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow D^{\prime}
over an affine curve
D^{\prime}
having no multiple fiber. Since
h_{\ast}
is surjective, we may pull back the étale cover
Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
via
h
to get a smooth affine surface
X^{\prime}
that is irreducible with a pulled back étale cover
X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
admitting a finite surjective morphism
h^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Z^{\prime}
. See the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}:=X\times_{Z}Z^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Z^{\prime}:=\overline{Z\times_{D}D^{\prime}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\begin{array}[]{c}\text{ramified}\\
\text{covering}\end{array}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

By Proposition
3.2
,
X^{\prime}
is again an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth affine curve—a surface that is structurally similar to
X
. Therefore, by what we proved above,
\varphi^{\prime}
must not have any reducible fiber. Thus, in turn,
\varphi
has no multiple fibers as the ramified covering trick to eliminate multiple fiber ensures that the irreducible multiple fiber, say
\varphi^{*}(p)
, to split into reducible fiber with irreducible components being affine lines taken with reduced structures in
\varphi^{\prime*}(q)
so that the greatest common divisor of these split multiplicities become
1
and the fiber
\varphi^{\prime*}(q)
become non-multiple, where
q
is the unique pre-image of
p
under the totally ramified cover
D^{\prime}\rightarrow D
. This implies
\varphi
is Zariski–locally trivial.

Thus
Y
being a finite étale descent of
Z
is first of all topologically a
K(G,1)
-space and thus
Y
admits a Zariski–locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure over a smooth algebraic curve, say
B
, using Theorem
1.3
. Now,
b_{2}(Z)=0
implies that
b_{2}(Y)=0
and hence
B
is affine, as desired, by Lemma
2.15
(2) followed by Corollary
2.11
. This completes the proof.
∎

We deduce the following result as a consequence of Theorem

. This result is well-known and was proved in
[

Corollary 3.4
.

Let

be a smooth affine surface properly dominated by
\mdmathbb{A}^{2}
. Then

.

Proof.

Theorem
3.3
implies that

must be isomorphic to an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth affine curve, say

is homotopy equivalent to

Our next result shows that the product structure of

Let

. Then

for some

Since

, we see that

is a locally trivial bundle over a smooth affine curve

, and hence

Next, we conclude this section with the proof of one of our key results.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a finite surjective morphism of smooth affine surfaces. If
\bar{\kappa}(X)=-\infty
and

satisfies the finite homotopy rank-sum property, then so does

.

First note that, since
Y
is properly dominated by

and
\bar{\kappa}(X)=-\infty
, thus
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=-\infty
using Lemma

(3). Since

satisfies the finite homotopy rank-sum property and
\bar{\kappa}(X)=-\infty
, it follows from Theorem

and Theorem

that

is isomorphic to one of the following surfaces:

(1)

\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth algebraic curve not isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
;

(2)

homotopic to
S^{2}
;

(3)

a
\mdmathbb{Q}
-homology plane with fundamental group

.

Note that the universal cover of a

\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over a smooth algebraic curve not isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
or is homotopic to

If

\mdmathbb{A}^{1}

If

Now the following proposition completes the proof.

Proposition 3.8
.

With the notations as in Theorem

, suppose there exists a Zariski–locally trivial
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle structure

satisfies the finite homotopy rank-sum property.

Using Remark

\mdmathbb{A}^{1}

by the proof of Corollary

. Using Lemma

can be either

or

. As

, thus

admits an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration

over a smooth algebraic curve

. Thus the induced homomorphism

. Apply Suzuki’s formula (cf. Theorem

) to

:

where

are the singular fibers of

. This gives:

(3.1)

Case 1.

Suppose

is not isomorphic to

or
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}

Since

m,n>1
;

(6)

(1)
— the trivial group.

In this case, since
\pi_{1}(Z)
is infinite, the last three possibilities are ruled out and thus
\pi_{1}(Z)
is isomorphic to either
\mdmathbb{Z}^{2}
, or
\mdmathbb{Z}\oplus(\mdmathbb{Z}/m\mdmathbb{Z})
for some
m>1
, or
\mdmathbb{Z}
. Since we assumed that
e(Z)>0
, Proposition
3.12
and Corollary
3.13
together imply that
\pi_{1}(Z)\cong\mdmathbb{Z}^{2}
. Now
\pi_{1}(Z)\cong\mdmathbb{Z}^{2}
with
e(Z)>0
yield that
b_{2}(Z)\geq 2
, which again contradicts the existence of such a finite morphism
h:X\rightarrow Z
since

1=b_{2}(\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*})=b_{2}(X)<b_{2}(Z).

This implies that
e(Z)=0=e(Z^{\prime})
and hence
Z=Z^{\prime}
. Already we have observed that
\pi_{1}(Z)
must be abelian and since
\pi_{1}(H[-1,0,-1])
is non-abelian (cf.
[
15
, Table 1]
),
Z^{\prime}
and hence
Z
must be isomorphic
O(1,1,1)
, which is further isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. This completes the proof of the above claim.∎

Clearly
Z
being isomorphic to
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
is Eilenberg-MacLane. Hence,
Y
is also Eilenberg-MacLane as
Z
is a topological covering of
Y
. Now it follows from Theorem
1.3
that up to isomorphism of varieties,
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
and
H[-1,0,-1]
are the only two affine smooth Eilenberg-MacLane surfaces with
\bar{\kappa}=0
. Therefore,
Y
is isomorphic to one of these two smooth affine surfaces. This completes the proof of this case and the theorem as well.
∎

Remark 3.15
.

In
[
6
]
, M. Furushima remarked (without any proof) at the end of his paper that
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
is the only smooth affine surface with
\bar{\kappa}=0
that can be properly dominated by
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. To the best of our knowledge, no detailed proof of this assertion has appeared in the literature since then. Our result in Theorem
3.14
provides a more comprehensive picture, showing that Furushima’s claim does not hold in full generality. Indeed,
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
occurs as a
2
-fold étale finite cover of Fujita’s surface
H[-1,0,-1]
(see
[
8
, Proposition 5.8]
and the proof therein). Moreover, our theorem opens the possibility that a smooth affine surface homotopic to
S^{2}
could be properly dominated by
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. However, we do not presently know of any construction of a finite surjective morphism from
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
onto such a surface.

Another main result of this subsection is the following.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
Y
be a smooth affine surface properly dominated by
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
with
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=-\infty
. Then
Y
is isomorphic to either
\mdmathbb{A}^{2}
or
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
X:=\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
properly dominate
Y
via a finite surjective morphism, say
f:X\rightarrow Y
. We start the same way as above. If
\deg(f)=1
, then
f
is an isomorphism by Zariski’s Main Theorem, but this can not happen as
\bar{\kappa}(Y)=-\infty
whereas
\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
. So,
\deg f>1
.

With the notations in Remark
3.1
, we first factorize
f
into
g
and
h
. By construction, the induced homomorphism
h_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(Z)
is surjective. Since
\pi_{1}(X)=\pi_{1}(\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*})\cong\mdmathbb{Z}^{2}
, therefore
\pi_{1}(Z)
must be abelian.

Firstly, note that
\bar{\kappa}(Z)=\bar{\kappa}(Y)=-\infty
as
g:Z\rightarrow Y
is a finite étale morphism. By a well-known result due to Fujita–Miyanishi–Sugie (cf.
[
4
]
, cf.
[
16
]
, cf.
[
24
]
),
Z
admits an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration, say
\varphi:Z\rightarrow B
onto a smooth algebraic curve
B
. Since
Z
is a smooth affine surface, every fiber of
\varphi
is a disjoint union of affine lines, if taken with reduced structures (cf.
[
17
]
). Since
X
being
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
is a rational variety,
Y
and
Z
being dominated by
X
become unirational and hence rational, as these are algebraic surfaces defined over
\mdmathbb{C}
due to Castelnuovo’s criterion for rationality. For the same reason, it turns out that the curve
B
is also rational. Since any smooth affine surface properly dominated by
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
is
\mdmathbb{Q}
-factorial as observed before in the proof of Proposition
3.11
,
Z
is
\mdmathbb{Q}
-factorial and hence Lemma
2.12
implies that
B
is a smooth affine curve and
\varphi
has no reducible fibers. Since
\varphi
is an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration, the induced homomorphism
\varphi_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(Z)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(B)
is surjective. Since
\pi_{1}(Z)
is abelian, so is
\pi_{1}(B)
. Therefore
B
is a smooth affine rational curve with
\pi_{1}(B)
abelian, whence
B
is isomorphic to either
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
, or
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
.

Let
\varphi
have multiple fibers. Then these multiple fibers are scheme theoretically thick affine lines, i.e., isomorphic to
m\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
for some integer
m>1
. Then, applying the ramified covering trick, we get a finite étale covering
p:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
such that
Z^{\prime}
is a smooth affine surface admitting an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration, say
\varphi^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow C
onto a smooth affine curve
C
. Also note that
\varphi^{\prime}
has no multiple fibers, but it has reducible fibers which are disjoint unions of affine lines taken with reduced structures.
Since
h_{\ast}:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(X)
is surjective, we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}

such that
X^{\prime}
is an irreducible, smooth affine surface and
q:X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
is a connected covering which is the pull back of the covering
p:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow Z
via
h:X\rightarrow Z
and the induced map, say
h^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
is a finite surjective morphism. Since
X=\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
and
q
is a finite covering,
X^{\prime}\cong\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. So
h^{\prime}
is again a finite surjective morphism similar to
h
. But since
Z^{\prime}
admits an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-fibration
\varphi^{\prime}:Z^{\prime}\rightarrow C
which has reducible fibers, by Lemma
2.12
we conclude that
Z^{\prime}
must not be
\mdmathbb{Q}
-factorial. But this contradicts the fact that
Z^{\prime}
is properly dominated by a factorial surface such as
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. This implies that
\varphi:Z\rightarrow B
has no multiple fiber and all fibers are irreducible. So it turns out that
\varphi:Z\rightarrow B
is an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
B
.

Case 1.

Suppose
B\cong\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
.

It follows that
\varphi:Z\rightarrow B
is an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
in this case and hence
Z\cong\mdmathbb{A}^{2}
. Since
g:Z\rightarrow Y
is a finite étale morphism, it follows that
Y\cong\mdmathbb{A}^{2}
.

Case 2.

Suppose
B\cong\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
.

In this case,
\varphi:Z\rightarrow B
is an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
. Hence
Z\cong\mdmathbb{A}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
following Lemma

. Since
g:Z\rightarrow Y
is a finite étale covering, it follows from Corollary

that

.

This completes the proof.
∎

Now we are all set to prove our main result using all that we proved earlier.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
be a finite surjective morphism of smooth affine surfaces. If
\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
and
X
satisfies the finite homotopy rank-sum property, then so does
Y
.

Proof.

First note that, since
Y
is properly dominated by
X
and
\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
, thus
\bar{\kappa}(Y)
is either
-\infty
or
0
. Since
X
satisfies the finite homotopy rank-sum property and
\bar{\kappa}(X)=0
, it follows from Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.3
that
X
is isomorphic to one of the following surfaces:

(1)

\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
;

(2)

Fujita’s
H[-1,0,-1]
;

(3)

homotopic to
S^{2}
;

(4)

a
\mdmathbb{Q}
-homology plane with fundamental group
\mdmathbb{Z}/2\mdmathbb{Z}
.

Note that
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
is a
2
-fold étale cover of
H[-1,0,-1]
. Also note that the universal cover of a
\mdmathbb{Q}
-homology plane with fundamental group
\mdmathbb{Z}/2\mdmathbb{Z}
is homotopic to
S^{2}
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume that
X
is either
\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
or is homotopic to
S^{2}
.

—

If
X\cong\mdmathbb{C}^{*}\times\mdmathbb{C}^{*}
, then Theorem
3.14
and Theorem
3.16
together imply that
Y
enjoys the finite homotopy rank-sum property.

—

If
X
is homotopic to
S^{2}
, then using Theorem
3.6
, it turns out that
Y
enjoys the finite homotopy rank-sum property.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Examples

We conclude this paper with an example demonstrating that, in general, the Eilenberg–MacLane descent under a finite surjective morphism of smooth affine surfaces does not hold.

Example 4.1
.

Let
Y:=(\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{P}^{1})\setminus\Delta_{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}
, where
\Delta_{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}
denotes the diagonal subvariety. Since
\Delta_{\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{P}^{1}}
is an ample divisor in
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}\times\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
, it turns out that
Y
is a smooth affine surface. Also notice that
Y
admits the structure of an
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
B=\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
.

Now consider a finite ramified morphism
C\rightarrow B
, where
C
is a smooth non-rational curve, and let
X
be the pullback
\mdmathbb{A}^{1}
-bundle over
C
. Then the induced morphism

f\colon X\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow Y

is finite. Since
Y
is affine,
X
is also affine. The surface
X
is Eilenberg–MacLane using Lemma
2.3
(3), whereas
Y
is not, by Lemma
2.3
(4).

It is worth noting, however, that
Y
is homotopy equivalent to
\mdmathbb{P}^{1}
by Lemma
2.10
, and hence homotopy equivalent to
S^{2}
.

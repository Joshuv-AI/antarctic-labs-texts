Integration of Multiframe and Multichannel Residual Attention Models for Mine Water Hazard Video Feature Recognition
Authors: Shuai Zhang, Yuxian Liu, Chengming Wang, Yintao Wei, Huaidong Luo, Huibin Sheng, Yuanze Du, Runsheng Wang, Zhiguo Wu, Jingbing Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
The detection of mine water hazards traditionally relies on manual inspection and sensors, which suffer from temporal delays and fluctuating accuracy. Video detection methods for mine water hazards can address these shortcomings, although the field of image recognition relating to mine water hazards faces challenges regarding the scarcity of samples and complexity of scenes. To tackle these issues, this study introduces a multichannel residual attention model based on Unet, which effectively identifies water inrush areas. The method employs the multichannel averaging of frames for residual preprocessing of video data and extracts features from multiple frames. The residual frames are then integrated into the multiframe features through an attention mechanism. A Unet variant based on the convolutional block attention module is designed, in which the middle frame is used as a label for backpropagation. By leveraging the residuals and multiple attention mechanisms, the model is able to focus on water flow areas, enabling the recognition of mine water hazards against complex and varying backgrounds. Experimental results show that this model outperforms traditional network segmentation models in terms of detecting mine water hazards, and offers significant generalization advantages over conventional digital image-based water flow recognition.

Introduction

Mine water disasters are one of the primary hazards in coal mining, , often resulting in significant numbers of casualties and the loss of property. − To mitigate the impacts of water hazards in mines, it is vital to rapidly identify coal mine water inrush accidents, implement effective preventive strategies, and safely evacuate personnel. Traditional methods of detection primarily rely on monitoring by underground workers and sensors, which have inherent limitations. In the event of an inrush, underground workers may prioritize escape over notifying the control center, leading to delays in response. Sensor-based monitoring, being a contact-based detection method, is prone to damage during incidents, affecting its stability and reliability. The detection of mine water hazards using image recognition technology offers significant improvements over traditional sensor-based methods. Image recognition provides noncontact, real-time monitoring, enhancing both the accuracy and reliability of hazard detection. ,

Current mine image recognition technology focuses on detecting coal rock textures, , sorting coal gangue, , identifying abnormalities in underground operational standards, , and denoising underground images. , Mine water disasters often occur suddenly, with real-time samples being difficult to obtain. This poses a challenge to the use of large-scale deep learning methods for training and recognition. To address this issue, a cross-background water flow feature recognition method is needed. Such a method should be capable of identifying water flow characteristics in underground environments while ignoring the effects of the background. The use of cross-background feature recognition would effectively solve the problem of having few or even no samples of water hazards in real environments.

Water hazard characteristics can be divided into temporal and spatial features. Spatially, the main features are shape and color, whereas the temporal features involve dynamic texture characteristics. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have been used for the automatic extraction of useful features from raw images in various industrial fields. Compared with traditional methods of extracting water flow image features, CNNs can achieve hierarchical feature extraction. In the shallow parts of the network, the convolutional layers typically learn low-level features such as edges, colors, and textures of the water flow; as the network deepens, the intermediate convolutional layers combine these low-level features to monitor more complex water flow image patterns, such as the shape of the water flow or specific components. The deeper convolutional layers recognize advanced features that are more abstract, representing the overall water flow or specific combinations of complex scenes. Moreover, CNNs can perform adaptive learning, making them highly efficient and flexible in handling complex visual tasks.

Image classification and image segmentation are fundamental tasks in vision. The aim of this study is to apply the model to coal mine water hazard detection by extracting water flow features under different backgrounds, even with a limited number of real coal mine water hazard samples. This aims to achieve cross-domain recognition across various backgrounds. Image classification focuses on overall information and outputs labels or categories for the entire image, which cannot extract water flow features in different backgrounds or visualize the distinction between water flow and background. In contrast, image segmentation divides an image into multiple regions or pixel groups, each representing a different category. Image segmentation can identify objects and backgrounds in an image, determining their precise boundaries, positions, and shapes. Image segmentation can identify objects and backgrounds in an image, determining their precise boundaries, positions, and shapes. For this purpose, an image segmentation approach is used to extract the features of water flow. Previous studies have demonstrated the application of image segmentation to water classification. , However, these studies primarily used spectral information and had large target training sets, making them difficult to generalize to water flow recognition across different backgrounds. Meanwhile, these methods mostly rely on single-frame images and spatial information, which may have limitations in handling complex dynamic environments. For instance, single-frame images can only capture information at a specific time point and cannot reflect changes in dynamic features such as water flow. Ignoring temporal characteristics may result in a model’s lack of generalization capability when detecting dynamic water flow. Furthermore, previous research has only focused on single images, often overlooking the temporal characteristics of water flow.

To address the issues described above, we incorporate pixel classification, or image segmentation, into the recognition of water hazard characteristics. First, we compare commonly used segmentation models based on a water hazard data set, and ultimately select Unet as the base model. To extract the temporal features of water flow, we then employ a multiframe fusion method. To eliminate the influence of the background, we develop a new background processing mode and incorporate it into the multiframe vector as part of an attention mechanism. The main contributions of this study are as follows:(1)Multiframe fusion preprocessing model: This study proposes a multiframe fusion preprocessing model that uses the middle frame as a mask label. The residual is incorporated into the multiframe preprocessing as part of an attention mechanism. The CBAM in each U-shaped structure significantly improves the network’s focus on important features while suppressing irrelevant or redundant parts, thereby enhancing the extraction of fine-grained features in segmentation tasks.

Methods

To identify water hazard risks in mines, video data are first subjected to multichannel online causal processing to filter out nonwater elements (e.g., reflections from static rock and coal walls) from the water scenes, and focus on capturing water waves and ripples. After preprocessing, the video data primarily contain dynamic information about the water. To ensure that the model focuses on the dynamic information on water, the processed dynamic water frames are introduced into the feature vector of the fusion frames as part of an attention mechanism. These fusion frames are then concatenated with the attention module and used as the input to Unet, with the label of the middle frame used for backpropagation. The combination of temporal and spatial information results in the effective detection of the presence of water.

Network Structure

The network structure of the proposed multichannel residual attention model based on Unet (MRAU-net) is shown in Figure . MRAU-net integrates three modules into a unified framework that optimizes the image segmentation. The first module is a preprocessing module based on attention mechanisms. The core idea of this module is to capture and fuse the dynamic features between multiple frames with the residual frame features, thereby extracting rich spatiotemporal information. The processed features contain static information on individual frames and encode the dynamic changes between frames. This information serves as the input to the subsequent segmentation model, providing a richly informative starting point for the model.

Overall network structure.

The second module is an improved Unet model that is characterized by the embedding of the CBAM mechanism in each downsampling structure and the skip connection. The introduction of CBAMs significantly enhances the network’s capability to extract features, allowing a more effective focus on important features while suppressing irrelevant or redundant parts. Each layer of the downsampling structure is optimized in this way, ensuring that the feature map retains the most valuable information after multiple downsampling processes. This is crucial for segmentation tasks, especially in scenarios where precise definition of edges and complex textures is required.

The third module is a critical part of network trainingbackpropagation. In this stage, annotated images of the middle frame are used as the true labels, enabling the dynamic nature of water flow to be better captured and described. The network focuses on the fluidity and continuity of water flow, thereby highlighting the water flow features during the learning process. These dynamic characteristics are used as the training objectives, allowing the network to depict the motion of water flow accurately when predicting new frames. This greatly enhances the model’s understanding of dynamic scenes and the segmentation accuracy. The collaborative work of these three modules in the proposed network structure improves the extraction of image features while enhancing the model’s adaptability to dynamic environmental changes. This structure is highly suitable for handling complex dynamic scenes such as mine water hazards.

Multichannel Residual Processing

In the process of detecting water hazard scenes, the variability of backgrounds makes training using a single scene infeasible. It is also difficult to obtain samples from actual water hazard sites. Therefore, an important step in identifying real-world water hazard images is to eliminate the impact of the background. The color, texture, waves, and lighting conditions are the main features that produce dynamic changes in water flow; other static parts such as rock walls and coal walls are interfering factors that negatively affect water flow recognition. The focus of this research is the generation of water flow characteristic frames that only preserve textures and colors, with a multichannel method applied to eliminate static backgrounds. Compared with the single-channel mode proposed by Mettes, the multichannel mode processes residuals across the three RGB channels and retains the color characteristics of the water flow, thereby increasing the threshold for each pixel. This method calculates the average reflection and color of each pixel by computing the average value across each channel in the frame. The basic premise is that the camera is stationary, with a clear distinction between static backgrounds and dynamic objects. To ensure causality and real-time applicability, we replace the original full-interval averaging with a causal sliding window averaging method. For each frame t, the average frame Ft® is calculated using only the current frame and the previous two frames (t – 2, t – 1,t). This avoids the use of any future frames, thus maintaining causality.The causal averaging process for each channel of the video is defined as follows 1 FR®(x,y)=1N∑i=t−N+1tIR(x,y) 2 FG®(x,y)=1N∑i=t−N+1tIG(x,y) 3 FB®(x,y)=1N∑i=t−N+1tIB(x,y) where N = 3 represents the number of frames in the sliding window (current frame + previous two frames), and t denotes the current frame. The residual frame is then obtained by taking the absolute difference between the video frame and the averaged frame. It should be noted that while the original computation of the average frame in the experiments involved the use of the subsequent frames, which conceptually corresponded to offline processing, in the causal version of our method, the average frame is now computed using only the current and previous frames. This ensures that no future frames are used, maintaining causality.

In real-time deployment, our method starts from the current frame and captures one frame every second to form a sequence of three consecutive frames, ensuring the timely extraction of dynamic features of water flow (particularly its fluidity). This design not only maintains causality but also adapts well to practical real-time monitoring requirements. Compared with frame-by-frame real-time detection, our method introduces only about a two-second delay, which is negligible in the context of mine water hazard emergencies and does not compromise the timeliness of detection and warning. ,

The results in Figure demonstrate that, compared with single-channel residual processing, the multichannel method preserves the RGB color range, which increases the pixel threshold. This approach enhances the ability to distinguish dynamic water features from static background elements by amplifying the distinct visual attributes of water, such as color and texture variations.

CBAM Mechanism

The CBAM structure enhances a model’s ability to process image features by finely adjusting the attention distribution over features. This significantly improves the visual performance of deep learning models.

As depicted in Figure , the CBAM relies on spatial attention and channel attention mechanisms. The spatial attention mechanism is responsible for identifying and emphasizing important features in specific spatial locations within the image. The channel attention mechanism evaluates and reinforces the feature channels that are most useful for the final recognition task. The combination of these mechanisms provides the CBAM with a comprehensive understanding of the image content, enabling more accurate feature adjustments. The key techniques for achieving this goal are max pooling and average pooling, which effectively compress the feature maps to reduce redundancy while retaining the most critical features. The pooled feature maps are processed through a multilayer perceptron, which is capable of learning complex nonlinear relationships between different features, further refining and enhancing the useful feature signals.

CBAM structure diagram.

Through the activation of a Sigmoid function, the CBAM determines the final importance weights for each feature. The output of the Sigmoid function (a value between 0 and 1) is directly applied to the original feature map to suppress unnecessary features and highlight important ones. In this way, the CBAM ensures that the network focuses on the most critical information for specific visual tasks. The introduction of the CBAM provides a powerful mechanism for enhancing the ability of image processing models to recognize and parse images through dynamic adjustments in attention allocation. This method improves the performance of the model while enhancing its capability to adapt to different visual tasks.

Unet Network Based on CBAM

In 2015, Ronneberger et al. introduced U-net, which quickly became popular because of its ability to produce precise and comprehensive segmentation results. The U-net architecture is composed of a contracting path (encoder) and an expansive path (decoder). The contracting path captures context and extracts features from the input image, while the expansive path upsamples features to create the final segmentation map. U-net ingeniously integrates both pathways along with skip connections, allowing information to flow across multiple resolutions.

This study enhances the Unet model’s capability in salient target detection by integrating the CBAM mechanism. By incorporating convolutional layers and multilayer perceptrons into the spatial and channel CBAMs, CBAM improves the feature extraction capabilities of the CNN. The attention mechanism is introduced into the skip connections and downsampling process within the Unet network. As shown in Figure , the Unet architecture has been augmented with CBAM. The overall structure of Unet is based on a U-shaped architecture, incorporating multiple skip connections. Each skip connection and downsampling process is integrated with CBAM to enhance the efficiency of the attention mechanism and feature extraction.

Unet structure based on CBAM.

The input data stream consists of preprocessed mine water hazard image features, which are then processed through subsequent network layers. The network includes multiple encoders, each consisting of three convolutional layers and a downsampling layer, with the CBAM structure embedded in each downsampling process. The encoders gradually reduce the spatial dimensions and increase the channel dimensions through convolution and pooling operations, thereby extracting high-level semantic features from the image. The network also includes multiple decoders, which progressively upsample the feature maps from the encoders and merge them with the corresponding encoder’s side outputs through skip connections. The side outputs undergo further convolution and upsampling to generate water inrush prediction maps at different scales. Each scale corresponds to a different image resolution, providing multilevel predictive information. The final water inrush prediction is obtained by integrating the predictions from all scales, which includes upsampling and concatenating the predictions from each side output, and generating a binarized water inrush prediction map through a Sigmoid activation function. This integrated approach allows the network to focus on the most relevant features, enabling accurate predictions of water hazard events.

As shown in Figure , each downsampling is followed by a CBAM module. CBAM recalibrates the output features of the downsampling, highlighting important features and suppressing irrelevant ones, while providing fine-grained attention adjustments. After the initial convolution, the feature map passes through a series of Conv2d + BN + ReLU layers with different downsampling rates; with each layer, the spatial dimensions of the feature map are halved, progressively extracting higher-level features. After each downsampling, a CBAM module is applied. The output of each layer is concatenated with the output of its directly adjacent downsampling layer, helping to retain original information and mitigate the gradient vanishing problem. Referring to Figure , the features from each layer are further extracted by a CBAM module for deeper feature extraction and are returned to the corresponding upsampling layer through skip connections. This design allows the model to effectively capture multiscale information and enhances the network’s ability to learn and integrate important local features through attention mechanisms and skip connections. The overall effect is to improve the expressive power of the feature map.

Multichannel Residual Attention Mechanism

Most attention mechanisms used in deep neural networks are designed to capture long-range dependencies between different features and to model global contextual information. In the context of water hazard recognition across diverse environments, the focus lies in capturing the fluid dynamics of water flow while eliminating complex background interference. When extracting features from water hazard images with varying backgrounds, background features may negatively impact the representation of global information. Therefore, before feeding features into the model, multiple image frames are fused, and the weights derived from a multichannel averaged frame are applied to suppress background content. These weights serve as the attention mechanism in the fused multiframe vector. Higher weights are assigned to the dynamic features of water flow, thereby enhancing the model’s focus on critical regions, while lower weights are allocated to irrelevant background areas. As shown in Figure , three consecutive frames, each captured at one second intervals, undergo feature extraction through a 3 × 3 convolutional layer followed by ReLU and batch normalization (BN) layers. The resulting three feature vectors are then concatenated along the third dimension to form a single composite vector, preserving a balance between low-level features such as edges and textures and high-level features such as object parts and overall structure.

Structure of multichannel residual attention mechanism.

The averaged frame first passes through a Conv2d + BN + ReLU layer to generate a gating weight map, which is then multiplied element-wise with the concatenated multiframe feature vector. This gating operation acts as a residual-weighted attention mechanism for the three consecutive frames, emphasizing dynamic water flow regions while suppressing static backgrounds. Subsequently, residual addition is applied to fuse the gated result with the original multiframe features, producing the final output feature map.

Instead of adopting a traditional self-attention structure, the proposed module uses a residual-weighted gating strategy, where the averaged frame provides a global modulation mask to guide feature emphasis. This design simplifies computation, maintains causal consistency, and effectively enhances the model’s ability to highlight dynamic regions while reducing interference from static backgrounds, as shown in Figure .The process can be expressed as 4 Z=V⊗σ(Conv(Favg))⊕V where V denotes the fused feature map of the three consecutive frames, Favg is the averaged frame, σ represents the sigmoid activation function, and ⊗ and ⊕ indicate element-wise multiplication and addition, respectively.

Evaluation Metrics

The metrics used to evaluate the segmentation performance of MRAU-net are the Dice coefficient (Dice) and the intersection over union (IoU), or Jaccard index. Dice is used to quantify the performance of the segmentation model on a given data set and is more focused on small objects than the Iou, making it more suitable for this task. The formula for calculating Dice is as follows 5 Dice=2×TP(TP+FN)+(TP+FP) where TP denotes true positives, representing correctly detected water flow; FP denotes false positives, indicating nonwater pixels mistakenly detected as water pixels; and FN represents false negatives, where water flow pixels are labeled as nonwater pixels. The Dice coefficient is a set similarity measure that is commonly used to calculate the similarity between two samples. The value of Dice ranges from 0–1.

The IoU, or Jaccard index, is widely used to assess the similarity between two sets, and is commonly employed in image processing and computer vision. The IoU is calculated by comparing the degree of overlap between two masksthe actual mask and the predicted mask. Specifically, the actual mask represents the true position of the object in the image, while the predicted mask is derived from an algorithm or model prediction of the object’s position.

To calculate the IoU, the number of pixels in the overlapping area of the two masks must first be determined; this represents the region covered by both masks. The total number of pixels in both masks, i.e., the union of the pixel counts of each mask, must also be calculated. The IoU is then obtained by dividing the number of pixels in the overlapping area by the total number of pixels, reflecting the extent of coverage between the two masks; see eq .

The value of the IoU ranges from 0–1, where 0 indicates no overlap and 1 indicates complete overlap. In practical applications, a higher IoU value corresponds to good consistency between the predicted and actual masks. Thus, this metric is often used to evaluate the performance of image segmentation techniques. The IoU value allows for a direct understanding of an algorithm’s ability to locate and identify targets in images, facilitating adjustments to the model to enhance its accuracy and efficiency. 6 I(A,B)=A∩BA∪B

Experimental Details

All experiments involving the proposed MRAU-net were conducted on the PyTorch deep learning framework using an NVIDIA 24GB RTX A5000 GPU. The four data sets were mixed for training, and each data set was split into training and test sets at a ratio of 8:2. To avoid frame-level data leakage, the split was performed strictly at the video (scene) level, ensuring that highly correlated frames from the same video were not simultaneously included in both the training and test sets. The frame rate of each video is 30 frames per second, the image resolution is 800 × 600, and the duration of each video is 1 min. During preprocessing, a fixed stride of 30 was applied, meaning that one frame was sampled per second. Thus, a 1 min video yielded 60 frames for training and evaluation. This ensured temporal continuity of water flow features while reducing redundancy. During the training process, the proposed model was optimized using a composite loss function that combines binary cross-entropy with logits (BCE) and a Dice loss. The BCE term ensures pixel-wise classification accuracy, while the Dice term enforces global consistency by encouraging overlap between the predicted masks and the ground truth masks. This composite loss effectively balances local pixel-level precision with global shape consistency, thereby enhancing segmentation performance. Formally, for each pixel i with predicted logit z i and ground truth label y i ∈ {0,1}, the BCEWithLogits loss is defined as 7 LBCE(z,y)=1N∑i=1N[yi·log(1+e−zi)+(1−yi)·log(1+ezi)] where N is the total number of pixels. The Dice coefficient is given by 8 Dice(p,y)=2∑(p·y)+1∑p+∑y+1,⁣p=σ(z) and the corresponding Dice loss is 9 LDice=1−Dice(p,y)

Thus, the final objective function is 10 Ltotal=LBCE+LDice

Training was conducted over approximately 500 epochs, with 9 iterations per epoch, achieving a total of 4500 iterations. The network parameters were optimized using augmented training data. To train the segmentation model, the Dice coefficient was used as the objective function to achieve maximum overlap. The adaptive moment estimation (Adam) optimizer (β1 = 0.9, β2 = 0.999) was employed to minimize the loss, with an initial learning rate of 2 × 10–6. The model was trained for 500 epochs with a batch size of 8, and the learning rate was reduced once at the 20th epoch by multiplying it by 0.1 (γ = 0.1), after which it remained constant for the rest of the training process.

Comparison with Spatiotemporal Descriptors

To verify the generalization performance of the MRAU-net method, we compared it against a water video detection method based on spatiotemporal (ST) descriptors, which is currently the best-performing method for cross-sample water detection. This existing method uses traditional digital image processing techniques, with Fourier transforms and local binary patterns employed to extract temporal and spatial features of water flow videos. These features are then classified using random forests. We conducted tests on the coal and iron mine inrush water data sets. Table shows the Dice and IoU values for both models. It can be seen that the ST model has Dice and IoU values of 78.27 and 75.63%, respectively, which are significantly lower than those of the MRAU-net model. Figure shows the results, illustrating the different preprocessing methods used by the two approaches. The ST method is based on single-channel residual preprocessing, producing a single grayscale image as the residual result. In contrast, the MRAU-net method’s residual result is a multichannel color image, which expands the pixel value range and enhances the model’s generalization capability. The final prediction results show that, while MRAU-net and ST produce similar water flow detection areas, MRAU-net achieves significantly improved detection of the water edges and is more closely aligned with the label images.

Input of Different Frame Counts

The use of multichannel residual frames as dynamic frames for the water flow allows the dynamic features of water flow to be captured. However, the dynamic differences between consecutive water flow frames are not pronounced, and each frame integrates the dynamic features of preceding and following frames. Thus, we use the middle frame as the mask for labels during backpropagation (Figure ). For a continuous input, this approach avoids missing labels and enhances the model’s understanding of dynamic features. Experiments were conducted to test the recognition effects of the model with different numbers of input frames. The results are presented in Table . The optimal recognition effect occurs with three frames as input, achieving Dice and IoU scores of 90.48 and 85.03%. When the number of input frames exceeds three, the recognition rate decreases because the labels of the middle frame do not fully reflect the features of all frames. Moreover, when a single frame is used as input, the Dice and IoU scores are 3.32 percentage points and 5.8 percentage points lower than with three frames, respectively, further demonstrating that multiframe dynamic capture is more effective than using a single frame.

Conclusion

In response to the challenges of mine water hazard image recognition, this paper has described the development of MRAU-net. The proposed MRAU-net uses multichannel residual images as dynamic feature frames and takes multiple frames as input features. Residual images are incorporated as attention features within the multiframe input, and the labels of the middle frames are backpropagated. This approach captures the dynamic features of water flow in videos, enabling cross-scenario water hazard recognition through residual preprocessing. To enhance the extraction of image features and place the focus on relevant areas, the CBAM mechanism was added to each module of the Unet structure, enabling the model to concentrate on features that contribute more significantly to the segmentation task.

Experimental validation has shown that MRAU-net is highly robust to variations in underground scenarios. The model’s Dice and IoU scores reached 90.48% and 85.03%, respectively, outperforming other segmentation models and demonstrating significant improvements in generalization ability over traditional digital image processing. Through experimental research, the multichannel residual attention mechanism was shown to be effective in enhancing the model’ recognition rate. Moreover, the labels of the middle frames were found to effectively capture the dynamic features of water flow. Therefore, this model is suitable for mine water hazard monitoring. Its application across multiple mines will increase the data complexity, thereby improving the model’s generalization ability. The method still has some limitations and challenges in real-world applications. Although we used multiple data sets to validate the method’s effectiveness, these data sets may not cover all the complex situations in actual application scenarios. Therefore, further expansion and diversification of data sets will be a focus of future research.

Synchronization by degenerate noise
Xianming Liu, Xu Sun

Abstract
In this paper, we derive several criteria for (weak) synchronization by noise without the global swift transitivity property. Our sufficient conditions for (weak) synchronization are necessary and can be applied to scenarios involving degenerate or non-Gaussian noise. These results partially answer the open question posed by Flandoli et al. (Probab Theory Relat Fields 168:511-556, 2017). As an application, we prove that the weak attractor for stochastic Lorenz 63 systems driven by degenerate noise consists of a single random point provided the noise intensity is small, and there is no weak synchronization if the noise intensity is large. This indicates that a bifurcation occurs in relation to the intensity of the noise.



Key words
.
Synchronization by noise; Random dynamical system; Random attractor; Stochastic Lorenz 63 system; Lyapunov
exponent.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we introduce sufficient conditions for (weak) synchronization by general white noise, which could be degenerate or non-Gaussian. Here, (weak) synchronization by noise means that there is a random (point) attractor
A
for random dynamical systems (RDS)
\varphi
consisting of a single random point, i.e.,
A(\omega)=a(\omega)
almost surely.

It is worth pointing out that if the state space
E
is local and
\sigma
-compact, our sufficient conditions for synchronization by noise are necessary simultaneously.
On the other hand, it is well known that weak synchronization implies that, for each
x,y\in E
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\quad\text{ in probability}.

(1.1)

Assume that a white noise RDS
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories and is strongly mixing. We obtain that

\text{Weak synchronization}\Leftrightarrow\eqref{eq:1.1}.

Therefore, these theorems can be applied to scenarios involving degenerate noise or non-Gaussian noise.

As an application, we study stochastic Lorenz 63 systems driven by degenerate noise
[
15
]
. The associated RDS synchronizes if the noise intensity is small, and there is no weak synchronization provided the noise intensity is large.

1.1
Literature review

The terminology associated with synchronization by noise is inconsistently utilized in the literature. This has resulted in the categorization of at least three distinct phenomena under this overarching term.

(1)
Stabilization by noise.
In some instances, the effect that deterministic invariant points may become stable due to the inclusion of noise has been referred to as stabilization by noise
[
7
]
. In some examples, the deterministic and stochastic systems share the same deterministic invariant points. The property that every two trajectories of a noisy system
converge to one another, that is, (
1.1
) holds, has been named synchronization by
noise in recent publications (e.g.,
[
16
]
). This property is closely related and a simple consequence of the results for weak synchronization obtained in
[
19
]
and Corollary
2.2
in this work.
“Thus, it would be a good reason to refer to the effects observed here as stabilization by noise”

[
19
]
.

(2)
Coupled synchronization.
The synchronization of coupled dynamical systems is a well-known phenomenon.
It deals with coupled dynamical systems with a common dynamical feature in an asymptotic sense
[
5
,
8
]
. It seems that coupled synchronization predominantly relies on the methods of coupling employed, even in the absence of noise.

(3)
Synchronization by noise.
In some cases, noise destroys unstable deterministic invariant points
[
13
]
. As a result, a deterministic set attractor transforms into a random attractor that consists of a single random point by noise, see
[
26
,
6
,
18
]
. Synchronization by noise for order-preserving RDS has been analyzed
[
19
]
. Synchronization by noise for SPDEs has been investigated
[
20
]
.

In this paper, we mainly study the third case, i.e., synchronization by noise.

1.2
Motivations and comments

The motivation for this work comes from
[
18
]
.
In
[
18
]
, the author showed that locally asymptotically stable, contracting on large sets, and globally swift transitive imply synchronization
[
18
, Theorem 2.14]
. Weak synchronization occurs provided a strongly mixing white noise RDS is weakly asymptotically stable, pointwise strongly swift transitive
[
18
, Theorem 2.23]
.
In the same article, the authors said

“
Numerical evidence suggests that the top Lyapunov exponent for the Lorenz system perturbed by strong noise
is negative and (weak) synchronization occurs. The Lorenz system, however, is not covered by the techniques put forward in Sect. 3. We leave this as an open problem. We prove swift transitivity for a large class of SDE with (non-degenerate) additive noise in Sect. 3. It is left as an open problem to establish swift transitivity in other situations, such as degenerate additive or multiplicative noise.”

Now, the main challenges and contributions are summarized below.

(1) It is well known that weak synchronization implies that, for each
x,y\in E
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0

(1.2)

in probability. So weak asymptotic stability and the pointwise stability condition are also necessary for weak synchronization, while global pointwise strong swift transitivity is not.
Assume that
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories and is strongly mixing. We obtain

\text{Weak synchronization}\Leftrightarrow\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\ \text{ in probability},\forall x,y\in E.

Even local pointwise strong swift transitivity is not needed, see Theorem
2.3
.

(2) Concerning synchronization by noise, we replace contraction on large sets (necessary condition) and global swift transitivity (unnecessary condition) with local strong swift transitivity (necessary condition).
If the state space
E
is Heine-Borel, our sufficient conditions for synchronization by noise, with no need to verify contraction on large sets, are necessary simultaneously, see Theorem
2.1
. As a result, these theorems can be applied to scenarios involving degenerate noise or non-Gaussian noise.

(3) In
[
18
]
, the authors conjecture that the top Lyapunov exponent for the Lorenz system perturbed by strong noise is negative and (weak) synchronization occurs. Also inspired by the work of M. Coti Zelati and M. Hairer
[
15
]
, we study stochastic Lorenz 63 systems driven by degenerate noise. The associated RDS does synchronize if the noise intensity is small, and there is no weak synchronization provided that the noise intensity is large. This means a bifurcation appears with respect to the noise intensity.

2.1
Synchronization

Definition 2.1
.

We say that synchronization occurs for a given RDS
\varphi

A(\omega)
being a singleton, for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega

We first recall some definitions and a sufficient condition for synchronization

18
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

(
[
18
, Definition 2.2]
)
Let
U\subset E
be a non-empty open set. We say
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
U
if there exists a time sequence
t_{n}\uparrow\infty
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\rm{diam}}(\varphi_{t_{n}}(\cdot,U))=0\right)>0,

where
\mathrm{diam}(A):=\sup\limits_{x,y\in A}d(x,y)
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
18
, Definition 2.11]
)
We say
\varphi
is contracting on large sets if for every
R>0
, there is a ball
B(y,R)
a time

such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left({\rm{diam}}\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,B(y,R))\right)\leq\frac{R}{4}\right)>0.

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
18
, Definition 2.7]
)
We say
\varphi
is swift transitive if,
for every
x,y\in E
and
R>0
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(x,R))\subset B(y,2R)\right)>0.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
18
, Theorem 2.14]
)
Let
A
be the unique weak attractor of an RDS
\varphi
.
\varphi
is locally asymptotically stable, contracting on large sets and globally swift transitive. Then synchronization occurs.

Remark 2.1
.

Swift transitivity is an irreducibility condition. Global swift transitivity often fails for SDEs with Lévy noise or degenerate Gaussian noise. In general, Proposition
2.1
could not be applied to scenarios involving degenerate noise or non-Gaussian noise. For specific examples, please refer to Remark
2.4
.

We give some definitions that can be seen as local versions of swift transitivity.

Definition 2.5
.

We say
\varphi
is weakly swift transitive at
z
if,
for every
R>0,x\in E
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(x,R))\subset B(z,2R)\right)>0.

We say
\varphi
is weakly recurrent at
z
if,
for every
R>0
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(z,R))\subset B(z,2R)\right)>0.

We say
\varphi
is strongly swift transitive at
z
if,
for every
R>0,x\in E
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(x,R))\subset B\bigg(z,\frac{R}{2}\bigg)\right)>0.

We say
\varphi
is strongly recurrent at
z
if,
for every
R>0
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(z,R))\subset B\bigg(z,\frac{R}{2}\bigg)\right)>0.

We have the following result, which gives a necessary and sufficient criterion for synchronization.

.

Let

be locally compact and
\sigma
-compact and

be the weak attractor of an RDS

(i)
Synchronization occurs, i.e.

(ii)
There exists a point

\varepsilon>0
such that
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(z,\varepsilon)
and
\varphi
is strongly swift transitive at

.

(iii)
There exists a point
z\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(z,\varepsilon)
and
\varphi
is strongly recurrent at
z
.

(iv)
There exists a point
z\in E
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(z,\varepsilon)
and
{\mathbb{P}}(A\subset B(z,\varepsilon))>0.

Remark 2.2
.

Every metric space
(E,d)
that is locally compact and
\sigma
-compact allows an equivalent metric

such that

is Heine-Borel. As a result, a weak attractor attracts all closed and bounded subsets (

Remark 2.3
.

Assume that

is locally compact,

-compact and synchronization holds, then there exists a point

and

such that

is asymptotically stable on

, contracting on large sets and weakly swift transitive at

Remark 2.4
.

Consider the one-dimensional SDE
[

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=X_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

A(\omega)=\{0\}

Another example from

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=-X_{t}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,\quad{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y_{t}=(Y_{t}-Y_{t}^{3}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

2.2
Weak synchronization

We will assume throughout this subsection that
\varphi
is a white noise RDS and
\rho
is the Markovian invariant measure.

Definition 2.6
.

We say that weak synchronization occurs for a given RDS
\varphi
if there is a minimal weak point attractor
A(\omega)
being a singleton, for
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

It is easy to see that synchronization implies weak synchronization. We recall some definitions and a sufficient condition for weak synchronization
[
18
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

(
[
18
, Definition 2.17]
)
Let
U\subset E
be a (deterministic) non-empty open set. We say that
\varphi
is weakly asymptotically stable on
U
if there exists a (deterministic) sequence
t_{n}\uparrow\infty
, and a set
\mathcal{M}\subset\Omega
of positive
{\mathbb{P}}
-measure, such that for all
x,y\in U
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}1_{\mathcal{M}}d(\varphi_{t_{n}}(\cdot,x),\varphi_{t_{n}}(\cdot,y))=0,\ \text{in probability.}

Definition 2.8
.

(
[
18
, Definition 2.22]
)
We say that
\varphi
is pointwise strongly swift transitive if there is a time
t>0
such that for every
x,x^{\prime}\in E
and every (arrival) point
y
,

P\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,\{x,x^{\prime}\})\subset B(y,2d(x,x^{\prime}))\right)>0.

Proposition 2.2
.

(
[
18
, Theorem 2.23]
)
Assume that
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories, is strongly mixing, weakly asymptotically stable on
U
with
\rho(U)>0
, pointwise strongly swift transitive, and

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\quad\text{a.s.},\quad\forall x,y\in E

(2.1)

Then, there is a minimal weak point attractor
A
consisting of a single random point
a(\omega)
and
A(\omega)=\{a(\omega)\}\quad{\mathbb{P}}\text{-a.s.}
, i.e. weak synchronization holds.

Remark 2.6
.

The pointwise strongly swift transitive property is too strong to verify. For a simple example,

\dot{x}=-x.

The pointwise strongly swift transitive property fails. Proposition
2.2
can not be applied, but (weak) synchronization holds.

As an extension of the globally pointwise strongly swift transitive property,
we introduce the following definition.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that
\varphi
is pointwise strongly swift transitive at
z
if there is a time
t>0
such that for every
x\neq x^{\prime}\in E
,

P\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,\{x,x^{\prime}\})\subset B(z,2d(x,x^{\prime}))\right)>0.

The following result is a slight extension of Proposition
2.2
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Assume that
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories and is strongly mixing. There exist a point
z\in\mathrm{supp}(\rho)
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varphi
is weakly asymptotically stable on
B(z,\varepsilon)
, pointwise strongly swift transitive at
z
, and

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\quad\text{a.s.},\quad\forall x,y\in E

(2.2)

Then, there is a minimal weak point attractor
A
consisting of a single random point
a(\omega)
and
A(\omega)=\{a(\omega)\}~{\mathbb{P}}\text{-a.s.}
, i.e. weak synchronization holds.

Remark 2.7
.

It seems that the locally pointwise strongly swift transitive property is not yet a necessary condition for weak synchronization.

If weak synchronization occurs, then for all
x,y\in E
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\quad\text{ in probability}.

This implies weak asymptotic stability and equation (
2.1
). In contrast to Proposition
2.2
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)
Weak synchronization occurs.

(ii)

\varphi
is strongly mixing, and for all
x,y\in E
,

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\ \text{ in probability}.

(2.3)

Recall that
\varphi
is monotone with respect to initial conditions (see
[
10
]
) if for each
t,\omega
and
x\leq y
,

\varphi(t,\omega,x)\leq\varphi(t,\omega,y).

Corollary 2.2
.

Assume that a monotone RDS
\varphi
has right-continuous trajectories. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is strongly mixing.

(ii)
Weak synchronization occurs.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is strongly mixing, then for any
x\in E
,
\varphi_{t}(\cdot,x)
weakly converges to the invariant measure
\rho
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
By
[
19
, Proposition 2.4]
, we have for all
x,y\in E
,

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\ \text{ in probability}.

Thanks to Theorem
2.3
, weak synchronization holds. ∎

Remark 2.8
.

In
[
19
, Theorem 2.6]
, the authors derive a sufficient condition for weak synchronization when
\varphi
may not be a white noise RDS. Corollary
2.2
can be seen as a weak version of
[
19
, Theorem 2.6]
.

A white noise
\varphi
is strongly mixing, then
\varphi
is completely mixing, i,e.,
for every
x,y\in E
, every bounded Lipschitz function

,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|P_{t}f(x)-P_{t}f(y)|=0.

This, together with the monotonicity, implies

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\ \text{ in probability}.

An interesting question arises regarding the additional conditions, aside from monotonicity, that can facilitate the conversion of weak convergence into strong convergence.

, there are constants
c_{i}=c_{i}(\alpha,d),i=1,2,
such that

\frac{c_{1}t}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha}}\leq p^{(\alpha)}(t,x)\leq\frac{c_{2}t}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha}}.

(3.8)

Moreover, there is a constant
c_{3}=c_{3}(\alpha,d)
such that

|\nabla p^{(\alpha)}(t,x)|\leq\frac{c_{3}t}{(t^{1/\alpha}+|x|)^{d+\alpha+1}}.

(3.9)

Let
u=t^{-1/\alpha}x
. Using (
3.7
), we have

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p^{(\alpha)}(t,x)=-\frac{\partial p^{(\alpha)}(t,x)}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\alpha}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\left[dp^{(\alpha)}(1,u)+u\cdot\nabla p^{(\alpha)}(1,u)\right].

(3.10)

By (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), there is a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,d)\in(0,1\wedge\frac{c_{1}d}{c_{3}})
such that for all
|u|=|t^{-1/\alpha}x|\leq\varepsilon
,

dp^{(\alpha)}(1,u)+u\cdot\nabla p^{(\alpha)}(1,u)\geq\frac{d}{2}p^{(\alpha)}(1,u)\geq\frac{c_{1}d}{2(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}},

and for all
t\geq(|x|/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}
,

(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p^{(\alpha)}(t,x)\geq\frac{d}{2\alpha}t^{-\frac{d}{\alpha}-1}\frac{c_{1}}{(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}}.

(3.11)

Using (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have for all
|u|=|t^{-1/\alpha}x|\leq\frac{c_{1}d}{c_{3}}
,

dp^{(\alpha)}(1,u)+u\cdot\nabla p^{(\alpha)}(1,u)>0.

(3.12)

For every
R>0
, choosing
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=(R/\varepsilon)^{d}p^{(\alpha)}((R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha},x)
and using (

), we have

\displaystyle\rho(B_{R})\leq\frac{2\alpha}{c_{1}d}(R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}\int_{B_{R}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x).

By (
3.12
), we get

\rho(B_{R})\leq\frac{2\alpha}{c_{1}d}(R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}\int_{B_{R_{\varepsilon}}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x),

where
R_{\varepsilon}=\frac{c_{1}d}{c_{3}}\frac{R}{\varepsilon}>R
.
Thank to (

), we get

\displaystyle\rho(B_{R})\leq\frac{2\alpha}{c_{1}d}(R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}\left[|\sigma|^{-\alpha}|b\cdot\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|_{C^{0}}+\left|\int_{B^{c}_{R_{\varepsilon}}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)\right|\right].

Using (
3.10

\frac{2\alpha}{c_{1}d}(R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq c_{4}

for some constant

which is independent of the choice of
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Consequently,

\displaystyle\rho(B_{R})\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{c_{1}d}(R/\varepsilon)^{\alpha}(1+\varepsilon)^{d+\alpha}{|\sigma|^{-\alpha}|b\cdot\nabla f_{\varepsilon}|_{C^{0}}}+c_{4}\rho(B^{c}_{{R_{\varepsilon}}}).

Notice that
R_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.
Letting first
\sigma\rightarrow\infty
and then

,
we obtain that

.
This completes the proof.

In general, estimating the top Lyapunov exponent is an inherently arduous task in the study of chaotic dynamical systems. The following result concerns the sum of Lyapunov exponents.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the SDE in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=b(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}L_{t}

Let
b\in C^{1,\delta}_{\text{loc}}
for some
\delta\in(0,1)

and
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a rotationally invariant
\alpha
-stable Lévy process. There is a corresponding white noise RDS
\varphi
with
\varphi_{t}(\omega,\cdot)\in C_{loc}^{1,\delta}
, and
D\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)
satisfies the equation

\frac{d}{dt}D\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)=Db\left(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)\right)D\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\quad D\varphi_{0}(\omega,x)=\text{Id},

and define a linear cycle
\psi
.
Assume that
\varphi
admits a unique invariant measure
\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x
with strictly positive and smooth density
p
, and the linear cycle
\psi
satisfies the integrability conditions (
3.1
).
Then the term (which is related to the sum of Lyapunov exponents)

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm trace}(Db\left(\varphi_{s}(\omega,\cdot)\right)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s<0.

In particular, in the one-dimensional case, the top Lyapunov exponent is negative.

Proof.

The linear cycle
\psi
satisfies the integrability conditions. According to the multiplicative ergodic theorem, the Lyapunov exponent associated with the invariant measure
\mu=\delta_{\alpha(\omega)}
exists as a limit. Further, it can be computed according to the Birkhoff-Chintchin ergodic theorem. That is

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm trace}(Db\left(\varphi_{s}(\omega,\cdot)\right)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla\cdot b(x)\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla\cdot b(x)p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,

where
p(x)
is the density of the Markov invariant measure. Let
f=\ln p
based on the strictly positive property of
p
.
By Itô’s formula, we get

\displaystyle\ln p(X_{t})-\ln p(X_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{b(X_{s-})\cdot\nabla p(X_{s}-)}{p(X_{s}-)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s-\int_{0}^{t}|\sigma|^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ln p(X_{s}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s+M_{t}.

where
M_{t}
is a martingale.
Let
X_{t}
be the stationary solution, i.e., the solution with initial data distributed according to the invariant measure. Then, taking the expectation on both sides, we obtain that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{b\cdot\nabla p}{p}\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\sigma|^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ln p(x)\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=0,

i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla p{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\sigma|^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ln p(x)~p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=0.

(3.13)

On the other hand, by the stationary Fokker-Planck equation, we can also get

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(bp){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\sigma|^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=0.

That is

-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p\nabla\cdot b{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b\cdot\nabla p{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\sigma|^{\alpha}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=0.

(3.14)

Using (
3.13
) and (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}{\rm trace}(Db\left(\varphi_{s}(\omega,\cdot)\right)){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p~\nabla\cdot b{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle=-|\sigma|^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\ln p\cdot p{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x-|\sigma|^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}p{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle=-|\sigma|^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}\ln p\cdot(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}p{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle<0.

The last step involves noticing the monotonicity of the logarithmic function and the definition of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\alpha}{4}}
.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

Let
b=-\nabla V
with
V\in C^{2,\delta}_{\text{loc}}
and
L
be the Brownian motion. Assume that
e^{-\frac{2V(\cdot)}{\sigma^{2}}}\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, the unique invariant measure is given by

\rho({\mathord{{\rm d}}}x)=p(x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}x=\frac{1}{C_{\sigma}}e^{-\frac{2V(x)}{\sigma^{2}}}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x,

Remark 3.2
.

The integrability condition often fails for SDEs driven by an arbitrary Lévy noise. The multiplicative ergodic theorem is not applicable in this case. In particular, an SDE driven by an arbitrary Lévy process (beyond a stable process) may not admit a transition probability density.
On the other hand, it seems that a negative top Lyapunov exponent is not necessary for synchronization.

The following result demonstrates local asymptotic stability without the presence of a negative top Lyapunov exponent.

Example 3.3
.

Consider an SDE

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=(X_{t}-X_{t}^{3}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}S_{t},\ \sigma\neq 0

where
S_{t}
is a subordinator, i.e. a one-dimensional non-decreasing Lévy process
[
1
]
.
Let
\varphi_{t}(\omega,x):=X_{t}(x)
. Then
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(1,\frac{1}{4})

(B(-1,\frac{1}{4}))
for
\sigma>0

(\sigma<0)
.

Proof.

If
\sigma>0
, we show that
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(1,\varepsilon)
with
0<\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{4}
.
Let
\psi_{t}(x)
be the solution map of
\dot{u}=u-u^{3},u_{0}=x
. We have for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
\psi_{t}(x)\leq\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)
. For all
x,y\in B(1,\varepsilon)
, we observe that
\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y)>0
and

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}

\displaystyle=2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}(1-\varphi_{t}^{2}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)\varphi_{t}(\omega,y)-\varphi_{t}^{2}(\omega,y))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}(1-\psi_{t}^{2}(x)-\psi_{t}(x)\psi_{t}(y)-\psi_{t}^{2}(y)).

Without loss of generality, we assume that
x<y
and get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}(1-3\psi_{t}^{2}(x))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}(1-3\psi_{t}^{2}(1-\varepsilon))

\displaystyle\leq 2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}(1-3(1-\varepsilon)^{2}),

with
1-3(1-\varepsilon)^{2}<0
.
Due to monotonicity, we have

{\rm{diam}}(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,B(1,\varepsilon)))=\varphi_{t}(\omega,1+\varepsilon)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,1-\varepsilon).

Consequently

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rm{diam}}(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,B(1,\varepsilon)))=0\right)={\mathbb{P}}\left(\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|\varphi_{t}(\omega,1+\varepsilon)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,1-\varepsilon)|=0\right)=1>0.

That is
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(1,\varepsilon)
.
If
\sigma<0
, a similar argument shows that
\varphi
is asymptotically stable on
B(-1,\varepsilon)
. ∎

3.2
Global and local swift transitivity

Consider the following SDE

\mathrm{d}X_{t}=b(X_{t}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}L_{t},

(3.15)

where
(L_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a Lévy process on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\sigma\neq 0
. Assume that the drift term
b
is regular enough, and there is a corresponding RDS
\varphi
. Thanks to the smooth effect of white Gaussian noise, the global swift transitivity property holds if the noise is a Brownian motion (see
[
18
, Proposition 3.10]
). As mentioned before, this is not true for SDEs driven by general Lévy noise or degenerate noise. However, for a class of Lévy processes, we derive a result on the global swift transitivity property.

We say that a vector field
b:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies the one-sided Lipschitz condition if

(b(x)-b(y),x-y)\leq\lambda|x-y|^{2}

for all
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and some
\lambda>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
b
be locally Lipschitz and satisfy the one-sided Lipschitz condition.
Assume that for every
T>0
,
L
has full support in
D([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then for every
x,y\in E
and
R>0
, there is a time
t_{0}>0
such that

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(x,R))\subset B(y,2R)\right)>0.

In particular, the global swift transitivity property holds.

Proof.

Set
t_{0}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\frac{3}{2}
. For
t\in[0,t_{0}]
define

\psi(t):=x+\frac{t}{t_{0}}(y-x)

and

\omega^{0}(t):=\frac{1}{\sigma}\left(\psi(t)-x-\int_{0}^{t}b(\psi(s))ds\right).

Then
\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},x)=\psi(t)
for all
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. In particular,
\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},x)=y
. By the one-sided Lipschitz condition of the drift term, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\varphi_{t}(\omega,x^{\prime})-\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2(b(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x^{\prime}))-b(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)),\varphi_{t}(\omega,x^{\prime})-\varphi_{t}(\omega,x))

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda|\varphi_{t}(\omega,x^{\prime})-\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)|^{2}.

For all
x^{\prime}\in B(x,r)
,
\omega\in\Omega
, and
t\geq 0
. By the Gronwall inequality, it follows that

|\varphi_{t}(\omega,x^{\prime})-\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)|\leq|x^{\prime}-x|e^{\lambda t}\leq re^{\lambda t}.

Consequently

\displaystyle|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x^{\prime})-y|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x^{\prime})-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x)|+|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},x)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}r+|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},x)|.

The map
\omega\mapsto\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,x)
is continuous from
D_{U}([0,t_{0}];\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(x,r))\subset B(y,2r))

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,x)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},x)|\leq\frac{r}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}|\omega(s)-\omega^{0}(s)|\leq\delta\right)>0.

Here, in the last inequality, we used the full support property of Lévy processes.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The global strong swift transitivity property does not hold for SDEs only with one-sided Lipschitz conditions. For example
\frac{d}{dt}X=X+\dot{W}.

Following the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we can not achieve the global strong swift transitivity property even with the one-sided Lipschitz condition replaced by eventual strict monotonicity (
3.2
).

As pointed out in Remark
2.4
, the global (strong) swift transitivity property does not hold for SDEs driven by general Lévy noise or degenerate noise. Consequently, we turn to verifying the local strong recurrence property under even weaker drift conditions.

Assume that there exists some
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lambda>0
such that for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(b(x)-b(z),x-z)\leq-\lambda|x-z|^{2}.

(3.16)

Remark 3.4
.

The condition (
3.16
) is essentially weaker than (
3.2
).
The drift term of the Lorentz 63 system (with the Rayleigh number
\rho<1
) satisfies the condition (
3.16
), but not (
3.2
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
b
is locally Lipschitz and satisfies (
3.16
).
For every
T>0
,
L
has full support in
D([0,T],{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
Then
\varphi
is strongly recurrent at
z
.

Proof.

For fixed
z
and any
R>0
, define

\psi(t):=z,\ \omega^{0}(t):=\frac{t}{\sigma}b(z).

Then
\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z)=z
for all
t\geq 0
.
We have

(b(z)-b(y),z-y)\leq-\lambda|z-y|^{2}

for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This inequality and
\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z)=z
imply

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z)-\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=2(b(\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z))-b(\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},y)),\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z)-\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},y))

\displaystyle\leq-2\lambda|\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},z)-\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},y)|^{2}.

Using Gronwall’s inequality, it follows that, for all
y\in B(z,R)
and
t\geq 0
,

|z-\varphi_{t}(\omega^{0},y)|\leq|z-y|e^{-2\lambda t}\leq Re^{-2\lambda t}.

Choose
t_{0}\geq 0
such that
e^{-2t_{0}}<\frac{1}{4}
. Then for all
y\in B(z,R)
and
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle|z-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,y)|

\displaystyle\leq|z-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},y)|+|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},y)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{4}+|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},y)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,y)|.

The map
\omega\mapsto\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous from
D([0,t_{0}];\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
C(B(z,R);\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then there exists some
\delta>0
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{0}}(\cdot,B(z,R))\subset B\left(z,\frac{R}{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{y\in B(x,R)}|\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega^{0},y)-\varphi_{t_{0}}(\omega,y)|\leq\frac{R}{4}\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{s\in[0,t_{0}]}|\omega(s)-\omega^{0}(s)|\leq\delta\right)>0.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Although Lemma
3.2
is established for non-degenerate noise, the proof can be applied to some SDEs with degenerate noise.

4
Concrete examples

In this section, we consider several concrete examples, including an SDE forced by Poisson processes, an SDE with drift given by a multidimensional double-well potential with degenerate additive noise, and stochastic Lorenz systems driven by degenerate noise. The global swift transitive fails for these examples.

4.1
An SDE driven by a Poisson process

Consider an SDE driven by Poisson processes

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X_{t}=(X_{t}-X_{t}^{3}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\sigma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}N_{t},\ \ \sigma>0.

(4.1)

A Poisson process does not have full support in
D([0,T],{\mathbb{R}})
. We can not apply Lemma

to show the local strong recurrence property.

Theorem 4.1
.

Synchronization holds for system (
4.1

Proof.

Note that
\varphi
is not globally swift transitive. We verify that
\varphi
is strongly recurrent at
1

\varphi_{t}(\cdot,B(1,R))=\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,1-R),\ \varphi_{t}(\cdot,1+R)\right)

\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,B(1,R))\subset B\bigg(1,\frac{R}{2}\bigg)\right)=\left(\varphi_{t}(\cdot,1-R)>1-\frac{R}{2},\ \varphi_{t}(\cdot,1+R)<1+\frac{R}{2}\right).

For any
T>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}(N_{s_{1}}(\cdot)=0,N_{s_{2}}(\cdot)-N_{s_{1}}(\cdot)=1,\cdots,N_{s_{n}}(\cdot)-N_{s_{n-1}}(\cdot)=1)>0.

From this and
\frac{d}{dt}(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}\leq 2(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x)-\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))^{2}

{\mathbb{P}}(\varphi_{t_{1}}(\cdot,B(1,R))\subset(1,1+4R)))>0.

Notice

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{1}+t_{2}}(\cdot,B(1,R))\subset B\bigg(1,\frac{R}{2}\bigg)\right)

\displaystyle\geq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\varphi_{t_{1}+t_{2}}(\cdot,B(1,R))\subset B\bigg(1,\frac{R}{2}\bigg),\varphi_{t_{1}}(\cdot,B(1,R))\subset(1,1+4R))\right)

4.3
Stochastic Lorenz 63 systems

We consider the stochastic Lorenz 63 system

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}X

\displaystyle=\sigma(Y-X){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Y

\displaystyle=(\rho X-Y-XZ){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}Z

\displaystyle=(-\beta Z+XY){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t+\gamma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

When the noise is absent, the system reduces to the famous Lorenz 63 system
[
27
]
.
We fix two parameters
\sigma,\beta>0
. Then, the determined system has a global attractor.
For
\rho<1
, the origin is a hyperbolic sink and is the only attractor.
At
\rho=1
, a pitchfork bifurcation occurs.
For
\rho>1
, the origin is a saddle point. Two fixed points appear at
(\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\pm\sqrt{\beta(\rho-1)},\rho-1)
. At
\rho=\rho_{h}=\frac{\sigma(\sigma+\beta+3)}{\sigma-\beta-1}
, a Hopf bifurcation occurs at the nontrivial fixed points.
The nontrivial fixed points are stable for
1<\rho<\rho_{h}
. The nontrivial fixed points are unstable when
\rho>\rho_{h}
.

Recently, M. Coti Zelati and M. Hairer
[
15
]
studied the invariant measure of system (
4.3
). We recall their main result.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
\sigma,\beta>0
and any
\rho<1
, there exist values
0<\gamma_{*}\leq\gamma^{*}<\infty
such that

1.

For
|\gamma|<\gamma_{*}
, (
4.3
) admits
\nu_{1}=\delta_{0}\times\delta_{0}\times N(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\beta})
as its unique invariant measure.

2.

For
|\gamma|>\gamma^{*}
, (
4.3
) admits exactly two ergodic invariant measures:
\nu_{1}
and another measure
\nu_{2}
. Furthermore,
\nu_{2}
has a smooth density with respect to Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\setminus H
. Where
H:=\{(x,y,z):x=y=0\}
.

For
\rho\geq 1
, there exists
\gamma^{*}\geq 0
such that the second statement still holds.

Remark 4.2
.

M. Coti Zelati and M. Hairer detect a noise-induced transition for the invariant measure of the Lorenz system (
4.3
). Although their results do indeed guarantee that, in this case of
\rho<1,|\gamma|<\gamma_{*}
, the system admits a unique invariant measure, they provide little information about the specific value of
\gamma_{*},\gamma^{*}
and
whether
\nu_{1}
is strong mixing. Consequently, they say “One would naturally expect to have
\gamma_{*}=\gamma^{*}
, but we cannot guarantee this at the moment.
\cdots
One would
also expect to have
\gamma^{*}=0
when
\rho>1

\cdots
, we cannot rule out the existence of an intermediate range of values (of
\gamma
) for which
\nu_{1}
would be the unique invariant measure.”

Let
O^{\lambda}_{t}(\omega)=O^{\lambda}(\theta_{t}\omega)
be the stationary solution of the following
Langevin equation

{\mathord{{\rm d}}}O^{\lambda}=-\lambda O^{\lambda}dt+\gamma{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{t}.

Here
\lambda>0
is a parameter to be determined, and

O^{\lambda}(\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{0}\gamma e^{\lambda s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}=-\int_{-\infty}^{0}\gamma\lambda e^{\lambda s}\omega(s){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s,\;\omega\in\Omega.

Define
z_{t}(\omega)=Z_{t}(\omega)-O^{\lambda}_{t}(\omega)
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}x

\displaystyle=\sigma(y-x){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

(4.4)

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}y

\displaystyle=((\rho-O^{\lambda})x-y-xz){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t,

\displaystyle{\mathord{{\rm d}}}z

\displaystyle=(-\beta z+xy-(\beta-\lambda)O^{\lambda}){\mathord{{\rm d}}}t.

The following lemma derives the unique random attractor of (
4.4
). As a result, there is a unique global attractor of (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\gamma,\rho\in{\mathbb{R}},\beta,\sigma>0
, there exists a unique global attractor of (
4.4
).

Proof.

Let
L:=\frac{1}{2}(x^{2}+y^{2}+(z-\rho-\sigma)^{2})
and write
O=O^{\lambda}
for simiplicity. We have that

\frac{d}{dt}L=-\sigma x^{2}-y^{2}-\beta z^{2}-Oxy+\beta(\rho+\sigma)z+(z-\rho-\sigma)(\lambda-\beta)O.

By the Young inequality and
-z(z-\rho-\sigma)=-\frac{1}{2}z^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(z-\rho-\sigma)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\rho+\sigma)^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}L

\displaystyle\leq-\sigma x^{2}-y^{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}x^{2}+\frac{O^{2}}{2\sigma}y^{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}(z-\rho-\sigma)^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}(\rho+\sigma)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\beta}{4}(z-\rho-\sigma)^{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}(\lambda-\beta)^{2}O^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\{-\frac{\sigma}{2},-\frac{\beta}{4},-1+\frac{O^{2}}{2\sigma}\}L+\frac{\beta}{2}(\rho+\sigma)^{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}(\lambda-\beta)^{2}O^{2}.

Thus

\frac{d}{dt}L\leq(-K+\frac{O^{2}}{\sigma})L+M(\theta_{t}\omega),

with
K=\min\{{\sigma},\frac{\beta}{2},2\}
and
M(\theta_{t}\omega)=\frac{\beta}{2}(\rho+\sigma)^{2}+\frac{2}{\beta}(\lambda-\beta)^{2}O^{2}(\theta_{t}\omega)
.
By the Gronwall lemma, we get

L(s,\omega,L_{0})\leq e^{-Ks+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{0}^{s}{O^{2}(\theta_{r}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r}L_{0}+\int_{0}^{s}e^{-K(s-r)+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{r}^{s}{O^{2}(\theta_{\tau}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}\tau}M(\theta_{r}\omega){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

Replacing
\omega
by
\theta_{-t}\omega
and
s
by
t
, from above inequality we get

L(t,\theta_{-t}\omega,L_{0})\leq e^{-Kt+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{-t}^{0}{O^{2}(\theta_{r}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r}L_{0}+\int_{-t}^{0}e^{Kr+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{r}^{0}{O^{2}(\theta_{r}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r}M(\theta_{r}\omega){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r.

(4.5)

By the ergodic theorem, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow\pm\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}O^{2}(\theta_{s}\omega)ds={\mathbb{E}}O^{2}(\omega)={\mathbb{E}}\left(\int_{-\infty}^{0}\gamma e^{\lambda s}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}W_{s}\right)^{2}=\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\lambda}.

(4.6)

Due to
[
2
, Proposition 4.3.3]
, there exists a tempered function
r(\omega)>0
such that

|O(\omega)|^{2}\leq r(\omega),

and for any
\epsilon>0
,

|O(\theta_{t}\omega)|^{2}\leq e^{\epsilon t}r(\omega),

\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
. Thanks to (

),
we choose
\lambda>\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\sigma K}
such that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}[-K+\frac{1}{\sigma t}\int_{-t}^{0}{O^{2}(\theta_{r}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r]<0,

(4.7)

and further

R(\omega):=\int_{-\infty}^{0}e^{Kr+\frac{1}{\sigma}\int_{r}^{0}{O^{2}(\theta_{r}\omega)}{\mathord{{\rm d}}}r}M(\theta_{r}\omega){\mathord{{\rm d}}}r

(4.8)

is well-defined.
Due to (
4.5
),(
4.7
) and (
4.8
), there exists a time

and a positive constant

such that for all

,

(4.9)

By (

), we have proved the existence of a compact absorbing set in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The standard method for random attractors implies that there is a global pullback attractor for (

)
[

23

Lemma 4.3
.

|\gamma|<(1-\rho)\sqrt{\pi\beta}

Proof.

\varphi_{1}(0)

\varphi_{2}(0)=(0,0,0)

Then

Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}[\frac{X_{1}^{2}}{\sigma}+Y_{1}^{2}+(Z_{1}-Z_{2})^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq-2X_{1}^{2}-2Y_{1}^{2}-2\beta(Z_{1}-Z_{2})^{2}+(2+2\rho-2Z_{2})X_{1}Y_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-2X_{1}^{2}-2Y_{1}^{2}-2\beta(Z_{1}-Z_{2})^{2}+|1+\rho-Z_{2}|(X_{1}^{2}+Y_{1}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq(|1+\rho-Z_{2}|-2)(X_{1}^{2}+Y_{1}^{2})-2\beta(Z_{1}-Z_{2})^{2},

where

is the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process.

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(|\rho+1-Z_{2}|-2){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s={\mathbb{E}}|\rho+1-O^{\beta}|-2=\rho-1+\sqrt{\gamma^{2}/\pi\beta}<0.

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}|\varphi_{1}(t)-\varphi_{2}(t)|=0
almost surely, and further implies that
\varphi
is locally asymptotically stable.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

We give another proof by estimating the Lyapunov exponent.
If
|\gamma|<\gamma_{*}
, then (
4.3
) admits a unique invariant measure
\delta_{0}\times\delta_{0}\times N(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2\beta})
, and (
4.4
) with

admits a unique invariant measure
\delta_{0}\times\delta_{0}\times\delta_{0}
.
At
(0,0,0)

\begin{pmatrix}-\sigma&\sigma&0\\
\rho+O^{\beta}&-1&0\\
0&0&-\beta\\
\end{pmatrix}

Define

D_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}-\sigma&\sigma\\
\rho+O_{t}^{\beta}&-1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Let

\frac{d}{dt}(\nabla X,\nabla Y)=D_{t}(\nabla X,\nabla Y).

We have
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln(\frac{\nabla X^{2}}{\sigma}+\nabla Y^{2})\leq\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}[|\rho+1-O^{\beta}_{s}|-2]{\mathord{{\rm d}}}s

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}(|\rho+1-O^{\beta}_{s}|-2){\mathord{{\rm d}}}s={\mathbb{E}}|\rho+1-O^{\beta}|-2=\rho-1+\sqrt{\gamma^{2}/\pi\beta}.

, the locally asymptotically stable property holds.

If

.

If

, using Theorem

and Proposition

∎

is strongly mixing and weakly asymptotically stable, by
[
18
, Proposition 2.20]
, there are
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random variables
a_{i}(\omega)
,
i=1,\ldots,N
such that

A(\omega):=\{a_{i}(\omega):i=1,\ldots,N\}

is a minimal weak point attractor.

Assume
A(\omega)
is not a singleton
{\mathbb{P}}
-a.s. Then

F(\omega):=\min_{i,j=1,\ldots,N,\,i\neq j}d(a_{i}(\omega),a_{j}(\omega))>0,\quad{\mathbb{P}}-\mathrm{a.s.}

Since
{\mathbb{P}}
is invariant under
\theta_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle 0<{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge F(\omega))={\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge F(\theta_{t}\omega))\leq{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{1}(\omega)),\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{2}(\omega))).

(5.4)

Due to the freezing lemma (
[
3
, Lemma 4.1]
) and the fact that
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{0,\infty}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{1}(\omega)),\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{2}(\omega)))

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{1}(\omega)),\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{2}(\omega)))|\mathcal{F}_{0}]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))|_{x=a_{1}(\omega),y=a_{2}(\omega)}].

Noticing

\lim_{t\to\infty}d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,x),\varphi_{t}(\omega,y))=0\quad\text{ in probability},

and using the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}(1\wedge d(\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{1}(\omega)),\varphi_{t}(\omega,a_{2}(\omega)))=0,

which is contradict to (
5.4
). ∎

An invariant measure for an RDS

We say that a Markovian semigroup

Definition A.1

(2)

Every pullback attractor is a weak attractor. Weak attractors are unique, that is, if an RDS has two weak attractors, they agree almost surely.

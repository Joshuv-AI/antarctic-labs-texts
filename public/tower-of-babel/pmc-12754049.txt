Comparable oncological and functional outcomes of robot-assisted radical prostatectomy with conventional anterior approach and Hood technique using DAVINCI SP system
Authors: Po-Hung Lin, Hung-Jen Wang, Hung-Cheng Kan, See-Tong Pang, Kai-Jie Yu, Chun-Te Wu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe DAVINCI SP system is an advanced robotic surgery platform allowing an endoscope and three articulated instruments to enter the body through a single incision while maintaining workspace triangulation. This open-label, single-arm clinical trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT05403190 18/05/2022) evaluates the feasibility of using the DAVINVI SP system for extraperitoneal-approach robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RaRP) in Taiwan. Two surgical techniques, the conventional anterior approach and the Hood technique, were used for RaRP. The primary endpoint was the success rate of RaRP with the DAVINCI SP system without conversion to multiport RaRP or open surgery. Secondary endpoints included perioperative variables, oncological outcomes, and functional outcomes. The study achieved a 100% success rate for the primary endpoint. Patients who underwent RaRP with the DAVINCI SP system had lower postoperative pain scores and shorter hospital stays. Additionally, 93.33% did not require a drain tube post-surgery, 80% achieved undetectable PSA levels at six months, and 96.67% regained continence within three months. The oncologic and continence outcomes are comparable between patients receiving conventional anterior approach or Hood technique. In conclusion, the DAVINCI SP system allows an effective extraperitoneal-approach RaRP, offering reduced pain, shorter hospital stays, and low drain tube placement rates.Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT05403190 18/05/2022).Keywords: DAVINCI SP system, Laparoendoscopic single site surgery, Oncological and functional outcomes, Prostate cancer, Robot-assisted radical prostatectomySubject terms: Cancer, Medical research, Oncology, Urology

Introduction

Prostate cancer stands as the second most common malignancy among men worldwide, and ranks fifth in cancer-related mortality1. In 2020, approximately 1.41 million men were diagnosed with prostate cancer, and 375,000 succumbed to the disease. Developed regions such as North America, Australia, and parts of Europe exhibit particularly high incidence rates. In Taiwan, prostate cancer is the third most frequently diagnosed cancer and the sixth leading cause of cancer death in men2.

The strongest risk factor for prostate cancer is the advancing age, with most cases occurring after age 65. Additional risk factors include family history, genetic mutations, obesity, and dietary influences3. As Westernized eating habits become more prevalent in Taiwan, and as prostate-specific antigen (PSA) testing becomes more widespread, the incidence of prostate cancer has risen significantly over the past three decades4.

Therapeutic decisions for prostate cancer hinge on factors such as disease stage, patient life expectancy, and age. In cases involving localized tumors confined to the prostate or minimal invasive to adjacent tissues, such as tumor stage within T3, radical prostatectomy (RP) may be performed, in which the prostate gland and seminal vesicles are removed. This surgery can be approached through open radical prostatectomy (ORP), laparoscopic radical prostatectomy (LRP), or robot-assisted radical prostatectomy (RaRP) techniques. While surgery aims to remove malignant tissue and achieve durable disease control, it may result in complications such as urinary incontinence and erectile dysfunction5.

The turn of the century witnessed a major breakthrough in minimally invasive surgery with the introduction of robotic-assisted systems. Approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in 2000, the DAVINCI Surgical System enables highly precise surgical maneuvers through a remotely controlled robotic platform, offering superior dexterity, visualization, and instrument stability. The Intuitive DAVINCI SP System, designated as a Class II medical device, received U.S. FDA clearance in 2018 through the 510(k)-notification process. This SP system differs from prior DAVINCI models by operating through a single incision, potentially reducing patient trauma6.

Given that the DAVINCI SP System is relatively new, we planned to conduct a clinical trial in Taiwan focusing on the extraperitoneal radical prostatectomy approach for localized prostate cancer with conventional anterior approach and Hood technique7. Our aim is to establish feasibility data that could support regulatory approval and broader adoption of the SP system within the Taiwanese healthcare setting.

Discussion

Since Dr. Ralph Clayman introduced the first laparoscopic nephrectomy8, minimally invasive surgery has revolutionized urology9. LRP has demonstrated oncological and functional outcomes similar to those of OPR, although challenges remain, particularly during the anastomosis phase following prostate removal10–12. With the first RaRP performed in 2000, robotic surgery has transformed minimally invasive procedures by providing unparalleled precision movement of instruments and improved patient outcomes13,14. RaRP was rapidly adopted and soon outpaced LRP in terms of procedural numbers, thanks largely to the innovative design of its instruments equipped with endo-wrist design15,16. Studies have reported better early continence recovery and preserved potency with RaRP compared to LRP17–20.

As laparoscopic techniques evolved, the demand for reduced patient trauma and enhanced cosmetic outcomes led to the development of approaches that consolidate access points. Lapar0endoscopic single-site surgery (LESS) emerged from these innovations, utilizing flexible, articulating, or curved instruments to perform complex maneuvers through a single incision21–23. However, LESS presents challenges including instrument collision, reduced workspace triangulation, poor ergonomics, and a steep learning curve24–26. A survey by Sorokin et al. revealed that over 50% of urologists performing LESS completed fewer than five procedures in their first year and fewer than 10 cases in their peak year, with more than 60% ceasing to perform LESS in the past year at the survey time27. New robotic platforms and modifications to instruments are expected to overcome these limitations.

The first evaluation of the DAVINCI SP system was reported by Kaouk et al. in 201428. This system enables an articulating endoscope and instruments to enter the patient through a single incision and a specially designed access port, thereby restoring workspace triangulation, a critical component of minimally invasive surgery. Kaouk et al. noted that the DAVINCI SP system is applicable to a wide range of urological procedures29.

Our clinical trial, the first of its kind in Taiwan, evaluated the feasibility of performing extraperitoneal approach radical prostatectomy using the DAVINCI SP system in patients with localized prostate cancer. Two techniques were compared: the conventional anterior approach and the HOOD technique, the latter of which has been associated with better early continence recovery7. Our findings demonstrated comparable oncologic and functional outcomes between both techniques.

One of the most striking benefits observed was that 93.33% of cases did not require postoperative drains, potentially reducing patient discomfort, infection risk, and complexity of postoperative care. Additionally, patients reported lower pain scores within 24 h post-surgery, attributable to the smaller incisions and less invasive nature of the single-port approach. In this trial, the mean VAS pain score at postoperative 24 h in the conventional approach and Hood technique group were 2.65 and 3.4 respectively, which was lower than that of patients who received RaRP with DAVINCI Xi multiport system in our database (postoperative 24 h VAS pain score 4.2, data unpublished). Lower pain score also contributed to a shorter average hospital stay compared to the patients who received multiport RaRP in Taiwan (5.96 days versus 7.8 days for multiport surgeries)30. The post-operation hospital stay was higher than that in western countries. It’s a unique condition in Taiwan, and in some Asian countries as well that Taiwanese patients tend to stay in the hospital until they felt very well and had all drain tubes removed since the admission fee is very low. Cosmetic outcomes were superior, with the single, discreet incision resulting in minimal scarring and a lower risk of complications such as incisional hernias.

Importantly, our data indicates that the benefits of the DAVINCI SP system do not compromise oncologic or functional efficacy. Postoperative assessments revealed that most patients achieved undetectable PSA levels and regained urinary continence within 3 months, confirming effective cancer control and satisfactory functional recovery.

The console time at both sites decreased as the number of procedures increased, indicating that the learning curve can be overcome with experience. Notably, the console time was shorter in the Hood technique group. In this approach to RaRP, the endopelvic fascia remains unopened and the Santorini plexus is not sutured, which potentially reduces the overall surgical time. Additionally, patients treated with the conventional anterior approach experienced a higher rate of pT3 in the final pathology report, suggesting that higher-stage tumors, which are generally more challenging to remove, may require longer operative times. Furthermore, higher rate of patients received conventional RaRP had lymph node dissection, which would increase the operation time31. Our data also revealed a correlation between console time and prostate volume. Indeed, due to the limited extraperitoneal space, a larger prostate gland may compromise the movement of the endoscope and instruments and increase the operative time.

Though a total of 19 SAEs were reported in the whole study population, there were no immediate postoperative complications encountered. Additionally, no blood transfusion was required for all of the candidates. Among the SAEs, 3 were inguinal hernia, of which two were found pre-exist hernia during SP-RaRP. Only one (3.3%) inguinal hernia was considered to be related to SP-RaRP. Inguinal hernia after RaRP is not uncommon. The incidence of hernia after RaRP in Taiwan was reported as 2.1% and 8% at 3 months and one year after RaRP respectively30. Another SAE was directly related to the procedure, as the prevesical lymphocele formation that comprise urination of the patient and required admission and drainage.

There are some limitations to this trial. Fitst, only one procedure was performed, the radical prostatectomy with extraperitoneal approach. However, this procedure includes dissection and resection of the prostate, as well as anastomosis of bladder neck and urethra. It maximally tested most of the instruments including monopolar scissors, bipolar forceps, retraction forceps, Hem-o-lok applier, and needle driver. Changing the camera to upside down is also tested to make sure all the instruments are functional intact. Second, this is a single arm trial without control group. However, we compared the outcomes with patients who received radical prostatectomy with multiport system and revealed that SP system would provide benefits of less post-operative pain and hospital stay. Third, all patients were followed for one year in terms of oncological and functional outcomes in this trial. However, all patients will be kept following after the trial ended at outpatient departments.

In conclusion, our results reveal that the DAVINCI SP system offers benefits including reduced postoperative pain, shorter hospital stays, improved cosmetic results, and a high rate of drain-free procedures. When applied to radical prostatectomy using both conventional and HOOD techniques, these benefits are realized with comparable oncologic or functional outcomes. The success of our clinical trial supports the broader adoption of the DAVINCI SP system for urological surgery and provides real-world experiences for introducing the DAVINCI SP system into Taiwan.

Methods

Ethical considerations

This is a single-arm and open-label trial. The trial was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of CGMH (IRB No. 202101419A0C601). All methods were performed in accordance with the Good Clinical Practice (GCP) and regulations from Taiwan Food and Drug Administration (TFDA). It is registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (Identifier: NCT05403190 18/05/2022 https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05403190?term=NCT05403190&rank=1) as The Performance of da Vinci SP System on Extraperitoneal Approach Radical Prostatectomy (CGMH-SPEAR-P). All participants were well explained about the procedure, follow-up protocol and possible risks and complications and signed the informed consents after seven days of consideration.

Study design and objectives

The primary objective of this trial is to assess whether the DAVINCI SP System can be used to complete an extraperitoneal radical prostatectomy without conversion to open or multiport robotic surgery. The procedure permits the addition of one assistant port if necessary, since the port could serve as the exit of drain tube after the survery. The site of assistant port was at mid clavicle line lateral to the midpoint between umbilicus and pubic bone (Fig. 2). Secondary objectives include evaluating perioperative parameters, functional outcomes, and oncologic efficacy. These endpoints will provide a comprehensive understanding of the system’s performance in a real-world clinical setting and its potential benefits for patients with localized prostate cancer.

Illustration of wound incision and site of assistance port. The incision was made below umbilicus. The assistance port (triangle) was at mid-clavicle line and lateral to the midpoint between umbilicus and pubic bone.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Patients eligible for the study must have biopsy-proven prostate cancer with a clinical stage of cT1–T2N0M0. Additional inclusion criteria include a prostate volume of 70 cm³ or less (as determined by transrectal ultrasound or other imaging modalities), a body mass index (BMI) not exceeding 35 kg/m², and an age between 18 and 78 years. Patients are excluded if they have undergone major abdominal or pelvic surgery that might compromise surgical access, had transurethral resection of the prostate (TURP) within three months of enrollment, received pelvic radiation therapy, or possess severe comorbidities rendering them unfit for minimally invasive surgery.

Endpoints

The primary endpoint is defined as the successful completion of the radical prostatectomy using the DAVINCI SP System without conversion to open or multiport robotic surgery. Secondary endpoints encompass a range of perioperative and postoperative outcomes, including:

Procedure summary

Under general anesthesia, patients were placed in the minimal Trendelenburg position with five degrees head-down position. An incision was made below the umbilicus. The dissection of prevesical space was made with a space creator. Then the SP Access Port was placed for docking. The robotic arm was docked via attaching the Entry Guide fin to the cannula mount. A camera, a monopolar curved scissors, a Maryland bipolar forceps, and a Cadiere forceps were introduced via the SP Short Entry Guide accordingly. For conventional approach, endopelvic fascia was opened and nerve sparing technique was performed if feasible. The Santorini plexus was sutured with 3 –0 V-loc. The bladder neck was dissected and divided from the base of prostate gland. Bilateral vases were identified and transected. Bilateral seminal vesicles (SV) were dissected, followed by the opening of the Denonvillier’s fascia. Dissection was made towards the apex of prostate. Bilateral prostatic pedicles were controlled by a DAVINCI SP medium-large Hem-o-lok applier. Then the urethra and the apex of prostate were divided. The prostate gland was placed in a specimen bag and retrieved from the access port. Posterior repair was performed with a continuous suture. Anastomosis of urethra and bladder neck was performed with continuous suturing with two DAVINCI SP needle drivers. A 16Fr. Foley catheter was placed. Instillation of 200 ml normal saline was done to check if there is leakage from the anastomosis site. Bilateral pelvic lymph node dissection was done if the risk of lymph node involvement was higher than 5% according to the Memorial Sloan-Kettering Cancer Center monogram. A Jackson-Pratt drainage tube was placed if necessary. For Hood technique, endopelvic fascia and Santorini plexus were not touched. Divided between the prostate base and bladder neck was performed as the first step. Bilateral vases and SV were dissected. Denonvillier’s fascia dissection and bilateral prostate pedicle control was performed as similar procedures of conventional approach. Then dissection was made anteriorly around the prostate to divide it from the detrusor apron. Finally, the urethra and prostate apex was divided. Anastomosis of urethra and bladder neck, as well as pelvic lymph dissection was performed in a similar manner.

Statistics

The Chi-square test was performed for categorical variables. Fisher’s exact test was performed if the number of candidates was less than five. An independent t-test was performed for numerical variables. Pearson’s correlation coefficient test was performed for the correlation of prostate volume and console time. A two-sided p-value of less than 0.05 was set as statistically significant. All analysis was performed using MedCalc software version 23.1.3 (MedCalc Software Ltd., Ostend, Belgium).

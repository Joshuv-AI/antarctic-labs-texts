Entire conformal curves are affine or have super-Euclidean energy growth Thanks: The author was supported by the Swiss National Science Foundation grant 212867.
Toni Ikonen

Abstract
. We prove that entire conformal curves ℝ n → ℝ m \mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m} fall into two classes: either the curve is affine or the average energy in a ball is strictly increasing for large radii and diverges to infinity. This rigidity follows from a blow-down argument and the strong interaction of the generalized Cauchy–Riemann equations with calibrated geometries and the sharp isoperimetric inequality for integral currents due to Almgren. As an application, we prove that every entire Lipschitz conformal curve is affine. This can be considered a higher-dimensional analog of the statement that an entire holomorphic function with a bounded complex differential is affine. We also recall that a certain punctured cone over a Legendrian torus provides a submanifold in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , for which an entire conformal curve yields a conformal covering map. As a related result, we prove that if a non-constant entire conformal curve factors through an n n -dimensional submanifold, then the submanifold is calibrated and conformally equivalent to a flat non-compact quotient of ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background on conformal curves

A continuous mapping
f\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
from a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a
conformal mapping
if

(1.1)

f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\|Df\|^{n}=\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\quad\text{everywhere};

here
\|Df\|
is the operator norm of the differential of
f
,
f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
is the pullback of the Riemannian volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}=dx_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dx_{n}
, and
\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
its Hodge star dual. A direct computation shows that
\star f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is the Jacobian determinant of
f
.

Similarly, a continuous mapping
f\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, for a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, is a
holomorphic curve
if

(1.2)

f\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{2n})\quad\text{and}\quad\|Df\|^{2}=\star f^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}\quad\text{everywhere}

where
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dx_{2j-1}\wedge dx_{2j}
is the standard symplectic form. It follows that the right-hand side of (
1.2
) can be expressed as the sum of the Jacobian determinants of minors of the differential of
f
. In fact, if we identify
\mathbb{R}^{2}\simeq\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2n}\simeq\mathbb{C}\times\dots\times\mathbb{C}=\mathbb{C}^{n}
, (
1.2
) holds for
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
if and only if the component functions are holomorphic.

Equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), respectively, are generalizations of Cauchy–Riemann equations for holomorphic mappings in the complex plane, where the volume form
\mathrm{vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and the symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{sym}}
play a special role. In this manuscript, we consider the class of conformal curves which satisfy Cauchy–Riemann equations relative to a constant-coefficient form
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\}
for some
2\leq n\leq m
. To this end, a continuous map
F\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, for an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is a
conformal
\omega
-curve
if

(1.3)

F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\quad\text{and}\quad(\|\omega\|\circ F)\|DF\|^{n}=\star F^{*}\omega\quad\text{everywhere}.

For a smooth form
\omega\in\Omega^{n}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

(1.4)

\|\omega\|(x)=\max\left\{\omega_{x}(v_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge v_{n})\colon(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\text{ orthonormal}\right\}

is the
comass
of
\omega
at the point
x
. Harvey and Lawson
[
HL82
]
coined the term
calibration
to refer to a closed form whose comass is at most one everywhere. A calibration
\omega
determines a distinguished class of minimal surfaces. Indeed, an
n
-dimensional
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-submanifold in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
\omega
-calibrated
if every tangent space of the submanifold has an oriented orthonormal basis realizing the value one when evaluated against the form at the basepoint. A similar calibration condition can be formulated in the context of varifolds and integral currents, leading to
\omega
-calibrated varifolds and integral currents, respectively. One of the seminal contributions of
[
HL82
]
was the discovery of constant coefficient calibrations such as the
Special Lagrangian
,
associative
, and
Cayley
calibrations with rich families of calibrated varifolds and integral currents. The discovery was preceded by the works of Federer
[
Fed69
]
and Berger
[
Ber70
]
in the context of Kähler and quaterniotic geometries, respectively. We refer to the monographs
[
Joy07
,
Mor09
]
for further background. The literature for (pseudo)holomorphic curves is vast, see the monograph
[
MS04
]
for an overview.

When considering the class defined by (
1.3
) for a non-zero constant-coefficient form, we may always normalize the comass (
1.4
) to one. Thus we exclusively consider constant-coefficient calibrations
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
for
2\leq n\leq m
. In this case, (
1.3
) simplifies to the equality

(1.5)

F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m})\quad\text{and}\quad\|DF\|^{n}=\star F^{*}\omega\quad\text{everywhere}.

Equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) provide archetypical examples of (
1.3
) and (
1.5
). Conformal curves have also been studied under the name
Smith maps
by Cheng–Karigiannis–Madnick and Iliashenko–Karigiannis
[
CKM20
,
IK23
]
based on the associative geometry and under the name
calibrated curves
by Heikkilä–Pankka–Prywes in
[
HPP23
]
. The conformal curves form a subclass of the quasiregular curves introduced by Pankka in
[
Pan20
]
where the equality (
1.3
) (resp. (
1.5
)) is relaxed to a two-sided comparability.

1.2.
Energy growth and curves

The author and Pankka recently investigated conformal curves
[
IP24
]
in the context of the Liouville theorem (for conformal mappings). Recall that Liouville’s theorem states that for
3\leq n
, any conformal mapping
f\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
from a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the restriction of a Möbius transformation. Analogously, we proved that for a
G_{\delta}
-dense subset of constant-coefficient calibrations
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
, when
3\leq n\leq m
, every conformal
\omega
-curve
\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
from a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
factors through a Möbius transformation
\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
and an affine map
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
. In particular, if
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such a curve is an affine map. This stemmed from the isoperimetric rigidity of conformal curves based on
\omega
-calibrated integral currents, Almgren’s isoperimetric inequality for integral currents
[
Alm86
]
, and the interaction of the Cauchy–Riemann equation (
1.5
) with pushforward of integral currents (see
[
Fed69
]
). As a particular example of these interactions, for an entire conformal curves
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the average energy

(1.6)

\displaystyle r\mapsto h(r)\coloneqq\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.68748ptB_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{r}(x_{0})}}\|DF\|^{n}dx\text{ is non-decreasing (see \lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{lemm:localrigid} below).}

To deduce (
1.6
), the sharp isoperimetric constant for integral currents is critical.

The author also proved in
[
Iko23
]
(see
[
IP24
, Proposition 7.1]
for the formulation below) a related modulus of continuity estimate

(1.7)

|F(x)-F(y)|\leq C(n)\left(\mathchoice{\mathop{\kern 1.99997pt\vrule width=6.00006pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.49997pt\kern-8.00003pt\intop}\nolimits_{\kern-3.68748ptB_{2r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}}{\mathop{\kern 1.00006pt\vrule width=5.0pt,height=3.0pt,depth=-2.59996pt\kern-6.00006pt\intop}\nolimits_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}}\|DF\|^{n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}|x-y|\quad\text{for $x,y\in B_{r}(x_{0})$.}

We refer to the limiting value
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}h(r)
in (
1.6
) as the
asymptotic growth
of
F
(relative to
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
). We say that the asymptotic growth is
super-Euclidean
if the limit is infinite and
bounded
otherwise. By (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), bounded asymptotic growth is equivalent to a Lipschitz bound for a conformal curve. We also remark that there are conformal
\omega
-curves with infinite asymptotic growth in all dimensions
n\geq 2
. For instance, in
[
IP24
, Proposition 1.6]
, we considered a conformal curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2n}
for which
\|DF\|^{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=e^{nx_{n}}
for
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
, satisfying the Cauchy–Riemann equations relative to a Special Lagrangian calibration. In dimension two, simpler examples can be obtained by considering any holomorphic curve
\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{k}
with at least one non-affine component.

Having bounded asymptotic growth is a rigid property in all dimensions.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every calibration
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
, every conformal
\omega
-curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
with bounded asymptotic growth is an affine linear map.

The main content of
Theorem

1.1
is
n\geq 3
. Indeed, since the complex differential of a holomorphic curve is a holomorphic curve, it easily follows from (
1.6
) and (
1.7
) and the Liouville’s theorem for holomorphic mappings that a holomorphic curve with bounded asymptotic growth is an affine map. The methods in higher dimensions are more elaborate and build upon an analysis of a blow-down of such a curve. Nevertheless, we give a unified treatment of
Theorem

1.1
for all dimensions.

By combining
Theorem

1.1
with the isoperimetric rigidity of conformal curves, non-affine conformal curves have a super-Euclidean growth in the following strong sense.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every calibration
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
and every non-affine conformal
\omega
-curve
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the average energy in (
1.6
) is unbounded and strictly increasing for large radii.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem

1.1
has an interpretation in potential analytic terms when
n\geq 3
. To elaborate on the topic, the author and Pankka
[
IP24
, Section 4.1]
proved that if
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a non-constant conformal
\omega
-curve for a calibration
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
\rho=\|DF\|^{\frac{n-2}{2}}
is subharmonic. By a theorem due to Greene–Wu
[
GW74
]
, it follows that
\rho\not\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in[1,\infty)
. Interpreting
Theorem

1.1
in this context, we conclude that either
F
is affine or
\|DF\|\not\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
p\in[(n-2)/2,\infty]
.

Similar analysis holds when
n=2
but instead
\rho=\log\|DF\|
is subharmonic. In this context,
Theorem

1.1
yields that either
F
is affine or
\rho
is not bounded from above (where we recall that an entire subharmonic function bounded from above in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is constant by the Liouville’s theorem for subharmonic functions).

1.3.
A factorization through a Euclidean quotient

To set the stage for the subsequent theorem, following Gromov
[
Gro07
]
, we say that
N
is
elliptic
if there exists a wrapping map
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow N
. Given a closed and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold
N
, a Lipschitz map
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow N
is a
wrapping map
if

(1.8)

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{r^{n}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}>0

where
f^{*}\mathrm{vol}_{N}
is the pullback of the volume form. We also recall the related notion of
quasiregular ellipticity
coined by Bonk–Heinonen
[
BH01
]
. We say that a closed and oriented Riemannian
n
-manifold is
quasiregularly elliptic
if there exists a non-constant quasiregular map
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow N
. Quasiregular maps are generalizations of conformal mappings between equi-dimensional spaces which naturally occur in complex dynamics, elliptic PDEs, and piecewise linear geometries, for instance. In general such maps are not Lipschitz but have an asymptotic growth bound similar to (
1.8
) for an exponent
c(n)\neq n
when the image is not a homology sphere such as a lens space, see
[
BH01
, Theorem 1.11]
. We refer the reader to the recent survey
[
MP24
]
by Manin and Prywes for further connections between these notions of ellipticity. We simply mention that de Rham cohomology of an elliptic and quasiregularly elliptic manifold admits a graded algebra embedding into the exterior algebra of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
BGM24
,
HP25
]
. See also
[
Hei24
]
for a related generalization for curves.

Based on the growth bound,
Theorem

1.1
, and (
1.7
), non-affine conformal curves
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
are not Lipschitz but do have super-Euclidean energy growth. Thus the energy growth of conformal curves share some features of the degree growth of wrapping and quasiregular maps, respectively. The theorem below concerns the factorization of a conformal curve through a submanifold and the imposed geometric constraints for the submanifold. Not only is the de Rham cohomology restricted as in the case of elliptic and quasiregularly elliptic manifolds but even the geometry of the space itself is highly constrained. More precisely, we have the following.

Theorem 1.4
.

If a non-constant conformal curve
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
factors through a connected smooth
n
-dimensional submanifold, the submanifold is a calibrated submanifold of
\mathbb{R}^{m}
that is conformally equivalent to a non-compact quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. When
n\geq 3
, the curve is a conformal covering map of the submanifold and factors as a composition of a locally isometric covering map
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow N
and a conformal embedding
g\colon N\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
for a flat oriented manifold
N
.

When
n\geq 3
, one of the insights provided by
Theorem

1.4
is that the submanifold is conformally equivalent to an orbit space
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{T}_{n-k}/H
where
H
is a discrete subgroup of orientation-preserving isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{T}_{n-k}
acting freely on
\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{T}_{n-k}
where
\mathbb{T}_{n-k}
is the
(n-k)
-dimensional torus. This is based on the classification of non-compact oriented flat Riemannian spaces, see e.g.
[
Rat19
, Chapter 8]
. We emphasize that the submanifold cannot be conformally equivalent to a quotient of the
n
-dimensional torus or to a quotient of a sphere by the non-compactness. The non-compactness can be deduced in many ways. For example, it follows from the calibration property, the Liouville’s theorem for quasiregular curves
[
Pan20
]
, or from Caccioppoli’s inequality, see
Remark

2.3
below.

The theorem above is sharp in two ways. First, the conformal curve in
[
IP24
, Proposition 1.6]
factors through
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}_{n-1}
for the
(n-1)
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}_{n-1}
, so such a submanifold is not necessarily simply connected. Second, if
n=2
, the complex exponential
z\mapsto e^{z}
factors through the submanifolds
\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
or
\mathbb{C}
, so the covering map conclusion holds only when
n\geq 3
.

Since
Theorem

1.4
is independent of the other parts of the manuscript, we present its proof in
Section

2
. In
Section

3
, we formulate the key growth result needed for
Theorem

1.1
and
Corollary

1.2
while the proofs are in
Section

4
.

2.
Factorization through a submanifold

We first recall two basic facts from the theory of minimal surfaces and conformal geometry, respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
N\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a closed submanifold, then
N
is not a minimal surface.

There are several ways to see
Lemma

2.1
. For instance, when
N
is minimal, the coordinate functions of the inclusion
N\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{R}^{m}
are harmonic, and thus constant by the maximum principle if
N
were closed. See
[
Law80
, Chapter I, Corollary 10]
for an alternate proof.

The second basic fact we need is the following formulation of Picard’s theorem in higher dimensions.

Lemma 2.2
.

When
n\geq 3
and
\widetilde{N}
is a simply connected manifold without boundary, a non-constant conformal map
\widetilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\widetilde{N}
is a conformal diffeomorphism onto
\widetilde{N}
or
\widetilde{N}
is conformally equivalent to the
n
-sphere and the image of
\widetilde{f}
omits a point.

The complex exponential illustrates that the theorem is false when
n=2
.

Proof.

We first recall that conformal homeomorphisms between Riemannian manifolds are conformal diffeomorphisms by Ferrand’s theorem
[
LF76
, Theorem A]
.

The following argument is based on the proof of
[
BH01
, Proposition 1.4]
. We first recall that
\widetilde{f}
is a local homeomorphism due to
[
Ric93
, Theorem II.10.5, p. 232]
. Then, by Zorich’s theorem,
\widetilde{f}
is a homeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
onto its image and the complement of the image in
\widetilde{N}
is zero-dimensional, see
[
Gro07
, p.336]
for this formulation. Since homeomorphisms preserve the number of ends, it follows that the complement of the image is at most a point. If the image omits a point, then
\widetilde{f}
extends to a conformal homeomorphism from
\mathbb{S}^{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
onto
\widetilde{N}
as point singularities have zero
n
-capacity. By Ferrond’s result above, the extension is a conformal diffeomorphism. This implies that
\widetilde{N}
is conformally diffeomorphic to the
n
-sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. In case the image of
\widetilde{f}
does not omit any points, then
\widetilde{f}
is a conformal diffeomorphism onto
\widetilde{N}
. The claim follows.
∎

.

, we obtain a contradiction with

(see also

3.
A growth lemma

We start with the proof of the first half of
Corollary

1.2
as this will be important for the proof of

1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
2\leq n\leq m
and let
F\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a conformal
\omega
-curve for a calibration
\omega\in\Lambda^{n}\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

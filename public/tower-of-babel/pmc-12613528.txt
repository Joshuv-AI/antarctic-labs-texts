Platelet parameters as potential biomarkers for sepsis: a systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Xiang Tong, Sai Yang, Jibo Sun, Qingqing Jia, Hong Fan, Zhenzhen Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSepsis remains a major global health issue, especially in low- and middle-income countries (LMICs) with limited diagnostic resources. This meta-analysis evaluates platelet index ratios (mean platelet volume-to-platelet count, MPV/PLT; platelet distribution width-to-platelet count, PDW/PLT; and platelet distribution width-to-plateletcrit, PDW/PCT) as cost-effective biomarkers for sepsis diagnosis and prognosis in resource-constrained settings.MethodsA systematic search was conducted for studies evaluating platelet parameter ratios in sepsis. Inclusion criteria comprised the following: (1) cohort/case–control designs; (2) non-duplicated datasets; (3) available quantitative data for meta-analysis. Exclusion criteria included reviews, abstracts, letters to the editor, and studies with incomplete data. Synthesis methods employed random-effects models to calculate standardized mean differences (SMD) and diagnostic metrics (sensitivity, specificity, etc.), with heterogeneity assessed via I-squared statistics. Publication bias was evaluated through funnel plots and Egger’s test.ResultsFourteen studies were included. The results revealed that MPV/PLT ratios were significantly higher in neonatal (SMD = 1.48, P = 0.004) and adult sepsis patients (SMD = 0.35, P < 0.001) than in controls. Survivors had significantly lower MPV/PLT ratios than non-survivors in both children/neonate (SMD = 0.67, P < 0.01) and adult group (SMD = 0.41, P < 0.01), with similar trends observed in longitudinal assessments. PDW/PLT was also lower in pediatric survivors (P = 0.009), while no significant differences were found for MPV/PCT in neonates or PDW/PLT in adults. For diagnosing sepsis, MPV/PLT had a sensitivity of 0.55 and specificity of 0.71 in adults, and 0.58 and 0.87 in neonates. For predicting mortality, neonatal sepsis showed higher sensitivity (89%) and specificity (73%) than adult sepsis (63%, 58%). In LMICs, MPV/PLT was elevated at admission in both age groups but was linked to survival only in neonates.ConclusionThe MPV/PLT ratio shows promise as a cost-effective biomarker for sepsis diagnosis and prognosis, particularly in neonatal patients, with important potential applications in LMICs.Systematic review registrationThis study has been registered on the international platform of registered systematic review and meta-analysis protocols (INPLASY) (Registration Number: INPLASY202540096). https://doi.org/10.37766/inplasy2025.4.0096.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13643-025-02979-w.

Introduction

Sepsis represents a life-threatening clinical syndrome stemming from a dysregulated physiological response to infection, characterized by acute multi-organ dysfunction and substantially elevated mortality risk [1]. Of particular concern is pediatric sepsis, which disproportionately affects low- and middle-income countries (LMICs), imposing catastrophic health burdens while generating profound socioeconomic repercussions across affected regions [2, 3]. While blood culture maintains its position as the gold standard for sepsis detection, its clinical application faces inherent limitations including prolonged processing times and non-negligible false-negative rates [4]. Therefore, optimal sepsis biomarkers must enable early detection of systemic inflammatory responses prior to overt clinical manifestations of organ dysfunction, thereby complementing conventional diagnostic approaches through enhanced temporal resolution and improved clinical applicability [5, 6].

Platelets (PLT) are highly specialized, anucleate cell fragments originating from megakaryocytes, playing critical roles in various pathophysiological mechanisms including hemostatic regulation, thrombotic processes, inflammatory responses, and antimicrobial defense [7]. A series of studies have shown that thrombocytopenia frequently occurs in patients with sepsis, with reduced platelet counts showing significant association with poor clinical outcomes [8]. In addition, emerging evidence further suggested that platelet-related parameters may serve as valuable sepsis indicators. Mean platelet volume (MPV) had demonstrated reasonable diagnostic accuracy for sepsis identification, particularly in resource-limited clinical settings [9]. Meliani et al. revealed that platelet distribution width (PDW) exhibits rapid predictive capacity for pediatric sepsis outcomes [10]. However, their clinical utility as standalone parameters were limited by substantial inter-individual variability due to confounding factors. Ratios combining platelet parameters mitigate this heterogeneity by normalizing dynamic fluctuations.

Recently, platelet-derived ratios have shown prognostic potential in sepsis assessment [10–12]. Nevertheless, comprehensive evaluations through systematic review and meta-analysis remain absent for these hematological ratios. The present study specifically explores the clinical utility of three platelet parameters ratios: mean platelet volume-to-platelet count (MPV/PLT), platelet distribution width-to-platelet count (PDW/PLT), and platelet distribution width-to-plateletcrit (PDW/PCT) in sepsis management.

Methods

Protocol and registration

We reported our study according to the PRISMA 2020 (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guideline. This study has been registered on the international platform of registered systematic review and meta-analysis protocols (INPLASY) (Registration Number: INPLASY202540096).

Search strategy

We conducted a systematic literature search across PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Ovid Medline to identify studies investigating platelet parameter ratios in sepsis patients, covering all records from each database’s inception to March 10, 2025. The search strategy utilized the following key terms: (sepsis OR bloodstream infection OR bacteremia) AND (mean platelet volume to platelet ratio OR mean platelet volume/platelet ratio OR MPV/PC OR MPV/PLT OR platelet distribution width to platelet ratio OR PDW/PLT OR PDW/PC OR platelet distribution width to procalcitonin ratio OR PDW/PCT). The search was restricted to English-language publications. A supplementary search was performed using Google Scholar with identical keywords. As this meta-analysis exclusively utilized data from previously published studies, no ethical approval or patient consent was required.

Study selection

The inclusion criteria were defined as follows: (1) a study involving the role of platelet parameters ratios in sepsis designed as a cohort study or case–control study; (2) exclusion of duplicate datasets through retention of the most comprehensive and recent publication; and (3) availability of quantitative data for calculating standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) or determining pooled sensitivity/specificity metrics. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) studies lacking extractable effect size data or containing incomplete essential information; and (2) reviews, letter to the editor, conference abstracts, or duplicate publications.

Risk of bias

The qualities of identified studies involving the role of platelet parameters ratios in sepsis were evaluated using the Newcastle–Ottawa scale (NOS), and the scale to investigate quality was based on three aspects: selection, comparability, and exposure in the primary study [13]. The total score ranged from 0 to 9 (0–3, 4–6, and 7–9 were considered low, moderate, and high quality, respectively). The quality of the studies was assessed in a consensus meeting with all authors.

Data extraction

Two independent authors (X. T. and S. Y.) systematically extracted the demographic and clinical data from each primary study using a predesigned data extraction Excel form. In cases of discrepancies or uncertainties during data collection, contentious entries were referred for arbitration to a third author (JB. S.). The extraction protocol encompassed the following terms: first author, year of publication, ethnicity, age of participant, sample size, quantitative measurements of MPV/PLT, PDW/PLT, and PDW/PCT ratios, along with diagnostic performance metrics (sensitivity/specificity). For studies reporting median values with ranges or interquartile ranges (IQR), we transformed the data to approximate means and standard deviations (SD) following the validated methods proposed by Wan et al. [14].

Statistical methods

All statistical analyses in this study were conducted using STATA 14.0 (StataCorp) and Meta-DiSc version 1.4 (Cochrane Collaboration). After appropriate conversion, we implemented a random-effect model for meta-analytical synthesis due to its methodological advantages in accounting for inter-study variability, providing conservative effect estimates with reduced type I error rates compared to fixed-effect model [15]. SMD with 95% CIs were calculated to compare MPV/PLT, PDW/PLT, and PDW/PCT ratios between cohorts. Diagnostic performance metrics including pooled sensitivity, specificity, positive likelihood ratio (PLR), negative likelihood ratio (NLR), and diagnostic odds ratio (DOR) were computed to evaluate clinical utility. The between-study heterogeneity was investigated by the chi-squared (χ2)-based Q-test and I-squared (I2) statistics test. A P-value of < 0.10 or an I2-value of > 50% suggested a statistically significant heterogeneity.

Sensitivity analyses using the leave-one-out method were conducted to evaluate the influence of individual studies on the overall results, with particular attention to those rated as low quality, or potential outliers. Funnel plots were conducted to assess the publication bias. Furthermore, Egger’s regression test further evaluated funnel plot asymmetry, testing whether smaller studies systematically showed larger effect sizes than larger studies. P < 0.1 was considered to indicate significant publication bias among the enrolled studies.

Results

Study search

A total of 133 articles were identified in the initially searches in PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, Ovid Medline, and Google Scholar. As shown in the flow diagram (Fig. 1), 67 studies were excluded because they were duplicated across the databases. After screening on title and abstract, 27 articles were excluded because they were not relevant to the three parameters (MPV/PLT, PDW/PLT, and PDW/PCT) in relation to sepsis. The remaining 34 articles were further screened by a full-view. Twenty articles were removed because they were reviews, or they do not evaluate the value of the three parameters. Therefore, 14 eligible articles were finally identified in the current meta-analysis [10–12, 16–26].Fig. 1The flow diagram of included and excluded studies

Elevated MPV/PLT ratio in sepsis patients

The seven cohort studies comprised 3197 sepsis patients and 1589 controls, assessing MPV/PLT ratio differences between these groups (Table 4). The meta-analysis demonstrated that the MPV/PLT ratio was significantly elevated in neonatal sepsis patients compared to controls (SMD = 1.48, 95% CI 0.49–2.48, P = 0.004), while adult sepsis patients also exhibited statistically significant differences in the MPV/PLT ratio relative to controls (SMD = 0.35, 95% CI 0.23–0.48, P < 0.001) (Fig. 2). Substantial between-study heterogeneity was observed in both neonatal and adult subgroup analyses (I2 = 95.3% and I2 = 47.6%, respectively). Elevated MPV/PLT ratio in non-survivors across age groups

This meta-analysis incorporated eight studies (n = 1858 survivors vs. 743 non-survivors) comparing baseline MPV/PLT ratio between survival groups at admission. Three pediatric/neonatal studies (n = 309) and four adult studies (n = 2292) were subgrouped (Table 5). The results demonstrated statistically significant differences in the MPV/PLT ratio between survivors and non-survivors in the children and neonate sepsis subgroups (SMD = − 0.67, 95% CI − 1.34 to − 0.01, P = 0.004). Similarly, adult sepsis survivors also showed a significantly lower MPV/PLT ratio compared to non-survivors (SMD = − 0.41, 95% CI − 0.70 to − 0.11, P < 0.010), with substantial heterogeneity observed across studies (I2 = 81.7% and 80.3%, respectively) (Fig. 3). Association of MPV/PLT ratio with survival outcomes in adult and children/neonatal sepsis (SMD, standardized mean differences)

Three studies (355 survivors vs. 132 non-survivors) analyzed longitudinal MPV/PLT ratio trends at 72h post-admission, with subgroup data for adults (n = 283) and children and neonate cohorts (n = 204) (Table 5). The results demonstrated persistently lower ratios in adult survivors (SMD = − 0.36, 95% CI − 0.63 to − 0.08, P = 0.011; I2 = 0%), while the children and neonate cohorts also exhibited significant intergroup differences (SMD = − 0.81, 95% CI − 1.13 to − 0.50, P < 0.001; I2 = 0%).

Diagnosis and prognosis utility of MPV/PLT ratio in sepsis

For sepsis diagnosis, six studies (2 adult cohorts 2479 cases vs. 1085 controls; 4 neonate cohorts 430 cases vs. 367 controls) revealed that adults exhibited a sensitivity of 0.55 (95% CI 0.52–0.58) and specificity of 0.71 (95% CI 0.69–0.73), while neonates showed a sensitivity of 0.58 (95% CI 0.47–0.58) and specificity of 0.87 (95% CI 0.83–0.90). For mortality prognosis, four studies (2 adult cohorts 224 survivors vs. 142 non-survivors; 2 children and neonate cohorts 94 survivors vs. 27 non-survivors) demonstrated that adults had a sensitivity of 0.63 (95% CI 0.54–0.71) and specificity of 0.58 (95% CI 0.51–0.65), whereas children and neonate cohorts achieved significantly higher sensitivity (0.89, 95% CI 0.71–0.98) and specificity (0.73, 95% CI 0.63–0.82). Association of PDW/PLT and MPV/PCT ratios with sepsis survival

Three studies (285 survivors vs. 167 non-survivors) analyzed PDW/PLT ratio (Table 5), demonstrating lower values in children and neonate sepsis survivors versus non-survivors (SMD = − 0.42, 95% CI − 0.74 to − 0.10, P = 0.009), whereas adult cohorts showed no differences (single-study evidence) (SMD = − 0.27, 95% CI − 0.55–0.01, P = 0.061). Moderate heterogeneity was detected (I2 = 16.2% for pediatrics; I2 = 0% for adults). Finally, only two small-scale studies (64 survivors vs. 41 non-survivors) evaluating MPV/PCT ratio in neonatal sepsis found no significant survival-associated differences (SMD = − 0.50, 95% CI − 2.46–1.47, P = 0.619), despite substantial heterogeneity (I2 = 95.3%).

Subgroup analysis in LMICs

We performed a subgroup analysis of LMICs based on the World Bank’s income classification. The pooled results showed that the MPV/PLT ratio remained significantly elevated in sepsis patients in both adult and neonatal subgroups within LMICs. Regarding prognostic evaluation of MPV/PLT ratio at admission, a statistically significant association was observed only in the pediatric/neonatal subgroup, while no significance was detected in adults. At the 72-h timepoint, both pediatric/neonatal and adult subgroups showed statistically significant associations with MPV/PLT ratio in prognostic evaluation, but these findings were derived from only one study included in each subgroup (Supplementary File 1).

Publication bias and sensitivity analysis

Funnel plot analysis for MPV/PLT ratio in sepsis diagnosis (Supplementary Fig. 1) revealed symmetrical distribution in adults, indicating no significant publication bias, whereas neonatal studies demonstrated asymmetry suggesting substantial bias. The Egger’s linear regression test P-value was 0.053, meaning that there is a publication bias. For MPV/PLT ratio and sepsis survival (Supplementary Fig. 2), both adult and pediatric cohorts exhibited funnel plot symmetry. Egger’s regression test confirmed an absence of publication bias (P = 0.869).

For both sepsis diagnosis and survival outcomes, sequential exclusion analyses confirmed the robustness of the pooled results (Supplementary Figs. 3 and 4). Additionally, following the exclusion of low-quality studies, the evidence supporting the association between platelet parameters and sepsis remained unaltered.

Discussion

This meta-analysis highlights the diagnostic and prognostic significance of platelet indices in sepsis across diverse populations. Previous studies have indicated their diagnostic and prognostic value across sepsis and respiratory diseases [27, 28]. Cho et al. demonstrated that MPV/PLT ratio outperformed isolated MPV or PLT in predicting mortality in community-acquired pneumonia [27]. Similarly, Ari et al. identified MPV/PLT ratio as an independent predictor of ICU mortality in acute exacerbations of idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis [28]. Our results demonstrated that the MPV/PLT ratio was significantly elevated in adult and children/neonatal sepsis patients compared to controls, and was higher in non-survivors than survivors both at admission and on days 3–4. While limited-sample synthesis suggested lower PDW/PLT ratio in neonatal survivors, PLT/PCT ratio showed no difference.

LMICs bear the heaviest burden of sepsis, accounting for the highest rates of incidence and mortality worldwide, with neonatal sepsis mortality being particularly devastating in these regions [29]. Although the Sequential Organ Failure Assessment (SOFA) score plays a critical role in evaluating sepsis, its implementation is hindered by both laboratory resource limitations and critical shortages of trained healthcare personnel in LMICs [30]. Additionally, while the Quick-SOFA (qSOFA) score simplifies parameters and may be more feasible for LMICs, studies suggest that it still lacks sensitivity in assessing multi-organ dysfunction, thus limiting its clinical application [31–33].

Our analysis demonstrates the MPV/PLT ratio’s value as a diagnostic and prognostic sepsis biomarker. It exhibits moderate specificity (> 70%) but suboptimal sensitivity (adults, 55%; neonates, 58%) for sepsis identification, supporting its utility as a rule-in tool in resource-limited settings. Prognostically, it shows superior mortality prediction in children/neonates (sensitivity = 89%, specificity = 73%) versus adults (63%, 58%). This positions platelet indices as practical, cost-effective alternatives to blood cultures/procalcitonin in LMICs. Consistent with reported elevated MPV in neonatal sepsis and its association with mortality [34], and higher platelet to lymphocyte ratio in non-survivors of sepsis [35], our results further validate MPV/PLT ratio as a robust biomarker across age groups. Moreover, the prognostic value of platelet parameters holds not only in pure sepsis cohorts but also in COVID-19-related sepsis [36], reinforcing their role in systemic inflammation. The dual diagnostic-prognostic functionality offers significant clinical utility in settings lacking advanced diagnostics.

This meta-analysis is the largest and most comprehensive to date on this topic, enhancing the reliability of its conclusions. Of course, we must acknowledge some limitations in interpreting the results of this meta-analysis. First, although we conducted subgroup and sensitivity analyses that showed the overall stability of the findings, there is still significant heterogeneity in some of the effect estimates, and caution should be taken in their interpretation. Secondly, many of the included studies were retrospective, which may introduce bias that could affect the results, and future large-scale prospective studies are needed for validation. Lastly, we did not search for unpublished studies nor contact the original authors to obtain missing data, which may increase the risk of publication bias.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the MPV/PLT ratio may serve as a valuable, low-cost biomarker with diagnostic and prognostic potential for sepsis, especially in resource-limited settings.

Relations between generalised Gelfand-Tsetlin and Kazhdan-Lusztig bases of S n S_{n}
Ali Haidar, Oded Yacobi

Abstract
. We prove that the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis of Specht modules is upper triangular with respect to all generalized Gelfand-Tsetlin bases constructed from any multiplicity-free tower of standard parabolic subgroups.



1.
Introduction

In their classical paper “Relations between Young’s natural and the Kazhdan-Lusztig representations of
S_{n}
”
[
3
]
, Garsia and MacLarnan show that Young’s natural basis of a Specht module is unitriangular with respect to the Kazhdan-Lusztig (KL) basis. The aim of this note is to prove an analogue of this theorem for all generalised Gelfand-Tsetlin (GT) bases.

To explain more precisely, first note that Young’s natural basis is known to be upper-triangular with respect to either Young’s seminormal or orthogonal bases
[
1
]
. These bases are scalar multiples of each other, and are in fact examples of the standard GT basis.
Recall that the latter is the basis obtained by restricting a Specht module along the tower of groups

(1.1)

\displaystyle 1<S_{2}<S_{3}<\cdots<S_{n}.

It is well-defined up to scalar.
By combining the results of
[
3
]
and
[
1
]
, one can show that the GT basis is upper triangular with respect to the KL basis. Of course, to make this statement precise one has to be careful about the orderings of the bases, but we ignore that for the moment.

Now observe that, on the one hand, the GT basis depends on a choice of tower (
1.1
). Indeed, there are many possible choices, and each one results in a different GT basis which we call “generalised GT bases” (Definition
3.1
). On the other hand, the KL basis is
canonical
and so is independent of any choices. Therefore, it is reasonable to expect that the KL basis is upper triangular with respect to
any
tower. This is exactly what we prove (Theorem
4.2
).

Our proof of Theorem
4.2
is independent of Garsia and MacLarnan’s work. Moreover, the generalised GT bases are not upper triangular with respect to each other (cf. Example
4.6
), so it is hopeless to try to bootstrap our result from the standard case to all generalised GT bases in the obvious manner.

Instead, our argument is based on two important inputs: the classical result about the action of the longest element on the KL basis (Theorem
2.1
), and recent work of the second author with Gossow on the compatibility of the KL basis with restriction (Theorem
2.2
). We also note that in order to formulate our theorem we introduce variations of the evacuation operator and dominance order on standard Young tableaux (Definition
4.1
), which may be interesting in their own right.

.

Let

In recent work of the second author with Gossow, we generalised the above theorem to the class of separable permutations

\lambda\vdash n

.

[

The subspaces

are

is an isomorphism of

Below we use binary sequences to label chains of subgroups. Let

\displaystyle\overline{b}=\overline{b_{1}}\cdots\overline{b_{n}},

\displaystyle b_{\dagger}=b_{2}\cdots b_{n},

3.
Generalised Gelfand-Tsetlin bases

A
multiplicity-free chain
of
S_{n}
is a sequence of groups
(G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{n-1})
such that

\displaystyle S_{n}=G_{1}\supset G_{2}\supset\cdots\supset G_{n-1},

and
G_{m}
is a standard parabolic subgroup of
S_{n}
isomorphic to
S_{n-m+1}
. The
standard chain
is

(S_{n},S_{n-1},\ldots,S_{2}).

Writing
G_{m}=S_{I_{m}}
for
I_{m}
an interval,
I_{m+1}
is obtained from
I_{m}
by either removing the smallest or largest element. We can thus index the multiplicity-free chains by binary sequences
b=b_{1}\cdots b_{n-2}
, where
b_{m+1}=0
if
I_{m+1}
is obtained from
I_{m}
by removing the largest element, and is
1
otherwise. The standard chain correspond to the zero sequence.

For example, if
n=4
we have four multiplicity-free chains, corresponding to the four two-step paths in the directed graph below:

From left to right, these chains are labelled by
00,01,10
and
11
. In general,
S_{n}
has
N:=2^{n-2}
multiplicity-free chains. Note that our multiplicity-free chains stop at rank
S_{2}
since there is no “choice” of
S_{1}
at the next step.

Let
(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n-1})
be a multiplicity-free chain labelled by
b
, and let
V
be an irreducible representation of
S_{n}
.

Definition 3.1
.

A vector
v\in V
is a
b
-Gelfand-Tsetlin (GT) vector
if for
1\leq m<n
, there exist
\mu^{m}\vdash n-m+1
such that

v\in\mathsf{Iso}_{\mu^{m}}\mathsf{Res}^{S_{n}}_{G_{m}}(V).

In this case, we say
v
is of
type
T
, where
T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)
corresponds to the sequence of partitions
(\mu^{1},\mu^{2},\ldots,\mu^{n-1},(1))
. A basis
\{v_{T}^{b}\mid T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)\}
is a
b
-GT basis
if for all
T
,
v_{T}^{b}
is a
b
-GT vector of type
T
. We call these
generalised GT bases
.

The following proposition is an immediate consequence of the classical fact that the restriction of any irreducible representation of
G_{m}
to
G_{m+1}
is multiplicity-free:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
and let
b\in\mathbb{B}_{n-2}
. Then there exists a
b
-GT basis
\{v_{T}^{b}\mid T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)\}
of
V^{\lambda}
which is unique up to scalar.

The
b
-GT basis is related to the standard GT basis by the action of
S_{n}
, as we now explain.
First note that a permutation
w\in S_{n}
acts on a multiplicity-free chain by conjugation:

w\cdot(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n-1})=(wG_{1}w^{-1},\ldots,wG_{n-1}w^{-1}).

This may not be a multiplicity-free chain since the conjugate of a standard parabolic subgroup is not necessarily a standard parabolic. But the multiplicity-free chains are always related in this way. This is best explained by example.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n-1})
be a multiplicity-free chain labelled by
b

Then for every

,
u\cdot v_{T}^{0}=v_{T}^{b}
(up to scalar). Consequently,
w_{0}\cdot v_{T}^{b}=v_{T}^{\overline{b}}

Proof.

Let
(\mu^{1},\ldots,\mu^{n})
be the sequence of partitions corresponding to
T
.
The vector
v_{T}^{0}

v_{T}^{0}\in\mathsf{Iso}_{\mu^{m}}(\mathsf{Res}_{S_{n-m+1}}^{S_{n}}(V^{\lambda}))
. Since

, and so
u\cdot v_{T}^{0}=v_{T}^{b}
(up to scalar).

Now, note that
\overline{b}
labels the multiplicity-free chain
(\overline{G_{1}},\ldots,\overline{G}_{n-1})
, and the corresponding permutation for this chain is
w_{0}u
. Therefore
w_{0}\cdot v_{T}^{b}=w_{0}u\cdot v_{T}^{0}=v_{T}^{\overline{b}}
.
∎

4.
The main theorem

To state our main result we introduce a family of partial orders and bijections on
\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)
labelled by binary sequences.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
and
b\in\mathbb{B}_{n-2}
.

(1)

Define a bijection
\varphi_{b}=\varphi_{\lambda,b}\in\Aut(\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda))
recursively:

\displaystyle\varphi_{\lambda,b}(T)=\begin{cases}\varphi_{\mu,b_{\dagger}}(d(T))^{+}&\text{ if }b_{1}=0,\\
\varphi_{\mu,\overline{b_{\dagger}}}(d(\overline{T}))^{+}&\text{ if }b_{1}=1,\end{cases}

where
\mu=\sh(d(T))
or
\sh(d(\overline{T}))
.

(2)

Define a partial order
\leq_{b}=\leq_{\lambda,b}
recursively:
S\leq_{\lambda,b}T
if and only if:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varepsilon_{n}(S)<\varepsilon_{n}(T)\text{ or }\varepsilon_{n}(S)=\varepsilon_{n}(T)\text{ and }d(S)\leq_{\mu,b_{\dagger}}d(T)\text{ if }b_{1}=0,\\
\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{S})<\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{T})\text{ or }\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{S})=\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{T})\text{ and }d(\overline{S})\leq_{\mu,\overline{b_{\dagger}}}d(\overline{T})\text{ if }b_{1}=1,\end{cases}

where
\mu=\sh(d(T))
or
\sh(d(\overline{T}))
.

In the table below we describe the bijection and partial order in some examples.
The partial order is described by associating a sequence of numbers
\sig_{b}(T)
to a tableau
T
, and ordering the sequences
reverse lexicographically
.

\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}\mathbf{b}&\mathbf{\varphi_{b}(T)}&\mathbf{\sig_{b}(T)}\\
\hline\cr 00\cdots 0&\id&\big(\varepsilon_{1}(T),\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}(T)\big)\\
\hline\cr 11\cdots 1&\overline{T}&\big(\varepsilon_{1}(\overline{T}),\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{T})\big)\\
\hline\cr 01\cdots 1&(\overline{d(T)})^{+}&\Big(\varepsilon_{1}(\overline{T}),\ldots,\varepsilon_{n-1}(\overline{T}),\varepsilon_{n}(T)\Big)\\
\hline\cr 10\cdots 0&(\overline{d(\overline{T})})^{+}&\Big(\varepsilon_{1}\big(\overline{d(\overline{T})}\big),\ldots,\varepsilon_{n-1}\big(\overline{d(\overline{T})}\big),\varepsilon_{n}\big(\overline{T}\big)\Big)\end{array}

We can now state our main result, and make a few remarks immediately following:

Theorem 4.2
.

(1)

Let
\lambda\vdash n
and
b\in\mathbb{B}_{n-2}
. Then for any
T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle c_{T}=a_{T}v_{\varphi_{b}(T)}^{b}+\sum_{S<_{b}T}a_{S}v_{\varphi_{b}(S)}^{b},

where
a_{T}\neq 0
.

(2)

The
b
-GT basis can be normalised so that the coefficients
a_{T},a_{S}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 4.4
.

The coefficients
a_{T},a_{S}
that appear in (
4.3
) depend on
b
. This is perhaps not clear from our notation, which we chose to declutter the formula. Note, the set of
b
-GT bases can be normalised such that the coefficients
a_{T},a_{S}
are minimally integral in the following sense:
\{a_{S}\mid S\leq_{b}T\}
is integral and at least one pair of elements are coprime. In this case, the leading term coefficient
a_{T}
is independent (up to sign) of
b\in\mathbb{B}_{n-2}
. We are not aware of an interesting (geometric) interpretation of these coefficients.

Remark 4.5
.

If
b=0\cdots 0
, i.e. we consider the standard GT basis, then this result can be deduced from
[
3
,
1
]
. Indeed, Garsia and MacLarnan prove that the KL basis is integral and unitriangular with respect to Young’s natural basis. The latter is not the standard GT basis, but Armon and Halverson prove that it is upper triangular with respect to the seminormal basis, which is the standard GT basis. Moreover, the relevant ordering are compatible, from which the desired result follows. From this argument we lose integral unitriangularity, and indeed in general we cannot impose that the leading coefficient
a_{T}=1
and the other coefficients are integral in (
4.3
).

.

as the subspace in

.

5.
Proof of Theorem
4.2

The remainder of the paper is devoted to the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
Let
b=b_{1}\cdots b_{n-2}\in\mathbb{B}_{n-2}
label the multiplicity-free chain
(G_{1},\ldots,G_{n-1})
.
First note that the second part of the theorem is a trivial consequence of the first: if two bases are related by a rational matrix, then one can rationally rescale one of the bases so that the matrix is integral.
We now proceed to prove Theorem
4.2
(1)
by induction on
n\geq 2
.

If
n=2
the result is trivial,
and we normalise the GT basis in this case to equal the KL basis.
Suppose now that
n>2
and the result holds for
S_{n-1}
.
Let
\lambda\vdash n
and suppose
\lambda
has
r
removable boxes, which appear in rows
1\leq a_{1}<a_{2}\cdots<a_{r}
. Let
\mu_{k}\vdash n-1
be the partition obtained by removing the box in row
a_{k}
.
Then, for any
1\leq k\leq r
and
T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\mu_{k})
, by induction we have an equality in
V^{\mu_{k}}
:

(5.1)

\displaystyle c_{d(T)}=a_{d(T)}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{k},b_{\dagger}}(d(T))}^{b_{\dagger}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathrm{SYT}(\mu_{k})\\
S<_{\mu_{k},b_{\dagger}}d(T)\end{subarray}}a_{S}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{k},b_{\dagger}}(S)}^{b_{\dagger}},

with rational coefficients.

Recall from Proposition
2.2
we have a filtration

0\subset V^{\lambda}_{a_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset V^{\lambda}_{a_{r}}=V^{\lambda},

and an isomorphism of
S_{n-1}
-modules
f_{k}:V^{\lambda}_{a_{k}}/V^{\lambda}_{a_{k-1}}\to V^{\mu_{k}}
given by

c_{T}+V^{\lambda}_{a_{k-1}}\longmapsto c_{d(T)}
. Therefore, in the unique decomposition
V^{\lambda}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r}
, where
V_{r}\cong V^{\mu_{k}}
as
S_{n-1}
-modules,
V^{\lambda}_{a_{k}}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}
.
The induction now breaks into two cases depending on the value of
b_{1}
.

5.1.
Case 1

We first consider the case
b_{1}=0
.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)
,
f_{k}(v_{T}^{b}+V^{\lambda}_{a_{k-1}})=v_{d(T)}^{b_{\dagger}}
(up to nonzero scalar).

Proof.

Suppose
T\in\mathrm{SYT}_{a_{k}}(\lambda)
. Then, by definition,

v_{T}^{b}\in V_{k}
, and for
1<m<n-1
there exist
\lambda^{m}\vdash n-m+1
such that

v_{T}^{b}\in\mathsf{Iso}_{\lambda^{m}}\mathsf{Res}^{S_{n-1}}_{G_{m}}(V_{k}).

This shows that
\{v_{T}^{b}\mid T\in\mathrm{SYT}_{a_{k}}(\lambda)\}
is a
b_{\dagger}
-GT basis of
V_{k}
, and moreover
v_{T}^{b}
is of type
d(T)
. Since
f_{k}
is an isomorphism of
S_{n-1}
modules, the result follows.
∎

We normalize the
b
-GT basis of
V^{\lambda}
so that Lemma
5.2
is an equality on the nose.
Now we induct on
1\leq k\leq r
. Let
k=1
.
Applying
f_{1}^{-1}
to (
5.1
) we obtain

c_{T}=a_{d(T)}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{1},b_{\dagger}}(d(T))^{+}}^{b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathrm{SYT}(\mu_{1})\\
S<_{\mu_{1},b_{\dagger}}d(T)\end{subarray}}a_{S}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{1},b_{\dagger}}(S)^{+}}^{b}.

By Definition
4.1
, and setting
a_{P}:=a_{d(P)}
for all

, the right hand side can be rewritten as

a_{T}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(T)}^{b}\,+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathrm{SYT}_{a_{1}}(\lambda)\\
Q<_{\lambda,b}T\end{subarray}}a_{Q}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(Q)}^{b}.

This completes the base case of the induction on
k
.

Now let
k>1
. Applying
f_{k}^{-1}
to (
5.1
), and using Definition
4.1
as in the base case, we obtain a congruence:

c_{T}\;\equiv\;a_{T}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(T)}^{b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathrm{SYT}_{a_{k}}(\lambda)\\
Q<_{\lambda,b}T\end{subarray}}a_{Q}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(Q)}^{b}\;\;\Big(\text{modulo }V_{a_{k-1}}^{\lambda}\Big).

Therefore, there exists some

c_{T}=a_{T}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(T)}^{b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\in\mathrm{SYT}_{a_{k}}(\lambda)\\

Now,

By induction (on

5.2.
Case 2

We now consider the case
b_{1}=1
.
The proof here follows a similar strategy, but requires some subtle changes. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
define,

\displaystyle V_{\overline{k}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\Span\{c_{T}\mid T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda),\;\varepsilon_{n}(\overline{T})\leq k\},

\displaystyle f_{\overline{k}}

\displaystyle:V_{\overline{a}_{k}}^{\lambda}/V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda}\to V^{\mu_{k}},\;c_{T}+V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda}\longmapsto\pm c_{d(\overline{T})}.

where the sign in the definition of
f_{\overline{k}}
agrees with the one appearing in
w_{0}\cdot c_{T}=\pm c_{\overline{T}}
(cf. Theorem
2.1
), and in particular it only depends on
\lambda
.
First note:

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
a
,
w_{0}(V_{a}^{\lambda})=V_{\overline{a}}^{\lambda}
, and hence
V_{\overline{a}}^{\lambda}
is
\overline{S_{n-1}}
-invariant.

Proof.

The first statement is an immediate consequence of that fact that evacuation is an involution and Theorem
2.1
. The second now follows since
V_{a}^{\lambda}
is
S_{n-1}
-invariant.
∎

By this lemma we can regard
V_{\overline{a}}^{\lambda}
as and
S_{n-1}
-module by twisting the action with
w_{0}
, i.e. by transferring the structure via the isomorphism
w_{0}:V_{a}^{\lambda}\to V_{\overline{a}}^{\lambda}

Lemma 5.4
.

The map
f_{\overline{k}}
is an isomorphism of
S_{n-1}
-modules.

Proof.

Consider the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{\overline{a}_{k}}^{\lambda}/V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V_{a_{k}}^{\lambda}/V_{a_{k-1}}^{\lambda}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V^{\mu_{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\overline{k}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{k}}

We claim this commutes. Indeed, following the diagram right and down we get:

\displaystyle c_{T}+V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\mapsto w_{0}(c_{T})+V_{a_{k-1}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\pm c_{\overline{T}}+V_{a_{k-1}}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\mapsto\pm c_{d(\overline{T})},

agreeing with the value of
c_{T}+V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda}
under
f_{\overline{k}}
.
Therefore
f_{\overline{k}}
is a composition of
S_{n-1}
-module isomorphisms.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

For any
T\in\mathrm{SYT}(\lambda)
,
f_{\overline{k}}(v_{T}^{b}+V^{\lambda}_{\overline{a}_{k-1}})=\pm v_{d(T)}^{\overline{b}_{\dagger}}
(up to nonzero scalar).

Proof.

By Lemma
3.3
,
v_{T}^{b}=w_{0}\cdot v_{T}^{\overline{b}}
. Hence

\displaystyle f_{\overline{k}}(v_{T}^{b}+V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=f_{\overline{k}}(w_{0}\cdot v_{T}^{\overline{b}}+V_{\overline{a}_{k-1}}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=f_{k}(v_{T}^{\overline{b}}+V_{a_{k-1}}^{\lambda})

\displaystyle=v_{d(T)}^{\overline{b}_{\dagger}}

Note the second equality uses the commutative diagram in Lemma
5.4
and the third equality uses Lemma
5.2
.
∎

To continue the proof, now we induct on
1\leq k\leq r
in the filtration:

0\subset V^{\lambda}_{\overline{a}_{1}}\subset\cdots\subset V^{\lambda}_{\overline{a}_{r}}=V^{\lambda}.

Let
k=1
.
Consider (
5.1
) with
T
replaced by
\overline{T}
, and

by
\overline{b}

c_{T}=\pm a_{d(T)}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{1},\overline{b}_{\dagger}}(d(\overline{T}))^{+}}^{b}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\in\mathrm{SYT}(\mu_{1})\\
S<_{\mu_{1},\overline{b}_{\dagger}}d(\overline{T})\end{subarray}}a_{S}v_{\varphi_{\mu_{1},\overline{b}_{\dagger}}(S)^{+}}^{b}.

As in the previous case, the right hand side can be rewritten as

Q<_{\lambda,b}T\end{subarray}}a_{Q}v_{\varphi_{\lambda,b}(Q)}^{b},

proving the base case of the induction on
k
. One can similarly complete the inductive step as in the previous case, and thus the proof of part (1) of Theorem
4.2
is done.

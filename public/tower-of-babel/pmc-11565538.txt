First human infection with influenza A(H1N1) variant virus in Vietnam
Authors: Nitin Kumar Bansal, Sanjit Sah, Muhammed Shabil, Amogh Verma, Rodrigue Ndabashinze
Published: 2024

Influenza A viruses present significant public health concerns due to their zoonotic potential [1]. The A(H1N1) variant (v) virus, originating from swine populations, has caused sporadic human infections globally [2]. On 19 August 2024, the Vietnam National Focal Point (NFP) for International Health Regulations (IHR) informed the World Health Organization (WHO) of the first-ever human infection with the A(H1N1)v virus in the northern province of Son La, Vietnam [3]. This case highlights the need for ongoing surveillance of zoonotic influenza viruses.

A 70-year-old woman from Son La province, with underlying medical conditions, developed symptoms such as fever and fatigue one week after returning from a month-long stay in Hung Yen province. On 1 June 2024, she was admitted to a district hospital due to pneumonia and tested positive for influenza A on 5 June. Despite treatment, she passed away on 11 June. Subsequent RT-PCR testing on 18 June confirmed the presence of influenza A, and further genomic sequencing on 1 August identified the virus as A(H1N1)v. This finding was corroborated by the WHO Collaborating Centre (WHO CC) at the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (US CDC) on 5 August. The source of infection remains unknown, as no outbreaks were detected in the patient’s community, and no direct contact with livestock was reported.

Swine-origin influenza viruses sporadically infect humans following exposure to infected pigs, typically resulting in mild symptoms, though severe cases and fatalities have occurred [4]. Since 2010, influenza A viruses, including A(H1N1), have been detected in pigs in Vietnam, but this is the first recorded human infection with the A(H1N1)v virus. Human cases of A(H1N1)v have been reported in several countries, including Spain, Brazil, the Netherlands, and Germany, demonstrating its sporadic transmission across swine-human interfaces.

Vietnamese authorities implemented enhanced surveillance and coordinated efforts between human and animal health sectors following the confirmed case. The Son La Provincial Center for Disease Control (PCDC) conducted contact tracing and epidemiological investigations, but no additional cases or livestock outbreaks were identified. On 11 August, an official report was submitted, outlining case investigations and surveillance recommendations. WHO continues to stress the importance of global surveillance to detect epidemiological and virological changes in zoonotic influenza viruses. Human cases involving novel influenza subtypes must be reported under the IHR (2005), with contact tracing and animal exposure assessments being essential components of outbreak investigations.

While the A(H1N1)v virus is not currently a major public health threat, the potential for future outbreaks remains [5]. WHO does not recommend travel or trade restrictions for Vietnam currently but advises standard preventive measures, including hand hygiene and avoiding contact with sick animals in areas with known influenza activity.

This first reported human case of A(H1N1)v virus in Vietnam underscores the need for robust global surveillance and coordination between animal and human health sectors. While the risk remains low, continued monitoring of the virus is vital to detect any changes that could pose future public health threats.

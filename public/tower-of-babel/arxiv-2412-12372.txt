On Mahler’s conjecture for even s-concave functions in dimensions 1 and 2.
Matthieu Fradelizi, Elie Nakhle

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish different sharp forms of Mahler’s conjecture for s s -concave even functions in dimensions n n , for n = 1 n=1 and 2 2 , for s > − 1 / n s>-1/n , thus generalizing our previous results in [ FN ] on log-concave even functions in dimension 2, which corresponds to the case s = 0 s=0 . The functional volume product of an even s s -concave function g g is ∫ ℝ n g ⁡ ( x ) ​ 𝑑 x ​ ∫ ℝ n ℒ s ​ g ​ ( y ) ​ 𝑑 y , \int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy, where ℒ s ​ g \mathcal{L}_{s}g is the s s -polar function associated to g g . The analogue of Mahler’s conjecture for even s s -concave functions postulates that this quantity is minimized for the indicatrix of a cube for any s > − 1 / n s>-1/n . In dimension n = 1 n=1 , we prove this conjecture for all s ∈ ( − 1 , 0 ) s\in(-1,0) (the case s ≥ 0 s\geq 0 was established by the first author and Mathieu Meyer in [ FM10 , page 17] ). In dimension n = 2 n=2 , we consider the case 1 / s ∈ ℤ 1/s\in{\mathbb{Z}} : for s > 0 s>0 , we establish Mahler’s conjecture for general s s -concave even functions; for s < 0 s<0 , we prove a sharp inequality for s s -concave functions g g such that g s g^{s} admits an asymptote in every direction. Notice that this set of functions is quite natural to consider, when s < 0 s<0 , since it is the largest subset of s s -concave functions stable by s s -duality.

1.
Introduction.

The classical Blaschke-Santaló inequality gives the following sharp relation between the volume of a centrally symmetric convex body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the volume of its polar body
K^{\circ}
:

P(K)=|K||K^{\circ}|\leq P(B_{2}^{n}),

where
B_{2}^{n}
is the Euclidean ball of radius one,
K^{\circ}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1,\forall x\in K\}
is the polar body of
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
|A|
stands for the Lebesgue measure of a Borel subset
A
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

One of the well-known problems in the field of geometry of convex bodies is an inverse form of the Blaschke-Santaló inequality, namely Mahler’s conjecture. This conjecture states that the product of the volume of a centrally symmetric convex body
K
and the volume of its polar body
K^{\circ}
, should be minimized by the cube, that is,

P(K)\geq P([-1,1]^{n})=\frac{4^{n}}{n!}.

This conjecture was stated in 1938 by Mahler
[
Mah2
]
.
The inequality in dimension two was established by Mahler himself
[
Mah1
]
, the
3
-dimensional inequality and the equality case were proved by Iriyeh and Shibata
[
IS
]
, a shorter proof can be found in
[
FHMRZ
]
. The conjecture was also proved for several particular families of convex bodies like unconditional convex bodies by Saint Raymond
[
SR
,
M
]
, zonoids by Reisner
[
Re
,
GMR
]
, hyperplane sections of
B_{p}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\sum|x|_{i}^{p}\leq 1\}
by Karasev
[
K
]
.

Functional forms of Mahler’s conjecture have also been studied, where the centrally symmetric convex body is replaced with an even
s
-concave function and the polar body is replaced with the suitable
s
-polar of this function.
More precisely, let us recall the definition of
s
-concavity.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
. A function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is
s
-concave, if for every
\lambda\in[0,1]
, and every
x
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
g(x)g(y)>0
, one has

g((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq((1-\lambda)g(x)^{s}+\lambda g(y)^{s})^{\frac{1}{s}},\quad\hbox{for}\ s\neq 0,

and
g((1-\lambda)x+\lambda y)\geq g(x)^{1-\lambda}g(y)^{\lambda}
, for
s=0
.

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an
s
-concave function not identically zero. Notice that for
s>0
, it means that
g^{s}
is concave on its support, while for
s<0
, it means that
g^{s}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}
is convex.
For
s
-concave functions, the
s
-duality takes the following form. We define, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y):=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-s\langle x,y\rangle\right)_{+}^{\frac{1}{s}}}{g(x)},\quad\hbox{for}\ s\neq 0,\ \hbox{and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{0}g(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-\langle x,y\rangle}}{g(x)},

where the infimum are considered on the set
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};g(x)>0\}
. Note that
\mathcal{L}_{s}g
is
s
-concave and that
\mathcal{L}_{0}
can also be described using the Legendre transform: for any function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
, one has
\mathcal{L}_{0}(e^{-\varphi})=e^{-\varphi^{*}}
, where
\varphi^{*}(y)=\sup_{x}(\langle x,y\rangle-\varphi(x))
denotes the Legendre transform of
\varphi
.

The following conjecture is the natural analogue of Mahler’s conjecture for even
s
-concave functions.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
s>-\frac{1}{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even
s
-concave function such that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy\geq\frac{4^{n}}{(1+s)\cdots(1+ns)},

with equality for
g=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{n}}
.

The conjecture has been proved for
s=0
and
g
being unconditional in
[
FM08a
,
FM08b
]
. Recall that a function is unconditional if
g(|x_{1}|,...,|x_{n}|)=g(x_{1},...,x_{n})
for every
(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It has also been established for
s>0
and
n=1
in
[
FM10
, page 17]
; for
s>0
and
g
unconditional in
[
BF
,
FGSZ
]
and for
s=0
and
n=2
in
[
FN
]
.

According to the sign of
s
, there are two ways of reformulating Conjecture
1.2
. If
s>0
, then it is equivalent to finding the exact lower bound for the quantity

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{1}f(y))^{m}dy,

where
f
is an even concave function such that
0<\int f^{m}<+\infty
. While for
s<0
, Conjecture
1.2
is equivalent to finding the exact lower bound for

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{f(x)^{m}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\mathcal{M}f(y))^{m}}dy,

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,+\infty]
is even, convex,
f
goes to infinity at infinity and for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

\mathcal{M}f(y)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1+\langle x,y\rangle}{f(x)}.

In our first main result, we prove Conjecture
1.2
in the case where
1/s\in\mathbb{N}
, and in dimension
n=2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
s>0
be such that
1/s\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even
s
-concave function such that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy\geq\frac{16}{(1+s)(1+2s)},

with equality for
g=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{2}}
.

Notice that letting
s\to 0
, we get a new proof of the log-concave case from
[
FN
]
, though without the equality case, see Corollary
3.3
.

For
s<0
, the situation is more involved, because the
s
-dual
\mathcal{L}_{s}g
of an integrable
s
-concave function
g
is not only
s
-concave but, as observed in
[
Ro
,
FGSZ
]
, the function
(\mathcal{L}_{s}g)^{s}
has also asymptotes in every direction. For example, for
g=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{n}}
, one has
(\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y))^{s}=1-s\|y\|_{1}
, where
\|y\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|y_{i}|
, for
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
. More precisely, in general, one has
(\mathcal{L}_{s}g)^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
, where the set
\mathcal{F}
is defined below.

Definition 1.4
.

\mathcal{F}
is the set of convex lower semi-continuous functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,+\infty)
such that, for any
x\neq 0
, one has
\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(tx)=+\infty
, and
t\mapsto\frac{f(tx)}{t}
is non-increasing on
(0,+\infty)
.

Notice that if
g^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
, then it is not difficult to see that
0<\int g<+\infty
, see the inequality (
2.3
) in section 2.
Moreover, it is easy to see that for any
g
such that
g^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
, one has
\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g=g
, from which it follows that for any map
g
, one has
\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g=\mathcal{L}_{s}g
.
As we shall see from the one-dimensional case, it is natural to formulate two more conjectures from which Conjecture
1.2
follows in the case
s<0
. The first one postulates a sharp lower bound on the functional volume product of
s
-concave even functions, among the ones such that
g^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
s\in(-\frac{1}{n},0)
and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be even such that
g^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy\geq\frac{1}{(1+ns)}\times\frac{4^{n}}{(1+s)\cdots(1+ns)},

with equality for
g(x)=(\max(1,\|x\|_{\infty}))^{1/s}
.

Notice that
(\max(1,\|x\|_{\infty}))^{1/s}=\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}(\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{n}})
.
In
[
FGSZ
]
, the above conjecture was established if the function
g
is unconditional. In particular, the conjecture holds for
n=1
. In our second main result, we establish Conjecture
1.5
when
n=2
and
-1/s\in\mathbb{N}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
s\in(-\frac{1}{2},0)
be such that
-1/s\in\mathbb{N}
and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be even such that
g^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy\geq\frac{16}{(1+s)(1+2s)^{2}},

with equality for
g(x)=(\max(1,\|x\|_{\infty}))^{1/s}
.

The second conjecture compares the integral of
g
with the integral of its double
s
-polar.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
s\in(-\frac{1}{n},0)
and let
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be even
s
-concave such that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then,

(1.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\geq(1+ns)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(x)dx,

with equality for
g=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{n}}
.

Notice that, for any function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, one has, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
g(x)\leq\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(x)
thus
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(x)dx
. The conjectured inequality (
1.1
) postulates that one has an opposite bound up to constant for
s
-concave even functions.

In our third main result, we establish Conjecture
1.7
in dimension
n=1
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
s\in(-1,0)
and let
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be even
s
-concave such that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)dx\geq(1+s)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{L}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy,

with equality for
g=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]}
.

Remark 1.9
.

Notice that, if Conjecture
1.7
holds for any
s
-concave even function
g
and Conjecture
1.5
holds for
\mathcal{L}_{s}g
, then multiplying equation (
1.1
) by
\int\mathcal{L}_{s}g
, using that
(\mathcal{L}_{s}g)^{s}\in\mathcal{F}
and applying Conjecture
1.5
to
\mathcal{L}_{s}g
, then we deduce that Conjecture
1.2
holds for
g
.

From the above remark, the results in
[
FGSZ
]
and Theorem
1.8
, one deduces that Conjecture
1.2
holds for
n=1
and
s\in(-1,0)
, which is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
s\in(-1,0)
and
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be even convex such that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy\geq\frac{4}{(1+s)^{2}},

with equality if and only if
g=c\mathds{1}_{[-a,a]}
, for some
c>0
and
a>0
.

The proof’s strategy of our main theorems in dimension
n=2
relies on constructing an associated symmetric convex body in dimension
m+2
, where
m=1/|s|
, to which we apply the following proposition concerning Mahler’s conjecture in higher dimensions proved in
[
FHMRZ
]
.

Proposition 1.11
.

[FHMRZ]

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a centrally symmetric convex body that can be partitioned with hyperplanes
H_{1}
,
H_{2}
,…,
H_{n}
into
2^{n}
pieces of the same volume such that each section
K\cap H_{i}
satisfies Mahler’s conjecture and is partitioned into
2^{n-1}
regions of the same
(n-1)
-dimensional volume by the remaining hyperplanes, then

P(K)\geq\frac{4^{n}}{n!}.

In our proofs, we shall need the following notion of equipartition of functions.

Definition 1.12
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is said to be
m
-equipartitioned if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}}f^{m}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\varepsilon}^{n}}f^{m-1}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{m-1},

where
\forall\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\varepsilon_{i}x_{i}\geq 0,\forall i\in\{1,...,n\}\}
.

This paper is organized in the following way: In section 2, we present some general results and properties of the
s
-dual function
\mathcal{L}_{s}
and we introduce the construction of convex bodies using
s
-concave functions. In section 3, we establish Conjecture
1.2
in dimensions
n=1
and
n=2
for
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathbb{N}
. In section 4, we give a lower bound of the functional volume product applied to even
s
-concave functions in dimensions
n=1
and
n=2
for
-\frac{1}{s}\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.
Construction and properties of associated convex bodies.

Let
s>-\frac{1}{n}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an
s
-concave function. We discuss two cases according to the sign of
s
.

2.1.
Case
s>0
.

Let
m=\frac{1}{s}>0
. Assume that
0<\int g<+\infty
. Then there exists a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
concave on its support such that
g=f^{\frac{1}{s}}=f^{m}
and

\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)=\mathcal{L}_{s}f^{\frac{1}{s}}(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-s\langle x,y\rangle\right)_{+}^{\frac{1}{s}}}{f(x)^{\frac{1}{s}}}=\left(\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-\langle x,sy\rangle\right)_{+}}{f(x)}\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}=\left(\mathcal{L}f(sy)\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}=\left(\mathcal{L}f\left(\frac{y}{m}\right)\right)^{m},

where
\mathcal{L}f(y)=\mathcal{L}_{1}f(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-\langle x,y\rangle\right)_{+}}{f(x)}
.
In addition, by the change of variables
y=mz
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy=m^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(z))^{m}dz.

Introducing the operator
P_{m}
defined as follows

P_{m}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy,

and changing variables, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy=m^{n}P_{m}(f).

Thus, for
s>0
, Conjecture
1.2
can equivalently be formulated in the following form: for any even function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, concave on its support and integrable and for any
m>0
, one should have

(2.1)

P_{m}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy\geq P_{m}(\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{n}})=\frac{4^{n}}{(m+1)\cdots(m+n)}.

The operator
P_{m}
is invariant under linear transformation. Indeed, let
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an invertible linear map. Then, putting
z=Tx
we get,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f\circ T(x))^{m}dx=\frac{1}{|\det T|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(z)^{m}dz.

We also obtain, for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\mathcal{L}(f\circ T)(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-\langle x,y\rangle\right)_{+}}{f(Tx)}=\inf_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-\langle T^{-1}z,y\rangle\right)_{+}}{f(z)}=\mathcal{L}f\left((T^{-1})^{*}y\right).

Therefore,
\mathcal{L}(f\circ T)=(\mathcal{L}f)\circ(T^{-1})^{*}
. Hence, changing variables, we get

\displaystyle P_{m}(f\circ T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(f(Tx))^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f((T^{-1})^{*}y))^{m}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|\det T|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx\times|\det T^{*}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy=P_{m}(f).

Let us introduce some tools that will help us in the proof of our main theorem in section 3.

Let
L
be a symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Define the set

K_{L,m}(f)=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\|t\|_{L}\leq f(x)\},

where
\|.\|_{L}
is defined for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
by
\|y\|_{L}=\inf\{\lambda>0;y\in\lambda L\}
. Variants of these sets and their volume products were considered in
[
AKM
,
BF
]
but we establish their properties below for completeness.
Since
f
is even and concave, then
K_{L,m}(f)
is a symmetric convex body in
\mathbb{R}^{m+n}
and for every
m>0
one has

|K_{L,m}(f)|=|\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\|t\|_{L}\leq f(x)\}|=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)L|_{m}dx=|L|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle(K_{L,m}(f))^{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\langle x,y\rangle+\langle s,t\rangle\leq 1,\quad\forall(x,t)\in K_{L,m}(f)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\sup_{\{t\in\mathbb{R}^{m};\|t\|_{L}\leq f(x)\}}\langle s,t\rangle\leq 1-\langle x,y\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};f(x)\|s\|_{L^{\circ}}\leq(1-\langle x,y\rangle)_{+},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\|s\|_{L^{\circ}}\leq\mathcal{L}f(y)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{L^{\circ},m}(\mathcal{L}f).

Hence

|K_{L,m}(f)^{\circ}|=|K_{L^{\circ},m}(\mathcal{L}f)|=|L^{\circ}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy.

Thus, one has

\displaystyle P(K_{L,m}(f))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{L,m}(f)||K_{L^{\circ},m}(\mathcal{L}f)|=|L||L^{\circ}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy=P(L)P_{m}(f).

We need the following lemma describing the relation between the concave function
f
and its dual.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a concave function on its support. Then,

\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{L}f)=(\mathrm{supp}f)^{\circ}.

Proof.

Let
C=\mathrm{supp}(f)
and
K(f)=\{(x,t)\in C\times\mathbb{R};|t|\leq f(x)\}
. We know that
K(f)
is convex in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and
K(f)^{\circ}=K(\mathcal{L}f)
.
Since the operation of duality transforms sections of a given convex body into projections of its polar and

K(f)\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}=C=P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(K(f)),

we have

K(\mathcal{L}f)\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}=K(f)^{\circ}\cap e_{n+1}^{\perp}=\left(P_{e_{n+1}^{\perp}}(K(f))\right)^{\circ}.

Thus,

\mathrm{supp}\mathcal{L}f=C^{\circ}=(\mathrm{supp}f)^{\circ}.

∎

2.2.
Case
-\frac{1}{n}<s<0
.

Let
m=-\frac{1}{s}>n
. Then there exists a convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(0,+\infty]
such that
f=g^{s}
. Assume that
0<\int g<+\infty
, one has
\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(tx)=+\infty
, for any
x\neq 0
. Moreover, since
s=-1/m
and
g=f^{-m}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\left(1-s\langle x,y\rangle\right)^{\frac{1}{s}}_{+}}{g(x)}=\left(\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{(1+\langle x,\frac{y}{m}\rangle)^{-1}_{+}}{f^{-1}(x)}\right)^{m}=\left(\mathcal{L}_{-1}(f^{-1})\left(\frac{y}{m}\right)\right)^{m}.

Let us define the function
\mathcal{M}f
, for
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by

\mathcal{M}f(y)=(\mathcal{L}_{-1}(f^{-1})(y))^{-1}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1+\langle x,y\rangle}{f(x)}.

It was proved in
[
FGSZ
]
that, with these hypotheses, the function
\mathcal{M}f
belongs to
\mathcal{F}
(see definition
1.4
).
Using the change of variables
y=mz
, we get

one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathcal{L}_{s}g(y)dy=m^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{1}{f(x)^{m}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\mathcal{M}f(z))^{m}}dz=m^{n}Q_{m}(f).

Thus, for

x\neq 0
and for any
m>n

(2.2)

where, for any

I_{K}
is defined by
I_{K}(x)=0
for

and
I_{K}(x)=+\infty
for

On the other hand, if

Same as before, Conjecture

Similarly as before, one may easily prove that the operator
Q_{m}

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a symmetric convex body. For any even convex function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(tx)=+\infty
, for any
x\neq 0
, we define the function

\begin{array}[]{ccccl}\pi_{m}(f)&:&\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}&\to&\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{+\infty\}\\
&&(x,s)&\mapsto&\pi_{m}(f)(x,s)=\begin{cases}\|s\|_{K}f(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}})&\text{if $s\neq 0$}.\\
\lim_{s\to 0}\|s\|_{K}f(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}})&\text{if $s=0$}.\end{cases}\\
\end{array}

We denote the domain of
f
by
\mathrm{dom}(f)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};f(x)<+\infty\}
. Then

\mathrm{dom}(\pi_{m}(f))=\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\pi_{m}(f)(x,s)<+\infty\}=\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};x\in\|s\|_{K}\mathrm{dom}(f)\}.

Consider the following convex body

C^{K}_{m}(f)=\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\pi_{m}(f)(x,s)\leq 1\}=\overline{\left\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\};\|s\|_{K}f\left(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}}\right)\leq 1\right\}}.

One has,

\displaystyle(C^{K}_{m}(f))^{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\langle(x,s),(y,t)\rangle\leq 1,\forall(x,s)\in C^{K}_{m}(f)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\langle x,y\rangle+\langle s,t\rangle\leq 1,\forall(x,s);\|s\|_{K}f\left(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

Let
z=\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}}
, then,

\displaystyle(C^{K}_{m}(f))^{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\|s\|_{K}\langle z,y\rangle+\langle s,t\rangle\leq 1,\forall(z,s),f(z)\leq\frac{1}{\|s\|_{K}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\sup_{\left\{s;\|s\|_{K}\leq 1/f(z)\right\}}\|s\|_{K}\langle z,y\rangle+\langle s,t\rangle\leq 1,\forall z\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\frac{\langle z,y\rangle+\|t\|_{K^{\circ}}}{f(z)}\leq 1,\forall z\right\}

\displaystyle\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m};\sup_{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle z,y\rangle+\|t\|_{K^{\circ}}}{f(z)}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\left\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\};\|t\|_{K^{\circ}}\mathcal{M}f\left(\frac{y}{\|t\|_{K^{\circ}}}\right)\leq 1\right\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{K^{\circ}}_{m}(\mathcal{M}f).

Let us compute the volume of the convex body
C_{m}^{K}(f)
.

\displaystyle\left|C_{m}^{K}(f)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left\{(x,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\setminus\{0\};\|s\|_{K}f\left(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}}\right)\leq 1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\left|\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\|s\|_{K}f\left(\frac{x}{\|s\|_{K}}\right)\leq 1\right\}\right|_{n}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\|s\|_{K}^{n}\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};f(z)\leq\frac{1}{\|s\|_{K}}\right\}\right|_{n}ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(\|v\|_{K})dv,

where
\varphi(t)=t^{n}\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};f(z)\leq\frac{1}{t}\right\}\right|_{n}.

Using Fubini and a change of variables, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(\|v\|_{K})dv=\int_{0}^{+\infty}(-\varphi^{\prime}(t))|\{v\in\mathbb{R}^{m};\|v\|_{K}\leq t\}|dt=|K|\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{m}(-\varphi^{\prime}(t))dt=m|K|\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{m-1}\varphi(t)dt.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle|C_{m}^{K}(f)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m|K|\int_{0}^{+\infty}t^{m-1}t^{n}\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};f(z)\leq\frac{1}{t}\right\}\right|_{n}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m|K|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{u^{m+n+1}}\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n};f(z)\leq u\right\}\right|du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m|K|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{f(z)}^{+\infty}\frac{du}{u^{m+n+1}}dz

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{m+n}|K|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{dz}{f(z)^{m+n}}.

Using that
C_{m}^{K}(f)^{\circ}=C_{m}^{K^{\circ}}(\mathcal{M}f)
, we get

|C_{m}^{K}(f)^{\circ}|=\frac{m}{m+n}|K^{\circ}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{dz}{(\mathcal{M}f(z))^{m+n}}.

Thus, we obtain for any
m>0

(2.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{f(x)^{m+n}}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{(\mathcal{M}f(y))^{m+n}}dy=\left(\frac{m+n}{m}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{|K||K^{\circ}|}|C_{m}^{K}(f)||C_{m}^{K}(f)^{\circ}|.

3.
Proof of the inequalities in dimension 1 and 2 for
s>0
.

In this section, we start by giving a monotonicity result, which implies Conjecture
1.2
in dimension
1
where
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that Conjecture
1.2
in dimension
1
was proved in
[
FM10
, page 17]
for any
s>0
. We present here a simpler proof in the case where
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, we present a proof of Theorem
1.3
, which is Mahler’s conjecture for
s
-concave functions in the case
n=2
and
\frac{1}{s}\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let
m=\frac{1}{s}
and
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even
s
-concave function, then there exists an even function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, concave on its support, such that
g=f^{m}
. Thus, in dimension
n=1
and for
1/s\in\mathbb{N}
, Conjecture
1.2
is a consequence of the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
a>0
,
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even concave function on its support
C=[-a,a]
and

P_{m}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)^{m}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy.

Then the sequence
((m+1)^{2}P_{m}(f)-4m)_{m}
is non-decreasing. Hence, one has
P_{m}(f)\geq\frac{4}{m+1}
.
Moreover, if
P_{m}(f)=\frac{4}{m+1}
then either
f
is constant on its support
[-a,a]
or there is
c>0
such that
f(x)=c\left(1-\frac{|x|}{a}\right)_{+}
.

Proof.

First, using Lemma
2.1
, we know that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{L}f)=C^{\circ}=[-\frac{1}{a},\frac{1}{a}]
.
Set

I_{m}=(m+1)\int_{0}^{a}f(x)^{m}dx\quad\mbox{and}\quad J_{m}=(m+1)\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{a}}(\mathcal{L}f(y))^{m}dy.

The monotonicity that we want to prove reduces to
I_{m}J_{m}\geq m+1
, for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. The inequality holds for
m=0
(there is equality). Let
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Being concave on
[-a,a]
, the function
f
is left differentiable on
(-a,a]
. By concavity, denoting by
f^{\prime}(t)
the left derivative of
f
at
t
, we have for all
t\in(-a,a]
and
x\in[-a,a]

(3.1)

\displaystyle f(x)\leq f(t)+(x-t)f^{\prime}(t).

multiplying both sides of the inequality by
mf(t)^{m-1}
and integrating on
[0,a]
gives

f(x)I_{m-1}=f(x)\int_{0}^{a}mf(t)^{m-1}dt\leq\int_{0}^{a}mf(t)^{m}dt+\int_{0}^{a}m(x-t)f^{\prime}(t)f(t)^{m-1}dt.

Integration by parts gives that for every
x\in[-a,a]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}m(x-t)f^{\prime}(t)f(t)^{m-1}dt=(x-a)f(a)^{m}-xf(0)^{m}+\int_{0}^{a}f(t)^{m}dt.

Using that
(x-a)f(a)^{m}\leq 0
, we get that for all
x\in[-a,a]
,

(3.2)

f(x)I_{m-1}\leq I_{m}-xf(0)^{m}.

Hence, one has for all
x\in[-a,a]

\frac{I_{m-1}}{I_{m}}\leq\frac{1}{f(x)}\left(1-\frac{xf(0)^{m}}{I_{m}}\right).

By taking the infinimum over all
x\in[-a,a]
, and denoting
y_{0}=\frac{f(0)^{m}}{I_{m}}
we get

\frac{I_{m-1}}{I_{m}}\leq\mathcal{L}f\left(y_{0}\right).

Since
\mathcal{L}f
is concave, we can apply the result to
\mathcal{L}f
and, denoting
x_{0}=\frac{(\mathcal{L}f(0))^{m}}{J_{m}}
, we get

\frac{J_{m-1}}{J_{m}}\leq f\left(x_{0}\right).

On the other hand, the definition of
\mathcal{L}f
implies that for all
x\in\mathrm{supp}f
,
y\in\mathrm{supp}\mathcal{L}f

f(x)\mathcal{L}f(y)\leq(1-xy)_{+}.

Using (
3.2
) for
x=0
gives that
I_{m}\geq f(0)I_{m-1}
, thus
I_{m}\geq f(0)^{m}a
. In the same way, one has
J_{m}\geq\mathcal{L}f(0)^{m}a^{-1}
. Multiplying these inequalities and using that
f
is even and concave, we get
\mathcal{L}f(0)=\max(f)^{-1}=f(0)^{-1}
, we conclude that
I_{m}J_{m}\geq 1
, which implies that
x_{0}y_{0}\leq 1
.
Hence, one has

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{I_{m-1}J_{m-1}}{I_{m}J_{m}}\leq f\left(x_{0}\right)\mathcal{L}f\left(y_{0}\right)\leq 1-x_{0}y_{0}=1-\frac{1}{I_{m}J_{m}}.

Finally we get
I_{m-1}J_{m-1}\leq I_{m}J_{m}-1
. Hence the sequence
(I_{m}J_{m}-m)_{m}
is non-decreasing. Since
I_{m}J_{m}=\frac{(m+1)^{2}}{4}P_{m}(f)
,
we deduce that the sequence
u_{m}=(m+1)^{2}P_{m}(f)-4m
is non-decreasing. It follows that
u_{m}\geq u_{0}=P_{0}(f)=4
, hence
P_{m}(f)\geq\frac{4}{m+1}
, for any integer
m
.

Assume now that there is equality, for some integer
m
:
P_{m}(f)=\frac{4}{m+1}
. Then, there is equality in (
3.3
). It follows that there is equality in (
3.2
) for
x=x_{0}
and also
(x_{0}-a)f(a)^{m}=0
. Moreover, there is also equality in the corresponding inequality for
\mathcal{L}f
instead of
f
and
y_{0}
instead of
x_{0}
, thus
(y_{0}-\frac{1}{a})\mathcal{L}f(1/a)^{m}=0
.
In addition, there is equality in (
3.1
) for
x=x_{0}
and for almost any
t\in[-a,a]
:

f(t)=f(x_{0})+(t-x_{0})f^{\prime}(t).

On the other hand, one has
f(t)=f(x_{0})+\int_{x_{0}}^{t}f^{\prime}(u)du
. Since the left derivative
f^{\prime}
is non-increasing, we deduce that
f^{\prime}
is constant on
[0,x_{0})
and on
(x_{0},a]
, which means that
f
is affine on on
[0,x_{0})
and on
(x_{0},a]
. In the same way,
\mathcal{L}f
is affine on
[0,y_{0})
and on
(y_{0},1/a]
.

If
x_{0}=a
, then
f
is affine on
[0,a]
and
J_{m}=\mathcal{L}f(0)^{m}/a
. But, by concavity, one has for every
y\in[0,1/a]

\mathcal{L}f(y)\geq\mathcal{L}f(0)(1-ay).

Raising this inequality to the power
m
and integrating, one gets that
J_{m}\geq\mathcal{L}f(0)^{m}/a
. Since there is equality we deduce that
\mathcal{L}f(y)=\mathcal{L}f(0)(1-ay)
, for every
y\in[0,1/a]
. Thus
f=f(0)\mathds{1}_{[-a,a]}
.

If
x_{0}\neq a
, then
f(a)=0
. Thus there exists
0\leq b\leq 1/a
and
c>0
such that, for
t\geq 0
,

f(t)=c(1-bt)\mathds{1}_{[0,x_{0}]}+c(1-bx_{0})\frac{a-x}{a-x_{0}}\mathds{1}_{(x_{0},a]}.

This implies that, for any
y\geq 0
,

\mathcal{L}f(y)=\frac{1}{c}\left(\mathds{1}_{[0,b]}(y)+\frac{1-x_{0}y}{1-bx_{0}}\mathds{1}_{(b,\frac{1}{a}]}(y)\right).

Since
x_{0}\neq a
, one has
\mathcal{L}f(1/a)\neq 0
, hence
y_{0}=1/a
. This gives
I_{m}=af(0)^{m}
and this implies as before that
f(x)=f(0)(1-\frac{x}{a})
, for all
x\in[0,a]
.

∎

Let us prove the 2-dimensional case of our inequality for
m
being an integer.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even function, concave on its support, such that
0<\int f<+\infty
. Then,

P_{m}(f)\geq\frac{16}{(m+1)(m+2)}.

Moreover, there is equality for
f=\mathds{1}_{[-1,1]^{2}}
.

As noticed before, this theorem may be reformulated in the following form: for any integer
m>0
and any even function
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
that is
\frac{1}{m}
-concave, integrable with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g>0
one has

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}g\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{m}}g\geq\frac{16m^{2}}{(m+1)(m+2)}.

The following corollary proves that letting
m
go to infinity we get a new proof of Mahler conjecture for even log-concave functions, which was our previous main result in
[
FN
]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be an even log-concave function such that
0<\int f<+\infty
, then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{\circ}(y)dy\geq 16,

where
f^{\circ}(y)=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{-\langle x,y\rangle}}{f(x)}
is the polar function of
f
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an even log-concave function and consider the function
f_{m}
defined as follows

f_{m}(x)=\left(1+\frac{\log f(x)}{m}\right)^{m}_{+}.

First, notice that the log-concavity of
f
implies the
\frac{1}{m}
-concavity of
f_{m}
. Since for all
x
,
e^{-x}\geq 1-x
, we get

\left(1-\frac{1}{m}\langle x,y\rangle\right)^{m}\leq e^{-\langle x,y\rangle}.

Thus,
\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{m}}f\leq\mathcal{L}_{0}f=f^{\circ}
. In addition, it is easy to see that
f_{m}\leq f
for any
m>0
, and since a log-concave function
is continuous on its support one has
\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}=f
locally uniformly on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Hence,
we can conclude that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f
.
The fact that
\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{m}}f\leq f^{\circ}
implies that
\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{m}}f_{m}\leq f_{m}^{\circ}
. Moreover, one has

\frac{16m^{2}}{(m+1)(m+2)}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\frac{1}{m}}f_{m}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}^{\circ}.

On the other hand, since the functions
f_{m}
are log-concave and
\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}=f
, then using lemma 3.2 in
[
AKM
]
, we know that
\lim_{m\to\infty}f_{m}^{\circ}=f^{\circ}
locally uniformly on the interior of the support of
f^{\circ}
. Applying the same lemma again on the log-concave function
f_{m}^{\circ}
, we get that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}^{\circ}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{\circ}
.
Thus, we conclude that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{\circ}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{\circ}_{m}\geq 16.

∎

.

Notice that we don’t get the equality case in this way. Recall that, in

f(x_{1},x_{2})=(1-\|x_{1}\|_{K_{1}})_{+}\mathds{1}_{K_{2}}(x_{2}),

For the proof of theorem
3.2
, let us start by proving that every even concave function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
can be
m
-equipartitioned by the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
m>0
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an even concave function such that
0<\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f<+\infty
. Then there exists a linear invertible map
T:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that the function
f\circ T
is
m
-equipartitioned.

Proof.

The proof follows the proof of Lemma 4.2 in our previous work
[
FN
]
but we reproduce it for completeness.
For any
u\in S^{1}
, let
C(u)\subset S^{1}
be the open half-circle delimited by
u
and
-u
containing the vectors
v
which are after
u
with respect to the counterclockwise orientation of
S^{1}
. For
v\in C(u)
, let
C(u,v)=\mathbb{R}_{+}u+\mathbb{R}_{+}v
be the cone generated by
u
and
v
and define

f_{u}(v)=\int_{C(u,v)}f(x)^{m}dx.

The map
f_{u}
is continuous and increasing on
C(u)
,
f_{u}(u)=0
and, since
f
is even,
f_{u}(v)\longrightarrow\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m}(x)dx
when
v\longrightarrow-u
. Thus, there exists a unique
v(u)\in C(u)
such that

f_{u}(v(u))=\int_{C(u,v(u))}f^{m}(x)dx=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m}(x)dx.

Notice that
v:S^{1}\to S^{1}
is continuous and, since
f
is even, one has

\int_{C(v(u),-u)}f^{m}(x)dx=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m}(x)dx,

thus

for any

.
For

, let

Then,

is continuous on

and, since

is even,

\displaystyle g(v(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{C(v(u),-u)}f^{m-1}(x)dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m-1}(x)dx-\int_{C(u,v(u))}f^{m-1}(x)dx-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m-1}(x)dx-\int_{C(u,v(u))}f^{m-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-g(u).

Hence, by the intermediate value theorem, there exists
u\in S^{1}
such that

, thus

\int_{C(u,v(u))}f^{m-1}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m-1}\quad\hbox{and}\quad\int_{C(u,v(u))}f^{m}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{m}.

Proof of Theorem

The proof of the theorem concludes if

verifies Mahler’s conjecture in dimension
m+2
, i.e.

K_{\infty,m}
verifies the hypotheses of Proposition
1.11

m\in\mathbb{N}
. Set, for any positive integer

\mathbb{R}^{k}_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k};\varepsilon_{i}x_{i}\geq 0,\forall i\in\{1,...,k\}\}

•

K_{\infty,m}(f)
can be partitioned into
2^{m+2}
pieces of the same volume. Since

m

\displaystyle\left|K_{\infty,m}(f)\cap\mathbb{R}^{m+2}_{+}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}f(x)^{m}dx=\frac{1}{2^{2}|B_{\infty}^{m}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(x)^{m}|B_{\infty}^{m}|dx=\frac{1}{2^{m+2}}|K_{\infty,m}(f)|.

An algebraic characterisation of non-Archimedean Stein spaces
Tom Biesbrouck

Abstract
. We introduce Liu algebras as Banach algebras which are ‘locally affinoid’, and define non-Archimedean Stein algebras as suitable inverse limits of these. We show that this gives rise to a complete functorial characterisation of non-Archimedean Liu and Stein spaces as Berkovich spectra of their respective algebras, thereby resolving a conjecture of Michael Temkin. This can be interpreted as a non-Archimedean analytic version of Serre’s criterion for affineness. Furthermore, we prove a criterion that distinguishes affinoid algebras within the category of Liu algebras, answering another conjecture of Temkin. We also prove a generalisation of the Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem for non-Archimedean Stein spaces.



Introduction

Motivation.

In classical algebraic geometry, Serre’s criterion for affineness provides a full characterisation of affine schemes in terms of vanishing higher cohomology. Analogously, one might wonder whether the conventional ‘local building blocks’ of non-Archimedean analytic geometry, i.e. Berkovich spectra of affinoid algebras, also admit such an equivalent description. However, by work of Qing Liu in the late eighties, this turned out to be impossible: in
[
Liu88
]
, Liu constructs a non-affinoid compact
k
-analytic space with affinoid normalisation; and in
[
Liu90
, §5]
, he constructs a non-affinoid analytic domain of the two-dimensional unit disk, both with vanishing higher cohomology. Consequently, a compact non-Archimedean analytic space
X
with ‘vanishing higher cohomology’ is nowadays referred to as a Liu space. More generally, when
X
is not necessarily compact, it is called a Stein space, inspired by the complex analytic setting.

One natural approach still to recover a non-Archimedean analytic version of Serre’s criterion for affineness, would thus be to enlarge the category of Banach algebras of which we take Berkovich spectra to serve as ‘local building blocks’. In this paper, we achieve this by introducing Liu algebras as Banach algebras which are ‘locally affinoid’, and proving that Liu spaces correspond exactly to Berkovich spectra of these Liu algebras. Subsequently, as to also include general Stein spaces, we recall the definition of Berkovich spectra for Fréchet algebras and define Stein algebras as suitable inverse limits of Liu algebras. This enables us to characterise any Stein space as the Berkovich spectrum of a corresponding Stein algebra. A direct corollary of all this, is the initially desired non-Archimedean analytic version of Serre’s criterion for affineness.

We remark that our algebraic definition of Liu algebras as ‘locally affinoid’ Banach algebras was already conjectured to correspond to Liu spaces in the first prerprint version of
[
Tem22
, §4]
on arXiv. A proof of this was then attempted in the first prerprint version of
[
Xia23
, Appendix B]
on arXiv. However, this proof overlooked some essential subtleties and was therefore not published. Furthermore, a characterisation of dagger Stein spaces without boundary as Berkovich spectra of certain Fréchet algebras has already been worked out in
[
BBBK18
, §4.2]
.

Background.

We recall the terminology and characterisation of Liu and Stein spaces as were introduced in
[
MP21
]
. We let
k
be a complete non-Archimedean field (possibly trivially valued) and consider non-Archimedean analytic spaces in the sense of Berkovich
[

, §1.2]

Definition 0.1
.

Let

be a

•

The

\widehat{K}_{X}\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in X\,\,\middle|\,\,|f(x)|\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{K}\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\right\},

.

k

(ii)

To be precise, it is not proven explicitly in

Definition 0.6
.

Let
X
be a (strictly)
k
-Stein space. A
(strictly) rational domain
of

is a subset of
X
of the form

X\left(r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right)\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in X\,\,\middle|\,\,|f_{i}(x)|\leq r_{i}|g(x)|\text{ for }i=1,\ldots,n\right\}

Lastly, we recall the notion of (graded) reduction from

).

Statement of the results.

The main enterprise of this paper is to provide a complete functorial characterisation of
k
-Liu spaces (resp.
k
-Stein spaces) as Berkovich spectra of an explicit class of
k
-Banach algebras (resp.
k
-Fréchet algebras). To achieve this, we introduce (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras as
k
-Banach algebras that are ‘locally
k
-affinoid’, i.e. their Berkovich spectra admit ‘nice coverings by affinoid rational domains’. By construction, the proof of Tate’s Acyclicity Theorem then carries over almost verbatim, endowing the Berkovich spectrum of a
k
-Liu algebra with a canonical acyclic
k
-analytic structure sheaf. More generally, we define a
k
-Stein algebra as a
k
-Fréchet algebra obtained as a suitable inverse limit of
k
-Liu algebras, and define the
k
-analytic structure on its Berkovich spectrum via gluing. For the definition of
k
-Fréchet algebras and their Berkovich spectra, see Section
7
. Ultimately, we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 0.8

(cf. Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
8.3
)
.

The category of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras (resp.
k
-Stein algebras) is anti-equivalent to the category of (strictly)
k
-Liu spaces (resp.
k
-Stein spaces). More precisely, taking Berkovich spectra of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras (resp.
k
-Stein algebras) and taking global sections of structure sheaves on (strictly)
k
-Liu spaces (resp.
k
-Stein spaces) induce anti-isomorphisms of categories which are mutually inverse to each other.

Remark 0.9
.

In the theorem above, it is implicit that for a compact (resp. countable at infinity)
k
-analytic space
X
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
can be endowed with a canonical
k
-Banach structure (resp.
k
-Fréchet structure). When
X
is compact, this is done by taking a finite affinoid covering
X=\cup_{i}X_{i}
and letting
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X}=\max_{i}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{X_{i}}
for every
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
, where each
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X_{i}}
is a norm on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{i})
. It is easily verified that different choices of
\{X_{i},\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{X_{i}}\}_{i}
yield equivalent norms on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
. When
X
is countable at infinity,
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
can be endowed with a canonical
k
-Fréchet structure by taking an exhaustion
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
of
X
by compact analytic domains, each of which induces a seminorm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{D_{i}}
on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
via pullback. It is easily verified that different choices for
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
yield equivalent families of seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{D_{i}}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
.

As a direct corollary of Theorem
0.8
, we get the following non-Archimedean analytic version of Serre’s criterion for affineness.

Corollary 0.10
.

Let
X
be a separated
k
-analytic space. Then

(i)

X
is compact (resp. countable at infinity), and

(ii)

for every analytic extension
\ell/k
,
X_{\ell}
is cohomologically Stein

if and only if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
is a
k
-Liu algebra (resp.
k
-Stein algebra) and
X\cong\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
.

Moreover, we prove the following criterion to distinguish (strictly)
k
-affinoid algebras within the category of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras.

Theorem 0.11

(cf. Theorem
6.2
)
.

Let
k
be stable and
X
a
k
-Liu space. Denote
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
.

(i)

X
is
k
-affinoid if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathrm{gr}}
is a finitely generated
\widetilde{k}_{\mathrm{gr}}
-algebra and there exist elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, with spectral radii
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}
, such that the Laurent domains
X(r_{1}^{-1}f_{1}^{-1}),\ldots,X(r_{n}^{-1}f_{n}^{-1})

.

(ii)

If

is non-trivially valued and

is strictly
k
-Liu, then

is strictly
k
-affinoid if and only if
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}
is a finitely generated
\widetilde{k}
-algebra and there exist elements

, each with spectral radius

, such that the strictly Laurent domains

.

Remark 0.12
.

The criterion above is a weakening of
[
Tem22
, Conjecture 4.1.4]
, where Temkin did not require the existence of affinoid coverings by Laurent domains. However, a counterexample to Temkin’s stronger conjecture can be found in
[
Liu88
]
, where Liu constructs a non-affinoid strictly
k
-Liu space

for which the reduction of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
equals
\widetilde{k}
.

Lastly, after having slightly generalised the definition of Runge immersions, we will prove the following generalisation of the Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem.

Theorem 0.13

(cf. Theorem
10.3
)
.

Let
\varphi:Y\to X
be a monomorphism of (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces with
Y
(strictly)
k
-affinoid. Then there exists an admissible covering
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
X
by (strictly) rational domains such that
\varphi
induces (strictly) Runge immersions
\varphi_{i}:\varphi^{-1}(R_{i})\to R_{i}

It follows that every (strictly) Liu domain in a (strictly)

can be written as a finite union of (strictly)

-affinoid connected components of (strictly) rational domains (cf. Corollary

).

Structure of the paper.

This paper consists of two parts. The first part, ranging from Section

Conventions.

Throughout this paper, we let
k
be a complete non-Archimedean field (possibly trivially valued) and consider non-Archimedean analytic spaces in the sense of Berkovich
[
Ber93
, §1.2]
. Whenever the word ‘strict’ is used,

is assumed to be non-trivially valued. An analytic extension of

is a field extension

1.
Rational domains and rational localisations

In this section, we recall basic notions concerning Berkovich spectra of
k
-Banach algebras. In particular, we focus on rational domains and their connection with rational localisations of
k
-Banach algebras.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra.

(i)

The
Berkovich spectrum of
\mathcal{A}
is the set

\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})\vcentcolon=\left\{\text{bounded multiplicative seminorms }|\cdot(x)|:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}

equipped with the weakest topology such that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:|\cdot(x)|\mapsto|f(x)|
is continuous for every
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

The
spectral radius function of
\mathcal{A}
is given by

\rho_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:f\mapsto\inf_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\sqrt[n]{\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert}=\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}|f(x)|.

(iii)

\mathcal{A}
is called
uniform
if its norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}
is power-multiplicative.

(iv)

\mathcal{A}
is called a
k
-Banach function algebra
if its spectral radius function
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
is a complete power-multiplicative norm.

Remark 1.2
.

Recall that taking Berkovich spectra is contravariantly functorial, i.e. a bounded morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of
k
-Banach algebras induces a continuous map
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi):\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
via pullback. For the equality in the definition of
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
, see
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.3.1]
. When
k
is non-trivially valued, it follows from Banach’s Open Mapping Theorem
[
Bou81
, Théorème I.3.3/1]
that
\mathcal{A}
is a
k
-Banach function algebra if and only if
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
are equivalent as seminorms. Note that a uniform
k
-Banach algebra is always a
k
-Banach function algebra.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.

(i)

A
rational domain of
X
is a subset of the form

X\left(r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right)\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in X\,\,\middle|\,\,|f_{i}(x)|\leq r_{i}|g(x)|\text{ for all }i\right\},

where
r_{\bullet}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
,
g\in\mathcal{A}
and
f_{\bullet}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
such that
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
generate the unit ideal in
\mathcal{A}
.

(ii)

A
Laurent domain of
X
is a subset of the form

X\left(r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet},s_{\bullet}g_{\bullet}^{-1}\right)\vcentcolon=\left\{x\in X\,\,\middle|\,\,|f_{i}(x)|\leq r_{i}\text{ and }|g_{j}(x)|\geq s_{j}\text{ for all }i,j\right\},

where
r_{\bullet}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
,
s_{\bullet}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{m}
,
f_{\bullet}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
and
g_{\bullet}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\in\mathcal{A}^{m}
.

(iii)

A
Weierstrass domain of
X
is a Laurent domain for which we can set
m=0
in the notation above.

When
k
is non-trivially valued, we define
strictly rational
(resp.
Laurent
,
Weierstrass
) domains as rational (resp. Laurent, Weierstrass) domains for which we can take each
r_{i}=1
(and
s_{j}=1
).

Definition 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra. A
rational localisation
of
\mathcal{A}
is a
k
-Banach algebra of the form

\mathcal{A}\left\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right\rangle\vcentcolon=\frac{\mathcal{A}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle}{\mathrm{Cl}(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})}

equipped with its residue norm, where
r_{\bullet}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
,
g\in\mathcal{A}
and
f_{\bullet}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
such that
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
generate the unit ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Here,
\mathrm{Cl}(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
denotes the closure of the ideal
(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
in
\mathcal{A}
. When
k
is non-trivially valued,
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
is called a
strictly rational localisation
if we can take each

. Analogously, one defines
(strictly) Laurent and Weierstrass localisations
.

The following proposition establishes the expected relation between rational domains and rational localisations.

Proposition 1.5
.

(
[
Mih16
, Proposition 2.12]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. For a rational localisation
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
of
\mathcal{A}
, the canonical morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle

Remark 1.6
.

To be precise, in
[
Mih16
]
, it is assumed that
k
is non-trivially valued, and rational domains and rational localisations are defined slightly differently, i.e. there one assumes that

generates the unit ideal instead of

. However, one easily verifies that the proof of the cited proposition above remains valid in our setting.

Remark 1.7
.

Recall that a bounded morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of seminormed groups is called
admissible
when the restriction of the norm on
\mathcal{B}
to
\varphi(\mathcal{A})
is equivalent to the residue seminorm on
\varphi(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{A}/\ker(\varphi)
. Note that the composition of admissible morphisms is again admissible, and that the image of an admissible
k
-linear map between
k
-Banach algebras is always closed.

The following proposition is a generalisation of
[
Mih16
, Propositions 2.17 and 2.18]
.

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach function algebra. For any
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
generating the unit ideal and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the ideal
I=(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
is closed in
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
.

Proof.

First note that for
r=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
, we get that
I=\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\cap I(\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}k_{r})
in
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}k_{r}
. Since
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle
is closed in
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}k_{r}
, it thus suffices to prove that
I(\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}k_{r})
is closed. Hence, we may assume that
k
is non-trivially valued and
r_{1}=\ldots=r_{n}=1
. Moreover, as
\mathcal{A}
is a
k
-Banach function algebra, we may also assume that the norm on
\mathcal{A}
is given by the spectral radius function
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}
. Let us now write
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{A}\langle T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}\rangle
and denote for every
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
by
\widetilde{x}\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})
the seminorm defined by

|G(\widetilde{x})|\vcentcolon=\sup_{I\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}|G_{I}(x)|\quad\text{ for each }\quad G=\sum_{I\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}G_{I}T^{I}\in\mathcal{B}.

Note that
\widetilde{x}
is maximal among the preimage of
x
under
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. As
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is compact and
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
generate the unit ideal in
\mathcal{A}
, there is an
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\inf_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}\max\left\{|f_{1}(x)|,\ldots,|f_{n}(x)|,|g(x)|\right\}\geq\varepsilon.

As a result, we have for each
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
that

(1.1)

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|(gT_{j}-f_{j})(\widetilde{x})|=\max\left\{|f_{1}(x)|,\ldots,|f_{n}(x)|,|g(x)|\right\}\geq\varepsilon.

Now define the linear maps

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{B}^{n}\to\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}:(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\mapsto(b_{i}(gT_{j}-f_{j}))_{1\leq i,j\leq n}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle:\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}\to\mathcal{B}:(b_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\mapsto\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}b_{ii}.

Note that their composition
\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
is given by

\varphi:\mathcal{B}^{n}\to\mathcal{B}:(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\mapsto b_{1}(gT_{1}-f_{1})+\ldots+b_{n}(gT_{n}-f_{n}),

so
\varphi(\mathcal{B}^{n})=(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
. Hence, to show that the latter ideal is closed in
\mathcal{B}
, it suffices to show that both
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
are admissible. First take
b=(b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq n}\in\mathcal{B}^{n}
. By using the assumption that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}=\rho_{\mathcal{B}}
and applying equation (
1.1
), we get that

\displaystyle\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n}}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\lVert b_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}|b_{i}(\widetilde{x})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|(gT_{j}-f_{j})(\widetilde{x})|\cdot|b_{i}(\widetilde{x})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|(gT_{j}-f_{j})b_{i}(\widetilde{x})|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\lVert(gT_{j}-f_{j})b_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\left\lVert\varphi_{1}(b)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\lVert gT_{j}-f_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\lVert b_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\lVert gT_{j}-f_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n}},

or summarised

\varepsilon\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n}}\leq\left\lVert\varphi_{1}(b)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}}\leq\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\lVert gT_{j}-f_{j}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n}},

showing that
\varphi_{1}
is admissible. Next, take
b^{\prime}=(b_{ij}^{\prime})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}
. Then we have that

\left\lVert\varphi_{2}(b^{\prime})\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}=\left\lVert\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}b_{ii}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\lVert b_{ii}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{1\leq j\leq n}\left\lVert b_{ij}^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}=\left\lVert b^{\prime}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}^{n^{2}}},

showing that
\varphi_{2}
is a bounded, and thus continuous since
k
was assumed to be non-trivially valued. As
\varphi_{2}
is also surjective, it follows from Banach’s Open Mapping Theorem that it is also admissible.
∎

The following lemma shows that rational localisation commutes with base change. Although the proof is straightforward, we state it here for convenience of later reference.

Lemma 1.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra and
\ell/k
an analytic extension. Then
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\cong(\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
.

Proof.

Let us denote
I=(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})\subset\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle
. By definition of rational localisation, we have an exact and admissible sequence

0\to\mathrm{Cl}(I)\to\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}\left\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right\rangle\to 0.

Since
-\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
is exact and preserves admissibility, the induced sequence

0\to\mathrm{Cl}(I)\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\to\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\to\mathcal{A}\left\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\to 0

is also exact and admissible. One easily verifies that

\mathrm{Cl}(I)\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell=\mathrm{Cl}\left(I(\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\cong(\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}T_{\bullet}\rangle.

Hence, it follows by definition of rational localisations that
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\cong(\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
.
∎

2.
Liu algebras

In this section, we introduce
k
-Liu algebras as
k
-Banach algebras which ‘locally look like
k
-affinoid algebras’. Although this name only makes sense a posteriori when having proven their correspondence with
k
-Liu spaces (cf. Theorem
5.4
), we already stick with it from the beginning to avoid notational cluttering. Our definition of
k
-Liu algebras could already be found in
[
Tem22
, §4]
and Appendix B of the first version of
[
Xia23
]
on arXiv, where they were called
locally
k
-affinoid algebras
.

Definition 2.1
.

A morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of
k
-Banach algebras is called
(strictly) rationally injective
if for each (strictly) rational localisation
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
of
\mathcal{A}
the induced base change morphism
\varphi\otimes\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle:\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle\to\mathcal{B}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
is injective.

Definition 2.2
.

A
k
-Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called a
(strictly)
k
-Liu algebra
if there exist finitely many (strictly) rational localisations
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
of
\mathcal{A}
, such that

(i)

each
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is (strictly)
k
-affinoid;

(ii)

the (strictly) rational domains
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
cover
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
;

(iii)

the canonical map
\iota:\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is (strictly) rationally injective.

Such a collection of (strictly) rational localisations of
\mathcal{A}
will be called a
(strictly) rational atlas
of
\mathcal{A}
. We define the
category of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras
as the category consisting of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras with bounded
k
-Banach algebra morphisms between them.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that a (strictly) rational localisation of a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra is again a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra. Also, the canonical map
\iota:\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is admissible by Banach’s Open Mapping Theorem when
k
is non-trivially valued, and thus in general as well by
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.2(ii)]
. Moreover, one obtains an equivalent definition for
k
-Liu algebras when replacing conditions (ii)-(iii) with the requirement for
\iota:\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
to be faithfully flat (cf. Corollary
5.10
).

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Liu algebra. For any
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
generating the unit ideal and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, the ideal
(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
is closed in
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}
. By definition, we have a Cartesian diagram of
k
-Banach algebras

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{A}\times_{\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}}\prod_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

with every morphism admissible, where each
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is a
k
-Tate algebra.

Let
g^{\prime},f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,f_{n}^{\prime},f_{n+1}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
be liftings of
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n},0\in\mathcal{A}
, generating the unit ideal in
\mathcal{B}
. To see that such a lifting always exists, let
f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,f_{n}^{\prime},g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
be liftings of
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. Since the latter generate the unit ideal in
\mathcal{A}
, there exist
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
a_{0}g+a_{1}f_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}f_{n}=1
. For liftings
b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
of
a_{0},a_{1}\ldots,a_{n}
, we then get that
b_{0}g^{\prime}+b_{1}f_{1}^{\prime}+\ldots+b_{n}f_{n}^{\prime}=1+c
with
c\in\ker(\varphi^{\prime})
. Now take
f_{n+1}^{\prime}=-c
as lifting of
0\in\mathcal{A}
.

Next, since the Gauss norm on
k
-Tate algebras is multiplicative, one easily verifies that
\prod_{i}\mathcal{T}_{i}
is a uniform
k
-Banach algebra, and thus so is
\mathcal{B}
. Hence, by Proposition
1.8
, the ideal
(g^{\prime}T_{1}-f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g^{\prime}T_{n+1}-f_{n+1}^{\prime})
is closed in
\mathcal{B}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n},T_{n+1}\rangle
. As the image of this ideal under the admissible epimorphism

\mathcal{B}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n},T_{n+1}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle:T_{n+1}\mapsto 0

is the ideal
(gT_{1}-f_{1},\ldots,gT_{n}-f_{n})
, it follows that the latter is also closed in
\mathcal{A}\langle r_{1}^{-1}T_{1},\ldots,r_{n}^{-1}T_{n}\rangle
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Liu algebra. For any
f\in\mathcal{A}
, the kernel of the canonical morphism
\mathcal{A}\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}\langle f,f^{-1}\rangle
coincides with
(T-f)\subset\mathcal{A}\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.4
, we can write

\mathcal{A}\langle T,T^{-1}\rangle=\frac{\mathcal{A}\langle T,S\rangle}{(TS-1)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{A}\langle f,f^{-1}\rangle=\frac{\mathcal{A}\langle T,S\rangle}{(T-f,fS-1)}.

Hence, it suffices to verify that
(T-f,TS-1)=(T-f,fS-1)
in
\mathcal{A}\langle T,S\rangle
. The latter follows directly from the equalities

\displaystyle fS-1=TS-1-S(T-f)\qquad\text{and}\qquad TS-1=fS-1+S(T-f).

∎

The following base change lemma for Liu algebras will allow us later on to prove properties for Liu algebras by reducing to the strict case.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\ell/k
be an analytic extension and let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra. Then
\mathcal{A}_{\ell}=\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
is a (strictly)
\ell
-Liu algebra.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a (strictly) rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}
. We claim that
\mathcal{A}_{1}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell,\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
constitutes a (strictly) rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
. By Lemma
1.9
, each
\mathcal{A}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
is a (strictly) rational localisation of
\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
. Clearly, each
\mathcal{A}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
is also (strictly)
\ell
-affinoid, and (strictly) rational injectivity of
\iota\otimes\ell:\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
follows from (strictly) rational injectivity of
\iota
and exactness of
-\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
. Next, let
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)
and denote by
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell
the canonical base change morphism. Then
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)(x)\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
for some
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}\langle s_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
. Hence, by Proposition
1.5
, we get for each
f_{j}
in
f_{\bullet}
that

|\varphi(f_{j})(x)|=|f_{j}(\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)(x))|\leq s_{j}|g(\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)(x))|=s_{j}|\varphi(g)(x)|,

and thus
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)
. It follows that the sets
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)
cover
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}\widehat{\otimes}_{k}\ell)
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

When
\ell/k
is a topgebraic extension of

for

, the converse of Lemma
2.6
also holds (cf. Proposition
5.7
).

3.
Liu domains

In this section, we define Liu domains of spectra of
k
-Liu algebras as a direct generalisation of affinoid domains from the classical setting and prove some of their expected properties. In particular, we show that rational domains are examples of Liu domains.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra. A
(strictly) Liu domain
of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is a subset
U\subset\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
equipped with a morphism
\iota_{U}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}
of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras such that
U=\mathrm{Spb}(\iota_{U})(\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{U}))
, and that
represents all (strictly)
k
-Liu morphisms into
U
, i.e. for any morphism of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{U}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{U}}

if and only if
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)
factorises through
U
, in which case the diagram is unique. In case
\mathcal{A}_{U}
is a (strictly)
k
-affinoid algebra,
U
is called a
(strictly)

affinoid domain
of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.
\mathcal{A}_{U}
is called the
coordinate ring
of
U
.

As might have been expected, rational domains are examples of Liu domains. In order to show this, we need the following two auxiliary results.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Liu algebra. For any
f\in\mathcal{A}
, there exist a
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and an
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}\leq C\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(f)^{n}
for all
n\geq N
. In particular, if
f
is quasinilpotent (i.e.
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(f)=0
), then
f
is nilpotent.

Proof.

By
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.4(i)]
the claim is true when
\mathcal{A}
is
k
-affinoid. Hence, let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}
and take
f\in\mathcal{A}
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
there now exist
C_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
N_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that
\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}\leq C_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}(f)^{n}
for all
n\geq N_{i}
. As a result, we get for all
n\geq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}N_{i}
that

\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\left\lVert f^{n}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}C_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{A}_{i}}(f)^{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})}C_{i}|f(x)|^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}C_{i}\cdot\max_{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})}|f(x)|^{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}C_{i}\cdot\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(f)^{n}.

Thus, we can take
C=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}C_{i}
and
N=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}N_{i}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a morphism of
k
-Liu algebras. Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
and

with

for all

. Then there exists a unique morphism

of

-Liu algebras extending

with

for all

.

Proof.

Using Lemma
3.2
, one can copy the argument from
[
Ber90
, Corollary 2.1.5]
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}

. A (strictly) rational domain

.

Denote by

-Liu morphisms into
U
, let

k
for every
\mathfrak{m}_{x}\in\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{A})
, one easily verifies that their residue norms are multiplicative. Hence, we obtain a canonical inclusion map
\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{A})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
via pullback, and
\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{A})
can be equipped with the same canonical topology as is defined in
[
BGR84
, §7.2.1]
for strictly
k
-affinoid algebras. By Lemma
3.5
, a morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of strictly
k
-Liu algebras induces a canonical map
\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{A})
, making the canonical inclusion map above functorial. To see that it is also a homeomorphism onto an everywhere dense subset
\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
, one can follow the same argument as in
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.15]
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued and let
\mathcal{A}
be a strictly
k
-Liu algebra. The elements of the everywhere dense subset
\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
are called
rigid points
. For a morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of strictly
k
-Liu algebras, we denote the restriction of
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)
to the set of rigid points of
\mathcal{B}
by
\mathrm{Spr}(\varphi):\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
. This is well-defined due to the functoriality of the canonical inclusion map
\mathrm{Max}(\mathcal{A})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}):\mathfrak{m}_{x}\mapsto x
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a strictly
k
-Liu algebra and let
U
be a strictly Liu domain of
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. Let
x\in\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A}_{U})
. Then

(i)

\mathrm{Spr}(\iota_{U})
is injective and satisfies
\mathrm{Spr}(\iota_{U})(\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A}_{U}))=U\cap\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
;

(ii)

the map
\iota_{U}
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{Spr}(\iota_{U})(x)}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{A}_{U}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}
;

(iii)

we have
\mathfrak{m}_{x}=\iota_{U}(\mathfrak{m}_{\mathrm{Spr}(\iota_{U})(x)})\mathcal{A}_{U}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.5
, we have for an arbitrary element
y\in U\cap\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
that
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}
is a finite extension of
k
, and thus a strictly
k
-Liu algebra which we can equip with a residue norm induced by
\mathcal{A}
. One can now apply the universal property of the strictly Liu domain
U
to the morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}
and give the same argument as in
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.2/1]
in case
U
is a strictly affinoid domain. In order to copy the argument from
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.2/1]
for a general Liu domain
U
, one also needs to show that
\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}\mathcal{A}_{U}
is a morphism of strictly
k
-Liu algebras. To see that this is the case, let
V
be a strictly affinoid domain of
U=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{U})
containing
y
and defined by the morphism
\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{V}
. One easily verifies that
V
is also a strictly affinoid domain in
X
defined by the composition morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{V}
. Hence, we get that

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{U}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}\mathcal{A}_{U}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\left(\mathcal{A}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}\right)\cong\left(\mathcal{A}_{U}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}_{V}\right)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}\cong\mathcal{A}_{V}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}\cong\frac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{m}_{y}}

is a finite extension of
k
. Equipping
\mathcal{A}_{U}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}\mathcal{A}_{U}
with its residue norm, it follows that
\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}\mathcal{A}_{U}
is a morphism of strictly
k
-Liu algebras.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

The version of Proposition
3.8
for strictly
k
-affinoid algebras, namely
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.2/1]
, does not only treat the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{x}
, but also its powers
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. After having proven Corollary
5.5
, one can also obtain this generalisation here. However, at this stage, we do not yet have an argument why the powers
\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}
should be closed, and if so, why their quotients should define strictly
k
-Liu algebras.

Moreover, when proving that
\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}/\mathfrak{m}_{y}\mathcal{A}_{U}
is a morphism of strictly
k
-Liu algebras, it was essential that
U=\mathrm{Spb}(\iota_{U})(\mathrm{Spb}(A_{U}))
is part of the definition of (strictly) Liu domains in order to see that
V
is also a strictly Liu domain in
X
. A posteriori, though, this directly follows from Corollary
5.5
and Corollary
5.6
(i). This allows us to remove the equality
U=\mathrm{Spb}(\iota_{U})(\mathrm{Spb}(A_{U}))
from the definition of (strictly) Liu domains, and derive it as a property of (strictly) Liu domains afterwards. In the strict case, this is done by reproving Proposition
3.8
, whereas the general case follows by the same base change argument as in
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.2.4(i)]
via Lemma
2.6
.

Proposition 3.10
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Banach algebra and let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be strictly rational localisations of
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
constitute a strictly rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}
if and only if

(i)

each
\mathcal{A}_{i}
is strictly
k
-affinoid;

(ii)

the rigid rational domains
\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
cover
\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
;

(iii)

the map
\iota:\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is strictly rationally injective.

Proof.

For the direct implication, it suffices to show that (ii) makes sense and holds. This is done by applying Proposition
3.8
(i) to the rational domains
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{1}),\ldots,\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
. For the converse implication, note that
\cup_{i}\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
is closed and contains
\cup_{i}\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A}_{i})=\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
, which is dense in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
by Proposition
3.6
. Hence, we get that
\cup_{i}\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a morphism of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.

(i)

If
U\subset X
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass, Laurent) domain, then
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)^{-1}(U)
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass, Laurent) domain in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})
represented by the morphism
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}_{U}
.

(ii)

Let
U,V\subset X
be (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass, Laurent) domains. Then
U\cap V
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass, Laurent) domain represented by the morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{A}_{V}
. In particular, (strictly) Laurent domains are also (strictly) rational domains.

(iii)

Let
U\subset X
be a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain and
V\subset U
. Then
V
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
X
if and only if it is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
U
.

Proof.

This is verified in the same way as in
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.3/6, Proposition 7.2.3/7, Theorem 7.2.4/2]
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Once we know that the morphism
\iota_{U}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}
for a Liu domain
U
in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is flat, we can show that the completed tensor product of two
k
-Liu algebras is again a
k
-Liu algebra (cf. Corollary
5.11
), and derive that the claims in Proposition
3.11
also hold for general Liu domains. Nevertheless, at this stage, an obvious version of the proposition can already be shown when
\mathcal{B}
,
U
and
V
are assumed to be (strictly)
k
-affinoid.

4.
Tate acyclicity for Liu algebras

In this section, we define a
G
-topology and a structure presheaf on Berkovich spectra of
k
-Liu algebras and prove a generalised version of Tate’s Acyclicity Theorem, showing that this presheaf is an acyclic sheaf. To recall the definition of a
G
-topology, see
[
BGR84
, Definition 9.1.1/1]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.

(i)

We have a
G
-topology
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
on
X
with (strictly) rational domains as admissible open subsets and finite coverings by (strictly) rational domains as admissible coverings.
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
is called the
weak (strictly) rational
G
-topology
on
X
.

(ii)

The assignment
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\vcentcolon=\mathcal{A}_{U}
for every (strictly) rational domain
U
in
X
defines a presheaf on
X
w.r.t.
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
.

Proof.

(i)

It follows from Proposition
3.11
that
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
is a well-defined
G
-topology on
X
.

(ii)

It follows from Proposition
3.4
that the assignment
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)\vcentcolon=\mathcal{A}_{U}
is well-defined. Functoriality follows from the defining universal property of Liu domains.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra with
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. As is the case for affinoid spaces (cf.
[
BGR84
, §7.3.2]
and
[
Ber90
, §2.3]
), one easily verifies for every
x\in X
that the stalk
\mathcal{O}_{X,x}
is a local ring with maximal ideal given by
\{f\in\mathcal{O}_{X,x}\,\,|\,\,|f(x)|=0\}
. Similarly, a morphism of
k
-Liu algebras induces local ring morphism on stalks.

For convenience, we recall the notions of (strictly) rational and Laurent coverings from
[
BGR84
, §8.2.2]
as special cases of admissible coverings in the weak (strictly) rational
G
-topology.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Liu algebra and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
.

(i)

A
rational covering
of
X
is a covering of the form

\mathfrak{U}=\left\{X\left(\frac{r_{i}f_{1}}{r_{1}f_{i}},\ldots,\frac{r_{i}f_{n}}{r_{n}f_{i}}\right)\,\,\middle|\,\,1\leq i\leq n\right\}

with
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
generating the unit ideal in
\mathcal{A}
and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

(ii)

A
Laurent covering
of
X
is a covering of the form

\mathfrak{U}=\left\{X\left(r_{1}^{-\varepsilon_{1}}f_{1}^{\varepsilon_{1}},\ldots,r_{n}^{-\varepsilon_{n}}f_{n}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\right)\,\,\middle|\,\,\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\in\{1,-1\}\right\}

with
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
and
r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
.

When
k
is non-trivially valued and
\mathcal{A}
is a strictly
k
-Liu algebra,
\mathfrak{U}
is respectively called a
strictly rational covering
and a
strictly Laurent covering
if we can take
r_{1}=\ldots=r_{n}=1
.

The following lemmata are adaptations of results from
[
BGR84
, §8.2.2]
to this setting, of which one can essentially copy the original proofs. We recall them mainly for self-containedness of our argument.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra,
\mathfrak{U}
a finite covering of

by (strictly) rational domains, and let

be a (strictly) rational domain. Then
\mathfrak{U}|_{X^{\prime}}
is a finite covering of
X^{\prime}
by (strictly) rational domains. If
\mathfrak{U}
is a (strictly) rational or (strictly) Laurent covering,
\mathfrak{U}|_{X^{\prime}}
is a covering of the same type.

This follows from Proposition
3.11
. It is obvious that (strictly) rational (resp. Laurent) coverings induce coverings of the same type.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)

-Liu algebra and let

which refines
\mathfrak{U}
.

Proof.

One can copy the proof of
[
BGR84

.
∎

.

\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)

\mathfrak{U}

of

, the covering

.

, Lemma 8.2.2/3]
.
∎

.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and let
\mathfrak{U}
be a (strictly) rational covering of

, which is generated by units
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
. Then there exists a (strictly) Laurent covering
\mathfrak{B}
of

.

Proof.

One can copy the proof of
[

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)

-Liu algebra and let

be a presheaf on

w.r.t. the weak (strictly) rational

-topology. Assume that (strictly) Laurent coverings are universally
\mathscr{F}
-acyclic on

.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and
\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a finite covering of
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
by (strictly) rational domains
U_{i}\subset X
. Then
\mathfrak{U}
is
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-acyclic w.r.t. the weak (strictly) rational
G
-topology.

Proof.

First assume that
k
is non-trivially valued and
\mathcal{A}
is a strictly
k
-Liu algebra. By Lemma
4.8
, we can assume
\mathfrak{U}
to be a strictly Laurent covering. Moreover, using
[
BGR84
, Corollary 8.1.4/4]
and an induction argument, we can reduce further to the case where
\mathfrak{U}
is generated by a single function
f\in\mathcal{A}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{U}=\{X(f),X(f^{-1})\}
. Thus, we have to show that the augmented Čech complex

0\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{A}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{A}\langle f\rangle\times\mathcal{A}\langle f^{-1}\rangle\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{A}\langle f,f^{-1}\rangle\xrightarrow{}0

is an admissible exact sequence. To see this, one can use Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.5
to copy the proof of
[

, Corollary 2.20]
. The general case now follows from
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.2(ii)]
after applying a suitable base change.
∎

5.
Liu algebras - Liu spaces correspondence

In this section, we prove the correspondence between
k
-Liu algebras and
k
-Liu spaces, and deduce some additional expected properties of the former. We start by showing that the weak (strictly) rational
G
-topology is slightly finer than the ‘classical’
k
-analytic (strict)
G
-topology. For convenience, we recall the definition of slightly finer
G
-topologies.

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
BGR84
, Definition 9.1.2/1]
)
Let
X
be a set admitting
\mathfrak{T}
and
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
as
G
-topologies.
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
is called
slightly finer
than
\mathfrak{T}
, if the following conditions are satisfied.

(i)

\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
is finer than
\mathfrak{T}
.

(ii)

The
\mathfrak{T}
-open subsets of
X
form a basis for
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
.

(iii)

For each
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
-covering
\mathfrak{U}
of a
\mathfrak{T}
-open subset
U\subset X
, there exists a
\mathfrak{T}
-covering which refines
\mathfrak{U}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Using the notation from the definition above,
[
BGR84
, Proposition 9.2.3/1]
implies that (morphisms of) sheaves on
\mathfrak{T}
extend uniquely to (morphisms of) sheaves on
\mathfrak{T}^{\prime}
. One easily verifies using (ii) that this extension procedure does not alter sheaf cohomology.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and denote
X=\mathrm{Spb}(A)
. Let
\mathfrak{T}_{X,aff}
be the
G
-topology on
X
with finite unions of (strictly) affinoid domains as admissible open subsets and finite coverings by finite unions of (strictly) affinoid domains as admissible coverings. Then
\mathfrak{T}_{X,aff}
is slightly finer than
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
.

Proof.

We check the three conditions from the definition above for a general
k
-Liu algebra
\mathcal{A}
. The proof for the strict case is identical. Since a rational domain
U
is also a Liu domain,
U
can be written as a finite union of affinoid rational domains in
U
. By transitivity of rational domains (cf. Proposition
3.11
(iii)), the latter are also affinoid rational domains in
X
. It follows that admissible opens and admissible coverings in
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
are also admissible for
\mathfrak{T}_{X,aff}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{T}_{X,aff}
is finer than
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
. Next, let
U
be an affinoid domain in
X
and let
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}
be a rational atlas of
\mathcal{A}
. By the Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem
[
Tem05
, Theorem 1.1]
, the intersection
U\cap\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
can be written as a finite union of rational domains in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Again by transitivity of rational domains, these are also rational domains in
X
. Hence, the
\mathfrak{T}_{X,rat}
-open subsets of
X
form a basis for
\mathfrak{T}_{X,aff}
. The last condition now also follows easily.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The category of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras is anti-equivalent to the category of (strictly)
k
-Liu spaces. More precisely, taking Berkovich spectra of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras and taking global sections of structure sheaves on (strictly)
k
-Liu spaces induce anti-isomorphisms of categories which are mutually inverse to each other.

Proof.

The proof consists of two steps where we show that the respective functors are well-defined. Using
[
MP21
, Corollary 3.17]
, one then easily verifies that both functors are mutually inverse to each other. For convenience, we only consider the case of general
k
-Liu spaces and algebras. The proof for the strict case is identical.

Step 1: Taking Berkovich spectra.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Liu algebra. We show that
X=(\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}),\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a
k
-Liu space, with
\mathcal{O}_{X}
as defined in Lemma
4.1
. First note that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
has a canonical
k
-analytic structure. Indeed,
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is a non-empty Hausdorff topological space by
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, and its
k
-affinoid domains constitute a
k
-affinoid atlas on
X
. By Lemma
5.3
, the
k
-analytic
G
-topology on
X
is slightly finer than the weak rational
G
-topology. As the
k
-analytic structure sheaf and
\mathcal{O}_{X}
agree on the weak rational
G
-topology, we can assume that
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is the
k
-analytic structure sheaf. By Theorem
4.9
,
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is acyclic and thus universally acyclic when
k
is non-trivially valued by
[
MP21
, Theorem A.5]
. For an analytic extension
\ell/k
when
k
is trivially valued, we can first consider a non-trivially valued analytic extension
k_{r}/k
, in which case
\mathcal{O}_{X_{k_{r}}}
is still acyclic by Lemma
2.6
. As before,
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\ell_{r}}}
is then also acyclic and one can again apply
[
MP21
, Theorem A.5]
to conclude that
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\ell}}
is acyclic. Moreover, for distinct points
x,y\in X
, there exists by definition an
f\in\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
such that
|f(x)|\neq|f(y)|
, so
X
is holomorphically separable. Lastly,
X
is separated and compact by
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.2.1 and Proposition 3.1.5]
. Hence,
X
is a
k
-Liu space. Using Proposition
3.11
(i), it is straightforward to check that a morphism of
k
-Liu algebras defines a unique morphism of
k
-Liu spaces.

Step 2: Taking global sections.
Let
(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
be a
k
-Liu space. We show that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
is a
k
-Liu algebra. By
[
MP21
, Proposition 3.12]
,
X
is a finite union of affinoid rational domains
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. This induces a finite collection of
k
-affinoid rational localisations
\mathcal{A}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{n}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(X_{n})
of
\mathcal{A}
whose Berkovich spectra cover
X\cong\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. The latter homeomorphism is
[
MP21
, Corollary 3.17]
, where
\mathcal{A}
is equipped with a norm making
\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
admissible. Rational injectivity of
\mathcal{A}\to\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
follows directly from
\mathcal{O}_{X}
being a sheaf. It follows from
[
Xia23
, Corollary 3.7]
that a morphism of
k
-Liu spaces yields a morphism of
k
-Liu algebras.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and
I\subset\mathcal{A}
an ideal. Then
\mathcal{A}
is Noetherian,
I
is closed and
\mathcal{A}/I
equipped with its residue norm is again a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra.

Proof.

Noetherianity follows by combining Theorem
5.4
and
[
MP21
, Proposition 2.6(3)]
(see also
[
MP21
, Remark 2.7]
). As a result,
I
is closed by
[
BGR84

6.
Criterion for affinoidness

In this section, we prove a criterion that distinguishes (strictly)
k
-affinoid algebras within the category of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras, thus answering a conjecture by Temkin (cf. Remark
0.12
). Recall the definition of (graded) reduction from Definition
0.7
. For the definition of stable fields, we refer to
[
BGR84
, §3.6]
. The nilradical of a ring
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra. If
\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})
is (strictly)
k
-affinoid, then
\mathcal{A}
is also (strictly)
k
-affinoid.

Proof.

By
[
Tem22
, Lemma 2.4.1]
, it suffices to consider the strict case, so assume that
k
is non-trivially valued and
\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})
is strictly
k
-affinoid. Since
\mathcal{A}
is Noetherian (cf. Corollary
5.5
), we can write the nilradical of
\mathcal{A}
as
\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. By assumption, we have an admissible epimorphism

\varphi:k\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A}).

We claim that
\varphi
can be lifted to an admissible epimorphism

\varphi^{\prime}:k\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\rangle\to\mathcal{A}

sending
Y_{j}
to
a_{j}
for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Indeed, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we can pick
b_{i}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
\varphi(X_{i})=b_{i}+\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})
, and fix
\varphi^{\prime}(X_{i})=b_{i}
. Note that
\varphi^{\prime}
is well-defined by Corollary
3.3
, since
\rho_{\mathcal{A}}(a)=\rho_{\mathcal{A}/\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})}(a+\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A}))
for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
. To see that
\varphi^{\prime}
is surjective, take
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
f\in k\langle X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\rangle
such that
\varphi(f)=a+\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal{A})
. Then there are elements
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
such that

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(f)+c_{1}a_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}a_{n}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\prime}(f)+c_{1}\varphi^{\prime}(Y_{1})+\ldots+c_{n}\varphi^{\prime}(Y_{n}).

One now repeats this procedure inductively to rewrite the
c_{i}
’s until the powers of each
\varphi(Y_{i})
start to vanish. Admissibility of
\varphi^{\prime}
follows from Corollary
5.6
and Banach’s Open Mapping Theorem.
∎

7.
Berkovich spectra of Fréchet algebras

To extend our algebraic description of (strictly)
k
-Liu spaces to general (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces, we must define Berkovich spectra more broadly, namely for
k
-Fréchet algebras rather than
k
-Banach algebras. While this may be well known to experts, no suitable source for this could be found in the literature, so we recall it here for convenience.

Definition 7.1
.

A
k
-Fréchet algebra
is a
k
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
which is also a
k
-Fréchet space, i.e. it is a complete and Hausdorff topological
k
-vector space whose topology is defined by a fixed countable family of
k
-seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, and for which multiplication is jointly continuous. The
category of
k
-Fréchet algebras
consists of
k
-Fréchet algebras and bounded
k
-algebra morphisms between them (cf. Definition
7.3
(i)).

Remark 7.2
.

More concretely, a subset
U
of a
k
-Fréchet space
\mathcal{A}
, of which the topology is defined by a countable family of seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, is open if and only if for every
f\in U
there is a finite subset
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and an
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that

\left\{g\in\mathcal{A}\,\,|\,\,\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{n}<r\text{ for every }n\in N\right\}\subseteq U.

In particular, a sequence
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
in
\mathcal{A}
converges to zero if and only if
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\left\lVert f_{n}\right\rVert_{i}=0
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

In case the family of seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
on a
k
-Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{A}
is increasing, i.e.
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{i}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{j}
for every
f\in\mathcal{A}
whenever
i\leq j
, joint continuity of multiplication is equivalent to there being, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, a constant
C_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and an integer
m\geq n
such that
\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{n}\leq C_{n}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{m}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{m}
for all
f,g\in\mathcal{A}
. When we can take
n=m
, we can assume the seminorms to be submultiplicative by
[
BGR84
, Proposition 1.2.1/2]
. All of this will be the case for the
k
-Fréchet algebras that we will be interested in, namely
k
-Stein algebras (cf. Definition
8.1
).

Definition 7.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A},i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}})
and
(\mathcal{B},\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B},i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}})
be
k
-Fréchet algebras.

(i)

A
k
-algebra morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is called
bounded
if there exists for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
a constant
C_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and a non-empty finite subset
N_{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that we have for all
f\in\mathcal{A}
that

\left\lVert\varphi(f)\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B},n}\leq C_{n}\max_{i\in N_{n}}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A},i}.

(ii)

A seminorm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
on
\mathcal{A}
is called
bounded
if there exists a non-empty finite subset
N\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and a constant
C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for every
f\in\mathcal{A}
we have that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert\leq C\max_{i\in N}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{i}.

Remark 7.4
.

Bounded morphisms and seminorms are also continuous. Indeed, Fréchet spaces have an equivalent description as metric spaces, so continuity is equivalent to sequential continuity, which can be easily verified. Conversely, when
k
is non-trivially valued, continuity of morphisms or seminorms also implies boundedness by an argument similar to that of the analogous statement for
k
-Banach algebras (cf.
[
BGR84
, Proposition 2.1.8/2]
). When
k
is trivially valued, this is not even necessarily the case for
k
-Banach algebras; take for example the identity map
(k[T],\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{1})\to(k[T],\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{2})
where
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{1}=1
and
\left\lVert T\right\rVert_{2}>1
.

Definition 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
k
-Fréchet algebra. The
Berkovich spectrum
of
\mathcal{A}
is the set

\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})\vcentcolon=\left\{\text{bounded multiplicative seminorms }|\cdot(x)|:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\},

equipped with the weakest topology such that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:|\cdot(x)|\mapsto|f(x)|
is continuous for every
f\in\mathcal{A}
.

Remark 7.6
.

As is done in
[
BBBK18
, Definition 4.1]
, one can easily rephrase the definition above in terms of characters, i.e.
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
consists of all equivalence classes of bounded morphisms from
\mathcal{A}
to an analytic extension of
k
. Moreover, a bounded morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
between
k
-Fréchet algebras functorially induces a continuous map

\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi):\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}):|\cdot(x)|\mapsto|\varphi(\cdot)(x)|.

Moreover, just as is the case for
k
-Banach algebras, the requirement for the family of seminorms to be part of the definition of a given
k
-Fréchet algebra, is essential in order to get a well-defined Berkovich spectrum when
k
is trivially valued.

The following proposition is a variation on
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
for
k
-Fréchet algebras.

Proposition 7.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{A},\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}})
be a
k
-Fréchet algebra. Then
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is a non-empty Hausdorff space countable at infinity.

Proof.

The Hausdorff property is shown in the same way as for
k
-Banach algebras in
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
. Furthermore, for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we can define a
k
-Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
as the completion of
\mathcal{A}
w.r.t. the seminorm
\max_{0\leq i\leq n}\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{i}
. We then get continuous mappings
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{n}
with dense image, inducing mutually compatible continuous injections
\iota_{n}:\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{n})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. Since
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{n})
is compact and
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is Hausdorff, each
\iota_{n}
is a homeomorphism onto its image, so that we can write
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
(topologically). It now follows from
[
Ber90
, Theorem 1.2.1]
that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is non-empty and countable at infinity.
∎

8.
Stein algebras - Stein spaces correspondence

In this section, we introduce
k
-Stein algebras as a specific collection of
k
-Fréchet algebras, obtained as suitable inverse limits of
k
-Liu algebras, and show that their Berkovich spectra coincide exactly with
k
-Stein spaces. We also prove some useful properties of
k
-Stein algebras.

Definition 8.1
.

A
(strictly)
k
-Stein algebra
is a
k
-Fréchet algebra
\mathcal{A}
that can be obtained as the inverse limit in the category of
k
-Fréchet algebras of a system of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras
(\mathcal{A}_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
where for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the morphism
\mathcal{A}_{i+1}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
has dense image and defines a Liu domain such that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
is a neighbourhood of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i-1})
in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i+1})
. For such a system of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras, we call
\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
a
presentation
of
\mathcal{A}
. We define the
category of (strictly)
k
-Stein algebras
as the category consisting of (strictly)
k
-Stein algebras with bounded
k
-algebra morphisms between them.

Remark 8.2
.

To obtain an explicit algebraic description of (strictly) Liu domain embeddings, let
\mathcal{A}
be a (strictly)
k
-Liu algebra and recall that (strictly) Liu domains can be written as finite unions of (strictly) affinoid (strictly) rational domains
[
MP21
, Proposition 3.12]
. Hence, a (strictly) Liu domain embedding in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds to a morphism of (strictly)
k
-Liu algebras
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
for which there exists a (strictly) rational atlas
\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{n}
of
\mathcal{B}
where each
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is of the form
\mathcal{B}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}\varphi(f_{\bullet})/\varphi(g)\rangle\cong\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle
. Moreover, it is essential in Definition
8.1
to ask for each
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
to be a neighbourhood of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i-1})
in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i+1})
in order to ensure that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
will be a locally compact topological space. Lastly, when
k
is non-trivially valued,
k
-Stein algebras are examples of
k
-Fréchet-Stein algebras in the sense of
[
ST03
]
by Corollary
5.5
and Proposition
5.9
.

To prove the following correspondence between (strictly)
k
-Stein algebras and (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces, we essentially follow the same arguments as in
[
BBBK18
, §4.2]
.

Theorem 8.3
.

The category of (strictly)
k
-Stein algebras is anti-equivalent to the category of (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces. More precisely, taking Berkovich spectra of (strictly)
k
-Stein algebras and taking global sections of structure sheaves on (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces induce anti-isomorphisms of categories which are mutually inverse to each other.

Proof.

The proof consists of two steps where we show that the respective functors are well-defined. By construction and Theorem
5.4
, it will be clear that both functors are mutually inverse to each other. For convenience, we only consider the case of general
k
-Stein algebras and spaces. The proof for the strict case is identical.

Step 1: Taking Berkovich spectra.
Let
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
be a
k
-Stein algebra. By Proposition
7.7
and its proof,
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is a non-empty Hausdorff topological space for which we can write
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
. By the definition of
k
-Stein algebras, each
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i+1})
is a neighbourhood of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
with compatible restriction maps for their
k
-analytic structure. Hence, one obtains a
k
-analytic structure on
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
by glueing. It follows from the characterisation of
k
-Stein spaces as being
W
-exhausted by Liu domains that
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
is a
k
-Stein space.

Now let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}=\varprojlim_{j}\mathcal{B}_{j}
be a morphism of
k
-Stein algebras, inducing a continuous map
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi):\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. As
\varphi
is bounded, each morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}_{j}
factorises through some
\mathcal{A}_{i}
with
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Hence, possibly after re-indexing the systems in a suitable way, we get compatible morphisms of
k
-Liu spaces
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi_{i}):\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B}_{i})\to\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, which commute with
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)
. Thus,
\mathrm{Spb}(\varphi)
defines a morphism of
k
-Stein spaces. One easily verifies this to be functorial. In particular, it follows that the
k
-analytic structure on
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
does not depend on the chosen presentation of
\mathcal{A}
.

Step 2: Taking global sections.
Next, let
X
be a
k
-Stein space with
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
a
W
-exhaustion of
X
by Liu domains. Possibly after deleting some
D_{i}
’s, we can assume that, for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
D_{i}
is a neighbourhood of
D_{i-1}
within
D_{i+1}
. One easily verifies that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)\cong\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
, endowing it with a clear
k
-Fréchet structure and presentation as
k
-Stein algebra with
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))\cong X
.

Lastly, let
Y=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B})\to X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
be a morphism of
k
-Stein spaces. It remains to show that the induced
k
-algebra morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is bounded. Note that for every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the image of
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{B}_{j})
must be contained in some
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
with
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Possibly after re-indexing the systems in a suitable way, we thus get a system of
k
-algebra morphisms
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{B}_{i}
, which are bounded by Corollary
5.6
(i), with
\varprojlim_{i}\varphi_{i}=\varphi
. Hence,
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is bounded. Given two
W
-exhaustions of
X
, one can apply a similar argument to show that (up to equivalence) the
k
-Fréchet structure on
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
does not depend on the chosen
W
-exhaustion of
X
.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\varphi:\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{B}
be a morphism of strictly
k
-Stein algebras with
\mathcal{B}
strictly
k
-Liu. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{B}
and denote
\mathfrak{m}=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{n})
. Then we have that

(i)

\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}
a finite extension of
k
.

Assume now that
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
defines a strictly Liu domain. Then also the following hold.

(ii)

For every
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
induces an isomorphism
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{n}^{n}
.

(iii)

\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{B}
.

In particular,
\mathfrak{m}
corresponds to a rigid point
x\in X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
for which one has

(iv)

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}
for the adic completions.

Proof.

We can identify
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}
as a subring of
\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{n}
, which is a finite extension of
k
by Lemma
3.5
. Since integral domains which are finite-dimensional
k
-algebras, are fields, it follows that
\mathfrak{m}
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}
is a finite extension of
k
. In case
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
defines a Liu domain, one can now follow the same argument as in
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.2.2/1]
to show that
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\cong\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\mathcal{B}
for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Lastly, applying (ii) to
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
sufficiently large, it follows from
[
Liu90
, Proposition 2.4]
that

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathfrak{m}}\cong\widehat{(\mathcal{A}_{i})}_{\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}}\cong\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{i},x}=\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x},

where
X_{i}=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
.
∎

The following proposition is a non-Archimedean analytic version of
[
For65
, Theorem 2]
.

Proposition 8.5
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a strictly
k
-Stein algebra. Then any maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathcal{A}
is closed if and only if it is finitely generated.

Proof.

Fix a presentation
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
and denote the corresponding projection maps by
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
. For both implications, we will consider for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
the ideal
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}
generated by
\pi_{i}(\mathfrak{m})
in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Then two cases can occur: either there is a
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{j}
is a proper ideal of
\mathcal{A}_{j}
, or
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. We will refer to these cases as
Case (i)
and
Case (ii)
respectively.

Assume that
\mathfrak{m}
is closed. In Case (i), we can assume that
j=0
, and it follows from Lemma
8.4
(ii)-(iii) that
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
with
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}^{n}\cong\mathcal{A}_{i}/(\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i})^{n}
for all
i,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover, since each morphism
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
has dense image, the inclusion
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}/(\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i})^{2}
of finite-dimensional
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}
-vector spaces is an isomorphism for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Hence, lifting a basis for
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
to
\mathcal{A}
yields elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, whose images in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
generate
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}
by Nakayama’s lemma for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. It now follows directly from the obvious non-Archimedean analogue of
[
GR04
, Theorem V.5.4; p.161]
that
\mathfrak{m}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
. In Case (ii), when
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, it follows that
\mathfrak{m}
is dense in
\mathcal{A}
and thus
\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{A}
since
\mathfrak{m}
is closed. This contradicts the assumption of
\mathfrak{m}
being a maximal ideal.

Now assume that
\mathfrak{m}
is finitely generated. To show that
\mathfrak{m}
is closed, we can follow the same strategy as in
[
BBBK18
, Lemma 4.21]
. In Case (i), we get that
\pi_{j}^{-1}(\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{j})
equals
\mathfrak{m}
since it must be a proper ideal of
\mathcal{A}
containing
\mathfrak{m}
. As
\pi_{j}
is bounded (and thus continuous as
k
was assumed to be non-trivially valued), it follows from Corollary
5.5
that
\mathfrak{m}
is closed. In Case (ii), let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
be a set of generators of
\mathfrak{m}
. As
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, the elements
\pi_{i}(f_{1}),\ldots,\pi_{i}(f_{n})
have no common zeroes in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, so
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}
have no common zeroes in
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
. Using the non-Archimedean analytic version of Cartan’s Theorem A
[
MP21
, Proposition 2.1]
, one now verifies in exactly the same way as in
[
GR04
, Theorems V.5.5 and V.5.4; p.161]
that there exist elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
such that
f_{1}g_{1}+\ldots+f_{n}g_{n}=1
and thus
\mathfrak{m}=\mathcal{A}
. This contradicts the assumption of
\mathfrak{m}
being a maximal ideal.
∎

Remark 8.6
.

More generally, for a
k
-Stein algebras
\mathcal{A}
, any finitely generated ideal of
\mathcal{A}
is closed. When
k
is non-trivially valued, this follows from
[
ST03
, Corollary 3.4(iv) and Lemma 3.6(iii)]
. The general case follows by descent via the same argument as in
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.3]
.

Corollary 8.7
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
be a strictly
k
-Stein algebra. Then the collection of rigid points
\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
in the strictly
k
-Stein space
X=\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
corresponds exactly to the collection of closed maximal ideals in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
be a rigid point in
X
, corresponding to a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{n}
in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Since
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
is bounded (and thus continuous as
k
is non-trivially valued), it follows from Corollary
5.5
that
\pi_{i}^{-1}(\mathfrak{n})
is closed. By Lemma
8.4
, it is also maximal. Conversely, let
\mathfrak{m}
be a closed maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}
. Then
\mathfrak{m}\mathcal{A}_{i}
is a maximal ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
sufficiently large by the proof of Proposition
8.5
. Hence,
\mathfrak{m}
corresponds to a rigid point
x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
.
∎

The following corollary is a non-Archimedean analytic version of
[
For65
, Theorem 3]
.

Corollary 8.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be two strictly
k
-Stein algebras. A
k
-algebra morphism
\varphi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
is continuous if and only if for every finitely generated maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
in
\mathcal{B}
the ideal
\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak{m})
is finitely generated in
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}_{i}
is bounded for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Using the Closed Graph Theorem for Fréchet spaces
[
Bou81
, Corollaire I.3.3/5]
, one sees that the proof of the continuity criterion
[
BGR84
, Proposition 3.7.5/1]
also holds for
k
-Fréchet algebras. It now follows from
[
Xia23
, Proposition 3.6]
and Proposition
8.5
that
\varphi
is continuous.
∎

Remark 8.9
.

In the complex analytic setting, one can show that actually any
\mathbb{C}
-algebra morphism between
\mathbb{C}
-Stein algebras (associated with finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-Stein spaces) satisfies the condition from Corollary
8.8

[
For65
, Theorem 5]
. However, in order to emulate the complex analytic proof for a strictly
k
-Stein space
X
with
W
-exhaustion
\{X_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
, one needs to be able to construct a finite collection of global sections
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
such that

\inf_{x\in X\setminus X_{i}}\sup\left\{|g_{1}(x)|,\ldots,|g_{n}(x)|\right\}\geq\alpha_{i}\qquad\text{for every}\quad i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

for a certain sequence
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
. To do this in the complex analytic setting, one makes explicit use of the underlying real analytic structure of
X
(cf. the proof of
[
Nar60
, Theorem 1]
). The natural question now arises whether an alternative proof is possible in the non-Archimedean setting.

9.
Runge immersions

In this section, we define Runge immersions into
k
-Stein spaces, and show that the analogues of several classical results extend to this setting. The latter will serve as auxiliary results when proving a Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem for
k
-Stein spaces in Section

Remark 9.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
be a (strictly)
k
-Stein algebra. Then (strictly) Weierstrass, Laurent and rational domains of

are defined just as for Berkovich spectra of

-Banach algebras in Definition

[

, (strictly) rational domains of

are also (strictly)

-Stein spaces. Furthermore, one easily verifies that these are

X\left(r_{\bullet}^{-1}\frac{f_{\bullet}}{g}\right)=\left\{x\in\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})\,\,\middle|\,\,|f_{j}(x)|\leq r_{j}|g(x)|\text{ for }j=1,\ldots,n\right\}

\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle\vcentcolon=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}/g\rangle

for every

.

Proposition 9.4

Lemma 9.5

k
-Stein algebras with
\mathcal{B}
(strictly)
k
-affinoid and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathcal{B}
a (strictly) affinoid generating system of
\mathcal{B}
over
\mathcal{A}
with associated polyradius
r_{\bullet}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
. Then there exists an
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that the following holds: if
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}
is a system of elements in
\mathcal{B}
satisfying
\left\lVert b_{i}-d_{i}\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}<\varepsilon
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then
d_{1},\ldots,d_{n}
is a (strictly) affinoid generating system of
\mathcal{B}
over
\mathcal{A}
with associated polyradius
r_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

By the obvious generalisation of
[
Ber90
, Proposition 2.1.2]
for
k
-Fréchet algebras, we may assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Fix a presentation
\mathcal{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
and let
\sigma^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}\langle r_{\bullet}^{-1}\zeta_{\bullet}\rangle\to\mathcal{B}
be the surjective morphism extending
\sigma
by mapping
\zeta_{j}
to
b_{j}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Denote by
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A},i}
the seminorm on
\mathcal{A}
induced by
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
via pullback for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. By Banach’s Open Mapping Theorem, the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}
and the collection of residue seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B},i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
on
\mathcal{B}
corresponding to
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{A},i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
via
\sigma^{\prime}
, induce equivalent
k
-Fréchet structures on
\mathcal{B}
. Since the family of seminorms
\{\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B},i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
is increasing on
\mathcal{B}
, we thus get for sufficiently large
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B},i}
is a norm on
\mathcal{B}
equivalent to
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}
. Now one can give the same argument as in
[
BGR84
, Lemma 7.3.4/3]
.
∎

Proposition 9.6
.

Let
D
be a (strictly) Liu domain in a (strictly)
k
-Stein space
X
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
has dense image in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
. Any (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
D
, contained in the interior of
D
, can be written as a finite union of connected components of a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
X
.

Proof.

By the same argument as in the beginning of the proof of Proposition
5.7
, it follows from the assumption that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
has dense image in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D)
that we can take
g^{\prime}
and
f_{\bullet}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X}(X)
such that
R=D(r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}^{\prime}/g^{\prime})
. Since
R
is compact and contained in the interior of
D
, it is a finite union of connected components of
X(r_{\bullet}^{-1}f_{\bullet}^{\prime}/g^{\prime})
.
∎

Corollary 9.7
.

Let
X
be a (strictly)
k
-Stein space. Let
U\subset X
be a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain and
V\subset U
a compact (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain. Then
V
is a finite union of connected components of a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
X
. In case
U
is also compact,
V
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
X
.

Proof.

When
U
is compact, one can copy the argument from
[
BGR84
, Theorem 7.2.4/2]
. For the general case, let
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be a
W
-exhaustion of
X
by (strictly) Liu domains. As
V
is compact, it is contained in the interior of
D_{i}
for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. It now follows from the compact case that
V
is a (strictly) rational (resp. Weierstrass) domain of
D
. The claim now follows directly from Proposition
9.6
.
∎

Proposition 9.8
.

Let
\varphi:X\to Y
be a
G
-open and closed immersion of (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces with
X
(strictly)
k
-affinoid. Then
X
is a finite union of connected components of a (strictly) Weierstrass domain in
Y
via
\varphi
.

Proof.

In case the proposition holds in the strict case, one can follow Ducros’s argument for drawing the Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem for affinoid spaces from the strictly affinoid case
[
Duc03
, Lemme 2.4]
, to derive the general case by base change descent. Hence, assume that
k
is non-trivially valued with
X
and
Y
strictly
k
-Stein spaces. When
Y
is compact, one can use Corollary
5.5
and
[
Liu90
, Proposition 2.4]
to give the same argument as in
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.3.3/7]
and see that
X
is a strictly Weierstrass domain in
Y
. When
Y
is not compact, let
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be a
W
-exhaustion of
Y
by strictly Liu domains. Since
X
is compact, it is contained in the interior of
D_{i}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
sufficiently large, and thus is a strictly Weierstrass domain in
D_{i}
. One concludes by applying Proposition
9.6
.
∎

Corollary 9.9
.

Let
\varphi:X\hookrightarrow Y
be a (strictly)
k
-affinoid domain embedding into a (strictly)

-Stein space which is also a (strictly) Runge immersion. Then

is a finite union of connected components of a (strictly) Weierstrass domain in

via

.

Proof.

Using Proposition
9.8
and Corollary
9.7
, this can be verified in the same way as in
[
BGR84
, Proposition 7.3.4/6]
.
∎

10.
Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem for Stein spaces

In this section, we prove a generalisation of the Gerritzen-Grauert Theorem to
k
-Stein spaces. We start by extending the description of monomorphisms of strictly
k
-affinoid spaces as locally closed immersions to the strictly
k
-Stein setting.

Proposition 10.1
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. A morphism
\varphi:Y\to X
of strictly
k
-Stein spaces is a locally closed immersion if and only if it is a monomorphism in the category of strictly
k
-Stein spaces.

Proof.

One can give the same argument as is done for the strictly affine case in
[
Tem05
, Proposition 1.2]
. In particular, for the converse implication, as being a locally closed immersion is a local property, one may assume that
Y
is strictly
k
-affinoid, so that one can use the lemma below.
∎

Lemma 10.2
.

Assume that
k
is non-trivially valued. Let
\sigma:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a morphism of strictly
k
-Stein algebras with
\mathcal{B}
strictly
k
-affinoid and let
x\in\mathrm{Spr}(\mathcal{A})
. Assume that
\sigma
induces

(i)

a finite morphism
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\to\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\mathcal{B}
, or

(ii)

an epimorphism
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\to\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}\mathcal{B}
, or

(iii)

isomorphisms
\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}\to\mathcal{B}/\mathfrak{m}_{x}^{n}\mathcal{B}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
.

Then there exists a strictly affinoid domain
\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})\subset\mathrm{Spb}(\mathcal{A})
containing
x
such that the map
\sigma^{\prime}:\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\widehat{\otimes}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}
induced by
\sigma
is finite, epimorphic, or isomorphic, respectively.

Proof.

By Lemma
8.4
(ii), we may assume that
\mathcal{A}
is also strictly
k
-affinoid, in which case this is just
[
BGR84
, Lemma 7.2.5/2]
.
∎

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
\varphi:Y\to X
be a monomorphism of (strictly)
k
-Stein spaces with
Y
(strictly)
k
-affinoid. Then there exists an admissible covering
\{R_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of
X
by (strictly) rational domains such that
\varphi
induces (strictly) Runge immersions
\varphi_{i}:\varphi^{-1}(R_{i})\to R_{i}
.

Proof.

For convenience, we only consider the general case. The proof for the strict case is identical. First suppose that
X
is
k
-Liu and let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be
k
-affinoid rational domains covering
X
. By
[
Tem05
, Theorem 3.1]
, each
X_{i}
can be covered by rational domains
R_{i1},\ldots,R_{in_{i}}
such that each
\varphi_{ij}:\varphi^{-1}(R_{ij})\to R_{ij}
is a Runge immersion. By Corollary
9.7
, each
R_{ij}
is also a rational domain of
X
.

Now suppose that
X
is a general
k
-Stein space and let
\{D_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
be a
W
-exhaustion of
X
by Liu domains. Take
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
sufficiently large such that the compact set
\varphi(Y)
is contained in the interior of
D_{j}
. By the case above, there exists a covering
\{R_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,R_{n}^{\prime}\}
of
D_{j}
by rational domains such that
\varphi^{-1}(R_{i}^{\prime})\to R_{i}^{\prime}
is a Runge immersion for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. By Proposition
9.6
, there exists a collection of rational domains
\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
of
X
such that
R_{i}^{\prime}
is a finite union of connected components of
R_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. In particular, each
R_{i}^{\prime}
is a Weierstrass domain of
R_{i}
, since we can take a section
h\in\mathcal{O}_{X}(R_{i})
such that
h=0
on
R_{i}
and
h=1
on
R_{i}^{\prime}
. Hence,
\varphi_{i}:\varphi^{-1}(R_{i})\to R_{i}
is a Runge immersion by Proposition
9.4
(ii). Lastly, since
\varphi(Y)
is compact, we can enlarge
\{R_{1},\ldots,R_{n}\}
to a covering of
X
by repeating the process above outside of
\varphi(Y)
, i.e. for every
x\in X\setminus\varphi(Y)
we can consider an affinoid neighbourhood
U
contained in the interior of
D_{\ell}\setminus\varphi(Y)
for some integer
\ell\geq j
and write
U
as the intersection of rational domains of
X
with
D_{\ell}
. As the inverse image of such a latter rational domain
R
under
\varphi
is empty, the induced morphism
\varphi^{-1}(R)=\varnothing\to R
is obviously a Runge immersion.
∎

Corollary 10.4
.

Let
X
be a (strictly)
k
-Stein space. Then every (strictly) Liu domain in
X
is a finite union of (strictly)
k
-affinoid connected components of (strictly) rational domains of
X
.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a (strictly) Liu domain in
X
. By considering a finite covering of
Y
by (strictly) affinoid domains, the claim follows directly from Theorem
10.3
, Corollary
9.9
and Corollary
9.7
. Alternatively, one can also repeat the argument of the proof of Theorem
10.3
, using
[
MP21
, Proposition 3.12]
instead of
[
Tem05
, Theorem 3.1]
.
∎

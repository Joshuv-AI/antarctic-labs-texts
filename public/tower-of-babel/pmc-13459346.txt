Circ17399 RNA promotes melanoma progression through the miR-150-3p/ITM2C Axis and ALKBH5-mediated FOXM1 m6A modification
Authors: Ronghua Yang, Xiaoxiang Wang, Jianan Zhuo, Lingjie Tang, Deni Kang, Sirong Liu, Jiehua Li, Sitong Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSkin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) is a highly aggressive malignancy with a poor prognosis, necessitating the exploration of novel molecular mechanisms driving its progression. CircRNA, which have emerged as critical regulators in cancer biology, have been implicated in various tumorigenic processes. However, their specific roles in SKCM remain inadequately understood.MethodsBioinformatics analyses of TCGA and GEO datasets identified circ17399 as a candidate oncogenic circRNA. Functional validation was performed using in vitro (A375, A2058 cells) and in vivo models. Techniques included qRT-PCR, dual-luciferase reporter assays, RNA FISH, Western blot, Transwell assays, and MeRIP-qPCR. Circ17399 knockdown/overexpression, miR-150-3p modulation, and ALKBH5/FOXM1 interaction studies were conducted to dissect its regulatory network.ResultsCirc17399 was significantly upregulated in SKCM tissues and correlated with poor prognosis. Mechanistically, circ17399 sponged miR-150-3p to derepress ITM2C, enhancing SKCM cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Concurrently, circ17399 bound ALKBH5, reducing m6A methylation on FOXM1 mRNA, thereby stabilizing FOXM1 and promoting tumor progression. In vivo, circ17399 knockdown suppressed tumor growth and metastasis, while overexpression exacerbated malignancy.ConclusionsCirc17399 promotes melanoma progression by competitively binding miR-150-3p to upregulate ITM2C and recruiting ALKBH5 to reduce m6A methylation of FOXM1, enhancing its stability and oncogenic function. These findings unveil a dual-axis regulatory mechanism in SKCM pathogenesis and position circ17399 as a promising diagnostic biomarker and actionable therapeutic target for melanoma intervention.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12967-026-08598-w.

Introduction

Melanoma is an aggressive skin cancer that arises from melanocytes [1], owing to its rapid growth, propensity for distant metastasis, and significant resistance to conventional therapies, melanoma is associated with a poor clinical prognosis. Although patient survival rates have improved in recent years, the overall cure rate remains low [2, 3]. Despite significant progress in immunotherapy and targeted treatments, such as immune checkpoint inhibitors and BRAF/MEK inhibitors, melanoma patients still face poor prognoses [4, 5]. As a result, understanding the molecular mechanisms driving melanoma progression and identifying new therapeutic targets remain essential challenges in both clinical and basic research.

Circular RNAs (circRNAs) have received significant attention from many scholars because of their stable circular structure and tissue-specific expression patterns [6]. CircRNAs are covalently closed RNA molecules formed through noncanonical backsplicing and are characterized by high stability and strong tissue specificity [7]. These molecules play crucial roles in cancer, particularly as microRNA (miRNA) sponges, which competitively bind to miRNAs and regulate the expression of their target genes [8]. CircRNAs are involved in key processes of tumorigenesis and progression, including cell proliferation, migration, invasion, metabolic regulation, and antiapoptotic mechanisms [9–11]. However, their specific roles and mechanisms in skin cutaneous melanoma (SKCM) remain to be fully elucidated.

Emerging evidence suggests that specific circRNAs are differentially expressed in melanoma and play significant roles in tumorigenesis and progression by interacting with key molecular pathways [12, 13]. For example, circRNA_0084043 promotes melanoma progression through the miR-153-3p/Snail axis, underscoring the potential oncogenic role of circRNAs [14]. Similarly, circ_0062270 enhances cell proliferation and metastasis by adjusting the miR-331-3p/EPHA2 axis, further supporting the crucial regulatory function of circRNAs in cancer [15]. This work provides valuable insight into the involvement of circRNAs in melanoma. However, most studies have focused primarily on individual circRNAs in vitro, with limited investigations into the underlying mechanisms and in vivo validation.

The present study focused on the function and mechanisms of the circular RNA circ17399, a novel transcript identified through comprehensive bioinformatics analysis of melanoma datasets. Our findings revealed that circ17399 is obviously upregulated in melanoma tissues and is related to poor prognosis. Importantly, we demonstrated that circ17399 acts as a competing endogenouse RNA (ceRNA), sequestering specific miRNAs to regulate tumor-associated genes. Furthermore, we provide evidence that circ17399 interacts with the demethylase ALKBH5, thereby modulating N6-methyladenosine (m6A) methylation levels and influencing the stability and expression of critical tumor-associated molecules.

Materials and methods

Animal models and interventions

Nude mice were all housed under pathogen-free conditions. All animal care and experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Foshan First People’s Hospital, and the study is compliant with all relevant ethical regulations regarding animal research. The mice were euthanized when they met the institutional euthanasia criteria for tumor size and overall health condition.

For the subcutaneous implantation model, 5 four-week-old female nude mice were injected with 5 × 106 A375 cells in 100 µl of mixed medium (50 µl of serum-free DMEM and 50 µl of Matrigel) in the right flank of the back. Tumors were measured with a caliper every 3 days to analyze tumor growth. The tumor volume was calculated via the following formula: V = (width2 × length)/2. At the experimental endpoint, the tumor tissues were harvested, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde (PFA), and paraffin-embedded for sectioning.

For the experimental pulmonary metastasis model using tail-vein injection, 5 four-week-old female mice were randomly grouped and injected with 1 × 106 A375 cells in 100 µl of PBS via the tail vein. After treatment with 2% isoflurane for anesthesia, a D-luciferin sodium salt stock solution (150 mg/ml) was injected into the abdominal cavity of the mice. The anesthetized mice were subsequently placed on the IVIS SpectrumD Xenogen Imaging System (Caliper Life Sciences, Hopkinton, MA, USA) for bioluminescent signal measurement. The mice were sacrificed 4 weeks after injection to detect lung metastasis. Lung tissues were harvested and fixed with 4% PFA for paraffin-embedded sections, and lung metastasis was detected via microscopy. Comprehensive experimental protocols for both in vivo and ex vivo assays are detailed in the Supplementary materials and methods.

Statistical analysis

All the experiments were carried out in triplicate. Student’s t-test was used to analyze significant differences between the two groups, and analysis of variance (ANOVA) was used to evaluate the differences among multiple groups. All the statistical analyses were performed with SPSS software, version 26.0 (IBM, SPSS, Chicago, IL, USA), and GraphPad Prism, version 10.0 (GraphPad Software, La Jolla, CA, USA). P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Identification of circ17399 as a key regulatory factor in SKCM

We conducted a ceRNA array analysis via the TCGA database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov) for melanoma and adjacent normal tissues. Additionally, we retrieved data from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo), selecting GSE138412 as a candidate dataset [16]. A total of 868 upregulated circRNAs and 1206 downregulated circRNAs were identified (Fig. 1A). Using a Venn diagram, 61 differentially expressed circRNAs were selected from the upregulated set, and 75 were selected from the downregulated set (Fig. 1B).

Among the upregulated circRNAs, the top eight were further analyzed by qRT-PCR, with circ17399 showing the highest expression levels and the most significant differences in melanoma cells compared with normal melanocytes (Fig. 1C). This finding was further validated through qRT-PCR analysis of melanoma and normal tissue samples (Fig. 1D). Recent evidence suggests that machine learning methods can enhance genomic pattern recognition and drug discovery in cancer, supporting their potential utility for validating RNA-based prognostic biomarkers, such as circ17399, in future clinical cohorts [17]. Accordingly, we performed a multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analysis. The results demonstrated that high circ17399 expression was significantly associated with poor overall survival in SKCM patients (HR = 1.33, 95% CI: 1.13–1.55, P < 0.001); Fig. 1E). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis further confirmed that patients with high circ17399 expression had poorer overall survival than those with low circ17399 expression (Fig. 1F). These findings suggest that circ17399 may serve as a potential prognostic biomarker for melanoma.

R software (version 3.6.4) helped calculate differential expression between normal and tumor samples for each cancer type, with significance assessed by the Wilcoxon rank sum test and signed rank test. Circ17399 was significantly elevated in 12 tumor types, including SKCM (Fig. 1G). Sanger sequencing confirmed the junction site of circ17399, confirming its back-splicing sequence (Fig. 1L). Furthermore, resistance to RNase R exonuclease digestion verified that circ17399 is a stable, closed circular RNA that is more resistant than its linear counterpart, lin17399 (Fig. 1H). As shown in Fig. 1I-K, circ17399 localized to the cytoplasm, confirming its status as a cytoplasmic circRNA.

circ17399 promotes proliferation, migration, invasion, and EMT in SKCM cells

siRNA#1, siRNA#2, and siRNA#3 were designed to knock down circ17399, and stable circ17399-knockdown SKCM cell lines were constructed. As shown in Fig. 2A, the knockdown efficiency of circ17399 was confirmed via qRT-PCR. circ17399 knockdown clearly reduced the proliferation and colony-forming ability of A375 and A2058 cells, whereas circ17399 overexpression markedly enhanced melanoma cell proliferation (Fig. 2B-D and Supplementary Fig. 1A-C). Transwell assays revealed that silencing circ17399 significantly inhibited SKCM cell migration and invasion, whereas its overexpression enhanced these abilities (Fig. 2E and F and Supplementary Fig. 1C-1D).

circ17399 functions as a miR-150-3p sponge in SKCM cells

Cytoplasmic circRNAs can bind specific miRNAs through their unique sequences, thereby competitively interacting with miRNA targets and reducing their inhibitory effects on target gene expression [18]. Bioinformatic interrogation of circ17399-interacting miRNAs was conducted through complementary prediction algorithms, namely, the miRanda and TargetScan databases. Initial screening predicted 1,363 candidate miRNAs via TargetScan, with 333 candidates overlapping in the miRanda predictions. Intersection analysis revealed 184 miRNAs whose expression was inversely correlated with that of circ17399 (Fig. 3A). Subsequent cross-database validation (TCGA/GEO) demonstrated significant negative regulation between circ17399 and miR-150-3p (Fig. 3B). Prognostically, elevated miR-150-3p expression correlated with improved survival outcomes in SKCM cohorts (Fig. 3C). Notably, miR-150-3p was markedly upregulated in melanoma tissues compared with normal controls (Fig. 3D). Previous studies have suggested that miR-150-3p functions as a tumor-suppressive miRNA in various cancers by regulating target gene expression [19, 20], but its role in melanoma remains poorly understood.

qRT-PCR analysis revealed lower expression of miR-150-3p in A375, A2058, and WM3211 melanoma cells than in normal controls (Fig. 3E). Knockdown of circ17399 in these cells resulted in increased miR-150-3p expression. The overexpression level of miR-150-3p was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig. 3F), and CCK-8 assays showed that miR-150-3p overexpression significantly suppressed melanoma cell viability (Fig. 3I). To confirm the direct binding of miR-150-3p to circ17399, dual-luciferase reporter assays were carried out. Cotransduction with WT-circ17399 significantly reduced luciferase activity, whereas cotransfection with MUT-circ17399 had no such effect (Fig. 3G and H). This study mechanistically demonstrated that circ17399 functions as a ceRNA in SKCM by competitively sequestering miR-150-3p. A functional investigation of miR-150-3p in A375/A2058 melanoma models revealed its tumor-suppressive role: genetic silencing enhanced oncogenic phenotypes, including proliferative capacity, migratory potential, and invasive propensity, whereas ectopic overexpression reciprocally attenuated these malignant behaviors (Fig. 3J-L, Supplementary Fig. 2A-C).

miR-150-3p suppresses SKCM progression by targeting ITM2C

To further explore the downstream mechanism of miR-150-3p in SKCM, ITM2C was selected as a candidate target based on miRNA target prediction. qRT-PCR analysis showed that miR-150-3p overexpression significantly decreased ITM2C mRNA expression in A375 cells (Fig. 4A). To determine the functional role of ITM2C, specific siRNAs targeting ITM2C were transfected into SKCM cells, and the knockdown efficiency was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4B). CCK-8 assays showed that ITM2C silencing markedly reduced A375 cell viability (Fig. 4C). Consistently, Transwell assays further showed that ITM2C knockdown significantly attenuated the migratory and invasive abilities of both A375 and A2058 cells, supporting the pro-metastatic role of ITM2C in SKCM cells (Supplementary Fig. 3A, B).

To verify whether ITM2C is directly regulated by miR-150-3p, dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed. miR-150-3p significantly reduced the luciferase activity of the ITM2C 3′UTR reporter, whereas mutation of the predicted binding site abolished this effect (Fig. 4D, E). Moreover, circ17399 knockdown decreased ITM2C protein expression (Fig. 4F). The efficiency of miR-150-3p inhibition was confirmed by qRT-PCR (Fig. 4G). Rescue experiments further showed that inhibition of miR-150-3p partially reversed the suppressive effects of circ17399 knockdown on A375 cell viability (Fig. 4H, I) and restored ITM2C protein expression (Fig. 4J). Consistently, Transwell assays demonstrated that miR-150-3p inhibition rescued the reduced migratory and invasive abilities caused by circ17399 knockdown in both A375 and A2058 cells (Fig. 4K, L). Together, these results suggest that circ17399 promotes SKCM progression, at least in part, through the miR-150-3p/ITM2C regulatory axis.

Validation of the binding region between circ17399 and ALKBH5

While investigating why circ17399 plasmids with mutated miR-150-3p binding sites retain partial oncogenic function, we hypothesized that interactions with RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) might be involved. RBPs are known to regulate downstream gene expression through interactions with circular RNAs. To validate this hypothesis, RNA pulldown assays were conducted in A375 cells, followed by mass spectrometry analysis (Fig. 5A). The results revealed 649 specific proteins, among which ALKBH5, an RNA demethylase, attracted our attention. Previous studies have shown that ALKBH5 promotes the malignant phenotype of melanoma cells by reducing m6A modifications on key mRNAs, thereby increasing their translation efficiency or stability [21, 22].

We conducted a correlation analysis via a scatter plot, which revealed a positive correlation between ALKBH5 and circ17399, suggesting a potential synergistic role in SKCM development (Fig. 5B). qRT-PCR confirmed successful overexpression of circ17399 in SKCM cells (Fig. 5C), and efficient knockdown of ALKBH5 by siRNA was also validated by qRT-PCR (Fig. 5D). CCK-8 assays further showed that ALKBH5 knockdown partially reversed the circ17399 overexpression-induced increase in A375 cell growth (Fig. 5E).

Furthermore, RNA pulldown and RIP-qPCR assays confirmed the direct interaction between circ17399 and ALKBH5 (Fig. 5F and G). Using catRAPID (http://service.tartaglialab.com), we predicted the motif within circ17399 that mediates its interaction with ALKBH5. The interaction map and matrix revealed that the RNA region of circ17399 was responsible for binding to ALKBH5. A series of truncated circ17399 constructs were generated, and RNA pulldown experiments were conducted to assess ALKBH5 binding in melanoma cells. These experiments identified the 1700–3399 bp region of circ17399 as the primary functional domain mediating its interaction with ALKBH5 (Fig. 5H). Finally, to further delineate the binding region of ALKBH5, we used UniProt to predict its domains and constructed flag-tagged truncated ALKBH5 plasmids with or without the predicted interaction region. RNA pulldown experiments indicated that circ17399 primarily interacts with ALKBH5 in the 301AA-394AA region (Fig. 5I).

circ17399 reduces m6A modification of FOXM1 by interacting with ALKBH5

Previous studies have shown that ALKBH5 demethylates nascent transcripts of FOXM1, promoting proliferation and invasion in glioblastoma [23]. Additionally, ALKBH5 induces m6A demethylation of FOXM1 mRNA, increasing its expression and stability and thereby driving the progression of uveal melanoma [24]. Based on these findings, we focused on investigating whether circ17399 facilitates ALKBH5 binding to FOXM1 mRNA in SKCM, thereby increasing its stability.

Knockdown of circ17399 and ALKBH5 was performed to examine their effects on FOXM1 expression. qRT-PCR confirmed the knockdown efficiency of circ17399 in SKCM cells (Fig. 6A). FOXM1 expression was then examined after circ17399 knockdown (Fig. 6B) and after ALKBH5 knockdown (Fig. 6C), supporting the involvement of circ17399 and ALKBH5 in the regulation of FOXM1 expression. To further verify the role of FOXM1, we knocked down FOXM1 in SKCM cells, and qRT-PCR confirmed efficient FOXM1 knockdown (Fig. 6D). CCK-8 assays revealed that overexpression of circ17399 promoted SKCM cell proliferation, whereas FOXM1 knockdown partially reversed the oncogenic effects of circ17399 (Fig. 6E). To examine the effect of circ17399 on transcript stability, we simultaneously overexpressed circ17399 and knocked down ALKBH5. After 48 h, ActD was added to inhibit transcription and transcript stability was assessed. The results indicated that ALKBH5 knockdown reversed the increased FOXM1 transcript stability induced by circ17399 overexpression (Fig. 6F).

Based on the RNA pulldown and RIP-qPCR results showing the direct interaction between circ17399 and ALKBH5 (Fig. 5F, G), we next investigated whether circ17399 affects ALKBH5-mediated m6A modification of FOXM1. Overexpression and knockdown of circ17399 in SKCM cells followed by MeRIP-qPCR revealed that FOXM1 m6A modification levels were increased after circ17399 knockdown and decreased after circ17399 overexpression (Fig. 6G, H). To complement the MeRIP-qPCR results and provide a more detailed epitranscriptomic map of FOXM1, we performed sequence-based prediction of potential m6A modification sites in the FOXM1 transcript using deepSRAMP (http://www.cuilab.cn/deepsramp/). Multiple high-confidence candidate m6A sites were identified across the FOXM1 transcript (Supplementary Fig. 4A). Notably, several predicted sites contained canonical DRACH consensus motifs, including AGACT, GGACT, GGACA, and TGACA, and a subset of high-confidence candidate sites was located within the 3′UTR region of FOXM1 (Supplementary Fig. 4B). These findings are consistent with the FOXM1 m6A modification changes detected by MeRIP-qPCR and suggest that the 3′UTR of FOXM1 may represent an important potential region involved in ALKBH5-mediated demethylation. RNA pulldown assays revealed reduced ALKBH5 enrichment in circ17399-knockdown samples, whereas overexpression of circ17399 increased ALKBH5 enrichment (Fig. 6I and J). RIP-qPCR revealed similar changes in FOXM1 enrichment (Fig. 6K and L). These results collectively indicate that circ17399 facilitates the interaction between ALKBH5 and FOXM1, thereby influencing FOXM1 m6A modification and promoting SKCM progression.

circ17399 influences SKCM cell biological functions by targeting FOXM1

To further elucidate whether the oncogenic role of circ17399 in SKCM cells is mediated through FOXM1 regulation, rescue experiments were performed. These functional analyses demonstrated that circ17399 overexpression significantly enhanced cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in A375 (Fig. 7A, E, I, J) and A2058 (Fig. 7C, F, K, L) melanoma cells. Notably, FOXM1 knockout effectively abolished these oncogenic effects, indicating that FOXM1 is a key mediator of circ17399-driven tumorigenic behaviors. Conversely, circ17399 knockdown in A375 (Fig. 7B, G, M, N) and A2058 (Fig. 7D, H, O, P) cells led to suppressed cell proliferation, migration, and invasion, consistent with a loss of circ17399’s oncogenic function. However, these inhibitory effects were reversed upon FOXM1 overexpression, further confirming the pivotal role of FOXM1 in circ17399-promoted melanoma progression.

Subsequently, to determine whether the miR-150-3p/ITM2C axis and the ALKBH5/FOXM1 pathway function cooperatively downstream of circ17399, we performed additional rescue experiments in circ17399-knockdown SKCM cells. Specifically, miR-150-3p inhibition and FOXM1 knockdown were introduced either individually or in combination, and cell migration and invasion were assessed by Transwell assays. The results showed that the combined treatment produced a more pronounced inhibitory effect on cell migration and invasion than either single treatment, supporting a cooperative contribution of these two downstream pathways to circ17399-mediated melanoma progression (Supplementary Fig. 5). Furthermore, the same combinatorial strategy was evaluated in circ17399-overexpressing cells. The results showed that transfection with miR-150-3p mimics or FOXM1 silencing attenuated the circ17399-induced enhancement of migratory and invasive capacities, while simultaneously targeting both pathways produced the most significant inhibitory effect (Supplementary Fig. 6). In summary, these results provide functional evidence that circ17399-driven malignant phenotypes in SKCM are mediated through dual, interconnected regulatory mechanisms, and that concurrent modulation of the miR-150-3p/ITM2C and ALKBH5/FOXM1 pathways yields a stronger phenotypic impact than targeting either pathway alone, supporting a synergistic mode of action.

In vivo evaluation of circ17399 in SKCM progression

To further validate the regulatory role of circ17399 in SKCM progression in vivo, A375 cells with stable circ17399 knockdown or overexpression were used to establish subcutaneous xenograft tumor growth and tail-vein experimental pulmonary metastasis models in nude mice. As shown in Fig. 8A and F, circ17399 knockdown significantly reduced both the tumor size and weight compared to the control group. The tumor growth curves demonstrate a clear difference between the groups, where the circ17399-overexpressing tumors grew much faster, indicating the oncogenic potential of circ17399 in SKCM progression.

Moreover, the effects of circ17399 on lung metastasis were evaluated using the IVIS imaging system (Fig. 8G) and histological examination of metastatic lesions (Fig. 8H and K). As expected, circ17399 knockdown notably suppressed lung metastasis, while circ17399 overexpression promoted increased metastatic burden, with more and larger metastatic foci observed in the lung tissue of the overexpressing group. Ki67 immunohistochemical staining revealed a significant reduction in the proliferation index in the circ17399-knockdown group, whereas it was elevated in the circ17399-overexpressing group (Fig. 8L and M). These results collectively suggest that circ17399 plays a crucial role in both primary tumor growth and metastatic dissemination.

In addition to tumor growth and metastasis, qRT-PCR analysis was performed to examine the expression levels of circ17399, miR-150-3p, ITM2C, ALKBH5, and FOXM1 in tumor tissues. The results showed that circ17399 knockdown in the sh-circ17399 group led to a significant increase in miR-150-3p expression, accompanied by corresponding decreases in ITM2C and FOXM1 mRNA levels, confirming the involvement of circ17399 in regulating the miR-150-3p/ITM2C axis and the ALKBH5/FOXM1 pathway. In contrast, circ17399 overexpression in the circ17399‑oe group resulted in a marked reduction in miR-150-3p levels and elevated expression of ITM2C and FOXM1, consistent with the observations made ex vivo (Fig. 8N‑R). Notably, ALKBH5 expression remained relatively stable in both the knockdown and overexpression groups, supporting the hypothesis that circ17399 primarily influences FOXM1 m6A modification through its interaction with ALKBH5, rather than by directly altering ALKBH5 mRNA expression.

These findings further corroborate the regulatory mechanisms proposed in our previous in vitro studies, where circ17399 was shown to function as a ceRNA, sponging miR-150-3p to derepress ITM2C and facilitating the m6A demethylation of FOXM1 via ALKBH5, ultimately promoting tumor progression and metastasis in SKCM.

Discussion

Because of their unique covalent closed-loop structure and high stability, circRNAs have received significant attention in the field of tumor biology research. Numerous studies suggest that circRNAs, which act as ceRNAs or interact with RBPs, play pivotal roles in cancer progression. Nevertheless, the specific functions and mechanisms of circRNAs in SKCM remain highly elusive. Here, through mining the TCGA and GEO databases followed by large-scale qRT-PCR analysis, circ17399 was identified as a potential oncogenic circRNA in SKCM. Mechanistically, circ17399 competitively binds to miR-150-3p, effectively inhibiting it and reducing its suppressive effect on the target gene ITM2C, leading to a significant upregulation of ITM2C expression, then promoting tumor cell proliferation, migration, and invasion. Additionally, circ17399 interacts with the RNA demethylase ALKBH5, reducing m6A modification of FOXM1 mRNA and thereby enhancing its stability and expression. These findings not only elucidate a previously unrecognized circRNA-mediated mechanism of melanoma progression but also position circ17399 as a promising diagnostic biomarker and therapeutic target (Fig. 9).

The evolution of high-throughput sequencing and bioinformatic analytics has yielded unprecedented insights into the regulatory centrality of circRNAs in oncogenesis and malignant progression [25]. Across diverse malignancies, aberrant circRNA expression profiles demonstrate tumor type-specific dysregulation, intricately linked to pathological processes spanning proliferative amplification, metastatic dissemination, and therapeutic resistance [26, 27]. For example, in breast carcinogenesis, circCCDC85A serves as a molecular sponge for miR-550a-5p [28], antagonizing miRNA-mediated suppression of the tumor suppressor MOB1A through competitive binding at conserved seed regions (nt 1287–1293, miR-550a-5p), a ceRNA mechanism substantiated by dual-luciferase reporter assays and Ago2-RIP sequencing. In lung cancer, circTP63 promotes FOXM1 expression by sponging miR-873-3p, which in turn regulates the cell cycle-related proteins CENPA and CENPB, facilitating the transition from the G1/S phase to the G2/M phase in lung squamous cell carcinoma cells [29]. Owing to their tissue specificity and high stability, circRNAs have emerged as potential diagnostic markers and therapeutic targets in cancer [30, 31]. For example, plasma circRNA_0000190 exhibits high sensitivity and specificity for early lung cancer detection [32], whereas nanomedicine targeting circRNA_0001785 significantly inhibits tumor growth in breast cancer models [33]. Here, circ17399 was significantly upregulated in SKCM, with high expression correlating with poor prognosis. In vitro experiments revealed that circ17399 knockdown inhibits SKCM cell proliferation and migration while promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition, which is consistent with findings in other cancers, such as gastric cancer and hepatocellular carcinoma [34, 35]. Similarly, in vivo experiments revealed that circ17399 knockdown suppressed tumor growth. These results demonstrate that circ17399 is a novel therapeutic target for SKCM treatment.

CircRNAs are well known for their role as miRNA sponges, preventing miRNAs from binding to their target mRNAs and indirectly promoting the expression of target genes [36, 37]. In this context, we further investigated the interaction between circ17399 and miRNAs. Using bioinformatics predictions and dual-luciferase assays, we verified that circ17399 directly binds to miR-150-3p, a microRNA with significant implications in tumor biology [38]. Emerging evidence indicates that miR-150-3p performs tumor-suppressive functions across malignancies by targeting SP1 to inhibit glioma proliferation [39], attenuating ovarian cancer growth, and inducing pancreatic carcinoma apoptosis [40]. Paradoxically, its role in SKCM remains enigmatic. Our findings demonstrate that miR-150-3p downregulation in SKCM specimens is correlated with aggressive clinicopathological features and an unfavorable prognosis. Functional characterization revealed that miR-150-3p inhibition potentiates melanoma cell proliferative capacity (P < 0.01) and invasive propensity (2.1-fold increase), diverging from its established oncostatic roles in cervical (HR = 0.62, 95% CI 0.51–0.75) and hepatocellular carcinomas (OR = 0.43, P = 0.008). This tissue-specific duality underscores the contextual nature of miRNA-circRNA interactions and highlights the key nodal position of circ17399 in the SKCM-specific regulatory network. Through a combination of relevant databases and experimental validation, we subsequently identified ITM2C as a target gene of miR-150-3p. As a protein-coding gene, ITM2C has various expression patterns in different types of tumors [41]. In colorectal cancer, studies have shown that ITM2C expression is significantly downregulated compared with that in normal tissues, suggesting a possible role as a tumor suppressor gene [41]. In patients with multiple myeloma, high ITM2C expression is significantly associated with better survival outcomes, further supporting its potential antitumor properties [42]. However, few studies have investigated the specific role of ITM2C in SKCM. Our research revealed that ITM2C and circ17399 have a synergistic effect, jointly promoting the development of SKCM. Knockdown of circ17399 reduces ITM2C expression, whereas inhibition of miR-150-3p restores ITM2C levels, reversing the antitumor effect caused by circ17399 knockdown. These findings indicate that the circ17399/miR-150-3p/ITM2C axis highlights the core mechanism through which circ17399 promotes tumor progression via the ceRNA mechanism, which is consistent with existing knowledge of ceRNA-mediated cancer regulation.

Our findings reveal that circ17399 promotes melanoma progression through a novel dual regulatory mechanism. While previous studies have focused primarily on individual mechanisms of circRNAs, such as miRNA sponging or protein interactions, our research highlights the multifaceted regulatory roles of circRNAs, involving both posttranscriptional and epigenetic regulation. Specifically, we demonstrated that circ17399 directly binds to the RNA demethylase ALKBH5, enhancing its demethylation activity toward FOXM1 mRNA, reducing the m6A modification level of FOXM1, and increasing its stability and expression. ALKBH5-mediated m6A demethylation plays a crucial role in the progression of various cancers. For example, in lung cancer, ALKBH5 stabilizes key oncogenes, whereas, in hepatocellular carcinoma, it promotes tumor development by regulating cell proliferation and metastasis [43, 44]. FOXM1, a member of the forkhead box transcription factor family, regulates critical biological processes, particularly the cell cycle and proliferation [45]. Elevated FOXM1 expression is linked to the development of several cancers, making it a key target for cancer research [46]. Studies have shown that inhibiting FOXM1 can enhance the effectiveness of immunotherapy in lung cancer by modulating PD-L1 expression and cell proliferation [47] and that RNF112-mediated ubiquitination of FOXM1 reduces its transcriptional network in gastric cancer, thereby inhibiting proliferation and invasion [48]. It can be concluded that the circ17399-ALKBH5-FOXM1 pathway represents a broader regulatory mechanism in cancer biology with potential therapeutic implications. Notably, FOXM1 has also been implicated in melanoma progression. Miyashita et al. reported that FOXM1 is expressed in human melanoma tissues and cell lines, is associated with greater tumor thickness and poorer overall survival, and that FOXM1 knockdown suppresses melanoma cell proliferation. In addition, Puig-Butille et al. showed that FOXM1 is involved in MAPK/ERK-driven melanoma growth and survival, and that genetic or pharmacological inhibition of FOXM1 impairs melanoma growth. These melanoma-specific findings further support the relevance of the circ17399/ALKBH5/FOXM1 regulatory axis identified in our study [49, 50]. Consistent with these observations, our study confirmed that FOXM1 overexpression enhances proliferation, migration, and invasion in SKCM cells. In this melanoma context, FOXM1 upregulation may further activate cell-cycle kinase regulatory networks, particularly cyclin/CDK- and PLK1/Aurora kinase-associated G1/S and G2/M progression programs. Thus, circ17399/ALKBH5-mediated stabilization of FOXM1 may provide a mechanistic link between m6A regulation and kinase-driven melanoma progression. In line with this concept, Ahmad et al. recently showed that multitarget docking and molecular enumeration can identify compounds modulating cell-cycle and regulatory signalling, supporting the potential value of multitarget computational strategies for future systemic interventions against FOXM1-related kinase networks in SKCM [51].

Although the results offer compelling evidence of the role of circ17399 in melanoma progression, several limitations remain to be considered. First, the present work relies primarily on in vitro and in vivo experiments, and future research should validate these findings in larger clinical sample cohorts. In particular, although our cellular and animal experiments support the circ17399/miR-150-3p/ITM2C regulatory axis, we did not directly examine the association among miR-150-3p expression, ITM2C expression, and activation of the ITM2C axis in a large set of human melanoma specimens. Moreover, given that ITM2C/BRI1 has recently been linked to lipid metabolism and apoptosis, whereas its oncogenic function has been investigated in only limited tumor contexts, additional clinicopathological and mechanistic studies are needed to clarify whether ITM2C contributes to melanoma progression through melanoma-specific or more general biological pathways. Second, Although our dual-luciferase reporter assays, expression analyses, and rescue experiments support the circ17399/miR-150-3p/ITM2C regulatory axis, these approaches do not directly demonstrate the endogenous coexistence of these RNA species within the RISC complex. Future studies using Ago2-RIP or CLIP-based assays would further strengthen the evidence for this ceRNA interaction in melanoma cells. Nevertheless, the clinical translation of circ17399-targeting strategies remains challenging. Future studies should clarify the in vivo stability and accessibility of endogenous circ17399 and develop safe nanocarrier systems, such as lipid nanoparticles, to deliver circ17399-targeting siRNAs to melanoma tumors while minimizing off-target effects and systemic toxicity. Finally, although our RNA pulldown and RIP-qPCR assays support the interaction between circ17399 and ALKBH5, the structural basis by which ALKBH5 recognizes RNA substrates remains unclear. Future studies integrating structural biology approaches with molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulations, and MMPBSA free energy calculations may help define the dynamic ALKBH5–RNA interaction interface and guide the rational design of ALKBH5-targeted inhibitors. Similar computational strategies have recently been applied to evaluate ligand–receptor stability and binding free energy in drug discovery studies, supporting their value for dissecting complex molecular interactions [52].

Conclusion

In conclusion, circ17399 promotes melanoma progression via a ceRNA mechanism by regulating the miR-150-3p/ITM2C axis and modulating FOXM1 m6A modification levels via its interaction with ALKBH5. This study highlights circ17399 as not only a promising novel biomarker for melanoma diagnosis and treatment but also a potential target for multitarget combination therapies. Our findings provide novel insights into the role of circRNAs in melanoma and suggest that circ17399 may represent a promising therapeutic target; however, extensive pharmacokinetic, pharmacodynamic, and safety profiling in immunocompetent murine models is required before circ17399/ALKBH5-targeted interventions can be considered for clinical translation.

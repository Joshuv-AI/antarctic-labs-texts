1.
Introduction

Suppose
M
is a
n-
dimensional closed manifold equipped with a Riemannian metric
|\cdot|_{x\in M}
. For any continuous
contact Hamiltonian

H:(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following assumptions:

H1:

is convex in
p\in T_{x}^{*}M
for any
(x,u)\in M\times\mathbb{R}

H2:

H
is superlinear in each fiber, i.e.
\lim_{|p|\rightarrow+\infty}{H(x,p,u)}/{|p|_{x}}=+\infty
for any
(x,u)\in M\times\mathbb{R}
;

H3:

H(x,p,u)
is non-decreasing in

for any
(x,p)\in T^{*}M
;

the following
contact Hamilton-Jacobi (H-J) equation

(1)

In this paper, we propose a

to explain the multiplicity of solutions.
We will show how certain

dominate the order of solutions and the structure of
\mathfrak{C}
. Recall that for Hamiltonians independent of

, the significance of Mather measures in dominating the solutions have been discussed in
[
13
,
15
]
, as a part of the weak KAM theory. Besides, for Hamiltonians strictly increasing in
u
, the solution to (
1

[
6
,
7
,
8
]
for instance. These works prompt us to study the essential role of

H3
in ordering the solutions of (
1

Finally, we point out that a similar comparison principle was made in an earlier work
[
20
]
, where the authors used a

developed in
[
25
,
26
]
. In some sense, their work inspires the comparison principle part of this paper, although our treatment uses a different technique (from the weak KAM theory) which relies on weaker assumptions. At the same time, a description of
\mathfrak{C}
was also made in
[
32
]
by using a min-max formula, which doesn’t mention the information of solutions.

Throughout the paper, solution (resp. subsolution, supersolution) is always meant by the viscosity solution (resp. viscosity subsolution, viscosity supersolution).

For

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{x}=\partial_{p}H(x,p,u),\\
&\dot{p}=-\partial_{x}H-\partial_{u}H(x,p,u)p,\qquad(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}.\\
&\dot{u}=\langle p,\partial_{p}H\rangle-H(x,p,u),\end{aligned}\right.

The

2.1.
Weak KAM solutions of classical H-J equations

This part is a brief introduction of the weak KAM theory. We assume the classical Hamiltonian
H:(x,p)\in T^{*}M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

p
(for any contact Hamiltonian, by assigning a constant

by

L(x,v):=\max_{p\in T^{*}_{x}M}\{\langle v,p\rangle-H(x,p)\}.

As in (
2

(also called

has a solution.

Definition 2.1

A function

of (

1)

\omega\prec L+c(H)

For any

ending with it, such that

\omega(x)-\omega(\gamma(t))=\int_{t}^{0}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))+c(H)ds,\quad\forall\,t\geq 0.

Such a curve is called a

\omega
.

Two types of Mather measures

As we know, any solution

On the other side, for any

Assume

∎

2.4.
Proof of the comparison principle

At first, we propose the following technical Lemma which will be used in the proof of Theorem

and Theorem

[

¿ For the consistency of the paper, we briefly sketch the proof for it.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose that
u:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a subsolution of

{\bf H}^{\varphi}(x,d_{x}u):=H(x,d_{x}u,\varphi(x))=c

and
w:M\to\mathbb{R}
is a solution of

{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,d_{x}w):=H(x,d_{x}w,\psi(x))=c,

for certain continuous functions
\varphi
and
\psi
, then one of the following two holds:

(1)

The maximum of
u-w
can be attained at a point
x_{\max}\in M
where
\varphi(x_{\max})-\psi(x_{\max})\leq 0
.

(2)

We can find a Lipschitz curve
\gamma:t\in(-\infty,0]\to M
which is both
u
-calibrated for

{\bf L}^{\varphi}+c
and
w
-calibrated for
{\bf L}^{\psi}+c
, such that for all
t\in(-\infty,0]
,

\displaystyle u(\gamma(t))-w(\gamma(t))=\max(u-w),\qquad\varphi(\gamma(t))-\psi(\gamma(t))>0.

Moreover, if (1) does not hold, denoting by
K<+\infty
a Lipschitz constant
for the Lipschitz curve
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\to M
obtained in (2),
we can find
a closed measure
\mu\in\mathcal{C}
of which
{\rm supp}(\mu)
is contained in the compact subset
\{(x,v)\in TM\mid|v|_{x}\leq K\}
,
such that

\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\varphi(x))+c\big]\,d\mu(x,v)=\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\psi(x))+c\big]\,d\mu(x,v)=0

and for all
(x,v)\in{\rm supp}(\mu)
,

u(x)-w(x)=\max_{M}(u-w),\ \ \varphi(x)-\psi(x)>0.

Proof.

Pick
x_{0}\in M
such that

u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=\max_{M}(u-w)
. If
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})\leq 0
, then alternative (1) holds.

Suppose now that
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})>0
. Since
w
is a solution of

{\bf H}^{\psi}(x,d_{x}w)=c
, we can find a w-calibrated curve
\gamma:\,(-\infty,0]\to M
ending with
x_{0}
, which is Lipschitz with a constant
K<+\infty
due to Lemma
2.2
. In particular,

(15)

w(x_{0})-w(\gamma(t))=\int_{t}^{0}L(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\psi(\gamma(s)))+c\,ds,\;\;\forall\;t\leq 0.

Since
\varphi(x_{0})-\psi(x_{0})>0
, we can define
t_{0}\in(-\infty,0]\cup\{-\infty\}
by

(16)

t_{0}=\inf\{t\leq 0\mid\varphi(\gamma(s))-\psi(\gamma(s))>0,\text{ for all $s\in[t,0]$}\}.

By continuity of
\varphi\circ\gamma-\psi\circ\gamma
, there hold
t_{0}<0
and
(\varphi-\psi)(\gamma(t_{0}))=0
if
t_{0}
is finite.

Claim:
We have

(17)

u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=u(\gamma(t))-w(\gamma(t)),\quad\forall\,t\in(t_{0},0],

and
\gamma:(t_{0},0]\to M
is not only

w
-calibrated but also
u
-calibrated.
Moreover

(18)

L\bigl(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),\varphi(\gamma(t))\bigr)=L\bigl(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),\psi(\gamma(t))\bigr),\quad{\rm a.e.}\;t\in(t_{0},0].

We now prove the Claim.
In fact by condition H3, we obtain

(19)

L\bigl(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\varphi(\gamma(s))\bigr)\leq L\bigl(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\psi(\gamma(s))\bigr),\quad{\rm a.e.}\;s\in(t_{0},0].

Hence for any
t\in(t_{0},0]
, we get

(20)

\displaystyle u(x_{0})-u(\gamma(t))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{0}\big[L\bigl(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\varphi(\gamma(s))\bigr)+c\big]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{0}\big[L\bigl(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),\psi(\gamma(s))\bigr)+c\big]\,ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle w(x_{0})-w(\gamma(t)).

Recall that
u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=\max_{M}(u-w)
, which implies

u(x_{0})-u(\gamma(t))=w(x_{0})-w(\gamma(t))
and forces all inequalities
in (
20
) to be equality. Consequently, the curve
\gamma:(t_{0},0]\to M

is also
u
-calibrated and

w(x_{0})-w(\gamma(t))=u(x_{0})-u(\gamma(t)),\,\textup{for all $t\in(t_{0},0]$},

so it verifies equality (
17
) then (
18
),
which finally establishes the Claim.

\bullet
If
t_{0}
is finite, then by continuity
\max_{M}(u-w)=u(x_{0})-w(x_{0})=u(\gamma(t_{0}))-w(\gamma(t_{0}))

and
(\varphi-\psi)(\gamma(t_{0}))=0
due to (
16
). Alternative (1) holds in that case.

\bullet
If
t_{0}=-\infty
, then the curve

\gamma:(-\infty,0]\to M
satisfies alternative (2). If so, the time average w.r.t.
t>0
gives us a probability measure
\mu_{t}
by

\int_{TM}f(x,v)\,d\mu_{t}:=\frac{1}{t}\int_{-t}^{0}f(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s))\,ds,\quad\forall\;f\in C_{c}(TM,\mathbb{R}).

Since
\gamma
is
K-
Lipschitz, supp
(\mu_{t})\subset\{(x,v)\in TM:|v|\leq K\}
. So we can choose a sequence

such that

\mu_{t_{n}}\to\mu
in the weak topology. Since

is compact and
\gamma
is both

calibrated and

calibrated, we can easily prove that

is a closed measure and satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\psi(x))+c\big]\,d\mu(x,v)=\int_{TM}\big[L(x,v,\varphi(x))+c\big]\,d\mu(x,v)=0.

Since any

should be a

u(x)-w(x)=\max_{M}(u-w)
and
\varphi(x)-\psi(x)>0
away from alternative (1). This finally finishes the proof.
∎

:

1). Due to Lemma

). However, that contradicts with the premise of alternative 2, i.e.,

Proof of Theorem

4.
Contact dynamics of the Mather measures

Throughout this section, the Hamiltonian
H:T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is assumed to be Tonelli and
C^{2}
smooth. Since
c\in\mathfrak{C}
is fixed, we can denote by
H_{c}(x,p,u):=H(x,p,u)-c

and the associated Lagrangian
L_{c}:=L(x,v,u)+c
. For convenience, we rewrite equation (
5
) associated with
H_{c}
here:

(25)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{x}=\partial_{p}H(x,p,u),\\
&\dot{p}=-\partial_{x}H-\partial_{u}H(x,p,u)p,\qquad(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}.\\
&\dot{u}=\langle p,\partial_{p}H\rangle-H(x,p,u)+c,\end{aligned}\right.

of which
\Phi_{H,c}^{t}:(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}
is the flow map. The

\mathcal{L}:(x,p,u)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(x,\partial_{p}H(x,p,u),u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}

transports the flow
\Phi_{H,c}^{t}
into the corresponding

\Phi_{L,c}^{c}:=\mathcal{L}\circ\Phi_{H,c}^{t}
which solves the following
contact Euler-Lagrange equation

(26)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{x}=v,\\
&\frac{d}{dt}\partial_{v}L(x,v,u)=\partial_{x}L(x,v,u)+\partial_{u}L(x,v,u)\partial_{v}L(x,v,u),\qquad(x,v,u)\in TM\times\mathbb{R}.\\
&\dot{u}=L(x,v,u)+c.\end{aligned}\right.

We will show that
\mu\in\mathfrak{M}(u)
is invariant w.r.t.
\Phi_{L,c}^{t}
. In other words,
{\rm supp}(\mu)
is the
\alpha-
limit set of a backward calibrated trajectory (w.r.t.
u
)
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\rightarrow M
which actually induces a real trajectory of (
26
) by the following Lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

Any backward
u-
calibrated curve
\gamma:(-\infty,0]\rightarrow M
is
C^{1}
smooth. Moreover,
\Big(\gamma(t),\partial_{v}L\big(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),u(\gamma(t))\big),u(\gamma(t))\Big):t\in(-\infty,0]\rightarrow T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}

5
).

Proof.

The scheme is quite similar with the one in Theorem 3.6.1 of
[
13
]
. First, due to Proposition
2.5
, there holds

\frac{d}{dt}u(\gamma(t))=L(\gamma(t),\dot{\gamma}(t),u(\gamma(t)))+c,\quad{\rm a.e.}\,t\in(-\infty,0).

Suppose
s<0
is a differentiable point of
\gamma
, i.e.,

(x_{0},p_{0},u_{0}):=\big(\gamma(s),\partial_{v}L\big(\gamma(s),\dot{\gamma}(s),u\circ\gamma(s)\big),u\circ\gamma(s)\big)\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}

is uniquely identified, then for suitably small
\delta,P_{s}>0
, the flow
\Phi_{H,c}^{\tau}
of (
5
) (associated with
H_{c}
) is well defined in the region

\Omega_{s}\times(-\delta,\delta):=\{(x_{0},p,u_{0})\in T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}:|p-p_{0}|\leq P_{s}\}\times\{t\in\mathbb{R}:|t|\leq\delta\}.

Moreover, for any

,
\pi\circ\Phi_{H,c}^{t}:\Omega_{s}\rightarrow M
is a diffeomorphism since
\partial_{pp}H
is positive definite everywhere. Denote by
\Lambda_{s}^{t}=\pi\circ\Phi_{H,c}^{t}(\Omega_{s})
for

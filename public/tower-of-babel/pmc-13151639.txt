Evaluation of confirmatory data following the Article 12 MRL review for isoxaben
Authors: European Food Safety Authority (EFSA), Giulia Bellisai, Giovanni Bernasconi, Luis Carrasco Cabrera, Irene Castellan, Monica del Aguila, Lucien Ferreira, Luna Greco, Renata Leuschner, Andrea Mioč, Stefanie Nave, Hermine Reich, Silvia Ruocco, Alessia Pia Scarlato, Marta Szot, Anne Theobald, Manuela Tiramani, Alessia Verani, Elena Zioga
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe applicant Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl submitted a request to the competent national authority in Sweden to evaluate the confirmatory data that were identified for isoxaben in the framework of the maximum residue level (MRL) review under Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 as not available. To address the data gaps, a new analytical method for enforcement and its independent laboratory validation (ILV), plus additional studies on the magnitude of residues were submitted by the applicant. The outstanding data gaps were sufficiently addressed. The new information provided confirmed a no‐residue situation in cotton seed and herbal infusions from flowers. The current MRL of 0.01 mg/kg for cotton seeds can therefore be confirmed. Since a more sensitive analytical enforcement method is now available, a lowering of the existing MRLs (0.05 mg/kg) for herbal infusions (flowers and roots) and hops to an limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg can be considered. The updated exposure calculations did not indicate a risk to consumers health.Keywords: confirmatory data, herbicide, isoxaben, MRL review, pesticide, risk assessment

SUMMARY

In 2022, when the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) reviewed the existing Maximum Residue Levels (MRLs) for active substance isoxaben according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, EFSA identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses which were not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The following data gaps were noted:

Analytical methods for the enforcement in hops and herbal infusions.

Analytical methods for the enforcement in hops and herbal infusions.

Additional residue trials supporting the GAPs on sage, rosemary, thyme, basil and edible flowers, cotton seeds and herbal infusions from flowers.

Additional residue trials supporting the GAPs on sage, rosemary, thyme, basil and edible flowers, cotton seeds and herbal infusions from flowers.

Tentative MRL proposals have been implemented in the MRL legislation by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2023/466 including footnotes indicating the type of confirmatory data that should be provided fully or partially reflecting the data gaps noted in the MRL review, reported above. It is noted that data gap 2 was only implemented for cotton seeds and herbal infusions from flowers. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by day month year.

In accordance with the agreed procedure set out in the working document SANTE/10235/2016, Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl submitted an application to the competent national authority in the Sweden (rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review.

The application, alongside the dossier containing the supporting data in IUCLID format, was submitted through the EFSA Central Submission System on 25 February 2025. The appointed RMS Sweden assessed the dossier and declared its admissibility on 26 January 2025. Subsequently, following the implementation of the EFSA's confidentiality decision, the non‐confidential version of the dossier was published by EFSA and a public consultation launched on the dossier. The consultation aimed to consult stakeholders and the public on the scientific data, studies and other information part of, or supporting, the submitted application, in order to identify whether other relevant scientific data or studies are available. The consultation ran from 11 August 2025 to 1 September 2025. No additional data nor comments were submitted in the framework of the consultation.

At the end of the commenting period, the RMS proceeded drafting the evaluation report, in accordance with Article 8 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005, which was submitted to the European Commission and EFSA on 30 September 2025.

On 29 October 2025 the European Commission sent a mandate to EFSA to assess the application and the evaluation report as required by Article 10 of the MRL regulation.

To address data gaps identified in the framework of the MRL review in 2022, a new analytical method for enforcement and its ILV, plus additional studies on the magnitude of residues were submitted by the applicant. Data gaps 1 and 2 were both sufficiently addressed.

The new information provided confirmed a no‐residue situation in cotton seed and herbal infusions from flowers. The current MRL of 0.01 mg/kg for cotton seeds can therefore be confirmed. Since a more sensitive analytical enforcement method is now available, a lowering of the existing MRLs (0.05 mg/kg) for herbal infusions (flowers and roots) and hops to an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg may be considered.

ASSESSMENT

The review of existing MRLs for the active substance isoxaben according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005 1 (MRL review) has been performed in 2022 (EFSA, 2022). European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) identified some information as unavailable (data gaps) and derived tentative MRLs for those uses not fully supported by data but for which no risk to consumers was identified. The list of GAPs assessed in the framework of the MRL review that were not fully supported by data and for which confirmatory data were requested are listed in Appendix A.

Following the review of existing MRLs, the legal limits have been modified by Commission Regulation (EU) No 2023/466, 2 including footnotes for tentative MRLs that specified the type of information that was identified as missing. Any party having an interest in maintaining the proposed tentative MRL was requested to address the confirmatory data by 6 March 2025.

In accordance with the specific provisions set out in the working document of the European Commission SANTE/10235/2016 (European Commission, 2020) and following the provisions set by the ‘Transparency Regulation’ (EU) 2019/1381, 3 the applicant Corteva Agriscience International Sàrl, on 25 February 2025 submitted an application to the competent national authority in Sweden (designated rapporteur Member State, RMS) to evaluate the confirmatory data identified during the MRL review alongside the dossier containing the supporting data using the IUCLID format.

To address the data gaps identified by EFSA, the applicant provided a new analytical method for enforcement and its ILV, plus additional studies on the magnitude of residues.

The RMS assessed the new information in an evaluation report, which was submitted to the European Commission and forwarded to EFSA on 30 September 2025 (Sweden, 2025). EFSA assessed the application as requested by the European Commission in accordance with Article 10 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

EFSA based its assessment on the evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Sweden, 2025) and the reasoned opinion on the MRL review according to Article 12 of Regulation (EC) No 396/2005.

For this application, the data requirements established in Regulation (EU) No 544/2011 4 and the relevant guidance documents at the date of implementation of the confirmatory data requirements by Regulation (EU) No 2023/466 are applicable. The assessment is performed in accordance with the legal provisions of the Uniform Principles for the Evaluation and the Authorisation of Plant Protection Products adopted by Commission Regulation (EU) No 546/2011. 5

An updated list of end points, including the end points of relevant studies assessed previously and the confirmatory data evaluated in this application, is presented in Appendix B.

The evaluation report submitted by the RMS (Sweden, 2025) and the exposure calculations using the EFSA Pesticide Residues Intake Model (PRIMo) are made publicly available as a background document to this reasoned opinion. 6

Analytical methods for enforcement purposes in plant commodities

In order to address data gap 1 (lack of analytical methods for the enforcement in hops and herbal infusions), the applicant provided a new validated method for the determination of isoxaben residues in difficult matrices (hops and herbal infusions from flowers and roots) and its independent laboratory validation (ILV). The RMS evaluated these studies and concluded that they are acceptable (Sweden, 2025).

Details on the validation of the analytical method and its ILV are presented in Appendix B.1.1.1.

The new enforcement method (code: CAM‐0505/001) is a single residue method involving high performance liquid chromatography with positive‐ion electrospray ionisation tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) for the determination of isoxaben. The extraction from the samples is performed by homogenising and shaking with a solution of acetonitrile/water (70:30, v/v). Isoxaben residues were partitioned from the aqueous sample extracts with hexane during the elution process.

The analytical method was validated according to EC guidance document SANTE/2020/12830 rev.2. The available data demonstrate that this method is valid in terms of accuracy, precision, repeatability and specificity for the enforcement of isoxaben in hops, chamomile flower and valerian root, with the limit of quantification (LOQ) of 0.01 mg/kg. Consequently, the method is considered successfully validated in hops and herbal infusions. The matrix effects were found to be significant (i.e. > ± 20%) for hops, therefore matrix‐matched standards were used for analysis of all matrices.

Regarding the ILV, a separate study was reported, which independently validate the analytical method CAM‐0505/001 for the enforcement of isoxaben in difficult matrices (hops and herbal infusions) in accordance with guidance document SANTE/2020/12830 rev.2. the ILV was carried out in a different location, by different personnel and using different instrumentation. No modification of the original method other than optimisation of instrument specific parameters were necessary. The method was successfully and independently validated for the determination of isoxaben in hops and chamomile flower with a LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg. As for the primary methods, matrix effects were reported to be significant for hops and chamomile flowers.

EFSA concluded that the data gap 2 identified in the framework of the MRL review was addressed. The tentative MRLs in hops and herbal infusions can be confirmed.

Magnitude of residues in primary crops

In order to address data gap 2 (lack of trials supporting uses on cotton and herbal infusion from flowers), the applicant provided one new study on the magnitude of residues in oilseed rape (to support the MRL on cotton seed) and one new study on the magnitude of residues in chamomile (to support the MRL on herbal infusion from flowers). The individual results of the residue trials are presented in Appendix B.1.2.1.

Cotton seeds:

Authorised GAP (same GAP as in MRL review): SEU soil treatment – 1 application at BBCH 00 at 150 g a.s./ha.

No trials on cotton seed were available for the MRL review. Quantifiable residues are not expected to occur following the authorised GAP with soil treatment. As required during the MRL review, additional residue trials were now submitted to confirm this assumption. Four residue trials, performed on rapeseed in southern Europe in growing seasons 2014 and 2025, were assessed by the RMS and concluded to be valid and independent. According to the assessment of the RMS, the methods used were sufficiently validated and fit for purpose and the samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which integrity of the samples has been demonstrated (Sweden, 2025).

The available residue trials were performed according to the parameters of the authorised GAP, except that the timing of application was at growth stage BBCH 16–20 instead of BBCH 00, representing potentially more critical residue situation. This minor deviation is deemed acceptable considering that all results indicate isoxaben concentration to remain below the limit of detection of 0.003 mg/kg, clearly confirming the expected no‐residue situation, even under worst case conditions. For the same reason, the reduced number of trials (four instead of eight for a major crop) is also deemed acceptable. Extrapolation of trials performed on rapeseed to cotton seed is foreseen by the current guidance on MRL setting and extrapolations (SANTE/2019/12752).

Herbal infusion from flowers:

Authorised GAP in France (same GAP as in MRL review): soil treatment – 1 application at 125 g a.s./ha; PHI: 90 days.

No trials on herbal infusions from flowers were available for the MRL review. Quantifiable residues are not expected to occur following the authorised GAP with soil treatment. As required during the MRL review, additional residue trials were now submitted to confirm this assumption. Three residue trials, performed on chamomile in northern Europe, were assessed by the RMS and concluded to be valid and independent. According to the assessment of the RMS, the methods used were sufficiently validated and fit for purpose and the samples of these residue trials were stored under conditions for which integrity of the samples has been demonstrated (Sweden, 2025).

The available residue trials were performed according to the parameters of the authorised GAP, except that the pre‐harvest interval (PHI) was of 35–42 days instead of 90 days. This minor deviation is deemed acceptable considering that all results indicate isoxaben concentration to remain below the limit of detection of 0.003 mg/kg, clearly confirming the expected no‐residue situation, even under worst case conditions. For the same reason, the reduced number of trials (three instead of four for a minor crop) is also deemed acceptable. Extrapolation of trials performed on chamomile to the whole group of herbal infusions from flowers (Code: 631000) is foreseen by the current guidance on MRL setting and extrapolations (SANTE/2019/12752). Furthermore, trials performed in northern zone are sufficient to support the GAP authorised in France as according to the same guidance, herbal infusions are marked as ‘N or S’ in Annex II (distribution of crops in France).

EFSA concluded that the data gap 2 identified in the framework of the MRL review was addressed for both cotton seed and herbal infusions from flowers.

Proposed MRLs

The new information provided confirmed the no‐residue situation in cotton seeds and in herbal infusions. Therefore, the current tentative MRL of 0.01 mg/kg in cotton seeds is confirmed. Regarding herbal infusions (from flowers and roots), a new enforcement method was validated with the LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg (see Section 1.1.4). Therefore, a lowering of the existing tentative MRLs from the LOQ of 0.05 mg/kg for herbal infusions (flowers and roots) to 0.01 mg/kg can be proposed. The same applies to hops for which the lower achievable LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg was also validated, and for which the residue trials evaluated by the MRL review confirmed a no‐residue situation.

RESIDUES IN LIVESTOCK

The confirmatory data assessed in this evaluation do not have an impact on pesticide residues expected in livestock. Thus, the previous assessment of residues in livestock (EFSA, 2022) is still valid.

CONSUMER RISK ASSESSMENT

Chronic exposure calculations were performed in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2022) using revision 3.1 of the EFSA PRIMo (EFSA, 2018, 2019).

EFSA updated these previous calculations considering the outcome of the present assessment of confirmatory data: confirming MRL and risk assessment values for cotton seed and confirming and lowering MRLs and risk assessment values for herbal infusions (dried flowers and dried roots) and hops (dried).

In addition, after the MRL review, new MRLs and risk assessment values have been implemented in EU MRL Regulation through fast track procedures between 2023 and 2025 on gherkins, peas without pods, dry beans and dry peas. For these crops respective risk assessment values were also added in the exposure calculation. Since residues are not expected to occur in commodities of animal origin, these were not considered in the exposure calculation.

All input values included in the exposure calculations are summarised in Appendix D.

The chronic exposure values calculated were compared with the ADI of 0.05 mg/kg bw per day (European Commission, 2011).

The highest chronic exposure was calculated for the Dutch toddler, representing 0.73% of the ADI. An overview of the consumer risk assessment is reported in Appendix B.3. The exposure calculations did not indicate a risk to consumer's health.

Acute exposure calculations were not carried out because an ARfD was not deemed necessary for this active substance (European Commission, 2011).

CONCLUSION AND RECOMMENDATIONS

To address data gaps identified in the framework of the MRL review (EFSA, 2022), a new analytical method for enforcement and its ILV, plus additional studies on the magnitude of residues were submitted by the applicant. Therefore, data gaps 1 and 2 were both sufficiently addressed.

The new information provided confirmed a no‐residue situation in cotton seed and herbal infusions from flowers. The current MRL of 0.01 mg/kg for cotton seeds can therefore be confirmed. Since a more sensitive analytical enforcement method is now available, a lowering of the existing MRLs (0.05 mg/kg) for herbal infusions (flowers and roots) and hops to an LOQ of 0.01 mg/kg can be considered.

The overview of the assessment of confirmatory data and the recommended MRL modifications are summarised in Appendix B.4.

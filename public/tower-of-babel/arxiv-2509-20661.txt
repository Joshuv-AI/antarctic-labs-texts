Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the Grothendieck groups K 0 ​ ( 𝒞 A ∞ n ) K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}) of n n -cluster categories 𝒞 A ∞ n \mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n} of type A ∞ A_{\infty} introduced by T. Holm and P. Jørgensen. We prove that K 0 ​ ( 𝒞 A ∞ n ) ≅ ℤ K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n})\cong\mathbb{Z} for an arbitrary n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 . As an application, this generalizes a result of Murphy for n = 1 n=1 .

1.
Introduction

In classical settings, the notion of a Grothendieck group has been used previously to give connections between
certain subcategories of a category and subgroups of the ambient category’s Grothendieck group; see
[
8
,
14
]
. The Grothendieck group of a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
is defined to be the free abelian group of isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{C}
, modulo the Euler relations:
[B]=[A]+[C]
for each triangle
A\rightarrow B\rightarrow C\rightarrow\Sigma A
in
\mathcal{C}
.

The Grothendieck group is an important numerical invariant of a category. Calculation of the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
of a given category
\mathcal{C}
has been extensively studied in recent years. One important method is connecting
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
to a split Grothendieck group
K_{0}^{\rm sp}(\mathcal{T})
of a (higher) cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
. We present some related results:

(1)

When
\mathcal{C}
is a locally finite triangulated category, Xiao and Zhu
[
13
]
showed
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
modulo the Euler relations formed by Auslander-Reiten triangles. In this case,
\mathcal{C}
can be seen the only 1-cluster tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

When
\mathcal{C}
is a
2
-Calabi-Yau triangulated category with a 2-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
, Palu
[
10
]
proved that
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to
K_{0}^{\text{sp}}(\mathcal{T})
modulo the Euler relations formed by exchange triangles, and he calculated the Grothendieck groups of cluster categories of finite Dynkin type
A_{n},D_{n}
and
E_{n}
. Later Murphy
[
9
]
calculated the Grothendieck groups of discrete cluster categories of Dynkin type
A_{\infty}
by this method.

(3)

When
\mathcal{C}
is a triangulated
category with a Serre functor
\mathbb{S}
and an
n
-cluster tilting subcategory
\mathcal{T}
, Fedele
[
2
]
presented a higher cluster tilting version of Palu’s result
[
10
]
, and he computed the Grothendieck groups of
n
-cluster categories of finite type
A_{m}
for odd
n
. Later, Chang and Zhuang
[
1
]
calculated the Grothendieck groups of
n
-cluster categories of finite type
D_{m}
for odd
n
by this method.

(4)

When
\mathcal{C}
is a Krull-Schmidt extriangulated
category with an
n
-cluster titling subcategory
\mathcal{T}
, Wang, Wei and Zhang
[
12
]
proved that
K_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to the index Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{T}
under some additional assumption. Recently, Wang
[
11
]
generalized Wang, Wei and Zhang’s result and calculated the Grothendieck groups of
n
-cluster categories of finite type
A_{m}
, which covers Palu’s
[
10
]
and Fedele’s
[
2
]
results.

Let
n
be an integer,
k
an algebraically closed field, and
\mathcal{C}
be a
k
-linear algebraic triangulated category that is idempotent complete and classically generated by an
(n+1)
-spherical object. This category was first studied in
[
6
]
for the case
n=1
. For different values of
n
, the category
\mathcal{C}
appears in various well-known forms. When
n=-1
,
\mathcal{C}
is equivalent to
D^{c}(k[X]/(X^{2}))
, the compact derived category of the dual numbers. When
n=0
, it corresponds to
D^{f}(k[![X]!])
, the derived category of complexes with bounded finite-length homology over the formal power series ring. For
n=1
,
\mathcal{T}
is the cluster category of type
A_{\infty}
; see
[
3
]
. When
n>1
, it can be interpreted as the
n
-cluster category of type
A_{\infty}
; see
[
4
]
.

We note that
n
-cluster categories are a central and dynamic area of research in higher representation theory, as they provide a rich framework to explore the mutation and classification of
n
-cluster tilting subcategories. Moreover, they also serve as a bridge between combinatorial and categorical approaches to higher cluster algebras.

In this paper, we investigate the Grothendieck groups
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n})
of
n
-cluster categories
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
of type
A_{\infty}
introduced by T. Holm and P. Jørgensen. We prove that
K_{0}(\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n})\cong\mathbb{Z}
for an arbitrary
n\geq 1
. As an application, this generalizes a result of Murphy for
n=1
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall basic notions of Grothendieck groups and related results. In Section 3, we review the construction of
n
-cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}
, and a geometric interpretation of
(n+1)
-cluster tilting subcategories. In Section 4, we prove the main result.

Conventions

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\mathcal{C}
is a Hom-finite, Krull–Schmidt triangulated category with shift functor
\Sigma
. The word subcategory will always mean full subcategory closed under isomor
phisms, directsums, and direct summands. In particular, a subcategory is determined by the indecomposable objects it contains.

2.
Grothendieck groups in triangulated categories

This section provides background material on Grothendieck groups in triangulated categories and related results.

2.1.
Grothendieck group

In this subsection, we recall the definition of Grothendieck group in a triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
.

\mathbf{Notations}
: For an object
X
in
\mathcal{C}
, we use
[X]
to denote the isoclass of
X
and
F
to denote the free abelian group generated by the isoclasses
[X]
of objects
X
in
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
,
13
]
)
.

By using the same notations as above, we define the split Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}
to be

K_{0}^{\rm sp}(\mathcal{C}):=F/<[X\oplus Y]-[X]-[Y]>,

where
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
. We define the Grothendieck group of
\mathcal{C}
to be

K_{0}(\mathcal{C}):=K_{0}^{\rm sp}(\mathcal{C})/<[X]-[Y]+[Z]\;|\;X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow Z\rightarrow\Sigma X\mathrm{\;is\;a\;triangle\;in\;}\mathcal{C}>.

2.2.
Grothendieck groups with (higher) cluster tilting subcategories

In this subsection, we recall the relationship of Grothendieck groups of
\mathcal{C}
with split Grothendieck groups of
\mathcal{T}
, where
\mathcal{T}
is the (higher) cluster tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{C}
is called
(n+1)
-cluster tilting if

(1)

\mathcal{T}
is functorially finite;

(2)

\mathcal{T}=\{M\in\mathcal{T}|\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}(M,\mathcal{T})=0\text{\;for\;}1\leq i\leq n\}=\{M\in\mathcal{T}|\mathop{\rm Ext}\nolimits^{i}(\mathcal{T},M)=0\text{\;for\;}1\leq i\leq n\}
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an
(n+1)
-cluster tilting subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
. Wang proved the following for an extriangulated category; see
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
.

.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an
(n+1)
-cluster tilting subcategory in
\mathscr{C}
. Then

K_{0}(\mathscr{C})\cong K_{0}^{\rm in}(\mathcal{T}).

such that
a_{i}=c_{i}b_{i}
, where
C_{1}=X_{1},C_{n+2}=X_{n+3},a_{1}=b_{1}
and

. We sometimes denote it by

3.
n
-cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}

In this section, we review the notion of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
, the geometric description of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
, and
(n+1)
-cluster tilting subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
.

3.1.
Realization of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}

Definition 3.1
.

[
4
, Definition 1.1]

We say that
\mathcal{C}
is a
k
-linear algebraic triangulated category with suspension functor
\Sigma
, which is idempotent complete and classically generated by an
(n+1)
-spherical object
s
, if

\dim_{k}\mathcal{T}(s,\Sigma^{\ell}s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\text{if\ $\ell=0$ or $\ell=n+1$,}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

It was proved in
[
7
,
4
]
that
\mathcal{C}
is uniquely determined by these properties, and can be thought of as a
n
-cluster category of Dynkin type
A_{\infty}
.

From now on, suppose that
n\geq 1
is an integer and
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
is the
n
-cluster categories of type
A_{\infty}
. By
[
5
, Proposition 1.8]
, the Serre functor of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
is
\mathbb{S}=\Sigma^{n+1}
, and by
[
5
, Proposition 1.10]
, the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}

consists of
n
components, each of which is a copy of
\mathbb{Z}A_{\infty}
, and
\Sigma
acts cyclically
on the set of components. The
n
components are denoted by
R,\Sigma R,\cdots,\Sigma^{n-1}R
and satisfies
\Sigma^{n+i}R=\Sigma^{i}R
for
i=0,1,\cdots,n-1
.

We recall the geometric realization of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
based on
[
4
]
.

Definition 3.2
.

(1)

Let
\Pi
be an
\infty
-gon. A pair of integers
(t,u)
as an arc in
\Pi

connecting the integers
t
and
u
with
u-t\geq 2
and
u-t\equiv 1(\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits n)
is
called an
n
-admissible arc.

(2)

Let
\mathfrak{U}
be a set of
n
-admissible arcs. An integer
t
is a left-fountain of
\mathfrak{U}
if
\mathfrak{U}
contains infinitely many
n
-admissible arcs of the form
(s,t)
, and
t
is a right-fountain of
\mathfrak{U}
if
\mathfrak{U}
contains infinitely many
n
-admissible arcs of the form
(t,u)
. We say that
t
is a fountain of
\mathfrak{U}
if it is both a left-fountain and a right-fountain of
\mathfrak{U}
.

(3)

We say that
\mathfrak{U}
is locally finite if, for each integer
t
, there are only finitely many
n
-admissible arcs of the form
(s,t)
and
(t,u)
in
\mathfrak{U}
.

The suspension functor
\Sigma
, the Serre funcotor
\mathbb{S}
, and the AR translation
\tau=\mathbb{S}\Sigma^{-1}
of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
act on an
n
-admissible arc
(t,u)
are given by

\Sigma(t,u)=(t-1,u-1),\;\;\;\mathbb{S}(t,u)=(t-n-1,u-n-1),\;\;\;\tau(t,u)=(t-n,u-n).

Note that
\tau=\Sigma^{n}
.

Proposition 3.3
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.4]

There is a bijective correspondence between
\mathop{\rm ind}\nolimits\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
and the set of
n
-admissible arcs. Moreover, This extends to a bijective correspondence between subcategories of
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
and subsets of the set of
n
-admissible arcs.

In the following, we sometimes use an
n
-admissible arc
(t,u)
to represent an indecomposable object
M
in
\mathcal{C}_{A_{\infty}}^{n}
and write
M=(t,u)
without confusion.

Definition 3.5
.

We say that
\mathfrak{U}
is an

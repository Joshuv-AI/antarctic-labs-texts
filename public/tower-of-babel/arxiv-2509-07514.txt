Carrier-Assisted Entanglement Purification PubID: pubid:
Jaemin Kim, Karthik Mohan, Sung Won Yun, Joonwoo Bae

Abstract
Entanglement distillation, a fundamental building block of quantum networks, enables the purification of noisy entangled states shared among distant nodes by local operations and classical communication. Its practical realization presents several technical challenges, including the storage of quantum states in quantum memory and the execution of coherent quantum operations on multiple copies of states within the quantum memory. In this work, we present an entanglement purification protocol via quantum communication, namely a carrier-assisted entanglement purification protocol, which utilizes two elements only: i) quantum memory for a single-copy entangled state shared by parties and ii) single qubits travelling between parties. We show that the protocol, when single-qubit transmission is noiseless, can purify a noisy entangled state shared by parties. When single-qubit transmission is noisy, the purification relies on types of noisy qubit channels; we characterize qubit channels such that the protocol works for the purification. We resolve the limitation by applying multiple qubits over noisy channels, and show that the purification protocol with multi-carrier qubits works through a noisy qubit channel in general, provided that the channels are not entanglement-breaking, i.e., channels that cannot be constructed as measure-and-prepare operations. Our results significantly reduce the experimental overhead needed for distilling entanglement, such as quantum memory and coherent operations, making long-distance pure entanglement closer to a practical realization.

An entangled bit (ebit), the unit of entanglement, is a key resource for quantum information processing,

To tackle the challenge, on the one hand, mature quantum technologies are clearly useful, namely, long-term quantum memory and fault-tolerant coherent quantum state manipulation. On the other hand, it is also important to devise a practical EPP with minimal technology requirements. For instance, quantum memories remain expensive resources, and noisy quantum operations will increase errors. Therefore, an EPP with minimal experimental resources, such as quantum memory, quantum gates, and measurements, leads the ultimate ebit-connected network closer to reality.

In the present work, we propose the carrier-assisted entanglement purification protocol (CAEPP), which purifies a single copy of a shared entangled state through noisy quantum communication without consuming additional entangled pairs. We analyze the CAEPP against noisy quantum communication and also present multi-carrier-assisted EPP (mCAEPP) that can purify noisy entanglement in general. We show that the mCAEPP works with all noisy channels which are not in the form of measure-and-prepare channels, characterized by entanglement-breaking channels. We investigate the practical advantages of the CAEPP over two-way EPPs (TWEPPs) for the use of quantum memory and errors in local operations such as local measurements. The CAEPP generalizes to multipartite systems, such as the purification of GHZ states, generally, multipartite stabilizer states.

Preliminaries

Let us begin with a summary of notations and terminologies to be used throughout. Let
\phi^{\pm}

\displaystyle|\phi^{\pm}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|00\rangle\pm|11\rangle),\qquad|\psi^{\pm}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(|01\rangle\pm|10\rangle).

A qubit channel connecting Alice and Bob, denoted by

satisfying
\sum_{ab}p_{ab}=1
as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\cdot)=p_{00}(\cdot)+p_{01}\,Z(\cdot)Z+p_{10}\,X(\cdot)X+p_{11}\,Y(\cdot)Y,

(2)

where
X,Y,Z
are the Pauli operators. If Alice prepares an ebit
\phi^{+}
, keeps one qubit, and sends the other through
\mathcal{N}
, the shared state is

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathsf{id}\otimes\mathcal{N})(\phi^{+})

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{00}\phi^{+}+p_{01}\phi^{-}+p_{10}\psi^{+}+p_{11}\psi^{-},

where
\mathsf{id}
denotes an identity channel. Note that there is an isomorphism between channels and states
[
15
,
16
]
; a state

, and conversely, a bipartite state

.

For convenience, throughout, we assume that probabilities are in a decreasing order, i.e.

\displaystyle p_{00}\geq p_{01}\geq p_{10}\geq p_{11}.

(4)

Note that local unitaries can realize permutations of probabilities
p_{ij}

[
17
]
, i.e.,

\displaystyle(p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11})\leftrightarrow(p_{\pi(00)},p_{\pi(01)},p_{\pi(10)},p_{\pi(11)})

For a two-qubit state, an entanglement fidelity defined as follows,

\displaystyle F(\rho):=\langle\phi^{+}|\rho|\phi^{+}\rangle,

(5)

can be used to quantify entanglement. For instance, an ebit can be identified by
F=1
. For a channel, one can introduce the channel fidelity as the fidelity of its CJ state. For a Pauli channel, the channel fidelity may be given by
F(\rho)=p_{00}

A quantum channel

is called

(EB) if a resulting state of a channel

\mathcal{N}
in (

p_{00}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.

III
Carrier-Assisted Entanglement Purification Protocol

In this section, we present the carrier-assisted entanglement purification protocol (CAEPP).

III-A

The protocol

To begin the protocol, two parties, Alice and Bob, establish a single copy of a two-qubit state between them,

\displaystyle\rho\;=\;q_{00}\phi^{+}+q_{01}\phi^{-}+q_{10}\psi^{+}+q_{11}\psi^{-},

(6)

where the fidelity is given by
F=q_{00}
, see also
Initialization
in
Algorithm

1
. Note also that a shared state satisfies the order among probabilities
\{q_{ij}\}
in (
4
) such that
q_{11}
is the minimal one.

The steps of a single round in the CAEPP are in order.

1.

Pre-processing:
Two parties apply local operations
R_{X}(+\tfrac{\pi}{2})\otimes R_{X}(-\tfrac{\pi}{2})
to a shared state, so that a state
\phi^{-}
in a shared Bell-diagonal state is the least probable, i.e.,
q_{01}
is the minimal one.

2.

Encoding:
Alice prepares a carrier qubit in a state
|0\rangle
. She applies a CNOT gate by taking the carrier as a target qubit, and sends the carrier through a channel
\mathcal{N}
.

3.

Decoding:
Once Bob receives a qubit from Alice, he applies a CNOT gate by taking the carrier as a target qubit. A measurement is performed on the carrier in the
Z
basis.

4.

Decision:
Bob tells Alice a measurement outcome. If an outcome is
0
, Bob declares
Success
and keeps the shared pair; otherwise, he announces
Failure
to discard a shared state.

The protocol generalizes to multiple carriers
m\geq 1
, where stabilizer purification protocols apply, see Algorithm

III-B

Two-round purification via noiseless transmission

Suppose that a channel
\mathcal{N}
for a single-qubit carrier is noiseless, that is,
\mathcal{N}=\mathsf{id}
. Following the CAEPP, we find that once Alice and Bob accept a shared state after the protocol, the resulting state is given by,

\displaystyle\rho_{out}\;=\;q_{00}^{\prime}\phi^{+}+q_{01}^{\prime}\phi^{-}+q_{10}^{\prime}\psi^{+}+q_{11}^{\prime}\psi^{-},

(7)

where

\displaystyle q_{00}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{q_{00}}{q_{00}+q_{01}},~~~q_{01}^{\prime}=\frac{q_{01}}{q_{00}+q_{01}}

and

\displaystyle q_{10}^{\prime}=q_{11}^{\prime}=0

One can observe that the protocol accepts Bell states
\phi^{\pm}
with an even parity and rejects the others
\psi^{\pm}
having an odd parity.

Afterwards, two parties run the protocol for the state
\rho_{out}
in (
7
). After the pre-processing step, two parties share a mixture of two Bell states
\phi^{+}
and
\psi^{-}
. When Bob announces
Success
after the protocol, which successfully rejects a Bell state
\psi^{-}
having an odd parity, a resulting state is
\phi^{+}
and thus
q_{00}^{\prime\prime}=1
, which has a fidelity
F=1
.

We have shown that two successful rounds of the CAEPP through noiseless single-qubit transmission purify noisy entanglement and enable two parties to share an ebit.

III-C

Encoding single-qubit carriers against noisy transmission

We revisit the step
Pre-processing
that applies local unitaries and discuss effective encoding on a single-qubit carrier against a noisy channel. We recall that the step applies local unitaries
R_{X}\left(+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)\otimes R_{X}\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
to a Bell-diagonal state in (
6
) that satisfies the order in (
4
). An application of the local unitaries realizes a permutation among error probabilities,

\displaystyle(p_{00},p_{01},p_{10},p_{11})\mapsto(p_{00},p_{11},p_{10},p_{01})

(8)

so that
\phi^{-}
is the least probable while the others remain unaltered. Then, each round of the CAEPP effectively suppresses errors of odd parities
\psi^{\pm}
.

From the duality between states and channels, called the CJ isomorphism, the permutation in (
8
) can be realized on a channel. In general, via the CJ isomorphism, a transformation of bipartite states by local unitaries
U\otimes V
is equivalent to manipulating a channel
\mathcal{N}
such that

\displaystyle V\mathcal{N}(U^{T}(~\cdot~)U^{*})V^{\dagger}.

The expression above shows that a sender applies a unitary
U^{T}
as an encoding on a state before a channel, and a receiver performs a unitary
V
as a decoding after receiving a state.

Then, the permutation in (
8
) can be realized for a Pauli channel
\mathcal{N}
as

\displaystyle R_{X}\left(+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)~\mathcal{N}(R_{X}\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)(\cdot)R_{X}\left(\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right))~R_{X}\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right).

(9)

Hence, we can identify an encoding before a channel use and a decoding after the channel as follows,

•

encoding: Alice applies a rotation
R_{X}\left(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
before sending, and

•

decoding: Bob implements a rotation
R_{X}\left(+\tfrac{\pi}{2}\right)
before a measurement.

The encoding and decoding strategy of two parties shuffles Pauli errors
Z
and
Y
in a Pauli channel, equivalently to the permutation in (
8
).

For instance, let us consider a Pauli channel with four probabilities,

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{00},\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00}),\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00}),\tfrac{1}{5}(1-p_{00})\bigr).

Applying the encoding and decoding strategy, two parties are effectively connected by some other Pauli channel

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{00},\tfrac{1}{5}(1-p_{00}),\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00}),\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00})\bigr),

(10)

where the fraction of a
Z
-error is minimal.

Hence, the CAEPP can efficiently suppress the largest fractions of errors causing odd parities
X
and
Y
. The
Z
and
Y
errors are permuted by the encoding and decoding strategy and eliminated by the CAEPP. After all, the CAEPP leads to the purification with
\phi^{+}
by removing all other fractions of errors.

IV
CAEPP with noisy single-qubit carriers

We here consider noisy channels for a single-qubit carrier, that is, the CAEPP with noisy single-qubit carriers. We assume that a noisy channel
\mathcal{N}
for a carrier is identical to the channel defining a shared state. The assumption follows from a realistic constraint; to establish a shared state, Alice creates an ebit and sends a qubit to Bob, where a qubit from Alice has also experienced an identical noisy channel, through which a carrier is also to be transmitted.

The scenario

As mentioned, both qubits, one for establishing a shared state and the other for a single-qubit carrier, are sent over an identical noisy channel. The scenario can also be rephrased as two copies of channels
\mathcal{N}\otimes\mathcal{N}
where one is for sharing an entangled state and the other is for a single-qubit carrier. The CAEPP applies, along with the encoding and decoding strategy explained in Section
III-C
.

In the step
Initialization
, Alice prepares
\phi^{+}
and sends one qubit to Bob through a channel
\mathcal{N}
, so a Bell-diagonal state in (
3
) is shared. After the protocol, once Bob obtains an outcome
0
and announces
Success
, a state shared by two parties has a higher purity, being closer to an ebit.

Summary

Interestingly, a shared entangled state may have a higher fidelity by the CAEPP with noisy transmission of a single-qubit carrier. However, it cannot be purified to an ebit in general if a carrier qubit suffers from a noisy channel. We write by

\displaystyle F_{\star}:\mathrm{~the~maximum~convergent~fidelity}

(11)

of a resulting shared state from repetitions of the CAEPP.

IV-A

General Pauli channels:
F_{\star}<1

A Pauli channel is characterized by four probabilities
\{p_{ij}\}
, and let us consider general cases where all of them are nonzero.

Depolarizing channel

A depolarization has equal probabilities for errors
X
,
Y
, and
Z
, for which we parameterize probabilities characterizing a channel as follows,

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{00},\,\tfrac{1}{3}(1-p_{00}),\,\tfrac{1}{3}(1-p_{00}),\,\tfrac{1}{3}(1-p_{00})\bigr).

(12)

Note that through the channel, when Alice creates an ebit and sends one qubit to Bob, two parties share an isotropic state,

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathrm{iso}}=p_{00}\,\phi^{+}+\frac{1-p_{00}}{3}\,(\mathds{1}-\phi^{+}).

(13)

which is entangled for
p_{00}>1/2
. Equivalently, the channel is EB for
p_{00}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
.

Then, repeating the CAEPP through the channel, two parties can purify shared entanglement with a limitation
F_{\star}<1
. In Fig.
2
, the purification is shown up to a maximum convergent fidelity
F_{\star}
. For instance, for a channel with
p_{00}=0.75
, single rounds can increase the fidelity up to a limited one
F_{\star}\approx 0.863
.

A general Pauli channel

Let us also consider another instance where probabilities are not equal as follows,

\displaystyle\bigl(p_{00},\,\tfrac{1}{5}(1-p_{00}),\,\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00}),\,\tfrac{2}{5}(1-p_{00})\bigr).

(14)

In Fig.

Note that the reason for a higher convergent fidelity, compared to a depolarization channel, is due to the fact that the channel in (

IV-B

Pauli channels for
F_{\star}=1

Let us consider Pauli channels characterized by three probabilities only. These cases can be generally parameterized as follows,

\displaystyle(p_{00},\,0,\,\gamma(1-p_{00}),\,\delta(1-p_{00}))~~\mathrm{where}~~\gamma+\delta=1.

(15)

Two parties exploit the channel to establish a shared state and send a carrier qubit. A shared state corresponds to the CJ state of the channel,

\displaystyle\rho\;=\;F\,\phi^{+}+\gamma(1-F)\,\psi^{+}+\delta(1-F)\,\psi^{-},

where we have used the initial fidelity
F=p_{00}
.

A single round of the CAEPP suppresses
X
and
Y
errors of the odd parity,
\psi^{\pm}
. Thus, a resulting state from the single round is a mixture of three Bell states
\phi^{+}
,
\psi^{+}
, and
\psi^{-}
with a higher fidelity,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}\;=\;\frac{Fp_{00}}{Fp_{00}+(1-F)(1-p_{00})}.

Hence,
X
and
Y
errors are suppressed. Repeating the CAEPP, we derive the fidelity after
n

\displaystyle F_{n}\;=\;\frac{p_{00}^{\,n+1}}{p_{00}^{\,n+1}+(1-p_{00})^{\,n+1}},

with the initial condition
F_{0}=p_{00}
. As
n
tends to be large, one can see that

\displaystyle F_{n}\rightarrow 1~~\mathrm{as}~~n\rightarrow\infty,

that is,
F_{\star}=1
whenever
p_{00}>\tfrac{1}{2}
.

4
, we consider a flip channel with probabilities
(p_{00},0,1-p_{00},0)

[

IV-C

Improving the CAEPP with
F_{\star}<1

It is natural to explore the possibilities of improving the CAEPP in the presence of noisy channels.

•

The first strategy is to exploit two-way entanglement distillation in Refs.
[
1
,
2
]
. Once a shared state reaches the maximum convergent fidelity, copies of them are collected and exploited in two-way EPPs.

•

The second strategy is to enhance the CAEPP by leveraging additional resources.

In what follows, we present the CAEPP with multiple qubits as carriers against noisy channels and show that the protocol leads to pure entanglement.

V
CAEPP with multiple carriers

We generalize the CAEPP with multiple qubits, called
multi-carrier-assisted EPP (mCAEPP)
, which purifies a single-copy shared state to an ebit through noisy channels. The protocol is summarized in Algorithm

V-A

Stabilizer code

The purification protocol with multiple carriers can be constructed with the stabilizer code, specified by a commuting set of Pauli
generators

\{S_{i}\}
. In each round, Alice encodes
m
carrier qubits using an
encoding operation
that prepares the carriers in the code space defined by
\{S_{i}\}
, sends them through the same Pauli channel
\mathcal{N}
, and Bob applies a corresponding
decoding operation
and measures the carriers in the
Z
basis.

Measurement outcomes correspond to
syndrome bits
: we record
0
for eigenvalue
+1
, meaning no error detected, and
1
for eigenvalue
-1
, and accept the round when all syndrome bits are
0
. In the stabilizer formalism, a Pauli error
E
is
detected
if it anticommutes with at least one generator
S_{i}
, i.e.,
ES_{i}=-S_{i}E
for some
i
; errors commuting with all generators pass the check and remain undetected.

V-B

Encoding and decoding operations

The carrier qubits are initially prepared as

, which is a code state of the stabilizer generators

\displaystyle\{Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{m}\}.

(16)

\displaystyle U_{\mathrm{enc}}Z_{i}U_{\mathrm{enc}}^{\dagger}=S_{i}~~~\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}.

For example, the CNOT gate

\displaystyle U_{CX}\,(\mathds{1}\otimes Z)\,U_{CX}^{\dagger}=Z\otimes Z,

implying that

Realistic constraints

TWEPPs face several drawbacks in realistic scenarios:

1.

Identical input requirement.
A universal EPP that always guarantees an output fidelity no worse than that of each input state cannot be implemented using LOCC
[
23
]
. Consequently, a TWEPP may reduce entanglement depending on the inputs. To avoid this, TWEPPs typically assume identical input pairs.

2.

Quantum-memory demand.
TWEPPs require quantum memory for two reasons: (i) short-term memory to synchronize two qubits for bilateral CNOT gates, and (ii) long-term memory to store successful output pairs until an identical pair is available for the subsequent round. Both types of quantum memory remain technically challenging. Moreover, TWEPPs demand large-capacity quantum memories to hold multiple pairs during the process. Recent work has aimed to reduce this memory cost
[
24
,
25
]
.

3.

Sensitivity to measurement noise.
Measurement noise reduces the entanglement fidelity after each purification round and can even prevent entanglement distillation from weakly entangled states
[
26
]
.

Generalization

The CAEPP can be applied to purifying multipartite entangled states in general. Note that in multipartite quantum systems, there are entangled states that are not equivalent under stochastic LOCC; hence, there is no unique unit of multipartite entanglement. For instance, GHZ and W states are not interconvertible. The CAEPP can be constructed for a single pair of shared states, once parties define a pure entangled state for the purification.

VIII
Conclusion and discussion

Summary

We have introduced carrier-assisted entanglement purification (CAEPP), a protocol that purifies shared entanglement without consuming additional pairs of entangled states. The central idea is to replace auxiliary entangled pairs with one or more transmitted carrier qubits, thereby reducing memory requirements and noisy measurements while retaining purification capability.

We presented the CAEPP with a single-qubit carrier. The protocol enables two parties to achieve an ebit in two rounds of the protocol if a carrier is sent through a noiseless channel. When a channel for a carrier qubit is noisy, the CAEPP contains limitations in the purification; although the fidelity may increase, it converges to a maximum value, called maximum convergent fidelity. We have investigated the CAEPP against noisy qubit channels of a single-qubit carrier.

We then showed how the mCAEPP with stabilizer codes can resolve the limitation; the protocol has the maximum convergent fidelity as
1
. Examples with two, three, and four carriers using different stabilizer codes are presented. We further proved that the maximum convergent fidelity approaches
1
with multiple carriers, showing that CAEPP can generally enable parties to share ebits through noisy channels.

The CAEPP contains practical advantages over TWEPPs. The CAEPP requires only one shared pair and one transmitted carrier, in contrast to TWEPPs that consume multiple pre-shared pairs and require a substantial use of quantum memory. In addition, the CAEPP applies a smaller number of measurements than TWEPPs. As a result, the CAEPP is more resilient to memory noise and measurement errors.

Finally, the CAEPP applies to the purification of multipartite entanglement. We have demonstrated an example with a tripartite GHZ state.

Discussion

In future directions, it would be interesting to investigate the CAEPP for creating entanglement beyond the purification of entanglement. Note that the CAEPP shares some similarities with the entanglement distribution with separable states (EDSS) protocol
[
27
]
, where entanglement is established through carrier transmission from initially separable states.

It would also be interesting to develop the mCAEPP by optimizing
adaptive stabilizer strategies
, which depend on a choice of stabilizer codes. Identifying adaptive schemes for an efficient mCAEPP is a key to realizing the purification of entanglement in practical realizations over noisy channels. Clearly, it is also of fundamental interest to consider the CAEPP for high-dimensional quantum systems.

We envisage that the CAEPP provides a practical and robust alternative to TWEPPs. By reducing memory demands and tolerating noisy operations, the CAEPP offers the pathway toward scalable entanglement distribution in near-term quantum networks.

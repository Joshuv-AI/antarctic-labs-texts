On a class of m-Isometric and Quasi-m-isometric operators
Y. Estaremi, M. S. Al Ghafri and S. Shamsigamchi

Abstract
. In this paper we characterize m m -isometric and quasi- m m -isometric weighted conditional type (WCT) operators on the Hilbert space L 2 ​ ( μ ) L^{2}(\mu) . Also, we prove that the subclasses of m m -isometric and quasi- m m -isometric of normal WCT operators are coincide. Specially we have the results for multiplication operators. Indeed, we find that for m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , a multiplication operator M u M_{u} is m m -isometric (quasi- m m -isometric) if and only if it is isometric (quasi-isometric). Some examples are provided to illustrate our results.

Introduction and Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the Banach algebra of bounded linear operators acting on an infinite
dimensional complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. An operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is called
m
-isometry if

B_{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}T^{*^{k}}T^{k}=0

and also
T
is called quasi-m-isometry if

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}T^{*^{k+1}}T^{k+1}=T^{*}B_{m}T=0.

Specially, the operator
T
is quasi-2-isometry if

T^{*^{3}}T^{3}-2T^{*^{2}}T^{2}+T^{*}T=0.

Also,
T
is called quasi-isometry if
T^{*^{2}}T^{2}=T^{*}T
. Clearly every
quasi-2-isometric operator is quasi-
m
-isometric (
[
15
]
, Theorem 2.4). Moreover, the
classes of quasi-isometric operators and 2-isometric operators are sub-classes of the
class of quasi-2-isometric operators. In fact, inclusions are proper. There are some examples of quasi-2-isometric operators which are not quasi-isometric, one can see (
[
15
]
, Example 1.1). For more details on
m
-isometric operators one can see
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
3
,
5
,
12
,
14
]
and references therein.

In this paper we are going to characterize
m
-isometric and quasi-
m
-isometric weighted composition operators on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mu)
.

Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a complete
\sigma
-finite measure space.
For any sub-
\sigma

\mathcal{A}\subseteq\Sigma

L^{2}
-space

L^{2}(X,\mathcal{A},\mu_{\mid_{\mathcal{A}}})

\mu

For a sub-
\sigma

will play a major role in our work. Let

Here we recall some basic properties of the conditional expectation operator

\bullet
If

\bullet

\bullet

|E(fg)|^{2}\leq E(|f|^{2})E(|g|^{2})

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T=M_{w}EM_{u}
be a bounded operator on

(1)

WCT operator

is quasi-

-isometry if and only if
|E(uw)|=1
,
\mu
, a.e. Consequently, we get that

m
-isometry, for all

(2)

WCT operator
T
is
m
-isometry if and only if
E_{r}=\{1\}
, for odd
m
and
E_{r}=\{-1\}
, for even
m

E_{r}=\text{essential\ range}(J^{\prime}_{m}E(|w|^{2})E(|u|^{2}))\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ J^{\prime}_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}|E(uw)|^{2(k-1)}.

Equivalently,

|J^{\prime}_{m}|E(|w|^{2})E(|u|^{2})=1
,
\mu

Proof.

(1)
As we know, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}

T^{k}=M_{E(uw)^{k-1}}T,\ \ T^{*^{k}}=M_{\overline{E(uw)}^{k-1}}T^{*}\ \ T^{*}T=M_{E(|w|^{2})}M_{\bar{u}}EM_{u}

Hence

is quasi-
m

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}T^{*^{k+1}}T^{k+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}M_{|E(uw)|^{2k}}T^{*}T=0

if and only if
M_{J_{m}E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u}=0

if and only if

\|J_{m}E(|u|^{2})E(|w|^{2})\|_{\infty}=\|M_{J_{k}\bar{u}}EM_{u}\|=0,

in which

J_{m}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}|E(uw)|^{2k}.

Clearly, for every

we have
J_{m}(x)=(|E(uw)|(x)-1)^{m}
.
So
T
is quasi-
m
-isometry if and only if
J_{m}=0
,
\mu
, a.e., on
S(E(|u|^{2}))\cap S(E(|w|^{2}))
if and only if
|E(uw)|=1
,
\mu
, a.e.

(2) By definition,
T
is
m
-isometry if and only if

\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}T^{*^{k}}T^{k}=((-1)^{m}I+\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}M_{|E(uw)|^{2(k-1)}}T^{*}T)=0

((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})=0

if and only if

\|((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})\|=0,

in which

J^{\prime}_{m}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}|E(uw)|^{2(k-1)}

It is easy to see that

\|((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})\|^{2}=\|((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})^{2}\|.

\displaystyle\|((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})\|

\displaystyle=r((-1)^{m}I+M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda\in E}|(-1)^{m}+\lambda|,

E_{r}=\sigma(M_{J^{\prime}_{m}}M_{E(|w|^{2})\bar{u}}EM_{u})=\text{ess \ range}(J^{\prime}_{m}E(|w|^{2})E(|u|^{2})).

Therefore

is

-isometry if and only if
\sup_{\lambda\in E_{r}}|(-1)^{m}+\lambda|=0
if and only if
E_{r}=\{1\}
, for odd

, for even

is

|J^{\prime}_{m}|E(|w|^{2})E(|u|^{2})=1
,
\mu
, a.e.
∎

Let

-hyponormal if and only if it is normal.

As we mentioned above, if a bounded linear operator

is normal, then

B_{m}(T)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{m-k}\binom{m}{k}(T^{*}T)^{k}=(T^{*}T-I)^{m}.

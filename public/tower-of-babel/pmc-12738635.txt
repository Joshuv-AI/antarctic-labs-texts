Assessment of management accounting information using decision algorithm with prioritized weights in circular fermatean fuzzy CRITIC WASPAS model
Authors: Qingping Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
In this manuscript, fuzzy sets are used to handle uncertainty in enhancing risk management and operational efficiency in management accounting based on a decision-making algorithm. For this purpose, a circular fermatean fuzzy (CFF) set (CFFS), which not only contains membership and non-membership grades but also provides circular behavior. Management accounting information is a process of collecting, analyzing, interpreting, and presenting financial and non-financial data that is specifically designed to aid internal management in planning, decision-making, performance evaluation, and control of business operations. For this constraint, extend criteria importance through intercriteria correlation (CRITIC) method and the weighted aggregated sum product assessment (WASPAS) method into the CFF framework. The proposed model can easily describe the importance of multiple attributes and the weight of decision-makers. Additionally, this paper explored an algorithm for the multi-attribute group decision-making problem based on the CRITIC-WASPAS technique under the presence of defined linguistic terms. The defined techniques are significantly applied in handling uncertainties in hypothetical data obtained by management accounting. Moreover, this paper validates the defined model through a case study. To evaluate the challenges of management accounting, considering five alternatives, each with five attributes, to select the best option. After the evaluation, the second alternative, i.e., internal accounting, is the best. A sensitivity analysis confirms effectiveness and reliability. A comparative analysis is conducted between the proposed approach and previous aggregation operators to validate its performance and reliability.

Introduction

Decision-making involves selecting from multiple alternatives by evaluating options and weighing outcomes. Seikh and Mandal3 introduced aggregation operators based on Frank t-norm and t-conorm: Application to MADM Process. Gitinavard et al.4 developed a project safety evaluation by a new soft computing approach-based last aggregation hesitant fuzzy complex proportional assessment in the construction industry.

MAGDM extends MADM by involving a group of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{D}$$\end{document} who collectively evaluate alternatives across multiple attributes. It is useful in complex evaluations, like management accounting assessments, and suits contexts with diverse stakeholders and multiple attributes. Mousavi et al.5 elaborated an extended multi-attribute group decision approach. Hussain et al.6 elaborate TOPSIS approach for MAGDM. Abbas et al.7 suggested MAGDM in the MAIRCA approach. Gitinavard8 introduced strategic evaluation of sustainable projects based on hybrid group decision analysis. Wang9 adopted the MAGDM model based on the improved ARAS method.

The concept of FS was first introduced by Zadeh10, where a membership grade (MG) is defined within the range [0,1]. Atanassov11 extended the concept of FS to intuitionistic FS (IFS), which incorporates both MG and NMG, providing a more comprehensive framework for dealing with uncertainty. Building upon these foundations, Yager introduced the idea of Pythagorean FS (PyFS)12, which further extends the domain of MG and NMG. Moreover, Senapati et al.13 work on FFS provides a generalized framework that encompasses FS, IFS, and PyFS, offering greater flexibility and applicability in modeling complex decision-making problems. Recently, Atanassov14 extended and more generalized the IFS by introducing circular IFS (CIFS), where MG and NMG are represented as the radius of elements. The grades are constrained by the condition that their sum is less than or equal to 1, and the radii lie within the closed interval [0,1]. However, CIFS is limited by its restricted domain. To address this, Bozyigit et al.15 proposed circular PyFS (CPyFS), expanding the domain of CIFS, with the constraint that the sum of squares of both grades is less than or equal to 1, and the radius belongs to a closed interval from 0 to \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\sqrt{2}$$\end{document}. Literature study

Management accounting focuses on providing relevant, timely, and accurate information to internal stakeholders, primarily managers, rather than to external parties such as investors or regulators. The information generated is specifically designed to support the organization’s strategic, operational, and tactical decision-making needs, and it may include both quantitative data (for example, cost reports, budgets) and qualitative insights (for example, customer satisfaction levels and employed performance metrics).

Management accounting is essential to an organization’s internal governance since it provides these insights. It gives managers the knowledge and resources they need to make wise resource allocation decisions, match daily operations with long-term goals, and react quickly to changes in the corporate environment. An essential part of efficient organizational administration, management accounting is very flexible and focused due to its independence from external reporting standards.

Similar to the FS theory, these factors are defined by complexity and uncertainty. By integrating several evaluation elements, the MADM and MAGDM techniques offer a potent tool for assessing fuzzy notions. Scholars have significantly advanced this discipline by putting forth novel ideas, including: Hemandez et al.17 developed fuzzy logic in business, management, and accounting. Shahrestani et al.18 introduced management accounting techniques in small and medium enterprises using fuzzy cognitive mapping. Shokraei et al.19 suggested management accounting using the fuzzy ANP method among companies of the stock exchange and securities organization. Behzadian et al.20 elaborated on promoting water accounting implementation processes: an argumentation-based fuzzy DEMATEL approach. Chatterjee and Seikh21, analyzing sustainable industrial wastewater treatment technologies using circular Fermatean fuzzy multi-attribute group decision making with decision experts. Gitinavard et al.22 introduced a decision-making method based on the hesitant fuzzy preference selection index. Borujeni23 initiated intuitionistic fuzzy group decision analysis for sustainability risk assessment.

Research gap

Even though MADM and MAGDM techniques have advanced significantly, there are still a number of noteworthy gaps in the literature, especially when it comes to handling ambiguity, vagueness, and imprecision in expert judgments. Though they have made progress in capturing uncertainty, traditional fuzzy models like IFS, PyFS, and their extensions frequently face mathematical restrictions when it comes to expressing more intricate interactions between membership and non-membership degrees. In particular, the level of uncertainty present in real-world decision-making situations is sometimes too great for these models to accurately represent. Additionally, little to no effort has been made to apply the CFFS, an even more powerful and generic framework, to decision-making problems, including linguistic assessments, even though the CIFS and CPyFS frameworks have increased flexibility through circular restrictions.

The incorporation of language characteristics into the CFFS environment represents another gap. Although classical fuzzy settings have been used to study linguistic decision-making, the literature so far has not fully addressed the formulation and application of linguistic circular Fermatean fuzzy values. This is a lost chance to more accurately and successfully mimic expert viewpoints.

Furthermore, attribute weighting is still a chronic problem. Subjective or pre-assigned weights are used in many MADM techniques, which might add bias and lower the dependability of the results. Although the CRITIC technique has been recognized for its capacity to construct objective weights based on data features (contrast intensity and conflict), its application in circular fuzzy environments, specifically CFFS, is yet to be established. This discrepancy restricts the use of CRITIC in situations where circular fuzzy sets are the most effective way to model uncertainty. Last but not least, even though the popular WASPAS approach combines additive and multiplicative decision techniques, it has not been applied to the CFFS domain. Motivation

This article makes the WASPAS approach more appropriate for usage in MAGDM by extending it to the CFF framework. The WASPAS technique, initially introduced by Zavadskas et al.24, combines the weighted sum model (WSM) with the weighted product model (WPM) for comprehensive evaluations. It is a popular way of decision-making that allows for systematic review and prioritization by considering both qualitative and quantitative factors. This extended technique was used to evaluate management accounting by leveraging CFF’s ability to handle uncertainty. Novelty and significance

This study introduces a new CRITIC–WASPAS-based decision-making framework within the CFFS environment, addressing several key research gaps in existing MADM and MAGDM methods. Unlike traditional fuzzy models (such as IFS, PyFS, or CIFS) that struggle to express complex uncertainty, the proposed framework effectively handles circular, linguistic, and highly ambiguous information through CFFS, which incorporates both membership, non-membership and radius components.

A major novelty lies in extending the WASPAS method, which integrates the WSM and WPM to operate in the CFFS context, allowing for more accurate and consistent evaluation of alternatives under uncertainty. This is the first known attempt to adapt WASPAS to handle circular fuzzy data. Additionally, the study integrates the CRITIC method into the CFFS framework to determine objective attribute weights based on contrast intensity and inter-criteria correlation, thereby reducing subjective bias and improving reliability.

The inclusion of linguistic CFF terms further enhances interpretability, allowing experts to express their judgments naturally while maintaining mathematical rigor. Finally, by applying the model to a management accounting decision-making problem, the paper demonstrates the practical relevance and superior performance of the proposed approach compared to existing fuzzy and circular fuzzy methods. Contribution

This study makes several key contributions to advancing MAGDM under uncertainty. It introduces a novel CRITIC–WASPAS-based framework within the CFFS environment, which effectively captures circular, linguistic, and highly ambiguous information that traditional fuzzy models such as IFS, PyFS, and CIFS struggle to represent. A major innovation lies in extending the WASPAS approach that combines the WSM and the WPM to operate in the CFFS setting, making this the first known adaptation capable of managing circular fuzzy data. This extension allows for more precise, balanced, and consistent evaluations of alternatives in uncertain and imprecise decision environments.

In addition, the integration of the CRITIC method into the CFFS framework provides an objective mechanism for determining attribute weights based on contrast intensity and inter-criteria correlation, effectively reducing subjectivity and bias in decision-making. The inclusion of linguistic CFF terms enhances interpretability, enabling experts to articulate their judgments more intuitively while maintaining analytical rigor. The proposed model is further validated through its application to a management accounting decision-making problem, demonstrating its practicality, robustness, and superior performance compared to existing fuzzy and circular fuzzy approaches. Together, these innovations establish the framework as a powerful and flexible tool for solving complex MAGDM problems in uncertain environments.

Algorithm of CRITIC-WASPAS method in circular fermatean fuzzy framework for MAGDM

The novelty of this paper is to extend the hybrid decision-making methodology that combines the CRITIC technique and WASPAS technique in the CFF context to the evaluation of alternatives. Utilizing the correlation and standard deviation, the CRITIC approach allocates weights based on the degree of contrast and disagreement between qualities. WSM and WPM are employed by the WASPAS method to evaluate alternatives.

The SWARA–ARAS method, which is used to identify the greatest renewable energy sources in India within a confidence level-based interval-valued Fermatean fuzzy environment, is an example of a subjective weighting strategy where expert judgments greatly impact the criteria importance38. Comparatively, the MEREC–VIKOR technique is an objective method that evaluates criteria based on inherent data variability. It is utilized for sustainable methods in the adoption of electric vehicles39. Both of these methods offer valuable perspectives, but CRITIC was chosen because it offers a more sophisticated and data-driven weighting system that is not only dependent on expert judgment. It accomplishes this by calculating criteria weights objectively while accounting for both the degree of contrast and the conflict between criteria. WASPAS was selected above VIKOR or CRADIS, as evidenced by the strategies discussed for the management of municipal solid waste and the use of electric vehicles40. WASPAS combines the advantages of weighted sum and weighted product models to increase decision-making robustness and solution accuracy. This mixed nature usually results in more reliable rankings and greater discrimination among options than single-method techniques like VIKOR or CRADIS, especially in complicated fuzzy environments. Case study

The purpose of this case study is to use the CFF’s recommended CRITIC-WASPAS technique to analyze the complexities of management accounting. Using the MAGDM approach, this study aims to fully evaluate and address the challenges associated with management accounting, such as cost management, performance evaluation, and budgeting.

Experimental problem

The extent to which management accounting data contributes to an organization’s long-term objectives, competitive edge, and general performance is referred to as strategic value. Managers can make well-informed decisions about innovation, growth, investment, and market positioning by utilizing information with strategic value rather than just operational data. It guarantees that short-term actions stay in line with long-term goals and sustained performance by tying daily operations to the organization’s larger vision. Management accounting is essential to internal governance because it helps managers allocate resources optimally, match objectives with actions, and react quickly to changes in the corporate environment. Because of its adaptability and lack of external reporting requirements, it is a useful tool for efficient management.

The process of gathering, evaluating, interpreting, and presenting financial and non-financial data for the express purpose of supporting internal management in planning, decision-making, performance assessment, and business operations control is known as management accounting information. Five options, each with five features, are considered in order to assess the difficulties of management accounting and choose the best one. We applied the WASPAS approach and the suggested CRITIC method to the CFF framework for this restriction.

Comparison with existing literature.

Table 16 shows that the proposed approach is feasible and powerful due to its advanced features, whereas existing techniques are limited and unable to solve the data presented in Table 12. The main limitation of existing methods is their restricted frameworks, such as IFS, PyFS, and CIFS, which are based on algebraic properties, methods, generalizations, and operators. Unlike existing methods, the proposed approach operates on the CFF information, which provides a more comprehensive framework with a larger domain and circular behavior. This makes existing techniques a special case of the proposed work, highlighting the limitations of earlier established work.

Existing studies have limitations in handling complex fuzzy information. Xu42 and Zhao et al.43 used weighted averaging and geometric operators in IFS, but these operators are inadequate for CFFS due to its larger domain and circular behavior. Peng et al.44 and Khan et al.45 worked on PyFS, which is a special case of CFFS and lacks circular behavior. Verma46 and Jan et al.47 introduced weighted averaging and weighted geometric operators in the framework of FFS, but their approach lacks radius information. Fahmi et al.48 proposed circular intuitionistic fuzzy Hamacher aggregation operators, but their approach is limited in handling CFF information. Bozyigit et al.15 developed and CPyF aggregation operators, which become the special case of CFFS, due to a limited domain. Gitinavard et al.49 worked on an interval-valued hesitant fuzzy group, but their approach lacks radius information. Our proposed theory overcomes these limitations, presenting a more advanced and comprehensive approach.

Managerial implications

The proposed CRITIC–WASPAS framework under the CFFS environment offers several practical benefits for managers and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\mathcal{D}$$\end{document} working in uncertain and complex organizational settings. First, it provides a systematic and data-driven approach to evaluate multiple alternatives when information is vague, incomplete, or in subjective situations common in strategic planning, investment analysis, and performance evaluation. By incorporating linguistic and circular fuzzy representations, managers can express qualitative judgments (such as “high importance” or “moderate risk”) in a structured way, leading to clearer and more transparent decision outcomes.

Furthermore, the integration of the CRITIC method ensures that attribute weights are determined objectively, reducing the influence of personal biases or organizational politics in the decision-making process. This enhances fairness, accountability, and confidence in managerial decisions. The extended WASPAS approach, combining both additive and multiplicative assessments, helps managers achieve more balanced and accurate evaluations across competing options. In practice, this framework can lead to measurable improvements such as more efficient resource allocation, better supplier selection, or more informed financial and strategic choices. Overall, the model empowers managers to make consistent, rational, and evidence-based decisions that align short-term actions with long-term organizational goals.

Conclusion

This study extends the CRITIC approach in the framework of CFFS, which is used to weight prioritization in several qualities and estimate unknown weights for attribute relevance. The novelty of this paper is to introduce the hybrid CRITIC-WASPAS model within the CFFS framework. The initiated model can easily integrate the strong group decision-making problem that permits an exhaustive and objective assessment of the available options. The model’s ability to determine the optimum solutions is demonstrated by its application to a management accounting case study. The reliability of the suggested work is confirmed by sensitivity analysis with a range of parametric values, and its superiority is confirmed by comparisons with existing methods.

Constraints and limitations

Compared to circular FS theory, the CFF framework exhibits greater proficiency because of its wider domain. Within the CFF framework, the extended CRITIC-WASPAS model offers a robust methodology for addressing complex challenges in decision-making scenarios. But the proposed extended model fulfills under the presence of information presented in Definition 1 and fails for the circular complex fuzzy and bipolar fuzzy information. Despite these drawbacks, the model remains a significant asset in the fuzzy field and proves particularly useful in evaluating intricate decision-making scenarios across diverse applications.

Future direction

This study lays the groundwork for future research that can investigate the integration of diverse aggregation operators, such as Aczel-Alsina and Dombi operators, with CRITIC-WASPAS techniques to develop more robust decision-making frameworks. Future studies can also explore alternative methodologies, including TOPSIS, AHP, and MARCOS, to further enrich the proposed theory. However, the concept can offer promising avenues for further research, like Aczel–Alsina power aggregation operators50, Dombi hamy mean operators51, TOPSIS method for MAGDM52, and Aczel-Alsina hamy mean operators53.

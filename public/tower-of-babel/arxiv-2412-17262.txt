Localization for random operators on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with the long-range hopping
Yunfeng Shi, Li Wen, Dongfeng Yan

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate random operators on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with Hölder continuously distributed potentials and the long-range hopping. The hopping amplitude decays with the inter-particle distance ‖ 𝒙 ‖ \|\bm{x}\| as e − log ρ ⁡ ( ‖ 𝒙 ‖ + 1 ) e^{-\log^{\rho}(\|\bm{x}\|+1)} with ρ > 1 , 𝒙 ∈ ℤ d \rho>1,\bm{x}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d} . By employing the multi-scale analysis (MSA) technique, we prove that for large disorder, the random operators have pure point spectrum with localized eigenfunctions whose decay rate is the same as the hopping term. This gives a partial answer to a conjecture of Yeung and Oono [ Europhys. Lett. 4(9), (1987): 1061-1065].

The random Schrödinger operator (i.e., the Anderson model) on

[

. In higher dimensions, Fröhlich-Spencer

]
proved, either for large disorder or extreme energies, the absence of diffusion for the random Schrödinger operator by developing the remarkable multi-scale analysis (MSA) method. Based on the Green’s function estimates in
[

with the power-law long-range hopping)
[

]
. However, the problem of proving the existence of the absolutely continuous spectrum for the Anderson model remains largely open. For more results on the study of the localization for random operators, we refer to

]

While random models with finite-range hopping (e.g., the Laplacian as in the Anderson model) work nicely in describing variety of materials, the long-range hopping is often found in different physical systems. The main motivations for investigating the long-range model (

[

]
:

, if the hopping term

. This conjecture was tested
[

]
based on numerical studies for the case
\phi(\bm{x})=e^{-\sqrt{\|\bm{x}\|}}
. This paper tries to give an affirmative but partial answer to this conjecture in the more general

[

]
(cf.
[
DK89
]
for the significant simplification of this method) from the finite-range hopping to a slower long-range (e.g., the (
1.3

[

]
has previously introduced a novel MSA type approach to prove the spectral localization for random operators with power-law long-range hopping.

Our approach is mainly based on a MSA type Green’s function estimates in the spirit of
[

,

]
. However, the presence of the weight (

)

]

If the distribution

. We believe the FMM can also deal with the hopping satisfying (

]

]

The following lemma is useful for dealing with matrices with slowly decaying off-diagonal elements.

Lemma 4.1
.

For

\displaystyle\log^{\rho}(1+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i})\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\log^{\rho}(1+x_{i})+C(\rho)\log^{\rho}n,

Proof.

For a detailed proof, we refer to the Appendix
A

∎

Remark 4.1
.

We have the

\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(

\displaystyle\log^{\rho}(\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{x}_{i}\|+1)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log^{\rho}(\|\bm{x}_{i}\|+1)+C(\rho)\log^{\rho}n.

We have

.

Let
\mu\in\mathscr{H}(\lambda)
. Fix
0<\beta<\mathcal{D}_{\lambda}(\mu)
and

\displaystyle\underline{L}_{0}=\underline{L}_{0}(\lambda,\mu,\beta,d,p,\gamma,\rho,\rho^{\prime},\kappa_{0})>0

The proof is based an application of the Neumann series expansion argument.
Define

\displaystyle\zeta=2^{-1}\left(\beta^{-1}L_{0}^{p}(2L_{0}+1)^{d}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}},\ \eta=\frac{\zeta}{2}.

Take

\displaystyle\textbf{R}_{\bm{x}}(\zeta):\ \exists\bm{z}\in B_{L_{0}}(\bm{x})\ {\rm s.t.},\ |V_{\omega}(\bm{z})-E_{0}|\leq\zeta.

From (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\textbf{R}_{\bm{x}}(\zeta))

\displaystyle\leq(2L_{0}+1)^{d}\mu([E_{0}-\zeta,E_{0}+\zeta])

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\lambda}(2L_{0}+1)^{d}\beta^{-1}\zeta^{\lambda}

(4.3)

Next, suppose that

\displaystyle H_{B_{L_{0}}(\bm{x})}-E=\varepsilon R_{B_{L_{0}}(\bm{x})}\Gamma_{\phi}R_{B_{L_{0}}(\bm{x})}+\mathcal{Q}.

where in the third inequality, we have used (

-good for

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

In order to complete the proof of the Theorem

(Wegner estimate, cf. Theorem 6.2 of

6.
Iteration Theorem

In this section, we introduce an iteration theorem, which mainly deals with Green’s function estimates in the induction steps. We first define the induction parameters (
s\geq 0
):

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{N},\ L_{s+1}=[L_{s}^{\alpha}],\ \kappa_{0}\in\left(0,\frac{\gamma}{5}\right],\ \kappa_{\infty}\in(0,\kappa_{0}),

\displaystyle\kappa_{s+1}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{s}-\left(\frac{10\gamma}{L_{s}^{\frac{4}{5}\alpha-1}}+\frac{10\gamma}{\log^{\rho-1}L_{s}}+\frac{20+\alpha^{\rho^{\prime}}}{\log^{\rho-\rho^{\prime}}L_{s}}\right)(1<\rho^{\prime}<\rho<\rho^{\prime}+1).

(6.1)

Definition 6.1
.

Fix
E_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
I=[E_{0}-\eta,E_{0}+\eta]
with some
\eta>0
. For each
s\geq 0,
we define

{\bf(P1)}_{s}
:

for all
\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|>2L_{s}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{\exists E\in I\text{ s.t., both $B_{L_{s}}(\bm{x})$ and $B_{L_{s}}(\bm{y})$ are $(\kappa_{s},E)$-bad}\}\leq L_{s}^{-2p};

(6.2)

{\bf(P2)}_{s}
:

for all
\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|>2L_{s}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\bigcup_{E\in I}(\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{x}}(E)\cap\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{y}}(E))\right\}\leq\frac{1}{4}L_{s}^{-2p},

(6.3)

where for
\bm{w}=\bm{x},\bm{y},

\displaystyle\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{w}}(E):=\{\text{$\exists U\in\mathscr{T}_{s,\bm{w}}$ s.t., $U$ is $E$-R}\}

(6.4)

with

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{s,\bm{w}}:=\{B_{L}(\bm{z}):\ B_{L}(\bm{z})\subset B_{L_{s}}(\bm{w})\text{ with $L=10L_{s-1},40L_{s-1},130L_{s-1},L_{s}$}\}.

(6.5)

Remark 6.1
.

The initial estimate
{\bf(P1)}_{0}
holds true as shown in Theorem
4.2
, which requires the smallness of
\varepsilon
(i.e., the large disorder condition) in the current setting. This base case serves as the starting point for the MSA. Importantly, as will be demonstrated later, the verification of both
{\bf(P1)}_{s}
and
{\bf(P2)}_{s}
for all scales
s\geq 1
does not require the large disorder condition.
Indeed,
{\bf(P2)}_{s}
can be established via the priori Wegner estimate (i.e., there is no need to perfrom the multi-scale induction), which is also independent of the large disorder condition.
This key observation suggests that, once the initial scale is controlled under large disorder, the induction mechanism can propagate to all scales regardless of the disorder strength. Consequently, we may expect localization to hold at extreme energies for any non-zero disorder, mirroring the well-known behavior of Schrödinger operators (cf.
[
CKM87
]
). In
d=1
, it is known that the localization holds true on the whole energy interval for all non-zero disorder. This was proved using transfer matrix methods, which are not available for long-range hopping operators. The presence of a long-range hopping makes this all coupling localization result difficult to prove (cf.
[
JM99
]
for the proof of spectral localization for one dimensional random operators with some power-law long-range hopping).

The iteration theorem reads as

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
L_{0}\geq\underline{L}_{0}\gg 1
and
s\geq 1
. Assume that both
{\bf(P1)}_{s-1}
and
{\bf(P2)}_{s}
hold true. Then
{\bf(P1)}_{s}
holds true as well.

The proof of this iteration theorem will be finished in §
8
.

7.
The validity of
({\bf P2})_{s}

In this section, we aim to prove the validity of

for

Theorem 7.1

(Verification of
({\bf P2})_{s}
)

Let

(i.e.,

for all
\|\bm{x}-\bm{y}\|>2L_{s}
, where
\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{x}}(E)
and
\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{y}}(E)
are defined in (
6.4
).

Proof.

Suppose that
\omega\in\bigcup_{E\in I}(\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{x}}(E)\cap\textbf{D}_{s,\bm{y}}(E))
. Then there are some
E\in I
,
U_{\bm{x}}\in\mathscr{T}_{s,\bm{x}}
and

(cf. (

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(E,\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{x}}}))<e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}},\ {\rm dist}(E,\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))<e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}},

and then

\displaystyle{\rm dist}(\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{x}}}),\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))<2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}.

This implies

Next, it needs to control

.
For this purpose, we will use Lemma
5.1
. Then applying Lemma
5.1
with
\epsilon=2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}
and

yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\{{\rm dist}(E,\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))\leq 2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}\}\leq\beta^{-1}2^{\lambda}(2L_{s}+1)^{d(1+\lambda)}e^{-\lambda\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}.

From the

assumption of the potentials,
L_{s}\geq L_{s-1}\geq L_{0}\geq\underline{L}_{0}>0

[

\displaystyle\ \ \mathbb{P}\{{\rm dist}(\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{x}}}),\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))\leq 2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}\}

\displaystyle\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{\#U_{\bm{x}}}\mathbb{P}\{{\rm dist}(E,\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))\leq 2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}\}

\displaystyle\ \ \beta^{-1}2^{\lambda}(2L_{s}+1)^{d(2+\lambda)}e^{-\lambda\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}.

and

, we obtain

\displaystyle\ \ \mathbb{P}\left\{\bigcup_{E\in I}(\bm{D}_{\bm{x}}(E)\cap\bm{D}_{\bm{y}}(E))\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \ \sum_{U_{\bm{x}}\in\mathscr{T}_{s,\bm{x}}\atop U_{\bm{y}}\in\mathscr{T}_{s,\bm{y}}}\mathbb{P}\{{\rm dist}(\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{x}}}),\sigma(H_{U_{\bm{y}}}))\leq 2e^{-\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}\}

\displaystyle\ \ 16\beta^{-1}2^{\lambda}(2L_{s}+1)^{d(4+\lambda)}e^{-\lambda\log^{\rho^{\prime}}L_{s-1}}\leq\frac{1}{4}L_{s}^{-2p}.

∎

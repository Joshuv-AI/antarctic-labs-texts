Dilation and Model Theory for Pairs of Contractions with a Twisted Commutation Relation
Sourav Ghosh

Abstract
. In this note, we develop a parallel theory of the classical Sz.-Nagy–Foias dilation and model theory for a single contraction operator in the setting of pairs of q q -commuting contraction operators for a unimodular complex number q q .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 47A13; Secondary: 47A20, 47A25, 47A56, 47A68, 30H10

1.
Introduction

A celebrated theorem by Sz.-Nagy
[
16
]
states that every contraction on a Hilbert space admits a co-isometric extension. More precisely, for any contraction
T
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, there exists an isometry
V
acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
that contains
\mathcal{H}
, such that
T^{*}=V^{*}|_{\mathcal{H}}
. A similar result for a pair of commuting contractions was provided in a seminal paper by Andô
[
1
]
, which shows that any pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
has a commuting isometric pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}
containing
{\mathcal{H}}
as a co-isometric extension. Although the extension of Andô’s theorem to more than two variables is no longer valid (see
[
14
]
), it has inspired numerous generalizations across various frameworks. This note is concerned with the extension of Andô’s Theorem in the non-commutative setting. Sebestyén
[
15
]
obtained the first non-commutative generalization of Andô’s theorem, focusing on the anti-commutative case. He showed that if
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a contractive pair which is
anti-commuting
, i.e.,
T_{1}T_{2}=-T_{2}T_{1}
, then there is an anti-commuting isometric lift
(V_{1},V_{2})
of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. Extending Sebestyén’s work, Keshari and Mallick showed in
[
11
]
that if
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a contractive pair which is
q
-commuting
i.e.,
T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{2}T_{1}
for some unimodular complex number
q
, then there is a
q
-commuting isometric lift
(V_{1},V_{2})
of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. However, these dilations are far from canonical, a fact that seems to rule out a coherent model theory. The methods in the works cited above are based on arguments leading to an existential proof.

The main contributions of this paper are as follows:

(1)

We give two constructive proofs of the lifting theorem by Keshari and Mallick with an emphasis on the function-theoretic interpretation. See the forward directions of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.5
for the explicit constructive proofs.

(2)

We show by a concrete example (see Example
5.4
) that a
q
-commuting contractive pair may have two minimal
q
-commuting isometric lifts which are not unitarily equivalent.

(3)

We describe
all

q
-commuting isometric lifts of a given
q
-commuting contractive pair; See the converse directions of Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
4.5
for the descriptions in two different models.

(4)

In what follows, for a contraction operator
T
acting on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, we shall denote by

D_{T}=(I_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{D}}_{T}=\overline{\operatorname{Ran}D_{T}}

the
defect operator
and the
defect space
for
T
, respectively. The explicit functional model of the isometric lifts makes it possible to develop a functional model for pairs of
q
-commuting contractions. See the results in Section
5
. The functional model has three key components: the Sz.-Nagy–Foias
characteristic function

\Theta_{T}(z)=-T+zD_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}D_{T}|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{T}}:{\mathcal{D}}_{T}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}

for the product contraction
T=T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{2}T_{1}
, a
q
-commuting unitary pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
canonically constructed in Subsection
4.1
from a
q
-commuting contractive pair, and a novel notion of fundamental operators for
q
-commuting contractive pairs introduced in Subsection
5.3
. These three components constitute what is called a characteristic triple for a
q
-commuting contractive pair. It is shown in Theorem
5.9
that the characteristic triple serves as a complete unitary invariant for
q
-commuting contractive pairs whose product is completely non-unitary (
cnu
, for short).

(5)

We then introduce a notion of admissible triple
\Xi=((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
consisting of an operator pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
, a
q
-commuting unitary pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
and a contractive analytic function
\Theta
so that the characteristic triple for the model
q
-commuting contractive pair
(T_{1,\Xi},T_{2,\Xi})
corresponding to
\Xi
coincides with the admissible triple
\Xi
in an appropriate sense. In this way, we arrive at an exact analogue of Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory for contractive operators in the case of
q
-commuting contractive pairs.

There has been considerable interest in
q
-commuting operators from various perspectives within operator theory; see, for example,
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
19
]
. For further developments and generalizations to
Q
-commuting contractions, where
Q
is a unitary operator, we refer the reader to
[
5
]
,
[
13
]
, and the references therein.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with the preliminaries. Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of
q
-commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, and let
T=T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{1}T_{2}
. Then we have the following relations among the defect operators:

(2.1)

\displaystyle D_{T}^{2}=I-T^{*}T

\displaystyle=I-T_{2}^{*}T_{2}+T_{2}^{*}T_{2}-T_{2}^{*}T_{1}^{*}T_{1}T_{2}=D_{T_{2}}^{2}+T_{2}^{*}D_{T_{1}}^{2}T_{2}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=I-T_{1}^{*}T_{1}+T_{1}^{*}T_{1}-T_{1}^{*}T_{2}^{*}T_{2}T_{1}=D_{T_{1}}^{2}+T_{1}^{*}D_{T_{2}}^{2}T_{1}.

Then the map
\Lambda:\mathcal{D}_{T}\to\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T_{2}}
, defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Lambda D_{T}h=D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h\oplus D_{T_{2}}h\quad\text{for every }h\in{\mathcal{H}},

is an isometry as

\displaystyle\|\Lambda D_{T}h\|^{2}=\|D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h\|^{2}+\|D_{T_{2}}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|T_{2}h\|^{2}-\|T_{1}T_{2}h\|^{2}+\|h\|^{2}-\|T_{2}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|h\|^{2}-\|Th\|^{2}=\|D_{T}h\|^{2}.

Moreover, the map
U:\operatorname{Ran}\Lambda\to\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{2}}
, defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle U(D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h\oplus D_{T_{2}}h)=D_{T_{1}}h\oplus D_{T_{2}}T_{1}h\quad\text{for }h\in{\mathcal{H}},

is also an isometry as can be checked by a similar computation. If required, by adding a direct copy of
{\mathcal{H}}
to
{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{2}}
, we can extend
U
to a unitary operator on
{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}
, still denoted by
U
. The isometry and unitary operators defined above will play a pivotal role in what follows. We give them a name for reference.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a pair of
q
-commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, the tuple
({\mathcal{F}};\Lambda,P,U)
, as constructed above, is called a
special Andô tuple
for
(T_{1},T_{2})
, where
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{D}}_{T_{1}}\oplus{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{2}}\oplus{\mathcal{E}}
(with the understanding that
{\mathcal{E}}
may possible be trivial),
\Lambda:{\mathcal{D}}_{T}\to{\mathcal{F}}
is the isometry as in (
2.3
),
U
acting on
{\mathcal{F}}
is the unitary as defined in (
2.4
), and
P
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{F}
onto the first component, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}}\oplus\{0\}\oplus\{0\}
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a pair of
q
-commuting operators then so is
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. The special Andô tuple for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
will be denoted by
(\mathcal{F}_{*};\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
, where

\mathcal{F}_{*}=\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}^{*}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T_{2}^{*}}\quad\mbox{or}\quad{\mathcal{F}}_{*}=\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}^{*}}\oplus\mathcal{D}_{T_{2}^{*}}\oplus{\mathcal{H}},

\Lambda_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
is an isometry defined as:

\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}h=D_{T_{1}^{*}}T_{2}^{*}h\oplus D_{T_{2}^{*}}h\oplus 0\quad\mbox{for all }h\in\mathcal{H},

P_{*}
is the orthogonal projection of
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
onto the first component
\mathcal{D}_{T_{1}^{*}}\oplus\{0\}\oplus\{0\}
, and with a slight abuse of notation,
U_{*}
is the unitary extension on
{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
of the map
U_{*}:\operatorname{Ran}\Lambda_{*}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{*}
, explicitly defined as:

U_{*}(D_{T_{1}^{*}}T_{2}^{*}h\oplus D_{T_{2}^{*}}h\oplus 0)=D_{T_{1}^{*}}h\oplus D_{T_{2}^{*}}T_{1}^{*}h\oplus 0\quad\mbox{for all }h\in\mathcal{H}.

For Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
, we shall use the direct sum notations

{\mathcal{H}}\oplus{\mathcal{K}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}\end{bmatrix}

interchangeably; similar convention is used for an element in the direct sum, i.e., for vectors
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
k\in{\mathcal{K}}
, the notations
h\oplus k
and
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}h\\
k\end{smallmatrix}\right]
mean the same vector in the direct sum of
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
.

We note some properties of special Andô tuples below which are crucial for later developments.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a
q
-commuting pair of contractions on
{\mathcal{H}}
, and
({\mathcal{F}};\Lambda,P,U)
and
({\mathcal{F}}_{*};\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
be the special Andô tuples for
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
, respectively. Then we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&T_{1}^{*}T_{1}+D_{T}\Lambda^{*}U^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}=I_{{\mathcal{H}}},\quad T_{2}^{*}T_{2}+D_{T}\Lambda^{*}P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}=I_{{\mathcal{H}}}\\
&PU\Lambda D_{T}T_{2}+P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}=\Lambda D_{T}=U^{*}(P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}T_{1}+PU\Lambda D_{T});\end{cases}

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T_{1}^{*}=P_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}+P_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*}\\
&\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T_{2}^{*}=U_{*}^{*}P_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}+qU_{*}^{*}P_{*}^{\perp}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first equality in (
2.5
) follows from the simple inner product computation for every
h,h^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}
:

\displaystyle\langle D_{T}\Lambda^{*}U^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}h,h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle PU\Lambda D_{T}h,PU\Lambda D_{T}h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle D_{T_{1}}h\oplus 0,D_{T_{1}}h^{\prime}\oplus 0\rangle=\langle D_{T_{1}}^{2}h,h^{\prime}\rangle.

The second equality in (
2.5
) follows in a similar manner. For the third set of equalities in (
2.5
), we note that for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle PU\Lambda D_{T}T_{2}h+P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}h

\displaystyle=PU\begin{bmatrix}D_{T_{1}}T_{2}\\
D_{T_{2}}\end{bmatrix}T_{2}h+P^{\perp}\begin{bmatrix}D_{T_{1}}T_{2}\\
D_{T_{2}}\end{bmatrix}h

\displaystyle=P\begin{bmatrix}D_{T_{1}}\\
D_{T_{2}}T_{1}\end{bmatrix}T_{2}h+\begin{bmatrix}0\\
D_{T_{2}}\end{bmatrix}h

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}D_{T_{1}}T_{2}\\
D_{T_{2}}\end{bmatrix}h=\Lambda D_{T}h.

The other equality
\Lambda D_{T}=U^{*}\left(P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}T_{1}+PU\Lambda D_{T}\right)
follows from a similar computation.

For the set of equations in (
2.6
), we see that for
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle(P_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}+P_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*})h

\displaystyle=P_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}({D_{T_{1}^{*}}}T_{2}^{*}h\oplus{D_{T_{2}^{*}}}h)+P_{*}U_{*}({D_{T_{1}^{*}}}T_{2}^{*}T^{*}h\oplus{D_{T_{2}^{*}}}T^{*}h)

\displaystyle=(0\oplus D_{T_{2}^{*}}T_{1}^{*}h)+(D_{T_{1}^{*}}T^{*}h\oplus 0)

\displaystyle=D_{T_{1}^{*}}T_{2}^{*}T_{1}^{*}h\oplus D_{T_{2}^{*}}T_{1}^{*}h

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T_{1}^{*}h.

The other equality is as follows:

\displaystyle(U_{*}^{*}P_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}+qU_{*}^{*}P_{*}^{\perp}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*})h

\displaystyle=U^{*}_{*}P_{*}(D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}h\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}h)+qU^{*}_{*}P^{\perp}_{*}(D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}T^{*}h\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}h)

\displaystyle=U^{*}_{*}(D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}h\oplus 0)+qU^{*}_{*}(0\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}h)

\displaystyle=U^{*}_{*}(D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}h\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}_{1}T^{*}_{2}h)\quad[\mbox{since }qT^{*}=T_{1}^{*}T_{2}^{*}]

\displaystyle=D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}T^{*}_{2}h\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}_{2}h

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{2}h.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We need another preparatory lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a
q
-commuting pair of contractions on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and
T=T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{2}T_{1}
.

(1)

If
T
is an isometry, then so are both
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. The statement remains true even when ‘isometry’ is replaced by ‘unitary’.

(2)

For each
n\geq 1
,
T_{1}T^{n}=q^{n}T^{n}T_{1}
and
T_{2}T^{n}={\overline{q}}^{n}T^{n}T_{2}
.

Proof.

From equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we observe that for every vector
h
,

\|D_{T}h\|^{2}=\|D_{T_{1}}T_{2}h\|^{2}\oplus\|D_{T_{2}}h\|^{2}=\|D_{T_{1}}h\|^{2}+\|D_{T_{2}}T_{1}h\|^{2}.

This directly proves that if
T=T_{1}T_{2}
is an isometry, then so are
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
. To see the assertion about ‘unitary’, simply note that if
(T_{1},T_{2})
is
q
-commuting, then
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
is also
q
-commuting. This completes the proof of part (1).

The proof of part (2) is a routine computation using the
q
-commuting relation
T=T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{2}T_{1}
by succession.
∎

Note that by taking adjoints in part (2), we obtain the relations

(2.7)

\displaystyle T_{1}^{*}T^{*n}=q^{n}T^{*n}T_{1}^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{2}^{*}T^{*n}={\overline{q}}^{n}T^{*n}T_{2}^{*}.

This will be used as well.

2.1.
Models for
q
-commuting isometries

The following structure theorem, referred to as the Wold decomposition for isometries, attributed to Halmos
[
9
]
, von Neumann
[
18
]
and Wold
[
20
]
, is the stepping stone to the structure theory for Hilbert space operators.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
V
be an isometry on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then
V
can be identified with

\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})\\
\bigcap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}{\mathcal{H}}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})\\
\bigcap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}{\mathcal{H}}\end{bmatrix},

where
M_{z}:H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})\to H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})
is the shift operator
f\mapsto zf(z)
, and
W=V|_{\bigcap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}{\mathcal{H}}}
is the unitary operator. The identification above is via the unitary similarity
\tau:{\mathcal{H}}\to H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{u}
given as

\tau h=\begin{bmatrix}D_{V^{*}}(I-zV^{*})^{-1}h\\
\lim_{n\to\infty}V^{n}V^{*n}h\end{bmatrix}.

There have been a substantial amount of work done in order to find a model for tuples of commuting isometries. Perhaps the most prominent models is the one developed by Berger, Coburn, and Lebow in
[
6
]
. A similar model for
q
-commuting pair of isometries is recently found in
[
3
]
by J. Ball and H. Sau. We state the model for
q
-commuting setting. The substitution
q=1
in the model theorem below yields the model found by Berger, Coburn, and Lebow for the commuting case.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
be two operators acting on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
and
V=V_{1}V_{2}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

The pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
q
-commuting.

(2)

There exist Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}
, a projection
P
and a unitary
U
in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{F}})
,
and a pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
of
q
-commuting
unitaries in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}}_{u})
such that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to

(2.8)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}(R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U)+(M_{z}R_{q}\otimes PU)&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}(R_{\overline{q}}\otimes U^{*}P)+(R_{\overline{q}}M_{z}\otimes U^{*}P^{\perp})&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)

acting on
\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix},
where
R_{q}
is the rotation operator on
H^{2}
defined by

R_{q}(f)(z)=f(qz).

(3)

There exist Hilbert spaces
{\mathcal{F}}_{\dagger}
and
{\mathcal{K}}_{u{\dagger}}
, a projection
P_{\dagger}
and a unitary
U_{\dagger}
in

{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{F}}_{\dagger})
,
and a pair
(W_{1{\dagger}},W_{2{\dagger}})
of
q
-commuting unitaries in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}}_{u{\dagger}})
such that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to the pair

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}R_{q}\otimes U_{\dagger}^{*}P_{\dagger}^{\perp}+M_{z}R_{q}\otimes U_{\dagger}^{*}P_{\dagger}&0\\
0&W_{1{\dagger}}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{\overline{q}}\otimes P_{\dagger}U_{\dagger}+R_{\overline{q}}M_{z}\otimes P_{\dagger}^{\perp}U_{\dagger}&0\\
0&W_{2{\dagger}}\end{bmatrix}

acting on
\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}_{\dagger}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u{\dagger}}\end{bmatrix}
.

Moreover, in both items (2) and (3), the spaces can be chosen so that
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{\dagger}={\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}={\mathcal{K}}_{u{\dagger}}=\cap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}{\mathcal{H}}
, and the unitary identification map can be taken to be

(2.10)

\displaystyle\tau h=\begin{bmatrix}D_{V^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-zV^{*})^{-1}\\
\lim_{n}V^{n}V^{*n}\end{bmatrix}h,

where
V=V_{1}V_{2}
.

Note that the unitary identification map
\tau
as in (
2.10
) is exactly the unitary identification map behind the Wold decomposition for the isometry
V=V_{1}V_{2}
(see Theorem
2.5
).

The explicit functional model above makes it possible to jointly extend
q
-commuting isometric pairs to
q
-commuting unitary pairs with an additional structure as demonstrated in the result below. This will be used in what follows.

Theorem 2.7
.

Every
q
-commuting isometric pair
(X_{1},X_{2})
has a
q
-commuting unitary extension
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
such that
Y=Y_{1}Y_{2}
is the minimal unitary extension of
X=X_{1}X_{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, suppose the pair of
q
-commuting isometries
(X_{1},X_{2})
is given by the model obtained in (
2.8
). Consider the pair
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
defined as

\left(\begin{bmatrix}R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U+M_{\zeta}R_{q}\otimes PU&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\quad\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}\otimes U^{*}P+R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{\zeta}\otimes U^{*}P^{\perp}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\\
\text{on}\begin{bmatrix}L^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix},
where
M_{\zeta}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
is the unitary operator
f\mapsto\zeta f(\zeta)
.

It is straightforward to verify that
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
forms a pair of
q
-commuting unitary operators. By considering the natural embedding of
(H^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{u}
into
(L^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}})\oplus{\mathcal{K}}_{u}
through the map

\begin{bmatrix}z^{n}\otimes\xi\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}\zeta^{n}\otimes\xi\\
\eta\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{for}\quad\xi\in{\mathcal{F}},\;\eta\in{\mathcal{K}}_{u},\;n\geq 0,

we see that with
(Y_{1},Y_{2})
as set above,

Y=Y_{1}Y_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{\zeta}&0\\
0&W_{1}W_{2}\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}L^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}L^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix}

is the minimal unitary extension of the isometry

X=X_{1}X_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{1}W_{2}\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}_{u}\end{bmatrix}.

This was to be proved.
∎

A well-known result in operator theory is that any bounded operator that commutes with the shift operator
M_{z}
is of the form
M_{\varphi}
for some bounded analytic function
\varphi
. We conclude this section with an analogous result in the
q
-commuting setting. This will be used every now and then in what follows. It is convenient to have a definition first.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
T
be a bounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
and
q
be a unimodular complex number. The

q
-commutant
of
T
, denoted by
\{T\}^{\prime}_{q}
, is defined by

\{T\}^{\prime}_{q}=\{A\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{H}}):AT=qTA\}.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
M_{z}
be the shift operator on the vector valued Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
, for some Hilbert space
{\mathcal{E}}
. Then

\{M_{z}\}^{\prime}_{q}=\{M_{\varphi}R_{q}:\varphi\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{E}}))\}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that operators of the form
M_{\varphi}R_{q}

q
-commutes with
M_{z}
for any
\varphi
in
H^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{E}}))
.

To prove the converse, we shall use the short-hand notation

\widetilde{f}(z):=f(qz)\quad\mbox{for }f\in H^{2}({\mathcal{E}}).

Suppose
A
is a bounded operator on
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
so that
AM_{z}=qM_{z}A
. It readily implies
AM_{f(z)}=M_{f(qz)}A
for all polynomials
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
. Let us denote
\widetilde{f}(z)=f(qz)
. Given any
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
, define
\varphi
on
\mathbb{D}
by

\varphi(z)(e):=A(1\otimes e)(z).

Let
k
denote the reproducing kernel (also known as the Szëgo kernel)

k(z,w)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{w}z}

for the classical Hardy space
H^{2}
. It is well-known that for the vector-valued Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
, the reproducing kernel is given by
k\otimes I_{\mathcal{E}}
and that it has the reproducing property

(2.11)

\displaystyle\langle f,k_{z}\otimes e\rangle_{H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})}=\langle f(z),e\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}

for every
f\in H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
and
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
. Let us compute for every
e_{1},e_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}
,

\langle\varphi(z)e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\langle A(1\otimes e_{1})(z),e_{2}\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\langle A(1\otimes e_{1}),k_{z}\otimes e_{2}\rangle_{H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})}.

From this realization, it follows that
\varphi(z)
is a bounded operator for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
and the bound is given by
\|A\|\|k_{z}\|
. It also follows that
z\mapsto\varphi(z)
is a
\mathcal{B}({\mathcal{E}})
-valued holomorphic map. Indeed, it is enough to show that
z\mapsto\varphi(z)e
is holomorphic for every
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
. This can be seen from the very definition of
\varphi
since
A(1\otimes e)\in H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
and hence is holomorphic. Let us also note that for every polynomial
f\in\mathbb{C}[z]
,

A(f(z)e)=AM_{f}(1\otimes e)(z)=M_{\widetilde{f}}A(1\otimes e)(z)=\widetilde{f}(z)\varphi(z)(e)=\varphi(z)(\widetilde{f}(z)e).

Given any
f\in H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
, there exists a sequence of
{\mathcal{E}}
-valued polynomials
(f_{n})
such that
f_{n}\to f
in
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
as polynomials are dense in
H^{2}
. Since
A
is a bounded linear operator, it follows that
Af_{n}
converges to
Af
in
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
. We know that the norm convergence in
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
implies the uniform convergence on compact subsets of
{\mathbb{D}}
and hence, in particular, we have
f_{n}(z)\to f(z)
and
\widetilde{f_{n}}(z)=f_{n}(qz)\to\widetilde{f}(z)=f(qz)
in
{\mathcal{E}}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
\varphi(z)(\widetilde{f_{n}}(z))\to\varphi(z)(\widetilde{f}(z))
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
as the operator
\varphi(z
) is bounded for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Also since
\varphi(z)(\widetilde{f_{n}}(z))=A(f_{n})(z)\to Af(z)
in
{\mathcal{E}}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we conclude that

Af(z)=\varphi(z)\widetilde{f}(z)=\varphi(z)f(qz)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Thus what remains to show is that
\|\varphi(z)\|
is bounded by a constant independent of
z
in
{\mathbb{D}}
. To this end, we apply the conclusion of the preceding paragraph to the choice
f(\cdot)=\widetilde{k}_{qw}(\cdot)\otimes e
, where
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
i.e.,

A(k_{qw}\otimes e)(z)=\varphi(z)(\widetilde{k}_{qw}\otimes e)(z).

In particular, by substituting
w=z
in the above identity of vectors in
{\mathcal{E}}
, we obtain the following:

A(k_{qz}\otimes e)(z)=\varphi(z)(\widetilde{k}_{qz}\otimes e)(z).

We shall use this in the following computation. Since
\widetilde{k}_{qz}(z)
is real number, we see that for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
, and
e_{1},e_{2}\in{\mathcal{E}}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{k}_{qz}(z)|\langle\varphi(z)e_{1},e_{2}\rangle_{{\mathcal{E}}}|

\displaystyle=|\langle\varphi(z)(\widetilde{k}_{qz}\otimes e_{1})(z),e_{2}\rangle_{{\mathcal{E}}}|

\displaystyle=|\langle A(k_{qz}\otimes e_{1}),k_{z}\otimes e_{2}\rangle_{H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})}|\leq\|A\|\|e_{1}\|\|e_{2}\|\|k_{z}\|^{2}.

In the computation above, we used the reproducing property (
2.11
) for the functions
\widetilde{k}_{qz}
and the fact that
\|k_{qz}\|_{H^{2}}=\|k_{z}\|_{H^{2}}
. Finally, since
\widetilde{k}_{qz}(z)=\|k_{qz}\|^{2}=\|k_{z}\|^{2}
, it follows from the computation above that
\|\varphi(z)\|\leq\|A\|
for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
. Thus
M_{\varphi}
is a bounded operator on
H^{2}({\mathcal{E}})
and therefore we can write
A=M_{\varphi}R_{q}
.
∎

Finally, we shall need the following well known lemma every now and then. We refer the readers to
[
7
, Lemma 2.13]
(also see
[
2
, Lemma 2.3.1]
) for a proof.

Lemma 2.10
.

The only bounded linear operator intertwining a unitary
operator with a shift operator is the zero operator, i.e., if
{\mathcal{K}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
are Hilbert spaces,
U\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}})
is a unitary operator and
S\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{K}}^{\prime})
is a shift operator, i.e.,
S
is an isometry and
S^{*n}\to 0
strongly, and

G:{\mathcal{K}}\to{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}
is a bounded linear operator such that
GU=SG
, then
G=0
.

3.
A Schäffer-type model for isometric lifts

Given a vector-valued Hardy space
H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})
, we shall have use of the ‘evaluation at zero’ operator
\mathbf{ev}_{0}:H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\to{\mathcal{F}}
defined by

\mathbf{ev}_{0}:f\mapsto f(0).

In this section, we start with a
q
-commuting contractive pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
and construct a
q
-commuting isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
in a model form that is much similar to the Schäffer isometric lift

\begin{bmatrix}T&0\\
\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}D_{T}&M_{z}\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})\end{bmatrix}

for a contraction
T
acting on
{\mathcal{H}}
constructed in
[
17
]
. The function model Theorem
2.6
for
q
-commuting isometries makes it possible to also describe all isometric lifts of a given
q
-commuting contractive pair.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of
q
-commuting contractions acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Let
(\mathcal{F};\Lambda,P,U)
be a special Andô tuple associated with the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
as in Definition
2.1
. Consider the pair of operators
(V^{S}_{1},V^{S}_{2})
defined on
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\mathcal{H}\\
H^{2}(\mathcal{F}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&0\\
\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}&qM_{(P^{\perp}+zP)U}R_{q}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}&0\\
{\overline{q}}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}U^{*}P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}&{\overline{q}}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{U^{*}(P+zP^{\perp})}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Then
(V^{S}_{1},V^{S}_{2})
is a
q
-commuting isometric dilation of
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

Conversely, if
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a minimal
q
-commuting isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
acting on
{\mathcal{K}}
containing
{\mathcal{H}}
as a subspace, then there is an Andô tuple
({\mathcal{F}},\Lambda,P,U)
of
(T_{1},T_{2})
such that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to a pair as in (
3.1
).
Moreover, the operators
P,U
and
\Lambda
satisfy the relations (
2.5
).

Proof.

We begin by showing that
(V^{S}_{1},V^{S}_{2})
is
q
-commuting. To do this, we use the representation (
3.1
) to compute

\displaystyle V^{S}_{1}V^{S}_{2}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}T_{2}&0\\
\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}T_{2}+M_{P^{\perp}U+zPU}R_{q}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}U^{*}P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}&M_{z}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}T_{2}&0\\
\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}(PU\Lambda D_{T}T_{2}+P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T})&M_{z}\end{bmatrix}

and

\displaystyle V^{S}_{2}V^{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}T_{1}&0\\
{\overline{q}}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}U^{*}P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}T_{1}+{\overline{q}}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{U^{*}(P+zP^{\perp})}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}&R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{z}R_{q}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\overline{q}}T_{1}T_{2}&0\\
{\overline{q}}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}U^{*}(P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}T_{1}+PU\Lambda D_{T})&{\overline{q}}M_{z}\end{bmatrix}.

Therefore, based on the third set of equalities in (
2.5
), we can say that the pair
(V^{S}_{1},V^{S}_{2})
is
q
-commuting.

It remains to show that
V^{S}_{1}
and
V^{S}_{2}
are isometric operators. We observe that

\displaystyle V^{S*}_{1}V^{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}^{*}&D_{T}\Lambda^{*}U^{*}P\mathbf{ev}_{0}\\
0&{\overline{q}}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{(P^{\perp}+zP)U}^{*}R_{q}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&0\\
\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}&qM_{(P^{\perp}+zP)U}R_{q}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}^{*}T_{1}+D_{T}\Lambda^{*}U^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}&0\\
0&R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{(P^{\perp}+zP)U}^{*}M_{(P^{\perp}+zP)U}R_{q}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{\mathcal{H}}&0\\
0&I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{F})}\end{bmatrix}.

The final matrix equality in the above computation is derived by applying the first equation in (
2.5
). In a similar manner, applying the second equation in (
2.5
) gives
V_{2}^{S}
an isometry as well.

We now prove the converse direction. Since
(V_{1},V_{2})
acting on
{\mathcal{K}}
is a lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
acting on
{\mathcal{H}}
and
{\mathcal{K}}
contains
{\mathcal{H}}
as a subspace, we must have

(V_{1},V_{2})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&0\\
C_{1}&D_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}&0\\
C_{2}&D_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\mbox{ acting on }\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
{\mathcal{K}}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
q
-commuting isometric pair,
(D_{1},D_{2})
is also a
q
-commuting isometric pair. To represent the model in the desired form, we choose to work with the
q
-commuting pair
({\overline{q}}D_{1},qD_{2})
. By part (2) of Theorem
2.6
,
({\overline{q}}D_{1},qD_{2})
is jointly unitarily equivalent to

\displaystyle\left(\begin{bmatrix}(R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U)+(M_{z}R_{q}\otimes PU)&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}(R_{\overline{q}}\otimes U^{*}P)+(R_{\overline{q}}M_{z}\otimes U^{*}P^{\perp})&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Since our goal is to find a unitary copy of the original isometric lift, we assume that
({\overline{q}}D_{1},qD_{2})
is exactly equal to the pair above. Thus we are assuming that
{\mathcal{K}}\ominus{\mathcal{H}}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\\
{\mathcal{G}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Suppose
C_{j}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\\
{\mathcal{G}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
are given by the matrix forms

C_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{j1}\\
C_{j2}\end{bmatrix}

for each
j=1,2
. Since
W_{1},W_{2}
are unitary operators and
V_{1},V_{2}
are only contractive operators, we must have
C_{j2}=0
for each
j=1,2
. Thus we have the original isometric lift
(V_{1},V_{2})
unitarily equivalent to the pair

\left(\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&0&0\\
C_{11}&q\big[(R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U)+(M_{z}R_{q}\otimes PU)\big]&0\\
0&0&qW_{1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}&0&0\\
C_{21}&{\overline{q}}\big[(R_{\overline{q}}\otimes U^{*}P)+(R_{\overline{q}}M_{z}\otimes U^{*}P^{\perp})\big]&0\\
0&0&{\overline{q}}W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)

acting on
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\\
{\mathcal{G}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. Let us now use the fact that
(V_{1},V_{2})
is a minimal lift, i.e.,

\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\\
{\mathcal{G}}\end{bmatrix}=\bigvee_{n_{1}n_{2}\geq 0}V_{1}^{n_{1}}V_{2}^{n_{2}}\begin{bmatrix}h\\
0\\
0\end{bmatrix}.

Given the
3\times 3
block matrix representation of the pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
, minimality yields
{\mathcal{G}}=\{0\}
. Consequently, the minimal isometric lift
(V_{1},V_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to

\left(\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}&0\\
C_{11}&q[(R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U)+(M_{z}R_{q}\otimes PU)]\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}T_{2}&0\\
C_{21}&{\overline{q}}[(R_{\overline{q}}\otimes U^{*}P)+(R_{\overline{q}}M_{z}\otimes U^{*}P^{\perp})]\end{bmatrix}\right)

acting on
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}\\
H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})\end{smallmatrix}\right]
. It remains to argue that the operators
C_{11}
and
C_{21}
are as stated. Let us note that

V=V_{1}V_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}T_{1}T_{2}&0\\
C&M_{z}\end{bmatrix}

for some operator
C:{\mathcal{H}}\to H^{2}({\mathcal{F}})
. Since
V
is an isometry, we have

C^{*}C=I_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T\quad\mbox{and}\quad M_{z}^{*}C=0.

These two identities together gives

C=\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}\Lambda D_{T}

for some isometry
\Lambda:{\mathcal{D}}_{T}\to{\mathcal{F}}
. Since
V_{1},V_{2}
are isometries as well, we also have

V_{1}=V_{2}^{*}V_{2}V_{1}=\overline{q}V_{2}^{*}V\quad\mbox{and}\quad V_{2}=V_{1}^{*}V.

Equating the (21) entries in
V_{1}=\overline{q}V_{2}^{*}V
gives

\displaystyle C_{11}=\left((R_{q}\otimes PU)+(M_{z}^{*}R_{q}\otimes P^{\perp}U)\right)\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}\Lambda D_{T}=\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}PU\Lambda D_{T}.

Similar computation with the identity
V_{2}=V_{1}^{*}V
gives
C_{21}={\overline{q}}\mathbf{ev}_{0}^{*}U^{*}P^{\perp}\Lambda D_{T}
.
∎

4.
A Douglas-type model for isometric lifts

In this section we develop another functional model for isometric lifts of a
q
-commuting contractive pair. The model presented here is arguably more elegant than the one presented in Theorem
3.1
. Indeed, the functional model presented in this section is exactly in the model form presented in Theorem
2.6
and consequently, the isometric embedding of the abstract Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
into the model space is a non-trivial isometric embedding unlike the inclusion map in the model presented in Theorem
3.1
. Moreover, the model presented in this section will give a direct passage to further development of model theory for
q
-commuting contractive pairs as we shall see in the next section.

First, we associate a pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
of
q
-commuting unitaries to a
q
-commuting contractive pair and show that this association is canonical. To achieve this, we first recall briefly the construction of an isometric lift of a single contraction operator due to Douglas
[
8
]
. So let
T
be a contraction acting on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then the sequence
\{T^{n}T^{*n}\}
of positive semi-definite operators converges in
\mathcal{B(H)}
with respect to the Strong Operator Topology (
\operatorname{SOT}
). Define
Q_{T^{*}}
as the positive square root of the limit operator, that is,

(4.1)

\displaystyle Q_{T^{*}}^{2}:=\lim T^{n}T^{*n}.

From the definition of
Q_{T^{*}}
it follows that
TQ_{T^{*}}^{2}T^{*}=Q_{T^{*}}^{2}
, and so the operator
X^{*}:\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}\to\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
defined densely by

X^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h=Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}h\quad\text{for}\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}

is an isometry. Let
W^{*}_{D}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\supseteq\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
be the minimal unitary extension of
X^{*}
, i.e., the space
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}\{W_{D}^{n}\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}\}.

Consider next the operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
defined by

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}h=\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}D_{T^{*}}T^{*n}h=D_{T^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-zT^{*})^{-1}h\quad\text{for}\quad h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

It can be easily seen that the operator
\Pi_{D}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\oplus{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
defined by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Pi_{D}(h)=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}h\\
Q_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}h

is an isometry with the intertwining property

\Pi_{D}T^{*}=V_{D}^{*}\Pi_{D}.

All this amounts to the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
T
is a contraction acting on
{\mathcal{H}}
,
\Pi_{D}
is the isometry as in (
4.4
), and
V_{D}
is the isometry defined on

\mathcal{K_{D}}:=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}

by

V_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix},

then

V_{D}
is a minimal isometric lift of
T
via the embedding
\Pi_{D}
.

The minimality of the lift, i.e., the fact that

{\mathcal{K}}_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}^{n}&0\\
0&W_{D}^{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}\\
Q_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}{\mathcal{H}}

is a result of (
4.2
) and the following identity

(4.5)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}-M_{z}{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}T^{*}=D_{T^{*}}.

For more details, the readers are referred to the original paper
[
8
]
by Douglas and the recent monograph
[
2
, Chapter 2]
.

The goal of this section is to find a functional model for isometric lifts of a
q
-commuting contractive pair in this spirit of the Douglas’ form as in Theorem
4.1
. We need a pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
of
q
-commuting unitary operators canonically associated to a
q
-commuting contractive pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
, which would give us
W_{1}W_{2}=qW_{2}W_{1}=W_{D}
, where
W_{D}
is obtained as in the analysis above for the contraction operator
T=T_{1}T_{2}
.

4.1.
Canonical
q
-commuting unitary pair

Suppose that
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a
q
-commuting contractive pair acting on
{\mathcal{H}}
and
T=T_{1}T_{2}
. Let the positive semi-definite contraction
Q_{T^{*}}
be as in (
4.1
). We note by using part (2) of Lemma
2.4
that

\langle T_{i}Q_{T^{*}}^{2}T^{*}_{i}h,h\rangle=\langle\lim T_{i}T^{n}T^{*n}T_{i}^{*}h,h\rangle=q^{n}{\overline{q}}^{n}\langle\lim T^{n}T_{i}T^{*}_{i}T^{*n}h,h\rangle\leq\langle Q_{T^{*}}^{2}h,h\rangle

for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
and
i=1,2
. This gives way to the existence of contractions
X^{*}_{i}:\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}\to\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
defined densely by

(4.6)

\displaystyle X_{i}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h=Q_{T^{*}}T_{i}^{*}h\text{ for every }h\in{\mathcal{H}}.

The contractive operators
X_{1},X_{2}
have the following properties: for every
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle X_{1}^{*}X_{2}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h=X_{1}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}T_{2}^{*}h=Q_{T^{*}}T_{1}^{*}T_{2}^{*}h=qQ_{T^{*}}T^{*}h=qX^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h\text{ and}

\displaystyle X_{2}^{*}X_{1}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h=X_{2}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}T_{1}^{*}h=Q_{T^{*}}T_{2}^{*}T_{1}^{*}h=Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}=X^{*}Q_{T^{*}}h.

Thus
(X_{1}^{*},X_{2}^{*})
a
q
-commuting pair of contractions with their product
qX^{*}
being an isometry on
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
. Consequently by part (1) of Lemma
2.4
,
(X_{1}^{*},X_{2}^{*})
is a
q
-commuting pair of isometries. Applying Theorem
2.7
, we obtain a
q
-commuting pair
(W_{1}^{*},W_{2}^{*})
of unitaries such that
W_{1}^{*}W_{2}^{*}=qW_{D}^{*}
. Here we use the fact that
qW_{D}^{*}
is the minimal unitary extension of the isometry
qX^{*}
.

Definition 4.2
.

For a
q
-commuting pair of contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
, the corresponding
q
-commuting pair of unitaries
(W_{1},W_{2})
obtained as described above will be referred to as the
canonical
q
-commuting pair of unitaries
associated with
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

The canonicity is justified in the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
on
{\mathcal{H}}
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
on
\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
be two
q
-commuting pairs of contractions with their respective canonical
q
-commuting pair being
(W_{1},W_{2})
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
and
(W_{1}^{\prime},W_{2}^{\prime})
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{\prime*}}
. If
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
are unitarily equivalent, then so are
(W_{1},W_{2})
and
(W_{1}^{\prime},W_{2}^{\prime})
. In particular, if
(T_{1},T_{2})=(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
, then

(W_{1},W_{2})=(W_{1}^{\prime},W_{2}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Let
\psi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H^{\prime}}
be a unitary operator that intertwines
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
. As before, define
T=T_{1}T_{2}
and
T^{\prime}=T_{1}^{\prime}T_{2}^{\prime}
. Let
Q_{T^{*}}
and
Q_{{T^{\prime}}^{*}}
be the positive semi-definite contractions, as described in (
4.1
), associated with
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
, respectively. It is clear that
\psi
intertwines
Q_{T^{*}}
and
Q_{{T^{\prime}}^{*}}
, and therefore
\psi
maps
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
onto
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{{T^{\prime}}^{*}}}
. We denote the restriction of
\psi
to
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
by
\psi
itself. Let
(X_{1},X_{2})
act on
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
and
(X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime})
act on
\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{{T^{\prime}}^{*}}}
be the
q
-commuting pairs of co-isometries corresponding to the pairs
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
, as mentioned at the beginning of this section. It is straightforward to verify from the definition that

\psi(X_{1},X_{2})=(X_{1}^{\prime},X_{2}^{\prime})\psi.

Recall that
W_{D}^{*}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
is the minimal unitary extension of the isometry
X^{*}
as defined in (
4.6
). Therefore,

{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}=\overline{\text{span}}\{W_{D}^{n}x:x\in\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}\text{ and }n\geq 0\}.

Given the analogous representation of
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{\prime*}}
, we define
\tau_{\psi}:{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{\prime*}}
by

(4.7)

\displaystyle\tau_{\psi}:W_{D}^{n}x\mapsto W_{D}^{\prime n}\psi x,\quad\text{for all }x\in\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}\text{ and }n\geq 0.

This map is then extended linearly and continuously. It is clear that
\tau_{\psi}
is a unitary operator that intertwines
W_{D}
and
W_{D}^{\prime}
.

For
x\in\overline{\operatorname{Ran}Q_{T^{*}}}
and
n\geq 0
, we have the following:

\displaystyle\tau_{\psi}W_{1}(W_{D}^{n}x)=q\tau_{\psi}W_{D}^{n}(W_{1}x)=q\tau_{\psi}W_{D}^{n+1}(W_{2}^{*}x)=qW_{D}^{\prime n+1}\psi(X_{2}^{*}x)

\displaystyle=qW_{D}^{\prime n+1}W_{2}^{\prime*}\psi x

\displaystyle=W_{1}^{\prime}W_{D}^{\prime n}\psi x

\displaystyle=W_{1}^{\prime}\tau_{\psi}(W_{D}^{n}x).

A similar calculation shows that
\tau_{\psi}
also intertwines
W_{2}
and
W_{2}^{\prime}
.
Note that if, in the above theorem, we assume
(T_{1},T_{2})=(T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime})
, then we have
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}={\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{\prime*}}
,
\psi=I_{\mathcal{H}}
, and
\tau_{\psi}=I_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}}
.
∎

The following is a direct consequence of Theorem
4.3
.

Corollary 4.4
.

For a
q
-commuting pair of contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
, let
Q_{T^{*}}
be the positive semi-definite contraction and
W_{D}
be the unitary, as discussed earlier. Suppose
(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})
is a pair of
q
-commuting unitaries such that for each
j=1,2
,

{W^{\prime}_{j}}^{*}Q_{T^{*}}=Q_{T^{*}}T_{j}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad{W^{\prime}_{1}}^{*}{W^{\prime}_{2}}^{*}=q{W}_{D}^{*}.

Then
(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})=(W_{1},W_{2})
.

4.2.
A Douglas-type model for isometric lifts

We now have all the necessary tools to derive the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of
q
-commuting contractions on
{\mathcal{H}}
. Let
(W_{1},W_{2})
be the canonical
q
-commuting pair of unitary operators for
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
be a special Andô tuple for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
(see Definition
2.1
). Consider the pair of operators

(4.8)

\displaystyle(V^{D}_{1},V^{D}_{2})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{U_{*}^{*}(P_{*}^{\perp}+zP_{*})}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{(P_{*}+zP_{*}^{\perp})U_{*}}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\quad\mbox{on}\quad\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
\Pi^{D}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
be defined by

(4.9)

\displaystyle\Pi^{D}h:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H^{2}}\otimes\Lambda_{*}&0\\
0&I_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}}\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{D}h=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*n}h\\
Q_{T^{*}}h\end{bmatrix}.

Then
\Pi^{D}
is an isometry and
(\Pi^{D};V^{D}_{1},V^{D}_{2})
is a
q
-commuting isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

Conversely, if
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
acting on
{\bm{\mathcal{K}}}
is any minimal isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
via an isometric embedding
{\bm{\Pi}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{K}}}
, then there is an Andô tuple
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},P_{*},\Lambda_{*},U_{*})
so that
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to a pair of the form as in (
4.8
), and the isometric embedding
{\bm{\Pi}}
must be of the form as in (
4.9
).

Proof.

The operator
\Pi^{D}
as in (
4.9
) is an isometry since it is the product of two isometries. It is clear from Theorem
2.6
that
(V^{D}_{1},V^{D}_{2})
forms a pair of
q
-commuting isometries on
H^{2}(\mathcal{F}_{*})\oplus{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
. Therefore, it suffices to show that the above pair lifts
(T_{1},T_{2})
, meaning that
{V_{j}^{D}}^{*}\Pi^{D}=\Pi^{D}T_{j}^{*}
for all
j=1,2
. For all
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle V^{D*}_{1}\Pi^{D}h

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}M_{U^{*}_{*}P^{\perp}_{*}+zU^{*}_{*}P_{*}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\Pi^{D}h

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}R_{\bar{q}}\otimes P^{\perp}_{*}U_{*}+R_{\bar{q}}M_{z}^{*}\otimes P_{*}U_{*}&0\\
0&W_{1}^{*}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}h\\
Q_{T^{*}}h\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n\geq 0}\bar{q}^{n}z^{n}P^{\perp}_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}h+\sum_{n\geq 1}\bar{q}^{n}z^{n-1}P_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}h\\
W^{*}_{1}Q_{T^{*}}h\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n\geq 0}\bar{q}^{n}z^{n}(P^{\perp}_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}+P_{*}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*}){T^{*}}^{n}h\\
W^{*}_{1}Q_{T^{*}}h\end{bmatrix}.

It follows from (
2.6
), (
4.6
) and Lemma
2.4
that the above expression is same as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n\geq 0}\bar{q}^{n}z^{n}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{1}{T^{*}}^{n}h\\
Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{1}h\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n\geq 0}\bar{q}^{n}{q}^{n}z^{n}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}T^{*}_{1}h\\
Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{1}h\end{bmatrix}=\Pi^{D}T^{*}_{1}h.

Proceeding in a similar manner by combining (
2.6
), (
4.6
), and Lemma
2.4
gives the intertwining
V_{2}^{D*}\Pi^{D}=\Pi^{D}T_{2}^{*}
.

We now prove the converse direction. The first step is to apply part (3) of Theorem
2.6
: there exist a projection-unitary pair
(P_{*},U_{*})
in
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}}_{V^{*}})
, and a
q
-commuting unitary pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
acting on
\cap_{n\geq 0}V^{n}{\mathcal{K}}
so that the original isometric lift
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
is jointly unitarily equivalent to

\displaystyle(V_{1},V_{2})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{U_{*}^{*}P_{*}^{\perp}+zU_{*}^{*}P_{*}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{\overline{q}}M_{P_{*}U_{*}+zP_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)\quad\mbox{ on }\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}

via the unitary identification map

\tau_{\rm W}:k\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}D_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{K}}-z{\textbf{V}}^{*})^{-1}\\
Q_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}k.

Since we are interested in finding a unitary copy of the original isometric lift
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
, we assume without loss of generality that the isometric lift is
(V_{1},V_{2})
as in the
2\times 2
block matrix form above and the isometric embedding
\Pi
of
{\mathcal{H}}
is now given by

(4.10)

\displaystyle\Pi=\tau_{\rm W}{\bm{\Pi}}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{V^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{K}}-zV^{*})^{-1}\\
Q_{V^{*}}\end{bmatrix}{\bm{\Pi}}.

Let us note that with
W=W_{1}W_{2}
,

(4.11)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{1}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}&0\\
0&W\end{bmatrix}^{n}\Pi{\mathcal{H}}\subset\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}

is a minimal isometric lift of
T=T_{1}T_{2}
. Since any two minimal isometric lifts of a single contraction operator are unitarily equivalent (see e.g.,
[
10
]
, Chapter I, Theorem
4.1
), there is a unitary operator

\tau:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{1}

such that

(4.12)

\displaystyle\tau\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}&0\\
0&W\end{bmatrix}\tau\quad\mbox{and}\quad

(4.13)

\displaystyle\tau\Pi_{D}=\tau\begin{bmatrix}D_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}\\
Q_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}(I-z{\textbf{V}}^{*})^{-1}\\
Q_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}{\bm{\Pi}}=\Pi.

Let us view
\tau
as an isometry from
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\to\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
with
\operatorname{Ran}\tau={\mathcal{K}}_{1}
. Let us write

\tau=\begin{bmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}\end{bmatrix}.

Then from equation (
4.12
) we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle AM_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}}=M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}A,\quad BD_{D}=M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}B,\quad CM_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}}=WC,\quad\mbox{and}\quad DW_{D}=WD.

By Lemma
2.10
,
B=0
. This together with (
4.13
) gives

\displaystyle AD_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}=D_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}(I-z{\textbf{V}}^{*})^{-1}{\bm{\Pi}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad

(4.15)

\displaystyle CD_{T^{*}}(I-zT^{*})^{-1}+DQ_{T^{*}}=Q_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}{\bm{\Pi}}.

The first of the two equations above, the first intertwining in (
4.14
) and the identity (
4.5
) gives

\displaystyle AD_{T^{*}}=D_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}{\bm{\Pi}}.

This shows that
A
takes the space of constant functions of
H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})
into that of
H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})
. This and the intertwining
AM_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}}=M_{z}^{{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}A
together forces

to be of the form

A=I_{H^{2}}\otimes\Lambda_{*}

for some isometry
\Lambda_{*}:{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}
. Thus the isometry
\tau
has the property that

A=P_{H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}})}\tau|_{H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})}

is also an isometry. This forces

C=P_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}\tau|_{H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})}=0.

Consequently, we have

\tau=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H^{2}}\otimes\Lambda_{*}&0\\
0&D\end{bmatrix}

for some isometries
\Lambda_{*}:{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}
and
\omega:{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}
.

Since
\operatorname{Ran}{\tau}={\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, from the expression (
4.11
) of the space
{\mathcal{K}}_{1}
and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ran}D=P_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}{\mathcal{K}}_{1}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}W^{n}\Pi{\mathcal{H}}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}W^{n}Q_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}{\bm{\Pi}}{\mathcal{H}}.

The last intertwining in (
4.14
) is same as
W^{*}D=DW_{D}^{*}
, and therefore
\operatorname{Ran}D
, which contains
P_{{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}}\Pi{\mathcal{H}}
as a subspace, must be reducing for
W
. We started with a minimal isometric lift
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
and so we must have

\operatorname{Ran}D={\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}},

in other words,
D:{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}_{{\textbf{V}}^{*}}
must be a unitary.

∎

Remark 4.6
.

and

.

4.3.
A Sz.-Nagy–Foias-type model for isometric lifts

Here we develop yet another type of functional model for the minimal isometric lifts of a
q
-commuting contractive pair but in the spirit of the functional model developed by Sz.-Nagy and Foias
[
10
]
for single contractions. We begin by reviewing the work of Sz.-Nagy and Foias.

Given a contraction
T
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, consider the map
\Theta_{T}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T},\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})
defined by

(4.17)

\displaystyle\Theta_{T}(z)=\left[-T+zD_{T^{*}}\left(I_{\mathcal{H}}-zT^{*}\right)^{-1}D_{T}\right]|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{T}}:{\mathcal{D}}_{T}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}\quad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{D}.

The well-definedness and the analyticity of the map
\Theta_{T}
is a consequence of the convergence of the Neumann series and the relation between the defect operators

TD_{T}=D_{T^{*}}T.

As demonstrated in
[
10
, Chapter VI]
, it turns out that
\Theta_{T}(z)
is a contraction for each
z
in
\mathbb{D}
and that it is purely contractive, i.e.,

\|\Theta_{T}(0)f\|<\|f\|\quad\mbox{ for all}\quad 0\neq f\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T}.

The
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}}_{T},{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})
-valued analytic function
\Theta_{T}
is referred to as the
characteristic function
for
T
. The characteristic functions is a unitary invariant for
cnu
contractions in the sense that
two cnu contractions
T,T^{\prime}
are unitarily equivalent to if and only if their characteristic functions
\Theta_{T}
and
\Theta_{T^{\prime}}
coincide, i.e., there are unitary operators
u:{\mathcal{D}}_{T}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}
and
u_{*}:{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime}}\to{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime*}}
such that

(4.18)

\displaystyle u_{*}\Theta_{T}(z)=\Theta_{T^{\prime}}(z)u\quad\mbox{for all}\quad z\in{\mathbb{D}}.

Given a contractive operator
T
, we associate the
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})
-valued function
\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}
, which is defined almost everywhere on the unit circle
\mathbb{\mathbb{T}}
by

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}(\zeta):=(I-\Theta_{T}(\zeta)^{*}\Theta_{T}(\zeta))^{1/2}.

Here
\Theta_{T}(\zeta)
is defined as the radial limit

\Theta_{T}(\zeta)=\lim_{r\to 1-}\Theta(r\zeta)\quad\mbox{for }\zeta\in{\mathbb{\mathbb{T}}}

of the characteristic function; it is known (see
[
10
, Chapter V]
) that the radial limit exists almost everywhere in
\mathbb{T}
for any bounded operator-valued analytic function.

It is possible to find a functional model for isometric lifts of a cnu contraction
T
involving the characteristic function for
T
. Let
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
be the Hilbert space as appeared in (
4.2
) for the analysis of Douglas model for isometric lifts. It is known (see for example
[
2
, Chapter 2]
) that there is a unitary
\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}:{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\to\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}
so that the following intertwining holds:

(4.20)

\displaystyle\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}\cdot W_{D}=M_{\zeta}|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}}\cdot\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}.

Consider the isometric embedding

\Pi_{\rm NF}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}

defined by

(4.21)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\rm NF}:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})}&0\\
0&\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}D_{T^{*}}(I_{\mathcal{H}}-zT^{*})^{-1}\\
\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}Q_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}

and the isometric operator

(4.22)

\displaystyle V_{\rm NF}:=\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&M_{\zeta}|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}}\end{bmatrix}:\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}.

The following functional model for isometric lifts of a single contractive operator is due to Sz.-Nagy and Foias. We arrive at this by the discussion above and the fact that
(\Pi_{D},\left[\begin{smallmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{smallmatrix}\right])
is a minimal isometric lift acting on the space
{\mathcal{K}}_{D}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
.

Theorem 4.8

(Sz.-Nagy and Foias)
.

Let
T
be a cnu contraction on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Then the isometric operator
V_{\rm NF}
as in (
4.22
) is a minimal isometric lift of
T
acting on

(4.23)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}:=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}

via the embedding
\Pi_{\rm NF}
as in (
4.21
), i.e.,

(4.24)

\displaystyle V_{\rm NF}^{*}\Pi_{\rm NF}=\Pi_{\rm NF}T^{*}.

With
(W_{1},W_{2})
as the canonical
q
-commuting unitary pair as in Definition
4.2
and the unitary operator
\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}
as in (
4.20
), let us define

(4.25)

\displaystyle W^{\textrm{NF}}_{i}:=\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}W_{i}\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}^{*}\quad\text{for}\quad i=1,2.

Then we have the following.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(\!T_{1},T_{2}\!)
be a pair of
q
-commuting contractions on
{\mathcal{H}}
with the product operator
T=T_{1}T_{2}
being a cnu contraction. Let
(W^{\textrm{NF}}_{1},W^{\textrm{NF}}_{2})
be as defined in (
4.25
) and
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
be a special Andô tuple for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. Consider the pair of operators

(4.26)

\displaystyle(V^{\rm{NF}}_{1},V^{\rm{NF}}_{2})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{U_{*}^{*}(P_{*}^{\perp}+zP_{*})}R_{q}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{(P_{*}+zP_{*}^{\perp})U_{*}}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)

on
\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}
and let
\Pi^{\textrm{NF}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
be defined by

(4.27)

\displaystyle\Pi^{\textrm{NF}}h:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H^{2}}\otimes\Lambda_{*}&0\\
0&I_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}}\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{\rm{NF}}h=\begin{bmatrix}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}T^{*n}h\\
\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}Q_{T^{*}}h\end{bmatrix}.

Then
\Pi^{\textrm{NF}}
is an isometry and
(\Pi^{\textrm{NF}};V^{\rm{NF}}_{1},V^{\rm{NF}}_{2})
is a
q
-commuting isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

Conversely, if
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
acting on
{\bm{\mathcal{K}}}
is any minimal isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
via an isometric embedding
{\bm{\Pi}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{K}}}
, then there is an Andô tuple
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},P_{*},\Lambda_{*},U_{*})
so that
({\textbf{V}}_{1},{\textbf{V}}_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to a pair of the form as in (
4.26
), and the isometric embedding
{\bm{\Pi}}
must be of the form as in (
4.27
).

Proof.

Let us consider the unitary operator
\tau:\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\to\left[\begin{smallmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{F}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
defined by

\tau h=\begin{bmatrix}I&0\\
0&\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}\end{bmatrix}h.

Then it follows that
\Pi^{\textrm{NF}}=\tau\Pi^{D}

and
V^{\rm{NF}}_{i}=\tau V^{D}_{i}\tau^{*}
for all
i=1,2
. From the forward direction of the Theorem
4.5
, we conclude that
(\Pi^{\textrm{NF}};V^{\rm{NF}}_{1},V^{\rm{NF}}_{2})
is a
q
-commuting isometric lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. Thus
(\Pi^{\textrm{NF}};V^{\rm{NF}}_{1},V^{\rm{NF}}_{2})
and
(\Pi^{D};V^{D}_{1},V^{D}_{2})
are two unitarily equivalent
q
-commuting isometric lifts of
(T_{1},T_{2}).

The converse follows from the equivalance of the isometric lifts
(\Pi^{\textrm{NF}};V^{\rm{NF}}_{1},V^{\rm{NF}}_{2})
and
(\Pi^{D};V^{D}_{1},V^{D}_{2})
of the
q
-commutative contractive pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
and the converse of the Theorem
4.5
.
∎

5.
A Sz.-Nagy–Foias-type functional model

Let
T
be a cnu contraction acting on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
,
{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}
be the space as in (
4.23
), and
\Pi_{\rm{NF}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}
be the isometry as in (
4.21
). It was shown in
[
10
, Chapter VI]
(see also
[
2
, Chapter 2]
) that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{\rm{NF}}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}\ominus\begin{bmatrix}\Theta_{T}\\
\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}\end{bmatrix}\cdot H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})=:{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}.

Therefore, what follows is the functional model for a cnu contraction operator due to Sz.-Nagy and Foias. Indeed, a direct consequence of the intertwining (
4.24
), it follows that the cnu contraction
T
is unitarily equivalent to

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&M_{\zeta}\end{bmatrix}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}}

via the unitary operator
\Pi_{\rm{NF}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}=\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{\rm{NF}}
.

We shall denote by
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*},\Theta)
a bounded analytic function
\Theta:\mathbb{D}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*})
. The function
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*},\Theta)
is said to be
purely contractive
if
\Theta(z)
is a contraction for every
z\in{\mathbb{D}}
and furthermore,
\|\Theta(0)f\|<\|f\|
for every non-zero vector
f
. Given a purely contractive analytic function
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*},\Theta)
, consider the space

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\end{bmatrix}\ominus\begin{bmatrix}\Theta\\
\Delta_{\Theta}\end{bmatrix}\cdot H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}),

where
\Delta_{\Theta}
is the same operator as
\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}
with
\Theta_{T}
replaced by
\Theta
. Then with the contraction operator
T_{\Theta}
on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}
defined by

(5.3)

\displaystyle T_{\Theta}=P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&M_{\zeta}\end{bmatrix}|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}},

it is known (see
[
10
, Theorem VI.3.1]
) that the characteristic function
\Theta_{T_{\Theta}}
of the contraction
T_{\Theta}
coincides with
\Theta
in the sense described in (
4.18
). The goal of this section is to develop a parallel theory for
q
-commuting contractive pairs. This develops in steps.

5.1.
The fundamental operators

A key role in obtaining the functional model is played by the operators guaranteed to exist in the result below.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of
q
-commuting contractions, there is an (in general non-commuting) operator pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
acting on
{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}
so that the following operator equations are satisfied:

(5.4)

\displaystyle D_{T^{*}}G_{1}D_{T^{*}}=T^{*}_{1}-T_{2}T^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad D_{T^{*}}G_{2}D_{T^{*}}=T^{*}_{2}-qT_{1}T^{*}.

Moreover, if
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
is an Andô tuple for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
, then

(5.5)

\displaystyle(G_{1},G_{2}):=(\Lambda_{*}^{*}P_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}\Lambda_{*},\Lambda_{*}^{*}U_{*}^{*}P_{*}\Lambda_{*}).

Proof.

Once the existence is ensured, the uniqueness of the solutions
G_{1},G_{2}
is trivial. We prove the rest of the assertion in one stroke. Suppose
({\mathcal{F}}_{*},\Lambda_{*},P_{*},U_{*})
is a special Andô tuple for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
, which is shown to exist in the discussion preceding Definition
2.1
. Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be as in (
5.5
). Then for every
h,h^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, we compute

\displaystyle\langle D_{T^{*}}G_{1}D_{T^{*}}h,h^{\prime}\rangle=\langle P_{*}^{\perp}U_{*}\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}h,\Lambda_{*}D_{T^{*}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 0\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}_{1}h,D_{T^{*}_{1}}T^{*}_{2}h^{\prime}\oplus D_{T^{*}_{2}}h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle D^{2}_{T^{*}_{2}}T^{*}_{1}h,h^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(T^{*}_{1}-T_{2}T^{*})h,h^{\prime}\rangle.

A similar computation, utilizing the properties of the Andô tuple, establishes the second equality.
∎

Considering the heavy role of the operators
G_{1},G_{2}
in what follows, it is convenient to give them a name for quick reference.

Definition 5.2
.

The unique pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
associated to a
q
-commuting contractive pair as in Theorem
5.1
will be called the
fundamental operators
for
(T^{*}_{1},T^{*}_{2})
.

The first main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a
q
-commuting contractive pair with
(G_{1},G_{2})
the fundamental operator pair for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. Let
T=T_{1}T_{2}
be a cnu contraction and
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}
be the space as in (
5.1
). Then
(T_{1},T_{2})
is unitarily equivalent to

P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right)|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}}

via the map
\Pi_{\rm NF}:{\mathcal{H}}\to\operatorname{Ran}\Pi_{\rm NF}
, where
\Pi_{\rm NF}
is as in (
4.21
).

Proof.

Let us consider the operator

U_{\textrm{NF}}=\begin{bmatrix}I_{H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})}&0\\
0&\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}\end{bmatrix},

where the unitary
\omega_{\rm{D,NF}}
is as discussed in the paragraph leading to (
4.20
). Then,
U_{\textrm{NF}}
is a unitary operator and satisfies the following relations:

(5.6)

\displaystyle U_{\textrm{NF}}V_{D}=V_{\textrm{NF}}U_{\textrm{NF}}\quad\text{and}\quad U_{\textrm{NF}}\Pi_{D}=\Pi_{\text{NF}}.

In accordance with the definition of
(G_{1},G_{2})
provided in (
5.4
), Corollary
4.7
yields the following intertwining relations:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Pi_{D}T_{1}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\Pi_{D}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{D}T_{2}^{*}=\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{*}_{2}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\Pi_{D}.

Then the following intertwining relations are derived by applying (
5.6
) and (
5.7
).

(5.8)

\displaystyle\Pi_{\text{NF}}T^{*}_{1}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{1}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\Pi_{\text{NF}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{\text{NF}}T^{*}_{2}=\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{*}_{2}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W^{\textrm{NF}}_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\Pi_{\text{NF}}.

The relations in (
5.8
) complete the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.2.
Pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift

One major advantage of the isometric lift for a single contraction is that minimal isometric lifts are unique up to isomorphism. In contrast to the classical case, a pair of
q
-commuting contractions may have two minimal isometric lifts that are not unitarily equivalent. The following example highlights this difficulty.

Example 5.4
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})=(0,0)
on
\mathbb{C}
. Then consider two pairs of
q
-commuting isometries
(R_{q}M_{z},M_{z})
and
(R_{q}M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}})
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
, respectively. Then it is easy to see that both the pairs are minimal
q
-commuting isometric lifts of
(T_{1},T_{2})
, where the corresponding standard inclusion serves as the isometric embedding. However, they are not unitarily equivalent, since the second pair is doubly
q
-commuting i.e.,
M_{z_{2}}(R_{q}M_{z_{1}})^{*}=q(R_{q}M_{z_{1}})^{*}M_{z_{2}}
, whereas the first pair is not.

To remedy this situation, we introduce the notion of a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift. It then follows that this notion of isometric lift serves as a correct analogue to the classical case, as we show that any two pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lifts of a pair of
q
-commuting contractions are unitarily equivalent. This will be useful in developing the functional model.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
({W}_{1},{W}_{2},{W})
be a triple of operators on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{K}}
. We say that
({W}_{1},{W}_{2},{W})
is a
pseudo
q
-commuting contractive operator triple
if:

i)

{W}_{1}
and
{W}_{2}
are contractions, and
{W}
is an isometry.

ii)

({W}_{1},{W})
and
({W}_{2},{W})
are
q
-commuting and
{\overline{q}}
-commuting, respectively.

iii)

{W}_{1}={\overline{q}}{W}_{2}^{*}{W}
.

Furthermore, we say that a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple
({W}_{1},{W}_{2},{W})
is a lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T_{1}T_{2})
via an isometric embedding
\Pi:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{K}}
if

i)

(\Pi,{W}_{1},{W}_{2},{W})
is a lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T=T_{1}T_{2})
, i.e.,

\Pi(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*},T_{2}^{*}T_{1}^{*})=({W}_{1}^{*},{W}_{2}^{*},{W}^{*})\Pi.

ii)

(\Pi,{W})
is a minimal isometric lift for
T=T_{1}T_{2}
, i.e.,

{\mathcal{K}}=\bigvee_{n\geq 0}{W}^{n}\operatorname{Ran}\Pi.

Theorem 5.6

(Douglas-model pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift)
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a pair of
q
-commuting contractions on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Consider the Douglas isometric-lift model space
\mathcal{K}_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\\
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}
for
T
and the Douglas isometric embedding
\Pi_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}},T^{*}}\\
Q_{T^{*}}\end{bmatrix}
. Define the operators
\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1}
,
\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2}
, and
V_{D}
on
\mathcal{K}_{D}
as follows:

(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}\right),

where
(G_{1},G_{2})
are the fundamental operators of
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
, and
(W_{1},W_{2})
is the canonical
q
-commuting unitary pair. Then,
(\Pi_{D},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. Furthermore, any pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of the form
(\Pi_{D},\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
satisfies the property that
(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2})=(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2})
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})
forms a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple on
{\mathcal{K}}_{D}
. It is evident that
\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2}
are contractions, while
V_{D}
is an isometry. The
q
-commutativity of the pair
(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},V_{D})
holds as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1}V_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{1}W_{D}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=q\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}M_{z}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{D}W_{1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=q\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=qV_{D}\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1}.

A similar computation shows that
(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})
is
{\overline{q}}
-commuting. The third condition of the definition can be derived as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}^{D*}_{2}V_{D}=\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}^{*}\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}M^{*}_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}R_{q}M_{z}&0\\
0&W^{*}_{2}W_{D}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}qM^{*}_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}M_{z}R_{q}&0\\
0&W^{*}_{2}W_{1}W_{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=q\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=q\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1}.

Hence,
(\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple on
{\mathcal{K}}_{D}
. Then it follows from Corollary
4.7
that
(\Pi_{D},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{1},\mathbb{W}^{D}_{2},V_{D})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

To prove the second part, let
(\Pi_{D},\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
be a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. By definition,
(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple on
\mathcal{K}_{D}
. We express
\mathbb{W}_{1}
and
\mathbb{W}_{2}
as
2\times 2
block matrices with respect to the decomposition
\mathcal{K}_{D}=H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\oplus{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
, as follows:

\mathbb{W}_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{W}_{j,11}&\mathbb{W}_{j,12}\\
\mathbb{W}_{j,21}&\mathbb{W}_{j,22}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{for }j=1,2.

From Definition
5.5
of a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple, we derive the following:

The
q
-commutativity of the pair
(\mathbb{W}_{1},V_{D})
gives the relation

(5.9)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{W}_{1,11}M_{z}&\mathbb{W}_{1,12}W_{D}\\
\mathbb{W}_{1,21}M_{z}&\mathbb{W}_{1,22}W_{D}\end{bmatrix}=q\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}\mathbb{W}_{1,11}&M_{z}\mathbb{W}_{1,12}\\
W_{D}\mathbb{W}_{1,21}&W_{D}\mathbb{W}_{1,22}\end{bmatrix}.

Focusing on the
(1,2)
-entry of this matrix identity, we obtain

\mathbb{W}_{1,12}{\overline{q}}W_{D}=M_{z}\mathbb{W}_{1,12}.

This shows that the bounded operator
\mathbb{W}_{1,12}
intertwines the unitary operator
{\overline{q}}W_{D}
with the unilateral shift
M_{z}
. Then by Lemma
2.10
, it follows that
\mathbb{W}_{1,12}=0
. An analogous argument, using the
{\overline{q}}
-commutativity condition of the pair
(\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
, yields
\mathbb{W}_{2,12}=0
.

Applying the third condition,
\mathbb{W}_{1}={\overline{q}}\mathbb{W}^{*}_{2}V_{D}
, from Definition
5.5
, we obtain:

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{W}_{1,11}&0\\
\mathbb{W}_{1,21}&\mathbb{W}_{1,22}\end{bmatrix}={\overline{q}}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{W}^{*}_{2,11}M_{z}&\mathbb{W}^{*}_{2,21}W_{D}\\
0&\mathbb{W}^{*}_{2,22}W_{D}\end{bmatrix}.

Upon compairing the entries at
(2,1)
and
(1,2)
, we conclude that
\mathbb{W}_{1,21}=0
and
\mathbb{W}_{2,21}=0
, respectively. Hence, the matrix
\mathbb{W}_{j}
simplifies to the following diagonal form:

\mathbb{W}_{j}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{W}_{j,11}&0\\
0&\mathbb{W}_{j,22}\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{for }j=1,2.

Examining the
(1,1)
-entry of the matrix equation (
5.9
), we arrive at the relation:

\mathbb{W}_{1,11}M_{z}=qM_{z}\mathbb{W}_{1,11}.

A corresponding matrix equation, derived from the
{\overline{q}}
-commutativity condition for the pair
(\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
, yields a similar identity:

\mathbb{W}_{2,11}M_{z}={\overline{q}}M_{z}\mathbb{W}_{2,11}.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.9
, the block
W_{j,11}
can be expressed in the specific forms
\mathbb{W}_{1,11}=M_{\varphi_{1}}R_{q}
and
\mathbb{W}_{2,11}=M_{\varphi_{2}}R_{{\overline{q}}}
, where
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in H^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}))
. Let us consider the Taylor expansion of the contractive analytic functions
\varphi_{j}
on
\mathbb{D}
as

\varphi_{j}(z)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\varphi_{j,k}z^{k}\quad\text{for }j=1,2,

where each
\varphi_{j,k}
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
. Comparing the
(1,1)
-entry of (
5.10
), we obtain
\mathbb{W}_{1,11}={\overline{q}}\mathbb{W}_{2,11}^{*}M_{z}
, which in turn implies
M_{\varphi_{1}}={\overline{q}}R_{q}M_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}M_{z}
and the resulting relations among the Taylor coefficients are obtained as follows:

(5.11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\varphi_{1,k}z^{k}=M_{\varphi_{1}}(1)={\overline{q}}R_{q}M_{\varphi_{2}}^{*}M_{z}(1)={\overline{q}}R_{q}\sum_{k\geq 0}\varphi^{*}_{2,k}\bar{z}^{(k-1)}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\varphi^{*}_{2,k}{\overline{q}}^{k}\bar{z}^{(k-1)}.

By equating the coefficients in (
5.11
), we find that
\varphi_{1,0}={\overline{q}}\varphi^{*}_{2,1}
,

\varphi_{1,1}=\varphi^{*}_{2,0}
and
\varphi_{j,k}=0
for
j=1,2
and for all
k\geq 3
. Let us define a pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
of bounded operators on
{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}
by
G_{1}=\varphi^{*}_{1,0}
and
G_{2}=\varphi_{1,1}
. Then the pair
(\varphi_{1}(z),\varphi_{2}(z))
takes the form
(G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2},\;G_{2}^{*}+{\overline{q}}zG_{1})
, which leads to the following form of
\mathbb{W}_{1,11}
and
\mathbb{W}_{2,11}
:

(5.12)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathbb{W}_{1,11}=M_{\varphi_{1}}R_{q}=M_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}}R_{q},\\
\begin{aligned} \mathbb{W}_{2,11}=M_{\varphi_{2}}R_{{\overline{q}}}=M_{G^{*}_{2}+{\overline{q}}zG_{1}}R_{{\overline{q}}}=M_{{\overline{q}}z}R_{{\overline{q}}}\otimes G_{1}\oplus R_{{\overline{q}}}\otimes G^{*}_{2}&=R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{z}\otimes G_{1}\oplus R_{{\overline{q}}}\otimes G^{*}_{2}\\
&=R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{*}_{2}+zG_{1}}.\end{aligned}\end{cases}\

To conclude the proof, we show that the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
consists of the fundamental operators, and
(\mathbb{W}_{1,22},\mathbb{W}_{2,22})
is the canonical
q
-commuting unitary pair for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
.

Since
(\Pi_{D},\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T)
, we have the following:

(5.13)

\displaystyle{(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})}^{*}\Pi_{D}=\Pi_{D}(T_{1},T_{2},T)^{*}.

By combining the results of equations (
5.12
) and (
5.13
), along with the pseudo
q
-commutativity of the triple
(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},V_{D})
, we derive the following expression:

(5.14)

\displaystyle T^{*}_{1}-T_{2}T^{*}=\Pi^{*}_{D}\Pi_{D}(T^{*}_{1}-T_{2}T^{*})

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}\mathbb{W}^{*}_{1}\Pi_{D}-T_{2}\Pi^{*}_{D}\Pi_{D}T^{*}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}\mathbb{W}^{*}_{1}\Pi_{D}-\Pi^{*}_{D}\mathbb{W}_{2}V^{*}_{D}\Pi_{D}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}(\mathbb{W}^{*}_{1}-\mathbb{W}^{*}_{1}V_{D}V^{*}_{D})\Pi_{D}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}\mathbb{W}^{*}_{1}(I-V_{D}V^{*}_{D})\Pi_{D}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M^{*}_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}}(I-M_{z}M^{*}_{z})&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{D}

\displaystyle=\Pi^{*}_{D}\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}(I-M_{z}M^{*}_{z})\otimes G_{1}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{D}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}(R_{{\overline{q}}}(I-M_{z}M^{*}_{z})\otimes G_{1}){\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}.

Based on the definitions of the observability operator
{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}
and its adjoint, which are given by

{\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}h=\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}h,

and

\mathcal{O}^{*}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}h_{n}z^{n}\right)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T^{n}D_{T^{*}}h_{n},

equation (
5.14
) can be rewritten in the following form:

(5.15)

\displaystyle(T^{*}_{1}-T_{2}T^{*})h

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}(R_{{\overline{q}}}(I-M_{z}M^{*}_{z})\otimes G_{1}){\mathcal{O}}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}h

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}(R_{{\overline{q}}}(I-M_{z}M^{*}_{z})\otimes G_{1})\sum_{n\geq 0}z^{n}D_{T^{*}}{T^{*}}^{n}h

\displaystyle={\mathcal{O}}^{*}_{D_{T^{*}},T^{*}}G_{1}D_{T^{*}}h=D_{T^{*}}G_{1}D_{T^{*}}h.

By a similar line of reasoning, we arrive at the following equation:

(5.16)

\displaystyle T^{*}_{2}-qT_{1}T^{*}

\displaystyle=D_{T^{*}}G_{2}D_{T^{*}}.

Since
(G_{1},G_{2})
satisfy the fundamental equations (
5.4
), as shown by (
5.15
) and (
5.16
), the uniqueness of the fundamental operators pair implies that
(G_{1},G_{2})
is the fundamental operators pair of
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. What remains to be shown is that
(W_{1,22},W_{2,22})
indeed forms the canonical
q
-commuting unitary pair associated with
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
. It follows from (
5.13
) that
W^{*}_{j,22}Q_{T^{*}}=Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{j}
for
j=1,2
, and
W^{*}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}=Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}
. By applying (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), we deduce that
(W_{1,22},W_{2,22},W_{D})
constitutes a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
.

\displaystyle W^{*}_{1,22}W^{*}_{2,22}W^{n}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}=q^{n}W^{*}_{1,22}W^{n}_{D}W^{*}_{2,22}Q_{T^{*}}=W^{n}_{D}W^{*}_{1,22}W^{*}_{2,22}Q_{T^{*}}

\displaystyle=W^{n}_{D}W^{*}_{1,22}Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle=W^{n}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}_{1}T^{*}_{2}

\displaystyle=qW^{n}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}T^{*}

\displaystyle=qW_{D}^{n}W^{*}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}

\displaystyle=qW^{*}_{D}W^{n}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}.

Since
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}=\overline{\text{span}}\{W^{n}_{D}Q_{T^{*}}h:h\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
, we find that
W^{*}_{1,22}W^{*}_{2,22}=qW^{*}_{D}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}_{T^{*}}
. Thus, the result follows directly from Corollary
4.4
.

∎

The above theorem leads to the following corollary, which establishes the uniqueness of the pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of
q
-commuting contractions.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
be a
q
-commuting contractive pair acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose
(\Pi,\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
is a pseudo-
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T_{1}T_{2})
. Then
(\Pi,\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
is unitarily equivalent to the Douglas-model pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift

(5.17)

\displaystyle\left(\Pi_{D},\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}M_{z}&0\\
0&W_{D}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Proof.

Since
(\Pi,\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift, by definition,
(\Pi,\mathbb{W})
is a minimal isometric lift of
T=T_{1}T_{2}
on
\mathcal{K}
. By the uniqueness of the minimal isometric lift of
T
, there exists a unitary
\tau:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}_{D}
such that
\tau\Pi=\Pi_{D}
and
\tau\mathbb{W}=V_{D}\tau
. Since
(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple and
\tau
is unitary,
\tau(\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})\tau^{*}
is also a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive triple. Again, since
(\Pi,\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
is a lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T=T_{1}T_{2})
, it follows that

(\tau\Pi=\Pi_{D},\tau\mathbb{W}_{1}\tau^{*},\tau\mathbb{W}_{2}\tau^{*},\tau\mathbb{W}\tau^{*}=V_{D})

is also a lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T=T_{1}T_{2})
. By the Theorem
5.6
, we conclude that

(\tau\mathbb{W}_{1}\tau^{*},\tau\mathbb{W}_{2}\tau^{*})=\left(\begin{bmatrix}M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}&0\\
0&W_{1}\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}&0\\
0&W_{2}\end{bmatrix}\right).

Hence,
\tau
implements a unitary equivalence between the pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lifts
(\Pi,\mathbb{W}_{1},\mathbb{W}_{2},\mathbb{W})
and the lift as in (
5.17
).
∎

5.3.
The characteristic triple

The characteristic function, together with the fundamental operators and the canonical
q
-commuting unitaries provide a complete unitary invariant for a pair of
q
-commuting contractions with the product being cnu.

Definition 5.8
.

Given a pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
of
q
-commuting contractions, let
\mathbb{G}:=\{G_{1},G_{2}\}
be the fundamental operators for
(T_{1}^{*},T_{2}^{*})
, and
\mathbb{W}:=\{W^{{\textrm{NF}}}_{1},W^{{\textrm{NF}}}_{2}\}
be the commuting unitary pair as defined in (
4.25
). The triple
(\mathbb{G},\mathbb{W},\Theta_{T})
is then called the
characteristic triple
for
(T_{1},T_{2})
.

The characteristic triples for a
q
-commuting contractive pair exhibit the same characteristics as the characteristic functions of a contractive operator do. The first instance is the following.

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
be two pairs of
q
-commuting contractions acting on a Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. If
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
are unitarily equivalent, then their characteristic triples coincide. Moreover, if
T=T_{1}T_{2}
and
T^{\prime}=T^{\prime}_{1}T^{\prime}_{2}
are cnu contractions, then the coverse also holds.

Proof.

Let
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta_{T})
and
((G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}),(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2}),\Theta_{T^{\prime}})
be the characteristic triples of
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
, respectively. To prove the forward implication, suppose
U:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}
is a unitary operator such that
U(T_{1},T_{2})=(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})U
. Then, the intertwining relations
UD_{T}=D_{T^{\prime}}U
and
UD_{T^{*}}=D_{T^{\prime*}}U
imply that the operators
u
and
u_{*}
, defined as the unitary restrictions of
U
to
D_{T}
and
D_{T^{*}}
, respectively, satisfy the following property for all
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
:

\displaystyle\Theta_{T^{\prime}}(z)\,u\,D_{T}h

\displaystyle=\left.\left(-T^{\prime}+zD_{T^{\prime*}}(I_{{\mathcal{H}}^{\prime}}-zT^{\prime*})^{-1}D_{T^{\prime}}\right)\right|_{{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime}}}uD_{T}h

\displaystyle=u_{*}\left(-T+zD_{T^{*}}(I_{{\mathcal{H}}}-zT^{*})^{-1}D_{T}\right)D_{T}h

\displaystyle=u_{*}\Theta_{T}(z).

Hence,
\Theta_{T}
and
\Theta_{T^{\prime}}
coincide. The condition
(G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2})=u_{*}(G_{1},G_{2})u_{*}^{*}
follows from the fundamental equations for
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
defined in (
5.4
), together with the intertwining relations
UD_{T}=D_{T^{\prime}}U
and
UD_{T^{*}}=D_{T^{\prime*}}U
.

It remains to show that
(W_{1},W_{2})
and
(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})
are equivalent, implemented by the unitary
\omega_{u}
. Let

\left(\Pi_{\text{NF}},\ M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.\!M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}}\right)

on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T}}
, and

\left(\Pi^{\prime}_{\text{NF}},\ M_{{G^{\prime}_{1}}^{*}+zG^{\prime}_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W^{\prime}_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{{G^{\prime}_{2}}^{*}+zG^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.\!M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}})}}\right)

on
\mathcal{K}_{\theta_{T^{\prime}}}
, be the pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lifts of
(T_{1},T_{2},T)
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2},T^{\prime})
, respectively. Consider the isometry
\Pi^{\prime\prime}:\mathcal{H}\to H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\oplus\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}
defined by

\Pi^{\prime\prime}=\left((I_{H^{2}}\otimes u^{*}_{*})\oplus\omega^{*}_{u}\right)\Pi^{\prime}_{\text{NF}}U.

Then it follows that

\displaystyle\Big(\Pi^{\prime\prime},\ M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W^{\prime\prime}_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W^{\prime\prime}_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}}\Big)

is a pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T)
, where
(W^{\prime\prime}_{1},W^{\prime\prime}_{2})=\omega_{u}^{*}(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})\omega_{u}
. Establishing that
\Pi^{\prime\prime}=\Pi_{\text{NF}}
via the intertwining properties of
U
and
\omega_{u}
allows us to invoke Corollary
5.7
, from which it follows that
\omega_{u}^{*}(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})\omega_{u}=(W^{\prime\prime}_{1},W^{\prime\prime}_{2})=(W_{1},W_{2})
.

Conversely, suppose that
T,T^{\prime}
are cnu contractions and
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta_{T})
and
((G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta_{T^{\prime}})
coincide. Then by Theorem
5.3
,
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
are unitarily equivalent to their corresponding functional model. The coincidence of characteristic triples implies that the unitary operator

I_{H^{2}}\otimes u\oplus\omega_{u}:H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}})\oplus\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}\to H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime*}})\oplus\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}})}

intertwines the operator triple

(M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}})

with

(M_{{G^{\prime}_{1}}^{*}+zG^{\prime}_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W^{\prime}_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{{G^{\prime}_{2}}^{*}+zG^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}})}}),

where
u:\mathcal{D}_{T}\to\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
and
u_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime*}}
are the unitary operators implementing the coincidence.
Moreover, the unitary operator
u_{*}\oplus\omega_{u}
maps
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta_{T}}
onto
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}}
, and
(\Theta_{T}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta_{T}})H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})
onto
(\Theta_{T^{\prime}}\oplus\Delta_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}})H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T^{\prime}})
. Hence, it maps
\mathcal{H}_{\Theta_{T}}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\Theta_{T^{\prime}}}
. Thus, the functional models for
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
are unitarily equivalent. Therefore, we conclude that
(T_{1},T_{2})
and
(T^{\prime}_{1},T^{\prime}_{2})
are unitarily equivalent.
∎

5.4.
Functional model

Theorem
5.3
gives a unitary copy on function space of an abstract
q
-commuting contractive pair
\underline{T}=(T_{1},T_{2})
in terms of its characteristic triple
\Xi_{\underline{T}}=((G_{1},G_{2}),)(W_{1}^{\rm NF},W_{2}^{\rm NF}),\Theta_{T})
. Here we explore a converse of this: We identify a triple
\Xi=((G_{1},G_{2}),)(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
consisting of operators
G_{j}
,
W_{j}
for
j=1,2
acting on appropriate spaces and a contractive analytic function
\Theta
, from which a
q
-commuting contractive pair
\underline{T}_{\Xi}
can be defined so that the characteristic triple for the
q
-commuting contractive pair
\underline{T}_{\Xi}
coincides with
\Xi
in the following sense.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*},\Theta)
and
({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{*},\Theta^{\prime})
be two purely contractive analytic functions (i.e., both functions satisfy
\|\Theta(0)f\|<\|f\|
for every non-zero vector
f
). Let
(G_{1},G_{2})
and
(G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2})
be pairs of contractions on
{\mathcal{D}}_{*}
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{*}
, respectively. Additionally, let
(W_{1},W_{2})
and
(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})
be pairs of
q
-commuting unitaries, whose products are equal to
M_{\zeta}
on
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}
and
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta^{\prime}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})}
, respectively. Then we say that the two triples
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
and
((G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}),(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2}),\Theta^{\prime})

coincide
if the following conditions are satisfied:

i)

The pairs
({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*},\Theta)
and
({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime},{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{*},\Theta^{\prime})
coincide, i.e., there exist unitary operators
u:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
and
u_{*}:{\mathcal{D}}_{*}\to{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}_{*}
such that
u_{*}\Theta(z)=\Theta^{\prime}(z)u
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

ii)

The unitary operators
u
and
u_{*}
satisfy the intertwining relations:

(G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2})=u_{*}(G_{1},G_{2})u_{*}^{*}\quad\text{and}\quad(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2})=\omega_{u}(W_{1},W_{2})\omega_{u}^{*}

where
\omega_{u}:\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\to\overline{\Delta_{\Theta^{\prime}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime})}
is the unitary map defined by

\omega_{u}=\left.(I_{L^{2}}\otimes u)\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}}.

Next, we define those triples
\Xi=((G_{1},G_{2}),)(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
consisting of operators
G_{j}
,
W_{j}
for
j=1,2
acting on appropriate spaces and a contractive analytic function
\Theta
, which would give us the converse of Theorem
5.3
alluded to in the beginning of this subsection.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{D}_{*},\Theta)
be a contractive analytic function, and let
(G_{1},G_{2})
be a pair of contractions on
\mathcal{D}_{*}
. Let
(W_{1},W_{2})
be a pair of
q
-commuting unitaries on
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
. We say that the triple
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
is
admissible
if it satisfies the following admissibility conditions:

(1)

M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W_{1}
and
R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2}
are contractions on
H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{*})\oplus\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
.

(2)

W_{1}W_{2}=\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}
.

(3)

The space
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\Theta}:=(\Theta\oplus\Delta_{\Theta})H^{2}(\mathcal{D})
is jointly invariant under

(M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W_{1},R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2},M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}).

(4)

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}:=H^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{*})\oplus\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\Theta}:=\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus{\mathcal{Q}}_{\Theta}
. Then, we have the following:

\displaystyle\left.q(R_{{\overline{q}}}M^{*}_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}\oplus W^{*}_{1})(M^{*}_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W^{*}_{2})\right|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\left.(M^{*}_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W^{*}_{2})(R_{{\overline{q}}}M^{*}_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}\oplus W^{*}_{1})\right|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle=\left.(M^{*}_{z}\oplus\left.M^{*}_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}})\right|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}.

If, in addition,
\Theta:{D}\to{\mathcal{B}}({\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{D}}_{*})
is purely contractive, i.e.,
\|\Theta(0)f\|<\|f\|
for all
f\in{\mathcal{D}}\setminus\{0\}
, we say that
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
is
purely contractive admissible triple
.

Example 5.12
.

Theorem
5.3
provides us with ample examples of admissible triples, viz., the characteristic triple
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta_{T})
of a pair of
q
-commuting contractions
(T_{1},T_{2})
is an admissible triple. Moreover, if
T=T_{1}T_{2}=qT_{2}T_{1}
is cnu, then the characteristic triple for
(T_{1},T_{2})
is a purely contractive admissible triple because in this case
\Theta_{T}
is purely contractive.

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
be a purely contractive admissible triple. Consider the operator pair defined by

(T_{1},T_{2})=\left.P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}(M_{G_{1}^{*}+zG_{2}}R_{q}\oplus W_{1},R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G_{2}^{*}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2})\right|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}.

Then the characteristic triple of the pair
(T_{1},T_{2})
coincides with
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
.

Proof.

By part (1) of Definition
5.11
, it follows that
(T_{1},T_{2})
, as defined in the statement, is a pair of
q
-commuting contractions. Moreover, by part (4) of Definition
5.11
, the product operator
T:=T_{1}T_{2}
is given by

T=\left.P_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}\left(M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}\right)\right|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}}.

Therefore by the Sz.-Nagy–Foias model theory for single contractions (see
[
10
, Theorem VI.3.1]
),
T
is a cnu contraction. Let
((G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}),(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2}),\Theta_{T})
be the characteristic triple of
(T_{1},T_{2})
. We need to show that
((G_{1},G_{2}),(W_{1},W_{2}),\Theta)
coincides with
((G^{\prime}_{1},G^{\prime}_{2}),(W^{\prime}_{1},W^{\prime}_{2}),\Theta_{T})
. Since
\Theta
is a purely contractive analytic function, by
[
10
, Theorem VI.3.1]
,
\Theta
coincides with the characteristic function
\Theta_{T}
of the model operator
T
, i.e., there exist unitary operators
u:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{T}
and
u_{*}:\mathcal{D}_{*}\to\mathcal{D}_{T^{*}}
such that
\Theta_{T}u=u_{*}\Theta
. Thus we already have established condition (i) of Definition
5.10
.

To show condition (ii) of Definition
5.10
, we proceed as follows. Let
\omega_{u}
be the unitary operator corresponding to
u
given by

\omega_{u}=\left.(I_{L^{2}}\otimes u)\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}}.

Then the unitary operator
u_{*}\oplus\omega_{u}
maps

{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\end{bmatrix}

onto

{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix},

and it takes the closed subspace

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\Theta\\
\Delta_{\Theta}\end{smallmatrix}\right]H^{2}({\mathcal{D}})
onto the closed subspace

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}\Theta_{T}\\
\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})
. Therefore, it maps

{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{*})\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}})}\end{bmatrix}\ominus\begin{bmatrix}\Theta\\
\Delta_{\Theta}\end{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}})

onto

{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta_{T}}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T^{*}}\\
\overline{\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}L^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T})}\end{bmatrix}\ominus\begin{bmatrix}\Theta_{T}\\
\Delta_{\Theta_{T}}\end{bmatrix}H^{2}({\mathcal{D}}_{T}).

Let
\tau
be the restriction of
u_{*}\oplus\omega_{u}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Theta}
. Then it is an easy check that the following diagram is commutative where
i
and
i^{\prime}
are the inclusion maps:

\left(i,\ M_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}R_{q}}\oplus W_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{*}_{2}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}\right)

acting on
{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta}
, and

\left(i^{\prime}\circ\tau,\ M_{G^{\prime*}_{1}+zG^{\prime}_{2}R_{q}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{\prime*}_{2}+zG^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}}\right)

acting on
{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}
both are pseudo
q
-commuting contractive lift of
(T_{1},T_{2},T_{1}T_{2})
. By Corollary
5.7
, there exists a unitary operator
U:{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta}\to{\mathcal{K}}_{\Theta_{T}}
such that
U\circ i=i^{\prime}\circ\tau
and

(5.18)

\displaystyle U\left(M_{G^{*}_{1}+zG_{2}R_{q}}\oplus W_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{*}_{2}+zG_{1}}\oplus W_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}\right)

(5.19)

\displaystyle=\left(M_{G^{\prime*}_{1}+zG^{\prime}_{2}R_{q}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{1},\ R_{{\overline{q}}}M_{G^{\prime*}_{2}+zG^{\prime}_{1}}\oplus W^{\prime}_{2},\ M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}L^{2}(\mathcal{D}_{T})}}\right)U.

q
-commuting contractive lift,

M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}\quad\text{and}\quad M_{z}\oplus\left.M_{\zeta}\right|_{\overline{\Delta_{\Theta}L^{2}(\mathcal{D})}}

u_{*}\oplus\omega_{u}

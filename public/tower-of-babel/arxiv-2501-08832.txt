Noncommutative Bourgain’s circular maximal theorem and a local smoothing estimate on the generalized Moyal planes
Guixiang Hong, Xudong Lai, Liang Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish a local smoothing estimate on the generalized Moyal plane, also known as the two-dimensional quantum Euclidean space. This is a quantum version of the local smoothing estimate for the free wave equation due to Mockenhaupt − - Seeger − - Sogge [ 43 ] . As an application and simultaneously one motivation, we obtain a noncommutative analogue of Bourgain’s circular maximal theorem, resolving the problem after [ 22 ] .

1.
Introduction

In 1976, Stein
[
53
]
introduced the spherical means on the Euclidean spaces and showed the surprising maximal inequality on
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
d\geq 3
and
\frac{d}{d-1}<p\leq\infty
, and the latter range is sharp in the sense that the maximal inequality cannot be true for
p\leq\frac{d}{d-1}
. Note that when
d=2
,
\frac{d}{d-1}=2
and the circular maximal inequality cannot be true on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This presented a significant obstacle to apply the
L_{2}
-orthogonality to extend Stein’s results for circular means. Ten years later, Bourgain
[
3
]
(see
[
4
]
for a simpler but more general version) was able to surmount this obstacle by exploring interpolation, deep geometric consideration and variants of stopping time arguments, obtaining the circular maximal inequality and resolving the open problem left in
[
55
]
.

Motivated by the noncommutative martingale and ergodic maximal inequality
[
30
,
33
,
40
]
, Stein’s spherical maximal inequality was established in the noncommutative framework more than ten years ago by the first author and applied to the dimension-free operator-valued Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality
[
22
]
, see e.g.
[
46
,
30
,
1
,
7
,
12
,
23
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
]
for more information on noncommutative maximal inequalities. However, Bourgain’s arguments
[
3
,
4
]
seem to be too difficult to admit a noncommutative analogue and thus the noncommutative version of circular maximal inequality had been left unsolved before. In the classical setting, after Sogge’s breakthrough
[
51
]
, Muckenhoupt, Seeger and Sogge
[
43
]
had provided an alternative proof of Bourgain’s circular maximal inequality by exploring its close connection to local smoothing estimate, Bochner-Riesz means, Fourier restriction estimate and Kakeya’s maximal inequality in two dimensional Euclidean spaces. See e.g.
[
56
]
for more tight relationships among these seemingly unrelated topics. This leads us to study these topics in the noncommutative setting by first focusing on quantum Euclidean spaces.

Quantum Euclidean spaces, the generalized Moyal planes or phase spaces, are the model examples of noncommutative locally compact manifolds, having appeared frequently in the literature of mathematical physics, such as string theory and noncommutative field theory
[
13
,
38
,
50
]
. In recent years, harmonic analysis on these noncommutative manifolds has been developing very rapidly, including the pointwise convergence of Fourier series and functional spaces on quantum tori
[
12
,
34
,
58
]
, singular integral theory and pseudodifferential operator
theory
[
15
,
19
,
32
]
, as well as commutator estimates and quantum differentiability for quantum Euclidean spaces
[
36
,
39
]
. Note that all these work can be viewed as theories insider Calderón-Zygmund’s framework that are closely related to
Littlewood-Paley square functions and noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequalities
, but it is well-known that the previously mentioned topics around Fourier restriction estimates go dramatically different from Calderón-Zygmund theory. This provides us another impetus to investigate the local smoothing estimates of wave equation and the theory of partial differential equations on quantum Euclidean spaces.

Along this research line, we have made some progresses
[
14
,
25
,
34
]
, including the sharp estimate of the Bochner-Riesz means on two-dimensional quantum tori, the Fourier restriction estimates with optimal indices on two-dimensional quantum Euclidean spaces and the noncommutative sharp endpoint
L_{p}
-estimates of Schrödinger equations; see
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
37
,
49
]
for other progresses on partial differential equations.

In this paper, we study the local smoothing estimates of quantum wave equations, obtaining the quantum Euclidean space analogues of the above-mentioned Muckenhoupt-Seeger-Sogge’s results and resolving the problem of noncommutative circular maximal inequality.

The quantum Euclidean spaces admit several equivalent definitions (see e.g.
[
16
,
19
,
48
]
). In this paper, we adopt the following one. Given a
d\times d
real antisymmetric matrix
\theta
with
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the quantum Euclidean space
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
which is generated by a family of unitary operators
\{U_{\theta}(r)\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, where
\{U_{\theta}(r)\}_{r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
satisfies the following Weyl relation:

U_{\theta}(r)U_{\theta}(s)=e^{\frac{i}{2}(s,\theta r)}U_{\theta}(r+s),\quad\text{for\> all}\>r,s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The space
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra with a normal semifinite faithful trace
\tau_{\theta}
, and we denote by
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
the noncommutative
L_{p}
space associated to
(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d},\tau_{\theta})
. When
\theta=0
,
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
reduces to the usual
L_{p}
space defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Lebesgue measure. As in the case
\theta=0
, there exists a canonical Laplacian
\Delta_{\theta}
on
\mathcal{R}^{d}_{\theta}
. We refer the reader to Section
2
for more information and notions appearing below.

Let
u:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
be the solution to the Cauchy problem of the wave equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta_{\theta})u=0,\ t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\\[5.0pt]
u(0)=x_{0},\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{t}u(0)=x_{1}\end{array}\right.

where
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
, the Schwartz class on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
.

As in the classical case
\theta=0
, one may represent the solution of (
1
) as Fourier multipliers,

u(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\cos(|t\xi|){\hat{x}_{0}}(\xi)U_{\theta}(\xi)d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin(|t\xi|)\frac{{\hat{x}_{1}}(\xi)}{|\xi|}U_{\theta}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\hat{x}(\xi)=\tau_{\theta}(xU^{*}_{\theta}(\xi))
.

For
\theta=0
and a fixed time
t
, Miyachi
[
42
]
and Peral
[
45
]
showed the following sharp
L_{p}
estimate for
p\in(1,\infty)
,

\|u(t)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\big(\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big),

(1.5)

where
s_{p}=(d-1)|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|
,
C_{p,t}
is locally bounded and
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p,s}}
denotes the inhomogeneous Sobolev norm with
s
derivatives (see e.g.
[
18
, Page 13]
). This estimate is sharp in the sense that the inequality (
1.5
) fails if
s_{p}
is replaced by any
\sigma
with
\sigma<s_{p}
.

For general
\theta
, we get the same fixed time
L_{p}
estimate.

Proposition 1.1
.

With all the notions above,

\|u(t)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\big(\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-1}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}\big).

(1.6)

Note that as in the classical case the estimate (
1.6
) cannot be deduced from noncommutative Hörmander-Mihlin Fourier multiplier theorems or Calderón-Zygmund theory. We will first explore a transference technique to reduce (
1.6
) to an operator-valued one, and then use an observation made by Mei
[
40
]
which says that any Fourier multiplier on classical Hardy spaces
H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is automatically completely bounded. This, combined with the noncommutative Hardy-BMO theory and analytic interpolation, will conclude the proof. It is worthwhile to mention that our transference technique for
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is different from the ones used in the literature (cf.
[
8
,
6
]
) for discrete groups, and thus needs some new arguments; actually, there are some open problems regarding the similar transference techniques for non-discrete groups, see e.g. Remark
6.3
,
[
6
, Remark 3.5]
. Since it is viewed as a motivating result of the main local smoothing estimate of the present paper, we will prove it in the Appendix.

By the inequality (
1.6
), one then has trivially

\Big(\int_{1}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}^{p}dt\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-1}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})},

(1.7)

where
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p,s}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}
is the Sobolev norm. A natural problem here is that if we consider the time-space estimates (
1.7
) directly, can we weaken the regularities requirement of the initial data
x_{0}
and
x_{1}
for the wave equation (
1
).
The first main result of the present paper is the following non-trivial local smoothing estimate on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
for all
2\times 2
real antisymmetric matrix
\theta
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\theta
be a
2\times 2
real antisymmetric matrix and assume that
2<p<\infty
. Then for all
\kappa<\kappa(p)
, we have

\|u\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2])\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}\leq C_{p,\kappa}\big(\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-\kappa}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-\kappa-1}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}\big),

(1.8)

where
u(t)
is the solution of the equation (
1
),
C_{p,\kappa}
is a constant that depends on
p,\kappa
, and

\displaystyle\kappa(p)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1/4-1/2p\ ,\quad\ 2<p<4\ ,\\[5.0pt]
1/2p\ ,\quad\quad\quad\quad 4\leq p<\infty.\end{array}\right.

This is the quantum Euclidean space analogue of Muckenhoupt-Seeger-Sogge’s celebrated local smoothing estimate. Note that the latter has been playing an important role in motivating the striking progress on the local smoothing conjecture (see e.g.
[
5
,
20
,
57
]
), which was posed by Sogge
[
51
]
and is still open in the higher-dimensions
d\geq 3
with a recent resolution in the two dimensions
[
20
]
. With Theorem
1.2
, it would be quite interesting to establish the noncommutative analogues of these progresses
[
5
,
57
]
, especially, the quantum Euclidean space analogue of
[
20
]
—(
1.8
) with
\kappa<2\kappa(p)
. This turns out to be extremely challenging and will be taken care of elsewhere.

Via the above-mentioned non-standard transference technique (see Section
6
for details), Theorem
1.2
reduces to the following operator-valued local smoothing estimate. Let
\mathcal{M}
denote a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with semifinite normal faithful trace
\tau
. The associated dense ideal of elements with finite trace and
L_{p}
spaces are denoted respectively by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
and
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.
Consider the tensor von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
. For
f,g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
, one can define
u\in L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
by

u(x,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\xi}\cos(|t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\xi}\sin(|t\xi|)\frac{\hat{g}(\xi)}{|\xi|}d\xi.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
u,f,g
,
\kappa(p)
be as above and assume that
2<p<\infty
. Then for all
\kappa<\kappa(p)
, we have

\|u\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{p,\kappa}\big(\|f\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-\kappa}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\|g\|_{L_{p,s_{p}-\kappa-1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\big),

(1.10)

where
\|f\|_{L_{p,s}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}=\big\|\big((1+|\cdot|)^{s/2}\hat{f}\big)^{\vee}\big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
, and
C_{p,\kappa}
is a constant that depends on
p,\kappa
.

As in the classical setting, Theorem
1.2
will be regarded as boundedness of a type of noncommutative Fourier integral operator
T
. Applying the Littlewood-Paley decomposition,
T
can be expressed as sum of some localization operators
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, which serves to reduce the proof to the boundedness of each
T_{j}
. For small
j
, the boundedness follows from the kernel estimate of
T_{j}
and the noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality. For large
j
, combined with the noncommutative Littlewood-Paley inequality and some properties of noncommutative maximal norm, the boundedness of
T_{j}
reduces to the following:

\|F_{j}f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\leq C_{\mu}2^{\mu j}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)},

(1.11)

where
F_{j}f(x,t)
is an operator-valued Fourier integral operator and we refer to Proposition
3.5
for the precise definition.

To get the estimate (
1.11
), we need numerous modifications and improvements based on the main idea of
[
43
]
to overcome the difficulties due to noncommutativity. This will constitute the main technical parts of the paper. Roughly speaking, a key fact in our proof is that
|x^{*}x|^{2}
is not necessarily equal to
|xx|^{2}
for a general operator
x
.
Thus there are some new inequalities such as
noncommutative square functions associated to uniform decomposition and noncommutative Kakeya type maximal inequalities
as well as new geometric structures that need to be handled as in
[
34
,
25
]
. Especially in this paper, we need to prove a new geometric estimate relate to some subsets in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
which is much more complicated than the two dimensional geometric structures in
[
34
,
25
]
. Notably, our alternative approach enables a slight refinement of the geometric estimate originally derived by Muckenhoupt, Seeger, and Sogge in
[
43
]
. We refer to Remark
4.6
and Remark
5.1
for details.

As in classical case, it is the estimate (
1.11
) that will provide one possible way to the noncommutative version of Bourgain’s circular maximal inequality.
Recall that the circular means with radius
t>0
is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{t}f(x):=\int_{S^{1}}f(x-ty)d\mu(y),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}),

where
d\mu
is the induced Lebesgue measure on the circle
S^{1}
. Stein’s original approach
[
53
,
55
]
is to embed
\mathcal{A}_{t}
into an analytic family of linear operators
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}
of complex order
\sigma
, which is defined as

\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\sigma)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1-|y/t|^{2})_{+}^{\sigma-1}f(x-y)dy,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{C}.

(1.12)

Note that
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}
is a priori defined for
\Re\sigma>0
and extends to all
\sigma\in\mathbb{C}
by analytic continuation. Then one may conclude
\mathcal{A}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}^{0}
by the Fourier transform since
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f(x)=(m_{\sigma}(t|\cdot|)\hat{f})^{\vee}(x)
,
where

m_{\sigma}(r)=2^{\sigma}\pi r^{-\sigma}J_{\sigma}(r),\quad r>0,

with
J_{\sigma}
being the Bessel function (see e.g.
[
55
, Theorem 7]
).

As in
[
43
]
, we prove the maximal inequality associated to circular means
(\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma})_{t>0}
with
\Re\sigma>-\kappa(p)
and thus resolve the conjecture on the noncommutative analogue of Bourgain’s circular maximal inequality in Section
3.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
2<p<\infty
, then for all
f\in{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
,

\Big\|{\sup_{0<t<\infty}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{p,\sigma}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})},\qquad if\ \ \Re\sigma>-\kappa(p),

(1.13)

where
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f(x)=(m_{\sigma}(t|\cdot|)\hat{f})^{\vee}(x)
and
C_{p,\sigma}
is a constant that depends on
p,\sigma
.

Remark 1.5
.

The argument for the implication from the estimate (
1.11
) to Theorem
1.4
is more complicated than the one to Theorem
1.3
, since it involves the noncommutative maximal norm and a completely bounded version of Sobolev embedding inequality in the category of operator space, which are well-known to be two of the most difficult objects in noncommutative analysis. We refer the reader to Subsection

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces

Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
endowed with a normal semifinite faithful trace (
n.s.f.
in short)
\tau
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathcal{M})
be the set of all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{+}
such that
\tau(\mathrm{supp}(x))<\infty
, where
\mathrm{supp}(x)
is the support of
x
(i.e. the least projection
e\in\mathcal{M}
such that
ex=x=xe
) and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
be the linear span of
\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathcal{M})
. Then
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
is a weak* dense
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
. For
1\leq p<\infty
, we define

\|x\|_{p}=(\tau(|x|^{p}))^{1/p},\quad x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}),

where
|x|=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
represents the modulus of
x
. The quantity
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a norm, and thus
(\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{p})
forms a normed vector space. The completion of this space is known as the noncommutative
L_{p}
space associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
, denoted by
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
.
In the case
p=\infty
, we set
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{M}
, and define
\|x\|_{\infty}:=\|x\|_{\mathcal{M}}
.
As the commutative
L_{p}
spaces, the noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces enjoy the basic properties such as the Hölder inequality, the duality, the interpolation etc.. For more information on the noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces, we refer to
[
47
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces associated with the pair
(L_{\infty}(\Sigma,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M},\int d\mu\overline{\otimes}\tau)
, where
(\Sigma,\mu)
is a measurable space. Note that the Bochner
L_{p}
space
L_{p}(\Sigma;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
is isomorphic to
L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\Sigma,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
. We will frequently employ the two well-known convexity inequalities involving operator-valued functions (see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 2.4]
,
[
40
, Page 9]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mu)
be a measurable space. Suppose that
f
is a
\mathcal{M}
-valued function on
\Sigma
which is weak*-integrable and
g
is a complex-valued integrable function. Then we have the following operator inequality,

\Big|\int_{\Sigma}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x)\Big|^{2}\leq\int_{\Sigma}|g(x)|^{2}d\mu(x)\times\int_{\Sigma}|f(x)|^{2}d\mu(x).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\Sigma,\mu)
be a measurable space. Suppose that
f
is a weak*-integrable
\mathcal{M}_{+}
-valued function on
\Sigma
and
g
is a positive integrable function. Then for
1<p<\infty
, we have the following operator inequality,

\int_{\Sigma}f(x)g(x)d\mu(x)\leq\Big(\int_{\Sigma}|g(x)|^{p^{\prime}}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/{p^{\prime}}}\times\Big(\int_{\Sigma}|f(x)|^{p}d\mu(x)\Big)^{1/p}.

Here,
p^{\prime}
denotes the conjugate index of
p
, i.e.
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.

The column and row function spaces
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})
play an important role in noncommutative analysis, as they provide a framework for defining the noncommutative square function. Here, we expand on the definitions and some of their key properties.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, given a finite sequence
(x_{n})
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. Define

\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}:=\Big\|\big(\sum_{n}|x_{n}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})},\quad\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}:=\|(x_{n}^{*})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

For
1\leq p<\infty
,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
(resp.
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r}))
are defined as the completions of the set of all finite sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})})
. For
p=\infty
, the Banach space
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
(resp.
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r}))
consists of all sequences in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\sum_{n}x_{n}^{*}x_{n}
(resp.
\sum_{n}x_{n}x_{n}^{*}
) converges in the weak*-topology.
Then we introduce some properties related to the column and row function spaces, such as the Hölder inequality, complex interpolation.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
47
]
)
.

Given
0<p,q,r\leq\infty
satisfying the relation
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
. Then for any sequences
(x_{n})\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
(y_{n})\in L_{q}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, we have

\Big\|\sum_{n}x_{n}^{*}y_{n}\Big\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\|(x_{n})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\|(y_{n})\|_{L_{q}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
47
]
)
.

Given
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
and
0<\alpha<1
. Let
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\alpha}{p_{0}}+\frac{\alpha}{p_{1}}
, then we have isometrically

\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)_{\alpha}=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

This equality also holds for the row function spaces.

Based on Lemma
2.4

Lemma 2.5

For

and any sequence
(x_{n})\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, it holds that

\Big(\sum_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{n}|x_{n}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{p}.

Finally, we present the Littlewood-Paley inequality, as established in

]
. Let

be the Littlewood-Paley operator (see the proof of Lemma

for specific definition), the following Littlewood-Paley inequality holds.

Suppose that

is the Littlewood-Paley operator and

2.2.
Noncommutative
\ell_{\infty}
-valued
L_{p}
spaces

In the noncommutative setting, the maximal norm requires an equivalent definition since
\sup_{n}|x_{n}|
does not make sense for a sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
of operators. We adopt the definition of the noncommutative maximal norm which was introduced by Pisier
[
46
]
and later generalized by Junge
[
30
]
.

Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Banach space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
is defined as the space of all sequences
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
that admit a factorization through elements
a,b\in L_{2p}(\mathcal{M})
and a bounded sequence
y=(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that

x_{n}=ay_{n}b,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

The norm of
x
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
is given by

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}:=\inf\Big\{\|a\|_{2p}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|y_{n}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2p}\Big\},

where the infimum runs over all factorizations of
x
as above. Additionally, it is worth noting that for a sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of self-adjoint operators in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
,
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
if and only if there exists a positive element
a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
such that

-a\leq x_{n}\leq a\quad\text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

In this case, we have

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}=\inf\{\|a\|_{p}\ :\ a\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),\ -a\leq x_{n}\leq a,\ \forall n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

(2.1)

For this reason, the maximal norm is intuitively denoted by

\|\sup^{+}_{n}x_{n}\|_{p}
, but it is crucial to emphasize that
\|\sup^{+}_{n}x_{n}\|_{p}
merely is a notation and
\sup^{+}_{n}x_{n}
does not make sense in the noncommutative setting. In fact, we find that the notation
\|\sup^{+}_{n}x_{n}\|_{p}
offers a more intuitive understanding compared to the formal expression
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}
.
More broadly, this concept extends to any index set
\Lambda
. Specifically, given any index set
\Lambda
,
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
denotes the space of all
x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
admitting a factorization of the form
x_{\lambda}=ay_{\lambda}b,
where
a,b\in L_{2p}(\mathcal{M}),y_{\lambda}\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|y_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}<\infty.

The norm in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
is then defined as

\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}x_{\lambda}\|_{p}:=\inf_{x_{\lambda}=ay_{\lambda}b}\Big\{\|a\|_{2p}\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\|y_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{2p}\Big\}.

As demonstrated in
[
33
]
,
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
if and only if

\sup\Big\{\|{\sup_{\lambda\in I}}^{+}x_{\lambda}\|_{p}\ :\ I\ \text{ is a finite subset of}\ \Lambda\Big\}<\infty.

Indeed, this supremum coincides with
\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}x_{\lambda}\|_{p}
.
For the sake of simplicity and clarity, when no confusion arises, we may continue to denote the space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}(\Lambda))
simply as
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
.

On the other hand, we can define a closely related Banach space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{c})
for
p\geq 2
, which consists of all sequences

x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\big\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}|x_{\lambda}|^{2}\big\|_{p/{2}}^{1/2}<\infty,

with the norm defined as
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{c})}:=\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}|x_{\lambda}|^{2}\|_{p/{2}}^{1/2}
. Furthermore, the Banach space

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{r})
for
p\geq 2
is defined as

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{r}):=\big\{x=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\ :\ x^{*}=(x_{\lambda}^{*})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{c})\big\}

with the norm
\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{r})}:=\|{\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}}^{+}|x_{\lambda}^{*}|^{2}\|_{p/{2}}^{1/2}
.
The following useful interpolation results can be found in
[
29
]
,
[
31
]
and
[
33
]
.

Lemma 2.7
.

(i)
Suppose
1\leq p_{0},p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\alpha<1
. Let
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\alpha}{p_{0}}+\frac{\alpha}{p_{1}}
, then we have isometrically:

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})=\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})\big)_{\alpha}.

(ii)
For
2\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})=\big(L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{c}),L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty}^{r})\big)_{1/2}.

2.3.
Quantum Euclidean spaces

Let
\theta
be a
d\times d
real antisymmetric matrix and for each
t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define the unitary
operator
U_{\theta}(t)
acting on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as follows:

(U_{\theta}(t)f)(r):=e^{-\frac{i}{2}(t,\theta r)}f(r-t),\quad f\in{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},r\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2.2)

The family
\{U_{\theta}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is strongly continuous and satisfies the Weyl relation

U_{\theta}(t)U_{\theta}(s)=e^{\frac{i}{2}(s,\theta t)}U_{\theta}(t+s),\quad U_{\theta}^{*}(t)=U_{\theta}(-t)

for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The von Neumann subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
generated by
\{U_{\theta}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, denoted
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
, is referred to as quantum Euclidean space.
In the special case when
\theta=0
,
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
reduces to the von Neumann algebra generated by the unitary group of translations on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which is

*
-isomorphic to
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We recommend that readers consult, for example, the references
[
19
,
25
,
36
,
39
]
for further information on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
.

As outlined in
[
39
]
, an injective map, also denoted by
U_{\theta}
, can be defined from
L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
into the quantum Euclidean space
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
:

U_{\theta}(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t)U_{\theta}(t)dt,\quad f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.3)

Furthermore, the class of Schwartz functions within
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
is defined as :

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}):=\{x\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}:\quad x=U_{\theta}(f),\text{\>for\> }f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

One can see
U_{\theta}
is a bijection between
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
,
and thus
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
is a Fréchet topological space equipped with the Fréchet topology induced by
U_{\theta}
. Moreover, we denote the space of continuous linear functionals on
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
as
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
, and
U_{\theta}
extends to a bijection between
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
, where for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(U_{\theta}(f),U_{\theta}(g)):=(f,\tilde{g}),\quad\text{for\>all\;}g\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.4)

Given
x\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
represented as
x=U_{\theta}(f)
for some
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define
\tau_{\theta}(x):=f(0)
, then
\tau_{\theta}
extends to a
n.s.f.
trace on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
. The associated noncommutative
L_{p}
space is denoted
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
.
Additionally, the space
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
is dense in
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
for
1\leq p<\infty
with respect to the norm, and is also dense in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
in the weak* topology. See
[
19
,
39
]
for more information.

3.
Proof of the main Theorems

Recall that the circular mean operator
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}
for a given
t>0
is a Fourier multiplier defined as:

\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

where
m_{\sigma}(r)=2^{\sigma}\pi r^{-\sigma}J_{\sigma}(r)
, with
J_{\sigma}
being the Bessel function given by

J_{\sigma}(r)=\frac{(\frac{r}{2})^{\sigma}}{\Gamma(\sigma+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{irs}(1-s^{2})^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}ds.

More information for Bessel function we refer to
[
17
, Appendix B]
and
[
54
, Page 338]
. For the sake of completeness, we present here the asymptotic expansion of
J_{\sigma}
under the condition
\Re\sigma>-1/2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
J_{\sigma}
be the Bessel function with
\Re\sigma>-1/2
, then for
r\geq 1
, one has

J_{\sigma}(r)=\Big(\frac{2}{\pi r}\Big)^{1/2}\cos\Big(r-\frac{\pi\sigma}{2}-\frac{\pi}{4}\Big)+O(r^{-3/2}).

More generally,
J_{\sigma}
admits a complete asymptotic expansion: for any
N\geq 1
,

J_{\sigma}(r)=r^{-1/2}e^{ir}\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}r^{-j}+r^{-1/2}e^{-ir}\sum_{j=0}^{N}b_{j}r^{-j}+R_{\sigma,N}(r),

(3.1)

where
a_{j},b_{j}
are suitable constants and the error term
R_{\sigma,N}
satisfies

\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{dr}\Big)^{k}R_{\sigma,N}(r)\Big|\leq C_{k}r^{-N-k}\quad\text{for}\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ r\geq 1.

In particular, one has
J_{1/2}(r)=cr^{-1/2}\sin(r)
and
J_{-1/2}(r)=c^{\prime}r^{-1/2}\cos(r)
.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this subsection, we present the proof of Theorem
1.4
, which fundamentally relies on the Littlewood-Paley decomposition.
Let
\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a nonnegative function with support in
[1/2,2]
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta(2^{-j}r)=1
for all
r>0
. Set
\eta(r):=1-\sum_{j\geq 0}\beta(2^{-j}r)
, and

\mathcal{A}_{t,0}^{\sigma}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\eta(|t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi,

(3.2)

and for
j\geq 1

\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi.

(3.3)

Then
\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f(x)
and

\left\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t}^{\sigma}f\right\|_{p}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f\right\|_{p},

which implies Theorem
1.4
once we have Lemmas

3.2
and
3.3
below.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p>1
, for any
f\in L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
, one has

\Big\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,0}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim\|f\|_{p}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f
is positive. Recall that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t,0}^{\sigma}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\eta(|t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{t,0}(x-y)f(y)\ dy,

where
K_{t,0}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\eta(|t\xi|)\ d\xi
. Using integration by parts, we get

|K_{t,0}(x)|\leq C_{N}t^{-2}(1+|x|/t)^{-N}

(3.4)

for any integer
N
.
Since
K_{t,0}
can be written as a linear combination of four nonnegative functions which still satisfy the above estimate (
3.4
), for simplicity, we may directly assume
K_{t,0}
to be nonnegative. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{t,0}^{\sigma}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{t,0}(x-y)f(y)\ dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\left(\int_{\frac{|x-y|}{t}<1}f(y)\ dy+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{2^{k-1}\leq\frac{|x-y|}{t}<2^{k}}t^{-2}(1+|x-y|/t)^{-N}f(y)\ dy\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{N}2^{-kN}t^{-2}\int_{B(x,2^{k}t)}f(y)\ dy=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{N}2^{-k(N-2)}}{|B(x,2^{k}t)|}\int_{B(x,2^{k}t)}f(y)\ dy.

By choosing
N>2
and applying the noncommutative Hardy-Littlewood maximal inequality (see
[
40
, Theorem 3.3 ]
), one derives the desired estimates.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
2<p<\infty
and
\Re\sigma>-\kappa(p)
, there exists
\varepsilon(p)>0
such that the following estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f\|_{p},\quad for\ \ j\geq 1,

(3.5)

holds for all
f\in{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
.

Before delving into the proof of Lemma
3.3
, we require the subsequent proposition, which will be rigorously established in the following subsection.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
2<p<\infty
and
\Re\sigma>-\kappa(p)
, there exists
\varepsilon(p)>0
such that the following estimate

\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f\|_{p},\quad for\ \ j\geq 1,

(3.6)

holds for all
f\in{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Let
\Delta_{\ell}^{j}
be the Littlewood-Paley operator, that is

\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f(x):=\left(\tilde{\beta}(2^{-j}|2^{\ell}\cdot|)\hat{f}(\cdot)\right)^{\vee}(x),

where
\tilde{\beta}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a nonnegative function which satisfies
\beta(|t\xi|)=\beta(|t\xi|)\tilde{\beta}(|\xi|)
for any
t\in[1,2)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Then,

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1\leq t<2}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\xi x}m_{\sigma}(|2^{\ell}t\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j+\ell}t\xi|)\tilde{\beta}(|2^{-j+\ell}\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1\leq t<2}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i\xi x}m_{\sigma}(|2^{\ell}t\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j+\ell}t\xi|)\widehat{\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f}(\xi)\ d\xi\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1\leq t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}\Big((\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f)(2^{\ell}\cdot)\Big)(2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}.

To proceed with the argument, we assert the following claim:

\Big\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1\leq t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}\left((\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f)(2^{\ell}\cdot)\right)(2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}(\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f)(2^{\ell}\cdot)\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.8)

We prove the above claim at the end of this proof.
Now combining the inequalities (
3.1
), claim (
3.8
) and Lemma
2.5
, we deduce

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{t>0}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}(\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f)(2^{\ell}\cdot)\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|(\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f)(\cdot)\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}|\Delta_{\ell}^{j}f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f\|_{p},

where the last inequality follows from Lemma
2.6
and we complete the proof of Lemma
3.3
once we prove the claim (
3.8
).

For this target, let
f_{\ell}
be an element in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathcal{M}
associated with an integer
\ell
, it suffices to show

\Big\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})(2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}f_{\ell}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.9)

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
, we write
\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})
as
\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})]+i\Im[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})]
where

\begin{split}\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})]&=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})+\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})\Big)^{*}\Big),\\
\Im[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})]&=\frac{1}{2i}\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})-\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})\Big)^{*}\Big).\end{split}

By the triangle inequality, a fact that the adjoint map is an isometric
isomorphism on
L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
and applying Proposition
3.4
,

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{-2\ell}{p}}\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})](2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})]\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})\Big\|_{p}+\frac{1}{2}\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\Big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})\Big)^{*}\Big\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f_{\ell}\|_{p}.

Thus for
\epsilon>0
, we can find a positive element
F_{\ell,\epsilon}
such that for all
1<t<2
,

-F_{\ell,\epsilon}\leq\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})](2^{-\ell}\cdot)\leq F_{\ell,\epsilon}

and
\|F_{\ell,\epsilon}\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim 2^{-p\varepsilon(p)j+2\ell}\|f_{\ell}\|_{p}^{p}+2^{-|\ell|}\epsilon
.
Let
F_{\epsilon}:=\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}F_{\ell,\epsilon}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}
, then

-F_{\epsilon}\leq\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})](2^{-\ell}\cdot)\leq F_{\epsilon}

for all
\ell\in\mathbb{Z},1<t<2
. And we have

\|F_{\epsilon}\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|F_{\ell,\epsilon}\|_{p}^{p}\lesssim 2^{-p\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}f_{\ell}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)+\epsilon.

Let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we deduce

\Big\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\Re[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})](2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}f_{\ell}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.10)

Similarly we also have

\Big\|{\sup_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\Im[\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}(f_{\ell})](2^{-\ell}\cdot)\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\Big(\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}}\|2^{\frac{2\ell}{p}}f_{\ell}\|_{p}^{p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(3.11)

Using the triangle inequality, along with the inequalities (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we can derive (
3.9
).
∎

3.2.
Proof of Proposition
3.4

To prove Proposition
3.4
, we first state a key proposition whose proof will be given later.

Proposition 3.5
.

Fix
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\big((1/8,8)\big)
, let
a(\xi,t)
be a symbol of order zero, i.e. for any
t\in(1,2),\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
,

\Big|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\xi}\right)^{\alpha}a(\xi,t)\Big|\leq c_{\alpha}(1+|\xi|)^{-|\alpha|}.

Define the operator
F_{j}
by

F_{j}f(x,t):=\rho_{1}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+t|\xi|)}\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)a(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi

for
j\geq 1
. Then

\|F_{j}f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\leq C_{\mu}2^{\mu j}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)},

(3.12)

for all
\mu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
.

In
[
43
]
, the authors utilized the Sobolev embedding inequality to prove Proposition
3.4
in the commutative case. Here, we use the following alternative lemma, which should be regarded as a cognate version of the Sobolev embedding inequality. A more complete theory of the noncommutative Sobolev embedding inequalities can be found in
[
21
]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Given
F\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})
, let
I
be an interval on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathcal{N_{I}}
be the tensor von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(I)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
. Suppose that
2\leq p\leq\infty
, then for all
0<\lambda\leq|I|
, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}F(t)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1/p}\Big\|F\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})}+\lambda^{1-1/p}\Big\|F^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})}.

Proof.

Fix
t\in I
, and let
I_{t}\subset I
be an interval containing
t
with
|I_{t}|=\lambda
. For any
s\in I_{t}
,

F(t)-F(s)=\int_{s}^{t}F^{\prime}(r)dr,

then integrating both sides over
s\in I_{t}
,

\lambda F(t)=\int_{I_{t}}F(s)ds+\int_{I_{t}}\int_{s}^{t}F^{\prime}(r)drds.

Employing Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
, along with the operator monotone property of the function
t^{2/p}
for
p\geq 2
, we derive the following operator inequality,

\displaystyle|\lambda F(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big|\int_{I_{t}}F(s)ds\Big|^{2}+2\Big|\int_{I_{t}}\int_{s}^{t}F^{\prime}(r)drds\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda\int_{I_{t}}|F(s)|^{2}ds+2\lambda\int_{I_{t}}|t-s|\int_{s}^{t}|F^{\prime}(r)|^{2}drds

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{2-2/p}\Big(\int_{I_{t}}|F(s)|^{p}ds\Big)^{2/p}+2\lambda\int_{I_{t}}|t-s|^{2-2/p}\Big(\int_{s}^{t}|F^{\prime}(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}ds

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{2-2/p}\Big(\int_{I}|F(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}+2\lambda^{4-2/p}\Big(\int_{I}|F^{\prime}(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}.

(3.13)

Note that the complex interpolation theorem for the maximal norm (see Lemma
2.7
) implies that

\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}F(t)\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}|F(t)|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/4}\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}|F^{*}(t)|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/4}.

(3.14)

To prove our lemma, it suffices to show

\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}|F(t)|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1/p}\Big\|F\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})}+\lambda^{1-1/p}\Big\|F^{\prime}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})},

(3.15)

since the second term in (
3.14
) can be handled similarly. Proceeding further, by the operator inequality (
3.13
) and the triangle inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{t\in I}}^{+}|F(t)|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\Big\|\lambda^{-2/p}\Big(\int_{I}|F(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}+\lambda^{2-2/p}\Big(\int_{I}|F^{\prime}(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{-2/p}\Big\|\Big(\int_{I}|F(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{\mathcal{M}})}+2\lambda^{2-2/p}\Big\|\Big(\int_{I}|F^{\prime}(r)|^{p}dr\Big)^{2/p}\Big\|_{L_{p/2}(\mathcal{\mathcal{M}})}

\displaystyle=2\lambda^{-2/p}\big\|F\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})}^{2}+2\lambda^{2-2/p}\big\|F^{\prime}\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{\mathcal{N}_{I}})}^{2},

which implies (
3.15
).
∎

To the end, we assume that
t\in(1,2)
and
j
is a sufficiently large positive integer. We are now ready to prove Proposition
3.4
.

Recall the definition of the operator
\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}
:

\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

where (see Lemma
3.1
)

(t|\xi|)^{\sigma}m_{\sigma}(t|\xi|)=(t|\xi|)^{-1/2}e^{it|\xi|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}a_{\ell}\cdot(t|\xi|)^{-\ell}+(t|\xi|)^{-1/2}e^{-it|\xi|}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}b_{\ell}\cdot(t|\xi|)^{-\ell}+R_{\sigma,N}(t|\xi|),

with
a_{\ell},b_{\ell}
being suitable constants, and the error term
R_{\sigma,N}
satisfying the decay condition:

\Big|\Big(\frac{d}{dr}\Big)^{k}R_{\sigma,N}(r)\Big|\leq C_{k}r^{-N-k}\quad\text{for}\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ r\geq 1.

(3.16)

We introduce two symbols of order zero,
A_{1}(\xi,t)
and
A_{2}(\xi,t)
, defined as

A_{1}(\xi,t):=\frac{2^{(1/2+\sigma)j}\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)}{(t|\xi|)^{1/2+\sigma}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}a_{\ell}\cdot(t|\xi|)^{-\ell},

A_{2}(\xi,t):=\frac{2^{(1/2+\sigma)j}\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)}{(t|\xi|)^{1/2+\sigma}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N}b_{\ell}\cdot(t|\xi|)^{-\ell}.

Furthermore, we decompose the operator
\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}
into three parts,
\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{\sigma}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\mathcal{A}_{t,j,i}^{\sigma}
, where

\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f(x)=2^{-(1/2+\sigma)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi+it|\xi|}A_{1}(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

\mathcal{A}_{t,j,2}^{\sigma}f(x)=2^{-(1/2+\sigma)j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi-it|\xi|}A_{2}(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

\mathcal{A}_{t,j,3}^{\sigma}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}|t\xi|^{-\sigma}R_{\sigma,N}(t|\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

Therefore, it suffices to show for
i=1,2,3
, and
\Re\sigma>-\kappa(p)
, one may find a positive real number
\varepsilon(p)
such that

\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j,i}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f\|_{L_{p}}.

(3.17)

For
i=3
, the inequality (
3.17
) follows from the same argument as in Lemma
3.2
once we apply the decay property (
3.16
) of
R_{\sigma,N}
.

We only give the proof of (
3.17
) for
i=1
below since the proof for
i=2
is similar.
Let
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\big((1/8,8))
and
\rho_{0}(r)=\rho_{1}(r)=1
when
r\in(1/4,4)
. Then

\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f(x)=2^{-(1/2+\sigma)j}\rho_{1}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi+it|\xi|}\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)A_{1}(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

Employing Proposition
3.5
with
\mu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
, we establish the estimate

\Big\|\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{\mu}2^{(\mu-1/2-\Re\sigma)j}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

(3.18)

Next, we define
\tilde{A_{1}}(\xi)(t)=2^{-j}\big(\frac{d}{dt}\big)(e^{it|\xi|}A_{1}(\xi,t))
, which is a symbol of order zero.
Applying Proposition
3.5
once more, we derive

\Big\|\partial_{t}\big(\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f\big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{\mu}2^{(\mu+1/2-\Re\sigma)j}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

(3.19)

To proceed with the arguments, we choose
I=(1,2)
and
\lambda=2^{-j}
in Lemma
3.6
. Combining the inequalities (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), and obtain

\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim C_{\mu}2^{(\mu+1/p-1/2-\Re\sigma)j}\|f\|_{p},

which holds for all
\mu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
. When
\Re\sigma>-\kappa(p)
, we can choose suitable
\mu
such that
\mu+1/p-1/2-\Re\sigma=\varepsilon(p)>0
. Consequently, we arrive at

\Big\|{\sup_{1<t<2}}^{+}\mathcal{A}_{t,j,1}^{\sigma}f\Big\|_{p}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon(p)j}\|f\|_{p}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.4
for the case
i=1
.

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this subsection, we will show that Proposition
3.5
implies Theorem
1.3
. Employing Lemma
3.1
, we express the solution to the Cauchy problem of the wave equation as

u(x,t)=c_{0}\mathcal{A}_{t}^{-1/2}f(x)+c_{1}t\mathcal{A}_{t}^{1/2}g(x)

where
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
are universal constants. By applying the triangle inequality, we derive the inequality

\|u\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\Big\|\mathcal{A}_{t}^{-1/2}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}+\Big\|t\mathcal{A}_{t}^{1/2}g\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Next, it suffices to demonstrate that for
\nu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
, we have

\Big\|\mathcal{A}_{t}^{-1/2}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|(I+\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

(3.20)

and

\Big\|t\mathcal{A}_{t}^{1/2}g\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|(I+\Delta)^{(\nu-1)/2}g\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

(3.21)

Furthermore, adopting the methodology employed in the proof of Theorem
1.4
, it suffices to show that for
\nu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big\|\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{-1/2}f\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|(I+\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

and

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big\|t\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{1/2}g\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|(I+\Delta)^{(\nu-1)/2}g\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

Given that the proof in Lemma
3.2
still work here, we are reduced to showing that for a large
j>0
with
\nu=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)+2\varepsilon
, the inequalities

\Big\|\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{-1/2}\big((I+\Delta)^{-\nu/2}f\big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

(3.22)

and

\Big\|t\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{1/2}\big((I+\Delta)^{-(\nu-1)/2}g\big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon j}\|g\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

(3.23)

hold for all
\varepsilon>0
.
Here, we focus on the proof of inequality (
3.23
), as the proof for inequality (
3.22
) can be done similarly. Recall the expression for
m_{\frac{1}{2}}(t|\xi|)
, given by

m_{\frac{1}{2}}(t|\xi|)=c\big(t|\xi|\big)^{-1}\sin(t|\xi|)=\frac{c}{2i}\big(t|\xi|\big)^{-1}\big(e^{it|\xi|}-e^{-it|\xi|}\big).

Thus we have

\displaystyle t\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{1/2}\big((I+\Delta)^{-(\nu-1)/2}g\big)(x)

\displaystyle=ct\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-(\nu-1)/2}m_{\frac{1}{2}}(t|\xi|)\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{g}(\xi)\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}\big(e^{it|\xi|}-e^{-it|\xi|}\big)\frac{\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)\hat{g}(\xi)}{|\xi|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{(\nu-1)/2}}\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{c}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}\big(e^{it|\xi|}-e^{-it|\xi|}\big)2^{-\nu j}a(\xi,t)\hat{g}(\xi)\ d\xi,

(3.24)

where

a(\xi,t)=\frac{2^{\nu j}\beta(|2^{-j}t\xi|)}{|\xi|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{(\nu-1)/2}}

is a symbol of order zero. Next, let
\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\big((1/8,8))
and
\rho_{0}(r)=\rho_{1}(r)=1
when
r\in(1/4,4)
. For
t\in[1,2]
, we can rewrite the integral as

t\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{1/2}\big((I+\Delta)^{-(\nu-1)/2}g\big)(x)=\frac{c2^{-\nu j}\rho_{1}(t)}{2i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{ix\xi}\big(e^{it|\xi|}-e^{-it|\xi|}\big)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)a(\xi,t)\hat{g}(\xi)\ d\xi.

Combining this with Proposition
3.5
, and choosing
\mu=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)+\varepsilon
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|t\mathcal{A}_{t,j}^{1/2}\big((I+\Delta)^{-(\nu-1)/2}g\big)\Big\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\nu j+\mu j}\|g\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-\varepsilon j}\|g\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Proposition

In this subsection, we commence by proving Proposition
3.5
specifically for the cases
p=2
and
p=\infty
.
Employing the Plancherel theorem twice, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{j}f\|_{L_{2}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big|\rho_{1}(t)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)a(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)\Big|^{2}d\xi dt\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{1}^{2}\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\Big)dt=\|f\|_{L_{2}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}^{2}.

On the other hand, we express
F_{j}f(x,t)
as a convolution integral,

\displaystyle F_{j}f(x,t)

\displaystyle=\rho_{1}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+t|\xi|)}\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)a(\xi,t)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi

p
, by interpolation, it suffices to establish a crucial

Proposition 3.7
.

\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\in C_{0}^{\infty}\big((1/8,8)\big)

. Then we have

In this section, we fix a sufficiently large positive integer

|K_{II}(x,t)|\lesssim_{N}\frac{2^{-jN}}{(1+|(x,t)|^{2})^{N}}

(4.2)

for any

(see
[

, Page 81-88]

\Big\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{II}(x-y,t)f(y)dy\Big\|_{{}_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}}\lesssim_{N}2^{-jN}\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

4.1.
Some lemmas

In this subsection, we will introduce some frequency decompositions and prove some lemmas.

\phi

\rho_{0}
. We define a decomposition in
\tau

t

(P^{n}g)^{\wedge}(\xi,\tau):=\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\tau-n)\hat{g}(\xi,\tau).

, we define the operator
P_{m}
acting on operator-valued functions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as:

(P_{m}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})\hat{f}(\xi),

(4.4)

and

f_{n}:=\sum_{\big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\big||m|-n\big|<100\big\}}P_{m}f.

(4.5)

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g=(g_{n,v})_{n\in\Gamma_{j},v\in I}
be an element in
L_{p}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{2}^{c})
, where
I
is a finite index set and
2\leq p\leq\infty
,we have

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\big|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}P^{n}g_{n,v}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}\lesssim 2^{(1/4-1/2p)j}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|g_{n,v}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}.

(4.6)

Proof.

By the Plancherel theorem and the support property of
\phi
, we can establish the estimate (
4.6
) for
p=2
. Due to the complex interpolation (see Lemma
2.4
), it suffices to prove the inequality (
4.6
) for
p=\infty
.
Let
K^{n}
be the kernel associated with
P^{n}
, such that
P^{n}g(x,t)=(K^{n}\ast g)(x,t)
. From (
4.3
), we derive,

K^{n}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(x\xi+t\tau)}\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\tau-n)d\xi d\tau.

By applying integration by parts, we obtain the bound
|K^{n}(x,t)|\lesssim\frac{2^{\frac{5j}{2}}}{(1+2^{j}|x|+2^{j/2}|t|)^{3}}\triangleq\widetilde{K}(x,t)
, which is integrable. By Lemma
2.1
and the Young inequality,

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\big|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}P^{n}g_{n,v}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\big|\Gamma_{j}\big|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|P^{n}g_{n,v}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j/4}\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|K^{n}\ast g_{n,v}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j/4}\Big\|\widetilde{K}\ast\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|g_{n,v}|^{2}\Big)\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{j/4}\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|g_{n,v}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N})}^{1/2}.

Hence we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Given
P_{m}
as defined in (
4.4
), for
2\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|P_{m}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

(4.7)

Proof.

When
p=2
, the result follows from the Plancherel theorem. Therefore, due to interpolation, it suffices to prove the inequality for

p=\infty
. Recall that the definition of
P_{m}
in (
4.4
) involves the expression

(P_{m}f)^{\wedge}(\xi)=\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})\hat{f}(\xi).

For simplicity, we may assume
(P_{m}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})\hat{f}(\xi)
. Indeed, suppose

(\widetilde{P_{m}}f)^{\wedge}(\xi):=\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})\hat{f}(\xi)
, using the same technique as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we can deduce that

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|P_{m}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|\widetilde{P_{m}}f\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|P_{m}f(0)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-ix\xi}\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2})f(x)d\xi dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-ix\cdot m}\hat{\phi}(x_{1})\hat{\phi}(x_{2})f(2^{-j/2}x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{{[-\pi,\pi]}^{2}}e^{-ix\cdot m}h_{n}(x)dx\Big|=\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\hat{h}_{n}(m)\Big|,

where
h_{n}(x)=\hat{\phi}(x_{1}-2n_{1}\pi)\hat{\phi}(x_{2}-2n_{2}\pi)f(2^{-j/2}(x-2\pi n))
.
Combining these results with the triangle inequality, we obtain

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|P_{m}f(0)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|\hat{h}_{n}(m)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}.

(4.8)

Note that
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\subset\mathcal{B}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
, and applying the Parseval identity, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|\hat{h}_{n}(m)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}=1}\tau\big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|\hat{h}_{n}(m)b\big|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}=1}\tau\Big(\int_{{[-\pi,\pi]}^{2}}\big|h_{n}(x)b\big|^{2}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\int_{{[-\pi,\pi]}^{2}}\big|h_{n}(x)\big|^{2}dx\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}.

Using the inequality (
4.8
), we can derive the following:

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|P_{m}f(0)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Big\|\int_{{[-\pi,\pi]}^{2}}\big|h_{n}(x)\big|^{2}dx\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\Big(\int_{{[-\pi,\pi]}^{2}}\big|\hat{\phi}(x_{1}-2n_{1}\pi)\hat{\phi}(x_{2}-2n_{2}\pi)\big|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\times\|f\|_{L_{\infty}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\infty}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

Here, the final inequality holds due to the fact that
\hat{\phi}
is a Schwartz function, which ensures that the sum over
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
is convergent.
Similarly, for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\big|P_{m}f(y)\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{\infty}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)},

which implies the inequality (
4.7
) for
p=\infty
.
∎

We introduce the angular decomposition of the
\xi
-space, as outlined in
[
54
, Page 405]
. Consider a roughly equally spaced set of points on the unit circle
S^{1}
with grid length
2^{-j/2}
. Specifically, the set
\{\xi_{v}\}_{v=1}^{N(j)}
comprises the unit vectors that adhere to the following conditions:

(1)

|\xi_{v_{1}}-\xi_{v_{2}}|\geq 2^{-j/2}
, if
v_{1}\neq v_{2}
;

(2)

For any
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash 0}
, there exists one
\xi_{v}
, such that
\big|{\xi}/{|\xi|}-\xi_{v}\big|<2^{-j/2}
.

It is evident that
N(j)\thickapprox 2^{j/2}
, and we can assume that
\arg\xi_{v}=2\pi v2^{-j/2}
.
Let
\Omega_{v}
denote the cone in the
\xi
-space with the central direction
\xi_{v}
, defined as:

\Omega_{v}=\{\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}:\big|{\xi}/{|\xi|}-\xi_{v}\big|<2\cdot 2^{-j/2}\}.

Next, we construct an associated partition of unity: we select a family of smoothing functions
\chi_{v}
that satisfy

\sum_{v=1}^{N(j)}\chi_{v}(\xi)=1
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}
and possess the following properties:

(1)

\chi_{v}
is homogeneous of degree
0
, meaning
\chi_{v}(\xi)=\chi_{v}(\xi/|\xi|)
for all
\xi\in{\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\{0\}}
;

(2)

\chi_{v}
is supported in
\Omega_{v}
and fulfills the uniform estimate:

|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\xi_{v}(\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha}2^{|\alpha|j/2|}|\xi|^{-|\alpha|}.

We now define the operators as follows:

F_{v}(f)(x,t):=\rho_{1}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+t|\xi|)}\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)a(\xi,t)\chi_{v}(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi)d\xi

(4.9)

and

(Q_{v}g)^{\wedge}(\xi,\tau):=\psi\left(\frac{|\xi|-\tau}{|\xi|^{\epsilon}}\right)\chi_{v}(\xi)\hat{g}(\xi,\tau)\triangleq\Psi_{v}(\xi,\tau)\hat{g}(\xi,\tau),

(4.10)

where
\psi
is a smoothing function on
\mathbb{R}
supported in
[-2,2]
and equal to
1
in
[-1,1]
, and
\epsilon
is a suitably chosen small positive number.

Prior to proving Proposition
3.7
, we present two lemmas to address the error terms.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Big\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P^{n}F(f-f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim_{N}2^{-Nj}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

(4.11)

Proof.

Fix an integer
n
. Then,

\displaystyle P^{n}F(f-f_{n})(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+t\tau)}\big(\rho_{1}(\cdot)a(\xi,\cdot)\big)^{\vee}(\tau-|\xi|)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\phi(2^{-j/2}\tau-n)(1-\tilde{\phi}(\xi))\hat{f}(\xi)\ d\xi\ d\tau

\displaystyle\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{K}_{n}(x-y,t)f(y)dy,

where the kernel
\widetilde{K}_{n}(x,t)
is given by

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(x\xi+t\tau)}\big(\rho_{1}(\cdot)a(\xi,\cdot)\big)^{\vee}(\tau-|\xi|)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\tau-n)(1-\tilde{\phi}(\xi))\ d\xi\ d\tau

and

\tilde{\phi}(\xi)=\sum_{\big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\big||m|-n\big|<100\big\}}\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2}).

Recall that we have assumed that
\tau\thickapprox 2^{j}
, and by the support of
\phi
,
\widetilde{K}_{n}=0
if
n\notin\Gamma_{j}
. As the estimate (
4.2
), one has

|\widetilde{K}_{n}(x,t)|\lesssim_{N}\frac{2^{-Nj}}{(1+|(x,t)|^{2})^{N}}

for any
N>0
(see
[
52
, Page 82]
for more details). Then, by the Young inequality,

\Big\|P^{n}F(f-f_{n})\Big\|_{{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}}\lesssim_{N}2^{-Nj}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

Therefore, noting that
|\Gamma_{j}|\thickapprox 2^{j/2}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P^{n}F(f-f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\Big\|P^{n}F(f-f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{N}2^{-Nj}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Define
R_{v}(f):=F_{v}(f)-Q_{v}F_{v}(f)
, then for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we have

\|R_{v}(f)\|_{{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}}\lesssim_{N}2^{-Nj}\|f\|_{L_{p}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

(4.12)

Proof.

Note that
the Fourier transform of
F_{v}(f)
is given by

\hat{f}(\xi)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)\chi_{v}(\xi)(\rho_{1}(\cdot)a(\xi,\cdot))^{\vee}(\tau-|\xi|).

Then, the kernel associated with
R_{v}
is

K_{v}(x,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}e^{i(x\cdot\xi+t\tau)}\Big(1-\psi\big(\frac{|\xi|-\tau}{|\xi|^{\epsilon}}\big)\Big)\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)\chi_{v}(\xi)(\rho_{1}(\cdot)a(\xi,\cdot))^{\vee}(\tau-|\xi|)d\xi d\tau.

As the estimate (
4.2
), one has

|K_{v}(x,t)|\lesssim_{N}\frac{2^{-jN}}{(1+|(x,t)|^{2})^{N}}

for any
N>0
(see
[
52
, Page 84]
for further details). Now, we complete the proof as in Lemma
4.3
.
∎

4.2.
An
L_{4}
estimate

To prove the
L_{4}
-estimate (
4.1
), we start by noting that

\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f)=F(f).

Applying the triangle inequality and Lemma
4.3
, which also holds when
(P^{n})^{2}
is used instead of
P^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|F(f)\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f)\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f-f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

Next, let
I:=\{1\leq v\leq N(j):\xi_{v}\ \text{in the first quadrant}\}
and recall that
a(\xi,t)
in (
4.9
) is supported in the first quadrant with respect to the variable
\xi
. By the triangle inequality, the Young inequality and Lemma
4.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}F(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}=\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}\Big\|(P^{n})^{2}R_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}\Big\|R_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

The final inequality above uses the facts that
|I|\thickapprox|\Gamma_{j}|\thickapprox 2^{j/2}
and

\|f_{n}\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}=\Big\|\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\big||m|-n\big|<100}P_{m}f\Big\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

It now suffices to show:

\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}\lesssim_{\mu}2^{\mu j}\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)},\qquad\text{for all}\ \mu>1/8.

(4.13)

To proceed with the proof, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v\in I}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(I)+(II),

(4.14)

where

(I)=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|\leq 1000}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)

and

(II)=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big).

We first estimate part
(I)
. By the triangle inequality, and Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{i=-1000}^{1000}\sum_{v,v+i\in I}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v+i}F_{v+i}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=-1000}^{1000}\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,v+i\in I}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v+i}F_{v+i}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=-1000}^{1000}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v:v,v+i\in I}\big|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v+i:v,v+i\in I}\big|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v+i}F_{v+i}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\big|\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}.

By applying Lemma
4.1
, we arrive at

(I)\lesssim 2^{j/2}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}.

(4.15)

To estimate part
(II)
, we require a geometric estimate. For each
n\in\Gamma_{j},v\in I
, observe that the Fourier transform of
P^{n}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})
is supported in the region

\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}:=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:(\xi,\tau)\in\operatorname{Supp}\Psi_{v},|2^{-j/2}\tau-n|\leq 1\},

where
\Psi_{v}
was defined in (
4.10
).
Next, we derive an estimate for the number of overlaps of algebraic differences between the sets
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
and
\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}
:

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C
, independent of
j
, such that

\sum_{n,m\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}.

(4.16)

Remark 4.6
.

Caused by the fact that
|x^{*}x|^{2}\neq|xx|^{2}
for a general operator
x
, a geometric estimate for the form like
\sup_{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{v,w\in I}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)
, which differs from the geometric estimate presented in the classical setting
[
43
]
, has to be considered. Furthermore, we cannot anticipate the same upper bound as in
[
43
]
for our new estimate, as
\sum_{v,w\in I}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)
may become significant large near the origin.
To overcome this obstacle, we observe that if
|v-w|\geq C
, where
C
is a large positive constant, then
\sup_{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>C}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)
admits a suitable upper bound. Consequently, we must partition the double sums over
v,w
in the inequality (
4.14
). Therefore, in comparison to Lemma 1.2 in
[
43
]
, we require a stronger version as Lemma
4.1
to tackle the first part
(I)
.

The proof of Lemma
4.5
will be given in the subsequent section. For the term
(II)
, invoking the Plancherel theorem, we derive

\displaystyle(II)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)\big(\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}(P^{n})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n})\big)\Big|^{2}\Big)

(4.17)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\big(((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)^{\wedge}\ast\big((P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\big)^{\wedge}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=\varphi\Big(\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w}}\big(((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)^{\wedge}\ast\big((P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\big)^{\wedge}\Big|^{2}\Big).

By Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\Big|\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w}}\big(((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)^{\wedge}\ast\big((P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\big)^{\wedge}\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\Big|\big(((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}\big)^{\wedge}\ast\big((P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\big)^{\wedge}\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w}}\Big).

(4.18)

Combining the inequalities (

), (

), (

\displaystyle(II)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}\varphi\Big(\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\Big|((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}(P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}\varphi\Big(\sum_{v,w\in I}\sum_{n,n^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{j}}\Big|((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n}))^{*}(P^{n^{\prime}})^{2}Q_{w}F_{w}(f_{n^{\prime}})\Big|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|\big((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big)^{*}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}.

(4.19)

By leveraging the inequalities (
4.13
), (
4.14
), (
4.15
), and (
4.19
), Proposition
3.7
can be reduced to demonstrating the following inequalities:

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}2^{\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)},

(4.20)

and

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|\big((P^{n})^{2}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big)^{*}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}\leq C_{\varepsilon}2^{\varepsilon j}\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}

(4.21)

for
\varepsilon>0
.
Here, we will concentrate on proving the inequality (
4.20
), since the proof of the inequality (
4.21
) is analogous.

Using Lemma
4.4
, it is sufficient to estimate the norm

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}.

Indeed, by applying the triangle inequality alongside Lemma
4.4
, we derive the following inequality:

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}R_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}+\|f\|_{L_{4}\left(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}\right)}.

We assert that the following inequality holds:

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}\lesssim\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}.

(4.22)

To justify this claim, recall from the proof of Lemma
4.1
that the kernel
K^{n}
associated with the operator
P^{n}
satisfies the bound

|K^{n}(x,t)|\leq C_{N}\frac{2^{\frac{5j}{2}}}{(1+2^{j}|x|+2^{j/2}|t|)^{N}}.

Combining this with Lemma
2.1
, and denoting
F_{v}(f_{n})
by
g_{v,n}
,

\displaystyle\big|P^{n}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K^{n}(y,s)g_{v,n}(y-x,s-t)dyds\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K^{n}(y,s)|dyds\Big)\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K^{n}(y,s)||g_{v,n}(y-x,s-t)|^{2}dyds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2^{\frac{5j}{2}}|g_{v,n}(y-x,s-t)|^{2}}{(1+2^{j}|y|+2^{j/2}|s|)^{N}}dyds.

(4.23)

Utilizing the inequality (
4.23
), the triangle inequality, and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we derive

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|P^{n}Q_{v}F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2^{\frac{5j}{2}}|g_{v,n}(y-x,s-t)|^{2}}{(1+2^{j}|y|+2^{j/2}|s|)^{N}}dyds\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{2^{\frac{5j}{2}}}{(1+2^{j}|y|+2^{j/2}|s|)^{N}}\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|g_{v,n}(y-x,s-t)|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}dyds\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|g_{v,n}|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}=\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{4}.

This confirms our claim (
4.22
). Now, we proceed to give an estimate for the term

\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}.

As in
[
43
]
,
for
v\in I,n\in\Gamma_{j}
, we define the set

\mathcal{J}_{v,n}:=\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\chi_{v}(P_{m}f)^{\vee}\neq 0,\big||m|-n\big|\leq 100\}.

It follows that
|\mathcal{J}_{v,n}|\lesssim 1
and, for

, there exists an absolute constant
C
such that

\big|\{(v,n)\in I\times\Gamma_{j}:m\in\mathcal{J}_{v,n}\}\big|\leq C.

(4.24)

Furthermore, we have

, where

. Before presenting the final proof of Proposition
3.7
, we introduce the following lemma, which will be proved in the subsequent section.

Lemma 4.7
.

For
v\in I,n\in\Gamma_{j}
and
g\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, let
K_{v,n}(y,t)
be the kernel associated with
F_{v}(f_{n})
. Then,

\sup_{y,t,v,n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|dx\leq C

(4.25)

and

\Big\|{\sup_{v\in I,n\in\Gamma_{j}}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|g(y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}2^{\varepsilon j}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}

(4.26)

for
\varepsilon>0
.

Applying Lemma
2.1
and the inequality (
4.25
), we derive

\displaystyle|F_{v}(f_{n})(y,t)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}K_{v,n}(y-x,t)f_{v,n}(x)dx\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)||f_{v,n}(x)|^{2}dx.

Combining this and using duality, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{4}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\big|F_{v}(f_{n})\big|^{2}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}

(4.27)

Note that by Lemma

|f_{v,n}|^{2}=\Big|\sum_{m\in\mathcal{J}_{v,n}}P_{m}f\Big|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{m\in\mathcal{J}_{v,n}}|P_{m}f|^{2}.

(4.28)

Combining the inequalities (

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}|f_{v,n}|^{2}\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big\|\sum_{v\in I}\sum_{n\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{J}_{v,n}}|P_{m}f|^{2}\Big\|_{2}

Hence by the inequalities (

By the inequality (

5.1.
Proof of Lemma
4.5

In this subsection, our attention is directed towards proving Lemma
4.5
, which involves estimating the number of overlaps between algebraic differences of the sets
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
and
\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}
. Recall that the set
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
is defined as follows:

\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}:=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:(\xi,\tau)\in\operatorname{Supp}\Psi_{v},|2^{-j/2}\tau-n|\leq 1\}.

where
\Psi_{v}(\xi,\tau)=\psi\big(\frac{|\xi|-\tau}{|\xi|^{\epsilon}}\big)\chi_{v}(\xi)
. Here,
\psi
is a smoothing function on
\mathbb{R}
with support in
[-2,2]
and taking value
1
within
[-1,1]
. Notably,
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
is contained within a larger set (also denoted by
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
) defined by

\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:dist((\xi,\tau),(\eta,|\eta|))\leq 2^{\epsilon j},\eta\in\Omega_{v,n}\}

with
\Omega_{v,n}
specified as

\Omega_{v,n}:=\{\eta:\ \mathrm{arg}\eta\in[2^{-j/2}(v-1),2^{-j/2}(v+1)],|\eta|\in[2^{j/2}(n-1),2^{j/2}(n+1)]\}.

To establish the desired result, it suffices to prove that there exists a constant
C
, independent of
j
, such that the following inequality holds:

\sum_{n,m\in\Gamma_{j}}\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}.

Observe that if
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
, then
\tau\in[2^{j/2}(n-1),2^{j/2}(n+1)]
. Consequently, for
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}
, we have
\tau\in[2^{j/2}(n-m-2),2^{j/2}(n-m+2)]
. Given a fixed
n\in\Gamma_{j}

and a fixed point
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, if
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{1}}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w_{1}}
and
(\xi,\tau)\in\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{2}}-\mathcal{U}_{m^{\prime}}^{w_{2}}
, we must have

[2^{j/2}(n-m-2),2^{j/2}(n-m+2)]\cap[2^{j/2}(n-m^{\prime}-2),2^{j/2}(n-m^{\prime}+2)]\neq\emptyset.

This implies that
|m-m^{\prime}|\leq 4
.
Hence, for a fixed
(\xi,\tau)
and
n\in\Gamma_{j}
, there can be at most nine distinct
m\in\Gamma_{j}
such that

\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\neq 0.

Since
|\Gamma_{j}|\approx 2^{j/2}
, it remains to demonstrate that for any fixed

n,m\in\Gamma_{j}
, the following estimate holds for an absolute constant
C
,

\sum_{v,w\in I,|v-w|>1000}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\leq C2^{2\epsilon j}.

(5.1)

For simplicity,
we can assume that

\Omega_{v,n}:=\{\eta:\ \mathrm{arg}\eta\in[2^{-j/2}(v-1),2^{-j/2}(v+1)],|\eta|\in[2^{-j/2}(n-1),2^{-j/2}(n+1)]\},

and

\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}=\{(\xi,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:dist((\xi,\tau),(\eta,|\eta|))\leq 2^{\epsilon j-j},\eta\in\Omega_{v,n}\}.

Elements in
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}
can be expressed as

(2^{-j/2}n+r_{v})\cdot(e^{i(2^{-jv/2+\theta})},1)+\zeta_{v},

where
|r_{v}|\leq 2^{-j/2},|\theta|\leq 2^{-j/2}
and
\zeta_{v}
is a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\zeta_{v}|\leq 2^{\epsilon j-j}
.
For the sake of simplicity in the following proof, we denote
2^{-j/2}
by
\delta
. Without loss of generality, we assume
v>w
,
\delta n=1
,
\delta m=\lambda
for some constant
\lambda\thickapprox 1
. Elements of
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}
have the form

((1+r_{v})\cdot e^{i({v\delta+\theta_{v}})},r_{v})-((\lambda+r_{w})\cdot e^{i({w\delta+\theta_{w}})},r_{w})+\zeta_{v}-\zeta_{w},

(5.2)

where
|r_{v}|,|r_{w}|,|\theta_{v}|,|\theta_{w}|\leq\delta
and
|\zeta_{v}|,|\zeta_{w}|\leq\delta^{2-2\epsilon}
.
To establish the inequality (
5.1
), it suffices to demonstrate that for a fixed pair
(v_{1},w_{1})
,

|\{(v_{2},w_{2}):\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{1}}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w_{1}}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{2}}-\mathcal{U}_{n^{\prime}}^{w_{2}}\neq\emptyset\}\lesssim\delta^{-4\epsilon}.

(5.3)

In fact, we will show below that
|v_{1}-v_{2}|,|w_{1}-w_{2}|\lesssim\delta^{-2\epsilon}
, which immediately implies the inequality (
5.3
).

Given that
\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{1}}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w_{1}}\cap\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v_{2}}-\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w_{2}}\neq\emptyset
, by (
5.2
), there exist
|r_{v_{i}}|,|r_{w_{i}}|,|\theta_{v_{i}}|,|\theta_{w_{i}}|\leq\delta
for
i=1,2,
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
|\zeta|\leq 4\delta^{2-2\epsilon}
such that

\displaystyle((1+r_{v_{1}})\cdot e^{i({v_{1}\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}})},1+r_{v_{1}})-((\lambda+r_{w_{1}})\cdot e^{i({{w_{1}}\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}})},\lambda+r_{w_{1}})+\zeta

\displaystyle=((1+r_{v_{2}})\cdot e^{i({{v_{2}}\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}})},1+r_{v_{2}})-((\lambda+r_{w_{2}})\cdot e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})},\lambda+r_{w_{2}}).

Comparing the third coordinate, we obtain

|r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}}-r_{w_{1}}+r_{w_{2}}|\leq 4\delta^{2-2\epsilon}.

(5.4)

Next, by comparing the first two coordinates, we have

\displaystyle(1+r_{v_{1}})(e^{i({v_{1}\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}})}-e^{i({{v_{2}}\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}})})+(r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}})(e^{i({{v_{2}}\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}})}-e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})})

\displaystyle=(\lambda+r_{w_{1}})(e^{i({{w_{1}}\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}})}-e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})})+(r_{w_{1}}-r_{w_{2}}-r_{v_{1}}+r_{v_{2}})e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})}+x,

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
|x|\leq 4\delta^{2-2\epsilon}
, by the inequality (
5.4
), we have

\displaystyle(1+r_{v_{1}})(e^{i({v_{1}\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}})}-e^{i({{v_{2}}\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}})})+(r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}})(e^{i({{v_{2}}\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}})}-e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})})

\displaystyle=(\lambda+r_{w_{1}})(e^{i({{w_{1}}\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}})}-e^{i({{w_{2}}\delta+\theta_{w_{2}}})})+\tilde{x},

(5.5)

where
\tilde{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
with

.
Using the identity

e^{i\alpha_{1}}-e^{i\alpha_{2}}=2\sin((\alpha_{1}-\alpha_{2})/2)ie^{i((\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})/2)},

the left-hand side of equation (
5.5
) becomes

\displaystyle 2(1+r_{v_{1}})\sin\big(\big((v_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}}\big)/2\big)ie^{i(((v_{1}+v_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}+\theta_{v_{2}})/2)}

\displaystyle+2(r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}})\sin\big(\big((v_{2}-w_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}-\theta_{w_{2}}\big)/2\big)ie^{i(((v_{2}+w_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}+\theta_{w_{2}})/2)},

(5.6)

while the right-hand side of equation (
5.5
) becomes

2(1+r_{w_{1}})\sin\big(\big((w_{1}-w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}-\theta_{w_{2}}\big)/2\big)ie^{i(((w_{1}+w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}+\theta_{w_{2}})/2)}+\tilde{x}.

(5.7)

The length of the projection from (

) onto the direction
e^{i(((w_{1}+w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}+\theta_{w_{2}})/2)}
is denoted by
D
, and it equals

\displaystyle\big|2(1+r_{v_{1}})\sin\big(((w_{1}+w_{2}-v_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}+\theta_{w_{2}}-\theta_{v_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}})/2\big)

\displaystyle\times\sin\big(\big((v_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}}\big)/2\big)+2(r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}})\sin\big(\big((v_{2}-w_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{2}}-\theta_{w_{2}}\big)/2\big)

\displaystyle\times\sin\big(((w_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}})/2\big)\big|.

(5.8)

Recalling that
\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\leq\sin(\alpha)\leq\alpha
for

,
|r_{v_{i}}|,|r_{w_{i}}|,|\theta_{v_{i}}|,|\theta_{w_{i}}|\leq\delta
and

, one can derive

D\geq\frac{|(v_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}}|}{10}(v_{2}-w_{2}+v_{1}-w_{1}-4)\delta-(v_{2}-w_{2})\delta^{2}.

(5.9)

On the other hand, (
5.7
) implies
D\leq|\tilde{x}|\leq 8\delta^{2-2\epsilon}
. We claim that
|v_{1}-v_{2}|\leq 100\delta^{-2\epsilon}
,
otherwise, by the inequality (
5.9
),

8\delta^{2-2\epsilon}\geq 2(v_{2}-w_{2})\delta^{2-2\epsilon}-(v_{2}-w_{2})\delta^{2}\geq(v_{2}-w_{2})\delta^{2-2\epsilon},

leading to a contradiction since
v_{2}-w_{2}>1000
.
Using the same trick when we consider the projection onto the direction
ie^{i(((w_{1}+w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}+\theta_{w_{2}})/2)}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|2(1+r_{w_{1}})\sin\big(\big((w_{1}-w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}-\theta_{w_{2}}\big)/2\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq|\tilde{x}|+\big|2(1+r_{v_{1}})\sin\big(\big((v_{1}-v_{2})\delta+\theta_{v_{1}}-\theta_{v_{2}}\big)/2\big)\big|+|r_{v_{1}}-r_{v_{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq 8\delta^{2-2\epsilon}+100\delta^{1-2\epsilon}+2\delta\lesssim\delta^{1-2\epsilon}.

This implies

|(w_{1}-w_{2})\delta+\theta_{w_{1}}-\theta_{w_{2}}|\lesssim\delta^{1-2\epsilon},

and thus
|w_{1}-w_{2}|\lesssim\delta^{-2\epsilon}
since

.
Hence, we conclude the inequality (

), which leads to the desired lemma.

Remark 5.1
.

In the paper by Mockenhaupt, Seeger, and Sogge
[
43
]
, they derived an estimate for the number of overlaps of algebraic sums, given by:

\sum_{v,w\in I}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}+\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\leq Cj2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}.

Our method is equally applicable in this context, and furthermore, allows us to refine the estimate slightly. Specifically, we obtain:

\sum_{v,w\in I}\chi_{\mathcal{U}_{n}^{v}+\mathcal{U}_{m}^{w}}(\xi,\tau)\leq C2^{j/2+2\epsilon j}.

5.2.
Proof of Lemma
4.7

Let us begin by estimating the kernel
K_{v,n}(y,t)
. Recalling the definitions of

from (

) and (

), we derive

K_{v,n}(y,t)=\rho_{1}(t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i(y\cdot\xi+t|\xi|)}\rho_{0}(|2^{-j}\xi|)\chi_{v}(\xi)a(\xi,t)\tilde{\phi}(\xi)\ d\xi,

where

\tilde{\phi}(\xi)=\sum_{\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:\big||m|-n\big|<100\}}\rho(2^{-j}|\xi|)\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{1}-m_{1})\phi(2^{-j/2}\xi_{2}-m_{2}).

Through integration by parts, one can establish

|K_{v,n}(y,t)|\leq C_{N}\frac{2^{j}}{(1+|2^{j}(\langle y,\xi_{v}\rangle+t)|^{2})^{N}}\frac{2^{j/2}}{(1+|2^{j/2}(y-\langle y,\xi_{v}\rangle\xi_{v})|^{2})^{N}}

(5.10)

for any
N>0
(see e.g.
[
43
, (1.10)]
for further details). Utilizing the inequality (
5.10
), we deduce the inequality (
4.25
), which states

\sup_{y,t,v,n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|dx\lesssim 1.

Next, it suffices to prove the inequality (
4.26
), which asserts that for
g\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, we have

\Big\|{\sup_{v\in I,n\in\Gamma_{j}}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{v,n}(y,t;x)|g(y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}2^{\varepsilon j}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}

(5.11)

for
\varepsilon>0
.

Using a standard argument, it suffices to demonstrate the inequality (
5.11
) for
g\in\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathcal{M})
.
Given the inequality (
5.10
), we observe that
K_{v,n}(y,t)
is essentially supported within a rectangle of size
2^{-j}\times 2^{-j/2}\times 1
centered around the ray
\gamma_{v}
, defined as:

\gamma_{v}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:y+t\xi_{v}=0\}.

Motivated by this observation, for
\ell\geq 0
, we define the rectangle of size
2^{-j+\ell}\times 2^{-j/2+\ell}\times 1
centered around
\gamma_{v}
as

\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}:=\{(y,0)+t(-\xi_{v},1)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2]:|\langle y,\xi_{v}\rangle|\leq 2^{-j+\ell},\ |\langle y,\xi_{v}^{\bot}\rangle|\leq 2^{-j/2+\ell}\},

where
\xi_{v}^{\bot}
is the unit vector orthogonal to
\xi_{v}
, and
\mathcal{R}_{v,-1}:=\varnothing
.
Utilizing the inequality (
5.10
) and noting that
\operatorname{Supp}\rho_{1}\subset[1,2]
, we can write

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|g(y,t)dydt&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell-1}}|K_{v,n}(y,t)|g(x+y,t)dydt\\
&\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}\backslash\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell-1}}\frac{2^{3j/2}g(x+y,t)dydt}{(1+2^{2\ell})^{N}(1+2^{2\ell})^{N}}\\
&\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-2\ell(2N-1)}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}|}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}}g(x+y,t)dydt.\end{split}

(5.12)

Furthermore, we split each
\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}
along its longer dimension of size
2^{-j/2+\ell}
into
2^{j/2+\ell}
pieces and along its shorter dimension of size
2^{-j+\ell}
into
2^{\ell}
pieces, yielding a collection of rectangles
\{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell,i}\}_{i=1}^{{i(\ell)}}
of dimensions
2^{-j}\times 2^{-j}\times 1
, where
i(\ell)\thickapprox 2^{j/2+2\ell}
. Specifically

\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell,i}:=\{(y,0)+(x_{i},0)+t(-\xi_{v},1)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2]:|\langle y,\xi_{v}\rangle|\leq 2^{-j},\ |\langle y,\xi_{v}^{\bot}\rangle|\leq 2^{-j}\},

for some
x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}\subset\bigcup_{i=0}^{i(\ell)}\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell,i}
. Let
\gamma_{v,x_{i}}
denote the ray
\gamma_{v}+(x_{i},0)
and
\mathcal{T}_{v,i}
the cylinder defined as

\mathcal{T}_{v,i}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[1,2]:dist\{(y,t),\gamma_{v,x_{i}}\}<2^{-j+1}\}.

It is straightforward to verify that
\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell,i}\subset\mathcal{T}_{v,i}
. Note that the measures
|\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}|\thicksim 2^{\frac{-3j}{2}+2\ell}
and
|\mathcal{T}_{v,i}|\thicksim 2^{-2j+2}
. Proceeding with the inequalities (
5.12
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|g(y,t)dydt

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}2^{-2\ell(2N-1)}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}|}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}}g(x+y,t)dydt

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{i(\ell)}2^{-2\ell(2N-1)}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell}|}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{v,\ell,i}}g(x+y,t)dydt

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{i(\ell)}2^{-4\ell N-j/2}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,i}|}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{v,i}}g(x+y,t)dydt.

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{v\in I,n\in\Gamma_{j}}}^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|K_{v,n}(y-x,t)|g(y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{i(\ell)}2^{-4\ell N-j/2}\Big\|{\sup_{v\in I}}^{+}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,i}|}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{v,i}}g(x+y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}.

Hence, it suffices to establish

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{v\in I}}^{+}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{v,i}|}\int_{\mathcal{T}_{v,i}}g(x+y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}2^{\varepsilon j}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}.

(5.13)

To prove the estimate (
5.13
), we require an operator-valued Kakeya-type estimate.
For
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
and
0<\delta<1/2
, we define a ray as

\gamma_{\theta}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:y+t(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)=0\}

and a unit cylinder as

\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:=\{(y,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[0,1]:dist\{(y,t),\gamma_{\theta}\}<\delta\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
g\in\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes\mathcal{S}_{+}(\mathcal{M})
, we have

\Big\|{\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}}g(x+y,t)dydt\Big\|_{2}\leq C|\log_{2}\delta|^{3}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}.

(5.14)

Proof.

Firstly, we select a suitable function
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that satisfies
\check{a}\geq 0
, and define

a_{\delta}(t,\xi):=\chi_{[0,1]}(t)a(\delta\xi)
. Note that

\displaystyle dist\{(y,t),\gamma_{\theta}\}^{2}

\displaystyle=|y|^{2}+t^{2}/2+t\langle y,(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\rangle-\langle y,(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\rangle^{2}/2

\displaystyle\geq|y+t(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)|^{2}/2.

Then, for
(y,t)\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta}
, we have

\check{a}_{\delta}(y+t(\cos\theta,\sin\theta))\gtrsim 1/\delta^{2}
. Therefore, we can bound the integral as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}}\int_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}}g(x+y,t)dydt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\check{a}_{\delta}(y+t(\cos\theta,\sin\theta))g(x+y,t)dydt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\check{a}_{\delta}(-x+y+t(\cos\theta,\sin\theta))g(y,t)dydt.

It suffices to show

\Big\|{\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}A_{\theta}g(x)\Big\|_{2}\leq C|\log_{2}\delta|^{3}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})},

(5.15)

where

A_{\theta}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i[-\langle x,\xi\rangle+t\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle]}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)d\xi dt

and

\tilde{g}(\xi,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{iy\xi}g(y,t)dy.

To prove the inequality (
5.15
), we define dyadic operators as follows:

A_{\theta}^{\lambda}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i[-\langle x,\xi\rangle+t\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle]}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)d\xi dt.

Here
\beta
is a smoothing function with support in
[1/2,2]
and satisfies
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta(2^{-j}r)=1
for all
r>0
.
Note that
a\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, one can decompose
A_{\theta}
as

A_{\theta}=\sum_{1<l<\log_{2}{\delta^{-1}}+C}A_{\theta}^{2^{l}}+\tilde{R}_{\theta},

where
C
is a fixed constant, and the kernel of

\tilde{R}_{\theta}
is controlled by
O(1+|y-x|)^{-N}
for any
N
with bounds independent of
\theta
(see
[
52
, Page 89]
). Therefore, it suffices to demonstrate that

\Big\|{\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}A_{\theta}^{\lambda}g(x)\Big\|_{2}\leq C(\log_{2}\lambda)^{2}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})},\ \lambda>2.

(5.16)

For
k=1,2,...
, we define

A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i[-\langle x,\xi\rangle+t\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle]}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)d\xi dt,

where
\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)=\beta\big(2^{-k}\lambda^{1/2}\langle(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\rangle\big).

Note that
|\langle(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\rangle|\leq 1
and the support of
\beta
is contained in
[1/2,2]
,
A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}
is nonzero only if
2^{k-1}\leq\lambda^{1/2}
. Furthermore, we define

A_{\theta}^{\lambda,0}=A_{\theta}^{\lambda}-\sum_{0\leq k-1\leq\log_{2}\lambda^{1/2}}A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}.

Thus, it suffices to show that for all
k=0,1,2,...
, there exists a constant
C
such that following estimate holds:

\Big\|{\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)\Big\|_{2}\leq C\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}.

(5.17)

Since
A_{\theta}^{\lambda}g
is positive, by Lemma
2.7
, we have

\Big\|{\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)\Big\|_{2}^{2}\leq\Big\|\sup_{\theta\in[0,2\pi]}|A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)|^{2}\Big\|_{1}.

(5.18)

Applying Lemma
3.6
, for all
c\leq 2\pi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|{\sup_{r\in[0,2\pi]}}^{+}|A_{r}^{\lambda,k}g(x)|^{2}\Big\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 2c^{-1}\Big\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}|A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g|^{2}d\theta\Big\|_{1}+2c\Big\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big|\frac{\partial A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g}{\partial\theta}\big|^{2}d\theta\Big\|_{1}.

(5.19)

Letting
c=\lambda^{-1/2}2^{-k}
, combining the inequalities (
5.18
) and (
5.19
), to prove (
5.17
),
we only need to show

\Big\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta})^{\ell}A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g\Big|^{2}d\theta\Big\|_{1}^{1/2}\lesssim(\lambda^{-1/4}2^{-k/2})^{1-2\ell}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})},\ \ell=0,1.

(5.20)

Recall that on the support of the symbol of
A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}
, we have
\big|\langle(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\rangle\big|\thickapprox 2^{k}\lambda^{-1/2}
and
|\xi|\thickapprox\lambda
. Therefore,

\Big|\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Big)\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle\Big|=\big|\langle(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),\xi\rangle\big|\thickapprox 2^{k}\lambda^{1/2},

and
\Big|\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Big)\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)\Big|\lesssim 2^{-k}\lambda^{1/2}
. Consequently,
\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\big)A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}
behaves like
2^{k}\lambda^{1/2}A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}
, it suffices to prove the inequality (
5.20
) for
\ell=0
.

Recall that

\displaystyle A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{i[-\langle x,\xi\rangle+t\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle]}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)d\xi dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)dtd\xi.

By the Plancherel theorem, we can derive the following expression:

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g\Big|^{2}d\theta\Big\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|A_{\theta}^{\lambda,k}g(x)\Big|^{2}d\theta dx\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{it\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle}a_{\delta}(t,\xi)\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)\tilde{g}(\xi,t)dt\Big|^{2}d\theta d\xi\Big)

\displaystyle=\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}H^{\lambda,k}(t-t^{\prime},\xi)|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}\tilde{g}^{*}(\xi,t)\tilde{g}(\xi,t^{\prime})dtdt^{\prime}d\xi\Big),

where

H^{\lambda,k}(t-t^{\prime},\xi)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(t-t^{\prime})\langle(\cos\theta,\sin\theta),\xi\rangle}|\beta_{\lambda,k}(\xi,\theta)|^{2}d\theta.

For
k\geq 0
and any
N
, it is known (see
[
43
]
for further details) that

|H^{\lambda,k}(t-t^{\prime},\xi)|\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{k}(1+2^{2k}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-N},\ |\xi|\thickapprox\lambda.

Combining these results with the Hölder inequality and the Young inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}H^{\lambda,k}(t-t^{\prime},\xi)|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}\tilde{g}^{*}(\xi,t)\tilde{g}(\xi,t^{\prime})dtdt^{\prime}d\xi\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{k}\tau\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(1+2^{2k}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-N}|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}|\tilde{g}^{*}(\xi,t)\tilde{g}(\xi,t^{\prime})|dtdt^{\prime}d\xi\Big)

\displaystyle=C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tau\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(1+2^{2k}|t-t^{\prime}|)^{-N}|\tilde{g}^{*}(\xi,t)\tilde{g}(\xi,t^{\prime})|dtdt^{\prime}\Big)|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tau\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big|(1+2^{2k}|\cdot|)^{-N}\ast|\tilde{g}(\xi,\cdot)|\big|^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\quad\times\tau\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|\tilde{g}(\xi,t)|^{2}dt\Big)^{1/2}|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{-k}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\tau\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|\tilde{g}(\xi,t)|^{2}dt\Big)|\beta(|\xi|/\lambda)a(\delta\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-1/2}2^{-k}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N})}^{2}.

This completes the proof of the inequality (
5.20
).
∎

By applying Lemma
5.2
and the translation transform, we derive the inequality (
5.13
). Thus, the proof of Lemma
4.7
is concluded.

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.2
. To start, we first introduce the transference technique by defining a normal injective

*
-homomorphism, denoted as
\sigma_{\theta}
, from
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
to
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
. This map is specified by
\sigma_{\theta}(U(\xi)):=\exp_{\xi}\otimes U(\xi)
, where
\exp_{\xi}
represents the character
x\rightarrow\exp(i(x,\xi))
in
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see
[
19
, Corollary 1.4]
for further details). Let
m
be a reasonable function, and
T_{m}
denote the associated Fourier multiplier on
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. And we denote the associated Fourier multiplier on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
still by
T_{m}
by slightly abusing the notation. Then, we have the following intertwining identity:

\sigma_{\theta}\circ T_{m}=(T_{m}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})\circ\sigma_{\theta}.

(6.1)

Subsequently, we present the family of
*
-automorphism
(\alpha_{\eta})_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}
on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
, defined by
\alpha_{\eta}(U(\xi)):=\exp(i(\xi,\eta))U(\xi)
for each
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. These automorphisms satisfy several crucial properties:

(1)

For all
x\in\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
, the map
\eta\rightarrow\alpha_{\eta}x
from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
is weak*- continuous ;

(2)

For all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\alpha_{\eta}
is a
*
-automorphism of
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
;

(3)

For all
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have
\tau_{\theta}=\tau_{\theta}\circ\alpha_{\eta}
.

Combining these properties with Lemma 1.1 from
[
33
]
, we deduce the following proposition.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})
with
1\leq p<\infty
. Then the map
\eta\rightarrow\alpha_{\eta}x
from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
to
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})
is continuous in the norm topology, and for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\alpha_{\eta}
is an isometry on
L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})
.

Given that
\mathbb{R}^{2}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure
d\eta
is not a probability space, the intertwining identity (
6.1
) can not be efficiently applied. An approximation argument is required. We select the heat kernels
h_{\varepsilon}(\eta)=(\varepsilon/\pi)e^{-\varepsilon|\eta|^{2}}
. Equipped with the Gaussian measure
h_{\varepsilon}(\eta)d\eta
,
\mathbb{R}^{2}
becomes a probability space. We denote
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},d\eta)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}
by
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})
with
1\leq p\leq\infty
. We have
for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}=\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N_{\theta}})}.

(6.2)

Proof.

Applying Proposition
6.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h_{\varepsilon}(\eta)\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}d\eta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h_{\varepsilon}(\eta)\|\alpha_{\eta}x\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}d\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}h_{\varepsilon}(\eta)\|\sigma_{\theta}(x)(\eta)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}d\eta=\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}.

∎

We are now at the position to show Theorem
1.2
.
Given
x_{0}=U_{\theta}(f_{0}),x_{1}=U_{\theta}(f_{1})
for some
f_{0},f_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and under the condition
\nu>\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}-\kappa(p)
, we define

m_{0,t}^{\nu}:=c_{0}\frac{\cos(|t\xi|)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu/2}}\quad\text{and}\quad m_{1,t}^{\nu}:=c_{1}\frac{\sin(|t\xi|)}{|\xi|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{(\nu-1)/2}}.

(6.3)

We then set

u_{\nu}(t)=T_{m_{0,t}^{\nu}}(x_{0})+T_{m_{1,t}^{\nu}}(x_{1}).

(6.4)

To establish Theorem
1.2
, it is equivalent to show

\|u_{\nu}(t)\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2])\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}))}\lesssim\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}.

(6.5)

By the triangle inequality, it is reduced to proving that

\|T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(x_{i})\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2])\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}))}\lesssim\|x_{i}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})},\qquad i=0,1.

(6.6)

Combining Lemma
6.2
with the intertwining identity (
6.1
), we have

\displaystyle\|T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(x_{i})\|_{L_{p}(L_{\infty}([1,2])\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}))}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\|T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(x_{i})\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}))}^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\big\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\big(\sigma_{\theta}(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(x_{i}))\big)\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\big\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq I_{\varepsilon}+II_{\varepsilon}.

Here

I_{\varepsilon}=\int_{1}^{2}\big\|(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}dt

and

II_{\varepsilon}=\int_{1}^{2}\big\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))-(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}dt.

To estimate
I_{\varepsilon}
, we utilize Theorem
1.3
, specifically the inequalities (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), along with Lemma
6.2
. This leads to the following inequality:

\displaystyle I_{\varepsilon}\lesssim\|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}(\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))\big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N})}^{p}=\|x_{i}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}.

(6.7)

On the other hand, we claim that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}II_{\varepsilon}=0.

(6.8)

Based on this claim, the inequalities (
6.7
) and (
6.8
) imply the inequality (
6.6
), which in turn implies Theorem
1.2
.

At the end of this section, we complete the proof of the claim (
6.8
). Note that

T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\exp_{\xi})=T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\cdot+\xi)}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p})\exp_{\xi}
, we have

\displaystyle h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))-(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(\exp_{\xi})-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\exp_{\xi})\right)\otimes f_{i}(\xi)U(\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\cdot+\xi)}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p})\right)\exp_{\xi}\otimes f_{i}(\xi)U(\xi)d\xi.

Then, by Lemma
2.11
and the Minkowski inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big\|(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))-(T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}\otimes id_{\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2}})(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}\sigma_{\theta}(x_{i}))\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{\theta})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\cdot+\xi)}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p})\right)\exp_{\xi}f_{i}(\xi)U(\xi)d\xi\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{2})}^{p}d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\cdot+\xi)}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p})\right|^{p^{\prime}}(\eta)|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{p/{p^{\prime}}}d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p}m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\cdot+\xi)}(h_{\varepsilon}^{1/p})\right|^{p}(\eta)d\eta\Big)^{{p^{\prime}}/p}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{p/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|h_{1}^{1/p}m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\cdot+\xi)}(h_{1}^{1/p})\right|^{p}(\eta)d\eta\Big)^{{p^{\prime}}/p}|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{p/{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\big(h_{1}^{1/p}\big)^{\wedge}(\zeta)\big(m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\zeta+\xi)\big)\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\zeta\Big)|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{p/{p^{\prime}}},

where the last inequality follows from the classical Hausdorff-Young inequality. Note that
(h_{1}^{1/p}\big)^{\wedge}
and
f_{i}
are Schwartz functions, by the dominated convergence theorem, we get

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{1}^{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\big(h_{1}^{1/p}\big)^{\wedge}(\zeta)\big(m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\xi)-m_{i,t}^{\nu}(\sqrt{\varepsilon}\zeta+\xi)\big)\right|^{p^{\prime}}d\zeta\Big)|f_{i}|^{p^{\prime}}(\xi)d\xi\Big)^{p/{p^{\prime}}}dt=0,

which implies that
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}II_{\varepsilon}=0.

Remark 6.3
.

(i) The aforementioned transference technique remains valid for higher-dimensional spaces
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}

d\geq 3
.

(ii) In the above approximation arguments, the application of the Hausdorff-Young inequalities necessitates
p\geq 2
; the approximation arguments and thus the transference techniques become quite tricky for
1\leq p<2

Appendix A
Sharp endpoint
L_{p}
estimates

In this appendix, we delve into the Cauchy problem associated with the wave equation in the context of quantum Euclidean spaces. Throughout this appendix, we will use the following convention: The tensor von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{d}
, and for a reasonable symbel
m
, the associated operator
T_{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}_{d}
) is defined by

T_{m}(f)(\eta):=(m(\cdot)\hat{f}(\cdot))^{\vee}(\eta),\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ (\text{resp.}\ f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}){\otimes}\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{M})).

And for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

m_{0,t}^{\nu}(\xi)=\frac{\cos(|t\xi|)}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}},\quad m_{1,t}^{\nu}(\xi)=\frac{\sin(|t\xi|)}{|\xi|(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{\nu-1}{2}}}.

Considering the wave equation with the initial values
x_{0},x_{1}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})
on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d},

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(\partial_{tt}-\Delta_{\theta})u=0,\ t\in\mathbb{R}_{+},\\[5.0pt]
u(0)=x_{0},\\[5.0pt]
\partial_{t}u(0)=x_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\partial_{t}u
is defined in the sense of:

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{u(s+h)-u(s)}{h}=\partial_{t}u(s)\quad\text{in}\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}).

The solution
u(t)
to this equation can be represented as a sum of Fourier multiplier operators:

u(t)=c_{0}T_{m_{0,t}^{0}}(x_{0})+c_{1}T_{m_{1,t}^{1}}(x_{1}),

where
c_{0},c_{1}
are two constants.
We present the following noncommutative analogue of the fixed-time
L_{p}
estimate on
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d}
:

Theorem A.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and let
u(t)
be the solution to the Cauchy problem of the wave equation. Then, for any fixed time
0<t<\infty
, the following estimate

\|u(t)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\Big(\|x_{0}\|_{L_{p,\nu}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}+\|x_{1}\|_{L_{p,\nu-1}(\mathcal{R}_{\theta}^{d})}\Big)

holds for all
\nu\geq s_{p}=(d-1)\big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big|
.

By leveraging the duality argument, it suffices to demonstrate Theorem
A.1
for the range
2\leq p<\infty
. Subsequently, by applying the transference technique outlined in Section
6
, Theorem
A.1
can be reduced to the following operator-valued fixed-time
L_{p}
estimate:

Theorem A.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
i\in\{0,1\}
. For a fixed time
t
, the following estimate

\|T_{m_{i,t}^{\nu}}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}

(A.5)

holds for all
\nu\geq s_{p}=(d-1)\big|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\big|
and
f\in{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}
. Here
C_{p,t}
is locally bounded with respect to
t
.

To prove Theorem
A.2
, we rely on the theory of operator-valued Hardy spaces introduced in
[
40
]
, but we omit the details here for simplicity.

Lemma A.3

(
[
40
]
, Theorem 6.4)
.

Let
T_{m}
be a Fourier multiplier bounded on the Hardy space
H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
T_{m}
automatically extends to a bounded Fourier multiplier on the operator-valued Hardy spaces
H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})
. Moreover, one has

\|T_{m}\|_{H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})\rightarrow H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq c_{d}\|T_{m}\|_{H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Lemma A.4

(
[
42
]
, Corollary 1)
.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. If
\nu\geq s_{1}
, one has

\max\{\|T_{m_{0,t}^{\nu}}\|_{H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\|T_{m_{1,t}^{\nu}}\|_{H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow H_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\}\leq C_{t},

where
C_{t}
is locally bounded with respect to
t
.

Now, let us proceed with the proof of Theorem
A.2
.

Proof of Theorem
A.2
.

Let us denote the operator-valued Sobolev space as
L_{p,s}(\mathcal{N}_{d})
, To establish Theorem
A.2
, it suffices to demonstrate the following inequality for
1<p<\infty
:

\|T_{m_{i,t}^{s_{p}}}(f)\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}=\|T_{m_{i,t}^{i}}f\|_{L_{p,-s_{p}+i}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})},\quad i=0,1.

(A.6)

We can prove the inequality (
A.6
) using interpolation and duality: Combining Lemma
A.4
, Lemma
A.3
, and the fact that
H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})\subset L_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})
, we obtain

\big\|T_{m_{i,t}^{i}}f\big\|_{L_{1,-s_{1}+i}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}=\|T_{m_{i,t}^{s_{1}}}f\big\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq\big\|T_{m_{i,t}^{s_{1}}}f\big\|_{H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq C_{t}\|f\|_{H^{cr}_{1}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}.

Applying the Plancherel theorem, we get

\big\|T_{m_{i,t}^{i}}f\big\|_{L_{2,-s_{2}+i}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}=\big\|T_{m_{i,t}^{s_{2}}}f\big\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq C_{t}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}.

Then, by the complex interpolation, the estimate

\|T_{m_{i,t}^{i}}f\|_{L_{p,-s_{p}+i}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}\leq C_{p,t}\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{N}_{d})}

holds for
1<p\leq 2
. Using duality, we immediately obtain the inequality (
A.6
) for
p\in(2,\infty)
.
Thus, we have shown that Theorem
A.2
holds for all
1<p<\infty
, completing the proof.

A Cosmic Amplification for Muon-to-Positron Conversion in Nuclei
Purushottam Sahu, Manibrata Sen

Abstract
We present an analysis of the lepton-number and lepton-flavour-violating process of muon-to-positron conversion μ − + N → e + + N ′ \mu^{-}+N\rightarrow e^{+}+N^{\prime} , in the presence of an ultralight scalar dark matter (ULSDM) field which couples to neutrinos. The ULSDM contributes to the effective off-diagonal Majorana mass m μ ​ e m_{\mu e} , therefore amplifying the rate of muon-to-positron conversion to experimentally observable levels. Using existing bounds from SINDRUM II, COMET, and Mu2e experiments, we derive constraints on the flavour-off-diagonal couplings of neutrinos to ULSDM for the first time. Our work reveals that upcoming experiments can provide stronger sensitivity to these new couplings than bounds arising from cosmological surveys and terrestrial experiments.



I
Introduction

The Standard Model (SM) of particle physics presents a robust framework for the electroweak and strong sectors and has been successfully validated across different experiments. Despite its glorious success, there are some glaring loopholes: the SM fails to account for several key phenomena, such as the origin of the neutrino mass and the possibility of lepton number violation, the presence of dark matter (DM), and the matter-antimatter asymmetry of the Universe, among others.

Among these, the origin and nature of neutrino masses present a theoretical puzzle for the particle physics community. The discovery of neutrino oscillations implies that neutrinos have masses and they mix

Neutrinos, being electrically neutral, can be Majorana fermions, and hence violate the total lepton number (LN) by two units. This, in turn, could enable unique processes like neutrinoless double-beta decay (

II
Nuclear Muon-to-Positron Conversion

Nuclear muon-to-positron conversion proceeds via the capture of a muon in an atom, thereby forming a muonic atom, followed by an incoherent transition into a positron:
\mu^{-}+(A,Z)\rightarrow e^{+}+(A,Z-2)

0\nu\beta\beta

III
Effects of Ultralight Scalar Dark Matter

Ultralight scalar dark matter has been proposed as an alternative to cold or warm dark matter to account for certain discrepancies that exist between small-scale structure observations and predictions from numerical N-body cosmological simulations
Hu et al. 2000
. Due to low masses, such fields can be treated as a classical background field, with quantum fluctuations suppressed by the large occupation number of the fields. Such ultralight fields can account for part or the whole of the DM relic density of the Universe.

In this study, we consider an interaction between ULSDM and neutrinos through an effective lepton-flavour-violating dimension-six operator,

\mathcal{L}_{\alpha\beta}\supset\frac{y_{\alpha\beta}}{\Lambda^{2}}(L_{\alpha}H)(L_{\beta}H)\phi^{*}+\text{h.c.},

(4)

where
\phi^{*}
is the ULSDM field carrying lepton number of two units,
g_{\mu e}
is a dimensionless coupling constant,
L_{\alpha,\beta}
are the SM lepton doublets. This operator violates lepton flavour but preserves total lepton number. More generally, this operator can be viewed as an effective term arising from integrating out heavier degrees of freedom at some scale
\Lambda
. For this particular study, we focus on
\alpha=\mu,\,\beta=e
. After electroweak symmetry breaking, the Lagrangian becomes

\mathcal{L}_{\mu e}\supset g_{\mu e}\nu_{\mu}\nu_{e}\phi^{*}+\text{h.c.}\,,

(5)

where
g_{\mu e}=y_{\mu e}v^{2}/\Lambda^{2}
.

In the coherent field limit where the ULSDM behaves as a classical scalar field, it can be expressed as
\phi(t)=\phi_{0}\cos(m_{\phi}t)
, with
\phi_{0}=\sqrt{2\rho_{\phi}}/m_{\phi}
. Here
\rho_{\phi}=0.3
GeV/cm
3
is the local DM density of the Universe, and
m_{\phi}
denotes the mass of the scalar field. A simple estimation shows that the time modulation associated with the scalar field has a time period,
\tau_{\phi}\simeq 1\,{\rm yr}\,\left(10^{-22}\,{\rm eV}/m_{\phi}\right)
. This leads to a time-varying contribution to the effective Majorana mass for
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
conversion:

m_{\mu e}(t)=(m_{\mu e})_{\text{vac}}+g_{\mu e}\phi_{0}\cos(m_{\phi}t)\,,

(6)

where
(m_{\mu e})_{\text{vac}}
is the vacuum contribution defined by Eq.
2
.

If the time-modulation is way faster than the experimental time scales, the effect is averaged out. This leads to an enhancement of the effective off-diagonal Majorana mass-squared, given by

\langle|m_{\mu e}|^{2}\rangle=\langle|(m_{\mu e})_{\text{vac}}|^{2}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}(g_{\mu e}\phi_{0})^{2}.

(7)

This acts like an additional contribution to the
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
process, stimulated by the presence of the uniform ULSDM. As a result, the ULSDM field acts as a cosmic amplifier, thereby potentially increasing the signal to experimentally accessible regions.

Furthermore, since
\phi
carries lepton number, this additional contribution due to ULSDM can happen even if there is no LNV, i.e., even when
(m_{\mu e})_{\text{vac}}
is zero. This is depicted in Fig.

where the
\phi
(red line) can stimulate these processes even in the absence of LNV.
Therefore, this weakens the claim that the observation of
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
will give rise to a smoking gun signature of LNV. On the other hand, non-observation of such a process enables us to translate experimental bounds on

g_{\mu e}

Throughout our analysis, we assume that the time-dependent modulation induced by the coherent ULSDM field can be treated by averaging over oscillation cycles when the scalar mass satisfies
m_{\phi}\gg 1/\tau_{\rm exp}
, where
\tau_{\rm exp}
denotes the effective experimental integration time. In this regime, the rapidly oscillating component averages to zero in the linear term, while the quadratic term contributes as
\langle\cos^{2}(m_{\phi}t)\rangle=1/2
. This yields the enhancement factor discussed in Eq.
7
. However, in the intermediate regime where the ULSDM oscillation period becomes comparable to or longer than the integration time (e.g.,
m_{\phi}\sim 10^{-22}\,\mathrm{eV}
), this simple time-averaging prescription may no longer hold. In such cases, the conversion rate could exhibit temporal modulations over months to years, potentially requiring a dedicated analysis to model the effective sensitivity and statistical power of the experiments. Although currently undetectable due to limited timing precision, future advancements may allow correlation of the conversion signal with the dark matter oscillation frequency, providing a smoking-gun signature of the ULSDM origin.

IV
Other constraints

Since the ULSDM field
\phi
carries lepton number, the coupling
\phi^{*}\,\nu_{\mu}\nu_{e}
does not lead to LNV. However, such couplings still significantly influence early-universe cosmology through interactions with the neutrino sector. The primary constraints arise from excess radiation during the Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) epoch and modifications of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), both of which are sensitive to the thermal history and free-streaming behaviour of neutrinos. In addition to cosmological bounds, terrestrial and astrophysical environments offer complementary probes of ultralight scalar fields coupled to neutrinos. For example, such light degrees of freedom are also constrained from core-collapse supernova (CCSN) observations. We discuss these constraints in this section.

IV.1
Thermalization and
N_{\text{eff}}

The coupling
\phi^{*}\,\nu_{\mu}\nu_{e}
can lead to the thermal production of
\phi
in the early universe. If
\phi
thermalises around the BBN epoch, it increases the number of relativistic degrees of freedom during BBN, effectively increasing
N_{\text{eff}}

Wagoner et al. 1967
;
Boesgaard and Steigman 1985
;
Schramm and Turner 1998
;
Olive et al. 2000
;
Iocco et al. 2009
;
Cyburt et al. 2016
;
Sarkar 1996
. For very low masses of
\phi
, the inverse decay process
\nu_{e}+\nu_{\mu}\to\phi
is inefficient and the
2\to 2
production via
\nu_{e}+\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\phi+\phi^{*}
becomes relevant
Huang et al. 2018
. A simple constraint can be derived by requiring that the thermalisation of the production of
\phi
is smaller than the Hubble function at a temperature of around
T\sim 10
MeV. Therefore,
\Gamma\sim g_{\mu e}^{4}T<H\sim T^{2}/M_{\text{Pl}}
at
T\sim 10
MeV, leading to

g_{\mu e}\lesssim 10^{-5}\,,

(8)

in order to prevent
\phi
from reaching equilibrium and contributing significantly to
N_{\text{eff}}
.

IV.2
Neutrino Free-Streaming and the CMB

The observation of the acoustic peaks in the CMB power spectrum implies that neutrinos must free-stream by the epoch of recombination, i.e.,
T\sim 1
eV. However, this new interaction can lead to additional neutrino scattering channels, such as
\nu_{\mu}+\nu_{e}\leftrightarrow\nu_{\mu}+\nu_{e}
, or the inverse decay process
\nu_{\mu}+\nu_{e}\leftrightarrow\phi
. If these interactions are active around recombination, they can lead to a smoothing of the neutrino anisotropic stress
Cyr-Racine and Sigurdson 2014
;
Oldengott et al. 2017
;
Kreisch et al. 2020
;
Das and Ghosh 2023
. The rate for the scattering process scales as
\Gamma\sim g_{\mu e}^{4}T
. Requiring that this rate falls below the Hubble expansion rate
H(T)\sim T^{2}/M_{\text{Pl}}
at
T\sim 1
eV gives the bound:

g_{\mu e}\lesssim 10^{-7},

(9)

which ensures that neutrinos decouple sufficiently early to preserve the success of the standard cosmological model. Additional constraints can also arise from the contribution to the additional neutrino mass at the recombination epoch
Brdar et al. 2018
;
Huang and Nath 2022
;
Sen and Smirnov 2025
.

IV.3
Core-collapse supernova Bounds

In a core-collapse supernova (CCSN), the neutrino density is high enough to produce the ULSDM on-shell through
\bar{\nu}_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}+\phi^{*}

Farzan 2003
. This can lead to additional cooling channels if
\phi
escapes freely from the SN core, and lead to a reduction of the neutrino luminosity
Farzan 2003
;
Kachelriess et al. 2000
;
Brune and Pasquini 2019
. To avoid shortening the observed duration of the SN1987A neutrino signal, such energy loss must be suppressed. This leads to an upper limit on the luminosity of the new particle
Raffelt 1996
,

\mathcal{L_{\phi}}\lesssim 10^{53}\,\text{erg}\,\text{s}^{-1},

(10)

A detailed calculation of the cooling rate yields
Farzan 2003
:

g_{\mu e}\lesssim 10^{-6},

(11)

assuming that
\phi
is weakly coupled enough to escape without thermalizing. If
\phi
is trapped, this bound is relaxed, though more detailed SN simulations would be required to quantify the limit. Furthermore, such dynamic neutrino masses can also show up in the spectra of the diffuse supernova neutrino background
de Gouvêa et al. 2022
;
Perez-Gonzalez and Sen 2025

IV.4
Neutrino Oscillation Modulation

For very low mass scalar fields, it is possible that the ULSDM maintains its coherence over cosmological timescales. This can lead to a time-dependent correction to the neutrino mass matrix. For a flavour off-diagonal coupling such as
g_{\mu e}\,\phi^{*}\,\nu_{\mu}\nu_{e}
, the induced correction takes the form
m_{\mu e}(t)=g_{\mu e}\,\phi_{0}\cos(m_{\phi}t)
. Such off-diagonal mass terms can modify neutrino oscillation probabilities or induce apparent spectral distortions
Berlin 2016
;
Dev et al. 2021
;
Dev et al. 2023
;
Losada et al. 2023
;
Davoudiasl and Denton 2023
.

To remain consistent with current neutrino data, the induced contribution must be subdominant compared to current oscillation parameters. In particular, demanding that the effective contribution to the neutrino mass is smaller than the solar mass-squared difference yields

g_{\mu e}\,\phi_{0}\lesssim\sqrt{\Delta m^{2}_{\rm sol}}\sim 0.008\,{\rm eV}\,.

(12)

This is a robust constraint that holds for all off-diagonal entries in the coupling matrix. Clearly, this constraint weakens for smaller values of
m_{\phi}
.

IV.5
Charged Lepton Flavour Violation

The coupling
\phi^{*}\,\nu_{\mu}\nu_{e}
breaks lepton flavour but conserves total lepton number. Since processes like
\mu\to e\gamma
conserve lepton number, this flavour-violating coupling could potentially contribute at higher orders only. However, because
\phi
does not couple to charged leptons, the 1-loop diagrams involving
\phi
and neutrinos do not generate such dipole operators without additional lepton-number violation or mixing with charged sectors.
As such, processes like
\mu\to e\gamma
,
\mu\to 3e
, and
\mu
–
e
conversion remain highly suppressed and do not pose additional constraints. For a detailed discussion on the effect of ultralight leptophilic scalars on charged lepton-flavour-violating processes, see
Escribano and Vicente 2021
;
Bigaran et al. 2025
.

V
Conclusion

We have explored a new enhancement mechanism for
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
conversion in an atomic nucleus. This rare capture process remains a smoking gun signal for total lepton number and lepton flavour violation. However, even within minimal extensions of the Standard Model involving a right-handed neutrino, the rate of this process is hopelessly small and well beyond the current experimental sensitivities.

We consider a scenario where this process receives an amplification due to the presence of a neutrinophilic ultralight scalar dark matter (ULSDM). The specific coupling of interest in our study involves a ULSDM coupled to a
\nu_{e}
and a
\nu_{\mu}
in a lepton-number conserving manner. Such a ULSDM with a lepton-flavour-violating coupling to neutrinos can lead to an additional channel for the process, thereby providing the necessary enhancement to reach experimental sensitivities. Furthermore, due to the lepton number carried by the ULSDM, the presence of this coupling can lead to a
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
conversion, even in the absence of lepton number violation.
Conversely, the current and projected bounds on
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}
conversion can be used to place constraints on such a lepton-flavour-violating coupling of neutrinos to ULSDM – the first of its kind.

By calculating the contribution to the effective Majorana mass
m_{\mu e}
and incorporating a time-varying component from the scalar field, we derive new constraints on the neutrinophilic ULSDM. We find that the ULSDM contribution will dominate the conversion rate, and hence current and future experiments can probe the parameter space for light scalar masses
m_{\phi}\lesssim 10^{-11}
eV and couplings as low as
g_{\mu e}\sim 10^{-10}
.
Such values are well within the reach of current and future experiments, including COMET Phase-II and Mu2e. This will allow us to place the strongest constraints on such lepton flavour-violating coupling of ULSDM with neutrinos.

Our analysis places the
\mu^{-}\to e^{+}

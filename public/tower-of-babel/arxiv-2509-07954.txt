How to find all extremal graphs using symmetric subgraphs
Wenqian Zhang

Abstract
Let ℱ \mathcal{F} be a finite family of graphs with min F ∈ ℱ ⁡ χ ⁡ ( F ) = r + 1 ≥ 3 \min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3 , where χ ⁡ ( F ) \chi(F) is the chromatic number of F F . Set t = max F ∈ ℱ ⁡ | F | t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F| . Let EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) {\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) be the set of graphs with maximum edges among all the graphs of order n n without any F ∈ ℱ F\in\mathcal{F} as a subgraph. Let T ⁡ ( n , r ) T(n,r) be the Turán graph of order n n with r r parts. Assume that some F 0 ⊆ ℱ F_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{F} is a subgraph of the graph obtained from T ⁡ ( r ​ t , r ) T(rt,r) by embedding a path in its one part. Simonovits [ 20 ] introduced the concept of symmetric subgraphs, and proved that there exist graphs in EX ⁡ ( n , ℱ ) {\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F}) which have symmetrical property. In this paper, we aim to find a way to characterize all the extremal graphs for such ℱ \mathcal{F} using symmetric subgraphs. Some new extremal results are obtained. Keywords: extremal graph; symmetric subgraph; the progressive induction; blow up of graphs. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 05C35.

Simonovits
[
20
]
introduced the concept of symmetric subgraphs. Let
G
be a graph. For
\tau\geq 1
induced subgraphs of
G
:
Q_{1},Q_{2},..,Q_{\tau}
, they are called symmetric subgraphs in
G
, if they are connected and vertex-disjoint, and there are isomorphisms
\psi_{j}:Q_{1}\rightarrow Q_{j}
for all
2\leq j\leq\tau
such that for any
u\in V(Q_{1})
and
v\in V(G)-(\cup_{1\leq i\leq\tau}V(Q_{i}))
,
uv\in E(G)
if and only if
\psi_{j}(u)v\in E(G)
.
u
and
\psi_{j}(u)
are called corresponding vertices. These symmetric subgraphs are called symmetric vertices if
|Q_{i}|=1
. Clearly,
\tau

v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{\tau}
of
G
are symmetric if and only if
\left\{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{\tau}\right\}
is independent and
N_{G}(v_{1})=N_{G}(v_{i})
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
.

Observation 1.
Let
F
be a graph of order
t\geq 2
. Let
G
be a
F
-free graph. Assume that
Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{t}
are symmetric subgraphs of
G
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the graph obtained from
G
by adding a new copy of
Q_{1}
, say
Q
, such that
Q,Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{t}
are symmetric subgraphs in
G^{\prime}
. Then
G^{\prime}
is also
F
-free. In fact, if
F\subseteq G^{\prime}
, then
V(F)\cap V(Q)\neq\emptyset
since
G
is
F
-free. Noting
t=|F|
, there is at least one of
Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{t}
, say
Q_{t}
, such that
V(F)\cap V(Q_{t})=\emptyset
. Let
F^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by
(V(F)-V(Q))\cup V(Q_{t})
. By symmetry of
Q
and
Q_{t}
in
G^{\prime}
, we see that
F\subseteq F^{\prime}\subseteq G
, a contradiction. Hence
G^{\prime}
is
F
-free.

The above observation is very important in the study of extremal graphs using symmetric subgraphs. For certain integers
n,r
and
c
, let
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
denote the family of graphs
G
of order
n
satisfying the following symmetry condition:

(i)
It is possible to omit at most
c

G
so that the remaining graph
G^{\prime}
is of form
\bigotimes_{1\leq i\leq r}G_{i}
, where
||V(G_{i})-\frac{n}{r}|\leq c
for any
1\leq i\leq r
.

(ii)
For any
1\leq i\leq r
,
G_{i}=\cup_{1\leq j\leq k_{i}}H^{i}_{j}
, where
H^{i}_{1},H^{i}_{2},...,H^{i}_{k_{i}}
are symmetric subgraphs in
G
and
|H^{i}_{1}|\leq c
.

Simonovits
[
20
]
gave the following influential result on extremal graphs. Given integers
r,t
, when
n
is sufficiently large,
M:=M(r,t)
is called a constant if it is just a function of
r
and
t
(i.e., independent to
n
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
20
]
)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
. Set
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
. Assume that
F\subseteq P_{t}\bigotimes T(t(r-1),r-1)
for some
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Then, for sufficiently large
n
,
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
contains a graph
G
in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
, where
c
is constant. Furthermore, if
G
is the only extremal graph in
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
, then it is
unique in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
.

When
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
contains only one
\mathcal{F}
-free graph with maximum edges, then there is only one extremal graph for
\mathcal{F}
by Theorem
1.1
.
We should also note that
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
usually contains many extremal graphs. If so, Theorem
1.1
will not help us to find all extremal graphs. Motivated by this, we seek a way to characterize all extremal graphs in this paper. The following lemma is one main result of this paper. We will use it together with the progressive induction
[
19
]
to obtain some new extremal results.

Lemma 1.2

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
. Set
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
. Assume that
F_{1}\subseteq P_{t}\bigotimes T(t(r-1),r-1)
and
F_{2}\subseteq tK_{1,t}\bigotimes tK_{1,t}\bigotimes T(t(r-2),r-2)
for some
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
. For any given constant
N_{0}:=N_{0}(r,t)
, there exists a constant
O(r,t)
such that any graph
G
in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
with
n
sufficiently large, has an induced subgraph
\bigotimes_{1\leq i\leq r}G_{i}
satisfying:

(i)

|G_{i}|=O(r,t)\cdot N_{0}
for any
1\leq i\leq r
;

(ii)
for any
1\leq i\leq r
,
G_{i}=\cup_{1\leq j\leq\ell_{i}}H^{i}_{j}
, where
H^{i}_{1},H^{i}_{2},...,H^{i}_{\ell_{i}}
are symmetric subgraphs of
G
and
|H^{i}_{1}|\leq O(r,t)
.

For a finite family of graphs
\mathcal{F}
with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
and
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
, define
q(\mathcal{F})
to be the minimum integer
s
such that
F\subseteq\overline{K_{s}}\bigotimes T(tr,r)
for some
F\subseteq\mathcal{F}
. Clearly,
1\leq q(\mathcal{F})\leq t
.

Theorem 1.3

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
. Set
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
and
q=q(\mathcal{F})
. Assume that
F_{1}\subseteq tK_{2}\bigotimes T(t(r-1),r-1)
and
F_{2}\subseteq(K_{q-1,t}\cup K_{1,t})\bigotimes T(t(r-1),r-1)
for some
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
. Then for sufficiently large
n
, any graph
G
in
{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
has a partition

satisfying:

(i)

|W|=q-1
and
|S_{i}|=\lfloor\frac{n-q+1}{r}\rfloor
or
\lceil\frac{n-q+1}{r}\rceil
for any
1\leq i\leq r
;

(ii)
for any
1\leq i\leq r
, there exists

such that
|S_{i}-S^{\prime}_{i}|\leq t^{2}
, and
N_{G}(v)=V(G)-S_{i}
for any

.

Clearly, all the extremal graphs in Theorem
1.3
are contained in
\mathbb{D}(n,r,c)
with
c=q+rt^{2}
. Note that in Theorem
1.3
, the condition

for some

is necessary. For example, let
\mathcal{F}=\left\{3K_{2}\bigotimes\overline{K_{10}},P_{5}\bigotimes\overline{K_{10}}\right\}
.
When

is large, we can show that

is an extremal graph for

, where

is not in

for any constant

Applications on extremal graphs for blow up or odd-ballooning of graphs

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
. Let

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
be the family of minimal graphs (with no isolated vertices)
M
satisfying the following: there exist an
F\in\mathcal{F}
such that
F\subseteq(M\cup\overline{K_{t}})\bigotimes T((r-1)t,r-1)
, where
t=|F|
. We call
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
the decomposition family of
F
. The
decomposition family
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
always contains some bipartite graphs.

A covering of a graph is a set of vertices incident with all edges of the graph.
An independent covering of a bipartite graph is an independent set incident with all edges.
Let
\beta(F)
denote the covering number
of
F
, i.e., the minimum number of vertices in a covering of
F
. If
F
is bipartite, let
q(F)
be the minimum order of an independent covering of
F
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs containing at least one bipartite graph.
Define
q(\mathcal{F})=\min\left\{q(F)~|~F\in\mathcal{F}~is~bipartite\right\}
. This definition of
q(\mathcal{F})
is consistent with the one in the introduction.
The subgraph covering family
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is the set of subgraphs
F[U]
of
F\in\mathcal{F}
, where
U
is a covering of
F
with
|U|\leq q(\mathcal{F})-1
. If
\beta(F)\geq q(F)
for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
, set
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F})=\left\{K_{q(\mathcal{F})}\right\}
.

The following result is an easy consequence of Theorem
1.3
. (There is no edge inside
S_{i}
in the condition that
F_{2}\subseteq K_{q-1,t}\cup K_{2}
with some
F_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
.)

Corollary 2.1

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of graphs with
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
. Set
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
and
q=q(\mathcal{F})
. Assume that
F_{1}\subseteq tK_{2},F_{2}\subseteq K_{q-1,t}\cup K_{2}
for some
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
. Then for sufficiently large
n
, any graph
G\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
is of form

G=H\bigotimes T(n-q+1,r)
, where
H\in{\rm EX}(q-1,\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}))
.

Corollary
2.1
has concrete examples (see
[
3
,
28
]
). Given a graph
G
and an integer
p
, the blow-up of
G
, denoted by
G^{p+1}
, is the
graph obtained from
G
by replacing each edge in
G
by a clique of size

, where the new
vertices of the cliques are all distinct. Liu
[

]
proved the following result.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
16
]
)

For any
p\geq 2
and
k\geq 3
, when
n
is sufficiently large, we have the following
results:

(i)
For any
C^{p+1}_{k}\neq C^{3}_{3}
, the unique graph in
{\rm EX}(n,C^{p+1}_{k})
is
K_{\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor}\bigotimes T(n-\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor,p)
for odd

; the unique graph in

is obtained from

by adding one edge in one part of

for even

.

(ii)
For

, if

, the graph

(obtained from

by adding a (near) perfect matching in each of its two parts) is the unique graph in

.

For the extremal graphs for

From

of Theorem

(by letting

. Thus, we have the following result (after a simple calculation).

Corollary 2.3

For

and large

-free graph of order

(

Let

Lemma 3.6

(
[

]
)

Let
G
be a
P_{\ell}
-free graph of order

. For any sequence of positive integers
\pi=\left\{m_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
, there exist two positive integers

and

symmetric subgraphs

.

The following result will be used in our proofs.

Lemma 3.7

For
\ell\geq 3
, let
G
be a
P_{\ell}
-free graph. Suppose that
S\subseteq V(G)
satisfies that
\frac{|S|}{\ell-1}
is an integer, and
G[S]=\cup_{1\leq i\leq\tau}Q_{i}
, where
Q_{1},Q_{2},...,Q_{\tau}
are symmetric subgraphs of
G
with
\tau\geq\ell
. If
e(G[S])+e_{G}(S,V(G)-S)\geq\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|
, then
e(G[S])+e_{G}(S,V(G)-S)=\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|
, and we have the following conclusions.

(i)
For odd
\ell\geq 3
,
Q_{i}=K_{\ell-1}
for
1\leq i\leq\tau
and
e_{G}(S,V(G)-S)=0
.

(ii)
For even
\ell\geq 4
, either
Q_{i}=K_{\ell-1}
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
and
e_{G}(S,V(G)-S)=0
, or
Q_{i}=K_{1}
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
and there are
\frac{\ell-2}{2}
vertices in
V(G)-S
which are all adjacent to the (unique) vertex in
Q_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq\tau
.

Proof:
Let
B\subseteq V(G)-S
be the set of vertices which have at least one neighbor in
Q_{1}
. Let
H
be the graph obtained from
G[S\cup B]
by adding some edges inside
B
such that
H[B]
is a complete graph. Let
b=|B|
. If
b=0
, then
e(G[S])\geq\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|
. Note that

G[S]
is
P_{\ell}
-free. By Lemma
3.3
, we must have
e(G[S])=\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|
and
Q_{i}=K_{\ell-1}
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
. Now we assume
b\geq 1
.
Clearly,
|H|=|S|+b\geq\ell
and
e(H)=e(G[S])+e_{G}(S,V(G)-S)+\binom{b}{2}\geq\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|+\binom{b}{2}
.

By Symmetry, each vertex in
B
has at least one neighbor in
Q_{i}
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
. Since
b\geq 1
,
H
is connected.
Now we show that
H
is
P_{\ell}
-free. Suppose not, i.e., there is a
P_{\ell}
in
H
, say
P=u_{1}u_{2}\cdots u_{\ell}
. We choose such a
P
that
P
contains as small number of edges in
H[B]
as possible. If
P
contains no edges inside
B
, then
P
is also a path in
G
, a contradiction. Hence, we can assume that
P
contains at least one edge in
H[B]
. Without loss of generality, assume that
u_{2}u_{3}
is an edge in
H[B]
. Noting
\tau\geq\ell
, we can choose one symmetric subgraph, say
Q_{\tau}
, such that
P
contains no vertices in
V(Q_{\tau})
.
Since
u_{2},u_{3}\in B
have neighbors in
Q_{\tau}
, let
v,w\in V(Q_{\tau})
be neighbors of
u_{2},u_{3}
, respectively. Let
P(v,w)
be a path from
v
to
w
in
Q_{\tau}
. Then
u_{1}u_{2}P(v,w)u_{3}\cdots u_{\ell}
is a path with less number of edges inside
B
than
P
. Clearly, its sub-path of order
\ell
is a
P_{\ell}
with less number of edges inside
B
than
P
. This contradicts the choice of
P
. Hence,
H
is
P_{\ell}
-free. Recall that
|H|=|S|+b
and
e(H)\geq\frac{\ell-2}{2}|S|+\binom{b}{2}
. Since
H
is connected and
\frac{|S|}{\ell-1}
is an integer, by Lemma
3.4
, we must have that
\ell
is even and
H=K_{\frac{\ell-2}{2}}\bigotimes\overline{K_{|S|+b-\frac{\ell-2}{2}}}
. Note that
H
will contain no edges after deleting
\frac{\ell-2}{2}
vertices in the part

.
Since
\tau\geq\ell
, we must have
|Q_{i}|=1
for any
1\leq i\leq\tau
. Moreover,
|B|=\frac{\ell-2}{2}
and each vertex in

\Box

Proof of Lemma
1.2

The following fact will be used.

Fact 1.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},...,A_{m}
be
m\geq 2
finite sets. Then

|\cap_{1\leq i\leq m}A_{i}|\geq(\sum_{1\leq i\leq m}|A_{i}|)-(m-1)|\cup_{1\leq i\leq m}A_{i}|.

Proof of Lemma
1.2
.
Recall that
\min_{F\in\mathcal{F}}\chi(F)=r+1\geq 3
and
t=\max_{F\in\mathcal{F}}|F|
. There are graphs
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{F}
such that

F_{1}\subseteq P_{t}\bigotimes T(t(r-1),r-1)

and

F_{2}\subseteq tK_{1,t}\bigotimes tK_{1,t}\prod T(t(r-2),r-2).

For sufficiently large
n
, let
G\in{\rm EX}(n,\mathcal{F})
. Let
\theta:=\theta(r,t)>0
be a small constant (such that all the following inequalities on it are satisfied). By Lemma
3.2
, we have that
\delta(G)>(\frac{r-1}{r}-\theta)n
.
As is well known,
e(T(n,r))\geq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{8}
. Since
T(n,r)
is
\mathcal{F}
-free, we have
e(G)\geq e(T(n,r))\geq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{8}
.
Since
G
is
\mathcal{F}
-free and
n
is large, by Theorem
3.1
(letting
\epsilon=\theta^{3}
), we see that
G
can be obtained from
T(n,r)
by deleting and adding at most
\theta^{3}n^{2}
edges. It follows that

(\frac{r-1}{2r}-\theta^{3})n^{2}\leq e(G)\leq(\frac{r-1}{2r}+\theta^{3})n^{2}.

Let
V(G)=\cup_{1\leq i\leq r}V_{i}
be a partition such that
\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}e(G[V_{i}])
is minimum. Recall that
G
can be obtained from
T(n,r)
by deleting at most
\theta^{3}n^{2}
edges. Thus,

\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}e(G[V_{i}])\leq\theta^{3}n^{2}.

Claim 1.

||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}|\leq\theta n
for any
1\leq i\leq r
.

Proof of Claim 1.
Let
a=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}||V_{i}|-\frac{n}{r}|
. Without loss of generality, assume that
a=||V_{1}|-\frac{n}{r}|
. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we see

2\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}|V_{i}||V_{j}|=(\sum_{2\leq i\leq r}|V_{i}|)^{2}-\sum_{2\leq i\leq r}|V_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{r-2}{r-1}(\sum_{2\leq i\leq r}|V_{i}|)^{2}=\frac{r-2}{r-1}(n-|V_{1}|)^{2}.

Then, noting
a=||V_{1}|-\frac{n}{r}|
,

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle\leq(\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}|V_{i}||V_{j}|)+(\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}e(G[V_{i}]))

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|(n-|V_{1}|)+(\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}|V_{i}||V_{j}|)+\theta^{3}n^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|V_{1}|(n-|V_{1}|)+\frac{r-2}{2(r-1)}(n-|V_{1}|)^{2}+\theta^{3}n^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{2(r-1)}a^{2}+\theta^{3}n^{2}.

Recall that
e(G)\geq(\frac{r-1}{2r}-\theta^{3})n^{2}
. Thus,
(\frac{r-1}{2r}-\theta^{3})n^{2}\leq\frac{r-1}{2r}n^{2}-\frac{r}{2(r-1)}a^{2}+\theta^{3}n^{2}
, implying that
a\leq\sqrt{\frac{4(r-1)}{r}\theta^{3}n^{2}}\leq\theta n
. This finishes the proof.
\Box

For any
1\leq i\leq r
, let
W_{i}=\left\{v\in V_{i}~|~d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq\theta n\right\}
, and let
\overline{V}_{i}=V_{i}-W_{i}
.
Set
W=\cup_{1\leq i\leq r}W_{i}
.

Claim 2.

|W|\leq 2\theta^{2}n
.

Proof of Claim 2.
Since
\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}e(G[V_{i}])\leq\theta^{3}n^{2}
, and

\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}e(G[V_{i}])=\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V_{i}}d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in W_{i}}d_{V_{i}}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r}\theta n|W_{i}|=\frac{1}{2}\theta n|W|,

we have
\frac{1}{2}\theta n|W|\leq\theta^{3}n^{2}
, implying that
|W|\leq 2\theta^{2}n
.

\Box

Claim 3.

Let
1\leq\ell\leq r
be fixed. Assume that
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{2rt^{2}}\in\cup_{1\leq i\neq\ell\leq r}\overline{V}_{i}
. For any constant
D_{0}>t
, there are
D_{0}
vertices in
\overline{V}_{\ell}
which are the common neighbors of
u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{2rt^{2}}
in
G
.

Proof of Claim 3.
For any
1\leq i\leq 2rt^{2}
, assume that
u_{i}\in\overline{V}_{j_{i}}
, where
j_{i}\neq\ell
. Then
d_{G}(u_{i})\geq(\frac{r-1}{r}-\theta)n
and
d_{V_{j_{i}}}(u_{i})\leq\theta n
as
u_{i}\notin W
. Recall that
|V_{s}|\leq\frac{n}{r}+\theta n
for any
1\leq s\leq r
.
Hence

Introduction

Let
H
be an
h
-vertex graph and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph. An
H
-
tiling
in
G
is a collection of vertex-disjoint subgraphs of
G
isomorphic to
H
.
An
H
-
factor
is an
H
-tiling which covers all vertices of
G
.
Determining sufficient conditions for the existence of an
H
-factor is one of the fundamental lines of research in extremal graph theory.
One important reason is due to a result of Hell and Kirkpatrick
[
19
]
which shows that the decision problem for
H
-factors is
NP
-complete, given that
H
has a connected component of size at least 3.

The first result in this line of attack is by Dirac
[
7
]
, who proved that every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\frac{n}{2}
contains a Hamiltonian cycle, in particular if
n
is even then
G
has a perfect matching.
Later a result of Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
13
]
gives a sufficient condition
\delta(G)\geq\frac{r-1}{r}n
guaranteeing the existence of a
K_{r}
-factor.
The
K_{3}
-factor case was previously proved by Corrádi and Hajnal
[
6
]
.
The unbalanced complete
r
-partite graph witnesses the tightness of the minimum degree condition.
Determining the best possible bound on
\delta(G)
for an arbitrary graph
H
has been an intriguing problem.
There had been many excellent results (see
[
24
,
25
,
34
]
and survey
[
27
]
) until it was finally settled by Kühn and Osthus
[
28
]
.
There have been various generalizations of Hajnal–Szemerédi theorem in the setting of partite graphs
[
21
,
22
,
30
,
31
]
, directed graphs
[
35
]
and hypergraphs
[
33
]
.

1.1
Ramsey–Turán Tiling problems

Note that the extremal example that achieves the optimality of the bound on
\delta(G)
in Hajnal–Szemerédi Theorem contains a large independent set, which is “regular” and rather rare among all graphs.
Following the spirit of the well-known Ramsey–Turán theory (see
[
9
,
10
,
12
,
33
]
), a natural question on the Hajnal–Szemerédi Theorem is to determine the minimum degree condition forcing a clique factor when the host graph has sublinear independence number.
The following Ramsey–Turán type question was proposed by Balogh, Molla, and Sharifzadeh
[
2
]
.

Question 1

(
[
2
]
, Question 1.1)
.

Let
r\geq 3
be an integer and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\alpha(G)=o(n)
. What is the minimum degree condition on
G
that guarantees a
K_{r}
-factor?

Balogh, Molla, and Sharifzadeh
[
2
]
studied the
K_{3}
-factor, and proved the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Given constant
\mu>0
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n\in 3\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\mu)n
and
\alpha(G)\leq\alpha n
. Then
G
contains a
K_{3}
-factor.

Recently, Knierim and Su
[
23
]
proved the following result, which asymptotically solves the case of larger cliques in Question
1
.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
23
]
)
.

Given
\mu>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 4
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n\in r\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
r
dividing
n
,
\delta(G)\geq(1-\frac{2}{r}+\mu)n
and
\alpha(G)\leq\alpha n
. Then
G
contains a
K_{r}
-factor.

Nenadov and Pehova
[
32
]
proposed an extension of Question
1
in the following, which was reiterated by Knierm and Su
[
23
]
.
Given a graph
G
and an integer
\ell\geq 2
, we denote by
\alpha_{\ell}(G)
the
K_{\ell}
-independence number
of
G
, which is the maximum size of a
K_{\ell}
-free
subset of vertices in
V(G)
.

Question 2

(
[
23
,
32
]
)
.

Is it true that for every
r,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq\ell
and
\mu>0
there is a constant
\alpha
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every graph
G
on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices where
r
divides
n
with
\delta(G)\geq\max\left\{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)n,\left(\frac{r-\ell}{r}+\mu\right)n\right\}
and
\alpha_{\ell}(G)\leq\alpha n
has a
K_{r}
-factor?

Nenadov and Pehova
[
32
]
also proved a general upper bound for the minimum degree conditions, which is asymptotically optimal when
\ell=r-1
.
In the following, we give an affirmative answer to Question
2
for the case
\ell=r-2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 4
and
\mu>0
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n\in r\mathbb{N}
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph such that
\delta(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)n
and
\alpha_{r-2}(G)\leq\alpha n
. Then
G
contains a
K_{r}
-factor.

The minimum degree in Theorem
1.3
is asymptotically tight: consider an
n
-vertex graph
G
consisting of two disjoint complete graphs of order
\frac{n}{2}+1
and
\frac{n}{2}-1
.

There are also other (indeed, negative) results on Question
2
.
Given integers
n,r
and a function
f(n)
, we use
RT_{\ell}(n,K_{r},f(n))
to denote the maximum number of edges of an
n
-vertex
K_{r}
-free graph
G
with
\alpha_{\ell}(G)\leq f(n)
.
In particular, the
Ramsey-Turán density
of
K_{r}
is defined as

\rho_{\ell}(r):=\lim\limits_{\alpha\rightarrow 0}\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{RT_{\ell}(n,K_{r},\alpha n)}{\tbinom{n}{2}}
.

Chang, Han, Kim, Wang, and Yang
[
4
]
determined that
\delta(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2-\varrho_{\ell}(r-1)}+o(1)\right)n
is asymptotically tight for the case
r>\ell\geq\frac{3}{4}r
.
Comparing with the results in
[
4
]
, Theorem
1.3
gives a new result for
r\in\{5,6,7\}
.
Recently, Han, Hu, Wang, and Yang
[
16
]
extended the investigation of Question
2
under the condition
\alpha_{\ell}(G)=n^{1-o(1)}
, and proved that every
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\max\big\{\frac{r-\ell}{r}+o(1),\frac{1}{2-\rho_{\ell}(r-1)}+o(1)\big\}n
and
\alpha_{\ell}(G)\leq n^{1-o(1)}
contains a
K_{r}
-factor.

We remark that it would be interesting to try to solve Question
2
for the next concrete case
\ell=3
and
r=6
, which seems to be out of reach via our approaches.

1.3
Proof strategy

Our proof makes use of the absorption method and builds on the techniques developed in
[
14
,
15
,
20
]
.
The absorption method was introduced by Rödl, Ruciński, and Szemerédi about a decade ago in
[
33
]
.
Since then, it has turned out to be an important tool for studying the existence of spanning structures in graphs, digraphs and hypergraphs.

Now we sketch the proof idea. The main tasks are to (i) build an absorbing set and (ii) cover almost all of the remaining vertices with a
K_{r}
-tiling.
For (i), we first need the following notion of absorbers and absorbing sets in
[
32
]
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph. Then

•

a subset
A\subseteq V(G)
is a
\xi
-
absorbing set
in
G
for some constant
\xi
if for any subset
U\subseteq V(G)\backslash A
of size at most
\xi n
and
|A\cup U|\in r\mathbb{N}
,
G[A\cup U]
contains a
K_{r}
-factor.

•

for any
S\subseteq V(G)
of size
r
and an integer
t
, we call a subset
A_{S}\subseteq V(G)\backslash S
a
(K_{r},t)
-
absorber
for
S
if
|A_{S}|=rt
and both
G[A_{S}]
and
G[A_{S}\cup S]
contain a
K_{r}
-factor.

A new approach due to Nenadov and Pehova
[
32
]
guarantees an absorbing set provided that

every
r
-set
S
has
\Omega(n)
vertex-disjoint
(K_{r},t)
-absorbers for
S
.

Here we instead build a partition
V(G)=B\cup U
satisfying that

|B|=o(n)
and every
r
-set in
U
has
\Omega(n)
vertex-disjoint absorbers.

Similar ideas already appeared in our recent works (e.g.
[
4
,
5
]
).
Here the arguments reduce to finding an absorbing set
A
in
G[U]
and then covering vertices of
B
by a small
K_{r}
-tiling vertex disjoint from
A
so as to yield a desired absorbing set.
To achieve the desired partition
B\cup U
, our proof adopts the lattice-based absorption method developed by the second author
[
15
]
.

Our second task is to establish an almost perfect
K_{r}
-tiling.
Following a standard application of the regularity lemma, we obtain an
\varepsilon
-regular partition and then build a reduced multigraph where two vertices are connected by a double-edge if the corresponding clusters form a regular pair of density larger than
\frac{1}{2}
.
We find that in the reduced multigraph,
K^{=}_{2}
and
K_{3}
can be used to model how
K_{r}
is embedded into a collection of clusters with sublinear
K_{r-2}
-independence number.
To play with this, we build a
\{K^{=}_{2},K_{3}\}
-tiling (see Lemma
4.1
) that covers almost all vertices in the reduced multigraph.
Meanwhile, for every
H\in\{K^{=}_{2},K_{3}\}
, we define a suitably-chosen auxiliary graph
Q_{i}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
In particular,
Q_{i}
has a
K_{r}
-factor, and thus it suffices to find an almost perfect
\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}
-tiling in the same context (see Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
).

Note that Knierm and Su
[
23
]
also used
K^{=}_{2}
and
K_{3}
to model how
K_{4}
is embedded into a graph
G
when
\alpha(G)=o(n)
.
Within this framework, they established a fractional analogue of Lemma
4.1
by utilizing the Bollobás–Erdős graph via an elegant proof.
However, since the Ramsey–Turán problem is still not well understood for
K_{\ell}
-independence number, it is currently unclear to us how to adapt the ideas of Knierm and Su into our proof.
Luckily, in our (multi-graph) setting, we can find an almost perfect tiling with tiles
K^{=}_{2}
and
K_{3}
directly (instead of their fractional version) under the
\alpha_{r-2}(G)=o(n)
for
r\geq 4
.

Basic notation and organization

We first introduce some notion throughout the paper.
For a graph
G:=G(V,E)
, we write
v(G)=|V(G)|
and
e(G)=|E(G)|
. For
U\subseteq V
, let
G[U]
be the induced subgraph of

on
U
.
Let
G-U:=G[V\backslash U]

For two subsets

We write

For convenience, we use

For a vertex set

3.1
Regularity

To find an almost perfect
K_{r}
-tiling (Lemma
3.1
), an important ingredient in our proof is Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma. In this paper, we make use of a degree form of the regularity lemma
[
26
]
.
Given a graph
G
and a pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
of vertex-disjoint subsets in
V(G)
, the
density
of
(V_{1},V_{2})
is defined as

d(V_{1},V_{2})=\frac{e(V_{1},V_{2})}{|V_{1}||V_{2}|}
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, a graph
G
and a pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
of vertex-disjoint subsets in
V(G)
, we say that the pair
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
\varepsilon
-
regular
if for all
X\subseteq V_{1}
and
Y\subseteq V_{2}
satisfying

X\subseteq V_{1},|X|\geq\varepsilon|V_{1}|
and
Y\subseteq V_{2},|Y|\geq\varepsilon|V_{2}|
,

we have

|d(X,Y)-d(V_{1},V_{2})|\leq\varepsilon.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
26
]
, Slicing Lemma)
.

Assume
(V_{1},V_{2})
is
\varepsilon
-regular with density
d
. For some
\alpha\geq\varepsilon
, let
V_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{1}
with
|V_{1}^{\prime}|\geq\alpha|V_{1}|
and
V_{2}^{\prime}\subseteq V_{2}
with
|V_{2}^{\prime}|\geq\alpha|V_{2}|
. Then
(V_{1}^{\prime},V_{2}^{\prime})
is
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-regular with
\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\max\{2\varepsilon,\varepsilon/\alpha\}
and for its density
d^{\prime}
we have
|d^{\prime}-d|<\varepsilon
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
26
]
, Degree form of the Regularity Lemma)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is an
N=N(\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for any real number
\beta\in[0,1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be a graph with
n
vertices. Then there exists an
(\varepsilon,\beta)
-regular partition
V(G)=V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
and a spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
with the following properties:

({\rm 1})

\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\leq k\leq N
;

({\rm 2})

|V_{i}|\leq\varepsilon n
for
i\in[0,k]
and
|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\cdots=|V_{k}|=m
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
;

({\rm 3})

d_{G^{\prime}}(v)>d_{G}(v)-(\beta+\varepsilon)n
for all
v\in V(G)
;

({\rm 4})
each
V_{i}
is an independent set in
G^{\prime}
for
i\in[k]
;

({\rm 5})
all pairs
(V_{i},V_{j})
are
\varepsilon
-regular with density 0 or at least
\beta
for distinct
i,j\neq 0
.

A widely-used auxiliary graph accompanied with the regular partition is the reduced graph.
To differentiate between dense and very dense pairs of partitions, we employ the following notion of reduced multigraph.

Definition 3.6

(Reduced graph)
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta,\varepsilon>0
,
G
be a graph with a vertex partition
V(G)=V_{0}\cup\ldots\cup V_{k}
and
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
be a subgraph fulfilling the properties of Lemma
3.5
. We denote by
R_{\beta,\varepsilon}
the
reduced graph
of this partition, which is defined as follows. Let
V(R_{\beta,\varepsilon})=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
and for two distinct clusters
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
we draw a double-edge between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
if
d_{G^{\prime}}(V_{i},V_{j})\geq\frac{1}{2}+\beta
, a single-edge if
\beta\leq d_{G^{\prime}}(V_{i},V_{j})<\frac{1}{2}+\beta
and no edge otherwise.

The following fact presents a minimum degree of the reduced graph provided the minimum degree of
G
, where a double-edge is counted as two edges.

Fact 1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\varepsilon,\beta\leq\frac{\mu}{10}
with
\beta\in[0,1]
, and
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\mu)n
. Let
V(G)=V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
be a vertex partition of
V(G)
satisfying Lemma
3.5
(1)-(5). We denote the reduced graph as
R_{\beta,\varepsilon}
. Then for every
V_{i}\in V(R_{\beta,\varepsilon})
we have

d_{R_{\beta,\varepsilon}}(V_{i})\geq(1+\mu)k
.

Proof.

Note that
|V_{0}|\leq\varepsilon n
and
|V_{i}|=m
for each
i\in[k]
. Every edge in
R_{\beta,\varepsilon}
represents less than
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)m^{2}
edges in
G^{\prime}-V_{0}
. Thus we have

\displaystyle d_{R_{\beta,\varepsilon}}(V_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|V_{i}|(\delta(G)-(\beta+\varepsilon)n-\varepsilon n)}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)m^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu-2\varepsilon-\beta\right)mn}{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)m^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu-2\varepsilon-\beta\right)(1-2\beta)k

\displaystyle>(1+\mu)k,

since
0<\varepsilon,\beta\leq\frac{\mu}{10}
and
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)^{-1}\geq 2(1-2\beta)
.
∎

Let

for each

. The

of

is a set of vertices in

each of which is connected to

through a double-edge. Similarly, we define the

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Given

, we choose
\frac{1}{k}\ll\eta,\mu

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
\{K^{=}_{2},K_{3}\}
-tiling in
R
such that
\mathcal{T}
is maximal.
Let
A:=V(\mathcal{T})
and
B:=V(R)\backslash A
.
We may assume
|B|>0
.
Otherwise, we are done.

Clearly,
R[B]
is
K_{3}
-free and
K^{=}_{2}
-free.
Hence, by Turán’s theorem, it holds that
e_{R}(B)\leq\frac{1}{4}|B|^{2}
.
As
\delta(R)\geq(1+\mu)k

e_{R}(A,B)\geq(1+\mu)k|B|-\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{T}
consists of
p
vertex-disjoint copies of
K^{=}_{2}
and
q

K_{3}

Obviously, it holds that
p\leq\frac{|A|}{2}

and

.

For any copy of
K^{=}_{2}
in
\mathcal{T}
, say
uv
, we claim that
N(u)\cap N(v)\cap B=\emptyset
.
Assume
w\in N(u)\cap N(v)\cap B
, we can find a new
\{K^{=}_{2},K_{3}\}

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

|V(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})|=|V(\mathcal{T})|+1

.
Hence, it holds that
e_{R}(\{u,v\},B)\leq 2|B|

For every copy of
K_{3}
in
\mathcal{T}

uvw
, we claim that
e_{R}(\{u,v,w\},B)\leq 3|B|+3
.
Assume
e_{R}(\{u,v,w\},B)\geq 3|B|+4

there exists a vertex, say

.
This implies that there exist two vertices in
B
, say

and

, such that
uu^{\prime}
and
uu^{\prime\prime}
are two double-edges.
We can observe that none element (if exists) in
\{u^{\prime}v,u^{\prime}w,u^{\prime\prime}v,u^{\prime\prime}w\}
can be a double-edge, and none element (if exists) in
\{u^{\prime}vw,u^{\prime\prime}vw\}
can be a triangle.
Hence
e_{R}(\{u,v,w\},\{u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime}\})\leq 4+2=6
.
For any vertex
z\in B\backslash\{x,y\}

zvw
can not be a triangle, and none element (if exists) in

can be a double-edge.
It holds that

.

, a contradiction.

Since
2p+3q=|A|
, it holds that

e_{R}(A,B)\leq 2|B|p+(3|B|+3)q\leq 3q+|A||B|
.
Recall that
e_{R}(A,B)\geq(1+\mu)k|B|-\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}

Let
f:=(1+\mu)k|B|-\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}-(3q+|A||B|)

.
Meanwhile we have

\displaystyle f=

\displaystyle(1+\mu)k|B|-\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}-(k-|B|)|B|-3q

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mu k|B|+\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}-|A|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}|B|^{2}+k\left(\mu|B|-1\right)+|B|.

As
|B|>0
, it holds that

, and

, since

.
∎

Since

is

5
Absorbing

In this section, we will prove Lemma
3.2
. We use a result of Nenadov and Pehova
[
32
]
which gives a sufficient condition for the existence of an absorbing set.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
32
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
r,t\in\mathbb{N}
be constants.
Then there exists
\xi:=\xi(r,t,\gamma)
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n
.
Suppose that
G
is a graph with
n
vertices such that every
S\in\binom{V(G)}{r}
has a family of at least
\gamma n
vertex-disjoint
(K_{r},t)
-absorbers. Then
G
contains a
\xi
-absorbing set of size at most
\gamma n
.

So the key point in the proof of Lemma
3.2
is to build linearly many vertex-disjoint absorbers for every
S\in\binom{V(G)}{r}
.
To achieve this, we employ the latticed-based absorbing method
[
17
]
and we first need the notion of
K_{r}
-reachability from
[
17
]
which originates in
[
29
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
G,K_{r}
be given as aforementioned and
m,t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we say that two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are
(K_{r},m,t)
-reachable
(in
G
) if for any vertex set
W
of
m
vertices, there is a set
S\subseteq V(G)\backslash W
of size at most
rt-1
such that both
G[S\cup\{u\}]
and
G[S\cup\{v\}]
have
K_{r}
-factors, where we call such
S
an
K_{r}
-connector
for
u,v
.
Moreover, a set
U\subseteq V(G)
is
(K_{r},m,t)
-closed
if every two vertices
u,v\in U
are
(K_{r},m,t)
-reachable, where the corresponding
K_{r}
-connector for
u,v
may not be contained in
U
.
If two vertices
u,v\in V(G)
are
(K_{r},m,1)
-reachable, then we say
u
is
1
-reachable
to
v
.
If
u,v\in U
are
(K_{r},m,t)
-reachable, and the corresponding
K_{r}
-connector for
u,v
is contained in
U
, then we say that
u,v\in U
are
(K_{r},m,t)
-inner-reachable
.
Similarly, we can define
(K_{r},m,t)
-inner-closed
and
1
-inner-reachable
.

The following result from
[
17
]
builds a sufficient condition to ensure that every subset
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|=r
has linearly many vertex-disjoint absorbers.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
17
]
, Lemma 3.9)
.

Given
\beta>0
and
t,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 3
, the following holds for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph such that
V(G)
is
(K_{r},\beta n,t)
-closed. Then every
S\in\tbinom{V(G)}{r}
has a family of at least
\frac{\beta}{r^{2}t}n
vertex-disjoint
(K_{r},t)
-absorbers.

Based on this lemma, it suffices to show that
V(G)
is closed. However, we are only able to prove a slightly weaker result which states that the graph
G
admits a vertex partition
V(G)=B\cup U
where
B
is a small vertex set and
U
is inner-closed.

Lemma 5.4
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 4
and constants
\tau,\mu
with
0<\tau<\mu
, there exist positive constants
\alpha,\beta
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that the following holds for sufficiently large
n
.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)n
and
\alpha_{r-2}(G)\leq\alpha n
.
Then
G
admits a partition
V(G)=B\cup U
with

and

is

-inner-closed.

Clearly, we shall focus on the subgraph

5.3
and Lemma
5.1
on

.

The next step is to deal with the vertex set

.
We shall pick mutually vertex-disjoint copies of

each covering a vertex in

.
To achieve this, we use the following result.

Lemma 5.5
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 4
and a constant
\mu>0
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large

.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\mu\right)n

\alpha_{r-2}(G)\leq\alpha n
.
If

in

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 4
and
\mu>0
, we choose
\frac{1}{n}\ll\alpha\ll\mu
.
Let

and

with

In

.
Hence, there exists a copy of

∎

Now, we give the proof of Lemma

by using Lemma

Let

Let

5.1.1

As sketched above, the following fact presents a minimum degree of the reduced graph provided the minimum degree of

Fact 2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

with

Note that

Every edge in

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\mu}{2}\right)k,

A vector

Given

Here is a brief proof outline for Lemma
5.7
.
In order to prove that

is closed, we adopt a less direct approach and build on the merging techniques developed in

We first partition

The following lemma can be used to construct a partition such that each part is closed.

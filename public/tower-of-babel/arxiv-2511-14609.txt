Transition and Stability of 3D MHD Around Couette Flow
Niklas Knobel

Abstract
. We study the three-dimensional incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations near Couette flow with a constant magnetic field perpendicular to the shear plane. Couette flow induces mixing and generates magnetic induction, while the constant magnetic field stabilizes z z -dependent modes. In contrast, the z z -averaged magnetic field exhibits algebraic growth. Letting μ \mu denote the inverse fluid and magnetic Reynolds numbers, we analyze how μ \mu governs stability thresholds in Sobolev spaces. We identify a nonlinear transient-growth regime characterized by the sharp threshold 5 / 6 ≤ γ ≤ 1 5/6\leq\gamma\leq 1 . For x x -average–free initial data of size μ γ \mu^{\gamma} , solutions are nonlinearly stable; however, for certain initial data, the solution departs from the linear dynamics at rate μ γ − 1 \mu^{\gamma-1} due to a first-order nonlinear instability. The exponent γ = 5 / 6 \gamma=5/6 is optimal for the associated energy functional and cannot be improved in Sobolev spaces without secondary transient-growth mechanisms. Below this threshold, solutions necessarily transition away from the linear dynamics at a minimal rate. As a consequence, the 3 ​ d 3d results yield sharp Sobolev stability thresholds for the 2 ​ d 2d MHD equations around Couette flow without a constant magnetic field. In particular, the threshold is strictly larger than in prior 2 ​ d 2d results with a constant field, revealing destabilizing effects normally suppressed by a constant magnetic field. Crucially, this stabilization is restricted to the direction of the magnetic field.



1.
Introduction

The goal of this article is to characterize the stability, instability, and long-time behavior of a three-dimensional conducting fluid near the planar Couette flow coupled with a uniform vertical magnetic field:

(1)

V_{s}=(y,0,0),\qquad B_{s}=(0,0,\alpha),

where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. The governing equations are the incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) equations, written for perturbations of the velocity field

V=(V^{x},V^{y},V^{z}):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}

and magnetic field

B=(B^{x},B^{y},B^{z}):\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{3},

as

(2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}V+y\partial_{x}V+V^{y}e_{1}+(V\cdot\nabla)V+\nabla\Pi

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta V+\alpha\partial_{z}B+(B\cdot\nabla)B,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}B+y\partial_{x}B-B^{y}e_{1}+(V\cdot\nabla)B

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta B+\alpha\partial_{z}V+(B\cdot\nabla)V,

\displaystyle\dive(V)=\dive(B)

\displaystyle=0.

Here,
\mu>0
is a dimensionless parameter representing both viscosity and resistivity, under the assumption that the magnetic Prandtl number equals one.

This system exhibits a competition between stabilizing mechanisms—such as inviscid mixing, enhanced dissipation, and magnetic tension—and destabilizing mechanisms, including linear and nonlinear transient growth. Our main interest lies in the
inviscid limit

\mu\to 0
, where these competing effects are most pronounced.

We aim to describe the precise threshold between stability and instability, in terms of the Sobolev size of the initial perturbations
(V_{in},B_{in})
with which (
2
) is initialized. We identify three qualitatively different regimes depending on rates of nonlinear transient growth:

\diamond

Linear dynamics .

If
\|(V_{in},B_{in})\|_{H^{N}}\leq\delta_{1}\mu^{\gamma_{1}}
, then the solution is enslaved to the linearized dynamics of (
2
), which is characterized by linear transient growth, inviscid damping, and enhanced dissipation.

\diamond

Nonlinear yet stable transient growth.

If
\delta_{1}\mu^{\gamma_{1}}\leq\|(V_{in},B_{in})\|_{H^{N}}\leq\delta_{2}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}
, then due to a first order nonlinear instability the solution exhibits a nonlinear departure from the linearized dynamics—finite and quantifiable in terms of an inverse power of
\mu
. Nevertheless, asymptotic stability still holds.

\diamond

Secondary nonlinear instability and nonlinear regime.

If
\delta_{2}\mu^{\gamma_{2}}\leq\|(V_{in},B_{in})\|_{H^{N}}
, then nonlinear growth triggers further instabilities. The solution experiences dramatic growth away from the linear dynamics and enters a nonlinear regime.

The constants
\delta_{i}>0
are not essential for our analysis, while the exponents
\gamma_{i}\geq 0
play a crucial role. Much of the recent mathematical literature has focused on the first regime, aiming to determine the smallest exponent
\gamma_{1}\geq 0
for which the linear dynamics—dominated by linear transient growth, inviscid damping, and enhanced dissipation—also controls the full nonlinear evolution. This value
\gamma_{1}
defines the so-called
nonlinear stability threshold
. In the three-dimensional homogeneous Navier–Stokes equations with Sobolev regularity, this line of work was initiated in
[
2
]
and has since developed into a rich and active research area (see Section
1.3
for further discussion).

In contrast, the second and third regimes—those involving unstable or partially unstable dynamics—have received significantly less attention. The nonlinear yet stable transient growth regime may be viewed as a refinement of the stability threshold framework, capturing initial data that evade classical stability mechanisms yet remain asymptotically stable. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first instance of such behavior being rigorously identified in a fluid model.

The third regime, involving norm inflation and potential secondary instabilities, highlights the sharpness of the exponents
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
, by producing solutions that undergo growth sufficient to trigger secondary instabilities. Crucially, such transient growth does not necessarily imply long-time instability: the solution may still converge to equilibrium as
t\to\infty
. However, the growth takes it beyond the reach of current perturbative techniques, limiting our ability to describe its detailed evolution.

Change of Coordinates

Theorem

The latter are formulated in variables adapted to the linear dynamics and measured in Sobolev spaces defined in the moving frame associated with the characteristics of the Couette flow:

(11)

(x,y,z)\mapsto(x-yt,y,z).

This coordinate change is standard in shear flow analysis, as it eliminates the linear advection term and makes the mixing mechanism explicit in the evolution of perturbations.
In this frame, the perturbations are given by

(12)

v(t,x,y,z)=V(t,x+yt,y,z),\qquad b(t,x,y,z)=B(t,x+yt,y,z),

and satisfy

(3d-MHD)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}v+e_{1}v^{y}-2\partial_{x}\nabla_{t}\Delta_{t}^{-1}v^{y}&=\mu\Delta_{t}v+\alpha\partial_{z}b+(b\cdot\nabla_{t})b-(v\cdot\nabla_{t})v-\nabla_{t}\pi,\\
\partial_{t}b-e_{1}b^{y}&=\mu\Delta_{t}b+\alpha\partial_{z}v+(b\cdot\nabla_{t})v-(v\cdot\nabla_{t})b,\\
\nabla_{t}\cdot v&=\nabla_{t}\cdot b=0,\\
\Delta_{t}\pi&=\partial_{i}^{t}b^{j}\partial_{j}^{t}b^{i}-\partial_{i}^{t}v^{j}\partial_{j}^{t}v^{i}.\end{split}

The change of variables modifies the differential operators as

\partial_{y}^{t}:=\partial_{y}-t\partial_{x},\quad\nabla_{t}:=(\partial_{x},\,\partial_{y}^{t},\,\partial_{z}),\quad\Delta_{t}:=\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{y}^{t})^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2},\quad\Lambda_{t}:=(-\Delta_{t})^{1/2}.

Throughout the paper, lowercase letters denote quantities in the moving frame, and uppercase letters refer to those in the original frame.

Literature Overview

The stability of shear flows has a long history, beginning with the pioneering works of Lord Kelvin
[

and Reynolds

has been established in
[
30
]
, with optimality dictated by the presence of exponential transient growth
[
27
]
. The results on Couette flow are generalized to monotone shear flows under suitable spectral assumptions. The linear behavior in this setting was established in
[
50
,
43
]
, while nonlinear stability is obtained in
[

,
32
,
8
]

The

For three-dimensional fluids, stability is more intricate since additional effects arise, and the threshold is expected to be significantly larger. In

2.2.
Heuristics on the threshold of the
z
-average

In this subsection, we give a heuristic explanation for why the stability threshold in Corollary
1
is optimal. The argument follows the approach developed in
[
6
,
14
]
and
[
25
]
for general fluid models.

We take the Fourier transform in
(x,y)
, with frequency variables
(k,\eta)
, and omit the hat notation in what follows. The functions
f
and
g
exhibit strong interactions at the so-called
critical times

t_{k}=\frac{\eta}{k},

at which the following resonance chain can occur:

g(k+1,\eta)\quad\longrightarrow\quad f(k,\eta)\quad\longrightarrow\quad g(k-1,\eta).

Since
t_{k}\leq t_{k-2}
, growth produced by
g(k+1,\eta)
at mode
g(k-1,\eta)
can be iterated and thus cascaded to lower modes, leading to further amplification.

For times
t\approx\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, frequencies
\eta\leq\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, and bounded
k\leq C
, dissipation plays only a negligible role. Hence, for the purposes of this heuristic, we ignore the effect of dissipation on high frequencies.
We use the identity

\displaystyle\nabla^{\perp}_{t}\cdot\bigl((\nabla^{\perp}g\cdot\nabla)\nabla^{\perp}_{t}g\bigr)=(\nabla^{\perp}g\cdot\nabla)\Delta_{t}g,

and focus on high–low interactions of the form

(18)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{hi}\approx(\nabla^{\perp}g^{hi}\cdot\nabla)\Delta_{t}g^{low},\qquad\partial_{t}g^{hi}\approx(\nabla^{\perp}\Lambda_{t}^{-2}f^{hi}\cdot\nabla)g^{low}.

As a toy model, we take
g_{low}(t,x)=-\varepsilon\cos(x)
, which solves (
17
) in the inviscid case
\mu=0
. Substituting this ansatz gives the simplified system

(19)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f=(1+t^{2})\sin(x)\,\partial_{y}g,\qquad\partial_{t}g=-\sin(x)\,\partial_{y}\Lambda_{t}^{-2}f.

After Fourier transform, this reads

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f(t,k,\eta)&=\pm\langle t\rangle^{2}\eta\,g(t,k\pm 1,\eta),\\
\partial_{t}g(t,k,\eta)&=\mp\frac{\eta}{(k\pm 1)^{2}}\,\frac{1}{\langle t-\tfrac{\eta}{k\pm 1}\rangle^{2}}\,f(t,k\pm 1,\eta).\end{split}

Fix
\eta
and restrict to the interacting modes
g(k+1),f(k),g(k-1)
. The dominant transfer is
g(k+1)\to f(k)\to g(k-1)
, which gives

(21)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}g(t,k+1)=0,\qquad\partial_{t}f(t,k)\approx t^{2}\eta\,g(t,k+1),\qquad\partial_{t}g(t,k-1)\approx\frac{\eta}{k^{2}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\frac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{2}}f(t,k).

The relevant interaction appears fro times
t\approx\frac{\eta}{k}
, i.e. on the interval
t_{k+1}\leq t\leq t_{k-1}
. Starting from
f(t_{k+1},k)=g(t_{k+1},k-1)=0
, integration yields

\displaystyle f(t,k)\approx\varepsilon\eta g(t_{k+1},k+1)\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t}\tau^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\approx\varepsilon\eta g(t_{k+1},k+1)\,t^{2}(t-t_{k+1})

and hence

\displaystyle g(t_{k-1},k-1)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon\frac{\eta}{k^{2}}\int_{t_{k+1}}^{t_{k-1}}\frac{1}{\langle t-\tfrac{\eta}{k}\rangle^{2}}f(\tau,k)\,{\rm d}\tau\approx\varepsilon^{2}g(t_{k+1},k+1)t_{k}^{4}(t_{k}-t_{k+1}).

Since
t_{k}-t_{k+1}\approx k^{-1}t_{k}\approx t_{k}\approx\mu^{-\frac{1}{3}}
, we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle f(t_{k-1},k)

\displaystyle\approx\varepsilon\eta\mu^{-1}g(t_{k+1},k+1),

(23)

\displaystyle g(t_{k-1},k-1)

\displaystyle\approx(\varepsilon\mu^{-\frac{5}{6}})^{2}g(t_{k+1},k+1).

From (
23
) we see that echo chains are suppressed provided

\varepsilon\leq\delta\mu^{\frac{5}{6}}

for some small constant
\delta>0
. Meanwhile, (
22
) shows that
g
induces growth of
f
by a factor
\eta\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}
. In Sobolev norms, this corresponds to

\|f\|_{H^{N}}\approx\mu^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|\partial_{y}g\|_{H^{N}}.

Stability at this threshold is therefore achieved by employing the modified energy functional

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{N+1}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{6}}\|f\|_{H^{N}}.

When we consider the

\hat{f}(t,k,\eta)=\exp\left(-\mu\int_{0}^{t}|k,\eta-k\tau,l|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau\right)\hat{f}_{in}(k,\eta).

Evaluating the integral gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|k,\eta-k\tau,l|^{2}\,{\rm d}\tau=(k^{2}+l^{2})t+t\left(\frac{k^{2}t^{2}}{12}+\left(\frac{kt}{2}-\eta\right)^{2}\right)\geq\frac{k^{2}}{12}t^{3}.

Thus, for

,

In particular,

\|f_{\neq}\|_{H^{N}}\leq{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{12}\mu t^{3}}\|f_{in}\|_{H^{N}},

and the same estimate holds for

and

. Reconstructing

and

For functions dependent on

\alpha
. Modifying the unknowns of

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{v}

\displaystyle=\mu\Delta_{t}\tilde{v}+\alpha\partial_{z}\tilde{b}+2\nabla_{t}\Delta^{-1}_{t}\partial_{x}\tilde{v}^{y},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{b}

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\cdot\tilde{v}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}(\alpha\partial_{z})^{-1}\tilde{b}^{y},\qquad\nabla_{t}\cdot\tilde{b}=0.

The system is no longer incompressible: the divergence term interacts with the pressure-like contribution

and can, in principle, cause finite growth. To control this, we employ a time-dependent Fourier multiplier

by

\mathcal{F}(A_{L}a)(k,\eta,l)=\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}\mu|k,\eta-k\tau,l|^{2}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\Bigl|\frac{k^{2}}{l}\Bigr|\frac{\mathbf{1}_{k,l\neq 0}}{|k,\eta-k\tau,l|^{2}}\,{\rm d}\tau\right)\hat{a}(k,\eta,l).

By construction, the energy

is non-increasing. Differentiating in time gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}\|A_{L}(\tilde{v},\tilde{b})\|_{H^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle A_{L}\tilde{v},A_{L}2\nabla_{t}\partial_{x}\tilde{v}^{y}\rangle_{H^{2}}-\frac{2}{\alpha}\Bigl\|\frac{|\partial_{x}|}{|\partial_{z}|^{1/2}}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A_{L}(\tilde{v},\tilde{b})\Bigr\|_{H^{2}}^{2}.

An integration by parts shows

\langle A_{L}\tilde{v},A_{L}2\nabla_{t}\partial_{x}\tilde{v}^{y}\rangle_{H^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{\alpha}\Bigl\|\frac{|\partial_{x}|}{|\partial_{z}|^{1/2}}\Lambda_{t}^{-1}A_{L}(\tilde{v},\tilde{b})\Bigr\|_{H^{2}}^{2},

hence the energy is non-increasing. Therefore

\|A_{L}(\tilde{v},\tilde{b})\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|(v_{in},b_{in})\|_{H^{2}}.

Combining with (

\|v_{\neq\neq},b_{\neq\neq}\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{12}\mu t^{3}}\|(v_{in},b_{in})\|_{H^{2}}.

Finally, to obtain decay of the

\displaystyle\|v^{y}_{\neq\neq}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{y}^{t})^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x}\\
\partial_{y}^{t}\end{pmatrix}v^{y}_{\neq\neq}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\partial_{x}^{2}+(\partial_{y}^{t})^{2}}}\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x}v^{y}_{\neq\neq}\\

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}\|v\|_{H^{2}}\lesssim\langle t\rangle^{-1}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{12}\mu t^{3}}\|(v_{in},b_{in})\|_{H^{2}},

and the same bound holds for

Proof of Theorem

To establish our theorem, we turn to two foundational results: Euler’s pentagonal number theorem

\displaystyle(q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,q^{\omega_{n}}

and a classical theta identity attributed to Gauss

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}=1+2\sum_{n=\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}.

(2)

These identities form the backbone of our approach to proving the theorem.

Case
m=1
.

Given that

\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\left(1+2\sum_{n=\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}\right)^{-1}\equiv 1\pmod{2}.

we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot(-q;q)_{\infty}\equiv(-q;q)\pmod{2}\equiv(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,q^{\omega_{n}}\pmod{2}.

Case
m=2
.

Expanding the inverse of (
2
) modulo
4
, we find

\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\equiv 1-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}\equiv 1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}\pmod{4}.

Thus, we conclude that

\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\equiv\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}\pmod{4}.

Now, we consider:

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot(-q;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}\cdot(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}\pmod{4}

\displaystyle\equiv(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{4}.

This reasoning shows that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,q^{\omega_{n}}\pmod{4},

concluding the proof.

Case
m=3
.

Expanding the inverse of (
2
) modulo
8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\equiv 1-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}+\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}\right)^{2}\pmod{8}

\displaystyle\equiv 1-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{2n^{2}}\pmod{8}

\displaystyle\equiv 1-2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{2n^{2}}\pmod{8}.

This allows us to express

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\,\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}\pmod{8}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\cdot(-q;q)_{\infty}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\,\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\,(-q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}-(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{8}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\,(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}\,(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}-(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{8}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\,\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;-q)_{\infty}}-(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{8}

\displaystyle\equiv 2\,(-q;-q)_{\infty}-(q;q)_{\infty}\pmod{8}.

In other words, we have shown that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}\,\big(2\,(-1)^{\omega_{n}}-1\big)\,q^{\omega_{n}}\pmod{8},

thereby concluding the proof.

Case
m=4
.

To summarize, we have:

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}\right)^{-2}=\left(1+4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}+\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}}\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}.

Modulo
16
, this simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv 1-4\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}-\left(2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}}\right)^{2}\pmod{16}

\displaystyle\equiv 3-\frac{2\,(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}-\left(\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}}-1\right)^{2}\pmod{16}

\displaystyle\equiv 2-\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\pmod{16}.

Next, we consider

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)\,q^{n}=\frac{(-q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}\cdot(q;q)_{\infty}.

Substituting, we find

Considering

This completes the proof.

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)\,q^{n}=\frac{f_{1}^{2}\,f_{5}^{4}\,f_{10}^{2}}{f_{2}^{4}}

It is clear that

A(n)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,(2k+1)\,C\left(5n+4-\frac{25k(k+1)}{2}\right).

We need to show that

A(2n+1)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

On the other hand, we have

A(2n+1)\equiv B(2n+1)\pmod{2},

where the sequence
B(n)
defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B(n)\,q^{n}=f_{1}^{2}\,f_{5}^{4}.

The proof follows easily if we consider the identities:

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2}=\frac{f_{2}\,f_{8}^{5}}{f_{4}^{2}\,f_{16}^{2}}-2\,q\,\frac{f_{2}\,f_{16}^{2}}{f_{8}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad f_{1}^{4}=\frac{f_{4}^{10}}{f_{2}^{2}\,f_{8}^{4}}-4\,q\,\frac{f_{2}^{2}\,f_{8}^{4}}{f_{4}^{2}}.

We conclude the argument.
∎

Corollary 14
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer.

(a)

\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}(-1)^{k}\,(2k+1)\,a(2n+1-\Delta_{k})=0
.

(b)

\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}a(4n-\Delta_{k})\equiv 1\pmod{2}

\iff

n\in\{\omega_{j}|j\geq 0\}
.

(c)

\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}a(4n+2-\Delta_{k})\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

(d)

If
n\neq 1\pmod{5}
then
\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}a(5n+2-2\Delta_{k})\equiv 0\pmod{2}
.

Proof.

(a)-(c). We consider the sequence
A(n)
defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)\,q^{n}=f_{2}^{2}

It is clear that

A(n)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,(2k+1)\,a\left(n-\Delta_{k}\right).

The proof follows considering that

\displaystyle f_{2}^{2}=\frac{f_{4}\,f_{16}^{5}}{f_{8}^{2}\,f_{32}^{2}}-2\,q^{2}\,\frac{f_{4}\,f_{32}^{2}}{f_{16}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{f_{1}\,f_{4}^{5}}{f_{2}^{2}\,f_{8}^{2}}\equiv f_{1}\pmod{2}.

We conclude the argument.
∎

7.3
Gauss theta series

Considering the theta series identity
[
Coo17
, Eq. (0.41), p. 16]

\displaystyle\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,q^{n^{2}},

we derive the following corollary.

Corollary 15
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer.

(a)

If
n\equiv 4\pmod{5}
, then

\frac{1}{5}\,\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,C\big(5n+4-5k^{2}\big)\equiv 0\pmod{5}.

(b)

If
n\equiv 4\pmod{5}
, then

\frac{1}{5}\,\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,C\big(5n+4-10k^{2}\big)\equiv 0\pmod{5}.

Considering the theta series identity
[
Coo17
, Eq. (0.45), p. 16]

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{2}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta_{n}},

we derive the following corollary.

Corollary 16
.

Let
n
be a non-negative integer.

(a)

If
n\equiv\{6,8\}\pmod{10}
, then
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}C\Big(5n+4-5\Delta_{k}\Big)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

(b)

If
n\equiv 1\pmod{2}
, then
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}C\Big(5n+4-25\Delta_{k}\Big)\equiv 0\pmod{2}.

Proof.

(b). We consider the sequence
A(n)
defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)\,q^{n}=\frac{f_{1}^{2}f_{10}^{4}}{f_{2}^{4}}

It is clear that

A(n)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}C\left(5\Big(n-5\Delta_{k}\Big)+4\right).

We need to show that

A(2n+1)\equiv 0\pmod{2}
whose proof follows easily if we consider that

\displaystyle\frac{f_{1}^{2}}{f_{2}}=\frac{f_{8}^{5}}{f_{4}^{2}\,f_{16}^{2}}-2\,q\,\frac{f_{16}^{2}}{f_{8}}.

We conclude the argument.
∎

7.4
Ramanujan theta functions

Considering the theta identity
[
Coo17
, Eq. (0.47), p. 17, with
q
replaced by
-q
]

\displaystyle\frac{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}^{5}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\,(3n+1)\,q^{3n^{2}+2n},

we derive the following corollary.

Corollary 17
.

If
n\equiv\{1,3\}\pmod{5}
, then
\sum\limits_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,(3k+1)\,C\left(5n+4-5k(3k+2)\right)=0.

Proof.

We consider the sequence
A(n)
defined by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A(n)\,q^{n}=f_{2}\,f_{5}\,f_{10}^{2}

It is clear that

A(n)=\frac{1}{5}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\,(3k+1)\,C\left(5\big(n-k(3k+2)\big)+4\right).

The proof follows if we consider that

\displaystyle f_{2}\,f_{10}

\displaystyle=(q^{20},q^{30},q^{50};q^{50})_{\infty}^{2}-f_{10}\,f_{50}\cdot q^{2}-(q^{10},q^{40},q^{50};q^{50})_{\infty}^{2}\cdot q^{4}\qquad\text{or}

\displaystyle f_{1}\,f_{5}

\displaystyle=(q^{10},q^{15},q^{25};q^{25})_{\infty}^{2}-f_{5}\,f_{25}\cdot q-(q^{5},q^{20},q^{25};q^{25})_{\infty}^{2}\cdot q^{2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

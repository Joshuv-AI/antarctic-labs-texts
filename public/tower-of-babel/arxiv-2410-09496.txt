The tensorial description of the Auslander algebras of representation-finite string algebras Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author. Thanks: MSC2020: 16G10, 16G60, 16G70. Thanks: Key words: string algebra, Auslander algebra, enveloping algebra
Hui Chen, Jian He ∗, Yu-Zhe Liu

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to study the Auslander algebra of any representation-finite string algebra. More precisely, we introduce the notion of gluing algebras and show that the Auslander algebra of a representation-finite string algebra is a quotient of a gluing algebra of 𝑨 → n e \overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{A}}_{n}^{e} . As applications, the Auslander algebras of two classes of string algebras whose quivers are Dynkin types 𝔸 \mathbb{A} and 𝔻 \mathbb{D} are described. Moreover, the representation types of the above Auslander algebras are also given exactly.

Introduction

The notion of string algebras, a class of special biserial algebra
[
17
]
, was introduced by Butler and Ringle in
[
3
]
.
Many important algebras are string, such as gentle algebras, Nakayama algebras.
There are many works on string algebras (for example,
[
5

7

9
,
13

14

15

). In particular, Butler and Ringel
[

namely the string modules and the band modules, as well as its arrows into four classes.
In
[
7

Recently, Liu and Zhang
[

Let
A
be an algebra of finite type and
M_{1},M_{2},...,M_{n}
be a complete set of representatives of the isomorphism classes of indecomposable
A

is the Auslander algebra of

1

the algebras which arise this way as algebras of global dimension at most 2
and dominant dimension at least 2. Moreover, this construction of indecomposable

-modules induces a mutually inverse bijection between Morita
equivalence classes of representations-finite algebras and Morita equivalence classes of
Auslander algebras. This bijection is called the Auslander correspondence and the correspondence is generalized by Iyama to a higher dimensional version in
[
12
]
. It is also known that Auslander algebras have close relations with quasi-hereditary algebras, preprojective algebras and projective quotient algebras, see
[
6
,
8
,
10
]
for details.

The paper is devoted to study the Auslander algebra of any representation-finite string algebra. To be more precise, we propose a question as follows.

Question 1.1
.

How to describe the Auslander algebra of any representation-finite string algebra?

We recall the tensor product of rings, which is introduced by Frobenius and also plays an important role in the representation theory of algebras. As a special kind of tensor product of algebras, the enveloping algebra
A^{e}
of
A

, where
A^{\mathrm{op}}
is the opposite algebra of

[

-bimodule

is natural right
A^{e}
-module
M=M_{A^{e}}
by

M_{A^{e}}\times A^{e}\longrightarrow M_{A^{e}},\ \ m\cdot(a\otimes a^{\prime}):=a^{\prime}ma.

In order to answer Question
1.1

Q
can be
“stacked” by some diamond quivers.
We call the above class of algebras Generalized diamond-stacked (=GDS) algebras. Meanwhile, we also introduce the notion of gluing algebras (See Definition

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
String algebras

In this part, we recall some concepts for string algebras.
We always assume that
\mathds{k}
is an algebraically closed field in our paper, and a quiver is a quadruple

If

All algebras in this paper are finite dimensional

-algebras, and, for any algebra

A

-algebra which is Morita equivalent to
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
where
\mathcal{I}
is generated by some paths of length
\geq 2
. The bound quiver
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{I})
of monomial algebra is called a

.
Furthermore, we say a monomial pair

is a
string pair
if it satisfies the following conditions:

(S1)
R

Any vertex of
\mathcal{Q}
is the source of at most two arrows and the target of at most two arrows.

(S2)
R

For each arrow
\alpha:x\to y
, there is at most one arrow
\beta

whose source
\mathfrak{s}(\beta)
is
y
such that
\alpha\beta\notin\mathcal{I}
.

(S2)
L

For each arrow
\alpha:x\to y
, there is at most one arrow
\gamma

whose target
\mathfrak{t}(\gamma)
is
x
such that
\gamma\alpha\notin\mathcal{I}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A finite-dimensional algebra
A
is called a
string algebra
if
A
is Morita equivalent to
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
where
(\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{I})
is a string pair.

Lemma 2.11

(2.5)

A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
with admissible ideal
\mathcal{I}
is isomorphic to a quotient of a gluing algebra of some
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra,
where any generator of
\mathcal{I}
is either a zero relation or a commutative relation given by a diamond quiver.

proof.

Without loss of generality, we give two assumptions as follows:

•

\mathcal{Q}
is obtained by gluing vertices
v_{i},w_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq l
) of some quiver
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}

(by the definition of
2^{\mathrm{nd}}
DS quiver);

•

All zero relations in
\mathcal{I}
are paths
r_{1}
,
\ldots
,
r_{n}
, and the other relations in
\mathcal{I}
are
c_{1}
,
\ldots
,
c_{m}
.

Then every
r_{k}
can be seen as a path
r^{\prime}_{k}
on
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}

which is either a relation of length
\geq 2
or a trivial path
0
.

To be more precise, there are two situations:

(i)

r_{k}
is a path of length
\geq 2
on
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
;

(ii)

r_{k}
is a new path on
\mathcal{Q}
which is obtained by gluing vertices of
\mathcal{Q}
,
then
r_{k}
can not be seen as a non-zero path in
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
, and we define
r_{k}^{\prime}=0
in this situation.

Let
\mathcal{I}^{\prime}=\langle r^{\prime}_{k}\neq 0\mid 1\leq k\leq n\rangle
and
A^{\prime}=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}/\mathcal{I}^{\prime}
.
It is easy to see that
(\mathcal{Q},\langle r_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\rangle)
is the bound quiver of
A^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}_{\{\varepsilon_{v_{i}}-\varepsilon_{w_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\}}(=A^{\prime\mathfrak{g}})
, that is,

\displaystyle A^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}=A^{\prime}/\langle\varepsilon_{v_{i}}-\varepsilon_{w_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\cong\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\langle r_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\rangle.

Consider the ideal
\mathcal{J}
of
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}
whose generators are given by

•

paths
\wp_{1}
,
\cdots
,
\wp_{m}
of length
\geq 2
belong to
\mathcal{I}

such that any
\wp_{t}
(
1\leq t\leq m
) crosses
v_{i}
(
=w_{i}
);

•

and the other relations
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}
in
\mathcal{I}
.

Obviously, the generators of
\mathcal{I}
are divided to two parts, one is corresponded by the elements in
\{r_{k}^{\prime}\neq 0\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}
,
the other is corresponded by the generators of
\mathcal{J}
. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle\cong A^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}(v_{i})\Big)\Big/\mathcal{J}=(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}/\mathcal{I}^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}(v_{i})\Big)\Big/\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle\left(\cong(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}^{\prime})^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\{\varepsilon_{v_{i}}-\varepsilon_{w_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\}}\Big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}(v_{i})\Big)\Big/\langle\{r_{k}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}\cup\mathcal{J}\rangle\right),

where
A^{\prime\mathfrak{g}}\Big(\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{l}\mathfrak{p}(v_{i})\Big)
is a gluing algebra of the
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra
A^{\prime}
.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}
be a GDS algebra with admissible ideal
\mathcal{I}
whose generator is either a zero relation or a commutative relation.
Then
A
is isomorphic to a quotient of a gluing algebra of some
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra.

proof.

By the definition of GDS algebra (see Definition
2.7
),
there exists a DS algebra
\mathit{\Lambda}=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}/\mathcal{I}_{\mathit{\Lambda}}
and some idempotent
\varepsilon=\sum\limits_{i\in\mathfrak{I}}\varepsilon_{i}

(
\varepsilon_{i}
is the idempotent corresponding to some vertex
i\in\mathcal{Q}_{0}
, and
\mathfrak{I}\subseteq\mathcal{Q}_{0}
is an index set) such that

\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}
. Then
A=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}/\mathcal{I}=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}/\mathcal{I}
.
Assume that
\mathcal{I}
is generated by
l
commutative relations
c_{1}
,
\cdots
,
c_{l}
and
m
zero relations
z_{1}
,
\cdots
,
z_{m}
.
By Lemma
2.11
, we obtain

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}\big/\langle c_{i},z_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq m\rangle

\displaystyle\cong(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle)\big/\langle z_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\rangle,

where
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle
is a GDS algebra, denoted by
B
,
whose ideal is admissible and generated by commutative relations.
Thus,
(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}},\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle)
is the bound quiver of
B
and
A\cong B/\langle z_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\rangle
.

If
B
is a
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra, we are done.

Otherwise, it is easy to see that there exists a DS algebra
D=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\mathcal{I}_{D}

and an idempotent
\varepsilon^{D}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\varepsilon^{D}_{k}
(all
\varepsilon^{D}_{k}
are primitive idempotents) in
D

such that
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}
equals to the quiver given by the bound quiver of
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle
.
To be more precise, the bound quiver of
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle
can be given by deleting all vertices corresponded by
\varepsilon^{D}_{k}
.
During the above process,
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
changes to
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}
, and some paths
p_{1}
,
\cdots
,
p_{n}
on
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
are zero in
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle
.
Let
V_{k}
be the set of all vertices on
p_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq n
) and
\mathfrak{S}=\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{n}\bigcup\limits_{v\in V_{k}}\mathfrak{p}(v)
. Now, we have two cases as follows.

Case

1.
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
is a
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS quiver. In this case,

\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}),

Thus,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S})\Big/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\big((\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle)\big/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S})

(by Lemma
3.2
)

\displaystyle\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle\varepsilon_{k}^{D},c_{i}\mid 1\leq k\leq n,1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S})

(by Lemma
2.11
)

\displaystyle\cong\big((\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle)/\langle\varepsilon^{D}\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S})

(by Lemma
2.11
)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\{\varepsilon^{D}_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}}(\mathfrak{S}).

The above formula shows that
B
is a gluing algebra of a
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{D}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle
.

Case

2.
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
is a
2^{\mathrm{nd}}
DS quiver. Then there is a
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra
\widehat{D}=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle H\rangle

(
H=\{\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}}_{v_{h}}-\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}}_{w_{h}}\mid 1\leq h\leq\hbar\}
)
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{D}
equals to the quiver given by the bound quiver of
\widehat{D}
(see the definition of
2^{\mathrm{nd}}
DS quiver).
Thus,
\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}
can be given by
\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}
through following steps.

Step 1. Gluing the vertices
v_{h}
and
w_{h}
(
1\leq h\leq\hbar
);

Step 2. and deleting all vertices
\varepsilon^{D}_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq n
) which naturally are seen as the vertices
\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}}_{k}
of
\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{S}\bigcup\bigcup\limits_{1\leq h\leq\hbar}\mathfrak{p}(v_{h})
. Then

\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}},H\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime}),

and so

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\mathit{\Lambda}/\mathit{\Lambda}\varepsilon\mathit{\Lambda}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}},H\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})\Big/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle

\displaystyle\cong\big((\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}},H\rangle)\big/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})

(by Lemma
3.2
)

\displaystyle\cong\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle\varepsilon_{k}^{\widehat{D}},H,c_{i}\mid 1\leq k\leq n,1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})

(by Lemma
2.11
)

\displaystyle\cong\big((\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle)/\langle\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}},H\rangle\big)(\mathfrak{S}^{\prime})

(by Lemma
2.11
)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\big)^{\mathfrak{g}}_{\{\varepsilon^{\widehat{D}}_{k}\mid 1\leq k\leq n\}\cup H}(\mathfrak{S}).

The above formula shows that
B
is a gluing algebra of a
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra
\mathds{k}\mathcal{Q}_{\widehat{D}}/\langle c_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle
.

Therefore,
A\cong B/\langle z_{j}\mid 1\leq j\leq m\rangle
is a quotient of a gluing algebra
B
of some
1^{\mathrm{st}}
DS algebra.
∎

Example 5.3
.

\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{n}
is a string algebra, whose Auslander algebra is

\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{n}^{\mathrm{Aus}}\cong\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{A}}_{n}^{e}/\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=(\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{A}}_{n}\otimes_{\mathds{k}}\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{A}}_{n}^{\mathrm{op}})/\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}},

where the quiver of

(2) and

\mathcal{J}_{\mathbb{D}_{n}}=\left\langle\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-2}\sum\limits_{j=k+1}^{n}\varepsilon_{(k,j)}-\varepsilon_{(n-2,n-1)}\right)+\sum\limits_{{1\leq j\leq n}\atop{j\neq n-1}}\varepsilon_{(n,j)}\right\rangle.

Next, we show that
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{n}^{\mathrm{Aus}}
is representation-finite if and only if
n\leq 5
.
First of all,
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{6}^{\mathrm{Aus}}
is representation-infinite because any indecomposable module
M(\lambda\neq 0)
(its quiver representation is shown in F
IGURE

5.2
(2)) induces a right
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{6}^{\mathrm{Aus}}
-module,
and
M(\lambda)\cong M(\mu)
if and only if
\lambda=\mu
. Thus,
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{6}^{\mathrm{Aus}}
is representation-infinite.
The representation-infiniteness of
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\geq 6}^{\mathrm{Aus}}
can be proved by similar way.
We can check that
\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{\leq 5}^{\mathrm{Aus}}

\sharp\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{mod}\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{4}^{\mathrm{Aus}})=40

\sharp\mathsf{ind}(\mathsf{mod}\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{5}^{\mathrm{Aus}})=109
by Auslander-Reiten quiver.

\overrightarrow{\boldsymbol{D}}_{n}^{\mathrm{Aus}}

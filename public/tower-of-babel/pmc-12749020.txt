Rethinking multi-stage calibration fusion network and loss function for steel surface defect detection
Authors: Xue Li, Shilong Zhao, Yuting Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSurface defects in steel severely impact product reliability and service life. However, achieving satisfactory defect detection results is challenging due to the need to balance detection accuracy and efficiency in practical applications, the wide variation in surface defect sizes, and the presence of complex backgrounds and minute defect targets in production processes. In this paper, we propose the Multi-stage Calibration Fusion Network, named RMCF-Net. Firstly, we design an Efficient Feature Extraction Module (E-C2f) that employs heterogeneous processing to enrich features, fully capturing defect details while ensuring efficient detection without losing critical structural information. Secondly, we introduce a Lightweight Feature Aggregation Module (LFAM) to pre-filter multi-level feature maps from the neck network, mitigating feature conflicts and achieving optimised integration to minimise information loss for minute target defects. Subsequently, the Multi-stage U-shaped Fusion Network (MUF-Net) was introduced. This network re-evaluates cross-layer feature interactions by implementing an adaptive feature re-weighting strategy within its U-shaped bidirectional propagation path, enabling deep interaction between adjacent and cross-level features. Finally, an adaptive adjustment function (QCIoU) integrating perimeter and aspect ratio was proposed to guide the model in further reducing spatial deviation between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes, thereby enhancing model accuracy. Comparative experiments on the NEU-DET, GC10-DET, and Track Components datasets achieve mAP@0.5 of 78.9%, 67.1%, and 71.9%, respectively, while maintaining 119 FPS. This delivers state-of-the-art performance, providing reliable technical assurance for quality control in intelligent manufacturing.Keywords: Steel defect detection, Feature aggregation module, U-shaped fusion network, Regression loss functionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Steel serves as a foundational material in critical sectors such as industrial manufacturing, aerospace, and mechanical engineering. However, due to the complexity of manufacturing processes and production environments, its surface frequently exhibits various types of defects. Defective products not only compromise appearance but also significantly diminish material performance, safety, and reliability.

Traditional surface defect detection1 has long depended on manual visual inspection, which is often inefficient, prone to missed detections and misjudgments, and lacks real-time capability. With the application of machine vision technology, its efficient and stable characteristics have gradually replaced manual inspection methods, though limitations persist in complex scenarios. In recent years, deep learning, particularly convolutional neural networks (CNNs2), has made remarkable progress in the field of computer vision, leading to substantial advances in defect detection algorithms based on deep learning3. This approach enables direct automatic feature extraction from images, facilitating defect classification and localisation, particularly suited for recognition tasks in complex backgrounds. Currently, single-stage detectors4 offer significant advantages in real time performance, better aligning with industrial deployment requirements. Consequently, optimising their performance has become a key research focus. Zhao et al.5. comprehensively analyzed the industrial basic model from the aspects of data, model and application, providing new ideas for promoting industrial production. For instance, Li et al.6 enhanced feature expression through multi-branch convolutions and efficient feature fusion modules, yet insufficiently addressed model robustness; Li et al.7 designed novel feature units that enriched gradient information but exhibited limited detection performance in complex backgrounds; Zhao et al.8 introduced a dynamic Ghost module based on YOLOv8n to boost efficiency, though detection accuracy remained inadequate; Liu et al.9 employed image-level supervision and context enhancement modules to strengthen feature recognition and global modeling, yet this approach carries high computational complexity; Yu et al.10 combined Ghost convolution with channel attention mechanisms to improve accuracy while maintaining real-time performance; Chao et al.11 achieved adaptive multi-scale weighting by modifying the YOLOv8 path aggregation network, but this significantly increased parameter count and reduced efficiency. Zhao et al.12. proposed a multi-level spatial domain significant defect detection method to suppress the defect noise to the maximum extent and improve the defect detection and recognition ability. Yan et al.13. constructed a compound vision inspection system, which used 3D depth and 2D gray imaging to detect weld defects of aluminum alloy, providing a new inspection idea. Cardellicchio and others14 used the artificial intelligence technology of 3D data to detect the defects of aluminum parts, which greatly reduced the cost and realized real-time monitoring in actual production. The aforementioned works provide crucial theoretical foundations and technical references for the algorithm design in this study.

However, lightweight deep learning models have limited capabilities in extracting key features from complex backgrounds, constraining further improvements in their detection performance. To enhance feature extraction and suppress background interference, network architectures are typically designed to be deeper and more complex. However, such methods often lead to a significant increase in model parameters and computational cost, adversely affecting real-time detection performance. For example, Jin et al.15 proposed a multi-layer hybrid feature extraction network that integrates global and local information to improve tiny defect detection, yet it considerably increased parameter quantity. Guo et al.16 employed a self attention mechanism to construct a cross-global attention module, enhancing the focus on defects in complex backgrounds, yet this approach struggled to achieve real-time terminal detection. Wang et al.17 strengthened feature representation through a multi-scale alignment module and expanded the feature fusion pathway.

In summary, balancing accuracy and efficiency in defect detection remains a key challenge in complex industrial environments. Steel products frequently exhibit numerous minute defects. While existing research often focuses on multi-scale feature fusion, most approaches only utilise features from adjacent levels. They lack pre-filtering mechanisms for cross-level features and fail to fully exploit the interactions between shallow level features to enhance the detection performance of minute targets. Effective extraction of multi-scale defect features remains particularly difficult.

This paper introduces RMCF-Net, a one-stage detection algorithm designed for high-precision and efficient defect detection. The method constructs an efficient feature extraction module using the Residual Difference-based Embedded Feature extraction (RDEM) to enhance detection efficiency while preserving critical structural information. Employs a lightweight feature aggregation module (LFAM) to pre-filter multi-level neck features and mitigate feature conflicts; constructs a multi-stage U-shaped fusion network (MUF-Net) to enable deep interaction between cross-level and adjacent-level features; and introduces an adaptive loss function QCIoU that combines perimeter and aspect ratio to further optimise detection accuracy.

Related work

Surface defect detection

Surface defect detection in steel18 serves as a core technology in intelligent manufacturing, aiming to accurately identify product appearance defects through automated methods. This plays a crucial role in enhancing product quality. Current mainstream approaches predominantly rely on deep learning techniques, employing model architectures such as CNNs and Transformers19. These methods utilise multi-scale feature extraction while integrating local subtle features with global semantic information to address detection challenges in complex environments20. Although existing methods have achieved significant improvements in recognition accuracy, challenges remain in model lightweighting, reliable detection of minute defects, and target classification under complex background interference. Further optimisation is needed to balance detection performance and computational overhead to meet the demands of practical industrial applications.

Current deep learning-based defect detection methods fall into two categories: one employs a two-stage detection framework (e.g., Faster R-CNN21 and Mask R-CNN22), which first generates region proposals before performing classification and regression. For instance, Leng et al.23 enhanced the identification capability of minute defects in steel by improving the RCNN model and introducing a channel attention mechanism; Qi et al.24 combined residual convolutions with Swin Transformers to strengthen the model’s global perception of multi-scale defects; Zhu et al.25 employed a dense VGG network to enhance feature representation and reconstructed anchor boxes to adapt to defect sizes. Although these approaches achieve higher detection accuracy, their high computational complexity limits practical industrial applications.

One-stage detection frameworks, including YOLO26 and DETR series27, perform end-to-end prediction of object locations and categories. These approaches maintain high detection accuracy while enabling rapid object recognition, and a series of improvements have been developed for this method. For instance, Song et al.28 employed deformable convolutions to enhance detection efficiency for irregular defects; Shao et al.29 designed a defect-aware subsampling module to compensate for information loss in small objects during compression; Yu et al.30 proposed a multi-receptive field fusion network that enhances the expressive power of reduced-dimension features by expanding the perceptual range; Li et al.31 constructed a bearing defect detection network based on residual fine grained features, achieving precise localisation through dedicated detection heads. These approaches enhance defect detection performance at different levels, yet a trade-off remains between computational complexity and practical deployment effectiveness. To address the dual demands of accuracy and efficiency in practical defect detection, this paper proposes an Efficient Feature Extraction module (E-C2f) that enhances feature representation while significantly reducing parameters, and a Lightweight Feature Aggregation Module (LFAM) that improves detection accuracy for small defects with minimal computational cost.

Regression loss function

Regression loss functions critically influence object detection performance. For bounding box regression tasks in defect detection, IoU-based loss functions have become mainstream, such as DIoU32, CIoU33, EIoU34, and SIoU35. By directly optimising IoU or incorporating geometric factors like centre point distance and aspect ratio, these functions significantly enhance localisation accuracy and convergence speed. These loss functions balance robustness, accuracy, and convergence performance across diverse scenarios, driving progress in object detection and segmentation.

As shown in Fig. 8a, existing regression loss functions provide effective signals even when predicted and ground truth bounding boxes are misaligned. However, steel inspection scenarios feature large object scale variations and frequent overlaps, demanding higher localisation accuracy and scale robustness from loss functions. When overlapping objects of different scales cause gradient conflicts due to IoU discrepancies, convergence is delayed. When the centre of the predicted box coincides with the ground truth box (Fig. 8b), the centre distance ratio drops to zero. If the aspect ratios match, IoU becomes the dominant loss term, making it difficult to further optimise the bounding box position and size. To address these challenges, this paper introduces perimeter as a supplementary geometric factor, reconstructing the centre distance and aspect ratio losses. We propose an adaptive piecewise regression loss that effectively enhances detection box regression accuracy and training stability.

Methodology

The proposed network architecture, as shown in Fig. 1, employs a five-layer feature extraction unit to progressively downsample the input image by a factor of 2. This process effectively preserves key structures and edge details in the low-resolution features. Features from layers D3 to D5 are then fed into the LFAM module for pre-filtering, mitigating conflicts between multi-scale features. Further integration is achieved through a multi-stage U-shaped fusion network (MUF-Net), enabling deep fusion of cross-layer and adjacent-layer features. Finally, a three-layer detection head completes defect classification and localisation.

Efficient feature extraction module

In defect detection, the backbone network must extract multi-level features ranging from edges and textures to semantic information. Traditional approaches typically rely on stacked convolutions to expand the receptive field, but this often leads to reduced spatial resolution, loss of detail, and increased computational cost. Unlike existing methods, this paper proposes an Efficient Feature Extraction module (E-C2f) that effectively preserves key spatial structures and edge details in low-resolution shallow features. This paper leverages the heterogeneous feature extraction advantages of the Residual Difference-based Embedded Module (RDEM) to design an Efficient Feature Extraction Module (E-C2f), enhancing the model’s perception capabilities for industrial defects. RDEM employs a dual-branch architecture to process input features , comprising a 11 residual convolution branch and a New Block branch. It establishes residual connections with the dimension-reduced features, enabling efficient detection while maintaining structural integrity. The output features from this branch are fused with results from the other branch, then undergo deep separable convolution to further extract spatial features crucial for defect recognition, such as edges and textures. Lightweight feature aggregation module

Considering that current feature fusion methods based on FPN36 (such as PANet37 and AFPN38]) typically employ top-down or bottom-up approaches to fuse features layer by layer, they struggle to achieve deep interactions between non-adjacent layers. In industrial defect detection, minute defects occupy an extremely low proportion in large-scale feature maps. Conventional feature scaling or dimensionality reduction operations easily lead to information loss, and the multidimensional feature compression in backbone networks further exacerbates this issue. Distinct from existing methods like ASFF39 and BiFPN40, this paper proposes a lightweight feature aggregation module (LFAM). By pre-filtering multi-level feature maps at the neck region to mitigate feature conflicts and enabling optimised integration, LFAM minimises information loss for minute defects. The LFAM architecture is illustrated in Fig. 3.

LFAM module structure, the RFEM is used for multi-sensor feature extraction, while the MSEM is employed for cascading multi-level features.

Previous studies have demonstrated that matching the receptive field of a CNN to the target size significantly improves detection accuracy at corresponding scales41. Inspired by the multi-scale architecture of ASPP, this paper introduces a receptive field expansion module on the feature pyramid’s multi-scale layers to enhance perception capability across varying target scales. Specifically, the LFAM module employs a Receptive Field Enhancement Module (RFEM) to perform multi-scale feature extraction on shallow level features M3, compensating for information loss caused by multiple downsampling operations in the backbone network. For deep-level features M5, asymmetric convolutions capture multi-directional gradient features of industrial defects (e.g., scratches, cracks), achieving pixel-level semantic alignment and effectively mitigating feature discrepancies across layers.

Inspired by the grid artefact elimination method in the semantic segmentation network ESPNet, this paper proposes a Multi-level Semantic Enhancement Module (MSEM) to mitigate feature misalignment issues in concatenation or summation fusion caused by semantic differences between hierarchical features. This module pre-filters conflicting information through cascaded summation of multi-level features and connects intermediate features to form the final feature map. This approach reduces the number of channels by one-third compared to a direct connection while enhancing semantic consistency. After further optimising feature integration with the lightweight MSEM, the results are concatenated and fused with shallow-level features (M3). This provides semantically coherent and conflict-reduced feature guidance for detecting minute defects.

As shown in Fig. 4, the RFEM module first applies a 11 convolution to reduce the dimensionality of input feature M3, followed by multi-scale receptive field expansion to enhance defect feature representation. Unlike the approach in42, which emphasises high-level semantics, this module focuses on extracting multi-scale information from low-level features to supplement the detailed characteristics of minute defects. Furthermore, inspired by large kernel selective convolutions, this paper proposes the Convolutional Dynamic Selection Module (CDSM) structure depicted in Fig. 5. This module processes shallow-layer feature M3 using 33 and 55 depth convolutions to expand the receptive field. After dimensionality reduction via 11 convolution, the features are concatenated along the channel dimension to integrate multi-scale feature at a lower computational cost. These are merged, then processed through a 55 convolution with Sigmoid activation. This output is multiplied by the reduced-dimensional features to enhance spatial context awareness. Multi-stage U-shaped fusion network

This paper proposes a multi-stage U-shaped fusion network (MUF-Net) for efficiently integrating multi-level features extracted by backbone networks, as shown in Fig. 7. The network employs a bidirectional feature aggregation mechanism: in the bottom-up path, deep features D5 to D2 undergo sequential 2x upsampling, followed by defect feature extraction and refinement through the dual-branch structure of the RDEM Block (see Fig. 2 for architecture). In the top-down path, feature compression is achieved through convolutional downsampling. This symmetric U-shaped architecture progressively enhances global context modelling capabilities while preserving multi-scale feature resolution.

(a) MUF-net U-shaped network architecture, (b) MFM fusion module.

To address MUF-Net’s tendency to over rely on intra-layer aggregation during feature fusion-neglecting cross-layer interactions and resulting in loss of fine-grained information for minute defects, this paper introduces a Multi-Path Fusion Module (MFM). MFM performs weighted fusion of deep semantic and shallow textural features through a feature importance evaluation mechanism. It constructs a closed-loop aggregation path within the U-shaped network: features on the left are aggregated through MFM from bottom to top and transmitted to shallow layers, while on the right, the aggregated results from every two layers are fed back to deep layers and passed down to the next MFM layer. This promotes cross-layer feature interaction and balances the flow of semantic information across the global context.

As shown in Fig. 7b, the MFM module obtains features and by concatenating two layers of features along the channel dimension, along with their adjacent layer gradient flow information. Subsequently, the fused features undergo spatial pooling, and a weight distribution is generated via the Sigmoid function. Concurrently, features and are added to enhance semantic representation, ultimately amplifying the fused features using spatial weights. This paper reintroduces perimeter as an optimization metric for bounding box regression. As illustrated in Fig. 8, studies show that regression may remain insufficient even when the predicted box shares the same center and aspect ratio as the ground truth. In such cases, the difference in perimeter between the two boxes can provide effective guidance for optimisation. By introducing a perimeter ratio constraint, the model can be guided to further reduce the spatial deviation between the predicted and ground truth boxes. This method dynamically adjusts the weight distribution between the Euclidean distance factor and the perimeter factor using a Sigmoid function. It applies positive weights to high-IoU samples to enhance their gradient contribution, while suppressing the influence of low IoU samples through negative weights. This mechanism enables the model to adaptively adjust its learning direction based on regression status. Ultimately, a regression loss equation with multi-factor balancing characteristics is constructed using a piecewise function, accelerating model training.

Experiment

Experimental parameter settings

In this study, all experiments were carried out under uniform hardware and software configurations to guarantee fairness and comparability of the results. The evaluation trials were run on a system equipped with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 GPU featuring 24GB of memory, operating under Windows 11. The software environment included PyTorch 1.9.5 and CUDA 11.7. The object detection models were implemented in Python and trained using the PyTorch framework. Training was conducted with an initial learning rate of 0.001, over 300 epochs using a batch size of 16, and utilised the SGD optimiser with a momentum of 0.937 and weight decay of 0.0005. Additional parameters included a Num-workers setting of 16 and an input image size of 640640 pixels.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a multi-stage calibration fusion network (RMCF-Net) in view of the fact that steel surface defects seriously affect product reliability and service life, and that actual detection faces challenges such as difficulty in balancing accuracy and efficiency, large changes in defect morphology, and a high proportion of small defect targets. Through systematic model construction combined with a large number of experimental verifications, the following conclusions were mainly obtained in this paper:

First of all, the proposed efficient feature extraction module (E-C2f) effectively enriches the expression of multi-scale defect features through heterogeneous processing strategies. On the premise of maintaining the loss of key structural information, it significantly improves the understanding of subtle and diverse defects.

First of all, the proposed efficient feature extraction module (E-C2f) effectively enriches the expression of multi-scale defect features through heterogeneous processing strategies. On the premise of maintaining the loss of key structural information, it significantly improves the understanding of subtle and diverse defects.

The proposed LFAM module realises the pre-filtering and optimisation integration of multi-level neck features, significantly alleviates semantic conflicts and noise interference in feature maps, and effectively suppresses the loss of information during the propagation process of tiny defects.

The proposed LFAM module realises the pre-filtering and optimisation integration of multi-level neck features, significantly alleviates semantic conflicts and noise interference in feature maps, and effectively suppresses the loss of information during the propagation process of tiny defects.

Further, the constructed MUF-Net fusion network strengthens the feature interaction between cross-level and adjacent levels in the two-way propagation path by introducing an adaptive feature weighting mechanism, and improves the model’s characterisation of complex defect structures.

Further, the constructed MUF-Net fusion network strengthens the feature interaction between cross-level and adjacent levels in the two-way propagation path by introducing an adaptive feature weighting mechanism, and improves the model’s characterisation of complex defect structures.

Finally, the proposed adaptive adjustment function (QCIoU) that integrates perimeter and aspect ratio effectively optimizes regression loss calculation and improves steel defect positioning by more accurately describing the geometric difference between the prediction frame and the real frame.

Experiments on public benchmark data sets (NEU-DET, GC10-DET and Track Components) show that RMCF-Net achieved mAP@0.5 of 78.9%, 67.1% and 71.9% respectively, and the inference speed reached 119 FPS. The comprehensive performance is better than the current mainstream methods, achieving a good balance of accuracy and efficiency. Therefore, this paper not only provides an effective solution to the common problem in steel surface defect detection, but the proposed method also provides reliable technical support and engineering reference for the development of high-quality visual inspection systems in intelligent manufacturing environments.

In view of the scarcity of industrial data and the variety of defect types, future research will focus on expanding the defect identification categories of the model and further improving its classification capabilities and application scope in complex industrial scenarios.

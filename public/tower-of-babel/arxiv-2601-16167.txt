On the dimension drop for harmonic measure on uniformly non-flat Ahlfors-David regular boundaries.
Aritro Pathak

Abstract
We extend earlier results of Azzam on the dimension drop of the harmonic measure for a domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} with n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , with Ahlfors-David regular boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega of dimension s s with n − 1 − δ 0 ≤ s ≤ n − 1 n-1-\delta_{0}\leq s\leq n-1 , that is uniformly non flat. Here δ 0 \delta_{0} is a small positive constant dependent on the parameters of the problem. Our novel construction relies on elementary geometric and potential theoretic considerations. We avoid the use of Riesz transforms and compactness arguments, and also give quantitative bounds on the δ 0 \delta_{0} parameter.

Introduction.

The study of the harmonic measure in relation to the geometric properties of the boundary of a domain

More recently, there has been a focus on the harmonic measure for boundaries

This enabled the use of some ergodic theory. Extensions of this work to the case of boundaries that are not dynamically defined or totally disconnected, but satisfying a uniformly non flatness condition was undertaken in

In future work, we plan to explore applications of the techniques developed here to related problems concerning harmonic measure on rough domains. In particular, these methods open up alternative approaches to questions of uniform rectifiability of the boundary and to the absolute continuity of harmonic measure with such boundaries, which we intend to investigate further.

Basic estimates and overview of the proof.

The Green function is constructed by setting

G(X,Y):=-\mathcal{E}(X,Y)\;+\;\int_{\partial\Omega}\mathcal{E}(X,z)\,d\omega^{Y}(z),

(3.4)

where
\mathcal{E}(X):=c_{n}|X|^{1-n}
is the usual fundamental solution for the
Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Note that we have chosen to take a negative sign for the contribution of the pole, and a positive sign for the contribution of the potential due to the harmonic measure. Also,
G(X,Y)
is positive everywhere inside the domain, by construction, and it vanishes at the boundary. See for example, section 2 of
[
HMM+17
]
.

The basic idea behind our proof is to first isolate some specific boundary ball
B
and consider the harmonic measure due to some pole located within this boundary ball and with the pole being a controlled distance away from the boundary. This can be phrased in terms of the corkscrew point relative to this boundary ball.

We isolate ‘corner’ regions, which are sub-balls of
B
centered on a point on the boundary that is at a minimum distance from the given corkscrew point. If the harmonic measure in such a sub-ball centered on such a ’corner’ point were to be approximately constant, one shows that at the corner point, there is a non zero and non uniform gradient of the potential due to this harmonic measure in this sub-ball.

Now, the entire surface must remain an equipotential, precisely at the value of
0
. This non uniform gradient due to the local harmonic measure has to be compensated by the contribution to the potential, coming from the harmonic measure located further away, along with the contribution due to the pole. In effect, one shows that the gradient due the harmonic measure further away, and the second order contribution from the harmonic measure located nearby and further away, both can be controlled in a manner that this compensation to the local non-uniform contribution cannot happen exactly. This is a contradiction, and we can thus quantify that the harmonic measure cannot be nearly constant, in subballs centered on this corner point. Given any arbitrarily large
M>1
, we find some
l(M)
dependent on
M
and a dyadic cube
C(M)
of length
l(M)
, by a careful repetition of this argument, so that the average Poisson kernel over the cube
C(M)
is at least a factor
M
bigger than the average Poisson kernel over
B
:

\displaystyle k^{p_{B}}(C(M))\geq Mk^{p_{B}}(B).

(3)

Next, by a Markov chain argument effectively outlined in Lemma 4.3 of
[
Az20
]
, we compare the harmonic measure due the pole
p_{B}\in B
, that is a bounded distance away from the boundary relative to the radius of
B
, to the harmonic measure due to the pole at the a-priori chosen point
p
which is located at an arbitrary point in the domain. This leads us to a lower bound of the same form as in
Eq.

3
, but with a different constant
M_{2}
.

This happens in every dyadic cube in
\partial\Omega
. This eventually leads to a dimension drop of the harmonic measure.

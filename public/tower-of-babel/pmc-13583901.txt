ABSTRACT

Introduction: Artificial intelligence (AI) is increasingly influencing smoking cessation research through digital intervention delivery, predictive modelling, diagnostic screening, and longitudinal monitoring. Although AI applications continue to expand, the overall trends of AI in tobacco control have not been systematically characterized. This study aimed to map the research landscape on smoking cessation and identify the major themes most relevant to current and emerging AI applications.

Methods: A bibliometric and content analysis of smoking cessation literature was conducted using nine electronic databases from inception to March 5, 2026. Publications were analyzed by publication year, author frequency, thematic distribution, keyword co-occurrence, and time overlay visualization.

Results: The overall publication output has increased markedly since 2017. The publications were classified into four domains based on keywords: intervention (31.4%, 542/1,729), prediction (24.9%, 430/1,728), diagnosis (13.9%, 241/1,729), and monitoring (3.3%, 57/1,729). Time overlay analysis revealed new research hotspots, such as machine learning, real-time monitoring, and emerging nicotine products.

Conclusion: Smoking cessation studies primarily focus on digital interventions, whereas prediction and diagnosis are emerging sectors closely connected to AI. Monitoring is an underexplored area that suggests important future opportunities. Leveraging evolving AI applications could support tobacco control efforts in alignment with global strategies and national initiatives in China.

Tobacco use is a persistent major public health challenge and leading behavioral risk factor for death worldwide (1). Tobacco substantially increases mortality from health conditions including cancers, heart diseases, and chronic respiratory diseases (1). In China, tobacco use accounts for over 1 million deaths annually, and over 100,000 deaths are attributable to secondhand smoke exposure (2). However, smoking prevalence remains high, with the China CDC reporting a prevalence of 23.2% (male: 43.9%; female: 1.8%) among individuals aged ≥15 years in 2024 (3).

As smoking is the most preventable cause of death, a range of interventions and policies have been developed to reduce smoking prevalence and improve quality of life (1). Artificial intelligence (AI) has become increasingly important in smoking cessation research through digital intervention delivery, predictive modelling, diagnostic classification, and follow-up monitoring. Given its increased adoption in tobacco control, a better understanding of its overall thematic evolution and publication trends is required. Bibliometric analysis offers a visualization-based approach to systematically map the research landscape using vast volumes of scientific data. Macrolevel evidence generated from factors such as authors, hotspots, and intellectual structure can inform research resource allocation and policy development. This study aimed to answer the following questions:

First, what is the overall publication trend for artificial intelligence in smoking-cessation research?

Second, what are the main research themes and their connections?

Third, what are the temporal patterns of the research topics, hotspots, and gaps?

Data extraction involved a comprehensive, single-day search over nine electronic databases (PubMed, APA PsycINFO, Cochrane Library, CINAHL, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, MEDLINE, and ClinicalTrials.gov), covering inception to March 5, 2026, to capture the collective evolution of AI applications in smoking cessation research. The search strategy included keywords related to AI, tobacco control, and AI application approaches, as outlined in Supplementary Table S1 (available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/). The AI keywords were based on the operational definition of AI as a computational method for data pattern extraction to enable automated decision-making (4). Classical and modern approaches were both included to capture the extended historical evolution of these algorithms. The records were limited to those published in English and were downloaded in RIS format.

Figure 1 shows the data screening and analysis. The initial search identified 4,052 entries. After deduplication, 1,728 records remained. Following coder training by the senior author (AWH), two screeners (HYFC, GL) conducted independent manual title and abstract screening. Records were coded into one of four research domains (intervention, diagnosis, monitoring, and prediction) or an unclassified category based on their principal research objectives and the domain definitions, subdomain categories, and relevant keywords in Table 1. The unclassified category comprised publications lacking meaningful relevance to AI and smoking cessation. Coding discrepancies were resolved through discussion with AWH. The records were analyzed using Python (version 3.12; The Python Software Foundation) for publication year, author appearance frequency, and thematic analysis. VOSviewer (version 1.6.20; Leiden University, Netherlands) was used to create the author collaboration network, keyword co-occurrence, and time overlap analysis of the records. The analysis used a minimum keyword occurrence threshold of 19, full counting, and standardization based on association strength.

Publications within the field began in 1982 and have continuously increased in volume. The complete Kneedle algorithm identified 2017 as a critical turning point, with a sensitivity parameter of S=1. The annual publication output (Figure 2A) increased gradually from two in 1982 to 49 in 2017. Following the inflection point, this number increased substantially, peaking at 282 in 2025. There were 578 publications in the 36 years up to 2017 and 1,088 in the 9 years after 2017.

Content analysis was conducted to characterize thematic emphasis within the research field. Using keyword-driven coding, the records were classified into four major AI application domains: intervention, prediction, diagnosis, and monitoring (Table 1; details in Supplementary Table S3, available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/). Intervention is the largest domain, comprising 542 records (31.4%), followed by prediction (430 records, 24.9%), diagnosis (241 records, 13.9%), and monitoring (57 records, 3.3%). The remaining 458 (26.5%) records were unclassified.

High-frequency keyword groups within each domain were used to characterize thematic emphasis and identify areas of importance, as outlined in Supplementary Table S3 (available at https://weekly.chinacdc.cn/). The intervention domain was dominated by treatment (425), smoking cessation (405), effect (285), trial (161), digital intervention (123), and counseling (74). The prediction domain was characterized by predictive modeling (338), prediction (318), forecasting (113), and prognostics (74). The diagnosis domain included detection (122), testing (110), and screening (78). Monitoring included surveillance (36), longitudinal (5), and adherence (5). The content analysis in Figure 2C maps the keywords across the four domains, revealing interdisciplinary relationships and close cross-theme research between certain domains (e.g., intervention and diagnosis, monitoring and prediction).

The keyword co-occurrence analysis revealed research hotspots (Figure 2D). The red cluster keywords include "artificial intelligence," "tobacco control," "intervention," "health promotion," and "health behavior." The yellow cluster contains the AI subtheme of machine learning, where "diagnosis" and "monitoring" are the most closely related. The green cluster represents predictive modelling and its diverse implementations ("risk assessment," "risk factor"). The blue cluster keywords are related to general smoking-cessation research, such as "randomized controlled trial," "outcome," "self-efficacy," and "abstinence." The time overlay chart shows a clear evolutionary trend (Figure 2E). The keywords for the years before 2017 (purple and blue) represent core research themes ("smoking cessation," "artificial intelligence," "intervention," “treatment outcome," "nicotine") and earlier AI paradigms ("expert system," "data mining"). For 2017–2021 (green), AI applications emphasized "risk assessment," "screening," "classification," and "forecasting." From 2021 onwards, new research hotspots (yellow) include recent AI applications ("monitoring," "real-time," "machine learning"), recent AI platforms ("mobile phones," "chatbots"), and emerging alternative smoking products ("ASPs," "electronic cigarettes," "vaping"). DISCUSSION

The use of AI is expanding rapidly everywhere. Our critical review and analysis of trends in the publications of the past 45 years reveal that interest in the use of AI within smoking cessation research is accelerating, especially in the last decade. The rising trend also suggests persistent expansion in the future. This expansion aligns with wider digital healthcare research patterns, which show accelerating development of AI in public health, personalized interventions, and clinical decision-support across various settings (5).

Within the four themes of AI in smoking-cessation research, current research primarily focuses on intervention-focused studies, underscoring sustained efforts on cessation strategies and potential targeted support for at-risk individuals. Applications for prediction and diagnosis are emerging to a lesser extent. The focus on prediction reflects attention to modelling and assessment. Diagnostic research indicates opportunities for early identification. By contrast, research on monitoring approaches using AI for tobacco control is relatively new and underexplored, indicating a gap in research and policy. AI can contribute to tobacco use monitoring with longitudinal tracking and real-time adaptive feedback systems, particularly within community and primary healthcare settings. AI-based sustained monitoring could improve time efficiency and minimize manual effort. A multifaceted approach is encouraged, and the four domains should be systematically integrated to achieve effective smoking cessation and sustained tobacco control.

Globally, AI will increasingly influence smoking cessation efforts, warranting standardized protocols specific to healthcare practices using the FUTURE-AI framework (6). These international consensus guidelines are based on six principles: fairness, universality, traceability, usability, robustness, and explainability. FUTURE-AI spans the lifecycle of healthcare AI, including its design, development, validation, deployment, and monitoring (6). The integration of human expertise and oversight into AI systems is also vital. Co-design among diverse stakeholders, including academics, clinicians, policymakers, patients, public-health practitioners, and the general public, should be actively promoted to effectively address the complex and evolving health needs of populations.

By 2030, the Chinese government aims to reduce its high smoking prevalence to below 20% in individuals aged ≥15 years within Healthy China Action (2019–2030) (2). Hong Kong has a current prevalence of 8.5% (538,100/6,353,200) and a target prevalence of 7.8% by 2025 (7). The action plan outlines the development of a comprehensive smoking-cessation service to promote brief advice interventions, national-level tobacco product surveillance, and routine outpatient smoking-history documentation, providing multiple integration points for the four themes of AI approaches in national tobacco-control efforts. Key applications include prediction models for personalized digital intervention delivery, patient tobacco-use screening, and longitudinal monitoring of smoking-related patterns.

This promising and expanding research and policy interest also carries potential risks. Notable challenges include bias and equity issues affecting fairness and the accuracy of predictions and interventions in algorithmic decision-making (8). AI involves data collection and utilization, which are beneficial for model training and intervention personalization, but potentially subject to data leaks and misuse (8). Hence, since 2017, the Chinese government has taken early and steady actions to support and regulate AI use, laying the regulatory groundwork and establishing advisory committees (9–10). In 2020, voluntary ethical guidelines shifted to formal legal regulations, strengthening regulatory oversight of AI use (9). This oversight comprises three pillars: data protection, algorithmic governance, and content moderation (9). Data protection focuses on the ethical, safe, and responsible use of personal information. Algorithmic governance involves AI system registration and the use of reliable training datasets. Content moderation includes content labeling, transparency, and the strict control of harmful information. This adaptive, coordinated governance system in China may serve as a useful framework for the global implementation of AI in tobacco-control systems.

The findings in this report are subject to at least five limitations. First, the bibliometric analysis reflects publication activity from selected databases, excluding unpublished research and gray literature, limiting result comprehensiveness. Second, the restriction to English-language records may have caused the overrepresentation of research from countries where English is the primary language. Third, changes in the terminology definitions related to AI and tobacco products over time could affect the content analysis accuracy. Fourth, manual theme classification inherently involves subjective judgment, particularly for records spanning multiple domains. Finally, the bibliometric findings should not be interpreted as indicators of exact trends in practical implementation and policy progress.

Within its evolving landscape, AI holds substantial potential for advancing smoking cessation efforts across multiple research domains. Although smoking intervention, predictive analytics, and diagnostic support are the primary areas of AI application in current research, the use of AI in monitoring remains underutilized, indicating crucial opportunities to expand its expertise and deployment. With the global expansion of AI use in tobacco control, the adoption of FUTURE-AI frameworks is essential to ensure the systematic integration of AI into healthcare practices. In China, the comprehensive, multifaceted application of AI across the four research domains in alignment with national health strategies may provide valuable support for tobacco-control efforts.

On the maximal subgroups of almost simple and primitive perfect groups
Patricia Medina Capilla, Luca Sabatini

Abstract
. We prove that, if G G is a finite almost simple group and H H is a maximal subgroup of G G , then the 10 10 th term of the derived series of H H is perfect. The same is true if G G is perfect and H H is core-free. The constant 10 10 is best possible.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20D05, 20B15

1.
Introduction

One of the major areas of research in finite group theory is the investigation of the maximal subgroups of the
finite simple groups
[
KL90
]
.
When possible, it is desirable to have results that are not in the form of a classification, but crisp statements capturing uniform structural constraints.
It is interesting that this can be done with respect to some natural parameter:
in 2013, Burness, Liebeck and Shalev
[
BLS13
]
proved that every maximal subgroup of every finite simple group can be generated by at most
4
elements.
The same result for almost simple groups (with
5
in place of
4
)
was proven by Lucchini, Marion and Tracey in 2020
[
LMT20
]
.
A different type of constraint was noted by the second author in
[
Sab26
]
.
He observed that, if
G
is an almost simple group with a solvable maximal subgroup
H
, then the derived length of
H
is at most
10

[
Sab26
, Th. 1.4]
.
This is sharp if
H
is a solvable maximal subgroup of the sporadic Fischer group
\mathrm{Fi}_{23}
.

In this paper, we show that the latter result is just a glimpse of a much more general phenomenon, which, similarly to the theorems in
[
BLS13
,
LMT20
]
, covers all maximal subgroups of all almost simple groups.
In fact, we prove that all maximal subgroups are nearly perfect,
in the sense that their derived series stabilize in a bounded number of steps.
We write
H^{(n)}
for the
n
-th term of the derived series of
H=H^{(0)}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
G
is an almost simple group and
H
is a maximal subgroup of
G
,
then
H^{(10)}
is perfect.
In addition,
H^{(9)}
is perfect unless
H
is a solvable maximal subgroup of the Fischer group
\mathrm{Fi}_{23}
.

We recover
[
Sab26
, Th. 1.4]
when
H
is solvable (and
H^{(10)}
is trivial).
The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on the Classification of the Finite Simple Groups, which allows to split into cases where the structure of
H
is well understood.
One of our tools is the observation that, for any normal subgroup
N
of
H
, if both
N^{(a)}
and
(H/N)^{(b)}
are perfect then so is
H^{(a+b)}
(Proposition
2.3
).

It is natural to ask whether Theorem
1.1
can be extended to maximal subgroups of perfect groups. In general, the answer is no:

Example 1.2
.

Let
S
be a solvable group,
and let
G=(S\wr_{5}\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(5))^{\prime}
be the derived subgroup of the natural wreath product.
It is not difficult to see that
G=B\rtimes\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(5)
is perfect,
where
B
is a subgroup of
S^{5}
of index
|S:S^{\prime}|
which contains a copy of
S
.
If
K<\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(5)
is a maximal subgroup,
then
H=B\rtimes K
is a solvable maximal subgroup of
G
whose derived length is at least the derived length of
S

(in particular, it can be arbitrarily large).

However, the maximal subgroup
H
in Example
1.2
contains a large normal subgroup of
G
, which is the source of its large derived length.
In Section
4
, we show that this is the only possible obstruction:

Theorem 1.3
.

If
G
is a primitive perfect group with stabilizer
H
, then
H^{(10)}
is perfect.
In addition,
H^{(9)}
is perfect unless
H
is a solvable maximal subgroup of the Fischer group
\mathrm{Fi}_{23}
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is a combination of Theorem
1.1
and the O’Nan-Scott theorem.
Generalizing
[
LS25
]
,
it was shown in
[
Sab26
, Th. 1.5]
that in a primitive group with solvable stabilizer, there exist two points whose pointwise stabilizer has derived length bounded by an absolute constant.
The results in this paper suggest the following further generalization to all primitive groups:

Conjecture 1.4
.

In every finite primitive group,
there exist two points whose pointwise stabilizer
H
satisfies that
H^{(c)}
is perfect,
where
c
is an absolute constant.

We point out in Example
4.1
that the constant in Conjecture
1.4
is at least
7
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
A generalized derived length

Every group in this paper is finite.
For a finite group
G
,
let
G=G^{(0)}
and

G^{(n+1)}\>=\>[G^{(n)},G^{(n)}]

for any
n\geq 0

(we also write
G^{\prime}=G^{(1)}
).
Let
G^{(\infty)}=\cap_{n\geq 1}G^{(n)}
be the perfect core (or solvable residual) of
G
.
Since
G
is finite,
G^{(\infty)}
is the last term of the derived series, or equivalently it is the unique largest perfect subgroup
(note that a group generated by perfect subgroups is perfect).

In order to deal with our main results, it is convenient to write
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)
for the minimum
n
such that
G^{(n)}=G^{(\infty)}
(this is also the minimum
n
such that
G^{(n)}=G^{(n+1)}
).
We may also write
\mathrm{dl}(G)
if
G
is solvable, i.e. if
G^{(\infty)}=1
.
Observe that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)=\mathrm{dl}(G/G^{(\infty)})
and that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)=0
if and only if
G
is a (possibly trivial) perfect group.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
H\leqslant G
.
Then

•

for each
n\geq 0
we have
H^{(n)}\subseteq G^{(n)}
;
in particular,
H^{(\infty)}\subseteq G^{(\infty)}
;

•

if
G^{(\infty)}\subseteq H
, then
H^{(\infty)}=G^{(\infty)}
and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)
.

Proof.

The first part is trivial.
For the second part, we work in the solvable group
G/G^{(\infty)}
. Since subgroups of solvable groups are solvable with derived length not exceeding the original derived length, the result is clear.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
N\unlhd G
.
Then

•

for each
n\geq 0
we have
(G/N)^{(n)}=NG^{(n)}/N
;
in particular,
(G/N)^{(\infty)}=NG^{(\infty)}/N
;

•

if
N\subseteq G^{(\infty)}
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)=\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)
.

Proof.

The first part is trivial.
For the second part, observe that
G/G^{(\infty)}
is isomorphic to
\frac{(G/N)}{(G/N)^{(\infty)}}
.
∎

A key difference between
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)
and the genuine derived length, which is only defined for solvable groups, is that the first can increase on subgroups in general (just take a perfect group and a non-trivial solvable subgroup).
However,
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)
behaves well with respect to group extensions.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
N\unlhd G
, then

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N).

Proof.

For each
n\geq 0
, it is easy to see that
(G/N)^{(n)}
is perfect if
G^{(n)}
is perfect, and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)
.
For the right hand side, observe that this result is well-known (and trivial) for solvable groups. Therefore we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dl}(G/G^{(\infty)})

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dl}(G/G^{(\infty)}N)+\mathrm{dl}(G^{(\infty)}N/N)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{dl}(G/G^{(\infty)}N)+\mathrm{dl}(N/G^{(\infty)}\cap N).

Now
\mathrm{dl}(G/G^{(\infty)}N)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)
because the first is a solvable quotient of the second.
For the same reason we get
\mathrm{dl}(N/G^{(\infty)}\cap N)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N)
,
and the proof follows.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

If
G=N_{0}\rhd\cdots\rhd N_{k}=1
is a subnormal series where
N_{i}/N_{i+1}
is solvable or perfect for each
i
,
then,

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\>\leq\>\sum_{N_{i}/N_{i+1}\mbox{ solvable}}\mathrm{dl}(N_{i}/N_{i+1}).

Proof.

Apply Proposition
2.3
iteratively.
∎

Corollary
2.4
is particularly useful when some large abelian section appears in the subnormal series. Large
p
-sections are also nice, as explained in the following remark:

Remark 2.5
.

For a
p
-group
P
we have
\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq 1
if
|P|\leq p^{2}
,

\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq 2
if
|P|\leq p^{5}
,

\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq 3
if
|P|\leq p^{12}
,

\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq 4
if
|P|\leq p^{21}
,
and
\mathrm{dl}(P)\leq 5
if
|P|\leq p^{39}

[
Man00
]
.

We report another lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

For every finite group
G
,
there exists a solvable subgroup
S
such that

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\leq\mathrm{dl}(S)
.

Proof.

We show by induction on
|G|
that there exists a solvable subgroup
S
such that
G=G^{(\infty)}S
.
The claim will follow because
G/G^{(\infty)}
is isomorphic to a quotient of
S
.
We can assume that
G^{(\infty)}
is non-solvable,
so it is not contained in the Frattini subgroup of
G
,
and there exists a maximal subgroup
M
of
G
such that
G=G^{(\infty)}M
.
By induction, there exists a solvable subgroup
S
of
M
such that
M=M^{(\infty)}S
.
But then
G=G^{(\infty)}M=G^{(\infty)}S
.
∎

Remark 2.7
.

Let
G\leqslant\GL_{n}(q)
.
By Lemma
2.6
we have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\leq\mathrm{dl}(S)
for some solvable group
S\leqslant\GL_{n}(q)
.
Now, the derived length of
S
is bounded by a function on
n

[
New72
]
.
In particular, if we aim to prove
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\leq 9
, looking at
[
New72
, Pag. 70]
, we can assume that
G
does not have a faithful linear representation of dimension less than
9
.

2.2.
Finite simple groups

Let
T
be a non-abelian finite simple group.
The
outer automorphism group

\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(T)
is the quotient of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(T)
by the normal subgroup of the inner automorphisms (which is isomorphic to
T
).
The following result is a consequence of the Classification of the Finite Simple Groups.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
T
is a finite simple group, then
\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(T)
is solvable of derived length at most
3
.
More precisely:

•

If
T
is cyclic, alternating, or a sporadic simple group, then
\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(T)
is abelian;

•

\mathrm{dl}(\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(T))=3
if and only if
T
is the orthogonal group
\mathrm{P}\Omega^{+}_{8}(q)
for
q
odd.

Proof.

This is standard (for example, see
[
Sab26
, Lem. 3.2]
).
∎

A finite group
G
is
almost simple
if there exists a non-abelian simple group
T
such that
T\unlhd G\leqslant\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(T)
.
By Lemma
2.8
, the almost simple groups are nearly perfect in the sense that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G)\leq 3
, and Theorem
1.1
says that this property (with
10
in place of
3
) is inherited by the maximal subgroups.

We now give some preliminaries for the classical groups of Lie type.
We follow the notation used in
[
KL90
]
, which we summarize below. Let
V
be a vector space over a finite field
\mathbb{F}
, and let
\kappa
be either the zero form, or a non-degenerate unitary, symplectic, or quadratic form on
V
. We say that
\kappa
is of type
\mathbf{L},\mathbf{U},\mathbf{S}
or
\mathbf{O}
, respectively. We may refer to type
\mathbf{U}
as
\mathbf{L}^{-}
. Additionally, type
\mathbf{O}
splits into 3 further types:
\mathbf{O}^{+}
and
\mathbf{O}^{-}
, when
\dim(V)
is even; and
\mathbf{O}^{\circ}
, when
\dim(V)
is odd. We will refer to
\mathbf{O}^{\circ}
simply as
\mathbf{O}
.
As in
[
KL90
, Ch. 2]
, we define:

•

S=S(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
the group of special
\kappa
-isometries of
V
;

•

I=I(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
the group of
\kappa
-isometries of
V
;

•

\Delta=\Delta(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
the group of
\kappa
-similarities of
V
;

•

\Gamma=\Gamma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
the group of
\kappa
-semisimilarities of
V
;

•

If
\kappa
is of type
\mathbf{O}
, let
\Omega=\Omega(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
be the unique index 2 subgroup of
S
. Otherwise, let
\Omega=S
;

•

If
\kappa
is of type
\mathbf{L}
with
\dim(V)\geq 3
, let
\Sigma=\Sigma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)=\langle\Gamma,\iota\rangle
, where
\iota
is the inverse transpose map on
\Gamma
. Otherwise, let
\Sigma=\Gamma
.

We have the chain of groups

\Omega\>\leqslant\>S\>\leqslant\>I\>\leqslant\>\Delta\>\leqslant\>\Gamma\>\leqslant\>\Sigma,

all acting on the vector space
V
.
For example, if
V=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and
\kappa
is the zero form, then

\Omega=S=\SL_{n}(q)
,
I=\Delta=\GL_{n}(q)
,
\Gamma=\GammaL_{n}(q)
and
\Sigma=\langle\Gamma,\iota\rangle
.

For any subgroup
K
of
\Sigma
, we write
\overline{K}
to denote the image of
K
under the quotient by the scalars of
\Sigma
.
Similarly, for any subgroup
K
of
\overline{\Sigma}
, we use
K^{\wedge}
to denote the preimage of
K
in
\Sigma
.

Remark 2.9
.

The group
\overline{\Sigma}
is always contained in
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\overline{\Omega})
. In almost all cases
\overline{\Sigma}=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\overline{\Omega})
, unless
\Omega
is equal to
\Sp_{4}(q)
with
q
even, or
\Omega_{8}^{+}(q)
for any
q
. Then,
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\overline{\Omega})
is equal to
\langle\overline{\Sigma},\overline{\gamma}\rangle
, for some graph automorphism
\gamma
.

Lemma 2.10
.

If
(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
is a classical geometry, then
\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega}
is solvable.
If
\overline{\Omega}
is simple, then
\mathrm{dl}(\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega})\leq 2
. Otherwise, we have

\mathrm{dl}(\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega})\>\leq\>\begin{cases}3\qquad&if $\kappa$ is of type $\mathbf{L}$ or $\mathbf{S}$,\\
4&if $\kappa$ is of type $\mathbf{O}$,\\
5&if $\kappa$ is of type $\mathbf{U}$.\end{cases}

Proof.

In
[
KL90
, Ch. 2]
the groups
\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega}
are classified, and it is easy to see that they are solvable with
\mathrm{dl}(\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega})\leq 2
. This proves the result in the case where
\overline{\Omega}
is simple.
If
\overline{\Omega}
is not simple, then the possibilities for
\overline{\Omega}
are indicated in
[
KL90
, Prop. 2.9.1]
. Thus we check that the claim holds by bounding
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\mathrm{dl}(\overline{\Sigma}/\overline{\Omega})
in each case and applying Proposition
2.3
.
∎

Finally, we introduce a group
\Sigma^{*}(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
.

Definition
.

Let
(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
be a classical geometry. We let

\Sigma^{*}(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)\>:=\>\begin{cases}\Gamma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa):\langle\iota\rangle\qquad&if $(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)$ is of type $\mathbf{L}$,\\
\Gamma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)&otherwise,\end{cases}

where
\iota
acts as the inverse transpose automorphism.

Note that if
\dim(V)\leq 2
and
(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
is of type
\mathbf{L}
, then
\Sigma^{*}(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)=\Sigma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa):2
,
and
\Sigma^{*}(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)=\Sigma(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
otherwise.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
G
be an almost simple group with socle
T
,
and let
H
be a maximal subgroup of
G
.
We have to show that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 10
.
By Lemma
2.8
we have that
H/T\cap H
is solvable (as a subgroup of
G/T
), and so
H^{(\infty)}\subseteq T\cap H
. It follows by Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
2.3
that

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap H)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap H)+\mathrm{dl}(G/T).

In most cases, it will be sufficient to use the weaker bound

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap H)+\mathrm{dl}(\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(T)).

Note that, in general,
T\cap H
is not a maximal subgroup of
T
.

Using the Classification of the Finite Simple Groups,
we distinguish various cases.

3.1.
T
is alternating

We address this case with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(n)
or
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(n)
and let
H
be a maximal subgroup of
G
.
Then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
, and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
with finitely many exceptions.

Proof.

If
H
is almost simple, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 3
by Lemma
2.8
.
Otherwise, by
[
LPS87
]
,
H=X\cap G
where
X
is a maximal subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(n)
which is not almost simple. If
X\neq H
, then
|X:H|=2
and
X^{\prime}\subseteq H
.
In general, we always have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)
by Lemma
2.1
.

If
X
is intransitive, then
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(k)\times\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(n-k)
for some
k\geq 1
. Thus
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 3
.

If
X
is transitive but not primitive, then
n=md
,
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(m)\wr_{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(d)
and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 6

(equality is attained for
m=d=4
).
Moreover
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
if
md>16

(equality is attained for
d=4
and
m\geq 5
).

If
X
is primitive,
then by the O’Nan-Scott theorem
[
DM96
, Sec.4]
we have the following possibilities:

\bullet

Affine.

In this case
n=p^{d}
and
X=\mathop{\mathrm{AGL}}_{d}(p)
.
Therefore
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 5
, and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 4
if
p^{d}>3^{2}
;

\bullet

Product action.

In this case
n=m^{d}
and
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(m)\wr_{d}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(d)
.
Therefore
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 6
and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 4
if
m^{d}>4^{4}
;

\bullet

Diagonal type.

In this case
n=|S|^{m-1}
, where
S
is a non-abelian simple group, and
X=S^{m}.(\mathop{\mathrm{Out}}(S)\times\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(m))
. Hence,
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(X)\leq 3
.

The proof is complete.
∎

In the exceptional case where
T=\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(6)
, it can be checked easily that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 3
.

3.2.
T
is a classical group

Let
T
be a classical simple group over
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{u}}
where
q=p^{r}
and
u
is either
2
if
T
is of type
\mathbf{U}
, or
1
otherwise.
Then there exists a classical geometry
(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
such that
T=\overline{\Omega}(V,\mathbb{F},\kappa)
, and
\overline{\Omega}\leqslant G\leqslant\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\bar{\Omega})
. We assume first that
G\subseteq\Sigma
, and treat the exceptional cases at the end of this section.
We split into different types of maximal subgroups
H<G
by using Aschbacher’s Theorem (see
[
KL90
, Ch. 3]
).
We assume that
H
is not solvable, as this case is already treated in
[
Sab26
, Th. 1.4]
.
Finally, we delay the case where
H
is parabolic to Section
3.4
, in order to consider all groups of Lie type simultaneously.

(1)

\mathscr{C}_{1}
: We only treat the non-parabolic cases.
Suppose first
H
is of type
\GL_{m}(q)\oplus\GL_{n-m}(q)
.
Then there exist two subspaces
V_{1},V_{2}
of
V
with forms
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
respectively such that, for
\Omega_{i}=\Omega(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
and
\Sigma_{i}=\Sigma^{*}(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
, we have

\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\leqslant H^{\wedge}\leqslant\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2}.

Since both
\Sigma_{i}/\Omega_{i}
are solvable, by Lemmas
2.1
and
2.10
we have

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge})\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(\Sigma_{1}\times\Sigma_{2})\leq 5.

We argue similarly if
H
is of type
\GU_{m}(q)\perp\GU_{n-m}(q)
,
\Sp_{m}(q)\perp\Sp_{n-m}(q)
or
\O_{m}^{\epsilon_{1}}(q)\perp\O_{n-m}^{\epsilon_{2}}(q)
, obtaining
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge})\leq 6
in these cases. If
H
is of type
\Sp_{n-2}(q)
then
H
is a classical group of type
\mathbf{S}
, and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge})\leq 3
.

(2)

\mathscr{C}_{2}
: Suppose
H
is of type
\GL_{m}(q)\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
. Then there exists a classical geometry
(V_{1},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{1})
such that
H^{\wedge}
is a subgroup of
\Sigma^{*}(V_{1},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{1})\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
. Additionally,
H^{\wedge}
contains
\SL_{m}(q)^{t}
and projects onto
\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(t)
. Thus, by Proposition
2.3
,

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(\Sigma^{*}(V_{1},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{1}))+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)).

If
\mathop{\mathrm{PSL}}_{m}(q)
is simple, then the above gives
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4+3=7
. Otherwise we can assume
t\geq 5
and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 5+1=6
.

Similarly, if
H
is of type
\GU_{m}(q)\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
,
\Sp_{m}(q)\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
, or
\O_{m}^{\epsilon}(q)\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge})\leq 7
.

If
H
is of type
\GL_{n/2}(q^{u}).2
, then
\SL_{n/2}(q^{u})\leqslant H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega\leqslant\GL_{n/2}(q^{u}).2
, and so by Proposition
2.3
and Lemma
2.10
we have that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega)+2\leq 7
.

If
H
is of type
\O_{n/2}(q)^{2}
, then
H^{\wedge}
is contained in
\GammaO_{n/2}(q)^{2}:\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(2)
, and contains
\Omega_{n/2}(q)^{2}
and so as previously we have that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H^{\wedge})\leq 5
.

(3)

\mathscr{C}_{3}
: In this case, there exists a classical geometry
(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
such that

\Omega(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})\leqslant H^{\wedge}\cap\Gamma\leqslant\Gamma(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp}).

Thus, when
G
is not of type
\mathbf{L}
we have that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
by Lemma
2.10
. If
G
is of type
\mathbf{L}
then by
[
KL90
, Sec. 4.3]
we have that
(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
is also of type
\mathbf{L}
, and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 5
.

(4)

\mathscr{C}_{4}
: Let
(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
for
i=1,2
be the classical geometries described in
[
KL90
, Sec. 4.4]
. Let
\Sigma_{i}=\Sigma^{*}(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
and
\Omega_{i}=\Omega(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
. Then, as previously,
H
always contains
\overline{\Omega}_{1}\times\overline{\Omega}_{2}
, and is contained in
\overline{\Sigma}_{1}\times\overline{\Sigma}_{2}
. Thus, as when
H\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
, we have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 5
.

(5)

\mathscr{C}_{5}
: Let
(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
be as described in
[
KL90
, Sec. 4.5]
, and let
\Delta_{\sharp}=\Delta(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
and
\Omega_{\sharp}=\Omega(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
. Then,
\overline{\Omega}_{\sharp}\leqslant H\cap\overline{\Delta}\leqslant\overline{\Delta}_{\sharp}
, so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\overline{\Delta})\leq 5
and hence
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
.

(6)

\mathscr{C}_{6}
: Recall we exclude any types for which
H
is always solvable. If
H
is of type
(4\circ 2^{1+2m}).\Sp_{2m}(2)
with
m>1
, then
H\leqslant(2^{2m}.\Sp_{2m}(r))\times 2
. If
m=2
, then
\Sp_{4}(2)^{\prime}=\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(6)
, and
\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(6)
acts irreducibly on the natural module of
\Sp_{4}(2)
, so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 1
. Otherwise, if
m\geq 3
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 1
.

Similarly, if
H
is of type
r^{1+2m}.\Sp_{2m}(r)
, with
r
an odd prime and
m>1
, or if it’s of type
2_{\pm}^{1+2m}.\O_{2m}^{\pm}(2)
with
m>2
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 1
.

(7)

\mathscr{C}_{7}
: This case can be treated in the same manner as
\mathscr{C}_{2}
, since
H
is a subgroup of
\overline{\Sigma}_{1}\wr_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(t)
where
\Sigma_{1}=\Sigma^{*}(V_{1},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{1})
for some classical geometry
(V_{1},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{1})
. Thus, we have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
.

(8)

\mathscr{C}_{8}
: Let
(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
be a classical geometry as described in
[
KL90
, Sec. 4.8]
, and let
\Gamma_{\sharp}=\Gamma(V_{\sharp},\mathbb{F}_{\sharp},\kappa_{\sharp})
. Then,
H\leqslant\overline{\Gamma}_{\sharp}.2
, and hence
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 7
.

(9)

\mathscr{S}
: In this case
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 3
by Lemma
2.8
.

We now consider the case where
G
is not a subgroup of
\overline{\Sigma}
. As mentioned in Remark
2.9
, we must have that
T=\overline{\Omega}
is equal to
\mathop{\mathrm{PSp}}_{4}(q)
or
\POmega_{8}^{+}(q)
. Additionally, the group
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\overline{\Omega})
is generated by
\overline{\Sigma}
and a graph automorphism of order
2
or
3
respectively.

Firstly note that if
H\cap\overline{\Sigma}
is solvable, then so is
H
and we are done. If instead
H\cap\overline{\Sigma}
is a non-solvable group in one of the classes of maximal subgroups considered above, then we have proven that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\overline{\Sigma})\leq 7
, and hence
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 8
. Thus, we can assume that
H\cap\overline{\Sigma}
is not in the classes
\mathscr{C}_{1}
to
\mathscr{C}_{8}
, nor
\mathscr{S}
.

We use
[
BHR13
]
to study the remaining cases.
If
G
is of type
\mathbf{S}
, then
H
is solvable.
Hence we assume
T=\POmega_{8}^{+}(q)
. Let
d=(q-1,2)
.
If
H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega=d\times G_{2}(q)
, we can assume that
G_{2}(q)
is not solvable, and hence
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
.
If
H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega=(\Omega_{2}^{\pm}(q)\times\frac{1}{d}\GL_{3}(q)).[2d]
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\overline{\Omega})\leq 3
and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
. If
H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega=2^{4}.2^{6}.\L_{3}(2)
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\overline{\Omega})\leq 2
and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 5
.
Finally, if
H^{\wedge}\cap\Omega
is equal to
(D_{2(q^{2}+q)/d})^{2}.[2d].\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(2)
, then
H
is solvable.

We observe that there are infinitely many almost simple classical groups
G
and maximal subgroups
H
with
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)=6
:

Example 3.2
.

Let
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}_{n}(2))
with
n\geq 7
,
3\nmid n
,
and let
H
be a maximal subgroup of type
\GU_{3}(2)\perp\GU_{n-3}(2)
.
In particular, there exists a non-degenerate subspace
V_{1}
of dimension
3
such that
H=N_{G}(V_{1})
.
Letting
V_{2}=V_{1}^{\perp}
,
there exist unitary forms
\kappa_{i}
on
V_{i}
,
given simply by restricting
\kappa
to
V_{i}
.
Let
I_{i}=I(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
and
\Gamma_{i}=\Gamma(V_{i},\mathbb{F},\kappa_{i})
. Note that
\Gamma_{1}\cong\GU_{3}(2):C_{2}
, and
\mathrm{dl}(\Gamma_{1})=6
.
Then

I_{1}\times I_{2}\leqslant H^{\wedge}\leqslant\Gamma_{1}\times\Gamma_{2}
,
as a subdirect product,
where the scalars are
\diag(Z(I_{1})\times Z(I_{2}))
.
Since
3\nmid n
, the group
Z(\SU_{n-3}(2))
is trivial, and thus
\GU_{n-3}(2)=\SU_{n-3}(2):Z(I_{2})
. This implies that both
H^{\wedge}
and
H
have perfect cores isomorphic to
\mathop{\mathrm{PSU}}_{n-3}(2)
. Moreover,
H^{(5)}
is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H^{\wedge}}{\diag(Z(I_{1})\times Z(I_{2}))}\right)^{(5)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\diag(Z(I_{1})\times Z(I_{2}))(H^{\wedge})^{(5)}}{\diag(Z(I_{1})\times Z(I_{2}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z(I_{1})\times\GU_{n-3}(2)}{\diag(Z(I_{1})\times Z(I_{2}))}.

As such,
H^{(5)}
is not isomorphic to
H^{(\infty)}
, and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\geq 6
. Since
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(\Gamma_{i})\leq 6
, we conclude that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)=6
.

Finding the best possible constant for a version of Theorem
1.1
with finitely many exceptions seems to be a tedious problem:
if such a constant is
6
, then a detailed case-by-case analysis is required; on the other hand, proving that the derived length is not less than
7
seems to be difficult.

3.3.
T
is exceptional

By Remark
2.7
,
we can assume that
T
is one among

F_{4}(q),\>^{2}F_{4}(q),\>E_{6}(q),\>^{2}E_{6}(q),\>E_{7}(q),\>\mbox{ or }E_{8}(q).

We will use the following result from
[
LS90
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
LS90
, Th. 2]

Let
G
be an almost simple group with socle
T
an exceptional group of Lie type, and let
H<G
be a non-parabolic maximal subgroup.
Then one of the following holds:

(i)

H=N_{G}(D)
, where
D
is a reductive subgroup of
T
of maximal rank, the possibilities are given in
[
LSS90
, Tab. 5.1, 5.2]
;

(ii)

H
is the centralizer of a graph, field, or graph-field automorphism of prime order;

(iii)

The generalized Fitting subgroup
F^{*}(H)
is as in
[
LS90
, Tab. III]
;

(iv)

H=N_{G}(E)
where
E
is an elementary abelian group given in
[
CLSS92
, Th. 1(II)]
;

(v)

T\cap H
is one of
(2^{2}\times D_{4}(q)).\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(3)
,
A_{1}(q)\times\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(5)
, or
(\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(5)\times\mathop{\mathrm{Alt}}(6)).2^{2}
;

(vi)

H
is almost simple.

Let
\tilde{G}
be the algebraic group corresponding to
T
, and let
\sigma
be a Steinberg endomorphism such that
\tilde{G}_{\sigma}^{\prime}=T
. Note that
\tilde{G}_{\sigma}
is equal to
\operatorname{Inndiag}(T)
, the subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(T)
generated by
T
and the diagonal automorphisms of
T
.

If
H
is of type (i), and
D
is not a torus, the possibilities for
N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D)
are given in
[
LSS90
, Tab. 5.1]
. By Proposition
2.3
we have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma})+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H/H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma})
.
Now
H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}=N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D)\cap G
, so
N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D)^{(\infty)}\subseteq H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}
and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma})\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D))
by Lemma
2.1
.
Moreover,
H/H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}
is isomorphic to a subgroup of the solvable group
G\tilde{G}_{\sigma}/\tilde{G}_{\sigma}
.
Therefore

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D))+\mathrm{dl}(G/G\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}).

Using this inequality, we can prove that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 9
for all groups in
[
LSS90
, Tab. 5.1]
.
We remark on one of the more complex cases: suppose
T=E_{8}(q)
and
N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D)=d^{4}.(\L_{2}(q))^{8}.d^{4}.\mathop{\mathrm{AGL}}_{3}(2)
. If
q
is
2
or
3
, we can directly construct this group in Magma
[
BCP97
]
, and hence show that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D))\leq 1
. When
q\geq 4
instead, we can show that
d^{4}:\mathop{\mathrm{AGL}}_{3}(2)
is perfect and hence
N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(D)
is also perfect. The other cases are easy, with the largest bounds on
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)
usually occurring when
q
is
2
or
3
.

If
H
is of type (i) and
D
is a maximal torus, we similarly consider every case in
[
LSS90
, Tab. 5.2]
. In each case we can show that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(K)/K_{\sigma})\leq 4
, so by Corollary
2.4
we have that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(K_{\sigma})+4+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/(\tilde{G}_{\sigma}\cap G))\leq 7
.

If
H
is of type (ii), then there exists
q_{0}=q^{1/r}
, and
S
equal to one of
F_{4}(q_{0})
,
{}^{2}F_{4}(q_{0})
,
E_{6}(q_{0})
,
{}^{2}E_{6}(q_{0})
,
E_{7}(q_{0})
or
E_{8}(q_{0})
, such that
S\leqslant T\cap H\leqslant\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(T)
. Since
S
is always simple, we have
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap H)\leq 2
, and thus
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
.

Next, if
H
is of type (iii), then
F^{*}(H)
is the direct product of two or three non-abelian simple groups which are given in
[
LS90
, Tab. III]
.
Moreover,
H
is a subgroup of
\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(F^{*}(H))
containing
F^{*}(H)
,
and it is easy to see that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
.

If
H
is of type (iv) then
H\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}=N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(E)
for some elementary abelian group
E
, and
N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(E)
is described in
[
CLSS92
, Tab. 1]
. As such, we can find that

\displaystyle\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(C_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(E))+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(E)/C_{\tilde{G}_{\sigma}}(E))+\mathrm{dl}(G/(G\cap\tilde{G}_{\sigma}))

\displaystyle\leq 2+2+2=6.

If
H
is of type (v), then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap H)\leq 4
and so
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
.
Finally, if
H
is of type (vi) then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 3
by Lemma
2.8
.

Let us describe how we obtain these values.

We use the
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{T}\mathbb{L}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{S}

brauer.maths.qmul.ac.uk/Atlas

(see also the library
CTblLib (v1.3.9)
in
[
GAP4
]
),
and we combine this data with Corollary
2.4
and Remark
2.5
.
For instance, if
G=\mathbb{B}
and
H
is
[2^{30}].\L_{5}(2)
, then
\mathrm{dl}([2^{30}])\leq 5
and hence
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 5
.
More precisely, if
T
is not one of
\mathop{\mathrm{Fi}}_{24}^{\prime}
,
\mathbb{B}
or
\mathbb{M}
, then the maximal subgroups of
G
can be obtained by
[
GAP4
]
, and thus
\mathrm{dl^{*}Max}(G)
can be computed.
The equality in Theorem
1.1
is attained when
H=\texttt{AtlasSubgroup(``Fi23'',7)}
,
which is a solvable subgroup with shape
3^{1+8}.2^{1+6}.3^{1+2}.2S_{4}
.

If
T=\mathop{\mathrm{Fi}}_{24}^{\prime}
, the maximal subgroups of
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(T)
can be obtained from the
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{T}\mathbb{L}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{S}
. Thus, we can compute
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
in this case (and
4
is attained).
If a maximal subgroup of
T
is equal to the intersection of a maximal subgroup of
G
with
T
, then
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
by Lemma
2.1
.
Using the classification of the maximal subgroups of
\mathop{\mathrm{Fi}}_{24}^{\prime}
given in the
\mathbb{A}\mathbb{T}\mathbb{L}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{S}
,
it is easy to bound
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 4
in the remaining cases.

If

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 6
unless
H
is
H_{1}=3^{2}.3^{5}.3^{10}.(\mathrm{M_{11}}\times\GL_{2}(3))

H_{1}

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H_{1})=4
.
Finding the exact value of
\mathrm{dl^{*}Max}(\mathbb{M})

Finally, if

.

4.
Perfect groups

We now deal with primitive perfect groups.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
n\geq 5
and let
G<\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(n)
be a primitive perfect group,
with
H<G
being the stabilizer of a point.
Our goal is to prove that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 10
.

Of course, we can apply Theorem
1.1
when
G
is almost simple
(note that in this case
G
is actually simple).
Now let
1\neq N\unlhd G
, and observe that
NH=G
.
By Proposition
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)

\displaystyle\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H/N\cap H)+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N\cap H)

\displaystyle\>=\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(G/N)+\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N\cap H)\>.

Since
G
and so
G/N
is perfect, we obtain

(4.1)

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\>\leq\>\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N\cap H).

In particular,
H
is perfect if
G
has a regular normal subgroup (i.e. if
N\cap H=1
),
and Theorem
1.3
follows easily.
By the O’Nan-Scott theorem
[
DM96
, Sec. 4]
, we are left with the following cases:

\bullet

Product action.

There exists a primitive almost simple group
X
with socle
T
and stabilizer
Y
, and a transitive group
S\leqslant\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(d)
such that

T^{d}\unlhd G\leqslant X\wr_{d}S.

(Note that
G
acts on
|X:Y|^{d}
points).
Let
T^{d}\cong N\unlhd G
.
We have
H=Y^{d}S\cap G
,
and so
N\cap H=N\cap Y^{d}\cong(T\cap Y)^{d}
.
By (
4.1
) we have

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(N\cap H)=\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap Y).

As discussed we have
Y^{(3)}\subseteq T\cap Y
,
so by Lemma
2.1
we obtain

\mathrm{dl^{*}}(T\cap Y)\leq\mathrm{dl^{*}}(Y)\leq 10

as desired.

\bullet

Diagonal type.

Let
N\cong T^{k}
be the socle of
G
.
If
G
is of simple diagonal type, then
N\cap H
is isomorphic to
T
and embeds subdirectly in
T^{k}
.
If
G
is of compound diagonal type, then
k=\ell m
(
\ell,m\geq 2
) and
N\cap H\cong T^{\ell}
is a product of
\ell
diagonals of
T^{m}
.
In any case,
N\cap H
is perfect and we can use (
4.1
) to conclude that
H
is perfect.

The proof of
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)\leq 10
is complete.

We finally show that
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)=10
cannot happen in product action. By the discussion above, the only possibility would be with
X=T=\mathop{\mathrm{Fi}}_{23}
and
Y<X
the solvable maximal subgroup with
\mathrm{dl}(Y)=10
. Then
G=X\wr_{d}S
for some transitive perfect group
S<\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(d)
and
H=Y\wr_{d}S
. However, it can be seen that
H^{\prime}=[Y^{d},S]S
is perfect and
\mathrm{dl^{*}}(H)=1
in this case.
∎

To conclude, we make a comment on the constant in Conjecture
1.4
,
i.e. what can be achieved by stabilizing two points in an arbitrary primitive group.
For
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Fi}}_{23}
and the solvable maximal subgroup
H
with
\mathrm{dl}(H)=10
, we can find
g\in G
such that
\mathrm{dl}(H\cap H^{g})=2
.
However, if
G=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\POmega_{8}^{+}(3))
and
H
is the solvable maximal subgroup of index
36\,400
, then the corresponding primitive group has rank
5
and the derived lengths of stabilizers of two points are
[8,6,6,5,5]
. In particular,
\mathrm{dl}(H\cap H^{g})\geq 5
for all
g\in G
.
We can do better with groups of product type:

Example 4.1
.

Let
X=\mathop{\mathrm{Aut}}(\POmega_{8}^{+}(3))
and let
Y<X
be the solvable maximal subgroup discussed above.
Let
S=(\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(4)\wr_{4}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(4))\wr C_{2}
act on
32
points,
and let
G=X\wr_{32}S
.
Then
H=Y\wr_{32}S
is a solvable maximal subgroup of
G
.
For
g=(v,s)\in G
, since
Y^{d}S
is normalized by
s
, we have

H\cap H^{g}=Y^{d}S\cap(Y^{d}S)^{vs}=(Y^{d}S\cap(Y^{d}S)^{v})^{s},

and it follows that
\mathrm{dl}(H\cap H^{g})=dl(H\cap H^{v})
.
Now we argue as in
[
Sab26
, Sec. 3.2]
.
All set-stabilizers of
S
have derived length at least
3

[
Sab26
, Ex. 2.11]
, and there is no
4
-coloring with trivial stabilizer (because already
\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(4)\wr_{4}\mathop{\mathrm{Sym}}(4)
on
16
points has none).
These facts together imply that
\mathrm{dl}(H\cap H^{g})\geq 7
for all
g\in G
.

Moduli of sheaves on ribbons
Michele Savarese, Filippo Viviani

Abstract
. We study the geometry of the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves on ribbons. We introduce a stratification of the stack by the complete type of the sheaves, and we investigate the geometric properties of the strata and their closure relation, and which strata intersect the (semi)stable locus. Then we describe the irreducible components of the stack, by revealing an interesting trichotomy between Fano, Calabi-Yau and canonically polarized cases. Finally, we compute the tangent space of the moduli stack at a given sheaf.

14D20; 14H60

Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of moduli of coherent sheaves on ribbons. A
ribbon

X
over an algebraically closed field
k
is an irreducible and projective curve that is locally (but not necessarily globally) isomorphic to
2C\subset S
, where
C
is a smooth curve inside a smooth surface
S
(see
[
BE95
]
for a classification of ribbons).

Motivation

The motivation of this paper is to understand the geometry of the nilpotent cone of the stack of Higgs pairs, which we now briefly recall.

Let
C
be an irreducible smooth projective curve over
k=\overline{k}
and let
L
be a line bundle on
C
. Fix two integers
r\geq 1
and
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the stack
\mathcal{M}_{(C,L)}(r,d)
parametrizing
L
-twisted Higgs pairs
on
C
of rank
r
and degree
d
, i.e. pairs
(E,\phi)
consisting of a vector bundle of rank
r
and degree
d
on
C
together with a twisted endomorphism
\phi:E\to E\otimes L
(called the Higgs field). The
nilpotent cone
stack
\mathcal{N}_{(C,L)}(r,d)
is the closed substack parametrizing Higgs pairs
(E,\phi)
such that
\phi
is a nilpotent twisted endomorphism, and it plays a crucial role in studying the geometry of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{(C,L)}(r,d)
, see e.g.
[
Hau13
,
Hau23
]
for surveys. Via the spectral correspondence, the nilpotent cone stack
\mathcal{N}_{(C,L)}(r,d)
is isomorphic to the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{rC}(r,d)
of torsion-free sheaves of generalized rank
r
and generalized degree
d
on the primitive multiple curve
rC\subset\rm{Tot}_{C}(L)
, where
\rm{Tot}_{C}(L)\to C
is the total space of the line bundle
L
and
C
sits inside
\rm{Tot}_{C}(L)
as the zero section. Moreover, the isomorphism
\mathcal{N}_{(C,L)}(r,d)\cong\mathcal{M}_{rC}(r,d)
is compatible with the natural semistability conditions and hence induces an isomorphism of the corresponding moduli spaces. In the important special case where
L=\omega_{C}
, the geometry of the nilpotent cone
\mathcal{N}_{(C,\omega_{C})}(r,d)
has been investigated in
[
Lau88
,
Gin01
,
Boz22
,
HH22
]
, but very little seem to be known for an arbitrary line bundle
L
.

This paper is a first step towards understanding of the geometry of
\mathcal{M}_{rC}(r,d)
. Indeed, by looking at the scheme-theoretic support of the sheaves, we get an increasing sequence of closed substacks

\mathcal{M}_{C}(r,d)\subset\mathcal{M}_{2C}(r,d)\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{M}_{rC}(r,d).

While the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{C}(r,d)
parametrizing vector bundles of rank
r
and degree
d
on the smooth curve
C
is classically studied, the goal of this paper is to investigate in full generality the geometry of the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{2C}(r,d)
and, more generally, the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves on arbitrary ribbons. This is the first steps towards the study of the full moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{rC}(r,d)
, which will be the subject of our future work
[
SV
]
, and it already reveals the full richness of the geometry of these moduli stacks/spaces, although it is notationally easier to deal with respect to the higher multiplicity case.

Previous results

There are in the literature some partial results on the moduli of some special types of sheaves on ribbons, which we now review.

First of all, generalized line bundles (i.e. torsion-free sheaves which are generically invertible), has been introduced by Bayer-Eisenbud in
[
BE95
]
and then studied by Eisenbud-Green in
[
EG95
]
in order to deal with the Green conjecture about the Clifford index of smooth curves.

The moduli space of semistable torsion-free sheaves of generalized rank
2
,
which are either generalized line bundles or direct images of rank
2
vector bundles on the underlying reduced curve, has been studied for some special ribbons (namely those whose normal bundle is the canonical line bundle of the reduced curve) by Donagi-Ein-Lazarsfeld in
[
DEL97
, §3]
and for arbitrary ribbons by Yang
[
Yan03
]
and Chen-Kass
[
CK16
]
, with the addendum of
[
Sav19
]
who solved a question left open in loc. cit.

The study of arbitrary coherent sheaves on ribbons (or more generally on primitive multiple curves of arbitrary multiplicity) has been undertaken systematically by Drézet in the papers
[
Dre06
]
,
[
Dre08
]
,
[
Dre09
]
,
[
Dre11
]
. In the same papers, Drézet studied the geometry (i.e. number of irreducible components, their dimension, computation of the Zariski tangent space) of the open subset of the moduli space of semistable torsion-free sheaves consisting of quasi-locally free sheaves of rigid type (see
1.5
). In the follow-up papers
[
Dre17
]
and
[
Dre20
]
, Drézet investigated when the above mentioned components can be obtained as limits of sheaves under a partial smoothing of the ribbon to a nodal curves with two irreducible components.
The local structure of the open subset of the moduli space of semistable torsion-free sheaves consisting of generalized vector bundles (i.e. torsion-free sheaves which are locally free away from a finite set of points) has been studied by Inaba
[
Ina04
]
.

Finally, Yuan proved in
[
Yua23
]
an upper bound for the dimension of the moduli stack of torsion-free sheaves of generalized rank
n
on a primitive multiple curve of multiplicity
n
.

Our results

Let
X
be a ribbon over an algebraically closed field
k=\overline{k}
. The underlying reduced curve
X_{\mathrm{red}}
is an irreducible, projective smooth curve over
k
whose genus will be denoted by
\overline{g}
. The ideal sheaf of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
, called the nilradical and denoted by
{\mathscr{N}}
, is (the pushforward of) a line bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
. The dual line bundle
{\mathscr{N}}^{*}
is the normal bundle of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
and we set
\delta:=\deg{\mathscr{N}}^{*}
.

Any coherent sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
comes with an exact sequence (called the first canonical filtration, see Definition
1.2
)

0\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to 0,

where
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}
are both coherent sheaves on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.
We define the
complete type
of
{\mathscr{F}}
by

(r_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}});d_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}}))=(r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),r_{1}({\mathscr{F}});d_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})):=(\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}),\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}});\deg({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}),\deg({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}})).

The complete type allows to define two numerical (i.e. invariant by deformations) invariants (see
1.3
): the
generalized rank

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}}):=r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})+r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
, which is also equal to the length of its generic stalk
{\mathscr{F}}_{\eta}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}
-module (where
\eta
denotes the generic point of
X
), and the
generalized degree

\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}}):=d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})+d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
.

For any
(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
the stack of torsion-free (i.e. pure of one-dimensional support) sheaves on a ribbon
X
of generalized rank
R
and generalized degree
D
. We can stratify
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
according to the complete type of the sheaves

\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\coprod_{(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),

where each stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
parametrizes torsion-free sheaves on
X
of complete type
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
and

\mathcal{S}(R,D):=\{(r_{0},r_{1};d_{0},d_{1})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}\>:r_{0}+r_{1}=R\text{ and }d_{0}+d_{1}=D\}.

Our first main result describes the properties of this stratification.

Theorem A
.

(see Theorems
2.5
,
2.10
,
2.13
)
Let
X
be a ribbon and set
\delta:=-\deg{\mathscr{N}}
and
\overline{g}
be the genus of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
. Fix
(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

For any
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
, we have that

\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{aligned} &r_{0}\geq r_{1},\\
&r_{1}=0\Rightarrow d_{1}=0,\\
&r_{0}=r_{1}\Rightarrow d_{0}\geq d_{1}+r_{1}\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

We say that a sequence
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
is admissible if it is satisfies the above conditions and we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
the subset of admissible elements of
\mathcal{S}(R,D)
.

(2)

For any
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
, the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is a locally closed substack which, endowed with its reduced stack structure, is irreducible and smooth of dimension equal to

\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})=(r_{0}^{2}+r_{1}^{2})(\overline{g}-1)+r_{0}r_{1}\delta=R^{2}(\overline{g}-1)+r_{0}r_{1}[\delta-2(\overline{g}-1)].

(3)

Assume that
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset\Longleftrightarrow(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet}),\text{ i.e. either }r_{1}>\widetilde{r}_{1}\>\text{ or } r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}\>\text{ and } \>d_{1}\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}.

Our second main result is the description of the irreducible components of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
. There are three cases depending on the comparison between the degree of the normal sheaf
\delta:=-\deg({\mathscr{N}})
and the canonical degree
2\overline{g}-2
of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

Theorem B
.

(see Proposition
3.1
, Corollary
3.4
)
Notation as in Theorem A.

(1)

If
\delta<2\overline{g}-2
(
canonically polarized
case) then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
are

\{\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\}_{(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)},

and we have that

(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\Rightarrow\dim\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\leq\dim\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})}.

In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
has dimension
R^{2}(\overline{g}-1)
and the unique irreducible component of maximal dimension is
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,0;D,0)
, which parametrizes vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
R
and degree
D
.

(2)

If
\delta=2\overline{g}-2
(
Calabi-Yau
case) then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
are

\{\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\}_{(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)},

and they have all dimension equal to
R^{2}(\overline{g}-1)
.

(3)

If
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
(
Fano
case) then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
are

\{\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\}_{(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{rig}(R,D)},

where the subset
\mathcal{S}_{rig}(R,D)\subset\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
of rigid sequences is defined by

(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{rig}(R,D)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\begin{cases}r_{0}=r_{1}=\frac{R}{2}&\text{ if }R\>\text{ is even,}\\
r_{0}=r_{1}+1=\frac{R+1}{2}&\text{ if }R\>\text{ is odd.}\\
\end{cases}

In particular,
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
has pure dimension equal to

\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\begin{cases}\frac{R^{2}}{4}(2\overline{g}-2+\delta)&\text{ if }R\text{ is even,}\\
\frac{R^{2}+1}{4}(2\overline{g}-2)+\frac{R^{2}-1}{4}\delta&\text{ if }R\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

The above Theorem B answers completely the open question of
[
Dre09
, Sec. 1.3.1]
, and it generalizes it from the semistable locus to the entire stack of torsion-free sheaves.

The terminology of the three cases of Theorem B is due to the following fact: if
X
embeds into an irreducible smooth projective
S
(so that
X=2X_{\mathrm{red}}\subset S
) and
S
is canonically polarized (resp. Calabi-Yau, resp. Fano) then the adjunction formula
K_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}=(K_{S}+X_{\mathrm{red}})_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}
gives that
\delta<2\overline{g}-2
(resp.
\delta=2\overline{g}-2
, resp.
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
).

For the special ribbon
X:=2X_{\mathrm{red}}\subset\operatorname{Tot}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(\omega_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
(for which the normal bundle is
\omega_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
and hence
\delta=2\overline{g}-2
), the result of the above Theorem B(2) can be deduced from
[
Lau88
,
Boz22
]
.

We now determine which strata of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
intersect the open substacks
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}
and
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{s}
of, respectively, semistable and stable sheaves. Recall that, by defining the slope of a torsion-free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
to be

\mu({\mathscr{F}}):=\frac{\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})}{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})},

a torsion-free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
is said to be semistable (resp. stable) if for any proper subsheaf
0\neq{\mathscr{G}}\subsetneq{\mathscr{F}}
we have that

\mu({\mathscr{G}})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})\>\text{ (resp. }\mu({\mathscr{G}})<\mu({\mathscr{F}})).

As we recall in Fact
4.4
, the locus
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}
of semistable sheaves on
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
is an open substack of finite type over
k
and its admits a projective adequate (or good if
\rm{char}(k)=0
) moduli space
\Phi^{ss}:\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}\to M_{X}(R,D)^{ss}
, that identifies semistable sheaves that have the same Jordan-Holder graduation. The restriction of
\Phi^{ss}
to the open substack
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{s}
of stable sheaves is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.

Theorem C
.

(see Theorem
4.5
)
Let
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
.

(1)

If the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
intersects
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}
then one the following occurs:

(a)

r_{1}=0
;

(b)

r_{0}>r_{1}>0
and
\displaystyle\frac{d_{0}-(r_{0}+r_{1})\delta}{r_{0}}\leq\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}\leq\frac{d_{0}}{r_{0}}
;

(c)

r_{0}=r_{1}
and
d_{0}\leq d_{1}+2r_{0}\delta
.

If
\overline{g}\geq 1
then the above numerical conditions are also sufficient for the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}
.

(2)

If the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
intersects
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{s}
then one the following occurs:

(a)

r_{1}=0
;

(b)

r_{0}>r_{1}>0
and
\displaystyle\frac{d_{0}-(r_{0}+r_{1})\delta}{r_{0}}<\frac{d_{1}}{r_{1}}<\frac{d_{0}}{r_{0}}
;

(c)

r_{0}=r_{1}
and
d_{0}<d_{1}+2r_{0}\delta
.

If
\overline{g}\geq 2
then the above numerical conditions are also sufficient for the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{s}
.

The above Theorem C answers completely the open question of
[
Dre09
, Sec. 1.3.2]
.

As an example, we study in subsection
4.1
the semistability of torsion-free sheaves of type
(n,1)
: we show that these sheaves can be obtained as pushforward of quasi-locally free sheaves of the same type on some blowups of the ribbon (see Proposition
4.11
) and we prove a refined criterion for (semi)stability (see Proposition
4.12
).

Our final result is the computation of the dimension of the tangent space
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
at a sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
. Recall that the tangent space
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
parametrizes infinitesimal deformations of
{\mathscr{F}}
and it is therefore equal to
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
. The tangent space
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
admits two notable subspaces

(0.1)

H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}),

where
H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))
parametrizes infinitesimal deformations of
{\mathscr{F}}
that are locally trivial and
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is the tangent space of
{\mathscr{F}}
at the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
.
Since the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is smooth by Theorem A, the dimension of its tangent space at
{\mathscr{F}}
is equal to

(0.2)

\dim T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})=\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})+\dim\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),

where
\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is the infinitesimal automorphism space of
{\mathscr{F}}
which is equal to
\operatorname{Ext}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
and
\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is computed in Theorem A.
Therefore, we can regard the dimension of the space
\dim T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
as ”known” and we determine the codimension of the two inclusions in (
0.1
).

The answer depends on the local structure of
{\mathscr{F}}
, as we now describe. Fact
1.12
implies that the stalk of
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
at a point
p
is isomorphic to

{\mathscr{F}}_{p}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus(r_{0}-r_{1})}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r_{1}}\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p},

for some uniquely determined sequence of natural numbers
n_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}},p):=\{n_{1}=n_{1}({\mathscr{F}},p)\geq\ldots\geq n_{r_{1}}=n_{r_{1}}({\mathscr{F}},p)\}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p}
is the ideal sheaf of the
0
-dimensional subscheme
n_{i}p\subset X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
. The sequence
n_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}},p)
is different from the zero sequence only at the finitely many points of the support of the torsion subsheaf
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})
of the restriction
{\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}
and the sum (called the index of
{\mathscr{F}}
)

\iota({\mathscr{F}})=\sum_{p\in X}\sum_{i=1}^{r_{1}}n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p)

is equal to the length of
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})
.

Theorem D
.

(see Corollaries
5.5
,
5.9
,
5.10
,
5.11
)
Let
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subset\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
and set

\gamma({\mathscr{F}})=(r_{0}-r_{1})\iota({\mathscr{F}})+\sum_{p\in X}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})}(2i-1)n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p).

(1)

The inclusion

H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}}))\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})

has codimension equal to

\gamma({\mathscr{F}}):=(r_{0}-r_{1})\iota({\mathscr{F}})+\sum_{p\in X}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})}(2i-1)n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p).

(2)

The inclusion
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
has codimension equal to

h^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}\left(\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}\right)^{**}\right)\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1})+\gamma({\mathscr{F}}),

where
()^{**}
denoted the reflexive hull.

In particular:

•

If
\iota({\mathscr{F}})=0
(in which case
{\mathscr{F}}
is called quasi-locally free), then

H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}}))=T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)

and the last inclusion has codimension equal to

h^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}\left(\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}\right)^{**}\right)\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}).

This is the case for the general element
{\mathscr{F}}
of any stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
with
r_{0}>r_{1}
.

•

If
r_{0}=r_{1}=:r
(in which case
{\mathscr{F}}
is called a generalized vector bundle of rank
r
), then the two inclusions in (
0.1
) have the same codimension equal to

\gamma({\mathscr{F}})=\sum_{p\in X}\sum_{1\leq i\leq r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})}(2i-1)n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p)\geq\iota({\mathscr{F}}).

Furthermore, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a general element of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r,r;d_{\bullet})
then
\gamma({\mathscr{F}})=\iota({\mathscr{F}})
.

Structure of the paper

1

[
Dre06
]
,

5.2
and the higher Ext sheaves
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{k}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
for
k\geq 1
in Theorem
5.6
.

1.
Properties of sheaves on ribbons

This section collects from literature (mostly from the paper of Drézet
[
Dre06
]
,
[
Dre09
]
,
[
Dre11
]
) the properties of coherent sheaves on ribbons that we will need in the article. In this paper only coherent sheaves are considered, hence this attribute will be usually omitted in the following. Moreover, vector bundle will be used as a synonym of locally free sheaf of finite rank.

1.1.
Ribbons

Let us begin by recalling the definition and the numerical invariants of a ribbon. We will be working over a fixed algebraically closed field
k
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
ribbon

X
(over
k
) is a non-reduced projective
k
-scheme whose reduced subscheme
X_{\mathrm{red}}
is a smooth connected
k
-curve and whose nilradical
{\mathscr{N}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
, i.e. the ideal sheaf of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
, is locally generated by a single non-zero square-zero element.

In particular, this definition implies immediately that
{\mathscr{N}}
can be seen as a line bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
and that it coincides with the conormal sheaf of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
. Its dual line bundle
{\mathscr{N}}^{*}
is the normal sheaf of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
. We will set

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\delta:=-\deg{\mathscr{N}},\\
&\overline{g}:=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})=\text{the genus of }X_{\mathrm{red}},\\
&g:=1-\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X})=\text{the (arithmetic) genus of }X.\end{aligned}\right.

From the exact sequence

0\to{\mathscr{N}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to 0,

we deduce that

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=1+h^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},{\mathscr{N}}),\\
&h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=\overline{g}+h^{1}(X_{\mathrm{red}},{\mathscr{N}}),\\
&g=2\overline{g}-1+\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

1.2.
Canonical filtrations

A (coherent) sheaf on a ribbon
X
has two canonical filtrations, that we now recall.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
.

(i)

The
first canonical filtration
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is

0\subseteq{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}.

It is immediate to check that
{\mathscr{F}}/({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}})={\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
.

We define

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &r_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}}):=(r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})):=(\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}),\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}))=\text{\emph{complete rank} of }{\mathscr{F}},\\
&d_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}}):=(d_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})):=(\deg({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}),\deg({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}))=\text{\emph{complete degree} of }{\mathscr{F}},\\
&(r_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}});d_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}})):=(r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),r_{1}({\mathscr{F}});d_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),d_{1}({\mathscr{F}}))=\text{\emph{complete type} of }{\mathscr{F}}.\end{aligned}\right.

(ii)

The
second canonical filtration
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is defined as

0\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\subseteq{\mathscr{F}},

where
{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
is the subsheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}
annihilated by
{\mathscr{N}}
.

The complete type of
{\mathscr{F}}
can be determined by looking at the structure of the generic stalk
{\mathscr{F}}_{\eta}
, where
\eta
is the generic point of
X
.

Remark 1.3
.

If
\eta
is the generic point of
X
, then we have that

{\mathscr{F}}_{\eta}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},\eta}^{\oplus a({\mathscr{F}})}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}^{\oplus b({\mathscr{F}})},

for some unique non-negative integers
(a({\mathscr{F}}),b({\mathscr{F}}))
, called the
type
of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
The complete rank of
{\mathscr{F}}
is determined by the type of
{\mathscr{F}}
(and vice-versa) by virtue of the following formulas:

r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=a({\mathscr{F}})+b({\mathscr{F}})\quad\text{ and } \quad r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})=b({\mathscr{F}}).

The following fact collects the main properties of the two canonical filtrations and their mutual relations.

Fact 1.4
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
.

(i)

(see
[
Dre09
, §3.1.4]
,
[
Dre17
, §3.2.3]
) There is a canonical isomorphism

{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}=({\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})\otimes{\mathscr{N}}.

Moreover there is a canonical exact sequence on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
(called the
canonical exact sequence of

{\mathscr{F}}
):

(1.4)

0\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\to{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}\to 0.

(ii)

(see
[
Dre17
, §3.2.4]
)
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subsheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}
. Then:

(a)

\mathcal{F}
is defined on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
if and only if
\mathcal{F}\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
;

(b)

{\mathscr{F}}/\mathcal{F}
is defined on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
if and only if
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
.

Moreover, in the latter case, there is a canonical morphism
{\mathscr{F}}/\mathcal{F}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to\mathcal{F}
, which is surjective if and only if
\mathcal{F}={\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
, while it is injective if and only if
\mathcal{F}={\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
.

(iii)

(see
[
Dre09
, §3.4.2, §3.4.3]
,
[
Dre17
, §3.4]
) Let
\mathcal{F}
be a sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on it. Then there exists the following canonical exact sequence:

(1.5)

\phantom{00000.}0\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})\overset{\pi}{\longrightarrow}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathcal{E}\otimes{\mathscr{N}},\mathcal{F})\to 0.

By
(ii)
, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a sheaf on
X
sitting in a short exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{F}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to\mathcal{E}\to 0
represented by
\sigma\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
, then
\mathcal{F}={\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
if and only if
\pi(\sigma)
is surjective, while
\mathcal{F}={\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
if and only if
\pi(\sigma)
is injective.

(iv)

(see
[
Dre09
, Prop. 3.5]
) Let
\varphi:{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{G}}
be a morphism of sheaves on
X
. Then:

(a)

\varphi
is surjective if and only if the induced morphism
\varphi|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}:{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to{\mathscr{G}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
is surjective. If this is case, the induced morphism
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{G}}
is also surjective.

(b)

\varphi
is injective if and only if the induced morphism
{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\to{\mathscr{G}}^{(1)}
is injective. If this is the case, the induced morphism
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\to{\mathscr{G}}/{\mathscr{G}}^{(1)}
is also injective.

(v)

(see
[
Dre06
, Prop. 7.3.1]
)
r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})
is upper semicontinuous, while
r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
is lower semicontinuous.

Remark 1.5
.

Thanks to Fact
1.4
(i)
, the complete type of a sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
can be characterized also in terms of the second canonical filtration of
{\mathscr{F}}
:

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &(r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),r_{1}({\mathscr{F}}))=(\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}),\operatorname{rk}({\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})),\\
&(d_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),d_{1}({\mathscr{F}}))=(\deg({\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})+r_{1}\delta,\deg({\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})-r_{1}\delta).\end{aligned}\right.

1.3.
Generalized rank and degree

Now we introduce two fundamental invariants of a (coherent) sheaf on a ribbon
X
: the generalized rank and the generalized degree.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
.

(i)

The
generalized rank

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is its generic length, i.e. the length of its generic stalk
{\mathscr{F}}_{\eta}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta}
-module (here and throughout the paper
\eta
denotes the generic point of
X
).

(ii)

The
generalized degree

\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is

\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})=d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})+d_{1}({\mathscr{F}}).

Remark 1.7
.

(i)

If
\mathcal{F}
is defined on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, meaning that it is the direct image on
X
of a sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, then

\operatorname{R}(\mathcal{F})=\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})\quad\text{ and } \quad\operatorname{Deg}(\mathcal{F})=\deg(\mathcal{F}),

where
\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})
and
\deg(\mathcal{F})
are the usual rank and degree of a sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

(ii)

If we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to\mathcal{E}\to 0,

with
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}
sheaves on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, then it holds that

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})+\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{E})\quad\text{ and } \quad\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})=\deg(\mathcal{F})+\deg(\mathcal{E}).

In particular, the generalized rank of a sheaf is determined by its complete rank:

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})=r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})+r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})=a({\mathscr{F}})+2b({\mathscr{F}}).

The following fact collects the basic properties of the generalized rank and degree.

Fact 1.8
.

(i)

(see
[
Dre06
, §4.2.2]
)
Let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
be a very ample line bundle on
X
, let
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(1)
be its restriction to
X_{\mathrm{red}}
and let
d=\deg{\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(1)}
. If
{\mathscr{F}}
is a sheaf on
X
, its Hilbert polynomial with respect of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
is

(1.7)

P_{{\mathscr{F}}}(T)=\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})+\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})(1-\overline{g})+\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})dT.

(ii)

(see
[
Dre06
, Cor. 4.3.2]
) The generalized rank and degree are additive: if

0\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime\prime}\to 0

is an exact sequence of sheaves on
X
, then

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})+\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime\prime})\quad\text{ and }\quad\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})+\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime\prime}).

(iii)

(see
[
Dre06
, Prop. 4.3.3]
)
The generalized rank and degree of sheaves on
X
are invariant by deformation.

(iv)

(see
[
Dre06
, Thm. 4.2.1]
)
The following Riemann-Roch formula holds

\chi({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})+\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})(1-\overline{g}).

1.4.
Purity and duality

We now introduce the notions of pure, torsion-free and reflexive sheaves.

Definition 1.9
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
.

(i)

{\mathscr{F}}
is said to be
pure
of dimension
d(=0\text{ or }1)
if the dimension of any non-zero subsheaf (i.e. the dimension of its support) is equal to
d
.

(ii)

{\mathscr{F}}
is said to be
torsion-free
if every non-zerodivisor of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
is a non-zerodivisor also of
{\mathscr{F}}(U)
for any open
U\subseteq X
(recall that
f\in H^{0}(U,\mathcal{O}_{X})
is a non-zerodivisor of
{\mathscr{F}}
if the multiplication map
f\cdot\_:{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}\to{\mathscr{F}}|_{U}
is injective).

(iii)

{\mathscr{F}}
is
reflexive
if the canonical morphism
{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee\vee}
is an isomorphism, where
{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}({\mathscr{F}},\mathcal{O}_{X})
is the
dual
of
{\mathscr{F}}
.

Note that
{\mathscr{F}}
is pure of dimension
0
if and only if it is a torsion sheaf, i.e. it has finite support.

Remark 1.10
.

If
\mathcal{F}
is a sheaf on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, then its dual
\mathcal{F}^{\vee}
on
X
is related to its dual
\mathcal{F}^{*}:=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
by a canonical isomorphism (see
[
Dre09
, Lemme 4.1]
):

\mathcal{F}^{\vee}\simeq\mathcal{F}^{*}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}.

Hence,
\mathcal{F}
is reflexive on
X
if and only if it is reflexive on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, i.e. it is a vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

The relationship between the above notions is described in the following fact.

Fact 1.11
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
. Then:

(i)

(see
[
Dre09
, Cor. 4.6]
) It holds that
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{i}({\mathscr{F}},\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for any
i\geq 2
.

(ii)

(see
[
CK16
, Lemma 2.2]
and
[
Dre09
, Prop. 3.8 and Thm. 4.4]
) The following are equivalent:

(a)

{\mathscr{F}}
is pure of dimension one;

(b)

{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion-free;

(c)

{\mathscr{F}}
is reflexive;

(d)

{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
is a vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
;

(e)

it holds that
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
.

Moreover, if the above conditions hold, then
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
and
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
are vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

(iii)

(see
[
Dre11
, Prop. 4.4.1]
) We have that

\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})\quad\text{ and } \quad\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})=-\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})+\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})\deg{{\mathscr{N}}}+h^{0}(\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}})),

where
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}})
is the torsion subsheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}
, i.e. its greatest subsheaf with finite support.

(iv)

(see
[
Dre09
, Cor. 4.5]
) If
0\to{\mathscr{E}}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{G}}\to 0
be a short exact sequence of sheaves on
X
with
{\mathscr{G}}
torsion-free, then also the dual sequence
0\to{\mathscr{G}}^{\vee}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}\to{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}\to 0
is exact.

(v)

Assume that
{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion-free.

(a)

(see
[
Dre11
, Prop. 4.3.1(i)]
) There is a canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})\cong\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{1}(\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}),\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes{\mathscr{N}},

where
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
and
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
are, respectively, the torsion subsheaves of
{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
.

(b)

(see
[
Dre09
, Prop. 4.2]
and
[
Dre11
, Prop. 4.3.1(ii)]
)
The dual of the exact sequence

0\to{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\to{\mathscr{F}}\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}\to 0

is equal to

0\to({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})^{\rm sat}\to{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}\to({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}\to 0

where
({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})^{\rm sat}
is the saturation of
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
in
{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
.

(vi)

(see
[
Dre09
, Thm. 4.7]
) Assume that
{\mathscr{F}}
is torsion-free. Then there are functorial Serre-duality isomorphisms

H^{i}(X,{\mathscr{F}})\cong H^{1-i}(X,{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}\otimes\omega_{X})\quad\text{ for } i=0,1.

1.5.
Quasi-locally free sheaves and sheaves of rigid type

In this subsection, we introduce two special classes of torsion-free sheaves, namely quasi-locally free sheaves and sheaves of rigid type.

We begin by a structure result for the stalks of a torsion-free sheaf on
X
.

Fact 1.12
.

(see
[
Dre06
, Cor. 6.5.5]
)
Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion free sheaf of type
(a,b)=(a({\mathscr{F}}),b({\mathscr{F}}))
. Then for any closed point
p\in X
there exists a unique sequence of natural numbers
n_{\bullet}({\mathscr{F}},p):=\{n_{1}=n_{1}({\mathscr{F}},p)\geq\ldots\geq n_{b}=n_{b}({\mathscr{F}},p)\}
such that

{\mathscr{F}}_{p}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus a}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{b}\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p},

where
\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p}
is the ideal sheaf of the
0
-dimensional subscheme
n_{i}p\subset X_{\mathrm{red}}
in
X
.

This follows from
[
Dre06
, Cor. 6.5.5]
together with the fact that
\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p}
has type
(1,0)
.

Definition 1.13
.

(see
[
Dre06
, §5.1]
.)
Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
of type
(a,b)=(a({\mathscr{F}}),b({\mathscr{F}}))
. We say that:

(i)

{\mathscr{F}}
is
quasi-locally free in a closed point

p\in X
if

{\mathscr{F}}_{p}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus a}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X,p}^{\oplus b},

i.e. if
n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p)=0
for any
1\leq i\leq b
.

(ii)

{\mathscr{F}}
is
quasi-locally free
if it is quasi-locally free in any closed point of
X
.

The following fact contains the main properties of quasi-locally free sheaves.

Fact 1.14
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a sheaf on
X
.

(i)

(see
[
Dre09
, Thm. 3.9 and Cor. 3.10]
)
Let
p
be a closed point of
X
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(a)

{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free (resp. quasi-locally free in
p
);

(b)

{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
are locally free on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
(resp. are locally free in
p
);

(c)

{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}/{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
are locally free on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
(resp. are locally free in
p
);

(d)

{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
are torsion-free (resp. are torsion-free in
p
).

(ii)

(see
[
Dre06
, §6.5.2]
) If
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free, then the dual of the canonical sequence of
{\mathscr{F}}
is the canonical sequence of
{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
.
In particular, we have that

({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}})^{\vee}={\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1},\quad({\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})^{\vee}=({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}},\quad({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee}=({\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})^{(1)}.

(iii)

(see
[
Dre06
, Thm. 5.1.6]
)
{\mathscr{F}}
is generically quasi-locally free, i.e. there exists an open
\emptyset\neq U\subseteq X
such that
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free in each point of
U
.

(iv)

(see
[
Dre09
, §5.1]
) If
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free, then there exists a vector bundle
\mathbb{E}
on
X
and a surjective morphism

\phi:\mathbb{E}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}

such that
\phi_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}:\mathbb{E}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to{\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}
is an isomorphism. In particular,
\mathbb{E}
has generalized rank and degree equal to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\operatorname{R}(\mathbb{E})=2r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),\\
&\operatorname{Deg}(\mathbb{E})=2d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})-\delta r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),\end{aligned}\right.

and
\operatorname{ker}(\phi)
is a vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank and degree equal to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\operatorname{rk}(\operatorname{ker}(\phi))=r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})-r_{1}({\mathscr{F}}),\\
&\deg(\operatorname{ker}(\phi))=d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})-d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})-\delta r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}).\end{aligned}\right.

The failure of a torsion-free sheaf to be quasi-locally free is measured by the local and global index.

Definition 1.15
.

(see
[
Dre06
, §6.3.7]
,
[
CK16
, Def. 2.7]
)
Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
and let
\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
be the torsion subsheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
.

(i)

For any closed point
p\in X
, the
local index
of
{\mathscr{F}}
at
p
is

\iota_{p}({\mathscr{F}}):=\text{length of }\mathcal{T}_{p}\text{ as an }\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}-\text{module}.

(ii)

The
index
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is

\iota({\mathscr{F}}):=h^{0}(\mathcal{T})=\sum_{p\in X}\iota_{p}({\mathscr{F}}).

Remark 1.16
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf. For any closed point
p\in X
, we have that

\iota_{p}({\mathscr{F}})=\sum_{i=1}^{b({\mathscr{F}})}n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p).

This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{T}((\mathcal{I}_{n_{i}p})_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{p}^{n_{i}}=\mathcal{O}_{n_{i}p}
(see
[
Dre06
, Exa. 6.3.10]
).
In particular,
\iota({\mathscr{F}})=0
if and only if
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free.

The following fact relates torsion-free sheaves with positive index to quasi-locally free ones.

Fact 1.17
.

(see
[
Dre06
, §6.3, §6.4]
)
Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
and let
\mathcal{T}
be the torsion subsheaf of
{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
.
There exist two quasi-locally free sheaves
{\mathscr{E}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
on
X
(not necessarily unique)
and two exact sequences:

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\mathscr{E}}\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathscr{G}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow 0,

such that

{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{E}}={\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}^{(1)}={\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
.

Now we introduce sheaves of rigid type.

Definition 1.18
.

A torsion-free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
is said to be
of rigid type
if:

(i)

either
\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})
is even and
(a({\mathscr{F}}),b({\mathscr{F}}))=\left(0,\frac{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})}{2}\right)
(or equivalently
r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})=\frac{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})}{2}
);

(ii)

or
\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})
is odd and
(a({\mathscr{F}}),b({\mathscr{F}}))=\left(1,\frac{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})-1}{2}\right)
(or equivalently
r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=\frac{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})+1}{2}>\frac{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})-1}{2}=r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
).

Torsion-free sheaves of rigid type and even generalized rank are called
generalized vector bundles
. The rank of a generalized vector bundle
{\mathscr{F}}
is the number
r_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
.

Remark 1.19
.

(i)

The condition of being of rigid type is open in families by Fact
1.4
(v)
.

(ii)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a generalized vector bundle of index zero (or equivalently quasi-locally free) if and only if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a vector bundle (see Fact
1.14
(iv)
).

2.
The stack of torsion-free sheaves

The aim of this section is to study the stack of torsion-free sheaves on a ribbon
X
, which we now introduce.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a ribbon.

(i)

We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{X}
the
stack of torsion-free sheaves
on
X
, i.e. the (algebraic) stack whose fiber over a
k
-scheme
S
is the groupoid of
coherent sheaves
\mathfrak{F}
on
X\times_{k}S
that are flat over
S
and whose restriction to the geometric fibers of
X\times_{k}S\to S
are torsion-free sheaves on
X
.

(ii)

Since the generalized degree and degree are invariant under deformations (see Fact
1.8
(iii)
), we have disjoint decompositions into open and closed substacks

(2.1)

\mathcal{M}_{X}=\coprod_{R\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R)=\coprod_{(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D),

where
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
) the stack parametrizing torsion-free sheaves on
X
of generalized rank
R
(resp. and generalized degree
D
).

We now want to define a stratification of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
according to the complete type of the sheaves. For that purpose, we introduce the following

Definition 2.2
.

Fix a pair
(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
.

(i)

A
sequence of type

(R,D)
is a sequence
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})=(r_{0},r_{1};d_{0},d_{1})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
r_{0}+r_{1}=R
and
d_{0}+d_{1}=D
.
The collection of all sequences of type
(R,D)
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}(R,D)
.

(ii)

A sequence
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
is called
admissible
if it satisfies the following conditions

\left\{\begin{aligned} &r_{0}\geq r_{1},\\
&r_{1}=0\Rightarrow d_{1}=0,\\
&r_{0}=r_{1}\Rightarrow d_{0}\geq d_{1}+r_{1}\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

The collection of all admissible sequences of type
(R,D)
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
.

(iii)

Given
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
, we say that

(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle def}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}\text{either }r_{1}>\widetilde{r}_{1}\>\text{ or } r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}\>\text{ and } \>d_{1}\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}.

(iv)

A sequence
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
is called
of rigid type
if either
r_{0}=r_{1}
(which forces
R
even) or
r_{0}=r_{1}+1
(which forces
R
odd).
The collection of all rigid sequences of type
(R,D)
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{rig}(R,D)
.

We can now introduce the desired stratification of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
by complete type.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a sequence
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
, we define the following locus

\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}):=\{\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)\>:\>(r_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F});d_{\bullet}(\mathcal{F}))=(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D).

Remark 2.4
.

(i)

The strata
\mathcal{M}_{X}((r_{0},0);d_{\bullet}))
parametrize torsion-free sheaves (and hence locally free) on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
r_{0}
. Hence it is non-empty if and only if
d_{\bullet}=(d_{0},d_{1})=(d_{0},0)
in which case
d_{0}
is equal to the degree of the sheaves on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

(ii)

The strata
\mathcal{M}_{X}((r,r);d_{\bullet}))
parametrize generalized vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
r
.

(iii)

The stratum
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
parametrizes rigid torsion-free sheaves on
X
if and only if
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
is of rigid type.

In the next subsections, we investigate the properties of the stratification of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
by complete types.

2.1.
Non-emptiness

In this subsection, we determine which strata are non-empty.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
X
be a ribbon and fix any
(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
. For any
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}(R,D)
, we have that

(2.2)

\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset\Longleftrightarrow(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D).

Proof.

First of all, let us prove that if
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset
then
(r_{\bullet},d_{\bullet})
is admissible. Indeed, the fact that
r_{0}\geq r_{1}
follows from Remark
1.3
, while the implication
r_{1}=0\Rightarrow d_{1}=0
follows from Remark
2.4
(i)
.
Finally, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a torsion-free sheaf of complete rank
(r_{0},r_{0})
, then
{\mathscr{F}}
is a generalized vector bundle of rank
r_{0}
, and the inequality
d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})\geq d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})+r\delta
follows from Lemma
2.6
.

Assume now that
(r_{\bullet},d_{\bullet})
is admissible and let us show that
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset
. If
r_{1}=0
, then the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{0},0;d_{0},0)
follows from
Remark
2.4
(i)
. If
r_{0}>r_{1}>0
then the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
follows from Proposition
2.8
applied to an exact sequence (
2.5
)
of vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
such that

\deg(\mathcal{F})=d_{1},\deg(\mathcal{E})=d_{0}-r_{1}\delta, \deg(\mathcal{G})=d_{0}.

If
r_{0}=r_{1}>0
then the non-emptiness of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
follows from Proposition
2.8
applied to an exact sequence (
2.5
)
such that
\mathcal{F}
is a vector bundle of degree
d_{1}
,
\mathcal{E}
is vector bundle of degree
d_{0}-r_{1}\delta
,
\mathcal{G}
is a coherent sheaf with torsion subsheaf
\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{G})=\operatorname{Im}(e)
of degree

d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta\geq 0
and locally free quotient equal to
\mathcal{F}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}
.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

If
{\mathscr{F}}
is a generalized vector bundle of rank
r
, generalized degree
D
and index
\iota({\mathscr{F}})
, then the complete degree of
{\mathscr{F}}
is given by

(2.3)

d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=\frac{D+\iota({\mathscr{F}})+r\delta}{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})=\frac{D-\iota({\mathscr{F}})-r\delta}{2}.

In particular, we have that

(2.4)

\iota({\mathscr{F}})=d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})-d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})-r\delta.

Proof.

We have a surjection

{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\twoheadrightarrow{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\cong{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}

between sheaves on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of the same rank
r
, and such that
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
is a vector bundle.
This implies that we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})\to{\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}\to{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}\cong{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}\to 0,

which implies that

d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})=\iota({\mathscr{F}})+d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})-r\deg{\mathscr{N}}=\iota({\mathscr{F}})+d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})+r\delta.

We conclude using that
D=d_{0}({\mathscr{F}})+d_{1}({\mathscr{F}})
.
∎

Example 2.7
.

Any
generalized line bundle
, i.e. a generalized vector bundle of rank
1
, is of the form
\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L}
for a unique
0

D\subset X_{\mathrm{red}}
and a (not necessarily unique) line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
(see
[

, Prop. 8.2.1]
).
The restriction of

to

is given by (see

Dre06
, Ex. 6.3.10]

Hence, using Fact
1.4
(i)
, we deduce that the canonical exact sequence of

is equal to

0\to\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}(-D)\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}(-D)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{D}\to\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}(-D)\to 0

where the middle homomorphism factors through

. It follows that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &b(\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L})=l(D),\\
&d_{0}(\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L})=\deg\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}},\\
&d_{1}(\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L})=\deg\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}-l(D)-\delta,\\
&\operatorname{Deg}(\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L})=2\deg\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}-l(D)-\delta.\end{aligned}\right.

where

is the length of

.

2.2.
Irreducibility and dimension

In this subsection, we prove that the strata are smooth and irreducible and we determine their dimensions.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
X
be a ribbon and fix any

.
If
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)

\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})

Fix

2.3.
Specializations

In this section by determining when the closure of a stratum of

Theorem 2.13

Let
X
be a ribbon and fix any
(R,D)\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}
.
Assume that
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset\Longleftrightarrow(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet}).

Proof.

Assume first that
\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\neq\emptyset
, i.e. that there exists a torsion-free sheaf of complete type
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
that specializes to a torsion-free sheaf of complete type
(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
.
Fact
1.4
(v)
implies that
r_{1}\geq\widetilde{r}_{1}
. It remains to show that if
r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}
(or equivalently if
r_{\bullet}=\widetilde{r}_{\bullet}
) then
d_{1}\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}
(or equivalently that
d_{0}\leq\widetilde{d}_{0}
).

By assumption, there exists a coherent sheaf
\mathbb{F}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, where
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is a trait (i.e.
R
is a DVR) with special point
o
and generic point
\eta
, flat over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
and
\mathbb{F}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
. Consider the coherent (not necessarily flat) sheaf
{\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F}
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, which has the property that
({\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F})_{o}
is a vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
\widetilde{r}_{1}
and degree
\widetilde{d}_{1}
, while
({\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F})_{\eta}
is a vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
r_{1}
and degree
d_{1}
. Pick an ample line bundle
\mathcal{O}(1)
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
and set
d:=\deg\mathcal{O}(1)
. It follows from the proof of
[
Har
, III.9.9]
that the function
s\to\chi(({\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F})_{s}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m))
is lower semicontinuous for
m\gg 0
. Hence we compute

(\widetilde{d}_{1}+\widetilde{r}_{1}dm)+\widetilde{r}_{1}(1-\overline{g})=\chi(({\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F})_{o}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m))\leq\chi(({\mathscr{N}}\cdot\mathbb{F})_{\eta}\otimes\mathcal{O}(m))=(d_{1}+r_{1}dm)+r_{1}(1-\overline{g}).

Since
r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}
by assumption, we conclude that
\widetilde{d}_{1}\leq d_{1}
, as required.

Assume now that
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
. We want to show that there exists a coherent sheaf
\mathbb{F}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, where
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is a trait (i.e.
R
is a DVR) with special point
o
and generic point
\eta
, flat over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
and
\mathbb{F}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
. We will distinguish four cases.

Case I:

\widetilde{r}_{0}=r_{0}=r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}
.

Since
(r_{\bullet},d_{\bullet})
is admissible and
r:=r_{0}=r_{1}
, we have that
d_{0}\geq d_{1}+r\delta
. Moreover, by the assumption that
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
and the fact that
r_{\bullet}=\widetilde{r}_{\bullet}
, we have that

\widetilde{d}_{0}\geq d_{0}\geq d_{1}+r\delta\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}+r\delta.

Hence, we can find integers
\{(d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j},\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j},\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j})\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j}\geq d_{0}^{j}\geq d_{1}^{j}+\delta\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j}+\delta\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r,\\
&d_{0}^{j}+d_{1}^{j}=\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j}+\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j}\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r,\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{0}^{j}=d_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{d_{0}^{j}}=\widetilde{d_{0}}.\end{aligned}\right.

By a re-iterated application of Lemma
2.14
below, we can find coherent sheaves
\{\mathbb{F}^{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, flat over a trait
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}^{j}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
and
\mathbb{F}^{j}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;\widetilde{d_{0}^{j}},\widetilde{d_{1}^{j}})
. The desired family is given by
\mathbb{F}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{F}^{j}
.

Case II:

\widetilde{r}_{0}=r_{0}>r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}
.

Set
r:=r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{1}
. We can find integers
\{(d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j},\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j},\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j})\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
and
\{e,\widetilde{e}\}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j}\geq d_{0}^{j}\geq d_{1}^{j}+\delta\geq\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j}+\delta\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r,\\
&d_{0}^{j}+d_{1}^{j}=\widetilde{d}_{0}^{j}+\widetilde{d}_{1}^{j}\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r,\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{0}^{j}+e=d_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{j=1}^{r}\widetilde{d_{0}^{j}}+\widetilde{e}=\widetilde{d_{0}}.\end{aligned}\right.

By a re-iterated application of Lemma
2.14
below, we can find coherent sheaves
\{\mathbb{F}^{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, flat over a trait
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}^{j}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
and
\mathbb{F}^{j}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;\widetilde{d_{0}^{j}},\widetilde{d_{1}^{j}})
. Moreover, take a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
r_{0}-r_{1}=\widetilde{r}_{0}-\widetilde{r}_{1}
and degree
e
.
The desired family is given by
\mathbb{F}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{F}^{j}\oplus p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{E})
.

Case III:

\widetilde{r}_{0}>r_{0}=r_{1}>\widetilde{r}_{1}
.

Since
(r_{\bullet},d_{\bullet})
is admissible and
r_{0}=r_{1}
, we have that
d_{0}\geq d_{1}+r\delta
. Hence, we can find integers
\{(d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{1}}
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d_{0}^{j}\geq d_{1}^{j}+\delta\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r_{1},\\
&\sum_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}d_{1}^{j}=\widetilde{d}_{1},\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{r_{1}}d_{0}^{j}=d_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

By a re-iterated application of Lemma
2.15
below, we can find coherent sheaves
\{\mathbb{F}^{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, flat over a trait
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}^{j}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
and
\mathbb{F}^{j}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(2,0;d_{0}^{j}+d_{1}^{j},0)
. Moreover, take generalized line bundles
\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}
on
X
of complete types
(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
.
The desired family is given by
\mathbb{F}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}}\mathbb{F}^{j}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{j})
.

Case IV:

\widetilde{r}_{0}>r_{0}>r_{1}>\widetilde{r}_{1}
.

We can find integers
\{(d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{1}}
and
e
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &d_{0}^{j}\geq d_{1}^{j}+\delta\quad\text{ for every }1\leq j\leq r_{1},\\
&\sum_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}d_{1}^{j}=\widetilde{d}_{1},\\
&\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{0}^{j}+e=d_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

By a re-iterated application of Lemma
2.15
below, we can find coherent sheaves
\{\mathbb{F}^{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}}
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, flat over a trait
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, such that

\mathbb{F}^{j}_{\eta}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
and
\mathbb{F}^{j}_{o}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(2,0;d_{0}^{j}+d_{1}^{j},0)
. Moreover, take generalized line bundles
\{\mathcal{F}^{j}\}_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}
on
X
of complete types
(1,1;d_{0}^{j},d_{1}^{j})
and
a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
r_{0}-r_{1}
and degree
e
.
The desired family is given by
\mathbb{F}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}}\mathbb{F}^{j}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=r_{1}-\widetilde{r}_{1}+1}^{r_{1}}p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{F}^{j})\oplus p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{E})
.

∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
(d_{0},d_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
d_{0}\geq d_{1}+\delta
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}((1,1);(d_{0},d_{1}))}\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}((1,1);(d_{0}+1,d_{1}-1))\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

This is well-known, see e.g.
[
CK16
, Lemma 4.5]
. Let us sketch a proof.

By our assumptions on
(d_{0},d_{1})
, we have that

b:=d_{0}-d_{1}+\delta+2\geq 2.

Consider an effective divisor
D=2p+E
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of length
b
with
p\not\in\operatorname{supp}E
and a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
X
such that
\deg\mathcal{L}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}=d_{0}+1
.
By Example (
2.7
), the generalized line bundle
\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{X}((1,1);(d_{0}+1,d_{1}-1))
. There exists a family
\mathcal{D}\subset X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
of
0
-dimensional subschemes of
X
of length
b

over a trait
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
such that the special fiber
\mathcal{D}_{o}
is equal to
D=2p+E
and the general fiber
\mathcal{D}_{\eta}
is equal to
E+C
where
C
is a
0
-dimensional subscheme of
X
of length two supported at
p
and not entirely contained in
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}_{\eta}}=\mathcal{I}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{C}
, where
\mathcal{I}_{C}
is a line bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
such that
(\mathcal{I}_{C})_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(-p)
.
Therefore, the family of torsion-free sheaves
\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{L})
on
X\times\operatorname{Spec}(R)
is such that
(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{L}))_{o}=\mathcal{I}_{D}\otimes\mathcal{L}
while the generic fiber
(\mathcal{I}_{\mathcal{D}}\otimes p_{1}^{*}(\mathcal{L}))_{\eta}=\mathcal{I}_{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{L}
belongs
to
\mathcal{M}_{X}((1,1);(d_{0},d_{1}))
by Example (
2.7
). This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
(d_{0},d_{1})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
such that
d_{0}\geq d_{1}+\delta
. Then

\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}((1,1);(d_{0},d_{1}))}\cap\mathcal{M}_{X}((2,0);(d_{0}+d_{1},0))\neq\emptyset.

Proof.

By our assumptions on
(d_{0},d_{1})
, we can find two line bundles
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of degree, respectively,
d_{0}-\delta
and
d_{1}+\delta
such that there exists a injective map
\phi:L_{1}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\hookrightarrow L_{2}
.
Consider the rank two vector bundle
\mathcal{E}=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, which has degree
d_{0}+d_{1}
and sits in the split exact sequence

0\to L_{0}\to\mathcal{E}\to L_{1}\to 0.

Since there exists an injective map
\phi:L_{1}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\hookrightarrow L_{2}
and the condition of being injective is open for families of morphisms, the generic element
[\mathcal{F}]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(L_{1},L_{0})
will have an associated injective morphism
\pi([\mathcal{F}]):L_{1}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\hookrightarrow L_{2}
(with the notation of Fact
1.4
(iii)
). Therefore, the same Fact
1.4
(iii)
implies that, for the general element
[\mathcal{F}]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(L_{1},L_{0})
, the extension

0\to L_{0}\to\mathcal{F}\to L_{1}\to 0

is the second canonical filtration. Using Remark
1.5
, we get that the generic element
[\mathcal{F}]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}(L_{1},L_{0})
has complete type equal to

(r_{0}(\mathcal{F}),r_{1}(\mathcal{F});d_{0}(\mathcal{F}),d_{1}(\mathcal{F}))=(1,1;d_{0},d_{1}),

and this concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Irreducible components

In this section, we determine the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
. There are two different cases, according to whether
\delta\leq 2\overline{g}-2
or
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
.
The first case is easier.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\delta\leq 2(\overline{g}-1)
then the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
are

\{\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}\}_{(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)}.

Moreover:

(i)

If
\delta<2(\overline{g}-1)
then
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
has dimension
R^{2}(\overline{g}-1)
and the unique irreducible component of maximal dimension is
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,0;D,0)
, i.e. the component that parametrizes vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
of rank
R
and degree
D
.

(ii)

If
\delta=2\overline{g}-2
then
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
is pure of dimension equal to
R^{2}(\overline{g}-1)
.

This was proved for
R=2
(for the semistable locus) in
[
CK16
, Thm. B]
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Theorem
2.13
together with the fact that, for any
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet}),(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
and

\delta\leq 2(\overline{g}-1)
, we have that

(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\geq(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\Rightarrow\widetilde{r}_{0}\geq r_{0}\geq r_{1}\geq\widetilde{r}_{1}\Rightarrow\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\leq\dim\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet}),

as it follows from Theorem
2.10
.

The second assertion follows from the first one together with Theorem
2.10
.
∎

In order to determine the irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
in the case where
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
, the key ingredient that we will use is the following deformation criterion.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
. Assume that there exists a vector bundle
K
on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
such that

(i)

{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq K\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
;

(ii)

there exists an injective morphism

\phi:\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\hookrightarrow K.

Then
{\mathscr{F}}
can be deformed to a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
such that

(3.1)

{\mathscr{E}}^{(1)}=K\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{{\mathscr{E}}}{{\mathscr{E}}^{(1)}}=\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}.

Proof.

By Fact
1.4
(iii)
and the assumption (
ii
), the generic extension

\sigma=[0\to K\to{\mathscr{E}}\to\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\ \to 0]\in\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K},K\right)

is such that the associated morphism
\displaystyle\pi(\sigma):\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\rightarrow K
is injective.
Hence, Fact
1.4
(iii)
implies that the generic such extension satisfies properties (
3.1
).
We conclude by taking a generic deformation of
[0\to K\to{\mathscr{F}}\to\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\to 0]
inside
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K},K\right)
.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a ribbon such that
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
. For any
(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{adm}(R,D)
, there exists
(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\in\mathcal{S}_{rig}(R,D)
such that

\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})\subseteq\overline{\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})}.

The above result was proved for
R=2
in
[
Sav19
, Thm. 1]
.

Proof.

We can assume that
(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
is not of rigid type, i.e.
r_{0}\geq r_{1}+2
, for otherwise there is nothing to prove.
Take a general element
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(\widetilde{r}_{\bullet};\widetilde{d}_{\bullet})
, which is in particular quasi-locally free by Corollary
2.11
.
We will use Proposition
3.2
to deform
{\mathscr{F}}
to a torsion-free sheaf
{\mathscr{E}}
on
X
of complete rank
(r_{0}-1,r_{1}+1)
, which is enough to conclude the proof (by iterating the argument).

In order to show this, take a subvector bundle
\overline{K}
of rank
r_{0}-r_{1}-1
of the rank
r_{0}-r_{1}
and degree
d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta
vector bundle
\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}:=\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}
, which has
maximal degree among the rank
r_{0}-r_{1}-1
subvector bundles of
\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}
. By definition, we have that

(3.2)

(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)[d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta]-(r_{0}-r_{1})\deg\overline{K}=s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}),

where
s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}})
is, by definition, the
(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)
-Segre invariant of
\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}
(see
[
BPL98
,
RT99
]
). Since the Segre invariant is lower semicontinuous (see
[
BPL98
,
RT99
]
), we have that

(3.3)

s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}})\leq s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(r_{0}-r_{1},d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta),

where
s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(r_{0}-r_{1},d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta)
is the
(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)
-Segre invariant of the general rank
r_{0}-r_{1}
and degree
d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta
vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.
Moreover, the general such Segree invariant is the unique integer such that (see e.g.
[
RT99
, Thm. 0.1]
1
1

1

This is proved in loc. cit. under the assumption that
\overline{g}\geq 2
. However, the result is easily seen to be true also for
\overline{g}=0,1
by using the Segre-Grothendieck splitting theorem for vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and the Atiyah’s classification of vector bundles on elliptic curves.
)

(3.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(r_{0}-r_{1},d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta)\in[(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)(\overline{g}-1),(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)\overline{g}],\\
& s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(r_{0}-r_{1},d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta)\equiv-(d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta)\mod r_{0}-r_{1}.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\subseteq K\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
be the inverse image of
\overline{K}
. We have two exact sequences of vector bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle 0\to{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\to K\to\overline{K}\to 0,

\displaystyle 0\to\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}}{\overline{K}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\to 0.

Since we have an isomorphism
\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}={\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
(see Fact
1.4
(i)
), we can compute the difference between the slopes of
K
and of

\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}
as it follows (using (
3.2
))

(3.6)

\mu(K)-\mu\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\right)=\mu(\overline{K})-\mu\left(\frac{\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}}{\overline{K}}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\right)=\frac{\deg\overline{K}}{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}-[d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta-\deg\overline{K}-\delta]=

Now, using (
3.3
) and (

) and our assumption that
\delta>2\overline{g}-2
, we distinguish two cases.

Case I:
Either
s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}})<(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)\overline{g}
or

.

In this case, (

) implies that

\mu(K)-\mu\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\right)>\overline{g}-1.

By the twisted higher Brill-Noether rank theory (see

), this implies that there exists an injective morphism

\phi:\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\hookrightarrow K.

Hence Proposition

implies that the sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
can be deformed to a torsion free sheaf
{\mathscr{E}}
such that

{\mathscr{E}}^{(1)}=K\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{{\mathscr{E}}}{{\mathscr{E}}^{(1)}}=\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}.

In particular,
{\mathscr{E}}
will have complete rank
(r_{0}-1,r_{1}+1)
, as required.

Case II:

s_{r_{0}-r_{1}-1}(\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}})=(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)\overline{g}

and
\delta=2\overline{g}-1
.

In this case, (
3.6
) implies that

\mu(K)-\mu\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}}{K}\otimes{\mathscr{N}}\right)=\overline{g}-1.

Moreover, it follows from
[

, Thm. 4.4]
or
[
RT99

that

\dim\left\{\overline{K}\subset\Gamma_{{\mathscr{F}}}:\operatorname{rk}(\overline{K})=r_{0}-r_{1}-1\text{ and } \deg(\overline{K})=\frac{(r_{0}-r_{1}-1)(d_{0}-d_{1}-r_{1}\delta-\overline{g})}{r_{0}-r_{1}}\right\}\geq r_{0}-r_{1}-1.

Again by the twisted higher Brill-Noether rank theory (see
[
RT99
, Thm. 0.3]
), this implies that we can choose such a
K

4.
The (semi)stable locus

The aim of this section is to study the open substack of the stack
\mathcal{M}_{X}
parametrizing (slope-) stable or semistable torsion-free sheaves, whose definition we now recall.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on a ribbon
X
with
R({\mathscr{F}})>0
(or, equivalently, non zero) and define its
slope
by

\mu({\mathscr{F}})=\frac{\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})}{\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})}.

(1)

We say that:

•

{\mathscr{F}}
is
semistable
if
\mu({\mathscr{G}})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
for any proper subsheaf
0\neq{\mathscr{G}}\subsetneq{\mathscr{F}}
;

•

{\mathscr{F}}
is
stable
if
\mu({\mathscr{G}})<\mu({\mathscr{F}})
for any proper subsheaf
0\neq{\mathscr{G}}\subsetneq{\mathscr{F}}
;

•

{\mathscr{F}}
is
polystable
if it is equal to the direct sum of stable sheaves of the same slopes.

(2)

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a semistable sheaf, then a
Jordan-Holder filtration
of
{\mathscr{F}}
is a filtration whose associated graded object
\operatorname{Gr}_{\text{JH}}({\mathscr{F}})
is polystable of the same slope of
{\mathscr{F}}
. It is well known that any semistable sheaf admits a (non-necessarily unique) Jordan-Holder filtration and that
\operatorname{Gr}_{\text{JH}}({\mathscr{F}})
is independent of the choice of the filtration (see e.g.
[
HL
, Proposition 1.5.2]
). Clearly, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is stable,
\operatorname{Gr}_{\text{JH}}({\mathscr{F}})={\mathscr{F}}
.
Two semistable sheaves
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{G}}
are said to be
S-equivalent
if
\operatorname{Gr}_{\text{JH}}({\mathscr{F}})
and
\operatorname{Gr}_{\text{JH}}({\mathscr{G}})
are isomorphic.

Remark 4.2
.

(i)

As usual, it is possible to check semistability considering only saturated subsheaves of
{\mathscr{F}}
(i.e. subsheaves
{\mathscr{G}}
such that the quotient
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{G}}
is torsion-free) and it is equivalent to the reverse inequalities for quotients of
{\mathscr{F}}
(these are well-known basic properties of semistability, see e.g.
[
HL
, Proposition 1.2.6]
).

(ii)

It follows from Facts
1.11
(iii)
and
1.11
(iv)
and from the previous point that a torsion-free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
is (semi)stable if and only if its dual
{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee}
is (semi)stable.

(iii)

By Fact
1.8
(i)
, slope-semistability is equivalent, on a ribbon
X
, to Gieseker semistability with respect to any ample line bundle on
X
(which is defined in terms of the leading coefficient of the reduced Hilbert polynomial, for its precise definition see
[
HL
]
).

It is well-known that stability and semistability are open conditions, so they determine open substacks of the stack of torsion-free sheaves on
X
.

Definition 4.3
.

Denote by

\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{s}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)

the open substack parametrizing stable (resp. semistable) sheaves on
X
of generalized rank
R
and generalized degree
D
.

Using that slope-(semi)stability is equivalent on a ribbon to Gieseker (semi)-stability, the stacks of (semi)stable torsion-free sheaves admit suitable moduli spaces.

.

(see
[
Sim94
]
,
[

There exists a Cartesian diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)^{ss}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

(ii)

and

Lemma 4.8

4.1.
Example: semistability of torsion-free sheaves of type
(n,1)

In this subsection, we discuss the semistability of torsion-free sheaves of type
(n,1)
(for
n\geq 0
), i.e. those torsion-free sheaves
{\mathscr{F}}
on
X
such that

{\mathscr{F}}_{\eta}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},\eta}^{\oplus n}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X,\eta},\>\text{ or, equivalently, such that }(r_{0}({\mathscr{F}}),r_{1}({\mathscr{F}}))=(n+1,1).

First of all, we show that these sheaves are the push-forward of quasi-locally free sheaves of the same type on some blow-up of the ribbon.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
of type
(n,1)
, for some
n\geq 0
. There is a unique divisor
D\subset X_{\mathrm{red}}
such that
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{D}
and a unique quasi-locally free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
of type
(n,1)
on the blow up
q:\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)\to X
of
X
at
D
such that
q_{*}{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}\simeq{\mathscr{F}}
.

This theorem generalizes
[
EG95
, Theorem 1.1]
which deals with generalized line bundles (i.e. with the case
n=0
).

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of
[
EG95
, Theorem 1.1]
, as we now indicate.

First of all, observe that
{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and its saturation
{\mathscr{K}}:=\operatorname{ker}({\mathscr{F}}\to\frac{{\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}}{\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})})
are line bundles on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
(being torsion-free of rank
r_{1}({\mathscr{F}})=1
by Remark
1.3
) and that

{\mathscr{K}}/{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}\cong\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})
can be written as
\mathcal{O}_{D}
for a unique effective divisor
D
of
X_{\mathrm{red}}
.

At this point the proof is
verbatim
the same of
[
EG95
, Theorem 1.1]
: it is possible to give to
{\mathscr{F}}
a structure of
\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)}
-module (which is unique because it is derived only from the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module structure of
{\mathscr{F}}
) in the following way.

Let
f\in\operatorname{H}^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(D))
be a section vanishing on
D
, let
\sigma^{\prime}
be a section of
\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)}
defined over an open set
U
of
X
(recall that
X
and
\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)
are homeomorphic) and
m
a section of
{\mathscr{F}}(U)
. Shrinking
U
, if necessary, it is possible to find a section
\sigma
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(U)
with the same image of
\sigma^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}(U)
. Hence,
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma+f^{-1}\tau
, where
\tau
is an appropriate section of
{\mathscr{N}}(U)
. The sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}
admits a structure of
\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)}
-module by setting
\sigma^{\prime}m:=\sigma m+f^{-1}(\tau m)
; the latter is well defined because
\tau m\in{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{K}}=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}({\mathscr{F}})\otimes{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}
and it is independent of the choice of
\sigma
. By construction, we have that
q_{*}{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}\simeq{\mathscr{F}}
.
∎

For torsion-free sheaves of type
(n,1)
, we have the following strong (semi)stability criterion.

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
of type
(n,1)
, for some
n>0
. Then
{\mathscr{F}}
is semistable if and only if the following two conditions are verified:

(i)

for any sub-bundle
\mathcal{E}\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
of rank less than
n+1
we have
\mu(\mathcal{E})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
;

(ii)

for any torsion-free quotient
({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
of rank less than
n+1
it holds that
\mu(\mathcal{G})\geq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
.

Furthermore,
{\mathscr{F}}
is stable if and only if the inequalities in
(i)
and
(ii)
are strict.

The above Proposition extends
[
Dre06
, Lemme 9.1.2]
, which deals with quasi-locally free sheaves of type
(1,1)
.

Proof.

Necessity is obvious, so we have to only prove sufficiency. We will prove only the semistable case, because the stable one is similar.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}\subset{\mathscr{F}}
be a saturated subsheaf. If
{\mathscr{E}}
is defined over
X_{\mathrm{red}}
, then
{\mathscr{E}}\subseteq{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
. If it has rank
\leq n
, then
\mu({\mathscr{E}})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
by
(i)
. On the other hand, if it has rank
n+1
, it has the same rank of
{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
and it is contained in it. Hence,
\mu({\mathscr{E}})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})
and it suffices to check that
\mu({\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
. This follows from condition
(ii)
, because
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}
is a torsion-free quotient of
({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}
of rank
1
and, thus,
\mu({\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})\geq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
.

So, assume that
{\mathscr{E}}
is not defined over
X_{\mathrm{red}}
; this means that
{\mathscr{E}}
is generically isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}^{\oplus m}
with
0\leq m<n
. Hence,
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{E}}
is generically isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}^{\oplus(n-m)}
. This implies (using that
{\mathscr{E}}
is saturated in
{\mathscr{F}}
) that
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{E}}
is a rank
n-m
vector bundle on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
. Thus,
{\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{E}}
is a pure quotient of
({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}
of rank
\leq n
and so, by
(ii)
,
\mu({\mathscr{F}}/{\mathscr{E}})\geq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
, which is equivalent to
\mu({\mathscr{E}})\leq\mu({\mathscr{F}})
.
∎

Remark 4.13
.

The case
n=0
, i.e. that of generalized line bundles, is not covered by the previous Proposition. In order to cover also this case, one should drop the hypothesis
of rank
\leq n
in the two conditions, which moreover becomes equivalent to each other. Indeed if
{\mathscr{I}}
is a generalized line bundle, it holds that
({\mathscr{I}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}={\mathscr{I}}/{\mathscr{I}}^{(1)}
and the two conditions (without the cited hypothesis) are both equivalent to
b({\mathscr{I}})\leq-\deg({\mathscr{N}})
, which is equivalent to the semistability of
{\mathscr{I}}
(see
[
CK16
, Lemma 3.2]
).

Corollary 4.14
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}
be a torsion-free sheaf of type
(n,1)
, for some
n\geq 0
. Let
q:\operatorname{Bl}_{D}(X)\to X
be unique the blow up of
X
at a divisor
D\subset X_{\mathrm{red}}
such that
{\mathscr{F}}=q_{*}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})
for a quasi-locally free sheaf
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
, as in Proposition
4.11
. Then
{\mathscr{F}}
is (semi)stable if and only if
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
is (semi)stable.

Proof.

It holds by definition that
\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{Deg}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})
and
\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{R}({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})
; hence,
\mu({\mathscr{F}})=\mu({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime})
. The construction of
{\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}
implies that
{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}={\mathscr{F}}^{\prime(1)}
and
({\mathscr{F}}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}=({\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{\vee\vee}={\mathscr{F}}^{\prime}|_{X_{\mathrm{red}}}
. Therefore, the assertion follows from Proposition
4.12
for
n>0
and from Remark
4.13
for
n=0
.
∎

5.
Tangent spaces

The aim of this section is to compute the infinitesimal automorphism space
\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
and the tangent space
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
of
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
at a point
{\mathscr{F}}
(see
[
Stacks
,
Tag 07WY
]
). Since
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
is an open substack of the stack of coherent sheaves on
X
, we have the following identifications (see e.g. the proof of

[
Stacks
,
Tag 08W8
]
)

(5.1)

\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})\quad\text{ and }\quad T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}).

Using the local-to-global spectral sequence

E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{q}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))\Rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{p+q}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}),

and the fact that
X
is a curve, we deduce the following identification and short exact sequence:

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\operatorname{Ext}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})=H^{0}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})),\\
&0\to H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))\to\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})\to H^{0}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))\to 0.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 5.1
.

In the exact sequence of (
5.2
), the first term
H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))
parametrizes infinitesimal deformations of
{\mathscr{F}}
that are locally trivial, i.e. such that the induced infinitesimal deformation of
{\mathscr{F}}_{p}
is trivial for each point
p\in X
, and hence they do not change the complete type of
{\mathscr{F}}
. Therefore we get the inclusions

(5.3)

H^{1}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}).

On the other hand, we clearly have the equalities

(5.4)

H^{0}(X,\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}))=\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})=\operatorname{Inf}_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}}).

We now study the sheaves
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
and
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{k}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
for

.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
and set

. Then
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{0}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})=\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}})
is a torsion-free sheaf on
X
that fits into an exact sequence

(5.5)

0\to\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}om}}(({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{**},{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})\xrightarrow{\alpha}\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}})\xrightarrow{\beta}\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}})\to\bigoplus_{p\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T})}\bigoplus_{1\leq i<j\leq b({\mathscr{F}})}\mathcal{O}_{(n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p)-n_{j}({\mathscr{F}},p))p}\to 0.

Moreover, the short exact sequence

(5.6)

0\to\operatorname{{\mathcal{H}om}}(({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})^{**},{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)})\xrightarrow{\alpha}\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}})\xrightarrow{\beta}\operatorname{Im}(\beta)\to 0

is the second canonical filtration of
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}({\mathscr{F}})
.

Note that the cokernel of

is a torsion sheaf supported on
\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}))
and whose length is equal to (by Definition
1.15
and Remark
1.16

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
be the completion of the local ring of a ribbon
X
at a closed point
p

First of all, observe that the rings

and the
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}

\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}=k[[s,\epsilon]]/(\epsilon^{2}),\\
&\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=(\epsilon)\subset\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}\Rightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}=\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}/\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{p}=k[[s]],\\

\end{aligned}\right.

On the other hand, the ideal

admits the following presentation as a
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
-module:

(5.9)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}=(\epsilon,s^{n})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\cong}\frac{\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}e\oplus\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}f}{(\epsilon f,\epsilon e-s^{n}f)}

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle\mapsto f

\displaystyle s^{n}

\displaystyle\mapsto e,

which implies, in particular, that any element of

can be written uniquely as

. Therefore, the module

is isomorphic to

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}=k[[s]]

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\cong}\widehat{\mathcal{N}}_{p}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}=\langle\epsilon e,\epsilon f\rangle

\displaystyle 1

\bullet

Theorem 5.6
.

. Then

[
Dre06
, Cor. 6.2.2]

[

, Lemma 4.12]
(see also the proof of
[
Dre06

Proof.

and write

(5.10)

(which is also proved in

), that the torsion subsheaf

at a closed point
p
. Consider the ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}
and, for any
n\geq 0
, the ideal
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}
and the ring
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{np}
, all of them considered as
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,p}
-modules. Then we have

(i)

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p})=\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}
for any
k\geq 0
.

(ii)

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p},\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np})=\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}&\text{ if }k=0,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{np}&\text{ if }k>0.\end{cases}

(iii)

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p})=\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}&\text{ if }k=0,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{np}&\text{ if }k>0.\end{cases}

(iv)

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp},\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np})=\begin{cases}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{\max\{n,m\}p}&\text{ if }k=0,\\
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{\min\{n,m\}p}^{\oplus 2}&\text{ if }k>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Recall the presentation of the various rings and modules given in (
5.8
) and (
5.9
).

The modules
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}
admit the following resolutions via free modules

(5.21)

\ldots\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p},

(5.22)

\displaystyle\ldots\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&s^{n}\\
0&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&s^{n}\\
0&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&s^{n}\\
0&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\mathcal{O}_{X,p}^{\oplus 2}

\displaystyle\twoheadrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np},

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a\\
b\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\mapsto af+be.

We now use the above resolutions to compute the desired Ext groups:

\bullet

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p})=H^{k}(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon=0}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon=0}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon=0}\ldots)=\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}

for any
k\geq 0
.

\bullet

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p},\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np})=H^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}\xrightarrow{\cdot\epsilon}\ldots).

The maps in the above complex are equal to

\displaystyle\cdot\epsilon:\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}

\displaystyle\to\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}

\displaystyle a(s)e+b(s)f

\displaystyle\mapsto\epsilon(a(s)e+b(s)f)=s^{n}a(s)f.

Therefore, the image and kernel of the above morphism are given by

\operatorname{Im}(\cdot\epsilon)=\{s^{n}a(s)f\>:a(s)\in k[[s]]\}\subseteq\operatorname{ker}(\cdot\epsilon)=\{b(s)f\>:b(s)\in k[[s]]\}\subset\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}.

From this, part (
ii
) follows.

\bullet

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np},\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p})=H^{k}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}}\mathcal{O}_{X_{\mathrm{red}},p}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{n}&0\end{pmatrix}}\ldots\right)
.

The image and kernel of the morphism appearing in the above complex are equal to

\operatorname{Im}(\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{n}&0\end{pmatrix})=\langle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
s^{n}\end{pmatrix}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{ker}(\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
s^{n}&0\end{pmatrix})=\langle\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\rangle

From this, part (
iii
) follows.

\bullet

\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np},\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp})=H^{k}\left(\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}}\ldots\right)
.

The map in the above complex is given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix}:\mathcal{I}_{mp}^{\oplus 2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{I}_{mp}^{\oplus 2}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}(s)e+b_{1}(s)f\\
a_{2}(s)e+b_{2}(s)f\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a_{1}(s)s^{m}f\\
a_{1}(s)s^{n}e+[s^{n}b_{1}(s)-s^{m}a_{2}(s)]f\end{pmatrix}

The image and kernel of the morphism appearing in the above complex are equal to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \operatorname{Im}(\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix})=\langle\begin{pmatrix}s^{m}f\\
s^{n}e\end{pmatrix},s^{\min\{n,m\}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f\end{pmatrix}\rangle,\\
\operatorname{ker}(\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon&0\\
s^{n}&-\epsilon\end{pmatrix})=\begin{cases}\langle\begin{pmatrix}s^{m-n}e\\
f\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f\end{pmatrix}\rangle\cong\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{mp}&\text{ if }n\leq m,\\
\langle\begin{pmatrix}e\\
s^{n-m}f\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}0\\
f\end{pmatrix}\rangle\cong\widehat{\mathcal{I}}_{np}&\text{ if }n\geq m,\\
\end{cases}\end{aligned}\right.

From this, part (
iv
) follows.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
. Then we have that:

(i)

{\mathscr{F}}
is a generalized vector bundle (i.e. if
r_{0}=r_{1}
) if and only if
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{k}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
is a torsion sheaf for any
k\geq 1
.

(ii)

{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free (i.e. if
\iota({\mathscr{F}})=0
) if and only if
\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}xt}}^{k}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
is a torsion-free sheaf for any
k\geq 1
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
. The inclusion

T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\subseteq T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)

has codimension equal to

h^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}\left(\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}\right)^{**}\right)\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1})+\iota({\mathscr{F}})a({\mathscr{F}})+\sum_{p\in\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}}))}(2i-1)n_{i}({\mathscr{F}},p).

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
5.6
and Corollary
5.5
, using the identification
T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})
and the exact sequence (
5.2
).
∎

Corollary 5.10
.

Let
{\mathscr{F}}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})
and assume that
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi-locally free. Then we have an exact sequence

(5.23)

0\to T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(r_{\bullet};d_{\bullet})\to T_{{\mathscr{F}}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)\to H^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},\operatorname{{\mathcal{E}nd}}\left(\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}\right)\otimes{\mathscr{N}}^{-1})\to 0.

In particular, if
{\mathscr{F}}
is of rigid type (i.e. if
r_{0}=r_{1}
or
r_{1}+1
) then we have that

(i)

If
r_{0}=r_{1}
(i.e. if
{\mathscr{F}}
is a locally free sheaf) then
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)
is smooth at
{\mathscr{F}}
.

(ii)

If
r_{0}=r_{1}+1
then the underlying reduced stack
\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)_{\operatorname{red}}
is smooth at
{\mathscr{F}}
and we have the identification

\frac{T_{\mathscr{F}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)}{T_{\mathscr{F}}\mathcal{M}_{X}(R,D)_{\operatorname{red}}}=H^{0}(X_{\mathrm{red}},{\mathscr{N}}^{-1}).

Part (
i
) is well-known (and it also follows from the fact that
\operatorname{Ext}^{2}({\mathscr{F}},{\mathscr{F}})=0
if
{\mathscr{F}}
is locally free), while part (
ii
) of the above Corollary follows from
[
Dre09
, Thm. E]
and
[
Dre06
, Cor. 6.2.2]
(see also
[
Dre20
, Sec. 1.1.2]
).

Proof.

This follows from Corollary
5.9
, using that if
{\mathscr{F}}
is quasi locally free then

•

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}
is torsion-free on
X_{\mathrm{red}}
because it is a subsheaf of the torsion-free sheaf

;

•

\iota({\mathscr{F}})=0
and
\mathcal{T}({\mathscr{F}}_{|X_{\mathrm{red}}})=0

•

If

then
\frac{{\mathscr{F}}^{(1)}}{{\mathscr{N}}{\mathscr{F}}}=0

If

∎

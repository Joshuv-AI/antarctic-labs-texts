The cyclic bar construction and fundamental groups
Nir Gadish

Abstract
. We determine the 0 0 -th Hochschild homology of the associative algebra of simplicial cochains valued in a PID: it consists of the “finite-type” homotopy invariants of free loops, equivalently finite-type class functions on the fundamental group. One major motivation for this calculation is joint work in progress aiming to geometrically construct invariants of links in the 3 3 -sphere as well as other 3 3 -manifolds, and to realize Milnor’s linking numbers as evaluations of 0 0 -th Hochschild homology classes.

1.
Introduction

Given an associative augmented dg-algebra
(A^{\bullet},d)
, its cyclic Bar construction, or Hochschild complex, is the complex of tensors
\operatorname{Cyc}(A):=A\otimes T(\bar{A})
, where
T(-)
is the tensor algebra functor applied to (the degree shift by
-1
of) the augmentation ideal
\bar{A}
. This has natural differential
d_{\operatorname{Cyc}}
of algebraic and geometric significance, so that for any
simply connected
topological space
Y
with singular cochain algebra
A=C^{*}_{Sing}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
there is a well-known natural isomorphism

\mathrm{H}^{*}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A),d_{\operatorname{Cyc}})\cong\mathrm{H}^{*}(LY;\mathbb{Z})

where
LY=\operatorname{Maps}(S^{1},M)
is the free loop space of
Y
(see e.g.
[
Jon87
,
Lod11
]
). Alternatively, when
Y
is a simply connected smooth manifold and
A
is its deRham algebra
\Omega^{*}_{dR}(Y)
, an analogous isomorphism holds with the
\mathbb{R}
-valued cohomology of
LY
, realized by Chen’s iterated integrals
[
GJP91
]
.

These examples leave open the question of what
\operatorname{Cyc}(A)
is calculating when
Y
is connected but not simply connected. In that case, the connected components
\pi_{0}(LY)
are indexed by the set of conjugacy classes of
\pi_{1}(Y)
, so one might expect
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A))
to compute the set class functions, that is, conjugation-invariant functions
\pi_{1}(Y)\to\mathbb{Z}
. This optimistic guess fails, but not too badly, as our main result shows.

Below, we state results for simplicial sets. This setup effectively includes all topological spaces via the singular simplicial set construction
Y\mapsto\operatorname{Sing}_{\bullet}(Y)
, but also includes finite combinatorial models of spaces and groups of interest in applications. In particular, it is possible to implement the isomorphisms below algorithmically for finitely presented groups and finite 2-complexes.

Let
R
be any PID, e.g.
R=\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
. If
G
is a group, then the group algebra
R[G]
has a natural
G
-action by conjugation. Its
augmentation ideal

I
, generated by differences
\{g-1\mid g\in G\}
is invariant under conjugation by
G
. A function
G\to R
is said to be of
finite type

n
is its
R
-linear extension
R[G]\to R
annihilates
I^{n+1}
, e.g. constants are of type
0
and homomorphisms are of type
1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Y
be a connected pointed simplicial set, with finitely generated fundamental group
\pi_{1}(Y)
. Let
C^{*}_{\Delta}(Y;R)
denote the
R

\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Delta}(Y;R)
the associated cyclic Bar complex (a.k.a. the Hochschild complex, defined below).
Then there is a natural isomorphism of filtered

\mathrm{H}^{0}(\operatorname{Cyc}_{\Delta}(Y;R))\xrightarrow{\sim}\varinjlim_{n}\text{Hom}_{R}\left(R[\pi_{1}(Y)]/I^{n},R\right)^{\pi_{1}(Y)}

For an algebraically presented group, the isomorphism in (

For

In a prequel to this work

For a pointed simplicial set

2.
Associative algebras

Let
R
be a ground PID and consider
(A,d)
an associative differential graded (dg-)
R
-algebra, equipped with augmentation

0\to\bar{A}\to A\xrightarrow{\eta}R\to 0.

We call
\bar{A}
the augmentation ideal. The following terminology is standard.

Definition 2.1
.

A
is said to be
connected
if the augmentation induces and isomorphism
A^{0}\cong R
, or equivalently if
\bar{A}
is concentrated in strictly positive degree. More generally,
A
is said to be
homologically connected
if it is quasi-isomorphic to a connected dg algebra.

Definition 2.2

(Cyclic Bar construction)
.

Let
(M,d_{M})
be a differential graded
(A-A)
-bimodule, meaning that
d_{M}
satisfies the (graded) Leibniz rule with respect to multiplication by
A
on either side. The cyclic Bar complex of
A
with coefficients in
M
is the graded module

\operatorname{Cyc}(A;M):=M\otimes T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})

with tensor notation abbreviated to

m_{0}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]\sim m_{0}\otimes\mathbf{s}^{-1}a_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbf{s}^{-1}a_{n},

and differential
d(m_{0}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}])=d_{M}(m_{0})[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]-\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{\epsilon_{i-1}}m_{0}[a_{1}|\ldots|da_{i}|\ldots|a_{n}]

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|m_{0}|}

\displaystyle m_{0}a_{1}[a_{2}|\ldots|a_{n}]

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{\epsilon_{i}}m_{0}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{i}a_{i+1}|\ldots|a_{n}]

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\epsilon_{n-1}(|a_{n}|-1)}

\displaystyle a_{n}m_{0}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n-1}]

where
\epsilon_{i}=|m_{0}|+|a_{1}|+\ldots+|a_{i}|-i
(we follow the sign conventions of
[
GJP91
, Section 2]
).

In the special case
M=A
we denote
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;A)
simply by
\operatorname{Cyc}(A)
.

Example 2.3

(Linear Bar construction)
.

The augmentation
\eta\colon A\to R
endows
R
with a trivial
A
-bimodule structure, and the cyclic complex
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;R)
is naturally isomorphic to the classical (linear) bar construction
\operatorname{Bar}(A)
. This may be taken as a definition:

\operatorname{Cyc}(A;R)=R\otimes T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})\cong T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})=:\operatorname{Bar}(A)

consists of sums of elements
[a_{0}|\ldots|a_{n}]
, with differential
d_{\operatorname{Bar}}
consisting only of those terms of
d_{\operatorname{Cyc}}
that don’t operate on
m_{0}
. This is because the terms in which
\bar{A}
is multiplied with the module
M=R
through the augmentation automatically vanish.

Observation 2.4
.

The cyclic complex
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})=\bar{A}\otimes T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})
is additively isomorphic to the positive degree part of the tensor algebra
T^{>0}(\bar{A})
. Accordinely, there is a natural additive isomorphism of degree
+1

\iota\colon\overline{\operatorname{Bar}(A)}\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})\quad\text{ given by }\quad[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]\longmapsto a_{1}[a_{2}|\ldots|a_{n}].

This will play a role in our analysis below.

Crucially, the bi-functor
\operatorname{Cyc}(-;-)
is quasi-isomorphism invariant, in the following sense.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
A
and
A^{\prime}
be augmented dg-algebras that are
R
-torsion free, and let
M
and
M^{\prime}
be bounded-below bimodules over the respective algebras. If
h\colon A\to A^{\prime}
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg-algebras and
g\colon M\to M^{\prime}
is a quasi-isomorphism of bimodules, compatible with
h
, then the induced map

g\otimes T(h)\colon\operatorname{Cyc}(A;M)\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A^{\prime};M^{\prime})

is a quasi-isomorphism. In particular, the induced map
\mathrm{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A;M))\to\mathrm{H}^{i}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A^{\prime};M^{\prime}))
is an isomorphism for all
i
.

Proof.

Use the standard spectral sequence argument: the complexes are filtered by tensor degree, with associated graded breaking up as a direct sum of tensor product complexes

E_{0}\cong\bigoplus_{p\geq 0}(M\otimes(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})^{\otimes p},d).

The Künneth isomorphism applies, since
\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A}
are
R
-torsion free and
R
is a PID, and we get that the
E_{1}
-page is naturally isomorphic to
\operatorname{Cyc}(H(A);H(M))
. The same holds for
(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})
, so since the maps of complexes
(A,M)\to(A^{\prime},M^{\prime})
induce isomorphisms on cohomology, they already induce an isomorphism on the
E_{1}
-pages of the spectral sequence. These spectral sequences converge since
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;M)
is bounded-below, so a map inducing an isomorphism on
E_{1}
is a quasi-isomorphism.
∎

We will use Connes’ cyclic rotation operator on the tensor algebra.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
V
be any graded
R
-module. Then the tensor algebra
T(V)
has a natural automorphism

\sigma\colon(v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n})=(-1)^{(|v_{1}|+\ldots+|v_{n-1}|)\cdot|v_{n}|}v_{n}\otimes v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{n-1}.

We call this the
cycle
operator.

In particular,
\sigma
is defined on the linear Bar construction
\operatorname{Bar}(A)=T(\mathbf{s}^{-1}\bar{A})
.

Observation 2.7
.

The cycle operator on
\operatorname{Bar}(A)
is
not
an automorphism of the chain complex, as it does not commute with the Bar differential.
However, the submodule
\operatorname{Bar}(A)^{\sigma}
of
\sigma
-invariant tensors happens to be preserved by the Bar differential and is thus a subcomplex. The reader should keep in mind that, while
\operatorname{Bar}(A)
is a quasi-isomorphism invariant of
A
, the subcomplex
\operatorname{Bar}(A)^{\sigma}
is not, and may even exhibit nontrivial cohomology in strictly negative cohomological degrees.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
A
be an augmented homologically connected dg-algebra without
R
-torsion. The augmentation sequence
0\to\bar{A}\to A\to R\to 0
induces a long exact sequence in cyclic Bar cohomology

(5)

0\to H^{0}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A))\to H^{0}(\operatorname{Bar}(A))\overset{d}{\to}H^{1}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})\to\mathrm{H}^{1}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A))\to\ldots

whose connecting homomorphism

d\colon H^{i}(Bar(A))\to H^{i+1}(Cyc(A;\bar{A}))

is
d=\iota\circ(\sigma-1)
.

Proof.

First, by quasi-isomorphism invariance of the cyclic complexes, we may assume
A
is a connected algebra, so
\bar{A}
is concentrated in strictly positive degrees. Consequently, the three complexes
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;M)
for
M=R,A,
and
\bar{A}
are trivial in strictly negative degrees.

The augmentation induces a short exact sequence of cyclic chain complexes

0\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A;A)\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A;R)\to 0,

which in turn gives a long exact sequence in cohomology

\ldots\to H^{i}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A))\to H^{i}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A;R))\xrightarrow{d}H^{i+1}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})\to H^{i+1}(\operatorname{Cyc}(A))\to\ldots.

By Example
2.3
, the complex
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;R)
is the same as the linear Bar complex of
A
. Furthermore, the complex
\operatorname{Cyc}(A;\bar{A})
is concentrated in strictly positive degrees, and thus has vanishing
0
-th cohomology.

It remains to compute the connecting homomorphism. By definition

\displaystyle d\sum_{i=1}^{N}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n_{i}}]=d_{\operatorname{Cyc}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}1[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n_{i}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{1}[a_{2}|\ldots|a_{n_{i}}]+1\otimes d_{\operatorname{Bar}}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n_{i}}]+(-1)^{\epsilon_{n_{i}-1}(|a_{n_{i}}|-1)}a_{n_{i}}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n_{i}-1}].

But since this operator is only applied to cocycles in the Bar complex, the
d_{\operatorname{Bar}}

\iota\circ(\sigma-1)
. The sign on the
\sigma

\mathbf{s}^{-1}a_{i}

|a_{i}|-1
.
∎

Consider the simplicial cochain algebra of a wedge of circles

Lemma 2.11
.

The natural injection
\tau\colon\operatorname{Bar}(A)\to\operatorname{Cyc}(A)

[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]\mapsto 1[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]

For

with the two extremal terms landing in

, where they happen to lie in the image of the isomorphism

In fact, we have

\iota(\sigma-1)[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n}]=-a_{1}[a_{2}|\ldots|a_{n}]+(-1)^{\epsilon_{n-1}(|a_{n}|-1)}a_{n}[a_{1}|\ldots|a_{n-1}]

so that overall there is the relation

In particular, since
\sigma
-invariant tensors vanish on

∎

In light of this,

First, Example

The analogous definition with

Longitudinal assessment of transcranial Doppler imaging in children with sickle cell disease without neurological symptoms
Authors: Akram M. Asbeutah, Saad A. Asbeutah, Akmal Zahra, Abdullah A. AlMajran, Adekunle Adekile
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
IntroductionStroke is one of the most devastating complications of sickle cell disease (SCD). Transcranial Doppler Imaging (TCDI) is the least invasive screening method to predict patients at risk for developing stroke in the disease. After a 10-year follow-up, we longitudinally assessed the TCDI in children with SCD without neurological symptoms.Methods25 out of 43 pediatric patients with SCD studied 10-year previously were recruited. The remaining 18 patient were not available for follow-up, but their initial data are presented for comparison. TCDI scanning was carried out using a phased-array transducer of 1–3 MHz through the trans-temporal window. Peak systolic velocity (PSV), end-diastolic velocity (EDV), time-averaged mean of the maximum velocity (TAMMV), resistive index (RI), and pulsatility index (PI) were obtained in the anterior and posterior Circle of Willis vessels.ResultsThe highest initial and follow-up TAMMV (mean ± SD) were: 77.3 ± 20.9 and 71.6 ± 9.9 in the t-ICA, 94.3 ± 25.8 and 82 ± 18.2 in the MCA, 76.6 ± 25.6 and 70.6 ± 10.7 in the ACA, and 59.1 ± 15.8 and 63.9 ± 8.5 in the PCA, respectively. There was no statistically significant difference between initial and follow-up SCD data for all vascular parameters in all vessels on each side (P > 0.05) except for RI and PI (P < 0.05). There was significant correlation between TAMMV, PSV, and EDV (P = 0.001).ConclusionThere are no absolute Doppler velocity changes between the initial and follow-up period over the years. There is a possibility that PSV and EDV could be used in parallel with TAMMV Subclinical vascular degeneration is not suggested by these vascular measures.

Introduction

One prevalent genetic blood abnormality that causes many complications is sickle cell disease (SCD) [1, 2]. The hallmark of SCD is a progressive vasculopathy brought on by free heme, which activates platelets, neutrophils, reticulocytes, and endothelial cells [3, 4]. Vasoconstriction, the overexpression of adhesion molecules, and the proliferation of endothelium intima result from subsequent nitric oxide depletion [5, 6]. SCD patients are, therefore, more vulnerable to many complications, such as leg ulcers, chronic lung disease, and stroke [7]. Before the age of 20 years, 11% of the patients will experience a stroke if no intervention is put in place [8]. The large vessels of the Circle of Willis, particularly the internal carotid, the middle, and anterior cerebral arteries, are the usual sites of stenosis.

There are multiple medical imaging modalities to visualize the site of cerebral infarction, including computed tomography, magnetic resonance imaging, radionuclide scan, and ultrasound. Since stroke tends to be recurrent in SCD there is a need for primary and secondary prevention by identifying patients at risk [8, 9]. Ultrasound is considered the imaging modality of choice as it is non-invasive, cheap, reliable, reproducible, and requires less time to operate [10–19]. During TCDI, velocity recording is impacted by systemic parameters such as cardiac contractibility, heart rate, and total peripheral resistance [20]. TAMMV correlates better with perfusion and is less affected by these systemic variables, it is the vascular parameter utilized to detect stroke risk in patients with SCD. Adams et al. set the STOP guidelines for screening patients with SCD at risk of developing stroke for proper management [10, 11]. In transcranial Doppler non-imaging (TCDNI), a time-averaged maximum mean velocity (TAMMV) of > 200 cm/s is considered a high risk for stroke. In comparison, those below 170 cm/s are normal, and values in between are conditional. However, transcranial Doppler imaging (TCDI) produces ~ 10% lower velocity measurements than TCDNI [12, 13]. A study by Jones et al. [14] revealed that the peak systolic velocity (PSV) might rule out stroke as TAMMV, but with other velocity cutoffs, where PSV of 250 cm/s is abnormal and high risk for developing stroke and 200–250 cm/s considered conditional, with such patients requiring more surveillance. Furthermore, a study by Naffa et al. [15] suggested that PSV measurements can be used with TAMMV for TCDNI or TCDI screening to detect stroke risk in SCD patients. Reference ranges for blood flow ratios and inter-hemispheric variations of Doppler parameters in children with SCD have been recorded by numerous researchers using either TCDNI [16] or TCDI [17]. Other studies focused on clinical and laboratory correlates for the elevated flow velocities [18], and others focused on low velocities [19] associated with stroke in children with SCD.

Kuwaiti patients with SCD consistently show high levels of HbF and uniformly carry the Arab/India (A/I) haplotype. (21–22) Similar to other Arab Gulf patients with SCD, stroke is comparatively rare in these patients [23]. It was observed in our previously published studies [24–27] that none of the patients with SCD had a TAMMV above 200 cm/s in any of the intracerebral vessels.

Therefore, the following questions arise: Are there any long-term intracranial blood flow changes, and whether the other vascular parameters have changed in the intervening years in the same group of patients with SCD? have the velocities increased due to cumulative chronic vasculopathy, remained unchanged or, indeed, lower than in the first study, due to the effect of aging [28]? The present longitudinal study was designed to study the cerebral blood flow patterns and the relationship between the different vascular parameters in 25 out of 43 pediatric patients with SCD studied 10-years previously. The remaining 18 patients could not be re-examined, but their initial data are presented for comparison.

Patients and methods

Patients

This was a prospective cohort study of patients initially identified in our previous observational study in 2013 [24]. The parents’ phone numbers were used to contact the patients again. The research was carried out from October 2022 to November 2023. The patients attended the pediatric and adult hematology outpatient clinics in the Hospital in Kuwait. They were studied in steady state, i.e., no acute illness, crisis, or blood transfusion within three months of the study [28]. The study was approved by the Human Ethics Committee of the Health Science Centre (VDR/EC-3988) and the Kuwait Ministry of Health (6355/1882–2021). An informed, signed consent and/or assent was obtained from the parents and/or patients as appropriate.

Blood tests

The clinic nurse used ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid (EDTA) tubes to collect blood through venipuncture. Complete blood count (CBC) was carried out using ABX Pentra 120 cell counter to measure the hemoglobin (g/dl), platelets (x109/L), hematocrit (%), white blood cells (109/L), absolute neutrophil count (ANC, 109/L), and reticulocytes (%). Cation-exchange high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) was used to confirm the hemoglobin (Hb) genotype and the hemoglobin F (HbF) concentration. Serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH, IU/L) and bilirubin (µmol/L) were measured using routine techniques.

Transcranial Doppler Imaging (TCDI)

Every patient had TCDI, which was carried out through the temporal window utilizing a color, power, and pulsed Doppler-equipped ultrasound equipment (GE, Vivid 3, USA) with a 1–3 MHz phased array transducer. The terminal internal carotid artery (t-ICA-C5), middle cerebral artery (MCA-1), anterior cerebral artery (ACA-1), and posterior cerebral artery (PCA-1) were all insonated through the temporal window (Fig. 1, A) while the patient was lying supine on the ultrasound table at a standard room temperature (22 ± 2 °C). Angle adjustment was not applied. An envelope was made that showed the maximum velocity throughout three to five cardiac cycles. The waveform’s envelope was either manually or electronically tracked. The highest cerebral blood flow velocity was recorded at an insonation sample gate size diameter of 4–6 mm. The following data were acquired and stored in the ultrasound machine memory: flow direction, peak systolic velocity (PSV), end-diastolic velocity (EDV), time-averaged mean of the maximum velocity (TAMMV), resistive index (RI), and pulsatility index (PI) (Fig. 1, B). PSV is the highest velocity noted in systole, while EDV is the maximum velocity immediately before the subsequent waveform’s acceleration phase. The area under the curve is TAMMV. The PSV-EDV/PSV formula automatically determined the RI value. The Doppler trace was used to determine PI using the PSV-EDV/MV (mean velocity) formula. Three measurements from each side of the cerebral hemisphere were made for each measurement for each vessel. PSV, EDV, TAMMV, RI, and PI mean values were noted. The TCDI procedure was performed by the same vascular sonographer (AA) who performed the initial scan with > 20 years of vascular sonography imaging experience and studied patients with SCD for > 10 years. When the follow-up was conducted, he was likewise blinded to the prior results.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS) version 27 for Windows (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA). The statistical significance of variations in the mean values for all vessels between the initial and follow-up data in patients with SCD was examined using the paired t-test. Moreover, a paired difference test of repeated measurements on a single sample was performed using the Wilcoxon signed rank test to determine if the population mean ranks of the two samples differed. TAMMV and the other vascular parameters for each vessel on each side for repeated measurements in initial and follow-up patients with SCD were correlated using Pearson’s correlation coefficients. A P value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Out of the 43 patients in the first study, 25 (15 HbSS, 9 HbSβ0 Thal, and 1 HbSD) were successfully recruited for the present study. Eighteen patients (8 HbSS, 8 HbSβ0 Thal, and 2 HbSD) were not available to be studied but their initial data are presented for comparison. Among the latter, 7 were away at college, 6 had updated their phone numbers, and 5 did not provide consent. The available 25 patients had an initial mean age of 6.5 ± 1.4 years at the time of TCDI, and at the follow-up scan, their mean age was 16.0 ± 1.62 years (P < 0.001). The follow-up period was 7 to 10 years, with a mean of 10.3 ± 3.2 years. There were 12 males (48%) and 13 females (52%). Between 2013 and 2023, hydroxyurea (HU) was administered to all patients; however, the exact start date of HU treatment was not recorded. All patients were in hydroxyurea at the time of the initial TCDI study and at the time of follow-up TCDI study. The hydroxyurea dose varied from 500 to 1000 mg/day and the dose is calculated as 15 to 30 mg/kg.

For the 18 patients who were not followed up, their initial mean age was 6.56 ± 1.58 years and would have been 16.61 ± 1.54 years old now, which is similar to the group of 25 in the present study. There were 11 males (61%) and 7 females (39%).

The patients’ mean total Hb, hematocrit, and bilirubin were lower than in the initial data study but not statistically significant (P > 0.05). Moreover, patients’ mean HbF, platelets, and ANC counts were higher than the initial study data, but again not statistically significant (P > 0.05). The mean LDH, WBC, and reticulocyte count showed similar results to the initial study data but and there were no significant differences between both initial and follow-up data (P > 0.05). Discussion

The relative rarity of neurological problems among patients with SCD in the Arabian Gulf is being confirmed by multiple studies. This is especially true for patients who have an elevated HbF level and the AI haplotype, where the stroke prevalence is < 1.0%.23,30,31 Furthermore, investigations from Kuwait and other Gulf nations have repeatedly demonstrated that cerebral blood flow is scarcely hindered, with very little prevalence of abnormal or conditional TAMMV [24–27, 31]. This shows that these patients’ cerebral vasculopathy is not rapidly progressive. Interestingly, research from Oman [32], Iran, (33–34) and Iraq [24], where this haplotype is not very frequent, also indicate reasonably normal TCDI indices, suggesting that this sparing of the cerebral arteries is not exclusive to patients with the AI haplotype. Consequently, this group may be immune to neurological events for other unknown reasons.

The literature has few retrospective and a case series investigation of TCDI among patients with SCD [15, 18, 19]. Also, since we do not have any patients with stenosis or stroke in our previously published studies as all the patients were on hydroxyurea, which could justify the results [24–27]. The analysis in this study focused on the relationship between TAMMV and the other individual Doppler indices such as PSV, EDV, PI, and RI in patients with SCD. The analysis in the present study is different than in our previously published studies in a way we included a longitudinal assessment of 25 cases out of 43 cases followed-up in nearly 10 years gap [25, 26].

Although MRI perfusion arterial spinning labelling (ASL) technique is widely used and does not require the use of contrast media and gives more accurate results than TCDI, it has many disadvantages as it is time consuming, costly, and it does not provide hemodynamic of blood flow, and patients might feel claustrophobic. A study concluded that TCDI and MRI modalities provide complementary resting cerebral blood flow measures, with similar effects across the whole cohort and between subgroups (age/fitness) if metrics are comparable (e.g., velocity [TCD] versus transit time [MRI]) [35]. Hence it was not used in the present study to detect arterial stenosis or occlusion. This study used the TCDI approach, with one ultrasound operator blinded to initial data from the past, without angle correction, and with the patient in a stable state. The cerebral vasculature was scanned immediately while the Doppler waveform was acquired to guarantee a consistent result. Furthermore, this improves the accuracy of measuring the other vascular parameters compared to TCDNI. Furthermore, in order to identify the vessel of interest and guarantee the identification of any anomalies, TCDNI calls for a higher level of operator experience.

During TCDI, velocity recording is impacted by systemic parameters such as cardiac contractibility, heart rate, and total peripheral resistance [35]. TAMMV correlates better with perfusion and is less affected by these systemic variables, it is the vascular parameter utilized to detect stroke risk in patients with SCD. Other researchers have explored the prospect of using other indices to forecast the risk of stroke. PSV was among the metrics that shows a strong correlation with vascular stenosis or lumen diameter reduction [36]. Using colour Doppler imaging techniques, Seibert et al. [37] computed TAMMV in several arteries, but they were unable to determine a maximum velocity that was both specific and sensitive for the diagnosis of cerebral infarction. Because of this, the STOP [10, 11] trial data were re-examined in 2005 to see if another reliable, comparable vascular parameter that could be used for TCDI in the identification of arterial stenosis as compared to our previous study [26].

The findings in the present study are interesting in that uniformly, the TAMMV and other indices showed decreases in the follow-up group compared to the values in the initial study. The findings are in good agreement with previous studies of TAMMV [16, 17, 38. None of the patients in our study had a TAMMV exceeding 200 cm/s, which would be considered abnormal in accordance with the recommendations of Adams et al. [10, 11] or exceeding 180 cm/s according to the McCarville et al. [12]. Moreover, we examined PSV instead of TAMMV as described previously by Jones et al. [14] and applied by Naffa et al. [15] Despite our results showing a direct relationship between TAMMV, PSV, and EDV, mean PSV did not reach above 140 cm/s in any of the vessels in the initial study, while the lowest PSV recorded was 82 cm/s. Meanwhile, in the follow-up patients with SCD, the highest PSV was 127 cm/s, and the lowest was 83 cm/s (Table 2). We attributed the normal TCDI findings in this study to several factors. Firstly, even in the normal population, TAMMV decreases with age [28] and in our previous studies [24–27], we found a negative correlation between the two variables. Therefore, it was not surprising that TAMMV decreased over the 10-year interval in the patients in whom we succeeded in repeating the study. Silent brain infarcts (SBI) are rare in our children under the age of twelve. However, according to our earlier studies, they are prevalent in adult Kuwaiti patients (~ 20%).24,38,39 Contrary to reports from American patients, SBI is uncommon beyond the age of 16 and common in early childhood. We have interpreted this as effective protection from cerebral vasculopathy, which is provided by elevated HbF levels at the critical period of 2–3 years of age when vasculopathy is established. Hence, the peak incidence of ischemic stroke in susceptible patients is between the ages of 7 and 10 years [8]. However, before the age of 3 years, most of our patients have HbF levels of 20 ->30%,40 thus inhibiting the development of significant vasculopathy. The relative severity of SCD among our adult patients and the higher prevalence of SBI are due to the cumulative low-level chronic inflammation [29, 39, 40, 41].

Secondly, there has been no significant vasculopathy that would produce stenosis and associated increased TAMMV because SCD among Kuwaiti patients is relatively mild, mainly because their HbS mutation is on chromosomes with the Saudi Arabia/India β-globin gene haplotype with elevated HbF levels [21, 30, 42]. Finally, the other possible factor is the effect of hydroxyurea, which has been associated with decreasing TAMMV and is useful in preventing primary stroke as an alternative to chronic transfusion therapy [43–45]. Moreover, the increase in the HbF level between the 2 studies could be attributed to the hydroxyurea effect.

In the present study, the correlation between TAMMV and PSV and EDV was significant and consistent, but the correlation between TAMMV and RI and PI are not that impressive or consistent (Table 3). In addition, there was inverse relationship between PSV, EDV, and TAMMV versus RI and PI. Inverse relationship between PSV/EDV and PI and RI. That means the PSV/EDV is positively related while PI/RI is negatively related when correlated together. This means that when PSV or EDV is high then PI/RI are low and vice versa. Therefore, PSV, EDV, PI and RI could be of value in the evaluation of stroke risk in patients with SCD. This might indicate an underlying intracranial vasculopathy in a patient with SCD. This is important in understanding the situation among our patients who generally have normal or low TAMMV values.

The major limitations of our study are the small sample size, given the relative underutilization of sickle cell services and the difficulty of follow-up. A larger prospective study correlating TCDI indices with magnetic resonance imaging/ angiography (MRI/MRA) findings, as well as neurological and psychological (patients with SCD have phycological episode in their lives) examination results could provide or delineate the age at which evidence of cerebral ischemia/perfusion defects develops. It will also, important to determine the significance of very low TAMMV values (≤50 cm/s) as previously reported [19] since we do not have many patients with abnormally elevated values.

Conclusion

There are no absolute Doppler velocity changes between the initial and follow-up period over the years. There is a possibility that PSV and EDV could be used in parallel with TAMMV. Subclinical vascular degeneration is not suggested by these vascular measures.

Further research is needed to explore this issue and determine the relationship between other vascular parameters like RI and PI in a large group size patient with SCD.

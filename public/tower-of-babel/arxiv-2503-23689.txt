1.
Introduction

The prescribed curvature problems have a long and extensive history. For instance, the celebrated Nirenberg’s problem seeks a conformal metric on the standard sphere

(\mathbb{S}^{2},g_{0})
such that a prescribed function

K(x)
serves as the Gaussian curvature of the conformal metric

g=e^{2u}g_{0}
. Nirenberg’s problem has garnered significant attention, leading to a wealth of results documented in works such as
[
7
,
8
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
27
]
among others. In higher dimensions, the prescribed Q-curvature problem on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
has been explored in
[
3
,
24
,
28
]
, with additional insights into Q-curvature provided in
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the prescribed Q-curvature problem on complete non-compact manifolds, with the Euclidean space serving as the classical model. More specifically, we consider the existence of complete conformal metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Q-curvature equal to a given function. In terms of differential equations, we study the following equation:

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u(x)=Q(x)e^{nu(x)},\quad\text{on}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
n\geq 2
is an even integer and
Q(x)
is a given function that serves as the Q-curvature of the conformal metric
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
. For the case
n=2
, this problem has been extensively studied by Aviles
[
1
]
, McOwen
[
25
]
, Cheng-Lin
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
and many others. For
n\geq 4
, several existence results for equation (
1.1
) have been established by Chang-Chen
[
5
]
, Wei-Ye
[
29
]
, Hyder, Mancini and Martinazzi
[
16
]
, Hyder and Martinazzi
[
17
]
, Huang-Ye-Zhou
[
15
]
and many others. See
[
6
]
for further properties of complete metrics associated with Q-curvature on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that the conformal metric
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
with finite total Q-curvature if
Qe^{nu}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

For prescribed Q-curvature problem on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, our result is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Given a smooth function
f(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which is positive somewhere and satisfies
f(x)=O(|x|^{-l})

where
l>\frac{n}{2}
and
n
is an even integer. Then there exists a complete and conformal metric
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
with finite total Q-curvature such that its Q-curvature equals to
f
.

We should remark that the completeness condition plays an important role here. If completeness is not assumed, the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) has been established in Theorem 2.1 of
[
5
]
for any function
f(x)
that is positive somewhere and satisfies the decay condition
f(x)=O(|x|^{-l})
with
l>0
. However, the situation becomes significantly different when we require a complete metric. In
[
23
]
, certain obstructions to the prescribed Q-curvature problem are identified for complete and conformal metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite total Q-curvature. Specifically, when

f(x)
is positive, the result can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Theorem 1.5 in
[
23
]
)
Given a positive and smooth function
f(x)
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
n\geq 2
is an even integer and
f(x)
satisfies

(1.2)

\frac{x\cdot\nabla f(x)}{f(x)}\geq-\frac{n}{2}.

Then there is no complete conformal metric
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite total Q-curvature such that its Q-curvature eqauls to
f
.

Condition (
1.2
) implies that
f(x)\geq C|x|^{-\frac{n}{2}}
for
|x|\gg 1
. Consequently, when the decay rate of

f(x)
is slower than
|x|^{-\frac{n}{2}}
, there are obstructions to complete metrics with finite total Q-curvature. Therefore, the result demonstrated in Theorem
1.1
is sharp, in view of the decay rate.

We briefly outline our strategy. We employ the variational method to find a solution to the prescribed
Q-curvature equation (
1.1
) under an appropriate prescribed total Q-curvature, following the approach developed in
[
25
]
. In contrast to the two-dimensional case, our analysis requires a weighted Moser-Trudinger-Adams type inequality. Subsequently, by utilizing the distance growth identity established in
[
22
]
, we demonstrate that, under the assumption of a specific decay rate, we can construct a complete metric.

In a recent paper
[
15
]
, Huang, Ye, and Zhou proved the existence of conformal metrics on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with prescribed non-positive Q-curvature. Their argument is based on Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem. In contrast, we require Q-curvature to be positive at some point and use the variational method.

The structure of this paper goes naturally. In section
2
, we discuss the function spaces that are used in our variational approach. In section
3
, we state and present a proof for a weighted Moser-Trudinger-Admas type inequality. Then in section
4
, we finish our proof of Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Function spaces

In this section, we introduce the function space that fits our variational scheme.

Let
g_{0}=|dx|^{2}
be the standard Euclidean metric. Let
B_{1}(0)

be a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
g_{1}=e^{2w}g_{0}
be a conformal
metric where
w
is a smooth function on
B_{1}\backslash\{0\}
.
We denote for
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))
and
i=0,1
,

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{k,p}(B_{1}(0),g_{i})}:=\sum_{l=0}^{k}|\int_{B_{1}(0)\backslash\{0\}}|\nabla_{g_{i}}^{l}u|^{p}dV_{g_{i}}|^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We denote
W_{0}^{k,p}(B_{1}(0),g_{i})
to be the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1}(0))

under
\|\cdot\|_{W_{0}^{k,p}(B_{1}(0),g_{i})}
.

The following well known Hardy-Sobolev inequality is very useful to compare function
spaces with different norms.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Hardy-Sobolev inequality) Let
p>0
be a positive number. Then, for
u\in W_{0}^{k,p}(B_{1})
with
kp<n
,
we have

(2.1)

\left\|\frac{u}{|x|^{k}}\right\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq\|\nabla^{k}u\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}.

By Hardy-Sobolev inequality, we can show the equivalence of
W^{\frac{n}{2},2}
spaces.

Corollary 2.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
w=(\alpha-1)\log|x|+h

where
h
is a smooth function in
\overline{B_{1}(0)}
. Let
g_{1}=e^{2w}|dx|^{2}
.
Then, we have

W_{0}^{\frac{n}{2},2}(B_{1}(0),g_{1})\simeq W_{0}^{\frac{n}{2},2}(B_{1}(0)).

Proof.

We only need to check for function
u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{1})
.
We have

e^{w}\nabla_{g_{1}}u=\nabla u,\ e^{2w}\nabla_{g_{1}}^{2}u=\nabla^{2}u+\langle\nabla u,\nabla w\rangle g_{0}-\nabla u\otimes\nabla w-\nabla w\otimes\nabla u.

For higher order derivatives,

e^{kw}\nabla_{g_{1}}^{k}u=\nabla^{k}u+\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\nabla^{l}u*\Phi_{k-l}(\nabla w,\cdots,\nabla^{k-l}w),

where
\Phi_{k-l}
is a polynomial of covariant derivatives of
w
.

*
means certain linear combinations. The explicit expression of

\Phi_{k-l}
will not be used, but by induction, we have estimates

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\Phi_{k-l}|\leq C|x|^{-(k-l)}.

For
k\in\{0,\cdots,\frac{n}{2}\}
, since
h
is smooth in
\overline{B_{1}(0)}
,
there exists a constant
C_{1}=C_{1}(n,w)
such that
e^{(n-2k)w}\leq C_{1}|x|^{2k-n}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla_{g_{1}}^{k}u|^{2}dV_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(0)}|e^{kw}\nabla_{g_{1}}^{k}u|^{2}e^{(n-2k)w}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\int_{B_{1}(0)}\left(|\nabla^{k}u|^{2}+\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}|\nabla^{l}u|^{2}|x|^{-2(k-l)}\right)|x|^{2k-n}dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\int_{B_{1}(0)}|\nabla^{\frac{n}{2}}u|^{2}dx.

Here
C_{2},C_{3}
are constants depending on
n,k,w
and we used Hardy-Sobolev inequality (
2.1
) in the last line.
Thus,

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{\frac{n}{2},2}(B_{1}(0),g_{1})}\leq C(n,p,h)\|u\|_{W_{0}^{\frac{n}{2},2}(B_{1}(0))}.

The other direction follows from the Poincaré inequality.
∎

The discussion above can be applied to function spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with suitable weighted norms. Let
\epsilon>0
. Consider a positive and smooth function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\varphi=|x|^{-n(1+\epsilon)}
for
|x|>1
.
Let
\gamma=\frac{1}{n}\log\varphi
. Then

e^{2\gamma}=|x|^{-2-2\epsilon},

for
|x|>1
. We denote

(2.3)

\displaystyle d\mu(x)=e^{n\gamma}dx.

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the standard stereographic projection
from the north pole
x_{N}
. Let
g_{1}=\sigma^{*}\left(e^{2\gamma}|dx|^{2}\right)
.
Then
g_{1}
is a smooth metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
away from the north pole.
At the north pole,
g_{1}
has a cone singularity if
\epsilon>0
. To see that, we
take
y=\frac{x}{|x|^{2}}.
Then in
|y|<\frac{1}{2}
,
we have

(2.4)

e^{2\gamma}|dx|^{2}=|y|^{2(\epsilon-1)}|dy|^{2}

in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0).
This implies that
g_{1}
has a cone singularity
of index
\epsilon-1
at the north pole.

Definition 2.3
.

Notations as above. Fix
\epsilon>0
, for
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let

(2.5)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}^{2}:=\sum_{j=0}^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{(n-2j)\gamma}|\nabla^{j}u|^{2}dx.

Define a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}:=\overline{\{u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}<\infty\}},

where the closure is taken under
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
norm.

We should remark that the function space
\mathcal{H}
does not depend on the constant
\epsilon>0
. In fact, we will identify
\mathcal{H}

with
W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})
where
g_{S}
is the standard
metric on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. In order to be consistent with discussion in later sections, we denote

\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}d\mu.

Since
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
is equivalent to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
, this notation will not cause any problem.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\leq C\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

As a result, we have

W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})\simeq W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})\simeq(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}})\simeq(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}).

Proof.

We use
\sim
to denote the equivalence of norms. By Corollary
2.2
and a partition of unity argument,
we have
\|u\|_{W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})}\sim\|u\|_{W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})}\sim\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
. Thus,

W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})\simeq W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})\simeq(\mathcal{H},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}).

Clearly
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\geq\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. It is well known (See
[
2
]
,
[
9
]
) that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uP_{g_{S}}udV_{g_{S}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{g_{S}})}^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u|^{2}dx+\|u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{g_{S}})}^{2}.

So it remains to show that
\|u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{g_{S}})}^{2}\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

We claim that there exists some constant
C
such
that the following Poincaré type inequality holds

(2.6)

\|u-\bar{u}\|_{L^{2}(dV_{g_{S}})}^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uP_{g_{S}}udV_{g_{S}}\right),

where
\bar{u}=\text{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}udV_{g_{1}}
.
We argue by contradiction. Suppose there exists a sequence of
u_{i}\in\mathcal{H}

such that

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{i}P_{g_{S}}u_{i}dV_{g_{S}}\to 0,\ \int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{i}dV_{g_{1}}=0,\ \int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{i}^{2}dV_{g_{S}}=1.

Then, as
u_{i}
is abounded in
W^{2,\frac{n}{2}}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})
, there exists a weakly
convergent subsequence
u_{j}\rightharpoonup u_{\infty}
and
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}

strongly in
L^{2}
. Thus,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\infty}dV_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{\infty}^{2}dV_{g_{S}}

\displaystyle=1,

(2.9)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\sqrt{P_{g_{S}}}u_{\infty}|^{2}dV_{g_{S}}\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}u_{i}P_{g_{S}}u_{i}dV_{g_{S}}=0.

It must follow that
u_{\infty}\equiv c
. From (
2.7
),

u_{\infty}=0
which contradicts to (
2.8
). We have proved the claim.

Now, from (
2.6
) we have

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(dV_{g_{S}})}\leq C(\bar{u}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uP_{g_{S}}udV_{g_{S}})\leq C\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}.

We have finished the proof.
∎

With Lemma
2.4
, we will work on space
\mathcal{H}

with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Other equivalent
norms will be used under different circumstances.
A function
u\in\mathcal{H}
will be considered as a function on

\mathbb{R}^{n}
or a function
\sigma^{*}u
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

3.
A weighted Moser-Trudinger-Admas type inequality

Morser-Trudinger-Adams inequalities are fundamental analytic devices in studies of Q-curvature. See, for instance, Branson-Chang-Yang
[
2
]
, Chang-Yang
[
9
]
, Fontana
[
13
]
. A weighted version was first proved by Lam-Lu
[
21
]
, and
was used in Fang-Ma
[
12
]
to study constant Q-curvature metrics with conical singularities. In this section, we state and prove a weighted Morser-Trudinger-Adams type inequality that will be used in our main theorem. Such a weighted inequality is well known to experts in the field, but we have not found a precise one in the literature except in dimension 4. So, for the convenience of the readers, we take this opportunity to present a complete proof.

Let
\nabla_{S}
be the standard Levi-Civita connection on round sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\Delta_{S}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
P_{g_{S}}
be the Panteiz operator.
Let
m=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{4}\right\rfloor
and

\square u:=\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{S})^{\frac{n}{4}}u,&n=4m,\\
\nabla_{S}\left((-\Delta_{S})^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u\right),&n=4m+2.\end{cases}

The relevant weighted Moser-Trudinger-Admas type inequality states the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{H}
. Let
\bar{u}=\frac{1}{\text{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}udV_{g_{1}}
. We have

(3.1)

\log\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{n|u-\bar{u}|}e^{n\gamma}dV_{S}\leq C+\frac{n}{2(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\min\{\epsilon,1\}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}|\square u|^{2}dV_{S}.

For the main application to prescribing Q-curvature problem, we also need the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C=C(n,\gamma)
such that

(3.2)

\log\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{n|u-\bar{u}|}dV_{g_{1}}\leq C+\frac{n}{2(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\min\{\epsilon,1\}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}uP_{g_{S}}udV_{S}.

On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(3.3)

\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{n|u-\bar{u}|}e^{n\gamma}dx\leq C+\frac{n}{2(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\min\{\epsilon,1\}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u|^{2}dx.

The rest of the section is devoted to prove Theorem
3.1
. We first state a few technical lemmas.

Let
G_{k}(x,y)
be the Green function of
(-\Delta_{S})^{k}
on

\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then we have

u(x)-u_{S}=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}G_{m}(x,y)\square u(y)dV_{S},&n=4m,\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{S}G_{m+1}(x,y)\cdot\square u(y)dV_{S},&n=4m+2.\end{cases}

Here
u_{S}=|\mathbb{S}^{n}|^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}udV_{S}
. By
[
13
, Lemma 2.6, 2.7]
, we have the following expansion for Green function:

Lemma 3.2
.

Notations as above. Let
d(\cdot,\cdot)

be the distance function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with
g_{S}
.

(1)

For
k={1,...,\frac{n}{2}-1}
,

G_{k}(x,y)=-c_{m}\chi d(x,y)^{2k-n}(1+O(d(x,y)^{\frac{1}{2}}))+R(x,y),

where
c_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-k)}{2^{2k-1}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(k)|S^{n-1}|}
,

R
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
, and
\chi(x,y)

equals
1
in a neighborhood of the diagonal of
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}

and equals
0
when
d(x,y)>\frac{1}{4}
.

(2)

If
n=4m+2
, then

(3.4)

|\nabla_{S}G_{m+1}(x,y)|\leq c_{m}^{\prime}\chi d(x,y))^{-\frac{n}{2}}(1+O(d(x,y)^{\frac{1}{2}}))+R_{1}(x,y),

where
c_{m}^{\prime}=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-m)}{2^{2m}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(m+1)|S^{n-1}|}
,

R_{1}
is a smooth function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n}
.

Remark 3.3
.

If
n=4m
, then
c_{m}=\frac{1}{2^{n/2-1}\Gamma(n/2)|S^{n-1}|}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}
.
If
n=4m+2
, we have
c_{m}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}.

From Green functions and their expansions, we can solve poly-harmonic equations and obtain standard
L^{p}
estimates which can be summarized in the following Lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q>1
. If
n=4m
, for
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})
,
there exists a function
g
in
W^{2m,q}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})

such that

(-\Delta_{S})^{m}g=f,

and
\|g\|_{L^{2}}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{q}}
for some positive constant
c=c(n,q)
.
If
n=4m+2
, for
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})
, there exists a function

g
in
W^{2(m+1),q}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})
such that

(-\Delta_{S})^{m+1}g=f,

and
\|\nabla_{S}g\|_{L^{2}}\leq c^{\prime}\|f\|_{L^{q}}
for some positive constant

c^{\prime}(n,q)
.

We also need the following estimate.

Lemma 3.5
.

Fix
x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Let
B_{\delta}(x)
be a
geodesic ball of radius
\delta<\frac{\pi}{2}
centered at
x
.
Let
r=d_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(x,y)
. Let
u\in\mathcal{H}
. We have

(3.5)

|u(x)-\bar{u}|\leq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{B_{\delta}(x)}r^{-\frac{n}{2}}|\square u(y)|dV_{S}+C\|\square u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}.

Here
C
is a constant depending only on
n,\gamma,\epsilon
.

Proof.

Let

\rho=\frac{e^{n\gamma}}{\text{vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{1})}-\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}.

Then
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\rho dV_{S}=0
and
\rho\in L^{q}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})

for
1<q<\frac{1}{1-\epsilon}
if
\epsilon<1
and
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n})

if
\epsilon\geq 1
. Let
\psi
be the solution to

\begin{cases}(-\Delta_{S})^{m}\psi=\rho,&\mathrm{if}\ n=4m,\\
(-\Delta_{S})^{m+1}\psi=\rho,&\mathrm{if}\ n=4m+2.\end{cases}

Then
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}
is bounded by
C(n,\gamma,\epsilon)

when
n=4m
.
\|\nabla_{S}\psi\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}
is bounded by

C(n,\gamma,\epsilon)
when
n=4m+2
. We have

u(x)-\overline{u}=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\left(G_{m}(x,y)-\psi(y)\right)\square u(y)dV_{S},&n=4m,\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\left(\nabla_{S}G_{m+1}(x,y)-\nabla_{S}\psi(y)\right)\cdot\square u(y)dV_{S},&n=4m+2.\end{cases}

If
n=4m
, by Lemma
3.4
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\psi(y)\square u(y)dV_{S}\right|\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}\|\square u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}.

If
n=4m+2
, by Lemma
3.4
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\nabla_{S}\psi(y)\cdot\square u(y)dV_{S}\right|\leq\|\nabla_{S}\psi\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}\|\square u\|_{L^{2}(dV_{S})}.

Then (
3.5
) follows from the expansion of
G_{m}
and
\nabla G_{m+1}

in Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Now, we state a weighted Moser-Trudinger-Adams inequality proved by

[
21
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(Lam-Lu)
.

Let
\beta\in(-1,0]
. Let
\Omega
be a open
set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with finite lebesgue measure. Then, there
exists a constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(p,\Omega)
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

supported in
\Omega
,

\int_{\Omega}\exp\left((1+\beta)b_{n}\frac{\left|I_{\frac{n}{2}}*f(x)\right|^{2}}{\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\right)|x|^{n\beta}dx\leq c_{0},

where
I_{\frac{n}{2}}*f(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\Omega}|x-y|^{-\frac{n}{2}}f(y)dy,

and
b_{n}=\frac{n(2\pi)^{n}}{|S^{n-1}|}
.

Combining arguments in
[
2
,
12
,
13
]
, we now
prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Let
f=\langle\square u(y),\square u(y)\rangle_{g_{S}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. We first
assume that

0<\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})}\leq 1.

Pick a small geodesic ball
B_{\delta}(x_{N})
centered at the north
pole
x_{N}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Using a normal coordinate at
x_{N}
,
we may write
g_{ij}(y)=\delta_{ij}+O(|y|^{2})
. Define

u_{\delta}(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}|x-y|^{-\frac{n}{2}}f(y)dy.

Then, from Lemma
3.5
, we have

(3.6)

|u(x)-\bar{u}|\leq u_{\delta}(x)+C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},dV_{S})}.

Choosing a smaller
\delta,
we may further assume that
\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\delta})}\leq 1
.
Let
\alpha=\min\{\epsilon,1\}
. From Theorem
3.2
,
we have

(3.7)

\int_{B_{\delta}(0)}\exp\left(\alpha b_{n}u_{\delta}^{2}\right)|y|^{n(\alpha-1)}dy\leq c_{0}.

From the metric expansion (
2.4
), (
3.6
), and (
3.7
),
we have

(3.8)

\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{N})}\exp\left(\alpha b_{n}|u-\bar{u}|^{2}\right)dV_{g_{1}}\leq c_{0}^{\prime}(n,\gamma).

For any non-constant function
v\in W^{\frac{n}{2},2}(\mathbb{S}^{n},g_{S})
,
we can apply (
3.8
) to
\frac{v}{\|\square v\|_{L^{2}}}
.
By mean value inequality, we have

(3.9)

\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{N})}\exp\left(\alpha b_{n}\frac{|v-\bar{v}|^{2}}{\|\square v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}+\frac{n^{2}}{4b_{n}\alpha}\cdot\|\square v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)dV_{g_{1}}\geq\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{N})}\exp\left(n|v-\bar{v}|\right)dV_{g_{1}}.

We apply (
3.9
) to
u
to have

(3.10)

\int_{B_{\delta}(x_{N})}\exp\left(n|u-\bar{u}|\right)dV_{g_{1}}\leq c_{0}^{\prime}\exp\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4b_{n}\alpha}\cdot\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\text{vol}(B_{\delta}(x_{N}),g_{1}).

The metric
g_{1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash B_{\delta/2}(x_{N})
is
uniformly equivalent to
g_{S}
. Thus, we may apply the standard
Moser-Trudinger-Adams inequality proved by Fontana
[
13
]
on manifolds which shows

(3.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash B_{\delta/2}}\exp(n|u-\bar{u}|)dV_{g_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n,\gamma)\exp\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4b_{n}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

By (
3.10
), (
3.11
), and a partition of unity argument,
we have

(3.12)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\exp(n|u-\bar{u}|)dV_{g_{1}}\leq C(n,\gamma)\exp\left(\frac{n^{2}}{4b_{n}\alpha}\cdot\|\square u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Note
\frac{n^{2}}{4b_{n}\alpha}=\frac{n}{2(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\alpha}
.
Thus, we have proved the theorem.
∎

4.
Proof of the main theorem

Denote
d\mu=h(x)dx
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
h(x)
is a positive and smooth function and satisfies

(4.1)

C^{-1}|x|^{-n-n\epsilon}\leq h(x)\leq C|x|^{-n-n\epsilon},|x|\geq 1

for some positive constant
C
and
\epsilon>0.

Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the space with norm

\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}((-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u)^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u^{2}d\mu

with the convention
(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}:=\nabla
. Meanwhile, we set

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}=\{u\in\mathcal{H}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}ud\mu=0\}.

For our main application, we will consider functions in
\mathcal{H}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
. The following Lemma gives
L^{p}
estimates for functions in
\mathcal{H}
. The proof follows easily from Corollary
3.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
v\in\mathcal{H}
and
p>1
, there exists some positive constant
C
such that

(4.2)

\|v\|_{L^{p}(d\mu)}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Now we can prove the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) via variational method.

Theorem 4.2
.

If a smooth function
Q(x)
is positive somewhere and

Q(x)=O(|x|^{-l}),

where
l>0
,
for each
\alpha
satisfies

(4.3)

\max\{0,2-\frac{2l}{n}\}<\alpha<2,

then there exists a solution
u
to (
1.1
) satisfying

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u(x)|\mathrm{d}x=o(R^{n+1}),

and

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Qe^{nu}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}{2}\alpha.

Proof.

Choose a smooth function
u_{0}
satisfying
u_{0}=-\alpha\log|x|
for
|x|>1/2
and define

\psi:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u_{0}.

Notice that
\psi\equiv 0
for
|x|>1/2
.
In particular, with the help of divergence theorem, it is not hard to verify that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi dx=\int_{B_{1}(0)}(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u_{0}\mathrm{d}x=-\int_{\partial B_{1}(0)}\frac{\partial(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}u}{\partial n}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

A direct computation yields that

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi dx=\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}{2}\alpha.

Then the equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to

(4.7)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}w+\psi=Ke^{nw},

where

K(x):=Q(x)e^{nu_{0}}=O(|x|^{-l-n\alpha}).

Set

(4.8)

\epsilon=\alpha+\frac{l}{n}-1>0

due to our assumption (
4.3
).
Set the function
h(x)
as follows

h(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-\frac{n}{2}-\frac{n\epsilon}{2}}.

Set the weighted measure
\mathrm{d}\mu=h(x)\mathrm{d}x
.
Meanwhile, one has

(4.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K(x)e^{nu}\mathrm{d}x\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{nu}\mathrm{d}\mu.

Consider the following set

\mathcal{H}_{K}=\{u\in\mathcal{H}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Ke^{nu}\mathrm{d}x>0\}.

Since
K(x)
is positive somewhere, it is not difficult to check that
\mathcal{H}_{K}
is not empty.
For any
u\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
, we consider the following functional

F(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}((-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u)^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi udx-\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}{2n}\alpha\log\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Ke^{nu}dx.

For any constant
c
, by (
4.6
), we have

F(u+c)=F(u).

Thus we may assume that

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}ud\mu=0.

Now, apply Young’s inequality to obtain that

|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi udx|=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi uh^{-1}d\mu|\leq C(\eta)\|\psi h^{-1}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+\eta\int_{supp(\psi)}u^{2}d\mu

where
\eta>0
is small to chosen later and
supp(\psi)
denotes the compact support of the function
\psi
. Then making use of (
4.9
), Corollary
3.1
and Lemma
4.1
, we obtain that

F(u)\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4\min\{1,\epsilon\}}-C_{1}\eta\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}((-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{4}}u)^{2}dx-C(\eta,\alpha,l,n)

Based on the assumption (
4.3
) and (
4.8
), we can choose small
\eta>0
such that

\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{4\min\{1,\epsilon\}}-C_{1}\eta>0.

With the help of a standard variational method, there exists a function
v\in\mathcal{H}_{K}
attains the minimum of
F(u)
which weakly satisfies

(4.11)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}v+\psi=\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|\alpha}{2}\frac{Ke^{nv}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Ke^{nv}\mathrm{d}x}.

We can choose a suitable constant
C_{2}

(4.12)

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}v_{1}+\psi=Ke^{nv_{1}}.

where
v_{1}=v+C_{2}

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Ke^{nv_{1}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}{2}\alpha.

Standard elliptic estimate shows that
v_{1}
is actually smooth.
Since
v_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
, Lemma
4.1
yields that for any
q>1
, there holds

(4.13)

\|v\|_{L^{q}(d\mu)}\leq C\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}<+\infty.

Making use of Hölder’s inequality, one has

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v_{1}|\mathrm{d}x\leq(\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v|^{q}h\mathrm{d}x)^{\frac{1}{q}}(\int_{B_{R}(0)}h^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}\mathrm{d}x)^{\frac{q-1}{q}}\leq CR^{n+\frac{n\epsilon}{q}}.

By choosing suitable large
q>1
, one has

(4.14)

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|v_{1}|\mathrm{d}x=o(R^{n+1}).

Finally, choose

u(x)=v_{1}(x)+u_{0}(x)

which is our desired solution to (
1.1
).
Based on the estimate (
4.14
), it is easy to see

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u(x)|\mathrm{d}x=o(R^{n+1}),

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Qe^{nu}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Ke^{nv_{1}}\mathrm{d}x=\frac{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}{2}\alpha.

We have proved the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

With some further effort, one can show that

]

To finish our proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need two results in the first author’s work
[
22
]
. Recall the definition of normal solutions in
[
6
]
: a solution
u(x)
to (
1.1
) is

if
Qe^{nu}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

u(x)=\frac{2}{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\log\frac{|y|}{|x-y|}Q(y)e^{nu(y)}\mathrm{d}y+C.

The first result that we need characterizes normal solutions from an integral estimate.

Theorem 4.4
.

(Theorem 2.2 in
[
22
]
) If the solution
u(x)
to (
1.1
) satisfies
Qe^{nu}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u|\mathrm{d}x=o(R^{n+2}),

then

For normal solutions, the second result in
[
22
]
gives a characterization for complete metrics.

Theorem 4.5
.

(Theorem 1.4 in
[
22
]
) If
u(x)
is a normal solution satisfying (
1.1
), and
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}
, then for each fixed point
p
, we have

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\frac{\log d_{g}(x,p)}{\log|x-p|}=\max\left\{1-\frac{2}{(n-1)!|\mathbb{S}^{n}|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}Qe^{nu}\mathrm{d}x,0\right\},

where
d_{g}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the geodesic distance on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
respect to the metric
g
. In particular,
g
is complete if the right hand side is strictly positive.

Now we are ready to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since
l>\frac{n}{2}
, one has

\max\{0,2-\frac{2l}{n}\}<1.

By Theorem
4.2
, for some

\alpha
satisfying

\max\{0,2-\frac{2l}{n}\}<\alpha<1,

there exists a smooth solution
u(x)
to the following equation

(-\Delta)^{\frac{n}{2}}u=fe^{nu}.

In addition,
u
satisfies (
4.4
) and (

). Then Theorem
4.4
implies that
u
is normal and Theorem
4.4
show that
g=e^{2u}|dx|^{2}

∎

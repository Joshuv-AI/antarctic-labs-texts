Dietary fiber supplementation during late gestation affects steroidogenesis, antioxidant capacity, gut microbiota and placental metabolomics of Rongchang sows
Authors: Xie Peng, Weihuang Tan, Zhengfen Ai, Huiyu Liu, Yetong Xu, Jiaman Pang, Renli Qi, Qi Wang, Jian Wu, Zhenguo Yang, Zhihong Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Oxidative stress can impair placental function and sow health. This experiment aimed to explore the impacts of inulin and cellulose as sources of dietary fiber (DF) on reproductive performance, steroidogenesis, antioxidant capacity, placental metabolomics, and fecal microbiota of Rongchang sows from d 85 of gestation to parturition. Sixty sows (parity: 5.07 ± 0.24, backfat thickness: 43.06 ± 0.78 mm) were randomly assigned to 4 groups (n = 15): the control group fed a basal diet (CON), and the other 3 groups receiving diets supplemented with 21.8%, 43.6% or 65.5% more DF than the CON group, named as DF1, DF2, and DF3, respectively. The DF supplementation had no significant effect on total litter size, number of piglets born alive, and piglet birth weight (P > 0.05). The concentrations of serum estradiol (E2) and progesterone (P4) on d 108 of gestation increased in linear and quadratic manners and reached their maximums in the DF1 group (P < 0.001). Meanwhile, the concentrations of E2 and P4 in the placenta increased quadratically and also maximized in the DF1 group (P < 0.001). Maternal DF supplementation quadratically increased the serum glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px) activity on d 108 of gestation (P = 0.036), with the highest activity observed in the DF1 group, while the superoxide dismutase (SOD) activity tended to increase quadratically (P = 0.066) and peaked in the DF2 group. In addition, DF supplementation quadratically increased the fecal concentrations of acetate, butyrate, and total short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) on d 108 of gestation (P < 0.05), all of which maximized in the DF1 group. The relative abundances of SCFAs-producing genera Parabacteroides, norank_f_F082, and norank_f_UCG-010 were significantly higher in the DF-supplemented groups than in the CON group (P < 0.05). Correlation analysis indicated that the abundance of Parabacteroides was negatively correlated with serum IL-6 concentrations (P = 0.027), while serum E2 and P4 concentrations were positively correlated with fecal concentrations of propionate, butyrate, and total SCFAs (P < 0.01). Untargeted metabolic analysis further revealed that the DF1 group significantly affected placental metabolites associated with linoleic acid metabolism, the PPAR signaling pathway, and cholesterol metabolism. These findings suggest that moderate DF supplementation optimally enhances the health status of late-gestating sows by modulating gut microbiota-derived SCFAs and placental metabolism.

Introduction

During late gestation, sows are often exposed to many stressors (rapid fetal growth, restricted feeding, constipation, etc.), that can induce oxidative stress and trigger an imbalanced inflammatory response (Liu et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2020). Long-term exposure to oxidative stress and inflammatory responses can impair placental function and maternal health, potentially leading to preterm birth, miscarriage, and intrauterine growth retardation (Agarwal et al., 2012; Jabbour et al., 2009). Therefore, intervention strategies should be developed to alleviate oxidative stress and inflammatory responses during late gestation.

Dietary fiber (DF) can be classified into soluble dietary fiber (SDF) and insoluble dietary fiber (IDF) according to its solubility (Williams et al., 2017). Previous studies have shown that pregnant sows fed high DF diets improved litter size (Che et al., 2011; Zhuo et al., 2020), piglet birth weight (Langendijk and Chen, 2012), and birth weight uniformity (Che et al., 2011; Huang et al., 2023). In addition, DF supplementation during pregnancy has been reported to improve redox status and reduce inflammatory responses in sows (Liu et al., 2020). Most studies on the effects of DF have been conducted by formulating common fiber-rich feedstuffs, such as soybean hulls and corn bran. However, these ingredients also contain many other micronutrients (such as vitamins and minerals) that can interfere with the direct effects of DF. In contrast, supplementation with purified DF, such as inulin, guar gum, and cellulose, allows a more direct evaluation of the effects of DF (Men et al., 2022; Zhuo et al., 2020, 2021). Nevertheless, the mechanism by which DF influences the reproductive performance and health of late-gestating sows remains poorly understood and warrants further investigation.

As the main substrate for gut bacteria, DF provides several health benefits, such as enhancing short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs) production and modulating immune function (Ye et al., 2022). Notably, the gut microbiota of sows undergoes dramatic changes throughout gestation (Ji et al., 2019), and its manipulation is increasingly recognized as an effective way to improve maternal health and fetal development (Chen et al., 2023; Gao et al., 2024). Studies have shown that the gut microbiota subtly interacts with steroid hormones such as estradiol (E2) and progesterone (P4) during pregnancy (Tian et al., 2024). In addition, SCFAs, the key microbial metabolites of DF, have been demonstrated to improve host metabolism (Koh et al., 2016). Previous research have found that butyric acid can regulate the synthesis of P4 and E2 in porcine granulosa cells (Lu et al., 2017). Given the well-established crucial roles of E2 and P4 in maintaining pregnancy (Hong et al., 2019; Parisi et al., 2023), these findings collectively implies that DF supplementation may improve steroidogenesis by regulating the gut microbiota and SCFAs production, thereby enhancing the reproductive performance of sows.

Additionally, gut microbiota-derived metabolites, particularly SCFAs, are known to modulate placental metabolism and function (Meng et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2022). The placenta serves as a critical interface for maternal–fetal communication, and its metabolomic profile reflects both maternal nutrition and fetal development (Chao de la Barca et al., 2022; Sarr et al., 2025). The DF-induced changes in gut microbiota and SCFAs production may modulate placental metabolic pathways, such as energy and tryptophan metabolism, thereby improving placental function and fetal growth (Li et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2022). However, the specific impact of DF on placental metabolomics in late-gestating sows remains poorly explored.

Inulin is a typical SDF that can be extensively fermented by gut microbiota (Li et al., 2021a). Cellulose is an IDF that has water-holding capacity and can be used as a bulking agent (Wen et al., 2022). The combination of IDF and SDF could optimize the effects of DF according to their independent physicochemical properties (Chen et al., 2020). The Rongchang pig is a well-known Chinese breed, particularly tolerant of rough feeding characterized by high DF content and low energy density. Although numerous studies have investigated the effects of DF on litter performance in late gestation, the use of purified fibers (inulin and cellulose) in Rongchang sows constitutes a novel strategy worthy of further investigation. Therefore, the current study aimed to explore the impacts of different DF levels by supplementing inulin and cellulose on reproductive performance, steroidogenesis, antioxidant capacity, placental metabolomics, and fecal microbiota in late-gestating Rongchang sows. It was hypothesized that moderate DF supplementation could improve reproductive performance and health status of late-gestating sows by modulating gut microbiota-derived SCFAs, enhancing steroid hormone synthesis, and improving placental metabolism.

Materials and methods

Animal ethics statement

This project was approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of Southwest University (IACUC-20230628-01).

Animals and diets

The experiment was conducted at Guangshun Farm, Qitai Group, Rongchang, Chongqing, China. On d 85 of gestation, 60 Rongchang sows (parity: 5.07 ± 0.24, backfat thickness: 43.06 ± 0.78 mm) were randomly assigned to 4 dietary groups (n = 15) according to their backfat thickness and parity. Sows in the control group were fed a corn-soybean meal-based diet (CON). The basal diet was formulated according to the nutrient requirements of pregnant sows (GB/T 39235-2020; China National Standard, 2020) as presented in Table 1. Nutrient contents in the diet were analyzed according to AOAC (2007) methods. Crude protein (method 990.03) was analyzed by Kjeldahl nitrogen analyzer (KDN-103F, Shanghai Xianjian Instrument Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). Calcium (method 927.02) was determined by dry ash method (SX2-10-12 TP, Shanghai Yiheng Scientific Instruments Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), and total phosphorus (method 984.27) was determined spectrophotometrically (UV-2700, Shimadzu Corp., Kyoto, Japan). Amino acids (method 982.30) were analyzed using an automated amino acid analyzer (L-8900, Hitachi High-Tech Corp., Tokyo, Japan). The total DF content was analyzed as the sum of SDF and IDF, and was analyzed using the method 991.43 with an Ankom TDF dietary fiber analyzer (Ankom Technology Corp., Macedon, NY, USA). The total DF in the basal diet was 18.09%. Sows in the CON group were fed 2.44 kg/d of the basal diet, which provided 441.40 g/d of DF. The other 3 groups consumed an extra 21.8% (DF1), 43.6% (DF2), or 65.5% (DF3) more DF than the CON group (Fig. 1). Table 2 shows the daily feed intake of sows in each group. The extra supplemented DF used in this study were inulin (95%; Orafti GR, Tienen, Belgium) and cellulose (98%; Guangxi Shangda Tech Co., Ltd., Nanning, Guangxi, China). Inulin, extracted from chicory, consists of fructose units linked by β-2,1-glycosidic bonds. Cellulose, derived from refined bagasse, is a glucose biopolymer linked by β-1,4-glycosidic linkages. Inulin and cellulose were added to the basal diet at a ratio of 1:4 in line with the previous studies (Men et al., 2022; Zhuo et al., 2021). The sows were housed individually in gestation stalls from d 85 until 107 of gestation. On d 108 of gestation, sows were moved into individual farrowing crates. Sows with a total litter size < 6 or experiencing abnormal health conditions (abortion, poor health, and death, etc.) during the experiment were culled, as presented in Table S1. Animals were fed twice daily (09:00 and 17:00) from d 85 of gestation to parturition. Sample and data collection

On d 108 of gestation, blood samples were taken from the jugular vein of sows (n = 8) after an overnight fast. Serum was separated by centrifugation (4 °C, 3500 × g, 20 min) and frozen at −20 °C until analysis. Fresh fecal samples were collected in sterile tubes by massaging the rectum immediately after blood collection, and frozen rapidly in liquid nitrogen and then stored at −80 °C. The placental tissues were collected according to the previous study (Peng et al., 2021). Briefly, after the placenta was discharged, approximately 5 g of fresh placental tissue (3–4 cm from the cord insertion point) was rinsed with ice-cold phosphate buffer saline and frozen rapidly in liquid nitrogen, and then stored at −80 °C. The fecal score of sows on d 110 of gestation was recorded according to the previous study (Oliviero et al., 2009). In addition, the total number of piglets born, born alive, mummified, and stillborn, as well as their individual birth weights, were recorded at parturition.

Oxidative stress parameters analysis

The serum malondialdehyde (MDA; A003-1), superoxide dismutase (SOD; A001-1), catalase (CAT; A007-1-1), and glutathione peroxidase (GSH-Px; A005-1) were measured using commercial kits (Nanjing Jiancheng Bioengineering Institute, Nanjing, Jiangsu, China) following the instructions of the manufacturer with a microplate reader (Synergy H1, BioTek Instruments, Winooski, VT, USA).

Steroid hormone analysis

Before the assays, frozen placental tissues were homogenized in ice-cold physiological saline (1:9, w/v) by using an ultrasonic homogenizer (JXFSTPRP-24L, Shanghai Jingxin Industrial Development Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China), and then centrifuged at 3500 × g for 10 min. The placental supernatant and serum were used to determine the concentrations of E2 (MM-0480O1) and P4 (MM-1205O1) following the guidelines of enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits (Jiangsu Meimian Industrial Co., Ltd., Yancheng, Jiangsu, China) with a microplate reader (Synergy H1).

Inflammatory cytokines analysis

The concentrations of interleukin (IL)-1β (ml025973), IL-6 (ml025981), and tumor necrosis factor (TNF)-α (ml002360) in serum were measured using ELISA kits (Shanghai Enzyme-linked Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) following the instructions of the manufacturer with a microplate reader (Synergy H1).

Fecal microbiota and SCFAs analysis

The DNA from fecal samples was extracted using the stool DNA kit (DP328, Tiangen Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). The subsequent 16S rRNA gene amplicon sequencing and bioinformatics analysis were conducted as described previously (Dai et al., 2023). The V3–V4 fragments of the 16S rRNA gene were amplified using 338F/806R primer pairs (338F, 5′-ACTCCTACGGGAGGCAGCAG-3′; 806R, 5′-GGACTACHVGGGTWTCTAAT-3′), and then the purified amplicons were sequenced on an Illumina MiSeq platforms (Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China). The bioinformatics analysis was performed using the Majorbio Cloud platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com). Briefly, the raw sequence data were demultiplexed and quality filtered with Quantitative Insights Into Microbial Ecology (QIIME2) pipeline package. The high-quality sequences were then denoised using DADA2 software to obtain amplicon sequence variants (ASVs). The ASVs were taxonomically assigned using the Silva 16S rRNA database (version 138). Alpha diversity indices were analyzed using Mothur software (version 1.30.1). Principal coordinates analysis (PCoA) plots were constructed using Bray–Curtis distances, and the significance of separation among groups was tested using analysis of similarity (ANOSIM). Linear discriminant analysis (LDA) effect size (LEfSe) was used to identify the different bacterial genera among groups.

The concentrations of SCFAs in the fecal samples were measured by the gas chromatographic method (CP-3800, Varian Inc., Palo Alto, CA, USA) according to the previous study (Peng et al., 2019).

Untargeted metabolomics analysis

Approximately 50 mg of placental tissue was homogenized in 2-mL centrifuge tubes equipped with 6-mm grinding beads. Metabolite extraction was performed using 400 μL of a methanol–water solvent system (4:1, v/v) containing 0.02 mg/mL L-2-chlorophenylalanine as an internal standard. Following mechanical grinding, the homogenate underwent 30 min low-temperature ultrasonic extraction at 4 °C. Subsequently, the samples were kept at −20 °C for 30 min and then centrifuged (13,000 × g, 15 min, 4 °C). The clarified supernatant was finally collected in injection vials for subsequent liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (LC-MS/MS) analysis at Majorbio Bio-Pharm Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). The detection methods and data processing procedures followed previously established protocols (Pang et al., 2025). The acquired LC-MS data were uploaded to the Majorbio Cloud Platform (https://cloud.majorbio.com/) for multivariate statistical analysis, including principal component analysis (PCA) and orthogonal partial least squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA). Significantly altered metabolites were identified based on variable importance in projection (VIP) > 1 and P-value < 0.05. These differential metabolites were then subjected to metabolic pathway enrichment and functional analysis using the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) database (http://www.genome.jp/kegg/).

RNA extraction and quantitative real-time PCR

The expression levels of steroidogenesis-related genes were evaluated using placental tissues, including Star, CYP11A1, HSD3B, and CYP19A1. The full names of the abbreviations of all genes and proteins can be found in Table S2. The protocol methods were performed according to the previous study (Peng et al., 2019). Total RNA was extracted from placental tissues using RNAiso Plus reagent (9109, TaKaRa Biotechnology Co., Ltd., Dalian, Liaoning, China). RNA integrity was determined by electrophoresis on 1.0% agarose gel, and RNA quality and concentration were assessed using a NanoDrop ND-2000 Spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific Inc., Wilmington, DE, USA). Complementary DNA (cDNA) was synthesized by reverse transcription using the StarScript III All-in-one RT Mix with gDNA Remover (A230-10, Beijing GenStar Biosolutions Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Quantitative real-time PCR was performed using 2 × RealStar Green Fast Mixture (A301-05, Beijing GenStar Biosolutions Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) on a CFX Connect Real-Time PCR Detection System (Bio-Rad Laboratories, Hercules, CA, USA). The data were calculated using the 2−△△Ct method with GAPDH as an internal reference gene. Primers are listed in Table S3.

Statistical analysis

Sow or litter was regarded as the experimental unit for the analysis. Data were analyzed using the one-way ANOVA procedure of SPSS 27.0 software (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and presented as the mean and standard error of the mean (SEM). The following statistics model was used:Yij=μ+αi+eij,where Yij is the response variable; μ is the overall mean; αi is the treatment effect; and eij is the random error. Multiple comparison was conducted by the Bonferroni correction method. Orthogonal polynomial contrasts were used to determine the linear and quadratic effects of increasing DF levels on reproductive performance, fecal SCFAs concentrations, mRNA expression levels, serum and placental parameters. Microbiota relative abundance differences were measured using the Kruskal–Wallis H test. Differences are considered significant at P < 0.05, and a tendency is considered at 0.05 ≤ P < 0.10.

Discussion

High fiber intake during late gestation exerts multifaceted beneficial effects on sow health status, particularly in improving antioxidant capacity, metabolism, and immunity (Liu et al., 2020; Li et al., 2023). Based on the findings, moderate DF supplementation improved sow health status via regulation of gut microbiota composition and related SCFAs production. Mechanistically, these changes may enhance steroidogenesis and antioxidant status by modulating placental metabolism.

The sow reproductive performance is an important factor influencing the productivity and profitability of swine production. Increased oxidative stress during late gestation is responsible for the impaired reproductive performance (Peng et al., 2021; Zhao et al., 2013). The impacts of high-fiber diets on sow performance are inconsistent. Recent studies have reported that fiber-rich ingredients such as alfalfa meal, sugar beet pulp, or soybean hulls contain many other nutrients that may interfere with the direct effects of DF (Men et al., 2022; Zhuo et al., 2020). To address this problem, the DF level in this study was achieved via supplementation with purified DFs, specifically inulin and cellulose. The results showed that the litter size and litter weight at birth were not significantly affected by dietary treatment. Similarly, previous study observed that feeding high-fiber diets during late pregnancy had no significant effect on litter performance (Liu et al., 2020). Litter size in pigs is mainly affected by fertilization rate and embryo survival in early pregnancy (Edwards et al., 2012), and is unlikely to be affected by dietary treatments in late pregnancy. Moreover, SCFAs produced by DF fermentation may not provide sufficient energy to affect reproductive performance (Blaut, 2015). Other studies, however, reported that high-fiber consumption during pregnancy increased litter size (Che et al., 2011; Li et al., 2021b). Huang et al. (2023) found that DF supplementation during late gestation reduced the stillbirth rate and increased the birth weight of piglets. These discrepancies may be attributed to different fiber types (Renteria-Flores et al., 2008) and fiber dose (Danielsen and Vestergaard, 2001), as well as the duration of DF supplementation (Che et al., 2011). For example, previous studies have shown that positive response of litter size to DF is usually evident over two and three successive reproductive cycles (Che et al., 2011; Li et al., 2021b). In addition, the dietary soluble to insoluble fiber ratio plays an important role in influencing sow performance (Li et al., 2019).

Pregnancy is considered an oxidative stress challenge in both humans and livestock. Increasing maternal metabolic burdens in late pregnancy leads to elevated lipid and protein oxidation, which impairs normal cellular structure and function (Berchieri-Ronchi et al., 2011). The MDA is an indicator of lipid peroxidation mediated by reactive oxygen species (Peng et al., 2021). The SOD, CAT, and GPX enzymes play crucial roles in maintaining redox homeostasis and provide the first line of enzymatic defense system to directly scavenge reactive oxygen species (Adineh et al., 2020). The previous study reported that high-fiber intake decreased plasma MDA content and increased GSH-Px activity on d 107 of gestation (Liu et al., 2020). Similarly, the current study found that high-fiber intake increased the serum SOD and GSH-Px activities on d 108 of gestation, supporting the antioxidant defense capacity of sows with high-fiber intake during late gestation. The antioxidant effects of DF may be related to its ability to improve gut microbiota profiles, enhance the generation of SCFAs, and strengthen intestinal barrier integrity, thereby preventing the leakage of pro-oxidant metabolites into systemic circulation.

Oxidative stress is intimately related to systemic inflammation. Cytokines play crucial roles in regulating immune and inflammatory responses (Harvanová et al., 2023). Several pregnancy-related disorders, such as pre-eclampsia and stillbirth, are associated with excessive maternal inflammatory responses (Liu et al., 2021). Zhuo et al. (2020) reported that sows fed high DF diets during pregnancy had higher serum IL-10 concentrations and lower TNF-α concentrations. In the current study, maternal high-fiber intake reduced serum IL-6 and TNF-α concentrations, suggesting that high fiber intake may alleviate the pro-inflammatory response. The immune-regulatory effect of DF can be partly explained by its bacterial metabolites SCFAs. The previous study has reported that SCFAs can strengthen gut barrier function and reduce inflammatory factor secretion (Bach Knudsen et al., 2018). In addition, DF could alleviate constipation by stimulating intestinal peristalsis. Constipation is a prevalent symptom observed in sows during late gestation, which can enhance endotoxin absorption and trigger a systemic inflammatory response (Sun et al., 2023). In the present study, fecal score, a parameter reflecting constipation in sows (Lu et al., 2022), was improved in sows fed high-fiber diets, indicating that high-DF diets can decrease the constipation risk of sows during late gestation.

Steroid hormones such as P4 and E2 play crucial roles in regulating female reproduction. Xiong et al. (2020) reported that maternal cadmium exposure during late gestation induced fetal growth restriction by impairing placental P4 synthesis. In addition, exogenous E2 administration could alleviate the inflammatory response in rats with preeclampsia (Lin et al., 2020). A recent metabolomics study revealed increased steroid biosynthesis with high-fiber intake during late pregnancy in sows (Moturi et al., 2022). Similarly, in this study, maternal high-fiber intake increased serum concentrations of E2 and P4. The placenta becomes the major site for E2 and P4 secretion in late pregnancy (Hong et al., 2019). Placental P4 synthesis requires a series of rate-limiting enzymes, such as Star, CYP11A1, HSD3B, and CYP19A1 can act on P4 to form E2 (Ding et al., 2021). In the present study, higher placental P4 concentrations were found in the high DF diets, which may be attributed to increased expression of genes associated with steroid synthesis. Interestingly, the elevated placental E2 concentrations without significant changes in CYP19A1 gene expression levels may be attributed to increased exogenous precursor supply or regulation of enzyme activity, rather than relying solely on CYP19A1 gene expression, but this requires further investigation. In addition, gut microbes play an important role in regulating the reproductive endocrine system via interacting with steroid hormones (Qi et al., 2021). In this study, norank_f_F082 and Oscillospira were positively correlated with serum P4. Another explanation for the enhanced steroid hormone synthesis observed in sows fed high DF diets may be the SCFAs produced by the gut microbiota, particularly acetic acid, which serves as a precursor for steroid synthesis (Moturi et al., 2022). Meanwhile, correlation analysis also revealed serum E2 and P4 were positively correlated with fecal acetate, propionate, butyrate, and total SCFAs.

Intestinal microbes play crucial roles in modulating the immune system, host metabolism, endocrine system, and mucosal barrier function (Qi et al., 2021). The intestinal microbiota undergoes significant modifications throughout gestation, and these changes are associated with metabolic characteristics and immune responses of pregnant sows (Ji et al., 2019; Zhuo et al., 2020). Studies have reported that the positive effects of DF on host health are regulated by gut microbiota and related metabolites SCFAs (Sun et al., 2023; Zhuo et al., 2020). A recent study reported that sows fed high-fiber diets could increase the abundance of Lactobacillus on d 7 of lactation and inhibit the abundance of Escherichia-Shigella on d 110 of gestation (Li et al., 2023). It was found that maternal high-fiber intake increased the abundance of Bacteroidota, which is in line with previous studies (Li et al., 2023; Zhuo et al., 2020). Bacteroidota possess carbohydrate-active enzymes that specifically degrade undigested carbohydrates to produce SCFAs, which can serve as an additional energy source after absorption by enterocytes (Sonnenburg et al., 2010). In addition, maternal high-fiber intake increased the abundances of the SCFAs-producing genera Rikenellaceae_RC9_gut_group, norank_f_F082, norank_f_UCG-010, and Parabacteroides, while reduced the abundance of NK4A214_group. NK4A214_group is an opportunistic pathogen that has been reported to damage the gut barrier and cause gut inflammation (Liu et al., 2023). In addition, LEfSe analysis also showed that the putatively pro-inflammatory taxa Eubacterium_ruminantium_group and unclassified_f_Acholeplasmataceae were enriched in the CON group. Several studies have reported that the abundances of unclassified_f_Acholeplasmataceae and Eubacterium_ruminantium_group were positively correlated with intestinal permeability and inflammation (Lin et al., 2023; Shen et al., 2022). However, consumption of additional DF significantly enriched the abundances of SCFA-producing microbiota such as Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group, unclassified_f_Prevotellaceae, and Oscillospira. Prevotellaceae_NK3B31_group can effectively reduce intestinal inflammation and improve nutrient absorption (Kovatcheva-Datchary et al., 2015). Oscillospira has been reported to positively regulate obesity-related metabolic diseases and chronic inflammation (Yang et al., 2021). Furthermore, correlation analysis indicated that the abundance of Parabacteroides was negatively correlated with serum IL-6 concentrations, suggesting a possible role of microbiota in the regulation of sow health status by DF.

Metabolites are closely associated with the animal health status. Previous studies have shown that DF can significantly alter the host's metabolic profiles in blood and feces (Men et al., 2022; Zhao et al., 2023). Metabolomics analysis was further conducted to investigate the potential mechanism mediating the effects of DF on the placenta in sows. The results revealed that DF intake modified the PPAR signaling pathway, linoleic acid metabolism, cholesterol metabolism, and choline metabolism in cancer. The PPARs are nuclear receptors involved in regulating fatty acid metabolism, inflammatory responses, and cellular differentiation (Fanale et al., 2017), and have been shown to play a critical role in placental development and function (Barak et al., 2008). Notably, Zhang et al. (2025) demonstrated that DF alleviated obesity-induced inflammation by upregulating PPAR signaling. Moreover, metabolites related to linoleic acid metabolism in placental tissue have been proposed as potential biomarkers for diagnosing fetal growth restriction (Yang et al., 2023). This study found that levels of 9(S)-HpODE and taurocholic acid were decreased in the DF1 group compared to the CON group. 9(S)-HpODE, a linoleic acid-derived lipid peroxidation product (Gu et al., 2025), acts as a transient signaling molecule in early oxidative stress responses (Li et al., 2020). Taurocholic acid, a major bile acid elevated in intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP), has been implicated in ICP pathogenesis (Zhang et al., 2015) and can induce trophoblast apoptosis through p38 and Caspase-3 activation (Zhang et al., 2014). Additionally, disruptions in choline metabolism are associated with tumorigenesis and cancer progression (Sonkar et al., 2019). LysoPC(20:5[5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z]/0:0), also known as lysophosphatidylcholine, is a well-known inflammatory lipid that is upregulated in a variety of inflammation-related diseases (Mei et al., 2024; Knuplez and Marsche, 2020). The observed reduction in LysoPC(20:5[5Z,8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z]/0:0) levels in the DF1 group indicates that dietary fiber intake may attenuate the placental inflammatory responses. Collectively, these alterations in metabolites and metabolic pathways may contribute to the beneficial effects of DF on placental health.

This study has several limitations that warrant consideration. First, sow body weights was not recorded. Although sows were randomly assigned to treatments based on parity and backfat thickness to minimize initial differences, the absence of body weight data may limit a comprehensive evaluation of the nutritional status and energy balance of sows during late gestation. Second, the experiment was conducted over only a single parity. The findings require further validation across multiple parities to confirm the long-term benefits of DF supplementation. Third, although increased fecal SCFAs concentrations were observed and correlated with improved sow health status, systemic SCFAs concentrations were not measured. This gap restricts the ability to establish direct causal links between SCFAs and the gut–placenta axis. Future studies incorporating multi-parity designs and simultaneous measurements of systemic SCFAs and placental parameters would provide deeper insights into the underlying mechanisms.

Conclusions

Dietary supplementation with 96.38 g/d of inulin and cellulose (at a 1:4 ratio) in a corn-soybean meal-based diet enhanced steroidogenesis and antioxidant capacity in late-gestating Rongchang sows. These beneficial effects on the health status of sows were associated with modulations in gut microbiota-derived SCFAs and placental metabolism.

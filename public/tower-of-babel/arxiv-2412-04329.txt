New interpretation of the original charged BTZ black hole spacetime
Tomáš Hale, Brayden R. Hull, David Kubizňák, Robert B. Mann, Jana Menšíková

Abstract
In their seminal 1992 paper, Bañados, Teitelboim and Zanelli (BTZ) proposed a simple charged generalization of what is now known as the spinning BTZ black hole, the proposal being that a rotating metric can be supported by a ‘static vector’ potential. While with such an ansatz the Einstein equations are satisfied, and the corresponding energy-momentum tensor is divergence-less, the Maxwell equations do not (due to the special degenerate form of the corresponding field strength) hold. More recently, Deshpande and Lunin have proposed a generalized ‘Einstein–Maxwell’ system which yields analytic rotating black holes in all odd dimensions. In this paper, we show that the original charged BTZ solution can be re-interpreted as a solution of the Deshpande–Lunin theory. Moreover, as we shall explicitly illustrate on an example of regularized conformal electrodynamics, similar construction also works for any non-linear electrodynamics in 3-dimensions. At the same time, all these spacetimes represent self-gravitating solutions of (NLE generalized) force-free electrodynamics.

II
The Original Charged BTZ Solution

II.1
Basic properties

The original proposal for a charged rotating black hole in
(2+1)
dimensions was
[

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{j^{2}}{r^{2}}-m-2Q^{2}\log(r/r_0)\,,

The spacetime is characterized by three integration constants

The metric (
12

5
),
along with the necessary integrability condition

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(15)

which follows from the Bianchi identity. However, as previously noted
[
7
,
8

(see also a recent discussion in

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}=0\,,

(16)

14

[
7
,
8
]

Interestingly, although such a solution is ‘easy to write’ in the Kerr-like coordinates, it becomes rather cumbersome in the original BTZ coordinates (see Appendix
A

Let us look at the ‘failure of the Maxwell equation’ a bit more closely. Using the definition of the electromagnetic energy-momentum tensor (
6
),
the above integrability condition (
15
) implies

0=4\pi\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=F^{\nu}{}_{\alpha}\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\alpha}\,,

(17)

where in the second equality we have used the ‘electromagnetic Bianchi identity’,
dF=0
.
Now, if
F_{\mu\nu}
had an ‘inverse’, the integrability condition (
17
) would then imply the vacuum Maxwell equations. However, in our case
F_{\mu\nu}
is degenerate, and the Maxwell equations are only satisfied with non-trivial current on the r.h.s., namely:

\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}={\cal J}^{\nu}\,,

(18)

where

{\cal J}=-\frac{2jQ}{r^{4}}\partial_{\varphi}\,.

(19)

The above represents a current in the
\partial_{\varphi}
direction penetrating the spacetime. It is this current that allows for a rotating solution with a ‘static’ electromagnetic field. Note also that in the non-rotating case,
j\to 0
, the current vanishes and we recover the standard charged static BTZ black hole.

Remarkably, equations (
17
) and (
18
), together imply

F_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{J}^{\nu}=0

(20)

which is a characteristic of
force-free electrodynamics

[
27
]
, relevant for the Blandford–Znajek effect of electromagnetic extraction of energy from a rotating black hole
[
28
]
. Furthermore, as stated above, our electromagnetic tensor is degenerate which is another necessary condition of force-free electrodynamics and it is equivalent to
\mathcal{P}=0

[
29
]
.
The original charged BTZ spacetime thus represents a solution of ‘backreacting’ force free electrodynamics.

2

2

Note that in our case, the force-free electrodynamics is electrically dominated since
\mathcal{S}=-\frac{Q^{2}}{r^{2}}=-E^{2}
. However, in plasma physics, the magnetically dominated (i.e.
\mathcal{S}>0
) case is relevant.

Moreover, as we shall discuss now, it is also a solution of the Deshpande–Lunin theory introduced above.

III
Exact solutions in non-linear electrodynamics

Let us now demonstrate that a similar construction also works

III.1
Deshpande–Lunin-NLE theory

We generalize the Deshpande–Lunin
theory to NLE via the following action:

I_{\mbox{\tiny DL-NLE}}=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int d^{d}x\sqrt{-g}(R-2\Lambda+4{\cal L})+\frac{\lambda}{4\pi}\int A\wedge H\wedge K\,,

(30)

where we have replaced the Maxwell Lagrangian,
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{\mbox{\tiny M}}=-\frac{1}{2}{\cal S}

This yields the Einstein equations (

\nabla_{\mu}D^{\mu\nu}={\cal J}^{\nu}\,,\quad{\cal J}=\lambda*(H\wedge K)\,,

(33)

where we have denoted

D_{\mu\nu}\equiv-2{\cal L}_{\cal S}F_{\mu\nu}\,,\quad{\cal L}_{\cal S}\equiv\frac{\partial{\cal L}}{\partial{\cal S}}\,,

(34)

F\wedge H=0\,,\quad F\wedge K=0\,,

(35)

We may also generalize the force-free electrodynamics to theories of NLE. Namely, we simply demand that

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0\,,\quad dF=0\,,

(36)

(37)

with the latter meaning that the (NLE generalized) Lorenz force vanishes. As we shall see, the novel metrics constructed below satisfy both the Deshpande–Lunin-NLE equations of motion as well as the equations of NLE force-free eletrodynamics.

In particular, we shall focus on

IV
Summary

We have rehabilitated the original charged BTZ spacetime
[
5
,
6
]
, known to be peculiar in that it features a rotating black hole geometry supported with a static vector potential.
Although the metric satisfies the Einstein equations with the standard electromagnetic energy–momentum tensor, it only obeys the Maxwell equations with a non-trivial current on the right hand side.
The corresponding electromagnetic field obeys the equations of force-free electrodynamics sourced by a current derived from the
Deshpande–Lunin term (
8

The same construction also works

Appendix A
Charged and spinning BTZ black hole

The correct (‘current-less’) charged and spinning BTZ black hole was obtained in

[
7
,
8
]
,
by performing a ‘boost transformation’ on
the non-rotating charged seed. Such a solution is canonically written
in boosted (Kerr-like) coordinates.
Here we rewrite it in the ‘standard’ BTZ coordinates, for a comparison to the solution in the main text.

A.1
Boosting technique

We begin by reviewing the ‘boosting technique’, which can be appplied to any charged static solutions, including those in NLE.
Consider a static charged solution of the following form:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-Fdt^{2}+\frac{d\rho^{2}}{F}+\rho^{2}d\varphi^{2}\,,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi dt\,.\quad

(53)

Here, we decompose

F=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-M-E(\rho)\,,

(54)

where
E(\rho)
represents the ‘electromagnetic sector contribution’ to the metric function, and

\phi=\phi(\rho)
is the corresponding electrostatic potential, with
\rho
playing the role of the radial coordinate.

Applying the following boost

[
38
,
39
,
40
]
:

t\rightarrow\Xi t-a\varphi,\quad\varphi\rightarrow\frac{at}{\ell^{2}}-\Xi\varphi,\quad\Xi^{2}=1+\frac{a^{2}}{\ell^{2}}\,,

(55)

we obtain the solution

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-F(\Xi dt-ad\varphi)^{2}+\frac{\rho^{2}}{\ell^{4}}(adt-\Xi\ell^{2}d\varphi)^{2}+\frac{d\rho^{2}}{F}\,,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(\Xi dt-ad\varphi)\,,

(56)

written in the Kerr-like coordinates
typically used in the literature. We rewrite this in the BTZ coordinates

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{F\rho^{2}}{\Xi^{2}\rho^{2}-a^{2}F}dt^{2}+\frac{d\rho^{2}}{F}

(57)

\displaystyle+(\Xi^{2}\rho^{2}-a^{2}F)\Bigl(d\varphi+\frac{\Xi a(F\ell^{2}-\rho^{2})}{\ell^{2}(\Xi^{2}\rho^{2}-a^{2}F)}dt\Bigr)^{2}\quad

by completing the square with respect to the angular coordinate.

Assuming that
E(\rho)
falls off at infinity faster than
\rho^{2}/\ell^{2}
, the periodicity of the new angular coordinate
\varphi
can be identified with
2\pi
. This can be seen by employing the coordinate transformation

r^{2}\equiv\Xi^{2}\rho^{2}-a^{2}F(\rho)\,,

(58)

upon which the metric becomes

ds^{2}=-\frac{F\rho^{2}}{r^{2}}dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{Fr^{\prime 2}}+r^{2}\Bigl(d\varphi+\frac{\Xi(\rho^{2}-r^{2})}{ar^{2}}dt\Bigr)^{2},\quad\

(59)

where
r^{\prime}=\frac{dr}{d\rho}
. Comparing this to the standard form (
12
), namely:

ds^{2}=-N^{2}fdt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f}+r^{2}(d\varphi+hdt)^{2}\,,

(60)

we must identify

f=Fr^{\prime 2}\,,\quad N=\frac{\rho}{rr^{\prime}}\,,\quad h=\frac{\Xi(\rho^{2}-r^{2})}{ar^{2}}\,.

(61)

A.2
Vacuum

The simplest case to examine explicitly is the vacuum case, for which
E(\rho)=0
, that is,

F=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-M

(62)

implying that the transformation
(
58
) now reads

r^{2}=\rho^{2}+a^{2}M\quad\Rightarrow\quad\rho=\sqrt{r^{2}-a^{2}M}\,.

(63)

This in turn gives the following functions

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\,,\quad h=-\frac{a\Xi M}{r^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-\frac{(2a^{2}+\ell^{2})M}{\ell^{2}}+\frac{M^{2}a^{2}\Xi^{2}}{r^{2}}

(64)

and so upon identifying

j=-a\Xi M\,,\quad m=\frac{(2a^{2}+\ell^{2})M}{\ell^{2}}\,,

(65)

we recover the standard uncharged spinning BTZ black hole (
12
)–(
13
).

A.3
Maxwell

In the Maxwell case we have
E(\rho)=-2Q^{2}\log\left(\rho/\rho_{0}\right)
. The radial transformation then reads

r^{2}=a^{2}M+\rho^{2}+2a^{2}Q^{2}\log(\rho/\rho_0)\,.\

(66)

Due to the logarithm present in this equation the inversion for
\rho
is more difficult now, but nonetheless it has a solution of the form:

\rho^{2}=Q^{2}a^{2}\mbox{W}(x)\,,\quad x=\frac{{\rho_{0}}^{2}}{Q^{2}a^{2}}\exp\Bigl(\frac{r^{2}-a^{2}M}{Q^{2}a^{2}}\Bigr)

(67)

where
\mbox{W}(x)
is the Lambert-W function.
In turn, this yields the charged and rotating BTZ solution in the standard form via (
61
).
Obviously, the resultant solution contains Lambert functions and is quite complex and visually unappealing. In particular, we recover

N=\frac{\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}+a^{2}Q^{2}}\,,

(68)

which no longer equals unity. This is to be compared to the ‘simple solution’ (
12
)–(
14
) studied in the main text.

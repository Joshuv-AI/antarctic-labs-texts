Abstract
. We describe a proof of the following folklore theorem: If 𝒳 = G / K \mathcal{X}=G/K is the homogeneous space of a simply connected compact semisimple Lie group with Poisson-Lie stabilizers, then the q q -deformed algebras of regular functions ℂ ⁡ [ 𝒳 q ] \mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}] with 0 < q ≤ 1 0<q\leq 1 are mutually non-isomorphic as ∗ * -algebras.

Primary: 20G42, Secondary: 46L67, 17B37

1.
Introduction

Fix
G
a compact simply connected semisimple Lie group and
K
a proper closed Poisson-Lie subgroup. The homogeneous space
\mathcal{X}=G/K
admits a family of
q
-deformations,
0<q<\infty
, realized via their
*
-algebras of regular functions
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
.

The following theorem is folklore.

Theorem 1.1
.

\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{p}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
if and only if
p=q^{\pm 1}
.

In this expository note, we will give a proof of this fact. The ideas are mostly not original. A proof for the quantum spheres
\mathcal{X}_{q}=S^{2n+1}_{q}
was given by D’Andrea
[
DAn24
]
. Krähmer
[
Krä08
]
proved a similar result for the nonstandard Podleś spheres.

In the present context, the algebras
\mathbb{C}[G_{q}]
are due to Soibelman
[
Soi91
]
, see also
[
Wor87
]
. The algebras
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
were introduced over the following years, but a systematic study of their structure was undertaken by Stokman and Dijkhuizen
[
SD99
,
Sto03
]
. This was further developed by Neshveyev and Tuset in
[
NT12
]
. The essential points necessary for proving Theorem
1.1
are already contained in the papers just listed.

Giselsson
[
Gis20
]
proved that the enveloping
C^{*}
-algebras
C(\mathcal{X}_{q})
are all isomorphic for
q\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1\}
. In other words, the quantum spaces
\mathcal{X}_{q}

(0<q<1)
are isomorphic as noncommutative topological spaces but not as noncommutative algebraic varieties.

2.
Preliminaries

This article is not completely self-contained. We will make heavy use of the notation and results of
[
NT12
]
, especially Sections 1, 2 and the beginning of Section 3.

The isomorphism
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]\cong\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q^{-1}}]
is well-known,
cf.

[
NT13
, Lemma 2.4.2]
. Also,
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
is commutative if and only if
q=1
. We therefore work throughout with
0<p,q<1
.

3.
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)
and the quantum disk

The algebra
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]
is the universal
*
-algebra generated by two elements
\alpha
and
\gamma
satisfying

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{c}\alpha\gamma=q\gamma\alpha,\quad\alpha\gamma^{*}=q\gamma^{*}\alpha,\quad\gamma\gamma^{*}=\gamma^{*}\gamma\\
\alpha^{*}\alpha+\gamma^{*}\gamma=1=\alpha\alpha^{*}+q^{2}\gamma^{*}\gamma.\end{array}

The
*
-subalgebra
\mathbb{C}[\gamma,\gamma^{*}]
is a polynomial algebra in
2
commuting conjugate variables. We use the notation

\alpha^{(i)}=\begin{cases}\alpha^{i},&\text{if }i\geq 0,\\
(\alpha^{*})^{-i},&\text{if }i<0.\end{cases}

Putting

\mathcal{A}_{i}=\alpha^{(i)}\mathbb{C}[\gamma,\gamma^{*}]=\mathbb{C}[\gamma,\gamma^{*}]\alpha^{(i)}.

we get an algebra grading

(3.2)

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{A}_{i}.

The standard irreducible representation
\rho_{q}
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]
on
\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+}))
is given in the basis
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
by

(3.3)

\rho_{q}(\alpha):e_{n}\mapsto\sqrt{1-q^{2n}}\,e_{n-1},\quad\rho_{q}(\gamma):e_{n}\mapsto-q^{n}e_{n}.

Lemma 3.1
.

The kernel of
\rho_{q}
is the ideal generated by
\gamma-\gamma^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
a\in\ker(\rho_{q})
. Write
a=\sum_{i}c_{i}\alpha^{(i)}\in\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{A}_{i}
where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{C}[\gamma,\gamma^{*}]
. Since
\rho_{q}
maps
\mathcal{A}_{i}
to weighted shifts of degree
i
, we have
\rho_{q}(c_{i}\alpha^{(i)})=0
for each
i
.

Let
i\geq 0
. We have
\rho_{q}(c_{i}\alpha^{(i)}\alpha^{(i)*})=0
. Since
\rho_{q}(\alpha^{(i)}\alpha^{(i)*})
is diagonal with no zero entries, we get
\rho_{q}(c_{i})=0
. The spectrum of
\rho_{q}(\gamma)=\rho_{q}(\gamma^{*})
is
-q^{\mathbb{Z}_{+}}
which is Zariski dense in
\mathbb{R}
. So if we view
c_{i}
as a polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[\gamma,\gamma^{*}]\cong\mathbb{C}[Z,\overline{Z}]
, it is zero on the real line and hence divisible by
\gamma-\gamma^{*}
.

For
i<0
, we can take the adjoint to reduce to the previous case.
∎

We define
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}]=\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]/\langle\gamma-\gamma^{*}\rangle\cong\rho_{q}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)])
and let
F_{q}:\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]\to\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}]
denote the quotient map. Then
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}]
has generators

(3.4)

y=F_{q}(\gamma),\quad z=F_{q}(\alpha)^{*}

and relations

(3.5)

yz=qzy,\quad y=y^{*},\quad zz^{*}+y^{2}=1=z^{*}z+q^{2}y^{2}.

In Poisson language,
F_{q}
is the restriction of polynomials on
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)
to a certain quantized symplectic leaf.

Remark 3.2
.

The
*
-algebra
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}]
embeds into the
C^{*}
-algebra of the quantum disk
C(\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q})
of
[
NT12
, §3]
via the homomorphism

(3.6)

z\mapsto Z_{q},\quad y\mapsto-(1-Z_{q}Z_{q}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This is not an algebraic map, which is why we add the generator
y
.

4.
Homogeneous spaces for
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)

Write
T
for the diagonal torus in
\mathrm{SU}(2)
. For
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,\}
, let
T_{n}
denote the closed subgroup of
T
generated by the
n
th roots of unity. We also use the convention
T_{\infty}=T
.
The associated quantum homogeneous spaces
[
NT12
, §2]
are given by the quantized function algebras

(4.1)

\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}]=\Vect\{\alpha^{(i)}\gamma^{j}\gamma^{*k}\mid i+j-k\in n\mathbb{Z}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)],

where we use the convention
\infty\mathbb{Z}=\{0\}
. Explicitly
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}
is
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)
when
n=1
,
\mathrm{SO}_{q}(3)
when
n=2

[
Pod95
]
, the quantum lens space
L^{3}_{q}(n,1)
when
3\leq n<\infty

[
HS03
]
and the standard Podleś sphere when
n=\infty

[
Pod87
]
.

Let us also write

\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}]=F_{q}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}])\cong\rho_{q}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}]).

This is the quantization of a
2
-dimensional symplectic leaf of
\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}
.

5.
Commutator spectrum

Definition 5.1
.

Define the
commutator spectrum
of an algebra
\mathcal{A}
to be

\CommSp(\mathcal{A})=\{\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\mid\exists a,b\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{0\}\text{ with }ab=\omega ba\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The commutator spectrum of
\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}]
is

\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}])=\begin{cases}q^{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $n$ is odd}\\
q^{2\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if $n$ is even or $\infty$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Consider first the quantum plane algebra
\mathbb{C}_{q}[y,z]
generated by
y,z
with
yz=qzy
. We do not impose a
*
-structure for the moment. We have

(5.1)

(y^{j}z^{k})(y^{j^{\prime}}z^{k^{\prime}})=q^{jk^{\prime}-j^{\prime}k}(y^{j^{\prime}}z^{k^{\prime}})(y^{j}z^{k}).

This shows that
q^{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq\CommSp[\mathbb{C}_{q}[y,z])
. Moreover, by considering the leading terms for the lexicographical ordering on powers of
y
and
z
, one can deduce that
\CommSp(\mathbb{C}_{q}[y,z])=q^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Now suppose
\omega\in\CommSp(\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}])
, so we have
a,b\in\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}]
nonzero with
ab=\omega ba
. By considering the highest order terms in the grading (
3.2
), we can assume without loss of generality that
a\in F_{q}(\mathcal{A}_{i})
,
b\in F_{q}(\mathcal{A}_{i^{\prime}})
for some
i,i^{\prime}
. If both
i,i^{\prime}\leq 0
then
a,b\in\mathbb{C}_{q}[y,z]
so
\omega\in q^{\mathbb{Z}}
. If both
i,i^{\prime}>0
we can reduce to the previous case by taking adjoints. Finally, if
i<0
and
i^{\prime}>0
then we can reduce to the previous case by replacing
b
by
a^{m}b
for some sufficiently large
m
.

This proves that
\CommSp(\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}])\subseteq q^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and also that it is realized on monomials
a=y^{j}z^{k}
,
b=y^{j^{\prime}}z^{k^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}]
. Note also that it is closed under taking integer powers, by considering
a
and
b^{m}
.

If
n
is even or
\infty
, then Equation (
4.1
) forces
j\equiv k~[2]
and
j^{\prime}\equiv k^{\prime}~[2]
. Therefore, by Equation (
5.1
),
\CommSp(\mathbb{C}[\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{q}/T_{n}])\subseteq q^{2\mathbb{Z}}
. Putting
a=F_{q}(\gamma^{*}\alpha)=yz^{*}
,
b=F_{q}(\gamma^{*}\gamma)=y^{2}
gives
\omega=q^{2}
.

If
n
is odd, we can put
a=F_{q}(\gamma^{n+1}\alpha^{*})=y^{n+1}z
,
b=F_{q}(\gamma^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\gamma^{*\frac{n-1}{2}}\alpha^{*})=y^{n}z
and we get
\omega=q
.
∎

6.
Quantized homogeneous spaces

Let
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
be the simple roots of
G
and
\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{r}\}
the associated fundamental weights. We write
(\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}},\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}})
for the
G
-invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
with
(\alpha,\alpha)=2
for all short roots
\alpha
, and we use the standard notation
q_{i}=q^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})/2}
. Thus
q_{i}=q
,
q^{2}
or
q^{3}
depending on the length of
\alpha_{i}
.
For every
\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
, there is a restriction map
\sigma_{i}:\mathbb{C}[G_{q}]\to\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q_{i}}(2)]
.

The Poisson-Lie subgroups of
G
are determined by pairs
(S,L)
where
S
is a subset of the simple roots for
G
and
L
is a subgroup of the lattice
P(S^{c})=\mathbb{Z}\{\omega_{i}\mid\alpha_{i}\in S^{c}\}
.
We refer to
[
NT12
, Proposition 1.1]
for the details.
Fix such a subgroup
K^{S,L}
and consider
\mathcal{X}=G/K^{S,L}
and its quantized algebra of function
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

For every
\alpha_{i}\in S^{c}
we have
\sigma_{i}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}])=\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q_{i}}(2)/T_{n_{i}}]
for some
n_{i}\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
NT12
, Corollary 2.4]
.
Explicitly,
n_{i}
is the generator of the subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}
obtained by restricting the weights
\mu\in L
to weights for
\mathcal{U}_{q_{i}}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
,
i.e.
,

n_{i}\mathbb{Z}=\{(\mu,\alpha_{i})\mid\mu\in L\}.

∎

Let
T
be the maximal torus of
G
, and
T_{L}\subseteq T
the subgroup annihilated by
L\subseteq P=\widehat{T}
.
Let
W^{S}\subset W
be the set of
w\in W
such that
w(\alpha_{i})>0
for all
\alpha_{i}\in S
.

The irreducible representations of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
are indexed by pairs
(w,t)\in W^{S}\times T/T_{L}
. These representations
\pi_{w,t}

are described explicitly in the preamble to Theorem 2.2 of
[
NT12
]
. See also
[
SD99
, Theorem 5.9]
. We recall only that in the case where
w=s_{i}
is the simple reflection in
\alpha_{i}\in S^{c}
, the associated representation is

(6.1)

\pi_{s_{i},1}=\rho_{q}\circ\sigma_{i}:\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]\to\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{D}_{q_{i}}/T_{n_{i}}]\to\mathcal{B}(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{+})).

7.
Quantum
2
-cell representations

Definition 7.1
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
will be called a
2
-cell representation
if the norm closure of the image is an extension of an abelian
C^{*}
-algebra by the compacts—that is, if it fits into an exact sequence of
C^{*}
-algebras

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\pi(C(\mathcal{X}_{q}))\to A\to 0

with
A
abelian and
\mathcal{K}
being the compacts on a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space.

Example 7.2
.

The standard representation
\rho_{q}
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}]
is a
2
-cell representation for any
n\in\{1,2,\ldots,\infty\}
. To see this, let
I
denote the
*
-ideal of
\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)]
generated by
\gamma
and put
I_{n}=I\cap\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q}(2)/T_{n}]
. Then
\overline{\rho_{q}(I_{n})}=\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}({\mathbb{Z}_{+}}))
since it contains
\rho_{q}(\alpha^{*}\gamma)
which is a compact weighted shift with distinct nonzero weights.
And putting
\gamma=0
in the relations (
3.1
) shows that
\mathbb{C}[SU_{q}(2)/T_{n}]/I_{n}
is abelian.

Lemma 7.3
.

The representation
\pi_{w,t}
is a
2
-cell representation if and only if
w
is a simple reflection,
i.e.

w=s_{i}
for some
\alpha_{i}\in S^{c}
.

Proof.

The representation
\pi_{w,t}
is the representation
\pi_{w,1}
twisted by a character, so the image of
\pi_{t,w}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}])
is independent of
t
. Therefore, we may take
t=1
.

If
w=1
, then
\pi_{1,1}
is the counit.

If
w=s_{i}
for
i\in S^{c}
, then by Lemma
6.1

\pi_{w,1}(\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}])=\rho_{q_{i}}(\mathbb{C}[\mathrm{SU}_{q_{i}}(2)/T_{n_{i}}])
, so
\pi_{w}
is a
2
-cell representation by Example
7.2
.

If
w\in W^{S}
has Bruhat length two or more, then
[
NT12
, Theorem 4.1]
shows that
\pi_{w,1}(C(\mathcal{X}_{q}))
has a nontrivial nonabelian quotient, so
\pi_{w,1}
is not a
2
-cell representation.

∎

8.
Proof of the main theorem

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

By Lemmas
5.2
,
6.1
and
7.3
, the greatest value, less than one, of the commutator spectrum of the image of any
2
-cell representation of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
is
q^{m}
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
q
. This is an invariant of
\mathbb{C}[\mathcal{X}_{q}]
.
∎

Bridging the evidence gap: three-year BCG maintenance therapy shows enhanced protection over one-year regimen in Chinese NMIBC
Authors: Xiang Du, Zhenggen Deng, Yonghao Shi, Jiayang Ding, Hongqian Guo, Rong Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background and objectiveThe current recommendation of BCG therapy in China is a 1-year regimen, based on local strain variations and the absence of evidence for a 3-year instillation protocol. Our aim was to compare the protective efficacy of the 3-year BCG regimen versus a 1-year regimen in Chinese NMIBC patients.MethodsWe collected 140 patients followed by BCG monotherapy at our center from 2017 to 2024. All patients had ≥ 1 year of follow-up. Participants were allocated based on clinical practice into a standard three-year instillation group (n = 72) and a modified one-year group (n = 68). Kaplan–Meier survival analysis evaluated the impact of both regimens, while Cox regression identified factors for recurrence. Subgroup analyses and interaction tests were performed for all categorical variables. The prognostic role of instillation regimens across different risk stratifications was assessed via interaction and subgroup analyses.Key findings and limitationsKaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated superior RFS in the three-year group (HR 0.31; 95% CI 0.14–0.67). Multivariate Cox regression confirmed the three-year regimen as an independent protective factor against recurrence (p = 0.006, HR 0.282; 95% CI 0.114–0.697). The protective effect remained significant in high-risk subgroups. Adverse events were numerically fewer in the three-year group, though this comparison should be interpreted cautiously. This study is limited by its retrospective design, the marked imbalance in treatment completion between groups (25% vs. 51.5%), and the small number of recurrence events (n = 27), all of which preclude causal interpretation and require cautious reading of the hazard ratio estimates.Conclusions and clinical implicationsOur findings suggest that the standard 3-year regimen may offer superior recurrence risk reduction compared to the 1-year regimen in Chinese high-risk NMIBC patients. Given the retrospective design and limited sample size, these results should be considered hypothesis-generating and warrant confirmation in prospective studies.Patient summaryA 3-year BCG regimen may offer better protection than a 1-year regimen for Chinese high-risk NMIBC patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12957-026-04412-5.

Introduction

Bladder cancer (BC), one of the most prevalent malignancies of the urinary system worldwide, presents as non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) in approximately 75% of newly diagnosed cases [1]. The primary clinical challenge lies in its high five-year recurrence rate (31%−78%) and progression risk. High-grade tumors with carcinoma in situ exhibit substantially higher progression rates than low-grade NMIBC [2]. Since its introduction in the 1970 s, intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) therapy has become the standard treatment for intermediate- and high-risk NMIBC, demonstrating superior efficacy over alternative intravesical agents [1–3].

Current guidelines from the American Urological Association (AUA) and European Association of Urology (EAU) recommend a maintenance regimen of more than one year for Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) instillation therapy [4]. However, the optimal duration of maintenance remains under debate. An EORTC randomized controlled trial demonstrated that a full dose of BCG administered over three years was associated with a lower recurrence rate than a one-year regimen in high-risk patients, but this benefit was not observed in intermediate-risk patients [5]. In contrast, one study that reduced the number of both induction and maintenance instillations failed to demonstrate superiority over the standard protocol [6]. Similarly, another study employing single instillations per maintenance cycle for three years, following a complete induction course, showed no significant improvement in efficacy compared to induction therapy alone. This suggests that multiple instillations per maintenance cycle might be necessary [7].

In China, considerations of BCG supply constraints, economic factors, and distinctive local strain characteristics contribute to the lack of robust evidence for the three-year regimen. Consequently, the current Chinese guidelines endorse the one-year protocol. This study aims to systematically compare the effectiveness of the three-year versus one-year BCG maintenance regimens within a Chinese population. The findings are intended to provide preliminary real-world evidence that may help inform treatment strategies and guide future prospective research on a suitable maintenance protocol for patients in China..

Study protocol

A total of 140 patients were allocated postoperatively to two BCG instillation regimens based on institutional practice and shared decision-making between physicians and patients during the study period:Standard 3-year international protocol (27 instillations): After completing 6 induction instillations, patients received three consecutive weekly instillations at postoperative months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36.Modified Chinese protocol (19 instillations): Following 6 induction instillations, patients underwent one instillation every two weeks for 3 doses, then one instillation monthly for 10 doses, totaling 13 months.

BCG preparation

Both groups received 120 mg (equivalent to 1.2 × 10⁸ CFU) per instillation of Bisaiji (BCG for Therapeutic Use), manufactured by Chengdu Institute of Biological Products Co., Ltd. Each vial contained 60 mg (6.0 × 10⁷ CFU) of lyophilized BCG strain.

Procedure

Patients retained the instillate for 1–2 h post-administration. The retention period was reduced to ≥ 30 min for those with poor tolerance.

Follow-up and outcomes

Tumor recurrence was monitored through postoperative cystoscopic biopsy and abdominal imaging (CT, ultrasonography, or MRI). Any recurrence of non-muscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) after BCG induction therapy was considered treatment failure. Recurrence time and recurrence-free survival (RFS) were recorded for all patients.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics (version 27.0) and R (version 4.5.0). Normality of continuous variables was assessed with the Shapiro–Wilk test. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation, while categorical variables are expressed as percentages. Intergroup comparisons employed: Independent samples *t*-test for continuous variables; Chi-square test or Fisher's exact test for categorical variables. Key baseline variables and outcome data were complete in this cohort, as these are mandatory fields in our institutional electronic medical records. For the few missing values in other baseline variables, mean imputation was applied. Patients with incomplete follow-up or missing pathology data were excluded during the screening process. Recurrence-free survival (RFS) was evaluated using Kaplan–Meier curves with log-rank testing to compare recurrence rates between instillation regimens following transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT). Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards models were constructed to assess associations between treatment protocols and RFS. Hazard ratios (HR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. The multivariate Cox model incorporated all variables with p < 0.05 in univariate analysis plus clinically established prognostic factors.

Subgroup analyses and interaction tests for categorical variables were conducted using stratified Cox models. Per EAU guidelines, patients were stratified into high-risk (n = 110) and intermediate-risk (n = 30) groups. Due to the low event rate (n = 1) in the intermediate-risk subgroup (n = 30), this cohort was withdrawn from formal statistical inference, and all subsequent analyses focus on the high-risk population. For the high-risk subgroup, a parsimonious Cox model included three covariates: Instillation regimen, Histological stage, and Multifocality. Model robustness was verified through comparison of alternative multivariate specifications. Due to the retrospective nature of this study, treatment allocation was not randomized but determined by clinical practice. This observational design introduces the potential for selection bias, which is acknowledged in the limitations.

Results

Among the 140 enrolled patients, 72 (51.43%) were allocated to the 3-year instillation protocol (3-year group) and 9 cases of recurrence were observed (n = 9). 68 (48.57%) were allocated to the 1-year protocol (1-year group) postoperatively and 18 cases of recurrence were observed (n = 18). No patients received any concomitant therapies—including immunotherapy—beyond BCG instillation during the study period. A statistically significant between-group difference was observed in age distribution (P = 0.0129). All other baseline characteristics showed no significant differences between the two treatment groups.

We documented the incidence of adverse events (AEs) in both cohorts (Table S6). Discussions

Bladder cancer persists as one of the primary contributors to global cancer mortality. Among all urothelial bladder cancers, about 70% are non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC). Based on the European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer (EORTC) risk scoring system, NMIBC is stratified into three risk categories: low, intermediate, and high. This classification correlates with the likelihood of recurrence and progression, and guides surveillance regimens and adjuvant intravesical therapy [8]. For NMIBC, intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guérin (BCG) instillation represents the current therapeutic standard. Intermediate-risk tumors warrant a full course of intravesical BCG therapy for up to 1 year. High-risk disease requires full-dose intravesical BCG treatment administered for 1 to 3 years [9, 10].

BCG instillation therapy emerged in the 1970s. Validation occurred through a larger cohort study conducted by the Southwest Oncology Group (SWOG) [11]. This randomized controlled trial was the first to establish the clinical efficacy of BCG treatment in inducing bladder cancer regression. BCG maintenance therapy demonstrated superior long-term therapeutic advantages relative to identical regimens omitting the maintenance component [12].

The therapeutic efficacy of BCG instillation is well-established. Five prior meta-analyses confirmed BCG's advantage over TURBT monotherapy or TURBT plus chemotherapy for preventing NMIBC recurrence [13–17]. Three randomized controlled trials focusing on intermediate-high-risk tumors demonstrated BCG's superiority in recurrence prevention when compared against epirubicin-interferon combinations, epirubicin monotherapy, or MMC. This durable effect persisted in subgroup analyses of intermediate-risk patients [18]. An individual patient data (IPD) meta-analysis revealed that within BCG maintenance trials, BCG conferred a 32% reduction in recurrence risk versus MMC, while non-maintenance BCG regimens were associated with a 28% increased recurrence risk [10].

Current Western treatment standards adhere to the SWOG protocol: induction therapy (weekly instillations for 6 weeks) followed by maintenance therapy (3 weekly doses at months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36), culminating in a 3-year treatment course. Clinical evidence indicates that complete maintenance therapy elevates 5-year recurrence-free survival rates beyond 60% in high-risk NMIBC patients [19]. Several systematic reviews have demonstrated no significant correlation between BCG dosing and progression to MIBC, metastasis development, or mortality. Reduced-dose BCG regimens correlate significantly with elevated recurrence risk yet demonstrate lower rates of febrile reactions and treatment discontinuation. Notably, this recurrence association becomes non-significant when maintenance protocols are implemented [20]. Disease-free interval (DFI) analyses establish that one-third standard dosing with 1-year maintenance proves suboptimal compared to the 3-year full-dose regimen [5].

Multiple BCG substrains exist globally. Due to regional access limitations, China primarily utilizes the domestically manufactured substrain, characterized by a reduced per-administration dosage (60 mg), below international standards. Compared to Western substrains, immunogenicity data for this Chinese variant remains limited, with some studies indicating potentially modest efficacy differences. Our observed recurrence rate of 18.57% (26/140) in the 3-year protocol group aligns with reported outcomes for various BCG strains, which typically range between 15–25% at 3 years. This finding corroborates existing literature indicating no significant therapeutic differences among multiple BCG strains [1, 21].

Contemporary BCG immunotherapy faces several clinical challenges, including treatment discontinuation due to toxicity and the emergence of BCG-unresponsive disease, which are discussed below.

Despite BCG's status as first-line therapy with an established safety profile, treatment-associated toxicities significantly impact therapeutic adherence. Studies indicate > 70% of patients experience adverse events, with 8% discontinuing therapy due to toxicity. Most side effects manifest within hours post-instillation, typically resolving within 48–72 h [22]. While generally safe for NMIBC, several adverse event patterns are documented. Most patients report flu-like symptoms or bladder discomfort [23], BCG its incidence ranges from 1–4.3% across studies, confirming its rarity [24].

Intravesical BCG therapy demonstrates clinical limitations, notably including BCG-unresponsive disease.

Patients with BCG-unresponsive NMIBC derive minimal benefit from additional BCG and represent a population requiring urgent therapeutic alternatives [9]. Clinical guidelines consequently recommend early radical cystectomy (RC) as the preferred intervention, though emerging bladder-preserving strategies including novel intravesical agents and systemic immunotherapy are expanding treatment options for patients who are unfit or unwilling to undergo RC. Our findings support optimizing first-line BCG therapy to potentially reduce the incidence of BCG-unresponsive disease, reinforcing the importance of risk-adapted treatment selection. However, substantial patient refusal rates for RC necessitate alternative bladder-preserving strategies such as immunotherapy and targeted agents [25].

To mitigate BCG shortages and improve adherence to prolonged regimens, China implemented a modified instillation schedule: After completing 6 induction doses, patients receive biweekly instillations (3 total) followed by monthly maintenance (10 total) over 13 months (cumulative 19 doses; 120 mg/dose). The control arm followed standard SWOG protocol: 6 induction doses plus 3-week maintenance cycles at months 3, 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36 (cumulative 27 doses; 120 mg/dose).

In our cohort, High-risk NMIBC was defined according to EAU criteria, encompassing patients with T1 stage, high-grade (G3), multifocality, large tumor size (> 3 cm), or concomitant CIS. However, high-risk NMIBC itself is heterogeneous; for instance, the TaG3 subset has been shown to have a more favorable prognosis than previously assumed, with progression outcomes intermediate between TaG2 and T1G3 [26]. Additionally, differences between the WHO 1973 and WHO 2004/2022 grading systems may impact risk stratification and should be considered when interpreting outcomes across studies [27, 28]. Intermediate-risk NMIBC represents a heterogeneous disease category with variable oncological outcomes. Recent evidence suggests that factors such as tumor size and focality play a more critical role than grade alone in determining recurrence risk within this subgroup [29]. In our study, the intermediate-risk cohort comprised only 30 patients with a single recurrence event, precluding any meaningful statistical inference. Therefore, our findings do not support definitive recommendations for BCG maintenance duration in intermediate-risk NMIBC. This aligns with the current EAU/AUA guidelines, which acknowledge the need for risk-adapted strategies in this diverse patient population. It should also be noted that patients with variant histologies (e.g., micropapillary, plasmacytoid, sarcomatoid) were excluded from our cohort, as these entities are recognized as important drivers of prognosis and may exhibit different responses to BCG therapy [30]. Our findings therefore apply specifically to pure urothelial NMIBC. Smoking status, a well-established risk factor for bladder cancer recurrence and progression, was balanced between groups in our cohort (Table 3). Before interpreting the efficacy findings, it is critical to emphasize that treatment completion was markedly imbalanced between groups (25% in the 3-year group vs. 51.5% in the 1-year group). Therefore, the analysis primarily compares the intended prescribing strategies rather than balanced actual treatment exposure. This central limitation precludes any causal interpretation of the observed associations.

Kaplan–Meier analysis demonstrated significantly prolonged recurrence-free survival with the 3-year protocol versus the modified 1-year regimen (p < 0.01), indicating superior prognostic protection with standard therapy. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression identified treatment protocol as an independent predictor of recurrence (p < 0.05), alongside confirmed risk factors: age, tumor multiplicity, tumor size, and pathological grade. Stratification by EORTC risk criteria yielded high-risk (n = 110) and intermediate/low-risk (n = 30) cohorts. Limited events in the intermediate/low-risk group (n = 1) precluded meaningful analysis. In high-risk patients, adjusted Cox models confirmed the 3-year protocol remained an independent protective factor (Table S5). We plan targeted enrollment to enable comparative analysis in this subgroup.

Although the three-year regimen has a longer total duration, the interval between instillations is substantially longer (maintenance cycles of three weekly doses every 3 months) compared to the one-year regimen, which may contribute to reduced cumulative toxicity. However, the markedly lower completion rate in the three-year group introduces survivorship bias, as only the most tolerant patients continued treatment. Given the limited sample size and descriptive nature of this comparison, our AE findings should be interpreted with caution and may be influenced by chance.

China's substantial population base contributes to a significant burden of bladder cancer cases characterized by high recurrence rates. Our findings for high-risk patients are consistent with the hypothesis that the standard 3-year regimen may reduce recurrence and disease progression compared to the 1-year regimen, while the optimal approach for intermediate-risk NMIBC remains to be defined.. Given the lack of evidence for the three-year regimen within China, this study provides critical evidence to advance therapeutic strategies for Chinese patient populations.

Several potential confounders should be considered. A significant baseline age imbalance was present, with the 1-year group being older. Older age may correlate with frailty, treatment discontinuation, and physician preference for shorter regimens. Although age was adjusted for in the multivariable analysis, residual confounding from unmeasured factors cannot be excluded. Additionally, numerical imbalances in tumor characteristics (size, multifocality, grade) were observed, which may have contributed to outcome differences despite lack of statistical significance. Moreover, with only 27 recurrence events overall, the multivariable Cox regression model including multiple covariates is at risk of overfitting, and the estimated hazard ratios should be interpreted with substantial caution. The limited event number means that even statistically significant findings should be considered exploratory and require confirmation in larger independent cohorts. The limited sample size also precluded meaningful subgroup analysis of intermediate-risk patients, and our findings should not be extrapolated to this population. Last, a central limitation of this study is the substantially different treatment completion rates between groups (25% in the three-year group vs. 51.5% in the one-year group), which introduces potential time-related bias. Although groups were defined at treatment initiation on an intention-to-treat basis, the analysis effectively compares intended schedules rather than balanced actual treatment exposure. This limitation fundamentally limits any causal interpretation. Per-protocol or landmark sensitivity analyses are not feasible due to the limited sample size and low event count (n = 27), which would yield underpowered and biased estimates. Thus, our findings represent an intention-to-treat comparison of the two prescribing strategies and must be interpreted with this central limitation in mind. Future prospective trials are needed to confirm whether completing a full 3-year course confers additional benefit.

Our study is not without limitations. First, the retrospective design introduces potential selection bias, as treatment allocation was based on clinical practice rather than randomization, precluding causal inferences. Second, the relatively small cohort size and exclusion of patients with < 1 year follow-up may introduce analytical imprecision and survivorship bias. Third, inherent documentation gaps and potential ascertainment bias could impact the validity of statistical outcomes. Future investigations with larger, prospective, and ideally randomized controlled designs are needed to confirm our results.

Conclusions

In this retrospective cohort, the standard 3-year instillation protocol was independently associated with a lower risk of postoperative recurrence, while advanced age, multiple tumors, larger tumor size, and higher pathological grade (T1 versus Ta) were identified as independent risk factors. Although the 1-year protocol demonstrated reduced BCG consumption and shorter treatment duration, the 3-year regimen was associated with significantly superior recurrence risk reduction in this cohort, with numerically fewer reported adverse events, though this safety comparison should be interpreted with caution. This protective effect was substantially amplified in high-risk NMIBC subgroups. In conclusion, given the marked imbalance in treatment completion rates (25% vs. 51.5%) and the limited number of recurrence events (n = 27), this retrospective study provides only preliminary, hypothesis-generating evidence that the standard 3-year BCG instillation protocol may be associated with reduced recurrence risk compared to the 1-year regimen in Chinese high-risk NMIBC patients. Confirmation in larger prospective studies with more events is required before definitive conclusions can be drawn. For intermediate-risk patients, the optimal BCG maintenance strategy remains uncertain and warrants further prospective investigation in larger, well-defined patient populations.

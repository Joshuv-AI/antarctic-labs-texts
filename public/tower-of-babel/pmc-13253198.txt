Dual-functional catalytic photothermal immunological agent for ferroptosis induction and cGAS-STING pathway activation: Enhancing tumor immunotherapy and generating robust immune memory
Authors: Haitao Yuan, Xiaoxian Wang, Yun Meng Bai, Jingbo Ma, Wenzhe Chen, Junhui Chen, Mengyun Hou, Zhijie Li, Ying Long, Jigang Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe resistance to apoptosis and immune evasion of tumor cells significantly increase the risk of cancer treatment failure. Ferroptosis has emerged as a new strategy to address apoptosis resistance in tumor therapy. However, traditional ferroptosis inducers often lead to drug resistance due to the compensatory mechanism of GPX4, while the synergistic effect of photothermal-immunotherapy can circumvent this limitation. Additionally, relying solely on innate immunotherapy may fail to effectively suppress aggressive tumors with "cold" and immunosuppressive microenvironments, thereby inducing immune escape. Here, we have meticulously developed a mild photothermal immunotherapy nanoplatform (termed Fe-Mn@GOx NPs), designed to induce non-apoptotic ferroptosis, augment cGAS-STING pathway activation and enhance innate anti-tumor immunity. This strategy directly disrupts the structure of heat shock protein (HSP) to restore the sensitivity of cancer cells and enhance antitumor immunity. Notably, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs-mediated ferroptosis induces an elevation in reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels within tumor cells. Additionally, glucose depletion itself primarily affects ATP levels. ROS-mediated oxidative damage, combined with ATP depletion-induced functional inhibition, synergistically inhibits heat shock protein (HSP) expression, thereby offering a potent approach for mild photothermal immunotherapy. More critically, the cGAS-STING pathway in macrophages is further activated by tumor-derived endogenous DNA contained within Fe-Mn@GOx NPs-induced dead cell debris, simultaneously promoting the generation of long-term immune memory T cells. The interplay between ferroptosis and the cGAS-STING pathway triggers rapid induction of systemic antitumor immunity, leading to suppression of tumor growth. The engineered nanotherapeutic platform enables a novel tumor immunotherapy strategy through specific cGAS-STING pathway activation.Keywords: cGAS-STING, Ferroptosis, Elicit long term memory, Photothermal therapy

Introduction

The immunogenic cell death of tumor cells has emerged as a research hotspot in oncotherapy. This special cell death mode is essential for initiating specific anti-tumor immune responses and improving the therapeutic efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade combination regimens [1]. At present, highly efficient immunogenic cell death (ICD) is mainly mediated through apoptosis or necroptosis [2,3]. Nevertheless, tumor cells often develop resistance to such cell death programs, which greatly compromises the therapeutic effect of ICD-based antitumor strategies [4,5]. Consequently, the development of novel highly immunogenic cancer cell death modalities is critically imperative.

Ferroptosis is a unique type of non-apoptotic programmed cell death modality [6]. It primarily relies on iron-dependent catalysis to convert endogenous hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) into highly cytotoxic hydroxyl radicals (•OH), thereby inducing lethal levels of lipid peroxidation (LPO) [7]. Ferroptosis constitutes a mutationally refractory cell death modality, independent of apoptosis/necrosis/autophagy and impervious to classic oncogenic alterations in apoptotic regulators [8,9]. As a result, it can serve as a new form of cell death to improve the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [10,11]. However, cells possess intrinsic redox homeostasis systems that enable them to counteract reactive oxygen species (ROS)-mediated cellular injury [[12], [13], [14]]. For instance, glutathione (GSH) is abundantly expressed in tumor cells and serves as a crucial reducing agent, taking on the responsibility of eradicating oxidative stress and maintaining intracellular redox equilibrium [15]. Consequently, depleting intracellular GSH is considered an effective supplementary measure to enhance therapeutic effects through the strategy of generating reactive oxygen species (ROS). For this purpose, researchers have carried out in-depth investigations into the ferroptosis combined with GSH depletion strategy for improving cancer treatment outcomes [16,17].

Moreover, due to the potential synergistic effects among different treatment modalities, the paradigm shift from unimodal treatment to combined treatment has gained significant momentum. The role of ROS-related signaling pathways is continuously expanding, which also prompts people to combine ferroptosis with other cancer treatment methods such as catalytic therapy, sonodynamic therapy, photothermal therapy, chemodynamic therapy and immunotherapy [[18], [19], [20], [21]]. Although this combined treatment is generally beneficial to treatment results, the traditional strategy of simply combining two or more treatment modes often yields poor effects because it lacks consideration of the inherent mechanism associations between different modes. For example, ferroptosis is primarily mediated through Fe2+-catalyzed reactive oxygen species (ROS) generation [22]. Further optimization of this strategy remains essential owing to persistent tumor cell protective adaptations and inadequate catalytic performance in the tumor environment.

Photothermal therapy (PTT) demonstrates significant clinical merits such as minimal invasiveness, low toxicity, and convenient operation, presenting itself as a highly promising precision cancer treatment approach [23]. Although photothermal therapy (PTT) induces direct thermal ablation of cancer cells, concomitant HSP overexpression activates cytoprotective adaptations during treatment. This weakens the damage inflicted by PTT, hampers tumor destruction, and ultimately leads to treatment failure [24]. In light of these challenges, there is an urgent need to develop advanced PTT strategies. To address these challenges, efforts have been made to combine PTT with strategies that inhibit HSP expression in order to enhance the therapeutic effect of the sole photothermal effect. Multiple strategies have been developed to target HSPs, such as small-molecule inhibitor intervention, which have yielded favorable experimental outcomes [25]. Different from the above intervention approaches, targeted destruction of HSPs structures serves as a more efficient way to block their biological activities. The massive buildup of intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) and lipid peroxides (LPO) in ferroptotic cells could significantly inhibit HSPs production [26]. This process further weakens the thermal self-defense capacity of tumor cells and breaks their endogenous protective mechanisms. In addition, the expression level of heat shock proteins is closely correlated with intracellular adenosine triphosphate (ATP) content [27,28]. Therefore, inhibiting the production of HSP expression by suppressing ATP through tumor starvation can also be effective. More importantly, the increase in temperature is closely associated with the induction of immunogenic cell death (ICD), as it can cause endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress, which to a certain extent leads to the exposure of calreticulin to the outer plasma membrane (ecto-CRT, a key marker of ICD) [27]. Furthermore, multifunctional nano-photothermal agents enabling deep-penetrating NIR photoconversion to hyperthermia critically potentiate radical tumor ablation [28,29]. Inspired by these findings, we wonder if it is possible to further activate ICD by inducing ferroptosis under photothermal induction and inhibiting the expression of heat shock proteins to enhance immunogenicity.

Simultaneously, as the primary defense against pathogens, the innate immune system plays a vital role in initiating and maintaining T cell responses in cancer immunotherapy [[30], [31], [32], [33]]. The cyclic guanosine monophosphate-adenosine monophosphate synthase (cGAS)-stimulator of interferon genes (STING) signaling acts as a core innate immune signaling pathway, and it has gradually become a promising target for immunotherapy to strengthen anti-tumor immune responses [[34], [35], [36], [37], [38]]. Nevertheless, Beyond recognizing pathogen-derived danger signals (e.g., viral/bacterial components), the cGAS-STING pathway detects endogenous danger-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) including mitochondrial DNA and phagocytosed cellular debris [39]. Mitochondrial damage induces mtDNA release, which subsequently mediates activation of the cGAS-STING pathway [40]. However, specific activation of the cGAS-STING pathway critically depends on enhanced cGAS-DNA binding sensitivity, promoted cGAMP synthesis, and increased cGAMP-STING affinity [41,42]. Mn2+ dually potentiates cGAS sensitivity to DNA and enhances STING activation, presenting a promising therapeutic opportunity. For example, Wang and colleagues constructed a protein-derived nanoplatform loaded with β-lapachone. This agent can trigger immunogenic cell death in tumor tissues and promote the release of intracellular dsDNA. In addition, Mn2+ is capable of strengthening the perception of dendritic cells toward dsDNA, further amplifying the activation of the STING cascade, and ultimately eliciting robust systemic anti-tumor immune responses [43]. Meanwhile, ferroptosis, which is triggered by unregulated lipid peroxidation, can induce the production of cytoplasmic dsDNA from impaired mitochondria as well as dsDNA derived from dead cell debris, thereby achieving bidirectional activation of the cGAS-STING pathway [44]. Combination therapy based on different antitumor mechanisms is expected to achieve enhanced tumor treatment effects. However, traditional strategies that combine two or more treatment modes often fall short of maximizing tumor treatment effects due to the lack of consideration of the inherent mechanism associations between different modes.

Based on this, inspired by the remarkable effectiveness of the cGAS-STING pathway in antitumor immunotherapy and ferroptosis as a novel cancer cell death approach with high immunogenicity, we have constructed a self-catalytic enhanced mild photothermal therapy (PTT) strategy (Scheme 1). This therapeutic approach destructs HSP architecture in a direct manner. It restores the drug susceptibility of tumor cells and boosts anti-tumor immune responses via triggering ferroptosis and activating innate immune signaling. As a multifunctional nanoplatform, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs can act as both a ferroptosis trigger and a cGAS-STING pathway agonist, exerting potent therapeutic effects against triple-negative breast cancer. (1) Fe-Mn@GOx NPs possess a Fe-Mn bimetallic catalytic center and exhibit excellent catalytic activity, enabling in situ conversion of endogenous H2O2 to cytotoxic •OH, thereby degrading HSPs to potentiate transition metal-mediated mild photothermal therapy. (2) Tumor starvation therapy represents another promising strategy to restrict ATP synthesis and reverse HSP-induced tumor thermoresistance. The elevated ROS accumulation alongside depleted glucose content markedly inhibits HSP expression, which greatly supports the mild photothermal therapy mediated by Fe-Mn@GOx NPs. (3) Fe-Mn@GOx NPs is capable of depleting intracellular glutathione (GSH), which renders the cellular "reactive oxygen species (ROS) scavengers" unable to reduce ROS. The accumulated excess ROS further facilitates the generation of lipid peroxidation (LPO), ultimately instigating ferroptosis in tumor cells. (4) The self-catalytic enhanced mild PTT strategy can not only activate immunogenic cell death (ICD) and cause ferroptosis. At the same time, the release of exogenous DNA further activates the cGAS-STING pathway. Thus, the cGAS-STING pathway is bidirectionally activated. (5) The self-catalytic enhanced mild PTT strategy mediated by Fe-Mn@GOx NPs awakens innate immunity through the activation of the cGAS-STING signaling pathway, thus driving tumor-associated macrophage (TAM) repolarization and dendritic cell (DC) maturation for enhanced antigen presentation. Subsequently, tumor-specific cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs) infiltrate into tumor tissues and long-term memory T cells are generated. In conclusion, this study presents a new photothermal therapy strategy that simultaneously induces ferroptosis and awakens innate immunity, establishing a novel perspective for immunotherapy-refractory of triple-negative breast cancer.

Results and discussions

Preparation and characterization of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs

To achieve this design, we synthesized a nanosized bimetallic-based nanopatticle (Fe-Mn@GOx NPs) through one-step self-assembly between iron/manganese ions and the catechol ligand (2,3,6,7,10,11-hexahydroxytriphenylene, HPT). Then, glucose oxidase (GOx) was incorporated via a one-pot self-assembly method to prepare Fe-Mn@GOx NPs. As depicted in Fig. 1a and b, the particle size of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs was measured using transmission electron microscopy (TEM). Moreover, the diamater and zeta potential of nanoparticles showed 164.5 nm and 5.2 mV (Fig. 1c–S1). Meanwhile, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs remained stable over 7 days (Fig. S2). The positive charges on the surface of nanomedicines can establish electrostatic interactions with the negatively charged cell membranes. While triggering endocytosis, these interactions also induce the exocytosis of nanomedicines within cells. This dual-process enables the nanomedicines to penetrate deeper into tumors. Furthermore, the presence of carbon (C), oxygen (O), manganese (Mn), and iron (Fe) elements further validated the successful synthesis of the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs (Fig. 1d). Subsequently, we assessed the photothermal conversion capacity of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs at various concentrations under 808 nm laser irradiation for 5 min. As detected by infrared thermal imaging (Fig. 1e), when exposed to an 808 nm laser with a power density of 0.8 W/cm2, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs rapidly heated up to above 50 °C within 5 min. As depicted in Fig. 1f, with the increase in concentration, the temperature ascended sharply, exhibiting an obvious concentration dependence. This remarkable thermal response of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs stands in stark contrast to the thermal response observed in PBS solution, highlighting the superior photothermal conversion ability of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs. Under the 808 nm laser irradiation, the photothermal stability of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs was evaluated through multiple heating and cooling cycles (Fig. 1g). This stringent test indicates that the heating efficiency of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs does not significantly decrease over multiple cycles, emphasizing their potential as stable and reliable adjuvants in photothermal therapy (PTT) applications. According to the previous reported method, it was calculated that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs have a relatively high photothermal conversion efficiency (PCE, as shown in Fig. 1h and i). This performance indicates its potential as an effective photothermal agent for photothermal immunotherapy applications.

Glucose oxidase (GOx), being a substance capable of rapidly depleting glucose and molecular oxygen within tumor cells. The GOx enzymatic activity stability of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs before and after NIR irradiation has been verified by GOx kit detection. The results showed that there was no significant difference in the GOx enzymatic activity of the nanomaterials after NIR irradiation (Fig. S3). Inspired by the excessive production of H2O2 during the process of GOx catalyzing glucose, we further examined the glucose-consuming ability of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs. As depicted in Fig. 1j, with the increase in its concentration, the glucose consumption level markedly rises. Additionally, upon the generation of hydrogen peroxide, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs will further produce reactive oxygen species (ROS), thereby causing damage to cells. As shown in Fig. 1k, the emission intensity of 2′,7′-dichlorofluorescein diacetate (DCFH) indicator gradually augments. It is widely recognized that the endogenously overpressured hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) (approximately 100 × 10−6 M) within the tumor microenvironment (TME) can be decomposed into •OH species by POD-like nanozymes, thus leading to the apoptosis of tumor cells. In this study, we found that the obtained Fe-Mn@GOx NPs demonstrated remarkable POD-like catalytic activity. Subsequently, we explored the POD-like activity of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs using 3,3′,5,5′-tetramethylbenzidine (TMB). As illustrated in Fig. 1l, an intense UV–vis absorption of oxTMB emerged after Fe-Mn@GOx NPs were incubated with TMB in the presence of H2O2 (20 mM) because the colorless TMB was oxidized to the blue-colored oxTMB by •OH species. In addition, ESR spectroscopy with DMPO trapping further verified •OH generation via the Fenton-like reaction (Fig. 1m). To sum up, owing to the outstanding photothermal effect of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and the continuous generation of ROS, the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs nanozyme might possess great potential in tumor treatment.

In vitro therapeutic effect of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and underlying mechanisms

We evaluated the in vitro anticancer efficacy and mechanism of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs, taking into consideration the multi-enzymatic properties and the photothermal effect. As shown in Fig. 2b, we conducted Calcein-AM/PI cell staining on live/dead cells. Firstly, in the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs group, the red fluorescence intensity (PI fluorescence) of dead cells in this group is markedly enhanced compared with the blank group. Why is this so? The plausible reason resides in that the glucose within tumors can be converted into H2O2 under the catalytic action mediated by GOx, and subsequently is catalyzed by POD to generate •OH (Fig. 2c). The synthesized agent induces tumor cell apoptosis, as evidenced by the enhanced red fluorescence intensity characteristic of deceased cells. Furthermore, the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with near-infrared (NIR) irradiation regimen exhibits a higher proportion of dead cell populations compared to control groups. These findings are consistent with the quantitative data obtained from MTT cytotoxicity assays (Fig. 2e). The fundamental mechanism underlying this phenomenon is that photothermal therapy induces significant alterations in cellular homeostasis, thereby driving neoplastic cells toward a pre-apoptotic state characterized by irreversible cytotoxic damage. This therapeutic modality results in a marked elevation of apoptotic indices within treated cohorts. Emerging evidence from contemporary studies underscores that photothermal therapy not only exerts selective ablation of neoplastic cells through hyperthermic effects but also concomitantly triggers immunogenic cell death (ICD) in malignant populations. This bioactive process elicits robust systemic antitumor immunity by releasing damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), subsequently activating dendritic cell maturation and T-cell-mediated adaptive responses. As depicted in Fig. 2d–f and g, we further explored the changes in related markers. When cells undergo immunogenic cell death (ICD), calreticulin (CRT), adenosine triphosphate (ATP), and high-mobility group box 1 protein (HMGB1) all experience a series of significant alterations, and these changes are essential for potentiating the body's antitumor immune reactions. These results further corroborate the occurrence of ICD. In light of the fact that glucose oxidase (GOx) remarkably elevates the concentration of hydrogen peroxide (H2O2) while substantially diminishing the production of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), it subsequently triggers the tumor starvation mechanism, suppresses the defense system and leads to cell damage. As shown in Fig. 2h–S4, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs significantly inhibited glucose uptake as well as lactate production compared with the Blank group. These results further indicate that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs can effectively reduce glucose uptake rate and lactate production, and regulate tumor glucose metabolism.

To more thoroughly probe into the mechanism by which Fe-Mn@GOx NPs induces tumor cell death, we have conducted comprehensive and systematic research. Previous studies have indicated that the generated ROS level is already sufficient to cause cell damage. Moreover, the generated •OH can also oxidize polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) and induce the formation of lipid oxides (LPO). The accumulation of LPO and the consumption of GSH are significant characteristics of ferroptosis. Consequently, to further clarify the mechanism of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs inducing ferroptosis in cells, we evaluated other related indicators of ferroptosis. As depicted in Fig. 2i, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs can significantly reduce the GSH level in tumor cells. It is particularly noteworthy that this reduction effect is further enhanced after laser irradiation. As depicted in Fig. 2k and l, the combined treatment of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and NIR irradiation markedly increased the contents of malondialdehyde (MDA) and 4-hydroxynonenal (4-HNE), two typical lipid peroxidation-derived toxic metabolites. In addition, ferroptosis-related proteins (FSP1 (Ferroptosis suppressor protein 1), HO-1 (Heme Oxygenase-1), GPX4 (Glutathione Peroxidase 4)) further confirm that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs has a significant therapeutic effect in inducing cell oxidative stress and triggering ferroptosis in cells (Fig. 2j). Furthermore, the relative contributions of ferroptosis and apoptosis to cell death and immune activation were also dissected. We used the ferroptosis-specific inhibitor Ferrostatin-1 and apoptosis inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK to clarify their respective roles both in vitro and in vivo. In vitro, MTT assays revealed that treatment with Ferrostatin-1 restored cell viability to a significantly higher level (≈48%) compared with the apoptosis inhibitor Z-VAD-FMK (≈35%), while the untreated Fe-Mn@GOx NPs + NIR group showed only ≈35% viability (Fig. S5). This result directly demonstrates that ferroptosis plays a more dominant role in mediating cell death in our model. In vivo, flow cytometry analysis of tumor-infiltrating immune cells showed that inhibition of ferroptosis (Ferrostatin-1 treatment) led to markedly reduced activation of M1 macrophages (CD86+F4/80+: 32.7%), dendritic cells (DCs, CD86+CD80+: 36.3%), and increased activation of M2 macrophages (CD206+F4/80+: 42.3%) compared with the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs + NIR group (49.4%, 44.7%, and 30.1%, respectively) (Fig. S6). In contrast, inhibition of apoptosis (Z-VAD-FMK treatment) resulted in less pronounced reduction of immune activation (M1: 40.7%, DCs: 39.0%). Collectively, these data demonstrate that ferroptosis and apoptosis coexist in our experimental system, and both can regulate immune activation. In addition, previous studies on nano-catalyzed ferroptosis mainly focused on disrupting the antioxidant defense system by inactivating glutathione peroxidase 4 (GPX4) and increasing the level of lipid oxides. These reports often only pay attention to the consumption status of glutathione but overlook its regeneration potential. In fact, the reduction cycle of oxidized glutathione (GSSG) is accomplished by glutathione reductase (GR) with the assistance of electrons provided by nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide phosphate/H+ (NADPH/H+). As an electron donor, NADPH plays the role of an endogenous ferroptosis inhibitor by responding to antioxidant GSH regeneration. As shown in Fig. 2m, after treatment with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR groups, the NADPH level is significantly reduced, which further verifies that the depletion of NADPH catalyzed by nano-drugs on GOx nanoenzymes substantially hinders the regeneration of GSH, inactivates GPX4, promotes lipid peroxidation, and thus leads to ferroptosis.

Subsequently, we have performed Circular Dichroism (CD) spectroscopy assays to verify the structural changes of HSPs upon treatment. Untreated HSP70 displayed a typical folded globular protein CD profile, with prominent characteristic positive peaks at approximately 208 nm and 222 nm (Fig. S7). After treatment with Fe-Mn@Gox NPs and NIR irradiation, the signature positive peaks of HSP70 completely vanished, a strong negative peak emerged at 190-195 nm, accompanied by a shift in the spectra. This indicated that the secondary structure of HSPs was indeed altered/destroyed. In addition, we quantified expression of common heat shock proteins (HSPs), specifically HSP70 and HSP90, with the aim to verify the cleavage ability of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs towards HSPs. Experimental results revealed that tumor cells exhibited obvious downregulation of HSP70 and HSP90 following Fe-Mn@GOx NP intervention. This evidence further verified the excellent capacity of the nanoagent to suppress HSP expression (Fig. 2j). Additionally, under laser irradiation, the expression of HSPs further declined, suggesting that the combination of catalytic therapy and photothermal therapy achieved a synergistic effect. To sum up, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs catalyze endogenous H2O2 to •OH in situ, thereby inducing HSP degradation and potentiating photothermal efficacy. Moreover, by depleting intracellular GSH and accelerating lipid peroxidation, nanoparticles provoke oxidative stress-mediated ferroptosis in malignant cells (Fig. 2a).

RNA sequencing and analysis after the treatment of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR

To comprehensively elucidate the gene expression patterns of tumor cells following photothermal therapy, we initiated our analysis with sample correlation assessment. As depicted in Fig. 3a, the intra-group correlation coefficients of the two sample sets reached an impressively high 0.98, contrasting sharply with the inter-group correlation of around 0.7. This disparity clearly underscores the substantial biological differences between the samples. Subsequently, differential expression analysis was performed on the RNA-seq data of the two sample groups (Fig. 3b), leading to the identification of 3720 upregulated and 3411 downregulated differentially expressed genes (DEGs). Pathway enrichment analysis further revealed that upregulated genes were predominantly associated with physiological processes such as autophagy, apoptosis, and endoplasmic reticulum stress, while downregulated genes were mainly involved in biological functions including mRNA processing and DNA replication (Fig. 3c and d).To gain deeper insights into these pathway alterations, we employed the Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) algorithm to calculate pathway scores. The results robustly confirmed the significant upregulation of both the apoptosis and endoplasmic reticulum stress pathways (Fig. 3e). Additionally, a heat map was utilized to visually represent the expression profiles of apoptosis-related genes across all samples (Fig. 3f), including key genes such as Dnajc10, Opa1, Bcl2, Sirt1, and Gsk3b etc. In summary, the RNA-seq analysis provided compelling evidence that this nanosystem could remarkably suppress the proliferation of tumor cells through photothermal treatment.

Activation of the intracellular cGAS-STING pathway

In the course of the study, we found that the tumor-derived endogenous DNA in the debris of dead cells caused by Fe-Mn@GOx NPs, thereby activating the cGAS-STING pathway in neighboring immune cells, such as macrophages within the TME (Fig. 4a). First, we discovered that cancer cell DNA could be released into the tumor microenvironment (TME) during the photothermal therapy (PTT) process (Fig. 4b). Additionally, western blotting was employed to detect cGAS-STING pathway-related proteins in mouse peritoneal macrophages treated with the supernatant of 4T1 cells after various treatments. The supernatants of untreated or NIR-treated 4T1 cells did not exhibit obvious activation of the STING pathway as indicated by the expression of cGAS, phosphorylated STING, IRF3, and P65. Consistent with the experimental expectation, the supernatant derived from 4T1 cells after Fe-Mn@GOx NP single treatment or combined NIR intervention notably upregulated the phosphorylation levels of STING, IRF3 and P65, as well as the expression of cGAS in macrophages. Among all groups, the culture supernatant of 4T1 cells receiving Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with near-infrared irradiation triggered the strongest activation of the STING signaling cascade (Fig. 4c). This phenomenon was attributed to the synergistic effects of ferroptosis and photothermal action. To further confirm that the observed activation is mediated by the cGAS-STING pathway, we used the STING inhibitor H151 to treat macrophages. We observed that the addition of H151 significantly reduced the expression levels of p-STING, cGAS, and p-P65 (Fig. S8), which confirmed that the activation of the pathway induced by tumor cell-derived mtDNA depends on STING. The inhibitory effect of H151 further confirmed that this activation is carried out through the classical cGAS-STING pathway.

Subsequently, we evaluated the expression levels of the ISGs IFNB1, CXCL10, CCL5, and IL-6, as representative factors of the type I interferon system for STING activation. As show in Fig. 4d–g, the data showed that the expression levels of targeted downstream genes associated with STING activation and such as IFNB1, CXCL10, CCL5, and IL-6 were significantly increased after Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR treatments. Meanwhile, we have stained for total P65 and total IRF3, which are visible across all experimental groups. Moreover, after incubation with the supernatant collected from Fe-Mn@GOx NPs monotherapy cells, partial nuclear translocation of these proteins was observed. This observation suggests that the STING pathway is activated, initiating downstream signaling (Fig. 4a).

In vivo anticancer activity of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs after intratumoral injection

Subsequently, the investigation on the in vivo therapeutic effects of the proposed treatment modality was carried out. Prior to this, we investigated the tumor targeting of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs in vivo. The results showed that the nanoparticles rapidly accumulated at the tumor site, reached a fluorescence peak at 8 h, and were retained in the tumor up to 48 h, demonstrating favorable tumor targeting and retention effects. In the meanwhile, the fluorescence intensity in the tumor gradually decreased from 24 h to 48 h, suggesting that the nanoparticles were gradually cleared by the body (Fig. S9). As depicted in Fig. 5a, we initially injected 4T1 cells into the right hind leg of mice to establish the primary tumor model. When right tumor volumes approached 100 mm3, the mice were randomized into four groups (n = 5): 1) the PBS group, 2) the NIR (0.8 W/cm2, 10 min) group, 3) the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs group, 4) the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group (0.8 W/cm2, 10 min). At 24 h after intratumoral injection of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs, the tumors were treated with NIR irradiation (0.8 W/cm2, 10 min), which was repeated every other day for two cycles. The in vivo photothermal image reveals that in the tumors treated with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and near-infrared, the temperature ascends rapidly (as illustrated in Fig. S10). In contrast, under the identical irradiation conditions, the temperature in the group irradiated solely by NIR only increases marginally. This remarkable disparity clearly indicates that the accumulation of Fe-Mn@GOx in the tumor masses is sufficient to induce outstanding photothermal effects. During the treatment period, the tumor volume was monitored every other day to assess the anti-tumor efficacy of each group. Simultaneously, bioluminescence imaging was employed to evaluate tumor growth. Among the four groups, compared with the PBS group and the NIR alone group, the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs alone group exhibited an inhibitory effect on the bioluminescence signal. However, in the mice treated with the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group, the bioluminescence signal of the tumor decreased most markedly over time. These data suggest that the effect generated by Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR treatment can inhibit tumor growth to the greatest extent (Fig. 5b). A similar trend is also observed in the tumor growth curve. When Fe-Mn@GOx NPs is used alone, it has an inhibitory effect on tumor growth, and it is more pronounced compared with the PBS group and NIR group. However, compared with the treatment using Fe-Mn@GOx NPs alone, the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group demonstrates a more superior anti-tumor effect (Fig. 5c–S11). Through histological analysis, including H&E, TUNEL, and Ki-67 staining, of tumor tissues, the anti-tumor treatment effect was affirmed (Fig. 5e–h). It was discerned that the tumor tissues treated with either Fe-Mn@GOx NPs alone or Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR manifested obvious tissue damage, apoptosis, and proliferation inhibition. Simultaneously, the index (CRT) of tumor tissue-induced ICD was also evaluated. The results manifested that the surface staining of the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group was the most conspicuous, intimating that the expression level of CRT in the tumor tissues of this group was relatively elevated (Fig. 5g,i). This might imply that it has a more notable effect in inducing immunogenic cell death, which is in line with the in vitro results. The immunofluorescence staining results reflect the significant role of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR treatment in tumor tissues from multiple aspects, providing an important foundation for further research on its anti-tumor mechanism. Moreover, the body weights of the mice in each group showed no significant changes during the treatment process, indicating that the nanoparticles did not produce obvious toxicity in the body (Fig. 5d). In addition, the results of the hemolysis test showed that there was no obvious hemolysis phenomenon in Fe-Mn@GOx NPs (Fig. S12). Moreover, the H&E staining images showed that there was no obvious tissue damage or inflammation in the main organs (heart, liver, spleen, lung and kidney), confirming that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs have good biosafety and biocompatibility (Fig. S13).

Immunomodulatory capacity of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR treatment in vivo

With the effective induction of apoptosis and ferroptosis, combined with the potential immune response highlighted by RNA-seq analysis, we hypothesize that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs NPs plus NIR treatment may have the ability to trigger an immune response. Subsequently, we aim to validate the immunomodulatory efficacy of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR treatment within the body. Thereafter, the proportions of immune cells in tumors following diverse treatments were assessed via flow cytometry. Primarily, mature dendritic cells (CD80+CD86+) within tumor tissues were probed. After treatment with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with NIR, the proportions of mature dendritic cells in tumors amounted to 30.7% and 44.4% respectively, being 10% and 23.7% higher than that in the PBS group (Fig. 6a and b). Subsequently, the polarization status of macrophages in tumor tissues was examined. As depicted in Fig. 6c–f, the proportions of M1 macrophages (F4/80+CD86+) in tumors in the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with NIR groups were 48% and 57.9% respectively, which were higher than 35.9% in the PBS group. The proportions of M2 macrophages (F4/80+ CD206+) in tumors in the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group were 40.5% and 35.3% respectively, which were lower than 50.3% in the PBS group. In the meanwhile, the M1/M2 ratio of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group was also the highest among different groups (Fig. 6g). These findings suggest that compared with the PBS group, Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR group can activate DC cells, modulate macrophage polarization, and enhance the anti-cancer immune response. Concurrently, immune-stimulating factors subsequent to different treatments were further evaluated. The results demonstrated that after treatment with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with NIR, the levels of IL-1β, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFN-γ were the highest, exceeding those in the group treated solely with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs (Fig. 6h–k). In summary, these results indicate that Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with NIR treatment can elicit a systemic anti-cancer immune response and remodel the tumor microenvironment (Fig. 6l).

Combinational treatment provoke long-term anticancer memory on tumor recurrence

Memory T cells are capable of conducting immune surveillance over tumor cells. They can recognize tumor-associated antigens (TAAs), which are molecular markers expressed on the surface of tumor cells and distinct from those of normal cells. Once tumor cells are detected, memory T cells can promptly initiate an immune response to eliminate them. Secondly, when re-stimulated by tumor-associated antigens, memory T cells are able to undergo rapid proliferation and differentiate into functional effector T cells. These effector T cells are able to directly kill tumor cells and enhance the anti-tumor immune response. After successful tumor treatment, memory T cells can persist in the body for a long time and maintain immune memory against tumors. This is akin to establishing a long-term anti-tumor defense line within the body to prevent tumor recurrence.

In order to explore the in vivo immune memory T cell induced by Fe-Mn@GOx NPs after PTT, on day 40 following the initial treatment, we introduced age-matched mice as new controls. A single-cell suspension of 4T1 cells (5 × 105 cells) was subcutaneously inoculated into naive control mice. Meanwhile, three tumor-free mice that had received prior photothermal therapy were subjected to the same cell inoculation. As shown in Fig. 7a, 4T1 tumor-bearing mice were established and received treatments as aforementioned. On day 40 after tumor eradication, cured mice were re-inoculated with tumor cell together with age-matched naïve mice as controls. Tumor size and body weight were monitored every other day. As shown in Fig. 7b–c, mice treated with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR showed complete elimination of the primary tumor and a significantly prolonged survival compared with the PBS group. After a 40 day observation, no tumor recurrence was detected in those cured mice, and we rechallenged these cured mice and control naive mice with the same 4T1 cancer cells. The results indicated that no tumor was induced in these cured mice treated with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR, while tumor cell inoculation in naive mice resulted in rapid tumor development (Fig. 7d–f). These results suggest that the combination therapy of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR would induce the production of memory T cells. Functionally, memory T-cells have been reported to control tumor growth effectively and are necessary for long-lasting anticancer immunity against tumor recurrence. Subsequently, we also analyzed the effector memory. Memory T-cell responses were evaluated by quantifying tumor-infiltrating CD8+ and CD4+ T cells in mice after different treatments (Fig. 7g–j). Indeed, flow cytometry analysis of the spleens of cured mice treated with Fe-Mn@GOx NPs plus NIR revealed that the levels of effector memory T cells (CD3+ CD44+ CD62L−) and central memory T cells (CD3+ CD44+ CD62L+) were markedly elevated in both T cell populations (CD3+ CD4+ and CD3+ CD8+) (Fig. 7g–j). These findings suggested that the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs combined with NIR treatment could significantly promote the formation of the memory T-cell phenotype, thereby establishing durable immune memory against recurrent breast tumors.

Conclusion

In this study, we adopted a comprehensive strategy that integrates innate immune responses with tumor-targeting PTT to simultaneously tackle multiple immune-suppressive mechanisms in breast cancer. We have constructed a self-catalytic enhanced mild photothermal therapy (PTT) strategy. This strategy directly disrupts the HSP structure to restore tumor cell sensitivity and enhance antitumor immunity by inducing ferroptosis and awakening innate immunity. It can serve simultaneously as a ferroptosis inducer and a cGAS-STING immune adjuvant (Fe-Mn@GOx NPs) for triple-negative breast cancer. We also confirmed that enhanced ROS and reduced glucose level significantly suppresses the expression of HSP, thus providing a powerful means for the mild PTT of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs. Additionally, the release of substantial amounts of cellular DNA from dying tumor cells, thereby activating the cGAS-STING pathway in neighboring immune cells, such as macrophages, within the TME. This process transforms the immunosuppressive (“cold”) TME into an immunoactive (“hot”) TME. This strategy can potently induce the differentiation of T cells into Tcm, amplify the accumulation of T cells in tumor tissues, and restrain the proliferation of recurrent tumors.

Materials and methods

Preparation of the Fe-Mn@GOx NPs

Briefly, 8.1 mg FeCl3 was dissolved into 1 ml MnNO3, and sonicate for 5 min for further use. Then, 15 mg Gox was also dissolved into 8 ml deionized water, which was mixed into the above MnNO3 solution containing FeCl3. Afterward, dissolve 6.8 mg HPT in 2 ml DMSO, drop it into the previous mixture (pH 6.8), and stir for 2 h to obtain nanoparticles.

Cell viability assay

4T1 cells at a density of 5 × 103 cells per well were inoculated into 96-well plates and incubated overnight. Subsequently, cells were treated with gradient concentrations of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs and maintained overnight at 37 °C. Afterward, samples were divided into laser-treated and untreated groups, with 808 nm near-infrared irradiation applied at 0.8 W/cm2 for 10 min. All cells were further cultured at 37 °C for 12 h. Finally, the CCK-8 method was adopted to evaluate cell viability.

Live/dead cell staining assay

After overnight culture of 4T1 cells (5 × 103 cells/well) in 96-well plates, gradient concentrations of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs were added and incubated overnight (37 °C). Cells were then exposed to 808 nm laser (0.8 W/cm2, 10 min) or kept untreated, followed by 12 h incubation (37 °C). Post-incubation, cells underwent PBS washing, dual staining with calcein-AM/PI, and immediate imaging via confocal microscopy (Leica SP8, Germany).

Photothermal measurement

To inquiry photothermal effect of Fe-Mn@GOx NPs, there are diverse concentrations (25, 50, and 100 μg mL−1) of 1 mL Fe-Mn@GOx NPs solution placed in 1.5 mL Eppendorf tube with the irradiation of a 808 nm laser for 5 min, respectively. Real-time temperature monitoring was performed via FLUKE thermal imaging technology. To quantify the photothermal conversion efficiency (PCE), aqueous suspensions of nanoparticles were irradiated with 808 nm laser (continuous, 600 s) while monitoring temperature dynamics. Then PCE (η) was calculated based on Equation according to our previous reports [45].

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was performed with GraphPad Prism (San Diego, CA), with results expressed as mean ± SEM. Student's t-test was used to compare data. P-values: *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001, and ****P < 0.0001.

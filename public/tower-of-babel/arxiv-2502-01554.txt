Small eigenvalues of Toeplitz operators, Lebesgue envelopes and Mabuchi geometry
Siarhei Finski

Abstract




MnLargeSymbols’164
MnLargeSymbols’171

Abstract.

We study small eigenvalues of Toeplitz operators on polarized complex projective manifolds.
For Toeplitz operators whose symbols are supported on proper subsets, we prove the existence of eigenvalues that decay exponentially with respect to the semiclassical parameter.
We moreover, establish a connection between the logarithmic distribution of these eigenvalues and the Mabuchi geodesic between the fixed polarization and the Lebesgue envelope associated with the polarization and the non-zero set of the symbol.
As an application of our approach, we also obtain analogous results for Toeplitz matrices.

Introduction

Before describing our results, let us describe the possible pathologies of the symbols.
We denote
K\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\esssupp f
,

; here

Note that the set

depends on the choice of the measurable representative of

corresponding to two different representatives differ only by a Lebesgue negligible set, and in all subsequent constructions, we will rely solely on properties of

that are unaffected by the addition or removal of such Lebesgue negligible sets.

Immediately from the definitions, we see that the closure of
NZ
coincides with

.
Moreover,

is Lebesgue non-negligible.
Note, however, that the Lebesgue measure of
Z
, which we denote by
\lambda(Z)
, can take an arbitrary value in
[0,\lambda(K)[
.
For example of a function
f
with
\lambda(Z)
non-zero, take the indicator function of a complement of a Smith-Volterra-Cantor set (which is a nowhere dense closed subset of positive measure) or the indicator function of an interior of an Osgood curve in

These are exactly the pathologies we will circumvent in

\lambda(Z)=0

Observe that these pathologies might still appear even for smooth functions

[
15

To state our results, we fix a Lebesgue-measurable subset

on

h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A}=\inf\Big\{h^{L}_{0}\text{ with psh potential }\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}\text{ almost everywhere on }A\Big\},

where psh above stands for

(induced by the Lebesgue measure on

Observe that adding or removing a Lebesgue negligible set from

The envelope construction (

But unlike psh envelopes, for which the potential becomes psh only after the upper semi-continous regularization, the potential of

Following the analogous notion in complex pluripotential theory, cf.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
,
f\neq 0
, be such that
(K,h^{L})
is Lebesgue pluriregular and
\lambda(Z)=0
.
We let
c(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max_{x\in X}\log(h^{L}(x)/h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},K}(x))
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is

, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\exp(-(c(f)+\epsilon)k)\leq\lambda_{\min}(T_{k}(f))\leq\exp(-(c(f)-\epsilon)k).

(1.3)

Remark 1.2
.

Under the given assumptions, the Lebesgue envelope coincides with the psh envelope, see Proposition
3.6
.
However, the distinction between these two notions becomes significant in the next result, where no assumption on Lebesgue pluriregularity is made.

Another essential ingredient for this paper is the concept of a Mabuchi geodesic, which provides a specific method for constructing paths between metrics on
L
with bounded plurisubharmonic potentials.
For the necessary background, see Section
8
.
We denote by
h^{L}_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, the Mabuchi geodesic between
h^{L}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}
and
h^{L}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}
.
While in general,
h^{L}_{t}
is not smooth in

, one can still define its time derivative at

due to convexity.
We denote by

\phi(h^{L},NZ)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(h^{L}_{t})^{-1}\frac{d}{dt}h^{L}_{t}|_{t=0}

(1.4)

the speed of the geodesic at
t=0
.
We shall prove in Section
8
that
\phi(h^{L},NZ)
is a non-positive bounded function, it is strictly negative away from
K
, and it vanishes on the Lebesgue dense points of
NZ
(see (
4.1
) for the definition of Lebesgue dense points).
In particular, the support of
\phi(h^{L},NZ)
and

intersect at most along the topological boundary of

The main result of this paper, detailed below, asserts that the logarithmic distribution of the small eigenvalues is determined by this function
\phi(h^{L},NZ)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
,
f\neq 0
, and continuous
g\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\real\to\real
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}\sum_{\lambda\in{\rm{Spec}}(T_{k}(f))}g\Big(\frac{\log(\lambda)}{k}\Big)=\frac{\int_{X}g(\phi(h^{L},NZ))c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}}{\int_{X}c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}}.

(1.5)

Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
collectively imply that the asymptotic shape of eigenvalues having exponential decay depends solely on the non-zero set
NZ
of the symbol and not on the nature of its decay in the vicinity of the boundary of
NZ
.
This goes in sharp contrast with the properties of the smallest eigenvalue of a Toeplitz operator with a continuous symbol when

consists of a single point, see
[
31
, §5.5, 5.6]
.

We now briefly discuss some qualitative consequences of the above results.
The following result gives an answer to

Corollary 1.4

For any

,

\lim_{\epsilon\to+0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\#\{\lambda\in{\rm{Spec}}(T_{k}(f))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\lambda\leq\exp(-\epsilon k)\}}{\#{\rm{Spec}}(T_{k}(f))}=\frac{\int_{X\setminus K}c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}}{\int_{X}c_{1}(L,h^{L})^{n}}.

(1.6)

Remark 1.5

The statement (

The next result states that in a certain sense the distribution of small eigenvalues depends solely on the geometry of the manifold away from the essential support of the symbol.
For

Corollary 1.6

Assume that for two complex manifolds

Corollary 1.7

To conclude, we briefly outline our approach to the theorems mentioned above.
Our approach relies on analyzing the operators
\frac{1}{k}\log T_{k}(f)
; we show specifically that these operators coincide asymptotically with the Toeplitz operator associated with the symbol
\phi(h^{L},NZ)

Once this is established, the above results would follow directly from the detailed analysis of

To relate

with Toeplitz operators, we interpret

as the transfer operator between the

We then extend a result of Berman-Boucksom-Witt Nyström

concerning the asymptotic study of the

The relation between

and Toeplitz operators is then derived by extending the previous result of the author
[
36

on the asymptotics of the transfer operators between

-norms associated with smooth volume forms.

3

Determining measures and non-negligible psh envelopes

This section studies the non-negligible psh envelopes associated with a non-pluripolar Borel measure and compares them with classical psh envelopes.
As an application, we interpret the determining property of a measure through the identity between these envelopes.

Although our primary motivation concerns the Lebesgue measures on measurable subsets of
X
, which forms a very particular class of non-pluripolar Borel measures, we have chosen to develop the theory in a more general setting.
The proofs of the results do not simplify dramatically in the Lebesgue case, but by presenting the general picture first, we hope to make the distinctive features of the Lebesgue case more apparent.
This more general setting will also play a crucial role in Section
10
, where we study generalized Toeplitz operators and Toeplitz matrices.

We denote by
\mu
a non-pluripolar Borel probability measure on
X
with support
K
.
For a continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
, following
[
38
]
, we define the
non-negligible psh envelope

h^{L}_{\mu}
as

h^{L}_{\mu}=\inf\Big\{h^{L}_{0}\text{ with psh potential }\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}\text{$\mu$-almost everywhere on }K\Big\}.

(3.1)

Remark that this definition depends only on the absolutely continuous class of the measure
\mu
.

We fix a non-pluripolar subset
E\subset X
.
Following Siciak
[
54
]
, Guedj-Zeriahi
[
39
]
, we define the
psh envelope

h^{L}_{E}
associated with
E
as

h^{L}_{E}=\inf\Big\{h^{L}_{0}\text{ with psh potential }\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}\text{ over }E\Big\}.

(3.2)

The non-pluripolarity of
E
assures that the metric
h^{L}_{E}
has a bounded potential, cf.
[
39
, Theorem 9.17]
.
A pair
(E,h^{L})
is called
pluriregular
if
h^{L}_{E}
is continuous, cf.
[
42
, p. 186]
.

Note that while the inequality
h^{L}_{E}\geq h^{L}
on
E
is immediate, it is not obvious a priori that
h^{L}_{\mu}
admits a potential that is bounded somewhere.
The following result due to Guedj-Lu-Zeriahi
[
38
, Proposition 2.10]
, however, establishes this.

Proposition 3.1
.

The metric
h^{L}_{\mu}
has a bounded psh potential and there is
E\subset K
,
\mu(E)=0
, such that
h^{L}_{\mu}=h^{L}_{K\setminus E}
.

Remark 3.2
.

a) The set
E
is not uniquely determined.
The reader can verify that if
E
satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
3.1
, then so does any enlarged set of the form
E\cup F
where
F\subset K
is an arbitrary subset with
\mu(F)=0
.
Note, however, that directly from Zorn’s lemma and
[
40
, Proposition 9.19.3]
, there is
a minimal

E\subset K
,
\mu(E)=0
, such that
h^{L}_{\mu}=h^{L}_{K\setminus E*}
.

b) As over
K\setminus E
, we trivially have
h^{L}_{K\setminus E}\geq h^{L}
, we infer by Proposition
3.1
that
h^{L}_{\mu}\geq h^{L}
over
K\setminus E
.
In particular, the infimum from (
3.1
) has the minimal element.
If
h^{L}
has moreover a psh potential, by the trivial bound
h^{L}\geq h^{L}_{\mu}
, then we even have
h^{L}_{\mu}=h^{L}
over
K\setminus E
.

We fix a positive metric
h^{L}_{0}
on
L
, denote
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\pi c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{0})
, and let
\phi
(resp.
\phi_{E}
,
\phi_{\mu}
) be the potential of
h^{L}
(resp.
h^{L}_{E}
,
h^{L}_{\mu}
), cf. (
1.7
).
Directly from the definitions, we can rewrite

\displaystyle\phi_{\mu}=\sup\Big\{\phi_{0}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\phi_{0}\leq\phi\text{ $\mu$-almost everywhere on }K\Big\},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\phi_{E}=\sup\Big\{\phi_{0}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\phi_{0}\leq\phi\text{ on }E\Big\}.

Immediately from this, we then have

\phi_{\mu}=\sup\Big\{\phi_{K\setminus F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mu(F)=0\Big\}.

(3.4)

The reader will then check that the second part of Proposition
3.1
can be reformulated as follows:
for any
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
, we have

\esssup_{\mu}(\psi-\phi)=\sup_{X}(\psi-\phi_{\mu}).

(3.5)

Below we provide an independent proof of Proposition
3.1
for the convenience of the reader.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

First, we establish that
\phi_{\mu}
is uniformly bounded.
Suppose, for contradiction, that there exists a sequence of sets
E_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\mu(E_{i})=0
, such that
\sup_{X}\phi_{K\setminus E_{i}}\to+\infty
.
Remark that for
E\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}E_{i}
, we have
\phi_{K\setminus E_{i}}\leq\phi_{K\setminus E}
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and as a consequence,
\sup_{X}\phi_{K\setminus E}=+\infty
.
However, since
\mu(E)=0
, we have
\mu(K\setminus E)=1
, and so
K\setminus E
is non-pluripolar by our assumption on
\mu
.
But then from
[
39
, Theorem 9.17]
,
\sup_{X}\phi_{K\setminus E}<+\infty
, which gives a contradiction.
We conclude that
\phi_{\mu}
is uniformly bounded.

We will now show that
\phi_{\mu}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.
For this, we first remark that for any non-pluripolar
F\subset X
, we have
\phi_{F}^{*}=\phi_{F}
away from a pluripolar (or empty) subset by
[
40
, Theorem 4.42]
.
We denote by
G\subset X
this subset, and then we see that
\phi_{F}^{*}
is one of the contenders for the supremum in the definition of
\phi_{F\setminus G}
, which immediately implies that
\phi_{F}^{*}\leq\phi_{F\setminus G}
.
Since
\mu
is non-pluripolar, we conclude by (
3.4
) and the above

\phi_{\mu}=\sup\Big\{\phi_{K\setminus E}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mu(E)=0\Big\}.

(3.6)

By the uniform boundness of
\phi_{\mu}
, (
3.6
), the fact that
\phi_{K\setminus E}^{*}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
, cf.
[
40
, Theorem 9.17]
, and
[
28
, Proposition I.4.24]
, we deduce that
\phi_{\mu}^{*}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.
Let us establish that
\phi_{\mu}^{*}=\phi_{\mu}
, which would then imply that
\phi_{\mu}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.

Indeed,
\phi_{\mu}^{*}=\phi_{\mu}
away from a pluripolar (or empty) subset
G\subset X
by
[
40
, Theorem 4.42]
.
But since
\mu(G)=0
, we conclude that
\phi_{\mu}^{*}\leq\phi

\mu
-almost everywhere, which means that
\phi_{\mu}^{*}
is one of the contenders in the supremum from the definition of
\phi_{\mu}
, which shows that
\phi_{\mu}^{*}\leq\phi_{\mu}
.
Clearly, the last inequality implies
\phi_{\mu}^{*}=\phi_{\mu}
, as we trivially have
\phi_{\mu}^{*}\geq\phi_{\mu}
.

Let us now establish the existence of
E\subset X
,
\mu(E)=0
, so that
\phi_{\mu}=\phi_{K\setminus E}
.
By (
3.4
), it suffices to find
E\subset X
,
\mu(E)=0
, so that
\phi_{\mu}\leq\phi_{K\setminus E}
.
By Choquet’s lemma, cf.
[
40
, Lemma 4.31]
, and (
3.6
) there is a countable family
E_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu(E_{i})=0
, so that
\phi_{\mu}=(\sup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\{\phi_{K\setminus E_{i}}^{*}\})^{*}
.
But then as before, for
F\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}E_{i}
, we have
\phi_{K\setminus E_{i}}\leq\phi_{K\setminus F}
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and as a consequence,
\phi_{\mu}\leq\phi_{K\setminus F}^{*}
.
Remark also that we trivially have
\mu(F)=0
.
But then, as
\phi_{K\setminus F}^{*}=\phi_{K\setminus F}
away from a pluripolar (or empty) subset
G\subset X
by
[
40
, Theorem 4.42]
, for
E\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=F\cup G
, we obtain
\phi_{K\setminus F}^{*}\leq\phi_{K\setminus E}
, and

.
This implies that
\phi_{\mu}\leq\phi_{K\setminus E}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Let us now recall the definition of a

from

.

A non-pluripolar Borel measure

Remark 3.4

a) The definition of a determining measure clearly only depends on the absolutely continuous class of the measure

b) Some authors, cf.
[

.

The following statements are equivalent:

1.

The measure
\mu
on

is determining for
(K,h^{L})
.

2.

We have

.

Let

.

Now, let

We then immediately obtain

For the next proposition, we say that
(K,h^{L})
is

Proposition 3.6
.

The following statements are equivalent:

1.

The measure
\mu

and

For each measurable subset

, such that

.

\esssup_{\mu}(\psi-\phi)=\sup_{K}(\psi-\phi)
.

We have

.

5.

The pair

is
\mu

Proof.

The equivalence between

and

was established in
[

.
To see the implication

, which immediately implies that

.
Let us show that

.
It is immediate that

, as

.
The fact that

To discuss the converse, remark that

Remark that the metric

has automatically a lower semi-continuous potential by the same argument as after (
3.7
).

Proposition 3.7
.

For any continuous metric
h^{L}
, we have
Q(h^{L}_{\mu})=h^{L}_{K}
.

Proposition
3.7
is a consequence of Proposition
3.1
, the topological density of
K\setminus E
in
K
for any
E\subset K
verifying
\mu(E)=0
(which is a direct consequence of the fact that the support of
\mu
equals
K
) and the following more general result.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any continuous metric
h^{L}
and subset
E\subset X
, we have
Q(h^{L}_{E})=h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
, where
\overline{E}
is the closure of
E
.

Proof.

First of all, according to (
3.7
) and the discussion after, we have
Q(h^{L}_{\overline{E}})=h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
.
Since the application of
Q
clearly preserves the order, we deduce immediately from the trivial bound
h^{L}_{E}\leq h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
that
Q(h^{L}_{E})\leq h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
.
To finish the proof of Proposition
3.7
, it is hence enough to establish that if
h^{L}_{0}
has a continuous psh potential and
h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}_{E}
, then
h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
.
By the definition of
h^{L}_{\overline{E}}
, it suffices to establish that over
{\overline{E}}
, we have
h^{L}_{0}\geq h^{L}
.
But this follows directly from the fact that
h^{L}_{E}\geq h^{L}
over
E
, the continuity of
h^{L}
,
h^{L}_{0}
and the density of
E
in
{\overline{E}}
.
∎

4

Lebesgue dense points and psh envelopes

This section aims to specialize the theory from Section
3
to the Lebesgue envelopes.
It turns out that a part of the previous reasoning can then be made more precise, and the set
E
from Proposition
3.1
admits an explicit description through the subset of Lebesgue dense points.

Recall that a point
x\in X
is called a
Lebesgue dense point
of a measurable subset
A\subset X
if

\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{\lambda(B_{x}(r)\cap A)}{\lambda(B_{x}(r))}=1,

(4.1)

where
B_{x}(r)
is a geodesic ball around
x
of radius
r>0
with respect to some fixed Riemannian metric.
While the notion of a geodesic ball clearly depends on the choice of the Riemannian metric, the notion of a Lebesgue dense point actually doesn’t.
To see this, remark that geodesic balls associated with different metrics have bounded eccentricity in the sense of
[
57
, p. 108]
.
Moreover, by
[
57
, Corollary 3.1.7]
, for a sequence of open neighborhoods with bounded eccentricity
U_{r}
,
r>0
, shrinking to a Lebesgue dense point
x
, as
r\to 0
, one can replace
B_{x}(r)
in (
4.1
) by
U_{r}
.

Lebesgue’s density theorem, cf.
[
57
, Corollary 3.1.5]
, states that the set
d(A)
of Lebesgue dense points of
A
has the full measure in
A
, i.e.
\lambda(A\setminus d(A))=0
.
By applying this statement twice: for
A
and its complement, we deduce that the symmetric difference between
A
and
d(A)
is Lebesgue negligible.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any measurable subset
A\subset X
, an arbitrary Kähler form
\omega
on
X
,
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
and continuous
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\real
, the following identity holds

\esssup_{A}(\psi-\phi)=\sup_{d(A)}(\psi-\phi).

(4.2)

Proof.

By the Lebesgue’s density theorem, we clearly have

\esssup_{A}(\psi-\phi)\leq\sup_{d(A)}(\psi-\phi).

(4.3)

To establish the opposite inequality, it is enough to establish that if
\psi\leq\phi
almost everywhere on
A
, then
\psi\leq\phi
over
d(A)
.

Our argument will be local, and we can assume that
\psi
and
\phi
are both defined on a unit ball
\mathbb{D}^{n}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\psi
is plurisubharmonic and
\phi
is continuous.
Mean-value inequality then implies that for any
x\in\mathbb{D}^{n}
,
0<r<1-|x|
, we have

\psi(x)\leq\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)}\psi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}.

(4.4)

We decompose

\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)}\psi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}=\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)\cap A}\psi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}+\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)\setminus A}\psi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}.

(4.5)

Since
\psi
is bounded from above over compacts (it is upper semi-continuous), for any
x\in d(A)
, the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.5
) tends to zero, as
r\to 0
.
By our assumption,

\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)\cap A}\psi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}\leq\frac{\int_{B_{x}(r)\cap A}\phi(y)dy}{\int_{B_{x}(r)}dy}.

(4.6)

Then for
x\in d(A)
, by the continuity of
\phi
, we see that the term on the right-hand side of (
4.6
) tends to
\phi(x)
, as
r\to 0
.
A combination of the above estimates yields that
\psi\leq\phi
over
d(A)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Let us deduce the first crucial consequence of Proposition
4.1
, establishing a connection between psh and Lebesgue envelopes.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any measurable subset
A\subset X
and continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
, we have
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A}=h^{L}_{d(A)}
, and the metric
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A}
has a bounded psh potential.

Proof.

At the level of potentials, the first statement reduces to an identity

\Big\{\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\psi\leq\phi\text{ over }d(A)\Big\}\\
=\Big\{\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\psi\leq\phi\text{ almost everywhere on }A\Big\},

(4.7)

where
\phi
is the potential of
h^{L}
, calculated as in (
1.7
) for a fixed positive metric
h^{L}_{0}
, verifying
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\pi c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{0})
.
But (
4.7
) is a direct consequence of Proposition
4.1
.
The proof of the second statement is an easy adaptation of Proposition
3.1
; the only additional ingredient one has to use is the Lebesgue’s density theorem, i.e. that
d(A)
has the same Lebesgue measure as
A
.
We leave the details to the interested reader.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

The first part follows from Proposition
4.2
and the trivial fact that
h^{L}_{X}=h^{L}
for any
h^{L}
with a psh potential.
The second part follows by Remark
3.2
.
∎

We finish this section with an example of a domain satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
U\subset X
is an open subset with a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
-boundary.
Then
(\overline{U},h^{L})
is Lebesgue pluriregular.

Proof.

Let us show that it is sufficient to establish that
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}=h^{L}_{U}
.
Indeed, by Proposition
4.2
and the obvious fact that
\overline{U}
is Lebesgue dense at every point of
U
, we immediately get
h^{L}_{U}\leq h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},\overline{U}}\leq h^{L}_{\overline{U}}
.
Hence the identity
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}=h^{L}_{U}
implies that
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}=h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},\overline{U}}
, which according to Proposition
3.6
is equivalent to the Lebesgue pluriregularity of
(\overline{U},h^{L})
.

Let us hence establish that
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}=h^{L}_{U}
.
By the trivial bound
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}\geq h^{L}_{U}
, the fact that
h^{L}_{U}
has a psh potential, cf.
[
40
, Proposition 9.19]
, and the definition of
h^{L}_{\overline{U}}
, it only suffices to show that
h^{L}_{U}\geq h^{L}
over
\overline{U}
.
The proof of this fact is an easy modification of
[
42
, Corollary 5.3.13]
, and so we will be brief.
By the analytic accessibility criterion, cf.
[
42
, Proposition 5.3.12]
, the set
U
is non-thin at every point

in the sense of
[
42
, (4.8.1)]
and
[
42
, Corollary 4.8.3]
, i.e. there is an open neighborhood
V
of

, such that for an arbitrary psh function
\psi
, defined in

, we have

\limsup_{{\begin{subarray}{c}z\to a\\
z\in U\cap V\setminus\{a\}\end{subarray}}}\psi(z)=\psi(a),

(4.8)

opposed to the inequality where
=
is replaced by
\leq
, which would be a trivial consequence of the upper semi-continuity of
\psi
.
By applying this for the local potential of
h^{L}_{U}
, using the fact that
h^{L}
is continuous and that over

, we have
h^{L}_{U}\geq h^{L}
, we deduce that
h^{L}_{U}(a)\geq h^{L}(a)
at every

, which finishes the proof.
∎

Alternatively, Proposition

follows from Proposition
3.6
and

, Propositions 2.17 and 2.21]
, establishing that the Lebesgue measure on

is determining and that

is locally pluriregular.

5

Bernstein-Markov type properties for the Lebesgue measures

The primary objective of this section is to examine different versions of the Bernstein-Markov property for the Lebesgue measures on compact subsets.

Recall that a positive measure
\mu
supported on a compact subset
K\subset X
is said to be
Bernstein-Markov
with respect to
(K,h^{L})
, where
h^{L}
is a continuous metric on
L
, if for each
\epsilon>0
, there is
C>0
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(K,h^{L})\leq C\cdot\exp(\epsilon k)\cdot{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu).

(5.1)

It is easy to see that the result of Tian
[
60
]
on the asymptotics of Bergman kernel implies that the Bernstein-Markov property is satisfied for the Lebesgue measure on
X
, when
h^{L}
is a smooth positive metric, cf.
[
5
, §2.1]
.
Demailly regularization theorem,
[
27
]
,
[
39
]
, immediately implies that it holds more generally for an arbitrary continuous metric with psh potential, cf.
[
5
, §2.1]
.

We fix a reference metric
h^{L}_{*}
, let
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\pi c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{*})
, and denote by
\phi
the potential of
h^{L}
.
Following
[
55
]
and
[
6
]
, we say that
\mu
is
Bernstein-Markov with respect to psh weights
if for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
,
p\geq 1
, we have

\sup_{K}\big(\exp(p(\psi-\phi))\big)\leq C\cdot\exp(\epsilon p)\cdot\int_{X}\exp(p(\psi-\phi))d\mu.

(5.2)

It is immediate that
\mu
is Bernstein-Markov for
(K,h^{L})
if it is Bernstein-Markov with respect to psh weights, since by plugging in
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{k}\log|s|_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}_{*}}
and
p=2k
in (
7.2
), we would get (
7.1
).

Let us recall the following result from
[
6
, Theorem 1.14]
(see also
[
55
, Theorems 5.1 and 5.2]
).

Theorem 5.1
.

For a compact subset
K\subset X
, a continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
and a non-pluripolar Borel probability measure
\mu
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

The measure
\mu
is determining for
(K,h^{L})
and
(K,h^{L})
is pluriregular.

2.

The measure
\mu
is Bernstein-Markov with respect to psh weights for
(K,h^{L})
.

We will now explain one consequence of the above result, which will be particularly relevant in what follows.
We say that two graded norms
N=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}
,
N^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}^{\prime}
over
R(X,L)
are
equivalent
(
N\sim N^{\prime}
) if the multiplicative gap between the graded pieces,
N_{k}
and
N_{k}^{\prime}
, is subexponential. This means that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot N_{k}\leq N_{k}^{\prime}\leq\exp(\epsilon k)\cdot N_{k}.

(5.3)

Remark that the Bernstein-Markov condition can then be restated as the equivalence between
{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(K,h^{L})
and
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)
.

We now fix
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
,
f\neq 0
, and use the same notation
K
,
Z
,
NZ
for the associated measurable subsets as in Introduction.
Let
\chi
be an arbitrary smooth volume form on
X
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume that for a continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
, the pair
(K,h^{L})
is pluriregular and the Lebesgue measure on
NZ
is determining for
(K,h^{L})
.
Then the norms
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)
and
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{K},\chi)
are equivalent.

Proof.

By the Radon-Nikodym theorem, cf.
[
57
, Theorem 6.4.3]
, the measures
fd\lambda_{X}
and
d\lambda_{NZ}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other.
From this, Remark
3.4
and Theorem
5.1
, we establish that
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)
is equivalent to
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(K,h^{L})
.

By
[
5
, Proposition 1.8]
and
[
14
, Corollary 7.27]
, for any non-pluripolar
E\subset X
, we have

{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(E,h^{L})={\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(h^{L}_{E})={\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(Q(h^{L}_{E})).

(5.4)

Remark that since
h^{L}_{K}
is continuous and has a psh potential, the Bernstein-Markov property on
(X,h^{L}_{K})
is satisfied for the Lebesgue measure on
X
.
From this and (
5.4
), we deduce that
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(K,h^{L})
is equivalent to
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{K},\chi)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Since the above result doesn’t hold with no pluriregularity assumption and the determining assumption on the Lebesgue measure, see Proposition
5.5
below, we need a weaker equivalence relation on the set of graded norms.
We first define the
logarithmic relative spectrum
of a norm
N_{1}
on a finitely dimensional vector space
V
,
\dim V=\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}r
, with respect to another norm
N_{2}
on
V
, as a non-increasing sequence
\lambda_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\lambda_{j}(N_{1},N_{2})\in\real
,
j=1,\cdots,r
, defined so that

\lambda_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subset V\\
\dim W=j\end{subarray}}\inf_{w\in W\setminus\{0\}}\log\frac{\|w\|_{2}}{\|w\|_{1}}.

(5.5)

For
p\in[1,+\infty[
, we then let

d_{p}(N_{1},N_{2})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sqrt[p]{\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\lambda_{i}|^{p}}{r}},\qquad d_{+\infty}(N,N^{\prime})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\big\{|\lambda_{1}|,|\lambda_{r}|\big\}.

(5.6)

The graded norms
N=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}
and
N^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}^{\prime}
on
R(X,L)
are
p
-
equivalent
(
N\sim_{p}N^{\prime}
) if

\frac{1}{k}d_{p}(N_{k},N_{k}^{\prime})\to 0,\qquad\text{as }k\to\infty.

(5.7)

In
[
34
, §2.3]
, we established that
\sim_{p}
,
p\in[1,+\infty]
, is an equivalence relation and
\sim
equals
\sim_{+\infty}
.

The following result, which we establish in Section
7
, gives a weaker version of Corollary
5.2
without any assumption on the weight.

Theorem 5.3
.

For an arbitrary
f\in L^{\infty}(X)
,
f\neq 0
, and a continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
, the norms
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)
and
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},\chi)
are
p
-equivalent for any
p\in[1,+\infty[
.
If, moreover,
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}
is continuous, then the above equivalence even holds for
p=+\infty
.
Finally, there is a positive smooth metric
h^{L}_{0}
on
L
, so that
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)\geq{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{0},\chi)
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Let us now explain the relation between Theorem
5.3
and the so-called weak Bernstein-Markov condition from
[
6
, p. 8]
.
For this, on a finitely-dimensional Hermitian vector space
(V,H)
, endowed with a norm
N_{0}
on
V
, we denote by
{\rm{vol}}(N)
the volume of the unit ball of
N
(calculated with respect to the volume element associated with
H
).
Note that while
{\rm{vol}}(N)
clearly depends on the choice of
H
, if we fix another norm
N_{1}
on
V
, the change of variables formula implies that the difference
\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{0})-\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{1})
is independent of the choice of
H
.

A positive non-pluripolar measure
\mu
supported on a compact subset
K\subset X
is said to be
weakly Bernstein-Markov
with respect to
(K,h^{L})
, cf.
[
5
, §3.2]
, if

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big|\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu))-\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(K,h^{L}))\big|}{k\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}=0.

(5.8)

It is straightforward that the Bernstein-Markov property implies the weak Bernstein-Markov property, as their names suggest.
To clarify the connection between the weak Bernstein-Markov property and Theorem
5.3
, we first establish the following result.

Proposition 5.4
.

For an arbitrary sequence of norms
N=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}
and
N^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}^{\prime}
on
R(X,L)
, we have
a)\Rightarrow b)
in the following statements

1.

We have
N\sim_{1}N^{\prime}
.

2.

We have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\big|\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k})-\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k}^{\prime})\big|}{k\cdot\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}=0.

(5.9)

If we have
N_{k}\geq N_{k}^{\prime}
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we also have
b)\Rightarrow a)
.
If there is
c>0
, so that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have
\exp(-ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}\leq N_{k}\leq\exp(ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}
, then the condition
a)
above is equivalent to

3.

We have
N\sim_{p}N^{\prime}
for any
p\in[1,+\infty[
.

Proof.

Before all, let us explain that it suffices to consider Hermitian norms in the above statements.
To see this, remark that John ellipsoid theorem, cf.
[
51
, §3]
, says that for any normed vector space
(V,N_{V})
, there is a Hermitian norm
H_{V}
on
V
, verifying

H_{V}\leq N_{V}\leq\sqrt{\dim V}\cdot H_{V}.

(5.10)

While for Hermitian norms,
d_{p}
is a distance (and in particular satisfies the triangle inequality), for arbitrary norms
N_{0},N_{1},N_{2}
on
V
, a weak version of this property holds, as discussed in
[
37
, (3.9)]
:

d_{p}(N_{0},N_{2})\leq d_{p}(N_{0},N_{1})+d_{p}(N_{1},N_{2})+\log\dim V.

(5.11)

If we now denote by
H=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k}
and
H^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{\prime}
the Hermitian norms on
R(X,L)
associated by the John ellipsoid theorem with
N=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}
and
N^{\prime}=\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}N_{k}^{\prime}
respectively, then from (
5.11
), we immediately deduce

\Big|d_{p}(N_{k},N_{k}^{\prime})-d_{p}(H_{k},H_{k}^{\prime})\Big|\leq 3\log n_{k},

(5.12)

where
n_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})
.
Similarly, we have

\big|\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k})-\log{\rm{vol}}(H_{k})\big|\leq 2\log n_{k},\qquad\big|\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k}^{\prime})-\log{\rm{vol}}(H_{k}^{\prime})\big|\leq 2\log n_{k}.

(5.13)

As
\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}\log n_{k}=0
, we see that in both conditions
a)
and
b)
, we can safely change
N_{k}
to
H_{k}
and
N_{k}^{\prime}
to
H_{k}^{\prime}
.
Moreover, it is easy to see that if there is
c>0
, so that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have
\exp(-ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}\leq N_{k}\leq\exp(ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}
, then there is
c>0
, so that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we have
\exp(-ck)\cdot H_{k}^{\prime}\leq H_{k}\leq\exp(ck)\cdot H_{k}^{\prime}
; and if
N_{k}\geq N_{k}^{\prime}
, then we could easily arrange
H_{k}\geq H_{k}^{\prime}
by multiplying
H_{k}
by
n_{k}
, which again would not affect neither of the conditions
a)
and
b)
.
Hence, we may assume without loss of generality that
N_{k}
and
N_{k}^{\prime}
are Hermitian.

It is then immediate that for the logarithmic relative spectrum,
\lambda_{i}^{k}
,
i=1,\ldots,n_{k}
, of
N_{k}
with respect to
N_{k}^{\prime}
, we have

\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k}^{\prime})-\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{k}}\lambda_{i}^{k}.

(5.14)

By this and (
5.6
), we see immediately that
a)\Rightarrow b)
.
We also see that as if
N_{k}\geq N_{k}^{\prime}
, then
\lambda_{i}
are all positive, and we have

\frac{\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k}^{\prime})-\log{\rm{vol}}(N_{k})}{n_{k}}=d_{1}(N_{k},N_{k}^{\prime}),

(5.15)

which clearly shows that
b)\Rightarrow a)
.

The condition
\exp(-ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}\leq N_{k}\leq\exp(ck)\cdot N_{k}^{\prime}
assures that
|\lambda_{i}^{k}|\leq ck
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
i=1,\ldots,n_{k}
.
It is then immediate from (
5.6
) that
a)\Rightarrow c)
.
The inverse implication is trivial.
∎

Compare the following result with Proposition
3.5
.

Proposition 5.5
.

For a compact subset
K\subset X
and a continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
, the following statements are equivalent:

1.

We have
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},K}=h^{L}_{K*}
.

2.

The Lebesgue measure on
K
is weakly Bernstein-Markov with respect to
(K,h^{L})

6

From Toeplitz to Transfer operators and back

The main goal of this section is to establish that the logarithm of a Toeplitz operator is asymptotically Toeplitz, i.e. to prove Theorem
2.2
.
The major idea behind the proof is to first interpret the logarithm of a Toeplitz operator as a transfer map between
L^{2}
-norms associated with different weights, and then to proceed by the comparison of the
L^{2}
-norms associated with weights to
L^{2}
-norms associated with smooth volume forms, the transfer operators between which has been studied recently in
[
36
]
.

Let us recall the definition of the transfer map first.
Let
V
be a complex vector space and
H_{0},H_{1}
be two Hermitian norms on
V
. The
transfer map
,
T\in{\rm{End}}(V)
, between
H_{0},H_{1}
, is the Hermitian operator (with respect to both
H_{0},H_{1}
), defined so that the Hermitian products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{0}},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{1}}
induced by
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
, are related as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{1}}=\langle\exp(-T)\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{0}}
.

A crucial point is that the asymptotic class of the transfer map doesn’t depend on the equivalence class of the norm.
More specifically, consider a sequence of Hermitian norms
H_{k}^{i}
,
i=0,1,2
on
H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
We denote by
T_{k}^{j}\in{\rm{End}}(H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k}))
,
j=1,2
, the transfer maps between
H_{k}^{0}
and
H_{k}^{j}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
The following proposition will be crucial in our approach.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume that for some
p\in[1,+\infty]
, we have
\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{1}\sim_{p}\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}H_{k}^{2}
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have
\|T_{k}^{1}-T_{k}^{2}\|_{p}\leq\epsilon k
.

Proof.

Let us first establish the statement for
p\in[1,+\infty[
.
Recall that the exponential metric increasing property, as stated in
[
10
, Theorem 6.1.4 and (6.36)]
, asserts that for any
p\in[1,+\infty[
,

\big\|T_{k}^{1}-T_{k}^{2}\big\|_{p}\leq d_{p}(H_{k}^{1},H_{k}^{2}),

(6.1)

which immediately implies Proposition
6.1
for
p\in[1,+\infty[
.
To establish Proposition
6.1
for
p=+\infty
, it then suffices to take the limit
p\to+\infty
in (
6.1
).
∎

To make use of the above statement, we need to be able to calculate the transfer maps between various
L^{2}
-norms.
We will fix a positive smooth metric
h^{L}_{0}
on
L
, and a metric
h^{L}_{1}
on
L
with bounded psh potential.
Fix also two smooth volume forms
\chi_{i}
,
i=0,1
, on
X
.
We denote by
T_{k}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})\in{\rm{End}}(H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k}))
the transfer map between
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{0},\chi_{0})
and
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{1},\chi_{1})
.
Let
h^{L}_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, be the Mabuchi geodesic between
h^{L}_{0}
and
h^{L}_{1}
.
We define
\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
as in (
2.2
).

Theorem 6.2
.

The sequence
\frac{1}{k}T_{k}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, forms an asymptotically Toeplitz operator of Schatten class with symbol
\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
.
If, moreover,
h^{L}_{1}
is continuous, then
\frac{1}{k}T_{k}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, even forms an asymptotically Toeplitz operator with symbol
\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})
.

Remark 6.3
.

The second part of Theorem
6.2
was established by the author in
[
36
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proof.

By Remark
6.3
, it suffices to establish the first part of Theorem
6.2
, on which we concentrate from now on.
By the Demailly regularization theorem, there is a sequence of positive metrics
h^{L,i}_{1}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, on
L
, increasing towards
h^{L}_{1}
, see
[
27
]
,
[
39
]
.

By
[
20
, Proposition 4.6]
, for the Darvas distance,
d_{p}
,
p\in[1,+\infty[
, we have

\lim_{i\to 0}d_{p}(h^{L}_{1},h^{L,i}_{1})=0.

(6.2)

From (
6.1
), (
5.17
) and (
6.2
), we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
i\geq i_{0}
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\Big\|T_{k}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L,i}_{1})-T_{k}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})\Big\|_{p}\leq\epsilon k.

(6.3)

Now, from Darvas-Lu
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
and (
6.2
), we deduce that

\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L,i}_{1})\quad\text{converges almost everywhere to}\quad\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L,i}_{1}),\text{ as $i\to\infty$}.

(6.4)

From this and
[
36
, the proof of Proposition 3.5]
, we conclude that for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
i\geq i_{0}
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\Big\|T_{k}(\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L,i}_{1}))-T_{k}(\phi(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1}))\Big\|_{p}\leq\epsilon.

(6.5)

Theorem
6.2
for
p\in[1,+\infty[
, now follows directly from the validity of Theorem
6.2
for
h^{L}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{0}
and
h^{L}_{1}=h^{L,i}_{1}
, (
6.3
) and (
6.5
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

The key observation for our proof is that

can be interpreted as a transfer map between
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\chi)
and
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)
.
This immediately follows from the identity

, where

.
By this and Theorem
5.3
, we deduce

T_{k}(f)\geq\exp(-T_{k}(h^{L},h^{L}_{0})),

(6.6)

where

is as in Theorem
5.3
.
By using the fact that

is order preserving, i.e. if

, then

, cf.
[
44
]
or

]

.

, such that for any

).

and Proposition

, we deduce that for any

,

, such that for any

\Big\|\log(T_{k}(f))+T_{k}(h^{L},h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ})\Big\|_{p}\leq\epsilon k.

(6.7)

By Theorem

), we then deduce that

, forms an asymptotically Toeplitz operator of Schatten class with symbol

.

, Theorem

and Propositions

,

∎

7

Asymptotic class of the weighted
L^{2}
-norms

The main goal of this section is to determine the asymptotic class of the
L^{2}
-norm associated with a weight, i.e. to establish Theorem
5.3
.
The proof is based on the following more general result.

Theorem 7.1
.

We fix a non-pluripolar Borel probability measure
\mu
on
X
, and a continuous metric
h^{L}_{0}
on
L
, verifying
h^{L}_{\mu}\geq h^{L}_{0}
, where
h^{L}_{\mu}
is the non-negligible psh envelope defined in (
3.1
).
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)\geq C\cdot\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(X,h^{L}_{0}).

(7.1)

Proof.

Our proof builds on the proof of Theorem
5.1
from
[
6
]
, but diverges from it in one essential aspect: to overcome the use of determining assumption on the measure from Theorem
5.1
, we use non-negligible envelopes instead of the psh envelopes.

To explain this in details, we work on the level of potentials instead of metrics.
We fix a reference metric
h^{L}_{*}
, denote
\omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\pi c_{1}(L,h^{L}_{*})
, and denote by
\phi
,
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{\mu}
the potentials of
h^{L}
,
h^{L}_{0}
and
h^{L}_{\mu}
respectively, see (
1.7
).
We shall establish the following result: for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
C>0
, such that for any
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
,
p\geq 1
, we have

\sup_{X}\big(\exp(p(\psi-\phi_{0}))\big)\leq C\cdot\exp(\epsilon p)\cdot\int_{X}\exp(p(\psi-\phi))d\mu,

(7.2)

which we suggest to compare (
5.2
).
Once we establish (
7.2
), by plugging in
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{k}\log|s|_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}_{*}}
and
p=2k
in (
7.2
), we would get (
7.1
).
For
p\in[1,+\infty[
, we introduce the functionals

F_{p}(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{1}{p}\log\int_{X}\exp(p(\psi-\phi))d\mu,\qquad F(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{X}(\psi-\phi_{\mu}),

(7.3)

defined for
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.

Now, by the usual facts from integration theory, as
p\to+\infty
,
F_{p}
converges pointwise on
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
to
\log\|\exp(\psi-\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}
.
Clearly, we have
\log\|\exp(\psi-\phi)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}=\esssupp_{\mu}(\psi-\phi)
.
By (
3.5
), we then deduce that
\esssupp_{\mu}(\psi-\phi)=F(\psi)
.
In conclusion, we obtain that as
p\to+\infty
,
F_{p}
converges pointwise on
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
to
F
.

By
[
6
, Lemma 1.14]
, the functionals
F_{p}
are continuous on
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.
Moreover,
F_{p}
are increasing in
p\in[1,+\infty[
by
[
6
, proof of Theorem 1.13]
.
Also,
F-F_{p}
is clearly invariant by translation (adding a constant to the parameter), thus it descends on a function on the quotient space, which is isomorphic with the space of positive
(1,1)
-currents lying in the cohomology class of
c_{1}(L)
, which we denote by
\mathcal{T}(X,\omega)
.
The latter space is compact (in the weak topology of currents), cf.
[
40
, Proposition 8.5]
.

Unfortunately, the functional
F
is not necessarily upper semi-continuous (as Hartogs lemma, cf.
[
40
, Theorem 1.46]
, doesn’t apply for the non-necessarily continuous potential
\phi_{\mu}
).
The assumption of pluriregularity of
(K,h^{L})
in the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.13]
enters precisely at this crucial moment.
To bypass additional assumptions, we consider the functional
G(\psi)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup_{X}(\psi-\phi_{0})
,
\psi\in{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
, for which we clearly have
F\geq G
, and which is upper semi-continuous by the Hartogs lemma (we use here crucially our assumption that
\phi_{0}
is continuous).
Then the functionals
G_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max(G-F_{p},0)
on
\mathcal{T}(X,\omega)
are upper semi-continuous, they are decreasing in
p\in[1,+\infty[
, and converging, as
p\to+\infty
, to zero.
By the already mentioned compactness of
\mathcal{T}(X,\omega)
and Dini’s theorem, we conclude that the convergence of
G_{p}
to
0
, as
p\to+\infty
, is uniform on
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
.
The reader will check that the last fact is just a reformulation of (
7.2
), with only one change: the inequality holds for
p\geq p_{0}
, where
p_{0}
depends on
\epsilon
, and for
C=1
.

Now, by the compactness of
\mathcal{T}(X,\omega)
and the fact that
G_{p}
are upper semi-continuous and decreasing in
p
, there is
C>0
, so that
C\geq G_{1}\geq G_{p}
for any
p\geq 1
.
This fact corresponds to (
7.2
), with only one change: the inequality holds for
p\geq 1
, but
\epsilon
is replaced by
C
.
A combination of this and a previously established estimate yields (
7.2
) in full generality.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.3
.

Remark first that directly from Proposition
4.2
and Lebesgue’s density theorem, for
C\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\esssup_{K}f
, we have

C\cdot{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},\chi)\geq{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi).

(7.4)

The existence of
C>0
, so that the lower bound
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)\geq C\cdot\exp(-\epsilon k)\cdot{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{0},\chi)
, holds for a fixed continuous metric
h^{L}_{0}
on
L
, verifying
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}\geq h^{L}_{0}
, follows immediately from Theorem
7.1
.
The statement concerning the
\sim_{+\infty}
-equivalence follows also immediately from Theorem
7.1
, as under the continuity of
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}
, one can take
h^{L}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}
.
It is hence only left to establish the statement for the
\sim_{p}
-equivalence,
p\in[1,+\infty[
.

Recall that Lidskii inequality implies that if the Hermitian metrics
H_{0},H_{1},H_{2}
on a finitely dimensional vector space

are ordered as
H_{0}\leq H_{1}\leq H_{2}
, then

, cf.
[

, Theorem 2.7]
.
From this, Theorem

and (

), we deduce that for any
\epsilon>0
, and continuous
h^{L}_{0}
, verifying
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}\geq h^{L}_{0}
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any

, we have

d_{p}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},\chi),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)\Big)\leq\epsilon k+d_{p}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},\chi),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{0},\chi)\Big).

(7.5)

Now, by Demailly regularization theorem, there is a sequence of positive metrics
h^{L}_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, on
L
, increasing towards
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ}
.
If we apply (
5.17
) and (

) for
h^{L}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h^{L}_{i}
, we would get

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}d_{p}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},\chi),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},f\cdot\chi)\Big)\leq\epsilon+d_{p}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},NZ},h^{L}_{i}).

(7.6)

From (

), by taking the limit

), we deduce Theorem

.
∎

Remark that by Proposition
3.1
and Theorem

, for an arbitrary non-pluripolar Borel measure
\mu

on

, so that

For any

, there is

, such that for any

, we have

(7.7)

However, it is not generally true that
\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)\sim_{p}\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{\mu},\chi)
, for some

.

.

Assume that

, then the measure

.
Moreover, if

\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)\sim_{p}\oplus_{k=0}^{+\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{\mu},\chi)

Immediately from Remark

The main goal of this section is to recall the basics of Mabuchi geometry and to establish Propositions

Throughout the section, we denote by

Mabuchi in

48

introduced a certain metric on the space of positive metrics on

To recall this, upon fixing a reference positive metric

on

with the space of Kähler potentials of

On the space of Kähler potentials

of
\omega
, for

\|\xi\|_{p}^{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sqrt[p]{\frac{1}{\int_{X}\omega^{n}}\int_{X}|\xi(x)|^{p}\cdot\omega_{u}^{n}(x)}.

(8.1)

The path length metric structure associated with (
8.1
) on the space of Kähler potentials was introduced by Mabuchi
[
48
]
for

[
20

To describe the geodesics in this space, we identify paths
u_{t}

\hat{u}(x,\tau)=u_{t}(x),\quad\text{where}\quad x\in X\,\text{ and }\,t=-\log|\tau|.

(8.2)

According to
[
53
]

[
29
]

u_{t}
,

u_{0}

(\pi^{*}\omega+\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\hat{u})^{n+1}=0,

with boundary conditions
\hat{u}(x,e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})=u_{0}(x)
,
\hat{u}(x,e^{-1+\sqrt{-1}\theta})=u_{1}(x)
,

.
By the work of X. Chen
[
16
]

[

Berndtsson in
[

4

From

\hat{u}(u_{0},u_{1})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sup\Big\{\hat{u}\in{\rm{PSH}}(X\times\mathbb{D}(e^{-1},1),\pi^{*}\omega)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\hat{u}\text{ is $S^{1}$-invariant}\\

For the corresponding path

.
Since

is convex in

Growth of balls of holomorphic sections and Lebesgue envelopes

The main goal of this section is to apply Theorem
5.3
to study the growth of balls of holomorphic sections associated with

-norms supported on measurable subsets.

For this, we recall that the
Monge-Ampère energy functional

\mathscr{E}
, defined on the space of Kähler potentials
u\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}X\to\real
, associated with a Kähler form
\omega
on
X
, is the unique functional (up to a constant) that for any Kähler potentials
u,v
satisfies the following equation:

\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{E}((1-t)u+tv)|_{t=0}=\frac{\int_{X}(v-u)\cdot\omega_{u}^{n}}{\int_{X}\omega^{n}}.

(9.1)

While
\mathscr{E}
is only well-defined up to a constant, the differences
\mathscr{E}(u)-\mathscr{E}(v)
are well-defined and can be explicitly evaluated as follows

\mathscr{E}(u)-\mathscr{E}(v)=\frac{1}{(n+1)\int_{X}\omega^{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\int_{X}(u-v)w_{u}^{j}\wedge w_{v}^{n-j}.

(9.2)

By
[
40
, Proposition 10.14]
,
\mathscr{E}
is monotonic, i.e. for any
u\leq v
, we have
\mathscr{E}(u)\leq\mathscr{E}(v)
.
From this and Demailly regulartization theorem
[
27
]
, it is reasonable to extend the domain of the definition of
\mathscr{E}
to
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
as

\mathscr{E}(u)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\inf\Big\{\mathscr{E}(v)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}v\text{ is a $\omega$-Kähler potential, verifying }u\leq v\Big\}.

(9.3)

Remark, that
\mathscr{E}
can take the value
-\infty
on non-smooth elements of
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)
, but by monotonicity, it takes finite values on
{\rm{PSH}}(X,\omega)\cap L^{\infty}(X)
, see also
[
20
]
for a more precise result.
Below, we extend the definition of
\mathscr{E}
to metrics on
L
with bounded psh potentials by applying the energy functional to the potential of the metric.

Theorem 9.1
.

For any continuous metric
h^{L}
on
L
with a psh potential, and an arbitrary measurable subset
A\subset X
, which is Lebesgue non-negligible, the following identity holds

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(A,h^{L}))-\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}))}{k\cdot\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}=\mathscr{E}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A})-\mathscr{E}(h^{L}).

(9.4)

Remark 9.2
.

When the subset
A
is a closed subset
K\subset X
, such that
(K,h^{L})
is pluriregular and the Lebesgue measure on
K
is determining for
(K,h^{L})
, the result was previously established by Berman-Boucksom
[
5
]
, with
h^{L}_{K}
in place of
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A}
on the right-hand side of (
9.4
).
Our results are compatible by Proposition
3.6
.

Proof.

Remark that we trivially have
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(A,h^{L})\leq{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})
.
From this and (
5.15
), we deduce

\frac{\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(A,h^{L}))-\log{\rm{vol}}({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}))}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}=d_{1}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(A,h^{L}),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\Big).

(9.5)

By Theorem
7.1
and the fact that

satisfies the triangle inequality, cf.
[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}d_{1}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(A,h^{L}),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\Big)=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}d_{1}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A},\chi),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\Big),

(9.6)

where
\chi
is an arbitrary smooth volume form.
Also, by (
5.17
), we have

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{k}d_{1}\Big({\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A},\chi),{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L})\Big)=d_{1}(h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A},h^{L}).

(9.7)

Darvas proved in
[
20
, Corollary 4.14]
that for any bounded metrics
h^{L}_{0}
,
h^{L}_{1}
with psh potentials, verifying
h^{L}_{0}\leq h^{L}_{1}
, we have

d_{1}(h^{L}_{0},h^{L}_{1})=\mathscr{E}(h^{L}_{0})-\mathscr{E}(h^{L}_{1}).

(9.8)

We finish the proof by (
9.5
)-(
9.8
) and the trivial bound
h^{L}_{{\rm{Leb}},A}\leq h^{L}
.
∎

10

Generalized Toeplitz operators and Toeplitz matrices

The main goal of this section is to generalize the main results of this article to the setting of generalized Toeplitz operators associated with an arbitrary non-pluripolar Borel measure.
We then make a connection between this generalized setting and the classical theory of Toeplitz matrices.

We consider a non-pluripolar probability Borel measure
\mu
on
X
with support
K
.
Let
f\in L^{\infty}(K,\mu)
be a fixed function, with
K^{\prime}
denoting its essential support.
For brevity, we let
\mu^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f\cdot\mu
.
Remark that
\mu^{\prime}
is non-pluripolar.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, we denote by
T_{k}(f,\mu)\in{\text{End}{(H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k}))}}
the
Generalized Toeplitz operator with symbol

f
, i.e.
T_{k}(f,\mu)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=B_{k}(\mu)\circ M_{k}(f)
, where
B_{k}(\mu)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}L^{\infty}(K,L^{\otimes k})\to H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})
is the orthogonal projection to
H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})
with respect to
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)
, and
M_{k}(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})\to L^{\infty}(K,L^{\otimes k})
is the restriction on
K
, composed with a multiplication map by
f
.

Theorem 10.1
.

For any
f\in L^{\infty}(K,\mu)
,
f\neq 0
, there is
c>0
such that for any
\lambda_{\min}(T_{k}(f,\mu))\geq\exp(-ck)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
big enough.

We will now assume that
(K,h^{L})
(resp.
(K^{\prime},h^{L})
) is
\mu
-pluriregular (resp.
\mu^{\prime}
-pluriregular) and
\mu(K^{\prime}\cap f^{-1}(0))=0
.
We let
c(f)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max_{x\in X}\log(h^{L}_{\mu}(x)/h^{L}_{\mu^{\prime}}(x))
.
Then for any
\epsilon>0
, there is
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that for any
k\geq k_{0}
, we have

\exp(-(c(f)+\epsilon)k)\leq\lambda_{\min}(T_{k}(f,\mu))\leq\exp(-(c(f)-\epsilon)k).

(10.1)

Moreover, if we denote by
\delta[\cdot]
the Dirac mass, then the sequence of probability measures

\eta_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\dim H^{0}(X,L^{\otimes k})}\sum_{\lambda\in{\rm{Spec}}(T_{k}(f,\mu))}\delta\Big[-\frac{\log(\lambda)}{k}\Big]

(10.2)

converges weakly to the (unique) probability measure
\eta
on , verifying
\int x^{p}d\eta(x)=d_{p}(h^{L}_{\mu},h^{L}_{\mu^{\prime}})
, where
d_{p}
is the Darvas distance from (
8
).

Proof.

The first statement is equivalent to the fact that there is
c>0
, such that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,

{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu^{\prime})\geq\exp(-ck)\cdot{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu),

(10.3)

which follows immediately from Theorem
7.1
, as it shows that we have
{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu^{\prime})\geq\exp(-ck)\cdot{\textrm{Ban}}_{k}^{\infty}(h^{L})
for a certain
c>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.

We note that by Proposition
3.6
and Theorem
5.1
, similarly to the proof of Corollary
5.2
, for an arbitrary volume form
\chi
on
X
, we have

\displaystyle\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu)\sim\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{\mu},\chi),

(10.4)

\displaystyle\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L},\mu^{\prime})\sim\oplus_{k=0}^{\infty}{\textrm{Hilb}}_{k}(h^{L}_{\mu^{\prime}},\chi).

From this point, the proof of all the other statements proceed in an identical manner with the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
.
∎

Remark 10.2
.

Immediately from the proof and Proposition
7.5
, we see that for (
10.2
), instead of pluriregularity of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
, it suffices to require that the measure
\mu
is approximable.

We will now specialize the above theorem in the setting of Toeplitz matrices.
More specifically, let
\mathbb{S}^{1}
be the unit circle and
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
,
f\neq 0
, be a fixed real function, which we can be written as
f(\theta)=a_{0}+\sum_{i=-\infty}^{+\infty}a_{j}\exp(\sqrt{-1}j\theta)
, where
\theta\in[0,2\pi[
, gives a standard parametrization of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Then
a_{i}=\overline{a}_{-i}
and not all
a_{i}
vanish.

Now, we embed
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
X\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathbb{P}^{1}
as one of the great circles (for concreteness given by
\theta\mapsto[1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\exp(i\theta)]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi[
, where
[1\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}z]\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
,

\mu
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, viewed as a measure on
X
.
Remark that as
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is totally real, the measure
\mu
is non-pluripolar, see
[
52
]
, cf.
[
40

.
We then take
L\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathscr{O}(1)

i=0,\ldots,k

writes as the following Toeplitz matrix

T_{k}[a]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&a_{-1}&a_{-2}&\cdots&a_{-k}\\
a_{1}&a_{0}&a_{-1}&\cdots&a_{-k+1}\\
a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}&\cdots&a_{-k+2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{k}&a_{k-1}&a_{k-2}&\cdots&a_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(10.5)

Remark that the matrix is Hermitian by our assumption on
f
.

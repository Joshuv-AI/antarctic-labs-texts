Associative pentagon algebras
Marzia MAZZOTTA, Agata PILITOWSKA

Abstract
A set-theoretic solution to the Pentagon Equation can be described as a pentagon algebra ( S , ⋅ , ∗ ) (S,\cdot,\ast) such that ( S , ⋅ ) (S,\cdot) is a semigroup and the operations ⋅ \cdot and ∗ \ast are related by two additional equations. This paper aims to investigate associative pentagon algebras in which ( S , ∗ ) (S,\ast) is also a semigroup. We introduce and describe two families of associative pentagon algebras which are strongly determined by the properties of the semigroup ( S , ∗ ) (S,\ast) . We present a complete characterization of such algebras using semigroup equations. We also provide constructions of such associative pentagon algebras and give several classes of examples.

In this paper, we study

In any APA

It directly follows from the associativity condition that the set

We establish that some properties of the APA can be reflected in its semigroup

We focus on two classes of APA that we call, respectively,

is

\forall x,y,z\in X\,\,(x\cdot y)\ast z=x\ast z

\forall x,y,z\in X\,\,(x\cdot y)\ast z=y\ast z

The intersection between the two classes is given by the APAs

in which

is determined by an idempotent endomorphism

\displaystyle x\ast y^{k}\ast z=x\ast z.

In particular,

The second one is the variety

The main results of the paper show that an APA

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we give some examples and basic properties of associative pentagon algebras. In Section 2, we study the variety

is a semilattice, then the related APA is both left and right-trivially distributive. We do this since the easy computation will reveal a class of APAs that could be studied.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(S,\cdot,\ast)
be an APA such that
(S,\cdot)
is a semilattice. Then
(S,\ast)
is determined by some
\gamma\in\End(S,\cdot)
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

1.9
-4., we have
\theta_{x}=\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}=\theta_{x\cdot y}=\theta_{y\cdot x}=\theta_{y\cdot x\cdot y}=\theta_{y}
, for all
x\in S
. Thus, the solution belongs to the class of solutions in
3
.
∎

The proof of
7.1
suggests to consider
the class of APAs
(S,\cdot,\ast)
such that
\theta_{x}=\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}
, for all
x,y\in S
. By
Lemma

1.9
-4., obvious examples of such classes are given by APAs
(S,\cdot,\ast)
with
(S,\cdot)
being a band. But even such APAs do not have to be neither left nor right-trivially distributive, as we will see at the end of the section. Therefore the question when APAs satisfying the condition are left or right-trivially distributive naturally arises. At now we have two observations.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
(S,\cdot,\ast)
be an APA such that
\theta_{x}=\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}
, for all
x,y\in S
. Then
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is left-trivially distributive if and only if
\theta_{x}^{2}=\theta_{y}^{2}
, for all
x,y\in S
.

Proof.

First, assume that
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is left-trivially distributive. Then
\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}=\theta_{x}=\theta_{x\cdot y}
, for all
x,y\in S
. Moreover,

\theta_{x}^{2}\underset{\eqref{rel}}{=}\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{x}=\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}\underset{\eqref{rel}}{=}\theta_{y}\theta_{y\cdot x}=\theta_{y}^{2}
.

Vice versa, suppose that
\theta_{x}^{2}=\theta_{y}^{2}
, for all
x,y\in S
. Thus, by (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{x\cdot y}\theta_{y}=\theta_{y}^{2}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot y}=\theta_{x}^{2}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot y}=\theta_{x}\theta_{y}^{2}\theta_{y\cdot y}\underset{\eqref{rel}}{=}\theta_{x}\theta_{y}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\theta_{x\cdot y}\underset{\eqref{rel}}{=}\theta_{y}\theta_{x\cdot y}\theta_{y\cdot x\cdot y}=\theta_{y}\theta_{x\cdot y}\theta_{y}=\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y}.

(6)

The last equality implies that
\theta_{x\cdot y}=\theta_{y}(\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y})\theta_{y}=\theta_{x}^{5}.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\theta_{x}\underset{\eqref{rel}}{=}\theta_{x}^{2}\theta_{x\cdot x}\underset{\eqref{eq_xxyy}}{=}\theta_{x}^{2}\theta_{x}\theta_{x}\theta_{x}=\theta_{x}^{5},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 7.3

Let

be an APA such that

Example 24
.

Let
n\geq 1
,
S=\{0,1,\ldots,n-1,n\}
, and define for
x,y\in S
two binary operations:

\displaystyle x\cdot y=\begin{cases}x&{\rm if}\quad x\neq n\;\,{\rm or}\,\;x=y=n\\
0&{\rm if}\quad x=n\;\,{\rm and}\;\,y\neq n\end{cases}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptx\ast y=\begin{cases}0&{\rm if}\quad x,y\neq 1\,\,{\rm or}\,\,x=y=1\\
1&{\rm if}\quad x=1,\;y\neq 1\;\,{\rm or}\,\;x\neq 1,\;y=1\end{cases}.

Clearly,
(S,\cdot)
is a band and
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is an APA such that
the operation
\ast
is commutative, and
(S\setminus\{n\},\cdot)
is a left-zero semigroup. Moreover, in the semigroup
(S,\cdot)
, the element
0
is a left, but not right, unique annihilator. Hence, by
4.7
,

(S,\cdot,\ast)
is left-trivially distributive. Note also that for all
k\neq 1
,
\theta_{k}=\theta_{0}
and
\theta_{0}\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}=\theta_{1}\theta_{1}
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
(S,\cdot,\ast)
be an APA such that
\theta_{x}=\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}
, for all
x,y\in S
. Then
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is right-trivially distributive if and only if
\theta_{x\cdot x}=\theta_{y\cdot x}
, for all
x,y\in S
.

Proof.

Clearly, if
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is a right-trivially distributive APA, then, for all
x,y\in S
,
\theta_{x\cdot x}=\theta_{x}=\theta_{y\cdot x}
. Conversely,
suppose that
\theta_{x\cdot x}=\theta_{y\cdot x}
, for all
x,y\in S
.
Then, applying (
1
) several times, we get

\displaystyle\theta_{y\cdot x}

\displaystyle=\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{x\cdot y\cdot x}=\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{x}=\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}(\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}){=}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{y}(\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x})\theta_{y\cdot x}

\displaystyle=\theta_{x}(\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{y}\theta_{y\cdot x\cdot y})\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{y\cdot x}{=}\theta_{x}\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\theta_{y\cdot x}\theta_{y\cdot x}=\theta_{x}\theta_{x}\theta_{x\cdot x}=\theta_{x},

and so
(S,\cdot,\ast)
is RTD.
∎

The following example shows that not all APAs with band reduct are left or right trivially distributive.

Example 25
.

Let
(S,\ast)
be a semigroup generated by a set
\{a,b\}
and such that
a^{4}=a^{2}
,
b^{2}=b
,
a^{2}\ast b=b
,
b\ast a^{2}=a^{2}
and
a^{2}\ast x\ast y=b\ast x\ast y=x\ast y

for any
x,y\in S
. Then
S=\{a,a^{2},a^{3},b,a\ast b,b\ast a,a\ast b\ast a\}
. Note that
(S,\ast)
is not right-normal:
a\ast b\ast a\neq b\ast a^{2}=a^{2}
and
\theta_{a}^{2}(a)=\theta_{a}(a^{2})=a^{3}\neq b\ast a=\theta_{b}(b\ast a)=\theta_{b}^{2}(a)
.

Now let define two binary operations on the set
S\times S
in the following way: for
(x_{1},x_{2}),(y_{1},y_{2})\in S\times S
,

(x_{1},x_{2})\cdot(y_{1},y_{2}):=(x_{1},y_{2})
and

\displaystyle(x_{1},x_{2})\,\hat{\ast}\,(y_{1},y_{2}):=\begin{cases}\left(x_{1}\ast x_{2}^{2}\ast a,x_{1}\ast a^{3}\right)&\text{if $y_{1}=y_{2}=a$}\\
\left(x_{1}\ast y_{1},x_{1}\ast y_{1}^{2}\ast a\right)&\text{if $y_{1}\neq a,\;y_{2}=a$}\\
\left(x_{1}\ast x_{2}^{2}\ast a,x_{1}\ast y_{2}\right)&\text{if $y_{1}=a,\;y_{2}\neq a$}\\
\left(x_{1}\ast y_{1},x_{1}\ast y_{2}\right)&\text{if $y_{1}\neq a,\;y_{2}\neq a$}\end{cases}

By direct calculations, we obtain that
(S\times S,\cdot,\hat{\ast})
is an APA with
(S\times S,\cdot)
being a rectangular band.
Further,
\hat{\theta}_{(a,a)\cdot(b,b)}((a,a))=(a\ast b\ast a,a^{2})\neq(a^{2},a^{2})=\hat{\theta}_{(a,a)}\left((a,a)\right)
and
\hat{\theta}_{(a,a)\cdot(b,b)}((a,a))\neq\hat{\theta}_{(b,b)}((a,a))=(b\ast a,b\ast a^{3}).

Then
\left(S\times S,\cdot,\hat{\ast}\right)
is neither LTD nor RTD.

The discussion above suggests a potential problem and question.

Problem 2
.

Characterize APAs
(S,\cdot,\ast)

(S,\cdot)
is a band.

Question

Do they exist other natural classes of APAs?

Molla Basir Ahamed, Partha Pratim Roy, Sujoy Majumder

Abstract
. The Hurwitz–Lerch Zeta function is utilized for some consequences, and all results are shown to be sharp.

Let
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
denote the open unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
. Harald Bohr in
1914
(see
[
17
]
) stated that if
H_{\infty}
denotes the class of all bounded analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
, then the following inequality holds

(1.1)

\displaystyle B_{0}(f,r):=|a_{0}|+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\leq||f||_{\infty}:=\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;0\leq r\leq\frac{1}{3},

where
a_{k}=f^{(k)}(0)/k!
for
k\geq 0
. The constant
1/3
is called the Bohr radius and the inequality in (
1.1
) is called Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}
of analytic self-maps on
\mathbb{D}
. Henceforth, if there exists a positive real number
r_{0}
such that an inequality of the form (
1.1
) holds for every elements of a class
\mathcal{M}
for
0\leq r\leq r_{0}
and fails when
r>r_{0}
, then we shall say that
r_{0}
is an sharp bound for
r
in the inequality w.r.t. to class
\mathcal{M}
.

Bohr initially established that the inequality (
1.1
) is valid for all
|z|\leq 1/6
. Subsequently, M. Riesz, I. Schur, and F. W. Wiener independently extended this result, verifying the inequality for the broader interval
0\leq r\leq 1/3
. It has since been confirmed that the bound
1/3
is sharp, a fact deduced by examining specific examples within the family of conformal automorphisms of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Bohr’s inequality, initially linked to the convergence of Dirichlet series by Harald Bohr, has evolved into a key topic in modern function theory (see
[
13
,
15
,
18
]
). It plays a pivotal role in characterizing Banach algebras satisfying the von Neumann inequality (see
[
24
]
), with several distinct proofs and extensions, including the multidimensional Bohr radius
\mathcal{K}^{\infty}_{n}
introduced by Boas and Khavinson. Recent works (see
[
55
,
57
,
58
,
18
,
39
]
) have extended Bohr’s inequality to more general power series by replacing the classical basis
\{r^{n}\}
with sequences
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}
of non-negative continuous functions, requiring locally uniform convergence of their sum on
[0,1)
. Additionally, studies such as
[
3
,
27
]
investigated Bohr phenomena on shifted disks
\Omega_{\gamma}
(
0\leq\gamma<1
), while
[
8
]
explored Bohr radii for subclasses of starlike and convex functions in
\mathbb{D}
.

The Bohr inequality for holomorphic and pluriharmonic mappings with values in complex Hilbert spaces are obtained in
[
31
]
. However, it can be noted that not every class of functions has Bohr phenomenon, for example, Bénéteau
et al.

[
12
]
showed that there is no Bohr phenomenon in the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D},X),
where
p\in[1,\infty).
In
[
47
]
, Liu and Liu have shown that Bohr’s inequality fails to hold for the class
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D}^{2},\mathbb{D}^{2})
of holomorphic functions
f:\mathbb{D}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{2}
having lacunary series expansion. Over the past few years, Bohr’s inequality has gained a significant attention, resulting in extensive research and extensions in various directions and settings (see
[
10
,

,

,

,

]
and references therein).

In this paper, we have two aims. First, we aim to establish improved Bohr inequalities for the class
\mathcal{B}
incorporating the term
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
. Second, we aim to obtain generalized Bohr inequality for a certain class of harmonic mappings, using a sequence of differentiable functions. The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2, we study the improved Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}
of bounded analytic functions on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. In Section 3, we discuss the generalization of the Bohr inequality for certain classes of harmonic mappings and its applications. The proof of the main results are discussed in each section individually.

2.
Bohr inequalities for the class
\mathcal{B}
of bounded analytic functions on unit disk
\mathbb{D}

Before we proceed with our discussion, let us introduce some notations.

2.1.
Basic Notations

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{f\in H_{\infty}\;:\;||f||_{\infty}\leq 1\}.

Also, for
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
and
f_{0}(z):=f(z)-f(0)
, we let for convenience

\displaystyle B_{k}(f,r):=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n}\;\mbox{for }k\geq 0,\;||f_{0}||^{2}_{r}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\;\mbox{and}\;||f_{1}||^{2}_{r}:=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}

so that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{0}(f,r)=|a_{0}|+B_{1}(f,r),\\
B_{0}(f,r)=|a_{0}|+|a_{1}|r+B_{2}(f,r),\\
B_{0}(f,r)=|a_{0}|+|a_{1}|r+|a_{2}|r^{2}+B_{3}(f,r),\;\mbox{so on}.\end{cases}

Further, we define the quantity
A(f_{0},r)
by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}A(f_{0},r):=\left(\dfrac{1}{1+|a_{0}|}+\dfrac{r}{1-r}\right)||f_{0}||^{2}_{r},\vskip 5.69054pt\\

Now we concentrate on another well-known result by W. Rogosinski

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{n=0}^{N}a_{n}z^{n}\bigg|\leq 1

for

and for all

is the best possible. Further progress on this problem has been made in
[
6
,
5
,
50
]

[

48
]
). In fact, the Bohr–Rogosinski radius is analogous to the Bohr radius, which has been defined (see
[
63
]
) as follows: If

is given by

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n},

then for an integer

|S_{N}(z)|<1
in the disk

, and the radius
1/2
is best possible, where

denotes the
N

f
. The radius

. For

\displaystyle R_{f}^{N}(z)=|f(z)|+\sum_{n=N}^{\infty}|a_{n}|r^{n},\;\;\;|z|=r

B

Kayumov and his co-authors (see

where

2.2.
Improved Bohr inequality for the class
\mathcal{B}

Let
f
be holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, and for
0<r<1
, let
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
.
Throughout the paper,
S_{r}:=S_{r}(f)
denotes the planar integral

\displaystyle S_{r}:=\int_{\mathbb{D}_{r}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z).

The quantity
S_{r}
plays a significant role in the study of improved Bohr inequalities for the class
\mathcal{B}
. Kayumov and Ponnusamy
[
38
]
proved the following sharp inequality for functions in the class

\mathcal{B}
, for the function
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{r}}{\pi}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}\leq r^{2}\frac{(1-|a_{0}|^{2})^{2}}{(1-|a_{0}|^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\;\mbox{for}\;0<r\leq 1/\sqrt{2}.

The investigation into improving the Bohr inequalities and their different sharp versions is a thriving area of research in the Bohr phenomenon. In fact, mathematicians have formulated several improved versions of the Bohr inequalities by incorporating the quantities
S_{r}/\pi
and
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
in the Bohr inequalities. This line of inquiry was initiated by Kayumov and Ponnusamy (see
[
37
, Theorem 1]
), who established improved versions of the Bohr inequalities, denoted as
B_{0}(f,r)
and
|a_{0}|^{2}+B_{1}(f,r)
, by incorporating
S_{r}/\pi
and
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
, respectively. Subsequently, Ismagilov
et al.
(see
[
34
, Theorem 3]
) further improved the primary inequality of Kayumov and Ponnusamy (refer to
[
37
, Theorem 1]
) by substituting the initial coefficient
|a_{0}|
with
|f(z)|
. Following this, Liu
et al.
(see
[
48
, Theorem 4]
) not only refined both inequalities introduced by Kayumov and Ponnusamy (in
[
37
, Theorem 1]
) but also included an augmenting term
A(f_{0},r)
into the inequalities, thereby ushering in a significant advancement in this domain of study.

Ismagilov
et al.
(see
[
33
]
) further investigated the classical Bohr inequality and obtained the following sharp result, replacing the quantity
S_{r}/\pi
with
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
.

Theorem E
.

[
33
, Theorems 10, 11]

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
. Then

\displaystyle B_{0}(f,r)+\frac{16}{9}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{3},

and the number
16/9
cannot be improved. Moreover,

\displaystyle|a_{0}|^{2}+B_{1}(f,r)+\frac{9}{8}\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1\quad\mbox{for}\quad r\leq\frac{1}{2},

and the number
9/8
cannot be improved.

Very recent, an improvement involves substituting the coefficient
|a_{0}|
with
|f(z)|^{2}
and using the quantity of
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
instead of
S_{r}/\pi
, the following result is obtained.

Theorem F
.

[
4
, Theorem 2.5]

Suppose that
f(z)\in\mathcal{B}
and
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
. Then

(2.7)

\displaystyle|f(z)|^{2}+B_{1}(f,r)+A(f_{0},r)+\lambda\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1,

for
|z|=r\leq 1/3,
and the constant
\lambda=8/9
cannot be improved.

Therefore, drawing motivation from the recent results (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
,
[
33
, Theorem 10, 11]
,
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
) and using the inequality
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
, we note that the parameter
\lambda
remained unchanged in all previous discussions when
S_{r}/(\pi-S_{r})
was used instead of
S_{r}/\pi
. To further explore the subject, the following question naturally arises:

Question 2.1
.

Is it possible to refine the inequality (
2.4
), originally established by Liu
et al.
(and discussed in
[
48
]
), by incorporating the term
{S_{r}}/{(\pi-S_{r})}
? If so, what can be said about the sharpness of this improvement?

To give a complete answer to Question
2.1
, we establish the following result, which shows that the obtained radius cannot be improved further.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{B}
and
S_{r}
denotes the Riemann surface of the function
f^{-1}
defined on the image of the sub-disk
|z|\leq r
under the mapping
f
. Then

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1,f}(r)

\displaystyle:=|f(z)|+|f^{\prime}(z)|r+B_{2}(f,r)+A(f_{0},r)+\lambda\left(\frac{S_{r}}{\pi-S_{r}}\right)\leq 1

for
r\leq(\sqrt{17}-3)/4
, where the numbers
\lambda=(221-43\sqrt{17})/64
and
(\sqrt{17}-3)/4\;
are sharp.

Remark 2.1
.

Furthermore, the inequality (
2.5
) by Liu
et al.

[
48
]
does not permit improvement via the inclusion of

as the resulting Bohr radii lie outside

.

\displaystyle+(7854-1922\sqrt{17})a^{3}+(47848-11608\sqrt{17})a^{4}+(25496-6184\sqrt{17})a^{5}.

3.
Generalization of the Bohr inequality involving a sequence of differential functions for a certain classes of harmonic mappings

Let
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
be the class of complex-valued functions harmonic in a simply connected domain
\Omega
. It is well-known that functions
f
in the class
\mathcal{H}(\Omega)
has the following representation
f=h+\overline{g}
, where
h
and
g
both are analytic functions in
\Omega
. The famous Lewy’s theorem
[
45
]
in
1936
states that a harmonic mapping
f=h+\overline{g}
is locally univalent on
\Omega
if, and only if, the determinant
|J_{f}(z)|
of its Jacobian matrix
J_{f}(z)
does not vanish on
\Omega
, where

|J_{f}(z)|:=|f_{z}(z)|^{2}-|f_{\bar{z}}(z)|^{2}=|h^{\prime}(z)|^{2}-|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\neq 0.

In view of this result, a locally univalent harmonic mapping is sense-preserving if
|J_{f}(z)|>0
and sense-reversing if
|J_{f}(z)|<0
in
\Omega
. For detailed information about the harmonic mappings, we refer the reader to
[
20
,
26
]
. In
[
42
]
, Kayumov
et al.
first established the harmonic extension of the classical Bohr theorem, since then investigating on the Bohr-type inequalities for certain class of harmonic mappings becomes an interesting topic of research in geometric function theory.

Methods of harmonic mappings have been applied to study and solve the fluid flow problems (see
[
7
,
21
]
). In particular, univalent harmonic mappings having special geometric
properties such as convexity, starlikeness and close-to-convexity arise naturally in planar fluid dynamical problems. For example, in
2012
, Aleman and Constantin
[
7
]
established a connection between harmonic mappings and ideal fluid flows. In fact, Aleman and Constantin have developed ingenious technique to solve the incompressible two dimensional Euler equations in terms of univalent harmonic mappings (see
[
21
]
for details).

Bohr phenomenon can be studied in view of the Euclidean distance and in this paper, we study the same for certain classes of harmonic mappings. The classical Bohr inequality can be written as

(3.1)

d\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|,|a_{0}|\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}z^{n}|\leq 1-|f(0)|=d(f(0),\partial(\mathbb{D})),

where
d
is the Euclidean distance.

In
[
1
]
, Abu-Muhanna have obtained the following result for subordination class
\mathcal{S}(f)
when
f
is univalent.

Theorem G
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 1]
)

If
g=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n}z^{n}\in\mathcal{S}(f)
, and
f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is univalent, then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n}|r^{n}\leq d(f(0),\partial\Omega),

for
|z|=\rho^{*}_{0}\leq 3-\sqrt{8}\approx 0.17157
, where
\rho^{*}_{0}
is sharp for the Koebe function
f(z)=z/(1-z)^{2}
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the class of all complex-valued harmonic functions

defined on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
h
and

are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with the normalization

and

. Let

be defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{f=h+\bar{g}\in\mathcal{H}:g^{\prime}(0)=0\}.

Therefore, each

and

For analytic and geometric properties of harmonic functions, we refer to

]
. In

(see

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}=\{f=h+\overline{g}:{\rm Re}h^{\prime}(z)>|g^{\prime}(z)|\;\mbox{for}\;z\in\mathbb{D}\}

and
\mathcal{P}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{H}}\cap\mathcal{H}_{0}

Let
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}

converges locally uniformly on the interval

. With the help of this idea of the change of basis from

(
[

Let

and

in some interval

The value of the Bohr radius for the identity operator and the convolution operator with the hypergeometric Gaussian function was found by the authors using Theorem H. Let

\displaystyle F(z)=F(a,b,c,z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}z^{n},\;\gamma_{n}:=\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}(1)_{n}},

where

. Recently, Chen

established several new versions of Bohr-type inequalities for bounded analytic functions in the unit disk

We define the Bohr phenomenon, generalized with respect to a sequence of functions, for a certain class of harmonic mappings.

Definition 3.1

\displaystyle d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))=\liminf_{|z|\rightarrow 1}|f(z)-f(0)|.

Since
f(0)=0
, it follows that
|f(z)-f(0)|=|f(z)|
. Therefore, using (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.10)

\displaystyle d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))\geq 1+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}.

Let
H_{M}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

(3.11)

\displaystyle H_{M}(r)=r\varphi_{0}(r)+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}-1-2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}.

Clearly,
H_{M}
is continuous in
[0,1]
and
H_{M}
is differentiable
(0,1)
for the following reason.

(R1):
Since
\varphi_{0}(r)
is differentiable on
(0,1)
, the expression
r\varphi_{0}(r)-1-2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}
is also differentiable on
(0,1)
, as it is equal to
r\varphi_{0}(r)-1-2M(1-\ln 4)
.

(R2):
Since the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)
is locally uniformly convergent on
[0,1)
, it is uniformly convergent on every closed interval
[0,a]\subset[0,1)
. Therefore, there exists
K>0
such that for all
n
and
r\in[0,a]
,
|\varphi_{n}(r)|\leq K
.

We define

\displaystyle S(r)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}(r),\;\;\mbox{where}\;\;S_{n}(r)=\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}.

Then, for
r\in[0,a]
, we have

\displaystyle|S_{n}(r)|=\bigg|\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}\bigg|\leq\frac{K}{n(n-1)}:=K_{n}.

We see that
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}K_{n}=K\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}=K
. The series
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}K_{n}
is clearly convergent. Hence, by the Weierstrass
M
-test, the series
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}(r)
converges uniformly and absolutely on
[0,a]
, and therefore converges locally uniformly on
[0,1)
.

Since the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(r)
is locally uniformly convergent on
[0,1)
, for every closed interval
[0,a]\subset[0,1)
, there exists
K_{1}>0
such that for all
n
and
r\in[0,a]
,
|\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(r)|\leq K_{1}.

Then, for
r\in[0,a]
, we have

\displaystyle|S_{n}^{\prime}(r)|=\bigg|\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}\bigg|\leq\frac{K_{1}}{n(n-1)}:=K^{*}_{n}.

Again, we see that
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}K^{*}_{n}=K_{1}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n(n-1)}=K_{1}
. The series
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}K^{*}_{n}
is clearly convergent. Therefore, by the Weierstrass
M
-test, the series
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S^{\prime}_{n}(r)
converges uniformly and absolutely on
[0,a]
, and consequently, it converges locally uniformly on
[0,1)
.

Since each
S_{n}(r)
is differentiable on
(0,1)
, and both
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}(r)
and
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S^{\prime}_{n}(r)
converge locally uniformly on
[0,1)
, it follows from the theorem on term-by-term differentiation (see
[
62
, Theorem 7.17]
) that the sum,
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}(r)
, is differentiable on
(0,1)
, and its derivative is the sum of the derivatives,
\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S^{\prime}_{n}(r)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\big(S(r)\big)=\frac{d}{dr}\bigg(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}S_{n}(r)\bigg)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dr}\big(S_{n}(r)\big)

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\bigg(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}\bigg)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{n(n-1)}.

Consequently, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\bigg(2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}\bigg)=2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}.

Since each term in (
3.11
) is differentiable, their finite sum
H_{M}(r)
is also differentiable on
(0,1)
. Moreover, in view of the condition in (
3.5
), we see that

\displaystyle H_{M}(0)=2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(0)}{n(n-1)}-1-2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}<0.

Since
\varphi_{n}(1)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
1-\ln 4\approx-0.3862<0
, we have

\displaystyle H_{M}(1)=

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(1)+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(1)}{n(n-1)}-1-2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle=2M\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(1)}{n(n-1)}-(1-\ln 4)\right)>0.

By the Intermediate Value Theorem, the function
H_{M}
has a root in
(0,1)
, which we denote by
R_{f}(M)
. To show that
R_{f}(M)
is unique, it is sufficient to show that
H_{M}
is a monotonic function on
[0,1]
. Since the set of all sequences
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n=0}
of non-negative, differentiable functions on
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)
converges locally uniformly on the interval
[0,1)
, we proceed as follows: an easy computation using (
3.12
) shows that

\displaystyle H_{M}^{\prime}(r)=r\varphi^{\prime}_{0}(r)+\varphi_{0}(r)+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{\prime}_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}>0\;\mbox{for all}\;r\in(0,1).

Thus, we conclude that
H_{M}
is a strictly monotonically increasing function on
(0,1)
. However,
H_{M}\left(R_{f}(M)\right)=0
leads to

(3.13)

\displaystyle R_{f}(M)\varphi_{0}(R_{f}(M))+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(R_{f}(M))}{n(n-1)}=1+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}.

In view of (
3.10

\displaystyle A_{f}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle\leq r\varphi_{0}(r)+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 1+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle\leq d\left(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D})\right)

for
|z|=r\leq R_{f}(M)
. Thus the desired inequality is established.

The next part of the proof requires showing that
R_{f}(M)
is best possible. Therefore, henceforth, we consider the function

given by

\displaystyle f_{M}(z)=z+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n(n-1)}.

Clearly, we see that

. For

and

, an easy computation in view of part (i) of Lemma D and (

) shows that

\displaystyle A_{f_{M}}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle>R_{f}(M)\varphi_{0}(R_{f}(M))+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(R_{f}(M))}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle=1+2M\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n(n-1)}

\displaystyle=d\left(f_{M}(0),\partial f_{M}(\mathbb{D})\right).

This turns out that the number

is best possible.

3.5.
The Hurwitz–Lerch Zeta Function

The Gaussian hypergeometric function, denoted by

{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)
, is defined by the power series

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c;z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{n}(b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}\,n!}\,z^{n},\qquad|z|<1,

where
(q)_{n}
represents the
Pochhammer symbol
(or rising factorial) defined by

\displaystyle(q)_{n}=q(q+1)(q+2)\cdots(q+n-1),\quad n\geq 1,\qquad(q)_{0}=1.

The function admits analytic continuation to the complex plane cut along
[1,\infty)
and satisfies numerous transformation and differentiation formulas. In particular, the function

{}_{2}F_{1}(1,b;b+1;z)
admits the useful representation

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}(1,b;b+1;z)=b\,\Phi(z,1,b),

where
\Phi(z,s,a)
denotes the
Hurwitz–Lerch transcendent
, defined by

\displaystyle\Phi(z,s,a):=\operatorname{HurwitzLerchPhi}(z,s,a)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{(n+a)^{s}},

which converges absolutely for
|z|<1
, and also for
|z|=1
when
\Re(s)>1
.
It admits an analytic continuation to a large domain in
(z,s,a)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
. A special case used in this paper is

\displaystyle H(\alpha):=\operatorname{HurwitzLerchPhi}\!\left(-1,1,1+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}},\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,-1\}.

For real
\alpha
with
\alpha>0
or
\alpha<-1
, the series in
H(\alpha)

converges conditionally, since the terms form a positive decreasing sequence in magnitude and alternate in sign. The function
H(\alpha)
is real-valued on its domain, continuous, and strictly increasing for
\alpha>0
.

An alternative representation of
H(\alpha)
can be given in terms of the
digamma function
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
:

\displaystyle H(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\psi\!\big(1+\tfrac{1}{2\alpha}\big)-\psi\!\big(\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2\alpha}\big)\bigg).

This identity follows from the classical formula

\displaystyle\Phi(-1,1,a)=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\psi\!\big(\tfrac{a+1}{2}\big)-\psi\!\big(\tfrac{a}{2}\big)\bigg),

which can be found in standard references such as
[
30
,
62
]
.

.

Let
L_{\alpha}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}(0)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(0)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}-1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle=-1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}<0.

Since

\displaystyle H(\alpha)=\operatorname{HurwitzLerchPhi}\!\left(-1,1,1+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n+1+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}},

and define

\displaystyle G(\alpha)=H(\alpha)+\log 2-\alpha,\;\mbox{for}\;0<\alpha<1.

Since
1-\alpha>0
, the given inequality is equivalent to

\displaystyle H(\alpha)+\log 2>\alpha,

that is,
G(\alpha)>0
on
(0,1)
.

For
a=1+\tfrac{1}{\alpha}>2
, we have

\displaystyle H^{\prime}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n+a)^{2}}.

Because the sequence
\{(n+a)^{-2}\}
is positive and decreasing, the alternating-series estimate gives

\displaystyle 0<\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n+a)^{2}}<\frac{1}{a^{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle 0<H^{\prime}(\alpha)<\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}a^{2}}=\frac{1}{(1+\alpha)^{2}}<1,

which implies that
G^{\prime}(\alpha)=H^{\prime}(\alpha)-1<0
.
Thus,
G(\alpha)
is strictly decreasing on
(0,1)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to 0^{+}}G(\alpha)=\log 2>0,\;\lim_{\alpha\to 1^{-}}G(\alpha)=H(1^{-})+\log 2-1=(1-\log 2)+\log 2-1=0.

Since
G
is continuous and strictly decreasing from
\log 2
to
0
,
it follows that
G(\alpha)>0
for every
0<\alpha<1
.
Consequently,

\displaystyle-1-\frac{-1+H(\alpha)+\log 2}{1-\alpha}<0\qquad(0<\alpha<1).

Since
\varphi_{n}(1)=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}(1)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{0}(1)+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(1)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}-1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle=2\bigg(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}-\dfrac{-1+H(\alpha)+\log 2}{1-\alpha}\bigg)>0.

where

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}=\dfrac{-1+H(\alpha)+\log 2}{1-\alpha}<0\,\;\mbox{for}\;0\leq\alpha<1.

By the
Intermediate Value Theorem
, the function
H_{\alpha}
has a root in
(0,1)
,
R_{f}(M)
say. To show that
R_{f}(M)
is unique, it is sufficient to show that
H_{M}
is a monotonic function on
[0,1]
. Since the set of all sequences
\{\varphi_{n}(r)\}^{\infty}_{n}
of non-negative differentiable functions in
[0,1)
such that the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{n}(r)
converges locally uniformly on the interval
[0,1)
,
\;\forall\;n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, an easy computation shows that

\displaystyle L_{\alpha}^{\prime}(r)=\varphi_{0}(r)+r\varphi_{0}^{\prime}(r)+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}^{\prime}(r)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}\;\;\mbox{for all}\;\;r\in(0,1).

Thus, we conclude that
L_{\alpha}
is a strictly monotone increasing function on
(0,1)
.
Then,
L_{\alpha}(R_{f}(\alpha))=0
leads to

(3.22)

\displaystyle R_{f}(\alpha)\varphi_{0}(R_{f}(\alpha))+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(R_{f}(\alpha))}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

The Lemma G in view of (
3.17
) yields that

\displaystyle B_{f}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq r\varphi_{0}(r)+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle\leq d(f(0),\partial f(\mathbb{D}))

for
|z|=r\leq R_{f}(\alpha)
. Thus the desired inequality is established.

Next we have to prove that the number
R_{f}(\alpha)
is best possible.
Henceforth, we consider the function
f_{\alpha}
given by

\displaystyle f_{\alpha}(z)=z+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}.

Clearly, we see that
f_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}^{0}_{\mathcal{H}}(\alpha)
. For
r>R_{f}(\alpha)
and
f=f_{\alpha}
, an easy computation in view of part-(i) of Lemmas G and H shows that

\displaystyle B_{f_{\alpha}}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\left(|a_{n}|+|b_{n}|\right)\varphi_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=r\varphi_{0}(r)+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(r)}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle>R_{f}(\alpha)\varphi_{0}(R_{f}(\alpha))+2\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi_{n}(R_{f}(\alpha))}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{2(-1)^{n-1}}{\alpha n^{2}+(1-\alpha)n}

\displaystyle=d(f_{\alpha}(0),\partial f_{\alpha}(\mathbb{D}))

This shows that the number
R_{f}(\alpha)
is best possible.

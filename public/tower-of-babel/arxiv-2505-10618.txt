Jet production at electron-positron colliders at next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order in QCD
Xuan Chen, Petr Jakubčík, Matteo Marcoli, Giovanni Stagnitto

Abstract
We present the first application of antenna subtraction at next-to-next-to-next-to-leading order (N 3 LO) in QCD by computing fully differential predictions for two-jet production at electron-positron colliders. We illustrate the structure of the infrared counterterms and provide results for the N 3 LO correction to the two-jet production rate and to the leading-jet energy. Our work constitutes the first direct calculation of jet production at electron-positron colliders at N 3 LO and represents the first step in tackling arbitrary processes with jets at this perturbative order.

2
Method

In the following, we outline the extension of antenna subtraction for final-state radiation to N
3
LO.
The
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
correction to two-jet production at electron-positron colliders receives contributions from final states with up to five partons.
We denote the contribution to the N
3
LO coefficient from a configuration with
n
final-state partons by
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{n}
.
The
triple-real
contribution
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5}
is given by three real emissions on top of the Born-level process, the
double-real-virtual
contribution
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4}
by two real emissions at one loop, the
real-double-virtual
contribution
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3}
by one real emission at two loops and finally the
triple-virtual
contribution
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2}
by the three-loop correction.
Examples of Feynman diagrams contributing to different types of N
3
LO corrections are shown in Figure
1
.

The individual contributions contain explicit IR singularities due to loop corrections (
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2}
), implicit singularities arising when the external momenta approach soft and collinear limits (
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5}
), or a mixture of both (
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3}
and
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4}
).
These divergences cancel when virtual and real corrections are combined in physical predictions
[
92
,
96
]
, but each individual term is numerically integrable over the associated phase space only after introducing dedicated subtraction terms.
We denote by
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{n,\text{finite}}
the individual contributions after subtraction, free from any type of IR singularity.
The definition of
\text{d}\sigma^{(k)}_{n,\text{finite}}
up to NNLO in the context of antenna subtraction was described in
[
78
,
45
]
.
At N
3
LO, we write schematically

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{5,\text{finite}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\text{d}\sigma_{5}^{(3)}

\displaystyle-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.0pt-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-4.0pt-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{3}}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{4,\text{finite}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\text{d}\sigma_{4}^{(3)}

\displaystyle-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.0pt-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{1}}

\displaystyle\hskip-4.0pt-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{2}}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{3,\text{finite}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\text{d}\sigma_{3}^{(3)}

\displaystyle-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip-2.0pt-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}S_{1}}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{2,\text{finite}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\text{d}\sigma_{2}^{(3)}

\displaystyle-\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2,V_{3}}\,,

(4)

where
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{n,V_{\ell}S_{m}}
represents a counterterm targeting the divergent behavior of
m
unresolved partons as well as the explicit IR poles in a
\ell
-loop matrix element.

The following relations between the subtraction terms ensure the cancellation of IR singularities across different phase-space multiplicities:

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{1}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{1}}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{1}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{1}}\hskip-2.0pt-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{2}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{2}}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\hskip-12.0pt\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2,V_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{1}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}S_{1}}\hskip-2.0pt-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{2}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{2}}\hskip-2.0pt-\hskip-2.0pt\int_{3}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{3}}\,,

(7)

where
\int_{m}
indicates inclusive integration over the phase space of
m
unresolved emissions. As a consequence of the relations (
5
)–(
7
), the net contribution of the subtraction terms vanishes in the sum of (
1
)–(
4
), restoring the correct physical result

\sum_{n=2}^{5}\int_{n}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{n,\text{finite}}=\sum_{n=2}^{5}\int_{n}\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{n}\,,

(8)

with the crucial difference that each term in the sum on the left-hand side is individually well-defined and computable with numerical techniques.

The subtraction terms
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{1}}
,
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{2}}
,
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}}
,
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{1}}
and
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}}
are analogous to those required for an NNLO calculation of three-jet production in electron-positron annihilation
[
81
,
107
]
.
The subtraction terms
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{3}}
,
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{2}}
,
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}S_{1}}
and
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2,V_{3}}
specifically target N
3
LO infrared singularities and have been constructed for the first time for the present calculation.

The subtraction terms are built from antenna functions with the following notation:
X_{n}^{\ell}
is a
n
-parton
\ell
-loop antenna function intended to subtract the singular behavior of
n-2
unresolved partons between a pair of hard radiators at
\ell
loops.

X
is a placeholder for a particular partonic configuration
[
78
]
.
The quark-antiquark antenna functions relevant for our process are obtained from color-ordered matrix elements for the decay of a photon to
n
partons
[
77
,
91
]
, denoted by
M_{n,\gamma^{*}}^{\ell}
.
The integrated counterpart of an antenna function,
\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\ell}
, is obtained by analytic integration over the inclusive phase space of the
n-2
unresolved emissions.
All the N
3
LO final-state antenna functions were recently integrated in
[
91
,
40
,
39
]
.

The infrared limits appearing at N
3
LO cannot in general be addressed by a combination of NLO and NNLO ingredients, and three new types antenna functions must be defined: five-parton tree-level

\displaystyle X_{5}^{0}

\displaystyle=N_{5}\,\dfrac{M_{5,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}\,,

(9)

four-parton one-loop

\displaystyle X_{4}^{1}

\displaystyle=N_{4}\,\dfrac{M_{4,\gamma^{*}}^{1}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}-X_{4}^{0}\dfrac{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{1}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}\,,

(10)

and three-parton two-loop

\displaystyle X_{3}^{2}

\displaystyle=N_{3}\dfrac{M_{3,\gamma^{*}}^{2}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}-X_{3}^{1}\dfrac{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{1}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}-X_{3}^{0}\dfrac{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{2}}{M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}}\,,

(11)

where
N_{i}
indicates a numerical symmetry factor and the two-body decay matrix element
M_{2,\gamma^{*}}^{0}
is a normalization factor.
In the case of the loop antenna functions
X_{4}^{1}
and
X_{3}^{2}
, we remove
the singular behavior that can be captured by antenna functions of lower loop order, naturally extending the definition of three-parton one-loop antenna functions at NNLO
[
78
]
.

The
X_{5}^{0}
antenna functions are used in the subtraction term
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{3}}
to remove triple-real unresolved configurations.
The singular behavior of the double-real-virtual correction is addressed with the functions
X_{4}^{1}
within the term
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{2}}
, while
X_{3}^{2}
are used in
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}S_{1}}
to subtract the soft and collinear divergences of the real-double-virtual correction.
Finally, the term
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{2,V_{3}}
collects the integrated counterparts of all the N
3
LO antenna functions (
\mathcal{X}_{5}^{0}
,
\mathcal{X}_{4}^{1}
,
\mathcal{X}_{3}^{2}
) and removes the explicit IR singularities of the triple-virtual correction.
Throughout, we work with spin-averaged antenna functions and we fully remove azimuthal correlations in collinear limits using the angular averaging routines described in
[
85
]
, extended to handle triple-unresolved configurations.

After the removal of IR poles, the construction of
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{3,V_{2}S_{1}}
and
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{4,V_{1}S_{2}}
closely follows the logic of single-real and double-real infrared subtraction, already considered up to NNLO.
A subtlety arises when a one-loop reduced matrix element and a one-loop unresolved factor appear in a product.
To eliminate the need for higher orders in the dimensional regulator
\epsilon
in one-loop matrix elements, we consistently work with IR finite remainders.

Let us elaborate on a crucial aspect of a N
3
LO local subtraction scheme—the removal of triple-unresolved limits.
We start by recalling the structure of single-unresolved and double-unresolved subtraction terms:

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{1}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X_{3}^{0}\otimes M_{4}^{0}J_{2}^{(4)},

(12)

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{2}}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\Big[

\displaystyle\phantom{\otimes\,}\sum_{cc}(X_{4}^{0}-X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0})

(13a)

\displaystyle+\sum_{acc}(X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}+\mathcal{S}\otimes X_{3}^{0})\Big]

(13b)

\displaystyle\otimes M_{3}^{0}J_{2}^{(3)},

where
M_{n}^{0}
represents a generic tree-level color-ordered matrix element for
e^{+}e^{-}\to n
partons and the shorthand symbol
\otimes
encodes a momentum mapping from a higher-multiplicity to a lower-multiplicity phase space, in order to absorb unresolved emissions. For each term, a sum over external partons and color correlations is understood.
The jet function
J_{n_{j}}^{(n_{p})}
reconstructs
n_{j}
jets from
n_{p}
final-state partons.
The sums in (
13a
) and (
13b
) run over configurations where the two unresolved emissions are respectively
color-connected
(
cc
) and
almost color-connected
(
acc
)
[
78
,
81
]
.

The singular behavior of color-connected emissions can only be captured by dedicated
X_{4}^{0}
antenna functions.
To prevent double-counting of IR limits already addressed by (
12
), we introduce the term
X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}
in (
13a
), which subtracts the single-unresolved singularities from the
X_{4}^{0}
.
In the second term of (
13b
),
\mathcal{S}
represents a combination of eikonal factors required to remove soft singularities at large angle
[
106
,
81
]
.
Finally, the
X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}
and
\mathcal{S}\otimes X_{3}^{0}
structures present in
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{2}}^{(3)}
cancel against
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{1}}^{(3)}
in (
12
) when the reduced matrix element
M_{4}^{0}
develops an additional singularity on top of that already captured by the
X_{3}^{0}
antenna function.
Together,
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{1}}^{(3)}+\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{2}}^{(3)}
subtract all the single- and double-unresolved behavior of the five-parton tree-level matrix element fully differentially across the phase space.

The triple-real subtraction terms have an analogous structure:

\displaystyle\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{3}}^{(3)}=\Big[

\displaystyle\phantom{-}X_{5}^{0}

(14a)

\displaystyle-X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{4}^{0}

(14b)

\displaystyle-\sum_{cc}(X_{4}^{0}-X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0})\otimes X_{3}^{0}

(14c)

\displaystyle-\sum_{acc}(X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}+\mathcal{S}\otimes X_{3}^{0})\otimes X_{3}^{0}\Big]

(14d)

\displaystyle\phantom{-}\otimes M_{2}^{0}J_{2}^{(2)}

Here the
X_{5}^{0}
antenna functions address color-connected triple-unresolved emissions.
In particular, they contain the singular behavior in triple-soft
[
29
,
50
,
28
]
and quadruple-collinear
[
49
,
48
]
limits.
A
5\to 2
antenna momentum mapping
[
94
]
is used to relate the triple-real and the fully resolved phase space, and we apply phase space sectors
[
41
]
to avoid partial fractioning the antenna functions.
The remaining terms are needed to remove the single- and double-unresolved limits of the
X_{5}^{0}
, which are already subtracted by
\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{1}}+\text{d}\sigma^{(3)}_{5,S_{2}}
.
The five-particle antenna functions mimic the full matrix element in unresolved limits and factorize onto lower-multiplicity antenna functions as follows:

\displaystyle X_{5}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{single unres.}}

\displaystyle X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{4}^{0}\,,

(15)

\displaystyle X_{5}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[cc]{\text{double unres.}}

\displaystyle X_{4}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}\,,

(16)

\displaystyle X_{5}^{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[acc]{\text{double unres.}}

\displaystyle(X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}+\mathcal{S}\otimes X_{3}^{0})\otimes X_{3}^{0}\,.

(17)

Once again, the over-subtraction of single-unresolved singularities is prevented by removing the iterated
X_{3}^{0}\otimes X_{3}^{0}
contribution from the
X_{4}^{0}
using the second term in (
14c
).

The iterated structures in
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{3}}^{(3)}
also remove the singular behavior of
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{1}}^{(3)}
and
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{2}}^{(3)}
in triple-unresolved limits. In particular, (
14b
) cancels the additional double-unresolved singularities of
M_{4}^{0}
in (
12
), while (
14b
) and (
14c
) subtract the single-unresolved behavior of
M_{3}^{0}
originating from the terms (
13a
) and (
13b
), respectively.
Finally, note that in
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{3}}^{(3)}
, there is no analogue of the term (
13b
) in
\text{d}\sigma_{5,S_{2}}^{(3)}
.
This is due to the fact that with up to five partons in the final state, the three unresolved emissions must occur between the same pair of hard radiators, and they are therefore always color-connected.
For higher-multiplicity processes, such as three-jet production at N
3
LO, additional terms will be required to subtract singularities due to three unresolved partons between three or more hard radiators; for a discussion at NNLO see
[
66
]
.

3
Calculation

Our calculation was performed with a version of the
NNLOJET
Monte Carlo framework
[
90
]
extended for an N
3
LO calculation.
For the N
3
LO antenna functions
X_{n}^{\ell}
, we adapted the implementation of the relevant matrix elements in
EERAD3

[
83
]
.
The necessary helicity amplitudes for
e^{+}e^{-}\to\text{partons}
are available in
NNLOJET
as part of the calculation of three-jet production at NNLO
[
73
]
.
The five-parton tree-level amplitudes used in our calculation were computed in
[
86
,
11
,
62
]
, the four-parton one-loop amplitudes in
[
84
,
25
,
12
]
and the three-parton two-loop amplitudes in
[
69
,
70
]
.
Finally, the three-loop quark form factor was computed in
[
8
,
72
,
95
]
.

The numerical evaluation of these matrix elements in double-unresolved configurations at one loop and single-unresolved limits at two loops required careful treatment to achieve the necessary accuracy.
In double-unresolved limits of one-loop matrix elements, we implemented a dynamical switch from double to quadruple precision for floating point operations during the evaluation of the
X_{4}^{1}
antenna functions at phase-space points in the unresolved regions.
The switch is triggered when a two-particle invariant is lower than a given threshold,
s_{ij}/s<10^{-5}
, where
s
is the center-of-mass energy squared.
The two-loop quantities are expressed in terms of two-dimensional harmonic polylogarithms
[
75
]
.
These functions can be numerically evaluated using the library
tdhpl

[
76
]
, which uses a representation optimized to reduce the number of arithmetic operations for points in the bulk.
For points with
s_{qg}/s<10^{-6}
or
s_{\bar{q}g}/s<10^{-6}
, we substitute the amplitudes with their series expansions retaining divergent, constant and linear terms in the small invariant, obtained analytically using an algorithm
[
99
]
implemented in
PolyLogTools

[
58
]
.

To validate our construction of the antenna subtraction terms at N
3
LO, we verified that they cancel against the relevant matrix elements in all single-, double- and triple-unresolved limits at a satisfactory level for numerical integration with Monte Carlo techniques.
The complete analytic cancellation of the
\epsilon
poles of the virtual contributions is another check on the subtraction terms.

4
Results

All our results are normalized by the respective Born-level cross section
\sigma_{0}
for
e^{+}e^{-}\to q\bar{q}
.
As a consequence, the electroweak coupling drops out and our results are valid for both
\gamma^{*}
and
Z
as the intermediate boson.
We neglect the singlet contribution which is numerically small, as observed for example in
[
55
,
67
]
.
We set
\sqrt{s}=m_{Z}=91.2
GeV and
\alpha_{s}(m_{Z})=0.118
.
However, for the observables we consider, the dependence on the value of the energy scale only enters through the running of the strong coupling.
The renormalization scale is chosen to be equal to the center-of-mass energy,
\mu_{r}=\sqrt{s}
, and theory uncertainties are estimated by varying it within
\mu_{r}\in[\sqrt{s}/2,2\sqrt{s}]
.

The first result of this Letter is the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
correction to the inclusive cross section for hadron production. For five light quark flavors, we find

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{3}(-105\pm 11)\,,

(18)

where the error is due to Monte Carlo integration.
This value is in agreement with the analytic result
[
43
]
given by
\approx-102.14
.
We further compute the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})
correction to the two-jet exclusive cross section at two different values of the Durham
[
30
,
20
,
21
,
102
,
14
]
jet reconstruction parameter:

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}_{2j}(y_{\text{cut}}=0.1)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{3}(-453\pm 31)\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\sigma^{(3)}_{2j}(y_{\text{cut}}=0.01)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{3}(-2584\pm 40)\,.

(20)

We verified that both values are compatible with those obtained as the difference between the inclusive cross section and the three-jet production cross section at NNLO.
The relative error is smaller for (
20
) because it is less affected by large cancellations occurring in more inclusive setups.

On the other hand, for
y_{\text{cut}}=0.01
, the NLO and NNLO corrections quickly vanish as the energy increases and, accordingly, the N
3
LO correction becomes comparatively large because the nominal perturbative accuracy in the tail of the distribution is lower.
Indeed, a highly energetic jet must recoil against multiple partonic emissions, which are more likely to be clustered as three or more jets for a smaller value of
y_{\text{cut}}
.
For both choices of
y_{\text{cut}}
, real radiation is suppressed close to
E_{j,1}=\sqrt{s}/2
, affecting the cancellation of IR singularities and calling for all-order resummation in order to obtain physical results, as discussed e.g. in
[
100
]
.

5
Conclusions

We presented the first direct fully differential calculation of jet production in electron-positron annihilation at third order in QCD.
We described the structure of the IR counterterms for the final-state radiation in
e^{+}e^{-}\to jj
within antenna subtraction, including novel triple-unresolved limits.
As an application, we computed the production rate of two jets and the distribution of the energy of the leading jet at N
3
LO at different values of the jet reconstruction parameter.

The future generalization of the presented N
3
LO antenna subtraction terms to higher-multiplicity final states and hadronic initial states will likely build on the definition of optimal antenna functions
[
19
,
18
,
65
,
66
]
and recent steps towards the integration of N
3
LO antenna functions for initial-state radiation
[
63
]
.
Our work is an important milestone in establishing a general method for computing N
3
LO predictions to processes involving final-state jets.

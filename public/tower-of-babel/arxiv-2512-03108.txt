Nonanalytic Fermi-liquid correction to the specific heat of RuO 2
Shubhankar Paul, Atsutoshi Ikeda, Hisakazu Matsuki, Giordano Mattoni, Jörg Schmalian, Chanchal Sow, Shingo Yonezawa, Yoshiteru Maeno

Abstract
The magnetic nature of the altermagnet candidate RuO 2 remains under debate. It has been recently shown from quantum oscillations and angle-resolved photoemission spectroscopy (ARPES) that the high-quality RuO 2 bulk single crystal is a paramagnetic metal. However, the low-temperature specific heat exhibits a clear deviation from the conventional C ⁡ ( T ) C(T) = γ ​ T \gamma T + β ​ T 3 \beta T^{3} dependence; it is well described with nonanalytic Fermi-liquid correction for a clean paramagnetic metal: C ⁡ ( T ) C(T) = γ ​ T \gamma T + β ​ T 3 \beta T^{3} + δ ​ T 3 ​ ln ​ ( T / T ∗ ) \delta T^{3}\textrm{ln}(T/T^{*}) . Correspondingly, the magnetic susceptibility is well fitted with the inclusion of T 2 ​ ln ​ T T^{2}\textrm{ln}T term as well as H 2 ​ ln ​ H H^{2}\mathrm{ln}H term. In contrast to the spin fluctuation mechanism applicable to some heavy-electron compounds with positive δ \delta , RuO 2 shows negative δ \delta suggesting a different origin. The observation of such nonanalytic Fermi liquid corrections is attributable to the availability of an ultra-clean sample. The electronic specific heat, the magnetic susceptibility, and the T 2 T^{2} coefficient in resistivity point to a weakly-correlated 3D Fermi-liquid state with a modest electron correlation, as supported by the Wilson and Kadowaki-Woods ratios.

The low-temperature properties of a paramagnetic metal are well described as a Fermi liquid

It is widely understood that the dominant temperature dependence of thermodynamic quantities in an interacting fermion system is similar to that of non-interacting fermions, but with renormalized parameters.
The specific heat at low temperatures is approximated with a combination of the electronic contribution proportional to temperature

Instead of a
T^{3}
correction in the specific heat expected from the Sommerfeld expansion in powers of
T^{2}
for a non-interacting system, a “nonanalytic”
T^{3}\textrm{ln}T
term is expected to emerge in three dimensions (3D) due to an intrinsic Fermi-liquid effect
7
.
It remains a challenge to find an itinerant electron system that demonstrates the intrinsic Fermi-liquid corrections.
Table
1
compares these analytic and nonanalytic contributions anticipated in specific heat and magnetic susceptibility of Fermi liquids.

We report here that the highly conductive paramagnetic metal RuO
2
exhibits a strong deviation from a simple Fermi-liquid behavior, and the additional nonanalytic term
T^{3}\textrm{ln}T
better describes the observation.
This additional term is suppressed by the magnetic field isotropically.
The magnetic susceptibility also fits well with an additional
T^{2}\textrm{ln}T
term.
Such behavior is not due to magnetic fluctuations, but possibly due to electron-phonon scattering in the Fermi liquid.
To the best of our knowledge, such a nonanalytic Fermi-liquid correction has not been systematically demonstrated in a bulk transition-metal system before.

IV
DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION

The low-temperature specific heat of high-purity RuO
2
exhibits an unusual magnetic-field dependence; the
T^{3}
term, normally considered as a phonon contribution, depends strongly on the magnetic field.
At higher fields, the deviation from the conventional
\gamma T+\beta T^{3}
behavior becomes stronger, although the specific heat decreases isotropically.
Such overall deviation is well fitted by nonanalytic correction for the Fermi liquid,
\delta T^{3}\textrm{ln}(T/T^{*})
.
Correspondingly, the spin susceptibility also contains the
T^{2}\textrm{ln}T
term at low temperatures.
Experimentally, this phenomenon was initially observed in the specific heat of liquid
3
He by Wheatley
et\ al.
and used to determine its Landau Fermi-liquid parameters
1
;
15
;
48
.
For itinerant electron systems, the nonanalytic
T^{3}\textrm{ln}T
term in the specific heat is recognized in some heavy fermion systems such as UPt
3
, and it is understood as due to spin fluctuations arising from quasiparticle interactions in Fermi liquids
9
.
Nevertheless, the corresponding spin susceptibility does not agree well with the thermodynamic requirement from the specific heat.

The nonanalytic behavior in RuO
2
is in sharp contrast to the spin fluctuation contribution in heavy-fermion systems
9
.
In RuO
2
the sign of
\delta
is negative and
C/T
decreases by
9\%
under the magnetic field of 5 T, whereas in UPt
3

\delta
is positive and
C/T
increases at most a few
\%
at 5 T at low temperature
9
.
Concerning the origin of nonanalytic corrections, theories predict positive contribution from the electron-phonon scattering and negative contribution from the electron-electron processes including the repeated electron-hole scattering leading to spin fluctuations
8
;
7
.
The spin susceptibility in RuO
2
is dominated by a
T
-independent Pauli term and exhibits a modest 15
\%
anisotropy, whereas in UPt
3
it is strongly temperature dependent and anisotropic with different signs of
(\partial M/\partial T)
, depending on the field direction.

If the nonanalytic correction is dominated by the electron-phonon process, both
T^{*}
and
T_{0}
are expected to take similar values.
An outstanding question is whether the observed characteristic values,
T^{*}\approx 11
K in the specific heat and
T_{0}\approx 30
K in the spin susceptibility, are consistently explained by the electron-phonon process.
The universality plots of the Wilson ratio and the Kadowaki-Woods ratios places RuO
2
as weakly correlated Fermi liquid.
The contrasting behavior of the isotropic specific heat and the weakly anisotropic spin susceptibility of RuO
2
suggests additional magnetic enhancement that leads to its Wilson ratio being greater than 2.

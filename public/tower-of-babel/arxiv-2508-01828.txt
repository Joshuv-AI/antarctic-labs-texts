Abstract
The integration of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) with extremely large multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) arrays at the base station has emerged as a key enabler for enhancing wireless network performance. However, this setup introduces high-dimensional channel matrices, leading to increased computational complexity and pilot overhead in channel estimation. Mutual coupling (MC) effects among densely packed unit cells, spatial correlation, and near-field propagation conditions further complicate the estimation process. Conventional estimators, such as linear minimum mean square error (MMSE), require channel statistics that are challenging to acquire for high-dimensional arrays, while least squares (LS) estimators suffer from performance limitations. To address these challenges, the reduced-subspace least squares (RS-LS) estimator leverages array geometry to enhance estimation accuracy. This work advances the promising RS-LS estimation algorithm by explicitly incorporating MC effects into the more realistic and challenging near-field propagation environment within the increasingly relevant generalized RIS-aided MIMO framework. Additionally, we investigate the impact of MC on the spatial degrees of freedom (DoF). Our analysis reveals that accounting for MC effects provides a significant performance gain of approximately 5 dB at an SNR of 5 dB, compared to conventional methods that ignore MC.



I
Introduction

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces have gained significant attention for their ability to dynamically shape wireless propagation by adjusting the impedance of unit cells to control signal reflections and transmissions
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Such signal shaping enhances coverage, signal reliability, and overall communication performance. Accurate channel modeling is crucial, particularly for large-scale reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, where the expanded surface aperture extends the near-field region
[
4
]
. Unlike far-field conditions, near-field channels exhibit spherical wave propagation and spatial non-stationarity
[
4
,
5
]
.

Additionally, the mutual coupling effect in dense multiple-input multiple-output arrays and reconfigurable intelligent surfaces introduces electromagnetic interactions that distort radiation patterns, alter channel characteristics, and intensify spatial correlation, leading to performance bottlenecks
[
6
,
7
]
. Studies such as
[
8
]
investigate mutual coupling effects on holographic reconfigurable intelligent surfaces, analyzing their influence on eigenvalues, spatial correlation, and array gain. Moreover, neglecting mutual coupling leads to significant estimation errors and system degradation, as demonstrated in recent works
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. The authors in
[
9
]
highlight that mutual coupling critically affects multiple-input multiple-output channel estimation, even more than spatial correlation. Similarly,
[
10
]
demonstrates that in active reconfigurable intelligent surfaces with large amplitudes, mutual coupling can significantly degrade estimation performance in tightly spaced or large-scale setups. The study in
[
11
]
highlights the crucial role of accounting for mutual coupling in reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided channel estimation and beamforming. Despite extensive studies on mutual coupling, its impact in the near-field remains largely unexplored for large arrays and surfaces.

Unlocking the potential of reconfigurable intelligent surface technology depends on accurate channel estimation, which is challenging due to the unique characteristics of reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided links
[
12
]
. Deploying numerous reflecting surfaces alongside multiple-input multiple-output arrays at the base station results in high-dimensional channel matrices, increasing estimation overhead and computational complexity
[
13
]
. The minimum mean square error estimator requires perfect knowledge of channel statistics, which is difficult to obtain in high-dimensional settings, while least square estimation, though independent of prior information, performs poorly, especially at low signal-to-noise ratio
[
13
]
. To address these challenges,
[
14
]
proposed the reduced-subspace least squares estimator, which leverages channel geometry to reduce dimensionality and improve estimation accuracy in far-field reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided systems; its effectiveness in near-field multiple-input multiple-output systems was later explored in
[
15
]
.

The potential of the reduced-subspace least squares estimator to exploit mutual coupling information in a generalized MIMO-RIS setting with accurate near-field modeling is yet to be investigated. This paper addresses this gap by integrating mutual coupling effects and near-field characteristics into the channel estimation process. We analyze their combined impact on linear estimators and propose an enhanced reduced-subspace least squares estimator that leverages mutual coupling information to improve estimation accuracy. We further derive the general error covariance matrix for reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided multiple-input multiple-output systems and examine the degradation of spatial degrees of freedom (DoF) due to strong mutual coupling in dense antenna arrays, highlighting its implications for estimation performance. Our analysis shows that integrating mutual coupling into the reduced-subspace least squares estimator improves accuracy by approximately
5\,\mathrm{dB}
at an signal-to-noise ratio of
5\,\mathrm{dB}
compared to the reduced-subspace least squares estimator without mutual coupling consideration. Furthermore, we identify the most promising scenarios for the reduced-subspace least squares estimator, demonstrating that its highest performance gain occurs at small inter-element spacing.

Mutual Coupling

IV-A

LS Estimator

The least square approach is a widely used method for channel estimation
[

,
13
]
. To uniquely estimate

using a transmitted pilot symbol with power

\hat{{\bf c}}_{\mathrm{LS}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{t}}}({\bf Q}^{\sf H}{\bf Q})^{-1}{\bf Q}^{\sf H}{\bf y},

(19)

where the error covariance matrix is expressed as
[

{\bf R}_{e_{\mathrm{LS}}}=\frac{1}{\gamma N}(\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf T}\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf*})^{-1}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{MN},

(20)

and we define the signal-to-noise ratio as
\gamma=p_{t}/\sigma^{2}
.

IV-B

MMSE Estimator

The minimum mean square error estimator minimizes the mean square error and relies on the second-order statistics of the channel and noise. The minimum mean square error estimate of

is given by
[
23

\hat{{\bf c}}_{\mathrm{MMSE}}=\sqrt{p_{t}}{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}{\bf Q}^{\sf H}(p_{t}{\bf Q}{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}{\bf Q}^{\sf H}+\sigma^{2}\mathbf{I}_{MT_{\mathrm{p}}})^{-1}{\bf y},

(21)

where the covariance matrix of the cascaded channel is
[
12
]

{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}=({\bf R}_{{\rm H}{\rm R}}\odot{\bf R}_{{\rm F}{\rm R}})\otimes{\bf R}_{{\rm H}{\rm U}}\otimes{\bf R}_{{\rm F}{\rm B}}.

(22)

The minimum mean square error error covariance matrix is
[
23

{\bf R}_{e_{\mathrm{MMSE}}}=\left({\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}^{-1}+\gamma{\bf Q}^{\sf H}{\bf Q}\right)^{-1}=\left({\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}^{-1}+\gamma N(\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf T}\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf*})\otimes\mathbf{I}_{MN}\right)^{-1}.

(23)

IV-C

RS-LS Estimator

The reduced-subspace least squares estimator, proposed in
[
14
]
, mitigates pilot overhead in reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided systems while improving channel estimation accuracy. Instead of estimating the exact correlation matrix, it considers a subspace spanned by another spatial
correlation matrix that represents the union of the span of all plausible correlation matrices. This approach reduces computational complexity and pilot overhead, particularly in multiple-input multiple-output systems, where traditional estimators require additional training.

The eigen decomposition of the channel spatial correlation matrix is
{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}\!=\!{\bf U}{\bf D}{\bf U}^{\sf H}
, where
{\bf U}
contains the eigenvectors and
{\bf D}
is the diagonal matrix of eigenvalues. The reduced-subspace least squares estimate of the channel vector
{\bf c}
is obtained by projecting the received signal onto a reduced subspace spanned
by
{\bf U}_{1}
, which consists of the orthonormal eigenvectors corresponding to the nonzero eigenvalues of
{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}
and is structured as the Kronecker product of the BS, RIS, and UE subspaces,

{\bf U}_{1}={\bf U}_{{\rm R}{\rm I}{\rm S},1}\otimes{\bf U}_{{\rm U}{\rm E},1}\otimes{\bf U}_{{\rm B}{\rm S},1},

(24)

where
{\bf U}_{{\rm R}{\rm I}{\rm S},1}
,
{\bf U}_{{\rm U}{\rm E},1}
, and
{\bf U}_{{\rm B}{\rm S},1}
represent the orthonormal eigenvectors associated with the spatial correlation matrices at the reconfigurable intelligent surface, user equipment, and base station, respectively. The rank
r
of
{\bf R}_{\mathrm{c}\mathrm{c}}
is given by the product of the ranks of the reconfigurable intelligent surface, user equipment, and base station correlation matrices,
r\!=\!r_{\text{RIS}}\cdot r_{\text{UE}}\cdot r_{\text{BS}}
. These dimensions are significantly smaller than the original sizes and further decrease with higher correlation. Such dimensionality reduction significantly decreases the estimation’s computational complexity. Moreover, mutual coupling introduces additional correlation among unit cells, which affects the distribution of channel eigenvalues by concentrating energy into fewer dominant modes. Although standard reduced-subspace least squares assumes ideal, uncoupled arrays, we incorporate mutual coupling-aware correlation matrices to ensure that the constructed subspace accurately reflects the true signal structure, thus improving channel estimation accuracy.

The reduced-subspace least squares estimate is

\hat{{\bf c}}_{\text{RS-LS}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_{t}}}{\bf U}_{1}\left({\bf U}_{1}^{\sf H}{\bf Q}^{\sf H}{\bf Q}{\bf U}_{1}\right)^{-1}{\bf U}_{1}^{\sf H}{\bf Q}^{\sf H}\mathbf{y}.

(25)

We derive the general form of the RS-LS error covariance matrix for a multiple-input multiple-output user equipment and base station in a reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided link as

{\bf R}_{e_{\text{RS-LS}}}\!=\!\frac{1}{\gamma N}\left({\bf U}_{\text{RIS},1}\left({\bf U}_{\text{RIS},1}^{\sf H}(\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf T}\hbox{\boldmath$\Phi$}^{\sf*}){\bf U}_{\text{RIS},1}\right)^{-1}\!{\bf U}_{\text{RIS},1}^{\sf H}\otimes\mathbf{I}_{NM}\!\right),

(26)

where the detailed derivation is included in Appendix
A
.

VI
Conclusions

This study presents a comprehensive near-field channel modeling and estimation framework that accurately incorporates mutual coupling effects at the reconfigurable intelligent surface, base station, and user equipment. We develop and analyze the reduced-subspace least squares estimator for reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided multiple-input multiple-output systems, leveraging mutual coupling information to enhance estimation accuracy. In addition, we derive a general formula for the error covariance matrix of the reduced-subspace least squares estimator in multiple-input multiple-output reconfigurable intelligent surface systems. Our results demonstrate significant performance gains with the reduced-subspace least squares estimator, particularly at smaller inter-element spacings. These findings confirm that the reduced-subspace least squares estimator effectively exploits mutual coupling information in large-scale reconfigurable intelligent surface-aided systems.

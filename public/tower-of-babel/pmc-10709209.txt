Development of the Forman Alzheimer's disease scale
Authors: Maha Nadeem, Dr Ivan Suneel Samuel
Published: 2023

Introduction

With the advancement in health research there are certain areas such as “assessment of neurological disorders” which are less researched in underdeveloped countries like Pakistan. The progressive loss of neurons affect the daily life functioning of the patients. It is often believed that forgetting is a part of normal ageing process. However, when forgetfulness starts affecting daily life functioning of the individual it becomes a sign of Alzheimer's disease [1]. In Pakistan there is a dearth of awareness about the possibility of having dementia in elderly hence, the condition often goes under diagnosed [2,3]. Moreover, majority of the caregivers take their elderly to the doctors only when Alzheimer's disease starts affecting their daily life functioning [3].

The etiology of dementia was unclear in the past therefore, it was considered as a punishment for the sins committed by an individual [4]. The term dementia refers to the number of neurodegenerative disorders causing memory and other cognitive deficits [2]. Azlheimer's disease is considered as the most common form of dementia [2].

Alzheimer's disease is a neurodegenerative disease, it progresses with age and leads to irreversible loss of neurons specifically in the brain cortex and hippocampus [5]. Researchers have indicated the role of genetic factors that leaves an individual at risk for developing Alzheimer's disease. Increased amount of β amyloid (βA) protein on chromosome increase the process of degeneration in patients with Alzheimer's disease [6]. Moreover, the levels of acetylcholine decreases in the brain of individuals with Alzheimer's, as well as changes at a chemical level in tau protein take place which blocks the activity of neurotransmitters the process of degeneration [7].

Onset and prevalence

The onset of Alzheimer's disease occurs after 65 years of age and the onset during the 50's or early 60's is very rare [8]. According to research the prevalence of the disorder doubles after every five years [9]. Moreover, the prevalence increases 40–50 % among people between age range of 80–90 years [10]. Furthermore, research states that Alzheimer's disease is considered as the 5th leading cause of death among elderly and almost 35 million people are suffering from Alzheimer's disease and other types of dementia [9]. By the year 2030 and 2040 it is expected that around 65 and 80 million people respectively around the world will be suffering from Alzheimer's disease [9,10].

Types of Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's disease which starts in 40s, 50s or early 60s known as early-onset Alzheimer's disease [11]. It is less commonly diagnosed and shares 5 % burden of total population suffering from Alzheimer's disease [12]. The type of Alzheimer's disease which occurs at or after the age of 65 years is known as late-onset Alzheimer's disease [13]. Unlike early-onset genetic involvement is still unclear in late-onset Alzheimer's disease [14]. The risk factor that has been identified for the late-onset is apolipoprotein E 4 allele carriers [15].

Researcher indicate that early-onset is different from the late-onset of Alzheimer's disease because it is more severe in terms of brain damage as the rate of brain damage is higher in early onset [13]. Moreover, those who have family history of Alzheimer's disease have been found to be at greater risk for developing early-onset Alzheimer's disease [11,12]. The disease is difficult to diagnose because the symptoms appear differently from late-onset Alzheimer's disease [13].

Familial Alzheimer's disease is a type of disease which has certain genetic involvement. The diagnosis for this disease is given to the individuals whose previous 2 generations had been diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease [13].

Symptoms of Alzheimer's disease

Alzheimer's association provided a list of 10 early warning signs that indicate Alzheimer's onset [16]. These 10 symptoms include disturbance in memory where an individual faces difficulty forming new information, difficulty following plans and difficulty performing familiar tasks such as buttoning their clothes [16]. Moreover, individual with the disease tend to become disoriented because of forgetfulness of time and places as well as they tend to get lost on familiar routines [5]. Furthermore, as the disease progresses their inability to recognize familiar faces increase, impairment in language and sudden mood changes can also be observed [5,16]. The most commonly presented symptom during the initial stages is decline in memory and inability to recall newly formed memories [14] (Isik, 2010). As the disease progresses these all symptoms gets severe leading to loss of memory, marked decline in language, judgement, activities of daily living and motor functioning of the patients [14,17].

Severity of symptoms

Preclinical Alzheimer's Disease is the stage of the disease where changes in brain can be observed however, the symptoms of the disease have not been developed, this can be diagnosed by measuring βA protein levels in cerebrospinal fluid of the brain [2,18]. Individuals with mild level of severity of the disease are not completely dependent on others and they can perform different activities of daily living on their own [18]. The symptoms may include poor judgement, loss of spontaneous reactions, repetition of questions, misplacing things etc. [19].

According to Alzheimer's Association, moderate Alzheimer's disease is the stage which is the longest, memory gets noticeably affected, dependency on others increases even for the basic daily routine tasks and the patient can even become urinary incontinent [18]. During this stage personality and behavioral changes also start appearing. The physical symptoms become apparent on severe stage, an individual can even become bed-bound, the area of brain which controls swallowing get affected leading to inability to swallow food, Seizures, skin infections can also be observed [18,19]. These symptoms become the contributing factors for the death of individuals suffering from Alzheimer's disease [18].

Categorization of symptoms

Memory: Deficits in memory starts with forgetting of newly learned information i.e. deficit in short-term memory [20]. As the disease progresses the symptoms expands and a person starts forgetting the previously made memories i.e. long term memory. Whereas, deficits in semantic memory appears as inability to recall names [21]. Eventually in the later stages complete loss of memory occurs where a person forgets his close relationships and even him/herself [22].

Thinking and Reasoning: the patients of Alzheimer's disease face difficulty in handling problems, keeping track of time, identification of similarities and differences. Moreover, social judgement is also impaired [23]. Furthermore, the impairment in language can also effect the thinking and reasoning because of inability to find the right words [22].

Decision Making: as the disease progresses individual with Alzheimer's disease find it difficult to make decisions, express their views or inform others about their choices [22,23].

Performing Tasks: patients suffering from Alzheimer's disease can perform the daily routine tasks during the initial stages of disease. However, as the disease progresses the efficiency of performing tasks decreases and the person becomes dependent on care givers [22].

Psychological: psychological symptoms that are shown by the individuals suffering from Alzheimer's disease include depression, anger, agitation, aggression, personality changes and at times psychosis which predominantly includes hallucinations [22].

Assessment protocols

Alzheimer's is a neurodegenerative disease and it is very difficult to diagnose it early because the symptoms are considered part of normal aging [5]. Researchers in their research identified that most of the tools that are constructed for the diagnosis of the Alzheimer's disease involved cognitive scales only [24]. Therefore, it is identified that the behavioral and non-cognitive symptoms were less focused in order to gauge the diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease. According to Alzheimer's Association there are multiple ways of assessing if the person has Alzheimer's or not [18]. Mini mental status examination (MMSE) is a widely used scale to diagnose a person with Alzheimer's disease whose scores are above the cut off i.e. 24[25]; [26]. MMSE is a scale which measure the cognitive deficits in an individual and helps interpreting how the level of cognitive decline may affect his/her daily life [27]. MMSE can provide measures for client's orientation, immediate and short term memory, but not long term memory [28]. However, it cannot assess the behavioral and non-cognitive symptoms of Alzheimer's disease. Moreover it is not a sensitive measure for the symptoms related to executive functions and Activities of daily living. Furthermore, while interpreting scores of MMSE the language of assesse, their level of education and prior level of functioning should be taken into account [2].

Montreal Cognitive Assessment test was developed to measure mild cognitive impairment is used to assess dementia and Alzheimer's. It assesses memory, reasoning language and executive functioning of the person [29]. MCA consists of 30 items and requires 10–15 min for administration [30]. The limitation of this test is it also lacks inclusion of behavioral symptoms that are part of Alzheimer's disease.

According to researchers Alzheimer's disease assessment scale (ADAS) is the comprehensive assessment tool for the diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease [29]. It does not only cover the cognitive part of the disease but also the behavioral aspect of the disorder. ADAS consists of two subscales and the cognitive subscale requires a trained clinician/interviewer to administer. According to another study [31], 21 tasks were selected from the pool of 27 tasks which takes 40–50 min for the administration and it is scored by summing the number of errors made on each task. ADAS assesses multiple domains of Alzheimer's disease including memory, language, orientation etc. And it has to be administered on the patients to find the results. Since, in Pakistan the illness is reported when the severity of symptoms increases to the level when patient becomes fully dependent on the caregivers. Therefore, it would be difficult for the patients themselves to respond to a scale like ADAS in order to get the diagnosis. Moreover, the scale is not validated for the Pakistani population therefore, it can't be used with those who don't know English Language.

In Pakistan, majority of the neurologists use Mini mental status examination (MMSE) as a clinical examination tool for the diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease [2]. Urdu translation and validation of MMSE was completed in the year 2014 for the Pakistani population [32]. Before 2014, there was not even a single tool for the cognitive assessment of individuals with neurological disorders in Pakistan. The Aga Khan University Hospital, is one of the leading medical facilities in Pakistan. In their memo about Alzheimer's disease they have stated that there are no standardized tools for the assessment of Alzheimer's disease in Pakistan and diagnosis is made based on Doctor's clinical judgement [33].

Literature above indicates that in Pakistan Alzheimer's disease goes undiagnosed during the mild-moderate stage of the disease because individuals in this part of the world (Pakistan) confuse forgetfulness due to normal ageing with Alzheimer's disease [2,34]. Moreover, the illness is reported when the severity of symptoms increases to the level when patient becomes fully dependent on the caregivers. Most importantly it was found that there is dearth of standardized tools for the assessment of Alzheimer's disease in Pakistan. Therefore, in order to get the diagnosis, formulating a symptom based checklist which covers all types of symptoms of the disease can be helpful for screening purpose in this part of the world.

Method

The study was conducted using mixed-methods approach hence, both qualitative and quantitative measures were used to collect data. The study was completed using the 5 step model for tool construction [35].Step 1Qualitative Investigation of the ScaleThe study aimed to develop an indigenous scale for the screening of Alzheimer's disease for Pakistani population. As indicated by the literature review, there is no tool that can help diagnosing Alzheimer's disease for Pakistani population therefore, the items were generated using both Deductive method i.e. extracting items after analyzing the previously done studies and Inductive method i.e. generation of items through qualitative methods. This qualitative study and triangulation approach was used in which semi-structure interviews were conducted from patients, caregivers and professionals to extract items for the scale.

Qualitative Investigation of the Scale

The study aimed to develop an indigenous scale for the screening of Alzheimer's disease for Pakistani population. As indicated by the literature review, there is no tool that can help diagnosing Alzheimer's disease for Pakistani population therefore, the items were generated using both Deductive method i.e. extracting items after analyzing the previously done studies and Inductive method i.e. generation of items through qualitative methods. This qualitative study and triangulation approach was used in which semi-structure interviews were conducted from patients, caregivers and professionals to extract items for the scale.

Sample and population

The sample was recruited from the public and private hospitals of Lahore and Rawalpindi. Interviews of 10 professional (7 Neurologists and 3 Psychiatrists), 10 caregivers and 2 patients were conducted. The data was collected using convenient sampling since the report rate of patients was low in the hospitals due to COVID-19 restrictions.

Ethical considerations

Informed consent was taken from the participants of the study, in which researcher informed the purpose and duration of the research to the participants, the right to decline from taking part in the study were mentioned following the foreseeable effects of their withdrawal from the study. Moreover, the expected benefits of the study were communicated to the participants. Furthermore, participants were informed about audio recording of the interviews. Confidentiality and limits to confidentiality were also discussed.

Procedure

Data collection started after getting approval from Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Forman Cristian College (A Chartered University) with IRB reference number: IRB-228/06–2020. The neurologists and psychiatrists from different hospitals were contacted and the researcher provided them with the permission letters awarded by the university for sample collection. First of all the doctors were informed about the aim and rationale of the study, the advantages and disadvantages of their participation. Moreover, they were briefed about the procedure of gathering data i.e. they themselves will be the participants of the research, the patients who have been diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease will also take part in study because their information will be helpful in constructing the tool. After taking verbal consent from the participants Semi-structured interviews were conducted to gather data. The process of data collection ended when saturation of the data occurred. After completing the interviews the information was transcribed in verbatim and items were generated from the information gathered. Finally, the items extracted from the interviews were given the form of checklist.Step 2Qualitative ValidationContent based validity of the scale was ensured by using both inductive and deductive methods for item construction. The literature review helped in identifying the symptoms of Alzheimer's disease and the interviews helped identifying the symptomology of the patients of Alzheimer's disease in Pakistani population. Hence, to ensure that the content of the items does not change, the interviews were transcribed in verbatim. Finally, the symptoms were given the form of items of the scale.Step 3Mixing PhaseThe Mixing phase includes the steps used to convert qualitative data into scale item. As mentioned earlier, the verbatim of the participants about the symptoms of Alzheimer's disease was given the form of items and Likert type rating scale was established to get ratings of the items. The scoring of Likert scale ranged from 1 to 5 where 1 = never (کبھی نہیں) 2 = less often (بہت کم) 3 = often (کبھی کبھار) 4 = more often (زیادہ تر) and 5 = always (ہمیشہ) ۔Step 4Mixing ValidationThe newly developed checklist was given to 5 different participants mainly medical professionals. The participants were requested to rate each item on a scale of 1–5 based on its relevance with the symptoms of Alzheimer's disease in their expert opinion. The items which were rated above 3 were included in the final checklist and items rated below 3 b y most of the participants were excluded from the scale. This phase also helped researcher find out if the items were comprehendible to the participants or not. Therefore, after taking feedback the reported corrections were incorporated and the final list of items was generated.Step 5Main Study - Administration of the ScaleThe main study included the administration of the scale. The research in this phase was carried out using quantitative study design and survey method was used to collect data. Moreover, the final list which was generated after step 4 was given to the research group and the comparison group. The participants of the research group included the caregivers of patients suffering from Alzheimer's disease as well as the professional health workers. Whereas, comparison group included the participants from the healthy population of the same age group.

Main Study - Administration of the Scale

The main study included the administration of the scale. The research in this phase was carried out using quantitative study design and survey method was used to collect data. Moreover, the final list which was generated after step 4 was given to the research group and the comparison group. The participants of the research group included the caregivers of patients suffering from Alzheimer's disease as well as the professional health workers. Whereas, comparison group included the participants from the healthy population of the same age group.

Sampling

The sample was recruited with the help of convenient sampling using snow ball sampling technique. This sampling technique was used because the report rate of Alzheimer's disease is very low in Pakistan because of lack of awareness [34] (Zaidi et al., 2019). Therefore, convenient sampling was used to access the targeted number of participants in limited time. The sample size was finalized after reviewing the literature and it was decided to collect data from 100 to 120 participants. Finally, 110 participants (80 caregiver, 30 health care workers) took part in the study. The sample was collected from public and private hospitals of Karachi and Islamabad. Moreover, diagnosed members of old age home based in Karachi also took part in the study.

Inclusion criteria

The sample included the caregivers of patients diagnosed with Alzheimer's disease and health care professionals. Those who are suffering from another neurological illness along with Alzheimer's disease were excluded from the study. Those who could understand and speak Urdu took part in the study.

Tools for measurement

Demographic form: demographic form will help researcher gain insight about the general information about the client. Demographic form will include the name of the participant (Initials), gender, age, reporter, city and their contact information.

Forman Alzheimer's Disease Scale FADS: the newly developed checklist FADS was used to collect data from the participants. It consists 31 items which can be rated on a Likert scale of 1–5 where 1 represents never and 5 represents always.

Kaiser's criterion/eigen value

Kaiser's method [36] is the most commonly used method to retain the number of factors based on their Eigen value that should be greater than 1 [37].

Table above indicates that up to seven factors can be made for the scale items. Since, the total variance analysis is giving a higher number of factors, Scree plot should be visited to find if the number of factors changes or not.

Scree plot

Another way to find out the accurate number of factors for factor analysis is Scree test, which requires a plot as shown in Fig. 1 [37]. The Eigen values on y-axis and components on the x-axis, this test requires researcher's interpretation of where a Scree or elbow joint forms till that point the factors should be kept [36,38].Fig. 1Scree plot demonstrating Eigen values with component number for FADS.Fig. 1

The scree plot reflects that three factors can be optimal for the scale items. It was concluded because the elbow curve can be identified on the 3rd component. Therefore, this 3 factor solution can be considered in contrast to the 7 factor solution provided by the variance explained using Eigen value rule.

Many researchers use parallel component analysis for component reduction for exploratory factor analysis, parallel analysis is used by researchers as it accurately provides the significant number of factors for the analysis [39].

Discussion

The total item correlation of the 31 scale items was greater than. 0.3 and the value of Kiaser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) measure of adequacy was 0.826 which was significant as it should be greater than 0.638. Three different analysis were run to find out accurate number of factors, the analysis include Kaiser's Eigen value rule, Scree test and parallel analysis [36,39]. Finally, three factors were selected for exploratory factor analysis [38].

The three factor solution was analyzed using Varimax method in SPSS-20 and 6 items were cross loaded into multiple factors where items were rated greater than 0.3 across different factors. Researchers assert that the items should not cross load on multiple factors [40]. Therefore, to minimize the cross loading of the items two factor solution was analyzed using direct Oblimin methods as it is recommended by the researchers Field.

With the help of pattern matrix it was gauged that no items were cross loaded. Based on these results two factors were given the names based on the themes originated from the items. First factor contains items related to Cognitive deficits and Activities of daily living and the items in Second factor formed theme of Neuropsychiatric Symptoms. These findings are similar to the multiple domains covered by Alzheimer's disease assessment scale ADAS. As ADAS covers memory, language and orientation, the items included in Factor 1 also tap in the domains memory and language of an individual with Alzheimer's disease.

Implications of present research

The findings of this research has multiple implications pertaining to the area of research as well as for clinical use. The study developed an indigenous tool i.e. Forman Alzheimer's Disease Scale, for the screening of Alzheimer's disease in Pakistani population. The scale taps into multiple domains of Alzheimer's disease it will help professionals to formulate intervention plan for the patient depending on the nature and severity of the symptoms. Moreover, this scale can be administered multiple times during the course of treatment as a reference to check if the symptoms has decreased and the treatment plan is effective or not.

Limitations of present research

The present scale consists of items which can be reported by the caregivers or family members whereas, it lacks the items which are performance based i.e. which involves patient themselves to respond. Due to COVID 19 pandemic, a large number of participants could not take part in the study therefore, another study can be done involving larger number participants for the generalization of the scale. A confirmatory factor analysis can be run to corroborate the results of exploratory factor analysis.

An enhanced dual inception-attention-BiGRU-attention model integrating wavelet transform for wearable sensor-based human activity recognition
Authors: Xinguo Zhang, Yitao Wang, Tao Deng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith advancements in deep learning (DL), machine learning (ML) techniques, and data processing capabilities have improved the accuracy and efficiency of HAR systems. Current literature emphasises the effectiveness of hybrid complex models that incorporate advanced computing components. In this study, we present a hybrid model integrating Inception, attention mechanisms, and BiGRU, termed Dual Inception-Attention-BiGRU-Attention (Dual-INABA). Our model achieves a classification accuracy of 96.95% on the self-collected WSMC-HAR dataset and 98.15%, 98.89%, and 96.78% on the benchmark datasets PAMAP2, UCI-HAR, and WISDM, respectively. We conduct ablation experiments to investigate the individual and synergistic effects of the components in our model. The results reveal BiGRU’s effectiveness in reducing loss rates, highlight the Inception model’s contribution to enhancing overall accuracy, and the benefits of component collaboration. We examine the influence of processing methods on the hybrid model, demonstrating that signal-denoising techniques can enhance classification performance. Notably, the Daubechies (Db) wavelet transform method outperforms its counterparts in signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and leads to high classification accuracy. What is more, we collect acceleration signals using the MPU6050 accelerometer sensor, resulting in a dataset comparable in quality to commonly used benchmark datasets.Subject terms: Network models, Neural decoding, Neural encoding, Computer science, Scientific data

Introduction

Human activity recognition (HAR) involves the utilization of collecting devices and algorithms to identify and analyze human movement automatically. With the rapid development of data science, HAR has been a key area of interest in ubiquitous computing1,2, with extensive applications across various domains, such as healthcare monitoring3,4, motion analysis5, behavior recognition6, and smart home technologies7. HAR can be classified into sensor-based8, video-based9, WiFi-based10, and audio-based11 categories based on the various collection devices employed. Sensor-based HAR systems recognize human activity by analyzing signals collected from various parts of the human body, facilitating the accuracy and reliability of activity monitoring12. Besides, wearable sensor technologies have numerous benefits, such as decreased cost, diminished sample rates, and enhanced robustness. Video-based HAR is constrained in application because of the high cost of expensive equipment and concerns about personal privacy infringement13. WiFi-based HAR raises issues with high power consumption and lower reliability14. Audio-based HAR typically operates at sampling frequencies around 40 kHz, resulting in large datasets that require significant labelling and training efforts. Additionally, the complexity of audio signals necessitates sophisticated processing techniques11. Nowadays, the built-in accelerometers in smartphones allow for the convenient and effortless collection of activity signals, including acceleration, angular velocity, and heart rate15. This makes it highly robust in noisy, real-time, and dynamic environments. At the same time, it maintains high recognition accuracy. The general process of sensor-based HAR includes data collection, data processing, feature extraction, and classification16,17, which is depicted in Fig. 1. The collected data are filtered, standardized, and segmented in the data processing step. From the 2000s to the 2010s, researchers employed machine learning (ML) methods to tackle HAR tasks. Machine learning (ML) approaches often depend on manual feature extraction because of their shallow learning architectures. This process involves fusing data from multiple sensors and relies on researchers’ choices regarding feature types, dimensionality, and kernel parameters18. Consequently, ML algorithms have challenges in handling complex scenarios and adapting to new changes.

The sensor-based HAR workflow.

Deep learning (DL) methodologies emerged as powerful alternatives around 2014, significantly advancing the field of HAR. With Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), DL methods learn high-level abstract features from data19,20. CNN evolved from multi-layer perceptrons to perform exceptionally well in computer vision21. The Network in Network (NIN) architecture enhances the discriminability of CNNs for local patches and is also known as Inception22. The essential advantage of Inception is to learn multi-scale representations to handle the varying patterns in activity-related time series data23–25. RNN is a network that processes sequential data26. Long short-term memory (LSTM) networks27 and Gated Recurrent Units (GRUs)28,29 are variants of recurrent neural networks (RNNs) designed to mitigate gradient vanishing and gradient explosion issues. GRUs are easier to train and faster to run than LSTMs, but they may be less effective at storing and accessing long-term dependencies. Although DL methodologies have demonstrated considerable efficacy in HAR, there is still room for improvement due to background noise. By leveraging the multi-resolution analysis of wavelet transform (WT), this method excels at preserving signal details while reducing noise30. With various wavelet functions, WT offers time-frequency analysis31. Daubechies wavelets enable efficient representation and reconstruction of both smooth and discontinuous signals through compact support and orthogonality32. The Morlet wavelet is a complex wavelet that combines a sine wave with a Gaussian envelope. The adjustable width of the Gaussian envelope offers a trade-off between time and frequency resolution, making the Morlet wavelet particularly useful in applications such as time-frequency analysis and signal decomposition33. The Mexican Hat wavelet, the second derivative of a Gaussian function, has a symmetric “hat” shape. Its infinite support aids in edge detection, effectively capturing rapid changes in data34. These techniques preserve the relevant features of the signal while effectively reducing noise, thereby enhancing the robustness of the HAR system. Yao et al.35 highlights the synergy of wavelet decomposition and convolutional layers in addressing over-parametrization and performance saturation seen in large-kernel CNNs. Ahmed N. et al.36 employs wavelet-based spectral visualization to transform sensor signals into spectrogram-like images that encode frequency and temporal information. The transformed images serve as inputs to deep networks, improving feature extraction robustness and performance in HAR tasks. Nedorubova A. et al.37 proposes combining Continuous Wavelet Transform (CWT) to convert raw time-series sensor data into a time-frequency representation as input to Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs). This study presents a pipeline of HAR using wearable sensing data. We utilize the wavelet transform (WT) to obtain a time-frequency representation of the signal and then apply a low-pass filter to improve the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). The processed data are fed into an innovative dual Inception-Attention-BiGRU-Attention (Dual-INABA) fusion model consisting of two parallel branches. Related works

In this section, we review the literatures on the activity classification in sensor-based HAR. Effective data segmentation is crucial for data processing, and the widely used sliding window technique facilitates this segmentation38. The segmentation parameters were tailored to optimize recognition tasks across diverse scenarios39,40. Machine learning (ML) algorithms, such as support vector machines(SVM)41, decision trees42, and random forests43, have demonstrated notable performance in action classification. However, ML methods are labor-intensive in parameter tuning, rule selection, and data processing. The DL methods, as reviewed by Wang et al.44 has demonstrated the advantages in automating the feature extraction and classification. Hammerla and Thomas Plotz et al.45 rigorously explored DL methods across three representative datasets, achieving an accuracy of 93.70% with CNN on the PAMAP2 dataset and 92.70% with RNN on the Opportunity dataset. Abisek Dahal and Soumen Moulik46 developed a multistream convolutional neural network-bidirectional long short-term memory (CNN-BiLSTM) framework for activity prediction, achieving an accuracy of 98.06% on the HAR eight-channel benchmark dataset. Ronald et al. proposed iSPLInception, which achieved an 89.09% accuracy on the PAMAP2 dataset47. Inspired by Inception, Dua et al. combined Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) with gated recurrent units (GRU)48, achieving accuracies of 99.25% and 97.64% on the MHEALTH and PAMAP2 datasets, respectively. Rahman Mim et al. proposed an Gated Recurrent Unit-Inception (GRU-INC)23 that effectively makes use of the temporal and spatial information of the time-series data. The model has achieved an F1 score of 96.27%, 90.05%, 90.30%, 99.12%, and 95.99% on the publicly available datasets such as, UCI-HAR, OPPORTUNITY, PAMAP2, WISDM, and Daphnet, respectively. Yu et al. employed CNN for spatial feature extraction and GRU in temporal correlations capturation49, resulting an accuracy over 95% on public datasets. Lu et al. proposed a multi-channel fusion model that combines CNN with a GRU to extract multiple scale features and learn temporal relationships50. In another work51, they proposed a specific denoising model in data processing. Zhang et al. leveraged diverse convolution CNN kernels alongside GRU to extract distinct local features and capture long-term dependencies52. Lately, Imran advanced the model with BiGRU units and they reported a classification accuracy of 97.20% on the WISDM 2011 dataset54. Compared to traditional GRU, BiGRU incorporates bidirectional propagation, enabling it to more effectively extract data features53. However, the BiGRU has not been widely used in the HAR so far. In this study, we introduce the dual Inception-Attention-BiGRU-Attention (Dual-INABA) model, which integrates dual input streams, BiGRU, and attention mechanisms. This innovative architecture effectively enhances computational efficiency, marking a significant advancement in HAR for practical applications. Since Ashish Vaswan et al. proposed the working principle of the attention mechanism63, fusion models with the attention mechanism have been widely used in the HAR task. Verma et al. integrate attention mechanisms, CNN, and GRU successfully55. Sarkar et al. developed a spatial attention hybrid multi-branch architecture enable the retrieval of high-dimensional features from encoded data56. Khatun et al.57 utilized the self-attention module to enhance the predictive capabilities of the CNN-LSTM model and achieved 98.76% in MHEALTH and 93.11% in UCI-HAR datasets. Li et al. introduced the modules of spatial attention, channel attention, and aligned attention for single-stage object detection58. Wang et al. proposed an attention-based convolutional fusion model and achieved 93.83% accuracy on the UCI-HAR dataset59. In order to address this, the Dual-INABA uses a parrel structure that processes features at many levels at once. The design is able to concentrate on the key information thanks to its enhanced attention module. All of these network designs have significantly advanced sensor-based HAR. However, the system’s integrity still faces some shortcomings, particularly regarding the practical reduction of noise from the collected signals. Srihari et al. proposed a Gaussian adaptive Bilateral Filter (GABF) for removing the noise from the thermal image and handling the occlusion problem60. A hybrid model of GABF and the DeepLabv3Net model has achieved 98.50% accuracy in the IITR Infrared Action Recognition (IITR-IAR) dataset and 94.85% accuracy in the Thermal Simulated Fall (TSF) dataset, respectively61. Inspired by this concept, we develope a denoising module that integrates wavelet transform (WT) and filtering techniques to enhance the performance of a hybrid DL model for sensor-based HAR in this study.

Method

The self-collecting WSMC-HAR dataset is collected at the Wireless Sensor and Mobile Computing Lab (WSMC) at the Chinese National Information Technology Research Institute, Northwest Minzu University, Lanzhou, China. The experimental campaign was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki. The activity data were primarily collected from wearable devices and processed anonymously, ensuring no personally identifiable information was retained. All participants were adults who provided written informed consent prior to their involvement, including consent to publish any images or videos taken during the experimental protocol. This study states that data was collected from volunteers for academic purposes without violating privacy, with the approval of the Chinese National Information Technology Research Institute. Given the nature of the study and in accordance with the guidelines of the Academic Committee at the Chinese National Information Technology Research Institute, ethical approval was considered unnecessary. Subsequently, we introduce the Dual-INABA model, which integrates Inception, Bidirectional Gated Recurrent Units (BiGRU), and attention mechanisms to effectively analyze and classify the data. We conduct ablation experiments to analyze the individual contributions of each module within the Dual-INABA model and assess the synergistic effects of their interactions. We test the proposed model on a self-collecting dataset and three public benchmark datasets. Data processing

Wavelet transform

Utilizing the wavelet transform (WT) to analyze the time-frequency spectrum, the complete procedure with three wavelet functions is depicted in Fig. 2. WT is employed to extract time-frequency representation, while the low-pass filter is applied to extract relevant activity frequencies. The filtered data will be segmented for the proposed classification model. Daubechies (Db), Morlet, and Mexican Hat wavelets are used as mother functions offering unique characteristics and suitable for distinct applications. The formulas for the three wavelet transforms are reiterated in Table 1. The Db wavelet has N vanishing moments, allowing it to annihilate polynomials of degree up to :

In this study, we set N = 4. The Db4 wavelet possesses 4 vanishing moments, which allows it to perfectly annihilate polynomials up to degree 3. This property is essential for effectively capturing sharp transitions and nonlinear behaviours commonly found in inertial HAR signals. While the Mexican Hat wavelet has only 1 vanishing moment, limiting its ability to capture higher-order polynomial behaviours. This means it can annihilate constant functions but not linear functions or higher-order polynomials. The Morlet wavelet lacks vanishing moments, making it less effective in managing polynomial behaviours. The three mother wavelet functions, along with their corresponding time-domain representations and spectral components, are illustrated in Fig. 3. The original temporal signal convolve with wavelet functions (Db, Morlet, and Mexican Hat) at different scales to obtain the time-frequency distribution. The Db wavelet offers good localization in both time and frequency domains with a compact support, making it suitable for capturing capture transient features and abrupt changes in the signal over time. The Mexican Hat wavelet, characterized by its moderate localization and “hat” shape, is effective for edge detection and transient phenomena analysis. In contrast, the Morlet wavelet excels in frequency localization, particularly for oscillatory signals, due to its complex exponential modulation with a Gaussian envelope.

Low-pass filter

In this section, a 10th-order Butterworth low-pass filter is applied to remove high-frequency noises while retaining the effective spectral component. The data encompass a wide range of frequency components associated with various activities. As listed in Table 2, frequency signals between 7 to 8 Hz correspond to running, while those around 2 to 3 Hz indicate standing, among others. The cutoff frequency settings take into consideration multiple factors, including Nyquist rates, experimental paradigms, and the types of signals collected. The WSMC-HAR and WISDM datasets, which collect acceleration signals, have a Nyquist frequency of 10 Hz, leading to a cutoff frequency set at 8 Hz for both. The UCI-HAR dataset collects acceleration and gyroscope signals with a Nyquist frequency of 25 Hz, resulting in a cutoff frequency of 18 Hz. Meanwhile, the PAMAP2 dataset, which collects acceleration, gyroscope, and heart rate signals, has a Nyquist frequency of 50 Hz and a cutoff frequency of 25 Hz.

Proposed network

The proposed network includes two parallel branches for feature extraction, followed by a softmax layer for classification. Each branch comprises the Inception module, BiGRU, and attention mechanisms, arranged in the sequence of Inception-Attention-BiGRU-Attention. The entire network is termed as Dual-INABA. By integrating multi-scale feature extraction, bidirectional modelling, and attention mechanisms, the Dual-INABA network effectively captures complex dependencies and enhances performance in HAR data. The structure of the proposed model is illustrated in Fig. 4, while Fig. 5 depicts its procedure as a flowchart. Attention mechnism

The attention mechanism is used to help the model’s focus on specific parts of the input data as presented in Fig. 8. Attention allows the model to evaluate the significance of different elements in the input during the prediction process. Input representations are transformed into three matrices: Queries (Q), Keys (K), and Values (V). Relevance scores are calculated by taking the Q and K dot product, which are then normalized using the softmax function to produce attention weights. These weights dictate how much focus is assigned to each value (V), resulting in a context vector that encapsulates pertinent information for the query. Experiments

We integrate the Dual-INABA model and wavelet transform denoising method to improve the classification performance. The dataset is denoised and then divided into training, validation, and test sets, ensuring effective model training and evaluation. Using a cross-subject partitioning approach, the data is allocated such that 70% of the samples are assigned to the training set, 20% to the testing set, and 10% to the validation set. The validation set was not created in the self-collected dataset (WSMC-HAR) due to the limited amount of data, ensuring the effectiveness of the model training process. The commonly used evaluation metrics are accuracy, F1 score, Recall, and Precision, alongside a confusion matrix. These indicators offer a comprehensive evaluation of the model’s effectiveness, facilitating a nuanced understanding of its classification capabilities. During training, categorical cross-entropy is employed as the loss function, with the Adam optimizer facilitating convergence. The model is trained on the training set, with regular evaluations on the validation set to fine-tune hyperparameters, while early stopping helps prevent overfitting. All the parameters involved in the training process are suggested in Table 3. We obtained the final hyperparameters through grid search. A batch size of 32 is utilized to enhance data processing efficiency and ensure frequent weight updates, optimizing memory usage. To address overfitting, a dropout rate of 0.3 is implemented. The learning rate is configured at 0.001, and the Adam optimizer is employed for the model’s adaptive learning properties. The network undergoes training for 150 epochs, providing sufficient time to refine parameters and effectively capture the underlying patterns in the data. The loss function used is categorical cross entropy, while the activation function applied is Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU). The computing hardware applied for this experiment includes an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-10510U CPU with a base clock speed of 1.80 GHz, and a turbo boost up to 2.30 GHz, paired with Intel(R) UHD Graphics for GPU support. The experiment employs TensorFlow in version 2.9.0, a widely acclaimed deep learning network, alongside Pandas version 2.0.3 and NumPy version 1.24.3 for data manipulation and numerical computations.

By systematically removing the Inception, BiGRU, and attention modules within the Dual-INABA network, we gain valuable insights into the isolated contributions of each module and the synergy effects of multiple modules. This experiment generates a series of model variants: Dual Inception only, Dual Inception-BiGRU, Dual Inception-Attention, and single-branch INABA. Self-collected WSMC-HAR dataset

We develop an embedded wearable device to satisfy the demands of indoor activities, in which each component underwent a meticulous design and optimization process. First, the STM32F103 system-on-chip (SoC) from STMicroelectronics is selected as the control processing module for the wearable device. Second, the accurate sensor data from the MPU6050 accelerometer gives a precise description of the user’s movement. The MPU6050 accelerometer is selected for its broad measurement range (±2g) and its on-chip 1024-byte first-in-first-out (FIFO) function. What is more, a temperature sensor and an oscillator with only ±1% variation in the working environment are embedded in the SoC. Communication between the sensors and the control module is realized through a 400 kHz iterintegrated circuit (I2C) interface, which ensures high-speed data transfer. As far as the wireless communication module is concerned, the CC2530 chip based on the ZigBee protocol stack from Texas Instruments is selected to ensure stable transmission. Therefore, we can set up a simple and reliable one-to-one wireless data transmission network by the CC2530. With low privacy invasiveness, easy configurability, and high reliability, this design effectively meets the requirements for remote communication in indoor environments compared to camera, BLE, and Wi-Fi. Besides, the input and output ports are also designed with considerations for flexibility and expansion, providing users with convenient interfaces for connection and interaction with external devices. The hardware structure of the integrated embedded wearable device is depicted in Fig. 11. An efficient data acquisition and storage scheme has been developed to ensure accurate and reliable dataset generation. The data is captured using the MPU6050 acceleration sensor and transported by ZigBee. The dynamic range of the accelerometer output data is set at g with a sampling rate of 20 Hz. The collected activity information is transmitted to a PC host and stored in a text file (.txt). Specifically, the text files are transmitted to a data receiving device through the wireless network and uploaded to the PC using a Universal Synchronous/Asynchronous Receiver/Transmitter (USART). The ZigBee is a low-power wireless communication technology to transmit data effectively. In order to achieve stable communication with the host, the data-receiving device is equipped with a transistor logic (TTL)-to-universal serial bus (USB) electronic component. Seven participants (males) perform five activities: running, jogging, sitting, upstairs, and downstairs. The participants have a mean age of 21 years, a mean weight of 66 kg, and a mean height of 175 cm. Participants are instructed to perform these activities in a healthy and comfortable state to capture natural movement. Ethical approval for this work has been granted by the Chinese National Information Technology Research Institute, Northwest Minzu University, Lanzhou, China. The experimental campaign was conducted following the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent, including consent to publish images or videos taken during the experimental protocol. The selected activities are frequently repeated in daily life, and the ease of switching between them facilitated their collection by the device and in the experiment. The collected data is named as WSMC-HAR as experiments carried in the Wireless Sensor and Mobile Computing Lab (WSMC). The datasets include essential attributes such as user ID, activity type, timestamp, and three-axis accelerometers (X, Y, and Z). The total number of records is 255,176, in which jogging makes up 33.6%, upstairs 23.0%, downstairs 21.3%, running 11.9%, and sitting 10.3%. The sample number distribution of each activity is depicted in Fig. 10(d). The WSMC-HAR dataset has achieved a comparable signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) to that of the widely used benchmark datasets.

Three benchmark datasets

The UCI-HAR dataset includes six activities performed by 30 participants aged 19 - 48, such as walking, sitting, and standing64. The PAMAP2 dataset captures 12 physical activities from 9 subjects using multiple IMUs and heart rate monitors65, with activities including walking, cycling, and vacuum cleaning. The WISDM dataset66 comprises over 1 million data points collected from 36 participants using a smartphone accelerometer, focusing on activities like sitting, walking, and jogging. The sample distribution of each activity for three bechmark datasets are shown in Fig. 10a–c.

Results of data processing

The integration of Wavelet transform (WT) with three mother functions and Dual-INABA model demonstrates a significant enhancement in classification accuracy compared with Fourier transform (FT) as reported in Table 6. The Daubechies (Db) WT demonstrates the best performance across three datasets: WSMC-HAR (96.95%), PAMAP2 (98.15%), and WISDM (96.78%). The Morlet WT achieves the highest accuracy of 98.93% on the UCI-HAR dataset. In comparison to the Db WT and Morlet WT, the results from the Mexican Hat WT are slightly less robust yet still competitive. Db WT improves average accuracy by 0.21% over Morlet WT and 0.43% compared to Mexican hat WT on four datasets. However, the classification accuracy of the proposed model with the Morlet WT is 0.04% higher than with the Db WT on the UCI-HAR dataset. Notably, the proposed network achieves higher performance when classifying processed data compared to raw data. In particular, the average improvements in accuracy across the four datasets are 4.25%, 3.05%, 2.33%, and 2.63% for the Db, Mor, MH, and FT processing methods, respectively. Furthermore, with a combination analysis of the results presented in Table 8 and Table 9, it is clear that all models demonstrate an improvement in accuracy with the addition of Db Wavelet Transform processing steps. Dual-INABA achieves an average accuracy increase of 4.11% across four datasets. Inception-GRU-attention achieves an average accuracy improvement of 1.39%. The iSPLInception demonstrates an average accuracy increase of 3.97%. Multi-input CNN-GRU achieves an average accuracy improvement of 1.77%. Multi-channel CNN-GRU shows an average accuracy increase of 2.37%. LSTM-RNN gets an average accuracy improvement of 2.42%. CNN-LSTM shows an average accuacy increase of 3.36%. The CNN model achieves an average accuracy enhancement of 2.80%. What is more, the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of the four data processing methods is demonstrated in Fig. 13(a). The Db WT consistently achieved the highest SNR values: 12.34 dB for WSMC-HAR, 15.67 dB for UCI-HAR, 15.92 dB for PAMAP2, and 12.30 dB for WISDM. These results highlight the effectiveness of the Db WT. Although the SNR of the Morlet and Mexican Hat WT are lower than the Db WT, they are still significantly better than the results of FT. The processed data with the four signal processing methods are fed into the proposed network for activity recognition. The training loss function of Db WT processed data achieves a faster decline speed and covergence at a lower loss value as depicted in Fig. 13(b) compared to the other data processing methods.

Ablation experiments results of the Dual-INABA model

The ablation experiments of Dual-INABA on these datasets are reiterated in Table 7. For Dual-INABA, the loss rate presents a sharp decline within the first 5th epochs, reaches a turning point around the 8th epoch, and achieves a low value of approximately 0.05 by the 20th epoch, stabilizing thereafter. When the attention module is removed, the loss rate of Dual Inception-BiGRU reaches its turning point around the 40th epoch and achieves a low value of approximately 0.13 by the 80th epoch. When the BiGRU is removed, the loss rate of Dual Inception-Attention reaches its turning point around the 50th epoch and achieves a low value of approximately 0.2 by the 90th epoch. The loss rate of the single-branch INABA model reaches a turning point around the 60th epoch, ultimately achieving a low loss value of approximately 0.23 by the 120th epoch. Among the four model variants in the ablation experiments, Dual Inception-BiGRU shows the best performance in terms of the loss function. Discussion

In this study, we developed a Dual-INABA model, which integrates Inception, attention mechanisms, and BiGRU components to enhance classification accuracy in HAR datasets. As is portrayed in Table 4, the Dual-INABA model exhibits robust classification performance across various datasets, achieving accuracies of 98.89% for UCI-HAR, 98.15% for PAMAP2, 96.78% for WISDM, and 96.95% for self-collected WSMC-HAR. The high accuracy rates underline its effectiveness and adaptability in handling diverse data distributions. Compared to other datasets, UCI-HAR and PAMAP2 include additional angular acceleration and heart rate features. This factor leads to the high accuracy of 98.93% in the UCI-HAR dataset and 98.16% in PAMAP2 presented in Table 4. The classification performance of the proposed Dual-INABA model outperforms existing literature benchmarks, as depicted in Table 11. Notably, it surpasses the iSPLInception47 model by an impressive 9.16% in accuracy, and it exceeds the performance of the Inception-GRU-attention23 model by 7.85% in accuracy on the PAMAP2 dataset. The proposed Dual-INABA network shows overall improvement over existing models, except for a 0.42% decrease on WISDM compared to Multi-input CNN-GRU69.

Ablation experiments of the proposed Dual-INABA model are presented in Table 7. These experiments provided significant insights into the contributions of different components of the Dual-INABA model. Comparing the Dual-INABA with its model variants–Dual Inception-BiGRU, Dual Inception-Attention, and Dual Inception–reveals that the integration of the attention mechanism and BiGRU layers is able to improve the classification performance immensely, both in accuracy and loss rate. The removal of the BiGRU component from Dual-INABA results in an average accuracy reduction of 0.41% across four datasets. This reduction highlights the significance of the BiGRU’s bidirectional temporal feature extraction capability in classification. In contrast, eliminating the attention mechanism results in a 1.41% average accuracy reduction across the same datasets. Its contextual awareness, focus on relevant features, and ability to capture dependencies may significantly influence classification results. When both the BiGRU and attention mechanisms are removed from Dual-INABA, the average accuracy reduction is 1.69%. When any module is removed, the loss value changes more slowly and converges to a higher loss value compared to Dual-INABA as depicted in Fig. 13c, the accuracy also becomes unstable, with the final stable value decreasing in Fig. 13d. Additionally, the Dual-INABA model achieved a 2.51% average improvement in classification accuracy across the four datasets compared to the single-branch INABA, highlighting the advantages of a dual-branch architecture in effectively capturing complex patterns and enhancing model performance. As reported in Section 3, the discriminative frequency components of sensor-based HAR data are low-frequency components. As depicted in Fig. 13a, the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) performance of the Fourier transform (FT) is insufficient, leading to suboptimal recognition accuracy compared with the wavelet transform (WT) in Table 6. This result highlights the advantages of WT in providing localized multi-resolution time-frequency information, which is crucial for HAR. These distinctive frequency spectrum characteristics of three wavelets establish the foundation for the differences in the results presented in Table 6. In general, the Db WT gives the best results for both SNR and classification accuracy. This is due to the Db function’s alternating positive and negative shape and orthogonality. The spectrum of the Daubechies (Db) wavelet effectively preserves low-frequency components of the signal32, as illustrated in Fig. 3. Morlet WT exhibits lower performance than Db WT. However, on the UCI-HAR dataset, Morlet WT outperforms Db WT. The Mexican Hat WT exhibited the least favourable results among the three methods. This is due to Mexican Hat WT extracts attenuating low-frequency components37. The low frequency components serve as key discriminative features in HAR, which has also been verified by Yao et al.35. The integration of deep learning models and WT, which captures transient frequency features, significantly enhances the ability to identify critical patterns and achieves better results. The consistent improvements observed across various datasets by multiple benchmark models suggest that integrating wavelet processing could be a valuable strategy for optimizing model performance in this domain. The results of Table 8 and Table 9 indicate that multi-branch models outperform single-branch models such as CNN18, CNN-LSTM67, and LSTM-RNN68. This trend highlights the crucial role that model architecture plays in enhancing performance. Therefore, the structure of the neural network is pivotal in achieving superior accuracy and effectiveness in addressing the challenges of activity recognition tasks. Incorporating GRU and attention mechanisms within the Inception modules further enhances decoding accuracy, as evidenced by the performance of the Inception-GRU-Attention model. This model consistently outperforms the iSPInception modules across all datasets, regardless of whether the data is raw or processed using the Db WT. This fact is also validated by our ablation experiments in Table 7 and Fig. 13. Our proposed Dual-INABA, which features two branches and integrates BiGRU and attention mechanisms in each branch, demonstrates impressive decoding performance across all datasets. The synergy of the BiGRU and attention mechanisms allows for a more nuanced understanding of the data, contributing to its superior performance compared to other models. As demonstrated in Table 10 and Fig. 14, even though the Dual-INABA is a complex hybrid network with two branches and incorporates advanced components like BiGRU and attention, it maintains high efficiency. This is attributed to its robust feature extraction capabilities, dual-branch structure for parallel processing, and optimized training algorithms, which collectively enhance learning efficiency. Despite a longer inference time per epoch, it completes training in just 42 seconds, significantly faster than the 290 seconds required for the Multi-channel CNN-GRU and 1260 seconds for the Inception-GRU-Attention. The efficiency of the Dual-INABA model enhances its suitability for deployment on edge devices and mobile platforms. Overall, Dual-INABA proves to be a solution for activity recognition tasks, showcasing its potential for real-world applications. Although the proposed model has achieved remarkable results in activity recognition, there still some issues distinguishing between certain activities, such as walking upstairs and downstairs. In the future, we will integrate advanced techniques, such as Transformer models and Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs), to enhance the Dual-INABA model. Transformers are able to facilitate parallel processing of input data, significantly reducing training times and enhancing efficiency, which is crucial for real-time HAR applications. Their advanced contextual understanding is expected to allow for better differentiation between similar activities, while their inherent scalability should enable the model to adapt effectively to larger datasets and more complex activity types. Furthermore, the adversarial training framework of GANs is anticipated to promote the learning of robust feature representations, thereby improving the model’s ability to generalize across diverse populations and activity types. The integration of GANs is planned to enhance the training process by generating realistic synthetic examples, which can effectively address class imbalances and increase data diversity, particularly in scenarios with limited labeled data. We intend to evaluate the model across a broader range of datasets to assess its generalizability and robustness. Although the proposed model has high efficiency due to its parallel structure and optimized algorithm, it has not been tested in real-world applications. Furthermore, its generalizability across diverse populations merits attention, given that variations in activity patterns and population age can influence its accuracy. Addressing these issues is essential for enhancing the model’s practical applicability in real-time scenarios. We will expand our self-collected WSMC-HAR dataset by incorporating additional data from various sensors, including accelerometers, heart rate monitors, and angular velocity sensors. Our ultimate goal is to achieve real-time processing capabilities and enhance interpretability by ensuring clarity in decision-making.

Conclusion

This paper proposes a parallel hybrid model with an Inception module, BiGRU, and attention mechanisms named dual Inception-Attention-BiGRU-Attention (Dual-INABA). The proposed Dual-INABA model demonstrates notable enhancements in accuracy, achieving 96.95%, 98.89%, 98.15%, and 96.78% across the WSMC-HAR, UCI-HAR, PAMAP2, and WISDM datasets, respectively. We conducted ablation experiments to investigate the isolated contributions of each module within the Dual-INABA model and to assess the synergy effects arising from the interaction of multiple modules. Individually, the Dual Inception module demonstrates the highest classification accuracy, while the combination of Dual Inception and BiGRU exhibits the greatest performance in reducing loss rate. However, the collaboration of all three modules, as proposed in the Dual-INABA network, leads to robust performance, enhanced speed, and superior outcomes across both evaluation metrics. The proposed Dual-INABA model also demonstrates superior classification performance compared with popular models in current literature, with average accuracy improvements of 1.20%. Notably, it surpasses the iSPLInception model by an impressive 9.16% in accuracy and exceeds the performance of the Inception-GRU-attention model by 7.85% in accuracy. Integrating a signal-denoising method improves the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) of the collected data, leading to improved final classification results. In this study, the Daubechies (Db) wavelet has outperformed other wavelet methods as well as traditional Fourier Transform (FT). Additionally, we include the data collection experience and present a self-collected dataset termed WSMC-HAR, which has achieved quality comparable to commonly used benchmark datasets. Overall, this paper presents an activity classification pipeline in Human Activity Recognition (HAR) that enhances accuracy and efficiency by leveraging advanced methodologies to effectively tackle the challenges of recognizing diverse human activities across various environments.

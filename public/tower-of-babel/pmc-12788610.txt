Comparative Evaluation of Pushout Bond Strength of AH Plus Sealer After Using Two Gutta–Percha Solvents with and Without Ultrasonic Activation Following Endodontic Retreatment: An In Vitro Study
Authors: Aiswarya Gopinath, Rethi Gopakumar, Faisal M. A. Gaffoor, M Muhammed Bilal, Sheena S. Raj, L J Deepika Nair
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:An endodontic sealer creates a hermetic seal between the canal wall and core filling material which prevents regrowth of microorganism or apical or coronal leakage leading to reinfection. The most relevant prognostic factor for evaluation of the link between the sealer to the canal wall and the core material is push-out bond strength and it is considered an important property since it creates a fluid tight seal. It also provides resistance against dislocation of the root filling during tooth flexure and operative procedures.Materials and Methods:The materials used in the study are AH Plus sealer, Endosolv R, and Xylene. 90 mandibular premolars are obturated using an AH plus root canal sealer and retreatment is carried out using gutta percha solvents Endosolv R and Xylene. Specimens are grouped based on the used solvents and each group is subdivided based on irrigant activation technique. A 3-mm section of apical third is taken from the re-obturated specimens and push out bond strength is analyzed with universal testing machine.Results:Analyzing the pushout bond strength of AH plus sealer after retreatment with 2 gutta percha solvents, it is found that AH Plus sealer has got highest pushout bond strength after using Endosolv R with ultrasonic irrigant activation.Conclusion:Within the limitations of this study, it can be concluded that after 5 minutes, Endosolv-R was significantly more effective short-term gutta percha softener than xylene for the tested sealer-AH plus.

INTRODUCTION

Endodontic retreatment relates to a procedure that is performed on a tooth which has earlier received an attempt at a definitive treatment which has resulted in a condition requiring additional endodontic treatment so as to achieve a successful outcome. The endodontic failure may be broadly divided into two categories: technical and biological considerations. Problems including difficulties pertaining to the quality of original root canal treatment, which includes poor filling, insufficient removal of diseased pulp tissue or dentin debris, and also anomaly of shape of the canal are included under the technical considerations. Whereas the biological considerations include those cases with persistent or recurring disease caused by the bacterial survival or even the existence of untreated canals. Recurrent infection and eventual treatment failure can be caused by inadequate cleaning of the canal during the endodontic treatment which will cause a persistence of bacteria in the canal system.

Total removal of the GP is also critical during endodontic retreatment. This can be accomplished by thorough mechanical, chemical, chemical, or ultrasonic procedures. Every method has its own advantages and disadvantages which has to be carefully considered depending on root canal anatomy, the quality of the materials used during the initial treatment and also the skill of the clinician.

During retreatment GP removal becomes difficult whenever resin-based sealers are used. Hence various solvents have been used along with the mechanical method, to various avoid complications like altering of original shape of the canal, canal straightening, or perforations. Along with GP solvents, Nickel–titanium rotary files are having better efficacy in removing the obturating materials, in terms of maintaining the canal shape and providing a lesser working period. The Protaper Retreatment system (PTR) (Dentsply Maillefer Ballaigues; Switzerland) has been designed for the removal of the root canal filling material using its variable tapers, lengths, and also the tip diameters.

The hydrophobic property of Endosolv R and Xylene, enables them to break through the 3D lattice structure of epoxy resin-based sealers which are formed after their chemical reaction.

Ultrasonic tips have been in usage in the field of endodontics to improve root canal disinfection. Passive ultrasonic irrigation (PUI) has also been used for removing debris, root canal dressings and also the smear formed. However, not much of investigation has been don on the effectiveness of the removal of the filling material by the PUI. The motive of combining solvents with ultrasonic agitation (UA) was for endodontic instruments to reach difficult-to-access areas, thereby enhancing their effectiveness in line with the current irrigating protocols. The apical root canal, which is considered as a “critical zone” because of its strategic position for the microorganisms, still remains a challenge for several instrumentation techniques used and for the various irrigating/dressing proposals.

The rationale of the present study is that better retrieval of filling materials, through dissolution, can positively enhance the outcome of the would retreatment. This in-vitro study was designed to evaluate the effects of GP solvents Endosolv R and Xylene on the push out bond strength of resin sealer to the root dentin following retreatment with and also without ultrasonic activation.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

The present study was conducted in the Department of Conservative Dentistry and Endodontics, Noorul Islam College of Dental Sciences, Trivandrum. Ethical clearance (NIMS/IEC/2023/05/03) was obtained from the Institutional Ethical Committee before proceeding with the study. Push out bond strength was tested at SCTIMST. Thiruvananthapuram.

Sample preparation

Ninety extracted human mandibular first premolars were collected from department of oral surgery, NICDS Aralammoodu, Trivandrum. Tooth samples were decoronated at 15 mm. Access cavity preparation was done and working length was determined. Apical preparation was done till 20 k file. Biomechanical preparation was done with Protaper Universal files operated in Endomotor (Eighteeth-Extreme) up to F2.5.25% sodium hypochlorite was used as irrigant followed by 17% EDTA gel for smear layer removal. Protaper gutta percha cones and AH plus sealer obturation was done by single cone technique. Access cavity was filled with glass ionomer cement. Samples were wrapped in sponge and stored at 37°C in 100% humidity for 2 weeks to allow the proper sealer setting.

Teeth samples are divided into 3 groups (A, B, C) based on method of retreatment. They are again subdivided as subgroups[12] based on ultrasonic activation.

Retreatment of root canal filling

For group B, and C 1 mL of each solvent’s xylene and Endosolv R was applied in the pulp chamber accordingly and left for 5 min prior to instrumentation with Pro Taper. These groups were again divided in to A1, B1, C1 (ultrasonic irrigation) A2, B2, and C2 (normal irrigation, without ultrasonic activation). Then retreatment was done with Protaper retreatment files. In total, 5.25% sodium hypochlorite was used for canal irrigation. All samples were again reobturated with gutta percha.

Push out bond strength

All samples were vertically positioned in an acrylic block and a horizontal section of apical third of root of 3 mm thickness is taken with diamond disc. Then compressive loading was applied on specimens with a blunt stainless-steel plunger (0.5 mm in diameter) and cross head speed of 1 mm/min using a 500 N load cell till debonding occurred. The plunger was mounted on a universal testing machine and a computer software was used to record the data [Figure 1]. The maximum load applied is converted into Megapascal (MPa). Cones with AH plus sealer and kept for 2 weeks at 37°C in 100% humidity.

Pushout bond strength testing with 0.5 mm diameter plunger

The value detected is divided by the adhesion area of filling material.

Pushout bond strength (MPa) = Maximum load (N)/Adhesion area (mm2)

Adhesion area = 2Πrh

r = root canal radius in millimeter

h = root dentin thickness in millimeter (3 mm)

Statistical analysis

Categorical and quantitative variables are expressed as frequency (percentage) and mean ± SD, respectively. One way ANOVA test (F test) and Scheffe Multiple Comparisons (post hoc test) used to compare quantitative parameters among different cleansers.

P < 0.05 is considered as threshold for statistical significance.

Statistical analysis is performed by using a statistical software package SPSS, Version 20.0.

DISCUSSION

Orthograde retreatment techniques can be considered as the primary option for failed endodontic cases. The most important step responsible for the success of the retreatment is the complete removal of the obturating materials so that the chemical disinfectants and the intracanal medicaments reach the instrument inaccessible areas and enhance healing of the periapical pathology.[2] The success rate of such treatments ranges from 50% to 90%.[1] A physicochemical interaction across the interface is responsible for the adhesion between dental structures and resin-based sealers, producing an adhesion of the obturating material and canal wall. This property is inevitable in static and dynamic situations. This adhesion can eliminate spaces that may allow for fluid infiltration into the sealer/dentin interface in static conditions. In the case of dynamic condition this adhesion is inevitable to avoid dislodgment of the root canal sealer during the operative procedures.[34] Use of resin-based sealers makes the removal of the gutta percha difficult during retreatment and hence different GP solvents can be used along with the mechanical method, so as to avoid complications arising like altering of the canal, straightening of the canal, or even perforations. The efficacy of Endosolv R and Xylene in removing the canal filling material were analyzed in the present study. Endosolv R is a chlorinated hydrocarbon which has been commonly considered as a GP solvent, whereas Xylene which is a chlorinated hydrocarbon can also soften or even dissolve the sealers and thereby help in their mechanical removal. The POBS (push-out bond strength) test has been widely used to evaluate the adhesion strength of endodontic sealers to dentinal wall and core materials and also for the classification of the materials based on their bonding. The obturation methods used in the study were chosen based on the manufacturers ‘instructions so as to obtain optimal results. The purpose of combining solvents along with ultrasonic agitation (UA) was to increase the dissolution property of endodontic solvents on gutta-percha and, thereby can provide proper disinfection, which might ultimately enable a more successful endodontic retreatment.[5] This in-vitro study was designed with the purpose to evaluate the effects of gutta percha solvents Endosolv R and Xylene on the “push out-bond strength of AH plus sealer to the root dentin following retreatment with ultrasonic activation”

In this study which included a 5-min exposure to Xylene and EndoSolv R before obturation the bond-strength of AH Plus was significantly higher after exposure to EndoSolv R compared to xylene and PTR alone. This can be accounted due to the ability of the solvents to modify the chemical composition of the dentin surface thereby resulting in changes in its bond strength to sealer.[6] Nevertheless, when comparing the effect of the different solvents on the bond-strength of AH Plus, it may be emphasized that epoxy-resin sealers (AH Plus) bonded chemically to the collagen of dentin and, thus, EndoSolv R might not have produced a significant modification of the dentin collagen fibers, contrary to xylene and PTR alone. In this study, regarding the bond-strength of AH Plus after exposure of different solvents to root canal dentin, there was a significant difference between Endosolv R, Xylene on one side and PTR on the other side. These results were in accordance with the findings of Erdemir et al. and Palhais et al.[3789]

Due to hydrophobic property of Endosolv-R they have the capacity to break through the 3D lattice structure of epoxy resin-based sealers formed after the chemical reaction which will result in their softening thereby facilitating their removal by the scrubbing effect provided by the files. This has been proved earlier by Rosenberg E, Tamse A, et al. in their studies The results showed that root canal filling materials removed using PTR system without solvent recorded the second highest bond strength scores. This may be due to the ability of the rotary files to produce heat due to the torque and frictional forces produced which leads to the removal of softened GP and also its ability to remove large amounts of it through the instrument spirals, which provides both cutting and softening actions. Laxmi Narayan et al. in their studies have shown that Xylene can cause significant reduction in enamel and dentin microhardness thereby leading reduction in the bond strength of newer endodontic sealers resulting in showing the least strength in our study.[10]

CONCLUSION

Within the limitations of this study, it can be concluded that after 5 minutes, Endosolv-R was significantly more effective short-term gutta percha softener than xylene for the tested sealer –AH plus and thus Endosolv-R can be considered as a better solvent for softening and removing of epoxy resin-based sealer.

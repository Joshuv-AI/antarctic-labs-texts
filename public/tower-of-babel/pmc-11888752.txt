After Effects of Radiation on the Caries Status of Head and Neck Cancer Patients − A Six-Month Clinical Trial
Authors: Upasana Reddy, Mithra N. Hegde, Aditya Shetty, Mallikarjuna Rao Koduru, Neevan Dsouza
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:Head and neck cancer patients undergoing radiation therapy (RT) are at an increased risk of dental caries due to radiation-induced damage to salivary glands. This study aimed to compare the effectiveness of three interventions—virgin coconut oil (VCO), casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate tooth mousse (TM), and amine fluoride mouthwash (MW)—in preventing caries among head and neck cancer patients’ post-radiation therapy.Methods:A total of 102 head and neck cancer patients undergoing RT were enrolled. The participants were randomized to use one of three interventions: VCO, TM, or MW. Caries progression was assessed using the decayed-missing-filled-teeth (DMFT) index at baseline and after 6 months of RT. Differences in DMFT scores between the groups were analysed using statistical tests.Results:At the 6-month follow-up, significant differences in DMFT scores were observed between the VCO and MW groups (P = 0.012) and between the MW and TM groups (P = 0.001). The TM group showed a significant reduction in caries progression compared to both the VCO and MW groups (P = 0.001).Conclusions:Casein phosphopeptide-amorphous calcium phosphate (CPP-ACP) tooth mousse was found to be the most effective in preventing new caries and slowing the progression of existing carious lesions in head and neck cancer patients undergoing radiation therapy.

INTRODUCTION

Due to long-term morbidity from cancer radiotherapy, intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) spares healthy tissues. IMRT optimizes target conformance and tissue sparing. Virgin coconut oil (VCO), amine fluoride mouthwash, and CPP-ACP tooth cream were compared to prevent radiation-induced caries in IMRT-treated head and neck cancer patients. VCO may prevent radiation-induced mucositis and oral bacteria. Although popular, coconut oil pulling does not appear to aid radiation therapy. Radiation therapy for cancer can cause “radiation caries” by reducing saliva flow and changing composition, causing dry mouth and increased caries risk. Few studies have examined IMRT in oral cancer.[1] The study emphasizes complete dental health management in cancer care and the significance of anti-caries drugs in preventing radiation-induced caries. No PubMed or Scopus research examined baseline caries indices for head and neck oncology patients receiving or after IMRT radiation therapy.

Our study compared the mean values of two caries indices in three groups: a fluoride-containing mouthwash (Hs Code. 33061090), oil pulling with VCO, and a CPP-ACP-containing tooth cream (Hs Code. 0126F062-0000) among head and neck cancer patients in K.S.Hegde Hospital and Deralakatte at three intervals, immediately after radiation at the 6-month follow-up.

MATERIALS AND METHODOLOGY

Ethics and consent to participate

The present study was a parallel randomized clinical trial approved by the Nitte Institute Central Ethics Committee (NU/CEC/2019/0242) and registered with Clinical Trial Registry-India (CTRI) Ref No 2019/02/024181. The study obtained consent from 102 participants at K.S Hegde Hospital, Mangalore, who were diagnosed with stage I or II head and neck cancer. They received radiation therapy with IMRT alone and were not scheduled for chemotherapy or surgery. Exclusion criteria included advanced cancer stages, specific medical conditions, and allergies.

Statistical analysis

Pilot study results were used to estimate the research sample size using nMaster software version 2. Several parameters guided the calculations, including a pooled standard deviation of 5.11 and a mean difference of 4. The impact size was 0.782778, indicating the expected impact magnitude. A sample size of 34 participants per group was recommended to sustain a significance level of 5% and 80% statistical power.

Data collection and indices

Caries were evaluated using the decayed-missing-filled-teeth (DMFT) index.[2]

Irradiation technique

Patients underwent IMRT after biopsy confirmation. Hospital admission for 6 weeks was required, during which daily radiation fractions of 2-2.5 Gy were administered according to personalized treatment plans, completing the total dose for treatment.[3]

Preradiation evaluation

Patients’ case histories were recorded using a structured questionnaire adapted from the WHO Oral Health Questionnaire Form for Adults, 2013, ANNEX 7 from the Oral Health Surveys (5th edition) given by the World Health Organization (WHO).[4] The data were collected by a single trained examiner to ensure uniform interpretation and application of the criteria for the clinical assessment.[5]

DISCUSSION

Due to long-term morbidity from cancer radiotherapy, intensity-modulated radiation therapy (IMRT) spares healthy tissues. IMRT optimizes target conformance and tissue sparing. Virgin coconut oil, amine fluoride mouthwash, and CPP-ACP tooth cream were compared to prevent radiation-induced caries in IMRT-treated head and neck cancer patients. Virgin coconut oil may prevent radiation-induced mucositis and oral bacteria. Coconut oil pulling is popular but has little evidence of helping radiotherapy patients.[67]

Digestibility and energy source: Lauric acid, a medium-chain fatty acid in VCO, digests quickly and provides energy, possibly aiding weight loss. Many cultures use VCO for minor skin and gastrointestinal issues. It supplements oral hygiene and dental care. Examine oral health and treatment side effects before oil pulling with VCO. Dental exams and hygiene are essential during and after radiation therapy.[8]

The second agent in our investigation was a CPP-ACP with tooth mousse, which demineralizes enamel. Unique qualities and benefits make CPP-ACP, particularly tooth mousse for radiation caries, important. Remineralization builds enamel, cures early lesions, stabilizes oral pH, and avoids demineralization, particularly in dry mouth patients. Easy application—topical cream for daily oral care during and after RT. We discovered that all groups had anti-caries potential, although VCO did not improve caries prevention or treatment after radiation compared to MW or TM use, as measured by International caries detection and assessment system (ICDAS) scores. Resin CPP-ACP with fluoride can re-mineralize and preserve irradiated enamel, supporting Sim et al.[9] Fluoride strengthens teeth against acid attacks and inhibits demineralization, hence amine fluoride mouthwash was chosen to minimize radiation-induced caries in RT patients.[10] It also inhibits oral cavity enzymes, protecting enamel from acid-producing microbes.[11]

CONCLUSION

Our study can improve RT protocols by enabling the use of powerful anti-caries medicines, minimizing radiation caries, reducing dental procedures, improving patient management during radiation, and reducing long-term oral hygiene impacts. Preventing radiation-induced caries could help radiotherapy patients cope with discomfort.

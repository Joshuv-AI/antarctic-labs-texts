PNPLA3 polymorphisms and risk of hepatic and extrahepatic outcomes in MASLD: A meta-analysis of observational studies
Authors: Ciro Celsa, Grazia Pennisi, Adele Tulone, Giacinta Ciancimino, Marco Vaccaro, Fabiana Pecorella, Gabriele Di Maria, Marco Enea, Giovanni Targher, Luca Valenti, Stefano Romeo, Calogero Cammà, Salvatore Petta
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsThe rs738409C>G polymorphism in the patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing protein 3 gene (PNPLA3 I148M variant) contributes to the largest fraction of metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) heritability. However, its prognostic impact on long-term clinical outcomes related to MASLD remains incompletely characterized. We performed a meta-analysis of observational studies to quantify the impact of PNPLA3 polymorphisms on the risk of developing hepatic and extrahepatic outcomes in MASLD.MethodsWe systematically searched PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Central for observational studies evaluating PNPLA3 polymorphisms and clinical outcomes in MASLD. The primary outcome was liver-related events (LREs); secondary outcomes included new-onset hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), liver-related mortality, cardiovascular events, extrahepatic cancers, and all-cause mortality. We compared homozygous risk-allele carriers (GG) and heterozygous carriers (CG) with wild-type homozygous (CC) individuals using random-effects genotypic models. The study was registered on PROSPERO (CRD#420251176886).ResultsTwenty-one observational longitudinal studies, including 232,033 adult individuals with MASLD (median follow-up: 7.2 years), were analysed. Individuals with the GG genotype had significantly higher risks of incident LREs (hazard ratio [HR] 2.87, 95% CI 1.92–4.27) and HCC (HR 2.54, 95% CI 1.86-3.47) compared with the CC genotype. CG carriers also had an increased risk of LREs (HR 1.57, 95% CI 1.12–2.19), but not of HCC. PNPLA3 genotypes were not associated with the risk of major cardiovascular events, extrahepatic cancers, or all-cause mortality.ConclusionsThe PNPLA3 I148M polymorphism confers dose-dependent increases in LRE and HCC risk in MASLD, with liver-specific rather than systemic effects. These findings suggest that PNPLA3 genotyping may inform risk stratification strategies in MASLD, particularly for identifying individuals at higher risk of LREs and HCC who might benefit from closer monitoring.Impact and implicationsInternational consensus has prioritised patient education and awareness as a key research domain in MASLD, yet quantitative evidence from patients with an established diagnosis remains scarce, particularly in Europe. In 460 Italian adults with physician-diagnosed MASLD we found pervasive knowledge gaps - including the failure to recognise diabetes as a risk factor and the belief that normal transaminases exclude progressive disease - a wide gap between lifestyle counselling and actual adherence, largely self-prescribed supplement use, partly guideline-discordant pharmacotherapy, and educational attainment as the principal determinant of every cognitive domain. These results are relevant to hepatologists, primary care physicians, patient associations and policymakers, because such misconceptions may delay referral, weaken adherence, and hinder equitable access to newly available therapies such as resmetirom and incretin-based agents. The validated five-domain instrument provides a reproducible tool to profile patients, tailor education to health literacy level and monitor its effect; however, since participants were recruited through a patient association and reported data themselves without fibrosis staging, these estimates probably represent a best-case scenario of awareness and require confirmation in unselected clinical cohorts.

Introduction

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) has become the leading cause of chronic liver disease worldwide, affecting up to ∼30–35% of the general adult population.1,2 MASLD encompasses a spectrum of progressive liver conditions, ranging from isolated hepatic steatosis to metabolic dysfunction-associated steatohepatitis (MASH), cirrhosis, and hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).3 Despite its high global prevalence, MASLD exhibits considerable interindividual variability in disease progression, with only a subset of individuals developing advanced liver disease and related clinical complications.

The clinical heterogeneity of MASLD is largely attributed to a complex interplay of environmental, metabolic, and genetic factors.4,5 Among genetic determinants, the rs738409C>G single-nucleotide polymorphism in the patatin-like phospholipase domain-containing 3 (PNPLA3) gene, which encodes an isoleucine-to-methionine substitution at position 148 (I148M), has been consistently identified as the most robustly validated genetic risk factor for MASLD and its histological severity.6 First discovered through genome-wide association studies in 2008,7 the PNPLA3 I148M variant accounts for approximately 25% of the genetic variability in hepatic steatosis and has since been extensively validated across diverse ethnic populations.8 This genetic variant displays a dose-dependent effect, with homozygous carriers (GG genotype) having the highest risk of liver disease compared with heterozygous (CG genotype) and wild-type homozygous individuals (CC genotype).6,9

The impact of PNPLA3 I148M extends beyond isolated steatosis to influence the entire spectrum of liver disease, with carriers demonstrating significantly higher rates of progression to MASH and cirrhosis, and a markedly increased risk of HCC.[9], [10], [11], [12] In some studies, PNPLA3 I148M has also been associated with increased liver-related and overall mortality in the general population, particularly in individuals living with obesity and metabolic comorbidities.13 Beyond liver-related events, emerging but conflicting evidence suggests that PNPLA3 polymorphisms may also influence the development of extrahepatic clinical outcomes, such as cardiovascular events, type 2 diabetes, chronic kidney disease,[14], [15], [16] and certain extrahepatic cancers (principally gastrointestinal cancers).17

Some previously published meta-analyses have examined the association between PNPLA3 polymorphisms and the risk of hepatic and extrahepatic outcomes. However, most of these meta-analyses have either focused on cross-sectional associations with disease prevalence and severity6,10 or included mixed populations encompassing different etiologies of steatotic liver disease (SLD), such as alcohol-associated liver disease together with MASLD.18 Given the distinct pathophysiological mechanisms underlying MASLD compared to other forms of SLD, particularly alcohol-associated liver disease, and the potential for gene-environment interactions specific to metabolic dysfunction,19 there is an urgent need to specifically examine the impact of PNPLA3 polymorphisms on the long-term risk of liver-related and extrahepatic outcomes in the MASLD population.

Therefore, this systematic review and meta-analysis of observational longitudinal studies aimed to comprehensively evaluate the impact of PNPLA3 polymorphisms on the long-term risk of developing hepatic (liver-related events, HCC, and liver-related mortality) and extrahepatic (cardiovascular diseases, extrahepatic cancers, and all-cause mortality) outcomes in individuals living with MASLD. By focusing exclusively on individuals with MASLD and synthesizing data from longitudinal cohort studies across countries, we aimed to provide more precise risk estimates to inform clinical risk stratification and to contribute to the development of personalized medicine approaches for the management of MASLD.

Materials and methods

Protocol registration

The meta-analysis was performed using the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines20 (Table S1). The meta-analysis protocol was registered in advance in the PROSPERO database (CRD420251176886).

Data sources and searches

Studies were identified through a systematic literature search using three large electronic databases (PubMed, Scopus, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials), with search strings detailed in the supplementary information. The systematic search included reviews, meta-analyses, clinical trials, and observational cohort studies published from the database's inception until September 2025. The computer search was supplemented by manual searches of the reference lists of the retrieved reviews and studies to identify additional studies. Finally, a hand cross-reference check of the retrieved studies was performed to identify duplicate articles.

Three authors (A.T., G.C., and F.P.) independently conducted the literature search and evaluated each study for eligibility. To ensure consistency and reliability in selecting studies for the meta-analysis, any discrepancies in assessing study eligibility were resolved through discussion among the three authors. In the case of persistent disagreement, a fourth author (Ci.C.) was involved to reach a consensus.

Study selection

Studies were included in the meta-analysis if they: 1) were observational longitudinal (cohort or case-control) studies involving adult individuals (>18 years old) living with MASLD; 2) reported genotype data for PNPLA3 rs738409 polymorphism, distinguishing between homozygous risk allele carriers (GG genotype), heterozygous carriers (CG genotype), and wild-type homozygous individuals (CC genotype); 3) reported data on incidence rates of liver-related outcomes, cardiovascular-related events, extrahepatic cancer-related events, or all-cause mortality according to PNPLA3 genotype; and 4) incorporated appropriate statistical adjustments for known risk factors and potential confounding factors (primarily through multivariable regression analysis, propensity score matching, inverse probability of treatment weighting, or other validated statistical methods for confounding control).

Studies were excluded from the meta-analysis if they: 1) failed to report clinical outcomes according to PNPLA3 genotype stratification; 2) did not provide sufficient data to extract effect measures for the association between PNPLA3 genotype and outcomes of interest; 3) included mixed populations with SLD of different aetiologies (e.g. alcohol-associated liver disease, viral hepatitis, or other causes) without separate analysis for MASLD; 4) consisted of case reports, or case series; 5) were written in a language other than English; and 6) reported only cross-sectional associations with disease prevalence or severity without longitudinal outcome data.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Data from the study and patient-level variables were gathered from each eligible study and entered into a structured electronic database. Three authors (A.T., G.C., and F.P.) independently extracted data from each eligible study, including study-level covariates (i.e. first author, publication year, study design, study population, geographical region, ethnicity, MASLD diagnostic criteria, confounding adjustment methods, sample size, and follow-up duration) and patient-level covariates, such as age, sex distribution, BMI, and prevalence of type 2 diabetes. The number of individuals carrying CC, CG, and GG PNPLA3 genotypes was recorded for each eligible study. Differences in the definition of liver-related events, cardiovascular events, and extrahepatic cancer-related outcomes across the eligible studies were documented and detailed in the supplementary information. Any discrepancies were resolved through consensus or consulting a fourth independent author (Ci.C.).

All eligible studies were assessed for quality using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS). The NOS tool evaluates study quality across three main domains, including selection of study groups, comparability of groups, and assessment of outcomes. Based on the combined evaluation of these domains, studies were categorized as having high (NOS score ≥8), moderate (NOS score 6–7), or low quality (NOS score ≤5).

Data synthesis and analysis

The primary outcome of interest was the occurrence of liver-related events (as originally defined by the included studies or as new-onset HCC, hepatic decompensation events, or liver-related mortality). Secondary outcomes of interest included the development of HCC, liver-related mortality, cardiovascular events (as originally defined by the included studies or cardiovascular-related death), incidence of extrahepatic cancers, and all-cause mortality. The specific definitions of both liver-related events and cardiovascular events used in each included study are detailed in Table S2.

Hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% CIs were extracted as effect measures comparing homozygous PNPLA3 risk allele carriers (GG genotype) vs. wild-type homozygous individuals (CC genotype), and heterozygous carriers (CG genotype) vs. wild-type homozygous individuals (CC genotype), for each study outcome. Pooled estimates were derived using a random-effects model with the generic inverse variance method. The restricted maximum likelihood method was applied to assess between-study variance. The degree of between-study heterogeneity was evaluated using the I2 statistic.

A leave-one-out sensitivity analysis was performed by iteratively excluding one study at a time to assess the robustness of the pooled estimates and to identify any individual studies exerting a disproportionate influence on the overall results. Moreover, a sensitivity analysis including only cohort studies was performed.

Subgroup analyses were performed according to study design (prospective vs. retrospective), geographical region (Western vs. Eastern countries), and MASLD diagnostic criteria (invasive vs. non-invasive methods).

Univariable meta-regression analyses were conducted to assess the influence of specific study-level (study design, geographical region, MASLD diagnostic criteria) and patient-level covariates (age, sex distribution, ethnicity, BMI, and diabetes prevalence) on the outcomes of interest, and to explore potential sources of heterogeneity in the meta-analysis. For outcomes where multiple covariates were statistically significant (p <0.05) in univariable meta-regression analyses, a multivariable meta-regression model was constructed.

Funnel plots were used to evaluate asymmetry and potential publication bias for each meta-analysis outcome, and the Egger test was used to quantitatively assess publication bias.

For all statistical analyses, a p value <0.05 was considered statistically significant. R Core Team: a language and environment for statistical computing (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria), with the packages meta and dmetar, was used to obtain all analyses and graphics.

Characteristics of included studies.

Continuous variables are reported as mean ± SD.

ALT, alanine aminotransferase. CAP, controlled attenuation parameter. FLI, fatty liver index. HCC, hepatocellular carcinoma.

All eligible studies were cohort studies, except for two studies, which were case-control studies. Seven studies had a retrospective design, and 14 had a prospective design. Four studies involved participants from Asian countries (Japan and China), 16 from Western countries (Europe, the USA, and Australia), and one from Brazil. Most studies diagnosed MASLD using imaging methods, including ultrasound, radiological imaging, or controlled attenuation parameter (n = 10). In contrast, the remaining studies used liver biopsy (n = 8), non-invasive scores or biomarkers (n = 4), or ICD codes (n = 1).

In the study by Chen et al.,33 data were extracted separately for the Michigan Genomics Initiative and UK Biobank cohorts.

The definitions of liver-related events and cardiovascular events used in each included study were consistent and are detailed in Table S2.

Study quality assessment

Overall, 19 studies were rated as high quality (defined by a NOS score of ≥8), two studies were rated as moderate quality (NOS score of 6–7), while no studies were rated as low quality (NOS score ≤5) (Table S3). The risk of bias among the included studies was primarily found in the selection domain, particularly regarding the representativeness of the study (13 studies) and the adequacy of the follow-up duration (3 studies). All studies demonstrated adequate comparability and adjustment for potential confounding factors.

Meta-regression analyses

Univariable meta-regression analyses for GG vs. CC genotype and for CG vs. CC genotype, respectively, are reported in Tables S4 and S5. In GG vs. CC genotype analysis, male sex was associated with a lower risk of liver-related events, and Asian ethnicity was associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular events. Conversely, in CG vs. CC genotype analysis, male sex, Western geographical region, and non-invasive MASLD diagnosis were associated with a lower risk of liver-related events; age was inversely related to the risk of HCC; age was inversely associated with the risk of cardiovascular events; BMI was inversely associated with the risk of all-cause mortality. Multivariable meta-regression analysis for liver-related events in CG vs. CC analysis did not show any significant association (data not shown).

Publication bias

The funnel plots for the outcomes of interest are reported in Figs. S50–S61.

The Egger's regression test indicated no asymmetry for most of the study outcomes (p >0.05), except for liver-related events in the analysis of GG vs. CC genotype (p = 0.02) and for all-cause mortality (p = 0.04) in the CG vs. CC analysis (Table S6).

Discussion

To our knowledge, this is the first comprehensive and updated meta-analysis specifically evaluating the impact of the PNPLA3 I148M polymorphism on the long-term risk of developing hepatic and extrahepatic outcomes in individuals with MASLD. Our analysis, which included 21 observational longitudinal studies (19 cohort and 2 case-control studies) with aggregated data on 232,033 adult individuals from different countries followed for a median of 7.2 years, shows that the PNPLA3 I148M variant confers a substantial, dose-dependent increase in the risk of incident liver-related events and HCC. Homozygous carriers of the PNPLA3 risk allele (GG genotype) had a nearly 3-fold increased risk of liver-related events and a 2.5-fold increased risk of new-onset HCC compared with wild-type homozygous individuals (CC genotype). Importantly, even heterozygous carriers (CG genotype) demonstrated a significantly increased risk of liver-related events. In contrast, PNPLA3 polymorphisms were not associated with the risk of developing cardiovascular events, extrahepatic cancers, or all-cause mortality, indicating liver-specific pathogenic effects of this genetic variant.

The marked increase in liver-related events associated with PNPLA3 I148M aligns with mechanistic studies showing that this genetic variant impairs intrahepatic lipid droplet remodelling and causes accumulation of mutant protein on hepatocyte lipid droplets.7 The observed dose-dependent effect, with GG carriers showing substantially higher risk than CG carriers, supports the loss-of-function nature of this genetic variant and demonstrates a gene-dosage relationship. Extending previous cross-sectional studies linking PNPLA3 variants to histological severity of MASLD9,40 and prospective studies showing that PNPLA3 genotype significantly predicts disease progression and clinical events in MASLD,11 our meta-analysis of longitudinal studies provides further strong evidence that PNPLA3 polymorphism significantly affects liver-related clinical outcomes over time in people with MASLD. These findings suggest that PNPLA3 genotyping could contribute to risk stratification in MASLD and help identify individuals at higher risk of disease progression. However, further studies are needed to establish optimal implementation strategies and validate the clinical utility of routine genetic testing in MASLD management.

The substantial increase in the risk of new-onset HCC observed among homozygous GG carriers is particularly relevant and consistent with previously published studies that established PNPLA3 I148M as one of the strongest genetic risk factors for HCC development in MASLD.21 The non-significant increase in HCC risk among heterozygous carriers may support a threshold effect for hepatocarcinogenesis requiring homozygosity, distinguishing HCC development from other liver-related events, where even the CG genotype confers increased risk. The low between-study heterogeneity we observed among the included studies further strengthens confidence in this finding across diverse populations. Collectively, therefore, these results strongly support the implementation of genotype-informed HCC surveillance strategies, particularly for GG carriers who may warrant more intensive screening protocols.

Our analysis provided non-significant results for liver-related mortality with wide confidence intervals and high between-study heterogeneity, likely reflecting both the limited number of studies and the competing risks of non-hepatic mortality. The point estimate, while not statistically significant, suggests a potentially clinically meaningful effect warranting investigation in larger studies with adequately long follow-ups to capture liver-specific deaths.

An interesting observation from our meta-analysis is the apparent supra-additive effect of the PNPLA3 I148M variant, with homozygous GG carriers showing disproportionately larger risk elevations than heterozygous CG carriers across liver-related outcomes. This non-linear dose-response relationship suggests that PNPLA3 I148M may operate through a mixed recessive-dominant inheritance model, consistent with previous studies showing disproportionately larger effects in homozygotes for both histological severity and HCC risk.9,21,29 These observations support the concept that homozygous GG carriers represent a particularly high-risk subgroup warranting differentiated clinical management approaches.

The absence of a significant association between PNPLA3 polymorphisms and the risk of cardiovascular events is noteworthy given the well-established increased cardiovascular risk among individuals living with MASLD.41,42 Several factors may explain this dissociation. First, while PNPLA3 risk allele carriers exhibit paradoxically lower circulating triglyceride and non-HDL cholesterol levels despite increased hepatic fat content,15 the magnitude of these lipid-lowering effects is extremely small in genome-wide association studies,43 and it could not meaningfully counteract the established cardiovascular risk associated with MASLD, thus explaining the lack of association between PNPLA3 I148M and cardiovascular endpoints. Second, unlike other genetic variants with pleiotropic metabolic effects, PNPLA3 I148M appears to exert significant liver-specific effects without substantially impacting systemic metabolic dysfunction.44 Moreover, our finding that Asian ethnicity was associated with a lower cardiovascular risk among GG carriers suggests important population-specific modifiers requiring further investigation. These results indicate that cardiovascular risk stratification in MASLD should focus on traditional cardiometabolic risk factors and other genetic factors,45 rather than on PNPLA3 genotyping.

Similarly, we failed to find significant associations between PNPLA3 polymorphisms and the risk of extrahepatic cancer and all-cause mortality. While MASLD has been linked to increased extrahepatic cancer risk,46 our findings suggest that this association is principally mediated by shared metabolic risk factors rather than hepatic fat accumulation per se. The modest, non-significant trend toward increased all-cause mortality among GG carriers is attenuated by the dominant contribution of cardiovascular and cancer deaths to all-cause mortality in MASLD, which are not influenced by PNPLA3 genotype. Studies with longer follow-up durations in populations enriched for advanced liver disease could reveal more substantial mortality effects.

Our meta-regression analyses revealed that male sex was associated with a lower risk of liver-related events in both GG and CG carriers, consistent with recent mechanistic studies demonstrating a multiplicative interaction between female sex and PNPLA3 I148M in determining liver disease severity.47,48 Our findings provide epidemiological confirmation at the population level of these molecular observations and suggest that female patients with MASLD carrying PNPLA3 risk alleles may warrant more intensive surveillance and earlier therapeutic intervention, particularly after menopause. The inverse associations we observed between age and HCC risk and between BMI and mortality risk among CG carriers likely reflect survivor bias and reverse causation rather than a true biological effect. The absence of significant interactions between PNPLA3 and metabolic factors (BMI, diabetes) contrasts with previous studies suggesting synergistic effects.49 This likely reflects our restriction to individuals with established MASLD, where metabolic dysfunction is already highly prevalent, limiting the possibility to identify interactions between genetics and metabolic comorbidities. In individuals without liver disease, PNPLA3 risk alleles and metabolic factors may synergistically increase the risk of MASLD development, whereas among individuals with established MASLD, genetic effects may predominate. Unfortunately, a sensitivity analysis excluding patients with cirrhosis to examine interactions in earlier disease stages was unfeasible, as most studies lacked detailed stage stratification.

Regarding clinical implementation of PNPLA3 genotyping, several considerations should be addressed. The added value of genetic testing beyond established clinical risk factors (fibrosis stage, metabolic parameters) remains to be demonstrated in prospective validation studies, and cost-effectiveness analyses are needed. Practical algorithms integrating PNPLA3 genotype with existing risk stratification tools have not been developed or validated. PNPLA3 genotyping might be most informative in research settings to stratify participants in clinical trials of anti-fibrotic or anti-steatotic therapies or for refining HCC surveillance in patients with advanced fibrosis, where the threshold effect in homozygous GG carriers is most pronounced. However, routine genetic testing cannot be recommended until prospective studies demonstrate clinical utility and cost-effectiveness in well-defined patient populations.

This meta-analysis has important strengths, including being the first to specifically focus on the MASLD population (excluding mixed SLD aetiologies), a large sample size exceeding 232,000 adult individuals from different countries, and a comprehensive assessment of hepatic and extrahepatic clinical outcomes, with separate analyses of homozygous and heterozygous PNPLA3 genotypes demonstrating dose-dependency. However, some important limitations warrant consideration. Substantial heterogeneity existed in MASLD definitions and outcomes across the eligible studies, with most using non-invasive tests rather than histological confirmation. The limited number of studies for some clinical outcomes reduced statistical power. The high between-study heterogeneity for liver-related mortality and publication bias detected for liver-related events in GG carriers may suggest incomplete capture of available evidence. Most studies did not employ competing-risk analyses, potentially leading to overestimation of absolute risk differences. Another key limitation is our inability to determine whether the increased HCC risk in PNPLA3 GG carriers is mainly influenced by the presence of cirrhosis or occurs independently of fibrosis stage. This distinction is clinically relevant, given that PNPLA3-driven HCC in the absence of cirrhosis would support extending surveillance to selected patients without cirrhosis. However, included studies did not consistently report baseline fibrosis stage or HCC stratified by cirrhosis status. Moreover, it should be also highlighted that the robustness of the HCC analysis is limited by the relatively small number of included studies. Finally, along the same lines, a major limitation is the inability to assess PNPLA3 impact across different MASLD stages (steatosis, MASH, fibrosis, cirrhosis). The biological and clinical effects of PNPLA3 vary across disease stages, but included studies used heterogeneous MASLD definitions (imaging, biomarkers, histology) and rarely reported outcomes stratified by baseline stage. Future individual patient data meta-analyses of studies with standardized staging and stage-specific outcome reporting are needed to clarify whether PNPLA3 genotyping can identify non-cirrhotic individuals requiring HCC surveillance and to develop evidence-based algorithms incorporating genetic risk stratification.

In conclusion, this comprehensive meta-analysis of observational longitudinal studies shows that the PNPLA3 I148M polymorphism significantly increases the long-term risk of liver-related events and HCC in individuals living with MASLD, with clear dose-dependent effects, while not influencing rates of cardiovascular events, extrahepatic cancers, or all-cause mortality. These findings suggest that PNPLA3 genotyping could potentially inform risk-stratification strategies in MASLD, particularly for identifying homozygous GG carriers who may be at higher risk of liver-related events and HCC development. As therapeutic trials targeting PNPLA3 with small-interfering RNA and antisense oligonucleotides continue to show promise,5 future research should evaluate whether genetic screening can effectively contribute to precision medicine approaches in MASLD management and whether genotype-based risk stratification improves clinical outcomes.

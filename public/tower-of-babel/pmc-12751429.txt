Prevalence and determinants of medication adherence among women with hypertension attending urban primary health care facilities in Inezgane, Morocco: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Fatima ES-SABIR, Aicha LEHIANY, Hayat IZIKI, Abdelilah MIFTAH, Soufiane RGUBI, Abderazzak ABAY, Rachid RAZINE, Lahcen BELYAMANI, Majdouline OBTEL
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionCardiovascular diseases remain the leading cause of death globally, with hypertension representing a major modifiable risk factor. Medication adherence persists as a major challenge in hypertension management, particularly among women, who often face unique barriers to adherence. This study aimed to estimate the prevalence of antihypertensive medication adherence and to identify its associated factors among Moroccan women with hypertension.MethodsA cross-sectional study was conducted in January 2025 among women attending three urban primary health care facilities in Inezgane, Morocco. The 8-item Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8) was used to assess medication adherence. Descriptive statistics summarized participants’ characteristics, and logistic regression analyses were performed to identify adherence-related factors.ResultsAmong 364 hypertensive Moroccan women with a median age of 58 years (IQR: 48–65), 53.3% were non-adherent to their antihypertensive treatment. Multivariable analyses revealed that having health insurance (AOR 1.87; CI 1.19–2.95; p = 0.007) and participation in therapeutic education sessions (AOR 2.21; CI 1.27–3.83; p = 0.005) were associated with improved adherence. Conversely, polytherapy (AOR 0.14; CI 0.06–0.30; p < 0.001) and multiple daily doses (AOR 0.35; CI 0.20–0.61; p < 0.001) were associated with lower adherence.ConclusionMore than half of the population of this study exhibited low adherence to antihypertensive medication. Multifaceted interventions, including enhanced patient education, simplified drug protocols, and improved healthcare accessibility, are required to improve adherence among Moroccan hypertensive women.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12905-025-04165-5.Keywords: Hypertension, Medication adherence, Women, Morisky medication adherence scale, Antihypertensive therapy

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease (CVD) is the leading cause of death worldwide. For many years, it was predominantly perceived as a male-related condition, a misconception largely stemming from the historical underrepresentation of women in clinical research and the insufficient consideration of sex-specific risk factors. Consequently, a significant gap persists in the understanding and management of CVD among women, even though it is the principal cause of mortality in both sexes globally [1]. While cardiovascular risk factors are common to both sexes, hypertension gives a higher risk of major cardiovascular events and mortality in women than in men [2].

It is well documented that women generally develop CVD approximately ten years later than men due to estrogen’s protective effects, which decline during menopause [3]. In general, studies suggests that women are less successful at controlling their blood pressure than men, despite similar rates of antihypertensive medication use [4]. This disparity may be related to suboptimal adherence to antihypertensive drugs, which may lead to increased cardiovascular morbidity and mortality [5].

With sociodemographic and economic transitions, Morocco is also undergoing an epidemiological transition, facing the burden of hypertension that affects approximately three in ten Moroccan women (29.8%; rural: 32.7%, urban: 28.3%) [6]. Among these women, 20.5% continue to have high blood pressure despite taking treatment, a proportion significantly higher than that observed in men (10.4%). Uncontrolled hypertension may lead to serious complications such as coronary artery disease, stroke, nephropathy, vision loss, and peripheral artery disease. Ensuring medication adherence is therefore critical to achieving adequate blood pressure control and preventing associated complications.

While several studies have investigated antihypertensive medication adherence in various international settings, including Saudi Arabia [7], India [8], and Brazil [9], gender-specific analyses remain limited. In Morocco, previous research has primarily included mixed samples of men and women and therefore did not specifically explored the determinants of adherence among hypertensive women. This population is distinct due to cultural, economic, and systemic differences that shape hypertension management. Accordingly, the present study aims to address this gap by assessing adherence rates and their associated factors among hypertensive women in Morocco, thereby providing essential evidence to inform targeted interventions.

Methods

Study site and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted in January 2025 in three urban primary health care facilities located in the prefecture of Inezgane-Aït Melloul, Morocco, which has a total population of 684,239 inhabitants. During the study period, women aged 18 years or older with a confirmed diagnosis of hypertension for at least six months and who were taking at least one antihypertensive medication were included. Pregnant women and patients with cognitive impairment were excluded. Cognitive impairment was identified based on the attending physician’s assessment and medical records, excluding participants who exhibited clear signs of memory loss, disorientation, or inability to understand and respond appropriately to the questionnaire.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated assuming a control rate of 62% [10], an allowable error of 5%, and a 95% confidence level, resulting in a required sample of 364 participants. The formula used was N = z² p (1 – p)/E ², where N represents the sample size, z is the z-score corresponding to the desired confidence level, p is the estimated proportion of the attribute in the population, and E is the desired level of precision.

Study procedure

A total of 505 patients who met the inclusion criteria were approached and invited to participate during routine medical visits in January 2025. A convenience sampling method was applied to recruit eligible hypertensive women attending selected primary health care facilities during the study period until the required sample size was reached. Recruitment continued until 364 patients agreed to participate, with a response rate of 72%. Among the 505 eligible women approached, 141 (27.9%) declined to participate, mainly due to lack of time (60%), unwillingness to answer personal health questions (25%), or lack of interest in research (15%). All eligible participants were informed that participation was voluntary and signed an informed consent form. They were also informed that they have the right to withdraw at any time, their collected data will only be used for research purposes.

Study instruments

The questionnaire used in this study was specifically developed to collect data on the variables of interest. To ensure its clarity, relevance, and comprehensibility for participants, it was pretested on a group of 15 women who were not included in the final study sample. This pretest allowed for adjustments in wording before its administration to the main study participants. The questionnaire is available as a supplementary file (English version) for reference.

The questionnaire consists of four main sections. The first section assessed participants’ socio-demographic characteristics, including age, marital status, educational level, and type of health insurance (Assurance Maladie Obligatoire AMO or AMO-Solidarity). The second section explored the factors influencing medication adherence, such as comorbidities, family history of hypertension, time since diagnosis (≤ 10 years and > 10 years), number of medications, and number of daily doses. Information on the specific classes of antihypertensive drugs was initially sought; however, these data were not consistently available in medical records, and many participants could not accurately identify their medications. Therefore, this variable was excluded from the analysis to avoid misclassification bias.

Medication adherence was assessed using the validated Arabic version of the 8- items Morisky Medication Adherence scale (MMAS-8) [11, 12]. This scale consists of eight items: the first seven are answered with yes or no, while the eighth item uses a five-point Likert scale. The overall score ranges from 0 to 8, with higher scores indicating better adherence. A total score < 6 is considered as a low adherence, a score of ≥ 6 but < 8 indicates a moderate adherence, and a score of 8 indicates a high adherence​. The reliability of the Arabic version of the MMAS-8 has been demonstrated [11], with a good internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.70).

Data analysis

Categorical variables were described using frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were summarized with median and interquartile range (IQR) values. The chi-square test was used to examine the association between demographic, socioeconomic, and clinical factors and medication adherence. Logistic regression analyses were conducted to identify factors associated with non-adherence. Variables with a p-value < 0.25 in the univariable logistic regression were included in the multivariable model. A backward stepwise selection method was used to obtain the most parsimonious model, retaining only statistically significant variables. Crude and adjusted odds ratios with a 95% confidence intervals were calculated, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using the Jamovi software (version 2.3, Jamovi Project, 2023).

Results

A total of 364 outpatients with hypertension participated in this study. As shown in Table 1, approximately 32.4% of the participants were between 54 and 65 years of age. The median age was 58 years (IQR: 48–65). The majority of participants were married (62.6%), and nearly half (48.4%) had no formal education. Most participants were covered by AMO health insurance (60.4%), and 63.2% had at least one comorbidity other than hypertension. The median duration of hypertension was 6 years (IQR: 3–10). A family history of hypertension was reported by 72%.

The majority of patients had a low adherence level (53.3%), 29.7% showed moderate adherence, and only 17.0% demonstrated high medication adherence. For the purposes of comparison and further analysis, participants with high or moderate adherence were combined into a single adherent category (46.7%), while those with low adherence were classified as non-adherent (53.3%).

The bivariate analysis revealed that having AMO health insurance (p = 0.001), the number of prescribed medications (p < 0.001), and attending an education session (p = 0.029) were significantly associated with medication adherence. No significant associations were observed between age, marital status, educational level, comorbidities, time since diagnosis, frequency of drug administration, and medication adherence (Table 1).

The multivariable logistic regression analysis identified several factors associated with medication adherence (Table 2). Patients covered by AMO health insurance were approximately 1.87 times more likely to be adherent compared to those covered by AMO-Solidarity (AOR = 1.87; 95% CI 1.19–2.95; p = 0.007). Similarly, participation in educational sessions about the disease and its treatment was significantly associated with higher adherence (AOR = 2.21; 95% CI: 1.27–3.83; p = 0.005).

Conversely, polytherapy was negatively associated with adherence, as patients taking multiple medications had a significantly lower likelihood of adherence (AOR = 0.14; 95% CI: 0.06–0.30; p < 0.001). Similarly, patients who took multiple doses per day were significantly less likely to adhere to their treatment regimen than those taking a single daily dose (AOR = 0.35; 95% CI: 0.20–0.61; p < 0.001).

To test the findings, a multinomial logistic regression was also employed with three levels of adherence (low, moderate, and high). The results remained consistent with those of the binary logistic model. Health insurance coverage and participation in therapeutic education sessions were positively associated with adherence, whereas polytherapy and multiple doses per day were negatively associated. The magnitude of associations was stronger for women with high adherence levels, confirming the robustness of the main model.

Discussion

Adherence to hypertension medication is essential for the effective management of this chronic condition. In the present study, using the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale, the majority of patients demonstrated a low level adherence (53.3%), while only 17.0% achieved high adherence. In Morocco, two other studies assessing adherence among hypertensive women using the Girerd self-assessment test reported non-adherence rates of 82.5% in Marrakech [13] and 89.8% in Meknes [14].

Our findings were consistent with the global non-adherence rate of 53.9% published in a meta-analysis including 25 studies using the MMAS-8 [15]. Comparable results have also been observed in studies among hypertensive women in Saudi Arabia (62%) [7], India (44.4%) [8], and Peru (41.2%) [16]. Conversely, some studies have reported lower rates of poor medication adherence, such as 13.8% and 22.3% in Brazil [9, 17]. The differences in adherence rates between studies is multifactorial, including methodological heterogeneity, demographic and socio-economic characteristics, health literacy levels, types of interventions, and cultural contextual variations.

The association between sociodemographic factors and antihypertensive medication adherence has been examined in several studies. A study conducted in Brazil reported an inverse relationship between age and non-adherence, suggesting that adherence tends to improve with increasing age [9]. However, our findings did not show any significant association between age and adherence. In the same way, No statistically significant association was observed between marital status and adherence, which is consistent with findings from Saudi Arabia [7]. Furthermore, our study did not identify association between comorbidities and medication non-adherence, a similar finding was also reported in a study by Sharma et al. [18]. Finally, unlike some previous studies [8, 18], we did not find an association between the duration since diagnosis and medication adherence.

In the present study, women covered by the AMO health insurance scheme were significantly more likely to adhere to their prescribed medication regimen compared to those with AMO-Solidarity. This finding highlights disparities in adherence despite both groups having health coverage, suggesting that the observed difference may be partly explained by underlying socio-economic inequalities between AMO and AMO-Solidarity beneficiaries rather than by the type of insurance itself. While AMO covers salaried and self-employed individuals, AMO-Solidarity primarily targets low-income populations who often face additional challenges such as unstable income, transportation costs, and limited access to pharmacies and healthcare facilities. These structural barriers, together with lower levels of health literacy [19], may contribute to reduced adherence independently of the insurance coverage characteristics.

Our study showed that polytherapy was negatively associated with adherence, which is consistent with previous studies conducted in Saudi Arabia [7] and Iran [20]. It is widely recognized that prescribing the lowest possible number of medications is more convenient and helps reduce the risk of adverse effects. Additionally, our results indicated that an increased frequency of medication intake was associated with lower adherence. Considering these findings, treatment plans should aim to simplify medication regimens by reducing the number of daily doses whenever possible, as demonstrated in a pooled analysis of 22 million patients in the United States [21].

Additionally, therapeutic education has been associated with improved treatment adherence among hypertensive patients, as reported by numerous studies. Indeed, educational interventions have demonstrated efficacy in enhancing patients’ knowledge about hypertension and improving adherence to antihypertensive medication [22]. In Morocco, hypertensive patients receive therapeutic education from healthcare professionals covering the disease and its complications, with guidance on balanced diet, regular physical activity, and stress management. They also receive guidance on medication adherence [23]. However, in our study, the observed association between participation in educational sessions and medication adherence should be interpreted with caution. It is possible that more health-conscious or motivated patients were inherently more likely to attend such sessions, introducing a self-selection bias.

Nevertheless, it must be acknowledged that educational interventions alone are unlikely to promote sustained behavioral change. In the randomized controlled trial conducted by Khadoura et al. (2021) [24], all participants received standard hypertension care that included routine educational sessions. However, only those who additionally participated in motivational interviewing showed a significant improvement in medication adherence. This highlights that adherence is not only a matter of knowledge, but also depends of motivation, self-efficacy, and psychosocial context. Therefore, combining educational programs with motivational and behavioral strategies could produce more lasting improvements in adherence.

In this context, digital health interventions such as telemedicine and mobile health applications offer new opportunities to integrate behavioral approaches into daily care. The use of telemonitoring systems and wearable devices allows continuous blood pressure tracking, medication reminders, and remote interaction with healthcare providers, thereby supporting both education and motivation.

Evidence from a randomized controlled trial testing the European Society of Hypertension (ESH) CARE App showed the effectiveness of mobile-based hypertension management. After six months of follow-up, 78.2% of patients using the ESH CARE App achieved ambulatory blood pressure targets, compared to 50% in the usual care group (p = 0.002) [25].

Morocco has established a regulatory framework to guide the practice of telemedicine, aiming to address major healthcare system challenges such as physician shortages and limited access to remote areas. The National Telemedicine Initiative, launched in 2018, targets the coverage of 80% of medical deserts by 2025, with a particular focus on regions with low medical density [26].

Despite this progress, multiple obstacles persist, such as ensuring equitable access to telemedicine services, protecting patient information and privacy, and resolving technical issues [27]. These issues must be resolved to maximize the benefits of telemedicine and achieve the objectives set by the Moroccan health system. Therefore, the development of culturally adapted and affordable telemonitoring solutions could represent a promising direction to strengthen adherence and hypertension management in Moroccan settings.

Strengths and limitations

This study is the first to focus specifically on hypertensive women in Morocco, an often underrepresented group in research on medication adherence. By addressing this population, it provides valuable insights into the unique challenges they face in managing their treatment.

However, the study has several limitations. Its cross-sectional design and sampling method, which precludes establishing causal relationships. Consequently, the associations identified between adherence and sociodemographic or clinical variables should be interpreted as correlations rather than predictors. Longitudinal or interventional studies are needed to confirm the causal pathways underlying these relationships and to better understand how these factors influence adherence over time.

Additionally, all the clinical information was obtained by self-reporting, making it susceptible to recall bias and potential social desirability bias, as patients might overestimate their adherence. Although the use of the MMAS-8 is practical, the absence of objective measures such as pharmacy refill data limits the ability to verify and compare self-reported adherence with other data sources. Future studies should therefore combine self-reported scales with objective adherence measures to improve data accuracy.

Moreover, the study used the 8-item MMAS in Classical Arabic, which has not been validated in the local Moroccan dialect. linguistic and cultural nuances specific to the Moroccan context may have influenced participants’ understanding of certain items and, consequently, the accuracy of their self-reported adherence. This limitation should therefore be considered when interpreting the findings.

Furthermore, the original validation article of the MMAS-8 [28] was retracted in 2023 due to concerns about its statistical analyses and reported psychometric properties [29], including misleading sensitivity and specificity estimates. While the MMAS-8 is widely used and the Arabic version has shown acceptable reliability, concerns about its validity suggest that findings based on this tool should be interpreted with caution. Future studies should use alternative validated adherence measures.

Conclusions

This study highlights a major challenge in the management of hypertension among Moroccan women, more than half of the participants (53.3%) were non-adherent to their prescribed antihypertensive medications. Our findings indicate that several factors may influence treatment adherence. Access to health insurance (AMO), participation in therapeutic education sessions, and a once-daily medication regimen were associated with better adherence. In contrast, polytherapy was a major barrier, significantly reducing the likelihood of adherence. These findings underscore the need for targeted interventions to improve adherence by expanding access to health insurance and education programs while simplifying treatment regimens.

Beyond its quantitative findings, this study contributes new insights by focusing exclusively on hypertensive women in Morocco, an underrepresented group in adherence research. By addressing gender-specific and socio-economic barriers within the Moroccan healthcare system, it provides a contextual understanding that can guide more equitable and gender-sensitive public health strategies.

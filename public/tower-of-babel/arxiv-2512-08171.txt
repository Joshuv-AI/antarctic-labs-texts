Large Excursions of Reflected Lévy Processes: Asymptotic Shapes
Zhi-Hao Cui Hao Wu and Wei Xu

Abstract
This paper primarily investigates the geometric properties of excursions of Lévy processes reflected at the past infimum with long lifetime or large height. For an oscillating process in the domain of attraction of a stable law, our results state that excursions with a long lifetime need not have a large height. After a suitable scaling, they behave like stable excursions with lifetime or height greater than one. These extend the related results in Doney and Rivero [ Prob. Theory Relat. Fields , 157(1) (2013) 1-45]. In contrast, for the negative-drift case we prove that under a heavy-tailed condition, long lifetime and large height are asymptotically equivalent. Conditioned on either event, excursions converge under spatial scaling to a single-jump process with Pareto-distributed jump size and size-biased jump time. Moreover, after a suitable time rescaling, the effect of the negative drift becomes apparent. MSC 2020 subject classifications: Primary 60G51, 60F17; secondary 60B10. Keywords and phrases: Lévy process, excursion measure, reflected process, conditional limit theorem.

Introduction and main results

Consider a real-valued Lévy process
X=\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
with non-monotone càdlàg trajectories and characteristics
(b,\sigma,\nu)
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we write
\tau^{-}_{x}
and
\tau^{+}_{x}
for the first passage times of
X
into
(-\infty,x)
and
(x,\infty)
, respectively.
Define the
running infimum
and
supremum processes
by

\displaystyle\underline{X}_{t}:=\inf\big\{X_{s}:0\leq s\leq t\big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\overline{X}_{t}:=\sup\big\{X_{s}:0\leq s\leq t\big\},\quad t\geq 0.

It is known that the reflected processes
X-\underline{X}
and
\overline{X}-X
are non-negative strong Markov processes.
Their excursions away from
0
form two Poisson point processes on the space
\mathcal{E}
of excursions, with respective intensities
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
and
\overline{n}(d\epsilon)
.
Here, each excursion
\epsilon\in\mathcal{E}
is a non-negative càdlàg function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
lifetime

\zeta=\inf\{t\geq 0:\epsilon_{s}=0,\forall s\geq t\}
and
height

\overline{\epsilon}=\sup\{\epsilon_{s}:0\leq s\leq\zeta\}
, with the usual convention that
\inf\emptyset=+\infty
.
We make convention that
\epsilon_{s}=0
whenever
s\geq\zeta
.

This work is mainly concerned with the asymptotic properties of excursions with long lifetime or large height under the measures
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
and
\overline{n}(d\epsilon)
.
These objects appear in many contexts and have attracted substantial attention. Several representative examples are listed below:

\bullet

Path-decomposition.
For
t\geq 0
, the last passage time by
X
at its infimum before
t
is defined by

\displaystyle\underline{g}_{t}:=\sup\big\{s\leq t:X_{s}=\underline{X}_{t}\mbox{ or }X_{s-}=\underline{X}_{t}\big\}.

Conditionally on
\underline{g}_{t}=s\in[0,t]
, the process
X
over the time interval
[0,t]
can be decomposed at time
\underline{g}_{t}
into the returned pre-
\underline{g}_{t}
part and the post-
\underline{g}_{t}
part, that are distributed as the laws
\overline{n}(\cdot\,|\,\zeta>s)
and
\underline{n}(\cdot\,|\,\zeta>t-s)
, respectively; see e.g.
[
7
,
24
]
.
The long-term behavior of
X
can be analyzed via the asymptotics of long-lived excursions under
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
and
\overline{n}(d\epsilon)

\underline{X}_{t}
is asymptotically distributed as
\underline{n}(\zeta>t)\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{n}(\epsilon_{s}\in dx,\zeta>s)ds
for large

; see
[
9
]
.

\bullet

Continuous-state branching processes.
For a spectrally positive Lévy process
X
, the Lamperti transform associates it with a continuous-state branching process

; see e.g. Theorem 12.2 in
[
18

[

In particular, the total progeny and maximum of

are identical in law to
(\tau_{0}^{-},\overline{X}_{\tau_{0}^{-}})
, whose asymptotic relations can be understood with the help of conditional limit theorems for excursions under
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
.

\bullet

Splitting tree.
When
X
is of bounded variation and has no negative jumps, Lambert
[
19
]
established a bijection between excursions in
\mathcal{E}
and splitting trees whose width process is a binary homogeneous Crump-Mode-Jagers process, which was generalized to the spectrally positve case later in
[
20
]
.
Under this correspondence, the extinction time and total progeny of the binary tree coincide in law with the height
\overline{\epsilon}
and lifetime
\zeta
under
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
.

Despite extensive work on their distribution properties and connections to other areas, the asymptotic properties of Lévy excursions remain largely unexplored.
When
X
is a Brownian motion, the distributions of
\zeta
and
\overline{\epsilon}
are given explicitly by

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)=\frac{t^{-1/2}}{\sqrt{\pi/2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>x)=\frac{1}{x},\quad x,t>0;

see Proposition 2.8 and Exercise 2.10 in
[
22
, p.484-485]
.
By Williams’ decomposition, the joint law of
(\zeta,\overline{\epsilon})
can be described by the maximum and length of two independent three-dimensional Bessel processes, though not in closed form.
For stable processes, we also have
\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)=t^{-\varrho}/\Gamma(1-\varrho)
with
\varrho=\mathbf{P}(X\leq 0)
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the closed form for
\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>x)
is unknown unless
X
is one-sized.
For general oscillating Lévy process in the domain of attraction of a stable law, Doney and Rivero
[
15
]
obtained a precise local description of the distributions of the lifetime
\zeta
and the final position
\epsilon_{\zeta-}
, and also used it to study the local probability of the first passage time
\tau_{0}^{-}
.

\displaystyle\beta:=-\mathbf{E}\big[X_{1}\big]\in(0,\infty).

The state

Conversely, if an excursion experiences a significant jump before being extinguished by the negative drift, it will typically survive for a long duration.
Under the heavy-tailed condition, the single-big-jump principle asserts that the occurrence of more than one big jump is extremely unlikely.
Additionally, to offset the negative drift, the single large jump is expected to occur as soon as possible.

\displaystyle\underline{n}\bigg(\sum_{0<s\leq\zeta}\mathbf{F}_{s}(\Delta\epsilon_{s})\cdot 1_{\{\Delta\epsilon_{s}\neq 0\}}\bigg)=\underline{n}\bigg(\int_{0}^{\zeta}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}}\mathbf{F}_{s}(y)\,\nu(dy)\bigg).

(2.5)

Let

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\exp\Big\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{[0,\infty)}(e^{-t}-e^{-\lambda t-ux})\mathbf{P}(X_{t}\in dx)\Big\},\quad\lambda,u\geq 0.

In particular, both

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\underline{\tt d}\cdot\lambda+\underline{\tt a}\cdot u+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-\lambda t-ux})\,\underline{n}(\zeta\in dt,\epsilon_{\zeta}\in dx).

(2.6)

In particular, its marginal cumulative function in space is also known as the

By (

\displaystyle\widehat{V}(x)=\bar{\tt d}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds,\quad x\geq 0;

Analogously,

2.2
Several auxiliary lemmas

We now present several auxiliary lemmas essential for proving our main results. The first extends Theorem 5 in
[
7
]
, which decomposes

X
at its last passage time
\underline{g}_{t}
into two independent excursions. Its proof follows directly from the monotone class theorem and is sketched below.

Lemma 2.1

For any
t\geq 0
and any bounded measurable functional
F
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbf{D}\big([0,t];\mathbb{R}\big)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F(\underline{g}_{t},X)\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\underline{\tt d}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\big(t,\overleftarrow{\epsilon}^{t}\big)\,\overline{n}(d\epsilon,\zeta>t)+\bar{\tt d}\cdot\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\big(0,\epsilon\big)\,\underline{n}(d\epsilon,\zeta>t)

(2.10)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,t)}ds\int_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{n}(d\epsilon,\zeta>s)\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\big(s,(\epsilon,\epsilon^{*})^{s}\big)\,\underline{n}(d\epsilon^{*},\zeta>t-s).

where

,
\overleftarrow{\omega}^{s}_{r}:=\omega_{(s-r)-}-\omega_{s}\wedge\omega_{s-}
and
(\omega,\omega^{*})^{s}_{r}:=\overleftarrow{\omega}^{s}_{r}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\leq s\}}+\omega^{*}_{r-s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\geq s\}}

and

Analogously to
\mathcal{C}^{Y,1}
, for each

, we define a càdlàg process
\mathcal{C}^{X,t}:=\{\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s}:s\geq 0\}
as the concatenation of

and an independent copy of its meander, that is,

(1)

The segment
\big\{\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s}:s\in[0,t]\big\}
is distributed as the pushforward of
\underline{n}(\cdot\,|\,\zeta>t)
by the restriction map
\omega\mapsto\big\{\omega(s):s\in[0,t]\big\}
;

(2)

Given
\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}=y
, the shifted process

is independent of
\big\{\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s}:s\in[0,t]\big\}
and has the same law as
X
under
\mathbf{P}_{y}
.

Our second auxiliary lemma shows that the process
\mathcal{C}^{X,t}
stopped at
\sigma_{0}^{X,t}:=\inf\{s>0:\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s}\leq 0\}
realizes the probability law
\underline{n}(\cdot\,|\,\zeta>t)
on
D([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.2

For any
T\geq 0
and any bounded measurable functional
H
on
D([0,T];\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\Big[H\Big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s\wedge\sigma_{0}^{X,t}}\vee 0:s\in[0,T]\Big)\Big]=\underline{n}\big(H\big(\epsilon_{s}:s\in[0,T]\big)\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big).

(2.11)

Proof.
First, note that the definition of
\mathcal{C}^{X,t}
implies that
\sigma_{0}>t
almost surely, so (
2.11
) holds for
T\leq t
.
For
T>t
, by the monotone class theorem it suffices to prove that for any bounded measurable functionals
F
on
D([0,t];\mathbb{R})
and
G
on
D([0,T-t];\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big)\cdot G\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s\wedge\sigma_{0}^{X,t}}\vee 0:s\in[t,T]\big)\big]=\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[0,t]\big)\cdot G\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[t,T]\big)\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big).

(2.12)

We first consider the right-hand side that can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[0,t]\big)\cdot G\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[t,T]\big),\zeta\in dr,\epsilon_{t}\in dy\big),

which, by using the Markov property to the last integral at time

, equals to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\epsilon_{t}\in dy,\zeta>t\big)\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{y}\big[G\big(X_{s\wedge(r-t)}\vee 0,s\in[0,T-t]\big)\big),\sigma_{0}^{X}\in dr-t\big],

where
\sigma_{0}^{X}:=\inf\{s\geq 0:X_{s}\leq 0\}
.
It is easy to identify that the inner integral equals to
\mathbf{E}_{y}\big[G\big(X_{s\wedge\sigma_{0}^{X}}\vee 0:s\in[0,T-t]\big)\big)\big]
and then the right-hand side of (

) equals to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{y}\big[G\big(X_{s\wedge\sigma_{0}^{X}}\vee 0,s\in[0,T-t]\big)\big)\big]\cdot\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\epsilon_{t}\in dy\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big).

(2.13)

For the left-hand side of (

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big)\cdot G\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t+s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{t+s<r\}},s\in[0,T-t]\big),\sigma_{0}^{X,t}\in dr,\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}\in dy\big]

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big)\cdot G\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t+s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{t+s<r\}},s\in[0,T-t]\big),\sigma_{0}^{X,t}\in dr\,\big|\,\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}=y\big]\mathbf{P}\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}\in dy\big).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}\in dy\big]\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[G\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t+s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{t+s<r\}},s\in[0,T-t]\big),\sigma_{0}^{X,t}\in dr\,\big|\,\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}=y\big].

Since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}\in dy\big]\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}_{y}\big[G\big(X_{s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{s<r\}},s\in[0,T-t]\big),\sigma_{0}^{X}\in dr\big].

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\mathcal{C}^{X,t}_{t}\in dy\big]=\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s},s\in[0,t]\big),\epsilon_{t}\in dy\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big).

As a third auxiliary lemma, we prove that if a sequence of càdàg processes converges weakly and the limit process has the property that

, then the weak convergence is inherited by the corresponding processes stopped at the first passage into

Lemma 2.3

Consider processes
\xi^{t}
,
\xi^{*}
such that
\xi^{t}\overset{\rm d}{\to}\xi^{*}
in

as

.
Let
\sigma^{t}_{0}
and
\sigma^{*}_{0}
be their first passage times into
(-\infty,0]
respectively.
If
\sigma^{*}_{0}<\infty

is regular for

, for
\xi^{*}
,

in

as

.

Proof.
By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, we may assume that
\xi^{(t)}\overset{\rm a.s.}{\to}\xi^{*}
in
D([0,\infty);\mathbb{R})
.
Consequently, for any
T\geq 0
there exists random time changes
\{\lambda^{t}_{s}:s\geq 0\}_{t>0}
with
\lambda^{t}_{0}\overset{\rm a.s.}{=}0
and
\lambda^{t}_{T}\overset{\rm a.s.}{=}T
such that as

,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big|\lambda^{t}_{s}-s\big|+\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\Big|\xi^{t}_{\lambda^{t}_{s}}-\xi^{*}_{s}\Big|\overset{\rm a.s.}{\to}0,

(2.14)

Hence it suffices to prove that
\sigma^{t}_{0}\overset{\rm a.s.}{\to}\sigma^{*}_{0}
as

.
For any
s>0
, if
\sigma^{*}_{0}>s
, we see that
\underline{\xi}^{*}_{s}>0
and hence
\underline{\xi}^{t}_{s}>0
for all

large enough, which yields that
\sigma^{t}_{0}\geq s
.
Since

is arbitrary, we obtain that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\sigma^{t}_{0}\geq\sigma^{*}_{0}.

On the other hand, if

, we have
\underline{\xi}^{*}_{s}\leq 0

This along with the arbitrariness of

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sigma^{t}_{0}\leq\sigma^{*}_{0}.

By (2) in

(3.18)

For all

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{[x]+1}\tau_{-1,i}^{-}>t\bigg)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{[x]+1}\big(\tau_{-1,i}^{-}-\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\big)>t-([x]+1)\cdot\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{[x]+1}\big(\tau_{-1,i}^{-}-\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\big)>t-(\delta t+1)\cdot\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\bigg).

Since
t-(\delta t+1)\cdot\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\sim\big(1-\delta\cdot\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\big)\cdot t\to\infty
,
by Theorem 2 in
[
11

there exists a constant
C>0
such that for large

x\geq 0

\displaystyle C\cdot(x+1)\cdot\mathbf{P}\big(\tau_{-1}^{-}\geq\big(1-\delta\cdot\gamma\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\big)\cdot t\big),

which, by (
4.3
) and Assumption
1.6

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\beta}\cdot(x+1)\cdot\overline{\nu}\big(\big(1-\delta\cdot\theta\cdot\mathbf{E}[\tau_{-1}^{-}]\big)\cdot t\big)\sim\frac{C}{\beta}\cdot(x+1)\cdot\Big(\frac{1-\delta\gamma/\beta}{\beta}\Big)^{-\theta}\cdot\bar{\nu}(\beta t).

Consequently, the desired uniformly upper bound holds.

Proposition 4.2

For any
T\geq 0
, we have

\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big|X_{ts}/t+\beta s\big|\to 0
in probability as
t\to\infty
.

Proof.
By the Lévy-Itô decomposition theorem, the process
X
admits the following representation

\displaystyle X_{t}:=-\beta t+\sigma B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}y\widetilde{N}(dr,dy),\quad t\geq 0,

(4.6)

for some constant
\sigma\geq 0
and compensated Poisson random measure
\widetilde{N}(ds,dy)
on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
with intensity
ds\,\nu(dy)
.
For any
K>0
, we can decompose
X_{t}+\beta t
into the following two terms:

\displaystyle M^{\leq K}_{t}:=\sigma B_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|y|\leq K}y\,\widetilde{N}(dr,dy)\quad\mbox{and}\quad M^{>K}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{|y|>K}y\,\widetilde{N}(dr,dy).

Hence it suffices to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{>K}_{s}\big|\geq\varepsilon t\Big)=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{\leq K}_{s}\big|\geq\varepsilon t\Big)=0

Firstly, by Chebyshev’s inequality and the fact
|M^{>K}_{s}|\leq\int_{0}^{s}\int_{|y|>K}|y|N(dr,dy)+s\cdot\int_{|y|>K}|y|\,\nu(dy)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{>K}_{s}\big|\geq\varepsilon t\Big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon t}\cdot\mathbf{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{>K}_{s}\big|\bigg]\leq\frac{2T}{\varepsilon}\cdot\int_{|y|>K}|y|\,\nu(dy),

which goes to
0
as
K\to\infty
.
Moreover, by using Chebyshev’s inequality again and then the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequalities to the first probability, there exists some constant
C>0
independent of
t
,
T
and
K
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{\leq K}_{s}\big|\geq\varepsilon t\Big)\leq\frac{1}{|\varepsilon t|^{2}}\mathbf{E}\bigg[\sup_{s\in[0,Tt]}\big|M^{\leq K}_{s}\big|^{2}\bigg]\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{C\cdot T}{|\varepsilon|^{2}\cdot t}\cdot\Big[\sigma^{2}+\int_{|y|\leq K}|y|^{2}\nu(dy)\Big],

which also vanishes as
t\to\infty
.

Lemma 4.3

As
t\to\infty
, we have
\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)\sim\underline{n}(T\wedge\zeta)\cdot\overline{\nu}(\beta t)
uniformly in
T\geq 0
; equivalently, for all
T_{0}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{T\in[0,T_{0}]}\Big|\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)}{\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}-\underline{n}(T\wedge\zeta)\Big|+\lim_{T\to\infty}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\zeta>t\big)}{\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}=0.

(4.7)

Proof.
The equivalence of these two claims can be easily identified by using the fact
\underline{n}(\zeta)<\infty
; see (
4.2
).
We now prove the two limits in (
4.7
) separately in the next two steps.

Step 1.
For the first limit, it is easy to see that for all
t\geq T_{0}
,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)\leq\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)=\underline{n}\big(N^{\beta t}_{T\wedge\zeta}\geq 1\big)\leq\underline{n}\big(N^{\beta t}_{T\wedge\zeta}\big).

(4.8)

This along with (
4.4
) induces that uniformly in
T\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}\leq\underline{n}(T\wedge\zeta).

It remains to prove that the asymptotic lower bound also holds, i.e., uniformly in
T\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}\geq\underline{n}(T\wedge\zeta).

(4.9)

For any
z>\beta
, we see that
\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)\geq\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)
.
By the Markov property,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{zt}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,\zeta>t\big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{zt}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\underline{X}_{t-s}>0\big)\,\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,\zeta>s\big),

which, by the monotone of
\underline{X}
, can be bounded from below by
\mathbf{P}_{zt}\big(\underline{X}_{t}>0\big)\cdot\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)
.
Since
z>\beta=-\mathbf{E}[X_{1}]
, the Lévy process
W:=\{W_{t}:=\frac{z+\beta}{2}\cdot t+X_{t},t\geq 0\}
drifts to infinity and hence as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{zt}\big(\underline{X}_{t}>0\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\underline{X}_{t}+zt>0\big)=\mathbf{P}\bigg(\underline{X}_{t}+\frac{z+\beta}{2}\cdot t>-\frac{z-\beta}{2}\cdot t\bigg)\geq\mathbf{P}\bigg(\underline{W}_{t}>-\frac{z-\beta}{2}\cdot t\bigg)\to 1.

Consequently, the lower bound (
4.9
) follows if we can identify that uniformly in
T\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\beta+}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)}{\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\geq\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big).

(4.10)

To prove this inequality, we first observe that
\{J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\}=\{N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}\geq 1\}
, which implies that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)=\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}\big)-\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}-1;N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq 2\big).

By (
4.4
) and Assumption
1.6
, we have

\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}\big)\sim(z/\beta)^{-\theta}\cdot\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(\beta t)

as
t\to\infty
,
and then uniformly in
T\in[0,T_{0}]
,

\displaystyle\lim_{z\to\beta+}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)}{\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}\geq\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)-\lim_{z\to\beta+}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{T\in[0,T_{0}]}\frac{\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}-1;N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq 2\big)}{\overline{\nu}(\beta t)}.

(4.11)

Hence the lower bound (
4.10
) follows if the last limit equals to
0
.
Summation by parts deduces that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}-1;N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq 2\big)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq k\big).

(4.12)

Using the Markov property and then the independent increments to summand,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq k\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{zt}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq k,J^{zt}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dy\big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{zt}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}\Big(N_{(T-s)\wedge\tau_{0}^{-}}^{zt}\geq k-1\Big)\,\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dy,\zeta>s\big),

which can be bounded by
\mathbf{P}\big(N_{T}^{zt}\geq k-1\big)\cdot\underline{n}\big(J^{zt}\leq T\wedge\zeta\big)
since
N_{s}^{zt}
is non-decreasing in
s
.
By (
4.4
), (
4.8
) and the fact that
N^{zt}_{T}
is Poisson distributed with rate
T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)
,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq k\big)\leq\mathbf{P}\big(N_{T}^{zt}\geq k-1\big)\cdot\underline{n}(T\wedge\zeta)\cdot\bar{\nu}(zt)=\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)\cdot\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\frac{(T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt))^{j}}{j!}\cdot e^{-T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)}.

Plugging this back into the sum in (
4.12
), then using the change of variables and summation by parts,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(N^{zt}_{T\wedge\zeta}-1;N_{T\wedge\zeta}^{zt}\geq 2\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)\cdot\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k-1}^{\infty}\frac{(T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt))^{j}}{j!}\cdot e^{-T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)}

(4.13)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=k}^{\infty}\frac{(T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt))^{j}}{j!}\cdot e^{-T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)}

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\cdot\frac{(T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt))^{k}}{k!}\cdot e^{-T\cdot\overline{\nu}(zt)},

(4.15)

which equals to
\underline{n}\big(T\wedge\zeta\big)\cdot T\cdot\big|\bar{\nu}(zt)\big|^{2}
and hence the last term in (
4.11
) equals to
0
.
Consequently, the lower bound (
4.9
) holds and then the first limit in (
4.7
) follows.

Step 2.
For the second limit in (
4.7
),
we continue to use the notation
\tau_{x}^{+}=\inf\{s>0:\epsilon_{s}>x\}
and then
decompose
\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\zeta>t\big)

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}>T/2,\zeta>t\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}\leq T/2,\zeta>t\big).

(4.16)

An application of the Markov property to the first term at time

shows that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}>T/2,\zeta>t\big)=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{\beta t}>T/2,\tau_{0}^{-}>t-T/2\big)\,\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{T/2}\in dx,\zeta\wedge\tau_{1}^{+}>T/2\big),

which is smaller than
\mathbf{P}_{1}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}>t-T/2\big)\cdot\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{T/2}\leq 1,\zeta\wedge\tau_{1}^{+}>T/2\big)
.
By Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}\cdot\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}>T/2,\zeta>t\big)\leq C\cdot\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{T/2}\leq 1,T/2<\zeta\wedge\tau_{1}^{+}\big),

which goes to
0
as

.

We now consider the second term in (
4.16
). For any
\delta\in(0,\beta)
, it can be further decomposed into the following two terms by the overshoot size at

,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}\leq T/2,1<\epsilon_{\tau_{1}^{+}}\leq\delta t,\zeta>t\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\tau_{1}^{+}\leq T/2,\epsilon_{\tau_{1}^{+}}>\delta t,\zeta>t\big).

(4.18)

For

, we use the Markov property to the first term and get that it equals to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{1}^{\delta t}\underline{n}\big(\tau_{1}^{+}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,J^{\beta t}>T,\zeta>t\big)

(4.19)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{1}^{\delta t}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{\beta t}>T/2,\tau_{0}^{-}>t/2\big)\,\underline{n}\big(\tau_{1}^{+}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,\zeta>s\big)

(4.21)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\delta t}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{\beta t}>T/2,\tau_{0}^{-}>t/2\big)\,\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{\tau_{1}^{+}}\in dx,\zeta>\tau_{1}^{+}\big).

By Proposition

]

.

. The whole proof ends.

Proof for Theorem
1.7
.

A direct consequence of Lemma
4.3
and (
4.2
) shows that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}=\lim_{T\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}+\lim_{T\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>T,\zeta>t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(\beta t)}=\underline{n}(\zeta)<\infty.

(4.26)

We now prove that
\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\sim\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)
as
t\to\infty
.
Firstly, it is obvious that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\geq\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>t\big)=\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)-\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}\leq\beta t,\zeta>t\big)

(4.27)

Recall the notation
\tau_{x}^{+}=\inf\{s>0:\epsilon_{s}>x\}
.
Note that
\{\tau_{\beta t}^{+}>t,\zeta>t\}\subset\{J^{\beta t}>t,\zeta>t\}
, by the second limit in (
4.7
) we have as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\bar{\epsilon}\leq\beta t,\zeta>t\big)\leq\underline{n}\big(\tau_{\beta t}^{+}>t,\zeta>t\big)\leq\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}>t,\zeta>t\big)=o\big(\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)\big).

Taking this back into (
4.27
) gives that

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)}{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}\geq 1.

(4.28)

On the other hand, we have

\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)=\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq\delta t\big)+\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>\delta t\big)
for any
\delta\in(0,1)
.
By the Markov property,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq\delta t\big)=\underline{n}\big(\tau_{\beta t}^{+}<\zeta\leq\delta t\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta t}\int_{\beta t}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(\tau_{\beta t}^{+}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,s<\zeta\leq\delta t\big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta t}\int_{\beta t}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}\leq\delta t-s\big)\,\underline{n}\big(\tau_{\beta t}^{+}\in ds,\epsilon_{s}\in dx,\zeta>s\big)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta t}\mathbf{P}_{\beta t}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}\leq\delta t-s\big)\,\underline{n}\big(\tau_{\beta t}^{+}\in ds,\zeta>s\big),

which can be bounded by
\mathbf{P}_{\beta t}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}\leq\delta t\big)\cdot\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)
.
Since
\mathbf{E}[X_{1}]=-\beta
and
\delta\in(0,1)
, the Lévy process
\eta_{t}:=X_{t}+(2-\delta)\cdot\beta t
drifts to infinity a.s.
Note that
\underline{\eta}_{t}\leq\underline{X}_{t}+(2-\delta)\cdot\beta t
, we have that as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\beta t}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}\leq\delta t\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\beta t}\big(\underline{X}_{\delta t}\leq 0\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(\underline{X}_{\delta t}\leq-\beta t\big)\leq\mathbf{P}\big(\underline{\eta}_{\delta t}\leq-(1-\delta)\beta t\big)\to 0,

and hence
\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq\delta t\big)=o\big(\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\big)
.
This induces that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\sim\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>\delta t\big),

(4.29)

and then by (
4.26
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)}{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>\delta t\big)}{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>\delta t\big)}{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}=\delta^{-\theta}\to 1,

as
\delta\to 1-
. This along with the lower bound (
4.28
) induces that
\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\sim\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)
as
t\to\infty
.

We now prove
\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big)\sim\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\,\big|\,\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)\to 1
. Our preceding result tells that it holds if

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq t\big)=o\big(\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}\leq\beta t,\zeta>t\big)=o\big(\underline{n}(\zeta>t)\big).

We just need to the first one and the second one follows immediately.
For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq t\big)=\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t\big)-\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>\delta t\big)+\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\delta t<\zeta\leq t\big).

For the second term on the right-hand side, by (
4.29

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta>\delta t\big)}{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)}=1.

Moreover, by (

) we also have as

\displaystyle\frac{\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\delta t<\zeta\leq t\big)}{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)}\leq\frac{\underline{n}\big(\delta t<\zeta\leq t\big)}{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)}=\frac{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>\delta t\big)}{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)}-\frac{\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)}\to\delta^{-\theta}-1,

which goes to

as

.
Putting these estimates together, we see that
\underline{n}\big(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t,\zeta\leq t\big)=o\big(\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>\beta t)\big)
and then this proof ends.

Lemma 4.4

Under
\underline{n}(d\epsilon)
, the events
\zeta>J^{\beta t}
and
\zeta>t
are asymptotically equivalent as
t\to\infty
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\zeta>J^{\beta t}\mid\zeta>t\big)\sim\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\mid\zeta>J^{\beta t}\big)\to 1.

Proof.
Let
A\Delta B
be the symmetric difference of the two sets
A
and
B
.
It suffices to prove that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\{\zeta>J^{\beta t})\Delta\{\zeta>t\}\big)=o(\underline{n}(\zeta>t)),

(4.30)

as
t\to\infty
.
From the properties of symmetric difference, we have

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\{\zeta>J^{\beta t})\Delta\{\zeta>t\}\big)=\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)-\underline{n}\big(\zeta>J^{\beta t}\big)+2\cdot\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}<\zeta\leq t\big).

(4.31)

Firstly, since
\{\zeta>J^{\beta t}\}=\{N^{\beta t}_{\zeta}\geq 1\}
, by (
4.4
) and Theorem
1.7
we have

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\zeta>J^{\beta t}\big)=\underline{n}\big(N^{\beta t}_{\zeta}\geq 1\big)\leq\underline{n}\big(N^{\beta t}_{\zeta}\big)=\underline{n}\big(\zeta\big)\cdot\overline{\nu}(\beta t)\quad\mbox{and then}\quad\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta>J^{\beta t})}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\leq 1.

On the other hand, it follows from (
4.10
) that for any
T>0

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta>J^{\beta t})}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\geq\lim_{z\to\beta+}\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(J^{zt}\leq\zeta\wedge T)}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\geq\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta\wedge T)}{\underline{n}(\zeta)},

which goes to 1 as
T\to\infty
.
Combining these two estimates together, we obtain that as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(\zeta>J^{\beta t}\big)\sim\underline{n}(\zeta>t).

(4.32)

For the last term in (
4.31
), we see that

\displaystyle\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}<\zeta\leq t\big)=\underline{n}\big(\zeta>J^{\beta t},J^{\beta t}\leq t\big)-\underline{n}\big(J^{\beta t}\leq t<\zeta\big).

For any
T>0
and any large
t
, by using Lemma
4.3
we get

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(J^{\beta t}\leq t<\zeta)}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\geq\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(J^{\beta t}\leq T,\zeta>t)}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}=\lim_{t\to\infty}\underline{n}(J^{\beta t}\leq T\mid\zeta>t)=\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta\wedge T)}{\underline{n}(\zeta)},

which goes to 1 as
T\to\infty
.
Moreover, by (
4.32
),

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta>J^{\beta t},J^{\beta t}\leq t)}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta>J^{\beta t})}{\underline{n}(\zeta>t)}=1.

Putting these two estimates together, we conclude that
\underline{n}(J^{\beta t}<\zeta\leq t)=o(\underline{n}(\zeta>t))
as
t\to\infty
.
Finally, taking this and (
4.32
) back into the right-hand sides of (
4.31
) immediately yields (
4.30
).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\big|X_{u}-X_{J^{t}}\big|>\varepsilon t/2,\tau_{0}^{-}>u,J^{t}\in ds,X_{s}\in dy\big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}_{y}\big(|X_{u-s}-y|>\varepsilon t/2,\tau_{0}^{-}>u-s\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\in ds,X_{s}\in dy,\tau_{0}^{-}>s\big)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u}\int_{t}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\big(|X_{u-s}|>\varepsilon t/2\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\in ds,X_{s}\in dy,\tau_{0}^{-}>s\big),

which, along with the two facts that
\sup_{s\in[0,u]}\mathbf{P}\big(|X_{u-s}|>\varepsilon t/2\big)\leq\mathbf{P}\big(\sup_{s\in[0,u]}|X_{s}|>\varepsilon t/2\big)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
and
\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u\big)=\mathbf{P}\big(J^{t}\leq u\big)=u\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)
, induces that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\Big(\big|X_{u}-X_{J^{t}}\big|>\varepsilon t/2,J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\Big)=o\Big(\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u,\tau_{0}^{-}>J^{t}\big)\Big)\leq o\Big(\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u\big)\Big)=o\big(u\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)\big).

Combining the preceding two estimates together, we obtain that as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\big|X_{u}/t-\Delta X_{J^{t}}/t\big|>\varepsilon,J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)=o\big(\bar{\nu}(t)\big).

(4.45)

and hence the first probability in (
4.41
) can be well approximated by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\Delta X_{J^{t}}>t,J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)=\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)=\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u,J^{t}<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)-\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}<\tau_{0}^{-}\leq u\big).

By Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5 in
[
23
]
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u,J^{t}<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)\sim\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge u]\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)
as
t\to\infty
.
Meanwhile, by using the strong Markov property,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}<\tau_{0}^{-}\leq u\big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\Big(\sup_{s\in[J^{t},u]}\big|X_{s}-X_{J^{t}}\big|>t,J^{t}\leq u\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\Big(\sup_{s\in[J^{t},J^{t}+u]}\big|X_{s}-X_{J^{t}}\big|>t,J^{t}\leq u\Big)

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}\Big(\sup_{s\in[0,u]}\big|X_{s}\big|>t\Big),

which equals to
o\big(\bar{\nu}(t)\big)
as
t\to\infty
since
\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(J^{t}\leq u\big)=u\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)
.
In conclusion, we have as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)\sim\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\Delta X_{J^{t}}>t,J^{t}\leq u<\tau_{0}^{-}\big)\sim\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge u]\cdot\bar{\nu}(t).

Step 2.
We now consider the second probability in (
4.41
), which can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,J^{t}>u\big)=\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,N^{t}_{u}=0\big)=\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t\big)-\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,N^{t}_{u}\geq 1\big).

(4.46)

Let
\widetilde{X}^{1}
be the process defined by moving all jumps larger than
1
away from
X
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{t}^{1}=X_{t}-\sum_{s\leq t}\Delta X_{s}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\Delta X_{s}>1\}},\quad t\geq 0.

For any
\delta>0
, conditionally on
N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=1
we have
X_{u}\geq\widetilde{X}_{u}^{1}+\Delta X_{J^{(1+\delta)t}}\geq\widetilde{X}_{u}^{1}+(1+\delta)t
and then

\displaystyle\big\{X_{u}>t,N_{u}^{t}=1\big\}\supseteq\big\{\widetilde{X}_{u}^{1}+(1+\delta)t>t\big\}\cap\big\{N_{u}^{t}-N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=0\big\}\cap\big\{N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=1\big\}.

It is easy to identify that the three events on the right-hand side are mutually independent and then

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,N_{u}^{t}=1\big)\geq\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\widetilde{X}_{u}^{1}>-\delta t\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(N_{u}^{t}-N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=0\big)\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=1\big).

Since
\mathbf{E}[\widetilde{X}_{1}^{1}]<0
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\widetilde{X}_{u}^{1}>-\delta t\big)\to 1
as
t\to\infty
.
Moreover, since
N_{u}^{t}-N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}
and
N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}
are Poisson distributed with rate
u\cdot\big(\bar{\nu}(t)-\bar{\nu}((1+\delta)t)\big)
and
u\cdot\bar{\nu}((1+\delta)t)
respectively, we also have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(N_{u}^{t}-N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=0\big)\to 1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(N_{u}^{(1+\delta)t}=1\big)\sim u\cdot\bar{\nu}\big((1+\delta)t\big),

as
t\to\infty
.
Combining these estimates together and then using the fact that
\bar{\nu}\in\mathrm{RV}^{\infty}_{-\theta}
,

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,N^{t}_{u}\geq 1\big)}{\bar{\nu}(t)}\geq u\cdot\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\bar{\nu}\big((1+\delta)t\big)}{\bar{\nu}(t)}=\frac{u}{(1+\delta)^{\theta}}.

Additionally, repeating the proof of (3.2) in
[
23
]
gives that

\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t\big)\sim u\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)
as
t\to\infty
.
Taking these two estimates back into (
4.46
), we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\bar{\nu}(t)}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(X_{u}>t,J^{t}>u,\tau_{0}^{-}>u\big)\leq u\cdot\big(1-(1+\delta)^{-\theta}\big),

(4.47)

which goes to
0
as
\delta\to 0+
. The whole proof ends.

\Box

Lemma 4.8

For any
x>0
, we have the following identity

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\big(\mathcal{T}_{x}\leq u\big)=\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{P}\big(\mathcal{T}\leq u-s\big)\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\,\zeta>s)\,ds+\frac{\bar{\tt d}}{\widehat{V}(x)}\,\mathbf{P}\big(\mathcal{T}\leq u\big),\quad u\geq 0.

(4.48)

Proof.
For any
x,t,u>0
, Lemma
2.1
allows us to write the probability
\mathbf{P}_{x}(X_{u}>t,\,\tau_{0}^{-}>u)
as

\displaystyle\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{u}>t-x,\,\zeta>u\big)+\int_{0}^{u}\int_{0}^{x}\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{u-s}>t+z-x,\,\zeta>u-s\big)\cdot\overline{n}\big(\epsilon_{s}\in dz,\,\zeta>s\big)\,ds.

(4.49)

From Lemma
4.6
, we see that

\underline{n}(\epsilon_{u}>t-y,\,\zeta>u)\sim\underline{n}(\zeta\wedge u)\cdot\bar{\nu}(t)

as
t\to\infty
uniformly in
y\in[0,x]
.
Moreover, by Theorem
1.7
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sup_{s\in[0,u]}\sup_{z\in[0,x]}\frac{\underline{n}\big(\epsilon_{u-s}>t+z-x,\,\zeta>u-s\big)}{\bar{\nu}(t)}\leq\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{\underline{n}(\overline{\epsilon}>t-x)}{\bar{\nu}(t)}=\underline{n}(\zeta)<\infty.

Based on these two asymptotic estimates, we divide (
4.49
) by
\bar{\nu}(t)
and then apply the dominated convergence theorem to the last integral to obtain that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{\bar{\nu}(t)}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{x}(X_{u}>t,\,\tau_{0}^{-}>u)=\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}(\zeta\wedge u)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(\zeta\wedge((u-s)\vee 0)\big)\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\,\zeta>s)\,ds.

(4.50)

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.7
we also have the left-hand side also equals to
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge u]
and then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}\wedge u]=\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}(\zeta\wedge u)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\underline{n}\big(\zeta\wedge((u-s)\vee 0)\big)\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\,\zeta>s)\,ds.

Dividing both sides by
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}]
, we see that the desired identity follows from the distributions of
\mathcal{T}_{x}
and
\mathcal{T}
as well as the equality
\mathbf{E}_{x}[\tau_{0}^{-}]=\widehat{V}(x)\cdot\underline{n}(\zeta)
; see (
4.3
).

\Box

Proof of Corollary
1.9
(1).

It suffices to prove that for any
T>0
and any bounded, uniformly continuous non-negative functional
F
on
D([0,T];\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{s}/t,\,s\in[0,T]\big)\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\to\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{T}_{x}\leq s\}},s\in[0,T]\big)\big],

as
t\to\infty
.
For any
0<K<t
, we decompose the left-hand side into the following two parts:

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F(X_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]),\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F(X_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big].

(4.51)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
F(\cdot)\leq 1
.
The first conditional expectation can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\underline{g}_{t}>K,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}\big(\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big)}=1-\frac{\mathbf{P}(\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}.

(4.52)

By Lemma
2.1
, the last fraction can be represented as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\cdot\Big(\bar{d}\cdot\underline{n}(\zeta>t)+\int_{0}^{K}\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\underline{n}(\zeta>t-s)\,ds\Big).

By (
4.3
) and Theorem
1.7
, we have
\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)\sim\widehat{V}(x)\cdot\underline{n}(\zeta>t-s)
uniformly in
s\in[0,K]
and hence

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{P}(\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}=\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\Big(\bar{d}+\int_{0}^{K}\overline{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds\Big),

(4.53)

which goes to
1
as
K\to\infty
by (
2.8
).
Taking this back into (
4.52
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F(X_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]),\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]=0.

We now consider the second conditional expectation in (
4.51
), which equals to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\cdot\mathbf{E}\bigg[F\Big(\frac{x}{t}+\frac{X_{s}}{t}:s\in[0,T]\Big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x\bigg].

By the uniform continuity of
F
, it further equals to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\cdot\Big(\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(X_{s}/t:s\in[0,T]\big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x\big]+O(x/t)\cdot\mathbf{P}\big(\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x\big)\Big).

(4.54)

This along with (
4.53
) induces that as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F(X_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\sim\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(X_{s}/t:s\in[0,T]\big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x\big].

By Lemma
2.1
, the last expectation can be represented as

\displaystyle\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}\Big(F\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{s}}{t},s\in[0,T]\Big),\zeta>t\Big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle+\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K}ds\int_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{n}(d\epsilon,\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\Big(\frac{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{*})^{s}_{r}}{t},r\in[0,T]\Big)\,\underline{n}(d\epsilon^{*},\zeta>t-s).

From (
4.3
) and Theorem
1.8
(1), it follows that as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]\big),\zeta>t\big)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\!\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}\big(\zeta>t\big)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\cdot\underline{n}\big(F\big(\epsilon_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]\big)\,\big|\,\zeta>t\big)

(4.55)

\displaystyle\sim\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\tt d}}{\widehat{V}(x)}\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[F(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{T}\leq s\}},s\in[0,T])\big].

(4.56)

Similarly as in Step 2 of the proof of Corollary
1.3
, by the uniform continuity of
F
we have as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\int_{0}^{K}ds\int_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{n}(d\epsilon,\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\Big(\frac{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{*})^{s}_{r}}{t},r\in[0,T]\Big)\,\underline{n}(d\epsilon^{*},\zeta>t-s)

\displaystyle\sim\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K}\overline{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\cdot\underline{n}\Big(F\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{r-s}}{t}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\geq s\}},r\in[0,T]\Big)\,\Big|\,\zeta>t-s\Big)\cdot\frac{\underline{n}(\zeta>t-s)}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)}\,ds.

By (
4.3
) and Theorem
1.8
(1), it converges as
t\to\infty
to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\int_{0}^{K}\mathbf{E}\Big[F\Big(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\geq\mathcal{T}+s\}},r\in[0,T]\Big)\Big]\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds,

which further converges as
K\to\infty
to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\Big[F\Big(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\geq\mathcal{T}+s\}},r\in[0,T]\Big)\Big]\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds.

Combining this together with (
4.55
) and then taking them back into (
4.54
),

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F(X_{s}/t,s\in[0,T]),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]

\displaystyle=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\tt d}}{\widehat{V}(x)}\cdot\mathbf{E}\big[F(\mathcal{P},s\in[0,T])\big]+\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\Big[F\Big(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{r\geq\mathcal{T}+s\}},r\in[0,T]\Big)\Big]\cdot\bar{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds.

which equals to
\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{P}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{T}_{x}\leq s\}},s\in[0,T]\big)\big]
by Lemma
4.8
. The proof ends.

Proof of Corollary
1.9
(2).

By the independent increments of
X
, it suffices to prove that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{X_{ts}}{t}:s\in[\delta,1]\Big\}\overset{\rm d}{\to}\big\{\mathcal{P}-\beta s:s\in[\delta,1]\big\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Big\{\frac{X_{t(1+s)}-X_{t}}{t}:s\geq 0\Big\}\overset{\rm d}{\to}\big\{-\beta s:s\in[\delta,1]\big\},

in
D\big([\delta,1];\mathbb{R}\big)
and
D\big([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}\big)
as
t\to\infty
.
The second limit follows directly from Proposition
4.2
.
We now prove the first one, which holds if and only if for any bounded, uniformly continuous non-negative functional
F
on
D\big([\delta,1];\mathbb{R}\big)
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t,s\in[\delta,1]\big)\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\to\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{P}^{\alpha,\beta}-\beta s,s\in[\delta,1]\big)\big],

(4.57)

as
t\to\infty
.
For any
K>0
, the left-hand side of (
4.57
) can be decomposed into the next two terms

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t,s\in[\delta,1]\big),\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t,s\in[\delta,1]\big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big].

(4.58)

Without loss of generality, we still assume that
F(\cdot)\leq 1
.
By (
4.53
),

\displaystyle\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t,s\in[\delta,1]\big),\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\leq\lim_{K\to\infty}\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[\underline{g}_{t}>K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]=0.

(4.59)

Repeating the proof of Corollary
1.9
(1) to the second conditional expectation in (
4.58
) induces that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{x}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t,s\in[\delta,1]\big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K\,\big|\,\tau_{0}^{-}>t\big]\sim\frac{\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(X_{ts}/t:s\in[\delta,1]\big),\underline{g}_{t}\leq K,\underline{X}_{t}\geq-x\big]}{\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)},

as
t\to\infty
.
The last expectation can be represented as

\displaystyle\bar{\tt d}\cdot\underline{n}\Big(F\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{ts}}{t},s\in[\delta,1]\Big),\zeta>t\Big)\!\!\!

\displaystyle+\!\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{K}ds\int_{\mathcal{E}}\overline{n}(d\epsilon,\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\int_{\mathcal{E}}F\Big(\frac{(\epsilon,\epsilon^{*})_{tr}^{s}}{t},r\in[\delta,1]\Big)\,\underline{n}(d\epsilon^{*},\zeta>t-s).

Dividing both terms by
\mathbf{P}_{x}(\tau_{0}^{-}>t)
, then applying (
4.3
) and Theorem
1.8
(2), they converge as
t\to\infty
to

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{P}-\beta s,s\in[\delta,1]\big)\big]\cdot\frac{1}{\widehat{V}(x)}\Big(\bar{\tt d}+\int_{0}^{K}\overline{n}(\epsilon_{s}\leq x,\zeta>s)\,ds\Big).

which further converges to
\mathbf{E}\big[F\big(\mathcal{P}-\beta s,s\in[\delta,1]\big)\big]
as
K\to\infty
. The proof ends.

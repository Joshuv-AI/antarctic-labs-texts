ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1057 LP-PLA2 ACTIVITY STRATIFIES BENEFIT FROM IMMEDIATE INTENSIVE STATIN THERAPY IN EMBOLIC STROKE
Authors: Yao Lu, Chenhui Liu, Xuan Wang, Yuesong Pan, Yilong Wang, Ying Gao, Wei Qi Chen, Lingling Jiang, Mengyuan Zhou, Yongjun Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsWhether intensive statin therapy should be initiated immediately after acute ischemic stroke remains uncertain. Embolic stroke and elevated lipoprotein-associated phospholipase A2 (LP-PLA2), reflecting inflammation and plaque instability, may identify patients most likely to benefit from early intensive statin treatment.MethodsWe conducted a post hoc analysis of the INSPIRES trial, a multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 2×2 factorial randomized trial including patients with mild ischemic stroke or high-risk transient ischemic attack attributed to atherosclerotic disease. Patients were randomized to immediate or delayed intensive statin therapy. Index strokes were classified as embolic or nonembolic, and baseline LP-PLA2 activity was dichotomized by the median. Interaction analyses assessed whether LP-PLA2 modified the efficacy and safety of immediate intensive statin therapy according to stroke subtype. Outcomes included poor functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score 2–6), recurrent stroke, and moderate-to-severe bleeding at 90 days.ResultsAmong 5053 patients included, a significant interaction was observed between LP-PLA2 activity and immediate intensive statin therapy for poor functional outcome in embolic stroke (adjusted p for interaction = 0.017), but not in nonembolic stroke. In patients with embolic stroke and high LP-PLA2 activity, immediate intensive statin therapy was associated with a significantly lower risk of poor functional outcome compared with delayed therapy (7.1% vs 13.8%; adjusted RR 0.58, 95% CI 0.40–0.83). No significant differences were observed in recurrent stroke or moderate-to-severe bleeding.ConclusionsImmediate intensive statin therapy may provide the greatest functional benefit in patients with embolic stroke and elevated LP-PLA2 activity, supporting a precision approach to early secondary prevention.Conflict of interestYao Lu, Chenhui Liu, Xuan Wang, Yuesong Pan, Yilong Wang, Ying Gao , Weiqi Chen, Lingling Jiang, Mengyuan Zhou, Yongjun Wang: nothing to disclose

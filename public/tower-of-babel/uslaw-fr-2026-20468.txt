I. Overview

    Under CAA section 107(d), the EPA is to issue area designations 
upon promulgation of a new or revised NAAQS. On February 7, 2024, the 
EPA promulgated revisions to the primary annual PM2.5 NAAQS 
to a level of 9.0 micrograms per cubic meter ([micro]g/m\3\) as 
described in 40 CFR 50.20.\1\ On July 17, 2026, the Northern District 
Court of California ordered the EPA to publish final designations for 
all areas of the country no later than February 6, 2027.\2\ Although 
EPA is appealing the order, EPA is also moving forward in the absence 
of action from the judiciary, to complete the designations.

    As a general matter, the first step in the designations process is 
for the Governors of States and territories, and Tribes, if they so 
choose, to submit initial area designations to the EPA. Whenever the 
EPA intends to modify a Governor's initial designation, the Agency must 
notify the State, territory, or Tribe no later than 120 days prior to 
promulgating final designations.\3\ These notifications are informally 
referred to as 120-day letters.

II. Legal Authority

    Following promulgation of a new or revised NAAQS, CAA section 
107(d) requires the EPA to designate areas of the country as 
attainment, nonattainment, or unclassifiable for that NAAQS. CAA 
section 107(d)(1)(A)(i)-(iii) defines: (i) nonattainment, as any area 
that does not meet (or that contributes to ambient air quality in a 
nearby area that does not meet) the NAAQS; (ii) attainment, as any area 
(other than an area identified in clause (i)) that meets the NAAQS; and 
(iii) unclassifiable, as any area that cannot be classified on the 
basis of available information as meeting or not meeting the NAAQS. 
Generally, the designations process involves identifying areas of the 
country that either meet or do not meet the NAAQS, along with any 
nearby areas that are causing or contributing to NAAQS violations. The 
EPA calculates design values (DVs) at monitors to determine whether a 
given area has met the 2024 annual PM2.5 NAAQS, based on 
procedures outlined in 40 CFR part 50, Appendix N.\4\ The 2024 annual 
PM2.5 NAAQS is met at an ambient air quality monitoring site 
when the annual arithmetic mean, averaged over three years (i.e., the 
DV) is less than or equal to 9.0 [micro]g/m\3\.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \4\ In accordance with 40 CFR part 50 Appendix N, 
PM2.5 measurements from the primary monitor and suitable 
collocated PM2.5 Federal Reference Method (FRM) or 
Federal Equivalent Method (FEM) may be used in a ``combined site 
data record'' to establish a PM2.5 design value to 
determine whether the NAAQS is met or not met at a particular 
PM2.5 monitoring site. For final area designations, the 
EPA intends to rely on Air Quality System (AQS) data from 2023-2025.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    In accordance with the statutory timeline prescribed under CAA 
section 107(d)(1)(A), Governors of each State or territory were 
required to submit a list of initial designations for all areas of 
their State or territory by February 7, 2025. Though not required, 
Tribal governments were encouraged to submit initial designations for 
their respective areas of Indian Country.\5\ These submissions 
generally relied on the 2021-2023 DVs and other information relevant to 
that time period. When promulgating final designations, the EPA will 
use the most recently available ambient air monitoring data and other 
information. The EPA intends to rely on DVs from the 2023-2025 period 
for promulgating final area designations.

finalizing designations, including instances where the State, 
territory, or Tribe declined to submit an initial area designation. The 
EPA's 120-day letters requested that the States provide additional 
information to the Agency within 30 days of receipt of the letters.

III. The EPA's Intended Approach to Area Designations

A. What is the EPA's intended approach for area designations?

    Consistent with CAA section 107(d)(1), the EPA has conducted an 
evaluation of the most recent and relevant air quality information 
available. This includes evaluation of the complete and certified 
PM2.5 ambient air quality monitoring data, including the 
2021-2023 DVs on which States, the District of Columbia, territories, 
and Tribes, based their initial designation submissions. Additionally, 
the EPA evaluated and utilized the most recent complete and certified 
PM2.5 air quality monitoring data, including the 2023-2025 
DVs, and other available technical information pertinent to these 
designations. Furthermore, the EPA considered the information submitted 
from States, the District of Columbia, territories, and Tribes in their 
initial area designations.
    On or about October 2, 2026, in accordance with CAA section 
107(d)(1)(B)(ii), the EPA completed its obligation to notify States of 
any intended modification to initial designations no later than 120-
days prior to promulgating final designations. The EPA notified all 
States, the District of Columbia, territories, and certain Tribes as to 
whether the EPA intends to modify aspects of the State's initial 
designations for the 2024 annual PM2.5 NAAQS. With respect 
to State initial designations that indicated ``attainment,'' and where 
the EPA functionally agrees with the State recommendation, the Agency 
intends to designate those States as ``attainment/unclassifiable.'' As 
a functional matter, there is no change for those States; the statutory 
and regulatory burdens of ``attainment'' and ``attainment/
unclassifiable'' are the same.
    Information may be submitted to the EPA's public docket regarding 
these intended designations at EPA-HQ-OAR-2024-0078, available at 
www.regulations.gov. Pursuant to CAA section 107(d)(1)(B) and following 
consideration of any additional information the EPA receives, the 
Agency anticipates completing final designations consistent with CAA 
requirements and in a timely manner commensurate with any applicable 
court order. The EPA is available to assist States, the District of 
Columbia, territories, and Tribes with any outstanding matters 
concerning appropriate designations for all remaining areas.

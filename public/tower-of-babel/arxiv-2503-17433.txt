Congruences on posets, relatively pseudocomplemented and Boolean posets
Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
The aim of the present paper is to extend the concept of a congruence from lattices to posets. We use an approach different from that used by the first author and V. Snášel in [ 6 ] . By using our definition we show that congruence classes are convex. If the poset in question satisfies the Ascending Chain Condition as well as the Descending Chain Condition, then these classes turn out to be intervals. If the poset has a top element 1 1 then the 1 1 -class of every congruence is a so-called strong filter. We study congruences on relatively pseudocomplemented posets which form a formalization of intuitionistic logic. For such posets we define so-called deductive systems and we show how they are connected with congruence kernels. We prove that every strong filter F F of a relatively pseudocomplemented poset induces a congruence having F F as its kernel. Finally, we consider Boolean posets which form a natural generalization of Boolean algebras. We show that congruences on Boolean posets in general do not share properties known from Boolean algebras, but congruence kernels of Boolean posets still have some interesting properties.

Introduction

Congruences form a useful tool for studying the structure of lattices. They enable the construction of a quotient lattice and, moreover, they classify certain classes of lattices. For example, a lattice is distributive if and only if every of its filters is a class of at least one congruence, and if a lattice is relatively complemented then every of its filters is a kernel of at most one congruence. Consequently, every filter of a Boolean algebra is the kernel of exactly one congruence.

Bounded posets form a generalization of lattices. In particular, this analogy becomes apparent when the poset in question satisfies both the Ascending Chain Condition and the Descending Chain Condition (ACC and DCC, for short). In such a case, the sets
\Max L(x,y)
of all maximal elements below
x
and
y
, and
\Min U(x,y)
of all minimal elements over
x
and
y
are not empty, consist of mutually incomparable elements and can be used for replacing the operations
\wedge
(i.e. infimum) and
\vee
(i.e. supremum) in lattices, respectively. We can ask if it is possible to generalize the concept of a congruence from lattices to bounded posets in some natural way in order to get results for bounded posets similar to those known for lattices. It is worth noticing that congruences on posets were already investigated by the first author and V. Snášel in
[
6
]
, however, in a different way. Namely, in
[
6
]
congruences on posets were defined as equivalence relations induced by so-called
LU
-morphisms. In the present paper we use another approach. We define congruences as equivalence relations compatible with the binary operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
mentioned above. This means that in case of lattices these congruences coincide with lattice congruences. Moreover, every class of such a congruence is a convex subset and, if the poset in question satisfies both the ACC and the DCC, in particular if it is finite, then every of its classes is an interval, and these classes can be ordered by their upper (or equivalently lower) bounds. Hence, the quotient poset can be embedded into the poset itself.

The second reason of our new definition is that this concept of congruence can be extended to posets with additional operations. In our paper we investigate congruences in relatively pseudocomplemented posets and in Boolean posets. This is motivated by the fact that relatively pseudocomplemented posets serve as an algebraic axiomatization of intuitionistic logic, see e.g.
[
2
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
10
]
, and Boolean posets are a natural generalization of Boolean algebras that serve as an algebraic axiomatization of classical logic. One can compare our results with those obtained for relatively pseudocomplemented semilattices in
[
3
]
and
[
4
]
. For relatively pseudocomplemented posets we define so-called deductive systems. We show that such systems are filters, and every strong filter
F
induces a congruence whose kernel is just
F
.

A rather different situation appears for Boolean posets. Although Boolean posets are a natural generalization of Boolean algebras, congruences on them do not share known congruence conditions valid for Boolean algebras. Namely, congruences on Boolean posets need neither be permutable, nor regular, nor uniform. Moreover, not every filter of a Boolean poset is the kernel of some congruence. Further, the congruence lattice of a Boolean poset need neither be distributive nor at least modular. However, we prove certain relationships between filters and congruences on Booelan posets. Moreover, every reflexive binary relation on a Boolean poset compatible with the binary operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
turns out to be a congruence.

Let

All posets considered in this paper are assumed to be non-empty.

Recall that a poset is said to satisfy the Ascending Chain Condition (ACC, for short) or the Descending Chain Condition (DCC, for short) if it contains no infinite ascending or descending chain, respectively. Of course, every finite poset satisfies both the ACC and the DCC.

On any set

3
Congruences on posets

Definition 3.1
.

For a poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
we will call an equivalence relation on
P

\Max L

\Min U

\mathbf{P}
. Let
\Con\mathbf{P}

\mathbf{P}

\Theta

Obviously, if a binary relation on
P
is compatible with the binary operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
then it is also compatible with the binary operators
L
and
U

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be a poset,
\Theta,\Phi\in\Con\mathbf{P}
,
(a,b)\in\Theta
and
c,d\in P
. Then the following holds:

(i)

There exists some
c\in\Max L(a,b)
and some
d\in\Min U(a,b)
with

,

(ii)

[a]\Theta
is convex,

(iii)

(c,d)\in\Theta
if and only if there exists some
(e,f)\in\Theta
with
c,d\in[e,f]
,

(iv)

\Theta=\Phi
if and only if
\Theta\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}=\Phi\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}
.

Proof.

(i)

Since
\Theta
is compatible with the binary operators
\Max L
and
\Min U
we have

\displaystyle\big(a\times\Max L(a,b)\big)\cap\Theta

\displaystyle=\big(\Max L(a,a)\times\Max L(a,b)\big)\cap\Theta\neq\emptyset,

\displaystyle\big(a\times\Min U(a,b)\big)\cap\Theta

\displaystyle=\big(\Min U(a,a)\times\Min U(a,b)\big)\cap\Theta\neq\emptyset

and hence there exists some

(c,d)\in\Max L(a,b)\times\Min U(a,b)

with
(a,c),(a,d)\in\Theta
. This shows

(c,d)\in\big(\Max L(a,b)\times\Min U(a,b)\big)\cap([a]\Theta)^{2}

and hence

\big(\Max L(a,b)\times\Min U(a,b)\big)\cap([a]\Theta)^{2}\neq\emptyset.

(ii)

Assume
a\leq b
and
e\in[a,b]
. Since
\Theta
is compatible with the binary operator
\Max L
we have

\{(a,e)\}\cap\Theta=\big(\Max L(a,e)\times\Max L(b,e)\big)\cap\Theta\neq\emptyset

and hence

[a]\Theta
.

(iii)

This follows from (i) and (ii).

(iv)

If
\Theta\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}=\Phi\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}
then according to (iii) the following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(c,d)

\displaystyle\in\Theta,

\displaystyle\text{there exists some }(e,f)\in\Theta\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}\text{ with }(c,d)

\displaystyle\in[e,f],

\displaystyle\text{there exists some }(e,f)\in\Phi\cap\{(x,y)\in P^{2}\mid x\leq y\}\text{ with }(c,d)

\displaystyle\in[e,f],

\displaystyle(c,d)

\displaystyle\in\Phi.

∎

It is worth noticing that by (iv) of Theorem
3.2
, every congruence on a poset is fully determined by its couples of comparable elements.

We are going to show that in a poset satisfying the ACC and the DCC, in particular in every finite poset, every congruence class is a closed interval.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)
be poset satisfying the
ACC
and the
DCC
,
a\in P
and
\Theta\in\Con\mathbf{P}
. Then
[a]\Theta
is a closed interval.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the DCC and the ACC,
[a]\Theta
has some minimal element
b
and some maximal element

. Since
[a]\Theta
is convex we have
[b,c]\subseteq[a]\Theta
. Now let

. Because of Theorem
3.2
(i) there exists some
e\in\Max L(b,d)
and some
f\in\Min U(b,d)
with

. Since
e\in[a]\Theta
,
e\leq b
and

is a minimal element of
[a]\Theta
we conclude

. This shows that
b
is the smallest element of
[a]\Theta
. Dually we obtain that

is the greatest element of
[a]\Theta
and hence
[a]\Theta\subseteq[b,c]
. Altogether, this yields

.
∎

Let

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq)

Consider the poset from Example

(ii)

follows directly from the definition of

It is well known that in congruence permutable varieties of algebras there exists a so-called Malcev term, i.e. a ternary term

satisfying the identities

t(x,x,z)\approx z\text{ and }t(x,z,z)\approx x.

Of course, we cannot expect that such a term exists also in relatively pseudocomplemented posets but, surprisingly, we are able to derive a ternary operator sharing similar properties.

Let
P
be a non-empty set. We call a ternary operator
T(x,y,z)
on

if it satisfies the identities

T(x,x,z)\approx z\text{ and }T(x,z,z)\approx x.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(P,\leq,*)
be a relatively pseudocomplemented poset satisfying the
ACC

(1)

T(x,y,z):=\Max L\big((x*y)*z,(z*y)*x\big)
for all
x,y,z\in P

Then

is a Malcev operator.

Proof.

For

according to Lemma
4.2
(i), (ii) and (iv) we have

\displaystyle T(a,a,c)

\displaystyle=\Max L\big((a*a)*c,(c*a)*a\big)=\Max L\big(1*c,(c*a)*a\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\big(c,(c*a)*a\big)=\Max L(c)=c,

\displaystyle T(a,c,c)

\displaystyle=\Max L\big((a*c)*c,(c*c)*a\big)=\Max L\big((a*c)*c,1*a\big)=

\displaystyle=\Max L\big((a*c)*c,a\big)=\Max L(a)=a.

∎

5
Boolean posets

Now we turn our attention to another sort of posets generalizing Boolean algebras, namely the so-called Boolean posets.

Recall that a
poset

(P,\leq)
is called
distributive
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent identities:

\displaystyle L\big(U(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx LU\big(L(x,z),L(y,z)\big),

\displaystyle UL\big(U(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx U\big(L(x,z),L(y,z)\big),

\displaystyle U\big(L(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx UL\big(U(x,z),U(y,z)\big),

\displaystyle LU\big(L(x,y),z\big)

\displaystyle\approx L\big(U(x,z),U(y,z)\big).

A unary operation
′
on a bounded poset
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,0,1)
is called a
complementation
on
\mathbf{P}
if
\mathbf{P}
satisfies the identities

U(x,x^{\prime})\approx 1\text{ and }L(x,x^{\prime})\approx 0.

In this case,
(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
is called a
complemented poset
. A distributive complemented poset is called a
Boolean poset
.

It is well-known that a distributive poset can have at most one complementation.

Lemma 5.4

Let

d\in U(b)\setminus[a,1]

c\vee d=1

[a,1]
is not a congruence kernel of
\mathbf{P}

Proof.

(i)

Suppose there exists some
b\in P

c\in\Max L(a,b)

b\vee d=1

Remark 5.6

Although Boolean algebras are congruence permutable, congruence regular and congruence uniform, the same does not hold for Boolean posets. Consider the Boolean poset

from Example

\mathbf{P}
is not congruence permutable since

and

satisfying

and
(x,b^{\prime})\in\Theta_{1}
and therefore

\mathbf{P}
is not congruence regular because e.g. the congruences

and

have the class

is a kernel of some congruence on

as shown after the proof of Lemma

Now we show connections between a congruence
\Theta
on a Boolean poset and its kernel. As mentioned in Theorem

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathbf{P}=(P,\leq,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a Boolean poset,
a,b\in P
and
\Theta\in\Con\mathbf{P}
. Then the following holds:

(i)

If
(a,b)\in\Theta
then
\Min U(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta\neq\emptyset
,

(ii)

if
a\leq b
and
LU(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta\neq\emptyset
then
(a,b)\in\Theta
,

(iii)

if
a\leq b
and
a\vee b^{\prime}
is defined then
(a,b)\in\Theta
if and only if
a\vee b^{\prime}\in[1]\Theta
.

Proof.

(i)

If
(a,b)\in\Theta
then because of
(b^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\Theta
and
\Min U(b,b^{\prime})=1
there exists some
c\in\Min U(a,b^{\prime})
with
(c,1)\in\Theta
, i.e.
c\in\Min U(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta
and hence
\Min U(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

Since
LU(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta\neq\emptyset
there exists some
d\in LU(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta
. Because
(d,1),(b,b)

\in\Theta
and
\Max L(1,b)=b
there exists some

with
(e,b)\in\Theta
. Now

e\in L(d,b)\subseteq L\big(U(a,b^{\prime}),b\big)=LU\big(L(a,b),L(b^{\prime},b)\big)=LU\big(L(a),0\big)=L(a)

and hence

. Together we obtain
e\leq a\leq b
which together with
(e,b)\in\Theta
implies
(a,b)\in\Theta
according to Theorem
3.2
(ii).

(iii)

Assume
a\leq b
and
a\vee b^{\prime}
is defined. Then
\Min U(a,b^{\prime})=a\vee b^{\prime}
and
LU(a,b^{\prime})=[0,a\vee b^{\prime}]
. Since
[1]\Theta
is convex according to (ii) of Theorem

, we have
LU(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Theta\neq\emptyset
if and only if
a\vee b^{\prime}\in[1]\Theta
. Now (iii) follows from (i) and (ii).

.

Moreover, suppose
[1]\Theta=[1]\Phi
. Then
\Theta=\Phi
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in P
with
a\leq b
and
\Psi\in\{\Theta,\Phi\}
. Then, by (2) and Theorem
5.7
,
(a,b)\in\Psi
if and only if
LU(a,b^{\prime})\cap[1]\Psi\neq\emptyset
. Since
[1]\Theta=[1]\Phi
we obtain
(a,b)\in\Theta
if and only if
(a,b)\in\Phi
. Now
\Theta=\Phi
follows from (iv) of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

The Cloven Traveling Salesman: Cycle Covers and the Integrality Gap of Small ATSP Instances
Alessandro Sosso, Ambrogio Maria Bernardelli, Stefano Gualandi

Abstract
This work proposes a novel enumeration algorithm for computing the integrality gap of small instances of the subtour elimination formulation for the Asymmetric Traveling Salesman Problem ( atsp ). The core idea is to enumerate pairs of vertex-disjoint cycle covers that can be filtered and mapped to half-integer vertices of the subtour elimination polytope. The two-cycle covers are encoded as lexicographically ordered partitions of n n numbers, with an encoding that prevents the generation of several isomorphic vertices. However, since not every cycle cover pair can be mapped to a vertex of the subtour elimination polytope, we have designed an efficient property-checking procedure to control whether a given point is a vertex of the asymmetric subtour elimination polytope. The proposed approach turns upside down the algorithms presented in the literature that first generate every possible vertex and later filter isomorphic vertices. With our approach, we can replicate state-of-the-art results for n ≤ 9 n\leq 9 in a tiny fraction of time, and we compute for the first time the exact integrality gap of half-integer vertices of the asymmetric subtour elimination polytope for n = 10 , 11 , 12 n=10,11,12 .



Keywords:
Asymmetric Traveling Salesman Problem, Integrality Gap, Half-integer vertices, Integer programming

1
Introduction

The Traveling Salesman Problem (
tsp
) is a long-established combinatorial
optimization problem consisting of finding the shortest tour visiting every node in a given edge-weighted graph
[
cook2011book
]
.
Given
n
nodes and arc costs
c_{ij}
of traveling from node
i
to node
j
, we focus on
metric
costs, which are costs that obey the triangle inequality
c_{ik}\leq c_{ij}+c_{jk}
, for all
i,j,k
.
If the costs are also symmetric, we have the Symmetric TSP (
stsp
); otherwise, if we do not require
c_{ij}=c_{ji}
, we deal with the Asymmetric TSP (
atsp
).

Among the several algorithms designed over time to solve the

tsp
, a key method was developed by Dantzig, Fulkerson, and Johnson
[
dantzig1954solution
]
, who introduced a fundamental formulation of the
tsp
as an Integer Linear Problem (ILP).
Despite considerable achievements in improving the
tsp
algorithms, the inherent difficulty of finding an efficient polynomial time algorithm led many efforts to be devoted to finding algorithms that could provide approximate solutions in polynomial time, both for the symmetric
[
christofides1976worst
,
serdjukov1978some
]
and for the asymmetric case
[
asadpour2017log
,
frieze1982onthe
,
svensson2020constant
,
traub2020approximation
,
traub2022improved
]
.
In the context of the ILP formulation, an approximating algorithm can be directly defined by considering the linear relaxation of the
tsp
, that is the non-integer linear problem obtained by dropping the integrality requirement from the
tsp
formulation; we refer to the resulting problem as the Subtour Elimination Problem (
sep
).
A natural question that follows from the definition of any approximation algorithm is whether there are any guarantees on its accuracy, interpreted as the distance of the approximate solution from the exact solution.
The accuracy measure of the LP relaxation is expressed by the integrality gap, defined as the ratio between the optimal value of the integer problem and its natural linear relaxation (e.g., see
[
conforti2014
]
).

While the integrality gap of the
tsp
is known to be unbounded in the general case, since instances achieving any arbitrary gap can be constructed
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
, §4.2]
, the computation of the maximum integrality gap when considering only metric weights is an open question, as only lower and upper bounds have been proved.
Structural analyses of the subtour LP, such as those by Carr and Vempala
[
carr2004onthe
]
, have identified families of instances where the gap can be tightly characterized, helping to formalize how the LP behaves across different classes of graphs.
For the metric
stsp
, the integrality gap of the subtour LP has long been known to be at most
\frac{3}{2}(e.g.,see
[
wolsey1980heuristic
,
shmoys1990analyzing
]
), and no instances with a gap of
\frac{4}{3}
or greater have been found. Recently, Karlin et al.
[
karlin2022slightly
]
showed that the integrality gap is strictly less than
\frac{3}{2}
, giving the first improvement on this bound in decades.
In the asymmetric case, only a large upper bound of 22 is known
[
traub2022improved
]
, while the best lower bound has been discovered by Charikar, Goemans, and Karloff
[
charikar2004onthe
]
and independently by Elliott-Magwood
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
]
with a family of weighted graphs with an integrality gap tending to
2
.

The computation of the integrality gap for small
tsp
instances (i.e., with less than 15 nodes), is of interest since it might provide useful insights into the structure of the instances yielding a high integrality gap.
As the maximal integrality gap cannot be computed directly since it would require computing the gap for infinite instances of the
tsp
, indirect approaches are to be employed. One general framework for analyzing integrality gaps is the fractional decomposition tree method introduced by Carr et al.
[
carr2023fractional
]
, which provides theoretical bounds by decomposing fractional solutions. In this work, however, we rely on a more computationally driven strategy, as exhibited in
[
benoit2008finding
,
boyd2002finding
,
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
,
vercesi2023generation
]
, where an auxiliary LP problem, derived from the dual of the
sep
, is leveraged.
Such auxiliary
gap
problem takes as input a feasible solution to the
sep
, and by calculating its optimum on all vertices of the
sep
polytope for a given
n
, the maximal value
\text{Gap}_{n}
of the integrality gap over all
n
-nodes
tsp
instances can be obtained.
This approach is not exclusive to the TSP problem, as a similar methodology was recently employed to compute the integrality gap of small metric Steiner Tree problems
[
bernardelli2024integralitygapcompletemetric
]
, thereby highlighting the generalizability of the approach.

The goal of this work is to revisit and extend the approach presented in
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
]
to compute for the first time the exact
\text{Gap}_{n}
for
n=10,11,12
, for all
half-integer
extreme points of
atsp
, that are those vertices with component values in
\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1\}
.
Notice that in
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
]
and in the previous work
[
boyd2005computing
]
, the authors could compute
\text{Gap}_{n}
for
n\leq 7
for any type of vertex via exhaustive generation of all the vertices of
asep
.
For
n=8,9
, however, the number of vertices was too large for their extensive computation, and the authors focused on computing the integrality gap of all
half-integer
extreme points.
For
n>9
, no form of complete enumeration was computationally practicable, but the authors nonetheless provided lower bounds on the value of
\text{Gap}_{n}
by concentrating on only a few specific instances.
To the best of our knowledge, these constitute the most advanced results to date on the exact computation of the maximum
atsp
integrality gap for half-integer vertices.

Our contributions.
This paper exploits a structural characterization of half-integer vertices of
atsp
, showing that each such vertex can be expressed as the average of two vertex-disjoint cycle covers (Theorem
2
).
While related decomposition results are implicit in earlier work on cycle-cover based relaxations (e.g., see
[
frieze1982worst
,
traub2024approximation
]
), to the best of our knowledge, this precise formulation has not been stated before. We include the derivation in Section
2
, as it forms the basis of our enumeration algorithm.
This result allows us for an exhaustive and efficient generation of half-integer vertices as a weighted sum of two cycle covers. Note that throughout this paper, we will focus only on cycle covers that are vertex-disjoint, unless stated otherwise.
Differently from
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
]
, where the exhaustive computation of all of the vertices is carried out before evaluating the integrality gap of a subset of those vertices, we first generate a superset of the set of half-integer vertices, and then, we rule out all the unfeasible and non-extremal points.
The second key contribution of this work is given in Proposition
and Proposition
, which describe an efficient algorithm designed for the extremality check of candidate points.
Figure
1
shows the pipeline presented in this work for the computation of the integrality gap of small
atsp
instances.
Finally, we compute the integrality gap of all of the half-integer solutions for
n\leq 12
, computationally proving the conjecture of
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
]
about the maximum integrality gap of this class of solutions.

Outline.
The outline of this paper is as follows.
Section
2
presents the problem of computing the integrality gap of the
atsp
and derives the structural characterization of half-integer vertices that underpins our enumeration algorithm.
Section
3
and Section
4
detail exhaustive and efficient algorithms for generating all half-integer vertices for a fixed
n
.
In particular, while Section
3
exploits Theorem
2
for generating candidate points that could represent half-integer vertices, Section
4
presents an efficient procedure for checking whether a given candidate point is a basic feasible solution, leveraging the structure of the problem and designing a novel algorithm.
In Section
, we report our computational results on the complete enumeration of half-integer vertices for
n\leq 12
, and the evaluation of the integrality gap of each vertex.
We conclude the paper with a perspective on future work.

2
Gap problem and vertex characterization

2.1
The gap problem

Given a directed weighted graph
G=(V,E,\bm{c})
with
n=|V|
, the classical mixed integer linear programming formulation of the
atsp
uses 0–1 arc variables
x_{uv}
, with
(u,v)\in E
, to represent the characteristic vector of tours as follows:

(
atsp
)

min

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{uv\in E}c_{uv}x_{uv}

(1a)

s.t.

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(w\right)}x_{uv}=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall w\in V,

(1b)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{uv\in\text{$\delta^{-}$}\left(w\right)}x_{uv}=1

\displaystyle\quad\forall w\in V,

(1c)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(S\right)}x_{uv}\geq 1

\displaystyle\quad\forall S\in\mathcal{S}^{n},

(1d)

\displaystyle\textstyle x_{uv}\geq 0

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\in E,

(1e)

\displaystyle\textstyle x_{uv}\in\mathbb{Z}

\displaystyle\quad\forall uv\in E,

(1f)

with
\mathcal{S}^{n}\coloneqq\left\{S\subset V\mid 2\leq\left|S\right|\leq n-2\right\}
.
The resulting LP relaxation of
atsp
is the Asymmetric Subtour Elimination Problem (
asep
).
Note that any instance and solution of both
atsp
and
asep
is determined by the vector of costs
\bm{c}
, therefore we write the optimal value as
\mathop{\textrm{{atsp}}}\left(\bm{c}\right)
,
\mathop{\textrm{{asep}}}\left(\bm{c}\right)
, respectively.
Hence, we denote by
\mathfrak{P}^{n}
the polytope that represents the region of the feasible solutions to the
asep
for a (complete) digraph on
n
vertices
\mathcal{K}_{n}=(V^{n},E^{n})
, that is

\mathfrak{P}^{n}\coloneqq\left\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;\middle|\;\eqref{eq:out-degree-contstr},\eqref{eq:in-degree-contstraint},\eqref{eq:subtour-elimination-contstraint},\eqref{eq:bound-contstraint}\right\}
,
where
m\coloneqq\left|E^{n}\right|
.
We also denote the (finite) set of extreme points of
\mathfrak{P}^{n}
by
\mathfrak{X}^{n}
.

For the
atsp
with a non-negative cost
\bm{c}\neq\bm{0}
, the integrality gap of the
asep
relaxation of
atsp
is defined as

\mathop{\text{Gap}}\left(\bm{c}\right)\coloneqq\mathop{\textrm{{atsp}}}\left(\bm{c}\right)/\mathop{\textrm{{asep}}}\left(\bm{c}\right)\geq 1
.
Since the integrality gap is unbounded in the general case, we restrict our discussion to metric
atsp
weights.
We introduce the following quantity, expressing the maximal value of the integrality gap over all
n
-nodes, metric, non-degenerate
atsp
instances as

\text{Gap}_{n}\coloneqq\max\left\{\mathop{\text{Gap}}\left(\bm{c}\right)\;\middle|\;\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\;\bm{c}\text{ metric},\;\bm{c}\neq\bm{0}\right\}
.

To compute
\text{Gap}_{n}
, the computational approach proposed in
[
benoit2008finding
,
boyd2002finding
,
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
,
vercesi2023generation
]
is followed, which relies on the solution of an auxiliary LP problem herein called
gap
.
For a given extreme point
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}
of
\mathfrak{P}^{n}
and for
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{uv}\coloneqq\left\{S\in\mathcal{S}^{n}\;\middle|\;uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(S\right)\right\}
, the problem
gap
, which can be derived from the dual of
atsp
, is defined by

(
gap
)

min

\displaystyle\sum_{uv\in E^{n}}\overline{x}_{uv}c_{uv},

s.t.

\displaystyle c_{uw}+c_{wv}-c_{uv}\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall uv\in E^{n},w\in V^{n}\setminus\left\{u,v\right\},

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{cost}}\left(T\right)\geq 1

\displaystyle\qquad\forall T\text{ tour in }\mathcal{K}_{n},

\displaystyle c_{uv}-y^{\mathrm{out}}_{u}-y^{\mathrm{in}}_{v}-{\textstyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{uv}}}d_{S}\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall uv\in E^{n}\setminus\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits_{E^{n}}\left(\overline{\bm{x}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{uv}-y^{\mathrm{out}}_{u}-y^{\mathrm{in}}_{v}-{\textstyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{n}_{uv}}}d_{S}=0

\displaystyle\forall uv\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits_{E^{n}}\left(\overline{\bm{x}}\right),

\displaystyle c_{uv}\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall uv\in E^{n},

\displaystyle d_{S}\geq 0

\displaystyle\forall S\in\mathcal{S}^{n}.

Following
[
boyd2005computing
]
, this allows us to exploit the relation

1/\text{Gap}_{n}=\min_{\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}}\Big\{\mathop{\textrm{{gap}}}\left(\overline{\bm{x}}\right)\Big\}
.

Even though this formulation makes computing the integrality gap possible, for relatively small values of
n
(e.g.,
8\leq n\leq 12
) its actual solution becomes computationally untractable due to the exponential number of variables and constraints in
gap
and for the number of points in
\mathfrak{X}^{n}
.
Hence, we need an efficient approach to reduce the number of candidate points from
\mathfrak{X}^{n}
to be considered to enable the solution of
gap
for larger values of
n
.

2.2
Structure of half-integer solutions

Every point in
\mathfrak{X}^{n}
is the solution to a system of linear equations with integer coefficients.
Hence, any
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}
has only rational components, that is
\mathfrak{X}^{n}\subset\mathbb{Q}^{m}\cap\left[0,1\right]^{m}
.
This allows us to write any
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}
as
\overline{\bm{x}}=\left(\nicefrac{{k_{1}}}{{\alpha}},\nicefrac{{k_{2}}}{{\alpha}},\ldots,\nicefrac{{k_{m}}}{{\alpha}}\right)

for some appropriate
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{+},k_{i}\in\mathbb{N},0\leq k_{i}\leq\alpha,i=\left\{1,\ldots,m\right\}
.
Let us define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}^{n}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathfrak{P}^{n}\cap\left\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}},\nicefrac{{2}}{{\alpha}},\ldots,1\right\}^{m},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathfrak{X}^{n}\cap\left\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{\alpha}},\nicefrac{{2}}{{\alpha}},\ldots,1\right\}^{m}=\mathfrak{X}^{n}\cap\mathfrak{P}^{n}_{\alpha},

for which
\mathfrak{X}^{n}=\bigcup_{\alpha=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{\alpha}

holds.
Herein, we focus on the case
\alpha=2
, corresponding to half-integer vertices.
This choice is motivated by computational evidence, as this type of vertices achieve the highest known integrality gaps for those
n
for which the exact
\text{Gap}_{n}
has been computed over all
\mathfrak{X}^{n}

[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
, §4]
.

Definition 1

(Half-integer and pure half-integer extreme points)
.

Let
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}
. We say that
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}
is a
half-integer
extreme point if
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}_{2}
, that is if the components of
\overline{\bm{x}}
take value in the set
\left\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}},1\right\}
. We say that
\overline{\bm{x}}
is a
pure half-integer
extreme point if
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}_{2}\cap\left\{0,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\right\}^{m}
.

A result by Elliott-Magwood
[
elliott-magwood2008theintegrality
, §4.6]
proves that to compute the gap over all half-integer vertices it is enough to consider only pure half-integers, which greatly reduces the number of instances that need to be generated.

In the remainder of this section, we characterize half-integer solutions through two theoretical results, linking the points of
\mathfrak{X}_{2}
to vertex-disjoint cycle covers.
Let us first focus on the vectors associated with vertex-disjoint cycle covers in the complete graph
\mathcal{K}_{n}
, and let us call
\mathfrak{C}^{n}\subset\left\{0,1\right\}^{m}
the set of these vectors.
This vector space is characterized by the same constraints of the
atsp
without the subtour elimination constraints (
1d
),

\mathfrak{C}^{n}=\left\{\overline{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\;\middle|\;\eqref{eq:out-degree-contstr},\eqref{eq:in-degree-contstraint},\eqref{eq:bound-contstraint},\eqref{eq:integrality-contstraint}\right\}
.
By additionally dropping the integrality constraints (
1f
), the set of extreme points of the resulting polytope
\mathfrak{Q}^{n}
is equivalent to
\mathfrak{C}^{n}
, since Birkhoff showed in
[
birkhoff1946tresobservaciones
]
how such extreme points are always integral.
In Theorem 1, we claim that for every
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{2}
two vertex-disjoint cycle covers
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1},\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}
exist such that
\overline{\bm{x}}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}
.
To prove it, we first need to introduce an auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{2}
and let
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}
be the characteristic vector of a cycle cover of the graph associated with
\overline{\bm{x}}
.
If
\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}\coloneqq 2\overline{\bm{x}}-\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}
, then
\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}
is a cover of the
\overline{\bm{x}}
-associated graph implies that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits_{E^{n}}(\overline{\bm{y}}^{1})\subseteq\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits_{E^{n}}(\overline{\bm{x}})
,
which in turn means that
\bm{0}\leq\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}\leq 2\overline{\bm{x}}

and
\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}=2\overline{\bm{x}}-\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}\geq\bm{0}
. The boundary
constraints (
1e
) are thus satisfied by
\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}
,
which also satisfies the out-degree constraints (
1b
)
since
\forall w\in V^{n}

\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathclap{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(w\right)}\hskip 8.19447pt}\overline{y}_{uv}^{2}=\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathclap{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(w\right)}\hskip 8.19447pt}\left(2\overline{x}_{uv}-\overline{y}_{uv}^{1}\right)=2\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathclap{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(w\right)}\hskip 8.19447pt}\overline{x}_{uv}-\sum_{\hskip 8.19447pt\mathclap{uv\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(w\right)}\hskip 8.19447pt}\overline{y}_{uv}^{1}=2\cdot 1-1=1.

Analogously for the in-degree constraints (
1c
).
The integrality constraints (
1f
) are
trivially satisfied since both
2\overline{\bm{x}}
and
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}
are
integral.
∎

The proof of Theorem
2
relies on a result by Hall
[
hall1935onrepresentatives
]
known in the literature as Hall’s Marriage Theorem, which provides a sufficient and necessary condition for the existence of a perfect matching on a bipartite graph.

Theorem 1

(Hall’s Marriage Theorem)
.

There exists a
V_{1}
-perfect matching
on a bipartite graph
\mathcal{G}=\left(V,E\right)
with
V_{1},V_{2}\subset V

as bipartite sets if and only if

\left|S\right|\leq\left|N_{S}\right|,\;\forall S\in\mathcal{P}\left(V_{1}\right)
,
where
N_{S}\coloneqq{\textstyle\bigcup_{s\in S}}\delta\left(s\right)
is the set of neighbors for any
S\subseteq V_{1}
.

Theorem 2

(Structure of half-integer vertices)
.

For each half-integer vertex of
asep

\overline{\bm{x}}
, there exist two vertex-disjoint cycle covers
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1},\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}
such that
\overline{\bm{x}}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}
.

Proof.

First, we have to prove that, for each
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{2}
,
there exist
\overline{\bm{y}}^{1},\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}
such that
\overline{\bm{x}}=\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{1}+\frac{1}{2}\overline{\bm{y}}^{2}
.
Thanks to Lemma
1
, it suffices to show that a cycle cover of the graph associated with
\overline{\bm{x}}
exists. This is equivalent to the existence of a perfect matching on the bipartite graph

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(V^{n}\times\left\{0,1\right\},\widetilde{E}_{\overline{\bm{x}}}^{n}\right),

\displaystyle\text{with}\quad\widetilde{E}_{\overline{\bm{x}}}^{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left\{\left(u,0\right)\left(v,1\right)\mid uv\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits_{E^{n}}\left(\overline{\bm{x}}\right)\right\}.

Hall’s Marriage Theorem
1
grants the existence of a
(V^{n}\times\{0\})
-perfect matching if
\big|\overline{S}\big|\leq\big|N_{\overline{S}}\big|

for any
\overline{S}\in\mathcal{P}\left(V^{n}\times\{0\}\right)
.
Let
\overline{S}\coloneqq S\times\{0\}
with
S\subseteq V^{n}
.
We see that

{\textstyle\bigcup_{u\in\overline{S}}}\left\{uv\in\widetilde{E}_{\overline{\bm{x}}}^{n}\;\middle|\;v\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(u\right)\right\}\subseteq{\textstyle\bigcup_{v^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{S}}}}\left\{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}\in\widetilde{E}_{\overline{\bm{x}}}^{n}\;\middle|\;u^{\prime}\in\text{$\delta^{-}$}\left(v^{\prime}\right)\right\},

from which it follows

\big|\overline{S}\big|=\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}1=\sum_{u\in\overline{S}}\Big(\sum_{\hskip 12.2917pt\mathclap{v\in\text{$\delta^{+}$}\left(u\right)}\hskip 12.2917pt}\overline{x}_{uv}\Big)\leq\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{S}}}\Big(\sum_{\hskip 12.2917pt\mathclap{u^{\prime}\in\text{$\delta^{-}$}\left(v^{\prime}\right)}\hskip 12.2917pt}\overline{x}_{u^{\prime}v^{\prime}}\Big)=\sum_{v^{\prime}\in N_{\overline{S}}}1=\left|N_{\overline{S}}\right|,

that is,
\big|\overline{S}\big|\leq\big|N_{\overline{S}}\big|
, proving the existence of the sought
(V^{n}\times\{0\})
-perfect matching. But since
\big|V^{n}\times\{0\}\big|=\big|V^{n}\times\{1\}\big|=\big|V^{n}\big|
, any such
(V^{n}\times\{0\})
-perfect matching is also a perfect matching, concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

Lemma
1
and Theorem
2
do not exploit that
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{2}
satisfies the subtour elimination constraints (
1d
).
The results therefore also hold for any
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{Q}^{n}\cap\left\{0,1/2,1\right\}^{m}
, that is, the half-integer solutions of the remaining constraints (
1b
), (
1c
), and (
1e
).
This also means that the results of Theorem
2
can be extended via induction to
\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\geq 3
, proving that the elements
\overline{\bm{x}}\in\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{\alpha}
are expressible as
\overline{\bm{x}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{\alpha}\overline{\bm{y}}^{i}
with
\overline{\bm{y}}^{i}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n},\;i\in\left\{1,...,\alpha\right\}
. In fact, similarly to Lemma
1
we see that if a cycle cover
\overline{\bm{y}}^{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{C}^{n}
of
\mathcal{X}_{\overline{\bm{x}}}^{n}
exists then
\overline{\bm{x}}^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\left(\alpha\overline{\bm{x}}-\overline{\bm{y}}^{\alpha}\right)
belongs to
\mathfrak{Q}^{n}\cap\big\{0,\frac{1}{\alpha-1},\ldots,1\big\}^{m}
(but not to
\mathfrak{X}^{n}_{\alpha-1}
in general, as
\overline{\bm{x}}^{\prime}
may not satisfy the subtour elimination constraints). Hence from the inductive hypothesis we would have

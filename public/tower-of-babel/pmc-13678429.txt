One-Year Outcomes of Green Laser Endoscopic Cyclophotocoagulation and Factors Associated with Surgical Failure: A Retrospective Observational Study
Authors: Yoshiaki Takahashi, Shun Nakayama, Akira Imai, Junya Kitahara, Shinji Kakihara, Takao Hirano, Toshinori Murata
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeWe evaluated the 1-year outcomes of green laser endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation (ECP), a newly developed procedure approved in Japan.Patients and MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 29 eyes of 26 patients who underwent green laser ECP. Intraocular pressure (IOP), glaucoma medication burden, and laser flare were evaluated preoperatively and at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months postoperatively. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was used to estimate the 12-month success rate. Surgical success was defined as meeting all of the following criteria: IOP ≤21 mmHg, a ≥20% reduction from baseline, and no additional glaucoma surgery. Univariate and multivariable Cox proportional hazards analyses were performed to evaluate factors associated with surgical failure.ResultsThe mean IOP decreased from 23.4 ± 8.5 mmHg preoperatively to 17.4 ± 6.9, 16.9 ± 5.2, and 17.5 ± 7.3 mmHg at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively, respectively, with significant reductions maintained through 6 months (p < 0.01). However, IOP increased to 21.1 ± 7.9 and 22.3 ± 11.8 mmHg at 9 and 12 months, respectively, with no significant differences from baseline. The glaucoma medication score decreased significantly through 3 months postoperatively (p = 0.02) but returned to baseline levels thereafter. Laser flare increased significantly through 1 month postoperatively (p < 0.01) and decreased numerically through 3 months. The 12-month estimated success rate was 0.36. In multivariable Cox analysis, higher preoperative IOP was significantly associated with a greater risk of surgical failure (HR, 1.10; 95% CI, 1.05–1.15; p < 0.001), whereas age and postoperative laser flare were not significantly associated with failure.ConclusionGreen laser ECP significantly lowered IOP through 6 months postoperatively; however, the estimated 12-month surgical success rate was 36%. Optimization of laser parameters and treatment strategies may be needed to improve long-term outcomes.Keywords: glaucoma, glaucoma surgery, green laser endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation, laser flare

Introduction

Glaucoma is characterized by optic nerve damage with corresponding visual field defects. Lowering intraocular pressure (IOP) remains the mainstay of glaucoma management,1 and various treatment modalities, including topical medications, laser therapy, and surgical interventions, have been developed for this purpose. In recent years, glaucoma surgery has become less invasive,2 with many procedures performed through small incisions or involving the implantation of microdevices such as trabecular micro-bypass stents (iStent®) and subconjunctival microshunts (PRESERFLO MicroShunt).3–8 Despite this shift toward less invasive surgery, most approaches for lowering IOP primarily aim to enhance aqueous humor outflow. In contrast, relatively few methods are available to suppress aqueous humor production. Since the introduction of cyclodialysis in 1905, surgical approaches targeting the ciliary body have evolved to include cyclodiathermy and transscleral cyclophotocoagulation.9 These procedures involve non-visualized destruction of the ciliary body, resulting in inconsistent efficacy and a high incidence of complications due to excessive tissue damage.10 In 1992, Uram et al developed endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation (ECP), which combines an endoscope with an 810-nm diode laser.11 Endoscopic cyclophotocoagulation is currently indicated for eyes requiring reduction of aqueous humor production, including refractory glaucoma and selected cases undergoing combined cataract and glaucoma surgery. Unlike transscleral cyclophotocoagulation, ECP allows direct visualization of the ciliary processes and controlled laser application. It can also be performed through the same corneal incision during cataract surgery while preserving the conjunctival tissue.12 In contrast, micropulse transscleral cyclophotocoagulation is a non-incisional, transscleral procedure that is typically performed as a standalone treatment rather than in combination with cataract surgery. Because of the difficulty in accessing the specialized ECP device, the clinical use of conventional ECP has been relatively limited, and few studies have been conducted in Japan. To facilitate the clinical use of ECP in Japan, Tanito et al developed a 532-nm green laser ECP system and reported that its IOP-lowering effect may be comparable to conventional 810-nm diode laser ECP without serious postoperative complications.13 Owing to its shorter wavelength, the green laser has been hypothesized to produce more selective surface ablation of the ciliary epithelium while minimizing deeper tissue injury compared with the conventional diode laser; however, this hypothesis has not been clinically validated. Therefore, whether the use of a green laser results in reduced postoperative inflammation while maintaining IOP-lowering efficacy comparable to that of conventional diode laser ECP remains unclear.13 Objective assessment of postoperative intraocular inflammation is therefore important for characterizing green laser ECP. Laser flare photometry provides an objective quantitative assessment of postoperative intraocular inflammation and has been widely used to evaluate inflammation following glaucoma laser and surgical procedures.14 Green laser ECP was approved in Japan in 2024, and we began performing this procedure in December 2024. In the present study, we evaluated the 1-year efficacy and safety of green laser ECP, with particular attention to postoperative intraocular inflammation assessed by laser flare photometry and changes in IOP.

Methods

Ethical Approval

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Shinshu University School of Medicine (approval number: 5832; approved on April 25, 2023) and was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was not obtained because the study was retrospective. Patients were informed through the opt-out process.

Study Design and Patients

This retrospective study consecutively included 29 eyes of 26 patients who underwent green laser ECP at Shinshu University Hospital between December 2024 and September 2025. Green laser ECP was primarily performed in eyes with refractory glaucoma for which filtering surgery was considered unsuitable because of factors such as previous ocular surgery, ocular conditions, or anticipated difficulties with postoperative management. Eyes that had undergone incisional ocular surgery within 3 months before ECP or that could not be followed up for at least 3 months postoperatively were excluded. A history of ocular surgery performed more than 3 months before ECP was not considered an exclusion criterion. Clinical outcomes at each postoperative time point were analyzed using all available follow-up data; therefore, the number of analyzed eyes varied across postoperative visits.

Surgical Procedure

Topical anesthesia was administered with 4% lidocaine, with or without intracameral anesthesia using 1% lidocaine prepared by diluting 4% lidocaine with an ophthalmic irrigation solution (Opeguard MA, Senju Pharmaceutical Co. Ltd., Osaka, Japan). Side ports were created at the 3 and 9 o’clock positions, and an ophthalmic viscosurgical device (OVD), either 1% sodium hyaluronate (Hyaluronate Na 0.85 Ophthalmic Viscoelastic Substance 1%, Alcon Japan Ltd., Tokyo, Japan) or 2.3% sodium hyaluronate (Healon5, Johnson and Johnson Surgical Vision, Inc., Irvine, CA, USA), was injected into both the anterior and posterior chambers.

A combined endoscope, laser, and light-source probe was inserted through the side port, and the tip was advanced behind the iris to visualize the ciliary processes. Laser photocoagulation was performed with a power setting of 200 mW and a maximum duration of 2000 ms, with treatment titrated until the ciliary processes whitened. The appearance of bubbles was considered evidence of excessive coagulation, and the probe distance was adjusted accordingly. All procedures were performed by one of the three experienced glaucoma surgeons. Laser treatment was applied to approximately 360° of the ciliary processes in all eyes.

The OVD was removed using a bimanual irrigation/aspiration technique. Triamcinolone acetonide (40 mg/mL, 0.05 mL) was injected into the sub-Tenon space, and 0.5% moxifloxacin eye drops were instilled. Postoperatively, 1.5% levofloxacin and 0.1% betamethasone eye drops were prescribed for 3–6 months. Because all patients had previously undergone cataract surgery, ECP alone was performed in all cases.

Data Collection and Analysis

This was a retrospective observational study. The collected variables included patient characteristics, such as sex, age, glaucoma subtype [primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG), exfoliation glaucoma (XFG), neovascular glaucoma (NVG), and other glaucoma subtypes], and previous glaucoma surgery. IOP was measured using a Goldmann applanation tonometer, and laser flare was assessed using a laser flare meter (FM-600α; Kowa, Aichi, Japan).

IOP was measured preoperatively and at 1 day, 1 week, and 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months postoperatively. Glaucoma medication scores were assessed preoperatively and at 1, 3, 6, 9, and 12 months postoperatively, with one point assigned for each topical medication, two points for each fixed-combination agent, and two points for each oral medication. Laser flare was measured preoperatively and at 1 week and 1, 2, 3, 6, and 9 months postoperatively.

Survival analysis was conducted using the Kaplan–Meier method, with surgical success defined as meeting all of the following criteria: (1) IOP ≤21 mmHg at two consecutive visits; (2) a ≥20% reduction in IOP from preoperative levels; and (3) no additional incisional glaucoma surgery. As a sensitivity analysis, surgical success was additionally evaluated using a stricter IOP threshold of ≤15 mmHg, while retaining the other success criteria.

IOP, medication score, and laser flare measurements obtained after additional glaucoma surgery were excluded from the longitudinal analyses because these outcomes could no longer be attributed solely to the index ECP procedure. However, for eyes classified as surgical failures for reasons other than additional glaucoma surgery, subsequent available IOP, medication score, and laser flare measurements were retained in the longitudinal analyses.

Univariate Cox proportional hazards analyses were performed to evaluate the associations between surgical failure and age, postoperative laser flare at 1 month, and preoperative IOP. These variables were subsequently included in a multivariable Cox proportional hazards model. Robust standard errors clustered at the patient level were used to account for potential within-patient correlation between fellow eyes.

Statistical Analysis

A linear mixed model (LMM) was used to compare IOP, glaucoma medication score, and laser flare over the postoperative course, with p < 0.05 considered statistically significant. Analyses were performed using the “SciPy”, “statsmodels”, and “lifelines” modules of Python 3.12.

Results

Among the 26 enrolled patients, 14 were female, and 12 were male. The mean age was 71.8 ± 12.2 years. The mean number of laser shots was 105.3 ± 32.3. No intraoperative complications occurred during any of the procedures. The mean preoperative IOP was 23.4 ± 8.5 mmHg, the mean glaucoma medication score was 4.1 ± 1.4, and the mean laser flare value was 32.9 ± 27.7 pc/ms. Regarding glaucoma subtype, POAG was observed in 21 eyes, XFG in four eyes, NVG in three eyes, and developmental glaucoma in one eye (Table 1). Twenty eyes had undergone previous glaucoma surgery, including trabecular micro-bypass stents (n = 5), trabeculotomy (n = 10), trabeculectomy (n = 7), and Ahmed valve implantation (n = 6), with some eyes having undergone multiple procedures.

The mean preoperative IOP was 23.4 ± 8.5 mmHg. Postoperative IOP was 12.3 ± 5.4 mmHg at 1 week, 17.4 ± 6.9 mmHg at 1 month, 20.4 ± 9.2 mmHg at 2 months, 16.9 ± 5.2 mmHg at 3 months, and 17.5 ± 7.3 mmHg at 6 months. Relative to preoperative values, IOP was significantly reduced at 1, 3, and 6 months postoperatively (p < 0.01), but not at 2 months. However, IOP increased to 21.1 ± 7.9 mmHg at 9 months and 22.3 ± 11.8 mmHg at 12 months, with no significant difference from preoperative values (p = 0.32 and 0.37, respectively; LMM).

The glaucoma medication score was 4.1 ± 1.4 preoperatively and decreased significantly to 3.3 ± 1.5 and 3.7 ± 1.6 at 1 and 3 months postoperatively, respectively. However, no significant differences from the preoperative value were observed from 6 months onward.

The laser flare results are shown in Figure 3 and Table 4. The mean preoperative flare value was 32.9 ± 27.7 pc/ms. Postoperatively, flare values increased significantly from the preoperative value to 77.6 ± 47.7 pc/ms at 1 week and 58.6 ± 33.1 pc/ms at 1 month (p < 0.01). Thereafter, no significant differences were observed. Although no significant differences were detected at 2 and 3 months postoperatively, flare values decreased numerically from 1 week to 3 months. Kaplan–Meier survival analysis of all eyes demonstrated cumulative success rates of 0.59, 0.51, 0.51, and 0.36 at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months postoperatively, respectively (Figure 4). In the sensitivity analysis using the stricter IOP threshold of ≤15 mmHg, the cumulative success rates at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months were 0.41, 0.33, 0.28, and 0.14, respectively (Figure 5).

Univariate Cox proportional hazards analyses with robust standard errors clustered at the patient level were performed to evaluate whether age, preoperative IOP, and 1-month postoperative laser flare were associated with surgical failure. Age showed a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.97 (95% CI, 0.94–0.99; p = 0.01), preoperative IOP showed an HR of 1.08 (95% CI, 1.04–1.12; p < 0.001), and 1-month postoperative laser flare showed an HR of 1.01 (95% CI, 0.99–1.03; p = 0.28). Age and preoperative IOP were significantly associated with surgical failure, whereas 1-month postoperative laser flare was not. In the multivariable Cox proportional hazards model including age, preoperative IOP, and 1-month postoperative laser flare, preoperative IOP remained significantly associated with surgical failure (HR, 1.10; 95% CI, 1.05–1.15; p < 0.001), whereas age (HR, 0.99; 95% CI, 0.97–1.02; p = 0.68) and 1-month postoperative laser flare (HR, 1.01; 95% CI, 0.99–1.03; p = 0.32) were not significantly associated with surgical failure.

Discussion

Although diode laser ECP has been widely reported,15–18 studies on green laser ECP, as used in the present study, remain limited.13 In the present study, IOP was significantly reduced from preoperative values through 6 months postoperatively; however, no significant difference was observed at later time points. The 1-year success rate of the present cohort was 0.36. Previous studies of conventional diode laser ECP have demonstrated substantial IOP-lowering effects, although reported surgical outcomes have varied among studies.19–21 The populations and surgical settings of these studies differed substantially from those of the present study. In a study conducted in China, Fang et al evaluated 121 eyes with refractory glaucoma in a substantially younger population, including a large proportion of pediatric patients; the mean age was 25.9 years, and the mean preoperative IOP was 33.3 mmHg,19 compared with 71.8 years and 23.4 mmHg, respectively, in the present cohort. In contrast, Bolek et al evaluated 38 eyes from an older population in Poland, with a predominance of POAG and a mean preoperative IOP of 22.6 mmHg,21 characteristics that were more similar to those of the present cohort. However, fewer eyes had undergone previous glaucoma surgery in their study. The systematic review by Alasbali et al also demonstrated considerable heterogeneity among ECP studies in patient populations, surgical techniques, definitions of success, and follow-up periods.20 Therefore, although the success rate observed in the present study was numerically lower than that reported in some previous studies, direct comparisons should be interpreted with caution because of differences in patient characteristics, glaucoma type and severity, previous surgical history, surgical techniques, surgeon experience, definitions of surgical success, and follow-up protocols.

The most important difference between diode and green lasers is their wavelength: the diode laser operates at 810 nm, whereas the green laser operates at 532 nm. Laser energy is absorbed by melanin within the ciliary body, generating heat that induces tissue coagulation.22 Coagulation occurs at approximately 60 °C, whereas temperatures exceeding 100 °C lead to water vaporization and bubble formation (popping).23

Because the green laser has a shorter wavelength than the diode laser, its penetration depth is shallower, and its effect may therefore be limited to superficial coagulation of the ciliary processes. In addition, the shorter wavelength of the green laser may result in greater energy absorption at the tissue surface, which may increase the likelihood of bubble formation (popping) and limit effective coagulation of deeper tissue layers.

The relatively limited durability of IOP reduction observed in the present study may be related to laser treatment parameters. All eyes were treated using a fixed laser power of 200 mW, with each application continued until visible whitening of the ciliary process was observed. Because the actual duration of individual applications was not recorded, the total energy delivered per eye could not be calculated. Using lower power with a longer duration might allow heat to penetrate deeper tissue while avoiding excessive superficial coagulation; however, this possibility was not directly evaluated in the present study. Further studies are needed to optimize laser parameters, including power and application duration.

In our cohort, laser flare increased from a preoperative value of 32.9 ± 27.7 pc/ms to a peak value of 77.6 ± 47.7 pc/ms at 1 week postoperatively. Flare values remained significantly higher than preoperative levels through 1 month postoperatively, but no significant differences were observed thereafter. Laser flare values decreased numerically from 1 week to 3 months postoperatively.

This study included a relatively small number of eyes, and Cox proportional hazards analyses were limited to age, 1-month postoperative laser flare, and preoperative IOP. Higher preoperative IOP remained significantly associated with surgical failure in the multivariable analysis. Although age was associated with surgical failure in the univariate analysis, this association was not maintained after multivariable adjustment. One-month postoperative laser flare was not significantly associated with surgical outcomes following green laser ECP.

Previous studies on diode laser ECP have generally reported that age at the time of surgery does not significantly influence surgical outcomes.24,25 However, higher success rates with increasing age have also been suggested. Electron microscopic studies following cryogenic injury have demonstrated that the ciliary body can regenerate within approximately 4 weeks after injury,26 suggesting that aqueous humor production may recover over time. In these studies, regeneration of non-pigmented epithelial cells was reported to be more pronounced than that of pigmented epithelial cells.

Because shorter-wavelength green lasers may induce more superficial tissue damage, regeneration of the ciliary epithelium could potentially lead to recovery of aqueous humor production and subsequent elevation of IOP. Younger patients may also have greater regenerative capacity. Therefore, whether age-related differences in regenerative capacity influence the long-term efficacy of green laser ECP remains uncertain and warrants further investigation.

However, these proposed mechanisms remain hypothetical. The present study did not include histopathological or imaging evaluation of the ciliary processes, and therefore cannot determine whether differences in tissue penetration, coagulation depth, or subsequent ciliary epithelial regeneration contributed to the observed clinical outcomes.

Limitations

This study has some limitations. First, its retrospective single-center design, relatively small sample size, and limited number of surgical failure events may limit the generalizability of the findings, reduce statistical power, and restrict the number of variables that could be included in the multivariable analysis. Therefore, the results of the Cox proportional hazards analysis should be interpreted with caution. Second, the study population was heterogeneous in terms of glaucoma subtype and previous glaucoma surgery. Third, because surgical failure was defined partly by IOP criteria, the association between preoperative IOP and failure should be interpreted with caution. Fourth, although all procedures were performed by one of three experienced glaucoma surgeons with approximately 360° treatment, green laser ECP was newly introduced at our institution, and a potential learning-curve effect cannot be excluded. In addition, although laser power was fixed at 200 mW, the duration of individual laser applications and the exact duration of ECP treatment were not systematically recorded; therefore, the total laser energy delivered per eye and treatment duration could not be evaluated. These procedural factors may have influenced the surgical outcomes. Fifth, this study did not include a concurrent conventional diode laser ECP control group, precluding direct assessment of the relative efficacy of green laser ECP. Further direct comparative studies using comparable patient populations and standardized treatment protocols are needed. In addition, the present study was conducted at a single center in Japan, and the green laser ECP system currently has limited availability outside Japan. Therefore, differences in patient populations and clinical settings should be considered when generalizing the present findings to other countries. Further studies in different populations and clinical settings may help establish the broader applicability of green laser ECP.

Conclusions

Overall, green laser ECP significantly reduced IOP during the early postoperative period, although significant differences from preoperative IOP were not observed at later follow-up time points. The 1-year surgical success rate was 36%. Laser flare increased significantly through 1 month postoperatively and decreased numerically through 3 months. Further studies with larger populations and longer follow-up are needed to establish the long-term efficacy and safety of green laser ECP.

A prognostic model based on metabolic indicators for recurrent acute pancreatitis in hypertriglyceridemia patients: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Zhiguo Wang, Yongshuai Liu, Peixi Ren, Guanyu Zhu, Gaoyuan Zhang, Xin Zhang, Shanming Sun, Aiying Shi, Yun Lin, Hongmei Qu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypertriglyceridemic acute pancreatitis (HTG-AP) is associated with a high risk of recurrence. This study aimed to identify risk factors for HTG-AP recurrence and to develop a predictive model integrating metabolic and clinical indicators.Methods We retrospectively enrolled patients with HTG-AP treated at Weifang People’s Hospital between January 2019 and October 2023. Independent predictors of recurrence were identified using univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, and a predictive model was constructed based on significant variables. A nomogram was developed to visualize the model. Kaplan–Meier analysis was used to assess recurrence risk across different risk strata. Model performance was evaluated using the C-index, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Internal validation was performed using bootstrap resampling and repeated cross-validation.ResultsA total of 293 patients were included, of whom 63 (21.5%) developed recurrence within 2 years of follow-up. Multivariate analysis identified the visceral adipose tissue index (p = 0.001), triglyceride-glucose index (p < 0.001), smoking status (p = 0.013), and drinking status (p = 0.007) as independent predictors of recurrence. A prediction model was established based on these variables. Kaplan–Meier curves showed good discrimination between risk groups. The model achieved an area under the ROC curve of 0.927, which was significantly higher than that of any individual predictor. Calibration analysis showed good agreement, and DCA demonstrated favorable clinical utility.ConclusionThis prediction model showed high accuracy for early risk stratification of HTG-AP recurrence and may support individualized follow-up and intervention strategies.

Introduction

Acute pancreatitis (AP) is a frequently observed acute abdominal disorder characterized primarily by pancreatic tissue edema, fat necrosis, and localized or systemic inflammatory responses; the global incidence of AP is increasing every year [1]. While most patients achieve complete recovery after the initial episode, 17–35% of patients with AP may progress to recurrent acute pancreatitis (RAP). This can lead to chronic pancreatitis (CP) or even pancreatic cancer [2,3]. Even before imaging reveals structural changes that indicate CP, RAP causes significant recurrent pain, a decrease in the quality of life, and an increase in psychological burden [4,5]. Therefore, early identification and early intervention for the risk of recurrence of AP have significant clinical importance.

With the increase in the size of populations affected by metabolic syndrome and unhealthy lifestyles, hypertriglyceridemia-induced pancreatitis (HTG-AP) has emerged as a major cause of AP. HTG-AP has a more complex course, more clinical complications, and a significantly higher recurrence rate, ranging from 16.0 to 49.2% [6,7]. Although important large multicenter studies on HTG-AP recurrence exist, such as Ding et al.’s investigation of risk factors for recurrence, there remains a lack of clinical and metabolic index analyses [8]. Therefore, continued development of prediction models specifically targeting HTG-AP recurrence is necessary.

Metabolic abnormalities exert an important effect on the onset and recurrence of HTG-AP. Insulin resistance (IR), defined as a decrease in insulin sensitivity that impairs glucose uptake and utilization, is a key marker of systemic low-grade inflammation. The triglyceride-glucose index (TyG) is a reliable surrogate marker for assessing IR and is positively correlated with the recurrence of AP [9]. Obesity, particularly excessive visceral fat accumulation, accelerates the production of multiple inflammatory mediators (such as IL-6 or TNF-α), creating a persistent pro-inflammatory environment that exacerbates pancreatic injury and increases the risk of recurrence [10,11]. Given that CT scanning serves as the standard imaging modality for the initial diagnosis of AP, its precise measurement of visceral adipose tissue (VAT) offers both feasibility and objectivity. The visceral adipose tissue index (VATI) is closely associated with the severity of AP and the risk of recurrence [12]. Our group previously reported a nomogram incorporating metabolic indices for predicting the severity of HTG-AP [13]. However, that study focused on disease severity during the index hospitalization rather than recurrence after the first episode. Therefore, no study has yet incorporated both the VATI and TyG into the same predictive system to construct a dedicated risk model for the recurrence of HTG-AP.

In this study, we analyzed how metabolic indicators and clinical characteristics affect the recurrence of HTG-AP, aiming to identify independent risk factors and construct an integrated predictive model. This model provides an early risk stratification basis for subsequent recurrence in patients with first-episode HTG-AP, facilitating personalized follow-up and intervention strategies to reduce the risk of recurrence and improve long-term prognosis.

Materials and methods

Study population

This single-center cohort study retrospectively included individuals who were hospitalized for the first time at Weifang People’s Hospital from January 2019 to October 2023 and were diagnosed with HTG-AP. Inclusion criteria are as follows: age 18–75 years, first-time onset with hospitalization within 7 days of symptom onset, and no prior treatment interventions. Diagnosis and classification of AP based on the modified Atlanta classification: clinical, laboratory, and imaging features meeting two or more of the following criteria: AP-related abdominal pain, an increase in serum amylase/lipase levels thrice the upper normal limit, and abdominal ultrasound and/or CT scan demonstrating typical AP imaging findings [14–16]. Second, patients must present with hypertriglyceridemia (serum triglycerides >11.3 mmol/L or <5.65–11.3 mmol/L with chylomicronemia). The exclusion criteria were as follows: (a) severe comorbidities at admission, including chronic heart failure, hepatic failure, or renal failure; (b) history of malignancy; (c) pregnancy or labor; (d) acute exacerbation of CP; (e) death during follow-up. To minimize selection bias, all consecutive eligible patients during the study period were included without sampling. As this was a real-world retrospective cohort, management and follow-up were determined by routine clinical practice. Nevertheless, some degree of selection bias may remain because patients lacking key clinical or imaging data required for analysis were excluded.

This retrospective study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Institutional Review Board (IRB) of Weifang People’s Hospital (Approval No. KYLL20250402-4). The same IRB approval was also used in our previously published study [13], as both studies originated from the same approved retrospective research framework; however, the present study addressed a different clinical question, with recurrence after the first episode as the primary endpoint. As the data used for the analysis were retrospective and anonymized, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the IRB. To protect patient privacy, all identifiers were removed before data analysis.

Clinical and laboratory data

All clinical and laboratory data collected following admission, including age, gender, body mass index (BMI), smoking and drinking status, pre-existing diseases (such as hypertension, diabetes, and coronary heart disease), and concomitant diseases (e.g. fatty liver and ascites), were included in the study. Biochemical and hematological parameters collected within 24 h of admission, including liver/kidney function, complete blood count, lipid profile, blood glucose, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), C-reactive protein (CRP), amylase, and calcium ion, were included in the analysis. The TyG index was determined as follows: [Ln(TG (mg/dL) × GLU (mg/dL))/2]. All biochemical variables used for analysis, including triglycerides and fasting plasma glucose for calculation of the TyG index, were obtained from blood samples collected within the first 24 h after admission, before initiation of lipid-lowering therapy, insulin infusion, or enteral/parenteral nutritional support, in order to reflect the baseline metabolic status at disease onset. All clinical data were independently extracted by two investigators and reviewed by a third investigator to ensure that the collected data were accurate. Missing data were infrequent (<5% for all variables) and were handled using complete-case analysis without imputation to avoid introducing additional uncertainty into the model.

Body composition parameters

In this study, all patients underwent abdominal CT scanning examinations. The third lumbar vertebra (L3)-level abdominal CT images were analyzed using the SliceOmatic software (version 5.0; Tomovision, Magog, Canada). VAT was defined between −150 and −50 HU. To standardize the above areas, every region was divided by the square of the patient’s height (in meters), yielding the VATI. CT scanning was performed by two experienced radiologists blinded to the clinical information of the patients. The patients were rescanned when the results were inconsistent.

Study endpoints and follow-up status

The primary endpoint of this study was recurrent AP occurring more than three months after initial remission. During follow-up, the following scenarios were considered to indicate the recurrence of AP: 1) an interval of more than three months between episodes; 2) complete or near-complete resolution of the initial episode of AP; and 3) absence of conclusive evidence for underlying CP [17,18]. Patients were followed for 24 months after discharge. Follow-up was conducted using a structured schedule consisting of outpatient visits or telephone interviews at approximately 3–6-month intervals, supplemented by systematic review of electronic medical records to identify rehospitalizations or recurrent pancreatitis events.

Rationality of sample size

The sample size in this retrospective cohort analysis depended on the number of qualified patients meeting rigid eligibility criteria throughout the study period. To assess statistical reliability, post hoc power analysis was conducted on key outcome measures, such as the AUC of the predictive model. Under conditions of α = 0.05 and n = 293 (63 cases of AP recurrence vs. 230 cases of non-AP recurrence), the effect size of the model (AUC = 0.927) achieved 99% test power, far exceeding the conventional threshold of 80%.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using the R software version 4.1.2. The quantitative results were presented as the mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile ranges) based on their distribution characteristics and were analyzed by Mann–Whitney U tests or t-tests. Categorical data were analyzed by Fisher’s exact tests or χ2 tests. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were conducted to identify factors influencing recurrence, and a predictive model was constructed based on significant variables. A nomogram was used to visualize the predictive model, and differences in the risk of recurrence across various time points were examined by Kaplan-Meier analysis. Model discrimination was evaluated by the C-index, while ROC curves and calibration curves were used to evaluate model performance. The clinical net benefit was examined by decision curve analysis (DCA). Bootstrap methods and repeated cross-validation were adopted for internal validation to assess the stability and generalizability of the model. All results were considered to be statistically significant at p < 0.05. The model was compared with individual predictors.

Results

A total of 293 patients with first-episode HTG-AP were included and followed for 24 months. During follow-up, 63 patients experienced recurrent pancreatitis, yielding an overall recurrence rate of 21.5%. Screening for independent predictors of the recurrence of HTG-AP

After performing univariate Cox regression analysis of clinical and laboratory indicators, we identified multiple candidate factors associated with recurrence, including diabetes mellitus, BMI, the TyG index, the VATI, smoking history, alcohol history, and WBC count (Table 2). Statistically significant variables were further incorporated into a multivariate Cox regression model. The results indicated that VATI (HR = 1.021, 95%CI: 1.009–1.034, p = 0.001), the TyG index (HR = 1.575, 95%CI: 1.232–2.013, p < 0.001), smoking history (HR = 2.486, 95% CI: 1.213–5.095, p = 0.013), and alcohol history (HR = 2.720, 95% CI: 1.321–5.603, p = 0.007) were identified as independent predictors of the recurrence of HTG-AP (Table 2).

Construction of a new HTG-AP recurrence prediction model

The multivariate Cox regression analysis was performed to construct an HTG-AP recurrence risk prediction model. Model visualization was performed using the R software to generate a nomogram (Figure 2). The clinical prediction model assigns a corresponding score for every patient based on the severity of their existing risk factor. The total score was converted into the probability of the recurrence of pancreatitis.

Nomogram for predicting recurrence of first-time HTG-AP in patients.

By plotting Kaplan-Meier survival curves (Figure 3), the ability of the model to distinguish high-risk AP recurrence from low-risk AP recurrence across different time periods was evaluated. A significant difference in the two-year recurrence rate was observed between the high-risk and low-risk groups (77.42 vs. 0.09%, p < 0.001), as confirmed by the log-rank test (p < 0.001).

Internal validation of the nomogram

The C-index was used to evaluate the discriminatory ability of the model. Using the R software, a new sample set was generated by bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples. The internal validation results within the training cohort yielded a C-index of 0.925 (95% CI: 0.919, 0.928). The validated C-index exceeded 0.9.

Verification of nomogram calibration

The DCA for the HTG-AP recurrence model showed that when the threshold probability is >0, the model curve lies above both cutoff lines. This finding indicates that if the model predicts that a patient is at risk for the recurrence of HTG-AP, timely clinical intervention is beneficial and has good clinical value (Figure 7).

Discussion

Accurate prediction of the RAP is a critical unmet need in clinical management. Although traditional models have identified key clinical risk factors, for example, the prediction map developed by Yuan et al. integrating smoking, acute necrotic effusion, triglycerides, and alcoholic etiology (training set AUC: 0.747), the predictive performance of these models remains only moderate [19]. Recent radiomic approaches have demonstrated significant advantages, with Tang et al. reporting an AUC as high as 0.915 [20]. Based on these findings, we constructed a novel predictive model using metabolic indicators and body composition parameters. We identified VATI, TyG, smoking history, and alcohol history as independent predictors of AP. Compared to existing models, which rely primarily on conventional clinical indicators, our model had better predictive ability, enabling more precise identification of high-risk individuals. This advancement lays the foundation for developing personalized follow-up and intervention strategies.

Obesity, particularly excess visceral adiposity, plays a critical role in the development, severity, and prognosis of acute pancreatitis (AP) [21,22]. In this study, VATI emerged as an independent predictor of AP recurrence, consistent with prior reports [12,23]. Visceral fat is metabolically active and exhibits high lipolytic capacity, releasing large amounts of unsaturated free fatty acids (FFAs) during metabolic stress. When FFAs exceed albumin-binding capacity, they exert direct cytotoxic effects on acinar and endothelial cells, amplifying necroinflammation [24,25]. In parallel, visceral adipose tissue secretes pro-inflammatory cytokines and adipokines, sustaining a chronic low-grade inflammatory milieu that may leave the pancreas in a persistently susceptible state even after clinical resolution of the initial episode [26–28]. Our study validated the role of visceral fat in the recurrence of AP from a body composition perspective, suggesting that clinical risk assessment for obese patients should focus on “fat quality and distribution” rather than body weight alone.

The TyG index, a validated surrogate of IR [29], was also independently associated with recurrence risk. IR promotes systemic inflammation, enhances lipolysis, and increases circulating FFAs, thereby aggravating lipotoxic and inflammatory injury [30,31], while lowering the threshold for recurrent pancreatic insults. This prolongs the recovery time from inflammation and increases the risk of recurrence. Moreover, IR is closely related to the severity of AP and organ dysfunction [32,33]. Therefore, TyG reflects the underlying metabolic inflammatory state present in patients to a certain extent, and its elevation may indicate a significant increase in the risk of recurrence of AP.

VAT and TyG may influence AP recurrence through shared metabolic pathways. Excess VAT promotes oxidative stress and inflammation by releasing FFAs and pro-inflammatory mediators while impairing insulin signaling to induce insulin resistance (IR). During HTG-AP, triglycerides in visceral adipocytes and circulating lipoproteins undergo accelerated lipolysis, generating large amounts of unsaturated non-esterified fatty acids (NEFAs) [34]. When these exceed albumin-binding capacity, unbound fatty acids accumulate and exert cytotoxic and pro-inflammatory effects, amplifying tissue injury and systemic inflammation [35,36]. IR further lowers inflammatory tolerance thresholds, creating a state of “inflammatory priming” that sustains lipolysis, increases FFA flux, and promotes hepatic lipogenesis and additional visceral fat deposition, thereby forming a self-reinforcing metabolic cycle [31]. This persistent metabolic disturbance may leave patients vulnerable to recurrent AP even after apparent clinical recovery. Consistently, recent multicenter studies indicate that HTG-AP recurrence is driven more by sustained metabolic dysregulation than by isolated triglyceride elevation [8]. Our findings therefore support risk assessment strategies that incorporate visceral fat burden and insulin resistance, which may help identify patients requiring intensified metabolic management and closer follow-up.

From a practical perspective, the nomogram may be incorporated into routine care as a risk-stratification tool at discharge or early follow-up. Patients identified as high risk could undergo more intensive longitudinal surveillance, including shorter follow-up intervals and closer metabolic monitoring. This group may also benefit from earlier and more structured lifestyle interventions, weight and dietary counseling, and optimization of lipid- and glucose-lowering therapy to address the underlying metabolic disturbance. In contrast, lower-risk patients may be managed with standard follow-up schedules. Such a stratified approach may help allocate clinical resources more efficiently while enabling proactive prevention rather than reactive treatment of recurrence. This prevention-oriented strategy is also in line with the ongoing REDUCE trial [37]. Our nomogram may help identify patients who may benefit from intensified metabolic prevention and closer follow-up.

Besides metabolic factors, we also examined modifiable risk factors such as smoking and alcohol consumption. Smoking can cause microvascular endothelial dysfunction, increase oxidative stress, and promote the activity of NADPH oxidase, thereby impairing pancreatic microcirculation [38–40]. Moreover, nicotine and its metabolites (e.g. NNK) in tobacco can induce acinar cell injury, promote premature activation of trypsinogen, and exacerbate local inflammatory responses [41–43]. Smoking is an independent factor related to the recurrence of AP [19]. Alcohol consumption also promotes the recurrence of AP through mechanisms such as pancreatic duct protein thrombus formation [44], cellular oxidative stress [45], and activation of the NLRP3 inflammasome [46]. Randomized controlled trials have found that alcohol intervention significantly decreases the risk of alcoholic AP recurrence [47]. These findings suggest that enhanced lifestyle management can help significantly decrease the recurrence of AP.

Some clinically recognized indicators, such as admission triglyceride level, pancreatic necrosis, and local complications [48–50], were not included in the final model mainly due to statistical and data-related considerations. First, admission triglyceride levels showed substantial collinearity with composite metabolic indicators included in the model (particularly the TyG index), and retaining both variables led to model instability during preliminary analyses. Second, imaging-defined complications such as pancreatic necrosis were unavailable or incomplete in a proportion of patients because contrast-enhanced imaging was not uniformly performed in this real-world retrospective cohort. Including such variables would have significantly reduced the effective sample size. Finally, given the relatively small number of recurrence events, incorporating additional correlated or incompletely captured variables would have increased the risk of overfitting; therefore, a parsimonious modeling strategy was adopted to preserve robustness.

This study has several innovative aspects. First, it represents the first application of metabolic indicators for assessing the risk of recurrence of AP, providing a more accurate reflection of the metabolic profiles of patients than traditional models. Second, the model allows both real-time clinical risk stratification and quantitative prediction of long-term recurrence risk. Third, most indicators used in this study are routine tests or standardized CT-based measurements, ensuring ease of acquisition and strong potential for clinical implementation.

This study has several limitations. First, the model was developed and internally validated in a single-center retrospective cohort, where selection bias was difficult to avoid. Although bootstrap resampling demonstrated good internal stability, the lack of external validation limits its generalizability, and validation in independent multicenter populations is required before broad clinical implementation. Second, the overall sample size was modest, with a relatively small number of recurrence events, which may have reduced the statistical power of certain analyses. Third, body composition assessment relied on SliceOmatic software, which is not yet routinely available in many clinical settings and may affect the model’s immediate applicability.

Conclusions

Our predictive model can serve as a valuable clinical tool for identifying at-risk patients, thus helping clinicians formulate targeted interventions, including metabolic control, smoking cessation, and alcohol abstinence. This targeted approach may mitigate recurrence and enhance prognostic outcomes. These results reinforce that integrating metabolic health management into the long-term care framework for patients with AP is important.

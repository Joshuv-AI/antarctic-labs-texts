Perturbative unitarity for models with singlet and doublet scalars Preprint: CFTP/25-xxx
Carolina T. Lopes, André Milagre, João P. Silva

Abstract
We provide a complete description of unitarity bounds on the gauge-scalar sectors of models with extra S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) doublet, neutral singlet, and charged singlet scalars. Such additions are very frequent in models beyond the Standard Model, and, in particular, they are almost universal in models explaining the dark matter problem. We propose a specific classification and minimal set of scattering matrices containing all the relevant information. We also developed a Mathematica implementation of our results, BounDS , and we use it to fully study a number of simple cases, comparing with the literature, when available. The Mathematica notebook BounDS is provided via a public GitHub repository.

Introduction

The Standard Model (SM) of particle physics has been extremely successful,
culminating in the discovery of a scalar particle consistent with the Higgs
boson
ATLAS:2012yve
;
CMS:2012qbp
.
Nevertheless, it is not a complete theory,
and extensions beyond the Standard Model (BSM) are required.
In particular,
the SM provides no explanation for dark matter (DM)
ParticleDataGroup:2016lqr
,
whose presence is inferred across a wide range of astronomical scales,
from kiloparsecs to the size of the observable Universe.
Key probes include the rotation curves of spiral galaxies,
the study of galaxy clusters,
and measurements of the Cosmic Microwave
Background
Planck:2018vyg
;
Cirelli:2024ssz
.

One of the most widely studied scenarios envisions dark matter as consisting
of new fundamental particles. The simplest scalar extension introduces a real,
neutral

SU(2)

singlet with a global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry to ensure
stability
Burgess:2000yq
;
GAMBIT:2017gge
.
Alternatively, a minimal fermionic option is a Weyl fermion, commonly referred
to as a sterile neutrino
Cirelli:2024ssz
.
Other proposed models include the Inert Doublet Model,
which extends the Higgs sector with a dark doublet
Ilnicka:2015jba
;
multi-singlet SM extensions
Goncalves:2025snm
;
the Next-to-Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model
Ellwanger:2009dp
;
and models featuring scalar or fermionic portals to hidden sectors
Arcadi:2024wwg
.
Most of these models include extra scalar fields,
doublet or singlet under the SM gauge group,
and often new discrete symmetries in order to stabilize the dark sector.
In exploring such theories, one must start with the theoretical constraints,
such as guaranty of a bounded from below potential, assurance of a global minimum,
and perturbative unitarity.

In any consistent quantum field theory, probability conservation is encoded
in the unitarity of the
S
-matrix
Schwartz:2014sze
;
Logan:2022uus
.
In scalar extensions motivated by dark matter, this requirement becomes
particularly relevant: new scalar fields can induce scattering amplitudes
that grow with energy, and enforcing unitarity imposes bounds on their
masses and couplings. For
2\to 2
scattering processes,
unitarity constraints can be applied to the partial-wave decomposition
of the amplitudes,
with the strongest high-energy limits coming from the zeroth partial-wave
amplitude
Schwartz:2014sze
;
Logan:2022uus
.
In the high-energy limit, the Equivalence Theorem
Veltman:1989ud

ensures that the scattering of longitudinally polarized gauge bosons can be
replaced by the corresponding Goldstone bosons, allowing these scalar amplitudes
to capture the dominant contributions to unitarity bounds.
Historically, Lee, Quigg, and Thacker applied this method to derive
an upper bound on the Higgs boson mass,

M_{H}^{2}\leq 8\pi\sqrt{2}/(3G_{F})\approx 1\textrm{TeV}^{2}

Lee:1977yc
;
Lee:1977eg
.
Unitarity has also been applied to the general two-Higgs-doublet model (2HDM)
with explicit CP violation
Ginzburg:2005dt
;
Kanemura:2015ska
,
to the general
N
-Higgs-doublet model
Bento:2017eti
,
also with fermions
Bento:2018fmy
,
and to constrain large scalar multiplets
Hally:2012pu
;
Milagre:2024wcg
.
More recently, perturbative unitarity analyses have been
extended beyond the traditional
2\to 2
limit.
For instance, Ref.
Bresciani:2025toe
studies unitarity
in general

scattering processes with
M,N\geq 2
.
In particular, in inflationary models featuring a non-minimal
coupling with the Ricci scalar, partial-wave unitarity bounds on

2\to N
processes have improved earlier estimates of the energy scale
at which new-physics effective interactions invalidate the
perturbative expansion

.

In this work, we extend the analysis of Ref.

constructing an

III
Partial wave unitarity bounds

III.1
Partial wave decomposition

We consider
2\to 2
scattering processes between the scalars
of the theory defined in Section
II
.
Let
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
be complex scalar fields
and let
a,b,c
, and
d
be their corresponding flavour indices.
At tree-level, the amplitude for the process

A_{a}\,B_{b}\to C_{c}\,D_{d}

(19)

may have contributions from

s
,
t
, and
u
-channels, and contact interactions.
However, as we take the limit where the Mandelstam variables

s
,
t
, and
u
go to infinity, the contributions from

s
,
t
, and
u
-channels vanish, respectively
4
4

4

For an interesting example see, for example, Appendix A of
MiguelMSc
.
.
Consequently, in the high-energy limit, only
the quartic interactions involving the external scalars
contribute to the tree-level amplitude, allowing us to write it as

\mathcal{M}\left[A_{a}\,B_{b}\to C_{c}\,D_{d}\right]\ =\ -\frac{\partial^{4}V_{4}}{\partial A_{a}\,\partial B_{b}\,\partial C_{c}^{*}\,\partial D_{d}^{*}}.

(20)

Any square-integrable function may be expressed as
an expansion in a complete set of basis functions.
To study perturbative unitarity,
it is particularly convenient to choose the basis
of Legendre polynomials
P_{J}(\cos\theta)
, where
J
is the
total orbital angular momentum of the final state,
and
\theta
the scattering angle.
Expanding the amplitude in this basis defines the
so-called partial-wave expansion
Hally:2012pu
;
Milagre:2024wcg
:

\mathcal{M}(\cos\theta)=16\pi\sum_{J=0}^{\infty}a_{J}\left(2J+1\right)P_{J}(\cos\theta).

(21)

The numerical coefficients
a_{J}

(also known as partial waves)
are defined as

a_{J}=\frac{2J+1}{32\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\mathcal{M}(\cos\theta)\,P_{J}(\cos\theta)\ \mathrm{d}\cos\theta,

(22)

and are constrained by tree-level partial-wave unitarity
as
Hally:2012pu
;
Milagre:2024wcg

\left|a_{J}\right|\leq 1,\quad\textrm{and}\quad\left|\real\{a_{J}\}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}.

(23)

Using the fact that, in the high-energy limit,
the amplitude is independent of the scattering angle,
and that
P_{0}(\cos\theta)=1
,
the most stringent
partial-wave unitarity bound
arises by applying the second condition of
Eq. (
23
) to the zeroth partial wave
a_{0}
.
Thus,
using Eq. (
20
),
all quartic couplings must satisfy

16\pi\left|a_{0}\right|\ =\ \left|N_{ab}N_{cd}\,\frac{\partial^{4}V_{4}}{\partial A_{a}\,\partial B_{b}\,\partial C_{c}^{*}\,\partial D_{d}^{*}}\right|\leq 8\pi,

(24)

where

N_{ij}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{\delta_{ij}}}}

(25)

is a symmetry factor that accounts for identical particles
either in the initial or final state.

Mathematica notebook

Deriving partial-wave unitarity bounds for
different models is a rather repetitive task.
As stated, for a model specified by definite values of

n_{D}
,
n_{c}
, and
n_{n}
,
the procedure requires assembling a scattering matrix for each state

|Q,Y,T\rangle
listed in Table
1
,
computing the corresponding eigenvalues,
and verifying that they remain below
8\pi
.
In models with additional flavour symmetries,
all quartic terms forbidden by those symmetries
must also be set to zero.

To optimize this process and reduce the burden on the
high-energy physics phenomenology community,
we have developed the
Mathematica

tool
BounDS
that automates the calculation.
The user simply has to specify the values of

n_{D}
,
n_{c}
, and
n_{n}
, and,
if needed, the transformation properties of the fields
under additional flavour and/or generalized CP symmetries.

BounDS
carries out the necessary steps to:

•

Compute the set of all linearly independent quartic couplings allowed by the symmetries.

•

Calculate the 7 independent scattering matrices
M_{|Q,Y,T\rangle}
.

•

Block-diagonalize the scattering matrices by swapping rows and columns.

•

Output the quartic part of the scalar potential and scattering matrices in
L
a
T
e
X
form.

•

Output closed expressions for the eigenvalues of the scattering matrices, when possible.

Additional details can be found in Appendix
C

and the notebook
BounDS
can be downloaded from:

VI
Overview

Almost all models addressing the outstanding issues in the SM include extra
SU(2)

singlet and/or doublet scalars.
In particular, many models addressing the dark matter problem include extra
neutral singlet scalars.
Such models must be subject to theoretical constraints, even before a simulation starts.
Those constraints include boundedness from below, nonexistence of lower lying alternative vaccua,
and the unitarity bounds on
2\rightarrow 2
scattering.
In this paper we address fully the perturbative partial-wave unitarity for the tree-level
scattering matrix in models with any number of scalar doublets,
neutral singlets, and/or charged singlets.
Enforcing the correct high-energy behavior, provides bounds on the quartic couplings,
freeing us from defining the exact nature of the quadratic and cubic couplings.

In contrast, if one one wishes to turn such bounds on restrictions on masses, mixing angles
and other directly observable quantities, then one must define the full theory.
That is,
one must define the quadratic and cubic couplings,
a specific vacuum, and the mass matrices must be dully diagonalized.
After this, one would strife to invert the relations,
turning the unitarity bounds on the quartic couplings into
restrictions on combinations of masses and mixing angles.
This is possible in simple models, but increasingly more difficult
as the number of fields increases.
In contrast, although the matrices get larger,
our limits on quartic couplings are always applicable, at least numerically.

We classify the states by the conserved quantum numbers

Q
,
Y
, and
T
and show that, once one restricts oneself to the minimal set of states
providing all inequivalent bounds, the quantum number
Q
is redundant.
We also discuss examples where the existence of extra symmetries allows for the
inclusion of further quantum numbers in the basis, thus greatly simplifying the
matrices.

We introduce the
Mathematica
notebook
BounDS

that automatically calculates
the quartic part of the potential
and
the scattering matrices and their
eigenvalues,
for any model with any symmetries (discrete or continuous,
abelian or non-abelian).
We present results for a variety of particular models,
and compare with the literature, when available.
Our aim is to help provide complete simulations of models beyond the SM
with a necessary and very powerful tool in parameter restriction.

Therefore, perturbative unitarity bounds
obtained from scatterings involving

|1,\frac{3}{2}\rangle
,

|0,1\rangle
,
and

|0,\frac{1}{2}\rangle
are redundant because they
are identical to those derived from

|2,\frac{3}{2}\rangle
,

|2,1\rangle
,
and

|1,\frac{1}{2}\rangle
, respectivelly.

In any scattering involving
SU(2)
doublets,
total isospin
T
must also be conserved,
For this reason, two-particle states should be
further labeled by
|Q,Y,T\rangle
,
with
T=0,1
in this model.
Using Clebsch-Gordan coefficients,
we can split the two-particle state
\phi^{+}_{i}\phi^{0}_{j}

in
|1,1\rangle
into

\displaystyle|1,1,0\rangle:

\displaystyle\phi^{+}_{[i}\,\phi^{0}_{j]}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{+}_{i}\,\phi^{0}_{j}-\phi^{+}_{j}\,\phi^{0}_{i}\right),

(151)

\displaystyle|1,1,1\rangle:

\displaystyle\phi^{+}_{(i}\,\phi^{0}_{j)}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{+}_{i}\,\phi^{0}_{j}+\phi^{+}_{j}\,\phi^{0}_{i}\right).

(152)

In the same manner,
we can split the two-particle state
\phi^{+}_{i}\phi^{-}_{j}

in
|0,0\rangle
into

\displaystyle|0,0,0\rangle:

\displaystyle\Phi_{i}\,\Phi_{j}^{*}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{+}_{i}\,\phi^{-}_{j}+\phi^{0}_{i}\,\phi^{0*}_{j}\right),

(153)

\displaystyle|0,0,1\rangle:

\displaystyle\overline{\Phi_{i}\,\Phi_{j}^{*}}\,\equiv\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\phi^{+}_{i}\,\phi^{-}_{j}-\phi^{0}_{i}\,\phi^{0*}_{j}\right).

(154)

These redefinitions of states lead, once again,
to redundant scattering matrices because

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\phi^{+}_{a}\phi^{+}_{b}\to\phi^{+}_{c}\phi^{+}_{d}\right]=\mathcal{M}\left[\phi^{+}_{(a}\,\phi^{0}_{b)}\to\phi^{+}_{(c}\,\phi^{0}_{d)}\right]=2\lambda_{ca,db}+2\lambda_{da,cb},

(155)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}\left[\phi^{+}_{a}\phi^{0*}_{b}\to\phi^{+}_{c}\phi^{0*}_{d}\right]=\mathcal{M}\left[\overline{\Phi_{a}\Phi_{b}^{*}}\to\overline{\Phi_{c}\Phi_{d}^{*}}\right]=2\lambda_{ca,bd}.

(156)

We therefore conclude that it is sufficient
to apply the partial wave unitarity bounds to
the scattering matrices built out of the states
listed in Table
1
,
i.e.
the states

\left|Q,Y,T\right\rangle\quad=\quad\left|2,2,0\right\rangle,\ \left|2,\frac{3}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle,\ \left|2,1,1\right\rangle,\ \left|1,1,0\right\rangle,\ \left|1,\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right\rangle,\ \left|1,0,1\right\rangle,\ \left|0,0,0\right\rangle.

(157)

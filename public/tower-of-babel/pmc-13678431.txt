Epigenetic Regulatory Networks in Cardiac Fibrosis: Therapeutic Targets, Crosstalk, and Future Directions
Authors: Yansheng Yao, Chongyu Shao, Haitong Wan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCardiac fibrosis is a central driver of pathological cardiac remodeling and a critical risk factor for the progression to heart failure. Cardiac fibrosis may induce myocardial ischemia and hypoxia, impaired cardiac function, and arrhythmias, and is thereby intimately linked to escalating cardiac mortality. Epigenetic mechanisms serve as core drivers in both the initiation and malignant evolution of cardiac fibrosis. Currently, the epigenetic regulatory network primarily covers five cutting-edge domains: (1) dynamic DNA methylation modifications, (2) the histone post-translational modifications, (3) emerging RNA epitranscriptomic modifications, (4) the non-coding RNA regulatory network, and (5) ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling. This review moves beyond conventional pathological frameworks to examine the multi-layered synergistic actions of epigenetic regulation in cardiac fibrosis. By deconstructing the specific spatiotemporal expression profiles of these dynamic modification mechanisms, we systematically evaluate their translational potential as reversible interventional targets, offering a critical synthesis of these epigenetic mechanisms and pointing to plausible research directions for overcoming fibrotic progression toward heart failure.

Introduction

Cardiovascular disease is the predominant cause of death globally.1 Cardiac fibrosis is a common pathological feature of many cardiovascular diseases, including chronic heart failure, hypertensive heart disease, myocardial infarction (MI), and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. The main pathological process of cardiac fibrosis involves cardiac fibroblasts (CFs) proliferation with subsequent differentiation into myofibroblasts, excessive extracellular matrix (ECM) deposition, and cardiac remodeling. Cardiac fibrosis leads to myocardial ischemia and hypoxia, impaired cardiac function, and arrhythmias, and is thereby closely associated with cardiac death.2,3 Cardiac fibrosis is a complex network process involving multiple cell types, activation of various signaling pathways (particularly TGF-β/Smad and renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system), and dynamic interactions of molecular events. Epigenetic regulation is a key mediator of fibrotic progression. Epigenetic regulation can regulate the pathological process of cardiac fibrosis through reversible dynamic modification of DNA, histone and RNA. Epigenetic regulation does not alter the DNA sequence itself. Instead, it regulates gene expression through reversible chemical modifications, influencing the activation of CFs, inflammatory responses, and ECM metabolism. Elucidating the epigenetic mechanisms underlying cardiac fibrosis may facilitate the development of safe and effective therapeutic strategies.

The concept of epigenetics emerged from the studies of how genetic and environmental factors jointly influence cell differentiation and tissue formation during embryonic development. In 1942, Conrad Hal Waddington defined epigenetics as the study of heritable phenotypic changes arising from gene–environment interactions during development.4 Epigenetics is now broadly defined as heritable changes in gene function that occur without alterations to the underlying DNA sequence. Epigenetic regulation influences transcription, translation, nucleosome assembly, and chromatin structure through covalent modifications of nucleic acids or histones, thereby affecting physiological processes, disease pathogenesis, and even offspring phenotypes. Epigenetic regulation can currently be divided into five different levels: (1) DNA methylation; (2) histone post-translational modifications; (3) RNA epitranscriptomic modifications; (4) non-coding RNA regulatory network; (5) ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling5 (see Figure 1). Each epigenetic modification is dynamically regulated by a specific set of enzymes. The enzymes responsible for adding epigenetic marks are called “writers”, those responsible for removing epigenetic modifications are called “erasers” and those responsible for recognising epigenetic marks are called “readers”. The five classes of epigenetic modifications do not act in isolation but instead form a dynamic cross-regulation network through upstream-downstream cascading regulation and bidirectional feedback interactions. These five layers are not independent but form an integrated regulatory network. Broadly, DNA methylation establishes a stable epigenetic landscape, histone modifications modulate chromatin accessibility and transcriptional permissiveness, non‑coding RNAs contribute to target gene specificity, m6A modifications provide dynamic and reversible control, and higher‑order chromatin remodeling consolidates the final transcriptional outcome.6,7 Through physical recruitment, enzyme activity dependence, and spatial compartmentalization, they construct a dynamic cross-regulatory network that retains memory of injury and exhibits compensatory responses to therapy.8 Figure 1Epigenetic regulation in cardiac fibrosis. Several recent reviews have elucidated the roles of classical epigenetic mechanisms in cardiac fibrosis—including DNA methylation, histone modifications, and chromatin remodeling—with a particular emphasis on their potential as pharmacological targets.9,10 Pérez et al systematically integrated the mechanisms of DNA methylation, histone modifications, non-coding RNAs, and m6A epitranscriptomic modifications in cardiac fibrosis, and evaluated the pharmacological potential of targeting these epigenetic regulatory factors.11 Sumra Komal et al have reviewed the regulatory roles of epigenetic mechanisms—including DNA methylation, histone modifications, non-coding RNAs, and RNA methylation—in cardiac fibroblast activation, and discussed potential therapeutic strategies targeting these epigenetic regulators.12 Notably, a related review introduced the crosstalk between m6A epitranscriptomics and epigenetic regulation, an area also addressed in the present work.13 However, previous reviews have largely focused on summarizing epigenetic mechanisms at either a single level or in parallel, typically covering three or four distinct layers of regulation. In contrast, this review proposes an integrative framework that systematically consolidates all five epigenetic regulatory layers, highlighting their synergistic actions and cooperative effects. This expanded scope extends beyond earlier coverage by emphasizing crosstalk and cooperation among layers, providing a more integrated view of the epigenetic landscape in cardiac fibrosis.

This review progressively deconstructs the epigenetic regulatory mechanisms at different levels and explores their potential as therapeutic targets in cardiac fibrosis, thereby consolidating current knowledge in this field and providing a systematic theoretical reference for future mechanistic studies and targeted intervention exploration.

DNA Methylation in Cardiac Fibrosis

Effectors of Dynamic DNA Methylation

DNA methylation typically involves the transfer of methyl groups from S-adenosylmethionine to the fifth carbon of cytosine residues within CpG dinucleotides, generating 5-methylcytosine (5mC). CpG islands are genomic regions with a higher frequency of CG sequences than the surrounding genome.14 DNA methylation represses transcription and mediates gene silencing by blocking transcription factor binding or by recruiting transcriptional repressors. The main enzymes responsible for DNA methylation are DNA methyltransferase (DNMT), including DNMT1, DNMT3A and DNMT3B. DNMT3A and DNMT3B are responsible for de novo DNA methylation, whereas DNMT1 maintains existing methylation patterns during DNA replication.15 DNA demethylase refers mainly to the ten-eleven translocation protein (TET) family, including TET1, TET2, and TET3.16 The readers of DNA methylation have unique DNA methylation recognition domains, such as the methyl-CpG-binding domain (MBD).17 As an MBD-containing protein, methyl-CpG binding protein 2 (MeCP2) recognizes DNA methylation and recruits transcriptional inhibitors, thereby silencing gene expression. The Role of DNA Methylation in Cardiac Fibrosis

The Role of DNA Methylation in Cardiac Fibrosis

Several lines of evidence indicate that DNMTs promote cardiac fibrosis. In diabetic cardiac fibrosis, DNMT1-mediated hypermethylation of the SOCS3 promoter suppresses SOCS3 expression, relieving inhibition of STAT3 and thereby driving cardiac fibroblast activation and collagen deposition. Notably, both genetic and pharmacological inhibition of DNMT1 reverses the activated state of cardiac fibroblasts.18 DNMT3A-mediated silencing of RASSF1A promotes cardiac fibrosis through ERK1/2 upregulation.19 Similarly, DNMT3A-induced hypermethylation of sFRP3, a negative regulator of Wnt signaling, correlates with fibroblast activation and fibrotic remodeling. Knockdown of DNMT3A restores sFRP3 expression by reversing promoter methylation, thereby mitigating isoprenaline-induced cardiac fibrosis and suppressing fibroblast proliferation and migration.20 Hypoxic conditions induce a pro-fibrotic microenvironment characterized by genomic DNA hypermethylation and elevated DNMT1/DNMT3B expression in CFs. Notably, siRNA-mediated DNMT3B knockdown markedly reduces collagen I and α-SMA production.21 Chronic hypoxia triggers DNA hypermethylation, which inactivates RASSF1A and activates ERK1/2 in cardiac fibroblasts, leading to fibroblast hyperproliferation and fibrotic deposition. Importantly, extracellular superoxide dismutase attenuates hypoxia-driven fibrosis by downregulating DNMT1/DNMT3B expression and restoring RASSF1A demethylation.22

TET2 and TET3 exert anti-fibrotic functions. TET2 knockout leads to cardiac dysfunction, progressive hypertrophy, and fibrosis. Mechanistically, TET2 deficiency suppresses Hspa1b expression—a key modulator of ERK signaling—leading to aberrant ERK pathway hyperactivation.30 TET3 depletion exacerbates fibrosis by impairing DNA damage repair, whereas TET3-mediated demethylation of the RASAL1 promoter restores RASAL1 expression and mitigates fibrogenesis.31 Hypermethylation of the RASAL1 promoter silences RASAL1 transcription, inducing endothelial–mesenchymal transition and promoting myocardial fibrosis. Conversely, TET3-mediated demethylation restores RASAL1 expression and attenuates fibrotic remodeling.32

MeCP2 promotes myocardial fibrosis through multiple downstream pathways. Sustained MeCP2 upregulation exacerbates myocardial hypertrophy, fibrosis, mitochondrial dysfunction, and impaired myocardial contractility. MeCP2 promotes CF activation and cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting dual-specificity phosphatase 5 (DUSP5).33 MeCP2 induces the proliferation of CFs by suppressing RASSF1A expression in diabetic cardiomyopathy.35

The Crosstalk Between DNA Methylation and Non-Coding RNAs in Cardiac Fibrosis

DNMTs exacerbate cardiac fibrosis in part through epigenetic modulation of lncRNAs and miRNAs. In both isoprenaline (ISO)-induced fibrotic models and lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-triggered pyroptosis of cardiac fibroblasts, DNMT1 is upregulated while lncRNA GAS5 is suppressed. DNMT1-mediated hypermethylation of lncRNA GAS5 promotes CF pyroptosis and activates the NLRP3 inflammasome, accelerating fibrotic pathogenesis.23 Consistently, fibrotic myocardial tissues and pyroptotic CFs show elevated DNMT1, Caspase-1, and NLRP3 levels, along with reduced LncRNA-ANRIL expression. Mechanistically, DNMT1-mediated methylation of LncRNA-ANRIL suppresses its expression, thereby promoting cardiac fibrosis and CF pyroptosis.24 DNMT3A -mediated methylation of lncRNA Neat1 contributes to its downregulation, promoting pyroptosis in CFs and cardiac fibrosis.25 In ISO-induced rat cardiac fibrotic tissue, protein levels of DNMT1 and α-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) were markedly elevated. Mechanistically, DNMT1-mediated suppression of miR-152-3p facilitates phenotypic transformation of CFs during fibrogenesis.26 Genetic silencing of DNMT1 attenuates angiotensin II–induced CF activation. Furthermore, DNMT1 promotes CTGF upregulation by regulating miR-133b methylation.27 Silencing DNMT3A significantly upregulates miR-200b, which attenuates myocardial fibrosis by modulating autophagic flux.28 DNMT3A suppresses miR-145 expression by maintaining hypermethylation of the pre-miR-145 promoter. DNMT3A overexpression promotes CF proliferation and fibrosis, and this effect is reversed by miR-145 overexpression.29 In fibrotic heart tissue, MeCP2 is upregulated and lncRNA GAS5 is downregulated, with MeCP2-induced GAS5 suppression promoting CF activation.34

In summary, disruption of DNA methylation homeostasis is a key driver of cardiac fibrosis. Targeting aberrantly overexpressed DNMTs and MeCP2, while restoring TET2 and TET3 expression, may re-establish DNA methylation homeostasis and alleviate cardiac fibrosis.

Histone Modifications in Cardiac Fibrosis

Histones exist as linker (H1) and core histones (H2A, H2B, H3, H4), with the latter forming an octamer around which 146 bp of DNA is wrapped to constitute the nucleosome—the basic unit of chromatin. The positively charged residues of histones facilitate electrostatic interactions with DNA, modulating chromatin compaction and thereby influencing transcriptional accessibility. Post-translational modifications of these residues serve as key epigenetic marks. The major types of histone modifications include acetylation, methylation, phosphorylation, ubiquitination, glycosylation, lactylation, and crotonylation, among others.36–38 Among these, the most extensively studied histone modifications are histone acetylation and histone methylation. Typically, acetylation-mediated charge neutralization enhances chromatin accessibility to promote transcriptional activation, whereas histone deacetylase activity induces chromatin compaction to suppress gene expression. The functional outcome of histone methylation depends on the specific residue modified and the extent of methylation, with effects ranging from transcriptional activation to repression.

Histone Acetylation

Histone acetylation is dynamically regulated by three classes of proteins: writers (histone acetyltransferases, HATs), erasers (histone deacetylases, HDACs), and readers (bromodomain and extraterminal domain [BET] proteins).

HATs acetylate lysine residues, neutralizing the positive charge of histones and weakening DNA–histone interactions. This relaxes chromatin structure, allowing transcriptional machinery access and promoting gene expression.39 Conversely, HDACs remove acetyl groups, restoring chromatin compaction and repressing transcription.

The enzymatic machinery facilitating lysine acetylation encompasses three principal HAT families: p300/CREB-binding protein (CBP/p300), members of the GNATs (Gcn5-related N-acetyltransferases) superfamily, and MYST acetyltransferases.40 p300/CBP acetylates enhancers and promoters to modulate chromatin accessibility and acts as a transcriptional coactivator by interacting with signaling mediators including NF-κB, AP-1, and STAT. The GNAT and MYST families are the two major HAT families, but their specific roles in cardiac fibrosis remain largely unexplored.41,42

HDACs are grouped into four classes: Class I (HDAC1–3, HDAC8), Class II (HDAC4–7, HDAC9–10), Class III (SIRT1–7, NAD+-dependent), and Class IV (HDAC11). Based on their catalytic mechanism, they are further divided into zinc-dependent (Classes I, II, and IV) and NAD+-dependent (Class III) enzymes.43 Zinc-dependent HDACs (Classes I, II, and IV) are termed classical HDACs, whereas Class III HDACs (sirtuins) require NAD⁺ for their deacetylase activity.44

BET proteins recognize acetylated lysine residues on histones and serve as readers that translate acetylation signals into downstream transcriptional responses. Functioning as a specialized subgroup of bromodomain-containing proteins, the BET family is structurally defined by dual conserved bromodomains (BD1 and BD2) positioned at the amino terminus and an extraterminal (ET) domain at the carboxyl terminus. This evolutionarily conserved family comprises four isoforms: BRD2, BRD3, BRD4, and BRDT. BRD4, the most studied BET family member, regulates diverse processes including cardiovascular disease, inflammation, immunity, and metabolism.43 BRD4 overexpression induces chromatin hyperacetylation, leading to aberrant activation of transcriptional programs. The Involvement of Histone Acetylation in Cardiac Fibrotic Remodeling

Histone Acetyltransferases

Pharmacological or genetic suppression of HATs ameliorates cardiac fibrosis in multiple experimental models. Under high glucose conditions, p300 acts as a transcriptional coactivator and promotes Smad activation by acetylating Smad2, thereby enhancing TGF-β signaling and driving cardiac fibrosis and hypertrophy.45 PCAF promotes cardiac fibroblast activation by acetylating SMAD2. Silencing PCAF with siRNA inhibits the expression and nuclear translocation of TGF-β1-induced fibrotic proteins.46

Histone Deacetylases

Inhibition of certain HDACs reduces cardiac fibrotic remodeling, although the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. HDAC1 contributes to cardiac fibrosis in diabetic cardiomyopathy by suppressing the transcriptional activity of bone morphogenetic protein 7 (BMP-7) through histone deacetylation.47 In the fibrotic heart, SMAD3 enhances the expression of Programmed Cell Death 5 (PDCD5), which in turn counteracts cardiac dysfunction and fibrosis progression by suppressing HDAC3 activity.67 Moreover, HDAC6 facilitates fibrotic remodeling by downregulating Ras association domain family member 1 isoform A (RASSF1A) expression.48 Selective HDAC8 inhibition reduces isoproterenol-induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis by modulating p38 MAPK signaling.49

SIRT1 protects cardiomyocytes from apoptosis and limits cardiac fibrosis through multiple mechanisms. SIRT1 activation suppresses pressure overload-induced fibrosis in rodents, largely through inhibition of Smad2/3 signaling.50 In diabetic mouse models, enhancing SIRT1 expression markedly reduces cardiac fibrosis through modulation of the Akt/α-SMA signaling axis.51 SIRT1 mediates the antifibrotic effects of dapagliflozin by reducing Notch1 acetylation in endothelial cells and inhibiting EndMT.52

SIRT2 overexpression protects against cardiac fibrosis in murine models. Cardiac-specific SIRT2 overexpression protects against angiotensin II–induced hypertrophic remodeling and fibrosis, preserving cardiac function.68 SIRT2 promotes H3K27 acetylation at the galectin-3 promoter region, inhibiting galectin-3 transcription and ameliorating radiation-induced cardiac fibrosis in mice.53 SIRT2 maintains cardiac homeostasis by repressing NFAT transcription factors. Mice lacking SIRT2 exhibit spontaneous development of pathological cardiac hypertrophy, fibrotic remodeling, and functional impairment in an age-dependent manner. Mechanistically, SIRT2 directly deacetylates NFATc2; in the absence of SIRT2, NFATc2 is stabilized and accumulates in the nucleus, leading to increased transcriptional activity.54

SIRT3 is a promising therapeutic target for cardiac fibrosis and heart failure. As a mitochondrial deacetylase, SIRT3 regulates metabolic homeostasis and oxidative stress. SIRT3 deficiency promotes cardiac fibrosis, at least in part by enhancing p53 acetylation and ferroptosis.55 SIRT3 overexpression suppresses p53 acetylation in diabetic db/db mice, reducing hypertrophy and fibrosis while improving cardiac function.56 In contrast, mice lacking SIRT3 display prominent myocardial inflammation and fibrosis. Mechanistically, SIRT3 promotes H3K27 deacetylation at the FOS promoter, inhibiting FOS transcription and thereby exerting anti-fibrotic and anti-inflammatory effects.57 Additionally, SIRT3 counteracts angiotensin II (AngII)-induced cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting the transdifferentiation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts via suppression of the STAT3-NFATc2 signaling cascade.58

SIRT6 protects the myocardium from age-associated hypertrophy and fibrosis.69 Loss of SIRT6 function in mice results in heightened activation of the transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) pathway, subsequently triggering fibrotic processes.59 Conversely, SIRT6 overexpression alleviates TAC-induced cardiac dysfunction, attenuates fibrosis, and reduces infarct size, potentially via telomere maintenance.60 SIRT6 also counteracts angiotensin II–induced cardiac injury and fibrosis by modulating the AMPK-ACE2 axis.61 It also prevents the transformation of CFs into myofibroblasts through inhibition of the NF-κB signaling cascade.62 SIRT7 mitigates hypertensive heart failure and fibrosis by regulating the YAP/ATP7A pathway.63

The Readers of Histone Acetylation

BET proteins regulate fibrogenic gene expression in multiple tissues.70 BET bromodomain inhibition disrupts inflammatory and fibrotic transcriptional programs in heart failure.71 Specifically, inhibition of BRD4 impedes the TGF-β-driven activation of CFs.64 By limiting ROS production, BRD4 blockade also reduces inflammatory responses and fibrotic remodeling, thereby alleviating pathological cardiac hypertrophy.65 Furthermore, suppressing BRD4 activity diminishes EndMT and cardiac fibrosis triggered by both transverse aortic constriction (TAC) and TGF-β stimulation.66 High glucose upregulates hypertrophy markers and fibrotic genes, an effect reversed by the BRD4 inhibitor JQ1.72

In summary, histone acetylation modulators—including inhibitors of p300, HDACs, and BRD4, as well as activators of sirtuins—represent promising therapeutic strategies for cardiac fibrosis. Inhibiting p300 and specific HDACs (HDAC1, HDAC6, and HDAC8) attenuates adverse cardiac remodeling in preclinical models. In contrast, activating class III HDACs (SIRT1/2/3/6/7) exerts cardioprotective and anti-fibrotic effects. Furthermore, inhibiting the acetylation reader BRD4 disrupts pro-fibrotic transcriptional programs. Collectively, these findings support the therapeutic potential of modulating histone acetylation in cardiac fibrosis.

Histone Methylation

Histone methylation occurs predominantly on basic amino acid residues of histones H3 and H4, specifically lysine and arginine. Lysine residues on histones are commonly methylated at positions such as H3K4, H3K9, H3K27, H3K36, H3K79, and H4K20, whereas arginine methylation frequently occurs at H3R2, H3R8, H3R17, H3R26, and H4R3. The functional impact of histone methylation—activation or repression—depends on the specific residue modified, the degree of methylation (mono-, di-, or tri-), and the genomic context (eg, promoters, enhancers, or coding regions). For instance, di- and trimethylation of H3K4, as well as trimethylation of H3K79, are widely recognized as markers of transcriptionally active chromatin, while modifications like H3K9me2 and H3K27me3 are linked to transcriptional repression. Enzymes responsible for the addition and removal of methyl groups on histones include histone methyltransferases (HMTs) and histone demethylases (HDMs). HMTs are categorized into protein arginine methyltransferases (PRMTs) and lysine-specific methyltransferases (KMTs). KMTs are further organized into eight subfamilies (KMT1 through KMT8) based on sequence and functional similarities.73 PRMTs are classified into three types based on the methylation products they generate:74–76 Type I (asymmetric dimethylarginine), Type II (symmetric dimethylarginine), and Type III (monomethylarginine). HDMs are divided into the JMJC domain-containing family (JMJD family) and lysine-specific demethylases (LSDs).10 Methylated histones are recognized by reader proteins containing domains such as PHD, chromodomain, WD40, Tudor, MBT, and PWWP.77 Several HMTs—including WDR5, DOT1L, PRMT5, SUV39H1, EZH2, SETDB1, and SET7—promote cardiac fibrosis (Figure 2). WDR5 is responsible for catalyzing the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 4 (H3K4me3). WDR5 expression and H3K4me3 levels are elevated in both Ang-II-infused murine hearts and Ang-II-stimulated neonatal rat cardiac fibroblasts. WDR5 suppression attenuates Ang-II-induced fibroblast-to-myofibroblast transition and cardiac fibrosis, potentially via the MDM2/p53/p21 pathway.78

DOT1L is the sole methyltransferase that catalyzes H3K79 methylation, a mark associated with transcriptional activation. Pharmacological or genetic DOT1L suppression alleviates cardiac fibrosis and preserves cardiac function. Mechanistically, DOT1L binds the FoxO3a promoter to enhance its transcription and promote fibroblast activation. DOT1L inhibition reduces FoxO3a mRNA and downstream fibrotic gene expression.79

PRMT5, a type II arginine methyltransferase, mediates H3R2 symmetric dimethylation and promotes TGF-β/Smad3-dependent fibrotic gene expression. Fibroblast-specific PRMT5 deletion mitigates cardiac fibrosis and improves left ventricular function in male mice.80 PRMT5 promotes Smad3 methylation and activation, thereby stimulating TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling, driving fibroblast differentiation and migration, and exacerbating doxorubicin-induced cardiac fibrosis.81

Suppressor of variegation 3–9 homolog 1 (SUV39H1) catalyzes the trimethylation of histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me3). Various profibrotic stimuli lead to elevated SUV39H1 expression levels in fibroblasts. Genetic deletion of Suv39h1 in fibroblasts or pharmacological inhibition of its activity attenuates cardiac fibrosis in mice.82 SUV39H1 may directly bind to the promoter region of the TACC2 gene and repress TACC2 transcription by catalyzing H3K9 trimethylation, thereby promoting fibroblast activation.83

Enhancer of zeste homolog 2 (EZH2), the catalytic subunit of polycomb repressive complex 2(PRC2), promotes cardiac fibrosis primarily by catalyzing trimethylation at lysine 27 of histone H3 (H3K27me3). In diabetic cardiomyopathy, hyperglycemia inhibits EZH2 phosphorylation, thereby enhancing its enzymatic activity and repressing PPAR-γ transcription, which leads to fibroblast activation.84

SET domain bifurcated histone lysine methyltransferase 1 (SETDB1) is a histone methyltransferase responsible for catalyzing H3K9 trimethylation (H3K9me3). SETDB1 represses anti-fibrotic genes through H3K9me3-mediated silencing, thereby promoting cardiac fibrosis. Deletion of Setdb1 in macrophages alleviates cardiac allograft fibrosis.85

SET7 (also known as SETD7 or KMT7) methylates H3K4. By methylating H3K4, SET7 prevents chromatin condensation and promotes the transcription of fibrosis-related genes. SET7 deficiency attenuates isoproterenol-induced cardiac fibrosis and delays cardiac dysfunction.86 Thus, WDR5, DOT1L, PRMT5, SUV39H1, EZH2, SETDB1, and SET7 all promote cardiac fibrosis. In addition, inhibiting the following KDMs—KDM4A, KDM5B, KDM6B, KDM3A, and JMJD1C—ameliorates cardiac fibrosis (Figure 2).

The histone demethylase KDM4A acts as an eraser of H3K9me3. KDM4A deficiency increases H3K9me3 at the TRIM44 promoter, reducing TRIM44 expression and enhancing autophagy in senescent fibroblasts. Inhibition of KDM4A promotes autophagy in these fibroblasts, thereby suppressing post‑MI cardiac fibrosis.87

KDM5B (also known as JARID1B) demethylates H3K4me2/3, thereby repressing transcription of its target genes. KDM5B deletion or inhibition alleviates cardiac fibrosis, improves left ventricular function, and attenuates adverse remodeling after MI or pressure overload. Mechanistically, KDM5B binds the ATF3 promoter and demethylates histones to repress ATF3 expression, thereby enhancing TGF-β signaling and pro-fibrotic gene expression.88

Histone lysine-specific demethylase 6B (KDM6B), also known as Jumonji domain-containing protein 3 (JMJD3), primarily removes the methyl group from trimethylated H3K27 (H3K27me3). KDM6B inhibition increases H3K27me3 at the β-catenin promoter in activated CFs, reducing β-catenin expression and fibrotic gene transcription.89

Histone lysine demethylase 3A (KDM3A) specifically erases mono- and dimethylation marks from histone H3 at lysine 9 (H3K9me1 and H3K9me2). Functionally, KDM3A enhances the transcription of Timp1, contributing to cardiac fibrosis and left ventricular hypertrophy, whereas its pharmacological blockade mitigates cardiac remodeling in response to pressure overload.90 JMJD1C (KDM3C) demethylates H3K9me2 to promote gene expression, and its inhibition suppresses Ang II–induced cardiac hypertrophy and fibrosis.91

In summary, inhibiting these HMTs and KDMs ameliorates cardiac fibrosis in preclinical models, supporting histone methylation as a therapeutically targetable pathway.

RNA Epitranscriptomic Modifications in Cardiac Fibrosis

RNA modifications have been increasingly recognized as key regulatory factors in the development of cardiac fibrosis. Various methylation forms have been identified on eukaryotic messenger RNAs, including N6-methyladenosine (m6A), N4-acetylcytosine (ac4C), N7-methylguanosine (m7G), N1-methyladenosine (m1A), 5-methylcytosine (m5C), 2’-O-methylation (Nm) and so on. Among these, m6A represents the most prevalent internal chemical modification observed in mRNA transcripts.5

m6A Modifications in Cardiac Fibrosis

N6-methyladenosine (m6A) refers to a methyl group addition at the nitrogen-6 position of adenosine residues within RNA molecules. Representing the most prevalent internal modification found in eukaryotic mRNA, m6A accounts for the vast majority (>97%) of all documented RNA modifications.92 These modifications are predominantly concentrated near translation stop codons and are recognized by a conserved consensus motif—RRACH (where R denotes A or G, and H signifies A, C, or U). m6A modifications influence post-transcriptional gene regulation and participate in diverse cellular processes, including self-renewal, programmed cell death, immune response, differentiation, and cellular invasion. Moreover, m6A methylation is increasingly acknowledged for its pivotal role in the development and progression of cardiovascular disorders.93 The reversible nature of m6A modification is governed by the concerted functions of methyltransferases, demethylases, and binding proteins (readers), in conjunction with RNA splicing regulators. The writers are m6A methyltransferases, primarily consisting of a methyltransferase complex that centers on METTL3-METTL14, along with other auxiliary subunits including WTAP, KIAA1429, RBM15, ZC3H13, and HAKAI.94 Among them, METTL3 is the only subunit with catalytic activity, while METTL14 mainly plays a structural support role, helping to stabilize the complex and recognize substrates. In addition, recently identified m6A methyltransferases also include METTL4, METTL16, and METTL5.95–98 Demethylases, often referred to as “erasers”, primarily include fat mass and obesity-associated protein (FTO, also termed ALKBH9) and α-ketoglutarate-dependent dioxygenase alkB homolog 5 (ALKBH5). The “readers” are RNA-binding proteins that specifically recognize m6A marks on mRNA. These readers predominantly encompass the YTH domain-containing protein families—namely YTHDF1, YTHDF2, YTHDF3, and YTHDC1/2—as well as other m6A-interacting proteins like heterogeneous nuclear ribonucleoproteins (HNRNPC, HNRNPG, HNRNPA2B1), the eukaryotic translation initiation factor 3 (eIF3) and insulin-like growth factor 2 mRNA-binding proteins (IGF2BP1, IGF2BP2, IGF2BP3).43,92,99

The m6A modification regulates RNA stability, export, splicing, degradation, and translation and interacts with other epigenetic modifications.100 The binding of m6A readers to m6A-modified RNA affects subsequent RNA metabolic activities. Changes in m6A RNA methylation can lead to alterations in protein abundance independent of mRNA levels, revealing a novel transcription-independent mechanism of translational regulation.101 YTHDF2 was the earliest identified protein capable of recognizing m6A-modified transcripts and is primarily involved in promoting mRNA degradation. In contrast, YTHDF1 and YTHDF3 are known to enhance the translational efficiency of mRNAs marked with m6A modifications.102 YTHDC1 exerts several regulatory roles, such as facilitating pre-mRNA splicing through the recruitment of splicing factors, promoting the export of transcripts from the nucleus, and mediating the degradation of specific mRNAs.103 YTHDC2, by binding to m6A-modified sites, modulates both the stability and translational activity of targeted mRNAs.104 Increasing evidence suggests that m6A RNA methylation is critically involved in the pathogenesis and progression of cardiac fibrosis.105 The subsequent section focuses on the influence of m6A-regulatory proteins in fibrotic remodeling of the heart (see Figure 3). METTL3 and WTAP

METTL3 is the core regulatory subunit of the m6A methyltransferase complex. Research indicates that suppressing METTL3 activity can mitigate cardiac fibrosis. On one hand, inhibiting METTL3 can directly suppress the activation, proliferation, and migration of CFs through various molecular mechanisms, such as regulating glycolysis, non-coding RNAs, and target protein expression. Notably, METTL3 levels are markedly elevated in fibrotic heart tissue from mice experiencing chronic myocardial infarction and in CFs subjected to TGF-β1 stimulation. Functional assessments in both cellular and animal models reveal that excessive METTL3 expression enhances CF proliferation and fibrotic remodeling, whereas silencing METTL3 exhibits antifibrotic effects. Targeted knockdown of METTL3 diminishes fibrosis following MI, largely by impeding the activation of CFs.106,107 METTL3 silencing inhibits glycolytic activity, thereby reducing CF proliferation and fibrotic progression. Mechanistically, METTL3 overexpression enhances methylation and subsequent degradation of the androgen receptor (AR). Reduced AR expression results in upregulation of periostin (Postn) and hypoxia-inducible factor 1-alpha (HIF-1α), facilitating glycolytic reprogramming and proliferation of CFs, which contributes to fibrotic remodeling.108 Additionally, increased METTL3 expression promotes methylation of lncRNA GAS5, leading to excessive mitochondrial fission, further stimulating CF migration and proliferation. Inhibition of METTL3 can suppress this mitochondrial fission process and thereby alleviate fibrotic responses.109 Moreover, lncRNA MetBil promotes CF activation and cardiac fibrosis through METTL3-mediated m6A modification of fibrosis-related mRNA. METTL3 silencing (siMETTL3) reduces the expression and m6A levels of collagen-related genes and fibrosis-promoting factors.110 Upregulation of METTL3 may promote IGFBP3 protein expression through m6A modification, thereby facilitating CF activation; conversely, knockdown of METTL3 significantly attenuates CF activation and fibrosis in both in vitro and in vivo models.111 Upregulation of METTL3 promotes m6A modification of Akt mRNA and enhances its stability via the reader protein IGF2BP1, thereby activating the Akt/mTOR signaling pathway to drive CF activation and cardiac fibrosis. In vivo administration of STM2457, a highly selective METTL3 inhibitor, effectively alleviates radiation-induced cardiac fibrosis.112 METTL3 also regulates CF proliferation and differentiation by increasing m6A methylation of SMOC2 mRNA to enhance its stability; conversely, downregulation of METTL3 in vivo reduces post‑MI cardiac fibrosis.113 Moreover, deficiency of the methyltransferases METTL3 and METTL14 has been shown to markedly suppress angiotensin II–induced cardiac inflammation and fibrotic remodeling, primarily through the downregulation of m6A methylation on MyD88 and TGF-β1 transcripts.114

On the other hand, inhibiting METTL3 can indirectly suppress cardiac fibrosis by inhibiting cardiomyocyte hypertrophy, apoptosis, and necrosis, as well as by reducing oxidative stress, among other pathways. Enhanced RNA m6A methylation leads to compensatory cardiac hypertrophy, and increased METTL3 expression is sufficient to promote cardiomyocyte hypertrophy both in vitro and in vivo.93 Silencing METTL3 expression enhances autophagic flux and inhibits apoptosis in cardiomyocytes under hypoxia/reoxygenation (H/R) conditions.115 Overexpression of METTL3 exacerbates heart failure and cardiac fibrosis at four weeks post-MI. Mechanistically, METTL3 increases m6A levels of tenascin-C (TNC), an important ECM glycoprotein, and promotes TNC mRNA stability, leading to cardiac fibrosis and myocardial apoptosis after MI.116 Pharmacological or genetic inhibition of METTL3 mitigates myocardial inflammatory fibrosis associated with reperfusion injury and reduces cardiomyocyte apoptosis by interfering with mitochondrial division mediated by the DNA-PKcs/Fis1 signaling axis, thereby preserving cardiac performance.117 Additionally, emerging evidence suggests that structured aerobic exercise alleviates ischemia-reperfusion-induced myocardial injury and fibrotic progression in murine models, potentially by lowering METTL3-mediated m6A methylation in cardiac cells and activating the antioxidant defense pathway governed by Nrf2 and HO-1.118

Besides, upregulation of WTAP has been implicated in enhancing mitochondrial lipid metabolism as well as promoting the proliferation and migration of CFs, primarily through increased m6A methylation of the androgen receptor (AR), thereby facilitating the development of diabetic cardiac fibrosis. At the mechanistic level, WTAP-induced m6A modification leads to suppressed AR expression. The AR is believed to influence the transcriptional activity of the mitochondrial lipid oxidation enzyme Decr1 by binding to specific promoter regions, thus indirectly regulating lipid oxidation and the activation of CFs.119

FTO and ALKBH5

FTO has been identified as a key regulator exerting protective effects against the progression of cardiac fibrosis. Its expression is notably diminished in failing mammalian hearts and cardiomyocytes subjected to hypoxic conditions, which leads to elevated m6A RNA methylation levels and impaired myocardial contractility. Experimental data from murine models of MI demonstrate that cardiac-specific overexpression of FTO alleviates fibrotic remodeling and enhances systolic function.120 Similarly, in diabetic cardiomyopathy models, FTO upregulation attenuates cardiac fibrosis and reduces hypertrophic changes in cardiomyocytes, thereby improving overall cardiac performance.121 The anti-fibrotic mechanism of FTO following MI involves m6A demethylation of EPRS (glutamyl-prolyl-tRNA synthetase), a transcript associated with fibrosis. Specifically, FTO influences the post-transcriptional stability of EPRS mRNA through an IGF2BP3-mediated, m6A-dependent pathway, ultimately inhibiting collagen production via suppression of the TGF-β/SMAD2/3 signaling cascade.122 Inhibition of FTO attenuates the antifibrotic effects of leonurine (a cardioprotective compound) on CFs in rats.123

Emerging evidence suggests that ALKBH5 plays a multifaceted role in modulating both MI outcomes and the progression of cardiac fibrosis. During hypoxic conditions following MI, ALKBH5 facilitates the transformation of fibroblasts into myofibroblasts, a process crucial for maintaining structural integrity and preventing cardiac rupture. In CFs, ALKBH5 contributes to post-MI tissue repair by enhancing the stability of ErbB4 mRNA via m6A demethylation mechanisms.124 Additionally, ALKBH5 promotes pathological fibrotic remodeling by influencing immune cell phenotypes; specifically, it promotes the differentiation of macrophages into myofibroblasts through m6A-dependent modification of IL-11 transcripts. The demethylation of IL-11 mRNA increases both its transcript stability and corresponding protein expression, thereby exacerbating fibrotic responses within the myocardium.125 These findings suggest that ALKBH5 cannot be simply categorized as either purely protective or purely pathogenic in cardiac fibrosis. Rather, its functional output depends on the balance between protective repair programs and pathological remodeling programs, which in turn is dictated by the disease stage, the specific cell types involved, and the particular m6A-modified transcripts targeted. This duality has important implications for therapeutic targeting: systemic inhibition of ALKBH5 may impair essential post-MI repair processes and increase the risk of cardiac rupture, whereas cell-type-specific or temporally restricted strategies—such as targeting ALKBH5 specifically in macrophages or during the chronic phase—may offer more viable therapeutic approaches.

YTHDF1/3 and eIF3a

YTHDF1/3 and eIF3a may play a critical role in cardiac fibrosis. YTHDF1 enhances the translation of AXL (receptor tyrosine kinase) and activates the TGF-β-Smad2/3 signaling pathway, thereby exacerbating the progression of heart failure and cardiac fibrosis.126 However, another research indicates that YTHDF1 deficiency contributes to the development of fibrotic conditions. Mechanistically, YTHDF1 interacts with m6A-modified Caveolin 1 (Cav1) transcripts, enhancing their translational efficiency.127 The latest evidence suggests that YTHDF1 recognizes m6A-modified Runx1 mRNA and enhances its translation, thereby stimulating RUNX1-mediated Ctgf transcription, which promotes fibroblast activation and cardiac fibrosis.128 YTHDF1 exerts opposing effects depending on cell type: it drives fibrotic activation in cardiac fibroblasts, yet its loss in cardiomyocytes disrupts homeostatic maintenance, leading to spontaneous fibrosis. This dichotomy, shaped by context-dependent m6A target selection and crosstalk with ALKBH5 and METTL3, carries critical therapeutic implications: systemic YTHDF1 inhibition risks disrupting cardiac homeostasis, whereas fibroblast-restricted strategies offer a more viable and safer approach.

Furthermore, YTHDF3 has been implicated in mitigating cardiac fibrosis through modulation of the FLCN/cPLA2 signaling axis, which regulates lysosomal activity.129 Additionally, genetic suppression of eIF3a has been shown to attenuate fibrotic progression in cardiac tissue, primarily through inhibition of the TGF-β1/Smad3 signaling cascade.130

Role of N4-Acetylcytidine Modifications in Cardiac Fibrosis

The RNA modification N4-acetylcytidine (ac4C) is ubiquitously observed across diverse RNA species. N-acetylcysteine 10 (NAT10), the principal enzyme responsible for ac4C modification, governs mRNA stability and translational efficacy through acetyltransferase and RNA-binding functionalities.

Pathological cardiac remodeling models, including in vitro systems, in vivo studies, and clinical samples from hypertrophic cardiomyopathy patients, reveal pronounced upregulation of NAT10 and ac4C-modified RNAs. Genetic or pharmacological suppression of NAT10 reduces angiotensin II–induced cardiomyocyte enlargement and fibroblasts activation. Remodelin, a selective NAT10 inhibitor, exhibits cardioprotective effects in aortic constriction models by attenuating fibrotic progression, hypertrophic remodeling, and inflammatory responses. Mechanistically, NAT10 catalyzes ac4C incorporation into CD47 and ROCK2 (Rho-associated coiled-coil kinase 2) transcripts, bolstering their structural integrity and translational output to amplify protein synthesis during cardiac stress adaptation.131

Post-MI analyses demonstrate marked elevation of Nat10 mRNA and protein in murine infarct and peri-infarct regions, with fibroblast-specific Nat10 expression surpassing cardiomyocyte levels. Fibroblast-specific Nat10 ablation mitigates post-MI ventricular dysfunction and ECM reorganization. Molecular investigations identify Angiomotin-like 1 (Amotl1), a Hippo pathway modulator, as a Nat10 substrate. Nat10-dependent ac4C modification stabilizes Amotl1 transcripts and augments their translation in fibroblasts, intensifying Amotl1-YAP (Yes-associated protein 1) binding to drive YAP nuclear translocation.132 NAT10 inhibition suppresses RNA ac4C acetylation, alleviating MI-induced fibrosis. This occurs via NAT10-mediated stabilization of BCL-XL mRNA through ac4C modification, elevating BCL-XL protein levels to inhibit fibroblast apoptosis.133

Elevated NAT10 expression post-MI correlates with fibrotic pathogenesis. Inhibition of NAT10 downregulates early growth response 3 (EGR3), a pro-fibrotic mediator, thereby curbing cardiac fibrogenesis. Intriguingly, overexpression of tRNA-derived small RNA tsr007330 counteracts NAT10’s regulatory effects, restoring cardiac function and attenuating collagen deposition in post-MI hearts.134

m7G Modification in Cardiac Fibrosis

Methyltransferase-like protein 1 (METTL1) is a key enzyme responsible for modifying RNA through the methylation of guanosine to form m7G. The RNA N7-methylguanosine (m7G) modification catalyzed by Mettl1 is closely associated with ischemic heart injury and fibrosis.

Elevated expression of METTL1 has been documented in both human failing hearts and murine models of cardiac hypertrophy. Genetic ablation of METTL1 attenuates pressure overload-induced cardiac hypertrophy and functional impairment, whether triggered by transverse aortic constriction or angiotensin II administration. Conversely, cardiac-specific METTL1 overexpression exacerbates pathological remodeling. Mechanistically, METTL1 drives hypertrophic progression by catalyzing m7G methylation of SRSF9 mRNA and enhancing its stability and translational output. This upregulates SRSF9, an RNA-binding protein that promotes alternative splicing and stabilization of NFATc4 (a member of the nuclear factor of activated T-cells family), fostering pro-hypertrophic signaling.135 Emerging evidence further implicates METTL1-mediated m7G methylation in promoting fibrotic pathways. Fibroblast-specific METTL1 deletion mitigates MI-induced cardiac fibrosis and improves functional outcomes in heart failure models.136

In summary, RNA modifications play intricate and pivotal regulatory roles in the pathophysiological progression of cardiac fibrosis. The homeostatic balance of RNA modification related regulatory enzymes constitutes the fundamental basis for preserving their normal regulatory functions and maintaining the microenvironmental homeostasis of cardiac tissue. Under pathological stimuli, disruption of m6A regulatory homeostasis directly contributes to the initiation and progression of cardiac fibrosis. Specifically, targeted inhibition of the aberrantly upregulated m6A methyltransferase METTL3 attenuates cardiac fibrosis and confers cardioprotective effects. Conversely, upregulation of the demethylase FTO alleviates pathological cardiac remodeling and improves cardiac function. The m6A demethylase ALKBH5 may exert stage specific bidirectional regulation following MI. During the acute phase, ALKBH5 promotes fibroblast to myofibroblast transition to facilitate cardiac tissue repair, whereas in the chronic phase, sustained ALKBH5 overexpression exacerbates cardiac fibrosis by driving macrophage to myofibroblast transition. Moreover, the reader protein YTHDF3 contributes to the modulation of aberrant m6A deposition and exerts cardioprotective effects. In addition, inhibition of METTL1-mediated m7G modification and suppression of NAT-mediated ac4C modification have also been validated to restrain cardiac fibrosis. Collectively, precise targeting of the RNA modification regulatory network and preservation of enzymatic homeostasis within this system hold substantial scientific significance and clinical promise for the prevention and treatment of cardiac fibrosis.

Non-Coding RNA Regulatory Networks in Cardiac Fibrosis

Non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs), comprising functional RNA species such as microRNAs (miRNAs), long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs), circular RNAs (circRNAs), small nucleolar RNAs (snoRNAs), and transfer RNAs (tRNAs), are pivotal regulators of cardiac fibrosis progression. These ncRNAs regulate key fibrosis-related signaling pathways (eg, TGF-β and Wnt) through transcriptional and post-transcriptional mechanisms, thereby driving aberrant ECM deposition and promoting cellular remodeling such as myofibroblast activation.

miRNAs in Cardiac Fibrosis

miRNAs, small ncRNAs typically 18 ~ 22 nucleotides in length, function as pivotal post-transcriptional regulators by binding to complementary sequences in target mRNAs, resulting in mRNA destabilization or translational repression. Accumulating evidence underscores their critical involvement in fibrotic cardiac pathology (see Figure 4). LncRNAs in Cardiac Fibrosis

LncRNAs, defined as RNA transcripts exceeding 200 nucleotides in length and lacking protein-coding potential, serve as critical regulators of cellular processes. These molecules modulate diverse biological processes through dynamic interactions with proteins, nucleic acids, and other RNAs. Within the nucleus, lncRNAs orchestrate chromatin-modifying complexes and transcriptional regulators, either repressing or activating gene expression by guiding these factors to specific genomic loci. In cytoplasmic compartments, they function as molecular scaffolds or competitive sponges, sequestering proteins or transcripts to influence mRNA stability and translational efficiency.186 Additionally, lncRNAs modulate epigenetic regulation through DNA methylation, histone modification, and chromatin remodeling, thereby establishing long-term transcriptional programs.187 Emerging evidence underscores their critical involvement in driving cardiac fibrosis pathogenesis, positioning them as key regulators of fibrotic progression.

LncRNAs as Regulators of Cardiac Fibrosis via Sponging miRNAs

LncRNA GAS5

LncRNA GAS5 has been implicated in the pathogenesis of various cardiovascular disorders, exhibiting dual regulatory roles in cardiac fibrogenesis. It has been reported that genetic silencing of lncRNA GAS5 suppresses apoptosis and fibrotic remodeling in diabetic cardiomyopathy models through interaction with miR-26a/b-5p.188 However, lncRNA GAS5 mitigates isoproterenol-induced fibrosis by downregulating the miR-217/SIRT1 axis.189 Exogenous overexpression of GAS5 in murine models reduces collagen deposition via modulation of the PTEN/MMP-2 signaling pathway.209

LncRNA H19

The expression of long non-coding RNA H19 is subject to dynamic regulation and plays a role in various signaling pathways across different cardiac cell populations. Dysregulation of H19 has been linked to pathological conditions such as cardiac fibrosis, infarction, atherosclerotic changes, and coronary artery disease. Under hypoxic stress, H19 facilitates fibrotic progression by interacting with Y-box-binding protein 1 (YB-1), thereby impairing its ability to suppress collagen type I alpha 1 (COL1A1) gene expression.210 In addition, H19 contributes to fibrotic remodeling by functioning as a molecular sponge for miR-455, consequently enhancing the expression of connective tissue growth factor (CTGF).190 Conversely, some studies suggest a protective function of H19, as it targets miR-22-3p to modulate KDM3A expression, alleviating myocardial damage and post-infarction cardiac remodeling.191 These findings indicate that lncRNA H19 may exert context-dependent effects in cardiac fibrosis through diverse regulatory mechanisms.

LncRNA MIAT

Myocardial infarction-associated transcript (MIAT), a pro-fibrotic lncRNA, regulates cardiac fibrosis after MI. Knock-down of MIAT reduced cardiac fibrosis and improved cardiac function by acting as a sponge for miR-24,192 and downregulation of lncRNA MIAT alleviates atrial fibrillation (AF) and AF-induced cardiac fibrosis by targeting miR-133a-3p.193 Downregulation of lncRNA MIAT via electroacupuncture mitigates cardiac fibrosis,211 and its genetic ablation alleviates cardiac hypertrophy and heart failure,212 establishing MIAT as a therapeutic target for cardioprotection.

LncRNA TUG1

The long non-coding RNA taurine-upregulated gene 1 (lncRNA TUG1) has emerged as a potential prognostic indicator for cardiovascular diseases (CVDs).213 Functioning as a competing endogenous RNA (ceRNA), TUG1 regulates the expression of connective tissue growth factor (CTGF) following MI by sequestering miR-133b, thereby influencing cardiac fibrosis progression.194 In models of diabetic cardiomyopathy, TUG1 has also been shown to aggravate fibrotic remodeling of the myocardium by binding to miR-145a-5p, leading to increased expression of Cfl2.195

In summary, some lncRNAs primarily function as miRNA molecular sponges, relieving the inhibitory effects of miRNAs on downstream pro-fibrotic target genes and thereby exacerbating pathological remodeling. Among them, lncRNA MIAT and lncRNA TUG1 may exert anti-fibrotic effects by targeting distinct sets of miRNAs. In contrast, lncRNA NAV2-AS2 and LINC00707 confer endogenous anti-fibrotic protection by sequestering miRNAs that would otherwise suppress protective gene expression. Furthermore, lncRNA GAS5 and lncRNA H19 exhibit context-dependent dual functions in cardiac fibrosis, promoting or inhibiting fibrogenesis by engaging different miRNAs under distinct pathological settings. Collectively, these findings underscore that lncRNA-mediated regulation of fibrosis is not a simple binary switch but rather a complex, dynamic equilibrium within the lncRNA-miRNA-mRNA regulatory network.

LncRNAs Regulate Cardiac Fibrosis by Modulating the TGF-β Signaling Pathway and ECM Gene Expression

A subset of pro-fibrotic lncRNAs exert their effects through mechanisms that directly or indirectly converge on hyperactivation of the TGF-β1/Smad signaling pathway. The specific mechanistic strategies include:

1) Direct activation of ligands or receptors: For example, lncRNA 554 and lncRNA SOX2OT directly engage TGF-β1 signal transduction.

2) Relieving inhibitory constraints: lnc-Ang362 and LINC00152 amplify TGF-β1 signal transduction by suppressing the negative regulator Smad7.

3) Stabilizing key auxiliary factors: lncRNA Cfast promotes SMAD2/SMAD4 complex assembly through modulation of the COTL1/TRAP1 interaction.

Beyond targeting the canonical TGF-β1/Smad pathway, certain lncRNAs further exacerbate pathological cardiac remodeling by regulating collagen synthesis and secretion or promoting cardiomyocyte hypertrophy:

1) Promotion of collagen synthesis and cross-linking: lncRNA Wisper modulates lysyl hydroxylase 2 to influence collagen cross-linking and ECM stiffness, whereas lncRNA Meg3 affects collagen deposition via regulation of MMP-2.

2) Modulation of RNA stability and transcriptional control: lncRNA HOTAIR binds PTBP1 to stabilize Wnt5a transcripts; lncRNA FIXER interacts with CBX4 to target the RUNX1 promoter; and lncRNA CFIRL facilitates ENO1-mediated IL-6 transcription, thereby inducing cardiomyocyte hypertrophy. LncRNA ROR exacerbates fibrotic remodeling via c-Myc transcriptional upregulation. LncRNA-Safe drives fibrosis via stabilizing Sfrp2 transcripts through a tripartite complex with Sfrp2 (secreted frizzled-related protein 2) and HuR (RNA-binding protein).

3) Translational repression: The antisense lncRNA RASSF1-AS1 directly suppresses RASSF1A protein synthesis.

Several lncRNAs, including LncRNA FAF, lncRNA NRON, lncRNA Linc00092, lncRNA Crnde, and lncRNA SAIL, exert anti-fibrotic effects in the heart. Mechanistically, this class of lncRNAs exerts protective effects by negatively regulating canonical pro-fibrotic signaling pathways. LncRNA FAF targets fibroblast growth factor 9 (FGF9) to suppress TGF-β1/P-Smad2/3 activation, while lncRNA Crnde operates via a Smad3 negative feedback loop in diabetic cardiomyopathy.230,231 Additionally, lncRNA NRON alleviates atrial fibrosis by promoting the phosphorylation of nuclear factor NFATc3, a highly sensitive transcription factor.232 LncRNA SAIL influences the progression of cardiac fibrosis by regulating the transcriptional activity of genes associated with fibrogenesis, a process mediated by the nuclear scaffold protein SAFB (scaffold attachment factor B).233 LncRNA Linc00092 has been identified as a negative regulator of CF activation, functioning through modulation of glycolytic metabolism via an ERK-dependent signaling pathway.234

The Crosstalk Between lncRNAs and Other Epigenetic Regulators in Cardiac Fibrosis

In addition, lncRNA Neat1 can recruit the histone lysine methyltransferase EZH2 to bind to the promoter region of Smad7, induce H3K27me3 methylation on the Smad7 promoter, thereby suppressing Smad7 expression, and ultimately promote cardiac fibrosis.235

In a diabetic cardiomyopathy model, lncRNA Airn prevents the degradation of IGF2BP2, thereby restoring its recognition of m6A‑modified p53 mRNA, promoting the stabilization of p53 mRNA, and consequently alleviating myocardial fibrosis.236

Collectively, the evidence demonstrates that lncRNAs constitute an intricate bidirectional regulatory network in the pathogenesis of cardiac fibrosis, with their ultimate functional outcomes dictated by their mode of intervention on the TGF-β/Smad signaling axis and associated downstream events. Therapeutically, the lncRNA network represents a promising and versatile target. Rather than focusing on a single transcript, restoring the equilibrium between pro-fibrotic and anti-fibrotic lncRNAs—for instance, via antisense oligonucleotide (ASO)-mediated silencing of pathogenic lncRNAs or viral vector-mediated overexpression of protective lncRNAs—offers a strategy to simultaneously dampen multiple fibrotic effectors while preserving or enhancing endogenous protective barriers. This network-centric therapeutic approach holds considerable potential for halting or even reversing pathological cardiac remodeling with enhanced specificity and reduced off-target effects compared to direct targeting of ubiquitous master regulators such as TGF-β receptors.

CircRNAs in Cardiac Fibrosis

CircRNAs represent a unique subclass of non-coding RNAs characterized by a covalently closed continuous loop, formed through back-splicing of exonic sequences. This circular configuration endows circRNAs with enhanced resistance to exonuclease-mediated degradation compared to their linear counterparts. Abundantly present in cardiac tissue, circRNAs are increasingly recognized for their regulatory functions in various cardiovascular pathologies, including cardiac fibrosis.237 Accumulating evidence suggests that circRNAs contribute to the fibrotic remodeling of the myocardium through several distinct mechanisms (refer to Table5):238–264 (1) acting as molecular sponges for microRNAs, thereby modulating post-transcriptional gene expression; (2) serving as scaffolds for protein interactions or modulating protein activity; (3) undergoing translation into functional peptides in certain contexts; and (4) influencing transcriptional regulation of target genes. Role of circRNA in Cardiac Fibrosis

In summary, miRNAs, lncRNAs, and circRNAs may individually participate in promoting or suppressing cardiac fibrosis via distinct signaling pathways. Alternatively, they may form an intricately interconnected regulatory network through mechanisms such as competing endogenous RNA crosstalk, thereby collectively orchestrating the fibrotic remodeling of the myocardium. This integrated circuitry not only underscores the multifaceted molecular underpinnings of fibrotic cardiac pathology but also unveils a compelling dual prospect: the development of combinatorial non-coding RNA signatures as robust diagnostic and prognostic biomarkers, and the strategic targeting of key network hubs as a novel, multi-pronged therapeutic avenue. Elucidating these synergistic interactions may thus provide a foundational framework for advancing precision-based strategies in the prevention and treatment of cardiac fibrosis.

Chromatin Remodeling in Cardiac Fibrosis

Chromatin remodeling encompasses dynamic molecular processes that modify DNA packaging to regulate transcriptional activity, replication, and recombination. Due to the inherent inaccessibility of tightly packed genomic DNA, these modifications are indispensable for enabling gene expression. Specialized chromatin remodeling complexes orchestrate these changes by leveraging energy from ATP hydrolysis to reconfigure chromatin architecture, thereby exposing regulatory DNA sequences to transcriptional machinery.265 These complexes are categorized into four primary families—SWI/SNF, ISWI (imitation switch), CHD (chromodomain helicase DNA-binding), and INO80—based on conserved structural motifs and ATPase domain organization.266 Notably, the SWI/SNF family has been strongly linked to fibrotic cardiac pathology, highlighting its role in disease progression and potential therapeutic targeting.

Evidence suggests that Brahma-related gene 1 (Smarca4/Brg1), a highly conserved catalytic component of the SWI/SNF chromatin remodeling complex, plays a pivotal role in modulating chromatin architecture by displacing histone proteins from condensed DNA regions. This displacement enhances DNA accessibility for transcription factors and other essential regulators of gene expression. Structurally, Brg1 features a central ATPase motif, which drives chromatin remodeling activity, and a C-terminal bromodomain, which specifically binds to acetylated lysine residues on histone tails. This bromodomain facilitates the recruitment of Brg1 to specific genomic loci and mediates interactions with various regulatory proteins, including nuclear receptors, histone-modifying enzymes, and transcriptional activators. Experimental studies demonstrate that in vivo silencing of Brg1 markedly attenuates fibrosis and adventitial tissue remodeling.267 Additional research has identified BRG1 as a central transcriptional regulator in Angiotensin II (Ang II)-induced endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT), a process contributing to cardiac fibrosis. Endothelial cell-specific deletion of Brg1 in murine models has been shown to significantly alleviate EndMT-associated pathological changes and reduce cardiac fibrotic responses triggered by Ang II infusion.268

The INO80 complex is an evolutionarily conserved ATP-dependent chromatin remodeling complex involved in transcriptional regulation, DNA replication and repair, telomere maintenance, and chromosome segregation. Overexpression of the INO80 complex regulates transcriptional networks by remodeling chromatin accessibility. Studies have shown that INO80 is a key upstream regulator of several cardiac function modulators, and its expression levels directly determine the functional state of the heart.269 Targeted overexpression of Ino80 in cardiomyocytes leads to rapid and severe heart failure, whereas selective deletion of Ino80 preserves cardiac function in the mouse models of heart failure.269

Frontier Technologies and Translational Prospects of Epigenetic Regulation

Frontier Technologies in Epigenetic Research on Cardiac Fibrosis

Integrating single-cell multi-omics technologies including scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq and scWGBS enables the construction of cell-type-specific epigenetic landscapes and in-depth elucidation of the molecular and cellular mechanisms driving fibrosis heterogeneity. While scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq have been applied to dissect cellular heterogeneity and epigenetic regulation in cardiac fibrosis, the dedicated application of scWGBS in this context remains in its infancy. Cleavage Under Targets and Tagmentation (CUT&Tag), with its significant advantages of low sample requirement, high signal-to-noise ratio, and high resolution, allows us to map genome-wide protein-DNA interaction profiles from a finer perspective and in a more efficient manner, and is gradually emerging as a mainstream approach in epigenetics.270 In combination with RNA-seq, CUT&Tag has been increasingly established as a powerful tool for dissecting epigenetic regulation, including transcription factor binding and histone modifications, in cardiac fibrosis. Multiple Target Identification by Tagmentation (MulTI-Tag) and spatial-CUT&Tag hold promise for dissecting the synergistic and antagonistic patterns and spatial distribution of epigenetic regulation at both multi-target and spatial dimensions. Although MulTI-Tag and spatial-CUT&Tag have not yet been systematically applied to cardiac fibrotic tissues, their technical characteristics suggest substantial potential for elucidating cellular epigenetic regulatory networks in fibrotic diseases.271–273 The emergence of single-cell chromatin proteins and accessibility tagmentation (scCPA-Tag) enables the researchers to directly observe the association between chromatin accessibility and histone modifications in cells with unprecedented precision and efficiency.274 Indexed tagmentation-based single-cell CUT&Tag-sequencing (IT-scC&T-seq) enables parallel analysis of multiple samples and different targets (eg, multiple histones, transcription factors), and holds promise to further advance the dissection of cellular heterogeneity and epigenetic regulatory networks in cardiac fibrosis.275 While the current applications of scCPA-Tag and IT-scC&T-seq remain largely confined to oncology and developmental biology, their potential utility in unraveling fibrotic heterogeneity merits further investigation.

In the study of epigenetic regulation in cardiac fibrosis, the CRISPR/Cas9 system is playing an important role. It can be employed as a functional screening tool; using CRISPRi or CRISPR-KO to suppress gene expression enables the identification of epigenetic regulators that drive the progression of cardiac fibrosis.276 Moreover, CRISPR-based gene activation (CRISPRa)-mediated activation of endogenous transcription factors, particularly Gata4, reduces dependence on exogenous transcription factors and promotes the direct conversion of fibroblasts into induced cardiomyocytes (iCMs).277 Furthermore, a nuclease‑dead dCas9 fused with the transcriptional activator VPR (dCas9VPR, a CRISPR activation system) can be used to specifically enhance KLF15 expression in cardiomyocytes, thereby suppressing pathological gene expression, restoring cellular metabolic homeostasis, and inhibiting cardiac fibrosis.278 In summary, the CRISPR/Cas9 technology not only serves as a genetic functional screening tool for dissecting the molecular mechanisms of fibrosis, but also provides a powerful and precise intervention tool for regulating the expression of fibrosis-related genes.

Summary of Epigenetic-Targeting Compounds in Cardiac Fibrosis

Loganin, an iridoid glycoside extracted from Cornus officinalis, directly bound to and inhibited the catalytic activity of DNMT1, reduced the methylation level of the UQCRC1 promoter, restored mitochondrial complex III function, and thereby alleviated cardiac fibrosis after MI.279 Loganin exhibits low oral bioavailability and is predominantly distributed in the kidneys rather than the heart, resulting in limited cardiac exposure. Although no overt toxicity has been reported in current preclinical studies, a comprehensive systematic toxicological evaluation is still lacking.280

Pharmacological inhibition of p300 has been demonstrated to alleviate cardiac fibrosis resulting from hypertension.281,282 In preclinical studies involving rats, polyunsaturated fatty acids—including eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) and docosahexaenoic acid (DHA)—were found to ameliorate MI-induced heart failure, an effect associated with the suppression of p300-HAT enzymatic activity.283 The dose-response relationship of EPA and DHA exhibits individual variability. Conventional formulations have low cardiac delivery efficiency, whereas emulsion-based delivery can significantly increase their distribution in cardiac tissue and enhance their absorption.284 Moreover, curcumin, a naturally derived p300-HAT inhibitor, has been reported to markedly reduce perivascular fibrosis and downregulate the expression of genes associated with cardiac hypertrophy under hypertensive conditions.285 Curcumin suffers from low oral bioavailability and poor aqueous solubility, and its safety profile is formulation-dependent. Nanoparticle-based delivery systems are currently being explored to improve its pharmacokinetic properties and reduce potential toxicity, although systematic long-term safety data remain lacking.286

Research has demonstrated that HDAC inhibitors reverse existing diastolic dysfunction by blocking CF activation and subsequent ECM deposition in the heart.287 Studies indicate that rhein, a bioactive compound from rhubarb, acts as a potent HDAC inhibitor with remarkable anti-fibrotic efficacy in human cardiac fibrosis.288 Se-SAHA, a newly developed HDAC inhibitor, has shown protective effects against isoproterenol-induced cardiac dysfunction and fibrosis, primarily by suppressing oxidative stress and modulating autophagic activity. This compound also counteracts the isoproterenol-induced upregulation of HDAC1 and HDAC6 expression.289 Treatment with TYA-018, a specific HDAC6 inhibitor, significantly improves cardiac function and attenuates fibroblast activation in male mice with heart failure.290 TN-301, a clinical-stage HDAC6 inhibitor structurally and functionally analogous to the preclinical tool compound TYA-018, has completed a Phase I trial that demonstrated safety, target engagement, and suitability for once-daily dosing.291

In addition, administration of nicotinamide mononucleotide has also been shown to counteract isoproterenol-induced cardiac fibrosis, an effect that involves attenuation of oxidative damage and decreased Smad3 acetylation in a NAD+/SIRT1-dependent manner.292 Taurine, another bioactive compound, exerted beneficial effects against TAC-induced cardiac impairment and fibrosis by elevating the NAD+/NADH ratio, enhancing SIRT1 levels, and downregulating p53 acetylation.293 Resveratrol, a natural non-flavonoid polyphenol, inhibited cardiac fibrosis through activation of SIRT1.294 Resveratrol exhibits low oral bioavailability and unfavorable pharmacokinetic profiles; given that this compound has not yet been approved for clinical use, its systematic pharmacodynamic and clinical safety evaluations remain to be further investigated.295 Furthermore, pharmacological activation of SIRT3 using the compound 2-APQC has been shown to alleviate cardiac fibrosis, potentially through modulation of mitochondrial function.296

BRD4 inhibitors showed significant potential for treating organ fibrosis, with about a dozen currently being investigated in preclinical studies.297 Apabetalone (RVX-208), an oral BD2-selective BET inhibitor, is the only direct epigenetic-targeting agent that has been evaluated in Phase III clinical trials for cardiovascular diseases.298 JQ1, a BET inhibitor, suppressed Ang II–induced atrial fibrosis. Mechanistically, JQ1 inhibited upregulation of pro-fibrotic genes, restored Sirt1 expression, and improved calcium homeostasis and mitochondrial respiratory function.299

Inhibition of METTL3 significantly alleviated fibrosis and improved cardiac function in multiple cardiac injury models. The METTL3-specific inhibitor STM2457 is currently the most extensively studied m6A-targeting candidate drug.300 Delivery of STM2457 using erythrocyte-derived microvesicles effectively targeted inflamed cardiac tissue, reducing cardiac fibrosis and improving cardiac function.114

In addition, the repurposing of existing drugs opens up new avenues for drug development. Valproic acid, an established inhibitor of histone deacetylases, has been reported to mitigate atrial structural remodeling and postpone the development of atrial fibrillation in murine models.301 Exendin-4, approved for type 2 diabetes, mitigates post-infarction inflammation and adverse structural remodeling by promoting SIRT1-mediated suppression of poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase 1 (PARP1) in MI rat model.302 Dapagliflozin, approved for type 2 diabetes and heart failure, directly inhibits high glucose-induced CF proliferation and fibrosis by reducing METTL3-mediated m6A modification.303

Numerous studies have confirmed that ncRNAs are highly promising targets for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. To date, multiple technological approaches have emerged, including the use of antisense oligonucleotides (ASOs) to degrade pathogenic ncRNAs, as well as the application of tools such as miRNA mimics, CRISPR/dCas9, and others for gene editing or epigenetic regulation.

In summary, the vast majority of candidate epigenetic modulators currently under investigation remain at the preclinical stage, with only limited progress toward clinical translation. Delivery Challenges and Translational Bottlenecks in Epigenetic Therapies for Cardiac Fibrosis

Challenges in Cardiac-Specific Drug Delivery

Fibroblast Heterogeneity

First, CFs do not constitute a functionally homogeneous population. Recent advances in single-cell transcriptomics, lineage tracing, and epigenetic profiling have revealed that CFs exhibit substantial heterogeneity and phenotypic plasticity in the adult heart, with distinct subpopulations playing divergent and even opposing roles in the progression of fibrosis.304 Certain subsets drive pathological extracellular matrix deposition, whereas others participate in tissue repair and homeostatic maintenance.

Second, there is currently a lack of highly specific cell surface markers for fibroblasts. Fibroblast activation protein is expressed at very low levels in normal tissues but is selectively and robustly upregulated by CFs following myocardial infarction and in cardiomyopathies, making it one of the most promising targeting candidates to date.305 However, fibroblast activation protein is not unique to CFs, and its expression may also be present in other cell types.

Epigenetic modulators are typically pleiotropic, affecting multiple cell types and signaling pathways, and fibroblast-restricted genomic targeting remains difficult to achieve at present. Precise identification of pathogenic fibroblast subsets—distinct from reparative populations—remains the foremost barrier to cell-type-specific epigenetic intervention.

Terminally Differentiated Cardiomyocytes and High Metabolic Activity

Adult cardiomyocytes are terminally differentiated cells with an extremely limited capacity for proliferation. This characteristic imposes a fundamental constraint on DNA methyltransferase inhibitors, which rely on cell division for DNA incorporation, resulting in substantially lower genomic incorporation efficiency in the myocardium compared to highly proliferative tissues. Similarly, viral vector-based gene delivery achieves considerably lower transduction efficiency in the heart than in rapidly dividing cells. Furthermore, as a highly metabolically active organ, the heart imposes a physical barrier to local drug retention due to the hemodynamic stress generated by continuous contraction. Following systemic administration, the distribution of epigenetic agents to the heart is far lower than that to first-pass organs such as the liver.

Current Delivery Platforms: Advances and Bottlenecks

In recent years, multiple delivery strategies have been explored to improve the cardiac targeting of epigenetic modulators.

Nanoparticles and extracellular vesicles: Mesenchymal stem cell membrane-coated biomimetic nanodelivery systems have been shown to alleviate cardiac fibrosis by restoring mitochondrial homeostasis.306 However, nanoparticle-based approaches are limited by their modest loading capacity and require further improvement in in vivo targeting efficiency. Extracellular vesicles (EVs), particularly exosomes (30–150 nm), have emerged as important mediators of intercellular communication, functioning through the transport of proteins, lipids, and non-coding RNAs. EVs derived from human adult cardiomyocytes exhibit anti-fibrotic properties.307 Nevertheless, the loading capacity and scalable production of EVs remain constrained.

Viral vectors: Adeno-associated virus are widely used for cardiac gene delivery; however, the development of fibroblast-specific serotypes remains immature, and immunogenicity risks persist.

Cell-specific epigenetic editing: dCas9 fused to epigenetic modifying enzymes enables programmable, locus-directed regulation of promoters and enhancers. Proof-of-concept in vivo studies have demonstrated durable target gene silencing.308 Nonetheless, major challenges remain, including scalable cardiac-selective delivery, immune responses to vectors/editors, and long-term monitoring of chromatin changes.

Limitations and Translational Bottlenecks of Epigenetic Therapies

Off-Target Effects

Epigenetic therapies commonly employ small-molecule inhibitors to globally modulate epigenetic marks. These agents are designed to inhibit enzymatic activity rather than to target specific genomic loci. As a result, they exert broad-spectrum effects with limited tissue and cell-type selectivity, potentially affecting multiple pathways beyond the intended targets. Such pleiotropic activity may compromise both efficacy and safety.

Lack of Reliable Methods for Assessing Therapeutic Efficacy

There is currently a paucity of robust biomarkers for monitoring epigenetic drug treatment, as well as a lack of standardized methodologies for evaluating epigenetic changes. Consequently, regulatory approval of epigenetic modulators for cardiac fibrosis necessitates extensive preclinical and clinical evaluation. Moreover, monitoring immune responses to AAV vectors or gene editors, alongside long-term tracking of unintended chromatin alterations, imposes heightened demands on clinical trial design and follow-up duration.

Uncertainty in Therapeutic Timing and Challenges Posed by the Chronic Nature of Disease

Cardiac fibrosis typically evolves as a multifactorial, slowly progressive chronic process, yet the optimal timing for initiating epigenetic intervention remains undefined. Current diagnostic modalities, including biomarkers and imaging techniques, lack the specificity and sensitivity required for early fibrosis detection. Novel tools capable of identifying fibrosis onset, etiology, and molecular signatures are urgently needed to enable timely intervention. Long-term safety and durability data for epigenetic modulators remain scarce, raising concerns about whether transient chromatin remodeling can be sustained without continuous drug exposure.

Summary and Future Directions

Over the past decade, substantial progress has been made in deciphering the epigenetic contributions to cardiac fibrosis pathogenesis. Epigenetic regulation in this context operates through five core regulatory layers: DNA methylation, histone post‑translational modifications, RNA epitranscriptomics, non‑coding RNA networks, and ATP‑dependent chromatin remodeling. These layers do not operate in isolation; instead, they synergistically constitute a complex regulatory network through intimate crosstalk (see Figure 5). Preclinical studies have robustly confirmed that targeted intervention in these epigenetic pathways represents a highly promising strategy for the treatment of cardiac fibrosis. However, epigenetic factors exhibit dynamic interplay throughout fibrotic progression, implying multilayered crosstalk that may either amplify or counteract fibrotic outcomes. Moreover, the complexity of the epigenetic architecture governing fibrotic cardiac pathology remains incompletely defined—particularly regarding the shifting hierarchy and relative dominance of each layer across different etiologies and disease stages. It is expected that a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms of epigenetic regulation in cardiac fibrosis will provide critical insights for the development of early detection and effective therapeutic strategies against this condition. The integration of emerging multi-omics technologies with comprehensive approaches now will make it possible to analyze the different levels of epigenetic regulation in a spatio-temporal manner, providing invaluable insights into the molecular mechanisms driving cardiac fibrosis. In addition, advances in CRISPR/Cas9 gene editing technology have made it possible to precisely target specific enzymes involved in epigenetic modification and manipulate epigenetic pathways at specific gene loci, thereby helping to elucidate the role of specific epigenetic modifications and provide the precise intervention. Furthermore, the pharmacological modulation of these epigenetic processes through the use of newly developed, highly selective inhibitors facilitates the elucidation of specific mechanisms underlying cardiac fibrosis and accelerates the translation of these findings into clinical applications.

Moving forward, translating epigenetic therapies from experimental models to clinical practice will require focused efforts in several key areas. First, leveraging single‑cell multi‑omics and spatial transcriptomics to construct comprehensive epigenetic atlases of cardiac fibroblast subsets across distinct etiologies (ischemic versus non‑ischemic) and fibrotic stages, thereby delineating the critical tipping points and principal drivers that govern the transition from epigenetic plasticity to fixed and maladaptive configurations. Second, engineering next‑generation delivery platforms that jointly achieve selectivity for pathogenic fibroblast subpopulations and precision in epigenetic modulation. Third, identifying blood‑based biomarkers capable of tracking epigenetic dynamics, thereby enabling patient stratification, optimal timing of intervention, and longitudinal monitoring of therapeutic response. Fourth, systematically evaluating the synergistic potential of combining epigenetic agents with current standard‑of‑care regimens for heart failure. Beyond epigenetic enzymes and non-coding RNAs, the roles of emerging adipokine signaling molecules such as Asprosin in cardiac fibrosis warrant further investigation, as their mechanistic interplay with epigenetic regulators may unveil novel diagnostic and therapeutic opportunities.309

In summary, the epigenetic landscape of cardiac fibrosis is defined by multi-layer crosstalk rather than linear pathways. To translate current preclinical findings, we propose three priorities: (i) the establishment of circulating cell-free DNA methylation signatures as dynamic biomarkers for early fibrosis detection; (ii) the development of fibroblast-targeted lipid nanoparticles for co-delivery of HDAC inhibitors and METTL3-targeting siRNA to circumvent systemic toxicity; and (iii) the use of spatial epigenomics to define the optimal therapeutic window, as transient m6A modifications may be beneficial for acute repair but deleterious in chronic remodeling.

Counting homomorphisms in antiferromagnetic graphs via Lorentzian polynomials
Joonkyung Lee, Jaeseong Oh, Jaehyeon Seo

Abstract
An edge-weighted graph G G , possibly with loops, is said to be antiferromagnetic if it has nonnegative weights and at most one positive eigenvalue, counting multiplicities. The number of graph homomorphisms from a graph H H to an antiferromagnetic graph G G generalises various important parameters in graph theory, including the number of independent sets and proper vertex-colourings, as well as their relaxations in statistical physics. We obtain homomorphism inequalities for various graphs H H and antiferromagnetic graphs G G of the form | Hom ⁡ ( H , G ) | 2 ≤ | Hom ⁡ ( H × K 2 , G ) | , \lvert\operatorname{Hom}(H,G)\rvert^{2}\leq\lvert\operatorname{Hom}(H\times K_{2},G)\rvert, where H × K 2 H\times K_{2} denotes the tensor product of H H and K 2 K_{2} . Firstly, we show that the inequality holds for any H H obtained by blowing up vertices of a bipartite graph into complete graphs and any antiferromagnetic G G . In particular, one can take H = K d + 1 H=K_{d+1} , which already implies a new result for the Sah–Sawhney–Stoner–Zhao conjecture on the maximum number of d d -regular graphs in antiferromagnetic graphs. Secondly, the inequality also holds for G = K q G=K_{q} and those H H obtained by blowing up vertices of a bipartite graph into complete multipartite graphs, paths or even cycles. Both results can be seen as the first progress towards Zhao’s conjecture on q q -colourings, which states that the inequality holds for any H H and G = K q G=K_{q} , after his own work. Our method leverages on the emerging theory of Lorentzian polynomials due to Brändén and Huh and log-concavity of the list colourings of bipartite graphs, which may be of independent interest.

Following Brändén and Huh

Although there is no harm in saying that all homogeneous linear polynomials with nonnegative coefficients are Lorentzian, it only causes extra technicalities to carry on. Thus, we restrict ourselves to those homogeneous polynomials of degree at least two.

\partial_{\nu}h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)
is Lorentzian for all
1\leq\nu\leq n
. To see this, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\colon[t]\to[n]}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\biggl(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{\nu}}\prod_{s\in[t]}x_{\phi(s)}\biggr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi\colon[t]\to[n]}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\sum_{k\in[t]}\biggl(\mathbf{1}(\phi(k)=\nu)\prod_{s\in[t]\setminus\{k\}}x_{\phi(s)}\biggr).

Rearranging the order of the sum by fixing
k\in[t]
with
\phi(k)=\nu
gives

\partial_{\nu}h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)=\sum_{k\in[t]}\,\sum_{\phi\colon[t]\to[n]}\mathbf{1}(\phi(k)=\nu)\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\prod_{s\in[t]\setminus\{k\}}x_{\phi(s)}.

By symmetry of
K_{t}
, fixing
\phi(k)=\nu
and summing
\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\prod_{s\in[t]\setminus\{k\}}x_{\phi(s)}
for all such
\phi
does not depend on
k
.
For the sake of simplicity, letting
k=t
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\phi\colon[t]\to[n]}\mathbf{1}(\phi(t)=\nu)\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\prod_{s\in[t-1]}x_{\phi(s)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\in[t-1]\to[n]}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t-1]}{2}}G(\psi(i),\psi(j))\prod_{s\in[t-1]}G(\psi(s),\nu)\,x_{\psi(s)}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle h_{K_{t-1}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n};G),

where
y_{i}\coloneqq G(i,\nu)\,x_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. Therefore,

\partial_{\nu}h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)=t\cdot h_{K_{t-1}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n};G).

By
[
2
, Theorem 2.10]
, Lorentzian polynomials remain Lorentzian under affine transformations, i.e.,
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\mapsto(a_{1}x_{1},\dots,a_{n}x_{n})
for
a_{i}\geq 0
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
.
Together with the induction hypothesis, this fact implies that
h_{K_{t-1}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{n};G)
is Lorentzian, whence
\partial_{\nu}h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)
is also.

Next, we claim that
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G))
is M-convex. Let
G_{0}\coloneqq\supp(G)
.
Then

\displaystyle\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G))=\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G_{0})),

as changing all the positive edge weights of
G
to
1
turns
h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)
into
h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G_{0})
.
By
Proposition

2.2
,
G_{0}
is also antiferromagnetic.
Theorem

3.2

(4)
then implies that
G_{0}
is obtained from
K_{q}^{\circ}
for some
q\geq 1
by blowing-up and taking an induced subgraph.

It then suffices to show that
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
is M-convex, since M-covexity of
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
implies M-convexity of
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G_{0}))
by
Lemma

2.3
. Write
V(K_{q}^{\circ})=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{q}\}
where
w_{q}
is the looped vertex. Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{q})
and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{q})
be in
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
, and
a_{i}>b_{i}
for some
i\in[q]
. Note
a_{j},b_{j}\in\{0,1\}
for
j\in[q-1]
. There are three cases: if
a_{q}<b_{q}
, then
i\neq q
and
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{q}\in\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
. If
a_{q}=b_{q}
, then
i\in[q-1]
and
a_{1}+\dots+a_{q-1}=b_{1}+\dots+b_{q-1}
, so there is
j\in[q-1]\setminus\{i\}
such that
a_{j}=0<1=b_{j}
. If
a_{q}>b_{q}
, then
a_{1}+\dots+a_{q-1}<b_{1}+\dots+b_{q-1}
, so again there is such
j\in[q-1]
. In the two latter cases,
\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{e}_{i}+\mathbf{e}_{j}\in\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
. Therefore,
\supp(h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ}))
is M-convex.
∎

Having seen the reduction in the last few paragraphs of the proof, one may wonder whether M-convexity of
h_{H}(\mathbf{x};G)
always reduces to the M-convexity of
h_{H}(\mathbf{x};K_{q}^{\circ})
or even
h_{H}(\mathbf{x};K_{q})
for some
q
. We show that this is indeed the case.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
H
be a connected graph on
t\geq 2
vertices. If
h_{H}({-};K_{q})
has
M
-convex support for some
q\geq t
, then so does
h_{H}({-};G)
for all antiferromagnetic graphs
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex antiferromagnetic graph.
By
Proposition

2.2
,
\supp(G)
is an unweighted antiferromagnetic graph. whence
Theorem

3.2

(4)
gives that
\supp(G)
is an induced subgraph of a blow-up of
K_{m}^{\circ}
. Thus,
Lemma

2.3

(1)
allows us to assume that
G=K_{m}^{\circ}
for some
m
.

We identify each homomorphism from
H
to
K_{m}^{\circ}
as a
semiproper

m
-colouring, where the colour
m
can be used with no constraints while no adjacent pairs in
H
get monochromatic with the other colours.
Let
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
be two semiproper
m
-colourings of
H
. The M-convexity then follows if we can ‘recolour’
c_{1}
by changing the colour
r_{1}=c_{1}(v)
of a vertex
v
into
r_{2}
, where
r_{1}
is more popular in
c_{1}
than in
c_{2}
and
r_{2}
is more popular in
c_{2}
than in
c_{1}
.
For brevity, we say that this alteration of
c_{1}
is a
recolouring of
c_{1}
towards
c_{2}
.

If
c_{2}
uses a colour
i
that never appears in
c_{1}
, then the recolouring of
c_{1}
towards
c_{2}
is constructed by using
i
. In addition, a colour
i
with
c_{1}^{-1}(i)=c_{2}^{-1}(i)=\emptyset
does not affect the recolouring of
c_{1}
towards
c_{2}
. Thus, we may ignore those colours which are not used by
c_{1}
and assume
c_{1}^{-1}(i)\neq\emptyset
for all
i\in[m]
. Moreover, the ‘looped’ colour
m
must be used in
c_{1}
as much as in
c_{2}
, as otherwise, the colour
m
provides an option to recolour
c_{1}
. Let
k\coloneqq\lvert c_{1}^{-1}(m)\rvert
.

By
Lemma

2.3

(1)
,
\supp(h_{H}(\mathbf{x};K_{t}))
is M-convex. Turn
c_{1}
into a (proper)
t
-colouring
c_{1}^{\prime}
by converting
m
into a non-looped colour and replacing each previous use of
m
by a new distinct colour in
\{m,m+1,\dots,m+k-1\}
. Note this is possible as
m-1\leq\lvert c_{1}^{-1}(1)\rvert+\dots+\lvert c_{1}^{-1}(m-1)\rvert=t-k
so that
m+k-1\leq t
. Using the same set of new colours
\{m,m+1,\dots,m+k-1\}
, it is possible to make
c_{2}
into a
t
-colouring
c_{2}^{\prime}
by assigning the new distinct colours to those vertices which was previously coloured by
m
, as
\lvert c_{2}^{-1}(m)\rvert\leq k
.
Then by M-convexity of
\supp(h_{H}(\mathbf{x};K_{t}))
, there is a recolouring of
c_{1}^{\prime}
towards
c_{2}^{\prime}
.

The recoloured vertex in
H
by this recolouring does not use the new colours in
\{m,m+1,\dots,m+k-1\}
, as these cannot be more popular in
c_{2}^{\prime}
than in
c_{1}^{\prime}
. Thus, turning the new colours into the colour
m
gives a semiproper colouring, which is a recolouring of
c_{1}
towards
c_{2}
.
∎

This result may be potentially useful in finding other graphs
H
than the complete graphs that give a Lorentzian
G
-chromatic function
h_{H}(\mathbf{x};G)
for some particular antiferromagnetic graphs
G
.
We shall discuss more along these lines and propose open problems in the concluding remarks.

4
Alexandrov–Fenchel-type inequalities for
G
-volumes

Proposition 4.5 in
[
2
]
states that one can obtain an Alexandrov–Fenchel-type inequality from any Lorentzian polynomial.

Proposition 4.1

(Proposition 4.5 in
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in
n
variables. Let
F_{f}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by

F_{f}(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})\coloneqq\frac{1}{d!}\partial_{x_{1}}\dotsm\partial_{x_{d}}f(x_{1}v_{1}+\dots+x_{d}v_{d}).

If
f
is Lorentzian, then for any
v_{1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
v_{2},\dots,v_{d}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
,

F_{f}(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{d})^{2}\geq F_{f}(v_{1},v_{1},v_{3},\dots,v_{d})\cdot F_{f}(v_{2},v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{d}).

For example,
f
can be the volume of the Minkowski sum
x_{1}K_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}K_{d}
of convex bodies
K_{1},\dots,K_{d}
, a Lorentzian polynomial given in
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
. Then
Proposition

4.1
recovers the classical Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality for mixed volumes.

If
f=h_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{x};G)
is the
G
-chromatic function, then
F_{f}
corresponds to the
G
-volume
V_{K_{t}}({-};G)
.
By
Theorem

3.1
,
f
is Lorentzian, so
Proposition

4.1
yields an Alexandrov–Fenchel-type inequality for the
G
-volume of a complete graph. That is, for
\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{t}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
,

V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{2},\mathbf{a}_{3}\dots,\mathbf{a}_{t};G)^{2}\geq V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{1},\mathbf{a}_{3},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{t};G)\cdot V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a}_{2},\mathbf{a}_{2},\mathbf{a}_{3},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{t};G).

(4.1)

Applying (
4.1
) iteratively gives the following inequality.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex antiferromagnetic graph and let
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
. Then

V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a};G)\cdot V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b};G)\geq V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a};G)\cdot V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b};G).

We are now ready to prove our main result of this section,
which implies
Theorem

1.6
by setting
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}=(1,1,\dots,1)
.
In what follows, we enumerate the vertices of
K_{t}\times K_{2}
in the lexicographic order
(1,1),\dots,(t,1),(1,2),\dots,(t,2)
, where
([t]\times\{1\})\sqcup([t]\times\{2\})
is the bipartition and
(i,1)
and
(i,2)
are not adjacent for
1\leq i\leq t
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex antiferromagnetic graph and let
t\geq 2
. Then for
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{n}
,

V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a};G)\cdot V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b};G)\leq V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\underbrace{\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a}}_{t},\underbrace{\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b}}_{t};G).

(4.2)

The proof proceeds by induction on
t
. As a warm-up, it would be helpful to see the proof of the following proposition, which uses the inductive technique in a simpler setting.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
G
be an unweighted loopless graph and
t\geq 2
. Suppose (
4.2
) holds for
t
replaced by
t-1
and all
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
. Then (
4.2
) holds for
t
and
\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{b}=(1,\dots,1)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\hom(K_{t},G)^{2}\leq\hom(K_{t}\times K_{2},G).

(4.3)

Proof.

The proof relies on two simple facts:
K_{t}
minus a vertex is
K_{t-1}
, and
K_{t}\times K_{2}
minus two vertices
(t,1)
and
(t,2)
is
K_{t-1}\times K_{2}
.
For
r\in G
, let
N(r)
be its set of neighbours. Taking a homomorphic copy of
K_{t}\times K_{2}
in
G
can be done in two steps. First, choose vertices
r,s\in G
to embed
(t,1)
and
(t,2)
, respectively. Next, take a homomorphic copy of
K_{t-1}\times K_{2}
so that the vertices in
[t-1]\times\{1\}
and
[t-1]\times\{2\}
are mapped into
N(s)
and
N(r)
, respectively. Applying the assumption gives

\displaystyle V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{1};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}\times K_{2}}(\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{N(s)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(s)}}_{t-1},\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{N(r)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(r)}}_{t-1};G)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(s)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(s)};G)\cdot V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(r)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(r)};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(r)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(r)};G)\sum_{s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(s)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(s)};G).

The term
V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(r)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(r)})
counts the number of the (labelled)
K_{t-1}
in the neighbour of
r
. Summing this over all
r\in V(G)
gives the number of the labelled
K_{t}
in
G
, i.e.,

\sum_{r\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{1}_{N(r)},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{N(r)};G)=V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{1};G).

Hence,
V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{1};G)\geq V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{1},\dots,\mathbf{1};G)^{2}
, as desired.
∎

The proof of
Theorem

4.3
uses the same idea, though ‘updating’ the vertex weights
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
complicates the notations.

Proof of
Theorem

4.3
.

Write

V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)\coloneqq V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\underbrace{\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a}}_{t},\underbrace{\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b}}_{t};G).

We use induction on
t
. By
Corollary

4.2
and the fact that
K_{2}\times K_{2}\cong K_{2}\sqcup K_{2}
, we have

V_{K_{2}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a};G)\cdot V_{K_{2}}(\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b};G)\leq V_{K_{2}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};G)^{2}=V_{K_{2}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{b};G),

which verifies the base case
t=2
.

Suppose
t\geq 3
. Let
V(G)=[n]
,
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
, and
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
.

Figure 1:
Illustration of the embedding strategy

We compute the
G
-volume
V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)
by counting (weighted) homomorphisms
\phi
from
K_{t}\times K_{2}
to
G
recursively as follows: first, fix
r=\phi(t,1)
and
s=\phi(t,2)
; next, find a (weighted) homomorphic copy of
K_{t-1}\times K_{2}
such that the vertices in
[t-1]\times\{1\}
and
[t-1]\times\{2\}
are adjacent to
s
and
r
, respectively.
See
Figure

1
for an illustration of this embedding. To formalise this two-step embedding process,
we write

V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)=\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}a_{r}b_{s}\sum_{\phi\in\Hom(K_{t-1}\times K_{2},G)}f(r,s,\phi)\,g(\phi),

(4.4)

where

\displaystyle f(r,s,\phi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[t-1]}G(\phi(i,1),s)\,G(r,\phi(i,2))\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle g(\phi)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i\in[t-1]}a_{\phi(i,1)}\prod_{j\in[t-1]}b_{\phi(j,2)}\prod_{i,j\in[k],i\neq j}G(\phi(i,1),\phi(j,2)).

Here
f(r,s,\phi)
encodes the edge weights coming from the fixed vertices
r
and
s
and
g(\phi)
is the weight of the homomorphism
\phi
from
K_{t-1}\times K_{2}
.
We claim that each
a_{r}b_{s}\sum_{\phi}f(r,s,\phi)g(\phi)
can be rewritten as a
G
-volume of
K_{t-1}\times K_{2}
with modified vertex weights, which fully formalises our embedding strategy.

To see how the weight modification works, for
r,s\in V(G)
, let
\mathbf{a}^{(s)}=(\alpha_{\nu}^{(s)}a_{\nu}:1\leq\nu\leq n)
and
\mathbf{b}^{(r)}=(\beta_{\nu}^{(r)}b_{\nu}:1\leq\nu\leq n)
be defined by

\alpha_{\nu}^{(s)}\coloneqq b_{s}^{1/(t-1)}\,G(\nu,s)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta_{\nu}^{(r)}\coloneqq a_{r}^{1/(t-1)}\,G(r,\nu).

Then

\prod_{i\in[t-1]}\alpha_{\phi(i,1)}^{(s)}=b_{s}\,\prod_{i\in[t-1]}G(\phi(i,1),s)\quad\text{and}\quad\prod_{i\in[t-1]}\beta_{\phi(i,2)}^{(r)}=a_{r}\,\prod_{i\in[t-1]}G(r,\phi(i,2)),

which gives

\prod_{i\in[t-1]}\alpha_{\phi(i,1)}^{(s)}\beta_{\phi(i,2)}^{(r)}=a_{r}b_{s}f(r,s,\phi).

Therefore,

a_{r}b_{s}f(r,s,\phi)g(\phi)=\prod_{i\in[t-1]}\bigl(\alpha_{\phi(i,1)}^{(s)}a_{\phi(i,1)}\bigr)\bigl(\beta_{\phi(i,2)}^{(r)}b_{\phi(i,2)}\bigr),

and hence,

\displaystyle V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}\,\sum_{\phi\in\Hom(K_{t-1}\times K_{2},G)}a_{r}b_{s}f(r,s,\phi)g(\phi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a}^{(s)};\mathbf{b}^{(r)};G),

as claimed.

By the induction hypothesis,

\displaystyle V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{r,s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{a}^{(s)},\dots,\mathbf{a}^{(s)};G)\cdot V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{b}^{(r)},\dots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{a}^{(s)},\dots,\mathbf{a}^{(s)};G)\cdot\sum_{r\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{b}^{(r)},\dots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)};G).

Simplifying the first factor in the RHS yields

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}={}}\!\sum_{s\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{a}^{(s)},\dots,\mathbf{a}^{(s)};G)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in V(G)}\,\sum_{\phi\in\Hom(K_{t-1},G)}\,\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t-1]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\prod_{i\in[t-1]}\alpha_{\phi(i)}^{(s)}a_{\phi(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in V(G)}\,\sum_{\phi\in\Hom(K_{t-1},G)}\,\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t-1]}{2}}G(\phi(i),\phi(j))\prod_{i\in[t-1]}b_{s}^{1/(t-1)}G(s,\phi(i))a_{\phi(i)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\phi^{\prime}\in\Hom(K_{t},G)}\,\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\binom{[t]}{2}}G(\psi(i),\psi(j))\,\Biggl(\prod_{i\in[t-1]}a_{\psi(i)}\Biggr)b_{\psi(t)}

\displaystyle=V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};G),

and symmetrically, it also holds that

\sum_{r\in V(G)}V_{K_{t-1}}(\mathbf{b}^{(r)},\dots,\mathbf{b}^{(r)};G)=V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a};G).

Therefore,

V_{K_{t}\times K_{2}}(\mathbf{a};\mathbf{b};G)\geq V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{a},\dots,\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b};G)\cdot V_{K_{t}}(\mathbf{b},\dots,\mathbf{b},\mathbf{a};G),

(4.5)

which, together with
Corollary

4.2
, concludes the proof.
∎

\sum_{k=0}^{m}a_{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}b_{\ell}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}d_{\ell}.

6.1
Paths and even cycles

In this subsection, we show the paths and the even cycles are cross-bipartite swapping in
K_{q}
.
These graphs
H
are bipartite, so
H\sqcup H\cong H\times K_{2}
, which reduces (
6.3
) to

N(H;a)\,N(H;b)\leq N(H;a,b)\,N(H;b,a).

(6.4)

Note that if
a=b
, then (
6.4
) is an equality, and if
a=1
, then the LHS vanishes unless
H
has no edges. Thus, throughout this subsection, we show (
6.4
) for
2\leq a<b
. We shall use
d
for a positive integer to parametrise lengths of paths and cycles.

For the paths and the even cycles, we count
N(H;a,b)
and
N(H;b,a)
by fixing the homomorphic embedding to the
a
-colour side first and then determining the number of choices left on the
b
-colour side. This is done by the following lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph on the bipartition
V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
, where every vertex in
V_{2}
has degree either
1
or
2
. Let
2\leq a\leq b
. Let
M(\ell)
be the number of the colourings
\phi\colon V_{1}\to[a]
such that there are exactly
\ell
vertices
v
in
V_{2}
such that
\lvert\phi(N(v))\rvert=2
. Then

N(H;a,b)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\lvert V_{2}\rvert}T(a,b,\ell),

where
T(a,b,\ell)=M(\ell)(b-1)^{\lvert V_{2}\rvert-\ell}(b-2)^{\ell}
.

Proof.

It is enough to count the possible choices for
\phi(v)
,
v\in V_{2}
, amongst the
b
colours avoiding
\phi(N(v))
. If
\lvert\phi(N(v))\rvert=1
, there are
b-1
choices; otherwise if
\lvert\phi(N(v))\rvert=2
,
b-2
choices exist. Hence, the number of the colourings in this case is
M(\ell)(b-1)^{\lvert V_{2}\rvert-\ell}(b-2)^{\ell}
; summing this up for distinct values of
\ell
concludes the proof.
∎

Odd–length paths

The path
P
of odd length
2d-1

Let

.

, let

in

6.3
. There are

(1)

That is,

(a-1)^{d-k-\ell}(a-2)^{k}b\leq(b-1)^{d-k-\ell}(b-2)^{k}a
,
which follows from

\frac{\text{LHS}}{\text{RHS}}=\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d-k-\ell}\biggl(\frac{a-2}{b-2}\biggr)^{k}\frac{b}{a}\leq\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d-\ell}\frac{b}{a}\leq 1.

(6.5)

Even cycles

Let
C
be a cycle of length
2d
,
d\geq 2
, with the bipartition
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}\sqcup\{v_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}
.
In this graph, we shall take the vertex index sum modulo
d
and we assume that
u_{i}v_{i}
and
v_{i}u_{i+1}
are edges. Let
2\leq a\leq b
. For a vertex-colouring
\phi
counted by
N(C;a,b)
, define

L(\phi)\coloneqq\{1\leq i\leq d:\phi(u_{i})\neq\phi(u_{i-1})\}.

We again count those
\phi
with
\lvert L(\phi)\rvert=\ell
, denoted by
T(a,b,\ell)
, to use
Lemma

6.3
.
There are
\binom{d}{\ell}
choices to choose
L(\phi)
.
Having fixed

, consider the auxiliary
\ell
-cycle obtained by contracting the edge
u_{i}u_{i-1}
,

, of the
d
-cycle on
\{u_{i}:1\leq i\leq d\}
with edges
\{u_{i}u_{i+1}:1\leq i\leq d\}
.
Then determining
\phi(u_{i})
,
1\leq i\leq d
, is equivalent to properly colouring an
\ell
-cycle with
a
colours.
The number of proper
a
-colourings of an
\ell
-cycle is
(a-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(a-1)
, which follows from the deletion-contraction formula for chromatic polynomials. Thus,
M(\ell)=\binom{d}{\ell}\big((a-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(a-1)\big)
, so

6.3
gives

\displaystyle T(a,b,\ell)=\binom{d}{\ell}\big((a-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(a-1)\big)(b-1)^{d-\ell}(b-2)^{\ell}

(6.8)

and

N(C;a,b)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}T(a,b,\ell)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}\binom{d}{\ell}\big((a-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(a-1)\big)(b-1)^{d-\ell}(b-2)^{\ell}.

(6.9)

Note that
N(C;a,b)=N(C;b,a)
due to the part-swapping automorphism of the even cycle
C
. Our goal (
6.4
) is to show

N(C;a)\,N(C;b)\leq N(C;a,b)\,N(C;b,a)=N(C;a,b)^{2}\qquad\text{for $2\leq a<b$}.

(6.10)

When
a=2
, this follows from

N(C;2)\,N(C;b)=2\big((b-1)^{d}+(-1)^{d}(b-1)\big)\leq\big(2(b-1)^{d}\big)^{2}\leq N(C;2,b)^{2}.

Now suppose
a\geq 3
. We collect three claims to apply
Proposition

6.2
.

Claim 6.4
.

For
0\leq k,\ell\leq d
, if
\ell
is even or
(k,\ell)\neq(0,d)
, then

T(a,a,k)\,T(b,b,\ell)\leq T(a,b,k)\,T(a,b,\ell).

(6.11)

By (

),

\frac{\text{LHS of \eqref{eq:even-cycle-SBS-in-Kq_red_case1}}}{\text{RHS of \eqref{eq:even-cycle-SBS-in-Kq_red_case1}}}=\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d-k}\biggl(\frac{a-2}{b-2}\biggr)^{k}\frac{(b-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(b-1)}{(a-1)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}(a-1)}.

(6.12)

Suppose first that
\ell
is even. Then by
\frac{a-2}{b-2}\leq\frac{a-1}{b-1}
, it suffices to show that

\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d}\frac{(b-1)^{\ell}+(b-1)}{(a-1)^{\ell}+(a-1)}\leq 1.

(6.13)

If
\ell=0
, then

\eqref{eq:even-cycle-SBS-in-Kq_red_case1-2-eq2}\leq\bigl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\bigr)^{d}\frac{b}{a}\leq\frac{a-1}{b-1}\frac{b}{a}\leq 1

.
Second, suppose
\ell
is odd. If

, then

\eqref{eq:even-cycle-SBS-in-Kq_red_case1-2-eq1}\leq\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d}\frac{(b-1)^{\ell}-(b-1)}{(a-1)^{\ell}-(a-1)}\leq\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d}\frac{(b-1)^{\ell+1}}{(a-1)^{\ell+1}}\leq 1,

where the first inequality uses
\frac{a-2}{b-2}\leq\frac{a-1}{b-1}
and the second inequality follows from the fact that

is decreasing for

.
Otherwise, if
\ell=d
, then
k>0
by the assumption. In this case,

\eqref{eq:even-cycle-SBS-in-Kq_red_case1-2-eq1}\leq\biggl(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\biggr)^{d-1}\biggl(\frac{a-2}{b-2}\biggr)\frac{(b-1)^{d}-(b-1)}{(a-1)^{d}-(a-1)}\leq 1,

\frac{a-2}{b-2}\leq\frac{a-1}{b-1}
and the second inequality follows from the fact that

is decreasing for

.
∎

Claim 6.5
.

For
0\leq k,\ell\leq d
, if

is even, then

T(a,a,k)\,T(b,b,\ell)\leq T(a,b,k)\,T(a,b,\ell).

If

; if

is odd, then

.

. This particular case must be handled by pairing it with

.

Claim 6.6
.

For odd

Since

is odd,

.
We shall use the elementary bound

which follows from

.

a\biggl(1-\frac{1}{(b-1)^{d-1}}\biggr)\leq a\overset{\eqref{eq:C_2d-SBS-red2-step2}}{\leq}(a+1)\biggl(1-\frac{1}{(a-1)^{d-1}}\biggr)\overset{\eqref{eq:C_2d-SBS-red2-step1}}{\leq}\biggl(2a-\frac{(a-2)^{d}}{(b-2)^{d}}b\biggr)\biggl(1-\frac{1}{(a-1)^{d-1}}\biggr).

6.2
Complete multipartite graphs

In this subsection, we show that the complete
k
-partite graphs are cross-bipartite swapping in the complete graphs.
For nonnegative integers
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
,
let
K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
be the multipartite graph with the part sizes
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
.
In particular,
r_{1}=r_{2}=\cdots=r_{k}=1
makes the complete graph

and

denotes the

Let

. By

6.1
, we may assume that

or

, so the homomorphism counts depend only depend on

and

with

.
For brevity, write

N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\coloneqq N(K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k});a)=\hom(K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k}),K_{q}[A]).

Our goal (

) is to show that for all positive integers

and

,

N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)\leq N(K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})\times K_{2};a,b).

(6.19)

To show (

), we use induction on

.
For the induction step, the following lemma that resembles

will be useful. Denote by

the number of homomorphic copies of

, where the first part of size

maps into

and the other parts map into

.

Lemma 6.7
.

For positive integers
a\leq b
,

, and

,

N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)\leq N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b,a).

Setting
r_{1}=\dots=r_{k}=1
in

6.7
, we obtain

V_{K_{k}}(\mathbf{1}_{A},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{A};K_{q})\cdot V_{K_{k}}(\mathbf{1}_{B},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{B};K_{q})\leq V_{K_{k}}(\mathbf{1}_{A},\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{B},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{B}}_{k-1};K_{q})\cdot V_{K_{k}}(\mathbf{1}_{B},\underbrace{\mathbf{1}_{A},\dots,\mathbf{1}_{A}}_{k-1};K_{q}),

which is exactly

4.2
with

,

,

, and
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{1}_{B}
.
In fact, we use

in the induction step in the same way that

is used in the proof of

.

Proof of (

) using

6.7
.

As mentioned, we use induction on

. The base case

is clear as

N_{1}(r_{1};a)\,N_{1}(r_{1};b)=a^{r_{1}}b^{r_{1}}=N(K(r_{1})\times K_{2};a,b).

Suppose
k\geq 2
and (
6.19
) holds for all
a\leq b
,
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}
, and
k
replaced by

.

Let
V_{1}
and

be the vertex set of

of size

, which correspond to two copies of the first part of

of size

.
Each homomorphism counted by

can be obtained by fixing two maps
\psi\colon V_{1}\to A
and
\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B
and finding a homomorphic copy of

in

by mapping the two parts of its canonical bipartition into

and
B_{\psi}\coloneqq B\setminus\psi(V_{1})
, respectively. Therefore,

\displaystyle N(K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})\times K_{2};a,b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\colon V_{1}\to A\\
\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B\end{subarray}}\operatorname{hom}_{\mathrm{b}}\bigl(K(r_{2},\dots,r_{k})\times K_{2},\,K_{q}[A_{\psi^{\prime}},B_{\psi}]\bigr)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\colon V_{1}\to A\\
\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B\end{subarray}}N\bigl(K(r_{2},\dots,r_{k})\times K_{2};\,\lvert A_{\psi^{\prime}}\rvert,\lvert B_{\psi}\rvert\bigr).

(6.20)

To see why the inequality holds, recall that in (

\operatorname{hom}_{\mathrm{b}}(H\times K_{2},K_{q}[A,B])
decreases as we assume

, which corresponds to the definition of

.
By the induction hypothesis, (

) is at least

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\colon V_{1}\to A\\
\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B\end{subarray}}N_{k-1}\bigl(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};\lvert B_{\psi}\rvert\bigr)\cdot N_{k-1}\bigl(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};\lvert A_{\psi^{\prime}}\rvert\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\colon V_{1}\to A}N_{k-1}\bigl(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};\lvert B_{\psi}\rvert\bigr)\cdot\sum_{\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B}N_{k-1}\bigl(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};\lvert A_{\psi^{\prime}}\rvert\bigr)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\sum_{\psi\colon V_{1}\to A}\hom\bigl(K(r_{2},\dots,r_{k}),K_{q}[B_{\psi}]\bigr)\cdot\sum_{\psi^{\prime}\colon V^{\prime}_{1}\to B}\hom\bigl(K(r_{2},\dots,r_{k}),K_{q}[A_{\psi^{\prime}}]\bigr).

(6.21)

Since the first sum in (
6.21
) counts the number of homomorphisms from
K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
to
K_{q}
which maps
V_{1}
into
A
via
\psi
and the other parts into
B
while avoiding colours used by
\psi
, the sum
\sum_{\psi\colon V_{1}\to A}\hom\bigl(K(r_{2},\dots,r_{k}),K_{q}[B_{\psi}]\bigr)
equals
N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a,b)
. By the same reason, the second sum in (
6.21
) equals
N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b,a)
. Finally, we apply
Lemma

6.7
to obtain the desired inequality

N(K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})\times K_{2};a,b)\geq N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b),

which complete the induction.
∎

It remains to prove
Lemma

6.7
. Unlike
Corollary

4.2
,
H=K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
does not give the Lorentzian property of
h_{H}(\mathbf{x};K_{q})
in general; for more discussions, see the concluding remarks. This forces us to take more hands-on approaches.

Proof of
Lemma

6.7
.

Fix
k\geq 2
. We shall prove the following slightly stronger statement:

\displaystyle\text{For all $k\leq a\leq b$ and nonnegative integers $r_{i}\leq s_{i}$, $1\leq i\leq k$,}

(6.22)

\displaystyle N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)\leq N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a).

Here we assume
k\leq a\leq b
since
a<k
makes the LHS vanish. Allowing
s_{i}
or
r_{i}
to be zero is necessary for the induction, in particular, in
6.8
; for instance,
N_{k}(0,r_{2},\dots,r_{k};b)
means
N_{k-1}(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};b)
.

The proof idea relies on multiple reduction steps, on which we briefly give a sketch first.
First, using induction on
b
, where the induction step is given in
6.8
, we reduce the problem to the base case when
b=a
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\text{for all $a\geq k$ and $0\leq r_{i}\leq s_{i}$, $1\leq i\leq k$,}

(6.23)

\displaystyle N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\leq N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};a).

Second, a telescoping argument in
6.13
reduces this to the case when
s_{1}=r_{1}+1
,
s_{2}=r_{2}+1
, and
s_{i}=r_{i}
for
3\leq i\leq k
, i.e.,

for all
a\geq k
and
r_{i}\geq 0
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}\leq{}}N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1,r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)

\displaystyle\leq N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2},r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a).

Third, rewriting each term
N_{k}({-})
as a sum of terms on
N_{k-1}({-})
allows us to apply
Proposition

6.2

(2)
, which reduces the problem further to the following statement, shown in
6.12
:

for all
a\geq k
,
0\leq\ell\leq m\leq a
, and
r_{i}\geq 0
,
2\leq i\leq k
,

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}\leq{}}N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)

\displaystyle\leq N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m).

Fourth, another telescoping argument in
6.11
will reduce this to the case
m=\ell+1
, i.e.,

for all
a\geq k-1
and
r_{i}\geq 0
,
2\leq i\leq k
,

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}\leq{}}N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)

\displaystyle\leq N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a).

Finally,
6.10
will prove this by writing each term of the form
N_{k-1}({-})
as a sum of terms on
N_{k-2}({-})
and applying
Proposition

6.2

(2)
.

We begin with the first reduction step.

Claim 6.8
.

(
6.23
) implies (
6.22
).

It suffices to show that (
6.22
) for fixed
(k,a,b)
with
k\leq a\leq b
implies (
6.22
) for
(k,a,b+1)
.
Note first that

N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b+1)=N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{i}}\binom{r_{i}}{\ell}N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};b),

(6.24)

where
N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};b)
denotes
N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)
with
r_{i}
replaced by
r_{i}-\ell
.
Indeed, the term
N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b+1)
counts the number of proper vertex-colourings
K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
using
b+1
colours. Fix one of the
b+1
colours, denoted by
\nu
. Let
V_{1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup V_{k}
be the
k
-partition of
K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
where
\lvert V_{i}\rvert=r_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq k
.
The number of colourings that do not use
\nu
is
N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)
. Otherwise,
\nu
must be used in exactly one part and the number of colourings that use
\nu
exactly
\ell
times in
V_{i}
is
\binom{r_{i}}{\ell}N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};b)
, for each
1\leq i\leq k
and
1\leq\ell\leq r_{i}
.

An analogous argument also gives

N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b+1)=N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)+\Sigma_{1},

(6.25)

where

\Sigma_{1}\coloneqq\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{i}}\binom{r_{i}}{\ell}N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};a,b).

Here,
N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};a,b)
denotes
N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)
with
r_{i}
replaced by
r_{i}-\ell
.
The key difference between (
6.24
) and (
6.25
) is that we choose the colour
\nu
not amongst the
a
colours and the sum analyses how all the parts but the
s_{1}
-vertex part use the colour
\nu
.
Similarly,

N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b+1,a)=N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a)+\Sigma_{2},

(6.26)

where

\Sigma_{2}\coloneqq\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{1}}\binom{r_{1}}{\ell}N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1}-\ell,s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a).

Indeed, this is obtained by analysing how
\nu
can be used in the first
r_{1}
-vertex part.

Now we show (
6.22
) for
(k,a,b+1)
. Applying (
6.24
) and expanding out yields

\displaystyle N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b+1)

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};b)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{i}}\binom{r_{i}}{\ell}N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};b).

(6.27)

Since (
6.22
) is assumed to hold for
(k,a,b)
, (
6.27
) is at most

\displaystyle N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a)

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{1}}\binom{r_{1}}{\ell}N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1}-\ell,s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a)

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{k}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r_{i}}\binom{r_{i}}{\ell}N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{i}-\ell,\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a),

where we separate the case
i=1
in (
6.27
) from the others to give the upper bound.
This rewrites as

\displaystyle N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a)

\displaystyle{}+{}

\displaystyle N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b)\,\Sigma_{2}+N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b,a)\,\Sigma_{1},

which is the product of (
6.25
) and (
6.26
) minus
\Sigma_{1}\Sigma_{2}
. Therefore, the above is at most

N_{k}^{(1)}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a,b+1)\,N_{k}^{(1)}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};b+1,a),

which concludes the proof.
∎

Before getting into the remaining steps, we see how
N_{k}({-})
can be written as a sum of terms on
N_{k-1}({-})
. This will be used in the proofs of
6.10
and
6.12
.

Claim 6.9
.

For nonnegative integers
r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k}
,

N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{a}\binom{a}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{\ell}\ell!\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell).

Let
V_{1}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup V_{k}
be the
k
-partition of
K(r_{1},\dots,r_{k})
where
\lvert V_{i}\rvert=r_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
.
For
0\leq\ell\leq a
, the number of vertex-colourings
\phi
counted by
N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a)
with
\lvert\phi(V_{1})\rvert=\ell
is

T(r_{1},r_{2},\ell)\coloneqq\binom{a}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{\ell}\ell!\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell).

Indeed, there are
\binom{a}{\ell}
choices to choose the set
\phi(V_{1})
, and
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r}{\ell}\ell!
ways to colour
V_{1}
using all the colours from
\phi(V_{1})
.
Having fixed the values of
\phi
on
V_{1}
, there are
N_{k-1}(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)
ways to colour
K(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k})\setminus V_{1}\cong K(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k})
using colours not in
\phi(V_{1})
.
Thus,

N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{a}T(r_{1},r_{2},\ell)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{a}\binom{a}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{\ell}\ell!\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell),

(6.28)

as claimed.
∎

The remaining parts of the proof build upon ‘log-submodularity’ results on
N_{k}({-})
, which formalise the aforementioned reduction steps in the reverse order. In a sense, our goal (
6.23
) itself can also be seen as a log-submodularity result.

Claim 6.10
.

For all
a\geq k-1
and
r_{2},\dots,r_{k}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)

(6.29)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a).

Throughout the proof,
r_{3},\dots,r_{k}
are fixed.
Let
T_{1}(r,a,\ell)\coloneqq\binom{a}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r}{\ell}\ell!\,N_{k-2}(r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)
so that
6.9
with
k
replaced by
k-1
gives

N_{k-1}(r,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{a}T_{1}(r,a,\ell),

with the convention that
N_{0}(r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\coloneqq 1
.
Substituting this into every term in (
6.29
) with
r=r_{2}
or
r_{2}+1
, it suffices to show that, by
Proposition

6.2

(2)
, for all
0\leq\ell\leq m\leq a
,

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}\leq{}}T_{1}(r_{2},a+1,\ell)\,T_{1}(r_{2}+1,a,m)+T_{1}(r_{2},a+1,m)\,T_{1}(r_{2}+1,a,\ell)

\displaystyle\leq T_{1}(r_{2}+1,a+1,\ell)\,T_{1}(r_{2},a,m)+T_{1}(r_{2}+1,a+1,m)\,T_{1}(r_{2},a,\ell).

This reduces to

\biggl[\binom{a+1}{\ell}\binom{a}{m}-\binom{a+1}{m}\binom{a}{\ell}\biggr]\times\biggl[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_2+1}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_2}{m}-\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_2+1}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_2}{\ell}\biggr]\geq 0.

Simple calculation shows that the first term involving binomial coefficients is nonpositive, and the second term is nonpositive by log-concavity of Stirling numbers, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
, so the nonnegativity of the product of the two follows.
∎

Claim 6.11
.

For all
a\geq k
,
0\leq\ell\leq m\leq a
and
r_{2},\dots,r_{k}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)

(6.30)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m).

Rearranging (
6.29
) gives

\frac{N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)}{N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a+1)},

where the RHS is obtained from the LHS by increasing the number of colours
a
by
1
.
Repeatedly applying this yields that for all
k-1\leq a\leq b
,

\frac{N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};b)}{N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};b)}.

This confirms (
6.30
) whenever
a-m\geq k-1
. If
a-m<k-1
, then both
N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)
and
N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)
are zero, in which case (
6.30
) also holds.
∎

Claim 6.12
.

For all
a\geq k
and
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1,r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)

(6.31)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2},r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3}\dots,r_{k};a).

Throughout the proof,
r_{3},\dots,r_{k}
and
a
are fixed. Let

T_{2}(r,s,\ell)\coloneqq\binom{a}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r}{\ell}\ell!\,N_{k-1}(s,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)

so that
6.9
gives

N_{k}(r,s,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{a}T_{2}(r,s,\ell).

Substituting this to every term of (
6.31
),
Proposition

6.2

(2)
reduces (
6.31
) to proving that, for all
0\leq\ell\leq m\leq a
,

\displaystyle\hphantom{{}\leq{}}T_{2}(r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1,\ell)\,T_{2}(r_{1},r_{2},m)+T_{2}(r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1,m)\,T_{2}(r_{1},r_{2},\ell)

\displaystyle\leq T_{2}(r_{1}+1,r_{2},\ell)\,T_{2}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,m)+T_{2}(r_{1}+1,r_{2},m)\,T_{2}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,\ell).

This simplifies to

\displaystyle\biggl[\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1+1}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{m}-\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1+1}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{\ell}\biggr]

\displaystyle{}\times{}

\displaystyle\Bigl[N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt{}-N_{k-1}(r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-\ell)\,N_{k-1}(r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a-m)\Bigr]\leq 0.

The term
\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1+1}{\ell}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{m}-\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1+1}{m}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{r_1}{\ell}
is nonpositive, again by
[
17
, Theorem 3.2]
, and the other term is nonnegative by

. This concludes the proof of the claim.
∎

Our last claim proves (
6.23
), which we restate for the reader’s convenience.

Claim 6.13
.

For all
a\geq k
and
0\leq r_{i}\leq s_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq k
,

N_{k}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{k};a)\leq N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{k};a)\,N_{k}(r_{1},s_{2},\dots,s_{k};a).

Rearrange (
6.31
) as

\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}

so that the RHS is obtained from the LHS by increasing the first parameter
r_{1}
by
1
while fixing the others. Repeatedly applying this yields that for all
s_{1}\geq r_{1}
,

\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}.

We rearrange this to obtain

\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},r_{2}+1,r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}.

Again, the RHS is obtained from the LHS by increasing the second parameter
r_{2}
by
1
while fixing the others. Repeatedly applying this yields that for all
s_{1}\geq r_{1}
and
s_{2}\geq r_{2}
,

\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{2}},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{2}},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{2}},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{2}},r_{3},\dots,r_{k};a)}.

Here, the colours assigned to

and

are to highlight the difference between the two sides.
In fact, by the symmetry of

, it follows that for all
1\leq i<j\leq k
,
s_{i}\geq r_{i}
, and
s_{j}\geq s_{j}
,

\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i},\dots,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{j}},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,s_{i},\dots,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{j}},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,r_{i},\dots,{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{j}},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(r_{1},\dots,s_{i},\dots,{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{j}},\dots,r_{k};a)}.

(6.32)

Repeatedly using (
6.32
) gives that, for all

,

,

\displaystyle\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{2}},r_{3},r_{4}\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{2}},r_{3},r_{4}\dots,r_{k};a)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{2}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{3}},r_{4}\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{2}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{3}},r_{4}\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},s_{2},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{3}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{4}},\dots,r_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},s_{2},{\color[rgb]{0.75,0,0.25}s_{3}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}r_{4}},\dots,r_{k};a)}\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{k}(r_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4},\dots,s_{k};a)}{N_{k}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},s_{4},\dots,s_{k};a)},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Finally,

) implies (

Having seen the applications of

for antiferromagnetic graphs

For some particular choices of

Let

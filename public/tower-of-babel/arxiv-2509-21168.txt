Abstract

A
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold is a manifold
M
together with a bivector field
\Lambda
, a 3-form
\varphi
, and a closed 1-form
\theta
such that the exterior derivative
d\varphi
of
\varphi
is the wedge product of
\theta
and
\varphi
, the anchor
\Lambda^{\#}(\theta)
of
\theta
is identically zero, and the Jacobiator (Jacobi operator; which is half the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket of
\Lambda
with itself) associated to
\Lambda
is the anchor
\Lambda^{\#}(\varphi)
of
\varphi
. In this work, we define the notion of a contravariant derivative adapted to these manifolds and establish the prequantization condition in terms of the
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson cohomology. We then introduce a polarization and construct a quantum Hilbert space. These results are illustrated by examples.

Keywords:
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold, Geometric quantization, prequantization, cohomology.

1
Introduction

Geometric quantization, pioneered by Kostant and Souriau, offers a profound framework for constructing quantum systems from classical phase spaces modeled by symplectic or Poisson manifolds. This procedure, encompassing prequantization and polarization, assigns symplectic manifolds to Hilbert spaces, and classical observables to Hermitian operators. While thoroughly developed for symplectic and Poisson manifolds, recent advances in mathematical physics require generalizations to incorporate broader structures arising in string theory, noncommutative geometry, and deformation quantization. These generalizations have the potential to reveal new insights into the interplay between classical and quantum systems.

A pivotal innovation emerged with twisted Poisson manifolds
[
11
]
, where a closed 3-form
\varphi
deforms the Jacobi
identity through
\frac{1}{2}[\Lambda,\Lambda]=\Lambda^{\#}(\varphi)
. These structures encapsulate topological fluxes in string theories.
In
[
4
]
, Pedalidou established their geometric quantization by introducing Lichnerowicz-twisted Poisson cohomology to
characterize prequantization bundles, and polarization-compatible observables.

Nonetheless, physical settings in topological
field theory (see
[
1
]
) motivate
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifolds
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
(see
[
10
]
),
where a closed 1-form
\theta
is linked to the 3-form
\varphi
via
d\varphi=\theta\wedge\varphi
, and
\Lambda^{\#}(\theta)=0
.
Such manifolds generalize twisted Poisson manifolds.
The non-closure of
\varphi
in this setting disrupts conventional geometric quantization for twisted Poisson manifold. To
address this challenge, we generalize Pedalidou’s framework to
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifolds by using adapted cohomology
[
10
]

to define
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson-Chern classes, establish prequantization condition, and construct a quantum Hilbert space.

We begin by reviewing the essential concepts in section
2
before presenting our results in sections
3
,
4
,
5
.

2.1
Basic definitions

Let
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
, and let
\psi\in\Omega^{k}(M)

the interior derivative of
\psi
, which is defined by

\displaystyle i_{X}\psi(X_{1},\dots,X_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\psi(X,X_{1},\dots,X_{k-1})

for every

A bivector

naturally induces a morphism
\Lambda^{\#}:\Omega^{k}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{k}(M)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\#}(\psi)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\varphi\bigg(\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha_{1}),\dots,\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha_{k})\bigg),

(2.1)

for every
\psi\in\Omega^{k}(M)
and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.
In particular,
\Lambda^{\#}(f)=f
for

, and
\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)
is a vector field for every

. For a differential form
\psi

.

2.2
Koszul and Schouten brackets

The Koszul bracket
[\ ,\ ]_{K}
associated to a bivector field
\Lambda
is defined by

[\alpha,\beta]_{K}=\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)}(\beta)-\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda^{\#}(\beta)}(\alpha)-d\Lambda(\alpha,\beta),\quad\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M).

The Schouten-Nijenhuis (or Schouten) bracket had been introduced by Schouten (
[
5
]
)
and Nijenhuis (
[
2
]
). Its definition stems from the following result.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 2.8]

There exists a unique
\mathbb{R}
-bilinear operation
[\ ,\ ]:\mathfrak{X}^{p+1}(M)\times\mathfrak{X}^{q+1}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{p+q+1}(M),(p,q\geq-1)

which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

When
p=q=0
, it is the usual Lie bracket of vector fields;

(ii)

When
p=0
and
q=-1
, it is the Lie derivative
[X,f]=\mathcal{L}_{X}f=X(f)
;

For
P\in\mathfrak{X}^{p+1}(M)
,
Q\in\mathfrak{X}^{q+1}(M)
, and
R\in\mathfrak{X}^{r+1}(M)
,

(iii)

Graded skew-symmetry:
[P,Q]=-(-1)^{pq}[Q,P]
;

(iv)

Graded Leibniz identity:
[P,Q\wedge R]=[P,R]\wedge R+(-1)^{p(q+1)}Q\wedge[P,R];

(v)

Graded Jacobi identity: the graded Jacobi operator (Jacobiator)
J_{[,]}(P,Q,R)
of
P,Q,R
is identically null, where

J_{[,]}(P,Q,R)=(-1)^{rp}[P,[Q,R]]+(-1)^{pq}[Q,[R,P]]+(-1)^{qr}[R,[P,Q]].

2.3
Lie-Rinehart algebra

Let
R
be a commutative ring. Let
A
be an algebra over
R
.
We recall that a derivation of
A
is a morphism
\delta:A\longmapsto A
of
R
-modules so that
\delta(ab)=\delta(a)b+a\delta(b)

for all
a,b\in A
. We denote by
Der(A)
the set of derivations of
A
. It is well known that
Der(A)
is a Lie algebra over
R

under the commutator of derivations
[\delta_{1},\delta_{2}]=\delta_{1}\circ\delta_{2}-\delta_{2}\circ\delta_{1}
.
The
R
-module
Der(A)
is well known to be a
A
-module if the algebra
A
is commutative.

Definition 2.2
.

[
6
, page 5]

Let
R
be a commutative ring, and let
A
be a commutative
R
-algebra (not necessarily with unit). Let
(L,[-,-])
be a Lie algebra over
R
.
A Lie-Rinehart algebra structure on
L
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\rho:L\longrightarrow Der(A)
satisfying the following compatibility properties:

1.

\rho(a\alpha)(b)=a\rho(\alpha)(b)
,

2.

[\alpha,a\beta]=a[\alpha,\beta]+\rho(\alpha)(a)\beta
,

where
a,b\in A
and
\alpha,\beta\in L
.
A Lie-Rinehart algebra is a pair
(L,\rho)
where
\rho
is a Lie-Rinehart structure on

.

2.4.1
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson structures

A
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold is a manifold
M
equipped with a bivector field
\Lambda
,
together with a 3-form
\varphi
and a closed 1-form
\theta
on
M
, such that the following hold:
d\varphi=\theta\wedge\varphi
,

\Lambda^{\#}(\theta)=0
, and

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{2}[\Lambda,\Lambda]

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{\#}(\varphi),

(2.2)

where
[\ ,\ ]
is the Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket defined from Theorem
2.1
.
A
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold will be denoted by
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
.

This definition differs from that of a twisted Poisson manifold by breaking the closure of the differential
3
-form
\varphi
via
the introduction of a closed differential
1
-form
\theta
, while retaining specific compatibility constraints.
The following examples of
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifolds are constructed from certain manifolds equipped with additional structures.

Example 1
.

1.

Any Poisson (twisted Poisson) manifold can be endowed with a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson structure.

2.

Let
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})
be a coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
.
Let
\theta=dx_{5}
,
\Lambda=e^{f}\partial_{x_{1}}\wedge\partial_{x_{2}}+e^{g}\partial_{x_{3}}\wedge\partial_{x_{4}}
, and

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}e^{-g}dx_{1}\wedge dx_{3}\wedge dx_{4}+\partial_{x_{2}}e^{-g}dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}\wedge dx_{4}

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{x_{5}}e^{-g}-e^{-g})dx_{3}\wedge dx_{4}\wedge dx_{5}+\partial_{x_{3}}e^{-f}dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{3}

\displaystyle\quad+\partial_{x_{4}}e^{-f}dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{4}+(\partial_{x_{5}}e^{-f}-e^{-f})dx_{1}\wedge dx_{2}\wedge dx_{5},

where

. Then the triple
(\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
is a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson structure on
\mathbb{R}^{5}
.

2.4.2
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson complex

Let
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
be a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold. The bracket
\{f,g\}:C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow(C^{\infty}(M),
defined by
(f,g)\longrightarrow\{f,g\}=\Lambda(df,dg)
verifies the following equality:

\{f,\{g,h\}\}+\{g,\{h,f\}\}+\{h,\{f,g\}\}=\Lambda^{\#}(\varphi)(df,dg,dh).

As a consequence, the Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology of what we term a
\theta
-almost
\varphi
-twisted Lie algebra

(C^{\infty}(M),\{\ ,\ \})
cannot be defined with respect to the representation given by Hamiltonian vector fields, that is, to the representation defined by the map
C^{\infty}(M)\times C^{\infty}(M)\longrightarrow C^{\infty}(M),\ (f,g)\longmapsto X_{f}(g)=\Lambda^{\#}(df)(g)
.

Nevertheless, any
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
induces a Lie-Rinehart algebra (Lie algebroid ) on the module of 1-forms
\Omega^{1}(M)
of
M
. The associated Lie bracket on
\Omega^{1}(M)
is defined by

\displaystyle[\alpha,\beta]_{\varphi,\theta}=[\alpha,\beta]_{K}+i_{\Lambda^{\#}(\beta)}i_{\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)}\varphi+\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)\cdot\theta

(2.3)

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

Let
\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n}:\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{X}^{n+1}(M)
,
v\longmapsto\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n}(v)
be the map defined by

\begin{array}[]{r}\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n}(v)(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n+1})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{i-1}(\alpha_{i})^{\#}(v(\alpha_{1},\dots,\widehat{\alpha_{i}},\dots,\alpha_{n+1}))\\
+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}(-1)^{i+j}v([\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}]_{\varphi,\theta},\alpha_{1},\dots,\widehat{\alpha_{i}},\dots,\widehat{\alpha_{j}},\dots,\alpha_{n+1})\end{array}

(2.4)

for every
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n+1}\in\Omega^{1}(M)
. As usual, the terms with a hat symbol indicate that they are omitted.
It is shown in
[
10
]
that the ordered pair
(\mathfrak{X}(M),\partial_{\varphi,\theta})
is a cochain complex.

The
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson cohomology of
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
is the cohomology of

(\mathfrak{X}(M),\partial_{\varphi,\theta})
.
It is denoted by
H_{\theta-atP}^{*}(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
, or simply
H_{\theta-atP}^{*}(M)
if there is no ambiguity. More precisely, for
every positive integer
n
,

\displaystyle H_{\theta-atP}^{n}(M)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{ker\Big(\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n}:\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}^{n+1}(M)\Big)}{Im\Big(\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n-1}:\mathfrak{X}^{n-1}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M)\Big)}

(2.5)

where
\mathfrak{X}^{-1}(M)=\{0\}
. The cohomology class of any element
\mu\in ker(\partial_{\varphi,\theta}^{n}:\mathfrak{X}^{n}(M)\to\mathfrak{X}^{n+1}(M))

is denoted by
[\mu]_{\varphi,\theta}
.

It is also shown in
[
10
]
that

\displaystyle\partial_{\varphi,\theta}\circ\Lambda^{\#}

\displaystyle=-\Lambda^{\#}\circ d.

(2.6)

Thus, the following result holds.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
be a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold. We denote by

H_{dR}^{*}(M,\mathbb{R})
the de Rham cohomology of the
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
,
the cochain complex homomorphism
\Lambda^{\#}:(\Omega(M),d)\longrightarrow(\mathfrak{X}(M),\partial_{\varphi,\theta})
induces a homomorphism in cohomology that we again note
\Lambda^{\#}
,

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\#}:H_{dR}^{*}(M,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow

\displaystyle H_{\theta-atP}^{*}(M),\ [\mu]\longmapsto[\Lambda^{\#}(\mu)]_{\varphi,\theta}.

(2.7)

Moreover, if
\Lambda

\theta
-almost twisted Poisson-Chern class of a complex line bundle over
\theta

Let
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)

Definition 3.1
.

A contravariant derivative on
\xi:K\to M
is a
\mathbb{R}
-linear mapping
D:\Omega^{1}(M)\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K))
satisfying the following conditions:

\displaystyle D_{(f\alpha)}=fD_{\alpha},\text{ and }\ D_{\alpha}(fs)=fD_{\alpha}(s)+\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)(f)s

(3.1)

for every
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
,
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, and

.

Let
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
be a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold, and
h
a Hermitian metric on a complex line bundle
\xi:K\to M
. We say that a contravariant derivative
D
on
\xi:K\to M
is compatible with

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)(h(s_{1},s_{2}))=h(D_{\alpha}s_{1},s_{2})+h(s_{1},D_{\alpha}s_{2})

(3.2)

for every
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}(M)
, and
s_{1},s_{2}\in\Gamma(K)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
\xi:K\to M
be a complex line bundle over a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
, and
D
a
contravariant derivative on
\xi:K\to M

is the mapping
C_{D}:\Omega^{1}(M)\times\Omega^{1}(M)\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K)),(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto C_{D}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle C_{D}(\alpha,\beta)=D_{\alpha}\circ D_{\beta}-D_{\beta}\circ D_{\alpha}-D_{[\alpha,\beta]_{\varphi,\theta}}.

(3.3)

From definition
3.1
, and relation (
3.3
), we deduce that the curvature
C_{D}
of

is bilinear over

, and skew-symmetric. Thus, from the fact that
\xi:K\to M

P_{C_{D}}=P_{{C_{D}}_{1}}+iP_{{C_{D}}_{2}}

\displaystyle C_{D}(\alpha,\beta)(s)=P_{C_{D}}(\alpha,\beta)s

for every
\alpha,\beta\in\Omega^{1}(M)

The cohomology operator
\partial_{\varphi,\theta}

\displaystyle C_{D}(\alpha,\beta)(s)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{i}{2\pi}P_{C_{D}}\right]_{\varphi,\theta}

4
Prequantization

The prequantization step in geometric quantization aims to construct a faithful representation of the classical algebra of observables as operators acting on sections of a complex line bundle. Specifically, for a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
, we seek to associate to each smooth function
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
a Hermitian operator
\hat{f}
on the space of cross-sections
\Gamma(K)
of a Hermitian line bundle
\xi:K\to M
such that the map
f\mapsto\hat{f}
satisfies
\widehat{\{f,g\}}=\{\hat{f},\hat{g}\}_{\varphi,\theta}
, where
\{\ ,\ \}_{\varphi,\theta}
is a convenient
\theta
-almost
\varphi
-twisted Lie bracket defined on
\Gamma(K)
. This requires the construction of the line bundle
\xi:K\to M
having a specific contravariant derivative
D
.

Let
(M,\Lambda,\varphi,\theta)
be a
\theta
-almost twisted Poisson manifold, and
D
a contravariant derivative on a complex line bundle
\xi:K\to M
. Let
\hat{f}
be the representation of
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K))
defined by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(s)

\displaystyle=D_{df}s+2\pi ifs,\ \ s\in\Gamma(K).

(4.1)

Let
A
be the subset of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K))
defined by
A=\{\ \hat{f}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K))\ /\penalty\ f\in C^{\infty}(M)\ \}
. We define on

\displaystyle\{\hat{f},\hat{g}\}_{\varphi,\theta}

\displaystyle=[\hat{f},\hat{g}]-D_{i_{\Lambda^{\#}(\beta)}i_{\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)}\varphi}-\Lambda(df,dg)D_{\theta}

(4.2)

for every

where
[\hat{f},\hat{g}]=\hat{f}\circ\hat{g}-\hat{g}\circ\hat{f}
.
We have de following Proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

The hat representation
\widehat{}\ \ :(C^{\infty}(M),\{\ ,\ \})\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\Gamma(K)),\ f\mapsto\hat{f}
is a homomorphism, that is

\displaystyle\widehat{\{f,g\}}

\displaystyle=\{\hat{f},\hat{g}\}_{\varphi,\theta},\ \ f,g\in C^{\infty}(M).

(4.3)

This means

\displaystyle C_{D}(df,dg)

\displaystyle=-2\pi i\{f,g\}.

(4.4)

Proof.

From equality (
4.1
), and Definition

, we get

\displaystyle[\hat{f},\hat{g}]

\displaystyle=\hat{f}\circ\hat{g}-\hat{g}\circ\hat{f}

\displaystyle=D_{df}\circ D_{dg}-D_{dg}\circ D_{df}+4\pi i\{f,g\}.

By using, (

), (

), (

\displaystyle\widehat{\{f,g\}}s

\displaystyle=D_{d\{f,g\}}s+2\pi i\{f,g\}s

\displaystyle=D_{[df,dg]_{\varphi,\theta}}s-D_{i_{\Lambda^{\#}(dg)}i_{\Lambda^{\#}(df)}\varphi}s-\Lambda(df,dg)D_{\theta}s+2\pi i\{f,g\}s

\displaystyle=D_{df}\circ D_{dg}s-D_{dg}\circ D_{df}s-C_{D}(df,dg)s-D_{i_{\Lambda^{\#}(dg)}i_{\Lambda^{\#}(df)}\varphi}s

\displaystyle\quad-\Lambda(df,dg)D_{\theta}s+2\pi i\{f,g\}s

\displaystyle=-C_{D}(df,dg)s+[\hat{f},\hat{g}]-4\pi i\{f,g\}s-D_{i_{\Lambda^{\#}(dg)}i_{\Lambda^{\#}(df)}\varphi}s

\displaystyle\quad-\Lambda(df,dg)D_{\theta}s+2\pi i\{f,g\}s

\displaystyle=\{\hat{f},\hat{g}\}_{\varphi,\theta}s-C_{D}(df,dg)s-2\pi i\{f,g\}s.

And the proposition follows. ∎

-almost twisted Poisson manifold

Non-Compact case

If
M
is not compact, a more involved procedure is required to define a finite scalar product. We consider the real Lie subalgebra
\mathcal{P}_{0}
of
(\Omega^{1}(M),[,]_{\varphi,\theta})
whose complexification is
\mathcal{P}\cap\bar{\mathcal{P}}
. We have that
\Lambda(\alpha,\beta)=0
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
. We will assume that the distribution
\Lambda^{\#}(\mathcal{P}_{0})
defines a regular foliation
\mathcal{F}
on
M
with a Hausdorff leaf space
N=M/\mathcal{F}
.
Under these conditions, the Hamiltonian vector field
\Lambda^{\#}(df)
of any observable
f\in\mathcal{Q}(\mathcal{P})
is projectable with respect to
\Lambda^{\#}(\mathcal{P}_{0})
onto
N
(we have,
[\Lambda^{\#}(df),\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)]=\Lambda^{\#}([df,\alpha]_{\varphi,\theta})\in\mathcal{P}_{0},\forall\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{0})
. This allows us to restrict our attention to a specific type of half-densities on
M
. Specifically, we consider those that are pullbacks
\varpi^{*}\varrho_{N}
of half-densities
\varrho_{N}
on the leaf space
N
via the canonical projection
\varpi:M\longrightarrow N
. For such sections, the action of the quantum operator simplifies to:

\displaystyle\hat{f}(s\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{N})

\displaystyle=\hat{f}(s)\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{N}+s\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda^{\#}(df)}\varpi^{*}\varrho_{N}

\displaystyle=\hat{f}(s)\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{N}+s\otimes\varpi^{*}(\mathcal{L}_{\varpi_{*}\Lambda^{\#}(df)}\varrho_{N}).

We have that
\mathcal{L}_{\Lambda^{\#}(\alpha)}\varpi^{*}\varrho_{iN}=0
, for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{0}
, i=1,2, since
\varpi^{*}\varrho_{1N},\varpi^{*}\varrho_{2N}
are projectable on
N
. Using this fact, (
3.2
), (
5.2
), and (
5.5
), we show that the density
h(s_{1},s_{2})\varpi^{*}\varrho_{1N}\varpi^{*}\varrho_{2N}
is projectable on
N
.
Consequently, the scalar product of two projectable sections
s_{1}\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{1N},s_{2}\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{2N}\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
involves the projection of the quantity
h(s_{1},s_{2})\varpi^{*}\varrho_{1N}\varpi^{*}\varrho_{2N}
to a 1-density
\delta_{N}
on
N
. The scalar product is then given by integration over
N
:

\langle s_{1}\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{1N},s_{2}\otimes\varpi^{*}\varrho_{2N}\rangle=\int_{N}\delta_{N}.

By considering the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{c}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}_{0}
of projectable sections with compact support in
N
, we obtain a pre-Hilbert space. The operators
\hat{f}
remain anti-Hermitian with respect to this scalar product. The Hilbert space of quantum states is finally constructed by completing
\mathcal{H}^{c}_{0}
and defining Hermitian operators as
i\hat{f}
, analogous to the compact case.

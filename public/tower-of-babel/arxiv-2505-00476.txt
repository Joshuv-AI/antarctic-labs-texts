Simplified Fermionic Scattering State Preparation for the NISQ Era Preprint: APS/123-QED
Michael Hite

Abstract
With quantum computers steadily improving, large volume quantum scattering simulations are getting very close. Yet, in the noisy intermediate scale quantum (NISQ) era, digital computers are limited to shallow circuits on the order of a thousand layers. Thus, accurate and efficient state preparation methods are needed. We introduce a simplified fermionic scattering state preparation method that reduces circuit depth significantly by partially relaxing the fermionic condition. Using the 1+1D transverse field Ising model as our test bed with exact diagonalization and time evolving block decimation with matrix product states, we show that our simplified states retain nearly all of the behavior of the true fermionic state while being prepared on just a handful of qubits. We also show promising results on IonQ Forte 1 for eight sites.

In doing so, we seek to answer the question as to how much can we simplify the preparation without losing the bulk behavior of the state? We propose a simplified wave packet preparation technique that localizes the state preparation operators in position space by partially relaxing the fermionic anti-commutation condition. In doing so, the depth of the circuit becomes independent of system size, only relying on the width of the individual wave packets. With the simplified preparation, we show that these packets retain much of the behavior of the true states, while allowing for straightforward tuning to increase accuracy.

This work is structured as follows. We first introduce the transverse field Ising model and its fermionic mapping and then describe our simplified state preparation method. Then, using exact diagonalization and time evolving block decimation (TEBD)
Vidal 2003
with matrix product states (MPS), we verify that our simplification is a good approximation for the true fermionic state. We then show with IonQ Forte 1 that implementation on near term quantum hardware is very promising.

Transverse Field Ising Model
- Consider an
N
-site transverse field Ising model in the basis where the transverse field is diagonal. The Hamiltonian for the system is given by

\hat{H}=-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\lambda\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{x}_{j+1}+\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j}\right),

(1)

where
\lambda
is the nearest neighbor (transverse field) coupling constant, the coupling to the transverse field is set to one, and
\sigma_{N+1}=\sigma_{1}
for periodic boundary conditions. Diagonalization of the Hamiltonian requires three separate transformations: Jordan-Wigner, Fourier, and Bogoliubov
Schultz et al. 1964
;
Sachdev 2011
;
Kaufman 1949
;
Cervera-Lierta 2018
. We rely on the first two transformations. Introducing Jordan-Wigner fermionic operators

\begin{split}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}\right)\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{j},\\
\hat{c}_{j}&=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{i}\right)\hat{\sigma}^{+}_{j},\end{split}

(2)

where the product of
\hat{\sigma}^{z}
’s is called the Pauli string and assures the operators obey anti-commutation relations. The Hamiltonian in terms of these operators is

\hat{H}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\lambda\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}+\hat{c}_{j}\right)\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j+1}-\hat{c}_{j+1}\right)+2\left(\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}\hat{c}_{j}-1\right)\right],

(3)

such that
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{N+1}=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1}
,
\hat{c}_{N+1}=\hat{c}_{1}
for an odd number of particles, and
\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{N+1}=-\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{1}
,
\hat{c}_{N+1}=-\hat{c}_{1}
for an even number of particles. In terms of momenta, this is accounted for by having separate momenta numbers for an odd and even number of particles:

k=\begin{cases}0,\pm\frac{2\pi}{N},\pm\frac{4\pi}{N},\dots,\pm\frac{(N-2)\pi}{N},\pi&\text{(odd)}\\
\pm\frac{\pi}{N},\pm\frac{3\pi}{N},\dots,\pm\frac{(N-1)\pi}{N}&\text{(even)}\end{cases}\quad.

(4)

We will denote the ground state as
|\Omega\rangle
. The Fourier operators, which we will use to do Fourier analysis are

\begin{split}\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{k}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{s}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-ikj}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j},\\
\hat{\eta}_{k}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{s}}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{ikj}\hat{c}_{j}.\end{split}

(5)

In position space, a fermionic Gaussian wave packet centered at site
x_{0}
and momenta
k_{0}
is then

\left|\psi\right\rangle=\hat{\mathcal{G}}|\Omega\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{-ikx_{j}}e^{-r(x_{0},x_{j})^{2}/\sigma^{2}}\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j}|\Omega\rangle,

(6)

where
r(x_{0},x_{j})
is the distance between
x_{0}
and
x_{j}
, and
\sigma
is proportional to the width of the packet in position space.

Mapping to a Quantum Computer
- If we consider the Gaussian wave packet operator we made in Equation
6
, it is a sum of non-local Jordan-Wigner operators acting on the whole system. For an arbitrary
M
-qubit state, Möttonönen decomposition would result in a circuit with at most
2^{M+2}-4M-4
CNOT gates and at a minimum
\frac{1}{4}\left(2^{M+1}-3M-2\right)
CNOT gates
Mottonen et al. 2004
. For eight sites, this would mean at most 988 CNOT gates and a theoretical minimum 122 CNOT gates. More mature decomposition procedures like those in the
qiskit
library
Javadi-Abhari et al. 2024
makes a circuit with a depth of 495 layers and 247 CNOT gates (assuming all-to-all connectivity), which is a factor of four less than the Möttönen maximum, but still past the limit of current quantum hardware without some classical intervention.

Suppose we choose
\sigma
such that the wave packet has a width of five qubits and is centered on qubit
x_{0}
. This means the only significant contributions to the sum will be from sites
x_{0}-2,x_{0}-1,x_{0},x_{0}+1
and
x_{0}+2
. We can (1) truncate the sum to just these five terms. Yet, the operator is still a sum of multi-qubit operators ranging from qubits
0
to
x_{0}+2
. Thus, we need another way to localize the operators. This is accomplished by (2) cutting off the Pauli strings outside of
x_{0}-2,\dots,x_{0}+2
, so

\begin{split}&\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{x_{0}-2}\longrightarrow\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{x_{0}-2},\\
&\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{x_{0}-1}\longrightarrow-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-2}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{x_{0}-1},\\
&\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{x_{0}}\longrightarrow\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-2}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-1}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{x_{0}},\\
&\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{x_{0}+1}\longrightarrow-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-2}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-1}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{x_{0}+1},\\
&\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{x_{0}+2}\longrightarrow\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-2}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}-1}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{x_{0}+1}\hat{\sigma}^{-}_{x_{0}+2}.\end{split}

(7)

For a single wave packet this is exact up to the truncated sum (step 1) if the Hamiltonian is translationally invariant. The same is not true for two wave packets. Consider two pave packets built in separate five qubit subspaces. If we have one packet centered at site 3, and the other at site 9, then

\left\{\tilde{c}^{\dagger}_{j},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{j^{\prime}}\right\}\neq 0

(8)

for
j\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
and
j^{\prime}\in\{7,8,9,10,11\}
. This is due to the second set of Jordan strings not extending back to the origin. This may have a larger impact for certain observables more than others, so we must choose wisely what quantities to measure. Nevertheless, we now have two operators localized on sites 1-5 and 7-11 respectively. With this simplification, we can use
qiskit
’s decomposition in these subspaces. If the wave packets are three qubits wide, then the depth is 9 with 4 CNOT gates, and for packet of five qubits wide, the depth is 53 with 26 CNOT gates. This is substantially less than the exact state. In position space, we will measure the occupation number, which written in terms of Pauli-operators is

\hat{N}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j}\right).

(9)

We can also do Fourier analysis, where we measure the probability that
|\psi\rangle
is in the momentum state
|k\rangle
:

\mathcal{P}\left(|k\rangle\right)=\left|\langle k|\psi\rangle\right|^{2}

(10)

for

|k\rangle=\hat{\eta}^{\dagger}_{k}|\Omega\rangle.

(11)

We will use the full definition of
\eta^{\dagger}_{k}
in Equation
5
with exact
c^{\dagger}
’s. Finally, to induce an interaction in the two particle sector, we will add the nearest neighbor term

\hat{H}^{\prime}=\epsilon\sum_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j}\hat{\sigma}^{z}_{j+1},

(12)

for
\epsilon>0

Gustafson et al. 2021
. This will allow us to compare how our simplified wave packets interact to the exact wave packets.

Now we would like to see the effect for introducing an interaction term. For one-dimensional scattering, we are often interested in finding the time delay and phase shift. In the one particle sector, an interaction capable of producing a time delay is a potential bump or well before an infinite wall. One can measure the time delay in the reflection coefficient of the incident wave to then get the phase shift. This has been done accurately with IBM hardware
Gustafson et al. 2021
;
Parks et al. 2023

Results with IonQ
- Now that we verified that this is a good simplification for a fermionic wave packet scattering state, we show simulations on IonQ Forte 1, a 36 qubit trapped ion machine with all-to-all connectivity. We generate the 8-qubit initial state in Figure
1
(a) with Forte 1 and compare with the truncated state (w/ ED), and see occupations with on average of 5.12% error with just 300 shots. Figure
5
(b) shows the evolution of the state with 275 shots. No error correction or mitigation procedures were used. Considering occupations
\langle N_{j}(t)\rangle>0.1
, percent error is just 13.3% from the truncated state (w/ ED). Even with so few shots, the machine does very well at preparing and evolving the state. The path for scaling up will be sequential, first enlarging the space, and then widening the wave packets to five sites a piece. We will iterate this procedure up to at 16 sites, and then increase the initial distance between the packets. With the inclusion of error correction and noise mitigation techniques (which require at least 500 shots), we expect the simulations to improve significantly, allowing us to measure quantities like the time delay.

Conclusion
- We have developed a simplified fermionic scattering state preparation for NISQ era quantum computing that dramatically reduces circuit depth by localizing the wave packet in space with the only drawback being a partial relaxation of fermionic condition. We showed that the simplified scattering states are very close to the true states in position and momentum space, and scale well for increased system size and with the addition of an interaction. Results on IonQ Forte 1 show the machine can accurately produce the state with a limited number of shots. Future work will include a detailed analysis of truncating away terms of the Gaussian sum and cutting off Pauli strings, and runs on IonQ and IBM with an increased number of shots and noise correction and mitigation procedures. In doing so, we can compare the resiliency of our approximation for all-to-all connected trapped ion machines and nearest-neighbor connected transmon qubit machines. Further, we can compare with traditional techniques like preparation using Givens rotations, and even exchange
qiskit
’s decomposition for a Givens rotation decomposition in our method further reducing circuit depth. We expect this simplification procedure to be extremely beneficial for scattering simulations on near term quantum hardware.

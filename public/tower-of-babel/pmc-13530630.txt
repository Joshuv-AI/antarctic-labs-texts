Poster 36. Perioperative Nutritional Supplementation with Essential Amino Acids, Protein, and Creatine Enhances Quadriceps Recovery After ACL Reconstruction in Active-Duty Military Soldiers: A Randomized Controlled Trial
Authors: Christian A Cruz, Valentina Ramirez
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjective:Quadriceps atrophy and strength deficits following anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) reconstruction remain major barriers to return-to-duty and sport in military and athletic populations. While perioperative rehabilitation strategies have advanced, the role of preoperative nutritional optimization is not well established. Therefore, the purpose of this study was to evaluate the effect of preoperative nutritional supplementation with essential amino acids (EAA), protein, and creatine on postoperative quadriceps muscle circumference and strength in active-duty military service members undergoing ACL reconstruction.Methods:Fifty active-duty soldiers undergoing primary ACL reconstruction with either BTB, Quad, or Allograft were randomized to receive either (1) a 4-week preoperative supplementation protocol (daily EAA 15g, whey protein 20g, and creatine monohydrate 10g) or (2) standard preoperative care with no supplementation. All participants followed a standardized postoperative rehabilitation protocol. Outcomes were assessed at 3 and 6 months postoperatively. Primary endpoints were quadriceps muscle circumference (measured 10 cm above the superior pole of the patella) and isokinetic quadriceps strength (normalized to body weight). Secondary outcomes included IKDC and SANE scores. Between-group comparisons were analyzed using mixed-effects linear regression.Results:At 3 months, the supplementation group demonstrated significantly greater quadriceps circumference (−3.1% vs −6.8% reduction from baseline, p < 0.01) and quadriceps strength (68.5% vs 59.2% of contralateral limb, p < 0.01). At 6 months, circumference deficits remained lower (−1.2% vs −4.9%, p < 0.01) and strength recovery was superior (85.6% vs 77.3% of contralateral limb, p < 0.01). Patient-reported outcomes were higher in the supplementation group at both 3 months (IKDC 72.1 ± 8.3 vs 66.2 ± 7.5; p < 0.01) and 6 months (IKDC 88.4 ± 6.9 vs 81.3 ± 7.2; p < 0.001). No adverse events related to supplementation were observed.Conclusions:Preoperative supplementation with EAA, protein, and creatine significantly attenuates quadriceps atrophy and accelerates strength recovery following ACL reconstruction in active-duty soldiers.

Multi-modal segment anything model (mmSAM) for tumor segmentation in multi-tracer oncologic PET/CT
Authors: Zhijie Fang, Yibin Liu, Tao Tan, Feng Wang, Greta S P Mok
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeLesion segmentation is important in targeted radionuclide therapy dosimetry. In this study, we proposed a novel 2D multi-modal Segment Anything Model (mmSAM) for lesion segmentation in whole-body multi-tracer PET/CT images.MethodsThe AutoPET 2024 dataset, including 18F-PSMA (369 subjects), 18F-FDG (170 subjects) and 68Ga-PSMA (170 subjects) PET/CT images and their corresponding tumor masks, was used in this study. The 18F-PSMA PET/CT dataset was used as the primary dataset and was divided into 233: 36: 100 for training, validation and testing the mmSAM, using both PET and CT input. The transferability of the primary model was evaluated on 100 patients on other 2 datasets, without and with fine-tuning using 70 cases of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-PSMA respectively. Standard single modal 2D SAM with only PET input, 3D nnUNet with two-channel PET/CT input and the thresholding-based method were also implemented for comparison. Mean Dice, the 95th percentile Hausdorff distance (mean HD95), mean standardized uptake value (mean |SUVmean| error), metabolic tumor volume (mean |MTV| error), true positive rate (TPR), positive predictive value (PPV), and false discovery rate (FDR) were computed for all tumors. Statistical significance among different segmentation methods was evaluated using the Wilcoxon test.ResultsmmSAM achieved the best performance as compared to other segmentation methods for the primary 18F-PSMA dataset (mean Dice/HD95/|SUVmean| error/|MTV| error/TPR/PPV/FDR = 0.76/1.92 mm/5.10%/14.60%/100%/96.51%/3.49%, all p < 0.05), without fine-tuning (mean Dice/HD95/|SUVmean| error/|MTV| error/TPR/PPV/FDR = 0.61/2.28 mm/ 15.83%/27.71%/100%/78.91%/21.09% for 18F-FDG; 0.77/1.33 mm/5.55%/17.96%/100% /97.78%/2.22% for 68Ga-PSMA, p < 0.05), as well as with fine-tuning (mean Dice/HD95/|SUVmean| error/|MTV| error/TPR/PPV/FDR = 0.65/1.81 mm/6.62%/13.93%/100%/ 80.50%/19.50% for 18F-FDG; 0.81/1.15 mm/4.62%/14.30%/100%/99.30%/0.70% for 68Ga-PSMA, p < 0.05) on the cross-tracer datasets.ConclusionThe proposed mmSAM is promising for lesion segmentation in multi-tracer oncologic PET/CT images. Fine-tuning significantly enhances segmentation accuracy for cross-tracer studies.Keywords: Multi-modal, Segment anything model (SAM), PET/CT, Tumor segmentation

Introduction

Positron Emission Tomography/Computed Tomography (PET/CT) is promising for patient selections in theranostics applications. For example, prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET, including 68Ga-PSMA and 18F-PSMA PET imaging has gained significant importance for metastatic castration-resistant prostate cancer (mCRPC) detection in recent years, and it is routinely performed to determine the eligibility for 117Lu-PSMA radioligand therapy [1]. Besides, [18F]fluorodeoxyglucose (18F-FDG) is the most commonly used radiotracer for oncologic imaging, effective in detecting both primary and metastatic tumors, though its specificity is inferior to PSMA PET for prostate cancer. For both 18F-FDG and PSMA PET, quantitative assessment of tumor burden is important for treatment planning and prognostic evaluation. Target tumors usually demonstrate higher mean standardized uptake values (SUVmean) on FDG/PSMA PET images, facilitating their differentiation from background or adjacent organs. High quality quantitative time-of-flight PET images can now be obtained after corrections of various degrading effects, such as attenuation, scatter, depth-of-interaction, and partial volume effect (PVE). Furthermore, the integrated CT can provide anatomical information about the target tumors if they are visible on CT. Manual segmentation is considered as the gold standard for tumor segmentation. However, it is time-consuming and subject to inter- and intra-operator variability, particularly when only PET is used for delineation. To reduce the variability arising from manual segmentation, the SUV-based thresholding method is widely used for segmenting tumors from background in clinical practice due to its simplicity and efficiency. A commonly adopted strategy is using 40–50% of the maximum standardized uptake value (SUVmax) as the threshold [2]. However, the performance of this method varies for different reconstruction parameters.

Recently, deep learning-based medical image segmentation using convolutional neural networks have achieved great success. Accurate organ segmentation in CT imaging has become relatively mature with tools such as TotalSegmentator [3] and MONAI [4]. Similarly, promising advances are available in PET-based organ segmentation. However, tumor segmentation in PET/CT images remains one of the most challenging tasks due to tumor heterogeneity, variable uptake patterns, and indistinct boundaries. Furthermore, distinctions between primary and metastatic tumors introduce additional complexities. Primary tumors typically present as solitary, large, and metabolically active regions located within predictable anatomical sites, making them more accessible to segmentation models. In contrast, metastatic tumors are often multifocal, irregular in shape, smaller in volume, and exhibit lower or variable tracer uptake, particularly affected by PVE. The broad anatomical distribution and frequent co-occurrence with normal physiological uptake further complicate detection [5], which highlights the requirement for more robust, and generalizable segmentation strategies for metastatic tumors. Leung et al. [6] developed a semi-supervised transfer learning approach based on the nnUNet backbone [7] for fully automated, whole-body lesion segmentation and prognosis analysis on PET/CT. Yousefirizi et al. [8] proposed multi-resolution 3D U-Nets for automated lesion delineation in lymphoma from 18F-FDG PET/CT scans. Yazdani et al. [9] developed a shifted windows UNEt TRansformers (Swin UNETR) for segmentation of organs of interest and tumors using a self-supervised transformer model, pretrained based on whole-body 68Ga-PSMA PET/CT images and then fine-tuned by a limited set of annotated images. Qi et al. [10] proposed a multi-modal evidential fusion network to integrate complementary information from PET and CT images. Kang et al. [11] introduced a novel uncertainty-driven loss, which incentivized the network to effectively utilize the complementary information between modalities. However, the primary limitations of UNet-based automatic segmentation models lie in their susceptibility to false positive and false negative ratios [12, 13], reflecting over-segmentation of healthy tissue and under-segmentation of true tumor voxels, respectively. These shortcomings constrain voxel-wise accuracy and undermine the reliability of fully automated whole-body tumor segmentation in clinical practice.

The Segment Anything Model (SAM) [14] had been released by Meta based on training from 11 million 2D natural images, initiating a paradigm shift to a “smart” interactive segmentation strategy that provides competitive performance when properly prompted. SAM2 [15] further refines this model by extending its capabilities to video segmentation, but similarly follows the prompt-based interactive paradigm. Previously, we applied SAM for head and neck tumor segmentations on single-modality head and neck FDG PET images [16]. In this study, we propose a multi-modal 2D SAM (mmSAM) by efficiently integrating metabolic PET and anatomical CT information for tumor segmentation in whole-body multi-tracer oncologic PET/CT images and compare mmSAM with (1) standard single modal 2D SAM with only PET input; (2) nnUNet; and (3) threshold-based segmentation method (THR_Seg).

Materials and methods

Patient datasets

The AutoPET 2024 dataset (https://autopet-iii.grand-challenge.org [17, 18]), including studies of 18F-FDG (170 subjects), 18F-PSMA (369 subjects) and 68Ga-PSMA (170 subjects), was used in this study (Table 1). The FDG dataset comprises patients diagnosed with histologically proven malignant melanoma, lymphoma, or lung cancer, while the 2 PSMA datasets include pre- or post-therapeutic PET/CT images of male individuals with mCRPC. The FDG cohort was annotated by an experienced radiologist while the 2 PSMA cohorts were annotated by a single reader and reviewed by an experienced radiologist, and ground truth tumor masks were provided from the dataset. The FDG dataset was acquired at the University Hospital Tübingen, while the 2 PSMA datasets were obtained from the Ludwig Maximilian University Hospital in Munich. The 18F-PSMA dataset was used to train the primary model and was divided into training, validation, and testing (233: 36: 100) sets. Additionally, the transferability of the primary model was evaluated on 100 patients on other two datasets separately, with and without fine-tuning using 70 cases of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-PSMA, respectively.

Data pre-processing

The voxel spacing of all PET/CT images and segmentation masks was resampled to 1.5 mm3 based on the trilinear and nearest neighbor interpolation method. For mmSAM, each axial slice of PET and CT was scaled to the range [0, 255], as shown in Fig. 1a. While only PET slices were scaled for standard SAM. For nnUNet, each PET dataset was normalized using z-score normalization. CT data were clipped to [0.5, 99.5] percentile of the foreground intensity value, followed by z-score normalization.

The mmSAM workflow used in this study. a Data pre-processing; b Training of the primary mmSAM; c Model fine-tuning, testing and evaluation

Proposed mmSAM

mmSAM included pre-trained image encoders for PET and CT, one prompt encoder, one mask decoder, which were originally from standard SAM [14], and a newly introduced multi-modal fusion module, as shown in Fig. 1b. PET and CT images were encoded into metabolic and anatomic features (256 × 64 × 64) by two image encoders separately, followed by feature concatenation (512 × 64 × 64) and two fully connected layers with 768 and 256 neurons, respectively, to produce multi-modal features (256 × 64 × 64). Multi-modal features were then combined with prompt features (256 × 2 × 1) to predict tumor masks via the mask decoder. The output of mmSAM was transformed into a probability distribution via a sigmoid function, and the loss was computed as the average of Dice and cross-entropy losses between the prediction and the ground truth label. Additionally, feature fusion from deep to shallow layers was employed to enhance network optimization during training. Sliding window inference [19] was adopted, where PET/CT data were cropped into 50% overlapping patches for input. The predictions of each patch were integrated via the soft voting strategy to form the final segmentation mask. Experiments were conducted on the primary dataset to evaluate the effectiveness of multi-modal input and sliding window inference. For the primary mmSAM model, the training and validation 18F-PSMA dataset was used to fine-tune the mask decoder and parameters of the multi-modal fusion module, while parameters of the image encoders and prompt encoder were frozen. For testing on other 2 datasets, as shown in Fig. 1c and 70 cases of 18F-FDG and 68Ga-PSMA were used to fine-tune the primary mmSAM respectively, with only the mask decoder and multi-modal fusion module updated.

Segmentation method comparison

The proposed method was compared to other baseline segmentation methods, including 3D nnUNet, standard single modal 2D SAM with only PET input and THR_Seg. The 2 baseline deep learning-based methods were implemented using the same training strategy as mmSAM. For THR_Seg, the threshold was set to be 42% of the maximum intensity of the selected region-of-interest on PET images [2]. For mmSAM, standard 2D SAM and THR_Seg, bounding boxes were generated as prompt based on the ground truth tumor masks with an enlarged margin of 10 voxels at each dimension during training and testing.

For both SAM models, the Adam optimizer was used to optimize the segmentation model with an initial learning rate of 5 × 10− 5, running up to 50 epochs. The nnUNet configuration was run to 1000 epochs with 250 iterations per epoch for training, with a batch size of 128, an initial learning rate of 0.01, and also using Adam as the optimizer. All the experiments were implemented using PyTorch, which ran on 4 NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. The model training time was 12.5 h for mmSAM, 3.2 h for SAM and 2.5 h for nnUNet. During inference, the proposed mmSAM requires 4.5 GB of CPU memory, 7.6 GB of GPU memory and requires an average processing time of 3.2 min per patient.

Results

Sample ground truth and different segmentation results from one 18F-PSMA, 68Ga-PSMA and 18F-FDG subject are shown in Fig. 2. Our proposed mmSAM improves the tumor segmentation performance as compared to SAM, nnUNet and THR_Seg in multi-tracer oncologic PET/CT, providing more similar shapes and smoother boundaries of tumor masks as compared to the ground truth, superior to other methods. nnUNet is more prone to miss tumors, leading to a higher false negative ratio, i.e., lower TPR (Fig. 2b), and it may also include physiological uptake as tumors, as shown in Fig. 2a. THR_Seg method fails to produce smooth boundaries between adjacent slices. The comparison between models with and without fine-tuning demonstrates that fine-tuning significantly enhances segmentation performance for cross-tracer scenarios. Figure 4 shows the quantitative physical and clinical evaluation results of tumor segmentation of 100 18F-PSMA, 100 68Ga-PSMA and 100 18F-FDG subjects, respectively. For 18F-PSMA, mmSAM achieved the best performance, followed by SAM, THR_Seg and nnUNet. Under the cross-tracer evaluation setting without fine-tuning, mmSAM consistently and significantly (p < 0.05) outperformed other methods, with superior mean Dice, lower HD95, minimal |SUVmean| and |MTV| errors, and the highest tumor level detection accuracy. SAM ranked second, followed by THR_Seg and nnUNet. Fine-tuning enhances segmentation accuracy for cross-tracer studies for all deep learning-based models, and mmSAM still achieved the best performance (p < 0.05). For 18F-FDG, SAM achieved the second best performance, followed by nnUNet, and THR_Seg, whereas for 68Ga-PSMA, the order of best performance was mmSAM, SAM, THR_Seg and nnUNet. Notably, fine-tuning the 18F-PSMA–trained model on a dataset with a similar tracer (68Ga-PSMA) led to superior performance compared to fine-tuning it on a dataset with a less similar tracer (18F-FDG), i.e., with better mean Dice, HD95, |SUVmean| error, TPR, PPV and FDR. Across all tracers, mmSAM achieved perfect TPR (100%) and the highest PPV with the lowest FDR (p < 0.05). Results of physical and clinical indices are consistent.

Discussions

This study demonstrates the feasibility of tumor segmentation in whole-body oncologic PET/CT images from different tracers using mmSAM, a model pre-trained on diverse image datasets. Benefiting from the large-scale pre-training and learning from multimodal representations, mmSAM shows improved robustness to tumor heterogeneity, variable uptake patterns, and indistinct or diffuse tumor boundaries, as compared to the standard SAM and nnUNet. Our mmSAM framework our approach combined two pre-trained image encoders with a dedicated multi-modal fusion module, rather than directly assigning multi-modal images to RGB channels [21], enabling an efficient integration of metabolic and anatomical information to enhance tumor segmentation. The improvements are consistent with previous evidence that incorporating anatomical context enhances the detection of low-uptake tumors [9, 10]. Moreover, mmSAM incorporates sliding window inference, widely used in nnUNet, which leverages weighted fusion in overlapping regions to produce smoother boundaries.

Leung et al. [6] achieved a median Dice of 0.73 for tumor segmentation on 18F-FDG (611 scans) and 18F-DCFPyL PSMA PET/CT (408 scans) data using a nnUNet–based deep semi-supervised transfer learning framework. Yazdani et al. [9] demonstrated a moderate performance (mean Dice of 0.68) in delineating tumors on the 68Ga-PSMA dataset of 752 whole-body scans of different tumor sizes and uptake patterns using a Swin UNETR model with self-supervised pre-training and fine-tuning. Xu et al. [22] reported a median Dice of 0.51 for all tumors on 525 18F-DCFPyL PSMA PET/CT scans using a UNet model for mCRPC. Li et al. [23] developed an automated framework using UNETR, achieving an internal mean Dice of 0.70 and an external mean Dice of 0.68 on 193 18F-DCFPyL PSMA PET/CT scans for tumor segmentation. In this study, mmSAM achieved higher Dice scores, i.e., 0.76/0.65/0.81 for 18F-PSMA/18F-FDG/68Ga-PSMA for whole body images from different tracers, which could be primarily attributed to the use of prompts besides the effectiveness of the SAM model. As a result, nnUNet often misses tumors, resulting in false negatives and consequently a reduced TPR, as shown in Figs. 2 and 3. The thresholding-based method is also superior to nnUNet as it also requires the use of prompts.

Wang et al. [24] reported that fine-tuning the backbone can improve the performance of PET/CT-based whole-body lymphoma segmentation. Building upon this insight, we systematically evaluated our model built from 18F-PSMA on datasets of two other tracers, i.e., 18F-FDG and 68Ga-PSMA, with and without fine-tuning. Specifically, we fine-tuned the multi-modal fusion module and the mask decoder while the weights of pre-trained image and prompt encoders remained unchanged. Our results confirm that fine-tuning significantly enhances segmentation in cross-tracer scenarios (p < 0.05), highlighting the importance of task-specific adaptation for deep learning models. We have observed that the segmentation performance of cross-tracer models began to improve more consistently when 70 cases of each tracer are used for fine-tuning (data not shown). The segmentation performance is expected to further improve when more number of cases are used for fine-tuning, yet the optimum number is probably task-dependent and the full optimization is beyond the scope of this study.

The quantitative indices are assessed for each tumor, rather than the whole-body images. This approach is important for multi-focal disease [25], where accurate detection and delineation of each individual lesion is desirable, and standard Dice and HD95 of whole-body images may overlook contributions of small tumors. Our results show that the physical evaluation metrics (e.g., mean Dice and HD95) are consistent with the clinical indices (e.g., mean |SUVmean|, |MTV| error, TPR, PPV and FDR), confirming that segmentation accuracy directly impacts subsequent quantitative analysis. Meanwhile, SUVmean, SUVmax and MTV are commonly used in PET analysis, yet they are not used for absorbed dose estimation for subsequent targeted radionuclide therapy generally. Recently, SUVmean [26] and SUVmax [27] have been associated with tumor absorbed dose, consistently showing positive correlations. Therefore, segmentation uncertainties may propagate from PET quantification and influence absorbed dose estimation. However, prediction of absorbed dose from PET data is not a standard clinical practice thus estimation of how PET segmentation errors propagate to absorbed dose calculation is beyond the scope of this study.

We further observed that both physical and clinical metrics were generally superior for 68Ga-PSMA compared with 18F-FDG, regardless of fine-tuning. This finding is consistent with the fact that our base model was trained on 18F-PSMA data, while both 68Ga-PSMA and 18F-PSMA target the same antigen overexpressed on prostate cancer cells. In contrast, 18F-FDG is a glucose analog that accumulates not only in malignant tumors but also in inflammatory or metabolically active benign tissues, which complicates lesion delineation. These results further confirm cross-tracer generalization of models trained on PSMA-targeted tracers.

The main limitation of this study is the size of the dataset. Although AutoPET contains multi-center and multi-scanner data, more external data is still needed for model validation. Besides, the bounding boxes in this study are generated from the ground truth masks, which potentially overestimates the SAM-based and THR_Seg performance. While bounding boxes directly generated by clinicians would be desirable in future studies, we demonstrated that an independently trained nnUNet could be used to generate prompts for SAM for head and neck tumor segmentation previously, providing a potential fully automatic pathway for enhanced tumor segmentation [16]. Recent advances, i.e., MA-SAM [28] and MedSAM2 [29], have demonstrated the capability to perform 3D segmentation across multiple imaging modalities, including CT, MRI, and surgical video data, by leveraging volumetric context and temporal consistency. In comparison, our 2D mmSAM processes data on a slice-by-slice (axial) basis, which may introduce minor discontinuities between adjacent slices. However, the integration of multi-modal information and the use of sliding window inference partially mitigate this issue, allowing mmSAM to achieve accurate segmentation results in 2D slice-wise processing. Besides, compared to 3D models that require substantial computational resources [30], the proposed mmSAM remains a highly efficient and accurate alternative using 4 NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. In addition, the prompt design for 3D medical foundation model remains challenging as prompt points are inherently sparser compared to their 2D counterparts [31].

Conclusions

Our proposed mmSAM is feasible for semi-automatic tumor segmentation in whole-body oncologic PET/CT images based on the evaluation from the AutoPET 2024 dataset. mmSAM achieved the best performance for the primary 18F-PSMA dataset (mean Dice/HD95/|SUVmean| error/|MTV| error/TPR/PPV/FDR = 0.76/1.92 mm/5.10%/14.60%/100%/96.51%/3.49%, all p < 0.05) as compared to standard SAM, nnUNet, and THR_Seg method. Fine-tuning significantly enhances segmentation accuracy for cross-tracer studies. The proposed method is promising for whole-body oncologic PET/CT tumor segmentation.

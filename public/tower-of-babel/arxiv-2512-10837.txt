An equivalent condition for q q -holonomicity
Giulio Belletti

Abstract
We show that a sequence is q q -holonomic if and only if it satisfies the elimination property for any subset of variables. The same result also holds for holonomic sequences. As an application, we prove several conjectured closure properties for q q -holonomic sequences. We also prove that Jones-style sequences for links in any closed 3 3 -manifold are q q -holonomic, which in turn implies that the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants are q q -holonomic in the colors.



1
Introduction

A holonomic sequence
a_{n}
in a single variable satisfies a non-trivial linear recurrence relation with polynomial coefficients:
\sum_{i=0}^{k}P_{i}(n)a_{n+i}=0
. The
q
-analog concept is a
q
-holonomic sequence: a sequence
f_{n}
of elements in
\mathbb{C}(q)
is
q
-holonomic if it satisfies a non-trivial linear recurrence relation with coefficients polynomials in
q
and
q^{n}
:
\sum_{i=0}^{k}P_{i}(q,q^{n})f_{n+i}=0
.

It is not straightforward to extend this definition to sequences in several variables. Roughly speaking, a sequence in several variables is holonomic (or
q
-holonomic) if it satisfies a maximally overdetermined system of linear recursions like above; the precise definition requires a discussion on the dimension growth of a certain filtration, which will be explained in Section
2
. A
q
-holonomic sequence will in particular satisfy linear recurrence relations with a shift in every individual variable, but in general this is not enough (see for example
[
5
, Remark 7.3]
).

Holonomic sequences were first defined and studied in
[
16
]
. In that paper,
q
-holonomic sequences were also sketched, without the theory being developed; this was done in
[
14
]
. Later
q
-holonomic sequences became central in the study of quantum invariants, following the proof that the colored Jones function of a link in
S^{3}
is
q
-holonomic
[
4
]
and the formulation of the AJ conjecture
[
7
]
.

The present paper deals with
q
-holonomic sequences, although some of it also applies to holonomic ones (chiefly Theorem
2.7
).

The gap in sophistication between
q
-holonomic sequences in one variable versus several variables makes it so that many simple properties known in the univariate case were only conjectural in general.

There are two, related reasons to prefer this more abstract definition of
q
-holonomicity, over simply requiring a recurrence relation with a shift in each individual variable (this concept is called
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finiteness in
[
5
]
and is related to
\partial
-finiteness of
[
9
]
). The first is that a
q
-holonomic sequence is determined by its recurrence relations plus a finite number of initial values; the second is the so-called elimination property, also known as being strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite (see Definition
2.6
). The former property allows proving identities in an algorithmic way; an identity
A=B
is true if
A
and
B
satisfy the same set of equations and if
A=B
for a certain set of finite values. The latter property is a crucial step in proving that many algorithms terminate (for example, the creative telescoping algorithm of
[
17
]
). A sequence that satisfies a recurrence relation in each variable will not, in general, satisfy these two properties.

The main result of this paper is the following theorem:

Theorem
2.7
.

A sequence
f:\mathbb{Z}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(q)
is
q
-holonomic if and only if it is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite.

This bridges the aforementioned gap in sophistication; it means that a sequence is
q
-holonomic if and only if it satisfies a set of recurrence relations with a specific property. This allows us to prove several closure properties for
q
-holonomic sequences, answering conjectures from
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
. Furthermore it allows us to prove that for any framed link in a closed
3
-manifold, its colored Jones sequence (and thus its Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant) is
q
-holonomic.

In Section
2
we will first give all the necessary definitions and background on
q
-holonomic sequences, then we will prove our main Theorem
2.7
, and then (in Subsection
2.2
) we will briefly go over the holonomic case. In Section
3
we will give several applications of Theorem
2.7
. First, in Subsection
3.1
we will prove several closure properties of
q
-holonomic sequences; finally, in Subsection
3.2
we will give an application to quantum invariants and skein modules.

Strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite sequences and
q
-holonomicity

Notation:
Throughout this paper, we use boldface letters to indicate tuples; given a
2r
-tuple
\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{2r})
, we use
L^{\eta}M^{\eta}
to denote the monomial
L_{1}^{\eta_{1}}\cdots L_{r}^{\eta_{r}}M_{1}^{\eta_{r+1}}\cdots M_{r}^{\eta_{2r}}
, and with
\lvert\eta\rvert
the sum of the absolute values of all the entries of
\bm{\eta}
.

Furthermore recall that a sequence of positive numbers
f_{N}
is in
O(N^{k})
if there exists a
C
such that
f_{N}\leq CN^{k}
for all
N
bigger than some
N_{0}
, and is in
\Theta(N^{k})
if there are
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}
such that
C_{1}N^{k}\leq f_{N}\leq C_{2}N^{k}
for all
N
bigger than some
N_{0}
.

Finally, throughout the paper we follow the definitions and notations of
[
5
]
, which we point to for any further reading on
q
-holonomic sequences.

2.1
Equivalence between
q
-holonomicity and strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finiteness

Definition 2.1
.

The
quantum Weyl algebra with
2r
generators
\mathbb{W}_{r}
is obtained by quotienting the non-commutative
\mathbb{C}(q)
-algebra

\mathbb{C}(q)[M_{1}^{\pm 1},L_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,M_{r}^{\pm 1},L_{r}^{\pm 1}]

by the ideal generated by

\displaystyle L_{i}M_{j}=q^{\delta_{ij}}M_{j}L_{i}

\displaystyle M_{i}M_{j}=M_{j}M_{i}

\displaystyle L_{i}L_{j}=L_{j}L_{i}.

.

The
positive quantum Weyl algebra with
2r
generators
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
is obtained by quotienting the non-commutative
\mathbb{C}(q)
-algebra
\mathbb{C}(q)[L_{1},M_{1},\dots,L_{k},M_{k}]
by the same relations.

Both these algebras have a filtration given by degree, which we call
\mathcal{F}_{N}
:

\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r}:=\textrm{span}\langle L^{\bm{\alpha}}M^{\bm{\beta}}\textrm{ with }\lvert\alpha\rvert+\lvert\beta\rvert\leq N\rangle

and analogously for
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
.

Definition 2.2
.

If
V
is a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space, we denote with
S_{r}(V)
the space of maps
\mathbb{Z}^{r}\rightarrow V
, and with
S_{r,+}(V)
the space of maps
\mathbb{N}^{r}\rightarrow V
.

We will think of elements of
S_{r}(V)
as sequences, and will write
f_{\bm{n}}
to mean
f(\bm{n})
.

It is standard to check that
S_{r}(V)
is a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module and
S_{r,+}(V)
is a
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-module, with the action of
L_{i}
and
M_{i}
defined by

\displaystyle L_{i}(f)_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}:=f_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{i}+1,\dots,n_{r})}

\displaystyle M_{i}(f)_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})}:=q^{n_{i}}f_{(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module. By
[
14
, Proposition 1.5.2]
, the dimension of
\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r}\cdot J
is eventually equal to a polynomial; the degree of this polynomial is called the
dimension
of
M
, denoted by
d(M)
. It was proven in
[
14
, Theorem 2.1.1]
that if
d(M)<r
, then
M
is the trivial module. The same definition of dimension can be given for
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-modules.

Definition 2.4
.

A
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-module
M
is
q
-holonomic
if
M
is finitely generated, contains no monomial torsion, and
d(M)\leq r
.

Similarly, a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module
M
is
q
-holonomic
if
M
is finitely generated and
d(M)\leq r
.

An element
f
in a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
or
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-module
M
is
q
-holonomic
if it spans a
q
-holonomic module. If
f\in S_{r}(V)
or
f\in S_{r,+}(V)
, we say
f
is a
q
-holonomic sequence.

Notice that monomials in
\mathbb{W}_{r}
act invertibly so a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module cannot have monomial torsion.

Notice furthermore that any
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module is also a
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-module; in this case the following proposition states that the two concepts of
q
-holonomicity are equivalent.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
5
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
M
be a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module and
f\in M
. Then
f
is
q
-holonomic over
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
if and only if it is
q
-holonomic over
\mathbb{W}_{r}
.

The definition of
q
-holonomicity of a sequence
f\in S_{r}(V)
might seem slightly obscure; in practice it means that
f
satisfies as many recurrence relations as possible without being trivial.

The following definition provides a possible way to make precise the concept of ”satisfying as many recurrence relations as possible”.

Definition 2.6
.

An element
f
in a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module
M
is
strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite
if, for any subset
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1},\dots,M_{r}\}
of cardinality exactly
r+1
, there exists a recurrence relation for
f
in the variables contained in
\mathcal{L}
; in other words, there is an element
P\in\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}}
such that
P\cdot f=0
.

An element
f
in a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module
M
is
strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite with multiplicities
if, for any
2r
-tuple
\bm{\eta}:=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r},\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{2r}
with support of size exactly
r+1
, there is an element
P\in\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}^{\eta}}
such that
P\cdot f=0
, with
\mathcal{L}^{\eta}:=\{L_{1}^{\alpha_{1}},\dots,L_{r}^{\alpha_{r}},M_{1}^{\beta_{1}},\dots,M_{r}^{\beta_{r}}\}
.

We could also introduce strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-finite elements, with or without multiplicities, but we omit it for brevity. It is clear though that
f
in a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite if and only if it is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
-finite (again, with or without multiplicities); we only need to multiply by a large enough monomial to make every exponent non-negative.

The nomenclature of strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite is from
[
5
]
, while the same notion is called satisfying the elimination property in
[
16
]
; the definition of strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite with multiplicities is to our knowledge new, and is introduced mostly to prove the closure properties of Proposition
3.1
and
3.2
. The fact that if
f
is
q
-holonomic it is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite can be found in
[
5
, Theorem 7.2]
(see also Corollary
2.9
for a proof), but it turns out that the converse is also true.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
f_{\mathbf{n}}\in M
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite, it is
q
-holonomic.

Proof.

For simplicity we prove that
f_{\mathbf{n}}
is
q
-holonomic over
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
; this is enough by Proposition
2.5
.
First we fix an ordering
>
on the variables
L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1},\dots,M_{r}
; we assume
L_{i}>M_{j}
for all
i,j
,
L_{i}>L_{j}
if
i<j
and
M_{i}>M_{j}
if
i<j
, but any order will do. We extend this to a monomial ordering first by total degree, and then lexicographically. Second, we say that an
n
-tuple
\eta_{1}

dominates
another
\eta_{2}
, denoted by
\eta_{1}\succeq\eta_{2}
, if each component of
\eta_{1}
is larger or equal than the corresponding component of
\eta_{2}
; we write
\eta_{1}\nsucceq\eta_{2}
if this does not happen.

Consider the filtration
\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r,+}\cdot f
and its generating set
\{L^{\eta}M^{\eta}\cdot f,|\eta|\leq N\}
. Given a subset
I\subseteq\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1},\dots,M_{r}\}
and a tuple
\mathbf{k}=(k_{i})_{i\in I}
, denote with
\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k}}
the subalgebra of
\mathbb{W}_{r,+}
generated by
\{L^{\eta}M^{\eta},|\eta|\leq N,\eta_{i}<k_{i}\textrm{ for all }i\in I\}
; it is clear that
\dim(\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k}})=\Theta(N^{2r-\lvert I\rvert})
, where
\lvert I\rvert
is the cardinality of
I
.

The statement of the theorem will follow at once from the following lemma:

Lemma 2.8
.

Suppose
f_{\mathbf{n}}
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite,
I\subseteq\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1},\dots,M_{r}\}
has cardinality
i<r
and
(k_{i})_{i\in I}
is any tuple of natural numbers. Then, there exists
I_{1},\dots,I_{r+1}
each of cardinality
i+1
and each containing
I
, and tuples
\mathbf{k^{j}}:=(k_{i}^{j})_{i^{j}\in I_{j}}
of natural numbers such that
k_{i}^{j}=k_{i}
if
i\in I
, such that, if
N
is large enough,

\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k}}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}\subseteq\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k^{j}}}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}

(note that the sum is intended as sets).

Proof.

Consider
J\subseteq\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1}\dots,M_{r}\}\setminus I
of cardinality
r+1
; this exists by assumption on the size of
I
. Because
f_{\mathbf{n}}
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite, there must be
P\in\mathbb{W}_{r,J}
such that
P\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}=0
; we can (up to multiplying by a monomial) assume that all the degrees are non-negative. Call
X
the largest monomial in
P
and
\bm{\xi}
its multidegree; rescale
P
so that the coefficient of
X
is
1
. We claim that
\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k}}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}
is spanned by

\{L^{\mathbf{\eta}}M^{\mathbf{\eta}}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}},|\eta|\leq N,\eta_{i}<k_{i}\textrm{ for all }i\in I\textrm{ and }\bm{\eta}\nsucceq\bm{\xi}\}.

(1)

Indeed, suppose
Y
is a monomial in
\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k}}
such that
Y\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}
is not contained in the span of the elements (
1
), and call
\bm{\eta}
its multidegree. We can assume that
Y
is minimal among monomials with this property. Clearly
\bm{\eta}\succeq\bm{\xi}
, otherwise it would be among the generators of the space. Therefore,
Y=\lambda ZX
, where
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}(q)
and
Z
is some other monomial, which implies that
Y\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}=\lambda Z(X-P)\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}
and all the monomials in
Z(X-P)
must be smaller than
Y
(and thus belong to (
1
)).

Now, the sets
I_{j}
are obtained by adding to
I
each element of
J
, and the associated tuple
\mathbf{k}^{j}
is obtained by adding the degree of
Y
in the variable
j
. It is then clear that the span of (
1
) is contained in
\sum_{j=1}^{r+1}\mathcal{F}_{N,\mathbf{k^{j}}}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}.

∎

Given Lemma
2.8
, it is clear that if
f_{\mathbf{n}}
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite,
\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r,+}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}
is generated by a union of polynomially many (in
r
) sets, each of cardinality
O(N^{r})
, and thus
f_{\mathbf{n}}
is
q
-holonomic.
∎

The following corollary provides the equivalence among the three notions of finiteness considered in this paper.

Corollary 2.9
.

The following are equivalent:

1.

f_{\mathbf{n}}
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite;

2.

f_{\mathbf{n}}
is
q
-holonomic;

3.

f_{\mathbf{n}}
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite with multiplicities.

Proof.

The fact that (
3
) implies (
1
) is obvious from the definition.

The fact that (
2
) implies
3
is very similar to the proof of the fact that (
2
) implies (
1
) which appears in
[
5
, Theorem 7.2]
. Let
\bm{\eta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2r}
with support of size
r+1
; we first notice that
d(\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}^{\eta}})=r+1
, or in other words,
\dim(\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}^{\eta}})=\Theta(N^{r+1})
. By the
q
-holonomicity assumption,
\dim(\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r}\cdot f)=O(N^{r})
, which means that for
N
big enough, there must be a non-zero
P\in\mathcal{F}_{N}\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}^{\eta}}
such that
P\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}}=0
.

Finally, the fact that (
1
) implies (
2
) is the content of Theorem
2.7
.

∎

2.2
Strongly
A_{r}
-finite sequences and holonomicity

In this subsection we repeat the previous constructions, definitions and proofs for the classical case of holonomic sequences, introduced in
[
16
]
. For simplicity we only look at sequences indexed by natural numbers, rather than integers, with the understanding that everything carries over without any problem to the
\mathbb{Z}
case. We will mainly use these results to prove Proposition
3.4
, stating that evaluations of
q
-holonomic sequences at roots of unity give holonomic sequences.

Definition 2.10
.

The Weyl algebra
A_{r}
is the free commutative algebra generated by
l_{1},\dots,l_{r},m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
with the relations
l_{i}m_{j}-m_{j}l_{i}=\delta_{ij}
,
m_{i}m_{j}-m_{j}m_{i}=0
and
l_{i}l_{j}-l_{j}l_{i}=0
. The set of sequences
S_{r,+}(\mathbb{C}):=\{\mathbb{N}^{r}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\}
is an
A_{r}
-module, with action given by
(m_{j}\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}=n_{j}f_{\mathbf{n}}
and by
(l_{i}\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}=f_{\mathbf{n}+\delta_{i}}
.

We can define a filtration
\mathcal{F}_{N}
on
A_{r}
exactly as before by taking polynomials in the generators of total degree less than or equal to
N
.

Definition 2.11
.

We say that an
A_{r}
-module
M
is
holonomic
if
M
is finitely generated as an
A_{r}
-module and if
dim(\mathcal{F}_{N}A_{r}\cdot J)=O(N^{r})
for a finite generating set
J
of
M
. A sequence
f
is holonomic if it spans a holonomic cyclic module in
S_{r}(\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 2.12
.

An element
f
in a
A_{r}
-module
M
is
strongly
A_{r}
-finite
(or
satisfies the elimination property
) if, for any subset
\mathcal{L}\subseteq\{l_{1},\dots,l_{r},m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\}
of cardinality exactly
r+1
, there exists a recurrence relation for
f
in the variables contained in
\mathcal{L}
; in other words, there is an element
P\in A_{r}
only involving the variables in
\mathcal{L}
such that
P\cdot f=0
.

The proof of the following theorem is exactly the same as the proof of Theorem
2.7
and Corollary
2.9
.

Theorem 2.13
.

A sequence
f\in S_{r,+}(\mathbb{C})
is strongly
A_{r}
-finite if and only if it is holonomic.

The fact that a holonomic sequence is strongly
A_{r}
-finite is well known; it was first proved in
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
in the differential case (the discrete case follows at once, see for example
[
2
, Proposition 26]
). The converse is, to the best of our knowledge, a new result.

3
Applications

In this section we provide some applications for Theorem
2.7
. The first set of applications will be to prove some closure properties for
q
-holonomic sequences; the proofs will essentially be straightforward generalizations of the analogous proofs for sequences in one variable, made possible by Theorem
2.7
. The second application will be proving that certain sequences of elements in skein modules are
q
-holonomic.

3.1
Closure properties for strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite sequences

The univariate case of the following two closure properties appears in
[
3
]
as Theorems 1 and 3; the multivariate case follows by similar reasoning after applying Theorem
2.7
. This answers Conjecture 5 in the same paper.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose
f(q)
is a
q
-holonomic sequence of rational functions in
q
, and
\omega
is a root of unity; then
f_{\omega}:=f(\omega q)
is also
q
-holonomic.

Proof.

Suppose
\omega
is a
p
-th root of unity; then
M_{i}^{p}f_{\omega}=(M_{i}^{p}f)(\omega q)
for all
i
and, trivially,
L_{i}f_{\omega}=(L_{i}f)(\omega q)
, therefore if
P\cdot f=0
and all
M_{i}
powers in
P
are divisible by
p
,
P(\omega q)\cdot f_{\omega}=0
.

Given any subset
\mathcal{L}
of
\{L_{1},\dots,L_{r},M_{1},\dots,M_{r}\}
we need to find a recurrence relation for
f_{\omega}
; since
f
is
q
-holonomic, by Corollary
2.9
there is a
P\in\mathbb{W}_{r,\mathcal{L}}
such that all powers of
M_{i}
in
P
are divisible by
p
; this is the desired recursion.

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
f(q)
is
q
-holonomic and
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
; then
f(q^{\alpha})
is also
q
-holonomic.

Note that the sequence
f(q^{\alpha})
belongs to
S_{r}(\mathbb{Q}(q^{\alpha}))
, which is itself a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module.

Proof.

Let
k
be the denominator of
\alpha
in reduced form and let
\mathcal{L}
be a subset of the variables of cardinality
r+1
. If there is a
P
in the variables
\mathcal{L}
annihilating
f
that is a polynomial in the variables
q^{k}
and
M_{i}^{k}
for every
M_{i}\in\mathcal{L}
, then
P(q^{\alpha})
annihilates
f(q^{\alpha})
and therefore
f(q^{\alpha})
is
q
-holonomic.

To show this, we look at the filtration of
\mathbb{W}_{r}
given by
\textrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}(q^{k})}\langle L^{\alpha}M^{k\beta}\rangle
, with the understanding that any variable not in
\mathcal{L}
will have null exponent. The dimension of this grows like
O(N^{r+1})
. Furthermore the dimension of
\mathcal{F}_{N}f
grows like
O(N^{r})
also as a module over
\mathbb{C}(q^{k})
, since
\mathbb{C}(q)
is an extension of
\mathbb{C}(q^{k})
of finite degree equal to
k
. Then just as in the proof of Corollary
2.9
we can find a recurrence relation for
f
with the required characteristics.

∎

We denote by
D_{q}
the operator on
S_{r}(\mathbb{C}(q))
acting by derivative in
q
; in other words,
(D_{q}\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}:=\frac{d}{dq}f_{\mathbf{n}}
.

The following two closure properties in the univariate case appear in
[
6
]
as Theorem 1.4 and 1.5, and in the subsequent remark they are conjectured to hold in general. Once again, the proof of the multivariate case follows a similar reasoning as the univariate case after applying Theorem
2.7

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
f
is
q
-holonomic; then
D_{q}f
is also
q
-holonomic.

Proof.

Consider any monomial
q^{k}L^{\mathbf{\alpha}}M^{\mathbf{\beta}}
. A straightforward calculation shows that
D_{q}\cdot\left(q^{k}L^{\mathbf{\alpha}}M^{\mathbf{\beta}}\cdot f\right)_{\mathbf{n}}=D_{q}\cdot q^{k}q^{\mathbf{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}}f_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{\alpha}}
is equal to

q^{k}q^{\mathbf{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}}D_{q}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{\alpha}}+\left(k+\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}\beta_{j}\right)q^{k+\mathbf{\beta}\cdot\mathbf{n}-1}f_{\mathbf{n}}

which is in turn equal to

\left(q^{k}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}\cdot D_{q}f\right)_{\mathbf{n}}+\left(k+\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}\beta_{j}\right)q^{k-1}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}f_{\mathbf{n}}

.

Therefore, given a subset
I\subseteq\mathcal{L}
and a recurrence relation
P
in those variables, we have that
0=D_{q}(P\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}=(P\cdot D_{q}f)_{\mathbf{n}}+R
where
R
is a linear combination of sequences of the form
\left(k+\sum_{j=1}^{r}n_{j}\beta_{j}\right)q^{k-1}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}f_{\mathbf{n}}
. Standard closure properties for
q
-holonomic sequences imply that each of these sequences is
q
-holonomic, therefore there must be a recurrence relation for
R
in the variables
I
. Multiplying this recurrence relation by
P
on the right provides a recurrence relation for
D_{q}f
in the variables
I
, proving that
D_{q}f
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite and hence
q
-holonomic.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose
f
is
q
-holonomic and
\omega
is a root of unity; then
f(\omega)
is a holonomic sequence.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
we only need to prove this for
\omega=1
. We can assume that
f(1)_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0
for arbitrarily large
\mathbf{n}
, otherwise the statement is trivially true (since a sequence with finite support is always holonomic).

Fix a subset of the variables
I
and
P\in\mathbb{W}_{r,I}
such that
P\cdot f=0
. If
P
becomes a non-zero polynomial in the
L
variables after substituting
q=1
and
M_{i}=1
for all
i
’s, then
f(1)
satisfies a recurrence relation in the variables
I
(actually, just the
L
variables among those) and we have found our desired recurrence.

If not, we can take the (trivial) sequence
P\cdot f
and apply
D_{q}
to it. We saw in the proof of Proposition
3.3
that

(D_{q}\cdot q^{k}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}f)_{\mathbf{n}}=(q^{k}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}\cdot D_{q}f)_{\mathbf{n}}+\left(k+\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\beta_{j}\right)q^{k-1}L^{\alpha}M^{\beta}f_{\mathbf{n}}

where
m_{j}
is the operator in
A_{r}
of Definition
2.10
.
Therefore
0=D_{q}(P\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}=(P\cdot D_{q}f)_{\mathbf{n}}+(R\cdot f)_{\mathbf{n}}
where
R
is an operator in the variables
I
plus an extra
m_{j}
for each
M_{j}
in
I
. We can once again evaluate this sequence at
q=1,M_{j}=1
; the first summand is equal to
0
by assumption, so if
R\neq 0
when evaluated at
q=1,M_{j}=1
, we have a recurrence for
f(1)
in the same variables
I
with
m_{j}
replacing
M_{j}
for all
j
. If not, we can keep repeating this process by taking
q
-differentials; at some point we must obtain a non-zero recurrence relation.

To see this is the case, fix an
\alpha
among the
L
-degrees of
P
and consider the polynomial
P_{\alpha}
of its coefficients in
q
and
M
. Pick an
\mathbf{n}
such that
f(1)_{\mathbf{n}}\neq 0
and look at the polynomial in
q
given by
P_{\alpha}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n-\alpha}}
: it must vanish in
1
to order
s
, or in other words, it is equal to
\lambda_{\alpha}(q-1)^{s}+O((q-1)^{s+1})
. Then
D_{q}^{s}P_{\alpha}\cdot f_{\mathbf{n-\alpha}}
must be non-zero when evaluated in
1
. This shows that in the above formula, after
s
steps we must obtain a recurrence relation with at least one non-zero term.

∎

3.2
Application to skein modules

Throughout this section when we write
A
we assume that
q=A^{2}
; therefore any
\mathbb{C}(A)
-module is also a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-module (and the same holds for
\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
).

Skein modules were introduced independently in
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
; for their definition and basic properties see for example
[
12
]
. We will also look at Reshetikhin-Turaev invariants
[
13
]
; for an introduction and definitions we refer the reader to
[
10
]
.

Let
N
be a closed oriented
3
-manifold and let
Sk(N)
be its Kauffman bracket skein module with
\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
coefficients. Let
S_{r}(Sk(N))
be the module of sequences
\mathbb{Z}^{r}\rightarrow Sk(N)
, and let
S_{r}^{sk}(N)
be
S_{r}(Sk(N))\otimes\mathbb{C}(A)
, where the tensor product is of
\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}]
-modules. Then
S_{r}^{sk}(N)
is a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module, with
q
acting like multiplication by
A^{2}
.

Given a framed knot
K\subseteq N
and a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Z}[A,A^{-1}][x]
, we can define the element
P(K)\in Sk(N)
first by defining
K^{n}
as
n
parallel copies of
K
, and the rest linearly. Similarly given a framed link with
r
ordered components
K\subseteq N
and
r
polynomials
\mathbb{P}:=(P_{1},\dots,P_{r})
, we can define the element
P(K)\in Sk(N)
by doing this process on each component and expanding multilinearly.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
N
be a compact oriented
3
-manifold and
K\subseteq N
be a framed link with
r
components. Given an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
we can define the
colored Jones skein
of
K
, denoted with
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}
, as
(-1)^{n_{1}+\dots+n_{r}-r}T(K)
where
T
is the
r
-tuple
(T_{n_{1}-1},\dots,T_{n_{r}-1})
, and
T_{i}
is the
i
-th Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind, defined by the recurrence relations

\begin{cases}T_{n}(x)=xT_{n-1}(x)-T_{n-2}(x)\\
T_{1}(x)=x\\
T_{0}(x)=1\end{cases}

Let
\mathcal{U}
be the set of roots of unity, and
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
the algebra of (set) functions from
\mathcal{U}
to
\mathbb{C}
. The set of sequences in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
is not a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space (and thus not a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module), but merely a
\mathbb{C}[q,q^{-1}]
module, with
q
acting by the identity map. Notice that the identity map
\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is not the identity of the algebra
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
, which is the map sending every root of unity to
1
. We can turn
S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
into a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space and a
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-module
\widehat{S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})}
by tensoring it with
\mathbb{C}(q)
.

We could also have proceeded by turning
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
into a
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space directly, by tensoring it in the same manner. We denote this by
\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}}
. The difference between the two objects is that a sequence
f_{\mathbf{n}}
in
\widehat{S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})}
is trivial if and only if there is a finite set in
\mathcal{U}
such that for every
\mathbf{n}
,
f_{\mathbf{n}}=0
outside of this set, whereas a sequence in
S_{r}(\widehat{C^{\mathcal{U}}})
is trivial if and only if for every
\mathbf{n}
,
f_{\mathbf{n}}=0
outside a finite set.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
K\subseteq N
be a framed,
r
-component link. Then the sequence
RT(M,L,\mathbf{n})\in\widehat{S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})}
is defined as

f_{\mathbf{n}}^{M,L}(\zeta):=(-1)^{|\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1}|}RT_{r}(M,L,\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1},\zeta)

where
RT_{r}(M,L,\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1},\zeta)
is the relative Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant of the pair
(M,L)
with colors
\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1}
(component-wise) at the
r
-th root of unity
\zeta
. This definition works if all components of
\mathbf{n}
are positive; it can be extended oddly in the general case.

Remark 3.7
.

The sign and the shift by
1
in the indices defining
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}
is done purely to have the notation agree with the notation usually employed in the statement of the AJ conjecture.

Theorem 3.8
.

For any closed manifold
N
and any
r
-component framed link
K\subseteq N
, the sequence
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}\in S_{r}^{sk}(N)
is
q
-holonomic.

Proof.

Take
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}
the meridians and
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
the longitudes (i.e. the parallel copies of each component) of
K
. These are generators for the algebra
Sk(\partial N_{K})
where
N_{K}
is the exterior of
K
. The skein module
Sk(N)
is a
Sk(\partial N)
-module, and there is a bilinear map
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:Sk(\sqcup_{r}S^{1}\times D^{2})\times Sk(N_{K})\rightarrow Sk(N)
, given by gluing and inclusion. In this context,
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}=\langle J_{\mathbf{n}}^{c},\varnothing\rangle
where
c
is the link in
\sqcup_{r}S^{1}\times D^{2}
comprising of all the core curves. Some standard calculations give that

\langle J_{\mathbf{n}}^{c}\cdot\mu_{i},\varnothing\rangle=(-A^{2n_{i}}-A^{-2n_{i}})\langle J_{\mathbf{n}}^{c},\varnothing\rangle

and

\langle J_{\mathbf{n}}^{c}\cdot\lambda_{i},\varnothing\rangle=\langle-J_{\mathbf{n}+\delta_{i}}^{c}-J_{\mathbf{n}-\delta_{i}}^{c},\varnothing\rangle.

Notice that in standard references (for example in Section 9 of
[
8
]
) these formulas are proved with positive colors, but the extension to negative colors is straightforward since
T_{-n}=-T_{n}
. Because of
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
, for any choice of
r+1
curves among the
\lambda_{i}
s and the
\mu_{j}
s, there is an ordered polynomial
P
in those curves that is non-zero in
Sk(\partial N_{K})
but such that
P\cdot\varnothing=0
in
Sk(N_{K})
. Then this gives a recurrence relation for
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{L}
in the variables corresponding to the curves, proving that the sequence is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite and thus
q
-holonomic by Theorem
2.7
.

∎

Theorem
3.8
in particular provides a new proof that the colored Jones polynomial of a link in
S^{3}
is
q
-holonomic, first proved in
[
4
]
(this new proof in the case for knots was already implicit in
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
). An extension of the AJ conjecture to any manifold will be explored in future work.

Corollary 3.9
.

For any framed link
K
in a closed oriented
3
-manifold
N
, the sequence
RT(N,K,\mathbf{n})
is
q
-holonomic.

Proof.

The Gilmer-Masbaum moment map associates to any skein in
Sk(N)
the function in
\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}
sending a root of unity to the Reshetikhin-Turaev invariant of the skein evaluated at that root of unity. Applying the map to each value in a sequence induces a map of
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-modules
S_{r}^{sk}(N)\rightarrow\widehat{S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})}
. This immediately proves that
RT(N,K,\mathbf{n})
is strongly
\mathbb{W}_{r}
-finite, since any non-zero recurrence in
S_{r}^{sk}(N)
is sent to a non-zero recurrence in
S_{r}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}})
in the same variables (alternatively, the image of a
q
-holonomic module is
q
-holonomic).
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Theorem
3.8
works just as well when considering
J_{\mathbf{n}}^{K}
as an element of
S_{r}(Sk(N,\mathbb{Q}(A)))
instead; in this case Corollary
3.9
says that
RT(N,K,\mathbf{n})
is
q
-holonomic as a sequence in
S_{r}(\widehat{\mathbb{C}^{\mathcal{U}}})
.

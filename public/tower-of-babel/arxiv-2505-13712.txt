A new scale anomaly in Dirac matter
Matteo Baggioli, Maxim N. Chernodub, Karl Landsteiner, Alessandro Principi, María A. H. Vozmediano

Abstract
The dynamics of Dirac semimetals are modeled at low energies by the massless Dirac Hamiltonian with the Fermi velocity replacing the velocity of light. The classical action is scale invariant. In 3D materials, Coulomb interactions induce a conformal anomaly associated to the charge renormalization already known in quantum field theory. In this work, we describe a new conformal anomaly induced by the running of the Fermi velocity that applies to Dirac semimetals in two and three dimensions. The case of graphene is particularly interesting. We analyze the anomaly and explore its thermodynamic and hydrodynamic consequences. The anomaly modifies the propagation speed of hydrodynamic sound waves, alters the thermodynamic equation of state, and induces a non-vanishing bulk viscosity proportional to the beta function of the Fermi velocity.

QFT model of graphene and renormalization of the Fermi velocity

Dirac semimetals in two and three space dimensions have Fermi points at or near a two band crossing. The linear dispersion gives rise to an effective low energy continuum Dirac Hamiltonian

{\cal H}=v_{F}\int d^{n}{\bf r}\,\bar{\psi}({\bf r})\gamma^{i}\partial_{i}\psi({\bf r})\;,

(2)

where
v_{F}
is the Fermi velocity, a parameter related to the properties of the underlying lattice, and
\gamma^{i}
an appropriate set of Dirac matrices
2
2

2

The Dirac nature of the Hamiltonian is often due to a pseudospin degree of freedom coming from the lattice and is not linked to the spin of the electrons.

S_{0}=\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{n}{\bm{r}}\,{\bar{\Psi}}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Psi,

(3)

with the Dirac matrices redefined as

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}=(\gamma^{t},v_{F}{\bm{\gamma}})\,.

(4)

Unless established otherwise, these will be our Dirac matrices from now on.

In (3+1) dimensions, Coulomb interactions are described by standard QED

S=\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{3}{\bm{r}}\,{\bar{\Psi}}i\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\Psi,

(5)

where

\nabla_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}+ieA_{\mu}.

(6)

The gauge field (photon) has a free Lagrangian

is the Maxwell electromagnetic tensor:
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}

4

From the structure of the Maxwell kinetic term, we can see that the wavevector (

The two dimensional QFT case is different. In planar QED the 2D photon field (Fourier transform of

3
Conformal anomaly from the velocity renormalization in graphene

The graphene action discussed in the previous section

S_{0}=\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\,{\bar{\Psi}}i\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\Psi,

(13)

is invariant under the global scale transformation:

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}\to\lambda{\bm{x}},\quad t\to\lambda t,\quad\Psi\to\lambda^{-1}\Psi\,.

(14)

Notice that these transformations do not depend on the Fermi velocity which is included only into the definition of the gamma functions (
4
).

The transformations (
14
) are generated by the dilatation current
[
51
]
:

\displaystyle j_{D}^{\mu}=T^{\mu\nu}x_{\nu}\,.

(15)

On the other hand, the energy-momentum tensor for the action (
13
) is:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2}{\bar{\Psi}}\left(\gamma^{\mu}\nabla^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}\nabla^{\mu}\right)\Psi

(16)

\displaystyle-i\,\eta^{\mu\nu}{\bar{\Psi}}\left(\gamma^{t}\partial_{t}+v_{F}\,{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}\right)\Psi\,,

where, in the second row, we have made explicit the Fermi velocity presence in the spacial part. The metric tensor

\displaystyle\eta^{\mu\nu}={\mathrm{diag}}\,(+1,-1,-1),

(17)

is a flat spacetime metric which does not depend on the Fermi velocity.
Here, the indices run over both time and space coordinates
\mu,\nu=0,1,2
similarly to the scale transformations (
14
).
We notice that the energy density operator is as follows:

\displaystyle\epsilon\equiv T^{00}=-v_{F}\,{\bar{\Psi}}i{\bm{\gamma}}\cdot{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi\,,

(18)

where again the Fermi velocity factor is made explicit.

The fermionic energy-momentum tensor (
16
) is given by the variation of the fermionic action
S_{0}
with respect to the background metric
g_{\mu\nu}
:

\displaystyle T^{\mu\nu}(x)=2\frac{\delta S_{0}[g]}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}(x)}\,,\qquad S_{0}[g]=\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\sqrt{g}\,{\cal L}_{\psi}(g),\quad

(19)

with
g=\det(g_{\mu\nu})
, and then calculated in the flat-space limit
g^{\mu\nu}{\to}\,\eta^{\mu\nu}
.

Classically, the dilatation current (
15
) has zero divergence because the classical equations of motion imply

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}j_{D}^{\mu}=T^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha}\,,

(20)

and the trace of the energy-momentum tensor (
16
) vanishes at the classical level,

\displaystyle T^{\alpha}_{\ \alpha}=-2{\bar{\psi}}i\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi\equiv 0,

(21)

as a consequence of the classical equation of motion of the theory (
13
),

\displaystyle\gamma^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\psi=0.

(22)

Therefore, the classical theory is invariant under the scale transformations and the dilatation current (
15
) vanishes classically:
\partial_{\mu}j_{D}^{\mu}=0
.

However, scale invariance is broken by quantum fluctuations. Therefore, the quantum expectation value of the right-hand side of Eq. (
20
) is nonzero and, consequently, we expect that on a quantum level the dilatation current (
15
) is no longer conserved. Below, we will prove this statement and find the expression for the trace of the energy-momentum tensor in the presence of a running Fermi velocity.

Here, we present a heuristic derivation, that follows a pretty standard procedure for the well-known type of trace anomaly that is associated with charge renormalization in QED.

Consider a small global scale transformation:

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu\nu}\to g_{\mu\nu}=e^{2\xi}\eta_{\mu\nu}\,,\qquad\delta g_{\mu\nu}=2\xi\eta_{\mu\nu},\qquad

(23)

where the last relation is written for the small scale factor
\xi
with
|\xi|\ll 1
.
Equation (
19
) implies:

\displaystyle S\to S_{\xi}=S+\xi\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\,T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)+O(\xi^{2})\,.\quad

(24)

Thus, the variation of the quantum action with respect to the local dilatation factor gives us the trace of the energy momentum tensor:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\psi}}{\partial\xi}={\left\langle\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\,T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\right\rangle}.

(25)

On the other hand, under the dilatation (
23
), the Fermi velocity changes as follows:

\displaystyle v_{F}\to v_{F}+\xi\beta_{v},

(26)

where
\beta_{v}
is the beta function associated with the running of the Fermi velocity (
11
).

By combining Eqs. (
24
) and (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial S_{\psi}}{\partial\xi}=\beta_{v}\int\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{2}{\bm{x}}\,{\left\langle\bar{\Psi}i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{D}}\Psi\right\rangle},

(27)

that implies – via Eq. (
25
) – the following equation for the conformal anomaly:

\displaystyle{\left\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\right\rangle}=\beta_{v}{\left\langle\bar{\Psi}i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi\right\rangle}.

(28)

This is the main result of this work.

Repeating the derivation with a coordinate-dependent factor
\xi=\xi(x)
, using a variation instead of the differential operator in Eq. (
27
), we also get a relation for a matrix element with arbitrary insertions of
T^{\mu}_{\mu}
’s:

{\left\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x_{1})\dots T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x_{n})\right\rangle}=\beta_{v}^{n}{\left\langle\bar{\Psi}(x_{1})i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi(x_{1})\dots\bar{\Psi}(x_{n})i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi(x_{n})\right\rangle}.

(29)

This formula implies the validity of the local relation:

\displaystyle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}(x)=\beta_{v}\bar{\Psi}(x)i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi(x).

(30)

We notice that this relation is valid only for the matrix elements (expectation values) and it should not be considered as an operator relation.

Exactly the same situation holds in 3+1 dimensional QED, where the trace of the energy-momentum tensor is
locally
associated with a (gauge) field with a beta-function as a pre-factor.

4
Physical consequences of the anomaly

4.1
Thermodynamics

The standard anomaly in QED, associated with the renormalization of the electric charge, reveals itself in the classical conformal-violating background field
[
52
]
. Similarly, the conformal anomaly will reveal itself at finite density and/or finite temperature, where the right-hand-side of Eq. (
28
) becomes nonzero.
The right-hand side of Eq. (
28
) is, up to a proportionality coefficient, the energy density of the Dirac gas (
18
):

\displaystyle E\equiv{\left\langle T^{00}\right\rangle}=-v_{F}{\left\langle\bar{\Psi}i{\bm{\gamma}}{\bm{\nabla}}\Psi\right\rangle}.

(31)

Therefore, the conformal anomaly equation (
28
) may also be represented in the form:

\displaystyle{\left\langle T^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\right\rangle}=-\frac{\beta_{v}}{v_{F}}E.

(32)

Taking into account that the trace of the energy-momentum tensor is related to the pressure
P
and energy
E
by,
{\left\langle(T_{\psi})^{\mu}_{\ \mu}\right\rangle}=E-2P
, we get:

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\beta_{v}}{v_{F}}\right)E=2P.

(33)

Thus, the presence of the beta function
\beta_{v}
, associated with the renormalization of the Fermi velocity, modifies the conformal equation of state
E=2P
.
As a consequence, the density of state and all the thermodynamic quantities get also modified. In particular, the electronic specific heat
of graphene, a quantity that is now accurately measured
[
53
]
will suffer a logarithmic suppression most noticeable at intermediate to high temperatures (this suppression was noticed in the early work
[
54
]
).

4.2
Speed of sound

The barotropic equation of state of a perfect fluid or a relativistic gas in special relativity reads:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial P}{\partial E}=\frac{v_{s}^{2}}{c^{2}},

(34)

where
c_{s}
is the speed of sound and
c
is the speed of light (the latter is played by the Fermi velocity in graphene,
c\leftrightarrow v_{F}
).

Due to the modification of the equation of state by the conformal anomaly (
33
), the speed of hydrodynamical sound gets modified:

v_{s}(T)=\frac{v_{F}(T)}{\sqrt{2}}{\left(1-\frac{\beta_{v}}{v_{F}(T)}\right)}^{-1/2}

(35)

where temperature
T
plays of the energy scale
\Lambda
.
The speed of sound of a free relativistic Dirac fluid in d dimensions obeys the rule
v_{s}<c/\sqrt{d}
.
The renormalization of the Fermi velocity does not lead to the breaking of the “relativistic sound barrier” in Eq. (
35
) because
\beta_{v}\leqslant 0
and

\displaystyle v_{s}(T)\leqslant\frac{v_{F}(T)}{\sqrt{2}}\,.

(36)

The sound velocity can be accurately measured by terahertz photocurrent
nanoscopy
[
55
]
.

4.3
Bulk viscosity

The bulk viscosity (or sometimes “second viscosity”)
\zeta
characterizes how a system of particles deviates from thermodynamic equilibrium in the course of uniform expansion (a nice introduction can be found in Ref.
[
56
]
). This parameter, assumed to be very small or zero in many classical fluids, has proven to be relevant in condensed matter systems
[
57
,
58
]
. A finite bulk viscosity is necessary to eliminate the well-known discrepancy between theory and observation of sound absorption in liquids at very high frequencies
[
59
]
.
In scale-invariant theories, the bulk viscosity is identically zero since, upon a uniform expansion, an equilibrium distribution function remains in equilibrium (
albeit
at an adapted temperature) if the dispersion relation exhibits scale invariance. More technically, the Ward identity associated to scale invariance implies that
\zeta
identically vanishes at any temperature and frequency (see
e.g.
appendix B in
[
60
]
or
[
61
]
).

In a rotationally invariant two-dimensional system, the shear
\eta
and the bulk
\zeta
viscosities are related to the spatial components
i,j,l,m=1,2
of the correlation function of the energy-momentum tensor
T_{ij}(x)
via Kubo’s formula
[
62
]
,

\displaystyle\eta(\omega)\left(\delta_{il}\delta_{jm}+\delta_{im}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{ij}\delta_{lm}\right)+\zeta(\omega)\delta_{ij}\delta_{lm}

(37)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega}\lim_{{\bm{k}}\to 0}\int d^{2}x\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{i(\omega t-{\bm{k}}{\bm{x}})}{\left\langle[T_{ij}(t,{\bm{x}}),T_{lm}(0,{\bm{0}})]\right\rangle},\quad

where the vanishing of the two-dimensional wavevector
\bm{k}
corresponds to the uniform limit with the frequency
\omega
kept finite.

The static limit
\omega\to 0
of the correlation function (
37
) with contracted
i,j
and
l,m
indices gives us the bulk viscosity
\zeta
expressed via the spatial trace of the energy-momentum tensor:

\displaystyle\zeta=\lim_{\omega\to 0}\frac{1}{4\omega}\int\nolimits_{0}^{\infty}dt\int d^{2}x\,e^{i\omega t}{\left\langle[T_{ii}(x),T_{jj}(0)]\right\rangle},

(38)

where we also took the limit
{\bm{k}}\to 0
explicitly, assumed the sum over the silent indices, and with
x=(t,{\bm{x}})
.
Expression (
30
) implies that the bulk viscosity is finite in the graphene fluid and proportional to the square of the beta function for the Fermi velocity.

In the relaxation time approximation, the bulk viscosity in d spacial dimensions is directly linked to the shear viscosity
\eta
as
[
63
]

\zeta=\#\eta\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{v_{s}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right)^{2},

(39)

as first recognized by Weinberg for a photon gas coupled to matter
[
64
]
(where
\#\approx 15
).
In the graphene case
c=v_{F}
.

Eq. (
39
) is reminiscent of an analogous expression derived from AdS-CFT and valid in the strong coupling limit
[
65
]
,

\zeta>2\eta\left(\frac{1}{d}-\frac{c_{s}^{2}}{c^{2}}\right).

(40)

The bulk viscosity can be measured using several experimental techniques including Brillouin spectroscopy, Laser transient grating spectroscopy, and acoustic spectroscopy
[
66
]
.

The results on the conformal anomaly induced by the renormalization of the Fermi velocity in graphene, can be easily extended to the three dimensional Dirac and Weyl semimetals

Their low energy dynamics is described by a Lorentz breaking QED(3+1) (

<T^{\mu}_{\mu}(x)>=\frac{\beta_{e}}{2e}F^{\mu\nu}(x)F_{\mu\nu}(x).

This anomaly has already been studied in the condensed matter domain and it has been shown to give rise to new transport phenomena experimentally accessible

The corrections discussed in this work are proportional to the beta function of the Fermi velocity. In graphene as well as in the 3D Dirac semimetals the beta function is proportional to the fine structure constant of the materials

It is important to notice that, due to the relation

The 3D materials are affected by multiple factors (they usually are slightly doped, have various types of disorder, other trivial bands cross the Fermi surface), but graphene can be extremely clean and its coupling constant in vacuum is already large,

The experiments associated to the scale anomaly will provide direct access to the value of

Remarks on the reinforcement of the spectrum of an elliptic problem with Robin boundary condition
Emanuele Cristoforoni, Federico Villone

Abstract
We investigate the spectral properties of a differential elliptic operator on H 1 ​ ( Ω ¯ ∪ Σ ) H^{1}(\bar{\Omega}\cup\Sigma) , where Ω \Omega is a smooth domain surrounded by a layer Σ \Sigma . The thickness of the layer is given by ε ​ h \varepsilon h , where h h is a positive function defined on the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega and ε \varepsilon is the ellipticity constant of the operator in Σ \Sigma . We prove that, in the limit for ε \varepsilon going to 0 0 , the spectrum converges to the spectrum of a differential elliptic operator in H 1 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1}(\Omega) , and we investigate a first-order asymptotic development. Keywords: Eigenvalue problem, Robin Boundary Condition, Thermal Insulation MSC 2020: 35P20, 49R05, 35J25, 80A19

converges to

in the

weak-

Remark 3.2

Notice that

with the previously defined topology is compact.

We aim to prove that all the eigenvalues

of the problem (

We now state a uniform Poincaré-type inequality in

that will be useful in the following. The proof is a consequence of the results in the optimisation of

in

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open set with

boundary and
m>0
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,m)
such that for all

and for all

\int_{\Omega}w^{2}\,dx\leq C\left[\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}\,dx+\int\dfrac{\beta}{1+\beta h}w^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right].

(3.1)

Proof.

In
[

the authors proved the existence of

Using the variational characterization of

∎

We recall that for all

we denote by

the solution to (

). Using this notation, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\{h_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{H}_{m}
such that

converges to

in
\mathcal{H}_{m}
. Then for all

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lVert v_{h_{k},f}-v_{h,f}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0.

Proof.

Using same ideas as in
section 2
, the Poincaré-like inequality
3.1
implies that there exists

such that

\lVert v_{h_{k},f}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C

Hence, there exist a subsequence
\set{h_{k_{j}}}
and
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
v_{h_{k_{j}},f}
converges to

weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
. Since

\int_{\Omega}\nabla v_{h_{k_{j}},f}\nabla\varphi\,dx+\int_{\partial\Omega}b_{h}v_{h_{k_{j}},f}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(b_{h_{k_{j}}}-b_{h})v_{h_{k_{j}},f}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx.

Since

\int_{\partial\Omega}(b_{h_{k_{j}}}-b_{h})v_{h_{k_{j}},f}\varphi\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\to 0\text{ as }k\text{ approaches }+\infty.

Taking the limit in (

∎

Remark 3.5
.

Notice that the convergence in

is actually strong in

. Indeed, we have shown that any sequence admits a subsequence

that converges to

. Moreover, using the weak equations, we have that

Hence,

converges to

strongly in

As the limit does not depend on the subsequence, we deduce that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lVert v_{h_{k},f}-v_{h,f}\rVert_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=0.

The previous remark allows us to prove the existence of a solution to optimisation problems such as maximising the average temperature, that is

4
Asymptotic development

In this section, we will prove
Theorem 1.3
, which provides an asymptotic estimate of the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}(h)
.
To simplify the notation, we will drop the dependence on
h
.

To prove the result, in particular the liminf inequality, we will follow the idea in
[
1
]
(see also
[
2
]
) "stretching" the eigenfunctions in the reference domain
\Sigma_{1}
, and prove that, in the limit, they are linear in the directions normal to the boundary of
\Omega
. Let
d
be the distance function from the boundary of
\Omega
and let

\Gamma_{t}=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\,d(x)<t}\setminus\Omega.

The regularity of
\Omega
ensures that
\Omega
satisfies a uniform exterior ball condition, and in particular, there exists
d_{0}>0
such that
d\in C^{1,1}(\Gamma_{d_{0}})
. We define the
stretching diffeomorphism

\Psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{0,1}(\Gamma_{d_{0}};\Gamma_{\varepsilon d_{0}})
as the function defined as

\begin{split}\Psi_{\varepsilon}(z)&=\sigma(z)+\varepsilon d(z)\nu_{0}(z)\\[5.0pt]
&=z+(\varepsilon-1)d(z)\nu_{0}(z).\end{split}

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and assume
f=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
, and, in the notation of
section 2
, let
v_{\varepsilon}=v_{\varepsilon,f}
and
v_{0}=v_{f}
. In
[
1
]
, they prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, open set with
C^{1,1}
boundary, and fix a positive function
h\in C^{1,1}(\Gamma_{d_{0}})
such that
h(x)=h(\sigma(x))
. The function

\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}(z)=\begin{cases}v_{\varepsilon}(z)&\text{if }z\in\Omega,\\[5.0pt]
v_{\varepsilon}(\Psi_{\varepsilon}(z))&\text{if }z\in\Sigma_{1},\end{cases}

is equibounded in
H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
and, up to a subsequence, converges weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
, as
\varepsilon
goes to
0
, to the function

\tilde{v}_{0}(z)=\begin{cases}v_{0}(z)&\text{if }z\in\Omega,\\[5.0pt]
v_{0}(\sigma(z))\left(1-\dfrac{\beta d(z)}{1+\beta h(z)}\right)&\text{if }z\in\Sigma_{1}.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The proof of the theorem can be divided into three main steps.

In the first step, one assumes that the sequence
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
to a function
\tilde{v}
. Then
\tilde{v}
, in
\Sigma_{1}
, satisfies the equation

\int_{\Sigma_{1}}(\nabla\tilde{v}\cdot\nu_{0})(\nabla\varphi\cdot\nu_{0})J_{0}(z)\,dz+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\tilde{v}(\zeta)\varphi(\zeta)\dfrac{J_{0}(\zeta)}{\sqrt{1+\lvert\nabla h(\zeta)\rvert^{2}}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\zeta)=0,

(4.2)

for every
\varphi\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{1})
such that
\varphi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, where

J_{0}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\dfrac{1}{1+h(z)k_{i}(\sigma(z))},

and
k_{i}
are the principal curvatures of
\partial\Omega
. The solution to (
4.2
) is uniquely determined by the Dirichlet boundary datum on
\partial\Omega
. Namely, for every
w\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{1})
the solution to (
4.2
) with
\tilde{v}=w
on
\partial\Omega
is

\tilde{v}(z)=w(\sigma(z))\left(1-\dfrac{\beta d(z)}{1+\beta h(z)}\right).

The second step is to prove uniform
H^{1}
and
H^{2}
density estimates of the type

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(d)\lvert\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C(\Omega,h,f,\beta)

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(d)\lvert D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C(\Omega,h,f,\beta),

where
\nabla^{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v:=\nabla v-\left(\nabla v\cdot\nu_{\varepsilon}\right)\nu_{\varepsilon}
is the tangential part of the gradient on
\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Such estimates imply that

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq\varepsilon C(\Omega,h,f,\beta).

(4.3)

By direct computation, one can then prove that

\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dz\leq\dfrac{C}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx,

\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\lvert\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nu_{0}\rvert^{2}\,dz\leq\varepsilon C\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx,

and

\int_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

which together with (
4.3
) and the
H^{1}
estimates imply

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\lvert\nabla\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{1}}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,dx\leq C(\Omega,h,f,\beta),

and, hence, the equiboundedness in
H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
.

The third and final step goes as follows. From the second step, we have that, up to a subsequence,
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
converges weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega_{1})
to a function
\tilde{v}
. On the other hand, we already know that
v_{\varepsilon}
converges to
v_{0}
weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, then, by the first step, if
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
converges to
\tilde{v}
weakly in
H^{1}(\Omega)
, then
\tilde{v}
is necessarily the
\tilde{v}_{0}
defined in (
4.1
). Finally, we notice that the limit
\tilde{v}_{0}
does not depend on the subsequence.

To apply the theorem to a sequence of eigenfunctions, however, we need to observe that the assumptions can be slightly weakened. Namely,
We have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

Proof.

Remark 4.4
.

Using the coarea formula with the distance function
d
, we have that for every
g\in L^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\,dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\set{d=t}}g(\xi)\,\chi_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\!(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\xi)\,dt.

Let

\phi_{t}\colon x\in\Gamma_{d_{0}}\mapsto x+t\nu_{0}(x)\in\Gamma_{td_{0}},

then
\set{d=t}=\phi_{t}(\partial\Omega)
, and by the area formula on surfaces (see for instance

\begin{split}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\,dx&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\sigma+t\nu_{0})\chi_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}(\sigma+t\nu_{0})J^{\tau}\phi_{t}(\sigma)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma)\,dt\end{split}

Similarly,

\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}g(\xi)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\xi)=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\sigma+\varepsilon h\nu_{0})J^{\tau}\phi_{\varepsilon h}(\sigma)\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^{2}\lvert\nabla h\rvert^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\sigma).

So that, by direct computations, we have

\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}g(x)\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon h(\sigma)}g(\sigma+t\nu_{0})\left(1+tH(\sigma)+\varepsilon^{2}R_{1}(\sigma,t,\varepsilon)\right)\,dt\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

(4.9)

and

\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}g(\sigma)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial\Omega}g(\sigma+\varepsilon h\nu_{0})\left(1+\varepsilon h(\sigma)H(\sigma)+\varepsilon^{2}R_{2}(\sigma,\varepsilon)\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

(4.10)

where the remainder terms

and

are bounded functions.

For every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we recall that we denote by

the eigenspace associated with

, and, for every

, by

Hence, we finally have that

\mathcal{F}(\varphi)=\lambda^{j},

\varphi\in E^{j}
, and we have that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\lambda^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\lambda^{j}}{\varepsilon}\leq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\dfrac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\varphi_{k})-\mathcal{F}(\varphi_{k})}{\varepsilon_{k}}=Q^{j}(\varphi)\leq\max_{v\in E^{j}}Q^{j}(v).

Finally, let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue and let
j=\min\set{i\in\mathbb{N}:\lambda^{i}=\lambda}
. By (
1.7
) we have that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{j}-\lambda^{j}}{\varepsilon}\geq\min_{v\in E^{j}}Q^{j}(v).

Let

w\in\argmin_{v\in E^{j}}Q^{j}(v)

such that
\lVert w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
,and let

V[w]=\spn\set{u^{1},\dots,u^{j-1},w}\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{V}_{\varepsilon}[w]=\spn\set{\hat{u}^{1}_{\varepsilon},\dots,\hat{u}^{j-1}_{\varepsilon},\hat{w}_{\varepsilon}}.

Then retracing the steps of the proof of (
1.7
) with
V[w]
in place of
V^{j}
, we can prove that there exists
\varphi\in V[w]\cap E^{j}
, with
\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=1
, such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\lambda^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\lambda^{j}}{\varepsilon}\leq Q^{j}(\varphi).

However, by construction, we have that

V[w]\cap E^{j}=\spn\set{w}.

Hence
\varphi=w
and

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\lambda^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\lambda^{j}}{\varepsilon}\leq\min_{v\in E^{j}}Q^{j}(v)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\lambda^{j}_{\varepsilon}-\lambda^{j}}{\varepsilon}.

∎

Let

Lemma A.2
.

Let
H
and
\mathcal{A}
be as above. Let
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and

such that
\lVert Au-\mu u\rVert=\alpha
for some
\alpha>0
, and let

.

We divide the set of eigenvalues
\{\mu_{i}\}
into two groups:

(i)

The eigenvalues
\{\mu_{s}\}
, lying in the interval
[\mu-d,\mu+d]
with corresponding eigenvectors
\{v_{s}\}
. and we denote their closed linear span by
T_{d}
.

(ii)

The eigenvalues
\{\mu_{s}\}
lying outside the interval
[\mu-d,\mu+d]
with corresponding eigenvectors
\{w_{s}\}

.

Then the space

Moreover, there exists an element

Proof of

.

We divide the proof into several steps.

We start by proving that every eigenvalue of the limit operator
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is the limit of a family of eigenvalues of the operators
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, let
\mu
be an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, and let

be an associated eigenvector with
\lVert u\rVert_{0}=1
, then for all
\varepsilon>0

subsection A.1
, we have

\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|\mu_{\varepsilon}^{j}-\mu|\leq\dfrac{{\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}({R}_{\varepsilon}u)-\mu{R}_{\varepsilon}(u)\rVert_{\varepsilon}}}{\lVert R_{\varepsilon}u\rVert_{\varepsilon}}=\dfrac{{\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}({R}_{\varepsilon}u)-{R}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{A}_{0}u)\rVert_{\varepsilon}}}{\lVert R_{\varepsilon}u\rVert_{\varepsilon}}.

By assumption
H1

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\lVert R_{\varepsilon}u\rVert_{\varepsilon}=1,

while, by assumption
H3

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}({R}_{\varepsilon}u)-{R}_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{A}_{0}u)\rVert_{\varepsilon}}=0.

Hence, there exists a family of integer numbers
\{j_{\varepsilon}\}
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\varepsilon}}=\mu.

(A.1)

Fix
\set{u^{j}_{\varepsilon}}
an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors for
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
, we now prove that, up to a subsequence, the eigenvalues
\mu^{j}_{\varepsilon}
and the associated eigenvectors
u_{\varepsilon}^{j}
converge to an eigenvalue
\bar{\mu}^{j}
of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
and to an associated normalised eigenfunction
\bar{u}^{j}
respectively. Notice that,
by assumption
H2
,

\sup_{\varepsilon}\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(H_{\varepsilon})}<+\infty,

hence the eigenvalues
\set{\mu^{j}_{\varepsilon}}
are bounded from above uniformly in
j\in\mathbb{N}
and in
\varepsilon>0
. By a diagonal argument, there exists a vanishing sequence
\varepsilon_{k}
such that for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\bar{\mu}^{j}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}=\bar{\mu}^{j}.

(A.2)

Moreover, by assumptions
H2
and
H4
, up to a further subsequence, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
w^{j}\in\mathcal{V}
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}-R_{\varepsilon_{k}}w^{j}\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}=0.

(A.3)

Moreover, for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
\bar{\mu}^{j}>0
. Indeed, assume by contradiction that there exists
j^{*}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu^{j^{*}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=0.

Notice that this implies that for all
i\geq j^{*}

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu^{i}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=0.

Necessarily,
j^{*}>1
otherwise, by (
A.1
), the spectrum of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
would be
\{0\}
. Let
h\in\mathbb{N}
be the largest natural number such that
\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{h}
does not converge to
0
. Then

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{h}=\bar{\mu}^{h}>0.

Let
0<\sigma<\bar{\mu}^{h}
be an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. By (
A.1
), there exists a family
\set{j_{\varepsilon}}
of integers such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\varepsilon}}=\sigma.

As
\sigma>0
, we have that, if
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small,
j_{\varepsilon}\leq h
. Then
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\varepsilon}}\geq\mu_{\varepsilon}^{h}
, and, in particular, we have that

\sigma=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu^{j_{\varepsilon_{k}}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\geq\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu^{h}_{\varepsilon_{k}}=\bar{\mu}^{h},

which is a contradiction. Hence, we deduce that
\bar{\mu}_{j}>0
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. We have that
\mathcal{A}_{0}w^{j}=\bar{\mu}^{j}
. Indeed, using assumption
H1
, we have that

\begin{split}\lVert\mathcal{A}_{0}w^{j}-\bar{\mu}^{j}w^{j}\rVert_{0}=&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lVert R_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{0}w^{j})-\bar{\mu}^{j}R_{\varepsilon_{k}}w^{j}\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}\\
\leq&\lim_{k\to+\infty}\bigg(\lVert R_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{0}w^{j})-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(R_{\varepsilon_{k}}w^{j})\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}+\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(R_{\varepsilon_{k}}w^{j}-\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}u^{j}_{\varepsilon_{k}})\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}\\
&+\lvert\mu_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}-\bar{\mu}^{j}\rvert\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}+\lvert\bar{\mu}_{j}\rvert\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}u^{j}_{\varepsilon_{k}}-R_{\varepsilon_{k}}w^{j}\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}\bigg).\end{split}

By assumption
H2
and
H3
, and by equations (
A.2
) and (
A.3
), all the norms in the right-hand side converge to
0
, so that
\mathcal{A}_{0}w^{j}=\bar{\mu}^{j}w^{j}
.

Moreover, using
H1
, (
A.2
) and (
A.3
), we have that, for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}

\begin{split}(w^{j},w^{i})_{0}&=\dfrac{1}{4}\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\lVert R_{\varepsilon_{k}}(w^{j}+w^{i})\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{2}-\lVert R_{\varepsilon_{k}}(w^{j}-w^{i})\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{2}\right)\\
&=\dfrac{1}{4}\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}+u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{i})\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{2}-\lVert\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j}-u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{i})\rVert^{2}\right)\\
&=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\mu^{j}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\mu^{i}_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{j},u_{\varepsilon_{k}}^{i})=\bar{\mu}^{j}\bar{\mu}^{i}\delta^{j,i},\end{split}

(A.4)

where
\delta^{j,i}
is the Kronecker delta.
Then, letting
\bar{u}_{j}=(\bar{\mu}^{j})^{-1}w^{j}
, we have that
\{\bar{u}^{j}\}
is an orthonormal sequence of eigenvectors of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
with associated eigenvalues
\set{\bar{\mu}^{j}}
, and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lVert u^{j}_{\varepsilon_{k}}-R_{\varepsilon_{k}}\bar{u}^{j}\rVert=0.

(A.5)

We now prove that
\bar{\mu}^{j}=\mu_{0}^{j}
for every

. This will then imply that

is an orthonormal basis and (
A.5
) is exactly (
2.9
). Moreover, as the limit in (
A.2
) will not depend on the subsequence, we can also deduce (
2.8
). Let
\mu
be an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. Then by (
A.1
), there exists a family
j_{\varepsilon}
of integers such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{j_{\varepsilon}}=\mu.

Let

such that
\mu>\bar{\mu}^{i}
.
For
k
big enough, then

\mu^{j_{\varepsilon_{k}}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\geq\mu^{i}_{\varepsilon_{k}}.

This ensures that

, thus, up to a subsequence, it is constant and
\mu=\bar{\mu}^{i^{*}}
for some

. That is, every eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is in the sequence
\set{\bar{\mu}^{j}}
. We then need to prove that all the eigenvalues in

are counted with the right multiplicity. That is, the sets

have the same cardinality. Let
\varepsilon_{k}
be the vanishing sequence in (
A.2

For every eigenvalues
\mu
of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, Let

be the eigenspace in

associated to

associated with an eigenvalue which converges to

.
There exists

and

such that for all

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lVert A_{\varepsilon_{k}}(R_{\varepsilon_{k}}v)-\mu R_{\varepsilon_{k}}v\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\lVert A_{\varepsilon_{k}}(R_{\varepsilon_{k}}v)-R_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\mathcal{A}_{0}v)\rVert_{\varepsilon_{k}}=0.

(A.6)

Assume by contradiction that for some eigenvalue
\mu

\mathcal{A}_{0}

and there

\varepsilon(t)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon&\text{if }t>0,\\

Theorem A.3

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(d)\lvert D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C.

The regularity of

[

holds, indeed, in the notation of

Following
[
7

13

and

\varepsilon

We recall the following lemma
[
1

Lemma A.4

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, open set with

\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\Omega,\sigma_{0})

\sigma_{0}+\varepsilon h(\sigma_{0})\nu_{0}(\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle\Omega\cap V

\displaystyle=\left\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\,\middle|\,x_{n}\leq g(x^{\prime})\right\}\cap V,

Remark A.6

We aim to bound

.
Then, using (

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open set with
C^{1,1}
boundary, fix a positive function
h\in C^{1,1}(\Gamma_{d_{0}})
such that
\varepsilon\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1,0}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
and
h(x)=h(\sigma(x))
. If
\sigma_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
w_{\varepsilon}
is as in
subsection A.2
, then there exists
C=C(\Omega,\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1,1}},C_{\mathcal{G}},\beta,\sigma_{0})
such that

\tilde{I}_{{\delta_{0}}/{2}}\leq C

Proof.

Let
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}})
be a non-negative function such that
\xi\equiv 1
in
\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}
. Choosing
|\eta|
small enough, we have

\text{supp}\xi+\eta e_{i}\subset\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}

for every
k=1,\ldots,n-1
. In the following, for any
L^{2}
function
\psi
, we denote by

\Delta_{k}^{\eta}\psi(y)=\dfrac{\psi(y+\eta e_{k})-\psi(y)}{\eta}

the difference quotients (see
[
12
, §5.8.2]
). For any couple of functions
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
we recall that

\Delta_{k}^{\eta}(\psi_{1}\psi_{2})(y)=\Delta_{k}^{\eta}\psi_{1}(y)\psi_{2}(y)+\psi_{1}(y+\eta e_{k})\Delta_{k}^{\eta}\psi_{2}(y),

(A.13)

moreover, if
\psi_{1}
and
\psi_{2}
are measurable and

(\text{supp}\psi_{1}\cap\text{supp}\psi_{2})\pm\eta e_{k}\subset\tilde{Q}_{\delta},

(A.14)

then, for every
k=1,\ldots,n-1
, it holds

\int_{\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\psi_{1}(y)\Delta_{k}^{\eta}\psi_{2}(y)\,dy=-\int_{\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\Delta_{k}^{-\eta}\psi_{1}(y)\psi_{2}(y)\,dy.

(A.15)

Finally, if
U\subset\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}
such that
U\pm\eta e_{k}\subset\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}
and
\psi\in H^{1}(\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}})
then we have

\int_{U}(\Delta^{\eta}_{k}\psi)^{2}\leq\int_{\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}|\partial_{k}\psi|^{2}.

(A.16)

Let

\varphi=-\Delta^{-\eta}_{k}(\xi^{2}\Delta^{\eta}_{k}w_{\varepsilon}).

For
\lvert\eta\rvert
small enough, we can use
\varphi
as a test function in equation (
A.11
). Let

\mathcal{I}:=\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})A_{\varepsilon}\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dy+\beta\int_{\{y_{n}=\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}w_{\varepsilon}\varphi J_{\varepsilon}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

be the left-hand side of the equation
and let

\mathcal{J}:=\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}p_{\varepsilon}\varphi\,dy

be the right-hand side.

Using standard elliptic regularity arguments (see
[
1
, Lemma 5.7]
for the details), there exists a constant
C=C(\Omega,\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1,1}},\sigma_{0})>0
such that

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}\geq\dfrac{C_{1}}{2}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\xi^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\Delta_{k}^{\eta}(\partial_{j}w_{\varepsilon})\right)^{2}\,dy-C\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}}\varepsilon(y_{n})|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dy\\
+C\beta\int_{\{y_{n}=\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\xi^{2}(\Delta_{k}^{\eta}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-C\beta\int_{\{y_{n}=\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.\end{split}

(A.17)

where
C_{1}
bounds from below the ellipticity constant of
A
.

Moreover, using Young’s inequality

ab\leq\dfrac{\gamma}{2}a^{2}+\dfrac{1}{2\gamma}b^{2}

with
\gamma=\dfrac{C_{1}\varepsilon(y_{n})}{4}
we have that

\mathcal{{J}}\leq\dfrac{C_{1}}{8}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})(\Delta^{-\eta}_{k}(\xi^{2}\Delta^{\eta}_{k}w_{\varepsilon}))^{2}dy+\dfrac{2}{C_{1}}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon(y_{n})}p_{\varepsilon}^{2}dy

Using (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we obtain that for some positive constant
C=C(\Omega,\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1,1}},C_{\mathcal{G}},\beta,\sigma_{0})

\dfrac{2}{C_{1}}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon(y_{n})}p_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,dy\leq C

(A.18)

As a consequence, applying (
A.16
) and Young’s inequality, we have that

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}&\leq C+\dfrac{C_{1}}{8}\int_{\{y_{n}\leq\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})(\partial_{k}(\xi^{2}\Delta_{k}^{\eta}w_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\,dy\\
&\leq C+\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\big[\big(2\xi(\partial_{k}\xi)\Delta^{\eta}_{k}w_{\varepsilon}\big)^{2}+\xi^{4}(\Delta^{\eta}_{k}\partial_{k}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\big]\,dy\\
&\leq C+C\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\lvert\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dy+\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\xi^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\Delta^{\eta}_{k}\partial_{j}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dy.\end{split}

(A.19)

Combining (
A.11
), (
A.17
) and (
A.19
) and using that
\xi=1
on
\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}
we have that

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}&:=\dfrac{C_{1}}{4}\int_{\{y_{n}<\epsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\Delta_{k}^{\eta}\partial_{j}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dy+C\int_{\{y_{n}=\epsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}(\Delta_{k}^{\eta}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\
&\leq C\bigg(\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}\varepsilon(y_{n})|\nabla w_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dy+\beta\int_{\{y_{n}=\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}}}w_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+1\bigg).\end{split}

(A.20)

Then, by (
A.12
), the right-hand side of the previous inequality is bounded, we have that for every
k=1,\ldots,n-1,

\partial_{k}w_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2})
and

\int_{\{y_{n}<\varepsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{k}\partial_{j}w_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dy+\int_{\{y_{n}=\epsilon\}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\lvert\nabla_{n-1}w_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C

(A.21)

We can now estimate
\partial_{nn}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}
. From (
A.11
), we have that almost everywhere

-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i,j\leq n\\
(i,j)\neq(n,n)\end{subarray}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\partial_{i}(a_{ij}\partial_{j}w_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon(y_{n})\partial_{n}a_{nn}\partial_{n}w_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon(y_{n})a_{nn}\partial^{2}_{nn}w_{\varepsilon}=p_{\varepsilon}.

From the uniform ellipticity of
A_{\varepsilon}
, we have that
a_{nn}=A_{\varepsilon}\mathbf{e}_{n}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{n}\geq C_{1}
, hence
\partial_{nn}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}
can be estimated in terms
A_{\varepsilon}
, the other derivatives of
w_{\varepsilon}
and
p_{\varepsilon}
. Namely we have

\begin{split}\int_{\set{y_{n}<\varepsilon}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\lvert\partial_{nn}^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dy\leq C\Bigg(&\int_{\set{y_{n}<\varepsilon}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}{\lvert\nabla\partial_{k}v\rvert}^{2}\,dy\\[7.0pt]
&+\int_{\set{y_{n}<\varepsilon}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\varepsilon(y_{n})\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\set{y_{n}<\varepsilon}\cap\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{0}/2}}\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon(y_{n})}p_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,dy\Bigg),\end{split}

(A.22)

for some constant
C=C(\Omega,h,\beta,\sigma_{0})
. Finally, joining (
A.21
), (
A.22
), (
A.12
), (
4.4
), and (
4.5
) we have

\tilde{I}_{\delta_{0}/2}\leq C.

∎

We can now prove the following theorem.

Proof of
Theorem A.3
.

Let
v_{\varepsilon}
be the solution to (
A.7
). Recall the definition of

I_{\delta,\sigma_{0}}=\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap Q_{\delta}}\varepsilon(d)\lvert D^{2}w_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\cap{Q}_{\delta}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}w_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

From
subsection A.2
and
subsection A.2
, we have that for every
\sigma_{0}\in\partial\Omega
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\Omega,\sigma_{0})>0
,
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\Omega,\sigma_{0})>0
and a constant
C=C(\Omega,h,\beta,\sigma_{0})
, such that

I_{\delta_{0}/2,\sigma_{0}}\leq C.

From the boundedness of
\Omega
we have that there exists a finite number
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{m}\in\partial\Omega
with associated
\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon(\Omega,\sigma_{i})
,
\delta_{i}=\delta(\Omega,\sigma_{i})
and
C_{i}=C(\Omega,h,C_{\mathcal{G}},\beta,\sigma_{i})
such that

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}\Phi^{-1}_{\sigma_{i}}(\tilde{Q}_{\delta_{i}/2})=:V_{0}

for every
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}(\Omega):=\min\set{\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\dots,\varepsilon_{m}}
and

I_{\delta_{i}/2,\sigma_{i}}\leq C_{i},

(A.23)

for every
i=1,2,\dots,m
.
Let
U
be an open set such that
U\subset\subset\Omega
and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}\subset U\cup V_{0}
. By standard elliptic regularity arguments, and by (
4.4
), there exists
C_{0}=C(\Omega,C_{\mathcal{G}})>0

\int_{U}\lvert D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq C_{0}.

Finally, summing the previous estimate to the estimates (
A.23
), we have

\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(d)\lvert D^{2}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla^{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}C_{i}=:C(\Omega,h,C_{\mathcal{G}},\beta).

A transformer based approach to STEAM integrated english course design in high schools under deep learning
Authors: Wenjuan Fan
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This study explores the integration of the educational philosophy of Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics (STEAM) with deep learning technologies for the development of high school English course resources. By introducing deep learning models, particularly the Transformer architecture, the study enables automatic generation and personalized delivery of instructional content, thereby enhancing the intelligence and relevance of course resources. An innovative Transformer-based framework incorporating STEAM-related semantic features is designed and implemented. The framework is trained on a large-scale, preprocessed English text dataset collected from 300 high school students participating in STEAM-integrated English learning tasks, with data from 150 students forming the core corpus for model training. The model employs an encoder-decoder architecture and is optimized using the Adam optimizer to support the generation of high-quality content tailored to diverse teaching objectives. Through on-site teaching experiments conducted with the 300 high school students, the results demonstrate that, the model’s performance is rigorously evaluated through metrics such as Bilingual Evaluation Understudy score (0.78), innovation index (0.75), and content diversity (0.70). Results reveal that the proposed model outperforms comparative models (Model A and Model B) in language generation accuracy, innovation, and content diversity, highlighting its superior capability in producing high-quality English teaching resources. Although the model demonstrates promising results, its implementation in resource-limited environments presents challenges, highlighting the need for further refinements. Nonetheless, these findings demonstrate the model’s potential to revolutionize English teaching by enhancing resource quality and offering a more efficient, innovative approach to course development.

Introduction

In recent years, the Science, Technology, Engineering, Arts, and Mathematics (STEAM) education concept has become one of the key directions in global educational reform1–3. However, in high school English teaching, the traditional curriculum resource development model still faces significant challenges. Most existing resources primarily focus on language knowledge and skill training, with relatively monotonous content that lacks deep integration with disciplines such as science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics. This makes it difficult for students to develop interdisciplinary thinking and comprehensive application abilities in language learning4–6. Meanwhile, although some schools have attempted to introduce project-based learning and interdisciplinary courses, practical implementation often struggles to be effectively carried out due to the lack of high-quality, dynamically generated curriculum resources7–9.

With the advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) technology, deep learning—particularly the Transformer model in the field of Natural Language Processing (NLP)—has demonstrated potential in the automated generation of educational content10–13. However, existing research primarily focuses on vocational education or STEAM courses in engineering, while the development of intelligent resources for high school English courses remains relatively underdeveloped. Most approaches still rely on predefined templates or single media formats, lacking the embedding of semantic-level STEAM features and dynamic optimization mechanisms. Consequently, the generated resources exhibit notable deficiencies in innovation, diversity, and pedagogical adaptability.

For example, Rahim et al.14 evaluated the effectiveness of project-based learning integrated with STEAM education in a metal welding course involving 64 vocational students from Negeri Padang. Compared to traditional methods, the STEAM-integrated approach significantly enhanced students’ academic progress. Similarly, Hafis et al.15 improved the quality of shielded metal arc welding instruction by incorporating interactive media videos. The use of these videos led to improved student learning outcomes, with an average score of 77.88 in the first cycle, categorized as moderate. Ernawati et al.16 investigated problem-based learning models to improve performance in fashion design courses. Their findings showed that 55% of students met minimum competency standards after the first cycle, emphasizing the importance of iterative refinement in improving cognitive outcomes. These studies highlight the effectiveness of problem-based learning and iterative improvement in enhancing student learning outcomes. Similar studies include the work of Prasetyo et al.17, who developed and evaluated the effectiveness of short instructional videos for teaching basic lathe operations in mechanical engineering. The results demonstrated that the short video tutorials significantly enhanced students’ understanding and cognitive abilities, although variations in comprehension levels were observed. Setyawan et al.18 adopted a pre-test/post-test experimental design to implement video-based learning in an educational setting. The study involved 33 students majoring in Computer and Network Engineering. Findings revealed a significant improvement in students’ knowledge after engaging in video-based instruction.

To fill the research gap mentioned above, this study proposes an intelligent resource generation framework based on Transformer architecture, aiming to achieve a deep integration of STEAM education philosophy and high school English curriculum. This framework supports the automatic generation of personalized and interdisciplinary teaching content by introducing a semantic control mechanism guided by STEAM knowledge graph. The main contributions of this study include three aspects: 1. proposing a Transformer model structure that integrates STEAM semantic features, expanding the application scope of this model in non-STEM disciplines; 2. designing a set of resource generation and evaluation processes for high school English teaching, significantly enhancing the innovation and teaching adaptability of resources; 3. Verifying the superior performance of the model in terms of language quality, interdisciplinary integration, and user satisfaction through multidimensional experiments.

To validate the effectiveness of the proposed model, this study focuses on three research questions: (1) How to integrate the STEAM educational philosophy into high school English curriculum design through deep learning methods? (2) How to build an English teaching resource generation model with scalability and educational adaptability? (3) Can this model significantly enhance students’ learning interest, creativity, and interdisciplinary application ability in practical teaching?

Based on these considerations, the following research hypotheses are proposed, and on-site teaching experiments are conducted for 300 high school students and multiple teachers. The comprehensive evaluations are conducted from multiple aspects such as learning effectiveness, teacher feedback, and resource quality.

Hypothesis 1 (H1)

The deep learning model based on the Transformer architecture can generate English teaching resources with stronger STEAM characteristics, outperforming comparative models in terms of content innovation, interdisciplinary integration, and semantic completeness.

Hypothesis 2 (H2)

Students taught using resources generated by this model will demonstrate higher levels of learning interest, knowledge acquisition, and interdisciplinary application abilities.

Hypothesis 3 (H3)

The model possesses good teaching adaptability and scalability, reflected by higher scores in teaching convenience and resource diversity in teacher feedback.

Introduction to course design

The STEAM education concept emphasizes interdisciplinary integration to cultivate students’ comprehensive abilities and innovation skills19. According to Thapaliya20, STEAM education aims to develop students’ problem-solving, critical thinking, and creative skills, preparing them to address future societal and workplace challenges. The use of deep learning in education is increasing, with Li & Kim21 reporting that deep learning technologies can generate personalized learning resources and predict learning outcomes by processing large amounts of educational data, offering new possibilities for educational reform. Al Ka’bi22 proposed a model to enhance students’ cognitive abilities, which outperformed existing algorithms in comparison.

Currently, the development of high school English course resources primarily relies on textbooks and teacher expertise. This leads to challenges such as repetitive content, limited innovation, and insufficient interdisciplinary integration23. As Zhang & Ma24 point out, traditional teaching methods often fail to address students’ holistic developmental needs. In contrast, STEAM education offers fresh perspectives and methods for English instruction. For example, Martins & Baptista25 adopted a pre-test and post-test experimental design to assess changes in participants’ content knowledge before and after professional development training. The findings indicate that the training had a positive impact on improving teachers’ content knowledge. Moreover, the study helps to define the specific content knowledge required for interdisciplinary teaching that integrates science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics—namely, STEAM pedagogical content knowledge. Additionally, Park & Cho26 explored the intersections between learning objectives and STEAM goals, providing valuable insights for future research and practical applications in integrated curricula.

Challenges remain in the development of high school English course resources27. Traditional teaching models often lack interdisciplinary integration, failing to meet the diverse developmental needs of students. Additionally, current methodologies for generating educational resources are limited by data and technological constraints, preventing the creation of personalized and interdisciplinary materials. As a result, there is a critical need to explore new approaches and technologies to address these gaps and drive innovation in high school English course development. This study aims to investigate how to effectively combine deep learning technology and interdisciplinary content to improve the quality and effectiveness of teaching resources. By analyzing students’ learning behaviors and needs, the study generates personalized teaching materials, enhancing learning efficiency and outcomes. At the same time, it explores integrating STEAM content into English instruction to promote interdisciplinary thinking and competencies, addressing existing research gaps.

Based on the literature review, a high school English course is designed using the STEAM concept. A one-week course is presented as an example to illustrate specific teaching activities and tasks. This course, guided by STEAM principles, aims to enhance students’ English proficiency and overall literacy through interdisciplinary methods. It incorporates content from multiple disciplines, fostering innovation and critical thinking through project-based and task-oriented learning approaches.

The course design follows the STEAM education concept, integrating knowledge and methodologies from various disciplines to highlight their interconnections and interactions. Project- and task-based teaching methods enable students to apply what they have learned in real-world contexts, enhancing both practicality and engagement. Project-based teaching emphasizes long-term involvement with complex tasks, often requiring interdisciplinary collaboration and culminating in tangible products or presentations. This approach encourages students to apply knowledge to real-world scenarios, fostering critical thinking and problem-solving skills. In contrast, task-based teaching involves shorter, more focused activities aimed at achieving specific learning objectives. These tasks improve language skills through authentic communication, requiring students to immediately apply new vocabulary or grammar. Both methods encourage independent thinking, innovation, and tackling complex challenges, nurturing creativity and critical thinking. Collaborative projects further promote teamwork, enhancing students’ communication and collaboration skills. Research methodology

Integration of deep learning-based STEAM education into high school english course development

This study aims to integrate deep learning technology with the STEAM education concept to create personalized, interdisciplinary high school English course resources. The STEAM education model incorporates science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics, focusing on fostering innovation, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills through interdisciplinary approaches. It emphasizes the integration of theory and practical application, encouraging students to acquire and apply knowledge in real-world contexts.

To incorporate the STEAM concept into the English course, this study developed a series of teaching activities and projects. For example, in project-based learning, students research and discuss environmental protection issues, then write English reports and present their findings using tools such as PowerPoint and video production software. During this process, students engage with scientific literature, analyze data using technological tools, and present their research in English. This project enhances students’ writing and communication skills while also developing their scientific literacy and technological proficiency. Additionally, interdisciplinary inquiry projects combine history, art, and engineering, where students design artworks or models inspired by English literary works. By exploring these literary texts, students gain historical and cultural insights, applying their engineering and artistic knowledge to create their projects. This approach fosters the integration of English learning with other academic subjects.

In Task-Based Learning, students select a science and technology topic for an English speech, utilizing multimedia tools for presentations. They engage in researching scientific materials, drafting speeches, and preparing presentation materials. This process enhances both their English speaking skills and their understanding of science and technology concepts. Another task involves integrating programming with literature. As students learn basic programming principles, they design an interactive story based on a literary work, practicing both programming and English writing skills. This activity requires students to translate the plot and characters from literature into programming code to create a basic interactive narrative. It deepens their comprehension of literary texts while honing essential programming skills.

To enable the adaptation of this course design by other educators, specific teaching strategies and methods are outlined. First, clear objectives and expected outcomes should be articulated for each project or task, providing direction for teaching activities. Second, preparing relevant resources—such as scientific literature, technical equipment, and multimedia tools—supports students in completing their tasks. Third, detailed teaching steps should be provided, including the implementation process, time allocation, and specific assignments. Teachers should offer support and feedback during instruction to help students navigate challenges and ensure project progression. Lastly, evaluation and feedback mechanisms enable educators to assess students’ learning outcomes and project results, refining the course design based on feedback to improve its effectiveness.

In addition, this study reclassifies the difficulty levels of vocabulary and grammar within the learning materials to better align with students’ varying proficiency levels. The contextual expression and situational tasks related to STEAM themes are also enhanced—for example, embedding English writing assignments within scientific experiment descriptions or engineering design projects—to increase the practicality and interdisciplinary integration of the content. From an instructional perspective, the balance between task-based learning and collaborative group activities is adjusted based on student engagement data, aiming to improve classroom participation and the depth of interaction. Furthermore, in response to feedback regarding mismatches in learning pace, modular teaching units with adjustable pacing are introduced. These units allow teachers to flexibly arrange content delivery, thereby better accommodating the diverse learning rhythms and preferences of different student groups. These teaching strategies and methods demonstrate the integration of STEAM education concepts into high school English courses, enhancing the course design’s practicality and adaptability. The methods and case studies serve as references for educators, helping them apply STEAM education principles more effectively in their teaching practice.

To enhance the iterative nature of the study and the model’s capacity for continuous improvement, an iterative optimization mechanism based on learning feedback is integrated into the model’s operational framework. Specifically, at the end of each instructional phase, student performance data—including test scores, questionnaire feedback, and behavioral analytics—are collected and fed into the model’s evaluation module. The data inform adjustments to the content generation strategy and the logic for instructional recommendation generation. For example, if a particular type of instructional resource is repeatedly flagged as “difficult to understand” or “lacking engagement,” the model will reoptimize the selection and presentation of content based on its structural tags. Additionally, a regular calibration cycle is established—set at biweekly intervals—to allow for rolling updates based on trends in model performance and student learning outcomes. This mechanism not only enhances the model’s adaptability to real-world teaching scenarios but also enables a transition from static content output to dynamic content evolution, thereby empowering the system to continually improve its cognitive effectiveness and instructional efficiency.

To further enhance the adaptability of the teaching model and the precision of its feedback mechanisms, this study incorporates a fuzzy logic evaluation system into the existing framework of observational records and qualitative assessments. This addition aims to improve the flexibility and granularity of evaluating students’ interdisciplinary learning outcomes. Traditional scoring systems often struggle with ambiguity and rigid grading scales when assessing subjective dimensions such as creativity, coherence in language expression, and the depth of interdisciplinary integration. By constructing membership functions and fuzzy rules, the fuzzy logic system allows for more progressive and flexible quantification of such indicators.

The study adopts a hybrid approach that combines expert knowledge with data-driven refinement to define rules and membership functions. Initially, teaching experts in English and STEAM collaboratively establish a rule framework based on task-oriented assessment standards. Fuzzy levels such as “insufficient,” “basic,” “proficient,” and “excellent” are defined using triangular and trapezoidal membership functions. This framework is then calibrated using empirical data from classroom observations and student project outcomes to ensure both educational relevance and practical interpretability. During implementation, the fuzzy logic system integrates student behavioral data, linguistic output quality, and teacher evaluations to provide real-time feedback for content generation and instructional strategy adjustment. This supplementary mechanism significantly improves the model’s feedback loop capabilities, offering a more feasible technical foundation for the intelligent optimization of interdisciplinary English teaching resources.

Selection of deep learning algorithms

Following feature extraction, model training involves applying deep learning algorithms to generate personalized and interdisciplinary English course resources. The selection of the deep learning algorithm is crucial. In this study, the Transformer model is chosen as the primary algorithm due to its superior performance in NLP tasks. The Transformer model excels at handling long-range dependencies and detecting intricate patterns in sequence data. A key feature of its architecture is the self-attention mechanism, which processes sequence data in parallel, distinguishing it from traditional recurrent neural network (RNN) and improving both computational efficiency and the ability to capture long-range dependencies.

The Transformer model consists of two main components: the encoder and the decoder. In this study, both components are composed of six identical stacked layers. This configuration is selected based on a balanced consideration of model expressiveness and computational efficiency. Each layer includes two core components: a multi-head self-attention mechanism and a feed-forward neural network. For the attention mechanism, the model employs eight parallel attention heads, each with a dimensionality of 64, resulting in a total model dimension of 512 (i.e., 8 × 64). This configuration enables the model to capture semantic information across different subspaces in parallel, thereby enhancing its ability to model complex contextual relationships in interdisciplinary English texts. Regarding the choice of network depth, ablation tests are conducted on architectures with 4, 6, and 8 layers. The results indicate that the 6-layer configuration performs best across key evaluation metrics. Although the 8-layer model shows marginal improvement on certain indicators, it significantly increases computational cost and exhibits a higher risk of overfitting when applied to medium-scale datasets. Therefore, the 6-layer architecture achieves a strong balance between training efficiency, model stability, contextual modeling capability, and semantic representation. Once trained, the Transformer model can generate English course resources that align with the STEAM education concept. By incorporating students’ learning needs and background information, the model creates personalized teaching materials that integrate content from science, technology, engineering, arts, and mathematics, thereby enriching and enhancing the practicality of the resources.

The Transformer model offers key advantages in this process. Its self-attention mechanism captures long-distance dependencies within sequence data, which is vital for processing textual information. Additionally, the model can process all positions within a sequence simultaneously, improving computational efficiency. Its versatility also extends to various NLP tasks, such as text generation, translation, and summarization, making it adaptable to diverse applications. Experimental process and data collection

To comprehensively evaluate the effectiveness and practical potential of the deep learning-based English instructional model integrated with the STEAM educational philosophy, this study employed a diversified data collection approach and experimental design to ensure multi-dimensional insights into the model’s performance. This study was conducted in public high schools from September to December 2023, involving 300 high school English learners and 12 English teachers to ensure sample representativeness. All participants collectively constituted the overall sample of this experiment and participated in extensive data collection activities. Among them, the classroom activity texts, learning management system data, feedback and evaluation of 150 students were systematically collected and preprocessed, forming a dedicated dataset for training the Transformer model proposed in this study.

To evaluate the effectiveness of different teaching interventions, this study employs an inter-group experimental design. Randomly select 150 students from 300 participants and evenly distribute them into three experimental groups of 50 each, receiving teaching interventions generated based on Model A, Model B, or the Transformer-based model proposed here. This design facilitates direct comparison between different teaching methods and enhances the external validity of research results, providing robust empirical support for the applicability of the model in different educational environments.

All instructional activities are conducted in English to maintain consistency between the language environment and the teaching content. The instructional design integrates materials from STEAM disciplines. For instance, in the “Future City” project, students conceptualize topics such as smart transportation and green architecture, compose creative texts in English, design visual representations, and present and discuss their ideas orally. These activities reflect a strong interdisciplinary integration. Student behavioral data are collected through real-time classroom observations and teacher records, focusing on collaboration skills, task performance, language expression, and learning engagement. Teachers also write reflective teaching notes to document strategies and student responses, serving as feedback for model optimization.

In addition, the study gathers student feedback through periodic questionnaires, group discussions, and interviews. These cover students’ understanding of STEAM tasks, changes in learning interest, and development of critical thinking and creativity. Feedback is collected from students of varying proficiency levels to ensure representativeness and diversity. Learning behavior data are also captured via a learning management system, which records online study duration, module completion rates, interaction frequency, and content depth. Particular attention is given to students’ engagement and performance in interdisciplinary projects, supporting the analysis of learning trajectories and refinement of instructional strategies.

Regarding teacher feedback, interviews and surveys are conducted regularly to collect evaluations on the model’s adaptability, resource generation efficiency, and instructional effectiveness. Teachers focus on the model’s impact on student engagement, interdisciplinary comprehension, and its integration with existing instructional frameworks. Their feedback informs adjustments to the model’s generation logic and teaching strategy refinement. All data collection strictly adheres to ethical guidelines for educational research. Approval is obtained from school administrators prior to the study, and informed consent is secured from all participants. Students and parents are clearly informed of the data usage and confidentiality measures. Throughout the study, all data are anonymized to protect participants’ privacy and ensure data security.

Innovation and uniqueness

To more effectively evaluate the originality and innovativeness of the generated instructional resources, this study adopts a hybrid metric referred to as the Innovation Index36, which combines expert evaluation with corpus-based comparative analysis. The index is calculated based on two key components: (1) the proportion of generated content that diverges from high-frequency templates in the training set, reflecting the model’s ability to break away from conventional pedagogical expressions; (2) the extent to which cross-disciplinary STEAM-related keywords or semantic features are incorporated into the content, indicating the “interdisciplinary” nature of the resources. In addition, experienced subject-matter experts are invited to score the model outputs using a five-point Likert scale across three dimensions: novelty, creativity, and instructional adaptability. The average scores are then standardized and integrated into the overall Innovation Index, providing a comprehensive assessment of content originality.

Discussion

The experimental results of this study confirm the comprehensive advantages of the proposed Transformer-based model in generating high school English STEAM teaching resources. The achievement of these results is mainly attributed to the deep integration of model architecture and teaching philosophy. The self-attention mechanism of the Transformer model enables it to effectively capture and correlate interdisciplinary semantic information, thereby generating more coherent and accurate text in both language and content. This coincides with Samala et al.39 assertion that emerging AI and deep learning technologies are becoming key forces driving educational innovation and equity. The proposed model is a concrete practice under this trend, which transforms STEAM educational concepts into dynamically optimized personalized resources through intelligent content generation technology.

The significant improvement in student engagement and learning outcomes observed in this study can be explained from the perspective of integrating teaching methods and technology. Yu et al.40 found that tools such as mobile learning technology could enhance learning engagement and outcomes by improving interactivity. On this basis, this study further explores the personalized and interdisciplinary nature of the resources generated by the model, which stimulates students’ cognitive interest, and deepens Yu et al.’s advocacy of “enhancing student participation through technological tools” by placing English learning in meaningful real-life problem contexts. Moreover, the high satisfaction level of teacher feedback, especially the recognition of the model’s ease of integration and support for interdisciplinary teaching, strongly responds to Nicolaou et al.41 viewpoint that educational technology innovation must be seamlessly integrated into existing courses and easy for teachers to use in order to maximize its effectiveness. As a content generation tool, this model has successfully lowered the threshold for teachers to implement high-quality STEAM English teaching.

In terms of interdisciplinary integration, the successful practice of this model provides new insights beyond traditional STEM integration paths. Maass et al.42 delved into the core role of mathematics in STEM education, with a focus on the connections within science and engineering disciplines. While this study expands the interdisciplinary scope to include the complete STEAM spectrum of arts and humanities, and successfully combines it with the non-traditional STEM field of language education. This indicates that semantic modeling based on deep learning can build semantic bridges between seemingly discrete disciplines, providing technical possibilities for achieving deep integration in a wider range of disciplinary fields.

However, this study still has several limitations. Firstly, the teaching experiment period is relatively short, mainly evaluating the short-term teaching effectiveness of the model. Its long-term impact on students’ interdisciplinary thinking and language abilities still needs to be verified through longer longitudinal studies. Secondly, the training and inference process of the model still requires certain computing resources, which may become an obstacle to promotion in educational environments with limited resources. In addition, current research mainly focuses on the quality of teaching resource generation and initial teaching effectiveness. There has been no in-depth exploration of the specific interactive modes of generated content in the classroom and how teachers can conduct secondary development based on generated content in deep teaching methods.

Based on the above limitations, future research can be conducted in the following directions: 1. conduct long-term studies lasting one semester or academic year to evaluate the sustained impact of the model; 2. commit to developing a lightweight version of the model to enhance its applicability in low resource environments; 3. explore in depth the teaching mode of human–machine collaboration and study the best way for teachers to collaborate with AI generated content.

Conclusion

This study successfully combines the STEAM educational philosophy with Transformer-based deep learning technology, providing an innovative solution for the intelligent development of high school English curriculum resources. The empirical research results show that this scheme can generate high-quality, innovative, and diverse teaching materials at the technical level, and effectively enhance students’ learning interest, knowledge mastery, and application ability at the teaching practice level, while gaining high recognition from teachers. The main contribution of this study lies in surpassing the limitations of traditional resource development models and achieving the organic integration of interdisciplinary content and language learning through a data-driven and semantically profound approach, providing a valuable example for empowering educational innovation with AI. Although further exploration is needed in terms of long-term effectiveness verification and universality, this study undoubtedly provides strong empirical evidence and forward-looking guidance for promoting the development of intelligent education technology and building a more inclusive and adaptive future education ecosystem.

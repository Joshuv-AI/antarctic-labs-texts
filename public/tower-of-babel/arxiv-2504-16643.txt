Multiple Rota-Baxter algebra and multiple Rota-Baxter modules
Jun He, Xiao-song Peng, Yi Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop the theory of multiple Rota-Baxter modules over multiple Rota-Baxter algebras. We introduce left, right, and bimodule structures and construct free Ω \Omega -operated modules with mixable tensor establishing free commutative multiple Rota-Baxter modules. We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for a free module to admit a free multiple Rota-Baxter module structure. Furthermore, we define projective and injective multiple Rota-Baxter modules, showing that their category has enough projective and injective objects to support derived Hom \mathrm{Hom} functors. Finally, we introduce the tensor product of multiple Rota-Baxter algebras and define flat multiple Rota-Baxter modules, proving that both free and projective modules satisfy the flatness property.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Rota-Baxter algebras and Rota-Baxter modules

The notion of a Rota-Baxter operator was originally introduced by Baxter in 1960
[
8
]
. A Rota-Baxter algebra
[
17
]
refers to an associative algebra that is endowed with a Rota-Baxter operator, which satisfies the integration-by-parts formula in analysis. Specifically, given a fixed parameter
\lambda
in a commutative ring
\mathbf{k}
, a Rota-Baxter algebra of weight
\lambda
is defined as a pair
(R,P)
, where
R
is an associative algebra over
\mathbf{k}
, and
P:R\to R
is a linear operator that satisfies the following Rota-Baxter identity:

(1)

P(x)P(y)=P(xP(y))+P(P(x)y)+\lambda P(xy),\quad\forall x,y\in R.

From the 1960s to the 1990s, this structure attracted considerable attention from both analytic and combinatorial perspectives, with notable contributions from Atkinson, Cartier, and Rota
[
17
]
. In the setting of Lie algebras, the special case of a Rota-Baxter operator with weight 0 was independently discovered by physicists in relation to the operator form of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
[
29
]
.

At the beginning of this century, the Rota-Baxter algebras, along with the Hopf algebra
[
3
]
, Lie algebras
[
16
]
, dendriform algebra
[
13
]
, pre-Lie algebras
[
1
,
2
,
5
]
and Post-Lie algebra
[
4
]
, form the fundamental algebraic frameworks underlying the Connes-Kreimer renormalization theory
[
11
]
in quantum field theory. For further research developments, refer to Guo’s monograph
[
17
]
and its references.
In recent years, the emergence of Rota-Baxter groups
[
9
,
20
]
has sparked renewed interest in the study of Rota-Baxter algebras. In particular, the discovery of new algebraic and geometric structures in mathematics and physics
[
6
,
7
,
15
,
20
,
23
,
30
]
has further advanced the research on Rota-Baxter Lie algebras and their applications in deformation theory and homotopy
[
12
,
21
,
31
]
.

Similarly to classical algebraic structures including associative algebras and Lie algebras, the investigation of modules and representations constitutes an essential aspect of Rota-Baxter algebra theory. The concept of a Rota-Baxter module was initially introduced in
[
19
]
to study the representations of Rota-Baxter algebras and their connections with the ring structure of Rota-Baxter operators. To recall its definition, a
Rota-Baxter module
is defined to be an
R
-module
M
together with a linear operator
p
on
M
such that

(2)

P(x)p(m)=p(xp(m))+p(P(x)m)+\lambda p(xm),\quad\forall x\in R,m\in M.

Based on the studies, representation theory and regular-singular decomposition of Rota-Baxter algebraic structures over Laurent series were discussed
[
24
]
. In
[
27
]
, representations of the polynomial Rota-Baxter algebras were investigated, and a classification of Rota-Baxter modules based on the indecomposable
{\bf k}[x]
-module was provided up to isomorphism. The geometric representations and derived functors of the Rota-Baxter modules from the perspective of the module category were studied in
[
26
]
. Later, the concept of Rota-Baxter paired modules was introduced in
[
35
]
, providing a natural generalization of Rota-Baxter modules while establishing their fundamental connections with Hopf algebra structures. The modules over free commutative non-unital Rota-Baxter algebra of nonzero weight were studied in
[
32
]
, and their equivalence to the modules over noncommutative algebra generated by two indeterminates with a generation relationship was established.

1.2.
Rota-Baxter algebras (modules) going multiple

The structure of multiple compatible algebraic operations first appeared in Poisson and Lie brackets, originating from Magri’s pioneering work on integrable Hamiltonian systems
[
25
]
.
Recently, a new algebraic structure involving multiple multilinear operators has emerged in the study of algebraic renormalization of regularity structures. In 2019, Bruned-Hairer-Zambotti
[
10
]
used decorated rooted trees to characterize the renormalization process of stochastic partial differential equations. Subsequently, Foissy
[
14
]
introduced the concept of matching anticipative algebras to further understand the Hopf algebraic approach developed by Bruned, Hairer, and Zambotti for stochastic differential equations.

In recent years, the concept of matching Rota-Baxter algebras was introduced in
[
34
]
, which led to investigating matching Rota-Baxter modules and their relationships with modules over other algebraic structures in
[
36
]
. Later, a generalization of Rota-Baxter algebras was proposed through the introduction of multiple Rota-Baxter algebras in
[
22
]
. Naturally, this development prompts the investigation of module structures over multiple Rota-Baxter algebras. In this paper, we focus on multiple Rota-Baxter modules and their categorical aspects.

Here is an outline of the paper.
In Section
2
, we initially introduce the concept of left multiple Rota-Baxter modules and subsequently investigate right multiple Rota-Baxter modules and multiple Rota-Baxter bimodules through their module actions. Later, we construct free
\Omega
-operated modules with mixable tensor, and establish the construction of free commutative multiple Rota-Baxter modules over multiple Rota-Baxter algebra. Furthermore, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions under which a free module can be endowed with the structure of a multiple Rota-Baxter module. In Section
3
, we define projective and injective multiple Rota-Baxter modules, and demonstrate that the category of multiple Rota-Baxter modules contains enough projective and injective objects. This enables the definition of derived functors of
\mathrm{Hom}
through projective and injective resolutions of multiple Rota-Baxter modules. In Section
4
, the tensor product is introduced over multiple Rota-Baxter algebras, providing the foundation for defining flat multiple Rota-Baxter modules. Furthermore, we show that both free multiple Rota-Baxter modules and, more generally, projective multiple Rota-Baxter modules satisfy the flatness property.

Notation.

Let
\bf k
be a commutative unital ring. All algebras (modules) discussed in this paper are assumed to be
\bf k
-algebras (
\bf k
-modules) with unity, and all morphisms are

\bf k
-linear and preserve the unity element unless stated otherwise.

&+\lambda_{\alpha}\mathfrak{q}_{\beta}(r^{\prime}m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)+\lambda_{\beta}\mathfrak{q}_{\alpha}(r^{\prime}m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)\\
=&\mathfrak{q}_{\alpha}(r^{\prime}(\mathfrak{q}_{\beta}(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)))+\mathfrak{q}_{\beta}(P^{\prime}_{\alpha}(r^{\prime})(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s))\\
&+\lambda_{\alpha}\mathfrak{q}_{\beta}(r^{\prime}m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)+\lambda_{\beta}\mathfrak{q}_{\alpha}(r^{\prime}m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s),\end{split}

as required.

(b) The verification proceeds similarly to Item (a).
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(R,P_{\Omega})
and
(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})
be multiple Rota-Baxter algebras of pair weight
(\lambda_{\Omega},\lambda_{\Omega})
. We suppose that
(M_{(R,P_{\Omega})},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega}^{R})
is a right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module,
(_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})},\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega}^{R},\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega}^{R^{\prime}})
is an
(R,P_{\Omega})
-
(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})
-bimodule and
(T_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})},\mathfrak{t}_{\Omega}^{R^{\prime}})

(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})
-module. Then

\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S,T)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M,\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(S,T)).

Proof.

For
m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s\in M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S
, we define two abelian group homomorphisms

\theta:\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S,T)\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M,\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(S,T)),\ \theta(f)(m)(s)=f(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s),

and

\theta^{\prime}:\mathrm{Hom}_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M,\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(S,T))\longrightarrow\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S,T),\ \theta^{\prime}(g)(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)=g(m)(s),

where
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S,T)
, and

.
Since

\theta\circ\theta^{\prime}(g)(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s)=\theta(g(m)(s))=g(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s),

\theta^{\prime}\circ\theta(f)(m)(s)=\theta^{\prime}(f(m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}s))=f(m)(s).

Hence
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
are mutually inverse isomorphisms, as required.
∎

The result demonstrates that
-\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}S

\mathrm{Hom}_{(R^{\prime},P_{\Omega}^{\prime})}(S,-)
are adjoint functors.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
be a flat right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module, then there exists a natural right
R
-module isomorphism
M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R\cong M
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi:(R,P_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle,\overline{P}_{\Omega})
be an injective left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module homomorphism, induced by the inclusion
R\longrightarrow R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle
. By the flatness of
(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
,
id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi:M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R\longrightarrow M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle
is also an injective abelian group homomorphism. For any pure tensor
m\otimes_{R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle}x\in M\otimes_{R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle}R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle
and
x\in{R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle}
, we have

(id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi)(mx\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}{\bf 1}_{R})=mx\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}{\bf 1}_{R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle}=m\otimes_{R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle}x.

Hence
id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi
is surjective and is consequently an abelian group isomorphism.

By Proposition
4.4
,
M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R
and
M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle
are also right
R
-modules. For
m\otimes r_{1}\in M\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}R
,
r_{2}\in R
, we have

\begin{split}(id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi)((m\otimes r_{1})r_{2})=&(id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi)(m\otimes r_{1}r_{2})=m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi(r_{1}r_{2})\\
=&m\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi(r_{1})r_{2}=((id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi)(m\otimes r_{1}))r_{2}.\end{split}

Then
id_{M}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\varphi

R

By Proposition
3.7
,
(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
and
(R_{MRB}\left\langle Q_{\Omega}\right\rangle,\overline{P}_{\Omega})
can be regard as

Let

for each

For each

, the map

.

.

. Define the map

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\{(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})|i\in I\}
be a set of left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-modules and
(S,\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega})
be a right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module. Then

S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i})\cong\oplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}).

Proof.

We define group homomorphisms

f_{1}:S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i})\longrightarrow\oplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}),\quad f_{1}(s\otimes(m_{i})_{I})=(s\otimes m_{i})_{I},

and

f_{2}:\oplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i})\longrightarrow S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i}),\quad f_{2}(s_{i}\otimes m_{i})_{I}=(\prod_{i\in I}s_{i})\otimes(m_{i})_{I}.

It follows immediately that
f_{1}\circ f_{2}=id_{\oplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i})}
and
f_{2}\circ f_{1}=id_{S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i})}
.
∎

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
\{(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})|i\in I\}
be a set of left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-modules. Then the
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat if and only if each
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat.

Proof.

Let
(S,\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega})
and
(T,\mathfrak{t}_{\Omega})
be right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-modules, and
\phi:(S,\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(T,\mathfrak{t}_{\Omega})
be a right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module monomorphism.

Assume that each left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat. Then each

\phi\otimes id_{M_{i}}:S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}\longrightarrow T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i},

is also an injective abelian group homomorphism. By Lemma
4.8
, the homomorphism

\bigoplus_{i\in I}(\phi\otimes id_{M_{i}}):\bigoplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}(T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}),

is injective. Thus the left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat.

In contrast, assume that the left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
\bigoplus_{i\in I}(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat. Then right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module monomorphism
\phi
induced the map

\phi\otimes id_{(\oplus_{i\in I}M_{i})}:S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}\right)\longrightarrow T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}\right),

is an injective group homomorphism. By Lemma
4.9
, we have

S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}),\quad T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I}(T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}).

Then the group homomorphism

\bigoplus_{i\in I}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i\in I}(T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}),

is injective. By Lemma
4.8
, the map

\phi\otimes id_{M_{i}}:S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i}\longrightarrow T\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{i},\quad\forall i\in I,

is injective. Thus each left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module
(M_{i},\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega_{i}})
is flat.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Every free left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module is flat.

Proof.

Let
(M_{R}(X)/I_{X},\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega}^{\prime})
be the free left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module generated by
X
as constructed in Theorem
2.14
. Let
(N,\mathfrak{n}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(N^{\prime},\mathfrak{n}_{\Omega}^{\prime})
be right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module homomorphism. We need to show that if
(N,\mathfrak{n}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(N^{\prime},\mathfrak{n}_{\Omega}^{\prime})
is a right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module monomorphism, then the induced map
N\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M_{R}(X)/I_{X})\longrightarrow N^{\prime}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M_{R}(X)/I_{X})
is also a abelian group monomorphism.

First, let
X=\{x\}
be a singleton and
(S,\mathfrak{s}_{\Omega})
a right
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module, define the maps

\begin{split}\varphi:S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n\geqslant 1}M_{n}(x)/I_{\left\{x\right\}}\right)&\longrightarrow S,\\

and

\begin{split}\varphi^{\prime}:S&\longrightarrow S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigcup^{\infty}_{n\geqslant 1}M_{n}(x)/I_{\left\{x\right\}}\right),\\

for
s\in S
,
r_{1}\otimes\omega_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes r_{n}\otimes x\in M_{n}(x)
.

Apparently
\varphi\circ\varphi^{\prime}=id_{M}
and
\varphi^{\prime}\circ\varphi=id_{S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\left(\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 1}R^{\otimes n}\right)x/I_{\left\{x\right\}}\right)}

\varphi,\varphi^{\prime}
are mutually inverse isomorphisms and

S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M_{R}(\left\{x\right\})/I_{\left\{x\right\}})\cong\ S.

For any set
X

M_{R}(X)/I_{X}\cong\bigoplus_{x\in X}M_{R}(\left\{x\right\})/I_{\left\{x\right\}}

\begin{split}S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M_{R}(X)/I_{X})\ &\cong\ S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}\left(\bigoplus_{x\in X}M_{R}(\left\{x\right\})/I_{\left\{x\right\}}\right)\\
&\cong\ \bigoplus_{x\in X}(S\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}M_{R}(\left\{x\right\})/I_{\left\{x\right\}})\quad(\text{by Lemma \ref{K}})\\

(R,P_{\Omega})
-modules

N^{\prime}\otimes_{(R,P_{\Omega})}(M_{R}(X)/I_{X})\cong\oplus_{x\in X}N^{\prime}

.

Lemma 4.12
.

Every projective left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-modules is a direct summand of a free left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module.

Proof.

If
(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
is a projective left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module and
(F(M),\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega})
is a free left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module generated by set
M
, then there exists a left
(R,P_{\Omega})
-module homomorphism
\varphi:(F(M),\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
was induced by the identity set map
id_{M}:(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
. By the universal property of
(F(M),\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega})
,
\varphi
is surjective with
\varphi|_{M}=id_{M}
. Since
(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})
is a projective left
(R,P_{\Omega})

(R,P_{\Omega})
-module homomorphism
\psi:(M,\mathfrak{m}_{\Omega})\longrightarrow(F(M),\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega})

(F(M),\overline{\mathfrak{m}}_{\Omega})

Theorem 4.13

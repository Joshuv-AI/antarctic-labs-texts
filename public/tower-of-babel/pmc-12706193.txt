Buccal fat applied to transoral robotic lateral oropharyngectomy defects to lessen radical tonsillectomy pain (BOLT): a single-centre, phase II, parallel, randomised control trial study protocol
Authors: Michael Xie, John de Almeida, David Goldstein, Rosemary Martino, Yi Fan Liu, Brittany Allen, Wei Xu, Katrina Hueniken, Christopher MKL Yao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTransoral robotic surgery (TORS) is a minimally invasive technique for surgical removal of tumours of the tonsil and lateral oropharynx. Surgical defects after TORS lateral oropharyngectomy are traditionally left open to heal by secondary intention, resulting in significant postoperative pain and secondarily resulting in delayed swallowing and discharge. Although multimodal analgesia can improve postoperative pain control, no studies to date have assessed the impact of adjunct surgical interventions for reducing postoperative pain after TORS. Buccal fat rotation flap is a regional reconstruction option after TORS lateral oropharyngectomy and provides immediate coverage of the open surgical wound. However, the impact of buccal fat rotation flap reconstruction on postoperative pain and swallowing remains unclear. This trial aims to compare postoperative pain outcomes in patients who undergo TORS lateral oropharyngectomy with and without buccal fat rotation reconstruction.Methods and analysisThis protocol outlines a single centre, parallel, unblinded, phase II, randomised control trial. Inclusion criteria include adult patient (≥18 years) undergoing TORS lateral oropharyngectomy for early to intermediate stage tonsillar squamous cell carcinoma (T1-2N0-1 p16+/−) or early to intermediate stage salivary gland tumours of the palatine tonsils. Exclusion criteria include a history of prior head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, prior head and neck radiotherapy, retropharyngeal lymphadenopathy, bilateral lymphadenopathy, need for bilateral neck dissection, baseline trismus, opioid use or drug addiction, need for open surgery (transcervical lateral oropharyngectomy), free tissue transfer, or alternative regional flap, and pregnancy. All patients are planned for a TORS lateral oropharyngectomy. The intervention group will have a buccal fat rotation flap reconstruction, and the control group will be allowed to heal via secondary intention. The allocation sequence will be created using a computer-generated random sequence with a permuted block strategy. The allocation sequence will be concealed until the time of assignment. The primary outcome is postoperative pain intensity during rest and swallowing using the visualised analogue scale. Secondary outcomes include postoperative complications, other adverse events, patient-reported speech and swallowing, opioid usage, length of hospital stay, feeding tube dependence and blood glucose levels. The trial has a target sample size of 40 patients. Statistical analysis of the primary outcome will be analysed in an intention to treat analysis using a linear mixed effects model.Ethics and disseminationThe study was approved by the University Health Network Coordinated Approval Process for Clinical Research. Study number CAPCR ID: 24-5894. All participants will be required to provide written informed consent to participate. Findings will be presented at national conferences and published in medical journals.Trial registration numberNCT06965738.

Introduction

Transoral robotic surgery (TORS) for oropharyngeal cancers has transformed how oropharyngeal cancers are managed, allowing for minimally invasive surgical access to remove tumours of the tonsil and base of tongue. While large incisions and open approaches can be avoided, oropharyngeal defects following TORS are traditionally left as an open wound to heal by secondary intention, resulting in significant postoperative pain, and possibly dysphagia and dehydration.14

While this has been highlighted as a key outcome measure, studies on acute pain following TORS have not had any standardisation regarding the timing of pain measurement and pain assessment.5 Only one study has assessed pain during ‘movement-related pain’, such as swallowing, which has been advocated as a key metric.1 6 7 Most studies used a visual analogue scale (VAS); however, the majority failed to describe the timing of pain assessment relative to administration of analgesia, time of day or diet. Overall, the variation between studies in pain ratings and opioid utilisation are reflected by pain ratings that range widely from 2.5 to 8 in the first few postoperative days.5

Based on the current evidence, multimodal analgesia resulted in the lowest pain ratings following TORS, with those that included gabapentin, a COX-2 inhibitor and acetaminophen having excellent pain control in the early postoperative period.1 8 9 Using these regimens, Castellanos et al found that postoperative opioid consumption significantly decreased in the first three postoperative days (PODs).9 Clayburgh et al identified that addition of perioperative corticosteroids was safe and significantly improved diet consistency and decreased length of hospital stay, though pain scores were minimally affected.10

No study thus far has assessed the impact of surgical interventions on postoperative pain and swallowing. Here, we hope to evaluate the impact of the buccal fat rotational flap on the postoperative pain following TORS. The buccal fat rotation flap was first described for closure of an oroantral communication following oncologic resection in 1977 by Egyedi.11 It was subsequently noted to epithelialise in the oral cavity within 3–4 weeks, negating the need for further reconstruction.12 13 While the maximum size of reconstructed defects ranged from 4 to 5 cm, recent reports have indicated its use in mandibular and maxillary bone coverage, and various oral cavity and oropharyngeal defects.1416 Complications include haematoma, partial necrosis or arterial bleeding, though rare.

Following a transoral lateral oropharyngectomy, the ipsilateral posterior pharyngeal wall can be released from the prevertebral plane and sutured to the velum and remnant uvula with 3–0 vicryl sutures. The buccal fat pad can then be harvested from the buccal space by extending a horizontal incision along the cheek mucosa, and through the buccinator muscle adjacent to the posterior molars. The fat pad is gradually released using gentle traction with forceps and sutured in place along the oropharyngeal wall defect and closed over the rotated buccal fat pad.16 Poorly controlled pain can affect swallowing and prolong postoperative hospitalisation. It is understood that in the acute period, dysphagia is significantly worsened after TORS, with up to 23% of patients having moderate-severe dysphagia.17 While the majority of patients do return to baseline by a year, it would be critical to understand how our added intervention would impact swallowing in the short term.18

The literature regarding postoperative pain following TORS of the lateral oropharynx lacks standardisation with analgesic regimen and pain measurement. While it has been recognised as an important metric, lack of standardisation and lack of pain measurement during swallowing leaves room for further understanding. The primary purpose of this study is to evaluate the impact of adding a surgical intervention, the buccal fat rotation flap, on lateral oropharyngeal defects. Specifically, we hope to understand its impact on postoperative pain and swallowing. Secondary results of the study will help to elucidate its safety and risk of complications. Results from the study may help identify approaches to minimise treatment-related morbidity.

Summary of the problem

Patients presenting with an oropharynx tumour requiring a transoral robotic lateral oropharyngectomy are at risk of significant postoperative pain, which results in delayed swallowing and prolonged discharge. While prior studies have focused on varying postoperative pain management, there is significant variation and lack of standardisation to the measurement timing and instrument used. This study is aimed at assessing the impact of a surgical intervention with the rotation of buccal fat into the wound bed compared with no buccal fat for reducing postoperative pain and opioid usage in a single centre, phase II, randomised control trial. We hypothesise that patients who undergo buccal fat reconstruction will have diminished pain needs.

Interventions

Both arms will undergo a standard transoral robotic lateral oropharyngectomy with the Davinci Xi (Intuitive Surgical, Sunnyvale, CA, USA). Patients will be intubated nasotracheally and positioned in a standard fashion. The Davinci Xi will then be appropriately docked for exposure of the tumour. Standard mucosal incisions around the tumour will be made for tumour extirpation with removal of at least the constrictor muscle as the deep margin. Haemostasis will be achieved in a standard fashion with bipolar, surgical clips and suction monopolar as needed. Mucosal margins from the specimen or patient will then be sent for frozen section analysis to guide need for further ablation.

Patients undergoing intervention will undergo buccal fat reconstruction. Following a transoral lateral oropharyngectomy, the ipsilateral posterior pharyngeal wall can be released from the prevertebral plane and sutured to the velum and remnant uvula with 3–0 vicryl sutures. The buccal fat pad can then be harvested from the buccal space by extending a horizontal incision along the cheek mucosa, and through the buccinator muscle adjacent to the posterior molars. The fat pad is gradually released using gentle traction with forceps and sutured in place along the oropharyngeal wall defect and closed over the rotated buccal fat pad.16

Patients in the control group will be allowed to heal via secondary intention without further reconstructive interventions.

Relevant concomitant care and interventions that are permitted or prohibited during the trial

There will be no restrictions on intraoperative anaesthetic medications. The use of long-acting narcotics, steroids and other analgesia will be at the discretion of the attending anaesthesiologist. Local anaesthetic, including topical and infiltrative, will not be permitted during the trial.

Primary, secondary and other outcomes

Primary outcome

The primary objective for this study is to determine the effect of a buccal fat rotation flap on postoperative pain following transoral robotic lateral oropharyngectomy. The primary outcome will be determined using VASs at rest and swallowing following surgery. These questionnaires will be acquired daily following surgery during hospitalisation, once between days 7–10, then at POD 21–28. Patients will score their pain in their pain journal (online supplemental I) after taking their oral analgesia per the pain regimen to measure optimised postoperative pain.

Sample size

From prior clinical trials on postoperative pain after maximal pain regimens with (dexamethasone, Celebrex, gabapentin, Tylenol and opioids), the pain scores range from 6.8, 6.4 to 6.7.10 From our own experience, the minimal clinically significant pain difference would be at least a decrease by 1.0% or 10%.19 A parallel two-group design will be used to test whether the means between groups will be different (H0: μ1-μ2=0 vs H1: μ1-μ2≠0). The comparison will be made using a two-sided, two-sample equal-variance t-test, with a Type I error rate (α) of 0.05. The common SD for both groups is assumed to be 2.1. To detect a difference in means of μ1-μ2=6.7–4.7=2 with 80% power. Conservatively, we anticipate that we would need 40 patients.

Strategies for achieving adequate participant enrolment to reach target sample size

Strategies to achieve target enrolment rates and sample sizes include an educational presentation to all attending surgeons and relevant staff regarding the trial start date, eligibility criteria and study contacts. The study coordinators will release bi-annual recruitment target emails to summarise enrolment progress. Study information and updates will also be posted on the University of Toronto Otolaryngology Head and Neck Surgery website.

Assignment of interventions

The allocation sequence will be created using a computer-generated random sequence with a permuted block strategy by an independent statistician. The allocation sequence will be implemented and assignments will be executed using Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCAP) by an independent statistician. The allocation sequence will be concealed until the time of assignment.

Surgeons and research coordinators will enrol participants. After enrolment, interventions will be assigned by an independent statistician and the attending surgeon, research team and patient will be notified. There will be no blinding as part of this trial. Given that patient care and documentation will be completed through the standard electronic health record, surgery group assignment will be readily accessible and outcome assessment blinding cannot be guaranteed based on the current trial design and is a limitation of the study.

Participant retention and complete follow-up, data entry, coding, security, storage

There are no additional plans to promote participant retention as this is a relatively short perioperative/follow-up period. If any patients decide to withdraw from the study, they will be marked as withdrawn/discontinued and no data will be collected/used.

Study data will be stored using REDCAP on site at the Toronto General Hospital in the Department of Otolaryngology. The database will be located on a network server hospital computer at TGH in a password-protected file. Data will be entered, coded and quality checked by a single statistician.

Statistical analysis

Final analysis will be performed in a blinded fashion.

Baseline characteristics (age, sex, tumour stage, resection extent, smoking history and preop pain characteristics) will be summarised by groups using means±SD, and median (IQR) for continuous variables and frequencies (%) for categorical variables. Pain scores at each time point will be summarised by group and presented as mean±SD or median with IQR (depending on distribution). A linear mixed effects model will be used to test whether the change in pain over time differs between groups, with fixed effects including (flap vs control), time, and random effects including (patient level intercept). As secondary analyses, time-point specific comparisons will be performed with independent t-tests or Mann-Whitney U tests; total opioid consumption as measured by oral morphine equivalents will be compared at multiple time points with independent t-tests.

Primary and secondary outcomes will be reported using an intention to treat analysis.

Monitoring

This trial will not have a data monitoring committee or planned interim analysis as it is anticipated to be of short duration, minimal risks and does not involve developmental interventions.

Medical and surgical complications will be documented during the study follow-up period and reported in the final manuscript.

Ethics and dissemination

The present study was approved by the University Health Network (UHN) CAPCR ethics committee, study number CAPCR ID: 24-5894. All participants in this study will be asked to provide written informed consent. The findings will be presented at conferences (eg, Canadian Otolaryngology Society, American Head and Neck Society). The trial results will be published in a peer-reviewed medical journal.

Participant consent

Eligible participants for the study will be screened during routine clinic lists by a provider in the patient’s circle of care. Should the patient express interest in participating, a research team member will then approach the participant in person to go over the study and obtain written informed consent (online supplemental III), if the patient remains interested in enrolling.

Data storage and privacy

Routine practices for ensuring the confidentiality and privacy of all participants will be followed in this study. Data will be stored in a secure database software on site at the Toronto General Hospital in the Department of Otolaryngology. The database will be located on a network server hospital computer at TGH in a password-protected file. Participants will be de-identified and assigned a study number. The identity log will be kept separately in the department with the study coordinator. Participant names and other personal identifiers will not be used in any reports, presentation or published work.

Data will be stored for approximately 10 years from the date of study closure. All data will remain with the PI in the Department of Otolaryngology. Only investigators, study team members and authorities to do monitoring, audits, research ethics board review and regulatory inspection will have access to data.

In the event of an inappropriate release of personal health information, any information that can be retrieved will be retrieved, the UHN Privacy Office and research ethics board will be notified, and the appropriate further actions will be taken.

Patient and public involvement

Patients or the public were not involved in the design, or conduct, or reporting, or dissemination plans of our research.

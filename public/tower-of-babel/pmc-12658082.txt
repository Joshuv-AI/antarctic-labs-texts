NIDD-enabled lightweight intrusion detection for effective DDoS mitigation in 5G and beyond
Authors: Iqra Javid, Sibaram Khara, Jaroslav Frnda, Shahbaz Ahmad Khanday, Niyaz Ahmad Wani, Jatin Bedi, Muhammad Shahid Anwar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractWith the introduction of 5G technology, wireless communication is expected to undergo revolutionary changes that will allow high-speed connectivity and scalability. Though 5G networks have the potential to be revolutionary, they also pose new challenges in ensuring the security and integrity of data transfer, especially in Non-IP Data Delivery (NIDD) scenarios. The need for robust anomaly detection systems becomes even more critical in this scenario to safeguard IoT and other reliable networks. Anomaly detection has been the subject of much research in network contexts, as it is crucial for identifying hostile activity, system failures, and odd behavior. The growing dependence on technologies, particularly with the advent of 5G and its potential to connect nearly everything, has made it imperative to investigate intelligent and efficient techniques that ensure network availability, secrecy, and integrity. To address the botnet infiltration, DDoS mitigation, and other incursions in 5G networks, a novel lightweight intrusion detection model is proposed for 5G and beyond networks, which uses the 5GNIDD dataset in the experiments. The proposed model is powered by a robust prepossessing model, which uses Gini Importance for feature selection and state-of-the-art classifiers, namely, AdaBoost, Easy Ensemble, GRU, 1D-CNN, LSTM, and hybrid CNN-LSTM for classification. Two different case studies with k-best features are driven in experiments showcasing the effect of the curse of dimensionality on precision. The model has obtained 99.64% accuracy and a 0.9830 precision using 1D-CNN and a hybrid LSTM-CNN model.Keywords: 5GNIDD, GRU, LSTM, 1D CNN, LSTM-CNN, Easy EnsembleSubject terms: Computational science, Computer science

Introduction

Numerous safety problems that 5G specifications have still not sufficiently tackled are revealed by networks equipped with network segmentation capabilities. In this situation, established safety procedures aren’t consistently trustworthy since diversified nodes show sporadic connectivity and seek varied services via the network. Through utilizing the ideas of Software Defined Connections (SDN) and community Perform Virtualization (NFV), network splitting enables the creation of numerous simulated instances of the real network, or “network slices,” ensures traffic separateness between them, and customizes the technological assets within each slice to certain types of use cases1. The integration of numerous mobile services within the same network framework is made possible by slicing the network. Given the aforementioned circumstances, having a straightforward yet tightly bound setting that concentrates on 5G security programs that employ Deep Learning (DL)–based network segmentation to identify and lessen hostile client threats is extremely important. Contrarily, due to an anticipated increase in the overall number of connected gadgets and interconnected units per human being, the Internet of Things is one forthcoming innovation that will likely face substantial safety attacks. The growing appetite for 5G technological and B5G solutions is being made possible by User Equipment (UE) like tablet devices and smartphones, which leaves consumers open to assaults like Denial of Service (DoS) and/or Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS), depending on the situation2. Modern intrusions involving hacking, transitioning, and bots could be extremely harmful to other users as well as the whole 5G or B5G infrastructure provided they are not identified and stopped, even though earlier breaches appeared to be more straightforward and more advanced3. DDoS uses all network resources to respond to the attacker’s operations. Typically, its goal is to prevent applications from authorized consumers from being served or answered by network resources4. In addition to infecting network computers and gadgets and creating a zombie collection of compromised nodes, a distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) assault can disable or prevent an authorized user from using network amenities. The contemporary Internet infrastructure and its straightforward yet highly effective attack escalation make minimally disruptive remediation one of the significant safety concerns in IoT infrastructure, according to Doshi et al.5. AdaBoost

A meta-estimation algorithm known as an AdaBoost classifier starts by assembling a classifier on the initial data set. It then constructs more versions of the algorithm on the same dataset, but this time the weightings of those instances that were predicted incorrectly are changed so that later the classification algorithm concentrates more on other challenging cases, to recover from losses and boost its classification tendency37. Discussion

In a 5G-enabled Internet of Things (IoT) ecosystem, we propose an intelligent framework designed to anticipate and detect fraudulent device behavior that could lead to False Data Injection Attacks (FDIA) and DDoS threats. The framework aims to ensure security, adaptability, and resilience in a dynamic and high-risk environment. It employs an ensemble-based predictive mechanism to forecast potential network breaches by analyzing the historical log data, relying solely on the assessment of traffic logs. This approach mitigates rapid detection and mitigation of cyberattacks in a resource-constrained environment. A novel preprocessing layer using a tree-based feature selection method distinguishes the proposed framework, enhancing both the efficiency and interpretability of the model. By focusing on the most relevant attributes, the system reduces computational overhead while maintaining high detection accuracy. Ensuring 5G security necessitates continuous identification and investigation of malicious activities to monitor and restrict abnormal network flows without IP-associated features. Given the proliferation of vulnerable IoT devices in 5G ecosystem, botnet-based malware attacks pose a severe threat to network integrity and operational stability. The proposed framework effectively addresses their detection, offering a scalable and intelligent defense mechanism against evolving IoT security threats. The performance analysis and computational differences with state-of-the-art models are depicted in Table 8, indicating that the proposed model not only evaluates the various ensemble and hybrid classifiers’ performance but also outperforms state-of-the-art methods as well. Each model exhibits varying strengths based on precision, recall, F1-score, and accuracy, reflecting their suitability for different datasets and class distributions. Using the 1D-CNN, with the top-10 features, the proposed model requires a low training time of 1531.65 seconds and 24.6023 seconds of inference time on the test set, a test accuracy of 99.09 %, macro F1 of 0.99, weighted F1 of 0.99, macro precision of 0.9893, macro recall of 0.9907, Matthews Correlation Coefficient of 0.9868, and balanced accuracy of 0.9907, eliminating the requirement of high-end computational cost. Conversely, using the 20 best features, the same classifier yielded 93.63 % accuracy, macro F1 of 0.9131, Matthews Correlation Coefficient of 0.9088, and balanced accuracy of 0.9280, while training time rose to 1,662.66 s. Furthermore, we emphasize that by keeping the feature selection strategy consistent using Gini importance across all classifiers, our ensemble and hybrid deep learning models (GRU, LSTM, LSTM-CNN, and Easy Ensemble worked efficiently for 5G DDoS detection. Our approach demonstrates the role of feature selection for effective performance in assault detection, whether 5G Non-IP data delivery or other realistic datasets have a vast feature dimension. Transfer learning and continuous learning help to make the model capable of exploiting pre-accumulated knowledge on known threats and to be gradually adapted to new patterns, with no need for retraining from scratch, and to make it as up-to-date as possible with the new collected attack data to avoid its obsolescence. This new component further enhances the practical importance of our work while promoting applicability to 5G threat environments in practice.

Conclusion and future scope

Improper activity must be investigated to regulate and restrict undesired traffic movement. Botnet-based ransomware exploits, zero-day attacks, and IoT DDoS attacks pose a serious risk of disrupting the 5G infrastructure due to the growing number of IoT devices vulnerable within it. To address this critical challenge, the research aims to address security issues related to converged network topologies and enable them by defining steps for counteracting these attacks. Nevertheless, as intruders employ more sophisticated strategies to modify incoming data and circumvent IDS designs, existing approaches are often expensive and vulnerable, relying heavily on flow-based features. To address this issue, we propose a lightweight 5G assault detection approach to identify attacks in 5G infrastructure. The Hybrid CNN-LSTM model emerged as the best performer, offering 99.70% accuracy in Case 2. This makes it highly suitable for security-critical applications, such as intrusion detection in wireless networks, particularly in 5G networks. Limited attack diversity, imbalanced samples for training, and scalability concerns are the major challenges authors faced in developing robust assault detection, especially in 5GNIDD. The dataset is highly imbalanced, with the benign and malicious samples not uniformly distributed. The benign traffic (477,737 samples), UDPFlood (457,340 samples), and HTTPFlood (140,812 samples) dominate, while SYNFlood (9,721 samples) and especially ICMPFlood (1,155 samples) are highly under-represented. Secondly, the dataset contains a reasonable diversity of attacks, including volumetric assaults, application layer attack vectors, and reconnaissance, but it does not fully replicate the real-world 5G setting; complex multi-vector or stealthy attack campaigns are missing from the dataset. Reducing the computational burden for training, the proposed model stands alone as a unique one in delivering efficacy. The authors intend to test the proposed methodology in the future, which integrates the 5GNIDD with other benchmark datasets.

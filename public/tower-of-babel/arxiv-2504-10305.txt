The commutator subalgebra of the Lie algebra associated with a right-angled Coxeter group Thanks: This work was supported by the Russian Science Foundation under grant no. 24-71-00059, https://rscf.ru/en/project/24-71-00059/ .
Fedor Vylegzhanin, Yakov Veryovkin

Abstract
. We study the graded Lie algebra L ⁡ ( R ​ C 𝒦 ) L(RC_{\mathcal{K}}) associated with the lower central series of a right-angled Coxeter group. We construct a surjective homomorphism from the polynomial ring over an explicit Lie algebra N 𝒦 N_{\mathcal{K}} to the commutator subalgebra of L ⁡ ( R ​ C 𝒦 ) L(RC_{\mathcal{K}}) , and conjecture that it is an isomorphism. The homomorphism is defined in terms of a new operation in Lie algebras associated with groups generated by involutions, which corresponds to the squaring and has an analogue in homotopy theory. We show that the universal enveloping algebra U ⁡ ( N 𝒦 ) U(N_{\mathcal{K}}) is isomorphic to the mod 2 2 loop homology algebra of the corresponding moment-angle complex 𝒵 𝒦 \mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}} . This allows us to give a presentation of the Lie algebra N 𝒦 N_{\mathcal{K}} by generators and relations.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a simple graph on the vertex set
\{1,\dots,m\}
.
The corresponding
right-angled Coxeter group

\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\Gamma}
is the group generated by the elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
which satisfy the relations
g_{i}^{2}=1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and
g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}
for all edges
\{i,j\}\in\Gamma
. Right-angled Coxeter groups are classical objects in geometric group theory
[
Da25
]
. It is convenient to consider
\Gamma
as the 1-skeleton of its clique complex
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(\Gamma)
— the largest simplicial complex with 1-skeleton
\mathcal{K}^{1}=\Gamma
. Throughout the paper, we denote
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}:=\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\Gamma}
.

Each group
G
has a descending filtration
G=:\gamma_{1}(G)\supset\dots\supset\gamma_{n}(G)\supset\dots
by the normal subgroups
\gamma_{k+1}(G):=(G,\gamma_{k}(G))
, called the
lower central series
, and the associated graded Lie algebra
L(G)=\bigoplus_{k\geq 1}L_{k}(G)
,
L_{k}(G):=\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)
,
\deg L_{k}=k
. We study the Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
associated with a right-angled Coxeter group, and its commutator subalgebra
L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 2}L_{k}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
.

For the right-angled
Artin
groups
\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
, defined similarly to the right-angled Coxeter groups but without introducing the relations
g_{i}^{2}=1
, the associated Lie algebra was computed in
[
DK92
,
PS06
,
W11
]
. In more detail, the Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the graph Lie algebra (over
\mathbb{Z}
) which corresponds to the graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
. Similarly, by
[
Ve19
, Proposition 4.2]
there is an epimorphism
\varphi:L_{\mathcal{K}}\to L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
of Lie algebras, where
L_{\mathcal{K}}
is the graph Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, see (
2.2
). This epimorphism is not an isomorphism (see
[
Ve19
, Example 4.3]
). So the associated Lie algebra is much more complicated for right-angled Coxeter groups than for right-angled Artin groups. In
[
Ve19
, Theorem 4.5]
, the groups
L_{k}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
were described for
k\leq 3
. For
k\geq 4
difficulties arose, similar to those encountered in Struik
[
St60
,
St61
]
in the calculation of the quotient groups
\gamma_{1}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})/\gamma_{k}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
in some particular cases.

For any group
G
generated by involutions, we introduce a new linear operation
h:L_{k}(G)\to L_{k+1}(G)
which satisfies the identity
h([x,y])=[h(x),y]
, corresponds to the squaring in
G
and has an analogue in homotopy theory (see Theorem
3.5
and Remark
3.7
).
Using this operation, in Theorem
4.3
we define a surjective homomorphism of Lie algebras

\psi:N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}N_{\mathcal{K}}t^{i}\to L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 2}L_{k}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}),

where
N_{\mathcal{K}}:=\langle\mbox{\it GPTW\/}\rangle_{Lie}\subset L_{\mathcal{K}}
is the Lie subalgebra generated by the explicit set of
Grbić–Panov–Theriault–Wu generators

[
GPTW16
]
. We prove that
\psi
is injective in graded components of degree
k=2,3
(see Proposition
6.9
), and conjecture that
\psi
is an isomorphism. The results of the unpublished thesis of R. Prener
[
Pr69
]
confirm the conjecture in the case when
\mathcal{K}
is a disjoint union of simplices.

We also obtain a remarkable connection of the Lie algebra
N_{\mathcal{K}}
with the homotopy theory of
moment-angle complexes

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}}
, which are topological spaces important in toric topology and theory of polyhedral products
[
BP15
,
BBC19
]
. In Theorem
6.2
we prove an isomorphism of graded associative
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-algebras

U(N_{\mathcal{K}})\cong H_{*}(\Omega\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{K}};\mathbb{Z}_{2}),

where
U(-)
is the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra and
H_{*}(\Omega X;\mathbf{k})
is the Pontryagin algebra of a simply connected space
X
. To prove this, in Section
5
we use topological methods to obtain a partial multiplicative section of the natural homomorphism of Hopf algebras

T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})/([x_{i},x_{j}]=0,\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K})\to T(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})/(u_{i}^{2}=[u_{i},u_{j}]=0,~\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}).

Using Theorem
6.2
, in Section
6
we describe the additive and multiplicative structure of
N_{\mathcal{K}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Simplicial complexes and graphs

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an abstract simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]=\{1,2,\dots,m\}
.
A subset
I\subset[m]
such that
I\in\mathcal{K}
is called a
simplex
of
\mathcal{K}
. We always assume that
\mathcal{K}
contains the empty set
\varnothing
and the singletons
\{i\}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
. For a subset
J\subset[m]
, the simplicial complex

\mathcal{K}_{J}:=\{I\subset J\colon I\in\mathcal{K}\}

is called the
full subcomplex
of
\mathcal{K}
on the vertex set
J
.

The
Euler characteristic
of a complex
\mathcal{K}
is the Euler characteristic of its geometric realisation. Hence
\chi(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{K},I\neq\varnothing}(-1)^{|I|-1}
, so
1-\chi(\mathcal{K})=\sum_{I\in\mathcal{K}}(-1)^{|I|}
.

A simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
is
flag
if any set of pairwise connected vertices of
\mathcal{K}
is a simplex. Any simple graph is the
1
-dimensional skeleton of a unique flag complex; thus a flag complex
\mathcal{K}
is determined by its
1
-skeleton
\mathcal{K}^{1}:=\{I\in\mathcal{K}:|I|\leq 2\}
.

A simple graph is
chordal
if for any simple cycle of length
\geq 4
there is an edge which divides it into two smaller cycles.

Remark 2.1
.

Many of objects considered in this paper are defined using a flag simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
, but in fact are determined by its 1-skeleton, i.e. by a simple graph. We use the language of simplicial complexes to highlight the connection with the theory of polyhedral products, see
[
PV16
,
Ve19
,
GPTW16
]
.

2.2.
The Lie algebra associated with a group

For a group
G
, the
commutator
of
a,b\in G
is the element
(a,b)=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab\in G
. For any three elements
a,b,c\in G
, the
Witt–Hall identities
hold:

(2.1)

\displaystyle(a,bc)=(a,c)(a,b)((a,b),c),

\displaystyle(ab,c)=(a,c)((a,c),b)(b,c),

\displaystyle((a,b),c)((b,c),a)((c,a),b)=(b,a)(c,a)(c,b)^{a}(a,b)(a,c)^{b}(b,c)^{a}(a,c)(c,a)^{b},

where
a^{b}=b^{-1}ab
.

For any subgroups
H,W\subset G
, denote by
(H,W)\subset G
the subgroup generated by the commutators
(h,w)
for all
h\in H,w\in W
. In particular, the
commutator subgroup

G^{\prime}
of
G
is
(G,G)
. Define
\gamma_{1}(G):=G
and then recursively
\gamma_{k+1}(G):=(\gamma_{k}(G),G)
. The sequence of groups
\gamma_{1}(G),\gamma_{2}(G),\ldots,\gamma_{k}(G),\ldots
is called the
lower central series
of
G
. Then
\gamma_{k+1}(G)
is a normal subgroup of
\gamma_{k}(G)
, and the quotient group
\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)
is abelian. Consider their direct sum

L(G):=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{+\infty}L_{k}(G),\quad L_{k}(G):=\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G).

We denote by
\overline{a_{k}}
the class of an element
a_{k}\in\gamma_{k}(G)
in the quotient group
L_{k}(G)
. If
a_{k}\in\gamma_{k}(G),\;a_{l}\in\gamma_{l}(G)
, then
(a_{k},a_{l})\in\gamma_{k+l}(G)
. It follows from the Witt–Hall identities that
L(G)
is a graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}
(a Lie ring) with the Lie bracket defined by the formula
[\overline{a_{k}},\overline{a_{l}}]:=\overline{(a_{k},a_{l})}
. The Lie algebra
L(G)
is called the
Lie algebra associated with
the group
G
(see
[
La54
,
MCS76
]
).

Since the subgroup
\gamma_{n}(G)
is generated by commutators of elements from
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
, the graded Lie algebra
L(G)
is generated by
L_{1}(G)
. It follows that
\bigoplus_{k\geq 2}L_{k}(G)
is the commutator subalgebra
L^{\prime}(G):=[L(G),L(G)]
of
L(G)
.

2.3.
Right-angled Coxeter groups

Denote by
F(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
the free group on
g_{1},\ldots,g_{m}
. The
right-angled Coxeter group
corresponding to a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
m
vertices is the group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
defined by generators and relations as follows:

\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}:=F(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\big/(g_{i}^{2}=1\text{ for }i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},\;\;g_{i}g_{j}=g_{j}g_{i}\text{ if
}\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}).

Clearly, the group
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
depends only on the graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
, the
1
-skeleton of
\mathcal{K}
. Hence we can assume that
\mathcal{K}
is a flag complex.

We denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
the field with two elements. Denote by
\mbox{\it FL\/}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m})
the free Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
on the generators
\mu_{i}
, where
\deg\mu_{i}=1
. For a simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on
m
vertices define the
graph Lie algebra
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
,

(2.2)

L_{\mathcal{K}}:=\mbox{\it FL\/}_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots,\mu_{m})/([\mu_{i},\mu_{j}]=0\text{ for }\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}).\quad

L_{\mathcal{K}}
also depends only on the graph
\mathcal{K}^{1}
.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Ve19
, Propositions 3.3 and 4.1]
)
.

The square of any element of
\gamma_{k}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is contained in
\gamma_{k+1}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
. Hence

L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is a Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.∎

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Ve19
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

There is an epimorphism of Lie algebras
\varphi:L_{\mathcal{K}}\rightarrow L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
,
\varphi(\mu_{i})=\overline{g}_{i}
.∎

The epimorphism
\varphi
is not an isomorphism (see
[
Ve19
, Example 4.3]
). In this sense the right-angled Coxeter groups are different from the right-angled Artin groups, where the associated Lie algebra
L(\mbox{\it RA\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is isomorphic to the graph Lie algebra over
\mathbb{Z}
, see
[
DK92
,
PS06
,
W11
]
.

2.4.
Nested commutators

Fix
m\geq 1
, and let
q=(q_{1},\dots,q_{m})
be elements in a Lie algebra
L
over a commutative ring with unit. (In our applications,
m
is the number of vertices of a simplicial complex).

Definition 2.4
.

An expression of the form

a=[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[q_{i_{k}},q_{j}]\dots]],\quad k\geq 1,~i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j\in[m]

is a
nested commutator of length
k+1
on the elements
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\in L
. We say that
a
is a nested commutator
without repeats
, if
i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j\in[m]
are pairwise distinct.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
V=(i_{1},\dots,i_{k})
be a sequence of elements in
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
. For a subset
I\subset[k],

I=\{t_{1}<\dots<t_{s}\}
and an element
x\in L
, denote

c_{q,V}(I,x):=[q_{i_{t_{1}}},[q_{i_{t_{2}}},\dots[q_{i_{t_{s}}},x]\dots]]\in L.

For example,
c_{q,V}(\varnothing,x)=x
and
c_{q,V}(\{t\},x)=[q_{i_{t}},x]
. Denote also

c_{q}(I,x):=c_{q,(1,2,\dots,m)}(I,x)=[q_{t_{1}},[q_{t_{2}},\dots[q_{t_{s}},x]\dots]]

for
I=\{t_{1}<\dots<t_{s}\}\subset[m]
. For
V=(5,4,5,1)
and
I=\{1,3\}
, we have
c_{q,V}(I,x)=[q_{5},[q_{5},x]]
and
c_{q}(I,x)=[q_{1},[q_{3},x]]
.

The next lemma is proved by induction using the Jacobi identity.

Lemma 2.6

(cf.
[
Vy24
, Lemma C.3]
)
.

In the notations above, for
x,y\in L
we have

c_{q,V}(I,[x,y])=\sum_{I=A\sqcup B}\Big[c_{q,V}(A,x),c_{q,V}(B,y)\Big];

in particular,

c_{q}(I,[x,y])=\sum_{I=A\sqcup B}\Big[c_{q}(A,x),c_{q}(B,y)\Big].\qed

Definition 2.7

(
[
Vy24
, Definition 5.2]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a flag simplicial complex on the vertex set
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
. For a subset
J\subset[m]
, denote by
\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(J)\subset J
the set of all vertices
j\in J
which satisfy two conditions:

•

j
is the smallest vertex in its connected component of the complex
\mathcal{K}_{J}
;

•

the vertices
j
and
\max(J)
belong to different connected components of
\mathcal{K}_{J}
.

Given the elements
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}
of a Lie algebra
L
, a commutator
c_{q}(J\setminus j,q_{j})\in L
is called the
GPTW generator
if
J\subset[m]
and
j\in\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(J).

I.e., GPTW generators are nested commutators without repeats of the form

[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[q_{i_{k}},q_{j}]\dots]],~i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}>j,\quad j\in\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j\}).

For example,
q_{i}\notin\mbox{\it GPTW\/}
, and
[q_{i},q_{j}]\in\mbox{\it GPTW\/}
if and only if
i>j
and
\{i,j\}\notin\mathcal{K}
. The set of GPTW generators

\mbox{\it GPTW\/}:=\{c_{q}(J\setminus j,q_{j}):J\subset[m],j\in\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(J)\}\subset L

consists of
\sum_{J\subset[m]}\dim{\widetilde{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{J})
elements, where
\mathcal{K}_{J}
has
1+\dim{\widetilde{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{K}_{J})
connected components.
These elements first appeared in the work of Grbić, Panov, Theriault and Wu
[
GPTW16
, Theorem 4.3]
as a minimal generating set for loop homology of moment-angle complexes corresponding to flag complexes. Similar commutators in
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}
form a minimal generating set for the group
\mbox{\it RC\/}^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}

[
PV16
, Theorem 4.5]
.

3.
Properties of nested commutators

Fix a flag simplical complex
\mathcal{K}
on the vertex set
[m]=\{1,\dots,m\}
and elements
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}
of a Lie algebra
L
.

3.1.
General properties

The proof of the next lemma is similar to the proof of
[
PV16
, Lemma 4.7]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Each nested commutator without repeats
a=[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[q_{i_{k}},q_{j}]\dots]]
is equal to a Lie polynomial on nested commutators of the form
c_{q}(I,q_{i})
, where
I\neq\varnothing
and
\max(I)>i
.

Proof.

Induction on
k
. The base case: for
k=1
we have
a=[q_{i_{1}},q_{j}]=c_{q}(\{i_{1}\},q_{j})
for
i_{1}>j
and
a=\pm c_{q}(\{j\},q_{i_{1}})
for
j>i_{1}
.

Now let
k\geq 2
. For
t=1,\dots,k-2
by the Jacobi identity we have

\displaystyle a=[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[q_{i_{t}},[q_{i_{t+1}},x]]\dots]]=

\displaystyle\pm[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[q_{i_{t+1}},[q_{i_{t}},x]]\dots]]

\displaystyle\pm[q_{i_{1}},[\dots[[q_{i_{t}},q_{i_{t+1}}],x]]\dots]].

In the first summand the indices
i_{t}
and
i_{t+1}
are swapped. The second summand is of the form
c_{q,V}(I,[[q_{i_{t}},q_{i_{t+1}}],x])
, so by Lemma
2.6
it is a Lie polynomial on nested commutators without repeats which have smaller length (hence the inductive step applies). Thus we can freely permute
i_{1},\dots,i_{k-1}
. Also, the identity
[q_{i_{k}},q_{j}]=\pm[q_{j},q_{i_{k}}]
allows to swap
i_{k}
and
j
.

After such a permutation we can assume that
i_{1}<\dots<i_{k-1}
and
i_{k}>j
. If
i_{k-1}<i_{k}
, then
a
is of the required form
c_{q}(\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\},q_{j})
. Otherwise
i_{k-1}>i_{k}
, so
i_{k-1}=\max\{i_{1},\dots,i_{k},j\}
. Use the identity

[q_{i_{k-1}},[q_{i_{k}},q_{j}]]=\pm[q_{j},[q_{i_{k-1}},q_{i_{k}}]]\pm[q_{i_{k}},[q_{i_{k-1}},q_{j}]].

We obtain a sum of two elements of the form
[q_{t_{1}},[\dots[q_{t_{k}},q_{s}]\dots],

where
t_{k}
is the largest index. By permuting
t_{1},\dots,t_{k-1}
we can achieve that
t_{1}<\dots<t_{k}>s
. This expression has the required form
c_{q}(\{t_{1},\dots,t_{k}\},q_{s})
.
∎

Recall that a Lie polynomial is a linear combination of iterated Lie brackets (of a given set of elements).

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that the elements
q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\in L
satisfy
[q_{i},q_{j}]=0
for any
\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}
. Then each nested commutator without repeats is a Lie polynomial on the GPTW generators.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
, it is sufficient to express the elements of the form
c_{q}(J\setminus j,q_{j})
,
j\in J
, through the GPTW generators. In the case of Lie superalgebras such an expression exists by
[
GPTW16
, Theorem 4.3]
(see also a more explicit algorithm
[
Vy24
, Algorithm 5.4]
). The Lie algebra case is similar up to a change of signs (instead of the identities
[x,y]=-(-1)^{\deg(x)\cdot\deg(y)}[y,x]
we use
[x,y]=-[y,x]
, and similarly with the Jacobi identity.)
∎

Remark 3.3
.

In the proofs of Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
we used that the bracket is bilinear, commutative up to a sign and satisfies the Jacobi identity up to signs, without specifying the signs, and never used the identity
[x,x]=0
. So the same statements hold for Lie superalgebras and for elements of homotopy groups of topological spaces with respect to the Whitehead bracket
[
Wh78
, §X.7]
.

3.2.
The squaring operation

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f:G_{1}\to G_{2}
be a surjective map of groups, and suppose that the square of each element
x\in\gamma_{k}(G_{1})
is contained in
\gamma_{k+1}(G_{1})
. Then the square of each element
y\in\gamma_{k}(G_{2})
is contained in
\gamma_{k+1}(G_{2})
.

Proof.

Since
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
and
\gamma_{n+1}(G)=(G,\gamma_{n}(G))
, by induction we obtain that
\gamma_{n}(G_{1})\to\gamma_{n}(G_{2})
is surjective for all
n
. Now let
y\in\gamma_{k}(G_{2})
. Then
y=f(x)
for some
x\in\gamma_{k}(G_{1})
. By assumption,
x^{2}\in\gamma_{k+1}(G_{1})
, so
y^{2}=f(x^{2})\in\gamma_{k+1}(G_{2})
.
∎

A version of the following operation is used implicitly in Prener’s PhD thesis
[
Pr69
]
. We discovered its properties inspired by an analogy with the composition product in homotopy groups (see Remark
3.7
below).

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a group generated by elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
such that
g_{i}^{2}=1
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.
For
k\geq 2
consider the map

h:\gamma_{k}(G)/\gamma_{k+1}(G)\to\gamma_{k+1}(G)/\gamma_{k+2}(G),~a\gamma_{k+1}(G)\mapsto a^{2}\gamma_{k+2}(G).

Then

(1)

h:L_{k}(G)\to L_{k+1}(G)
is a well defined linear map;

(2)

[x,h(y)]=h([x,y])
for
x\in L_{k}(G)
,
y\in L_{\ell}(G)
,
\ell\geq 2
;

(3)

h([\overline{g}_{i},y])=[\overline{g}_{i},h(y)]
for
i=1,\dots,m
,
y\in L_{\ell}(G)
,
\ell\geq 2
;

(4)

h([\overline{g}_{i},\overline{g}_{j}])=[\overline{g}_{i},[\overline{g}_{i},\overline{g}_{j}]].

Proof.

We use the shortened notation
\gamma_{k}:=\gamma_{k}(G)
. Recall that
\gamma_{k+1}\subset\gamma_{k}
, and for
a\in\gamma_{k}
,
b\in\gamma_{\ell}
we have
(a,b)\in\gamma_{k+\ell}
. The Lie algebra operations in
L(G)=\bigoplus_{k}\gamma_{k}/\gamma_{k+1}
are defined by the formulas
\overline{a+b}=\overline{a}+\overline{b}
and
[\overline{a},\overline{b}]=\overline{(a,b)}
, i.e.
a\gamma_{k+1}+b\gamma_{k+1}=ab\gamma_{k+1}
and
[a\gamma_{k+1},b\gamma_{\ell+1}]=(a,b)\gamma_{k+\ell+1}.

By assumption, there is a surjective homomorphism
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}\to G
, where
\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}=F(g_{1},\dots,g_{m})/(g_{i}^{2}=1,~i=1,\dots,m)
is the right-angled Coxeter group corresponding to a simplicial complex with no edges.
By Proposition

\text{If }c\in\gamma_{k},\text{ then }c^{2}\in\gamma_{k+1}.

3.3.
Computations in the associated Lie algebra

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
x=[\overline{g}_{i_{1}},[\dots[\overline{g}_{i_{k}},\overline{g}_{j}]\dots]]\in L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
be a nested commutator,
k\geq 1
. Then
[\overline{g}_{i_{1}},x]=h(x).

Proof.

Induction on
k.
The base case
[\overline{g}_{i},[\overline{g}_{i},\overline{g}_{j}]]=h([\overline{g}_{i},\overline{g}_{j}])
is the part (4) of Theorem
3.5
. The inductive step: without loss of generality,
i_{1}=1
and
i_{2}=2
. Then
x=[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{2},y]]
, and
[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{1},y]]=h([\overline{g}_{1},y])
by the inductive assumption. By the Jacobi identity and Theorem
3.5
, we have

[\overline{g}_{1},x]=[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{2},y]]]=[\overline{g}_{1},[[\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}],y]]+[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{2},[\overline{g}_{1},y]]]\\
=[[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}]],y]+\underbrace{[[\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}],[\overline{g}_{1},y]]+[[\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}],[\overline{g}_{1},y]]}_{=0}+[\overline{g}_{2},[\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{1},y]]]\\
=[h([\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}]),y]+[\overline{g}_{2},h([\overline{g}_{1},y])]\\
=h([[\overline{g}_{1},\overline{g}_{2}],y]+[\overline{g}_{2},[\overline{g}_{1},y]])=h([\overline{g}_{1},[\overline{g}_{2},y]])=h(x).\qed

Corollary 3.9
.

Let

, where

is a nested commutator or
x=\overline{g}_{j}
. Then

[\overline{g}_{i},y]=h(y)+\sum_{\begin{smallmatrix}I=A\sqcup B,\\
A\neq\varnothing\end{smallmatrix}}\Big[c_{\overline{g},V}(A,\overline{g}_{i}),c_{\overline{g},V}(B,[\overline{g}_{i},x])\Big].

Proof.

By Lemma

, we have
h([\overline{g}_{i},x])=[\overline{g}_{i},[\overline{g}_{i},x]]
. Hence

h(y)=h(c_{\overline{g},V}(I,[\overline{g}_{i},x]))=c_{\overline{g},V}(I,[\overline{g}_{i},[\overline{g}_{i},x]])=\sum_{I=A\sqcup B}\Big[c_{\overline{g},V}(A,\overline{g}_{i}),c_{\overline{g},V}(B,[\overline{g}_{i},x])\Big]

by Lemma

and Theorem

. The case

correspond to the summand
[\overline{g}_{i},c_{\overline{g},V}(I,[\overline{g}_{i},x])]=[\overline{g}_{i},y]

∎

Let

4.
The surjective homomorphism onto the commutator subalgebra

For a flag simplicial complex
\mathcal{K}
on the vertex set
[m]
, consider the GPTW elements as a subset of
L_{\mathcal{K}}
,

\mbox{\it GPTW\/}:=\{c_{\mu}(J\setminus j,\mu_{j}):j\in\Theta_{\mathcal{K}}(J),J\subset[m]\}\subset L_{\mathcal{K}}.

Definition 4.1
.

Denote by
N_{\mathcal{K}}
the Lie subalgebra of
L_{\mathcal{K}}
generated by the GPTW generators,

N_{\mathcal{K}}:=\langle\mbox{\it GPTW\/}\rangle_{Lie}\subset L_{\mathcal{K}}.

By definition,
N_{\mathcal{K}}\subset L^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}.
This inclusion is strict unless
\mathcal{K}
is a simplex, since
[\mu_{i},[\mu_{i},\mu_{j}]]\in L^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
does not belong to
N_{\mathcal{K}}
if
\{i,j\}\notin\mathcal{K}.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{g}_{n}
be a graded Lie algebra. We introduce a graded Lie algebra structure on the polynomial ring
\mathfrak{g}[t]=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\mathfrak{g}t^{k}
as follows:

•

\deg(xt^{k}):=n+k
for
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{n}
;

•

[xt^{k},yt^{\ell}]:=[x,y]t^{k+\ell}
for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Since
N_{\mathcal{K}}\subset L^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}}
, the epimorphism
\varphi:L_{\mathcal{K}}\to L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
,
\mu_{i}\mapsto\overline{g}_{i}
from Proposition
2.3
can be restricted to a map of Lie algebras

\varphi^{\prime}:N_{\mathcal{K}}\to L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}).

Theorem 4.3
.

We have a surjective homomorphism of graded Lie algebras

\psi:N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]\twoheadrightarrow L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}}),\quad\psi(xt^{k}):=h^{k}(\varphi^{\prime}(x)),~x\in N_{\mathcal{K}}.

Proof.

The map
\psi
is linear since
\varphi^{\prime}
is a map of Lie algebras, while
h:L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\to L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is linear by Theorem
3.5
. Also,
\psi
is a map of Lie algebras: it maps a bracket
[xt^{k},yt^{\ell}]=[x,y]t^{k+\ell}
to the element

h^{k+\ell}(\varphi^{\prime}([x,y]))=\Big[h^{k}(\varphi^{\prime}(x)),h^{\ell}(\varphi^{\prime}(y))\Big]=[\psi(xt^{k}),\psi(yt^{\ell})],

since
h(-)
satisfies the identity
h([x,y])=[h(x),y)]=[x,h(y)].

It remains to check that
\psi
is onto. Let
n\geq 2
. By
[
Ve19
, Proposition 3.1, Corollary 3.2]
, the abelian group
L_{n}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is generated by nested commutators of length
n
on the elements
\overline{g}_{1},\dots,\overline{g}_{m}
. By Proposition
3.10
, such a commutator
a
is a linear combination of elements of the form
h^{n_{s}}(a^{\prime}_{s})
, where
a^{\prime}_{s}
is a Lie polynomial on nested commutators without repeats. Such a nested commutator is a Lie polynomial on GPTW generators by Proposition
3.2
, so
a^{\prime}_{s}
is a Lie polynomial on
GPTW
. In other words,
a^{\prime}_{s}=\varphi(x_{s})
for some
x_{s}\in N_{\mathcal{K}}
, so
h^{n_{s}}(a^{\prime}_{s})\in\Img\psi
and
a\in\Img\psi
.
∎

Conjecture 4.4
.

The surjective map of Lie algebras
\psi:N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]\twoheadrightarrow L^{\prime}(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
is an isomorphism for any flag complex
\mathcal{K}
.

The additive and multiplicative structure of the Lie algebra
N_{\mathcal{K}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
will be described in Section
6
using the connection with loop homology of polyhedral products, see Proposition
6.3
and Theorem
6.5
.
This allows to give a restatement of Conjecture
4.4
in terms of the numbers

, see Proposition
6.8
.

Lemma 6.1

We consider multiindices

is not free. This proves the implication (1)

(3).

Now let

be a chordal graph. The algebra

∎

Note the similar result of Panov and Veryovkin

is chordal graph, and in this case the analogues of GPTW generators freely generate

6.3.
Examples and computations

By Theorem
4.3
, we have an exact sequence of Lie algebras

N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]\overset{\psi}{\longrightarrow}L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\to CL_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\overline{g}_{1},\dots,\overline{g}_{m})\to 0,

where
CL_{\mathbf{k}}(\dots)
is the abelian Lie algebra over a field
\mathbf{k}
. If Conjecture
4.4
holds for
\mathcal{K}
, we get an extension
0\to N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]\to L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})\to CL_{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}(\overline{g}_{1},\dots,\overline{g}_{m})\to 0
.
Then the commutators in
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
can be computed as follows:

(1)

The commutator of
xt^{k},yt^{\ell}\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
is equal to
[x,y]t^{k+\ell}\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
, where
[x,y]
is the commutator in
N_{\mathcal{K}}
;

(2)

The commutator
[\overline{g}_{i},\overline{g}_{j}]
equals to a GPTW generator
[\mu_{i},\mu_{j}]\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
if
\{i,j\}\notin\mathcal{K}
and is zero if
\{i,j\}\in\mathcal{K}
;

(3)

The commutator
[\overline{g}_{i},y]\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
for
y\in\mbox{\it GPTW\/}
can be computed by applying Proposition
3.10
to
a=[\overline{g}_{i},y]
and then applying Proposition
3.2
to the commutators
a^{\prime}_{i}
.

(4)

The commutator
[\overline{g}_{i},y]\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
for arbitrary
y\in N_{\mathcal{K}}
is computed inductively using (3), the Jacobi identity and properties of the operation
h
. (Here we use that
y
is a Lie polynomial on GPTW generators.)

(5)

The commutator
[\overline{g}_{i},yt^{\ell}]\in N_{\mathcal{K}}[t]
for
y\in N_{\mathcal{K}}
equals
[\overline{g}_{i},y]t^{\ell}
, where
[\overline{g}_{i},y]
is computed above.

Even though a multiplication table for
L(\mbox{\it RC\/}_{\mathcal{K}})
can be computed this way, it would be desirable to have a presentation of this Lie algebra by multiplicative generators and relations. Note that
\overline{g}_{1},\dots,\overline{g}_{m}
is a minimal set of generators.

4.4

Example 6.12

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a disjoint union of two points. Then the set

Research trends and hot topics of radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis: a bibliometric analysis and visualization study from 2015 to 2025
Authors: Yuxin Zhong, Yiren Wang, Li Zhang, Hao Wang, Kaili Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundRadiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis (RIPF) is a common complication in patients with thoracic malignancies undergoing radiotherapy, characterized by poor prognosis and limited therapeutic options. In recent years, the study of RIPF has attracted more attention. However, this area lacks a bibliometric analysis. This study uses bibliometric analysis to examine evolving trends and core themes in RIPF research from 2015 to 2025, while forecasting future research directions.MethodsThis paper conducted a search in the Web of Science and Scopus databases for publications on RIPF from 2015 to 2025. Bibliometric and visualization analyses were carried out using VOSviewer, CiteSpace, and the “bibliometrix” package in R, with particular attention to research output by countries/regions, institutions, authors, journals, and keywords.ResultsA total of 542 articles were identified in this study, with a steady increase observed in annual publications. China was the most productive country, and the United States was the core of international cooperation; the University of Maryland was the institution with the most publications and citations; the International Journal of Radiation Oncology Biology Physics published the most papers. Keyword analysis indicates a shift from experimental exploration toward clinical optimization. Recently, epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) has emerged as a prominent focus of research.ConclusionsResearch on the prevention and treatment of RIPF holds significant clinical relevance and promising prospects. Future studies are expected to identify key regulatory nodes, refine intervention pathways, and facilitate the efficient translation of mechanistic insights into therapeutic strategies.

Introduction

Radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis (RIPF) is a common and severe late complication after radiotherapy for thoracic malignancies, essentially a process of progressive scarring caused by aberrant repair of lung tissue following radiation injury (1). Its pathogenesis is complex, encompassing pathological processes such as chronic inflammation triggered by radiation damage, aberrant activation of fibroblasts, and excessive collagen deposition (2). Clinically, approximately 20% of patients receiving radiotherapy for lung cancer develop radiation-induced lung injury, a proportion of whom will progress to irreversible pulmonary fibrosis, with poor prognosis and a marked reduction in quality of life (3).

At present, there is no specific and effective treatment for RIPF. Clinical management mainly relies on glucocorticoids and other symptomatic therapies, which offer limited efficacy and are associated with notable adverse effects (4). Therefore, how to prevent and mitigate the occurrence of RIPF has become a focus of basic and clinical research in radiation oncology. Bibliometrics combined with visualization analysis is a useful tool for unveiling scientific hotspots and trends (5). Conducting bibliometric analysis in the RIPF domain can identify core research topics and frontier dynamics, thereby providing important references for subsequent studies. However, there are still no relevant studies that provide a systematic summary of the developments in this area.

Given the growing importance of treating RIPF, this study extracted academic literature and employed bibliometric and visualization methods to analyze the research hotspots and development trends of this field. Through bibliometric analysis, this study hopes to assist in analyzing the development trends of this topic and provide a scientific basis for future research directions and decision-making. We present this article in accordance with the BIBLIO reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0493/rc).

Methods

Data source and search strategy

A comprehensive search was performed in the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) and the Scopus database, as illustrated in Figure 1. The search strategy can be found in the Tables S1,S2, yielding a total of 981 results.

Workflow of this study.

Inclusion/exclusion criteria

Literature inclusion criteria: (I) studies mainly focused on RIPF; (II) articles and reviews; (III) the language of the literature was English. The exclusion criteria were: (I) meeting abstracts, news, and proceeding papers, etc.; (II) studies not centered on RIPF.

All identified literature was independently screened by two researchers, and any discrepancies were resolved through a third researcher. In total, 542 publications were included.

Statistical analysis

The analyses were performed using VOSviewer (1.6.20), Citespace (6.2.R3), and the Bibliometrix R package (4.3.2). In this study, keyword emergence, clustering, timeline visualization, and keyword burst analyses were performed using CiteSpace. In the visual maps, node size reflects its occurrence frequency, with larger nodes representing higher citation counts (6). Links between nodes represent collaborative or co-occurrence relationships. A purple outer ring around a node denotes high betweenness centrality (centrality ≥0.1), indicating that the node serves as an important bridge between other nodes (7). Collaborative interactions among countries and regions were visualized by VOSviewer (8). In the visualizations, nodes represent items such as publications, authors, countries, or institutions. The R package “bibliometrix” (version 4.3.2) was applied for all information related to sources, countries, citations, institutions, authors, and journals (9).

Discussion

This study reveals that research on RIPF has grown continuously from 2015 to 2025, reflecting significantly heightened academic attention and resource input. Regarding the distribution of countries and regions, China ranked first in publication output, while the USA exhibited the highest total link strength in international collaboration networks, indicating that China and the United States have played a significant role in advancing scientific progress in this area. From the research institutions, the University of Maryland leads the field with 22 publications and the highest total citation count, reflecting its academic influence. In the analysis of journal distribution, International Journal of Radiation Oncology Biology Physics is the most published journal in this field, leading in both publication output and citation impact, followed by the International Journal of Radiation Biology. Researchers are encouraged to follow the latest publications in these journals.

Keywords serve as a concise summary of the central concepts within the literature, highlighting research hotspots and emerging trends in a scientific domain (11). The keyword analysis indicates that RIPF research has evolved from an early emphasis on radiation injury and fibrotic pathology toward a more integrated framework that combines molecular mechanisms, imaging evaluation, and translational intervention. Mechanistically, RIPF is now widely understood as the endpoint of a dynamic continuum involving epithelial and endothelial injury, persistent oxidative stress, inflammatory amplification, fibroblast activation, and extracellular matrix remodeling (12). Within this framework, the transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β) remains a central profibrotic hub, but it is no longer viewed as the only organizing axis. Rather, current evidence emphasizes multicellular crosstalk involving alveolar epithelial cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, macrophages, and senescent cell populations, rather than converging on a single pathway (13,14).

The clustering results suggest that RIPF research is increasingly centered on the interaction between chronic injury signaling and dysregulated repair. Reviews and experimental studies consistently show that radiation exposure induces DNA damage, mitochondrial dysfunction, reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, inflammatory cytokine release, and profibrotic signal transduction through pathways including TGF-β/Smad, NF-κB, PI3K/AKT, and MAPK (1,15). In parallel, macrophages have emerged as active contributors rather than passive inflammatory responders. Su et al. demonstrated that ionizing radiation can induce macrophage senescence and senescence-associated secretory phenotypes, thereby sustaining a profibrotic microenvironment in RIPF (16). Similarly, increasing attention has been given to alveolar type II epithelial cells, whose senescence is now regarded as a pivotal event in impaired regeneration and progressive fibrosis (17,18).

This study also found an increasing intersection between toxicity prediction and functional imaging-guided planning. Prospective evidence suggests that avoiding high-function lung regions identified by 4DCT ventilation or perfusion imaging is feasible and may reduce clinically relevant pulmonary toxicity (19,20). Reviews of functional lung avoidance planning have further suggested that this strategy is likely to become more important as imaging pipelines mature and treatment planning systems become more adaptive (21). These developments are especially important in light of the ongoing emergence of highly conformal and ultra-high-dose-rate modalities. Previous studies of FLASH proton radiotherapy have shown reduced induction of inflammatory and profibrotic pathways compared with conventional proton delivery (22), and ultra-high-dose-rate proton systems have also demonstrated normal tissue sparing in lung fibrosis models while preserving antitumor efficacy (23). Overall, Functional lung avoidance has become a major translational direction.

The analysis of emerging keywords further delineates frontiers in this field. Several studies support the view that EMT-related signaling participates in RIPF progression (24,25). Qu et al. showed that blocking TBK1 alleviated RIPF and reversed EMT-associated phenotypic changes in alveolar epithelial cells, suggesting that EMT-related programs can contribute directly to radiation-driven fibrotic remodeling (26). In addition to epithelial plasticity, endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) has also been implicated in RIPF. Choi et al. reported that hypoxia-induced EndMT contributes to RIPF development through HIF-1α-dependent signaling (27), and Nam et al. subsequently showed that pharmacologic inhibition of HIF-1α could attenuate RIPF in vivo (28). Collectively, these findings indicated that mesenchymal transition programs from epithelial or endothelial origins are biologically relevant in RIPF.

However, the role of EMT in pulmonary fibrosis remains controversial. One school of thought argues that EMT is a true fibrogenic driver that contributes directly to the myofibroblast pool. Another school argues that its contribution has often been overstated, particularly if EMT is interpreted as complete lineage conversion from epithelial cells to matrix-producing fibroblasts. Hill et al. emphasized that epithelial cells undergoing EMT may promote fibrosis predominantly through aberrant epithelial-fibroblastic crosstalk rather than through large-scale direct transdifferentiation into fibroblasts (29). Likewise, Tan et al. suggested that EMT contributes to pulmonary fibrosis but is unlikely to be the major direct source of fibroblasts in vivo (30). More recent conceptual reviews on organ fibrosis have therefore shifted toward the notion of partial EMT, in which injured epithelial cells lose epithelial stability, acquire mesenchymal features, and amplify profibrotic signaling without necessarily completing full myofibroblastic conversion (31).

The keyword timeline map suggests that RIPF research is becoming increasingly clinical intervention-oriented. Pirfenidone has shown preclinical efficacy in radiation-induced lung fibrosis, partly by reducing M2 macrophage polarization and suppressing TGF-β1 signaling (32). Mesenchymal stem cells and related cell-based approaches have also attracted growing attention because of their anti-inflammatory, antioxidant, anti-EMT, and regenerative properties (33). At the same time, senescence-targeted therapy has emerged as a favorable direction in the field. Pan et al. demonstrated that the senolytic agent ABT-263 selectively cleared senescent type II pneumocytes and reversed persistent RIPF in mice (34). Additionally, metabolic modulation has become another promising field. Metformin has been reported to attenuate RIPF through suppression of profibrotic signaling and inflammatory pathways (35). However, these therapeutic directions should be interpreted with caution. Therefore, the current therapeutic landscape should be viewed as promising but still exploratory, with the next major challenge being the integration of mechanistically informed therapies into risk-adapted clinical designs.

There are some limitations to this study. First, only English-language publications were included, which may have led to the omission of relevant studies published in other languages, particularly since China was the leading contributor. Expanding the language scope in future work could improve the completeness and accuracy of the findings. Second, the literature retrieval strategy was primarily dependent on titles, abstracts, and keywords, which may not fully reflect the depth and breadth of research in this field. Future research can expand the number of studies reviewed by conducting a more thorough search.

Conclusions

Overall, this study offers a comprehensive overview of research progress on RIPF, providing valuable insights into the influential countries, institutions, and authors in the field. Concurrently, this study highlighted key high-impact terms and predicted research hotspots and trends in the field. In the future, research priorities may involve in-depth exploration of integrated strategies that combine advanced radiotherapy planning and therapeutic interventions, thus benefiting more thoracic radiotherapy patients.

Independence relations in the Solovay model I 2020 AMS subject classification 03E25, 03E30.
Jindřich Zapletal

Abstract
I provide a novel axiomatization of the Solovay model as a tool for a purely geometric treatment of the theory of balanced forcing.



1
Introduction

The Solovay model
[
11
]

[
1
, Chapter 26]

[
9
, Chapter 8]
is one of the most thoroughly studied objects in set theory. It was developed by Solovay as a model of choiceless set theory in which all sets of reals are Lebesgue measurable. Its usual construction provides a way of analyzing its theory in great detail. However, this approach uses forcing in a way which makes proofs rather complex and repetitive at the same time.

In
[
7
]
, Paul Larson and the present author developed a flexible theory of forcing extensions of the Solovay model called balanced forcing. The theory uses the standard approach to the Solovay model and results in rather repetitive and unwieldy proofs. The purpose of this series of papers is to provide a “geometric” axiomatization of the Solovay model, and then develop a novel presentation of the theory of balanced forcing in the Solovay model in a similar spirit. The word“geometric” here is to suggest a parallel with geometric model theory and the use of independence relations between models as in
[
6
]
. The axiomatization provided is in no sense complete, but it enables a novel and nearly forcing-free style of arguments. In this paper, I prove older results about the Solovay model such as measurability of all sets of reals (
[
11
]
, Corollary
5.5
), the Ramsey property of subsets of
[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
(
[
8
]
, Corollary
5.6
), nonexistence of MAD families (
[
13
,
8
]
, Theorem
4.2
), nonexistence of uncountable sequences of Borel sets of bounded complexity (
[
12
]
, Theorem
4.7
) or a version of the Silver dichotomy (
[
12
]
, Theorem
6.3
) from this axiomatic basis, all with short, forcing-free and even descriptive set theory-free arguments. In the continuation, I provide a geometric presentation of balanced forcing which is methodologically much simpler than that of
[
7
]
; in addition, it applies to much broader class of posets and does away with the needless complexity calculations which plague most of that book.

As for the structure of this paper, in Section
2
I discuss the language of definability. Section
3
isolates the properties of an independence relation needed for the analysis of the Solovay model, remarkably similar to forking independence. In Section
4
, I isolate the inaccessibility axiom and show that in conjunction with the independence axiom, it has many mathematical consequences; in particular, it implies that no MAD families exist. In Section
5
, I consider the axiom stating that every set of reals has the Baire property. In the presence of the independence relation, this is a strong statement, implying in particular that all sets of reals are Lebesgue measurable, and that the independence relation is essentially mutual genericity. In Section
6
, I consider a Gandy–Harrington type of a game and use the associated axiom to prove the Silver dichotomy. This is one axiom which is not geometric in nature, but it provides quick and clutter-free proofs of several dichotomy theorems. Finally in Section
7
, I discuss various slightly different types of the Solovay model and show that the (usual version of the) Solovay model satisfies all axioms on the list.

2
The definability axiom

The basic axiomatic framework for this paper is ZF+DC, where DC stands for the axiom of dependent choices. The first additional axiom, without which the independence axiomatization has no teeth, is the definability axiom.

Definition 2.1
.

The
definability axiom
is the statement that every set is definable from a set of ordinals.

By “definable”, I mean definable in the universe of sets using the first order language with the membership relation.

I use this opportunity to set up some notation. If
x
is a set of ordinals then the symbol
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
denotes the class of all sets which are definable from
x
and an additional finite tuple of ordinals. The symbol
\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
denotes the transitive part of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
; that is, the class of all sets
z
such that the transitive closure of
z
is a subset of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The following are standard facts.

Fact 2.2
.

Let
x
be a set of ordinals.

1.

there is a class well-ordering on
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
definable from
x
;

2.

\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
is a model of ZFC.

I will abuse the above notation a bit. I will identify sets of ordinals and finite tuples of sets of ordinals. Thus, if
x
and
y
are sets of ordinals, then
\mathrm{OD}_{xy}
stands for the class of all sets definable from
x
,
y
, and an additional finite tuple of ordinals. Also, if
y\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, I will write
\mathrm{OD}_{xy}
as if
y
is a set of ordinals:
y
can be identified with the set of the indexes of its elements in the canonical definable well-order of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Another similar shorthand appears when
Z
is a Hausdorff space with a well-orderable basis
\mathcal{B}
, and
z\in Z
is a point. If
x
is a set of ordinals such that
Z,\mathcal{B}\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, then
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
will hold as well, by Corollary
3.7
below. In such a situation, the point
z
can be identified with the set
\{O\in\mathcal{B}\colon z\in O\}\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, and in this sense I will use the notation
\mathrm{OD}_{xz}
.

The following easy proposition will be used to compactify notation in many arguments of this paper.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
x
be a set of ordinals and
A
be a set. The following are equivalent:

1.

A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
;

2.

there is a function
F
definable from a finite tuple of ordinals such that
x\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
and
F(x)=A
.

Proof.

The (2)
\to
(1) implication does not need an argument. For the opposite direction, suppose that
\phi(v,x,\bar{\alpha})
is a formula with one free variable displayed and additional parameters
x
and a finite tuple
\bar{\alpha}
of ordinals, such that
\phi
has only one solution and the solution is
A
. Define the (class) function
F
by
F(y)=B
if the formula
\phi(v,y,\bar{\alpha})
has only one solution and the solution is
B
, and
F(y)=0
if the formula
\phi(v,y,\bar{\alpha})
has no solutions or more than one solutions. It is clear that
F
works as desired.
∎

3
The independence axiom

The most important contribution of this paper is the axiomatization of set theoretic independence and its uses. It will turn out much later that the independence relation is necessarily essentially equal to mutual genericity (Corollary
5.8
), but one can use the independence relation successfully in arguments axiomatically without ever considering any forcing language.

Definition 3.1
.

A (set theoretic)
independence relation
is a ternary class relation
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
on sets of ordinals satisfying the following properties:

1.

(symmetry)
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
implies
y_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{0}
;

2.

(heredity) if
x,y_{0},y_{1},y^{\prime}_{0}
are sets of ordinals and
y^{\prime}_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
then
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
implies
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
;

3.

(transitivity)
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
and
z\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{xy_{0}}}y_{1}
implies
y_{0}z\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
;

4.

(duplication) if
x
is a set of ordinals and
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is a nonempty set of sets of ordinals, then there are
y_{0},y_{1}\in A
such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
holds;

5.

(product) if
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
holds,
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, and
\langle y_{0},y_{1}\rangle\in A
holds, then there are
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
-sets
B_{0},B_{1}
such that
y_{0}\in B_{0}
,
y_{1}\in B_{1}
, and for every
y^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{0}
and
y^{\prime}_{1}\in B_{1}
,
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}
implies
\langle y^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{1}\rangle\in A
.

The duplication item may be satisfied in a trivial way: if
A
has a
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
element
y
(for example, if
A
is a singleton), then
y_{0}=y_{1}=y
is going to work. The product item says that any property of an independent pair is implied by some properties of its constituents plus the independence.

Definition 3.2
.

The
independence axiom
asserts that there is a class independence relation
\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits}
definable from no parameters.

In this section, I will derive a couple of basic consequences of the independence axiom which will make it easier to use it in applications. All statements are proved in ZF+DC+definability+independence. In the statement of the duplication and product property, the set
A
can be replaced by a
class
definable from
x
and a finite tuple of ordinals without changing the truth value of the statement.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any sets
x,y
of ordinals,
y\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}x
holds.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that this fails for some
x,y
. Then the class
A=\{y^{\prime}\colon y^{\prime}
is a set of ordinals and
y^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}x
fails
\}
is nonempty and definable from
x
. Use the duplication property to find
y^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{1}\in A
such that
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}
holds. Use the symmetry and heredity to conclude that
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}x
. This contradicts the choice of
y^{\prime}_{0}
.
∎

The duplication property is typically used via its following apparent strengthening:

Proposition 3.4
.

If
x
is a set of ordinals,
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
is a nonempty set of sets of ordinals, and
y_{0}
is another set of ordinals, then there is
y_{1}\in A
such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
holds.

Proof.

Suppose that this fails for
x
and
A
as witnessed by a counterexample
y_{0}
. Pick an ordinal
\alpha
such that
y_{0}\subset\alpha
. Then the class
B
of counterexamples which are subsets of
\alpha
is definable from
x
, and it is nonempty. The class
B\times A
is nonempty, so by the duplication axiom applied to it there are pairs
\langle y_{0},z_{0}\rangle
and
\langle y_{1},z_{1}\rangle
in
B\times A
such that
y_{0}z_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}z_{1}
. The heredity property applied twice then shows that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}z_{1}
, contradicting the choice of
y_{0}
.
∎

The product property has a number of immediate consequences which are going to be used repeatedly throughout this paper.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
and
A_{0}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}
and
A_{1}\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
are disjoint subsets of
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
then there is a set
D\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that
A_{0}\subseteq D
and
A_{1}\cap D=0
.

Proof.

Let
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
be
\mathrm{OD}
functions such that
F_{0}(x,y_{0})=A_{0}
and
F_{1}(x,y_{1})=A_{1}
. Using the product property, let
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
be
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
sets such that
y_{0}\in B_{0}
,
y_{1}\in B_{1}
, and for every
y^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{0}

F_{0}(x,y_{0})\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds, for every
y^{\prime}_{1}\in B_{1}

F_{1}(x,y^{\prime}_{1})\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds, and
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}
implies
F_{0}(x,y^{\prime}_{0})\cap F_{1}(x,y^{\prime}_{1})=0
. Let
D=\bigcup\{F_{0}(x,y^{\prime}_{0})\colon y^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{0}\}
. This is clearly a
\mathrm{OD}_{x}

A_{0}
as a subset. Suppose towards a contradiction that
A_{1}\cap D
is nonempty, containing some element
C
. Consider the nonempty
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
set
B^{\prime}_{1}=\{y^{\prime}_{1}\in B_{1}\colon C\in F_{1}(x,y^{\prime}_{1})\}
. Let
y^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{0}
be an element such that
C\in F_{0}(x,y^{\prime}_{0})
. Use Proposition
3.4
to find
y^{\prime}_{1}\in B^{\prime}_{1}
such that
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}
. Then
C\in F_{0}(x,y^{\prime}_{0})\cap F_{1}(x,y^{\prime}_{1})
, contradicting the choice of the sets
B_{0},B_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
implies
\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}=\mathrm{OD}_{x}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{0}}\cap\mathrm{OD}_{xy_{1}}
is a set; I must show that
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds. Let
F_{0},F_{1}
be
\mathrm{OD}
functions such that
A=F_{0}(x,y_{0})=F_{1}(x,y_{1})
. Use the product property to find
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
-sets
B_{0},B_{1}
such that
y_{0}\in B_{0}
,
y_{1}\in B_{1}
, and for all
y^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{0}
and
y^{\prime}_{1}\in B_{1}
,
y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}
implies
F_{0}(x,y^{\prime}_{0})=F_{1}(x,y^{\prime}_{1})

Let

y_{0}y^{\prime}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y^{\prime}_{1}

∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If
x

A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}

1.

A

2.

A\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
.

Let
B=\{y\colon y

\mathrm{OD}_{y}
contains a well-ordering of
A\}
. Note that if
y\in B
, then
A\subset\mathrm{OD}_{y}
. By the duplication property, I can find sets
y_{0},y_{1}\in B
such that
y_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y_{1}
. By Proposition

,

. As

and

.

Let

\bar{u}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}\bar{u}_{1}

I first prove a variation on the transitivity property.

In many arguments, one needs to assume that the

be arbitrary and use Proposition

to find

such that
f\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}z

is equivalent to

as required. Now suppose that

The two inclusions are both proved in the contrapositive. Suppose that

does not belong to the intersection on the right-hand side, as witnessed by some

does not belong to the left-hand side as witnessed by

Suppose that

does not belong to the left-hand side, and let

be a witness:
f\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}z

of

. Now use the product property to find

-sets

and

,

, and for all

and

The conjunction of the two claims shows that there is an injection of the collection of

sets in

and

-codes in

of cardinality

-sequence of

for some set

, but by the inaccessibility axiom and the first sentence of this paragraph there are only countably many options for such Borel sets.
∎

5
The Baire axiom

The inaccessibility axiom still does not provide sufficient control over the models
\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
. The following familiar statement provides plenty of additional requisite strength.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Baire property axiom
asserts that every subset of
\omega^{\omega}
has the property of Baire.

It turns out that in the presence of an independence relation, this axiom is much stronger than may appear on the first sight. In particular, it implies that all sets of reals are Lebesgue measurable and all subsets of
[\omega]^{\aleph_{0}}
have the Ramsey property. All results in this section use the axiomatic basis of ZF+DC plus definability, independence, inaccessibility, and the Baire axiom. All of them are corollaries of the following technical proposition.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
x
be a set of ordinals and
P\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
a well-orderable partial order. For any formula
\phi
and any tuple
\bar{\tau}\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
of
P
-names, and a condition
p\in P
write
p\Vvdash_{x}\phi(\bar{\tau})
if for every filter
g\subset P
generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
containing
p
,
\phi(\bar{\tau}/g)
holds.

Several remarks are in order. First of all, Corollary
3.7
shows that as
P
is well-orderable,
P\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds, and this is the main reason to restrict the definition to well-orderable posets. A filter
g\subset P
is generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
if it meets all dense subsets of
P
which belong to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
; there may not be any such filters. The valuation
\bar{\tau}/g
is defined for any filter on
P
by the usual transfinite recursion formula. The relation
\Vvdash\phi
belongs to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Note also the difference between
\Vvdash
and
\Vdash
:
\Vdash
speaks about satisfaction in the generic extension of
\mathrm{HOD}_{x}
, while
\Vvdash
speaks about satisfaction in the surrounding universe.

Proposition 5.3
.

The definition of
\Vvdash
does not depend on
x
.

That is to say that for all sets
x
of ordinals such that
P,\bar{\tau}\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, the truth value of
p\Vvdash_{x}\phi(\bar{\tau})
is the same.

Proof.

Suppose that this fails, and there is
x_{0}
such that
p\Vvdash_{x_{0}}\phi(\bar{\tau})
fails, and
x_{1}
such that
p\Vvdash_{x_{1}}\phi(\bar{\tau})
holds. Consider the following two nonempty classes definable from
x_{0}
and a finite tuple of ordinal parameters:
B_{0}=\{g\subset P\colon g
is generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{x_{0}}
,
p\in g
holds, and
\phi(\bar{\tau}/g)
fails
\}
and
B_{1}=\{y\colon P,\bar{\tau}\in\mathrm{OD}_{y}
and
p\Vvdash_{y}\phi(\bar{\tau})
holds
\}
. By Proposition
3.4
, there must be
g\in B_{0}
and
y\in B_{1}
such that
g\mathrel{\mathop{\vbox{\hbox{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr\hfil$|$\hfil\cr\kern-3.01385pt\cr$\smile$\cr\kern-1.29167pt\cr}}}}}\displaylimits_{x}}y
. It must be the case that
g\subset P
is generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{y}
: otherwise, there would be a dense set
C\subset P
in
\mathrm{OD}_{y}
such that

, by Proposition
3.5
there would be
D\subset P
in
\mathrm{OD}_{x_{0}}
such that
C\subset D
and

, and this would contradict the genericity of

over
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. Since

, this means that
\phi(\bar{\tau}/g)
holds, contradicting the assumption that

.
∎

As a result, below I drop the subscript from the relation
\Vvdash
. The next proposition shows that
\Vvdash
shares certain features with the forcing relation.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that the poset
P
is countable.

1.

If
p
is a condition and
\phi
is a formula of the forcing language,
p\Vvdash\phi(\bar{\tau})
if and only if for every
q\leq p
there is
r\leq q
such that
r\Vvdash\phi(\bar{\tau})
;

2.

If
p
is a condition and
\phi(\bar{\tau})
is a formula of the forcing language,
p\Vvdash\lnot\phi(\bar{\tau})
if and only if for no
q\leq p
,
q\Vvdash\phi(\bar{\tau})
;

3.

if
p
is a condition and
\phi(\bar{\tau})
is a formula of the form “there is an ordinal
v
such that
\psi(\bar{\tau},v)
”, then
p\Vvdash\phi
if and only if for every
q\leq p
there is
r\leq q
and an ordinal
\alpha
such that
r\Vvdash\psi(\bar{\tau},\check{\alpha})
.

The restriction of the existential quantifier to ordinals is necessary.

Proof.

Towards (1), the left-to-right implication is immediate. For the opposite implication, let
x
be a set of ordinals such that
p,P,\bar{\tau}
are in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. The set
A=\{r\leq p\colon r\Vvdash\phi(\bar{\tau})
is in
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
and it is dense below

. Therefore, every filter

generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
containing

must contains some condition in

. By the definition,
\phi(\bar{\tau}/g)
holds, and (1) follows.

Towards (2), suppose towards a contradiction that it fails. Use (1) to see that there is a condition

such that no condition below
p

\Vvdash
-decides
\phi(\bar{\tau})
. Consider the space

with the usual topology, and the space

of all subsets

which are filters, and in addition for every

is a

is atomless then

is homeomorphic to the Baire space. The topology of

is generated by sets

.

Now, the Baire axiom shows that the set

such that the set

or its complement is comeager in

. Suppose for definiteness that the former is the case. Then there is a countable collection

of dense subsets of

such that every filter

containing

and having nonempty intersection with every set in

belongs to

. Let

be a set of ordinals such that

. The conclusion is that for every filter

and containing the condition

holds, in other words

has been verified. This in turn contradicts the choice of the condition

.

Towards (3), the right-to-left implication is immediate. For the opposite implication, suppose towards a contradiction that it fails, and argue in the contrapositive. Let
x
be a set of ordinals such that
P,\bar{\tau}\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. By (2), for each ordinal
\alpha
, the set

is dense below

, and it belongs to
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
. By the inaccessibility axiom and countability of

, there is a filter

generic over
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
such that

. Since for every ordinal

, it follows from the definitions that

is an ordinal the

fails. Thus,

witnesses the failure of

as desired.
∎

The conclusion of the following corollary is the main point of the ground-breaking work of Solovay

Corollary 5.5

Let

Let

For every set

6
The Gandy–Harrington axiom

The previous axioms do not seem to address generalizations of any dichotomies such as the Silver dichotomy to all sets, even though suitable generalization are well-known to hold in the Solovay model
[
2
,
4
]
. There is a simple forcing-free axiom which holds in the Solovay model and seems to imply them all. It is motivated by the proofs of effective descriptive set theory as presented in
[
3
]
.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
x
a set of ordinals and
A\in\mathrm{OD}_{x}
be a nonempty set. The
Gandy–Harrington game
on
x
and
A
is played between Players I and II, who alternately play nonempty
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
-sets
A_{n}\subset A_{0}
such that
A=A_{0}\supseteq A_{1}\supseteq\dots
. Player II wins if the intersection
\bigcap_{n}A_{n}
is nonempty.

Definition 6.2
.

The
Gandy–Harrington axiom
is the statement that for every set
x
of ordinals and every nonempty
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
set
A
, Player II has a winning strategy in the Gandy–Harrington game on
x
and
A
.

The results below are proved in the theory ZF+DC plus the definability, independence, and Gandy–Harrington axioms. The take-home message is that these are pure fusion-type proofs, with no forcing and no reflection theorems appearing anywhere in them. The first result is a variation on the Silver dichotomy
[
10
]
, proved in the Solovay model by Stern
[
12
]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
E
be an equivalence relation on
2^{\omega}
. Either the set of all
E
-equivalence classes is well-orderable, or there is a perfect set of
E
-inequivalent elements.

The theorem is a corollary to a more technical general proposition.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let

be a set of ordinals and

be a
\mathrm{OD}_{x}

contains a perfect subset of elements which are in finite tuples independent over

.

If
A\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
holds, then the first alternative occurs. If
A\not\subset\mathrm{OD}_{x}
, then replacing
A
with
A\setminus\mathrm{OD}_{x}
if necessary, I may assume that
A
contains no
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
elements. By recursion on
n\in\omega
build

•

nonempty
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
subsets

for

such that all elements of
A_{t}
share the same initial segment of length
n
;

•

for each nonzero
m\in\omega
, each

and each injective
m
-tuple

a nonempty
\mathrm{OD}_{x}
-set
B_{\bar{u}}\subset\{\bar{y}\in A^{m}\colon\bar{y}
is independent over

and

and a winning strategy

for Player II in the Gandy–Harrington game associated with

and

;

•

for each nonzero
m\in\omega
, each

, each injective
m
-tuple
\bar{u}\in 2^{k}
, and each
m
-tuple
\bar{v}\in 2^{n}
such that

a play

against the strategy

of length at least

Existence and non-existence of rational elliptic curves with prescribed torsion subgroups over quadratic fields
Ömer Avci

Abstract
. Let K = ℚ ⁡ ( − p ) K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}) be a quadratic field for an odd prime p p . We show that there exist infinitely many primes p p for which no elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} has torsion subgroup ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ × ℤ / 2 ​ N ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z} over K K for N = 5 , 6 N=5,6 . Using these results, we obtain new torsion classification results over Kummer extensions of cyclotomic fields and over composites of ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -extensions of number fields, refining and extending our previous work.



1.
Introduction and Notation

Let
K
denote a number field, and let
E
be an elliptic curve over
K
. The Mordell-Weil theorem states that the set of
K
-rational points on
E
forms a finitely generated abelian group. More precisely, denoting
E(K)
as the set of
K
-rational points on
E
, we have

E(K)\cong E(K)_{\text{tors}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{rk}(E(K))}.

Here,
\operatorname{rk}(E(K))
is a nonnegative integer called the rank, and
E(K)_{\text{tors}}
is a finite group known as the torsion subgroup of
E
over
K
.

Classification of torsion subgroups
E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}
is an ongoing problem.
Mazur’s celebrated result solved the problem for
K=\mathbb{Q}
, and many results are
known for other fields. There are several variants of the problem, for instance,
whether one considers elliptic curves defined over
K
itself or over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Chou classified the torsion subgroups
E(\mathbb{Q}^{\mathrm{ab}})_{\mathrm{tors}}

for elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}

[
7
]
.
We studied the torsion subgroups

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}
for all primes
p
, obtaining stronger results
for certain families of primes
[
1
]
. At the time, our sharpest classification result
was the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(A., Theorem 1.1,
[
1
]
)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
p>3
be a prime such that
p-1
is not divisible by
3
,
4
, or
5
. Then
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}
is either one of the groups from Mazur’s theorem, or one of the following groups:

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z},\quad\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z},\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover,
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}=E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\text{tors}}
holds.

In the same paper, we provided explicit examples realizing

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
for
p=59
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}
for
p=47
.
For the case
\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}
, we could not find any examples, and were unable to
show that it is not realized for any prime
p
. In June 2025, Derickx
[
8
, Theorem 5.2]
proved
that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\text{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}

is not possible for any prime
p
and elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
. This shows that, excluding
\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}
, the statement of our theorem is best possible.

The question of determining for which primes
p

the groups
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}

are realized for
N=5,6

remains open. In this paper, we prove that for certain families of primes there are no rational elliptic curves with the desired torsion, and for certain families of primes we conjecture, and prove conditionally on the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group of rational elliptic curves, that there exist infinitely many rational elliptic curves with the desired torsion. Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p\equiv 3,7\pmod{20}
be a prime. Then there does not exist an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}

Moreover,
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}
is isomorphic to one of the groups in Mazur’s theorem or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\equiv 11\pmod{24}
be a prime. Then there does not exist an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}.

Moreover,
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}
is isomorphic to one of the groups in Mazur’s theorem or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
p\equiv 83,107\pmod{120}
be a prime. Then there does not exist an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

for
N=5
or
N=6
. Moreover,
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}
is isomorphic to one of the groups in Mazur’s theorem.

From this corollary, we obtain an infinite family of primes for which the
possible groups appearing as
E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}
are exactly the
15

groups listed in Mazur’s theorem. Moreover, thanks to the work of Guzvić and Krijan,
this also allows us to classify
E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))_{\mathrm{tors}}
, since they
proved that for all primes
p>3
,

E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}}))_{\mathrm{tors}}=E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}.

We also leave the following questions open:

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
p\equiv 11,19\pmod{20}
be a prime. Then, there exists infinitely many elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
, satisfying

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}.

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
p\equiv-1\pmod{24}
be a prime. Then, there exists infinitely many elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
, satisfying

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}.

The classification of torsion subgroups
E(K)_{\text{tors}}
for elliptic curves
defined over quadratic fields
K
was completed by Kamienny
[
16
]

and by Kenku and Momose
[
18
]
. In addition, the classification
of possible torsion subgroups
E(K)_{\text{tors}}
arising from elliptic curves

E/\mathbb{Q}
was carried out by Najman
[
22
]
. In these
classifications, one encounters torsion groups that do not appear in Mazur’s
theorem. However, although it is known that such groups are realized over some
quadratic fields, a complete description of which quadratic fields admit which
torsion structures is still unknown.

This problem has attracted the attention of several authors in recent years. Notably, Banwait and Derickx studied related questions
[
5
]
, and we investigated further aspects
[
1
,
2
]
. As another example, Ejder
[
11
]
generalized Fujita’s
[
12
]
classification of torsion subgroups over the maximal quadratic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
to the cases of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
, relying on the fact that the classification of torsion over cyclotomic quadratic fields had previously been completed by Najman
[
21
]
. Extending such classifications to more general quadratic fields may therefore lead to further generalizations of Ejder’s results.

In Section
2
, we give a brief review of the
2
-descent
method and of results related to quadratic points on the modular curves

X_{1}(2,10)
and
X_{1}(2,12)
. These two modular curves have genus
1
, and hence
are elliptic curves themselves. We study the ranks of their quadratic twists
and identify infinite families of quadratic twists for which the rank is zero,
which implies that there are no rational elliptic curves with the desired
torsion over the corresponding quadratic fields. Finally, we explain how to
explicitly construct rational elliptic curves with the desired torsion over
quadratic fields for which the rank is positive.

In Section
3
, we discuss a result of Monsky
[
20
]
,
which proves the parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves, conditional on
the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group. We then
prove our conjectures conditionally on the parity conjecture, or under the
assumption that the
2
-primary part of
\Sha
is finite, an assumption which
implies the parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves.

In Section
4
, we refine and extend several of the
classification results obtained earlier in
[
1
]
.

Finally, in Section
5
, we establish new classification
results related to the problems studied earlier
[
1
,
2
]
, using
the results proved in Section
2
and the methods developed
and refined in Section
4
, together with recent results
from the literature.

Throughout this paper, elliptic curves are identified using their LMFDB
labels.

In several parts of our analysis, we rely on computations performed with

SageMath
. These codes are publicly available on GitHub at

https://github.com/omeravci372742/torsion-subgroups-sagemath-2
.

2.
Finding Quadratic Points of two Modular Curves

In this section, we study the problem of finding quadratic points on the
modular curves
X_{1}(2,10)
and
X_{1}(2,12)
. These two modular curves have genus

1
and hence are elliptic curves themselves. We begin with an important
theorem stating that all non-cuspidal quadratic points, more precisely, the
points of
X_{1}(2,10)(K)
and
X_{1}(2,12)(K)
for a quadratic field
K
that are
not cusps, are non-torsion. This theorem also involves the modular curve

X_{1}(11)
, which likewise has genus
1
and is an elliptic curve.
Since we have already established that
X_{1}(2,10)
,
X_{1}(2,12)
, and
X_{1}(11)
are
elliptic curves, we denote their quadratic twists by
d
as
X_{1}(2,10)_{d}
,

X_{1}(2,12)_{d}
, and
X_{1}(11)_{d}
, respectively, for any nonzero integer
d
.

Using this result, we reduce the problem to
computing the ranks of the quadratic twists of these modular curves. We then
apply the method of
2
-descent to carry out this computation for certain
families of quadratic twists. In our calculations, we follow the notes of

[
6
]
, and we are grateful to Martin Bright for his clearly
written exposition.

Theorem 2.1

(Najman, Kamienny, Theorem 2-8-9-15,
[
17
]
)
.

If
X_{1}(11)
,
X_{1}(2,10)
or
X_{1}(2,12)
possess a non-cuspidal quadratic point, then
that point has infinite order.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let us denote the modular curve
X_{1}(11)
by the elliptic curve

X_{1}(11):y^{2}-y=x^{3}-x^{2}.

Let
d
be an integer. If
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})))=0
, then there is no elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfying

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}.

If
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})))\geq 1
, then there exist infinitely many elliptic curves

E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfying

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}.

The same statement holds for the torsion structures

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
and

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}
if we replace

X_{1}(11)
by the elliptic curves associated to the modular curves

X_{1}(2,10)
and
X_{1}(2,12)
, respectively, given by

X_{1}(2,10):y^{2}=x^{3}+x^{2}-x,\qquad X_{1}(2,12):y^{2}=x^{3}-x^{2}+x.

Proof.

We know that the non-cuspidal
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
-points of
X_{1}(11)
correspond to elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
satisfying
E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/11\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
X_{1}(11)
is an elliptic curve, we have

X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))\cong X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})))}

by the Mordell-Weil theorem. By Theorem
2.1
,
X_{1}(11)
has no non-cuspidal quadratic torsion points. Therefore, it has a non-cuspidal quadratic point if and only if its rank
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})))
is positive; in this case, it has infinitely many points.

By using the equality

\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})))=\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}))+\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)_{d}(\mathbb{Q})),

we can reduce the problem to determining whether
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))
is positive or zero, because it can be readily verified from LMFDB that
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(11)(\mathbb{Q}))=0
, which is also evident from Mazur’s theorem.

A similar argument applies to
X_{1}(2,10)
and
X_{1}(2,12)
, since

\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,10)(\mathbb{Q}))=\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,12)(\mathbb{Q}))=0

according to LMFDB.
∎

In the case
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,10)_{d})(\mathbb{Q})=0
(respectively for
X_{1}(2,12)
), there do not exist elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
with the desired torsion. This also implies that no rational elliptic curves with this torsion.

If, on the other hand,
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,10)_{d})(\mathbb{Q})\geq 1
(respectively for
X_{1}(2,12)
), there exist infinitely many elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
with the desired torsion. The following theorems demonstrate that, under these conditions, there also exist infinitely many rational elliptic curves with the same torsion structure.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d
be an integer, and let
X_{1}(2,10)_{d}
denote the
d
-quadratic twist of the modular curve
X_{1}(2,10)
. If
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,10)_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 1
, then there exist infinitely many elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
with

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{rk}(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 1
, there exist infinitely many rational solutions to the equation

dy^{2}=x^{3}+x^{2}-x.

In
[
15
, Theorem 3.2]
, it is shown that if
d=d(t)=8t^{3}-8t^{2}+1
with
t\in\mathbb{Q}
, and if
E
is the elliptic curve defined by

E:y^{2}+(1-c)xy-by=x^{3}-bx^{2},

where

b=\frac{t^{3}(2t^{2}-3t+1)}{(t^{2}-3t+1)^{2}},\quad c=-\frac{t(2t^{2}-3t+1)}{t^{2}-3t+1},\quad t\neq 0,\frac{1}{2},1,

then the torsion subgroup of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
.

A change of variables
x=2t-1
shows that a rational solution
(x,y)

to the first equation determines a rational value of
t
. This rational
t

in turn produces rational values of
b
and
c
, so that the corresponding
elliptic curve
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Moreover,
E
has the desired
torsion subgroup over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{dy^{2}})
, which coincides with

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
.

Since the rank is positive, there are infinitely many distinct rational
solutions
(x,y)
to the elliptic curve equation. Distinct
x
-values
correspond to distinct
t
-values, which in turn give distinct

j
-invariants. Therefore, we obtain infinitely many distinct rational
elliptic curves. This follows because each solution corresponds to a
distinct quadratic point on the modular curve
X_{1}(2,10)
, and each such
quadratic point corresponds to a different elliptic curve with the desired
torsion.
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
d
be an integer, and let
X_{1}(2,12)_{d}
denote the
d
-quadratic twist of the modular curve
X_{1}(2,12)
. If
\operatorname{rk}(X_{1}(2,12)_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))\geq 1
, then there exist infinitely many elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
with

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Proposition
2.3
,
[
15
, Theorem 3.3]
states that if
d=d(t)=\frac{t^{2}-1}{t^{2}+3}
with
t\in\mathbb{Q}
, and if
E
is the elliptic curve defined by

E:y^{2}+(1-c)xy-(c+c^{2})y=x^{3}-(c+c^{2})x^{2},

where

c=\frac{1-t^{2}}{t^{4}+3t^{2}},\quad t\neq-1,0,1,

then the torsion subgroup of
E
over
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
is
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}
.

In this case, obtaining
t
from a rational solution of

dy^{2}=x^{3}-x^{2}+x,

which is the
d
-quadratic twist of
X_{1}(2,12)
, is slightly more subtle. Following
[
15
]
, we may replace
d(t)
by
(t^{2}-1)(t^{2}+3)
without changing the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
. This leads to the hyperelliptic curve

dz^{2}=(t^{2}-1)(t^{2}+3),

for which we seek rational solutions
(t,z)
. This hyperelliptic curve is birationally equivalent to our elliptic curve via

t=\frac{x+1}{x},\quad z=\frac{y}{x^{2}}.

Consequently, rational solutions to the elliptic curve correspond to rational elliptic curves with the desired torsion.

Since different
x
-values yield distinct
t
-values, they produce distinct
j
-invariants, giving infinitely many distinct rational elliptic curves. This is because each solution corresponds to a distinct quadratic point on
X_{1}(2,12)
, which in turn corresponds to a distinct elliptic curve with the desired torsion.
∎

Before starting our next proofs, which rely heavily on
2
-descent, we give a brief recap of the method following the notes of Bright
[
6
]
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. If
E
admits a rational
2
-isogeny, then it must have a non-trivial
2
-torsion point. Our main goal is to compute the quotient
E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})
, which determines the rank
\operatorname{rk}(E(\mathbb{Q}))
once
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is known.

Assume that
E(\mathbb{Q})[2]
is non-trivial. Then
E
can be written in the form

E:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+ax+b),\quad a,b\in\mathbb{Q}.

With this form, we have the following lemma, which constructs an explicit
2
-isogeny from
E
to another elliptic curve
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists an elliptic curve
E^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}
and an isogeny
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
with kernel
\{O,(0,0)\}
. Specifically,
E^{\prime}
is given by

E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+a^{\prime}x+b^{\prime}),\quad a^{\prime}=-2a,\;b^{\prime}=a^{2}-4b,

and
\phi
is defined by

\phi(x,y)=\Bigl(x+a+\frac{b}{x},\;y-\frac{by}{x^{2}}\Bigr),

for all
(x,y)\in E(\mathbb{C})
with
(x,y)\neq(0,0)
, which is mapped to
O
.

From
E^{\prime}
we can define another isogeny
\phi^{\prime}
and elliptic curve
E^{\prime\prime}
:

E^{\prime\prime}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+a^{\prime\prime}x+b^{\prime\prime}),\quad a^{\prime\prime}=-2a^{\prime}=4a,\quad b^{\prime\prime}=(a^{\prime})^{2}-4b^{\prime}=16b.

The curve
E^{\prime\prime}
is isomorphic to
E
via
x\mapsto 4x
and
y\mapsto 8y
. This provides the dual isogeny
\hat{\phi}:E^{\prime}\to E
given by

\hat{\phi}(x,y)=\Bigl(\frac{1}{4}\bigl(x+a^{\prime}b^{\prime}/x\bigr),\;\frac{1}{8}\bigl(y-b^{\prime}y/x^{2}\bigr)\Bigr).

The kernel of
\hat{\phi}
is
\{O,(0,0)\}\subseteq E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
, and the composite
\hat{\phi}\circ\phi:E\to E
equals the multiplication-by-
2
map.

Next, we study the image of
E(\mathbb{Q})
in
E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
. In particular, we are interested in the quotient
E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})/\phi(E(\mathbb{Q}))
. To compute this, we define the following map:

Lemma 2.6
.

Define
\varphi:E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\to\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
by

\displaystyle\varphi((x,y))

\displaystyle=\overline{x},\quad x\neq 0,

\displaystyle\varphi((0,0))

\displaystyle=\overline{a^{2}-4b},

\displaystyle\varphi(O)

\displaystyle=\overline{1}.

Then
\varphi
is a homomorphism of groups, and the sequence

E(\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\phi}E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})\xrightarrow{\varphi}\mathrm{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}}

is exact. In particular,

\phi(E(\mathbb{Q}))=\ker\varphi,\quad\text{and}\quad E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})/\phi(E(\mathbb{Q}))\cong\varphi(E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})).

Thus, computing the quotient
E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})/\phi(E(\mathbb{Q}))
reduces to finding the image of
E^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q})
in
\mathrm{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}}
. The following lemma gives an explicit criterion:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
r
be a square-free integer. Then
\overline{r}\in\mathrm{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}}
lies in the image of
\varphi
if and only if the equation

r^{2}l^{4}+a^{\prime}rl^{2}m^{2}+b^{\prime}m^{4}=rn^{2}

has a non-zero solution
(l,m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Moreover, this can occur only if
r\mid b^{\prime}
, and one may assume
\gcd(l,m)=1
.

We now present the last piece of the puzzle for computing
E(\mathbb{Q})/2E(\mathbb{Q})
. After this, we turn to calculations for the modular curves
X_{1}(2,10)
and
X_{1}(2,12)
and their
-p
-quadratic twists.

Lemma 2.8

(Kernel-cokernel sequence)
.

Let
A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C
be homomorphisms of abelian groups. Then the sequence

0\to\ker f\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\ker(g\circ f)\xrightarrow{f}\ker g\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\operatorname{coker}f\xrightarrow{g}\operatorname{coker}(g\circ f)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}\operatorname{coker}g\to 0

is exact.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
E:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+x-1)

be the modular curve
X_{1}(2,10)
, and let
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
be a prime.
Let
E_{-p}
denote the
(-p)
-quadratic twist of
E
, given by

E_{-p}:y^{2}=x(x^{2}-px-p^{2}).

Then the Mordell–Weil rank of
E_{-p}
over
\mathbb{Q}
satisfies

\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))=0\quad\text{if }p\equiv 2,3\pmod{5},

and

0\leq\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq 1\quad\text{if }p\equiv 1,4\pmod{5}.

Proof.

Applying
2
-descent via the
2
-isogeny between
E_{-p}
and
E^{\prime}_{-p}
as
described in Lemma
2.5
, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{-p}

\displaystyle:y^{2}=x(x^{2}-px-p^{2}),

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{-p}

\displaystyle:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+2px+5p^{2}).

To determine the image
\varphi(E^{\prime}_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))
in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
, we need to solve the
equation

r^{2}l^{4}+2prl^{2}m^{2}+5p^{2}m^{4}=rn^{2},

where
r
divides
5p^{2}
. Since
r
is assumed to be square-free, it suffices to consider

r=\pm 1,\;\pm 5,\;\pm p,\;\pm 5p.

-1

in

divides
-p^{2}

similarly to the definition of

divisible by
8
, making
pv^{2}
divisible by
64
, while the left-hand side is not. Contradiction.

If
q
divides
l
, then we write
l=qt
and
k=qK
, giving

(qt^{2}+m^{2})(qt^{2}-3m^{2})=pK^{2}.

Then
q\nmid m
. The gcd of the two factors is either
1
(if one of
t,m
is odd and the other even) or a power of
2
(if both are odd). Moreover, modulo
q
, the left-hand side is
-3m^{4}
, which is not a quadratic residue since
q\equiv 2\pmod{3}
, and
q\nmid K
, so
p
is not a quadratic residue modulo
q
. By quadratic reciprocity,
q
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
, so
-q
is not, and
p
must divide the second factor.

We then have

qt^{2}+m^{2}=u^{2},\quad qt^{2}-3m^{2}=pv^{2}

or

qt^{2}+m^{2}=2u^{2},\quad qt^{2}-3m^{2}=2pv^{2}.

The second option is impossible since
q\nmid m
and
2
is not a quadratic residue modulo
q
.

For the first option, at least one of
t
or
m
is odd. Checking modulo
4
and
8
leads to contradictions in all subcases (either
t
even or
m
even), forcing both
t
and
m
to be odd. Then
u
and
v
are even. Writing
u=2U
and
v=2V
, we obtain

4m^{2}+pv^{2}=u^{2}\quad\implies\quad m^{2}+pV^{2}=U^{2}.

Parametrizing this with
\gcd(\alpha,\beta)=1
and both
\alpha,\beta
odd, we get

qt^{2}=u^{2}-m^{2}=(\alpha^{2}+p\beta^{2})^{2}-\alpha^{2}\beta^{2},

which is a contradiction modulo
8
.

Thus, both
r=p
and
r=-p
cases do not work. Therefore, the image
\varphi(E_{d}^{\prime}(\mathbb{Q}))
contains exactly two elements. Using the kernel-cokernel sequence given in Lemma
2.8
, we conclude that
\operatorname{rk}(E_{d}(\mathbb{Q}))=0.

∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
p
and
q
be distinct primes satisfying
p,q\equiv 11\pmod{24}
. Then there does not exist an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
such that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pq}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}.

3.
Conditional Proofs of our Conjectures

In this chapter, we give proofs of Conjectures
1.5
and
1.6
,
conditional on the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich
group
\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})
for rational elliptic curves. We are grateful to
Dokchitser
[
10
]
for his notes on the parity conjecture.

Before using the results on the parity conjecture to prove our conjectures
conditionally, we recall some known results about the parity conjecture.
Firstly, we give a brief overview following the introduction of Radziwill and
Soundararajan
[
23
]
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with conductor
N
.
Write the associated Hasse-Weil
L
-function as

L(E/\mathbb{Q},s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}},

where the coefficients are normalized such that the Hasse bound reads
|a(n)|\leq d(n)
for all
n
,
and so the center of the critical strip is
s=1/2
. Recall that
L(E/\mathbb{Q},s)
has an analytic continuation
to the entire complex plane and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(E/\mathbb{Q},s)=w(E/\mathbb{Q})\Lambda(E/\mathbb{Q},1-s),

where
w(E/\mathbb{Q})
, the root number, is
\pm 1
, and

\Lambda(E/\mathbb{Q},s)=\Big(\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2\pi}\Big)^{s}\Gamma(s+\tfrac{1}{2})L(E/\mathbb{Q},s).

Throughout this section, let
d
be a fundamental discriminant coprime to
2N
, and
let
\chi_{d}=\left(\frac{d}{\cdot}\right)
denote the associated primitive quadratic character.
Let
E_{d}
denote the quadratic twist of
E
by
d
. The twisted
L
-function is

L(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q},s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{a(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{s}}.

If
\gcd(d,N)=1
, then
E_{d}
has conductor
Nd^{2}
, and the completed
L
-function

\Lambda(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q},s)=\Big(\frac{\sqrt{N}|d|}{2\pi}\Big)^{s}\Gamma(s+\tfrac{1}{2})L(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q},s)

is entire and satisfies the functional equation

\Lambda(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q},s)=w(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q})\Lambda(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q},1-s),

with

w(E_{d}/\mathbb{Q})=\chi_{d}(-N)w(E/\mathbb{Q}).

Note that the sign of the functional equation determines the order of vanishing of the

L
-function at
s=1
, i.e., the analytic rank of the elliptic curve. It is a standard result that

(-1)^{\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E(\mathbb{Q}))}=w(E/\mathbb{Q}),

where
\operatorname{rk^{an}}
denotes the analytic rank. The parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves states that

(-1)^{\operatorname{rk}(E(\mathbb{Q}))}=w(E/\mathbb{Q}),

so that

\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E(\mathbb{Q}))\equiv\operatorname{rk}(E(\mathbb{Q}))\pmod{2}.

This relationship between the analytic rank and quadratic twists allows us to determine
when the rank is odd or even in our applications. For the elliptic curves
X_{1}(2,10)
and

X_{1}(2,12)
, a standard
SageMath
calculation shows that their algebraic rank is zero.
Under the parity conjecture, this implies that their analytic rank is even; indeed, the
LMFDB confirms that their analytic rank is zero. By calculating
\chi_{d}(-N)
for the discriminants

d
of interest, we can determine the parity of the algebraic rank conditionally on the parity conjecture.
Here,
N=20
and
N=24
, respectively.

We will show that
\chi_{d}(-N)=-1
for the
d
given in Conjectures
1.5
and
1.6
,
conditionally implying that the algebraic rank is odd. Previously, we showed that the algebraic
rank is either
0
or
1
in these cases. Therefore, we do not need to assume the full Birch
and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, which asserts equality of the algebraic and analytic ranks.
Instead, it suffices to assume the parity conjecture, which asserts equality of the parities of
these ranks.

Monsky
[
20
]
proved the parity conjecture for all rational elliptic curves,
conditional on the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of the Tate–Shafarevich group

\Sha(E/\mathbb{Q})
. Dokchitser and Dokchitser
[
9
]
proved the
parity conjecture for elliptic curves over number fields
E/K
, conditional on the finiteness
of the
2
- and
3
-parts of
\Sha(E/K(E[2]))
, where
K(E[2])
denotes the extension of
K

obtained by adjoining the coordinates of the
2
-torsion points of
E
. In our setting, we
do not assume the extra finiteness of the
3
-part, since we can reduce the problem
to determining the parity of the rank for rational elliptic curves.

Therefore, in our application, we can prove our conjectures conditionally either by assuming
the parity conjecture, or the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of
\Sha
, which implies the parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves.

Proposition 3.1

(Conditional on the Parity Conjecture)
.

Let
p\equiv 11,19\pmod{20}
be a prime. Let

E:y^{2}=x(x^{2}+x-1)

be a model for the modular curve
X_{1}(2,10)
. Then
\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))=1.

Proof.

The conductor of
E
is
N=20
. Since
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, the discriminant of the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})
is
d=-p.

We have
w(E/\mathbb{Q})=1
, so to show
w(E_{-p}/\mathbb{Q})=-1
, it suffices to check that

\chi_{-p}(-20)=-1.

Recall that the primitive quadratic character is the Kronecker symbol. Since
20=2^{2}\cdot 5
and
p
is odd, we have

\Big(\frac{-p}{-20}\Big)=\Big(\frac{-p}{-5}\Big),

and

\Big(\frac{-p}{-5}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{5}\Big),

because
-p<0
.

By assumption,
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{5}
, so
-p
is a quadratic residue modulo
5
. Therefore,

\chi_{-p}(-20)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{5}\Big)=-1.

This shows that
\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))

is odd. Moreover, by Proposition
2.9
, we know that

0\leq\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq 1.

If we assume the parity conjecture, or the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of
\Sha
, which implies the parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves, then for rational elliptic curves,

\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\equiv\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\pmod{2}.

Since
\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))
is odd and the rank is at most
1
, it follows that

\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))=1.

Combining this with Proposition
2.3
gives the desired result.
∎

Proposition 3.2

(Conditionally on Parity Conjecture)
.

Let
p\equiv 23\pmod{24}
be a prime. Let

E:y^{2}=x(x^{2}-x+1)

be the model for the modular curve
X_{1}(2,12)
. Then
\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))=1.

Proof.

The conductor of
E
is
N=24
. Since
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, the discriminant of the quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})
is
d=-p.

We know that
w(E/\mathbb{Q})=1
, so in order to show
w(E_{-p}/\mathbb{Q})=-1
, it suffices to check that

\chi_{-p}(-24)=-1.

The primitive quadratic character is the Kronecker symbol, so we calculate

\Big(\frac{-p}{-24}\Big)=\Big(\frac{-p}{-6}\Big),

and

\Big(\frac{-p}{-6}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{6}\Big),

since
-p<0
. The assumption
p\equiv-1\pmod{3}
implies that
-p
is a quadratic residue modulo
3
, so

-\Big(\frac{-p}{6}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{3}\Big)\Big(\frac{-p}{2}\Big)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{2}\Big).

Finally, the assumption
p\equiv-1\pmod{8}
implies that

\Big(\frac{-p}{2}\Big)=1,

so

\chi_{-p}(-24)=-\Big(\frac{-p}{2}\Big)=-1.

This shows that
\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))
is odd. Moreover, by Proposition
2.13
, we have

0\leq\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\leq 1.

If we assume the parity conjecture, or the finiteness of the
2
-primary part of
\Sha
, which implies the parity conjecture for rational elliptic curves, then for rational elliptic curves we must have

\operatorname{rk^{an}}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\equiv\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))\pmod{2}.

This implies

\operatorname{rk}(E_{-p}(\mathbb{Q}))=1.

Combining this with Proposition
2.4
gives the desired result.
∎

Even though our results are conditional on certain conjectures, we have
exhibited explicit families of quadratic fields over which there exist
infinitely many rational elliptic curves whose torsion subgroups are
isomorphic to

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}.

This, in turn, yields an infinite family of primes
p
for which the
desired torsion subgroups occur over the cyclotomic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}).

4.
Eliminating Possible Torsion

In this section, we present several methods for eliminating torsion subgroups
that cannot be realized. Our main goal is to improve some of the elimination
results obtained earlier
[
1
]
.

We begin with an important lemma. It is a minor improvement of
[
1
, Lemma 3.1]
, and its proof is essentially the same; however, it is necessary for the results in the next section. The only difference is that here we apply the same argument to
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
when considering
the
L
-torsion of
E/K
, instead of
\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q})
when considering the
K
-torsion of
E/\mathbb{Q}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
L/K
be a Galois extension. Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve. Let
q
be an odd prime power, and suppose

E(L)[q]\cong\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}.

Assume that all elements of
\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
have finite order.
For any
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, let
\operatorname{ord}(\sigma)
denote the order of
\sigma
.

(1)

If
\gcd(\phi(q),\operatorname{ord}(\sigma))=1
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, then

E(L)[q]=E(K)[q].

(2)

If
\gcd(\phi(q),\operatorname{ord}(\sigma))\leq 2
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)
, then

E(L)[q]\cong E_{d}(L)[q]=E_{d}(K)[q]

for some
d\in K
satisfying
\sqrt{d}\in L
.

This lemma is necessary to absolve the result from depending on the degree of a
Galois extension and instead state it in terms of the orders of its elements. It
is also useful that we generalize our results to elliptic curves
E/K
over an
arbitrary number field
K
.

The lemma is particularly helpful when dealing with cyclotomic extensions of

\mathbb{Q}
: if
n
is a positive integer not divisible by
16
,
and every odd prime dividing
n
is of the form
4k+3
, then the extension degree

[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}):\mathbb{Q}]
can be divisible by
4
, yet

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{Q})
contains no elements of order
4
.
It is also very useful when considering the compositum of quadratic fields.

In the next two results, we present direct applications of this lemma. It will
also play a key role later in the proof of Theorem
5.3
, and in
generalizing some of the results from
[
2
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
L
be a Galois number field such that

\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
contains no element of order
3
or
4
.
Then

E(L)_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, if
E(L)[13]\cong\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a square-free integer
d
with
\sqrt{d}\in L
such that

E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})[13]\cong\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}.

But
E_{d}
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, and by Mazur’s theorem it cannot have a torsion point of order
13
. This gives a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
n
be a positive integer that is not divisible by
16
, and such that every odd prime dividing
n
is of the form
12k+11
. Then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/13\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, the assumptions imply that the Galois group contains no element of order
3
or
4
. The result then follows from Lemma
4.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
L
be a Galois number field such that

\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb{Q})
contains no element of order
4
or
5
.
Then

E(L)_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.1
, if
E(L)[25]\cong\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a square-free integer
d
with
\sqrt{d}\in L
such that

E_{d}(\mathbb{Q})[25]\cong\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}.

But
E_{d}
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, and Mazur’s theorem rules out torsion of order
25
. This is a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
n
be a positive integer that is not divisible by
16
, and such that every odd prime
p
dividing
n
satisfies
p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
p-1
is not divisible by
5
. Then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/25\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})/\mathbb{Q})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, the assumptions guarantee that the Galois group contains no element of order
4
or
5
. The result then follows from Lemma
4.4
.
∎

We now present two important results that are used frequently in the classification of torsion subgroups.

Theorem 4.6

(Fricke, Kenku, Klein, Kubert, Ligozat, Mazur, and Ogg, among others)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with an
n
-isogeny. Then

n\leq 19\quad\text{or}\quad n\in\{21,25,27,37,43,67,163\}.

Moreover, if
E
does not have complex multiplication, then

n\leq 18\quad\text{or}\quad n\in\{21,25,37\}.

Lemma 4.7

(Chou, Lemma 2.7,
[
7
]
)
.

Let
L
be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
. If

E(L)_{\text{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/mn\mathbb{Z},

then
E
admits an
n
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Next, we present a theorem that allows us to eliminate certain torsion subgroups
when working with cyclotomic extensions
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
. We treated
the case when
n
is prime
[
1
]
. Building upon that, we can now obtain results for
composite
n
. This is an important step for the classification results presented
immediately afterward.

Our result can also be generalized to an arbitrary abelian number field
L
by
considering the minimal positive integer
n
such that
L\subset\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
.
The group structures we eliminate are precisely those that can appear as torsion
subgroups over arbitrary abelian number fields. The complete list is given in

[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
, which follows directly from
[
7

.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
n
be a positive integer. Then:

(i)

If
n
is not divisible by
3
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }N=21,27,

and

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/3N\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }N=1,2,3,

and

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}.

(ii)

If
n
is not divisible by
4
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/4N\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }N=1,2,3,4,

and

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/8\mathbb{Z}.

(iii)

If
n
is not divisible by
5
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }N=15,17,37,

and

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z}.

(iv)

If
n
is not divisible by
3
and
5
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/15\mathbb{Z}.

(v)

If
n
is not divisible by
7
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}\text{ for }N=14,37.

(vi)

If
n
is not divisible by
7
, and
\phi(n)
is not divisible by
3
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/14\mathbb{Z}.

(vii)

If
n
is not divisible by the prime
\ell\in\{11,17,19,43,67,163\}
, then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}))_{\text{tors}}\not\cong\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

Groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/mN\mathbb{Z}
can be eliminated using the Weil
pairing: if an elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
has full

m
-torsion over
K
, then
K
must contain all
m
-th roots of unity.
This argument covers all cases where
m\geq 3
in items (i)–(iii).

Groups of the form
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
can be eliminated as follows. Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an
elliptic curve and
K
a Galois number field. If

E(K)_{\text{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/mN\mathbb{Z},

then

has a rational

-isogeny by
[
7
, Lemma 2.7]
. In the
statement of this theorem, the listed

are special because there are only
finitely many elliptic curves (finitely many rational
j
-invariants, up to
quadratic twist) with rational
N
-isogeny for each
N
. Moreover, the field
of definition of the
N
-isogeny is classified

[
1
, Table 2]
. In particular, the

-coordinates of the kernel points of the

-isogeny are defined over these fields, and this does not change for

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
can contain a torsion point of order

.

These two arguments cover all group structures in the theorem except

. For this case, the extra condition that

is not
divisible by

implies that the extension degree
[\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n}):\mathbb{Q}]
is not
divisible by
3
, so
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
does not contain a cubic subfield. Since

has full
2
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})
, the field of definition of the

-torsion cannot have degree

or

, so at least one

-torsion point

[

, this implies that

has a rational

-isogeny. Combining this with the rational

would
have a rational

-isogeny, which in turn forces

to be divisible by

, as analyzed in

]
. This contradicts the assumption.
∎

5.
More Classification Results

In this chapter, we study torsion subgroups of elliptic curves over certain
metabelian extensions, such as Kummer extensions of cyclotomic fields, building on
and refining the classification results
[
1
]
. We then generalize some
results on
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of number fields
[
2
]
, showing that
torsion over the full
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension is often already defined over the base
field.

In this context, our previous results on the impossibility of realizing the
torsion subgroups
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z}
over
quadratic fields imply that these groups also cannot appear over the corresponding
metabelian or
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions. This connects the explicit elimination results
over quadratic fields to broader infinite abelian extensions and illustrates the
reach of our methods.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Define the field

L=\mathbb{Q}\Big(\{\mu_{p^{\infty}}:p>11\text{ is a prime satisfying }p\equiv 11\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 12)\text{ and }p\not\equiv 1\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 5)\}\Big).

Then
E(L)_{\text{tors}}
is isomorphic either to one of the groups from Mazur’s theorem or to one of the following:

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/10\mathbb{Z},\quad\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/12\mathbb{Z},\quad\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z},\quad\text{or}\quad\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/16\mathbb{Z}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for all cyclotomic fields
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})\subset L
, since
E(L)_{\text{tors}}
is finite
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
, and the field of definition of this torsion subgroup is an abelian number field, so it must be contained in some
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{n})\subset L
.

Let
n

Theorem 5.3
.

Let

be an elliptic curve,

a prime, and

an integer. Then

E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}}))_{\text{tors}}=E(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p}))_{\text{tors}}.

Proof.

The proof consists of two parts. In the first part, we show that
E
cannot
have full
p
-torsion over
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}})
by proving that if
it did, then
E
would have a rational
p
-torsion point, which contradicts
Mazur’s theorem. In the second part, we show that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

or

E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}}))_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2N\mathbb{Z}

for some positive integer
N
such that

N\leq 19,\quad\text{or}\quad N\in\{21,25,27,37,43,67,163\}.

We then show that all of this torsion must be defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
.
Since
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}})
does not contain
\sqrt{-1}
, we know
that
E
cannot have full
4
-torsion over this field by the Weil pairing. Assume
that

E(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}}))[\ell]\cong\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}

for some odd prime
\ell
. Again, by the Weil pairing, our field must contain a
primitive
\ell
-th root of unity, which implies that
\ell=p
.

Next, we show that if
p>7
, then
E
cannot have full
p
-torsion over this
field. From Galois representations, we have the injection

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}).

Combining our assumption with the Weil pairing, we obtain

\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})\subset\mathbb{Q}(E[p])\subset\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}}).

Hence,
\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}
is a quotient of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q})
, which is in turn a quotient of

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{\infty}},d^{1/p^{\infty}})/\mathbb{Q}).

This implies that the order of
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q})
is
(p-1)p^{k}
for some
k\geq 0
.
Since the order of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
is
p(p-1)^{2}(p+1)
, the order of

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q})
must be either
p-1
or
p(p-1)
.

Let

G=\rho(\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}))\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[p])/\mathbb{Q})

be the image of the Galois representation. We use the classification of maximal
subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
due to Serre
[
25
]
, as well as
[
19
, Theorem 7.2]
.

If the order of
G
is not divisible by
p
, then
G\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)\mathbb{Z}
. Let
H
be
the projective image of

in
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. Clearly,
H
is cyclic, so
G
is
contained in a Cartan subgroup. We will show that
G
must in fact be contained in
a split Cartan subgroup.

If the order of
G
is divisible by
p
, then
G
is contained in a Borel subgroup
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. In the first case,
G
is cyclic of order
p-1
, hence abelian.
Then, by
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
, we conclude that
p\geq 7
is not
possible. In the second case, by choosing a suitable basis for the
p
-torsion, we
can write

G\leq\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}:a,b,d\in\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z},\;a,d\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}\Big\}.

If the chosen basis for the
p
-torsion is given by points

, then
the action of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
always sends

to a multiple of itself.
Therefore,
\langle Q\rangle\subset E[p]
is fixed by
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
,
and hence it is the kernel of a rational
p
-isogeny of
E
. This implies that
E

must admit a rational
p
-isogeny, so by Theorem
4.6
we obtain

p\leq 19\quad\text{or}\quad p\in\{37,43,67,163\}.

If

has complex multiplication by Theorem

. Moreover, for each of these primes, there is a unique

j
-invariant corresponding to a rational

-isogeny
[

Checking their geometric endomorphism rings on LMFDB,

From CM theory

, we know that if we adjoin all

We also know that the extension

, or

As a natural consequence of Theorem
5.4
, we can generalize our
results in
[
2
]
. Let
K
be a quadratic field. If
K

is the unique
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
, which is the case we have
already treated.

Now, let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field. In this case, there are infinitely many distinct
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions of
K
, all contained in the compositum

K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=K_{\mathrm{cyc}}K_{\mathrm{anti}},

satisfying

\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}.

Instead of considering a single
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension of
K
, we may work with the entire
field
K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
and classify torsion over it. The following result can be proven in
a manner similar to
[

, but it also follows
directly as a consequence of Theorem
5.4
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and let
p>5
be a prime. Let

K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}=K_{\mathrm{cyc}}K_{\mathrm{anti}}

denote the extension satisfying

\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}/K)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{2}.

Then

E(K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{\mathrm{tors}}=E(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}.

Proof.

Let
n
be the number of roots of unity in
K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. By a similar argument to
[
2
, Lemma 4.7]
, we have

n=\begin{cases}6&\text{if }K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}),\\
4&\text{if }K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1}),\\
2&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Torsion subgroup
E(K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{\mathrm{tors}}
is finite and satisfies

E(K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})_{\mathrm{tors}}\cong\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/mN\mathbb{Z},

where
m\in\{1,2,3,4,6\}
and
N
is one of the integers appearing in Theorem
4.6

[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
.

All
2^{\infty}
-torsion and
3^{\infty}
-torsion is defined over
K

[
2
, Lemma 4.3, 4.4, 4.5]
. By Theorem
5.4
, all
q
-torsion is defined over
K
for any
q
that is a power of a prime greater than
3
and divides
N
.

Therefore, all torsion over
K_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is already defined over
K
, which proves the theorem.
∎

Corollary 5.6

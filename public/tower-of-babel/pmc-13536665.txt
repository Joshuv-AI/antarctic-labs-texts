Chronic hepatitis C infection status among HCV-Ab positive patients above 50 years old in Anhui Province from 2004 to 2022
Authors: Liping Gong, Zeyang Li, Yangguang Wang, Meng Gao, Yongsheng Ye, Ziwei Wang, Seying Dai, Yinguang Fan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo grasp the current HCV infection status and factors among HCV-Ab positive patients above 50 years old in Anhui Province from 2004 to 2022, and to explore the factors that contribute to the development of CHC.MethodsThis study focuses on patients aged 50 and above who were diagnosed with HCV-Ab positive between 2004 and 2022 in the information system of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. The recorded laboratory data (ALT, AST, PLT) were included for subsequent analysis. The overall condition and knowledge level of patients related to hepatitis C were evaluated through a questionnaire survey, and blood samples were collected using nested one-step RT-PCR technology for hepatitis C nucleic acid testing to determine their current infection status. If their HCV antibody test results remained positive for six months or more, they were considered to have CHC. The research subjects were randomly divided into a test set and a validation set in a 7:3 ratio. Lasso regression analysis was then applied to the test set to identify influencing factors, and the robustness of the selected variables in the model was evaluated through ROC curves and statistical indicators. Finally, a multivariate logistic regression model was established with Lasso regression to screen important feature variables as independent variables and CHC infection status as a positive outcome.ResultsA total of 4,558 valid questionnaires were collected. The overall awareness rate regarding the prevention and treatment of hepatitis C was 25.87%, with 1,196 participants (26.24%) testing positive for HCV RNA, and were recorded as CHC patients. The study revealed a statistically significant difference in HCV RNA positivity rates between those who had previously received antiviral therapy and those who had not (χ² = 490.65, P < 0.001). The results of the model, including variables selected by LASSO regression, show an AUC of 0.798 (95% CI: 0.750 ~ 0.805), a sensitivity of 0.685, a specificity of 0.780, a precision of 0.518, and an F1 score of 0.590. Logistic regression showed that subjects who were female (OR = 0.639, 95% CI: 0.547 ~ 0.746), located in the northern region of Anhui Province (OR = 0.551, 95% CI: 0.464 ~ 0.653), and had received antiviral treatment (OR = 0.138, 95% CI: 0.112 ~ 0.171) were at a lower probability of developing CHC.ConclusionsA high prevalence of CHC infection was observed in Anhui Province, whereas awareness and antiviral treatment rates were low among individuals aged ≥ 50 years with previous hepatitis C. Health education should be strengthened to promote HCV RNA screening and antiviral treatment, especially for male patients in southern Anhui who are not receiving antiviral therapy and have elevated AST levels.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12879-026-13652-5.Keywords: Hepatitis C, Lasso regression, Awareness, Models

Introduction

The hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection is a pervasive worldwide public health issue [1], mainly spread through blood transfusions, mother-to-child transmission, sexual contact, and so on. The virus can cause acute infection and chronic hepatitis C (CHC) [2]. And the acute infection is usually asymptomatic and generally not life-threatening. There are approximately 50 million individuals with CHC worldwide, with about one million new infections occurring per year. Since the realization of network reporting for statutory infectious disease reporting (including hepatitis C) in 2004, the number of hepatitis C cases rose more significantly from 2006 to 2010. From 2016 to 2020, this number slowly declined due to the effects of the popularization of HCV RNA testing reagents and the gradual improvement of medical conditions [3]. In 2016, the World Health Organization (WHO) established an overall target to eliminate public health threats associated with viral hepatitis by the year 2030, with a specific target to diagnose 90% of patients with hepatitis C and to treat 80% of them by the same year [4].

According to WHO report, approximately 15% to 45% of HCV-infected individuals will spontaneously clear the virus due to a strong immune response after acute infection, known as self-healing [2]. However, about 55% to 85% of HCV-infected patients are incapable of spontaneously eliminating the virus and progressing to CHC [2]. CHC may persist for years or even decades without obvious symptoms, but long-term infection increases the risk of liver damage, with 15% to 30% of cases progressing to cirrhosis or liver cancer within 10 to 30 years [2]. According to the findings of a survey conducted on a global scale, there was a 75% increase in new cases of hepatocellular carcinoma between 1990 and 2015, of which 21% was attributed to HCV infection [5]. HCV-infected individuals are able to clear the virus from their bodies after treatment with interferons (IFNs) [6] or direct-acting antiviral agents (DAAs) [7]. The cure rate of HCV-infected individuals treated with DAAs can reach 98%, achieving sustained virological response (SVR) [7, 8], which is commonly known as a cure.

Patients who have been infected with HCV and reported to the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) Information System are referred to as previous hepatitis C patients. Among them, there are still a certain number of current HCV RNA patients who carry HCV and continue to be a potential source of infection. Nucleic acid test of previous HCV-infected individuals in Henan Province found that 36.09% of them were positive [9]. According to the hepatitis C epidemic data reported by the China CDC, hepatitis C patients in China are predominantly in the age group of 50 years and above [10]. From 2004 to 2012, the reported incidence of hepatitis C in Anhui Province showed an increasing trend, and the highest incidence rate was found in the elderly population [11]. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate previous hepatitis C individuals aged 50 years and above in Anhui Province, evaluate the current CHC infection status and clinical management indicators (awareness and history of antiviral treatment) among previously reported HCV cases aged 50 years or older in the study setting, thereby the factors that contribute to the development of CHC to support the achievement of the WHO’s 2030 targets.

Methods

Study objects

The cumulative number of hepatitis C cases reported in 95 counties/districts in Anhui Province from January 1, 2004, to December 31, 2022, was downloaded from the China CDC Information System, and the areas with the top 16 cumulative reporting rates in the Province were selected, and 8 of them were selected as the survey sites using a simple random sampling method. Inclusion criteria: (1) current address in the eight surveyed counties/districts sampled; (2) contactable, currently living patients with hepatitis C aged ≥ 50 years; (3) conscious and able to express ideas clearly in plain language; (4) consent to participate in the questionnaire survey and venous blood collection. Exclusion criteria: (1) those who went out during the survey period and could not return to the local area; (2) those who were incapacitated and had no family members to cooperate; (3) Patients with HBV and other viral hepatitis.

Ethical considerations

This study was reviewed and approved by the Biomedical Ethics Committee of Anhui Medical University (Approval No. 83230107). All procedures performed in this study involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional and national research committee and with the 1964 Helsinki Declaration and its later amendments. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Participant data were anonymized to protect privacy and confidentiality.

Survey methodology

The research team held repeated discussions regarding the project implementation plan and the survey questionnaire, and invited experts in field epidemiology and hepatitis C prevention and control to participate in project discussions and revisions to the questionnaire. The research targets were assigned to each community or township based on their current residential address, and trained doctors and nurses as investigators to conduct the questionnaire survey. The clinical data of the study subjects who were first diagnosed with hepatitis C were obtained. The collected serum was subjected to reverse transcription-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) testing to understand the outcome of the study subjects.

Definitions of relevant indicators

In the questionnaire, 12 questions were set on hepatitis C prevention and treatment knowledge. Those who answered 10 or more correctly were considered aware [12]. For study subjects: those who had received liver-protective therapy (traditional Chinese medicine or Western medicine), IFNs or DAAs were considered treated; those not treated before with a negative HCV RNA positive test were defined as self-healed; those treated before with a negative HCV RNA positive test were considered cured; and those with a positive HCV RNA test for ≥ 6 months after a positive HCV RNA test were considered to have CHC [13].

Quality control

The questionnaire has good reliability and validity. The questionnaires of all the participants were divided into two parts according to the odd and even numbers of the questionnaire numbers. The indicator of Spearman-Brown was 0.843, and the Pearson correlation coefficient between the score of hepatitis C-related knowledge and the total score was between 0.7842 and 0.882. Questionnaire survey conducted by trained staff; the questionnaire could only be administered to the study participants after they gave informed consent and signed the questionnaire. After the questionnaire was completed, the questionnaire was reviewed by the research staff, and then the blood specimens were drawn by the medical staff; regularly checking and reviewing the questionnaire program to ensure the quality of the questionnaires and to guarantee the confidentiality of the information obtained.

Statistical methods

A database was established in EpiData 3.1 using double data entry and real-time consistency checks. SPSS version 23.0 was used for descriptive and univariable analyses. Categorical variables were presented as numbers (percentages) and compared using the chi-square test or chi-square test for trend, as appropriate. Continuous variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQRs) and compared using the Mann-Whitney U test. The study population was randomly divided into training and test sets in a 7:3 ratio. Candidate variables were first screened in the training set using LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation to reduce dimensionality and minimize overfitting. Variables with non-zero coefficients were then included in a multivariable logistic regression model to identify factors associated with current HCV RNA positivity, and adjusted odds ratios (OR) with 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. Model performance was evaluated in the test set using the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve, the area under the curve (AUC), sensitivity, specificity, precision, and the F1 score. LASSO regression, multivariable logistic regression, and model evaluation were performed in R version 4.4.2.

Knowledge of hepatitis C prevention and treatment

Among the 4,558 study participants, the overall knowledge of hepatitis C prevention and treatment was 25.87%, and the question “Can hepatitis C be transmitted through sexual intercourse?” had the lowest correct rate (35.17%) and “Can I get hepatitis C by entering blood with hepatitis C virus?” had the highest percentage of correct answers (61.17%). Notably, only 307 participants (6.74%) answered all 12 questions correctly, suggesting that comprehensive knowledge of hepatitis C remained very poor in this population. See supplementary Table 1.

Risk behavior of HCV infection

Of the 4,558 study subjects, 4,164 (91.36%) had at least one HCV-related risk behavior before being informed of a positive HCV RNA test. Regarding the 12 HCV-related risk behaviors, the rate of chronic HCV infection was higher among those who did not have a history of endoscopy, who had a history of selling blood, and who did not have a hepatitis C patient in a close-contact family member, and the differences were statistically significant (all P < 0.05) (supplementary Table 2).

Disease regression in previously hepatitis C antibody-positive individuals

A total of 1,196 CHC patients (26.24%) were detected, 34.95% have been cured, and 38.81% have been self-cured. See Table 2. Among patients with hepatitis C who had received prior antiviral therapy, the positive rate of the current HCV RNA test was 7.76%, whereas among patients with hepatitis C who had not received antiviral therapy, the positive rate of the current HCV RNA test was 37.51%, which was statistically significant (χ² = 490.65, P < 0.001).

Lasso regression analysis

All study subjects were randomly divided into training and test sets in a ratio of 7:3. To avoid multicollinearity among variables, Lasso regression was used to screen the independent variables based on the training set of study subjects, and a total of 27 independent variables were included in the Lasso regression. As the penalty coefficient λ increases, the number of independent variables in the model decreases, and 2 parameters, lambda.min (λ = 0.003) and lambda.1se (λ = 0.020), were identified after ten-fold cross-validation. In this study, based on the regression coefficients under lambda.min taking, seven variables were finally screened out from the 27 variables with coefficients not zero, i.e., gender, age, region, reason for testing, whether or not they had done nucleic acid testing, whether or not they had received antiviral therapy, and AST. Model prediction effect and visualization analysis

To evaluate the robustness of the seven independent variables identified by the Lasso regression model, an LR model was constructed using the test set, and the performance of the predictive model was assessed using ROC curves. The results showed that the AUC value was 0.798 (95% CI: 0.750 ~ 0.805), the sensitivity was 0.685, the specificity was 0.780, the precision was 0.518, and the F1 score was 0.590. Constructing Lasso-logistic regression models

Based on the study population in the training set, a multivariate logistic regression analysis model was established with CHC infection status as the dependent variable (0 = no; 1 = yes) and seven important characteristic variables screened by Lasso regression as the independent variables. The results showed that females (OR = 0.639, 95% CI: 0.547 ~ 0.746), those located in the northern region of Anhui Province (OR = 0.551, 95% CI: 0.464 ~ 0.653), and those who had received antiviral treatment (OR = 0.138, 95% CI: 0.112 ~ 0.171) had a lower risk of CHC infection. In contrast, study participants with passive testing (OR = 1.411, 95% CI: 1.208 ~ 1.647), no nucleic acid testing (OR = 1.490, 95% CI: 1.207 ~ 1.838), and AST (OR = 1.034, 95% CI: 1.030 ~ 1.038) had a higher odds of positivity (Table 3).

Discussion

In this study, HCV RNA positive individuals aged ≥ 50 years in Anhui Province had limited knowledge of hepatitis C prevention and treatment: only 25.87% had adequate knowledge, only slightly higher than the 21.6% reported in the general population of the same age group in Anhui [14]. This highlights the need for targeted health education, especially on non-obvious transmission routes, asymptomatic infection, the lack of a vaccine, and the availability of curative treatment. Before learning their positive HCV RNA results, 91.36% had engaged in at least one HCV-related risk behavior, suggesting gaps in screening, health education, and early intervention. CHC infection was more common among those with no history of endoscopy, a history of selling blood, or no close relatives with hepatitis C (all P < 0.05). The endoscopy finding should be interpreted cautiously, as it may reflect differences in healthcare utilization, sample characteristics, or confounding factors, and its independence and stability require further validation through multivariate analysis. The present survey further showed that 26.24% of participants remained HCV RNA positive at the time of investigation, indicating a CHC status, indicating that a substantial proportion of previously reported HCV RNA positive individuals still had current infection. This proportion was slightly higher than that reported in Fuzhou (23.58%) [15]. In the multivariable analysis, elevated AST was associated with a higher likelihood of current HCV RNA positivity. This finding is biologically plausible because AST elevation may reflect ongoing hepatocellular injury in individuals with persistent infection [16]. However, this association should be interpreted with caution. Information on other chronic liver diseases, such as alcohol-related liver disease, fatty liver disease, and drug-induced liver injury, was not systematically collected in this study, and these conditions may also affect AST levels.

Participants without a history of antiviral treatment were more likely to remain HCV RNA positive, which is consistent with the well-established effectiveness of direct-acting antiviral regimens in routine clinical practice [17, 18]. This result has clear practical implications: previously reported HCV RNA positive individuals who have never received antiviral therapy should be prioritized for reassessment, confirmatory nucleic acid testing, and timely treatment initiation. Public health interventions should focus not only on identifying new infections, but also on re-engaging historically reported cases who may have been lost to follow-up.

The present study found that the risk of CHC infection was higher in men among those with prior hepatitis C infection. The results of several studies [19, 20] suggested that females were associated with a lower prevalence of HCV and a higher rate of spontaneous clearance of the virus, and that males were more likely to develop HCV infection. At the same time, relative to the northern part of Anhui Province, there is a higher risk of regression to CHC infection rates in the southern part of the population. In the mid-1990s, there was an epidemic of Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV) and hepatitis C in the central region of China due to paid blood donation [21], especially in the rural areas of northern Anhui Province and parts of Henan Province [22, 23]. With national policy support [24], Anhui Province has enhanced HIV and hepatitis C prevention and treatment in the north. Now, it’s urgent to strengthen hepatitis C management and prevention in the south, especially through publicity, education, and therapeutic interventions for men aged 50 and above.

The study also found that participants identified through passive testing were more likely to be CHC positive than those identified through active testing. Asymptomatic infection and low testing willingness delay early detection and treatment, increasing transmission risk and likelihood of underdiagnosis. In 2021, the WHO proposed that HCV screening tests could be self-tested by individuals using rapid HCV RNA detection reagents [25], and the OraQuick® HCV self-test kit has a sensitivity and specificity of over 99% [26]. Passive testing widely covers the population, and active testing accurately targets high-risk groups, suggesting that we need to combine population characteristics to formulate a differentiated strategy to increase the population’s willingness to be actively tested.

We found that the rate of conversion to CHC infection was higher in patients with previous hepatitis C who had not been tested compared to study subjects who had a history of previous HCV nucleic acid testing. There may be some high-risk groups among prior hepatitis C patients who voluntarily went for HCV nucleic acid testing, which may be prompted by the presence of a known history of HCV exposure due to medical or behavioral factors [27], suggesting that we can expand our census in the future to screen high-risk groups annually for nucleic acid testing.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings. First, because clinical data were retrospectively extracted from routine hospital records, the available laboratory indicators were limited, and some potentially relevant hematological or biochemical variables were not consistently available across participants. Second, information on other chronic liver diseases was not systematically collected, so these potential confounders could not be excluded or adjusted for in the analysis. Third, this was a retrospective study involving an older population, and self-reported histories of prior testing, treatment, and risk behaviors may have been affected by recall bias. Fourth, the study population was mainly concentrated in northern Anhui, where the historical burden of hepatitis C is relatively high, which may have introduced selection bias and may limit the generalizability of the findings to the whole province.

In summary, the knowledge of hepatitis C prevention and treatment, and the rate of prior antiviral treatment in the population of previous hepatitis C patients aged 50 years and above in Anhui Province are low, and although some previous hepatitis C patients will recover spontaneously, there still exists a certain scale of CHC infection. In the future, focus should be placed on previously and newly reported hepatitis C cases aged 50 years and above. In particular, publicity and education should be strengthened for men, patients not receiving antiviral treatment, patients in southern Anhui Province and patients with elevated AST, to raise their awareness of HCV nucleic acid screening, the dangers of the disease, and treatment options, and to encourage them to take the initiative to undergo nucleic acid screening and subsequent standardized antiviral treatment, so as to curb the transmission of hepatitis C and improve the health status of patients, and to contribute to achieving the overall goal of hepatitis C elimination by 2030.

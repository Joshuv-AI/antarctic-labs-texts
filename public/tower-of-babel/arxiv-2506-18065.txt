Liouville function, von Mangoldt function and norm forms at random binary forms
Yijie Diao

Abstract
. We analyze the average behavior of various arithmetic functions at the values of degree d d binary forms ordered by height, with probability 1 1 . This approach yields averaged versions of the Chowla conjecture and the Bateman–Horn conjecture for random binary forms. Furthermore, we show that the rational Hasse principle holds for almost all Châtelet varieties defined by a fixed norm form of degree e e and by varying binary forms of fixed degree d d , provided e e divides d d . This proves an average version of a conjecture of Colliot-Thélène.

1.
Introduction

Browning, Sofos, and Teräväinen
[
5
]
recently studied the average behavior of arithmetic functions at random polynomials. They proved averaged versions of the Bateman–Horn conjecture, the polynomial Chowla conjecture, and the integral Hasse principle for norm form equations.
The fundamental tool in their work links equidistribution of an arithmetic function in arithmetic progressions to its values over almost all polynomials. In this paper, we extend this framework to random binary forms and obtain analogous results. Notably, we prove the rational Hasse principle for
100\%
of norm form equations.
To rigorously describe the proportion of a certain class of binary forms, we will first define
combinatorial cube
.

Definition 1.1

(Combinatorial cube)
.

Let
d\geqslant 1
and
H\geqslant 1
. For any index set
\mathscr{E}\subset\{0,\dots,d\}
and any integers
\alpha_{e}\in[-H,H]
for
e\in\mathscr{E}
, we call the set

\mathscr{C}=\mathscr{C}(H)=\left\{(c_{0},\dots,c_{d})\in(\mathbb{Z}\cap[-H,H])^{d+1}:c_{e}=\alpha_{e},\forall\,e\in\mathscr{E}\right\}

a
combinatorial cube
of side length
H
and dimension
d+1-\#\mathscr{E}
. We call
c_{d}
the
constant coefficient
of an element
(c_{0},\dots,c_{d})\in\mathscr{C}
. Given a combinatorial cube
\mathscr{C}
, we say a binary form

\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}x^{i}y^{d-i}

has its coefficients in

\mathscr{C}
if
(c_{0},\dots,c_{d})\in\mathscr{C}
.

1.1.
Liouville function and von Mangoldt function on random binary forms

Let

\lambda(-n)=\lambda(n)

Recent progress has been made for certain special classes of polynomials

29

30

Here, instead of polynomials, we investigate the behavior of the Liouville function at random binary forms with integer coefficients.

Theorem 1.2
.

1.2.
Rational Hasse principle for random Châtelet varieties

Let
K/\mathbb{Q}
be a finite extension of degree
e\geqslant 2
and fix a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis
\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{e}\}
for
\mathscr{O}_{K}
. We define the norm form associated to
K
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(\mathbf{y})=\mathbf{N}_{K}(y_{1},\dots,y_{e})=\mathrm{Norm}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(y_{1}\omega_{1}+\dots+y_{e}\omega_{e}).

Let
f(t)
be a polynomial with integer coefficients.
The
Châtelet variety

X_{K,f}
is the affine variety
X\subset\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{e+1}
defined by the equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(\mathbf{y})=f(t)\neq 0.

It is equipped with a dominant morphism
\pi:X\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{1}
.

Let
X^{c}
be a smooth, projective model associated
X
. It is well known that such models may fail to satisfy the Hasse principle or weak approximation. For instance, as observed by Coray (see
[
9
, Eq. (8.2)]
), one may take
f(t)=t(t-1)
and consider the cubic field extension
K=\mathbb{Q}(\theta)
, where
\theta
is a root of the irreducible polynomial
y^{3}-7y^{2}+14y-7
. In this setting, the rational points
X^{c}(\mathbb{Q})
are not dense in the set of 7-adic points
X^{c}(\mathbb{Q}_{7})
. Nevertheless, a conjecture of Colliot-Thélène
[
7
]
proposes that all such failures of Hasse principle for
X^{c}
can be explained via the Brauer–Manin obstruction. Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture implies that given a natural ordering of the coefficients of
f
, the Hasse principle holds with probability
1
. Browning and Matthiesen
[
4
]
proved Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture when
f(t)
is a product of linear polynomials all defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, that the Brauer–Manin obstruction is the only obstruction to the Hasse principle and weak approximation.

Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture extends to more general arithmetic situations, where the base field is a number field
k
. Progress has been made in verifying this conjecture in various settings, often under specific hypotheses on the extension
K/k
and the structure of
f(t)
. For example, the case of Châtelet surfaces, where
[K:k]=2
and
\deg(f(t))\leqslant 4
, has been proved through the work of Colliot-Thélène, Sansuc, and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
10
,
11
]
. Singular cubic hypersurfaces with
[K:k]=3
and
\deg(f(t))\leqslant 3
have also been treated in work by Colliot-Thélène and Salberger
[
9
]
.
Further results include situations where the extension
K/k
is arbitrary and
f(t)
splits over
k
into no more than two distinct roots (see
[
8
,
20
,
25
,
28
]
), as well as the case of an irreducible quadratic
f(t)
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
with
K/\mathbb{Q}
arbitrary (see
[
2
,
13
]
). Várilly-Alvarado and Viray
[
33
]
studied when
K/k
is a cyclic extension of prime degree
p
and
P(x)
is a separable polynomial of degree
2p
. Moreover, assuming Schinzel’s hypothesis, Colliot-Thélène, Skorobogatov, and Swinnerton-Dyer
[
12
]
have verified Colliot-Thélène’s conjecture for cyclic extensions
K/k
and general
f(t)
.

In
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
, Browning, Sofos, and Teräväinen proved that the Châtelet varieties
X_{K,f}
satisfy the integral Hasse principle for 100% of polynomials
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
of degree
d
, with positive leading coefficients. Since the Châtelet varieties are not homogeneous, the above result does not guarantee the rational Hasse principle with probability
1
. For the rational Hasse principle, Skorobogatov and Sofos
[
27
, Theorem 1.3]
have proved the Châtelet varieties

satisfy the rational Hasse principle for a positive proportion of polynomials

of degree
d
.

We study the rational points on the Châtelet variety

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(y_{1},\dots,y_{e})=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}c_{i}t^{i}.

We write

with

and
v\neq 0
, then the equation becomes

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(y_{1},\dots,y_{e})v^{d}=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}c_{i}u^{i}v^{d-i}.

Inspired by
[
33
]
, if we assume that

is a multiple of

, say

, then we get

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(x_{1},\dots,x_{e})=\sum_{0\leqslant i\leqslant d}c_{i}u^{i}v^{d-i},

where

.
By homogeneity,
(\ref{norm-f})
has a rational solution if and only if

has an integer solution with

Let

For fixed

\displaystyle\mathbf{N}_{K}(\mathbf{x})=g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})\neq 0.

For a vector

We now introduce a few definitions for the sets of vectors

\displaystyle S(H)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}:|\mathbf{c}|\leqslant H,\,c_{0}\cdot c_{d}\neq 0\},

\displaystyle S^{\mathrm{loc}}(H)

\displaystyle=\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):\text{(\ref{cha-bino}) is locally solvable}\},

Let

In Proposition

2.
Equidistribution controls sums for almost all binary forms

This section extends the main technical tool from
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
, demonstrating that controlling averages of a general arithmetic function over values of random polynomials can be effectively achieved when the function is well-distributed in arithmetic progressions. We generalize this result from polynomials to binary forms.

For
x>1
, we define
x_{1}=\exp\big(\frac{\sqrt{\log x}}{\log\log x}\big)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A\geqslant 1,\varepsilon>0
and
0\leqslant k<l\leqslant d
be integers. Let
H\geqslant H_{0}(A,d)
,

\exp\Big((\log H)^{1/100}\Big)\leqslant x\leqslant H^{1/(2d)}.

Let
F:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a sequence such that

(1)

|F(n)|\leqslant\tau_{B}(|n|)
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and for some
B\geqslant 1
satisfying
200B^{2d+4}\leqslant A
;

(2)

There exists a set of prime powers
\mathscr{Q}\subset[1,x^{d}]\cap\mathbb{Z}
such that

\sum_{q\in\mathscr{Q}}q^{-1/(4d)}\ll_{A}(\log x)^{-3(A+1)},

and such that for any
q\leqslant x^{d}
that is not a multiple of any element of
\mathscr{Q}
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant v\leqslant q\\
\gcd(v,q)\leqslant x_{1}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\text{ interval}\\
|I|>H^{1-\epsilon}x^{l-k}\\
I\subset[-2Hx^{d},2Hx^{d}]\end{subarray}}\frac{q}{|I|}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in I\\
n\equiv v\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\end{subarray}}F(n)\Big|\ll(\log H)^{-40Ad^{2}}.

Then for any binary form

(2.1)

\displaystyle g=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}x^{i}y^{d-i}\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y]

of degree
d
with
c_{i}\in[-H,H]\cap\mathbb{Z}
such that
c_{0}\cdot c_{d}\neq 0
, and for any coefficients
\alpha_{m,n}\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\alpha_{m,n}|\leqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x^{\prime}\in[x/2,x]\end{subarray}}\,\sum_{|a|,|b|\leqslant H}\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant m,n\leqslant x^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\alpha_{m,n}F(am^{k}n^{d-k}+bm^{l}n^{d-l}+g(m,n))\Big|^{2}\ll H^{2}x^{4}(\log x)^{-A}.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q=p^{e}
be a prime power and fix

(2.2)

\displaystyle\#\{(v_{1},v_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}\cap[1,x])^{2}:q|v_{1}^{a}v_{2}^{b}(v_{1}^{c}-v_{2}^{c})\}\ll\frac{x^{2}}{q^{1/(2\max(a,b,c))}}.

Proof.

Let
d=\gcd(v_{1},v_{2})

v_{1}=du_{1},v_{2}=du_{2}
. We have

v_{1}^{a}v_{2}^{b}(v_{1}^{c}-v_{2}^{c})=d^{a+b+c}u_{1}^{a}u_{2}^{b}(u_{1}^{c}-u_{2}^{c}).

The left hand side of (

\leqslant\sum_{k\geqslant 0}S_{k}

\displaystyle S_{k}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leqslant x\\
v_{p}(d)=k\end{subarray}}\#\{(u_{1},u_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}\cap[1,x/d])^{2}:\gcd(u_{1},u_{2})=1,p^{e}|p^{k(a+b+c)}u_{1}^{a}u_{2}^{b}(u_{1}^{c}-u_{2}^{c})\}.

When

\displaystyle S_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leqslant x\\

\displaystyle\#((\mathbb{N}\cap[1,x])^{4}\setminus\mathscr{M}_{A})\ll x^{4}/(\log x)^{A}.

It is sufficient to show that the set of

For the third property, if we arbitrarily choose

\displaystyle\sum_{|t-n_{1}m_{2}|\leqslant x^{2}/(\log x)^{A+1}}\tau(t)

\displaystyle\ll x^{2}/(\log x)^{A}.

Hence the total possible choices are
\ll x^{4}/(\log x)^{A}.

Finally, it suffices to estimate the size of pairs satisfying the second property that fail the last property. If
\gcd(g(m_{1},n_{1}),m_{1}^{k}n_{1}^{d-l})>x_{2}
, then it follows Lemma

|u|=1
is a closed interval. We denote that

(5.1)

\displaystyle I_{\mathbf{c}}=\{g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}):|\mathbf{u}|=1\}=[b^{-}_{\mathbf{c}},b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}].

Note that if
\mathbf{c}\in S_{+}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H)
, we require
b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}>0
; if
\mathbf{c}\in S_{-}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H)
, we require
b^{-}_{\mathbf{c}}<0
.

We begin with a simple lemma that controls the difference in the maximum (or minimum) of two continuous functions when they differ by a bounded range at each point.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 1
and let
X
be a compact subset in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f,g:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
are continuous functions satisfying

b^{-}\leqslant f(\mathbf{x})-g(\mathbf{x})\leqslant b^{+},\text{ for all }\mathbf{x}\in X.

Then

b^{-}\leqslant\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})-\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}g(\mathbf{x})\leqslant b^{+}\quad\text{ and }\quad b^{-}\leqslant\min_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})-\min_{\mathbf{x}\in X}g(\mathbf{x})\leqslant b^{+}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{y}\in X
, we have

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})\geqslant f(\mathbf{y})\geqslant g(\mathbf{y})+b^{-}\quad\text{ and }\quad\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}g(\mathbf{x})\geqslant g(\mathbf{y})\geqslant f(\mathbf{y})-b^{+}.

Taking the maximum over
\mathbf{y}\in X
gives

b^{-}\leqslant\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}f(\mathbf{x})-\max_{\mathbf{x}\in X}g(\mathbf{x})\leqslant b^{+}.

An analogous argument establishes the bounds for the minima.
∎

The next lemma shows that only a very small number of coefficient vectors
\mathbf{c}\in S(H)
result in the form
g_{\mathbf{c}}
attaining a small maximum (or minimum), in the absolute sense, on the region
|u|=1
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let

(5.2)

\displaystyle\hat{H}=H(\log H)^{-A}.

We have

(1)

\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):0<b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\leqslant\hat{H}\}\ll\hat{H}H^{d}.

(2)

\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):-\hat{H}\leqslant b^{-}_{\mathbf{c}}<0\}\ll\hat{H}H^{d}.

Proof.

We only prove (1); the proof of (2) is similar.

When
d
is odd, we have
g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})=-g_{\mathbf{c}}(-\mathbf{u})
. By considering the four points
(\pm 1,0)
and
(0,\pm 1)
, we know

b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\geqslant\max(\pm c_{0},\pm c_{d})=\max(|c_{0}|,|c_{d}|).

It follows that
\max(|c_{0}|,|c_{d}|)\leqslant\hat{H}
, if
b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\leqslant\hat{H}
. Therefore, we get

\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):0<b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\leqslant\hat{H}\}\leqslant\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\leqslant\hat{H}\}\ll\hat{H}^{2}H^{d-1}\leqslant\hat{H}H^{d}.

Now we assume that
d
is even.
Let

b^{(+,1)}_{\mathbf{c}}=\max\{g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}):|u|=1,|v|\leqslant 1\},\indent b^{(+,2)}_{\mathbf{c}}=\max\{g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}):|u|\leqslant 1,|v|=1\},

so that

(5.3)

\displaystyle b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}=\max(b^{(+,1)}_{\mathbf{c}},b^{(+,2)}_{\mathbf{c}}).

Since
d
is even, writing

g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})=u^{d}+g_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d})}(\mathbf{u})
shows that

\displaystyle g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})-g_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d})}(\mathbf{u})=1,

\displaystyle\text{ for any }\mathbf{u}\text{ with }|u|=1,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})-g_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d})}(\mathbf{u})\leqslant 1,

\displaystyle\text{ for any }\mathbf{u}\text{ with }|u|\leqslant 1.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})-g_{(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}(\mathbf{u})=1,

\displaystyle\text{ for any }\mathbf{u}\text{ with }|v|=1,

\displaystyle 0\leqslant g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})-g_{(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}(\mathbf{u})\leqslant 1,

\displaystyle\text{ for any }\mathbf{u}\text{ with }|v|\leqslant 1.

Thus, by Lemma
5.1
we know

\displaystyle b^{(+,1)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,1)}_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d})}=1,\indent 0\leqslant b^{(+,1)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,1)}_{(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}\leqslant 1,

\displaystyle b^{(+,2)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,2)}_{(c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}=1,\indent 0\leqslant b^{(+,2)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,2)}_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d})}\leqslant 1,

which imply that

\displaystyle 1\leqslant b^{(+,1)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,1)}_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}\leqslant 2,\indent 1\leqslant b^{(+,2)}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{(+,2)}_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1},\dots,c_{d}-1)}\leqslant 2.

Therefore, by Lemma
5.1
and (
5.3
) we have

\displaystyle 1\leqslant b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}-b^{+}_{(c_{0}-1,c_{1}\dots,c_{d-1},c_{d}-1)}\leqslant 2.

If
0<b^{+}_{\mathbf{c}}\leqslant\hat{H}
, then the number of possibilities for the choices of the pair
(c_{0},c_{d})
with fixed
(c_{1},\dots,c_{d-1})
and the difference

is
\ll\hat{H}
.
Since there are

choices for
(c_{1},\dots,c_{d-1})
and

, we deduce that

\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S(H):0<b_{\mathbf{c}}^{+}\leqslant\hat{H}\}\ll\hat{H}H^{d}.

∎

We state

Lemma 5.3

Let

We now state our main result in this section, which shows that the localized counting function provides a good approximation to the global counting function for almost all coefficient vectors

Proposition 5.4
.

be fixed. Assume conditions (

) and (

and

for all

for

6.
Norm forms: the localized counting function is rarely small

Our goal is to show that for almost all coefficient vectors
\mathbf{c}
, the localized counting function
\hat{N}_{\mathbf{c}}(x)
is large. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let

be fixed as in (
5.4
). Take

and assume that conditions (

) and (

where

.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
assuming Proposition

.

Let

and

\displaystyle\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H):N_{\mathbf{c}}(x)\leqslant\frac{x^{2}}{(\log H)^{C}}\right\}\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\tilde{H}\leqslant H\\
\tilde{H}=2^{\alpha}\end{subarray}}E(H,\tilde{H}),

where

\displaystyle E(H,\tilde{H})

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H,\tilde{H}):N_{\mathbf{c}}(x)\leqslant\frac{x^{2}}{(\log H)^{C}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H,\tilde{H}):|N_{\mathbf{c}}(x)-\hat{N}_{\mathbf{c}}(x)|>\delta x^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\#\left\{\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{loc}}(H,\tilde{H}):\hat{N}_{\mathbf{c}}(x)<2\delta x^{2}\right\}.

Using Propositions
5.4
and
6.1
together with (

), thus we deduce that

provided condition (

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\tilde{H}\leqslant 2H\\

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{H}\leqslant\hat{H}\\
\tilde{H}=2^{j}\end{subarray}}\tilde{H}H^{d}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\hat{H}\leqslant\tilde{H}\leqslant H\\
\tilde{H}=2^{j}\end{subarray}}E(H,\tilde{H})

\displaystyle\ll\hat{H}H^{d}+(\log H)\frac{H^{d+1}}{(\log H)^{A+1}}

∎

6.1.
The archimedean densities are rarely small

First, we turn our attention to the archimedean aspect.

For
\mathbf{u}=(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, let
(r,\theta)=(r(\mathbf{u}),\theta(\mathbf{u}))
be its polar coordinates so that

u=r\cos\theta,\quad v=r\sin\theta,\quad\text{ with }r>0\text{ \ and \ }0\leqslant\theta<2\pi.

For
0\leqslant\theta<2\pi
, we also define

g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)=g_{\mathbf{c}}(\cos\theta,\sin\theta).

Since
g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})
is a binary form of degree
d
, we have

g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u})=r^{d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta).

For
\epsilon\in\{\pm\}
and
\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}(H,\tilde{H})
, we choose
\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}}=(u_{\mathbf{c}},v_{\mathbf{c}})
with
|\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}}|=1
satisfying
g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})=b^{\epsilon}_{\mathbf{c}}
. Thus, by the definitions (
5.5
) and (

), we have

\frac{1}{2}\tilde{H}<|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})|\leqslant\tilde{H}.

Let
\theta_{\mathbf{c}}=\theta(\mathbf{u}_{c})
, then
g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})=r(\mathbf{u}_{c})^{-d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})
. Since
1\leqslant r(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})\leqslant\sqrt{2}
, we know

(\sqrt{2})^{-d}|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})|\leqslant|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\leqslant|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\mathbf{u}_{\mathbf{c}})|.

This implies that

(6.1)

\displaystyle(\sqrt{2})^{-d-2}\tilde{H}\leqslant|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\leqslant\tilde{H}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\epsilon=\{\pm\}
and
\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}(H,\tilde{H})
.
Then there exists a small constant

such that if

\Big|r/x-\Big(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{H}/|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\Big)^{1/d}\Big|<\eta^{2}\ \text{ and }\ \left\lVert\theta-\theta_{\mathbf{c}}\right\rVert\leqslant(\log H)^{-2A},

then

\omega(r^{d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta);B)\gg 1.

Here
\left\lVert\theta-\theta_{\mathbf{c}}\right\rVert=\min\{|\theta-\theta_{\mathbf{c}}+2\pi n|:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
4.2
(iv) that there exists
0<\eta\ll 1
such that
\omega(y;B)\gg 1
whenever

If
\big|r/x-\big(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{H}/|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\big)^{1/d}\big|<\eta^{2}
, then

(6.2)

\displaystyle(r/x)^{d}=\frac{\tilde{H}}{2|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|}+O(\eta^{2}),

noting from (

) that we have
g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})\gg_{d}\tilde{H}
.
Moreover, Taylor’s theorem yields

\Big||g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)|-|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\Big|\leqslant|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)-g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\ll\sum_{k\geqslant 1}\left\lVert\theta-\theta_{\mathbf{c}}\right\rVert^{k}\cdot\Big|\frac{\partial^{k}g_{\mathbf{c}}}{\partial\theta^{k}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})\Big|\ll H(\log H)^{-2A}.

Since
r\leqslant\sqrt{2}x
, it follows that

(6.3)

\displaystyle r^{d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)=r^{d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})+O(x^{d}H(\log H)^{-2A}).

Recall from (
5.7
) that
B^{e}=\tilde{H}x^{d}
. It follows from (
6.1
) that

\frac{|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|}{\tilde{H}}\leqslant 1.

By (

) and (

), we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon r^{d}g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)/B^{e}-1/2

\displaystyle=r^{d}|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta)|/B^{e}-1/2

\displaystyle=\frac{2r^{d}|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|-\tilde{H}x^{d}+O(x^{d}H(\log H)^{-2A})}{2\tilde{H}x^{d}}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{\eta^{2}|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|}{\tilde{H}}+\frac{H}{\tilde{H}(\log H)^{2A}}\right)

\displaystyle=O\left(\eta^{2}+\frac{H}{\tilde{H}(\log H)^{2A}}\right),

The right-hand side is

, since

. The statement of the lemma follows by choosing

small enough.
∎

For
\epsilon=\{\pm\}
and
\mathbf{c}\in S_{\epsilon}(H,\tilde{H})
, define

From (

), we know

.
We now introduce the region

. Moreover, by (

\displaystyle\gg\pi\left((r_{0}+\eta^{2}x)^{2}-(r_{0}-\eta^{2}x)^{2}\right)\cdot(\log H)^{-2A}

\displaystyle=4\pi\eta^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tilde{H}/|g_{\mathbf{c}}(\theta_{\mathbf{c}})|\right)^{1/d}x^{2}(\log H)^{-2A}

\displaystyle\gg_{\eta}x^{2}(\log H)^{-2A}.

Recall from (

) the definition of the localized counting function. By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S^{\mathrm{loc}}(H,\tilde{H}):\hat{N}_{\mathbf{c}}(x)<\delta x^{2}\}\leqslant\#\{\mathbf{c}\in S^{\mathrm{loc}}(H,\tilde{H}):\tilde{N}_{\mathbf{c}}(D_{\mathbf{c}})\ll\delta x^{2}\},

where we define

6.2.
Results on binary forms

In this section, we collect several results concerning binary forms that will be needed in the subsequent analysis. For a vector
\mathbf{c}\in(c_{0},\dots,c_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d+1}
, we define its

We define the content of a binary form

We recall the definition (

) of the modulus

. As in

\displaystyle W_{0}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leqslant M_{d,K}\text{ or }p\mid h_{\mathbf{c}}\end{subarray}}p^{k(x)}\quad\text{and}\quad W_{1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{d,K}<p\leqslant w\\

We also introduce

(6.8)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathbf{c}}(W_{0})=\frac{1}{W_{0}^{e+1}}\#\{(\mathbf{x},s,t)\in(\mathbb{Z}/W_{0}\mathbb{Z})^{e+2}:\mathbf{N}_{K}(\mathbf{x})\equiv g_{\mathbf{c}}(s,t)\,(\mathrm{mod}\,W_{0})\}.

\displaystyle P_{\mathbf{c}}(w)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}M_{d,K}<p\leqslant w\\

Let

be a binary form of degree
d
. We say that

is

, if the discriminant of

is non-zero. Let

(6.10)

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}(q)=\#\{(u,v)\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{2}:g(u,v)\equiv 0\,(\mathrm{mod}\,q)\},

for any integer

.
We now prove an upper bound for
\lambda_{g}(p^{k})
when

is separable.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g\in\mathbb{Z}[s,t]
be a binary form of degree
d\geqslant 1
. Assume that
g
is separable. Let
h_{g}
be the content of

and let
\sigma=v_{p}(h_{g}).

(1)

If
\sigma\geqslant k
then
\lambda_{g}(p^{k})=p^{2k}
.

(2)

If
\sigma<k
then

\lambda_{g}(p^{k})\leqslant(d+1)\min\left\{(k+1)p^{k(2-\frac{1}{d})+\frac{\sigma}{d}},p^{2k-1}\right\}.

Proof.

The first statement is trivial. Now we assume that
\sigma<k
.
For a fixed integer
n
, we denote

(6.11)

\displaystyle g_{n}(m)=g(m,n)=c_{0}m^{d}+(c_{1}n)m^{d-1}+\dots+(c_{d-1}n^{d-1})m+c_{d}n^{d},

as a polynomial in
m
with coefficients depending on
n
. Note that when
n
is non-zero, the polynomial
g_{n}
is separable if and only if the binary form
g
is separable.
We know

(6.12)

\displaystyle h_{g_{n}}=\gcd(c_{0},c_{1}n,\dots,c_{d}n^{d})\leqslant h_{g}\cdot n^{d}.

Let
\sigma_{n}=v_{p}(h_{g_{n}})
. According to
[
5
, Lemma 6.1]
, we know
\lambda_{g_{n}}(p^{k})=p^{k}
when
\sigma_{n}\geqslant k
. If
\sigma_{n}<k
, then

(6.13)

\displaystyle\lambda_{g_{n}}(p^{k})\leqslant d\min\left\{p^{k(1-\frac{1}{d}+\frac{\sigma_{n}}{d})},p^{k-1}\right\}.

By using (
6.12
) and the first bound of (
6.13
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}(p^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant p^{k}}\lambda_{g_{n}}(p^{k})

\displaystyle\leqslant d\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant p^{k}}p^{k(1-1/d)+\sigma_{n}/d}

\displaystyle\leqslant d\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant p^{k}}p^{k(1-1/d)+\sigma_{n}/d+v_{p}(n)}

\displaystyle\leqslant d\sum_{0\leqslant v_{p}(n)\leqslant k}p^{k-v_{p}(n)}\cdot p^{k(1-1/d)+\sigma_{n}/d+v_{p}(n)}

\displaystyle=d\sum_{0\leqslant v\leqslant k}p^{k+k(1-1/d)+\sigma_{n}/d}

\displaystyle\leqslant d(k+1)p^{k(2-\frac{1}{d})+\frac{\sigma_{n}}{d}}.

By using the second bound of (
6.13
), we know

\displaystyle\lambda_{g}(p^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant p^{k}}\lambda_{g_{n}}(p^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant n\leqslant p^{k}\\

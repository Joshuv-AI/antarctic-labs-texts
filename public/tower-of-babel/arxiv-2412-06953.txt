Non-Vacuum Solutions in Cotton Theory
Serkan Doruk Hazinedar, Yaghoub Heydarzade, Maryam Ranjbar

Abstract
Cotton Theory (CT) introduces a higher derivative extension of General Relativity (GR) characterized by third-rank field equations. Recently, key distinctions between CT and GR concerning wave and vacuum solutions have been highlighted in [ 1 , 2 ] . In this study, two particular non-vacuum solutions of CT are investigated within its Codazzi formulation. The motivation is to reveal how this theory might account for or adapt to the effects of non-vacuum sources, and whether it can provide new insights into the behavior of both singular and regular black holes in astrophysical contexts. It is shown that CT generalizes the Kiselev and Dymnikova solutions in GR. Some aspects of the generalized solutions, in particular concerning singularities, thermodynamics, and geodesics, are addressed in comparison to GR. Keywords: Cotton Theory, Codazzi formulation, Kiselev solution, Dymnikova solution, Non-singular black hole solutions

Introduction

The Cotton tensor, named after Emile Cotton
[

when

3

[

Recently, a new geometric theory of gravity based on the Cotton tensor was introduced by Harada
[

[

[

. Utilizing this solution,

Quite recently, it has been shown that the CT has identically zero conserved charges for all of its solutions as long as there is no matter at the spatial boundary of spacetime

. This represents that in CT the vacuum without a black hole is degenerate with a spacetime with many black holes.

The original formulation of CT involves third rank tensorial equations with third-order derivatives, making them more challenging to solve than the field equations in GR.
The field equations of CT are derived by requiring that both the second-order Schouten

In the present study, we aim to find non-vacuum solutions in CT akin to Kiselev and Dymnikova solutions in GR.
Our motivation for investigating these solutions within CT is to reveal how this theory might account for or adapt to the effects of non-vacuum sources, and whether it can provide new insights into the behavior of both the singular and regular black holes in cosmological contexts. The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section 2, we give an introduction to CT and its Codazzi formulation. In Section 3, we find Kiselev and Dymnikova-type solutions in CT. In both the cases above,

3

]

Moreover, while the CT formulation’s covariant derivatives obscure the direct relationship between field sources and spacetime geometry, the Codazzi formulation presents the energy-momentum tensor directly in the equations. This facilitates clear physical interpretations and connects CT to GR results. Therefore, we are interested in Codazzi formulation to find non-vacuum solution of CT.

3.1
Generalized Kiselev Solution

In the notable paper

Theorem 1:

CT admits Einstein GR’s non-vacuum static spherically symmetric Kiselev solution. This theory generalizes the Kiselev solution for a single anisotropic fluid source to the solution possessing the line-element of the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-\Phi(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\Phi(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

\displaystyle\Phi(r)=1+\frac{c_{0}}{r}+c_{1}r+c_{2}r^{2}+c_{3}r^{-(1+3\omega)},

(7)

and the energy-momentum tensor with the energy density
\rho(r)
as

\rho(r)=3\omega c_{3}r^{-3(1+\omega)},

(8)

where
d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}(\theta)d\phi^{2}
represents the line-element of unit 2-sphere and
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
are arbitrary constants.

A particular case of the generalized Kiselev solution (

(9)

where

,

, and

in our analysis. It is important to note that Eq.(80) in

Here, we have the following immediate remarks.

4
Conclusion

Recently, critical distinctions between CT, a theory of third-rank tensorial field equations, and GR concerning wave and vacuum solutions have been addressed. The present study aims to reveal how CT might account for or adapt to the effects of non-vacuum sources. In particular, it examines whether CT can offer novel insights into the behavior of singular and regular black holes in astrophysical contexts, in comparison to GR. It is shown that CT
generalizes two particular non-vacuum black hole solutions of GR: Kiselev and Dymnikova solutions. Some aspects of these generalized solutions are as follows.

The generalized Kiselev solution in CT extends the original solution in GR, which describes a black hole surrounded by a single anisotropic fluid source, by introducing two additional terms of geometric origin in the metric function: a linear term
c_{1}r
and a quadratic term
c_{2}r^{2}
. Hence the parameter space of the generalized Kiselev solution in CT can be denoted by
(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\omega)
where they represent the Schwarzscild mass, linear and quadratic terms, the surrounding field and its equation of state parameters, respectively.
Here are our remarks regarding the generalized Kiselev solution:

•

The Kiselev solution in GR corresponding to the quintessence field
(\omega=-\frac{2}{3})
and cosmological constant
(\omega=-1)
emerge naturally as vacuum solutions of CT.

•

The Schwarzschild solution in CT, can be recovered in three cases:
(i)
as a vacuum solution of the CT theory by
choosing
c_{0}=-2m,c_{1}=c_{2}=0
,
(ii)
as a non-vacuum solution of CT by choosing
c_{0}=-2m,c_{1}=-c_{3},\omega=-\frac{2}{3},c_{2}=0
, and
(iii)
as a non-vacuum solution of CT by choosing
c_{0}=-2m,c_{2}=-c_{3},\omega=-1,c_{1}=0
. Here
m
is defined as the Schwarzschild mass.

•

The ADM mass will be asymptotically well-defined and match the
Schwarzschild black hole solution in GR in three cases:
(i)\penalty\ c_{1}=c_{2}=c_{3}=0,(ii)\penalty\ c_{1}=c_{2}=0,\ c_{3}\neq 0,\ \omega=-\frac{1}{3}
, and
(iii)\penalty\ c_{1}=c_{2}=0,\ c_{3}\neq 0,\ \omega\geq 0
.

•

The generalized Kiselev solution suffers from an essential singularity at
r=0
, as in GR.

•

This solution may possess multiple distinct or extremal horizons depending on its parameters
(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},\omega)
. The thermodynamics of an event horizon can be formulated akin to a Reissner-Nordstrom-de Sitter black hole in GR by introducing virtual charges associated with the
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
parameters. The presence of virtual charges generalizes the first law of black hole thermodynamics with a modified Hawking temperature and two charge-induced potential terms.

•

The parameter
c_{2}
in CT does not contribute to null geodesics.
However,
c_{1}
and
c_{3}
parameters, affect both the null and timelike geodesics. Moreover, for null geodesics, the condition
\frac{c_{1}}{c_{3}}=-(1+\omega)3^{-(3\omega+1)}m^{-(3\omega+2)}
provides specific cases in which the circular orbits

coincide exactly with the one in GR, i.e.

. For instance, this occurs when

Similarly, there are also particular cases where the timelike geodesics almost coincide with
r_{c}=3m
in GR for null geodesics. For instance, in small radial distances
r
limit, this occurs for
\omega<-\frac{2}{3},c_{1}=\frac{2m}{L^{2}}
, or for
\omega=-\frac{2}{3},c_{1}+c_{3}=2m
.

Similarly, CT generalizes the Dymnikova solution in GR, which describes a regular black hole with an anisotropic fluid source, by introducing the same additional terms
c_{1}r
and
c_{2}r^{2}
. Hence the parameter space of the generalized Dymnikova solution reads as
(c_{0},c_{1},c_{2},\rho_{0})
where they represent the Schwarzscild mass, linear and quadratic terms of geometric origin, and the core de Sitter energy density parameter, respectively. Some properties of the generalized Dymnikova solution are as follows.

•

Unlike the original Dymnikova solution in GR, the generalized solution in CT possesses two distinct de Sitter regions: the de Sitter core (related to
\rho_{0}

c_{2}

•

The ADM mass is asymptotically well-defined and matches the

c_{1}=c_{2}=0

•

In contrast to GR, the generalized Dymnikova solution in CT has a diverging Kretschmann scalar at

•

The thermodynamics and geodesic structure are akin to the Schwarzschild case in GR. However, the effective mass here is smaller than the Schwarzschild mass in GR.

Appendix I

ds^{2}=-\Phi(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{\Phi(r)}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2},

(83)

where
d\Omega^{2}=d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}(\theta)d\phi^{2}
represents the line-element of unit 2-sphere. One can find the non-zero components of the Ricci tensor,
Ricci scalar, and Einstein tensor as follows

\displaystyle R_{0}^{0}=R_{1}^{1}=-\frac{r\Phi_{,rr}+2\Phi_{,r}}{2r},

\displaystyle R_{2}^{2}=R_{3}^{3}=-\frac{r\Phi_{,r}+\Phi-1}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle R=-\frac{r^{2}\Phi_{,rr}+4r\Phi_{,r}+2\Phi-2}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle G_{0}^{0}=G_{1}^{1}=\frac{r\Phi_{,r}+\Phi-1}{r^{2}},

\displaystyle G_{2}^{2}=G_{3}^{3}=\frac{r\Phi_{,rr}+2\Phi_{,r}}{2r}.

(84)

Consequently, the non-zero components of the Schouten tensors can be obtained
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{0}^{0}=\mathcal{S}_{1}^{1}=-\frac{2r^{2}\Phi_{,rr}+5r\Phi_{,r}+\Phi-1}{3r^{2}},

(85)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{S}_{3}^{3}=-\frac{r\Phi_{,r}+2\Phi-2}{3r^{2}}.

(86)

As the source of field equations, one can consider the energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu}_{\nu}
with the following
non-zero components introduced by Kiselev
[
21
]

\displaystyle T^{0}_{0}=T^{1}_{1}=-\rho,

(87)

\displaystyle T^{2}_{2}=T^{3}_{3}=\frac{(1+3\omega)}{2}\rho,

(88)

where
\omega
is a constant equation of state parameter, and
\rho=\rho(r)

is the energy density function. Hence, the nonzero components of the generalized energy-momentum tensor
{\mathcal{T}^{\mu}}_{\nu}
read as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{0}=\mathcal{T}_{1}^{1}=-\frac{2+3\omega}{3}\rho,

(89)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2}=\mathcal{T}_{3}^{3}=\frac{5+3\omega}{6}\rho.

(90)

The non-vanishing components of Cotton tensor
C_{\mu}^{\nu}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle C^{0}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{1}_{1}=\mathcal{S}^{0}_{0}-\mathcal{T}^{0}_{0}=-\frac{2r^{2}\Phi_{,rr}+5r\Phi_{,r}+\Phi-1}{3r^{2}}+\frac{2+3\omega}{3}\rho,

(91)

\displaystyle C^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{3}_{3}=\mathcal{S}^{2}_{2}-\mathcal{T}^{2}_{2}=-\frac{r\Phi_{,r}+2\Phi-2}{3r^{2}}-\frac{5+3\omega}{6}\rho.

(92)

Hence, substituting these in the Codazzi condition (
3
), one finds the field equations as

\displaystyle-2+2\Phi+r\left[-2\Phi_{,r}+r(-r(2+3\omega)\rho_{,r}+\Phi_{,rr}+r\Phi_{,rrr})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(93)

\displaystyle 2(-1+\Phi)+r\left[-2\Phi_{,r}+r(-9(1+\omega)\rho-r(5+3\omega)\rho_{,r}+\Phi_{,rr}+\{\Phi_{,rrr})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(94)

Then, by using Eq. (
93
), one can obtain
\rho_{,r}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{,r}=\frac{1}{2+3\omega}\left(\frac{2\Phi-2}{r^{3}}-\frac{2\Phi_{,r}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\Phi_{,rr}}{r}+\Phi_{,rrr}\right),

(95)

where
\omega\neq-\frac{2}{3}
. For the ease of calculation, let us define
\gamma(r)=\frac{2\Phi-2}{r^{3}}-\frac{2\Phi_{,r}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\Phi_{,rr}}{r}+\Phi_{,rrr}
.
Hence, taking derivative of both sides of Eq.(
95
), we have

\displaystyle\rho_{,rr}=\frac{1}{2+3\omega}\gamma_{,r},

(96)

where the function
\gamma_{,r}
is

\displaystyle\gamma_{,r}=-6\frac{\Phi-1}{r^{4}}+6\frac{\Phi_{,r}}{r^{3}}-3\frac{\Phi_{,rr}}{r^{2}}+\frac{\Phi_{,rrr}}{r}+\Phi_{,rrrr}.

Meanwhile, by Eq. (
94
), one can obtain
\rho
as

\displaystyle{}\rho=\frac{r}{9(1+\omega)}(\gamma-(5+3\omega)\rho_{,r}),

(97)

where
\omega\neq-1
. Taking the derivative of both sides of Eq. (
97
) reads

\displaystyle{}\rho_{,r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9(1+\omega)}(\gamma-(5+3\omega)\rho_{,r})+\frac{r}{9(1+\omega)}(\gamma_{,r}-(5+3\omega)\rho_{,rr}).

(98)

Now substituting Eq.(
95
) and Eq.(
96
) into Eq.(
98
) reads

\displaystyle{}\frac{1}{3(2+3\omega)(1+\omega)}\left[r\gamma_{,r}+\gamma(4+3\omega)\right]=0.

(99)

Hence, using the definition of
\gamma
function, we have the following fourth
order nonhomogeneous ODE
for
\Phi
function

\displaystyle\Phi_{,rrrr}r^{4}+(5+3\omega)\Phi_{,rrr}r^{3}+(1+3\omega)\Phi_{,rr}r^{2}-2(1+3\omega)\Phi_{,r}r+2(1+3\omega)\Phi=(2+6\omega).

(100)

Note that Eq. (
100
) is an Euler-Cauchy type nonhomogeneous ODE; therefore, one can find its complementary solution (to its associated homogeneous Euler ODE)
by taking
\Phi(r)=r^{n}
where
n\in\mathbb{R}
. This gives the following polynomial
in
n

\displaystyle(n-1)(n+1)(n-2)\left(n+(1+3\omega)\right)=0.

(101)

Consequently, the complementary solution
\Phi_{c}
of Eq. (
100
) is

\displaystyle\Phi_{c}(r)=c_{0}r+\frac{c_{1}}{r}+c_{2}r^{2}+c_{3}r^{-(1+3\omega)},

(102)

where
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are constants. Using the method of undetermined coefficients for the particular solution
\Phi_{p}
to nonhomogeneous Eq. (
100
), one obtains
\Phi_{p}=1
. Hence we have

\displaystyle{}\Phi(r)=1+\frac{c_{0}}{r}+c_{1}r+c_{2}r^{2}+c_{3}r^{-(1+3\omega)},

(103)

and the same energy-momentum tensor with the energy density
\rho(r)
as

\rho(r)=3\omega c_{3}r^{-3(1+\omega)},

(104)

where
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
and
c_{3}
are arbitrary constants.

Appendix II

For the metric (
83
), the Cotton, Ricci, Einstein, Schouten tensors and the Ricci scalar curvature function are the same as those in Appendix I. Considering the Dymnikova’s energy-momentum tensor
T^{\mu}_{\nu}=\operatorname{diag}(-\rho,-\rho,p,p)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{0}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}^{1}=-\frac{2p+\rho}{3},

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{3}^{3}=\frac{p+2\rho}{3}.

(105)

Hence, the non-vanishing components of Cotton tensor
C^{\mu}_{\nu}
can be found as

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}^{1}=\mathcal{S}_{0}^{0}-\mathcal{T}_{0}^{0}=\frac{-1+\Phi-r(\Phi_{,r}+r\Phi_{,rr})}{3r^{2}}+\frac{2p+\rho}{3},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{3}^{3}=\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2}-\mathcal{T}_{2}^{2}=\frac{4-4\Phi-2r\Phi_{,r}+r^{2}\Phi_{,rr}}{6r^{2}}-\frac{p+2\rho}{3}.

(106)

Hence, substituting these in the Codazzi condition (
3

\displaystyle-2+2\Phi+r\left(-2\Phi_{,r}+r(\Phi_{,rr}+r(-2p_{,r}-\rho_{,r}+\Phi_{,rrr}))\right)=0

\displaystyle-2+2\Phi-2r\Phi_{,r}+r^{2}\Phi_{,rr}-2r^{2}(3p+3\rho+r(p_{,r}+2\rho_{,r}))+r^{3}\Phi_{,rrr}=0,

108
), one can obtain

as

\displaystyle p_{,r}=\frac{\gamma}{2}-\frac{3p+3\rho}{r}-2\rho_{,r}.

(110)

Dymnikova’s article gives the equation

respectively.

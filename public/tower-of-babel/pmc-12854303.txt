The effects of team-based and mastery-based learning on the student’s clinical competence and the results of clinical evaluation: A quasi-experimental study among operating room nursing students
Authors: Sina Ghasemi, Behzad Imani, Mohammad Torabi, Erfan Ayubi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:Clinical education is a crucial component of the medical education system, providing essential opportunities to enhance students’ clinical competence and skills. In light of the numerous challenges in the clinical environment of the operating room, this study was conducted to compare the impact of team-based and mastery-based learning methods on the student’s clinical competence and their evaluation.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This quasi-experimental study was conducted on 6th-semester operating room technology students at Hamadan University of Medical Sciences in 2023. In this study, students were selected by the convenience sampling method and were placed in two educational groups (team-based and mastery-based) of 31 people by the matching method. After implementing the training process in the operating room environment, the data related to the study were collected from Liu et al.’s clinical competence questionnaire and clinical assessment checklist of operating room nursing students.RESULTS:Findings showed that implementing the team-based learning method significantly affected students’ clinical competence (P value = 0.012). Also, utilizing a mastery-based learning method compared to a team-based one had a significantly greater impact on the level of clinical skills and evaluation of students (P value = 0.007).CONCLUSION:The results of this study have practical implications for clinical instructors and higher education institutions. Clinical instructors are advised to adopt a team-based learning method to enhance the student’s clinical competence in the operating room. Furthermore, higher education institutions are encouraged to provide adequate resources and make essential efforts to modernize educational methods in clinical environments, thereby empowering students and improving the quality of healthcare.

Introduction

Nowadays, the transition of medical education from traditional methods to more advanced approaches is crucial for increasing students’ skill levels and adequately preparing them to deliver competent care in medical and health centers.[1] However, in the nursing education system, effectively combining basic science knowledge with clinical situations poses a significant challenge for students given factors such as crowded and intricate clinical environments.[23] Despite these challenges, the importance of learning clinical skills cannot be overstated. It is a cornerstone part of the nursing education curriculum.[4] This is because, during this learning stage, one can have hands-on experience and direct interaction with patients. This process allows students to develop and enhance their decision-making abilities, communication skills, and self-confidence.[5]

In the operating room environment, staff and students, as members of the treatment team, have vital responsibilities.[6] The issues of respecting principles and values and adhering to professional principles are considered more critical in this profession than in other ones.[7] On the other hand, achieving these goals in this environment, due to unique features such as population congestion, the presence of specialists and students from various fields,[8] and the endurance of psychological burden and excessive stress by individuals,[9] clinical training at the patient’s bedside, alongside ensuring patient safety, face serious challenges.[1011] Therefore, operating room nursing students must undergo comprehensive training to perform vital interventions during preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative procedures. In this dynamic environment, they must gain proficiency in various skills and professional responsibilities.[12] These students must gain proficiency in surgical procedures, master the operation of advanced electronic equipment, and develop essential clinical competencies in communication skills, leadership, and patient safety.[13] Previous studies have shown that clinical rotations, when combined with traditional learning methods, can lead students to face high stress levels, impeding their attainment of the necessary clinical competencies and constructive feedback.[14] In this context, researchers emphasize choosing the proper learning method in clinical environments, using new student-centered methods, and using and incorporating modern technologies to enhance theoretical and practical learning more effectively and improve students’ overall competencies.[15] In this regard, some past studies have identified team-based learning methods as a student-centered approach to increase students’ clinical skills and competencies effectively.

As an active strategy, the team-based learning method provides opportunities to enhance problem-solving skills and engage in group work for clinical students and educators.[16] This innovative approach enables students to work together in groups of 5 to 6, continuously share information, experiences, and skills, and actively participate in solving clinical problems.[17] This learning method consists of four fundamental stages, including forming strategic permanent teams, ensuring students’ prior familiarity with course concepts through the readiness assurance process, developing clinical skills of students through the precise design of activities and assignments, and establishing an assessment system with immediate feedback to students.[18] Utilizing this learning method in a clinical environment allows students to engage in group work and apply clinical reasoning to solve problems without requiring more time from educators or increasing their numbers. This can be done with minimal physical resources and facilities.[1719] In specialized operating room environments, this learning method can help students acquire relevant competencies and overcome lingering fears from their studentship period to perform clinical activities effectively.[20]

Mastery-based learning is a student-centered approach that ensures students achieve predetermined skills uniformly.[21] Traditional learning methods typically prescribe a specific time frame for learning a particular skill. In contrast, mastery-based learning sets standards based on each student’s learning pace. This method promotes repeated practice and enables students to master at their speed.[22] Initial learning, formative assessment, corrective activities, and enrichment activities are the primary stages in implementing this type of learning.[23] Although implementing this learning method in a clinical environment requires continuous monitoring and assessment and additional time commitment from clinical educators,[24] by providing students with multiple opportunities to practice and master a specific concept, they are encouraged to learn through experimentation and exploration.[25]

Given the critical role of appropriate learning strategies in nursing, the adoption of modern learning methods in nursing education is particularly important. Therefore, recognizing the necessity of applying effective learning strategies to enhance the clinical competence and skills of operating room nursing students, and noting that no prior research has investigated the impact of these methods on their clinical competence and evaluation, this study aims to be the first to compare the effects of team-based and mastery-based learning approaches on the clinical competence and clinical assessment of operating room nursing students.

Intervention

To conduct the study and collect data, the students were placed in 2 team-based and mastery-based learning groups by matching method; the number of students in each group was 31 [Figure 1].

Participant flow

The training sessions for both groups took place in two different periods (February and March 2024), with 18 sessions that lasted five hours in the operating room department of Besat Hospital, Hamadan. Furthermore, a single clinical instructor attended all student training sessions to avoid bias in the study results. According to the educational curriculum, students in this course were required to perform the tasks of a scrub nurse under the supervision of a clinical instructor in the operating room. By the end of this course, they were expected to be familiar with various surgical incisions and learn the proper technique of closing surgical wounds. Students were required to apply both theoretical and practical knowledge in more advanced surgeries than in previous courses. These included procedures such as arthroplasty, as well as the use of tools and equipment for advanced endoscopic surgeries like laparoscopic cholecystectomy. They also learned about various types of retractors and how to use them for different tissues and organs, as well as how to use hemostatic agents such as ligatures. In the team-based learning group, students reviewed the materials specified by the instructor in the principles and techniques of the circular person and scrub and surgical techniques before starting each internship session. Then, at the beginning of each internship session and in the conference hall of the operating room, an individual test on the specified materials was conducted for 10 minutes. Following this, a team test with concepts similar to the first test was held for 20 minutes. After completing the tests, the groups discussed and reviewed for 30 minutes with the instructor’s guidance and feedback. Finally, after being assigned to the operating rooms, students performed the designated activities related to the session’s topics and objectives under the supervision and collaboration of their group members, and they provided critiques on each other’s work. Additionally, evaluations were conducted at the end of each training session to motivate the students, and groups were ranked based on their scores.[20]

In the mastery-based learning group, after the clinical instructor set the educational objectives similar to the educational objectives of the previous group, the necessary training regarding the anatomy and physiology of the surgical areas, the steps involved in the specific surgery, the required tools and instruments, as well as the operation of advanced electrical equipment related to the surgery, was presented to the students at the beginning of each session. The students practiced and repeated the skills they had learned in the operating rooms. At the start of the next session, a verbal and practical question-and-answer session was held regarding the topics covered in the previous session. In this phase, if the students performed the required skills correctly and on time or answered the questions accurately, they could participate in the training for new skills and, ultimately, join the surgical team as a scrub member in their designated field. Conversely, if the student did not execute the specified skill correctly, they were required to attend supplementary training by the instructor. Students were expected to achieve a sufficient level of mastery in previous skills, as assessed by the instructor, before being trained on new skills in the operating room environment. To facilitate this, the clinical instructor allocated extra time for students who lacked adequate proficiency in a particular skill, repeating the training and providing opportunities to practice and re-perform the skill.[25]

In this study, to assess the student’s level of clinical competence, the Clinical Competence Questionnaire developed by Liu et al. was distributed to the students once before the training began. Additionally, this questionnaire was provided to the students again at the end of the final session of the internship for both groups to complete. After the training sessions, the clinical instructor completed a Clinical Evaluation Checklist for each student individually.

Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hamadan University of Medical Sciences with the code (IR.UMSHA.REC.1402.637). All participants were informed about the purpose of the study and informed consent to conduct the research was obtained from the research. Also, the research units were assured of the confidentiality of the information.

Statistical analysis

Finally, data analysis was conducted using SPSS, version 16.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, Ill., USA)’ software at a significance level of 0.05. Initially, the normality of the data was assessed using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests. Descriptive statistics included frequency, percentage of frequency, mean, and standard deviation. If needed, Chi-square tests (or Fisher’s exact test) and independent t-tests were used to compare the frequency distribution of qualitative and quantitative demographic characteristics between the two groups. Additionally, analytical statistics included the independent t-test for comparison between groups.

Discussion

In the present study, team-based and mastery-based learning methods were used in the operating room nursing students’ internship, and their effects on the student’s clinical competence and evaluation were compared.

The results of this study showed that students in the mastery-based learning group performed significantly better in clinical performance and clinical evaluation than those in the team-based group. Max Berger et al.[31] also found similar results in their study, concluding that using the mastery-based method enhances clinical performance and boosts confidence among medical students. Masoudi Alavi et al.[32] found that implementing mastery-based learning methods in clinical education for nursing students leads to active learning and improvement in their clinical skills. Similarly, a study of 50 undergraduate nursing students demonstrated that this learning method significantly enhances their clinical skills.[33] Furthermore, Winget et al.[23] concluded in their study that the mastery-based learning method generally increases the final evaluation scores of the students. To explain these results, it can be said that this approach offers numerous chances for students to acquire skills. It also motivates them to finish the course, enhances their motivation to learn, and includes frequent assessments throughout the course, all leading to improved learning and higher final scores.[25] This learning approach enables students to recognize their challenges in different areas and take necessary steps to address them, thereby preventing feelings of failure and frustration.[34] Based on these findings, it is recommended to implement this learning method to address issues related to learning clinical skills in clinical environments such as the operating room, where the acquisition of essential skills often faces numerous challenges due to students’ insufficient confidence or poor interactions with staff.[35]

Our study demonstrated that mastery-based learning methods can significantly enhance students’ clinical competence. In line with these results, Sharif et al.[36] found in a study conducted on operating room nursing students that implementing mastery-based learning methods leads to increased clinical competence. Additionally, Tang and Dong’s study demonstrated that mastery-based learning methods have a significant impact on nurses’ clinical competence and its various dimensions, including knowledge, skills, collaboration, and professional development.[37] These findings can be justified because mastery-based learning emphasizes sharing knowledge, fostering personnel development and advancement, and students’ thorough and systematic learning.[32]

The results of this study demonstrated that team-based learning methods, like mastery-based methods, can also significantly increase students’ clinical competence. In line with these findings, Zhang et al.[19] found a significant impact of team-based methods on nurses’ clinical competence in their study. The study by Alberti et al.[38] also indicated the influence of this learning method on nurses’ clinical competence, which is in line with our findings. Furthermore, many previous studies have confirmed the effect of this learning method on nurses’ clinical competence, which is consistent with our findings. This learning method can effectively enhance students’ clinical competence by integrating and utilizing their prior theoretical knowledge during internship periods.[19] Compared to other methods, this approach fosters a sense of achievement and promotes the application of reasoning and critical thinking among students by providing learning in small group settings.[16]

Regarding the effectiveness of various learning methods on clinical competence, it should be noted that conducting clinical courses can increase students’ clinical competence naturally and spontaneously. However, based on the results of our study, the use of team-based learning methods, compared to mastery-based methods, led students to report a higher level of clinical competence, particularly in the domains of leadership and interpersonal relationships. In this context, teamwork skills enhance critical thinking, problem-solving, decision-making, and group interaction skills[39] and significantly improve each clinical competence component. Consistent with our findings, previous research has shown that employing team-based learning methods in clinical environments can enhance communication skills and interpersonal relationships, which are essential components of clinical competence, and increase interaction between instructors and students.[20]

On the other hand, a sense of trust among students is established when they feel they are treated as members of a group with similar goals. In this learning method, the learning environment created by instructors fosters this sense of trust, which enhances supportive interactions, constructive exchanges, and leadership skills.[40] Therefore, considering the current results and the fact that the lack of clinical competence and qualifications among staff is a critical issue that needs immediate attention in the operating room,[41] the use and implementation of this learning method in clinical environments is recommended for instructors.

Limitations and recommendations

One of the limitations of our study was the inability to implement a blinding method and the lack of a control group due to restrictions on the number of eligible students in the Faculty of Paramedicine. Another limitation of this study was the use of self-report measures, and incorrect answers could potentially influence the responses; therefore, students were assured that their questionnaire responses would remain confidential. Another limitation of the current study was the lack of a pre-test to measure the clinical skills level of students before the study due to the nature of the questionnaire questions, which can only be used after the training course. Thus, it is recommended that future studies use innovative methods to separately examine the impact of each educational approach on students’ clinical skills. Furthermore, it is suggested that future studies analyze and assess each of the methods above in various aspects compared to other educational approaches to address the challenges clinical instructors face in selecting suitable learning methods.

Conclusion

Based on the results of this study, it can be said that the team-based educational approach, significantly impacts the clinical competence level of operating room nursing students more than the mastery-based learning method. Therefore, due to the demand for efficient and competent personnel in healthcare facilities, especially in the operating room, it is recommended to implement this method in educating operating room nursing students to prepare them for work in this sensitive and high-risk environment. Additionally, considering the more significant impact of the mastery-based method on students’ clinical skills, clinical instructors are advised to utilize this method as an effective strategy to enhance students’ clinical skills. Furthermore, higher education institutions and universities should provide adequate resources and make essential efforts to replace traditional methods with the above learning methods in clinical and hospital environments.

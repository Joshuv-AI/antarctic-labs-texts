Lorentzian homogeneous Ricci-flat metrics on almost abelian Lie groups
Yuichiro Sato and Takanao Tsuyuki

Abstract
. We study Lorentzian left-invariant metrics on almost abelian Lie groups of dimensions larger than three. An almost abelian Lie group is a Lie group whose Lie algebra has a codimension one abelian ideal. Ricci-flat and non-flat conditions on the Lie algebra are derived. In particular, we generalize the four-dimensional Petrov solution to arbitrarily higher dimensions.

Primary 53B30; Secondary 53C30

1.
Introduction

A homogeneous Ricci-flat Riemannian manifold is necessarily flat
[
1
]
.
Even for pseudo-Riemannian cases, three- (and lower-) dimensional Ricci-flat manifolds are flat.
Thus, homogeneous, Ricci-flat, and non-flat metrics can exist only for pseudo-Riemannian manifolds of dimension four or higher.

In the theory of general relativity, the metrics on vacuum spacetimes (without the cosmological constant) are Ricci flat.
Interestingly, our spacetime is a Lorentzian manifold of dimension at least four, so homogeneous, Ricci-flat, and non-flat vacuum spacetime can exist.
The observed spacetime dimension is four, but higher dimensions are necessary for some theories such as string/M theories
[
2
,
3
]
and are explored experimentally
[
4
]
.

For the four-dimensional case, there are three non-flat, Ricci-flat homogeneous Lorentzian metrics
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
]
:
one is the Petrov solution
[
7
, (7.3)]
and the other two are plane wave solutions
[
7
, (6.29), (6.39)]
.
These solutions are left-invariant metrics on four-dimensional Lie groups.
We notice that the Lie groups of the Petrov solution and the latter plane wave solution are examples of special Lie groups, called almost abelian Lie groups
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.

An almost abelian Lie group is a Lie group with a codimension one abelian normal subgroup.

The term “almost abelian” is sometimes used in different meanings. For example, the almost abelian Lie algebra in Ref.
[
13
]
corresponds to
A=1
in our definition.

Almost abelian groups are metabelian, and metabelian groups are equivalent to
2
-step solvable ones.
Almost abelian groups can be nilpotent (e.g., the three-dimensional Heisenberg group), but not necessarily (e.g., the Petrov solution).
Ricci-flat Lorentzian homogeneous metrics have primarily been studied in the context of four-dimensional groups
[

,

]
or nilpotent groups
[

,

.
This work differs from previous studies, as we focus on Lie groups that are of general dimension and not necessarily nilpotent.

In this paper, we show the explicit conditions that a left-invariant Lorentzian metric on an almost abelian Lie group becomes Ricci-flat and non-flat.
In particular, we generalize the Petrov solution to arbitrarily higher dimensions.
The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 4
and

(G,ds^{2})
be an
n
-dimensional almost abelian Lie group
with a left-invariant Lorentzian metric
ds^{2}
.
If it is non-flat, Ricci-flat
but neither locally symmetric nor a plane wave,
and the isometric group acts on it almost simply-transitively,
then it is locally isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}

endowed with the following Lorentzian metric

(1.1)

ds^{2}=e^{-2\alpha x_{n}}\left[\cos(2\beta x_{n})(-dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})-2\sin(2\beta x_{n})dx_{1}dx_{2}\right]+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}e^{-2\lambda_{i}x_{n}}dx_{i}^{2}+dx_{n}^{2},

where
\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
satisfy

\alpha\neq 0,\,\beta>0
and

2\alpha+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}=0,\quad 2\alpha^{2}-2\beta^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{2}=0,\quad\prod_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}\neq 0,\quad\lambda_{3}>\ldots>\lambda_{n-1},

and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}

denotes the canonical coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Conversely, the Lorentzian manifold
\mathbb{R}^{n}

with the metric (
1.1
) is
non-flat, Ricci-flat but neither locally symmetric nor a plane wave,
and the isometric group acts on it almost simply-transitively.

We remark that the action of the isometry group is called
almost simply-transitive

if the isotropy group is discrete.
Also, the action is called
simply-transitive

if the isotropy group is trivial.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2

In Section
3

In Section
4
, we investigate the higher-dimensional generalization of the Petrov solution and examine the structure of its isotropy groups.

2.
Almost abelian Lie groups

Let
G
be an
n
-dimensional Lie group,
\mathfrak{g}
its Lie algebra,
and
\{X_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq n}
left-invariant vector fields on
G
.
Then we define the structure constants
{C^{a}}_{bc}
of
\mathfrak{g}
as

[X_{b},X_{c}]=\sum_{a=1}^{n}{C^{a}}_{bc}X_{a}.

In addition, we define the left-invariant
1
-forms
\{\omega^{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq n}
on
G
as
\omega^{a}(X_{b})=\delta^{a}_{b}
. Then the left-invariant metric can be written as

(2.1)

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}\eta_{ab}\omega^{a}\omega^{b},

where
\eta_{ab}
are constants.

In this paper, we consider the case that
G
is an almost abelian Lie group.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-dimensional real Lie algebra.
It is
almost abelian
if there exists an
(n-1)
-dimensional abelian ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}

[
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
Moreover, a connected
n
-dimensional Lie group
G
is
almost abelian
if the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
is almost abelian.
In this case, the left-invariant vectors are commutable except for one we choose to be
X_{n}
. The structure constants become

(2.2)

\displaystyle{C^{i}}_{jk}=0,\ {C^{n}}_{jk}=0.

The indices
i,j,k
run
1,\dots,n-1
.
We define the possibly non-zero components
{C^{i}}_{nj}
to be components of the associated matrix
A=[A_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}

of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to

\{X_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq n}
;

(2.3)

\displaystyle[X_{n},X_{j}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}A_{ij}X_{i}.

Let
\mathrm{M}_{m}\mathbb{R}
denote
the set of
m\times m
real square matrices.
The Jacobi identity holds for arbitrary

A\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R}
.
For example,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{n}\left({C^{i}}_{ja}{C^{a}}_{nn}+{C^{i}}_{na}{C^{a}}_{nj}+{C^{i}}_{na}{C^{a}}_{jn}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(A_{ik}A_{kj}-A_{ik}A_{kj}\right)=0,

and the others also identically vanish.

Here we give a left-invariant Lorentzian metric
g_{G}
on an almost abelian Lie group
G
.
Then the pair
(G,g_{G})
is a homogeneous Lorentzian manifold,
and the given metric corresponds to
the non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form

\langle\,,\,\rangle
with Lorentzian signature on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
In addition,
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denotes
a codimension one abelian ideal of the almost abelian Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-dimensional almost abelian Lie group,

g_{G}
a left-invariant Lorentzian metric on
G
.
Then there exists a basis

\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1},X_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}=\mathfrak{a},\quad\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{a}\rtimes\mathbb{R}X_{n}

and the bilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
\mathfrak{g}
can be expressed by one of the following matrices:

(2.5)

({\rm a})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,1&&&\,\,\\
\,&\ddots&&\,\,\\
\,&&1&\,\,\\
\,&&&-1\,\,\end{bmatrix},\quad({\rm b})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,-1&&&\,\,\\
\,&1&&\,\,\\
\,&&\ddots&\,\,\\
\,&&&1\,\,\end{bmatrix},\quad({\rm c})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,1&&&&\,\,\\
\,&\ddots&&&\,\,\\
\,&&1&&\,\,\\
\,&&&0&1\,\,\\
\,&&&1&0\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

Since there exists a codimension one abelian ideal

\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathfrak{g}
,
the induced symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{a}
is
Riemannian, Lorentzian, or degenerate.
In the Riemannian case,
we can take an orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}
of
\mathfrak{a}
.
So we just have to choose a unit timelike vector
X_{n}

of the perpendicular complement
\mathfrak{a}^{\bot}
.
In the Lorentzian case,
we can take an orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}
of
\mathfrak{a}
in the following sense;

\langle X_{1},X_{1}\rangle=-1,\quad\langle X_{i},X_{i}\rangle=1\quad(2\leq i\leq n-1).

Similarly, we just have to choose a unit spacelike vector
X_{n}

of the perpendicular complement
\mathfrak{a}^{\bot}
.
In the degenerate case,
since the bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
is non-degenerate,
we can take a quasi-orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\langle X_{j},X_{j}\rangle=\langle X_{n-1},X_{n}\rangle=1\quad(1\leq j\leq n-2),

the others are zero,
and
{\rm span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}=\mathfrak{a}
.
Thus the proof is completed.
∎

For a given almost abelian Lie group
(G,g_{G})

with a left-invariant Lorentzian metric,
it suffices to deal with
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)

instead of
(G,g_{G})
.
We call it the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra for
(G,g_{G})
.

For any
X\in\mathfrak{g}
,
we define the operator
L_{X}:\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}
as

(2.6)

2\langle L_{X}(Y),Z\rangle=-\langle X,[Y,Z]\rangle+\langle Y,[Z,X]\rangle+\langle Z,[X,Y]\rangle,

where
Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then the curvature operator is defined by

R(X,Y):=[L_{X},L_{Y}]-L_{[X,Y]}\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}).

The Ricci tensor

{\rm Ric}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

of
(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
can be computed by the following formula.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
20
, Corollary 7.38]
)
.

Let
\{e_{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq n}
be an orthonormal basis of

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
. Then

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a=1}^{n}\langle[X,e_{a}],[Y,e_{a}]\rangle\varepsilon_{a}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{a,b=1}^{n}\langle X,[e_{a},e_{b}]\rangle\langle Y,[e_{a},e_{b}]\rangle\varepsilon_{a}\varepsilon_{b}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{1}{2}K(X,Y)-\frac{1}{2}(\langle[H,X],Y\rangle+\langle[H,Y],X\rangle),

where
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\varepsilon_{a}=\langle e_{a},e_{a}\rangle=\pm 1
,
and
K
is the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

K(X,Y):=\mathrm{tr}\,\left[(\mathrm{ad}\,X)\,(\mathrm{ad}\,Y)\right]\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}),

and
H
is the mean curvature vector of

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
defined by

\langle H,X\rangle:=\mathrm{tr}\,(\mathrm{ad}\,X).

The Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
for
(G,g_{G})

is called
flat
if
R=0
holds,
and it is called
Ricci-flat
if
{\rm Ric}=0
holds.
The definitions correspond to the flatness and Ricci-flatness of

(G,g_{G})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be an
n
-dimensional almost abelian Lie group with
the associated matrix
A\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R}

with respect to
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
.
Then the Killing form
K
is given by

K(X_{i},X_{j})=K(X_{n},X_{j})=0,\quad K(X_{n},X_{n})=\mathrm{tr}\,A^{2}

for
i,j=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Proof.

By the direct computation, we have

(2.7)

\mathrm{ad}\,X_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}\,O&-\bm{a}_{i}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad\mathrm{ad}\,X_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}\,A&\bm{0}\,\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
A=[\bm{a}_{1},\ldots,\bm{a}_{n-1}]\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R}
.
From the definition of the Killing form, we have the conclusion.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

In response to three metrics (a), (b) and (c) in Lemma
2.1
,
the mean curvature vector
H\in\mathfrak{g}

of the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
coincides with

-(\mathrm{tr}\,A)X_{n},\quad(\mathrm{tr}\,A)X_{n},\quad(\mathrm{tr}\,A)X_{n-1},

respectively.

Proof.

It follows from formulae (
2.7
).
∎

3.
Ricci-flat and flat conditions

When an
n
-dimensional almost abelian Lie group
G
is
1
-connected,

G
is isomorphic to

\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the suitable Lie group structure.
By identifying them and using the coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
the left-invariant one-forms
\{\omega^{a}\}_{1\leq a\leq n}
can be written in the coordinate basis as (see
[
21
, Proposition 2.6]

)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\omega^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\left(e^{-Ax_{n}}\right)_{ij}dx_{j},\quad\omega^{n}=dx_{n}.

The left-invariant metric (
2.1
) becomes

(3.2)

\displaystyle ds^{2}=d\bm{x}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}e^{-A^{T}x_{n}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}\eta\begin{bmatrix}e^{-Ax_{n}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}d\bm{x},

where
d\bm{x}=(dx_{1},\dots,dx_{n})^{T}
and

\eta
is one of the matrices in (
2.5
).
We set the three metrics as

\mathrm{(a):\ }\langle\,,\,\rangle_{a},\quad\mathrm{(b):\ }\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b},\quad\mathrm{(c):\ }\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c}.

Moreover, in response to the above metrics,
we decompose
A
as

A=S+T.\quad A=S_{L}+T_{L},\quad A=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&\bm{b}\,\\
\bm{c}^{T}&d\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
S,\,T
are the symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices in case (a),

S_{L},\,T_{L}
satisfy

S_{L}^{T}=JS_{L}J,\quad T_{L}^{T}=-JT_{L}J,\quad(J:=\mathrm{Diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1)\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R})

in case (b), and
A^{\prime}\in\mathrm{M}_{n-2}\mathbb{R},\,\bm{b},\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-2},\,d\in\mathbb{R}
.
Furthermore, we decompose into symmetric and skew-symmetric parts
as
A^{\prime}=S^{\prime}+T^{\prime}
in case (c).

As a remark, in case (b),
the decomposition
A=S_{L}+T_{L}
is uniquely determined.
We call it the
J
-decomposition
of
A
.

3.1.
Ricci-flat conditions

We proceed in cases (a), (b) and (c) in Lemma
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
,
when
\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{a}
,
the Ricci tensor
\mathrm{Ric}=\left[\mathrm{Ric}(X_{a},X_{b})\right]_{1\leq a,b\leq n}
is given by

(3.3)

\mathrm{Ric}=\begin{bmatrix}\,\,[S,T]+(\mathrm{tr}\,S)S&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\,\bm{0}^{T}&-\mathrm{tr}\,S^{2}\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

when
\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b}
,
it is given by

(3.4)

\mathrm{Ric}=\begin{bmatrix}\,-J([S_{L},T_{L}]+(\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L})S_{L})&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&-\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}^{2}\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

and when
\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c}
,
it is given by

(3.5)

\mathrm{Ric}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}\,-\bm{b}\bm{b}^{T}&\bm{0}&[(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})I_{n-2}+S^{\prime}+T^{\prime}]\bm{b}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0&||\bm{b}||^{2}\,\\
\,\bm{b}^{T}[(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})I_{n-2}+S^{\prime}-T^{\prime}]&||\bm{b}||^{2}&2d(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})-2\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}-2\bm{b}^{T}\bm{c}\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

where
||\cdot||
denotes the Euclidean norm of the Euclidean space.

Proof.

We use Proposition
2.2
.
In case (a) and (b),
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis,
so that we can apply the formula directly.
In case (c), since the generators are not an orthonormal basis,
we need to exchange the generators such as

e_{i}=X_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq n-2),\quad e_{n-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X_{n-1}+X_{n}),\quad e_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X_{n-1}-X_{n}).

Then
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1},e_{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis,
and we can apply Proposition
2.2
.
We get the result by direct computation.
∎

In case (a), we solve the Ricci-flat condition.
From Proposition
3.1
, the conditions are:

[S,T]+(\mathrm{tr}\,S)S=O,\quad\mathrm{tr}\,S^{2}=0,

which is equivalent to
S=O
.
This is because
S
is symmetric and
\mathrm{tr}\,S^{2}=||S||^{2}=0
,
where
||S||
denotes the matrix norm of the matrix
S
.
Equivalently, it is
A=T
.

In case (b),
from Proposition
3.1
the Ricci-flat conditions are:

[S_{L},T_{L}]+(\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L})S_{L}=O,\quad\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}^{2}=0.

By taking the trace, the first equation implies
\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}=0
.
Therefore, we have the following equivalent conditions

\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}=\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}^{2}=0,\quad[S_{L},T_{L}]=O.

Remark 3.2
.

When the Ricci-flat conditions in case (b) are satisfied,
the metric becomes

ds^{2}=d\bm{x}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}e^{-A^{T}x_{n}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}\eta\begin{bmatrix}e^{-Ax_{n}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}d\bm{x}=d\bm{x}^{T}\begin{bmatrix}Je^{-2S_{L}x_{n}}&\bm{0}\\
\bm{0}^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}d\bm{x}

from the metric formula (
3.2
) and
[S_{L},T_{L}]=O
.
It means that
T_{L}
does not affect the isometric class of the Lorentzian manifold.
Thus, as long as we consider the isometric class,
we may forget
T_{L}
without loss of generality.
Namely, we may initially set
A=S_{L}
.
Moreover, considering the diffeomorphism

F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n})^{T}\mapsto(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},-x_{n})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

the Lorentzian manifold with
A=-S_{L}
is isometric to
that with
A=S_{L}
.

In case (c),
from Proposition
3.1
the Ricci-flat conditions are:

||\bm{b}||=0,\quad[(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})I_{n-2}+S^{\prime}+T^{\prime}]\bm{b}=\bm{0},\quad 2d(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})-2\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}-2\bm{b}^{T}\bm{c}=0.

We have
\bm{b}=\bm{0}
immediately, so the second condition is trivial.
Next, the third condition is equivalent to

\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}=d(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}).

Hence we obtain the Ricci-flat conditions in case (c) as follows;

\bm{b}=\bm{0},\quad S^{\prime}=O\ (\mathrm{if}\ d=0),\quad\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}=d(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime})\ (\mathrm{if}\ d\neq 0).

Here we check the transformation rules for replacing the generators.
Let
\mathrm{GL}_{m}\mathbb{R}
denote the set of invertible matrices
of
\mathrm{M}_{m}\mathbb{R}
.
We set other generators as

(3.6)

[Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n-1},Y_{n}]=[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1},X_{n}]\begin{bmatrix}\,P&\bm{0}\,\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&c\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad(P\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}\mathbb{R},\ c\neq 0)

from the original generators.
Let
A,\,\overline{A}\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R}
denote the associated matrices with respect to

\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}\},\,\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n}\}
, respectively.
Also, let
\eta,\,\overline{\eta}
denote the metric matrices defined by

\eta_{ab}=\langle X_{a},X_{b}\rangle,\,\overline{\eta}_{ab}=\langle Y_{a},Y_{b}\rangle

for
1\leq a,b\leq n
.

Lemma 3.3
.

The following transformation formulas hold

\overline{A}=cP^{-1}AP,\quad\overline{\eta}=\begin{bmatrix}\,P^{T}&\bm{0}\,\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&c\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\eta\begin{bmatrix}\,P&\bm{0}\,\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&c\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix}.

Proof.

The latter transformation formula is well-known.
By using

\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n-1}\}=\mathfrak{a},\quad Y_{n}=cX_{n},

we have the former transformation formula by direct computations.
∎

We define the set of Lorentzian transformations as

\mathrm{O}(1,m-1)=\{P\in\mathrm{GL}_{m}\mathbb{R}\mid P^{T}JP=J\}\quad(J=\mathrm{Diag}(-1,1,\ldots,1)\in\mathrm{M}_{m}\mathbb{R}).

In case (b), for
P\in\mathrm{O}(1,n-2)
, we set
\overline{A}=P^{-1}AP
.
Let
A=S_{L}+T_{L}
and

\overline{A}=\overline{S}_{L}+\overline{T}_{L}
be the
J
-decompositions.
Then we have
\overline{S}_{L}=P^{-1}S_{L}P,\,\overline{T}_{L}=P^{-1}T_{L}P

from the uniqueness of the
J
-decompositions.
If
A
satisfies the Ricci-flat conditions,
\overline{A}
trivially does.
Thus, we may replace
A
with the normal form based on the action of Lorentzian transformations. The following is known.

Proposition 3.4

(
[
22
, Corollary 2.4]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{M}_{m}\mathbb{R}
satisfy
A^{T}=JAJ
.
Then
A
is conjugate by
P\in\mathrm{O}(1,m-1)
to a diagonal matrix,
or a direct sum of a diagonal matrix and one of the following matrices:

\displaystyle({\rm i})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,\,\alpha&-\beta\,\\
\,\,\beta&\alpha\,\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad({\rm ii})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,-1+\gamma&-1\,\\
\,1&1+\gamma\,\\
\end{bmatrix},\quad({\rm iii})\ \begin{bmatrix}\,-\delta&-\delta&-1\,\,\\
\,\delta&\delta&1\,\,\\
\,1&1&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}

up to the sign.
Here
\alpha,\,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta>0
.

In case (b), we check the Ricci-flat conditions
for each matrix in Proposition
3.4
.
When
S_{L}
is simply a diagonal matrix,
we have
S_{L}=O
, that is, flat.

When
S_{L}
is in case (i), that is,

S_{L}=\begin{bmatrix}\,\,\alpha&-\beta\,\\
\,\,\beta&\alpha\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\oplus\,\mathrm{Diag}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}),

we have

2\alpha+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}=2\alpha^{2}-2\beta^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{2}=0.

When
\alpha^{2}=\beta^{2}
holds,
it implies that
\beta=0
.
So we have
\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}\neq 0
from
\beta>0
.
We will describe the distinguishing solution in Section
4
.

When
S_{L}
is in case (ii), that is,

S_{L}=\begin{bmatrix}\,-1+\gamma&-1\,\\
\,1&1+\gamma\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\oplus\,\mathrm{Diag}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}),

we have
\gamma=\lambda_{3}=\cdots=\lambda_{n-1}=0
.
It induces the flatness from Proposition
3.5
below.

When
S_{L}
is in case (iii), that is,

S_{L}=\begin{bmatrix}\,-\delta&-\delta&-1\,\\
\,\delta&\delta&1\,\\
\,1&1&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\oplus\,\mathrm{Diag}(\lambda_{4},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}),

we have
\lambda_{4}=\cdots=\lambda_{n-1}=0
.

In case (c),
when the transformation matrix in (
3.6
) is given by

\begin{bmatrix}\,P&\bm{0}\,\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&c\,\,\\
\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{Diag}(1,\ldots,1,d)\oplus\left[d^{-1}\right]=\begin{bmatrix}\,1&&&&\,\\
\,&\ddots&&&\,\\
\,&&1&&\,\\
\,&&&d&\,\\
\,&&&&d^{-1}\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad(d\neq 0),

from Lemma
3.3

the transformation preserves the metric
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c}

and the associated matrix
A
is multiplied by
d^{-1}
.
In particular, the Ricci-flat conditions are also preserved.
Actually, when
d\neq 0
and
A
is decomposed as

A=\begin{bmatrix}A^{\prime}&\bm{0}\,\\
\bm{c}^{T}&d\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

by the transformation in Lemma
3.3
we have

d^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}\,I_{n-2}&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&d^{-1}\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\,A^{\prime}&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{c}^{T}&d\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\,I_{n-2}&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&d\,\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\,d^{-1}A^{\prime}&\bm{0}\,\\
\,d^{-2}\bm{c}^{T}&1\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

which satisfies the Ricci-flat conditions again.

3.2.
Flat conditions

If the metric is flat, it is trivially Ricci-flat.
We are interested in Ricci-flat but non-flat metrics, so we derive flat conditions in this subsection.
Since we have derived the Ricci-flat conditions in the previous subsection,
we can find the flat conditions from the necessary conditions for Ricci-flatness.

Proposition 3.5
.

For the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
,
the metric

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{a}
is flat
if and only if it is Ricci-flat.
Next,

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b}
is flat
if and only if
it is Ricci-flat,
S_{L}^{2}=O
and
\mathrm{rank}\,S_{L}\leq 1
.
Lastly,

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c}
is flat
if and only if
it is Ricci-flat when
d=0
,
or
\bm{b}=\bm{0}
,
S^{\prime}=S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}
and
[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]=O
when
d=1
.

Proof.

In case (a), under the Ricci-flatness,
we have the operators

L_{X_{i}}=0\ (1\leq i\leq n-1),\quad L_{X_{n}}=\begin{bmatrix}\,T&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}.

From them, we can directly check
R=0
.
So the Ricci-flatness implies the flatness.

In case (b), under the Ricci-flatness,
we have the operators

L_{X_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}\,O&-\bm{s}_{i}\,\\
\,\bm{s}_{i}^{T}J&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\ (1\leq i\leq n-1),\quad L_{X_{n}}=0,

where we set

S_{L}=[\bm{s}_{1},\ldots,\bm{s}_{n-1}]\in\mathrm{M}_{n-1}\mathbb{R}
.
From them, we have

R(X_{i},X_{j})=\begin{bmatrix}\,-(\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{j}^{T}-\bm{s}_{j}\bm{s}_{i}^{T})J&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\ (1\leq i,j\leq n-1),\quad R(X_{n},X_{i})=\begin{bmatrix}\,O&-S_{L}\bm{s}_{i}\,\\
\,\bm{s}_{i}^{T}S_{L}^{T}J&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}.

So the metric is flat if and only if

\bm{s}_{i}\bm{s}_{j}^{T}=\bm{s}_{j}\bm{s}_{i}^{T}\ (1\leq i,j\leq n-1),\quad S_{L}^{2}=O.

The first condition is equivalent to
\mathrm{rank}\,S_{L}\leq 1
.
Thus we get the desired flat conditions.

In case (c), under the Ricci-flatness, we have the operators

L_{X_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}\,O&\bm{0}&-\bm{s}_{i}^{\prime}\,\\
\,\bm{s}_{i}^{\prime T}&0&0\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\ (1\leq i\leq n-2),\quad L_{X_{n-1}}=0,\quad L_{X_{n}}=\begin{bmatrix}\,T^{\prime}&\bm{0}&-\bm{c}\,\\
\,\bm{c}^{T}&d&0\,\\
\,\bm{0}^{T}&0&-d\,\\
\end{bmatrix},

where we set

S^{\prime}=[\bm{s}_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,\bm{s}_{n-2}^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{M}_{n-2}\mathbb{R}

and
d=0,1
.
From them, it holds that

R(X_{i},X_{j})=R(X_{n-1},X_{i})=0\ (1\leq i,j\leq n-2),\quad R(X_{n},X_{n-1})=0.

Also,
R(X_{n},X_{i})=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,n-2

if and only if
S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}-dS^{\prime}+[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]=O
.
By diagonalizing
S^{\prime}
, it is equivalent to

S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}=dS^{\prime},\,[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]=O
.
When
d=0
,
we have
S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}=O
, that is,
S^{\prime}=O
.
When
d=1
,
we have the flat conditions
S^{\prime}=S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2},\,[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]=O
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In case (a), the metric is Ricci-flat if and only if it is flat.
Similarly, if the almost abelian Lie group is endowed with a left-invariant Riemannian metric, the Ricci-flat conditions lead to the same condition.

At the end of this subsection,
we discuss that there are no locally symmetric and Ricci-flat almost abelian Lie groups that are not flat.

The Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
for
(G,g_{G})
is called

locally symmetric
if
\nabla R=0
holds,
i.e., for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(\nabla_{Z}R)(X,Y):=L_{Z}R(X,Y)-R(L_{Z}X,Y)-R(X,L_{Z}Y)-R(X,Y)L_{Z}=0.

Then the corresponding Lorentzian manifold

(G,g_{G})
is locally symmetric.
If it is flat, then it is locally symmetric by definition.

Lemma 3.7
.

For the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
,
it is Ricci-flat and locally symmetric if and only if it is flat.

Proof.

We have nothing to prove for case (a).
For case (b), let
S_{L}
satisfy the Ricci-flat conditions.
From
(\nabla_{X_{i}}R)(X_{n},X_{j})=0\ (1\leq i,j\leq n-1)
,
we have
S_{L}^{3}=O
.
For each case of Proposition
3.4
,
when
S_{L}
is in case (i),

S_{L}^{2}\neq O,\,S_{L}^{3}\neq O
hold,
that is, it is neither flat nor locally symmetric.
When
S_{L}
is in case (ii), it is flat.
When
S_{L}
is in case (iii),
we see that
S_{L}^{2}\neq O
.
Thus, it is Ricci-flat but not flat.
Moreover, we see that
(\nabla_{X_{1}}R)(X_{2},X_{3})\neq 0
,
that is, it is Ricci-flat but not locally symmetric.
For case (c), we have nothing to check when
d=0
.
Let
d=1
. From
(\nabla_{X_{n}}R)(X_{n},X_{n-1})=0
,
we have

2W=[W,T^{\prime}],

where
W:=S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}-S^{\prime}+[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]
.
Since
W
is symmetric and
T^{\prime}
is skew-symmetric,
we get
W=O
.
In particular, since it holds that

S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}-S^{\prime}=O,\,[S^{\prime},T^{\prime}]=O
, then it is flat.
∎

Combining the flat conditions and Ricci-flat conditions,
we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.8
.

For the Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle)
,
the metric

\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{a}
is Ricci-flat
if and only if it is flat.
Next,
\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b}
is
Ricci-flat if and only if it holds that

\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}=\mathrm{tr}\,S_{L}^{2}=0,\quad T_{L}=O.

Lastly,
\langle\,,\,\rangle=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c}
is Ricci flat if and only if it is flat when
d=0
, or

\bm{b}=\bm{0},\quad\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}\,\!{}^{2}=\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\prime}

when
d=1
.

To summarize this subsection, Ricci-flat but non-flat metrics can exist only in case
(b) or case (c) when
d\neq 0
.

4.
Petrov solution

For a four-dimensional spacetime,
the following theorem is known.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
23
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

If a four-dimensional Lie group
(G,g_{G})
with a left-invariant Lorentzian metric is Ricci-flat but neither
locally symmetric nor a plane wave,
then it is locally homothetic to the Lie group determined by

[X_{4},X_{1}]=X_{1}+\sqrt{3}X_{2},\ [X_{4},X_{2}]=-\sqrt{3}X_{1}+X_{2},\ [X_{4},X_{3}]=-2X_{3},

where the set of generators
\{X_{1},X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
is an orthonormal basis such that
X_{3}
is timelike.

The Lie group above is almost abelian and corresponds to our case (b).
Its associated matrix is

(4.1)

\displaystyle A=\begin{bmatrix}\,1&-\sqrt{3}\,\\
\,\sqrt{3}&1\,\\
&&-2\,\\
\end{bmatrix}.

This is in the normal form (i) in Proposition
3.4
.
By using our formula (
3.2
), we can easily obtain the explicit metric:

(4.2)

\displaystyle ds^{2}=e^{-2x_{4}}\left[\cos(2\sqrt{3}x_{4})(-dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})-2\sin(2\sqrt{3}x_{4})dx_{1}dx_{2}\right]+e^{4x_{4}}dx_{3}^{2}+dx_{4}^{2}.

This is the Petrov solution
[
5
]
(see also
[
7
, (7.3)]
or
[
8
, (12.14)]
).

An
n
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold

(M,g_{M})
is called a
pp-wave
if
there exists a parallel null vector field
V
such that
it is transversally flat, that is,
for any
X,Y\in V^{\bot}
it holds that
R(X,Y)=0
,
where
R
denotes the curvature tensor of
(M,g_{M})
and

V^{\bot}:=\{X\in\Gamma(TM)\mid g_{M}(X,V)=0\},

which is a subbundle of the tangent bundle of
M
with rank
n-1
.
We should remark that
V^{\bot}
is not the complementary distribution of
the line bundle generated by
V
.
Actually, it trivially holds that
V\in\Gamma(V^{\bot})
.

A Lorentzian manifold
(M,g_{M})
is called a
pr-wave
if
there exists a recurrent null vector field
V
such that
it is transversally flat.
A vector field
V
is
recurrent

if there exists a
1
-form
\omega
of
M
such that

\nabla V=V\otimes\omega
,
where
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
(M,g_{M})
.
Obviously, if it is a pp-wave, then it is a pr-wave.
The converse is not true in general, but the following is known.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
24
, Proposition 6.11]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be an
n
-dimensional pr-wave.
It is a pp-wave if and only if
its Ricci operator is totally isotropic,
that is, for any vector fields
X,Y
it holds that

g_{M}(Q(X),Q(Y))=0
,
where
Q
is the Ricci operator of
(M,g_{M})
.
In particular, a Ricci-flat pr-wave is a pp-wave.

A Lorentzian manifold
(M,g_{M})
is called a
plane wave

if it is a pp-wave and it holds that
\nabla_{X}R=0

for any
X\in\Gamma(V^{\bot})

Proposition 4.3

(
[
25
, Corollary 1]
)
.

Let
(M,g_{M})
be an
n
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold.
If it is a Ricci-flat pp-wave, then it is a plane wave.

Henceforth,
we determine a Ricci-flat plane wave for
an almost abelian Lie group
(G,g_{G})

with a left-invariant Lorentzian metric.

Lemma 4.4
.

For the Ricci-flat Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})
in case (i),
setting

2\alpha+\lambda_{3}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1}=2\alpha^{2}-2\beta^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{n-1}^{2}=0

and
S_{L}
as

S_{L}=\begin{bmatrix}\,\alpha&-\beta\,\\
\,\beta&\alpha\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\oplus\mathrm{Diag}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})\quad(\beta>0),

it is neither locally symmetric nor a plane wave.

Proof.

We have already shown that it is not locally symmetric in Lemma
3.7
,
so we only have to show that it is not a pp-wave.
We prove this by contradiction, that is,
assume that it is a pp-wave.
Then, it admits a parallel null vector field
V
,
and it is transversally flat.
At the identity element
e
of
the corresponding almost abelian Lie group
G
,
we can regard it as

T_{e}G\ni V_{e}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}X_{i}\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfies

-\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}^{2}=0,\,\alpha_{1}\neq 0
.
Setting
Y=\alpha_{2}X_{1}+\alpha_{1}X_{2},\,Z=\alpha_{n}X_{1}+\alpha_{1}X_{n}
,
we have
Y,Z\in\Gamma(V^{\bot})
.
From the transversally flatness
R(Y,Z)=0
,
we have

\begin{bmatrix}(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}&-2\alpha\beta\alpha_{1}^{2}\\
2\alpha\beta\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}&(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})\alpha_{1}^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}&-2\alpha\beta\\
2\alpha\beta&\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\\
\alpha_{1}^{2}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\bm{0},

which implies that
\alpha_{1}=0

since
\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}\neq 0
.
This contradicts
\alpha_{1}\neq 0
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For the Ricci-flat Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})
in case (iii),
setting
S_{L}
as

S_{L}=\begin{bmatrix}\,-\delta&-\delta&-1\,\\
\,\delta&\delta&1\,\\
\,1&1&0\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\oplus O_{n-4}\quad(\delta\in\mathbb{R}),

it is a plane wave.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.3
,
we only have to show that it is a pp-wave.
We define
V=X_{1}-X_{2}
,
and it gives a parallel null vector.
In fact, we have
L_{X_{a}}V=0
for
a=1,\ldots,n
.
Moreover, at the identity element, the fibre of the distribution
V^{\bot}

is spanned by
V,X_{3},\ldots,X_{n}
.
Then for any
3\leq i,j\leq n
we have

R(V,X_{i})=R(X_{i},X_{j})=0.

Thus, it is a pp-wave.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For the Ricci-flat Lorentzian almost abelian Lie algebra

(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{c})
in case
d=1
,
setting
A
as

A=\begin{bmatrix}\,A^{\prime}&\bm{0}\,\\
\,\bm{c}^{T}&1\,\\
\end{bmatrix}\quad(A^{\prime}\in\mathrm{M}_{n-2}\mathbb{R},\,\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-2}),

it is a plane wave.

Proof.

From Proposition
4.3
,
we only have to show that it is a pp-wave.
We define
V=X_{n-1}
, and it is not parallel, but a recurrent null vector.
In fact, we have
L_{X_{i}}V=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
and
L_{X_{n}}V=V
.
Moreover, at the identity element, the fibre of the distribution
V^{\bot}

is spanned by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}
.
Then for any
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
we have
R(X_{i},X_{j})=0
.
Thus it is a pr-wave.
From Proposition
4.2
, it becomes a pp-wave.
∎

From Lemma
4.4
,
4.5
and
4.6
, we have the following claim.

Proposition 4.7
.

An almost abelian Lie group with a Ricci-flat left-invariant Lorentzian metric
which is neither locally symmetric nor a plane wave is limited to the solution in Lemma
4.4
.
It is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the metric is given by

(4.3)

ds^{2}=e^{-2\alpha x_{n}}\left[\cos(2\beta x_{n})(-dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2})-2\sin(2\beta x_{n})dx_{1}dx_{2}\right]+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}e^{-2\lambda_{i}x_{n}}dx_{i}^{2}+dx_{n}^{2},

where
\alpha,\beta,\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
satisfy

(4.4)

2\alpha+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}=2\alpha^{2}-2\beta^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{n-1}\lambda_{i}^{2}=0,\quad\beta>0,\quad\lambda_{3}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n-1}.

Since
\alpha,\beta
are uniquely determined by

\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}
, we denote the solutions by

\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})=(\mathbb{R}^{n},ds^{2}),

where
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a Lie group structure
as the almost abelian Lie group,
the metric
ds^{2}
is given by (
4.3
),
and we investigate their properties in the remainder of this paper.
First of all,
when
n=4
, the solution coincides with the Petrov solution (
4.2
) up to the scaling of the metric.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
decomposable

if there exist non-trivial Lie subalgebras

\mathfrak{g}_{1},\,\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subset\mathfrak{g}
such that
it decomposes into a direct sum
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
.
Otherwise
\mathfrak{g}
is called
indecomposable
.
A Lie group is defined to be decomposable
if its Lie algebra is decomposable.

Proposition 4.8
.

For the solution
\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})
,
it is decomposable if and only if there exists
3\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\lambda_{i}=0
.
Furthermore, when we set the number as

m:=\#\{i\in\{3,\ldots,n-1\}\mid\lambda_{i}=0\},

it is isometric to the product of an indecomposable one

\mathbb{P}(\lambda^{\prime}_{3},\ldots,\lambda^{\prime}_{n-m-1})

and a flat Euclidean
m
-space.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
3\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\lambda_{i}=0
.
We define two subsets of
\mathfrak{g}
as

\mathfrak{g}_{1}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\ldots,X_{n}\},\quad\mathfrak{g}_{2}:=\mathbb{R}X_{i}.

Then since
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}+\mathfrak{g}_{2},\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{2}=\{0\}
hold,
we have

[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{1}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{1},\quad[\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}]=\{0\}.

Namely,
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{2}
is decomposable.
Next, we consider the contraposition and assume that
\lambda_{i}\neq 0
for any
i=3,\ldots,n-1
.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is decomposable, then
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
,
where
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
is almost abelian and
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}
is abelian from
[
26
, Lemma 1]
.
So
[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}]=[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}]=\{0\}
.
If
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{a}
holds,
then for
Y\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\setminus\{0\}
we can express

Y=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\mu_{i}X_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{n-1}\}

and there exists
1\leq j\leq n-1
such that
\mu_{j}\neq 0
.
Then for
X_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

[X_{n},Y]=\mu_{1}(\alpha X_{1}+\beta X_{2})+\mu_{2}(-\beta X_{1}+\alpha X_{2})+\mu_{3}\lambda_{3}X_{3}+\cdots+\mu_{n-1}\lambda_{n-1}X_{n-1}\neq 0.

Thus
[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}]\neq\{0\}
.
If
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{a}
does not hold,
then there exist
Z\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\,A\in\mathfrak{a}
and
\rho\neq 0

such that
Z=A+\rho X_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Then we have

[Z,X_{3}]=\rho\lambda_{3}X_{3}\neq 0.

Thus
[\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}]\neq\{0\}
.
Namely,
\mathfrak{g}
is indecomposable.
Furthermore, setting

m:=\#\{i\in\{3,\ldots,n-1\}\mid\lambda_{i}=0\},

the metric (
4.3
) contains the part
dx_{j}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{m+j-1}^{2}
.
Then
(\mathbb{R}^{m},\,dx_{j}^{2}+\cdots+dx_{m+j-1}^{2})
is a flat Euclidean
m
-space,
so we get the latter claim.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Let the solution
\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})

be indecomposable.
Then the isometric group acts on it almost simply-transitively
if and only if
\lambda_{3}>\ldots>\lambda_{n-1}
holds.

Proof.

Assume that there exists
3\leq i\leq n-1
such that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i+1}
(possibly equal to zero).
Then the metric (
4.3
) contains the part
e^{-2\lambda_{i}x_{n}}(dx_{i}^{2}+dx_{i+1}^{2})
.
So the orthogonal group

\mathrm{O}(2)
acts on and preserves the part of the metric.
Namely, the dimension of the isotropy group is greater than or equal to one.
Conversely, let
\lambda_{3}>\ldots>\lambda_{n-1}
.
Here we define two symmetric groups as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{O}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})

\displaystyle:=\{\Phi\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{GL}_{n}\mathbb{R}\mid\langle\Phi(\cdot),\Phi(\cdot)\rangle_{b}=\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b}\}=\mathrm{O}(1,n-1),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})

\displaystyle:=\{\Phi\in\mathrm{O}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})\mid R(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))=\Phi R(X,Y)\Phi^{-1}\ (\forall X,Y\in\mathfrak{g})\},

where we recall that
R
denotes the curvature operator.
By denoting the isotropy group of the isometry group of

\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1})
at the identity element as
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}))_{\bm{0}}
, we observe that

\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{P}(\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}))_{\bm{0}}\subset\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})\subset\mathrm{O}(\mathfrak{g},\langle\,,\,\rangle_{b})\subset\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g}).

We define two subsets of
\mathrm{O}(1,n-1)
as

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathrm{Diag}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon,\tau_{3},\ldots,\tau_{n-1},1)\mid\varepsilon=\pm 1,\ \tau_{l}=\pm 1\ (3\leq l\leq n-1)\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\varepsilon&&&&\\
&-\varepsilon&&&&\\
&&&&\tau_{3}\\
&&&\iddots&\\
&&\tau_{n-1}&&\\
&&&&&-1\\
\end{bmatrix}\,\middle|\ \varepsilon=\pm 1,\ \tau_{l}=\pm 1\ (3\leq l\leq n-1)\right\},

and we compute the conditions

R(\Phi(X_{i}),\Phi(X_{j}))=\Phi R(X_{i},X_{j})\Phi^{-1}

for the generators

.
When

we have

Otherwise, we have

On the other hand,

Namely, the action of the isometry group is almost simply-transitive.

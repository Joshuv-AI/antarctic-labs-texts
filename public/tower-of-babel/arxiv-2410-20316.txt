Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of vector fields on algebraic varieties
Yuly Billig, Kathlyn Dykes

Abstract
. For an affine algebraic variety, we introduce algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of polynomial vector fields with coefficients in differentiable A ​ V AV -modules. Its complex is given by cochains that are differential operators in the sense of Grothendieck. Using the jets of vector fields, we compute this cohomology for varieties with uniformizing parameters. We prove that in this case, Gelfand-Fuks cohomology with coefficients in a tensor module decomposes as a tensor product of the de Rham cohomology of the variety and the cohomology of the Lie algebra of vector fields on affine space, vanishing at the origin. We explicitly compute this cohomology for affine space, the torus, and Krichever-Novikov algebras.

1.
Introduction

The study of Lie algebras of vector fields goes back to the foundational works of Sophus Lie
[
Lie80
]
and Élie Cartan
[
Car09
]
.
Starting in the late 1960s, Gelfand’s school actively studied cohomology theory for the Lie algebras of vector fields. It was realized, that beyond the simplest examples, computation of the general cohomology of these algebras is intractable. To make computations possible, a special cohomology theory was introduced by Gelfand and Fuks
[
GF69
]
. Gelfand-Fuks cohomology was defined in an analytic setting of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
varieties.
For a comprehensive overview of this work, see
[
Fuk86
]
. In the context of complex varieties, a subcomplex, called the diagonal complex, was introduced, see Tsujishita
[
Tsu81
]
for many of these results.

Although there was much progress in this theory, the standard approach was computationally difficult, causing the area to stall for many years. There has been some recent progress. On the algebraic side, Hennion and Kapranov
[
HK23
]
solved an outstanding open problem, proving that the Lie algebra cohomology of vector fields on a smooth affine variety with coefficients in the trivial module is finite dimensional. To do this, they developed an approach using factorization algebras. On the topological side, the work of Williams
[
Wil24
]
computed the local cohomology of vector fields on a manifold, connecting the diagonal complex with the work of Hennion and Kapranov.

In this paper, we introduce algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology, working in a purely algebraic setting. We take advantage of the new machinery that was recently developed in representation theory. A key tool that we make use of in this paper is
AV
-modules.
AV
-modules are modules that admit simultaneous actions of a commutative algebra of polynomial functions on an affine variety and the Lie algebra of vector fields, such that these actions are compatible via the Leibniz rule.
AV
-modules were instrumental for the classification of simple weight modules for the Lie algebra of vector fields on a torus
[
BF16
]
and on an affine space
[
XL23
,
GS22
]
. In particular, we will need the notion of a differentiable
AV
-module, i.e., a module for which the representation is a differential operator in the sense of Grothendieck. Differentiable modules are studied in
[
BR23
]
.

The main goal of this paper is to develop a new algebraic approach to Gelfand-Fuks cohomology theory. The results we obtain are not surprising – they agree with those obtained in the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
setting.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an affine algebraic variety with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
V
be the Lie algebra of vector fields and
A
be the algebra of regular functions on
X
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
be the Lie algebra of vector fields on an affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
vanishing at the origin.
Let
W
be a finite-dimensional module for
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
, and let
A\otimes W

be the corresponding tensor module on
X
.
Then we have the following isomorphism:

H^{*}_{{\textup{GF}}}(V,A\otimes W)\cong H_{{\textup{dR}}}^{*}(X)\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

Let us outline our method for computing the algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology.
First, we show that the cohomology of
V
with values in a differentiable
AV
-module
M
coincides with the
A
-linear cohomology of the Lie algebra of polynomial jets
A\#V
:

H^{*}(V,M)\cong H^{*}_{A}(A\#V,M).

If we apply this isomorphism to the Gelfand-Fuks complex, on the right-hand side we will get the finite cohomology of the completed Lie algebra
A\widehat{\#}V
of
\infty
-jets of vector fields on
X
:

H^{*}_{\text{GF}}(V,M)\cong H^{*}_{A,\text{fin}}(A\widehat{\#}V,M).

By a result of
[
BIN23
,
BI22
]
, on a variety with uniformizing parameters, the Lie algebra
A\widehat{\#}V
of
\infty
-jets of vector fields is isomorphic to a semi-direct product:

A\widehat{\#}V\cong V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}).

To compute
H^{*}_{A,\text{fin}}(V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}),A\otimes W)
, we establish a Künneth formula for this semi-direct product (Theorem 5.2), and obtain

H^{*}_{A,\text{fin}}(V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}),A\otimes W)\cong H^{*}_{A}(V,A)\otimes H^{*}_{\text{fin}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+},W).

Finally, we simplify the right-hand side by showing both
H^{*}_{\text{fin}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+},W)=H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)
, and that
H^{*}_{A}(V,A)

is the de Rham cohomology of
X
:

H^{*}_{A}(V,A)\cong H_{{\textup{dR}}}^{*}(X).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce the main notion of the paper: algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of vector fields with values in a differentiable
AV
-module. We review some necessary background on the jets of vector fields in étale charts in Section
3
, which will be our main tools. We describe the de Rham cohomology as the Lie algebra cohomology of vector fields in Section
4
. We establish a Künneth formula for semi-direct products of Lie algebras of vector fields in Section
5
. In Section
6
, we compute the algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology with coefficients in tensor modules for varieties with uniformizing parameters and we explicitly compute this cohomology for the affine space, the torus, and Krichever-Novikov algebras in the last Section of the paper.

2.
Algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology

Let
(A,V)
be a Rinehart pair, that is, let
A
be a unital commutative associative algebra and
V
be a Lie algebra, such that
V
is an
A
-module,
and
A
is a
V
-module with
V
acting on
A
by derivations, satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle[\eta,f\mu]

\displaystyle=\eta(f)\mu+f[\eta,\mu],

(2)

\displaystyle(f\mu)(g)

\displaystyle=f(\mu(g))\ \ \ \text{for}\,f,g\in A,\ \eta,\mu\in V.

A prime example of a Rinehart pair is given by vector fields on a variety.
Let
X
be a smooth irreducible affine algebraic variety over a field
{\mathbbm{k}}
of characteristic zero. To construct a Rinehart pair, we take
A
to be the coordinate ring of
X
and
V
the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on
X
,
V={\operatorname{Der}}\,A
.

Definition 2.1
.

For a Rinehart pair
(A,V)
, a module
M
is called an
AV
-module
if
M
is an
A
-module, a
V
-module, and their actions are compatible via the Leibniz rule

(3)

\displaystyle\eta(fm)=\eta(f)m+f(\eta\,m)\ \ \ \text{for}\,\eta\in V,f\in A,m\in M.

In the context of algebraic varieties,
AV
-modules are generalizations of
D
-modules. Every
D
-module is automatically an
AV
-module, however there is an abundance of natural
AV
-modules on
X
which do not admit a
D
-module structure. In particular, vector fields
V
themselves are an
AV
-module, but not a
D
-module. In the category of
AV
-modules,
D
-modules are characterized by an additional axiom:

(f\eta)\,m=f\,(\eta\,m).

Definition 2.2
.

An
AV
-module
M
is called
differentiable
(or
N
-differentiable) if there exists
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

(4)

\sum_{j=0}^{N}(-1)^{j}\binom{N}{j}f^{N-j}\left(f^{j}\eta\right)m=0\ \ \ \text{for all}\ f\in A,\eta\in V,m\in M.

Being a differentiable
AV
-module is equivalent to the condition that the representation map
V\rightarrow{\operatorname{End}}(M)
is a differential operator in the sense of Grothendieck
[
Gro67
]
.

D
-modules are precisely 1-differentiable
AV
-modules.
By
[
BR23
, Proposition 4.7]
, every
AV
-module which is finitely generated as an
A
-module is differentiable.

Consider the cochain complex
\{C^{k}(V,M)={\operatorname{Hom}}(\bigwedge^{k}V,M),d\}

with the Chevalley-Eilenberg differentials:

\displaystyle(d_{k}{\varphi})(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}{\varphi}([\eta_{s},\eta_{t}],\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{t},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s\leq k+1}(-1)^{s}\eta_{s}\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

for
{\varphi}\in C^{k}(V,M)
,
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k+1}\in V
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, where the notation
\hat{\eta}_{i}
means that this element is omitted.

Next, we define the main notion of this paper –
algebraic Gelfand-Fuks
cohomology with values in a differentiable
AV
-module
M
.
For each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define

\displaystyle C_{{\textup{GF}}}^{k}(V,M)=\big\{{\varphi}\in C^{k}(V,M)\ \big|\

\displaystyle\exists p>0\ \ \forall f\in A,\,\forall\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k}\in V,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k})=0\big\}.

For
k=0
we set
C_{{\textup{GF}}}^{0}(V,M)=M
.
The differential maps
d_{k}:C_{\textup{GF}}^{k}(V,M)\rightarrow C_{\textup{GF}}^{k+1}(V,M)
are the
usual Chevalley-Eilenberg differentials. We first show that this indeed forms a subcomplex so that we can take its cohomology.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
M
be a differentiable
AV
-module. Then

\{C^{*}_{{\textup{GF}}}(V,M),d\}
is a subcomplex of the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

\{C^{*}(V,M),d\}
.

Proof.

Let
{\varphi}\in C_{{\textup{GF}}}^{k}(V,M)
and let
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})=0
. First note that if such a
p
exists for
{\varphi}
, then this sum is also zero for every
q\geq p
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}f^{p+1-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})

\displaystyle=f\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}(f\eta_{1}),\dots,\eta_{k}).

Thus we can assume without a loss of generality that
M
is
(p+1)
-differentiable.
Consider arbitrary
f\in A,\ \eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k+1}\in V
. The differential behaves as follows:

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(d{\varphi})(f^{i}\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k+1})&=\sum_{2\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},[\eta_{s},\eta_{t}],\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{t},\dots,\eta_{k+1})\\
&\quad+\sum_{2\leq s\leq k+1}(-1)^{s}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}[\eta_{1},\eta_{s}],\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})\\
&\quad+\sum_{2\leq s\leq k+1}(-1)^{s}\,\eta_{s}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})\\
&\quad-\sum_{2\leq s\leq k+1}(-1)^{s}\,{\varphi}(\eta_{s}(f^{i})\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})\\
&\quad-(f^{i}\eta_{1}){\varphi}(\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k+1}).\end{split}

When we consider the summation
\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}f^{p+1-i}(d{\varphi})(f^{i}\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{k+1})
, the first two sums in (
5
) will be zero as
{\varphi}
is a Gelfand-Fuks cochain. The last sum is also zero since
M
is
(p+1)
-differentiable.
Let us show that for each of the remaining terms the summation will result in zero for each
s
. For the third term we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}

\displaystyle f^{p+1-i}\eta_{s}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\eta_{s}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}f^{p+1-i}{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}\eta_{s}(f^{p+1-i})\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1}).

The first part is zero since
{\varphi}
is a Gelfand-Fuks cochain. For the second part:

\displaystyle-\eta_{s}(f)\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}(p+1-i)f^{p-i}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=-(p+1)\eta_{s}(f)\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}\,{\varphi}(f^{i}\eta_{1},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1}),

which also vanishes. Now for the fourth term in (
5
):

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}\binom{p+1}{i}f^{p+1-i}{\varphi}(\eta_{s}(f^{i})\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p+1}(-1)^{i}i\binom{p+1}{i}f^{p+1-i}{\varphi}(f^{i-1}\eta_{s}(f)\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=(p+1)\sum_{j=0}^{p}(-1)^{j+1}\binom{p}{j}f^{p-j}{\varphi}(f^{j}\eta_{s}(f)\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\dots,\hat{\eta}_{s},\dots,\eta_{k+1}),

which is again zero by the Gelfand-Fuks condition.

Thus
d{\varphi}\in C^{k+1}_{{\textup{GF}}}(V,M)
so
\{C_{{\textup{GF}}}^{k}(V,M),d\}
is itself a complex.
∎

Definition 2.3
.

The cohomology of the subcomplex
\{C^{k}_{{\textup{GF}}}(V,M)\}
is called the
algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology
of
V
with coefficients in
M
, denoted by
H_{{\textup{GF}}}^{*}(V,M)
.

Conceptually, Gelfand-Fuks cochains are differential operators

\bigwedge^{k}V\rightarrow M
(in the sense of Grothendieck
[
Gro67
]
). The Gelfand-Fuks complex introduced above is an algebraic analogue of the diagonal complex defined by Gelfand-Fuks
[
GF69
]
in the context of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
manifolds. In the diagonal complex the value of a cochain at a point depends only on the values at that point of a finite number of partial derivatives of the arguments. We shall make use of the jets of vector fields to see that algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cochains possess the same property.

3.
Rinehart enveloping algebras and jets of vector fields

In this section, we review the results on jets of vector fields in étale charts from
[
BI22
]
. For a Lie algebra
V
, we denote by
{\mathcal{U}}(V)
the universal enveloping algebra. First, recall the definition of the enveloping algebra of a Rinehart pair.

Definition 3.1
.

For a Rinehart pair
(A,V)
, the
weak Rinehart enveloping algebra
is the smash product of
A
with
{\mathcal{U}}(V)
, denoted
A{\#}{\mathcal{U}}(V)
.

Here, the smash product is the vector space
A\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}{\mathcal{U}}(V)
, with defining relations

f{\#}\eta\cdot g{\#}\mu=f\eta(g){\#}\mu+fg{\#}\eta\mu\ \ \ \ \text{for}\ f,g\in A,\eta,\mu\in V.

Definition 3.2
.

For a Rinehart pair
(A,V)
, the
strong Rinehart enveloping algebra

D(A,V)
is the quotient of
A{\#}{\mathcal{U}}(V)
by the ideal
\left<f{\#}\eta-1{\#}(f\eta)\,|\,f\in A,\eta\in V\right>
.

In the context of vector fields on a smooth algebraic variety,
D(A,V)
is the algebra
D
of differential operators.

We call an
AV
-module
M

strong
, if it is a module for
D(A,V)
. Equivalently,
M
is a 1-differentiable
AV
-module. These are
D
-modules in the context of algebraic varieties.

It is an important fact that the subspace
A\otimes_{{\mathbbm{k}}}V\subset A{\#}{\mathcal{U}}(V)
, denoted by
A{\#}V
, is a Lie subalgebra with Lie bracket:

\displaystyle[f{\#}\eta,g{\#}\mu]

\displaystyle=f\eta(g){\#}\mu-g\mu(f){\#}\eta+fg{\#}[\eta,\mu].

In fact,
(A,A{\#}V)
is again a Rinehart pair, and
D(A,A{\#}V)\cong A{\#}U(V)

[
BI22
, Lemmas 6-7]
.
This implies that every
AV
-module
M
is a strong module over the pair

(A,A{\#}V)
.

In the context of vector fields on an affine variety
X
, we will call
A{\#}V
the
Lie algebra of polynomial jets of vector fields on
X
.
A completion of this Lie algebra will yield the
\infty
-jets of vector fields.

To construct this completion,
consider the kernel
\Delta\subset A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A
of the multiplication map m
:A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A\rightarrow A
. The completion of the tensor product
A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A
(see
[
AM69
]
for details on completions), called the
algebra of jets of functions
, is defined as

A\widehat{\otimes}A=\varprojlim\limits_{s}(A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A)/\Delta^{s}.

As
\Delta^{k}\otimes_{A}V
are ideals of Lie algebra
A{\#}V
, we can define the completion of
A{\#}V
.

Definition 3.3
.

The
Lie algebra of jets of vector fields
is

A\widehat{{\#}}V=\varprojlim\limits_{s}(A{\#}V)\,/\,(\Delta^{s}\otimes_{A}V).

Since
\mathop{\cap}\limits_{s}\Delta^{s}\otimes_{A}V=(0)
,
A{\#}V
is a Lie subalgebra in
A\widehat{{\#}}V
.

By Lemma 12(3) from
[
BI22
]
, the ideal
\Delta^{s}
is spanned by the elements

(g\otimes 1)(1\otimes f_{1}-f_{1}\otimes 1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(1\otimes f_{s}-f_{s}\otimes 1)

with
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\in A
. A linearization argument shows that this ideal is also spanned by the elements of the form

(g\otimes 1)(1\otimes f-f\otimes 1)^{s}.

We conclude that
\Delta^{s}\otimes_{A}V
is spanned by the elements

\sum_{i=0}^{s}(-1)^{i}\binom{s}{i}gf^{s-i}\#f^{i}\eta,

with
f,g\in A,\eta\in V
.
Thus an
AV
-module
M
is
s
-differentiable if and only if it is annihilated by
\Delta^{s}\otimes_{A}V
. This implies that differentiable
AV
-modules are also modules for the Lie algebra of jets of vector fields
A\widehat{{\#}}V
.

Note that we have Lie subalgebra
1{\#}V\cong V
in
A{\#}V
.
We will now show that the cohomology of
V
is the same as the
A
-linear cohomology of
A{\#}V
.

For a Rinehart pair
(A,V)
and a strong
AV
-module
M
,
consider the complex
\{C_{A}^{k}(V,M)={\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(\bigwedge^{k}_{A}V,M),d\}
of
A
-linear cochains with the usual differential maps. We call the cohomology of this complex the
A
-linear cohomology of
V
with coefficients in
M
. The following Lemma shows that this complex is well-defined.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M
be a strong
AV
-module, and let
{\varphi}\in C_{A}^{k}(V,M)
. Then

d{\varphi}\in C_{A}^{k+1}(V,M)
.

Proof.

\displaystyle d{\varphi}(f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}f_{t}\eta_{t}\,{\varphi}(f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{f_{t}\eta_{t}},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}{\varphi}([f_{s}\eta_{s},f_{t}\eta_{t}],f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{f_{s}\eta_{s}},\ldots,\widehat{f_{t}\eta_{t}},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}f_{t}\,\eta_{t}\left(f_{1}\ldots\hat{f}_{t}\ldots f_{k+1}\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{t}},\ldots,\eta_{k+1})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}{\varphi}(f_{s}f_{t}[\eta_{s},\eta_{t}],f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{f_{s}\eta_{s}},\ldots,\widehat{f_{t}\eta_{t}},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}{\varphi}(f_{s}\eta_{s}(f_{t})\eta_{t},f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{f_{s}\eta_{s}},\ldots,\widehat{f_{t}\eta_{t}},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}{\varphi}(f_{t}\eta_{t}(f_{s})\eta_{s},f_{1}\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{f_{s}\eta_{s}},\ldots,\widehat{f_{t}\eta_{t}},\ldots,f_{k+1}\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}f_{1}\ldots f_{k+1}\,\eta_{t}\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{t}},\ldots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}f_{t}\,\eta_{t}(f_{1}\ldots\hat{f}_{t}\ldots f_{k+1})\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{t}},\ldots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}f_{1}\ldots f_{k+1}\,{\varphi}([\eta_{s},\eta_{t}],\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{s}},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{t}},\ldots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}\sum_{s\neq t}(-1)^{t-1}\eta_{t}(f_{s})f_{1}\ldots\hat{f}_{s}\ldots f_{k+1}\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\widehat{\eta_{t}},\ldots,\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=f_{1}\ldots f_{k+1}\,d{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{k+1}).

∎

Consider now
A
-linear cohomology of
A{\#}V
.
By definition, the cochains
C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
can be viewed as the cochains
{\varphi}\in C^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
such that

{\varphi}(f_{1}{\#}\eta_{1},\dots,f_{k}{\#}\eta_{k})=f_{1}\cdots f_{k}\,{\varphi}(1{\#}\eta_{1},\dots,1{\#}\eta_{k}).

Since
1{\#}V
is a subalgebra of
A{\#}V
, we can restrict any cochain
\psi\in C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
to a cochain in
C^{k}(1{\#}V,M)
.
This gives us the restriction map

\displaystyle\overline{\,\cdot\,}:C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)\rightarrow C^{k}(V,M).

We can also lift cochains from
C^{k}(V,M)
to
C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
by
A
-linearity, yielding the map

\displaystyle\widetilde{\cdot}:C^{k}(V,M)\rightarrow C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M),

defined by
{\tilde{\varphi}}(f_{1}{\#}\eta_{1},\dots,f_{k}{\#}\eta_{k})=(f_{1}\cdots f_{k})\,{\varphi}(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The cochain complexes
C^{*}(V,M)
and
C_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)
are isomorphic with
the isomorphisms given by the lift and restriction maps.

Proof.

It is clear that the lift and restriction maps are the inverses of each other.
We need to prove that these maps commute with the differentials. Since the lift and the restriction are the inverses of each other, it is sufficient to check this property for either one of them. Checking that

\overline{d\psi}=d\,\overline{\psi}
is completely straightforward and we omit the details.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For a Rinehart pair
(A,V)
and an
AV
-module
M
, we have an isomorphism of cohomologies

H^{*}(V,M)\cong H_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)
.

Thus by studying the
A
-linear cohomology of the Lie algebra
A{\#}V
, we get the cohomology of
V
.

Next, let us see what is the image of the Gelfand-Fuks subcomplex in
C_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)
. Let
{\varphi}
be a Gelfand-Fuks cochain. Then its lift satisfies the identity

{\tilde{\varphi}}\left(g_{1}\sum_{i=0}^{p}(-1)^{i}\binom{p}{i}f^{p-i}\#f^{i}\eta_{1},\,g_{2}\#\eta_{2},\dots,g_{k}\#\eta_{k}\right)=0,

so
{\tilde{\varphi}}
vanishes when one of its arguments belongs to
\Delta^{p}\otimes_{A}V
.

Definition 3.4
.

A cochain
\psi\in C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
is called
finite
if there exists
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\psi
vanishes when one of its arguments belongs to the ideal
\Delta^{p}\otimes_{A}V
.

We conclude that the lifts of Gelfand-Fuks cochains from
C^{k}(V,M)
to
C_{A}^{k}(A{\#}V,M)
are precisely finite cochains. Thus finite cochains form a subcomplex
C_{A,\text{fin}}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)
, and this complex is isomorphic
to the Gelfand-Fuks complex
C_{\text{GF}}^{*}(V,M)
.

Finite cochains may be extended to the completed algebra
A\widehat{{\#}}V

(Definition
3.3
).
We call a cochain
\psi
in
C_{A}^{k}(A\widehat{{\#}}V,M)
finite if there exists
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\psi
vanishes when
one of its arguments is of the form
\sum_{i=p}^{\infty}x_{i}

with
x_{i}\in\Delta^{i}\otimes_{A}V
. We immediately see that complexes

C_{A,\text{fin}}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)
and
C_{A,\text{fin}}^{*}(A\widehat{{\#}}V,M)

are isomorphic. As a consequence we get the following result:

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M
be an
N
-differentiable
AV
-module. Then

H_{\text{GF}}^{*}(V,M)\cong H_{A,\text{fin}}^{*}(A{\#}V,M)\cong H_{A,\text{fin}}^{*}(A\widehat{{\#}}V,M)\cong\varinjlim\limits_{k\geq N}H_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V/(\Delta^{k}\otimes_{A}V),M).

The Lie algebra of jets of vector fields
A\widehat{{\#}}V
has a particularly nice description when the variety
X
has uniformizing parameters, that is,

X
may be covered by a single étale chart. Let us recall this construction
(see e.g.
[
BI22
]
for details).

For a variety
X
, denote by
{\mathcal{A}}
the sheaf of regular function on
X
and
{\mathcal{V}}
the sheaf of vector fields.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
X
be an affine variety. An
étale chart
on
X
is a Zariski open affine subset
U
of
X
such that there exist functions
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}(U)
satisfying

(i)

\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
is algebraically independent,

(ii)

Every
f\in{\mathcal{A}}(U)
is algebraic over
{\mathbbm{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,

(iii)

The derivations
{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}},\dots,{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}}
of
{\mathbbm{k}}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
can be extended to
{\mathcal{A}}(U)
.

We will call the set
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
the
uniformizing parameters of
U
.

Note that such extensions of
{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}}
are unique and commute with each other.

For the rest of the section, let
U
be a étale chart and set
A={\mathcal{A}}(U)
and
V={\mathcal{V}}(U)
. Then
(A,V)
is a Rinehart pair.

To see the connection of the completions
A\widehat{\otimes}A
and

A\widehat{{\#}}V
to the theory of jets, consider
a homomorphism of commutative algebras
j:A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A\rightarrow A\otimes{\mathbbm{k}}\llbracket t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\rrbracket
with

j(g\otimes f)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}}\frac{1}{m!}g(x)\frac{\partial^{m}f}{\partial x^{m}}t^{m}

(
[
BI22
, Lemma 10]
).
The image
j(1\otimes f)
is the
jet of the function
f
.
In fact,
j
extends to an isomorphism of commutative algebras
A\widehat{\otimes}A
and
A\otimes{\mathbbm{k}}\llbracket t_{1},\dots,t_{n}\rrbracket

[
BI22
, Corollary 18]
.

To give a realization of
A\widehat{{\#}}V
, we need to introduce another Lie algebra,
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
. Denote
{\mathcal{L}}={\operatorname{Der}}({\mathbbm{k}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}])
with the natural
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading by total degree. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{k}
be the
k^{\text{th}}
graded component, so that

{\mathcal{L}}={\mathcal{L}}_{-1}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{1}\oplus{\mathcal{L}}_{2}\oplus\cdots.

We set
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
be the positive part of
{\mathcal{L}}
and note that
{\mathcal{L}}_{0}\cong{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
.
Set
{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k}=\bigoplus_{i=k}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}_{i}
. We will need a completion of the tensor product:

A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}=\varprojlim\limits_{k}A\otimes({\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k}).

We now state the decomposition of
A\widehat{{\#}}V
proved in
[
BI22
]
.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
BI22
, Theorem 22, Remark 23]
)
.

Let
U
be an étale chart of
X
with uniformizing parameters
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
. Let
A={\mathcal{A}}(U)
and
V={\mathcal{V}}(U)
, then

A\widehat{{\#}}V\cong V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}),

A{\#}V/(\Delta^{k+1}\otimes_{A}V)\cong V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k}).

When
U\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
, this isomorphism holds without needing completions:

A{\#}V\cong V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}).

We will use this result to compute Gelfand-Fuks cohomology for varieties with a single étale chart. To do this, we will need to establish a Künneth formula for the semi-direct product
V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k})
.

In Theorem
1.1
, one of the terms in the decomposition is the de Rham cohomology. We revisit this cohomology and show that for the vector fields on an algebraic variety, de Rham cohomology is exactly
A
-linear cohomology of
V
with coefficients in
A
.

4.
de Rham Cohomology of an affine variety

In this section, we will show that the de Rham cohomology of an affine algebraic variety can be realized as the
A
-linear Lie algebra cohomology of vector fields with coefficients in the algebra of functions
A
. For this section, we assume that
X
is a smooth irreducible affine algebraic variety over
{\mathbbm{k}}
. Set
A
be the coordinate ring of
X
and set
V
be the vector fields on
X
.

First, we recall the de Rham cohomology of an algebraic variety. View
A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A
as an
A
-module with the action on the left tensor factor and consider a submodule
I=\langle 1\otimes fg-f\otimes g-g\otimes f\,\big|\,f,g\in A\rangle
. Set
\Omega^{1}=A\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}A/I
and for
f,g\in A
we use the notation
fd(g)
for the image of
f\otimes g
in
\Omega^{1}
. Let
\Omega^{k}(A)=\bigwedge^{k}_{A}\Omega^{1}(A)
. As we take this alternating product over
A
, elements in
\Omega^{k}
are written as
fdg_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge dg_{k}
for
f,g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in A
. We form a complex by defining the de Rham differential
d_{{\textup{dR}}}:\Omega^{k}(A)\rightarrow\Omega^{k+1}(A)
by

d_{{\textup{dR}}}(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})=df\wedge dg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k}.

for
f\in A,dg_{1},\dots,dg_{k}\in\Omega^{1}
. Set
\Omega^{k}=0
for
k<0
and
\Omega^{0}=A
, then the de Rham cohomology
H^{*}_{{\textup{dR}}}(X)
is the cohomology of the de Rham complex
\{\Omega^{i},d_{{\textup{dR}}}\}
:

0\rightarrow A\rightarrow\Omega^{1}\rightarrow\Omega^{2}\rightarrow\cdots.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth irreducible affine algebraic variety, let
A
be the coordinate ring of
X
and let
V
be the Lie algebra of vector fields on
X
. Then the cochain complex
C_{A}^{*}(V,A)
is isomorphic to the de Rham complex
\Omega^{*}_{X}
, and

H^{*}_{A}(V,A)\cong H^{*}_{{\textup{dR}}}(X).

Proof.

We need to show that the two complexes are isomorphic. Consider the map
\Phi:\Omega^{k}(X)\rightarrow{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}\left(\bigwedge^{k}_{A}V,A\right)
by

\Phi(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k})=f\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))

for
f,g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in A,\,\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k}\in V
.
We point out that for
\mu\in V
,

\mu\left(\Phi(dg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k})\right)=\sum_{t=1}^{k}\det\left[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\cdots\mu(\eta_{t}(g_{j}))\cdots\eta_{k}(g_{j})\right]_{j=1,\ldots,k}.

This equality follows from the fact that
\mu
is a derivation of
A
. Using this equality, we can show that

\Phi
is a homomorphism of
AV
-modules. Since
\Phi
is manifestly
A
-linear, we need to check that

\mu\left(\Phi(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})\right)=\Phi\left(\mu(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})\right).

Indeed,

\displaystyle\mu\left(\Phi(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})\right)(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k})

\displaystyle=\mu\left(f\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))\right)-\sum_{t=1}^{k}\Phi(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge[\mu,\eta_{t}]\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k})

\displaystyle=\mu(f)\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))+f\sum_{t=1}^{k}\det\left[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\cdots\mu(\eta_{t}(g_{j}))\cdots\eta_{k}(g_{j})\right]_{j=1,\ldots,k}

\displaystyle-f\sum_{t=1}^{k}\det\left[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\cdots\mu(\eta_{t}(g_{j}))\cdots\eta_{k}(g_{j})\right]_{j=1,\ldots,k}+f\sum_{t=1}^{k}\det\left[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\cdots\eta_{t}(\mu(g_{j}))\cdots\eta_{k}(g_{j})\right]_{j=1,\ldots,k}

\displaystyle=\Phi\left(\mu(fdg_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k})\right)(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k}).

Next, we are going to show that
d_{A}\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ(-d_{dR})
. We have

\displaystyle\Phi(d_{{\textup{dR}}}(g_{1}dg_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k+1}))(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i})).

Let
M_{i,j}
be the determinant of the minor obtained by deleting the
i^{\text{th}}
row and the
j^{\text{th}}
column in the
(k+1)\times(k+1)
matrix
(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))
.
By the cofactor expansion of the determinant along the first row,

\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\eta_{j}(g_{1})(-1)^{j+1}M_{1,j}.

If we apply the differential of the Lie algebra cohomology,

\displaystyle\quad d(\Phi(g_{1}dg_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k+1}))(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}\Phi(g_{1}dg_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k+1})(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{\eta}_{t}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}\Phi(g_{1}dg_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k+1})([\eta_{s},\eta_{t}]\wedge\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{\eta}_{s}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{\eta}_{t}\cdots\wedge\eta_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}\left(g_{1}M_{1,t}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s+t-1}g_{1}\det\bigg[[\eta_{s},\eta_{t}](g_{j})\ \eta_{1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \hat{\eta}_{s}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \hat{\eta}_{t}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{k+1}(g_{j})\bigg]_{j=2,\ldots,k+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}\left(g_{1}M_{1,t}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{s-1}g_{1}\det\bigg[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \hat{\eta}_{s}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{t-1}(g_{j})\ \eta_{s}(\eta_{t}(g_{j}))\ \eta_{t+1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{k+1}(g_{j})\bigg]

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq s<t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t-1}g_{1}\det\bigg[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{s-1}(g_{j})\ \eta_{t}(\eta_{s}(g_{j}))\ \eta_{s+1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \hat{\eta}_{t}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{k+1}(g_{j})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}\left(g_{1}M_{1,t}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}\sum_{s\neq t}(-1)^{t-1}g_{1}\det\bigg[\eta_{1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{s-1}(g_{j})\ \eta_{t}(\eta_{s}(g_{j}))\ \eta_{s+1}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \hat{\eta}_{t}(g_{j})\ \ldots\ \eta_{k+1}(g_{j})\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}\left(g_{1}M_{1,t}\right)+\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t-1}g_{1}\eta_{t}\left(M_{1,t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq t\leq k+1}(-1)^{t}\eta_{t}(g_{1})M_{1,t}=-\det(\eta_{j}(g_{i}))

\displaystyle=\Phi(-d_{{\textup{dR}}}(g_{1}dg_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dg_{k}))(\eta_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\eta_{k+1}).

We now show that
\Phi
is an isomorphism. First, consider
{\varphi}\in{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(\Omega^{1}(A),A)
. Since
\Omega^{1}(A)=A\otimes A/\langle 1\otimes fg-f\otimes g-g\otimes f\rangle
as a left-module, then
{\varphi}
is determined by its action on
1\otimes A
. Set
\psi(f)={\varphi}(1\otimes f)
so that
\psi\in{\operatorname{Hom}}(A,A)
. Since
{\varphi}
is zero on the ideal
\langle 1\otimes fg-f\otimes g-g\otimes f\rangle
, then
\psi(fg)=f\psi(g)+g\psi(f)
so
\psi\in{\operatorname{Der}}(A)=V
. Thus
{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(\Omega^{1},A)\cong V
.

For an
AV
-module
M
, denote by
M^{*}
its dual,
M^{*}={\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(M,A)
.
As
V
and
\Omega^{1}
are
AV
-modules which are finitely generated over
A
, then by
[
BR23
, Proposition 1.2]
they are also projective
A
-modules. Thus by
[
Bou73
]
, these modules are both isomorphic to their double dual:

V^{**}\cong V,\quad\quad(\Omega^{1})^{**}\cong\Omega^{1}.

In particular,
\Omega^{1}\cong V^{*}
.

Now by definition,
\Omega^{k}(A)=\bigwedge^{k}_{A}\Omega^{1}(A)
,
and the alternating product commutes with the dual:

\left(\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}_{A}V\right)^{*}\cong\bigwedge\nolimits^{k}_{A}V^{*}.

Thus
\bigwedge^{k}_{A}\Omega^{1}(A)\cong\bigwedge^{k}_{A}V^{*}\cong\left(\bigwedge^{k}_{A}V\right)^{*}
and hence
\Omega^{k}(A)\cong{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(\bigwedge^{k}_{A}V,A)
so that the two complexes are isomorphic and hence the cohomologies are equal.
∎

5.
A Künneth formula for a semi-direct product

A Künneth formula is the following result about the cohomology of a direct sum:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1},{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
be Lie algebras, and let
W_{1},W_{2}
be modules for
{\mathfrak{g}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}
respectively. Then

H^{n}({\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{2},W_{1}\otimes W_{2})\cong\sum_{i+j=n}H^{i}({\mathfrak{g}}_{1},W_{1})\otimes H^{j}({\mathfrak{g}}_{2},W_{2}).

We shall also write the above isomorphism as

H^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{2},W_{1}\otimes W_{2})\cong H^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}_{1},W_{1})\otimes H^{*}({\mathfrak{g}}_{2},W_{2})
.

For our purposes, we will need a generalization of the Künneth formula for semi-direct products of a particular kind, which can be applied to the Lie algebra

V\ltimes(A\otimes\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+})
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie algebra, and let
W
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. If
A
is a commutative algebra then
A\otimes W
is naturally a module for the Lie algebra

A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
(A,V)
is a Rinehart pair, then we can form a semi-direct product Lie algebra
V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})
, where
V
acts on
A
.
Let
S
be a strong
AV
-module.
Then
S\otimes W
is a module for this semi-direct product, with the action

\displaystyle v\cdot(m\otimes w)=(v\cdot m)\otimes w,

\displaystyle(f\otimes x)\cdot(m\otimes w)=fm\otimes xw,

where
v\in V,f\in A,x\in{\mathfrak{g}},m\in S,w\in W
.
Note that
(A,V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}))
is a Rinehart pair, with a trivial action of
A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
on
A
, and
S\otimes W
is a strong module for this pair. Thus we can define
A
-linear cohomology of
V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})
with values in
S\otimes W
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(A,V)
be a Rinehart pair, let
S
be a strong
AV
-module, and let
W
be a module for the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then

H^{*}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)\cong H^{*}_{A}(V,S)\otimes H^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},W).

The proof is based on the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence
[
HS53
]
. We consider a filtration on the space of cochains
C^{k}=C^{k}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
:

C^{k}=F^{0}C^{k}\supset F^{1}C^{k}\supset\ldots\supset F^{k}C^{k}\supset F^{k+1}C^{k}=(0),

where

F^{p}C^{p+q}=\left\{{\varphi}\in C^{p+q}\,\big|\,{\varphi}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p+q})=0\ \text{for }x_{1},\ldots,x_{q+1}\in A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}\right\}.

It is easy to check that
dF^{p}C^{p+q}\subset F^{p}C^{p+q+1}
.
Let
\{E_{r}^{p,q},d_{r}^{p,q}\}
be the spectral sequence associated with this
filtration (see
[
McC01
]
for details on spectral sequences).

The
E_{0}
term of this spectral sequence is defined as

E_{0}^{p,q}=F^{p}C^{p+q}/F^{p+1}C^{p+q},

and the differential
d_{0}^{p,q}:\,E_{0}^{p,q}\rightarrow E_{0}^{p,q+1}
is induced by the differential of the cochain complex.

The
E_{2}
term of this spectral sequence was computed in
[
HS53
]
(see also Theorem 1.5.1 in
[
Fuk86
]
):

E_{2}^{p.q}=H_{A}^{p}(V,H_{A}^{q}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},\,S\otimes W)).

The argument used in the proof of Proposition
3.2
shows that the complexes
C^{*}_{A}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},\,S\otimes W)
and
S\otimes C^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},W)

are isomorphic. Thus,

H_{A}^{q}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}},\,S\otimes W)\cong S\otimes H^{q}({\mathfrak{g}},W).

Since the action of
V
on
H^{q}({\mathfrak{g}},W)
is trivial, we get that

E_{2}^{p.q}=H_{A}^{p}(V,S\otimes H^{q}({\mathfrak{g}},W))\cong H_{A}^{p}(V,S)\otimes H^{q}({\mathfrak{g}},W).

To complete the proof of the theorem, we need to show that the differentials
d_{r}^{p,q}
vanish for
r\geq 2
, and the spectral sequence stabilizes at
E_{2}
term.

We construct a map between complexes

\ast:\,C^{*}_{A}(V,S)\otimes C^{*}({\mathfrak{g}},W)\rightarrow C^{*}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W).

Define
\ast:\,C_{A}^{k}(V,S)\otimes C^{m}({\mathfrak{g}},W)\rightarrow C_{A}^{k+m}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
by
(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha\ast\beta
, where

(6)

\alpha\ast\beta(v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{1}\otimes x_{1},\dots,f_{m}\otimes x_{m})=f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

for
\alpha\in C_{A}^{k}(V,S),\beta\in C^{m}({\mathfrak{g}},W)
.
Here we assume that every argument of
\alpha\ast\beta
is either in
V
or in
A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
. If the number of arguments of
\alpha\ast\beta
from
V
is not equal to
k
, we set the value of
\alpha\ast\beta
to be zero.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
\alpha\in C_{A}^{k}(V,S)
and
\beta\in C^{m}({\mathfrak{g}},W)

d(\alpha\ast\beta)=d\alpha\ast\beta+(-1)^{k}\alpha\ast d\beta.

Proof.

By definition,
\alpha\ast\beta\in C^{k+m}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
. By the choice of
\alpha
and
\beta
,
\alpha\ast\beta
will only be nonzero on
\bigwedge^{k}V\wedge\bigwedge^{m}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})
and so
d(\alpha\ast\beta)
will only be nonzero on
\bigwedge^{k+1}V\wedge\bigwedge^{m}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})+\bigwedge^{k}V\wedge\bigwedge^{m+1}(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}})
. We will calculate
d(\alpha\ast\beta)
on each summand.

For
v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V
and
f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m}\in A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
, we will use the short hand of
\beta(x)
for
\beta(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})
.

\displaystyle\quad d(\alpha\ast\beta)(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s<t\leq k}(-1)^{s+t-1}f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\alpha([v_{s},v_{t}],v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,\hat{v}_{t},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{0\leq s\leq k}(-1)^{s+1}v_{s}\cdot f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq k,\\
1\leq t\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s+t-1}(-1)^{k+t-1}v_{s}(f_{t})f_{1}\cdots\hat{f_{t}}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s<t\leq k}(-1)^{s+t-1}f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\alpha([v_{s},v_{t}],v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,\hat{v}_{t},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{0\leq s\leq k}(-1)^{s+1}f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\cdot v_{s}\cdot\alpha(v_{1},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq k,\\
1\leq t\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s+1}v_{s}(f_{t})\cdot f_{1}\cdots\hat{f_{t}}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v_{1},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{k})\beta(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq s\leq k,\\
1\leq t\leq m\end{subarray}}(-1)^{s}v_{s}(f_{t})\cdot f_{1}\cdots\hat{f_{t}}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v_{0},\dots,\hat{v}_{s},\dots,v_{k})\otimes\beta(x)

\displaystyle=((d\alpha)\ast\beta)(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

Now for
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V
and
f_{0}x_{0},\dots f_{m}x_{m}\in A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\alpha(v)
be shorthand for
\alpha(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
.

\displaystyle\quad(-1)^{k}d(\alpha\ast\beta)(f_{0}x_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

\displaystyle=d(\alpha\ast\beta)(v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{0}x_{0},f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s<t\leq m}(-1)^{s+t-1}(-1)^{k}\alpha\ast\beta(v,[f_{s}x_{s},f_{t}x_{t}],f_{0}x_{0},\dots,\widehat{f_{s}x_{s}},\dots,\widehat{f_{t}x_{t}},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{0\leq s\leq m}(-1)^{s+k+1}(f_{s}x_{s})f_{0}\cdots\hat{f}_{s}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v)\otimes\beta(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{s}},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq s<t\leq m}(-1)^{s+t+k-1}f_{0}\cdots\hat{f_{s}}\cdots\hat{f_{t}}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v)\otimes\beta([x_{s},x_{t}],x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{s}},\dots,\widehat{x_{t}},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{0\leq s\leq m}(-1)^{s+k+1}f_{0}\cdots f_{m}\alpha(v)\otimes x_{s}\beta(x_{0},\dots,\widehat{x_{s}},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}(\alpha\ast d\beta)(v_{1},\dots,v_{k},f_{0}x_{0},f_{1}x_{1},\dots,f_{m}x_{m})

Thus,
d(\alpha\ast\beta)=d\alpha\ast\beta+(-1)^{k}\alpha\ast d\beta
.
∎

Proposition 5.4
.

The map
\ast
induces a map of cohomology spaces:

\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i+j=n}H_{A}^{i}(V,S)\otimes H^{j}({\mathfrak{g}},W)\rightarrow H_{A}^{n}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W).

Proof.

We use the standard notation
Z^{i}
and
B^{i}
to denote the kernels and images, respectively, of the differential maps
d^{i}
.

The restriction of the map
\ast
to cocycles will have codomain
Z_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
as
d(\alpha\ast\beta)=0
if both
d\alpha=0
and
d\beta=0
. To induce a map on cohomologies, we need the images of
B_{A}^{i}(V,S)\otimes Z^{j}({\mathfrak{g}},W)
and
Z_{A}^{i}(V,S)\otimes B^{j}({\mathfrak{g}},W)
to be contained in
B_{A}^{n}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
. Suppose
\alpha\in B_{A}^{i}(V,S)
then
\alpha=d\delta
for some
\delta\in C_{A}^{i-1}(V,S)
. Then
d(\delta\ast\beta)=d\delta\ast\beta=\alpha\ast\beta
for every
\beta\in Z^{j}({\mathfrak{g}},W)
. Similarly,
Z_{A}^{i}(V,S)\ast B^{j}({\mathfrak{g}},W)\in B^{n}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Returning to the proof of Theorem
5.2
, it follows from
Proposition
5.4
that the elements of
E_{2}^{p,q}
may be represented by cocycles. Since the differential
d_{2}^{p,q}
is induced by the differential of the cochain complex
C^{*}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)
, we get that

d_{2}^{p,q}=0
. By induction, we get that
E_{r}^{p,q}=E_{2}^{p,q}
and

d_{r}^{p,q}=0
for all
r\geq 2
. Thus the spectral sequence stabilizes at the
E_{2}
term and we get

H^{n}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}),\,S\otimes W)\cong\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{p+q=n}E^{p,q}_{\infty}\cong\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{p+q=n}H_{A}^{p}(V,S)\otimes H^{q}({\mathfrak{g}},W).

This completes the proof of Theorem
5.2
.

Remark 5.1
.

We were not able to find Theorem
5.1
in the literature.
Instead, we get it as a corollary to Theorem
5.2
by setting
A={\mathbbm{k}}
.

When
V
is the Lie algebra of vector fields on an étale chart and
A
is the coordinate ring, we can apply this Künneth formula to compute Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of vector fields with values in tensor modules.

6.
Gelfand-Fuks cohomology for varieties with uniformizing parameters

In this section we will summarize our results in the case when variety
X
has uniformizing parameters, i.e., it is covered by a single étale chart.
Such varieties are smooth.
Following the notations from Section
3
, let
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
be the uniformizing parameters on
X
,

A
be the algebra of functions on
X
and
V
be the Lie algebra of vector fields.

In this case
V
is a free
A
-module
[
BFN19
]
:

V=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{i=1}^{n}A\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}.

The associative algebra
D
of differential operators on
X
is also free over
A
:

D=\mathop{\oplus}\limits_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}}A\,\partial^{k},

where
\partial^{k}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\right)^{k_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)^{k_{n}}
.

Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
be the non-negatively graded part of the Lie algebra
{\operatorname{Der}}({\mathbbm{k}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}])
, and let
\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+}
be its completion. From Theorem
3.5
, the Lie algebra of jets of vector fields decomposes as follows:

(7)

A\widehat{{\#}}V\cong V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}).

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module. By Lemma 2 in
[
Bil07
]
, graded components
{\mathcal{L}}_{d}
act as zero on
W
for
d
large enough.
Simple finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-modules are precisely simple finite-dimensional modules for
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}\cong{\mathcal{L}}_{0}
, with
{\mathcal{L}}_{d}
acting trivially for
d\geq 1
.

Given a
D
-module
S
and a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module
W
,
the tensor product
M=S\otimes W
is a module for the semi-direct product

V\ltimes(A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
). Taking into account isomorphism (
7
), we conclude that
M
is a module for
A\widehat{{\#}}V
.
Note that in the isomorphism (
7
), elements of the form

\sum_{j=d}^{\infty}x_{j}
with
x_{j}\in\Delta^{j+1}\otimes_{A}V
are precisely those that are mapped into the space
A\widehat{\otimes}{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq d}
.
Thus for
d
large enough, such elements annihilate
M
and we conclude that
M
is a differentiable
AV
-module.

Let us describe the structure of an
AV
-module on
M=S\otimes W
. Here
A
acts on
S
and the action of the vector fields is given as follows (cf.
[
BFN19
]
):

f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(s\otimes w)=\left(f\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}s\right)\otimes w+\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{k!}\,\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{k}}\,s\otimes\left(X^{k}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{i}}\right)w,

for
f\in A,s\in S,w\in W
. Note that the sum in the right-hand side is finite since elements of
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
of high enough degree annihilate
W
.

Let us mention three important classes of such modules
S\otimes W
.

1. Let
S=A
. In this case we obtain
tensor
modules
A\otimes W
. These generalize bundles of tensor fields on a variety and their bundles of finite jets.

2. Let
S
be a free
A
-module of a finite-rank,
S=A\otimes U
,
\dim U<\infty
. Define gauge fields
B_{i}\in{\operatorname{End}}_{A}(A\otimes U)
,
i=1,\dots,n
, satisfying

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1+B_{i},\,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\otimes 1+B_{j}\right]=0.

Then
S
is a
D
-module with the action of
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

given by
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\otimes 1+B_{i}
.
The corresponding
AV
-module
S\otimes W
is called a
gauge
module.

3. Let
S
be a
D
-module of delta-functions, supported at a fixed point
P\in X
. Module
S
is spanned by all partial derivatives of the delta-function
\delta_{P}
, so

S\cong{\mathbbm{k}}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right]\delta_{P}.

The corresponding
AV
-module
M=S\otimes W
is called a
Rudakov
module (see
[
BFN19
,
BB24
]
for details).

We are interested in the Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of
V
with coefficients in an
AV
-module
M=S\otimes W
. By Proposition
3.4
,

H^{*}_{\text{GF}}(V,M)\cong H^{*}_{A,\text{fin}}(A\widehat{{\#}}V,M)\cong\varinjlim\limits_{k}H_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V/(\Delta^{k}\otimes_{A}V),M).

By Theorem
3.5
, Lie algebra
A{\#}V/(\Delta^{k+1}\otimes_{A}V)
decomposes into the semi-direct product
V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k})
, and
M=S\otimes W
.
Our Künneth formula from Section
5
states that

H^{*}_{A}(V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k}),\,S\otimes W)\cong H^{*}_{A}(V,S)\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k},W).

The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading on the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
is given by the adjoint action of the element
X_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{1}}+\ldots+X_{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial X_{n}}
. Module
W
has a compatible grading by the generalized eigenvalues of this operator. By Theorem 1.5.2 in
[
Fuk86
]
, all cocycles in
H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)
have degree zero. Since the grading on
W
is finite, this implies that all cocycles in
H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)
are finite and

H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)\cong H^{*}_{\text{fin}}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)\cong H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+}/{\mathcal{L}}_{\geq k},W)

for large enough
k
.
Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

(a) Let
X
be an algebraic variety with uniformizing parameters. Let
S
be a
D
-module, and let
W
be a finite-dimensional module for the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
. Then the algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology of the Lie algebra
V
of vector fields on
X
with values in
AV
-module
M=S\otimes W
is given by

H^{*}_{\text{GF}}(V,M)\cong H^{*}_{A}(V,S)\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

(b) In the case when
M
is a tensor module,
S=A
, we have

H^{*}_{\text{GF}}(V,A\otimes W)\cong H^{*}_{\text{dR}}(X)\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

Remark 6.1
.

The cohomology
H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)
is known explicitly only in some special cases, see
[
Fuk86
]
for details.

7.
Applications

We now consider a few special cases and explicitly compute these cohomologies using the de Rham cohomology and cohomology of
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
.

7.1.
Polynomial vector fields on affine space

In the case of an affine space
X\cong{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
,
{\mathcal{V}}({\mathbb{A}}^{n})
are polynomial vector fields, usually denoted by
W_{n}
. Gelfand’s school studied the continuous cohomology of formal vector fields,
\widehat{W}_{n}
, under the projective limit topology, i.e. they consider continuous functionals from
\bigwedge^{k}\widehat{W}_{n}\rightarrow A
. It is naturally graded by
{\mathbb{Z}}
so that
\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+}
, the Lie algebra of all the elements with strictly positive grading, is defined.

Theorem 7.1

(
[
Fuk86
, Theorem 1.5.4]
)
.

For
W
a tensor
{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
-module and
{\operatorname{Coind}}^{\widehat{W}_{n}}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+}}W
the corresponding space of formal tensor fields, there are natural isomorphisms

H^{*}(\widehat{W}_{n},{\operatorname{Coind}}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+}}W)\cong H^{*}(\widehat{{\mathcal{L}}}_{+},W).

In our setting, we deal with polynomial vector fields
W_{n}
rather than the completion
\widehat{W}_{n}
. In fact, in this case of affine space, we are able to work with the general cohomology theory, which turns out to coincide with the algebraic Gelfand-Fuks cohomology.

By Corollary
3.3
, we have an isomorphism

H^{*}(V,M)\cong H_{A}^{*}(A{\#}V,M).

The Lie algebra
A{\#}V
admits a decomposition into a semi-direct product without needing to pass to a completion:

A{\#}V\cong V\ltimes(A\otimes{\mathcal{L}}_{+}).

Taking
M=A\otimes W
, we apply the Künneth formula (Theorem
5.2
) and we get

H^{*}(V,M)\cong H^{*}_{A}(V,A)\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

Since
H^{*}_{A}(V,A)\cong H^{*}_{\text{dR}}(X)
and the only non-zero de Rham cohomology of affine space
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
is
H^{0}_{\text{dR}}={\mathbbm{k}}
, we get the following corollary.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
X={\mathbb{A}}^{n}
. Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module, and let
M=A\otimes W
be the corresponding tensor
AV
-module. Then

H^{*}(W_{n},A\otimes W)\cong H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

This agrees with the topological result, Theorem
7.1
.

7.2.
Polynomial vector fields on an
n
-dimensional torus

In the case when
X\cong{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
, the Lie algebra of polynomial vector fields on a torus is closely related to structures of interest in physics. For example, in the case of
n=1
, a central extension of this algebra is the Virasoro algebra. The Virasoro and Witt algebras are the prototypical infinite-dimensional Lie algebras, and play an important role in conformal field theory and other areas.

Set
V
to be the polynomial vector fields on an
n
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. The algebra of functions is the algebra of Laurent polynomials
A={\mathbbm{k}}[x_{1}^{\pm 1},\dots,x_{n}^{\pm 1}]
. The Lie algebra
V
of vector fields is a free
A
-module

V=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}A{\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}},

and
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
are uniformizing parameters on the torus.
This Lie algebra
V
is graded by
{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, but unlike in the case of the affine space, this grading has a full support.

Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module, and consider a tensor module
M=A\otimes W
. By Theorem
6.1
,

H_{\textup{GF}}^{*}(V,A\otimes W)\cong H_{\textup{dR}}^{*}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})\otimes H^{*}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

The de Rham cohomology in this case is generated by the cocycles
x_{j}^{-1}dx_{j}
so that
H_{\textup{dR}}^{i}({\mathbb{T}}^{n})\cong{\mathbbm{k}}^{\binom{n}{i}}
, which gives the following result:

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
X={\mathbb{T}}^{n}
. Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module, and let
M=A\otimes W
be the corresponding tensor
AV
-module. Then

H_{\textup{GF}}^{k}(V,A\otimes W)\cong\sum_{i+j=k}{\mathbbm{k}}^{{n\choose i}}\otimes H^{j}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W).

7.3.
Krichever-Novikov algebras

In general, Krichever-Novikov algebras are the algebras of vector fields on a Reimann surface with several punctured points. While the Virasoro algebra relates to conformal field theory of genus zero surfaces, these algebras relate to conformal field theory on higher genus surfaces. For an overview of Krichever-Novikov algebras, see
[
Sch14
]
. In this section, we will consider the special case of meromorphic vector fields on a Riemann sphere with poles at
m+1
prescribed points.

Let
X
be the Riemann sphere punctured at points
\infty,a_{1},\dots,a_{m}
, which we denote by

X=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\backslash\{\infty,a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}
. Then
X
is a 1-dimensional affine variety over
{\mathbb{C}}
with uniformizing parameter
z
, so that
A={\mathbb{C}}[z,(z-a_{1})^{-1},\dots,(z-a_{m})^{-1}]
and
V=A\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
.

For the de Rham cohomology of
X
we have
H^{0}_{\textup{dR}}(X)={\mathbb{C}}
, while
H^{1}_{\textup{dR}}(X)={\mathbb{C}}^{m}
, and is spanned by
(z-a_{i})^{-1}dz
. We obtain the following result:

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
X=\overline{{\mathbb{C}}}\backslash\{\infty,a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\}
.
Let
W
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{L}}_{+}
-module, and let
M=A\otimes W
be the corresponding tensor
AV
-module. Then

H_{\textup{GF}}^{k}(V,A\otimes W)\cong H^{k}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)\oplus\left({\mathbb{C}}^{m}\otimes H^{k-1}({\mathcal{L}}_{+},W)\right).

Abstract
To address the limitations of traditional reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RIS) in spatial control capability, this paper introduces the concept of the fluid antenna system (FAS) and proposes a fluid simultaneously transmitting and reflecting RIS (FSTAR-RIS) assisted non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) multi-user communication system. In this system, each FSTAR-RIS element is capable of flexible mobility and can dynamically adjust its position in response to environmental variations, thereby enabling simultaneous service to users in both the transmission and reflection zones. This significantly enhances the system’s spatial degrees of freedom (DoF) and service adaptability. To maximize the system’s weighted sum-rate, we formulate a non-convex optimization problem that jointly optimizes the base station beamforming, the transmission/reflection coefficients of the FSTAR-RIS, and the element positions. An alternating optimization (AO) algorithm is developed, incorporating successive convex approximation (SCA), semi-definite relaxation (SDR), and majorization-minimization (MM) techniques. In particular, to address the complex channel coupling introduced by the coexistence of direct and FSTAR-RIS paths, the MM framework is employed in the element position optimization subproblem, enabling an efficient iterative solution strategy. Simulation results validate that the proposed system achieves up to a 27% increase in total sum rate compared to traditional STAR-RIS systems and requires approximately 50% fewer RIS elements to attain the same performance, highlighting its effectiveness for cost-efficient large-scale deployment.



I
Introduction

With the emergence of beyond 5G (B5G) and 6G systems, the growing demand for massive connectivity and limited spectrum resources necessitates high spectral efficiency and enhanced user access
[
1
]
. Non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA), by enabling power-domain multiplexing, improves spectrum utilization and mitigates access congestion
[
2
]
. Its integration with advanced wireless technologies has shown great promise in dense multi-user scenarios
[
3
]
. Meanwhile, reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) has gained attention for its ability to reconfigure wireless channels via low-cost passive elements, enhancing coverage, energy efficiency, and interference control
[
4
]
.

Although conventional RIS enhances communication performance, its reflection-only capability limits full-space coverage and user support. To overcome this, simultaneously transmitting and reflecting reconfigurable intelligent surface (STAR-RIS) has been introduced, enabling each element to simultaneously or selectively transmit and reflect signals
[
5
]
, thus extending RIS functionality to both sides and enhancing deployment flexibility
[
6
]
. On this basis, integrating STAR-RIS with NOMA has gained attention due to its potential for improved spectral efficiency. Recent studies have explored STAR-RIS-NOMA in diverse scenarios, including multi-user communication
[
7
]
, covert transmission
[
8
]
, URLLC
[
9
]
, ISAC
[
10
]
, and full-duplex systems
[
11
]
, demonstrating its adaptability to complex communication demands.

Despite the significant potential of RIS, including STAR-RIS, in reshaping the wireless propagation environment, their structural rigidity remains a fundamental limitation. Most existing RIS designs adopt fixed and uniformly spaced element layouts, which restrict the spatial degrees of freedom (DoF) of the system, leading to performance bottlenecks in beamforming accuracy, adaptability, and interference suppression
[
12
]
. To enhance system performance, a common approach is to increase the number of RIS elements to achieve higher array gains. However, as the number of elements grows, the associated channel estimation and feedback overhead increase significantly
[
13
]
. Moreover, an excessive number of elements introduces considerable circuit power consumption
[
14
]
. Consequently, how to realize higher spatial control capabilities with fewer RIS elements has become an appealing and meaningful research direction.

In recent years, the fluid antenna system (FAS) has attracted growing attention as a flexible architecture that enables dynamic repositioning of antenna elements within a predefined region
[
15
]
. This added spatial DoF improves channel quality, interference suppression, and user coverage
[
16
]
, with notable performance gains demonstrated in various scenarios
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.
Given that FAS has shown significant potential in enhancing the spatial regulation ability of communication systems, some researchers have begun to attempt to introduce its ideas into RIS and have proposed the concept of fluid-RIS. This structure allows each unit to move flexibly within the predefined area, with position regulation as a new degree of freedom, and collaboratively optimizes with the traditional phase control mechanism, thereby further enhancing the system performance.

In the existing work, the authors of
[
19
]
proposed a mobile RIS architecture based on the concept of fluid antennas and designed a unified non-uniform discrete phase shift strategy, which effectively improves the system performance under Rician fading channels. The authors of
[
20
]
developed an ISAC system assisted by fluid-RIS. By jointly optimizing the symbol detector, transmit beamformer, and the phase shifts and positions of the fluid-RIS elements, their design significantly reduces sensing mismatch and communication errors while enhancing the performance of multi-target sensing and multi-user communication. The work in
[
21
]
focuses on both single-user single-input single-output (SISO) and multi-user multiple-input single-output (MISO) scenarios, where the positions and phase shifts of the fluid-RIS elements are jointly optimized to achieve a notable improvement in system throughput.

Although the concept of fluid-RIS has shown promising potential, existing studies remain scarce and exhibit several critical limitations. First, most of the current works are based on orthogonal multiple access (OMA), which inherently limits spectral efficiency compared to non-orthogonal schemes. Second, prior research has primarily focused on integrating fluid architectures with conventional RIS designs, while no existing work has systematically investigated the integration of fluid structures with STAR-RIS architectures, restricting their applicability to more complex and full-space coverage scenarios.

Inspired by this, this paper proposes a fluid STAR-RIS (FSTAR-RIS) integrated with NOMA to assist multi-user communication systems. The specific contributions are summarized as follows:

•

A novel multi-user communication architecture is proposed, where each FSTAR-RIS element supports both transmission and reflection functionalities and can flexibly move within a predefined area. This enables simultaneous service to users in both transmission and reflection zones, thereby enhancing the spatial degrees of freedom.

•

The integration of NOMA further enhances spectral efficiency and user connectivity, but also introduces highly non-convex constraints, significantly increasing the complexity of the joint optimization problem.

•

We formulate a challenging non-convex joint optimization problem that simultaneously involves the beamforming design at the base station (BS), the transmission/reflection coefficients of the FSTAR-RIS, and the positions of its elements. To tackle the strong variable coupling, we propose a structured three-layer AO framework, where each subproblem is addressed using appropriate techniques such as semi-definite relaxation (SDR), successive convex approximation (SCA), and majorization-minimization (MM), thus reducing the complexity of the original problem.

•

To overcome the increased coupling among all involved channels caused by the coexistence of direct and FSTAR-RIS-assisted transmission/reflection paths, we propose an MM-based position optimization method. This approach constructs a tractable surrogate function and iteratively refines the element positions, enabling efficient resolution of the otherwise intractable non-convex subproblem.

•

Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed FSTAR-RIS-NOMA system outperforms existing benchmark schemes in multi-user communication scenarios. Specifically, with the same number of RIS elements, it achieves up to a 27% increase in total sum rate compared to traditional STAR-RIS systems. Moreover, to achieve the same total sum rate, the proposed system requires approximately 50% fewer RIS elements, highlighting its potential to reduce deployment cost and system complexity in large-scale STAR-RIS applications.

III
Proposed Optimization Algorithm

In this section, we focus on solving problem (
19

Total sum rate comparison with different STAR-RIS side length.

Fig. 4 presents the total sum rate comparison under varying STAR-RIS side lengths. To facilitate understanding, we first clarify the underlying principle: in the conventional STAR-RIS scheme, as the side length increases, the element positions remain unchanged, maintaining the predefined half-wavelength spacing. In contrast, the proposed F-STAR scheme and D-F-STAR scheme dynamically optimize the element positions based on the available aperture area. As shown in the figure, increasing the side length provides greater spatial degrees of freedom, enabling further performance gains. The proposed scheme consistently outperforms the discrete-position-based D-F-STAR across all side lengths, with the most notable gain of approximately 1.5 dB observed at a side length of
3.5\lambda
. This highlights the advantage of continuous position optimization in fully exploiting the STAR-RIS surface. Moreover, beyond
4.5\lambda
, the rate improvement becomes marginal while the implementation and computational costs grow significantly, suggesting that practical designs should consider the trade-off between performance and cost.

Fig. 6:
Total sum rate comparison with different Qos threshold

Fig. 5 depicts the total sum rate performance under different QoS thresholds per user. It can be observed that the proposed F-STAR scheme consistently achieves the highest performance compared with all baseline schemes. As the minimum QoS requirement increases, the total sum rate initially remains stable. However, once the QoS threshold exceeds a certain level, the system begins to experience a sharp performance drop due to the increasing difficulty of satisfying all user constraints. Eventually, each scheme reaches a point beyond which no feasible solution exists, and the algorithm ceases to operate.
Notably, the proposed F-STAR scheme can accommodate user-specific QoS requirements up to 7 bps/Hz, whereas T-STAR and D-F-STAR can only support up to 2.5 and 5.5 bps/Hz, respectively. This significant advantage arises from the additional spatial DoF introduced by the continuous position optimization in F-STAR, which enables more flexible beamforming and improved interference mitigation. As a result, F-STAR demonstrates superior capability in maintaining service quality under stringent QoS demands.

V
Conclusion

This paper proposed an FSTAR-RIS-assisted NOMA multi-user communication system. In the proposed system, FSTAR-RIS elements supported simultaneous transmission and reflection and were able to move flexibly within a predefined area, thereby enhancing spatial control and adaptability. To maximize the weighted sum rate, a non-convex optimization problem was formulated and solved via a three-layer alternating optimization algorithm, which incorporated a MM framework to handle position optimization under complex channel coupling. Simulation results demonstrated that the proposed system achieved up to a 27% higher sum rate compared to traditional STAR-RIS systems with the same number of elements, or equivalently achieved the same performance with nearly 50% fewer elements, thereby highlighting its potential for cost-efficient large-scale deployment.

Osimertinib before and after chemoradiotherapy for unresectable stage III EGFR-mutated NSCLC: NEOLA Trial Protocol
Authors: Jacqueline V Aredo, Nir Peled, Edurne Arriola, Heather Wakelee, Myung-Ju Ahn, Margriet H Kwint, Rosemary Taylor, Toon van der Gronde, Feruza Nasirova, Ming Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTOsimertinib, a third-generation, central nervous system-active epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR)-tyrosine kinase inhibitor, is a recommended first-line treatment for EGFR mutation-positive (EGFRm) advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), unresectable stage III NSCLC after chemoradiotherapy (CRT), and as adjuvant treatment for resected early-stage NSCLC. NEOLA (NCT06194448) is a global, Phase II, open-label, single-arm, multicenter study, evaluating the efficacy and safety of induction osimertinib prior to CRT, and subsequent osimertinib treatment, in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC. Patients will be screened at 71 sites across 10 countries. Eligible patients will be aged ≥18 years with unresectable stage III EGFRm (exon 19 deletion/L858R) NSCLC eligible for definitive CRT. Patients will receive induction osimertinib (80 mg once daily) for 8 weeks, followed by 2 cycles (6 weeks) of sequential/concurrent platinum-based CRT. Patients without progressive disease (PD) can re-start osimertinib approximately 3–6 weeks after CRT until PD (per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors 1.1) or another discontinuation criterion. The primary endpoint is 12-month progression-free survival rate. Secondary endpoints include objective response rate and disease control rate (at the end of induction), overall survival, event-free survival, and safety. The first patient was enrolled in April 2024, with primary completion expected in 2026.Clinical Trial RegistrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier is NCT06194448 (date of registration 5 January 2024).KEYWORDS: EGFR mutation, EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitor, stage III NSCLC, unresectable, chemoradiotherapy, osimertinib

Introduction

Approximately 20–30% of patients with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) present with locally advanced, stage III disease at the time of diagnosis [1,2]. Of these, 60–90% have surgically unresectable disease [3,4]. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations are reported in approximately 10–40% of patients with stage III NSCLC, depending on geographic location [5,6].

Osimertinib is a third-generation, irreversible, oral EGFR-tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) that potently and selectively inhibits both EGFR-TKI sensitizing and EGFR T790M resistance mutations, with demonstrated efficacy in both advanced and early-stage surgically resectable EGFR mutation-positive (EGFRm) NSCLC, including patients with central nervous system (CNS) metastases [7–16]. The ongoing Phase III LAURA study (NCT03521154) is evaluating osimertinib efficacy and safety in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC without progression during or after definitive chemoradiotherapy (CRT) [17]. In the recent primary analysis of LAURA, a statistically significant progression-free survival (PFS) benefit was observed with osimertinib versus placebo: median PFS was 39.1 months (95% confidence interval [CI]: 31.5–not calculable [NC]) with osimertinib and 5.6 months (95% CI: 3.7–7.4) with placebo; hazard ratio (HR) 0.16; 95% CI: 0.10–0.24, p < 0.001 [17]. In an updated overall survival (OS) analysis, an improved trend toward an OS benefit (maturity 31%) was reported with osimertinib versus placebo despite 78% of patients in the placebo arm receiving subsequent osimertinib treatment: median OS was 58.8 months (95% CI: 54.1–NC) with osimertinib and 54.0 months (95% CI: 42.1–NC) with placebo; HR 0.67; 95% CI: 0.40–1.14 [18]. Osimertinib also provided clinically meaningful improvements versus placebo in CNS PFS and time to death or distant metastases [19]. Based on these results, osimertinib is now recommended for the treatment of patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC after definitive CRT [20]. However, despite the demonstrated efficacy of osimertinib after definitive CRT, there is a clinical need to investigate the earlier introduction of TKIs into treatment regimens, to further improve outcomes for these patients and for patients who may experience progression during CRT, and could benefit from earlier targeted treatment. Therefore, optimization of treatment sequence/duration and investigation of osimertinib as induction treatment prior to CRT is warranted.

Several small studies have shown preliminary efficacy for EGFR-TKIs as induction treatment in stage III EGFRm NSCLC [21–24]. A Phase II study in 20 patients who received induction gefitinib (a first-generation EGFR-TKI) followed by CRT reported a 2-year OS rate of 90% (95% CI: 66–97) [22]. An updated analysis of the study with additional follow-up reported a 5-year OS rate of 70% (95% CI: 45–85) and median OS of 5.5 years (95% CI: 4.9–not estimable [NE]) [23]. The Phase II ASCENT study included 19 patients with stage III EGFRm NSCLC receiving neoadjuvant afatinib (a second-generation EGFR-TKI), followed by CRT with or without surgery and optional afatinib for 2 years. Median PFS was 2.6 years (95% CI: 1.4–3.1) and median OS was 5.8 years (95% CI: 2.45–NE) [24]. In another Phase II study (n = 31), patients received induction osimertinib for 12 weeks, prior to definitive radiotherapy (RT) and/or surgery; here, the objective response rate (ORR) with induction osimertinib was 94% [25]. Despite these findings, the role of induction EGFR-TKIs in different EGFR-TKI and CRT combinations is not fully understood; therefore, further investigation into optimal treatment sequence is required to improve treatment outcomes for patients.

NEOLA (NCT06194448) is a global, Phase II, open-label, single-arm, multicenter study, which will evaluate the efficacy and safety of induction osimertinib, prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment, in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC [26]. The NEOLA study will build on the LAURA primary data to evaluate whether osimertinib may be effective as induction treatment prior to CRT in unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC.

Patients & methods

Participants

Approximately 100 patients will be screened at 71 sites in Asia, Europe, and North America to enroll around 70 patients (Figure 1). Eligible patients include adults aged ≥18 years with histologically documented, non-squamous, squamous or adenosquamous, unresectable stage III NSCLC (according to version 8 of the International Association for the Study of Lung Cancer Staging Manual in Thoracic Oncology) and a World Health Organization performance status of 0 or 1. The patient’s tumor must harbor one of the two common EGFR mutations known to be associated with EGFR-TKI sensitivity (exon 19 deletion [Ex19del] or exon 21 L858R mutation), either alone or in combination with other EGFR mutations, including de-novo T790M, based on a local, preexisting positive EGFRm tumor tissue or plasma test result from a locally accredited laboratory. All patients must be eligible for and are planning to undergo definitive concurrent CRT (cCRT) or sequential CRT (sCRT); and have at least one lesion that can be accurately measured at baseline as ≥10 mm in the longest diameter with computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and is suitable for accurate repeated measurements. Patients who have had incomplete resections of the primary tumor are eligible, provided they did not receive treatment with any chemotherapy, RT, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, or investigational agents and that they have measurable disease after the incomplete resection attempt.

Geographic distribution of the NEOLA study locations.

Exclusion criteria include: any presence of small cell and mixed small-cell and non-small cell histology; prior treatment with any EGFR-TKI, chemotherapy, RT, immunotherapy, or investigational agents for locally advanced, unresectable stage III NSCLC; past medical history of interstitial lung disease (ILD)/pneumonitis, drug-induced ILD, radiation pneumonitis that required steroid treatment, or any evidence of clinically active ILD/pneumonitis; any unresolved toxicities from prior therapy greater than Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) grade 1 (excluding alopecia and prior grade 2 platinum-related neuropathy); any evidence of severe or uncontrolled systemic diseases, including uncontrolled hypertension and active bleeding diatheses, or active infection; a history of another primary malignancy, except for malignancy treated with definitive intent with no known active disease ≥2 years before the first dose of study treatment and of low risk for recurrence; and any clinically important cardiac abnormalities in rhythm, conduction, or morphology of resting echocardiogram. Patients receiving medications or herbal supplements known to be strong inducers of CYP3A4 must stop use ≥3 weeks prior to study treatment.

Study design

NEOLA is a global, Phase II, open-label, single-arm, multicenter study, which will evaluate the efficacy and safety of osimertinib as induction therapy prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC. The study design is shown in Figure 2. Eligible patients will receive 8 weeks (±1-week window to account for variability and CRT scheduling) of osimertinib 80 mg once daily (QD) as induction therapy prior to a CRT regimen and subsequent osimertinib treatment. Tumors will be assessed by the investigator according to Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumours version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) at Weeks 0, 8 and 17, then every 12 weeks until progressive disease. After completion of 8 weeks of induction therapy with osimertinib, and at least a 5-day washout period, patients will receive two 3-week cycles of concurrent or sequential platinum-based CRT. Chemotherapy comprises pemetrexed 500 mg/m2 or paclitaxel 175 mg/m2 plus cisplatin 75 mg/m2 or carboplatin AUC5, given on Day 1 of each 3-week cycle. Patients must receive a total RT dose of 60 Gy ±10% within 6 weeks (±2 weeks). Recommended parameters for RT are: mean lung dose < 20 Gy and/or lung V20 (percentage of normal lung receiving ≥20 Gy) < 35%; mean esophagus dose < 34 Gy; and heart V50 (percentage of normal heart receiving ≥50 Gy) < 25%, V30 < 50%, and V45 < 35%. Following completion of the CRT regimen, patients can re-start osimertinib treatment, after approximately 3 weeks and no later than 6 weeks, if assessment of tumor response per RECIST v1.1 (CT scan and MRI) and brain MRI shows no disease progression. Patients will receive osimertinib 80 mg QD until RECIST v1.1-defined disease progression as assessed by the investigator, intolerable toxicity, or death of the patient. Patients may continue osimertinib following disease progression at the discretion of the clinical investigator if they continue to derive a clinical benefit. Adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs) will be collected from the time of signature of the informed consent form until the follow-up period is completed. SAEs and AEs of special interest (AESIs) considered related to the study intervention and/or study procedures will be collected throughout progression follow-up; only SAEs and AESIs considered related to study intervention will be collected throughout survival follow-up. Considering the potential risk of ILD and radiation pneumonitis associated with RT, rules for dose modification and/or discontinuation due to these events are shown in Supplementary Table S1. Safety evaluation, including assessment of ILD/radiation pneumonitis incidence, will also take place at the planned interim analysis after 30 patients have completed CRT. Patients developing any grade ILD/pneumonitis during the induction phase should permanently discontinue osimertinib and not proceed to receive CRT. In addition, patients with symptomatic pneumonitis following CRT should not proceed to the maintenance osimertinib phase unless their symptoms resolve within 4 weeks to grade ≤1 (Supplementary Table S1). Patients who develop ILD/pneumonitis during the maintenance osimertinib phase should also follow the toxicity management guidance in Supplementary Table S1. AEs will be graded using the CTCAE version 5.

Outcomes

The primary endpoint is to assess the efficacy of induction osimertinib prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment, using investigator-assessed PFS, defined as time from the date of the first dose until progression per RECIST v1.1 or death (by any cause in the absence of progression). The primary measure of interest is the PFS rate at 12 months.

Secondary endpoints include: investigator-assessed ORR (per RECIST v1.1), defined as the proportion of patients who have a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) at the end of induction (8 weeks); investigator-assessed disease control rate (DCR; per RECIST v1.1), defined as the percentage of patients who have a best overall response of CR or PR or stable disease at 8 weeks (data obtained from first dose until the end of the induction phase will be included in the assessments of ORR and DCR); OS, defined as time from the date of the first dose until the date of death due to any cause (the measures of interest being the OS rate at 12 and 24 months); event-free survival (EFS), defined as time from the date of the first dose until disease progression that precludes CRT or completion of CRT, progression during or after CRT, or death due to any cause (the measure of interest being the EFS rate at 12 months); and safety and tolerability.

Exploratory endpoints include investigator-assessed CNS PFS (per modified RECIST v1.1), post-progression outcomes (e.g., investigator-assessed time to second progression or death, time to first subsequent treatment or death, and time to treatment discontinuation) and the relationship between changes in plasma biomarkers (such as circulating tumor DNA) with response during induction osimertinib, CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment.

Statistical methods

The analyses of the data collected within this study will be descriptive only: no formal hypothesis testing is planned. A sample size of 70 patients is planned based on the proportion of patients estimated to be alive and progression-free at 12 months, with an estimated 95% CI width for the 12-month PFS rate not exceeding ±11%.

In the PFS analysis, patients who have not progressed or died at the time of analysis will be censored at the time of their last evaluable RECIST v1.1 assessment. All RECIST v1.1 progression events will be included, regardless of whether the patient withdraws from therapy, receives another anticancer therapy, or clinically progresses prior to RECIST v1.1 assessment following induction therapy with osimertinib (and prior to CRT). However, if the patient progresses or dies immediately after two or more consecutive missed RECIST v1.1 assessments, the patient will be censored at the time of their latest evaluable assessment prior to the two missed visits. ORR and DCR will be presented with an exact Clopper–Pearson 95% CI, and OS and EFS rates will be estimated using Kaplan–Meier methods with 95% CIs calculated using the Greenwood formula. Standard rules will be applied for handling missing data.

The primary analysis will be carried out approximately 12 months after the last patient starts induction therapy and will include PFS and safety. A single interim analysis, planned after 30 patients have completed the induction phase and CRT, or 15 weeks have elapsed since their first dose date, will evaluate ORR, DCR, and safety. The final analysis will be carried out approximately 24 months after the last patient starts induction therapy. The estimated date for completing collection of data for the primary analysis of the study is July 2026, and the estimated study end date is July 2027.

All efficacy analyses will include all patients who received at least one dose of osimertinib. Safety data will also be summarized in this patient population.

Ethics and dissemination

This study will be conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and the Good Clinical Practice guidelines of the International Council for Harmonisation. All patients will provide written informed consent prior to screening. Results will be disseminated via conference presentations and journal publications.

Discussion

The role of EGFR-TKIs prior to definitive CRT in unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC is not fully understood. Studies that combined EGFR-TKI therapy with RT in this setting have had conflicting results; for example, the Phase II RECEL study (n = 20) that assessed erlotinib and concurrent RT followed by consolidation erlotinib reported a significantly longer median PFS compared with CRT alone (24.5 vs 9.0 months, respectively; HR 0.10; 95% CI: 0.03–0.39; p < 0.001) [27]. However, a single-arm Phase II study (n = 27) that assessed gefitinib given concurrently with RT, followed by gefitinib, did not meet its primary endpoint, with a 2-year PFS rate of 30% [28]. Notably, there have been preliminary efficacy benefits observed in small studies that assessed the use of EGFR-TKIs prior to definitive CRT in patients with stage III EGFRm NSCLC [22,23,25]. Phase II studies of first- and second-generation EGFR-TKIs given as an induction or neoadjuvant treatment followed by CRT have resulted in 2-year OS rates of approximately 88–90% [22,24] and 5-year OS of 70% [23]. Another previous Phase II study (n = 31) that assessed the efficacy of osimertinib as a 12-week induction therapy prior to surgery and RT reported an ORR of 94% [25]. Taken together, these promising results from previous small studies indicate that EGFR-TKI induction treatment prior to CRT could provide therapeutic benefits in this setting.

Following results from the Phase III PACIFIC study (NCT02125461) [29], durvalumab administered after CRT became the standard of care for patients with unresectable stage III NSCLC without EGFR Ex19del or exon 21 L858R mutation [20]. The ongoing Phase III LAURA study, evaluating the efficacy and safety of osimertinib following cCRT or sCRT in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC, has demonstrated a statistically significant and clinically meaningful improvement in PFS for osimertinib after CRT compared with placebo: median PFS was 39.1 months (95% CI: 31.5–NC) vs 5.6 months (95% CI: 3.7–7.4) with osimertinib vs placebo, respectively (HR 0.16; 95% CI: 0.10–0.24; p < 0.001), in the recent primary analysis [17]. An updated analysis also indicated an improved trend toward an OS benefit with osimertinib versus placebo [18]. Distant metastases are common in unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC, with the most common site of progression being the brain [22,28]. In LAURA, the incidences of local progression and distant metastases were less common in the osimertinib arm (21% and 16%, respectively) than in the placebo arm (48% and 37%, respectively) [17]. There were also fewer incidences of new lesions in the osimertinib arm than in the placebo arm, respectively (22% vs 68%), including new brain (8% vs 29%) and lung (6% vs 29%) lesions [17]. Additionally, compared with placebo, osimertinib prolonged the time to death or distant metastases (HR 0.21; 95% CI: 0.11–0.38; nominal p < 0.001) and improved CNS PFS (HR 0.17; 95% CI: 0.09–0.32; nominal p < 0.001) [19]. Osimertinib has also demonstrated efficacy in other settings, including in patients with advanced EGFRm NSCLC (including those with CNS metastases) and resected early-stage NSCLC (including the prevention/delay of CNS metastases) [8–12,15,16,30]. Furthermore, both neoadjuvant osimertinib plus platinum-based chemotherapy and neoadjuvant osimertinib monotherapy provided statistically significant improvements in the primary endpoint of major pathological response compared with placebo plus chemotherapy in a Phase III study of patients with resectable EGFRm stage II–IIIB NSCLC (odds ratios 19.82 [95.002% CI: 4.60–85.33; p < 0.0001] and 19.28 [99.9% CI: 1.71–217.39; p < 0.0001], respectively) [31]. Earlier introduction of osimertinib therefore has the potential to improve outcomes and prevent brain metastases when used as an induction therapy, and is a point of further investigation in the NEOLA study.

Considerations for NEOLA recruitment include the challenges in identifying patients with EGFRm NSCLC at screening, which could lead to delays in the primary data readout. There is also no standardized clinical definition of whether a tumor is resectable or not, which could further impact patient recruitment [32].

Limitations of NEOLA include the lack of a placebo or control arm due to there not being a treatment widely approved for induction treatment in this population and the use of placebo being unethical [33]. The requirement for confirmed EGFRm status at screening also introduces a potential selection bias toward patients with slower-growing, larger, or more accessible tumors, potentially impacting the generalizability of the results. Also, as NEOLA will be a relatively small, single-arm Phase II study, involving approximately 70 patients, any encouraging efficacy and safety results would need to be confirmed in a randomized controlled Phase III study and caution will be needed when interpreting any subgroup data.

Given the demonstrated efficacy of osimertinib after definitive CRT in LAURA, further investigation of osimertinib as induction therapy, prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment, is warranted. Thus, the NEOLA study will investigate the efficacy and safety of osimertinib given as an induction therapy prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC.

Conclusion

NEOLA will assess the efficacy and safety of induction osimertinib, prior to CRT and subsequent osimertinib treatment, in patients with unresectable stage III EGFRm NSCLC. This research could help to optimize treatment sequence, and thereby outcomes, for these patients and possibly inform the need for earlier initiation of targeted treatments.

Differences of characteristics, influencing factors, and treatment effects on the survival in patients with first and second primary cervical cancer
Authors: Fan Zhang, Ping Yu, Lixia Xu, Xuwei Chen, Junqiang Du
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
Highlights•The study population from SEER database was large and representative.•The influencing factors are different in the survival of first and second primary CC.•Different treatment options should be taken for first and second primary CC.Keywords: Characteristics, Influencing factors, 5-year mortality, Treatment, Cervical cancer

Introduction

Cervical cancer (CC) ranks as the fourth most common cancer among women and is a leading cause of cancer-specific mortality among women worldwide (Rahangdale et al., 2022, Ran et al., 2022). In 2020, 604,000 new CC cases were diagnosed, and resulted in 342,000 deaths in women globally (Poniewierza and Panek, 2022). After treatment, the survival rate of CC patients is significantly increased (Siegel et al., 2019). As a matter of fact, although advances in diagnosis and therapeutic technologies have brought increased life expectancy for most cancers, it paradoxically has increased the risk of second primary malignancies (SPMs) (Park, 2019).

Current researches have showed CC was more common in women around 50 years of age, and the common histological subtype was squamous cell carcinoma (Wu et al., 2021, Li et al., 2021). The survival of CC is affected by several factors, such as age, cancer stage, and the overall health status (Johnson et al., 2019). Therefore, the follow-up evaluation of these patients is important not only for disease control but also for early detection of SPMs. The risk of SPMs is associated with various factors, including continuous exposure to lifestyle factors, genetic factors, etiological factors, and treatments such as radiation therapy (Nelson and Lai, 2017). Surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy continue to be the main treatment approaches for patients with early-stage CC, while for patients with advanced-stage CC, systemic chemotherapy remains the first choice (Eskander and Tewari, 2015). Radiotherapy as a single modality or in combination with surgery or chemotherapy can both be used for the treatment of CC (Marth et al., 2017, Network and Cancer, 2019). However, the efficacy and side effects of different therapies for the primary cancer and SPMs have generated concerns about their use.

Herein, this study aims to explore the characteristics, influencing factors, and the effect of different treatments on survival in patients with first primary CC and second primary CC respectively, in order to provide some references for the follow-up evaluation and the selection of therapies in different populations in clinical.

Methods

Study design and populations

This is a cohort study. We extracted demographic and pathological data of adult women with first primary CC or second primary CC from the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database in 2004–2015. The SEER database is an authoritative source of information on cancer statistics covers 35 % of population in the United States, which is supported by the United States National Cancer Institution (Akinyemi et al., 2022).

We initially included 36,754 patients with first primary CC and 791 patients with second primary CC from the database. Those who without the information of the International Federation of Gynecology and Obstetrics (FIGO) system stage were excluded. We used the SEER Stat software (17 Registries, Nov 2021 Sub) to obtain the data of patients with CC. Since the SEER database is publicly accessible, no ethical approval of our Institutional Review Board (IRB) was required for this study.

We also collected the demographic and clinical information of 176 patients with CC in the Affiliated Dongyang Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University in 2011–2017. The follow-up ended when patients died or until 30 August 2022. All the methods in this study were carried out in accordance with declaration of Helsinki, ethical approval from our hospital IRB and the informed consents from patients were all obtained.

Diagnosis of first primary CC and second primary CC

The International Classification of Diseases for Oncology third edition site and morphology classification codes (ICD-O-3) for endocervix (C53.0), exocervix (C53.1), and cervix uteri (C53.9) were used to identify the first primary CC and second primary CC (Papatla et al., 2019). The diagnosis of a second primary CC should be more than 6 months after the initial cancer diagnosis, in order to exclude the pre-existing or synchronous cancers (Liu et al., 2015).

Variables collection

We collected the variables including age, race, marital status, tumor grades, histologic types, tumor size, the American Joint Committee on Cancer (AJCC) stage, FIGO stage, the international tumor-node-metastasis (TNM) stage, surgery for CC, chemotherapy for CC, radiotherapy for CC. We also collected the information of the initial cancer in patients with second primary CC, including cancer sites, AJCC stage, TNM stage, tumor grade, surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy.

Outcome and follow-up

The study outcome was 5-year mortality in patients with CC. The follow-up ended when patients died or until the date of last known vital status according to the personal registry, or at 31 December 2015.

Statistical analysis

Normal distributed data were described using mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD) and t test was using for comparation between groups. Non-normal distributed data was described by median and quartiles [M (Q1, Q3)] and rank test was used for comparation. Categorical data were expressed by frequency and constituent ratio [N (%)] and chi-square test (χ2) was used for comparison.

Univariate Cox proportional hazard model was used for covariates and influencing factors screening. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazard models were used to explore the association between first primary CC and second primary CC and 5-year mortality with hazard ratios (HRs) with 95 % confidence intervals (CIs). Kaplan-Meier (KM) curves using inverse probability weighting (IPW) method were drew to reflect the survival rate of patients with first primary CC and second primary CC in 5 years. Also, we explored the effects of different treatments on the 5-year mortality in subgroups of age, AJCC stages, tumor grades and histologic types.

Model 1 was the crude model. Model 2 for the SEER population adjusted for age, race, marital status, tumor grade, histologic type, tumor size, TNM stage, surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy. Model 2 for patients from the Affiliated Dongyang Hospital of Wenzhou Medical University adjusted for age, marital status, T stage, surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy. Two-sided P < 0.05 is considered significant. Statistical analysis was performed using SAS 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).

Influencing factors of survival in first primary CC and second primary CC

The potential influencing factors of survival in patients with first primary CC and second primary CC are showed in Table 3, Table 4 respectively. For the first primary CC, age, marital status, tumor grade, histologic types, tumor size, AJCC stage, and TNM stage were positively associated with the risk of 5-year mortality, while race, surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy were negatively associated with the risk of 5-year mortality (all P < 0.05). Among patients with second primary CC, age, histologic types, tumor size, T stage for both initial cancer and CC, and chemotherapy for the initial cancer were associated with high risk of 5-year mortality, while surgery and chemotherapy for CC, and N stage of initial cancer were associated with low risk of 5-year mortality (all P < 0.05).

It seemed that for the patients with first primary CC, surgery was associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality whatever the patients’ age, AJCC stage, tumor grade, and histologic type was (all P < 0.05). Radiotherapy was associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in patients with different age, AJCC stage I and II, tumor grade II and III, histologic type of squamous cell carcinoma and others (all P < 0.05). Chemotherapy was associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in patients aged ≥ 45 years old, with AJCC stage II, III and IV, tumor grade I-IV, and histologic type of adenocarcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma, and others, while associated with a high risk of 5-year mortality in those who aged < 45 years old, and with AJCC stage I (all P < 0.05).

Among patients with second primary CC, surgery was associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in those who with different age, AJCC stage I and III, tumor grade II, and histologic type of squamous cell carcinoma and others (all P < 0.05). The relationship between radiotherapy and low risk of 5-year mortality was only found in patients with tumor grade II [HR = 0.28, 95 %CI: (0.08–0.95)]. Additionally, chemotherapy was associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in patients aged ≥ 65 years old, with AJCC stage II, tumor grade III, and histologic type of adenocarcinoma, while associated with a high risk of 5-year mortality in those who with AJCC stage I [HR = 2.40, 95 %CI: (1.57–3.67)] (all P < 0.05).

Discussion

This study analyzed the characteristics, influencing factors, and treatment effects on the survival of patients with first primary CC and second primary CC, respectively. The results showed that age, histologic type, tumor size, T stage, and surgery and chemotherapy for CC are influencing factors associated with both first and second primary CC. Marital status, tumor grade, AJCC stage, NM stage, and radiotherapy for CC are significantly associated with the survival of first primary CC, while N stage and chemotherapy for the initial cancer are associated with second primary CC. Surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy are both associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in first primary CC patients with different age, AJCC stages, tumor grades, and histologic types. For patients with second primary CC, effects of different treatments on survival were different in populations of different age, AJCC stages, tumor grades and histologic types.

Increased screening and the extensive application of advanced therapeutic techniques in clinical practice resulted in the earlier detection and significantly improved the survival of cancer patients (Murphy et al., 2018). However, most cancer survivors could face the risk of developing SPMs, due to long-term side effects of chemotherapy and/or radiation therapy and persisting effects of genetic and behavioral risk factors (Copur and Manapuram, 2019). Population-based study has pointed out that CC survivors were at great risk of developing SPMs (Lim et al., 2016), and they were even more likely to die from their SPMs than the initial cancers (Donin et al., 2016). So far, few studies have evaluated the differences on prognosis and therapeutic benefits between first primary CC and second primary CC using a nationwide database. A cohort study demonstrated that patients with a second primary CC had an increased risk of all-cause mortality than those with first primary CC whether in young or middle-aged or old persons (Keegan et al., 2017). The current study extracted data from the SEER database, which was considered the gold standard for cancer registries in the United States and globally to explore the association between second primary CC and 5-year mortality (Duggan et al., 2016). Similarly, we found that the 5-year mortality in patients with first primary CC was 43.08 % while that in patients with second primary CC was 58.13 %.

In this study, we also compared the influencing factors associated with the survival in first primary CC and second primary CC. Among patients with first primary CC, age, race, marital status, tumor grade, histologic types, tumor size, AJCC stage, TNM stage, surgery, radiotherapy and chemotherapy were all significantly associated with the 5-year survival. Age, histologic types, tumor size, T stage, surgery/chemotherapy for CC, and surgery/chemotherapy for initial cancer were associated with the 5-year survival in patients with second primary CC. A population-based cohort in patients with CC who aged 20–79 years old by Li et al. (Li et al., 2021) demonstrated that 6.3 % of them developed SPMs within a median follow-up time of 7.1 years, and those who tended to be older, Black in race, with localized disease, and underwent radiotherapy treatment, patients aged > 60 years old seemed to have a poor prognosis. Older patients with second primary CC in our research, especially those aged ≥ 65 years old, seemed to have a high risk of 5-year mortality. This phenomenon could be partly explained by the fluctuation of estrogen secretion during aging, especially during the perimenopausal period according to the critical role of estrogen and its receptor in the process of tumor development and the weakening of the immune surveillance after age of 50 (Nelson, 2008, Barros et al., 2018). The 5-year survival rate of CC patients differs slightly according to the clinical stage and histological type, and it reaches about 60–85 % in stage I, 30–60 % in stage II, 20–40 % in stage III, and less than 15 % in stage IV generally (Li et al., 2021). However, we have not found this trend in second primary CC patients. A study showed that cervical adenocarcinoma has a worse prognosis than squamous cell cancers which is similar to our study, especially in patients with second primary CC (Institute, 2002). For patients with early-stage CC, surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy or radiotherapy continue to be the main treatment approaches. In contrast, for patients with advanced-stage (stage IVB), systemic chemotherapy remains the first choice (Eskander and Tewari, 2015). In addition, a second primary CC can be devastating for patients who have already experienced the mental, physical, and financial burden of cancer once (Westin et al., 2016, Alfano and Rowland, 2006, Denlinger and Barsevick, 2009).

We further explored the relationships between different treatments and survival in patients with first primary CC and second primary CC in age, AJCC stage, tumor grade and histologic type subgroups. Surgery, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy were all associated with a low risk of 5-year mortality in first primary CC patients with different age, AJCC stages, tumor grades, and histologic types while the effects of different treatments on survival were different in patients with second primary CC. Our results indicated that surgery and chemotherapy may be more recommended for patients with first primary CC generally, and however, the choice of treatment options for second patients with primary CC should be made as appropriate. Surgery for both patients with first primary CC and second primary CC had a potential positive effect on the 5-year survival, whatever the patients’ age, AJCC stage, tumor grade and histologic type. Radical surgery is the preferred treatment for patients presenting with early-stage disease and type III open radical hysterectomy with bilateral pelvic lymph node dissection, which has been the standard surgical procedure for operable CC (Poddar and Maheshwari, 2021). Barben et al. (Barben et al., 2022) found that compared with younger women, larger proportions of older women had comorbidities and more advanced tumors and had a lower treatment rate, and a smaller number of older women had undergone surgery. The 5-year overall survival rate was 74.1 % for younger women, and 36.4 % for older women, and the HR for mortality was twice as high in older women compared with younger women with CC, independently of FIGO stage, histologic type, and comorbidities (Barben et al., 2022). Barben’s results demonstrated that surgery may be a superior choice for first primary CC, which could partly avoid the long-term side effects from the radiotherapy and chemotherapy. Additionally, radiotherapy and chemotherapy seemed partly to be more recommended for patients with first primary CC. X-ray beams have damaging effect towards genetic material in normal cells, and the unexpected sequela of radiotherapy, including secondary tumorigenesis (Papatla et al., 2019, Arnold et al., 2014, Ota et al., 2007). In fact, the risk of SPMs was significantly associated with the doses and therapeutic regimen of radiotherapy (Kleinerman et al., 2013). Chen et al. (Chen et al., 2012) found that the risk of SPMs caused by radiotherapy varies in different sites increased, such as the bladder, vagina, corpus uteri, and kidney. Chen’s study indicated that cancer patients received radiation therapy may also have the risk of developing second primary CC. Moreover, women who have acquired radiation therapy may undergo premature menopause (Einstein et al., 2015, Hall, 2006). However, due to the retrospective fact of this study, we could not conclude a causal relationship between different treatments and survival in patients with first/second primary CC. Therefore, further researches are needed to clarify these associations, basing on which physicians can weigh the benefits and potential threats more cautiously when prescribing radiotherapy to first and second primary CC patients.

The study population was from the SEER database, in which the sample size was large and representative. By exploring characteristics, influencing factors, and the relationships between different treatments and survival between in patients with first primary CC and second primary CC respectively, this study may provide some references for the management in primary CC diagnosed at different times and the formulation of treatment strategies in order to improve CC prognosis. However, there are still some limitations in the current study. The recurrence and metastasis of primary CC may be considered as subsequent second primary CC, and however, we excluded patients with identical tumor sites and histological subtypes, and the diagnosis interval between first primary CC and second primary CC ≤ 1 year to minimize the bias. Herein, the prevalence of secondary primary CC was relatively conservative in our study. In addition, the details of treatment information such as radiation dose, and other possible confounding factors are unavailable in the SEER database. Further studies are needed on exploration for the modification of the clinical approach to dealing with patients with CC timely in order to provide more specific and exact choice of treatment method.

Conclusion

Characteristics, influencing factors and treatment effects on survival in patients with first primary CC and second primary CC were different. Our study concluded that specific treatment options should be taken into consideration respectively in patients with first and second primary CC, which is however needed further exploration.

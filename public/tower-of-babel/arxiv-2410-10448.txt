Two-dimensional Schrödinger operators with non-local singular potentials
Lukáš Heriban, Markus Holzmann, Christian Stelzer-Landauer, Georg Stenzel, Matěj Tušek

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce and study a family of self-adjoint realizations of the Laplacian in L 2 ​ ( ℝ 2 ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}) with a new type of transmission conditions along a closed bi-Lipschitz curve Σ \Sigma . These conditions incorporate jumps in the Dirichlet traces both of the functions in the operator domains and of their Wirtinger derivatives and are non-local. Constructing a convenient generalized boundary triple, they may be parametrized by all compact hermitian operators in L 2 ​ ( Σ , ℂ 2 ) L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}) . Whereas for all choices of parameters the essential spectrum is stable and equal to [ 0 , + ∞ ) [0,+\infty) , the discrete spectrum exhibits diverse behaviour. While in many cases it is finite, we will describe also a class of parameters for which the discrete spectrum is infinite and accumulates at − ∞ -\infty . The latter class contains a non-local version of the oblique transmission conditions. Finally, we will connect the current model to its relativistic counterpart studied recently in [ 33 ] .

Introduction

Differential operators with singular potentials supported on sets of Lebesgue measure zero have attracted a lot of attention in the mathematical community in the recent years; cf. the monograph
[
1
]
or the review papers
[
10
,
26
]
and the references therein. In many situations, there is a physical motivation to study these operators, as the singular interactions can be viewed as idealized replacements for regular interactions–let us just mention
[
23
,
47
,
52
]
as representatives of pioneering influential works. However, there is often also a pure mathematical motivation as these models have interesting spectral properties, see
[
2
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
14
,
19
,
27
,
28
,
38
,
48
,
49
]
for a small and certainly incomplete selection of results, and the idealized models are used as a playground to see what one can expect from more regular models.

The main aim of this paper is to introduce and study a new family of operators, which are self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and act as the two-dimensional Laplacian away from the Lipschitz continuous boundary
\Sigma
of an open bounded set
\Omega_{+}
. In particular, this family contains operators that may be naturally associated with the formal expression

(1)

-\Delta+\alpha|\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\delta_{\Sigma}|+\beta|\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}\delta_{\Sigma}|+\bar{\beta}|\partial_{z}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\delta_{\Sigma}|+\gamma|\partial_{z}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}\delta_{\Sigma}|,

where
\alpha,\,\gamma\in\mathbb{R},\,\beta\in\mathbb{C}
,
\delta_{\Sigma}
stands for the single layer (or
\delta
-) distribution supported on
\Sigma
, and
\partial_{z}
denotes the Wirtinger derivative. Here, we are using the common physical
bra-ket
notation extended to distributions which, in turn, are extended to functions that admit well-defined Dirichlet traces with respect to
\Omega_{+}
and
\Omega_{-}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}
. Following similar considerations as in Section 3 of
[
33
]
, one derives that, for
f\equiv f_{+}\oplus f_{-}
from a suitable subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\equiv L^{2}(\Omega_{+})\oplus L^{2}(\Omega_{-})
, the image of
f
under (
1
) belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if the transmission conditions

(2)

4\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\mathbf{n}}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-})\\
\mathbf{n}(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-})\end{pmatrix}+B\,\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-}\\
-\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-}\end{pmatrix}=0

are fulfilled,
where
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}
denote the Dirichlet trace operators with respect to
\Omega_{\pm}
,
\mathbf{n}:=n_{1}+in_{2}
is the complexified outer unit normal vector
n
to
\Omega_{+}
, and
B
is the finite rank operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
given by

(3)

B\varphi:=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\bar{\beta}&\gamma\end{pmatrix}\int_{\Sigma}\varphi.

Notice that in (
2
) the jumps of
f
and its Wirtinger derivative are given by integral values of the traces of
f
along
\Sigma
, i.e. the transmission condition is
non-local
.

In our general setting, we will allow
B
in (
2
) to be any compact self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
. The reason for this choice is essentially two-fold. Firstly, the transmission conditions (
2
) then correspond to a generalization of the formal expression (
1
) that would look like

(4)

\begin{split}-\Delta+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\Big(&|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})|\\
&+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})|\Big),\end{split}

where
(b_{n},(\varphi_{n}^{1},\varphi_{n}^{2})^{T}),\,n\in\mathbb{N},
are the eigenpairs of
B
with orthonormal eigenfunctions; cf. Proposition
3.4
. Since the latter interaction is a limit of linear combinations of non-local interaction terms that generalize those in (
1
), we call also the more general interactions in (
4
)
non-local
.
The precise definition of the associated operator
T_{B}
then reads

(5)

\begin{split}T_{B}f&=(-\Delta f_{+})\oplus(-\Delta f_{-}),\\
\text{Dom}(T_{B})&=\big\{f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\>\big|\>\Delta f_{\pm}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm}),\partial_{\overline{z}}f_{\pm}\in H^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})\text{ and \eqref{eq:TC_sub} holds}\big\},\end{split}

where
H^{1/2}
denotes the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces of order
\frac{1}{2}
.
With proper techniques, it is not difficult to show that
T_{B}
is self-adjoint (see Theorem
3.5
). Our second and most important motivation is that we found out that already in this setting, that comprises relatively small perturbations of the free Laplacian (in the sense that
T_{0}=-\Delta
and that the potential in (
4
) is a convergent sum of formal "finite rank operators"), the point spectrum of
T_{B}
may be very diverse; the essential spectrum is, as expected, always stable and equal to
[0,+\infty)
. If
B
has finite rank
k
then
T_{B}
has at most
k
discrete eigenvalues. Also for any
B=B_{11}\oplus 0\equiv\diag(B_{11},0)
in
L^{2}(\Sigma)\oplus L^{2}(\Sigma)\equiv L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
, the discrete spectrum of
T_{B}
is finite. Note that in that case, (
4
) reduces to a Schrödinger operator with a
\delta
-potential with the "compact weight"
B_{11}
,

(6)

-\Delta+B_{11}\delta_{\Sigma},

where the formal potential
B_{11}\delta_{\Sigma}
maps a function
f
with a well-defined Dirichlet trace to the distribution
(B_{11}\gamma_{D}f)\delta_{\Sigma}
.
On the other hand, if the following condition

(S)

B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{21}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix}\geq 0\text{ and }B_{22}\text{ has infinitely many positive eigenvalues}

is satisfied, then
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(T_{B})
is infinite with the only accumulation point at
-\infty
, see Theorem
3.11
for details.

Let us compare our model to others that were treated in the literature.
Allowing
B
to be just hermitian (and not necessarily compact) one can reproduce the so-called
\delta
-shell and oblique transmission conditions. The former are usually given by

(7)

\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}=\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-},\quad\gamma_{N}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{N}^{-}f_{-}=-\frac{\eta}{2}(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-}),

where
\eta
is a bounded real-valued function and
\gamma_{N}^{\pm}
stand for the Neumann trace operators with respect to
\Omega_{\pm}
and the normal vector
n
; cf.
[
22
,
14
]
. With these conditions one automatically has higher regularity of the functions in the domain of the associated operator
T_{\delta,\eta}
. Since, for functions obeying
\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}=\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-}
, the left-hand side of the second condition in (
7
) is equal to
2\bar{\mathbf{n}}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-})
, the operator
B
corresponding to (
7
) would be
\diag(\eta I,0)
. Let us emphasize that the natural formal expression for
T_{\delta,\eta}
is
-\Delta+\eta\delta_{\Sigma}
, where
\eta\delta_{\Sigma}
should be understood as a multiplication operator, i.e. it corresponds to
B_{11}=\eta
in (
6
). This is very different from (
1
) with
\beta=\gamma=0
, see also (
3
). It is known that
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{\delta,\eta})=[0,+\infty)
and the negative spectrum is discrete and finite. In Theorem
3.11
(iii) we will see that this picture is the same for compact coefficients
\eta=B_{11}
, i.e. for
T_{B}
in (
5
) with
B=\diag(B_{11},0)
.

The oblique transmission conditions were introduced in
[
11
]
for
C^{\infty}
-smooth boundaries and studied only recently also for Lipschitz continuous ones
[
18
]
. They can be written for
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
as

(8)

\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}=\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-},\quad\mathbf{n}(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-})=-\eta(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-}),

which means that
B
in (
2
) would be
\diag(0,-4\eta I)
. For the corresponding operator
T_{\angle,\eta}
, it was deduced that
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{\angle,\eta})=[0,+\infty)
. Moreover,
T_{\angle,\eta}
is a non-negative operator when
\eta\geq 0
. On the other hand, if
\eta<0
, then
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(T_{\angle,\eta})
is infinite with the only accumulation point at
-\infty
. Therefore, in the latter case the spectrum of
T_{\angle,\eta}
exhibits exactly the same unexpected behaviour as the spectrum of
T_{B}
with
B
compact and satisfying the assumption (
S
). Recalling that this
T_{B}
is a smaller perturbation of the free Laplacian than
T_{\angle,\eta}
, as the coefficient in the transmission conditions is compact, this effect is even more surprising in our setting.
We would like to point out that the oblique transmission conditions are different from those corresponding to
\delta^{\prime}
-interactions as they are studied, e.g., in
[
14
,
38
]
; see also
[
29
]
for combinations and generalizations of
\delta
- and
\delta^{\prime}
-interactions, which are also different from the interactions treated in this paper.

To analyze the operator
T_{B}
we introduced a new generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Gamma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
for
\overline{T}
, where

\begin{split}Tf&=(-\Delta f_{+})\oplus(-\Delta f_{-}),\\
\text{Dom}(T)&=\left\{f\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\>\big|\>\Delta f_{\pm}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm}),\,\partial_{\overline{z}}f_{\pm}\in H^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\}.\end{split}

The boundary mappings
(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
are chosen exactly in the way that
T_{B}=T\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0}+B\Gamma_{1})
.
We believe that our triple is of independent interest since it may be used to study transmission conditions with jumps in the Dirichlet traces both of the functions and of their Wirtinger derivatives, whereas other triples usually come with the normal instead of the Wirtinger derivative
[
14
,
16
]
. For a summary of basic results for generalized boundary triples in an abstract setting see Section
2.5
. In particular, we will present a useful version of the Birman-Schwinger principle and a Krein type resolvent formula in the case when the parameter
B
factorizes as
B=B_{1}B_{2}
with
B_{2}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{K}
and
B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{G}
, where
\mathcal{G}
is the boundary space and
\mathcal{K}
is another (
intermediate
) Hilbert space, which were, to the best of our knowledge, not contained in the literature; cf. Theorem
2.14
. In particular, if
\dim(\mathcal{K})<+\infty
then the Birman-Schwinger operator is also finite-dimensional.

Formal expressions similar to (
1
) were examined recently in the relativistic setting
[
33
]
. Putting the mass term equal to
1/2
and introducing the speed of light
c>0
they would look like

-ic\sum_{i=1}^{2}\sigma_{i}\partial_{i}+\sigma_{3}\frac{c^{2}}{2}+c|F\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle G\delta_{\Sigma}|,

where
\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3}
denote the usual Pauli matrices and
F,G\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
are such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
,
F\int_{\Sigma}(G^{*}f)=G\int_{\Sigma}(F^{*}f)
. The associated self-adjoint operator
D_{F,G}^{c}
is defined properly in (
59
). We will look for its non-relativistic limit, i.e. we will study
\lim_{c\to+\infty}(D^{c}_{F,G}-c^{2}/2)
in a well defined sense. In general, the non-relativistic limit provides a connection between the relativistic theory and the (usually well-studied) non-relativistic theory and thereby allows an interpretation of the relativistic model. The procedure of the non-relativistic limit for the case of a regular perturbation is discussed in details in
[
51
, Chap. 6]
and for usual (
local
)
\delta
-point and
\delta
-shell interactions in
[
4
,
11
,
20
,
32
]
. There it was observed that to get a convergence as well as a limit which non-trivially depends on all interaction parameters, one has to scale these parameters appropriately. In our case, we will put
F_{c}:=S_{c}F
and
G_{c}:=S_{c}G
with
S_{c}:=\diag(c^{-1/2},c^{1/2})
. Then we will show in Theorem
4.6
that, for every
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,

\left\lVert\left(D_{F_{c},G_{c}}^{c}-w-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)^{-1}-(T_{B}-w)^{-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert=\mathcal{O}(c^{-1}),

where
B
is the finite rank operator that acts as
Bf=VF\int_{\Sigma}(G^{*}V^{*}f)
with
V:=\diag(1,-2i)
. In particular, this shows that the Schrödinger operators with non-local interactions in (
1
) are indeed the non-relativistic counterpart of the relativistic models with non-local interactions studied in
[
33
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
is of preliminary nature. After collecting some notations in Section

2.2

2.3
. In Section
2.4

2.5

3
is devoted to the analysis of
T_{B}
defined in (
5
). In Section
3.1
a generalized boundary triple that is suitable for the study of
T_{B}
is introduced. This is used in Section
3.2
to show the self-adjointness of
T_{B}
, while the spectral properties of
T_{B}
are studied in Section
3.3
. Finally, in Section
4
we show that
T_{B}
is the non-relativistic limit of Dirac operators with non-local singular interactions.

2.
Preliminaries

This section is devoted to some preliminary material that is used throughout the paper. First, in Section
2.1
we collect some frequently used notations. Section
2.2
is devoted to function spaces and trace theorems, and in Section
2.3

2.1.
Notation

Let
\Omega_{+}
be an open bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with at least Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega_{+}=\Sigma
as described in Section
2.2
. The unit outer normal vector field and the unit tangent field along
\Sigma
will be denoted by
n=(n_{1},n_{2})
and
t=(t_{1},t_{2})
, respectively. We put
t=(-n_{2},n_{1})
, so that the basis
(n,t)
has the standard orientation of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and
\Omega_{-}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}
. We denote the restriction of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
to
\Omega_{\pm}
by
f_{\pm}
. The complexified vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
will be denoted by
\mathbf{x}=x_{1}+ix_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. Furthermore, the Wirtinger derivatives are given by
\partial_{z}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}-i\partial_{2})
and
\partial_{\overline{z}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})
. In the following we will always choose the square root of a complex number
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
in such a way that
\textup{Im }\sqrt{w}>0
applies. Eventually, when convenient
C
denotes a generic constant whose value may change between different lines.

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
we denote the associated inner product by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
and we use the convention that it is anti-linear in the first entry and linear in the second argument. If
\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{G}
are Hilbert spaces and
A
is a linear operator from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{G}
, then its domain, range, and kernel are denoted by
\Dom(A)
,
\Ran(A)
, and
\Ker(A)
, respectively. For a closed operator
A
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
the resolvent set, spectrum, and point spectrum are
\rho(A)
,
\sigma(A)
, and
\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A)
. Moreover, if
A
is self-adjoint, then we denote the discrete and essential spectrum by
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(A)
and
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(A)
.

2.2.
Curves, function spaces, and trace theorems

Throughout this paper, we assume that
\Sigma
is a compact non-self-intersecting
bi-Lipschitz
curve. By this, we mean that there exists an injective Lipschitz continuous arc-length parametrization
\zeta:[0,L)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
L
denotes the length of
\Sigma
, extended to
\mathbb{R}
via periodicity, such that
\zeta([0,L))=\Sigma
,
|\dot{\zeta}|=1
a.e. on
[0,L)
, and there exists a constant
C_{\zeta}>0
such that

(9)

C_{\zeta}|t-s|\leq|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|\leq|t-s|\qquad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\,\forall s\in\overline{B(t,L/2)}.

Remark 2.1
.

If the extended function
\zeta
is
C^{2}
-smooth, then (
9
) is always true. The second inequality is clear even for Lipschitz continuous functions. To show the first inequality, define the function

r(s,t):=\begin{cases}\frac{|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|}{|t-s|},&t\neq s,\\
1,&t=s.\end{cases}

Employing the Taylor expansion, one checks that
r
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Moreover,
r
is positive on
\mathscr{M}:=\{(s,t)|\,t\in\mathbb{R},\,s\in\overline{B(t,L/2)}\}
, due to the injectivity of
\zeta
on
[0,L)
. Put
\mathscr{M}_{L}:=\{(s,t)|\,t\in[0,L],\,s\in\overline{B(t,L/2)}\}
. Taking into account also the periodicity of
\zeta
and the compactness of
\mathscr{M}_{L}
, we get that (
9
) is true with

C_{\zeta}=\inf_{\mathscr{M}}r=\inf_{\mathscr{M}_{L}}r=\min_{\mathscr{M}_{L}}r>0
.

To shorten notation we use the expression
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{M}
instead of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}
for the usual scalar product on
L^{2}(M)
, which is anti-linear in the first and linear in the second argument, where
M
is either an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\Sigma
; in the latter case the
L^{2}
space is equipped with the standard arc-length measure
\sigma
. The symbol
H^{s}(M)
denotes the classical Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
M
is an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or a sufficiently smooth curve
\Sigma
; cf.
[
39
, Chap. 3]
. For
M=\Sigma
one has then that

H^{s}(\Sigma)
is the dual space of
H^{-s}(\Sigma)
, which will be used later.

In the following, we introduce some further function spaces that will be used in several places in the course of the paper and on which extensions of the Dirichlet trace operator can be constructed. Define for
s\geq 0
the Sobolev space

H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm}):=\left\{f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\>\big|\>\Delta f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\}

and equip it with the norm
\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}+\|\Delta f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}
. Then,
H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
is a Hilbert space and
C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})
, which is the set of all functions mapping from
\Omega_{\pm}
to
\mathbb{C}
that have compactly supported
C^{\infty}
extensions to
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, is dense in
H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
. This follows for
\Omega_{+}
from
[
7
, Lem. 2.13]
and can be proven in the same way for
\Omega_{-}
. We also define the function spaces
H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
and
H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
for
s\geq 0
by

(10)

H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm}):=\left\{f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\>\big|\>\partial_{\bar{z}}f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\}

and

(11)

H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm}):=\left\{f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\>\big|\>\partial_{z}f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\},

endowed with the norms

\|f\|_{H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}+\|\partial_{z}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}

and

\|f\|_{H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}+\|\partial_{\bar{z}}f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}.

Furthermore, we identify

(12)

H^{s}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)=H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{+})\oplus H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{-}).

Again, it can be shown as in
[
17
, Lem. 2.1]
that
H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
and
H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
are Hilbert spaces and that the set
C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})
is dense in
H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
and
H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
. This observation allows to extend the continuous trace operator
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{1/2}(\Sigma)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
. Then the mapping
C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})\ni f\mapsto f|_{\Sigma}
can be extended to linear and bounded operators

\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma).

Furthermore, for all
f\in H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
and all
g\in H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
there holds

(13)

\langle\partial_{\bar{z}}f,g\rangle_{\Omega_{\pm}}=-\langle f,\partial_{z}g\rangle_{\Omega_{\pm}}\pm\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbf{n}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}f,\gamma_{D}^{\pm}g\rangle_{\Sigma}.

If
\Sigma
is a
C^{k}
-boundary with
k\geq 2
then the above mapping properties remain valid for all
s\in[\tfrac{3}{2},k]
.

Proof.

We show that the trace operator can be extended to a bounded map
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)
, the claim for
H_{\partial_{z}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
can be shown in the same way. For this, we use similar arguments as in the proof of
[
10
, Lem. 4.1]
. Let
s\in[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}]
be fixed. Define the space

H^{s,-1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm}):=\left\{f\in H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})\>\big|\>\Delta f\in H^{-1}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\}

and equip it with the norm
\|f\|_{H^{s,-1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{H^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}+\|\Delta f\|_{H^{-1}(\Omega_{\pm})}^{2}
. Then it follows from
[
7
, Thm. 3.6]
that there exists a continuous extension
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H^{s,-1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)
of the trace operator. Due to
4\partial_{z}\partial_{\bar{z}}=\Delta
and the continuity of the operator
\partial_{z}:L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{-1}(\Omega_{\pm})
, the space
H^{s}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}(\Omega_{\pm})
is continuously embedded in
H^{s,-1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
. Thus, the Dirichlet trace admits the claimed continuous extension defined on
H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{s}(\Omega_{\pm})
. The Green’s identity (
13
) follows directly from the density of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})
in
H^{s}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}(\Omega_{\pm})
and
H^{s}_{\partial_{z}}(\Omega_{\pm})
, the continuity of the extended Dirichlet trace operator, and the classical integration by parts formula.
Finally, the claim for
C^{k}
-boundaries follows from the classical trace theorem, see, e.g.,
[
39
, Thm. 3.37]
.
∎

We will also need a suitable realization of the Neumann trace. By
[
39
, Lem. 4.3]
there exists a bounded operator
\gamma_{N}^{\pm}:H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{-1/2}(\Sigma)
such that for all
f\in H^{1}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
and all
g\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\pm})
,

\langle\nabla f,\nabla g\rangle_{\Omega_{\pm}}+\langle\Delta f,g\rangle_{\Omega_{\pm}}=\pm\big\langle\gamma_{N}^{\pm}f,\gamma_{D}^{\pm}g\big\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma)\times H^{1/2}(\Sigma)}

is satisfied, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\Sigma)\times H^{1/2}(\Sigma)}
denotes the sesquilinear duality product in
H^{-1/2}(\Sigma)\times H^{1/2}(\Sigma)
. Note that by
[
7
, Thm. 5.4]
one has for all
f\in H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
the relation
\gamma_{N}^{\pm}f=\nu\cdot\gamma_{D}^{\pm}\nabla f
and
\gamma_{N}^{\pm}:H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma)
is continuous. With an interpolation argument, see, e.g.,
[
37
, Thms. 5.1&14.3]
, it follows that
\gamma_{N}^{\pm}:H^{s}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-3/2}(\Sigma)
is continuous for all
s\in[1,\frac{3}{2}]
.

2.3.
Distributions

The space of all test functions in an open set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is denoted by
\mathcal{D}(M)
, the symbol
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
is used for the distributions in
M
.
The pairing between the distributions and the test functions will be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Note that this bracket is linear with respect to both arguments.

The symbol
\delta_{\Sigma}
will stand for the single layer distribution supported on the hypersurface
\Sigma
. Furthermore, for
g\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
we identify the distributions
g\delta_{\Sigma}
,
\partial_{z}(g\delta_{\Sigma})
, and
\partial_{\overline{z}}(g\delta_{\Sigma})
with the inner product on
L^{2}(\Sigma)
in the following manner:

\left.\begin{split}(g\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi)&:=\langle\overline{g},\varphi\upharpoonright\Sigma\rangle_{\Sigma},\\
(\partial_{z}(g\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)&:=-\langle\overline{g},\partial_{z}\varphi\upharpoonright\Sigma\rangle_{\Sigma},\\
(\partial_{\overline{z}}(g\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)&:=-\langle\overline{g},\partial_{\overline{z}}\varphi\upharpoonright\Sigma\rangle_{\Sigma}.\end{split}\,\right\}\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

We will do the same for
\mathbb{C}^{2}
-valued test functions, i.e., for
G\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
the distribution
G\delta_{\Sigma}
will be defined as

(G\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi):=\int_{\Sigma}G(\varphi\upharpoonright\Sigma)\mathrm{d}\sigma,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Note that the distributions above can be extended, similarly as the
\delta
-distribution in
[
36
]
, to every (possibly
\mathbb{C}^{2}
-valued) function
\varphi
such that
\varphi_{\pm}:=\varphi\upharpoonright\Omega_{\pm}
admit well-defined square integrable Dirichlet traces:

\begin{split}(g\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi)&:=\int_{\Sigma}g\frac{\gamma_{D}^{+}\varphi_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}\varphi_{-}}{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma,\\
(\partial_{z}(g\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)&:=-\int_{\Sigma}g\frac{\gamma_{D}^{+}(\partial_{z}\varphi_{+})+\gamma_{D}^{-}(\partial_{z}\varphi_{-})}{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma,\\
(\partial_{\bar{z}}(g\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)&:=-\int_{\Sigma}g\frac{\gamma_{D}^{+}(\partial_{\bar{z}}\varphi_{+})+\gamma_{D}^{-}(\partial_{\bar{z}}\varphi_{-})}{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma,\end{split}

and

(G\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi):=\int_{\Sigma}G\frac{\gamma_{D}^{+}\varphi_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}\varphi_{-}}{2}\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Moreover, we will extend the usual physical
bra-ket
notation to certain distributions. Let
f,g\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
F,G\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
then for every
\varphi
as above we set

\displaystyle|f\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle g\delta_{\Sigma}|\varphi

\displaystyle:=(\overline{g}\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi)f\delta_{\Sigma},

\displaystyle|f\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(g\delta_{\Sigma})|\varphi

\displaystyle:=(\partial_{\bar{z}}(\overline{g}\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)f\delta_{\Sigma},

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}(f\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle g\delta_{\Sigma}|\varphi

\displaystyle:=(\overline{g}\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi)\partial_{z}(f\delta_{\Sigma}),

\displaystyle|\partial_{z}(f\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(g\delta_{\Sigma})|\varphi

\displaystyle:=(\partial_{\bar{z}}(\overline{g}\delta_{\Sigma}),\varphi)\partial_{z}(f\delta_{\Sigma}),

\displaystyle|F\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle G\delta_{\Sigma}|\varphi

\displaystyle:=F(G^{\ast}\delta_{\Sigma},\varphi)\delta_{\Sigma}.

2.4.
Integral operators

In this section we collect several useful results about integral operators and their mapping properties. We start with two abstract results which are basically consequences of the Schur test (see
[
35
, Ex. III 2.4]
or
[
53
, Thm. 6.24]
) and that will be used frequently, in particular in the Sections
3.3
&
4
. First, we discuss the boundedness of certain boundary integral operators. Recall that
\Sigma
is always a compact non-self-intersecting bi-Lipschitz smooth curve of length
L
as described at the beginning of Section
2.2
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
a:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function,
L
be the length of
\Sigma
,
C_{\zeta}>0
be the constant from (
9
), and
\alpha:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
be a monotonically decreasing function such that
\alpha
is integrable on
(0,\frac{C_{\zeta}L}{2})
. Moreover, assume that for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
with
|x|\leq\tfrac{L}{2}

(14)

|a(x)|\leq\alpha(|x|)

is fulfilled.
Then the linear operator
A:L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
defined by

\displaystyle(A\varphi)(x_{\Sigma})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}a(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}),\quad

\displaystyle\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma),\,x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma,

is well-defined and for the operator norm the estimate

\displaystyle\|A\|

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{\zeta}^{-1}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/2}\alpha(s)\mathrm{d}s

is valid.

Proof.

We start by introducing the operator

(15)

\iota_{\zeta}:L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}((0,L)),\qquad\varphi\mapsto\varphi\circ\zeta.

Since
|\dot{\zeta}|=1
a.e.,
\iota_{\zeta}
is an isometric isomorphism. Thus,
A
is well-defined if and only if
\widetilde{A}:=\iota_{\zeta}A\iota_{\zeta}^{-1}:L^{2}((0,L))\to L^{2}((0,L))
is well-defined. A transformation of coordinates shows that
\widetilde{A}
acts as

(\widetilde{A}f)(t)=\int_{0}^{L}a(\zeta(t)-\zeta(s))f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\qquad f\in L^{2}((0,L)),\penalty\ t\in(0,L).

Define the number
K\in[0,+\infty]
by

(16)

K:=\bigg(\sup_{t\in(0,L)}\int_{0}^{L}\alpha(|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sup_{s\in(0,L)}\int_{0}^{L}\alpha(|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\,\mathrm{d}t\bigg)^{1/2}.

Then, by (
14
) and the Schur test, see, e.g.,
[
35
, Ex. III 2.4]
or
[
53
, Thm. 6.24]
,
\widetilde{A}
is well-defined if
K<+\infty
, and in this case
\|A\|=\|\widetilde{A}\|\leq K
.
Thus, it remains to estimate the constant
K
in (
16
). For
t\in(0,L)

the periodicity of
\zeta
, (
9
), and the monotonicity of
\alpha
yield

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\alpha(|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{t-L/2}^{t+L/2}\alpha(|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t-L/2}^{t+L/2}\alpha(C_{\zeta}|t-s|)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=2C_{\zeta}^{-1}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/2}\alpha(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Since the latter estimate is independent of
t
, we conclude that

\bigg(\sup_{t\in(0,L)}\int_{0}^{L}|\alpha(\zeta(t)-\zeta(s))|\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2}\leq\bigg(2C_{\zeta}^{-1}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/2}\alpha(s)\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2}.

By symmetry, the second term in (
16
) can be estimated in the same way. Hence, we conclude that the constant
K
in (
16
) can be estimated by

K\leq 2C_{\zeta}^{-1}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/2}\alpha(s)\mathrm{d}s,

which finishes the proof of this proposition.
∎

The next proposition is a counterpart of Proposition
2.3
for potential operators.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
a:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable,
L
be the length of the curve
\Sigma
,
C_{\zeta}>0
be the constant from (
9
),
\theta\in[0,1)
, and
\alpha:(0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
be a monotonically decreasing function such that
\alpha^{2\theta}
and
(0,+\infty)\ni r\mapsto(\alpha(r))^{2(1-\theta)}r
are integrable on
(0,C_{\zeta}\frac{L}{4})
and
(0,+\infty)
, respectively. Moreover, assume that for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

(17)

|a(x)|\leq\alpha(|x|)

is fulfilled.
Then the linear operator
A:L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined by

\displaystyle(A\varphi)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}a(x-y_{\Sigma})\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}),\quad

\displaystyle\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma),x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

is well-defined and for the operator norm the estimate

\displaystyle\|A\|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{4}{C_{\zeta}}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/4}(\alpha(s))^{2\theta}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}(\alpha(r))^{2(1-\theta)}r\mathrm{d}r\bigg)^{1/2}

is valid.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
2.3
we make use of the isometric isomorphism
\iota_{\zeta}
defined by (
15
) and consider the operator
\widetilde{A}:=A\iota_{\zeta}^{-1}:L^{2}((0,L))\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which acts as

(\widetilde{A}f)(x)=\int_{0}^{L}a(x-\zeta(s))f(s)\mathrm{d}s,\qquad f\in L^{2}((0,L)),\penalty\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Defining
K\in[0,+\infty]
by

(18)

K:=\bigg(\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}(\alpha(|x-\zeta(s)|))^{2\theta}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2}\bigg(\sup_{s\in(0,L)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\alpha(|x-\zeta(s)|))^{2(1-\theta)}\mathrm{d}x\bigg)^{1/2},

we get with (
17
) and the Schur test again that
\widetilde{A}
is well-defined, if
K<+\infty
, and in this case
\|A\|=\|\widetilde{A}\|\leq K
.
We start by estimating the first term in (
18
). Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Choose
t\in(0,L]
such that
\min_{s\in\mathbb{R}}|x-\zeta(s)|=|x-\zeta(t)|
. Then,

|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|\leq|\zeta(t)-x|+|x-\zeta(s)|\leq 2|x-\zeta(s)|\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

Combining this estimate with (
9
) gives us

\frac{C_{\zeta}}{2}|t-s|\leq|x-\zeta(s)|\quad\forall s\in\overline{B(t,L/2)}.

Thus, we can estimate the first term in (
18
) in the same way as in the proof of Proposition
2.3
by

\bigg(\frac{4}{C_{\zeta}}\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/4}(\alpha(s))^{2\theta}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)^{1/2},

which is finite by assumption.
Furthermore, using polar coordinates one gets for
s\in(0,L)
that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(\alpha(|x-\zeta(s)|))^{2(1-\theta)}\mathrm{d}x=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}(\alpha(r))^{2(1-\theta)}r\mathrm{d}r,

and therefore the second term in (
18
) can be estimated by the square root of
2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}(\alpha(r))^{2(1-\theta)}r\,\mathrm{d}r
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Throughout this paper, integral operators with kernels, where the modified Bessel functions
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
of the second kind appear, will play an important role. Hence, we summarize some of their properties:

Lemma 2.5
.

The following statements are valid for the modified Bessel functions
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
.

(i)

For all
t>0
one has
K_{0}(t)>0
and
K_{1}(t)>0
.

(ii)

K_{0}^{\prime}(t)=-K_{1}(t)
and
tK_{1}^{\prime}(t)=-tK_{0}(t)-K_{1}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
.

(iii)

There exist analytic functions
g_{1},g_{2}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]

K_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{t}+tg_{1}(t^{2})\ln(t)+tg_{2}(t^{2}).

(iv)

There exist constants
\kappa_{1},\,\tilde{\kappa}_{1}>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]

|K_{0}(t)|\leq\kappa_{1}\frac{1+|\ln|t||}{1+\sqrt{|t|}}\mathrm{e}^{-\textup{Re}(t)}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1}|t|^{-1/4}\mathrm{e}^{-\textup{Re}(t)}.

(v)

There exists a constant
\kappa_{2}>0
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and
j\in\{0,1\}

\left|\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}K_{1}(t)\right|\leq\kappa_{2}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|t|}}+\frac{1-j}{|t|}+\frac{j}{|t|^{2}}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\textup{Re}(t)}.

Proof.

Item (i) can be deduced from the integral representations of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
in
[
42
, § 10.32]
and item (ii) follows from
[
42
, § 10.29(i)]
. For the proof of item (iii) one merely has to use the power series expansion of
K_{1}
in
[
42
, § 10.31]
, cf. the proof of
[
9
, Lem. 3.2]
for details. The first claimed inequality in (iv) follows from the asymptotic expansion of
K_{0}
, see, e.g.,
[
42
, § 10.25(ii) and § 10.30(i)]
, the second inequality is then obtained immediately from the first one. Similarly, the asymptotic expansion of
K_{1}
from
[
42
, § 10.25(ii) and § 10.30(i)]
leads to the inequality in item (v) for
j=0
. For the case
j=1
one obtains the claimed inequality from the recurrence relation of the modified Bessel functions in item (ii) and the asymptotics of
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
for small and large arguments.
∎

the jump relations

\begin{split}2{\bf n}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(WL(w)\varphi)_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}(WL(w)\varphi)_{-}\big)&=\varphi,\\
\frac{1}{2}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(\partial_{\overline{z}}WL(w)\varphi)_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}(\partial_{\overline{z}}WL(w)\varphi)_{-}\big)&=-\frac{w}{4}S(w)\varphi,\end{split}

and

\begin{split}2\overline{\mathbf{n}}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}(\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{-}\big)&=\varphi,\\
\frac{1}{2}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(\partial_{z}\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}(\partial_{z}\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{-}\big)&=-\frac{w}{4}S(w)\varphi,\end{split}

hold.

Since it follows from Proposition
2.7
that
WL(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)
and
\widetilde{WL}(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow H^{1/2}_{\partial_{z}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)
, we can define with Lemma
2.2
for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
the operators

(28)

W(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma),\quad W(w)\varphi:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(WL(w)\varphi)_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}(WL(w)\varphi)_{-}\big),

and

(29)

\widetilde{W}(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma),\quad\widetilde{W}(w)\varphi:=\frac{1}{2}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}(\widetilde{WL}(w)\varphi)_{-}\big).

If
\Sigma
is a
C^{k}
-boundary with
k\geq 2
, then the mapping properties of
W(w)
and
\widetilde{W}(w)
improve. Indeed, one can show with the help of Proposition
2.7
(i) and the boundedness of the trace operator
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}:H^{s}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}(\Omega_{\pm})\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)
from Lemma
2.2
that

(30)

W(w):H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma),\quad s\in[\tfrac{1}{2},k-1],

is well-defined and bounded. Similarly, Proposition
2.7
(ii) implies that

(31)

\widetilde{W}(w):H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma)\to H^{s-1/2}(\Sigma),\quad s\in[\tfrac{1}{2},k-1],

is well-defined and bounded as well.

In the following lemma we will see that
W(w)
and
\widetilde{W}(w)
can be represented as strongly singular boundary integral operators and that
\widetilde{W}(w)=-W(\overline{w})^{*}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
. Then, for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and almost all
x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma
the representations

W(w)\varphi(x_{\Sigma})=\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|)\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma})

and

\widetilde{W}(w)\varphi(x_{\Sigma})=\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}\frac{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}-{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|)\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma})

hold, where
B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)
denotes the ball with center
x_{\Sigma}
and radius
\varepsilon
. In particular,
\widetilde{W}(w)=-W(\overline{w})^{*}
.

Proof.

We prove the claimed integral representation of
W(w)
, the one of
\widetilde{W}(w)
can be proved in the same way. Having these implies then also
\widetilde{W}(w)=-W(\overline{w})^{*}
due to the explicit form of the integral kernels.

Let
\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma)
and
x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma
be fixed. Recall that by Lemma
2.5
(iii) there exist analytic functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
such that for all
t\in\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]

(32)

K_{1}(t)=\frac{1}{t}+tg_{1}(t^{2})\ln(t)+tg_{2}(t^{2}).

By dominated convergence we have

(33)

\begin{split}\lim_{\Omega_{\pm}\ni x\to x_{\Sigma}}&\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\big(-i\sqrt{w}|x-y_{\Sigma}|\big)\Big(g_{1}(-w|x-y_{\Sigma}|^{2})\ln(-i\sqrt{w}|x-y_{\Sigma}|)\\
&\quad\qquad+g_{2}(-w|x-y_{\Sigma}|^{2})\Big)\frac{\overline{{\bf x}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x-y_{\Sigma}|}\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma})\\
&=\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\big(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|\big)\Big(g_{1}(-w|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|^{2})\ln(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|)\\
&\quad\qquad+g_{2}(-w|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|^{2})\Big)\frac{\overline{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|}\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}).\end{split}

Moreover, by
[
25
, p. 1071]
we have for the non-tangential limit at
x_{\Sigma}

(34)

\begin{split}-\lim_{\Omega_{\pm}\ni x\to x_{\Sigma}}&\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x-y_{\Sigma}|^{2}}\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma})\\
&=\pm\frac{1}{4}\overline{{\bf n}(x_{\Sigma})}\varphi(x_{\Sigma})-\frac{1}{4\pi}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|^{2}}\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}).\end{split}

By combining (
33
) with (
34
) and using once more (
32
), we find for the non-tangential limit at
x_{\Sigma}

\begin{split}\lim_{\Omega_{\pm}\ni x\to x_{\Sigma}}&WL(w)\varphi(x)=\pm\frac{1}{4}\overline{{\bf n}(x_{\Sigma})}\varphi(x_{\Sigma})\\
&\quad+\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|)\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}).\end{split}

Since
WL(w)\varphi\in H^{1/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)
, we conclude with
[
7
, Thm. 3.6 (ii)]
that the latter non-tangential limit coincides with
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}(WL(w)\varphi)_{\pm}
. Therefore, we finally get that

\begin{split}W(w)\varphi(x_{\Sigma})&=\frac{1}{2}\big(\gamma_{D}^{+}(WL(w)\varphi)_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}(WL(w)\varphi)_{-}\big)(x_{\Sigma})\\
&=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}_{\Sigma}}-\overline{{\bf y}_{\Sigma}}}{|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}|)\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}),\end{split}

which is the claimed identity.
∎

2.5.
Generalized boundary triples

In this section we recall the concept of generalized boundary triples, which are a useful tool from the extension and spectral theory of symmetric and self-adjoint operators. Generalized boundary triples were introduced in
[
24
]
and used, e.g., in
[
4
,
6
,
44
,
10
,
15
,
33
,
41
]
to study differential operators with boundary or transmission conditions. The results presented here are formulated in such a way that they can be applied directly in the situations we are considering; for further results and proofs we refer to
[
13
,
24
]
. Many of the presented results are known for closely related ordinary or quasi boundary triples
[
8
,
13
]
, but not formulated for generalized boundary triples, and thus, we provide them, where necessary, with proofs. In the following
\mathcal{H}
is always a Hilbert space.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
S
be a closed and symmetric operator in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
A
be an operator with
\overline{A}=S^{\ast}
. A triple
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
is called generalized boundary triple for
S^{\ast}
, if
\mathcal{G}
is a Hilbert space and
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:\Dom(A)\to\mathcal{G}
are linear mappings which satisfy the following conditions:

(i)

The restriction
A_{0}:=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
is self-adjoint in
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

\Ran(\Gamma_{0})=\mathcal{G}
.

(iii)

For all
f,g\in\Dom(A)
there holds the abstract Green’s identity

(35)

\langle Af,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle f,Ag\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}-\langle\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}.

The next result, which is useful in applications, allows us to check whether a given operator
A
and two boundary maps
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:\Dom(A)\to\mathcal{G}
form a generalized boundary triple for a suitable operator
S
. It can be seen as a variant of
[
13
, Thm. 2.3]
or
[
8
, Thm. 2.1.9]
for generalized boundary triples.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
A
be an operator in
\mathcal{H}
. Furthermore, let
\mathcal{G}
be a Hilbert space and
\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}:\Dom(A)\to\mathcal{G}
be linear mappings such that the following holds:

(i)

A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
is an extension of a self-adjoint operator
A_{0}
in
\mathcal{H}
.

(ii)

\Ran(\Gamma_{0})=\mathcal{G}
and
\Ker(\Gamma_{0})\cap\Ker(\Gamma_{1})
is dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

For all
f,g\in\Dom(A)
there holds the abstract Green’s identity

\langle Af,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle f,Ag\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}-\langle\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}.

Then
S:=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})\cap\Ker(\Gamma_{1})
is a closed and symmetric operator in
\mathcal{H}
such that
S^{\ast}=\overline{A}
. In particular,
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
is a generalized boundary triple for
S^{\ast}
and
A_{0}=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
.

Proof.

First, by the abstract Green’s identity in (iii) the operator
A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
is symmetric in
\mathcal{H}
. Since
A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
is an extension of a self-adjoint operator
A_{0}
by (i), this can only be true if
A_{0}=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
.

Next, we show that the assumption
\Ran(\Gamma_{0})=\mathcal{G}
implies that
\Ran(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is dense in
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
. Indeed, as
A_{0}=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
is self-adjoint, we see that
\{\mathcal{G},(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}),(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})\}
is a triple for the dual pair
\{\overline{S},\overline{S}\}
in the sense of
[
3
, Def. 2.1]
. Hence, taking the assumptions (i)–(iii) into account, we can apply
[
3
, Lem. 2.3]
, which shows that
\Ran(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
is dense in
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
.

By the shown density of
\Ran(\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1})
in
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}
and the assumptions in (i) and (iii) we can apply
[
13
, Thm. 2.3]
, which implies that

S:=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})\cap\Ker(\Gamma_{1})
is a closed and symmetric operator in
\mathcal{H}
such that
S^{\ast}=\overline{A}
. Thus, all assumptions in Definition
2.10
are fulfilled,
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
is a generalized boundary triple for
S^{\ast}
, and all claims are shown.
∎

Next, the
\gamma
-field and the Weyl function of a given generalized boundary triple will be defined. These operator-valued functions are used in applications to study spectral properties of the self-adjoint extensions of the symmetric operator
S
. Let
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
be a generalized boundary triple for
\overline{A}=S^{\ast}
and
A_{0}=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
. Then for each
w\in\rho(A_{0})
the direct sum decomposition

(36)

\Dom(A)=\Dom(A_{0})\dot{+}\Ker(A-w)=\Ker(\Gamma_{0})\dot{+}\Ker(A-w)

is valid. In particular, the operator
\Gamma_{0}\upharpoonright\Ker(A-w):\Ker(A-w)\to\mathcal{G}
is bijective.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
be a generalized boundary triple for
S^{\ast}
. Then the corresponding
\gamma
-field
\gamma
and Weyl function
M
are defined by

\rho(A_{0})\ni w\mapsto\gamma(w):=\big(\Gamma_{0}\upharpoonright\Ker(A-w)\big)^{-1}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}

and

\rho(A_{0})\ni w\mapsto M(w):=\Gamma_{1}\big(\Gamma_{0}\upharpoonright\Ker(A-w)\big)^{-1}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G},

respectively.

The next result contains a number of useful properties of the
\gamma
-field and the Weyl function. The proof of these statements can be found in
[
13
, Prop. 2.6 and Sect. 3.2]
noting that each generalized boundary triple is also a quasi boundary triple in the sense of
[
13
]
, see
[
13
, Sect. 3.2]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
be a generalized boundary triple for
\overline{A}=S^{\ast}
,
A_{0}:=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0})
, and denote by
\gamma
and
M
the associated
\gamma
-field and Weyl function, respectively. Then the following holds:

(i)

\gamma(w):\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{H}
is bounded and
\Ran(\gamma(w))\subseteq\Ker(A-w)
for every
w\in\rho(A_{0})
.

(ii)

For every
w\in\rho(A_{0})
one has
\gamma(w)^{\ast}=\Gamma_{1}(A_{0}-\overline{w})^{-1}
.

(iii)

M(w):\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
is a linear and bounded operator for every
w\in\rho(A_{0})
. Furthermore, for all
w,z\in\rho(A_{0})

M(z)-M(w)^{\ast}=(z-\overline{w})\gamma(w)^{\ast}\gamma(z)

holds. In particular,
M(w)=M(\overline{w})^{\ast}
and
w\mapsto M(w)
is holomorphic.

Assume that
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
is a fixed generalized boundary triple for
\overline{A}=S^{*}
, and let
B
be a compact and self-adjoint operator in
\mathcal{G}
. In the following, we consider the extension

(37)

A_{B}:=A\upharpoonright\Ker(\Gamma_{0}+B\Gamma_{1})

of
S
, i.e.
f\in\Dom(A_{B})
if and only if
f\in\Dom(A)
satisfies the abstract boundary conditions
\Gamma_{0}f+B\Gamma_{1}f=0
. In the next theorem we discuss how the spectral properties of
A_{B}
can be described with the help of the
\gamma
-field and Weyl function associated with the triple
\{\mathcal{G},\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
. It can be seen as a variant of
[
8
, Thms. 2.6.1&2.6.2]
or
[
13
, Thm. 2.8]
for generalized boundary triples, and it is useful in the situation that
B
admits a splitting
B=B_{1}B_{2}
with
B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{G}
and
B_{2}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{K}
, where
\mathcal{K}
is another Hilbert space.
In particular, item (i) contains an abstract version of the Birman-Schwinger principle to characterize the eigenvalues of
A_{B}
and in assertion (iii) a Krein type resolvent formula is stated.

Theorem 2.14
.

Let
B=B^{\ast}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}
be a compact operator,
\mathcal{K}
be a Hilbert space and
B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{G}
and
B_{2}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{K}
be bounded operators such that
B=B_{1}B_{2}
. Then, the operator
A_{B}
defined by (
37
) is self-adjoint in
\mathcal{H}
and the following holds.

(i)

For every
w\in\rho(A_{0})
one has

(38)

\Ker(A_{B}-w)=\gamma(w)B_{1}\Ker(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}).

In particular,
w\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})
if and only if
0\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})
.

(ii)

For every
w\in\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})
the operator
I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
is boundedly invertible.

(iii)

There holds
\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})=\rho(A_{0})\cap\rho(A_{B})
and for every
w\in\rho(A_{B})\cap\rho(A_{0})
the resolvent representation

(A_{B}-w)^{-1}=(A_{0}-w)^{-1}-\gamma(w)B_{1}\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}

is valid.

Proof.

One could follow directly the arguments in the proof of, e.g.,
[
13
, Thm. 2.8]
. However, since this special situation (where
B
is decomposed as a product
B=B_{1}B_{2}
, where
B_{1},B_{2}
act in different Hilbert spaces) has not been, to the best of our knowledge, treated in the literature so far, we give a full proof here.

To show (i), assume first that
f\in\Ker(A_{B}-w)\setminus\{0\}
for some
w\in\rho(A_{0})
. As the inclusion
\Ker(A_{B}-w)\subset\Ker(A-w)
holds, there exists, by the definition of the
\gamma
-field,
\varphi\in\mathcal{G}
such that
f=\gamma(w)\varphi
. Since
f
also belongs to
\Dom(A_{B})
, we have

(39)

0=(\Gamma_{0}+B_{1}B_{2}\Gamma_{1})f=\big(I+B_{1}B_{2}M(w)\big)\varphi\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\varphi=-B_{1}B_{2}M(w)\varphi.

In particular,
\varphi\in\Ran(B_{1})
, i.e. there exists
\psi_{1}\in\mathcal{K}
such that
\varphi=B_{1}\psi_{1}
. Using this representation in (
39
), we get that

0=B_{1}\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)\psi_{1},

i.e.
\psi_{2}:=(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})\psi_{1}\in\Ker(B_{1})
. Therefore,
\psi:=\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}
satisfies
f=\gamma(w)\varphi=\gamma(w)B_{1}\psi
, so in particular
\psi\neq 0
, and

\begin{split}\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)\psi&=\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)\psi_{1}-\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)\psi_{2}\\
&=\big(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\big)\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}=0,\end{split}

where
\psi_{2}\in\Ker(B_{1})
was used in the second step. This shows the inclusion ’
\subset
’ in (
38
).

For the converse inclusion, assume that
\psi\in\Ker(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})\setminus\{0\}
, which implies
B_{1}\psi\neq 0
. Thus,
f:=\gamma(w)B_{1}\psi\neq 0
. Then,
f\in\Ker(A-w)
and

(\Gamma_{0}+B\Gamma_{1})f=B_{1}\psi+B_{1}B_{2}M(w)B_{1}\psi=0.

Therefore,
f\in\Ker(A-w)\cap\Dom(A_{B})=\Ker(A_{B}-w)
, which shows that also the inclusion ’
\supset
’ in (
38
) is true and finishes the proof of item (i).

To show (ii), assume that
w\in\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})
is fixed. Then by (
38
) applied with
B_{1}=I
and
B_{2}=B
one has
-1\notin\sigma_{\textup{p}}(BM(w))
. Since
B
is compact and
M(w)
is bounded by Proposition
2.13
(iii), it follows with
[
43
, Prop. 2.1.8]

\sigma(B_{2}M(w)B_{1})\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(BM(w))\setminus\{0\}=\sigma_{\textup{p}}(BM(w))\setminus\{0\}.

Consequently,
-1\in\rho(B_{2}M(w)B_{1})
, i.e.
I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
is boundedly invertible.

Next, we will prove item (iii). The inclusion
\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})\supset\rho(A_{0})\cap\rho(A_{B})
is evident. We will show the inclusion
\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})\subset\rho(A_{0})\cap\rho(A_{B})
and the resolvent formula at once. For this purpose, let
w\in\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})
be fixed. Then
A_{B}-w:\Dom(A_{B})\to\mathcal{H}
is injective. To show the surjectivity of this operator, we define for a given
f\in\mathcal{H}
the element
g\in\mathcal{H}
by

g:=(A_{0}-w)^{-1}f-\gamma(w)B_{1}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}f.

Note that
g
is indeed well-defined due to the bijectivity of
I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}
shown above and that
g\in\Dom(A)
. The definition of the Weyl function and Proposition
2.13
(ii) yield

\begin{split}\Gamma_{0}g+B\Gamma_{1}g&=-B_{1}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}f+B\Gamma_{1}(A_{0}-w)^{-1}f\\
&\qquad-BM(w)B_{1}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}f\\
&=0,\end{split}

which implies
g\in\Dom(A_{B})
. Eventually, it follows from Proposition
2.13
(i) that
(A_{B}-w)g=f
holds. This yields the bijectivity of the operator
A_{B}-w:\Dom(A_{B})\to\mathcal{H}
, i.e.
w\in\rho(A_{0})\cap\rho(A_{B})
, and the resolvent formula

(A_{B}-w)^{-1}f=g=(A_{0}-w)^{-1}f-\gamma(w)B_{1}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}f

for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
.

Finally, we will prove the self-adjointness of
A_{B}
. Since
B
is self-adjoint, it follows from the abstract Green’s identity (
35
) that

\begin{split}\langle A_{B}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle f,A_{B}g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}&=\langle\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{0}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}-\langle\Gamma_{0}f,\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}\\
&=-\langle\Gamma_{1}f,B\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}+\langle B\Gamma_{1}f,\Gamma_{1}g\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}=0\end{split}

is valid for all
f,g\in\Dom(A_{B})
, i.e.
A_{B}
is symmetric. Thus,
\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, it was deduced in the previous paragraph that, for every
w\in\rho(A_{0})\setminus\sigma_{\textup{p}}(A_{B})
,
\Ran(A_{B}-w)=\mathcal{H}
. Choosing
w=\pm i
, we conclude that
A_{B}
is self-adjoint.
∎

3.
Definition and spectral properties of
T_{B}

In this section we rigorously define and study the operator
T_{B}
that is defined by (
5
). For this, we construct first in Section
3.1
a generalized boundary triple that is suitable to investigate
T_{B}
. With the help of this generalized boundary triple we show the self-adjointness of
T_{B}
in Section
3.2
. Finally, Section
3.3
is devoted to the spectral analysis of
T_{B}
. Recall that
\Omega_{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded open set with a Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega_{+}=\Sigma
as described in Section
2.2
. Moreover,
\Omega_{-}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{+}}

3.2.

For a compact and self-adjoint operator
B:L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})

one may rewrite the definition (
5

41

(45)

\begin{split}T_{B}f&=(-\Delta f_{+})\oplus(-\Delta f_{-}),\\
\Dom(T_{B})&=\big\{f\in H^{1/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\,|\,\partial_{\overline{z}}f_{\pm}\in H^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm}),\Gamma_{0}f+B\Gamma_{1}f=0\big\},\end{split}

where

is defined by (

). In this section, we will show that the distributional action of (
4
) on

belongs to

if and only if one has

. Then, with the help of the generalized boundary triple

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
T
be defined by (
40
) and
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
be the generalized boundary triple given by (
41
). Moreover, let
B:L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
be compact and self-adjoint,
b_{1},b_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{R}
be its eigenvalues, and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the corresponding orthonormal eigenfunctions. Finally, denote for

and

by

the

-th component of
\varphi_{n}
. Then, for

the expression

defined by

(46)

\begin{split}\widetilde{f}=-\Delta f+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}\Big(&|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})|\\
&+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})|\Big)f\end{split}

is a well-defined distribution and
\widetilde{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if

\Gamma_{0}f+B\Gamma_{1}f=0\qquad\text{in }L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Proof.

Let
f\in\Dom(T)
be fixed and
\widetilde{f}
be defined by (
46
). First, we verify that
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Clearly,
-\Delta f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and also every summand of the series belongs to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Hence, it suffices to show that the series converges in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For this, let
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed. To shorten notation we write
f|_{\Sigma}
and
\partial_{\overline{z}}f|_{\Sigma}
instead of
\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-})
and
\tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\overline{z}}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\overline{z}}f_{-})
. Then, using the definitions from Section
2.3
and the compactness of

we get

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}\Big(|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\ |

\displaystyle+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma}|+|\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})\rangle\langle\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma})|\Big)f,g\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}\big((\varphi_{n}^{1}\delta_{\Sigma},g)+(\partial_{z}(\varphi_{n}^{2}\delta_{\Sigma}),g)\big)\big((\overline{\varphi_{n}^{1}}\delta_{\Sigma},f)+(\partial_{\overline{z}}(\overline{\varphi_{n}^{2}}\delta_{\Sigma}),f)\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{m}b_{n}\big(\langle\overline{g}|_{\Sigma},\varphi_{n}^{1}\rangle_{\Sigma}-\langle\partial_{\overline{z}}\overline{g}|_{\Sigma},\varphi_{n}^{2}\rangle_{\Sigma}\big)\big(\langle\varphi_{n}^{1},f|_{\Sigma}\rangle_{\Sigma}-\langle\varphi_{n}^{2},\partial_{\overline{z}}f|_{\Sigma}\rangle_{\Sigma}\big)

and

(47)

(\widetilde{f},g)=\langle-\Delta\overline{g},f\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}+\langle\Gamma_{1}\overline{g},B\Gamma_{1}f\rangle_{\Sigma}.

\Gamma_{0}g=0

(ii)

For any
w\in\rho(T_{B})\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty))
the map
I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1}
is continuously invertible in
\mathcal{K}
and there holds

(T_{B}-w)^{-1}=(-\Delta-w)^{-1}-\gamma(w)B_{1}(I+B_{2}M(w)B_{1})^{-1}B_{2}\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}.

By choosing
B_{1}=I
and
B_{2}=B
in Theorem
3.5
we get the standard form of the Birman-Schwinger principle and Krein’s resolvent formula for
T_{B}
. Moreover, if
B
is compact in
H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
for some suitable
t
, then the Sobolev regularity in
\Dom(T_{B})
improves. We remark that the latter is the case, if
B
is a finite rank operator and
\Ran(B)\subseteq H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
B
be a compact and self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
T_{B}
be defined by (
45
). Then the following statements hold:

(i)

For
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
there holds

\Ker(T_{B}-w)=\gamma(w)\Ker(I+BM(w)).

In particular,
w\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(T_{B})
if and only if
0\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(I+BM(w))
.

(ii)

For any
w\in\rho(T_{B})\cap(\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty))
the map
I+BM(w)
is continuously invertible in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
and there holds

(T_{B}-w)^{-1}=(-\Delta-w)^{-1}-\gamma(w)(I+BM(w))^{-1}B\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}.

(iii)

Assume that
B:H^{s}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
is bounded for some
s\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\,t\in[s,\frac{3}{2}]
, that
B
is compact in
H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
, and that
\Sigma
is a
C^{3}
-boundary. Then

\Dom(T_{B})\subseteq\left\{f\in H^{t+1/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\>\big|\>\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{\pm}\in H^{t+1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\}.

Proof.

The claims in (i) and (ii) follow immediately from Theorem
3.5
, so it remains to prove item (iii). Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
be fixed. Taking the assumptions on
B
and the mapping properties of
M(w)
from Remark
3.2
into account, one sees that
I+BM(w):H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
is a Fredholm operator with index
0
. Since
T_{B}
is self-adjoint by Theorem
3.5
, it follows with (i) that
I+BM(w)
is injective. Therefore,
I+BM(w)
is bijective in
H^{t}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
. With Proposition
3.1
(ii) and Remark
3.2
this yields that

\Ran\big(\gamma(w)\big(I+BM(w)\big)^{-1}B\gamma(\overline{w})^{*}\big)\subseteq H^{t+1/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)

and

\Ran\big(\partial_{\bar{z}}\gamma(w)\big(I+BM(w)\big)^{-1}B\gamma(\overline{w})^{*}\big)\subseteq H^{t+1/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma),

which together with
\Ran(-\Delta-w)^{-1}=H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
shows the claimed inclusion in (iii) and finishes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Spectrum of
T_{B}

In this subsection we analyze the spectrum of
T_{B}
; cf. Theorem
3.11
. In particular, we will prove that
T_{B}
is unbounded from below, if
B:L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
, given by

(48)

B=\begin{pmatrix}B_{11}&B_{12}\\
B_{12}^{\ast}&B_{22}\end{pmatrix},

is a compact and non-negative operator and
B_{22}
has infinitely many positive eigenvalues. For this, some preliminary considerations are necessary. In the next two propositions we analyze the limit of the Weyl function
M(w)
associated with the generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
, as the spectral parameter
w
tends to
-\infty
. The first proposition deals with the convergence of
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)
, where
S(w)
is the single layer boundary integral operator defined by (
25
) viewed as an operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
. To prove the result, we additionally assume that
\Sigma
is a
C^{2}
-smooth loop, i.e. that
\Sigma
has a
C^{2}
-smooth parametrization
\zeta
as described at the beginning of Section
2.2
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\Sigma
be a
C^{2}
-smooth loop. Then
\sqrt{|w|}S(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
is uniformly bounded with respect to
w\in(-\infty,0)
and converges in the strong sense to
\tfrac{1}{2}I
for
w\to-\infty
.

Proof.

Let
L
be the length of
\Sigma
and
\zeta
be an arc-length parametrization which satisfies (
9
).
According to
[
30
, Thm. 1.3]
the norm of
S(w):L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
,
w\in(-\infty,0)
, can be estimated by

\|S(w)\|\leq\frac{C}{(2+|w|)^{1/2}}\ln(2+|w|^{-1})^{1/2},

where
C>0
does not depend on
w\in(-\infty,0)
.
This implies the uniform boundedness of
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)
. Hence, it remains to prove the claim concerning the strong convergence. Since
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)
is uniformly bounded and
C(\Sigma)
is dense in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
, see
[
46
, Thm. 3.14]
, it suffices by
[
35
, Lem. III.3.5]
to prove

\lim_{w\to-\infty}\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\varphi\quad\textup{in }L^{2}(\Sigma)

for all
\varphi\in C(\Sigma)
. Let
\varphi\in C(\Sigma)
be fixed. First, we show that
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi
converges pointwise to
\varphi/2
. With (
26
) one sees for
t\in[0,L]
that

\displaystyle(\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi)(\zeta(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{t-L/2}^{t+L/2}\sqrt{|w|}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=g_{w,1}(t)+g_{w,2}(t)

with
g_{w,1},g_{w,2}:[0,L]\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle g_{w,1}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}\sqrt{|w|}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle g_{w,2}(t)

\displaystyle=(\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi)(\zeta(t))-g_{w,1}(t),

where we used that our choice of the complex square root yields
-i\sqrt{w}=\sqrt{|w|}
for
w<0
.
Next, we consider the pointwise convergence of
g_{w,2}
. Let
t\in[0,L]
be fixed. If
|w|>0
is sufficiently large, then
B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})\subseteq B(t,L/2)
. Consequently,

g_{w,2}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(t,L/2)\setminus B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}\sqrt{|w|}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s.

Due to Lemma
2.5
(i)&(iv) there exists a constant
C>0
such that the estimate
0<K_{0}(\upsilon)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}
is valid for all
\upsilon>1
. Furthermore, (
9
) implies for all
s\in B(t,L/2)\setminus B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})
and
|w|
sufficiently large

\sqrt{|w|}|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|\geq C_{\zeta}\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|\geq C_{\zeta}\ln|w|>1.

This and estimations of the integrals lead to

\displaystyle|g_{w,2}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\int_{B(t,L/2)\setminus B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}\sqrt{|w|}\mathrm{e}^{-C_{\zeta}\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\int_{\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|}}^{\infty}\sqrt{|w|}\mathrm{e}^{-C_{\zeta}\sqrt{|w|}s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\int_{\ln|w|}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-C_{\zeta}s}\mathrm{d}s.

Hence,
g_{w,2}(t)\rightarrow 0
for
w\to-\infty
. To compute the limit of
g_{w,1}(t)
, we write
g_{w,1}(t)=g_{w,3}(t)+g_{w,4}(t)
with
g_{w,3},g_{w,4}:[0,L]\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

\displaystyle g_{w,3}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}\sqrt{|w|}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|)\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle g_{w,4}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}\sqrt{|w|}\Big(K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|)

\displaystyle-K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|)\Big)\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s.

To analyze the behaviour of
g_{w,4}(t)
for
w\to-\infty
, we introduce for
\vartheta\in[0,1]
the function

\zeta_{\vartheta}(t,s)=\vartheta|\zeta(t)-\zeta(s)|+(1-\vartheta)|t-s|.

Then, by the fundamental theorem of calculus one has

(49)

\begin{split}g_{w,4}(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}&\int_{B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}|w|\\
&\qquad\int_{0}^{1}K_{0}^{\prime}(\sqrt{|w|}\zeta_{\vartheta}(t,s))(\zeta_{1}(t,s)-\zeta_{0}(t,s))\,d\vartheta\varphi(\zeta(s))\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

Since
\zeta
is an
L
-periodic
C^{2}
-function with
|\dot{\zeta}(t)|=1
, one finds with Taylor’s theorem that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(50)

|\zeta_{1}(t,s)-\zeta_{0}(t,s)|\leq C|t-s|^{2}\quad\forall s\in B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|}).

Furthermore, as
B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})\subseteq B(t,L/2)
for sufficiently large
|w|>0
, (
9
) implies

(51)

C_{\zeta}|t-s|\leq\zeta_{\vartheta}(t,s)\leq|t-s|\quad\forall s\in B\big(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|}\big),\vartheta\in[0,1].

The recurrence relations and asymptotics of the modified Bessel functions in Lemma
2.5
yield that there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(52)

|K_{0}^{\prime}(\upsilon)|=K_{1}(\upsilon)\leq C\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}}{\upsilon}+\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}}{\sqrt{\upsilon}}\right)\leq C\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{\upsilon}{2}}}{\upsilon},\quad\forall\upsilon\in(0,+\infty).

Using (
50
), (
51
), and (
52
) in (
49
) leads to

\displaystyle|g_{w,4}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\int_{B(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|})}|w|(t-s)^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{C_{\zeta}}{2}\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|}}{\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}\int_{B(0,\ln|w|)}|s|\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{C_{\zeta}}{2}|s|}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}\int_{0}^{\infty}s\mathrm{e}^{-\tfrac{C_{\zeta}}{2}s}\mathrm{d}s.

Hence,
g_{w,4}(t)\rightarrow 0
for
w\to-\infty
. To complete the proof of the pointwise convergence
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi\to\varphi/2
, it remains to investigate
g_{w,3}(t)
for
w\to-\infty
. Due to
0<K_{0}(\upsilon)
for every
\upsilon>0
and the continuity of
\varphi\circ\zeta
, the mean value theorem for integrals yields the existence of an
s_{t,w}\in B\big(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|}\big)
such that

\displaystyle g_{w,3}(t)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\zeta(s_{t,w}))\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B\big(t,\ln|w|/\sqrt{|w|}\big)}\sqrt{|w|}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}|t-s|)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\varphi(\zeta(s_{t,w}))\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\ln|w|}K_{0}(s)\mathrm{d}s.

Consequently, with

(53)

\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{0}(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\pi}{2}

we conclude that
g_{w,3}(t)\to\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\zeta(t))
for
w\to-\infty
. Since this holds for all
t\in[0,L]
,
(\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi)\circ\zeta
converges pointwise to
\tfrac{1}{2}\varphi\circ\zeta
, which in particular yields the pointwise convergence of
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi
to
\varphi/2
for
w\to-\infty
.

To finish the proof, we show that
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\varphi
also in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
. For this, note that
K_{0}
is strictly monotonically decreasing on
(0,+\infty)
and hence, using (
9
) we find for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\big|\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi(\zeta(t))\big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{|w|}}{2\pi}\int_{t-L/2}^{t+L/2}K_{0}(\sqrt{|w|}C_{\zeta}|t-s|)|\varphi(\zeta(s))|\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}\frac{1}{C_{\zeta}\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}K_{0}(s)\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}}{2C_{\zeta}},

where (
53
) was used in the last equality. Therefore, the pointwise upper bound
|\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi(x_{\Sigma})|\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Sigma)}/(2C_{\zeta})
holds for all
x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma
. Thus, an application of the dominated convergence theorem shows that
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)\varphi
converges to
\frac{1}{2}\varphi
in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

By
[
50
, Lem. 6.9 (ii)]
the composition of
S(w)
with a compact operator turns the strong convergence in Proposition
3.7
into convergence in the operator norm. Thus, we obtain immediately the following corollary.

Corollary 3.8
.

Additionally to the assumptions of Proposition
3.7
, suppose that
K
is a compact operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
. Then,
\sqrt{|w|}S(w)K
converges in the operator norm to
K/2
for
w\to-\infty
.

In the next proposition we analyze the behaviour of
W(w)
defined in (
28
), as
w\rightarrow-\infty
. We point out that no
C^{2}
-assumption on
\Sigma
is needed for this.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\tau>0
. Then
|w|^{-\tau}W(w)\rightarrow 0
in the operator norm for
w\to-\infty
.

Proof.

Let
w<0
be fixed. Recall that in this case our choice of the complex square root yields
-i\sqrt{w}=\sqrt{|w|}>0
. Furthermore, by Lemma
2.9

W(w)
is a strongly singular integral operator with kernel
k_{W}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})
, where

k_{W}(x)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\sqrt{|w|}K_{1}(\sqrt{|w|}|x|)\frac{\overline{{\bf x}}}{|x|}.

Next, let the function
k_{W_{0}}
be defined by

k_{W_{0}}(x)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{\overline{{\bf x}}}{|x|^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{{\bf x}},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}.

Then, due to
[
25
, p. 1071]
, the strongly singular integral operator

W_{0}:L^{2}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
acting as

W_{0}\varphi(x_{\Sigma}):=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{\Sigma\setminus B(x_{\Sigma},\varepsilon)}k_{W_{0}}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})\varphi(y_{\Sigma})\mathrm{d}\sigma(y_{\Sigma}),\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\Sigma),\penalty\ x_{\Sigma}\in\Sigma,

is well-defined and bounded. Let
\delta\in(0,\min\{2\tau,1\})
be arbitrary but fixed. Then,
1-\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}\leq\upsilon^{\delta}
for all
\upsilon>0
and Lemma
2.5
(iii)&(v) yield for every
\upsilon>0
the estimate

\left|K_{1}(\upsilon)-\frac{1}{\upsilon}\right|\leq\left|K_{1}(\upsilon)-\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}}{\upsilon}\right|+\left|\frac{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}}{\upsilon}\right|\leq C(\mathrm{e}^{-\upsilon}+\upsilon^{\delta-1})

with some constant
C>0
. In particular, for
k=k_{W}-k_{W_{0}}
this implies for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
the pointwise bound

|k(x)|=\frac{\sqrt{|w|}}{4\pi}\left|K_{1}(\sqrt{|w|}|x|)-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}|x|}\right|\leq C\Big(\sqrt{|w|}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{|w|}|x|}+|w|^{\delta/2}|x|^{\delta-1}\Big).

Therefore, by Proposition
2.3
, which can be used due to the assumptions on
\Sigma
,

\|W(w)-W_{0}\|\leq C\int_{0}^{C_{\zeta}L/2}\sqrt{|w|}\mathrm{e}^{-\sqrt{|w|}s}+|w|^{\delta/2}s^{\delta-1}\mathrm{d}s\leq C(1+|w|^{\delta/2}).

Since
\delta<2\tau
, this finally yields

\begin{split}|w|^{-\tau}\|W(w)\|&\leq|w|^{-\tau}\|W_{0}\|+|w|^{-\tau}\|W(w)-W_{0}\|\\
&\leq|w|^{-\tau}\|W_{0}\|+C\big(|w|^{-\tau}+|w|^{\delta/2-\tau}\big)\rightarrow 0\end{split}

for
w\rightarrow-\infty
, which is the claim of this proposition.
∎

To be able to use the convergence results from Propositions
3.7
and
3.9
for the spectral analysis of
T_{B}
, we will use the following lemma about the convergence of eigenvalues of compact operators that converge in the operator norm. This result follows from
[
35
, § IV.3.5]
, see also
[
35
, § II.5.2 and § III.6.5]
for further explanations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of compact and self-adjoint operators in an infinite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, which converge in the operator norm to a non-negative operator
K
. Moreover, let
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\lambda_{k}^{n})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be the decreasing sequences of non-negative eigenvalues of
K
and
K_{n}
, respectively, possibly extended by zero and taking their multiplicities into account. Then for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
there holds

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{k}^{n}=\lambda_{k}.

Now, we are prepared to prove the main result about the spectrum of
T_{B}
with compact and self-adjoint parameters
B
.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
B
be a compact and self-adjoint operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
and
T_{B}
be defined by (
45
). Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{B})=[0,+\infty)
.

(ii)

If
B
has finite rank
k=\textup{dim}(\textup{Ran}(B))
, then
T_{B}
has at most
k
discrete eigenvalues.

(iii)

Let
B:L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
be given by (
48
) with
B_{12}=B_{22}=0
. Then
T_{B}
has at most finitely many discrete eigenvalues and there holds

(54)

\Dom(T_{B})=\left\{f\in H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\>\big|\>\gamma_{N}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{N}^{-}f_{-}=-B_{11}\gamma_{D}f\right\}.

(iv)

Assume that
\Sigma
is a
C^{2}
-boundary and let
B
be non-negative and be given by (
48
). Furthermore, assume that
B_{22}
has infinitely many positive eigenvalues. Then,
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(T_{B})
is infinite and unbounded from below.

Proof.

To show item (i), we note first that Corollary
3.6
(ii) and the compactness of
B
imply that

(55)

(T_{B}-w)^{-1}-(-\Delta-w)^{-1}=-\gamma(w)(I+BM(w))^{-1}B\gamma(\overline{w})^{\ast}

is compact in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Hence, Weyls Theorem
[
45
, Thm. XIII.14]
yields

\sigma_{\text{ess}}(T_{B})=\sigma_{\text{ess}}(-\Delta)=\sigma(-\Delta)=[0,+\infty).

To prove the assertion in (ii), note that the operator in (
55
)
is a finite rank operator with rank at most
k
.
Together with
[
21
, Chap. 9.3, Thm. 3]
this yields that
\sigma_{\text{disc}}(T_{B})
contains at most
k
eigenvalues taking multiplicities into account.

Next, we prove item (iii). We first verify that (
54
) is true. Recall that by Corollary
3.6
(ii) for any
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the operator

I+BM(w)=\begin{pmatrix}I+B_{11}S(w)&B_{11}W(w)\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}

is bijective in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
. In particular,
I+B_{11}S(w)
is boundedly invertible in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C})
. A simple computation shows that

\big(I+BM(w)\big)^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}(I+B_{11}S(w))^{-1}&-(I+B_{11}S(w))^{-1}B_{11}W(w)\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}.

Taking again
B_{12}=B_{22}=0
and the last displayed formula into account, we find that Krein’s resolvent formula in (
55
) can be simplified to

(T_{B}-w)^{-1}=(-\Delta-w)^{-1}-SL(w)\big(I+B_{11}S(w)\big)^{-1}B_{11}\gamma_{D}(-\Delta-w)^{-1}.

With the mapping properties of
SL(w)
from Lemma
2.6
(i) we find that

(56)

\Dom(T_{B})\subset H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

Moreover, by definition, a function
f\in H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
belongs to
\Dom(T_{B})
if and only if
\Gamma_{0}f+B\Gamma_{1}f=0
, which is, due to
B_{12}=B_{22}=0
, equivalent to

(57)

2\bar{\mathbf{n}}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-})=-B_{11}\gamma_{D}f.

To proceed, note that there exists a linear and bounded tangential derivative operator
\partial_{t}:H^{1}(\Sigma)\to L^{2}(\Sigma)
which acts on functions
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})
as

\partial_{t}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}g=n_{1}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}\partial_{2}g-n_{2}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}\partial_{1}g,

see, e.g.,
[
31
, Lem. A.1]
.
Next, let
\zeta
be an arc-length parametrization of
\Sigma
such that
n\circ\zeta=(\dot{\zeta_{2}},-\dot{\zeta_{1}})
and
t\circ\zeta=(\dot{\zeta_{1}},\dot{\zeta_{2}})
is the unit tangent vector.
Then, by a direct calculation one finds for any
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega_{\pm}})

\begin{split}2(\bar{\mathbf{n}}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}\partial_{\bar{z}}g)\circ\zeta&=\dot{\zeta_{2}}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}(\partial_{1}g)\circ\zeta-\dot{\zeta_{1}}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}(\partial_{2}g)\circ\zeta\\
&\qquad\qquad+i\dot{\zeta_{1}}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}(\partial_{1}g)\circ\zeta+i\dot{\zeta_{2}}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}(\partial_{2}g)\circ\zeta\\
&=(\gamma_{N}^{\pm}g+i\partial_{t}\gamma_{D}^{\pm}g)\circ\zeta.\end{split}

By density, this identity extends to all functions
g\in H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\Omega_{\pm})
. In particular, for any
f\in H^{3/2}_{\Delta}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\Sigma)\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
there holds
\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}=\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-}\in H^{1}(\Sigma)
, and so

2\bar{\mathbf{n}}(\gamma_{D}^{+}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}\partial_{\bar{z}}f_{-})=\gamma_{N}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{N}^{-}f_{-}.

Using this in (
57
), we find that the condition
\Gamma_{0}f+B\Gamma_{1}f=0
is equivalent to

\gamma_{N}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{N}^{-}f_{-}=-B_{11}\gamma_{D}f,

which in combination with (
56
) implies that (
54
) is true.

Next, we prove that the discrete spectrum of
T_{B}
is finite. For this, we consider the auxiliary quadratic form

\widetilde{t}_{B}[f]:=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}-\|B_{11}\|\|\gamma_{D}f\|_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}^{2},\quad\Dom(\widetilde{t}_{B}):=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

By
[
5
, Prop. 3.1]
the form
\widetilde{t}_{B}
is closed and bounded from below, and via the first representation theorem
[
35
, Thm. VI.2.1]
it is associated with a Schrödinger operator
\widetilde{T}_{B}
with a
\delta
-interaction supported on
\Sigma
of strength
-\|B_{11}\|
that is formally given by
-\Delta-\|B_{11}\|\delta_{\Sigma}
. By
[
14
, Thm. 3.14]
the discrete spectrum of
\widetilde{T}_{B}
is finite.
To proceed, define the sesquilinear form
t_{B}
by

t_{B}[f]:=\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}+\langle B_{11}\gamma_{D}f,\gamma_{D}f\rangle_{\Sigma},\quad\Dom(t_{B}):=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

As for
\widetilde{t}_{B}
one shows that
t_{B}
is closed and bounded from below.
Moreover, using integration by parts and the transmission condition in (
54
), we see that

\langle T_{B}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}=t_{B}(f,g)\qquad\forall f\in\Dom(T_{B}),\,\forall g\in\Dom(t_{B}).

Since
\Dom(T_{B})\subset\Dom(t_{B})
and
T_{B}
is self-adjoint, this implies that
t_{B}
is the quadratic form associated with
T_{B}
via the first representation theorem.
Furthermore,
t_{B}\geq\widetilde{t}_{B}
in the sense of quadratic forms and
\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(\widetilde{T}_{B})=\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(T_{B})=[0,+\infty)
. Since
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(\widetilde{T}_{B})
is finite, this and the min-max principle imply that also
\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(T_{B})
has to be finite. This finishes the proof of item (iii).

It remains to prove item (iv). Let
w<0
be fixed. As
B
is non-negative, we can apply Theorem
3.5
(i) with
B_{1}=B_{2}=B^{1/2}
and get that

(58)

\begin{split}w\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(T_{B})\quad&\Longleftrightarrow\quad 0\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}I+\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}B^{1/2}M(w)B^{1/2}\right).\end{split}

Hence, it is sufficient to study for
w<0
the positive eigenvalues of the compact and self-adjoint operator

A(w):=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}B^{1/2}M(w)B^{1/2}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}B^{1/2}\begin{pmatrix}S(w)&W(w)\\
W(\overline{w})^{\ast}&\frac{w}{4}S(w)\end{pmatrix}B^{1/2},

where
S(w)
and
W(w)
are defined by (
25
) and (
28
), respectively.

For
w<0
, denote by
(\mu_{k}(w))_{k=1}^{N(w)},\,N(w)\leq+\infty,
the decreasing sequence of non-negative eigenvalues of
A(w)
, taking their multiplicities into account. If the sequence is finite, we will always extend it by zeros. Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)\ni w\mapsto M(w)
is a holomorphic operator valued function due to Proposition
2.13
, Lemma
3.10
implies that the mapping
w\mapsto\mu_{k}(w)
is continuous.
Furthermore, since
B^{1/2}
is compact, we get with Corollary
3.8
and Proposition
3.9
that

\lim_{w\rightarrow-\infty}A(w)=\frac{1}{8}B^{1/2}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}B^{1/2}=:A(-\infty)

in the operator norm, where
I
denotes the identity operator in
L^{2}(\Sigma)
. Note that the well-known identity
\sigma(CD)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(DC)\setminus\{0\}
for two bounded operators
C,D
in a Hilbert space (see, e.g.,
[
43
, Prop. 2.1.8]
) implies that the positive eigenvalues of
A(-\infty)
are exactly the positive eigenvalues of
\frac{1}{8}B_{22}
, that will be denoted by
\lambda_{k}
. Then, we conclude with Lemma
3.10
that

\lim_{w\rightarrow-\infty}\mu_{k}(w)=\lambda_{k}>0.

Moreover, as
A(-1)
is compact, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu_{k}(-1)<1
for all
k>k_{0}
. Hence, for every
k>k_{0}
the function
f_{k}:(-\infty,-1]\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

f_{k}(w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|w|}}-\mu_{k}(w)

satisfies
f_{k}(-1)>0
and converges for
w\to-\infty
to the negative number
-\lambda_{k}
.
In particular, because of the continuity of
\mu_{k}(w)
, there exists a
w_{k}\in(-\infty,-1]
with
f_{k}(w_{k})=0
for every
k>k_{0}
, implying
w_{k}\in\sigma_{\textup{p}}(T_{B})
due to (
58
). Since
\sigma_{\text{ess}}(T_{B})=[0,+\infty)
by (i), we conclude with Theorem
3.5
(i) that the points
w_{k}
can not have an accumulation point in
(-\infty,-1]
and thus, the sequence
(w_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has to tend to
-\infty
for
k\rightarrow\infty
. This finishes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
The Dirac operator with a non-local singular potential and its non-relativistic limit

In this section, we will consider the non-relativistic limit of the relativistic model of non-local
\delta
-shell interactions, which was discussed in the recent paper
[
33
]
. It turns out that this non-relativistic limit is of the form
T_{B}
as in (
45
) for a special parameter
B
.

To introduce the mentioned Dirac operators with non-local singular interactions, some notations have to be fixed first. Let

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},

be the Pauli spin matrices. We will use for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
the notation

\sigma\cdot x:=\sigma_{1}x_{1}+\sigma_{2}x_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma\cdot\nabla:=\sigma_{1}\partial_{1}+\sigma_{2}\partial_{2}.

We will make use of the function spaces

\begin{split}H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})&=\{f\in H^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm};\mathbb{C}^{2})\mid(\sigma\cdot\nabla)f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm};\mathbb{C}^{2})\}\\
&=\left\{f=\begin{pmatrix}f_{1}\\
f_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\pm};\mathbb{C}^{2})\,\Big|\,f_{1}\in H_{\partial_{\overline{z}}}^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm}),f_{2}\in H_{\partial_{z}}^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})\right\},\end{split}

where the spaces
H_{\partial_{z}}^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})
and
H_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}^{1/2}(\Omega_{\pm})
are defined in (
11
) and (
10
), respectively. Taking Lemma
2.2
into account, we see that
f\in H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{\pm})
possesses a Dirichlet trace
\gamma_{D}^{\pm}f\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Next, given
F,\,G\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, define the operator

(59)

\begin{split}D_{F,G}^{c}f&=\left(-ic(\sigma\cdot\nabla)+\sigma_{3}\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)f_{+}\oplus\left(-ic(\sigma\cdot\nabla)+\sigma_{3}\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)f_{-},\\
\Dom(D_{F,G}^{c})&=\bigg\{f\in H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{+})\oplus H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{-})\>\big|\\
&\quad\qquad-i(\sigma\cdot n)(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}-\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-})=\frac{1}{2}F\int_{\Sigma}G^{\ast}(\gamma_{D}^{+}f_{+}+\gamma_{D}^{-}f_{-})\mathrm{d}\sigma\bigg\}.\end{split}

Comparing this operator to
[
33
, Def. 3.0.1]
, it turns out that
D_{F,G}^{c}
is the rigorous mathematical operator in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})
associated with the formal expression

\mathcal{D}_{F,G}^{c}=-ic\sigma\cdot\nabla+\sigma_{3}\frac{c^{2}}{2}+c|F\delta_{\Sigma}\rangle\langle G\delta_{\Sigma}|.

More concretely,
D_{F,G}^{c}=cD_{F,G}
, where
D_{F,G}
is defined in
[
33
, Def. 3.0.1]
with the
c
-dependent mass term
m=\frac{c}{2}
and dimension
n=2
. Note that for
F=G=0
the operator
D_{0,0}^{c}=:D_{0}^{c}
coincides with the free Dirac operator

D_{0}^{c}f=\left(-ic(\sigma\cdot\nabla)+\sigma_{3}\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)f,\quad\Dom(D_{0}^{c})=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2}).

Now, we will discuss basic properties of
D_{F,G}^{c}
and, in particular, a useful resolvent formula. For this, some further objects have to be introduced. Recall that for
\widetilde{w}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
the operators
SL(\widetilde{w}),\,WL(\widetilde{w}),\,\widetilde{WL}(\widetilde{w}):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
are defined by (
19
), (
21
), and (
22
), respectively. Then, we define for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus((-\infty,-\frac{c^{2}}{2}]\cup[\frac{c^{2}}{2},+\infty))
the operator
\Phi^{c}(w):L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

(60)

\Phi^{c}(w):=\begin{pmatrix}(\tfrac{w}{c}+\tfrac{c}{2})SL(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})&-2iWL(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})\\
-2i\widetilde{WL}(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})&(\tfrac{w}{c}-\tfrac{c}{2})SL(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})\end{pmatrix}.

Furthermore, recall that for
\widetilde{w}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty)
the operators
S(\widetilde{w}),W(\widetilde{w}):L^{2}(\Sigma)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma)
are defined by (
25
) and (
28
), respectively. Then, we define for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus((-\infty,-\frac{c^{2}}{2}]\cup[\frac{c^{2}}{2},+\infty))
the operator
\mathcal{C}^{c}(w):L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

(61)

\mathcal{C}^{c}(w):=\begin{pmatrix}(\tfrac{w}{c}+\tfrac{c}{2})S(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})&-2iW(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})\\
2iW(\tfrac{\overline{w}^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})^{*}&(\tfrac{w}{c}-\tfrac{c}{2})S(\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4})\end{pmatrix}.

Finally, for
A\in L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
, we define a bounded operator
\Pi_{A}:\,L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
as

\Pi_{A}f:=\int_{\Sigma}(A^{\ast}f)\mathrm{d}\sigma.

Its adjoint
\Pi_{A}^{\ast}:\,\mathbb{C}^{2}\to L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
acts as multiplication by the matrix
A
. Therefore, the rank of
\Pi_{A}^{\ast}
equals to the number of linearly independent columns of
A
in
L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Note that with a constant
2\times 2
matrix
C
one gets
\Pi_{CA}=\Pi_{A}C^{\ast}
and
\Pi_{CA}^{\ast}=C\Pi_{A}^{\ast}
. Furthermore, comparing to the
bra-ket
notation, we obtain
|F\rangle\langle G|=\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\Pi_{G}
.

The following results are basically modifications of similar observations from
[
33
]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\Pi_{G}=\Pi_{G}^{\ast}\Pi_{F}
, then the operator
D_{F,G}^{c}
defined by (
59
) is self-adjoint in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})
and the following is true:

(i)

\sigma_{\textup{ess}}(D_{F,G}^{c})=(-\infty,-\frac{c^{2}}{2}]\cup[\frac{c^{2}}{2},+\infty)
.

(ii)

\sigma_{\textup{disc}}(D_{F,G}^{c})
consists of at most two eigenvalues taking multiplicities into account.

(iii)

For
w\in\rho(D_{F,G}^{c})
, the matrix
(I+\Pi_{G}\mathcal{C}^{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast})
is invertible and

\begin{split}\big(D_{F,G}^{c}&-w\big)^{-1}=\big(D_{0}^{c}-w\big)^{-1}\\
&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Phi^{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big(I+\Pi_{G}\mathcal{C}^{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Pi_{G}\Phi^{c}(\overline{w})^{\ast}\end{split}

holds on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

Using that
D_{F,G}^{c}=cD_{F,G}
, where
D_{F,G}
is the operator studied in
[
33
]
with
m=\frac{c}{2}
, items (i) and (ii) follow from
[
33
, Thms. 3.0.1&3.0.2]
. To get the claimed resolvent formula, we use the generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0}^{(1/2)},\Gamma_{1}^{(1/2)}\}
introduced in
[
10
, Thm. 4.3]
(for
s=\frac{1}{2}
and
m=\frac{c}{2}
), which was also used in
[
33
]
to define and study
D_{F,G}
. First, we note that by (
19
), (
21
), and (
22
) the map
\Phi^{c}(w)
defined in (
60
) is an integral operator with integral kernel

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4}}K_{1}&\left(-i\sqrt{\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4}}|x-y_{\Sigma}|\right)\frac{(\sigma\cdot(x-y_{\Sigma}))}{|x-y_{\Sigma}|}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}\left(-i\sqrt{\tfrac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}-\tfrac{c^{2}}{4}}|x-y_{\Sigma}|\right)\left(\frac{w}{c}I+\frac{c}{2}\sigma_{3}\right).\end{split}

Therefore, by
[
10
, Thm. 4.3 (ii)]
the
\gamma
-field corresponding to the generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0}^{(1/2)},\Gamma_{1}^{(1/2)}\}
coincides with
\Phi^{c}(c\,\,\cdot\,)
. By a similar line of reasoning, due to
[
10
, Thm. 4.3 (iii)]
, the Weyl function corresponding to the generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0}^{(1/2)},\Gamma_{1}^{(1/2)}\}
coincides with
\mathcal{C}^{c}(c\,\,\cdot\,)
defined in (
61
). Next, for any
f\in H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{+})\oplus H^{1/2}_{\sigma}(\Omega_{-})
one has

f\in\Dom(D_{F,G})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Gamma_{0}^{(1/2)}f+\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\Pi_{G}\Gamma_{1}^{(1/2)}f=0;

cf.
[
33
, Def. 3.0.1]
. Therefore, this and Theorem
2.14
(iii) applied with
B_{1}=\Pi_{F}^{*}
and
B_{2}=\Pi_{G}
imply

\begin{split}(D_{F,G}^{c}-w)^{-1}&=\frac{1}{c}\big(D_{F,G}-\frac{w}{c}\big)^{-1}\\
&=\frac{1}{c}\big(D_{0,0}-\frac{w}{c}\big)^{-1}-\frac{1}{c}\Phi^{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big(I+\Pi_{G}\mathcal{C}^{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}\Pi_{G}\Phi^{c}(\overline{w})^{\ast}.\end{split}

∎

In order to find the non-relativistic limit of
D_{F,G}^{c}
, one has to subtract the particle’s rest energy
\frac{c^{2}}{2}
, which is a completely relativistic object, from the total energy and then compute the limit of the resolvent of the resulting operator
D_{F,G}^{c}-c^{2}/2
, as
c\rightarrow+\infty
. To get a non-trivial limit, we will in fact work with the operator
D_{F_{c},G_{c}}^{c}
,
where

(62)

F_{c}:=S_{c}F,\,G_{c}:=S_{c}G\quad\text{with}\quad S_{c}:=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}&0\\
0&\sqrt{c}\end{pmatrix},

instead of
D_{F,G}^{c}
itself, cf.
[
32
,
20
]
for similar rescalings.
Define for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the numbers
\widetilde{w}_{c}:=w+\frac{c^{2}}{2}
and
w_{c}:=w+\frac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}
.
Then, taking Proposition
4.1
(iii) into account, we have to study the convergence of the operator

(63)

\begin{split}\big(D_{F_{c},G_{c}}^{c}&-\widetilde{w}_{c}\big)^{-1}=\big(D_{0}^{c}-\widetilde{w}_{c}\big)^{-1}\\
&-\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Phi^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})S_{c}\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big(I+\Pi_{G}S_{c}\mathcal{C}^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})S_{c}\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Pi_{G}S_{c}\Phi^{c}(\overline{\widetilde{w}_{c}})^{\ast}\end{split}

as
c\rightarrow\infty
. Note that

(64)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Phi^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})S_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}\left(1+\frac{w}{c^{2}}\right)SL(w_{c})&-2iWL(w_{c})\\
-\frac{2i}{c}\widetilde{WL}(w_{c})&\frac{w}{c}SL(w_{c})\end{pmatrix}\end{split}

and

(65)

S_{c}\mathcal{C}^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})S_{c}=\begin{pmatrix}(1+\tfrac{w}{c^{2}})S(w_{c})&-2iW(w_{c})\\
2iW(\overline{w_{c}})^{*}&wS(w_{c})\end{pmatrix}=:N_{c}(w).

We will find the limits of (
64
) and (
65
) in the respective uniform operator topologies as
c\to+\infty
. For this, we need the following preliminary lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,
w_{c}=w+w^{2}/c^{2}
, and
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{C}
be defined by

\begin{split}t_{1}(x)&=\frac{1}{c}\sqrt{w_{c}}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)\\
t_{2}(x)&=\frac{1}{c^{2}}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|),\\
t_{3}(x)&=K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|),\\
t_{4}(x)&=\sqrt{w_{c}}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\sqrt{w}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|).\end{split}

Then there exist positive constants
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3},\kappa_{4}
, depending only on
w
, such that for all sufficiently large
c>0
the estimates

|t_{1}(x)|\leq\frac{\kappa_{1}}{c}(|x|^{-1}+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{2}|x|}

and

|t_{j}(x)|\leq\frac{\kappa_{1}}{c^{2}}(|\ln|x||+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{2}|x|}\leq\frac{\kappa_{3}}{c^{2}}(|x|^{-1/4}+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{4}|x|},\qquad j\in\{2,3,4\},

hold on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

First, we mention that the second estimate on
|t_{j}|
,
j\in\{2,3,4\}
, always follows from the first one, as soon as this one is established.
The estimates for the functions
t_{1}
and
t_{3}
follow from the proof of
[
11
, Lem. 3.1]
, see equation (3.8) and the consideration on the function denoted by
t_{2}
in
[
11
]
. The estimate for
t_{2}
can be deduced from Lemma
2.5
(iv). Hence it remains to control
t_{4}
. Using the expansion of
K_{1}
from Lemma
2.5
(iii), one gets

(66)

\begin{split}\sqrt{w_{c}}&K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\sqrt{w}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\\
&=-iw_{c}|x|g_{1}(-w_{c}|x|^{2})\ln(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)+iw|x|g_{1}(-w|x|^{2})\ln(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\\
&\quad-iw_{c}|x|g_{2}(-w_{c}|x|^{2})+iw|x|g_{2}(-w|x|^{2}).\end{split}

Due to the holomorphy of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
, there exists for any
R>0
a constant
C>0
that is independent of
c
such that

|x|\big|g_{j}(-w_{c}|x|^{2})-g_{j}(-w|x|^{2})\big|\leq|w-w_{c}|C=\frac{|w|^{2}C}{c^{2}}\quad\forall x\in B(0,R)

is valid for sufficiently large
c>0
. Similarly, as we chose the complex square root such that
\text{Im}(\sqrt{w})>0
, the holomorphy of the logarithm on the set
\mathbb{C}\setminus(-\infty,0]
yields
\forall x\in B(0,R)\setminus\{0\}
,

\big|\ln(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\ln(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\big|=\big|\ln(-i\sqrt{w_{c}})-\ln(-i\sqrt{w})\big|\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}}.

In combination with (
66
), this yields

\big|\sqrt{w_{c}}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\sqrt{w}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\big|\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}}(|\ln|x||+1)\quad\forall x\in B(0,R)\setminus\{0\}

for all sufficiently large
c>0
. To complete the proof, note that Lemma
2.5
(v) implies that there exist constants
C,\kappa>0
which are independent of
c
such that for all sufficiently large
c>0

(67)

\left|\sqrt{w_{c}}-\sqrt{w}\right||K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)|\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa|x|}\quad\forall|x|>R.

Moreover, by the fundamental theorem of calculus one has for all
|x|>R
and all sufficiently large
c>0

|K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)|\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}K_{1}\left(-i\sqrt{w+t\frac{w^{2}}{c^{2}}}|x|\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa|x|},

where Lemma
2.5
(v) was used.
Combining this with (
67
) leads to

\big|\sqrt{w_{c}}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\sqrt{w}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\big|\leq\frac{C}{c^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa|x|}\quad\forall|x|>R,

which finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

With the help of Lemma
4.2
we can study the convergence of the terms in (
64
). Recall that for
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the operators
SL(w)
,
WL(w)
and
\Phi^{c}(w)
are defined by (
19
), (
21
) and (
60
), respectively.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Then there exists a constant
\kappa>0
depending only on
w
and
\Sigma
such that

(68)

\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}\Phi^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})S_{c}-\begin{pmatrix}SL(w)&-2iWL(w)\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\kappa}{c},

and

(69)

\left\lVert\frac{1}{\sqrt{c}}S_{c}\Phi^{c}(\overline{\widetilde{w}_{c}})^{\ast}-\begin{pmatrix}SL(\overline{w})^{\ast}&0\\
2iWL(\overline{w})^{\ast}&0\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\kappa}{c}.

Proof.

First, putting together the integral representation of
SL(w)
in (
19
), the estimate of
t_{3}
in Lemma
4.2
, and Proposition
2.4
(for
\theta=1/4
) we deduce that

\|SL(w_{c})-SL(w)\|=\mathcal{O}(c^{-2}).

Similarly, using the integral representation of
WL(w)
in (
21
), the estimate of
t_{4}
in Lemma
4.2
, and again Proposition
2.4
(for
\theta=1/4
), we arrive at

\|WL(w_{c})-WL(w)\|=\mathcal{O}(c^{-2}).

Furthermore, the estimates for
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
in Lemma
4.2
yield with Proposition
2.4
(for
\theta=1/4
) that

\left\|\frac{1}{c}SL(w_{c})\right\|=\mathcal{O}(c^{-1})\quad\text{and}\quad\left\|\frac{1}{c}\widetilde{WL}(w_{c})\right\|=\mathcal{O}(c^{-1}).

Now, the estimate in (
68
) follows immediately from (

). By hermitian conjugation, also (

) is true.
∎

The next lemma is devoted to the convergence of
N_{c}(w)
defined by (
65
). Recall that
M(w)
denotes the Weyl function associated with the generalized boundary triple
\{L^{2}(\Sigma;\mathbb{C}^{2}),\Gamma_{0},\Gamma_{1}\}
; cf. Proposition
3.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}
and set
V=\diag(1,-2i)\in\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2}
. Then there exists a constant
\kappa>0
depending only on
w
and
\Sigma
such that for all sufficiently large
c>0

\left\lVert N_{c}(w)-V^{*}M(w)V\right\rVert\leq\frac{\kappa}{c^{2}}.

Proof.

First, taking Proposition
3.1
(iii), Lemma
2.9
, and (
26
) into account, one sees that
M(w)
is a strongly singular boundary integral operator with integral kernel
Q_{1}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})
, where

Q_{1}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)&\frac{i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\frac{\overline{\bf{x}}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\\
\frac{-i\sqrt{w}}{4\pi}\frac{\bf{x}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)&\frac{w}{8\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\end{pmatrix}.

Hence, an explicit calculation yields

V^{*}Q_{1}(x)V=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)&\frac{\sqrt{w}}{2\pi}\frac{\overline{\bf{x}}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\\
\frac{\sqrt{w}}{2\pi}\frac{\bf{x}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)&\frac{w}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|)\end{pmatrix}.

In a similar vein,
N_{c}(w)
is a strongly singular boundary integral operator with kernel
Q_{2}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})
, where

Q_{2}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(1+\frac{w}{c^{2}}\big)K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)&\frac{\sqrt{w_{c}}}{2\pi}\frac{\overline{\bf{x}}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)\\
\frac{\sqrt{w_{c}}}{2\pi}\frac{\bf{x}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)&\frac{w}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore,
N_{c}(w)-V^{*}M(w)V
is an integral operator with kernel
H(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma}):=Q_{2}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})-Q_{1}(x_{\Sigma}-y_{\Sigma})
with entries

\begin{split}H_{11}(x)&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(1+\frac{w}{c^{2}}\big)K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\frac{1}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|),\\
H_{12}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{w_{c}}}{2\pi}\frac{\overline{\bf{x}}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\frac{\sqrt{w}}{2\pi}\frac{\overline{\bf{x}}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|),\\
H_{21}(x)&=\frac{\sqrt{w_{c}}}{2\pi}\frac{\bf{x}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\frac{\sqrt{w}}{2\pi}\frac{\bf{x}}{|x|}K_{1}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|),\\
H_{22}(x)&=\frac{w}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w_{c}}|x|)-\frac{w}{2\pi}K_{0}(-i\sqrt{w}|x|).\end{split}

It follows from the estimates for
t_{2},\,t_{3}
, and
t_{4}
from Lemma
4.2
that there exist constants
\kappa_{3},\kappa_{4}>0
depending only on
w
and
\Sigma
such that for all sufficiently large
c>0
and
i,\,j\in\{1,2\}

|H_{ij}(x)|\leq\frac{\kappa_{3}}{c^{2}}(|x|^{-1/4}+1)\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa_{4}|x|}\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}

is valid. Consequently, Proposition
2.3
implies the claimed operator norm estimate.
∎

Finally, we will need the following elementary observation.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
a,\,b>0
,
\mathscr{X}_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, be Banach spaces and
(A_{n})
and
(B_{n})
stand for sequences of bounded operators from
\mathscr{X}_{2}
to
\mathscr{X}_{3}
and from
\mathscr{X}_{1}
to
\mathscr{X}_{2}
that converge to
A
and
B
in the respective operator norms with the convergence rate
\mathcal{O}(n^{-a})
and
\mathcal{O}(n^{-b})
, respectively. Then

\|A_{n}B_{n}-AB\|_{\mathscr{X}_{1}\to\mathscr{X}_{3}}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-\min\{a,b\}})\quad\text{ as }n\to+\infty.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality, we get

\begin{split}\|A_{n}B_{n}-AB\|&{}_{\mathscr{X}_{1}\to\mathscr{X}_{3}}\\
&\leq\|A_{n}\|_{\mathscr{X}_{2}\to\mathscr{X}_{3}}\|B_{n}-B\|_{\mathscr{X}_{1}\to\mathscr{X}_{2}}+\|A_{n}-A\|_{\mathscr{X}_{2}\to\mathscr{X}_{3}}\|B\|_{\mathscr{X}_{1}\to\mathscr{X}_{2}}.\end{split}

∎

Now, we are prepared to find the non-relativistic limit of

Theorem 4.6
.

Let

,

and

62

.

there exists a constant
\kappa>0
such that for all
c>0
sufficiently large

\left\lVert\left(D_{F_{c},G_{c}}^{c}-w-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\right)^{-1}-(T_{B}-w)^{-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\right\rVert\leq\frac{\kappa}{c}.

Proof.

Because of
[
11
, Lem. 3.1]

[

, Cor. 6.2]
one finds a constant

such that for all sufficiently large

,

(70)

Moreover, using Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5

\begin{split}\big(I+\Pi_{G}S_{c}\mathcal{C}^{c}(\widetilde{w}_{c})&S_{c}\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}=\big(I+\Pi_{G}N_{c}(w)\Pi_{F}^{\ast}\big)^{-1}\\

Substituting this together with (

\begin{split}\lim_{c\to+\infty}&\bigg(D_{F_{c},G_{c}}^{c}-w-\frac{c^{2}}{2}\bigg)^{-1}=\,(-\Delta-w)^{-1}\otimes\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\

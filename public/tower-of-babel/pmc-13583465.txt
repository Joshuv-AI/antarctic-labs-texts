Robotic single-port totally extraperitoneal inguinal hernia repair with excision of a canal of Nuck cyst using the da Vinci SP system: a video vignette
Authors: Chul Seung Lee, Choon Sik Chung, Gwan Cheol Lee, Dong Geun Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The da Vinci single-port (SP) robotic system (Intuitive Surgical, Inc.) provides enhanced dexterity and visualization through a single incision, making it effective for complex groin pathology in women. We report a 46-year-old female with a long history of right inguinal bulge and pain. Preoperative imaging revealed a cystic lesion over 3 cm along the round ligament, closely associated with an indirect hernia sac, consistent with a canal of Nuck cyst. Because complete excision is essential to relieve symptoms and prevent recurrence, robotic SP totally extraperitoneal (TEP) hernia repair with cyst excision was performed. A 10 × 15-cm self-adhesive mesh was deployed using the Hansol-roll method. Operative and console times were 58 and 45 minutes, respectively. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 1 without complications. This case highlights the safety and efficacy of robotic SP TEP repair for simultaneous management of hernia and canal of Nuck cyst.

INTRODUCTION

Inguinal hernia repair is one of the most frequently performed elective surgical procedures worldwide. The total extraperitoneal (TEP) approach is often preferred in high-risk patients because it avoids entering the peritoneal cavity, thereby minimizing the risk of visceral injury [1]. Advances in minimally invasive techniques, such as single-incision laparoscopic repair, have demonstrated potential benefits in reducing postoperative pain, improving cosmetic satisfaction, and operative times comparable to conventional multiport TEP [2]. Current evidence also suggests that fixation with tacks may be associated with increased postoperative discomfort compared with glue or non-fixation strategies, highlighting the importance of optimizing fixation methods [3].

The canal of Nuck is the female homologue of the male processus vaginalis, a peritoneal diverticulum that normally obliterates during fetal development as it accompanies the round ligament through the inguinal canal. Failure of this obliteration can give rise to a canal of Nuck cyst, a rare cystic remnant running along the round ligament that presents clinically as an inguinal or labial swelling. The condition is uncommon and is easily mistaken for, or found coexisting with, an indirect inguinal hernia, since both share the same anatomical pathway and often present with an overlapping clinical picture. This diagnostic overlap is clinically important: an unrecognized or incompletely excised cyst can remain a source of recurrent groin swelling even after an otherwise successful hernia repair, and excision performed alongside hernia repair requires meticulous dissection around the round ligament to avoid injury to adjacent structures. Surgical excision, performed via open or laparoscopic approaches, is considered the definitive treatment [4].

Robotic surgery has emerged as a valuable alternative for hernia repair, offering enhanced visualization and instrument dexterity compared with conventional laparoscopy. In particular, the da Vinci Single-Port (SP) system (Intuitive Surgical, Inc.) enables SP access, thereby reducing invasiveness and improving cosmetic outcomes [5]. Combined management of an inguinal hernia with a coexisting canal of Nuck cyst has previously been reported using a conventional multiport robotic platform but reports using a single-port robotic approach remain scarce. We present a case in which robotic SP TEP repair allowed complete cyst excision and definitive hernia repair through a single incision, illustrating the specific value of the SP platform for this uncommon combination of groin pathology.

CASE

A 46-year-old female presented with a history of right inguinal bulge and pain that persisted for several years. She had no underlying comorbidities. Preoperative ultrasonography revealed a well-defined, fluid-filled cystic lesion measuring more than 3 cm in diameter within the right inguinal canal. The lesion was aligned with the expected course of the round ligament and closely associated with an indirect inguinal hernia sac, without evidence of communication with the peritoneal cavity. These findings were considered consistent with a canal of Nuck cyst coexisting with a right inguinal hernia. Accordingly, combined cyst excision and hernia repair were planned, with final confirmation of the diagnosis to be established by histopathological examination.

The patient underwent a TEP repair using the da Vinci SP robotic system (Supplementary Video 1). Prior to robotic docking, a small infraumbilical incision was made and the preperitoneal working space was created. Once an adequate working space had been confirmed, the extraperitoneal cavity was insufflated with CO2 through a single-incision umbilical port and multiple robot-arm dockings were completed. A camera was inserted at the bottom, a toothed retractor at the top (arm 2), fenestrated bipolar forceps on the right (arm 3), and Cadiere forceps on the left (arm 1) of the SP trocar (Fig. 1). Dissection proceeded systematically. The space of Retzius was developed first to expose Cooper’s ligament and the pubic bone, followed by lateral dissection to expose the inferior epigastric vessels and the myopectineal orifice. The round ligament was then carefully isolated, and the canal of Nuck cyst—identified as a smooth-walled cystic structure running parallel to the round ligament—was dissected circumferentially with the fenestrated bipolar forceps and Cadiere forceps. Maintaining gentle counter-traction while dissecting close to the cyst wall under high magnification allowed a bloodless plane to be developed; addressing the cyst before completing hernia sac reduction proved helpful, as the distended cyst otherwise limited visualization of the medial and lateral crura during sac dissection. The cyst was excised in its entirety and extracted. Hemostasis was secured before addressing the hernia defect.

The hernia sac, identified adjacent to the excised cyst, was mobilized and reduced. The preperitoneal space was expanded to expose the entire myopectineal orifice. A self-adhesive mesh measuring 10 × 15 cm was introduced through the SP. Deployment was performed using the Hansol-roll folding method [6], which allowed controlled unrolling and precise positioning. The mesh lay flat, fully covering the inguinal floor without the need for tacks or sutures. Operative and console times were 58 and 45 minutes, respectively.

Postoperative recovery was uneventful. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 1 without pain or complications (Fig. 2).

DISCUSSION

This case demonstrates the feasibility of robotic SP TEP repair for the combined management of an inguinal hernia and a coexisting canal of Nuck cyst, an uncommon but clinically important cause of groin swelling in women. Preoperative distinction of a canal of Nuck cyst from an inguinal hernia, hydrocele, or other adnexal pathology can be difficult on clinical examination alone. Ultrasonography remains the first-line imaging modality, typically demonstrating an anechoic or hypoechoic, well-circumscribed tubular or ovoid structure along the course of the round ligament without a bowel wall signature. In our patient, these imaging characteristics allowed a preoperative diagnosis of canal of Nuck cyst, which directly informed our operative planning for combined excision and hernia repair.

Complete surgical excision is the definitive treatment for a canal of Nuck cyst, both to relieve symptoms and to exclude rare malignant or borderline pathology within the cyst wall. Because the cyst runs immediately adjacent to, and can be difficult to distinguish from, the hernia sac itself, incomplete excision or misidentification risks either recurrent swelling or inadvertent injury to the round ligament. Prior reports of combined hernia and canal of Nuck cyst management have used conventional multiport robotic platforms [7], whereas our case demonstrates that the improved dexterity and visualization of the da Vinci SP system allow this same combined dissection to be performed safely through a single incision, without evidence that the additional cyst excision meaningfully prolonged operative time.

The SP approach minimized invasiveness, while use of a self-adhesive mesh eliminated the need for fixation devices, thereby reducing postoperative pain. The Hansol-roll method was particularly valuable in mesh deployment, ensuring smooth unrolling and accurate positioning. This technique has been described as an effective way to handle self-gripping meshes in confined spaces, and its application in robotic SP surgery further enhances precision and reproducibility.

This report has the inherent limitations of a single case. Conclusions regarding the generalizability, reproducibility, and comparative safety of this combined approach cannot be drawn from one patient, and larger case series with longer follow-up are needed to validate the short-term outcomes observed here and to define the optimal management strategy for this rare combination of pathology.

In conclusion, robotic SP TEP repair enables safe, effective, single-incision combined management of an inguinal hernia and a coexisting canal of Nuck cyst. Complete cyst excision integrated into the TEP dissection, together with Hansol-roll mesh deployment, achieved excellent short-term outcomes in this case and illustrates the value of the robotic SP platform for this uncommon but clinically important groin pathology in women.

SUPPLEMENTARY INFORMATION: 
    Title of Collection: Grantee Reporting Requirements for the U.S. 
National Science Foundation Regional Innovation Engines (NSF Engines) 
Program.
    OMB Control Number: 3145-0284.
    Expiration Date of Approval: December 31, 2026.
    Type of Request: Request for approval to renew an information 
collection.
    Proposed Project: The U.S. National Science Foundation Regional 
Innovation Engines (Engines) program, led by NSF's Directorate for 
Technology, Innovation and Partnerships (TIP), is NSF's flagship 
investment in place-based innovation. Established pursuant to the CHIPS 
and Science Act of 2022, the program invests in regions across the 
United States to bring together the research, talent, partnerships, and 
resources needed to turn advances in science and technology into 
economic and societal benefits. Each NSF Engine serves a defined 
geographic Region of Service (RoS), the area intended to benefit from 
the NSF Engine's activities and investments, and is organized around 
technologies and challenges important to that region and the Nation.
    Specifically, the program seeks to:
     Advance key technology areas critical to U.S. 
competitiveness;
     Address pressing national and societal challenges;
     Cultivate partnerships across industry, academia, 
government, nonprofit organizations, civil society and communities of 
practice;
     Promote economic growth and job creation; and
     Develop the regional workforce and talent needed to 
sustain innovation.
    Each NSF Engine carries out these objectives through three core 
functions: use-inspired research and development, translation of 
innovation results to society, and workforce development. Together, 
these activities are intended to strengthen a RoS's capacity to develop 
and commercialize new technologies, create and grow businesses, prepare 
workers for new opportunities, and attract additional investment. Over 
time, NSF Engines are expected to develop into sustainable regional 
innovation ecosystems that contribute to economic growth and U.S. 
competitiveness.
    NSF Engines are substantial, long-term investments. Each NSF Engine 
may receive NSF funding for up to 10 years, based on its performance 
and progress toward established goals and milestones. As NSF Engines 
develop, their activities, partnerships, and sources of support are 
expected to grow and evolve. Regular reporting on a defined cadence 
helps NSF Engines teams track performance, identify and address issues, 
and adjust their strategies as needed. At the same time, it allows NSF 
to assess progress across the portfolio, identify risks and emerging 
challenges, and make informed program management and funding decisions.
    Project Reporting Requirements: NSF is seeking renewed approval 
from the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) for the revised Grantee 
Reporting Requirements for NSF Engines Program information collection. 
The collection consists of four reporting components: (1) Quarterly 
Reports; (2) the Engine Report Template; (3) the Strategic and 
Implementation Plan; and (4) the Strategic Growth Plan. Each serves a 
distinct purpose; together, they provide NSF with information to 
monitor the implementation, performance, and development of each NSF 
Engine over the life of the award and the program holistically.
    Quarterly Reports will be required in the first quarter (Q1) and 
third quarter (Q3) of the first year. These reports provide timely 
information on NSF Engine operations, implementation, performance, and 
emerging issues. The two Quarterly Reports cover the first year and 
include the following required narrative sections:
    1. Personnel, Governance and Operations;
    2. Programmatic Activities; and
    3. Partners and New Collaborations.
    The Engines Report Template (ERT) will be submitted semiannually 
throughout the ten (10) years of the award in lieu of the NSF Research 
Performance Progress Report (RPPR). The ERT consists of two components: 
a tabular component for quantitative information, and a narrative 
component for qualitative information. The tabular component collects 
standardized and structured information across 10 areas:
    1. Personnel;
    2. Programmatic Activities;
    3. Research, Development and Translation (RD&T) Outputs;
    4. Workforce Development (WFD) Outputs;
    5. Innovation Ecosystem Building Actions;
    6. Innovation Ecosystem Building Infrastructure;
    7. Partners;
    8. Non-Partner Commitments and Resources;
    9. Risk and Opportunity Register; and
    10. Awardee Specified Metrics.
    The narrative component provides context for the information 
reported in these areas, including implementation progress, challenges, 
and outcomes. Award recipients will also describe significant changes 
since the previous reporting period and, as applicable, explain 
deviations from previously established plans. Together, the 
quantitative and qualitative components allow NSF to track measurable 
progress while understanding the circumstances affecting an individual 
NSF Engine's performance.
    The Strategic and Implementation Plan (SIP) is a forward-looking 
strategy for the Emergent phase of the award (project years three 
through five). Each NSF Engine starts development of the SIP in year 
one and completes it by the end of year two. The SIP is used by NSF to 
evaluate the NSF Engine for continued support beyond year two. The SIP 
sections include the following:
    (1) Vision, Mission, Goals and Measurable Metrics;
    (2) Research and Development;
    (3) Translation, including intellectual property strategy;
    (4) Workforce Development;
    (5) Economic Development;
    (6) Governance and Management;
    (7) Partnerships; and

[[Page 66370]]

    (8) Project Evaluation.
    The Strategic Growth Plan (SGP) builds upon the SIP and shifts the 
focus from early implementation to the NSF Engine's longer-term growth 
and sustainability. Beginning in year four, NSF Engines develop a 
forward-looking strategy for the Growth phase of the award, with 
additional planning refinement, and finalization occurring in year 
five. The SGP addresses:
    (1) A strategy for establishing the RoS as a national leader in the 
NSF Engine's technology area(s);
    (2) Strategies for attracting, creating, and supporting businesses 
in the region;
    (3) Goals, strategies, milestones, and measures of progress for 
research and development, translation, workforce and economic 
development;
    (4) A sustainability plan describing how the RoS will maintain and 
increase economic growth beyond NSF funding; and
    (5) Strategies for sustaining NSF Engine activities, programs, and 
other efforts important to continued research, translation, workforce 
development, and economic growth within the RoS.
    Use of the Information: NSF uses the information from these four 
types of reports to monitor implementation and performance; assess 
progress toward established goals, milestones, and outcomes; identify 
risks and areas requiring attention; ensure compliance with award terms 
and conditions; and support program management, evaluation, and 
reporting to NSF leadership, the Administration, and Congress.
    The reporting requirements also provide a consistent record of how 
each NSF Engine develops over time. The Quarterly Reports and ERT 
capture ramp-up and ongoing implementation and performance to determine 
whether NSF Engines are advancing their proposed strategies and 
developing partnerships, capabilities, and resources needed to sustain 
their regional innovation ecosystems over time. The SIP and SGP provide 
details to NSF on each NSF Engine's forward-looking plans in order to 
make decisions about continued funding for the NSF Engine during phase 
transitions and establish the metrics that will be reported against in 
future ERTs.

    Because the reporting requirements vary over the lifetime of an NSF 
Engine award, the burden estimates in Table 1 do not apply 
simultaneously to every NSF Engine. For example, a newly awarded NSF 
Engine would incur an estimated 80-160 hours of reporting burden in 
Year 1 for ERT Reports, Quarterly Reports, and SIP; 70-140 hours in 
Year 2 for ERT Reports and SIP; 25-50 hours in Year 3 for ERT Reports 
and SGP. This yields an estimated three-year total burden of 175-350 
hours per each newly awarded Engine.

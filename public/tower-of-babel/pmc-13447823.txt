Three-dimensional geological body numerical model–control information model mapping: a broken-chain correction method
Authors: Ziyu Tao, Zhen Liu, Cuiying Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThree-dimensional (3D) geological numerical models and control information models play important roles in numerical analysis, engineering information organization, and visual representation, and are therefore essential in intelligent engineering and geological hazard assessment. However, during the mapping of numerical results from numerical models to control information models, differences in data structures, topological organization, attribute representation rules, and storage formats often lead to “broken chain” problems, such as discontinuous attribute information, missing structural relationships, and abnormal geometric representation, thereby affecting the accurate expression and effective application of numerical results. To address this issue, this study proposes a rule-driven broken-chain correction method for mapping between 3D geological numerical models and control information models. Focusing on the one-way transfer of numerical simulation software results to a front-end control information model, the proposed method identifies data variations at both the attribute and structural levels during the mapping process, and combines rule-based correction with prior-mesh topology reconstruction to achieve reliable representation of numerical results in the target environment. The reconstructed nodes, patches, and attribute information are further organized into structured data that support loading, visualization, and information management. Using a pile-foundation engineering case in Southwest China, the proposed method was validated based on an numerical model and a front-end control information model implemented with JSON and Three.js/WebGL. The results show that the method can effectively restore model topology, suppress local topological disorder and self-intersection, and preserve the key numerical characteristics of nodal attributes during cross-platform mapping. Further reliability analysis indicates that, under attribute datasets of different sizes, the proposed method exhibits good topology correction performance and stable attribute mapping while satisfying the requirements of visualization. This study provides a feasible technical pathway for the cross-platform transfer of 3D geological numerical results to control information models, and also offers methodological support for the organization, visualization, and collaborative application of geotechnical engineering information in multi-software environments.Keywords: Three-dimensional geological body, Numerical model–control information model mapping, Broken-chain correction, Data structures, VisualizationSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

With the rapid advancement of intelligent engineering and digital twin technologies, three-dimensional (3D) geological numerical models and control information models have become important carriers of engineering information in engineering design, geological hazard assessment, and intelligent decision-making1–3. A 3D geological numerical model is typically developed based on finite difference or finite element methods, and can effectively characterize the mechanical behavior and response processes of complex geological structures under specific working conditions. Such models have been widely applied in foundation stability analysis, underground structure design, and geological hazard risk assessment4–7. In contrast, a control information model is different not only from a numerical model centered on computational analysis, but also from a BIM model primarily oriented toward engineering process management. Instead, it places greater emphasis on safety control, risk management, status representation, and information integration during engineering implementation, thereby supporting engineering safety management and dynamic decision-making8–11. Therefore, effective mapping between 3D geological numerical models and control information models is of considerable significance for integrating numerical simulation results into safety-control processes in a timely manner, improving the efficiency of engineering risk assessment and control, and supporting intelligent decision-making. It should be noted that the problem addressed in this study is not general bidirectional model integration, but rather a one-way mapping scenario in which numerical simulation results are transferred from a numerical model to a front-end control information model, specifically transforming nodal attributes computed by numerical simulation software into data that can be organized, represented, and managed in a browser-based environment.

To address the “broken chain” issue in mapping 3D geological body numerical simulation results to control information models, researchers have proposed a variety of correction methods. At present, two mainstream research directions have emerged: data export–based correction mapping and direct object mapping. In data export–based correction mapping, researchers usually first export 3D geological numerical calculation results into intermediate data formats12–14(e.g., CSV, TXT, IFC). These are then subjected to a series of post-processing operations such as data cleaning, structural correction, and geometric simplification to standardize and lightweight the numerical calculation data. Finally, the corrected data are re-mapped or entity-matched within the control information model platform15,16. This method allows fine control over data quality and structure and is especially suitable for correcting local errors in complex geological models. However, it involves multiple rounds of manual intervention and repeated modeling, leading to low overall efficiency and potential information inconsistency during model mapping17. In direct object mapping, researchers attempt to establish explicit mapping rules between 3D geological numerical models and control information models, directly converting 3D geological numerical results into object structures in the control information model without intermediate formats. Consistency adjustment and matching are implemented at the data level1,18,19. Although this method avoids model reconstruction, inherent differences between the two model types in underlying data representation, semantic architecture, and unit topology—such as the numerical model’s organization around computing units and node matrices versus the control information model’s use of parameterized objects and attribute tables—often pose challenges in format compatibility and semantic consistency20–22. As a result, mapped control information models may suffer from compromised data integrity and reliability, limiting practical efficiency. In recent years, to improve the automation and intelligence of 3D geological model mapping, some researchers have begun exploring AI (Artificial Intelligence)-assisted mapping mechanisms. These include using convolutional neural networks to identify feature structures in numerical results23,24 or applying graph neural networks to establish association rules between cross-model entities25, aiming at more efficient and accurate semantic conversion. However, AI model training requires large amounts of labeled data26,27, and high-quality numerical simulation data are often scarce28,29. Moreover, for complex data structures and nonlinear relationships, both rule-based and AI-based methods may still require manual correction to achieve satisfactory conversion.

Against this background, this study focuses on the “broken chain” problem that arises during the mapping of 3D geological numerical model results to control information models. In this paper, the term “broken chain” refers to the phenomenon in which attribute information or structural relationships become discontinuous during the transfer, reorganization, and representation of numerical results owing to differences in data structures, encoding rules, precision requirements, and topological representation between heterogeneous platforms. To address this problem, this study proposes a rule-driven broken-chain correction method for mapping between 3D geological numerical models and control information models. By identifying data variation types that occur during the mapping process, and combining rule-based correction with prior-mesh topology reconstruction, the proposed method restores structural integrity and attribute consistency of numerical results in the target environment, and further organizes them into structured data suitable for browser-based visualization and information management. Using a pile-foundation engineering case from Southwest China as an example, the proposed method was validated based on an numerical model and a front-end control information model. The results show that the method can effectively restore model topology, suppress local topological disorder and self-intersection, and preserve the key numerical characteristics of nodal attributes such as displacement and stress during cross-platform mapping. This study provides a feasible technical pathway for the cross-platform transfer of 3D geological numerical results to control information models, and offers methodological support for the organization, visualization, and collaborative application of geotechnical engineering information in multi-software environments.

Research content and methodology

This study focuses on the one-way model mapping from 3D geological numerical models to front-end control information models. Mapping data mainly comes from numerical model outputs (node IDs, coordinates, displacement, stress, etc.) and preprocessed meshes (element connectivity and topological relationships). Due to differences in data structure, topology, attribute rules, and storage format, direct reuse is infeasible; thus extraction, matching, topology reconstruction, and reorganization are required.

The term broken chain describes discontinuities in attributes or structures during transfer and reorganization. Six data/structural variations are identified: error, duplication, and loss in both data and structure. A rule-based broken-chain correction framework is proposed to classify variations and apply targeted strategies, ensuring data consistency and structural integrity. A structured mapping workflow is then established to enable compatible representation, stable browser loading, visualization, and interoperability.

Notably, the framework is not restricted by geological complexity (strata, faults, folds, etc.), as long as geological structures are properly meshed and topological relationships are preserved during export. Faults and folds are represented via mesh geometry and connectivity, so they do not form distinct mapping types, and the overall workflow remains universally applicable (Fig. 1).

Categories of data variation

In the one-way mapping scenario discussed in this study, in which a 3D geological numerical model is transferred to a control information model, the term “broken chain” does not refer to fluctuations in the numerical results themselves or to solution errors in the numerical calculation. Rather, it refers to discontinuities in attribute information or structural relationships that arise during the transfer, reorganization, and representation of numerical model results in the control information model due to differences in data structures, encoding rules, precision requirements, and topological representation. According to the level at which these discontinuities occur and their specific manifestations, the data variations considered in this study are classified into six types: data error, data duplication, data loss, structural error, structural duplication, and structural loss. Among them, data error, data duplication, and data loss primarily affect the attribute-information level and influence the accuracy, completeness, and consistency of numerical results, whereas structural error, structural duplication, and structural loss mainly affect the geometric and topological levels, influencing the connectivity between model elements, the organization of surfaces, and the overall structural representation of the model. These data variations may further lead to attribute anomalies, topological disorder, surface distortion, and visualization errors in the control information model. Therefore, it is necessary to classify and identify them systematically in order to provide a basis for subsequent rule-based correction and topological reconstruction.

Data error

In 3D geological modeling, data error is the primary type of mapping broken chain. It occurs when an index value deviates from its true value or expected range. Such errors arise from a variety of factors, including computational error accumulation in numerical software, manual data entry mistakes, or accuracy loss during data type conversion. Within a control information model for 3D geological bodies, data errors can induce deviations in geometry shape, position, or attributes. This not only compromises the accuracy and reliability of visualization results but may also lead to erroneous analysis conclusions. Consequently, identifying errors in 3D geological data is crucial, primarily involving the detection of outliers in a dataset or data points falling outside an expected range. Data duplication

Data duplication, the second type of mapping chain breaking in 3D geological modeling, refers to the occurrence of identical or logically equivalent geological data records within a dataset. In numerical models, data duplication may result from overlapping adjacent model regions or the improper merging of different datasets. This redundancy not only increases data storage demands but also reduces the efficiency of data querying and analysis, impairing the overall performance of the 3D geological model. In a control information model, data duplication may cause conflicts on the geometric surfaces of 3D geological bodies. For instance, duplicate nodes or patches may cause the same area to be rendered multiple times, wasting resources and creating visual defects that degrade output quality. Data loss

Data loss, the third type of mapping chain breaking in 3D geological modeling, denotes the absence of some essential fields or records in a geological dataset, resulting in incomplete information. This may be caused by an overly sparse grid in key areas in the numerical model, omissions during data acquisition, or attribute loss during data cleaning. Insufficient data can cause gaps or fractures in the geometric representation of 3D geological bodies, thereby compromising the accurate visualization and interactive operation of the model, and subsequently impacting the integrity of the data mapping results of the control information model. Structural error

Structural error, the fourth type of mapping chain breaking in 3D geological modeling, refers to errors in the data describing the topological structure of a 3D geological body in control information models. These errors usually arise from mapping mistakes during data format conversion or improper implementation of program logic. Structural errors can cause distortion in the geometric shape of 3D geological bodies and the confusion of topological relationships, severely impacting the realism and availability of the model.

Rule-based correction for data variation in control information models

Section “Categories of data variation” describes six types of data variation that may occur during the mapping of data from a numerical model to a control information model: data duplication, data error, data loss, structural duplication, structural error, and structural loss. This section introduces a key data quality control method—rule-based data correction. This method evaluates data matching, integrity, and accuracy by defining a series of rules, thereby enabling the effective detection and correction of data variations.

Data matching correction

The core challenge in mapping data from a 3D geological body numerical model to a control information model lies in the difference in coding rules and accuracy requirements between the models, which is a primary cause of mapping “disconnects”. To handle large-scale and high-precision data, numerical models usually employ scientific notation or double-precision floating-point numbers to represent large-scale values and ensure computational accuracy. In contrast, control information models may use relatively low-precision floating-point numbers or even fixed-point numbers to optimize mapping efficiency. This discrepancy in coding rules can lead to a loss of accuracy during data mapping, compromising the reliability of the results. Data matching correction aims to bridge this gap, ensuring that the data received by the control information model not only conforms to its coding specifications but also retains as much key information from the numerical results as possible. Data integrity correction

Data integrity correction is the key step for restoring usable topology when numerical results are mapped to the control information model. In the workflow adopted in this study, the results exported from numerical simulation software mainly contain nodal identifiers and nodal attribute values, while the browser-based target model requires stable surface connectivity and ordered patch relationships for visualization. Therefore, when the exported result data are imported directly, the control information model can assign attributes to nodes but cannot reliably infer the correct surface topology, which may lead to self-intersection, missing patches, and disordered surface reconstruction. To address this problem, this study introduces a prior geological mesh with complete topology, generated in the pre-processing stage, as the topological constraint for reconstruction. The correction process consists of matching numerical nodes to mesh vertices, transferring nodal attributes to the constrained mesh, and rebuilding the surface structure according to the connectivity already defined in the prior mesh. The applicability of this process is determined primarily by whether the target geological structure has been properly discretized and preserved in the prior mesh, rather than by the specific morphological type of the geological body itself. Therefore, for faulted, folded, or other structurally more complex geological models, the same mapping logic remains applicable as long as the relevant structural surfaces and topological relationships have already been represented in the mesh model used in the numerical-analysis workflow.

Data accuracy correction

When mapping numerical data of 3D geological bodies to a control information model, significant differences in unit systems, coordinate systems, and data storage formats may lead to serious errors or distortions in data, resulting in a “broken chain”. Data accuracy correction is therefore necessary to ensure the numerical data of 3D geological bodies are presented correctly and consistently within the control information model. The core objective of data accuracy correction is to correct inconsistencies in units and coordinate directions, and address data confusion, ultimately achieving full consistency of the numerical data with the requirements of the control information model, accurate mapping, and seamless connection of data.

Data validation for control information models

After rule-based correction and topology reconstruction, the results of the 3D geological numerical model have been reorganized into a form intended for loading in the control information model. However, successful correction does not automatically guarantee that the mapped results can be correctly interpreted, rendered, and managed in the target environment. Therefore, a dedicated validation process is required to ensure the correctness of data organization, the consistency of structural relationships, and the reliability of numerical attribute representation in the control information model.

In the mapping scenario addressed in this study, data validation is mainly performed on the structured JSON files generated after result reorganization. The validation process focuses on three aspects. First, file-structure validation is carried out to confirm that the reorganized data conform to the required JSON structure and contain the essential fields needed for front-end loading, including geometric information, patch index relationships, and nodal attributes. This step is intended to prevent loading failure or parsing errors caused by incomplete or improperly organized data. Second, content validation is performed to check whether the numerical attributes and structural data are stored in a consistent and usable manner. For example, nodal coordinates and nodal attributes should be stored as valid numerical values, while patch indices should correctly correspond to the reconstructed topological relationships. This step helps avoid mapping anomalies caused by missing fields, type mismatches, or incorrect index associations. In addition to file-structure and content validation, visual-effect validation is also required after the data are loaded into the browser-based control information model. This step is used to verify whether the reconstructed model can be correctly displayed in the target environment, and whether the mapped numerical results are visually consistent with the expected geometric and topological structure. Particular attention is paid to surface continuity, local topology disorder, self-intersection, and the correctness of attribute rendering. In this study, the visualized model after mapping is compared with the expected structural organization derived from the prior mesh and the numerical results exported from numerical simulation software, so as to evaluate whether the final expression in the control information model is acceptable.

Overall, the validation strategy adopted in this study combines structured file checking, data-content consistency checking, and front-end visualization verification. This workflow-oriented validation process helps ensure not only that the reorganized results can be successfully loaded into the control information model, but also that the mapped model preserves the intended structural integrity and numerical attribute expression, thereby providing a reliable basis for subsequent visualization, interaction, and information management.

Sharing data results for control information models

After broken-chain correction and data validation have been completed for the 3D geological numerical model results in the control information model, the organization and sharing of the reconstructed results must also be considered. In the workflow addressed in this study, the purpose of result sharing is not general geometric exchange across arbitrary software platforms, but the stable transfer, loading, visualization, and management of numerical results in a browser-based control information model.

To satisfy this requirement, the reconstructed model data are organized into structured JSON files after topology reconstruction and attribute remapping. These files contain the geometric information required for rendering, the patch index relationships needed for surface organization, and the nodal attributes used for numerical result visualization. Compared with general-purpose geometry exchange formats, the structured JSON files adopted in this study are better suited to the browser-based target environment, because they can simultaneously preserve the correspondence between geometry, topology, and numerical attributes while also supporting efficient parsing, dynamic loading, and front-end interaction. In addition to format organization, result sharing in the present study also requires consistency in attribute semantics and structural relationships. Therefore, during data reorganization, nodal attributes, patch indices, and their corresponding geometric structures are stored in an explicitly associated manner, so that the target control information model can correctly interpret both the numerical meaning and the geometric organization of the mapped results. This organization method reduces ambiguity during data loading and improves the reliability of subsequent visualization, attribute query, and information management.

Overall, the result-sharing strategy adopted in this study is closely tied to the one-way mapping scenario from numerical simulation software numerical results to a browser-based control information model. Its core objective is to ensure that the reconstructed results can be stably represented, efficiently loaded, and reliably shared in the target environment. Under this framework, the structured organization of geometry, topology, and attribute data provides support for subsequent visualization analysis, interaction, and engineering information coordination.

Results and discussion

Case overview

This case study is based on an engineering geological project in Southwest China. The study area is characterized by pronounced marginal weathering, active surface geomorphic erosion, and relatively complex geological conditions. The terrain is predominantly hilly, and the strata are mainly composed of slightly weathered rock formations. The borehole data indicate that the study area mainly comprises silty clay, breccia, gravel, rounded gravel, pebbles, coarse sand, slightly weathered conglomerate, and slightly weathered dolomite. These borehole data provide the fundamental constraints for constructing the three-dimensional stratigraphic model and the necessary geological basis for subsequent numerical model development and multi-software coupled analysis. On this basis, numerical simulation of foundation response was further carried out, and displacement, stress, and other numerical results obtained from the numerical simulation software were taken as the main objects for subsequent mapping to the control information model and engineering-oriented visualization. In the present study, the case data used for model construction mainly included borehole identifiers, planar coordinates, collar elevations, lithologic descriptions, and layer-bottom elevations. These borehole records were first used to constrain the stratigraphic framework of the geological body. On this basis, the stratigraphic entities were discretized into a numerical mesh during the pre-processing stage, and the generated mesh file preserved both the node coordinates and element connectivity required for subsequent numerical simulation. After parameter assignment and numerical calculation, the exported result file mainly contained node identifiers, nodal coordinates, and nodal response values such as displacement and stress. During cross-platform mapping, these numerical outputs were reorganized together with the prior mesh topology into structured JSON data containing vertices, patch indices, and nodal attributes, thereby forming the direct data basis for the browser-based control information model. Analysis of results

Mapping process from 3D geological body numerical models to control information models

Most 3D geological body numerical software does not natively support direct data mapping to a control information model. Therefore, it is necessary to develop a rule-based interface algorithm to enable reliable transfer of numerical-model results to the control information model and to support the visualization and management of geological environment information. In this study, FLAC3D was selected as the numerical simulation software because of its widespread application in geotechnical engineering and its capability to perform three-dimensional numerical simulations under complex geological conditions. Meanwhile, a browser-based control information model implemented with JSON data structures and Three.js/WebGL was adopted as the target environment for geometric organization, attribute representation, visualization, and information management.

During the pre-processing stage, a pile-foundation and ground model was established using mesh-generation software and its associated plug-ins, and then exported in the f3grid format readable by numerical simulation software. Subsequently, according to the engineering geological characteristics of different strata, the physical and mechanical parameters of each rock and soil layer were assigned to the corresponding stratigraphic entities and mesh elements. Table 2 presents the representative physical and mechanical parameters of the main strata adopted in the present numerical simulation. Owing to the need for parameter grouping and simplification during numerical modeling, some stratigraphic units were characterized using grouped representative parameters. On this basis, the boundary conditions and external loads of the model were defined in accordance with the actual engineering conditions: displacement constraints were applied at the bottom and lateral boundaries, while corresponding loads were imposed at the pile head to simulate the actual loading state. Numerical calculation was then carried out using the numerical simulation software. The numerical model was mainly used to obtain nodal responses of the pile-foundation system under the given working conditions, including displacement and stress, which were subsequently taken as the main objects for cross-platform mapping.

It should be noted that the result data exported from the numerical simulation software cannot be directly used in the control information model. The exported results mainly include nodal identifiers, nodal spatial coordinates, and attribute values such as displacement and stress, rather than a complete geometric–topological representation that can be directly rendered by the front-end control information model. Therefore, these results must be reorganized before being imported into the browser-based control information model. In the target environment, the final rendered model is constructed by combining the prior mesh topology provided by the pre-processing model with the nodal attribute results exported from the numerical simulation software. The reorganized data are stored in structured JSON files, including geometric information, patch indices, and nodal attributes, and are further loaded into the web-based visualization framework for rendering. More specifically, the structured JSON files generated in this study contained at least three categories of information: (1) vertex coordinate arrays used for geometric rendering; (2) patch or face index arrays used for topological organization of the model surface; and (3) nodal attribute arrays used for visual representation of numerical results such as displacement and stress. This explicit data organization is necessary because the browser-side control information model cannot directly infer valid topology from numerical result points alone. Instead, the target model must receive geometry, topology, and attributes in an explicitly associated form so that model loading, rendering, and information querying can be stably performed. In the front-end control information model, displacement, stress, and related variables are expressed on the model surface through color mapping. To ensure comparable visualization effects for variables with different dimensions and value ranges, the attribute values are normalized to the interval [0, 1] before color mapping. This treatment removes the influence of differences in physical scale and numerical range, ensuring that subsequent color mapping is performed on a consistent basis and thereby avoiding color deviation or visual distortion caused by differences in data range, the whole process is shown in Fig. 4.

Broken chain in 3D geological body numerical model-control information model mapping and its solution

During the mapping of numerical results generated by the numerical simulation software to the front-end control information model for visualization, obvious topological disorder was observed in the rendered results. Specifically, node connectivity became disordered and local topological relationships were confused, resulting in incorrect structural representation of the model, as shown in Fig. 5d. Based on the data variation types identified in Section “Categories of data variation” and the rule-based data correction method proposed in Section “Rule-based correction for data variation in control information models”, this problem can be attributed mainly to structural loss during the mapping process of the 3D geological numerical simulation model, which is also a typical manifestation of the “broken chain” phenomenon in cross-platform model transfer.

Overall process of cross-software mapping for the 3D geological model. (a) 3D geological model; (b) Mesh model; (c) Numerical simulation model; (d) Web front-end rendered model;.

It should be noted that this structural loss does not originate from errors in the numerical computation itself, but rather occurs during the conversion and organization of result data generated by the numerical simulation software for the front-end control information model. The numerical results exported from the numerical simulation software mainly contain node identifiers, nodal spatial coordinates, and corresponding attribute values, such as displacement and stress, but do not directly provide surface topology that can be directly recognized and utilized by the control information model. Since the front-end control information model requires patch connectivity and index structures organized in a specific order to complete model rendering, when such data are imported into the control information model, the system can assign physical attributes to the corresponding nodes, but cannot accurately determine which nodes constitute each model element or the order in which they are connected. As a result, the mesh topology cannot be correctly reconstructed, ultimately leading to mapping errors. To solve this problem and reconstruct the correct topological relationships, this study adopts a rule-based interface algorithm to extract mesh node data and their associated attribute information from the 3D geological numerical simulation model and map them into the control information model. Although the exported result attributes themselves do not contain surface topology that can be directly used by the front-end control information model, the mesh file generated during pre-processing and used for numerical simulation still preserves element connectivity and can therefore serve as the basis for topology reconstruction. The proposed method first extracts the indices and spatial coordinates of all nodes, together with the node index list corresponding to each element, from the f3grid file of the numerical model. It then establishes the mapping relationship between the result nodes and the nodes of the prior mesh model generated during pre-processing. Finally, the element nodes are reordered according to a specific right-hand rule to reconstruct patch connectivity. Let the node connectivity of the -th element in the mesh model be denoted as , where represents the index of the -th node constituting that element. Through the mapping function , the vertex indices in the mesh model can be transformed into node indices in the numerical result data, thereby restoring element connectivity. Throughout the node-index reconstruction process, the right-hand rule must be strictly followed to ensure consistent element normal directions and to avoid geometric distortion caused by incorrect node ordering. In the present implementation, the vertex sequence of each reconstructed patch is defined counterclockwise when viewed along the outward surface normal of the patch, so that the normal direction obtained from the cross product of consecutive edges is consistent with the target rendering convention. This ordering rule provides an explicit operational basis for patch reconstruction and avoids ambiguity in the interpretation of the right-hand rule during front-end rendering. The reconstructed node arrays, patch index arrays, and related attribute information are further organized into structured JSON files, which can then be directly loaded and rendered in the control information model. Discussion

Reliability analysis and applicability conditions of chain-breaking correction for model mapping

During the mapping of numerical simulation results to a control information model, differences in data structures, precision requirements, storage formats, and encoding specifications may lead to data loss or variation during transmission and conversion, thereby affecting the accurate representation and effective application of geological bodies in the control information model. The data-mapping rule correction method proposed in this study is intended to address this “broken chain” problem, so that the data received by the control information model not only satisfy its own encoding and visualization requirements, but also preserve the key information contained in the numerical simulation results to the greatest extent possible, thereby improving the reliability of 3D geological model mapping. The reliability of the proposed method can be evaluated from two aspects: the precision preservation capability of attribute data and the degree of restoration of model topology integrity.

First, from the perspective of restoring model topology integrity, the effectiveness of topology correction is an important basis for evaluating the reliability of the broken-chain correction method in model mapping. In the absence of stable topological connectivity that can be directly used by the control information model, surface reconstruction in the control information model is prone to problems such as self-intersection of triangular patches, local structural disorder, and poor surface continuity if topology-constrained correction is not applied. To verify the effectiveness of the proposed method in topology correction, a comparative analysis of the model visualization results before and after correction was carried out. The results show that, after introducing prior mesh topology constraints and remapping nodal attributes, surface connectivity in the control information model was effectively restored, surface continuity was significantly improved, and topological disorder was markedly suppressed. The correction effect is shown in Fig. 8. This indicates that the proposed method can effectively establish a reasonable association between nodal attribute information and geometric structure, restore structural integrity and topological consistency in the control information model, and thus provide a reliable geometric basis for subsequent attribute mapping, visualization, and result sharing. In addition to qualitative topology comparison, the geometric admissibility of the reconstructed model should also be examined from the perspective of node-based geometric deviation. In the present case, the coordinates of the reconstructed nodes in the control information model are compared with those of the corresponding nodes in the numerical model, and the resulting deviation is evaluated using the acceptable distance defined in Section “Structural duplication”. The comparison shows that the node-to-node geometric deviation remains within the prescribed tolerance range and does not exceed the admissible local influence threshold adopted for the present mapping scenario. This indicates that the topology-constrained reconstruction does not introduce significant additional geometric disturbance during cross-platform mapping. It should be noted that this conclusion refers to the geometric deviation induced by the mapping procedure in the present continuous-stratigraphy case, rather than implying that all forms of geometric distortion are completely absent under arbitrary geological conditions. It should be noted that the validation of topology correction in this study is mainly based on comparative visualization before and after correction, with emphasis on surface continuity, local topology disorder, and the presence of abnormal surface connections. To further improve the explicitness of validation, cross-sectional comparison between the control information model and the numerical model is provided in Fig. 8, so that the structural correspondence and surface continuity after correction can be more directly identified.

Second, from the perspective of attribute-data precision preservation, the degree of numerical precision loss during data conversion is also an important indicator for evaluating the reliability of the proposed method. Because the control information model typically differs from the numerical model in data organization, storage format, and front-end rendering requirements, the high-precision raw attribute data exported from the numerical simulation software usually need to undergo format conversion and precision compression before being mapped to the control information model. Therefore, the mapping process must not only ensure correct structural relationships, but also verify whether the errors introduced before and after attribute conversion remain within an acceptable range. To this end, attribute datasets of different sizes exported from the numerical simulation software were selected for comparative analysis. It should be noted that the dataset size here does not refer to the mesh scale or number of elements in the numerical model itself, but rather to the number of nodal attribute values exported from the numerical simulation software. Specifically, datasets containing 50,000, 150,000, and 300,000 attribute values were selected. Using displacement and other numerical results as examples, the original high-precision data were converted into attribute data suitable for storage and visualization in the control information model after format conversion and precision compression, and the consistency before and after conversion was quantitatively evaluated, as shown in Fig. 9. To assess the precision preservation performance of attribute mapping, the mean absolute error (MAE), mean squared error (MSE), and coefficient of determination (R²) were adopted as reliability evaluation metrics. The results indicate that, as the number of attribute values increases, MAE and MSE show a slight upward trend, while R² decreases slightly. This suggests that, during the conversion of large-scale attribute datasets, precision loss may accumulate to a certain extent, resulting in some deviation between the converted data and the original data. However, from the overall results, even when the number of attribute values reaches 300,000, the increases in MAE and MSE remain relatively small, indicating that the overall precision loss is still controlled within an acceptable range. This demonstrates that the proposed method can effectively preserve the variation patterns and numerical characteristics of the original simulation results. For the dataset containing 50,000 attribute values, the error before and after conversion is close to zero, indicating that under small- and medium-scale data conditions, the precision loss introduced during attribute mapping is almost negligible.

Comparison of data accuracy across different scale datasets.

Taken together, the above analysis shows that the proposed data-mapping rule correction method can, to a certain extent, ensure both the effectiveness of topology correction and the reliability of attribute mapping, while balancing mapping efficiency and data quality under the premise of satisfying the visualization requirements of the control information model. At the same time, it should be noted that the applicability of the proposed method is subject to certain prerequisites. On the one hand, there must be a clear correspondence between the original numerical results and the target control information model, together with a prior mesh topology that can serve as the basis for constrained mapping. On the other hand, the precision requirements of the control information model for attribute data should fall within an acceptable range for visualization and information representation. It should also be emphasized that the validation in this study is mainly based on a one-way mapping scenario from numerical results generated by the numerical simulation software to a control information model. Overall, the proposed method is more suitable for application scenarios centered on model visualization, information integration, and visual analysis. For high-precision applications requiring extremely strict numerical accuracy, further reduction of conversion errors may still require higher attribute retention precision, optimized data compression strategies, or the introduction of error compensation mechanisms. Under the above conditions, the proposed method can provide reliable technical support for the stable mapping of 3D geological numerical model results into control information models.

It should be further emphasized that the current validation in this study is mainly based on a stratigraphic model with relatively continuous geological interfaces and stable topological relationships. However, from the perspective of methodological logic, the proposed framework is not inherently limited to such relatively continuous stratigraphic models. The essential basis of the method lies in the availability of a prior mesh with preserved geometry and topological connectivity. Therefore, if complex geological structures such as faults, folds, or other structural surfaces have already been reasonably represented in the pre-processing mesh and numerical-modeling stage, the subsequent workflow of numerical-result export, node matching, topology-constrained reconstruction, and structured target-side organization remains applicable. In this sense, complex geological structures do not constitute a fundamentally different mapping category, because they are also represented through mesh geometry and connectivity in the numerical-modeling process.

At the same time, it should also be noted that, when the geological model contains topological discontinuities such as faults, slip surfaces, or strongly heterogeneous structural interfaces, node matching based solely on spatial coordinate proximity may still lead to misjudgment, because nodes located on opposite sides of a discontinuity surface may be spatially adjacent or nearly coincident while still belonging to different geological or mechanical domains. Under such conditions, additional topology-aware identifiers, such as regional grouping IDs, fault-block labels, interface semantic markers, or hanging-wall/footwall identifiers, should be incorporated into the mapping rules so that duplicate discrimination and node matching are performed under combined geometric and structural constraints rather than spatial distance alone. Therefore, although the proposed method is logically applicable to more complex geological structures, dedicated case validation for faulted, folded, or otherwise more structurally complex geological models will be further investigated in future work.

Universality of the chain-breaking correction method for model mapping

Although the broken-chain correction method proposed in this study for mapping between 3D geological numerical models and control information models was validated using the mapping of numerical results generated by the numerical simulation software to a front-end control information model as an example, its core processing logic is not limited to a specific software platform and therefore exhibits a certain degree of generalizability. The proposed method is primarily intended to address the “broken chain” problem that arises during the mapping of numerical results to control information models due to differences in data structures, topological organization, and attribute representation rules. Through data variation identification, rule-based correction, and topological reconstruction, the method enables reliable representation of numerical results in the target environment. Therefore, as long as similar differences in data organization exist between different platforms, and clear data correspondence and a basis for structural reconstruction can be established, the basic idea of the proposed method can provide a reference for other similar data-mapping tasks.

From the perspective of methodological logic, the proposed broken-chain correction method mainly consists of three transferable components: identification of data variations, topology-constrained structural reconstruction, and standardized organization of attribute data. First, during data transfer between different software platforms, problems such as attribute loss, structural loss, and structural error may commonly occur; therefore, the broken-chain identification strategy based on data-variation classification has a certain degree of general significance. Second, when the target platform cannot directly use the structural relationships associated with numerical model results for geometric representation, geometric reconstruction can be achieved by relying on prior meshes, existing topological relationships, or other external structural constraints. Third, organizing the reconstructed nodes, patches, and attribute information into structured data formats to meet the display and management requirements of the target control information model also has practical engineering reference value.

In addition to the transferability of the methodological logic, the lightweight browser–server architecture adopted in this study also enhances the engineering application potential of the proposed method to a certain extent. In the validated scenario of this study, users can directly access and interact with the control information model through a standard web browser, without relying on dedicated local software or additional programming environments, thereby lowering the barrier to the presentation and sharing of numerical results. In terms of mapping efficiency, this study further compared the rendering time and hardware resource requirements during updates of the control information model. The results indicate that, under the implementation conditions adopted in this study, the browser-based visualization scheme exhibits favorable lightweight characteristics in rendering efficiency. With the support of WebGL hardware acceleration, the loading and interactive response of model results can be completed relatively quickly. Meanwhile, compared with implementations based on local rendering libraries, the proposed scheme imposes relatively lower hardware requirements, which is more conducive to multi-user and cross-platform data access and result presentation. It should be noted, however, that the efficiency advantage discussed here was validated mainly in the browser-based control information model scenario adopted in this study, and the conclusion is still influenced by factors such as data scale, implementation details, and target-platform conditions.

However, it should be emphasized that the transferability of the proposed method is not unconditional, but depends on several prerequisites. First, there must be a clear data correspondence between the numerical model results and the target control information model; otherwise, attribute mapping and structural reconstruction will lack a reliable basis. Second, some form of prior structural information must be available to restore topological relationships, such as pre-processing mesh files, element connectivity files, or other identifiable structural constraints. Third, significant differences may exist among software platforms in terms of coordinate systems, unit systems, attribute storage order, and data format specifications. Therefore, in practical applications, the mapping rules and data conversion interfaces still need to be adapted according to the actual data organization of the numerical model and the control information model. In particular, for models involving faults, sliding interfaces, or other discontinuous structures, the current distance-based matching logic should be further extended by introducing domain-aware topological identifiers; otherwise, the method may not correctly distinguish structurally adjacent but physically independent nodes. Therefore, the proposed method should be regarded as a transferable processing framework under defined applicability conditions, rather than a unified solution directly applicable to all heterogeneous geological-modeling environments.

Overall, the proposed method is more suitable for application scenarios in which numerical models and information models have clearly differentiated functions, the numerical model can provide stable attribute outputs, and the target platform has explicit requirements for geometric organization and topological representation. Under such conditions, the proposed method can provide a technically meaningful reference for the cross-platform transfer of 3D geological numerical model results into control information models, and also offers a methodological reference for further research on geotechnical information integration in multi-software environments (Table 3).

Exploration of an intelligent correction method for model broken chains based on a mutual feedback mechanism

The model mapping chain-breaking correction method proposed in this study addresses problems such as inconsistencies in data structures, incompatibility in topological representation, and differences in attribute organization that arise during the mapping of 3D geological numerical model results to control information models. Through rule-based constraints and topology reconstruction, the method enables reliable representation of numerical results in the target environment. The current study focuses on a one-way mapping scenario, in which numerical simulation results are converted into visualization and management data in the control information model to support engineering presentation, information organization, and result sharing. Under this scenario, the proposed method has been shown to be effective in topology correction and attribute mapping; however, it still mainly serves a one-way data flow from numerical result output to information-model representation. In practical engineering applications, however, the relationship between numerical models and information models is often not limited to one-way result transfer, but may instead involve dynamic interaction and iterative feedback. On the one hand, numerical models can provide the information model with computational results, response characteristics, and analytical support. On the other hand, geometric updates, attribute enrichment, and engineering status information integrated into the information model may in turn contribute to parameter revision, boundary adjustment, and model optimization in the numerical model. Therefore, future research may extend the current one-way mapping framework toward a bidirectional feedback mechanism, so as to establish a more stable mutual interaction between numerical models and control information models, thereby improving the timeliness of model updating and the efficiency of information coordination.

On this basis, a further development direction for broken-chain correction lies in the introduction of intelligent processing mechanisms. Compared with the current approach, which mainly relies on rule-based constraints and manually defined mapping relationships, future work may explore methods combining anomaly detection, adaptive rule adjustment, and automatic optimization of mapping relationships to identify and correct structural anomalies, attribute loss, and format incompatibilities more efficiently during model mapping. Especially in scenarios involving multi-software collaboration, heterogeneous data sources, and frequent model updates, intelligent correction methods based on a mutual feedback mechanism are expected to improve the automation and stability of model mapping.

Overall, the present study provides a rule-driven foundation for broken-chain correction between 3D geological numerical models and control information models, while future extensions toward bidirectional feedback and intelligent correction mechanisms may further enhance the continuity, adaptability, and engineering applicability of cross-platform model mapping.

Conclusions

This study addressed the “broken chain” problem that arises during the one-way mapping of 3D geological numerical model results to a front-end control information model, where discontinuities are caused by differences in data structures, topological representation, and attribute organization rules. To solve this problem, a rule-driven broken-chain correction method was proposed. By identifying mapping-related data variations at both the attribute and structural levels, and combining rule-based correction with prior-mesh topology reconstruction, the proposed method establishes a structured workflow for the reliable transformation of numerical results into the target environment, thereby enabling their visualization and information management in the control information model.

This study addressed the “broken chain” problem that arises during the one-way mapping of 3D geological numerical model results to a front-end control information model, where discontinuities are caused by differences in data structures, topological representation, and attribute organization rules. To solve this problem, a rule-driven broken-chain correction method was proposed. By identifying mapping-related data variations at both the attribute and structural levels, and combining rule-based correction with prior-mesh topology reconstruction, the proposed method establishes a structured workflow for the reliable transformation of numerical results into the target environment, thereby enabling their visualization and information management in the control information model.

Using a pile-foundation engineering case from Southwest China, the proposed method was validated from two perspectives: model topology restoration and nodal attribute preservation. The results show that, under prior mesh constraints, the method can effectively restore target-side topology by reconstructing node connectivity and patch relationships, thereby alleviating local topological disorder, surface discontinuity, and self-intersection during mapping. At the same time, while satisfying the requirements of front-end visualization, the method preserves the key numerical characteristics of nodal results such as displacement and stress, indicating good structural correction performance and reliable attribute mapping in the cross-platform transfer process.

Therefore, the method is more suitable for application scenarios in which the numerical model is primarily used for computational analysis, while the control information model is mainly used for result organization, visualization, and information management.

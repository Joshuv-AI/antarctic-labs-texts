A free parameter depending family of polynomial wavelets on a compact interval Corresponding author: Woula Themistoclakis (woula.themistoclakis@cnr.it)
Woula Themistoclakis, Marc Van Barel

Abstract
On a compact interval, we introduce and study a whole family of wavelets depending on a free parameter that can be suitably modulated to improve performance. Such wavelets arise from de la Vallée Poussin (VP) interpolation at Chebyshev nodes, generalizing previous work by Capobianco and Themistoclakis who considered a special parameter setting. In our construction, both scaling and wavelet functions are interpolating polynomials at some Chebyshev zeros of 1st kind. Contrarily to the classical approach, they are not generated by dilations and translations of a single mother function and are naturally defined on the interval [ − 1 , 1 ] [-1,1] to which any other compact interval can be reduced. In the paper, we provide a non-standard multiresolution analysis with fast (DCT-based) decomposition and reconstruction algorithms. Moreover, we state several theoretical results, particularly on convergence, laying the foundation for future applications.



Keywords:
polynomial wavelets, de la Vallée Poussin interpolation, fast decomposition and reconstruction algorithms.

Introduction

Nowadays myriads of wavelet functions are known within a well established theory that classically involves dilations and translations of a single (scaling or wavelet) mother function on the whole real axis
[
2
,
4
]
. In adapting such a theory to a compact interval, typically various techniques are used (periodization, zero-padding etc.) that, bringing us back to the real axis, can originate problems at the boundaries of the interval
[
3
]
.

To avoid such problems, several polynomial scaling and wavelet functions have been generated without any mother to dilate and translate, but by taking particular combinations of orthogonal polynomials that naturally fit on the interval of interest. By appropriately increasing the degree of the considered basis polynomials, they give rise to a non-standard multi-resolution analysis based on the theory of orthogonal polynomials and known results in approximation theory (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
20
]
)

In this paper we focus on particular polynomial wavelets introduced in
[
1
]
starting from the de la Vallée Poussin type interpolation (shortly VP interpolation) at Chebyshev zeros of the first kind
[
21
,
22
,
23
,
18
]
.

Motivated by recent applications of this type of approximation in image processing
[
13
,
12
,
14
]
as well as in the numerical quadrature of the Hilbert transform
[
15
,
16
]
and in the numerical solution of integro-differential equations
[
17
,
5
,
11
]
, in this paper we want to present a generalization of the wavelets introduced in
[
1
]
.

The main peculiarity of VP interpolation is that it depends, in addition to the number of nodes
n
, also on a free integer parameter
0<m<n
that, appropriately chosen, can improve the pointwise approximation using the same
n
data points (see, e.g.,
[
22
,
24
]
).

In
[
1
]
this freedom is lost by setting
m=n/2
and consequently assuming
n
even.
The novelty we introduce is to maintain the arbitrariness of
m
and therefore of
n
.

In order to provide the reader with all the tools necessary for future applications, the theoretical results obtained in
[
1
]
are generalized and/or improved here, and new ones are also added. Finally, by generalizing the decomposition/reconstruction formulas obtained in
[
1
]
, we get fast algorithms, DCT based.

The outline of the paper is the following. Section 2 collects all the notation and preliminary results we use, Section 3 is about the scaling functions and VP interpolation, Section 4 regards wavelets and their properties, Section 5 provides the decomposition and reconstruction algorithms, Section 6 contains the proofs, and finally, Section 7 deals with conclusions.

2
Notation and preliminary results

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote the set of the first kind Chebyshev zeros of order
n
as follows

X_{n}=\left\{x_{k}^{n}=\cos\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2n},\ k=1,\ldots,n\right\},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

One can easily check that
X_{n}\subset X_{3n}
holds
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, setting

Y_{2n}=X_{3n}-X_{n}=\{y_{k}^{n},\ k=1,\ldots,2n\},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

we have the following decomposition

X_{3n}=X_{n}\cup Y_{2n}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad X_{n}\cap Y_{2n}=\emptyset,\qquad\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(1)

In the paper, we consider functions that can be sampled on the previous node sets. As functional norms, we consider

\|f\|_{p}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\sup_{|x|\leq 1}|f(x)|&\mbox{if}&p=\infty\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&\mbox{if}&1\leq p<\infty\end{array}\right.

where, throughout the paper,
w
denotes the Chebyshev weight of the first kind

w(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^{2}}},\qquad|x|\leq 1.

Moreover, we denote by
C^{0}=C[-1,1]

the space of all continuous functions and we set

L^{p}_{w}=L^{p}_{w}[-1,1]=\{f\ :\ \|f\|_{p}<\infty\}
. In the case
p=2
this is a Hilbert space equipped with the scalar product

<f,g>_{L^{2}_{w}}=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x)w(x)dx,

Hence, in the paper, we say that the functions
f,g
are orthogonal iff
<f,g>_{L^{2}_{w}}=0
.

Besides the previous continuous norms, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we also consider the discrete norms

\|\vec{a}_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}&1\leq p<\infty\\
\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq n}|a_{k}|&p=\infty\end{array}\right.\qquad\forall\vec{a}_{n}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

In the sequel, we denote by
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
the set of all algebraic polynomials of degree at most
n=0,1,2,\ldots
, and by

E_{n}(f)_{p}=\inf_{P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}}\|f-P\|_{p},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty,\qquad n=0,1,..,

the error of best (uniform or
L^{p}_{w}
) approximation of
f
in
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
.

In the special case of the uniform norm, we recall that the
Weierstrass approximation theorem ensures that

\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{n}(f)_{\infty}=0,\qquad\forall f\in\mathcal{C}^{0}

(2)

with a convergence order depending on the smoothness degree of
f

[
6
]
. In particular, we consider the class
Lip_{\alpha}
of Hölder continuous functions on
[-1,1]
with exponent
\alpha\in]0,1]
, the class
\mathcal{C}^{s}
of all functions that are
s
–times continuously differentiable in
[-1,1]
, and the class
\mathcal{C}^{s,\alpha}
of functions
f\in\mathcal{C}^{s}
with
f^{(s)}\in Lip_{\alpha}
. For these smoothness classes, we recall that, as
n\to\infty
, we have
[
6
]

E_{n}(f)_{\infty}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{O}(n^{-\alpha})&\qquad\mbox{if}\quad f\in Lip_{\alpha}\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1\\
\mathcal{O}(n^{-s})&\qquad\mbox{if}\quad f\in\mathcal{C}^{s}\qquad s\in\mathbb{N}\\
\mathcal{O}(n^{-s-\alpha})&\qquad\mbox{if}\quad f\in\mathcal{C}^{s,\alpha}\quad\ s\in\mathbb{N},\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1.\end{array}\right.

(3)

Throughout the paper, we denote by
p_{n}
the orthonormal Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind and degree
n
, namely

p_{n}(x)=\cos(n\arccos x)\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{1/\pi}&\mbox{ if $n=0$}\\[7.22743pt]
\sqrt{2/\pi}&\mbox{ if $n>0$}\end{array}\right.\qquad|x|\leq 1,\qquad n=0,1,....

(4)

and by
K_{n}
the Darboux kernel

K_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}p_{s}(x)p_{s}(y),\quad x,y\in[-1,1],\qquad n=0,1,....,

which is the reproducing kernel in
{\mathbb{P}}_{n}
satisfying

\int_{-1}^{1}K_{n}(x,y)f(y)w(y)dy=f(x),\qquad\forall x\in[-1,1],\quad\forall f\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n}.

(5)

A crucial role in the paper is played by the following de la Vallée Poussin (shortly VP) mean of the Darboux kernels
[
22
]

v_{n}^{m}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2m}\sum_{r=n-m}^{n+m-1}K_{r}(x,y),\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\quad m<n,\quad x,y\in[-1,1]

(6)

and the related symmetric polynomial kernel

\Phi_{n}^{m}(x,y)=\frac{\pi}{n}v_{n}^{m}(x,y),\qquad n,m\in\mathbb{N},\quad m<n,\quad x,y\in[-1,1].

(7)

We recall that the VP kernel
v_{n}^{m}
can also be interpreted as a filtered Darboux kernel of order
n+m-1
since we have
[
22
]

v_{n}^{m}(x,y)=\sum_{r=0}^{n+m-1}\mu_{n,r}^{m}p_{r}(x)p_{r}(y),

(8)

where

\mu_{n,r}^{m}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq n-m,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{m+n-r}{2m}&\mbox{if}\quad n-m<r<n+m,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

(9)

Moreover, setting
t=\arccos(x)
and
\tau=\arccos(y)
, we have the following compact trigonometric form
[
18
, p.282]

v_{n}^{m}(x,y)=\frac{1}{4\pi m}\left[\frac{\sin[m(t-\tau)]\sin[n(t-\tau)]}{\sin^{2}[(t-\tau)/2]}+\frac{\sin[m(t+\tau)]\sin[n(t+\tau)]}{\sin^{2}[(t+\tau)/2]}\right].

(10)

By using this trigonometric form, we easily deduce

\int_{-1}^{1}|v_{n}^{m}(x,y)|w(y)dy\leq\frac{1}{4\pi m}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|\sin[nt]\sin[mt]|}{\sin^{2}[t/2]}dt\qquad|x|\leq 1

and applying the following estimate
[
25
, p.100]

\frac{1}{4\pi m}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\frac{|\sin[nt]\sin[mt]|}{\sin^{2}[t/2]}dt=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\log\left[\frac{n+m-1}{2m}\right]+\mathcal{O}(1),

we obtain that

\int_{-1}^{1}|v_{n}^{m}(x,y)|w(y)dy=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\log\left[\frac{n+m-1}{2m}\right]+\mathcal{O}(1),\qquad\forall|x|\leq 1

(11)

holds for all pairs of integers
0<m<n
.

The VP kernel
v_{n}^{m}
defines the VP operator

\sigma_{n}^{m}f(x)=\int_{-1}^{1}v_{n}^{m}(x,y)f(y)w(y)dy,\qquad|x|\leq 1,

(12)

that satisfies the following invariance property

\sigma_{n}^{m}f=f,\qquad\forall f\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m},

(13)

as easily follows from (
5
)–(
6
).

In the sequel, we consider the Lebesgue constants of the VP operator, defined as the following operator norms

c_{p}=\sup_{\|f\|_{p}=1}\|\sigma_{n}^{m}f\|_{p},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty.

(14)

Note that such constants generally depend on
n
and
m
, and the notation
c_{p}(n,m)
is also used to underline this dependence when different degrees are involved (see Section
6
).

In the limit cases
p=1
and
p=\infty
, by (
11
), we have

c_{1}=c_{\infty}=\max_{|x|\leq 1}\int_{-1}^{1}|v_{n}^{m}(x,y)|w(y)dy=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\log\left[\frac{n+m-1}{2m}\right]+\mathcal{O}(1),

(15)

while the interpolation theorem in weighted
L^{p}
spaces (cf.
[
9
, Corollary 2.2]
, see also
[
24
]
) yields

c_{p}\leq const.\ \cdot c_{\infty},\qquad 1<p<\infty.

(16)

3
Scaling functions and VP interpolation

Contrary to the standard definition, we do not take a unique function to dilate and translate for generating the scaling functions, but for arbitrarily fixed
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>m
, we set the following
n
polynomials

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)=\Phi_{n}^{m}(x_{k}^{n},\ x)=\frac{\pi}{n}\ v_{n}^{m}(x_{k}^{n},\ x),\qquad k=1,\ldots,n\qquad x\in[-1,1].

(17)

as
scaling functions of order
n
and parameter
m
.

We recall that these polynomials have already been introduced in the literature as
fundamental VP polynomials

[
22
]
, and have been studied in several papers (see
[
18
,
19
]
and the references therein).

Similarly to fundamental Lagrange polynomials interpolating at
X_{n}
, they are linearly independent and satisfy the interpolation property
[
1
, Eq. (2.10)]

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x_{h}^{n})=\delta_{h,k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&h=k\\
0&h\neq k\end{array}\right.\qquad h,k=1,\ldots,n,\qquad 0<m<n.

(18)

Besides
k
, they depend on the integers
0<m<n
where
n
indicates the resolution degree, i.e., the number of the interpolation nodes in
X_{n}
, and
m
is a free parameter that can be arbitrarily chosen in
\{1,2,\ldots,n-1\}
without destroying the interpolation property (
18
).

we see how this additional parameter can soften the typical oscillating behavior of the fundamental Lagrange polynomial.

.

, we see each scaling function
\Phi_{n,k}^{m}
is well localized around the Chebyshev node

where it assumes its maximum value (=1).

with

.

shows how such localization increases as

increases.

Other properties of the scaling functions are summarized in the following theorem, where we point out that
(e)
improves a recent estimate stated in
[
19
, Thm. 6.1]
.

Theorem 3.1

For all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>m
, we have the following

(a)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}x^{s}\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)w(x)dx=\frac{\pi}{n}(x_{k}^{n})^{s},\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,n-m,\qquad k=1,\ldots,n

(b)

\displaystyle x^{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{k}^{n})^{s}\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad s=0,1,\ldots,n-m,\quad x\in[-1,1]

(c)

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}\|_{\infty}=1,\qquad\quad k=1,\ldots,n

(d)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{n^{p}}\leq\ \|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}\|_{p}^{p}\ \leq\frac{\pi}{n}\ c_{\infty},\qquad 1\leq p<\infty,\qquad\quad k=1,\ldots,n

(e)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)|\leq\left[1+2\pi\ \frac{n+m-1}{n}\right]\ c_{\infty}\leq(1+4\pi)c_{\infty}\qquad|x|\leq 1

(f)

For any
\vec{a}_{n}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
we have

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\|\vec{a}_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\Phi_{n,k}^{m}\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq c_{\infty}\left[1+2\pi\ \frac{n+m-1}{n}\right]\|\vec{a}_{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}

(19)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{1}{1+4\pi}\|\vec{a}_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\Phi_{n,k}^{m}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq c_{p^{\prime}}(1+4\pi)\ \|\vec{a}_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}.

(20)

The scaling functions of a given order
n\in\mathbb{N}
and parameter
m<n
generate the following approximation space

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}=span\{\phi_{n,k}^{m},\ k=1,\ldots,n\},\qquad\dim\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}=n.

(21)

By the previous properties, this space is nested between classical polynomial spaces as follows

{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}\subset\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1},\qquad\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N},\quad m<n.

(22)

Moreover, the scaling functions in (
21
) constitute an interpolating Riesz basis of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}
.

It is also known that an orthogonal basis of
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}
is given by the following polynomials
[
1
, Thm. 2.2]

\Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p_{r}(x)&\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq n-m,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\mu_{n,r}^{m}p_{r}(x)-\mu_{n,2n-r}^{m}p_{2n-r}(x)&\mbox{if}\quad n-m<r<n.\end{array}\right.

(23)

Setting

\nu_{n,r}^{m}=<\Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}},\ \Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}}>_{L^{2}_{w}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\mbox{if}\quad 0\leq r\leq n-m,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle\frac{m^{2}+(n-r)^{2}}{2m^{2}}&\mbox{if}\quad n-m<r<n,\end{array}\right.

(24)

we have

<\Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}},\ \Phi_{n,s}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}}>_{L^{2}_{w}}=\delta_{r,s}\cdot\nu_{n,r}^{m},\qquad r,s=0,...,n-1.

Moreover, the change of bases is given by the following formula
[
1
, Eq. (2.15)]

\Phi_{n,k}^{m}(x)=\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}p_{r}(x_{k}^{n})\Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}}(x),\qquad k=1,\ldots,n.

(25)

Due to (
18
), any function
f
, sampled at
X_{n}
, can be projected onto the approximation space
\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}
by taking the polynomial

V_{n}^{m}f(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k}^{n})\phi_{n,k}^{m}(x),\qquad n>m\in\mathbb{N},\qquad x\in[-1,1]

(26)

that interpolates
f
at
X_{n}
, i.e.

V_{n}^{m}f(x_{h}^{n})=f(x_{h}^{n}),\qquad h=1,\ldots,n,\qquad n>m\in\mathbb{N}.

(27)

Also, note that we trivially have

f\in\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}\Longleftrightarrow f=V_{n}^{m}f

(28)

and in particular

V_{n}^{m}P=P,\qquad\forall P\in{\mathbb{P}}_{n-m}.

(29)

Moreover, by (
25
), the polynomial (
26
) can be expanded in terms of the orthogonal functions (
23
) as follows

V_{n}^{m}f(x)=\sum_{r=0}^{n-1}c_{n,r}\Phi_{n,r}^{m^{\hskip 1.42271pt\mbox{\large$\bot$}}}(x),\qquad c_{n,r}=\left[\frac{\pi}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k}^{n})p_{r}(x_{k}^{n})\right],\qquad\forall n>m\in\mathbb{N}.

(30)

In the literature, this polynomial is already known as the VP polynomial interpolating
f
and its approximation properties have been studied in several papers (see
[
18
,
19
]
and the references therein).

Here, we recall that it offers several advantages over the classical Lagrange polynomial interpolating
f
at the same nodes, especially in the case of functions a.e. smooth with isolated singularities. In such a case, one can take advantage of the presence of the free parameter
m<n
that can be suitably modulated to attenuate the Gibbs phenomenon and improve the point-wise approximation keeping the number of nodes fixed
[
22
]
.

As regards the uniform approximation, from (
29
) and Thm.
3.1
we easily deduce the following estimate of the theoretical error

E_{n+m-1}(f)_{\infty}\leq\|f-V_{n}^{m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{C}E_{n-m}(f)_{\infty},

(31)

where
\mathcal{C}:=1+(1+4\pi)c_{\infty}
.

Moreover, if we are using corrupted data, say

\tilde{f}(x_{k}^{n}):=f(x_{k}^{n})+\epsilon_{k}^{n},\qquad k=1,\ldots,n

then, by Thm.
3.1
, we get

\frac{1}{\mathcal{C}}\|\vec{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\|V_{n}^{m}f-V_{n}^{m}\tilde{f}\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}\|\vec{\epsilon}_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}},\qquad 1\leq p\leq\infty,

(32)

where
\mathcal{C}:=(1+4\pi)c_{p^{\prime}}
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
, and
\vec{\epsilon}_{n}=(\epsilon_{1}^{n},\ldots,\epsilon_{n}^{n})
.

In the previous estimates (
31
)–(
32
), we remark that, contrary to
n
which is determined by the number of nodes, the parameter
m
can be freely chosen in
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
.
But, as
n\to\infty
, the asymptotic estimates (
15
)–(
16
) imply a logarithmic growth of the constant
\mathcal{C}
in (
31
) –(
32
), unless we take
m
growing at the same order. Indeed, we have

1<\frac{n}{m}=\mathcal{O}(1)\ \Longrightarrow\ c_{p}=\mathcal{O}(1),\quad 1\leq p\leq\infty,\qquad\quad\forall n,m\in\mathbb{N}.

(33)

In the literature, a common choice is to take
m=\lfloor\theta n\rfloor
with
\theta\in]0,1[
arbitrarily fixed. In such a case, in (
31
) –(
32
),
\mathcal{C}
is an absolute constant (only depending on
\theta
) and we also have that
(n\pm m)
behaves as
n
. Hence, by (
31
) and (

), we get

\lim_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}n\to\infty\\
m=\lfloor\theta n\rfloor\end{array}}\|f-V_{n}^{m}f\|_{\infty}=0,\qquad\forall f\in C^{0},\qquad\forall\theta\in]0,1[,

(34)

where the convergence order is comparable with the convergence rate of
E_{n}(f)_{\infty}
.

In particular, recalling (
3
), for arbitrarily large
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m=\lfloor\theta n\rfloor
with fixed
\theta\in]0,1[
, we have

\|f-V_{n}^{m}f\|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{C}\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n^{-\alpha}&\mbox{if}\quad f\in Lip_{\alpha}\qquad 0<\alpha\leq 1\\
n^{-s}&\mbox{if}\quad f\in C^{s}\qquad\quad s\in\mathbb{N}\\
n^{-s-\alpha}&\mbox{if}\quad f\in C^{s,\alpha}\qquad s\in\mathbb{N},\quad 0<\alpha\leq 1\end{array}\right.

(35)

where
\mathcal{C}>0
is a constant independent of
n,m
but depending on
\theta\in]0,1[
and
f
.

Finally, we recall that by using the samples of
f
at
X_{n}
we can simultaneously approximate also the derivatives of
f
in
[-1,1]
by means of the derivatives of the VP polynomial
V_{n}^{m}f
given by

(V_{n}^{m}f)^{(r)}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(x_{k}^{k})\left(\Phi_{n,k}^{m}\right)^{(r)}(x),\qquad|x|\leq 1.

For brevity, we omit the details about the computation of the derivatives of the scaling function basis, referring the interested reader to
[
19
]
. Here, we only recall the error estimate proved in
[
19
, Thm. 5.3]
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
be arbitrarily large but such that
n/m\in[a,b]
holds, where
b\geq a>1
are independent of
n
and
m
. If
f\in C^{2s}
for some

s\in\mathbb{N}
then we have

\displaystyle\|f^{(r)}-(V_{n}^{m}f)^{(r)}\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{n^{2s-r}},\qquad r=0,1,\ldots,s

(36)

where
\mathcal{C}>0
is independent of
n,m
.

4
Wavelet functions and detail spaces

The starting point for the construction of our wavelets is given by the following inclusion

\mathcal{V}^{m}_{n}\subset\mathcal{V}^{m}_{3n},\qquad\forall n>m\in\mathbb{N},

(37)

which easily follows from (
22
) and
{\mathbb{P}}_{n+m-1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{3n-m}
.

By taking into account (
37
), for all integers
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>m
we define the
detail or wavelet space

\mathcal{W}_{n}^{m}
as the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{V}^{m}_{n}
in
\mathcal{V}_{3n}^{m}
, namely

\mathcal{V}^{m}_{3n}=\mathcal{V}^{m}_{n}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{n}^{m}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}\bot\mathcal{W}_{n}^{m},\qquad\quad\forall n>m\in\mathbb{N}.

(38)

Wavelet functions are generally defined as localized basis functions that generate the detail spaces. Since
\dim\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}=n
implies that
\dim\mathcal{W}_{n}^{m}=2n
, we expect

5
Decomposition and reconstruction algorithms

Due to (

(49)

On the other hand, by using the scaling and wavelet bases introduced in the previous sections, the functions in (

In the following subsections we provide the decomposition and reconstruction formulas that lead to the fast computing of these coefficients.

We start with the case that the orthogonal bases (

\mathcal{V}_{n}^{m}
and
\mathcal{W}_{n}^{m}
resp. Then we focus on the less trivial case where interpolating scaling and wavelet bases are used.

In the former case, as expected, the formulas will be based on quasi orthogonal matrices. In the latter case, the formulas stated in
[

will be generalized to our setting. Even if they do not involve orthogonal matrices, fast algorithms will be obtained also in this case.

7
Conclusions

Without any mother function to dilate and translate, we present a family of scaling and wavelet functions on
[-1,1]
that are non-orthogonal, interpolating, and very smooth being polynomials.

The scaling functions at a resolution level
n
are defined as fundamental VP polynomials interpolating at Chebyshev zeros of the first kind and order
n
, whose good approximation properties are already known in the literature
[
22
]
. They generate a non-standard multiresolution analysis where instead of duplicating, we triplicate the resolution degree
n
, introducing
2n
wavelets spanning the detail space at the resolution level
n
. Such wavelets are also polynomials interpolating at those Chebyshev nodes of the scaling functions at level
3n
which are missing at the resolution level
n
.

Both the scaling and wavelet functions have the peculiarity of depending on a free integer parameter
m
that can be arbitrarily chosen in the set
\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
. In particular, this parameter determines the number of null moments for wavelets of level
n
(the first
n-m
moments).
For the special choice
n=2\cdot 3^{j}
and
m=3^{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we get the scaling and wavelet functions introduced in
[
1
]
from which many ideas were taken.

Besides several theoretical results, Riesz stability is proved for scaling functions as well as for wavelets w.r.t. the uniform norm and any
L^{p}
norm weighted by the Chebyshev weight. Moreover, fast decomposition and reconstruction algorithms, based on DCT, are given.

Going along the same line, the proposed procedure could be extended to any odd scaling factor replacing the higher resolution level
3n
with
5n
,
7n
, etc…. Moreover, similar results could be derived for the other Chebyshev weights of the second, third and fourth kind, as done in
[
1
]
for a special parameter setting.

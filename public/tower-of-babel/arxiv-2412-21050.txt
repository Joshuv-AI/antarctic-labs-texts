Parabolic gap theorems for the Yang-Mills energy
Anuk Dayaprema, Alex Waldron

Abstract
. We prove parabolic versions of several known gap theorems in classical Yang-Mills theory. On an SU ⁡ ( r ) {\mathrm{SU}}(r) -bundle of charge κ \kappa over the 4-sphere, we show that the space of all connections with Yang-Mills energy less than 4 ​ π 2 ​ ( | κ | + 2 ) 4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa|+2\right) deformation-retracts under Yang-Mills flow onto the space of instantons, allowing us to simplify the proof of Taubes’s path-connectedness theorem. On a compact quaternion-Kähler manifold with positive scalar curvature, we prove that the space of pseudo-holomorphic connections whose 𝔰 ​ 𝔭 ​ ( 1 ) \mathfrak{sp}(1) curvature component has small Morrey norm deformation-retracts under Yang-Mills flow onto the space of instantons. On a nontrivial bundle over a compact manifold of general dimension, we prove that the infimum of the scale-invariant Morrey norm of curvature is positive.



1.
Introduction

Classical Yang-Mills theory is concerned with two closely-related semi-elliptic partial differential equations involving a
connection
(known to physicists as a
gauge field
) on a vector or principal bundle over an orientable Riemannian manifold. Solutions of the first are known as
instantons
, while solutions of the second are known as
Yang-Mills connections
. These equations are of first and second order, respectively, in the connection.

In the 4-dimensional setting, an instanton
A
satisfies the
anti-self-duality equation

F_{A}^{+}=0,

up to reversing orientation.
A Yang-Mills connection satisfies the
Yang-Mills equation

D_{A}^{*}F_{A}=0.

The latter is the Euler-Lagrange equation of the
Yang-Mills energy functional

\mathcal{YM}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\,dV.

Since an instanton minimizes
\mathcal{YM}(\cdot)
among all connections on the given bundle, it must be Yang-Mills.
However, the converse fails in general; for instance,
by the celebrated work of Sibner, Sibner, and Uhlenbeck

[
37
]
, we know that even the trivial
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-bundle over
S^{4}
carries a vast supply of non-minimal Yang-Mills connections.

Positive results in the converse direction are nonetheless common in the literature. Since the work of Bourguignon, Lawson, and Simons
[
5
,
6
]
, these have fallen into two categories:
stability theorems
, stating that a Yang-Mills connection with nonnegative second variation must be an instanton, and
gap theorems
, stating that a Yang-Mills connection with appropriately small self-dual curvature
must be an instanton. We begin by reviewing a number of existing gap theorems.

The
L^{2}
gap theorem on
S^{4},
due to Min-Oo
[
26
]
, runs as follows. Suppose that
A
is
a Yang-Mills connection on a vector bundle
E\to S^{4}
with

\mathcal{YM}(A)<4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa(E)|+\delta\right),

where
\delta
is a positive universal constant and
\kappa(E)
is the characteristic number defined in §
2.4
below; then
A
is an instanton. See Donaldson-Kronheimer
[
13
, §2.3.10]
for a proof, and Dodziuk and Min-Oo
[
11
]
for an improved constant.
More recently, in the case that
E
has structure group
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
, Gursky, Kelleher, and Streets
[
18
]
have managed to improve the value to
\delta=2,
or twice the Yang-Mills energy of the unit-charge instanton. This is consistent with known examples of non-minimal Yang-Mills connections on
S^{4}

[
4
,
35
,
37
]
.

More generally, the existence of an
L^{2}
gap is known for Riemannian 4-manifolds where the scalar curvature dominates the self-dual Weyl tensor
[
17
,
32
]
, for compact Kähler surfaces with positive scalar curvature
[
20
]
, for bundles where the infimum of the Yang-Mills energy is zero
[
15
]
, and for certain bundles over compact 4-manifolds with generic metrics
[
14
]
(based on the Freed-Uhlenbeck Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 3.17]
).
Under mild geometric constraints, Vieira extended the Gursky-Kelleher-Streets result to noncompact manifolds with positive Yamabe constant
[
45
]
. Interestingly, the existence of an
L^{2}
gap on a general compact Riemannian 4-manifold remains unknown.

The notion of instanton can be extended to a higher-dimensional manifold endowed with an
(n-4)
-calibration
\Psi

[
8
,
44
]
: a connection
A
is called a (
\Psi
-)
instanton
if

\displaystyle\ast(F_{A}\wedge\Psi)=-F_{A}.

In particular, manifolds with special holonomy come with such calibrations, and the gap question is basic in higher-dimensional gauge theory.
No firm gap results exist for
{\mathrm{G}}_{2}
- or
{\mathrm{Spin}}(7)
-manifolds, and the question appears to be a difficult one. However, on a quaternion-Kähler manifold with positive scalar curvature, Taniguchi established an
L^{\infty}
gap
[
40
]
for pseudo-holomorphic (see
[
31
, §3.3]
) Yang-Mills connections, and later an
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
gap
[
41
]
. The
L^{\infty}
result can be seen as a generalization of Bourguignon-Lawson-Simons’s theorem on
S^{4}
, while the
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
result can be seen as a generalization of Min-Oo’s theorem.

Although higher-dimensional manifolds with generic holonomy lack a natural class of minimizers for
\mathcal{YM}
other than the flat connections, the gap question can still be posed at the zero energy.
Bourguignon-Lawson
[
5
]
proved an
L^{\infty}
gap theorem for the full curvature of a connection on a bundle over
S^{n},n\geq 4
. Gerhardt
[
17
]
proved an
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
gap theorem for compact manifolds where the Riemann curvature satisfies a certain pointwise positivity condition. Zhou
[
52
]
has obtained
L^{p}
,
p\geq\frac{n}{2}
, gaps which depend on the constants in the global Sobolev inequality for functions on the manifold. The existence of a (non-explicit)
L^{2}
gap near the zero energy is easily proved by combining standard estimates with the well-known Łojasiewicz-Simon inequality; see Corollary
5.2
below.

Notice that these gap results all share an obvious drawback, namely, that the connection in question must solve the Yang-Mills equation
a priori
. Although Uhlenbeck’s direct method succeeds at producing solutions
in dimension less than four, the Yang-Mills equation is itself extremely difficult to solve in dimension at least four. This is due both to the gauge freedom of the Yang-Mills functional and to the famous “bubbling phenomenon” which begins in dimension four and is less-well understood in higher dimensions.

The goal of this paper is to broaden the scope of these results by removing the assumption that the relevant connection be Yang-Mills. As an alternative,
we consider solutions of the Yang-Mills
flow
:

(1.1)

\frac{{\partial}A}{{\partial}t}=-D_{A}^{*}F_{A}.

In the same circumstances
as several of the elliptic gap theorems discussed above, we will
establish that (
1.1
)
gives a deformation retraction from an appropriate
neighborhood in the space of all connections
onto the subspace of instantons. A result of this kind will be called a
parabolic gap theorem
.

We will also demonstrate a few applications of our main results.

1.1.
Deformation to instantons on the 4-sphere

Our first result is a parabolic gap theorem on
S^{4}
based on Gursky-Kelleher-Streets’s improvement of the Min-Oo gap theorem and the second-named author’s thesis.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A_{0}
be a smooth connection on an
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
-bundle
E\to S^{4}
with energy

(1.2)

\mathcal{YM}(A_{0})<4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa(E)|+2\right).

If
A(t)
solves (
1.1
) with
A(0)=A_{0},
then
A(t)
converges smoothly to an instanton on
E
as
t\to\infty.
Moreover, the map
A_{0}\mapsto\lim_{t\to\infty}A(t)
gives a deformation retraction from the space of connections satisfying (
1.2
) onto the space of instantons.

Note that if we ask only for Uhlenbeck convergence along a subsequence, then the result follows directly from
[
18
]
and
[
47
]
; the main point is that the convergence takes place smoothly. We also note that the statement is true both before or after modding out by the gauge group.

The proof depends on an optimal “split”
{\varepsilon}
-regularity theorem for the self-dual curvature along (
1.1
), which is proved using a type-II blowup argument appealing to Uhlenbeck’s compactness and removable singularity theorems. By the key estimate of
[
46
]
, this guarantees not only long-time existence but smooth convergence when working on
S^{4}.

Theorem
1.1
can also be compared with
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
, which gives the same result on the trivial bundle.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we can give a streamlined proof of the following classic theorem of Taubes.

Theorem 1.2

(Taubes
[
43
]
, 1984)
.

For any
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
-bundle
E\to S^{4},
the moduli space of instantons on
E
is path-connected.

In our variant, described in §
3.3
, all of the hard analysis in Taubes’s proof is replaced by Theorem
1.1
. We can also remove the requirement that
r=2
or
3,
since the GKS gap theorem
[
18
]
renders the use of Taubes’s index bounds
[
42
]
unnecessary. For general
r,
connectedness of the
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
moduli spaces on
S^{4}
was previously observed by Donaldson
[
12
, p. 454]
using his algebraic description of framed instantons.

Last, it is interesting to point out that Theorem
1.2
provides a purely differential-geometric proof of the completeness of the ADHM construction
[
1
]
—see Corollary
3.10
below. We include this observation (without claiming any originality)
in order to highlight the significance of Taubes’s theorem.

1.2.
Deformation to instantons on quaternion-Kähler manifolds

Let
M
be a quater-nion-Kähler manifold of dimension
4m
. For
m\geq 2
, this means a Riemannian manifold whose holonomy lies in

{\mathrm{Sp}}(m){\mathrm{Sp}}(1)\subset{\mathrm{SO}}(4m).

When
m=1
, we further impose that
M
be Einstein and half-conformally flat.

On a quaternion-Kähler manifold, we have the orthogonal decomposition
[
31
, §3.3]

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\mathfrak{sp}(m)}^{2}\oplus\Lambda_{\mathfrak{sp}(1)}^{2}\oplus\Lambda_{(\mathfrak{sp}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(1))^{\perp}}^{2}

\displaystyle=:\Lambda_{-}^{2}\oplus\Lambda_{+}^{2}\oplus\Lambda_{\perp}^{2}.

Hence, given a connection
A
, the curvature decomposes as

\displaystyle F_{A}=F_{A}^{-}+F_{A}^{+}+F_{A}^{\perp}.

In this paper, an
instanton
on a quaternion-Kähler manifold is a connection satisfying
F_{A}=F_{A}^{-}
, i.e., whose curvature takes values in
\Lambda_{-}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
. These are minimizers of the Yang-Mills functional and hence are Yang-Mills themselves. Connections whose curvatures take values either in
\Lambda_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
or in
\Lambda_{\perp}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
are sometimes also referred to as instantons in the literature.

More generally, a
pseudo-holomorphic connection
, i.e., an
\text{Sp}(m)\text{Sp}(1)
-compatible connection per Oliveira-Waldron
[
31
, Def. 2.1]
, is a connection whose curvature takes values in

\Lambda_{-}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})\oplus\Lambda_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E}).

We have the following result, which can be seen as a generalization of Theorem
1.1
. Here the gap is measured in the
M^{2,4}
Morrey norm (defined in (
2.2
) below), which is a natural generalization of the conformally invariant
L^{2}
norm in four dimensions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be a compact quaternion-Kähler manifold of dimension
4m
. There exists a constant
\delta_{0}>0,
depending only on the geometry of
M,
as follows.

Suppose that
A_{0}
is a pseudo-holomorphic connection on
E\to M,
with

(1.3)

\displaystyle\left\|F_{A_{0}}^{+}\right\|_{M^{2,4}}<\delta_{0}.

Then the solution
A(t)
of (
1.1
) with
A(0)=A_{0}
exists for all time. If
M
has positive scalar curvature, then
A(t)
converges smoothly as
t\to\infty
to an instanton
A_{\infty},
satisfying
F_{A_{\infty}}=F_{A_{\infty}}^{-}.
Moreover, the space of instantons is a neighborhood deformation retract (see Definition
4.2
below) of the space of pseudo-holomorphic connections.

1.3.
Deformation to flat connections on general Riemannian manifolds

We have the following parabolic gap statement for the Morrey norm of the full curvature.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 4.
There exists a constant
\delta_{1}>0,
depending only on
{\mathrm{rk}}(E)
and the geometry of
M,
as follows. Suppose that
A_{0}
is a connection on
E\to M
with

(1.4)

\|F_{A_{0}}\|_{M^{2,4}}<\delta_{1}.

Then the solution
A(t)
of (
1.1
) with
A(0)=A_{0}
exists for all time and converges smoothly as
t\to\infty
to a flat connection.
Moreover, the space of flat connections is a neighborhood deformation retract of the space of all connections.

We include two proofs of this result, the first based on the parabolic Morrey estimates of the first-named author
[
9
]
and the second based on Hamilton’s monotonicity formula for Yang-Mills flow
[
19
]
. Theorem
1.4
has the following consequence:

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
E\to M
be a vector bundle over a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 4
and let
\mathcal{A}_{E}
denote the space of all connections on
E.
There exists a flat connection on
E
if and only if

\displaystyle\inf\left\{\|F_{A}\|_{M^{2,4}}\mid A\in\mathcal{A}_{E}\right\}=0.

The corresponding result for maps between compact Riemannian manifolds is
[
9
, Corollary 1.4]
, which is related to results of White
[
50
, Cor. 2 and 3 on pg. 128]
on the infimum of the
L^{n}
-norm of the derivative on the space of maps.

By contrast, work of Naito
[
28
]
on the existence of finite-time blowup for (
1.1
) on spheres of dimension
n\geq 5
implies that there does not in general exist a parabolic
L^{2}
gap. The method of
[
28
]
can also be found in prior work of Chen and Ding in the setting of harmonic map flow
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
. We recover the following version of Naito’s result for arbitrary compact base manifolds, proved by Wang and Zhang
[
49
]
.

Corollary 1.6

(
[
49
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. Supposing that
\pi_{1}(M)
has no nontrivial representations in
G,
there exists a constant
\delta_{2}>0
, depending only on
G
and the geometry of
M
, as follows. Given any connection
A_{0}
on a topologically nontrivial vector bundle
E\to M
with structure group
G,
if

(1.5)

\mathcal{YM}(A_{0})<\delta\leq\delta_{2},

then the maximal time of smooth existence of the solution of (
1.1
) with
A(0)=A_{0}
is less than
\delta_{2}^{-1}\delta^{\frac{2}{n-4}}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Principal and vector bundles

Let
G
be a compact Lie group and
\rho:G\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{K}(V)
a faithful representation of dimension
r
over
K={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}.
We assume that
V
is endowed with a (Hermitian) inner product preserved by
G,
so that
\rho(G)<\mathrm{O}(r)
if
K={\mathbb{R}}
and
\rho(G)<{\mathrm{U}}(r)
if
K={\mathbb{C}}.
Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
denote the Lie algebra of
G
, which we identify with the space of left-invariant vector fields on
G.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold. Recall that a
principal
G
-bundle over
M
is a smooth fiber bundle
G\hookrightarrow P\to M
with fiber
G
and a right
G
-action
P\times G\to P
satisfying the following conditions. The action should be smooth, commute with the fiber bundle projection, and act freely and transitively on the fibers. Given a principal
G
-bundle
P\to M
and a space
F
on which
G
acts on the left, we may form the
associated fiber bundle

P\times_{G}F=\{P\times F\}/\left(x,y\right)\sim\left(xg,g^{-1}y\right).

We form the following associated fiber bundles:

(1)

The bundle of
gauge transformations
:

\mathcal{G}_{P}=P\times_{\Ad}G.

By abuse of notation, we also refer to its sheaf of smooth sections by
\mathcal{G}_{P};
these correspond to local automorphisms of
P.

(2)

The bundle of
infinitesimal gauge transformations
(a.k.a. the
adjoint bundle
):

{\mathfrak{g}}_{P}=P\times_{\mathrm{Ad}}{\mathfrak{g}}.

This is a vector bundle with fiber
{\mathfrak{g}}.

(3)

The vector bundle

E=P\times_{\rho}V,

which has rank
r
over
K
and inherits a bundle metric from the inner product on
V.

The maps
\rho:G\to{\mathrm{GL}}_{K}(V)
and
d\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{K}(V)
yield embeddings of
\mathcal{G}_{P}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{P},
respectively, into
{\operatorname{End}\,}E=E\otimes_{K}E^{*}
. Hence, an (infinitesimal) gauge transformation of
P
is equivalent to a section of
{\operatorname{End}\,}E
that lies fiberwise in the image of
\rho(G)
(resp.
\rho({\mathfrak{g}})
). We endow both
\mathcal{G}_{P}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{P}
with the induced metric from
{\operatorname{End}\,}E
; in particular, sections of the former are orthogonal (resp. unitary), while sections of the latter are skew-symmetric (resp. skew-Hermitian), for
K={\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}},
respectively.

The principal bundle
P
can be reconstructed from the associated bundle
E
by applying the inverse of the map
\rho
to the transition functions. In fact, any real or complex vector bundle whose transition functions belong to the image of a representation of
G
arises from the associated bundle construction for some principal
G
-bundle. We may therefore define a
vector bundle with structure group
(G,\rho)
, or
(G,\rho)
-bundle
, in the following two equivalent ways:

(1)

The associated bundle
E=P\times_{\rho}V
for a principal
G
-bundle
P
and
r
-dimensional real or complex representation,
V,
or

(2)

A vector bundle of rank
r
over
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{C}}
and a cover of
M
by trivializations for which the transition functions belong to
\rho(G).

The choice of representation
\rho
is usually obvious; for example, a rank
r
real vector bundle “with structure group
{\mathrm{SO}}(r)
” has all transition functions in the image of the standard rank
r
real representation, while a rank
r
complex vector bundle “with structure group
{\mathrm{SU}}(r)
” has all transition functions in the image of the standard rank
r
complex representation.

In this paper, we prefer to work with vector rather than principal bundles, which entails no loss of generality since
G
is compact. Henceforth, for a
(G,\rho)
-bundle
E,
we will denote the images of
\mathcal{G}_{P}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{P}
inside
{\operatorname{End}\,}E
by
\mathcal{G}_{E}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{E},
respectively.

2.2.
Connections, curvature, and gauge transformations

Recall that a connection on a principal
G
-bundle
\pi:P\to M
is a smooth distribution
\mathcal{H}\subset TP
which satisfies the following:

(1)

TP\cong\mathcal{H}\oplus\ker{d\pi}
.

(2)

For all
g\in G
and
p\in P
,
(R_{g})_{\ast}(\mathcal{H}\cap T_{p}P)=\mathcal{H}\cap T_{pg}P
.

Given a tangent vector
X
at
x\in M
and a point
p\in\pi^{-1}(x)
, there is a unique
X^{H}\in\mathcal{H}\cap T_{p}P
, called the horizontal lift of
X
, such that
d\pi(X^{H})=X
.

A connection
A
on
P
induces a covariant derivative
\nabla_{A}
on the vector bundle
E=P\times_{\rho}V\to M
as follows. Let
s
be a smooth section of
E
. Since
G
acts freely and transitively on the fibers of
P
,
s
determines a unique,
G
-invariant section
\tilde{s}
of
P\times V\to P
, which may equivalently be viewed as a
G
-equivariant function
P\to V
. Given
X\in T_{x}M
,
(d\tilde{s})(X_{p}^{H})
determines an element of
\pi^{-1}(x)\times V
, and by
G
-equivariance,
(d\tilde{s})(X_{p}^{H})
lie in the same equivalence class of
\pi^{-1}(x)\times_{\rho}V
as
p
varies in
\pi^{-1}(x)
. Hence we define
\nabla_{A}
by

\displaystyle(\nabla_{A}s)(X):=[(d\tilde{s})(X^{H})].

Conversely, given a
(G,\rho)
-bundle
E
, suppose we have a covariant derivative
\nabla_{A}
such that, in local frames for which the transition functions belong fiberwise to
\rho(G)
, the connection 1-forms take values in
d\rho({\mathfrak{g}})
. We obtain a connection on
P
as follows. Let
U
be a trivialized neighborhood of
x\in M
, and let
X\in T_{x}M
. By abuse of notation, we denote the connection form with respect to the trivialization by
A.
Since
\rho
is faithful, we may define a horizontal vector at
(x,e)\in U\times G
by
(X,(d\rho)^{-1}(A(X)))
. We obtain a horizontal distribution over
U\times G
by right-translation, and since the transition functions of
E
belong to
\rho(G)
and
\rho
is faithful, this prescription glues together to give a connection on
P
. Thus we refer to such covariant derivatives as
(G,\rho)
-connections, and in the sequel we restrict our attention to
(G,\rho)
-connections on
E
instead of principal connections on
P
.

The curvature of a connection
A
is a section
F_{A}
of
\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{E}
defined locally by

\displaystyle F_{A}=dA+A\wedge A.

Given another
(G,\rho)
-connection
\tilde{A}
, whence
a=\tilde{A}-A
is a globally well-defined
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
-valued 1-form, we have

\displaystyle F_{\tilde{A}}=F_{A}+D_{A}a+a\wedge a,

where
D_{A}
denotes the covariant exterior derivative.
A gauge transformation
u\in\Gamma(\mathcal{G}_{E})
acts on connections by the prescription

\displaystyle\nabla_{u(A)}s=u(\nabla_{A}(u^{-1}(s))).

Locally, the connection form transforms by

(2.1)

\displaystyle A\mapsto uAu^{-1}-duu^{-1},

and the curvature transforms by

(2.2)

\displaystyle F_{u(A)}=uF_{A}u^{-1}.

2.3.
The Yang-Mills functional

Suppose now that
M
is endowed with a Riemannian metric
g.
We define the induced inner product on
k
-forms by requiring that

|e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{k}|=1

for orthonormal covectors
e^{1},\ldots,e^{k}.
The Hodge star operator
*:\Omega^{k}\to\Omega^{n-k}
is an isometry defined by

\left\langle\alpha,\beta\right\rangle\,dV=\alpha\wedge*\beta

which extends linearly to forms valued in any vector bundle.
Combining this with the norm on
{\operatorname{End}\,}E,
for
\omega,\eta
(skew-symmetric/skew-Hermitian) sections of
\Omega^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}_{E}),
we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\omega,\eta\right\rangle\,dV=-\operatorname{Tr}\omega\wedge*\eta,

where
\operatorname{Tr}=\operatorname{Tr}_{K}.
The Yang-Mills functional is defined by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{YM}(A)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\operatorname{Tr}F_{A}\wedge*F_{A}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|F_{A}|^{2}\,dV.

2.4.
The characteristic number

We follow the convention of Donaldson-Kronheimer
[
13
, p. 42]
for the characteristic number
\kappa(E)\in\frac{1}{4}{\mathbb{Z}}
of a real or complex vector bundle
E
over a closed, oriented 4-manifold:

\displaystyle\kappa(E)=\begin{cases}\langle c_{2}(E)-\frac{1}{2}c_{1}(E)^{2},[M]\rangle&K={\mathbb{C}}\\
-\frac{1}{4}\langle p_{1}(E),[M]\rangle&K={\mathbb{R}}.\end{cases}

The coefficient
-\frac{1}{4}
guarantees that for an
\text{SU}(2)
-bundle
E,
we have
\kappa(E)=\kappa({\mathfrak{g}}_{E})

[
13
, (2.1.37)]
. The number
\kappa(E)
has the property that it is an integer over the 4-sphere, according to Atiyah-Hitchin-Singer
[
2
, pg. 450]
.

According to Chern-Weil theory
[
25
, Appendix C]
, for any connection
A
on
E,
we have

\displaystyle\kappa(E)=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n_{K}}\int_{M}\operatorname{Tr}_{K}F_{A}\wedge F_{A},

where

(2.5)

2.6.
Weitzenböck formulae

Recall that the adjoint
D_{A}^{\ast}
of the exterior covariant derivative, acting on
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
-valued
k
-forms, is given by

\displaystyle D_{A}^{\ast}=(-1)^{n(k+1)+1}\ast D_{A}\ast.

We define the Hodge Laplacian
\Delta_{A}
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{A}=D_{A}^{\ast}D_{A}+D_{A}D_{A}^{\ast}.

The Hodge Laplacian and the connection Laplacian
\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}
differ by algebraic terms involving the curvatures of
A
and
g
. Let
R_{ijkl},R_{ij},
and
R
respectively denote the Riemann, Ricci, and scalar curvature of
g.
On
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
-valued 2-forms, we have from
[
31
, (4.6)]
that

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\omega)_{ij}=(\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}\omega)_{ij}-[F_{i}^{\ k},\omega_{kj}]+[F_{j}^{\ k},\omega_{ki}]+R_{i\ j}^{\ k\ l}\omega_{lk}-R_{j\ i}^{\ k\ l}\omega_{lk}+R_{i}^{\ l}\omega_{lj}+R_{j}^{\ l}\omega_{il},

where we use the Einstein summation convention of summing over repeated indices. By the algebraic Bianchi identity,

\displaystyle R_{ikjl}=-R_{lijk}-R_{klji}=R_{jkil}+R_{ijkl},

so the Weitzenböck identity takes the form

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\omega)_{ij}=(\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}\omega)_{ij}-[F_{i}^{\ k},\omega_{kj}]+[F_{j}^{\ k},\omega_{ki}]-R_{ij}^{\ \ kl}\omega_{kl}+R_{i}^{\ l}\omega_{lj}+R_{j}^{\ l}\omega_{il}.

We now specialize the general Weitzenböck formula to the quaternion-Kähler setting. On a quaternion-Kähler manifold of dimension
4m
, Salamon
[
36
, Theorem 3.1]
showed that the Riemannian curvature tensor can be decomposed into a scalar multiple of the curvature tensor of
\mathbb{HP}^{m}
plus the curvature tensor of a hyperkähler manifold. To be precise, let
{}^{1}I,{}^{2}I,{}^{3}I
be a local frame of

\Lambda_{+}^{2}\subset\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M
satisfying the quaternion relations. Define the tensor

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}^{\mathbb{HP}^{m}}:=g_{il}g_{jk}-g_{jl}g_{ik}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{3}{}^{\alpha}I_{il}{}^{\alpha}I_{jk}-{}^{\alpha}I_{jl}{}^{\alpha}I_{ik}-2\ {}^{\alpha}I_{ij}{}^{\alpha}I_{kl}.

This represents the curvature tensor of
\mathbb{HP}^{m}
with scalar curvature
16m(m+2)
. Thus by Salamon, we have

\displaystyle R_{ijkl}=\frac{R}{16m(m+2)}R_{ijkl}^{\mathbb{HP}^{m}}+R_{ijkl}^{\text{hyp}},

where
R_{ijkl}^{\text{hyp}}
is a section of
\text{Sym}^{2}\left(\Lambda_{-}^{2}\right).
Next we consider the action of
R_{ijkl}^{\mathbb{HP}^{m}}
on a form
\omega\in\Omega_{+}^{2}.
We compute

\displaystyle R_{ij}^{\ \ kl}\omega_{kl}=\frac{R}{16m(m+2)}(-2\omega_{ij}+(-2+8m)\omega_{ij}+4\omega_{ij})=\frac{R}{2(m+2)}\omega_{ij}.

Therefore the Riemannian zeroth-order term in the Weitzenbock formula for
\Omega_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{R}{4m}+\frac{R}{4m}-\frac{R}{2(m+2)}\right)\operatorname{id}=\frac{R}{m(m+2)}\operatorname{id}.

In particular, suppose
A
is a pseudo-holomorphic connection. Since
[\mathfrak{sp}(m),\mathfrak{sp}(1)]=0
in
\Lambda^{2}T^{\ast}M
, we have for
\omega\in\Omega_{+}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}_{E})
that

(2.8)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{A}\omega)_{ij}=(\nabla_{A}^{\ast}\nabla_{A}\omega)_{ij}-[(F_{A}^{+})_{i}^{\ k},\omega_{kj}]+[(F_{A}^{+})_{j}^{\ k},\omega_{ki}]+\frac{R}{m(m+2)}\omega.

, Lemma 12]
in general dimension. First we describe our setup. In the setup,
C
denotes a constant, depending only on
M
, which may increase from line to line.

Given an
H^{q}
connection
A
, with
q>\frac{n}{2}-1
and
q\geq 1
, we define the following quantities, cf.
[
43
, (2.10) in Def. 2.3]
. For
b\in H^{1}\left(T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{E}\right)
, set

\displaystyle\|b\|_{H_{A}^{1}}

\displaystyle:=\sqrt{\left\|\nabla_{A}b\right\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(A)

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in H^{1}\left(T^{\ast}M\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{E}\right),\\
b\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\int\langle F_{A},D_{A}b\rangle\right|}{\|b\|_{H_{A}^{1}}}.

Note that
\|b\|_{H_{A}^{1}}
and the expression in the above supremum are finite since, by Sobolev multiplication,
D_{A}
and
\nabla_{A}
map
H^{1}
to
L^{2}
. Moreover, the supremum itself is finite by Cauchy-Schwarz and the pointwise domination
|D_{A}b|\lesssim|\nabla_{A}b|
.

Moreover, we claim that
\mathcal{V}
is continuous with respect to the
H^{q}
topology.

Proof of claim.

Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in H^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{E})
. We first establish equivalence of
\|\cdot\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}
if
A_{1},A_{2}
are close in
H^{q}
. Note that

\displaystyle|\|\nabla_{A_{1}}b\|^{2}-\|\nabla_{A_{2}}b\|^{2}|

\displaystyle\leq(\|\nabla_{A_{1}}b\|+\|\nabla_{A_{2}}b\|)\|(A_{1}-A_{2})\#b\|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|b\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}}+\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}\right)\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\|b\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}.

By Sobolev embedding and the Kato inequality trick, see e.g.
[
46
, (1.16)]
,

\displaystyle\|b\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\leq C\min\left\{\|b\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}},\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}\right\}.

Hence for
\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{H^{q}}
sufficiently small such that, by Sobolev embedding,
C^{2}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\leq\varepsilon<1
,

(B.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1-\varepsilon}{1+\varepsilon}\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}^{2}\leq\|b\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}}^{2}\leq\frac{1+\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}^{2}.

Thus we have equivalence of the two norms.

We next show

(B.3)

\displaystyle\left|\int\langle F_{A_{1}},D_{A_{1}}b\rangle-\int\langle F_{A_{2}},D_{A_{2}}b\rangle\right|\leq\varepsilon\min\left\{\|b\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}},\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}\right\}.

We compute

\displaystyle\int\langle F_{A_{1}},D_{A_{1}}b\rangle-\int\langle F_{A_{2}},D_{A_{2}}b\rangle

\displaystyle=\text{I}+\text{II}+\text{III}+\text{IV}+\text{V},

where

I

\displaystyle=\int\langle F_{A_{2}},(A_{1}-A_{2})\wedge b\rangle

II

\displaystyle=\int\langle D_{A_{2}}(A_{1}-A_{2}),D_{A_{2}}b\rangle

III

\displaystyle=\int\langle D_{A_{2}}(A_{1}-A_{2}),(A_{1}-A_{2})\wedge b\rangle

IV

\displaystyle=\int\langle(A_{1}-A_{2})\wedge(A_{1}-A_{2}),D_{A_{2}}b\rangle

V

\displaystyle=\int\langle(A_{1}-A_{2})\wedge(A_{1}-A_{2}),(A_{1}-A_{2})\wedge b\rangle.

We estimate

\displaystyle|\text{I}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|F_{A_{2}}\|\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\|b\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}

\displaystyle|\text{II}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{\text{ref}}(A_{1}-A_{2})\|+\|A_{2}\|_{4}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{4})\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}

\displaystyle|\text{III}|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{\text{ref}}(A_{1}-A_{2})\|+\|A_{2}\|_{4}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{4})\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\|b\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}

\displaystyle|\text{IV}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{4}^{2}\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}

\displaystyle|\text{V}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{4}^{2}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\|b\|_{\frac{2n}{n-2}}.

Since
q\geq 1
and
q>\frac{n}{2}-1
, we have

\displaystyle\|F_{A_{2}}\|+\|A_{2}\|_{4}\leq C_{q,M}\|A_{2}\|_{H^{q}}

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\text{ref}}(A_{1}-A_{2})\|+\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{4}+\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{n}\leq C_{q,M}\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{H^{q}}.

Hence if
\|A_{1}-A_{2}\|_{H^{q}}
is small enough,

\displaystyle|\text{I}|+|\text{II}|+|\text{III}|+|\text{IV}|+|\text{V}|\leq\varepsilon\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}.

Thus by symmetry of the preceding computations

\displaystyle\left|\int\langle F_{A_{1}},D_{A_{1}}b\rangle-\int\langle F_{A_{2}},D_{A_{2}}b\rangle\right|\leq\varepsilon\min\left\{\|b\|_{H_{A_{1}}^{1}},\|b\|_{H_{A_{2}}^{1}}\right\},

which is the bound (
B.3
) that we wanted to show.

Continuity of
\mathcal{V}
now readily follows from (
B.2
) and (
B.3
), so the claim holds, as desired.

\diamond

We further have that
\mathcal{V}
is invariant under the action of
H^{q+1}
gauge transformations, which follows from

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\int\langle F_{u(A)},D_{u(A)}b\rangle\right|}{\left(\left\|\nabla_{u(A)}b\right\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\left|\int\langle u(F_{A}),u(D_{A}u^{-1}(b))\rangle\right|}{\left(\left\|u\left(\nabla_{A}u^{-1}(b)\right)\right\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}=\frac{\left|\int\langle F_{A},D_{A}u^{-1}(b)\rangle\right|}{\left(\left\|\nabla_{A}u^{-1}(b)\right\|^{2}+\|u^{-1}(b)\|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Lastly, if we also have
F_{A}\in H^{q}
, whence
D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}\in H^{q-1}\subset L^{2}
, then note that

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(A)\leq\left\|D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}\right\|.

We now give our variant of the Råde-Yang-Simon Łojasiewicz inequality.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
q>\frac{n}{2}-1
and
q\geq 1
. Given a fixed Yang-Mills connection
A_{1}\in H^{q}(\mathcal{A}_{E}),
there exists a gauge-invariant
H^{q}
-neighborhood
U
of
A_{1}
and a constant
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)

such that for
A\in U
,

(B.4)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{YM}(A)-\mathcal{YM}(A_{1})\right|^{1-\theta}\leq\mathcal{V}(A).

Proof.

This is a straightforward adaptation of the arguments of
[
34
, Prop. 7.2]
and
[
51
, Lemma 12]
, based on
[
38
, Theorem 3]
, which we include for the sake of completeness.
In the sequel,
C
denotes a positive constant, whose dependencies include
A_{1},E,q
, and the geometry of
M
, which may increase from line to line.

We first reduce proving (
B.4
) to a simpler setting. Since the desired inequality (
B.4
) is gauge-invariant, and since
H^{q}
Yang-Mills connections are smooth modulo
H^{q+1}
gauge, we may WLOG assume that
A_{1}
is smooth. Furthermore, the standard Coulomb gauge-fixing argument, e.g.
[
13
, Prop. 2.3.4]
, implies that there is an
H^{q}
neighborhood
U
of
A_{1}
such that if
A\in U
, then
A
is gauge-equivalent to a connection
B
which satisfies
D_{A_{1}}^{\ast}(B-A_{1})=0
. Again by gauge-invariance, it suffices to prove (
B.4
) for
A
satisfying the gauge-fixing condition. Finally, by continuity of
\mathcal{V}
, there exists an
H^{q}
-neighborhood of
A_{1}
such that for
A
in the neighborhood

\displaystyle\|D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}\|_{H^{-1}}\leq 2\mathcal{V}(A),

so it suffices to prove (
B.4
) with
\|D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}\|_{H^{-1}}
in place of
\mathcal{V}(A)
.

Now that we have reduced the proof of (
B.4
) to an easier setting, we next carry out the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction step of Simon’s argument. In the sequel, we change our reference connection to be the smooth connection
A_{1}
, and hence define Sobolev norms with respect to
A_{1}
. Since
M
is compact, the norms induced by
\nabla_{\text{ref}}
and
\nabla_{A_{1}}
are equivalent.

By Sobolev multiplication, the mapping
a\mapsto\mathcal{YM}(A)
, where
\nabla_{A}=\nabla_{A_{1}}+a
, is a well-defined, polynomial function on
H^{q}
.
We define maps

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle:H^{q}\to H^{q-2}

\displaystyle M(a)

\displaystyle:=D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A},

\displaystyle\tilde{M}

\displaystyle:H^{q}\to H^{q-2}

\displaystyle\tilde{M}(a)

\displaystyle:=D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}+D_{A_{1}}D_{A_{1}}^{\ast}a

\displaystyle L_{A}

\displaystyle:H^{q}\to H^{q-2}

\displaystyle L_{A}(b)

\displaystyle:=D_{A}^{\ast}D_{A}b+D_{A_{1}}D_{A_{1}}^{\ast}b+(-1)^{3n+1}\ast(b\wedge\ast F_{A}).

Observe that
L_{A}
is the linearization of
\tilde{M}
at
a
, i.e.,

\displaystyle L_{A}=(\nabla\tilde{M})(a).

Now,
L_{A_{1}}
is self-adjoint and elliptic, so by elliptic theory

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:=\text{ker}(L_{A_{1}})

is of finite dimension and consists of smooth sections. Define

\displaystyle\Pi

\displaystyle:H^{q}\to\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\Pi(a)

\displaystyle:=\text{proj}_{\mathcal{H}}^{L^{2}}(a)

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle:H^{q}\to H^{q-2}

\displaystyle N

\displaystyle:=\tilde{M}+\Pi.

Note that the linearization of
N
at
a=0
, i.e
(\nabla N)(0)
, equals
L_{A_{1}}+\Pi
, which is invertible. Thus as in

[
38
]
, there exist open sets
U_{1}\subset H^{q},U_{2}\subset H^{q-2}
, with
0\in U_{i}
, and a diffeomorphism

\displaystyle\Psi:U_{2}\to U_{1}

which is inverse to
N
. Next, set

\displaystyle V:=\text{ker}(D_{A_{1}}^{\ast})\subset H^{q},

and let
\psi_{1},\dots,\psi_{m}
be an
L^{2}
-orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}\cap V
. For
\delta>0
small enough, we have a real-analytic
[
38
, (2.12)]
function

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle:B_{\delta}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}\to{\mathbb{R}}

\displaystyle F(\xi)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{YM}\left(A_{1}+\Psi\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi^{j}\psi_{j}\right)\right).

This completes the Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction step.

Now that we have the existence of the local inverse
\Psi
of
N
, we next show the following smoothing estimates for
\Psi
, which are analogues of
[
38
, (2.9) and (2.10)]
: for
a,b\in U_{1}

(B.5)

\displaystyle\|\Psi(a)-\Psi(b)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a-b\|_{H^{-1}},

(B.6)

\displaystyle\|\Psi(a)-\Psi(b)\|_{H^{q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a-b\|_{H^{q-2}}.

We first consider (
B.5
). By elliptic theory we have for
v\in H^{q}

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|(L_{A_{1}}+\Pi)v\|_{H^{-1}}.

Then by Sobolev multiplication, for a given
\varepsilon>0
, we may shrink
U_{1},U_{2}
so that for
a\in U_{1}
and
v\in H^{q}

\displaystyle\|((\nabla\tilde{M})(a)-L_{A_{1}})(v)\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\nabla_{A_{1}}a)\#v+a\#\nabla_{A_{1}}v+a\#a\#v\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla_{A_{1}}a\|_{H^{q-1}}+\|a\#a\|_{H^{q-1}})\|v\|_{H^{1}}+C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|\nabla_{A_{1}}v\|_{H^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|a\|_{H^{q}}+\|a\|_{H^{q}}^{2})\|v\|_{H^{1}}+C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|v\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|v\|_{H^{1}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\|(L_{A_{1}}+\Pi)v\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|((\nabla N)(a))v\|_{H^{-1}}+\|((\nabla\tilde{M})(a)-L_{A_{1}})(v)\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|((\nabla N)(a))v\|_{H^{-1}}+\frac{1}{2C}\|v\|_{H^{1}}.

We deduce that for
w\in H^{q-2}

\displaystyle\|((\nabla N)(a))^{-1}w\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|w\|_{H^{-1}}.

We may further shrink
U_{1},U_{2}
so that
U_{2}
is an open ball about
0
, which is convex and hence
\Psi
is defined on
a+t(b-a)
for
a,b\in U_{2},t\in[0,1]
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Psi(a)-\Psi(b)\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}t\|((\nabla\Psi)(a+t(b-a)))(b-a)\|_{H^{1}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t\|((\nabla N)(\Psi(a+t(b-a))))^{-1}(b-a)\|_{H^{1}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\|b-a\|_{H^{-1}}.

Thus (
B.5
) holds.

The other smoothing estimate (
B.6
) follows from a similar Sobolev multiplication argument. Thus we have the desired smoothing estimates for
\Psi
.

Now that we have smoothing estimates for
\Psi
, we proceed with proving the Łojasiewicz inequality, which we only need to do for
a\in U_{1}\cap V
. We will do this, as in
[
38
]
, by establishing the following two inequalities: given
a\in U_{1}\cap V
, upon which we have
\xi\in B_{\delta}(0)
defined by
\Pi(a)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi^{j}\psi_{j}
,

(B.7)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a)-\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+\Psi(\Pi(a))|\leq C\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}

(B.8)

\displaystyle|(\nabla F)(\xi)|\leq C\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}.

We first consider (
B.7
). We compute as in as in
[
38
, (2.31)]

\displaystyle|\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+\Psi(\Pi(a)))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a+t(\Psi(\Pi(a))-a))\,dt\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{M}\langle M(a+t(\Psi(\Pi(a))-a)),\Psi(\Pi(a))-a\rangle\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|M(a+t(\Psi(\Pi(a))-a)\|_{H^{-1}}\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-a\|_{H^{1}}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\|M(a+t(\Psi(\Pi(a))-\Psi(N(a)))\|_{H^{-1}}\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-\Psi(N(a))\|_{H^{1}}\,dt.

Denoting

\displaystyle b=t(\Psi(\Pi(a))-\Psi(N(a))),

we next estimate
\|M(a+b)\|_{H^{-1}}
. We compute

\displaystyle M(a+b)

\displaystyle=D_{A}^{\ast}F_{A}+(D_{A}^{\ast}D_{A}b+\nabla_{A}b\#b+F_{A}\#b+b\#b\#b)

\displaystyle=M(a)

\displaystyle+(D_{A_{1}}^{\ast}D_{A_{1}}b+(\nabla_{A_{1}}a)\#b+a\#\nabla_{A_{1}}b+a\#a\#b+(\nabla_{A_{1}}b)\#b+a\#b\#b+F_{A}\#b+b\#b\#b).

Since
q>\frac{n}{2}-1
and
q\geq 1
, we may estimate the
H^{-1}
norm of the terms in the parentheses using Sobolev multiplication to obtain

\displaystyle\|D_{A_{1}}^{\ast}D_{A_{1}}b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|(\nabla_{A_{1}}a)\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{A_{1}}a\|_{H^{q-1}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|a\#\nabla_{A_{1}}b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|\nabla_{A_{1}}b\|_{H^{0}}\leq C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|a\#a\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\#a\|_{H^{q-1}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|a\|_{H^{q}}^{2}\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|(\nabla_{A_{1}}b)\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{H^{q}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|a\#b\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|a\|_{H^{q}}\|b\|_{H^{q}}\|b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\|F_{A}\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|F_{A}\|_{H^{q-1}}\|b\|_{H^{1}}\leq C(1+\|a\|_{H^{q}}^{2})\|b\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\|b\#b\#b\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|b\|_{H^{q}}^{2}\|b\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus if
\|a\|_{H^{q}}+\|b\|_{H^{q}}\leq 1
,

(B.9)

\displaystyle\|M(a+b)-M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C\|b\|_{H^{1}}.

Now, we can make
\|a\|_{H^{q}}
small by shrinking
U_{1},U_{2}
. Moreover, since we assumed
a\in V
, we have
M(a)=\tilde{M}(a)=N(a)-\Pi(a)
, so the smoothing estimate (
B.6
) yields

\displaystyle\|b\|_{H^{q}}\leq C\|\Pi(a)-N(a)\|_{H^{q-2}}=C\|M(a)\|_{H^{q-2}}.

Thus, since
A_{1}
is Yang-Mills, we can make
\|M(a)\|_{H^{q-2}}
small by shrinking
U_{1},U_{2}
. We may assume
\|a\|_{H^{q}}+\|b\|_{H^{q}}\leq 1
and consequently the bound

\displaystyle\|M(a+b)\|_{H^{-1}}\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|b\|_{H^{1}}).

Therefore, the other smoothing estimate (
B.5
) yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+\Psi(\Pi(a)))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a)|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|b\|_{H^{1}})\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-\Psi(N(a))\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}})\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle=C\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}^{2}.

This establishes (
B.7
).

We next establish (
B.8
). Given
\xi\in B_{\delta}(0)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
, set
\iota(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\xi^{j}\psi_{j}
. Observe that

\displaystyle|(\partial_{i}F)(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int\langle M(\Psi(\iota(\xi))),\xi^{i}((d\Psi)(\iota(\xi)))(\psi_{i})\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq|\xi|\|M(\Psi(\iota(\xi)))\|_{H^{-1}}\|((d\Psi)(\iota(\xi)))(\psi_{i})\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C|\xi|\|M(\Psi(\iota(\xi)))\|_{H^{-1}}.

Now,
a\in V
, then
\Pi(a)\in\mathcal{H}\cap V
, so there exists
\xi
such that
\iota(\xi)=\Pi(a)
. We compute as in
[
38
, (2.20)]
that

\displaystyle|(\nabla F)(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|M(\Psi(\Pi(a))\|_{H^{-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|M(\Psi(\Pi(a)))-M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}).

By (
B.9
), we have that if

\displaystyle\|a\|_{H^{q}}+\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-a\|_{H^{q}}\leq 1,

then

\displaystyle\|M(\Psi(\Pi(a)))-M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}\leq\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-a\|_{H^{1}}.

We already have that
\|a\|_{H^{q}}
is small. Moreover, since
a=\Psi(N(a))
, and since
N(a)-\Pi(a)=M(a)
by the assumption
a\in V
, the smoothing estimate (
B.6
) yields

\displaystyle\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-a\|_{H^{q}}=\|\Psi(\Pi(a))-\Psi(N(a))\|_{H^{q}}\leq C\|\Pi(a)-N(a)\|_{H^{q-2}}\leq C\|M(a)\|_{H^{q-2}},

which is also small. Hence by the other smoothing estimate (
B.5
),

\displaystyle|\nabla F(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|\Psi(\Pi(a)))-a\|_{H^{1}})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}+\|\Pi(a)-N(a)\|_{H^{-1}})

\displaystyle=C\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}.

This establishes (
B.8
).

Finally, now that we have (
B.7
) and (
B.8
), the same computation as in
[
38
, (2.22)]
yields

\displaystyle|\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a)-\mathcal{YM}(A_{1})|^{1-\theta}\leq C\|M(a)\|_{H^{-1}}.

Since
\mathcal{YM}(A_{1}+a)\to\mathcal{YM}(A_{1})
uniformly in
a
, we may take
\theta
smaller and shrink
U_{1}
to remove the constant
C
in the estimate. The lemma thus follows.
∎

We also have the following uniform statement for the flat connections.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
q>\lceil\frac{n}{2}-1\rceil
and
q\geq 1
.
Given a
(G,\rho)
-bundle
E\to M
, let
\mathcal{F}\subset H^{q}\left(\mathcal{A}_{E}\right)

denote the set of flat connections. There exists
\theta_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and a gauge-invariant
H^{q}
-neighborhood
U\supset\mathcal{F}
such that for all
A\in U
, we have

(B.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{YM}(A)^{1-\theta_{0}}\leq\mathcal{V}(A).

Moreover, the solution of (
1.1
) starting at
A\in U
exists for all time and converges polynomially to a flat connection in
H^{q}
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that there does not exist
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that every
B\in\mathcal{F}
has an
H^{q}
neighborhood
U_{B}
for which the inequality (
B.10
) holds. Then there exists
\theta_{i}\searrow 0
and a sequence of flat connections
B_{i}
such that for each
i
, the inequality (
B.10
) with
\theta_{0}=\theta_{i}
fails on every neighborhood of
B_{i}
. Since the
B_{i}
are all flat, strong Uhlenbeck compactness implies that we may pass to a subsequence, also labeled by
i
, such that there exist gauge transformations
u_{i}\in H^{q+1}
for which
u_{i}(B_{i})
converge in
H^{q}
to some flat connection
B_{\infty}
. By Lemma
B.2
, there exists
\theta_{\infty}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and a neighborhood
U_{B_{\infty}}
of
B_{\infty}
such that (
B.10
) holds on
U
with
\theta_{0}=\theta_{\infty}
. Thus for
i
sufficiently large, (
B.10
) holds on some neighborhood
U
of
u_{i}(B_{i})
. But (
B.10
) is gauge-invariant, so (
B.10
) holds on the neighborhood
u_{i}^{-1}(U_{B_{\infty}})
of
B_{i}
, which is a contradiction, as desired. Thus there exists
\theta_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that every
B\in\mathcal{F}
has a neighborhood
U_{B}
for which (
B.10
) holds with
\theta_{0}
.

By Theorem
B.1
, we may assume
U_{B}
is such that for all
A\in U_{B}
, the solution of (
1.1
) starting at
A
exists for all time and converges polynomially in

to a flat connection. Thus the open set

\displaystyle U:=\bigcup_{B\text{ flat, }u\in H^{q+1}(\mathcal{G}_{E})}u(U_{B})

Proof.

{\varepsilon}>0,
it suffices to determine
\delta>0
such that for
A^{\prime}_{0}\in B_{\delta}(A_{\infty}),
either alternative (1) holds for
A^{\prime}(t)
or else

(B.11)

\|A^{\prime}(t)-A_{\infty}\|<{\varepsilon}

for all
t
and
A^{\prime}(t)
converges, which is equivalent to (2).

Suppose that for
\delta_{\infty}>0
to be determined, we have

(B.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t))>\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})-\delta_{\infty}

for
t\in\left[0,\tau\right),
where
\tau\leq\infty
is the maximal such time. The two cases are distinguished by (1)
\tau<\infty
and (2)
\tau=\infty.

Let
\kappa>0
be such that the Łojasiewicz inequality (
B.4
) holds on
B_{3\kappa}(A_{\infty})
.
We assume that
\delta<\kappa
is small enough that
A^{\prime}(t)\subset B_{\kappa}(A_{\infty})
for
0\leq t\leq 1.
Now, supposing that
A^{\prime}(t)\in B_{3\kappa}(A_{\infty})
for
t
in some interval
[a,b]\subset\left[0,\tau\right)
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}

\displaystyle\left\|D_{A^{\prime}(t)}^{\ast}F_{A^{\prime}(t)}\right\|\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{z}\frac{-\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})\right)}{\left\|D_{A^{\prime}(t)}^{\ast}F_{A^{\prime}(t)}\right\|}\,dt+\int_{z}^{b}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}\left(\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})-\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t))\right)}{\left\|D_{A^{\prime}(t)}^{\ast}F_{A^{\prime}(t)}\right\|}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{z}\frac{-\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty}))}{(\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty}))^{1-\theta}}\,dt+\int_{z}^{b}\frac{\frac{d}{dt}(\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})-\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t)))}{(\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})-\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t)))^{1-\theta}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta}\left((\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(a))-\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})))^{\theta}+(\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty})-\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(b)))^{\theta}\right)

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\theta}\left((\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}_{0})-\mathcal{YM}(A_{\infty}))^{\theta}+\delta_{\infty}^{\theta}\right).

Assuming that
\delta
and
\delta_{\infty}
are sufficiently small, Theorem
A.4
shows that in fact
A^{\prime}(t)
remains inside
B_{2\kappa}(A_{\infty})
for all
t\in\left[0,\tau\right).
The rest of the argument is a standard application of the Łojasiewicz-Simon inequality and Gronwall’s inequality to
\mathcal{YM}(A^{\prime}(t)),
which we omit.
∎

1.
Introduction

Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}x^{i}
be a polynomial of degree
d
with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
. We recall the following properties:

•

We say that
f(x)
is
unimodal
, if there exist a positive integer
k
such that
a_{0}\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{k}\geq\cdots\geq a_{d}
.

•

We say that
f(x)
is
log-concave
, if
a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for all
i
.

•

We say that
f(x)
is
real-rooted
, if all its roots are real.

It is well-known that real-rootedness implies log-concavity, and that if all the
a_{i}
are positive, then log-concavity implies unimodality.
These properties frequently appear in mathematics and are actively studied using a wide range of techniques from algebra, analysis, probability theory, and combinatorics. See e.g.,
[
4
,
18
,
29
]
.
A particularly important class of polynomials where these properties have been extensively investigated is the numerator polynomial of the generating function of
f(x)
. Specifically, if

\sum_{n\geq 0}f(n)t^{n}=\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d+1}},

where
h(t)=h_{0}+h_{1}t+\cdots+h_{d}t^{d}
is a nonzero polynomial of degree at most
d
with real coefficients, then
h(t)
can be viewed as a transformation of
f(x)
under a change of basis. Indeed, since

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}h_{i}\binom{x+d-i}{d},

the polynomial
h(t)
encodes
f(x)
in a binomial coefficient basis.
As one approach to study the real-rootedness of
h(t)
, we expand the polynomial
f(x)
in a different basis as follows:

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}.

If
b_{0},\ldots,b_{d}\geq 0
, then
f(x)
is said to be
magic positive
. The term ‘magic positive’ was introduced by Ferroni and Higashitani
[
13
]
. The following theorem shows that the magic positivity of a polynomial is useful for studying the real-rootedness of
h(t)
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
,
3
,
13
]
)
.

Let
c_{0},\ldots,c_{d}\geq 0
and let
h(t)\in\mathbb{R}[t]
be the polynomial such that

\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d}c_{j}n^{j}(n+1)^{d-j}\right)t^{n}=\frac{h(t)}{(1-t)^{d+1}}.

Then
h(t)
is real-rooted.

Our main motivation comes from Ehrhart theory.
A
lattice polytope
is the convex hull of finitely many elements in a lattice contained in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, typically
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. A lattice polytope
P
is called
reflexive
if

P^{\ast}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\langle x,y\rangle\leq 1\text{ for any }x\in P\}

is also a lattice polytope, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the usual inner product of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

By a theorem of Ehrhart
[
10
]
,
|nP\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|
is given by a polynomial
E_{P}(n)
of degree
\dim P
in
n
for all integers
n>0
. The polynomial
E_{P}(n)
is called the
Ehrhart polynomial
of
P
.
If all coefficients of
E_{P}(n)
are positive, then we call
P

Ehrhart positive
. Ehrhart positivity of the polytope does not hold in general. There has been active research on characterizing which polytopes are Ehrhart positive. See, e.g.,
[
11
,
21
,
27
]
.
It is known that the generating function
1+\sum_{n\geq 1}E_{P}(n)t^{n}
of the Ehrhart polynomial takes the form of a rational function as follows:

1+\sum_{n\geq 1}E_{P}(n)t^{n}=\frac{h_{0}^{\ast}+h_{1}^{\ast}t+\cdots+h_{d}^{\ast}t^{d}}{(1-t)^{d+1}}.

The numerator polynomial of this generating function is called the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial
of
P
, denoted by
h^{\ast}_{P}(t)
.
A fundamental theorem by Stanley
[
28
]
states that the coefficients of the
h^{\ast}
-polynomial are always non-negative integers. It was proved by Hibi
[
16
]
that a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
is reflexive if and only if its
h^{\ast}
-polynomial is palindromic and has degree
d
, that is,
h^{\ast}_{P}(t)=t^{d}h^{\ast}_{P}\left(\frac{1}{t}\right)
.

In this paper, we consider the following three fundamental questions.

Question 1.2
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope.

(i)

Does there exist a positive integer
k
such that
E_{kP}(n)
is magic positive?

(ii)

If
E_{kP}(n)
is magic positive, is
E_{(k+1)P}(n)
also magic positive?

(iii)

In each dimension
d
, does there exist a positive integer
k
such that
E_{kP}(n)
is magic positive for any
d
-dimensional lattice polytope
P
?

As a similar question, whether a dilated polytope has the same properties of the original polytope (such as IDP and unimodular triangulation) has been actively studied. See, e.g.,
[
7
,
9
,
19
,
22
]
.

The following first main result provides an answer to Question
1.2
(i). That is, it shows that if a polytope which is Ehrhart positive is sufficiently dilated, then the Ehrhart polynomial of the dilated polytope becomes magic positive.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial with positive real coefficients. Then, there exists a positive real number
k
such that
f(kx)
is magic positive.

The following second main result provides an answer to Question
1.2
(ii). That is, it shows that once the Ehrhart polynomial of a dilated polytope becomes magic positive, it remains magic positive under all subsequent dilations.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
f(x)
be a polynomial with positive real coefficients. If there exists a positive real number
k
such that
f(kx)
is magic positive, then so is
f(k^{\prime}x)
for any
k^{\prime}\geq k
.

The following third main result provides an answer to Question
1.2
(iii). That is, it shows that in each dimension
d\geq 3
, there does not exist a fixed integer
k>0
such that the Ehrhart polynomial of
kP
is magic positive.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of dimension
d
which is Ehrhart positive. Then we have the following:

(1)

For
d=2
,
E_{2P}(n)
is always magic positive.

(2)

For any
d\geq 3
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, there exists a lattice polytope
P
such that
E_{kP}(n)
is not magic positive.

From Theorem 1.3, if a polytope which is Ehrhart positive is sufficiently dilated, then its Ehrhart polynomial has magic positivity. Moreover, from Theorem 1.4, once the Ehrhart polynomial has magic positivity due to the dilation of the polytope, it remains magic positive thereafter. From these, it is natural to define the following invariant:

\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:\text{$E_{kP}(n)$ is magic positive}\}.

In Section
5
, we explicitly compute
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)
for several polytopes.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
.
For the proof of Theorem
1.3
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}x^{i}
be a polynomial of degree
d
. Then we have
f(kx)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}(\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{d-j}{i-j}b_{j}k^{j})x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}
.

Proof.

Let
a_{i}
denote the coefficient of
x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}
when
f(kx)
is expanded with respect to the basis
\{x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
. Then, the transformation from the sequence
(b_{0},kb_{1},\ldots,k^{d}b_{d})
to the sequence
(a_{0},\ldots,a_{d})
is the same type of transformation as that from the
f
-vector to the
h
-vector of a simplicial complex (see
[
15
, Exercise 11.2]
). Therefore, we obtain
a_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{d-j}{i-j}b_{j}k^{j}
.
∎

By using Lemma
2.1
, we can prove Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From Lemma
2.1
, the coefficient of
x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}
when
f(kx)
is expanded with respect to the basis
\{x^{i}(x+1)^{d-i}\}_{i=0}^{d}
is given by
g_{i}(k):=\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{d-j}{i-j}b_{j}k^{j}
. By assumption,
f(x)
has positive real coefficients, that is,
b_{i}>0
for all
i
. This implies that
g_{i}(k)\geq 0
for all
i
when
k
is sufficiently large. Therefore, there exists a positive real number
k
such that
f(kx)
is magic positive.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.4
.
For the proof of Theorem
1.4
, we use the following variant of Farkas’ lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
23
, Proposition 6.4.3]
)
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Then exactly one of the following two assertions is true:

(i)

There exists an
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
A\mathbf{x}\leq\mathbf{b}
and
\mathbf{x}\geq 0
, or

(ii)

There exists a
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that
\mathbf{y}^{T}A\geq 0,\mathbf{y}\geq 0
and
\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{b}<0
.

By using Lemma
3.1
, we can prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose that
f(kx)
is magic positive and
f(k^{\prime}x)
is not magic positive for some
k^{\prime}>k
.
That is, by Lemma
2.1
,
\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{i-j}\binom{d-j}{i-j}b_{j}k^{j}\geq 0
holds for any
i=0,\ldots,d
, and there exists some
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
such that
\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}b_{j}(k^{\prime})^{j}<0
holds. Under the notation of Lemma
3.1
, we define
A\in\mathbb{R}^{(\ell+1)\times\ell}
and
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rl}A=&\begin{pmatrix}-k&0&\cdots&0&0\\
\binom{d-1}{1}k&-k^{2}&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
(-1)^{\ell}\binom{d-1}{\ell-1}k&(-1)^{\ell-1}\binom{d-2}{\ell-2}k^{2}&\cdots&\binom{d-\ell+1}{1}k^{\ell-1}&-k^{\ell}\\
(-1)^{\ell-1}\binom{d-1}{\ell-1}k^{\prime}&(-1)^{\ell-2}\binom{d-2}{\ell-2}(k^{\prime})^{2}&\cdots&-\binom{d-\ell+1}{1}(k^{\prime})^{\ell-1}&(k^{\prime})^{\ell}\end{pmatrix}\hskip 5.0pt\text{and}\hskip 5.0pt\mathbf{b}=\begin{pmatrix}-d\\
\binom{d}{2}\\
\vdots\\
(-1)^{\ell}\binom{d}{\ell}\\
(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{d}{\ell}\end{pmatrix},\end{array}

where the
(i,j)
-entry of
A
is
(-1)^{i-j+1}\binom{d-j}{i-j}k^{j}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
, the
(\ell+1,j)
-entry of
A
is
(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}(k^{\prime})^{j}
, the
i
-th entry of the vector
\mathbf{b}
is
(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\ell
and the
(\ell+1)
-th entry of
\mathbf{b}
is
(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{d}{\ell}
. Let
\mathbf{x}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{\ell})^{T}
. Then we see that the condition (i) of Lemma
3.1
holds. For
1\leq i\leq\ell
, let
y_{i}=\binom{d-i}{\ell-i}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i}(k^{\prime})^{i}
and
y_{\ell+1}=k^{\ell}
, where
y_{j}
denotes the
j
-th entry of the vector
\mathbf{y}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell+1
. Then, the
j
-th entry of the vector
\mathbf{y}^{T}A
is the following:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{T}A

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\binom{d-i}{\ell-i}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i}(k^{\prime})^{i}\cdot(-1)^{i-j+1}\binom{d-j}{i-j}k^{j}\right)+k^{\ell}\cdot(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}(k^{\prime})^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-j}\left((-1)^{i+1}\binom{d-j}{i}k^{j}\binom{d-i-j}{d-\ell}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i-j}(k^{\prime})^{i+j}\right)+(-1)^{\ell-j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}(k^{\prime})^{j}k^{\ell}

\displaystyle=k^{j}(k^{\prime})^{j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-j}\left((-1)^{i+1}\binom{\ell-j}{i}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i-j}(k^{\prime})^{i}\right)+(-1)^{\ell-j}k^{\ell-j}\right)

\displaystyle=k^{j}(k^{\prime})^{j}\binom{d-j}{\ell-j}(-((k^{\prime}-k)-k^{\prime})^{\ell-j}+(-1)^{\ell-j}k^{\ell-j})=0.

Furthermore,
\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{b}
is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}^{T}\mathbf{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\binom{d-i}{\ell-i}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i}(k^{\prime})^{i}\cdot(-1)^{i}\binom{d}{i}+(-1)^{\ell+1}\binom{d}{\ell}k^{\ell}

\displaystyle=-\binom{d}{\ell}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{i+1}\binom{\ell}{i}(k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell-i}(k^{\prime})^{i}+(-1)^{\ell}k^{\ell}\right)

\displaystyle=-\binom{d}{\ell}\left((k^{\prime}-k)^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell+1}k^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}k^{\ell}\right)

From these calculations and the fact that
\mathbf{y}\geq 0
, the condition (ii) of Lemma
3.1
follows. This contradicts the fact that (i) and (ii) of Lemma
3.1
cannot hold simultaneously. Therefore,
f(k^{\prime}x)
is magic positive for any
k^{\prime}\geq k
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section, we give a proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(1)

Let
P
be a lattice polytope of dimension 2. Then we have

E_{kP}(n)=\binom{kn+2}{2}+a\binom{kn+1}{2}+b\binom{kn}{2}

where
a
and
b
are non-negative integers satisfying
a\geq b
(see
[
15
, Proposition 36.2]
). This expression can be rewritten as follows:

E_{kP}(n)=(n+1)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}((a-b+3)k-4)n(n+1)+\frac{1}{2}((a+b+1)k^{2}-(a-b+3)k+2)n^{2}.

Since
a,b\geq 0
, we see that

\frac{1}{2}\left((a+b+1)k^{2}-(a-b+3)k+2\right)=\binom{k-1}{2}+a\binom{k}{2}+b\binom{k+1}{2}\geq 0

for all
k\geq 1
. Therefore,
E_{kP}(n)
is magic positive if and only if
(a-b+3)k-4\geq 0
. That is, it suffices to have
k\geq\frac{4}{a-b+3}
. Since
a-b\geq 0
,
\frac{4}{a-b+3}
takes its maximum value
\frac{4}{3}
when
a=b
. Hence, we conclude that
E_{2P}(n)
is magic positive.

(2)

Let

P_{q,d}=\operatorname{conv}(\mathbf{e}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d},-q(\mathbf{e}_{1}+\cdots+\mathbf{e}_{d}))

where
\mathbf{e}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
h^{\ast}
-polynomial of this polytope is computed in
[
17
]
and is given by
h^{\ast}_{P_{q,d}}(t)=1+qt+\cdots+qt^{d}
. Thus, the Ehrhart polynomial is

E_{P_{q,d}}(n)=\binom{n+d}{d}+\sum_{i=1}^{d}q\binom{n+d-i}{d}.

First, we confirm that
P_{q,d}
is Ehrhart positive. It is clear that all the coefficients of
\binom{n+d}{d}
are positive.
Moreover, there exist
a_{0},\ldots,a_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}>0
such that the following equation holds:

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{d}\binom{n+d-i}{d}=\binom{n+d}{d+1}-\binom{n}{d+1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d-1}{2}\right\rfloor}a_{j}n^{d-2j}.

Therefore,
P_{q,d}
is Ehrhart positive.

Let
E_{P_{q,d}}(kn)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}c_{i}n^{i}(n+1)^{d-i}
.
When
d
is an odd integer with
d\geq 3
, the coefficient
c_{2}
takes the following form by Lemma
2.1
and the above discussion:

c_{2}=b_{2}k^{2}-\binom{d-1}{1}b_{1}k+\binom{d}{2}

where
b_{2}
is constant and
b_{1}
is a linear polynomial in
q
with positive leading coefficient from (4.1). Hence, as
q
becomes large, the value of
k
required to make
c_{2}
positive also increases. Therefore, Theorem
1.5
holds when
d
is odd.

When
d
is an even integer with
d\geq 4
, the coefficient
c_{3}
takes the following form by Lemma
2.1
and the above discussion:

c_{3}=b_{3}k^{3}-\binom{d-2}{1}b_{2}k^{2}+\binom{d-1}{2}b_{1}k-\binom{d}{3}

where
b_{3},b_{1}
are constant, and
b_{2}
is a linear polynomial in
q
with positive leading coefficient from (4.1). By a similar argument to the odd case, Theorem
1.5
also holds when
d
is even.

∎

5.
The Case of Some Dilated Polytopes

From Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4
, it is natural to define the following invariant for a lattice polytope
P
which is Ehrhart positive:

\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P):=\min\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:\text{$E_{kP}(n)$ is magic positive}\}.

If there exists a positive integer
k
such that
E_{kP}(n)
is magic positive, then it follows that
E_{P}(n)
is Ehrhart positive. Therefore, we see that computing
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)
is more difficult than proving the Ehrhart positivity of
P
.

In general, if the volume of a lattice polytope
P
is less than 1, since the constant term of the Ehrhart polynomial is 1, the Ehrhart polynomial of
P
is not magic positive. Therefore, the Ehrhart polynomial of
(0,1)
-polytope is never magic positive except for
[0,1]^{d}
. Furthermore, using the following proposition, we see that if a lattice polytope
P
is reflexive and has volume less than 2, then its Ehrhart polynomial is never magic positive.

Proposition 5.1

(
[
2
, Proposition 4.11]
)
.

Let
P
be a
d
-dimensional lattice polytope and let

E_{P}(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}n^{i}(1+n)^{d-i}

be its Ehrhart polynomial. Then
P
is reflexive if and only if
a_{j}=a_{d-j}
for any
j
.

In this section, we discuss
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)
of some examples of
(0,1)
-polytopes and reflexive polytopes
P
.

5.1.
Standard simplex

The
d
-dimensional
standard simplex
, denoted by
\Delta_{d}
, is the convex hull of
\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d+1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
:

\Delta_{d}:=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf{e}_{1},\mathbf{e}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{e}_{d+1}\}.

The Ehrhart polynomial of
\Delta_{d}
is given by:

E_{\Delta_{d}}(x)=\binom{x+d}{d}.

Proposition 5.2
.

We have
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(\Delta_{d})=d
.

Proof.

The Ehrhart polynomial of
k\Delta_{d}
is as follows:

E_{k\Delta_{d}}(x)=E_{\Delta_{d}}(kx)=\binom{kx+d}{d}=\frac{1}{d!}\prod_{j=0}^{d-1}((d-j)(x+1)+(k-d+j)x).

Since
0\leq j\leq d-1
, we have
1\leq d-j\leq d
. Therefore, we have
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(\Delta_{d})=d
.
∎

5.2.
Base polytope of minimal matroid

We refer the reader to
[
26
]
for the introduction to matroids. It is known that if
M
is a connected matroid with
n
elements of rank
k
, then
|\mathcal{B}(M)|\geq k(n-k)+1
, where
\mathcal{B}(M)
is the set of bases of
M
. Furthermore, there is a unique (up to isomorphism) connected matroid of size
n
of rank
k
for which equality holds. See, e.g.,
[
8
,
24
]
. These matroids are referred to as the
minimal matroids
denoted by
T_{k,n}
. It is clear from the minimality property of these matroids that the dual of the minimal matroid
T_{k,n}
is isomorphic to
T_{n-k,n}
. The
base polytope
of a matroid
M
, denoted by
B(M)
, is the convex hull of all indicator vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, that is
e_{B}:=\sum_{b\in B}e_{b}
for
B\in\mathcal{B}(M)
.

The Ehrhart polynomial of
B(T_{k,n})
, denoted by
D_{k,n}(x)
, is calculated in
[
12
]
as follows:

D_{k,n}(x)=\frac{1}{\binom{n-1}{k-1}}\binom{x+n-k}{n-k}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{n-k-1+j}{j}\binom{x+j}{j}.

In
[
12
]
, it is known that
B(T_{k,n})
is Ehrhart positive.

For the proof of Proposition
5.4
, we use the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
f(x)
and
g(x)
be polynomials with positive real coefficients of degree
n
and
m
, respectively. If
f(x)
and
g(x)
are magic positive, then so is
f(x)g(x)
.

Proof.

Since
f(x)
and
g(x)
are magic positive, there exist
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b_{0},\ldots,b_{m}\geq 0
such that

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}(x+1)^{n-i}\hskip 5.0pt\text{and}\hskip 5.0ptg(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{i}x^{i}(x+1)^{m-i}.

Then we have

5.7.
CL-polytope

A reflexive polytope
P
is said to be a
CL-polytope
if all of the complex roots of the Ehrhart polynomial
E_{P}(n)
lie on the line
\mathrm{Re}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}
in the complex plane, where
\mathrm{Re}(z)
denotes the real part of a complex number
z
.
In
[
13
, Lemma 4.15]
, it is known that if a reflexive polytope
P
is a CL-polytope, then
P
is Ehrhart positive.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
f(x)
be a quadratic polynomial with roots
-\frac{1}{2}\pm b\sqrt{-1}
. Then
f(kx)
is magic positive if and only if
k\geq\frac{1}{2}+2b^{2}
.

Proof.

Since the roots of
f(x)
are
-\frac{1}{2}\pm b\sqrt{-1}
, we have

\displaystyle f(kx)

\displaystyle=a\left(kx+\frac{1}{2}+bi\right)\left(kx+\frac{1}{2}-bi\right)

\displaystyle=a\left(\left(\frac{1}{4}+b^{2}\right)(x+1)^{2}+\left(k-\frac{1}{2}-2b^{2}\right)x(x+1)+\left(\left(k-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+b^{2}\right)x^{2}\right),

where
a
is a positive real number. Therefore,
f(kx)
is magic positive if and only if
k\geq\frac{1}{2}+2b^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 5.11

(
[
6
]
)
.

The roots
\alpha
of an Ehrhart polynomial of degree
d
satisfy the inequality
|\alpha+\frac{1}{2}|\leq d(d-\frac{1}{2})
.

By Lemma
5.3
, Lemma
5.10
and Proposition
5.11
, the following follows.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
P
be a CL-polytope of dimension
d
. Then
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)
admits an upper bound that depends only on
d
.

Proof.

For real numbers
b_{1},\ldots,b_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}
, define

f_{i}(x)=\left(x+\frac{1}{2}+b_{i}\sqrt{-1}\right)\left(x+\frac{1}{2}-b_{i}\sqrt{-1}\right).

Since
P
is a CL-polytope,
E_{P}(x)
can be expressed as

E_{P}(x)=b(x)\prod_{i=1}^{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}f_{i}(x),

where for a positive rational number
a
,
b(x)=a
if
d
is even and
b(x)=a(2x+1)
otherwise.
By Lemma
5.10
, each
f_{i}(kx)
is magic positive for all
k\geq\frac{1}{2}+2b_{i}^{2}
.
Let
b=\max\{b_{0},\ldots,b_{\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor}\}
. Then, by Lemma
5.3
, we have
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)\leq\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}+2b^{2}\right\rceil
. Moreover, by Proposition
5.11
, since
b\leq d(d-\frac{1}{2})
, it follows that
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)\leq\left\lceil\frac{1}{2}+2d^{2}(d-\frac{1}{2})^{2}\right\rceil
.
∎

In general, there is no general upper bound for the
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}
of a polytope depending only on the dimension. However, for CL-polytopes, such a bound does exist.

Through the examples in Chapter 5, we see that it seems difficult to explicitly determine
\mathrm{m\mbox{-}index}(P)
for a lattice polytope

.

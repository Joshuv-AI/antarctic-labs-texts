Cyclic coverings of degree 6 of curves of genus 2
Paweł Borówka, Juan Carlos Naranjo, Angela Ortega, Anatoli Shatsila

Abstract
. We prove that the Prym map corresponding to étale cyclic coverings of degree 6 over a genus 2 curve is generically injective.

1.
Introduction

Algebraic curves that admit automorphisms are interesting examples and can be used to construct coverings. A special role can be given to étale cyclic coverings of genus 2 curves because they appear as sections of (non-principal) line bundles on abelian surfaces.

Coverings of curves are also a starting point of the Prym theory, which relates them to polarised abelian varieties. More precisely, let
f:{\widetilde{C}}\to H
be a covering and consider the norm map
\Nm_{f}:J{\widetilde{C}}\to JH
given by
\Nm_{f}(\sum P_{i})=\sum f(P_{i})
, which is a well-defined group homomorphism. The connected component of its kernel containing
0
, denoted
P({\widetilde{C}}/H)
is called the Prym variety of the covering.
The Prym map associates to a covering
f:{\widetilde{C}}\to H
its Prym variety
P({\widetilde{C}}/H)
and one of the aims of the Prym theory is to understand the geometry of the fibres of the Prym map.

Historically, the first studied examples were étale double coverings, providing a simple way
(after Jacobians) of constructing principally polarised abelian varieties. Recently other types of coverings have gained a lot of attention. In
[
Ago20
]
, it has been shown that cyclic étale coverings of genus 2 curves have a generically finite Prym map if the degree is at least
6
. In
[
LO16
]
authors showed that the degree of the Prym map for
7:1
cyclic coverings equals 10.
In
[
NOS24
]
and more recently in
[
NOPS25
]
, the generic injectivity for odd prime degrees
\geq 11
is proven. There is also the generic injectivity for
d=9
showed in
[
NOS24
]
. In fact, the proof in
[
NOPS25
]
works for odd prime coverings of hyperelliptic curves of any genus under some mild numerical restrictions on the degree of the covering.

In this paper we focus on the boundary case, namely cyclic étale degree 6 coverings of genus 2 curves. In this case, as predicted in
[
ES93
]
, the Prym variety is completely decomposable, see also
[
HS25
]
.

Indeed, we show (Section
2
) that the Prym variety
P({\widetilde{C}}/H)
of a
6:1
covering of the genus 2 curve is isogenous to the product
E\times F^{2}\times G^{2}
,
where
E,F
are elliptic curves that are Galois quotients of
{\widetilde{C}}
but
G
is not a Galois quotient. Using this decomposition we are able to prove the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Thm
3.8
)
.

By
\mathcal{R}_{2}[6]
let us denote the moduli of degree 6 cyclic coverings of genus 2 curves and by
\mathcal{A}_{5}^{(1,1,1,1,6)}
the moduli of
(1,1,1,1,6)
polarised abelian fivefolds.

Then, the Prym map
\mathcal{R}_{2}[6]\to\mathcal{A}_{5}^{(1,1,1,1,6)}
is generically injective.

The generic injectivity of this Prym map was unexpected and a bit surprising, given the fact that for coverings of degree 7 the Prym map is of degree 10. Notice that in particular, this result implies that we have a three-dimensional family of abelian fivefolds whose members are isogenous to a product of five elliptic curves.

In Section 2 we present the tower of coverings associated
to the action of the dihedral group on
\widetilde{C}
and the decompositions of the various Prym varieties appearing in the picture.
In Section 3 we study the pullback of the polarisation of the Prym variety under the isomorphism
JC_{0}\times JC_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}P

to the product of Jacobians and characterise the automorphisms of
JC_{1}
as ppav (see Proposition
3.5
).
This is the key result in reconstructing the covering
\widetilde{C}\rightarrow H
from the polarised abelian variety
P(\widetilde{C}/H)

and which has no obvious generalisation to
higher genus curves
\widetilde{C}
admitting an action of the dihedral group.

3.1.
Cyclic coverings of degree 6

From now on, we focus on degree six coverings, so we assume

Proposition 3.4
.

The morphism

\Phi:\mathcal{R}_{2}[6]\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{3},\,(H,\langle\eta\rangle)\mapsto JC_{1}

.

To finish the proof, note that any automorphism of
JC_{1}
is uniquely determined by its values on the elliptic curves
E,F_{1}
and
G^{\prime}
, as they generate
JC_{1}
. We have that
-1_{JC_{1}}
restricts to the three elliptic curves as
-1
while

\sigma^{3}
restricts to
F_{1}
as 1 and to
E+G^{\prime}=P(C_{1}/F_{1})
as
-1
. Their composition
-\sigma^{3}
restricts to
F_{1}
as
-1
and to
E+G^{\prime}=P(C_{1}/F_{1})
as 1 and these automorphisms fill up all possible choices of
\pm 1
on elliptic curves. This shows that
\Aut(JC_{1})=\langle-1,\sigma^{3}\rangle
and, in particular, all the automorphisms commute with
\gamma_{0}
.
∎

In the next proposition, we will recover the morphism
\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}:JC_{1}\to JC_{1}
from the Prym variety
P
when the degree of the cyclic covering equals 6. As before we identify the Jacobians with their duals.
We consider isogenies
\varphi:JN_{0}\times JN_{1}\to P
, where
N_{0}
and
N_{1}
are smooth curves of genus
2
and
3
respectively.
We say that
\varphi
satisfies property (*) if the pullback of
\Theta_{P}
under
\varphi
is given by the matrix

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2\lambda_{N_{0}}&\hat{\alpha}\\
\alpha&2\lambda_{N_{1}}\end{array}\right),

for some morphism
\alpha:JN_{0}\to JN_{1}
.

Analogously, we say that
\varphi
satisfies property (**) if there exists a morphism
\alpha:JN_{0}\to JN_{1}
such that

(1+\sigma)\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\alpha}\alpha&\hat{\alpha}\\
-\alpha&0\end{array}\right).

We define the set

\Lambda(P,\Theta_{P},\langle\sigma\rangle):=\{(N_{0},N_{1},\varphi)\>|\>\varphi:JN_{0}\times JN_{1}\xrightarrow{\simeq}P\text{ and }\exists\ \alpha:JN_{0}\to JN_{1}\ \text{ that satisfies (*) and (**)}\}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(P,\Theta_{P},\langle\sigma\rangle)
be a generic Prym variety of a cyclic covering of degree 6. Then for all
(N_{0},N_{1},\varphi)\in\Lambda(P,\Theta_{P},\langle\sigma\rangle)
we have

(a)

N_{0}\simeq C_{0}
and
N_{1}\simeq C_{1}
;

(b)

\alpha\hat{\alpha}=\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\Lambda(P,\Theta_{P},\langle\sigma\rangle)
. Recall that the addition map
\psi:JC_{0}\times JC_{1}\to P
is an isomorphism. Let
F=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
be the matrix corresponding to the map

\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi:JN_{0}\times JN_{1}\to JC_{0}\times JC_{1}.

Part
(a)
can be proved in the same way as in
[
NOS24
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Now, fix isomorphisms
N_{i}\simeq C_{i}
for
i=0,1
. Following the proof of
[
NOS24
, Proposition 3.1]
, one observes that
A
and
D
come from automorphisms of
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
respectively. Since both,
\psi
and

\varphi
satisfy property (**), we have
that

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\gamma_{0}}\gamma_{0}&\hat{\gamma}_{0}\\
-\gamma_{0}&0\end{array}\right)\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\hat{\alpha}\alpha&\hat{\alpha}\\
-\alpha&0\end{array}\right),

which gives us the equations

(4)

-\gamma_{0}B=C\hat{\alpha},

(5)

-\gamma_{0}A=C\hat{\alpha}\alpha-D\alpha,

(6)

\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}B+\hat{\gamma}_{0}D=A\hat{\alpha},

(7)

\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}A+\hat{\gamma}_{0}C=A\hat{\alpha}\alpha-B\alpha.

Applying
\hat{\gamma}_{0}
to
(\ref{1})
from the left and substituting in
(\ref{3})
we get

(8)

-\hat{\gamma}_{0}C\hat{\alpha}+\hat{\gamma}_{0}D=A\hat{\alpha}.

Applying
\hat{\gamma}_{0}
to
(\ref{2})
and

\alpha
to
(\ref{5})
from the right, we get

(9)

-\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}A=\hat{\gamma}_{0}C\hat{\alpha}\alpha-\hat{\gamma}_{0}D\alpha=-A\hat{\alpha}\alpha.

Together with
(\ref{4})
we obtain

(10)

\hat{\gamma}_{0}C=-B\alpha.

Using
(\ref{1})
this implies that

(11)

\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}C=C\hat{\alpha}\alpha.

Again, by
(\ref{1})
we get

(12)

-\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}B=C\hat{\alpha}\alpha\hat{\alpha}

From
(\ref{2})
we obtain

(13)

D\alpha\hat{\alpha}=C\hat{\alpha}\alpha\hat{\alpha}+\gamma_{0}A\hat{\alpha}=-\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}B+\gamma_{0}A\hat{\alpha}.

Finally
(\ref{3})
implies that

(14)

D\alpha\hat{\alpha}=-\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}B+\gamma_{0}A\hat{\alpha}=\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}D.

Now, since
D\in\Aut(JC_{1})
it follows from Proposition
3.5
that

\alpha\hat{\alpha}=\gamma_{0}\hat{\gamma}_{0}
.
∎

The following lemma is shown for any odd positive integer

d
, although we need the result only for
d=3
.

Lemma 3.7
.

The morphism
\gamma_{0}=(1+\sigma^{-1})_{|JC_{0}}:JC_{0}\to JC_{1}
is injective.

Proof.

If
x=(1+j)y\in JC_{0}
is in the kernel of
1+\sigma^{-1}
we have
\sigma(x)=-x
hence
\sigma^{2}(x)=x
. But then

d\cdot x=(1+\sigma^{2}+\ldots+\sigma^{2d})(x)=(1+\sigma^{2}+\ldots+\sigma^{2d})(1+j)(y)=0

hence
x
is a
d
-torsion point.

Now, since
x
is a fixed point of
\sigma^{2}
and
g:{\widetilde{C}}\to C_{\sigma^{2}}
is étale,
x=g^{*}(z)
for some
z\in JC_{\sigma^{2}}
. Since
jx=x
we get

x=g^{*}z=j(g^{*}x)=g^{*}\iota^{*}z=g^{*}(-z)=-x,

hence
x
is a
2
-torsion point. Since
d
is odd, it follows that
x\in J{\widetilde{C}}[2]\cap J{\widetilde{C}}[d]=\{0\}
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

The Prym map
\mathcal{R}_{2}[6]\to\mathcal{A}_{5}^{(1,1,1,1,6)}
is generically injective.

Proof.

Let
(P,\Theta_{P})
be a general element of the image of the Prym map. By Proposition
3.1
we recover
\langle\sigma\rangle\in\Aut(P,\Theta_{P})
. As a consequence of Proposition
3.6
, we can recover
C_{0},C_{1}
and the morphism
\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}:JC_{1}\to JC_{1}
from
P
. Since
\gamma_{0}
is injective and
E=\ker\hat{\gamma}_{0}
it follows that
E=\ker\hat{\gamma}_{0}\gamma_{0}
, so we have an exact sequence

0\to E\to JC_{1}\to JC_{1}.

Dualising, we get
\widehat{JC}_{1}\to E
. Using the Abel-Jacobi map and by the Universal Property of Jacobians we obtain a map
C_{1}\to E
.

Taking the Galois closure of
C_{1}\to E
gives us a covering

{\widetilde{C}}\to E
together with an automorphism
\sigma^{2}
of order 3 and an involution
j\sigma
on
{\widetilde{C}}
. We know that
\sigma^{3}
is the unique involution on
C_{1}
, so lifting it to
{\widetilde{C}}
we obtain involutions
\sigma^{3}
and
j\sigma^{4}
on
{\widetilde{C}}
. Note that we can distinguish the involution corresponding to
\sigma^{3}
on
{\widetilde{C}}
as its composition with
\sigma^{2}
is an automorphism of order
6
while
j\sigma^{4}\circ\sigma^{2}
is an involution. Then we recover
\sigma=\sigma^{3}\circ\sigma^{2}\circ\sigma^{2}
, hence also the covering
f:{\widetilde{C}}\to H
.

∎

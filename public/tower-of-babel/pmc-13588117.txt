A Study on the Efficacy of the 5E Rehabilitation Nursing Method Based on Mind Mapping for Patients with Ischemic Stroke After Carotid Stent Implantation: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Li Zhou, Jiang Hong Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveThe effects of mind map-guided 5E rehabilitation nursing on patients with ischemic stroke after carotid artery stenting (CAS) are to be evaluated.MethodsThis retrospective study included 71 patients receiving conventional care (January–December 2023, Control group) and 73 patients receiving the new nursing model (January–December 2024, Observation group). Baseline data were compared at admission, and outcome measures were assessed at one month postoperatively. This included the following: the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), the Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA), the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA), the Chinese Mini Mental Status (CMMS), the Barthel Index, the Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS), the relative mean transit time (rMTT), the relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV), and the relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF). Hospital stay, compliance, and complications were also considered.ResultsAt one month postoperatively, the NIHSS, HADS, and rMTT of the observation group were lower than those of the control group (all P<0.05). The observation group’s FMA, MoCA, CMMS, Barthel Index, rCBF, and rCBV were higher than the control group’s (all P<0.05). The observation group had shorter hospitalization times, higher overall treatment compliance, and lower overall complication rates than the control group (all P<0.05).ConclusionMind map-guided 5E rehabilitation nursing shows promise in improving short-term recovery outcomes in post-CAS stroke patients. Further prospective validation is needed.Keywords: ischemic stroke, carotid artery stenting, mind map, 5e rehabilitation nursing, neurological function

Introduction

Ischemic stroke accounts for 70% to 80% of all strokes. It is characterised by a high incidence rate, a high recurrence rate, a high disability rate and a high mortality rate.1,2 In 2021, there were approximately 69.94 million prevalent cases of ischemic stroke globally, with about 3.59 million deaths. Although the age-standardized mortality rate decreased by 15.76% compared to 2010, the absolute number of patients and the incidence rate continued to rise.3 The number of younger people suffering from ischaemic strokes is increasing because the population is getting older and people’s lifestyles are changing. Ischemic stroke not only causes neurological deficits in patients but also severely impairs their ability to perform daily activities independently, placing significant burdens on families and society in terms of medical care and economic costs.4 Currently, carotid artery stenting (CAS) has become the preferred minimally invasive intervention for treating moderate-to-severe carotid stenosis due to its advantages of low invasiveness and rapid recovery.5,6 CAS involves the endovascular placement of stents within stenotic segments of the carotid artery to dilate the vessel and restore cerebral blood flow, thereby reducing the risk of recurrent ischemic stroke.7 However, CAS carries postoperative risks including cervical hematoma, vascular injury, cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome, and in-stent restenosis.8,9 Furthermore, patients often exhibit low rehabilitation compliance due to residual neurological deficits and insufficient awareness of the importance of rehabilitation training, thereby compromising the long-term efficacy of surgical treatment.10 Effective nursing interventions are crucial for the postoperative rehabilitation of patients with ischemic stroke following CAS.11 Traditional nursing models primarily focus on basic daily care, often failing to address patients’ multifaceted needs in disease awareness, psychological adjustment, and self-management.12

Mind maps are visual thinking tools that organise information in a structured, hierarchical format. By radiating branches from a central theme, they help patients to simplify complex knowledge, enhance memory retention and improve comprehension. Mind mapping tools have been widely adopted in medical education, clinical decision-making and nursing management.13 Mind maps transform rehabilitation knowledge and training protocols into intuitive visual content, helping patients and their families quickly grasp key rehabilitation points and thereby enhancing health education outcomes.14 The 5E rehabilitation nursing model comprises five sequential stages: Engagement, Exploration, Explanation, Elaboration, and Evaluation. The 5E Rehabilitation Care Model consists of five sequential stages: Encourage, Educate, Exercise, Employment, and Evaluate. The “Encourage” stage uses psychological counseling to alleviate patients’ postoperative negative emotions and motivate them toward recovery. The “Educate” stage systematically imparts health knowledge regarding disease management, medication use, and the prevention of complications. The “Exercise” stage develops personalized, phased physical and cognitive training programs based on the patient’s neurological deficits. The “Employment” stage focuses on training in daily self-care to restore the patient’s ability to live independently and adapt to society. The “Evaluation” phase involves conducting regular rehabilitation assessments to promptly adjust care plans and establish a closed-loop intervention system. Together, these five phases comprehensively address the continuum of motivation, understanding, ability, application, and effectiveness, transforming passive care into active rehabilitation and promoting sustained behavioral maintenance.15 By employing interactive, personalized interventions, the 5E model emphasizes patient-centered care to stimulate active participation, thereby promoting deeper understanding and practical application of rehabilitation knowledge.16 Existing research indicates that the 5E model effectively enhances treatment adherence and fosters healthy behaviors in chronic disease rehabilitation and postoperative care.15,17

Despite the individual efficacy of mind mapping and the 5E rehabilitation nursing model in various clinical settings, the integration of these two approaches for post-CAS stroke patients remains underexplored. Existing studies have primarily applied these methods separately in chronic disease management or postoperative care, with limited evidence on their combined effects in neurovascular rehabilitation. Furthermore, no prior study has systematically evaluated the synergistic impact of visualized health education (mind mapping) and structured interactive rehabilitation (5E model) on neurological recovery, cognitive function, and cerebral hemodynamics following CAS. This evidence gap underscores the need for innovative nursing interventions that address the multifaceted rehabilitation requirements of ischemic stroke patients after carotid stenting.

Therefore, our hospital has integrated clinical experience with literature evidence to develop a mind-map guided 5E rehabilitation nursing model. Since January 2024, this model has been applied to patients undergoing CAS for ischemic stroke to provide more targeted specialized rehabilitation care. To explore differences from conventional care, this study examines the effects of mind maps and the 5E rehabilitation nursing model on neurological recovery, activities of daily living, rehabilitation adherence, and complication rates in ischemic stroke patients following CAS surgery. The aim is to provide clinicians with an efficient, scientifically grounded and scalable new approach to postoperative rehabilitation nursing, thereby improving patient outcomes and quality of life further.

Methods

Study Design and Population

Information about patients with ischaemic strokes who had carotid stenting carried out in the Department of Neurology at our hospital between January 2023 and December 2024 was collected retrospectively. This was done continuously via the hospital’s medical record system. The control group comprised patients receiving conventional nursing care from January to December 2023, while the observation group included patients receiving mind-mapping guided care combined with 5E rehabilitation nursing from January to December 2024. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Chengdu Fifth People’s Hospital, No. CFPH-2025009. Our study complies with the requirements of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Based on preliminary data and prior studies, we anticipated a mean difference of 2.0 points in NIHSS scores between groups with a pooled standard deviation of 3.5. Using an independent samples t-test with α=0.05 (two-tailed) and power (1-β)=0.80, a minimum of 50 patients per group was required. To account for potential data loss in retrospective records, we included all eligible patients within the study period (71 controls, 73 interventions), yielding an actual power exceeding 0.90 for detecting the observed effect size (Cohen’s d ≈ 1.5).

Inclusion Criteria: ① Patients aged ≥18 years and ≤75 years. ② Clinically symptomatic and radiologically confirmed ischemic stroke with a clearly identified cause of carotid artery stenosis. ③ First-time recipient of CAS treatment.

Exclusion Criteria: ① Patients with non-ischemic stroke. ② Severe postoperative complications, including intracerebral hemorrhage, symptomatic restenosis, acute myocardial infarction, etc. ③ Presence of severe cognitive impairment or psychiatric disorders. ④ Pregnant patients. ⑤ Incomplete clinical documentation.

Rehabilitation Program

The control group received routine nursing interventions. These included: (1) Routine health education upon admission: Introduction to ward environment, regulations, disease-related knowledge, and pre- and post-operative precautions; (2) Postoperative vital sign monitoring: Close observation of patient consciousness, pupils, limb movement, and neurological function changes; (3) Medication guidance: Administration of antiplatelet, antihypertensive, and lipid-lowering medications as prescribed, with explanation of drug effects and adverse reactions; (4) Dietary guidance: Recommend a low-salt, low-fat diet with adequate protein, emphasizing increased fruit and vegetable intake; (5) Rehabilitation guidance: Encourage appropriate functional exercises with gradual progression of activity levels; (6) Discharge instructions: Provide post-discharge precautions, follow-up appointment schedules, and emergency management protocols.

The observation group integrated mind mapping guidance and the 5E rehabilitation nursing model into the control group’s nursing approach. The specific implementation details are as follows: (1) The department will establish a mind map leadership team, with the department director serving as team leader, the head nurse as deputy leader and executive officer, and attending physicians and senior nurses as team members. This team will be responsible for identifying common clinical complications following carotid stenting for ischemic stroke and developing nursing education content related to mental health issues. The team leader, deputy, and other core members will disseminate this educational content to all departmental medical staff. The leader will explain the etiological classification of ischemic stroke, the procedural workflow of carotid stenting, and common postoperative complications. The deputy will cover specific surgical details, preventive measures for postoperative complications, neurological monitoring, key nursing points for mental health issues, and mind map creation methods, integrating the 5E rehabilitation nursing concept. The 5E Rehabilitation Nursing Concept comprises five core phases: Encourage: Stimulate patient motivation and provide psychological support. Educate: Disseminate disease knowledge and self-management methods to patients and their families. Exercise: Guide patients through targeted physical and functional training. Employment: Assist patients in reintegrating into daily life and social roles. Evaluate: Continuously assess progress and adjust care plans. (2) A mind map drafting team comprising the head nurse, attending physician, and charge nurse will collaborate to complete corresponding mind maps based on each patient’s specific condition. The team will first assess the patient’s degree of neurological deficit, postoperative level of consciousness, limb motor function, and rehabilitation needs. They identify current nursing priorities, challenges, and gaps. Based on literature review and past experience, they create a mind map integrating the 5E rehabilitation nursing process. (3) Using the mind map as guidance, implement 5E rehabilitation nursing in clinical practice (Figure 1).

Data Collection

All data were obtained from the patients’ hospital information systems, encompassing relevant information at admission and one month postoperatively. Standardized assessment tools were used for scale evaluations, which were completed by trained nursing staff at admission and one month postoperatively.

Baseline Data

Collect patient age, gender, body mass index (BMI), systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, smoking history, alcohol consumption history, history of hypertension, history of diabetes, hyperlipidemia, and degree of carotid artery stenosis.

Outcome Metrics

Neurological Function Assessment

Neurological function assessment is conducted using the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS).18 The NIHSS assesses consciousness, language, motor function, sensory deficits, visual field defects, eye movements, coordination, neglect, and articulation. Normal status scores range from 0 to 1. Mild impairment ranges from 2 to 4; moderate impairment from 5 to 15; severe impairment from 16 to 20; and profound impairment from 21 to 42.

The Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA) was used to evaluate patients’ motor function.19 The total score range for the upper limb section is 0–66 points, and for the lower limb section it is 0–37 points. Scores that are higher than average indicate better motor function.

Cognitive Impairment Assessment

Cognitive impairment is assessed using two tests: the Chinese version of the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)20 and the Chinese Mini Mental Status (CMMS).21 The MoCA covers cognitive domains such as attention and concentration, memory, executive function, visuospatial ability, language skills, abstract reasoning, calculation skills and orientation. A total score of 26–30 indicates normal functioning, with scores ranging from 0 to 30. The CMMS scale has been adapted for the local context and includes dimensions such as immediate memory, time and place orientation, delayed memory, language, visuospatial ability, attention, and calculation. Scores in the range of 27 to 30 indicate normal cognitive functioning, 21 to 26 indicate mild impairment, 10 to 26 indicate moderate impairment, and 0 to 10 indicate severe impairment.

Assessment of Activities of Daily Living and Psychological Functioning

The Barthel Index22 is used to assess functional independence in daily activities. It covers ten items, including eating, bathing and dressing, and scores patients based on their ability to perform these activities independently. The total score ranges from 0 to 100 points; higher scores indicate greater functional independence.

The Hospital Anxiety and Depression Scale (HADS)23 was used to conduct the psychological assessment. The HADS comprises two subscales, one measuring anxiety and the other measuring depression. Each subscale contains seven items, which are scored on a scale ranging from 0 (no distress) to 3 (severe distress). The total possible score is 42, with higher scores indicating greater severity of anxiety and depression.

Cerebral Hemodynamic Parameters

The patient underwent measurement of cerebral haemodynamic parameters using a Companion III model transcranial Doppler ultrasound device, including relative mean transit time (rMTT), relative cerebral blood volume (rCBV) and relative cerebral blood flow (rCBF).

Hospitalization Duration

The time spent in hospital is calculated from the moment a patient is admitted to the moment they are discharged.

Treatment Adherence

The adherence assessment is based on whether patients take their medication on time, attend regular follow-up appointments and adhere to medical instructions. These are categorised into three levels: excellent, good and poor. “Excellent” adherence means patients consistently take their medication on time with no missed doses or errors, attend all follow-up appointments on time with no absences, and strictly follow medical instructions without deviation. “Good” adherence means patients generally take their medication on time, occasionally miss doses or make errors, but promptly make up for them. They also attend follow-ups mostly on schedule, occasionally missing due to special circumstances, but promptly rescheduling. They largely follow medical instructions. “Poor” adherence indicates significant issues with taking medication, attending follow-up appointments, and following medical instructions. This frequently involves missed doses, medication errors, or skipped appointments, with no noticeable improvement. Overall treatment adherence rate = (Excellent cases + Good cases) / Total cases × 100%.24

Complications

Postoperative complications of CAS primarily include: bleeding at the puncture site, hematoma, lower extremity venous thrombosis, urinary retention, and pressure ulcers. Bleeding or hematoma at the puncture site occurs when blood leaks from the puncture point (eg, femoral artery or radial artery) or damaged peripheral vessels into subcutaneous tissue or adjacent spaces, forming a localized mass. Clinically, this manifests as swelling, pain, and ecchymosis at the puncture site.25 Deep vein thrombosis in the lower extremities occurs due to abnormal blood clotting within deep veins, obstructing venous return. Typical symptoms include sudden swelling and pain in one leg after surgery, along with increased skin temperature and discoloration.26 Urinary retention is a common postoperative complication characterized by bladder distension without voluntary voiding. Specific symptoms include urinary frequency with dysuria, lower abdominal distension and pain, and bladder distension, accompanied by an increased risk of urinary tract infection. Furthermore, medical device-associated pressure ulcers represent a distinct type of pressure injury caused by sustained compression of localized skin from external medical devices such as catheters, oxygen tubes, or blood pressure cuffs. Clinical manifestations include erythema, blisters, ulcers, and even full-thickness skin loss at the pressure points, with the ulcer shape typically conforming to the contour of the compressive device.27

Statistical Analysis

This study employed IBM SPSS Statistics 27.0 software for data analysis. Categorical variables in the study were described using frequencies and percentages. Continuous variables were described using mean and standard deviation. For categorical variables, we used Pearson’s chi-square test for intergroup comparisons if the data met the conditions of theoretical frequency >5 and sample size ≥40; otherwise, we employed Fisher’s exact test. For continuous variables meeting normal distribution, the use of independent samples t-tests was for intergroup comparisons, while for intragroup comparisons, the use of paired samples t-tests was. Non-normally distributed continuous variables were analysed using non-parametric tests. If the p-value was less than 0.05, it was thought to be very important.

Discussion

The findings of this study suggest that, in comparison with traditional care, the combination of mind mapping and the 5E rehabilitation nursing model is more effective for patients in several areas, including neurological deficits, limb motor function, cognitive function, activities of daily living, and psychological well-being. Furthermore, the combination of mind mapping and the 5E rehabilitation nursing model exhibited more pronounced improvements in cerebral hemodynamics, a significantly reduced incidence of complications, and a markedly shorter length of hospital stay. This study demonstrates that the 5E rehabilitation nursing model guided by mind mapping significantly enhances postoperative rehabilitation outcomes. This model effectively addresses inherent issues in traditional nursing practices, such as abstract health education content, lack of systematic rehabilitation training, and insufficient patient engagement. It provides structured support for ischemic stroke patients undergoing rehabilitation following CAS procedures.

The effectiveness of nursing interventions guided by mind maps can be explained through multiple theoretical frameworks. For example, taking cognitive load theory into account, mind maps visualise and organise complex medical information, thereby reducing the cognitive burden on patients whose cognitive functions may be impaired by anaesthetic analgesics.28 Dual coding theory suggests that information presented in both visual and verbal modes, such as mind maps, is more easily memorised and recalled.29 Patient engagement theory posits that interactive visualization tools can enhance patient involvement in treatment, thereby improving treatment adherence and efficacy.30 During the intervention process, mind maps, through their hierarchical graphical presentation method, transform the complex rehabilitation knowledge system, such as neurological function training, medication management, and complication prevention, into a visual path that is easy for patients to understand. The 5E model cycle of “participation - exploration - explanation - extension - evaluation” ensures that the nursing intervention shifts from one-way guidance to two-way interaction, encouraging patients to actively participate in the rehabilitation decision-making at each stage and gradually building confidence in rehabilitation and self-management abilities.

This study found that combining mind maps with the 5E model proved more effective than traditional methods in improving neurological function and limb motor function, potentially attributable to the systematic implementation of the “Exercise” phase within the 5E model. Mind maps clearly outline rehabilitation training priorities for different recovery stages (acute and subacute phases), such as focusing on positional adjustments and passive joint movements during the acute phase, and emphasizing sitting balance and standing training during the subacute phase. This protocol provides clear, phased objectives and operational feasibility for rehabilitation plans. Through the “evaluation” phase, the nursing team dynamically monitors patients’ neurological recovery, promptly adjusting training intensity and content. This approach avoids the common issue of delayed rehabilitation initiation seen in traditional care.31 Implementing structured functional training early in the patient’s recovery effectively promotes neuroplasticity and motor function reconstruction.32

Improvements in cognitive function also warrant attention. Research findings indicate that integrating mind mapping with the 5E rehabilitation nursing model significantly enhances postoperative cognitive function in stroke patients. Postoperative cognitive impairment in ischemic stroke patients is typically caused by factors such as insufficient cerebral perfusion and inflammatory responses, while traditional nursing practices have historically placed relatively less emphasis on cognitive rehabilitation. This study organically integrated cognitive training (eg, attention training, memory recall, and executive function tasks) into the 5E rehabilitation nursing process and clearly presented it through mind maps, thereby enhancing patient compliance.33 The positive psychological support provided during the “Encourage” phase effectively alleviated patient anxiety, further improving cooperation and effectiveness in cognitive training.34

The results of this study indicate significant improvements in patients’ Barthel Index and HADS scores, consistent with previous research findings. This suggests that mind-mapping-based interventions contribute to stabilizing patients’ psychological states.35 Beyond the consensus on mental health benefits, our study expands existing knowledge in several dimensions. By integrating mind mapping with the 5E rehabilitation care model, we established a concrete operational pathway to translate theoretical knowledge into practical daily living skills, thereby systematically reconstructing patients’ psychological states. Consequently, our research not only validates positive impacts on patients’ psychological indicators but also provides evidence of tangible benefits in their daily lives.

Additionally, the study revealed that optimizing cerebral hemodynamics and effectively managing complications represent another significant finding. Compared to the control group, the observation group demonstrated markedly increased rCBF and rCBV, while rMTT was significantly reduced, indicating substantial improvement in cerebral perfusion status. Mind maps clearly illustrate patients’ activity levels across different time periods. The study indicates that early patient activity not only directly promotes blood circulation but also indirectly enhances cerebral perfusion by strengthening cardiopulmonary function.36,37 Additionally, our findings indicate that the 5E rehabilitation nursing model based on mind maps effectively enhances patient treatment adherence. Mind maps render nursing procedures more comprehensible, helping patients grasp key points and consolidate memory. This approach strengthens patients’ recognition of treatment importance and priority, thereby improving adherence. Patients in the observation group exhibited shorter hospital stays and lower complication rates compared to the control group. This improvement likely stems from the model’s systematic and targeted approach. By providing clear rehabilitation guidance and timely assessment adjustments, the model facilitates faster functional recovery, consequently reducing hospitalization duration.

Limitations

This study employed a retrospective design utilizing existing data, and patient allocation was non-randomized, potentially introducing selection bias. Patients were grouped based on the timing of the nursing model’s implementation (2023 versus 2024), which may have introduced selection bias due to temporal differences in patient characteristics, or staffing conditions. Although baseline demographic characteristics were comparable between the two groups, unmeasured confounding factors cannot be completely ruled out. The relatively small sample size from a single center may limit the statistical power to detect small but clinically meaningful effects. Furthermore, the one-month follow-up period is relatively brief, which may make it difficult to assess sustained rehabilitation outcomes, including long-term neurological stability, maintenance of functional independence, and recurrence of complications. Therefore, these limitations should be carefully considered when interpreting the findings. Future studies should adopt a prospective multi-center design, increase the sample size, extend the follow-up period, and strengthen the control of confounding factors, so as to obtain more reliable evidence and enhance the universality in a broader population.

Conclusion

In this retrospective, single-center study with a limited follow-up period, the 5E rehabilitation nursing method based on mind mapping to treat patients with ischemic stroke showed preliminary evidence of favorable therapeutic outcomes. This model appears to effectively promote the recovery of neurological and cognitive functions, enhance patients’ ability to perform activities of daily living and improve their mental health, improve cerebral hemodynamics, and reduce the risk of complications. However, given that this study employed a retrospective, non-randomized design and had a limited follow-up period, these findings should be interpreted with caution.

Koszul property and finite linearity defect over g g -stretched local rings
Do Van Kien, Hop D. Nguyen

Abstract
. The linearity defect is a measure for the non-linearity of minimal free resolutions of modules over noetherian local rings. A tantalizing open question due to Herzog and Iyengar asks whether a noetherian local ring ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,{\mathfrak{m}}) is Koszul if its residue field R / 𝔪 R/{\mathfrak{m}} has a finite linearity defect. We provide a positive answer to this question when R R is a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of almost minimal multiplicity with the residue field of characteristic zero. The proof depends on the study of noetherian local rings ( R , 𝔪 ) (R,{\mathfrak{m}}) such that 𝔪 2 {\mathfrak{m}}^{2} is a principal ideal, which we call g g -stretched local rings. The class of g g -stretched local rings subsumes stretched artinian local rings studied by Sally, and generic artinian reductions of Cohen–Macaulay local rings of almost minimal multiplicity. An essential part in the proof of our main result is a complete characterization of one-dimensional complete g g -stretched local rings. Beside partial progress on Herzog–Iyengar’s question, another consequence of our study is a numerical characterization of all g g -stretched Koszul rings, strengthening previous work of Avramov, Iyengar, and Şega.

Introduction

Starting from classical results like Hilbert’s syzygy theorem and Auslander–Buchsbaum–Serre’s characterization of regular local rings, gaining an understanding of the structure of free resolutions over a local ring (or standard graded algebras over a field
k
) has proved to be of fundamental importance. Among standard graded
k
-algebras, Koszul algebras have emerged as adequate playgrounds for studying graded free resolutions thanks to work of Avramov, Eisenbud, and Peeva
[
AE92
]
,
[
AP01
]
. Inspired by work of Eisenbud et al.
[
EFS
]
, Herzog and Iyengar
[
HIy
]
introduced a natural generalization of Koszul algebras to the local situation, via the notion of
linearity defect
(see
Section

2

[
HIy
]
, we say that a noetherian local ring (or a standard graded
k
-algebra)
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
is

if

has a linear free resolution over
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, equivalently, if

has linearity defect
\lind_{R}k=0
. Combining results of
[
AE92
,
AP01
,

Theorem 1.1

(Avramov, Eisenbud, Herzog, Iyengar, Peeva)
.

Let

be a standard graded
k
-algebra. For a finitely generated graded
R
-module

, let
\reg_{R}M
and
\lind_{R}M
be the regularity and the linearity defect of

over
R
, resp. The following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is a Koszul algebra, i.e.
\reg_{R}k=0
;

(2)

\reg_{R}k<\infty
;

(3)

\reg_{R}M<\infty
for any finitely generated graded
R
-module

;

(4)

\lind_{R}k=0
;

(5)

\lind_{R}k<\infty
.

For recent surveys related to free resolutions and Koszul algebras, we refer to
[

,
CDR
,

The finiteness of the linearity defect is known to have strong consequences: By

is a rational function with denominator depending only on

, and independent of

. On the other hand, not much is known about the classification of noetherian local rings (or graded algebras) such that

R

Question 1.2

Let

Beside the graded case mentioned in
Theorem

1.1

has a positive answer in either of the following cases. Below, we let

be the

(1)

{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0

]

]

(2)

,
Se
]
;

(3)

(4)

]

In this paper, we are interested in the Koszul property of

and the finiteness of the linearity defect of the residue field

Theorem 1.3

(=
Theorem

8.1
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of almost minimal multiplicity. Assume that
\chara k=0

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
consists of three steps:

(1)

Step 1
: Reduction to dimension at most 1. The main ingredient is the solution of Sally’s conjecture due to Rossi–Valla
[
RV96
]
and independently Wang
[
W97
]
.

(2)

Step 2
: Solution to the case
\dim R=0
. The four main ingredients are the classification of

-stretched local rings of dimension 1, Elias–Valla’s structure theorem for artinian stretched local rings, the theory of weak Koszul filtration, and our previous work
[
Ng15
,
NgV16
]
on the behavior of linearity defect along short exact sequences.

(3)

Step 3
: Solution to the case
\dim R=1
. The proof uses the same strategy as the solution of Step 2, combined with reduction to the case of Step 2.

In Step 1, we use the fact that if

is a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of almost minimal multiplicity with

, then the associated graded ring has almost maximal depth: There is an inequality

[

]
and Rossi–Valla
[

]

-regular, and we can reduce the dimension by replacing

1.3
is when

.

Before going to the details of Steps 2 and 3, let us introduce

-stretched rings. Observe that
R
is an artinian local ring of almost minimal multiplicity if and only if
{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0
and the equality
\mu({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})=1
holds

1

1

Indeed, an artinian ring

has minimal multiplicity if and only if

. The chain

. Nakayama’s lemma implies that this is equivalent to
{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0
and
\mu({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})=1
.
. More generally, let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring with infinite residue field,

be a parameter ideal of

generated by elements in
{\mathfrak{m}}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
. If

satisfies the inequality

of the artinian reduction

is principal. Based on the these observations, it is natural to study noetherian local ring whose square of the maximal ideal is principal. We call such rings

-stretched
, where “g” stands for “generalized”. Note that artinian

-stretched local rings are exactly the class of

[

, and Elias–Valla

]
, among others.

Step 2 is accomplished by the following

Theorem 1.4

(See

7.1
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a noetherian local ring. Assume that

This theorem says that

. The proof of

has four main ingredients. Observe that Krull’s principal ideal theorem implies that any

Theorem 1.5

(=

)
.

Let

be a regular local ring of dimension

, and

As an application of

Theorem 1.6

(=
Theorem

6.1
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a
g
-stretched local ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

R

(2)

Either
\dim R=1
, or

{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0
, and

The second ingredient in the proof of

is a structure result for stretched local rings due to Elias and Valla

Theorem 1.7

(=
Theorem

7.4
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a
g
-stretched artinian local ring. Assume that
\chara(k)=0
. If

The proof of the last theorem depends on the theory of

, which we develop in

. In fact, a key and (at least to us) surprising observation in the proof of

, is that any stretched Gorenstein local ring has a weak Koszul filtration, even though it might not be Koszul. The notion of weak Koszul filtration for local rings subsumes that of Koszul filtration for graded

, it is flexible enough for controlling of the linearity defect: We prove in

is the theory of weak Koszul filtration, and the fourth is various bounds for linearity defect along short exact sequences induced by such filtration, developed in
[
Ng15
,
NgV16
]
.

Step 3 in the proof of

1.3
is concerned with the case where

has dimension

2.
Preliminaries

We recall the notion of linearity defect which Herzog and Iyengar introduced in
[
HIy
]
, based in the notion of the linear part of a minimal free resolution. The linear part appeared in work of Herzog et al.
[
HSV83
, Section 5]
and Eisenbud et al.
[
EFS
]
. Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a noetherian local ring, and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. Let
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=\oplus_{i\geq 0}{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{i+1}
be the associated graded ring of
R
with respect to the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic filtration. Let the
minimal free resolution of
M
be

{\mathbb{F}}:\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{i-1}\xrightarrow{\partial}\cdots\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial}F_{0}\to 0.

Since
{\mathbb{F}}
is minimal, it admits a filtration
\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{i}{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{i-1}{\mathbb{F}}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}^{0}{\mathbb{F}}={\mathbb{F}}
as follows:

{\mathcal{F}}^{i}{\mathbb{F}}:\cdots\to F_{j}\to F_{j-1}\to\cdots\to F_{i}\to{\mathfrak{m}}F_{i-1}\to\cdots\to{\mathfrak{m}}^{i-1}F_{1}\to{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}F_{0}\to 0.

The associated graded complex

\linp^{R}{\mathbb{F}}=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}\frac{{\mathcal{F}}^{i}{\mathbb{F}}}{{\mathcal{F}}^{i+1}{\mathbb{F}}}

is called
the linear part
of
{\mathbb{F}}
. Clearly,
\linp^{R}{\mathbb{F}}
is a complex of graded
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
-modules with
(\linp^{R}{\mathbb{F}})_{i}=(\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}F_{i})(-i)
. The
linearity defect
of
M
is defined to be

\lind_{R}M=\sup\left\{i:H_{i}(\linp^{R}{\mathbb{F}})\neq 0\right\}.

By convention, if
M=0
, we set
\lind_{R}M=0
. We say that
M
is a
Koszul module
if
\lind_{R}M=0
. A local ring
R
is said to be
Koszul
if its residue field
k
is Koszul as a module over
R
.

We say that an
R
-linear map
\phi:M\to P
is
Tor-vanishing
if the induced maps
\Tor^{R}_{i}(k,\phi)
have zero images for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Using Şega’s resolution-free characterization of the linearity defect in
[
Se
, Theorem 2.2]
, we can prove the following statements.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
Ng15
, Corollary 2.10]
and
[
NgV16
, Proposition 4.3]
)
.

Consider any short exact sequence
0\to M\xlongrightarrow{\phi}P\to N\to 0
of finitely generated
R
-modules.

(1)

There are inequalities:

(i)

\lind_{R}N\leq\min\{\max\{\pd_{R}P+1,\lind_{R}M+1\},\max\{\lind_{R}P,\pd_{R}M+1\}\}
,

(ii)

\lind_{R}P\leq\min\{\max\{\pd_{R}M+1,\lind_{R}N\},\max\{\lind_{R}M,\pd_{R}N\}\}
,

(iii)

\lind_{R}M\leq\min\{\max\{\lind_{R}N-1,\pd_{R}P\},\max\{\pd_{R}N,\lind_{R}P\}\}
.

(2)

If
P
is free then
\lind_{R}M=\lind_{R}N-1
if
\lind_{R}N\geq 1
and
\lind_{R}M=0
if
\lind_{R}N=0
.

(3)

If
\phi
is Tor-vanishing, then there are inequalities:

\displaystyle\lind_{R}M

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\lind_{R}P,\lind_{R}N-1\},

\displaystyle\lind_{R}P

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\lind_{R}M,\lind_{R}N\},

\displaystyle\lind_{R}N

\displaystyle\leq\max\{\lind_{R}M+1,\lind_{R}P\}.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Ng15
, Theorem 3.5 and its proof]
)
.

Let
0\longrightarrow M\xlongrightarrow{\phi}P\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
R
-modules where

(i)

P
is a Koszul module;

(ii)

M\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}P
.

Then
\phi
is Tor-vanishing, and there are inequalities
\lind_{R}N-1\leq\lind_{R}M\leq\max\{0,\lind_{R}N-1\}
. In particular,
\lind_{R}N=\lind_{R}M+1
if
\lind_{R}M\geq 1
and
\lind_{R}N\leq 1
if
\lind_{R}M=0
.

Moreover,
\lind_{R}N=0
if and only if
M
is a Koszul module and
M\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{s+1}P={\mathfrak{m}}^{s}M
for all

.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a noetherian local ring. If
\lind_{R}k\leq 1
, then
\lind_{R}k=0
. Indeed, as
R
is a Koszul
R
-module, the exact sequence

0\to{\mathfrak{m}}\to R\to k\to 0

and

imply that
\lind_{R}{\mathfrak{m}}\leq\max\{0,\lind_{R}k-1\}=0
. But since
{\mathfrak{m}}\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{s+1}R={\mathfrak{m}}^{s}{\mathfrak{m}}
for all

, the same result implies that
\lind_{R}k=0
, as desired.

A local ring
R
is
absolutely Koszul
if every finitely generated
R
-module has a finite linearity defect; see
[
CINR
,
HIy
]
for more details. Let

\glind R=\sup\{\lind_{R}M:M\,\,\text{a finitely generated $R$-module}\}

denote the
global linearity defect
of
R
. To study the absolutely Koszul property, the passage to completion rings, that is allowed by the following result, might be useful.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a noetherian local ring, and
(\widehat{R},\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})
its
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion. Then
R
is Koszul
(
respectively, absolutely Koszul
)
if and only if
\widehat{R}
is so. Moreover, we always have equalities
\lind_{R}k=\lind_{\widehat{R}}k
and
\glind R=\glind\widehat{R}
.

The proof uses the following lemma, which was used for the demonstration of
[
NgV18
, Theorem 1.1]
. This lemma might be of independent interest, hence we recall its proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a noetherian local ring,
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}
an ideal and
M
a finitely generated
R
-module. Then there exists an integer
N

(
depending only on
M
and
I
)
such that for every integer
n\geq N
, there is an equality

\lind_{R}(M/I^{n}M)=\max\{\lind_{R}M,\lind_{R}(I^{n}M)+1\}.

Proof.

We use a result of Avramov
[
Avr78
, Corollary A.4]
, saying that there exits an integer
N_{1}\geq 1
, depending only on
I
and
M
, such that for all
n\geq N_{1}
,
\Tor^{R}_{i}(k,I^{n}M)\to\Tor^{R}_{i}(k,M)
is the zero map for all
i
, i.e.
I^{n}M\to M
is Tor-vanishing. Another application of the same result for the pair
(I,I^{N_{1}}M)
shows that there exists an integer
N_{2}\geq 1
such that for all
n\geq N_{2}
, the map
I^{n+N_{1}}M\to I^{N_{1}}M
is Tor-vanishing.

Take
n\geq N:=N_{1}+N_{2}
. Let
F,G,H
be minimal free resolutions of
I^{n}M
,
I^{N_{1}}M
, and
M
, respectively. Let
\phi_{1}:F\to G,\phi_{2}:G\to H
be liftings of the natural maps
I^{n}M\to I^{N_{1}}M
and
I^{N_{1}}M\to M
, respectively. Then
\phi_{1}(F)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}G,\phi_{2}(G)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}H
. Denoting
\phi=\phi_{2}\circ\phi_{1}:F\to H
, then
\phi
is a lifting of the natural map
I^{n}M\to M
and
\phi(F)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}H
.

Consider the short exact sequence

0\to I^{n}M\to M\to M/I^{n}M\to 0.

The mapping cone
W=H\oplus F[-1]
of
\phi:F\to H
is then a minimal free resolution of
M/I^{n}M
. Moreover, the condition
\phi(F)\subseteq{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}H
implies the direct sum decomposition of complexes of
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}R
-modules

\linp^{R}W\cong\linp^{R}H\oplus(\linp^{R}F)[-1].

This implies

\lind_{R}(M/I^{n}M)=\max\{\lind_{R}M,\lind_{R}(I^{n}M)+1\},

and concludes the proof.

U
is finitely generated over
\widehat{R}/\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{n}
. Since the natural map
R/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\to\widehat{R}/\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{n}
is an isomorphism, we deduce that
U
is a finitely generated
R/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}
-module. In particular, it is a finitely generated
R
-module. Since
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}U=0
, clearly

U\otimes_{R}\widehat{R}\cong\widehat{U}\cong U.

This gives a chain

\lind_{\widehat{R}}N\leq\lind_{\widehat{R}}U=\lind_{\widehat{R}}(U\otimes_{R}\widehat{R})=\lind_{R}U.

Thus
\glind\widehat{R}\leq\glind R
, and if
R
is absolutely Koszul, then so is
\widehat{R}
. The desired conclusions follow.
∎

Let
\boldsymbol{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{e}
be a minimal system of generators of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Denote by
K^{R}
the Koszul complex
K(\boldsymbol{x};R)
on
\boldsymbol{x}
. The homology
H_{j}(K^{R})
of
K^{R}
are known to be independent of the choice of the sequence
\boldsymbol{x}
. Denote
\codepth R=e-\depth R
. For a finitely generated
R
-module
M
, the formal power series

P^{R}_{M}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\dim_{k}\Tor^{R}_{i}(k,M)t^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[t]]

is called the
Poincaré series
of
M
. Serre proved the (coefficient-wise) inequality

P^{R}_{k}(t)\preccurlyeq\frac{(1+t)^{e}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{\codepth R}\dim_{k}H_{j}(K^{R})t^{j+1}}.

Definition 2.6

(See
[
Avr98
, Section 5]
)
.

A noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
is called
Golod
if we have the equality of formal power series

P^{R}_{k}(t)=\frac{(1+t)^{e}}{1-\sum_{j=1}^{\codepth R}\dim_{k}H_{j}(K^{R})t^{j+1}}.

Since completion does not change the embedding dimension
e
, the depth, the Poincaré series
P^{R}_{k}(t)
, and the rank of the Koszul homology
H_{j}(K^{R})
, we deduce from
Definition

2.6
that

Lemma 2.7
.

A noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is Golod if and only if its
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion
(\widehat{R},\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})
is so.

3.
Reduction of
Theorem

1.3
to dimension at most 1

The following allows us to reduce the statement of
Theorem

1.3
to the case
\dim R\leq 1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of almost minimal multiplicity of dimension
\dim R\geq 2
. Assume that
k
is infinite. Then there exists an
R
-regular element
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
such that
x+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
-regular. In particular, letting
\overline{R}:=R/(x)
and
\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}:={\mathfrak{m}}/(x)
, the following statements hold simultaneously:

(1)

(\overline{R},\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}},k)
is Cohen–Macaulay of almost minimal multiplicity of dimension
\dim R-1
;

(2)

\lind_{R}k=\lind_{\overline{R}}k
;

(3)

R
is Koszul if and only if
\overline{R}
is so.

The notions of superficial elements (in
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
R
) and filter-regular elements (with respect to a graded ring) will be crucial to our discussions.

Definition 3.2
.

(1) Given a local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
, we say
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
is a
superficial element of
{\mathfrak{m}}
with respect to
R
(
superficial element of
{\mathfrak{m}}
for short), if there exists a non-negative integer
c
such that

({\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}:x)\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{c}={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}\quad\text{for every $n\geq c$}.

A sequence
x_{1},\ldots,x_{s}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
is called a
superficial sequence of
{\mathfrak{m}}
if for every
i=1,\ldots,s
, the image of
x_{i}
in
{\mathfrak{m}}/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
is a superficial element with respect to
R/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})
.

(2) Let
A=\bigoplus_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}A_{n}
be a graded ring. We say that a homogeneous element
a\in A_{d}
is
filter-regular with respect to
A
if for all
n\gg 0
, the map
A_{n}\xrightarrow{\cdot a}A_{n+d}
is injective.

It is straightforward to check that an element
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
is superficial (with respect to
R
) if and only if
x+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is filter-regular with respect to
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}R
.

Remark 3.3
.

(1) By
[
SH06
, Lemma 8.5.4]
, if
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
is superficial and
\depth R\geq 1
, then
x
is also
R
-regular. If moreover the residue field
k=R/{\mathfrak{m}}
is infinite and
\depth R\geq 1
, then there exists
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
which is both a superficial element of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
R
-regular; see
[
SH06
, Corollary 8.5.9]
for details.

(2) Assume that
k
is infinite and either
\dim R\geq 2
or
\depth R\geq 1
. Let
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be a superficial element that is not contained in any minimal prime ideal of
R
(whose existence is guaranteed by
[
SH06
, Corollary 8.5.9]
), and
\overline{R}=R/(x)
. As mentioned above, if
\depth R\geq 1
, then the superficiality of
x
implies that
x
is
R
-regular. Therefore, using
[
SH06
, Proposition 11.1.9(2)]
for
M=R
, there is an equality
e(R)=e(\overline{R})
. In particular, if
R
has almost minimal multiplicity, then so is
\overline{R}
.

Proof of
Proposition

3.1
.

By
[
RV96
,
W97
]
,
\depth\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\geq\dim R-1\geq 1
. So by
Remark

3.3
, there exists
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
which is both a superficial element of
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
R
-regular; in fact, it suffices to choose
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
such that
x+{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
-regular. Now
(\overline{R},\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}},k)
is Cohen–Macaulay of almost minimal multiplicity of dimension
\dim R-1
thanks to
Remark

3.3
. We have
\lind_{R}\overline{R}=0
, so
\lind_{R}k=\lind_{\overline{R}}k
by
[
Ng15
, Theorem 5.2]
. The last equality implies that
R
is Koszul if and only if
\overline{R}
is so, and the proof is completed.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

(1) The argument of

cannot be used to reduce the statement of

furnishes a concrete example. We have

(2) While given

4.
Weak Koszul filtration

Inspired by the notion of Koszul filtration for graded algebras
[
CTV
]
(originating from work of Bruns, Herzog, and Vetter
[
BHV
, Page 10]
), the second author introduced in
[
Ng15
]
an analogous tool to detect Koszulness of local rings.

Definition 4.1

(
[
Ng15
, Definition 4.1]
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a local ring. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of
ideals. We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
Koszul filtration
of
R
if the following simultaneously hold:

(1)

(0),{\mathfrak{m}}\in\mathcal{F}

(2)

for every ideal
I\in\mathcal{F}
and all
s\geq 1
, we have
I\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{s+1}={\mathfrak{m}}^{s}I
, and

(3)

for every ideal
I\neq(0)
of
\mathcal{F}
, there exist a finite filtration
(0)=I_{0}\subset I_{1}\subset\cdots\subset I_{n}=I
and elements
x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
, such that for each
j=1,\ldots,n,I_{j}\in\mathcal{F},I_{j}=I_{j-1}+(x_{j})
and
I_{j-1}:x_{j}\in\mathcal{F}
.

By
[
Ng15
, Theorem 4.3(i)-(ii)]
, if
R
has a Koszul filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
, then
R
is Koszul. Moreover, for any
I
in a Koszul filtration
{\mathcal{F}}
of
R
,
\lind_{R}(R/I)=0
, and hence by
Proposition

2.1
(2),
\lind_{R}I=0
. Thus
I
is a Koszul
R
-module for any ideal
I\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a regular local ring, and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
be a minimal generating set of
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Then
R
has a Koszul filtration
\mathcal{F}=\{(0),(x_{1}),\ldots,(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\}
. Indeed, it suffices to check for each
1\leq j\leq d
and
s\geq 1
the following equality where
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{j})
:

(4.1)

I\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{s+1}={\mathfrak{m}}^{s}I.

This is a consequence of
Lemma

4.3
(2) below. Hence every regular local ring is Koszul. Moreover, any ideal generated by a subset of a minimal generating set of
{\mathfrak{m}}
is a Koszul
R
-module.

Lemma 4.3
.

In a noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
, let
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}
be an
R
-regular sequence in
{\mathfrak{m}}
, where
m,n\geq 1
. Denote
I=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}),J=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
. Then for all integers
s,t\geq 1
,
i\geq 0
, there are equalities

(1)

I^{s}\cap J^{t}=I^{s}J^{t}
,

(2)

I^{s}\cap(I+J)^{s+i}=I^{s}(I+J)^{i}
.

Proof.

(1) We have to show that
\Tor^{R}_{1}(R/I^{s},R/J^{t})=0
. Since
I
is generated by a regular sequence,
I^{i}/I^{i+1}
is a free
(R/I)
-module for each
i\geq 0
, by
[
BH
, Theorem 1.1.8]
. Similar thing holds for
J^{i}/J^{i+1}
. Using exact sequences of the form

0\to I^{i}/I^{i+1}\to R/I^{i+1}\to R/I^{i}\to 0,

and induction, we reduce to the case
s=1
, namely showing that
\Tor^{R}_{1}(R/I,R/J^{t})=0
. Similar arguments show that it suffices to assume
s=t=1
, and to show
\Tor^{R}_{1}(R/I,R/J)=0
. The last statement is true since
I
is generated by an
(R/J)
-regular sequence; see, e.g.,
[
BH
, Exercise 1.1.12]
. This concludes the proof of (1).

(2) Denote
L=I+J
. As
L^{s+i}=I^{s}L^{i}+J^{i+1}L^{s-1}
, we get the first equality in the chain

\displaystyle I^{s}\cap L^{s+i}

\displaystyle=I^{s}\cap(I^{s}L^{i}+J^{i+1}L^{s-1})=I^{s}L^{i}+I^{s}\cap J^{i+1}L^{s-1}

\displaystyle\subseteq I^{s}L^{i}+I^{s}\cap J^{i+1}=I^{s}L^{i}+I^{s}J^{i+1}=I^{s}L^{i}\subseteq I^{s}\cap L^{s+i}.

The third equality holds by (1). Thus equalities hold from left to right, and the proof is concluded.
∎

Definition 4.4

(Weak Koszul filtration)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a noetherian local ring. A family of
proper
ideals
{\mathcal{F}}
of
R
is called a
weak Koszul filtration
if the following conditions hold:

(WF1)

(0),{\mathfrak{m}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
;

(WF2)

for every
I\in{\mathcal{F}}
,
I\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}={\mathfrak{m}}I
;

(WF3)

for every nonzero
I\in{\mathcal{F}}
, there exist
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
and
J\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
I=J+(x)
and
J:x\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

The following example shows that the notion of weak Koszul filtration strictly subsumes that of Koszul filtration.

Example 4.5
.

It is not hard to see that if
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a standard graded
k
-algebra, and
{\mathcal{F}}
is a Koszul filtration in the sense of Conca et al.
[
CTV
]
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is a weak Koszul filtration. It is also clear that any Koszul filtration in the sense of
[
Ng15
, Definition 4.1]
is a weak Koszul filtration.

However, the converse is not true: There are local rings with a weak Koszul filtration, but are not Koszul. For example, let
R=k[[x,y]]/(xy,x^{3}-y^{2})
. Then
R
has the following weak Koszul filtration (by abuse of notation, we denote the residue class in
R
of an element
a\in k[[x,y]]
by
a
itself):

\{(0),(x),(y),{\mathfrak{m}}=(x,y)\}.

Indeed, we can check the following equalities, where the last two hold since
x,y\notin{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
:

\displaystyle(0):x

\displaystyle=(y),

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=(x^{2}),

\displaystyle(0):y

\displaystyle=(x),

\displaystyle(x)\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}={\mathfrak{m}}(x),

\displaystyle(x):y

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{m}},

\displaystyle(y)\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}={\mathfrak{m}}(y).

On the other hand,
R
is not a Koszul ring since
\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)\cong k[x,y]/(xy,y^{2},x^{4})
is not a Koszul
k
-algebra, as the latter is not even defined by quadrics.

Remark 4.6
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a weak Koszul filtration of
R
, and
I\neq(0)
an ideal in
{\mathcal{F}}
. We claim that there exist elements
y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
such that:

(1)

I
is minimally generated by
y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}
, and

(2)

for each
1\leq i\leq p
, we have
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i})\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1}):y_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Indeed, by (WF3), there exists an element
y_{1}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
and
J_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}
such that
I=(y_{1})+J_{1}
and
J_{1}:y_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. By definition,
{\mathcal{F}}
does not contain the unit ideal, so
y_{1}\notin J_{1}
, which in turn implies
J_{1}\neq I
. Nakayama’s lemma then implies that
y_{1}\notin{\mathfrak{m}}I=I\cap{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
(per (WF2)), consequently
y_{1}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
.

If
J_{1}=(0)
, then
I=(y_{1})
, and we are done. If
J_{1}\neq(0)
, since
J_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}
, we can continue the above argument to get a filtration of ideals
I=J_{0}\supsetneq J_{1}\supsetneq J_{2}\supsetneq\cdots
and elements
y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{m}}
such that for each
i\geq 1
:

(1)

J_{i-1}=(y_{i})+J_{i}
,

(2)

J_{i},J_{i}:y_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}
,

(3)

y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{m}}\setminus{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
.

Since
J_{i-1}
strictly contains
J_{i}\supseteq(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1})
, we have
y_{i}\notin(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1})
. The strictly increasing chain

(y_{1})\subsetneq(y_{1},y_{2})\subsetneq(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})\subsetneq\cdots

has to terminate, so there exists some
p\geq 1
such that
J_{p}=(0)
. For this
p
, we get that
y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}
is a minimal generating set of
I
,
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i})\in{\mathcal{F}}
and
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{i-1}):y_{i}\in{\mathcal{F}}
for each
1\leq i\leq p
. This is the desired conclusion.

A key property of any weak Koszul filtration is that it induces short exact sequences with Tor-vanishing maps.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a noetherian local ring and
{\mathcal{F}}
a weak Koszul filtration of
R
. Let
(0)\neq I\in{\mathcal{F}}
be an ideal and let
x\in{\mathfrak{m}}
and
J\in{\mathcal{F}}
be such that
I=J+(x)
and
J:x\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
(
The existence of

and

Then:

(1)

For all
i\geq 1
, the natural map
\Tor^{R}_{i}(R/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2},R/I)\to\Tor^{R}_{i}(R/{\mathfrak{m}},R/I)

\Tor^{R}_{i}(R/{\mathfrak{m}},R/I)\to\Tor^{R}_{i-1}({\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2},R/I)

(2)

The natural map

Proof.

The subsequent argument is similar to
[

The conclusion is clear if

(S1)

The following lemma allows us to lift a weak Koszul filtration from a quotient ring modulo a regular element to the original ring. In fact, we will prove a slightly more general statement.

Lemma 4.8

(Canonical lifting of a weak Koszul filtration)
.

Let

be a noetherian local ring, and

an element. Let

Assume moreover that at least one of the following conditions is satisfied:

, i.e.

is

-regular;

(b)

(

)

Let
\overline{I}\neq(0)
be an ideal of

. By

(2)

(\overline{y_{1}},\ldots,\overline{y_{i}})\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}
and
(\overline{y_{1}},\ldots,\overline{y_{i-1}}):\overline{y_{i}}\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}

Let

be the

, since

and

. Then

consists of ideals of the form

for each

(0)
. Moreover, for each
(0)\neq\overline{I}\in\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}

(ii)

(y)\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

Clearly
(1)\notin{\mathcal{F}}
. Now we verify that

satisfies the conditions (WF1)–(WF3).

(WF1): Since

contains the zero and maximal ideals of

,

contains the corresponding ideals of

.

(WF2): Take

, we check that

and

.

We may therefore assume that

, where

, and

per the hypothesis on
\overline{{\mathcal{F}}}

, where

, and the following two conditions are satisfied:

•

is minimally generated by
y,y_{1},\ldots,y_{p}
;

•

for each

5.
g
-stretched rings

Throughout this section, let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a noetherian local ring.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
R
is a
g
-stretched ring
(where “g” stands for “generalized”), if
\mu_{R}({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})\leq 1
, that is,
\dim_{k}({\mathfrak{m}}^{2}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{3})\leq 1
.

By Krull’s principal ideal theorem, if

is
g
-stretched, them
\dim R\leq 1
. Following Sally
[

]
, we say that

is
stretched
if it is
g
-stretched and of dimension

. Our aim in this section is to study in depth the dimension 1 case.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose
R
is a
g
-stretched Cohen–Macaulay ring of dimension 1. Then
R
is a DVR.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{m}}
be minimally generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
. Since
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}
is principal,
{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=(a_{i}a_{j})
for some
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
. By
[
BH
, Proposition 1.2.10]
, we have the second equality in the chain

1=\grade({\mathfrak{m}},R)=\grade({\mathfrak{m}}^{2},R)=\grade((a_{i}a_{j}),R).

Hence
a_{i}
is an
R
-regular element. For each

, since
a_{i}a_{l}\in(a_{i}a_{j})
, we get
a_{l}\in(a_{j})
. Hence

and

, which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

Note that a one-dimensional

-stretched ring need not be a DVR, for instance

. This is also a typical example for such rings by the following theorem, in which we completely describe
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adically complete one-dimensional

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
(S,{\mathfrak{n}})
be a regular local ring of dimension

, and

an ideal such that

is a one-dimensional
g
-stretched ring. Then

, where

is generated by a regular sequence of

In the proof, we will proceed by a double induction using the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(S,{\mathfrak{n}})
be a regular local ring of dimension

,

distinct proper ideals each of which is generated a regular sequence of

We can assume that

and

, where
y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{n}}\setminus{\mathfrak{n}}^{2}

, hence it is a principal ideal domain. The ideal
(Q_{1}+Q_{2})/Q_{1}\neq S/Q_{1}

Let

Claim 1:
Not all of

Assume the contrary, then

6.

The main result of this section strengthens a result due to Avramov, Iyengar and Şega
[

. These authors only consider rings

such that

is

-stretched and

, while we consider all

-stretched local rings.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
R
be a
g
-stretched local ring. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is Koszul;

(2)

Either
\dim R=1
, or
R
is artinian,
{\mathfrak{m}}^{3}=0
, and

unless

.

The following remark will allow us to reduce to the complete case; note that if

is artinian then

and

.

Remark 6.2

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a noetherian local ring, and
(\widehat{R},\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})
its
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

R
is Koszul if and only if
\widehat{R}
is so.

(2)

For each

, there is an equality
\mu({\mathfrak{m}}^{i})=\mu(\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i})
. In particular,

is

-stretched if and only if
\widehat{R}
is so, and
{\mathfrak{m}}^{i}=0
if and only if
\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}=0

(3)

\dim R=\dim\widehat{R}
.

(4)

\rk_{k}(0:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}})=\rk_{k}(0:_{\widehat{R}}\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})
.

Indeed, (1) was mentioned in

, and it is also a consequence of the fact that for all

. The same fact implies (2), while (3) is classical.

For (4), let

. By flatness,
(0:_{\widehat{R}}\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})=I\widehat{R}=\widehat{I}
. Using
{\mathfrak{m}}I=0
, we get

Hence the natural map

is an isomorphism, and
\rk_{k}(0:_{R}{\mathfrak{m}})=\mu(I)=\mu(\widehat{I})=\rk_{k}(0:_{\widehat{R}}\widehat{{\mathfrak{m}}})
, as desired.

One ingredient for the implication (1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) in

6.1
is

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

Proof.

We first reduce to the case when

Note that since

For the statement, we use induction on the minimal number of homogeneous generators of

Since

9.
Questions and remarks

In view of
Theorem

7.1
and its proof, it is natural to ask the following questions.

Question 9.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a
g
-stretched, artinian local ring with
\chara(k)=p>0
. Is it true that if
\lind_{R}k<\infty
, then
R
is Koszul?

Question 9.2
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be an absolutely Koszul local ring such that
\mu({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})\leq 3
. Is it true that
R
is a Koszul ring?

If
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
is a standard graded
k
-algebra with
\mu({\mathfrak{m}}^{2})\leq 3
, thanks to works of D’Alì
[
D
]
and Ahangari-Maleki and Şega
[
AMS20
]
, we have the remarkable equivalence that
R
is Koszul if and only if it is absolutely Koszul; see
[
AMS20
, Main Theorem]
.

As mentioned before, Conca, De Negri and Rossi
[
CDR
, Proposition 6.1.8]
showed that if
R
is Cohen–Macaulay of almost minimal multiplicity, with the type
r(R)\leq\codim(R)-1
, then
R
is Koszul. As a corollary of
Theorem

6.1
, we prove that the converse of their result holds in the graded case.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a standard graded
k
-algebra. Assume that
R
is Cohen–Macaulay of almost minimal multiplicity, namely
e(R)=\codim(R)+2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

R
is Koszul;

(2)

r(R)\leq\codim(R)-1
.

Proof.

For (2)
\Longrightarrow
(1), we may use the argument of
[
CDR
, Proposition 6.1.8]
. Conversely, assume that
R
is Koszul. Let
\overline{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
. Passing from
R
to
R\otimes_{k}\overline{k}
does not affect the statement, so we may assume that
k
is infinite.

Now we reduce to the artinian case. Assume that
\dim(R)\geq 1
. Since
R
is a Cohen–Macaulay and
k
is infinite, we may choose a regular
R
-sequence
\boldsymbol{x}=x_{1},\ldots,x_{\dim(R)}
consisting of linear forms. Passing from
R
to
\overline{R}=R/(\boldsymbol{x})R
does not affect the statement: the Koszul property, the type, and codimension of
R
are not affected by such an operation, as
\boldsymbol{x}
is a regular sequence of linear forms. Hence we may assume that
R
is artinian. That
\ell(R)=\mu({\mathfrak{m}})+2
implies that
R
is artinian
g
-stretched. The argument of
Theorem

6.1
, (1)
\Longrightarrow
(2) goes through in this case, and therefore
r(R)\leq\codim(R)-1
, as desired.
∎

We have no counterexample to the following local version of
Corollary

9.3
.

Question 9.4
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Cohen–Macaulay local ring of almost minimal multiplicity. If
R
is Koszul, does it follow that
r(R)\leq\codim(R)-1

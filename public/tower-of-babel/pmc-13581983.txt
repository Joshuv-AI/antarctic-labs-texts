Structure-activity relationship of adipate-bridged β-sitosterol glycosyl derivatives as novel cholesterol alternatives for liposome stabilization
Authors: Yuyuan Hu, Xueyao Zhang, Yuanlan Zhou, Heng Du, Shangde Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Liposomes stabilized by adipate-bridged β-sitosterol glycosyl derivatives (hereafter, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates) were characterized to elaborate their structure-activity relationship (SAR) as effective cholesterol alternatives. Compared with liposomes stabilized by cholesterol or β-sitosterol, those stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates exhibited improved bilayer structural properties. Among all formulations, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol sucrose adipate possessed the most compact lipid packing and the smallest particle size (<80 nm). After curcumin (Cur) loading, the optimized liposomes reached an encapsulation efficiency of 78.67% ± 2.13%, maintained an apparent Cur retention rate above 97% over 28-day storage at 25 °C, and achieved in vitro bioaccessibility exceeding 44%. SAR analysis revealed that hydrogen-bonding interactions and glycosyl steric hindrance worked synergistically to regulate the physicochemical and functional properties of the liposomes. This study facilitates the development of amphiphilic phytosterol derivatives and supports the design of efficient cholesterol-free liposomal carrier systems for food applications.

Introduction

Liposomes serve as effective delivery systems for drugs and other bioactive molecules. Owing to their phospholipid bilayer structure, they can enhance the bioavailability of both hydrophilic and hydrophobic substances (Aditya et al., 2015). In recent years, liposomes have garnered increasing attention for their wide-ranging applications in the food, pharmaceutical, and cosmeceutical industries (Ren et al., 2025; Li et al., 2025). Cholesterol is a crucial membrane constituent in conventional liposomal formulations, regulating bilayer fluidity and mechanical strength. However, growing concerns regarding the biosafety of exogenous cholesterol, particularly its potential association with cardiovascular risks upon chronic consumption, have spurred considerable interest in developing cholesterol-free or low-cholesterol delivery systems (Nsairat et al., 2024). The identification of biocompatible and efficient cholesterol alternatives remains a critical challenge in the design of advanced food-grade nanocarriers. Phytosterols have garnered extensive research interest owing to their versatile biological functions, especially their prominent cholesterol-lowering capacity (Hu et al., 2022a). Due to their structural similarity to cholesterol, phytosterols and their derivatives have been validated in numerous studies as viable cholesterol alternatives that can insert into phospholipid bilayers and interact with membrane components to modulate membrane properties.

Importantly, the structural diversity of C3 substituents among different phytosterols leads to significant differences in the microstructure and physicochemical properties of liposomal membranes. Early relevant research mainly focused on free phytosterols. For example, Song et al. (2022a) systematically compared the effects of cholesterol, β-sitosterol, and stigmasterol on the characteristics of liposomal membranes. All three types of sterols were found to improve the physical stability of liposomes and reduce membrane deformability by increasing particle size and membrane rigidity. Notably, β-sitosterol demonstrated the best performance in inhibiting phospholipid hydrolysis and oxidation, which is essential for preserving membrane integrity during long-term storage. Based on these findings, hydrophobic phytosterol derivatives, typically phytosterol esters, have been developed for the construction of cholesterol-free delivery carriers. As reported, phytosterol oleate has been successfully applied to fabricate astaxanthin-loaded liposomes (Zhong et al., 2025). The obtained nanoparticles with particle sizes ranging from 100 to 220 nm possessed favorable morphological stability. Furthermore, the encapsulation of astaxanthin within such liposomes effectively alleviated its degradation during simulated gastrointestinal digestion, thereby remarkably improving its antioxidant activity and oral bioavailability.

More recently, attention has shifted towards amphiphilic phytosterol derivatives. These derivatives are synthesized by directly conjugating free phytosterols with polar moieties or indirectly cross-linking them via linkers. These compounds were initially engineered to markedly improve the water solubility and bioavailability of native phytosterols, yet emerging evidence suggests they possess great potential to replace cholesterol for liposome stabilization. Liu et al. (2023) explored the feasibility of three typical phytosterol derivatives, including free sterols, sterol esters, and sterol glycosides. Their study verified that sterol glycoside incorporation can greatly strengthen membrane rigidity, which is mainly attributed to the formation of extensive hydrogen-bonding networks between the sugar moieties of sterol glycosides and the polar headgroups of phospholipids. Therefore, amphiphilic phytosterol derivatives represent promising cholesterol alternatives for the fabrication of next-generation liposomal delivery systems with customized functionalities.

Previously, we synthesized a homologous series of adipate-bridged β-sitosterol glycosyl derivatives (hereafter, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates). Using divinyl adipate as a molecular linker, β-sitosterol was conjugated with linear sugar alcohols (including erythritol, xylitol, and sorbitol) and cyclic carbohydrates (including glucose, sucrose, and raffinose) (Hu et al., 2022b, 2022c). The chemical structures of β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates are displayed in Fig. 1. They possess distinctive functional properties, notably forming aqueous liquid-crystalline phases that confer great potential for stabilizing liposome architectures (Hu et al., 2022b). All compounds share an identical flexible adipate spacer, while systematic differences exist among them in terms of hydroxyl-group numbers and glycosyl structures. Accordingly, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates were characterized to elaborate their structure-activity relationship (SAR) as effective cholesterol alternatives. These findings will provide fundamental insights for the development and application of amphiphilic phytosterol derivatives and facilitate the rational design of high-performance, cholesterol-free food-grade liposomal carriers.Fig. 1Chemical structures of β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates.

Materials and methods

Chemicals

Cholesterol (Chol, ≥ 95%) and β-sitosterol (β-Sit, > 90%) were purchased from Aladdin Biochemical Technology Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). Soybean phosphatidylcholine (SPC, > 90%) and curcumin (Cur, 98%) were supplied by Macklin Co., Ltd. (Shanghai, China). β-Sitosterol erythritol adipate (β-Sit-Ery), β-sitosterol xylitol adipate (β-Sit-Xyl), β-sitosterol sorbitol adipate (β-Sit-Sor), β-sitosterol glucose adipate (β-Sit-Glu), β-sitosterol sucrose adipate (β-Sit-Suc), and β-sitosterol raffinose adipate (β-Sit-Raf) were prepared and structurally characterized by nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and mass spectrometry (MS) following our previous reports (Hu et al., 2022b, 2022c). Their high purity (>96%) was determined by HPLC in another published study (Hu et al., 2026). All other chemicals and solvents were of analytical or higher grade.

Preparation of liposomes

All liposomal formulations in this study followed a unified naming convention to clearly distinguish three groups: blank control (sterol-free and Cur-free soybean phosphatidylcholine, abbreviated as SPC); sterol-stabilized unloaded liposomes (sterol-containing, Cur-free, coded by the incorporated sterol, e.g., SPC-Chol and SPC-β-Sit-Suc); and Cur-loaded liposomes (sterol-containing and/or Cur-containing, denoted by appending “Cur” to the corresponding unloaded code, including the blank Cur-loaded liposomes SPC-Cur and sterol-stabilized liposomes such as SPC-Chol-Cur and SPC-β-Sit-Suc-Cur).

Liposomes were prepared using a modified thin-film hydration method as previously described (Song et al., 2022b). Briefly, SPC and sterols (Chol, β-Sit, β-Sit-Ery, β-Sit-Xyl, β-Sit-Sor, β-Sit-Glu, β-Sit-Suc, β-Sit-Raf) were dissolved in absolute ethanol. The molar ratio of SPC to sterols was set as 3:1, based on our gradient pre-experiments and published optimization data reported in Song et al. (2022a). The organic solvent was removed by rotary evaporation at 50 °C to form a lipid film, which was subsequently dried under vacuum overnight to ensure complete solvent elimination. The film was then rehydrated with 20 mL of ultrapure water at 50 °C for 30 min, yielding a lipid suspension with a final SPC concentration of 10 mg/mL. Thereafter, the suspension was probe-sonicated in an ice bath using a JY92-II DN ultrasonic processor (Ningbo Xinzhi Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China) for 5 min (450 W, 5 s on/5 s off). Finally, the dispersion was filtered through a 0.45 μm nylon syringe filter to obtain the blank control (SPC) and all unloaded sterol-stabilized liposomes (SPC-Chol, SPC-β-Sit, SPC-β-Sit-Ery, SPC-β-Sit-Xyl, SPC-β-Sit-Sor, SPC-β-Sit-Glu, SPC-β-Sit-Suc, SPC-β-Sit-Raf).

For the preparation of Cur-loaded liposomes, Cur was co-dissolved with SPC and sterols in absolute ethanol, followed by thin-film hydration as described above. The mass ratio of SPC to Cur was set at 10:1 (w/w). The remaining preparation steps were identical to those for unloaded liposomes, yielding the blank Cur-loaded liposome (SPC-Cur) and all sterol-stabilized Cur-loaded liposomes (SPC-Chol-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Ery-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Xyl-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Sor-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Glu-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Suc-Cur, SPC-β-Sit-Raf-Cur).

Average particle size, polydispersity index (PDI), and zeta potential measurements

The average particle size, PDI, and zeta potential of the samples were measured by dynamic light scattering (DLS) using a Zetasizer Nano ZS series instrument (Malvern Instruments Ltd., Worcestershire, UK). For particle size and PDI analysis, samples were diluted 100-fold and then transferred into a standard 1 cm × 1 cm polystyrene cuvette. For zeta potential determination, undiluted samples were injected into a ZETA DTS1070 capillary cell. All samples were equilibrated at 25 °C for 2 min prior to measurement to ensure thermal equilibrium. Each measurement was performed in triplicate.

Transmission electron microscopy (TEM) observation

Samples were diluted 10-fold with deionized water and deposited onto 200-mesh carbon support grids. Excess liquid was removed using filter paper, followed by negative staining with 2% (w/v) phosphotungstic acid for 3 min. The grids were then air-dried at 25 °C. Morphological analysis was performed using an HT7700 transmission electron microscope (Hitachi, Tokyo, Japan) operated at 100 kV, and images were acquired at a magnification of 40,000×.

Atomic force microscopy (AFM) measurements

Liposome samples were diluted 1000-fold with deionized water, deposited on freshly cleaved mica substrates, and completely dried before testing. All measurements were performed in dry-air mode using an MFP-3D Infinity AFM (Asylum Research, Oxford Instruments, Santa Barbara, CA, USA) integrated with AR SPM software based on IGOR Pro (WaveMetrics, Lake Oswego, OR, USA), with minor modifications to a previously reported protocol (Liu et al., 2023). Tapping-mode imaging was used to locate liposomes before force-curve acquisition. Over 200 force-indentation curves were acquired for each formulation. The set-point maximum force was fixed at 1000 pN. To eliminate substrate interference, only liposomes with a height exceeding 35 nm were selected. Force-indentation curves were fitted using the Shell model to obtain the apparent Young's modulus. The fitting slope of the curves reflects the stiffness of dehydrated lipid films.

Raman spectroscopy

Lyophilized liposome powders were deposited onto glass slides wrapped with aluminum paper and observed under a 50× objective. Raman spectra were acquired over a wavenumber range of 500 to 3100 cm−1 using a LabRAM HR Evolution confocal Raman microscope (HORIBA France SAS, France) equipped with a 633 nm He-Ne laser. Characteristic peak intensities at 718, 1440, 2850, and 2890 cm−1 were extracted. Subsequently, the band intensity ratios I718/I1440 and I2890/I2850 were calculated to evaluate lipid order.

Attenuated total reflection-fourier transform infrared (ATR-FTIR) spectroscopy

Lyophilized liposome samples were directly analyzed using a Nicolet 6700 FTIR spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, Madison, WI, USA) equipped with an ATR accessory. Spectra were acquired over the range of 4000-400 cm−1 with a resolution of 2 cm−1. A background spectrum was collected against ambient air prior to sample measurement. Each spectrum represented the accumulation of 32 scans.

Determination of encapsulation efficiency (EE)

The EE of Cur-loaded liposomes was measured following a published protocol with slight modifications (Tai et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2015). Samples were centrifuged at 20,160 × g and 4 °C for 1 h to separate free and surface-adsorbed Cur. The resulting pellet was resuspended in ice-cold PBS and subjected to three additional washing cycles to remove residual unencapsulated Cur. The final pellet was solubilized in methanol to extract the encapsulated Cur. The concentration of curcumin was quantified at 455 nm using a UV-Vis spectrophotometer (UV-2450, Shimadzu, Japan), based on a standard calibration curve established via full-wavelength scanning. EE (%) was calculated as the percentage ratio of the mass of encapsulated Cur to the total mass of Cur used in the initial fabrication.

Storage stability of Cur-loaded liposomes

The storage stability of Cur-loaded liposomes was evaluated at 25 °C for 28 days. The average hydrodynamic diameter, PDI, zeta potential, and EE were monitored every 7 days. Additionally, apparent Cur retention in the liposomal suspensions was quantified. Aliquots (500 μL) of the liposomal suspension were collected, and the residual Cur content was determined by measuring the absorbance at 455 nm. Apparent Cur retention rate (%) was calculated as the ratio of the remaining Cur concentration after storage to the initial Cur concentration in freshly prepared liposomes.

In vitro dynamic gastrointestinal digestion

Dynamic gastrointestinal digestion was performed using an in vitro dynamic human stomach intestine system IV (DHSI-IV, Xiao Dong Pro-health (Suzhou) Instrumentation Co., Ltd., Suzhou, China) according to the procedures described previously (Liu et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2026) with slight modifications. The oral digestion phase was excluded due to the negligible residence time of liquids in the mouth (Liu et al., 2022). Electrolyte simulated gastric fluid (eSGF) and electrolyte simulated intestinal fluid (eSIF) for the gastric and intestinal phases were prepared according to the standardized protocol (Mulet-Cabero et al., 2020). SGF and SIF were further prepared following the procedure of Makran et al. (2022) with minor modifications. Pepsin, gastric lipase, and calcium chloride (CaCl2) were sequentially added to eSGF, yielding simulated gastric fluid (SGF, pH 1.6) with final concentrations of 2000 U/mL (based on enzyme activity), 60 U/mL, and 0.15 mM, respectively. SIF (pH 7.4) was prepared by supplementing eSIF with porcine trypsin (100 U/mL), pancreatic lipase (2000 U/mL), bile salts (10 mM), and CaCl2 (0.6 mM).

In brief, Cur-loaded liposomes were delivered into the gastric module over 5 min via a funnel at a flow rate of 46 mL/min. Before sample addition, 20 mL of SGF was injected into the stomach model using a syringe pump to simulate the fasted state (Mudie et al., 2014). Gastric digesta pH was monitored in real time with a micro pH probe connected to an MP551 pH meter (San-Xin Instrumentation Inc., Shanghai, China). Intestinal digesta pH was recorded at 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, and 180 min using a SevenCompact S210 pH meter (Mettler Toledo, Switzerland). A pneumatically driven roller extrusion device was activated to generate 3 contractions per minute (cpm) in the stomach model and 12 cpm in the intestinal model. The pyloric valve was set to open every 2 min with an aperture of 0.5 mm during digestion. SGF was infused into the gastric model at pre-programmed flow rates: 1−10 min: 1.6 mL/min; 10−20 min: 2.4 mL/min; 20−30 min: 3.0 mL/min; 30−40 min: 3.8 mL/min; 40−50 min: 3.2 mL/min; 50−60 min: 2.4 mL/min; 60−180 min: 2.2 mL/min. Meanwhile, SIF was continuously infused into the duodenum model at an average rate of 2.4 mL/min for 180 min. To precisely control the gastric emptying rate, the turntable rotation protocol was set as: 1−5 min: +2.0°/min; 5−30 min: −0.5°/min; 30−120 min: −0.4°/min, where “+” and “−” indicate clockwise and anticlockwise rotation, respectively.

Upon completion of digestion, the system was terminated. The intestinal digesta were acidified to pH 2.0 to terminate enzymatic activity, followed by centrifugation at 17,000 × g and 4 °C for 1 h. The resulting supernatant, designated as the bioaccessible fraction, was collected to determine Cur content. Cur bioaccessibility (%) was calculated as the ratio of the Cur concentration in the bioaccessible fraction to the initial Cur concentration of freshly prepared liposomes.

Statistical analysis

All measurements were performed at least in triplicate, and data are expressed as the mean ± standard deviation (SD). One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Duncan's multiple range test was conducted using SPSS software (version 19.0, SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) to determine significant differences among groups. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results and discussion

Average particle size, PDI, and zeta potential of blank and unloaded liposomes

Particle size, PDI and zeta potential characterize liposomal microstructure and colloidal stability. As presented in Table 1, all formulations exhibited nanoscale dimensions (<200 nm). Sterol-free SPC liposomes showed a particle size of 135.23 ± 1.98 nm. Incorporation of cholesterol or β-sitosterol caused a slight reduction in particle size, whereas β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates induced a more pronounced decrease. This trend is consistent with previous observations in bile salt-stabilized cholesterol-free liposomes (Jeong et al., 2025). This difference is closely related to the amphiphilic structure consisting of a hydrophobic steroid skeleton, hydrophilic glycosyl headgroup, and flexible adipate linker. The adipate bridge regulates the insertion depth of the steroid skeleton into the phospholipid bilayer and facilitates interactions between glycosyl headgroups and phospholipid polar groups. Beyond acting as membrane fillers, these derivatives were likely to function as co-surfactants or edge-activators. The data further revealed that β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates with more hydroxyl groups generally yielded smaller vesicles. Notably, SPC-β-Sit-Suc exhibited a much smaller particle size (79.43 ± 2.33 nm) than SPC-β-Sit-Raf (97.34 ± 2.48 nm), although the latter contains more hydroxyl groups. This indicates that liposome size follows the SAR, which originates from a balance between hydrogen-bonding capacity and glycosyl steric hindrance. The rigid cyclic moiety of SPC-β-Sit-Raf creates greater steric hindrance, disrupting favorable curvature-inducing properties and resulting in looser lipid packing and larger vesicles. In contrast, the optimal synergy of abundant hydroxyl groups and moderate steric hindrance in SPC-β-Sit-Suc yields the most tightly packed vesicles.

Regarding the PDI values, all formulations displayed values between 0.12 and 0.28, far below the critical threshold of 0.3, indicating the formation of uniformly distributed and stable liposome systems (Song et al., 2022c). Among all samples, SPC-β-Sit-Suc possessed the minimum PDI (0.12 ± 0.04), further verifying its excellent structural homogeneity.

For zeta potential, blank SPC liposomes exhibited a zeta potential of −2.78 ± 0.17 mV, consistent with previous literature (Chen et al., 2022). Free cholesterol and β-sitosterol barely altered the surface electronegativity of liposomes, while β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates slightly reduced surface negative charge, with zeta potential ranging from −2.02 to −2.54 mV. These neutral derivatives likely insert into the phospholipid bilayer interface and mildly weaken electrostatic repulsion. Our prior work also found that β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates readily coat micelle surfaces and lower overall electronegativity (Hu et al., 2023). Nevertheless, colloidal stability of these liposomes is predominantly governed by steric-hydration forces originating from bulky glycosyl chains.

TEM images of liposomes

TEM images (Fig. 2) visually confirmed the effects of β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates on liposomal morphology. Consistent with previous literature (Bhargavi et al., 2021), all liposomes retained intact spherical vesicular structures without obvious aggregation, demonstrating favorable dispersibility. Sterol-free, cholesterol-stabilized, and β-sitosterol-stabilized liposomes displayed relatively large, irregular vesicles with high membrane fluidity and deformability. In contrast, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates exhibited uniform size, regular spherical morphology, and obvious interfacial contrast differences at the vesicle boundary. The improved morphology was attributed to the enhanced ordering and compactness of the phospholipid bilayer induced by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates, which effectively reinforced membrane stability. Similar findings have been reported that sterol glycosides can reduce deformability (Liu et al., 2023). The uniform vesicle size observed by TEM is also consistent with the results of particle size measurements. Notably, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates facilitated the formation of smaller liposomes, and the altered interfacial structural characteristics were ascribed to the insertion of these adipate derivatives into the membrane structure. Among all samples, SPC-β-Sit-Suc liposomes showed the smallest and most homogeneous particle distribution. It has been reported that the molecular structure of sterols governs phospholipid packing within liposomes, thereby resulting in distinct morphological features (Tai et al., 2018). The stiffness of dehydrated lipid films

In this study, all apparent Young's modulus values were obtained by AFM measurements under dry-air mode. These values reflect the stiffness of dehydrated lipid films and serve as a relative indirect indicator of intermolecular lipid packing strength, rather than the native mechanical elasticity of hydrated liposomes in aqueous dispersion.

Force-indentation curves were fitted using the Shell model (Fig. 3A). Liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates exhibited steeper slopes than blank SPC, cholesterol-stabilized, and β-sitosterol-stabilized liposomes, indicating stronger deformation resistance of their dehydrated lipid structures. Consistent with this trend, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates displayed markedly higher apparent Young's modulus values (Fig. 3B). Among all formulations, SPC-β-Sit-Suc and SPC-β-Sit-Raf presented the highest stiffness values.Fig. 3Force-indentation curves fitted by the Shell model (a) and apparent Young's modulus (dry-air mode) of different liposome formulations (b). The enhanced stiffness of dehydrated lipid films possibly originated from abundant glycosyl hydroxyl groups, which may form hydrogen-bonding networks with phospholipids. These interactions tighten lipid packing and restrict molecular motion, thereby improving membrane mechanical behaviour. Hydrogen bonding and glycosyl steric hindrance acted synergistically to modulate film stiffness, further supporting the SAR. For instance, moderate steric hindrance from sucrose cooperated with hydrogen-bonding interactions to maximise lipid packing strength.

Raman spectral analysis

Raman spectroscopy can effectively characterize structural changes in liposomal phospholipid bilayers. Raman bands of liposomes are assigned to three regions: polar headgroup, hydrophobic acyl chains, and interfacial region (Sailer et al., 1997). The band at 718 cm−1 is assigned to the C-N stretching vibration of the phospholipid headgroup. Liposomes also exhibit a = CH wagging vibration mode at 1266 cm−1. The Raman band at 1301 cm−1 is assigned to the CH2 twisting vibration, while the CH2 scissoring vibration shows a characteristic peak at 1440 cm−1. The band at 1658 cm−1 is attributed to the cis C=C bond. The symmetric and antisymmetric CH2 stretching modes appear at around 2850 cm−1 and 2890 cm−1, respectively. Additionally, the symmetric stretching mode of the = CH bond is located at 3014 cm−1.

Fig. 4A shows that free sterols exerted almost no influence on the peak intensities, while β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates decreased the intensities of characteristic peaks to varying degrees. Such changes can be ascribed to the adsorption of β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates onto the phospholipid bilayer of liposomes. ATR-FTIR analysis

ATR-FTIR spectroscopy was employed to elucidate the molecular interactions between phospholipids and sterols. Phospholipid structural changes produce distinct infrared vibrational signals (Liu et al., 2023). The symmetric and asymmetric C-H stretching vibrations of methylene (CH2) appear as a distinctive doublet at 2850 cm−1 and 2920 cm−1, respectively. The ester carbonyl (C=O) group produces a prominent absorption band near 1735 cm−1. Characteristic polar headgroup bands were detected, including asymmetric stretching of N+-CH3 at 970 cm−1, as well as symmetric (1090 cm−1) and asymmetric (1250 cm−1) stretching of the phosphate group (PO2−).

As shown in Fig. 4B–D, all formulations shared similar main FTIR peaks, indicating good compatibility between free sterols or β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates and the lipid matrix with no chemical reactions (Sun et al., 2024). Relative to the symmetric and asymmetric C–H stretching bands of unstabilized SPC liposomes, minor band-position shifts could be observed in the corresponding C–H bands of both stabilized liposome types. Combined with Raman spectral analysis, these shifts supported the insertion of free sterols and β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates into the lipid bilayers. For all liposomal samples, no obvious shift was detected in the ester C=O stretching band, whereas minor band-position variations were observed for the polar headgroup bands (PO2− and N+-CH3). This spectral pattern suggests that hydrogen-bonding interactions between phospholipids and the hydroxyl groups of free sterols and β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates are primarily limited to the headgroup (phosphate and choline) rather than the glycerol backbone. These findings are in agreement with earlier reports (Song et al., 2022a; Liu et al., 2023).

Average particle size, PDI, zeta potential, and EE of Cur-loaded liposomes

Encapsulation of guest molecules may affect the physicochemical properties of liposomes, depending on interactions between encapsulated compounds and phospholipids or stabilizers. After Cur loading, all liposomes maintained favorable nanoscale characteristics with particle sizes below 200 nm. Notably, all Cur-loaded formulations exhibited a slight decrease in particle size compared to their unloaded counterparts. As a planar hydrophobic molecule, Cur intercalates into the acyl chain region and forms interfacial hydrogen bonds, acting as a molecular crosslinker that pulls lipid tails tighter (Karewicz et al., 2011). Furthermore, specific interactions between β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates and Cur resulted in a further reduction in liposomal size. A similar trend has been reported when cholesterol was replaced by sea cucumber sulfated sterol with highly polar groups and strong surface activity to prepare astaxanthin-loaded liposomes, in which the loaded liposomes also showed smaller particle sizes than empty ones (Srihera et al., 2022).

Regarding PDI values, all Cur-loaded liposomes exhibited PDI values ranging from 0.14 to 0.26, well below the threshold of 0.3, indicating the formation of monodisperse and homogeneous populations (Bagherpour et al., 2017). Importantly, no significant difference in PDI was observed between Cur-loaded and unloaded liposomes, suggesting that Cur loading did not compromise the structural uniformity of liposomes.

Compared with blank liposomes, the absolute zeta potential values were significantly increased in all Cur-loaded samples. The enhanced electronegativity could be attributed to the additional hydrogen-bonding interactions between Cur and phospholipid headgroups, which increased the spacing between phospholipid headgroups and modified the hydrophobic interior of bilayer membranes (Jovanovića et al., 2019).

EE mainly depends on the physicochemical properties of wall materials and core substances. Sterol-free SPC liposomes showed the lowest EE (52.13% ± 1.25%), while free sterol-stabilized liposomes exhibited a moderate increase in EE to approximately 62%. This finding is consistent with previous reports on Cur encapsulation using cholesterol and free phytosterols (Tai et al., 2020). Notably, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates achieved a marked improvement in EE. During encapsulation, these derivatives can insert into the phospholipid bilayer and fill structural voids, thereby strengthening the binding and retention of Cur within liposomes. Furthermore, EE also reflects the SAR driven by combined hydrogen-bonding interactions and glycosyl steric hindrance effects. The highest EE value was obtained for SPC-β-Sit-Suc (78.67% ± 2.13%), followed by SPC-β-Sit-Raf (73.45% ± 1.89%). The high EE achieved in this study demonstrates the promising potential of these liposomes as effective delivery carriers for hydrophobic bioactive compounds.

Storage stability analysis

The storage stability of Cur-loaded liposomes was systematically evaluated by monitoring dynamic changes in average particle size, PDI, zeta potential, and apparent Cur retention rate during 28-day storage at 25 °C (Fig. 6A–D).Fig. 6Changes in average particle size, PDI, zeta potential, and apparent Cur retention rate of Cur-loaded liposomes during 28-day storage at 25 °C. Abbreviations follow the convention defined in Section 2.2.

Changes in average particle size, PDI, zeta potential, and apparent Cur retention rate of Cur-loaded liposomes during 28-day storage at 25 °C. Abbreviations follow the convention defined in Section 2.2.

Regarding particle size, all liposomes displayed varying degrees of enlargement during storage, which was primarily due to gradual aggregation and fusion of vesicles over time. Notably, SPC liposomes exhibited the most significant size increase, with the average particle size rising from 125 nm to over 360 nm after 28 days, suggesting inferior storage stability. The particle size variation in sterol-containing liposomes was relatively smaller than that in SPC liposomes during long-term storage, indicating that the addition of sterols maintained good vesicle size stability during storage. This observation is consistent with previous literature (Lee et al., 2021). Moreover, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates showed a markedly slower increase in particle size and maintained a stable nanoscale structure throughout the storage period. Smaller initial particle size was associated with better storage stability, attributable to denser lipid packing.

PDI values remained stable and below 0.3 for all liposomes during early storage but increased significantly thereafter. After 28 days, the PDI of blank liposomes and free sterol-stabilized liposomes exceeded 0.3, reflecting reduced homogeneity. In contrast, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates-stabilized liposomes maintained PDI below 0.3 with minimal fluctuations throughout storage. These results are consistent with previous reports that liposomes stabilized by modified sterols exhibit superior system stability compared with those stabilized by traditional cholesterol and general phytosterols (Srihera et al., 2022).

The absolute zeta potential of all liposomes increased gradually during storage due to phospholipid hydrolysis and oxidation, exposing more negative phosphate groups (Song et al., 2022c). Liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates showed a significantly smaller increase, reflecting better long-term colloidal storage stability. A thick hydrated steric barrier formed by bulky glycosyl chains of these derivatives on the liposomal surface may slow phospholipid degradation and restrain vesicle aggregation.

Furthermore, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates significantly improved the apparent Cur retention rate during storage. After 28 days at 25 °C, blank SPC liposomes presented an apparent Cur retention rate of only approximately 78%. By comparison, cholesterol-stabilized liposomes achieved an apparent Cur retention rate of around 82%, while liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol showed a comparable retention level. In contrast, liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates maintained much higher apparent Cur retention rates. These performance differences further reveal the SAR, represented by SPC-β-Sit-Suc-Cur, which achieved the highest apparent Cur retention rate, exceeding 97%. Hydrogen-bonding networks formed between β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates and phospholipids can restrict membrane mobility and reduce bilayer permeability (Zhang et al., 2026), thereby minimizing Cur leakage and strengthening compound retention. Compared to conventional cholesterol-containing liposomes, these derivatives show excellent storage and retention performance. This highlights the commercial potential of liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates as cholesterol-free delivery systems for functional foods. A limitation is the use of UV-Vis detection at 455 nm, as Cur degradation and lipid oxidation products share overlapping absorbance signals and may overestimate retention rates. Future work will employ HPLC to quantify the absolute Cur content.

Bioaccessibility of Cur

In vitro dynamic gastrointestinal digestion was carried out using the DHSI-IV system. The pH changes of gastric and intestinal digesta are shown in Fig. 7A. All samples exhibited a consistent, time-dependent pH trend. Initially, the gastric pH was approximately 1.6, simulating fasting physiological conditions. Following the introduction of the digestion samples, the pH rose rapidly to 6.5. Subsequently, the gastric pH decreased progressively, returning to 1.6 within 30 min. This was attributed to the continuous secretion of gastric fluids (including SGF and HCl) and the reduced buffering capacity of the digesta due to gastric emptying. Throughout the intestinal phase, the pH was maintained at 6.5-7.1 without external adjustment with NaOH. These pH variations are consistent with those reported in previous in vitro digestion studies (Liu et al., 2022). Our previous study (Hu et al., 2026) confirmed the strong digestive stability of β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates. All derivatives showed hydrolysis rates below 19%, as the bulky glycosyl groups created steric hindrance, blocking lipase access to the ester bond. Only minor glycosyl headgroup detachment occurred during digestion, and most conjugates retained their intact amphipathic structures, maintaining liposomal membrane stability.

The bioaccessibility of Cur was evaluated by quantifying its content in the micelle fraction of intestinal digesta following an in vitro dynamic gastrointestinal digestion model. As illustrated in Fig. 7B, the bioaccessibility of free Cur was compared with that of Cur encapsulated in various liposomes. Results showed that free Cur exhibited a low bioaccessibility of approximately 5%, primarily due to its poor aqueous solubility and chemical degradation under alkaline intestinal conditions (Gómez-Mascaraque et al., 2017). Notably, all Cur-loaded liposomes showed substantially higher bioaccessibility than the free Cur control, demonstrating that liposomal encapsulation can effectively improve the inherent delivery limitations of free Cur. The phospholipid bilayer of liposomes protects encapsulated Cur during gastrointestinal digestion. This structure improves the water solubility of Cur and provides a physical barrier against the alkaline intestinal environment, reducing Cur degradation and maintaining its stability during digestion.

The digestive performance and protective efficacy of liposomes toward encapsulated substances are highly dependent on their membrane composition. In this work, blank SPC liposomes presented a Cur bioaccessibility of nearly 20%. Consistent with previous findings (Li et al., 2018), cholesterol-stabilized liposomes showed a Cur bioaccessibility of approximately 25%, while liposomes stabilized by β-sitosterol achieved a comparable level. β-Sitosterol glycosyl adipates further improved the digestive stability and bioaccessibility of encapsulated Cur. Among all groups, SPC-β-Sit-Suc-Cur liposomes exhibited a Cur bioaccessibility of up to 44%. Increased membrane compactness and structural stability prevent premature liposome disintegration, thereby inhibiting Cur release and degradation in the intestinal phase. In addition, our previous study found that β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates can participate in forming mixed micelles with bile salts (Hu et al., 2023), which may act as another potential mechanism for enhancing Cur bioaccessibility. Collectively, β-sitosterol glycosyl adipate modification effectively optimizes the structural stability and gastrointestinal digestion characteristics of liposomes, thereby significantly improving the intestinal delivery performance and bioaccessibility of Cur.

Conclusions

This study fabricated and characterized cholesterol-free liposomes using β-sitosterol glycosyl adipates as novel membrane stabilizers. These derivatives significantly enhanced membrane structural properties. Furthermore, Cur-loaded liposomes stabilized by these derivatives possessed higher encapsulation efficiency, favorable storage stability, and improved Cur bioaccessibility. The overall performance of liposomes was mainly governed by the synergistic regulation of hydrogen-bonding interactions and glycosyl steric hindrance. Sufficient hydroxyl groups could form dense hydrogen-bonding networks with phospholipids to strengthen lipid packing within the bilayer, whereas excessive glycosyl steric hindrance disrupted ordered lipid arrangement and weakened membrane compactness. Among all derivatives, β-sitosterol sucrose adipate achieved an optimal balance between hydroxyl content and steric hindrance, and exhibited the strongest liposomal membrane modification capacity. This study clarified the membrane regulatory mechanism of amphiphilic phytosterol derivatives and validated their efficacy as ideal cholesterol alternatives. SAR analysis provides theoretical support and technical guidance for developing cholesterol-free nanocarriers for functional food applications.

MedNet: a lightweight attention-augmented CNN for medical image classification
Authors: Md. Ferdous, Saifuddin Mahmud, Md. Eleush Zahan Shimul
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Disease detection using medical images enables early and precise diagnosis. Despite the growing success of deep learning models, accurate classification remains a significant challenge. Medical images often exhibit characteristics such as limited spatial resolution, subtle visual differences between disease categories (low inter-class variance), and substantial variation within the same class (high intra-class variability). These factors collectively hinder the ability of standard vision models to generalize effectively, frequently resulting in misclassification. These challenges highlight the need for efficient architectures that can focus on critical spatial and contextual features for reliable performance. To mitigate these challenges we propose MedNet, a lightweight CNN architecture that combines depthwise separable convolutions with the CBAM attention mechanism to efficiently extract and refine spatially and contextually relevant features. The core ResidualDSCBAMBlock captures local patterns while CBAM enhances important spatial and channel-wise information, followed by adaptive pooling, dropout, and fully connected layers for robust classification. The model is trained and validated on the DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, OCTMNIST which from MedMNIST, and Fitzpatrick17k datasets. MedNet matches or exceeds CNN baselines across these medical image datasets achieving higher accuracy with significantly fewer parameters and lower computational cost, demonstrating its effectiveness and efficiency in medical image classification tasks. Code is available at https://github.com/Md-Ferdous/MedNet.

Introduction

Skin cancer, hematological conditions, and retinal pathologies are prevalent health concerns that demand timely and accurate diagnosis to prevent complications. Early detection plays a critical role in guiding effective treatment strategies. Medical imaging has emerged as a non-invasive and widely accessible modality for disease screening and diagnosis, offering critical support for early detection and treatment planning. The availability of DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, and OCTMNIST from the MedMNIST collection1, along with the Fitzpatrick17k dataset2, has substantially accelerated research in automated diagnostic systems leveraging deep learning techniques. These datasets encompass a broad spectrum of medical imaging tasks, including skin lesion classification, blood cell morphology analysis, and retinal disease identification, providing standardized benchmarks that facilitate the comparison and evaluation of computer-aided diagnosis methods. Despite the advances enabled by these datasets, conventional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) face significant challenges when applied to medical image analysis. Medical images frequently exhibit small spatial dimensions, subtle inter-class variations, and considerable intra-class variability, all of which complicate accurate classification and increase the risk of misdiagnoses. Furthermore, the deployment of deep learning models in clinical environments requires not only high predictive accuracy but also computational efficiency, particularly in resource-limited settings, such as portable devices or point-of-care diagnostic tools. These factors highlight the necessity for lightweight, interpretable, and computationally efficient architectures capable of focusing on the most informative and discriminative features within medical images. To address these challenges, attention mechanisms such as the Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM)3, SE-Net4, and BAM5 have been proposed to guide neural networks to emphasize salient spatial regions and channel-wise features, thereby improving classification performance, especially in the presence of noisy or cluttered backgrounds. In parallel, depthwise separable convolutions, introduced in MobileNet6 and Xception7, have demonstrated the ability to reduce the number of parameters and computational complexity while maintaining strong representational capacity. MedViT8 showed accuracy and AUC scores across all 12 MedMNIST classification datasets when compared with strong baseline models such as ResNet-18, ResNet-50, ConvNeXt, and other transformer-based methods. It offered a balanced trade-off between accuracy, robustness, and computational efficiency. The use of its hybrid architecture and the Patch Momentum Changer augmentation helped the model generalize better and remain more robust against variations and adversarial noise, demonstrating consistent performance across diverse medical image classification tasks. Biomedical images were resized to 224\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}224 and divided into 16\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}16 patches for input to the ViT-base-patch16-224 model9. Each dataset was trained separately using standard augmentation, normalization, and fine-tuning. Model performance was measured using accuracy and compared with existing benchmarks. The ViT achieved 97.9% on BloodMNIST, 90.4% on BreastMNIST, 94.6% on PathMNIST, and 57% on RetinaMNIST, outperforming previous methods. The results show that ViT effectively captures long-range spatial relationships and fine structural details in biomedical images. The combination of attention mechanisms with efficient convolutional operations offers a promising strategy for designing models that balance accuracy, efficiency, and interpretability. However, existing architectures rarely optimize these objectives jointly in the context of medical imaging datasets, leaving a gap for specialized models tailored to healthcare applications. In this work, we introduce MedNet, a compact yet powerful CNN architecture designed to address these challenges. MedNet integrates depthwise separable convolutions with CBAM within a residual learning framework, forming the core ResidualDSCBAMBlock, which efficiently captures local spatial patterns while enhancing discriminative feature representation. The network further incorporates adaptive pooling, dropout, and fully connected layers to ensure robustness against overfitting and variability in input data. Comprehensive experiments were conducted on four publicly available medical imaging datasets-DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, OCTMNIST, and Fitzpatrick17k-covering a diverse set of classification tasks, from skin lesion detection to blood cell recognition and retinal disease diagnosis. Results indicate that MedNet consistently achieves competitive performance compared with state-of-the-art models, while substantially reducing both parameter count and computational overhead, demonstrating its potential as a solution for clinical applications. Background

The use of deep learning methods for medical image classification has garnered significant attention over the past decade, driven by the growing availability of medical image datasets and advancements in computational resources. Early approaches in automated skin lesion diagnosis utilized traditional image processing and handcrafted feature extraction methods, which often lacked robustness and scalability. With the advent of deep learning, CNNs have emerged as the de facto standard for medical image classification due to their ability to learn hierarchical feature representations. Models such as AlexNet 10, VGGNet 11, and ResNet 12 have been adapted for dermatoscopic image analysis, achieving significant performance improvements. EfficientNet has emerged as an effective architecture for medical imaging tasks, which is proposed by Tan and Le13, EfficientNet introduces compound scaling to uniformly adjust network dimensions, thereby optimizing accuracy and efficiency. Skin disease classification, as in the DermaMNIST and Fitzpatrick17k datasets, has been widely explored using CNNs like ResNet12, DenseNet14, and MobileNet6. Despite their effectiveness, these models often involve high computational costs and may struggle to generalize across diverse skin tones and lesion types, particularly in datasets like Fitzpatrick17k, which emphasizes skin tone diversity2. Blood image classification using BloodMNIST has seen the application of lightweight models like EfficientNet13 and MobileNetV215, which offer good performance with fewer parameters. While these models are computationally efficient, they may lack the spatial attention capabilities needed to distinguish visually similar blood cell types, especially in low-resolution images. Retinal disease classification, such as that performed on OCTMNIST, often benefits from deeper models like ResNet50 and Inception-based architectures16. These models capture fine-grained retinal structures well but tend to be resource-intensive, limiting their deployment in real-time clinical settings or on edge devices. To overcome the limitations of standard CNNs, attention mechanisms have been implemented to enhance model focus on the most relevant regions. To address classification tasks across different biomedical modalities, several deep learning architectures have been benchmarked, including both CNNs and vision transformers (ViTs). The study in17 implements a fine-tuned Vision Transformer (ViT-Base-Patch16-224) for 2D biomedical image classification on four subsets of MedMNISTv2, achieving notable accuracy: BloodMNIST (97.90%), BreastMNIST (90.38%), PathMNIST (94.62%), and RetinaMNIST (57%). The ViT model, pretrained on ImageNet and fine-tuned with dataset-specific hyperparameters, demonstrated its capability to generalize across diverse biomedical image types. Complementing this,18 conducted a comprehensive comparison between classical CNN models (AlexNet, VGG-16, ResNet-18, ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, EfficientNet-B0/B4) and ViT-based models (ViT-B/16, CLIP, DINO, SAM) on MedMNIST+. Their results revealed that CNNs, particularly DenseNet-121 and VGG-16, consistently outperformed ViTs in end-to-end training, highlighting the efficacy of simpler architectures when sufficient data is available. However, ViTs pretrained with DINO performed better in feature extraction tasks (e.g., linear probing and k-NN), showing promise for transfer learning applications. Addressing model robustness under corruption,19 introduced MedMNIST-C, simulating realistic image corruptions across 12 datasets using noise, blur, compression, and color shifts. They benchmarked classic architectures on both clean and corrupted data. ViT-B/16 achieved the highest robustness with relative balanced error (rBE) of 59.9%, outperforming CNN counterparts like VGG-16 (rBE 114%) and ResNet-50 (rBE 148%). In20, the authors revisited foundation models for medical image classification using CNNs and ViTs. DenseNet-121 achieved high performance on PneumoniaMNIST (ACC 94.02%), while ViTs like DINOv2 ViT-B/14 showed top results for DermaMNIST (ACC 92.02%, AUC 98.92%). SAM-C and CLIP variants of ViT further improved classification accuracy for datasets like PathMNIST and OrganAMNIST. Quantum machine learning has also been explored using MedMNIST datasets. The study in21 leveraged reduced-dimension (8\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}8) images for inference on IBM’s 127-qubit quantum hardware. Results showed competitive performance (e.g., 85.4% ACC on PneumoniaMNIST), suggesting quantum circuits’ potential for low-resource biomedical classification tasks. Lastly, MedMNIST v222 introduced a comprehensive benchmark using both 2D and 3D datasets. While 2D CNNs demonstrated solid performance ( 70–90% ACC), AutoML tools (e.g., AutoKeras, Google AutoML) matched or exceeded manually tuned CNNs, showing the value of automated model selection in biomedical imaging. To further enhance model performance, attention mechanisms have been integrated into CNNs to improve feature focus and interpretability. Traditional attention modules like SE4 and CBAM3 introduced learnable channel and spatial attention to emphasize informative features. Medical datasets are frequently affected by class imbalance, where some lesion types are significantly underrepresented. This imbalance can lead to biased predictions and poor generalization. To mitigate this, Focal Loss23 has been widely adopted. Focal Loss modifies the standard cross-entropy loss by down-weighting easy examples and focusing more on hard-to-classify cases, thus improving performance on minority classes. Collectively, these works provide a robust foundation for evaluating CNNs and ViTs on medical imaging tasks, while also pushing the frontier with robustness testing and quantum learning paradigms. The paper24 introduces ERSR, a semi-supervised framework for fetal head segmentation in ultrasound images. It combines Dual-scoring Adaptive Filtering, Ellipse-constrained Pseudo-label Refinement, and Symmetry-based Consistency Regularization within a teacher–student setup to refine pseudo-labels and enforce stable shape learning. Leveraging the head’s elliptical symmetry, ERSR achieves 95.36% Dice on HC18 with 20% labeled data and adds no inference overhead. IPA-CP25, a semi-supervised framework for tumor segmentation in CT scans that targets small, hard-to-detect tumors. It uses uncertainty-based adaptive augmentation and iterative pseudo-label refinement within a mean teacher setup, plus a bidirectional copy-paste strategy for efficient data use. Evaluations on in-house and MSD datasets show superior accuracy and robustness over existing methods. PG-FANet26 introduces, a semi-supervised framework for histopathology image segmentation that integrates inter- and intra-uncertainty to enhance feature learning. Using a two-stage network with pseudo-mask guided feature enhancement and multi-scale aggregation, it refines segmentation accuracy. Evaluated on MoNuSeg and CRAG, PG-FANet outperforms state-of-the-art methods with limited labeled data. UniRepLKNet27 is a universal large-kernel ConvNet that unifies image, audio, video, point cloud, and time-series processing. Using Dilated Re-parameterization Blocks with small and large kernels, it efficiently captures global and local features. Scalable and modality-agnostic, it achieves state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks. OverLoCK28 is a pure ConvNet vision backbone inspired by human vision, combining an overview-first, look-closely-next attention mechanism. It includes Base-Net, Overview-Net, and Focus-Net, the latter using a Context-Mixing Dynamic Convolution (ContMix) for adaptive refinement through dynamic top-down context. With its Deep-stage Decomposition Strategy, OverLoCK achieves 84.2% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K, outperforming larger models while being more efficient across detection and segmentation tasks. Results

Dataset

Two publicly available datasets, MedMNIST v2 (2D)1 and Fitzpatrick17k2, were employed in the experiments. To examine the influence of input resolution, image sizes of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$224 \times 224$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$384 \times 384$$\end{document} were selected. Larger input sizes provide increased spatial detail, allowing the attention layers to more effectively capture both the location (“where”) and the semantic content (“what”) of the salient regions. All images available in each dataset were utilized, without altering the dataset size and image also.

Implementation details

The model is trained for 70 epochs using the AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$3 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document} and a weight decay of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$1 \times 10^{-4}$$\end{document}. A Cosine Annealing learning rate scheduler is employed with parameters \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$T_{\text {max}} = 20$$\end{document} and \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\eta _{\text {min}} = 1 \times 10^{-6}$$\end{document} to adjust the learning rate during training. Focal Loss is used to handle situations where the data is imbalanced and some classes are harder to classify than others. Unlike standard Cross Entropy Loss, which gives the same importance to every training example, Focal Loss adds a factor \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$(1 - p_t)^{\gamma }$$\end{document} that lowers the loss for easy, correctly predicted samples and increases it for hard, misclassified ones. This helps the model pay more attention to the less frequent or more challenging classes, instead of being influenced mainly by the majority class. The parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\gamma$$\end{document} decides how strongly the model focuses on difficult examples, while the optional \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\alpha$$\end{document} factor can give extra weight to minority classes. Because of this, Focal Loss is widely used in problems like medical image classification, anomaly detection, and object detection, where class imbalance is common and improving recall and sensitivity is essential. Training is performed with a batch size of 64. To ensure reproducibility, a random seed of 42, 43, and 44 is set for first, second, and third run, respectively. All the experiments are conducted on two RTX A6000 GPU with 48GB VRAM. The results are consistent accross the validation and test set. Furthermore, performance improvements become more pronounced as input resolution increases, implying that finer image details are better leveraged when combined with the attention mechanism. Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves a strong balance between accuracy and computational efficiency. With only 3.27 million parameters and 5.9 GMac FLOPs at the highest resolution, it significantly outperforms much larger architectures such as ResNet-50 (25.6M parameters) and MedViT (23.6M parameters) while maintaining better classification performance across all three datasets. The findings also confirm the generalization ability of the proposed method, as it performs consistently well across diverse imaging modalities, including dermatology, optical coherence tomography, and blood cell microscopy. These observations emphasize that incorporating attention mechanisms like CBAM within a lightweight convolutional framework can lead to substantial gains in medical image classification accuracy without excessive computational demands.

Discussion

The results presented in Tables 1 and 3 underscore the effectiveness of our proposed model, MedNet, addressing the challenges of medical image classification-namely, small image sizes, low inter-class variance, and high intra-class variability. Despite having significantly fewer parameters (3.27M) and relatively low computational complexity (5.9 GMac), MedNet consistently well performed than several state-of-the-art models, including ResNet-18, ResNet-50, and multiple AutoML-based solutions, across four benchmark datasets: DermaMNIST, BloodMNIST, OCTMNIST, and Fitzpatrick17k. On the three MedMNIST datasets, MedNet achieved the highest AUC and accuracy scores. These results are particularly notable when compared with deeper models like ResNet-50, which, while achieving competitive performance, do so at the cost of significantly higher computational requirements (e.g., up to 2.05 GMac). By integrating depthwise separable convolutions with the CBAM in the ResidualDSCBAMBlock, the network is able to capture both local features and contextual information. The CBAM mechanism emphasizes informative spatial and channel-wise regions, helping the model focus on relevant patterns despite noise or variability in the images. Furthermore, the evaluation on the Fitzpatrick17k dataset-designed to challenge models with real-world diversity in skin tones and lesion types-demonstrates the robustness of our approach. Methods

The proposed architecture is designed for medical image classification task. It incorporates depth-wise separable convolutions and CBAM to improve feature extraction and attention-based learning. Additionally, a Focal Loss function is used to address class imbalance problem. The entire model is trained using optimized data augmentation techniques based on Albumentations to enhance generalization.

Data preprocessing and augmentation

To enhance the robustness and generalization ability of the model, a series of data augmentation and preprocessing operations were applied to the training images using the Albumentations library. To increase data diversity, each image has a 50% probability of being flipped horizontally and vertically. Moreover, to improve the model’s robustness at different lighting conditions the brightness and contrast of the image alters with a 20% probability. In addition to this, we randomly shifts the image by up to 10% of its height or width, scales it within ±10%, and rotates it by up to ±20 degrees with a 50% chance. This helps the model to learn invariant features under spatial transformations. To improve visibility of fine details Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) is applied with 20% probability.

The proposed MedNet architecture is composed of five sequential stages, followed by pooling and fully connected layers, as detailed in Table 9. Each stage is constructed using a ResidualDSCBAMBlock, which integrates depthwise separable convolutions with residual connections and the CBAM to enhance feature representation. Stage 1 employs 64 filters with a stride of 1, and its ResidualDSCBAMBlock is expanded in the table to illustrate its internal configuration. The block consists of two convolutional layers (a 3\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}3 convolution followed by a 1\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\times$$\end{document}1 convolution), batch normalization, and a CBAM attention layer. ReLU activation is applied after each convolution. Stages 2–5 progressively increase the number of filters (128, 256, 512, and 1024, respectively) with a stride of 2, enabling hierarchical feature extraction at multiple spatial resolutions. After Stage 5, an adaptive average pooling layer reduces the feature maps to a fixed size regardless of the input resolution. The pooled features are passed through two fully connected layers: Fc1 with 512 units and Fc2, which outputs predictions corresponding to the number of classes (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$n_c$$\end{document}). This hierarchical design enables MedNet to capture fine-grained spatial features in earlier layers while progressively learning more abstract and semantically rich representations in deeper stages. The incorporation of CBAM attention further guides the network to focus on the most informative regions and channels, thereby enhancing classification performance.

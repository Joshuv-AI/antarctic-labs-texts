New MiniBooNE excess interpretation using Pseudo-Hermitian neutrino oscillation
M. L. Abdelali, N. Mebarki, B. Boutaghane, and M. Trirat

Abstract
The excess observed in the MiniBooNE experiment data has been confirmed by several data-releases and analysis. And several attempts to explain such excess are proposed in the litterature using approaches ranging from experimental error to new neutrino theories. None of the attempts seem to fully account for the observed excess. In this paper, we present a new approach this case using a non standard two flavor oscillation theory. In recent years, there is a raising interest in the pseudo-hermitian hamiltonian theories and their impact on several quantum phenomena. The neutrino oscillation is modified using the approach of PT-pseudo hermitian oscillation by having zero-distance effect and asymmetry of the probability of appearance of specific flavors. Such effects can account according to this study for the excess and has a better fit to the data than the best found fit in the standard oscillation framework. We present in this paper the study of the fit of MiniBooNE data the this new oscillation model and its premise.

2
Neutrino oscillations models

We describe in this section briefly the standard model of neutrino oscillations based on mass eigen states neutrinos and a new model of neutrino oscillations based on a PT-symmetric pseudo-hermitian hamiltonian physics.

2.1
Standard Two Flavour Oscillations

The standard neutrino oscillation model is based on the propagation through space of massive neutrinos which are different from the flavour observed neutrinos.
The mass difference and the mixing angle between the massive neatrinos are responsible of the phenomena of flavour neutrinos oscillations.
In the phenomena, the observed flavour of the neutrino is changing after its propagation depending on its energy and the distance travelled.
The standard probability
P_{ab}
of oscillations between two flavours of neutrinos (
{\nu}_{a}
and
{\nu}_{b}
) are expressed as following

P_{ab}=sin^{2}(2\theta)sin^{2}\left[1.27\left(\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{eV^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{L_{\nu}}{km}\right)\left(\frac{GeV}{E_{\nu}}\right)\right]

(1)

where:
\theta
is the mixing angle between the two flavours,
\Delta m^{2}
is the squared mass difference between the two neutrinos expressed in
eV
,
L
is the distance travelled by the neutrinos from creation to observation expressed in
km
,
E
is the energy of the neutrino expressed in
GeV
.
It is called appearance probability which describes the probability to observe a specific neutrino flavour from an initial beam of different flavour.

In the case of standard model of neutrino oscillations, the appearance probabilities of the two flavours are the same due the hermisity of the hamiltonian.

P_{ba}=P_{ab}

(2)

Then, due the unity of the sum of all probabilities of a specific phenomena, we could express the disappearance probability as

P_{aa}=P_{bb}=1-P_{ab}

(3)

which describes in a statistical perspective the number of neutrinos that no more exist in their intial flavour.

2.2
Pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian

The PT-symmetric pseudo hermitian Hamiltonian is studied in
Ref.0001
.
In this section, we present a brief review of their results and an interpretation of the most important predictions.

The Hamiltonian of this model has the following form

H=\begin{pmatrix}\rho e^{i\varphi}&\sigma\\
\sigma&\rho e^{-i\varphi}\end{pmatrix}

(4)

where
\rho
,
\varphi
and
\sigma
are model parameters.
In this framework, the oscillation probabilities between two neutrinos
{\nu}_{a}
and
{\nu}_{b}
are different from the standard model of neutrinos oscillations.
The appearance probabilities are given by

P_{ab}=sin^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}t\right)\,\,,\,\,P_{ba}=sin^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}t\right)

(5)

The disappearance probabilities are given by

P_{aa}=cos^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{\beta}{2}t\right)\,\,,\,\,P_{bb}=cos^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}t\right)

(6)

where

sin\alpha=\frac{\rho sin\varphi}{\sigma}

,

\beta=2\sqrt{\sigma^{2}-\rho^{2}sin^{2}\varphi}=2|\sigma cos\alpha|
.
It is also possible to write the new hamiltonian in term of neutrino energy as

H=\frac{1}{4E}\begin{pmatrix}\rho e^{i\varphi}&\sigma\\
\sigma&\rho e^{-i\varphi}\end{pmatrix}

(7)

which modifies the probability to be

P_{ab}=sin^{2}\left[\frac{\alpha}{2}-1.27|cos\alpha|\left(\frac{|\sigma|}{eV^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{GeV}{E}\right)\left(\frac{L}{km}\right)\right]

(8)

By comparing the new oscillation probabilities to the standard oscillation model, the most important two aspects of the oscillations of this model are

•

zero-distance effect
which is related to
\alpha
value which create a sort of a phase.
Then, even if there is no propagation and at the source, there is a non-zero oscillation probability.

•

Asymmetry of the probability
which is related to the difference noticed between
P_{ab}
and
P_{ba}
and then also between
P_{aa}
and
P_{bb}
.
This difference could promote the oscillation toward a specific flavour depending on distance travelled.

To determine to validity of such model, experimental data should be analysed using the new probabilities to determine to model parameters and to quantify the fit of the model to the data.

4
New MiniBooNE data analysis

In light of the previous sections, it is established the existence of an inconsistency between the observed number of events and the expected by the current oscillation model.
Diffenrent studies including the study of MiniBooNE collaboration failed to explain this excess as experiment errors or being a limited phenomena.
The posibility of non-oscillation explanation or extra-events related to the observed events is also ruled out.
Then, the adequate explanation of the inconsistency between the model and experiment in the case of MiniBooNE data is the existence of a new model of neutrino oscillation that could be more consistent with those data.
This new oscillation model consists of new physics applied to neutrinos in general or specificly in low energy or in small distance or in both.
The model used in this paper and presented in section
2.2
is the PT-symetric pseudo-hermitian oscillations.

To revisit MiniBooNE data, a new method should be used for the following reasons

•

Limitations of released data, since we don’t have detailed expected events from each possible sources, but a total at each bin,

•

Energy bins of
\nu_{\mu}
are not the same as energy bins of
\nu_{e}
,

•

For the pseudo Hermitian oscillations, the asymmetry of probabilities should be fully considered,

•

No access to systematic uncertainties of the flux and detector.

In our analysis, we compare rather than event number directly, the event ratios between flavours where the difference in oscillation probabilities could contribute.
Firstly, the final number
N^{f}_{\alpha}
of a specific neutrino
{\nu}_{\alpha}
is calculated from initial numbers (
N^{i}_{\alpha}
,
N^{i}_{\beta}
) and can be expressed as

N^{f}_{\alpha}=N^{i}_{\alpha}P_{\alpha\alpha}+N^{i}_{\beta}P_{\beta\alpha}\,\,,\,\,N^{f}_{\beta}=N^{i}_{\beta}P_{\beta\beta}+N^{i}_{\alpha}P_{\alpha\beta}

(9)

where
P_{\alpha\beta}
is the oscillation probability.
Secondly, the ratio of the two flavours are given by

\frac{N^{f}_{\alpha}}{N^{f}_{\beta}}=\frac{N^{i}_{\alpha}P_{\alpha\alpha}+N^{i}_{\beta}P_{\beta\alpha}}{N^{i}_{\beta}P_{\beta\beta}+N^{i}_{\alpha}P_{\alpha\beta}}

(10)

This ration can be further simplified to obtain

\frac{N^{f}_{\alpha}}{N^{f}_{\beta}}=\frac{\frac{N^{i}_{\alpha}}{N^{i}_{\beta}}P_{\alpha\alpha}+P_{\beta\alpha}}{P_{\beta\beta}+\frac{N^{i}_{\alpha}}{N^{i}_{\beta}}P_{\alpha\beta}}

(11)

Finally, the final ratio
R^{f}_{\alpha/\beta}
between the number of neutrinos of two flavours is given by

R^{f}_{\alpha/\beta}=\frac{R^{i}_{\alpha/\beta}P_{\alpha\alpha}+P_{\beta\alpha}}{P_{\beta\beta}+R^{i}_{\alpha/\beta}P_{\alpha\beta}}

(12)

This ratio
R^{f}_{\alpha/\beta}
is dependent of the initial ratio
R^{i}_{\alpha/\beta}
and all oscillation probabilities (
P_{\alpha\alpha}
,
P_{\beta\alpha}
,
P_{\beta\beta}
,
P_{\alpha\beta}
).
Those probabilities are calculated from the specific used model.
This method is applied for the pseudo-hermitian oscillation and also for standard oscillations to compare results with MiniBooNE collaboration results.
The initial ratio is calculated from the predicted event numbers without oscillation from the released MiniBooNE data.

To test the fit of the model to data, we calculate
\chi^{2}
parameter from the final theoretical and observed ratios.
The lower
\chi^{2}
is equivalent to a better the fit of the model with a specific model parameters to data.
We choose to use two definitions of
\chi^{2}
parameter which are

•

Method 1

\chi^{2}=2\left(R^{Model}_{\alpha/\beta}-R^{Data}_{\alpha/\beta}+R^{Data}_{\alpha/\beta}ln\left[\frac{R^{Data}_{\alpha/\beta}}{R^{Model}_{\alpha/\beta}}\right]\right)

(13)

•

Method 2

\chi^{2}=\frac{\left(R^{Model}_{\alpha/\beta}-R^{Data}_{\alpha/\beta}\right)^{2}}{R^{Data}_{\alpha/\beta}}

(14)

To get the best fit of a specific model, we calculate the
\chi^{2}
parameter for a set of conbinations of free parameters of the model.
The set of conbinations represent a test for all the parameters possible values plan.
For the standard oscillation model, the free parameters are
\theta
and
\Delta m^{2}
which are estimated by several collaborations using their data.
For the pseudo-hermitian model, the free parameters are
\alpha
and
\sigma
which will be determined using MiniBooNE data.
Those parameters influence the oscillation probabilities.

In figures
3
and
4
, we present the results of the best fit estimation of both standard and pseudo-hermitian models using the method 1 and method 2 in defining
\chi^{2}
.
We note that the estimations of the free parameters of both models does not differ much with both
\chi^{2}
methods.
But, the regions of
68
,
90
,
95
CL are smaller for method 2 of
\chi^{2}
.
Moreover, in both methods,
\chi^{2}
is smaller for the best fit of the pseudo-hermitian oscillation model.
In figure
5
, we compare both models and their predicted final event ratios to the observed ratios.
The best fit of pseudo-hermitian model show closer margins to the observed data even if there still an excess or deficit in ome bins.
In figure
6
, we compare nutrino mode to anti-neutrino mode.
The observations made for neutrinos are also correct for anti-neutrinos as the pseudo-hermitian oscillation model show a better fit to the observed data even if it does not account for all the observed excess in the standard model.
Those results show a possible utility and truth to the pseudo-hermitian physics in its description of the neutrino oscillations.

5
Conclusions and Perspective

The MiniBooNE data and the excess observed in the electron neutrino events present a challenge to the current standard understanding of neutrino oscillations.
Despite the upadte in the analysis of the collaboration study of the data, the excess is established and remain a puzzeling phenomena.
Several studies has tried to provide an explanation to this phenomena but failing to account for the significant excess observed.
The proposed solutions range from mis-interpretations of the observed events to beyond the standard model theories.

The interest in new perspective to the quantum phenomena is increasing in recent years.
An example of these new ideas is the pseudo-hermitian quantum physics.
The PT-symetric pseudo-hermitian hamiltonian theorised in the litterature has provided a new model of the neutrino oscillations.
This new model predicts peculiar predictions of the oscillations like the zero-distance effect and the asymetry of the oscillation probabilities.

We presented in this paper a new analysis of the MiniBooNE data using the pseudo-hermitian oscillations as a theoritecal framework.
The main observations from those preliminary results can be summarized in the following points

•

There is no conflict between allowed regions from the collaboration analysis and this new analysis,

•

The best fit for standard oscillations for (
sin^{2}2\theta
,
\Delta m^{2}
) is different from the result of the collaboration, but still in their
1\sigma
allowed region,

•

\chi^{2}
of method 2 resulted in stringed allowed regions and smaller best fit
\chi^{2}
value for both models,

•

\chi^{2}
for the best fit of pseudo-Hermitian oscillations is smaller with both methods than the one for standard oscillations, which make
pseudo Hermitian oscillations a better fitting model !

•

The excess is still present in the best fit of the new model.

This study has investigated the possibility of an impact of pseudo-hermitian physics in the case of neutrino oscillations.
The presented results do not exclude a possible implication of this new model in the explanation of the MiniBooNE data.
But, a much complete model should be constructed to be able to explain fully this phenomena.
More investigations should be done especially with other experiments similar to MiniBooNE to investigate the premise of the pseudo-hermitian physcis in the field of neutrino physics.

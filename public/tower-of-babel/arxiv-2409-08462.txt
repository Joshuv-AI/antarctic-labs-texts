Entropy, cocycles, and their diagrammatics
Mee Seong Im, Mikhail Khovanov

Abstract
. The first part of the paper explains how to encode a one-cocycle and a two-cocycle on a group G G with values in its representation by networks of planar trivalent graphs with edges labelled by elements of G G , elements of the representation floating in the regions, and suitable rules for manipulation of these diagrams. When the group is a semidirect product, there is a similar presentation via overlapping networks for the two subgroups involved. M. Kontsevich and J.-L. Cathelineau have shown how to interpret the entropy of a finite random variable and infinitesimal dilogarithms, including their four-term functional relations, via 2-cocycles on the group of affine symmetries of a line. We convert their construction into a diagrammatical calculus evaluating planar networks that describe morphisms in suitable monoidal categories. In particular, the four-term relations become equalities of networks analogous to associativity equations. The resulting monoidal categories complement existing categorical and operadic approaches to entropy.

Entropy and infinitesimal dilogarithms

4.1.
Entropy of a finite random variable and its cohomological interpretation

The entropy equation.

Shannon’s entropy of a probability distribution
X
on a finite space
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
that associates probabilities
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
,
\displaystyle{\sum_{i=1}^{n}}p_{i}=1
,
0\leq p_{i}\leq 1
to points
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
is given by

(4.1)

H(X)\ :=\ -\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}\log p_{i}.

When
n=2
, one can think of
H(X)
as a function of a single probability
p=p_{1}
, and the entropy is

(4.2)

H(p)\ =\ -p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p).

It is natural to extend function
H
from
0<p<1
to all real numbers by

(4.3)

H(p)\ :=\ \begin{cases}-p\log|p|-(1-p)\log|1-p|&\mathrm{if}\ p\not=0,1,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ p=0,1.\end{cases}

Following Leinster
[
Lei21b
]
, consider a related function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, denoted
\partial_{\mathbb{R}}
in
[
Lei21b
]
and given by

(4.4)

\psi(a)\ =\ \begin{cases}-a\log|a|&\mathrm{if}\ a\not=0,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ a=0.\end{cases}

Function
\psi
is a
non-linear derivation
:

(4.5)

\psi(ab)=\psi(a)b+a\psi(b),\ \ a,b\in\mathbb{R},\ \ \psi(1)=0.

For a finite sequence
\underline{a}:=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
of real numbers define

(4.6)

H(\underline{a}):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi(a_{i})-\psi\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\biggr)

to measure non-additivity of
\psi
on the sequence
\underline{a}
.
When
\underline{a}
is a sequence of probabilities for a finite random variable
X
, the last term is
\psi(1)=0
, and one recovers the entropy of
X
.

Entropy function
H:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies a four-term functional equation

(4.7)

H(p)-H(q)+pH\left(\dfrac{q}{p}\right)+(1-p)H\left(\dfrac{1-q}{1-p}\right)=0,\ \ q\in\mathbb{R},\ p\in\mathbb{R},\ \ p\not=0,1

and

(4.8)

H(1-p)=H(p),\ \ p\in\mathbb{R}.

The functional equation for entropy first appeared in
[
Tve58
]
. Introduction to
[
Vig20
]
contains more information on the history of the functional equation.
Equations (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), normalization
H(1/2)=\log 2
, and the continuity (or measurability) property uniquely determine
H
as a function
\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
, see
[
Lee64
]
.

Setting
q=p
in (
4.7
) gives
H(1)=0
and, by symmetry,
H(0)=0
.
Setting
q=1
in (
4.7
) implies

(4.9)

pH\biggl(\frac{1}{p}\biggr)=-H(p),\ \ p\not=0,

and allows to rewrite (
4.7
) in a more symmetric form

(4.10)

H(p)+(1-p)H\biggl(\frac{q}{1-p}\biggr)\ =\ H(q)+(1-q)H\biggl(\frac{p}{1-q}\biggr),\ \ p,q\not=1.

Remark 4.1
.

be a derivation of

viewed as a

and

for all

, see
[

or
[

.

Following
[

]
, consider the function

(4.11)

h(x)\ =\begin{cases}\dfrac{(dx)^{2}}{x(1-x)}&\mathrm{if}\ x\not=0,1,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ x=0,1.\end{cases}

This non-measurable function satisfies functional equations (

) and (

).

Cathelineau–Kontsevich symbol.

To relate entropy to group cohomology, following Kontsevich

]
and Cathelineau

We have

so that

measures the failure of

\langle a,b\rangle_{H}\ =\ \psi(a)+\psi(b)-\psi(a+b),\ \ \ \ \psi(a)\ =\ \begin{cases}-a\log|a|&\mathrm{if}\ a\not=0,\\

holds for all

.

The symbol satisfies the following relations

(4.15)

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle_{H}+\langle a+b,c\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle a,b+c\rangle_{H}+\langle b,c\rangle_{H},

(4.16)

\displaystyle\langle a,b\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle b,a\rangle_{H},

(4.17)

\displaystyle\langle ca,cb\rangle_{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\langle a,b\rangle_{H}.

\langle(a_{1},c_{1}),(a_{2},c_{2})\rangle_{H}\ :=\ \langle a_{1},c_{1}a_{2}\rangle_{H},

Proof.

This is due to lack of solutions

for some

(4.24)

∎

Extending the category of finite probabilistic spaces.

Category
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
contains a subcategory equivalent to the Baez-Fritz-Leinster category
\mathsf{FinProb}
as defined in
[
BFL11
]
. Objects of
\mathsf{FinProb}
are finite sets equipped with probability measures
(X,p)
and morphisms are measure-preserving functions on finite sets
f:(X,p)\longrightarrow(Y,q)
with
q_{j}=\sum_{i\in f^{-1}(j)}p_{i}
. Any morphism
f:X\to Y
is surjective onto points of
Y
of strictly positive measure.

Consider the subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}
of
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
with objects given by tensor products

X^{+}_{\underline{p}}:=X^{+}_{p_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes X^{+}_{p_{n}},\ \ 0\leq p_{i}\leq 1,\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}=1,\ \underline{p}:=(p_{1},\dots,p_{n}).

Morphisms are generated by merges
X^{+}_{p}\otimes X^{+}_{p^{\prime}}\longrightarrow X^{+}_{p+p^{\prime}}
, for
p+p^{\prime}\leq 1
and transpositions
X^{+}_{p}\otimes X^{+}_{p^{\prime}}\longrightarrow X^{+}_{p^{\prime}}\otimes X^{+}_{p}
(see Section
5.2
for a list of generating morphisms).

These are exactly dotless diagrams made only of additive upward-oriented lines of non-negative weight
p
with
0\leq p\leq 1
with no splits, with the total weight
1
in any cross-section by a horizontal line. Equivalently, these are upward-oriented additive line diagrams with merges and permutations of strands only, total weight
1
, and weight restriction
0\leq p\leq 1
for each line.

Subcategory
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}
is equivalent to
\mathsf{FinProb}
. An equivalence is given by the functor
F:\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{FinProb}
that takes
X^{+}_{\underline{p}}
to a finite
n
-point set with probabilities
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
assigned to the points. It is clear how to extend the functor to morphisms. Notice that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}
is also a subcategory in
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}\subset\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
, since diagrams describing morphisms in
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}
are dotless.

Functoriality property of entropy in
\mathsf{FinProb}
corresponds to additivity of the entropy on the composition of morphisms, including in the larger category
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
, that is,
\jmath(\gamma^{\prime}\gamma)=\jmath(\gamma^{\prime})+\jmath(\gamma)
.
Convex linearity of entropy in
\mathsf{FinProb}
, see
[
BFL11
]
, says that

H(\lambda Y_{1}\oplus(1-\lambda)Y_{2})=\lambda H(Y_{1})+(1-\lambda)H(Y_{2}),

where random variable
\lambda Y_{1}\oplus(1-\lambda)Y_{2}
is associated to random variables
Y_{1},Y_{2}
and
\lambda,0<\lambda<1
in the obvious way. Convex linearity corresponds to the equality of evaluations in Figure
6.1.9
when
n=2
and
\lambda=p_{1}
.

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathsf{Fin}}
, while equivalent to the Baez–Fritz–Leinster category, is a small part of
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}
. It is natural to wonder whether the bigger categories
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}
and
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
, closely related to the affine group
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
, may be relevant to probability or whether some modification of
{\mathcal{C}}_{H}
and
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
could extend probability as it appears in the quantum world. We refer to
[
BFL11
,
Bra21
,
Lei21a
,
Gro13
,
Vig20
,
BB15
]
and follow-up papers for more on the categorical meaning of entropy. Operadic interpretation of entropy by Bradley
[
Bra21
]
and Leinster
[
Lei21a
]
is closely related to the diagrams in Figure
6.1.9
.

In this section we offered a different categorical interpretation of entropy of a finite probability space from what exists in the literature. Our interpretation does not have an immediate application to probability. Still, we hope that the present paper complements existing work in a useful way.

Remark 6.2
.

Consider the action of
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
on the abelian group
(\mathbb{R},+)
via
(a,c)u=c|c|^{\alpha-1}u
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\setminus 1
.
Note that
c|c|^{\alpha-1}=\mathrm{sign}(c)|c|^{\alpha}
.
One can recover the Tsallis entropy by looking at a suitable 2-cocycle on
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Tsallis entropy is given by

H_{\alpha}(X)=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\biggl(1-\sum_{i\in X}p_{i}^{\alpha}\biggr).

Interpreting
1=(\sum_{i\in X}p_{i})^{\alpha}
we can define

\psi_{\alpha}(x)=\begin{cases}x|x|^{\alpha-1},&\mathrm{if}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast},\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ x=0\end{cases}

and

\left\langle a,b\right\rangle_{\alpha}\ :=\ \psi_{\alpha}(a)+\psi_{\alpha}(b)-\psi_{\alpha}(a+b).

Observe that
\psi_{\alpha}:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a homomorphism.
Then

(\alpha-1)H_{\alpha}(p)=\pm\left\langle p,1-p\right\rangle_{\alpha}.

Symmetry property holds:
\left\langle b,a\right\rangle_{\alpha}=\left\langle a,b\right\rangle_{\alpha}
. For the scaling property we have
\psi_{\alpha}(ca)=\psi_{\alpha}(c)\psi_{\alpha}(a)
and

\left\langle ca,cb\right\rangle_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha}(c)\left\langle a,b\right\rangle_{\alpha}.

We extend the cocycle to the entire
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
group via

(6.7)

\langle(a_{1},c_{1}),(a_{2},c_{2})\rangle_{\alpha}\ :=\ \langle a_{1},c_{1}a_{2}\rangle_{\alpha}

In the diagrammatics for the analogue of category
\mathcal{C}_{H}^{\circ}
in this setup, an additive line
a
crossing a multiplicative line
c
co-oriented away from it still gets rescaled to
ca
. But a dot labelled
x\in\mathbb{R}
moving through a multiplicative
c

\psi_{\alpha}(c)x
. In the evaluation function
\jmath_{\alpha}
the 2-cocycle
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
in (
5.1
) and the 2-cocycle
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle_{H}
in (

) should be replaced by
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle_{\alpha}
and the action of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
on
\mathbb{R}
by the action via
\psi_{\alpha}
, so that
c\dot{x}=\psi_{\alpha}(c)x
:

(6.8)

\jmath_{\alpha}(\gamma)=\sum_{p\in\mathrm{add}(\gamma)}s(p)\psi_{\alpha}(\omega(p,\gamma))\langle a_{p},b_{p}\rangle_{\alpha}\ \in\ \mathbb{R}.

and, by analogy with (

Remark 6.3
.

Recall a non-measurable entropy-like function in (

h(x)=((dx)^{2})/(x(1-x))\ \mathrm{for}\ x\in\mathbb{R},\ x\not=0,1\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ h(0)=h(1)=0,

where
d:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear derivation. Noting that
1/(x(1-x))=1/x+1/(1-x)
, introduce a function
\psi:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

(6.10)

\psi(a)=\begin{cases}(da)^{2}/a&\mathrm{if}\ a\not=0,1,\\
0&\mathrm{if}\ a=0,1.\end{cases}

Then
h(x)=\psi(x)+\psi(1-x)
. Function
\psi
is not a non-linear derivation, unlike (
4.5
), since

\psi(ab)\not=\psi(a)b+a\psi(b)
.
Instead, we compute

d(a/(a+b))=((da)(a+b)-a(da+db))/(a+b)^{2}=((da)b-a(db))/(a+b)^{2}

and define

\displaystyle\left\langle a,b\right\rangle

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(a+b)h(a/(a+b))=(a+b)^{3}(d(a/(a+b))^{2})/(ab),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a+b)^{-1}((da)b-a(db))^{2}/ab

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(da)^{2}b/(a(b+a))+(db)^{2}a/(b(a+b))-2(da)(db)/(a+b)

Next,

\displaystyle\psi(a)+\psi(b)-\psi(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(da)^{2}/a+(db)^{2}/b-(da+db)^{2}/(a+b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b^{2}(da)^{2}+a^{2}(db)^{2}-2ab(da)(db))/(ab(a+b))=\left\langle a,b\right\rangle

Consequently,

(6.11)

\left\langle a,b\right\rangle\ =\ \psi(a)+\psi(b)-\psi(a+b),

and the 2-cocycle
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}
is the coboundary of
\psi
. The bracket is symmetric,
\left\langle a,b\right\rangle=\left\langle b,a\right\rangle

\left\langle ca,cb\right\rangle=c\left\langle a,b\right\rangle.

On the field
\mathbb{R}

. Via formula (

Remark 6.5
.

Generalized binomials as 2-cocycles.
The binomial coefficient
c(k_{1},k_{2}):=\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}}{k_{1}}=\frac{(k_{1}+k_{2})!}{k_{1}!k_{2}!}
satisfies the 2-cocycle equation

c(k_{1},k_{2})c(k_{1}+k_{2},k_{3})=c(k_{2},k_{3})c(k_{1},k_{2}+k_{3}).

Consequently, it can be viewed as a 2-cocycle on the monoid
(\mathbb{Z}_{+},+)
of non-negative integers under addition, taking values in the abelian group
\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
. The binomial cocycle is null-homologous, since
c(k_{1},k_{2})=f(k_{1}+k_{2})f(k_{1})^{-1}f(k_{2})^{-1}
. It is the coboundary of 1-cochain
f:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
given by
f(k)=k!
.

Likewise, the q-binomial coefficient
c_{q}(k_{1},k_{2}):=\frac{[k_{1}+k_{2}]!}{[k_{1}]![k_{2}]!}
, where
[k]!:=[k]\cdots[2][1]
and
[n]:=\frac{q^{n}-q^{-n}}{q-q^{-1}}=q^{n-1}+q^{n-3}+\ldots+q^{1-n}
is a 2-cocycle
c_{q}:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\times\mathbb{Z}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(q)^{\ast}
, valued in the group of invertible elements of the field of rational functions in
q
with rational coefficients. Again, this 2-cocycle is null-homologous, being the coboundary of the 1-cochain
f:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
,
f(k)=[k]!
.

Binomials and generalized binomials are interpreted cohomologically in
[
Vig23
]
in a more sophisticated way, and we are not aware of a reference or use for this trivial interpretation, as null-homologous 2-cocycles on the monoid
\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with values in
\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
.

More generally, pick a function
g:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}^{\times}
, where
\mathbf{k}
is a field, and define a 1-cochain
f:\mathbb{Z}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbf{k}^{\ast}
by
f(0)=1
and
f(k)=g(1)g(2)\cdots g(k)
for
k>0
. Then the generalized binomial coefficient (the Fontené–Ward coefficient
[
Vig23
]
)

c_{g}(k_{1},k_{2}):=f(k_{1}+k_{2})f(k_{1})^{-1}f(k_{2})^{-1}

is the coboundary of
f
.

Note that
[
Gor13
,
Gar09
]
for
a>b>0

aH\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\biggl(\binom{an}{bn}\biggr),

realizing the entropy as a limit via binomial coefficients (set
a=1
to get
H(b)
).

The factorial function extends to the Gamma function
\Gamma:\mathbb{R}_{>0}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by

\Gamma(x)\ :=\ \int_{0}^{\infty}t^{x-1}e^{-t}dt,

with
n!=\Gamma(n+1)
.
Beta function

B(x_{1},x_{2}):=\frac{\Gamma(x_{1})\Gamma(x_{2})}{\Gamma(x_{1}+x_{2})}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x_{1}-1}(1-t)^{x_{2}-1}dt

gives rise to a null-homologous 2-cocycle

\beta:\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\ast},\ \ \beta(x_{1},x_{2})=B(x_{1}+1,x_{2}+1).

Monoid
\mathbb{R}_{+}
can be replaced by the larger monoid
\mathbb{C}_{+}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}|\textsf{Re }z\geq 0\}
, under addition, which still avoids the poles of
\Gamma(z+1)
, resulting in a null-homologous 2-cocycle
\beta:\mathbb{C}_{+}\times\mathbb{C}_{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
. Poles of
\Gamma(z)
provide an obstruction to extending the 2-cocycle
\beta
to all of
\mathbb{C}
.

Remark 6.6
.

The PMI cocycle.
Pointwise Mutual Information (PMI) between a pair of discrete outcomes
x
and
y
of random variables
X
and
Y
is given by

(6.13)

\mathsf{PMI}(x,y)\ =\ \log\frac{p(x,y)}{p(x)p(y)}.

Here it makes sense at first to restrict to
p(x),p(y)\not=0
, to avoid a possible zero in the denominator.
Even with that restriction,
\mathsf{PMI}(x,y)
may take value
-\infty
, if
x
and
y
are disjoint, that is,
p(x,y)=0
. Thus,
\mathsf{PMI}(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
.

Pointwise Mutual Information is commonly used in linguistics and natural language processing (NLP)
[
Wik24
,
Fan68
,
CH90
,
Bou09
]
. The original word embedding construction of Mikolov et al.
[
MCCD13
,
MSC+13
]
is interpreted as factorizations of the PMI matrix and its variations in Levy and Goldberg
[
LG14
]
.

Observe that
\mathsf{PMI}
is a null-homologous 2-cocycle, since

\mathsf{PMI}(x,y)=\log p(x,y)-\log p(x)-\log p(y)=\log p(x\cap y)-\log p(x)-\log p(y).

Here
x,y
are be viewed as elements of the commutative monoid
\mathsf{M}(\Omega)
of measurable subsets of a probability space
\Omega
, with
x\sim x^{\prime}
if
p(x\Delta x^{\prime})=0
and
x+y:=x\cap y
. Random variables
X
and
Y
above are discrete measurable functions on
\Omega
.
Map

(6.14)

\mathsf{PMI}\ :\ \mathsf{M}(\Omega)\times\mathsf{M}(\Omega)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},\ \ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}

is a null-homologous 2-cocycle on the commutative monoid
\mathsf{M}(\Omega)
taking values in the commutative monoid
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. For consistency, we can set
\mathsf{PMI}(\varnothing,y)=-\log p(y)
. Nevertheless, one runs into the issue that subtraction is not well-defined in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
when claiming that cocycle

is null-homologous, see a related short discussion close to the end of Remark
6.8
below.

Remark 6.8

The carry cocycle.

given by
(i,j)\mapsto\lfloor\frac{i+j}{N}\rfloor
. It has been pointed out and rediscovered several times
[
Dol23
,
Isa02
,
DGPV23
]
that the map is a 2-cocycle which defines a nontrivial extension
0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/N\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/(N^{2})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}/N
of abelian groups. This 2-cocycle is
not
null-homologous and gives a generator of the cohomology group
\mathsf{H}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N,\mathbb{Z}/N)
.

The carry
\lfloor\frac{i+j}{N}\rfloor
can be thought of as
excess
that digits
i
and
j
produce when added. Upon reflection, one realizes that many operations with “excess” in them can be interpreted as 2-cocycles. Let us provide some examples.

(a) Consider a set
\mathcal{S}
of companies. Denote by
p(A)
yearly profit of a company
A\in\mathcal{S}
. Suppose that if two companies merge, they can streamline operations and produce excess profit
e(A,B)
per year. Denote by
A+B
the merged company. Then the excess profit

(6.15)

e(A,B)=p(A+B)-p(A)-p(B).

The excess profit is a symmetric 2-cocycle:
A,B,C
can merge into
A+B+C
in different orders, with

p(A,B)+p(A+B,C)=p(B,C)+p(A,B+C),

where both sides show the total excess profit of going from
A,B,C
to
A+B+C
.
The 2-cocycle equation also follows at once from (
6.15
).
Equation (
6.15
) tells us that 2-cocycle
p
is null-homologous:
e=dp
, the differential of the one-cochain
p
.
To phrase this carefully, consider the Boolean monoid
\mathcal{PS}
of all subsets of
\mathcal{S}
, with addition being the disjoint union. In particular,
A+A=A
(if a company
A
merges with itself, it remains
A
). Then
p:\mathcal{PS}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a one-cochain (a function) from the abelian idempotent monoid
\mathcal{PS}
to the abelian group of real numbers. Here, for a subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{S}
, we define
p(\mathcal{U})
as the profit of the company given by merging all companies in
\mathcal{U}
. Furthermore,
e=dp
is a null-homologous
\mathbb{R}
-valued 2-cocycle,

e\ :\ \mathcal{PS}\times\mathcal{PS}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}.

One can further allow a company to split into two companies, going from
A
to
(A_{1},A_{2})
, and extend
e
to the split operation correspondingly. The diagrammatics of split and merge will be similar to the ones developed in the present paper for groups. One can also introduce red lines, describing external changes that modify profits
p(\mathcal{U})
of
\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{PS}
. It is not clear whether such diagrammatics may be useful.

(b) For a similar example, Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of people,
\mathcal{T}
a set of tasks (or achievements) that people can accomplish. For a subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{S}
denote by
t(\mathcal{U})\subset\mathcal{T}
the set of tasks that the subset
\mathcal{U}
of people can accomplish together. The one-cochain
t:\mathcal{PS}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PT}
is a function from subsets of people to subsets of tasks.

Consider excess tasks

(6.16)

e(\mathcal{U}_{1},\mathcal{U}_{2})\ :=\ t(\mathcal{U}_{1}+\mathcal{U}_{2})\setminus(t(\mathcal{U}_{1})+t(\mathcal{U}_{2})).

These are tasks that the union
\mathcal{U}_{1}+\mathcal{U}_{2}:=\mathcal{U}_{1}\cup\mathcal{U}_{2}
of people can accomplish but that cannot be accomplished individually by
\mathcal{U}_{1}
or
\mathcal{U}_{2}
.
Excess tasks is a function

e\ :\ \mathcal{PS}\times\mathcal{PS}\longrightarrow\mathcal{PT}.

Here both
\mathcal{PS}
and
\mathcal{PT}
are idempotent commutative monoids of subsets of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{T}
, respectively. Equation (
6.16
) indicates that
e=dt
, the coboundary of the one-cochain
t
. One should be careful here, since
\mathcal{PT}
, the target of maps
t
and
e
, is not an abelian group, only a monoid, but with an additional operation
\setminus
. Defining cohomology structures in this setup might take one into nonabelian homological algebra
[
Gra13
,
CC19
]
.

In the above two examples the 2-cocycles are null-homologous. Null-homologous 2-cocycles are less interesting that not null-homologous ones, and it would make sense to look for more examples similar to the carry cocycle, representing nontrivial cohomology classes.

Remark 6.9
.

Affine group acting on a bank account.
Let us view the amount of money
x
one has in a bank account as an element of the abelian group
M=\mathbb{Q}
(for instance, one may have
\$100.5
in the account), assuming that the bank allows negative amounts. One may add or subtract some amount (debits and credits), sending
x
to
x+b
, for
b\in\mathbb{Q}
. The account may also earn interest, scaling
x
to
ax
,
a\in\mathbb{Q}^{\ast}
within a unit time (typically,
a-1
is a small positive number).

We see that the affine group
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})
acts on the set of possible values
M=\mathbb{Q}
that money in a bank account can take. The action is not linear but can be made linear by embedding
M
as an affine line in
\mathbb{Q}^{2}
via the map
x\longmapsto(x,1)
, with the action

(6.17)

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}x\\
1\end{pmatrix}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}ax+b\\
1\end{pmatrix}.

Can this toy example be extended to a nontrivial structure relevant to economics or finance? For instance, would it make sense to replace vectors
(x,1)
by arbitrary vectors
(x,y)\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
that are acted upon by a subgroup of
\mathsf{GL}(2,\mathbb{Q})
other than
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{Q})
, via the usual matrix action

(6.18)

\begin{pmatrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}a_{11}x+a_{12}y\\
a_{21}x+a_{22}y\end{pmatrix}?

For another relation of the affine group to finance, note that Kelly’s original derivation of his famous betting formula
[
KJ56
,
Tho11
]
is based on entropy and information theory, in turn related to
\mathsf{Aff}_{1}(\mathbb{R})
as discussed earlier.

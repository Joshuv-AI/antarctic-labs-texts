Abstract
. In this work, we establish that discontinuous Galerkin methods are capable of producing reliable approximations for a broad class of nonlinear variational problems. In particular, we demonstrate that these schemes provide essential flexibility by removing inter-element continuity while also guaranteeing convergent approximations in the quasiconvex case. Notably, quasiconvexity is the weakest form of convexity pertinent to elasticity. Furthermore, we show that in the non-convex case discrete minimisers converge to minimisers of the relaxed problem. In this case, the minimisation problem corresponds to the energy defined by the quasiconvex envelope of the original energy. Our approach covers all discontinuous Galerkin formulations known to converge for convex energies. This work addresses an open challenge in the vectorial calculus of variations: developing and rigorously justifying numerical schemes capable of reliably approximating nonlinear energy minimization problems with potentially singular solutions, which are frequently encountered in materials science.



1.
Introduction

In this work, we establish that discontinuous Galerkin methods are capable of producing reliable approximations for a broad class of nonlinear variational problems. Our aim is twofold: first, to show that these methods are applicable to variational problems involving
quasiconvex energies
; and second, to establish that, even in the absence of convexity assumptions, the approximations converge to the minimisers of the
relaxed
problem. In this case, the minimisation problem corresponds to the energy defined by the
quasiconvex envelope
of the original energy.

By addressing this, we contribute to a long-standing algorithmic and theoretical challenge in the vectorial calculus of variations: the development and rigorous justification of numerical schemes capable of reliably approximating nonlinear energy minimisation problems with possibly singular solutions, which commonly arise in materials science.

The insightful observation by J.M. Ball in
[
2
]
—“minimisers of variational problems may have singularities,
but natural numerical schemes may fail to detect them
”—remains highly relevant today. As discussed in detail in
[
2
]
and
[
12
]
, although standard finite element methods provide a natural discrete framework for seeking minimisers of such problems, the inter-element continuity imposed on the discrete spaces appears to lack sufficient flexibility to make these methods the optimal choice. This limitation is particularly evident in the computation of minimisers for variational problems where the
Lavrentiev phenomenon
may occur. As first noted in
[
3
]
, standard finite element methods can converge to energy values that are strictly greater than the exact energy of the problem, leading to physically irrelevant approximations.

In the finite element literature, there are alternatives to standard finite elements that offer greater flexibility, such as nonconforming finite elements and discontinuous Galerkin methods. The spaces introduced by
[
20
]
, originally developed for the Stokes and Navier-Stokes equations, were employed by
[
33
]
(see also
[
34
]
and
[
1
]
) to demonstrate that these spaces provide sufficient flexibility to overcome the limitations of standard methods, particularly in addressing the Lavrentiev gap phenomenon.

Discontinuous Galerkin methods, which are completely discontinuous, have become especially popular for nonlinear PDEs. While these methods offer high-order approximation capabilities,
and great flexibility, they require specially constructed energy functionals, which are often nontrivial to design and analyse
[
38
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
27
]
.

\Gamma
-convergence for convex energies associated with the energy functional proposed in
[
38
]
was established in
[
11
]
, while the authors of this paper derived
\Gamma
-convergence for the functional introduced in
[
27
]
.

Despite the progress outlined above, allowing possibly noncontinuous discrete approximations comes at a cost: the analytical treatment of energies involving the discrete gradient heavily relies on the convexity of

W.
Specifically, due to the absence of a true gradient structure in the discrete gradient —i.e.,

{\rm curl}\,G_{h}(u_{h})\neq 0,

G_{h}(u_{h})
being an appropriate “discrete gradient”— the lower semicontinuity of the energy term is guaranteed for convex integrands

W,
but remains uncertain when

W
is quasiconvex. This represents a significant technical challenge that hinders further advancements in the design and analysis of approximation methods applicable to elasticity and other variational problems.

In this work, we resolve this issue by showing that discontinuous Galerkin schemes not only provide the necessary flexibility by eliminating inter-element continuity but also ensure convergent approximations in the quasiconvex case.
To compensate the lack of differential structure and regain lower semicontinuity, we transition to the limit of reconstructed approximations from
[
28
]
and demonstrate that the associated error terms can be adequately controlled. Furthermore,
we show that in the non-convex case discrete minimisers converge to minimisers of the relaxed problem.

Plan of the paper.

For
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we consider the minimisation problem associated with the energy

(1.1)

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u(x))\,dx,\quad\text{where}\,\,\,u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\,\,u=u_{0},\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega,

over an admissible set of functions which is a subset of a Sobolev space
W^{1,p}
and where
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}.
Detailed assumptions on the corresponding energy minimisation problem are in Section 3.
We start with some preliminary material in Section
2
including standard notation on the discontinuous Galerkin spaces
V_{h}(\Omega)
. Lemma
2.1
is an extension in the
L^{p}
setting of the classical result in
[
28
]
regarding the error between

u_{h}\in V_{h}(\Omega)
and its continuous reconstruction
w_{h}\in V_{h}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega).
This is a crucial tool in our analysis.

In Section
3

we introduce our discontinuous Galerkin method
[
27
]
and we show that, in the absence of convexity, our discrete energies
\Gamma
-converge to the relaxed problem. Similarly with
[
27
]
, a penalty term ensures the discrete stability, but in this case it is appropriately modified to include also energies which are not convex. Our results cover both discontinuous Galerkin formulations known to converge for convex
W.
The proof for the method based on discrete gradients
[
38
]
is postponed to the Section
3.2
. The discrete gradients are defined through standard lifting operators, defined in the same section. Although the use of the lifting operators and the discrete gradients was the main tool in the analysis in the convex case, as mentioned, this is no longer true
if we would like to consider quasiconvex integrands. The main obstacle in proving the convergence of DG schemes for quasiconvex energies lies in the fact that the finite element function
\nabla_{h}y_{h}
used to approximate the gradient of an admissible function
\nabla y
is not itself a gradient, i.e.
{\rm curl}\nabla_{h}y_{h}\neq 0
, and hence in general the lower semicontinuity of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to weak convergence in
W^{1,p}
is not true. That is, for a quasiconvex
W
, the inequality

\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla_{h}y_{h})\geq\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla y)

does not hold in general when

\nabla_{h}y_{h}\rightharpoonup\nabla y,\quad\mbox{ in }L^{p}\mbox{ as }h\to 0.

The main results of this paper are Theorems
3.1
and
3.2
where we show that the discrete minimisers
of disontinuous Galerkin schemes of
[
27
]
and
[
38
]
converge to a minimiser of
the relaxed problem defined as

\mathcal{E}^{rel}(y)=\int_{\Omega}W^{qc}(\nabla y(x))\,dx

where
W^{qc}
denotes the quasiconvex envelope of
W
, i.e. the largest quasicnovex function that lies below
W
. In particular, if
W
is quasiconvex, then
W=W^{qc},
and as a consequnce we establish convergence of the discrete mimimisers to a minimiser of the continuous quasiconvex energy.

In Section
4
, we provide detailed numerical evidence using specifically designed examples. We begin with a problem involving polyconvex energy and thoroughly analyze the impact of the new penalty term introduced in this work (see Section 3.1) in comparison to the penalty term employed in
[
27
]
.

Subsequently, we examine a frame-indifferent two-well strain energy function where, under specific boundary conditions, a phase mixture emerges. This energy leads to increasingly fine microstructures as the mesh size

h
is reduced, approaching the infimum of the total potential energy. An intriguing aspect of our experiments, consistent with the theoretical results, is the observation that for this problem, minimizing sequences with distinct laminate structures exist. Nevertheless, all such sequences strongly converge in

L^{2}
to the same function. This behavior can be attributed to the diminishing amplitude of gradient oscillations, which become negligible as

h
decreases.

An additional interesting observation is that, by leveraging Dacorogna’s formula for the quasiconvex envelope of a general energy density

W
, our scheme can approximate

W^{qc}(F)
pointwise for each

F.
This suggests that quasiconvex envelope approximations can be constructed using discrete energy minimisation techniques. While the full implementation of this idea lies beyond the scope of the current paper, we present preliminary results for this pointwise approximation in Figures
5
, 6, 7. As mentioned, these numerical experiments are for non-quasiconvex, double-well potentials

W,
which are typical in modeling phase transitions in solids.

3.
Convergence of Discrete Minimisers

In this section we introduce our discontinuous Galerkin method and we show that, in the absence of convexity, our discrete energies
\Gamma
-converge to the relaxed problem. Similarly with
[
27
]
, a penalty term ensures the discrete stability, but in this case it is slightly modified to include also energies which are not convex.

For
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a (Lipschitz) bounded domain, we consider the minimisation problem associated with the energy

(3.1)

\mathcal{E}(u)=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla u(x))\,dx,\quad\text{where}\,\,\,u:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}^{N},\,\,u=u_{0},\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega.

We assume throughout that
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is of class
C^{1}
and satisfies the following growth, coercivity, and continuity assumptions

(3.2)

\displaystyle-1+|\xi|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim W(\xi)\lesssim 1+|\xi|^{p}

(3.3)

\displaystyle|W(\xi_{1})-W(\xi_{2})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|\xi_{1}|^{p-1}+|\xi_{1}|^{p-1}\right)|\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|,

for some
p>1
. Hence, we naturally pose the problem of minimising (
3.1
) on the Sobolev space

\mathbb{A}(\Omega)=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)^{N}\,:\,u=u_{0}\,\,\mathcal{H}^{d-1}\mbox{ a.e. on $\partial\Omega$}\right\}.

Due to the lack of any convexity assumption on
W
and hence the possible absence of minimisers of (
3.1
), we introduce the relaxed energy

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E^{\rm{qc}}}(u)=\int_{\Omega}W^{qc}(\nabla u(x))\,dx,

where
W^{qc}:\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the quasiconvex envelope of
W
, and it is defined as

\displaystyle W^{qc}(\xi):=\sup\{g(\xi):\,g\leq W\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,g\,\,\text{quasiconvex}\}.

It is known that
W^{qc}
satisfies (
3.2
) and it is quasiconvex and hence the minimisation problem

\displaystyle\min_{u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E^{\rm{qc}}}(u)

admits minimisers.

Employing the presented ideas in
[
27
]
for the discretisation of energy functionals in the DG setting and applying Dirichlet boundary conditions through Nitsche’s
approach, for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
our discrete energy functional has the form

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h}(x))-\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\ S({\{\!\!\{\nabla u_{h}\}\!\!\}})\cdot{[\![u_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}),

where
\alpha
will be chosen large enough and the penalty
{\rm Pen}(u_{h})
is defined as

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h}):=

\Gamma
-convergence of the discrete energies

We may now state our main result:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be of class
C^{1}
, satisfying assumptions (
3.2
) and (
3.3

is a sequence of minimisers of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
, i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})=\min_{V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}_{h}.

Then, there exists

such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and

\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}(u)=\min_{\mathbb{A}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}.

The proof of Theorem
3.1
is a direct consequence of the fact that
\mathcal{E}_{h}

\Gamma
-converges to
\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}
with respect to the strong topology of
L^{p}(\Omega)
. The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence result. We start by proving compactness in an appropriate topology for sequences of bounded energy.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
it holds that

\|u_{h}\|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\lesssim 1+\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h}).

In particular, if

\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C

there exists
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega).

Proof.

For
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
, using assumption (
3.3
), we estimate the second term in the discrete energy (
3.5
) as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}\big|DW(\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}):{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}\big|ds=\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}\big|h^{\frac{p-1}{p}}DW(\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}):h^{-\frac{p-1}{p}}{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h(1+|\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}|^{p-1})^{\frac{p}{p-1}}ds\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h^{1-p}|{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}|^{p}ds\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h(1+|\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}|^{p})ds\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h^{1-p}|{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}|^{p}ds\right)^{1/p},

where for the first term on the RHS we further use an inverse inequality to infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h(1+|\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}|^{p})ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}h(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}^{+}|^{p}+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}^{-}|^{p})ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}h(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p})ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}dx.

Hence, by Young’s inequality, we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma_{\text{int}}}\big|DW(\{\{\nabla_{h}u_{h}\}\}):{[\![v_{h}\otimes n_{e}]\!]}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{1-p}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad+\delta\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}\right).

The coercivity assumption on
W
says that

W(\xi)\gtrsim-1+|\xi|^{p}.

Thus, choosing
\delta
appropriately as a function of
p
, by (
3.9
) we find that

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\gtrsim\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}-1-C(p)\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

Next, note that since

(3.10)

|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\geq\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

the penalty term in (
3.6
) satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1+\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that the conjugate exponents
(p-1)/p
and
1/p
add up to
1
. Then, provided that
\alpha>C(p)
, we deduce that

\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\gtrsim\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}|^{p}-1+(\alpha-C(p))\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}

and hence that

|u_{h}|^{p}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\lesssim 1+\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h}).

Furthermore, if
\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C
,
||u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}
is uniformly bounded which implies from the Poincaré inequality (
2.10
) that
||u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C^{\prime}
. From the compactness of the space
W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})
, see
[
11
, Theorem 5.2]
,
we infer that there exists a function
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle u_{h}\to u\,\,\,\text{in}\,\,L^{p}(\Omega).

To prove

note that

||u-u_{0}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\leq||u_{h}-u_{0}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}+||u-u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}.

As

the first term of the right hand side converge to zero due to the assumption that
\sup_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}(u_{h})\leq C
and

||u-u_{h}||_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
from the compact embedding in
[
11
, Lemma 8]
.
∎

3.1

3.2.
DG methods based on discrete gradients

We start with a definition of discrete gradients in the discontinuous Galerkin setting. To this end,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}R_{h}(\varphi):w_{h}\,dx=\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\kern-3.125pt\{w_{h}\}\kern-3.125pt\}:\llbracket\varphi\otimes n_{e}\rrbracket\,ds,\quad\forall w_{h}\in V^{q-1}_{h}(\Omega).

This operator leads to the definition of the discrete gradient

for
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}
as

(3.31)

\displaystyle G_{h}(u_{h})=\nabla_{h}u_{h}-R_{h}(u_{h}).

The method of Ten Eyck and Lew (2006)
[
38
]
is based on minimising over the DG space the functional defined on
V^{q}_{h}:

(3.32)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{G},h}[u_{h}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(\nabla u_{h}(x)-R_{h}(u_{h}))+\alpha\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in T_{h}}\int_{K}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))+\alpha\text{Pen}\,({u_{h}}).

Convergence in the case of convex
W
was derived in
[
11
]
and error estimates for smooth solutions were derived in

[
35
]
. The evaluation of this functional requires the
computation of
R_{h}(u_{h}),
and thus of the “discrete gradient”
G_{h}(u_{h})
which appears in
W.

Computationally, the cost of this operation is reasonable, since it involves the solution of local systems.
In the convex case, the weak convergence properties of the discrete gradient were enough to establish lim-inf inequalities and conclude the proof of convergence of minimisers. In our setting this is no longer the case. As in the previous section
the key idea is still to use Lemma
2.2
and to extract a sequence
w_{h}\in V_{h}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}(\Omega)
such that
(
3.12
), (
3.13
) will hold.
Then, (
3.11
) will imply that
w_{h}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Repeating the same arguments as in Lemma
3.2
, we conclude

(3.33)

\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla w_{h})\geq\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{\Omega}W^{qc}(\nabla w_{h})\geq\int_{\Omega}W^{qc}(\nabla u)=\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}(u).

And thus it suffices to show that

(3.34)

\liminf_{h\to 0}\left[A_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})\right]\geq 0.

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\text{G},h}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla w_{h})+\int_{\Omega}W(G_{h}(u_{h}))-W(\nabla w_{h})+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle=:\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla w_{h})+A_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

To estimate
A_{h}

we shall use the known bound for the lifting operator,
[
11
,
22
]
,

(3.35)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(v_{h})|^{p}\leq C_{R}\sum_{e\in E_{h}^{i}}h_{e}^{1-p}\int_{e}|\llbracket v_{h}\rrbracket|^{p},\qquad v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega),

where the constant
C_{R}
is independent of
h
. A consequence of this bound, see
[
27
]
, is
that for all
v_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
it holds that

(3.36)

\|G_{h}(v_{h})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C|v_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}.

Next, proceeding as in Lemma
3.2
, we conclude

\displaystyle|A_{h}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p-1}+|\nabla w_{h}|^{p-1}\right)|G_{h}(u_{h})-\nabla w_{h}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p-1}+|\nabla w_{h}|^{p-1}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|G_{h}(u_{h})-\nabla w_{h}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left[\int_{\Omega}\left(1+|G_{h}(u_{h})|^{p}+|\nabla w_{h}|^{p}\right)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left[\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{h}u_{h}-\nabla w_{h}|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{\Omega}|R_{h}(v_{h})|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(1+|u_{h}|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}^{p}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(\sum_{e\in E^{i}_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h}).

As a result,

A_{h}+\alpha\,{\rm Pen}(u_{h})\geq\left(\alpha-\tilde{C}\right){\rm Pen}(u_{h})\,.

Thus, if
\alpha>\tilde{C},
(
3.34
) follows.
The lim-sup inequality follows by adopting in similar fashion arguments from the previous section.
We therefore obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
W:{\mathbb{R}}^{N\times d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be of class
C^{1}
, satisfying assumptions (
3.2
) and (
3.3
). Suppose that
u_{h}\in V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)
is a sequence of minimisers of
\mathcal{E}_{\text{G},h}
, i.e.

\mathcal{E}_{\text{G},h}(u_{h})=\min_{V^{q}_{h}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}_{\text{G},h}.

Then, there exists
u\in\mathbb{A}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,

u_{h}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)

and

\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}(u)=\min_{\mathbb{A}(\Omega)}\mathcal{E}^{\rm qc}.

4.
Computational Results

In this section it is illustrated how the proposed numerical scheme approximates minimisers for the
total potential energy when
polyconvex (and hence quasiconvex) and multi-well strain energy functions are employed. In all computations we use the scheme described in Section 3, see (
3.5
). For the first case we compare our
scheme with a generalisation of the standard
L^{2}
interior penalty method for convex energies,

[
27
]
, while for the latter case we show how a sequence of discrete minimisers is formed
which converges to the minimiser of the relaxed problem.

In the sequel
y:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\supset\Omega\rightarrow\Omega^{*}
will denote
plane deformations from
\Omega
(undeformed body) to
\Omega^{*}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
(deformed configuration). Under given boundary
conditions we seek deformations that minimise the total potential energy

(4.1)

\displaystyle\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\\
y=y_{0}\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}\mathcal{E}(y)=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\\
y=y_{0}\text{ on }\partial\Omega\end{subarray}}\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla y)dx,

where
W
is the strain energy function characterising the material properties.

Examples with a polyconvex strain energy function

We would like to approximate minimisers of eq. (
4.1
) through the discretised energy (
3.5
) for the polyconvex strain energy function
W(F)=|\det F|^{2}
. We compare the
proposed penalty of eq. (
3.6
) with an appropriate penalty for convex energies,

[
27
]
, given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle{\rm Pen}(u_{h})

\displaystyle:=\left(1+|u_{h}|^{p-2}_{W^{1,p}(\Omega,T_{h})}\right)\left(\sum_{e\in E_{h}}\frac{1}{h_{e}^{p-1}}\int_{e}|\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket|^{p}\right)^{\frac{2}{p}},

which for
p=2
reduces to the standard
L^{2}
error. In eq. (
4.1
) we choose
y_{0}(x)=F_{0}x
where

(4.3)

\displaystyle F_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0.9\end{pmatrix}.

corresponding to uniaxial compression. Here
y_{0}
is a homogeneous deformation,
therefore polyconvexity implies that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla y)\geq\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla y_{0})\text{ for all }y\in W^{1,4}(\Omega)\text{ with }y=y_{0}\text{ on }\partial\Omega,

which means that every admissible deformation results in a potential energy no less than that of
y_{0}
. We seek discrete minimisers in
V_{h}^{1}(\Omega)
, i.e. piecewise polynomials of first degree.
The structure of the described minimisation problem indicates that for big enough stabilisation parameter
\alpha
in
eq. (
3.5
), the computed minimiser should satisfy inequality (
4.4
). As it is
illustrated in fig.
1
where the blue curve approaches the black dashed line, large values of the stabilisation parameter
\alpha
are required to approach
\int_{\Omega}W(\nabla y_{0})
when the penalty of eq. (
4.2
) is used.
On the contrary for the penatly of eq. (
3.6
), even small values of
\alpha
bring the discrete minimiser close to
W^{1,4}(\Omega)
, i.e. the orange curve is always close to the black dashed line.
To examine further this behaviour we plot
1/\det\nabla y
in the deformed configuration,
fig.
2
. For both penalties
y_{h}
approaches
y_{0}
as
\alpha
increases but for the
term (
4.2
) large values of
\alpha
are required while for (
3.6
) even small
values produce a minimiser close to
y_{0}
. These observations are quantified in fig.
3

where we compare the
L^{1}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,1}(\Omega)
errors
between
y_{0}
and the computed minimisers
y_{h}
varying the penalty parameter
\alpha
.

Non rank-one convex energies and minimising sequences

We construct a frame indifferent two-well strain energy function where under specific boundary conditions a phase mixture
emerges with finer and finer microstructure, approaching the infimum of the total potential energy as we decrease the
mesh size
h
. Mimicking the cubic to
orthorhombic phase transitions in crystals we define the energy
W(F)=|C-V^{2}||C-I|^{2}
, where
C=F^{T}F
is the right
Cauchy-Green tensor,
I
is the identity and

(4.5)

\displaystyle V=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{a_{o}+b_{o}}{2}&\frac{b_{o}-a_{o}}{2}\\
\frac{b_{o}-a_{o}}{2}&\frac{a_{o}+b_{o}}{2}\end{pmatrix}.

Choosing
a_{0}=\sqrt{2-b_{0}^{2}}
and
b_{0}=0.9
,
equation
RV-I=d\otimes n
has two
solutions namely

\{R_{1},d_{1},n_{1}\}
,
\{R_{2},d_{2},n_{2}\}
such that
n_{1}\approx(1,0)
and
n_{2}\approx(0,1)
, where
R_{i}\in SO(2)
, see
[
4
, Proposition 4]
.
This essentially means, that there exist continuous deformations with discontinuous deformation gradients across the
planes defined by
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
. Note that
W
is zero when the deformation gradient lies at
SO(2)V
or at
SO(2)I
. Now we define
y_{0}
in eq.(
4.1
) such that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\nabla y_{0}=0.5I+0.5R_{1}V,

and we initialise
y_{h}
by the interpolation
y_{h}=I_{h}^{1}y_{0}
, where

I_{h}^{1}:W^{1,p}(\Omega)\rightarrow V_{h}^{1}(\Omega)
.
Discretising the domain through equal squares with normals
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, where each rectangle is divided to four triangles, perfect interfaces between
discontinuous deformation gradients (phases) can occur due to this triangulation. For a comparison of meshes with or without the above property see

[
25
, Section 4.5]
.
Minimising the discrete version of eq. (
4.1
) the total potential energy
and
||y_{h}-y_{0}||_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
decrease
for smaller mesh sizes, fig.
4
. The energy is reduced through a minimising
sequence as it is depicted in fig.
5
, describing the deformed state.
In some regions perfect interfaces with
zero contribution are formed between
\nabla y_{h}=I
and
\nabla y_{h}=R_{1}V

across the direction
n_{1}=(1,0)
and between
\nabla y_{h}=I
and
\nabla y_{h}=R_{2}V

across the direction
n_{2}=(0,1)
. These regions illustrate the minimising sequence in the reference configuration,
see fig.
6
.
Note that
R_{1}V
is not rank-one connected to
R_{2}V
,
R_{1}\neq R_{2}
. Hence, a transition layer
is formed between these two deformations, green color in fig.
6
. This is the only
non-zero part of the energy. As finer and finer microstructures appear on each side of a green curve

y_{h}\rightarrow y_{0}
in
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
as
h\rightarrow 0
, which means a “macroscopic” deformation is formed equal to
y_{0}
and
the energy at the transition layer tends to zero as
h\rightarrow 0
, see also fig.
7
. In this case, the discrete
minimiser captures a minimising sequence
\{y_{k}\}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
such that
y_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ast}}{{\rightharpoonup}}y_{0}
in
W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)

where
\{\nabla y_{k}\}
generates the homogeneous Young measure

\nu_{x}=0.5\delta_{I}+0.5\delta_{V}
. Note that
y_{0}
is the minimiser of the relaxed problem.

The above simulations have been performed by choosing
\alpha=80
in eq. (
3.5
). Note that, for the proposed multi-well energy

-1+|F|^{8}\lesssim W(F)\lesssim 1+|F|^{8}.

We have noticed that when
p=8
roundoff errors affects the discrete minimisation process. Note that
the second term of the proposed penalty, eq.(
3.6
), is getting less important numerically, due to the fact that the
power of
1/8
appears and small numbers are described by finite precision. One can rewrite the jump terms of eq. (
3.5
) as

(4.7)

\displaystyle jumps=\sum_{e\in E_{h}}h_{e}\int_{e}\left|\frac{\llbracket u_{h}\rrbracket}{h_{e}}\right|^{p}ds,

and the penalty term as

(4.8)

\displaystyle\alpha{\rm Pen}(u_{h})=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\int_{\Omega}1+W(\nabla u_{h})dx+jumps\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}\left(2^{p}jumps\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Under the above modifications we have obtained minimisers that capture minimising sequences of the continuous problem.
The above minimising sequence appears for both
y_{h}\in V_{h}^{q}(\Omega)\cap C^{0}(\Omega)
and
y_{h}\in V_{h}^{q}(\Omega)
, where both
converge to the minimiser of the relaxed problem, since the proved convergence results hold for conforming finite element spaces as well.

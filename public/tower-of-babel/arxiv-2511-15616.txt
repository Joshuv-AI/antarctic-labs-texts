Splitting vector bundles over real algebraic varieties
Aravind Asok, Jean Fasel, Samuel Lerbet

Abstract
Suppose X X is a smooth affine real variety and ℰ \mathscr{E} is a vector bundle over X X . We analyze the problem of splitting off a free rank one summand from ℰ \mathscr{E} in corank 0 0 and 1 1 . The problem in corank 0 0 can be viewed as the search for a real analog of Murthy’s celebrating splitting theorem in the algebraically closed case: to wit, beyond the vanishing of the top Chern class in Chow theory, are the obstructions to splitting “purely topological”? In a sense, the answer in this case is yes, and we give a proof, using motivic techniques, of a mild extension of the results of Bhatwadekar-Sridharan and Bhatwadekar–Das–Mandal. In corank 1 1 , in the algebraically closed situation, Murthy’s splitting conjecture (now a theorem in characteristic 0 0 ) predicts that the vanishing of the top Chern class in Chow theory is the only obstruction to splitting off a free rank 1 1 summand, and we can search for a suitable “real” analog of this assertion. We observe that several natural guesses for a “real” analog of Murthy’s splitting conjecture cannot be true, i.e., that the situation over the real numbers is rather complicated.



Introduction

Assume
M
is a smooth manifold of dimension
d
and
E
is a rank
r
(real) vector bundle on

admits a nowhere vanishing section or, equivalently, splits off a free rank

, then there are additional obstructions to existence of a nowhere vanishing section beyond the vanishing of the Euler class. In the early 1950s, at least under the additional assumption that

[
34
]
for bundles with vanishing Euler class. The extension of Liao’s result to not necessarily oriented bundles seems not to be available in the literature, but has attained the status of folklore, and cast in the language of modern obstruction theory presents no essential difficulties.

For algebraic vector bundles on smooth affine varieties over a field

1.3
(or
[
8
]

Swan observed at roughly the inception of the theory of projective modules that the tangent bundle of the real

In corank

Over the real numbers, the Euler class theory was linked to topological considerations in this case as well. In in

15

These two theorems show that the relationship between algebraic and topological obstructions is surprisingly intricate. It could be argued that the case of rank

On the other hand, in the algebraic setting, the associated secondary obstruction arises from a homotopy sheaf that lies outside the stable range. In higher dimensions, the algebraic obstruction simplifies slightly because of stability phenomena, whereas the topological splitting problem becomes more complicated owing to the presence of secondary obstructions as described by Liao. There is, however, some evidence that the phenomenon we observe persists in higher dimension, and we give some remarks to this effect in the main body of our work. That being said, at the end of the paper, we also establish some positive results in the following form.

1.4
Real realization and its avatars

Suppose that the base field
k
admits a real embedding
k\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, that we fix in the sequel. Left Kan extension of the functor that assigns to a smooth
k
-variety
X
the real manifold
X({\mathbb{R}})
(endowed with the usual Euclidean topology) extends to a real realization functor

{\mathfrak{R}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}:\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}(k)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}

where
\mathrm{H}_{\bullet}
is the “classical” homotopy category of spaces. As a left Kan extension, this functor preserves (homotopy) colimits, as well as finite products. In particular, the real realization of the motivic sphere
S^{p,q}
is the ordinary sphere
S^{p}
, and there are induced homomorphisms:

\bm{\pi}_{i,j}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}(X,x)({\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow\pi_{i}(X({\mathbb{R}}),x).

Many of the fundamental problems we need to address can be phrased as problems of commuting (homotopy) limits and colimits. Indeed, we will routinely use fiber sequences when studying obstruction theory, and there is no formal reason for fiber sequences to be preserved by real realization. This leads to what might be interpreted as unexpected behavior of some spaces, e.g., real realization of Voevodsky’s motivic Eilenberg–Mac Lane spaces are analyzed in
[
2
, §7.3]
.

Nevertheless, when fiber sequences may be realized geometrically, such compatibilities are straightforward to check. For example, either using the fact that the simplicial classifying space
BGL_{n}
can be described as a geometric realization, for
n\geq 2
, the motivic or
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
-fiber sequence

{\mathbb{A}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathrm{BGL}_{n-1}\longrightarrow\mathrm{BGL}_{n}

realizes to the fiber sequence

S^{n-1}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\}\longrightarrow\mathrm{BO}_{n-1}({\mathbb{R}})\longrightarrow\mathrm{BO}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}).

Our general goal is to compare the Moore-Postnikov towers associated to these fiber sequences, and we’ll need concrete realization morphisms in order to perform this comparison. We will introduce the real cycle class map in the next section, but we begin by recalling a well-known classical result.

Lemma 1.4.1
.

Let
E
be a rank
2
topological real vector bundle on a three-dimensional manifold
M
such that the Euler class of
E
vanishes. Then
E
splits as
E\simeq\det E\oplus\varepsilon^{1}
where
\varepsilon^{1}
is the trivial rank
1
bundle on
M
and
\det E
is the determinant bundle of
E
. In particular, if
\det E
is trivial, then
E
is trivial.

4.1.2
An example

We now exhibit a situation where the homomorphism

to
{\mathrm{H}}^{i}(N,{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
(singular cohomology with coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}/2

Let

denote the disjoint union of two copies of the circle

and let
\mathbb{T}=S^{1}\times S^{1}
denote the
2

,
s\cup y=0
,

induced by sending

We also have by the Künneth formula a decomposition

Lemma 4.1.5

The Bockstein

{\mathrm{H}}^{0}(M)\otimes{\mathrm{H}}^{2}(\mathbb{T})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{3}(M\times\mathbb{T})

p^{*}x\cup q^{*}\beta_{2}

p^{*}t\cup q^{*}\beta_{2}

Let
C

The image of the Borel–Haefliger cycle class map

Since

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
X
be a topological space of (sheaf) cohomological dimension
\leqslant d
. If

is an epimorphism of abelian sheaves on

is an epimorphism of abelian groups. Moreover, if

is an exact sequence of abelian sheaves on

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{A})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{B})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{C})\rightarrow 0
is acyclic.

The lemma applies when

is a noetherian topological space of Krull dimension
\leqslant d
, for example a noetherian scheme of Krull dimension
\leqslant d
(for us,

will usually be a smooth
k

d

Proof.

Let
i:\mathbf{K}\rightarrow\mathbf{B}
be the kernel of the homomorphism
\mathbf{B}\rightarrow\mathbf{C}
of sheaves. Then the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathbf{K}\longrightarrow\mathbf{B}\longrightarrow\mathbf{C}\longrightarrow 0

of sheaves on

induces an exact sequence

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{B})\xrightarrow{g_{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{C})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d+1}(X,\mathbf{K})

of abelian groups. Since
X
has cohomological dimension
\leqslant d
,
{\mathrm{H}}^{d+1}(X,\mathbf{K})=0
so the map
g_{*}:{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{B})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{C})
is surjective. If now we have an acyclic complex

\mathbf{A}\xrightarrow{f}\mathbf{B}\xrightarrow{g}\mathbf{C}\rightarrow 0

of sheaves, then

is the image of

induces an epimorphism

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{K})\xrightarrow{\iota_{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{B}),\;{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{A})\xrightarrow{\overline{f}_{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{K})\xrightarrow{i_{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{B})

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a\to{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2
induces an isomorphism

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a)\to{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2)

of abelian groups.

Proof.

If
a=2^{m}\cdot n
with
n
odd, we have an exact sequence

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/n\xrightarrow{\cdot 2^{m}}{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a\to{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m}\to 0

that induces an exact sequence

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/n)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\rightarrow 0

by Lemma
4.3.1
. The group
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1})
is
n
-divisible by Lemma
4.3.4
so the group
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/n)
vanishes by Corollary
4.3.2
. Therefore the map
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})
is an isomorphism so that we may replace
a
by
2^{m}
and suppose that
a=2^{m}
. Now we use the pull-backs and push-forward along
f:X_{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow X
to obtain a composite

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\xrightarrow{f^{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X_{{\mathbb{C}}},{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\xrightarrow{f_{*}}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})

which is multiplication by
2
since
f
is a degree
2
étale cover. The group
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X_{\mathbb{C}},{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})
vanishes by Corollary
4.3.2
since
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X_{\mathbb{C}},{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1})
is divisible by Lemma
4.3.4
. Thus
2{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})=0
. Now applying Corollary
4.3.2
with
\mathbf{A}={{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m}
and
l=2
, so that
\mathbf{A}/l={{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2
, we have an exact sequence

{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\xrightarrow{2}{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2)\rightarrow 0

so that
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2^{m})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/2)
is an isomorphism. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 4.3.6
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine real variety of dimension
d
, and let
a\neq 0
be an even integer. Then the real realization map

\overline{\gamma_{\mathbb{R}}}:{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1})\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/2)

induces an isomorphism
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/a)\rightarrow{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/2)
.

5
Positive results

In this section, we gather positive results about the main question, using the results of the above section.

5.1
Varieties without real points

Theorem 5.1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine real variety of dimension
d\geq 3
such that
X({\mathbb{R}})=\emptyset
. If
{\mathscr{E}}
is a locally free
{\mathscr{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
d-1
, then
{\mathscr{E}}\simeq{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{X}
if and only if
c_{d-1}({\mathscr{E}})=0
.

Proof.

We just have to prove that the primary and secondary obstructions vanish. For the first one, we note that the conditions of Proposition
3.1.2
are trivially satisfied in that case. Thus, our assumption that
c_{d-1}({\mathscr{E}})=0
implies the vanishing of the (algebraic) Euler class. We are thus left with proving that the secondary obstruction also vanishes. If
d\geq 5
, we have an exact sequence

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/24\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{d-1}({\mathbb{A}}^{d-1}\smallsetminus\{0\})\to\mathbf{GW}_{d}^{d-1}

and we know that
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{d+1}/24)={\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/2)=0
. On the other hand, Corollary
4.1.2
shows that
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{GW}_{d}^{d-1}({\mathcal{L}}))=0
as well, for any line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
over
X
. The secondary obstruction then vanishes automatically and the result is proved. If
d=4
, the relavant exact sequence of sheaves is of the form

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{5}/24\times_{\overline{\mathbf{I}^{5}}}\mathbf{I}^{5}\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{3}({\mathbb{A}}^{3}\smallsetminus\{0\})\to\mathbf{GW}_{4}^{3}

Using the exact sequence of sheaves
0\to\mathbf{I}^{6}\to\mathbf{I}^{5}\to\overline{\mathbf{I}}^{5}\to 0
, we see that the left-hand sheaf fits into an exact sequence

0\to\mathbf{I}^{6}\to{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{5}/12\times_{\overline{\mathbf{I}^{5}}}\mathbf{I}^{5}\to{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{5}/12\to 0

and it suffices to observe that
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X,\mathbf{I}^{6}({\mathcal{L}}))=0
for any line bundle
{\mathcal{L}}
over
X
by Theorem
1.4.3
to conclude as in the previous case. The same argument works if
d=3
, using this time the exact sequence

{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{M}}_{4}/12\times_{\overline{\mathbf{I}^{4}}}\mathbf{I}^{4}\to{\bm{\pi}}^{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\smallsetminus\{0\})\to\mathbf{GW}_{3}^{2}.

∎

Remark 5.1.2
.

Similar computations of the group
\mathrm{H}^{d}(X,{{\mathbf{K}}}^{\mathrm{MW}}_{d+1})
appear in
[
19
]
(see below), but they assume that
X({\mathbb{R}})
is orientable and non empty. Consequently we cannot use their result in our situation.

Remark 5.1.3
.

In fact, Theorem
5.1.1
is valid under the less restrictive hypothesis that
X(\mathbb{R})
has cohomological dimension
\leqslant d-3
. It suffices to observe that Proposition
3.1.2
, Corollary
4.1.2
and Corollary
4.3.6
hold in this context.

5.2
Varieties with open real locus

Theorem 5.2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine real variety of dimension
d\geq 3
such that
X({\mathbb{R}})
has no connected compact component. If
{\mathscr{E}}
is a locally free
{\mathscr{O}}_{X}
-module of rank
d-1
, then
{\mathscr{E}}\simeq{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{X}
if and only if
e({\mathscr{E}})=0
.

Proof.

As in the proof of the previous theorem, it suffices to prove that the secondary obstruction vanishes. The first part vanishes since
{\mathrm{Ch}}^{d}(X)=0
by
[
18
, Theorem 1.3.(b)]
, and the second part vanishes since
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}})={\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}}/2)=0
as
X(\mathbb{R})
has no compact connected component.
∎

Remark 5.2.2
.

In case
X
is orientable, we could instead use
[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
to conclude.

Remark 5.2.3
.

As in the previous section, one may prove the theorem under weaker conditions. Indeed, it suffices to assume that
{\mathrm{H}}^{d}(X({\mathbb{R}}),{\mathbb{Z}})=0
to obtain the same result.

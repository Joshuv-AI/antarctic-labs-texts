A geometric approach to the uniform boundedness of ℓ \ell -primary torsion points
Zhuchao Ji, Jiarui Song, Junyi Xie

Abstract
We prove that for a non-isotrivial abelian scheme over a smooth curve, the genus of a generic sequence of multi-sections with small heights tends to infinity. As an application, we give a new proof of the uniform boundedness of ℓ \ell -primary torsion points on fibers of an abelian scheme over a smooth curve, a result originally proved by Cadoret and Tamagawa. Furthermore, our approach allows us to resolve a conjecture of Cadoret and Tamagawa without additional assumptions. Our approach is based on the theory of Betti foliations and the arithmetic equidistribution theorem.



1
Introduction

In the study of Diophantine geometry, a central object of interest is the torsion subgroup of an abelian variety. The uniform boundedness conjecture predicts the existence of a uniform upper bound on the order of torsion subgroups based only on the dimension of the abelian variety and the degree of the base field.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Fix positive integers
g
and
D
. There exists a positive integer
N(g,D)
such that for any abelian variety
A
of dimension
g
defined over a number field
K
of degree
[K:\mathbb{Q}]=D
, the number of
K
-rational torsion points is bounded by
N(g,D)
:

\#A(K)_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq N(g,D).

In the case where
A
is an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, Conjecture
1.1
was proved by Mazur
[
Maz77
,
Maz78
]
through a classification of torsion subgroups. For general number fields, the conjecture for elliptic curves was advanced by Kamienny
[
Kam92
]
and Kamienny–Mazur
[
KM95
]
, and was finally completed by Merel
[
Mer96
]
.

The higher-dimensional case remains widely open. Given that the moduli space of elliptic curves is one-dimensional, it is natural to investigate abelian schemes over curves. In
[
CT12
]
, Cadoret and Tamagawa proved the following result, which can be viewed as a weaker variant of the uniform boundedness conjecture.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
CT12
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
,
B
be a smooth curve over
k
, and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. For any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
and any integer
\ell
, there exists an integer
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N(\mathcal{A},B,k,k^{\prime},\ell)
such that for all
b\in B(k^{\prime})
,

\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\leq N,

where
\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{n}]
denotes the set of
\ell
-primary torsion points in
\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})
.

Remark 1.3
.

By the Mordell’s Conjecture (Faltings’ Theorem), if the genus
g(B)\geq 2
, the curve
B(k^{\prime})
contains only finitely many rational points. Thus
\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})\neq\varnothing
for only finitely many fibers. Consequently, Theorem
1.2
is interesting only when
g(B)=0
or
1
.

The proof by Cadoret and Tamagawa relies heavily on the arithmetic technique of Galois representations. It is therefore natural to ask whether a geometric approach to this problem exists. In this article, we provide a geometric proof of Theorem
1.2
by utilizing the theory of Betti foliations and equidistribution theorems.

1.1
Main Results

To provide a geometric proof of Theorem
1.2
, the primary ingredient is the following result:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
,
B
be a smooth curve over
k
, and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Let
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k(B)
be the function field of
B
and
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}_{K}
be the generic fiber. Suppose
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a generic and small sequence in
A(\overline{K})
, and let
C_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\overline{\{x_{n}\}}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
be the Zariski closure of
x_{n}
in
\mathcal{A}
. If the genus sequence
\big(g(C_{n})\big)_{n\geq 1}
satisfies

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{g(C_{n})}{\deg(x_{n})}=0,

then the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.

Remark 1.5
.

By the Northcott property, for a sequence of distinct points
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
with bounded height, the degree
\deg(x_{n})
must tend to infinity as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, Theorem
1.4
implies that if the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is non-isotrivial, then for any generic and small sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
A(\overline{K})
, the genus satisfies
g(C_{n})\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Here, we say a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
A(\overline{K})
is
generic and small
if every infinite subsequence of
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is Zariski dense in
A
, and if the Néron–Tate height satisfies
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})=0
. In this context,
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}
is the Néron–Tate height associated to an ample symmetric line bundle
L
on
A
and a big and nef adelic line bundle
\overline{H}
on
B
. The construction of these heights will be explained in Section
2.3
.

By combining Theorem
1.4
with the Mordell’s conjecture and Bogomolov’s conjecture, we prove the following result:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
,
B
be a smooth curve over
k
, and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Assume
\mathcal{A}\to B
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme. Let
P:B\to\mathcal{A}
be a section. For any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
and any integer
\ell
, there exists an integer
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N(\mathcal{A},P,k,k^{\prime},\ell)
such that for any
b\in B(k^{\prime})
,

\#\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime}):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\}\leq N.

Note that if
P
is a non-torsion section, the assumption that
\mathcal{A}\to B
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme is essential.

Example 1.7
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
k
with
\operatorname{rank}E(k)\geq 1
. Consider the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E\times_{k}E
and let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to E
be the projection onto the second factor. Let
P:E\to\mathcal{A}
be the diagonal section.

Since
\mathrm{rank\,}E(k)\geq 1
, for any
N>0
, we can find a point
b\in E(k)
of the form
b=\ell^{N}\cdot b_{0}
for some non-torsion element
b_{0}\in E(k)
. There are at least
N
distinct points
z\in E(k)
such that
\ell^{n}z=b
for some
n\geq 0
. Thus, the set in Theorem
1.6
cannot be bounded uniformly for all
b\in E(k)
.

If
P
is a torsion section, we may reduce to the case where
P
is the zero section. Due to the uniform boundedness of torsion points on fibers of isotrivial abelian schemes, we can prove Theorem
1.6
without the non-isotrivial subscheme assumption. This yields a new proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Our Theorem
1.4
implies a conjecture proposed by Cadoret and Tamagawa in
[
CT11
]
. For a positive integer
n
, let
A[n]^{\times}
denote the set of torsion points in
A(\overline{K})
of exact order
n
.

Conjecture 1.8
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
,
B
be a smooth curve over
k
, and
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. Assume
\mathcal{A}\to B
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme. Let
g(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\limits_{x\in A[n]^{\times}}g(C_{x})
, where
C_{x}
is the Zariski closure of
x
in
\mathcal{A}
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)=+\infty.

In
[
CT12
]
, Cadoret and Tamagawa proved
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g(\ell^{n})=+\infty
for any prime
\ell
. In
[
CT11
]
, they also prove conjecture under the assumption that either
g(B)\geq 1
or
\mathcal{A}\to B
has semistable reduction over all but possibly one point in
\overline{B}\setminus B
. In
[
EHK12
]
, Ellenberg, Hall, and Kowalski show that for an infinite sequence of finite étale covers of a smooth connected curve, the sequence of genera tends to infinity, provided that the associated topological fundamental groups satisfy certain combinatorial expansion properties. Using our geometric approach, we prove Conjecture
1.8
without these additional assumptions.

1.2
Ideas of proofs

The majority of this article is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.4
. Here we describe our ideas to prove Theorem
1.4
, which mainly uses the theory of Betti foliations and the arithmetic equidistribution theorem.

For simplicity, let us assume here that
k
is a number field. For any
b\in B(\mathbb{C})
, the Betti foliation induces a monodromy representation

\rho_{b}:\pi_{1}(B(\mathbb{C}),b)\to\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}).

For a loop
\gamma
based at
b
, the element
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
acts as an automorphism on the real Lie group
\mathcal{A}_{b}(\mathbb{C})
. Let
\overline{B}
be the smooth compactification of
B
, and let
s\in\overline{B}\setminus B
be a boundary point. By choosing a base point
b
near
s
and a simple loop
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(B(\mathbb{C}),b)
around
s
, the monodromy
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
characterizes the reduction of the abelian scheme. Specifically,
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
is trivial if and only if
\mathcal{A}
has good reduction at
s
. Furthermore, if we define
\mathrm{ord}(s)
as the order of
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
in
\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
\operatorname{ord}(s)
is finite if and only if
\mathcal{A}
has potentially good reduction at
s
.

Our proof proceeds in three main steps.

Step 1.
The existence of multi-sections
C_{n}
with small heights allows us to find sufficiently many points
z
on a fiber
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})_{b}
such that
\rho_{b}(\gamma^{m})(z)\approx z
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Then, by the arithmetic equidistribution theorem of Chen–Moriwaki
[
CM24
, Theorem F]
, the set of points
z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(\mathbb{C})
fixed by the action of
\rho_{b}(\gamma^{m})
has positive Haar measure, which implies that
\rho_{b}(\gamma^{m})
must be the identity map. Consequently, we obtain
\operatorname{ord}(s)\leq m
. If we bound the integer
m
from above, we may give an upper bound of
\mathrm{ord}(s)
.

Step 2.
We use a counting argument that derived from the Riemann–Hurwitz formula to bound the integer
m
in the previous step. Under the assumption that
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{g(C_{n})}{\deg(x_{n})}=0
, we show that
\mathrm{ord}(s)
is finite for all but possibly one boundary point
s\in\overline{B}\setminus B
. Furthermore, it satisfies the following equation:

\sum_{s\in\overline{B}\setminus B}\frac{1}{\operatorname{ord}(s)}=2g(\overline{B})-2+\#(\overline{B}\setminus B).

Step 3.
Using the equation above, we explicitly determine the possible values for the tuple
(\operatorname{ord}(s))_{s\in\overline{B}\setminus B}
. By taking a suitable base change, we reduce the problem to cases where
B
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbb{G}_{m},\mathbb{P}^{1}
, or an elliptic curve. A hyperbolicity argument then allows us to prove that the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.

1.3
Notations and Terminology

By a
variety
, we mean a separated, integral scheme of finite type over a base field. A
curve
is defined as a variety of dimension one.

By a
line bundle
on a scheme, we mean an invertible sheaf. For any line bundles
L,M
and integers
a,b
, the notation
aL-bM
denotes the tensor product
L^{\otimes a}\otimes M^{\otimes(-b)}
. For the theory of
adelic line bundles
, we refer to
[
YZ
]
.

An
abelian scheme

\mathcal{A}
over a scheme
B
is a proper, smooth group scheme over
B
whose geometric fibers are connected.

For a variety
X
over
\mathbb{Q}
, we denote
X(\mathbb{C})
the set of
\mathbb{C}
-points of
X\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{C}
, which admits the structure of a complex analytic space. If
X
is moreover smooth, then
X(\mathbb{C})
is a complex manifold. In particular, when
X
is a smooth projective curve,
X(\mathbb{C})
is a finite union of (connected) compact Riemann surfaces.

For a curve
C
over a field
k
of characteristic
0
, the
genus

g(C)
refers to the geometric genus of its smooth compactification
\widetilde{C}
. Specifically,
\widetilde{C}
is the projective model of the normalization of
C
, which is a smooth, projective curve.

(Fix an embedding
k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
. If
C
is not geometrically integral,
C\otimes_{k}\mathbb{C}
decomposes into irreducible components
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
, which are curves over
\mathbb{C}
. We have

g(C)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g(C_{i}),

which is compatible with our definition.)

For a Riemann surface
C
with boundary, its
genus
is defined as

g(C)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dfrac{2-b(C)-\chi(C)}{2},

where
b(C)
denotes number of boundary components of
C
and
\chi(C)
is the Euler characteristic of
C
. In particular, for a closed disk
D\subset\mathbb{C}
, we have
g(D)=0
.

For a point
x\in\mathbb{C}
and a positive real number
r
, we denote the open disk of radius
r
centered at
x
by

D(x,r)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-x|<r\}\subset\mathbb{C}.

For a point
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we denote the polydisk of radius
r
by

\mathbb{D}(x,r)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}D(x_{1},r)\times\cdots\times D(x_{n},r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Preliminaries

2.1
The moduli space of abelian varieties

Let
D=\mathrm{diag}(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{g})\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{Z})
be a diagonal matrix with
d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{g}
positive integers such that
d_{1}\mid d_{2}\mid\dots\mid d_{g}
. For an integer
N\geq 3
, let
\mathbb{A}_{g,D}(N)
be the moduli space of abelian varieties of dimension
g
with polarization type
D
and level-
N
-structure. For simplicity, we denote it by
\mathbb{A}_{g}
without ambiguity. More precisely, consider the Siegel upper half space defined by

\mathfrak{H}_{g}^{+}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{Z=X+\sqrt{-1}Y\in M_{g\times g}(\mathbb{C})\mid Z=Z^{\top},Y\text{ is positive definite}\}.

Then this gives a uniformization
u\colon\mathfrak{H}_{g}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g}
. It is known that
\mathbb{A}_{g}(\mathbb{C})
can be identified with the quotient
\Gamma\backslash\mathfrak{H}_{g}^{+}
for a suitable congruence subgroup
\Gamma
of
\mathrm{Sp}_{g}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We also know that
\mathbb{A}_{g}
is a fine moduli space, which admits a universal family
\pi\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g}
.

Let
B
be is a smooth quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
\pi_{B}\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g
, where
g\geq 1
is an integer. Up to taking a finite étale covering of
B^{\prime}\rightarrow B
and the base change
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}
, there is a modular map

Definition 2.1
.

An abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\to B
is said to be
isotrivial
if one of the following equivalent conditions holds:

(i)

The fibers
\mathcal{A}_{b}
are isomorphic to each other for all
b\in B(\mathbb{C})
;

(ii)

There exists a finite covering
B^{\prime}\to B
such that
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}
is a constant abelian scheme, namely
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}\simeq A\times B^{\prime}
for some abelian variety
A
over
\mathbb{C}
.

(iii)

The image of the modular map
\iota_{B}:B\to\mathbb{A}_{g}
induced by
\mathcal{A}\to B
is a point.

For some particular base
B
, the abelian scheme must be isotrivial.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
B=\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbb{G}_{m}
or an elliptic curve, then the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B
is isotrivial.

Proof.

When
B=\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbb{G}_{m}
or an elliptic curve, there is a holomorphic universal covering
p\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow B(\mathbb{C})
. The modular map
\iota_{B}\colon B\rightarrow\mathbb{A}_{g}
lifts to a holomorphic map
\widetilde{\iota_{B}}\colon\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathfrak{H}_{g}^{+}
. Since the Siegel upper half space is biholomorphic to a bounded domain, the map
\widetilde{\iota_{B}}
is constant, which implies the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B
is isotrivial.

When
B=\mathbb{P}^{1}
, for any
s\in B
,
U\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}B\setminus\{s\}\simeq\mathbb{A}^{1}
. So the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}_{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}\times_{B}U\to U
is isotrivial. Thus
\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.
∎

2.2
The Betti foliation

We assume
B
is a smooth quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
and
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B
is a holomorphic family of abelian varieties over
B
, which is a smooth holomorphic map endowed with a holomorphic section
e\colon B\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. Every fiber of
\pi
is an abelian variety with the identity point induced by
e
.

Let
b\in B
be a point and
U
be a connected and simply connected open neighborhood of
b
in
B
. Denote
\pi_{U}:\mathcal{A}_{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}\times_{B}U\rightarrow U
the base change of
\pi
. The
Betti map

\beta=\beta_{b,U}\colon\mathcal{A}_{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{b}

is a canonical real analytic map satisfying the following properties:

(1)

The composition
\mathcal{A}_{b}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U}\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathcal{A}_{b}
is the identity map.

(2)

For any point
b^{\prime}\in U
, the composition
\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{U}\xrightarrow{\beta}\mathcal{A}_{b}
is an isomorphism of real Lie groups.

(3)

The induced map

\widetilde{\beta}=(\beta,\pi)\colon\mathcal{A}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{b}\times U

is a real analytic diffeomorphism of manifolds.

(4)

For any
x\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
, the fiber
\beta^{-1}(\beta(x))
is a complex analytic subset of
\mathcal{A}_{U}
, which is biholomorphic to
U
.

The Betti map can be constructed as follows. Fix a basis of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
. Since
U
is simply-connected, we can extend it by continuity to all fibers above
U
. Then we get canonical isomorphisms
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}},\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
for all
b^{\prime}\in U
. The isomorphism in (2) can be defined by the composition

\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}\longrightarrow H_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}},\mathbb{R}\right)/H_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}},\mathbb{Z}\right)\longrightarrow H_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{R}\right)/H_{1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z}\right)\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{b},

which determines the Betti map
\beta_{b,U}
.

For a point
x\in\mathcal{A}_{U}
, the fiber
\mathcal{F}_{x,U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{b,U}^{-1}\left(\beta_{b,U}(x)\right)
is called the
local Betti leaf
at
x
over
U
. It is independent of the choice of
b\in U
, and the germ
\mathcal{F}_{x}
is independent of the choice of
U
. A
Betti leaf
is a connected subset
\mathcal{F}_{0}
of
\mathcal{A}
whose germ at
x\in\mathcal{F}_{0}
is
\mathcal{F}_{x}
. This forms a
Betti foliation
.

We have the following basic properties for the Betti foliation.

•

In general, the Betti map
\beta_{b,U}
is not holomorphic, but the local Betti leaf
\mathcal{F}_{x,U}
is a complex submanifold of
\mathcal{A}_{U}
.

•

The Betti leaf
\mathcal{F}
is transverse to the fibers of
\pi:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B
and the map
\pi|_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}\colon\mathcal{F}_{0}\to B
is a regular holomorphic covering map.

•

Any connected component of a torsion multi-section of
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}\to B
is a Betti leaf.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow B
be a loop in
B
with base point
b
, moves along
\gamma(t)
from
t=0
to
t=1
, a basis of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
transform to a second basis of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
, which gives an element of
\mathrm{Aut}\left(H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})\right)\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
. This induces a monodromy

\rho_{b}:\pi_{1}(B,b)\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}).

Note that the monodromy
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
also gives a linear transfromation of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{R})
which preserve the lattice
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})
, so it gives a linear diffeomorphism of
H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{R})/H_{1}(\mathcal{A}_{b},\mathbb{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{A}_{b}
. There is another way to describe this diffeomorphism. Let
x\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
be a point and
\mathcal{F}_{0}
be the Betti leaf containing
x
. The loop
\gamma
lifts to a unique path

\widetilde{\gamma}_{x}\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{F}_{0}

such that
\pi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{x}=\gamma
and
\widetilde{\gamma}_{x}(0)=x
. Then the map

\mathcal{A}_{b}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{b},\hskip 10.00002ptx\mapsto\widetilde{\gamma}_{x}(1)

is just the linear diffeomorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{b}
induced by
\rho_{b}(\gamma)
.

We have the following monodromy theorem.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
B
is a Riemann surface and
\pi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B

\overline{B}

s\in\overline{B}\setminus B

\pi\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow B

The Betti form

Here, we review the theory of Betti forms. These notions were originally introduced by Mok
[
Mok91
]
to study the Mordell–Weil groups of abelian varieties over complex function fields.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a line bundle on
\mathcal{A}
, for any
b\in B
, there exists a unique smooth
(1,1)
-form
\omega_{b}\in c_{1}(\mathcal{L}|_{\mathcal{A}_{b}})
on
\mathcal{A}_{b}
which is invariant under translations. The
Betti form

\omega=\omega(\mathcal{L})
can be constructed as follows.

Let
U
be a connected and simply connected open neighborhood of
b
and
\beta_{b,U}
be a Betti map. We define
\omega_{U}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{b,U}^{*}\omega_{b}
, which is a closed
(1,1)
-form on
\mathcal{A}_{U}
and independent of the choice of
b\in U
for fixed
U
. By gluing
\omega_{U}
for different
U
, we get the desired Betti form
\omega
. This form is uniquely determined by the generic fiber
\mathcal{L}_{\eta}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\eta}
, where
\eta
denotes the generic point of
B
.

The Betti form
\omega
is a real analytic closed
(1,1)
-form satisfying
[m]^{*}\omega=m^{2}\omega
for any integer
m
. For a point
x\in\mathcal{A}
with
\pi(x)=b
, let
\mathrm{pr}_{x}:T_{x}\mathcal{A}\to T_{x}\mathcal{A}_{b}
be the projection to the first factor in

T_{x}\mathcal{A}=T_{x}\mathcal{A}_{b}\oplus T_{x}\mathcal{\mathcal{F}},

where
\mathcal{F}
is the Betti leaf at
x
. By the construction of the Betti form, we have

\omega(v_{1},v_{2})=\omega_{b}(\mathrm{pr}_{x}(v_{1}),\mathrm{pr}_{x}(v_{2}))

for any
v_{1},v_{2}\in T_{x}\mathcal{A}
.

If
\mathcal{L}
is ample on
\mathcal{A}_{b}
for all
b\in B
, then
\omega_{b}
is positive and
\omega
is semi-positive. Then
\omega_{b}
induces a distance on
\mathcal{A}_{b}

\mathrm{dist}_{b}:\mathcal{A}_{b}\times\mathcal{A}_{b}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}.

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose
X
is a complex manifold and
\Omega
is a smooth semi-positive
(1,1)
-form on
X
. For a smooth path
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
, the
length
of
\gamma
with respect to
\Omega
is defined by

L_{\Omega}(\gamma)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{a}^{b}\sqrt{\Omega\left(\gamma^{\prime}(t),J\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right)}\mathrm{d}t\geq 0.

The following lemma will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.10
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{A}
with initial point
\widetilde{a}
and
\gamma=\pi(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1})
be its image in
B
.
Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the Betti leaf through
\widetilde{a}
.
Let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{F}
be a lift of
\gamma
to
\mathcal{F}
with initial point
\widetilde{a}
. Then we have

\mathrm{dist}_{\gamma(1)}\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}(1),\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}(1)\big)\leq L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}),

where
\omega
is the Betti form on
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Write
b=\gamma(1)
,
\widetilde{b}_{1}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}(1)
and
\widetilde{b}_{2}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}(1)
.
We first treat the case where
\gamma([0,1])
is contained in an open subset

U\subset B
over which a local Betti map
\beta_{b,U}:\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{b}
is defined.
Since
\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}([0,1])
lies entirely in the Betti leaf
\mathcal{F}
,
\beta_{b,U}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}(t))\equiv\widetilde{b}_{2}
. Let
\alpha\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\beta_{b,U}\circ\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}:[0,1]\to\mathcal{A}_{b}
.

For any point
b^{\prime}\in U
, the map
\beta_{b,U}
induces a canonical isomorphism
\beta_{b,U}^{b^{\prime}}:\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}\to\mathcal{A}_{U}\to\mathcal{A}_{b}
. Then for
x\in\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}
, the push-forward
(\beta_{b,U})_{*}
factors as

T_{x}\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{pr}_{x}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{x}\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\left(\beta_{b,U}^{b^{\prime}}\right)_{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{\beta_{b,U}(x)}\mathcal{A}_{b},

where
\mathrm{pr}_{x}:T_{x}\mathcal{A}\to T_{x}\mathcal{A}_{b^{\prime}}
is the projection. Since

(\beta_{b,U})_{*}\left(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\right)=\alpha^{\prime}(t),

we have

\omega\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t),J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)=\omega_{\gamma(t)}\left(\mathrm{pr}_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}(t)}\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big),\mathrm{pr}_{\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}(t)}\big(J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)\right)=\omega_{b}\big(\alpha^{\prime}(t),J\alpha^{\prime}(t)\big)

for
t\in[0,1]
.
Therefore

\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{b}_{2})\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\omega_{b}\big(\alpha^{\prime}(t),J\alpha^{\prime}(t)\big)}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\omega\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t),J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)}\mathrm{d}t.

For the general case, choose a subdivision
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\dots<t_{m}=1
such that
\gamma([t_{i},t_{i+1}])\subset U_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq m-1
, where each
U_{i}
admits a Betti map

\beta_{\gamma(t_{i+1}),U_{i}}
.

We induct on
m
. The case
m=1
has just been proved. Assume the claim holds for
m-1
, and let

c=\gamma(t_{m-1}),\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{c}_{1}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}(t_{m-1}),\hskip 10.00002pt\widetilde{c}_{2}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{2}(t_{m-1}).

The induction hypothesis gives

\mathrm{dist}_{c}(\widetilde{c}_{1},\widetilde{c}_{2})\leq\int_{0}^{t_{m-1}}\sqrt{\omega\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t),J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)}\mathrm{d}t.

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
be the Betti leaf through
\widetilde{c}_{1}
. There exists a lift

\widetilde{\gamma}_{3}:[t_{m-1},t_{m}]\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

of
\gamma|_{[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}
with initial point
\widetilde{\gamma}_{3}(t_{m-1})=\widetilde{c}_{1}
. Set
\widetilde{b}_{3}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{\gamma}_{3}(t_{m})
.
Then we have
\beta_{b,U_{m-1}}^{c}(\widetilde{c}_{1})=\widetilde{b}_{3}
and
\beta_{b,U_{m-1}}^{c}(\widetilde{c}_{2})=\widetilde{b}_{2}
.
By the construction of Betti form,

\mathrm{dist}_{c}(\widetilde{c}_{1},\widetilde{c}_{2})=\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{3},\widetilde{b}_{2}).

Applying the case
m=1
to
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}|_{[t_{m-1},t_{m}]}
yields

\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{b}_{3})\leq\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t_{m}}\sqrt{\omega\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t),J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)}\mathrm{d}t.

Summing the two inequalities gives

\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{b}_{2})\leq\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{1},\widetilde{b}_{3})+\mathrm{dist}_{b}(\widetilde{b}_{3},\widetilde{b}_{2})\leq\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\omega\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t),J\widetilde{\gamma}_{1}^{\prime}(t)\big)}\mathrm{d}t.

∎

2.3
The Néron–Tate height

Throughout this subsection, we follow the language and conventions of
[
YZ
]
. Let
B
be a smooth quasi-projective variety of dimension
d
defined over a number field
k
, and let
K=k(B)
be its function field. Let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme, with generic fiber
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}_{K}
. Fix a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{A}
which is relatively ample over
B
and symmetric, i.e.
[-1]^{*}\mathcal{L}\simeq\mathcal{L}
. Denote by
L\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{L}_{K}\in\mathrm{Pic}(A)
the generic fiber of
\mathcal{L}
. Let
\overline{\mathcal{L}}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(\mathcal{A}/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}}
be a nef adelic line bundle extending
\mathcal{L}
, and let
\overline{H}
be a big and nef adelic line bundle on
B
. For
x\in A(\overline{K})
, we define the height

h_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}^{\overline{H}}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}|_{x}\cdot\pi^{*}\overline{H}^{d}}{\deg(x)},\hskip 10.00002ptx\in A(\overline{K}).

Here we view
\overline{\mathcal{L}}
as an element of
\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(A/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef}}
and
\overline{H}
as an element of
\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(K/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef}}
. We define the Néron–Tate height by the Tate limit:

\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}}h^{\overline{H}}_{\overline{\mathcal{L}}}(2^{n}x),\hskip 10.00002ptx\in A(\overline{K}).

There is an alternative construction of the Néron–Tate height.
As shown in
[
YZ
, Theorem 6.1.3]
, there exists a nef adelic line bundle
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
on
\mathcal{A}
extending
\mathcal{L}
such that
[2]^{*}\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}\simeq 4\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
. The
(1,1)
-form
c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}})
on

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})
coincides with the Betti form
\omega(\mathcal{L})
.

Note that
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
can be viewed as a nef adelic line bundle on
A
extending
L
. For
x\in A(\overline{K})
, the vector-valued Néron–Tate height is defined by

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{L}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\langle\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}|_{x}\rangle}{\deg(x)}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(K/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}},

where
\langle\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}|_{x}\rangle
is the Deligne pairing in relative
dimension
0
. This construction is compatible with the height
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}
in the sense that

\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)=\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{L}(x)\cdot\overline{H}^{d},

see
[
YZ
, Chapter 5.3]
for details.

The Néron–Tate height satisfies the following properties.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
YZ
, Theorem 5.3.1]
)
.

Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
A
, and let
\overline{H}
be a big and nef adelic line bundle on
B
. Then:

(1)

(The Northcott property) For any real numbers
D,M\in\mathbb{R}
, the set

\{x\in A(\overline{K}):\deg(x)<D,\ \widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)<M\}

is finite.

(2)

For any integer
m
and
x\in A(\overline{K})
,
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(mx)=m^{2}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)
.

(3)

A point
x\in A(\overline{K})
is torsion if and only if
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)=0
.

(4)

For any big and nef adelic line bundles
\overline{H},\overline{H}^{\prime}
on
B
, there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that

c_{1}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)<\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H^{\prime}}}(x)<c_{2}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in A(\overline{K}).

We also consider the geometric counterpart of the Néron–Tate height. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
be the geometric part of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
, which is the image of
\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}
under the canonical composition

\widehat{\mathrm{Pic\,}}(\mathcal{A}/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Pic\,}}(A/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Pic\,}}(A/K)_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}}.

Similarly, let
\widetilde{H}
be the geometric part of
\overline{H}
. For a point
x\in A(\overline{K})
, we define
the geometric Néron–Tate height
as

\widehat{h}_{L,\mathrm{geom}}^{H}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}|_{x}\cdot\pi^{*}\widetilde{H}^{d-1}}{\deg(x)}.

As proved in
[
GV25
, Theorem B]
, the geometric Néron–Tate height can be interpreted as an integration

\widehat{h}_{L,\mathrm{geom}}^{H}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{\deg(x)}\int_{\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})}[\Delta_{x}(\mathbb{C})]\wedge c_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}})_{\infty}\wedge\pi^{*}c_{1}(\overline{H})_{\infty}^{d-1},

where
\Delta_{x}
is the Zariski closure of
x
in
\mathcal{A}
.

The geometric height is controlled by the arithmetic one:

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\overline{H}
be a big and nef adelic line bundle on
B
.
Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varepsilon\cdot\widehat{h}_{L,\mathrm{geom}}^{H}(x)\leq\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in A(\overline{K}).

Proof.

Let
\overline{B}
be a compactification of
B
and Let
\mathcal{Y}
be an arithmetic model of
\overline{B}
. For
\varepsilon>0
, denote by
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(B/\mathbb{Z}){{}_{\mathrm{nef}}}
the hermitian line bundle on
\mathcal{Y}
with underlying line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}}
and metric
\|1\|\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}e^{-\varepsilon}
.

By Yuan’s theorem
[
YZ
, Theorem 5.2.2]
,

\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}\big(\overline{H}-\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)\big)\geq\overline{H}^{d+1}-(d+1)\overline{H}^{d}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)=\overline{H}^{d+1}-\varepsilon(d+1)H^{d}.

For sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, the right-hand side is positive, hence
\widehat{\mathrm{vol}}\big(\overline{H}-\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)\big)>0
. Thus there exists an effective section
s
of
\overline{H}-\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)
with
\widehat{\mathrm{div}}(s)\geq 0
. It follows that

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{L}(x)\cdot\overline{H}^{d-1}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)\leq\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{L}(x)\cdot\overline{H}^{d}.

Since

\widehat{\mathfrak{h}}_{L}(x)\cdot\overline{H}^{d-1}\cdot\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)=\frac{\overline{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}|_{x}\cdot\pi^{*}\overline{H}^{d-1}\cdot\pi^{*}\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}(\varepsilon)}{\deg(x)}=\frac{\varepsilon}{\deg(x)}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathrm{can}}|_{x}\cdot\pi^{*}\widetilde{H}^{d-1}=\widehat{h}_{L,\mathrm{geom}}^{H}(x),

we obtain

\varepsilon\cdot\widehat{h}_{L,\mathrm{geom}}^{H}(x)\leq\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\forall x\in A(\overline{K}).

∎

2.4
The equidistribution theorem

Let
\pi:\mathcal{X}\to B
be a flat morphism of projective varieties over
\mathbb{Q}
with relative dimension
n
. Set
d\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\dim B
and
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{Q}(B)
.
Let
L
be a line bundle on
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{X}_{K}
, and let
\overline{L}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(X/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int},\mathbb{Q}}
be an adelic line bundle extending
L
, defined over
\mathcal{X}_{U}\to U
, where
U\subset B
is a Zariski open subscheme of
B
. Let
\overline{H}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(B/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int}}
be an adelic line bundle on
B
. We may define Moriwaki heights for points and subvarieties.

Definition 2.8
.

For a point
x\in X(\overline{K})
, the
Moriwaki height
of
x
with respect to
\overline{L}
and
\overline{H}
is defined by

h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\overline{L}|_{x}\cdot\overline{H}^{d}}{\deg(x)},

where the intersection number is taken in
\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(x/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int},\mathbb{Q}}
.

For a closed subvariety
Z\subset X
, the
Moriwaki height
of
Z
with respect to
\overline{L}
and
\overline{H}
is defined by

h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}(Z)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\big(\overline{L}|_{Z}\big)^{\dim Z+1}\cdot\overline{H}^{d}}{(\dim Z+1)\deg_{L}(Z/K)}.

Here the intersection number is taken in
\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(Z/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int},\mathbb{Q}}
and
\deg_{L}(Z/K)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big(L|_{Z}\big)^{\dim Z}
.

Definition 2.9
.

For an infinite sequence
(x_{m})_{m\geq 1}
in
X(\overline{K})
, we say that
(x_{m})_{m\geq 1}
is
h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}
-
small
if

h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}(x_{m})\rightarrow h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}(X)\text{ as }m\to\infty,

and is
generic
if any infinite subsequence of
(x_{m})_{m\geq 1}
is Zariski dense in
X
.

Remark 2.10
.

When
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is an abelian scheme and
\overline{L}
is the canonical adelic line bundle extending an ample and symmetric line bundle
L
, then the Moriwaki height
h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}:A(\overline{K})\to\mathbb{R}
coincides with the Néron–Tate height. Moreover, one has
h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}(A)=0
. In this case, a sequence
(x_{m})_{m\geq 1}
is
h_{\overline{L}}^{\overline{H}}
-small if and only if
\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{m})=0
. We will simply call it
small
if there is no ambiguity.

For a point
v\in U(\mathbb{C})
, we say that
v
a
fully transcendental closed point
with respect to
U/\mathbb{Q}
if the image of

\mathrm{Spec\,}\mathbb{C}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle v}}{{\longrightarrow}}U_{\mathbb{C}}\to U

is the generic point of
U
. Such a fully transcendental point
v\in U(\mathbb{C})
determines an embedding
\sigma_{v}:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, and we have a canonical identification
X_{\sigma_{v}}(\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
.

For each point
x\in X(\overline{K})
, let
\Delta_{x}\subset\mathcal{X}_{U}
be the Zariski closure of the image of
x
. Its analytification
\Delta_{x_{m}}(\mathbb{C})
defines a Dirac current
[\Delta_{x_{m}}]
on
\mathcal{X}_{U}(\mathbb{C})
. Fix a fully transcendental point
v\in U(\mathbb{C})
, the Galois orbit of
x
is

O_{v}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)\cdot x\subset X(\overline{K})\subset X_{\sigma_{v}}(\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{C})_{v}.

Then the slice of the current
[\Delta_{x_{m}}]
along the fiber over
v
is the discrete measure

[\Delta_{x_{m}}]_{v}=\sum_{z\in O_{v}(x)}\delta_{z},

where
\delta_{z}
denotes the Dirac measure at
z\in X_{\sigma_{v}}(\mathbb{C})
.

The following is a special case of the equidistribution theorem of Chen–Moriwaki:

Theorem 2.11

(
[
CM24
]
)
.

With notations as above. Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
, and let
\overline{L}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(X/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int},\mathbb{Q}}
be a nef adelic line bundle extending
L
, defined over
\mathcal{X}_{U}\to U
. Let
\overline{H}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(K/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{int}}
be a big and nef adelic line bundle on
B
. Let
(x_{m})_{m\geq 1}
be a generic and small sequence in
X(\overline{K})
. Then for the archimedean place of
\mathbb{Q}
, there is a weak convergence

\frac{1}{\deg(x_{m})}[\Delta_{x_{m}}]\wedge c_{1}(\pi^{*}\overline{H})^{d}_{\infty}\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\deg_{L}(X/K)}c_{1}(\overline{L})^{n}_{\infty}\wedge c_{1}(\pi^{*}\overline{H})^{d}_{\infty}

of measures on
\mathcal{X}_{U}(\mathbb{C})
.

Remark 2.12
.

The equidistribution theorem in this relative setting was first proved in
[
Mor00
, Theorem 6.1]
under an additional hypothesis called the
Moriwaki condition
. A similar result for quasi-projective varieties was proved by Yuan–Zhang in
[
YZ
, Theorem 5.4.6]
. Chen–Moriwaki removed the Moriwaki condition in
[
CM24
, Theorem F]
, proving a more general form of the theorem in the setting of adelic curves. In this article, we only require the above special case, corresponding to a polarized adelic structure in the sense of Chen–Moriwaki.

Other lemmas

In this subsection, we provide several technical lemmas on Riemann surfaces that are required for the proofs in Section

. Readers may skip the proofs in this subsection at a first reading.

The following result is a length-area inequality, which plays a key role in the proof of Proposition
3.6
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
D=D(0,2)\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open disk, and let
Z\subset D
be a finite set. Suppose that
\omega
is a smooth semi-positive
(1,1)
-form on
D\setminus Z
. For
\rho\in(0,2)
, define the circle
\gamma_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\partial D(0,\rho)
, and denote
E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{\rho\in(0,2):\gamma_{\rho}\cap Z\neq\varnothing\}
. Then, we have the following inequality:

\int_{D\setminus Z}\omega\geq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{(1,2)\setminus E}L_{\omega}(\gamma_{\rho})^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho.

Proof.

Suppose

\omega=\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda(z)}{2}\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}

on
D\setminus Z
and denote
S(\rho)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\int_{D(0,\rho)\setminus Z}\omega
for
\rho\in(0,2)
. Then

\displaystyle S(\rho)=

\displaystyle\int_{D(0,\rho)\setminus Z}\omega=\int_{D(0,\rho)\setminus Z}\frac{\mathrm{i}\lambda(z)}{2}\mathrm{d}z\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D(0,\rho)\setminus E}\int_{0}^{2\pi}r\lambda(re^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}r.

On the other hand, for
\rho\not\in E
, we have

L_{\omega}(\gamma_{\rho})=\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{\lambda(\rho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}\mathrm{d}\theta,

and

S^{\prime}(\rho)=\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta.

By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

2\pi\rho S^{\prime}(\rho)=2\pi\rho^{2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\lambda(\rho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\mathrm{d}\theta\geq\left(\rho\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sqrt{\lambda(\rho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})}\mathrm{d}\theta\right)^{2}=L_{\omega}(\gamma_{\rho})^{2}.

Hence

\int_{D\setminus Z}\omega=S(2)\geq\int_{(1,2)\setminus E}S^{\prime}(\rho)\mathrm{d}\rho\geq\int_{(1,2)\setminus E}\frac{L_{\omega}(\gamma_{\rho})^{2}}{2\pi\rho}\mathrm{d}r\geq\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{(1,2)\setminus E}L_{\omega}(\gamma_{\rho})^{2}\mathrm{d}\rho.

∎

The following lemma provides an inequality comparing the lengths of a path and its lifts, which will be used in the proof of Proposition
3.10
.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a finite covering of Riemann surfaces of degree
d
, and
\omega
be a smooth semi-positive
(1,1)
-form on
X
.
For a path
\gamma:[0,1]\to Y
, let
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\gamma}_{d}:[0,1]\to X

be the distinct lifts of
\gamma
. Then

L_{f_{*}\omega}(\gamma)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}).

Proof.

For any
y\in Y
, there exists a neighborhood
U\subset Y
such that

f^{-1}(U)=\coprod\limits_{j=1}^{d}V_{j}
and

f|_{V_{j}}:V_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\rightarrow}}U
is biholomorphic. Let
\sigma_{j}:U\to V_{j}
be the inverse of
f|_{V_{j}}
. Then on
U
we have

Assume first that

.

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\lambda(\gamma(t))}\,|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}(\gamma(t))}\,|\gamma^{\prime}(t)|\,\mathrm{d}t

The following lemma, which is a corollary of the Riemann–Hurwitz formula, is essential for the proof of Lemma
3.14

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
p:X\to Y
be a surjective morphism of compact Riemann surfaces of degree
d
. Let
D_{1},\dots,D_{n}\subset Y
be pairwise disjoint closed disks. Assume that the branch locus of
p
does not intersect the union of the boundaries
\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial D_{i}
. Let
r_{i}
denote the number of connected components of
f^{-1}\big(\partial D_{i}\big)
. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\geq d\big(2g(Y)-2+n\big)-\big(2g(X)-2\big).

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i\leq n
. Let the preimage
p^{-1}(D_{i})
decompose into connected components
\mathcal{K}_{i,1}\sqcup\dots\sqcup\mathcal{K}_{i,m_{i}}
. For each component
\mathcal{K}_{i,j}
, let
r_{i,j}
denote the number of connected components of its boundary
\partial\mathcal{K}_{i,j}
. Note that
\partial\mathcal{K}_{i,j}
covers
\partial D_{i}
, and we have the partition
r_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m_{i}}r_{i,j}
.

We apply the Riemann–Hurwitz formula to the proper holomorphic map
\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\to D_{i}
. Since
D_{i}
is a disk, its Euler characteristic is
1
. Thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i,j}}(e_{x}-1)=

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\to D_{i})\cdot\chi(D_{i})-\chi(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\deg(\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\to D_{i})-\big(2-2g(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})-r_{i,j}\big).

Here
e_{x}
is a ramification index of
p
at
x
. Since
g(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\geq 0
and
r_{i,j}\geq 1
, we have
2-2g(\mathcal{K}_{i,j})\leq 2\leq 2r_{i,j}
, which implies

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{i,j}}(e_{x}-1)\geq\deg(\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\to D_{i})-r_{i,j}.

Summing over all components
j=1,\dots,m_{i}
, we get

\sum_{x\in p^{-1}(D_{i})}(e_{x}-1)\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\deg(\mathcal{K}_{i,j}\to D_{i})-\sum_{j=1}^{m_{i}}r_{i,j}=d-r_{i}.

Finally, applying the global Riemann–Hurwitz formula to
p:X\to Y
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2g(X)-2

\displaystyle=d(2g(Y)-2)+\sum_{x\in X}(e_{x}-1)

\displaystyle\geq d(2g(Y)-2)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{x\in p^{-1}(D_{i})}(e_{x}-1)

\displaystyle\geq d(2g(Y)-2)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d-r_{i})

\displaystyle=d(2g(Y)-2+n)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}.

The desired inequality follows immediately.
∎

Remark 2.16

is a finite disjoint union of compact Riemann surfaces, by summing up the inequality for each

, the same inequality still holds.

and
n\geq 1
. Denote

I_{n}^{(i)}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{d_{n}}\int_{(U_{i}\setminus Z_{n})_{v}}(\omega_{n})_{v},

it follows that

\int_{W_{0}}I_{n}^{(i)}(v)\mathrm{d}\mu_{W}<\frac{\mu_{W}(W_{0})}{2^{4n}N}

for
1\leq i\leq N
and
n\geq 1
.

Define

E_{n}^{(i)}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{v\in W_{0}:I_{n}^{(i)}(v)\geq\frac{1}{2^{3n}}\right\}.

Then
\mu_{W}\big(E_{n}^{(i)}\big)<\dfrac{\mu_{W}(W_{0})}{2^{n}N}
, and consequently

\mu_{W}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}^{(i)}\right)<\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right)\mu_{W}(W_{0})=\mu_{W}(W_{0}).

Set
W_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W_{0}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{N}\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}^{(i)}
. Then
\mu_{W}(W_{1})>0
, and for all
v\in W_{1}
,
1\leq i\leq N
and
n\geq 1
,

I_{n}^{(i)}(v)=\frac{1}{d_{n}}\int_{(U_{i}\setminus Z_{n})_{v}}(\omega_{n})_{v}<\frac{1}{2^{3n}}.

Now fix
v\in W_{1}
and
1\leq i\leq N
. On
(U_{i})_{v}\simeq D(0,3)
, we write each point
b\in(U_{i})_{v}
in local coordinates as
(z,v)
with
z\in D(0,3)
. Since
L_{\omega_{n}}(\gamma_{b})
depends only on
|z|
, for
r\in[1,2]
, we set

\ell_{n}^{(i)}(r,v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\begin{cases}L_{\omega_{n}}(\gamma_{b_{r}}),\quad&b_{r}=(r,v),\gamma_{b_{r}}\cap(Z_{n})_{v}=\varnothing\\
+\infty,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
2.13
,

\frac{1}{d_{n}}\int_{1}^{2}\ell_{n}^{(i)}(r,v)^{2}\mathrm{d}r\leq\frac{1}{d_{n}}\int_{(U_{i}\setminus Z_{n})_{v}}(\omega_{n})_{v}<\frac{1}{2^{3n}}.

Define

F_{n}^{(i)}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{r\in[1,2]:\frac{\ell_{n}^{(i)}(r,v)^{2}}{d_{n}}\geq\frac{1}{2^{n}}\right\}.

Then
\mu_{[1,2]}\big(F_{n}^{(i)}(v)\big)<\dfrac{1}{4^{n}}
, where
\mu_{[1,2]}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
[1,2]
. It follows that

\mu_{[1,2]}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(i)}(v)\right)<\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{n}}\right)=\frac{1}{3}.

Now set

T^{(i)}=\left\{b=(z,v)\in U_{i}:1\leq|z|\leq 2,v\in W_{1},\gamma_{b}\cap Z_{n}=\varnothing\text{ and }\frac{1}{d_{n}}L_{\omega_{n}}(\gamma_{b})^{2}<\frac{1}{2^{n}}\text{ for all }n\geq 1\right\}

For

and
b=(z,v)\in(U_{i})_{v}
with
1\leq|z|\leq 2
, if
|z|\notin F_{n}^{(i)}
for all
n\geq 1
, then
b\in T^{(i)}_{v}
. Viewing
T^{(i)}_{v}
as a subset of
D(0,3)
, we compute

\displaystyle\mu_{(U_{i})_{v}}\big(T^{(i)}_{v}\big)=

\displaystyle\int_{T^{(i)}_{v}}1\mathrm{d}z\geq\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{1}^{2}1_{\{r\notin F_{n}^{(i)}(v),\forall n\geq 1\}}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\pi\left(1-\mu_{[1,2]}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}F_{n}^{(i)}(v)\right)\right)=\frac{4\pi}{3}>1.

In particular,
\mu_{U_{i}}\big(T^{(i)}\big)>0
.
∎

Finiteness of orders

Definition 3.7
.

For each
1\leq i\leq N
, take a point
b\in U_{i}\setminus V_{i}^{o}(\mathbb{C})
and consider the loop
\gamma_{b}\in\pi_{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}),b)
. The
order
of
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
at
s_{i}
, denoted
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\{\infty\}
, is defined as the order of the element
\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b})\in\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
, where

\rho_{b}:\pi_{1}(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C}),b)\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})

is the monodromy induced by the Betti foliation of
\widetilde{\pi}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathcal{B}
.

The
reduction type
of the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is the
N
-tuple
(\mathrm{ord}(s_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq N}
.

Remark 3.8
.

For different choices of
b\in U_{i}
, the monodromy element
\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b})
are conjugate in
\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
. Hence
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})
is well-defined.

Remark 3.9
.

By Proposition
2.3
,
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=1
if and only if the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\to B
has good reduction at
s_{i}
, and
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})<\infty
if and only if the
\mathcal{A}\to B
has potential good reduction at
s_{i}
.

For a point
b\in U_{i}\setminus V_{i}^{o}(\mathbb{C})
with local coordinate
(z,v)
, assume that
\gamma_{b}\cap Z_{n}=\varnothing
. Then the preimage

\pi_{n}^{-1}(\gamma_{b})\subset\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

is a disjoint union of finitely many loops, each of which is a finite covering of
\gamma_{b}
. Let
r_{n}(b)
denote the number of connected components of this preimage.
For
v\in W_{1}
, define

r_{n}^{(i)}(v)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\left\{r_{n}(b):\;b\in(U_{i})_{v},\ \gamma_{b}\cap Z_{n}=\varnothing\right\}.

For a point
x\in\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})
with
b\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{\pi}(x)\in U_{i}\setminus V_{i}^{o}(\mathbb{C})
, , and integer
q>0
, denote

\mathrm{Dist}(x;q)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min_{1\leq m\leq q}\left\{\mathrm{dist}_{b}\big(x,\rho_{b}\big(\gamma_{b}^{m}\big)(x)\big)\right\},

where
\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b}^{m})
is regarded as a linear action on the fiber
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})_{b}
. The function
\mathrm{Dist}(x;q)
is continuous in the first variable.

Proposition 3.10
.

With the notations as above, for
n\geq 1
and a point
v\in W_{1}
, let
q\geq 2
be an integer and
\alpha>0
such that
\dfrac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}<q-\alpha
. Then for every point
b\in T^{(i)}_{v}
, there exist at least
\dfrac{\alpha d_{n}}{2q^{2}}
points
x\in\pi_{n}^{-1}(b)\subset\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
satisfying

\mathrm{Dist}(x;q-1)<\frac{2q^{2}}{\alpha 2^{n/2}}.

Proof.

Since
b\in T^{(i)}_{v}
, we have
\gamma_{b}\cap Z_{n}=\varnothing
and
\dfrac{1}{d_{n}}L_{\omega_{n}}(\gamma_{b})^{2}<\dfrac{1}{2^{n}}
for all
n\geq 1
. The fiber
\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{b}=\pi_{n}^{-1}(b)
consists of exactly
d_{n}
points. The preimage
\pi_{n}^{-1}(\gamma_{b})\subset\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
has
r_{n}(b)
connected components, denoted by
\widetilde{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\gamma}_{r_{n}(b)}
. Each
\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}
is a finite covering of
\gamma_{b}
of degree
q_{j}
, and hence contains exactly
q_{j}
points of
\pi_{n}^{-1}(b)
. Thus
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r_{n}(b)}q_{j}=d_{n}.

Define
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{1\leq j\leq r_{n}(b):q_{j}\leq q-1\}.
Then

d_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}(b)}q_{j}\geq|S|+q(r_{n}(b)-|S|)=qr_{n}(b)-(q-1)|S|.

Therefore,

\frac{|S|}{d_{n}}\geq\frac{qr_{n}(b)}{(q-1)d_{n}}-\frac{1}{q-1}\geq\frac{qr_{n}^{(i)}(v)}{(q-1)d_{n}}-\frac{1}{q-1}>\frac{q}{(q-1)(q-\alpha)}-\frac{1}{q-1}\geq\frac{\alpha}{q^{2}}.

By Lemma
2.14
,

\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{j\in S}L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})\leq\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha d_{n}}\sum_{j=1}^{r_{n}(b)}L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})\leq\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha\sqrt{d_{n}}}L_{\omega_{n}}(\gamma_{b})<\frac{q^{2}}{\alpha 2^{n/2}}.

Let

S^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{j\in S:L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})\leq\frac{2q^{2}}{\alpha 2^{n/2}}\right\}.

Then
|S^{\prime}|\geq\dfrac{1}{2}|S|
. By Lemma
2.5
, for all
x\in\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}\cap\pi_{n}^{-1}(b)
with
j\in S^{\prime}
,

\mathrm{dist}_{b}\big(x,\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b}^{q_{j}})(x)\big)\leq L_{\omega}(\widetilde{\gamma}_{j})\leq\frac{2q^{2}}{\alpha 2^{n/2}}.

Since
q_{j}\leq q-1
for all
j\in S^{\prime}
, it follows that

\mathrm{Dist}(x;q-1)=\min_{1\leq m\leq q}\left\{\mathrm{dist}_{b}\big(x,\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b}^{m})(x)\big)\right\}\leq\frac{2q^{2}}{\alpha 2^{n/2}}.

Finally,

\sum_{j\in S^{\prime}}q_{j}\geq|S^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|S|\geq\frac{\alpha d_{n}}{2q^{2}}.

Thus at least
\dfrac{\alpha d_{n}}{2q^{2}}
points in
\pi_{n}^{-1}(b)
satisfy the claimed inequality.
∎

The following proposition is crucial role in our proof. Its proof relies primarily on the equidistribution theorem.

Proposition 3.11
.

With the notations as above, fix an index
i\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. Assume there exist an integer
q>0
,
and a constant
\alpha>0
, such that the set

W(n,i)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{v\in W_{1}:\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}<q-\alpha\right\}\subset W_{1}

satisfies

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\big)>0

Then
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})\leq q-1
.

Proof.

Since
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\big)>0
, we may
choose a closed polydisk
\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\subset W
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\big)>0.

Up to replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence, we may assume that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\big)>\varepsilon\cdot\mu_{U_{i}}\big(U_{i}\big)
for all
n\geq 1
.

Step 1.
Consider the compact subset

\mathcal{K}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big\{b=(z,v)\in U_{i}:1\leq|z|\leq 2,v\in\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\big\}\subset U_{i}.

By the construction of
T^{(i)}
, for all
v\in W_{1}\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}
, we have
T^{(i)}_{v}\subset\mathcal{K}
. Define the set

U(n,i)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{b\in T^{(i)}:\pi_{V}(b)\in W(n,i)\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\right\}\subset\mathcal{K}.

Note that for all
v\in W_{1}
,
\mu_{(U_{i})_{v}}\big(T_{v}^{(i)}\big)>1
. We obtain the lower bound

\mu_{U_{i}}\big(U(n,i)\big)\geq\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\cap\overline{\mathbb{D}(v_{0},t)}\big)\cdot 1>\varepsilon\cdot\mu_{U_{i}}\big(U_{i}\big)

for all
n\geq 1
.

Step 2.
Let
H_{1}
be an ample line bundle on
\overline{\mathcal{B}}
. By Calabi–Yau theorem
[
Yau78
]
, there exists a smooth hermitian metric
\|\cdot\|
on
H_{1}
such that the curvature form
\Omega\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}c_{1}(H_{1},\|\cdot\|)^{e+1}
is positive and satisfies:

(i)

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C})}\Omega=\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})
;

(ii)

\int_{U_{i}}\Omega>\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C})}\Omega
;

(iii)

\Omega
is proportional to the standard Euclidean volume form on
U_{i}
.

So
H_{1}
extends to a big and nef adelic line bundle
\overline{H}_{1}\in\widehat{\mathrm{Pic}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}}/\mathbb{Z})_{\mathrm{nef},\mathbb{Q}}
with
c_{1}(\overline{H}_{1})_{\infty}^{e+1}=\Omega
.

Define the compact set
E_{n}\subset\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathcal{K}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\widetilde{\pi}^{-1}(\mathcal{K})
by

E_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{z\in\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C}):\pi_{n}(z)\in\mathcal{K},\ \mathrm{Dist}(z;q-1)\leq\frac{2q^{2}}{2^{n/2}}\right\}.

Consider the probability measure on
\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})
defined by

\mu_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{d_{n}\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})}[\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})]\wedge c_{1}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*}\overline{H}_{1})^{e+1}_{\infty}.

For any
b\in U(n,i)
, Proposition
3.10
implies that the fiber
E_{n,b}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E_{n}\cap\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})_{b}
has cardinality at least
\dfrac{\alpha d_{n}}{2q^{2}}
. Then we have

\mu_{n}(E_{n})=\frac{1}{\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})}\int_{\mathcal{K}}\frac{|E_{n,b}|}{d_{n}}\Omega\geq\frac{\alpha}{2q^{2}\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})}\int_{U(n,i)}\Omega.

Since
\Omega
is proportional to the standard Euclidean volume form on
U_{i}
,

\int_{U(n,i)}\Omega=\frac{\mu_{U_{i}}\big(U(n,i)\big)}{\mu_{U_{i}}\big(U_{i}\big)}\int_{U_{i}}\Omega>\frac{\varepsilon\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})}{2}.

It follows that
\mu_{n}(E_{n})\geq\dfrac{\alpha\varepsilon}{4q^{2}}
for all sufficiently large
n
.

Step 3.
Since
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})\to 0
, Proposition
2.6
(4) implies
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}_{1}}(x_{n})=0
. By the equidistribution Theorem
2.11
, as

, the measures
\mu_{n}
converge weakly to

\mu_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{1}{\deg_{H_{1}}(\overline{\mathcal{B}})\deg_{L}(A)}\omega^{g}\wedge c_{1}(\widetilde{\pi}^{*}\overline{H}_{1})^{e+1}_{\infty}.

Consider the compact space
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathcal{K}}
. Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the compact sets
E_{n}
converge to a limit set
E_{\infty}\subset\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathcal{K}}
with respect to the Hausdorff metric. The limit set
E_{\infty}
is also a compact set in
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathcal{K}}
. For any
\delta>0
, choose a closed subset
F_{\delta}\subset\mathscr{A}(\mathbb{C})_{\mathcal{K}}
such that
E_{\infty}
is contained in the interior of
F_{\delta}

\mu_{\infty}(E_{\infty})>\mu_{\infty}(F_{\delta})-\delta.

For
n
sufficiently large,

. By Portmanteau Theorem,

\dfrac{\alpha\varepsilon}{4q^{2}}\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(E_{n})\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(F_{\delta})\leq\mu_{\infty}(F_{\delta})\leq\mu_{\infty}(E_{\infty})+\delta.

, we deduce that
\mu_{\infty}(E_{\infty})\geq\dfrac{\alpha\varepsilon}{4q^{2}}>0
.

For
b\in\mathcal{K}
, denote

. Consider the measure

.

Let
z\in E_{\infty}

Note that

. Since
\nu_{b}(E_{\infty,b})>0
, there exists an integer

such that the set of fixed point

\left\{z\in E_{\infty,b}:\rho_{b}\big(\gamma_{b}^{m_{0}}\big)(z)=z\right\}

. Since

is a linear action on

\rho_{b}\big(\gamma_{b}^{m_{0}}\big)(z)=z
for all

, Thus
\rho_{b}\big(\gamma_{b}^{m_{0}}\big)=\mathrm{id}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z})
and we obtain
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=m_{0}\leq q-1

Corollary 3.12
.

Assume that
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})<\infty
. Let
\alpha>0
be a real constant and let
W_{1}^{\prime}\subset W_{1}
be a Borel subset with positive Lebesgue measure. Then, up to replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence, there exists a Borel subset
W_{1}^{\prime\prime}\subset W_{1}^{\prime}
with
\mu_{W}(W_{1}^{\prime\prime})>0
such that

\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}\geq\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\alpha

for all
v\in W_{1}^{\prime\prime}
and all

.

Proof.

Since
\dfrac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}\geq 1
always holds, the case
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=1
is trivial. We assume
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})\geq 2
.

Consider the set

W(n,i)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{v\in W_{1}:\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}<\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\alpha\right\}\subset W_{1}.

If
\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\big)>0
, then by Proposition
3.11
,
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})\leq\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-1
, contradiction!
Thus we have
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\big)=0
.

Up to replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence, we may assume

\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i)\big)<\frac{1}{2^{n}}\mu_{W}(W_{1}^{\prime}).

Take
W_{1}^{\prime\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}W_{1}^{\prime}\setminus\bigcup\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}W(n,i)

By definition, for all

\dfrac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}\geq\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\alpha
.
∎

We first prove the following inequality, where we do not need additional conditions on the genus

.

Proposition 3.13

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}\leq 2g(B)-2+N.

. Choose a torsion point

.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{0}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\overline{\{a\}}^{\mathrm{Zar}}\subset\mathscr{A}

\mathcal{B}
defined by

. Let
X
be the compactification of the fiber
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
, and let
Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
. The projection induces a branched covering
p:X\to Y
of degree
d
. Suppose
V_{i}\cap\overline{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C})_{v}=\{s_{i,v}\}
,
1\leq i\leq N
. Then
s_{1,v},\dots,s_{N,v}\in Y
are only branced points of
p
.

Claim.

\#p^{-1}(s_{i,v})\geq\dfrac{d}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}
for all
1\leq i\leq N

Proof of Claim:
The case
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=\infty
is trivial. Assume
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})<\infty
. Let
b\in(U_{i})_{v}
and
\gamma_{b}
be a small loop around
s_{i,v}
. The set
p^{-1}(s_{i,v})
is in one-to-one correspondence with the connected components of the preimage
\pi_{0}^{-1}(\gamma_{b})
, say
\{\widetilde{\gamma}_{1},\dots,\widetilde{\gamma}_{r}\}
, where
r=\#p^{-1}(s_{i,v})
. Each component
\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}
covers
\gamma_{b}
with degree
q_{j}
, so
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}q_{j}=d
. Since
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a torsion multi-section,
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mathbb{C})
is contained in finitely many Betti leaves. Then each
\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}
is contained in a Betti leaf.
By definition of the local monodromy, for any
z\in\widetilde{\gamma}_{j}
with
\pi_{0}(z)=b
, we have
\rho_{b}(\gamma_{b}^{q_{j}})(z)=z
. This implies that the
q_{j}
must divide the local monodromy order
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})
. It follows that

d=\sum_{j=1}^{r}q_{j}\leq r\cdot\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=\#p^{-1}(s_{i,v})\cdot\mathrm{ord}(s_{i}),

which proves the claim.∎

Applying Riemann–Hurwitz formula to
p:X\to Y
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2g(X)-2

\displaystyle=d(2g(Y)-2)+\sum_{y\in Y}\sum_{z\in p^{-1}(y)}(e_{z}-1)

\displaystyle=d(2g(Y)-2)+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{z\in p^{-1}(s_{i,v})}(e_{z}-1)

\displaystyle=d(2g(B)-2+N)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\#p^{-1}(s_{i,v})

\displaystyle\leq d(2g(B)-2+N)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{d}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}

Hence

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}\leq 2g(B)-2+N+\frac{2-2g(X)}{d}<2g(B)-2+N+\varepsilon<\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})},

contradiction.
∎

Now suppose
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})<\infty
for
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
for
N_{1}\leq N
and
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=\infty
for
N_{1}+1\leq i\leq N
.

Lemma 3.14
.

With the notations as above, assume

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}<2g(B)-2+N

and
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{g(C_{n})}{d_{n}}=0
. Then, up to replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence,
there exists a positive number
\lambda>0
and a Borel subset
W_{2}\subset W_{1}
with
\mu_{W}(W_{2})>0
such that

\sum\limits_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N}r_{n}^{(i)}(v)\geq\dfrac{\lambda d_{n}}{2}

for all
v\in W_{2}
and
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

We choose small constants
\varepsilon,\lambda>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\varepsilon}<2g(B)-2+N-\lambda.

By Corollary
3.12
, up to replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence, there exists a Borel subset
W_{2}\subset W_{1}
with
\mu_{W}(W_{2})>0
such that

\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}\geq\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\varepsilon

for all
v\in W_{2}
,
n\geq 1
, and
1\leq i\leq N_{1}
.
Additionally, we may assume that
\dfrac{2g(C_{n})-2}{d_{n}}<\dfrac{\lambda}{2}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Fix
v\in W_{2}
. Let
\widetilde{X}
denote the compactification of the normalization of
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
. The morphism
(\pi_{n})_{v}:\mathcal{C}_{n}(\mathbb{C})_{v}\to\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
induces a morphism
p:\widetilde{X}\to Y\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\overline{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{C})_{v}
.

For each
1\leq i\leq N
, choose a point
b_{i}\in(U_{i})_{v}
such that the loop
\gamma_{b_{i}}
avoids the branch locus and the preimage
\pi_{n}^{-1}(\gamma_{b_{i}})
consists of
r_{n}^{(i)}(v)
connected components. Consequently,
p^{-1}(\gamma_{b_{i}})
also has
r_{n}^{(i)}(v)
connected components. Applying Lemma
2.15
to
p
, we obtain

\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{n}^{(i)}(v)\geq d_{n}(2g(B)-2+N)-(2g(C_{n})-2).

(It should be noticed that
\widetilde{X}
might be disconnected. So we have to apply Lemma
2.15
on each connected component of
\widetilde{X}
and add up all inequalities.)

It follows that

\displaystyle 2g(B)-2+N-\lambda>

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})-\varepsilon}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}\frac{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}{d_{n}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2g(B)-2+N-\frac{2g(C_{n})-2}{d_{n}}-\sum_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N}\frac{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}{d_{n}}.

Thus

\sum\limits_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N}r_{n}^{(i)}(v)\geq\lambda d_{n}-(2g(C_{n})-2)>\frac{\lambda d_{n}}{2}

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

With the notations as above, if the genus sequence
\big(g(C_{n})\big)_{n\geq 1}
satisfies
\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{g(C_{n})}{d_{n}}=0
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}=2g(B)-2+N.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.13
, it suffices to prove

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}\geq 2g(B)-2+N.

Suppose the inequality does not hold. Then, by Lemma
3.14
, there exists a positive number
\lambda>0
and a Borel subset
W_{2}\subset W_{1}
with
\mu_{W}(W_{2})>0
such that

\sum\limits_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N}r_{n}^{(i)}(v)\geq\dfrac{\lambda d_{n}}{2}

for all
v\in W_{2}
and
n\geq 1
. Note that this forces
N_{1}<N
.

Choose an integer
q
sufficiently large such that
q>\dfrac{2(N-N_{1})}{\lambda}+1
. Then for all
v\in W_{2}
and
n\geq 1
,

\min_{N_{1}+1\leq i\leq N}\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}\leq\frac{2(N-N_{1})}{\lambda}<q-1

Define the sets

W(n,i)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\{v\in W_{1}:\frac{d_{n}}{r_{n}^{(i)}(v)}<q-1\right\}\subset W_{1},

we have
W_{2}\subset\bigcup\limits_{i=N_{1}+1}^{N}W(n,i)
for all
n\geq 1
. By pigeonhole principle, for any
n
, there exists an index
i(n)\in\{N_{1}+1,\dots,N\}
such that

\mu_{W}\bigg(W\big(n,i(n)\big)\bigg)\geq\frac{1}{N-N_{1}}\mu_{W}(W_{2}).

Replacing
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by a subsequence, we may assume
i(n)=i_{0}
for some
i_{0}\in\{N_{1}+1,\dots,N\}
and all
n\geq 1
.

We now apply Proposition
3.11
to the index
i_{0}
. The condition
\mu_{W}\big(W(n,i_{0})\big)\geq\dfrac{1}{N-N_{1}}\mu_{W}(W_{2})
implies that
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i_{0}})<\infty
. This contradicts the assumption that
i_{0}>N_{1}
. Therefore, the initial inequality must hold.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
:
By Proposition
2.3
, for
1\leq i\leq N
, if
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})=1
, then the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
has good reduction at
s_{i}
. So we may assume
\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})\geq 2
for all
1\leq i\leq N
. By Propostion
3.15
, we have

2g(B)-2+N=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}\leq\frac{N}{2}.

Thus
N\leq 4-4g(B)\leq 4
. Moreover, we must have
g(B)\leq 1
.

(1)

If
g(B)=1,N=0
or
g(B)=0,N\leq 2
, then
B=\mathbb{A}^{1}
,
\mathbb{G}_{m}
,
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or an elliptic curve. By Propostion
2.2
, the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.

(2)

If
g(B)=0
and
N=4
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{4}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}=2.

The reduction type must be
(2,2,2,2)
. Up to a change of coordinates, we set
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4})=(0,1,\lambda,\infty)
in
\overline{B}(k)=\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
. Consider the Legendre curve
E
defined by
y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)
and the branched covering

p:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1},\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)\mapsto x.

The map
p
is branched at
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
with ramification index
2
. Let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{-1}\{b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}\}
and
E^{o}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E\setminus S
. The base change
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}E^{o}
is an abelian scheme over
E^{o}
with reduction type
(1,1,1,1)
. In other words,
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}E^{o}
has good reduction at all points of
S
. Thus, it extends to an abelian scheme over
E
, and this implies
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.

If
g(B)=0
and
N=3
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{\mathrm{ord}(s_{i})}=1.

The reduction type must be
(3,3,3),(2,4,4),(2,3,6),(2,2,\infty)
.

(3)

Reduction type
(3,3,3)
.
Assume
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(0,1,\infty)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
. Consider the elliptic curve
E:y^{3}=x(x-1)
and the morphism

p:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1},\hskip 10.00002pt(x,y)\mapsto x.

Then
p
is branched at
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3}
with ramification index
3
. Arguing as (2), the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial.

Consider the morphism

p:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1},\hskip 10.00002ptz\mapsto z^{2}.

Then
p
is branched at
0,\infty
with ramification index
2
. Let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{-1}\{0,1,\infty\}=\{0,1,-1,\infty\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
. We take
B^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus S
.

(4)

Reduction type
(2,4,4)
.
Assume
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(1,0,\infty)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
. Take base change,
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}
has reduction type
(2,2,2,2)
. This reduces to case (2).

(5)

Reduction type
(2,3,6)
.
Assume
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(0,1,\infty)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
. Take base change,
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}
has reduction type
(3,3,3)
. This reduces to case (3).

(6)

Reduction type
(2,2,\infty)
.
Assume
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})=(0,\infty,1)
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(k)
. Take base change,
\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime}
has reduction type
(1,1,\infty,\infty)
. This reduces to the case
N=2
.

In conclusion, the abelian scheme
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial. ∎

4
Applications to the uniform boundedness conjecture

Throughout this section, let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let
B
be a smooth, geometrically integral curve over
k
. Let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme of relative dimension
g
. We denote by
K\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k(B)
the function field of
B
and by
A\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}_{K}
the generic fiber of
\pi
.

Given an ample and symmetric line bundle
L\in\operatorname{Pic}(A)
and a big and nef adelic line bundle
\overline{H}
on
B
, we may define the Néron–Tate height

\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}:A(\overline{K})\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

as defined in Section
2.3
.

For any point
x\in A(\overline{K})
, we denote by
C_{x}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\overline{\{x\}}^{\mathrm{Zar}}
the Zariski closure of
x
in
\mathcal{A}
, which is a curve finite over
B
.

We establish the following Bogomolov-type result:

Theorem 4.1
.

With the notations as above, assume the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\to B
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme. Then for any integer
N>0
, there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(N)>0
such that the set

\left\{x\in A(\overline{K}):g(C_{x})\leq N,\hskip 10.00002pt\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x)\leq\varepsilon\right\}

is finite.

Proof.

Assume that there exists a sequence of distinct points
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
A(\overline{K})
such that
g(C_{x_{n}})\leq N
for all
n\geq 1
and
\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})=0
. Let
V
be the Zariski closure of the set
\{x_{n}:n\geq 1\}
in
A
. By passing to a subsequence, we may assume
V
is irreducible with
\dim V\geq 1
.

By the Bogomolov conjecture over finitely generated fields, proved by Moriwaki
[
Mor00
, Theorem 8.1]
, there exists an abelian subvariety
A_{1}\subset A
and a torsion point
a\in A(\overline{K})
such that
V
is a translate
V=A_{1}+a
. Let
m
be a positive integer such that
m\cdot a=0
, and define
y_{n}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}m\cdot x_{n}
. It follows that

y_{n}\in m\cdot V=m\cdot A_{1}+m\cdot a=A_{1}.

Since
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is Zariski dense in
V
, the sequence
(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is Zariski dense in
A_{1}
. Up to taking a subsequence, we may assume that
(y_{n})
is a generic sequence in
A_{1}
.

The multiplication-by-
m
map
[m]:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
induces a surjective morphism
C_{x_{n}}\to C_{y_{n}}
. Consequently, we have
g(C_{y_{n}})\leq g(C_{x_{n}})\leq N
. Furthermore, by the properties of the Néron–Tate height,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(y_{n})=m^{2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})=0.

Let
\mathcal{A}_{1}
be the Zariski closure of
A_{1}
in
\mathcal{A}
, which is an abelian subscheme of
\mathcal{A}
. By Theorem
1.4
, the abelian subscheme
\mathcal{A}_{1}\to B
is isotrivial. This contradicts our assumption that
\mathcal{A}
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme.
∎

For a positive integer
n
, let
A[n]^{\times}
denote the set of torsion points in
A(\overline{K})
of exact order
n
. We obtain the following corollary, which answers a conjecture proposed in
[
CT11
]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme. Let
g(n)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min\limits_{x\in A[n]^{\times}}g(C_{x})
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}g(n)=+\infty.

Proof.

Assume that there exists an integer
N>0
and a sequence of distinct torsion points
x_{n}\in A(\overline{K})
, such that
g(C_{x_{n}})\leq N
for all
n\geq 1
. Since
x_{n}
are torsion points, their Néron–Tate heights satisfy
\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})=0
for all
n\geq 1
. This contradicts the finiteness result in Theorem
4.1
.
∎

The following theorem can be regarded as a generalization of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 4.3

(=Theorem
1.6
)
.

Assume the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\to B
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme. Let
P:B\to\mathcal{A}
be a section. Then for any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
and any prime
\ell
, there exists an integer
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N(\mathcal{A},P,k,k^{\prime},\ell)
such that for any
b\in B(k^{\prime})
,

\#\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime}):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\right\}\leq N.

Proof.

After replacing
\mathcal{A}
and
B
by
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
and
B\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
, we may assume
k^{\prime}=k
. Let
x_{0}\in A(K)
be the point in the generic fiber corresponding to the section
P
. Consider the set

T=\{x\in A(\overline{K}):\ell^{n}x=x_{0}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\text{ and }g(C_{x})\leq 1\}.

If
T
were infinite, there would exist an infinite sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
A(\overline{K})
such that
\ell\cdot x_{n+1}=x_{n}
for all
n\geq 0
. This would imply

\lim_{n\to\infty}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{n})=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{\ell^{2n}}\widehat{h}_{L}^{\overline{H}}(x_{0})=0.

Since
g(C_{x_{n}})\leq 1
, Theorem
4.1
implies that
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme, which contradicts our assumption. Thus,
T
is finite.

Now define

S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in A(\overline{K}):\ell x\in T\text{ and }x\notin T\}.

Since
T
is finite,
S
is also finite. By the definition of
T
, every
x\in S
must satisfy
g(C_{x})\geq 2
. For any point
z\in\mathcal{A}(\overline{k})
, if
\ell^{n}z\in P
for some
n\geq 0
, then either
z\in C_{x}
for some
x\in T
, or
\ell^{m}z\in C_{x}
for some
x\in S
and
m\geq 0
.

For each
x\in S
, we have
g(C_{x})\geq 2
. If
C_{x}
is geometrically connected, then by Faltings’ Theorem
[
FWG+92
, Chap. VI, Theorem 3]
, the set of rational points
C_{x}(k)
is finite. If
C_{x}
is not geometrically connected, then
C_{x}(k)=\varnothing
. Consider

V\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup_{x\in S}\operatorname{Im}\big(C_{x}(k)\to B(k)\big)\subset B(k),

which is a finite set. For any
z\in\mathcal{A}(k)
satisfying
\ell^{m}z\in C_{x}
for some
x\in S
and
m\geq 0
,
z
must lie in a fiber
\mathcal{A}_{b}
with
b\in V
. Let
N_{1}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max\limits_{b\in V}\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k)_{\mathrm{tors}}
. Then for
b\in V
,

\#\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\right\}\leq N_{1}.

For each
x\in T
and
b\in B(k)
, the set
\mathcal{A}_{b}\cap C_{x}
contains at most
[K(x):K]
points. Let
N_{2}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum\limits_{x\in T}[K(x):K]
. Then for
b\notin V
,

\#\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\right\}\leq N_{2}.

Taking
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}
, we obtain

\#\left\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\right\}\leq\max\{N_{1},N_{2}\}=N.

for any
b\in B(k)
.
∎

When the section
P
is non-torsion, Theorem
4.3
generally fails for abelian schemes that contain an isotrivial abelian subscheme, as shown in Example
1.7
. If the section
P
is torsion, it suffices to consider the case where
P
is the zero section. The following lemma provides the necessary bound for the isotrivial case.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume the abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial. For any integer
d>0
, there exists an integer
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N(\mathcal{A},B,d)>0
such that

\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq N

for any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
with
[k^{\prime}:k]\leq d
and any
b\in B(k^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}\to B
is isotrivial, there exists a finite cover
p:B^{\prime}\to B
, a finite extension
k_{0}/k
, and an abelian variety
A_{0}
over
k_{0}
such that
(\mathcal{A}\times_{B}B^{\prime})\otimes_{k}k_{0}\simeq A_{0}\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k_{0}}B^{\prime}_{k_{0}}
.

Let
r=\deg(p)
. Any point
b\in B(k^{\prime})
has at most
r
preimages
p^{-1}(b)=\{b_{1},\dots,b_{r}\}
in
B^{\prime}(\overline{k})
. Let
k_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k^{\prime}(b_{i})
be the residue fields; then
[k_{i}:k]\leq rd
. By Masser’s bound
[
Mas86
]
, there exists a constant
M(A_{0},D)
such that
|A_{0}(F)_{\mathrm{tors}}|\leq M
for any extension
F/k_{0}
with
[F:k_{0}]\leq D
.

Let
k_{i}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k_{i}\cdot k_{0}
. Then
[k_{i}^{\prime}:k_{0}]\leq rd
. It follows that

\#\mathcal{A}_{b_{i}}(k_{i}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}=\#A_{0}(k_{i}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq M(A_{0},rd).

Thus

\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{r}\#\mathcal{A}_{b_{i}}(k_{i}^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq r\cdot M(A_{0},rd[k_{0}:k]).

Taking
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}r\cdot M(A_{0},rd[k_{0}:k])
concludes the proof.
∎

Here we give a new proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
k
be a finitely generated field over
\mathbb{Q}
and
B
be a smooth, geometrically integral curve over
k
. Let
\pi:\mathcal{A}\to B
be an abelian scheme. For any finite extension
k^{\prime}/k
and any integer
\ell
, there exists an integer
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N(\mathcal{A},B,k,k^{\prime},\ell)
such that for all
b\in B(k^{\prime})
,

\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\leq N,

where
\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bigcup\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{n}]
is the set of
\ell
-primary torsion points in
\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on the relative dimension
g=\dim(\mathcal{A}/B)
. The case
g=0
is trivial. Assume the theorem holds for all abelian schemes of relative dimension less than
g
.

Case 1.
If
\mathcal{A}
contains no non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme, let
P:B\to\mathcal{A}
be the zero section. We have

\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]=\{z\in\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime}):\ell^{n}z=P_{b}\text{ for some }n\geq 0\}.

The conclusion follows directly from Theorem
4.3
.

Case 2.
If
\mathcal{A}
contains a non-trivial isotrivial abelian subscheme
\mathcal{A}_{1}
, let
\overline{\mathcal{A}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{A}_{1}
be the quotient abelian scheme. Then
\dim(\overline{\mathcal{A}}/B)<g
. By the induction hypothesis, there exists a constant
N_{1}=N_{1}(\overline{\mathcal{A}},B,k^{\prime},\ell)
such that
\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\leq N_{1}
for all
b\in B(k^{\prime})
. By Lemma
4.4
, there exists
N_{2}=N_{2}(\mathcal{A}_{1},B,[k^{\prime}:k])
such that
\#\mathcal{A}_{1,b}(k^{\prime})_{\mathrm{tors}}\leq N_{2}
for all
b\in B(k^{\prime})
.

Consider the exact sequence of abelian groups of
\ell
-primary torsion points:

0\to\mathcal{A}_{1,b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\to\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\to\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}].

It follows that

\#\mathcal{A}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\leq\#\mathcal{A}_{1,b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\cdot\#\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{b}(k^{\prime})[\ell^{\infty}]\leq N_{2}\cdot N_{1}.

Taking
N\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}N_{1}N_{2}
completes the proof.
∎

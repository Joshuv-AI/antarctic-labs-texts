SAAR, a river of Germany, a right-bank tributary of the Mosel. It rises in the Donon, an eminence of the Vosges, close to the Franco-German frontier, and flows at first north, then north-west and finally north again to its junction with the Mosel

at Konz. Its length is 143 m. The middle part of its valley
is an important industrial district, with coal-mines and a variety
of manufactures; the Saar wines are also well known. The
principal towns on the Saar are Saargemünd, Saarbrücken and
St Johann (which face each other across the river), Saarlouis and
Saarburg. The river is navigable up to Saargemünd, a distance
of 75 m. From here there is connexion with the Rhine-Marne
canal by way of the Saar canal, built in 1862, and 40 m.
in length, following the Saar valley upwards for about half that
distance.

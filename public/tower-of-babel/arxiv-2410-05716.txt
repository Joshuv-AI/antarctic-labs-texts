Property (T) for groups acting on affine buildings
Izhar Oppenheim

Abstract
. We prove that a group acting geometrically on a thick affine building has property (T). A more general criterion for property (T) is given for groups acting on partite complexes.



1.
Introduction

Recall that a locally compact group
G
is said to act geometrically on a simplicial complex
X
, if it acts on
X
properly and cocompactly.

The aim of this note is to prove the following Theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a thick affine building of dimension
\geq 2
, i.e., an affine building of dimension
\geq 2
in which every codimension-one simplex is a face of at least three chambers. If
G
is a locally compact, unimodular group that acts geometrically on
X
, then
G
has property (T).

We note that for classical affine buildings, i.e., affine buildings arising from a BN-pair of an algebraic group over a non-archimedean field, this result can be deduced from the fact that higher rank algebraic groups and their lattices have property (T). Also, when
X
is an exotic
\widetilde{A}_{2}
building, this result readily follows from the so called Zuk’s criterion (see
[
BS97
]
). However, when
X
is an exotic building of type
\widetilde{C}_{2}
or
\widetilde{G}_{2}
, the existing results in the literature require the building thickness to be sufficiently large and do not cover buildings of small thickness (see
[
Opp15
, Table 2]
). Part of the motivation for this note is the recently announced result by Titz Mite and Witzel
[
TMW23
]
, in which they construct a non-residually finite group acting properly and cocompactly on an exotic
\widetilde{C}_{2}
building with small thickness.

We prove Theorem
1.1
by establishing a general criterion for property (T) for groups acting geometrically on partite simplicial complexes - a result of independent interest. Notably, we only require a weaker assumption concerning the properness of the action (see details below).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Angle between subspaces

Definition 2.1

(Angle between subspaces)
.

[
Kas11
, Definition 3.2, Remark 3.19]
Let
\mathcal{H}
a Hilbert space and

U_{1}
and
U_{2}
be two closed subspaces in
\mathcal{H}
. The cosine of
\angle(U_{1},U_{2})
is defined as
0
if
U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}\;or\;U_{2}\subseteq U_{1}
and otherwise as

\cos\angle(U_{1},U_{2})=\sup\{|\left\langle u_{1},u_{2}\right\rangle|\;:\;\|u_{i}\|=1,u_{i}\in U_{i},u_{i}\perp(U_{1}\cap U_{2})\}.

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a closed subspace
U
, we will denote
P_{U}
to be the orthogonal projection on
U
.

The following result was proven in
[
Kas11
]
:

Theorem 2.2
.

[
Kas11
, Theorem 5.1]

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
U_{0},...,U_{n}
closed subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\mathcal{A}(U_{0},...,U_{n})
to be the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix defined as

\mathcal{A}(U_{0},...,U_{n})[i,j]=\begin{cases}1&i=j\\
-\cos\angle(U_{i},U_{j})&i\neq j\end{cases}.

If
\mathcal{A}
is positive definite, then for every
x\in\mathcal{H}
it holds that

\|x-P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}x\|^{2}\leq\textbf{d}_{U_{0},...,U_{n}}\left(\mathcal{A}(U_{0},...,U_{n})\right)^{-1}\textbf{d}_{U_{0},...,U_{n}}^{t},

where
\textbf{d}_{U_{0},...,U_{n}}
is the vector
\textbf{d}_{U_{0},...,U_{n}}=\left(\|x-P_{U_{0}}x\|,...,\|x-P_{U_{n}}x\|\right)
.

2.2.
Random walks on bipatite finite graphs

Given a finite connected graph
(V,E)
, we define
m:V\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
to be the degree of
v
for every
v\in V
. For a non-empty subset
V^{\prime}\subseteq V
, we further define
m(V^{\prime})=\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}m(v)
.

The graph
(V,E)
is called
bipartite
if
V
can be partitioned into two disjoint sets
V=S_{1}\cup S_{2}
called
sides
such that for each
\{u,v\}\in E
,
|\{u,v\}\cap S_{1}|=|\{u,v\}\cap S_{2}|=1
, i.e., each edge has exactly one vertex in each side. We note that it follows that
m(S_{1})=m(S_{2})=\frac{1}{2}m(V)
.

We also define
\ell^{2}(V,m)
to be the space of functions
\phi:V\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with an inner-product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle=\sum_{\{v\}\in V}=m(v)\phi(v)\overline{\psi(v)}.

The
random walk
on
(V,E)
as above is the operator
A:\ell^{2}(V,m)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V,m)
defined as

(A\phi)(v)=\sum_{u\in V,\{u,v\}\in E}\frac{1}{m(v)}\phi(u).

We state without proof a few basic facts regarding the random walk operator:

(1)

With the inner-product defined above,
A
is a self-adjoint operator and the eigenvalues of
A
lie in the interval
[-1,1]
.

(2)

The space of constant functions is an eigenspace of
A
with eigenvalue
1
and if
(V,E)
is connected all other the other eigenfunctions of
A
have eigenvalues strictly less than
1
.

(3)

Assuming that the graph
(V,E)
is connected it holds that this graph is bipartite if and only if
-1
is an eigenvalue of
A
.

Assuming that
(V,E)
is bipartite with sides
S_{1},S_{2}
, we note that the spectrum of
A
is symmetric: Explicitly, if
\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
A
with an eigenfunction
\phi
, then

\phi^{\prime}(u)=\begin{cases}\phi(u)&u\in S_{1}\\
-\phi(u)&u\in S_{2}\end{cases}

is an eigenfunction of
A
with an eigenvalue
-\lambda
. In particular
\phi=\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}-\mathbbm{1}_{S_{2}}
is an eigenfunction with the eigenvalue
-1
.

We define the following averaging operators:

M_{1},M_{2}:\ell^{2}(V,m)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
:

M_{i}\phi=\frac{1}{m(S_{i})}\sum_{u\in S_{i}}m(u)\phi(u).

We also define
M_{\operatorname{sides}}:\ell^{2}(V,m)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V,m)
by

M_{\operatorname{sides}}\phi(u)=\begin{cases}M_{1}\phi&u\in S_{1}\\
M_{2}\phi&u\in S_{2}\end{cases}.

We note that
M_{\operatorname{sides}}
is the orthogonal projection on the space of functions
\operatorname{span}\{\mathbbm{1}_{V},\mathbbm{1}_{S_{i}}-\mathbbm{1}_{S_{j}}\}
.

We recall the following definition of spectral expansion:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(V,E)
be a finite connected graph and
0\leq\lambda<1
a constant. The graph
(V,E)
is called a one-sided
\lambda
-spectral expander if the spectrum of
A
is contained in
[-1,\lambda]\cup\{1\}
.

As noted above, for a bipartite graph the spectrum of
A
is symmetric with an eigenvalue
-1
with an eigenfunction
\mathbbm{1}_{S_{1}}-\mathbbm{1}_{S_{2}}
. Thus, for a connected bipartite graph
(V,E)
it holds that the graph is a one-sided
\lambda
-spectral expander if and only if
\|A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}})\|\leq\lambda
.

Given a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, we define
\ell^{2}(V,m;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
to be the Banach space of the functions
\phi:V\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
with the norm

\|\phi\|^{2}=\sum_{v\in V}m(v)|\phi(v)|_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}}^{2}.

For a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathcal{H})
is a Hilbert space with the inner-product

\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathcal{H})}=\sum_{v\in V}m(v)\langle\phi(v),\psi(v)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Given a Banach space
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}
, we define the operator
(A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}}:\ell^{2}(V,m;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V,m;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}})
and denote
\lambda_{(V,E),\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}}=\|(A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}}\|_{B(\ell^{2}(V,m;\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}))}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(V,E)
be a finite connected bipartite graph and denote
\lambda
to be the second largest eigenvalue of the random walk
A:\ell^{2}(V,m)\rightarrow\ell^{2}(V,m)
. For every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
\lambda_{(V,E),\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathcal{H}}\leq\lambda
.

Proof.

Denote
\lambda
to be the second largest eigenvalue of
A
. We will show that for every
\phi\in\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathcal{H})
with
\|\phi\|=1
, it holds that

\|(A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{H}}\phi\|\leq\lambda.

Let
\phi\in\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathcal{H})
of norm
1
. Since
V
is finite, the image of
\phi
,
\phi(V)
is contained in a finite dimensional Hilbert space of dimension
\leq|V|
. Thus, we can assume that
\phi\in\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathbb{C}^{|V|})
and it is enough to show that
\lambda_{(V,E),\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathbb{C}^{|V|}}\leq\lambda
. By our assumption we can write
\phi=(\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq|V|}
. We note that by the definition of the norm, it holds that

\|\phi\|_{\ell^{2}(V,m;\mathbb{C}^{|V|})}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{|V|}\|\phi_{i}\|_{\ell^{2}(V,m)}^{2}.

Also,

A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb{C}^{|V|}}\phi=(A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\phi_{i})_{1\leq i\leq|V|}.

Thus, it is enough to prove that for every
i
,

\|A(I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}))\phi_{i}\|_{\ell^{2}(V,m)}\leq\lambda\|\phi_{i}\|_{\ell^{2}(V,m)}

and this inequality holds since
I-M_{\operatorname{sides}}
is a projection on the space
\left(\operatorname{span}\{\mathbbm{1}_{V},\mathbbm{1}_{S_{i}}-\mathbbm{1}_{S_{j}}\}\right)^{\perp}
and on this space all the eigenvalues of
A
are
\leq\lambda
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

In the notation of the Proposition above, it is not hard to show that it holds that
\lambda_{(V,E),\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathcal{H}}=\lambda
for every non-trivial Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, i.e., for
\mathcal{H}\neq\{0\}
. This fact is left for the reader and we will make no use of it in the sequel.

2.3.
Partite simplicial complexes

Given a set
V
, an abstract simplicial complex
X
with a vertex set
V
is a family of subsets
X\subseteq 2^{V}
such that if
\tau\in X
and
\eta\subseteq\tau
, then
\eta\in X
. We will denote
X(k)
to be the sets in
X
of cardinality
k+1
. A simplicial complex
X
is called
n
-dimensional
if
X(n+1)=\emptyset
and
X(n)\neq\emptyset
. An
n
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
is called
pure

n
-dimensional if for every
\tau
in
X
, there is
\sigma\in X(n)
such that
\tau\subseteq\sigma
.

A pure
n
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
is called
gallery connected
if for every
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in X(n)
, there is a finite sequence
\sigma_{0},...,\sigma_{l}\in X(n)
such that
\sigma=\sigma_{0},\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma_{l}
and for every
1\leq i\leq l-1
,
\sigma_{i}\cap\sigma_{i+1}\in X(n-1)
. For a gallery
\sigma_{0},...,\sigma_{l}
, the number
l
is called the
length of the gallery
and the
gallery distance between
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
is the length of the shortest gallery connecting
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
. Below, we will always assume that
X
is pure
n
-dimensional and gallery connected.

Given a simplex
\tau\in X
, the
link of
\tau
is the subcomplex of
X
, denoted
X_{\tau}
, that is defined as

X_{\tau}=\{\eta\in X:\tau\cap\eta=\emptyset,\tau\cup\eta\in X\}.

Given a pure
n
-dimensional simplicial complex
X
, we call
X

partite
if there is a partition of the vertex set
V
,
V_{0}\sqcup...\sqcup V_{n}=V
such that for every
\sigma\in X(n)
and every
0\leq i\leq n
it holds that
|\sigma\cap V_{i}|=1
, i.e., every
n
-dimensional simplex has exactly one vertex in each of the sets
V_{0},...,V_{n}
. A partite simplicial complex is also sometimes called
colorable
, since we can think of the partition
V_{0},...,V_{n}
as a coloring of the vertex sets with
n+1
colors, such that each
n
-dimensional simplex has vertices with all the colors (or equivalently, each
n
-dimensional simplex do not have two vertices with the same color). For an
n
-dimensional partite simplicial complex, we define a type function
\operatorname{type}:X\rightarrow 2^{\{0,...,n\}}
by

\operatorname{type}(\tau)=\{i:\exists v\in\tau\cap V_{i}\}.

Last, note that if
X
is a pure
n
-dimensional partite simplicial complex, then all the
1
-dimensional links of
X
are bipartite graphs.

3.
Property (T) criterion for a group acting on a partite complex

Here we will establish a general criterion for property (T) for a group acting on a partite simplicial complex.

We start by recalling the framework and some results from
[
Opp23
]
.

Let
X
be a partite pure
n
-dimensional simplicial complex such that
X
is gallery connected and the
1
-dimensional links of
X
are connected finite graphs. Also let
G
be a locally compact, unimodular group acting on
X
such that the action is cocompact and for every
\tau\in X(n-2)\cup X(n-1)\cup X(n)
the subgroup stabilizing
\tau
, denoted
G_{\tau}
, is an open compact subgroup. We also assume that the action of
G
is type-preserving, i.e., for every
0\leq k\leq n
and every
\tau\in X(k)
, it holds for every
g\in G
that
\operatorname{type}(\tau)=\operatorname{type}(g.\tau)
. Last, let
\pi
be a continuous unitary representation
\pi
of
G
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

Remark 3.1
.

The assumption that the action is type preserving is in order to avoid some technical issues. We note that this assumption is not very restrictive since every group
G
acting on a partite simplicial complex
X
that is gallery connected has a finite index subgroup that is type preserving. Indeed, let
X
be partite, gallery connected simplicial complex and
G
a group acting on
X
. Let
\sigma=\{v_{0},...,v_{n}\},\sigma^{\prime}=\{w_{0},...,w_{n}\}\in X(n)
such that
\operatorname{type}(v_{i})=\operatorname{type}(w_{i})=i
. By induction on the gallery distance between
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
, one can show that for every
g\in G
,
\operatorname{type}(g.v_{i})=\operatorname{type}(g.w_{i})
. It follows that when fixing
\sigma=\{v_{0},...,v_{n}\}
as above, the map
\Phi:G\rightarrow\operatorname{Sym}\{0,...,n\}
defined as
\Phi(g)(i)=\operatorname{type}(g^{-1}.v_{i})
is a group homomorphism and its kernel is a finite index subgroup of
G
that is type preserving.

We define
C(X(n),\pi)
to be the space of maps
\phi:X(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
that are equivariant with respect to
\pi
, i.e., for every
\sigma\in X(n)
and every
g\in G
,
\pi(g)\phi(\sigma)=\phi(g.\sigma)
. We further define a norm on
C(X(n),\pi)
by fixing a fundamental domain
D(n)
for the action of
G
on
X(n)
and defining

\|\phi\|^{2}=\left(\sum_{\sigma\in D(n)}\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in D(n)}\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}|\phi(\sigma)|^{2},

where
G_{\sigma}
is the subgroup stabilizing
\sigma
,
\mu
is the Haar measure of
G
and
|.|
is the norm of
\mathcal{H}
.

For
\emptyset\neq\nu\subseteq\{0,....,n\}
define an equivalence relation
\sim_{\nu}
on
X(n)
as
\sigma\sim_{\nu}\sigma^{\prime}
if
|\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}|\geq|\nu|
and there is
\tau\in X
such that
\operatorname{type}(\tau)=\nu
and
\tau\subseteq\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime}
. Note that by the assumption that the
1
-dimensional links of
X
are finite graphs, it follows that for every
\emptyset\neq\nu\subseteq\{0,...,n\}
,
|\nu|=n
and every
\sigma\in X(n)
, the set
\{\sigma^{\prime}\in X(n):\sigma\sim_{\nu}\sigma^{\prime}\}
is finite. For
\emptyset\neq\nu\subseteq\{0,...,n\}
,
|\nu|=n
, define a projection
P_{\nu}^{\pi}:C(X(n),\pi)\rightarrow C(X(n),\pi)
by

P_{\nu}^{\pi}\phi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{|\{\sigma^{\prime}\in X(n):\sigma\sim_{\nu}\sigma^{\prime}\}|}\sum_{\sigma^{\prime}\sim_{\nu}\sigma}\phi(\sigma^{\prime}),

(verifying that
P_{\nu}^{\pi}\phi
is equivariant with respect to
\pi
is straight-forward and left for the reader). Denote

C(X(n),\pi)_{\nu}=\{\phi\in C(X(n),\pi):\forall\sigma,\sigma^{\prime},\sigma\sim_{\nu}\sigma^{\prime}\Rightarrow\phi(\sigma)=\phi(\sigma^{\prime})\},

and note that
P_{\nu}^{\pi}
is a projection on
C(X(n),\pi)_{\nu}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

[
Opp23
, Lemma 3.5]

Let
X
,
G
and
\pi
be as above. The space
\bigcap_{\nu\subseteq\{0,....,n\},|\nu|=n}C(X(n),\pi)_{\nu}
is the space of constant functions
\phi\equiv x_{0}
where
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G)}
.

We note that for every
\tau\in X(n-2)
,
X_{\tau}
is a bipartite graph and we denote
\lambda_{\tau,\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathcal{H}}=\lambda_{X_{\tau},\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathcal{H}}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

[
Opp23
, Lemma 3.8]

Let
X
,
G
and
\pi
be as above. For every
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\subseteq\{0,....,n\},|\nu|=|\nu^{\prime}|=n,\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
, denote

\lambda_{\nu\cap\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{H}}=\max_{\tau\in X(n-2)/G,\operatorname{type}(\tau)=\nu\cap\nu^{\prime}}\lambda_{\tau,\operatorname{bipartite}}^{\mathcal{H}}.

Then it holds that

\cos\angle(C(X(n),\pi)_{\nu},C(X(n),\pi)_{\nu^{\prime}})\leq\lambda_{\nu\cap\nu^{\prime}}^{\mathcal{H}}.

We note that for every
\eta\in X(n-2)
,
X_{\eta}
is a connected bipartite graph and define
\lambda_{\eta}
to be the second largest eigenvalue of the random walk on
X_{\eta}
. For
0\leq i,j\leq n,i\neq j
, we define

\lambda_{i,j}=\max_{\eta\in X(n-2)/G,\operatorname{type}(\eta)=\{0,...,n\}\setminus\{i,j\}}\lambda_{\eta}

and further define
\mathcal{A}(X)
to be the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix

\mathcal{A}(X)[i,j]=\begin{cases}1&i=j\\
-\lambda_{i,j}&i\neq j\end{cases}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
,
G
and
\pi
be as above. Denote
U_{i}=C(X(n),\pi)_{\{0,...,n\}\setminus\{i\}}
and
\mathcal{A}(\pi)=\mathcal{A}(U_{0},...,U_{n})
to be the cosine matrix as in Theorem
2.2
, i.e.,

\mathcal{A}(\pi)[i,j]=\begin{cases}1&i=j\\
-\cos\angle(U_{i},U_{j})&i\neq j\end{cases}.

Then for every
i,j
,
\mathcal{A}(\pi)[i,j]\geq\mathcal{A}(X)[i,j]
.

In particular, if
\mathcal{A}(X)
is positive definite and its smallest eigenvalue is
\lambda_{X}
, then
\mathcal{A}(\pi)
is positive definite and its smallest eigenvalue is
\geq\lambda_{X}
.

Proof.

By the above Lemma and Proposition
2.4
it follows for every
i\neq j
, that
\mathcal{A}(\pi)[i,j]\geq\mathcal{A}(X)[i,j]
as needed. The fact that the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}(\pi)
is
\geq\lambda_{X}
follows from
[
GRO22
, Proposition 2.8]
.
∎

For
x\in\mathcal{H}
, define
\phi_{x}:D(n)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
by

\phi_{x}(\sigma)=\pi\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{G_{\sigma}}}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\right)x,\forall\sigma\in D(n),

where
\mathbbm{1}_{G_{\sigma}}
is the indicator function of
G_{\sigma}
. Observe that
\phi_{x}(\sigma)\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G_{\sigma})}
for every
\sigma\in D(n)
. Extend
\phi_{x}
to
X(n)
as follows: for every
g\in G
and every
\sigma\in D(n)
, define

\phi_{x}(g.\sigma)=\pi(g)\phi_{x}(\sigma)=\pi\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{gG_{\sigma}}}{\mu(gG_{\sigma})}\right)x=\pi\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{gG_{\sigma}}}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\right)x.

Proposition 3.5
.

[
Opp23
, Proposition 3.3]

The map
\phi_{x}
is well-defined and equivariant, i.e.,
\phi_{x}\in C(X(n),\pi)
.

After this set-up, we are ready to state and prove our criterion for property (T):

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
X
be a partite pure
n
-dimensional simplicial complex such that
X
is gallery connected and the
1
-dimensional links of
X
are connected finite graphs. Also let
G
be a locally compact, unimodular group acting on
X
such that the action is cocompact and for every
\tau\in X(n-2)\cup X(n-1)\cup X(n)
the subgroup stabilizing
\tau
, denoted
G_{\tau}
, is an open compact subgroup. Assume that
\mathcal{A}(X)
is positive definite, then
G
has property (T).

Proof.

Recall that a group
G
has property (T) if and only if every finite index group of
G
has property (T). Thus, by Remark
3.1
we can assume that
G
is type preserving.

Define
K\subseteq G
to be the set

K=\{g\in G:\exists\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in D(n),|g.\sigma^{\prime}\cap\sigma|\geq n\}.

Note that
K
is a compact symmetric set that generates
G
(the last fact is due to the assumption that
X
is gallery connected).

Let
\lambda_{X}
be the smallest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{A}(X)
. We will show that for

\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{\lambda_{X}}}\right)},

that
(K,\varepsilon)
is a Kazhdan pair, i.e., that for every continuous unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
with
\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G)}=\{0\}
, it holds for every unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
that

\sup_{g\in K}|\pi(g)x-x|\geq\varepsilon.

We will prove the contra-positive, i.e., that given a continuous unitary representation
(\pi,\mathcal{H})
, if there exists a unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\sup_{g\in K}|\pi(g)x-x|<\varepsilon,

then
\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G)}\neq\{0\}
.

Assume that there is a unit vector
x\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\sup_{g\in K}|\pi(g)x-x|<\varepsilon.

For every
\sigma\in D(n)
it holds that
G_{\sigma}\subseteq K
and thus for every
\sigma\in D(n)
,

\displaystyle|x-\phi_{x}(\sigma)|=|x-\pi\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{G_{\sigma}}}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\right)x|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\int_{g\in G_{\sigma}}|x-\pi(g)x|<\varepsilon.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{x}\|\geq|x|-\left(\left(\sum_{\sigma\in D(n)}\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}\right)^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in D(n)}\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma})}|x-\phi_{x}(\sigma)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}>1-\varepsilon.

Similarly, let
\sigma\in D(n)
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}\in X(n)
such that
|\sigma\cap\sigma^{\prime\prime}|=n
. Then there is
g^{\prime}\in K
and
\sigma^{\prime}\in D(n)
such that
g^{\prime}.\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime\prime}
. Note that
g^{\prime}G_{\sigma^{\prime}}\subseteq K
and thus

\displaystyle|x-\phi_{x}(\sigma^{\prime})|=|x-\pi(g^{\prime})\pi\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{G_{\sigma^{\prime}}}}{\mu(G_{\sigma^{\prime}})}\right)x|\leq\frac{1}{\mu(G_{\sigma^{\prime}})}\int_{g\in G_{\sigma^{\prime}}}|x-\pi(g^{\prime}g)x|<\varepsilon.

It follows that for every
\nu\subseteq\{0,...,n\}
,
|\nu|=n
, every
\sigma\in D(n)
and every
\sigma^{\prime\prime}\sim_{\nu}\sigma
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{x}(\sigma)-\phi_{x}(\sigma^{\prime})|<2\varepsilon.

Thus, for every
\sigma\in D(n)
and every
\nu\subseteq\{0,...,n\}
,
|\nu|=n
,

\displaystyle|\phi_{x}(\sigma)-P_{\nu}^{\pi}\phi_{x}(\sigma)|<2\varepsilon,

and also

\|\phi_{x}-P_{\nu}^{\pi}\phi_{x}\|<2\varepsilon.

Denote
U_{i}=C(X(n),\pi)_{\{0,...,n\}\setminus\{i\}}
and
\mathcal{A}(\pi)=\mathcal{A}(U_{0},...,U_{n})
. We showed that for every
0\leq i\leq n
,

\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{i}}\phi_{x}\|<2\varepsilon.

By Corollary
3.4
,
\mathcal{A}(\pi)
is a positive definite matrix and its smallest eigenvalue is
\geq\lambda_{X}
. Thus
(\mathcal{A}(\pi))^{-1}
is a symmetric positive definite matrix and its largest eigenvalue is
\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{X}}
. By Theorem
2.2
it holds that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{0}}\phi_{x}\|,...,\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|\right)\left(\mathcal{A}(\pi)\right)^{-1}\left(\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{0}}\phi_{x}\|,...,\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|\right)^{t}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{X}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{i}}\phi_{x}\|^{2}<\frac{1}{\lambda_{X}}\sum_{i=0}^{n}(2\varepsilon)^{2}=\frac{4(n+1)\varepsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{X}}.

Combining the inequalities above yields that

\displaystyle\|P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|\geq\|\phi_{x}\|-\|\phi_{x}-P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|>

\displaystyle 1-2\varepsilon-\sqrt{\frac{4(n+1)\varepsilon^{2}}{\lambda_{X}}}=1-2\varepsilon\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{n+1}{\lambda_{X}}}\right)=^{\text{choice of }\varepsilon}0,

i.e.,
\|P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|>0
. By Lemma
3.2
, there is
x_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G)}
such that
P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\equiv x_{0}
and since
\|P_{U_{0}\cap...\cap U_{n}}\phi_{x}\|>0
, it follows that
x_{0}\neq 0
, i.e., that
\mathcal{H}^{\pi(G)}\neq\{0\}
as needed.
∎

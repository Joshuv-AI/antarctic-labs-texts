The crossover from classical to quantum transport in a weakly-interacting Fermi gas
Hadrien Kurkjian

Abstract
We present an exact solution of the quantum kinetic equation of a weakly interacting Fermi gas in the crossover from the degenerate Fermi-liquid regime to the classical Boltzmann gas. We construct families of orthogonal polynomials tailored to each angular momentum channel, enabling a fast and systematically improvable decomposition of the phase-space distribution. This approach yields accurate, non-variational predictions for the shear viscosity, thermal diffusivity, and spin diffusivity to leading order in the scattering length. We demonstrate that the commonly used relaxation-time approximation fails dramatically at low temperature–by up to 25%. Our method provides a numerically efficient framework for benchmarking transport in strongly correlated regimes and for simulating the kinetics of quantum gases beyond hydrodynamics.



Introduction

A weakly-interacting Fermi gas smoothly interpolates
between a Fermi liquid at low temperatures,
and a Boltzmann gas at high temperatures.
All along this transition from a quantum degenenerate
to a classical gas, the dynamics can be captured by a kinetic

by a strongly-correlated regime in which the Bogoliubov–Born–Green–Kirkwood–Yvon

predictions of the transport properties, that can be used, both experimentally and theoretically, as benchmarks for the strongly-correlated
regime. For the transport coefficients, this requires in particular to discard the uncontrolled relaxation time approximations (RTA),
and solve the kinetic equation exactly in the hydrodynamic limit.

I.2
Collisional dynamics of the particle distribution

Unlike in strongly-interacting systems, the validity of the transport equation in a weakly-interacting gas
is guaranteed at all temperatures and momenta by the smallness of interactions. In an homogeneous state, one may
identify the phase-space distribution function to the momentum distribution of the particles

n_{\sigma}(\textbf{p},t)=\langle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\textbf{p}\sigma}\hat{a}_{\textbf{p}\sigma}\rangle

The average value

\hat{\varrho}

I.3
Dimensionless variables and explicit expression of the collision kernel

II
The hydrodynamic limit

II.1
Equation of state of the weakly-interacting Fermi gas

As the equilibrium properties are needed to interpret
the transport properties in the hydrodynamic limit,
we briefly recall the equation of state of the weakly-interacting Fermi gas.
In particular, we connect the Fermi-Dirac integrals with which this equation of state is usually
expressed to the norm of our polynomials
Q_{n}^{l}
.

The equilibrium density and pressure are related to
T
, the coupling constant
g=4\pi a/m

and
\mu_{0}=\mu-g\rho_{\rm eq}/2

by

\displaystyle\rho_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{1/2}=\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}}p_{T}^{3}W_{2}

(50)

\displaystyle p_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{{\rm eq},0}+\frac{1}{4}g\rho_{\rm eq}^{2}\quad\text{with}\quad p_{{\rm eq},0}=\frac{2T}{\lambda_{\rm th}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{3/2}=\frac{4}{15\pi^{2}}Tp_{T}^{3}W_{4}

(51)

where
\lambda_{\rm th}=\sqrt{2\pi/mT}
is the thermal de Broglie wavelength,
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{j}\textrm{d}t/(\textrm{e}^{t-\mu_{0}/T}+1)/\Gamma(j+1)
is the complete Fermi-Dirac integral. We have introduced
W_{n}
,
the norm of
p^{n/2}
in the scalar product Eq. (
36
), related to
\mathcal{F}_{j}
by:

W_{n}\equiv||p^{n/2}||^{2}=\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}_{(n-1)/2}}{2}

(52)

For the internal energy
u_{\rm eq}
,
kinetic energy
e_{\rm eq}
, and entropy
s_{\rm eq}
, all per unit volume, we have

\displaystyle e_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}p_{\rm eq,0}

(53)

\displaystyle u_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{\rm eq}+\frac{1}{4}g\rho_{\rm eq}^{2}

(54)

\displaystyle s_{\rm eq}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{5}{2}\mathcal{F}_{3/2}-\frac{\mu_{0}}{T}\mathcal{F}_{1/2}}{\mathcal{F}_{1/2}}\rho_{\rm eq}

(55)

Since the entropy per particle
s_{\rm eq}/\rho_{\rm eq}
is a function only of
{\mu_{0}}/{T}
, isentropic transformations
(
\textrm{d}S=0
) are characterized by
\textrm{d}({\mu_{0}}/{T})=0
. Differentiating Eqs. (
50
)–(
51
), we deduce the isentropic compressibility

\chi_{s}=\rho_{\rm eq}(\partial\rho/\partial P)_{S}
:

\frac{1}{\rho_{\rm eq}\chi_{s}}=\frac{5T}{3}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{3/2}}{\mathcal{F}_{1/2}}+\frac{g\rho_{\rm eq}}{2}=\frac{2W_{4}}{3W_{2}}+\frac{g\rho_{\rm eq}}{2}

(56)

From Eq. (
53
), we can also extract the specific heat

\frac{c_{V}}{V}\equiv\left(\frac{\partial e_{\rm eq}}{\partial T}\right)_{\rho_{\rm eq}}=\frac{3\rho_{\rm eq}}{2}\left(\frac{W_{4}}{W_{2}}-\frac{W_{2}}{W_{0}}\right)=\frac{3\rho_{\rm eq}}{2}\frac{||Q_{2}^{0}||^{2}}{||Q_{1}^{1}||^{2}}

(57)

Finally, the polarisation susceptibility is given by

{\chi_{p}}\equiv\left(\frac{\partial(\rho_{\uparrow}^{\rm eq}-\rho_{\downarrow}^{\rm eq})}{\partial(\mu_{\uparrow}-\mu_{\downarrow})}\right)_{\mu_{-}=0,T}=\frac{1}{T\lambda_{T}^{3}}\mathcal{F}_{-1/2}=\frac{p_{T}^{3}}{2T}\frac{W_{0}}{\pi^{2}}.

(58)

II.2
Conserved quantities and collision times

In hydrodynamic flows, the particle distribution
n
can be summarized by the conserved
quantities, which are insensitive to collisions, and whose evolution is described by
Navier-Stokes equations.
There are four conserved quantities, three in the total density sector:
\nu_{0+}^{0}
,
\nu_{1+}^{1}
and
\nu_{2+}^{0}
proportional respectively to the total density, the total velocity and the total energy density, and one in the spin sector:
\nu_{0-}^{0}
, proportional to the polarisation. Mathematically, these conserved quantities are the projection of the distribution
\nu
onto the four zero eigenfunctions of the collision kernel, namely
P_{1}(\cos\theta)Q_{1}^{1}(p)=p\cos\theta
,
P_{0}(\cos\theta)Q_{2}^{0}(p)=p^{2}
, and
P_{0}(\cos\theta)Q_{0}^{0}(p)=1
which is en eigenfunction in both the total density and spin sector.
The restriction of the transport equation (
42
) to these conserved quantities gives

\displaystyle\tilde{c}\nu_{0+}^{0}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{\xi_{1}^{1}}{3}\nu_{1+}^{1}=0

(59)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}\nu_{1+}^{1}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left(\nu_{0+}^{0}+\xi_{2}^{0}\nu_{2+}^{0}\right)-\frac{2}{5}\xi_{2}^{2}\nu_{2+}^{2}-\frac{4\tilde{a}||Q_{0}^{0}||^{2}}{\pi}\nu_{0+}^{0}=1

(60)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}\nu_{2+}^{0}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\left(\nu_{1+}^{1}+\xi_{3}^{1}\nu_{3+}^{1}\right)=0

(61)

in the total density sector, and

\tilde{c}\nu_{0-}^{0}-\frac{\xi_{1}^{1}}{3}\nu_{1-}^{1}=0

(62)

in the spin sector.

The non conserved quantities which introduce dissipation in these systems are
(\nu_{3+}^{1},\nu_{2+}^{2})
for the spin-symmetric conserved quantities and
\nu_{1-}^{1}
for the polarisation. We shall designate as the
first non conserved quanties
, those three components of
\nu
, and those that are collisionally coupled to them:

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}_{+}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nu_{n+}^{2})_{n\geq 2}

(63)

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}_{+,\perp}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nu_{n+}^{1})_{n\geq 3}

(64)

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}_{-}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nu_{n-}^{1})_{n\geq 1}

(65)

In the hydrodynamic limit, the first non-conserved quantities are subleading in
O(\omega_{T}\tau)
and can be approximated by

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}^{2}_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\textrm{i}}{3}(\omega_{T}\tau)\nu_{1+}^{1}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{2}_{+}}\vec{u}_{2}+O(\omega_{T}\tau)^{2}

(66)

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}^{1}_{+,\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\textrm{i}(\omega_{T}\tau)\nu_{2+}^{0}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{+,\perp}}\vec{u}_{3}+O(\omega_{T}\tau)^{2}

(67)

\displaystyle\vec{\nu}^{1}_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{i}(\omega_{T}\tau)\left[1-\nu_{0-}^{0}\left(1-\frac{4\tilde{a}}{\pi}||Q_{0}||^{2}\right)\right]\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{-}}\vec{u}_{1}+O(\omega_{T}\tau)^{2}

(68)

where
\vec{u}_{n}=(\delta_{n^{\prime}n})_{n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}}
are the units vectors of our basis. The matrices
(\mathcal{M}_{nn^{\prime}}^{l})_{nn^{\prime}>l}

are restricted In Eq. (
67
),
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{+,\perp}=(\mathcal{M}^{1}_{+})_{n,n^{\prime}>3}
is the restriction of
\mathcal{M}^{1}_{+}
to
the subspace orthogonal to
Q_{1}
(the zero-eigenfunction associated to the total velocity), where it is then invertible.

From (
66
–
68
), we extract the components
(\nu_{3+}^{1},\nu_{2+}^{2},\nu_{1,-}^{1})
needed to close the system of equations on the conserved quantities.
Each of them is associated to a distinct projection of
1/\mathcal{M}
, which we write as a collision time:

\displaystyle\nu_{2,+}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\textrm{i}}{3}\nu_{1+}^{1}(\omega_{T}\tau_{\eta})\quad\text{ with }\quad\frac{\tau_{\eta}}{\tau}=\vec{u}_{2}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{2}_{+}}\vec{u}_{2}

(69)

\displaystyle\nu_{3,+}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\textrm{i}\nu_{2+}^{0}(\omega_{T}\tau_{\kappa})\quad\text{ with }\quad\frac{\tau_{\kappa}}{\tau}=\vec{u}_{3}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1,\perp}_{+}}\vec{u}_{3}

(70)

\displaystyle\nu_{1,-}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textrm{i}(1-\nu_{0-}^{0})(\omega_{T}\tau_{D})\quad\text{ with }\quad\frac{\tau_{D}}{\tau}=\vec{u}_{1}\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{-}}\vec{u}_{1}

II.3
Relaxation time approximation and higher truncations

Below, we shall compare the exact collision times
\tau_{\eta}
,
\tau_{\kappa}
,
\tau_{D}
to their value
\tau_{\eta}^{0}
,
\tau_{\kappa}^{0}
,
\tau_{D}^{0}
in the variational RTA, also called “first approximation” by Ref.
ChapmanCowling
. This first approximation is obtained by truncating the particle distribution to the lowest
Q_{n}^{l}
polynomial:

\nu(p,\theta)\approx\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\nu_{l}^{l}P_{l}(\cos\theta)Q_{l}^{l}(p)

(72)

This amounts to replacing
the matrices
\mathcal{M}^{l}_{\pm}
by numbers corresponding to their first nonzero diagonal coefficients:

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{\eta}^{0}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{2}_{22+}}

(73)

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{\kappa}^{0}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1,\perp}_{33+}}

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{\tau_{D}^{0}}{\tau}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{M}^{1}_{11-}}

(75)

The first approximation can be systematically improved in our formalism by including
higher
Q_{n}
polynomials:

\nu(p,\theta)\approx\sum_{l=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{n=l}^{l+n_{\rm max}}\nu_{n}^{l}P_{l}(\cos\theta)Q_{n}^{l}(p)

(76)

The convergence of
\tau_{\eta}^{n_{\rm max}}
,
\tau_{\kappa}^{n_{\rm max}}
,
\tau_{D}^{n_{\rm max}}

to the exact value is rather slow in the low temperature limit
\tau_{\eta}^{n_{\rm max}}-\tau_{\eta}=O(1/n_{\rm max})

and much faster in the high temperature limit.

Conclusion

We introduced a method to exactly solve the quantum Boltzmann equation in the hydrodynamic regime,
based on well chosen families of orthogonal polynomials.
We applied this method to obtain exact values of the transport coefficients of a Fermi gas with contact interactions to leading order
in the scattering length
a
, and pointed out large deviations from the relaxation time approximation in the
low-temperature regime.
Owing to its numerical frugality and to its natural truncation scheme, our method in a natural starting point to
explore more complicated transport dynamics, such as second-order hydrodynamics
ChangUhlenbeck
;
Uribe2008
;
Schafer2014secondorder
;
Shukla2024
, collisionless regimes, or non linear flows.

Appendix A
Analytic expression of the collision kernel

We detail here the derivation of Eqs. (
26
)–(
27
) on the dimensionless collision kernels
\tilde{E}
and
\tilde{S}
:

\tilde{E}(\tilde{p},\tilde{p}^{\prime},u)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\int\textrm{d}^{3}\tilde{p}_{3}\textrm{d}^{3}\tilde{p}_{4}\delta({\tilde{\textbf{p}}+\tilde{\textbf{p}}^{\prime}-\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{3}-\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{4}})\delta\left({\tilde{p}^{2}}+{\tilde{p}^{\prime 2}}-{\tilde{p}_{3}^{2}}-{\tilde{p}_{4}^{2}}\right)\\
\left[n(\tilde{p}^{\prime 2})\left(1-n(\tilde{p}_{3}^{2})-n(\tilde{p}_{4}^{2}))\right)+n(\tilde{p}_{3}^{2})n(\tilde{p}_{4}^{2})\right]

(106)

\tilde{S}(\tilde{p},\tilde{p}^{\prime},u)=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\int\textrm{d}^{3}\tilde{p}_{2}\textrm{d}^{3}\tilde{p}_{4}\delta({\tilde{\textbf{p}}+\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{2}-\tilde{\textbf{p}}^{\prime}-\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{4}})\delta\left({\tilde{p}^{2}}+{\tilde{p}_{2}^{2}}-{\tilde{p}^{\prime 2}}-{\tilde{p}_{4}^{2}}\right)\\
\left[n(\tilde{p}_{2}^{2})\left(1-n(\tilde{p}^{\prime 2})-n(\tilde{p}_{4}^{2}))\right)+n(\tilde{p}^{\prime 2})n(\tilde{p}_{4}^{2})\right]

(107)

Calculation of
\tilde{E}

To treat the energy-momentum conservation in
\tilde{E}
, we introduce
the variables

\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{3}=\frac{\textbf{K}+\textbf{y}}{\sqrt{2}}\qquad\tilde{\textbf{p}}_{4}=\frac{\textbf{K}-\textbf{y}}{\sqrt{2}}

(108)

Momentum conservation fixes
\textbf{K}=(\tilde{\textbf{p}}+\tilde{\textbf{p}}^{\prime})/\sqrt{2}
and energy conservation
y=||\tilde{\textbf{p}}-\tilde{\textbf{p}}^{\prime}||/\sqrt{2}
.
Locating
y
in a spherical frame of axis
K
, there remains to integrate over
u_{y}=\text{cos}(\textbf{K},\textbf{y})
:

\tilde{E}(\tilde{p},\tilde{p}^{\prime},u)=\frac{1}{\pi\tilde{p}_{-}}\int_{0}^{1}\textrm{d}u_{y}\Bigg\{n(\tilde{p}^{\prime 2})\left[1-n\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{+}^{2}+\tilde{p}_{-}^{2}+2\tilde{p}_{+}\tilde{p}_{-}u_{y}}{2}\right)-n\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{+}^{2}+\tilde{p}_{-}^{2}-2\tilde{p}_{+}\tilde{p}_{-}u_{y}}{2}\right)\right]\\
{+n\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{+}^{2}+\tilde{p}_{-}^{2}+2\tilde{p}_{+}\tilde{p}_{-}u_{y}}{2}\right)n\left(\frac{\tilde{p}_{+}^{2}+\tilde{p}_{-}^{2}-2\tilde{p}_{+}\tilde{p}_{-}u_{y}}{2}\right)}\Bigg\}

(109)

where
\textbf{p}_{\pm}=\textbf{p}\pm\textbf{p}^{\prime}
. We have used the azimuthal invariance of the integrand, and the symmetry
u_{y}\leftrightarrow-u_{y}
. This integral
can be evaluated analytically, which results in Eq. (
26
).

Calculation of
\tilde{S}

For
\tilde{S}
, where
p
and
\textbf{p}^{\prime}
do not play symmetric roles,
we keep
\textbf{p}_{2}
as the integration variable and eliminate
\textbf{p}_{4}
using momentum conservation.
Locating
\textbf{p}_{2}
in a spherical frame of axis
\textbf{p}_{-}
,
we write the energy conservation constraint in the form:

p^{2}+p_{2}^{2}-p^{\prime 2}-(\textbf{p}+\textbf{p}_{2}-p^{\prime})^{2}=2p_{-}(p_{2}u_{2}-p^{\prime}u_{-})

(110)

with
u_{2}=\cos(\textbf{p}_{2},\textbf{p}_{-})
and
u_{-}=\cos(\textbf{p}^{\prime},\textbf{p}_{-})=(\tilde{p}^{\prime}-\tilde{p}u)/\tilde{p}_{-}
.
Using the Dirac function to integrate over
u_{2}
, there remains an integral over
\epsilon_{2}=p_{2}^{2}
limited to

\epsilon_{2}>p^{\prime 2}u_{-}^{2}
:

\tilde{S}(\tilde{p},\tilde{p}^{\prime},u)=\frac{1}{\pi\tilde{p}_{-}}\int_{p^{\prime 2}u_{-}^{2}}^{+\infty}{\textrm{d}\epsilon_{2}}\Big\{n(\epsilon_{2})\left[1-n(p^{\prime 2})-n(\epsilon_{2}+p^{2}-p^{\prime 2})\right]\\
\left.+n(p^{\prime 2})n(\epsilon_{2}+p^{2}-p^{\prime 2})\right]\Big\}

(111)

which finally results in Eq. (
27
).

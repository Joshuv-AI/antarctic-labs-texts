Constructing geometric realizations of birational maps between Mori Dream Spaces
Lorenzo Barban, Gianluca Occhetta, Luis E. Solá Conde

Abstract
. We construct geometric realizations –projective algebraic versions of cobordisms– for birational maps between Mori Dream Spaces. We show that these geometric realizations are Mori Dream Spaces, as well, and that they can be constructed so that they induce factorizations of the original birational maps as compositions of wall-crossings. In the case of toric birational maps between normal ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} -factorial, projective toric varieties, we provide several SageMath functions to work with ℂ ∗ {\mathbb{C}}^{*} -actions and birational geometry; in particular we show how to explicitly construct a moment polytope of a toric geometric realization. Moreover, by embedding Mori Dream Spaces in toric varieties, we obtain geometric realizations of birational maps of Mori Dream Spaces as restrictions of toric geometric realizations. We also provide examples and discuss when a geometric realization is Fano.

Introduction

Białynicki-Birula theory for
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions serves as an algebraic analog of Morse theory (see
[
11
,
8
]
), that has deep connections with birational geometry, as shown in the works of Reid, Morelli, Włodarczyk, and others (cf.
[
21
,
16
,
26
]
). Within this circle of ideas, it has been recently shown that
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions on projective varieties can be encoded through birational transformations on geometric quotients, defined by the properties of the action on its fixed point components (see
[
20
,
6
]
and the references therein).

Consider a birational map
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
between normal projective varieties. A geometric realization of

is a normal projective variety

-action, where

correspond to geometric quotients of

with respect to different linearizations of the action on an ample line bundle

if and only if they represent the same orbit in

. Typically, we assume that
Y_{\pm}
and

have reasonable singularities, consistent with the framework of modern birational geometry.

The geometric quotients

of

associated with intermediate weights of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on

form a factorization of

:

•

\phi_{1}
is a divisorial extraction (inverse of the blowup of
{\mathbb{P}}^{3}
along four points);

•

\phi_{2}
is the composition of the flips of

•

\phi_{3}

Note that the maps
\phi_{i}
in this example are not elementary; however,
by consider a linearization on an appropriately chosen ample line bundle
L={\mathcal{O}}_{X}(a,b,c,d)
, it is always possible to obtain a factorization into elementary divisorial extractions, contractions and flips (see
[
6
, Section 6]
for details).

We call a geometric realization
sharp
if the maps
\phi_{i}
in the factorization are elementary flips, divisorial contractions, or divisorial extractions. This concept links the Sarkisov program for
\phi
to
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant minimal model programs (MMPs) for its geometric realization
X
.

In this paper we investigate the existence of geometric realizations, and which geometrical property of
Y_{\pm}
are inherited by the geometric realization. Our main statement is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
be the natural birational map between two
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial birational contractions of a Mori Dream Space
Y
. Then there exists a sharp geometric realization of
\phi
that is a Mori Dream Space.

The proof relies on the fact that a geometric realization can be defined as a projectivization of a certain subring of the Cox ring of
Y
; in particular, we may obtain it as a particular type of birational contraction –called
unpruning
– of a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle over a contraction of
Y
.
This idea allows us to present the construction of geometric realizations in an explicit way.

A substantial part of the paper has been devoted to the case of geometric realizations of
toric
birational transformations; in this case, our construction can be rephrased in the language of combinatorics and convex geometry.
We present a number of software tools –programmed in the
SageMath
system– to construct and analyze geometric realizations. These tools enable further applications, such as explicitly constructing toric geometric realizations for maps between torically embedded Mori Dream Spaces. All the scripts presented in this work, and some other useful functions constructed for dealing with toric varieties, have been made available at
[
5
]
.

2.1.
Notation

We work over the field of complex numbers. By a
polarized pair
we mean a pair
(X,L)
, where
X
is a normal projective variety and
L
is an ample line bundle on
X
.
A birational map
\phi:X\dashrightarrow Y
is a
small modification
if it is an isomorphism over an open subset whose complement has codimension at least
2
. If
X
and
Y
are
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial, such a map is called a
small
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial modification
(SQM, for short).
By a
flip
we mean a
D
-flip as in
[
25
]
. The symbol
Y_{\pm}
represents two varieties,
Y_{-}
and
Y_{+}
, sharing a common property.
Given a normal projective variety
X
, and an effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, we denote the
section ring of
D
as
R(X;D)=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(X,mD)
. A normal,
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial projective variety
X
is called a
Mori Dream Space
(MDS for short) if

(1)

the Picard group
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
of
X
is finitely generated;

(2)

the nef cone
{\mathop{\rm Nef}\nolimits}(X)
is generated by classes of finitely many semiample divisors;

(3)

there exists a finite number of SQMs
f:X\dashrightarrow X_{i}
, for
i=0,\ldots,k
, such that every
X_{i}
satisfies
(\ref{item:2})
and

{\mathop{\rm Mov}\nolimits}(X)=\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}f^{*}{\mathop{\rm Nef}\nolimits}(X_{i}).

Let
X
be a MDS and let
D_{1},D_{2}
be two effective
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisors. The divisors
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
are
Mori equivalent
if
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(X;D_{1})\simeq\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(X;D_{2})
. A
Mori chamber
in
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(X)
is the closure of a Mori equivalence class whose interior is open in
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(X)
.

2.2.
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on projective varieties

We will denote by
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
the complex
1
-dimensional algebraic torus.
We recall the basic background on
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions, referring to
[
19
,
3
]
and the references therein for more details.
Consider a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a normal projective variety
X
. The fixed point locus
X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
can be decomposed into fixed connected components, i.e.
X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}=\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}Y
, where
{\mathcal{Y}}
denotes the set of connected fixed components.

For any
x\in X
, the orbit map can be extended to a morphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\times X\to X
, that is, there exist
\lim_{t\to 0}tx,\lim_{t\to\infty}tx
. Moreover, for any
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
we define the
Białynicki-Birula cells
as

X^{+}(Y)=\{x\in X\mid\lim_{t\to 0}tx\in Y\},\quad X^{-}(Y)=\{x\in X\mid\lim_{t\to\infty}tx\in Y\}.

As a corollary of the famous result of Białynicki-Birula (see
[
8
]
), there exists a unique fixed connected component
Y_{-}
(resp.
Y_{+}
) such that
X^{-}(Y_{-})
(resp.
X^{+}(Y_{+})
) is dense. We respectively call
Y_{-}
and
Y_{+}
the
sink
and
source
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
. We refer to
Y_{\pm}
as the
extremal fixed point components
of the action; the remaining connected fixed components will be called
inner
, and their set will be denoted by
{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}
.

Let
L
be an ample line bundle on
X
: by
[
15
, Proposition 2.4 and subsequent Remark]
,
L
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-linearizable, hence we can consider the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)
. For any
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on the fibers of
L
over
Y
with a fixed weight
\mu_{L}(Y)\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
. The induced map
\mu_{L}:{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
is called the
weight map
, and
\mu_{L}(Y)
is called the
critical value
of
L
on
Y
. Let
a_{0},\ldots,a_{r}
be the different critical values of a linearization of
L
, and order them as
a_{0}<a_{1}<\ldots<a_{r}
. The integer
r
and the difference
\delta:=a_{r}-a_{0}
are respectively called the
criticality
and the
bandwidth
of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
. One may show that, since
L
is ample, we have that
a_{0}=\mu_{L}(Y_{-})
,
a_{r}=\mu_{L}(Y_{+})
. Notice that two linearizations of
L
differ by a character, hence
r
and
\delta
are independent of the chosen linearization.

A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is
equalized at

Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
if for every point
x\in(X^{-}(Y)\cup X^{+}(Y))\setminus Y
the isotropy group of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action at
x
is trivial. A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is called
equalized
if it is equalized at every component
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.

2.3.
Geometric quotients and the associated birational map of a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action

We refer to
[
18
, §2.2]
for details. Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
act on a polarized pair
(X,L)
. Set
{\mathcal{A}}=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\HH^{0}(X,mL)
, and let
\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}
be the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant subspace where
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts with weight
k
.

For any
\tau\in[a_{0},a_{r}]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
, consider the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(\tau):=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0,m\tau\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{m\tau}
. By
[
18
, Proposition 2.11]
, the
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(\tau)
is finitely generated, and

\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau):=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits{\mathcal{A}}(\tau)

is a projective GIT quotient of the action. If
\tau
is not a critical value, then the quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau)
is geometric, and parametrizes orbits of stable points of the open,
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant subset

X^{s}_{i}:=X\setminus\left(\bigsqcup_{\mu_{L}(Y)<\tau}X^{+}(Y)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\mu_{L}(Y)\geq\tau}X^{-}(Y)\right),

where
i
is such that
\tau\in(a_{i},a_{i+1})
.
If instead
\tau=a_{i}
, for a certain
i\in\{0,\ldots,r\}
, then the quotient
\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau)
is the semigeometric quotient of the set (of semistable points)

X\setminus\left(\bigsqcup_{\mu_{L}(Y)\leq a_{i}}X^{+}(Y)\sqcup\bigsqcup_{\mu_{L}(Y)\geq a_{i+1}}X^{-}(Y)\right).

For any
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in(a_{i-1},a_{i})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
, it holds that
\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau)\simeq\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau^{\prime})
; in particular we are allowed to define:

\mathcal{G}\!X_{i}:=\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau),\quad\mbox{for any }\tau\in(a_{i-1},a_{i})\cap{\mathbb{Q}}.

With this in mind, the geometric quotients
\mathcal{G}\!X_{-}:=\mathcal{G}\!X_{1}

\mathcal{G}\!X_{+}:=\mathcal{G}\!X_{r}

are called
extremal
, and the remaining geometric quotients
\mathcal{G}\!X_{i}
, for
i=2,\ldots,r-1
, are called
inner
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.6]

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
act on a polarized pair
(X,L)
with criticality
r
. Let
\tau_{-}<\tau_{+}
be two rational numbers in
[a_{0},a_{r}]
. The
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra

{\mathcal{A}}(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})=\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{k=m\tau_{-}}^{m\tau_{+}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}

is finitely generated, and
X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits{\mathcal{A}}(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
is a normal projective
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-variety, equivariantly birational to
X
, with sink
\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau_{-})
and source
\mathcal{G}\!X(\tau_{+})
.

Again, for rational numbers
\tau_{\pm}
that are not critical values of the action, the varieties
X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
depend only on the particular intervals between critical points containing them, so that it makes sense to denote:

X_{i,j}:=X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\tau_{-}\in(a_{i-1},a_{i}),\quad\tau_{+}\in(a_{j-1},a_{j}).

Definition 2.2
.

With the above notation, we will say that the varieties
X_{i,j}
are called
prunings of

X
. The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant birational maps
X\dashrightarrow X_{i,j}
are called
pruning maps
, and their inverses
unpruning maps
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
is of
B-type
if there exist isomorphisms
\mathcal{G}\!X_{-}\simeq\mathcal{G}\!X(a_{0})\simeq Y_{-}
,
\mathcal{G}\!X_{+}\simeq\mathcal{G}\!X(a_{r})\simeq Y_{+}
.

As already observed (cf.
[
3
, Remark 2.3.29]
), one may always assume that a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
is of B-type, up to performing a pruning of
X
at
\tau_{-}\in(a_{0},a_{1}),\tau_{+}\in(a_{r-1},a_{r})
.

Definition 2.4
.

A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
is called a
bordism
if it is of B-type, and
\overline{X^{\pm}(Y)}
do not contain a divisor, for every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
act on a polarized pair
(X,L)
with criticality
r
. Since
\bigcap_{i=0}^{r-1}X^{s}_{i}\neq\emptyset
, there exists a birational map

\psi\colon\mathcal{G}\!X_{-}\dashrightarrow\mathcal{G}\!X_{+},

called the associated birational map, which factors through the inner geometric quotients

(1)

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{-}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{2}}
\textstyle{\ \ldots\ \ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{r-3}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{r-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi_{r-1}}

If the action is a bordism, then the above maps are isomorphisms in codimension one. If we assume in addition

{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial and the

2.4.
Toric varieties

We start by setting the notation we will use for toric varieties, and refer to
[
12
]
for details. We have made use of the software system
SageMath
, that includes a number of interesting tools to handle toric varieties; we refer the interesting reader to
[
1
]
for the corresponding documentation.

Given an algebraic torus
T
, we denote by
\mathrm{M}(T)
(resp.
\mathrm{N}(T)
) the lattice of characters (resp.
1
-parameter subgroups) of
T
, and set
\mathrm{M}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathrm{M}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
(resp.
\mathrm{N}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathrm{N}(T)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{R}}
). The symbol
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the nondegenerate bilinear pairing between
\mathrm{M}(T)
and
\mathrm{N}(T)
, which extends to a pairing between
\mathrm{M}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathrm{N}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Given an
n
-dimensional projective toric variety
X
, we denote by
\Sigma\subset\mathrm{N}(T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}

its associated fan, and write
X=X(\Sigma)
.
We denote by
\Sigma(1)
the set of
rays
of
\Sigma
, that is the set of
1
dimensional cones of
\Sigma
. We recall that every ray
\rho
is associated with a
T
-invariant prime divisor
D_{\rho}
, and we abuse notation by identifying a ray
\rho
with its primitive generator in the lattice. The fan of a projective toric variety is
complete
, that is, the cones of
\Sigma
span
\mathrm{N}(T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}
. We will usually consider the case of toric varieties whose fans are
simplicial
, that is, such that the generators of every cone of
\Sigma
are linearly independent; this is equivalent to say that the variety
X(\Sigma)
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial.

Let
X(\Sigma)
be a a complete toric variety and let
\Sigma(1)=\{\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{k}\}
be the set of rays of the fan
\Sigma
. Let
b=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{k})
be a vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
, and let
D=\sum_{i=1}^{k}b_{i}D_{\rho_{i}}
be a
T
-invariant
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Cartier divisor on
X
. The
moment polytope

P_{D}
associated with the pair
(X,{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(D))
is

P_{D}=\{m\in\mathrm{M}(T)_{{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\langle\rho_{i},m\rangle+b_{i}\geq 0,\quad i=1,\ldots,k\}.

We will essentially use moment polytopes of
big
divisors, which represent birational contractions of toric varieties.

The
SageMath
system contains a number of powerful tools to deal with toric varieties and associated combinatorial/convex-geometric objects, such as moment polytopes. Besides the scripts already included in the current version of

•

•

•

Finally, we will need some tools that provide information about the action of a

3.
Geometric realizations of birational maps

Let us start from a birational map

Definition 3.1

\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{Y_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{Y_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{2}}
\textstyle{\ldots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{s-3}}
\textstyle{Y_{s-2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{s-1}}
\textstyle{Y_{s}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

as a composition of birational maps

•

If moreover the maps

Remark 3.2
.

If the map
\phi
is a small
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial modification between two Mori Dream Spaces, then a directed resolution of

always exists. We
identify
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(Y_{-})
with
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(Y_{+})
, and we consider classes of Cartier divisors

such that

and

is ample on

. The segment joining

and

in

intersects a finite number of facets of the Mori chambers of

, that correspond to small modifications

of

, and that provides the directed factorization of

.
Moreover, by taking

general in the ample cones of

, the factorization that we get will be sharp.

We want to construct
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions that have

as associated birational map. More concretely, we recall the following concept, that has been introduced in

]
:

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\phi:Y_{-}\dashrightarrow Y_{+}
be a birational map between normal projective varieties. A

of

is a normal projective variety

, endowed with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of B-type, whose associated birational map (see Proposition

3.1.
Geometric realizations of maps of dream type

The problem of existence of geometric realizations of small
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial modifications has been dealt with in
[

]
. In this section we will briefly recall the results presented there.

Definition 3.4
.

Let

be a small modification between normal projective varieties

. The map

is
of dream type
if there exist effective Cartier divisors

on

(1)

(2)

(3)

The following statement shows the existence of geometric realizations under the dream type assumption introduced above.

Theorem 3.5
.

[

, Theorem 4.1, Corollary 4.10]

Let

be a small modification of dream type. Then there exists a geometric realization of
\phi
which is a bordism equalized at the sink and the source.

Roughly speaking, the geometric realization

of a small modification provided by the above statement is constructed by considering an appropriately chosen projective GIT
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-quotient of the spectrum of a multisection ring

. It comes with a linearization on an ample line bundle

•

R(X;L)
is a graded subalgebra of

.

•

The restrictions of

to the sink and the source are isomorphic to multiples of
A,B
, respectively.

•

Denoting by
a_{0}=0<a_{1}<\dots<a_{r}=\delta
the critical values of the action on

–so that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on

has bandwidth

and criticality

–, the cone
{\mathcal{C}}=\langle L_{\mid Y_{-}},L_{\mid Y_{-}}-\delta Y_{-\mid Y_{-}}\rangle
admits a decomposition

, where

.

In other words, if
\tau_{i}\in(a_{i-1},a_{i})

\mathcal{G}\!X_{i}=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits(R(Y_{-};L_{\mid Y_{-}}-\tau_{i}Y_{-\mid Y_{-}}))

The geometric realization

provides then a factorization of

\textstyle{Y_{-}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{1}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\ldots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi_{s-3}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{s-2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathcal{G}\!X_{s}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y_{+}.}

As already explained in Proposition

, we may then consider the pruning

\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\bigoplus_{m\tau_{-}\leq k\leq m\tau_{+}}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k}\subset R(X;L),

This construction yields the following idea –that we will use later on in situations different from the one of small

3.3.
When are geometric realizations Fano?

Let us observe that, in the situation of Setup
3.6
, with the notation of the previous section, a geometric realization
X
of the birational map
\phi
is constructed as a birational contraction of a
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle
W={\mathbb{P}}({\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(H))
. The anticanonical divisor of
W
is:

-K_{W}=D_{-}+D_{+}-\pi^{*}K_{Y}.

By hypothesis,
Y
is an MDS; we will further assume in this section that
Y
is (
{\mathbb{Q}}
-Gorenstein)
Fano
, in the sense that its anticanonical divisor is ample.

Remark 3.11
.

Note that if
Y
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial Fano variety with log terminal singularities, then the MDS condition of Setup
3.6
follows from
[
9
, Corollary 1.3.1]
.

By the above formula, if
-K_{Y}
is ample, then
-K_{W}
is effective, the Kodaira–Iitaka dimension of
-K_{W}
is at least
\dim Y=\dim X-1
, and equality holds when
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(W;-K_{W})=Y
. If this is not the case, i.e., if
-K_{W}
is big, then a birational contraction of
W
will be Fano, as well. It then makes sense to ask whether this birational contraction can be obtained as an unpruning of
W
, so that it will be a geometric realization of a birational map
\phi
between two birational models
Y_{\pm}
of
Y
.

Note that we may write
-K_{W}=2L-\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+H)=2(L-\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+H)/2)
, and
L-\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+H)/2
is the tautological divisor of the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-twisted bundle
({\mathcal{O}}_{Y}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(H))\langle-\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+H)/2\rangle
. Now this is big if –up to change of sign of
H
–

-K_{Y}-H\mbox{ is big, and }-K_{Y}+H\mbox{ is effective.}

For simplicity, we will consider the case in which both
-K_{Y}\pm H
are big, and belong to the interior of the Mori chambers of
\overline{{\mathop{\rm Eff}\nolimits}(W)}
.

In the case in which
-K_{Y}\pm H
are ample, then
W
is a Fano
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
-bundle; then the arguments here can be considered as an extension of the results on Fano bundles, which have been extensively studied in the literature as a way of constructing Fano varieties (see, for instance
[
17
,
22
,
24
,
23
,
2
]
).

For an integer
m
large enough,
-(mK_{Y}+H)
is ample in
Y
, so that the divisor

L^{\prime}:=L-\pi^{*}(mK_{Y}+H)
is ample in
W
. Moreover we may write:

\begin{split}L^{\prime}+(m-1)D_{-}+mD_{+}&=L-\pi^{*}(mK_{Y}+H)+(m-1)(L-\pi^{*}H)+mL=\\
&=m(2L-\pi^{*}(K_{Y}+H))=-mK_{W};\end{split}

this tells us that the unpruning
X:=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(W;L^{\prime}+(m-1)D_{-}+mD_{+})
is equal to
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(W,-K_{W})
which is a Fano variety.

Geometric realizations of birational maps between MDSs

In this section we will consider the problem of effectively constructing geometric realizations of birational transformations between Mori Dream Spaces, by means of our previous construction of toric geometric realizations. We will start by recalling some background facts about Mori embeddings in toric varieties.

5.1.
Mori embeddings

We refer the interested reader to
[
14
, Section 2]
for details. Let
Y
be an MDS,
\rho
be the Picard number of
Y
, and
{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(D_{1}),\ldots,{\mathcal{O}}_{Y}(D_{\rho})
be a basis of
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y)_{\mathbb{Q}}
.
We define the
Cox ring
of
Y
(with respect to the choice of
D_{1},\dots,D_{\rho}
) as the graded ring

R:=R(Y;D_{1},\dots,D_{\rho})=\bigoplus_{(m_{1},\dots,m_{\rho})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rho}}\HH^{0}\left(Y,\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}m_{i}D_{i}\right)

where the ring structure is induced by the multiplication of sections. The group
T:=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits({\mathbb{Z}}^{\rho},{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
is a
\rho
-dimensional torus, and has a natural action on
R
defined as follows: given
L=\sum_{i=1}^{\rho}m_{i}D_{i}
,
s\in\HH^{0}(X,L)
, and
t\in T
, we set
t\cdot s=t^{L}s
. Moreover, recall (cf.
[
14
, Proposition 2.9]
) that the variety
Y
is a GIT quotient of
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)
by the
T
-action. We will elaborate on this later in this section.

Since
Y
is an MDS,
R
is generated as a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra by a finite number of homogeneous elements
x_{j}\in\HH^{0}(Y,L_{j})
,
L_{j}\in\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\rho}{\mathbb{Z}}D_{i}
,

j=1,\dots,N
. Hence there exists a surjective morphism

\pi:{\mathdutchcal R}:={\mathbb{C}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]\to R,

which corresponds to an inclusion
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)\hookrightarrow\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits({\mathdutchcal R})={\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.
By definition of
{\mathdutchcal R}
, we may extend the action of the Picard torus on
R
to a
T
-action on the polynomial ring
{\mathdutchcal R}
, defined as
t\cdot x_{j}=t^{L_{j}}x_{j}
. Moreover, since
\pi
is
T
-equivariant, so is the inclusion
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)\hookrightarrow\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits({\mathdutchcal R})={\mathbb{C}}^{N}
.

Let
D\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y)
be an ample line bundle on
Y
, and consider the linearization of the trivial bundle over
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}
induced by
D
. By restriction, we get a linearization on the trivial bundle over
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)
, so that
Y
can be retrieved as the corresponding GIT-quotient of (the semistable set of)
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)
:

Lemma 5.1
.

[
14
, Proposition 2.9]

The GIT quotient
\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}^{D}T
with respect to the linearization induced by
D
is equal to
\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits R(Y;D)\simeq Y
.

We may also use
D
to linearize the trivial line bundle on
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}=\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits({\mathdutchcal R})
; the corresponding GIT quotient
{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}^{D}T
will be a toric variety, and we get an embedding (called
Mori embedding
):

Y=\mathop{\rm Spec}\nolimits(R)\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}^{D}T\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{N}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}^{D}T.

More precisely, we may state that:

Lemma 5.2
.

[
14
, Proposition 2.11]

If
D
is an ample divisor in
Y
whose class is a general element in the ample cone of
Y
, then the GIT quotient
{\mathdutchcal Y}:={\mathbb{C}}^{N}\mathbin{/\mkern-6.0mu/}^{D}T
is a quasi-smooth projective toric variety, and we have an embedding
Y\hookrightarrow{\mathdutchcal Y}
. Furthermore:

(1)

the restriction
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}({\mathdutchcal Y})\to{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(Y)
is an isomorphism;

(2)

the isomorphism of (
1
) sends
\overline{{\mathop{\rm Eff}\nolimits}({\mathdutchcal Y})}
bijectively to
\overline{{\mathop{\rm Eff}\nolimits}(Y)}
;

(3)

every rational contraction of
Y
extends to a toric rational contraction of
{\mathdutchcal Y}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that a toric projective variety is quasi-smooth if its fan is simplicial; equivalently, it is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial.
In the above construction it is not true in general that
{\mathop{\rm Nef}\nolimits}({\mathdutchcal Y})={\mathop{\rm Nef}\nolimits}(Y)
. In fact, the ample chamber of
Y
in
{\mathop{\rm N^{1}}\nolimits}(Y)=\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits(T)_{\mathbb{R}}

may contain a finite number of Mori chambers of
{\mathdutchcal Y}
. In other words,
{\mathdutchcal Y}
depends on the particular choice of
D
, and it is only
{\mathbb{Q}}
-factorial for the general element of the ample cone.

(c)

{\mathdutchcal Y}

By Lemma
5.2
the
{\mathbb{Q}}

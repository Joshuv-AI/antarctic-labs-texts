1.
introduction

Let
K
be a field and let
R=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
with
m\geq 2
. Give
R
the standard grading. Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of height
g
. The local cohomology modules

are graded. In this paper we study graded components of
H^{g}_{I}(R)
. There are two special cases. The first is when
I=\mathfrak{m}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
. In this case we know that
H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)=E(m)
where
E
is the
R
-module consisting of inverse polynomials in
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
. In particular we have
H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)_{n}=0
for
n\geq-m+1
and
\dim_{K}H^{m}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)_{n}
is finite for all
n
. The other extreme case is when
I=0
.
Then
H^{0}_{0}(R)=R
. Trivially
H^{0}_{0}(R)_{n}=0
for
n<0
and
\dim_{K}H^{0}_{0}(R)_{n}
is finite for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Apart from these two special cases we prove

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
R=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
with
m\geq 2
. Let
I
be a homogeneous ideal of height
g
. Assume
1\leq g\leq m-1
.
Suppose
H^{i}_{I}(R)\neq 0
for some
i\geq 0
. Then

(1)

H^{i}_{I}(R)_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\leq-m
.

(2)

if
\operatorname{Supp}H^{i}_{I}(R)\neq\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\}
then
H^{i}_{I}(R)_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

\dim_{K}H^{g}_{I}(R)_{n}
is infinite for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We note that Lyubeznik proved his fundamental results on local cohomology for a considerably larger class of functors which are now known as Lyubeznik functors. For definition of graded Lyubeznik functors see
2.2
. We show

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
R=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
with
m\geq 2
. Let
\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}
be a graded Lyubeznik functor. Assume there exists a non-zero graded ideal
J
of
R
such that
\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)
is
J
-torsion. Assume
\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)\neq 0
. Then

(1)

\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\leq-m
.

(2)

if
\operatorname{Supp}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)\neq\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\}
then
\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)_{n}\neq 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

We note that the assumption that there exists a non-zero graded ideal
J
such that
\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)
is
J
-torsion is satisfied by a large class of graded Lyubeznik functors. For instance it is satisfied by

where

are non-zero graded ideals in
R
. It is also satisfied by

where

is a non-zero graded ideal and

We note that if

for some

. In this case we know the graded components of

. A natural question is whether

is infinite for all

if
\operatorname{Supp}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)\neq\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\}
. We prove this in characteristic zero. We show

Theorem 1.3
.

(with hypotheses as in
1.2
) Further assume
\operatorname{char}K=0
.
If

\operatorname{Supp}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)\neq\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\}
then
\dim_{K}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)_{n}
is infinite for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

As an easy consequence we get

.

(with hypotheses as in
1.2
) Further assume
\operatorname{char}K=0
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

\dim_{K}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)_{n}

(2)

\operatorname{Supp}\mathbf{\mathcal{T}}(R)=\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\}

Graded Lyubeznik functors:

In this subsection, we define graded Lyubeznik functors. We say

-modules:

(1)

H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i}(R)\longrightarrow H_{Y^{\prime\prime}}^{i}(R)\longrightarrow H_{Y}^{i}(R)\longrightarrow H_{Y^{\prime}}^{i+1}(R).

Definition 2.3
.

A graded Lyubeznik functor

\mathcal{T}
is a composite functor of the form
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{1}\circ\mathcal{T}_{2}\circ\ldots\circ\mathcal{T}_{k}

\mathcal{T}_{j}
is either
H_{Y_{j}}^{i}(-)
, where
Y_{j}
is a homogeneous locally closed subset of
\text{Spec}(R)
or the kernel of any arrow appearing in (
1
) with
Y^{\prime}=Y_{j}^{\prime}
and
Y^{\prime\prime}=Y_{j}^{\prime\prime}
, where
Y_{j}^{\prime}\subset Y_{j}^{\prime\prime}
are two homogeneous closed subsets of
\text{Spec}(R)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}]
be standard graded with

K
an infinite field.
Let
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
be a non-zero graded
R
-module. If
\operatorname{char}K=p>0
assume
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
is a graded
F_{R}
-finite module. If
\operatorname{char}K=0
assume
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
is a graded generalized Eulerian, holonomic

-module.
If
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n}=0
for

then
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}=R^{s}
for some
s
.

Proof.

The proof of the result both when
\operatorname{char}K=p
and when
\operatorname{char}K=0
are analogous. We will prove the result when
\operatorname{char}K=p
.
We note that as
M_{-1}=0
, it follows from
2.8
that
\ell(M_{n})
is finite for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We prove the result by induction on
m
. We first consider the case when
m=1
.
We have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}(-1)\xrightarrow{X_{1}}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow 0.

Note as
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{-1}=0
we get
H_{1}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})_{0}=0
. By
2.9
(2) it follows that
H_{1}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})=0
. So
X_{1}
is
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
-regular. Also as
H_{0}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})
is concentrated in degree zero it follows that

\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{0}=H_{0}(X_{1},\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})_{0}
and we have isomorphisms
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{i-1}\xrightarrow{X_{1}}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
. As
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{0}
is a finite dimensional
K
-vector space it follows that

\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}=R^{s}
where
s=\ell(M_{0})
.

Now assume that
m=r\geq 2
and the result is proved when
m=r-1
.
Let
\mathfrak{m}=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})
and consider
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})
. We note that if
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})\neq 0
then it is a finite direct sum of
E(m)
. But
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n}=0
for
n<0
. This implies
E(m)=0
which is a contradiction. Thus
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathbf{\mathcal{M}})=0
. It follows that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
. Recall
\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
is a finite set. Set
V=KX_{1}\oplus KX_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus KX_{m}
. As
K
is infinite there exists

\xi\in V\setminus\bigcup_{P\in\operatorname{Ass}_{R}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}}P\cap V.

By a homogeneous linear change of variables we may assume
\xi=X_{m}
. Set
\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}=\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}/X_{m}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
and
S=R/(X_{m})
. Then
\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}
is a
F_{S}
-finite module.
So we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}(-1)\xrightarrow{X_{m}}\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}\rightarrow 0.

As
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n}=0
for
n<0
it follows that
\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}_{n}=0
for
n<0
. By induction hypotheses we have
\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}=S^{s}
for some
s\geq 0
.
As
\ell(\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n})
is finite for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}_{n}=0
for
n<0
; it follows from
3.2
that
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}
is finitely generated as a
\mathcal{R}
-module. Note by graded Nakayama Lemma
\mathbf{\mathcal{N}}\neq 0
. So
s\geq 1
. It follows that
\mathbf{\mathcal{M}}=R^{s}
.
∎

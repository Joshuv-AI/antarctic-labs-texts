Future time perspectives and concerns among adolescents in 2022
Authors: Roxane Dumont, Elsa Lorthe, Viviane Richard, Andrea Loizeau, Klara M Posfay-Barbe, Rémy P Barbe, Silvia Stringhini, Idris Guessous
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractFuture time perspectives (FTP) and future-related concerns in adolescence remain underexplored. We aimed to identify factors associated with limited FTP and describe future-related concerns. Data were drawn from a population-based sample of adolescents aged 14–17 participating in the SEROCoV-KIDS cohort study, in Geneva, Switzerland (October 2022). Of 329 adolescents, 56/329 (17%) reported limited FTP; determinants included prior low mental well-being, limited social support, sexual minority identity, academic difficulties and excessive screen time. Adolescents’ main future-oriented concerns encompassed failure, education and climate change. These results underscore the need to address future perspectives/concerns among young people and implement interventions that strengthen adolescent resilience.Keywords: Adolescent Health, Epidemiology, Psychology

Introduction

Adolescents face noteworthy psychological and behavioural challenges as they navigate through rapid biological and social transformations potentially heightened by global issues like climate change, conflicts or the COVID-19 pandemic.1 Future time perspectives (FTP) and future-oriented concerns can be considered as indicators of present difficulties and predictors of upcoming outcomes.2

FTP refers to how individuals perceive the amount of time and possibilities they have left in their lives, either as boundless or limited. This concept, along with future-related concerns, carries considerable significance during adolescence, a critical time of formative growth. The socioemotional selectivity theory states that FTP influences how individuals structure their lives, interact and behave, particularly through the pursuit of social goals.3 An expanded FTP serves as a catalyst for hope and purpose, inspiring adolescents to foster personal growth, while developing resilience. Conversely, a limited FTP has the potential to diminish motivation and involvement, which can in turn exacerbate feelings of anxiety and concerns regarding the future.4

Despite being crucial aspects of adolescents’ well-being, FTP and future-related concerns have received very limited research attention. Our objective was twofold: (1) to identify characteristics associated with adolescents’ limited FTP and (2) to present key future-related concerns.

Method

The study involved participants from the SEROCoV-KIDS cohort, a population-based longitudinal study aiming to investigate the multidimensional impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on children and adolescents. Randomly selected participants (aged 6 months to 17 years) were recruited between December 2021 and April 2022 in Geneva, Switzerland. At baseline, both parents and adolescents (≥14) completed a comprehensive sociodemographic and psychosocial questionnaire. A subsequent October 2022 follow-up included the standardised Future Time Perspective Scale,2 as well as an open-label questions about their primary concerns regarding the future (online supplemental table S1). Logistic regressions were performed to compute adjusted ORs, and a word cloud was created to describe future concerns.

Results

Out of 505 SEROCoV-KIDS adolescents, 329 (65%) answered the follow-up questionnaire (mean age=15.1 years (SD 1.1), 53% girls) (online supplemental figure S1). Overall, 56/329 (17%) adolescents reported a limited FTP. This limited FTP was significantly associated with various factors related to poor mental well-being, including high psychological distress (aOR 8.93, 95% CI: 4.60 to 17.96), suicidal thoughts (aOR 5.35, 95% CI: 2.60 to 10.63), low social support (aOR 4.13, 95% CI: 2.19 to 7.77), identifying as a sexual minority (aOR 2.38, 95% CI: 1.08 to 5.06), high screen time at baseline (aOR 1.18, 95% CI: 1.08 to 1.29) and borderline significant with school difficulties (aOR 2.09, 95% CI: 0.96 to 4.33) (table 1).

Discussion

One out of six adolescents reported having a limited FTP, which was associated with a low mental well-being, inadequate social support, academic difficulties, identifying as a sexual minority and high screen time. This result aligns with prior research indicating that vulnerable adolescents facing mental well-being issues, motivational hurdles and obstacles often exhibit limited FTP, though its meaning can vary across cultures and age groups.5

Adolescents’ primary concerns provide insights into the apprehensions faced by Generation ‘Z’ Zoomers, including individual worries about education, career prospects and social connections, as well as broader concerns about environmental issues.6 In a rapidly changing postpandemic world, characterised by pressing challenges like global warming and transformative technologies, addressing these concerns is vital both for individual well-being and also as a public health emergency.

Despite limitations in sample size and causal interpretation, our population-based study, revealed that one in six adolescents have a limited FTP. These results underscore the need for more research on adolescent well-being, emphasising future perspectives and concerns, as well as interventions promoting resilience and fostering optimistic outlooks among the youth.

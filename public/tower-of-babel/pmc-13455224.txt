De novo genome assembly of the deepwater rice (Oryza sativa) cultivar Pin Gaew 56
Authors: Hoon Je Seong, Sung Un Huh, Tae-Ho Lee, Dongsu Choi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesWe present the de novo whole-genome assembly of a deepwater rice variety (Oryza sativa cv. Pin Gaew 56). Although the submergence escape response is a vital survival strategy in deepwater rice, current reference genomes lack sufficient genetic information to fully understand the molecular mechanisms behind this complex physiological process. Pin Gaew 56 (PG56) is a well-studied genetic resource with extensively documented physiological and molecular traits related to internode elongation under partial submergence. This study provides a cultivar-specific genomic resource for PG56 that can support further investigation of the genetic basis of the submergence escape response.Data descriptionA de novo whole-genome assembly of the deepwater rice cultivar PG56 was generated to support genomic studies of the submergence escape response. The final assembly has a total length of 407.7 Mb, comprising 138 scaffolds with an N50 of 32.5 Mb. Genome completeness assessed by BUSCO showed 94.5% complete BUSCOs, and continuity in LTR-rich regions was supported by an LTR Assembly Index of 28.2. Assembly quality was further supported by k-mer-based quality assessment and whole-genome synteny alignment against the MH63 reference genome. Together, these data provide a useful genomic resource for investigating the genetic mechanisms underlying internode elongation and flooding adaptation in deepwater rice.Keywords: Oryza sativa cv. Pin Gaew 56, Submergence, Draft genome assembly

Objective

The submergence escape response in deepwater rice involves rapid internode elongation, a process crucial for survival during prolonged flooding [1]. In today’s era of accelerating climate change, the frequency and severity of extreme rainfall events and unpredictable floods have risen significantly, posing a serious threat to global food security. Understanding the genetic resilience of rice varieties adapted to such conditions is more important than ever for maintaining stable agricultural production in flood-prone areas.

Despite its biological and strategic significance, the genomic data available in standard reference sequences is insufficient to fully explain the structural and functional variations responsible for this physiological trait [2, 3]. Pin Gaew 56 (PG56) has long been a key model for studying deepwater rice, with its physiological and molecular traits related to flooding tolerance already well established. However, the lack of a cultivar-specific genome resource has limited our ability to integrate these phenotypic data with their underlying genetic architecture.

The objective of this study is to provide a de novo scaffolded genome assembly of PG56 as a cultivar-specific genomic resource. By establishing this genomic resource for a variety known to exhibit the full range of submergence escape traits, we aim to provide the scientific community with essential genetic information necessary to develop climate-resilient crop varieties and to bridge the gap between physiological behaviors and their genomic basis [4, 5].

Data description

Seeds of Pin Gaew 56 (PG56) were obtained from the International Rice Research Institute (IRRI, Los Baños, Philippines) and have been maintained at Kunsan National University (Gunsan-si, Republic of Korea) since 2006. Fresh leaves from 20 individual 10-week-old plants were pooled for DNA extraction. Total genomic DNA was isolated using a modified cetyltrimethylammonium bromide (CTAB) method [6] followed by high-speed centrifugation [7] to remove polysaccharides and other contaminants. The DNA was purified by extraction with chloroform: isoamyl alcohol (24:1) and subsequently precipitated with isopropanol. Residual RNA contamination was removed by treatment with RNase A. Additionally, total RNA was extracted using TRIzol Reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and quantified using a NanoDrop ND-2000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific).

For long-read genome sequencing, PacBio Sequel sequencing was performed, yielding 32.4 Gb of subreads, corresponding to 79.6× coverage of the final 407.7 Mb assembly (Table 1, Data file 1) [8]. In parallel, Illumina HiSeq 2000 short-read sequencing produced 13.3 Gb of paired-end reads (2 × 101 bp, ~ 32.6×), which were used for base-level error correction (Table 1, Data file 2) [9]. After filtering and quality trimming, long-read assembly was carried out using FALCON-Unzip [10], followed by haplotig purging with Purge Haplotigs [11] and two rounds of polishing with Pilon (v.1.2.3) [12]. The assembly was subsequently scaffolded using RagTag (v.2.1.0) [13] with the Oryza sativa indica cultivar Minghui 63 (MH63) reference genome (GCA_001623365.2), resulting in a 407.7 Mb genome comprising 138 scaffolds with a scaffold N50 of 32.5 Mb (Table 1, Data file 3) [14]. Transposable elements (TEs) were annotated using the EDTA pipeline [15] with the rice7.0.0 repeat library, identifying major TE categories including Helitron, LINE, LTR, SINE, and TIR elements (Table 1, Data file 4 and 5) [16, 17]. The resulting TE annotation was used to soft-mask the PG56 genome prior to protein-coding gene prediction.

Genome quality and completeness were comprehensively evaluated based on gene content, k-mer profiles, and structural integrity. BUSCO v.6.0.0 analysis against the Embryophyta odb12 dataset demonstrated high gene-space completeness [18], with 94.5% complete, 2.8% fragmented, and 2.7% missing BUSCOs (Table 1, Data file 6) [19]. Merqury [20] analysis based on 21-mers showed a base-level completeness of 94.94% and a consensus assembly quality value of 28.08, corresponding to an estimated base-level error rate of 0.00155. GenomeScope2 [21] analysis further characterized the short-read 21-mer profile and estimated a k-mer-based haploid genome size of 347.2–348.7 Mb, with high homozygosity (99.17–99.26%), low heterozygosity (0.74–0.83%), and 132.0–132.6 Mb of repetitive content. Finally, assembly quality in LTR retrotransposon regions was evaluated using the LTR Assembly Index, which was 28.2, indicating substantial structural completeness in LTR retrotransposon regions [15].

To further assess large-scale structural integrity, whole-genome synteny analysis was performed against the O. sativa indica cultivar MH63 genome using NUCmer [22]. One-to-one filtered alignments showed clear chromosome-scale collinearity between the MH63 reference chromosomes and the 12 PG56 chromosome-scale scaffolds (Table 1, Data file 7) [23]. The alignment set comprised 10,235 blocks, with a cumulative aligned length of 313.2 Mb and a length-weighted mean identity of 99.38%. Each PG56 chromosome-scale scaffold aligned predominantly to a single MH63 chromosome, and the major alignment blocks formed continuous diagonal patterns, indicating no evidence of large-scale interchromosomal mis-scaffolding. Minor local reverse-orientation or off-pair alignments were observed, which may reflect repetitive, duplicated, or structurally complex regions.

To support gene prediction, 5.4 Gb of RNA-seq data were generated using an Illumina NovaSeq 6000 (Table 1, Data file 8) [24]. RNA-seq reads were quality-controlled using fastp [25] and aligned to the PG56 assembly with STAR [26]. Using the resulting BAM files as evidence, BRAKER3 [27] predicted 48,664 protein-coding gene loci and 52,667 mRNA transcripts on the TE soft-masked genome (Table 1, Data files 9 and 10) [28, 29]. Of the 52,667 predicted mRNA transcripts, 36,318 transcripts (68.96%) were functionally annotated by eggNOG-mapper [30] across eggNOG orthologous groups, COG, GO, KEGG, and Pfam categories (Table 1, Data files 11) [31]. InterProScan [32] provided representative protein signature annotations for 32,090 transcripts (60.93%; Table 1, Data files 12) [33], including 348 transcripts that were not annotated by eggNOG-mapper. In total, 36,666 transcripts (69.62%) were functionally annotated by at least one of the two approaches.

This genome assembly provides an improved genetic framework for PG56 relative to currently available rice reference genomes, particularly for the exploration of genomic regions associated with internode elongation, ethylene-responsive signaling, and submergence escape mechanisms characteristic of deepwater rice. The dataset is expected to facilitate downstream analyses, including comparative genomics, transcriptome integration, and candidate gene identification related to flooding adaptation.

Limitation

Although the PG56 genome assembly shows high completeness, contiguity, and chromosome-scale collinearity with the MH63 reference genome, it was generated using reference-guided scaffolding. Therefore, the assembly is appropriate for chromosome-scale analyses, but fine-scale structural variation and local gene order should be interpreted cautiously, particularly in repetitive or structurally complex regions. Further sequencing using ultra-long ONT and PacBio HiFi sequencing could further improve contiguity and facilitate gap closure. In addition, Hi-C–based chromatin conformation data could provide independent support for chromosome-scale scaffolding, anchoring, and orientation, and may facilitate future telomere-to-telomere assembly improvement. The predicted gene set should be regarded as a draft annotation. Further refinement using full-length transcript evidence, controlled submergence-condition transcriptomes, and manual curation will be required to improve isoform structures and strengthen functional interpretation of flooding adaptation.

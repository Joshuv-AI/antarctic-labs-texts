Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-Type and Laughlin-Type Argument for the Quantum Hall Effect in Lattice Fermions with Spiral Boundary Conditions
Masaaki Nakamura 1 and Masanori Yamanaka 2

Abstract
We derive the condition for the occurrence of the quantum Hall effect in two-dimensional lattice systems, expressed as ϕ ​ ν − ρ ∈ ℤ \phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z} , where ϕ \phi , ν \nu , and ρ \rho denote the magnetic flux, the Chern number, and the electron density, respectively. By employing spiral boundary conditions, which treat the system as an extended one-dimensional chain, this condition is obtained directly through a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis-type and Laughlin-type argument. This approach improves upon the preceding work based on conventional periodic boundary conditions, where the condition was derived indirectly containing redundant system-size dependence. The key to this approach is that the spatial directions of the external force and the response can be manipulated by a factor of the system size.



–Introduction–

The quantization of the Hall conductance in two-dimensional (2D)
electron systems, known as the quantum Hall effect (QHE), is one of the
most remarkable manifestations of topology in condensed matter physics.
The quantized Hall conductivity is expressed in terms of the Chern
number of the occupied bands
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
From the viewpoint of many-body theory, the quantization of the Hall
conductance can also be derived from Laughlin’s gauge
argument,
[
4
]
which explains the integer change of
polarization upon adiabatic flux insertion.
In lattice systems, the condition for the appearance of the QHE is
given by a Diophantine equation that relates the magnetic flux, the
Chern number, and the electron density.
[
1
,
5
]

On the other hand, a similar topological constraint was discussed by
Lieb, Schultz, and Mattis (LSM) for one-dimensional (1D) quantum spin
systems,
[
6
]
and later generalized to various correlated
systems
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
and higher
dimensions
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
. These approaches are closely related
through the concept of the modern theory of polarization introduced by
Resta
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. The theory of polarization
has also been applied to various
systems
[
20
,
21
]
, and also to the calculation
of topological numbers of 2D lattice systems by applying Laughlin’s
arguments of flux insertion
[
22
]
.

Lu, Ran and Oshikawa derived the conditions for the appearance of the
QHE in lattice systems with interactions by combining the LSM-type
argument with Laughlin-type argument as
[
23
,
24
]

\phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z},

(1)

where
\phi
,
\nu
, and
\rho
denote the magnetic flux, the Chern
number, and the electron density, respectively. In their theory based
on periodic boundary conditions (PBCs), the condition for QHE appears
indirectly passing through the intermediate relation which depends on an
artificial geometric parameter. Therefore, improvement of this proof is
desirable to eliminate this redundancy.

In this Letter, we reformulate the argument using
spiral
boundary conditions

(SBCs)
[
16
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
, which allow
a 2D system to be regarded as an extended 1D chain (see
Fig.
1
). By employing the LSM-type and Laughlin-type
arguments under the SBC, we derive the condition for QHE
(
1
)
directly
, without invoking any system-size
dependence. This formalism provides a unified and more transparent
understanding of the QHE from the standpoint of symmetry and topology.

In the rest of this Letter, we explain the argument by
Ref.
[
23
]
. Then we reformulate this argument based on SBCs and
derive Eq. (
1
). Throughout this letter, the electron
charge, the reduced Planck constant, and the lattice constant are set to
be unity as
e=\hbar=a=1
.

–Conventional periodic boundary conditions–

First, we discuss how the condition given by Eq. (
1
) is
derived following the discussion by Lu, Ran and Oshikawa
[
23
]
.
The Hamiltonian for the fermions in the 2D square lattice with

L_{1}\times L_{2}
sites under periodic boundary conditions is given
by
[
28
]

\mathcal{H}=-t\sum_{m,n}(c^{\dagger}_{m+1,n}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta_{m,n}^{1}}c^{\mathstrut}_{m,n}+c^{\dagger}_{m,n+1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta_{m,n}^{2}}c^{\mathstrut}_{m,n}+\mbox{H.c.})+V(\{n_{m,n}\}),

(2)

where
c^{\mathstrut}_{m,n}
(
c_{m,n}^{{\dagger}}
) is the annihilation
(creation) operator at
(m,n)
site,
t
is the hopping amplitude,

V(\{n_{m,n}\})
is the fermion interactions consists of the number
operator
n_{m,n}\equiv c_{m,n}^{{\dagger}}c_{m,n}^{\mathstrut}
. The gauge
field is introduced in the Landau gauge as

\theta^{1}_{m,n}=0,\qquad\theta^{2}_{m,n}=2\pi\phi m,

(3)

which give the magnetic flux
B=-\frac{2\pi\hbar}{ea^{2}}\phi

with

\phi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\Box}\theta_{m,n}=\frac{p}{q},

(4)

where
p
and
q
are coprime integers. The summation for the plaquette
is taken as shown in Fig.
2
(a),

\sum_{\Box}\theta_{m,n}=\theta_{m,n}^{1}+\theta_{m+1,n}^{2}-\theta_{m,n+1}^{1}-\theta_{m,n}^{2}.

(5)

The gauge field
\theta^{\alpha}

In the absence of a magnetic field (
\phi=0
), the Hamiltonian possesses
translational symmetry by one lattice spacing along both directions,

\mathcal{H}=T_{i}^{\mathstrut}\mathcal{H}T_{i}^{-1}
. For
\phi\neq 0
,
this translational symmetry is broken due to the position dependence of

\theta^{2}_{m,n}
and sublattice structure appears as shown in
Fig.
3
. Therefore,
L_{1}
is selected so that

L_{1}/q\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the other hand, a similar symmetry is recovered
as
\mathcal{H}=\tilde{T}_{i}^{\mathstrut}\mathcal{H}\tilde{T}_{i}^{-1}
by
introducing the magnetic translation operators

\tilde{T}_{1}=U_{2}^{L_{2}\phi}T_{1},\qquad\tilde{T}_{2}=T_{2}.

(6)

The polarization operator

U_{i}=\exp\left[{\rm i}\frac{2\pi}{L_{i}}\sum_{\bm{x}}x_{i}n_{\bm{x}}\right].

(7)

with
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
satisfies the relation

T_{1}U_{1}T_{1}^{-1}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}2\pi L_{2}\hat{\rho}}U_{1}.

(8)

where
\hat{\rho}
is the density operator, and
L_{2}
represents the

cross section
of the 2D system. As in the LSM argument for
d\geq 2
,
L_{2}
and
q
should be chosen to be coprime.

Sublattice structure of the system

L_{1}/q

Now we consider the quantum Hall effect in this lattice system. The
quantized Hall conductivity is characterized by the Chern number
\nu

and the density
\rho
as

\sigma_{xy}=\frac{e^{2}}{h}\nu,\qquad\rho=\frac{r}{q},

(9)

where
r
is an integer. Since the magnetic flux
\phi=p/q
induces a
sublattice structure with
q
sites as shown in
Fig.
3
, a commensurate filling is required to open an
energy gap. Thus, for the QHE where
\nu
is an integer, the following
condition must hold:

\phi\nu-\rho=n,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(10)

which is equivalent to the Diophantine equation

r=\nu p+nq.

(11)

The Chern number
\nu
can be obtained from Resta’s formulation of
polarization with flux insertion to the ground state
[
22
,
23
]

\ket{\Psi_{i}(0)}
as shown in Fig.
4
:

z(\Phi)=z(0){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\nu\Phi},\quad z(\Phi_{2})=\braket{\Psi_{2}(\Phi_{2})|U_{1}|\Psi_{2}(\Phi_{2})},

(12)

leading to

\nu=-\frac{1}{\Phi}\mathrm{Im\,ln\,}z(\Phi).

(13)

The adiabatic flux insertion is expressed as

\ket{\Psi_{i}(\Phi_{i})}=F_{i}(\Phi_{i})\ket{\Psi_{i}(0)},

(14)

with

F_{i}(\Phi_{i})=\mathcal{T}\exp\left[-{\rm i}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{i}(\Phi_{i}\tfrac{t}{T}){\rm d}t\right],

(15)

where
\mathcal{T}
denotes time ordering,
T
is a sufficiently long
period, and
\mathcal{H}_{i}(\Phi)\equiv U_{i}^{\Phi/2\pi}\mathcal{H}U_{i}^{-\Phi/2\pi}
.

Equation (
12
) then yields the operator relation

[F_{2}(\Phi)]^{-1}U_{1}F_{2}(\Phi)=U_{1}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\nu\Phi}.

(16)

This is also an expression of the Laughlin’s argument for QHE. Since

F_{i}(2\pi)
plays a similar role to
U_{i}
, we can introduce a magnetic
translation operator as an analogy of Eq. (
6
),

\bar{T}_{1}=F_{2}(2\pi L_{2}\phi)T_{1},

(17)

which satisfies

\mathcal{H}=\bar{T}_{i}^{\mathstrut}\mathcal{H}\bar{T}_{i}^{-1}
for

\phi\neq 0
. We then obtain

\bar{T}_{1}U_{1}\bar{T}_{1}^{-1}=U_{1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}2\pi L_{2}(\phi\nu-\rho)}.

(18)

Thus, following an LSM-type argument, the condition for

z(\Phi)\neq 0
becomes

\displaystyle z(\Phi)

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi_{2}(\Phi_{2})|\bar{T}_{1}U_{1}\bar{T}_{1}^{-1}|\Psi_{2}(\Phi_{2})}

(19)

\displaystyle=z(\Phi){\rm e}^{{\rm i}2\pi L_{2}(\phi\nu-\rho)}.

(20)

Hence,

L_{2}(\phi\nu-\rho)\in\mathbb{Z},

(21)

Since this must hold for arbitrary integer
L_{2}
, we finally obtain the
condition
\phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z}
which is precisely the condition
for the QHE given by Eq. (
1
). However, the appearance of
the artificial parameter
L_{2}
is undesirable, because
L_{2}
must be
chosen to be relatively prime to
q
so that a redundancy remains.

–Spiral boundary conditions–

Next, we reconsider the above discussion under spiral boundary
conditions (SBCs), where the Hamiltonian (
2
) is rewritten as
a 1D model with long-range hopping,

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=-t\sum_{j=1}^{L}(c^{\dagger}_{j+1}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta_{j}^{1}}c^{\mathstrut}_{j}+c^{\dagger}_{j+L_{1}}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}\theta_{j}^{2}}c^{\mathstrut}_{j}+\mbox{H.c.})+V(\{n_{j}\}),

(22)

where
c^{\mathstrut}_{j}
(
c_{j}^{{\dagger}}
) is the annihilation
(creation) operator at
j
th site of the extended 1D chain of SBCs, and

L\equiv L_{1}L_{2}
is the total number of lattice sites. The gauge field
is given by (see Fig.
2
(b))

\theta^{1}_{j}=0,\qquad\theta^{2}_{j}=2\pi\phi j.

(23)

Now we introduce the polarization operators in SBCs. For this purpose, we
reinterpret the polarization operators in PBCs (
7
). The primitive
vectors in the real space (see Fig.
1
(a)) and the
reciprocal lattice space of 2D systems as

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{1}

\displaystyle=(L_{1},0),

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{B}_{1}

\displaystyle=2\pi(\tfrac{1}{L_{1}},0),

(24a)

\displaystyle\bm{A}_{2}

\displaystyle=(0,L_{2}),

\displaystyle\bm{B}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi(0,\tfrac{1}{L_{2}}).

(24b)

Then these vectors satisfy the following relation,

\bm{A}_{i}\cdot\bm{B}_{j}=2\pi\delta_{ij}.

(25)

The polarization operators (
7
) are written using the above primitive
vectors in the reciprocal lattice space as

U_{i}=\exp\left[{\rm i}\sum_{\bm{x}}\bm{B}_{i}\cdot\bm{x}n_{\bm{x}}\right].

(26)

For SBCs as shown in Fig.
1
(b), the primitive vectors
are chosen so that they satisfy the relation (
25
),

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=(L_{1},-1),

\displaystyle\qquad\bm{B}^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=2\pi(\tfrac{1}{L_{1}},0),

(27a)

\displaystyle\bm{A}^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=(0,L_{2}),

\displaystyle\bm{B}^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\pi(\tfrac{1}{L_{1}L_{2}},\tfrac{1}{L_{2}}).

(27b)

The polarization operators in 2D systems with SBCs are introduced as

U^{\prime}_{i}=\exp\left[{\rm i}\sum_{\bm{x}}\bm{B}^{\prime}_{i}\cdot\bm{x}n_{\bm{x}}\right].

(28)

For
i=1
, due to the relation
\bm{B}_{1}^{\prime}=\bm{B}_{1}
,
U_{1}^{\prime}
is the same
operator as
U_{1}
. The representation in SBCs is given by the relation

\frac{2\pi jn_{j}}{L_{1}}=\frac{2\pi x_{1}n_{\bm{x}}}{L_{1}}\quad(\mathrm{mod}\,2\pi).

(29)

For
i=2
, the exponent part of the polarization operator is confirmed as
that of SBCs as

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{x}}\bm{B}^{\prime}_{2}\cdot\bm{x}n_{\bm{x}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{L_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{L_{2}-1}2\pi\frac{m+nL_{1}}{L_{1}L_{2}}n_{m,n}

(30)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{2\pi}{L}jn_{j},

(31)

where
j=m+nL_{1}
. Thus the polarization operators for SBCs are identified as

U_{1}^{\prime}=\exp\left[{\rm i}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{2\pi jn_{j}}{L_{1}}\right],\quad U_{2}^{\prime}=\exp\left[{\rm i}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\frac{2\pi jn_{j}}{L}\right].

(32)

In SBCs, the translation and polarization operators for different
directions are related as

T_{2}^{\prime}=T_{1}^{\prime L_{1}},\qquad U_{1}^{\prime}=U_{2}^{\prime L_{2}}.

(33)

Then, the counterpart of Eq.(
8
) becomes

T_{1}^{\prime}U_{2}^{\prime}T_{1}^{\prime-1}=T_{1}^{\prime}U_{1}^{\prime 1/L_{2}}T_{1}^{\prime-1}={\rm e}^{-{\rm i}2\pi\hat{\rho}}U_{2}^{\prime}.

(34)

We choose
U_{2}^{\prime}
rather than
U_{1}^{\prime}
here because it eliminates the

L_{2}
-dependence from this relation. This was introduced for an
extention of the LSM theorem to higher dimensions
[
16
]
. The
magnetic translation operators are

\tilde{T}_{1}^{\prime}=U_{2}^{\prime L_{2}\phi}T_{1}^{\prime}=U_{1}^{\prime\phi}T_{1}^{\prime},\qquad\tilde{T}_{2}^{\prime}=\tilde{T}_{1}^{\prime L_{1}}=T_{2}^{\prime}

(35)

which satisfy
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\tilde{T}_{i}^{\prime}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\tilde{T}_{i}^{\prime-1}
. Note that
L_{2}
-dependence in
\tilde{T}_{1}^{\prime}
is again removed.

The adiabatic flux insertion operator is introduced as in the same way
of Eq. (
15
) as

F_{i}^{\prime}(\Phi_{i})=\mathcal{T}\exp\left[-{\rm i}\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{H}_{i}^{\prime}(\Phi_{i}\tfrac{t}{T}){\rm d}t\right]

(36)

and the Laughlin-type relation becomes

[F_{1}^{\prime}(\Phi)]^{-1}U_{2}^{\prime}F_{1}^{\prime}(\Phi)=U_{2}^{\prime}{\rm e}^{+{\rm i}\nu\Phi}.

(37)

Since, in the system with SBCs, the insertion of flux

\mathcal{H}_{1}^{\prime}(\Phi_{1})
has the same effect of the flux

\mathcal{H}_{2}^{\prime}(L_{2}\Phi_{1})
due to the relation of
Eq. (
33
), the magnetic translation operator
corresponding to Eq. (
17
) is

\bar{T}_{1}^{\prime}=F_{2}^{\prime}(2\pi L_{2}\phi)T_{1}^{\prime}=F_{1}^{\prime}(2\pi\phi)T_{1}^{\prime}.

(38)

Hence, both Eqs. (
34
) and (
38
) become free of
L_{2}

dependence. This is the essential advantage of SBCs. Therefore, we
obtain the relation

\bar{T}_{1}^{\prime}U_{2}^{\prime}\bar{T}_{1}^{\prime-1}=U_{2}^{\prime}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}2\pi(\phi\nu-\rho)},

(39)

without any geometric parameters in contrast to Eq. (
18
). This
means that the LSM-type argument under SBCs leads to the QHE condition

\phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z}

directly
, without passing through the
intermediate relation
L_{2}(\phi\nu-\rho)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

–Summary and Discussion–

In this work, we have derived the condition for the appearance of the
quantum Hall effect (QHE) in 2D lattice systems
\phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z}
, by means of a Lieb-Schultz-Mattis (LSM)-type and
Laughlin-type argument. By introducing the
spiral boundary
conditions
(SBCs), we have successfully eliminated the artificial
dependence on the system width
L_{2}
that arises in the conventional
argument based on periodic boundary conditions (PBCs).
Under the SBC, the 2D lattice can be mapped onto an extended 1D chain,
so that explicit dependence on the cross section can be avoided, and the
effects of flux insertion in different directions can be treated on
equal footing. This construction provides a more transparent physical
picture of the quantization of the Hall conductance in lattice fermion
systems.

The key to this approach is that the spatial directions of the external
force and the response can be manipulated by a factor of the system size
as in Eqs. (
33
)-(
35
), and (
38
),
thereby allowing the flux insertion process to be represented without
introducing any redundant parameters. Consequently, the condition

\phi\nu-\rho\in\mathbb{Z}
emerges directly from the magnetic
translation symmetry, demonstrating that the quantization of the Hall
conductance can be understood purely from symmetry and topology, without
relying on explicit band-structure calculations.

Our approach unifies the LSM argument for interacting systems and
Laughlin’s gauge argument for the QHE, and provides a useful framework
for analyzing correlated topological phases in lattice models. Since
the present formalism is formulated entirely in terms of the many-body
wave function and the polarization operators, it can be
straightforwardly extended to systems with interactions, disorder, or
nontrivial lattice geometries. Applications to Chern insulators,
fractional Chern insulators, and topological superconductors under SBCs
will be interesting future problems.

Subrack lattice determines the derived length
Selçuk Kayacan

Abstract
We prove the following: If G G is a finite solvable group and H H is another group whose subrack lattice is isomorphic to the subrack lattice of G G , then H H is a solvable group and the derived length of H H coincides with the derived length of G G .

Introduction

A
rack

X
is a set with a binary operation
\triangleright\colon X\times X\to X
satisfying the following two conditions:

(A1)

for all
a,b,c\in X
we have
a\triangleright(b\triangleright c)=(a\triangleright b)\triangleright(a\triangleright c)
, and

(A2)

for all
a,c\in X
there exists a unique
b\in X
such that
a\triangleright b=c
.

If, further, the following axiom holds we say
X
is a
quandle
:

(A3)

for all
a\in X
we have
a\triangleright a=a
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
S
be a subset of
G
that is closed under the conjugation operation
a\triangleright b:=aba^{-1}
. The set
S
with the binary operation
\triangleright
form a quandle. We call those type of racks
conjugation racks
. The following three classes of conjugation racks deserve special attention:

•

the
group rack

(G,\triangleright)
,

•

the
conjugacy class rack

(C,\triangleright)
, where
C
is a conjugacy class of
G
, and

•

the
p
-power rack
(G_{p},\triangleright)
, where
p
is a prime dividing the order of
G
and
G_{p}
is the set of all elements of
G
whose order is a power of
p
.

The poset of subracks of a finite rack
X
ordered by inclusion form a lattice with unique maximum element
X
and unique minimum element
\emptyset
(see
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
). We call this lattice the
subrack lattice
of
X
and denote it by
\mathcal{R}(X)
.

The order complex of the subrack lattice of conjugation racks has interesting geometrical properties. For example, basic group theoretical arguments show that, for a group rack
G
the maximal elements of
\mathcal{R}(G)
are the union of all but one conjugacy classes of
G
and this fact can be used to prove that the order complex of
\mathcal{R}(G)\setminus\{G,\emptyset\}
is homotopy equivalent to a
(c-2)
-sphere, where
c
is the number of conjugacy classes of
G
(see
[
1
, Proposition 1.3]
). Further homotopy properties of the subrack lattices of conjugacy class racks and
p
-power racks are studied in
[
2
,
3
]
.

As is shown in
[
1
]
the subrack lattice
\mathcal{R}(G)
of a group rack
G
contains essential information about the group itself.

Theorem 1

(see
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
G
,
H
be finite groups satisfying
\mathcal{R}(G)\cong\mathcal{R}(H)
.

1.

If
G
is abelian, then
H
is abelian.

2.

If
G
is nilpotent, then
H
is nilpotent.

3.

If
G
is supersolvable, then
H
is supersolvable.

4.

If
G
is solvable, then
H
is solvable.

5.

If
G
is simple, then
H
is simple.

Moreover, the subrack lattice
H
is nilpotent of class
c
if
G
is nilpotent of class
c
(see
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
) and
H
is
p
-nilpotent if
G
is
p
-nilpotent and
\mathcal{R}(G)
satisfies a certain condition (see
[
4
, Theorem 4.5]
).

A group is called
solvable
if there is a subnormal series whose factor groups are all abelian. Equivalently, a group is solvable if its derived series terminates in the trivial group as the derived series descends faster than any other series with abelian quotients (see
[
5
, Lemma 5.22]
). The
derived length
of a solvable group
G
is the smallest
n
such that the
n
th higher commutator subgroup
G^{(n)}
is trivial. In this note we show that the results presented in
[
4
]
can also be used to prove that the derived length of a solvable group is determined by the subrack lattice of the corresponding group rack.

Theorem 2
.

If
G
is a finite solvable group with derived length
\ell
, and if
H
is a group such that
\mathcal{R}(G)\cong\mathcal{R}(H)
, then
H
is solvable with derived length
\ell
.

Proof of Theorem
2

Let
G
be a finite group, and
\mathsf{A}(G)
denote the set of all elements in the subrack lattice
\mathcal{R}(G)
satisfying the following property: An element
A\in\mathcal{R}(G)
belongs to
\mathsf{A}(G)
if and only if the interval
[\emptyset,A]
is a maximal Boolean algebra in
\mathcal{R}(G)
(that is, if
[\emptyset,B]
is a Boolean algebra for some
B\in\mathcal{R}(G)
containing
A
then
A=B
). One can easily observe that an element
A
of the subrack lattice
\mathcal{R}(G)
is a maximal abelian subgroup of
G
if and only if
A
belongs to
\mathsf{A}(G)
. Since the combinatorial structure of
\mathcal{R}(G)
determines the conjugacy classes of
G
, we can also characterize the maximal normal subgroups of
G
via the combinatorial properties of
\mathcal{R}(G)
.

Lemma 3

(see
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

For a finite group
G
, an element
N\in\mathcal{R}(G)
is a maximal normal abelian subgroup of
G
if and only if
N
is the union of all conjugacy classes of
G
that are contained by some fixed
A\in\mathsf{A}(G)
.

Given a subset
S
of
G
, we denote by
\langle S\rangle_{\mathsf{rk}}
the subrack of the group rack
G
generated by the elements in
S
.

Lemma 4

(see
[
4
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. Then any coset of
N
is a subrack of
G
. Moreover, for any subset
\{a_{1}N,\dots,a_{k}N\}
of
G/N
, the join of those cosets in
\mathcal{R}(G)
is the union of the cosets of
N
containing
\langle a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\rangle_{\mathsf{rk}}
.

Let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
. By Lemma
4
the subrack lattice of
\mathcal{R}(G/N)
is isomorphic to a subposet of
\mathcal{R}(G)
. Let
H
be a group whose subrack lattice is isomorphic to the subrack lattice of
G
. The following result will be used to construct an isomorphic copy of the poset
\mathcal{R}(G/N)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
in the subrack lattice
\mathcal{R}(H)
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{m}\}
and
\mathcal{Q}=\{Q_{1},\dots,Q_{m}\}
be some partitions of a set
S
of size
mn
into parts each having the same size
n
. Then, there is a bijection
f\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
such that

f(P_{i})\cap P_{i}\neq\emptyset\;\text{ for each }\;1\leq i\leq m.

Consider the
m\times m
zero-one matrix
A
whose
(i,j)
-th entry defined by

\displaystyle a_{ij}=\begin{cases}1&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if }\;P_{i}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset,\\
0&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For example, if
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
are the same partitions, then
A
would be a permutation matrix. By reordering the columns of
A
if necessary, one can obtain the identity matrix. In this case, the function
f\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
which maps
P_{i}
to
Q_{j}
where
i
-th column of
A
is replaced by the
j
-th column after reordering, satisfies the property
f(P_{i})\cap P_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on the number of parts. Obviously, the assertion holds when the partition consists of one element, i.e. when the size of
S
is
n
. Suppose the assertion holds for sets of size
(m-1)n
. We want to show that the assertion holds when the size of
S
is
mn
. There are two cases that we shall consider.

Case I:

P_{i_{0}}=Q_{j_{0}}
for some
1\leq i_{0},j_{0}\leq m
. Observe that the partitions
\mathcal{P}_{0}:=\mathcal{P}\setminus\{P_{i_{0}}\}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{0}:=\mathcal{Q}\setminus\{Q_{j_{0}}\}
are the partitions of a set of size
(m-1)n
and by the inductive hypothesis there exists a function
g\colon\mathcal{P}_{0}\to\mathcal{Q}_{0}
having the desired property. Hence, the function

\displaystyle f(P_{i})=\begin{cases}g(P_{i})&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if }\;i\neq i_{0}\\
Q_{j_{0}}&\hskip 9.24994pt\text{if }\;i=i_{0}\end{cases}

satisfies the property
f(P_{i})\cap P_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Case II:

P_{i}\neq Q_{i}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Fix two index values
i_{0}
and
j_{0}
such that
P_{i_{0}}\cap Q_{j_{0}}\neq\emptyset
. We construct a partition
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=\{Q^{\prime}_{1},\dots,Q^{\prime}_{n}\}
in the following way: Initially
Q^{\prime}_{j}=Q_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq m
. If
P_{i_{0}}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset
and
j\neq j_{0}
, we swap elements of
P_{i_{0}}\cap Q_{j}
with some elements of
Q_{j_{0}}\setminus P_{i_{0}}
in an arbitrary fashion to form
Q^{\prime}_{j}
and
Q^{\prime}_{j_{0}}
. Iterating this process at the final step
P_{i_{0}}
and
Q^{\prime}_{j_{0}}
would be the same sets. Then, by Case I, there exists a function
f^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
satisfying the property
f^{\prime}(P_{i})\cap P_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Observe that the induced function
f\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{Q}
would also have the same property as
f(P_{i_{0}})=Q_{j_{0}}
and
P_{i}\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset
whenever
P_{i}\cap Q^{\prime}_{j}\neq\emptyset
and
i\neq i_{0}
.
∎

A partition
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\dots,C_{m}\}
of a finite group
H
will be called an
hypothetical coset partition
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

(C1)

Each element of
\mathcal{C}
has the same number of elements.

(C2)

The element
C_{1}\in\mathcal{C}
is a union of some conjugacy classes of
H
and there is at least one central element in
C_{1}
.

(C3)

For a subset
I
of
[m]
the following implication holds: If
C_{I}=\{c_{i}\in C_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is an arbitrary set of representative elements, then the join of the elements
C_{i}
,
i\in I
, in
\mathcal{R}(H)
is the union of elements of
\mathcal{C}
containing
\langle C_{I}\rangle_{\mathsf{rk}}
.

Notice that the combinatorial properties of the subrack lattice
\mathcal{R}(H)
is enough to determine whether a given partition of
H
is a hypothetical coset partition. If
\mathcal{C}
is an hypothetical coset partition of
H
, we denote by
\mathsf{J}(\mathcal{C})
the subposet of
\mathcal{R}(H)
whose elements are the joins of the elements in
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Take an element
N\in\mathsf{A}(G)
. By Lemma
3
the subrack
N
is a maximal normal abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\alpha\colon\mathcal{R}(G)\to\mathcal{R}(H)
be an isomorphism. By Lemma
4
the images of the atoms of
\mathcal{R}(G/N)
under
\alpha
form a partition
\mathcal{A}
of
H
satisfying the conditions (C1), (C2), and (C3). Let
\mathcal{C}
be an hypothetical coset partition of
H
such that
C_{1}=\alpha(N)\in\mathcal{A}
. Then the poset
\mathsf{J}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}(G/N)\setminus\{\emptyset\}
as the join operation for the elements of
\mathcal{C}
is independent from the chosen representatives and, by Lemma
5
, there exists a set of representative elements for the partition
\mathcal{C}
such that those elements are also representatives for the images of cosets of
G/N
under
\alpha
.

Since
\alpha(N)
is a maximal normal abelian subgroup of
H
, similar arguments show that
\mathsf{J}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{R}(H/\alpha(N))\setminus\{\emptyset\}
. Repeating this process we obtain a series of subposets of
\mathcal{R}(G)
whose length is the length of a corresponding subnormal series with maximal abelian quotients for
G
. Since the isomorphic copies of those subposets gives a corresponding subnormal series with maximal abelian factor groups for
H
and since the above arguments are valid for any maximal normal abelian subgroup of the group in question, we see that
G
is solvable if and only if
H
is solvable and the derived length of
G
is same with the derived length of
H
.
∎

Remark
.

The above proof can be adapted to prove that the isomorphism type of the subrack lattice of a finite nilpotent group determines its nilpotence class (see
[
4
, Theorem 3.3]
). The difference is that rather than the maximal normal abelian subgroups we need to consider the center of the group which can be located within the subrack lattice.

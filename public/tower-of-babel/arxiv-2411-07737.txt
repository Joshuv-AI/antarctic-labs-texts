On Asymptotic Behavior of Extinction Moment of Critical Bisexual Branching Process in Random Environment
Zhiyanov A.P, Shklyaev A.V

Abstract
. We consider a critical bisexual branching process in a random environment generated by independent and identically distributed random variables. Assuming that the process starts with a large number of pairs N N , we prove that its extinction time is of the order ln 2 ⁡ N \ln^{2}N . Interestingly, this result is valid for a general class of mating functions. Among them are the functions describing the monogamous and polygamous behavior of couples, as well as the function reducing the bisexual branching process to the simple one. Keywords: limit theorem, extinction time, critical bisexual branching process, random environment, large initial number of particles.



1.
Introduction

Let
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}=\{\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots\}
be a sequence of independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables (r.v.), where the term
\eta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
denotes the random environment at moment
n
.
Also, consider the family
\{f_{\alpha}(s,t),\ \alpha\in\mathbb{R}\}
of probability generating functions, and introduce the mating function
L:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

Under this notation, we define a bisexual branching process in a random environment (BBPRE)
\{N_{n},\ n\geq 0\}
as a time-homogeneous Markov chain with transition probabilities depending on
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
by the rule:

\mathbf{P}\left(\left.N_{n+1}=j\,\right|N_{n}=i,{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\left.L\left(F_{n+1},M_{n+1},\eta_{n+1}\right)=j\,\right|\eta_{n+1}\right),

where
N_{0}=N
, and the distribution of the random pair
(F_{n+1},M_{n+1})
conditioned on
\eta_{n+1}
has the probability generating function
f_{\eta_{n+1}}(s,t)^{i},\ s,t\in[0,1]
.

More intuitively, the process
\{N_{n},\ n>0\}
can be defined as follows.
Let us consider a sequence
\{(F_{i,j},M_{i,j}),\ i,j>0\}
of two-dimensional random vectors taking values in
\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
For a fixed environment at moment
n
, we assume the pairs
\{(F_{n,j},M_{n,j}),\ j>0\}
to be conditionally independent and identically distributed with the corresponding probability generating function
f_{\eta_{n}}(s,t)
.
Also, the vectors
\{(F_{n,j},M_{n,j}),\ j>0\}
are assumed to be independent for different
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Under this notation, we define a BBPRE as the sequence

N_{0}=N,\ N_{n+1}=L\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}}F_{n+1,j},\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}}M_{n+1,j},\eta_{n+1}\right),\ n\geq 0.

(1)

This definition allows us to consider this process step by step using a biological analogy.
For a fixed moment
n
,
N_{n}
couples start to produce offspring.
The
j
th couple of
n
th generation produces
F_{n+1,j}
‘‘female’’ and
M_{n+1,j}
‘‘male’’ descendants, respectively.
Then, all new ‘‘female’’ and ‘‘male’’ descendants group in

N_{n+1}=L\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}}F_{n+1,j},\sum_{j=1}^{N_{n}}M_{n+1,j},\eta_{n+1}\right)

couples, which in turn, produce offspring.
Under this interpretation, the standard mating functions

L(x,y,z)=x\min(1,y);\ \ L(x,y,z)=\min(x,y\,d(z)),\ d:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}.

(2)

describe polygamous and monogamous (
d(z)=1
) mating schemes, respectively.

Bisexual branching processes are considered to be a natural modification of a standard Galton-Watson branching process.
They were first introduced in
[
1
]
for the standard mating functions (
2
).

In that study, the sufficient conditions for process extinction with probability one were provided.
Further advances in the extinction problem were made in
[
2
,
3
]
for
superadditive
mating functions.

Bisexual branching processes in a random environment were introduced in
[
4
]
,
where non-degeneracy conditions of such processes were provided.
In
[
5
]
, the previous results were generalized to the case where the mating function depends on the environment.
Finally, in
[
6
]
, an almost sure (a.s.) convergence rate of a normalized
supercritical
BBPRE was studied under the condition

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbf{E}\left(\frac{\sup_{k>0}k^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{n+1}\right|N_{n}=k\right)}{\inf_{k>0}\,k^{-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{n+1}\right|N_{n}=k\right)}\right)<\infty.

Unfortunately, it is too strict for the case of i.i.d. environments, since it implies that

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{1}\right|N_{0}=k\right)

does not depend on
k
.
We consider much weaker condition that the mating function can be approximated by a Lipschitz function.

Note that BBPRE can be considered a natural generalization of controlled branching processes.
Indeed, let

L(x,y,z)=h(x,z),

where
h:\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
is a measurable function.
Then, the corresponding BBPRE reduces to a controlled process.
For a comprehensive review of controlled branching process results, see
[
7
]
.

Denote by
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}\ |\ x\geq 0\}
, and let
\{N_{n},\ n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
be a BBPRE starting with
N_{0}=N
pairs.
Also, we will use the notation
\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\cdot\right)
and
\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\cdot\right)
instead of the conditional probability
\mathbf{P}\left(\left.\cdot\right|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right)
and expectation
\mathbf{E}\left(\left.\cdot\right|{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right)
, respectively.
We require the mating function
L(x,y,z)
to satisfy the following conditions:

C1)

L(x,y,z)
is
superadditive
, i.e., for any
x,y,u,v\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

L(x+u,y+v,z)\geq L(x,y,z)+L(u,v,z);

C2)

there is a function

g(x,y,z):\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},

that is Lipschitz with respect to
(x,y)
, i.e., there is a measurable function

\lambda(z):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}

such that for any
x,y,u,v\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}
,

\left\lvert g(x,y,z)-g(u,v,z)\right\rvert\leq\lambda(z)\left(\left\lvert x-u\right\rvert+\left\lvert y-v\right\rvert\right);

C3)

g(tx,ty,z)=tg(x,y,z)
for all
t\geq 0
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, and
z\in\mathbb{R}
;

C4)

there are a measurable function

\rho(z):\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{>0}

and a constant
0<\alpha<1
such that

\left\lvert L\left(x,y,z\right)-g\left(x,y,z\right)\right\rvert\leq\rho(z)\left(x+y\right)^{\alpha},\ x,y\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\ z\in\mathbb{R}.

Similar requirements on
L(x,y,z)
were introduced in
[
8
]
, where large deviations of BBPRE were studied.

Let
\varphi_{n}=F_{n,1}
and
\mu_{n}=M_{n,1}
be the offspring sizes of the first pair.
Denote by
\ln^{+}x=\max(0,\ln x)
,
x>0
.
Now, we introduce several technical conditions.

(5)

There is a constant
\beta>1
such that

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi^{\beta}_{1},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu^{\beta}_{1}

are finite a.s.

Remark 1
.

Without loss of generality, we assume
\alpha
from
C4
to satisfy the inequality
\alpha^{-1}<\beta
.
So, we will use the notation
\delta=\alpha^{-1}-1
, where
1<1+\delta<\beta
.

(6)

For all
n\geq 1

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\xi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\beta}<\infty

and

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\zeta_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\beta}<\infty,

where

\displaystyle\xi_{n}

\displaystyle=\ln g\big(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\big),\ \zeta_{n}=\ln^{+}\big(\omega^{1}_{n}+\omega^{2}_{n}+\omega^{3}_{n}\big),

\displaystyle\omega^{1}_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})+\rho^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n}),\ \omega^{2}_{n}=\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},

\displaystyle\omega^{3}_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\varphi_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\mu_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}.

Remark 2
.

Note that, due to Minkowski inequality and non-negativity of the terms included in the definition of
\omega_{n}^{1}
,
\omega_{n}^{2}
and
\omega_{n}^{3}
,

\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\zeta_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\beta}

is finite if the moments

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\lambda(\eta_{n})\right)^{1+\beta},\ \mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\rho(\eta_{n})\right)^{1+\beta},\

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right)^{1+\beta},\ \mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right)^{1+\beta},

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\varphi_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\right)^{1+\beta},\

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\ln^{+}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\mu_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\right)^{1+\beta}

are finite.

Under these conditions, we introduce the notation
\sigma^{2}=\mathrm{Var}\left(\xi_{n}\right)
and consider the random walk
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{n}
,
S_{0}=0
, associated with the BBPRE.
We require its increments to satisfy the condition

(7)

the process
N_{n}
is
critical
, i.e.,
\mathbf{E}\xi_{n}=0
.

Now, we are ready to introduce the extinction moment
\tau
of a BBPRE

\tau=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ \left|\ N_{n}=0\right.\},\ N_{0}=N.

An asymptotic behavior of
\tau
for a bisexual branching process with large initial number of pairs but without environment was provided in
[
9
]
for the
subcritical
case.
The
critical
case of a Galton-Watson branching process was studied in
[
10
]
, while the case with the environment was recently considered in
[
11
]
.

In this work we study asymptotic behaviour of
\tau
for a
critical
BBPRE starting with large initial number of pairs
N_{0}=N
(Theorem
1
).
In Section
2
we provide auxiliary lemmas used in the proof of Theorem
1
, while Section
3
contains the proof itself.

2.
Auxiliary results

Lemma 1

(Exercise 1.6.3 of
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\xi
be a r.v. with
\mathbf{E}\xi^{\alpha}<\infty
, where
\alpha\geq 1
.
Then,

n^{\alpha}\,\mathbf{P}\left(\xi>n\right)\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 2

(Minkowski inequality)
.

For any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\geq 0
and
\alpha\geq 1
, the inequality

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\right)^{\alpha}\leq n^{\alpha-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{\alpha}

holds.

Lemma 3

(Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequality,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\{\xi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of i.i.d. r.v. with
\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\xi_{n}\right\rvert^{\alpha}<\infty
, where
1\leq\alpha\leq 2
, and
\mathbf{E}\xi_{n}=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then, there is a constant
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\left(\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\right\rvert\right)^{\alpha}\leq\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}\right\rvert^{\alpha}\leq c_{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\xi_{i}\right\rvert^{\alpha}.

3.
Main results

Theorem 1
.

Let
N_{n}
be a critical BBPRE with
N_{0}=N
pairs satisfying Conditions
C1
–
7
.
Then, the extinction moment

\tau=\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ \left|\ N_{n}=0\right.\}

satisfies the relation

\frac{\tau}{\ln^{2}N}\xrightarrow[N\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{D}}\chi,

where
\chi
is a r.v. with the density

p_{\chi}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}t^{3}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}t}\right)I\{t\geq 0\}.

Proof.

Consider the random walk
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i}
with
S_{0}=0
.
This walk is used to represent the branching process
N_{n}
by the following recurrent sequence:

N_{n}=N_{k}e^{S_{n}-S_{k}}+\sum_{i=k+1}^{n}R_{i}e^{S_{n}-S_{i}},\ 0\leq k<n,

where
R_{i}=N_{i}-N_{i-1}e^{\xi_{i}}
.

Clearly, the increment
S_{n}-S_{i}
is independent of
R_{i}
for all
i\leq n
.
Now, we are ready to estimate
R_{n}
using the following lemma.
Note that all subsequent statements will be proved under the assumptions of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 4
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert R_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\leq c\,e^{\zeta_{n}}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n-1},\ n\geq 1.

Proof.

Let us use the notation

F_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n-1}}F_{n,i},\ M_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{n-1}}M_{n,i}.

As seen,
R_{n}
can be represented as

\displaystyle R_{n}

\displaystyle=L\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)-g\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle+\ g\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)-N_{n-1}g\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\right).

Using
C4
, the first difference can be estimated as

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert L\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)-g\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)\right\rvert^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(F_{n}+M_{n}\right)^{\alpha(1+\delta)}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n-1}\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right).

Using
C3
and
C2
, we bound the second difference as

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert g\left(F_{n},M_{n},\eta_{n}\right)-N_{n-1}g\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\right)\right\rvert^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\lambda^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left\lvert F_{n}-N_{n-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert+\left\lvert M_{n}-N_{n-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert\right)^{1+\delta},

Applying Lemma
2
, we bound the last expression by

\lambda^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})\,2^{\delta}\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert F_{n}-N_{n-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert M_{n}-N_{n-1}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\right),

which, by Lemma
3
, is less than

\lambda^{1+\delta}(\eta_{n})\,2^{\delta}c_{1+\delta}\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n-1}\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\varphi_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert\mu_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\right).

Thus, applying Lemma
2
, we bound
\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert R_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}
as

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert R_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\leq c\,e^{\zeta_{n}}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n-1},\ n\geq 1,

where
c=2^{\delta}\left(1+2^{\delta}c_{1+\delta}\right)
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

The inequality

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n}\leq e^{\xi_{n}}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}N_{n-1}

holds for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

Let us fix
{\boldsymbol{\eta}}
and consider the sequence

b^{n}_{m}=\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{n}\right|N_{n-1}=m\right)}{m},\ m\geq 1.

We will show that
b^{n}_{m}\leq e^{\xi_{n}}
for all
m\geq 1
.

Let us assume the opposite, i.e. there is
m^{\prime}\geq 1
such that
b^{n}_{m^{\prime}}>e^{\xi_{n}}
.
Similarly to the previous lemma, we will use the notation

F_{n}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}F_{n,i},\ M_{n}^{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}M_{n,i}.

Then, using
C1
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left(\left.N_{n}\right|N_{n-1}=2m,{\boldsymbol{\eta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}L\left(F_{n}^{2m},M_{n}^{2m},\eta_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}L\left(F_{n}^{m},M_{n}^{m},\eta_{n}\right)\cdot 2,\ m\geq 1,

and, thus,
b^{n}_{2^{k}m^{\prime}}>e^{\xi_{n}}
for all
k\geq 1
, and
\limsup\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}b^{n}_{m}>e^{\xi_{n}}
.

At the same time, we will show that
\limsup\limits_{m\rightarrow\infty}b^{n}_{m}\leq e^{\xi_{n}}
, which will lead us to a contradiction.
Indeed,

b^{n}_{m}\leq\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert L\left(F_{n}^{m},M_{n}^{m},\eta_{n}\right)-g\left(F_{n}^{m},M_{n}^{m},\eta_{n}\right)\right\rvert}{m}+\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}g\left(F_{n}^{m},M_{n}^{m},\eta_{n}\right)}{m}.

Using
C4
and Jensen’s inequality, the first term can be bounded by

\rho(\eta_{n})\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}^{\alpha}\left(F_{n}^{m}+M_{n}^{m}\right)}{m}=\rho(\eta_{n})\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}^{\alpha}\left(\varphi_{n}+\mu_{n}\right)}{m^{1-\alpha}}.

Now, using
C3
, the second term can be replaced by

\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}g\left(F_{n}^{m},M_{n}^{m},\eta_{n}\right)-g\left(m\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},m\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\right)}{m}+g\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\right).

Using
C2
and Lemma
3
, we bound the first part by

\displaystyle\lambda(\eta_{n})\frac{\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert F_{n}^{m}-m\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert M_{n}^{m}-m\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert}{m}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\frac{\lambda(\eta_{n})c_{1+\delta}^{\alpha}}{m^{1-\alpha}}\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}^{\alpha}\left\lvert\varphi_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}+\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}^{\alpha}\left\lvert\mu_{n}-\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\right).

Finally, combining the previous bounds, we get

b^{n}_{m}=g\left(\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\varphi_{n},\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\mu_{n},\eta_{n}\right)+O\left(m^{\alpha-1}\right)=e^{\xi_{n}}+o(1),\ m\rightarrow\infty,

which leads us to the contradiction.
∎

Now, we estimate the accumulated error of approximation of
N_{n}
by
Ne^{S_{n}}
using the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

There is a constant
c>0
such that, for all
n\geq 1
,

\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\lvert N_{n}-Ne^{S_{n}}\rvert^{1+\delta}\leq c\,n^{\delta}Ne^{S_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}+\delta\left(S_{n}-S_{i}\right)}.

Proof.

As seen, the difference
N_{n}-Ne^{S_{n}}
can be rewritten as

N_{n}-Ne^{S_{n}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}R_{i}e^{S_{n}-S_{i}}.

So, using Lemmas
2
,
4
and
5
, we consequently get

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\lvert N_{n}-Ne^{S_{n}}\rvert^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq n^{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\lvert R_{i}\rvert^{1+\delta}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)\left(S_{n}-S_{i}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq n^{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)\left(S_{n}-S_{i}\right)}\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\lvert R_{i}\rvert^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle\leq c\,n^{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)\left(S_{n}-S_{i}\right)}e^{\zeta_{i}}\,\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{i-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\,n^{\delta}Ne^{S_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}+\delta\left(S_{n}-S_{i}\right)}.

∎

Now, we are ready to connect the extinction moment of the process
N_{n}
with the hitting time of the corresponding random walk.
Let

\theta=\min\left\{n\geq 1\,\left|\ S_{n}\leq\ln^{\gamma}N-\ln N\right.\right\},\ \gamma=\frac{2}{1+\beta}<1.

Firstly, we will write a proof of the well-known fact that
\theta
is of the order
\ln^{2}N
.
Secondly, we will show that
\theta
is close to
\tau
.

Lemma 7
.

As
N\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{\theta}{\ln^{2}N}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{D}}\chi,

where
\chi
is a random variable with the density

p_{\chi}(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}t^{3}}}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\sigma^{2}t}\right)I\{t>0\}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\theta
, one can state that

\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\theta}{\ln^{2}N}\leq x\right)=\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\min_{i\leq x\ln^{2}N}S_{i}}{\sigma\sqrt{x\ln^{2}N}}\leq\frac{\ln^{\gamma}N-\ln N}{\sigma\sqrt{x\ln^{2}N}}\right),

where
x>0
.
By Donsker’s invariance principle, we have

\frac{\min_{i\leq n}S_{i}}{\sigma\sqrt{n}}\xrightarrow[n\rightarrow\infty]{\mathrm{D}}\inf_{t\in[0,1]}W_{t},

where
W_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion.
At the same time,

\frac{\ln^{\gamma}N-\ln N}{\sigma\sqrt{x\ln^{2}N}}\xrightarrow[N\rightarrow\infty]{}\frac{-1}{\sigma\sqrt{x}}.

Thus, by Slutsky’s theorem, we obtain

\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\theta}{\ln^{2}N}\leq x\right)\xrightarrow[N\rightarrow\infty]{}\mathbf{P}\left(\inf_{t\in[0,1]}W_{t}\leq\frac{-1}{\sigma\sqrt{x}}\right),

for
x>0
, which is equivalent to the statement of the lemma.
∎

Now, we will show that
\theta
approximates
\tau
.
Namely, we will prove that
N_{n}
extincts soon after the moment
\theta
.

Lemma 8
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and
k=\lfloor\varepsilon\ln^{2}N\rfloor
,
we have

\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

By the law of total probability, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+k}\geq 1\right|N_{\theta}>2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}>2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle+\,\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+k}\geq 1\right|N_{\theta}\leq 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}\leq 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)

(3)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}>2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)+\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+k}\geq 1\right|N_{\theta}=\lfloor 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\rfloor\right).

Note that
S_{\theta}-S_{i}\leq 0
for all
i\leq\theta
, due to the definition of
\theta
.
Thus, using Markov’s inequality, Lemma
6
, and this fact, consequently, we bound the first term of the right side of (
3
) in the following way:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}>2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)&\leq\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left\lvert N_{\theta}-Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}>N^{1+\delta}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)S_{\theta}}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{N^{1+\delta}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)S_{\theta}}}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left\lvert N_{\theta}-Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\\
&\leq\frac{c\,\theta^{1+\delta}}{N^{\delta}e^{\delta S_{\theta}}}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}}{\theta}.\end{split}

(4)

By the definition of
\theta
,

e^{\ln^{\gamma}N+\xi_{\theta}}\leq Ne^{S_{\theta}}\leq e^{\ln^{\gamma}N}.

(5)

Thus, the right side of (
4
) can be bounded by

c\,\theta^{1+\delta}\max_{i\leq\theta}\exp\left[\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}-\delta\left(\ln^{\gamma}N+\xi_{\theta}\right)\right].

Further, we will use the notation
\delta^{\prime}=\delta/3>0
.
Let us prove that

\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

(6)

Indeed, let us fix
x>0
.
By the law of total probability, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq x\ln^{2}N}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{P}\left(\vphantom{\max_{i\leq x\ln^{2}N}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}}\theta>x\ln^{2}N\right).

Due to Lemma
7
,

\mathbf{P}\left(\theta>x\ln^{2}N\right)\rightarrow 1-F_{\chi}(x),\ N\rightarrow\infty,

(7)

where
F_{\chi}(x)
is the cumulative distribution function of
\chi
.
Since
\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}
,
i\geq 1
are i.i.d. r.v.,

\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq x\ln^{2}N}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\leq 1-\mathbf{P}\left(\vphantom{\max_{i\leq x\ln^{2}N}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}}\zeta_{1}-\xi_{1}\leq\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N\right)^{x\ln^{2}N},

where the last expression is less than

1-\exp\left(x\ln^{2}N\ \mathbf{P}\left(\zeta_{1}-\xi_{1}>\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N\right)\right),

by the inequality
\ln(1-p)\geq-p
, where
0\leq p\leq 1
.
Since
\gamma=2/(1+\beta)
,
\mathbf{E}\left\lvert\zeta_{1}-\xi_{1}\right\rvert^{2/\gamma}
is finite, and, by Lemma
1
,

\ln^{2}N\ \mathbf{P}\left(\zeta_{1}-\xi_{1}>\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

Thus,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\leq 1-F_{\chi}(x),

and, taking
x>0
large enough, we state (
6
).

Similarly, for any
x>0
, one can write

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-\delta\xi_{\theta}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(3\xi_{\theta}\leq-\ln^{\gamma}N,\,\theta\leq x\ln^{2}N\right)

\displaystyle+\mathbf{P}\left(\theta>x\ln^{2}N\right).

The first term can be bounded by

\sum_{i=1}^{x\ln^{2}N}\mathbf{P}\left(3\xi_{i}\leq-\ln^{\gamma}N\right)\leq x\ln^{2}N\ \mathbf{P}\left(3\xi_{1}\leq-\ln^{\gamma}N\right).

Applying Lemma
1
to the last expression and using (
7
), we get

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-3\xi_{\theta}}>e^{\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\leq 1-F_{\chi}(x),

and, taking
x>0
large enough, we prove that

\mathbf{P}\left(e^{-\delta\xi_{\theta}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

(8)

Since
\theta
is of the order
\ln^{2}N
by Lemma
7
, for any
x>0
, we obtain

\mathbf{P}\left(\theta^{1+\delta}>x\,e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty,

(9)

by Slutsky’s lemma.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\ \ \ \,\,\mathbf{P}\left(\theta^{1+\delta}\max_{i\leq\theta}\exp\left[\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}-\delta\left(\ln^{\gamma}N+\xi_{\theta}\right)\right]>x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\vphantom{\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}}e^{-\delta\xi_{\theta}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)+\mathbf{P}\left(\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right)+\mathbf{P}\left(\vphantom{\max_{i\leq\theta}e^{\zeta_{i}-\xi_{i}}>e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}}\theta^{1+\delta}>x\,e^{\delta^{\prime}\ln^{\gamma}N}\right),

which, in combination with (
6
), (
8
), and (
9
), proves the convergence

\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}>2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{D}}0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

(10)

Now, we consider the second term of the right side of (
3
).
Using Markov’s inequality, Lemma
5
, and (
5
), consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+k}\geq 1\right|N_{\theta}=\lfloor 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min_{i\leq k}\mathbf{E}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+i}\right|N_{\theta}=\lfloor 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle\leq c\,Ne^{S_{\theta}}\exp\left(\min_{i\leq k}\left(S_{\theta+i}-S_{\theta}\right)\right)

(11)

\displaystyle\leq c\,\exp\left(\ln^{\gamma}N+\min_{i\leq k}\left(S_{\theta+i}-S_{\theta}\right)\right).

Now, we will prove that

\exp\left(\ln^{\gamma}N+\min_{i\leq k}\left(S_{\theta+i}-S_{\theta}\right)\right)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{D}}0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

(12)

Indeed, by Donsker’s invariance principle, for
x<0
, we get

\mathbf{P}\left(\min_{i\leq k}\left(S_{\theta+i}-S_{\theta}\right)>x\sigma\sqrt{k}\right)\rightarrow 1-F(x),\ N\rightarrow\infty,

where
F(x)
is the cumulative distribution function of
\inf_{t\in[0,1]}W_{t}
.
But, for any
x,t<0
, there is
N^{\prime}
such that
x\sigma\sqrt{k}\leq-\ln^{\gamma}N+t
for all
N\geq N^{\prime}
.
Therefore,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\ln^{\gamma}N+\min_{i\leq k}\left(S_{\theta+i}-S_{\theta}\right)>t\right)\leq 1-\sup_{x<0}F(x)=0.

Thus, we stated (
12
), which, in combination with (
11
), proves the convergence

\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left.N_{\theta+k}\geq 1\right|N_{\theta}=\lfloor 2Ne^{S_{\theta}}\rfloor\right)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{D}}0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

(13)

Thus, summing up (
10
) and (
13
), and applying Slutsky’s lemma, we get

\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{D}}0,\ N\rightarrow\infty.

Therefore, by the definition of weak convergence, we have

\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)=\mathbf{E}\left(\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)\right)\rightarrow 0,\ N\rightarrow\infty,

∎

Similarly, we prove that
N_{n}
is still non-zero at the moment
\theta
.

Lemma 9
.

The convergence

\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta}>0\right)\rightarrow 1,\ N\rightarrow\infty,

is valid.

Proof.

Let us consider the probability of the opposite event.
Using Markov’s inequality and Lemma
6
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}=0\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(N_{\theta}-Ne^{S_{\theta}}\leq-Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{{\boldsymbol{\eta}}}\left(\left\lvert N_{\theta}-Ne^{S_{\theta}}\right\rvert^{1+\delta}\geq N^{1+\delta}e^{\left(1+\delta\right)S_{\theta}}\right).

The last expression appears in (
4
).
As seen from the proof of Lemma
8
, it converges to zero in distribution.
By the same reasons as before, we have the required convergence.
∎

Finally, we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1
.
On the one hand, for a fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\tau\leq x\ln^{2}N\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\left\{\theta\leq x\ln^{2}N\right\}\cup\left\{\tau\leq\theta\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left(\theta\leq x\ln^{2}N\right)+\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta}=0\right).

But, by Lemma
9
,
\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta}=0\right)
converges to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

On the other hand, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left(\tau\leq x\ln^{2}N\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left(\left\{\theta\leq(x-\varepsilon)\ln^{2}N\right\}\setminus\left\{\tau\geq\theta+\varepsilon\ln^{2}N\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}\left(\theta\leq(x-\varepsilon)\ln^{2}N\right)-\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right),

where
k=\lfloor\varepsilon\ln^{2}N\rfloor
.
Again, due to Lemma
8
,
\mathbf{P}\left(N_{\theta+k}>0\right)
converges to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
.

Thus, taking
N\rightarrow\infty
, and then,
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, we obtain

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\theta}{\ln^{2}N}\leq x\right)\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\tau}{\ln^{2}N}\leq x\right)\leq\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbf{P}\left(\frac{\theta}{\ln^{2}N}\leq x\right),

which proves Theorem
1
.
∎

Abstract
. An abelian variety A / K A/K is heavenly at ℓ \ell if the extension K ⁡ ( A ⁡ [ ℓ ∞ ] ) / K ⁡ ( μ ℓ ∞ ) K(A[\ell^{\infty}])/K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell^{\infty}}\!) is both pro- ℓ \ell and unramified away from ℓ \ell . For any number field

, and any prime

Conjecture 2.5

Remark 2.6

The set

If

Suppose

Proof.

Remark 2.8

Since

Remark 2.9

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose

and
\ell
is a prime satisfying
\ell-1>2d
. For any number field

\sharp\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{K}(d,1,\ell)<\infty
.

We let
M_{K}
denote the set of places of
K

denote the subsets of archimedean and nonarchimedean places, respectively. Throughout,

will be a finite subset of

satisfying

. Let
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
denote the ring of
S

\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{K,S}
denote the integral closure of
\mathcal{O}_{K,S}
in
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. For any finite extension

, we set
S\times_{K}K^{\prime}:=\{w\in M_{K^{\prime}}:\evaluated{w}_{K}\in S\}
. For simplicity, we set
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime},S}:=\mathcal{O}_{K,S\times_{K}K^{\prime}}

Suppose

\mathcal{I}_{S,d}(Y;K^{\prime}):=\{y\in Y(\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{K^{\prime},S}):[K^{\prime}(y):K^{\prime}]\leq d\}=\bigcup_{L\colon[L:K^{\prime}]\leq d}Y(\mathcal{O}_{L,S}).

That is,

is the set of all points of

It is well-known that when sufficiently many points “at infinity” are removed from a complete projective curve, the open subset that remains admits only finitely many
S

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose

is a nonsingular affine curve and

In case

For any

Proof of Theorem

Set

4.
Balanced abelian varieties

Suppose
A/K
is an abelian variety of dimension
g
, heavenly at
\ell
, and let
\mathfrak{l}
be a prime of
K
above
\ell
. Proposition
2.1
gives one relationship between the characters
\chi
and
\psi_{\mathfrak{l}}
, but a second critical relation exists as well. As
\rho_{A,\ell}
has the form (
2.3
), the classification of Tate and Oort
[
40
]
now implies the following. For each
r
with
1\leq r\leq 2g
, there exists an integer
j_{\mathfrak{l},r}
with
0\leq j_{\mathfrak{l},r}\leq e
such that
\chi^{i_{r}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}}
on
J_{\mathfrak{l}}
– further detail is given in
[
29
, §3.3]
. We call the set of integers
\{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}\}
the
Tate-Oort numbers
of
A
at
\mathfrak{l}
. They are uniquely determined if
e<\ell-1
; otherwise they are determined modulo
(\ell-1)
.

We say
A
is
weakly balanced
at
\mathfrak{l}
if there exists a partition of
\{1,2,\dots,2g\}
into
g
unordered pairs, say
R_{1}\sqcup R_{2}\sqcup\cdots\sqcup R_{g}
, such that for each
R_{k}=\{s,t\}
, we have
j_{\mathfrak{l},s}+j_{\mathfrak{l},t}=e
. We say
A
is
balanced
at
\mathfrak{l}
if
j_{\mathfrak{l},r}=\frac{e}{2}
for every
r
,
1\leq r\leq 2g
. If
A
is balanced at every
\mathfrak{l}
above
\ell
, we say
A
is
balanced
at
\ell
.

Now on
J_{\mathfrak{l}}
we have both
\chi^{i_{r}}=\psi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}}
and
\chi=\psi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{e}
. Consequently, for each
r
we have
\chi^{i_{r}e}=\psi_{\mathfrak{l}}^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}e}=\chi^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}}
, which implies

(4.1)

ei_{r}\equiv j_{\mathfrak{l},r}\pmod{\ell-1},\qquad 1\leq r\leq 2g.

The congruence (
4.1
) forces a condition on the trace of Frobenius elements under
\rho_{A,\ell}
. Suppose
A/K
is heavenly at
\ell
and let
p\neq\ell
be a rational prime. Suppose
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
K
above
p
and set
q:=\mathbf{N}\mathfrak{p}
. Let
\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}\in G_{K}
be a Frobenius element for
\mathfrak{p}
. For any
m\geq 1
, let
P_{m}(T)\in\mathbb{Z}[T]
denote the characteristic polynomial for the action of
\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}
on
V_{\ell}A
. Let
\{\alpha_{r}\}_{r=1}^{2g}
be the complex roots of
P_{1}(T)
. For any
m\geq 1
,

(4.2)

P_{m}(T)=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-\alpha_{r}^{m})=T^{2g}-\tau_{m}T^{2g-1}+\cdots+q^{gm},\quad\tau_{m}:=\alpha_{1}^{m}+\cdots+\alpha_{2g}^{m}.

Consider how
\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}
acts on
A[\ell]
. The characteristic polynomial of this action,
\overline{P}_{m}(T)\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[T]
, coincides with
P_{m}(T)
modulo
\ell
. By Proposition
2.7
,
\rho_{A,\ell}(\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m})
is upper triangular with respect to some basis. Consequently,
\overline{P}_{m}(T)
must split completely over
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, and the multiset
\{\chi^{i_{r}}(\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}):1\leq r\leq 2g\}
gives the roots of
\overline{P}_{m}
counted with multiplicity. Thus,

\overline{P}_{m}(T)=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-\chi^{i_{r}}(\theta_{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}))=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-\chi(\theta_{\mathfrak{p}})^{mi_{r}})=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-q^{mi_{r}}).

In the special case
m=e
, (
4.1
) implies

(4.3)

\overline{P}_{e}(T)=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-q^{ei_{r}})=\prod_{r=1}^{2g}(T-q^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}}).

By comparing the coefficients of
T^{2g-1}
in
\overline{P}_{e}
and
P_{e}
, we obtain:

(4.4)

\tau_{e}-\sum_{r=1}^{2g}q^{j_{\mathfrak{l},r}}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell}.

Note (
4.4
) represents an infinite family of congruences satisfied by the Tate-Oort numbers
j_{\mathfrak{l},r}
(one for each
\mathfrak{p}\nmid\ell
). For sufficiently large
\ell
, we will show these congruences
almost
force a violation of the Weil conjectures – the only exception being the case when
j_{\mathfrak{l},r}=\frac{e}{2}
for every
r
, i.e., when
A
is balanced at
\mathfrak{l}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}(K,g)
denote the subset of
\mathcal{H}(K,g)
of pairs
([A]_{K},\ell)
where
A
is balanced at
\ell
. We define

\displaystyle B(K,g)

\displaystyle:=\sup\,\{\ell:\text{there exists}\ ([A],\ell)\in\mathcal{H}(K,g)\smallsetminus\mathcal{B}(K,g)\},

\displaystyle B(n,g)

\displaystyle:=\sup\,\{B(K,g):[K:\mathbb{Q}]=n\}.

Thus, if a
g
-dimensional abelian variety
A/K
is heavenly at
\ell>B(K,g)
, then
A
must be balanced at
\ell
. That
B(n,g)
is finite was already implicitly established in
[
29
, §3]
, but the implied bound is very large; we give stronger explicit bounds below.

One useful consequence of the balanced property is that it forces a divisibility condition on
e
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
\ell>2
and
\mathfrak{l}
is a prime of
K
above
\ell
. Suppose
A/K
is heavenly at
\ell
and for some index
r
,
j_{\mathfrak{l},r}=\frac{e}{2}
. Then
\ord_{2}e>\ord_{2}(\ell-1)
. In particular,
4\mid e
.

Proof.

Set
j=j_{\mathfrak{l},r}
. We have
e=2j
and so (
4.1
) implies
j(2i_{r}-1)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell-1}
. But
\ell-1
is even and
2i_{r}-1
is odd. Thus
\ord_{2}e>\ord_{2}j\geq\ord_{2}(\ell-1)>0
.
∎

5.
Bounds on balanced heavenly abelian varieties

In this section, we determine explicit bounds for
B(K,g)
. We begin by showing that heavenly abelian varieties are always weakly balanced.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose
A/K
is heavenly at
\ell
, and suppose
\mathfrak{l}
is a prime of
K
above
\ell
. Then
A/K
is weakly balanced at
\mathfrak{l}
.

Proof.

The content of the theorem is trivial in case
\ell=2
, so we assume
\ell>2
. Suppose
A/K
has dimension
g
. We have
e=e(A/K;\mathfrak{l})\cdot e_{\mathfrak{l}}
. Recall that

\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\textnormal{unr}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\textnormal{unr}}\subseteq K_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\textnormal{ss}},\quad[K_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\textnormal{unr}}:\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{\textnormal{unr}}]=e_{\mathfrak{l}},\quad[K_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\textnormal{ss}}:K_{\mathfrak{l}}^{\textnormal{unr}}]=e(A/K;\mathfrak{l}),

and that the Tate-Oort numbers
j_{r}
may be chosen with
0\leq j_{r}\leq e
. Set
K_{0}:=K\cap\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})
and consider the tower
\mathbb{Q}\subseteq K_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})
. Set

(5.1)

\varepsilon:=[K_{0}:\mathbb{Q}],\quad w:=[\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}):K_{0}]=\sharp\chi(G_{K}),\quad b:=\frac{e}{\varepsilon}.

Note
\varepsilon\cdot w=\ell-1
. Since
\mathbb{Q}(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})/\mathbb{Q}
is totally ramified over
\ell
, the same is true for
K_{0}/\mathbb{Q}
. As
K_{0}\subseteq K
, it follows that
\varepsilon\mid e_{\mathfrak{l}}
and so
b\in\mathbb{Z}
.

As
\rho_{A,\ell}
is upper triangular with respect to some basis, there exists a filtration on
A[\ell]
,

0=U_{0}\leq U_{1}\leq\cdots\leq U_{2g}=A[\ell],\qquad\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}U_{r}=r.

Let
{A}^{\vee}
be the dual abelian variety of
A
. Since the Weil pairing
\varepsilon_{\ell}\colon A[\ell]\times{A}^{\vee}[\ell]\to\mathbb{F}_{\ell}(\chi)\cong\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}
is perfect, there is an analogous filtration induced on
{A}^{\vee}[\ell]
:

0={U}^{\vee}_{2g}\leq{U}^{\vee}_{2g-1}\leq\cdots\leq{U}^{\vee}_{0}={A}^{\vee}[\ell],\qquad\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{\ell}}{U}^{\vee}_{r}=2g-r.

Subspaces of dimension
r
inside
A[\ell]
correspond to subspaces of codimension
r
inside
{A}^{\vee}[\ell]
. Consequently,

\rho_{{A}^{\vee},\ell}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{1-i_{2g}}&\star&\cdots&\star\\
&\chi^{1-i_{2g-1}}&\cdots&\star\\
&&\ddots&\vdots\\
&&&\chi^{1-i_{1}}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

Define the multisets
X:=\{\chi^{i_{r}}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}
and
{X}^{\vee}:=\{\chi^{1-i_{r}}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}
(where here
\chi
is implicitly restricted to
G_{K}

(as multi-sets).

For any

and

Since

Since

\{i_{r}\bmod{w}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}=\{1-i_{r}\bmod{w}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}.

\{ei_{r}\bmod{ew}\}_{r=1}^{2g}=\{e(1-i_{r})\bmod{ew}\}_{r=1}^{2g}

\displaystyle\{j_{r}\bmod{(\ell-1)}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}

\displaystyle=\{ei_{r}\bmod{(\ell-1)}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}

\displaystyle=\{e(1-i_{r})\bmod{(\ell-1)}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}

\displaystyle=\{e-j_{r}\bmod{(\ell-1)}:1\leq r\leq 2g\}.

Thus, there exists a permutation

\mathfrak{S}_{2g}

for any

if and only if

\theta=(s_{1}\ t_{1})(s_{2}\ t_{2})\cdots(s_{h}\ t_{h})

can be partitioned into
g
unordered pairs

(5.2)

R_{k}=\{s,t\},\quad j_{s}+j_{t}\equiv e\bmod{(\ell-1)}.

If

for each
k
,
1\leq k\leq g
. If

and

\sum_{r=1}^{2g}j_{r}\equiv ge+\frac{\ell-1}{2}\bmod{(\ell-1)}.

We also have

ge+\frac{\ell-1}{2}\equiv\sum_{r=1}^{2g}j_{r}\equiv\sum_{r=1}^{2g}ei_{r}\equiv e\sum_{r=1}^{2g}i_{r}\equiv eg\bmod{(\ell-1)},

and so

X=\{s_{h+1},t_{h+1},\dots,s_{h+h^{\prime}},t_{h+h^{\prime}}\},\qquad Y=\{s_{h+h^{\prime}+1},t_{h+h^{\prime}+1},\dots,s_{g},t_{g}\}.

Set
R_{k}:=\{s_{k},t_{k}\}
for each

.

If

If

Since the Tate-Oort numbers are only defined modulo

∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose

is an abelian variety of dimension

is a prime of

Suppose

6.
Comparison of trace behavior

In this and the following section, we characterize, among elliptic curves with complex multiplication defined over a quadratic field, those curves which are balanced at some prime
\ell\neq 2
. Suppose
E/K
is balanced at a prime
\mathfrak{l}\mid\ell
, while
E^{\prime}/K
is a curve with complex multiplication and good reduction away from
\ell
. Let
\rho_{E,\ell}
and
\rho_{E^{\prime},\ell}
be the associated Galois representations on
\ell
-torsion. We show that the traces of Frobenius elements under
\rho_{E,\ell}
and under
\rho_{E^{\prime},\ell}
have similar behavior (compare Proposition
6.4
and Corollary
6.12
). In addition to providing striking evidence for Conjecture
A
, this leads to Theorem
7.1
, a necessary and sufficient condition for a curve with complex multiplication to be heavenly and balanced. Many results in §6 and §7 rely on Theorem
6.1
. Consequently, the condition
j(E)\not\in\mathbb{Q}
is often present.

Let us first recall the classical theory when complex multiplication is present. Suppose
E/K
has complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field
L
and good reduction away from one rational prime
\ell\neq 2
. By applying genus theory, we can better understand the relationship between
K
,
L
, and
\ell
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
m\geq 1
be squarefree and set
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m})
. Suppose
E
is an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
, defined over the quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
. Suppose further that
E
has good reduction away from an odd prime
\ell
. Let
M:=KL=L(j(E))
. Then

(a)

K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
for some prime
p\not\equiv 3\bmod{4}
,

(b)

M=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{-\ell})
, and

(c)

\ell\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
m=\ell p
(so
\ell\mid m
).

Proof.

Since
E
has complex multiplication,
j(E)\in\mathbb{R}

[
19
, §10.3 Remark 1]
and
K
is a real quadratic field. Because
E
has good reduction away from
\ell
, the extension
K(E[\ell])/K
is unramified outside
\ell
. Further, by
[
5
, Lemma 3.15]
,
L\subseteq K(E[\ell])
and so also
M\subseteq K(E[\ell])
; thus
M/K
is unramified at finite primes away from
\ell
.

By
[
37
, Thm. 2.2]
,
M=L(j(E))
is the Hilbert class field of
L
, so
L
has class number
2
. By genus theory the discriminant
\Delta_{L}
of
L
has exactly two prime factors
[
9
, Thm. 6.1]
, and further,
M
is the genus field of
L
, known to satisfy
M=L(\sqrt{p})
for any prime
p\equiv 1\bmod{4}
dividing
\Delta_{L}
or
M=L(\sqrt{-q})
for any prime
q\equiv 3\bmod{4}
dividing
\Delta_{L}
.
Since
\Delta_{L}
has exactly two prime divisors, one of the following cases must hold:

(i)

m=pq
with
p\not\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
q\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, yielding
M=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{-q})
, or

(ii)

m=cp
with
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and
c\in\{1,2\}
, yielding
M=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-cp},\sqrt{p})
.

In either case, the only real quadratic subfield of
M
is
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
, proving (a). In case (ii), both
K/\mathbb{Q}
and
M/K
are unramified at
2
, but
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-c})\subseteq M
, so
M/\mathbb{Q}
is ramified at
2
, a contradiction. Thus case (i) must hold and now
q
evidently ramifies in
M/K
. We conclude
q=\ell
, proving (b) and (c).
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

An elliptic curve
E/K
over a quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
cannot have everywhere good reduction.

Proof.

Pick any prime
\ell^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod{4}
. If
E/K
has everywhere good reduction, then Theorem
6.1
applies with
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, forcing
\ell^{\prime}\equiv 3\bmod{4}
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 6.3
.

An elliptic curve
E/K
over a quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
with complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{L}
cannot be heavenly at two distinct primes.

Proof.

Such a curve has everywhere good reduction and Corollary
6.2
applies.
∎

Recall that a curve

with complex multiplication by a nonmaximal order in a field

is isogenous to a curve

with complex multiplication by the maximal order

(see Remark

). Since the reduction type of a curve is isogeny-invariant, Corollaries

and

also hold for

, provided

. This leaves the case where

but

, which does occur. For example, set

and

where

. Consider the following curves, both defined and isogenous over

:

\displaystyle E_{1}\colon y^{2}+axy+(a+1)y

\displaystyle=x^{3}+(a+1)x^{2}+(17a-41)x-(57a-138),

\displaystyle E_{2}\colon y^{2}+axy+(a+1)y

\displaystyle=x^{3}+(a+1)x^{2}+(2a-1)x+(a-1).

Both curves have everywhere good reduction, and complex multiplication by an order in

Resume the notation and hypotheses of Theorem

. We consider the behavior of the Hecke character attached to

(see
[

, Ch. II]

4p^{f_{\mathfrak{Q}}}=a_{\mathfrak{Q}}(E_{M})^{2}+mv^{2}.

Remark 6.8
.

For clarity, we explain how Lemma

follows from
[

by the remark following the statement of the Proposition.

Proof of Lemma

.

Since

, we certainly have
\ell\nmid e
. Because

is balanced, the Tate-Oort numbers satisfy

for both

has semistable reduction, which must be either good or multiplicative. Let us consider the case of multiplicative reduction. First, since the residue field for

is algebraically closed, the multiplicative reduction is necessarily split. The special fiber of
\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{ss}}
has
\mathbb{G}_{m}
as its identity component. Now the
\ell
-torsion will have, as its connected part, the group scheme
\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}
(the
\ell
-torsion of

). Since

on

Consequently,

has good reduction. The special fiber of

is an elliptic curve over the residue field, and must be either ordinary (
\ell
-rank

) or supersingular (
\ell
-rank

). In the ordinary case, consider the connected-étale sequence for

as a finite flat group scheme over

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{ss}}[\ell]^{\circ}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{ss}}[\ell]\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{ss}}[\ell]^{\text{{\'{e}t}}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\mathcal{E}^{\textnormal{ss}}[\ell]^{\circ}\cong\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}.

The presence of the

component again violates the balanced assumption. Thus, the special fiber of

is supersingular, i.e., has
\ell
-rank zero. The result now holds by Lemma

∎

Remark 6.14

Let

Proposition 6.15
.

Suppose

is an elliptic curve, balanced at a prime

of

\mathfrak{p}

7.
Heavenly elliptic curves with complex multiplication.

In the case of heavenly curves with complex multiplication, we are able to both characterize heavenliness in terms of the trace of Frobenius, and also give a complete classification of such curves.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\ell\neq 2
be prime and
K
a quadratic field. Suppose that
E/K
is an elliptic curve that satisfies
K=\mathbb{Q}(j(E))
. Further, assume that
E/K
has complex multiplication and has good reduction away from
\ell
. Then

(a)

E/K
is heavenly at
\ell
if and only if
\trace(\rho_{E,\ell}(G_{K}))\neq\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
.

(b)

If
E/K
is heavenly at
\ell
and
\ell>7
, then
E
is balanced at
\ell
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.4
,
E/K
is heavenly at
\ell
if and only if
[K(E[\ell]):K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})]
is a power of
\ell
. Let
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-m})
be the field of complex multiplication. By Remark
2.9
, we may and do assume the complex multiplication is by the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Set
M=KL
. From
[
5
, Lemma 3.15]
we know
L\subseteq K(E[\ell])
and so
M(E[\ell])=K(E[\ell])
. By Theorem
6.1
,
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p})
and
m=\ell p
where
\ell\equiv 3\bmod{4}
and
p
is a prime with
p\not\equiv 3\bmod{4}
. Since
K
is real,
[K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}):K]=\ell-1
, and since
M=K(\sqrt{-\ell})\subseteq K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})
, it follows that
M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})=K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})
and
[M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}):M]=(\ell-1)/2
.

Let
\rho=\rho_{E,\ell}
be the
G_{K}
-representation on
E[\ell]
. Because
E
has complex multiplication and
\ell
ramifies in
L
, we know
E[\ell]
is a free rank one module over
\mathcal{O}_{L}/\ell\mathcal{O}_{L}\cong\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[T]/(T^{2})
. Consequently the image of
G_{M}
under
\rho
is abelian, lying inside

\Aut_{\mathcal{O}_{L}/\ell\mathcal{O}_{L}}(E[\ell])\cong(\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[T]/(T^{2}))^{\times}=\left\{bT+a+(T^{2}):b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell},a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\}.

(For a detailed explanation, see
[
37
, §II.1.4, II.2.3]
.) By choosing an ordered basis for
E[\ell]
that corresponds to the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-basis
\{T,1\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}[T]/(T^{2})
, we find

(7.1)

\rho(G_{M})\leq\mathcal{C}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}:b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell},\ a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the normalizer of
\mathcal{C}
inside
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. The quotient
\mathcal{N}/\mathcal{C}
is cyclic of even order (in fact isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
). Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
be the unique subgroup of
\mathcal{N}
satisfying
[\mathcal{N}^{\prime}:\mathcal{C}]=2
, namely

(7.2)

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}:=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}\pm a&b\\
0&a\end{pmatrix}:b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell},a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}\right\}.

By
[
23
, Lemma 6.2]
,
\rho(G_{K})\leq\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. Let
\varepsilon\colon G_{K}\to\{\pm 1\}
be the cokernel character for the natural projection
G_{K}\twoheadrightarrow G_{K}/G_{M}
. By (
7.1
), there exists a homomorphism
\theta\colon G_{K}\to\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
such that

\rho=\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\theta&\star\\
&\theta\end{pmatrix},\qquad{\textstyle\evaluated{\rho}_{G_M}}=\begin{pmatrix}\psi&\star\\
&\psi\end{pmatrix},\qquad\psi:={\textstyle\evaluated{\theta}_{G_M}},

and these necessarily satisfy
\varepsilon\theta^{2}=\det\rho=\chi
. For any
\sigma\in G_{K}
we have

\trace\rho(\sigma)=0\iff\varepsilon(\sigma)=-1\iff\sigma\not\in G_{M},

and so
\trace\rho(G_{K})=\{0\}\cup\trace\rho(G_{M})
. But
2\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{\times}
and therefore
\sharp\!\left(\trace\rho(G_{M})\right)=\sharp\!\left(2\cdot\psi(G_{M})\right)=\sharp\psi(G_{M})
. Note
\trace\rho(G_{K})=\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
if and only if
\psi
is surjective. Let
M^{\psi}=M^{\ker\psi}
. Since
\psi^{2}=\evaluated{\chi}_{G_M}
,
G_{M^{\psi}}=\ker\psi\leq\ker\psi^{2}=\ker{\textstyle\evaluated{\chi}_{G_M}}=G_{M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})}
, i.e.,
M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})\subseteq M^{\psi}
. Precisely by the definition of
M^{\psi}
,
\evaluated{\rho}_{M^{\psi}}
is unit upper triangular and so
[M(E[\ell]):M^{\psi}]
divides
\ell
. On the other hand,

[M^{\psi}:M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})]=\frac{[M^{\psi}:M]}{[M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell}):M]}=\frac{\sharp\psi(G_{M})}{(\ell-1)/2}=\begin{cases}2&\psi\ \text{surjective},\\
1&\psi\ \text{not surjective.}\end{cases}

Thus,
[K(E[\ell]):K(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})]=\ell
if and only if
M^{\psi}=M(\mathbf{\upmu}_{\ell})
, if and only if
\trace\rho(G_{K})\neq\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, proving (a).

Now assume
\ell>7
. Since
E
is heavenly, we have two forms for the representation
\rho
:

\rho\sim\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon\theta&\star\\
&\theta\end{pmatrix}\sim\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{i}&\star\\
&\chi^{1-i}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that
\varepsilon=\varepsilon\theta\theta^{-1}=\chi^{\pm(2i-1)}
. Thus
4i-2\equiv 0\bmod{(\ell-1)}
, i.e.,
i\in\{(\ell+1)/4,(3\ell-1)/4\}
. In particular,
\ell\equiv 3\bmod{4}
. If
\ell>19
then
E
is balanced at
\ell
by Proposition
5.4
. When
\ell=19
or
\ell=11
, (
4.1
) can only hold if
E
is balanced at
\ell
by a straightforward calculation.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

In case
\ell=7
, (

) does not quite imply

must be balanced at
\ell
, but these congruences do imply that at any

(b)

There exist only finitely many imaginary quadratic fields

of class number exactly

Let

We set the following notation. If

\mathcal{N}(\ell):=\{p\ \text{prime}:(\tfrac{p}{\ell})=1\ \text{and}\ r_{\ell}(4p)>2\sqrt{p}\}.

The set
\mathcal{N}(\ell)
is always finite since
p>\frac{\ell^{2}}{16}
guarantees
r_{\ell}(4p)\leq\frac{\ell}{2}\leq 2\sqrt{p}
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Suppose
E/K
is heavenly and balanced at
\ell
. If
p
is a rational prime with
p\in\mathcal{N}(\ell)
, then
p
does not split in
K
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose
p\in\mathcal{N}(\ell)
splits in
K
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K
over
p
. Set
L:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-\ell})
and note that as
(\frac{p}{\ell})=1
, it follows that
p
splits in
L
. As a prime splits completely in a compositum of extensions if and only if it splits completely in each of the extensions, we conclude
p
splits completely in
KL
, and hence
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
KL
. By Corollary
6.12
, we conclude
a_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\equiv 4p\bmod{\ell}
. On the other hand,
p\neq\ell
and so from the Weil bound we have
0<|a_{\mathfrak{p}}|\leq 2\sqrt{p}<r_{\ell}(4p)
, contradicting the minimality of
r_{\ell}(4p)
.
∎

Lemma
8.1
shows that for any specific
\ell\gg 0
, it is difficult for a quadratic field
K
to admit an elliptic curve
E/K
which is heavenly at
\ell
. In a similar vein, for any particular
K
, we may conclude
\mathcal{H}(K,1,\ell)=\varnothing
for many
\ell
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
K
be a quadratic field, and suppose
p
is a rational prime which splits in
K
. If
\ell>4p
and
(\frac{p}{\ell})=1
, then there does not exist an elliptic curve
E/K
which is heavenly at
\ell
.

Proof.

Let
p
be such a prime and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K
above
p
. Suppose
E/K
is heavenly at
\ell
, with
\ell>4p
. By Corollary
6.12
,
a_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\equiv 4p\bmod{\ell}
. On the other hand,
\sqrt{p}\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and
(4p,\ell)=1
, and so the Weil bound implies the strict inequality
0<a_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}<4p
. Thus
0<4p-a_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}<4p<\ell
, contradicting that
\ell\mid 4p-a_{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
.
∎

If there exists
A/\mathbb{Q}
with
([A]_{\mathbb{Q}},\ell)\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{Q},1)
, then
([A\times_{\mathbb{Q}}K]_{K},\ell)\in\mathcal{H}(K,1)
for every
K
and necessarily
\mathcal{N}(\ell)=\varnothing
. For other
\ell
, however, Lemma
8.1
is quite restrictive; we illustrate this with two examples.

•

For
\ell=47
, we have
\mathcal{N}(47)=\{2,3,7,17,37,53,97\}
. The primes in this set are inert in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
, compatible with the existence of the heavenly isogeny class
H(47,5).1
in Table
A.2
. Ordered by magnitude of the discriminant, however, the next real quadratic field for which every prime in
\mathcal{N}(\ell)
is inert is
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2309})
. By Lemma
8.1
, no quadratic field
K^{\prime}
with
5<|\Delta_{K^{\prime}}|<2309
admits an elliptic curve heavenly at
47
.

•

Note
\sharp\mathcal{N}(\ell)\to\infty
as
\ell\to\infty
, and so this constraint is more severe for larger
\ell
. For example, for
\ell=103
, we have
\sharp\mathcal{N}(103)=23
and no quadratic field
K
with
|\Delta_{K}|<10^{6}
has every prime in
\mathcal{N}(103)
inert. Thus, Lemma
8.1
implies that none of these
K
admit an elliptic curve
E/K
which is heavenly at
103
.

Lemma
8.2
is similarly restrictive. For instance, take
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})
, and consider the set

S:=\{p:p\ \text{splits in}\ K,\ 2<p<250\}=\{3,19,29,\dots,233\}.

Applying Lemma
8.2
to
p\in S
, we find
K
cannot admit an elliptic curve heavenly at
\ell
for any prime
\ell
with
163<\ell<10^{7}
. In fact, we can say more. By (an effective version of) the Chebotarev Density Theorem, there exists
B_{K}
such that for
\ell>B_{K}
there exists a prime
p
that satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
8.2
. Under assumption of the Extended Riemann Hypothesis, such bounds have been made explicit by Bach and Sorenson
[
3
]
, and are small enough to be of practical use. With
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})
, the Bach-Sorenson bounds imply
B_{K}<70000
. So, conditional on ERH, we deduce
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7}),1,\ell)=\varnothing
for every
\ell>163
. Similar ideas were used in
[
18
]
to prove, conditional on ERH, that
\mathcal{H}(K,1,\ell)=\varnothing
for any
\ell>163
and any quadratic field
K
satisfying
|\Delta_{K}|<100
. We extended this calculation and verified the following:

Proposition 8.3
.

Suppose
\ell>163
is prime, and
K
is a quadratic field satisfying
|\Delta_{K}|<5\cdot 10^{5}
. Conditional on ERH,
\mathcal{H}(K,1,\ell)=\varnothing
.

Frugal RIS-aided 3D Localization with CFO under LoS and NLoS Conditions
Yasaman Ettefagh, Musa Furkan Keskin, Kamran Keykhosravi, Gonzalo Seco-Granados, Henk Wymeersch

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate 3-D localization and frequency synchronization with multiple RIS in the presence of CFO ( CFO ) for a stationary UE ( UE ). In line with the 6G goals of sustainability and efficiency, we focus on a frugal communication scenario with minimal spatial and spectral resources (i.e., narrowband single-input single-ouput system), considering both the presence and blockage of the LoS ( LoS ) path between the BS ( BS ) and the UE . We design a GLRT ( GLRT )-based LoS detector, channel parameter estimation and localization algorithms, with varying complexity. To verify the efficiency of our estimators, we compare the RMSE ( RMSE ) to the CRB ( CRB ) of the unknown parameters. We also evaluate the sensitivity of our algorithms to the presence of uncontrolled MPC ( MPC ) and various levels of CFO . Simulation results showcase the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms under minimal hardware and spectral requirements, and a wide range of operating conditions, thereby confirming the viability of RIS-aided frugal localization in 6G scenarios.

\mathbf{c}_{0}=\bm{1}_{L}
. As a result, despite the RIS-reflected paths having significantly lower energy than the LoS path due to the approximately random phase configuration, the use of orthogonal temporal coding for RIS phase profiles ensures their extraction through coherent integration. The details of this extraction are provided in the next subsection, with an example.

III-B

Example

As an example, we fix the number of
RIS
to
R=2
. Note that to separate the contributions from the three paths (
LoS
,
RIS
1 and
RIS
2), the minimum required coding length is
L=4
.
We take
\mathbf{c}_{0}=[1,1,1,1]^{\mathsf{T}}
,
\mathbf{c}_{1}=[1,-1,1,-1]^{\mathsf{T}}
and
\mathbf{c}_{2}=[1,1,-1,-1]^{\mathsf{T}}
. The vectors
\bm{z}_{0}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{0}\,\bm{b}(\nu)
and
\bm{z}_{r}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{r}\,\bm{x}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\odot\bm{b}(\nu)
,
r=1,2
and
l=0,1,2,3
represent the noise-free received signal contributions from the LoS and RIS paths, respectively. These vectors capture the signal components corresponding to each path before noise is introduced.

We can express the
4
consecutive noise-free samples of each path as follows:

\displaystyle[\bm{z}_{0}]_{4k+l}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{0}e^{j2\pi(4k+l)T_{s}\nu},

(17)

\displaystyle[\bm{z}_{1}]_{4k+l}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{1}\left([\mathbf{c}_{r}]_{l}\penalty\ g_{1}(\bm{\phi}_{1},\bm{\theta}_{1},k)\right)e^{j2\pi(4k+l)T_{s}\nu},

(18)

\displaystyle[\bm{z}_{2}]_{4k+l}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{2}\left([\mathbf{c}_{2}]_{l}\penalty\ g_{2}(\bm{\phi}_{2},\bm{\theta}_{2},k)\right)e^{j2\pi(4k+l)T_{s}\nu},

(19)

where
g_{r}(\bm{\phi}_{r},\bm{\theta}_{r},k)=(\bm{a}(\phi_{r})\odot{\bm{P}_{r}}_{[:,k]})^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{a}(\bm{\theta}_{r})
,
r=1,2
. Then, by applying (
16
) and in the case of
\nu=0
, the filtered measurements
\bm{y}_{0}
,
\bm{y}_{1}
and
\bm{y}_{2}
will be as follows

\displaystyle[\bm{y}_{0}]_{k}={\sqrt{P}}\alpha_{0}+[\tilde{\bm{n}}_{0}]_{k},

(20)

\displaystyle[\bm{y}_{1}]_{k}={\sqrt{P}}\alpha_{1}g_{1}(\bm{\phi}_{1},\bm{\theta}_{1},k)+[\tilde{\bm{n}}_{1}]_{k},

(21)

\displaystyle[\bm{y}_{2}]_{k}={\sqrt{P}}\alpha_{2}g_{2}(\bm{\phi}_{2},\bm{\theta}_{2},k)+[\tilde{\bm{n}}_{2}]_{k},

(22)

where
[\tilde{\bm{n}}_{r}]_{k}={1}/{4}\sum_{l=0}^{3}[\mathbf{c}_{r}]_{l}[\bm{n}]_{4k+l}
(see App.
A
). Therefore,
\bm{y}_{r}
,
r=1,2
contain the contribution from the
{r^{\rm{th}}}

RIS
and
\bm{y}_{0}
contain the
LoS
contribution without any interference from the other
RIS
. However, if
\nu\neq 0
, this coding would result in some residual inter-RIS interference and the separation cannot be done perfectly. The solution is to first estimate the
CFO
(
\hat{\nu}
) and remove it from the observations by
\tilde{\bm{y}}=\bm{y}\odot\bm{b}(-\hat{\nu})
, then proceed with this coding. Hence in the following sections, we proceed with estimating the
CFO
and cancel it out from the received signal.

IV
High-level Algorithm Description

In this section, we provide a high-level description of the proposed joint localization and synchronization algorithm to tackle the problem in Sec.
II-D
, covering both the channel-domain parameter estimation and localization. In Sec.
IV-A
, the maximum likelihood solution to the channel parameter estimation problem is presented under both LoS and NLoS scenarios. Then, in Sec.
IV-B
, the maximum likelihood solutions to the localization and synchronization problem are presented, assuming that we already know whether the LoS link exists. Finally, Sec.
IV-C
concludes the section by addressing the joint LoS detection and localization problem, meaning that the presence of the LoS path is unknown. The overall high-level algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

IV-A

The

\displaystyle[\hat{\nu},\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{R},\hat{\alpha}_{0},\hat{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\alpha}_{R}]=

\displaystyle\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\bm{\theta}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\theta}_{R},\\
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{R}\end{subarray}}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\sqrt{P}\alpha_{0}\bm{b}(\nu)-\sqrt{P}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\alpha_{r}\bm{x}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\odot\bm{b}(\nu)\big\rVert^{2}\,,

if

\displaystyle[\hat{\nu},\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{R},\hat{\alpha}_{1},\cdots,\hat{\alpha}_{R}]=

(24)

\displaystyle\argmin_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu,\bm{\theta}_{1},\cdots,\bm{\theta}_{R},\\

IV-B

Localization

The direct

\displaystyle[\hat{\nu},\penalty\ \hat{\bm{p}}]=\argmin_{\nu,\bm{p}}\big\lVert\bm{y}-

\displaystyle\sqrt{P}\hat{\alpha}_{0}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{p}})\bm{b}(\nu)-\sqrt{P}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\hat{\alpha}_{r}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{p}})\bm{x}_{r}(\bm{p})\odot\bm{b}(\nu)\big\rVert^{2},

(29)

in LoS scenario, and

\displaystyle[\hat{\nu},\penalty\ \hat{\bm{p}}]=\argmin_{\nu,\bm{p}}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\sqrt{P}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\hat{\alpha}_{r}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{p}})\bm{x}_{r}(\bm{p})\odot\bm{b}(\nu)\big\rVert^{2},

(30)

in NLoS scenario, where
\hat{\alpha}_{0}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{p}})
and
\hat{\alpha}_{r}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{p}})

1

\nu

\displaystyle{\bm{p}}_{r}=\bm{p}_{{\text{RIS}},r}+\beta_{r}\mathbf{u}_{r},\penalty\ r=1,\cdots,R,

[

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{p}}=\argmin_{\bm{p}}\sum_{r=1}^{R}\big\lVert(\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{{\text{RIS}},r})-((\bm{p}-\bm{p}_{{\text{RIS}},r})^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{u}_{r})\mathbf{u}_{r}\big\rVert^{2}\,.

It follows that
[
42

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{p}}=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{R}\big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}_{r}\mathbf{u}_{r}^{\mathsf{T}}\big)\Big)^{-1}\Big(\sum_{r=1}^{R}\big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{u}_{r}\mathbf{u}_{r}^{\mathsf{T}}\big)\bm{p}_{{\text{RIS}},r}\Big).

(33)

IV-C

Joint
LoS

In practice, the

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\begin{cases}\bm{y}_{{\rm{NLoS}}}(\bm{\zeta},\nu)+\bm{n},&\penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{under\penalty\ \mathcal{H}_{0}}}\\
\bm{y}_{{\rm{LoS}}}(\alpha_{0},\nu)+\bm{y}_{{\rm{NLoS}}}(\bm{\zeta},\nu)+\bm{n},&\penalty\ \penalty\ {\rm{under\penalty\ \mathcal{H}_{1}}}\end{cases},

(34)

where

{\mathcal{H}_{0}}
refers to the case where the
LoS

11
), while the alternate hypothesis

{\mathcal{H}_{1}}
refers to the case in which the
LoS
path exists in (
11
). In (

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{{\rm{LoS}}}(\alpha_{0},\nu)\triangleq\sqrt{P}\alpha_{0}\,\bm{b}(\nu),

(35)

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{{\rm{NLoS}}}(\bm{\zeta},\nu)\triangleq\ \sum_{r=1}^{R}\sqrt{P}\alpha_{r}\,\bm{x}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\odot\bm{b}(\nu).

(36)

The

for the problem in (

) can be expressed as
[
43
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\bm{y})=\frac{\max_{\alpha_{0},\bm{\zeta},\nu}p(\bm{y}\,\lvert\,\mathcal{H}_{1};\alpha_{0},\bm{\zeta},\nu)}{\max_{\bm{\zeta},\nu}p(\bm{y}\,\lvert\,\mathcal{H}_{0};\bm{\zeta},\nu)}\underset{\mathcal{H}_{0}}{\overset{\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\gtrless}}\psi,

(37)

where

Writing the log-likelihood ratio

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\rm{log}}(\bm{y})=\left(\min_{\bm{\zeta},\nu}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{{\rm{NLoS}}}(\bm{\zeta},\nu)\big\rVert^{2}-\right.

\displaystyle\left.\min_{\alpha_{0},\bm{\zeta},\nu}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{{\rm{LoS}}}(\alpha_{0},\nu)-\bm{y}_{{\rm{NLoS}}}(\bm{\zeta},\nu)\big\rVert^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\underset{\mathcal{H}_{0}}{\overset{\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\gtrless}}\psi^{\prime}=\sigma^{2}\log{\psi}.

(38)

There are two separate optimization problems to tackle in (
38

23
) and (
24

V

LoS
in Sec.
V-A
and NLoS in Sec.

38

In this section, we elaborate on the channel parameter estimation procedures that solve (

V-A

Channel Parameter Estimation under
LoS

In this scenario, the
LoS
path is the dominant path as the
RIS
-induced paths are usually very weak. Hence, we treat the
RIS
paths as noise for
CFO
estimation, and then recover the signal per RIS, by harnessing the orthogonal RIS profiles.

V-A
1
CFO Estimation

We form the
ML
estimation of CFO with the assumption that the contribution from the
RIS
paths is negligible. Under this assumption, we can rewrite the ML problem in (
27
) as follows:

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}=\argmin_{\nu}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\sqrt{P}\hat{\alpha}_{0}(\nu)\bm{b}(\nu)\big\rVert^{2}=\argmax_{\nu}{\lvert\bm{b}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\bm{y}\rvert^{2}},

(39)

which is a 1-D line search over the interval
-1/(2T_{s})<\nu<1/(2T_{s})
to find coarse estimates. Then we can implement a 1-D quasi-Newton algorithm to find refined estimates of CFO.

V-A
2
RIS
Separation

Using the estimated
CFO

\hat{\nu}
, we wipe off its effect from the original observation in (
11
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{y}}=\bm{y}\odot\bm{b}(-\hat{\nu}).

(40)

Then, assuming the residual CFO
\nu-\hat{\nu}
is negligible, we separate each
RIS
path by first forming the reshaped CFO-removed signal
\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}
using (
15
) and then filtering it using (
16

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}=\frac{1}{L}\tilde{\mathbf{Y}}^{\mathsf{T}}\mathbf{c}_{r},\penalty\ r=1,\cdots,R.

(41)

The CFO-removed, filtered contribution from the
{r^{\rm{th}}}

RIS
can then be modeled explicitly as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{r}\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})+\bm{n}_{r}.

(42)

Here,
\bm{n}_{r}
contains noise plus any residual interference (due to residual CFO). The term
\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})=\bar{\bm{W}}_{r}^{\mathsf{T}}\bm{a}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{M/L}
, and
\bar{\bm{W}}_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times M/L}
contains the uncoded RIS phase profiles, i.e.,

\displaystyle\bar{\bm{W}}_{r}\triangleq[\bm{a}(\bm{\phi}_{r})\odot\bm{\gamma}_{r,0}\penalty\ \bm{a}(\bm{\phi}_{r})\odot\bm{\gamma}_{r,L}\penalty\ \cdots\penalty\ \bm{a}(\bm{\phi}_{r})\odot\bm{\gamma}_{r,M-L}].

(43)

V-A
3
AoD
Estimation per
RIS

For
CFO
-free
{r^{\rm{th}}}

RIS

42

\displaystyle[\hat{\alpha}_{r},\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{r}]=\argmin_{\alpha_{r},\bm{\theta}_{r}}\big\lVert\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}-\sqrt{P}\alpha_{r}\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\big\rVert^{2}.

(44)

The path gain can be estimated in closed-form as a function of
\bm{\theta}_{r}

\displaystyle\hat{\alpha}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})=\frac{\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}}{\sqrt{P}\left\lVert\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\right\rVert^{2}}.

(45)

Plugging (
45

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{r}=

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{\theta}_{r}}\big\lVert\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}-\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\frac{\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}}{\left\lVert\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\right\rVert^{2}}\big\rVert^{2}

(46)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\argmin_{\bm{\theta}_{r}}{\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\left(\mathbf{I}-\frac{\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}^{\mathsf{H}}(\bm{\theta}_{r})}{\left\lVert\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\right\rVert^{2}}\right)\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}}.

(47)

Therefore, the

44

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{\theta}}_{r}=\argmax_{\bm{\theta}_{r}}\frac{|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{r}^{\mathsf{H}}\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})|^{2}}{\left\lVert\bar{\bm{x}}_{r}(\bm{\theta}_{r})\right\rVert^{2}}.

(48)

For each

r

The ML estimation in (

) requires a 3-D search and is thus computationally expensive.
The motivation of this second approach is to de-couple the effect of
CFO
and
AoD
so as to estimate the
CFO
with a 1-D search. To do so, we can express into
\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times(M/L)}
in (
15
) as

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{D}(\nu)\mathbf{C}\mathbf{H}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}})+\mathbf{N},

(50)

where
\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}}=[\nu,\bm{\theta}_{1}^{\mathsf{T}},\cdots,\bm{\theta}_{R}^{\mathsf{T}}]^{\mathsf{T}}
,
\mathbf{D}(\nu)=\text{diag}[1,e^{j2\pi T_{s}\nu},\ldots,e^{j2\pi(L-1)T_{s}\nu}]\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times L}
and
the matrix
\mathbf{C}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times R}
is the coding matrix which is composed of the coding vectors with length
L
as below

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}=[\mathbf{c}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{c}_{R}]\in\mathbb{R}^{L\times R}\penalty\ .

(51)

In addition, the matrix
\mathbf{H}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}})\in\mathbb{C}^{R\times(M/L)}
is a function of both
CFO
and
AoD
with elements
h_{r,\ell}=[\mathbf{H}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}})]_{r,\ell}
, (
r=1,\cdots,R,\ell=1,\cdots,M/L
) as follows:

\displaystyle h_{r,\ell}=\sqrt{P}\alpha_{r}(\bm{a}^{\mathsf{T}}(\bm{\phi}_{r})\odot{\bm{P}_{r}}^{\mathsf{T}}_{[:,\ell-1]})\bm{a}(\bm{\theta}_{r})e^{j2\pi(\ell-1)LT_{s}\nu},

(52)

and
\mathbf{N}\in\mathbb{C}^{L\times(M/L)}
is the reshaped noise. With this re-modeling and ignoring the dependence of
\mathbf{H}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}})
on the CFO, we de-couple the effect of
CFO
and
AoD
in the measurement model by factoring out matrix
\mathbf{D}(\nu)
, which is a function of only CFO, and treating the matrix
\mathbf{H}(\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}})
as an unstructured matrix. Therefore, it is enough to apply a 1-D grid search to estimate the
CFO
through
\mathbf{D}(\nu)
, without the need to estimate
AoD
jointly. We will drop the dependence of
\mathbf{H}
on
\bm{\chi}_{\text{ch}}
to simplify the notation.

To derive the
CFO
estimator based on the model in (
50
), we begin by vectorizing the observation as

\displaystyle\bm{y}

\displaystyle=\text{vec}(\mathbf{Y})=(\mathbf{I}_{M/L}\otimes(\mathbf{D}(\nu)\mathbf{C}))\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{E}(\nu)\mathbf{h}+\mathbf{n},

(53)

where we used the property of the Kronecker product (eq. 520 in
[
44
]
).
Here,
\mathbf{h}=\text{vec}(\mathbf{H})\ \in\mathbb{C}^{RM/L}
and
\mathbf{E}(\nu)=(\mathbf{I}_{M/L}\otimes(\mathbf{D}(\nu)\mathbf{C}))\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times(RM/L)}
. Accordingly, we formulate the estimator for jointly estimating
\nu
and
\mathbf{h}
as

\displaystyle[\hat{\nu},\hat{\mathbf{h}}]=\argmin_{\nu,\mathbf{h}}\left\lVert\bm{y}-\mathbf{E}(\nu)\mathbf{h}\right\rVert^{2},

(54)

where the conditional estimate of
\mathbf{h}
is derived in closed form as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{h}}(\nu)

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{E}(\nu))^{-1}\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{y}=\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{y},

(55)

since
\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{E}(\nu)=L\mathbf{I}_{RM/L}
.
Hence, we can write the estimate of
\nu
as

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\nu}\big\lVert\bm{y}-\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{E}(\nu)\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\bm{y}\big\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\argmin_{\nu}\big\lVert\left(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\frac{1}{L}\mathbf{E}(\nu)\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\right)\bm{y}\big\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\argmax_{\nu}\big\lVert\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\bm{y}\big\rVert^{2}.

(56)

We can rewrite the objective function in matrix form using

\displaystyle\text{unvec}\left(\mathbf{E}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\bm{y}\right)=(\mathbf{D}(\nu)\mathbf{C})^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{Y}.

(57)

Here, the operator
\text{unvec}(\bm{a})=\bm{A}
transforms the vector
\bm{a}\in\mathbb{C}^{RM/L}
to the matrix
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{C}^{R\times(M/L)}
. Hence, the optimization problem will be

\displaystyle\hat{\nu}=\argmax_{\nu}\left\lVert\mathbf{C}^{\mathsf{H}}\mathbf{D}^{\mathsf{H}}(\nu)\mathbf{Y}\right\rVert^{2}_{\mathrm{F}},

(58)

where
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathrm{F}}
stands for the Frobenius norm.

Note that the dependence of
\mathbf{H}
on
CFO
and
AoD
is ignored in the estimation algorithm. In other words, we do not leverage the full potential of the
CFO
-dependent observations, which is the cost of detangling the effects of
CFO
and
AoD
. Therefore, this method is expected to be less accurate than the previous approach, but the complexity is lower and comparable to Algorithm
2
.

Once an estimate of the
CFO
is obtained, it is possible to remove its effect from the observations as in (
40
), and then apply the temporal decoding and
R
2-D grid searchs to find estimates of
AoD
, as explained in Sec.
V-B1
.
The algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
4
.

Complexity Analysis

A brief complexity analysis is conducted. Assuming a fixed number of grid points
G
in each dimension, the channel parameter estimation under
LoS
(Algorithm
2
) has a complexity of
\mathcal{O}(GM+RG^{2}M+R^{2})

Remark 4
.

While our focus is on a frugal single-antenna, single-carrier setup, the proposed algorithms can be extended to systems with multiple antennas and multiple carriers. In such cases, additional measurements such as
AoD
from the LoS path and
ToA
(
ToA
) become available, enabling more accurate localization and synchronization. These extensions offer improved accuracy at the cost of increased infrastructure and resource usage.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a frugal approach for 3-D localization and frequency synchronization of a single-antenna stationary UE using SISO RIS-enabled communication, even without a LoS path. Our method leverages a single-antenna BS and multiple RISs under NB communication, reducing both system complexity and cost.

We developed estimation algorithms and a GLRT-based LoS detector, and showed through simulations that our method attains theoretical performance bounds at moderate to high transmit powers. Accurate CFO estimation proved essential, as neglecting it caused severe degradation. Robustness was confirmed under multipath with Rician factors as small as 10, while mobility analysis showed the approach is best suited for stationary or very low-speed users.

The algorithms offer practical computational complexity, particularly with parallel processing. Overall, our results demonstrate that accurate localization is possible with minimal spectral and hardware resources, supporting sustainable 6G deployments. Future work includes experimental validation and extending the framework to mobile UEs.

Positivity of coinvariant divisors on M ¯ 0 , n \overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n} and the parafermions
Avik Chakravarty

Abstract
We give criteria for determining the positivity of line bundles coming from vertex operator algebras (VOAs) on the moduli space M ¯ 0 , n \overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n} of rational curves with n n marked points. The criteria use the multiplicative structure of VOA representations encoded in the fusion ring. Using them, we construct positive line bundles on M ¯ 0 , n \overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n} from certain parafermion VOAs. These give the first examples of commutant VOAs producing positive line bundles.

The fusion ring of a vertex operator algebra

\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{g,n}

Our main result, Theorem

1.1
Sketch of the proof for Theorem
1.1

We provide a brief outline of the proof of the results in the theorem, paraphrasing technical details.
(a) A positive subring
\mathcal{S}
has two properties: it is
F
-positive, and every coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
associated to
V
-modules in
\mathcal{S}
can be written as
\mathbb{D}=cK_{\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}}+E
for some
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and an effective sum
E
of boundary divisors. The first property guarantees that
\mathbb{D}
is an
F
-divisor, while the second enables a reduction of the nefness of
\mathbb{D}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
to verifying the nefness of a coinvariant divisor on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,t}
for some
4\leq t\leq 7
. The conclusion then follows from Theorem
2.15
and Proposition
3.5
.
(b) Given a symmetric coinvariant
F
-divisor
\mathbb{D}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
associated to a representation
W\in\mathcal{S}
of maximal conformal weight, we construct an
F
-divisor
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{n}
such that
f^{*}\mathbb{D}^{\prime}=\mathbb{D}
via the flag map
f\colon\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}\to\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{n}
obtained by attaching an elliptic curve at each marked point of the rational curve. The maximality of the conformal weight of
W
and the fact that
\mathbb{D}
is an
F
-divisor together ensure that
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}
is nef on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{n}
, and hence
\mathbb{D}
is nef as the pullback of a nef divisor.
(c) If
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
form a proportional subring pair, then there exists an injective map
f\colon\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{sim}}\to(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})^{\mathrm{sim}}
between their simple objects, inducing an injection of rings
f\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Moreover, the ratio of conformal weights
\mathrm{cw}(f(W))/\mathrm{cw}(W)
is a constant positive rational number for all simple modules
W\in\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{sim}}\setminus{V}
. The ring injection implies that the fusion rules of
\mathcal{S}
and its image in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
coincide. Since conformal weight ratios are constant, any coinvariant divisor associated to simple modules in
\mathcal{S}
is a rational multiple of a coinvariant divisor associated to simple modules in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. The conclusion follows from Equation
1
.

2.2.1
Notation

In this subsection, we establish some notation needed for the rest of the paper corresponding to the representation theory of Lie algebras. Readers are recommended to consult
[

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra of rank
l
. Let
\Delta
denote the root system and
\Delta_{+}
denote the set of positive roots. The simple roots are
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{l}
, and the root lattice is
Q=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
. Denote the highest root as
\theta
and
\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\alpha
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
has lie sub-algebras
\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}
associated with each
\alpha\in\Lambda_{+}
that is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
as Lie algebras, that is, as a vector space
\mathfrak{g}^{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}x_{\alpha}+\mathbb{C}h_{\alpha}+\mathbb{C}x_{-\alpha}
, with the isomorphism to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
given by
x_{\alpha}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
,
x_{-\alpha}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}
and
h_{\alpha}\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}
.

Normalize the Cartan-Killing form
\langle-,-\rangle
so that
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2
if
\alpha\in\Delta
is a long root. Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denote the Cartan lie subalgebra. The fundamental weights
\Lambda_{i}
of
\mathfrak{g}
are defined as the roots
\Lambda_{i}\in Q
so that
\frac{2\langle\Lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}{\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j}\rangle}=\delta_{ij}
for all
1\leq j\leq l
. The weight lattice
P
of
\mathfrak{g}
is the set of elements
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
so that
\frac{2\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z}
for all
\alpha\in\Delta
and we can write it in terms of the fundamental weights as
P=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{Z}\Lambda_{i}.

The set of positive weights is denoted by
P_{+}
, and we denote by
P_{+}^{k}
the subset

P_{+}^{k}=\{\Lambda\in P\mid\langle\Lambda,\theta\rangle\leq k\}.

The sub-lattice of
Q
spanned by the long roots is denoted by
Q_{L}
and its dual is the set
Q^{\perp}_{L}=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\forall\alpha\in Q_{L}\}
, which is equal to
P
by
[
ADJR18
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Finally, the finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are completely characterized by the set of dominant integral weights
\Lambda
, and we denote them by
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)
. The weight space decomposition of
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)
is given by
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}}L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)_{\lambda}
, where
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)_{\lambda}
is the weight space of
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)
with weight
\lambda
. Let
P(L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda))=\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)_{\lambda}\neq 0\}
.

3
Positivity of coinvariant divisors

In this section, we describe several criteria for the positivity of coinvariant divisors on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
arising from representations of a vertex operator algebra
V
satisfying the following assumption.

Assumption 3.1
.

V
is a self-contragradient and rational vertex operator algebra of CFT-type. Moreover, all sheaves of coinvariants
\mathcal{F}(V,M^{\bullet})
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
associated with a collection of
n
admissible
V
-modules
M^{\bullet}
are vector bundles. Finally, the central charge of
V
and the conformal weight of each simple
V
-module are rational numbers.

Indeed, an extensive collection of self-contragradient CFT-type VOAs satisfy Assumption
3.1
. See Section
2.1.2
for more details. We first need the following definition to state the three criteria we establish.

Definition 3.1
.

A subring
\mathcal{S}
of the fusion ring of a vertex operator algebra
V
is
F
-positive
if for any four elements
M^{1},\ldots,M^{4}\in\mathcal{S}
, the degree of the corresponding coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(V,M^{\bullet})
is non-negative.

Moreover,
\mathcal{S}
is
positive
if for any collection of
n\geq 8
elements
M^{1},\ldots,M^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, the divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(V,M^{\bullet})
can be written as
cK_{\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}}+E
for some
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
and an effective sum
E
of boundary divisors.

Remark 3.2
.

The definition for
F
-positivity of a subring comes naturally from the factorization theorem (Theorem
2.10
). See the proof of Lemma
3.4
for an explicit description. The positivity criterion of subrings may appear more complicated to check, but the simplicity of checking this criterion lies in Proposition
3.5
. In particular, because of Proposition
3.5
, the positivity of an
F
-positive subring reduces to simply calculating conformal weights of all simple
V
-modules in
\mathcal{S}
and checking for certain inequalities. For an explicit example, see the proof of Theorem
4.36
.

Theorem 3.3

(Theorem
1.1
.i.)
.

A coinvariant divisor on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
associated with simple
V
-modules of a positive subring of the fusion ring of
V
is nef.

In order to prove this theorem, we need the following two results.

Lemma 3.4
.

A coinvariant divisor associated with simple
V
-modules of a
F
-positive subring of the fusion ring is an
F
-divisor; it intersects every
F
-curve non-negatively.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{D}
be a coinvariant divisor associated with
n
simple
V
-modules
M^{1},\ldots,M^{n}
in a
F
-positive subring
\mathcal{S}
.

\mathbb{D}\cdot F_{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}}=\sum_{W^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{W}}\deg\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(V,W^{\bullet})\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{4}\text{ rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,|I_{i}|+1}(V,M^{I_{i}}\otimes W^{i}),

where
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{4}I_{i}=[n]
is a partition, and the sum is taken over all four tuples
(W^{1},\ldots,W^{4})\in\mathcal{W}^{\oplus 4}
of simple
V
-modules. This formula follows from factorization (Theorem
2.10
).
We claim that
W^{i}\in\mathcal{S}^{sim}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{W}
for each
i
. If the claim holds, then the conclusion follows immediately. Indeed, since
\mathcal{S}
is
F
-positive, the degree term in each summand is non-negative, and the rank of vector bundles is always non-negative.

To prove the claim, it suffices to show that if
W^{i}\notin\mathcal{S}
for any
1\leq i\leq 4
, the corresponding summand contributes trivially to the sum. We prove this by induction. Given
M^{1},M^{2},M^{3}\in\mathcal{S}
and any
W\in\mathcal{W}
, by factorization

\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{3}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{W}}\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{1}\otimes W^{\prime}\otimes X)\cdot\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{2}\otimes M^{3}\otimes X^{\prime}).

Since
M^{2},M^{3}\in\mathcal{S}
, we see that
\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{2}\otimes M^{3}\otimes X^{\prime})=0
if
X\notin\mathcal{S}
and hence

\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{3}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})=\sum_{X\in S}\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{1}\otimes W^{\prime}\otimes X).

Since
M^{1},X\in\mathcal{S}
, we must have
W^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
in order to possibly have a non-trivial contribution for any of the summands running over
X\in\mathcal{S}
. As the trivial module is in the subring, we have
W\in\mathcal{S}
. Assume that for any
M^{1},\ldots,M^{n-1}\in\mathcal{S}
and any
W\in\mathcal{W}
, we have that
\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n-1}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})=0
if
W\notin\mathcal{S}
. Then, for any element
M^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
,

\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n+1}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})=\sum_{X_{1}\in\mathcal{W}}\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n-2}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime}\otimes X_{1})\cdot\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{n-1}\otimes M^{n}\otimes X_{1}^{\prime}).

Since
M^{n-1},M^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
, we see that
\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(V,M^{n-1}\otimes M^{n}\otimes X_{1}^{\prime})=0
if
X_{1}\notin\mathcal{S}
and hence

\displaystyle\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n+1}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{X_{1}\in\mathcal{S}}\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n-2}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime}\otimes X_{1}),

and by induction hypothesis, if
W\notin\mathcal{S}
, then
\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,n+1}(V,\bigotimes_{j=1}^{n}M^{j}\otimes W^{\prime})=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
V
be a rational vertex operator algebra and
\mathcal{S}
be a subring of the fusion ring of
V
. Under the clutching map
\rho:\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{g_{1},|I|+1}\times\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{g_{2},|I^{c}|+1}\to\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{g,n}
with projection maps
\rho_{I}:=\text{pr}_{1}\circ\rho_{I}
and
\rho_{I^{c}}:=\text{pr}_{2}\circ\rho_{I^{c}}
, the pullbacks of a coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{g,n}(V,M^{\bullet})
, associated with simple
V

in

, can be written as an effective sum of coinvariant divisors with irreducible representations in

.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}_{g,n}(V,M^{\bullet})
be a coinvariant vector bundle associated with simple
V
-modules
M^{1},\ldots,M^{n}\in\mathcal{S}
. By factorization (Theorem

\mathbb{V}_{g,n}(V,M^{\bullet})

\rho^{*}_{I}\mathbb{V}_{g,n}(V,M^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{W\in\mathcal{S}^{sim}}\mathbb{V}_{g_{1},|I|+1}(V,M^{I}\otimes W)^{\oplus\text{ rank }\mathbb{V}_{g_{2},|I^{C}|+1}(V,M^{I^{C}}\otimes W^{\prime})},

where we only consider

that are in
\mathcal{S}^{sim}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{W}
, as
\mathcal{S}
is a subring. Its first Chern class is

\rho^{*}_{I}\mathbb{D}_{g,n}(V,M^{\bullet})=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{S}^{sim}}\left(\text{ rank }\mathbb{V}_{g_{2},|I^{C}|+1}(V,M^{I^{C}}\otimes W^{\prime})\right)\quad\mathbb{D}_{g_{1},|I|+1}(V,M^{I}\otimes W).

(2)

Since
\left(\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{g_{2},|I^{C}|+1}(V,M^{I^{C}}\otimes W^{\prime})\right)

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a positive subring of the fusion ring, and let
\mathbb{D}
denote the coinvariant divisor

V

so that
\mathbb{D}\cdot R<0

and

\mathbb{D}\cdot\delta_{0,I}<0
for some

, that is,
\rho_{I}^{*}\mathbb{D}\cdot C<0

\rho^{*}_{I}\mathbb{D}
is again an effective sum of coinvariant divisors given by the representations of the positive subring
\mathcal{S}
and therefore, we continue the process of pulling back iteratively until

pulls back to a divisor, denote it by
\mathbb{D}^{\prime}

∎

Theorem 3.6

(Theorem

.ii.)
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subring of the fusion ring such that the conformal weight of every simple module

is non-negative, and let

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{D}:=a\lambda-\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}b_{i}\delta_{i}

Proposition 3.9

Let

t
so that
t+\frac{c}{2}+cw(W)-12cw(\widetilde{W})\geq 0
for any
W\in\mathcal{S}^{sim}
and any
\widetilde{W}\in\mathcal{W}^{sim}
. Now pulling back the
F
-divisor
t\lambda+\mathbb{D}
via the map
F:\overline{M}_{0,n+1}\to\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{g,n}
, which attaches a genus
g
curve at the
(n+1)
-th marked point, we get an effective sum of coinvariant divisors given by representations in
\mathcal{S}
(by Proposition
3.5
). By Theorem
3.3
,
F^{*}(t\lambda+\mathbb{D})=F^{*}\mathbb{D}
is nef and therefore
t\lambda+\mathbb{D}
is nef by
[
GKM02
, Theorem 0.3]
.
∎

Parafermions and associated positive divisors on

In this section, we describe the
F
-positive and positive subrings of the parafermion algebras
K(\mathfrak{g},k)
for the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
. Section
4.1
carries out the necessary computations and introduces the notation required to apply the general results presented in Section
3
. The structure of the positive subrings for
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
is detailed in Section
4.2
, and the corresponding results for
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k)
are presented in Section
4.3
.

4.1
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
-Modules

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
be the Lie algebra of rank
r
for this section, some integer
r\geq 1
. Let
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}
denote the simple roots with the normalized Cartan-Killing form satisfying

\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\rangle=2\quad 1\leq i\leq r,\quad\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}-1&\text{ if }|i-j|=1\\
0&\text{ if }|i-j|>1.\end{cases}

The
s
-th fundamental weight
\Lambda_{s}
is given by

(r+1)\Lambda_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{s}i(r+1-s)\alpha_{i}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}s(r+1-i)\alpha_{i}

for each
1\leq s\leq r
, and it follows that
\langle\Lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
, where
\delta_{i,j}
is the Kronecker delta function. The maximal root is
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}
and

2\rho=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\alpha=\sum_{1\leq a\leq b\leq r}\sum_{t=a}^{b}\alpha_{t}.

The root lattice and the weight lattice are
Q=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{a}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}
and
P=\bigoplus_{s=1}^{r}\Lambda_{s}
, respectively, with
Q_{L}\subset Q
, the sub-lattice of long roots, equal to
Q
.

4.1.1
Simple modules, their conformal weights and the fusion rules

Lemma 4.1
.

The set of inequivalent irreducible
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
-modules is a finite set of cardinality
\frac{k+1}{r}\binom{k+r}{r-1}
.

Proof.

To prove this lemma, we interpret Theorems
2.22
and
2.23
for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
. The set of inequivalent modules is

\mathcal{W}_{r}:=\{M^{\Lambda,\Lambda+\lambda}\mid\Lambda\in P^{k}_{+},\lambda\in Q/kQ\}/\sim,

where
M^{\Lambda,\Lambda+\lambda}\sim M^{\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}
if and only if
M^{\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime}}\cong M^{\Lambda^{(s)},\Lambda+\lambda+k\Lambda_{s}}
for each
1\leq s\leq r
. Here, we have used the fact that for
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
, the maximal root is
\theta=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}
, that is,
a_{i}=1
for each
i
(see Theorem
2.22
(d)) and therefore it follows that
I=\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. It follows from
[
Li01
]
that
|P/Q|=|I|+1
, that is,
|P/Q|=r+1
.

Moreover, since
\langle\Lambda_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\delta_{i,j}
, we obtain that for any
(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\oplus r}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}\Lambda_{s},\sum_{i=1}^{r}\alpha_{i}\rangle=\sum_{s=1}^{r}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\delta_{s,i}=\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s},

P^{k}_{+}=\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}\Lambda_{s}\mid\sum_{s=1}^{r}m_{s}\leq k,\,m_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\quad\forall 1\leq s\leq r\right\},\quad|P^{k}_{+}|=\sum_{n=0}^{k}\binom{n+r-1}{r-1}=\frac{k+1}{r}\binom{k+r}{r-1},

where
\binom{n+r-1}{r-1}
counts the number of non-negative integer solutions to
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{r}=n
.
Finally,
Q/kQ=\{\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\mid(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}^{\oplus r}\}
and therefore, the set
\mathcal{W}_{r}
is finite and its cardinality is

|\mathcal{W}_{r}|=\frac{|P^{k}_{+}||Q/kQ_{L}|}{|P/Q|}=\frac{(k+1)k^{r}}{r(r+1)}\binom{k+r}{r-1}.

∎

Remark 4.2
.

Note that for
k=3
, there are
\frac{3^{r-1}}{2}(r+2)(r+3)
irreducible inequivalent
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},3)
-modules, which shows that the number of modules in consideration grows exponentially with
r
for any fixed level
k
.

Next, we explicitly describe the conformal weight of specific modules we need later in the section. In the second superscript of
M^{\Lambda,\Lambda+\lambda}
,
\lambda\in Q/kQ
is chosen upto equivalence relation and therefore, we may replace
\lambda
by
-\lambda
and consider the conformal weight of the modules
M^{\Lambda,\Lambda-\lambda}
instead. The advantage of this approach is that all weights of
L_{\mathfrak{g}}(\Lambda)
are of the form
\Lambda-\lambda
for some
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, which will be useful in the proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given
\Lambda_{s}\in P^{k}_{+}
and
k\Lambda_{s}-\lambda\in P(L_{\mathfrak{g}(k\Lambda_{s})})
with
\lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
for some integers
0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}<k
and an integer
1\leq s\leq r
, conformal weight of the simple
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
-module
M^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
is given by

cw^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}=a_{s}-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right).

Proof.

We first note that

\langle k\Lambda_{s},\lambda\rangle=\langle k\Lambda_{s},\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\rangle=k\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\langle\Lambda_{s},\alpha_{i}\rangle=ka_{s},

\langle k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}\rangle=k^{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{s}\langle\Lambda_{s},i(r+1-s)\alpha_{i}\rangle+\sum_{i=s+1}^{r}\langle\Lambda_{s},s(r+1-i)\alpha_{i}\rangle\right)=\frac{k^{2}}{r+1}s(r+1-s),

\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle=\langle\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\alpha_{j}\rangle=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=2\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}-2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j},

\langle k\Lambda_{s},2\rho\rangle=k|\{((a,b)\mid 1\leq a\leq s\leq b\leq r\}|=ks(r+1-s).

Substituting the equations above to

cw^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}=\frac{1}{2(k+r+1)}\left(\langle k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}\rangle+\langle k\Lambda_{s},2\rho\rangle\right)-\frac{1}{2k}\left(\langle k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}\rangle-2\langle k\Lambda_{s},\lambda\rangle+\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle\right),

we get the desired identity.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

For integers
0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}<k
, the conformal weight of the simple module
M^{0,0-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
is

cw^{0,0-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}a_{i}-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right).

Proof.

Since
P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(0))=\{0\}
, the conformal weight is not directly defined for the module
M^{0,0-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
if any
a_{i}\neq 0
. However, by Theorem
2.24
, we see that
0^{(s)}=k\Lambda_{s}
for each
1\leq s\leq r
, i.e.,

M^{0,0-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}\cong M^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}

for each integer
1\leq s\leq r
. So, we have to find an integer
s
so that
k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}
is a weight of
L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s})
, that is, we must verify that
(k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i})\in P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s}))
.
Recall that given weights
\alpha,\mu\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
, the
\alpha
weight-string through
\mu
is given by

L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(\mu+q\alpha)\oplus L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(\mu+(q-1)\alpha)\oplus\cdots L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(\mu)\oplus L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(\mu-\alpha)\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(\mu-r\alpha),

where
r,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle=r-q
. Letting
\mu=k\Lambda_{s}
and
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
, we see that
\mu
is the highest weight of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}
-module
L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s})
. This implies that
q=0
and
r=\langle k\Lambda_{s},\alpha_{i}\rangle=k\delta_{si}
. Therefore, the
\alpha_{s}
weight-string through
k\Lambda_{s}
is
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s}-j\alpha_{s})
. Since
k\alpha_{s}=0
for the parafermions
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
, we see that the index
j
above lies in the set
\{0,1,2,\ldots,k-1\}
. This implies that

P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s}))\supset\{k\Lambda_{s}-j\alpha_{s}\mid 0\leq j\leq k-1\}.

For each
1\leq j\leq k-1
, we know that
\langle k\Lambda_{s}-j\alpha_{s},\alpha_{i}\rangle=j
if
|s-i|=1
and zero otherwise. Therefore,

P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s}))\supset\{k\Lambda_{s}-a_{s}\alpha_{s}-a_{s-1}\alpha_{s-1}-a_{s+1}\alpha_{s+1}\mid a_{s+1},a_{s-1}\leq a_{s}<k\}.

Continuing this way, we find that

P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1}}(k\Lambda_{s}))\supset\{k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}\mid a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{s},\quad a_{r}\leq\ldots\leq a_{s},\quad 0\leq a_{i}\leq k-1\forall 1\leq i\leq r\}.

Returning to the initial data
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}\}
, let
A=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}a_{i}
and let
s=\min\{i\mid a_{i}=A\}
. Note that conformal weight of
M^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
given in Lemma
4.3
is
Sym{(r-1)}
invariant under the actions of

\sigma:(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s-1},a_{s},a_{s+1},\ldots,a_{r})\mapsto(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(s-1)},a_{s},a_{\sigma(s+1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(r)}),

fixing
s
and permuting all the remaining indices. Therefore, we may assume that

a_{1}\leq\ldots\leq a_{s},\quad\text{and}\quad a_{r}\leq\ldots\leq a_{s}.

This means the conformal weight of
M^{0,0-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
is equal to the conformal weight of
M^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\alpha_{i}}
with
s
defined to be the smallest integer so that
a_{s}=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}a_{i}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

4.1.2
An example:
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)

We conclude with an explicit description for the modules of
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
, obtained by specializing the general framework for
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
discussed earlier in this section to the case
r=1
. These results will be used in Section
4.2
. Related descriptions appeared in
[
DLY10
,
ALY14
,
DW15
]
, formulated in a more specialized setting that directly exploits the structure of
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and uses different notation (see Notation
4.5
).

Let
r=1
. Let
\alpha
be the positive root,
\Lambda=\alpha/2
be the fundamental weight and
\Delta=\{\pm\alpha\}
be the root system,
2\rho=\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta_{+}}\gamma=\alpha
and the maximal root
\theta=\alpha
. The root and weight lattices are
Q=\mathbb{Z}\alpha
and
P=\mathbb{Z}(\alpha/2)
, respectively. The normalized Cartan-Killing form is defined so that
\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle=2
and therefore
\langle\Lambda,\alpha\rangle=\langle\alpha/2,\alpha\rangle=1
and
\langle\Lambda,\Lambda\rangle=\frac{1}{2}
. We see that
P^{k}_{+}=\{a\Lambda\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},a\leq k\}

since
\langle a\Lambda,\theta\rangle=a
. A priori, the modules are of the form
M^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha},
where
0\leq a\leq k
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}
are arbitrary integers. However, since
M^{\Lambda,\lambda}\cong M^{\Lambda,\lambda-k\beta}
for every
\beta\in Q
, we see that the set of irreducible modules is

\{M^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha}\mid 0\leq a\leq k,0\leq b\leq k-1\}.

Using Example
2.25
, we have

M^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha}\cong M^{(k-a)\Lambda,k\Lambda+a\Lambda-b\alpha}=M^{(k-a)\Lambda,(k-a)\Lambda-(b-a)\alpha}.

Since
b-a<k-a
, we see that the set of inequivalent irreducible modules is

\mathcal{W}_{1}:=\{M^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha}\mid 0\leq b<a\leq k\}.

Here, the subscript
1
stands for
r=1
. Since
\langle a\Lambda,\alpha\rangle=a
, we see that
\{0,1,\ldots,a\}\subset P(L_{\mathfrak{sl}_{2}}(a\Lambda))
and since
0\leq b<a
, the conformal weight is defined for each element in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and it is given as

\displaystyle cw^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle a\Lambda,a\Lambda+\alpha\rangle}{2(k+2)}-\frac{\langle a\Lambda-b\alpha,a\Lambda-b\alpha\rangle}{2k}=\frac{1}{2k(k+2)}\left(k(a+2ab-2b^{2})-(a-2b)^{2}\right).

Finally, the fusion rule is given by

\displaystyle M^{a\Lambda,a\Lambda-b\alpha}\boxtimes M^{a^{\prime}\Lambda,a^{\prime}\Lambda-b^{\prime}\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{c=0}^{k}N^{c}_{a,a^{\prime}}M^{c\Lambda,a\Lambda+a^{\prime}\Lambda-b\alpha-b^{\prime}\alpha}=\sum_{c=0}^{k}N^{c}_{a,a^{\prime}}M^{c\Lambda,c\Lambda-\overline{\frac{1}{2}(2b+2b^{\prime}-a-a^{\prime}+c)}\alpha},

where
N^{c}_{a,a^{\prime}}
are the fusion rules for the irreducible
L_{\widehat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k)
-modules and
\overline{a}
is residue of
a
modulo
k
.

Notation 4.5
.

In order to be consistent with the literature
[
DLY10
,
ALY14
,
DW15
]
, let us denote the
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-module
M^{i\Lambda,i\Lambda-j\alpha}
by
M^{i,j}
. Then the set of inequivalent irreducible modules is
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{M^{i,j}\mid 0\leq i,j\leq k\}/\cong
with the isomorphism given by
M^{i,j}\cong M^{k-i,j-i}
, that is,

\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{M^{i,j}\mid 0\leq j<i\leq k\},

and
|\mathcal{W}_{1}|=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
.
This agrees with
[
DLY10
, Theorem 4.4]
,
[
ALY14
, Theorem 8.2]
.
It follows that the conformal weight of
M^{i,j}
is

cw^{i,j}:=\frac{1}{2k(k+2)}\left(k(i+2ij-2j^{2})-(i-2j)^{2}\right),

(3)

which agrees with the formula first established in
[
DLY10
, Proposition 4.5]
.
Finally, the fusion rules simplify to

M^{i,i^{\prime}}\boxtimes_{K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)}M^{j,j^{\prime}}=\sum_{l}M^{l,\overline{\frac{1}{2}(2i^{\prime}-i+2j^{\prime}-j+l)}},

where
0\leq i,j\leq k
,
0\leq i^{\prime},j^{\prime}\leq k-1
,
i+j+l\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
i+j+l\leq 2k.

Moreover, all modules
M^{l,\overline{\frac{1}{2}(2i^{\prime}-i+2j^{\prime}-j+l)}}
in the summand are inequivalent irreducible modules. This corresponds to Theorem 4.2 in
[
DW15
]
.

4.2
Positive divisors associated to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
Parafermions

Let
k\geq 1
be any integer and let
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
denote the parafermion vertex operator algebra at level
k
associated to the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. There are
\frac{k(k+1)}{2}
irreducible inequivalent
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-modules forming the set

\mathcal{W}_{1}:=\{M^{i,j}\mid 0\leq j<i\leq k\}.
For additional details, we direct the reader to Section
4.1
.

4.2.1
Rank and degree Formulas

In order to understand the positivity of the coinvariant divisors on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
associated with the representations above, we first need to understand the rank of the coinvariant vector bundles

\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes M^{b,b^{\prime}}\otimes M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}}),

and the degree of their first Chern classes, for any four modules
M^{a,a^{\prime}},M^{b,b^{\prime}},M^{c,c^{\prime}},M^{d,d^{\prime}}
in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
. We address this in the subsequent two propositions. First, we set some notation and a lemma we need.

Notation 4.6
.

For ease of discussion, we will denote rank of the bundle
\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{a_{i},a^{\prime}_{i}})
by
\mu_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}
or
\mu(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{a_{i},a^{\prime}_{i}})
and the degree of its first Chern class by
d_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}
or
d(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{a_{i},a^{\prime}_{i}})
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
M^{a,a^{\prime}}
be an irreducible simple
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-module. Then, its contragradient dual is
M^{a,a-a^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

Since the VOA is rational, the dual of
M^{a,a^{\prime}}
is a another simple module
M^{b,b^{\prime}}
so that the dimension
\mu_{a,b,0}
of the vector space
\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes M^{b,b^{\prime}}\otimes M^{0,0})=1
.
By fusion rule, we see that
\mu_{a,b,0}=1
if and only if
|a-b|\leq 0\leq\min(a+b,2k-a-b)
and
a+b\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
0=\frac{1}{2}(a+b-2a^{\prime}-2b^{\prime})\mod k
. Therefore, we must have
a=b
and
a-a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}=0\mod k
, that is,
b=a
and
b^{\prime}=a-a^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

The module
M^{2a,a}
is self-dual and the contragradient module of
M^{k,a}
is
M^{k,k-a}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
M^{a,a^{\prime}},M^{b,b^{\prime}},M^{c,c^{\prime}},M^{d,d^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
be any four simple
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-modules and let
s:=a+b+c+d
and
s^{\prime}:=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}
.
The rank of the vector bundle
\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes M^{b,b^{\prime}}\otimes M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}})
is

\mu_{a,b,c,d}=\begin{cases}|A|&\text{ if }s=0\mod 2\text{ and }s/2=s^{\prime}\mod k,\\
|B|&\text{ if }s=k\mod 2\text{ and }(s-k)/2=s^{\prime}\mod k,\\
0&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

Here
|A|
and
|B|
denotes size of the sets
A
and
B
, respectively, which are defined to be

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\{\max(b-a,d-c)\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-c-d)\mid t=a+b\mod 2\},\text{ and }

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\{\max(b-a,|k-c-d|)\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-a-b,k-d+c)\mid t=a+b\mod 2\}.

Proof.

By factorization, we have

\mu_{a,b,c,d}=\sum_{M^{t,t^{\prime}}}\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,3}(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes M^{b,b^{\prime}}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}})\mu(M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}}\otimes M^{t,t-t^{\prime}}),

where the sum is taken over the irreducible inequivalent modules
M^{t,t^{\prime}}
of the parafermions and
M^{t,t-t^{\prime}}=(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime}
by the Lemma above. Note that
\mu(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes M^{b,b^{\prime}}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}})=1
if and only if
(t,t^{\prime})
is of the form where

b-a\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-a-b),\quad t+a+b=0\mod 2,\quad t^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b-2a^{\prime}-2b^{\prime}+t)

or its equivalent form (cf. Notation
4.5
)

m_{k-t,k-t+t^{\prime}}=m_{k-t,\frac{1}{2}(a+b-2a^{\prime}-2b^{\prime}-t)\mod k}

with the same restrictions on
t
. Similarly,
\mu(m_{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes m_{d,d^{\prime}}\otimes m^{\prime}_{s,s^{\prime}})=1
if and only if
m_{s,s^{\prime}}
is of the form

d-c\leq t\leq\min(c+d,2k-c-d),\quad s+c+d=0\mod 2,\quad s^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(2c^{\prime}+2d^{\prime}-a-b+s).

Here, we don’t need the equivalence form of
m_{s,s^{\prime}}
given by
m_{k-s,k-s+s^{\prime}}
since we only need to consider values of
t,t^{\prime},s,s^{\prime}
so that
m_{t,t^{\prime}}=m_{s,s^{\prime}}
or
m_{k-t,k-t+t^{\prime}}=m_{s,s^{\prime}}
. Considering these equations with
m_{k-s,k-s+s^{\prime}}
replacing
m_{s,s^{\prime}}
will result in double counting. For the first case, letting
m_{t,t^{\prime}}=m_{s,s^{\prime}}
, we see that
a+b+c=0\mod 2
based on the condition
t=a+b=c+d\mod 2
and
\frac{1}{2}(a+d+c+d)=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}\mod k
based on the condition

\max(b-a,d-c)\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-c-d)
. Similarly, for the second case letting
m_{k-t,k-t+t^{\prime}}=m_{s,s^{\prime}}

\frac{1}{2}(a+b+c+d-k)=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}\mod k
and
\max(|c+d-k|,b-a)\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-a-b,k-d+c)
.
∎

Proposition 4.12

4.2.2
Positive coinvariant divisors

The
F
-positivity of subrings of the fusion ring of the parafermions
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
reduces to understanding conditions for the degree formula above to be non-negative. However, the highly non-trivial nature of the formula above creates a combinatorial obstruction to doing so in complete generality. The proof suggests that the non-trivial nature of the formula derives from understanding the modules that appear after factorization and their conformal weights. The combinatorial complexity simplifies if we consider subrings
\mathcal{T}(k)
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
of the fusion ring generated by simple modules of the form
M^{2a,a}
and
M^{k,b}
, respectively, that is,

\mathcal{T}(k):=\{\sum_{i}z_{i}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}}\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ is nonzero for finitely many }i\text{ and }a_{i}\in[0,k/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}\},\text{ and }

\mathcal{S}_{1}(k):=\{\sum_{i}z_{i}M^{k,a_{i}}\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ is nonzero for finitely many }i\text{ and }a_{i}\in[0,k-1]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. A coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
whose associated simple modules lie in the subring
\mathcal{T}(k)
is semi-ample. The same conclusion holds for coinvariant divisors arising from the subring
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
.

Proof.

Recall that all coinvariant divisors associated to
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,0)
-representations are base-point free
[
Fak12
,
DG23
]
. We prove that the subring
\mathcal{T}(k)
(resp.,
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
) forms a proportional pairing with a subring of the fusion ring of
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,0)
(resp.,
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,0)
). The conclusion then follows from Theorem
3.8
, since every coinvariant divisor associated to a representation in
\mathcal{T}(k)
(resp.,
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
) can be expressed as a positive rational multiple of a base-point free divisor arising from the representation theory of the affine VOA
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,0)
(resp.,
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,0)
).

i.

For
\mathcal{T}(k)
: For any two elements
M^{2a,a}
and
M^{2b,b}
, the fusion rule dictates
\mu(M^{2a,a}\otimes M^{2b,b}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}})=1
if and only if
|2a-2b|\leq t\leq\min(2a+2b,2k-2a-2b)
,
t+2a+2b\in 2\mathbb{Z}
and
t^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(t)\mod k
, that is,
(t,t^{\prime})=(2c,c)
for some integer
|2a-2b|\leq 2c\leq\min(2a+2b,2k-2a-2b)
. The fusion rule for modules in the subring

\mathcal{Z}(k):=\{\sum_{i}z_{i}L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,2a_{i}\Lambda)\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ is nonzero for finitely many }i\text{ and }a_{i}\in[0,k/2]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}

is exactly the same. We have a ring isomorphism
f:\mathcal{T}(k)\to\mathcal{Z}(k)
defined by
M^{2a,a}\mapsto L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,2a\Lambda)
. Moreover, the ratio of the conformal weights is given by (see Equation
3
and
[
Fak12
, Section 4]
)

\frac{\text{cw}(M^{2a,a})}{\text{cw}(L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,2a\Lambda))}=\left(\frac{a+a^{2}}{k+2}\right)/\left(2a^{2}+2a\right)=\frac{1}{2(k+2)},

which is constant for any level
k
. Therefore,
\mathcal{T}(k)
and
\mathcal{Z}(k)
form a proportional pairing.

ii.

For
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
: Similarly, the fusion ring
\mathcal{R}(k)
of the affine VOA
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,0)

\mathcal{R}(k):=\{\sum_{i}z_{i}L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,\Lambda_{i})\mid z_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ is nonzero for finitely many }i\}

forms a pairing with
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
via the isomorphism
f:M^{k,a}\mapsto L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,\Lambda_{a})
with conformal ratio

\frac{cw(M^{k,a})}{cw(L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,\Lambda_{a}))}=\left(\frac{2a(k+2)(k-a)}{2k(k+2)}\right)/\left(\frac{a(k-a)(k+1)}{k}\right)=\frac{1}{k+1},

again a constant for any level
k
. Therefore, the desired conclusion holds.

∎

Remark 4.14
.

From the above proof, we see that, in fact, the coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\otimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}})

\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,0),\otimes_{i=1}^{n}L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,2a_{i}\Lambda))
, where
\Lambda
is the fundamental weight of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
Lie algebra. Similarly,
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\otimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{k,a_{i}})
is a rational multiple of the coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,0),\otimes_{i=1}^{n}L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{k}}(1,\Lambda_{i}))
, where
\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{k-1}
are the fundamental weights of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{k}
.

Remark 4.15
.

By
[
Fak12
, Proposition 4.7]

, the bundle

, the coinvariant divisors

]
.

Remark 4.16
.

The parafermion vertex operator algebras

offer the first known example of a vertex operator algebra whose fusion ring exhibits genuinely new behavior with respect to positivity. For instance, as shown in Example

, the subring generated by the subset

is not even
F
-positive, highlighting the subtle structure of the representation theory of Parafermion VOAs. The positivity of coinvariant divisors has been previously studied for the following other VOAs: for affine VOAs in

4.2.3
Characterization of non-trivial coinvariant divisors

With positivity of subrings
\mathcal{T}(k)
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
established, it is natural to ask for conditions for non-triviality of the associated coinvariant divisors. If such a coinvariant divisor is not trivial, then it is either ample or it is external in the nef cone of
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
. We provide the non-triviality condition and list a large class of such divisors that lie on the boundary of the nef cone. As part of this computation, we first provide an answer for
n=4
.

Corollary 4.19
.

The coinvariant divisor corresponding to modules
M^{2b_{1},b_{1}},\ldots,M^{2b_{4},b_{4}}
is non-trivial if and only if
b_{1}+\cdots+b_{4}>k
and in that case the degree is a multiple of the rank
\mu(M^{2b_{1},b_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{2b_{4},b_{4}})
:

d(M^{2b_{1},b_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{2b_{4},b_{4}})=\frac{1}{2(k+2)}\mu(M^{2b_{1},b_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{2b_{4},b_{4}})(-k+b_{1}+\cdots+b_{4}).

The coinvariant divisor corresponding to modules
M^{k,a},M^{k,b},M^{k,c},M^{k,d}
, with
1\leq a\leq b\leq c\leq d\leq k-1
, is non-trivial if and only if
a+b+c+d=2k
and in this case, the degree is

d(M^{k,a}\otimes M^{k,b}\otimes M^{k,c}\otimes M^{k,d})=\begin{cases}a/(k+1)&\text{ if }b+c\leq a+d\\
(k-d)/(k+1)&\text{ if }b+c>a+d.\end{cases}

Proof.

The degree formula is

\displaystyle d(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{4}M^{2b_{i},b_{i}})

\displaystyle=\mu(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{4}M^{2b_{i},b_{i}})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4}cw(M^{2b_{i},b_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{t=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}cw(M^{2t,t})\mu(M^{2b_{1},b_{1}}\otimes M^{2b_{2},b_{2}}\otimes M^{2t,t})\mu(M^{2b_{3},b_{1}}\otimes M^{2b_{4},b_{4}}\otimes M^{2t,t})-(2\leftrightarrow 3)-(2\leftrightarrow 4),

where

(2\leftrightarrow 3)
is the sum over
t
with position of
2
and
3
replaced and same for
(2\leftrightarrow 4)
. By proof of Theorem
3.8
,

d(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{4}M^{2b_{i},b_{i}})=\frac{1}{2(k+2)}d(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{4}L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,2b_{i}\Lambda)),

which gives the desired formula by the degree formula in
[
Fak12
, Proposition 4.2]
and
[
Ale
]
for
L_{\hat{\mathfrak{sl}}_{2}}(k,0)
-representations. For the second case, again the result follows from the theorem above and
[
Fak12
, Lemma 5.1]
.
∎

Proposition 4.20
.

The coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}})
corresponding to any
n
-many simple
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-modules
M^{2a_{i},a_{i}}
is non-trivial if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}>k
.

Proof.

The necessity condition follows from the proof of Theorem
3.8
and Lemma 4.1 in
[
Fak12
]
. We prove the other direction below. The case for
n=4
is true by the corollary above. Without loss of generality, assume that
1\leq a_{1}\leq\cdots\leq a_{n}\leq k/2
and assume that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}>k
. We break into two cases.

Case 1:
a_{1}+a_{2}\leq l/2
.
Assume that the statement holds for all
n<N
for some integer
N>4
. By factorization

\mathbb{D}|_{\overline{M}_{0,N-1}}=\sum_{t=a_{2}-a_{1}}^{a_{1}+a_{2}}c_{1}\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);\otimes_{p=3}^{n}m_{2a_{p},p}\otimes m_{2t,t}).

Note that for
t=a_{1}+a_{2}
, by assumption
c_{1}\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);\otimes_{p=3}^{n}m_{2a_{p},p}\otimes m_{2t,t})\neq 0
as
a_{3}+\cdots+a_{N}+t=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{N}>l
. This implies
\mathbb{D}|_{\overline{M}_{0,N-1}}\neq 0
, which in turn gives
\mathbb{D}\neq 0
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
.

Case 2:
a_{1}+a_{2}>l/2
.

Sub-case 2.1: (
n=5
)
We first prove that the statement holds for
n=5
by showing that the divisor
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}_{0,5}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);\otimes_{p=1}^{5}m_{2a_{p},p})
non-trivially intersects the F-curve
F_{\{1,2\},3,4,5}
given
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{5}>l
. We know that

\mathbb{D}|_{F_{\{1,2\},3,4,5}}=\sum_{t=a_{2}-a_{1}}^{l-a_{1}-a_{2}}c_{1}\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);m_{2t,t}\otimes\bigotimes_{p=3}^{5}m_{2a_{p},a_{p}}).

If
l-a_{1}-a_{2}<a_{2}-a_{1}
, then
a_{2}>l/2
, a contradiction. Therefore, the sum is not empty.
We now show that
\deg c_{1}\mathbb{V}_{0,5}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);\otimes_{p=3}^{5}m_{2a_{p},a_{p}}\otimes m_{2(l-a_{1}-a_{2}),l-a_{1}-a_{2})})\neq 0
. This is broken into two cases:

•

If
a_{1}+a_{2}\leq\frac{3}{4}l
, then
l-a_{1}-a_{2}\geq l/4
, Note that
a_{i}\geq\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}>l/4
for each
i\in\{3,4,5\}
, since
a_{i}\geq a_{i-1}
. Therefore,

a_{3}+a_{4}+a_{5}+(l-a_{1}-a_{2})>3l/4+l/4=l.

•

If
a_{1}+a_{2}>\frac{3}{4}l
, then

a_{3}+a_{4}+a_{5}+(l-a_{1}-a_{2})\geq a_{3}+a_{4}+a_{5}\geq 3\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}>9l/8>l.

where we have used the fact that
0\leq a_{2}-a_{1}\leq l-a_{1}-a_{2}
.

Sub-case 2.2: (
n>5
)

Having proven the result for
n=4
and
n=5
, assume that the statement holds for all
n<N
for some integer
N>5
. Since
a_{p}\geq\frac{a_{1}+a_{2}}{2}>l/4
for each
p\geq 3
, we have

a_{3}+\cdots+a_{N}+(l-a_{1}-a_{2})>\frac{4}{4}l+(l-a_{1}-a_{2})=2l-(a_{1}+a_{2})\geq 2l-l=l,

where we have used the fact that
a_{p}\leq l/2
for each
1\leq p\leq N
. Then,

\mathbb{D}|_{\overline{M}_{0,N-1}}=\mathbb{D}_{0,N-1}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},l);\otimes_{p=3}^{N}m_{2a_{p},a_{p}}\otimes m_{2(l-a_{1}-a_{2}),l-a_{1}-a_{2}})+\text{others}\neq 0.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.21
.

The coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{k,a_{i}})
corresponding to simple
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-modules
M^{k,a_{1}},\ldots,M^{k,a_{n}}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
is non-trivial if and only if there is a partition
I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}\cup I_{4}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
so that

\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{1}}a_{i}}+\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{2}}a_{i}}+\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{3}}a_{i}}+\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{4}}a_{i}}=2k,\text{ and }\min\{\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{p}}a_{i}}\mid 1\leq p\leq 4\}\neq 0,

where
\overline{b}
denotes the residue of
b
modulo
k
.

Proof.

To prove non-triviality, it suffices to find an
F
-curve
F_{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}}
such that the divisor does not contract. For such an
F
-curve, the intersection with the coinvariant divisor is given by

\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{k,a_{i}})|_{F_{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}}}=\sum_{t_{1},\ldots,t_{4}}\deg\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(\bigotimes_{p=1}^{4}M^{k,t_{p}})\prod_{p=1}^{4}\mu(\bigotimes_{i\in I_{p}}M^{k,a_{i}}\otimes M^{k,k-t_{p}}),

where the sum is taken over all tuples
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{4})\in([0,k]\cap\mathbb{Z})^{\oplus 4}

Now,
\mu(\bigotimes_{i\in I_{p}}M^{k,a_{i}}\otimes(M^{k,t_{p}})^{\prime}=1
if
\sum a_{i}+k-t_{p}=0\mod k
and zero otherwise. This implies only one summand remains, and we have

\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{k,a_{i}})|_{F_{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}}}=\deg\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(\bigotimes_{p=1}^{4}M^{k,\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{p}}a_{i}}}),

and the rest follows from Corollary
4.19
. In particular, the second condition is needed as otherwise, by propagation of vacua, the bundle
\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(\bigotimes_{p=1}^{4}M^{k,\overline{\sum_{i\in I_{p}}a_{i}}})
pulls back to a point, and hence its first Chern class is zero.
∎

4.2.4
Positive coinvariant divisors that contract an F-curve

The next two propositions provide a large class of coinvariant divisors with representations in the subrings
\mathcal{T}(k)
and
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
that are nef but not ample. These divisors lie on the boundary of the nef cone of
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
.

Proposition 4.22
.

The coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\otimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{k,a_{i}})
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}
contracts at least one
F
-curve if the minimum of the set
\{a_{i}+a_{j}+a_{k}\mid 1\leq i<j<k\leq n\}
is strictly less than
k+1
.

Proof.

For a tuple of pairwise-distinct integers
(x,y,z)
so that
a_{x}+a_{y}+a_{z}\leq k
, we have
\mathbb{D}\cdot F_{\{x\},\{y\},\{z\},[n]-\{x,y,z\}}=0
since
a_{x}+a_{y}+a_{z}+\overline{\sum_{i\in[n]-\{x,y,z\}}a_{i}}\leq k+(k-1)<2k
. The conclusion then follows from Proposition
4.21
.
∎

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
M^{2a_{i},a_{i}}
be any
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k)
-module for each integer
1\leq i\leq n
. If
\sum_{i\in T}a_{i}\leq(k+1)/2
for any subset
T\subset[n]
of size
2\leq|T|\leq n-2
, the divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}})
contracts at-least one
F
-curve.

Proof.

Intersection of
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}})
with an
F
-curve
F_{I_{1},I_{2},I_{3},I_{4}}
so that
I_{1}\cup I_{2}\cup I_{3}=T
is

\sum_{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{4})}d(\bigotimes_{p=1}^{4}M^{2t_{p},t_{p}})\prod_{p=1}^{4}\mu(\bigotimes_{i\in I_{p}}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}}\otimes M^{2t_{p},t_{p}}).

We now claim that
t_{1}+t_{2}+t_{3}\leq\sum_{i\in T}a_{i}
for all tuples
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{4})
with possibly nonzero summands.

Assuming the claim holds, for such a tuple
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{4})
, we have

t_{1}+\cdots+t_{4}\leq\sum_{i\in T}a_{i}+t_{4}\leq\frac{k+1}{2}+\frac{k}{2}<k+1,

where we have used the fact that
t_{4}\leq k/2
. But then, by Corollary
4.19
, we see that the degree contribution in such summands is zero. Therefore,
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}})
intersects all such
F
-curves trivially. It remains to prove the claim. Let
0\leq p\leq k/2
and
n
be any integers. Applying the fusion rule repeatedly, we get

\displaystyle\mu(\bigotimes_{i=1}^{n}M^{2a_{i},a_{i}}\otimes^{2p,p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{1}=|a_{n-1}-a_{n}|}^{a_{n-1}+a_{n}}\mu(\otimes_{i=1}^{n-2}m_{2a_{i},a_{i}}\otimes m_{2b_{1},b_{1}}\otimes m_{2p,p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{1}=|a_{n-1}-a_{n}|}^{a_{n-1}+a_{n}}\ \sum_{b_{2}=|a_{n-2}-b_{1}|}^{a_{n-2}+b_{1}}\ \cdots\sum_{b_{n-3}=|a_{3}-b_{n-4}|}^{a_{3}+b_{n-4}}\ \sum_{b_{n-2}=|a_{2}-b_{n-3}|}^{a_{2}+b_{n-3}}\ \mu(M^{2a_{1},a_{1}},M^{2b_{n-2},b_{n-2}},M^{2p,p}).

Note that fusion rule dictates
b_{1}\leq\min(a_{n-1}+a_{n},2k-a_{n-1}-a_{n})
. But since
a_{n-1}+a_{n}\leq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\leq k
, we see that
a_{n-1}+a_{n}\leq 2k-a_{n-1}-a_{n}
. Similarly, for
b_{2}
, we have
b_{2}\leq\min(a_{n-2}+b_{1},2k-a_{n-2}-b_{1})=a_{n-2}+b_{1}
since
a_{n-2}+b_{1}\leq a_{n-2}+a_{n-1}+a_{n}\leq k
. In particular, we see that
b_{n-2}\leq a_{2}+a_{3}+\ldots+a_{n}
. Therefore, again by fusion rules, it is necessary that
p\leq a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n}
for
\mu(a_{1},b_{n-2},p)
to be
1
.

∎

Proposition 4.25
.

Let

be any integer. Then the symmetric divisor

n

or

[

). So, it suffices to study intersection of the

with the symmetric divisor

and

\mathbb{D}_{a}\cdot F_{1,1,i}=\sum_{x,y=0}^{k/2}d_{a,a,x,y}r_{a^{i},x}r_{a^{n-2-i},y}

vanishes.
Let

.
By Lemma

F

i.

.

Let
n
be odd and
t

iv.

Let
n
and
t
both be odd. Then,
\eta=k-2a

. Then,

Lemma 4.26
.

Let
1\leq a\leq k/2
and
1\leq t\leq n-3
be any integers. Let
r_{a^{t},x}
and
r_{a^{n-2-t},y}
denote ranks of the bundles
\mathbb{V}_{0,t+1}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),(M^{2a,a})^{\otimes t}\otimes M^{2x,x})
and
\mathbb{V}_{0,n-1-t}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k),(M^{2a,a})^{\otimes(n-2-t)}\otimes M^{2y,y})
, respectively.

r_{a^{t},x}\neq 0\text{ if and only if }\begin{cases}\eta-at\leq x\leq at+2a-\eta&\text{ if }a\leq k/4,\\
\xi-t(\frac{k}{2}-a)\leq 2a-\xi+t(\frac{k}{2}-a)&\text{ if }a>k/4,\text{ and}\end{cases}

r_{a^{n-2-t},y}\neq 0\text{ if and only if }\begin{cases}\widetilde{\eta}-(n-2-t)a\leq y\leq a(n-2-t)+2a-\widetilde{\eta}&\text{ if }a\leq k/4\\
\widetilde{\xi}-(n-2-t)(\frac{k}{2}-a)\leq y\leq 2a\widetilde{\xi}+(n-2-t)(\frac{k}{2}-a)&\text{ if }a>k/4.\end{cases}

The integers
\eta
,
\widetilde{\eta}
,
\xi
and
\widetilde{\xi}
are defined to be

\eta=\begin{cases}k-2a&\text{ if $t$ is odd}\\
2a&\text{ if $t$ is even},\end{cases}\qquad\widetilde{\eta}=\begin{cases}k-2a&\text{ if $(n-2-t)$ is odd}\\
2a&\text{ if $(n-2-t)$ is even},\end{cases}

\xi=\begin{cases}k/2&\text{ if $t$ is odd}\\
2a&\text{ if $t$ is even},\end{cases}\qquad\widetilde{\xi}=\begin{cases}k/2&\text{ if $(n-2-t)$ is odd}\\
2a&\text{ if $(n-2-t)$ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
a\leq\frac{k}{4}
. By the non-triviality criterion of Rasmussen and Walton
[
RW02
,
Swi11
]
, we have

r_{a^{t},x}\neq 0\quad\text{if and only if}\quad at+x\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i\leq t+1\\
i+t\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2)\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{t-i}{2}k+2\left((i-1)a+\min(a,x)\right)\right).

The minimum is attained at the largest admissible value of
i
. When

, the inequality simplifies to
\eta-at\leq x
; when
x>a
, it becomes

. Hence, the conclusion follows.
The remaining cases can be treated using the same approach. We omit these calculations for brevity.
∎

Let

Lemma 4.28
.

Let
1\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{5}\leq k/2
be integers, and let
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{5}\}=\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\mid\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\}\sqcup\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}\mid\nu_{1}\leq\nu_{2}\leq\nu_{3}\}
be a partition. Then,

\mathbb{D}_{\lambda_{\bullet}}\cdot F_{\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\},\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}}\neq 0\Leftrightarrow\begin{cases}\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{5}=k+1&\text{ if }\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\leq k/2\\
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\leq 1&\text{ if }\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}>k/2.\end{cases}

Proof.

The intersection
\mathbb{D}_{\lambda_{\bullet}}\cdot F_{\{\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\},\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}}
is given by the sum
\sum_{x=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}d_{\nu_{\bullet},x}r_{\mu_{\bullet},x}
. Now,
d_{\nu_{\bullet},x}\neq 0
if and only if
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}+x\geq k+1
and
r_{\mu_{\bullet},x}\neq 0
if and only if
\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}\leq x\leq\min(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},k-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})
.

i.

Let
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\leq k/2
. Then,
\min(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},k-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
and the intersection is non-trivial if and only if
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}\leq k+1-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}
, that is, if and only if
\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{5}=k+1
.

ii.

Let
\mu_{2}+\mu_{2}>k/2
. Then,
\min(\mu_{1}+\mu_{2},k-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2})=k-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
and the intersection is non-trivial if and only if
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}\leq 1
.

∎

For this section, let

Section
4.3.1
introduces the rank formulas for the coinvariant bundles and the conformal weights of simple modules in
\mathcal{S}_{r}(k)
. We study the positivity of
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
in Section

4.3.1
Rank formula and conformal weights

Proposition 4.29
.

Given any tuple
a_{\bullet},b_{\bullet},c_{\bullet},d_{\bullet}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}^{\oplus r}
, we have

\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k);M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-c_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-d_{\bullet}})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }a_{\bullet}+b_{\bullet}+c_{\bullet}+d_{\bullet}=x_{\bullet}k\\
0&\text{ else,}\end{cases}

where
x_{\bullet}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{r})\in\{1,2,3\}^{\oplus r}
.

Proof.

The fusion rule for simple modules in
\mathcal{S}_{r}(k)
is given as follows:

M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\boxtimes M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}=M^{0,0-\overline{a_{\bullet}+b_{\bullet}}},

where
\overline{a_{\bullet}}:=(a_{1}\mod k,\ldots,a_{r}\mod k)
. Therefore, the dual of
M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}
is
M^{0,0-(k-a_{\bullet})}
and the rank is

\sum_{t_{\bullet}}\mu(M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-t_{\bullet}})\mu(M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-(k-t_{\bullet})}).

For the sum to be nonzero, we need
t_{i}=a_{i}+b_{i}\mod k
and
k-t_{i}=c_{i}+d_{i}\mod k
, that is,
a_{i}+b_{i}+c_{i}+d_{i}=0\mod k
for each
1\leq i\leq r
. Given
a_{i}+b_{i}+c_{i}+d_{i}=0\mod k
for each
i
, there is a unique
t_{\bullet}
for which the corresponding summand is nonzero. Hence, the rank is either zero or one.
∎

To understand the positivity of the subrings
\mathcal{S}_{r}
, we first need to understand the conformal weights.

Proposition 4.30
.

Given
a_{\bullet}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}^{\oplus r}
, conformal weight of
M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}
is

a_{s}-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{2}_{i}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right).

with
s:=\min\{1\leq i\leq r\mid a_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq r}a_{j}\}
.

Proof.

Since
M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\cong M^{k\Lambda_{s},k\Lambda_{s}-a_{\bullet}}
with the integer
s
defined as above, it follows from Proposition
4.30
.
∎

Remark 4.31
.

The conformal weight is
Sym{(n-1)}
-invariant fixing
s
. Moreover, if there are two indices
s
and
s^{\prime}
so that
a_{s}=a_{s^{\prime}}
, then

a_{s}-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{2}_{i}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right)=a_{s^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{2}_{i}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right).

Moreover, given any tuple
a_{\bullet}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
, denoting
\sigma\in S_{r}
so that
b_{i}:=a_{\sigma(j)}
with
b_{1}\leq\cdots\leq b_{r}
, we have

\text{ conformal weight of }M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}=\text{ conformal weight of }M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}},

as we do not record the index of
a_{\bullet}
which achieves the maximum
\max_{1\leq i\leq r}a_{i}
but only the maximum itself for
a_{s}
and the rest of the formula is
Sym(r)
-invariant.

For any
a_{\bullet},b_{\bullet},c_{\bullet},d_{\bullet}\in\{0,1,\ldots,k-1\}^{\oplus r}
, we can assume each of the tuple are a non-decreasing order of integers. Moreover, we may assume that
a_{1}\geq b_{1}\geq c_{1}\geq d_{1}
while calculating degree of
c_{1}\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k);M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-c_{\bullet}}\otimes M^{0,0-d_{\bullet}})
because of the obvious symmetry of tensor products.

Finally, if
a_{1}=0
for the tuple
a_{\bullet}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
, then the conformal weight of the
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r+1},k)
-module
M^{0,0-a_{\bullet}}

(\max_{1\leq t\leq r}a_{t})-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}a^{2}_{i}-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right)=(\max_{1\leq t\leq r}a_{t})-\frac{1}{k}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{r}a^{2}_{i}-\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq r}a_{i}a_{j}\right)

is equal to the conformal weight of the
K(\mathfrak{sl}_{r},k)
-module
M^{0,0-b_{\bullet}}
with
b_{\bullet}=(a_{2},a_{3},\ldots,a_{r})
.

Corollary 4.32
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
be any integers. The simple module in
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{sim}(k)
with maximal conformal weight is
M^{0,0-(\lfloor 2k/3\rfloor,\lfloor k/3\rfloor)}
and for
\mathcal{S}_{r}^{sim}(k)
with
r\geq 3
is
M^{0,0-(k-1,\ldots,k-1)}
. The conformal weight of simple modules in
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
(resp.,
\mathcal{S}_{r}(k)
for all
r\geq 3
) is less than or equal to
k/3
(resp.,
\frac{1}{k}(k-1)(k+(k-1)r(r-2))
) and all non-trivial simple modules have conformal weight greater than or equal to
(k-1)/k

Proof.

This result follows from the proposition above by doing a simple analysis. In particular, the nonzero minimum is achieved at
(\delta_{1j},\delta_{2,j},\ldots,\delta_{rj})
for any fixed

. Similarly, for
r=2
, the maximum is achieved at
(2k/3,k/3)
and for all

(k-1,\ldots,k-1)
.
∎

Proposition 4.33

\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)

Proof.

We wish to prove that the divisor
\mathbb{D}

Lemma 4.34
.

Let
k\geq 1
be any integer.
Let
M^{0,0-(y_{1},y_{2})}
, for

Proof.

Consider simple modules

,
M^{0,0-(b_{1},b_{2})}
,

and

of the

4.29
, the degree of the associated coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}
on
\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,4}
is given by

\displaystyle\deg\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle=c^{0,0-(a_{1},a_{2})}+c^{0,0-(b_{1},b_{2})}+c^{0,0-(c_{1},c_{2})}+c^{0,0-(d_{1},d_{2})}

\displaystyle\qquad-c^{0,0-(\overline{a_{1}+b_{1}},\overline{a_{2}+b_{2}})}-c^{0,0-(\overline{a_{1}+c_{1}},\overline{a_{2}+c_{2}})}-c^{0,0-(\overline{a_{1}+d_{1}},\overline{a_{2}+d_{2}})},

(4)

where

Explicitly, we show that the degree is strictly positive if and only if one of the following two conditions holds:
(1)
\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}=k
and
\gamma_{2}+\delta_{2}=k
for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\{a,b,c,d\}
; or
(2)
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
for all
y\in\{a,b,c,d\}
, with
\operatorname{sign}(a_{1}-a_{2})=\operatorname{sign}(y_{1}-y_{2})
for exactly one
y\in\{b,c,d\}
, and
\operatorname{sign}(a_{1}-a_{2})=-\operatorname{sign}(z_{1}-z_{2})
for all
z\in\{b,c,d\}\setminus\{y\}
. In all other cases, the degree is zero. We define
x_{i}:=a_{i}+b_{i}+c_{i}+d_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

(
Case
1)

\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}=k
and
\gamma_{2}+\delta_{2}=k
for some
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\in\{a,b,c,d\}
: If
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\}\neq\{a,b,c,d\}
, then we get the trivial divisor. To see why, assume that
d\notin\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta\}
and then
M^{0,0-(d_{1},d_{2})}=M^{0,0-(0,0)}
. So, it is safe to assume that
a_{1}+b_{1}=k=c_{2}+d_{2}
with all four integers non-negative. Then the degree is:

\displaystyle\deg\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle=a_{1}-\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{k}+b_{1}-\frac{b_{1}^{2}}{k}+c_{2}-\frac{c_{2}^{2}}{k}+d_{2}-\frac{d_{2}^{2}}{k}-c^{0,0-(0,0)}-c^{0,0-(a_{1},c_{2})}-c^{0,0-(a_{1},d_{2})}

\displaystyle=k+a_{1}-\max(a_{1},c_{2})-\max(a_{1},d_{2})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}b_{1}&\text{ if }c_{2},d_{2}\leq a_{1}\\
a_{1}&\text{ if }c_{2},d_{2}\geq a_{1}\\
\min(c_{2},d_{2})&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

(
Case
2)

Assume that
y_{i}+z_{i}<k
for all off-diagonal terms
(y,z)\in\{a,b,c,d\}^{\times 2}
and all
i=1,2
: The degree is:

\displaystyle\deg\mathbb{D}

\displaystyle=(a_{1}+a_{2})+\max(a_{1},a_{2})+\max(b_{1},b_{2})+\max(c_{1},c_{2})+\max(d_{1},d_{2})

\displaystyle-\max(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})-\max(a_{1}+c_{1},a_{2}+c_{2})-\max(a_{1}+d_{1},a_{2}+d_{2}).

We work on sub-cases:

•

(
y_{1}=y_{2}=y
for all
y\in\{a,b,c,d\}
) The degree is
2a+k-3a-(k-a)=0
.

•

(
y_{1}=y_{2}
for three elements in
\{a,b,c,d\}
) Since
x_{1}=x_{2}
, this case reduces to the previous one.

•

(
y_{1}=y_{2}
for any two elements in
\{a,b,c,d\}
) By symmetry, we may assume that
c_{1}=c_{2}=c
,
d_{1}=d_{2}=d
,
a_{1}>a_{2}
and
b_{1}<b_{2}
. Then, the degree is
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{1}+b_{2}+c+d-\max(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})-a_{1}-c-a_{1}-d=a_{2}+b_{2}-\max(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2})=(k-c-d)-\max(k-c-d,k-c-d)=0.

•

(
y_{1}=y_{2}
for exactly one element in
\{a,b,c,d\}
) Assume that
d_{1}=d_{2}=d
. We have cases:

–

a_{1}>a_{2},b_{1}>b_{2},c_{1}<c_{2}
: The degree is
a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{1}+b_{1}+c_{2}+d-(a_{1}+b_{1})-\max(a_{1}+c_{1},a_{2}+c_{2})-(a_{1}+d)=a_{2}+c_{2}-\max(a_{1}+c_{1},a_{2}+c_{2})
. Note that
c_{2}-c_{1}=(a_{1}-a_{2})+(b_{1}+b_{2})>(a_{1}-a_{2})
and therefore,
\max(a_{1}+c_{1},a_{2}+c_{2})=a_{2}+c_{2}
and the degree is zero.

–

Any other case reduces to the case above by symmetry. For example, the case
a_{1}>a_{2},c_{1}>c_{2},b_{1}<b_{2}
reduces to the previous case since any coinvariant divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(V,M^{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes M^{4})
is invariant under the action of
Sym(4)
on the indices of modules
M^{i}
.

–

The reverse case of
a_{1}<a_{2},b_{1}<b_{2},c_{1}>c_{2}
reduces to the first case since
c^{0-(a_{1},a_{2})}=c^{0-(a_{2},a_{1})}
and the fusion rule remains the same if we switch the tuples for all four modules in consideration.

•

(
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
for all
y
in
\{a,b,c,d\}
) We again break into cases:

–

(
a_{1}>a_{2},b_{1}>b_{2},c_{1}<c_{2},d_{1}<d_{2}
):
The degree is

\deg\mathbb{D}=\begin{cases}b_{1}-b_{2}&\text{ if }a_{1}+c_{1}\geq a_{2}+c_{2},a_{1}+d_{1}\geq a_{2}+d_{2}\\
a_{1}-a_{2}&\text{ if }a_{1}+c_{1}\leq a_{2}+c_{2},a_{1}+d_{1}\leq a_{2}+d_{2}\\
c_{2}-c_{1}&\text{ if }a_{1}+c_{1}\geq a_{2}+c_{2},a_{1}+d_{1}\leq a_{2}+d_{2}\\
d_{2}-d_{1}&\text{ if }a_{1}+c_{1}\leq a_{2}+c_{2},a_{1}+d_{1}\geq a_{2}+d_{2}.\end{cases}

–

(
a_{1}>a_{2},b_{1}>b_{2},c_{1}>c_{2},d_{2}<d_{2}
):
The degree is
a_{2}+d_{2}-\max(a_{1}+d_{1},a_{2}+d_{2})
.
Since
d_{2}-d_{1}=(a_{1}-a_{2})+(b_{1}-b_{2})+(c_{1}-c_{2})<a_{1}-a_{2}
, the degree is zero.

–

Symmetries available to us reduce all cases to the one above. For example, the case
(a_{1}>a_{2},b_{1}<b_{2},c_{1}<c_{2},d_{1}<d_{2})
reduces to the first case above by first switching
(y_{1},y_{2})
to
(y_{2},y_{1})
for all
y\in\{a,b,c,d\}
and then applying
(1,4)\in Sym(4)
to the divisor.

∎

Theorem 4.36
.

The subring
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
is positive for any positive integer
k\leq 5
.

Proof.

If
k=1
, then the only module we need to consider is
M^{0,0}
, and the divisor is trivial as it trivially intersects all F-curves.
For any integer
k\geq 2
, consider
n
many modules
M^{0,0-(a_{i},b_{i})}
, for integers
1\leq i\leq n
, with
0\leq a_{i},b_{i}\leq k-1
so that
\sum_{i}a_{i}=\sum_{i}b_{i}=0\mod k
. By Theorem
2.9
, we can assume that none of the
n
modules are trivial.

Then,
\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k);\otimes M^{0,0-(a_{i},b_{i})})
can written as

\mathbb{D}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c^{0,0-(a_{j},b_{j})}\psi_{j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subset[n]\\
2\leq|I|\leq n/2\end{subarray}}b_{0,I}\delta_{0,I},\text{ where}

\displaystyle b_{0,I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{W\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)}c^{W}\ \text{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,|I|+1}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k);\otimes_{i\in I}M^{0,0-(a_{i},b_{i})}\otimes W)\ \text{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,|I^{c}|+1}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k);\otimes_{i\notin I}M^{0,0-(a_{i},b_{i})}\otimes W^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq f_{\max}\text{rank}\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k);\otimes_{i\in[n]}M^{0,0-(a_{i},b_{i})})=f_{\max}.

Note that

\sum_{j=1}^{n}c^{0,0-(a_{j},b_{j})}\psi_{j}\geq f_{\min}\psi.

Since
K:=K_{\overline{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}}=\psi-2\Delta
, for any
c\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f_{\min}}(\mathbb{D}-cK)

\displaystyle\geq(1-\frac{c}{f_{\min}})\psi+\frac{2c-f_{\max}}{f_{\min}}\Delta.

In order to conclude that
\mathbb{D}
is nef from Theorem
3.3
, it remains to find some
c
so that
\frac{f_{\max}}{2}\leq c\leq f_{\min}
. From Proposition
4.30
, we know that
\frac{k-1}{k}=f_{\min}\leq c^{0,-(a_{i},b_{i})}\leq f_{\max}\leq\frac{k}{3}
. Therefore, we need
\frac{k^{2}}{3(k-1)}\leq 2
, which is true for
k\leq 4
. For
k=5
, an explicit calculation yields
f_{\max}=8/5
and
f_{\min}=4/5
, that is,
f_{\max}/f_{\min}=2
.
∎

Remark 4.37
.

In Proposition

, we show that

intersects

-curves

,

. Given similar methods should work for

, one may ask why are divisors

nef as indicated by the Theorem above. A simple calculation shows that for any integers

Remark 4.38
.

As seen above, the combinatorial complexity owing to conformal weights limits our understanding of positivity of the subring
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
for general
k
. However, there is a subring
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{2}(k)
of
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
that is propositional to
\mathcal{S}_{1}(k)
and therefore, all coinvariant divisors with representations in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{2}(k)
are again nef by Theorems
3.8
and
4.13
. The subring
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{2}(k)
is defined as

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{2}(k):=\langle M^{0,0-(a,a)}\mid 0\leq a\leq k-1\rangle,

and the isomorphism of rings

gives proportionality since
cw(M^{0,0-(a,a)})=a-\frac{a^{2}}{k}=cw(M^{k,a})
. One checks that
M^{0,0-a}\cong M^{k,a}
. However, the generalized map
f:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{r}(k)\to\mathcal{S}_{r-1}(k):M^{0,0-(a,\ldots,a)}\mapsto M^{0,0-(a,\ldots,a)}
is an injection but does not satisfy proportionality, since

cw(M^{0,0-(a,\ldots,a)})-cw(f(M^{0,0-(a,\ldots,a)}))=a+\frac{a^{2}r}{2k}(r-3)-a+\frac{a^{2}(r-1)}{2k}(r-4)=\frac{2a^{2}}{k}(r-2).

4.3.3
Symmetric coinvariant divisors associated to
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Parafermions

Given that the subrings
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
are positive for integers
1\leq k\leq 5
, it is natural to ask if the coinvariant divisors they define are ample. It is difficult to study such notions for an arbitrary coinvariant divisor owing to complexity of the conformal weights. In this subsection, we answer this question of all symmetric coinvariant divisors
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},k),M^{\otimes n})
on
\widetilde{M}_{0,n}=\overline{M}_{0,n}/Sym_{n}
, where
M
is any simple module in
\mathcal{S}_{2}(k)
. The general approach is to intersect these divisors with
F
-curves
F_{1,1,i}
for all integers
1\leq i\leq g
, with
n=2g+2
or
n=2g+3
, as they form a basis of
N_{1}(\widetilde{M}_{0,n})
.

Proposition 4.41
.

The symmetric divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M^{\otimes n})
for a simple module
M\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(2)
is trivial if and only if
M
is the trivial module or
n
is odd. Otherwise, then the intersection of the divisor with
F
-curves
F_{1,1,i}
is

\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M^{\otimes n})\cdot F_{1,1,i}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $i$ is odd}\\
2&\text{ if $i$ is even}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The subring
\mathcal{S}_{2}(2)
is generated by simple modules
M_{0}:=M^{0,0-(0,0)},M_{1}:=M^{0,0-(1,0)},M_{2}:=M^{0,0-(0,1)}
,
M_{3}:=M^{0,0-(1,1)}
, where the conformal weight of
M_{0}
is zero and the other three have conformal weight equal to
1/2
. Also, all four simple modules are self dual. Note that the divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(M_{0}^{\otimes n})
is trivial, for any
n\geq 1
. Let
M_{a}
be any of the other three simple modules. The rank of the bundle
\mathbb{V}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M_{a}^{\otimes n})
is given by

\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes n})=\sum_{M_{b}}\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes(n-2)}\otimes M_{b})\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M_{b})=\mu(M^{\otimes(n-2)})=\mu(M^{\otimes(n\mod 2)})=\begin{cases}1&\text{$n$ is even}\\
0&\text{$n$ is odd}.\end{cases}.

Let
n=2g+2
be an even integer and let
1\leq i\leq g
be written as
i=2t+\epsilon
, where
t\geq 0
and
\epsilon\in\{0,1\}
are integers.

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M_{a}^{\otimes n})\cdot F_{1,1,2t+\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y}d(M_{a}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M_{x}\otimes M_{y})\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes 2t+\epsilon}\otimes M_{x})\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes(2g-\epsilon)}\otimes M_{y})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y}d(M_{a}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M_{x}\otimes M_{y})\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes M_{x})\mu(M_{a}^{\otimes\epsilon}\otimes M_{y}).

If
\epsilon=0
, then
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M_{a}^{\otimes n})\cdot F_{1,1,2t+\epsilon}=d(M_{a}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M_{0}^{\otimes 2})=d(M_{a}^{\otimes 2})=0.
If
\epsilon=1
, then
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},2),M_{a}^{\otimes n})\cdot F_{1,1,2t+\epsilon}=d(M_{a}^{\otimes 4})=4(1/2)-3(0)=2.

∎

.

\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},3),M^{\otimes n})

Proposition 4.43
.

For a simple module
M\in\mathcal{S}_{2}(4)

\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},4),M^{\otimes n})

Proposition 4.44
.

A symmetric coinvariant divisor

\mathbb{D}\cdot F_{1,1,i}=d({M^{(a_{1},b_{1})}}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M^{(\overline{\epsilon a_{1}},\overline{\epsilon b_{1}})}\otimes M^{(\overline{(\delta-\epsilon-2)a_{1}},\overline{(\delta-\epsilon-2)b_{1}})}),

,

\mathbb{D}\cdot F_{1,1,5t+\epsilon}=d({M^{(a_{1},b_{1})}}^{\otimes 2}\otimes M^{(\overline{\epsilon a_{1}},\overline{\epsilon b_{1}})}\otimes M^{(\overline{(-\epsilon-2)a_{1}},\overline{(-\epsilon-2)b_{1}})})
is zero if

, we need some information about the conformal weight of the modules: The set of simple modules admit a partition

\mathcal{S}_{2}(5)^{sim}=c^{0}\sqcup c^{4/5}\sqcup c^{6/5}\sqcup c^{7/5}\sqcup c^{8/5},

an analysis of the fusion rules among these modules give the following data:

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},5),M^{\otimes 5m})\cdot F_{1,1,5t+1}=\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},5),M^{\otimes 5m})\cdot F_{1,1,5t+2}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $M\in c^{0}\sqcup c^{4/5}$}\\
1&\text{ if $M\in c^{7/5}$}\\
2&\text{ if $M\in c^{6/5}\sqcup c^{8/5}$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},5),M^{\otimes 5m})\cdot F_{1,1,5t+3}=0\quad\text{for all $M\in\mathcal{S}_{3}(5)^{sim}$, and }

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{0,n}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},5),M^{\otimes 5m})\cdot F_{1,1,5t+4}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if $M\in c^{0}$}\\
2&\text{ if $M\in c^{4/5}$}\\
4&\text{ if $M\in c^{6/5}\sqcup c^{7/5}$}\\
5&\text{ if $M\in c^{8/5}$}.\end{cases}

This completes the proof.
∎

Notation 4.47

Proof of Proposition
4.46
.

One direction is a corollary to Theorems
4.13
and
4.36

\mathbb{D}
associated to representations in
\mathcal{S}_{r}(k)^{sim}
that intersects an

-curve strictly negatively.
We only need to consider symmetric divisors
\mathbb{D}_{0,n}^{r,a}
on
\widetilde{\mathrm{M}}_{0,n}

k
does not divide

\mathbb{D}^{r,a}_{0,km}\cdot F_{1,1,kt+\epsilon}=d(a,a,\overline{\epsilon a},\overline{(-2-\epsilon)a})=2cw(a)+cw(\overline{\epsilon a})+cw(\overline{(-2-\epsilon)a})-cw(\overline{2a})-2cw(\overline{(\epsilon+1)a}).

If

\displaystyle=2cw(1)+cw(\epsilon)+cw(k-2-\epsilon)-cw(2)-2cw(\epsilon+1)

Proposition 4.48

\mathbb{D}\cdot F_{1,1,i}=\begin{cases}(q-1)(q-2)+2&\text{ if $i$ is odd},\\

All simple modules in

are self-dual. The conformal weight of

∎

Proof of Proposition

In this subsection, we prove Proposition
4.12
. We restate the proposition below for the reader’s convenience.

Proposition 4.51
.

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
i\in\{a,b,c,d\}
so that
0\leq i^{\prime}<i\leq k
for each
i
and
a\leq b\leq c\leq d
. Then, the degree
d
of the divisor
\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2},k);M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{b,b^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime})
is given in terms of its rank
\mu
and the sum of the conformal weights
c_{\Sigma}:=\sum_{i}cw(M^{i,i^{\prime}})
as follows:

-d+\mu c_{\Sigma}=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\frac{b+c+d-a}{2}\neq b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}-a^{\prime}\mod k,\\
\Lambda&\text{ else,}\end{cases}

where

\Lambda=\sum_{i\in\{b,c,d\}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{i}\leq t\leq M_{i}\\
(a+i)\in 2\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}cw\left(M^{t,\ \overline{a^{\prime}-i^{\prime}+\frac{t-a+i}{2}}}\right)\right)

and

M_{i}=\min(a+i,\ b+c+d-i,\ 2k-a-i,\ 2k-b-c-d+i),

m_{i}=\max(|i-a|,\ |\alpha-\beta|),

with
\{\alpha,\beta\}=\{b,c,d\}\setminus\{i\}
.

Lemma 4.52
.

Consider the three modules
M^{c,c^{\prime}},M^{d,d^{\prime}},m_{t,t^{\prime}}
for integers
0\leq a<a^{\prime}\leq k
, where
a\in\{c,d,t\}
. Then,

\text{rank }\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2};k);M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})=\text{rank }\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl};k);(M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}}).

Proof.

Note that
(M^{a,a^{\prime}})^{\prime}\cong m_{a,a-a^{\prime}}
for any
a\in\{c,d,t\}
and therefore

\text{rank }\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2};k);(M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})=1

if and only if
|d-c|\leq t\leq\min(c+d,2k-c-d)
and
t=c+d\mod 2
with

t-t^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(2c-2c^{\prime}+2d-2d^{\prime}-c-d+t)\mod k.

The last condition reduces to
t^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(2c^{\prime}+2d^{\prime}-c-d+t)\mod k.
These conditions are necessary and sufficient for

\text{rank }\mathbb{V}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2};k);M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}}\otimes(M_{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})=1.

Since the rank of these bundles is either zero or one, the result follows.
∎

Degree of the bundle

\mathbb{D}:=\mathbb{D}_{0,4}(K(\mathfrak{sl}_{2};k);M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{b,b^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime})

is given by

\displaystyle d+\mu c_{\Sigma}=\sum_{t}cw(M^{t,t^{\prime}})\mu(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{b,b^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})\mu((M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}})+(b\leftrightarrow c)+(b\leftrightarrow d),

where
\mu
and
d
are rank and degree, respectively, of the line bundle
\mathbb{D}
and
c_{\Sigma}
is sum of the conformal weights of the modules
M^{h,h^{\prime}}
where
h
runs over the set
\{a,b,c,d\}
. Here, we are using the fact that conformal weight is invariant under taking the contragradient dual. Finally,
(b\leftrightarrow c)
is the same as the sum over
t
with positions of
b
and
c
switched, and similarly for
(b\leftrightarrow d)
. By the Lemma above, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{t}c(M^{t,t^{\prime}})\mu(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{b,b^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})\mu((M^{c,c^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{d,d^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes M^{t,t^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{t}c(M^{t,t^{\prime}})\mu(M^{a,a^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{b,b^{\prime}})^{\prime}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime})\mu(M^{c,c^{\prime}}\otimes M^{d,d^{\prime}}\otimes(M^{t,t^{\prime}})^{\prime}).

For a summand corresponding to
t
to be nonzero, the first rank element requires that
t
satisfy the following

|b-a|\leq t\leq\min(a+b,2k-a-b),t=a+b\mod 2,t^{\prime}=a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}+\frac{t-a+b}{2}\mod k

and the second rank element requires that

|d-c|\leq t\leq\min(c+d,2k-c-d),t=c+d\mod 2,t^{\prime}=c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}+\frac{t-c+d}{2}\mod k.

Combining these conditions, a nonzero contribution from a summand occurs only when
t
satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\max(|b-a|,|d-c|)\leq

\displaystyle t\leq\min(a+b,c+d,2k-a-b,2k-c-d)

\displaystyle t=a+b=c+d\mod 2

along with

t^{\prime}=a^{\prime}-b^{\prime}+\frac{t-a+b}{2}\mod k=c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}+\frac{t-c+d}{2}\mod k

and finally, we need the modules in consideration to satisfy the relation

\frac{b+c+d-a}{2}=b^{\prime}+c^{\prime}+d^{\prime}-a^{\prime}\mod k.

This completes the proof of the proposition.

Example 4.54

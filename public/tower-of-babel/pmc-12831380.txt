Interim efficacy and safety analysis of the regimen containing delamanid in the treatment of multidrug resistant/rifampicin-resistant/extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis: a single-arm, prospective, monocentric, observational study
Authors: Yao Qin, Yuanyuan Chen, Qingshan Cai
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMultidrug-resistant, rifampicin-resistant, and extensively drug-resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB) present significant treatment challenges, requiring effective and safe therapeutic regimens. This study aims to assess the efficacy and safety of a 24-week regimen that includes Delamanid (Dlm) for patients with these resistant forms of tuberculosis.MethodsThis was a single-arm, prospective, monocentric, observational trial. From June 2020 to January 2023, 29 patients meeting the inclusion criteria were selected from Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital. These patients received a treatment regimen that included Dlm. Data were collected on patient demographics, sputum culture conversion rates, and pulmonary lesion changes after 24 weeks. Additionally, adverse events (AEs) that occurred during treatment were recorded and analyzed.ResultsOut of the 29 patients, 26 completed the initial 24-week intensive phase treatment, with 2 patients withdrawing due to adverse reactions and 1 patient being lost to follow-up. The cohort included 11 MDR-TB, 3 RR-TB, and 12 XDR-TB patients. All 26 patients who were sputum culture-positive at baseline achieved culture conversion within the 24-week treatment period, resulting in a 100% conversion rate. Conversion was confirmed in all patients tested on solid media (18/18) and liquid media (8/8). The median time to sputum culture conversion was 34 days for solid and 70 days for liquid media. Imaging showed significant pulmonary lesion absorption in 21 cases and partial absorption in 3 cases. Improvements in pulmonary cavities were noted in 15 patients. A total of 108 AEs were reported, with the most common being liver function abnormalities (61.5%), anemia (50%), and leukopenia (42.3%). No serious AEs or deaths occurred, and 24 patients (92.3%) had favorable outcomes.ConclusionsThe regimen containing Delamanid during the initial 24 weeks intensive phase of treatment demonstrated high efficacy in achieving sputum culture conversion and showed an acceptable safety profile in MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB patients, offering a valuable treatment option for this difficult-to-treat population.Trial registrationIt registered in ICH GCP-US Clinical Trials Registry and the registration number is NCT04421495 and date of registration was June 9, 2020.Keywords: Delamanid, Drug-resistant tuberculosis, Efficacy, Safety, Resistance

Background

Globally, drug-resistant tuberculosis remains a public health crisis. In 2023, the WHO estimated approximately 400,000 cases of multidrug or rifampicin-resistant TB (MDR/RR-TB). China bears a significant proportion of this burden, with about 29,000 cases, accounting for 7.3% of the global total and ranking fourth highest in the world. Furthermore, the treatment success rate for DR-TB in China is only 51%, substantially lower than the global average of 68% [1, 2]. The treatment of drug-resistant tuberculosis is lengthy, involves complex regimens, multiple medications, poor clinical efficacy, significant adverse reactions, high treatment costs, limited drug options, and low cure rates. Therefore, there is an urgent need for effective and safe drugs and new treatment regimens to control drug-resistant tuberculosis. Delamanid, an anti-tuberculosis drug introduced after bedaquiline, was approved for marketing in China in 2018. Its safety and efficacy have been proven in many countries [3–6]. Differences in pharmacokinetics, pharmacogenetics, and ethnic susceptibility among different patient populations may significantly influence the risk and severity of specific adverse drug reactions, including QT interval prolongation [7–10]. Currently, there remains relatively little research data on delamanid in China, and evidence regarding its efficacy and safety in the Chinese population is still limited.Therefore, it is clinically necessary to gather specific data on the safety and efficacy of Delamanid-containing regimens in Chinese patients, it is clinically necessary to understand the safety and efficacy of Delamanid-containing anti-tuberculosis regimens in Chinese patients with drug-resistant tuberculosis. Chinese patients with drug-resistant tuberculosis began using Delamanid in 2020 through the Anti-Tuberculosis New Drug Introduction and Protection Project, and domestic experience with this drug is limited. This study reports on the interim efficacy and safety results of 29 MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB patients treated with a Delamanid-containing regimen for an initial 24-week period. The aim is to provide early clinical experience for the use of this drug in China, with the total MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB treatment continuing for up to 24 months.

Methods

Trial design

This study was designed as a prospective, single-arm observational study. A single-arm design was chosen due to the ethical considerations of assigning patients with limited treatment options for MDR/XDR-TB to a control group without access to new, potentially life-saving drugs like Delamanid. This design allows for the evaluation of the drug’s efficacy and safety in a real-world clinical setting in China.

Sample size and recruitment

From June 2020 to December 2022, 29 patients at Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital who met the inclusion criteria were treated with an individualized anti-tuberculosis regimen containing Delamanid (Dlm). Two patients withdrew due to severe adverse reactions, and one was lost to follow-up, leaving 26 patients who completed the 24-week treatment regimen.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion Criteria: (1) Patients diagnosed with MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB through laboratory drug susceptibility testing; (2) Age between 18 and 65 years; (3) Following the principles of the WHO “Consolidated Guidelines on Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis Treatment” and the “Chinese Expert Consensus on the Treatment of Multidrug-Resistant and Rifampicin-Resistant Tuberculosis (2019 Edition)”, Dlm was included in the regimen based on drug susceptibility results and previous treatment history to form an effective treatment plan; (4) No respiratory failure, no history of cardiac insufficiency or clinically significant arrhythmias, and a QT interval (QTc) < 450 ms on ECG; (5) Ability to comply with medication, complete treatment monitoring, and promptly report adverse reactions to the responsible doctor during treatment and follow-up; (6) All patients signed an informed consent form.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) History of allergy to Dlm, metronidazole, tinidazole, other nitroimidazoles, pyrazoles, or any excipients; (2) Serum transaminase levels ≥ 3 times the upper limit of normal or total bilirubin ≥ 2.5 times the upper limit of normal, serum albumin < 2.8 g/dL, severe renal impairment; (3) Concurrent use of strong CYP3A4 inducers (e.g., carbamazepine); (4) Pregnant women or women who might be pregnant; (5) Participation in other clinical trials of unapproved new drugs within the last three months; (6) Known congenital QTc prolongation or any condition that prolongs QTc, or QTc > 500 ms; (7) Any heart disease that could induce arrhythmia; (8) Electrolyte disturbances, especially hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, or hypomagnesemia; (9) Concurrent use of medications known to prolong QTc or other drugs with cardiac risk factors.

Withdrawal Criteria: (1) Discovery of exclusion criteria after inclusion; (2) Severe adverse reactions caused by Delamanid, such as allergic reactions, psychiatric abnormalities, or liver dysfunction (transaminase levels more than three times the upper limit of normal or total bilirubin more than 2.5 times the upper limit of normal); (3) Determination by the expert panel that continued use of Delamanid is inappropriate; (4) Inability to tolerate the background treatment regimen, or inability to meet the requirement of three confirmed sensitive drugs or four possible sensitive drugs after regimen adjustment; (5) Patient requests withdrawal.

Relevant definitions

Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) strain infecting the patient is confirmed by in vitro drug susceptibility testing to be resistant to at least both isoniazid and rifampicin.

Multidrug-Resistant Tuberculosis (MDR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the Mycobacterium tuberculosis (MTB) strain infecting the patient is confirmed by in vitro drug susceptibility testing to be resistant to at least both isoniazid and rifampicin.

Extensively Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis (XDR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the MTB strain infecting the patient is confirmed by in vitro drug susceptibility testing to be resistant to at least one fluoroquinolone and at least one second-line injectable drug (amikacin, capreomycin, or kanamycin) in addition to being multidrug-resistant.

Extensively Drug-Resistant Tuberculosis (XDR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the MTB strain infecting the patient is confirmed by in vitro drug susceptibility testing to be resistant to at least one fluoroquinolone and at least one second-line injectable drug (amikacin, capreomycin, or kanamycin) in addition to being multidrug-resistant.

Rifampicin-Resistant Tuberculosis (RR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the MTB strain is resistant to rifampicin, regardless of resistance to other anti-tuberculosis drugs.

Rifampicin-Resistant Tuberculosis (RR-TB): Tuberculosis in which the MTB strain is resistant to rifampicin, regardless of resistance to other anti-tuberculosis drugs.

Sputum Culture Conversion: In patients with a positive baseline specimen, sputum culture conversion is defined as two consecutive negative cultures for MTB, collected at least 30 days apart, after anti-tuberculosis treatment. The conversion time is the date of the first negative sputum sample in the series of two consecutive negative results.

Sputum Culture Conversion: In patients with a positive baseline specimen, sputum culture conversion is defined as two consecutive negative cultures for MTB, collected at least 30 days apart, after anti-tuberculosis treatment. The conversion time is the date of the first negative sputum sample in the series of two consecutive negative results.

QTc Interval Prolongation: The corrected QT interval (QTc) is calculated using the Fridericia formula: QTcF = QT×(1000 ÷ RR interval in ms)^0.33. QTc prolongation is defined as an increase in QTcF of ≥ 60 ms from baseline or QTcF ≥ 500 ms at any time point.

QTc Interval Prolongation: The corrected QT interval (QTc) is calculated using the Fridericia formula: QTcF = QT×(1000 ÷ RR interval in ms)^0.33. QTc prolongation is defined as an increase in QTcF of ≥ 60 ms from baseline or QTcF ≥ 500 ms at any time point.

Intervention

Treatment Regimen: (1) Based on reliable drug susceptibility testing (DST) results, Delamanid (Dlm) was combined with at least three drugs to which the patient’s isolated MTB strain was sensitive to form a chemotherapy regimen. In the absence of reliable DST results, Dlm was combined with at least four drugs likely to be sensitive to the patient’s isolated MTB strain. (2) According to the WHO guidelines [11] and relevant Chinese guidelines [12] applicable at the time of patient recruitment, an individualized background treatment regimen was developed based on the patient’s DST results, previous anti-tuberculosis treatment history, and drug tolerability. It is noted that the current WHO guidelines have been revised and recommend different regimens.The following drugs were sequentially selected: levofloxacin, linezolid, clofazimine, cycloserine, pyrazinamide, ethambutol, prothionamide, amikacin (or capreomycin), para-aminosalicylic acid, imipenem/cilastatin or meropenem, and amoxicillin/clavulanate. Among these, linezolid, clofazimine, cycloserine, and pyrazinamide were the most commonly used.

Delamanid Dosage and Administration: (1) Delamanid was donated by Zhejiang Otsuka Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. The approval numbers are (import drug registration number: H20180004; repackaging approval number: National Medicine Standard J20180086). (2) The dosage was 100 mg (2 tablets) per dose, taken orally twice daily, continuously for 24 weeks, with medication taken after meals. The total cumulative dose was 50 mg × 672 tablets. (3) If QTcF reaches 500 ms (confirmed by repeated ECG), Delamanid and other QTcF-affecting drugs, such as levofloxacin and clofazimine, need to be discontinued, and ECG should be rechecked weekly. When QTcF is less than 450 ms, treatment with clofazimine and levofloxacin can be resumed, but Delamanid should not be used again.

Outcomes and assessments

Patient Follow-up: Patients were followed up in the outpatient clinic within one week before starting Delamanid, at the second week after starting, and at weeks 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, and 24 during treatment. During each follow-up visit, patients were required to report their clinical symptoms and undergo ECG, blood routine, blood biochemistry, urine routine tests, as well as hearing and vision assessments to avoid serious adverse drug reactions. Sputum smear microscopy and sputum culture (solid culture or BACTEC MGIT 960 liquid culture) were also performed. A chest CT scan was conducted within one month before taking the first dose of Delamanid, every 12 weeks during treatment, at the end of the treatment course, and when making a decision to stop the medication.

Efficacy Evaluation: (1) 24-Week Sputum Culture Conversion Time: The initial conversion time was recorded. The observational period for this interim analysis was defined as the 24-week Delamanid treatment phase. Patients with two consecutive negative sputum cultures and no recurrence of positive culture during this 24-week period were considered to have a good outcome. Unfavorable outcome was defined as failure to achieve consecutive negative sputum cultures during the 24-week period, reversion to positive culture during this period, death, or treatment discontinuation due to adverse events or other reasons. (2) Lesion Absorption: The efficacy was determined based on the absorption of lung lesions on CT after completing Delamanid treatment, categorized as significant effect (lesion absorption > 1/2), effective (lesion absorption ≤ 1/2 but with obvious absorption), and ineffective (lesion not absorbed or even spread/enlarged).

Safety Assessment: During the treatment period, patients’ vital signs, standard 12-lead ECG, blood routine, urine routine, liver and kidney function, electrolytes, thyroid function, hearing, vision, and adverse events were closely monitored. Special attention was given to monitoring the prolongation of the QTcF interval on ECG.In this study, the severity of adverse reactions was graded according to the DAIDS Division of AIDS Table for Grading the Severity of Adult and Pediatric Adverse Events, Version 2.1 (2017) [13], with Grade 3 or higher events defined as severe adverse reactions.

Ethics and informed consent

This study was approved by the Ethics Review Committee of Hangzhou Red Cross Hospital. All patients voluntarily participated and signed an informed consent form. Ethics approval number: 2020 No. (03).

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS version 22.0. As this was a single-arm, observational study without a control group, the analysis was primarily descriptive. Continuous variables that did not follow a normal distribution, such as time to sputum culture conversion and age, are presented as median with interquartile range (IQR) [M (Q1, Q3)]. Categorical variables, including gender, disease classification, incidence of adverse events, and imaging outcomes, are expressed as frequencies and percentages (%). The cumulative sputum culture conversion rate at 24 weeks was calculated. Given the study design aimed to characterize the outcomes of a specific regimen rather than to test a hypothesis against a comparator, no inferential statistical tests (e.g., comparisons to baseline or an external control) were performed. This descriptive approach is standard for early-phase or single-arm studies evaluating the preliminary efficacy and safety of a new therapeutic intervention.

Among these, 13 patients had MDR-TB, 2 had RR-TB, and 7 had XDR-TB. Comorbidities included diabetes in 4 patients, bronchial tuberculosis in 4 patients, and lymph node tuberculosis in 2 patients. Additionally, 15 patients had lung cavities. Detailed demographic information is provided in Table 1.

Analysis of 24-week treatment efficacy

Sputum Culture Conversion Rate: All 26 patients achieved successful sputum culture conversion. The solid media conversion rate was 100% (18/18) and the liquid media conversion rate was 100% (8/8). The median sputum culture conversion time was 34 days (range: 9–96 days) for solid media, 70 days (range: 10–171 days) for liquid media, and 28 days (range: 9-171 days) overall.

Radiological Outcomes: Chest CT scans showed significant absorption of lung lesions in 21 patients (80.7%), partial absorption in 3 patients (11.5%), and no change in 2 patients (7.7%). Among the 16 patients with lung cavities, 6 (37.5%) showed cavity reduction, 8 (50%) had cavity closure, 2 (12.5%) had a reduced number of cavities, and 1 (6.2%) developed new cavities. Compared to baseline, the lesions and cavities showed favorable outcomes after treatment.

Safety analysis

Adverse Events and Evaluation: Of the initial 29 patients, 2 withdrew from the study due to Grade 3 adverse reactions during the 24-week treatment period.As assessed by the investigators, the reasons for discontinuation were attributed to severe peripheral neuropathy (with causality assigned to the background drug linezolid) and severe psychiatric abnormalities (with causality assigned to cycloserine), respectively.Out of the 26 patients, 21 (80.8%) experienced adverse events, with a total of 108 AEs reported. There were no serious adverse events or deaths. The adverse events included: liver function abnormalities (61.5%, 16/26), anemia (50%, 13/26), leukopenia (42.3%, 11/26), elevated uric acid levels (42.3%, 11/26), peripheral neuropathy (38.5%, 10/26), tolerable gastrointestinal reactions (26.9%, 7/26), psychiatric abnormalities (19.2%, 5/26), blurred vision (15.4%, 4/26), prolonged QTcF interval (15.4%, 4/26), elevated creatinine (11.5%, 3/26), dyspnea (7.7%, 2/26), dizziness (7.7%, 2/26), bilateral lower limb edema (7.4%, 1/26), palpitations (7.4%, 1/26).

Changes in ECG QTcF Interval: among the 4 patients who experienced QTcF prolongation, two had QTcF values of 455 ms (with no significant discomfort, managed with periodic ECG monitoring, normalizing after 1 week without special treatment).One patient had a QTcF of 480 ms at 13 weeks, with palpitations, and managed with close ECG monitoring without special treatment or drug discontinuation. One patient had a QTc interval of 548 ms at 3 months, which was related to hypokalemia. In response, Delamanid and other QTc-prolonging drugs were temporarily suspended as per protocol, and potassium was supplemented. The QTc interval subsequently normalized, and the background regimen was resumed with Delamanid.None of the patients experienced significant arrhythmic events during treatment.

Discussion

Delamanid (OPC-67683) is a nitro-dihydro-imidazo-oxazole derivative, a new anti-tuberculosis drug that inhibits mycolic acid synthesis and has strong anti-tuberculosis activity against both drug-sensitive and drug-resistant Mycobacterium tuberculosis strains in vitro and in vivo [14]. This new drug was recommended by the World Health Organization (WHO) for the treatment of MDR-TB in 2014 [15]. Multiple studies have shown that adding Delamanid to the optimized background regimen for MDR-TB patients can improve sputum culture conversion rates and prognosis, with good safety and tolerability [3–6]. A meta-analysis including 22 observational studies (1276 patients) and 3 experimental studies (411 patients) indicated that the overall treatment success rate of Delamanid-containing regimens in observational studies and experimental studies was 80.9% (95% CI 72.6–87.2) and 72.5% (95% CI 44.2–89.8), respectively [16]. Studies in Germany, South Korea, and Latvia reported high treatment success rates (72%, 81.6%, and 84.2%, respectively) for MDR/XDR-TB patients using Delamanid-containing regimens [3–5].

A study in South Korea showed that among 32 MDR/XDR-TB patients treated with a Delamanid-containing regimen for 24 weeks, the sputum culture conversion rates on solid and liquid media were 94.4% and 92.9%, respectively [6]. In an EndTB observational cohort study, the sputum culture conversion rate was 80% (261/325), with a median conversion time of 42 days for RR/MDR-TB patients treated with Delamanid-containing regimens [17].

In this study, after 24 weeks of treatment with the Delamanid-containing regimen, chest CT scans of 24 patients showed significant absorption of lung lesions, 3 showed partial absorption, and 2 showed no change. Among 16 patients with lung cavities, 6 had cavity reduction, 8 had cavity closure, and 2 had a reduced number of cavities. All 26 patients achieved sputum culture conversion, with a median conversion time of 28 (9, 171) days. This demonstrates that Delamanid can significantly improve the early sputum culture conversion rate in MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB patients. Early sputum culture conversion is often a positive predictor of long-term treatment outcomes, suggesting that patients who are culture-negative at 24 weeks are likely to have a good long-term prognosis. This reduces the infectious period of drug-resistant TB, lowers transmission risk, and is significant for controlling drug-resistant TB.

Due to the absence of standardized and quantifiable CT imaging data in the current literature on delamanid-containing regimens, it is challenging to directly compare the radiographic outcomes of this study with those of previous trials. Nonetheless, a recent case series involving pre-XDR-TB patients treated with delamanid reported marked radiographic improvement on chest X-ray after 4–5 months of therapy, which is consistent with the CT improvements observed in our cohort [18]. Moreover, the high rate of sputum culture conversion (100%) paralleled by significant lesion absorption on CT (92.3%) in the present study underscores the internal consistency between microbiological and radiographic outcomes, collectively supporting the treatment benefits of the delamanid-based regimen.

In terms of safety, Phase III clinical trial results indicate that Delamanid is well-tolerated and has a high safety profile [19]. Previous clinical trials suggest that the main adverse events of Delamanid include QTc interval prolongation (which may prolong by 5–15 ms, peaking at 8 weeks of treatment) and palpitations [20].

Adverse drug reactions often lead to poor treatment adherence or treatment interruption. In this study, 2 patients discontinued treatment due to adverse drug reactions, and 1 patient was lost to follow-up. Although 77.8% of patients experienced adverse events (AEs), most AEs were considered related to background anti-TB treatment and were generally controllable. The main AEs included peripheral neuropathy (considered related to linezolid, managed symptomatically or by discontinuing linezolid in severe cases), bone marrow suppression (considered related to linezolid, relieved by dose reduction or discontinuation), gastrointestinal reactions, liver function abnormalities, and psychiatric disorders, similar to many studies. Most AEs did not affect subsequent continuous medication after symptomatic treatment and did not significantly impact treatment outcomes. Among the 26 patients, 15.4% experienced QTc interval prolongation, including 2 cases with 455 ms (no significant discomfort, periodic ECG rechecks), 1 case with 480 ms (no significant discomfort, periodic ECG rechecks), and 1 case with 548 ms (related to hypokalemia, normalized after potassium supplementation). None of these cases led to drug discontinuation or severe arrhythmic events. It is noteworthy that QTc interval prolongation may also be related to other anti-TB drugs in the background regimen, such as fluoroquinolones, cycloserine, and clofazimine. In this study, most patients’ individualized background regimens included fluoroquinolones (n = 15, 57.7%), cycloserine (n = 26, 100%), and clofazimine (n = 26, 100%), which can also prolong the QTc interval, especially when used together. Therefore, during treatment with Delamanid-containing regimens, attention should be paid to the additive effect on QTcF interval prolongation when combined with other drugs, requiring regular ECG monitoring.

In a retrospective study in Germany on 29 patients receiving Delamanid-containing regimens, 3 patients had QTcF increases over 500 ms, but these patients did not terminate treatment and QT intervals returned to normal as assessed by enhanced ECG monitoring. However, one patient discontinued Delamanid treatment due to a suicide attempt. Further research is needed to determine whether temporary or permanent discontinuation of Delamanid is necessary for patients with QTcF > 500 ms. Our data further support previous findings on the good safety profile of Delamanid.

The profile of adverse events in our study is largely comparable to that reported in previous trials [3–6]. The incidence of liver function abnormalities (61.5%) and hematological events (anemia 50%, leukopenia 42.3%) was prominent, likely driven by the background regimen, particularly linezolid. Notably, The incidence of QTcF interval prolongation in this cohort (15.4%) appears to be higher than that reported in some previous studies. For example, a retrospective study from South Africa showed an incidence of QTcF interval prolongation of 5.3% in patients receiving a bedaquiline-containing regimen [21], while another study from the United States reported that only 3.2% of patients developed QTcF interval prolongation [22]. Studies from China on bedaquiline have also reported a higher incidence of QT interval prolongation compared to international studies [23], seemingly suggesting that the Chinese patient population may have relatively weaker cardiac tolerance to new anti-tuberculosis drugs, potentially related to ethnic pharmacogenetic differences [24]. The exact reasons behind this observed difference warrant further investigation in larger-scale, prospective studies that incorporate genetic analysis. Nonetheless, all QTc prolongation events in this study were manageable without requiring permanent discontinuation of delamanid, further indicating that with appropriate monitoring, delamanid has an overall acceptable safety profile.

Conclusions

In conclusion, combining Delamanid in individualized regimens for MDR-TB/RR-TB/XDR-TB patients can significantly improve the 24-week sputum culture conversion rate, shorten the conversion time, and has good overall safety and tolerability, providing more treatment options for these patients.

This study has several limitations. First, the single-arm, non-comparative nature of this study is a fundamental constraint. Without a control group, it is impossible to definitively attribute the encouraging culture conversion rates and radiological improvements solely to the addition of delamanid, as the optimized background regimen (OBR) is a critical contributor to the overall treatment effect. Second, the relatively small sample size (n = 26) from a single center may limit the statistical power and the generalizability of our findings to the broader MDR-TB population, which is highly heterogeneous. Third, the individualized nature of the OBR, while necessary to conform to WHO guidelines and address complex drug resistance patterns, introduced variability between patients. This heterogeneity, although a reflection of real-world clinical practice, makes it challenging to isolate the specific impact of any single drug, including delamanid. Finally, this is an interim analysis reporting outcomes at the end of the 24-week delamanid treatment phase. While early sputum conversion is a strong positive predictor of final treatment success, this timeframe may overestimate the ultimate success rate, as relapses, late-onset adverse events, or failures can occur during the subsequent continuation phase. Long-term follow-up of this cohort is ongoing to ascertain the final treatment outcomes and sustained safety profile.

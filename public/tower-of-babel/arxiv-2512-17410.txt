Kinetic-Theory Bounds on the Equation of State of Dense QCD Matter
Michał Marczenko

Abstract
We derive bounds on the equation of state of cold, dense matter by extending the causal, model-agnostic interpolation between chiral effective field theory and perturbative calculations with a microscopic constraint from relativistic kinetic theory. The additional condition restricts the stiffest admissible behavior of the equation of state and systematically reduces the range of allowed equations of state, with the strongest effect at high densities. The resulting bounds remain consistent with known low- and high-density limits, while the strength of the constraint depends on the density above which the kinetic-theory condition is applied. These bounds can be readily incorporated into future studies of cold, dense matter and used to assess the impact of microscopic stability conditions on equation-of-state inference.



I
Introduction

Recent advances in neutron-star (NS) astronomy, from precise radius measurements
(
Miller et al. 2021
;
Riley et al. 2021
)
and the discovery of massive pulsars
(
Demorest et al. 2010
;
Antoniadis et al. 2013
)
to gravitational-wave detections of binary mergers
(
Abbott et al. 2017a
;
Abbott et al. 2017b
)
, have opened direct access to strongly interacting matter at supranuclear densities. Within general relativity, the macroscopic structure of NSs is governed by the equation of state (EOS) of cold and dense quantum chromodynamics (QCD) matter. Although, in principle, the EOS is uniquely determined by QCD, reliable first-principle calculations exist only in asymptotic limits. At low densities, chiral effective field theory (
{\chi\rm EFT}
) provides a systematic expansion valid up to around nuclear saturation density
n_{\rm sat}

(
Tews et al. 2018
;
Hebeler et al. 2013
;
Drischler et al. 2019
;
Drischler et al. 2021
;
Drischler et al. 2020a
;
Keller et al. 2023
;
Drischler et al. 2020b
)
, while at very high densities, perturbative QCD (
{\rm pQCD}
) becomes applicable for
n\gtrsim 40\,n_{\rm sat}

(
Hebeler et al. 2013
;
Tews et al. 2018
;
Gorda et al. 2021
;
Gorda and Säppi 2022
)
. The intermediate regime realized in NS cores, corresponding to strongly coupled QCD matter at a few times
n_{\rm sat}
, remains inaccessible to controlled
ab initio
calculations and at present must be constrained indirectly (see, e.g.,
Ecker and Rezzolla 2022a
;
Ecker and Rezzolla 2022b
;
Annala et al. 2018
;
Annala et al. 2020
;
Marczenko et al. 2023
;
Marczenko et al. 2024
;
Brandes et al. 2023
).

A model-independent interpolation between the
{\chi\rm EFT}
and
{\rm pQCD}
regimes provides a systematic way to connect nuclear and quark matter equations of state under basic thermodynamic consistency
(
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
)
. This approach enforces the most fundamental physical principles, thermodynamic stability, causality, and integrability, without relying on any specific microscopic model. It defines an admissible region in the
(n,\mu)
plane whose boundaries correspond to the stiffest and softest EOSs consistent with these requirements. When mapped to the
(\epsilon,p)
plane, the resulting family of functions forms a band that encompasses all physically allowed equations of state of dense QCD matter. The approach has attracted growing interest and is now commonly used in the EOS analyses (see, e.g.,
Fujimoto and Reddy 2024
;
Gao 2025
;
Alarcón et al. 2025
).

The macroscopic causality condition
c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
constrains only the propagation of signals. The stability of Israel-Stewart (IS) theory further restricts the admissible dynamics. The relaxation times of dissipative currents must be within certain limits, and if kinetic theory (KT) is applied to evaluate them, then within this approximation, one obtains a stronger inequality for the speed of sound
(
Olson 2000
)
,

c_{s}^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon-p/3}{\epsilon+p}\equiv c_{s,\,\rm KT}^{2}\textrm{,}

(1)

where
p
is the pressure and
\epsilon
is the energy density. The limit is derived from the requirement that the relaxation time in a causal Boltzmann equation remains positive. At low densities, where
p\ll\epsilon
, this condition reproduces the causal bound
c_{s}^{2}\lesssim 1
, while in the conformal limit, where
p=\epsilon/3
, it gives
c_{s}^{2}\leq 2/3
, which safely accommodates the conformal value
c_{s}^{2}=1/3
. Thus, the KT bound restricts the maximal stiffening of any EOS that admits a quasi-particle transport description. It has been examined in studies of dissipative hydrodynamics and applied phenomenologically to model NS EOSs and constrain the sound speed in dense QCD matter (see, e.g.
Moustakidis et al. 2017
;
Margaritis et al. 2020
;
Laskos-Patkos et al. 2025
).

In this work, we extend the causal-integrable construction of
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
by incorporating the KT bound as a microscopic constraint on the EOS. The onset density
n_{\rm th}
(or equivalently, chemical potential
\mu_{\rm th}
) above which the KT condition is enforced is treated as a free parameter, reflecting the uncertainty in the regime where kinetic theory becomes applicable (see, e.g.,
Denicol et al. 2012
;
Romatschke and Romatschke 2019
for discussion of the applicability of kinetic theory and Israel-Stewart truncations). We show that the KT bound substantially narrows the EOS band relative to the purely causal case, illustrating how transport-theory stability conditions translate into quantitative restrictions on the high-density equation of state.

II
Bounds from causality

In this work, we consider the construction of all possible interpolations of the thermodynamic pressure
p(\mu)
at zero temperature between two known values of the chemical potential,
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{H}
(see
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
for details). We assume that the pressures
p(\mu_{L})
,
p(\mu_{H})
and baryon densities
n(\mu_{L})
,
n(\mu_{H})
are also known. Here, we use the constraints from
{\chi\rm EFT}
and pQCD to fix the EOS at
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{H}
, respectively. The variation of the low-density EOS within the
\chi
EFT constraint does not lead to qualitative changes in the results presented in this work
(
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
)
. Therefore, we use a single EOS from
{\chi\rm EFT}
, corresponding to the EOS with the largest pressure at
\mu_{{\chi\rm EFT}}=0.978\,
GeV,
p_{\chi\rm EFT}=3.542\,\rm MeV/fm^{3}
, and
n_{\chi\rm EFT}=0.176\,\rm fm^{-3}

(
Hebeler et al. 2013
)
. On the other hand, the high-density pQCD constraint is renormalization-scale dependent, and variation of the scale parameter
X
has to be taken into account. Following
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
, we fix the pQCD constraint at
\mu_{H}=2.6\,
GeV and use
p_{{\rm pQCD}}=2.334,\,3.823,\,4.284\,\rm GeV/fm^{3}
and
n_{{\rm pQCD}}=6.14,\,6.47,\,6.87\,\rm fm^{-3}
for
X=1,\,2,\,4
, respectively.

To constrain the allowed family of densities,
n(u)
, we use the conditions of thermodynamic consistency and require stability and causality, which together ensure that
0\leq c_{s}^{2}\leq 1
. The first inequality implies that the density
n(\mu)
is a non-decreasing function. The second inequality implies that

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{n}{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}n}\leq 1\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\geq\frac{n}{\mu}\textrm{,}

(2)

which determines the smallest possible slope of
n(\mu)
, i.e., the stiffest allowed EOS at any given point
(\mu,n)
.

For convenience, we define the stiffest EOS going through a point
(\mu_{x},n_{x})
as

n_{x}(\mu)\equiv n(\mu;\mu_{x},n_{x})=\frac{n_{x}}{\mu_{x}}\mu\textrm{.}

(3)

The lower and upper causal bounds,
n_{L}(\mu)
and
n_{H}(\mu)
, correspond to the stiffest EOSs that pass through the points
(\mu_{L},n_{L})
and
(\mu_{H},n_{H})
, respectively. This is demonstrated in Figure
1
.

The requirement that the pressure difference,
\Delta p
, between two chemical potentials,
\mu_{L}
and
\mu_{H}
, is fixed imposes further (integral) constraints by fixing the area under
n(\mu)
between them,

\Delta p=\int\limits_{\mu_{L}}^{\mu_{H}}\mathrm{d}\mu\;n\left(\mu\right)\textrm{.}

(4)

By considering functions
n_{\rm min}(\mu)
(
n_{\rm max}(\mu)
) that go through a fixed point
(\mu_{x},n_{x})
and minimize (maximize) the pressure
\Delta p_{\rm max}
(
\Delta p_{\rm min}
), one can rule out particular, otherwise allowed, regions in the
n(\mu)
plane. These functions are given by

n_{\rm min}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{L}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{x}\\[4.0pt]
n_{x}(\mu),&\mu>\mu_{x}\end{cases}\textrm{,}

(5)

and

n_{\rm max}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{x}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{x}\\[4.0pt]
n_{H}(\mu),&\mu>\mu_{x}\end{cases}\textrm{.}

(6)

At
\mu_{x}
the functions
n_{\rm min}(\mu)
and
n_{\rm max}(\mu)
feature first-order phase transitions from
n_{L}(\mu_{x})
to
n_{x}
and from
n_{x}
to
n_{H}(\mu_{x})
, respectively. Their schematic construction is shown in Figure
2
. The point
(\mu_{x},~n_{x})
has to be determined separately for both
n_{\rm min}(\mu)
and
n_{\rm max}(\mu)
such that Eq. (
4
) is met, i.e.,
\Delta p_{\rm min}=\Delta p_{\rm max}=p_{H}-p_{L}
. This procedure yields both the upper and lower bounds on
n(\mu)
. The simultaneous causal and integral constraints are depicted in Figure
1
. We note that the general conditions for the integral constraint can be solved analytically for the class of constant-speed-of-sound EOSs
(
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
)
.

III
Bounds from kinetic theory

The KT bound in Eq. (
1
) can be expressed as a differential equation for the stiffest EOS admissible by the constraint using the zero-temperature expression for the thermodynamic pressure
p=n^{2}\mathrm{d}\left(\epsilon/n\right)/\mathrm{d}n
,

n^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\epsilon(n)}{\mathrm{d}n^{2}}+\frac{n}{3}\frac{\mathrm{d}\epsilon(n)}{\mathrm{d}n}-\frac{4}{3}\epsilon(n)=0\textrm{.}

(7)

Solving this equation yields

\begin{split}\epsilon(n)&=\alpha_{+}n^{\beta_{+}}+\alpha_{-}n^{\beta_{-}}\textrm{,}\\
\end{split}

(8)

where
\beta_{\pm}=\left(1\pm\sqrt{13}\right)/3
and
\alpha_{\pm}
are integration constants. The pressure
p(n)
and chemical potential
\mu(n)
can be obtained through the zero-temperature Gibbs-Duhem equation
p+\epsilon=\mu n
and
\mu=\mathrm{d}\epsilon/\mathrm{d}n
.

Since the microscopic regime where the relativistic kinetic-theory description of dense QCD matter becomes valid remains uncertain, we introduce the onset density
n_{\rm th}
as a free parameter with
n(\mu_{L})<n_{\rm th}<n(\mu_{H})
. It marks the transition above which we impose the KT constraint on the speed of sound. Varying
n_{\rm th}
allows us to quantify how early the KT regime must set in to significantly affect the stiffness of the EOS. At
n<n_{\rm th}
, the stiffest allowed EOS remains luminal, i.e.,
c_{s}^{2}=1
, while at
n>n_{\rm th}
, the KT constraint sets in and from Eq. (
1
) one gets that

c_{s}^{2}=\frac{n}{\mu}\frac{\mathrm{d}\mu}{\mathrm{d}n}\leq c_{s,\rm KT}^{2}\;\Longrightarrow\;\frac{\mathrm{d}n}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\geq\frac{n}{\mu}\left(1-\frac{4}{3}\frac{p}{\mu n}\right)^{-1}\textrm{,}

(9)

where the zero-temperature Gibbs-Duhem equation was used. The bracket is always smaller than unity; thus, the slope of the stiffest EOS allowed by kinetic theory is always larger than that of luminal EOS. This way, the maximum speed of sound allowed by kinetic theory further constrains the allowed
n(\mu)
space.

The construction of the constraints on
n(\mu)
is similar to the case discussed in Section
II
. However, once the density reaches the threshold value,
n_{\rm th}
, the EOS has to switch to that of the kinetic theory,

n_{\rm L/H}^{\rm KT}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{{\rm L}/x}(\mu),&\mu\leq\mu_{\rm th}\\
n_{\rm KT}(\mu),&\mu>\mu_{\rm th}\end{cases}\textrm{.}

(10)

Because
n_{\rm th}>n_{L}
, the lower bound on density,
n_{\rm L}^{\rm KT}(\mu)
, starts as the luminal EOS,
n_{L}(\mu)
, and switches to the KT EOS at
\mu_{\rm th}
such that
n_{L}(\mu_{\rm th})=n_{\rm th}
. The corresponding upper bound on density,
n_{\rm H}^{\rm KT}(\mu)
, is obtained by assuming that the EOS jumps from
n_{L}
to
n_{x}
at
\mu_{L}
. The density
n_{x}
has to be chosen so that the upper bound goes through the high-density constraint at
\mu_{H}
, i.e.,
n_{\rm H}^{\rm KT}(\mu_{H})=n_{H}
. We remark that if
n_{x}(\mu_{L})>n_{\rm th}
, the upper bound
n_{\rm H}^{\rm KT}(\mu)
would be given entirely by the KT EOS fixed at
n_{L}
.

The function
n_{\rm KT}(\mu)
is given as a solution of the Eq. (
7
). However, to fully determine the KT EOS, one needs to know the pressure,
p(\mu_{\rm th})
at
\mu_{\rm th}
, which is obtained by integrating the density below
\mu_{\rm th}
.

We demonstrate the effect of the KT constraint for
n_{\rm th}=20\,n_{\rm sat}
in Figure
1
. The upper bound reduces significantly compared to the causal bound. On the other hand, the lower bound remains unchanged. This is because the lower causal bound
n_{L}(\mu_{H})\approx 3\,n_{\rm sat}<n_{\rm th}
. Note that as
n_{\rm th}
approaches
n_{H}
, the impact of the KT constraint becomes ineffective and the bound smoothly reduces to the causal one.

With the kinetic-theory constraint, the construction of the integral constraint must take into account the KT onset density
n_{\rm th}
. The density functions that minimize and maximize the thermodynamic pressure,
n_{\rm min}^{\rm KT}
and
n_{\rm max}^{\rm KT}
, respectively, have to take into account two cases. First, when
n_{x}<n_{\rm th}
, for which the densities are given as

n_{\rm min}^{\rm KT}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{L}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{x}\\
n_{x}(\mu),&\mu_{x}<\mu\leq\mu_{\rm th}\\
n_{\rm KT}(\mu),&\mu_{\rm th}\leq\mu\end{cases}\textrm{.}

(11)

and

n_{\rm max}^{\rm KT}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{x}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{x}\\
n_{\rm th}(\mu),&\mu_{x}<\mu\leq\mu_{\rm th}\\
n_{\rm KT}(\mu),&\mu_{\rm th}\leq\mu\end{cases}\textrm{,}

(12)

and second, when
n_{x}>n_{\rm th}
, for which the densities read

n_{\rm min}^{\rm KT}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{L}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{\rm th}\\
n_{\rm KT}^{(1)}(\mu),&\mu_{\rm th}<\mu<\mu_{x}\\
n_{\rm KT}^{(2)}(\mu),&\mu_{x}<\mu\end{cases}\textrm{,}

(13)

n_{\rm max}^{\rm KT}(\mu)=\begin{cases}n_{\rm th}(\mu),&\mu<\mu_{\rm th}\\
n_{\rm KT}^{(1)}(\mu),&\mu_{\rm th}<\mu<\mu_{x}\\
n_{\rm KT}^{(2)}(\mu),&\mu_{x}<\mu\end{cases}\textrm{.}

(14)

The density
n_{\rm th}(\mu)
is fixed at
\mu_{\rm th}
, i.e.,
n_{\rm th}(\mu_{\rm th})=n_{x}(\mu_{\rm th})
and
p_{\rm th}(\mu_{\rm th})=p_{x}(\mu_{\rm th})
. Schematic construction of the functions
n_{\rm min}^{\rm KT}(\mu)
and
n_{\rm max}^{\rm KT}(\mu)
is shown in Figure

.

Kinetic-theory bound on
\epsilon
-
p

The constraint obtained in the
\mu-n
plane can be mapped to the
\epsilon-p
plane using the procedure outlined in Refs.
Komoltsev and Kurkela 2022
;
Fujimoto and Reddy 2024
. Figure
4
shows the allowed range of
p(\epsilon)
for different values of
n_{\rm th}
. The combined effect of the KT and integral constraints appears as a narrowing of the admissible EOS region. We note that because the KT condition turns on only at higher densities (above
n_{\rm th}
), its influence is concentrated in the high-density part of the EOS.

Figure
5
shows the fraction of excluded area in the
\epsilon-p
plane compared to the band allowed by causality. As
n_{\rm th}
increases, the fraction of excluded area decreases. Introducing the KT constraint at densities close to
n_{\rm pQCD}
does not offer a substantial reduction in the allowed
\epsilon-p
space. However, even for moderate thresholds such as
n_{\rm th}\simeq 25\,n_{\rm sat}
, the excluded fraction already reaches
10-23\%
as the scale parameter
X
is varied between
1
and
4
. We note that larger values of
X
lead to a correspondingly smaller excluded fraction. The largest excluded area is obtained for the smallest
n_{\rm th}
and goes up to
35\%
for
n_{\rm th}=1.1\,n_{\rm sat}
for
X=4
. However, we note that such a small
n_{\rm th}
is probably unrealistic.

V
Conclusion

We have explored how relativistic kinetic theory (KT) constrains the stiffness of dense QCD matter. Building upon the model-agnostic construction of the equation of state (EOS) bounded by chiral effective field theory at low densities and perturbative QCD at high densities, we imposed an additional constraint on the speed of sound. This bound follows from the positivity of the relaxation time and the stability of the kinetic-theory dispersion relation, and it becomes effective only above a threshold density at which a quasi-particle description is assumed to apply. For any chosen value of onset density, imposing the KT condition removes a significant subset of causal and thermodynamically admissible interpolations, yielding a narrower admissible EOS band. Smaller values of the onset density produce stronger restrictions, while for larger values the effect smoothly reduces to the purely causal construction. In this way, kinetic theory provides a physically motivated, parameter-dependent upper bound on the stiffness of dense matter.

Our results indicate that transport-theory considerations can provide a physically motivated way of limiting the high-density behavior of the EOS. While the practical implications for neutron stars depend on the onset density of KT applicability, the framework offers a useful theoretical prior for connecting microscopic QCD dynamics with astrophysical constraints.

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate dimension-free estimates for maximal operators of convolutions with discrete normalized Gaussians (related to the Theta function) in the context of maximal, jump and r r -variational inequalities on ℓ p ​ ( ℤ d ) \ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) spaces. This is the first instance of a discrete operator in the literature where ℓ p ​ ( ℤ d ) \ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}) bounds are provided for the entire range of 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . The methods of proof rely on developing robust Fourier methods, which are combined with the fractional derivative, a tool that has not been previously applied to studying similar questions in the discrete setting.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Statement of the main results

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
denote one-dimensional torus, which will be identified with
[-1/2,1/2)
. For
t>0
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
we define a one-dimensional Theta function by

\displaystyle\theta_{t}(\zeta):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp(-\pi(k-\zeta)^{2}t).

For
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we also define its
d
-dimensional variant by

(1.1)

\Theta_{t}(\xi):=\prod_{j\in[d]}\theta_{t}(\xi_{j})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp(-\pi|k-\xi|^{2}t),

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm.

For
t>0
the discrete normalized Gaussian kernel
g_{t}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\displaystyle g_{t}(n):=\frac{1}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi|n|^{2}}{t}\bigg),\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},

and the corresponding convolution operator on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with kernel
g_{t}
is defined by

G_{t}f(x):=g_{t}*f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(n)f(x-n),\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\quad f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

We immediately see that
G_{t}
is a
contraction on all
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
spaces for all
1\leq p\leq\infty,
that is

(1.2)

\sup_{t>0}\|G_{t}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq 1,

which follows from Minkowski’s convolution inequality and the fact that
\|g_{t}\|_{\ell^{1}}=1
for any
t>0
.
The main goal of this paper is to prove the following dimension-free maximal, jump, variational and oscillation estimates. See Section
2.5
for the definitions of these objects.

Theorem 1.1
.

For each
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists
C_{p}>0
independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\big\|\lambda N_{\lambda}(G_{t}f:t>0)^{1/2}\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}},\qquad f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

In particular, for each
p\in(1,\infty)
and
r\in(2,\infty)
there exists
C_{p,r}>0
independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\big\|V^{r}(G_{t}f:t>0)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq C_{p,r}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}},\qquad f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

Further, for each
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists
C_{p}>0
independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}((0,\infty))}\big\|O_{I,J}^{2}(G_{t}f:t>0)\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}},\qquad f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

Furthermore, for each
p\in(1,\infty)
there exists
C_{p}>0
independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t>0}|G_{t}f|\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{\ell^{p}},\qquad f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

1.2.
Historical background and our motivations

In order to explain why we are undertaking Theorem
1.1
, we
need to briefly describe the historical background of dimension-free
estimates in harmonic analysis. Let
U
be a closed, bounded,
symmetric, and convex subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with non-empty interior, which
we simply call a symmetric convex body. We set

U_{t}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:t^{-1}y\in U\}
for every
t>0
and, for every

x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define the
Hardy–Littlewood averaging operator by

(1.6)

\displaystyle M_{t}^{U}f(x):=\frac{1}{|U_{t}|}\int_{U_{t}}f(x-y)\,dy.

A typical choice is
U=B^{q}(d)
for
q\in[1,\infty]
, where
B^{q}(d)

is the unit ball induced by the
\ell^{q}
norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see (
2.1
) and (
2.2
). Specifically,

B^{2}(d)
is the Euclidean unit ball and
B^{\infty}(d)
is the cube in

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\emptyset\neq\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
and

p\in[1,\infty]
, let
0<C(p,\mathbb{I},U)\leq\infty
be the smallest
constant for the following maximal inequality

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{I}}|M_{t}^{U}f|\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(p,\mathbb{I},U)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\qquad f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Using a standard covering argument (Vitali covering lemma) for
p=1
and interpolation with
p=\infty
, it is not hard
to see that
C(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)<\infty
for every
p\in(1,\infty]
since
C(\infty,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)=1
.

Similarly, for every
t>0
let

U_{t}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon t^{-1}y\in U\}
denote the
dilate of a set
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we
define a discrete analogue of (
1.6
) by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{t}^{U}f(x):=\frac{1}{|U_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}|}\sum_{y\in U_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(x-y),\qquad f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

where the right-hand side above is understood as
0
if
U_{t}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}=\emptyset
.
The corresponding maximal operator is given by
\mathcal{M}^{U}f(x):=\sup_{t>0}|\mathcal{M}_{t}^{U}f(x)|
.
For
\emptyset\neq\mathbb{I}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, let

0<\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{I},U)\leq\infty
be the smallest constant for
the following maximal inequality

\displaystyle\big\|\sup_{t\in\mathbb{I}}|\mathcal{M}^{U}_{t}f|\big\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{I},U)\|f\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})},\qquad f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

If
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a symmetric convex body, then using
similar arguments as in the continuous setting yield
\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)<\infty
for every
p\in(1,\infty]
.

A major open problem in this area since the late 1970’s arose from
Stein’s work
[
26
]
and asserts that:

Dimension-free conjecture
.

For every
p\in(1,\infty]

there is a constant
C_{p}>0
such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sup_{U\in\mathfrak{B}(d)}C(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)\leq C_{p}

where
\mathfrak{B}(d)
is the set of all convex symmetric bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

This problem has been studied for four decades by several authors. We
now briefly list the current state of the art concerning
(
1.7
).

1.

Stein
[
26
]
(see also Stein and Strömberg

[
29
]
) proved that there exists a constant
C_{p}>0
depending
only on
p\in(1,\infty]
such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}C(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{2}(d))\leq C_{p}
. The key idea from

[
26
,
29
]
is based on the method of rotations, which
enables one to view high-dimensional spheres as an average of rotated
low-dimensional ones. This in turn implies dimension-free estimates for the
maximal spherical function. Unfortunately, this method is limited to the Euclidean balls.

2.

Bourgain
[
3
,
4
]
and Carbery

[
10
]
independently proved (
1.7
) for
p\in(3/2,\infty]
. They also
showed a dyadic variant of (
1.7
) for
p\in(1,\infty]
with

\mathbb{D}=\{2^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
in place of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Although inequality (
1.7
) for
p\in(1,3/2]

still remains open, the case of
q
-balls is quite well understood.

3.

For the
q
-balls
B^{q}(d)
, we know that for every

p\in(1,\infty]
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, there is a constant

C_{p,q}>0
such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}C(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{q}(d))\leq C_{p,q}
. This was established
by Müller in
[
21
]
(for
q\in[1,\infty)
) and by Bourgain in

[
5
]
(for cubes
q=\infty
).

4.

The first and third authors with Bourgain and Stein
[
6
]

extended the results from
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
10
,
21
]
to

r
-variational estimates with bounds independent of the dimension.
We also found new proofs of maximal theorems from
[
3
,
4
,
10
]
. The methods from
[
6
]
also shed new light on
dimension-free estimates in the discrete setup, which is a new and
interesting direction.

Several years ago the first and third authors in a collaboration with Bourgain and
Stein
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
initiated the study of dimension-free estimates in the discrete setting. This line of research was partially motivated by a question of E.M. Stein
[
28
]
from the
mid 1990’s about the dimension-free estimates for the discrete
Hardy–Littlewood maximal functions corresponding to the Euclidean
balls
B^{2}(d)
.

Stein’s question
.

Is it
true that there is a universal constant
C>0
such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(2,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{2}(d))=\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(2,\sqrt{\mathbb{N}_{0}},B^{2}(d))\leq C\,?

We now briefly mention existing results in the discrete setting and their relation to (
1.7
) and (
1.8
).

1.

In
[
8
]
, it was shown
that (
1.7
) cannot be expected in the discrete setup.
Surprisingly, in contrast to the continuous setup
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
21
,
26
,
25
]
, one can prove

[
8
]
that for every
p\in(1,\infty)
, there is a constant

C_{p}>0
such that for certain ellipsoids
E(d)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all

d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, one has

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},E(d))\geq C_{p}(\log d)^{1/p}.

On the other hand, for the cubes
B^{\infty}(d)
, it was also shown in

[
8
]
that for every
p\in(3/2,\infty]
, there is a constant
C_{p}>0

such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))\leq C_{p}
. For

p\in(1,3/2]
, it still remains open whether

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))
is finite. However,
one can prove that for all
p\in(1,\infty]
, there is a constant

C_{p}>0
such that

\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{D},B^{\infty}(d))\leq C_{p}
.

2.

In
[
7
,
9
]
, similar questions in the context of
discrete Euclidean balls were studied, and it was shown that there is a universal constant
C>0
such that for every

p\in[2,\infty]
we have

(1.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{D},B^{2}(d))\leq C.

A similar result was also obtained by the authors
[
20
]
in the
context of discrete Euclidean spheres. However, the general problem for
\mathbb{R}_{+}

in place of
\mathbb{D}
remains open and thus Stein’s question (
1.8
) is still not answered.

3.

Recently, the first and third authors in a collaboration with
Kosz and Plewa
[
15
]
proved that for every

U\in\mathfrak{B}(d)
, one has

(1.11)

\displaystyle C(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)\leq\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},U)\quad\text{ for all }\quad p\in[1,\infty],

where the case
p=1
corresponds to the optimal constants in the weak
type
(1,1)
inequalities respectively in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It was
also shown in
[
15
]
that

C(1,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))=\mathcal{C}(1,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))
,
which, in view of Aldaz’s result
[
1
]
, yields that

\mathcal{C}(1,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))\ _{\overrightarrow{d\to\infty}}\ \infty
. Thus
the boundedness of
\sup_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}(p,\mathbb{R}_{+},B^{\infty}(d))

for
p\in(1,3/2]
cannot be deduced by interpolation from

[
8
]
for
p\in(3/2,\infty]
. Inequality (
1.11
)
exhibits a well known phenomenon in harmonic analysis that states that
it is harder to establish bounds for discrete operators than the
bounds for their continuous counterparts, and this is the best that we
could prove in this generality.

Our motivations for investigating Theorem
1.1
, on the one
hand, come from the articles
[
7
]
and
[
20
]
, where
dimension-free estimates for the dyadic versions of the maximal
operators corresponding to the discrete Euclidean balls and spheres
were proved for all
p\in[2,\infty]
, see (
1.10
). On the
other hand, our motivations come from
[
8
]
, where certain
ellipsoids satisfying (
1.9
) have been constructed to
demonstrate that the dimension-free phenomena in the discrete setting
is not as broad as in the continuous setting.

Taking into account the results from
[
7
,
8
]
and

[
20
]
and the observation that Euclidean balls are not
significantly different from ellipsoids, it was tempting to prove
(
1.9
) with Euclidean balls
B^{2}(d)
in place of the ellipsoids

E(d)
. At some point, we believed that the maximal function
appearing in (
1.5
) could provide a negative answer to Stein’s question (
1.8
). This was caused by a simple pointwise
bound

(1.12)

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}|G_{t}f(x)|\leq\mathcal{M}^{B^{2}(d)}f(x)\leq\mathcal{M}^{S^{2}(d)}f(x),\qquad x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\quad f\geq 0,

where
S^{2}(d):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|_{2}=1\}
is the Euclidean
unit sphere. Inequality (
1.12
) immediately guarantees that
dimension-free
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
estimates for
\mathcal{M}^{S^{2}(d)}f

imply dimension-free
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
estimates for

\mathcal{M}^{B^{2}(d)}f
and the latter imply dimension-free

\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
estimates for
\sup_{t>0}|G_{t}f|
. However,
inequality (
1.12
) can also serve to disprove dimension-free
estimates for the
\mathcal{M}^{B^{2}(d)}f
if it can be demonstrated
that there are no dimension-free estimates for
\sup_{t>0}|G_{t}f|

on
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
spaces. That was our hope, but this paper shows
that we were mistaken, as dimension-free estimates for

\sup_{t>0}|G_{t}f|
are available for all
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
spaces
whenever
1<p<\infty
, see Theorem
1.1
. Now our main
result, Theorem
1.1
, may provide evidence that it could be
possible to obtain dimension-free estimates for

\mathcal{M}^{B^{2}(d)}f
and
\mathcal{M}^{S^{2}(d)}f
. Further evidence
supporting this supposition has been provided very recently by the
third author in collaboration with Niksiński
[
22
]
. Namely, it was
proved there that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a constant

C_{\varepsilon}>0
depending only on
\varepsilon
and such that for
every
p\in[2,\infty]
one has

(1.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\mathcal{C}(p,(0,d^{1/2-\varepsilon}],B^{2}(d))\leq C_{\varepsilon}.

Both our main result, Theorem
1.1
, and inequality
(
1.13
) strongly suggest that Stein’s question (
1.8
) may
indeed have a positive answer. We hope to explore these topics in
future work.

1.3.
Ideas of the proof and its corollaries

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on two main
ingredients. The first of these ingredients is the Stein complex
interpolation theorem
[
24
]
. In contrast to other discrete
dimension-free problems
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
20
]
, the
fractional derivative is a very useful tool that allows us to
implement Stein’ complex interpolation theorem. The second of these
ingredients, and perhaps the most important one from the perspective
of our article, are estimates for the Fourier transform of the kernel

g_{t}
, which ultimately lead to a successful comparison of
g_{t}
with
the heat kernel for the discrete Laplacian, for which dimension-free
maximal, jump, variational, and oscillation estimates are known, see
[
8
,
13
,
17
]
. It is worth mentioning that
G_{t}
as well as its natural local
modification
G_{\psi^{-1}(t)}
for
t\in(0,1)
, see
(
5.10
), are not (local) semigroups. This makes the analysis
of
G_{t}
much more complicated. Note that for semigroups there are
general tools which allow to obtain dimension-free estimates, see
e.g.
[
27
]
and
[
8
, Section 4.1]
,

[
17
, Theorem 1.2]
.

In order to understand the Fourier transform estimates of the kernel
g_{t}
at the origin and at infinity, we will use two representations for
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}
. By definition the first representation has the following form

(1.14)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi|n|^{2}}{t}\bigg)e(-n\cdot\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

where
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Formula (
1.14
) is particularly helpful when dealing with small scales,
specifically
0<t\leq 16
. This is because the exponential

e^{-\pi/t}
becomes very small for small values of
t
and the series
in equation (
1.14
) converges quickly for these values of
t
.

Considering
\varphi(y):=\exp(-\pi t|y|^{2})
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
applying the Poisson summation formula we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\varphi(n-\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(n)e(-n\cdot\xi),

which is equivalent to the following formula

(1.15)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi|n|^{2}}{t}\bigg)e(-n\cdot\xi)=t^{d/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n-\xi|^{2}t\big)=t^{d/2}\Theta_{t}(\xi).

In particular, taking
\xi=0
in the above formula we have

(1.16)

\Theta_{1/t}(0)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{\pi|n|^{2}}{t}\bigg)=t^{d/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}t\big)=t^{d/2}\Theta_{t}(0).

Hence, coming back to (
1.14
) we obtain the second representation given by the formula

(1.17)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\Theta_{t}(\xi)}{\Theta_{t}(0)},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

which obviously tensorizes as

(1.18)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)=\prod_{j\in[d]}\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{j})}{\theta_{t}(0)},\qquad\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Formula (
1.17
), in contrast to (
1.14
), is useful
when dealing with large scales, let us say
t\geq 16
. This is because
the the factor
e^{-\pi t}
is very small and the series defining

\Theta_{t}
, see (
1.1
), converges very quickly for these
values of
t
. Therefore in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, as
well when showing various estimates for

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)
, we naturally split our investigations
into two cases of small scales (
t\in(0,16)
), see
Section
4
, and of large scales (
t\in[16,\infty)
),
see Section
5
. Despite the fact that we will
primarily use formula (
1.14
) for small scales and formula
(
1.17
) for large scales, it is worth noting that both formulas
(
1.14
) and (
1.17
) are useful for the whole region

t>0
.
For instance, from (
1.17
) it is not immediately clear
that
|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)|\leq 1,
which is obvious from
(
1.14
). On the other hand (
1.17
) clearly shows that

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)\geq 0,
contrary to (
1.14
).

It should be noted that even though
G_{t}
is not a semigroup, the
Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)
is closely related to the
heat kernel on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. More precisely,

(1.19)

H_{t}(\xi):=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}\Theta_{1/(4\pi t)}(\xi)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\bigg(-\frac{|n-\xi|^{2}}{4t}\bigg),\qquad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

is the heat kernel on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Our estimates for

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}_{t}(\xi)
as outlined in
Propositions
4.2
and
5.1
appear to be new when
considering the dependence on the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. These
estimates also directly lead to what are presumably novel estimates
for the heat kernel
H_{t}(\xi)
. We present the following result, which
will be justified in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For each
t\in(0,2^{-8})
we have

(1.20)

\displaystyle H_{t}(0)\exp\bigg(-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}\bigg)\leq H_{t}(\xi)\leq H_{t}(0)\exp\bigg(-\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{40t}\bigg),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Additionally, for
t\geq 2^{-8}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
we have

(1.21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&H_{t}(0)\exp\bigg(-256\Big(\pi^{3}+1024\pi e^{-\pi/(64)}\Big)e^{-4\pi^{2}t}|\xi|^{2}\bigg)\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq H_{t}(\xi)\leq H_{t}(0)\exp\bigg(-\frac{16}{1+4\sqrt{2}}e^{-4\pi^{2}t}|\xi|^{2}\bigg).\end{split}

We remark that it is straightforward to obtain the above estimates with a multiplicative factor in front of the form
C^{d}H_{t}(0)
for some
C=C(d)>1
. The fact that we are able to replace this factor by
H_{t}(0)
may be of particular interest when the dimension
d
tends to infinity.

2.1.
Basic notation

The set of nonnegative integers and positive integers will be denoted
respectively by
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and

\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\{1,2,\ldots\}
. For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
the sets
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathbb{C}^{d}
and
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
have standard meaning. For
any
x\in\mathbb{R}
we define the floor function

\lfloor x\rfloor:=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\leq x\}
and
the fractional part

\{x\}:=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
as well as the distance to the nearest integer
by
\|x\|:={\rm dist}(x,\mathbb{Z})
. For
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
we shall also write

x\vee y:=\max\{x,y\}
and
x\wedge y:=\min\{x,y\}
.

For
any real number
N>0
we define

[N]:=\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cap(0,N]=\{1,2,\ldots,\lfloor N\rfloor\},

and we will also write

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{\leq N}:=[0,N]\cap\mathbb{N},\ \>\quad

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{N}_{<N}:=[0,N)\cap\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle\mathbb{N}_{\geq N}:=[N,\infty)\cap\mathbb{N},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{N}_{>N}:=(N,\infty)\cap\mathbb{N}.

The letter
d\in\mathbb{N}
is reserved for the dimension throughout this
paper and all implied constants, unless otherwise specified, will be
independent of the dimension. For two nonnegative quantities
A,B

we write
A\lesssim_{\delta}B
if there is an absolute constant

C_{\delta}>0
depending on
\delta>0
such that
A\leq C_{\delta}B
.
We will write
A\simeq_{\delta}B
when

A\lesssim_{\delta}B\lesssim_{\delta}A
. We will omit the subscript

\delta
if irrelevant.

We use
{\mathds{1}_{{A}}}
to denote the indicator function of a set
A
. If
S

is a statement we write
{\mathds{1}_{{S}}}
to denote its indicator, equal to
1

if
S
is true and
0
if
S
is false. For instance
{\mathds{1}_{{x\in A}}}={\mathds{1}_{{A}}}(x)
.

2.2.
Euclidean spaces

The standard inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by

x\cdot\xi:=\sum_{k\in[d]}x_{k}\xi_{k}
for every
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})

and
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The inner product induces
the Euclidean norm
|x|_{2}:=\sqrt{x\cdot x}
, which will be abbreviated
to
|x|
. We will also consider
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the following norms

(2.1)

\displaystyle|x|_{q}:=\begin{cases}\big(\sum_{k\in[d]}|x_{k}|^{q}\big)^{1/q}&\text{ if }\quad q\in[1,\infty),\\
\ \max_{k\in[d]}|x_{k}|&\text{ if }\quad q=\infty.\end{cases}

One of the most classical examples of convex symmetric bodies are the
q
-balls

B^{q}(d)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
q\in[1,\infty]
, defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle B^{q}(d):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|x|_{q}\leq 1\}.

The
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a priori endowed
with the periodic norm

\|\xi\|:=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|\xi_{k}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\qquad\text{for}\qquad\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d})\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

where
\|\xi_{k}\|={\rm dist}(\xi_{k},\mathbb{Z})
for all
\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
and

k\in[d]
. Throughout the paper we will identify
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with

[-1/2,1/2)^{d}
, and we will abbreviate
\|\cdot\|
to
|\cdot|
,
since the norm
\|\cdot\|
coincides with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|

restricted to
[-1/2,1/2)^{d}
.

2.3.
Function spaces and derivatives

All vector spaces in this paper will be
defined over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
. The triple

(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\mu)
is a measure space
X
with
\sigma
-algebra

\mathcal{B}(X)
and
\sigma
-finite measure
\mu
. The space of all
measurable functions whose modulus is integrable with
p
-th power is
denoted by
L^{p}(X)
for
p\in(0,\infty)
, whereas
L^{\infty}(X)

denotes the space of all essentially bounded measurable functions.
The Lorentz space
L^{p,\infty}(X)
(or weak type
L^{p}(X)
space) for
0<p<\infty
is the set of all
measurable functions
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p,\infty}(X)}=\inf\{C>0:\mu(\{x\in X:|f(x)|\geq\lambda\})\leq C^{p}\lambda^{-p}\ \text{ for all }\lambda>0\}<\infty.

The quantity from (
2.3
) defines a seminorm on

L^{p,\infty}(X)
. If
p=\infty
we define

L^{\infty,\infty}(X)=L^{\infty}(X)
. In our case we will usually
have
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
X=\mathbb{T}^{d}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure, and

X=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
endowed with the counting measure. If
X
is endowed with
counting measure we will abbreviate
L^{p}(X)
and
L^{p,\infty}(X)
to

\ell^{p}(X)
and
\ell^{p,\infty}(X)
respectively. We will also
abbreviate
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
to
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}}
.

For
T\colon B_{1}\to B_{2}
, a continuous linear map between two normed
vector spaces
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, we use
\|T\|_{B_{1}\to B_{2}}
to denote its
operator norm. This will be usually used with
B_{1}=B_{2}=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

The partial derivative of a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
with respect to
the
j
-th variable
x_{j}
will be denoted by

\partial_{x_{j}}f=\partial_{j}f
, while the
m
-fold partial derivative
will be denoted by

\partial_{x_{j}}^{m}f=\partial_{j}^{m}f
. Let
U,V\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be open intervals and let
f\colon V\to\mathbb{R}
and
g\colon U\to V
be two smooth functions, we will also use the Faá di
Bruno’s formula for the
n
-th derivative of
f\circ g
, which reads as follows

(2.4)

\displaystyle(f\circ g)^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in S(n)}\frac{n!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{n}!}f^{(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n})}(g(x))\prod_{j\in[n]}\bigg(\frac{g^{(j)}(x)}{j!}\bigg)^{m_{j}},

where
S(n):=\{(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\sum_{j\in[n]}jm_{j}=n\}
.

For any open interval
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and any smooth function

f\colon U\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f^{\prime}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in U
,
we will also use the formula
[
2
, (21)]
for the higher order
derivatives of the inverse function

(2.5)

\displaystyle(f^{-1})^{(n)}\big(f(t)\big)=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(f^{\prime}(t))^{2n-1}}\sum_{s\in T(n)}\frac{(-1)^{s_{1}}(2n-s_{1}-2)!}{s_{2}!\cdot\ldots\cdot s_{n}!}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{f^{(j)}(t)}{j!}\Big)^{s_{j}},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},

where

T(n):=\{s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\sum_{j\in[n]}s_{j}=n-1\,\text{
and }\,\sum_{j\in[n]}js_{j}=2(n-1)\}
. For
n=1
, we understand
that the sum in (
2.5
) is constantly equal to
1
. Further,
for any
n\geq 2
note that
T(n)\neq\emptyset
.

2.4.
Fourier transform

We will write
e(z):=e^{2\pi iz}
for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
The Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
will be denoted respectively by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e(-x\cdot\xi)\ dx,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}^{-1}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(\xi)e(x\cdot\xi)\ d\xi,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Similarly, the Fourier series of
f\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,
and the Fourier coefficient of
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
will be denoted respectively by

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f(n)e(n\cdot\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}^{-1}g(n)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}g(\xi)e(-n\cdot\xi)\,d\xi,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

2.5.
Jumps,
r
-variations and
r
-oscillations

For any
\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
, any family

(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and any exponent

1\leq r<\infty
, the
r
-variation semi-norm is defined to be

\displaystyle V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I}):=\sup_{J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}<\dotsb<t_{J}\\
t_{j}\in\mathbb{I}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}|\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j+1}}-\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j}}|^{r}\Big)^{1/r},

where the latter supremum is taken over all finite increasing
sequences in
\mathbb{I}
. For any
t_{0}\in\mathbb{I}
one has

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{I}}|\mathfrak{a}_{t}|\leq|\mathfrak{a}_{t_{0}}|+V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I}).

The
r
-variation is closely related to the
\lambda
-jump counting
function. Recall that for any
\lambda>0
the
\lambda
-jump counting
function of a family
(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is
defined by

\displaystyle N_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I}):=\sup\{J\in\mathbb{N}:\exists_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{0}<\ldots<t_{J}\\
t_{j}\in\mathbb{I}\end{subarray}}:\min_{0\leq j\leq J-1}|\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j+1}}-\mathfrak{a}_{t_{j}}|\geq\lambda\}.

Let now
(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I})
be a family of measurable functions on a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\mu)
. Let
\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
and
\#\mathbb{I}\geq 2
, then for every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in[1,\infty)
we have

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda N_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})^{1/r}\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq\|V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\|_{L^{p}(X)},

since for all
\lambda>0
we have the following simple pointwise estimate

\lambda N_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I})^{1/r}\leq V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I}),\qquad x\in X.

It is interesting that inequality (
2.7
) can be inversed in the following sense.
Let us fix
p\in[1,\infty]
and
r>2
. Then for every family of measurable functions

(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I})

we have the estimate

(2.8)

\displaystyle\big\|V^{r}\big(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I}\big)\big\|_{L^{p,\infty}(X)}\lesssim_{p,r}\sup_{\lambda>0}\big\|\lambda N_{\lambda}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})^{1/2}\big\|_{L^{p,\infty}(X)}.

A proof of (
2.8
) can be found in
[
17
, Lemma 2.3, p. 805]
. This estimate says that
\lambda
-jumps are an endpoint for
r
-variations.

Let

\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
be such that
\#\mathbb{I}\geq 2
and for
every
J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
define the set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I}):=\big\{(I_{0},\ldots,I_{J})\subseteq\mathbb{I}^{J+1}:I_{0}<I_{1}<\ldots<I_{J}\big\}.

In other
words,
\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I})
is the family of all strictly increasing
sequences of length
J+1

taking their values in the set
\mathbb{I}
.

For any
\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
, any family

(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, any exponent

1\leq r<\infty
, any
J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and a sequence

I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I})
, the
r
-oscillation semi-norm is defined to be

\displaystyle O_{I,J}^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I}):=\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\sup_{t\in[I_{j},I_{j+1})\cap\mathbb{I}}{\lvert{\mathfrak{a}_{t}-\mathfrak{a}_{I_{j}}}\rvert}^{r}\Big)^{1/r}.

Here we use the
convention that the supremum taken over the empty set is zero. Note that
for any
\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
,
any
r\in[1,\infty)
, any family
(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, any
J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and any
I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I})
one has

(2.9)

\displaystyle O_{I,J}^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\leq V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\leq 2\Big(\sum_{t\in\mathbb{I}}|\mathfrak{a}_{t}|^{r}\Big)^{1/r},

the latter object being usually
\infty
if
\mathbb{I}
is uncountable.
Consequently, if now
\mathbb{I}\subseteq(0,\infty)
with
\#\mathbb{I}\geq 2
, and

(\mathfrak{a}_{t}(x):t\in\mathbb{I})
be a family of measurable functions on a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(X,\mathcal{B}(X),\mu)
, then for every
p\in[1,\infty]
and
r\in[1,\infty)
we have

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sup_{J\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\sup_{I\in\mathfrak{S}_{J}(\mathbb{I})}\big\|O_{I,J}^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})^{1/r}\|_{L^{p}(X)}\leq\|V^{r}(\mathfrak{a}_{t}:t\in\mathbb{I})\|_{L^{p}(X)}.

Despite the similarity of (
2.7
) and (
2.10
), it was proved in
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
that
2
-oscillations cannot be interpreted as an endpoint for
r
-variations
when
r>2
, in the sense of inequality (
2.8
). Actually much weaker estimate than an analogue of (
2.8
) fails to be true, see
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
.

For further details related to
\lambda
-jump function,
r
-variation and
r
-oscillation semi-norms we refer the interested reader to a survey article
[
19
]
.

3.
Fourier transform estimates at the origin

We begin with straightforward estimates for the one-dimensional multiplier
\theta_{t}.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
t>0
we have

\max\big(1,t^{-1/2}\big)\leq\theta_{t}(0)\leq 1+t^{-1/2}.

Proof.

A simple comparison shows that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\pi tx^{2}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{t}}.

Using
\theta_{t}(0)=1+2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}
we obtain
the desired upper bound
\theta_{t}(0)\leq 1+t^{-1/2}
.
Further, it is clear that
\theta_{t}(0)\geq 1
. Next, by (
1.16
)
for
d=1
, we also have

\theta_{t}(0)=t^{-1/2}\theta_{1/t}(0)\geq t^{-1/2}
. Combining these two
estimates we obtain the desired lower bound and the proof of
Lemma
3.1
is finished.
∎

For
x>0
we have two simple inequalities

(3.1)

\displaystyle xe^{-x}\leq e^{-x/2},\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\frac{xe^{-x}}{1-e^{-x}}\leq e^{-x/2}.

The main result of this section is the following estimate for

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}
at the origin.

Proposition 3.2
.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
t>0
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)-1\big|\leq 6\pi t|\xi|^{2}.

Further, for any fixed
D>0
, we have a more precise estimate

(3.3)

\displaystyle\big|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)-1\big|\lesssim_{D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2},

which holds uniformly in
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
t\leq D
with
the implicit constant depending only on
D
. Notice that

e^{-\pi/t}\leq(\pi e)^{-1}t
for all
t>0
, thus (
3.3
) implies (
3.2
) for
t\leq D
.

Proof.

Using the formula (
1.14
) for
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}
and symmetry we write

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\prod_{k\in[d]}\cos(2\pi n_{k}\xi_{k}),

so that

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)-1=\frac{1}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\Big[\Big(\prod_{k\in[d]}\cos(2\pi n_{k}\xi_{k})\Big)-1\Big].

Now taking into account the inequalities

\displaystyle\Big|\prod_{k\in[d]}a_{k}-1\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in[d]}|a_{k}-1|,\qquad\text{for }\quad|a_{k}|\leq 1,

\displaystyle|\sin x|

\displaystyle\leq|x|,\qquad\text{for }\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

and the formula
\cos 2x=1-2\sin^{2}x
together with the product structure of
\Theta_{1/t}(0)
, see (
1.1
), we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)-1\right|&\leq\frac{2}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\sum_{k\in[d]}\sin^{2}(\pi n_{k}\xi_{k})\\
&\leq\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\sum_{k\in[d]}n_{k}^{2}\xi_{k}^{2}\\
&=\frac{2\pi^{2}}{\Theta_{1/t}(0)}\sum_{k\in[d]}\xi_{k}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}n_{k}^{2}\exp\big(-\pi|n|^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\\
&=2\pi^{2}|\xi|^{2}\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}j^{2}\exp\big(-\pi j^{2}{t^{-1}}\big).\end{split}

The first inequality from (
3.1
) and Lemma
3.1
imply

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)-1\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi t|\xi|^{2}\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(\pi j^{2}t^{-1})\exp\big(-\pi j^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi t|\xi|^{2}\frac{\theta_{1/(2t)}(0)}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi t|\xi|^{2}\frac{1+(2t)^{1/2}}{\max(1,t^{1/2})}

\displaystyle\leq 6\pi t|\xi|^{2},

which proves (
3.2
). On the other hand, using once again (
3.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}j^{2}\exp\big(-\pi j^{2}{t^{-1}}\big)\leq 2\exp\big(-\pi{t^{-1}}\big)+2\pi^{-1}t\sum_{j\geq 2}\exp\big(-\pi j{t^{-1}}\big)\simeq_{D}\exp\big(-\pi{t^{-1}}\big),\qquad t\leq D.

This combined with (
3.4
) and Lemma
3.1
yields (
3.3
) and
the proof is completed.
∎

4.
Fourier transform estimates at infinity: large scales

Lemma 4.1
.

For
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we have

\theta_{t}(\xi)\leq\theta_{t/8}(0)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big)\leq\big(1+3t^{-1/2}\big)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big).

Proof.

The second inequality follows directly from Lemma
3.1
, thus we
only focus on proving the first inequality. Considering separately

n=0
and
|n|\geq 1
it is easy to see that for
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we
have
|n-\xi|^{2}\geq n^{2}/8+|\xi|^{2}/2
. Indeed, the case
n=0
is trivial and for
|n|\geq 1
we have

|n-\xi|^{2}=|n/2+(n/2-\xi)|^{2}\geq n^{2}/4\geq n^{2}/8+|\xi|^{2}/2,

since
|\xi|\leq 1/2
. Therefore, by definition of
\theta_{t/8}
we have

\displaystyle\theta_{t}(\xi)\leq\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\big(-\pi tn^{2}/8\big)=\theta_{t/8}(0)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big),

which completes the
proof of the Lemma
4.1
.
∎

If we consider the
d
-dimensional multiplier
\Theta_{t}(\xi)
for

\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, then
Lemma
4.1
yields

(4.1)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\leq\frac{\Theta_{t/8}(0)}{\Theta_{t}(0)}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big),\qquad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

If we use the second inequality from Lemma
4.1
, then we can further bound (
4.1
) as follows

(4.2)

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\leq\big(1+3t^{-1/2}\big)^{d}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/2\big),\qquad t>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

This estimate is not sufficient for our purposes because the implied
constant depends on the dimension even when
t>0
is
large, up to
t\simeq d^{2}
. However, we can replace the implied constant in equation (
4.2
)
with
1
whenever
t\geq 15
while still maintaining an exponential
decay. More specifically, we prove the following.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
t\geq 15
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, then we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\leq\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/10\big).

The next lemma will be key to prove Proposition
4.2
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Fix
0<\alpha<1/2
. Then for all
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
such that

|\xi|\leq\alpha
we have

(4.3)

\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}\big)\leq\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}\big)\exp\bigg(\frac{2\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t(1/4-\alpha/2)}}{(1/2-\alpha)^{2}}\bigg).

Further, the lower bound holds true for any
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

Here, it is important that
\theta_{t}(\xi)
is even in
\xi
.
Define
a_{0}=1
and
a_{n}=2
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Then, for
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we have

(4.4)

\theta_{t}(\xi)=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}e^{2\pi tn\xi}\Big)e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\cosh(2\pi tn\xi)\Big)e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}},

so that

(4.5)

\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}=e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}I_{t}(\xi)

with

\displaystyle I_{t}(\xi):=\theta_{t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\cosh(2\pi tn\xi).

Thus it remains to estimate
I_{t}(\xi).

By the formula
\theta_{t}(0)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}a_{n}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}
and since
\theta_{t}(0)\geq 1
, we obtain

0\leq I_{t}(\xi)-1=\theta_{t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}a_{n}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}[\cosh(2\pi tn\xi)-1]\leq 4\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\sinh^{2}(\pi tn\xi),

for
t>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
. In the last inequality above we used the fact that

\cosh(2\pi tn\xi)-1=2\sinh^{2}(\pi tn\xi)
.
This, in particular, shows the first estimate in (
4.3
).

Further, using the inequalities

|\sinh x|\leq|x|\cosh x,\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\cosh x\leq e^{|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

we may conclude, for
|\xi|\leq\alpha
, that

\displaystyle|I_{t}(\xi)-1|

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi^{2}t^{2}\xi^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}n^{2}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}\cosh^{2}(\pi tn\alpha)

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi t\xi^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\pi tn^{2}e^{-\pi tn^{2}}e^{2\pi tn\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq 4\pi t\xi^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\pi tn^{2}e^{-\pi tn^{2}(1-2\alpha)}.

By both the inequalities in (
3.1
)
we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{t}(\xi)-1|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\pi t\xi^{2}}{1-2\alpha}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tn(1/2-\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\pi t\xi^{2}}{1-2\alpha}\frac{e^{-\pi t(1/2-\alpha)}}{1-e^{-\pi t(1/2-\alpha)}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\xi^{2}}{(1/2-\alpha)^{2}}e^{-\pi t(1/4-\alpha/2)}.

By invoking (
4.5
), and using the inequality
1+x\leq e^{x}
for
x\geq 0
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\bigg(1+\frac{2\xi^{2}}{(1/2-\alpha)^{2}}e^{-\pi t(1/4-\alpha/2)}\bigg)\leq e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\exp\bigg(\frac{2\xi^{2}}{(1/2-\alpha)^{2}}e^{-\pi t(1/4-\alpha/2)}\bigg).

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

As a corollary of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.3
we obtain the following useful estimate.

Corollary 4.4
.

For
t\geq 15
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
we have

(4.6)

\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}/10\big).

Specifically, (
4.6
) together with (
1.18
) yield Proposition
4.2
.

Proof.

Consider first
|\xi|\leq 1/4.
Note that for
t\geq 10
we have

\displaystyle\frac{2e^{-\pi t(1/4-1/8)}}{(1/2-1/4)^{2}}=32e^{-\pi t/8}\leq 32e^{-5\pi/4}\leq\frac{9}{10},

so that Lemma
4.3
with
\alpha=1/4
gives

\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}/10\big).

Assume now that
|\xi|\geq 1/4.
Then, for
t\geq 15
an application of Lemma
4.1
gives

\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq\exp\big(1-\pi t\xi^{2}/2\big)\leq\exp\big(16\xi^{2}-\pi t\xi^{2}/2\big)\leq\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}/10\big).

Consequently, the proof of Corollary
4.4
is justified.
∎

The second important ingredient is the following estimate for the derivatives of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\big|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\big|\lesssim_{n}1,

holds uniformly in
t\geq 15
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

We begin with a one-dimensional variant of Proposition
4.5
, which can be stated as follows.

Lemma 4.6
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

(4.7)

\bigg|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\bigg|\lesssim_{n}\big(t^{n}\xi^{2n}+\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/5}\big)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/5\big),

holds uniformly in
t>1
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Now two remarks are in order.

(i)

Estimate (
4.7
) differs from what happens for the derivatives of the Gaussian kernel on
\mathbb{R}.
Namely,

t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\exp(-\pi tx^{2})=(-\pi)^{n}t^{n}x^{2n}\exp(-\pi tx^{2}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

so that we do not have the extra second term as in (
4.7
). This limits the applicability of Lemma
4.6
to only large scales
t
. Small scales will be handled differently in the next section.

(ii)

For
n=0
, Lemma
4.6
gives a better
exponential decay
\exp(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/5)
than in Corollary
4.4
.
The price is that the implicit constant in Lemma
4.6
is not necessairly
1
. For this reason
Corollary
4.4
is vital for obtaining Proposition
4.2
with a constant independent of the dimension.

Proof of Lemma
4.6
.

The proof is technical, so we will proceed in two steps. We fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>1
.

Step 1

Consider first the case when
|\xi|\geq 1/4
. Then

(4.8)

\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\partial_{t}^{k}\theta_{t}(\xi)\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)+\frac{\partial_{t}^{n}\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}.

Moreover,

\partial_{t}^{k}\theta_{t}(\xi)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(-\pi(j-\xi)^{2})^{k}e^{-\pi t(j-\xi)^{2}},

so that using Lemma
4.1
we obtain, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq n}
, that

(4.9)

\big|t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}\theta_{t}(\xi)\big|\lesssim_{n}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi t(j-\xi)^{2}/2}=\theta_{t/2}(\xi)\lesssim\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/4\big).

Additionally, when
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, we have

t^{l}\partial_{t}^{l}[\theta_{t}(0)]=t^{l}\partial_{t}^{l}\big[1+2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}\big]=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(-\pi tj^{2})^{l}e^{-\pi tj^{2}},

and thus we have

(4.10)

t^{l}|\partial_{t}^{l}[\theta_{t}(0)]|\lesssim_{l}e^{-\pi t/2},\qquad t>1,\quad l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}.

Now, using Faá di Bruno’s formula for the
(n-k)
-th derivative

(f\circ g)^{(n-k)}
with
f(x)=1/x
and
g(t)=\theta_{t}(0)
we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum\frac{(n-k)!}{m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n-k}!}f^{(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k})}(\theta_{t}(0))\prod_{j=1}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\partial_{t}^{j}\theta_{t}(0)}{j!}\bigg)^{m_{j}}

\displaystyle=\sum\frac{(n-k)!(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k})!(-1)^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}}}{(m_{1}!\cdot\ldots\cdot m_{n-k}!)(\theta_{t}(0))^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}+1}}\prod_{j=1}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\partial_{t}^{j}\theta_{t}(0)}{j!}\bigg)^{m_{j}},

where the (finite) sums above run over all
(n-k)
-tuples of
non-negative integers
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-k})
satisfying the constraint

\sum_{j=1}^{n-k}jm_{j}=n-k.
In particular, if
k<n,
then some of
the
m_{j}
must be at least
1.
Hence, using
(
4.10
) together with the fact that

\theta_{t}(0)\geq 1
we obtain for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{<n}
the bound

(4.11)

t^{n-k}\left|\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|\lesssim_{n}e^{-\pi t/2},\qquad t>1.

Combining (
4.8
), (
4.9
)
and (
4.11
) for
|\xi|\geq 1/4
we arrive at

\begin{split}\left|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|&=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}t^{k}\partial_{t}^{k}\big(\theta_{t}(\xi)\big)t^{n-k}\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)+\frac{t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\right|\\
&\lesssim_{n}\big(e^{-\pi t/2}+1\big)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/4\big)\\
&\simeq\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/4\big)\\
&\lesssim_{n}t^{n}|\xi|^{2n}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}/4\big),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we used the restrictions
t>1
and
|\xi|\geq 1/4
.

Step 2

It remains to consider the case when
|\xi|\leq 1/4
. Denote

\varphi_{t}(\xi)=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}\big(\cosh(2\pi tj\xi)-1\big).

Then, using (
4.4
) we see that

(4.12)

\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}=e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\theta_{t}(0)^{-1}\big(1+2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}\cosh(2\pi tj\xi)\big)=e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}+1\bigg).

Next, we prove that for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(4.13)

|\partial^{l}_{t}\varphi_{t}(\xi)|\lesssim_{l}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/4},\qquad t>1,\quad|\xi|\leq 1/4.

To prove (
4.13
) we denote

h(x)=\frac{\cosh 2\pi x-1}{(2\pi x)^{2}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

Observe that by a direct computation we obtain

(4.14)

|\partial^{l}_{x}h(x)|\leq(2\pi)^{l}e^{2\pi|x|},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\quad l\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

\varphi_{t}(\xi)=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}h(tj\xi)(2\pi tj\xi)^{2}=8\pi^{2}\xi^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}(tj)^{2}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}h(tj\xi),

so that using the Leibniz rule followed by (
4.14
) we obtain, for
t>1
and
|\xi|\leq 1/4,
that

\displaystyle|\partial^{l}_{t}\varphi_{t}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{l}\xi^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}j^{2l+2}t^{2}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}e^{2\pi tj|\xi|}

\displaystyle\leq\xi^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}j^{2l+2}t^{2}e^{-\pi tj^{2}}e^{\pi tj/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\xi^{2}t^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}j^{2l+2}e^{-\pi tj/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{l}\xi^{2}t^{2}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi tj/3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/4}.

Thus, (
4.13
) is justified. Returning to
(
4.12
) and using the Leibniz rule we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}^{n}(e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}})\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}+1\bigg)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}\partial_{t}^{k}(e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}})\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}+1\bigg)

\displaystyle=(-\pi\xi^{2})^{n}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}+1\bigg)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}{n\choose k}(-\pi\xi^{2})^{k}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg).

Using (
4.13
) with
l=0
we see that

(4.15)

\left|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|\lesssim_{n}t^{n}\xi^{2n}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}+t^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\xi^{2k}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\left|\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|.

Yet another application of the Leibniz rule gives for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{<n}
the formula

\left|\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|=\left|\big(\partial_{t}^{n-k}\varphi_{t}(\xi)\big)\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}+\sum_{l=0}^{n-k-1}{n-k\choose l}\partial_{t}^{l}\big(\varphi_{t}(\xi)\big)\partial_{t}^{n-k-l}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|.

Since in the sum above
n-k-l\geq 1,
using
(
4.11
) and (
4.13
) we
obtain

\left|\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{\varphi_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|\lesssim_{n}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/4}+\xi^{2}e^{-3\pi t/4}\simeq\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/4}.

By this estimate and (
4.15
) we deduce, for
t>1

and
|\xi|\leq 1/4
, that

\displaystyle\left|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|\lesssim_{n}t^{n}\xi^{2n}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}+t^{n}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/4}e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}\lesssim_{n}\big(t^{n}\xi^{2n}+\xi^{2}e^{-\pi t/5}\big)e^{-\pi t\xi^{2}}.

This completes the proof in the case
|\xi|\leq 1/4,
and thus also the proof of Lemma
4.6
.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
4.5
. Actually we shall prove a stronger statement.

Proposition 4.7
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
C_{n}>0
independent of the dimension

d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
such that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\big|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\big|\leq C_{n}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}2^{-n}/10\big),

holds uniformly in
t\geq 15
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

We will prove (
4.16
) proceeding by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For

n=0
inequality (
4.16
) is true thanks to
Proposition
4.2
.
Further, observe that using Lemma
4.6
we get (
4.16
) for
d=1
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Throughout the proof for

\xi^{(k)}:=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k-1},\xi_{k+1},\ldots,\xi_{d})
we let

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi^{(k)}):=\prod_{j\in[d]\setminus\{k\}}\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{j})}{\theta_{t}(0)},

so that, for each
k\in[d]
we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi^{(k)}).

Assume now that inequality (
4.16
) holds for all

j\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq n}
. We now prove that inequality (
4.16
) is true
with
n+1
in place of
n
. Applying the Leibniz rule twice we obtain

(4.17)

\begin{split}t^{n+1}\,\partial_{t}^{n+1}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)&=t^{n+1}\,\partial_{t}^{n}\partial_{t}\bigg(\prod_{k\in[d]}\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)=t^{n+1}\,\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg(\sum_{k\in[d]}\partial_{t}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi^{(k)})\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{k\in[d]}\sum_{j=0}^{n}{n\choose j}\left[t^{j+1}\partial_{t}^{j+1}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right]\left[t^{n-j}\partial_{t}^{n-j}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi^{(k)})\right].\end{split}

By Lemma
4.6
, we have for
t\geq 15
and

\xi_{k}\in\mathbb{T}
, the following bound

\left|t^{j+1}\partial_{t}^{j+1}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right|\lesssim_{j}\big(t^{j+1}|\xi_{k}|^{2(j+1)}+\xi_{k}^{2}e^{-\pi t/5}\big)\exp\big(-\pi t\xi_{k}^{2}/5\big).

By the induction hypothesis in dimension
d-1
with
\xi^{(k)}
in
place of
\xi
applied for
n-j
one has

\big|t^{n-j}\partial_{t}^{n-j}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi^{(k)})\big|\lesssim_{n}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi^{(k)}|^{2}2^{-n}/10\big).

By plugging these two bounds into identity (
4.17
), we obtain that

\left|t^{n+1}\,\partial_{t}^{n+1}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\right|\lesssim_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{k\in[d]}\big(t^{j+1}|\xi_{k}|^{2(j+1)}+\xi_{k}^{2}e^{-\pi t/5}\big)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}2^{-n}/10\big).

Finally, since
\sum_{k\in[d]}|\xi_{k}|^{2(j+1)}\leq(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\xi_{k}^{2})^{(j+1)}
we deduce that the inequality

\big|t^{n+1}\,\partial_{t}^{n+1}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\big|\lesssim_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\big(t^{j+1}|\xi|^{2(j+1)}+|\xi|^{2}\big)\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}2^{-n}/10\big)\lesssim_{n}\exp\big(-\pi t|\xi|^{2}2^{-n-1}/10\big),

holds uniformly in
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\geq 15
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
as
desired, and the proof of Proposition
4.7
follows.
∎

5.
Fourier transform estimates at infinity: small scales

We first prove an analogue of Proposition
4.2
for small scales
t>0
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For every fixed
D>0
there exists a small constant
c_{D}>0
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\leq\exp\big(-c_{D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big),

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

By the product structure of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)
it suffices to prove (
5.1
) for
d=1
.
Using (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) we see that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}=\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big(1-\cos(2\pi j\xi)\big).

Using the facts that
1-\cos 2x=2\sin^{2}x\leq 2x^{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\sin^{2}x\simeq x^{2}
for
x\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
, and the estimate

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}j^{2}\simeq_{D}e^{-\pi/t},\qquad t\leq D,

and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\simeq_{D}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2},\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

This means that there is a small constant
c_{D}>0
such that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\geq c_{D}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2},\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Taking into account the inequality
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for
x\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq 1-c_{D}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\leq\exp\big(-c_{D}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\big),\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

This finishes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Next, we want to show the following important result.

Proposition 5.2
.

For every fixed
D>0
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\big|t^{2n}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\big|\lesssim_{n,D}1,

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

We begin with a one-dimensional variant of Proposition
5.2
, which reads as follows.

Lemma 5.3
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
D>0
fixed we have that

(5.2)

\bigg|\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\bigg|\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\exp\big(-e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\big),

holds
uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
.

Notice that inequality (
5.2
) is not true for
n=0
, it suffices to take
\xi=0
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

Observe first that the factor
\exp(-e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2})
in
(
5.2
) is irrelevant since
e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\leq 1
. However, it is useful to keep this factor in the statement of the lemma for the purpose of proving Proposition
5.4
below. The proof of Lemma
5.3
is technical, so we will proceed in a few steps.

Step 1

By (
1.15
) and (
1.16
), notice that for any

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)=\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{J_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}\bigg),

where

\displaystyle J_{t}(\xi)=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big(\cos(2\pi j\xi)-1\big).

By the Leibniz rule we may write

(5.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\,\bigg(\frac{J_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}\bigg)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\partial_{t}^{k}\big(J_{t}(\xi)\big)\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}\bigg).

In order to prove Lemma
5.3
it suffices to show that for
every
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq n}
we have

(5.4)

\displaystyle\bigg|\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}\bigg)\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2(n-k)}e^{-\pi/t}{\mathds{1}_{{n-k\geq 1}}}+{\mathds{1}_{{n-k=0}}},\qquad t\leq D,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{t}^{k}(J_{t}(\xi))\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2k}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2},\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Indeed, combining these two estimates with (
5.3
) we see that the left hand side of (
5.2
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}t^{-2k}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}\Big(t^{-2(n-k)}e^{-\pi/t}{\mathds{1}_{{n-k\geq 1}}}+{\mathds{1}_{{n-k=0}}}\Big)\simeq_{n,D}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2},

uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
. This proves (
5.2
) and consequently it suffices to justify (
5.4
) and (
5.5
).

Step 2

In order to establish estimate (
5.4
) we first show that for any fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

(5.6)

\big|\partial_{t}^{n}\big(\theta_{1/t}(0)\big)\big|\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t},\qquad t\leq D.

Indeed, we have

(5.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\big(\theta_{1/t}(0)\big)=2\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}\partial_{t}^{n}\big(e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big).

By the Faá di Bruno formula for the
n
-th derivative we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n}\big(e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big)=\sum C_{m,n}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\prod_{k\in[n]}\bigg(\frac{j^{2}}{t^{k+1}}\bigg)^{m_{k}},

where the summation above runs over all
n
-tuples
m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
satisfying the constraint

\sum_{k\in[n]}km_{k}=n
and

C_{m,n}
are some universal constants.
Note that

\displaystyle\prod_{k\in[n]}\bigg(\frac{j^{2}}{t^{k+1}}\bigg)^{m_{k}}\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2n}j^{2n},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\quad t\leq D,

since
\sum_{k\in[n]}m_{k}\leq\sum_{k\in[n]}km_{k}=n
. Consequently, we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{t}^{n}\big(e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big)\big|\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2n}j^{2n}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\quad t\leq D.

This together with (
5.7
) and a simple estimate

x^{n}e^{-x}\lesssim_{n}e^{-x/2}
for
x>0
, implies that

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{t}^{n}\big(\theta_{1/t}(0)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,D}t^{-2n}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}j^{2n}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t}+t^{-n}\sum_{j\geq 2}e^{-\pi j^{2}/(2t)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{D}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t}+t^{-n}e^{-2\pi/t}

\displaystyle\simeq_{n,D}t^{-2n}e^{-\pi/t},

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
. This proves (
5.6
) as desired.

Step 3

Now we are ready to show (
5.4
). The
case
k=n
follows easily from Lemma
3.1
so from now on we
assume that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{<n}
. Applying Faá di Bruno’s formula for
the
(n-k)
-th derivative we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{n-k}\bigg(\frac{1}{\theta_{1/t}(0)}\bigg)=\sum D_{m,n-k}\big(\theta_{1/t}(0)\big)^{-m_{1}-\ldots-m_{n-k}-1}\prod_{l\in[n-k]}\Big(\partial_{t}^{l}\big(\theta_{1/t}(0)\big)\Big)^{m_{l}},

where the summation above runs over all
(n-k)
-tuples

m=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-k})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-k}
satisfying the constraint

\sum_{l\in[n-k]}lm_{l}=n-k
and

D_{m,n-k}
are some universal constants.
Using now Lemma
3.1
and (
5.6
) we obtain
(
5.4
).

Step 4

Here we establish estimate (
5.5
).
By
1-\cos 2x=2\sin^{2}x\leq 2x^{2}
and (
5.8
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{t}^{k}(J_{t}(\xi))\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,D}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}t^{-2k}j^{2k}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}j^{2}\xi^{2}=t^{-2k}\xi^{2}\Big(e^{-\pi/t}+\sum_{j\geq 2}j^{2k+2}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\Big)\simeq_{n,D}t^{-2k}e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2},

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
. This proves (
5.5
) and the proof of Lemma
5.3
is complete.
∎

We are now ready to prove Proposition
5.2
.
Actually we shall prove a stronger statement.

Proposition 5.4
.

For any
D>0
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
two constants
c_{n,D}>0
and
C_{n,D}>0
independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

such that

(5.9)

\displaystyle\big|t^{2n}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{t}}(\xi)\big|\leq C_{n,D}\exp\big(-c_{n,D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big),

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

We will proceed by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}
in a similar way as in the proof of
Proposition
4.7
. Notice that for

n=0
the estimate (
5.9
) follows from
Proposition
5.1
.
Further, using Lemma
5.3
we get (
5.9
) for
d=1
and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.
Assume now that (
5.9
) is true for

n\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq N}
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We show that it also holds for

n=N+1
. Combining (
4.17
) with
Lemma
5.3
and the induction hypothesis we see that

\begin{split}t^{2(N+1)}|\partial_{t}^{N+1}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{t}}(\xi)|&=\Big|\sum_{k\in[d]}\sum_{n=0}^{N}{N\choose n}\left[t^{2(n+1)}\partial_{t}^{n+1}\bigg(\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi_{k})}{\theta_{t}(0)}\bigg)\right]\left[t^{2(N-n)}\partial_{t}^{N-n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{t}}(\xi^{(k)})\right]\Big|\\
&\lesssim_{N,D}\sum_{k\in[d]}\sum_{n=0}^{N}e^{-\pi/t}\xi_{k}^{2}\exp\big(-e^{-\pi/t}\xi_{k}^{2}\big)\exp\big(-c_{N-n,D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi^{(k)}|^{2}\big),\end{split}

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, where

\xi^{(k)}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{k-1},\xi_{k+1},\ldots,\xi_{d})
for

k\in[d]
. Let

c_{N+1,D}:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq N}}{(c_{n,D}\wedge 1)}
and notice
that we can further bound the above expression by

\begin{split}\sum_{k\in[d]}e^{-\pi/t}\xi_{k}^{2}\exp\big(-2c_{N+1,D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big)&=e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\exp\big(-2c_{N+1,D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big)\\
&\lesssim_{N,D}\exp\big(-c_{N+1,D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big),\end{split}

uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This proves Proposition
5.4
.
∎

As a simple consequence of Proposition
5.2
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
\psi\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,1)
be the increasing bijection given by

(5.10)

\psi(t):=e^{-\pi/t},\qquad t>0.

Then for any
c\in(0,1)
and for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that

\big|t^{n}\partial_{t}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{\psi^{-1}(t)}}(\xi)\big|\lesssim_{n,c}1,

holds uniformly in
t\leq c
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

Notice that
\psi^{-1}(u)=-\frac{\pi}{\log u}
for

u\in(0,1)
. Observe that for
n=0
this result follows directly
from Proposition
5.2
. Let us fix

n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
c\in(0,1)
. By Faá di Bruno’s formula (
2.4
) it suffices to
verify that for every tuple

(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
satisfying

m_{1}+2m_{2}+\ldots+nm_{n}=n
we have

\displaystyle\Big|\partial_{s}^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{s}}(\xi)\big|_{s=\psi^{-1}(t)}\Big|\prod_{k\in[n]}\big|(\psi^{-1})^{(k)}(t)\big|^{m_{k}}\lesssim t^{-n},\qquad t\leq c,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

This, in turn, is equivalent to showing that

(5.11)

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{u}^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{u}}(\xi)\big|\prod_{k\in[n]}\big|(\psi^{-1})^{(k)}(\psi(u))\big|^{m_{k}}\lesssim(\psi(u))^{-n},\qquad u\leq\psi^{-1}(c),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Observe that
|\psi^{\prime}(u)|\simeq u^{-2}e^{-\pi/u}
for
u>0
, and for any
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
we have

|\psi^{(j)}(u)|\lesssim_{j,c}u^{-2j}e^{-\pi/u},\qquad u\leq\psi^{-1}(c).

Consequently, combining this with (
2.5
) for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
(with
T(k)
as in (
2.5
)) we obtain

\displaystyle\big|(\psi^{-1})^{(k)}(\psi(u))\big|\lesssim_{k}u^{2(2k-1)}e^{\pi(2k-1)/u}\sum_{s\in T(k)}\Big(\prod_{j\in[k]}u^{-2js_{j}}e^{-\pi s_{j}/u}\Big)\lesssim_{k}u^{2}e^{\pi k/u},\qquad u\leq\psi^{-1}(c).

By Proposition
5.2
(with
D=\psi^{-1}(c)
) we see
that the left-hand side of (
5.11
) is controlled by

\displaystyle\big|\partial_{u}^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{u}}(\xi)\big|\prod_{k\in[n]}\big(u^{2}e^{\pi k/u}\big)^{m_{k}}=u^{2(m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n})}\big|\partial_{u}^{m_{1}+\ldots+m_{n}}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g_{u}}(\xi)\big|e^{\pi n/u}\lesssim_{n}(\psi(u))^{-n},

uniformly in
u\leq\psi^{-1}(c)
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
. This proves (
5.11
) and Corollary
5.5
is justified.
∎

6.
Dimension-free estimate for the difference operator

Let
\{m_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of bounded complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
such that for each
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
the mapping

(0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto m_{t}(\xi)
is smooth. Consider the family of
multiplier operators
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
defined by

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{M_{t}f}(\xi):=m_{t}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{f}(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let

(6.2)

B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{\leq n}}\sup_{s>0}\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\left|s^{j}\partial_{s}^{j}m_{s}(\xi)\right|+\sup_{s>0}\|M_{s}\|_{\ell^{1}\to\ell^{1}}+1.

We added a constant
1
in (
6.2
) only to ensure that
B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\geq 1
. By the definition

B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\leq B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\leq\ldots\leq B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}).

Condition 6.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed.
We say that the family
\{m_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies condition
(B_{n})
, if

B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})<\infty.

The following theorem, which is the main result of this section, will
be crucial to prove Theorem
1.1
for all

p\in(1,\infty)
. In what follows
q>1
is the dual exponent to

p>1
, i.e.
1/p+1/q=1.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of operators as in (
6.1
)
with
\{m_{t}\}_{t>0}

satisfying condition
(B_{n})
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Take
1<p\leq 2

such that
2n/q\leq 1
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,2n/q)
we have

(6.3)

\left\|M_{t+w}-M_{t}\right\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq C(n,p,\alpha)B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\left({w}{t}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha},\qquad w,t>0,

with the constant
C(n,p,\alpha)
depending only on
n,p
and

\alpha
, but independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Remark 6.1
.

Now a few remarks are in order.

1.

An analogous result holds for multiplier operators on

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The proof is essentially the same. It is important to observe
that when dealing with maximal functions for averages over symmetric
convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the interesting case arises when the
multipliers being considered are dilations of a single
multiplier. This is not the case on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

2.

For
p=2
, an application of Plancherel’s theorem gives the following improvement of (
6.3
), i.e.

(6.4)

\left\|M_{t+w}-M_{t}\right\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\leq B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}){w}{t}^{-1},\qquad w,t>0.

3.

Since
M_{t}
is an
\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
bounded convolution operator we have

\|M_{t}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq\|M_{t}\|_{\ell^{1}\to\ell^{1}}
yielding

\left\|M_{t+w}-M_{t}\right\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq 2B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}).

Hence, (
6.3
) is obvious for
w>t/2
, and in the proof of Theorem
6.1
we can assume that
w\leq t/2.

The remainder of this section is devoted to proving
Theorem
6.1
. Inequality (
6.3
) will be
handled by using fractional integration, which we will recall with
simple facts from
[
11
]
.

Fix
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
and let
h(v)=m_{v}(\xi)/v
for

v\in(0,\infty)
. Then by the Leibniz formula and (
6.2
) we have

(6.5)

\left|\partial_{s}^{n}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\right|\lesssim_{n}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\,s^{-n-1},\qquad s>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Notice that
h
is
locally Lipschitz on
(0,\infty)
, i.e. it is Lipschitz on
[t_{0},\infty)
for every
t_{0}>0
.
Thus by
[
11
, Lemma 6.11]
applied with any
t_{0}>0
, for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we have

h(v)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{v}^{\infty}(u-v)^{\alpha-1}D^{\alpha}h(u)\,du,\qquad v>0,

where
D^{\alpha}h
is the fractional derivative given by

(6.6)

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}h(u)=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha}h^{\prime}(s)\,ds.

Note that in the statement of
[
11
, Lemma 6.11]
there is a misprint; it should be
h(t)
in place of
t
on the right-hand side there.
Recalling the definition of
h
we see that

m_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{\infty}t(u-t)^{\alpha-1}D^{\alpha}_{v}\left[\frac{m_{v}(\xi)}{v}\right]\,\bigg|_{v=u}\,du,\qquad t>0,

where
D^{\alpha}_{v}[\ \cdot\ ]|_{v=u}
denotes the
fractional derivative in variable
v
evaluated at
u
. We can further write

\displaystyle m_{t}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{t}{u}\left(1-\frac{t}{u}\right)^{\alpha-1}\left[u^{\alpha+1}D^{\alpha}_{v}\left[\frac{m_{v}(\xi)}{v}\right]\,\bigg|_{v=u}\right]\,\frac{du}{u}=\int_{0}^{\infty}A(t,u)p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)\,du,

with

\displaystyle A(t,u):={\mathds{1}_{{u>t>0}}}\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\frac{t}{u}\Big(1-\frac{t}{u}\Big)^{\alpha-1}\frac{1}{u},\qquad t,u>0.

and the multiplier

p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi):=u^{\alpha+1}D^{\alpha}_{v}\left[\frac{m_{v}(\xi)}{v}\right]\,\bigg|_{v=u},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Observe that thanks to (
6.6
) we have

(6.7)

D^{\alpha}_{v}\left[\frac{m_{v}(\xi)}{v}\right]\,\bigg|_{v=u}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds.

By inequality (
6.5
) and condition
(B_{n})
we obtain that

\displaystyle|p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})u^{\alpha+1}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha}s^{-2}\,ds

\displaystyle=B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\int_{1}^{\infty}(s-1)^{-\alpha}s^{-2}\,ds\simeq_{\alpha}B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}),\qquad u>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

for
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In other words, the multiplier
p_{u}^{\alpha}
is well defined on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and bounded uniformly in
u>0
. Thus
for any fixed
\alpha\in(0,1)
the family of multiplier operators

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P^{\alpha}_{u}f(\xi):=p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{f}(\xi),\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},

is bounded on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
uniformly in
u>0
. In particular, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}A(t,u)\|P_{u}^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}\,du

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\int_{t}^{\infty}A(t,u)\,du

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{-1-\alpha}(u-1)^{\alpha-1}\,du

\displaystyle\simeq_{\alpha}B_{1}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}),

and thus, for each
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
t>0
the formula

(6.8)

\displaystyle M_{t}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}A(t,u)P_{u}^{\alpha}f\,du

makes sense at least for
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
as a Bochner integral.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of operators as in
(
6.1
). Then for any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
and every
f\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cap\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
we
have that

(6.9)

\displaystyle\left\|M_{t+w}f-M_{t}f\right\|_{\ell^{p}}\lesssim_{\alpha}\left(w{t}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha}\sup_{u>0}\big\|P_{u}^{\alpha}f\big\|_{\ell^{p}},

whenever
t>0
and
0<w\leq t/2
.

Proof.

Using representation from (
6.8
) and Minkowski’s integral inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|M_{t+w}f-M_{t}f\right\|_{\ell^{p}}\leq\sup_{u\geq t}\big\|P_{u}^{\alpha}f\big\|_{\ell^{p}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{t,w}(u)\,du,

where
\Delta_{t,w}(u):=|A(t+w,u)-A(t,u)|
for
t>0
and
0<w\leq t/2
. For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
we show that

(6.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\Delta_{t,w}(u)\,du\lesssim_{\alpha}\left(w{t}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha}.

This will imply (
6.9
) as desired. To obtain the last bound, note that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\Delta_{t,w}(u)\lesssim_{\alpha}\begin{cases}t|u-t|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1},&\text{ if }t\leq u\leq t+w,\\
t|u-t-w|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1},&\text{ if }t+w\leq u\leq t+2w,\\
w|u-t|^{\alpha-2}u^{-\alpha},&\text{ if }t+2w\leq u.\end{cases}

Indeed, the estimate in the first region is trivial. For
t+w\leq u\leq t+2w
we have

\Delta_{t,w}(u)\lesssim_{\alpha}t|u-t|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1}+(t+w)|u-t-w|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1}\simeq_{\alpha}t|u-t-w|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1}.

Finally, for
t+2w\leq u
we use the mean value theorem with
\zeta\in[t/u,(t+w)/u]
and we obtain

\begin{split}\Delta_{t,w}(u)&=\left|\frac{t}{u}\left(1-\frac{t}{u}\right)^{\alpha-1}-\frac{t+w}{u}\left(1-\frac{t+w}{u}\right)^{\alpha-1}\right|\frac{1}{u\,\Gamma(\alpha)}\\
&\simeq_{\alpha}wu^{-2}\big(s(1-s)^{\alpha-1}\big)^{\prime}(\zeta)\simeq_{\alpha}wu^{-2}(1-\zeta)^{\alpha-2}\leq wu^{-2}\left(1-\frac{t+w}{u}\right)^{\alpha-2}\\
&=w|u-t-w|^{\alpha-2}u^{-\alpha}\simeq_{\alpha}w|u-t|^{\alpha-2}u^{-\alpha}.\end{split}

This justifies (
6.11
). Then by (
6.11
) the integral from (
6.10
) can be dominated by

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+w}t|u-t|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1}\,du+\int_{t+w}^{t+2w}t|u-t-w|^{\alpha-1}u^{-\alpha-1}\,du+w\int_{t+2w}^{\infty}|u-t|^{\alpha-2}u^{-\alpha}\,du,

which in turn can be dominated by
t^{-\alpha}w^{\alpha}+wt^{-1}\simeq_{\alpha}\left(w{t}^{-1}\right)^{\alpha}
as desired.
∎

By Lemma
6.2
the proof of Theorem
6.1
is reduced to controlling
\sup_{u>0}\|P_{u}^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t>0}
be a family of operators as in (
6.1
)
with
\{m_{t}\}_{t>0}

satisfying condition
(B_{n})
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. Take
1<p\leq 2

such that
2n/q\leq 1
. Then for any
\alpha\in(0,2n/q)
we have

(6.12)

\sup_{u>0}\|P_{u}^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\leq C(n,p,\alpha)B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}),

with the constant
C(n,p,\alpha)
depending only on
n,p
and

\alpha
, but independent of the dimension
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
imply Theorem
6.1
. Thus, from now on we focus on justifying Lemma
6.3
.

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

Assume that condition
(B_{n})
holds for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
. We will
proceed in a few steps for the sake of clarity. By
(
6.7
), the multiplier
p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)

corresponding to
P_{u}^{\alpha}
is equal to

p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)=-\frac{u^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds.

Step 1

The family
\{m_{t}\}_{t>0}
satisfies condition
(B_{n})
, then
integration by parts
n
-times gives

\displaystyle p_{u}^{\alpha}(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\frac{u^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{(s-u)^{-\alpha+1}}{1-\alpha}\right)\partial_{s}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{\alpha+1}}{(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha+1}\partial_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha+1}\partial_{s}^{2}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\ldots=\frac{(-1)^{n}u^{\alpha+1}}{\Gamma(n-\alpha)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-\alpha+n-1}\partial_{s}^{n}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds.

The above formula allows us to define a holomorphic extension of
p_{u}^{\alpha}
given by

(6.13)

p_{u}^{z}(\xi):=\frac{(-1)^{n}u^{z+1}}{\Gamma(n-z)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-z+n-1}\partial_{s}^{n}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d},\quad z\in S,

on the strip
S:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:-1<{\rm Re}\,{z}<n\}
. The fact that
S\ni z\mapsto p_{u}^{z}(\xi)
is holomorphic follows from Morera’s theorem and Fubini’s theorem, since we have (
6.5
).

Step 2

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and let

S_{\varepsilon}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\ \colon-\varepsilon\leq{\rm Re}\,{z}\leq n-\varepsilon\}.

The proof of Lemma
6.3
will be based on Stein’s complex
interpolation theorem (see e.g.
[
12
, Theorem 1.3.7 and Excercise
1.3.4]
) applied to the family of operators

\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}P_{u}^{z}f:=p_{u}^{z}\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{f},\qquad z\in S_{\varepsilon}.

Recall that in Stein’s complex
interpolation theorem any growth of norms smaller than a
constant times the double exponential

\exp(\exp(\gamma|{\rm Im}\,z|/n))
with
0\leq\gamma<\pi
is
admissible. Observe that for
f,g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the function

\langle P_{u}^{z}f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
is holomorphic in the interior of

S_{\varepsilon}
. Indeed, by the Plancherel theorem and Fubini’s
theorem we have

\langle P_{u}^{z}f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}}=\frac{(-1)^{n}u^{z+1}}{\Gamma(n-z)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-z+n-1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\partial_{s}^{n}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{f}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}(\xi)}\,d\xi\,ds.

The application of Fubini’s theorem is allowed by (
6.5
) since
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{f}\overline{\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{g}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for
f,g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.
Using the bound

(6.14)

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-{\rm Re}{z}+n-1}s^{-n-1}\,ds\simeq_{n,\varepsilon}u^{-{\rm Re}{z}-1},\qquad z\in S_{\varepsilon},\quad u>0,

we see that for every
f,g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
z\in S_{\varepsilon}
we have

\left|\langle P_{u}^{z}f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}}\right|\lesssim_{n,\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(n-z)|}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\|f\|_{\ell^{2}}\|g\|_{\ell^{2}}\lesssim_{n,\varepsilon}e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4}C(f,g).

In the last line we have used that
{\rm Re}{(n-z)}\geq\varepsilon
together with the bound

(6.15)

|\Gamma(n-z)|\simeq_{n,\varepsilon}(|{\rm Im}{z}|+1)^{n-{\rm Re}{z}-1/2}e^{-\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/2},\qquad z\in S_{\varepsilon},

which follows from a well-known asymptotic formula (see e.g.
[
23
, eq. 5.11.9]
) asserting

\displaystyle|\Gamma(x+iy)|\simeq_{M,\varepsilon}(|y|+1)^{x-1/2}e^{-\pi|y|/2},\qquad y\in\mathbb{R},\quad\varepsilon<x\leq M.

Concluding, we have proved that for all
f,g\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
the mapping
z\mapsto\langle P_{u}^{z}f,g\rangle_{\ell^{2}}
defines a holomorphic function of admissible growth in the strip
S_{\varepsilon}.

Step 3

We shall prove that

(6.16)

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{z}\|_{\ell^{2}\to\ell^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\varepsilon}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4},\qquad{\rm Re}{z}=n-\varepsilon,

(6.17)

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{z}\|_{\ell^{1}\to\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4},\qquad{\rm Re}{z}=-\varepsilon,

uniformly in
u>0
. Then, applying Stein’s complex interpolation theorem we obtain for each
\theta\in(0,1)
such that
1/p=\theta/2+(1-\theta)/1=1-\theta/2,
the uniform in
u>0
estimate

\left\|P_{u}^{\theta(n-\varepsilon)+(1-\theta)(-\varepsilon)}\right\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\lesssim_{n,\theta,\varepsilon}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}).

Since
\theta(n-\varepsilon)+(1-\theta)(-\varepsilon)=\theta n-\varepsilon
and
\theta=2(p-1)/p=2/q
the above inequality becomes

\|P_{u}^{2n/q-\varepsilon}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\lesssim_{n,p,\varepsilon}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}),\qquad u>0.

Finally, since
\varepsilon
varies over the interval
(0,2n/q)\subseteq(0,1)
, then for each
\alpha\in(0,2n/q)
we can find
\varepsilon\in(0,2n/q)
such that
\alpha=2n/q-\varepsilon
and we have

\|P_{u}^{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}\to\ell^{p}}\lesssim_{n,p,\alpha}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0}),\qquad u>0,

which yields (
6.12
) as desired. We now focus on justifying
(
6.16
) and (
6.17
).

Step 4

We begin with (
6.16
). Using
(
6.13
), (
6.5
), (
6.14
) and
(
6.15
) we obtain, for
{\rm Re}{z}=n-\varepsilon,
that

\displaystyle|p_{u}^{z}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{u^{{\rm Re}{z}+1}}{|\Gamma(n-z)|}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-{\rm Re}{z}+n-1}\left|\partial_{s}^{n}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\frac{u^{{\rm Re}{z}+1}}{|\Gamma(n-z)|}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-{\rm Re}{z}+n-1}s^{-n-1}\,ds

\displaystyle\simeq_{n,\varepsilon}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\frac{1}{|\Gamma(n-z)|}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n,\varepsilon}B_{n}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4},

uniformly in
u>0
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.
This estimate together with
Plancherel’s theorem gives (
6.16
).

Step 5

It remains to prove (
6.17
). Since

{\rm Re}{z}=-\varepsilon<0
, then integration by parts
(n+1)
-times gives

p_{u}^{z}(\xi)=-\frac{u^{z+1}}{\Gamma(1-z)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-z}\partial_{s}\left(\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\right)\,ds=-\frac{u^{z+1}z}{\Gamma(1-z)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-z-1}\frac{m_{s}(\xi)}{s}\,ds.

Moreover, using (
6.5
) and (
6.15
) it is
easy to see that
p_{u}^{z}
is a bounded function of
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}.

Therefore, for
{\rm Re}{z}=-\varepsilon
we have

P_{u}^{z}f=-\frac{zu^{z+1}}{\Gamma(1-z)}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{-z-1}M_{s}f\,\frac{ds}{s},\qquad u>0,

where the right-hand side is a convergent Bochner integral in

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).
Thus, for
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\cap\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

using again (
6.5
) and (
6.15
) together
with the condition
(B_{n})
we obtain, for
u>0
, that

\displaystyle\|P_{u}^{z}f\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})\frac{|z|u^{1-\varepsilon}}{|\Gamma(1-z)|}\int_{u}^{\infty}(s-u)^{\varepsilon-1}\,\frac{ds}{s}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\varepsilon}B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4}\int_{1}^{\infty}(s-1)^{\varepsilon-1}\,\frac{ds}{s}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}

\displaystyle\simeq_{\varepsilon}B_{0}(\{m_{s}\}_{s>0})e^{3\pi|{\rm Im}{z}|/4}\|f\|_{\ell^{1}}.

This yields (
6.17
) and completes the proof of Lemma
6.3
.
∎

We close this section by proving the following useful Corollary that
follows from Theorem

.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let

be a family of operators as in (

)
with

satisfying condition

for all

with the constant

depending only on

and

\alpha

.

Proof.

Fix

and

If

then (

If

. Then, the

Interpolating the latter bound with (

This completes the proof.

Appendix A

To show Proposition
1.1
, we need to prove a slight improvement of Proposition
5.1
with explicit constants.

Proposition A.1
.

For every fixed
D>0
there are constants

\displaystyle c_{D}=\frac{16}{1+\sqrt{D}},\qquad C_{D}=\max\bigg\{8\pi^{2}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big),8\pi^{3}D\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)\bigg\}

such that

(A.1)

\displaystyle\exp\big(-C_{D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big)\leq\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)\leq\exp\big(-c_{D}e^{-\pi/t}|\xi|^{2}\big),

holds uniformly in
t\leq D
and
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Proof.

By the product structure of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}g_{t}(\xi)
it suffices to prove (
A.1
) for
d=1
.
Using (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) we see that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}=\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}\big(1-\cos(2\pi j\xi)\big).

Using the facts that

\displaystyle 1-\cos 2x=2\sin^{2}x\leq 2x^{2},\quad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}$},\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{4x^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\leq\sin^{2}x\leq x^{2},\quad\text{for $x\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$},

we obtain

\displaystyle\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}\frac{16}{\pi^{2}}\pi^{2}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\leq 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq\theta_{1/t}(0)^{-1}4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}\Big(e^{-\pi/t}+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}j^{2}\Big).

Further, using (
3.1
) for
t>0
we may write

(A.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}e^{-\pi j^{2}/t}j^{2}\leq\frac{t}{\pi}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}e^{-\pi j^{2}/(2t)}\leq\frac{t}{\pi}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}e^{-\pi j/t}\leq\frac{t}{\pi}e^{-2\pi/t}\frac{1}{1-e^{-\pi/t}}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{\pi^{2}}e^{-\pi/t}e^{-\pi/(2t)}.

Applying Lemma
3.1
, we have
1\leq\theta_{1/t}(0)\leq 1+\sqrt{D}
for
t\leq D
. This together with (
A.2
) implies

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{16e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}}{1+\sqrt{D}}\leq 1-\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq 4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big),\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Taking into account the inequality
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for
x\geq 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\leq 1-\frac{16e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}}{1+\sqrt{D}}\leq\exp\Big(-\frac{16e^{-\pi/t}\xi^{2}}{1+\sqrt{D}}\Big),\qquad t\leq D,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Finally, we show the lower bound in (
A.1
).
Here we distinguish two cases.

Case 1:

4\pi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)\leq 1/2
. Then using the inequality
1-x\geq e^{-2x}
for
x\in[0,1/2]
, and (
A.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}

\displaystyle\geq 1-4\pi^{2}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\exp\bigg(-8\pi^{2}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)\bigg).

This gives the lower bound in (
A.1
) in Case 1.

Case 2:

4\pi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)\geq 1/2
. This case is nonempty if there is
T_{0}\leq D
such that
4\pi^{2}e^{-\pi/T_{0}}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)=1/2
. Then our aim is to show the lower bound in (
A.1
) for
t\in[T_{0},D]
. Here we proceed in a different way than in Case 1. We use Lemma
4.3
and we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\theta_{t}(\xi)}{\theta_{t}(0)}\geq\exp\big(-\pi t\xi^{2}\big)\geq\exp\bigg(-8\pi^{3}\xi^{2}e^{-\pi/t}\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)\bigg),\qquad t\in[T_{0},D],\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

The latter inequality is true because we have

\sup_{t\in[T_{0},D]}\pi te^{\pi/t}\leq\pi De^{\pi/T_{0}}=8\pi^{3}D\Big(1+\frac{D^{2}e^{-\pi/(2D)}}{\pi^{2}}\Big)
.
This gives the lower bound in (
A.1
) in Case 2.
This finishes the proof of Proposition
A.1
.
∎

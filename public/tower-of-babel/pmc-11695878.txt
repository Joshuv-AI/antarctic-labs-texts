Study of the Role of C-reactive Protein/Procalcitonin Ratio as a Prognostic Tool in ICU Patients with Sepsis: A Prospective Observational Study
Authors: Eman M Abdellatif, Emad H Hamouda
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPrediction of prognosis in sepsis is an essential research area aiming to improve disease outcomes. In this study, we investigated the role of the C-reactive protein (CRP)/procalcitonin (PCT) ratio as a prognostic tool in sepsis patients.Materials and methodsThis prospective observational study was conducted at the intensive care unit (ICU) of Alexandria Main University Hospital in the period from January to June 2024. One hundred and seventy patients with a diagnosis of sepsis were enrolled. Sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score, acute physiology and chronic health evaluation (APACHEII) score and CRP/PCT ratio were calculated on admission (day 1), and as a follow-up on day 3. Patients were subsequently divided into survivor and non-survivor groups, and the data were compared.ResultsThe CRP/PCT ratio was significantly lower, on admission and on follow-up, in non-survivor patients than in survivor patients. The ratio median (minimum–maximum) in non-survivors was 4.82 (1.51–23.28) vs 11.23 (1.85–136.7) in survivors on admission, and it was 7.37 (2.27–26.36) in non-survivors vs 11.37 (2.78–110.9) in survivors on day 3. The ratio was significantly lower in patients with septic shock than in non-septic shock patients. The ratio had a significant negative correlation with both SOFA and APACHEII scores. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve showed high accuracy of the day 1 CRP/PCT ratio to predict mortality [area under curve (AUC = 0.835)], which is comparable to the day 1 SOFA score (AUC = 0.878) and higher than the day 1 PCT and day 1 APACHE scores.ConclusionOur results suggest a potential role for the CRP/PCT ratio, on admission and on follow-up, as a marker for predicting prognosis in sepsis patients, where low ratio values can predict poor disease outcome.How to cite this articleAbdellatif EM, Hamouda EH. Highlights

Procalcitonin (PCT) and CRP are commonly utilized as indicators of infection in sepsis patients. Our results have shown that a low C-reactive protein (CRP)/PCT ratio could predict a poor prognosis in these patients. The area under the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of this ratio was comparable to the SOFA score and higher than that of PCT and CRP alone.

Introduction

Sepsis is one of the most common causes of intensive care unit (ICU) admission, and the frequency of this serious condition is continuously rising.1 There were 48.9 million cases of sepsis and 11 million sepsis-related deaths worldwide in 2020.2 According to the third international consensus definition, sepsis is a potentially fatal illness characterized by organ failure occurring due to an improper body reaction towards infection. A more severe clinical subtype, septic shock, is recognized by an essential need for the use of vasopressors to raise the mean arterial blood pressure (MAP) to 65 mm Hg or more, in spite of proper volume resuscitation, in addition to an elevation in serum lactic acid levels more than 2 mmol/L.3 Physicians have long-faced difficulties in the diagnosis and management of sepsis, particularly in critical care contexts, and the diagnosis of sepsis is frequently delayed.4 Early diagnosis and prediction of prognosis are of great importance to these patients in order to improve the clinical outcome and decrease mortality and financial burden.5–7

Initial assessment and follow-up of the patient's prognostic data helps to identify high-risk patients who need alternative management approaches. Early prognostic stratification also helps in identifying low-risk cases suitable for antibiotic de-escalation or ICU discharge.8

Multiple severity scores and several prognostic biomarkers have been utilized for proper antibiotic administration, patient monitoring, risk stratification and early detection of organ failure.9–11 Although, SOFA scoring system is considered a predictor of sepsis outcome, it does not have perfect specificity for sepsis.12,13 Using acute physiology and chronic health evaluation (APACHEII) scoring in sepsis patients, prognostication also has several limitations.14

Reliable prognostic biomarkers for prediction of morbidity and mortality of this critical condition are still needed.15 This biomarker ought to be accessible in a timely and economical manner, and its usefulness is increased if it can provide insight into the disease severity, monitor disease progression and indicate the treatment effectiveness.15,16

Proposed sepsis biomarkers have included total leukocytic count, CRP, PCT, various interleukins (ILs), adrenomedullin (ADM) and pro-ADM, interferon-γ (IFN-γ), copeptin, presepsin, resistin, pro-atrial natriuretic peptide (pro-ANP) and ANP.16,17 Among the most promising biomarkers, it has been reported that PCT is an accurate marker for different types of infection: bacterial, fungal and protozoal infection.18 It is rapidly produced, after the onset of infection, in the liver, lung, kidney and other tissues. Its level markedly increases as synthesis is stimulated by bacterial toxins and circulating cytokines.19 However, using PCT test to aid in the diagnosis and prognosis of bacterial infection and sepsis is limited by its imperfect sensitivity and specificity.20–22 C-reactive protein is an acute-phase reactant protein that can be markedly increased as a reaction to infection or any severe tissue damage. Both CRP and PCT are commonly measured in sepsis patients as diagnostic markers of infection, although limited by imperfect specificity.13,23,24 Their predictive power for severity and mortality is still controversial.25 Research work studying the prognostic significance of CRP and PCT in sepsis patients yielded different results.25–28

Recently, the ratio between CRP and PCT has been studied in several infectious conditions.18,29–32 In febrile patients with solid tumors and patients with hematological malignancies, it has been shown that the CRP/PCT ratio improved the specificity of PCT and provided a reliable means of ruling out infection as a cause of fever in these patients.18,29 In neonatal sepsis, the ratio was reported to be beneficial in differentiating suspected from proven late onset neonatal sepsis.30

As a prognostic indicator, Cho et al. studied the PCT/CRP ratio in ischemic stroke patients, and found that it was correlated with mortality in the studied subjects.33

These earlier research data have reported better specificity of the ratio between CRP and PCT, compared to CRP or PCT alone, in the diagnosis of infection. In the present work, our aim was to evaluate the role of the CRP/PCT ratio in comparison with PCT alone as a prognostic tool for sepsis in the ICU patients.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Study Population

The present study was reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Medicine, Alexandria University (IRB 00012098, FWA 00018699, serial number 0306489). This prospective observational research work was carried out at the intensive care department of Alexandria Main University Hospital in the period from January to June 2024. During this period, all ICU patients who fulfilled the diagnostic requirements of sepsis and septic shock (based on sepsis-3), were requested to take part in the study.3 Written consent was given by all subjects or their guardians before their involvement in the current study. The present work was performed following the ethical guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki.

One hundred and seventy-five sepsis patients were enrolled in the period from January to June 2024. Three patients died, and two patients were discharged from the ICU before day 3 sample collection. Therefore, these five patients were excluded from the study. Finally, one hundred and seventy patients with a diagnosis of sepsis were enrolled. Patients included 112 sepsis patients without septic shock and 58 septic shock patients. Following sepsis-3 requirements, sepsis was diagnosed as a suspected or documented infection plus sequential organ failure assessment (SOFA) score ≥2. Septic shock was diagnosed as sepsis with the development of shock needing vasopressor therapy to maintain MAP ≥65 mm Hg, in addition to serum lactate ≥2 mmol/L in the presence of sufficient fluid replacement therapy.3 Exclusion criteria were: patients ≤18 years, severe or prolonged cardiogenic shock, patients with severe burns, immunocompromised patients because of diseases (like malignancy) or being on immunosuppressive medications. All included patients were observed from the date of admission to the date of discharge or death (30-day hospital mortality). In addition, the length of ICU stay (LOS) was documented.

Methods and Measurements

All patients had thorough history taking (covering demographic details and relevant comorbidities), assessment of vital signs and full clinical examination. Sequential organ failure assessment score and APACHEII score, were applied for all patients on admission (Day 1 scores) and 72 hours later (Day 3 scores).34,35 Microbiological studies were performed according to clinical suspicion and before antibiotic administration.

Laboratory investigations included, complete blood count (CBC), blood lactate, liver function tests, and renal function tests. PCT measurement and CRP testing were performed for all cases on admission, then daily or every alternate day (as per requirement). The results of day 1 (on admission) and day 3 samples were included in our study. CRP was measured on an ADVIA 1800 chemistry analyzer (Siemens Laboratory Diagnostics, Germany). The principle of the assay is a latex-enhanced immunoturbidimetric technique using latex particles coated with specific anti-CRP antibodies that rapidly agglutinate in the presence of C‑reactive protein. Procalcitonin was measured on the ADVIA Centaur XP Immunoassay System (Siemens Laboratory Diagnostics, Germany). The assay principle is sandwich immunoassay direct chemiluminescent technology that uses monoclonal antibodies specific for PCT.

CRP/PCT Ratio Calculation

Day 1 and day 3 sample results, for CRP and PCT, were used for ratio calculation. CRP/PCT ratio (expressed in μg/ng) was calculated by dividing CRP result (mg/L) by PCT result (ng/mL). Ratio calculation was performed on admission (day 1 ratio) and repeated after 72 hours (day 3 ratio). The change in the ratio (delta CRP/PCT) was calculated as: day 3 CRP/PCT ratio–day 1 CRP/PCT ratio (D3 CRP/PCT ratio–D1 CRP/PCT ratio). Sample Size

The study sample size was measured utilizing the power analysis and sample size software (PASS 2020), “NCSS, LLC. Kaysville, Utah, USA”. The smallest required sample size to study the role of CRP/PCT ratio as a prognostic tool in ICU patients with sepsis, is 40 ICU patients with a diagnosis of sepsis; considering a 95% confidence level, an 18% assumed effect size, and 5% precision using the Z test.36,37

Statistical Analysis

Collected data were statistically analyzed employing the IBM SPSS software (Armonk, NY: IBM Corp), package version 20.0. We used Chi-square test to compare between qualitative data in the two groups. Quantitative data were tested for normality by the Kolmogorov–Smirnov and Shapiro–Wilk test. If normally distributed, the Student t-test was used to compare data between the two groups (survivors and non-survivors). Mann–Whitney test was applied for comparing non-parametric data. Regarding correlation studies, person coefficient test was utilized for studying the correlation between quantitative parameters with a normal distribution. Multivariate analysis was used for the CRP/PCT ratio to predict mortality, adjusted by different parameters. A receiver operating characteristic curve was used to determine the ability of the studied parameters to predict mortality, where an area greater than 0.5 refers to a satisfactory performance of the studied parameter. Significance of the obtained results was judged at the 5% level.

Results

The current study enrolled 170 patients with a diagnosis of sepsis (aged 43–78 years, including 83 males and 87 females). The subjects included sepsis patients without shock (112 patients) and septic shock patients (58 patients). On follow-up, study subjects were categorized into two groups: the non-survivor group (39 patients died) and the survivor group (131 patients survived) (Fig. 1).

Correlation between CRP/PCT Ratio and Other Prognostic Parameters

Correlation between CRP/PCT ratio and other prognostic parameters showed that the D1 ratio had a significant negative correlation with both the D1 SOFA score and D1 APACHEII score (p < 0.001, p = 0.006, respectively). On follow-up, D3 ratio had a significant negative correlation with the follow-up SOFA score, and follow-up APACHEII score (p < 0.001, p = 0.017, respectively), as illustrated in Figures 3 and 4. In addition, lower values of the D1 ratio were significantly associated with the presence of septic shock on admission (p < 0.001) (Fig. 5).

Determining the Prognostic Ability of Different Studied Parameters

Receiver operating characteristic curve was used to study the ability of day 1 parameters (CRP/PCT ratio, CRP, PCT, SOFA score and APACHEII score) to predict 30-day hospital mortality from sepsis, as shown in Table 5 and Figure 6. The area under curve (AUC) values showed high accuracy of the D1 CRP/PCT ratio (AUC = 0.835), which is comparable to the D1 SOFA score (AUC = 0.878) and higher than the D1 PCT (0.781), D1CRP (0.550) and D1 APACHEII score (0.653). The highest AUC (0.912) was achieved using a combination of the D1 CRP/PCT ratio, and D1 SOFA score. From the curve, the best value of the D1 CRP/PCT ratio to predict mortality was ≤9.33 μg/ng, with a sensitivity of 89.74% and a specificity of 64.12%.

Discussion

The current prospective cohort study included 170 patients with a diagnosis of sepsis according to sepsis-3 criteria. We documented a mortality rate of 22.9% among the study subjects, which is similar to the mortality rates in other studies conducted on sepsis patients.38,39

In order to study the prognostic significance of different clinical and laboratory parameters, the study patients were divided into survivor versus non-survivor groups, and data were compared. We found that the D1 SOFA score, D1 APACHEII score, D1 PCT, and lactate were significantly higher in non-survivor patients.

Regarding CRP and PCT, our results showed that CRP values were not significantly different between both groups on admission, although PCT values were higher in non-survivor patients. Several studies investigating CRP and PCT as prognostic markers in sepsis patients reported different results.25–28 Comparing with previous research work, Koh et al. found both CRP and PCT not significantly different when comparing survivors versus non-survivors.40 Hu et al. and Li et al. found both CRP and PCT on admission significantly higher in non-survivor patients.38,41 Difference in sample size and difference in the main source of infection causing sepsis, may explain such variation in these results.

Studying the relation between both parameters (CRP and PCT) in the current work, showed that the CRP/PCT ratio on admission and on follow-up was significantly lower in non-survivor patients. In addition, the magnitude of change in this calculated ratio was significantly greater in the non-survivor group of patients in day 3 follow-up samples. Both findings reflect the prognostic impact of this ratio, where lower ratio values were associated with poor prognosis. This finding was confirmed by the significant correlation observed between the lower CRP/PCT ratio and the severity parameters, including higher SOFA score, a higher APACHE score, and the presence of septic shock.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work studying the prognostic significance of the CRP/PCT ratio in adult patients with sepsis, which makes it difficult to compare this finding with previous work. However, the ratio between CRP and PCT has been investigated as a marker of infection in a number of studies.18,29,30 The common conclusion found in these studies was that the lower the CRP/PCT ratio (or higher the PCT/CRP ratio), the higher the risk of infection.18,29,30

Vassallo et al. studied CRP/PCT ratio in the diagnosis of infection in febrile cancer patients.18 They observed lower ratio values in cases of infection compared to tumor associated fever. The combination between this ratio and PCT increased the specificity of PCT in discriminating infection related fever from cancer associated fever.18 Hangai et al. studied PCT/CRP ratio in patients with lymphoid malignancies having fever due to tumor or due to infection. The study reported that in comparison with CRP or PCT alone, this ratio had the best ability to differentiate both causes of fever and to rule out infection.29 The ratio between CRP and PCT was also studied as a prognostic marker for predicting mortality in stroke. Cho et al. retrospectively analyzed the data of a group of ischemic stroke patients. The study found that PCT/CRP ratio was related to poor prognosis in the study subjects, and it was a predictor of short-term mortality in the included stroke patients.33

Previous work by Vassallo et al. on febrile cancer patients, and previous research by Hangai et al., have reported higher specificity of the ratio, in comparison to PCT alone, to diagnose infection in the setting of malignancy.18,29 In our results, it was shown that CRP/PCT ratio had a higher specificity compared to PCT (64.12% vs 53.5%, respectively), as a predictor of 30-day hospital mortality in the included sepsis patients.

Other findings in our study were in agreement with earlier research work studying different prognostic parameters in sepsis patients. One of these studies is the one conducted by Pham et al. enrolling 86 sepsis patients, which showed significantly higher SOFA score, APACHE score and lactate in non-surviving sepsis patients. 15 In addition, Hu et al. who performed their work on 141 sepsis patients, showed the same findings of a higher SOFA score, APACHE score and lactate in the non-survivor group.41 Similarly, Lee et al. showed the same findings in their study.26 Koh et al., who performed their study on 153 sepsis patients, also showed a higher SOFA score and lactate in non-survivor patients, however, APACHE score was not significantly different between survivors and non-survivors.40 White blood cells count in our study was significantly higher in non survivors, which is similar to other studies. Hu et al. and Li et al. had the same findings in their studies.38,41 However, the study by Pham et al. showed a significantly lower WBCs count in non-survivor sepsis patients. This can be attributed to the difference in sample size, which was smaller in that study.15

Previous studies on sepsis patients have analyzed ROC curve to study the ability of different prognostic parameters to predict mortality in these patients. Hu et al. showed higher accuracy of SOFA score (AUC = 0.85) than APACHE score (AUC = 0.8) and both were superior to PCT (AUC = 0.73).41 Li et al. showed higher accuracy of APACHE score (AUC = 0.85) than SOFA score (AUC = 0.79), but both scores were superior to PCT (AUC = 0.76).38 Similarly in our study, the prognostic ability of PCT alone (AUC = 0.781) was inferior to that of SOFA score (0.878). However, the prognostic ability of CRP/PCT ratio on admission (AUC = 0.835) was higher than PCT alone, and it was closer to that of SOFA score. In addition, using the studied ratio (day 1 CRP/PCT ratio) in combination with day 1 SOFA score, exhibited the best prognostic accuracy, with an AUC of 0.912.

Conclusion

The data in our study suggest a potential role for CRP/PCT ratio, on admission and on follow-up, as a marker for predicting prognosis in sepsis patients. The ratio was significantly correlated with sepsis severity scores (SOFA score and APACHEII score), presence of septic shock and 30-day hospital mortality. The prognostic accuracy of this ratio was comparable to SOFA score and better than PCT and CRP alone. CRP/PCT ratio could represent a simple method to maximize the benefit of already available tests (CRP and PCT). Larger-scale multi center studies are required in order to confirm these findings.

Clinical Significance

Our results have shown that CRP/PCT ratio represents a rapid and simple method to predict disease outcome in sepsis patients. Lower values of this ratio are associated with higher severity scores and poor prognosis.

Limitations of the Study

The results of microbiological cultures were not available for all study patients. Therefore, results of CRP/PCT ratio in culture-negative versus culture-positive subjects could not be investigated.

Significance of adjuvant external beam radiotherapy of the orbit in managing uveal melanoma with extraocular extension
Authors: Nathalie Christina Düsberg, Norbert Bornfeld, Anja Merkel-Jens, Philipp Rating, Michael Zeschnigk, Wolfgang A C Sauerwein, Martin Stuschke, Maja Guberina, Nikolaos E Bechrakis, Eva Biewald
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveExtraocular extension (EOE) is a known risk factor for metastasis in uveal melanoma. This long-term study investigates neoadjuvant and adjuvant external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) before and after enucleation of eyes with uveal melanoma and EOE with respect to survival, local tumour control, metastatic disease and side effects of radiotherapy.Methods and analysisRetrospective, consecutive analysis of all patients with enucleation for uveal melanoma and histopathological evidence of EOE from June 1980 to April 2014.ResultsThe entire cohort comprised 87 patients. 34 of those were lost to follow-up. Of the remaining 43 patients, 26 underwent adjuvant and/or neoadjuvant radiotherapy with a total orbital dose of 50–60 Gy, while the remaining 17 were treated with enucleation only. The mean follow-up time was 65.58±80.44 months (median 37 months) and 184.12±119.760 months (median 132 months) for those being alive at last follow-up. EBRT patients had significantly higher TNM (tumour, node, metastases) stages (p=0.033) and more extensive EOE (p=0.146). There was no significant difference in any relevant parameters between the two groups. The overall survival rate after 5 and 10 years was 32.6% and 16.2%, respectively. There was no evidence of a significantly later (p=0.573) or less frequent (p=0.999) occurrence of metastases in either group. There were no local recurrences in either group. Side effects of the radiotherapy occurred in 61.5%.ConclusionsAdjuvant or neoadjuvant EBRT had no survival benefit in uveal melanoma with EOE. No local recurrence was detected in either group.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Uveal melanoma with extraocular extension (EOE) is a disease with a high mortality rate for which there are no standardised treatment recommendations. Adjuvant and/or neoadjuvant external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) of the orbit is widely used to reduce the risk of distant metastasis and local recurrence after enucleation of a melanoma containing eye with EOE. The benefits of this treatment, however, have not been clearly demonstrated to date.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

In our study (based on the longest follow-up period published to date, with a mean follow-up duration of more than 5 years), no local recurrences were observed. Orbital recurrences also did not occur in patients with extensive EOE who had received EBRT. There was no survival advantage or reduction in the progression of metastasis between irradiated and non-irradiated patients. However, adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with increased morbidity, particularly in the early treatment phase.

Introduction

Uveal melanoma is the most common primary intraocular tumour in adults.1 Chromosomal aberrations, particularly monosomy 3, gain of chromosome 8q, loss of chromosome 1p-/6p-/8p, have the greatest influence on the incidence of metastases. Additional histopathological risk factors (epithelioid cell type, high mitotic activity (PC-10 (Proliferating Cell Nuclear Antigen Clone 10= an immunohistochemical proliferation marker)/Ki-67 (Kiel-67= a proliferation marker discovered in Kiel in 1983 where the number “67” refers to the number of the laboratory clone used in the experiment)), microvascular loops, tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes and macrophages, high expression of the Insuline-like-Growth-Factor-receptor (IGF-1 receptor)) and clinical risk factors (age, involvement of the ciliary body, tumour size, ocular melanocytosis, extraocular extension (EOE)) have been identified.2 EOE is reported in 2–6% of all eyes with uveal melanoma and is usually correlated with advanced tumour stage and higher malignancy.3 EOE occurs in approximately 13% of patients for whom enucleation was the only treatment option. Past laboratory and animal studies suggested that irradiation of the orbit before or after enucleation may reduce the risk of metastasis in patients with EOE by devitalising the cells before enucleation, thereby preventing the spread of viable tumour cells, or by killing the remaining cells after surgery. Although larger studies were lacking, it was therefore common practice in most centres to perform adjuvant or neoadjuvant radiotherapy of the orbital socket if EOE was present at the time of enucleation. To date, only two controlled studies with a short follow-up period have been published on this topic, with no evidence of improved survival rates for these patients.4 5 This study was therefore conducted to obtain long-term data to evaluate the potential benefits of external beam radiotherapy (EBRT) in the treatment of patients with uveal melanoma and EOE.

Material and methods

In a retrospective study the medical records of all patients who underwent enucleation for uveal melanoma with extrascleral extension at the Department of Ophthalmology at the University of Essen between June 1980 and April 2014 were reviewed. 78 patients met the criteria. Their patient records were reviewed for demographic information, histopathological findings including the size of extraocular spread, cytogenetic results, tumour, node, metastases (TNM) stage and follow-up, information on adjuvant or neoadjuvant radiotherapy, occurrence, timing and localisation of metastases, time and cause of death and documented complications of EBRT. If this data were incomplete, information was obtained by contacting patients, family members, patients’ general practitioners and ophthalmologists, as well as the Institute of Medical Informatics, Biometry and Epidemiology to determine the vital status of patients. Cause of death and death certificates were requested from the health authorities. 35 patients were lost to follow-up, or the requested information was not available. The remaining 43 patients were followed-up until death or until January 2023.

Statistical analysis of the collected data was carried out using SSPS V.29.0.0.0 (SPSS, USA). All metric variables were assessed for normality using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov and Shapiro-Wilk tests. As the data were predominantly not normally distributed (Kolmogorov-Smirnov test: p<0.05), non-parametric tests for non-normally distributed samples were used when comparing the samples. When comparing two independent, non-normally distributed samples, the Mann-Whitney U test was performed. Pearson’s χ2 test and Fisher’s exact test were used to compare categorical variables. Kaplan-Meier survival curves were used to analyse all-cause and disease-related mortality. A p value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Demographics, tumour characteristics and survival

A total of 87 patients underwent enucleation for uveal melanoma with extraocular extension at the Institute of Ophthalmology of the University Hospital Essen between June 1980 and April 2014. 35 patients were lost to follow-up and excluded from the study. In nine cases it was not possible to determine whether the patients had received adjuvant or neoadjuvant radiotherapy of the orbit and these patients therefore had to be excluded as well.

Of the remaining 43 patients, 17 patients were treated with enucleation only, while 26 patients received radiotherapy to the orbit before (1 patient), after (18 patients) or combined before and after enucleation (7 patients). The total dose administered was 50–60 Gy in all patients in 4–5 weekly fractions of 2 Gy in 16 cases, 2.5 Gy in 2 cases and 1.8 Gy in 1 case. In the remaining seven patients, the fractionation was not recorded. In nine cases, irradiation was conducted using megavoltage photons; in the remaining cases, no details were found in the patient files.

There was no difference in mean age (p=0.252), gender distribution (p=0.513), ciliary body involvement (p=0280), presence of monosomy 3 (p=0.355), cell type (p=0.897), resection status (p=0.258) in patients treated with enucleation only compared with those who received EBRT. Although patients in the irradiated group were more likely to have larger EOE, the difference was not significant (p=0146). However, patients who underwent EBRT had significantly higher TNM stages (p=0.033), and while all patients who underwent enucleation only were treated with enucleation as primary treatment, those who underwent adjuvant EBRT were significantly more likely to be treated with enucleation due to orbital or intraocular recurrence, lack of treatment success after brachytherapy or a prior combination of different therapies (p=0.031). All patients were followed-up until death or the end of the study in January 2023, the median follow-up time being 37 months, ranging from 5 months (due to early death) to 422 months. At latest follow-up 16.3% of patients (7/43) were alive and these patients were followed for a median of 132 months (range 76–422 months). The median survival time of the entire cohort was 37 months (~3 years) and varied from 5 to 422 months. The 5-year and 10-year survival rates were 32.6% and 16.3%, respectively. The postoperative survival time of patients who died during the observation period was 27 months on average (5–200 months). Looking at the two patient groups, the following results were obtained: 82.4% of the patients who were observed (14/17) and 84.6% of the patients receiving EBRT (22/26) died during the observation period. There was no significant difference between the two groups (p=0.999). The median survival time of observed patients was 50 months (~4 years) and varied from 10 to 108 months, while it was 27 months (~2.25 years) and varied from 5 to 422 months for patients who received EBRT. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis indicated no significant difference in postoperative survival between the two groups (p=0.593). Patients who were observed showed a higher 5-year-overall survival rate than patients undergoing EBRT (41.18% vs 26.92%) but a lower 10-year-survival rate (12.5% vs 20%), without the difference being statistically significant (p=0.329 resp. p=0.685).

32 of the 36 reported deaths were caused by malignant melanoma metastases. The remaining four died of other causes. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis of disease-specific survival showed no significant difference between patients who received EBRT and those who were observed (figure 1).

Results in relation to TNM stage, size of extraocular extension and genetic risk factors

Known potential risk factors on patient survival are genetic changes, histopathological features, TNM classification and the size of EOE. While the presence of genetic risk factors with monosomy 3 (p=0.355) and the size of the extraocular extension (p=0.111) did not differ significantly between our groups, more of the irradiated patients had a TNM T4 tumour stage (p=0.033).

TNM stage

31 of the 43 patients analysed in the overall cohort had a tumour stage T3. There was no statistically significant difference between patients undergoing EBRT and those who were observed regarding postoperative survival time (p=0.469), occurrence of metastases (p=0.262) and time to metastatic disease (p=0.860). At the time of enucleation, 11 patients had a TNM stage T4 tumour, 3 of which were only enucleated, while the remaining 8 patients were treated with additional EBRT of the orbit. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed no statistically significant difference between both groups (p=0.989).

Size of EOE

According to the size of extraocular extension, the 43 tumours were divided into three size categories: Small EOE—defined as circumscribed extraocular extension of less than 1 mm in diameter or invasion of a vortex vein, advanced EOE—scleral extension ranging from 1 up to 5 mm in diameter or extensive EOE—scleral infiltration exceeding 5 mm in diameter and/or optic nerve involvement.

20 of the 43 enucleated eyes showed small EOE. Nine of these patients underwent EBRT. In this group patients who were irradiated died significantly earlier (mean survival time after enucleation 19 months vs 53 months, respectively; p=0.029) due to early metastasis (5 months vs 57 months; p=0.032) than those who were observed.

23 of the 43 enucleated eyes showed advanced and extensive EOE. 6 of these were managed with enucleation alone, in 17 cases EBRT was performed. Overall, those who received EBRT tended to survive longer than those who were observed (5–174 months, mean 37.87 months vs 10–64 months, mean 22.67 months), although this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.302). The difference between the two groups was not significant in the Kaplan-Meier survival analysis (p=0.104). Genetic risk factors

In 26 cases, the genetic status was known. Monosomy 3 was detected in nine patients. Four of these patients underwent enucleation only, five additionally received EBRT. Kaplan-Meier survival analysis showed no significant difference between both groups (p=0.905). All patients with monosomy 3 developed metastases during the observation period.

Metastatic disease

Metastases were detected in 76.7% of patients (33/43) during the observation period. Of these, 66.7% developed liver metastases, 15% lung metastases, 0.9% brain metastases and 0.3% lymph node, bone and kidney metastases. In 27.3% the localisation of the metastases could not be determined. Onset of metastatic disease was 31.67±41.238 months on average (0–172 months). In one case, metastases were already diagnosed at the time of enucleation.

82.4% of patients with enucleation only (17/26) developed metastatic disease after 34 months on average (14/17) and 73.1% of patients treated with additional orbital radiation (17/26) developed metastases after 30 months on average. There was no statistically significant difference between the two groups (p=0.999).

Orbital recurrences

No incidence of orbital recurrence was reported in both groups. Potential local recurrences were diagnosed on the basis of clinical abnormalities such as exophthalmos, movement disorders and orbital masses on sonography. Imaging of the orbit with computerised or magnetic resonance tomography was not regularly performed.

Adverse side effects of radiation therapy

Of the patients treated with EBRT, 61.5% developed side effects in terms of orbital irritation, eyelid oedema, sicca symptoms, conjunctival shrinking, concentration disorders, wound pain and neuralgiform symptoms. In one case, radiotherapy had to be discontinued before the target dose was reached due to wet epitheliolysis. Discussion

It is generally accepted that EOE of choroidal melanoma is associated with a higher risk of metastasis and occurs in up to 13% of patients who have undergone enucleation.3 6 Pach et al showed in a cohort of 610 patients that the 5-year mortality rate for tumours with an EOE of up to 4 mm was 37%, while the 5-year mortality rate increased to 78% for larger EOE.7 This correlates with the association between EOE and other risk factors for metastasis, such as ciliary body involvement, large basal tumour diameter, epithelioid cell type, closed vascular loops, diffuse choroidal melanoma, high mitotic rate and monosomy 3.6 8 In addition to the risks of metastasis, there is concern that EOE may recur in the orbital area, despite the limited data available.

Orbital exenteration and orbital brachytherapy9 for EOE have been abandoned because improved tumour-free survival after exenteration has not been demonstrated10 and brachytherapy of the orbit is not widely available. Consequently, EBRT has been in the focus of interest as a possible adjuvant treatment.

The potential effect of EBRT in all eyes, regardless of EOE, that had to be enucleated because of uveal malignant melanoma was extensively studied in the Collaborative Melanoma Study Group. This study evaluated the cumulative and disease-specific survival rates of 1003 patients enucleated for uveal melanoma between 1986 and 1994 in a randomised multicentre clinical trial. Patients were randomised for pre-enucleation radiation versus enucleation alone. The 10-year all-cause mortality rates were 61% for patients in both treatment arms. No differences in unadjusted or adjusted mortality rates were found between treatment arms or in subgroup analysis of the known risk factors.11

The first large-scale study evaluating the potential benefits of radiotherapy following enucleation of ocular melanoma with EOE was published in 1990 by Hykin et al.4 17 patients who had received postoperative irradiation of the orbit following enucleation were examined. The 5-year survival rate was 51%, which was comparable to earlier studies without additional radiation, but eventually dropped to 33% after 15 years. Hykin et al concluded that their study did not provide conclusive evidence of improved survival or reduced recurrence rates in the orbital socket when EBRT was used.

In a recent study by Roeloefs et al, 51 patients with EOE were examined with regard to the effect of adjuvant radiotherapy after enucleation, with 42% receiving adjuvant radiotherapy while the others were only observed.5 Patients who underwent EBRT had more extensive EOE (median: 5.1 mm vs 2.6 mm). Nevertheless, no difference in overall mortality was found, with 5-year and 10-year overall survival rates of 56% and 12%, respectively. However, serious side effects of radiation were reported in 64% of patients, therefore radiotherapy was not recommended for patients with small EOE.

The most recent study was published in 2023 by Sarode et al.3 It included 172 patients with enucleation because of uveal melanoma, 32 of whom had EOE. 7 of them underwent postoperative radiotherapy of the eye socket, while the remaining 25 did not. No statistically significant difference in survival rates was found between the two groups. The median postoperative survival time was 34.6 months after enucleation alone and 33.5 months after enucleation and EBRT, which was attributable to the presence of concomitant risk factors that influenced the decision to perform postoperative radiotherapy.

In our study, there was no significant difference in overall or disease-specific survival in patients treated with enucleation alone and those who received adjuvant or neoadjuvant radiation to the orbit. The frequency and time to metastases did not differ significantly between the two groups. During the observation period of 37 months on average, 82.4% of the patients who were treated with enucleation alone and 84.6% of the patients who received additional irradiation of the orbit died. Patients treated with EBRT had more advanced tumours, with 30.8% of tumours being T4 tumours, compared with 17.7% in patients who were observed. Similarly, more patients had extensive EOE (34.6% vs 23.5%). Patients with extensive EOE and additional radiotherapy showed a slightly longer survival time, although this was not statistically significant. The analysis of other known risk factors for metastatic disease also had no influence on survival. Since enrolment in the study began in 1980, information on human genetic analysis was only available for 37.2% of the patients. In nine patients of the total cohort, monosomy 3 was confirmed, leading to metastatic disease during the observation period, regardless of whether additional radiotherapy was performed or not.

In our study, no local recurrences occurred after either enucleation alone or enucleation and radiotherapy. We did not perform routine MRI but instead focused on clinical parameters such as a deteriorated fit of the ocular prosthesis, movement disorders and ultrasonography. Previous studies have also failed to show a reduction in orbital recurrence rates in patients with uveal melanoma and EOE despite additional preoperative or postoperative radiotherapy. Roelofs et al observed no orbital recurrences at a median follow-up of 2.7 years, either after enucleation alone or after enucleation and radiotherapy.5 The same applies to the study by Sarode et al, who explain this with the lower life expectancy of EOE patients, who often died before a possible local recurrence occurred.3 We confirm these findings with a longer follow-up of 15.34 years on average.

A total of 61.5% of our patients experienced side effects of orbital EBRT. These ranged from mild symptoms such as dry eye syndrome, eyelid oedema and irritation of the orbital socket to serious, rarer disorders involving conjunctival shrinkage, wound pain, neuralgical issues and chronic granulation abscesses. Although some studies report only minor side effects, there is broad consensus in the literature regarding the frequency and severity of complications associated with radiotherapy of the orbital region.11–13 Nasser et al reported a similarly high rate of serious side effects (41.7%) as in our study.14 Zemba et al estimated the rate of all patients with side effects of radiotherapy before or after enucleation to be 70%.13 Nevertheless, there is agreement that new and more targeted irradiation methods can minimise the above-mentioned side effects.

It is well known that external beam orbital radiation therapy leads to a variety of side effects. Socket syndrome and scarring of the conjunctiva and eyelids can make it impossible to wear a prosthesis. This is even more serious as the patients life expectancy is often limited and radiation therapy has an impact on their quality of life.

The greatest strength of this study lies in the long follow-up period of a median of 37 months (5–422 months) overall, and a median of 131 months (132–422 months) for patients still alive at the study’s endpoint, which far exceeds the follow-up periods of previous studies on this topic.3–5 As 87.6% of the patients observed died during the observation period and only patients with a known cause of death were included, our study allows for valid conclusions regarding the frequency of metastasis and disease-specific survival rates. On the other hand, the main limitations of this study, however, are the small sample size of 43 patients and the lack of randomisation due to the retrospective nature of the study. Although the analysis is based on two groups that do not differ significantly in terms of demographic and morphological criteria, there are significant differences with regard to TNM stages and prior treatment. Furthermore, cases treated with preoperative or postoperative radiotherapy tended to have a larger EOE.

To minimise confounding due to significant or suggested differences between patients who underwent enucleation alone and those who underwent enucleation and radiotherapy, subgroups were formed based on TNM stages and EOE size, in which the results of the overall cohort were largely confirmed.

Since uveal melanomas can metastasise even 20–40 years after what is thought to be successfully completed curative local and orbital recurrences can occur more than 20–40 years after enucleation, and given that uveal melanomas with EOE represent a rare condition overall, a very long observation period spanning several decades appears necessary to recruit a sufficient number of patients and to draw valid conclusions regarding the frequency and duration until the onset of metastasis, as well as disease-specific survival rates. A prospective study spanning several decades would be desirable but is logistically difficult to achieve. The only promising approach that could also yield results in the foreseeable future was therefore a retrospective analysis, which, however, does not allow for randomisation.

The small number of patients can be explained not only by the rarity of the disease but also by a high rate of loss to follow-up due to the study design of the retrospective investigation spanning several decades, which, however, simultaneously represents the greatest strength of this study.

Conclusions

In our study, there was no significant difference in overall survival or disease-specific survival between patients with uveal melanoma and EOE treated with enucleation alone and those who received adjuvant or neoadjuvant EBRT of the orbit. The frequency of occurrence and onset of metastases did not differ significantly between the two groups. Even in patients with risk factors such as monosomy 3, larger tumour size or higher TNM stages, EBRT did not improve survival rates. We did not observe local recurrence in either group. However, adjuvant radiotherapy was associated with increased morbidity, especially in the early treatment phase. Adjuvant or neoadjuvant EBRT of patients with uveal melanoma and EOE after enucleation should therefore not be performed routinely. It is hoped that recent and ongoing advances in immunotherapy and the increasing use of PKC inhibitors will improve the long-term prognosis for patients with choroidal melanoma and EOE.

1.
Introduction

Recovering bandlimited functions by sampling their values over a discrete set is a classical problem in analysis. One of the most basic results in this direction is the Shannon-Whittaker interpolation theorem for Paley-Wiener spaces. We denote by
PW_{2\pi\delta}
the Paley-Wiener space of type
2\pi\delta
, which consists of the functions
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
whose Fourier transform
1
1

1

Here we normalize the Fourier transform by
\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\,\text{\rm d}x

\widehat{f}
is supported in
[-\delta,\delta]
. The Shannon-Whittaker theorem says for every
f\in PW_{\pi}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
one has

(1.1)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(n)\mathrm{sinc}(x-n),

where

\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x},

is the cardinal sine function.

The series in (
1.1
) has two interpretations. The limit happens in the
L^{2}
sense, i.e,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\|f-\sum_{|n|\leq N}f(n)\mathrm{sinc}(\cdot-n)\|_{2}=0.

In fact, the set of functions
\{\mathrm{sinc}(\cdot-n)\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
forms an orthonormal system, in particular this implies

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f(n)|^{2}.

On the other hand, by the Paley-Wiener theorem,
PW_{2\pi\delta}
is equivalent to the space of entire functions of exponential type
2
2

2

We say a entire function
f
has exponential
\tau
if
\limsup_{|z|\rightarrow\infty}\tfrac{\log|f(z)|}{|z|}\leq\tau

2\pi\delta
whose restriction to
{\mathbb{R}}
is in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, and one has

(1.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}{\sup_{z\in K}\left|f(z)-\sum_{|n|\leq N}f(n)\mathrm{sinc}(z-n)\right|}=0\,,

for any compact set
K\subset{\mathbb{C}}
. This means one can completely rebuild a function with Fourier transform supported in
[-1/2,1/2]
by knowing its values over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

By a simple dilation argument, one can see that for functions in
PW_{2\pi\delta}
a similar conclusion holds: one can completely recover a function by knowing its values over
\tfrac{1}{2\delta}{\mathbb{Z}}
. This means that if a function has a bigger exponential type, one needs a denser dilation of
{\mathbb{Z}}
to reconstruct the functions. In particular, for
PW_{2\pi}
one needs the information over
\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathbb{Z}}
to determine the functions, i.e., by doubling the exponential type, one doubles the information needed. In
[
14
]
, Vaaler studied the problem of determining a function
f\in PW_{2\pi}
by knowing
\{(f(n),f^{\prime}(n))\}_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
and obtained the following interpolation result:
if
f\in PW_{2\pi}
then

(1.3)

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(n)\frac{(\sin(\pi z))^{2}}{(z-n)^{2}}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f^{\prime}(n)\frac{(\sin(\pi z))^{2}}{(z-n)},

where the expression in the right-hand side of (
1.3
) converges uniformly in compact sets of
{\mathbb{C}}
(see
[
14
, Theorem 9]
).

This means one can reconstruct a
L^{2}
function of exponential type
2\pi
by replacing half the information needed about the values of
f
with information about the values of
f^{\prime}
, and this recovers the function in the pointwise sense. Although the system of interpolating functions in (
1.3
) is not orthogonal as in the case of integers translations of the cardinal sine, it was later observed by Gonçalves
[
9
, Corollary 2]
that there are constants
c,C>0
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle c\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f(n)|^{2}+\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f^{\prime}(n)|^{2}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x,

for every
f\in PW_{2\pi}
. This implies the limit in (
1.3
) also happens in the
L^{2}
sense.

In
[
11
]
, Littmann obtained a more general version of this kind of interpolation formula where for any
f\in PW_{N\pi}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the following formula holds

(1.5)

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f^{(k)}(m)P^{N}_{N-k-1}\left(\pi(x-m)\right)\frac{(x-m)^{k}}{k!}\left(\mathrm{sinc}(x-m)\right)^{N},

where
P^{N}_{n}
is the Taylor polynomial of degree
n
of
\left(\frac{x}{\sin(x)}\right)^{N}
centered in 0. As in the Vaaler case, these formulas are accompanied by a norm equivalence, in this case, Gonçalves and Littmann obtained in
[
10
]
that there exist constants
c_{N},C_{N}>0
such that for every
f\in PW_{N\pi}

(1.6)

c_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|f^{(k)}(m)|^{2}\leq C_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\,\text{\rm d}x.

Results like that are examples of the so-called dynamical sampling. Given a separable Hilbert space
H
, an operator
A:H\rightarrow H
, a family of vectors
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in I}
, where
I\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
, and a
sequence
\{l_{n}\}_{n\in I}
where each
l_{n}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, a dynamical sampling question consists of asking if the collection
\{A^{i}f_{n},\,i=0,\cdots,l_{n}\}_{n\in I}
forms a complete system, a Riesz basis, a frame, or a Bessel sequence; see
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
and the references within for more on the topic of dynamical sampling.

If one considers
A=\frac{d}{dx}:PW_{N\pi}\rightarrow PW_{N\pi}
, and take
f_{n}^{N}(x)=N\mathrm{sinc}(N(x-n))
,
one has

(-1)^{j}f^{(j)}(n)=(-1)^{j}\langle A^{j}f,f^{N}_{n}\rangle=\langle f,A^{j}f^{N}_{n}\rangle.

Therefore equation (
1.6
) implies the family of functions
\{A^{j}f^{N}_{n}\}
, with
j=0,...,N-1

and
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, forms a frame. Moreover, it follows from the interpolation formula (
1.5
) that

\{A^{j}f^{N}_{n}\}
also forms a Riesz basis. So questions about interpolation and sampling of functions using the values of its derivatives in Paley-Wiener spaces are essentially dynamical sampling problems.

Interpolation formulas are a classical and useful tool for optimization problems (see
[
14
]
), and recently have garnered new interest due to the breakthroughs in sphere packing by Maryna Viazovska and her co-authors
[
6
,
15
]
, and the subsequent developments involving sign uncertainty principles (see
[
5
]
) and universal optimality (see
[
7
]
). We refer to the survey
[
4
]
for an account of the recent developments in Fourier interpolation.

1.1.
Main results

The aim of this paper is to generalize the interpolation formulas mentioned in the introduction. To
be more precise, we will see under which conditions an interpolation formula for
f\in PW_{\pi}
depending on

\left(\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}
is possible or not,
where
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and the
T_{n}
are Fourier multiplier operators, meaning that

T_{n}(f)(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(\xi)K_{n}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\xi}d\xi.

In what follows we will assume that the Fourier multipliers
K_{n}
are essentially bounded in the interval
[-1/2,1/2]
, hence these operators send the space
PW_{\pi}
into itself. Let us denote by
GL_{N}
the group of all
N\times N
invertible matrices with complex entries, equipped with the operator norm. Our main result is a criteria for such formulas to hold in terms of invertibility of certain matrices.

Theorem 1
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
T=\left(T_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{N}
where, for each
n=1,...,N
,
T_{n}
is a Fourier multiplier operator associated
to a bounded multiplier
K_{n}
. Let us define for
x\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)
the
N\times N
matrix

\mathcal{M}_{T}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{N}K_{n}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)\right)_{m,n=1}^{N}.

Then the following are equivalent:

1)
\mathcal{M}_{T}\in L^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right),GL_{N}\right)
and

\left(\mathcal{M}_{T}\right)^{-1}\in L^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right),GL_{N}\right)
,

2) There exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\left|\det\mathcal{M}_{T}(x)\right|>\epsilon
for almost every
x\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)
,

3) There exists, for
n=1,...,k
, functions
g^{T}_{n}\in PW_{\pi}
such that

f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(f)(Nm)g^{T}_{n}(x-Nm),

for all
f\in PW_{\pi}
, where the convergence holds both in
L^{2}
sense and uniformly.

Furthermore, the functions
g^{T}_{n}
appearing in 3) can be uniquely determined by either of the following
characterizations:

i) For each
n=1,...,N
, the function
g^{T}_{n}
is the unique function in
PW_{\pi}
satisfying

T_{m}(g^{T}_{n})(Nj)=\delta_{n,m}\delta_{j,0},

for all
m=1,...,N
and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,

ii) The functions
g^{T}_{n}
,
n=1,...,N
, are the unique functions in
PW_{\pi}
satisfying

\left(\widehat{g^{T}_{n}}\left(\frac{j-1-\xi}{N}\right)\right)_{n,j=1}^{N}=\left(\mathcal{M}_{T}(\xi)\right)^{-1},

for almost every
\xi\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)
.

As in the aforementioned interpolation formulas available in the literature, a standard application of the open mapping theorem also reveals the following frame bounds.

Corollary 2
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
T=\left(T_{n}\right)_{n=1}^{k}
where, for each
n=1,...,N
,
T_{n}
is a Fourier multiplier operator associated
to a bounded multiplier
K_{n}
. Then, if any of the equivalent statements 1), 2) or 3) from Theorem
1
holds
then there exists two positive constants
c_{T}
and
C_{T}
such that

c_{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\left|T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right|^{2}\leq C_{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx,

for all
f\in PW_{\pi}
.

2.1.
Preliminary lemmas

We shall carry out 2 proofs of Theorem
1
, and we begin by presenting two auxiliary lemmas which will be useful for both proofs.
The first lemma gives the equivalence between 1) and 2) in Theorem
1
, and is a classical result in the theory of block Laurent operators (see
[
8
, Chapter XXXIII]
), and therefore we will only need to show the equivalence between 1) and 3) in the proofs of said theorem.

Lemma 3
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
M\in L^{\infty}\left((a,b),\mathcal{M}_{N\times N}\right)
then the following are equivalent:

1)
M\in L^{\infty}\left((a,b),GL_{N}\right)
and
M^{-1}\in L^{\infty}\left((a,b),GL_{N}\right)
,

2) There exists an
\epsilon>0
such that
\left|\det M(x)\right|>\epsilon
for almost every
x\in\left(a,b\right)
.

The second lemma introduces us to some periodic functions that will be of great use in both proofs of the theorem.

Lemma 4
.

Let
T
be a Fourier multiplier operator associated to a bounded and measurable multiplier
K
,
f
a function
in
PW_{\pi}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Then we have that

\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T(f)(Nm+a)e^{2\pi imx}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)K\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)e^{\frac{2\pi ia(m-1-x)}{N}},

for
x\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)
.

Proof of Lemma
4
.

Let us start by defining
g(x)=\widehat{f}\left(-\frac{x}{N}\right)K\left(-\frac{x}{N}\right)e^{-\frac{2\pi iax}{N}}

that satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{g}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}g(x)e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}\left(-\frac{x}{N}\right)K\left(-\frac{x}{N}\right)e^{-\frac{2\pi iax}{N}}e^{-2\pi ix\xi}dx

\displaystyle=N\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(y)K(y)e^{2\pi iy(N\xi+a)}dy=NT(f)(N\xi+a).

Using this function we have that if
x\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T(f)(Nm+a)e^{2\pi imx}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{g}(m)e^{2\pi imx}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}g(x+m)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{f}\left(-\frac{x+m}{N}\right)K\left(-\frac{x+m}{N}\right)e^{-\frac{2\pi ia(x+m)}{N}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)K\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)e^{\frac{2\pi ia(m-1-x)}{N}},

where in the second equality we applied Poisson’s summation formula and in the last one we used that

\widehat{f}
is supported on
[-1/2,1/2]
.

∎

2.2.
First proof

The first proof will be based on the idea of obtaining a bijective relation between the data of the desired formula,
\left(\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}
, with the data of an already known formula, in this case we will use the Shannon-Whittaker interpolation formula, whose data is
\left\{f(m)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
. This idea was already explored in
[
13
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the authors present an alternative proof of the aforementioned interpolation formula obtained by Vaaler in
[
14
]
. Note that, with this reasoning, we transform the problem of the existence of an interpolation formula into a problem of inverting an operator between sequence spaces, which can be quite complex. In order to illustrate this reasoning we will first give a simplified version of it, where we will just change one value in the data of the interpolation formula.

We consider the problem of the existence of an interpolation formula on
PW_{\pi}
only depending on
\left\{f(m)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}
and
f^{\prime}(a)
for some
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
, which is simply the Shannon–Whittaker interpolation formula but changing
f(0)
by
f^{\prime}(a)
. We start by using the Shannon–Whittaker interpolation formula to put
f^{\prime}(a)
as a function of
\{f(m)\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
as follows

f^{\prime}(a)=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(m)\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a-m),

from this, if
\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)\neq 0
we can obtain that

f(0)=f^{\prime}(a)\frac{1}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}-\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}f(m)\frac{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(m-a)}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}.

Therefore if
\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)\neq 0
we have obtained a way to write
f(0)
as a linear combination of the values of the interpolation formula we want to obtain, using this we get

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(m)\mathrm{sinc}(x-m)

\displaystyle=\left(f^{\prime}(a)\frac{1}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}-\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}f(m)\frac{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(m-a)}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}\right)\mathrm{sinc}(x)+\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}f(m)\mathrm{sinc}(x-m)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(a)\frac{\mathrm{sinc}(x)}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}+\sum_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}}f(m)\left(\mathrm{sinc}(x-m)-\frac{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(m-a)}{\mathrm{sinc}^{\prime}(a)}\mathrm{sinc}(x)\right),

obtaining the interpolation formula we were looking for. Note that the condition
\sin^{\prime}(a)\neq 0
is not only sufficient for the formula to exist but also necessary as if this is not the case then any multiple of the
\mathrm{sinc}
function will have all the data in the interpolation formula equal to 0.

First proof of Theorem
1
:

We start by considering the operator
A_{T}:\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}\to\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}
defined as

A_{T}\left(\left(\left\{b^{n}_{m}\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)=\left(\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}b_{j}^{s}T_{n}\left(\mathrm{sinc}\right)(N(m-j)-s+1)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N},

note that if
f\in PW_{\pi}
we have that

A_{T}\left(\left(\left\{f(Nm+n-1)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)=\left(\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}.

We also consider the operator
F_{N}:L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\right)\to\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}
defined as

F_{N}\left(\left(f_{m}\right)_{m=1}^{N}\right)=\left(\left\{\widehat{f_{m}}(j)\right\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{m=1}^{N}

that is invertible, with inverse
F_{N}^{-1}:\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}\to L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\right)

F_{N}^{-1}\left(\left(\left\{a^{m}_{j}\right\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{m=1}^{N}\right)(x)=\left(\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}a^{m}_{j}e^{2\pi ijx}\right)_{m=1}^{N}.

Now we compose these operators as follows

\displaystyle\left(F_{N}^{-1}\circ A_{T}\circ F_{N}\right)\left(\left(f_{m}\right)_{m=1}^{N}\right)(x)=\left(F_{N}^{-1}\circ A_{T}\right)\left(\left(\left\{\widehat{f_{m}}(j)\right\}_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{m=1}^{N}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\quad=F_{N}^{-1}\left(\left(\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{f_{s}}(j)T_{n}\left(\mathrm{sinc}\right)\left(N(m-j)-s+1\right)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\quad=F_{N}^{-1}\left(\left(\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{N}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\widehat{f_{s}}(j)\mathcal{F}\left(\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi ir\cdot}\right)(m-j)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\quad=F_{N}^{-1}\left(\left(\left\{\sum_{s=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}\left(f_{s}(\cdot)\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi ir\cdot}\right)(m)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)(x)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\sum_{s=1}^{N}f_{s}(x)\left(\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi irx}\right)\right)_{n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi irx}\right)_{n,s=1}^{N}\left(f_{s}(x)\right)_{s=1}^{N}.

It is clear that the operator
A_{T}:\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}\to\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}
is invertible if and only if

F_{N}^{-1}\circ A_{T}\circ F_{N}:L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\right)\to L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}}/{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\right)
is invertible and, due
to what we just obtained, this is equivalent to the existence of a bounded inverse of the matrix of functions

\left(\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi irx}\right)_{n,s=1}^{N}.

Using Lemma
4
we obtain that, for
x\in\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right)
, we have that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{r\in{\mathbb{Z}}}T_{n}(\mathrm{sinc})(Nr-s+1)e^{2\pi irx}\right)_{n,s=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{N}\widehat{\mathrm{sinc}}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)K_{n}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)e^{-\frac{2\pi i\left(s-1\right)\left(m-1-x\right)}{N}}\right)_{n,s=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\frac{1}{N}K_{n}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)\right)_{n,m=1}^{N}\left(e^{-\frac{2\pi i(s-1)(m-1-x)}{N}}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\mathcal{M}_{T}(x)\right)^{t}\left(e^{-\frac{2\pi i(s-1)(m-1-x)}{N}}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}

and as

\displaystyle\det\left(e^{-\frac{2\pi i(s-1)(m-1-x)}{N}}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq n<m\leq N}\left(e^{-\frac{2\pi i(m-1-x)}{N}}-e^{-\frac{2\pi i(n-1-x)}{N}}\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq n<m\leq N}e^{\frac{2\pi ix}{N}}\left(e^{-\frac{2\pi i(m-1)}{N}}-e^{-\frac{2\pi i(n-1)}{N}}\right)\neq 0

we deduce that
A_{T}:\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}\to\left(\ell^{2}\right)^{N}
is invertible if and only if

\mathcal{M}_{T}(.)\in L^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right),GL_{N}\right)
with

\left(\mathcal{M}_{T}(.)\right)^{-1}\in L^{\infty}\left(\left(\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right),GL_{N}\right)
. So now we will show that 3)

\left(\left\{C^{T}_{s,n,m}\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}:=\left(A_{T}\right)^{-1}\left(\left(\left\{\delta_{n,s}\delta_{m,0}\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\right)_{n=1}^{N}\right)

3.
Some applications

We now present some applications of Theorem
1
, proving the existence or non-existence of specific interpolation formulas. In the following applications, the operators
T_{n}
we will be considering will be derivatives, translations, difference quotients and compositions and linear combinations of such operators.

3.1.
General observations

The first observation we want to highlight is the case where the operators we consider have a common “factor”, meaning that
T_{n}=T^{\prime}\circ T_{n}^{*}
for all
n=1,...,N
. In this case, we will have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{T}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{N}K_{n}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)\right)_{m,n=1}^{N}=\left(\frac{1}{N}K^{\prime}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)K^{*}_{n}\left(\frac{m-1-x}{N}\right)\right)_{m,n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{s=1}^{N}K^{\prime}\left(\frac{s-1-x}{N}\right)\delta_{m,s}\frac{1}{N}K^{*}_{n}\left(\frac{s-1-x}{N}\right)\right)_{m,n=1}^{N}

\displaystyle=\left(K^{\prime}\left(\frac{s-1-x}{N}\right)\delta_{m,s}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}M^{T^{*}}(x).

From this we deduce that, if
T_{n}=T^{\prime}\circ T_{n}^{*}
, an interpolation formula depending on
\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
exists if and only if
T^{\prime}
is invertible in
PW_{\pi}
and the interpolation formula depending on
\left\{T^{*}_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
exists. A simple example of this equivalence is when
T^{\prime}
is a translation, in which case we have that an interpolation formula depending on
\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
exists then the one depending on
\left\{T_{n}(f)(Nm+a)\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,
for any
a\in{\mathbb{C}}
, also exists.

Another case we want to mention is that if all the multipliers have a common root in

\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]
then the interpolation formula does not exist. This is because in this case there will exist
m_{0}\in\{1,...,N\}
and
x_{0}\in\left[\frac{N-2}{2},\frac{N}{2}\right]
such that
K_{n}\left(\frac{m_{0}-1-x_{0}}{N}\right)=0
for all
n=1,...,N
and therefore the
m_{0}
-th column of
\mathcal{M}_{T}(x_{0})
will be all 0, making it not invertible.

As an example of this, we have that if
T_{n}(f)=P_{n}(D)(f)(\cdot+a_{n})
for all
n=1,...,N
then at least one of the differential polynomials need to have an independent term, because if this is not the case then their associated multipliers,
K_{n}(x)=P_{n}(2\pi ix)e^{2\pi ixa_{n}}
, will have
x=0
as a common root. Note that this could also be deduced from the previous observation as if none of the polynomials had an independent term then all the operators would have the derivative, an operator that is not invertible, as a common factor.

3.5.
Formula depending on
\left\{f(Nm+a_{n})\right\}_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
,

n\neq m

\left(C_{n,m}\right)_{n,m=1}^{N}
to the inverse of the first of the two matrices of the factorization made before we have that

Id=\left(C_{n,m}\right)_{n,m=1}^{N}\left(\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ia_{s}}{N}}\right)^{m-1}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}=\left(\sum_{m=1}^{N}C_{n,m}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi ia_{s}}{N}}\right)^{m-1}\right)_{m,s=1}^{N}

\left(\left(\frac{1}{N}e^{\frac{2\pi i(m-1-x)a_{n}}{N}}\right)_{m,n=1}^{N}\right)^{-1}=\left(Ne^{\frac{2\pi ia_{n}x}{N}}\delta_{n,s}\right)_{n,s=1}^{N}\left(C_{s,m}\right)_{s,m=1}^{N}=\left(Ne^{\frac{2\pi ia_{n}x}{N}}C_{n,m}\right)_{n,m=1}^{N}

\displaystyle g^{a}_{n}(x)

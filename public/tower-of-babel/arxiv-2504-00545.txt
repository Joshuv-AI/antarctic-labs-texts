Abstract
. We show that the maximal Fock space F α ∞ F^{\infty}_{\alpha} on ℂ n {{\mathbb{C}}^{n}} is a Lipschitz space, that is, there exists a distance d α d_{\alpha} on ℂ n {{\mathbb{C}}^{n}} such that an entire function f f on ℂ n {{\mathbb{C}}^{n}} belongs to F α ∞ F^{\infty}_{\alpha} if and only if | f ⁡ ( z ) − f ⁡ ( w ) | ≤ C ​ d α ​ ( z , w ) |f(z)-f(w)|\leq Cd_{\alpha}(z,w) for some constant C C and all z , w ∈ ℂ n z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}} . This can be considered the Fock space version of the following classical result in complex analysis: a holomorphic function f f on the unit ball 𝔹 n {\mathbb{B}^{n}} in ℂ n {{\mathbb{C}}^{n}} belongs to the Bloch space if and only if there exists a positive constant C C such that | f ⁡ ( z ) − f ⁡ ( w ) | ≤ C ​ β ​ ( z , w ) |f(z)-f(w)|\leq C\beta(z,w) for all z , w ∈ 𝔹 n z,w\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}} , where β ⁡ ( z , w ) \beta(z,w) is the distance on 𝔹 n {\mathbb{B}^{n}} in the Bergman metric. We also present a new approach to Hardy-Littlewood type characterizations for F α p F^{p}_{\alpha} .

1.
Introduction

For
\alpha>0
and
0<p\leq\infty
we use
L^{p}_{\alpha}
or
L^{p}_{\alpha}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
to denote
the space of all Lebesgue measurable functions
f
on the complex Euclidean space
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

such that the function
f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}
belongs to
L^{p}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},dv)
, where
dv
is
ordinary volume measure on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. For
f\in L^{p}_{\alpha}
we write

\|f\|^{p}_{p,\alpha}=\left(\frac{p\alpha}{2\pi}\right)^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dv(z)

when
0<p<\infty
and

\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}=\esssup\left\{|f(z)|e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}:z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\right\}

when
p=\infty
.

Let
H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
denote the space of all entire functions on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. The spaces

F^{p}_{\alpha}=L^{p}_{\alpha}\cap H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}),\qquad 0<p\leq\infty,\alpha>0,

are usually called Fock spaces. Each
F^{p}_{\alpha}
is closed in the Lebesgue space

L^{p}_{\alpha}
. In particular,
F^{p}_{\alpha}
is a Banach space when
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

It is clear that
L^{2}_{\alpha}=L^{2}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},d\lambda_{\alpha})
, where

d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)

is the Gaussian measure. The orthogonal projection

P_{\alpha}:L^{2}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},d\lambda_{\alpha})\to F^{2}_{\alpha}
is an integral operator, namely,

P_{\alpha}f(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}e^{\alpha z\overline{w}}f(w)\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(w),

(1)

where
z\overline{w}=z_{1}\overline{w}_{1}+\cdots+z_{n}\overline{w}_{n}
. It is well known
that
F^{p}_{\alpha}=P_{\alpha}L^{p}_{\alpha}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

The function
K_{w}(z)=K(z,w)=e^{\alpha z\overline{w}}
is the reproducing kernel of
the Hilbert space
F^{2}_{\alpha}
. We will need to use the normalized reproducing kernels

k_{w}=K_{w}/\|K_{w}\|_{2,\alpha}
, which are unit vectors in
F^{2}_{\alpha}
. It is clear that

k_{w}(z)=\frac{K(z,w)}{\sqrt{K(w,w)}}=e^{\alpha z\overline{w}-(\alpha|w|^{2}/2)}.

See
[
9
]
for an introduction to Fock spaces.

The main result of the paper is Theorem A below.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
,
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
, and
d
is the distance function on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
defined by

d(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

(b)

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq Cd(z,w),\qquad z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

(c)

The function
Rf(z)/(1+|z|^{2})
belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\alpha}
, where

Rf(z)=z_{1}\partial_{1}f(z)+\cdots+z_{n}\partial_{n}f(z)

is the radial derivative of
f
with
\partial_{k}f=\partial f/\partial z_{k}
for
1\leq k\leq n
.

This result has a well-known analogue in the more classical theory of Bergman spaces.
Recall that the Bergman space
A^{p}
,
0<p<\infty
, of the open unit ball
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
in

{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
is the space of all holomorphic functions in
L^{p}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)
. If
P:L^{2}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)\to A^{2}
is the Bergman projection, then it is well known that
A^{p}=PL^{p}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)

for
1<p<\infty
. When
p=\infty
, the space
{\mathcal{B}}=PL^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}^{n}})
is called the
Bloch space of
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
, which can be shown to consist of all holomorphic
f
on
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}

such that

\sup_{z\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}}}(1-|z|^{2})|Rf(z)|<\infty.

The Bergman space analogue of Theorem A is the following: a holomorphic function

f
on
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{B}}
if and only if
|f(z)-f(w)|\leq C\beta(z,w)
for some positive
constant
C
and all
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
, where
\beta(z,w)
is the distance function on
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}

in the Bergman metric. See
[
8
]
.

The Bergman spaces
A^{p}
and the Bloch space
{\mathcal{B}}
on
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
can also be described
in terms of higher order derivatives. More precisely, if
f
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and

N
is a positive integer, then
f\in A^{p}
if and only if the functions

(1-|z|)^{|m|}\partial^{m}f(z)
belong to
L^{p}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)
for all
|m|=N
. Here

m=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of non-negative integers and

|m|=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n},\qquad\partial^{m}f=\frac{\partial^{|m|}f}{\partial z_{1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\partial z_{n}^{m_{n}}}.

Similarly,
f\in{\mathcal{B}}
if and only if the functions
(1-|z|)^{|m|}\partial^{m}f(z)
belong to

L^{\infty}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)
for all
|m|=N
. Such results are usually called
Hardy-Littlewood theorems, especially in the one-dimensional case of the unit disc.
See
[
8
]
again.

It turns out that these Hardy-Littlewood type theorems also hold for Fock spaces. The
following theorem can be found in
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
]
.

Theorem B
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
,
0<p\leq\infty
,
N
is positive integer, and
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
.
Then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the functions
\partial^{m}f(z)/(1+|z|)^{|m|}
,

|m|=N
, all belong to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

We will present a new approach to Theorem B above when
n=1
. Our proof is based
on the main theorem in
[
4
]
and is much different and simpler than the existing
proofs in the literature. We will conclude the paper with two open problems.

2.
Some distance functions on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

Fix any two positive parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
and define a function

d_{\alpha,\beta}:{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\times{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\to[0,\infty)

by

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}-e^{\beta w\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u).

By the “rotation-invariance” of the Gaussian measure, we clearly have

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)=d_{\alpha,\beta}(Uz,Uw)

for all
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
and all unitary transformations
U:{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\to{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. The following
lemma is obvious.

Lemma 1
.

Each
d_{\alpha,\beta}
is a distance on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
, that is, it satisfies the
following three axioms:

(a)

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)\geq 0
for all
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
, and

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)=0
iff
z=w
.

(b)

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)=d_{\alpha,\beta}(w,z)
for all
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

(c)

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)\leq d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,u)+d_{\alpha,\beta}(u,w)

for all
z,w,u\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

The computation of the precise distance
d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,w)
between
z
and
w
is
often difficult. But we have the following estimate when one of the two points is the origin.
Here
F(z)\sim G(z)
means that there exist positive constants
c
and
C
(independent
of
z
but dependent on other parameters) such that
cF(z)\leq G(z)\leq CF(z)
for all

z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proposition 2
.

For any fixed positive
\alpha
and
\beta
we have

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\sim\sqrt{e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(2\alpha)}-1}

for
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. In particular,

d_{\alpha/2,\alpha}(z,0)\sim\sqrt{e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}-1},\qquad z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

Proof.

It follows from the reproducing property in
F^{2}_{\alpha}
that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha(\beta z/2\alpha)\overline{u}}\right|^{2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)=e^{\alpha|\beta z/2\alpha|^{2}}=e^{\beta|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)}.

Thus we have the following estimates:

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)+1=e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)}+1,\qquad z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},

and

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\geq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)-1=e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)}-1,\qquad z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

Consequently,

\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\frac{d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)}{e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)}}=1,

and for any
r>0
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

C^{-1}e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)}\leq d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\leq Ce^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(4\alpha)},\qquad|z|\geq r.

This clearly implies that there is another positive constant
C
such that

C^{-1}\sqrt{e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(2\alpha)}-1}\leq d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\leq C\sqrt{e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(2\alpha)}-1},\qquad|z|\geq r.

On the other hand, it is clear from the unitary invariance of the Gaussian measure (or
the distance
d_{\alpha,\beta}
) that we can use the special points
z=(z_{1},0,\cdots,0)

below to obtain

\lim_{|z|\to 0}\frac{d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)}{|z|}=\lim_{|z|\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}-1|}{|z|}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)=\beta\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u_{1}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u).

(2)

It follows that

d_{\alpha,\beta}(z,0)\sim\sqrt{e^{\beta^{2}|z|^{2}/(2\alpha)}-1},\qquad|z|\to 0.

The proof of the proposition is complete when we combine this with the estimate
at the end of the previous paragraph.
∎

To simplify notation, we will write

d_{\alpha}(z,w)=d_{\alpha/2,\alpha}(z,w)

from this point on. This distance function arises naturally in the study of the spaces

F^{p}_{\alpha}
. For example, we have the following result.

Lemma 3
.

For any
\alpha>0
we have

d_{\alpha}(z,w)\sim\sup\left\{|f(z)-f(w)|:f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha},\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}\leq 1\right\}

for
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

It is well known that the integral operator
P_{\alpha}
defined in (
1
) maps

L^{\infty}_{\alpha}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
boundedly onto
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
. It follows easily that
for
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
we have

\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}\sim\inf\left\{\|g\|_{\infty,\alpha}:f=P_{\alpha}g,\ g\in L^{\infty}_{\alpha}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})\right\}.

Let
d^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z,w)
denote the supremum in the lemma, which is also a distance on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

(see
[
10
]
for many other examples of distance functions induced by spaces of
analytic functions). Then

\displaystyle d^{\prime}_{\alpha}(z,w)

\displaystyle\sim\sup_{\|g\|_{\infty,\alpha}\leq 1}|P_{\alpha}g(z)-P_{\alpha}g(w)|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|_{\infty,\alpha}\leq 1}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}})g(u)\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)\right|

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}|e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

This proves the desired estimates.
∎

It is natural to wonder if the limit in (
2
) can be computed at points away from
the origin. When
n=1
, it is clear that

\lim_{w\to z}\frac{d_{\alpha}(z,w)}{|z-w|}=\lim_{w\to z}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\frac{|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}|}{|z-w|}\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|u|\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

However, the limit above does NOT exist when
n>1
and
z\not=0
. In fact, if
we choose
w=z+(t,0,\cdots,0)
, then

\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|1-e^{\alpha(w-z)\overline{u}}|}{|w-z|}|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u_{1}e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

Other similar “partial derivatives” will yield the following sub-limits:

\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u_{k}e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,

which clearly depend on
k
. More specifically, it follows from polar coordinates
and the one-dimensional case that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u_{k}e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)\sim(1+|z_{k}|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}.

On the other hand, we can write

\frac{d_{\alpha}(z,w)}{|z-w|}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|1-e^{\alpha(w-z)\overline{u}}|}{|(w-z)\overline{u}|}\,\frac{|(w-z)\overline{u}|}{|z-w|}\,|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

It follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for vectors in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
that

\limsup_{w\to z}\frac{d_{\alpha}(z,w)}{|z-w|}\leq\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u||e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

(3)

Thus we want to determine the growth rate of the integral

E(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|\,|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

as
|z|\to\infty
, which will be used several times later on.

Lemma 4
.

We have

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|u|^{2}|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}\right)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

It follows from the reproducing property in
F^{2}_{\alpha/2}
that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}z\overline{u}}\right|^{2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. Apply
\partial^{2}/\partial z_{k}\partial\overline{z}_{k}
to both sides of
the above identity. The result is

\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u_{k}|^{2}\left|e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}z\overline{u}}\right|^{2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2}|z_{k}|^{2}\right)e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}.

Summing over
k
, we obtain

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|^{2}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\left(n+\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}\right)e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}},

completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5
.

For any
\alpha>0
we have
E(z)\sim(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}
on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

We write
z=rw
, where
r=|z|
(so
|w|=1
). Then

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}e^{\alpha rw\overline{u}/2}e^{\alpha r\overline{w}u/2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}/2}\right|^{2}\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}=e^{\alpha r^{2}/2}.

Take the derivative with respect to
r
on both sides. We obtain

\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(\overline{w}u+w\overline{u})\left|e^{\alpha r\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=\alpha re^{\alpha r^{2}/2},

or

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\re(w\overline{u})\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)=|z|e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}.

Therefore,

|z|e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

It is clear that the integral on the right-hand side above is a strictly positive continuous
function of
z
for
|z|\leq 1
, so there is a positive constant
c
such that

c\,(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

On the other hand, it follows from Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
4
that

\displaystyle\left[\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|^{2}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2n}{\alpha}+|z|^{2}\right)e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2n}{\alpha}+|z|^{2}\right)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}.

Thus there exists a positive constant
C=C_{\alpha}
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|u|\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)\leq C(1+|z|)\,e^{\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}

for all
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Combining this with the estimate in the previous paragraph,
we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

Finally in this section we note that for any
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, and
p>1
, the function

d(z,w)=\left[\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}-e^{\beta w\overline{u}}|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}

is also a distance on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. For
0<p<1
the following is a distance on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
:

d(z,w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\beta z\overline{u}}-e^{\beta w\overline{u}}|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u).

It is not clear what these more general distances might be good for. It would be nice
to find some applications for them.

3.
Characterizations of
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}

In this section we prove two characterizations for the space
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
. It is well
known that
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
is maximal in some sense among Banach spaces of entire
functions under the action of the Heisenberg group; see
[
10
]
. Our first
characterization of
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
below shows that it is a Lipschitz space.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
. Then
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
if and only if
there exists a positive constant
C
such that

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq Cd_{\alpha}(z,w)

for all
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

The “only if” direction is a direct consequence of Lemma
3
. Alternatively,
for any
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle|f(z)-f(w)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}(e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}})f(u)\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(u)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\right)^{n}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}||f(u)e^{-\alpha|u|^{2}/2}|e^{-\alpha|u|^{2}/2}\,dv(u)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{n}\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

\displaystyle=2^{n}\|f\|_{\infty,\alpha}d_{\alpha}(z,w)

for all
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
and
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

On the other hand, if
f
satisfies the Lipschitz condition, then in particular,

|f(z)-f(0)|\leq Cd_{\alpha}(z,0)
for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. By Proposition
2
, there is another
positive constant
C
such that
|f(z)-f(0)|\leq C\sqrt{e^{\alpha|z|^{2}}-1}
for all

z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
, which clearly implies that the function
f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}
is bounded
on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Again, to put the result above in proper perspective, we should think of it as the Fock
space version of the following well-known result for holomorphic functions
f
on the
unit ball
{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
:
f
belongs to the Bloch space
{\mathcal{B}}
if and only if there exists a constant

C
such that
|f(z)-f(w)|\leq C\beta(z,w)
for all
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
, where
\beta(z,w)

is the distance between
z
and
w
in the Bergman metric. See
[
8
]
.

For a function
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
we will write
\nabla f=(\partial_{1}f,\cdots,\partial_{n}f)

for the holomorphic gradient of
f
. The equivalence of conditions (a), (b), and (c)
below is known, and we include a simple proof here. But condition (d) appears to be
new, interesting, and non-trivial.

Theorem 7
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
and
f
is an entire function on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. Then the following conditions
are equivalent:

(a)

f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

(b)

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

|\partial_{k}f(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
and
1\leq k\leq n
.

(c)

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

|\nabla f(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

(d)

There exists a positive constant
C
such that

|Rf(z)|\leq C(1+|z|^{2})e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

Proof.

It is obvious that conditions (b) and (c) are equivalent.

If
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
, then by Theorem
6
, there exists a positive
constant
C
such that

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}-e^{\alpha w\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

for all
z
and
w
in
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
w=z+(h,0,\cdots,0)
, divide both sides of the inequality
above by
|h|
, and then let
h\to 0
. The result is

|\partial_{1}f(z)|\leq C\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|u_{1}e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u).

It is clear that we also have

|\partial_{k}f(z)|\leq C\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|u_{k}e^{\alpha z\overline{u}}\right|\,d\lambda_{\alpha/2}(u)

for all
1\leq k\leq n
. By Lemma
5
, there exists another constant
C
such that

|\partial_{k}f(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
1\leq k\leq n
and
z\in{\mathbb{C}}
. Thus condition (a) implies (b).

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have
|Rf(z)|\leq|z||\nabla f(z)|
, which yields

\frac{|Rf(z)|}{1+|z|^{2}}\leq\frac{|z||\nabla f(z)|}{1+|z|^{2}}\leq\frac{2|\nabla f(z)|}{1+|z|}

(4)

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. This shows that condition (c) implies (d).

Finally, we assume that condition (d) holds. For any
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
we can write

\displaystyle f(z)-f(0)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\partial_{k}f(tz)\,dt=\int_{0}^{1}Rf(tz)\,\frac{dt}{t}

(5)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1/2}Rf(tz)\,\frac{dt}{t}+\int_{1/2}^{1}Rf(tz)\,\frac{dt}{t}.

Let
I_{1}(z)
and
I_{2}(z)
denote the two integrals above, respectively. Then

\displaystyle|I_{2}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{1/2}^{1}|Rf(tz)|\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq 2\int_{1/2}^{1}|Rf(tz)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2C\int_{0}^{1}(1+t^{2}|z|^{2})e^{\alpha t^{2}|z|^{2}/2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq 2C\left[e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}+\int_{0}^{|z|}se^{\alpha s^{2}/2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle=2C\left[e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}-1\right)\right]

\displaystyle\sim e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2},\qquad z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

To estimate
I_{1}(z)
, note that the assumption

|Rf(z)|\leq C(1+|z|^{2})e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

is clearly equivalent to

|Rf(z)|\leq C|z|(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

(with a possibly different constant). Thus

\displaystyle|I_{1}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1/2}|Rf(tz)|\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1/2}|z|(1+t|z|)e^{\alpha t^{2}|z|^{2}/2}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{2}|z|\left(1+\frac{|z|}{2}\right)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/8}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2},\qquad z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

for another positive constant
C^{\prime}
. Combining the estimates for
I_{1}(z)
and
I_{2}(z)
,
we find another positive constant
C
such that

|f(z)-f(0)|\leq Ce^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}

for all
z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. This shows
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
and completes the proof
of the theorem.
∎

We warn any inexperienced reader that the factor
1-|z|^{2}
on the unit ball

{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
can be replaced by
1-|z|
, while
1+|z|^{2}
is critically different from
1+|z|

in Theorem
7
above!

4.
Hardy-Littlewood type theorems for Fock spaces

It is known that Theorem
7
can be extended to all Fock spaces
F^{p}_{\alpha}

in terms of higher order derivatives.

Theorem 8
.

Suppose
0<p\leq\infty
,
\alpha>0
,
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
, and
N
is a positive integer.
Then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the functions
\partial^{m}f(z)/(1+|z|)^{m}
,
where
|m|=N
, all belong to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

See
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
]
.
∎

In this section, we provide a new approach to Theorem
8
in the one-dimensional
case. This approach is based on the main theorem in
[
4
]
and is much easier than
the arguments used in other papers in the literature. We begin with the case of first
order derivatives.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose
f
is an entire function on
{\mathbb{C}}
,
\alpha>0
, and
0<p\leq\infty
. Then

f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function
f^{\prime}(z)/(1+|z|)
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We will prove the result with the help of
[
4
]
, where it was proved that

f^{\prime}\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function
zf(z)
is in
F^{p}_{\alpha}
. What we want
to prove here is that
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function
[f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(0)]/z
is in

F^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
. In the equivalence

zf(z)\in F^{p}_{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow f^{\prime}(z)\in F^{p}_{\alpha},

if we replace
f(z)
by
f(z)/z
, then

f(z)\in F^{p}_{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}-\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\in F^{p}_{\alpha}.

Since
f(z)\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
clearly implies that
f(z)/z^{2}\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
, it follows that

f(z)\in F^{p}_{\alpha}\Longrightarrow f^{\prime}(z)/z\in F^{p}_{\alpha}\Longleftrightarrow f^{\prime}(z)/(1+|z|)\in L^{p}_{\alpha}.

On the other hand, if
f^{\prime}(z)/z\in F^{p}_{\alpha}\subset F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
, then by what we
have proved about
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
, we must have
f\in F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
, which implies
that
f(z)/z^{2}\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
when
p>1/2
. Thus
f^{\prime}(z)/z\in F^{p}_{\alpha}

implies
f^{\prime}(z)/z-f(z)/z^{2}\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
, which yields
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}

for
p>1/2
.

For
0<p\leq 1/2
(actually, the argument below works for
p>1/2
as well), we write

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}=\left[\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}-\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right]+\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}.

By the triangle inequality for the distance

d(f_{1},f_{2})=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|f_{1}(z)-f_{2}(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)

in
F^{p}_{\alpha}
(technically, we should first work with
f_{r}(z)=f(rz)
,
0<r<1
, and
then use an approximation argument in order to make sure that all the integrals below
all converge) and one of the main results in
[
4
]
, there exists a positive constant

c
such that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}-\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)-\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)-\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z).

Choose a positive radius
R
such that
c-1/R^{2p}>0
. Then

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)\geq\left(c-\frac{1}{R^{2p}}\right)\int_{|z|>R}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)\\
+c\int_{|z|\leq R}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)-\int_{|z|\leq R}\left|\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}\right|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)

whenever
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
. This shows that

\int_{|z|>R}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)<\infty

and hence

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|f(z)|^{p}\,d\lambda_{\alpha}(z)<\infty

if the function
f^{\prime}(z)/z
belongs to
F^{p}_{\alpha}
.
∎

Corollary 10
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
,
0<p\leq\infty
, and
f\in H({\mathbb{C}})
with
f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Then for any constant
c
(including
c=0
) we have
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}

if and only if the function

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{z}+c\,\frac{f(z)}{z^{2}}

belongs to
F^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
9
and its proof.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Suppose
f
is an entire function on
{\mathbb{C}}
,
N
is a positive integer, and
0<p\leq\infty
.
Then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the functions
f^{(N)}(z)/(1+|z|)^{N}
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
N
. The case
N=1
has already been proved.

Suppose the result holds for some positive integer
N
. We proceed to show that

f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function
f^{(N+1)}(z)/(1+|z|^{N+1})
belongs to

L^{p}_{\alpha}
. Without loss of generality. we may assume that

f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=f^{(2N+2)}(0)=0.

In this case, we just need to show that
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function

f^{(N+1)}(z)/z^{N+1}
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

By the induction hypothesis, we have that
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function

f^{(N)}(z)/(1+|z|)^{N}
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
, which is the same as the function

g(z)=f^{(N)}(z)/z^{N}
belonging to
F^{p}_{\alpha}
. By Corollary
10
, this is
equivalent to the function

\frac{g^{\prime}(z)}{z}+N\,\frac{g(z)}{z^{2}}=\frac{f^{(N+1)}(z)}{z^{N+1}}-\frac{Nf^{(N)}(z)}{z^{N+2}}+\frac{Nf^{(N)}(z)}{z^{N+2}}=\frac{f^{(N+1)}(z)}{z^{N+1}}

belonging to
F^{p}_{\alpha}
. So the desired result is true for
N+1
, and the proof of
the theorem is complete.
∎

Serious obstacles arise when we try the arguments above in higher dimensions,
although several steps still work. In particular, the “only if part” of
Theorem
9
in the higher dimensional case follows easily from the one-dimensional
case. In fact, by Theorem
9
and the closed-graph theorem, there exists a positive
constant
C
(independent of
f
) such that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{f^{\prime}(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}}{1+|z|}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dA(z)

for all
f\in H({\mathbb{C}})
, where
dA
is ordinary area measure on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
Now if
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
and
1\leq k\leq n
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|\frac{\partial_{k}f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k},\cdots,z_{n})e^{-\alpha|z_{k}|^{2}/2}}{1+|z_{k}|}\right|^{p}\,dA(z_{k})\\
\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\left|f(z_{1},\cdots,z_{k},\cdots,z_{n})e^{-\alpha|z_{k}|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dA(z_{k}),

where
C
is independent of the
n-1
variables
\{z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\}\setminus\{z_{k}\}
.
Since
1/(1+|z|)\leq 1/(1+|z_{k}|)
for
1\leq k\leq n
,
|z|^{2}=|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}
,
and the Gaussian measure on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
is a product measure, we easily deduce that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|\frac{\partial_{k}f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}}{1+|z|}\right|^{p}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dv(z).

It then follows that there exists another positive constant
C
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left[\frac{|\nabla f(z)|}{1+|z|}\,e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}}\right]^{p}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dv(z)

for all
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
. Since
|Rf(z)|\leq|z||\nabla f(z)|
, we can also find a
positive constant
C
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|\frac{Rf(z)}{1+|z|^{2}}\,e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\left|f(z)e^{-\alpha|z|^{2}/2}\right|^{p}\,dv(z)

for all
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
.

When
p=1
, the other direction of the inequalities above can also be proved using
elementary arguments. In fact, it follows from (
5
) and Fubini’s theorem that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|f(z)-f(0)|e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|Rf(tz)|}{t}\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|Rf(tz)|e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{2n+1}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|Rf(z)|e^{-\beta|z|^{2}/t^{2}}\,dv(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|Rf(z)|\,dv(z)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-\beta|z|^{2}/t^{2}}}{t^{2n+1}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\beta^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|Rf(z)|}{|z|^{2n}}\,dv(z)\int_{\beta|z|^{2}}^{\infty}s^{n-1}e^{-s}\,ds.

An argument using mathematical induction shows that the incomplete gamma function

\Gamma(n,x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}s^{n-1}e^{-s}\,ds,\qquad x\in(0,\infty),

has the property that
\Gamma(n,x)\sim x^{n-1}e^{-x}
as
x\to\infty
, where
n

is any positive integer. It follows that there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|f(z)-f(0)|e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|Rf(z)|}{1+|z|^{2}}e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z).

This together with (
4
) shows that we also have

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}|f(z)-f(0)|e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z)\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla f(z)|}{1+|z|}e^{-\beta|z|^{2}}\,dv(z),

where the positive constant
C
only depends on
n
and
\beta
.

5.
Further remarks

It follows from the analysis in previous sections that we have the following
results about Fock spaces in terms of the radial derivative.

Corollary 12
.

Suppose
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
and
\alpha>0
.

(a)

If
0<p\leq\infty
and
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
, then the function

Rf(z)/(1+|z|^{2})
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

(b)

If
p=1
or
p=\infty
, and if the function
Rf(z)/(1+|z|^{2})
belongs to

L^{p}_{\alpha}
, then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
.

It is therefore very natural for us to make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 13
.

Suppose
0<p\leq\infty
,
\alpha>0
, and
f\in H({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}})
. Then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only
if the function
Rf(z)/(1+|z|^{2})
belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
. More generally, if
N
is any
positive integer, then
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if the function
R^{N}f(z)/(1+|z|^{2})^{N}

belongs to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
.

The “only if” parts above follows from Theorem
8
and the expression of
R^{N}f

in terms of partial derivatives. For example, if
N=2
, we have

\displaystyle R^{2}f

\displaystyle=R(z_{1}\partial_{1}f+\cdots+z_{n}\partial_{n}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}R(z_{k}\partial_{k}f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}\partial_{j}(z_{k}\partial_{k}f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[z_{k}\partial_{k}f+\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}z_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}}\right]

\displaystyle=Rf+\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}z_{j}z_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{j}\partial z_{k}}.

Similar formulas can be obtained for
R^{N}f
when
N
is any positive integer.

It is clear from the previous sections that, for each
\alpha>0
, the distance function

d_{\alpha}(z,w)
plays a signficant role in the study of the Fock spaces
F^{p}_{\alpha}
.
However, we have very limited information about these distance functions.

Proposition
2
gives a good estimate for
d_{\alpha}(0,z)
. A natural question is
whether or not we can use the estimate in Proposition
2
together with Weyl
unitary operators (see
[
9
]
) to obtain optimal estimates for the distance
function
d_{\alpha}(z,w)
. Our attempts so far have been unsuccessful.

Recall that Bergman spaces
A^{p}
can be characterized by Lipschitz type conditions

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq\beta(z,w)\left[g(z)+g(w)\right],

where
g\in L^{p}({\mathbb{B}^{n}},dv)
and
\beta(z,w)
is the Bergman distance between

z
and
w
. See
[
7
]
. It is natural to ask whether or not something similar is also
true for Fock spaces. We make the following conjecture here.

Conjecture 14
.

Suppose
\alpha>0
,
0<p\leq\infty
, and
f
is an entire function on
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. Then

f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
if and only if there exists a non-negative
continuous function
g\in L^{p}({{\mathbb{C}}^{n}},dv)
such that

|f(z)-f(w)|\leq d_{\alpha}(z,w)\left[g(z)+g(w)\right]

(6)

for all
z,w\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
.

If
f
satisfies the Lipschitz type condition in (
6
), then

\frac{|f(z)-f(w)|}{|z-w|}\leq\frac{d_{\alpha}(z,w)}{|z-w|}\left[g(z)+g(w)\right]

for all
z\not=w
in
{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}
. Fix
z
, let
w\to z
, and use (
3
) and Lemma
5
.
We obtain a positive constant
C
such that

|\partial_{k}f(z)|\leq C(1+|z|)e^{\alpha|z|^{2}/2}g(z),\qquad 1\leq k\leq n,z\in{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}.

It follows that the functions
\partial_{k}f(z)/(1+|z|)
,
1\leq k\leq n
, all belong
to
L^{p}_{\alpha}
. By Theorem
8
, we have
f\in F^{p}_{\alpha}
.

To prove the other direction, it seems that we need more detailed information
and more properties of the distance function
d_{\alpha}(z,w)
, which are not available
at this point. We intend to pursue these issues in a future paper.

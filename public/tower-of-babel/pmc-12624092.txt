Multimodal deep learning for entity relation extraction and spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph construction in earthquake emergency rescue
Authors: Shuai Liu, Meng Huang, Guang Yang, Wentao Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
This paper proposes a novel framework integrating multimodal deep learning with spatiotemporal knowledge representation to enhance information processing and decision support in earthquake emergency rescue operations. We address the challenges of heterogeneous data integration through a Cross-modal Attention Fusion Network (CAFN) that dynamically aligns semantic information across textual, visual, and spatiotemporal modalities. A Transformer-based joint entity-relation extraction model simultaneously identifies disaster-related entities and their relationships with 89.0% F1 score, outperforming conventional approaches by 8.7%. We further develop a spatiotemporal knowledge graph representation with specialized reasoning mechanisms that explicitly model the dynamic nature of emergency scenarios. The resulting decision support system demonstrates a 94.0% F1 score in resource allocation and route planning tasks, exceeding traditional rule-based systems by 23.7%. Experimental results confirm the effectiveness of our approach in processing complex multimodal information and supporting time-critical decisions during earthquake emergency rescue operations.

Introduction

Earthquake emergency rescue operations demand rapid and effective decision-making based on comprehensive information processing to minimize casualties and property damage1. Consider the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake scenario: emergency responders received fragmented text reports about collapsed schools, satellite images showing blocked roads, social media posts from survivors, and seismic sensor data indicating aftershock risks. The complex and dynamic nature of such post-earthquake environments generates heterogeneous data streams that create significant challenges for emergency management systems to efficiently organize and utilize this information2. In the Wenchuan case, critical rescue decisions were delayed by hours due to the inability to quickly integrate and analyze these diverse information sources, highlighting the urgent need for intelligent multimodal information processing systems. Traditional manual information processing methods are inadequate for handling the volume, velocity, and variety of disaster data, while existing automated approaches often struggle with the integration of multimodal data sources that characterize earthquake scenarios3.

Entity relationship extraction (ERE) techniques have been applied to disaster management; however, most current methods focus exclusively on textual data, neglecting the rich spatial and temporal information embedded in visual and sensor data streams4. Similarly, knowledge graph (KG) construction approaches typically rely on structured databases or homogeneous information sources, failing to capture the complex interrelationships among different entities across diverse modalities in emergency scenarios5. These limitations significantly hamper the development of intelligent decision support systems capable of providing timely and comprehensive situational awareness during earthquake emergency response operations.

Multimodal deep learning has emerged as a promising paradigm for integrating and analyzing heterogeneous data sources, demonstrating remarkable success in various domains including medical diagnosis, autonomous navigation, and security surveillance6. The capacity of multimodal deep learning models to extract semantic features from different data modalities and establish cross-modal relationships presents unprecedented opportunities for enhancing earthquake emergency rescue information processing systems. Despite these advances, the application of multimodal deep learning techniques to earthquake emergency response remains relatively unexplored, particularly in the context of spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph construction7.

This paper proposes a novel framework for earthquake emergency rescue information entity relationship extraction and spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph construction based on multimodal deep learning. The main contributions of this research include: (1) development of a multimodal entity recognition and relationship extraction model that integrates textual, visual, and temporal-spatial data from earthquake disaster scenarios; (2) design of a spatiotemporal reasoning mechanism that incorporates geographical constraints and temporal dynamics into knowledge graph construction; (3) implementation of a decision-oriented knowledge graph query and inference system tailored to emergency rescue requirements; and (4) comprehensive evaluation of the proposed framework using real-world earthquake emergency response datasets8.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II reviews related work in entity relationship extraction, knowledge graph construction, and applications of multimodal deep learning in emergency management. Section III presents the proposed multimodal deep learning framework for entity relationship extraction. Section IV describes the methodology for spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph construction. Section V details the experimental setup and evaluation results. Finally, Section VI concludes the paper and discusses future research directions.

Current status of earthquake emergency rescue information processing

Earthquake emergency rescue information is characterized by its heterogeneity, high dimensionality, and temporal-spatial complexity, originating from diverse sources including seismic monitoring networks, remote sensing systems, field survey reports, social media platforms, and emergency response logs9. These information sources generate structured data (e.g., seismic parameters, casualty statistics), semi-structured data (e.g., damage assessment forms), and unstructured data (e.g., situation reports, eyewitness accounts) that collectively form a comprehensive yet challenging information landscape for emergency management10. The multi-source, multi-modal nature of earthquake data necessitates sophisticated processing approaches that can effectively integrate and analyze diverse information types to support timely decision-making during rescue operations11.

Traditional earthquake information processing methods have primarily relied on manual data collection, organization, and analysis, supplemented by basic statistical techniques and simple rule-based systems12. While these approaches provide a foundation for information management, they suffer from significant limitations including processing delays, inconsistent data interpretation, inability to handle large volumes of information, and difficulty in identifying complex patterns and relationships across different data sources13. Furthermore, conventional methods struggle to address the dynamic aspects of earthquake scenarios, where information relevance and reliability evolve rapidly as the emergency situation develops14.

The development of earthquake information extraction techniques has progressed through several stages, from early keyword-based methods and template matching approaches to more sophisticated natural language processing (NLP) techniques incorporating syntactic and semantic analysis15. Recent advances have introduced machine learning-based approaches, particularly supervised classification and sequence labeling methods, which have demonstrated improved performance in extracting structured information from earthquake-related texts16. These approaches have enabled more automated and accurate identification of critical entities such as disaster locations, damage extents, casualty figures, and resource requirements from textual reports17.

Despite these advancements, current mainstream methods exhibit notable limitations when applied to the complex domain of earthquake emergency rescue. Supervised learning approaches require substantial quantities of domain-specific labeled data, which is often scarce in the earthquake emergency context due to the unique characteristics of each disaster event18. Rule-based systems, while effective for well-defined information patterns, lack flexibility in handling the variability and ambiguity inherent in disaster-related communications19. Additionally, most existing methods focus predominantly on textual information processing, neglecting the critical visual, spatial, and temporal dimensions that are essential for comprehensive situation awareness during earthquake rescue operations20.

The application scenarios for earthquake information extraction technologies range from pre-disaster preparedness and early warning systems to post-disaster damage assessment and recovery planning. However, the most challenging and time-sensitive application—real-time emergency rescue decision support—remains inadequately addressed by current approaches due to their limited capacity for integrating multi-modal information and adapting to rapidly evolving situations. This gap underscores the need for more advanced, flexible, and comprehensive information processing frameworks that can effectively leverage the full spectrum of available data to facilitate informed decision-making in earthquake emergency rescue operations.

Overview of multimodal deep learning methods development

Multimodal deep learning refers to the computational approaches that can process and relate information from multiple modalities, such as text, images, audio, and spatiotemporal data, to perform various tasks including classification, detection, and relationship extraction21. The fundamental principle underlying multimodal deep learning is the effective fusion and joint representation of heterogeneous data types to capture complementary information and cross-modal correlations that singular modality analysis cannot achieve22. These approaches typically employ neural network architectures that learn shared representations or establish connections between different modality-specific encoders to facilitate information integration and knowledge transfer across modalities.

Spatiotemporal graph neural networks (ST-GNNs) have emerged as a powerful framework for modeling these relationships by representing spatial entities as nodes and their interactions as edges, while incorporating temporal dynamics through recurrent structures or temporal convolutions31. Research progress in spatiotemporal knowledge graph construction

Knowledge graphs have emerged as powerful tools for emergency decision-making by organizing heterogeneous information into structured, interconnected entity-relation networks that facilitate rapid knowledge retrieval and reasoning36. In earthquake emergency response scenarios, knowledge graphs enable the integration of diverse information including damaged infrastructure, available resources, affected populations, and rescue operations into a unified semantic framework supporting situational awareness and resource allocation decisions37. Despite these applications, conventional knowledge graphs often lack explicit representations of temporal dynamics and spatial contexts that are crucial for emergency management, limiting their effectiveness in rapidly evolving disaster scenarios.

Spatiotemporal knowledge representation presents several fundamental challenges that distinguish it from traditional knowledge modeling approaches. First, the representation must capture both static spatial relationships (e.g., containment, adjacency) and dynamic temporal patterns (e.g., evolution, causality) in an integrated framework38. Second, spatiotemporal entities often exhibit complex behaviors with varying granularities and uncertainties that require flexible modeling constructs39. Third, the interdependencies between spatial and temporal dimensions necessitate specialized representation schemes that preserve their correlations while maintaining computational efficiency. To address these challenges, researchers have proposed various extensions to traditional knowledge graph schemas, including spatiotemporal property graphs that augment relations with spatial and temporal attributes, and hypergraphs that represent complex multi-entity spatiotemporal relationships.

Dynamic knowledge graph construction methodologies have evolved significantly to accommodate the temporal dimension inherent in evolving situations like earthquake disasters. Temporal knowledge graphs extend the traditional triple representation < subject, predicate, object > to quadruples < subject, predicate, object, time > or introduce specialized temporal relations to capture evolutionary patterns40. Stream-based knowledge graph construction approaches process continuous data streams to incrementally update graph structures as new information becomes available, employing techniques such as sliding window processing and incremental reasoning to maintain temporal consistency41. Event-centric knowledge graph frameworks represent discrete occurrences as first-class entities with temporal attributes, facilitating the tracking of cause-effect relationships and event sequences crucial for understanding disaster progression42.

The applicability of existing spatiotemporal knowledge graph technologies to earthquake scenarios faces several limitations. First, most approaches assume relatively regular temporal patterns and stable spatial contexts, whereas earthquake situations exhibit abrupt changes and spatial discontinuities that challenge conventional modeling assumptions43. Second, the high uncertainty and incompleteness of information during emergency situations necessitate robust inference mechanisms that can reason effectively with partial knowledge, an area where current approaches remain limited. Third, the computational demands of maintaining and querying large-scale spatiotemporal knowledge graphs may conflict with the real-time requirements of emergency response operations. Despite these challenges, recent advances in distributed graph processing systems and approximate reasoning techniques offer promising directions for developing more responsive and adaptive knowledge graph frameworks tailored to the unique demands of earthquake emergency rescue operations.

Multimodal deep learning based entity relationship extraction method for earthquake information

To address the challenges of data heterogeneity in earthquake emergency scenarios, we propose a standardized normalization pipeline that processes textual reports, visual evidence, sensor readings, and spatiotemporal data through modality-specific channels before integration44. This pipeline consists of four main components: data cleaning, format standardization, scale normalization, and missing value imputation, each tailored to the characteristics of different information sources. The normalized data undergoes modality-specific feature extraction before being aligned in a common representation space for downstream relation extraction tasks.

Cross-modal attention fusion mechanism design

Effective integration of heterogeneous features extracted from textual, visual, and spatiotemporal data sources is crucial for comprehensive entity relationship extraction in earthquake emergency rescue scenarios. We propose a Cross-modal Attention Fusion Network (CAFN) that dynamically establishes semantic alignments between different modalities while preserving their complementary information content27. The CAFN architecture employs a hierarchical structure with modality-specific encoders, cross-modal attention modules, and a multi-level fusion mechanism to generate unified representations that capture the complex interrelationships among earthquake entities across different information sources.

The core of our cross-modal fusion approach is the Text-Image Interaction Attention (TIIA) module that models bidirectional relationships between textual descriptions and visual evidence. For example, when processing a report stating “collapsed building near main street” alongside a satellite image, TIIA helps identify which image regions correspond to the textual description. Spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph construction for earthquake emergency rescue

Spatiotemporal knowledge representation model design

The effective representation of dynamic spatiotemporal knowledge in earthquake emergency rescue scenarios necessitates a specialized modeling framework that captures both the structural relationships among entities and their evolution across space and time. We propose a comprehensive spatiotemporal knowledge representation model based on a four-dimensional ontology structure that extends traditional knowledge graphs with explicit temporal and spatial dimensions58. This ontology framework consists of hierarchically organized concepts including physical entities (e.g., buildings, roads, bridges), abstract entities (e.g., rescue operations, evacuation plans), spatial regions, temporal intervals, and events with their corresponding attributes and relationships.

The core of our spatiotemporal ontology structure follows a multi-layered design that incorporates domain-specific earthquake emergency concepts organized in three primary dimensions: (1) the static conceptual layer defining entity types, relation types, and their taxonomic hierarchies; (2) the spatial layer representing geographic elements and their topological relationships; and (3) the temporal layer capturing time points, intervals, and temporal relations. These layers are interconnected through cross-dimensional relationships that enable the representation of complex phenomena such as earthquake propagation, damage evolution, and resource deployment dynamics59. The ontology incorporates both qualitative spatial relationships (e.g., adjacency, containment) and quantitative spatial information (e.g., coordinates, distances) to support diverse reasoning requirements in emergency response scenarios.

For representing dynamic knowledge, we adopt a temporal graph formalism that extends the traditional RDF triple structure < subject, predicate, object > to a quintet structure < subject, predicate, object, spatial_reference, temporal_reference>. This representation allows for tracking the evolution of entities and their relationships through both space and time:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\begin{aligned} & {\text{ < Hospital\_A, hasStatus, Operational, \{ lat: 31}}{\text{.23, long: 121}}{\text{.47\} , [2025 - 04 - 20T08:30:00, 2025 - 04 - 20T15:45:00] > }}{\text{.}} \\ & {\text{ < Hospital\_A, hasStatus, Damaged, \{ lat: 31}}{\text{.23, long: 121}}{\text{.47\} , [2025 - 04 - 20T15:45:00, now] > }}{\text{.}} \\ \end{aligned}$$\end{document}

This formalism enables the representation of state changes, event sequences, and causal relationships that characterize the dynamic nature of earthquake emergency scenarios. Additionally, we employ a multi-resolution temporal model that supports different granularities of time representation, ranging from seconds for critical emergency events to days for long-term recovery operations60.

To facilitate spatiotemporal reasoning, we define a comprehensive set of inference rules that operate across spatial, temporal, and thematic dimensions. These rules include: (1) temporal projection rules that propagate knowledge across time intervals based on persistence assumptions; (2) spatial interpolation rules that infer properties of regions based on neighboring observations; (3) causal inference rules that capture cause-effect relationships between events; and (4) semantic aggregation rules that derive higher-level knowledge from lower-level observations. To address the dynamic nature of earthquake emergency situations, we implement a multi-strategy knowledge update mechanism that incorporates new information while maintaining temporal and spatial consistency. This mechanism employs three complementary approaches: (1) append-only logging that preserves the complete history of knowledge evolution; (2) state-based updates that maintain the current situational representation; and (3) belief revision that reconciles conflicting information based on source reliability and temporal recency. The update process follows a transaction-based model with explicit versioning to ensure traceability and support rollback operations when necessary62. This approach enables the knowledge graph to adapt to rapidly changing situations while maintaining a coherent representation of the emergency scenario across time.

The proposed spatiotemporal knowledge representation model provides a comprehensive framework for organizing and reasoning with complex earthquake emergency rescue information. By explicitly modeling the temporal and spatial dimensions of knowledge, it enables sophisticated decision support capabilities including situation assessment, resource allocation planning, and risk prediction that are essential for effective emergency management.

The framework operates in four sequential stages: entity normalization, relation harmonization, attribute consolidation, and temporal-spatial alignment, each employing specialized algorithms to resolve specific types of heterogeneity while preserving the semantic richness of the original information.

The storage architecture employs a graph partitioning strategy that clusters highly interconnected subgraphs based on spatial locality and thematic similarity, significantly improving query performance for localized emergency scenarios68.

Emergency decision knowledge reasoning and applications

The spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph serves as a foundation for intelligent emergency rescue operations by enabling complex reasoning capabilities that support critical decision-making processes. We develop a comprehensive reasoning framework that combines rule-based deductive inference, probabilistic reasoning, and graph-based analytics to derive actionable insights from the integrated knowledge representation69. This framework implements three complementary reasoning mechanisms: (1) explicit rule-based inference using domain-specific axioms and heuristics, (2) embedding-based analogical reasoning that identifies similar historical patterns, and (3) graph structure analysis that discovers critical nodes, pathways, and communities within the emergency response network.

Based on the knowledge graph and reasoning framework, we develop a comprehensive rescue path planning algorithm that optimizes route selection for emergency vehicles while considering multiple dynamic constraints70. The algorithm formulates the path planning problem as a multi-objective optimization task that balances travel time, safety risks, resource requirements, and mission priorities. By incorporating real-time updates to the knowledge graph, the algorithm dynamically adjusts paths in response to changing conditions such as aftershocks, new road blockages, or shifting resource demands.

For optimizing resource allocation in emergency rescue operations, we propose a two-stage approach that combines strategic global optimization with tactical local adjustments. The global optimization phase employs a constraint satisfaction formulation that allocates resources based on priority, urgency, and availability while satisfying system-wide constraints such as coverage requirements and budget limitations. The local adjustment phase utilizes reinforcement learning techniques to adapt allocations based on real-time feedback and changing circumstances. This approach significantly improves resource utilization efficiency while maintaining the flexibility needed to respond to unpredictable emergency dynamics71.

We implement these reasoning and optimization capabilities in a prototype decision support system that provides four core functionalities: (1) situation assessment through knowledge graph visualization and query; (2) resource tracking and allocation optimization; (3) rescue operation planning and coordination; and (4) risk prediction and early warning. The system features a web-based interface with interactive maps, timeline views, and dashboard components that enable emergency managers to interact with the knowledge graph intuitively. A modular architecture ensures extensibility and integration with existing emergency management information systems72.

Conclusion

This paper presents a comprehensive framework for earthquake emergency rescue information processing and decision support based on multimodal deep learning and spatiotemporal knowledge graph technologies. The proposed approach addresses the critical challenges of integrating heterogeneous information sources, extracting structured knowledge from unstructured data, and supporting time-sensitive decision-making in complex emergency scenarios. Our main contributions include: (1) a novel cross-modal attention fusion mechanism that effectively integrates textual, visual, and spatiotemporal information; (2) a Transformer-based joint entity-relation extraction model that simultaneously identifies earthquake-related entities and their semantic relationships; (3) a spatiotemporal knowledge representation model that explicitly captures the dynamic and geographic nature of earthquake events; and (4) a knowledge reasoning framework that supports critical emergency rescue decisions including route planning and resource allocation.

Experimental evaluations demonstrate significant performance improvements over traditional approaches across multiple metrics. The proposed multimodal deep learning method achieves 89.0% F1 score in entity-relation extraction tasks, representing an 8.7% improvement over conventional self-attention mechanisms. The full integration variant of our spatiotemporal decision knowledge graph system reaches 94.0% F1 score in decision support scenarios, outperforming traditional rule-based systems by 23.7% and conventional knowledge graph approaches by 11.5%. These results confirm the effectiveness of integrating multimodal information and spatiotemporal reasoning for emergency rescue applications.

Despite these promising outcomes, several limitations warrant consideration in future research.

Deployment considerations and real-world constraints

The system’s deployment in actual emergency scenarios faces several practical challenges. Network connectivity issues during disasters can disrupt real-time data processing, requiring offline capability and data synchronization mechanisms. Our system addresses this through edge computing deployment and local caching, maintaining 78% functionality during complete network outages. Hardware requirements include minimum 16GB RAM and NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU for real-time processing, though CPU-only inference achieves acceptable 6-second response times for critical queries.

Robustness testing reveals that the system maintains 85% accuracy when visual data is unavailable and 79% accuracy with text-only inputs, ensuring continued operation despite missing modalities. Noisy data scenarios (simulated communication interference) result in 12% performance degradation, within acceptable bounds for emergency applications.

Ethical considerations and bias analysis

The deployment of AI systems in life-critical scenarios raises important ethical considerations. Our analysis identifies potential biases in entity extraction, particularly geographic bias favoring urban over rural areas (83% vs. 76% accuracy) and linguistic bias affecting non-English reports. Resource allocation algorithms may inadvertently favor areas with better data coverage, potentially disadvantaging marginalized communities.

To mitigate these issues, we implement fairness constraints requiring minimum resource allocation ratios across different demographic areas and establish human oversight protocols for all critical decisions. The system includes bias detection mechanisms that flag potentially discriminatory recommendations and requires manual approval for resource allocation exceeding 20% deviation from population-based baselines.

Computational requirements and scalability

First, the computational requirements of the proposed system present challenges for deployment in resource-constrained environments during actual disasters. Second, the performance of the entity-relation extraction model remains dependent on the availability and quality of training data, which may be limited for rare disaster types or emerging crisis scenarios. Third, the real-time integration of the system with existing emergency management infrastructure requires additional standardization and interface development73.

Future research directions include exploring more efficient model architectures for edge deployment, incorporating additional information modalities such as social media streams and sensor networks, developing explainable AI techniques to increase trust and adoption by emergency managers, and extending the framework to other types of natural and man-made disasters.

Reproducibility and code availability

To support reproducibility, we commit to releasing the implementation code, trained models, and evaluation scripts upon paper acceptance. The codebase includes detailed documentation, preprocessing scripts, and hyperparameter configurations used in our experiments. Training was conducted on NVIDIA A100 GPUs with PyTorch 1.12, requiring approximately 48 h for full model training. Complete experimental setup details including library versions, hardware specifications, and data preprocessing steps are documented in the supplementary materials.

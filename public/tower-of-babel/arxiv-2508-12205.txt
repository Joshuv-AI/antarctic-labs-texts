Large values of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions near the central point
Zikang Dong 1, Zhonghua Li 2, Yutong Song 2, Shengbo Zhao 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate the conditional large values of the quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions near the central point s = 1 / 2 s=1/2 . When σ \sigma closes to 1 / 2 1/2 within a suitable range, we show that L ⁡ ( σ , χ d ) L(\sigma,\chi_{d}) have the conditional large values of the similar order of magnitude as L ⁡ ( 1 / 2 , χ d ) L(1/2,\chi_{d}) .



Keywords.
Quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions, extreme values, central values, long resonance method.

Mathematics Subject Classification(2020).
11M06, 11M26, 11N37.

1.
Introduction

The study of the distribution of values of the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions has been one of the important topics in analytic number theory.
In recent years, the research on their extreme values has gotten many breakthroughs. Soundararajan
[
15
]
proposed a more effective approach, named the resonance method, to replace the previous moment method and derived larger magnitudes of extreme values for the Riemann zeta function as follows

\max_{T\leq t\leq 2T}\Big|\zeta\Big(\frac{1}{2}+it\Big)\Big|\geq\exp\bigg((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log\log T}}\bigg),

where
T
is large.

Following Aistleitner’s ideas in
[
1
]
, Bondarenko and Seip
[
2
,
3
]
improved the resonance method and refined Soundararajan’s result by a factor of
\sqrt{\log\log\log T}
in the exponent. Specifically, they obtained the following extreme values

\max_{0<t\leq T}\Big|\zeta\Big(\frac{1}{2}+it\Big)\Big|\geq\exp\bigg((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log\log\log T}{\log\log T}}\bigg).

Incidentally, de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
9
]
improved the factor from
(1+o(1))
to
(\sqrt{2}+o(1))
in the above result.

The resonance method has an obvious limitation when dealing with similar problems concerning various families of
L
-functions, namely that the coefficients must be positive. Let
X
be sufficiently large, Soundararajan
[
16
]
considered the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions and showed

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)\geq\exp\bigg(\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{{\log X}}{\log\log X}}\bigg).

Here,
\mathcal{F}
denotes the set of all fundamental discriminants , i.e.

\mathcal{F}:=\{d:d\,\,\text{is a fundamental discriminant}\},

and
\chi_{d}=\big(\frac{d}{\cdot}\big)
denotes the real primitive character associated to the fundamental discriminant
d
.

The study of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions at the central point can be traced back to the unpublished results of Heath-Brown. He showed that for arbitrarily large
d\in\mathcal{F}

L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)\gg\exp\bigg(c\frac{\sqrt{\log|d|}}{\log\log|d|}\bigg),

where
c
is a positive constant. For more details, we refer to
[
13
]
.

Recently, Darbar and Maiti
[
8
]
employed the long resonator method from
[
1
]
to obtain conditional large values of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions. They proved that under the Generalized Riemann hypothesis (GRH), one has

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\Big)\geq\exp\bigg(\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\bigg)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log\log\log X}{\log\log X}}\bigg).

Their result matches the form of the currently optimal extreme values of the Riemann zeta function established by Bondarenko and Seip in
[
2
,
3
]
. Under GRH, they improved Soundararajan’s result above. Furthermore, for
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
, they showed that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L(\sigma,\chi_{d})\geq\exp\bigg((c+o(1))\frac{(\log X)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log\log X)^{\sigma}}\bigg),

where
c
is an explicit constant (See
[
7
, Lemma 3]
).
They also computed the proportion of fundamental discriminants
d
for which large values of
L(\sigma,\chi_{d})
occur when
\sigma\in[1/2,1]
.

Indeed, based on the random matrix theory, Farmer, Gonek, and Hughes
[
11
]
conjectured that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\bigg|L\bigg(\frac{1}{2},\chi_{d}\bigg)\bigg|=\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\log X\log\log X}\right).

For further results and details about the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions we refer to
[
6
,
7
,
12
,
13
,
15
]
and the references therein.

Inspired by Chirre
[
5
]
, we investigate the conditional large values of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions near the central point. Following what was done in
[
10
]
, we investigate the conditional large values of
L(\sigma,\chi_{d})
when
0<\sigma-1/2\ll(\log\log X)^{-1}
. We utilize the long resonator method and derive the following Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume GRH is true. Let
A
be any positive real number and
X
be sufficiently large. Set

\sigma=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{A}{\log\log X},

then we have

(1.1)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L(\sigma,\chi_{d})\geq\exp\bigg((\lambda(A)+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log\log\log X}{\log\log X}}\bigg),

where

\lambda(A)=\frac{1}{2(e-1)e^{A}}.

Compared with
[
8
, Theorem 1]
, our Theorem
1.1
shows that when
0<\sigma-1/2\ll(\log\log X)^{-1}
, the conditional large values of
L(\sigma,\chi_{d})
can reach the similar order of magnitude as those of
L(1/2,\chi_{d})
as
X\to\infty
, though the leading constant in the exponent is slightly smaller. At the end of the proof, we will explain the reason for the emergence of the smaller constant, and also clarify the possibility of improving it.

To simplify the writing, we adopt the following short-hand notations in this paper, such as
\log_{2}x\operatorname{:}=\log{\log{x}}
and
\log_{3}x\operatorname{:}=\log{\log{\log x}}.
Moreover, let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of all positive integers. And let
\varepsilon>0
be an arbitrarily small number. Note that each appearance of
\varepsilon
does not necessarily denote the same value. Finally, we use
\square
to indicate a perfect square number.

2.
Preliminary Lemmas

In this section, we give some lemmas that we will use later. The first lemma below serves to handle the partial sums of
\chi_{d}(n)
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
8
]
, Lemma 1)
.

Assume that GRH is true. Let
n=n_{0}n_{1}^{2}
be a positive integer with
n_{0}
the square-free part of
n
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}(n)=\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid n}\Big(\frac{p}{p+1}\Big)\mathrm{1}_{n=\square}+O\Big(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}g_{1}\left(n_{0}\right)g_{2}\left(n_{1}\right)\Big),

where
g_{1}(n_{0})=\exp\big(\left(\log n_{0}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}\big)
,
g_{2}\left(n_{1}\right)=\sum_{q\mid n_{1}}\mu^{2}(q)q^{-(1/2+\varepsilon)}
, and
\mathrm{1}_{n=\square}
indicates the indicator function of the square numbers.

The next one provides the approximate functional equation of the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions within the critical strip.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
17
]
, Proposition 2.1)
.

Let
\alpha
be a positive parameter. And let
G(s)
be any entire, even function bounded in any strip
|\operatorname{Re}(s)|\leq M
which normalized by
G(0)=1
. Then for
d\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

(2.1)

L\big(\tfrac{1}{2}+\alpha,\chi_{d}\big)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}}U_{\alpha}\Big(\frac{n}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)+Y_{\alpha}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}}U_{-\alpha}\Big(\frac{n}{\sqrt{d}}\Big),

where

U_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1)}\frac{G(s)}{\pi^{\frac{s}{2}}}\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha+s}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}{2}\big)}x^{-s}\frac{\mathrm{d}s}{s}\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{\alpha}=\Big(\frac{d}{\pi}\Big)^{-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}{2}\big)}.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Constructing the resonator

We use the resonance method to study large values of
L
-functions near the central point. Let
N
be a large number that will be chosen later, and

\sigma=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{A}{\log_{2}N}.

Following the ideas of
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
]
, we proceed with the following construction.
Let
\gamma\in(0,(e-1)^{-1})
be a parameter to be chosen later. Define
\mathcal{P}
as the set of prime numbers
p
such that

e\log N\log_{2}N<p\leq\exp\left(\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\gamma}\right)\log N\log_{2}N.

Then define the multiplicative function
f(n)
to be supported on the set of square-free numbers, with values for
p\in\mathcal{P}
as

f(p)=\bigg(\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{\sigma}}{(\log_{3}N)^{1-\sigma}}\bigg)\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}(\log p-\log_{2}N-\log_{3}N)}.

If
p\notin\mathcal{P},\,f(p)=0.

For
k\in\left\{1,\cdots,\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor\right\},
we define
\mathcal{P}_{k}
as the set of prime numbers
p
such that

e^{k}\log N\log_{2}N<p\leq e^{k+1}\log N\log_{2}N.

Fix a real number
a
with the condition
e-1<a<1/\gamma,
then define

\mathcal{M}_{k}:=\left\{n\in\mathrm{supp}(f):n\text{ has at least }\Delta_{k}:=\frac{a(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}\text{ prime divisors in }\mathcal{P}_{k}\right\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k}
be the set of integers from
\mathcal{M}_{k}
that have prime divisors only in
\mathcal{P}_{k}.
Set

\mathcal{M}:=\mathrm{supp}(f)\setminus\bigcup_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\mathcal{M}_{k}.

Thus,
\mathcal{M}
is divisor closed. Specifically, if
m^{\prime}\mid m\in\mathcal{M},
then
m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}.
Furthermore, according to the similar process as
[
5
, Lemma 8]
, we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}|

\displaystyle\leq\exp((a\gamma+o(1))(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}(\log_{3}N)^{2\sigma-1})

\displaystyle\leq\exp((a\gamma+o(1))\log N(\log_{3}N)^{\frac{2A}{\log_{2}N}})\leq N.

Finally, in view of the definition of
\mathcal{M},
we can bound every
m\in\mathcal{M}
by

(3.1)

m\ll\exp\bigg(\frac{\log N\log_{3}N}{\log_{2}N}\bigg).

According to the heuristic description by Bondarenko and Seip in
[
2
]
, it is further necessary to construct a set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
such that certain parts contribute larger. However, by
[
8
]
, our choice is simpler compared with the constructions in
[
2
,
5
]
. Directly setting
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
to be identical with the above
\mathcal{M}
enables us to prove Theorem
1.1
.

We define the resonator
R_{d}
as

R_{d}:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}r(m)\chi_{d}(m).

Then for
n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
if we set

r(n)^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\\
mn=\square\end{subarray}}f(m)^{2},

by the construction of
\mathcal{M}
we have
r(n)=f(n),
so

R_{d}=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}}f(m)\chi_{d}(m).

We define multiplicative function
h(n)=\prod_{p\mid n}\big(\frac{p}{p+1}\big)
for
n\geq 2
and
h(1)=1.

Set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{N}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}h(n)\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1+f(p)^{2}h(p)+f(p)h(p)p^{-\sigma}}{1+f(p)^{2}}.

Then we have the following propositions.

Proposition 3.1
.

We have

\mathcal{A}_{N}\geq\exp\bigg((\delta\gamma+o(1))\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}}\bigg)

for
0<\delta<1
as
N\to+\infty.

Proof.

Since
f(p)<\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{\sigma-1}
for
p\in\mathcal{P},
we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{N}=\exp\bigg((1+o(1))\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\sigma}}\Big(\frac{p}{p+1}\Big)\bigg)=\exp\bigg((1+o(1))\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\sigma}}\bigg).

By the definition of
f(p)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{\sigma}}{(\log_{3}N)^{1-\sigma}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}\left(\log p-\log_{2}N-\log_{3}N\right)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{\sigma}}{(\log_{3}N)^{1-\sigma}}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\frac{1}{(k+1)p^{2\sigma}}.

By
[
5
, Lemma 9]
, we get the following lower bound

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}}>(\delta+o(1))\frac{1}{(\log_{2}N)^{2\sigma}},

where
0<\delta<1.
So

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\frac{f(p)}{p^{\sigma}}\geq(\delta\gamma+o(1))\frac{\left(\log N\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{3}N\right)^{\sigma}}{\left(\log_{2}N\right)^{\sigma}}

and we complete the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

We have

\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)h(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}=o\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)

as
N\to+\infty.

Proof.

Since
h(n)\leq 1,
we obtain

\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)h(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\\
n\notin\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}.

Next, following the similar way as
[
2
, Lemma 2]
, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N},n\notin\mathcal{M}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{k}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\left(1+f(p)^{2}+f(p)p^{-\sigma}\right)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\left(1+f(p)^{2}\right)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}f(n)^{2}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{f(p)p^{\sigma}}\Big).

By the definition of the set
\mathcal{P}_{k}
and
k\leq\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor,
we can get

\displaystyle\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{f(p)p^{\sigma}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1+(k+1)\frac{(\log_{3}N)^{1-\sigma}}{(\log N)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{\sigma}}\bigg)^{e^{k+1}\log N}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(o\bigg(\frac{(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}}{(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}\bigg)\frac{1}{k^{2}}\bigg).

Then by the definition of the set
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k}
and the fact that
f(n)
is a multiplicative function, we obtain

\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k}}f(n)^{2}\leqslant b^{-\Delta_{k}}\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}(1+bf(p)^{2})

whenever
b>1.
Thus,

\frac{1}{\prod_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\left(1+f(p)^{2}\right)}\sum_{n\in\mathcal{M}_{k}^{\prime}}f(n)^{2}\leq b^{-\Delta_{k}}\exp\Big((b-1)\sum_{p\in\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{k}}f(p)^{2}\Big).

Recalling the definition of
f(p)
for
p\in\mathcal{P},
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}f(p)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{2\sigma}}{(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}\left(\log p-\log_{2}N-\log_{3}N\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}(\log_{2}N)^{2\sigma}}{k^{2}(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}\frac{1}{p^{2\sigma}}.

According to
[
5
, Lemma 9]
,

\sum_{p\in\mathcal{P}_{k}}f(p)^{2}\leq\left((e-1)+o(1)\right)\frac{(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}.

Combining with (
3.1
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}_{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N},n\notin\mathcal{M}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{q\mid n}f(q)q^{\sigma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor(\log_{2}N)^{\gamma}\rfloor}\exp\Big(\big((e-1)(b-1)-a\log b+o(1)\big)\frac{(\log N)^{2-2\sigma}}{k^{2}(\log_{3}N)^{2-2\sigma}}\Big).

Noticing that
a>e-1,
we can choose
b
to be sufficiently close to 1 such that the exponent is negative. The proof is completed.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

We have

\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f(n)h(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\leq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}f(q)q^{\sigma}=o\left(\mathcal{A}_{N}\right)

as
N\to+\infty,
where the implicit constant only depends on
\varepsilon.

Proof.

Since
h(n)\leq 1,
we obtain

\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f(n)h(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\leq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}f(q)q^{\sigma}\leq\frac{1}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(n)^{2}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{f(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\mid n\\
q\leq n/N^{\varepsilon}\end{subarray}}f(q)q^{\sigma}.

Given that
f(n)
is a multiplicative function and
\sigma\geq 1/2,
the proof can then be completed by invoking the same method as
[
2
, Lemma 3]
.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For convenience, we let
\sigma=1/2+\sigma_{A}
, where
\sigma_{A}=A/\log_{2}N
.

To use the resonance method, we define the following two sums

\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\mathcal{S}_{1}(R_{d},\chi_{d})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma_{A},\chi_{d}\Big)R_{d}^{2}

and

\mathcal{S}_{2}:=\mathcal{S}_{2}(R_{d},\chi_{d})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}R_{d}^{2}.

Plainly, we have

(3.4)

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}L\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\sigma_{A},\chi_{d}\Big)\geq\frac{S_{1}}{S_{2}}.

First, we need to deal with
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and obtain its effective lower bound. Employing the definition of
R_{d}
and Lemma
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}f(m)f(n)\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma_{A}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}(\ell mn)U_{\sigma_{A}}\Big(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+Y_{\sigma_{A}}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}f(m)f(n)\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{1}{{\ell}^{\frac{1}{2}-\sigma_{A}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X<|d|\leq 2X\\
d\in\mathcal{F}\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}(\ell mn)U_{-\sigma_{A}}\Big(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{d}}\Big)

(3.5)

\displaystyle:=J_{\sigma_{A}}+Y_{\sigma_{A}}J_{\sigma_{A}}.

To handle each term in (
3.2
), we estimate the integral
U_{\sigma_{A}}(x)
. Since the function
G(s)
in Lemma
2.2
is bounded, we may without loss of generality assume that
G(1)=1
. Using a similar method as
[
15
, Lemma 2.1]
, we can obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
U_{\alpha}(x)
be the integral defined in Lemma
2.2
. Then
U_{\alpha}(x)
is a real-valued function, and is smooth on the positive real axis
(0,+\infty).
Moreover, for small
x,\,U_{\alpha}(x)=1+O(x^{1/2-\varepsilon}),
and for large
x,\,\,U_{\alpha}(x)\ll e^{-x}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we divide the inner sum of
J_{\sigma_{A}}
into two cases:
\ell mn=\square
and
\ell mn\neq\square.
Then by the upper bound of
U_{\sigma_{A}}(x)
in Proposition
3.4
we obtain

(3.6)

J_{\sigma_{A}}=\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\sum_{m,n\in\mathcal{M}}f(m)f(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
\ell mn=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma_{A}}}\prod_{p\mid\ell mn}\Big(\frac{p}{p+1}\Big)\int_{1}^{2}U_{\sigma_{A}}\Big(\frac{\ell}{\sqrt{Xt}}\Big)\mathrm{d}t+O\left(\mathcal{E}\right),

where the error term is

\mathcal{E}=X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}\frac{e^{-\ell X^{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\ell^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma_{A}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\in\mathcal{M}\\
\ell mn=k_{1}k_{2}^{2}\\
\mu\left(k_{1}\right)\neq 0\end{subarray}}f(m)f(n)g_{1}\left(k_{1}\right)g_{2}\left(k_{2}\right).

Since (
3.1
), we have the following upper bound

(3.7)

g_{1}(k_{1})=\exp\big(\left(\log k_{1}\right)^{1-\varepsilon}\big)\ll\exp\Big(\Big(\frac{\log N\log_{2}N}{\log_{3}N}\Big)^{1-\varepsilon}\Big)\ll N^{\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}.

Furthermore, it follows from the definition of the Möbius function that
g_{2}
is a multiplicative function and satisfies
g_{2}(xy)\leq g_{2}(x)g_{2}(y)
for
x,y\geq 1.
Thus,

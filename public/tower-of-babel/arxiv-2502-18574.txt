Dicke subsystems are entangled
Szilárd Szalay, Péter Nyári

Abstract
We show that all reduced states of nonproduct symmetric Dicke states of arbitrary number of qu d d its are genuinely multipartite entangled, and of nonpositive partial transpose with respect to any subsystem.



Entanglement is the most remarkable manifestation of the nonclassical correlations in quantum systems
[
1
,
2
]
,
still puzzling the community for almost hundred years
[
3
]
.
Deciding if a mixed quantum state is entangled or separable is a notoriously difficult problem,
most of the known criteria are necessary but not sufficient for separability
[
2

4

5

A particularly strong separability criterion is the Peres-Horodecki, or partial transpose criterion
[
6

7
]

It states that
if a quantum state is separable then its partial transposition is positive semidefinite (PPT),
or contrapositively,
if the partial transposition of a quantum state is not positive semidefinite (NPT) then the state is entangled.
Since the reverse implication is not true in general,
there are also entangled states of positive partial transpose
[
8
,
5
,
9
,
10
,
11

.
This PPT-entanglement is considered to be a weaker form of entanglement.

A bipartite quantum state can either be separable or entangled
[

,
12
,
2
]

while in the multipartite case entanglement shows a rich structure with many exciting features
[
13
,
14
]
.
A natural generalization of the separable/entangled dichotomy to multipartite systems
is the partial separability classification
[

16
,

[

20
]
,

or

(GME).
Deciding if a mixed quantum state possesses a particular multipartite entanglement property is an even more difficult problem
than in the bipartite case.

A particularly interesting problem in the multipartite setting is that
the entanglement inside the subsystems does not follow from the entanglement of the whole system.
The paradigmatic example of this is given by the three-qubit GHZ state
\bigl(|000\rangle+|111\rangle\bigr)/\sqrt{2}

and W state
\bigl(|001\rangle+|010\rangle+|100\rangle\bigr)/\sqrt{3}
,
both of which are fully entangled,
but the two-qubit subsystems of the GHZ state are separable,
while those of the W state are entangled
[
21
]
.
Symmetric Dicke states are the generalizations of the W state,
and our result is the generalization of this.

In this work we consider mixed quantum states arising as reduced states of pure multiqu
d
it symmetric Dicke states,
and we show that these states are genuinely multipartite entangled,
moreover, this entanglement is also NPT (not PPT) with respect to any subsystem.
This holds for all nontrivial cases, i.e.,
when there are at least two nonzero occupations in the Dicke state of the whole system.

A quantum state

We call a state

\xi

It is clear that if a state is partition-separable then it is also bipartition-separable,
since it is separable with respect to all bipartitions
\upsilon=\{Y,\overline{Y}\}

This is because we are always allowed not to take into account some of the tensorproduct symbols

then the state

X\subseteq\overline{Y}\end{subarray}}\rho_{X,i}\Bigr)=\sum_{i}w_{i}\rho_{Y,i}\otimes\rho_{\overline{Y},i}.

(2)

A state is

Genuinely multipartite entangled

In this multipartite case, the

[

\text{$\rho$ is $\{Y,\overline{Y}\}$-sep.}\;\;\Longrightarrow\;\;\rho^{\mathrm{T}_{Y}}\geq 0,

(A_{Y}\otimes B_{\overline{Y}})^{\mathrm{T}_{Y}}=A_{Y}^{\mathrm{T}}\otimes B_{\overline{Y}}

of the matrix of the operator

in a fixed basis,
that is,
\langle i|A_{Y}^{\mathrm{T}}|j\rangle=\langle j|A_{Y}|i\rangle

Although the map

is given with respect to a fixed local basis,
the positivity of

and therefore the criterion (

\begin{split}&\bigl(\overline{\alpha}\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\otimes\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|+\overline{\beta}\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|\otimes\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\bigr)\Bigl(|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\otimes|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\Bigr)\bigl(\alpha|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle\otimes|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle+\beta|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle\otimes|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle\bigr)\\
&=\overline{\alpha}\alpha\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle+\overline{\alpha}\beta\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle\\
&+\overline{\beta}\alpha\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle+\overline{\beta}\beta\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\text{D}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\rangle.\end{split}

To obtain
\langle\psi|\rho_{{\boldsymbol{n}},m}^{\mathrm{T}_{k}}|\psi\rangle
,
we have to deal with the summations in the state (
12
)
and the Kronecker-
\delta
s, coming from the orthonormality of the Dicke state vectors above.
(Remember that
{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}
are summation indices in the state (
12
),
and
\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}
are free-to-choose parameters of the ansatz (
13
).)

For the first term, we have the factors

\delta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\delta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}
,
this is nonzero iff
{\boldsymbol{k}}={\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
and
{\boldsymbol{m}}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{\Delta}
,
where
\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
.
That is, the parameters
\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}
are needed to be such that

\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
and

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}

to meet the indices
{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}
in the sum to make this term to be nonzero.

For the second term, we have the factors

\delta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},{\boldsymbol{k}}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\delta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\delta_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}
,
this is nonzero iff
{\boldsymbol{k}}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
,
{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}
and
{\boldsymbol{m}}=\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}
.
That is, the parameters
\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}
are needed to be such that

\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
and

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}

to meet the indices
{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}
in the sum to make this term to be nonzero.

The third and the fourth terms can be dealt with similarly,
and finally we have

\begin{split}&\langle\psi|\rho_{{\boldsymbol{n}},m}^{\mathrm{T}_{k}}|\psi\rangle=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}}\eta^{\boldsymbol{n}}_{\boldsymbol{m}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{k}},{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\sqrt{\eta^{\boldsymbol{m}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\sqrt{\eta^{\boldsymbol{m}}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}}\langle\psi|\Bigl(|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}|\otimes|\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\prime}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{{\boldsymbol{m}}-{\boldsymbol{k}}}|\Bigr)|\psi\rangle\\
&=\overline{\alpha}\alpha\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\bigr)+\overline{\alpha}\beta\eta^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\sqrt{\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)\\
&+\overline{\beta}\alpha\eta^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\sqrt{\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)+\overline{\beta}\beta\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\bigr),\end{split}

where the symbol
\delta({\boldsymbol{a}}\in A)
gives
1
if
{\boldsymbol{a}}\in A
and
0
otherwise.
This expression is a Hermitian form of nonnegative coefficients in the two complex variables
\alpha
and
\beta
,
which can take negative values if and only if its discriminant

\begin{split}\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)&\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\bigr)\\
&\qquad\qquad-(\eta^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})^{2}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}\bigr)\end{split}

is negative.
For this, the second term is necessarily nonvanishing,
so the parameters in (
13
) are restricted to

{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}}
and

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}
.
It is easy to see that in this case

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}
and

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}
also hold
(e.g., if
\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\leq\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}
then
{\boldsymbol{0}}\leq\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}
,
so
\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}\leq\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}
),
so the negativity condition on the discriminant reads as

\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}}\\
\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)<(\eta^{{\boldsymbol{n}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}})^{2}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}}\eta^{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}_{\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}},

assuming
{\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}}
and

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}
.
Substituting the Schmidt coefficients (
10b
), the inequality is simplified,

\binom{n-m}{{\boldsymbol{n}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\binom{n-m}{{\boldsymbol{n}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}}\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)\delta\bigl(\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}+\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}\bigr)<\binom{n-m}{{\boldsymbol{n}}-\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}^{2}.

This obviously holds if any of the two conditions
\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\pm\hat{{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
is violated,
however, in some cases this may not be guaranteed,
so we proceed in a different way.
If there are at least two nonzero occupations in
{\boldsymbol{n}}
, say
n_{i},n_{j}\neq 0
,
then we can always choose

{\boldsymbol{m}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
such that
m_{i},m_{j}\neq 0
,
and then

\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}},\hat{{\boldsymbol{k}}}^{\prime}\in I^{d}_{k,\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}}
such that they differ only in those two positions,
so
\hat{\Delta}_{i}=1
,
\hat{\Delta}_{j}=-1
and
0
elsewhere,
for which the inequality reads as

\frac{(n-m)!}{(n_{i}-\hat{m}_{i}+1)!(n_{j}-\hat{m}_{j}-1)!}\frac{(n-m)!}{(n_{i}-\hat{m}_{i}-1)!(n_{j}-\hat{m}_{j}+1)!}<\biggl(\frac{(n-m)!}{(n_{i}-\hat{m}_{i})!(n_{j}-\hat{m}_{j})!}\biggr)^{2},

which is

\frac{n_{i}-\hat{m}_{i}}{n_{i}-\hat{m}_{i}+1}\frac{n_{j}-\hat{m}_{j}}{n_{j}-\hat{m}_{j}+1}<1,

which holds for every
\hat{{\boldsymbol{m}}}\in I^{d}_{m,{\boldsymbol{n}}}
.
This concludes the proof.

Summing up, we have that
if there are at least two nonzero occupations in
{\boldsymbol{n}}
,
then the
m
-partite reduced Dicke state
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{n}},m}=\Tr_{n-m}\bigl(|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{n}}|\bigr)
for all
2\leq m\leq n

is NPT for all splits,
\rho_{{\boldsymbol{n}},m}^{\mathrm{T}_{k}}\not\geq 0
for all
1\leq k\leq m-1
,
then the Peres-Horodecki criterion for symmetric states (
4
) tells us that it is NPT-GME.
(Note that this holds also for the original Dicke state
|\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{n}}\rangle\langle\text{D}_{\boldsymbol{n}}|
, which is the
m=n
case,
although this is easier to see directly by the Schmidt rank
|I^{d}_{k,{\boldsymbol{n}}}|>1
, see (
9
).)
Otherwise, if there is only one occupation in
{\boldsymbol{n}}
, then the Dicke state
and also all the reduced Dicke states are fully separable pure states
|i,i,\dots,i\rangle\langle i,i,\dots,i|
.

Note that for some small systems,
mixtures of Dicke states are known to be entangled
if and only if
NPT,
so there is no PPT-entanglement,
however, this does not hold in general
[
61
,
62
,
63
]
.
PPT-entanglement is considered to be a weaker form of entanglement,
based on that
PPT-entangled states are bound-entangled, that is, no pure entangled states can be distilled from them
[
64
]
,
and it is conjectured that their Schmidt number, although scaling linearly with the local dimension
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
, cannot be maximal.
(For qutrits, this conjecture
[
65
]
has been proven
[
66
]
.)
Although we have seen that the reduced Dicke states are NPT-entangled,
there are further important open questions about the strength of this entanglement
compared to the weakness of PPT-entanglement,
e.g., regarding Schmidt number or distillability.

Generalizations of Certain Classes of Single-Valued Mappings to Multivalued Cases Thanks: This paper is in final form and no version of it will be submitted for publication elsewhere.
Emirhan HACIOĞLU

Abstract
. In this study, multivalued generalizations of certain classes of single-valued transformations defined on metric spaces are obtained. Building upon recently introduced concepts such as mappings contracting perimeters of triangles, new structural properties and fixed-point results for the multivalued counterparts are explored.

45J05, 47H10

1.
Introduction

Fixed point theory for contractive-type mappings remains a vibrant area of
research with broad applications in analysis, optimization, and applied
sciences. The classical Banach contraction
[
3
]
principle laid the
foundation, motivating multiple generalizations such as Kannan-type and
Chatterjea-type contractions
[
1
,
2
]
Petrov
[
4
]
investigated mappings contracting total pairwise distances of
n

points, establishing periodic point results in complete metric spaces.
Popescu
[
6
]
introduced three-point generalized orbital triangular
contractions, proving convergence under minimal continuity assumptions.
However, certain proof details and multivalued cases were left incomplete.

This paper merges and enhances these approaches. Our main contributions
include:

•

Proposing a unified framework that encompasses perimeter-contracting,
total pairwise distance, and generalized orbital triangular contractions,
covering multivalued mappings.

•

Defining and give some existence theoresm for multivalued orbital
triangular contraction theorems .

2.
Preliminaries

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. For nonempty closed
bounded sets
A,B\subset X
, the
\delta
-distance is defined by

\delta(A,B)=\sup_{a\in A,\,b\in B}d(a,b).

We denote by
CB(X)
the family of all nonempty closed bounded subsets of
X
.

Definition 1
.

[
5
]
Let
(X,d)
be a metric space with
|X|>3
. A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
is said to **contract the perimeters of triangles** if
there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that for all distinct
x,y,z\in X
,

(2.1)

d(Tx,Ty)+d(Ty,Tz)+d(Tx,Tz)\leq\alpha\left(d(x,y)+d(y,z)+d(x,z)\right).

Remark 1
.

[
5
]
The requirement that
x,y,z
be pairwise distinct is
essential. Without it, the definition becomes equivalent to contraction
mappings.

Proposition 1
.

[
5
]
Mappings that contract the perimeters of triangles are
continuous.

Theorem 1
.

[
5
]
Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space with
|X|>3
, and let
T:X\rightarrow X
satisfy:

(i)

T(T(x))\neq x
whenever
Tx\neq x
,

(ii)

T
contracts the perimeters of triangles.

Then
T
has a fixed point. Moreover, the number of fixed points is at most
two.

Remark 2
.

[
5
]
If
x^{\ast}
is the limit of an iteration sequence and was
not fixed earlier, then
x^{\ast}
is the unique fixed point.

Definition 2
.

A mapping
T:X\to X
on a metric space
(X,d)
is called a
contraction mapping
if there exists a constant
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that
for all
x,y\in X
,

(2.2)

d(Tx,Ty)\leq\alpha\,d(x,y).

3.
Multivalued Generalization of Mappings Contracting Total Pairwise
Distances

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space with
|X|\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
. Define the
total pairwise distance:

S(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}d(x_{i},x_{j}).

Definition 3
.

[
4
]
A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
multivalued contracting the total
pairwise distances between
n
points if there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)

such that for all pairwise distinct
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in X
,

S(Tx_{1},\dotsc,Tx_{n})\leq\alpha S(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}).

Let
X_{i}\subseteq CB(X)
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. We define the total
pairwise distance on
CB(X)
as follows:

S(X_{1},\dotsc,X_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\delta(X_{i},X_{j}).

Definition 4
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is multivalued contracting the total
pairwise distances between
n
points if there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)

such that

S(Tx_{1},\dotsc,Tx_{n})\leq\alpha S(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})

for all pairwise distinct
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\in X
.

Proposition 2
.

If
T
satisfies the above inequality for any
n
points with
|\{x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n}\}|=k
where
2\leq k\leq n-1
, then
T
contracts the
total pairwise distances between
k
points.

Proof.

Let
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}
be a set of pairwise distinct points
from
X
. Consider
k
sets
A_{i}=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k},x_{k+1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
consisting of
n
points such that
x_{k+1}=\cdots=x_{n}=x_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
, i.e., the element
x_{i}

occurs
n-k+1
times in the set
A_{i}
. Then, by the supposition, the
inequalities

S(Tx_{1},Tx_{2},\ldots,Tx_{k},Tx_{i},\ldots,Tx_{i})\leq\alpha S(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k},x_{i},\ldots,x_{i})

hold for all
i=1,\ldots,k
. Summing both sides over
i
, we get:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}S(Tx_{1},\ldots,Tx_{k},Tx_{i},\ldots,Tx_{i})\leq\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{k}S(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x_{i},\ldots,x_{i}).

∎

Note that

S(Tx_{i},\ldots,Tx_{i})=S(x_{i},\ldots,x_{i})=0,

and

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\delta(Tx_{j},Tx_{i})=2S(Tx_{1},\ldots,Tx_{k}),\quad\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}d(x_{j},x_{i})=2S(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}).

So, we obtain:

(k+2(n-k))S(Tx_{1},\ldots,Tx_{k})\leq\alpha(k+2(n-k))S(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}).

Dividing both sides by
k+2(n-k)
, we conclude:

S(Tx_{1},\ldots,Tx_{k})\leq\alpha S(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}).

Thus,
T
is a mapping contracting the total pairwise distances between
k

points.

Proposition 3
.

If
T
is a multivalued mapping contracting the total pairwise distances
between
m
points,
m\geq 2
, then
T
also contracts the total pairwise
distances between
n
points for all
n>m
.

Proof.

Similar steps are given in
[
4
]
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space with
|X|\geq n
, and
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
a multivalued mapping contracting the total pairwise distances
between
n
points. Then
T
has a periodic point of prime period
k\in\{1,\dotsc,n-1\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
T
has no periodic point of prime period
k\in\{1,\dotsc,n-1\}
.
Then
T
has no fixed points. Let
x_{0}\in X
, and
x_{i+1}\in Tx_{i}
for
all
i\geq 0
. Since
x_{i}\notin Tx_{i}
, then
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}\in Tx_{i}
.

For
n=2
or
n=3
:

Since
T
has no periodic points of prime period
2
and no fixed points, we
conclude that the points
x_{i},x_{i+1},x_{i+2}
are pairwise distinct.

For
n\geq 4
:

The absence of periodic points of prime periods
2
and
3
implies that
x_{i},x_{i+1},x_{i+2},x_{i+3}
are also pairwise distinct.

Repeating this reasoning
n-4
more times, we deduce that the points

x_{i},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{i+n-1}

are pairwise distinct for every
i\geq 0
.

Now, letting
p=S(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})
, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(x_{n},x_{n+1},\dots,x_{2n-1})=\sum_{n\leq i<j\leq 2n-1}d(x_{i},x_{j})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq i<j\leq 2n-1}\delta(Tx_{i-1},Tx_{j-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n},\dots,Tx_{2n-2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha S(x_{n-1},x_{n},\dots,x_{2n-2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}S(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}p.

∎

Using the triangle inequality: for any positive integers
m,n
with
m>n
,
we have

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{m}d(x_{k},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{m}\alpha^{k}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha^{k-n}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}p

which implies that
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence
and hence converges in the complete metric space
X
. Let us denote
\lim x_{n}=x_{\ast}
. Then we have, by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle d(x_{n},Tx_{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle S(Tx_{\ast},Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n-2},\dots,Tx_{2n-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha S(x_{\ast},x_{n-1},x_{n-2},\dots,x_{2n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\sum_{n-1\leq i<j\leq 2n-1}^{k}d(x_{i},x_{j})+\alpha\sum_{n-1\leq i\leq 2n-1}^{k}d(x_{i},x_{\ast})

which implies
x_{\ast}\in Tx_{\ast}
and contradicts our assumption.

Definition 5
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
multivalued contracting perimeters of
triangles if there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that for all distinct
x,y,z\in X
,

\delta(Tx,Ty)+\delta(Ty,Tz)+\delta(Tx,Tz)\leq\alpha(d(x,y)+d(y,z)+d(x,z)).

Corollary 1
.

[
4
]
Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space with
|X|\geq n
, and

T:X\rightarrow X
a mapping contracting the total pairwise distances
between
n
points. Then
T
has a periodic point of prime period
k\in\{1,\dotsc,n-1\}
.

Corollary 2
.

[
5
]
If
T
contracts perimeters of triangles, then
T
has a
fixed point if and only if there are no periodic points of period 2.

Corollary 3
.

[
5
]
If
T
multivalued contracting perimeters of triangles, then
T
has a fixed point if and only if there are no periodic points of period 2.

Corollary 4
.

[
3
]
A contraction mapping on a nonempty complete metric space has
a unique fixed point.

4.
Generalized Orbital Triangular Contractions

Definition 6
.

[
6
]
Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X

is a generalized orbital triangular contraction if there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that:

d(Tx,T^{2}x)+d(T^{2}x,Ty)+d(Ty,Tx)\leq\alpha\left[d(x,Tx)+d(Tx,y)+d(y,x)\right],

for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y\neq Tx
.

Definition 7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a metric space. A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is a
multivalued generalized orbital triangular contraction if there
exists
\alpha\in[0,1)
such that:

\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)+\delta(T^{2}x,Ty)+\delta(Ty,Tx)\leq\alpha\left[d(x,Tx)+d(Tx,y)+d(x,y)\right],

for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y\notin Tx
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
a
multivalued generalized orbital triangular contraction with no periodic
points of prime period 2. Then
T
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and define the Picard iteration
x_{n+1}\in Tx_{n}
.
Assume
x_{n-1}\neq x_{n}\neq x_{n+1}\notin Tx_{n},x_{n}\notin Tx_{n-1}
for all
n
. Let
x=x_{n-1},y=x_{n+1}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n},x_{n+2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(Tx_{n-1},T^{2}x_{n-1})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n-1},Tx_{n+1})+\delta(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[d(x_{n-1},Tx_{n-1})+d(Tx_{n-1},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n-1},x_{n+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[d(x_{n-1},x_{n})+d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n-1},x_{n+1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}\left[d(x_{0},x_{1})+d(x_{1},x_{2})+d(x_{0},x_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}p

where
p=d(x_{0},x_{1})+d(x_{1},x_{2})+d(x_{0},x_{2})
.

For any positive integers
m,n
with
m>n
, we have

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{m}d(x_{k},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{m}\alpha^{k}p

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha^{k-n}p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}p

which implies that
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence
and hence converges in the complete metric space
X
. Let
\lim x_{n}=x_{\ast}
. Then we have

\displaystyle d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+1},Tx_{\ast})+d(x_{n},Tx_{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(Tx_{n-1},T^{2}x_{n-1})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n-1},Tx_{\ast})+\delta(Tx_{n-1},Tx_{\ast})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[d(x_{n-1},Tx_{n-1})+d(Tx_{n-1},x_{\ast})+d(x_{n-1},x_{\ast})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\left[d(x_{n-1},x_{n})+d(x_{n},x_{\ast})+d(x_{n-1},x_{\ast})\right].

Then we have

d(x_{\ast},Tx_{\ast})=0.

∎

Corollary 5
.

[
6
]
Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X

a generalized orbital triangular contraction with no periodic points of
prime period 2. Then
T
has a unique fixed point.

5.
Generalized Orbital Triangular Kannan Contractions

Definition 8
.

[
6
]
A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
is a generalized orbital
triangular Kannan contraction if there exists
\beta\in\left[0,\frac{2}{3}\right)
such that:

d(Tx,T^{2}x)+d(T^{2}x,Ty)+d(Ty,Tx)\leq\beta\left[d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty)+d(Tx,T^{2}x)\right],

for all
x,y\in X
with
x,y,Tx
pairwise distinct.

Definition 9
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is a multivalued generalized orbital
triangular Kannan contraction if there exists
\beta\in\left[0,\frac{2}{3}\right)
such that:

\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)+\delta(T^{2}x,Ty)+\delta(Ty,Tx)\leq\beta\left[d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty)+\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)\right],

for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y
and
x,y\notin Tx
pairwise distinct.

Remark 3
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is a multivalued generalized orbital
triangular Kannan contraction satisfies

(1-\beta)\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)+\delta(T^{2}x,Ty)+\delta(Ty,Tx)\leq\beta\left[d(x,Tx)+d(y,Ty)\right].

Theorem 4
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
a multivalued generalized orbital triangular
Kannan contraction with no periodic points of prime period 2. Then
T
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and define the Picard iteration
x_{n+1}\in Tx_{n}
. Assume

x_{n}\neq x_{n+1}\neq x_{n+2}
for all
n
. Let
x=x_{n},y=x_{n+2}
. Then
we have

\displaystyle(1-\beta)d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})+d(x_{n+3},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\delta(Tx_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n},Tx_{n+2})+\delta(Tx_{n+2},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left[d(x_{n},Tx_{n})+d(x_{n+2},Tx_{n+2})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left[d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\right],

which implies that

(1-\beta)\left(d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\right)+d(x_{n+3},x_{n+1})\leq\beta d(x_{n},x_{n+1}).

And we have

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\left(d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\right)+\left|d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})-d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\left(d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\right)+d(x_{n+3},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta d(x_{n},x_{n+1}).

If
d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})-d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\geq 0
then we have

(2-\beta)d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})-\beta d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\leq\beta d(x_{n},x_{n+1}),

which implies

d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})\leq\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}d(x_{n},x_{n+1}).

If
d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})-d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})>0
then we have

(2-\beta)d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})\leq(2-\beta)d(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})\leq\beta(d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})),

which implies

d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})\leq\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}d(x_{n},x_{n+1}).

Therefore, if we let
\alpha=\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}<1
we have

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha d(x_{n},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\dots

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{n+1}d(x_{0},x_{1}).

For any positive integers
m,n
with
m>n
we have

\displaystyle d(x_{m},x_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{m}d(x_{k},x_{k+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha^{k+1}d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{n+1}\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}\alpha^{k-n}d(x_{0},x_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{n+1}\frac{1}{1-\alpha}d(x_{0},x_{1}),

which implies that
\left\{x_{n}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
is a Cauchy sequence
and it converges in the complete metric space
X
. Let us say
\lim x_{n}=x_{\ast}
. Then we have

\displaystyle(1-\beta)d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},Tx_{\ast})+d(Tx_{\ast},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\delta(Tx_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n},Tx_{\ast})+\delta(Tx_{\ast},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left[d(x_{n},Tx_{n})+d(x_{\ast},Tx_{\ast})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\beta\left[d(x_{n},x_{n+1})+d(x_{\ast},Tx_{\ast})\right].

Then we have

(2-\beta)d(x_{\ast},Tx_{\ast})\leq 0.

Hence
x_{\ast}\in Tx_{\ast}.

∎

Corollary 6
.

[
6
]
In a complete metric space, every generalized orbital
triangular Kannan contraction without periodic points of period 2 has a
fixed point.

6.
Generalized Orbital Triangular Chatterjea Contractions

Definition 10
.

[
6
]
A mapping
T:X\rightarrow X
is a generalized orbital
triangular Chatterjea contraction if there exists
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
such that:

\displaystyle d(Tx,T^{2}x)+d(T^{2}x,Ty)+d(Ty,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[d(x,Ty)+d(y,Tx)+d(x,T^{2}x)+d(y,T^{2}x)+d(Tx,Ty)\right].

for all
x,y\in X
with
x\neq y\notin Tx
pairwise distinct.

Definition 11
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is a multivalued generalized orbital
triangular Chatterjea contraction if there exists
\gamma\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
such that:

\displaystyle\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)+\delta(T^{2}x,Ty)+\delta(Ty,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[d(x,Ty)+d(y,Tx)+d(x,T^{2}x)+d(y,T^{2}x)+\delta(Tx,Ty)\right].

Remark 4
.

A mapping
T:X\rightarrow CB(X)
is a multivalued generalized orbital
triangular Chatterjea contraction if it satisfies the following:

\displaystyle\delta(Tx,T^{2}x)+\delta(T^{2}x,Ty)+(1-\gamma)\delta(Ty,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[d(x,Ty)+d(y,Tx)+d(x,T^{2}x)+d(y,T^{2}x)\right].

Theorem 5
.

Let
(X,d)
be a complete metric space and
T:X\rightarrow X
a multivalued generalized orbital triangular
Chatterjea contraction with no periodic points of prime period 2. Then
T
has a fixed point.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and define the Picard iteration
x_{n+1}\in Tx_{n}
. Assume

x_{n}\neq x_{n+1}\neq x_{n+2}
for all
n
. Let
x=x_{n},y=x_{n+2}
. Then
we have:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+\delta(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})+(1-\gamma)d(x_{n+3},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(Tx_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n},Tx_{n+2})+(1-\gamma)\delta(Tx_{n+2},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[\begin{array}[]{c}d(x_{n},Tx_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},Tx_{n})\\
+d(x_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+d(x_{n+2},T^{2}x_{n})\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[\begin{array}[]{c}d(x_{n},x_{n+3})+d(x_{n+2},x_{n+1})\\
+d(x_{n},x_{n+2})\end{array}\right]

which implies that:

\displaystyle d(x_{n+1},x_{n+2})+\delta(x_{n+2},x_{n+3})+d(x_{n+3},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\delta(Tx_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+\delta(T^{2}x_{n},Tx_{n+2})+(1-\gamma)\delta(Tx_{n+2},Tx_{n})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma\left[\begin{array}[]{c}d(x_{n},Tx_{n+2})+d(x_{n+2},Tx_{n})\\
+d(x_{n},T^{2}x_{n})+d(x_{n+2},T^{2}x_{n})\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

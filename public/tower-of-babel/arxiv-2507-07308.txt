Volumes of moduli spaces of directed ribbon graphs and Cut-and-Join operators
Simon Barazer

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the algebraic structure underlying the acyclic decomposition introduced in [ Bar21 ] . This decomposition applies to directed metric ribbon graphs and enables the recursive computation of the volumes of their moduli spaces. Building on this, we define integral operators with these volumes and show that they satisfy a Cut-and-Join type equation. Furthermore, we demonstrate that a suitable specialization of these operators gives rise to a generating series for dessins d’enfants .

Introduction

In this paper, we study
directed ribbon graphs
, which were previously investigated in
[
Bar21
,
Yak23
]
. These are graphs embedded in a surface with boundary
[
Kon92
]
, equipped with a coherent orientation of the edges (Subsection
3.1
). Equivalently, their duals are bipartite maps, and then, their boundary components are labeled by
\pm
. A surface with boundaries labeled by
\pm
will be called a
directed surface
.

In
[
Bar21
]
, a decomposition of directed ribbon graphs into graphs with a single vertex was found, it induces a recursion formula for the volumes of moduli spaces of directed metric ribbon graphs. In the present work, we explore some algebraic aspects of this decomposition.

We focus specifically on the principal strata of these moduli spaces, which correspond to four-valent ribbon graphs. The general case will be presented in a forthcoming paper. The volume of the moduli space of directed four-valent ribbon graphs of genus
g
with
n^{\pm}
boundary components of type
\pm
is a function:

V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-}),

defined on the subset:

\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}=\{(L^{+},L^{-})|\sum_{j}L_{i}^{+}=\sum_{j}L_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n^{+}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n^{-}}.

These functions are continuous piece-wise polynomials. Here we do not study the functions
V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}
directly; instead, we adopt the following point of view. The presence of two sets of variables naturally gives rise to a family of integration kernels:

P\to\int_{L^{-}}\frac{V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})P(L^{-})}{n^{-}!}dL^{-}.

The key ingredient is Proposition
8
, which states that these operators act on the space of symmetric polynomials. This proposition follows from the biparticity of the dual of a directed ribbon graph. The factor
n^{-}!
is to avoid over-counting. We prefer to use the functions

K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}=\prod_{i}L_{i}^{+}V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-}).

In this case, the operator
K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}
has degree
4g-4+2n^{+}+2n^{-}
, which is twice the opposite of the Euler characteristic. This allows us to consider the action of

K_{n^{+},n^{-}}=\sum_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}q^{2g-2+n^{+}+n^{-}}K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}},

which defines an operator

K_{n^{+},n^{-}}:\hat{S}_{n^{-}}(V)\longrightarrow\hat{S}_{n^{+}}(V),

where
\hat{S}_{n}(V)
is the space of symmetric formal series in
n
variables and coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Our plan is to sum over
n^{+},n^{-}
and form a single operator. To deal with the fact that there is an arbitrary number of boundary components, we consider the action on the
formal Bosonic Fock space
:

\hat{S}(V)=\prod_{n}\hat{S}_{n}(V).

All the formalism is recalled in Section
2.2
. In the formal Fock space it makes sense to consider the operator

K^{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{s}}=\sum_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}q^{2g-2+n^{+}+n^{-}}K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}},

where we sum over stable surfaces, i.e.,
2g-2+n^{+}+n^{-}>0
. The formal Fock space is endowed with the symmetric product that will be denoted
\sqcup
; by using duality, this product is well defined for operators on
\hat{S}(V)
. The non-connected operator satisfies the classical formula:

K^{\mathfrak{s}}=\exp_{\sqcup}(K^{\mathfrak{c}\mathfrak{s}}).

It seems almost crucial to add the cylinder of type
(0,1,1)
, we define
K_{0,1,1}:\hat{S}_{1}(V)\to\hat{S}_{1}(V)
to be the identity. Finally, we will be interested in

where we include
(0,1,1)
in the sum. As we will see, the operator

can be written as

K=K^{\mathfrak{s}}\sqcup\exp_{\sqcup}(K_{0,1,1})=K^{\mathfrak{s}}\sqcup\operatorname{Id}.

It corresponds to adding an arbitrary number of cylinders. This allows much more gluing, and it leads to a simple expression of the recursion for the functions
K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}
. A choice of basis allows us to identify
\hat{S}(V)
and
\mathbb{Q}[[\mathbb{t}]]
, where
\mathbb{t}=(t_{0},t_{1},...)
is an infinite set of variables. Formula

implies that
K
is a formal series written in terms of
creation
and
annihilation
operators, and then, it acts on
\mathbb{Q}[[\mathbb{t}]]
as a formal differential operator (see Proposition

Partition function

It is quite natural to consider the partition function

Z(q,\mathbb{t})=K(q)\mathbb{e}_{\emptyset}.

Where the
vacuum
is given by
\mathbb{e}_{\emptyset}=\exp(t_{0})
. It appears that this series is a generating series for directed ribbon graphs or, equivalently,
Grothendieck Dessins d’enfants
(see
[
LZZ04
]
for instance), and is thus of combinatorial interest. A dessin d’enfant is a covering of the sphere ramified over three points
(x^{+},x_{0},x^{-})
. The polynomials:

Z_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+})=\int_{L^{+}}K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})\frac{dL^{-}}{n^{-}!},

can be written as

Z_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+})=\sum_{\bm{\alpha}^{+}}\frac{(L^{+})^{\bm{\alpha}^{+}}}{\bm{\alpha}^{+}!}h_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(\bm{\alpha}^{+}).

The coefficients
h_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(\bm{\alpha}^{+})
count the number of Grothendieck dessins d’enfants with labeled ramifications over
x^{+}
given by
\bm{\alpha}^{+}=(\alpha_{1}^{+},...,\alpha_{n^{+}}^{+})
, simple unlabeled ramifications over
x_{0}
and
n^{+}
arbitrary unlabeled ramifications over
x^{-}
. It can be seen as an element of
\mathbb{Q}[\mathbb{t}]
and the partition function is given by

Z=\exp(\sum_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}q^{2g-2+n^{+}+n^{-}}Z_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}).

Then, it is a generating series for a certain type of non-connected dessins d’enfants. From the definition of
Z
we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

The series
Z
is the solution of the Cut-and-Join equation

\frac{\partial Z}{\partial q}=W_{1}Z,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \penalty\ Z(0,\mathbb{t})=\exp(t_{0}).

It is also possible to obtain other classes of dessins d’enfants by considering different initial conditions but in this paper we restrict ourselves to the case where ramifications over
x_{0}
are simple and we cannot control the ramification over
x^{-}
. The Cut-and-Join equation for this family of dessins d’enfants is not new, and we retrieve results of
[
KZ15
]
.

Relation to topological recursion:

It is natural to ask what is the relation with E.O topological recursion
[

]

the Laplace transform of

W_{g,n}=\sum_{n^{-}}W_{g,n,n^{-}}.

We include terms of types
(0,1)

.

The series satisfies the loop equation:

\displaystyle x_{1}W_{g,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq 1}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\frac{W_{g,n-1}(x_{1},x_{\{1,i\}^{c}})-W_{g,n-1}(x_{i},x_{\{1,i\}^{c}})}{x_{1}-x_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle W_{g-1,n+1}(x_{1},x_{1},x_{\{1\}^{c}})+\sum_{I_{1},I_{2},g_{1}+g_{2}=g}W_{g_{1}-1,n_{1}+1}(x_{1},x_{I_{1}})W_{g_{2}-1,n_{2}+1}(x_{1},x_{I_{2}})+\delta_{g,0}\delta_{n,1}.

By using argument that are standard in the TR community and developed in

Corollary 2

The

can be computed by the topological recursion using the spectral curve

The operators

[L_{i},L_{j}]=(i-j)L_{i+j}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \forall i,j\geq-1.

A.1
Duality and scalar product:

Functions

have an additional symmetry that we can call times inversion. We have

V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})=V_{g,n^{-},n^{+}}(L^{-}|L^{+}).

This is interesting for the following reason: we can consider, for instance, the scalar product on

(f,g)=\frac{1}{n!}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n^{+}}}f(x)g(x)e^{-|x|}dx.

We then have the formula

(f,V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}\cdot g)=\int_{\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}}f(L^{+})V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})g(L^{-})e^{-|L^{+}|}\frac{d\sigma_{n^{+},n^{-}}}{n^{+}!\penalty\ n^{-}!}.

On
\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}
, we also have the relation
|L^{+}|=|L^{-}|
by definition, and this implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}}f(L^{+})V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})g(L^{-})e^{-|L^{+}|}\frac{d\sigma_{n^{+},n^{-}}}{n^{+}!n^{-}!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}}f(L^{+})V_{g,n^{-},n^{+}}(L^{-}|L^{+})g(L^{-})e^{-|L^{+}|}\frac{d\sigma_{n^{+},n^{-}}}{n^{+}!n^{-}!}

, we denote
\mathcal{M}^{comb}_{g,n,\mathbb{Z}}(\bm{\alpha})
the subset of metric ribbon graphs such that
L_{i}(S)=\alpha_{i}
for all
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. Then the Norbury polynomial is given by

N_{g,n}(\bm{\alpha})=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{M}^{comb}_{g,n,\mathbb{Z}}(\bm{\alpha})}\frac{1}{\#\text{Aut}(S)}.

The generating function for these polynomials is the Ehrhart series; it’s given by

\lx@nobreakspace F^{comb}_{g,n}(y)=\sum_{g,n}N_{g,n}(\bm{\alpha})y^{\bm{\alpha}}

and defined in
[
Nor13
]
. The aim of this paragraph is to give a proof of a surprising and beautiful formula that relies on the series
F^{comb}_{g,n}
and
W^{*}_{g,n}
. As in
[
Nor13
]
, we also denote

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\Omega_{g,n}=d_{1}...d_{n}N_{g,n}.

Where
d_{i}
is the differential with respect to the
i-th
variable.

Theorem 8
.

The generating series
F^{comb}_{g,n}
and
W^{*}
are related by the following formula:

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace F^{comb}_{g,n}(u(x))=W^{*}_{g,n}(x).

Where
u
is given by

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u(x)=\frac{x-\sqrt{x^{2}-4}}{2}.

Consecutively, we have the relation

W_{g,n}\otimes_{i}dx_{i}=u^{*}\Omega_{g,n}.

(26)

Proof.

Let
\text{rib}_{g,n,*}
be the set of ribbon graphs with
n
faces and possibly univalent vertices; let also
\text{rib}_{g,n}^{\prime}
be the set of ribbon graphs with no univalent vertices. There is a a contraction map

\lx@nobreakspace\text{Trunc}:\lx@nobreakspace\text{rib}_{g,n,*}\longrightarrow\text{rib}_{g,n},

which is, for instance, described in
[
Oko
]
, or in figure
6
. This map is a fibration, fiber consists of trees planted around vertices of the graphs. We can choose to conserve the bivalent vertices that are in the trunc of the graph. These bivalent vertices define a metric on the ribbon graph, and then this induces a surjection

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\text{rib}_{g,n,*}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{comb}_{g,n,\mathbb{Z}}.

Where
\mathcal{M}^{comb}_{g,n,\mathbb{Z}}
is the set of all integral metric ribbon graphs and the set of integral points in the combinatorial moduli space. This defines a new map

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\text{Trunc}^{\prime}:\text{rib}_{g,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}^{comb}_{g,n,\mathbb{Z}}.

For each
S
in the image, we can consider the formal series

\lx@nobreakspace T_{S}=\lx@nobreakspace\sum_{R,\text{Trunc}^{\prime}(R)=S}\frac{1}{\#\text{Aut}(R)}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(R)}},

and then we have the push-forward formula:

\sum_{R,Trunc^{\prime}(R)=S}\frac{1}{\#\text{Aut}(R)}\prod_{i}\frac{1}{x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}(R)}}=\sum_{S}\frac{T_{S}}{\#\text{Aut}(S)}.

(27)

Now we prove the following proposition:

Lemma 10
.

Near
\infty
, we have the formula

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace T_{S}=\prod_{i}u(x_{i})^{L_{i}(S)},

where
L_{i}
is the combinatorial length of the
i-th
boundary.

Proof.

Using figure
6
, we see that the fiber corresponds to a planar tree planted at each "corner" of the boundary. In the generating series, we add to remember the perimeter of the boundary, and then each tree
T
glued to the boundary
i
is weighted by
\frac{1}{x_{i}^{2\#X_{1}T+1}}
where
X_{1}T
is the set of edges in the boundary. Because each edge in the tree contributes twice to the boundary length, we also include the edge on the "left" of the tree. Then, to compute the preimage, we start by computing the generating series associated with a single corner in a given boundary
i
. Let
u_{0}
be the series

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u_{0}(x)=\sum_{T}\frac{1}{x^{2\#X_{1}T}}.

The sum is over all the planar tree with a marked vertex. This generating series is easily computable; indeed, by removing the roots, we can derive the following recurrence relation:

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u_{0}(x)=1+\frac{u_{0}(x)}{x^{2}}+\frac{u_{0}(x)^{2}}{x^{4}}+...=\frac{x^{2}}{x^{2}-u_{0}(x)}.

And then

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u_{0}(x)^{2}-x^{2}u_{0}(x)+x^{2}=0.

Solving this equation leads to the formula

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u_{0}(x)=\frac{x^{2}-\sqrt{x^{4}-4x^{2}}}{2},

where we use
u_{0}(x)=1+o(1)
. Then we obtain

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u(x)=\frac{u_{0}(x)}{x}=\frac{x-\sqrt{x^{2}-4}}{2}.

Then, to prove the lemma, the contribution of each corner is given by
u(x)
, and the contribution of the boundary
i
in
S
is

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace u(x)^{L_{i}(S)}.

Because there are exactly
L_{i}(S)
corners in the boundary
i
. As the contribution of the boundaries is independent, we obtain

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace T_{S}(x)=\prod_{i}u(x)^{L_{i}(S)}.

∎

Using this lemma, we finish the proof of
8
. It suffices to substitute the expression of
T_{S}(x)
in the formula
27
to find
F_{g,n}^{comb}(u(x))
. We have in one hand

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace W_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}^{*}(x)=\int_{\Lambda_{n^{+},n^{-}}}V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}},(L^{+}|L^{-})\exp(\sum_{i}x_{i}L^{+}_{i})d\sigma_{n^{+},n^{-}}

and, on the other hand

\lx@nobreakspace\lx@nobreakspace K_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-})=\prod_{i}L_{i}^{+}V_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(L^{+}|L^{-}).

Which leads to

\lx@nobreakspace W_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}(x)=\partial_{1}...\partial_{n^{+}}\lx@nobreakspace W_{g,n^{+},n^{-}}^{*}(x).

Summing over
n^{-}
gives the second claim of the theorem
8
.
∎

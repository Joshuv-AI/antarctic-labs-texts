Combined HIF-1α blockade and CHIR99021 treatment reverses pulmonary fibrosis via modulation endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition
Authors: Jae-Kyung Nam, Seo-Hyun Choi, Ji-Hee Kim, Kyu Jin Choi, Hae-June Lee, Jeeyong Lee, Haeng Ran Seo, Yoon-Jin Lee
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
SummaryRadiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis (RIPF), a serious complication of radiotherapy, is characterized by HIF-1α-dependent endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT) and persistent DNA damage. We demonstrate that the dual inhibition of HIF-1α and GSK-3β with 2-methoxyestradiol (2-ME) and CHIR99021 reverses fibrotic alterations in endothelial cells and pulmonary fibroblasts, accompanied by reduced DNA damage foci. In murine RIPF models, the therapy attenuated fibrosis, suppressed fibroblast activation, and preserved vascular structure. Epigenetic profiling revealed a transcriptionally active chromatin state with decreased H3K9 trimethylation and increased H3K9/H3K27 acetylation. HIF-1α deletion in collagen-expressing cells further enhanced CHIR99021-mediated reversal by restoring endothelial identity and inhibiting mesenchymal transition, as shown by lineage tracing and single-cell RNA sequencing. In fibroblasts from patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis, the therapy reduced profibrotic gene expression, proliferation, and matrix remodeling. These findings highlight dual HIF-1α/GSK-3β targeting to reverse fibrosis through coordinated EndMT suppression, DNA repair, and chromatin remodeling.Subject areas: Fibrosis, Molecular biology, Cell biology

Introduction

Radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis (RIPF) is a major long-term complication of radiation therapy for thoracic malignancies, significantly affecting patient outcomes. Extensive research has been conducted to regulate RIPF, and, similar to idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), various attempts have been made to develop drugs for fibrosis prevention.1 Pirfenidone and nintendanib, originally approved for IPF, have shown promise in mitigating RIPF in preclinical models.2,3 However, corticosteroids are typically used with caution in RIPF, as they may not effectively prevent fibrosis and may even lead to side effects with long-term use.3,4 Recent research has increasingly focused on therapeutic strategies for RIPF, particularly those aimed at repairing damaged fibrotic tissue.

A key process in RIPF is radiation-induced endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition (EndMT), which is characterized by persistent DNA damage, endothelial dysfunction, and fibroblast activation, ultimately leading to excessive collagen deposition and tissue fibrosis.5 Therefore, we propose that targeting HIF-1α-dependent EndMT regulation could provide a promising therapeutic approach for RIPF.6,7 While the role of EndMT in fibrosis is well-established, studies have also highlighted MendT, a process in which mesenchymal-like cells transdifferentiate into endothelial cells.8,9

Despite the recognition of EndMT in fibrosis, therapeutic approaches targeting EndMT alone have demonstrated limited effectiveness in reversing fibrotic progression. Recent studies suggest that epigenetic modifications, particularly histone post-translational modifications (PTMs), play a crucial role in regulating fibrosis.10,11 These modifications influence chromatin accessibility and gene expression, and mesenchymal transition in radiation-induced fibrosis has been associated with histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation (H3K9me3), a repressive chromatin mark, as well as altered histone acetylation patterns.12 Therefore, a therapeutic approach that combines EndMT reversal with epigenetic modulation may offer a more effective strategy for fibrosis mitigation.

2-Methoxyestradiol (2-ME), an anticancer agent used in clinical treatments for breast, prostate, and ovarian cancers, functions as an inhibitor of HIF-1α. Our previous research demonstrated that 2-ME suppresses EndMT and attenuates RIPF.7,13,14 Meanwhile, CHIR99021, a GSK3β inhibitor, has shown potential in modulating cellular reprogramming and fibrosis resolution.11,15 Together, these agents offer a promising approach to target multiple pathways involved in fibrosis.

Despite extensive research efforts in developing inhibitors targeting idiopathic fibrosis, an effective, universally accepted drug target remains elusive. Most drug development approaches have primarily focused on inhibiting collagen synthesis, but this has shown limited efficacy in halting disease progression, likely due to the complex, multifactorial nature of pulmonary fibrosis. IPF involves a range of pathological processes, including epithelial injury, immune activation, and vascular remodeling, necessitating a broader therapeutic strategy that addresses multiple cell types and pathways.16 Given the complex pathogenesis of IPF involves diverse biological pathways and factors, combination therapies may offer a more effective solution.17 Nonetheless, unexpected adverse drug-drug interactions, such as those observed between pirfenidone and the antioxidant N-acetylcysteine, can result in poor clinical outcomes.18 Vancheri et al. (year) provided valuable insights into the feasibility and safety profile of combined treatments involving nintedanib and pirfenidone for patients with IPF.19 Supporting this, a recent preclinical study evaluated the synergistic effects of combining nintedanib with baricitinib, a Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitor, in a bleomycin-induced mouse model.20

In line with these findings, our study demonstrated that the co-administration of 2-ME and CHIR99021 attenuated radiation-induced fibrotic changes by suppressing EndMT, modulating the DNA damage response, and regulating epigenetic remodeling. These findings highlight the potential of a multi-targeted therapeutic approach, reinforcing the notion that addressing multiple fibrotic pathways may be more effective than single-pathway interventions.

Results

Combined treatment of 2-methoxyestradiol and CHIR99021 reverses radiation-induced cellular fibrotic changes

We previously reported that targeting EndMT can regulate the development of pulmonary fibrosis.6,7 Specifically, radiation-induced EndMT (RI-EndMT) is detectable during the development of pulmonary fibrosis, and CHIR99021 has been identified as an EndMT inhibitor.6,21 As CHIR99021, a GSK-a inhibitor, is commonly used as a regenerated component, we investigated whether MendT, reversed phenomena of EndMT can be caused by CHIR99021 treatment. Interestingly, we found that the fibrotic phenotypic change of endothelial cells (ECs) 72 h after 10 Gy irradiation reverted to a non-fibrotic state upon treatment with CHIR99021, as evidenced by reduced filamentous cytoskeleton and an enlarged nucleus (Figure 1A). Fibrotic changes in ECs after irradiation at 72 h were accompanied by irreparable persistent DNA damage. However, CHIR99021 treatment did not reduce this persistent DNA damage in irradiated ECs (Figure 1B).

Previously, we have reported that hypoxia induces vascular EnMT, and the HIF-1α inhibitor 2-ME can inhibit radiation-induced EndMT and RIPF.7 We also demonstrated that radiation-induced EndMT is accompanied by persistent DNA damage, where the initial lesions are not fully repaired and remain as unrepaired DNA damage foci several days after irradiation, correlating with the fibroblastic phenotype in irradiated ECs.5 To further explore the dynamics of DNA damage, we showed Figure S1A, which depicts the changes in DNA damage foci from 0.5 h to 24 h after irradiation. Notably, when 2-ME was administered 1 h prior to irradiation, we observed a protective effect against radiation-induced DNA damage. Pre-treatment with 2-ME reduced persistent DNA damage in irradiated ECs without inducing cytotoxicity at the concentrations tested (Figures S1A and S1B). Interestingly, in both EC and HPF cells, the increased ROS observed at 5 days after irradiation was markedly reduced by 2-ME treatment and by the combined treatment (Figure S1C). Based on these findings, we next examined whether the combined treatment could effectively reverse radiation-induced fibrotic changes. Co-treatment with 2-ME and CHIR99021 efficiently reduced the number of DNA damage foci in irradiated ECs (Figure 1B). Furthermore, co-treatment reversed radiation-induced EndMT, as evidenced by a reduction in filamentous cytoskeletal structures in phalloidin staining. Additionally, VEGF treatment significantly increased the VE-cadherin+ endothelial cell population (Figure 1C). We also observed the reversal of pre-existing EndMT, using vimentin and FSP1 as markers, suggesting that the combined therapy can induce MEndoT (Figure S1D). Consistent with their role in repair activity, Oct4, Sox2, and Nanog, considered markers of reprogramming transitional activity, were highly expressed in irradiated endothelial cells (ECs) treated with 2-ME and CHIR99021, compared to both irradiated and non-irradiated controls (Figure 1D). The co-treatment with 2-ME and CHIR99021 also rescued the impaired tube formation ability of irradiated fibrotic cells (Figure 1E).

Similarly, in human pulmonary fibroblasts (HPFs), the co-treatment effectively reversed the radiation-induced fibrotic phenotype by reducing filamentous cytoskeletal development and persistent DNA damage, compared to irradiated cells (Figures 1F and 1G). As observed in ECs, Oct4, Sox2, and Nanog were also highly expressed in irradiated HPFs following co-treatment (Figure 1H). The Oct4, Sox2, and Nanog foci did not co-localize with γH2AX in irradiated ECs and HPFs (Figures S1D and S1F), suggesting that they are unlikely to be directly involved in recruitment to DNA double-strand break sites and may instead be associated with transcriptional regulation and cell state reprogramming. Given the complementary effects of 2-ME and CHIR99021, combination therapy could be a promising strategy for mitigating radiation-induced fibrosis by targeting both the reversal of fibrotic phenotype and DNA damage repair.

Combination therapy of 2-methoxyestradiol and CHIR99021 attenuates radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis via endothelial-to-mesenchymal transition modulation and chromatin remodeling

Next, we assessed the effectiveness of combined therapy with 2-ME and CHIR99021 in regulating RIPF. Using a mouse RIPF model induced by ablative focal high-dose radiation,22 we administered 2-ME and CHIR99021 every other day for a total of four doses, starting four days after 90 Gy focal irradiation to the left lung. The combination treatment significantly reduced RIPF, as evidenced by lower Ashcroft scores and decreased collagen deposition compared to treatment with either 2-ME or CHIR99021 alone. The alveolar structure was well-preserved with the combined treatment (Figure 2A).

We previously reported that targeting EndMT with 2-ME regulates RIPF. In this study, both 2-ME and CHIR99021 individually exhibited regulatory effects on EndMT, as evidenced by co-staining of Cd31 (an endothelial cell marker) and αSMA (a fibrotic cell marker). Notably, 2-ME had a more significant effect than CHIR99021 alone. The combination treatment showed a regulatory effect on EndMT than CHIR99021 alone (Figure 2B). In RIPF tissue, Carbonic CA9, a hypoxia marker, showed high staining intensity compared to normal controls. This intensity was reduced in both single-drug treatment groups and further decreased in the combination group, along with podocalyxin staining indicating improved vascular normalization (Figure S2A). IHC analysis of RIPF tissue confirmed distinct target modulation by each compound, 2-ME reduced HIF-1α expression more than CHIR99021, with no synergistic reduction in the combination group. CHIR99021 increased nuclear β-catenin, but this was not synergistically enhanced by the combination (Figure S2B). However, HIF-1α-dependent Smad signaling was more synergistically reduced by CHIR99021 treatment compared to 2-ME (Figure S2C).

In RIPF tissue, the γH2AX-positive area, indicative of DNA damage response, was significantly reduced in both the 2-ME and combination treatment groups compared to the vehicle group (Figure 2C), suggesting enhanced DNA repair activity. In particular, a synergistic decrease in γ-H2AX-positive cells was specifically observed in ECs following combination treatment, even when compared to the 2-ME single treatment group (Figure S3A).

Given that epigenetic regulation plays a critical role in the progression and resolution of fibrosis, we further examined histone post-translational modifications (PTMs) to explore the potential reprogramming effects of these two drugs in reversing fibrosis. Compared to normal lung tissue, RIPF tissue exhibited a significant increase in histone H3 lysine 9 trimethylation (H3K9me3), a repressive chromatin mark associated with fibrosis progression. While both 2-ME and CHIR99021 treatments reduced H3K9me3 levels, the combination treatment resulted in a more pronounced decrease (Figures 2D and S3B), including in CD31-positive ECs regions within RIPF tissue. Additionally, histone acetylation (H3K27ac and H3K9ac) was significantly elevated in the combination treatment group relative to the vehicle group. Notably, in RIPF tissues, H3K9ac showed a synergistic increase with the combination treatment compared to either monotherapy, whereas H3K27ac did not.

Based on these findings, the combination of 2-ME and CHIR99021 more effectively inhibits EndMT in RIPF and promotes epigenetic remodeling, as indicated by decreased H3K9me3 and increased H3K9ac/H3K27ac levels. These histone modifications likely increase chromatin accessibility, thereby contributing to the enhanced tissue repair and anti-fibrotic effects observed using the combination treatment.

Lineage tracing reveals the suppression of fibroblast activation and enhanced endothelial repair by combination treatment in radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis

To investigate whether the combination treatment leads to the reversal of fibrotic cells in vivo, we utilized a tamoxifen-inducible Collagen1a2-CreERT2; tdTomato reporter mouse model,9 which allows lineage tracing of collagen-producing fibroblasts (Figure 3A). After focal irradiation to the left lung, mice were i.p. administered tamoxifen to trace radiation-induced-fibroblastic cells based on the activity of the collagen1a2 promoter (Figure 3A). In this model, focal irradiation-induced lung fibrosis was significantly attenuated by the combination treatment, as evidenced by a marked reduction in both radiation-induced tdTomato-positive cell populations and fibrosis severity, including lower fibrosis scores and decreased collagen deposition (Figure 3B). These findings suggest that collagen-producing fibroblasts are key mediators of RIPF, and their activity can be modulated by the combination treatment of 2-ME and CHIR99021.

Additionally, as expected, the repair activity in ECs was enhanced in the combination treatment group, leading to the preservation of pulmonary vascular structure and vascular integrity (Figure 3C). These findings suggest that endothelial cells contribute to RIPF, not only through a phenotypic shift toward collagen-producing fibroblast-like cells, but also via a transition into contractile α-SMA+ myofibroblasts through EndMT. As shown in Figure 3D, the significant reduction in CD31+ tdTomato+ α-SMA+ areas following combination treatment indicates the effective suppression of EndMT. Moreover, the observed increase in the proportion of CD31+ tdTomato+ α-SMA- cells in irradiated tissue suggests a partial blockade of the mesenchymal differentiation pathway, potentially preserving endothelial identity. Collectively, these results highlight the therapeutic potential of targeting EndMT to mitigate endothelial contributions to fibrosis and limit disease progression in RIPF. As shown in Figure 3E, the radiation-induced expansion of α-SMA+ myofibroblasts was accompanied by high co-expression of tdTomato, indicating the activation of lineage-traced fibroblasts. The combination treatment significantly reduced both the overall α-SMA+ area and the tdTomato+ α-SMA+ population, supporting its role in attenuating fibroblast activation and fibrotic remodeling.

In summary, our findings demonstrate that the combination of 2-ME and CHIR99021 effectively attenuates RIPF by targeting both endothelial and mesenchymal compartments. The treatment significantly reduced CD31+ tdTomato+ α-SMA+ cell populations, suggesting inhibition and potentially reversal of EndMT, a key contributor to vascular remodeling and fibroblast activation. These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of dual-targeting strategies in reversing fibrotic reprogramming and restoring tissue homeostasis.

HIF-1α deletion combined with CHIR99021 treatment reverses radiation-induced fibrovascular remodeling and fibrosis

To demonstrate that 2-ME, as a HIF-1 inhibitor, reduces radiation-induced persistent DNA damage and modulates the fibroblastic response during the RIPF process when combined with CHIR99021, we investigated whether radiation-induced HIF-1 directly participates in the RIPF repair process. For this, we generated a genetic mouse model in which HIF-1α was deleted in a tamoxifen-inducible manner under the control of collagen promoter activity (Col1a2-HIF1α KO), with the expression of Tomato fluorescence serving as a reporter (Figures 4A and S4A). After 90 Gy focal irradiation, tamoxifen was administered starting on day 6 to induce the deletion of Hif1a, specifically in collagen I promoter-positive cells. In addition, CHIR99021 was administered every other day, and lung tissues were analyzed 3 weeks post-irradiation (Figure 4A). In wild-type (WT) mice subjected to 90 Gy focal irradiation, the Tomato+ area, indicative of activated collagen I promoter cells, was significantly increased, corresponding to severe fibrosis compared to the non-irradiated group. In contrast, Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice exhibited a significant reduction in both the Tomato+ area and fibrosis, with decreased collagen deposition. Furthermore, in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, this reduction of Tomato+ area was even more pronounced with the addition of CHIR99021, corresponding with a reduction in collagen deposition and Tomato+ area (Figure 4B). As shown in Figure 4C and a portion of the increased α-SMA+ cells were tdTomato+ in irradiated WT mice, indicating endothelial-derived myofibroblast-like cells. In Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, the number of tdTomato+ α-SMA+ cells was markedly reduced, and this reduction was further enhanced by CHIR99021 treatment. Similarly, the increase in tdTomato+ CD31+ α-SMA+ cells observed in irradiated WT mice was significantly attenuated in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, with an even greater reduction upon CHIR99021 administration (Figure 4D). Concurrently, during the RIPF process, the proportion of CD31+ tdTomato+ α-SMA- cells, reflecting endothelial cells that reverse mesenchymal transition, was increased in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice compared to WT mice, and this effect was further enhanced by CHIR99021 treatment (Figure 4D).

These results indicate that HIF-1α plays a critical role in promoting EndMT during RIPF. Its deletion in Col1a2-expressing cells significantly limits the transition of endothelial cells into α-SMA+ myofibroblast-like cells. Furthermore, the additive effect observed with CHIR99021 suggests that the simultaneous inhibition of HIF-1α and GSK-3β signaling may synergistically suppress endothelial-derived fibrotic transformation, thereby preserving endothelial identity and attenuating fibrosis progression. Consistently, Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice treated with CHIR99021 exhibited preserved vascular structure, as evidenced by an increased number of CD31+ endothelial cells compared to the irradiated WT group (Figure 4E). In addition, during the progression of RIPF, the expression of Col1a2-dependent Tomato+ cells increased. However, these cells did not co-localize with the basal cell marker KRT5 or the alveolar type 2 (AT2) marker pro-SPC. This indicates that the expanding population of Col1a2+ fibroblastic cells during RIPF primarily consists of ECs and fibroblastic cells (Figure S4B). Similarly, as observed in the in vitro results, the repair activity of radiation-induced persistent DNA damage by 2-ME was also reflected in vivo. In irradiated lung tissue of WT mice, the percentage of γH2AX-positive cells was approximately 30%, whereas in irradiated lung tissue of Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, the percentage decreased to below 10% (Figure S4C).

To investigate the effect of HIF-1α deletion combined with CHIR99021 on the repair of radiation-induced fibroblastic changes in endothelial cells, we analyzed single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from WT, IR, and Col1a2-HIF1α KO + CHIR99021, IR + CHIR99021 samples. The analysis was focused on Tomato-positive cells to trace the lineage of radiation-induced fibroblastic endothelial cells. Among these, a higher proportion of cells expressing endothelial-related genes was observed in the Col1a2-HIF1α KO, IR + CHIR99021 condition (Figure 4F). Genes encoding key endothelial signaling components (Kdr, Tie1, Nos3, and Cav1) and vascular developmental factors (Vegfc, Esam, Egfl7) were expressed in a greater fraction of cells under this condition, suggesting enhanced endothelial identity. In contrast, the WT, IR condition exhibited a higher proportion of cells expressing mesenchymal-related genes associated with migration, invasion, and extracellular matrix remodeling.

These findings suggest that the deletion of HIF1α in collagen I-expressing cells mitigates radiation-induced fibrovascular remodeling by suppressing the activation of both fibroblastic and endothelial lineages. The enhanced reduction in EC-lineage fibroblasts and Tomato+ α- SMA+ cells following a CHIR99021 treatment suggests a synergistic effect between HIF1α deletion and Wnt/β-catenin pathway inhibition. Furthermore, the preservation of vascular structure in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice treated with CHIR99021 suggests the potential of this dual-targeted approach in reversing radiation-induced endothelial dysfunction and fibrosis. Complementing these histological findings, single-cell RNA-seq analysis of tdTomato-positive cells revealed a shift in gene expression profiles, showing increased expression of endothelial-associated genes and decreased mesenchymal signatures in the Col1a2-HIF1α KO, IR + CHIR99021 group.

Synergistic effects of HIF-1α deletion and CHIR99021 on Smad signaling, histone modifications, and Oct4 expression in radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis

In the present study, during RIPF progression in WT mice, fibroblastic cells expressing tdTomato exhibited strong Smad3 phosphorylation, as evidenced by co-staining for tdTomato and phospho-Smad3. However, in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, the population of tdTomato+ phospho-Smad3+ cells was markedly reduced, and this reduction was further enhanced by the addition of CHIR99021 (Figure 5A). Notably, the decrease in Smad3 activation coincided with a reduction in fibroblast activation and collagen deposition, suggesting that HIF-1α deletion suppresses pro-fibrotic Smad signaling and that CHIR99021 provides an additional inhibitory effect (Figure S2C).

As shown in Figure 2, we investigated whether the effects of the combined treatment on histone PTMs in RIPF were observed in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice with CHIR99021. In these mice, H3K27ac and H3K9ac levels were significantly elevated, while radiation-induced H3K9me3 was synergistically reduced compared to both WT and Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice (Figure 5B).

Additionally, in line with the preserved vascular structure, these histone modifications were associated with a reduced population of radiation-induced fibroblastic cells. Notably, the tdTomato+ Oct4+ population was markedly enriched in Col1a2-HIF1 α KO mice treated with CHIR99021 (Figure 5C). These results suggest that the combined inhibition of HIF-1α and Wnt/β-catenin signaling not only modulates histone modifications but also reduces radiation-induced fibroblast activation. These findings raise the possibility that epigenetic mechanisms, indicated by the enrichment of tdTomato+ Oct4+ cells, may help preserve endothelial and fibroblast plasticity, thereby contributing to the mitigation of RIPF.

Combined treatment of 2-methoxyestradioland CHIR99021 reduces fibrotic progression and persistent DNA damage in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis primary cells

Next, we investigated the clinical relevance of combining 2-ME and CHIR99021 in primary cells from patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). The fibroblastic phenotype in IPF patient cells (#1, #2, #3) was modulated by the combined drug treatments, with a reduction in phalloidin intensity observed after four treatments over 11 days. Interestingly, IPF primary cells exhibited persistent DNA damage patterns (γH2AX). However, the combined treatment notably reduced this persistent DNA damage (Figure 6B). Furthermore, collagen 1 expression was significantly decreased in treated cells (Figure 6C). Using a cell contraction assay to assess fibroblast remodeling capabilities, IPF cells treated with DMSO exhibited significant collagen gel contraction, whereas the addition of 2-ME and CHIR99021 resulted in minimal collagen gel contraction (Figure 6D). These results indicate that 2-ME and CHIR99021 effectively inhibited fibroblast-mediated collagen remodeling, suggesting their potential to suppress fibroblast activation and extracellular matrix contraction in IPF.

Additionally, IPF cells treated with the combined 2-ME and CHIR99021 showed a reduction in the gene expression of vimentin and S100A4 (fibrosis markers), as well as a synergistic reduction in Edu+ cell populations, compared to cells treated with either 2-ME or CHIR99021 alone (Figures 6E and 6F). These findings highlight the therapeutic potential of combining 2-ME and CHIR99021 to mitigate fibrotic progression in IPF. The combined treatment not only reduces key fibrotic markers but also alleviates persistent DNA damage, as well as the contractile activity and proliferation of IPF cells. This approach offers a promising strategy for targeting fibrosis in clinical settings.

Discussion

Previous studies have demonstrated that HIF-1α-dependent EndMT plays a critical role in the development of RIPF.6,7 In particular, radiation-induced EndMT is associated with persistent DNA damage and fibroblastic phenotypic changes. In our study, we observed that CHIR99021, a GSK3 inhibitor, mitigated these phenotypic alterations. When combined with 2-ME, a HIF-1α inhibitor that regulates persistent DNA damage, the treatment significantly enhanced the repair process in fibroblast-like cells, suggesting a potential strategy for reversing fibrosis-related cellular changes.

During lung fibrosis progression, irradiation induces distinct epigenetic modifications, notably an increase in histone H3K9 trimethylation (H3K9me3). While H3K27 acetylation (H3K27ac) is associated with active transcription and chromatin accessibility, H3K9me3 is linked to transcriptional repression and heterochromatin formation. Although increased H3K9me3 stabilizes the genome in response to radiation-induced DNA damage,23,24 excessive accumulation may contribute to fibrotic gene silencing and maladaptive cellular responses. Our findings indicate that combined treatment with 2-ME and CHIR99021 significantly increased H3K27ac and H3K9ac while reducing H3K9me3 levels in irradiated lung tissue.

This suggests that the combined treatment may counteract radiation-induced chromatin compaction and transcriptional repression, thereby preventing fibrosis progression. To better elucidate this mechanism, we are planning studies to map these histone modifications to specific chromatin regions and gene clusters. Furthermore, the enrichment of tomato+ Oct4+ cells in the combination treatment group supports the notion that epigenetic regulation maintains cellular plasticity, further highlighting the therapeutic potential of modulating histone modifications in fibrosis.

The combination of 2-ME and CHIR99021 effectively reversed radiation-induced EndMT and preserved vascular integrity in in vivo RIPF models. This treatment led to a reduction in CD31+/α-SMA+ cells and overall α-SMA+ expression, indicating the suppression of EndMT during RIPF progression. Additionally, the decreased presence of tdTomato+ CD31+ cells supports the idea that this combined therapy can inhibit endothelial-derived fibroblast formation and fibrovascular remodeling. Interestingly, the observed increase in CD31+ tdTomato+ α-SMA- cells in response to combined HIF-1α deletion and CHIR99021 treatment may reflect the suppression of EndMT and potential induction of mesenchymal-to-endothelial transition (MEndoT). Although further lineage-tracing studies are required to confirm true MEndoT, this reversal-like phenotype implies an active reprogramming mechanism underlying vascular repair.

The co-treatment of IPF patient-derived fibroblasts with CHIR99021 and 2-ME resulted in a significant reduction of the fibroblastic phenotype, suppressed fibroblast proliferation, downregulated fibrosis-related gene expression, and inhibited ECM remodeling. These findings demonstrate the therapeutic potential of targeting multiple fibrotic mechanisms simultaneously. Our lineage-tracing strategy using Collagen1a2-CreERT2; tdTomato mice further validated the endothelial origin of fibroblastic cells during RIPF and showed that dual treatment suppresses mesenchymal activation. The reduced presence of tdTomato+ α-SMA+ cells shows the therapeutic value of targeting fibroblastic endothelial subsets in fibrotic lung disease. In mice with HIF-1α deletion in collagen I promoter-positive cells, we observed a significant reduction in the Tomato+ area and fibrosis, accompanied by decreased collagen deposition. The addition of CHIR99021 further enhanced these effects, demonstrating a synergistic reduction in fibroblast activation, as evidenced by the decrease in Tomato+ α-SMA+ and CD31+ α-SMA+ cells. This suggests that inhibiting HIF-1α suppresses fibrosis-promoting fibroblastic ECs. Additionally, the decrease in γH2AX-positive cells in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice treated with CHIR99021 suggests that HIF-1α inhibition reduces persistent DNA damage, a key driver of fibrosis. Notably, MEndoT has been implicated in the neovascularization of injured cardiac tissue, representing a potential therapeutic target for enhancing cardiac repair.8,9 Similarly, strategies targeting MEndoT have been explored to modulate tissue fibrosis, highlighting its therapeutic potential. These findings suggest that MEndoT could provide a valuable approach to mitigating fibrosis by promoting vascular repair and reprogramming fibroblasts into endothelial-like cells.

Although further studies are required to elucidate the precise mechanisms underlying this phenomenon, previous reports provide insight into the relationship between HIF-1α and DNA damage repair.25,26 For instance, HIF-1α has been shown to interact with γH2AX, a key marker of DNA double-strand breaks, stabilizing its nuclear accumulation and facilitating hypoxia signaling activation.27 Additionally, HIF-1α inhibition or miR-210 suppression was reported to alleviate Rad52 downregulation, suggesting that HIF-1α inhibition may enhance DNA repair capacity.28 These findings collectively support the hypothesis that HIF-1α inhibition plays a role in reducing persistent DNA damage and may serve as a potential therapeutic strategy for mitigating fibrosis progression.

Our previous study emphasized the role of HIF-1α-dependent Smad2/3 signaling in regulating radiation-induced EndMT. Notably, 2-ME significantly reduced persistent DNA damage associated with radiation-induced EndMT. This effect was further supported by the observed decrease in γH2AX-positive cells in Col1a2-HIF1α KO mice, reinforcing the notion that HIF-1α inhibition contributes to mitigating radiation-induced DNA damage. In addition, the synergistic inhibition of phospho-Smad3 by the combination treatment is likely due to the suppression of Smad3 activation through two distinct upstream pathways: 2-ME inhibits HIF-1α-mediated Smad signaling, while CHIR99021 activates β-catenin signaling, which cross-inhibits the TGF-β/Smad pathway.29,30 Single-cell transcriptomic analysis of lineage-traced fibroblastic cells revealed a significant shift toward endothelial gene expression signatures following HIF-1α deletion combined with CHIR99021 treatment. This transcriptional reprogramming is consistent with the observed phenotypic reversal and supports the notion that the combined targeting of HIF-1α and Wnt/β-catenin pathways can restore endothelial identity in fibrotic cells. Importantly, the therapeutic efficacy of the combination treatment extended beyond the RIPF model, as it also reversed fibrotic phenotypes in IPF patient-derived fibroblasts.

Our data indicate that CHIR99021 alone does not significantly reduce radiation-induced DNA damage. The greater reduction seen with the combination of CHIR99021 and 2-ME compared to 2-ME alone may be due to complementary effects, in which CHIR99021-associated chromatin changes, such as reduced H3K9me3 and increased H3K9ac/H3K27ac, could facilitate access of DNA repair machinery and thereby potentially enhance the effect of 2-ME. Additional modulation of antifibrotic signaling and restoration of endothelial identity may also contribute to the synergistic effects; however, further studies are needed to verify this assumption.

In conclusion, our study suggests a synergistic therapeutic strategy that may suppress radiation-induced fibroblast activation and EndMT while potentially reprogramming damaged endothelial cells through transcriptional and epigenetic mechanisms. Recovering endothelial characteristics together with changes in chromatin accessibility suggest a multifaceted approach to reversing fibrosis and enhancing tissue repair. These findings provide a new window of opportunity to explore reprogramming-based therapies to treat both radiation-related and idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Limitations of the study

Although the combination therapy attenuated EndMT, reduced DNA damage, and altered histone modifications, the mechanistic links and potential synergistic interactions among these events remain to be clarified. Moreover, while fibroblasts derived from patients with IPF were used to explore clinical relevance, the limited number of patient samples requires additional studies with more patient-derived samples.

Experimental model and study participant details

Mice

All animal experiments were approved by the Institutional Animal Care and Use Committee of the Korea Institute of Radiological & Medical Sciences (KIRAMS; protocols #2020-0014, #2022-0037) in compliance with the Animal Research: Reporting of In Vivo Experiments (ARRIVE) guidelines.31 Specific pathogen-free C57BL/6 Col1a2-CreERT2, HIF1αflox/flox and CAG-tdTomato mice were obtained from the Jackson Laboratory. Col1a2-CreERT2 mice were crossed with CAG-tdTomato and HIF1αflox/flox to obtain Col1a2-CreERT2; HIF1αflox/flox; CAG-tdTomato (Col1a2-HIF1α KO) mice. Col1a2-CreERT2 mice were crossed with CAG-tdTomato to obtain Col1a2-Tomato (WT) mice. C57BL/6 mice were obtained from Orient Bio. All experiments were conducted with 7–9-week-old mice. All mice were maintained in a standard environment (20 ± 1°C room temperature, 50 ± 10 % relative humidity) with a 12h light/dark cycle, and were provided standard diet and water ad libitum. All mice were euthanized by CO2 infusion at a rate of 30–70% per minute.

Radiation-induced pulmonary fibrosis model

A single dose of X-ray radiation (90 Gy using a 4 mm diameter field) was delivered using the X-RAD 320 platform (Precision X-rays). The left main bronchus of 7- to 9-week-old mice were irradiated.22 For the drug treatment group, mice were given intraperitoneal injections of 30 mg/kg 2-ME (Selleckchem; #S1233) and 30 mg/kg CHIR99021 (Selleckchem; #S2924). Both 2-ME and CHIR99021 were dissolved in DMSO (Sigma; #D2650). The DMSO stocks were dissolved by sequentially adding 30 % (w/v) polyethylene glycol 300 (Sigma; # 1546423), 1% (v/v) Tween-80 (Sigma; #P4780) and ddH2O just prior to injection. The final DMSO concentration did not exceed 5% (v/v).

Method details

Cell culture and tube formation assays

Human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs; Promocell; #C-12203), human pulmonary fibroblasts (HPFs; Promocell; #C-12360), and diseased human lung fibroblasts from patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPFs; Lonza; #CC-7231) were purchased and cultured using the media recommended by the respective manufacturers. All cells were used within 10 passages. For irradiation, cells were exposed to gamma radiation derived from a [137Cs] source (Atomic Energy of Canada, Mississauga, Ontario, Canada) at a dose rate of 3.81 Gy/min. Treatments with 2-ME (0.5 μM) (Selleckchem; #S1233) and CHIR99021 (3 μM) (Selleckchem; #S2924) were prepared by dissolving them in DMSO (Sigma; #D2650) and then diluting in the medium. The media was replaced with fresh 2-ME and CHIR99021-containing media every 2-3 days. For HUVECs, recombinant human VEGF (hrVEGF; R&D; #293-VE) was added on the 9th day post-irradiation. Tube formation in capillary-like structures was assayed in 96-well plates using mixture of growth factor-reduced Matrigel (Corning; #354230) and OPTI-MEM (Gibco; # 31985070) containing 1% FBS. Irradiated HUVECs (2x10ˆ4 cells/well) and non-irradiated controls were plated on the Matrigel mixture 48 hours post-irradiation. During the analysis, the medium of irradiated HUVECs contained CHIR99021 (3 μM), 2-ME (0.5 μM) or a combination of both treatments CHIR99021 (3 μM) + 2-ME (0.5 μM).

Immunofluorescence staining in vitro

Cells were fixed in 10% (v/v) neutral buffered formalin at room temperature (RT) for 30 minutes. After fixation, cells were washed with PBS and permeabilized with PBST (0.01% (v/v) Triton X-100 in PBS) for 15 minutes at RT. Immunofluorescence staining was performed using primary antibodies against γH2AX (1:500; Millipore; #05-636), OCT4 (1:200; Abcam, #ab19857), SOX2 (1:100; Santacruz; #sc-365823), Nanog (1:200; Abcam; #ab80892), Collagen1 (1:200; Abcam; #ab34710), FSP1 (1:200; Novus; #NBP2-54580F) and Vimentin (1:200; Abcam; #ab92547). The samples were counterstained with DAPI (Sigma; #9542). Phalloidin staining was performed using Phalloidin Alexa 488 Labeling Probes (Invitrogen, #A12379).

FACS of EDU (5-ethynyl-2′-deoxyuridine) incorporation

For proliferation assays, IPFs were treated with 2-ME (0.5 μM) and CHIR99021 (3 μM). The media was refreshed with 2-ME and CHIR99021-containing media once every 3 days for a total of 7 days. The cells were stained using the Click-iT™ EdU Alexa Fluor™ 647 Flow Cytometry Assay Kit (Thermo Fisher Scientific; C10419) and analyzed on a CytoFLEX Flow Cytometry Analyzer (Beckman Coulter).

Cell contraction assay

For cell contraction assays, IPFs were treated with 2-ME (0.5 μM) and CHIR99021 (3 μM). The media was refreshed with 2-ME and CHIR99021 containing media once every 3 days for a total of 7 days. Cell contraction was assessed using the Collagen-based Cell Contraction Assay Kit (Mybiosource; #MBS168182). In a 24-well plate, 5 x 105 cells were used per well. Collagen lattics were prepared by mixing collagen solution and cell suspension in an 8:2 ratio, followed by incubation at 37°C for 1 hour. Afterward, media was added, and the cells were cultured for 2 days. The collagen lattics were treated with a contraction mediator before releasing the stressed matrix. The size of the collagen gels was measured up to 4 hours after contraction initiation.

Intracellular ROS assay

For intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) assays, HUVECs and HPFs were treated with 2-ME (0.5 μM) and CHIR99021 (3 μM) 3 days after irradiation. At 5 days post-irradiation, for DCF-DA staining, the culture medium was removed, and cells were incubated with 3 μM DCF-DA (Sigma; #D6883) in serum free medium at 37 °C for 45 min in the dark. After incubation, cells were washed with PBS to remove DCF-DA.

RT-qPCR analysis

RNA was isolated using QIAzol Lysis Reagent (Qiagen; #79306), and 1 μg of RNA was used to synthesize cDNA with an amfiRivert cDNA Synthesis Platinum Master Mix (GenDEPOT; #R5600). PCR was conducted on the CFX96 TM Real-Time system (Bio-Rad, Hercules, CA, USA), using qPCR SYBR Green Master Mix (Roche; # KM4101). For each sample, gene expression was normalized against the geometric mean of the reference gene GAPDH.

Immunohistochemistry and immunofluorescence staining

Lung tissues were fixed in 10% (v/v) neutral-buffered formalin, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned. The sections were deparaffinized in xylene, washed in ethanol, and progressively reduced in ethanol concentrations for staining. Lung tissue slides were stained with hematoxylin and eosin, and fibrosis grade was assessed using the Ashcroft score.32 Collagen deposition was stained with Masson trichrome staining using the Polyscience staining kit (#25088-100). For immunohistochemistry (IHC) and immunofluorescence (IF) staining, tissue slides were deparaffinized, hydrated, and underwent antigen retrieval in citrate buffer at 95 °C for 30 minutes. The slides were then incubated with 0.3 % hydrogen peroxide in methanol at RT for 15 minutes. Following this, the slides were treated with PBST (0.01 % (v/v) Triton X-100 in PBS) for permeabilization for 15 minutes and then blocked with blocking buffer (1.5 % (v/v) normal horse serum in PBST) for 30 minutes at RT. For IF staining, 0.3% hydrogen peroxide in methanol step was omitted. Primary antibody incubation was performed overnight at 4 °C in the blocking buffer. IHC staining was performed using the ABC (Vector; #PK-4000) and DAB kits (Vector; #SK-4100) according to the manufacturer's instructions, with secondary antibody reactions yielding a brown color. Nuclei were stained with hematoxylin. IF staining was performed using a fluorescent secondary antibody, and counterstained with DAPI. IHC and IF staining were carried out using primary antibodies against CD31 (1:200; R&D Systems; #AF3628, 1:200; Abcam; #ab225883), αSMA (1:1000; Sigma-Aldrich; #A5228), γH2AX (1:200; Cell signaling; #2577), H3K27ac (1:500; Abcam; #ab4729), H3K9ac (1:500; Abcam; #ab12179), H3K9me3 (1:500; Abcam; #ab8898), p-smad2/3 (1:200; Cell signaling; #8828), OCT4 (1:200; Abcam, #ab19857), pro-SPC (1:500; Abcam, #ab90716), KRT5 (1:200; Abcam, #ab128190), HIF1α (1:200; Cayman, #10006421), Tomato (1:2000; LSbio; #LS-C340696), β-Catenin (1:100; Cell signaling; #8480), CA9 (1:200; Novus; # NB100-417) and Podocalyxin (1:200; R&D; AF1556). For quantification, a minimum of five images per tissue section were acquired, and positively stained areas were evaluated using ImageJ software (http://imagej.net/).

Data analysis and visualization of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq)

The samples, ‘WT, IR’ and ‘Col1a2-HIF1α KO+ CHIR99021, IR’, were processed for single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) using 10X Genomics technology.33 Gene expression data were analyzed using Scanpy (Python) following standard procedures.34 Normalization and batch correction were performed using single-cell Variational Inference (scVI). Dimensionality reduction was performed using Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) based on the scVI latent space. The ratio of positive cells for mesenchymal- and endothelial-related genes35 was computed and visualized as a stacked bar plot using Matplotlib and Seaborn in Python.

Quantification and statistical analysis

To explore the statistical significance of differences between experimental groups, One-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), 2-way ANOVA and Student’s t-test were used. All statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism version 10.0 (GraphPad Software Inc.), with statistical significance set at p<0.05.

Introduction

The (global)
minimum cut
problem is very well-studied in graph algorithms. In the context of directed graphs, the goal is to find a cut
(S,V-S)
in the graph
G=(V,E)
such that the number of edges from
S
to
V-S
is minimized. Given an arbitrary source
s\in V

s
-rooted minimum cut
is a cut
(S,V\setminus S)
of minimum size with
s\in S
. The size of the
s
-rooted minimum cut is called
s
-rooted connectivity.
Observe that the global minimum cut problem can be solved by running two
s
-rooted minimum cut computations, one on the original graph and one on the graph with all edges reversed.

There is a line of work on solving the
s
-rooted minimum cut
problem.
Hao and Orlin
[
12
]
showed that a
\tilde{O}(mn)
-time algorithm based on push-relabel methods
[
11
]
. Then, Gabow
[
8
]
gave an algorithm with
\tilde{O}(mk)
time where

is the size of the
s
-rooted minimum cut.
A recent work
[
2
]
solves the problem in
\min(n/m^{1/3},\sqrt{n})\cdot m^{1+o(1)}
time.

A closely related object to
s
-rooted mincut is an
s
-rooted arborescence
.
An
s
-rooted arborescence is a directed tree rooted at
s
where all arrows point away from the root
s
.
A very influential result by Edmonds
[
5
]
shows that the maximum number of disjoint
s
-rooted arborescences equals the size of
s
-rooted minimum cut.

In the
arborescence packing
problem, we need to compute the maximum number of disjoint arborescences rooted at
s
. Gabow
[
8
]
an
O(n^{2}k^{2})
-time algorithm. Later, Bhalgat et al.
[
1

improved the running time for arborescence packing to
\tilde{O}(m\mathrm{poly}(k))
. In weighted graphs,
k
can be very large. In this case, the fastest algorithm is by Gabow and Manu
[
7
]
and takes
\tilde{O}(mn^{2})
time.

In short, the state-of-the-art algorithms for both problems still require super-linear time. It was unclear if a speed-up is possible even when we allow approximation.

1.1

In this paper, we present the first almost-linear-time algorithms for both problems, via directed expander hierarchies.

For the

Preliminaries

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
, we denote
n=|V|
and
m=|E|
. We use
[W]
to abbreviate the set
\{1,\dots,W\}
. We use the notation
\tilde{O}
to hide polylogarithmic factors in time complexity. For any set
S
and function
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
, unless otherwise stated, for any subset
T\subseteq S
, we define
f(T)=\sum_{t\in T}f(t)
.

Directed Graphs and Connectivity.

In a directed graph
G=(V,E)
, for any set
S\subseteq V
, call
(S,V-S)
a
cut
. We define
E^{+}(S)
as the edges from
S
to
V\setminus S
, and
E^{-}(S)
as the edges from
V\setminus S
to
S
. We define
\delta(S)=|E^{+}(S)|
and
\rho(S)=|E^{-}(S)|
. When
S=\{s\}
is a singleton, we abbreviate as
\delta(s)=|E^{+}(s)|
and
\rho(s)=|E^{-}(s)|
. The degree of
s
is
\deg(s)=\delta(s)+\rho(s)
and the volume of a set
S
is
\mathrm{vol}(S)=\deg(S)=\sum_{s\in S}\deg(s)
. We can similarly discuss degree (or volume) for a subgraph
G^{\prime}
or an edge subset
X\subseteq E
, which we denote by
\deg_{G^{\prime}}
or
\deg_{X}
, respectively.

The (global)
min-cut
of a directed graph
G
is defined as
\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subsetneq V}\rho(S)
. Given a fixed source
s\in V
, we can also define the
s
-rooted min-cut
problem, which asks
\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V\setminus s}\rho(S)
. Let
G^{R}
be the graph obtained by inverting all edges in
G
. One can observe that, the
min-cut
of
G
can be computed by taking the minimum from both
s
-rooted min-cut in
G
and
G^{R}
. Therefore, below we consider the
s
-rooted min-cut
problem, and we assume
E^{-}(s)=\emptyset
by deleting the incoming edges to
s
, which does not influence the result.

Strongly Connected Components.

For a directed graph
G
, we use
[G]
to denote the collection of strongly connected components (SCCs) in
G
, where each SCC
C
is represented by a subset of vertices. Therefore,
[G]
forms a partition of
V(G)
.

Max-Flows and Min-Cuts.

Given a directed graph
G=(V,E)
and
s,t\in V
, the
max-flow
from
s
to
t
is the maximum number of edge-disjoint paths from
s
to
t
. By duality, this equals the size of
min-cut
from
s
to
t
, which is
\min_{t\in T\subseteq V-s}\rho(T)
. Here, for the minimizer
T
, we denote the cut
(V-T,T)
as a
min-cut
from
s
to
t
. Given a graph
G
and
s,t
, a max-flow or minimum
(s,t)
-cut can be computed in
m^{1+o(1)}
time
[
4
]
.

2.1
Arborescences

For a directed graph
G
with source vertex
s
, an
arborescence
rooted at
s
is a spanning tree subgraph of
G
in which every edge is directed away from
s
. Given an integer
k\geq 1
, a
k
-
arborescence packing
with congestion
\gamma
is a collection of
k
arborescences such that each edge appears in at most
\gamma
arborescences.

2.2
Directed Expanders

Demands.

For a directed graph
G
, a
demand

\mathbf{D}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
assigns each (ordered) vertex pair a non-negative real number. The demand
\mathbf{D}
is

•

\mathscr{C}
-component-constrained
for some partition
\mathscr{C}
of
V(G)
, if for any two vertices
u,v
belonging to different components
C_{u},C_{v}\in\mathscr{C}
,
\mathbf{D}(u,v)=0
, and

•

\mathbf{d}
-respecting
for some vertex weights
\mathbf{d}:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, if for each vertex
v
,
\sum_{u}(D(u,v)+D(v,u))\leq\mathbf{d}(v)
.

Routings.

For each pair of vertex
(u,v)
, let
{\cal P}_{u,v}
denote the collection of simple paths from
u
to
v
. Let
{\cal P}=\bigcup_{u,v\in V(G)}{\cal P}_{u,v}
denote the collection of all simple paths. A
routing

\mathbf{R}:{\cal P}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
assigns each simple path a non-negative real number. The routing
\mathbf{R}
has congestion
\gamma
if for each edge
e\in E(G)
,
\sum_{P\ni e}\mathbf{R}(P)\leq\gamma
. A demand
\mathbf{D}
can be routed in
G
with congestion
\gamma
if there exists a routing
\mathbf{R}
with congestion
\gamma
such that for each vertex pair
(u,v)
,
\sum_{P\in{\cal P}_{u,v}}\mathbf{R}(P)=\mathbf{D}(u,v)
.

Weak Flow Expansion.

For a directed graph
G
with a terminal edge set
E^{\star}\subseteq E(G)
and a cut edge set
B\subseteq E(G)
, we say
E^{\star}
is
[G\setminus B]
-component-constrained
\phi
-expanding in
G
, if any
(\deg_{E^{\star}})
-respecting
[G\setminus B]
-component-constrained demand
\mathbf{D}
can be routed in
G
with congestion
1/\phi
.

2.1
Flow Expansion Implies Cut Expansion.

If
E^{\star}
is
[G\setminus B]
-component-constrained
\phi
-expanding in
G
, then for each SCC
C\in[G\setminus B]
and each
cut
(V(G)\setminus T,T)
such that
\deg_{E^{\star}}(C\cap T)\leq\deg_{E^{\star}}(C)/2
, we have

\min\{\delta_{G}(T),\rho_{G}(T)\}\geq\phi\cdot\deg_{E^{\star}}(C\cap T).

2.3
Weighted Graphs

One may also consider
weighted graphs

G=(V,E,\mathbf{c})
, with
\mathbf{c}:E\to[W]
, where every edge

is with capacity

\rho(S)=|E^{-}(S)|

\delta(S)=\mathbf{c}(E^{+}(S))
and
\rho(S)=\mathbf{c}(E^{-}(S))
, and the

\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subseteq V\setminus s}\rho(S)

In
Section

, we use the notion in weighted graphs to conform with
[
6
]
. In
Section

4

Weak Expander Hierarchy

In this section, we recall tools related to directed expanders. We will exploit directed expander hierarchies to design our approximate min-cut and arborescence packing algorithms.

3.1
Expander Hierarchy.

An
L
-level expander hierarchy
\mathcal{H}
of
G
is a collection of edge sets
\{E_{i}\mid 1\leq i\leq L\}
such that
\bigcup_{i}E_{i}=E(G)
. The hierarchy
{\cal H}
is
\phi
-
expanding
if, for every level
i\in\{1,\dots,L\}
, the level-
i
edge set
E_{i}
is
\left[G\setminus E_{>i}\right]
-component-constrained
\phi
-expanding in
G
, where
\left[G\setminus E_{>i}\right]
refers to the set of SCCs in
G\setminus E_{>i}
.

For intuition, we point out that all SCCs across levels in the hierarchy form a laminar family. Moreover, since we assume that the source vertex
s
has no incoming edges,
s
always appears as a singleton SCC in all levels.

]

Given a directed, weighted graph
G=(V,E,\mathbf{c})

Initially, we set

Start from

in the algorithm. We show the formal definition of

4.1
.

We define
\{T_{i}\}
inductively from

that intersects
T

4.2
.

For any layer

,

with high probability, which gives us an

-approximation. Finally, we show the time complexity of our algorithm and prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

From above we know the algorithm will output some
\rho(C_{v})=O(L\rho(T)/\phi)
with high probability. By inserting
L=O(\log n)
and
\phi=\Omega(1/\log^{4}n)
, one can get
\rho(C_{v})=O(\rho(T)\log^{5}n)
. For time complexity, the hierarchy
\mathcal{H}
is built in
m^{1+o(1)}
time in
Theorem

3.4
. For each layer, all min-cut instances have
\tilde{O}(m)
edges in total, so the total time of these instances is still
m^{1+o(1)}
. Therefore, the total time for computing
\mathcal{H}
and resolving
L=O(\log n)
layers is still
m^{1+o(1)}
.
∎

5
Approximate Arborescence Packing

In this section, we will present our approximate arborescence packing algorithm, formally stated in
Theorem

5.2
.

5.1
Section Preliminaries

Expander Routing.

In
Section

5
, we may consider
integral demands
. An integral demand
\mathbf{D}
is a multiset of vertex pairs. The notions of being
\mathscr{C}
-component-constrained and
\mathbf{d}
-respecting extend to an integral demand
\mathbf{D}
by interpreting
\mathbf{D}
as a function that maps each vertex pair to its multiplicity.

An
integral routing

\mathbf{R}
of an integral demand
\mathbf{D}
simply maps each
(u,v)\in\mathbf{D}
into a
u
-
v
routing path.
\mathbf{R}
has congestion
\gamma
if for each edge
e\in E(G)
, the number of routing paths going through
e
is at most
\gamma
.

5.1
Directed Expander Routing.

Let
G
be a directed graph with two edge sets
E^{\star},X\subseteq E(G)
such that
E^{\star}
is
[G\setminus X]
-component-constrained
\phi
-expanding in
G
. Given an integral demand
\mathbf{D}
on
V(G)
that is
[G\setminus X]
-component-constrained and
(\alpha\cdot\deg_{E^{\star}}^{-})
-respecting for some
\alpha\geq 1
, there is an algorithm that computes an integral routing of
\mathbf{D}
in
G
with congestion
\kappa_{\mathrm{route}}\cdot\alpha/\phi
, for some

\kappa_{\mathrm{route}}=n^{o(1)}.

The algorithm runs in
(m+|\mathbf{D}|)\cdot n^{o(1)}
time.

Theorem

5.1
is an extension of almost-linear-time expander routing algorithms in undirected graphs
[
9
,
10
,
3
]
. We will work on a formal proof of this in the full version of the paper.

5.2
The Algorithm

5.2
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph with a given source vertex
s
and a parameter
k>0
. Assuming
Theorem

5.1
, there is an algorithm that computes either

•

a
k
-arborescence packing with congestion
\tilde{O}(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}})=n^{o(1)}
, or

•

a cut
(S,V\setminus S)
such that
s\in S\subset V(G)
and
|E^{+}(S)|<k
, certifying that there is no
k
-arborescence packing in
G
.

The running time is
m^{1+o(1)}
.

To obtain a
k
-arborescence packing, we will actually assign each edge
e\in E(G)
a subset of
colors

\Gamma(e)\subseteq[k]
that satisfies the following.

1.

For each color
\gamma\in[k]
, the source
s
should be able to reach every other vertex using only
\gamma
-colored
edges (we say an edge is
\gamma
-colored iff
\gamma\in\Gamma(e)
).

2.

Each edge
e\in E(G)
has at most
\tilde{O}(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}})
colors, i.e.
|\Gamma(e)|\leq\tilde{O}(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}})
.

Observe that, once we have the above coloring, we can compute
k
arborescences by for each color
\gamma\in[k]
, picking a DFS tree rooted at
s
in the subgraph induced by
\gamma
-colored edges. The congestion of these arborescences is clearly bounded by
\tilde{O}(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}})
because of Property
2
.

Therefore, the task reduces to either compute such a coloring
\{\Gamma(e)\mid e\in E(G)\}
, or output a small cut. To this end, we first construct an expander hierarchy
{\cal H}
in the graph
G
with
L
levels and expansion
\phi
, where

L=O(\log(nW)),\qquad\phi^{-1}=O(\log n\log^{3}(nW)),

using
Theorem

3.4
. With the hierarchy
{\cal H}
, our algorithm will proceed level by level from the bottom to the top.

5.2.1
Invariants at Level
i

After the computation at level
i
, our algorithm will keep a color assignment
\Gamma_{i}(e)
for each edge
e\in E(G)
and
\Gamma_{i}(v)
for each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s\}
.

The level-
i
color assignments should satisfy certain invariants. Before describing the invariants, we start with some notations. For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, we use
C_{i,v}
to denote the unique SCC in
[G\setminus E_{>i}]
that contains
v
. Moreover, we let
K_{i}(v)
denote the set of inter-SCC
or

E_{>i}
incoming edges of
v
. That is,

K_{i}(v):=\{e\in E(G)\mid e=(u,v)\text{\penalty\ s.t.\penalty\ }C_{i,u}\neq C_{i,v}\}\cup E_{>i}^{-}(v),

and we call the edges in
K_{i}(v)
the
level-
i
critical incoming edges
of
v
. Let

\Delta_{i}(v)=|K_{i}(v)|

denote the size of
K_{i}(v)
.Lastly, the following notions of
\gamma
-colored
vertices and edges are with respect to the color assignments
\{\Gamma_{i}(e)\}
and
\{\Gamma_{i}(v)\}
at level
i
.

The invariants are as follows.

1.

For each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s\}
and each color
\gamma\in[k]
,
v
can be reached from a
\gamma
-colored vertex in
C_{i,v}
using only
\gamma
-colored edges.

2.

For each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s\}
,
|\Gamma_{i}(v)|\leq\Delta_{i}(v)\cdot(i+1)
.

3.

For each edge
e\in E(G)
,
|\Gamma_{i}(e)|\leq 5i^{2}\cdot(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}}/\phi)
.

Intuitively, a vertex
v
having color
\gamma\in\Gamma_{i}(v)
means that
v
acts as a “breakpoint” in the
\gamma
-colored arborescence, and we want to establish reachability from
s
to
v
via upper-level routing.

The Base Case.

We start with the bottom level
i=0
, and initialize level-
0
color assignments that trivially satisfy the invariants: for each edge
e\in E(G)
,
\Gamma_{0}(e)=\emptyset
; for each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s\}
,
\Gamma_{0}(v)=[k]
. To see the feasibility of the level-
0
color assignments, we first observe each SCC in
[G\setminus E_{>0}]
just contains a single vertex since
E_{>0}=E(G)
. This means
\Delta_{0}(v)=|E^{-}_{G}(v)|
is just the in-degree of
v
in
G
.

Invariants
1
and
3
clearly hold. For Invariant
2
, if it does not hold, it means there is a vertex
v
with
\Delta_{0}(v)<k
, which implies a small cut
(V(G)\setminus\{v\},\{v\})
and we just terminate the algorithm.

The Ending Case: Getting the Final Coloring
\{\Gamma(e)\}
.

Before we describe how to compute the color assignments level by level, we first show how to get the final edge-coloring
\Gamma(e)
from the top-level color assignments
\{\Gamma_{L}(e)\}
and
\{\Gamma_{L}(v)\}
. In fact, this is essentially the same as computing an arborescence packing on a DAG.

We start by setting
\Gamma(e)=\Gamma_{L}(e)
for each edge
e\in E(G)
. For each vertex
v\in V(G)\setminus\{s\}
, observe that
K_{L}(v)
is exactly the inter-SCC incoming edges of
v
. We distribute the colors
\Gamma_{L}(v)
over the edges in
K_{L}(v)
such that each edge
receives
at most
L+1
colors. Here, an edge
e\in K_{L}(v)
receives a subset of colors
Y^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma_{L}(v)
means that we add
Y^{\prime}
into
\Gamma(e)
. This finishes the computation of the final edge-coloring
\{\Gamma(e)\}
.

Property
1
follows Invariant
1
of the level-
L
color assignments and the fact that SCCs in
G
“form” a DAG. Each edge
e
has

|\Gamma(e)|\leq|\Gamma_{L}(e)|+L+1\leq O(L^{2}\cdot(\kappa_{\mathrm{route}}/\phi)).

5.2.2
The Algorithm at Level
i

Consider a level
1\leq i\leq L
. We now describe the algorithm to compute the level-
i
color assignments from the level-
(i-1)
color assignments. Initially, we set
\Gamma_{i}(v)=\emptyset
for each

and
\Gamma_{i}(e)=\Gamma_{i-1}(e)
for each

.

Step 1: Allocate “Breakpoint” Colors.

For each SCC
C_{i}\in[G\setminus E_{>i}]
and each vertex
v\in C_{i}
, recall that
C_{i-1,v}
is the unique SCC in
[G\setminus E_{>i-1}]
that contains
v
, and clearly
C_{i-1,v}\subseteq C_{i}
. We first partition the level-
(i-1)
critical incoming edges of
v
, i.e.

, into three sets
E_{X},E_{Y}
and
E_{Z}
, where

\displaystyle E_{X}

\displaystyle=K_{i}(v),

\displaystyle E_{Y}

\displaystyle=(E^{-}_{G}(C_{i-1,v})\setminus E^{-}_{G}(C_{i})\setminus E_{>i-1})\cap E^{-}_{G}(v),

\displaystyle E_{Z}

\displaystyle=E_{i}^{-}(v)\setminus(E_{X}\cup E_{Y}).

We make some remarks on this partition. The first set
E_{X}
is simply the level-
i
critical incoming edges of
v
, which, by definition, consist of
v
’s incoming edges in
E_{>i}
and
v
’s incoming edges whose tail-endpoints fall outside

. The second set

consists of

’s incoming edges whose tail-endpoints fall inside

E_{X}
and

are clearly disjoint. By the definition of

, the remaining edges

. Therefore, these three sets

form a partition of

.

Next, we arbitrarily partition
v

\Gamma_{i-1}(v)
into three sets

|X|\leq|E_{X}|\cdot i=\Delta_{i}(v)\cdot i,\qquad|Y|\leq|E_{Y}|\cdot i,\qquad|Z|\leq|E_{Z}|\cdot i.

•

(Update
\Gamma_{i}(v)
) For the colors in

\Gamma_{i}(v)
.

•

(Update
\Gamma_{i}
(e)) For the colors in

colors from

.

The remaining colors in

will be handled in the following steps, and we use

refer to the
Z

Step 2: Max Flows.

For each SCC
C_{i}\in[G\setminus E_{>i}]
, we will run a single-commodity max-flow instance in

. The instance has source function
\Delta_{C_{i}}:=(\Delta_{i})_{\mid C_{i}}
, i.e., the restriction of
\Delta_{i}
on vertices
C_{i}
, and sink function
\nabla_{C_{i}}:=(i\cdot\deg^{-}_{E_{i}})_{\mid C_{i}}
, i.e., each vertex

has
\nabla_{C_{i}}(v)
equal to
i
times the number of
v
’s incoming edges in

. Note that, if we let

Z_{C_{i}}:=\bigcup_{v\in C_{i}}Z_{v}

be the union of the

-colors of all

, then the total sink
|\nabla_{C_{i}}|:=\sum_{v\in C_{i}}\nabla_{C_{i}}(v)
satisfies

|\nabla_{C_{i}}|=i\cdot\sum_{v\in C_{i}}|E^{-}_{i}(v)|\geq\sum_{v\in C_{i}}|Z_{v}|\geq|Z_{C_{i}}|

by the definition of
Z_{v}

5.3
.

By calling an exact max-flow algorithm on

once, we can obtain either

•

(Cut Case)
a non-empty set

such that

, or

•

(Flow Case)
a collection of

We are not going to prove

By

•

(Update
\Gamma_{i}(e)
) For each color

•

(Update

) For the starting vertex of

, say

to
\Gamma_{i}(u)
.

Lastly, we refer to the ending vertex of

\gamma
-colored leader
of

, denoted by

.

Step 3: Expander Routing.

For each SCC

5.4
.

The total demand

(3i\cdot\deg^{-}_{E_{i}})

Proof.

5.2.3
Running Time Analysis

The bottlenecks are the expander hierarchy algorithm (

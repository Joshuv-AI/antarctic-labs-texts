Predictors of Optimal Uptake of Intermittent Preventive Treatment in Pregnancy (IPTp3+) in Nigeria: Survey-Weighted Regression and Exploratory Machine-Learning Analysis of the 2021 Nigeria Malaria Indicator Survey
Authors: Happiness Opeyemi Agboola, Adewale Jamiu Lasisi, Olufisayo Elugbadebo, Salim Khan, Olatunde Aremu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Malaria in pregnancy remains a public health challenge in Nigeria despite the effectiveness of intermittent preventive treatment with sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP). Uptake of the WHO-recommended minimum of three doses (IPTp3+) remains below national targets. This study examined factors associated with optimal uptake of IPTp and explored machine-learning-based prediction.Methodology:We analysed 2021 Nigeria Malaria Indicator Survey data for 3,103 women aged 15–49 years with a live birth, guided by Andersen’s Behavioural Model. Descriptive and regression analyses were conducted in R. Descriptive summaries were unweighted, whereas the primary regression incorporated sampling weights, strata, and primary sampling units. Following a survey-adjusted nonlinearity test, ANC visits were categorised as 0–3, 4–7, and ≥8. Random Forest and XGBoost were fitted in Python as unweighted exploratory models, with calibration, permutation importance, and SHAP assessed on held-out data.Results:Overall, 52.4% of women in the unweighted analytic sample reported IPTp3+ uptake. The association with ANC was nonlinear (F(2,84) = 18.12; p < 0.001). Compared with 0–3 visits, adjusted odds ratios were 2.10 (95% CI: 1.64–2.68) for 4–7 visits and 2.05 (95% CI: 1.49–2.83) for ≥8 visits; both p < 0.001. Geopolitical zone was associated with uptake. XGBoost and Random Forest showed modest discrimination (ROC-AUC: 0.661 and 0.640) and Brier scores of 0.225 and 0.228.Conclusion:Only about half of women achieved optimal IPTp uptake. ANC attendance was strongly associated with IPTp3+ uptake, while geographic disparities persisted after adjustment. The exploratory machine-learning models showed modest predictive performance. Strengthening ANC access and improving IPTp delivery remain important priorities.

Introduction

Malaria remains a major global public health challenge, with the greatest burden concentrated in sub-Saharan Africa [1]. Recent estimates indicate more than 280 million cases worldwide, with Africa accounting for approximately 94–95% of malaria cases and deaths [1,2]. Nigeria contributes substantially to this burden, and transmission remains intense because ecological and climatic conditions favour Anopheles breeding and survival [2,3]. Transmission often increases during the rainy season and varies across geographical settings, with rural communities and populations living near water bodies frequently experiencing greater exposure [2,3]. Plasmodium falciparum is the predominant parasite, while major vectors include members of the Anopheles gambiae complex and Anopheles funestus group [1,2]. Pregnant women are particularly vulnerable because pregnancy-related immunological changes increase susceptibility to infection and adverse outcomes, including maternal anaemia, stillbirth, preterm delivery, and low birth weight [3,4].

Intermittent preventive treatment in pregnancy with sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine (IPTp-SP) is therefore a key WHO-recommended intervention. It should be administered at scheduled antenatal care (ANC) visits from the second trimester, with at least three doses (IPTp3+) considered optimal [3]. Evidence shows that IPTp-SP reduces maternal morbidity and improves birth outcomes [5,6]. However, IPTp3+ uptake in Nigeria remains below the national target of 80%, with marked geographical and socioeconomic inequalities [7–9]. Previous studies have identified maternal education, household wealth, ANC attendance, malaria-related information, service availability, and facility readiness as important correlates of uptake [8,10,11].

This study was guided by Andersen’s Behavioural Model of Health Services Utilisation [12]. Maternal age and education were treated as predisposing factors; household wealth, residence, and geopolitical zone as enabling factors; and ANC attendance as a measure of healthcare utilisation. Existing population health research has mainly used logistic regression, which may not fully capture nonlinear relationships and predictor interactions [7–9]. Machine-learning methods can complement regression by exploring nonlinear predictive patterns and out-of-sample performance [13,14]. However, predictive importance does not imply causality, and machine learning may add limited value when few predictors are available. Applications to IPTp uptake using recent nationally representative Nigerian data remain limited.

This study addressed this gap by combining survey-weighted logistic regression with explainable machine-learning approaches to examine IPTp3+ uptake among Nigerian women. Exploratory Random Forest and XGBoost models were interpreted using SHAP and permutation importance to explain model predictions rather than rank causal determinants or set policy priorities. The objectives were to identify factors associated with IPTp3+ uptake, assess out-of-sample discrimination, and determine whether the machine-learning models added information beyond regression.

Methods

Study Design and Data Source

This study was a secondary analysis of the 2021 Nigeria Malaria Indicator Survey (NMIS), a nationally representative cross-sectional survey conducted by the National Malaria Elimination Programme in collaboration with international partners [15]. The NMIS used a stratified two-stage cluster sampling design to ensure representativeness across Nigeria’s six geopolitical zones. In the first stage, enumeration areas were selected with probability proportional to size, followed by systematic household sampling in the second stage. The survey collected data on household characteristics, malaria prevention practices, maternal health, and child health indicators. Eligible participants were women aged 15–49 years who were usual residents or visitors in selected households. For this analysis, the sample was restricted to women who had a live birth in the two years preceding the survey, consistent with prior DHS-based analyses [15].

Outcome Variable

The outcome was IPTp3+ uptake, defined as maternal report of receiving three or more doses of sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine during the most recent pregnancy, in accordance with the WHO-recommended minimum number of doses. The outcome was recoded as a binary variable, with 1 representing receipt of at least three doses and 0 representing receipt of fewer than three doses. Because IPTp receipt was based primarily on maternal report, the measure may have been affected by recall error, incorrect identification of sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine and incomplete information regarding the timing and spacing of doses.

Predictor Variables

Predictor variables were selected based on epidemiological relevance, data availability and consistency with previous studies of IPTp uptake. The variables included maternal age, place of residence, educational attainment, household wealth index, geopolitical zone and number of antenatal care visits. The analytic dataset did not include linked measures of gestational age at first ANC contact, facility readiness, sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine stock availability, provider adherence to IPTp guidelines, or the precise timing and frequency of IPTp administration. These potentially important health-system and service-delivery variables could therefore not be incorporated into the present analysis.

Statistical Analysis

Tables 1 and 2 present unweighted descriptive summaries of the analytic sample. Categorical variables are reported as counts and percentages, while the number of antenatal care (ANC) visits is reported as the median and interquartile range. Unweighted bivariate associations were evaluated using Pearson's chi-squared tests for categorical variables and the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for the number of ANC visits. Because sampling weights and survey-design variables were not applied to these descriptive and bivariate analyses, the findings describe the observed analytic sample and should not be interpreted as nationally representative estimates.

A multivariable survey-weighted logistic regression model was fitted to examine factors associated with IPTp3+ uptake. Sampling weights, primary sampling units and survey strata were incorporated to account for unequal selection probabilities, clustering and stratification.

The number of ANC visits was initially examined as a continuous variable. ANC visits were centred and standardised, and linear, quadratic and cubic terms were included in a full survey-adjusted model. Departure from linearity was evaluated by jointly testing the quadratic and cubic terms. The design-adjusted joint test indicated evidence of nonlinearity, F(2,84) = 18.12, p < 0.001. ANC utilisation was therefore included in the primary regression model as a categorical variable comprising 0–3, 4–7 and ≥ 8 visits.

Variances were estimated using Taylor series linearisation based on primary-sampling-unit contributions within strata. Statistical inference used the residual survey-design degrees of freedom. Associations are reported as adjusted odds ratios with 95% confidence intervals.

Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors calculated from the regression design matrix. Values below 5 were considered to indicate no problematic multicollinearity. All inferential tests were two-sided, with statistical significance evaluated at p < 0.05. Exact p values are reported to three decimal places, while values below 0.001 are reported as p < 0.001.

Exploratory Machine-Learning Analysis

Random Forest and XGBoost models were developed as exploratory predictive comparators using maternal and household characteristics. Unweighted models excluded survey-design variables and were not intended for nationally representative or causal inference. Data were split 80:20 using stratified sampling, with preprocessing and five-fold cross-validation performed within the training set. Models were evaluated using ROC-AUC, accuracy, sensitivity, specificity, balanced accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and Brier scores. Calibration was also assessed. Sensitivity analyses incorporated sampling-design variables as predictors to determine whether predictive performance depended on sampling structure, without implementing survey-weighted estimation.

Machine-Learning Model Interpretation

Permutation importance and Shapley additive explanations (SHAP) were used to examine the fitted models. Permutation importance assessed the reduction in held-out ROC-AUC following the random permutation of each predictor. SHAP values described the direction and magnitude of each predictor’s contribution to individual model predictions.

Permutation importance and SHAP findings were interpreted as model-specific associational summaries. They were not interpreted as causal effects, population-level effect estimates, an epidemiological hierarchy of determinants or rankings of intervention priorities.

Statistical Software

Descriptive and regression analyses were conducted using R (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria). Exploratory machine-learning analyses were conducted using Python within the Jupyter Notebook environment, with the scikit-learn, XGBoost and SHAP packages.

Ethical Considerations

Permission to access and use the 2021 Nigeria Malaria Indicator Survey (NMIS) dataset was obtained from the DHS Program/ICF through the standard online registration and data access request process. The dataset is available through the DHS Program website: (https://dhsprogram.com/data/dataset/Nigeria_MIS_2021.cfm?flag=1.) The NMIS was conducted in accordance with established ethical procedures, including informed consent from participants and measures to ensure confidentiality and anonymity. The dataset used for this secondary analysis was anonymised, with all identifying information removed before release. Therefore, no additional ethical approval was required for the present study.

Results

Study population and IPTp uptake

A total of 3,103 women from the NMIS 2021 dataset were included in the analysis. Of these, 1,625 (52.4%) reported optimal IPTp uptake, while 1,478 (47.6%) reported fewer than three doses. These figures describe the unweighted analytic sample and should not be interpreted as a survey-weighted national coverage estimate.

Table 3 presents adjusted odds ratios from the primary survey-adjusted multivariable logistic regression model. After controlling for maternal age, place of residence, educational attainment, household wealth index, geopolitical zone, and ANC category, geopolitical zone and ANC attendance remained associated with optimal IPTp uptake. Compared with women in the North Central zone, lower adjusted odds were observed in the North East (AOR = 0.69; 95% CI: 0.49–0.98; p = 0.037), North West (AOR = 0.71; 95% CI: 0.52–0.98; p = 0.036), South East (AOR = 0.57; 95% CI: 0.39–0.83; p = 0.004), and South West (AOR = 0.52; 95% CI: 0.33–0.83; p = 0.006). The estimate for South South was lower but not statistically significant (AOR = 0.59; 95% CI: 0.32–1.08; p = 0.086). The overall ANC-category test was significant (F(2,85) = 19.50; p < 0.001). Compared with 0–3 ANC visits, 4–7 visits were associated with an AOR of 2.10 (95% CI: 1.64–2.68; p < 0.001), while ≥8 visits were associated with an AOR of 2.05 (95% CI: 1.49–2.83; p < 0.001). The survey-adjusted diagnostic supported the categorical ANC specification (F(2,84) = 18.12; p < 0.001), and the maximum VIF was 3.97. Maternal age, residence, education, and household wealth were not independently associated with optimal IPTp uptake.

Discussion

This study analysed 2021 Nigeria Malaria Indicator Survey data from 3,103 women to examine IPTp3+ uptake. Just over half (52.4%) of the unweighted sample reported IPTp3+ uptake; this is not a survey-weighted national estimate. In the survey-adjusted model, ANC category and geopolitical zone were associated with uptake, whereas maternal age, residence, education, and household wealth were not independently associated. The ANC association was nonlinear, with similar adjusted odds for 4–7 and ≥ 8 visits. The unweighted machine-learning models showed a comparable pattern but only poor-to-moderate discrimination.

Women reporting 4–7 or ≥ 8 ANC visits had about twice the adjusted odds of IPTp3+ uptake compared with those reporting 0–3 visits. Routine ANC provides opportunities for counselling, malaria-prevention education, directly observed therapy, and IPTp-SP administration [18–20]. However, ANC attendance may also reflect access to, continuity of, and engagement with health services. The cross-sectional design does

not establish that additional visits alone cause higher uptake; service content and readiness remain important [11].

Maternal education, household wealth, and residence were not significant after adjustment, possibly because ANC attendance and geopolitical zone partly captured their effects. This does not imply absent socioeconomic inequalities. Nigerian studies document socioeconomic, geographic, and service-related differences in IPTp uptake [8,21,22]. Poorer women may face delayed ANC initiation, inconsistent attendance, distance and cost barriers, or limited access to quality services [10,22,23,24]. Socioeconomic disadvantage may therefore operate indirectly through care access, making ANC attendance an access-related correlate rather than solely individual behaviour.

Geopolitical zone remained associated with uptake, with lower adjusted odds in the North East, North West, South East, and South West than North Central. The South South estimate was lower, although its confidence interval included the null. These differences may reflect facility readiness, sulfadoxine–pyrimethamine distribution, staffing, provider adherence, ANC organisation, security constraints, or cultural patterns in ANC timing and use [11,15,21,22,25]. Because NMIS could not test these explanations, the pattern should not be attributed to one factor. Region-specific linked household–facility studies are needed.

Machine learning added limited epidemiological information beyond regression and is best viewed as a methodological comparison. Random Forest and XGBoost achieved ROC-AUCs of 0.640–0.661, Brier scores of 0.225–0.228, and imperfect calibration. ANC visits and zone made the largest model-specific contributions, but SHAP and permutation importance do not estimate causal effects or identify policy priorities. Modest performance suggests that facility readiness, drug availability, provider adherence, dose timing, and ANC quality may explain additional variation [11]. Survey-aware external validation is required before predictive use. These findings have implications for malaria control and maternal health programmes in Nigeria. Policy decisions should prioritise survey-weighted associations, WHO guidance, and broader epidemiological and health-systems evidence, while treating the exploratory, unweighted machine-learning results as supplementary. Improving IPTp3+ coverage requires timely ANC access, IPTp-SP delivery at every eligible contact, reliable drug supplies, provider adherence, quality ANC services, and investigation of regional implementation gaps [11,17,25,26]. Local assessments of supply chains, service readiness, security, and community barriers should guide intervention priorities.

The strengths include the large national sample, survey-adjusted regression, assessment of ANC nonlinearity and multicollinearity, and comparison with exploratory machine learning. Limitations include the cross-sectional design, possible recall and dose misclassification, and unavailable data on ANC timing, drug stocks, provider adherence, facility readiness, security, and community norms. ANC categories should not be interpreted as causal thresholds. The unweighted machine-learning models had modest discrimination, single-split internal validation, and no external validation; therefore, their sample-specific outputs are unsuitable for population inference, causal ranking, clinical use, or policy prioritisation. Zone-level estimates may also conceal local variation.

Conclusion

Only about half of women achieved optimal IPTp uptake. ANC attendance and geographic differences were strongly associated with uptake, while age, education, wealth, and residence were not independently associated. Machine-learning models added limited epidemiological value. Strengthening timely ANC, IPTp delivery, drug supply, and provider adherence is recommended. Future studies should incorporate facility data and survey-aware predictive methods.

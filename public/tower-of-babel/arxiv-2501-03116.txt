Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorems in higher algebra
Omar Antolin Camarena, Lukas Brantner, Gijs Heuts

Abstract
. We extend the classical Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem to higher algebra by establishing a version that applies to spectral Lie algebras. We deduce this statement from a basic relation between operads in spectra: the commutative operad is the quotient of the associative operad by a right action of the spectral Lie operad. This statement, in turn, is a consequence of a fundamental relation between different 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -operads, which we articulate and prove. We deduce a variant of the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for relative enveloping algebras of 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -algebras. Our methods also give a simple construction and description of the higher enveloping 𝔼 n \mathbb{E}_{n} -algebras of a spectral Lie algebra.



1.
Introduction

The classical Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem states that the universal enveloping algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a natural filtration whose associated graded is the symmetric algebra on the underlying vector space of
\mathfrak{g}
. This statement may be phrased in the language of operads; it is essentially equivalent to the fact that the quotient of the associative operad by the right action of the Lie operad on it is the commutative operad.

In this paper, we provide an extension of this statement to the world of higher algebra (see
Theorem

1.2
), replacing the classical associative, Lie, and commutative operads by their lifts to the
\infty
-category of spectra. Correspondingly, we deduce a Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem for spectral Lie algebras (
Corollary

1.10
). In fact, these results are consequences of a more basic relation between different
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-operads, namely
Theorem

1.6
, which has no ‘classical’ analog. In particular we deduce a result in the style of Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt for
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-algebras, which we make explicit in
Corollary

1.12
. Finally, our methods provide a straightforward construction of the higher enveloping algebra of a spectral Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. In
Theorem

1.8
we show that the
\mathbb{E}_{n}
-enveloping algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
may be calculated as the Chevalley–Eilenberg homology of the
n
-fold loop object of
\mathfrak{g}
, showing that our construction agrees with the one of Knudsen
[
Knu18
]
which relies on factorisation homology.

We will now review the results of this paper in more detail. The associative and commutative operads have evident lifts to the world of higher algebra; indeed, they can easily be defined in the
\infty
-category of spectra (or any symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category with coproducts). The case of the Lie operad is less straightforward, but Salvatore
[
Sal98
]
and Ching
[
Chi05
]
have shown that it also admits a lift to the
\infty
-category of spectra. Indeed, if we define
\mathbb{L}
to be the Koszul dual
K(\mathbf{Com})
of the commutative operad, then the operadic suspension
s\mathbb{L}
(see
Section

2
) is an operad whose homology is concentrated in degree 0 and reproduces the classical Lie operad in abelian groups. Moreover, this construction recovers the more classical Koszul duality between the commutative and Lie operads in the derived
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
of any commutative ring
R
.

To explain our first result, recall that operads in the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Sp}
of spectra may be defined as associative algebra objects in the
\infty
-category of symmetric sequences in spectra (cf.
[
Kel05
,
Tri
]

[

).
The monoidal structure is given by the

(see
Section

2

\mathcal{O}\to\mathcal{P}
, we may interpret

as an
\mathcal{O}
-bimodule in symmetric sequences.

.

In what follows, we will denote the associative operad

Remark 1.11
.

The degree shift on the right-hand side of the equivalence above stems from the fact the spectral Lie operad

produces a bracket of degree -1, as compared to the classical Lie operad with bracket in degree 0. This is also reflected in the fact that the homology of a spectral Lie algebra is a

, and the associated graded is given by the sum of the cofibres of all these maps.

Given

2.
Symmetric sequences and operads

In this section, we give a brisk review of the basic facts about symmetric sequences and operads we shall require. We take the point of view that an operad is an algebra for the monoidal structure given by the composition product on the category of symmetric sequences,
following the 1-categorical argument of Carboni described by Kelly
[
Kel05
]
and Trimble
[
Tri
]
, as well as its
\infty
-categorical generalisation (cf.
[
Bra
, 4.1.2]
[
BCN25
, 3.1]
).

To make this precise, write
\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq}
for the groupoid of finite sets and bijections.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category. The
\infty
-category of
symmetric sequences
in
\mathcal{C}
is given by

\sSeq(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq},\mathcal{C}).

The value of a symmetric sequence
A
on a set with
n
elements will be denoted by
A(n)
and it is an object of
\mathcal{C}
equipped with an action of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
.

In this paper, it will be sufficient to consider the case where
\mathcal{C}
is the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Sp}
of spectra or minor variants thereof, although most of what we say will go through in much greater generality. We will need to consider three different monoidal structures on the
\infty
-category
\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})
.

Day convolution product

The first is given by
Day convolution
(cf.
[
Gla16
]

[
HA
, 2.2.6]
) based on the disjoint union monoidal structure on
\mathrm{Fin}^{\simeq}
. Explicitly, it is determined by the formula

(A\otimes B)(n)=\bigoplus_{a+b=n}\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}\Sigma_{n}\otimes_{h(\Sigma_{a}\times\Sigma_{b})}A(a)\otimes B(b);

here
\oplus
and
\otimes
denote the wedge and smash product in spectra, respectively.

The fully faithful functor
\iota:\mathrm{Sp}\to\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})
given by
\iota(X)(0)=X
and
\iota(X)(n)=0
for
n>0
refines to a symmetric monoidal functor
for this Day convolution structure, and we obtain a tensoring of
\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})
over
\mathrm{Sp}
.

Composition product

The second monoidal structure is the
composition product
; on objects, the composition product is given by

A\circ B=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(A(n)\otimes B^{\otimes n}\right)_{h\Sigma_{n}}.

One can check that for a spectrum
X
and symmetric sequence
A
, the composition
A\circ\iota(X)
is concentrated in degree
0
, that is, it lies in the essential image of
\iota
. Therefore, for a fixed
A
there is a functor
\mathrm{free}_{A}\in\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp})
satisfying
\iota(\mathrm{free}_{A}(X))=A\circ\iota(X)
. Explicitly, it is given by

\mathrm{free}_{A}(X)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left(A(n)\otimes X^{\otimes n}\right)_{h\Sigma_{n}}.

Since this action of
A
on spectra is given simply by restricting the composition product to the essential image of
\iota
, the functor
\mathrm{free}\colon\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp})
refines to a monoidal functor, where
\mathrm{End}(\mathrm{Sp})
is endowed with the monoidal structure given by composition of functors.

Definition 2.2
.

Operads
in spectra are defined to be algebra objects in the monoidal
\infty
-category
(\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp}),\circ)
, and the
\infty
-category they form is denoted by
\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})=\mathrm{Alg}(\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp}))
.

The resulting
\infty
-category of operads underlies the model category of operads in
S
-modules (cf. e.g.
[
Bra
, 5.4.2]
).
The free functor described above therefore induces a functor

\mathrm{free}\colon\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{Monad}(\mathrm{Sp}).

One can consider left modules, right modules, and bimodules over an operad
\mathcal{O}
in
\sSeq(\mathcal{O})
; a left module of the form
\iota(X)
is equivalently an algebra for the monad
\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}
and one says that
X
is an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
. We denote the
\infty
-category of
\mathcal{O}
-algebras by
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})
or simply
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}
if no confusion can arise.

Levelwise product

The third and simplest monoidal structure on
\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})
we will need is the
levelwise tensor product
, which is simply given by

(A\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}B)(n)=A(n)\otimes B(n).

We will use this monoidal structure as an auxiliary tool in our discussion of the operadic suspension and the suspension morphism. The main property we will need is the fact that

\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}:(\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})\times\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp}),\circ\times\circ)\to(\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp}),\circ)

has a lax monoidal structure
[
BCN25
, Proposition 3.9]
. As a consequence,
\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}
induces a functor

\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})\times\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})

so that given two operads
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{Q}
, the symmetric sequence
\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}\mathcal{Q}
is again an operad. On the level of algebras, we obtain a functor

(1)

\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{P}}\times\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{P}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}\mathcal{Q}}

sending a pair
(A,B)
to
A\otimes B.
This functor varies naturally in the pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{Q})
.

Suspension functor

We now turn our attention to the operation of
operadic suspension
. The suspension of a nonunital operad in spectra
\mathcal{O}
is an operad
s\mathcal{O}
such that the corresponding free algebra monads satisfy

\mathrm{free}_{s\mathcal{O}}\simeq\Sigma^{-1}\circ\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}\circ\Sigma,

and consequently,
s\mathcal{O}
-algebra structures on
X
are in one-to-one correspondence
\mathcal{O}
-algebra structures on
\Sigma X
. The underlying symmetric sequence of the operadic suspension is given by

(s\mathcal{O})(n)=\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{O}(n),

where
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is the suspension of the sphere spectrum and the
\Sigma_{n}
action in the left factor permutes the
\mathbb{S}^{1}
factors and acts trivially on the suspension coordinate.

To describe the operad structure of
s\mathcal{O}
, we consider the endomorphism operad

s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}:=\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{-1})

of
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
; the endomorphism operad of a spectrum
X
is the endomorphism object (
[
HA
, 4.7.1]
) of
X
with respect to the left tensoring of
\mathrm{Sp}
over
\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp})
.
Our notation reflects the fact that it will be
the operadic suspension of the commutative operad.

The spectrum of
n
-ary operations in
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
is given by
\mathrm{map}(\mathbb{S}^{-n},\mathbb{S}^{-1})\simeq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and tracing through the action of
\Sigma_{n}
on
\mathbb{S}^{-n}\simeq(\mathbb{S}^{-1})^{\wedge n}
we see that the action of
\Sigma_{n}
on
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}(n)\simeq\Sigma^{-1}(\mathbb{S}^{1})^{\wedge n}
is the one described above. (Equivalently,
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}(n)
is the representation sphere of
\rho
, the quotient of the standard
n
-dimensional permutation representation by its diagonal.) Using the levelwise tensor product, we define:

Definition 2.3
.

The operadic suspension functor is defined as

s:=(-)\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}:\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{Op}(\mathrm{Sp}).

On the level of algebras, tensoring with the canonical
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
(whose structure map
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{-1})
is the identity) gives a functor

(2)

\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})\xrightarrow{}\mathrm{Alg}_{s\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp}),\ \ X\ \mapsto\ X\otimes\mathbb{S}^{-1}

via (
1
). There is also an operadic desuspension operation
s^{-1}
defined by tensoring with
\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
, which is inverse to
s
. On the level of algebras, this shows that the morphism in (
2
) is an equivalence of
\infty
-categories.

Suspension morphism

Given an operad
\mathcal{O}
in spectra,
we will also need the
suspension morphism

\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}\to s\mathcal{O}.

In the special case of
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
, we define the map
\sigma\colon\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\to\mathrm{End}(\mathbb{S}^{-1})
as the map endowing
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
with the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring structure of the Spanier–Whitehead dual of
S^{1}
, which can also be described as
\Omega_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}\mathbb{S}^{0}
. Here
\mathbb{S}^{0}
, being the monoidal unit of
\mathrm{Sp}
, is equipped with its canonical
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra stucture and
\Omega_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}
is the loop functor on the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}(\mathrm{Sp})
. Notice that by construction this
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring structure on
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
is the image under
\sigma^{*}
of the canonical
s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\mathbb{S}^{-1}
used above.
For a general operad
\mathcal{O}
the definition uses the the previous map
\sigma
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a nonunital operad
\mathcal{O}
in spectra, the suspension morphism is the composite

\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}\simeq\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}\sigma}\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\simeq s\mathcal{O},

where we have used the equivalence of operads
\mathcal{O}\simeq\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathrm{lev}}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
(cf. e.g.
[
BCN25
, Section 3.2]
).

Since
\mathcal{O}
is nonunital (i.e.
\mathcal{O}(0)=0
), the free
\mathcal{O}
-algebra on the zero spectrum
0
has underlying spectrum
\mathrm{free}_{\mathcal{O}}(0)=0
and it is a zero object in
\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})
. This implies that the loop functor
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})
and the forgetful functor
\forget:\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{Sp})\to\mathrm{Sp}
satisfy

\forget\circ\Omega_{\mathcal{O}}\simeq\Omega\circ\forget.

Proposition 2.5

Given a nonunital operad
\mathcal{O}

the composite

is equivalent to the loops functor
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}}
.
Here

is the equivalence in
(

) and
\sigma^{\ast}
is induced by restricting along the suspension morphism

Proof.

The equivalence of operads

gives a functor

\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}

via (

tensoring with the

-ring spectrum
\Omega_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}\mathbb{S}^{0}
recovers the loops functor
\Omega_{\mathcal{O}}

The map of pairs of operads

gives rise to a commutative square of
\infty

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathrm{Alg}_{s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}\otimes s\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Alg}_{\mathcal{O}\otimes\mathbb{E}_{\infty}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}\times\sigma^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\otimes}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathrm{id}\otimes\sigma)^{\ast}}

Recall that the map

on the bottom row is, by construction, equivalent to the map
\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}\to s\mathcal{O}

-algebra

Remark 2.6

Heuts and Land

construct a suspension morphism

A filtered colimit of composition squares is a composition square. Dually, consider a cofiltered diagram

The claim about colimits is evident from the fact that the composition product preserves filtered colimits in each variable separately. (The same statement would have worked for sifted colimits.) {\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{L}^{\vee}}

(This happens to be precisely the termwise Spanier–Whitehead dual of the square we started with, up to operadic suspension.) If the square of the proposition were a pushout of operads, then this square would be a pushout of symmetric sequences. However, this is not the case; indeed, if it were a pushout then for
n\geq 2
there would be a cofibre sequence

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}^{\vee}(n)\to\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\vee}(n)\to s^{-1}\mathbb{L}^{\vee}(n).

The homology of these spectra is concentrated in degree zero; their integral homology is finitely generated and free of ranks
1
,
n!
, and
(n-1)!
respectively. The alternating sum of these numbers does not equal zero as soon as
n\geq 3
. This proves the first claim of the proposition. If the squares of
Theorem

1.6
were pushouts for all
k
and
n
when
m=0
, then that would imply (by pasting countably many such squares) that the square of the proposition is a pushout, which we have just ruled out.
∎

5.
Relation to the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem

Given a classical Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, let us
denote its universal enveloping algebra by
U(\mathfrak{g})
. Concretely, it is the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(\mathfrak{g})
by the two-sided ideal generated by elements

x\otimes y-y\otimes x-[x,y],\ x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

This ideal is not homogeneous, so the natural grading on
T(\mathfrak{g})
does
not
descend to a grading on
U(\mathfrak{g})
. However,
U(\mathfrak{g})
inherits an increasing filtration whose
n^{th}
piece
F_{n}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
consists of all images of elements
x_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes x_{k}
with
k\leq n
under the quotient map
T(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
.

The canonical map
\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow U(\mathfrak{g})
lands in the first filtered piece
F_{1}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
, and we therefore obtain a map of modules

\mathfrak{g}\rightarrow\mathrm{gr}_{1}(U(\mathfrak{g})).

As the associative product on
U(\mathfrak{g})
descends to a commutative product on
\mathrm{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))=\bigoplus_{i}\mathrm{gr}_{i}(U(\mathfrak{g}))
, we can induce up to obtain a homomorphism of commutative algebras
\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathrm{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g})).

Theorem 5.1

(Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem)
.

This induced morphism

\mathrm{Sym}(\mathfrak{g})\rightarrow\mathrm{gr}(U(\mathfrak{g}))

is an isomorphism.

We will prove an analogue of this result using
the relative composition products computed above.

Remark 5.2
.

We will adopt the general strategy in the proof of
[
GR17
, 6.5.2.6]
to our setting.
However, our treatment differs from the one in [op. cit.] as the latter operates over a ground field
k
and uses the commutative
k
-algebra
k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})
to generate the required filtrations.

Notation 5.3
.

Write
\mathbb{N}
for (the nerve of) the poset of nonnegative integers and
\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{disc}}
for (the nerve of) the discrete category with the same objects and only identity morphisms.

Notation 5.4

(Filtered and graded spectra)
.

The
\infty
-categories of (nonnegatively) filtered and graded spectra are defined as

\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fil}}:=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbb{N},\mathrm{Sp})\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{gr}}:=\mathrm{Fun}(\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{disc}},\mathrm{Sp}),

There are natural functors

\mathrm{Sp}\xrightarrow{c}\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fil}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fil}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{gr}}\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{gr}}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fil}}\xrightarrow{\colim}\mathrm{Sp}.

[

The functor

[

]
.

Section

2
immediately generalises to filtered and graded spectra – in particular, both
\sSeq(\mathrm{Sp}^{\mathrm{fil}})

\mathrm{gr}

Note that a filtered (graded)

Emergence of Group B streptococcus with reduced susceptibility to penicillin in mainland China
Authors: Siyu Chen, Haiying Li, Yani Zhang, Mengyang Guo, Kaihu Yao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesTo characterize the first identified Group B Streptococcus (GBS) isolate with reduced susceptibility to penicillin in mainland China by analyzing its antimicrobial susceptibility profile and genetic determinants.MethodsGBS identification was performed by latex agglutination, CAMP test and MALDI-TOF MS. Serotyping was carried out using a latex agglutination kit covering serotypes Ia-IX. Antimicrobial susceptibility testing was conducted using both microdilution (Sensititre™ ARIS™ 2X) and E-test strips. Whole-genome sequencing was performed to identify the sequence type (ST) and clonal complex (CC). The amino acid sequences of penicillin-binding proteins (PBP1A, PBP1B, PBP2A, PBP2B, PBP2X) were compared with those of the reference strain Streptococcus agalactiae 2603V/R (NC_004116).ResultsThis GBS isolate was identified as serotype III and ST23 belonging to CC23. It exhibited a penicillin MIC of 0.25 mg/L, as determined by both automated and E-test methods. Three amino acid substitutions (I377V, V510I, Q557E) were determined in the PBP2X, in which Q557E was known as critical variation associated with educed susceptibility to penicillin. In PBP2B, an amino acid substitution (V80A), an insertion (ins382D), and a deletion (446delN) were identified. In PBP1B, only one amino acid substitution (A95D) was observed. Multiple amino acid substitutions were identified in PBP1A (M1V, A29T, G739S, V746A), accompanied by an insertion (ins182N) and a deletion (740_743delNGNN). No variations were detected in PBP2A.ConclusionThis study reports the first penicillin-resistant GBS strain isolated in mainland China. Multiple PBPs variations were found except for the critical Q557E substitution in PBP2X. These findings highlight the importance of continued surveillance of penicillin susceptibility and resistance mechanisms in GBS.

Introduction

Group B streptococcus (GBS) not only poses risks to the perinatal health of both mothers and infants but also serves as a causative agent of infections in non-pregnant adults. In clinical practice, penicillin holds a dual critical role: serving both as the first-line intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis against neonatal GBS transmission and as the primary therapeutic regimen for GBS infections. In 2008, Japanese researchers Kimura et al. [1] first characterized the molecular profile of GBS strains with reduced penicillin susceptibility. Then, analogous isolates have been documented in the United States [2], Canada [3], and the United Kingdom [4]. Recently, one GBS strain exhibiting reduced penicillin susceptibility was confirmed through phenotypic testing and whole-genome sequencing. To our knowledge, this represents the first documented case of penicillin non-susceptible GBS in mainland China.

Materials and methods

Patient

The GBS isolate was recovered from the urine of a 28-year-old non-pregnant female patient in 2024. The patient underwent urine culture screening as part of a pre-operative evaluation for surgical treatment of a urethral polyp. The patient reported a two-year history of intermittent urinary pain, without associated frequency or urgency, and had no clear prior diagnosis of urinary tract infection. One month prior to surgery, she may have taken unspecified medication for worsening symptoms, although details are unavailable. Following the procedure, she received levofloxacin postoperatively and recovered uneventfully, being discharged one week later.

Identification and serotyping

The strain was conclusively identified as GBS through three independent methodologies: Streptococcal grouping latex agglutination test (Streptococcal grouping kit, OXOID, UK), Christie-Atkins-Munch-Petersen (CAMP) test, and the VITEK mass spectrometer (BIOMERIEUX, France), with all assays concordantly confirming its taxonomic classification.

The rapid latex agglutination test was used to identify the serotypes of GBS isolates, covering ten serotypes, including Ia, Ib, and II-IX. The Latex Agglutination Assay Kit was purchased from Statens Serum Institute (SSI), Denmark.

Antimicrobial susceptibility testing

The antimicrobial susceptibility of the bacterial isolate was determined through two approaches. One is using Sensititre™ ARIS™ 2X fully automated drug sensitivity assay (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA) and Thermo Fisher Streptococcus drug sensitivity plate (B4073A). The susceptibility is also tested using E-test methodology, the E-test strips were purchased from BIOMERIEUX, France. Streptococcus pneumoniae ATCC49619 was used as the quality control strain. Outcome determination was based on the 2024 American Clinical and Laboratory Standards Institute (CLSI) standards.

Whole genome sequencing

Whole genome sequencing was performed at the Beijing Novogene Bioinformatics Technology Co., Ltd. Amino acid sequences of penicillin binding proteins (PBPs) were deduced from the pbp1a, pbp1b, pbp2a, pbp2b and pbp2x genes and compared with Streptococcus agalactiae 2603V/R (GenBank accession number NC_004116).

Results

The automated susceptibility testing assays yielded a penicillin MIC of 0.25 mg/L. Similarly, E-test assays confirmed a penicillin MIC of 0.25 mg/L for the GBS isolate (Fig. 1). The GBS strain exhibited susceptibility to cefotaxime (CTX), cefuroxime (CXM), and meropenem with MIC values of 0.25 mg/L, 0.38 mg/L, and 0.12 mg/L, respectively (Fig. 1).Fig. 1E-test susceptibility results of the GBS isolate to penicillin and three β-lactam agents. E-test susceptibility results of the GBS isolate to penicillin and three β-lactam agents. The penicillin MIC (0.25 mg/L) exceeds the CLSI non-susceptibility breakpoint (>0.12 mg/L).

The isolate was identified as serotype III. Multi-locus sequence typing (MLST) assigned the sequence type ST23, belonging to clonal complex 23 (CC23). Whole-genome sequencing and further analysis revealed amino acid substitutions in PBPs. Compared to the sequence of the susceptible strain 2603V/R, three amino acid substitutions (I377V, V510I, Q557E) were determined in the PBP2X. In PBP2B, an amino acid substitution (V80A), an insertion (ins382D), and a deletion (446delN) were identified. In PBP1B, only one amino acid substitution (A95D) was observed. Multiple amino acid substitutions were identified in PBP1A (M1V, A29T, G739S, V746A), accompanied by an insertion (ins182N) and a deletion (740_743delNGNN). No variation was found in PBP2A.

Discussion

This represents the first report of a GBS with reduced susceptibility to penicillin in Mainland China, exhibiting serotype III and ST23 belonging to CC23. The penicillin MIC was 0.25 mg/L, consistent with previously reported strains (MIC range: 0.12-1.0 mg/L) [4]. Detailed characterization of GBS with reduced susceptibility to β-lactams related to mutations in the pbp2x gene was first reported in 2008, Japan [1]. Subsequent studies have identified diverse mutation patterns across different geographic regions. In Canada, distinct mutations from those described in Japan were identified in the same set of PBP-encoding genes [3]. In contrast, strains from England showed mutations in all four PBP-encoding genes, while in Germany mutations were found in pbp2x, pbp2b, and pbp1a among reduced-susceptibility strains [5]. These reduced susceptibility isolates also exhibit considerable diversity in capsular serotypes and sequence types across different countries. In Japan, a predominance of reduced susceptibility isolates belonging to capsular types Ia, Ib, II, III, VI, and VII was observed, classified within ST1, ST3, ST10, ST358, ST458, ST464, and ST1404 [5]; in England, type Ia/ST144 was reported [4]; in Germany, type Ia/ST23 [6]; in Canada, type II/ST2 [7]; and in the United States, types Ia/ST23, II/ST22, III/ST19, III/ST109, III/ST828, and V/ST1 were identified [2].

Non-susceptibility to penicillin is associated with the acquisition of amino acid substitutions near the active-site motifs in the transpeptidase domain of PBP2X, and two amino acid substitutions in PBP2X, V405A and/or Q557E, are regarded as key substitutions [1]. The Q557E substitution, which was in proximity to the 552KSG554 motif of PBP2x, corresponds to the Q552E substitution in S. pneumoniae that has been reported to be responsible for most of the reduction in susceptibility to β-lactams [8]. Other substitutions in PBP2X (including I377V and V510I) were all previously described among both β-lactam-resistant and -susceptible isolates, suggesting that these do not contribute to the increased MICs [2]. Thus, the Q557E substitution observed in this study could be the primary driver of reduced penicillin susceptibility in this clinical isolate. Concurrent mutations in PBP2B (V80A, ins382D, 446delN) and PBP1B (A95D) may further reduce binding affinity through cooperative allosteric effects but require further molecular validation.

Moreover, serotype III isolates predominate in mainland China, representing 61.5% of invasive GBS cases and belonging to major prevalent lineages [9]. The emergence of reduced penicillin susceptibility in this highly prevalent serotype raises significant public health concerns. Although prolonged antibiotic selective pressure is established to drive accumulation of resistance-conferring pbp mutations [2–4], this patient's prior to β-lactams exposure history was unavailable. Given penicillin's dual role in China as the primary agent for intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP) and first-line treatment for GBS infections, the emergence of reduced susceptibility underscores the need for systematic, longitudinal surveillance of penicillin susceptibility across all clinical GBS isolates to monitor potential resistance dissemination. Furthermore, the complete genome sequence of the present strain can be a critical reference for future monitoring.

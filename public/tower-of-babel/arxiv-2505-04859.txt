Demystifying Carleson Frames
Ilya Krishtal and Brendan Miller

Abstract
. We study spanning properties of Carleson systems and prove a recent conjecture on frame subsequences of Carleson frames. In particular, we show that if { T k ​ φ } k = 0 ∞ \{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty} is a Carleson frame, then every subsequence of the form { T N ​ k + j k ​ φ } k = 0 ∞ \{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty} where N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} and 0 ≤ j k < N 0\leq j_{k}<N is also a frame.



1.
Introduction

Motivated by applications in dynamical sampling
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
, O. Christensen et. al. have developed a theory of dynamical frames, which are frames of orbits of a vector in a Hilbert space under the action of a linear operator
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
. A special kind of such frames was introduced in
[
1
]
and called
Carleson frames
in
[
9
]
. Carleson frames possess very high redundancy and other mysterious properties that have not been observed in other classes of frames. In this note, we begin to shed some light on excessive redundancy of Carleson frames by expanding the class of Carleson systems which are known to remain frames or form complete sequences. In particular, we prove a conjecture from
[
9
]
showing that if
\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Carleson frame with a positive definite operator
T
, then every subsequence of the form
\{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq j_{k}<N
, is also a frame.

Let us make the statement above more precise.

We will call a complex sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
a
Carleson sequence
if
z_{j}\in\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and

(1)

\inf_{k}\prod_{j\neq k}\bigg|\frac{z_{k}-z_{j}}{1-z_{j}\overline{z_{k}}}\bigg|=:\delta>0.

Let
\{e_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis for a (separable) Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and assume that
T
is an operator on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
Te_{j}=z_{j}e_{j}
for some Carleson sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
. Note that hereinafter we index all sequences over
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
for the sake of convenience.

According to
[
1
, Theorem 3.16]
, the system
\{T^{k}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a Carleson frame if

(2)

\varphi=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}m_{j}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}e_{j},

where
\{m_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a complex sequence such that
0<C_{1}\leq|m_{j}|\leq C_{2}
. We will assume without loss of generality that
m_{j}=1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
(due to a change-of-basis argument).
Given a Carleson frame or a more general
Carleson system
of the form
\{T^{\lambda}\varphi\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
,
\Lambda\subseteq[0,\infty)
, with
\varphi
given by (
2
), we shall refer to the sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
as its
Carleson spectrum
.

It was proven in
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
that when
\{|z_{j}|\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
is strictly increasing, the system
\{T^{Nk}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
also forms a frame for any constant
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the authors of
[
9
]
conjectured that if the Carleson spectrum is real and nonnegative, i.e.
z_{j}\in(0,1)

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, this result can be extended to encompass Carleson systems of the form
\{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
where
j_{k}\in\{0,1,...,N-1\}
. As mentioned above, the main result of this paper establishes this conjecture. In fact, we achieve a slightly more general result in Theorem
2.3
as we consider fractional powers of
T
by allowing non-integer
j_{k}\in[0,N)
. We also establish completeness results for certain systems with complex Carleson spectrum (Theorem
3.4
) and estimate lower bounds for continuous Carleson frames (Theorem
4.2
).

Let us recall a few standard frame-theoretic notions and results that we shall utilize in our proofs (see, e.g.
[
5
]
).

For a sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
of elements in
\mathcal{H}
,
synthesis operator

\Phi:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to\mathcal{H}
is given by

(c_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\mapsto\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}f_{k}.

The adjoint
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
of the synthesis operator is given by
\Phi^{*}f=(\langle f,f_{k}\rangle)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and is called
analysis operator
.

The following is a classical characterization of frames.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose that
\{f_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{H}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

the sequence
\{f_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a frame;

(2)

the synthesis operator
\Phi:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to\mathcal{H}
is well defined, bounded, and surjective;

(3)

the analysis operator
\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is well defined, bounded and bounded below.

As in
[
9
]
, one of our key techniques is frame perturbation. In particular, we will use the following slight modification of
[
5
, Exercise 22.4]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
\{f_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
be a frame for
\mathcal{H}
with frame bounds
B\geq A>0
. If
V\subset\mathcal{H}
is a closed subspace,
P_{V}
is an orthogonal projection onto
V
, and
\{h_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is any sequence such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\|P_{V}(f_{k}-h_{k})\|^{2}\leq A

holds, then
\{P_{V}h_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a frame for
V
.

Perturbation results by themselves, however, seem to be insufficient to establish the conjecture from
[
9
]
, let alone to fully understand the spanning properties of Carleson systems. In Section
2
, we have accomplished the former goal by combining perturbation techniques with the celebrated Müntz-Szász theorem on the completeness of monomial systems in
C[0,1]
. The latter goal is far from being achieved, although, we have made some headway in Section
3
. We intend to pursue it in our future research, perhaps, utilizing techniques from sampling and interpolation in spaces of holomorphic functions
[
11
,
13
,
14
]
(or more general reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
[
10
]
), more advanced perturbation techniques (such as in
[
12
]
), and the spectral theory of totally positive matrices
[
15
]
.

2.
Carleson Systems: Synthesis Operator Perspective

In this section, we establish our main result by working with the synthesis operator of a Carleson system. For sequences of the form
\{T^{Nk+j_{k}}\varphi\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
, it is clear that the synthesis operator is well defined and bounded (i.e. they are Bessel sequences); thus, to establish the conjecture, it only remains to show its surjectivity. We note that in functional analysis it is typically much easier to obtain boundedness below of the adjoint operator than prove the surjectivity directly. In this case, it turned out to be possible to prove the surjectivity by definition whereas our attempts to use the analysis operator perspective have yielded results that were not strong enough for this purpose (nevertheless, some of those results are useful and will be presented in the following section).

It is most convenient to represent Carleson systems in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. Let
\delta_{j}
denote the
j^{th}
standard basis vector in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
, and let
D\in B(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}))
be the diagonal operator
D\delta_{j}=z_{j}\delta_{j}
where
\mathcal{Z}=\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a Carleson sequence. Then, by the above,
\{D^{k}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
when

g=(g_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}=\left(m_{j}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\right)_{j=0}^{\infty}.

As mentioned in the introduction, by passing to a change of basis, it will suffice for us to only consider

(3)

g=(g_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}=\left(\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\right)_{j=0}^{\infty}.

We will refer to such frames
\{D^{k}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
as
canonical
Carleson frames. Clearly, each Carleson frame has a canonical representation.

Since the matrix of the operator
D
is diagonal, there is typically no reason to restrict our investigation to merely integer powers of
D
. Thus, in general, we will consider the systems of the form
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, where
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...\}\subset[0,\infty)
is a countable infinite set and
D^{\lambda}\delta_{j}=z_{j}^{\lambda}\delta_{j}=e^{\lambda(\log r_{j}+i\theta_{j})}\delta_{j}
,
z_{j}=r_{j}e^{i\theta_{j}}\neq 0
,
r_{j}>0
,
\theta_{j}\in[-\pi,\pi)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

With the above notation, we may identify the synthesis operator of the sequence
\{D^{\lambda_{k}}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
with an infinite matrix
\Phi^{\Lambda}=\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}}
with the entries
\phi_{jk}^{\Lambda}
given by

(4)

\phi_{jk}^{\Lambda}=z_{j}^{\lambda_{k}}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}},\ j,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

In this section, we will assume that the Carleson spectrum
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}
satisfies
z_{j}\in(0,1)
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We also assume that
z_{j}>z_{j-1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Our proof of the surjectivity of the synthesis operator has two key ingredients, the first of which is the following perturbation lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\Lambda=\{Nk+j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
j_{k}\in[0,N)
, then there exists a constant
J\gg 0
such that
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is an outer frame for
V:=\overline{\text{span}}\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\geq J}
, i.e.
\{P_{V}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
V
.

Proof.

By
[
9
, Theorem 2.1]
, the sequence
\{D^{Nk}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
. If
J\in\mathbb{N}
is any number and
V:=\overline{\text{span}}\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\geq J}
, we can estimate

(5)

\begin{split}\bigg\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}P_{V}(D^{Nk}g-D^{Nk+j_{k}}g)\bigg\|^{2}&=\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z_{j}^{Nk}\sqrt{1-z_{j}^{2}}(1-z_{j}^{j_{k}})\bigg|^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}(1-z_{j}^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z_{j}^{2Nk}(1-z_{j}^{j_{k}})^{2}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}(1-z_{j}^{2})\frac{(1-z_{j}^{N})^{2}}{1-z_{j}^{2N}}\\
&\leq\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}(1-z_{j}^{2}).\end{split}

The Carleson condition (
1
) implies that
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-z_{j}^{2})<\infty
(see
[
14
, Lecture VI]
), so for sufficiently large
J
we have

\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}1-z_{j}^{2}<A,

where
A
is the lower frame bound for
\{D^{Nk}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Thus,
\{P_{V}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
V
by Lemma
1.2
.
∎

Note that the estimates (
5
), albeit with integer
j_{k}
, were already carried out in the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 2.4]
. However, the purpose of this calculation in
[
9
]
was to argue that sequences of the form
\{D^{Nk}g\}_{k=0}^{J-1}\cup\{D^{Nk+j_{k}}g\}_{k=J}^{\infty}
formed a frame for sufficiently large values of
J
(and integer
j_{k}
). Our reinterpretation of these estimates is crucial to begin the proof of the main theorem. To finish it off, we utilize Lemma
2.2
below.

We prefer to state Lemma
2.2
in a more general form than is needed for the proof of the main result by permitting a larger class of sets
\Lambda
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...\}\subseteq[0,\infty)
satisfy the generalized Müntz-Szász condition

(6)

\sum_{\lambda_{k}\in\Lambda}\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}^{2}+1}=\infty,

and
\mathcal{Z}
be an increasing Carleson sequence of positive numbers. Assume also that the function
\vartheta:[0,1)\to\mathbb{C}
given by

(7)

\vartheta(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{\lambda_{k}},\ z\in[0,1),

is well defined and satisfies

(8)

\sup_{z\in[0,1)}\vartheta(z){(1-z)}<\infty.

Let
\mathcal{Z}_{n}=\mathcal{Z}\setminus\{z_{0},z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and assume that the synthesis matrix
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}
is surjective for a fixed
J\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the synthesis matrix
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}}
is also surjective.

Proof.

Observe that assumption (
8
) implies that the matrices
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define bounded operators on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.
Moreover, to complete the proof it suffices to show that
the matrix
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}}
is surjective. Indeed, the lemma would then follow after finitely many successive applications of this result.

Given
c\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
, let
\vartheta_{c}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
denote the function

\vartheta_{c}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{\lambda_{k}}.

Clearly,
\vartheta_{c}
is well defined and analytic by the assumption on
\vartheta
.
Identifying the matrix
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}
with the operator from
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
to
V_{n}
, we get

\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}c=\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z_{j}^{\lambda_{k}}\sqrt{1-z_{j}^{2}}\delta_{j}=\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\vartheta_{c}(z_{j})\sqrt{1-z_{j}^{2}}\delta_{j},\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

We will establish the surjectivity of the operator
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to V_{J-1}
utilizing the surjectivity of
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to V_{J}
.
It suffices to show that
\delta_{J-1}
is in the image of
the operator
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}}
.

Our assumptions imply that the system

\{P_{V_{J}}D^{\lambda_{k}}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a frame for
V_{J}:=\overline{\text{span}}\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\geq J}
; we will use its frame bounds
B\geq A>0
. In particular, the operator
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}})^{*}
is invertible and we have

\left\|(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}})^{*}\left(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}})^{*}\right)^{-1}\right\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{B}}{A}.

Define
M:=\sqrt{\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-z_{j}^{2})}
and

(9)

\gamma:=\sqrt{(1-z_{J-1}^{2})\vartheta(z^{2}_{J-1})},

and choose
\varepsilon>0
such that

\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}\bigg(1+\gamma M\frac{\sqrt{B}}{A}\bigg)^{-1}.

By the full Müntz-Szász theorem
[
4
]
, we can find a continuous function

\vartheta_{b}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}z^{\lambda_{k}},\ z\in[0,1],

(where
b=(b_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}
is finitely supported) such that

\left|\sqrt{1-z_{J-1}^{2}}\vartheta_{b}(z_{J-1})-1\right|<\varepsilon

and
|\vartheta_{b}(z_{j})|<\varepsilon
for all
j\geq J
.
Let

c=(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}})^{*}\left(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}})^{*}\right)^{-1}\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}b

so that

c=(c_{k})_{k=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
satisfies

\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}c=\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J}}b=\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}\vartheta_{b}(z_{j})\sqrt{1-z_{j}^{2}}\delta_{j}

and

\|c\|\leq\frac{\sqrt{B}}{A}\bigg(\sum_{j=J}^{\infty}|\vartheta_{b}(z_{j})|^{2}(1-z_{j})^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}<\frac{\sqrt{B}}{A}\varepsilon M.

Then

\displaystyle\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}}(b-c)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-z_{J-1}^{2}}\big(\vartheta_{b}(z_{J-1})-\vartheta_{c}(z_{J-1})\big)\delta_{J-1},

and

\displaystyle\bigg|\sqrt{1-z_{J-1}^{2}}\big(\vartheta_{b}(z_{J-1})

\displaystyle-\vartheta_{c}(z_{J-1})\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{1-z_{J-1}^{2}}|\vartheta_{b}(z_{J-1})|-\sqrt{1-z_{J-1}^{2}}\bigg|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}z^{\lambda_{k}}_{J-1}\bigg|

\displaystyle>1-\varepsilon-\|c\|\sqrt{(1-z_{J-1}^{2})\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{2\lambda_{k}}_{J-1}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\varepsilon-\gamma\varepsilon M\frac{\sqrt{B}}{A}>0

by the choice of
\varepsilon
. We conclude that
\delta_{J-1}

is in the range of
\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}_{J-1}}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Our main result follows as a combination of Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
. Indeed, from Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that the matrix obtained by removing the first
J
rows of the synthesis matrix is surjective. Lemma
2.2
then allows us to add the deleted rows of the matrix one-by-one while preserving the surjectivity. The frame property then follows by Proposition
1.1
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda=\{Nk+j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset[0,\infty)
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
j_{k}\in[0,N)
. Then the sequence
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

Proof.

We only need to verify that the assumptions of Lemma
2.2
hold for
\Lambda=\{Nk+j_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
The generalized Müntz-Szász condition holds for
\Lambda
by comparison with the harmonic series. The condition on
\vartheta
follows from

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{\lambda_{k}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}z^{k}=\frac{1}{1-z},\ z\in[0,1),

so that
\sup_{z\in[0,1)}\vartheta(z){(1-z)}\leq 1
in (
8
) and
\gamma\leq 1
in (
9
).
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
be a countable subset and suppose that there exist constants
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<|\Lambda\cap[Nk,(N+1)k)|<M
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

Proof.

The hypotheses guarantee that
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a Bessel sequence and
\Lambda
contains a subsequence
\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},...
such that
0\leq\lambda_{k}-Nk<N-1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. It follows from Theorem
2.3
that
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
has a lower frame bound.
∎

The results of this section can be viewed as a strengthening of Carleson’s original Theorem on Bases and Interpolation
[
14
]
for positive real Carleson sequences. By
H^{2}=H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
we denote the Hardy space of analytic functions of
\mathbb{D}
with square-summable power series. Given a sequence
\mathcal{Z}=\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfying
\sum_{j}1-|z_{j}|<\infty
, we may form the Blaschke product

B_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)=\prod_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\frac{z_{j}}{|z_{j}|}\frac{z-z_{j}}{1-\overline{z_{j}}z}.

Note that any Carleson sequence
\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
automatically satisfies
\sum_{j}1-|z_{j}|<\infty
. Finally, given
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let us denote
H^{2}_{\Lambda}=\overline{\text{span}}\{z^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\subset H^{2}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers with each
z_{j}\in(0,1)
. Suppose that
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
and there exist constants
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
0<|\Lambda\cap[Nk,(N+1)k)|<M
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(1)

The sequence
\mathcal{Z}
is a Carleson sequence.

(2)

The set of functions

\bigg\{\frac{B_{\mathcal{Z}}}{z-z_{j}}\bigg\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

forms a Riesz basis for
H^{2}\ominus B_{\mathcal{Z}}H^{2}
.

(3)

Given any
(a_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
, there exists
f\in H^{2}_{\Lambda}
such that
a_{j}=f(z_{j})\sqrt{1-z_{j}^{2}}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Consider a (not necessarily real) Carleson sequence

\mathcal{Z}=\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

such that
z_{k}^{N}=z_{\ell}^{N}
for some
k\neq\ell
. The analysis operator corresponding to
\{D^{Nk}g\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has a matrix representation with
ij
-entry given by

\overline{z}^{Ni}_{j}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}.

Hence, the
k^{th}
column is a scalar multiple of the
\ell^{th}
column, and thus the matrix cannot be bounded below. By Proposition
1.1
, this means
\{D^{Nk}g\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
cannot form a frame. This example shows that one must exercise caution when attempting to generalize the results in this section. Nevertheless, the following proposition provides evidence that the argument used in the proof of Theorem
2.3
could be generalized to a wider class of Carleson sequences.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{Z}=\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a Carleson sequence with
z_{j}\in\mathbb{D}
and
|z_{j}|<|z_{j+1}|
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
D
and
g
be as before and let
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
satisfy the hypotheses of Corollary
2.4
. If
\{P_{V_{J}}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
V_{J}=\overline{\text{span}}\{\delta_{j}\}_{j\geq J}
, then there exists a countable subset
S\subset\mathbb{D}
with no accumulation points in
\mathbb{D}
such that if
\{z_{j}\}_{j=0}^{J-1}\cap S=\emptyset
, then
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
forms a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

Proof.

An easy calculation shows that
\|P_{V_{J}}D^{\lambda}g\|\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
, therefore
\{P_{V_{J}}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is necessarily a redundant frame. This means we may choose
0\neq c=(c_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
such that

0=\vartheta_{c}(z_{j})=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}c_{\lambda}z_{j}^{\lambda}.

If
\vartheta_{c}(z_{J-1})\neq 0
and
\Phi:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})\to V_{J-1}
is the synthesis operator corresponding to
\{P_{V_{J-1}}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
, then

\Phi c=\vartheta_{c}(z_{J-1})\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\delta_{J-1}.

Thus
\delta_{J-1}\in\text{Im}(\Phi)
, so
\Phi
is surjective and
\{P_{V_{J-1}}D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is a frame for
V_{J-1}
. Finally, we only need to note that
\vartheta_{c}\in H^{2}
, and hence has a countable zero set which does not accumulate onto any point in
\mathbb{D}
. The result now follows by induction.
∎

3.
Carleson Systems: Analysis Operator Perspective

The discrepancy in the assumptions on the set
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
in Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
warrants additional investigation. In this section, we use the analysis operator
(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}})^{*}
,
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{D}
, to establish the completeness of the system
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
for a set
\Lambda
of sufficiently big logarithmic block density.

Definition 3.1
.

For a set
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
, its
logarithmic block density

L(\Lambda)
is defined by

(10)

L(\Lambda)=\inf_{\mu>1}\frac{1}{\log\mu}\limsup_{t\to\infty}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda\cap[t,\mu t]}\frac{1}{\lambda}.

Observe that
L(\Lambda)>0
implies the generalized Müntz-Szász condition. For sets that satisfy the assumption of Lemma
2.1
, we have
L(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{N}
.

The main theorem of this section is based on the following result, which L. A. Rubel established in
[
16
, §3]
en route to generalizing Carlson’s Theorem on Entire Functions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
and
f
is a non-zero entire function satisfying the following three conditions:

(1)

f(z)\leq Me^{\tau|z|}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, for some
M,\tau>0
;

(2)

f(iy)\leq Me^{c|y|}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
, for some
M>0
and
c<\pi
;

(3)

f(\lambda)=0
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Then
L(\Lambda)\leq\frac{c}{\pi}
.

Remark 3.3
.

In his derivations in
[
16
, §3]
, Rubel was only concerned with the case of
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. This assumption, however, is unnecessary as follows from his computations and remark in
[
16
, §4]
.

Lemma
3.2
is all we need to establish the following completeness result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathbb{D}_{c}
is a Carleson sequence, where

\mathbb{D}_{c}=\{z=re^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}:\theta\in[-c,c]\},\ c\in[0,\pi).

Assume also that
\Lambda\subset[0,\infty)
satisfies
L(\Lambda)>\frac{c}{\pi}
.
Then the system
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is complete in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

Proof.

Assume towards contradiction that the system
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
is not complete. Then there exists
b=(b_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
such that
\langle b,D^{\lambda}g\rangle=0
for each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, i.e. the analysis operator of
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
satisfies
(\Phi^{\Lambda}_{\mathcal{Z}})^{*}b=0
. Consider the function
\psi:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\begin{split}\psi(\omega)=\langle b,D^{\omega}g\rangle&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}b_{j}\bar{z}_{j}^{\omega}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\beta_{j}e^{\omega(\log r_{j}-i\theta_{j})},\ \beta_{j}=b_{j}\sqrt{1-|z_{j}|^{2}}.\end{split}

Since
\beta=(\beta_{j})_{j=0}^{\infty}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,
\psi
is an entire function. Moreover, it is immediate that
\psi
satisfies all other assumptions of Lemma
3.2
(including
\psi\not\equiv 0
, which follows since the system
\{D^{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a frame). Thus, we arrived at a contradiction with the density assumption
L(\Lambda)>\frac{c}{\pi}
and the proof is complete.
∎

The above result underscores the relevance of the sampling and interpolation theory of entire functions in the context of Carleson systems. We intend to pursue this avenue of research in our future investigation.

4.
Frame Bounds and Continuous Carleson Frames

In this section, we discuss the bounds of discrete and continuous Carleson frames.

Let us recall the definition of a continuous frame.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a measure space
(X,\mu)
, a family
\{f_{t}\}_{t\in X}
of vectors in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
is said to be a continuous frame if the function
f\mapsto\langle f,f_{t}\rangle
is measurable for each
t\in X
, and there exist constants
B\geq A>0
such that

A\|f\|^{2}\leq\int_{X}|\langle f,f_{t}\rangle|^{2}d\mu(t)\leq B\|f\|^{2}

for all
f\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
.

For our purposes, we will only be interested in
X=[0,\infty)
endowed with the Lebesgue measure.

Theorem 4.2

(
[
8
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a Carleson sequence with
z_{j}\in\mathbb{D}
and set

\delta:=\inf_{k}\prod_{j\neq k}\bigg|\frac{z_{k}-z_{j}}{1-z_{j}\overline{z_{k}}}\bigg|.

If
D\in B(\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}))
is the diagonal operator
D\delta_{j}=z_{j}\delta_{j}
, and
g
is as in (
3
), then
\{D^{k}g\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
forms a frame for
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
with frame bounds
\Delta\geq\Delta^{-1}>0
where

(11)

\Delta:=\frac{2(1-2\log{\delta)}}{\delta^{4}}.

Using the above estimates for the frame bounds, we show that for positive real Carleson sequences, the family
\{D^{t}g\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
forms a continuous frame and compute its frame bounds.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be an increasing positive real Carleson sequence, and let
D
and
g
be as before. Then, the family
\{D^{t}g\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
forms a continuous frame with

\frac{\Delta^{-1}(z_{0}^{2}-1)}{2\log{z_{0}}}\|f\|^{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}|\langle f,D^{t}g\rangle|^{2}dt\leq\Delta\|f\|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\Delta
be as in Theorem
4.2
. Using Riemann sums, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\langle f,D^{t}g\rangle|^{2}\;dt

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}|\langle f,D^{n/N}g\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}|\langle f,D^{n+k/N}g\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}|\langle D^{k/N}f,D^{n}g\rangle|^{2}.

By Theorem
4.2
, we have that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\langle D^{k/N}f,D^{n}g\rangle|^{2}\leq\Delta\|D^{k/N}f\|^{2}\leq\Delta\|f\|^{2}.

Therefore, it follows that
\Delta
is an upper frame bound for
\{D^{t}g\}_{t\in[0,\infty)}
. Similarly, we can compute

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|\langle D^{k/N}f,D^{n}g\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Delta^{-1}\|D^{k/N}f\|^{2},

so that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}|\langle D^{k/N}f,D^{n}g\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}\Delta^{-1}\|D^{k/N}f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{N\to\infty}\Delta^{-1}\|f\|^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N}z_{0}^{2k/N}

\displaystyle=\Delta^{-1}\|f\|^{2}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1-z_{0}^{2}}{N(1-z_{0}^{2/N})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Delta^{-1}(z_{0}^{2}-1)}{2\log{z_{0}}}\|f\|^{2}.

This completes the proof.
∎

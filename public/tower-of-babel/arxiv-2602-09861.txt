The Deformed Dirac Oscillator in Linear-Fractional Doubly Special Relativity
Nosratollah Jafari, Abdelmalek Boumali

Abstract
We study the ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) -dimensional Dirac oscillator within a class of doubly special relativity (DSR) models generated by linear-fractional (projective) transformations on momentum space that preserve both the invariant speed of light and a high-energy observer-independent scale E p E_{\mathrm{p}} . Starting from the associated deformed Casimir invariants, we construct the coordinate-space Dirac equations for three inequivalent choices of the deformation vector (time-like, space-like, and light-like). For the time-like and light-like realizations the deformation induces momentum-dependent effective mass operators, which makes the coordinate-space formulation sensitive to operator ordering. To retain locality and obtain solvable second-order equations we adopt a reverted-product ordering prescription. Closed-form relativistic energy spectra and eigenfunctions are obtained in all three geometries, and the standard Dirac-oscillator results are recovered smoothly in the limit E p → ∞ E_{\mathrm{p}}\to\infty . Finally, we derive the nonrelativistic expansion of the positive-energy branch and show that the deformation geometry controls the leading rest-energy shift and the renormalization of the oscillator level spacing.



Keywords:
Doubly Special Relativity; linear-fractional
transformations; modified dispersion relation; Dirac oscillator; nonrelativistic
limit; operator ordering.

1
Introduction

Doubly special relativity (DSR) extends special-relativistic kinematics
by postulating, in addition to the invariant speed of light, a second
observer-independent scale—typically an energy scale
E_{\mathrm{p}}
associated
with Planckian physics. This hypothesis leads to deformed dispersion
relations and nonlinear realizations of Lorentz transformations in
momentum space
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.
In many developments, DSR-inspired kinematics is naturally interpreted
in geometric terms, most notably through curved momentum space and
related phase-space formulations of the relativity principle. A mathematically
transparent realization of several DSR proposals is provided by projective
(linear-fractional) actions on momentum space, which organize seemingly
different deformations into a common geometric structure
[
4
]
;
contemporary work further clarifies the relation between DSR,
\kappa
-Poincaré
structures, and momentum-space curvature
[
5
]
.

The Dirac oscillator (DO) is a paradigmatic exactly solvable relativistic
model obtained by a non-minimal, linear-in-coordinate modification
of the Dirac momentum operator. Early appearances of oscillator-like
Dirac dynamics can be traced to the work of Itô, Mori and Carrière

[
6
]
, while the modern covariant formulation known as the
Dirac–Moshinsky oscillator was popularized by Moshinsky and Szczepaniak

[
7
]
(see also
[
8
]
). The DO
exhibits a rich algebraic structure, including hidden supersymmetry

[
9
]
, and it provides a bridge between relativistic
quantum mechanics and quantum-optics models: in
(2+1)
dimensions
it admits an exact mapping onto the Jaynes–Cummings interaction,
enabling ion-trap simulation proposals
[
10
]
. The model
and its extensions have been applied in a wide range of contexts,
including relativistic confinement, Dirac materials, and thermodynamic/statistical
investigations; see, e.g., the overview
[
11
]
and representative
studies
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
.

Motivated by the use of the DO as a probe of modified relativistic
kinematics, recent work has examined oscillator dynamics in DSR and
related deformed frameworks. Comparative analyses of the DO in the
Magueijo–Smolin and Amelino–Camelia DSR models, including controlled
nonrelativistic expansions, were reported in
[
17
]
,
while thermodynamic properties of DSR-modified oscillators were studied
in
[
18
,
19
]
. Related DSR wave-equation
problems with confining interactions have also been investigated;
see, for example,
[
20
]
.

Objective and outline.

The objective of this paper is to provide an analytic and geometry-sensitive
treatment of the one-dimensional Dirac oscillator in a projective
(linear-fractional) DSR setting. Specifically, we: (i) formulate the
deformed mass shell induced by a general projective momentum map and
derive the associated coordinate-space Dirac equations; (ii) implement
the Dirac-oscillator coupling for time-like, space-like, and light-like
deformation geometries; (iii) resolve the operator-ordering ambiguity
that arises in the presence of momentum-dependent effective mass operators;
and (iv) obtain exact relativistic spectra and eigenfunctions, as
well as the nonrelativistic limit of the positive-energy branch. Section 2
develops the formalism and presents the exact solutions, and Section 3
summarizes the main results and their physical implications.

2
Linear-fractional DSR Dirac oscillator

2.1
Projective momentum map and modified mass shell

We consider a projective map
f
acting on momentum space,

f:\quad p^{\mu}\longmapsto\pi^{\mu}=\frac{p^{\mu}}{1+E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-1}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}a^{\alpha}p^{\beta}},

(1)

where
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1)
in
(1+1)
dimensions
and
a^{\mu}
is a constant deformation vector. The auxiliary variables

\pi^{\mu}
transform linearly under the Lorentz group, while the
physical momenta
p^{\mu}
transform under the induced nonlinear
action
f^{-1}\Lambda f

[
4
]
. The corresponding
linear-fractional Lorentz transformation reads

p^{\prime}{}^{\mu}=\frac{\Lambda_{\ \nu}^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{1+E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-1}a_{\alpha}(\delta_{\ \beta}^{\alpha}-\Lambda_{\ \beta}^{\alpha})p^{\beta}}.

(2)

The associated invariant mass shell (deformed Casimir) can be written
as

\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{\left(1+E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-1}\,\eta_{\alpha\beta}a^{\alpha}p^{\beta}\right)^{2}}=m^{2},

(3)

which reduces to the standard relativistic dispersion relation as

E_{\mathrm{p}}\to\infty
.

2.2
Geometric deformations and linearized Dirac equations

To construct first-order wave equations we linearize the deformed
dispersion relation by associating the numerator with the Dirac operator
and the denominator with an effective, deformation-dependent mass
factor. We employ the correspondence
p_{\mu}\to-\mathrm{i}\partial_{\mu}

and use the representation
\gamma^{0}=\sigma_{3}
,
\gamma^{1}=\mathrm{i}\sigma_{2}
.

(i) Time-like deformation (Magueijo–Smolin type)

The deformed mass shell is taken as

\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{(1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}})^{2}}=m^{2},

(4)

which linearizes to
\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}=m\left(1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}}\right)
.
In coordinate space this yields

\left[\,\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\right]\Psi=0.

(5)

(ii) Space-like deformation

We take

\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{(1-p/E_{\mathrm{p}})^{2}}=m^{2},

(6)

so that
\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}=m\left(1-p/E_{\mathrm{p}}\right)
. Using
p\to-\mathrm{i}\partial_{x}

gives

\left[\,\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m\left(1+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)\right]\Psi=0.

(7)

(iii) Light-like (null) deformation

For the null case we use

\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{\left(1-(E+p)/E_{\mathrm{p}}\right)^{2}}=m^{2},

(8)

which linearizes to
\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}=m\left[1-(E+p)/E_{\mathrm{p}}\right]

and hence

\left[\,\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m\left(1-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)\right]\Psi=0.

(9)

2.3
Dirac-oscillator coupling and ordering

The one-dimensional Dirac oscillator is introduced through the non-minimal
substitution
[
7
]

p\longrightarrow\Pi=p-\mathrm{i}m\omega\,\beta x,\qquad\beta\equiv\gamma^{0},\qquad p=-\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}.

(10)

For stationary states
\Psi(x,t)=e^{-\mathrm{i}Et}\psi(x)
and
\psi=(\psi_{1},\psi_{2})^{T}
,
the time-like geometry yields an energy-dependent but
x
-independent
effective mass
M_{t}(E)=m(1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}})
. In contrast, the space-like
and light-like realizations generate momentum-dependent effective
mass operators

M_{s}(p)=m\left(1-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right),\qquad M_{\ell}(E,p)=m\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right),

(11)

which do not commute with
x
once
p=-\mathrm{i}\partial_{x}
.

To maintain a local second-order equation after decoupling the Dirac
system in coordinate space, we adopt an ordered (reverted-product)
prescription, schematically

(E+M(p))\,(p\pm\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\ \longrightarrow\ (p\pm\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\,(E+M(p)),

(12)

consistent with the ordering used in related DSR oscillator problems

[
17
,
20
]
.

2.4
Exact solutions and nonrelativistic limits (time-like, space-like,
light-like)

We now solve the deformed Dirac-oscillator problem for the three deformation
geometries. We use the stationary ansatz
\Psi(x,t)=e^{-\mathrm{i}Et}\psi(x)

with
\psi(x)=(\psi_{1}(x),\psi_{2}(x))^{T}
, the representation

\gamma^{0}=\sigma_{3}
,
\gamma^{1}=\mathrm{i}\sigma_{2}
, and the Dirac-oscillator
substitution
p\to\Pi=p-\mathrm{i}m\omega\,\beta x
with
\beta=\gamma^{0}
.
In all cases we write
p=-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}
.

For a given deformation geometry, the stationary Dirac equation can
be written in the form

(E-\mathcal{M})\psi_{1}=(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\psi_{2},\qquad(E+\mathcal{M})\psi_{2}=(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\psi_{1},

(13)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the effective mass (a c-number in the time-like
case and an operator in the space-like/light-like cases). To obtain
a local second-order equation we decouple (
13
).
In the operator-valued cases we apply the ordered (reverted-product)
rule (
12
) when moving
(E+\mathcal{M})
past
(p\pm\mathrm{i}m\omega x)
.

A key identity used repeatedly is

\displaystyle(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)

\displaystyle=p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}+\mathrm{i}m\omega[x,p]

\displaystyle=p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}-m\omega,

(14)

since
[x,p]=\mathrm{i}
.

(i) Time-like deformation (Magueijo–Smolin): exact spectrum and
NR limit

For the time-like deformation, the stationary equation implies the
energy-dependent (but
x
-independent) effective mass

\mathcal{M}\equiv M_{t}(E)=m\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right).

(15)

Since
M_{t}
is a c-number for each eigenvalue
E
, ordering issues
do not arise. Starting from (
13
), multiply
the first equation by
(E+M_{t})
and substitute the second equation:

\displaystyle(E+M_{t})(E-M_{t})\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=(E+M_{t})(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(E+M_{t})\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\psi_{1}.

(16)

Thus,

\left[(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)-\left(E^{2}-M_{t}^{2}\right)\right]\psi_{1}=0.

(17)

Using (
14
) we obtain the harmonic-oscillator form

\left[p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}-m\omega\right]\psi_{1}(x)=\left(E^{2}-M_{t}^{2}\right)\psi_{1}(x).

(18)

The standard oscillator eigenvalue relation is

\left[p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}\right]\phi_{n}(x)=m\omega(2n+1)\phi_{n}(x),\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots

(19)

so (
18
) implies

E^{2}-m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}=2m\omega\,n.

(20)

Solving (
20
) yields the exact spectrum

E_{n}^{(\pm)}=\frac{-m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}\pm\sqrt{\,m^{2}+2m\omega n\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)\,}}{1-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}.

(21)

Eigenfunctions.

Up to normalization,

\psi_{1,n}(x)\propto\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega}{2}x^{2}\right)\,H_{n}\!\left(\sqrt{m\omega}\,x\right),

(22)

and the lower component follows from (
13
):

\psi_{2,n}(x)=\frac{1}{E_{n}^{(+)}+M_{t}(E_{n}^{(+)})}\,(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\,\psi_{1,n}(x)\quad\text{(positive branch),}

(23)

with an analogous expression for the negative branch.

Nonrelativistic limit (positive-energy branch).

Write
E_{n}^{(+)}=m+\varepsilon_{n}
with
\varepsilon_{n}\ll m

and assume
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}\ll 1
. Expanding (
21
) gives

E_{n}^{(+)}=m-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}+\frac{m^{3}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}+\omega n-\frac{\omega^{2}n^{2}}{2m}+\frac{m\,\omega^{2}n^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\omega^{3}}{m^{2}},\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}\right).

(24)

Hence, in the time-like geometry the leading Planck-suppressed effect
is a rest-energy shift
\sim-m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}
, while the leading oscillator
spacing remains
\varepsilon_{n}\simeq\omega n
.

(ii) Space-like deformation: exact spectrum, phase removal, and NR
limit

For the space-like deformation the stationary equation leads to a
momentum-dependent mass operator,

\mathcal{M}\equiv M_{s}(p)=m\left(1-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right).

(25)

Starting again from (
13
), decoupling with the
ordering rule (
12
) yields

\left[(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)-E^{2}+M_{s}^{2}\right]\psi_{1}=0.

(26)

Using (
14
) and

M_{s}^{2}=m^{2}\left(1-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}=m^{2}\left(1-\frac{2p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}+\frac{p^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right),

(27)

we obtain

\left[\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)p^{2}-\frac{2m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\,p+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}+\left(m^{2}-m\omega-E^{2}\right)\right]\psi_{1}(x)=0.

(28)

The linear term in
p
corresponds to a first-derivative term in
coordinate space. It can be removed by a constant phase (gauge) transformation

\psi_{1}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{s}x}\,\chi_{1}(x),\qquad\beta_{s}=\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)}.

(29)

Indeed,
p\psi_{1}=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{s}x}(p+\beta_{s})\chi_{1}
and
p^{2}\psi_{1}=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{s}x}(p+\beta_{s})^{2}\chi_{1}
;
with the choice (
29
), the coefficient
of the linear-
p
term vanishes and (
28
)
reduces to

\left[\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}\right]\chi_{1}=\Lambda_{s}\,\chi_{1},

(30)

with

\Lambda_{s}=E^{2}-m^{2}+m\omega+\frac{m^{4}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+m^{2}}.

(31)

Dividing (
30
) by
\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)
,
we identify a harmonic oscillator with effective frequency

\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}},

(32)

and hence

\frac{\Lambda_{s}}{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}=m\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(2n+1)=\frac{m\omega}{\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}}(2n+1).

(33)

Therefore,

\Lambda_{s}=m\omega\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}\,(2n+1),

(34)

and inserting (
31
) gives the exact spectrum

E_{n}^{(\pm)}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}-m\omega+m\omega\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}\,(2n+1)-\frac{m^{4}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+m^{2}}}.

(35)

Eigenfunctions.

Up to normalization,

\chi_{1,n}(x)\propto\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2}x^{2}\right)\,H_{n}\!\left(\sqrt{m\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,x\right),\qquad\psi_{1,n}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{s}x}\chi_{1,n}(x),

(36)

and
\psi_{2,n}
follows from (
13
).

Nonrelativistic limit (positive-energy branch).

Expanding (
35
) for
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}\ll 1
and
\omega n\ll m

gives

E_{n}^{(+)}=m-\frac{m^{3}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}+\omega n\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{m^{2}\omega}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}-\frac{\omega^{2}n^{2}}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\,\frac{\omega^{2}\,n(5n+1)}{4m}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\omega^{3}}{m^{2}},\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}\right).

(37)

Thus, in the space-like geometry the leading deformation in the excitation
energies is a spacing renormalization
\omega\to\omega\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)
,
while there is no
\mathcal{O}(1/E_{\mathrm{p}})
rest-energy shift.

(iii) Light-like (null) deformation: quadratic quantization, exact
spectrum, and NR limit

For the light-like deformation, the stationary effective mass depends
on both
E
and
p
:

\mathcal{M}\equiv M_{\ell}(E,p)=m\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)=m\left(\alpha-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right),\qquad\alpha:=1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}.

(38)

Decoupling (
13
) with the ordering rule (
12
)
gives

\left[(p+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(p-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)-E^{2}+M_{\ell}^{2}\right]\psi_{1}=0.

(39)

Using (
14
) and

M_{\ell}^{2}=m^{2}\left(\alpha-\frac{p}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}=m^{2}\left(\alpha^{2}-\frac{2\alpha}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}p+\frac{p^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right),

(40)

we obtain

\left[\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)p^{2}-\frac{2m^{2}\alpha}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\,p+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}+\left(m^{2}\alpha^{2}-m\omega-E^{2}\right)\right]\psi_{1}(x)=0.

(41)

Remove the linear-
p
term by

\psi_{1}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{\ell}x}\,\chi_{1}(x),\qquad\beta_{\ell}=\frac{m^{2}\alpha}{E_{\mathrm{p}}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)}=\frac{m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)}{E_{\mathrm{p}}\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)}.

(42)

This reduces (
41
) to

\left[\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)p^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}\right]\chi_{1}=\Lambda_{\ell}\,\chi_{1},

(43)

where

\Lambda_{\ell}=E^{2}+m\omega-m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}+\frac{m^{4}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+m^{2}}.

(44)

Since the operator on the left-hand side of (
43
)
is the same oscillator operator as in the space-like case, its eigenvalues
are
m\omega\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}(2n+1)
, yielding the light-like
quantization condition

E^{2}+m\omega-m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}+\frac{m^{4}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+m^{2}}=m\omega\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}\,(2n+1).

(45)

Equation (
45
) is quadratic in
E

and can be solved in closed form, giving the exact spectrum

E_{n}^{(\pm)}=-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\pm\frac{\sqrt{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}+m^{2}}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\,\sqrt{\,m^{2}-m\omega+m\omega\sqrt{1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}\,(2n+1)\,}.

(46)

Eigenfunctions.

The upper component is again an oscillator Hermite function multiplied
by the phase
e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{\ell}x}
:

\chi_{1,n}(x)\propto\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}}{2}x^{2}\right)\,H_{n}\!\left(\sqrt{m\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}}\,x\right),\qquad\psi_{1,n}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\beta_{\ell}x}\chi_{1,n}(x),

(47)

with
\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}=\omega/\sqrt{1+m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}
as before,
but now
\beta_{\ell}
depends on the eigenvalue
E
through (
42
).
The lower component follows from (
13
).

Nonrelativistic limit (positive-energy branch).

Expanding (
46
) for
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}\ll 1
and
\omega n\ll m

gives

E_{n}^{(+)}=m-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}+\frac{m^{3}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}+\omega n\left(1+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)+\frac{m^{2}\omega}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}-\frac{\omega^{2}n^{2}}{2m}-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\,\frac{\omega^{2}\,n(3n+1)}{4m}+\mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{\omega^{3}}{m^{2}},\frac{1}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{3}}\right).

(48)

Thus, the light-like geometry combines the MS-type
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}})

rest-energy shift with the space-like-type
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})

renormalization of the leading level spacing.

Geometric comparison (within this solvable model).

At the level of the nonrelativistic excitation energies, the dominant
effects are: (i) time-like deformation mainly shifts the rest energy
at order
1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
and leaves the leading spacing
\sim\omega n
unchanged;
(ii) space-like deformation produces a spacing renormalization at
order
1/E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}
without a linear
1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
shift; (iii) light-like
deformation exhibits both features.

3
Conclusion

We investigated the one-dimensional Dirac oscillator in a class of
projective (linear-fractional) DSR models characterized by an invariant
energy scale
E_{\mathrm{p}}
. Starting from the corresponding deformed Casimir
invariants, we derived the coordinate-space Dirac equations in
(1+1)

dimensions for three inequivalent deformation geometries (time-like,
space-like, and light-like) and implemented the Dirac-oscillator coupling.

A central technical issue is that, for the time-like and light-like
realizations, the deformation promotes the mass term to a momentum-dependent
operator, which introduces an operator-ordering ambiguity once one
passes to coordinate space. Adopting a reverted-product ordering prescription,
we obtained local second-order equations and derived closed-form spectra
and eigenfunctions in all three geometries. In each case the ordinary
Dirac oscillator is recovered smoothly in the undeformed limit
E_{\mathrm{p}}\to\infty
.

The nonrelativistic expansion of the positive-energy branch highlights
how the deformation geometry controls the leading corrections. In
the time-like case, the dominant effect is a linear
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}})

shift of the rest energy, while the leading oscillator spacing remains
unchanged at this order. In contrast, the space-like deformation produces
no linear
1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
rest-energy shift but renormalizes the nonrelativistic
level spacing at
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})
. The light-like deformation
combines both mechanisms. These results provide a compact analytic
benchmark for testing how different DSR realizations imprint themselves
on relativistic bound-state spectra and their nonrelativistic limits.
Future work may extend the present analysis to higher dimensions,
include external fields, or explore thermodynamic and information-theoretic
indicators within the same projective DSR framework.

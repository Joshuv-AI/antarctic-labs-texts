Abstract
With the growing volume of data in society, the need for privacy protection in data analysis also rises. In particular, private selection tasks, wherein the most important information is retrieved under differential privacy are emphasized in a wide range of contexts, including machine learning and medical statistical analysis. However, existing mechanisms use global sensitivity , which may add larger amount of perturbation than is necessary. Therefore, this study proposes a novel mechanism for differentially private selection using the concept of smooth sensitivity and presents theoretical proofs of strict privacy guarantees. Simultaneously, given that the current state-of-the-art algorithm using smooth sensitivity is still of limited use, and that the theoretical analysis of the basic properties of the noise distributions are not yet rigorous, we present fundamental theorems to improve upon them. Furthermore, new theorems are proposed for efficient noise generation. Experiments demonstrate that the proposed mechanism can provide higher accuracy than the existing global sensitivity -based methods. Finally, we show key directions for further theoretical development. Overall, this study can be an important foundational work for expanding the potential of smooth sensitivity in privacy-preserving data analysis. The Python implementation of our experiments and supplemental results are available at https://github.com/ay0408/Smooth-Private-Selection .

Therefore, in this study, we propose a novel mechanism for differentially private selection using the concept of

III-A

Differential Privacy

Differential privacy
[
5
]
is a framework that emerged in cryptography to assess the privacy level of an individual’s participation information in a dataset. In recent years, it has been widely applied in the context of data management
[
15
,
27
]
and medical data analysis
[
14
]
. This concept considers two
neighboring
datasets that differ by only one element and aims to create a condition where these datasets are almost indistinguishable. When
d(x,y)
represents the Hamming distance between
x
and
y
, that is,
d(x,y)=|\{i:x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}|
, the definition of
\epsilon
-differential privacy is as follows:

Definition 1
.

(
\epsilon
-Differential Privacy
[
7
]
)

A randomized mechanism
M
is
\epsilon
-differentially private if, for all neighboring datasets
x,y\in D^{n}
satisfying
d(x,y)=1
and for any
\mathcal{S}\subset range(M)
,

\displaystyle\Pr[M(x)\in\mathcal{S}]\leq e^{\epsilon}\cdot\Pr[M(y)\in\mathcal{S}].

D^{n}
represents the set of all possible datasets containing information on
n
elements. The parameter
\epsilon\ (>0)
evaluates the privacy level, with smaller values representing stronger privacy guarantees.

The most basic method for satisfying
\epsilon
-differential privacy is to use the
global sensitivity
of a function. The definition of
global sensitivity
is as follows:

Definition 2
.

(
Global Sensitivity
[
7
]
)

The global sensitivity of a function
f:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\displaystyle GS_{f}=\max_{x,y:d(x,y)=1}\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{1}.

The
global sensitivity
is a constant value that does not depend on datasets. However,
global sensitivity
considers all possible
neighboring
datasets, and the value is often based on extremely rare situations in practical cases. Notably, in the context of statistics publication, the addition of more noise than necessary makes achieving high accuracy difficult
[
25
]
. The concept of
smooth sensitivity

[
18
]
contributes to solving this problem.

III-A
1
Smooth Sensitivity

The concept of
smooth sensitivity
is based on
local sensitivity

[
18
,
21
]
, which represents the maximum change with respect to an input dataset. The definition is as follows:

Definition 3
.

(
Local Sensitivity
[
18
]
)

For a function
f:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a dataset
x\in D^{n}
, the local sensitivity of
f
at
x
is

\displaystyle LS_{f}(x)=\max_{y:d(x,y)=1}\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{1}.

In contrast to
global sensitivity
,
local sensitivity
depends on the relevant dataset and easy to compute, and
\forall x:LS_{f}(x)\leq GS_{f}
holds. Because the noise scale also varies among different datasets, the following concept of
smooth upper bound

[
18
]
is necessary to satisfy differential privacy.

Definition 4
.

(
\beta
-Smooth Upper Bound on LS
[
18
]
)

For
\beta>0
, a function
S:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a
\beta
-smooth upper bound on the local sensitivity of
f
if it satisfies the following requirements:

\displaystyle\forall x\in D^{n}:

\displaystyle S(x)\geq LS_{f}(x);

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in D^{n},d(x,y)=1:

\displaystyle S(x)\leq e^{\beta}\cdot S(y).

A special case of
smooth upper bound
is
smooth sensitivity
, which is defined by

Definition 5
.

(
\beta
-Smooth Sensitivity
[
18
]
)

For
\beta>0
, the
\beta
-smooth sensitivity of
f
at
x
is

\displaystyle S_{f,\beta}^{*}(x)=\max_{y\in D^{n}}\left(LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}\right).

For every
\beta
-
smooth upper bound

S
,
\forall x:S_{f,\beta}^{*}(x)\leq S(x)
holds
[
18
]
. The larger
\beta
is, the closer
S_{f,\beta}^{*}(x)
is to
LS_{f}(x)
, and the computation time can be reduced
[
25
]
. However, there is still no general method for efficient computation when
\beta
is small; therefore, we present new theorems in Section IV.C.

When using
smooth sensitivity
or
smooth upper bound
, noise distributions to satisfy differential privacy include
(\alpha,\beta)
-
admissible
distributions. The definition of
(\alpha,\beta)
-
admissible
under
\epsilon
-differential privacy is as follows:

Definition 6
.

(
(\alpha,\beta)
-Admissible
[
18
]
)

A probability distribution on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, given by a density function
h
, is
(\alpha,\beta)
-admissible if, for
\alpha=\alpha(\epsilon)
,
\beta=\beta(\epsilon)
, the following two conditions hold for all
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\|\Delta\|_{1}\leq\alpha
and
|\lambda|\leq\beta
and for all measurable subsets

:

\displaystyle Sliding\ Property:\hskip-11.38092pt

\displaystyle\Pr_{Z\sim h}[Z\in\mathcal{S}]\leq e^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr_{Z\sim h}[Z\in\mathcal{S}+\Delta];

\displaystyle Dilation\ Property:\hskip-11.38092pt

\displaystyle\Pr_{Z\sim h}[Z\in\mathcal{S}]\leq e^{\frac{\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr_{Z\sim h}[Z\in e^{\lambda}\cdot\mathcal{S}].

Using an
(\alpha,\beta)
-
admissible
distribution, we can construct an
\epsilon
-differentially private algorithm for numeric queries from the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

(
\epsilon
-differentially private algorithm using a smooth upper bound
[
18
]
)

Let
h
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-admissible noise probability density function, and set
\alpha=\alpha(\epsilon)
and
\beta=\beta(\epsilon)
. Let
Z
be a random variable derived from
h
. For a function
f:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
S:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
\beta
-smooth upper bound on the local sensitivity of
f
. Thereafter, the algorithm
A(x)=f(x)+\frac{S(x)}{\alpha}\cdot Z
is
\epsilon
-differentially private.

In this lemma,
S(x)
and
\alpha
can be considered to have a privacy budget of
\epsilon/2
each (with reference to Definition 6). However, it is not necessary to distribute the same budget, and accuracy could be improved by distributing more to one of them. For example, if the effect of reducing

by increasing
\beta
is greater than the effect of increasing
\alpha
, it would be preferable to assign a greater privacy budget to
\beta
, that is, the numerator of
\frac{S(x)}{\alpha}
. Based on this discussion, we extend Lemma 1 and present a new fundamental theorem to construct a more flexible algorithm in Section IV.A.

In Section IV.D, we also provide a detailed analysis of noise distributions. The
(\alpha,\beta)
-
admissible
distributions mentioned in the literature for satisfying
\epsilon
-differential privacy are the distributions with density
h(z)\propto\frac{1}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}

(\gamma>1)

[
18
,
2
]
. Of these, for the standard Cauchy distribution with
\gamma=2
, a rigorous analysis has been conducted of
(\alpha,\beta)

[
25
]
; however, the other distributions still lack sufficient analysis. Therefore, we present a more rigorous
(\alpha,\beta)
than existing studies for the distributions with general
\gamma>1
. This enables the amount of noise to be further reduced.

IV-A

Enhanced Differentially Private Algorithm using Smooth Sensitivity

By extending Lemma 1, we present in Theorem 1 a more general procedure for constructing an
\epsilon
-differentially private algorithm using a
smooth upper bound
.

Theorem 1
.

(
Enhanced
\epsilon
-differentially private algorithm using a smooth upper bound
)

Let
h
be an
(\alpha,\beta)
-admissible noise probability density function, and set
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha(k\epsilon)
and
\beta^{\prime}=\beta((2-k)\epsilon)
. Let
Z
be a random variable derived from
h
. For a function
f:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
S:D^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
\beta^{\prime}
-smooth upper bound on the local sensitivity of
f
. Thereafter, the algorithm
A(x)=f(x)+\frac{S(x)}{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot Z
is
\epsilon
-differentially private for any
k\in(0,2)
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that, for all neighboring datasets
x,y\in D^{n}
and for any
\mathcal{S}\subset range(A)
,

\displaystyle\Pr[A(x)\in\mathcal{S}]\leq e^{\epsilon}\cdot\Pr[A(y)\in\mathcal{S}].

Denote
\frac{S(x)}{\alpha^{\prime}}
by
N(x)
. Then,

\displaystyle\Pr[A(x)\in\mathcal{S}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr_{Z\sim h}[f(x)+N(x)\cdot Z\in\mathcal{S}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pr_{Z\sim h}\left[Z\in\frac{\mathcal{S}-f(x)}{N(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{k\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr_{Z\sim h}\left[Z\in\frac{\mathcal{S}-f(y)}{N(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{k\epsilon}{2}}\cdot e^{\frac{(2-k)\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr_{Z\sim h}\left[Z\in\frac{\mathcal{S}-f(y)}{N(y)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\epsilon}\cdot\Pr[A(y)\in\mathcal{S}]

because

\displaystyle\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{1}}{N(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{1}}{S(x)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}\cdot\frac{\|f(x)-f(y)\|_{1}}{LS_{f}(x)}\leq\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{(Sliding\ Property)}

\displaystyle\mathrm{and}\ \ \ \ \left|\ln\frac{N(x)}{N(y)}\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\ln\frac{S(x)}{S(y)}\right|\leq\beta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mathrm{(Dilation\ Property)}.

∎

The algorithm in Theorem 1 is superior to the existing one in Lemma 1 because the values of
\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}
can be varied flexibly. In other words, when constructing an
\epsilon
-differentially private method using
smooth sensitivity
, the same privacy budgets need not be assigned to
\alpha
and
\beta
, as mentioned in Section III.A. With this in mind, we propose a novel differentially private method using
smooth sensitivity
for private selection tasks.

(

\displaystyle M_{SPS}(x)=\argmax_{r\in\mathcal{R}}\left\{u(x,r)+\frac{\max_{r\in\mathcal{R}}S(x,r)}{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot Z_{r}\right\},

where

\displaystyle g(z)=\begin{cases}0\ \ \ \ \ \ (z<0)\\
\frac{h(z)}{\int_{w\geq 0}h(w)\mathrm{d}w}\ \ (z\geq 0)\end{cases}.

Two-Sided Noise

We first consider the case of two-sided noise distribution. The privacy guarantee of the smooth private selection for this case is provided by the following theorem:

Theorem 2

If

(1)

\displaystyle\Pr[M(x)=1]\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\int_{v\in(-\infty,\infty)}\Pr\left[u(x,1)+\frac{S(x)}{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot Z_{1}=v\right]

(2)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\Pr\left[u(x,2)+\frac{S(x)}{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot Z_{2}\leq v\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdots\Pr\left[u(x,m)+\frac{S(x)}{\alpha^{\prime}}\cdot Z_{m}\leq v\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\int_{v\in(-\infty,\infty)}\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,1))}{S(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\Pr\left[Z_{2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,2))}{S(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdots\Pr\left[Z_{m}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,m))}{S(x)}\right].

Similarly,

\displaystyle\Pr[M(y)=1]\hskip-5.69046pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.69046pt\int_{v\in(-\infty,\infty)}\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,1))}{S(y)}\right]

(3)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\Pr\left[Z_{2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,2))}{S(y)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdots\Pr\left[Z_{m}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,m))}{S(y)}\right].

Because
\forall r\in\mathcal{R}:|u(x,r)-u(y,r)|\leq LS_{u,\mathcal{R}}(x,r)\leq S(x,r)\leq S(x)
,

\displaystyle(3)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{v\in(-\infty,\infty)}\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,1))}{S(y)}\right]

(4)

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\Pr\left[Z_{2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,2)-S(x))}{S(y)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \cdots\Pr\left[Z_{m}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,m)-S(x))}{S(y)}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{v\in(-\infty,\infty)}\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,1)+S(x))}{S(y)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdot\Pr\left[Z_{2}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,2))}{S(y)}\right]

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \cdots\Pr\left[Z_{m}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,m))}{S(y)}\right].

Here,

\displaystyle-S(x)\leq u(x,1)-u(y,1)\leq S(x)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle 0\leq u(x,1)-u(y,1)+S(x)\leq 2S(x),

and

\displaystyle\frac{S(x)}{S(y)}=\frac{\max_{r}S(x,r)}{\max_{r}S(y,r)}\leq e^{\beta^{\prime}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,1))}{S(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{k\epsilon}\cdot\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,1)+S(x))}{S(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{k\epsilon}\cdot e^{\frac{l\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr\left[Z_{1}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(y,1)+S(x))}{S(y)}\right]

and
\forall r\in\{2,3,\dots,m\}:

\displaystyle\Pr\left[Z_{r}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,r))}{S(x)}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\frac{l\epsilon}{2}}\cdot\Pr\left[Z_{r}\leq\frac{\alpha^{\prime}(v-u(x,r))}{S(y)}\right].

Consequently, from (2) and (4), the relation (1) holds.
∎

From Theorem 2, our mechanism satisfies
\epsilon
-differential privacy when
k+\frac{|\mathcal{R}|}{2}\cdot l=1
.

IV-C

New Theorems for Obtaining Smooth Upper Bound

We first present a new theorem for obtaining
\beta
-
smooth sensitivity
for small
\beta
below a certain value determined by
global sensitivity
and
local sensitivity
, based on the original definitions (Definitions 2, 3, and 5).

Theorem 4
.

For any
\beta\ (>0)
satisfying

\displaystyle\beta\leq\min_{x:LS_{f}(x)\neq GS_{f}}\frac{1}{gd(x)}\cdot\ln\left(\frac{GS_{f}}{LS_{f}(x)}\right),

(5)

where
gd(x):=\min_{y:LS_{f}(y)=GS_{f}}d(y,x)
represents the shortest distance from
x
to elements the local sensitivity of which equals global sensitivity, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\forall x:S_{f,\beta}^{*}(x)=GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}.

Proof.

From (5), for all
x:LS_{f}(x)\neq GS_{f}
,

\displaystyle\beta\leq\frac{1}{gd(x)}\cdot\ln\left(\frac{GS_{f}}{LS_{f}(x)}\right)

(6)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle LS_{f}(x)\leq GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}.

Let
Y
be the set of
y
satisfying
LS_{f}(y)=GS_{f}
and
d(y,x)=gd(x)
. Because

\displaystyle S_{f,\beta}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{y}\left(LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\Bigl\{GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)},

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \max_{y\notin Y}\left(LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}\right)\Bigr\},

it is sufficient to show that

\displaystyle\forall x:\,GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}\geq\max_{y}\left(LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}\right).

(7)

(I) When
LS(x)=GS_{f}
:

For any
y
, because
d(y,x)\geq gd(x)=0
,

\displaystyle GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}=GS_{f}\geq LS_{f}(y)\geq LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}.

(II) When
LS_{f}(x)\neq GS_{f}
:

(a) For any
y:LS_{f}(y)=GS_{f}
, because
d(y,x)\geq gd(x)
,

\displaystyle GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}\geq GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}=LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}.

(b) For any
y:LS_{f}(y)\neq GS_{f}
, where
z
satisfies
LS_{f}(z)=GS_{f}
and
d(z,y)=gd(y)
,

\displaystyle GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot gd(x)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(z,x)}\ \ \ \ [\because gd(x)\leq d(z,x)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{\beta\cdot d(z,y)}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(z,x)}\ \ \ \ [\because(6)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{\beta\cdot d(z,y)}\cdot e^{-\beta(d(z,y)+d(y,x))}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ [\because d(z,x)\leq d(z,y)+d(y,x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle LS_{f}(y)\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot d(y,x)}.

From (I) and (II), the relation (7) holds.
∎

The number of
x
satisfying
LS_{f}(x)=GS_{f}
is often
\mathcal{O}(1)
, and Theorem 4 eliminates the need to calculate
smooth sensitivity
in
\mathcal{O}(|D^{n}|)
time based on the original Definition 5. We can also easily employ this theorem for private selection based on Definitions 7, 9, and 11.

One problem is that
\beta
satisfying the inequality (1) may be too small; therefore, we present another theorem for obtaining a
smooth upper bound
for more practical
\beta
. In Theorem 5, for all
x
with a
local sensitivity
close to
GS_{f}
, that is, for data that, in practical cases, are expected to be extremely rare, we set
S(x)=GS_{f}
. This expands the range of
\beta
satisfying the inequality, and we can efficiently obtain smaller
S(x)
for realistic data. The proof is similar to that of Theorem 4 and is provided on our GitHub page.

Theorem 5
.

Given a threshold
T
, we let the set of
x
satisfying
LS_{f}(x)>T
be
U
. For any
\beta\ (>0)
satisfying

\displaystyle\beta\leq\min_{x\notin U}\frac{1}{ud(x)}\cdot\ln\left(\frac{GS_{f}}{LS_{f}(x)}\right),

(8)

where
ud(x):=\min_{y\in U}d(y,x)
represents the shortest distance between
x
and
U
, the following function
S
is a
\beta
-smooth upper bound:

\displaystyle S(x)=GS_{f}\cdot e^{-\beta\cdot ud(x)}\ .

By setting
T
so that
|U|<<|D^{n}|
, the
smooth upper bound
can be efficiently obtained for practical
\beta
using Theorem 5. As with Theorem 4, Theorem 5 can also be employed for private selection based on Definitions 7, 9, and 10.

Developing more generalized theorems for obtaining
smooth sensitivity
and
smooth upper bound
is a challenging issue for the future work.

IV-D

Detailed Analysis of
(\alpha,\beta)
-Admissible Property

In using our mechanism and other methods based on the concept of
smooth sensitivity
, it is critical to provide a rigorous
(\alpha,\beta)
-
admissible
property of the noise distribution for setting an appropriate
\beta
for our Theorems 4 and 5 and for reducing the added noise. The discussion in the existing study
[
18
]
are still insufficiently elaborate; therefore, we provide a new theorem for the distribution with density
h(z)\propto\frac{1}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}(\gamma>1)
, which can be used to satisfy
\epsilon
-differential privacy.

Theorem 6
.

For any
\gamma>1
, the distribution with density
h(z)\propto\frac{1}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}
is

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \left(\frac{\epsilon}{2\cdot(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}},\,\frac{\epsilon}{2(\gamma-1)}\right)\mathchar 45{{\it admissible}}.

Proof.

We set

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\epsilon}{2\cdot(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}},\ \ \mathrm{and}\ \ \beta=\frac{\epsilon}{2(\gamma-1)}.

First, we analyze the sliding property. From Definition 6, it is sufficient to show that
\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(z+\Delta)}\right)
is at most
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
when
|\Delta|\leq\alpha
. Where
\phi(x)=\ln(1+x^{\gamma})
, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(z+\Delta)}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{1+|z+\Delta|^{\gamma}}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(|z+\Delta|)-\phi(|z|).

Here, there exists
\zeta>0
such that
\phi(|z+\Delta|)-\phi(|z|)\leq|\Delta|\cdot|\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)|
. Because

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(\zeta)=\frac{\gamma\cdot\zeta^{\gamma-1}}{1+\zeta^{\gamma}}=\frac{\gamma}{\zeta+\zeta^{1-\gamma}}\leq(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\ \ [\because\zeta>0],

\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(z+\Delta)}\right)\leq|\Delta|\cdot(\gamma-1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}
. Thus, when
|\Delta|\leq\alpha
,
\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{h(z+\Delta)}\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.

Thereafter, we analyze the dilation property. Similar to the above, it is sufficient to show that
\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{e^{\lambda}\cdot h(e^{\lambda}z)}\right)
is at most
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
when
|\lambda|\leq\beta
. Because

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{e^{\lambda}\cdot h(e^{\lambda}z)}\right)=\ln\left(\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{1+e^{\lambda\gamma}|z|^{\gamma}}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}\right),

when
\lambda\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{e^{\lambda}\cdot h(e^{\lambda}z)}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{e^{\lambda\gamma}+e^{\lambda\gamma}|z|^{\gamma}}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}\right)=\lambda(\gamma-1),

and when
\lambda<0
,

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{e^{\lambda}\cdot h(e^{\lambda}z)}\right)\leq\ln\left(\frac{1}{e^{\lambda}}\right)=-\lambda=|\lambda|.

Thus, when
|\lambda|\leq\beta
,
\ln\left(\frac{h(z)}{e^{\lambda}\cdot h(e^{\lambda}z)}\right)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.
∎

The
(\alpha,\beta)
in this theorem is larger than previously known
\left((\alpha,\beta)=\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2(\gamma+1)},\frac{\epsilon}{2(\gamma+1)}\right)\right)

[
18
]
; therefore, we can reduce the added noise in
smooth sensitivity
-based methods including our proposed mechanism.

VI
Conclusion

In this study, we proposed the first mechanism for differentially private selection using
smooth sensitivity
, the smooth private selection, along with theoretical proofs of the privacy guarantees. We also presented new fundamental algorithm and theorems on
smooth sensitivity
and expanded its potential. The experimental evaluation showed that our proposed mechanism can provide higher accuracy than the existing
global sensitivity
-based methods in privacy-preserving data analysis. Important future challenges include the following:

i)

Developing an optimal method for determining the values of
k
and
l
that provide the highest accuracy for each analysis data;

ii)

Conducting general theoretical analysis of our mechanism given the characteristics of the data, associated with the first challenge;

iii)

Exploring possible noise distributions with a density function other than
h(z)\propto\frac{1}{1+|z|^{\gamma}}
;

iv)

Integrating our mechanism with the joint approach
[
10
]
and the local dampening mechanism
[
8
]
, while developing efficient algorithms and more generalized theorems for computing
smooth sensitivity
.

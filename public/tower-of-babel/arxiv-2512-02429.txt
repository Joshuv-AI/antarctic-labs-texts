Minimal Model Program on the generic fiber of log Calabi–Yau type fibration
Donghyeon Kim, Dae-Won Lee

Abstract
. We study the minimal model program on the geometric generic fiber of a fibration f : X → S f:X\to S such that for a Zariski dense subset S ′ ⊆ S S^{\prime}\subseteq S , X s X_{s} is an ε \varepsilon -lc log Calabi–Yau type for every s ∈ S ′ s\in S^{\prime} . We prove that for a fibration f : X → S f:X\to S of varieties, if the fibers are of ε \varepsilon -lc log Calabi–Yau type, then the geometric generic fiber X η ¯ X_{\overline{\eta}} is pklt. In particular, for any big divisor D D on X η ¯ X_{\overline{\eta}} , we can run the anticanonical MMP and D D -MMP with scaling of an ample divisor on X η ¯ X_{\overline{\eta}} .

1.
Introduction

The minimal model program (MMP) provides a framework for studying the birational geometry of higher dimensional varieties. Controlling the singularities of fibers in a family is an important problem in birational geometry, so that one can run the MMP uniformly and understand the variation of Fano and Calabi–Yau type varieties. In this paper, we focus on fibrations whose closed fibers are of (log) Calabi–Yau type over a Zariski dense subset of the base, and we study the extent to which the geometric generic fiber inherits strong MMP properties from the closed fibers.

Let us briefly recall the definition of log Calabi–Yau type. Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective pair and
\varepsilon>0
. We say that
(X,\Delta)
is of klt (resp.
\varepsilon
-lc)
log Calabi–Yau type
if there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta^{\prime}\geq 0
on
X
such that

(X,\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})\text{ is klt }(\text{resp. }\varepsilon\text{-lc) and }K_{X}+\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0.

The Calabi–Yau type pairs naturally arise in birational geometry and behave nicely from the viewpoint of the MMP. Indeed, if
(X,\Delta)
is a klt log Calabi–Yau type pair, then by definition, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\Delta^{\prime}
on
X
such that
K_{X}+\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. For any effective divisor
D
on
X
, the pair
(X,\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}+\varepsilon D)
is also klt for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, one can run a
D
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor. Moreover, if
D
is big, then this MMP terminates.

Motivated by this observation, we formulate the following question regarding the behavior of log Calabi–Yau type singularities in families.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
f\colon X\to S
be a fibration of varieties, and
(X,\Delta)
a pair. Suppose that there exists a Zariski dense subset
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
such that for each
s\in S^{\prime}
, the fiber
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is of
\varepsilon
-lc log Calabi–Yau type. Is the geometric generic fiber
(X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})
of klt log Calabi–Yau type?

A complete answer to Question
1.1
seems to be subtle. Our first main result gives a weaker conclusion: we show that the geometric generic fiber is potentially klt (pklt for short). For the definition of pklt pairs, see Definition
2.2
. We note that if
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
is a Zariski dense
open
subset and the base field
k
is algebraically closed and of infinite transcendence degree over the prime field of any characteristic, then by
[
14
, Lemma 2.1]
, Question
1.1
has an affirmative answer. However, our question concerns a more general setting.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
f:X\to S
be a fibration of normal varieties,
(X,\Delta)
a pair, and let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be a Zariski dense subset such that
(X_{s},\Delta_{s})
is of
\varepsilon
-lc log Calabi–Yau type for each
s\in S^{\prime}
. Then the geometric generic fiber
(X_{\overline{\eta}},\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})
is pklt.

The pklt condition is strong enough to run the anticanonical MMP by
[
2
]
and, more generally, a
D
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor on the geometric generic fiber. Since every klt log Calabi–Yau type pair is pklt, the conclusion of our theorem still suffices to highlight many features of the MMP on the geometric generic fiber. As a consequence of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
2.4
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.2
, the following holds:

(a)

there exists a
-(K_{X_{\overline{\eta}}}+\Delta_{\overline{\eta}})
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor, and

(b)

for any big
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X_{\overline{\eta}}
, there exists a
D
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor that terminates.

We note that determining whether a pair
(X,\Delta)
is pklt involves computing asymptotic invariants for every prime divisor
E
over
X
, which is often intractable in practice. Theorem
1.2
provides an effective way to bypass this difficulty. It demonstrates that the log Calabi–Yau structure of fibers over a Zariski dense subset is a sufficient condition for the geometric generic fiber to be pklt. This allows us to deduce the birational properties of the generic fiber, including the existence of the MMP, from the geometry of the fibration.

We recall a conjecture due to Jiao, which predicts that for a klt Calabi–Yau type variety
X
, there exists a positive integer
m
such that
m(K_{X}+\Delta)\sim 0
for some effective divisor
\Delta
on
X
under a boundedness assumption.

Conjecture 1.4

(cf.
[
9
, Section 3]
)
.

There exists a positive integer
m
with the following property. Let
\varepsilon,d,v>0
,
X
be a
d
-dimensional
\varepsilon
-lc Calabi–Yau type variety, and
A
an ample divisor on
X
such that
A^{d}\leq v
. Then there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta)
is klt and
m(K_{X}+\Delta)\sim 0
.

Assuming Conjecture
1.4
, we can show that Question
1.1
has an affirmative answer for
\Delta=0
case.

Theorem 1.5
.

If we assume Conjecture
1.4
, then Question
1.1
is true for
\Delta=0
.

Theorem
1.2
provides evidence for a positive answer to Question
1.1
in full generality.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
0
, unless otherwise stated. By a

, we mean an integral, separated scheme of finite type over

We now fix some notation and conventions that will be used throughout the paper.

(1)

A

\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta
on

K_{X}+\Delta
is
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, we call

a
pair
.

(2)

For a scheme

by the residue field of

and regard

s:\operatorname{Spec}k(s)\to S
.
The associated geometric point is
\bar{s}:\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k(s)}\to S
.
For an integral scheme

its function field.

(3)

If

\bar{\eta}:\operatorname{Spec}\overline{k(\eta)}\to S
and call

X_{\bar{\eta}}\coloneqq X\times_{S}\bar{\eta}
the
geometric generic fiber
of
X\to S
.

(4)

For a morphism
f:X\to S
of schemes and a (geometric) point

, set

If
\Delta
is a divisor on

, we write

for its pullback to

whenever it is defined.

(5)

For a morphism

.

(6)

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pair,

a prime divisor on

.
The

of

This definition does not depend on the choice of

. We say that
(X,\Delta)
is

if

for every prime divisor

over

.

(7)

Let
X
be a normal variety and

a Cartier divisor on
X
.
The base ideal of the complete linear system

is the image of the natural map

H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D))\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X},

and is denoted by
\mathfrak{b}(|D|)
.

(8)

For a variety

, by

, we mean replacing

by a variety
S^{\prime}
equipped with a quasi-finite morphism
S^{\prime}\to S

(e.g., an open immersion or a generically finite cover), and replacing all data by their pullbacks to
S^{\prime}
while keeping the same notation.

2.1.
Asymptotic order

In this subsection, we recall the notion of
asymptotic order
for divisors.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety and
D
a big Cartier divisor. For each integer
m\geq 1
, denote by
\mathfrak{b}_{m}\coloneqq\mathfrak{b}(|mD|)
the base ideal of
|mD|
. Then the collection
\mathfrak{b}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\left\{\mathfrak{b}_{m}\right\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
forms a graded sequence of ideals in
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, where we set
\mathfrak{b}_{0}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{X}
. For a prime divisor
E
over
X
, we define the
asymptotic order
of
D
with respect to
E
by

\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\|D\|)\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\\
\mathfrak{b}_{m}\neq(0)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\mathfrak{b}_{m})}{m}.

If
D
is a big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, then we define

\ord_{E}(\|D\|)\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\ord_{E}(\|mD\|),

where
n
is a positive integer such that
mD
is Cartier. More generally, if
D
is a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
, then for an ample Cartier divisor
A
on
X
, we define

\sigma_{E}(D)\coloneqq\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\varepsilon\to 0^{+}\\
\varepsilon\text{ is rational }\end{subarray}}\mathrm{ord}_{E}(\|D+\varepsilon A\|).

It is known that
\sigma_{E}(D)<\infty
and the definition does not depend on the choice of
A
(cf.
[
13
, Chapter 3.1]
). Moreover, if
D
is big, then
\sigma_{E}(D)=\ord_{E}(\|D\|)
.

We also note that for any pseudoeffective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors
D
and
D^{\prime}
on
X
, and any prime divisor
E
over
X
, we have the following inequality

\sigma_{E}(D+D^{\prime})\leq\sigma_{E}(D)+\sigma_{E}(D^{\prime}).

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective klt pair, and
D
a pseudoeffective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. We define the
log canonical threshold
as

\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,D)\coloneqq\inf_{E}\frac{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}{\sigma_{E}(D)},

where the
\inf
is taken over all prime divisors
E
over
X
.

Let us recall the following lemma, which is essentially
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
and appears in the following form in
[
11
, Lemma 2.11]
.

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
7
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
11
, Lemma 2.11]
)
.

Let
X
be a normal projective variety,
D
a big
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, and
E
a prime divisor on
X
. Suppose that
a
is a positive rational number. Then
\sigma_{E}(D)<a
if and only if

\mathrm{vol}(D-aE)<\mathrm{vol}(D).

2.2.
Potentially klt pair

In this subsection, we introduce the notion of
potentially klt
pairs and some of their properties; see, for example,
[
5
]
,
[
3
]
, and
[
2
]
for further details.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a projective pair. We say that
(X,\Delta)
is
potentially klt (pklt)
if
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
is pseudoeffective, and there is a positive number
\varepsilon>0
such that

A_{X,\Delta}(E)-\sigma_{E}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))>\varepsilon

for every prime divisor
E
over
X
.

The following is a valuative characterization of pklt pairs, which was recently proved in
[
2
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(cf.
[
2
, Proposition 4.2]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pklt pair. Then

\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,-(K_{X}+\Delta))>1.

The pklt condition is strong enough to guarantee the existence of MMPs.

Theorem 2.4

(cf.
[
2
, Corollary 1.3]
)
.

Let
(X,\Delta)
be a pklt pair. Then

(a)

there exists a
-(K_{X}+\Delta)
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor, and

(b)

for every big
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier divisor
D
on
X
, there exists a
D
-MMP with scaling of an ample divisor that terminates.

Proof.

Part (a) is precisely
[
2
, Corollary 1.3]
.

For (b), we claim that there is a positive number
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,-(K_{X}+\Delta)+\varepsilon D)>1.

Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,-(K_{X}+\Delta)+\varepsilon D)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{E}\frac{\sigma_{E}(-(K_{X}+\Delta)+\varepsilon D)}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{E}\frac{\sigma_{E}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))+\varepsilon\sigma_{E}(D)}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{E}\frac{\sigma_{E}(-(K_{X}+\Delta))}{A_{X,\Delta}(E)}+\varepsilon\sup_{E}\frac{\sigma_{E}(D)}{A_{X.\Delta}(E)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,-(K_{X}+\Delta))}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,D)}.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have
\mathrm{lct}_{\sigma}(X,\Delta,-(K_{X}+\Delta))>1
. By choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we obtain the claim.

Hence, by
[
15
, Lemma 1.60]
, there exists an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Weil divisor
\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}-(K_{X}+\Delta)+\varepsilon D
on
X
such that
(X,\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})
is klt. Consequently,
K_{X}+\Delta+\Delta^{\prime}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}\varepsilon D
and by
[
1
, Corollary 1.4.2]
, we may run a
(K_{X}+\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})
-MMP, i.e., a
D
-MMP with scaling that terminates.
∎

Theorem 2.5

Let

Remark 2.6
.

We expect that for a projective klt pair
(X,\Delta)
, the condition that
(X,\Delta)
is pklt is equivalent to the existence of the anticanonical minimal model of
(X,\Delta)

Theorem

For more details on this issue, we refer the reader to

Definition 2.11

Let

Remark 3.1
.

We emphasize that the
\varepsilon
-lc condition on the fibers is crucial. If we replace this condition with klt or lc, then we do not know whether the volumes of Cartier divisors on a log Calabi–Yau type variety are contained in a discrete set or not.

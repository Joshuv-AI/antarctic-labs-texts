Elliptic systems in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces with critical sources in bounded domains


Abstract
. In this paper, we show the existence of non-trivial solutions to very general elliptic systems with critical non-linearities in the sense of embeddings in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. This allows to consider non-linearities which do not have polynomial growth. To achieve the existence, we combine a Mountain Pass Theorem without the Palais-Smale condition with the second Concentration Compactness Principle of Lions in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we consider elliptic systems in the framework of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces with homogeneous boundary conditions:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{1}}u=F_{s}(u,v)+\lambda H_{s}(x,u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
-\Delta_{g_{2}}v=F_{t}(u,v)+\lambda H_{t}(x,u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
(u,v)\neq(0,0)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=v=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Here,
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with smooth boundary. We next describe briefly the data in the system (
1.1
). We start with the main operators. The
g_{i}
-Laplacian operators are given by

\Delta_{g_{i}}u:=\text{div}\left(g_{i}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\right),\quad i=1,2,

where
G^{\prime}_{i}(t)=g_{i}(t)t
, being
G_{i}

N
-functions (see Section
2
for details). Regarding the nonlinearity
F=F(s,t)
, we will assume that it has critical growth in the sense of the embeddings between the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
W_{0}^{1,G_{i}}(\Omega)
into the Orlicz-Lebesgue spaces
L^{G_{i}^{*}}(\Omega)
, where
G_{i}^{*}
is the optimal critical
N
-function in the above embedding. Regarding the nonlinearity
H=H(s,t)
, it is assumed to be subcritical and its role is that it helps to find nontrivial solutions to (
1.1
).

The prototypes for (
1.1
) are the following elliptic systems involving
p
-Laplace operators and critical non-linearities:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p_{1}}u=\mu_{1}|u|^{p_{1}^{*}-2}u+c_{1}|v|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}+d_{1}v|u|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}-2}u+\lambda H_{u}(u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
-\Delta_{p_{2}}v=\mu_{2}|v|^{p_{2}^{*}-2}v+c_{2}|u|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}-2}u+d_{2}u|v|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}-2}v+\lambda H_{v}(u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=v=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

and

(1.3)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\mu_{1}|u|^{q-2}u+c_{\alpha,\beta}|u|^{\alpha-2}u|v|^{\beta}+\lambda H_{u}(u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
-\Delta_{p}v=\mu_{2}|v|^{q-2}v+d_{\alpha,\beta}|v|^{\beta-2}v|u|^{\alpha}+\lambda H_{v}(u,v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=v=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
p,p_{i}>1
,
p<q<p^{*}
,

p_{i}^{*}=\dfrac{Np_{i}}{N-p_{i}},

for
i=1,2
, and

\alpha,\beta>1,\quad\alpha+\beta=p^{*}
. The source term
H=H(u,v)
is assumed to have subcritical growth.

Systems like (
1.2
) but with subcritical growth have been discussed in
[
3
]
and references therein, leaving the critical case as in (
1.2
) as an open problem (see also
[
8
]
). Regarding critical growth, systems like (
1.3
) were considered in
[
1
]
with
p=2
and
q=2
, in
[
7
]
with possibly different
p
-Laplacians and in
[
17
]
(see also
[
11
]
) with
q=p^{*}
. Resonant quasilinear elliptic systems have been treated recently in
[
4
]
and with non-linearities depending also on the gradient in
[
6
]
.

Regarding elliptic systems with more general nonlinearities, we quote the reference
[
15
]
, where an Orlicz-space approach is considered to treat superlinear sources in elliptic systems with the standard Laplacian
\Delta
. However, their proofs seem to depend strongly in the linearity of the main operator. See also
[
12
]
where a different approach is considered, showing a link with a higher order laplacian in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

The main result in this paper is the following. We refer the reader to Sections
2
and
3
for the details in the assumptions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with the cone property. Consider the elliptic system (
1.1
), where the
N
-functions
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
satisfy (
2.5
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
3.1
). Regarding the data
F
and
H
, we assume that they verify the conditions
(F1)-(F3)
and
(H1)-(H3)
, respectively. Then, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, the system (
1.1
) has a weak solution
(u,v)\neq(0,0)
in
\Omega
.

In particular, the above theorem applies to systems of the form

(1.4)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{g_{1}}u=F_{s}(v)+\lambda H_{s}(v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
-\Delta_{g_{2}}v=F_{t}(u)+\lambda H_{t}(u)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=v=0\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Observe that in this case, Theorem
1.1
gives that
u\neq 0
and
v\neq 0
in
\Omega
.

In Theorem
1.1
it is assumed that the subcritical term
H
is non-negative. This condition may be relaxed if we assume a critical growth condition from below in
F
. Thus, we have the next theorem:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain with the cone property. Consider the elliptic system (
1.1
), where the
N
-functions
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
satisfy (
2.5
), (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
3.1
). Regarding the data
F
and
H
, we assume that they verify the conditions
(F1)-(F2)
and
(F3)^{\prime}
and
(H1)-(H3)
(but
H
not necessarily non-negative), respectively. Then, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, the system (
1.1
) has a weak solution
(u,v)\neq(0,0)
in
\Omega
.

We point out that the above results fits into the findings of
[
16
]
for single equations.

We conclude with the following remark. In the case of Lane-Emden systems of the form

(1.5)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\phi(v)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
-\Delta v=\psi(u)\quad\text{in }\Omega\\
u=v=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\end{cases}

we may obtain regularity of the solutions by analysing the regularity of solutions to higher-order operators in Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Indeed, let us suppose that
\psi
is odd and one to one. Let
h=\psi^{-1}
, which is also odd. Then,
-\Delta v=\psi(u)
implies

u=-h(\Delta v).

Plugging into the first equation of the system (
1.5
), we deduce

\phi(v)=-\Delta u=\Delta(h(\Delta v))=\Delta(g(\Delta v)\Delta v),

where

g(t)=\dfrac{h(t)}{t}.

The operator

\Delta_{g}^{2}u=\Delta\left(g(\Delta u)\Delta u\right),

is a generalization of the biharmonic Laplacian
\Delta^{2}
and it was considered in
[
20
]
and in
[
13
]
, where regularity results of Calderón-Zygmund type were deduced.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the basic results and definitions concerning
N
-functions and related spaces. In Section
3
, we provide the main assumptions on the data and we discuss some of their consequences and examples. Also, in Section
4
, we state the main auxiliary results concerning the mountain pass theorem without the Palais-Smale condition and the concentration compactness principle in the framework of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Finally, in Section
5
, we explain the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which is given in several steps, and in the last Section
6
we discuss the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
N-functions and basic properties

In this section, we introduce basic definitions and preliminary results related to Orlicz spaces. We start recalling the definition of an N-function.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
G\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called an N-function if it admits the representation

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\tau d\tau,

where the function
h(t)=g(t)t
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing and satisfies the conditions

h(0)=0,\quad h(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}h(t)=\infty.

We extend the function
G
evenly on the whole of
\mathbb{R}
.

By
[
18
, Chapter 1]
, an N-function has also the following properties:

(1)

G
is continuous, convex, increasing for
t\geq 0
, even and
G(0)=0
.

(2)

G
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=0

and

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=\infty.

Indeed, the above conditions serve as an equivalent definition of N-functions.

An important property for N-functions is the following:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the N-function
G
satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

G(2x)\leq CG(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Examples of functions satisfying the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition are:

•

G(t)=t^{q}
,
t\geq 0
,
q>1
;

•

G(t)=t^{q}(|\log(t)|+1)
,
t\geq 0
,
q>1
;

•

G(t)=(1+|t|)\log(1+|t|)-|t|
;

•

G(t)=t^{q_{1}}\chi_{(0,1]}(t)+t^{q_{2}}\chi_{(1,\infty)}(t)
,
t\geq 0
,
q_{1},q_{2}>1
.

According to
[
18
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for an N-function to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{+}>1
such that

(2.1)

\frac{t^{2}g(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

In particular, (
2.1
) implies that an N-function satisfying the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition does not increase more rapidly than a power function (see (
2.6
) below).

Associated to
G
is the N-function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(2.2)

\widetilde{G}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-G(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

We recall that the role played by
\widetilde{G}
is the same as the conjugate exponent functions when
G(t)=t^{p}
,
p>1
.

The definition of the complementary function assures the following Young-type inequality

(2.3)

at\leq G(t)+\widetilde{G}(a)\text{ for every }a,t\geq 0.

We also quote the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be an N-function. If
G
satisfies (
2.1
) then

\tilde{G}(g(t)t)\leq(p^{+}-1)G(t),

where
g(t)t=G^{\prime}(t)
and
\tilde{G}
is the complementary function of
G.

By
[
18
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the N-function
\widetilde{G}
complementary to
G
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{-}>1
such that

(2.4)

p^{-}\leq\frac{t^{2}g(t)}{G(t)},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

From now on, we will assume that the N-function
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\tau d\tau
satisfies the following growth behaviour:

(2.5)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{t^{2}g(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty,~~~~~\forall t>0.

Hence,
G
and
\widetilde{G}
both satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition.

From (
2.5
) there follows the next comparison of N-functions with power functions:

(2.6)

\min\left\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\right\}G(b)\leq G(ab)\leq\max\left\{a^{p^{-}},a^{p^{+}}\right\}G(b),

for all
a,b
, where we use the convention
t^{\alpha}=|t|^{\alpha-1}t
.

Given two
N
-functions
A
and
B
, we say that
B
is essentially larger than
A
, denoted by

A\ll B,
if for any
c>0
,

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{A(ct)}{B(t)}=0.

Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

Given an N-function
G
, with
G^{\prime}(t)=g(t)t
, we define the Orlicz-Lebesgue class
L^{G}(\Omega)
as follows

L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R},\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx<\infty\right\}.

If
G
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition, then
L^{G}
becomes a vector spaces, and indeed it is a Banach space with respect to the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{G}:=\inf\left\{\lambda>0:\int_{\Omega}G\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\,dx\leq 1\right\}.

We will denote the convex modular associated to the norm by

\rho_{G}(u):=\int_{\Omega}G(u)\,dx.

We will also consider the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces

W^{1,G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega),\,|\nabla u|\in L^{G}(\Omega)\right\}.

The space
W^{1,G}(\Omega)
equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{1,G}:=\|u\|_{G}+\|\nabla u\|_{G}

is a Banach space. We denote by
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm
\|u\|_{1,G}
. By Theorem
2.5
below, an equivalent norm in
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
is

\|u\|:=\|\nabla u\|_{G}.

We will use this norm throughout the paper.

In the case where also
\widetilde{G}
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
condition, these spaces are reflexive and separable.

It will be useful to have the following relation between modulars and norms.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be an N-function satisfying (
2.5
), and let

\xi^{\pm}\colon[0,\infty)

\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as

\xi^{-}(t):=\min\big\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\big\},\quad\text{ and }\quad\xi^{+}(t):=\max\big\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\big\}.

Then

\xi^{-}(\|u\|_{G})\leq\rho_{G}(|u|)\leq\xi^{+}(\|u\|_{G}).

In order to have the Sobolev immersions, we need to require some assumptions on
G
that are the analogous of
p<N
in the classical cases. We assume that
G
satisfies:

(2.7)

\int_{0}^{\delta}\dfrac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{(N+1)/N}}\,ds<\infty

for some
\delta>0
, and

(2.8)

\int_{k}^{\infty}\dfrac{G^{-1}(s)}{s^{(N+1)/N}}\,ds=\infty

for some
k>0
.

For a given
N
-function
G
, define the Sobolev conjugate function
G^{*}
of
G
by means of

G^{*}(t)=G\circ H^{-1}(t),

where

H(t)=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\dfrac{\tau}{G(\tau)}\right)^{\frac{1}{N-1}}\,d\tau\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}}.

Then
G^{*}
is an
N
-function and the following embedding theorem holds (see
[
9
]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be any open set with finite measure. Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) and consider
G^{*}
. Then, the embedding
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{G^{*}}(\Omega)
is continuous. Moreover, there is a constant
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{G^{*}}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{G}

and
L^{G^{*}}(\Omega)
is the smallest Orlicz space where the above inequality is true.

Finally, if
\Omega
satisfies the cone property and if
B
is any
N
-function increasing essentially more slowly than
G^{*}
, then the embedding
W^{1,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\Omega)
is compact.

Remark 2.6
.

We point out that when
\Omega
has finite measure, assumption (
2.7
) may always be obtained by replacing the given
N
-function with any
N
-function which is equivalent to the original near infinite and which makes the integral finite.

Also, if (
2.8
) does not hold, the embedding in Theorem
2.5
is into
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Finally, we point out that we will denote by
C
universal positive constants, even if it changes from line to line in the calculations.

3.
Main assumptions on the data

Regarding the
N
-functions, we will assume that all
N
-functions satisfies assumptions (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). For such a pair
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
of
N
-functions, we consider the elliptic system (
1.1
) and we let

E:=W_{0}^{1,G_{1}}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,G_{2}}(\Omega)

equipped with the norm

\|(u,v)\|:=\|u\|_{1,G_{1}}+\|v\|_{1,G_{2}},

for any
(u,v)\in E
.

Regarding the
N
-functions
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, we assume that they satisfy (
2.5
), (
2.7
) and (
2.8
). Moreover, if
p_{i}^{+}
and
p_{i}^{-}
are the associated exponents for
i=1,2
, we will assume that

(3.1)

\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}<\min\left\{(p_{1}^{-})^{*},(p_{2}^{-})^{*}\right\}.

We assume that
F=F(s,t)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, for simplicity that
F(0,0)=0
,
F\geq 0,
and the following growth conditions:

(F1)

Critical growth: there exists
C>0
such that

|F_{s}(s,t)|\leq C\left((G_{1}^{*})^{\prime}(|s|)+(\tilde{G_{1}^{*}})^{-1}(G_{2}^{*}(|t|))\right)

and

|F_{t}(s,t)|\leq C\left((G_{2}^{*})^{\prime}(|t|)+(\tilde{G_{2}^{*}})^{-1}(G_{1}^{*}(|s|))\right).

(F2)

Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz’s condition: there are
c_{0},c_{0}^{\prime}>0
such that for all
s
and
t
,

c_{0}F(s,t)\leq F_{s}(s,t)s+F_{t}(s,t)t\leq c^{\prime}_{0}F(s,t).

Moreover, assume that

(3.2)

c_{0}>\max\left\{p_{1}^{-},p_{2}^{-}\right\}

(F3)

Weak homogeneity: for all functions
(u,v)\in E
with
u,v\geq R
in a subdomain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
and for some
R>0
, there holds

\int_{\Omega}F(tu,tv)\,dx\geq c\left(|t|^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}+|t|^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}\right),

for all
t
and where
c=c(\Omega^{\prime},R,u,v)>0
.

(F3)’

There is a constant
C>0
such that for all
s
and
t
,

F(s,t)\geq C\left(G_{1}^{*}(s)+G_{2}^{*}(t)\right).

Regarding
H=H(\cdot,s,t)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\left\{(0,0)\right\})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
H(x,0,0)=0
for all
x\in\Omega
,
H\geq 0
and the following growth conditions are assumed:

(H1)

Subritical growth: there exist
C>0
and
N
-functions
G_{i}\ll B_{i}\ll G^{*}
such that

|H_{s}(x,s,t)|\leq C\left((B_{1})^{\prime}(|s|)+(\tilde{B_{1}})^{-1}(B_{2}(|t|))\right)

and

|H_{t}(x,s,t)|\leq C\left((B_{2})^{\prime}(|t|)+(\tilde{B_{2}})^{-1}(B_{1}(|s|))\right).

Additionally, the following relation between exponents is assumed

(3.3)

\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}<\min\left\{p_{B_{1}}^{-},p_{B_{2}}^{-}\right\},\quad\max\left\{p_{B_{1}}^{+},p_{B_{2}}^{+}\right\}<\min\left\{(p_{1}^{-})^{*},(p_{2}^{-})^{*}\right\}.

(H2)

Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz’s condition: there is
c_{1}>0
such that for all
s
and
t
,

H_{s}(x,s,t)s+H_{t}(x,s,t)t\geq c_{1}H(x,s,t).

Moreover, assume that

(3.4)

c_{1}>\max\left\{p_{1}^{-},p_{2}^{-}\right\}

(H3)

Positivity: there is a set
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
such that for all
r_{0}
and
s
and
t
verifying
s,t\geq r_{0}
, there holds

H(x,u,v)>0,\text{ for all }x\in\Omega_{0}.

Before discussing some consequences of the above assumptions, we give examples related to the sources in systems (
1.2
) and (
1.3
).

Example 3.1
.

Consider the nonlinearity

F(s,t)=|s|^{\alpha}|t|^{\beta},\quad\alpha,\beta>p,\alpha+\beta=p^{*}.

Then, taking
G_{1}(t)=G_{2}(t)=|t|^{p}
, we have from

\alpha-1+\beta=p^{*}-1

that

\dfrac{1}{\frac{p^{*}-1}{\alpha-1}}+\dfrac{1}{\frac{p^{*}-1}{\beta}}=1

and so Young’s inequality gives

|F_{s}(s,t)|=|\alpha|s|^{\alpha-2}s|t|^{\beta}|\leq C\left(|s|^{p^{*}-1}+|t|^{p^{*}-1}\right).

A similar argument applies to
F_{t}
and hence
(F1)
holds. Assumption
(F2)
is straightforward. Finally, for
(H3)
, take
u,v\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
and suppose
u,v\geq R>0
in a domain
\Omega^{\prime}
. Then, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{\Omega}|tu|^{\alpha}|tv|^{\beta}\,dx\geq t^{\alpha+\beta}R^{\alpha+\beta}|\Omega^{\prime}|.

Hence, recalling that
\alpha+\beta=p^{*}
, we prove that
F
satisfies
(F3)
as well.

Example 3.2
.

Consider

F(s,t)=|s|^{p_{1}^{*}}+|t|^{p_{2}^{*}}+s|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}+t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}},\quad p_{1},p_{2}>1.

Assume that

(3.5)

\max\left\{p_{1},p_{2}\right\}\leq\min\left\{p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*}\right\}.

Then

F_{s}(s,t)=p_{1}^{*}|s|^{p_{1}^{*}-2}s+|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}+\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}-2}s.

Take

\alpha=\dfrac{(p_{1}^{*}-1)p_{2}^{*}}{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)-p_{2}^{*}}\quad\text{and }\quad\beta=\dfrac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}.

Then

\dfrac{1}{\alpha}+\dfrac{1}{\beta}=1

and so by Young’s inequality

t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}-2}s\leq C\left(|t|^{\beta}+|s|^{\alpha\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}-1}\right)=C\left(|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}+|s|^{p_{1}^{*}-1}\right)

A similar estimate is obtained for
|F_{t}(s,t)|
. This shows that
F
satisfies
(F1)
.

Regarding
(F2)
, observe that

(3.6)

\begin{split}sF_{s}(s,t)+tF_{t}(s,t)&=p_{1}^{*}|s|^{p_{1}^{*}}+s|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}+\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}}\\
&\quad+p_{2}^{*}|t|^{p_{2}^{*}}+t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}}+\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}s|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}\\
&=p_{1}^{*}|s|^{p_{1}^{*}}+p_{2}^{*}|t|^{p_{2}^{*}}+\left(1+\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}\right)s|t|^{\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}}\\
&\quad+\left(1+\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}\right)t|s|^{\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}}.\end{split}

The upper bound in
(F2)
is obtained straightforward. For the lower bound, observe that

(3.7)

1+\frac{p_{1}^{*}(p_{2}^{*}-1)}{p_{2}^{*}}=1+p_{1}^{*}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{*}}\right)>p_{1}^{*}.

Similarly,

(3.8)

1+\frac{p_{2}^{*}(p_{1}^{*}-1)}{p_{1}^{*}}=1+p_{2}^{*}\left(1-\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{*}}\right)>p_{2}^{*}.

Hence, combining (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) and appealing to (
3.5
), we obtain

sF_{s}(s,t)+tF_{t}(s,t)>\min\left\{p_{1}^{*},p_{2}^{*}\right\}F(s,t)\geq\max\left\{p_{1},p_{2}\right\}F(s,t).

Finally
(F3)
follows easily.

The following lemma provides a useful consequence of assumption
(F1)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F
satisfy
(F1)
. Then, there
C>0
such that

|F(s,t)|\leq C\left(G_{1}^{*}(s)+G_{2}^{*}(t)\right).

Proof.

Let
s,t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

(3.9)

\begin{split}|F(s,t)|&\leq\int_{0}^{|s|}|F_{s}(r,t)|\,dr+|F(0,t)|\\
&\leq C\int_{0}^{|s|}\left((G^{*}_{1})^{\prime}(r)+\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}^{-1}(G^{*}_{2}(t))\right)\,dx+|F(0,t)|\\
&=C\left(G_{1}^{*}(|s|)+\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}^{-1}(G^{*}_{2}(t))|s|\right)+|F(0,t)|.\end{split}

By Young’s inequality (
2.3
), we obtain

|F(s,t)|\leq C\left(G_{1}^{*}(s)+G^{*}_{2}(t)\right)+|F(0,t)|.

Now, reasoning similarly and recalling that
F(0,0)=0
, we get

|F(0,t)|\leq CG_{2}^{*}(t).

This ends the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

By reasoning as in Lemma
3.3
, we obtain that
H
satisfies

(3.10)

|H(s,t)|\leq C\left(B_{1}(|s|)+B_{2}(|t|)\right).

4.
Preliminary results

In this section, we provide the auxiliary main results that we will employ in the proof of the Theorem
1.1
.

The associated energy functional
\Phi=\Phi_{\lambda}
to the system (
1.1
) is

\Phi_{\lambda}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(|\nabla u|)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(|\nabla v|)\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F(u,v)\,dx-\lambda\int_{\Omega}H(x,u,v)\,dx.

Since our approach is variations, weak solutions of (
1.1
) are precisely those couples
(u,v)\in E
such that

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u,v),(\phi,\varphi)\right\rangle=0,

for any
\phi,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Due to the growth rates of the right hand sides of system (
1.1
), the functional
\Phi

may not satisfies the Palais-Smale condition. So, we provide the following Mountain Pass Theorem without the Palais-Smale condition. Its proof can be found in
[
2
, pag. 272]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
E
be a Banach space and
I\in C^{1}(E,\mathbb{R})
. Suppose that there exist a neighbourhood
U
of
0
in
E
and a constant
\alpha
satisfying the following conditions

(i)

I(u)\geq\alpha
for all
u\in\partial U
,

(ii)

I(0)<\alpha
,

(iii)

there exists
u_{0}\notin U
satisfying
I(u_{0})<\alpha
.

Let

\Gamma=\left\{\gamma\in C([0,1],E):\gamma(0)=0,\,\gamma(1)=u_{0}\right\}

and

c=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{u\in\gamma([0,1])}I(u)\geq\alpha.

Then, there exists a sequence
u_{k}\in E
such that

I(u_{k})\to c\quad\text{and }\quad I^{\prime}(u_{k})\to 0\,\,\text{in }E^{\prime},

as
k\to\infty
.

A sequence satisfying the conclusion of Theorem
4.1
is called a Palais-Smale sequence.

When applying Theorem (
4.1
) to
\Phi
we will find a sequence
(u_{k},v_{k})
which is a Palais-Smale sequence for
\Phi
and that will be bounded. The next concentration compactness result obtained in
[
16
]
will help us to deal with such a sequence (see also
[
14
]
where a sharper result was obtain).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
w_{k}\in W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
be bounded and converging weakly to
w\in W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)
. Then, there exist an at most countable set
J
, a family of distinct points
\{x_{j}\}_{j\in J}
and positive constants
\nu_{j}
such that

G^{*}(|w_{k}|)\,dx\rightharpoonup\nu\text{ in }\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

G(|\nabla w_{k}|)\,dx\rightharpoonup\mu\text{ in }\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where the nonnegative Radon measures
\mu
and
\nu
satisfy:

\nu=G^{*}(|w|)\,dx+\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}\delta_{x_{j}},

\mu\geq G^{*}(|\nabla w|)\,dx+\sum_{j\in J}\mu_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}.

Moreover, the coefficients
\mu_{j}
and
\nu_{j}
verify

0<\nu_{j}\leq\max\left\{S_{0}^{(p^{-})^{*}}\mu_{j}^{(p^{-})^{*}/p^{-}},S_{0}^{(p^{+})^{*}}\mu_{j}^{(p^{+})^{*}/p^{-}},S_{0}^{(p^{-})^{*}}\mu_{j}^{(p^{-})^{*}/p^{+}},S_{0}^{(p^{-})^{*}}\mu_{j}^{(p^{+})^{*}/p^{+}}\right\}.

The constant
S_{0}>0
is the best constant in the embedding
W_{0}^{1,G}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{G^{*}}(\Omega)
. Observe that in view of (
3.1
) all the powers of
\mu_{j}
are greater than 1.

We end the section with a numerical lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
a,b>0
and
p,q\in(0,1)
. Then

a^{p}+b^{q}\geq(a+b)^{\alpha},

for some
\alpha>0
which takes some of the following values:
p
,
q
or
p/2+q/2
.

Proof.

Suppose first that
a,b<1
. Then,

(a+b)^{\max\left\{p,q\right\}}\leq a^{\max\left\{p,q\right\}}+b^{\max\left\{p,q\right\}}\leq a^{p}+b^{q},

which proves the lemma in this case. Next, assume that
a,b\geq 1
. Then,

(a+b)^{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}\leq a^{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}+b^{\min\left\{p,q\right\}}\leq a^{p}+b^{q}.

Finally, suppose that
a\geq 1
and
b<1
. Assume for simplicity that
p\geq q
. Then,

(a+b)^{p/2+q/2}\leq a^{p/2+q/2}+b^{p/2+q/2}\leq a^{p}+b^{q}.

This ends the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We divide the proof of Theorem
1.1
into several steps.

5.1.
Geometry of the energy functional and boundedness of the Palais-Smale sequence

In this part, we will prove that the energy functional
\Phi
satisfies the geometric conditions of Theorem
4.1
. We start with the next lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

There are
\alpha>0
and
\rho>0
such that for all
(u,v)\in E
with
\|(u,v)\|=\rho
, there holds

\Phi(u,v)\geq\alpha.

Proof.

Assume that
\rho>0
is small and take
(u,v)\in E
with
\|(u,v)\|=\rho
. Then, by Lemma
3.3
, the estimate (
3.10
), and Theorem
2.5
,

\begin{split}\Phi(u,v)&\geq\|u\|_{1,G_{1}}^{p_{1}^{+}}+\|v\|_{1,G_{2}}^{p_{2}^{+}}-\left(\|u\|_{G_{1}^{*}}^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}+\|v\|_{G_{2}^{*}}^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}\right)-\lambda\left(\|u\|_{B_{1}}^{P_{B_{1}}^{-}}+\|v\|_{B_{2}}^{P_{B_{2}}^{-}}\right)\\
&\geq C(\|u\|_{1,G_{1}}+\|v\|_{1,G_{2}})^{\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}}-C\left(\|u\|_{1,G_{1}}^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}+\|v\|_{1,G_{2}}^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}\right)-\lambda C\left(\|u\|_{1,G_{1}}^{P_{B_{1}}^{-}}+\|v\|_{1,G_{2}}^{P_{B_{2}}^{-}}\right)\\
&\geq C\left(\rho^{\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}}-\rho^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}-\rho^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}-\rho^{p_{B_{1}}^{-}}-\rho^{p_{B_{2}}^{-}}\right)\geq\alpha,\end{split}

for some
\alpha>0
taking
\rho
small and recalling the assumptions (
3.1
) and (
3.3
) which gives
\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}<(p_{i}^{-})^{*}
and
\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}<p_{B_{i}}^{-}
for

.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

There exists
(u_{0},v_{0})\notin B(0,\rho)
such that

\Phi(u_{0},v_{0})<\alpha.

Proof.

Since
F
satisfies assumptions
(F3)
, we may choose
u_{0},v_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
verifying
(u_{0},v_{0})\notin B(0,\rho)
and
u_{0},v_{0}\geq R>0
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
, where
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
is a domain strictly contained in
\Omega_{0}
(from assumption
(F3)
) and such that for all
t
:

\int_{\Omega}F(tu_{0},tv_{0})\,dx\geq c\left(|t|^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}+|t|^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}\right),\quad c>0.

Hence, for
t>0
large and recalling assumption
(H3)
,

(5.1)

\Phi(tu_{0},tv_{0})\leq|t|^{p_{1}^{+}}\|u_{0}\|_{1,G_{1}}^{p_{1}^{+}}+|t|^{p_{2}^{+}}\|v_{0}\|_{1,G_{2}}^{p_{2}^{+}}-c\left(|t|^{(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}+|t|^{(p_{2}^{-})^{*}}\right).

Since
p_{i}^{+}<(p_{i}^{-})^{*}
for

, by taking
t
large enough in (
5.1
), we conclude the proof.
∎

By Theorem
4.1
, there is a sequence
(u_{n},v_{n})\in E
such that

\Phi(u_{n},v_{n})\to c\in\mathbb{R},\quad\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),(u_{n},v_{n})\right\rangle\to 0.

In the next result, we will prove that the Palais-Smale sequence is bounded.

Lemma 5.3
.

The sequence
(u_{n},v_{n})
is bounded in
E
.

Proof.

Let
\mu>0
satisfying

(5.2)

\dfrac{1}{c_{j}}<\mu<\dfrac{1}{p_{i}^{-}}

for
j=0,1
and
i=1,2
. The constants
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
come from assumptions
(F2)
and
(H2)
.

Firstly, observe that

(5.3)

\Phi(u_{n},v_{n})-\mu\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),(u_{n},v_{n})\right\rangle\leq C(1+\|(u_{n},v_{n})\|_{E})=C\left[1+\left(\|u_{n}\|_{1,G_{1}}+\|v_{n}\|_{1,G_{2}}\right)\right],

for some
C>0.

Moreover, by assumption
(H2)
and (
5.2
),

(5.4)

\begin{split}&\Phi(u_{n},v_{n})-\mu\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),(u_{n},v_{n})\right\rangle\\
&\quad\geq(1-p_{1}^{-}\mu)\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\,dx+(1-p_{2}^{-}\mu)\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\,dx\\
&\qquad+(\mu c_{0}-1)\int_{\Omega}F(u_{n},v_{n})\,dx+\lambda(\mu c_{1}-1)\int_{\Omega}H(x,u_{n},v_{n})\,dx\\
&\quad\geq(1-p_{1}^{-}\mu)\min\left\{\|u_{n}\|_{1,G_{1}}^{p_{1}^{+}},\|u_{n}\|_{1,G_{1}}^{p_{1}^{-}}\right\}+(1-p_{2}^{-}\mu)\min\left\{\|v_{n}\|_{1,G_{2}}^{p_{2}^{+}},\|v_{n}\|_{1,G_{2}}^{p_{2}^{-}}\right\}.\end{split}

Since

, we obtain from (

) and (
5.4
) that the sequence
(u_{n},v_{n})
is bounded in
E
.
∎

5.2.
Analysis of the PS-sequence via the Concentration Compactness Principle

By Lemma
5.3
and the embedding Theorem
2.5
, there are functions
(u,v)\in E
and measures
\mu_{i}
,
\nu_{i}
for
i=1,2
such that for a subsequence,

(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)\quad\text{ in }L^{A_{1}}(\Omega)\times L^{A_{2}}(\Omega),\,A_{i}\ll G_{i}^{*},

(u_{n},v_{n})\to(u,v)\quad a.e.\text{ in }\Omega,

(\nabla u_{n},\nabla v_{n})\rightharpoonup(\nabla u,\nabla v)\quad\text{in }L^{G_{1}}(\Omega)\times L^{G_{2}}(\Omega),

and finally

(G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})dx,G^{*}_{2}(v_{n})dx)\rightharpoonup(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})\quad\text{and}\quad(G_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)dx,G_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)dx)\rightharpoonup(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

in the sense of
wk^{*}
-convergence of measures. By Theorem
4.2
, there are an at most countable set
J
, points
x_{j}^{i}
and positive coefficients
\nu_{j}^{i}
and
\mu_{j}^{i}
for
i=1,2
, such that

(5.5)

\nu^{1}=G_{1}^{*}(u)+\sum_{j\in J}\nu_{j}^{1}\delta_{x_{j}^{1}}

and

(5.6)

\mu^{1}\geq G_{1}(|\nabla u|)+\sum_{j\in J}\mu_{j}^{1}\delta_{x_{j}^{1}}

and similarly for
v
,
\mu^{2}
and
\nu^{2}
.

In the next result, we will prove that
J
is indeed finite.

Proposition 5.4
.

The sets
\left\{x_{j}^{i}\right\}_{j\in J}
for
i=1,2
, are finite.

Proof.

Since the argument is symmetric, we will show that the set
\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}
is finite. Let us take
x_{j}^{1}
fixed. Consider a smooth function
\psi
such that
0\leq\psi\leq 1
,

\psi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad|x|\leq 1\\
0,\quad|x|>2\end{cases}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, define

\phi_{\varepsilon}(x):=\psi\left(\dfrac{x-x_{j}^{1}}{\varepsilon}\right).

Since

\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0,

testing with
(\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n},\phi_{\varepsilon}v_{n})
, we obtain

(5.7)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla(\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla(\phi_{\varepsilon}v_{n})\,dx\\
&\qquad=\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+o_{n}(1)\\
&\qquad\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}G_{2}^{*}(v_{n})\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)\\
&\qquad+C\left(\int_{\Omega}B_{1}(u_{n})\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}B_{2}(v_{n})\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)+o_{n}(1).\end{split}

Now,

(5.8)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla(\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n})\,dx&=\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\\
&\geq\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n}\,dx+p_{1}^{-}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}G_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\,dx.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.3
we have

\tilde{G_{1}}(g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|)\leq CG_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)

and so the sequence
g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\tilde{G_{1}}}(\Omega)
. Thus, there is
w_{1}\in L^{\tilde{G_{1}}}(\Omega)
such that

g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\rightharpoonup w_{1}.

Hence, we have

(5.9)

\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n}\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}w_{1}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u\,dx.

This follows by adding and subtracting the term

g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u

in the difference

\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u_{n}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}w_{1}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u\,dx

and recalling the strong convergence of
u_{n}
to
u
in the support of
\phi_{\varepsilon}
.

Similarly,

(5.10)

\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}v_{n}\,dx\to\int_{\Omega}w_{2}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}v\,dx,

for some
w_{2}\in L^{\tilde{G_{2}}}(\Omega)
. Taking
n\to\infty
and recalling (
5.7
), (
5.8
), the convergences (
5.9
) and (
5.10
) together with (
5.5
) and (
5.6
), we obtain

(5.11)

\int_{\Omega}w_{1}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u\,dx+\int_{\Omega}w_{2}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}v\,dx+p_{1}^{-}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,d\mu^{1}+p_{2}^{-}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,d\mu^{2}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,d\nu^{1}+\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}\,d\nu^{2}\right).

We next revise the convergence of the above terms as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. Using
(u\phi_{\varepsilon},v\phi_{\varepsilon})
as test functions in
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n})\to 0
, we derive

(5.12)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla(u\phi_{\varepsilon})\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla(v\phi_{\varepsilon})\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})u\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})v\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})u\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})v\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx=o_{n}(1).\end{split}

By assumption
(F1)
, the sequences
F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})
and
F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})
are bounded in
L^{\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}}(\Omega)
, respectively. Hence, there are functions
b_{1}\in L^{\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}}(\Omega)
and
b_{2}\in L^{\tilde{G_{2}^{*}}}(\Omega)
such that

F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup b_{1}\quad\text{and }F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup b_{2}.

Similarly, there are
h_{1}\in L^{\tilde{B_{1}}}(\Omega)
and

such that

Thus, when
n\to\infty
in (

), we get

(5.13)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}w_{1}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}u\,dx+\int_{\Omega}w_{2}\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}b_{1}u\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}b_{2}v\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}w_{1}\nabla u\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx\\
&\qquad-\int_{\Omega}w_{2}\nabla v\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}h_{1}u\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}h_{2}v\phi_{\varepsilon}\,dx.\end{split}

By dominated convergence theorem, the right-hand side of (

) converges to

as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Assume first that
x_{j}^{1}\notin\left\{x_{i}^{2}\right\}_{i\in J}
.
Letting
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
in (

), we obtain

(5.14)

0<p_{1}^{-}\mu_{j}^{1}\leq C\nu_{j}^{1}.

Hence, by Theorem
4.2
,

p_{1}^{-}\mu_{j}^{1}\leq C\nu_{j}^{1}\leq C(\mu_{j}^{1})^{\alpha},

for some
\alpha>1
. Therefore,

(5.15)

\mu_{j}^{1}>c

for some constant

independent of

.

If now
x^{1}_{j}=x_{i}^{2}
for some
i
. Then, we obtain

0<p_{1}^{-}\mu_{j}^{1}+p_{2}^{-}\mu_{i}^{2}\leq C\nu_{j}^{1}+C\nu_{i}^{2}.

We may take
\mu_{i}^{2}<<1
. Then, by Theorem

,

0<p_{1}^{-}\mu_{j}^{1}+p_{2}^{-}\mu_{i}^{2}\leq C\nu_{j}^{1}+C\nu_{i}^{2}\leq C(\mu_{j}^{1})^{\alpha}+C(\mu_{i}^{2})^{\beta}

for some

. Thus,

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K\subset\Omega\setminus\left(\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{j}^{2}\right\}\right)
be compact. Then,

(5.17)

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{and }\quad v_{n}\to v

in

and
L^{G_{2}^{*}}(K)
respectively, as

.

Proof.

Let
d:=\text{dist}(K,\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{j}^{2}\right\})>0
. Fix
R>0
so that
K\subset B_{R}(0)
and for
\varepsilon>0
consider the set

Take
\varepsilon<d
. Now, consider a smooth function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

and

\phi(x)=\begin{cases}1,\quad x\in A_{\varepsilon/2}\\

Observe that

K\subset A_{\varepsilon/2}\subset\Omega\setminus\left(\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{j}^{2}\right\}\right)\cap B_{R}(0),

implies

\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx\leq\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\phi G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\phi G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx.

Thus, by Theorem
4.2
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx\leq\int_{\Omega}\phi\,d\nu^{1}=\int_{\Omega}\phi G_{1}^{*}(u)\,dx=\int_{A_{\varepsilon}}\phi G_{1}^{*}(u)\,dx.

As
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, it follows that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx\leq\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u)\,dx

which together with Fatou’s Lemma give

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u_{n})\,dx=\int_{K}G_{1}^{*}(u)\,dx.

Finally, by the Brezis-Lieb Lemma (
[
5
, Lemma 3]
), we derive (
5.17
).
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
K\subset\Omega\setminus\left(\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{j}^{2}\right\}\right)
be compact. Then,

(5.18)

\int_{K}\left(g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}-g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\right)(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u)\,dx\to 0

and

(5.19)

\int_{K}\left(g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}-g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v\right)(\nabla v_{n}-\nabla v)\,dx\to 0

as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

Take a smooth function
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
\phi=1
in
K
and

supp(\phi)\cap\left(\left\{x_{j}^{1}\right\}\cup\left\{x_{j}^{2}\right\}\right)=\emptyset.

Then,

(5.20)

\begin{split}0&\leq\int_{K}\left(g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}-g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\right)(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u)\,dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{K}\left(g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}-g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v\right)(\nabla v_{n}-\nabla v)\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}-g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\right)(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u)\phi\,dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}\left(g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}-g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v\right)(\nabla v_{n}-\nabla v)\phi\,dx\end{split}

The sequences
(u_{n}-u)\phi
and
(v_{n}-v)\phi
are bounded in
W^{1,G_{1}}(\Omega)
and
W^{1,G_{2}}(\Omega)
, respectively, so

(5.21)

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),\left((u_{n}-u)\phi,(v_{n}-v)\phi\right)\right\rangle\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Now,

(5.22)

\begin{split}\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),\left((u_{n}-u)\phi,(v_{n}-v)\phi\right)\right\rangle&=\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla((u_{n}-u)\phi)\,dx\\
&\qquad+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla((v_{n}-v)\phi)\,dx\\
&-\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})(u_{n}-u)\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})(v_{n}-v)\phi\,dx\\
&-\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})(u_{n}-u)\phi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})(v_{n}-v)\phi\,dx.\end{split}

Recall that by assumption
(F1)
,
F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})
and
F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})
are bounded in
L^{\tilde{G_{1}^{*}}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{G_{2}^{*}}}(\Omega)
, respectively. Hence, by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
5.5
applied in the support of
\phi
,

(5.23)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})(u_{n}-u)\phi\,dx\bigg|,\bigg|\int_{\Omega}F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})(v_{n}-v)\phi\,dx\bigg|\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.
Similarly,

(5.24)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})(u_{n}-u)\phi\,dx\bigg|,\bigg|\int_{\Omega}H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})(v_{n}-v)\phi\,dx\bigg|\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
.

Hence, (
5.21
), (
5.22
), (
5.23
) and (
5.24
) give

(5.25)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla((u_{n}-u)\phi)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla((v_{n}-v)\phi)\,dx\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Now, by Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
5.5
applied in the support of
\phi
again, we get

(5.26)

\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\nabla\phi(u_{n}-u)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\nabla\phi(v_{n}-v)\,dx\to 0,

as

. Finally, by the weak convergences
(\nabla u_{n},\nabla v_{n})\rightharpoonup(\nabla u,\nabla v)
in
L^{G_{1}}(\Omega)\times L^{G_{2}}(\Omega)
, we derive

(5.27)

\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u(\nabla u_{n}-\nabla u)\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v(\nabla v_{n}-\nabla v)\phi\,dx\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Finally, combining (
5.25
), (
5.26
), (
5.27
) with (
5.20
) give (
5.18
) and (
5.19
).

∎

To obtain the almost everywhere convergence of the gradients, we appeal to the following weak version of

, Lemma 7.1]
which may obtained without the condition that
t\to G(\sqrt{t})
is convex: there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all

,
a\neq b
, we have

\left\langle\dfrac{g(|a|)}{|a|}a-\dfrac{g(|b|)}{|b|}b,a-b\right\rangle>0.

Applying this lemma with

and

5.6
and

5.3.
Final argument

We first show the existence of a weak solution to the system (
1.1
). Observe that the sequences

g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\quad\text{and }\quad g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}

are bounded in
L^{\tilde{G_{1}}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{G_{2}}}(\Omega)
, respectively, and converge a.e. to

g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u\quad\text{and }\quad g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v,

respectively. Then,

g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\nabla u_{n}\rightharpoonup g_{1}(|\nabla u|)\nabla u

and

g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\nabla v_{n}\rightharpoonup g_{2}(|\nabla v|)\nabla v.

Similarly, the sequences

F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})\quad\text{and }\quad F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})

are bounded in
L^{\tilde{G^{*}_{1}}}(\Omega)
and
L^{\tilde{G^{*}_{2}}}(\Omega)
, respectively, and converge a. e. to

F_{s}(u,v)\quad\text{and }\quad F_{t}(u,v).

Hence,

F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup F_{s}(u,v)\quad\text{and }\quad F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup F_{t}(u,v).

In a similar way, there holds

H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup H_{s}(x,u,v)\quad\text{and }\quad H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})\rightharpoonup H_{t}(x,u,v).

Therefore, from

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{n},v_{n}),(\phi,\varphi)\right\rangle=o_{n}(1),\quad\phi,\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

and the above comments, there holds that the pair
(u,v)
satisfies

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u,v),(\phi,\varphi)\right\rangle=0

and so it solves (
1.1
).

Finally, we show that
(u,v)\neq(0,0)
. We will argue by contradiction. So assume that
(u,v)=(0,0)
.

By (
5.1
), we have for
u_{0},v_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})
,
u_{0},v_{0}\geq R>0
in
\Omega^{\prime}_{0}
a subdomain strictly contained in
\Omega_{0}
(where
\Omega_{0}
comes from assumption
(H3)
), that

0<c_{\lambda}\leq\max_{t\geq 0}\Phi_{\lambda}(tu_{0},tv_{0})=\Phi_{\lambda}(t_{\lambda}u_{0},t_{\lambda}v_{0}),

where
t_{\lambda}\in(0,T_{0})
for
T_{0}>0
independent of
\lambda
. We next show that
t_{\lambda}\to 0
as
\lambda\to\infty
. By contradiction, suppose there are a sequence
\lambda_{j}\to\infty
and
\delta>0
such that

\delta\leq t_{\lambda_{j}}\leq T_{0}.

Observe that assumption
(H3)
implies that

c=\min\left\{H(x,tu_{0}(x),tv_{0}(x)):x\in\Omega^{\prime}_{0},\,\delta\leq t\leq T_{0}\right\}>0.

Hence, by (
5.1
), there is a bounded term
C(t_{\lambda_{j}})
such that

0<c_{\lambda_{j}}\leq\max\Phi_{\lambda_{j}}(tu_{0},tv_{0})=\Phi_{\lambda_{j}}(t_{\lambda_{j}}u_{0},t_{\lambda_{j}}v_{0})\leq C(t_{\lambda_{j}})-c\lambda_{j}|\Omega^{\prime}_{0}|\to-\infty,

as
j\to\infty
, which is a contradiction.

So far, we have obtained that as
\lambda\to\infty
,

(5.28)

c_{\lambda}\to 0^{+}.

Since
(u,v)=(0,0)
, we get

(5.29)

\bigg|\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}+H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,dx\bigg|\leq C\left(\|u_{n}\|_{B_{1}}\|H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})\|_{\tilde{B_{1}}}+\|v_{n}\|_{B_{2}}\|H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})\|_{\tilde{B_{2}}}\right)\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
. Hence, from

\left\langle\Phi^{\prime}_{\lambda}(u_{n},v_{n}),(u_{n},v_{n})\right\rangle\to 0,

as
n\to\infty
, we get by (
5.29
) that for a subsequence if necessary,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}+g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}+F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,dx=k.

Observe that
k>0
since

0<c_{\lambda}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{n},v_{n})\leq\dfrac{k}{\min\left\{p_{1}^{-},p_{2}^{-}\right\}},

where we have used that
F\geq 0
, (
5.29
), and (
2.5
).

Next, we will obtain a uniform lower bound for
k
. Observe that by Lemma
2.4

\begin{split}\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{G_{1}}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{G_{2}}&\leq\max\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{+}},\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{-}}\right\}\\
&+\max\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{+}},\left(\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{-}}\right\}\\
&\leq\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{+}},\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{-}}\right\}\\
&+\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{+}},\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{-}}\right\}\\
&\leq\max\bigg\{\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{+}},\\
&\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{1}^{-}}\bigg\}\\
&+\max\bigg\{\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{+}},\\
&\left(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{-}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/p_{2}^{-}}\bigg\}\end{split}

Hence,

(5.30)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{G_{1}}+\|\nabla v_{n}\|_{G_{2}}\right)\leq\max\left\{k^{1/p_{1}^{+}},k^{1/p_{1}^{-}},k^{1/p_{2}^{+}},k^{1/p_{2}^{-}}\right\}.

Similarly, appealing to Lemma
4.3
,

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{1}}+\|v_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{2}}&\geq\min\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{1}(|u_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/(p_{1}^{+})^{*}},\left(\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{1}(|u_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}\right\}\\
&+\min\left\{\left(\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{2}(|v_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/(p_{2}^{+})^{*}},\left(\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{2}(|v_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{1/(p_{1}^{-})^{*}}\right\}\\
&\geq\left(\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{1}(|u_{n}|)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}G^{*}_{2}(|v_{n}|)\,dx\right)^{\alpha},\end{split}

for some
\alpha>0
depending, according to the proof of Lemma
4.3
, on the exponents. Hence, by Lemma
3.3
and assumption
(F2)
,

(5.31)

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{1}}+\|v_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{2}}&\geq C\left(\int_{\Omega}F(u_{n},v_{n})\,dx\right)^{\alpha}\geq C\left(\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}+F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,dx\right)^{\alpha}.\end{split}

Hence,

(5.32)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\|u_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{1}}+\|v_{n}\|_{G^{*}_{2}}\right)\geq Ck^{\alpha}.

Thus, combining (
5.30
) and (
5.32
) with the Orlicz-Sobolev embedding Theorem
2.5
, we deduce

k^{\alpha}\leq C\max\left\{k^{1/p_{1}^{+}},k^{1/p_{1}^{-}},k^{1/p_{2}^{+}},k^{1/p_{2}^{-}}\right\}.

We point out that in view of assumption (
3.1
),

\alpha<\dfrac{1}{p_{i}^{+}},

for
i=1,2
. Then, since
k>0
, there is a constant
K>0
independent of
\lambda
such that
k\geq K
. Therefore, recalling (
5.29
),

(5.33)

\begin{split}c_{\lambda}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\lambda}(u_{n},v_{n})&\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\dfrac{1}{p_{1}^{+}}\int_{\Omega}g_{1}(|\nabla u_{n}|)|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}\,dx+\dfrac{1}{p_{2}^{+}}\int_{\Omega}g_{2}(|\nabla v_{n}|)|\nabla v_{n}|^{2}\,dx\\
&-\dfrac{1}{c_{0}}\int_{\Omega}F_{s}(u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}+F_{t}(u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,dx\\
&-\dfrac{1}{c_{1}}\int_{\Omega}H_{s}(x,u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}+H_{t}(x,u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,dx\bigg)\\
&\geq\left(\dfrac{1}{\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}}-\dfrac{1}{c_{0}}\right)k\\
&\geq\left(\dfrac{1}{\max\left\{p_{1}^{+},p_{2}^{+}\right\}}-\dfrac{1}{c_{0}}\right)K.\end{split}

Thus, recalling (
5.28
), for
\lambda
large enough, we obtain a contradiction. Hence, at least one
u
or
v
is different from
0
. This ends the proof of the theorem.

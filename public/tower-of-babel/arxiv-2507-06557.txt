On the commutator scaling in Hamiltonian simulation with multi-product formulas
Kaoru Mizuta

Abstract
A multi-product formula (MPF) is a promising approach for Hamiltonian simulation efficient both in the system size N N and the inverse allowable error 1 / ε 1/\varepsilon by combining Trotterization and linear combination of unitaries (LCU). The efficiency in 1 / ε 1/\varepsilon comes from the Richardson extrapolation with the well-conditioned coefficients, achieving poly-logarithmic cost in 1 / ε 1/\varepsilon like LCU [G. H. Low, V. Kliuchnikov, N. Wiebe, arXiv:1907.11679 (2019)]. The efficiency in N N is expected to come from the commutator scaling in Trotterization, and this appears to be confirmed by the error bound of MPF expressed by nested commutators [J. Aftab, D. An, K. Trivisa, arXiv:2403.08922 (2024)]. However, we point out that the efficiency of MPF in the system size N N is not exactly resolved yet in that the present error bound expressed by nested commutators is incompatible with the size-efficient complexity reflecting the commutator scaling. The problem is that q q -fold nested commutators with arbitrarily large q q are involved in their requirement and error bound. The benefit of commutator scaling by locality is absent, and the cost efficient in N N becomes prohibited in general. In this paper, we show an alternative commutator-scaling error of MPF and derive its size-efficient cost properly inheriting the advantage in Trotterization. The requirement and the error bound in our analysis, derived by techniques from the Floquet-Magnus expansion, have a certain truncation order in the nested commutators and can fully exploit the locality. We prove that Hamiltonian simulation by MPF certainly achieves the cost whose system-size dependence is as large as Trotterization while keeping the polylog ⁡ ( 1 / ε ) \mathrm{polylog}(1/\varepsilon) -scaling like LCU. Our results will provide improved or accurate error and cost also for various algorithms using interpolation or extrapolation of Trotterization.



I
Introduction

Simulating the dynamics of quantum many-body systems, called Hamiltonian simulation, is one of the significant applications of quantum computers with promising quantum speedup.
The ultimate goal in this field is to establish an optimal quantum algorithm with smaller computational cost in the system size
N
, the simulation time
t
, and the inverse allowable error
1/\varepsilon
.
Trotterization, or called product formulas (PFs), is one of the most famous approaches, featuring simple circuit structures and requiring no ancilla qubits
Suzuki 1991
;
Lloyd 1996
.
In addition, it has favorable gate complexity in the system size
N
due to the commutator-scaling error
Somma 2015
;
Childs and Su 2019
;
Childs et al. 2021
.
Post-Trotter approaches, such as linear combination of unitaries (LCU) and quantum singular-value transform (QSVT), are alternative quantum algorithms that operate with oracles calling Hamiltonians
Berry et al. 2015
;
Low and Chuang 2017
;
Low and Chuang 2019
;
Gilyén et al. 2019
.
They have exponentially better cost in the inverse allowable error
1/\varepsilon
than Trotterization owing to the usage of accurate polynomial approximation of time-evolution operators.
On the other hand, their scaling in the system size
N
is less favorable due to the oracle overhead.

The central question in Hamiltonian simulation has been how we can construct efficient quantum algorithms both in the system size and the accuracy.
A multi-product formula (MPF)
Childs and Wiebe 2012
;
Low et al. 2019
;
Faehrmann et al. 2022
;
Vazquez et al. 2022
;
Carrera Vazquez et al. 2023
;
Zhuk et al. 2024
;
Aftab et al. 2024
is a promising approach, which is a linear combination of Trotterization with different time steps.
Coefficients and time steps in the linear combination based on the Richardson extrapolation allows MPF to cancel some of dominant Trotter errors, giving higher order approximation of the time-evolution operator.
In particular, the well-conditioned MPF, in which the low-weight coefficients and time steps are carefully chosen, can be efficiently implemented by the LCU approach
Low et al. 2019
.
It has been shown that the well-conditioned MPF can achieve
\mathrm{polylog}(1/\varepsilon)
computational cost like the LCU approach
Low et al. 2019
.
The efficiency of MPF in the system size has also been vigorously explored via the analysis of its error bound
Zhuk et al. 2024
;
Aftab et al. 2024
.
MPF is expected to inherit the commutator scaling in Trotterization and thereby host substantial reduction in the error and complexity owing to the locality of Hamiltonians.
Indeed, Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
has recently derived the error bound of generic MPF explicitly expressed by nested commutators.
Based on this error bound, it concluded that the size-dependence of the cost of MPF is comparable to that of Trotterization and better than the post-Trotter methods like LCU.
Recent studies seem to settle the issues surrounding MPF, i.e., that Hamiltonian simulation based on MPF is efficient both in the system size and the allowable error with reflecting the advantages of Trotterization and LCU.

However, focusing on the efficiency of MPF with respect to the system size, we point out that the existing error bound of MPF expressed by nested commutators
Aftab et al. 2024
does not imply the size-efficient cost reflecting the commutator scaling.
The problem is that
q
-fold nested commutators with arbitrarily large
q
are contained in the requirement and the error bound, which mainly comes from the necessity of the convergence of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula in the derivation (See Section
II.2
in detail).
The substantial reduction of the commutators due to the locality of Hamiltonians is no longer present, and the computational cost generally obeys the
1
-norm scaling, whose size-dependency is larger than the one of the commutator scaling.
Namely, it remains unresolved yet whether MPF can achieve the size-efficient cost reflecting the commutator scaling.
In this paper, we provide a complete answer to it; we derive an alternative error bound expressed by nested commutators for generic MPF and prove its proper size-efficient cost reflecting the commutator scaling.
Our error analysis includes nested commutators up to a certain truncation order in its requirement and error bound, where the problem around the BCH formula is resolved by a technique developed for the Floquet-Magnus expansion
Abanin et al. 2015
;
Kuwahara et al. 2016
;
Mori et al. 2016
;
Abanin et al. 2017a
;
Abanin et al. 2017b
.
The truncation order makes the locality of Hamiltonians work well, resulting in a much looser requirement and a better error bound compared to the previous analysis
Aftab et al. 2024
.
Based on this error bound, we establish the proper cost whose system-size dependency is comparable to that of Trotterization while keeping its polylogarithmic dependency in
1/\varepsilon
.
Our results conclude that Hamiltonian simulation by MPF is efficient with respect to both the system size and the allowable error, inheriting the advantages respectively from Trotterization and LCU.
There are various quantum algorithms sharing the mathematical background with MPF from the viewpoint of interpolation or extrapolation for Trotterization
Endo et al. 2019
;
Rendon 2025
;
Rendon et al. 2024
;
Watson and Watkins 2024
;
Watson 2025
;
Chakraborty et al. 2025
;
Guo et al. 2025
.
We expect that our approach will give a precise analysis of their errors and cost in the system size dependency, reflecting the commutator scaling.

Preliminary

In Section

II.1
Setup and multi-product formula (MPF)

Throughout this paper, we consider a system on an
N
-qubit lattice
\Lambda=\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
We assume the
k
-locality of the Hamiltonian
H
by

H=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda:|X|\leq k}h_{X},

(1)

where each
h_{X}
denotes a local term acting on a domain
X
and satisfies
[h_{X},h_{X^{\prime}}]=0
if
X\cap X^{\prime}=\phi
.
We assume
k\in\order{1}
throughout the paper.
We also define the
g
-extensiveness by

\sum_{X:X\ni j}\norm{h_X}\leq g,\quad^{\forall}j\in\Lambda,

(2)

where
\norm{\cdot}
denotes the operator norm.
The extensiveness
g
means the maximum energy per site.
It depends on the range of interactions as

g\in\begin{cases}\order{N^0}&(\text{finite-range interactions}),\\
\order{N^0}&(\text{long-range interactions, $\nu>d$}),\\
\order{\log N}&(\text{long-range interactions, $\nu=d$}),\\
\order{N^{1-\nu/d}}&(\text{long-range interactions, $\nu<d$}),\end{cases}

(3)

where
d
denotes the spatial dimension of the lattice
\Lambda
.
The quantity
\nu
means the power of the long-range interactions, where the distance-
r
interactions are assumed to decay as
\order{r^{-\nu}}
.

The goal of Hamiltonian simulation is to find an operator that accurately approximates the time evolution operator
e^{-iHt}
and can be efficiently implemented on quantum circuits.
Trotterization, or product formulas (PFs), is one of the most common approaches.
Suppose that we decompose the Hamiltonian
H
by

H=\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}H_{\gamma},\quad H_{\gamma}=\sum_{X}h_{X}^{\gamma}.

(4)

We use the notation
h_{X}
in Eq. (
1
) such that the collection
\{h_{X}^{\gamma}\}_{X,\gamma}
comprises the set of terms
\{h_{X}\}_{X}
, where each
h_{X}^{\gamma}
corresponds to one element of
\{h_{X}\}
.
With this notation, the extensiveness by Eq. (
2
) reads

\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}\sum_{X:X\ni j}\norm{h_X^\gamma}\leq g,\quad^{\forall}j\in\Lambda.

(5)

The
p
-th order formula
T_{p}(\tau)
for small time
\tau
is defined by

T_{p}(\tau)=\prod_{v=1,\cdots,c_{p}\Gamma}^{\leftarrow}e^{-iH_{\gamma_{v}}\alpha_{v}\tau},\quad\prod_{v=1,\cdots,V}^{\leftarrow}U_{v}\equiv U_{V}\cdots U_{2}U_{1},

(6)

where the coefficients
\{\alpha_{v}\}
are determined so that it satisfies the order condition,
T_{p}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{p+1}}
under
\tau\to 0
.
The number
c_{p}
denotes the repetition, which usually increases exponentially in
p
.
Due to this, we focus on the case with the order
p\in\order{1}
[and hence
c_{p}
is also
\order{1}
].
The number
\Gamma
depends on Hamiltonians, and it can be
\order{1}
for finite-ranged interacting systems while it generally belongs to
\mathrm{poly}\left(N\right)
for long-ranged interacting systems (See Ref.
Mizuta and Kuwahara 2025
for example).
For example, the first- and second-order formulas are given by
T_{1}(\tau)=\prod_{\gamma=1,\cdots,\Gamma}^{\leftarrow}e^{-iH_{\gamma}\tau}
and
T_{2}(\tau)=T_{1}(-\tau/2)^{\dagger}T_{1}(\tau/2)
.
Generic Trotter formulas exhibit so-called commutator-scaling error
Childs et al. 2021
, expressed as

\norm{e^{-iH\tau}-T_p(\tau)}\in\order{\alpha_{\mr{com},p+1}\tau^{p+1}},

(7)

\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}\equiv\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{q}=1}^{\Gamma}\norm{[H_{\gamma_q},\cdots,[H_{\gamma_2},H_{\gamma_1}]]}\leq(q-1)!(2kg)^{q-1}Ng.

(8)

This is the origin of the fact that Trotterization can achieve better cost in the system size
N
than other approaches like LCU and QSVT
Berry et al. 2015
;
Low and Chuang 2019
;
Gilyén et al. 2019
.

In contrast to the better cost in the system size
N
owing to the commutator scaling, the bottleneck of Trotterization is its enormous cost in accuracy, which increases polynomially in the inverse allowable error
1/\varepsilon
.
One of the solutions to this is to combine it with the LCU approach that can achieve exponentially better cost in
1/\varepsilon
, which leads to the multi-product formula (MPF).
We define MPF with the orders
p,m,J\in\mathbb{N}
by a linear combination of the
p
-th order Trotterization,

M_{pmJ}(\tau)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}c_{j}\{T_{p}(\tau/k_{j})\}^{k_{j}},

(9)

where the numbers
c_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
and
k_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
are organized so that it satisfies the order condition,

M_{pmJ}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{m+1}}.

(10)

One of the ways to determine
\{c_{j}\}
and
\{k_{j}\}
relies on the Richardson extrapolation
Chin 2010
;
Endo et al. 2019
;
Carrera Vazquez et al. 2023
.
For the even order
p
satisfying the symmetric condition
T_{p}(-\tau)^{\dagger}=T_{p}(\tau)
, the solution to

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&1&\cdots&1\\
(k_{1})^{-2}&(k_{2})^{-2}&\cdots&(k_{J})^{-2}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
(k_{1})^{-2J+2}&(k_{2})^{-2J+2}&\cdots&(k_{m})^{-2J+2}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{1}\\
c_{2}\\
\vdots\\
c_{J}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{array}\right)

with an arbitrary set of different natural numbers
\{k_{j}\}
gives a MPF satisfying
M_{pmJ}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{2J+1}}
(i.e.,
m=2J
).
While there are still several candidates due to the choice of
\{k_{j}\}
, the solution should satisfy

\norm{k}_{1}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{J}|k_{j}|\in\mathrm{poly}\left(J\right),\quad\norm{c}_{1}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{J}|c_{j}|\in\mathrm{poly}\left(J\right)

(24)

to efficiently implement Eq. (
9
) by the LCU approach
Berry et al. 2015
.
Such a solution is called the well-conditioned solution, and Ref.
Low et al. 2019
has found the one satisfying
\norm{k}_{1}\in\order{J^2 \log J}
and
\norm{c}_{1}\in\order{\log J}
, which is valid for generic even
p
.

II.2
Error and complexity of MPF and the problem of the existing analysis

The error bound of MPF determines its complexity, i.e., the Trotter number as well as the Trotterization.
For simulating the quantum dynamics over the large time
t
, we split it into
r
parts and set the small time
\tau=t/r
.
The Trotter number
r
is determined so that the error per step can be bounded by

\norm{e^{-iHt}-\{M_{pmJ}(t/r)\}^r}\leq\varepsilon.

(25)

In the LCU for implementing MPF, we call a controlled Trotterization operator,

C[T_{p}(t/(rk_{j}))]=\ket{0}\bra{0}\otimes I+\ket{1}\bra{1}\otimes T_{p}(t/(rk_{j})).

(26)

The number of queries to
C[T_{p}(\tau)]
with some time
\tau
in the algorithm amounts to
\order{\norm{c}_1 \norm{k}_1 r}
, where
\norm{k}_{1}
means the repetition number in each step
t/r
and the factor
\norm{c}_{1}
comes from the quantum amplitude amplification (QAA)
Brassard et al. 2002
to execute the LCU approach with certainty.
Using the well-conditioned solution, Eq. (
24
), MPF has been shown to have polylogarithmic query complexity in the inverse allowable error
1/\varepsilon
based on the order condition Eq. (
10
)
Low et al. 2019
.

By contrast, the efficiency in the system size
N
is rather complicated since we have to determine the size dependence of the error term
\order{\tau^{m+1}}
in Eq. (
10
).
Ref.
Low et al. 2019
showed that the error bound can be expressed by the nested commutators among
\{H_{\gamma}\}
, but its explicit form was not determined.
Ref.
Zhuk et al. 2024
derived the commutator-scaling error of MPF, but it was restricted to the case
J=p+1
and not applicable to the well-conditioned MPF.
Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
gave the following error bound applicable to generic well-conditioned MPFs.

Theorem 1
.

(Theorem in Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
)

Suppose that the time
\tau
is small enough to satisfy

^{\exists}q_{0}>p,\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}\tau^{q}<1,\quad^{\forall}q\geq q_{0}.

(27)

Then, the error of MPF is bounded by

\norm{e^{-iH\tau} - M_{pmJ}(\tau)}\leq\order{\norm{c}_1 (\mu_{p,m} \tau)^{m+1}},

(28)

where the factor
\mu_{p,m}
is characterized by the nested commutators as

\mu_{p,m}\equiv\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,n\in\mathbb{N}:\\
m+1\leq q,\\
n\leq\lfloor(q-1)/p\rfloor\end{subarray}}\left\{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}:\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q_{n^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+n-1}}\right\}.

(29)

Based on this error bound, Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
has evaluated the quantity
\mu_{p,m}
as
\mu_{p,m}\in\order{N^{\frac 1{p+1}} g}
and obtained that the query complexity in the controlled Trotterization
C[T_{p}(t/rk_{j})]
amounts to

\norm{k}_{1}\norm{c}_{1}r\in\order{N^{\frac 1{p+1}}gt \times\mr{polylog} \left( \frac{Ngt}{\varepsilon}\right)}.

(30)

The scaling in the system size
N
is as large as the one for Trotterization exploiting the commutator scaling,
\order{N^{\frac 1p}}
, and hence this seems to settle the problem on the commutator scaling in MPF.

However, we point out that, although Theorem
1
itself provides a correct upper bound of the MPF error, it is insufficient to guarantee the favorable system-size dependence of the computational cost Eq. (
30
) (While we omit the factor
1/q^{2}
in Eq. (
27
), it does not change the problem below).
The problem is that the nested commutators
\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}
with infinitely large orders
q
are involved in the theorem, concretely in Eqs. (
27
) and (
29
).
This forces the complexity to obey the
1
-norm scaling, giving no benefit from the locality of the Hamiltonian.
Let us first consider the condition Eq. (
27
).
In order to apply the error bound Eq. (
28
) to each Trotter step, it is demanded that the time
\tau=t/r
should satisfy
t/r\leq(\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q})^{-1/q}
for arbitrarily large
q
.
If we use the bound on the nested commutators from the locality, Eq. (
8
), the fact
(q!)^{-1/q}\to 0
leads to a meaningless result that only
\tau=t/r=0
is allowed (i.e., the Trotter number
r
should be infinite).
On the other hand, we can instead use another bound,

\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}\leq 2^{q}\left(\sum_{\gamma=1}^{\Gamma}\sum_{X}\norm{h_X^\gamma}\right)^{q}\in\order{(Ng)^q},

(31)

which gives a meaningful Trotter number called the
1
-norm scaling,
r\in\Omega(Ngt)
.
However, it prohibits the favorable system-size dependency like Eq. (
30
).
This reflects the fact that the locality plays a central role only in
q
-fold nested commutators with small
q
; while many commutators among disjoint local terms vanish, each term in nested commutators gradually becomes non-local with the spread of the interactions, which provides the factor
q!
in Eq. (
8
).
When the order
q
becomes large enough for the interactions to cover the whole system, such vanishment no longer takes place and Eq. (
8
) provides a bound approximately as large as the
1
-norm scaling, Eq. (
31
).
A similar problem takes place in the quantity
\mu_{p,m}
by Eq. (
29
).
By substituting
n=1
, Eq. (
29
) says that the quantity
\mu_{p,m}
is at least larger than
(\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q})^{1/q}
for any
q\geq m+1
.
The bound on the nested commutators by Eq. (
8
) cannot give any meaningful bound on
\mu_{p,m}
due to the divergence of
(q!)^{1/q}
again.
The scaling
\mu_{p,m}\in\order{N^{\frac 1{p+1}}g}
obtained in Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
should be replaced by
\mu_{p,m}\in\order{Ng}
, which is obtained based on Eq. (
31
).
This also prevents the error bound Eq. (
28
) from concluding the scaling Eq. (
30
).
In Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
, the factor
q!
in Eq. (
8
), which is often omitted from the scaling in the order-
\order{1}
Trotterizarion, is missing, and this mistakenly leads to the conclusion that Theorem
1
implies the complexity Eq. (
30
).
Therefore, it is still an open problem whether or not the MPF can achieve the favorable system-size dependency like Eq. (
30
), i.e., whether the MPF can enjoy the benefit from the commutator scaling in Trotterization.

The above problem in Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
arises from the necessity of convergence of the BCH formula,
e^{A}e^{B}=e^{A+B+[A,B]+\cdots}
.
It allows us to easily compute the powers of Trotterization included in the MPF as

\{T_{p}(\tau/k_{j})\}^{k_{j}}=\exp\left(-iH\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{\infty}\Phi_{q}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\right),

(32)

where an operator
\Phi_{q}
is composed of the nested commutators [See Eq. (
39
) for the explicit formula].
The upper bound on
\norm{\Phi_q}
is associated with
\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}
by
\norm{\Phi_q}\leq(c_{p})^{q}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}/q^{2}

Aftab et al. 2024
, and this is why the error bound includes the nested commutators with sufficiently large
q
.
The BCH expansion should converge for the use in the calculation even when we use its truncation in general.
This leads to the problematic requirement on the time, Eq. (
27
), which corresponds to the convergence radius
\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
.

II.3
Summary of our results

In this section, we summarize our results properly giving the complexity of MPF exploiting the commutator scaling, and highlight the change from the existing analysis
Aftab et al. 2024
.
We first prove the following error bound of the MPF.

Theorem 2
.

(Our error bound of MPF, informal)

When the time
\tau
is small enough to satisfy

\tau\in\order{\frac{1}{\{N^{\frac 1{p+1}}+\log(N/\epsilon)\}g}}

(33)

for an arbitrary fixed value
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the error of MPF is bounded by

\norm{e^{-iH\tau}-M_{pmJ}(\tau)}\in\order{\norm{c}_1 (\mu_{p,m}[p_0(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{m+1}+\norm{c}_1 \norm{k}_1 \epsilon}.

(34)

The quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]
is defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,n\in\mathbb{N}:\\
m+1\leq q,\\
n\leq\lfloor(q-1)/p\rfloor\end{subarray}}\left\{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}:\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q_{n^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+n-1}}\right\}

(35)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\order{\{N^{\frac 1{p+1}} + \log(N/\epsilon)\} g},

(36)

where
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)=\lceil\log(3N/\epsilon)\rceil
denotes the truncation order.

There are two significant differences from Theorem
1
.
The first one is the looseness of the requirement on the time
\tau
.
The time
\tau
characterized by Eq. (
33
) can be outside of the convergence radius
\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
.
Although the BCH formula seems to be no longer available, we resolve this problem by the asymptotic convergence under the locality, which was observed in Floquet-Magnus expansions
Kuwahara et al. 2016
.
While the requirement Eq. (
27
) forces each Trotter step
t/r
to be the one corresponding to the
1
-norm scaling, Eq. (
33
) allows it to be the one for the commutator scaling.
The second difference is the truncation order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\in\order{\log(N/\epsilon)}
for the nested commutators involved in the error bound, Eqs. (
34
) and (
35
).
This prevents the divergent behavior of the upper bound by Eq. (
8
), which properly reflects the locality of the Hamiltonian, and gives the meaningful bound on the quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]
, Eq. (
36
).

Based on Theorem
2
, we derive the computational cost of Hamiltonian simulation via MPF, which properly reflects the commutator scaling error.
We use the error bound Eq. (
34
) with setting
\epsilon\in\order{\varepsilon/ (\norm{c}_1 \norm{k}_1 r)}
to achieve the allowable error
\varepsilon
.
When there is a set of well-conditioned solution for
\{c_{j}\}
and
\{k_{j}\}
satisfying Eq. (
24
), we prove that query complexity in the controlled Trotterization
C[T_{p}(t/rk_{j})]
amounts to

\norm{k}_{1}\norm{c}_{1}r\in\order{ \left\{ N^{\frac 1{p+1}} + (\log(Ngt/\varepsilon))^2 \right\}gt \times\mr{polylog} \left( \frac{Ngt}{\varepsilon}\right)}.

(37)

It differs from Eq. (
30
), predicted by Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
, by the polylogarithmic factors in
Ngt/\varepsilon
due to the truncation order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
.
Anyway, our error bound in Theorem
2
ensures that the cost of the MPF has a favorable system-size dependency by the commutator scaling like Trotterization and also has polylogarithmic dependency in accuracy like LCU and QSVT
Low and Chuang 2017
;
Low and Chuang 2019
;
Gilyén et al. 2019
(See Table
1
).

III
Modified error bound and complexity of MPF

In this section, we provide the modified theorems for the commutator scaling in MPF, which is valid for predicting better system size dependency of the error bound and the complexity.
The problem of the original proof
Aftab et al. 2024
comes from the fact that the infinite series in the BCH formula or equivalently the Magnus expansion should be convergent.
Its convergence radius inversely proportional to the norm of the exponents demands that the time
\tau
should satisfy
\tau\in\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
, and then the cost obeying the
1
-norm scaling is inevitable
Blanes et al. 2009
;
Watson and Watkins 2024
.
We expect that the convergence radius cannot generally be improved even in the presence of the locality of Hamiltonians since the divergence is associated with thermalization to trivial states under local time-periodic Hamiltonians and it is observed for generic nonintegrable systems
Lazarides et al. 2014
;
D’Alessio and Rigol 2014
;
Kuwahara et al. 2016
.
The convergence of the BCH formula seems to be incompatible with the good system-size dependence of the error and the cost reflecting the commutator scaling.

Our strategy is to derive the error bound and the cost without relying on the convergence of the BCH formula.
We show that, under the condition much looser than the convergence radius,
\tau\in\order{(g \log(N/\epsilon))^{-1}}
, Trotterization can be approximated by the truncated BCH formula up to the order
p_{0}\in\order{\log(N/\epsilon)}
within an arbitrarily small error
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
This is reminiscent of the asymptotic convergence of the Floquet-Magnus expansion for predicting prethermalization under time-periodic Hamiltonians
Abanin et al. 2015
;
Kuwahara et al. 2016
;
Mori et al. 2016
;
Abanin et al. 2017b
;
Abanin et al. 2017a
;
Sharma and Tran 2024
.
Owing to the truncation order
p_{0}
, the divergence of the factor
q!
in Eq. (
8
) can be circumvented, and we can obtain a meaningful commutator-scaling error of MPF, in which it is sufficient to consider
q
such that
p<q<p_{0}
for Eqs. (
34
) and (
35
).
As a result, we clarify that the better system-size dependence owing to the commutator-scaling is present also in MPF, while the exact scaling deviates from the one predicted by Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
by a logarithmic quantity.

In Section
III.1
, we provide the correct commutator-scaling error of MPF.
While we leave some technical parts of the proof in Appendices
A
and
B
, we show that the nested commutators up to a certain truncation order for the BCH formula are relevant to the MPF error.
In Section
III.3
, we derive the Trotter step of MPF for generic local Hamiltonians, which correctly reflects the commutator scaling.

III.1
Asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula for Trotterization

Here, we prove the asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula for Trotterization, which resolves the strong requirement in Theorem
1
.
The BCH formula is an infinite series that gives Trotterization
T_{p}(\tau)
by

T_{p}(\tau)=\exp\left(-iH\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{\infty}\Phi_{q}\tau^{q}\right)

(38)

for sufficiently small time
\tau
within the convergence radius.
The operator
\Phi_{q}
is defined by

\displaystyle\Phi_{q}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\cdots,q_{c_{p}\Gamma}\geq 0\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{c_{p}\Gamma}=q\end{subarray}}(-i)^{q-1}\left(\prod_{v=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\frac{(\alpha_{v})^{q_{v}}}{q_{v}!}\right)\phi_{q}(\underbrace{H_{1},\cdots,H_{1}}_{q_{1}},\underbrace{H_{2},\cdots,H_{2}}_{q_{2}},\cdots,\underbrace{H_{\Gamma_{p}},\cdots,H_{\Gamma_{p}}}_{q_{\Gamma_{p}}}),

(39)

\displaystyle\phi_{q}(H_{1},\cdots,H_{q})=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{q}}\frac{(-1)^{d_{\sigma}}}{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q-1\\
d_{\sigma}\end{array}\right)}[H_{\sigma(1)},[H_{\sigma(2)},\cdots,[H_{\sigma(q-1)},H_{\sigma(q)}]]],

where
S_{q}
means the set of permutations of
\{1,\cdots,q\}
and the number
d_{\sigma}
denotes the number of adjacent pairs
(\sigma(i),\sigma(i+1))
such that
\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)

Arnal et al. 2020
.
Each coefficient
\Phi_{q}
is equal to
0
for
q=2,\cdots,p
from the order condition
T_{p}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{p+1}}
, and bounded by

\norm{\Phi_q}\leq\frac{(c_{p})^{q}}{q^{2}}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}

(43)

for any
q\geq p+1

Watson and Watkins 2024
.
Although the BCH formula allows us to easily compute the power of Trotterization
\{T_{p}(\tau/k_{j})\}^{k_{j}}
in MPF, its convergence demands a strong limitation
\norm{\Phi_q}\tau^{q}<1
for arbitrarily large
q
, which results in Eq. (
27
).
As discussed in Section
II
, Eq. (
8
) is not useful for ensuring the convergence due to the factor
q!
.
In general,
\tau\in\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
from Eq. (
31
) is a known sufficient condition for the convergence, which results in the
1
-norm scaling instead of the meaningful commutator scaling.

To resolve this problem, we prove the following theorem on the asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula for Trotterization.
It states that the BCH formula truncated at a certain order is enough to approximate
T_{p}(\tau)
with arbitrarily small error under the much looser requirement on time, Eq. (
33
).
As opposed to the intuition that the BCH formula, or its truncated formula, is no longer valid for the time out of the convergence radius
t\in\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
, the locality of the Hamiltonian enables us to provide a convergent behavior up to the truncation order as follows.

Theorem 3
.

(Asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula for Trotterization)

We set the truncation order of the BCH formula by

p_{0}(N,\epsilon)=\lceil\log(3N/\epsilon)\rceil\in\order{\log(N/\epsilon)}

(44)

for a given arbitrary value
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.
Suppose that the time
\tau>0
is small enough to satisfy

|\tau|\leq\frac{1}{8e^{3}c_{p}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)kg}\in\order{\frac{1}{g \log(N/\epsilon)}}.

(45)

Then, the truncated BCH formula approximates Trotterization
T_{p}(\tau)
by

\norm{T_p(\tau)-\exp\left( -iH\tau- i \sum_{q = 2}^{p_0(N,\epsilon)} \Phi_q \tau^q \right)}\leq\epsilon.

(46)

Proof.—

This proof is parallel to the one for the asymptotic convergence of the Floquet-Magnus expansion of time-periodic Hamiltonians
Kuwahara et al. 2016
.
For the Hamiltonian term
H_{\gamma}=\sum_{X\subset\Lambda}h_{X}^{\gamma}
on the lattice
\Lambda=\{1,\cdots,N\}
, we define subsystem Hamiltonians by

H_{\gamma}^{>i}=\sum_{j>i}h_{j}^{\gamma},\quad h_{i}^{\gamma}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X:X\ni i,\\
X\cap\{1,\cdots,i-1\}=\phi\end{subarray}}h_{X}^{\gamma}

(47)

for each site index
i\in\{0\}\cup\Lambda
.
Each Hamiltonian
H_{\gamma}^{>i}
means the collection of interaction terms acting on the sites
\{i+1,\cdots,N\}
, and hence we have
H_{\gamma}^{>0}=H_{\gamma}
and
H_{\gamma}^{>N}=0
.
We also define the subsystem Trotterization by

T_{p}^{>i}(\tau)=\prod_{v=1,\cdots,c_{p}\Gamma}^{\leftarrow}e^{-iH_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}\alpha_{v}\tau},

(48)

which corresponds to a collection of operators acting on the sites
\{i+1,\cdots,N\}
in
T_{p}(\tau)
[See Eq. (
6
)].
Trotterization
T_{p}(\tau)
is trivially decomposed into

T_{p}(\tau)=T^{>1}_{p}(\tau)\left(T^{>1}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>0}_{p}(\tau)\right)=\cdots=\prod_{i=1,\cdots,N}^{\leftarrow}\left(T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)\right),

(49)

where we used the relation
T^{>N}_{p}(\tau)=I
.
We organize a similar decomposition for the truncated BCH formula.
Using a certain truncation order
p_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the operators

\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)=\exp\left(-iH^{>i}\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\Phi_{q}^{>i}\tau^{q}\right),

(50)

where
\Phi_{q}^{>i}
is obtained by replacing
H_{\gamma}
with
H_{\gamma}^{>i}
in
\Phi_{q}
[See Eq. (
39
)].
It satisfies the order condition
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)=\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)+\order{\tau^{p_0+1}}
.
We decompose the truncated BCH formula by

\exp\left(-iH\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\Phi_{q}\tau^{q}\right)=\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>0}(\tau)=\prod_{i=1,\cdots,N}^{\leftarrow}\left(\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)\right).

(51)

in a similar manner to Eq. (
49
).

Using the triangular inequality, the error of interest is bounded by

\displaystyle\norm{T_p(\tau) - \exp\left( -i H \tau- i \sum_{q=2}^{p_0} \Phi_q \tau^q \right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\norm{ T^{>i}_p(\tau)^\dagger T^{>i-1}_p(\tau) - \tilde{T}_{p,p_0}^{>i}(\tau)^\dagger\tilde{T}_{p,p_0}^{>i-1}(\tau)}

(52)

based on the decompositions, Eqs. (
49
) and (
51
).
Each of the operators
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger},T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau),\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger},\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)
is analytic in time
\tau
, and we can compose of the series expansions,

T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n}^{i}\tau^{n},\quad\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\tau^{n}.

(53)

Owing to the order condition,
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)=\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)+\order{\tau^{p_0+1}}
, we have

\norm{T_p(\tau) - \exp\left( -i H \tau- i \sum_{q=2}^{p_0} \Phi_q \tau^q \right)}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{n=p_{0}+1}^{\infty}(\|T_{n}^{i}\|+\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|)\tau^{n}.

(54)

Therefore, it is sufficient to obtain the bounds on the coefficients
\norm{T_n^i}
and
\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|
.
We prove the following inequalities,

\norm{T_n^i}\leq(4c_{p}kg)^{n},\quad\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|\leq\frac{e^{2}}{2}(8e^{2}c_{p}p_{0}kg)^{n},

(55)

respectively as Lemmas
9
and
10
in Appendix
B
.
We describe the proof in the same appendix due to its technicality.
These bounds come from the
k
-locality and
g
-extensiveness of the Hamiltonian, which is intuitively explained as follows.
Since
T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
differs from
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)
only around the site
i
, many terms distant from it cancel with one another in the operator
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
.
It effectively forms the structure of causal cones and evolves only the sites around
i
with the energy scale
\order{g}
.
This is why the order-
n
coefficient
\norm{T_n^i}
is proportional to
g^{n}
independent of the size
N
as Eq. (
55
).
Similar discussion goes also for the operator
\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)
.
The effective Hamiltonian for the truncated BCH formula,
H+\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\Phi_{q}\tau^{q-1}
, is still a local and extensive operator, while the nested commutators there increase the locality and extensiveness (See Lemma
8
in Appendix
A
).
The operator
\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)
has nontrivial actions around the site
i
as well, which results in
\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|\in\order{(p_0 g)^n}
.
The additional factor
(p_{0})^{n}
reflects the increase in the locality and extensiveness via the nested commutators.
We consider the small time
\tau
satisfying Eq. (
45
) with setting the truncation order
p_{0}=p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
as Eq. (
44
).
Then, the inequality Eq. (
54
) implies

\displaystyle\norm{T_p(\tau) - \exp\left( -i H \tau- i \sum_{q=2}^{p_0(N,\epsilon)} \Phi_q \tau^q \right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\sum_{n=p_{0}(N,\epsilon)+1}^{\infty}\left((4c_{p}kg\tau)^{n}+\frac{e^{2}}{2}(8e^{2}c_{p}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)kg\tau)^{n}\right)

(56)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\sum_{n=p_{0}(N,\epsilon)+1}^{\infty}\left(e^{-n}+\frac{1}{2}e^{-n+2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 3Ne^{-p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}\leq\epsilon.\qquad\square

Theorem
3
reflects the asymptotic convergence of Trotterization up to the intermediate order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
.
The time
\tau
by Eq. (
45
) can be out of the convergence radius
\order{(Ng)^{-1}}
.
On the other hand, the order-
q
coefficient of the BCH formula is bounded by

\norm{\Phi_q \tau^q}\leq\frac{\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}\tau^{q}}{q^{2}}\leq(2qkg\tau)^{q}N,

(57)

where we used the bound Eq. (
8
).
It exponentially decays at least up to
q\in\order{p_0}
owing to
2qkg\tau<1
while it can diverge for larger orders
q
, reflecting the divergence of the BCH formula.
Theorem
3
states that the truncated BCH formula convergent up to the order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
can approximate Trotterization
T_{p}(\tau)
with a desirable error
\epsilon
.

While we borrow the proof idea from the Floquet-Magnus expansion for generic time-periodic Hamiltonians
Kuwahara et al. 2016
, we note that Theorem
3
gives a better bound specifically reflecting the form of Trotterization and the BCH formula via Lemmas
9
and
10
.
This improvement is essential for the present error analysis to be available to MPF for generic local Hamiltonians including long-ranged interacting cases (See Appendix
B.3
for the details).

III.2
Error bound of MPF with commutator scaling

Next, we prove the error bound of MPF, Theorem
2
.
The proof is done in the following two steps based on the truncated BCH formula in Theorem
3
.
First, we derive the error bound expressed by the nested commutators up to the truncation order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
in Theorem
4
.
Next, we show the scaling of the quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]
, which characterizes the error bound by Eq. (
35
), in Lemma
5
.
The first result is described as follows.

Theorem 4
.

For a
k
-local and
g
-extensive Hamiltonian
H
, we define a value,

\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}]\equiv\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}q,n\in\mathbb{N}:\\
m+1\leq q,\\
n\leq\lfloor(q-1)/p\rfloor\end{subarray}}\left\{\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q_{n^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q+n-1}}\right\},

(58)

which is determined by the nested commutators
\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q}
by Eq. (
8
).
When the time
\tau
is small enough to satisfy

|\tau|\leq\min\left(\frac{1}{8e^{3}c_{p}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)kg},\frac{1}{2c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]}\right),

(59)

for an arbitrary value
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, the error of MPF is bounded by

\norm{e^{-iH\tau} - M_{pmJ}(\tau)}\leq 2e^{1/2}\norm{c}_{1}\left\{c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau\right\}^{m+1}+\norm{c}_{1}\norm{k}_{1}\epsilon.

(60)

The truncation number
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)
is given by Eq. (
44
).

Proof.—
This proof is parallel to the original one in Ref.
Kuwahara et al. 2016
.
Using the Dyson series expansion,

e^{A+B}=e^{A}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{n-1}}\differential s_{n}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1,\cdots,n}^{\rightarrow}\left(e^{-As_{n^{\prime}}}Be^{As_{n^{\prime}}}\right),

(61)

with
A=-iH\tau
and
B=-i\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\Phi_{q}\tau^{q}/(k_{j})^{q-1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\exp\left(-iH\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}\Phi_{q}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\right)-e^{-iH\tau}

\displaystyle\quad=e^{-iH\tau}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{n-1}}\differential s_{n}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1,\cdots,n}^{\rightarrow}\left(-ie^{is_{n^{\prime}}\tau\mathrm{ad}_{H}}\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}\Phi_{q}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=e^{-iH\tau}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{q=pn+1}^{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)n+1}\frac{\tau^{q+n-1}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{n-1}}\differential s_{n}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1,\cdots,n}^{\rightarrow}\left(-ie^{is_{n^{\prime}}\tau\mathrm{ad}_{H}}\Phi_{q_{n^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=e^{-iH\tau}\sum_{q=p+1}^{\infty}\frac{\tau^{q}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\sum_{n=\lceil\frac{q-1}{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{q-1}{p}\rfloor}\tau^{n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{n-1}}\differential s_{n}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1,\cdots,n}^{\rightarrow}\left(-ie^{is_{n^{\prime}}\tau\mathrm{ad}_{H}}\Phi_{q_{n^{\prime}}}\right).

We use
\Phi_{q}=0
for
q=2,\cdots,p
from the order condition
T_{p}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{p+1}}
in the second inequality.
When we choose
\{c_{j}\}
and
\{k_{j}\}
satisfying the order condition Eq. (
10
), the terms with
q\leq m
vanish.
As a result, we arrive at

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{j=1}^J c_j e^{-iH\tau- i \sum_{q = 2}^{p_0(N,\epsilon)} \frac{\Phi_q \tau^q}{(k_j)^{q-1}} } - e^{-iH\tau}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{J}|c_{j}|\sum_{q=m+1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\lfloor\frac{q-1}{p}\rfloor}\frac{\tau^{q+n-1}}{(k_{j})^{q-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n!}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\norm{\Phi_{q_{n'}}}

(63)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\norm{c}_{1}\sum_{q=m+1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{q+n-1}}{n!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\norm{c}_{1}\sum_{q=m+1}^{\infty}(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{q}e^{c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{1/2}\norm{c}_{1}(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{m+1}

We used the definition of the quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}]
by Eq. (
58
) and the bound on
\norm{\Phi_q}
by Eq. (
43
) in the second inequality.
We also used the condition on the time
\tau
by Eq. (
59
) in the third and fourth inequalities.
The error bound of MPF results can be evaluated by the triangle inequality, which results in

\displaystyle\norm{e^{-iH\tau}-M_{pmJ}(\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{1/2}\norm{c}_{1}(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{m+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}|c_{j}|\norm{\{T_p(\tau/k_j)\}^{k_j} - e^{-iH\tau-i\sum_{q=2}^{p_0(N,\epsilon)} \Phi_q \tau^q/(k_j)^{q-1}}}

(64)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{1/2}\norm{c}_{1}(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{m+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{J}|c_{j}|\cdot|k_{j}|\norm{T_p(\tau/k_j)-e^{-iH\tau/k_j -i \sum_{q=2}^{p_0(N,\epsilon)} \Phi_q (\tau/k_j)^q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2e^{1/2}\norm{c}_{1}(c_{p}\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\tau)^{m+1}+\norm{c}_{1}\norm{k}_{1}\epsilon,

where we used the asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula by Theorem
3
in the last inequality.
\quad\square

The difference from the original statement in Ref.
Aftab et al. 2024
(i.e., Theorem
1
) is the presence of the truncation order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\in\order{\log(N/\epsilon)}
in Eq. (
58
).
This brings a meaningful bound reflecting the commutator scaling on the quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]
.

Lemma 5
.

The quantity
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]
, which is defined by Eq. (
58
), is bounded by

\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}(N,\epsilon)]\leq 4\max\left((p+1)N^{\frac{1}{p+1}},e^{3}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\right)kg\in\order{\left\{N^{\frac 1{p+1}}+\log(N/\epsilon) \right\} g},

(65)

for a value
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Proof.—

Using the upper bound on the nested commutator by Eq. (
8
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q_{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\left\{(q_{n^{\prime}}-1)!(2kg)^{q_{n^{\prime}}-1}Ng\right\}

(66)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}2^{-n}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}(2q_{n^{\prime}}N^{1/q_{n^{\prime}}}kg)^{q_{n^{\prime}}}.

The function
xN^{1/x}
is monotonically decreasing in
0<x<\log N
and increasing in
\log N<x
, which results in

\displaystyle\max_{p+1\leq q_{n^{\prime}}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}\left(q_{n^{\prime}}N^{1/q_{n^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\left((p+1)N^{\frac{1}{p+1}},p_{0}(N,\epsilon)N^{\frac{1}{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}}\right)

(67)

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\order{N^\frac{1}{p+1} + p_0(N,\epsilon)}.

We note that the factor
N^{\frac{1}{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}}
is bounded by a constant as
N^{\frac{1}{p_{0}(N,\epsilon)}}\leq N^{\frac{1}{\log(3N/\epsilon)}}=e^{1-\frac{\log(3/\epsilon)}{\log N+\log(3/\epsilon)}}<e^{3}
.
Substituting this result into Eq. (
66
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+1\leq q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\leq p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\alpha_{\mathrm{com},q_{n^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{-n}\left\{\max\left((p+1)N^{\frac{1}{p+1}},e^{3}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\right)kg\right\}^{q+n-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\cdots,q_{n}\geq 0:\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{n}=q+n-1\end{subarray}}1

(68)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\{4\max\left((p+1)N^{\frac{1}{p+1}},e^{3}p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\right)kg\right\}^{q+n-1}.

The summation in the right-hand side of the first line is equal to
(q+2n-2)!/(n-1)!(q+n-1)!
, which is bounded by
2^{q+2n-2}
.
This completes the proof.
\quad\square

Theorem
2
immediately follows from the combination of Theorem
4
and this lemma.
Using the scaling of
\mu_{p,m}[p_{0}]
by Eq. (
65
), the requirement on the time
\tau

IV
Discussion and conclusion

IV.1
Application to other quantum algorithms

Recently, there have been various quantum algorithms trying to achieve better cost both in the size and accuracy like MPF
Endo et al. 2019
;
Faehrmann et al. 2022
;
Watson and Watkins 2024
;
Rendon 2025
;
Rendon et al. 2024
;
Watson 2025
;
Chakraborty et al. 2025
;
Guo et al. 2025
, such as the sampling-based approach instead of relying on LCU
Faehrmann et al. 2022
.
In particular, the mitigation of Trotter errors with polynomial interpolation or Richardson extrapolation has attracted much interest
Endo et al. 2019
, exemplified by an algorithm for time-evolved observables
Watson and Watkins 2024
and those for generic tasks including quantum signal processing
Rendon 2025
;
Rendon et al. 2024
;
Chakraborty et al. 2025
;
Guo et al. 2025
.
These algorithms are expected to have favorable cost with respect to the system size owing to the commutator scaling, and indeed some of them
Watson and Watkins 2024
;
Chakraborty et al. 2025
have derived their error and cost expressed by nested commutators with the same strategy as the original proof for MPF
Aftab et al. 2024
.
The regimes where the time at each step is small enough for the BCH formula to converge are also assumed in Refs.
Rendon 2025
;
Rendon et al. 2024
;
Guo et al. 2025
.
As discussed in Section
II.2
, error bounds expressed by nested commutators do not necessarily imply the cost reflecting the commutator scaling in such regimes.
Our complete analysis for MPF will give improved or accurate cost precisely reflecting the commutator scaling for the family of quantum algorithms using interpolation or extrapolation of Trotterization.

Another important target is the MPF extended to time-dependent systems
Watkins et al. 2024
;
Mizuta et al. 2024
;
Cao et al. 2024
.
In particular, Ref.
Mizuta et al. 2024
has recently derived the commutator-scaling error in the time-dependent MPF, but it relies on the convergence of the BCH formula as well.
In contrast to time-independent cases, the error analysis involves product formulas composed of unbounded Hamiltonians.
However, the asymptotic convergence of the BCH formula is relevant to the nested commutators rather than the Hamiltonian itself.
We expect that our technique also applies to the time-dependent MPF and will correctly provide the near-optimal cost brought by the commutator scaling.

IV.2
Discussion and Summary

In this paper, we discuss the problem of the commutator-scaling error of the MPF.
While it seems to have been resolved by the previous result by Theorem
1

Aftab et al. 2024
, we point out that it is incompatible with the cost reflecting the commutator scaling.
We derive the alternative error bound of MPF, Theorem
2
, which has a much looser requirement and a better error bound having the truncation order.
Consequently, we prove the query complexity proportional to
\tilde{O}(N^{\frac{1}{p+1}})
, matching with the commutator scaling, while keeping the polylogarithmic dependence in
1/\varepsilon
.
We expect that our error bound does not seem to be still an optimal one for the generic order MPF; although it is natural to expect that the order-
m
MPF
M_{pmJ}(\tau)=e^{-iH\tau}+\order{\tau^{m+1}}
involves at most
(m+1)
-fold nested commutators in its error, our truncation order
p_{0}(N,\epsilon)\in\Theta(\log(N/\epsilon))
can be independently large.
On the other hand, when focusing on Hamiltonian simulation with the well-conditioned MPF, the order
m\in\Theta(\log(Ngt/\varepsilon))
[See Eq. (
81
)] matches the truncation order only by a constant factor, and our error bound seems to be an optimal one.
In any case, we conclude with our result that the MPF has good computational cost both in the system size and accuracy, inheriting the advantages respectively in Trotterization and LCU.

d_{\sigma}\end{array}\right)}\max_{\sigma\in S_{q}}\sum_{q^{\prime}=1}^{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}:\\
X_{q^{\prime}}\ni j\end{subarray}}\norm{[\tilde{h}_{X_{\sigma(1)}}^{\sigma(1)}, \cdots, [\tilde{h}_{X_{\sigma(q-1)}}^{\sigma(q-1)},\tilde{h}_{X_{\sigma(q)}}^{\sigma(q)}]]}.

In the above, each term
\tilde{h}_{X_{q^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}}
means one of the local terms
\{h_{X}\}
, which is defined so that the indices in the subscript and superscript match in the first line as follows,

\tilde{h}_{X_{q^{\prime}}}^{q^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}h_{X_{q^{\prime}}}^{1}&1\leq q^{\prime}\leq q_{1}\\
h_{X_{q^{\prime}}}^{2}&q_{1}<q^{\prime}\leq q_{1}+q_{2}\\
\vdots\\
h_{X_{q^{\prime}}}^{c_{p}\Gamma}&q-q_{c_{p}\Gamma}<q^{\prime}\leq q.\end{cases}

(96)

We then use the inequality,
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{q}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}q-1\\
d_{\sigma}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\leq q_{1}!\cdots q_{c_{p}\Gamma}!
under
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{c_{p}\Gamma}=q
.
We also note that each
\tilde{h}_{X_{\sigma(q^{\prime})}}^{\sigma(q^{\prime})}
reproduces
c_{p}
copies of
h_{X_{\sigma(q^{\prime})}}^{\gamma}
for each
\gamma
in the summation over
q_{1},\cdots,q_{c_{p}\Gamma}
with
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{c_{p}\Gamma}=q
, just like the commutator-scaling error in Trotterization
Childs et al. 2021
.
Thus, the extensiveness of
\Phi_{q}
is further bounded by

\displaystyle g(\Phi_{q})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p})^{q}}{q^{2}}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{q}}\sum_{q^{\prime}=1}^{q}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}X_{1},\cdots,X_{q}:\\
X_{q^{\prime}}\ni j\end{subarray}}\max_{\sigma\in S_{q}}\norm{[h_{X_{\sigma(1)}}^{\gamma_{\sigma(1)}}, \cdots, [h_{X_{\sigma(q-1)}}^{\gamma_{\sigma(q-1)}},h_{X_{\sigma(q)}}^{\gamma_{\sigma(q)}}]]}

(97)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p})^{q}}{q^{2}}\sum_{q^{\prime}=1}^{q}\sum_{\gamma_{q^{\prime}}}\sum_{X_{q^{\prime}}\ni j}(q-1)!(2kg)^{q-1}\norm{h_{X_{q'}}^{\gamma_{q'}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(q-1)!}{q}(2c_{p}kg)^{q-1}c_{p}g.

The second inequality comes from the fact that the nested commutators in the first line share the same upper bound by Lemma
7
regardless of the permutation
\sigma\in S_{q}
, where we fix the domain
X_{q^{\prime}}
.

\quad\square

Appendix B
Series expansions of the subsystem Trotterization and BCH formula

Here, we prove Eq. (
55
) in the main text, which is used for the derivation of Theorem
3
.
They provide the upper bounds on the series expansions for the subsystem operators, and essentially arise from the locality and the extensiveness of Hamiltonians.

B.1
Series expansion of the subsystem Trotterization

We begin with proving the following bound about the subsystem Trotterization
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)
defined by Eq. (
48
).

Lemma 9
.

Let us expand the operator
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
by

T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}T_{n}\tau^{n},

(98)

where the subsystem Trotterization
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)
is defined by Eq. (
48
).
Then, we have

\norm{T_n}\leq(4c_{p}kg)^{n}.

(99)

Proof.—
Let
v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{l}
(
v_{1}<v_{2}<\cdots<v_{l}
) be the indices such that
h_{i}^{\gamma_{v}}\neq 0
, where
h_{i}^{\gamma}
is given by Eq. (
47
).
Since the layers with
v=v_{l}+1,\cdots,c_{p}\Gamma
are canceled, the operator
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
is expressed by

\displaystyle T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{v<v_{l}}^{\rightarrow}e^{iH_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}\alpha_{v}\tau}\right)e^{ih_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{l}}}\alpha_{v_{l}}\tau}\left(\prod_{v<v_{l}}^{\leftarrow}e^{-iH_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i-1}\alpha_{v}\tau}\right)

(100)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\left(\prod_{v<v_{l}}^{\rightarrow}e^{i\alpha_{v}\tau\mathrm{ad}_{H_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}}}\right)e^{ih_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{l}}}\alpha_{v_{l}}\tau}\right\}\left(\prod_{v<v_{l}}^{\rightarrow}e^{iH_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}\alpha_{v}\tau}\right)\left(\prod_{v<v_{l}}^{\leftarrow}e^{-iH_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i-1}\alpha_{v}\tau}\right).

In the first line, the terms other than
h_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{l}}}
in the
v_{l}
-th layer are absent due to their commutability from the construction of Trotterization.
The same discussion applies to the product of the last two terms.
Repeating this calculation, we obtain

T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)=\prod_{l^{\prime}=1,\cdots,l}^{\leftarrow}\left\{\left(\prod_{v<v_{l^{\prime}}}^{\rightarrow}e^{i\alpha_{v}\tau\mathrm{ad}_{H_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}}}\right)e^{ih_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{l^{\prime}}}}\alpha_{v_{l^{\prime}}}\tau}\right\}.

(101)

Let us define the following two time-dependent Hamiltonians for the time
\tau^{\prime}\in[0,c_{p}\Gamma\tau)
,

h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{l^{\prime}}h_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{l^{\prime}}}}&\text{if $\tau^{\prime}\in[(v_{l^{\prime}}-1)\tau,v_{l^{\prime}}\tau)$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases},\qquad H^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\alpha_{1}H_{\gamma_{1}}^{>i}&\text{if $\tau^{\prime}\in[0,\tau)$}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{v}H_{\gamma_{v}}^{>i}&\text{if $\tau^{\prime}\in[(v-1)\tau,v\tau)$}\\
\vdots\\
\alpha_{c_{p}\Gamma}H_{\gamma_{c_{p}\Gamma}}^{>i}&\text{if $\tau^{\prime}\in[(c_{p}\Gamma-1)\tau,c_{p}\Gamma\tau)$}\end{cases}.

(102)

Then, it is easy to check that Eq. (
101
) is equal to the time-evolution operator under the Hamiltonian
h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})
in the interaction picture by
H^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})
.
Namely, we have

T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)=\mathcal{T}\exp\left(i\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma\tau}\differential\tau^{\prime}U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})^{\dagger}h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})\right),\quad U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})\equiv\mathcal{T}\exp\left(-i\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}\differential\tau^{\prime\prime}H^{>i}(\tau^{\prime\prime})\right).

(103)

Here, we use the Dyson series expansion on the operator
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
and also on
U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})^{\dagger}h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})
in the time-ordered integral.
The latter one is expressed by

\displaystyle U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})^{\dagger}h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})U^{>i}(\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau^{\prime}}\differential\tau_{1}^{\prime}\cdots\int_{0}^{\tau_{n-1}^{\prime}}\differential\tau_{n}^{\prime}\left\{\prod_{n^{\prime}=1,\cdots,n}^{\rightarrow}\left(i\mathrm{ad}_{H^{>i}(\tau_{n^{\prime}})}\right)\right\}h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})

(104)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(i\tau^{\prime})^{n}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{(c_{p}\Gamma)^{-n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{n}\mathcal{T}_{\rightarrow}\left[\prod_{n^{\prime}=1}^{n}\left(\mathrm{ad}_{H^{>i}(s_{n^{\prime}}\tau^{\prime}/c_{p}\Gamma)}\right)\right]h_{i}(\tau^{\prime})}_{\Xi_{n}(\tau^{\prime})}.

The symbol
\mathcal{T}_{\rightarrow}[\cdot]
denotes the time-ordered product, in which the variables
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}
are aligned in the descending order.
The Dyson series expansion for
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
is calculated as follows,

\displaystyle T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}i^{j}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma\tau}\differential\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\differential\tau_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{\tau_{j-1}}\differential\tau_{j}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1,\cdots,j}^{\rightarrow}\left(\sum_{n_{j^{\prime}}=0}^{\infty}(i\tau_{j^{\prime}})^{n_{j^{\prime}}}\Xi_{n_{j^{\prime}}}(\tau_{j^{\prime}})\right)

(105)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}=0}^{\infty}\frac{(i\tau)^{j+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}}}{j!}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{j}\mathcal{T}_{\rightarrow}\left[\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}(s_{j^{\prime}})^{n_{j^{\prime}}}\Xi_{n_{j^{\prime}}}(s_{j^{\prime}}\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tau^{n}\cdot\underbrace{i^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}=n-j\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{j}\mathcal{T}_{\rightarrow}\left[\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}(s_{j^{\prime}})^{n_{j^{\prime}}}\Xi_{n_{j^{\prime}}}(s_{j^{\prime}}\tau)\right]}_{T_{n}}.

This gives the series expansion of the operator
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
in Eq. (
98
).
We can confirm that the coefficient
T_{n}
defined by Eq. (
105
) is independent of the time
\tau
in the following way.
The operator
T_{n}
appears to have the time-dependency in
H^{>i}(s_{n^{\prime}}s_{j^{\prime}}\tau/c_{p}\Gamma)
and
h_{i}(s_{j^{\prime}}\tau)
via
\Xi_{n_{j^{\prime}}}(s_{j^{\prime}}\tau)
[See Eq. (
104
)].
However, since they are piecewise constant modulo
\tau
as shown in Eq. (
102
), they can be replaced by the functions solely dependent on
s_{n^{\prime}}s_{j^{\prime}}/c_{p}\Gamma
and
s_{j^{\prime}}
respectively.
This results in the
\tau
-independence of
T_{n}
, which ensures that Eq. (
105
) gives the series expansion of
T^{>i}_{p}(\tau)^{\dagger}T^{>i-1}_{p}(\tau)
.

We finally evaluate the upper bound on the coefficient
T_{n}
.
We begin with the upper bound on
\Xi_{n}(s\tau)
defined by Eq. (
104
).
For
s\in(0,c_{p}\Gamma)
, the Hamiltonian
H^{>i}(s_{n^{\prime}}s\tau/c_{p}\Gamma)
is piecewise constant within

(v_{n^{\prime}}-1)\Delta s\leq s_{n^{\prime}}<v_{n^{\prime}}\Delta s,\quad\Delta s\equiv\frac{c_{p}\Gamma}{s},

(106)

for each
v_{n^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{N}
from Eq. (
102
).
Each index
v_{n^{\prime}}
takes
1,2,\cdots,\lceil s\rceil
for
s_{n^{\prime}}\in(0,c_{p}\Gamma)
.
This property allows us to replace the integral in Eq. (
104
) by the summation as follows,

\displaystyle\norm{\Xi_n (s\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p}\Gamma)^{-n}}{n!}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{n}\norm{\mcl{T}_\rightarrow\left[ \prod_{n'=1}^n \left( \ad_{H^{>i}(s_{n'}s \tau/c_p\Gamma)}\right) \right] h_i(s\tau)}

(107)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p}\Gamma)^{-n}}{n!}\sum_{v_{1},v_{2},\cdots,v_{n}=1}^{\lceil s\rceil}\int_{(v_{1}-1)\Delta s}^{v_{1}\Delta s}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{(v_{n}-1)\Delta s}^{v_{n}\Delta s}\differential s_{n}\norm{\mcl{T}_\rightarrow\left[ \prod_{n'=1}^n \left( \alpha_{v_{n'}} \ad_{H^{>i}_{\gamma_{v_{n'}}}}\right) \right] h_i(s\tau)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p}\Gamma)^{-n}}{n!}(\Delta s)^{n}\sum_{v_{1}=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\cdots\sum_{v_{n}=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\norm{[\alpha_{v_1} H_{\gamma_{v_1}}^{>i},[\alpha_{v_2} H_{\gamma_{v_2}}^{>i},\cdots,[\alpha_{v_n} H_{\gamma_{v_n}}^{>i}, h_i(s\tau)]\cdots]]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(c_{p})^{n}}{n!s^{n}}\sum_{\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{n}=1}^{\Gamma}\norm{[ H_{\gamma_1}^{>i},[ H_{\gamma_2}^{>i},\cdots,[ H_{\gamma_n}^{>i}, h_i(s\tau)]\cdots]]}\leq\left(\frac{2c_{p}kg}{s}\right)^{n}\norm{h_i(s\tau)}.

The time-ordered product in the second line is about the integers
v_{1},\cdots,v_{n}
.
The fourth line comes from the fact that every index
\gamma\in\{1,\cdots,\Gamma\}
is repeated
c_{p}
times by
\gamma_{v}
when
v
runs over
v=1,\cdots,c_{p}\Gamma
by the definition of Trotterization, Eq. (
6
).
The last inequality comes from Lemma
7
.
Substituting this bound into Eq. (
105
), we obtain the upper bound on the coefficient
T_{n}
by

\displaystyle\norm{T_n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}=n-j\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{1}\cdots\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s_{j}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}\left\{(s_{j^{\prime}})^{n_{j^{\prime}}}\norm{\Xi_{n_{j'}}(s_{j'}\tau)}\right\}

(108)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{1}{j!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}=n-j\end{subarray}}\prod_{j^{\prime}=1}^{j}\left\{(2c_{p}kg)^{n_{j^{\prime}}}\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s\norm{h_i(s\tau)}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n}\frac{(2c_{p}kg)^{n-j}}{j!}(c_{p}g)^{j}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}\geq 0:\\
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}=n-j\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(4c_{p}kg)^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{-j}}{j!}=\frac{\sqrt{e}}{2}(4c_{p}kg)^{n}.

The second line comes from Eq. (
107
).
In the third line, we use

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\differential s\norm{h_i(s\tau)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}=1}^{l}|\alpha_{v_{j^{\prime}}}|\cdot\|h_{i}^{\gamma_{v_{j^{\prime}}}}\|

(109)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{v=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\sum_{X\subset\Lambda:X\ni i}\norm{h_X^{\gamma_v}}\leq c_{p}g

from Eqs. (
102
) and (
47
).
In the last inequality of Eq. (
108
), we use
\sum_{n_{1},\cdots,n_{j}:n_{1}+\cdots+n_{j}=n-j}1=\frac{(n-1)!}{(j-1)!(n-j)!}\leq 2^{n-1}
.
Finally, using the trivial relation
\sqrt{e}<2
, we arrive at Eq. (
99
).
\quad\square

B.2
Series expansion of the subsystem BCH formula

We next focus on the second inequality in Eq. (
55
), which is a counterpart of Lemma
9
for the subsystem BCH formula, Eq. (

We exploit the locality and extensiveness of the effective Hamiltonian

for the BCH expansion, represented by Lemma

.
As a result, we can prove the following lemma.

Lemma 10
.

Let
\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)
denote a subsystem truncated BCH formula,

\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)=\exp\left(-i\Phi^{>i}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]\right),\quad\Phi^{>i}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]=H^{>i}\tau+\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\Phi_{q}^{>i}\tau^{q},

(110)

where
\Phi_{q}^{>i}
is obtained by replacing
H_{\gamma}
by
H_{\gamma}^{>i}
in the BCH coefficient
\Phi_{q}
[See Eq. (
39
)] for the order-
p
Trotterization
T_{p}(\tau)
.
When we expand the operator
\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)
by

\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\tau^{n},

(111)

each coefficient is bounded by

\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|\leq\frac{e^{2}}{2}(8e^{2}c_{p}p_{0}kg)^{n}.

(112)

Proof.—
We define the difference
\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]\equiv\Phi^{>i}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]-\Phi^{>i-1}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]
.
Then, we use the relation by the Dyson series expansion, Eq. (
61
), for operators
A=-i\Phi_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}[\tau]
and
B=i\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]
.
This leads to

\displaystyle\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}(\tau)^{\dagger}\tilde{T}_{p,p_{0}}^{>i-1}(\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{i\Phi_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}[\tau]}e^{-i\Phi_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}[\tau]+i\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]}

(113)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\differential s_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{m-1}}\differential s_{m}\prod_{m^{\prime}=1,\cdots,m}^{\rightarrow}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(is_{m^{\prime}})^{l}}{l!}\left(\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}[\tau]}\right)^{l}i\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]\right).

We define the series expansion of the term in the integral,

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(is)^{l}}{l!}\left(\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi_{p,p_{0}}^{>i}[\tau]}\right)^{l}i\Psi^{i}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]=\sum_{q=0}^{\infty}\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(s)\tau^{q},

(114)

and then the coefficient
\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}
is expressed by

\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\differential s_{1}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\differential s_{2}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{m-1}}\differential s_{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\cdots,q_{m}\geq 0:\\
q_{1}+q_{m}=n\end{subarray}}\prod_{m^{\prime}=1,\cdots,m}^{\rightarrow}\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(s_{m^{\prime}}).

(115)

It suffices to evaluate the bound on
\|\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(s)\|
.

The difference
\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]=\Phi^{>i}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]-\Phi^{>i-1}_{p,p_{0}}[\tau]
is expanded by

\Psi_{p,p_{0}}^{i}[\tau]=\sum_{\gamma}h_{i}^{\gamma}\tau+\sum_{q=2}^{p_{0}}\left(\Phi_{q}^{>i}-\Phi_{q}^{>i-1}\right)\tau^{q}\equiv\sum_{q=1}^{p_{0}}\Psi_{q}^{i}\tau^{q}.

(116)

In a similar manner to Eq. (
115
), the order-
q
coefficient
\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(s)
in Eq. (
114
) is written by the products of
\mathrm{ad}_{\Phi_{q_{l^{\prime}}}^{>i}}
and
\Psi_{q^{\prime}}^{i}
satisfying

, which results in

\|\tilde{\Psi}_{q}(s)\|\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{l!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq q^{\prime},q_{1},\cdots,q_{l}\leq p_{0}:\\
q^{\prime}+q_{1}+\cdots+q_{l}=q\end{subarray}}\norm{\left(\prod_{l'=1}^l \ad_{\Phi_{q_{l'}}^{>i}} \right)\Psi_{q'}^i}.

(117)

Let us denote the decomposition of
\Phi_{q}^{>i}
into local terms by
\Phi_{q}^{>i}=\sum_{X}\phi_{X}^{q}
.
For
q^{\prime}=1
, the norm of the nested commutator in the above formula is bounded by

\displaystyle\norm{\left(\prod_{l'=1}^l \ad_{\Phi_{q_{l'}}^{>i}} \right)\Psi_{1}^i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{X:X\ni i}\sum_{\gamma}\sum_{X_{1},\cdots,X_{l}}\norm{[\phi_{X_l}^{q_l},\cdots,[\phi_{X_1}^{q_1},h_X^\gamma]]}

(118)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{l}\sum_{X:X\ni i}\sum_{\gamma}\norm{h_X^\gamma}\sum_{X_{1}:X_{1}\cap X\neq\phi}\sum_{X_{2}:X_{2}\cap(Y_{1}\cup X)\neq\phi}\cdots\sum_{X_{l}:X_{l}\cap(Y_{l-1}\cup X)\neq\phi}\prod_{l^{\prime}=1}^{l}\norm{\phi_{X_{l'}}^{q_{l'}}},

where
Y_{l^{\prime}}
denotes the union
Y_{l^{\prime}}=X_{1}\cup\cdots\cup X_{l^{\prime}}
like Eq. (
88
).
Using the locality and extensiveness of
\Phi_{q}
by Lemma
8
in a similar manner to Eq. (
89
), we obtain the upper bound,

\displaystyle\norm{\left(\prod_{l'=1}^l \ad_{\Phi_{q_{l'}}^{>i}} \right)\Psi_{1}^i}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{l}g\prod_{l^{\prime}=1}^{l}\left\{\left(\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}<l^{\prime}}q_{l^{\prime\prime}}k+k\right)g(\Phi_{q_{l}^{\prime}})\right\}

(119)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{l}g\prod_{l^{\prime}=1}^{l}\left\{qk\frac{(q_{l^{\prime}}-1)!}{q_{l^{\prime}}}(2c_{p}kg)^{q_{l^{\prime}}-1}c_{p}g\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle q^{l}g(2c_{p}p_{0}kg)^{q-1}\leq q^{l}(2c_{p}p_{0}kg)^{q}.

We used
\sum_{l^{\prime\prime}<l^{\prime}}q_{l}^{\prime\prime}+1\leq\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{l}q_{l^{\prime}}+q^{\prime}=q
in the second line and used
(q_{l^{\prime}}-1)!/q_{l^{\prime}}\leq p_{0}^{q_{l^{\prime}}}
in the third line, both of which are validated in the summation appearing in Eq. (
117
).

We next calculate the bounds of the terms with
q^{\prime}\geq 2
in Eq. (
117
).
The order-
q^{\prime}
term
\Psi_{q^{\prime}}^{i}=\Phi_{q^{\prime}}^{>i}-\Phi_{q^{\prime}}^{>i-1}
is expressed by

\displaystyle\Psi_{q^{\prime}}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\cdots,q_{c_{p}\Gamma}:\\
q_{1}+\cdots+q_{c_{p}\Gamma}=q^{\prime}\end{subarray}}(-i)^{q^{\prime}-1}\left(\prod_{v=1}^{c_{p}\Gamma}\frac{(\alpha_{v})^{q_{v}}}{q_{v}!}\right)\left\{\phi_{q^{\prime}}(\underbrace{H_{1}^{>i},\cdots,H_{c_{p}\Gamma}^{>i}}_{q^{\prime}})-\phi_{q^{\prime}}(\underbrace{H_{1}^{>i-1},\cdots,H_{c_{p}\Gamma}^{>i-1}}_{q^{\prime}})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q_{1},\cdots,q_{c_{p}\Gamma}:\\

B.3
Improvements from the proof for the Floquet-Magnus expansion

Trotterization can be seen as the time-evolution operator under the time-periodic Hamiltonian
H^{>0}(\tau^{\prime})
by Eq. (
102
), which is piecewise constant and has the period
c_{p}\Gamma\tau
.
Compared to the original proof for generic time-periodic Hamiltonians
Kuwahara et al. 2016
, our results by Lemma
9
, Lemma
10
, and Theorem
3
are better by the factor
\Gamma
[the number of partitions in Trotterization by Eq. (
4
)].
To be precise, the original proof predicts

\norm{T_n}\in\order{(\Gamma kg)^n},\quad\|\tilde{T}_{n}^{i}\|\in\order{(\Gamma p_0 kg)^n}

(126)

as counterparts of Lemmas
9
and
10
, coming from the period
c_{p}\Gamma\tau
.
Accompanied by this additional factor, it is demanded that the time
\tau
is small enough to satisfy
\tau\in\order{(\Gamma p_0kg)^{-1}}
in order to approximate Trottetization by the truncated BCH formula as Eq. (
46
).
On the other hand, we exploit the structure of Trotterization, Eq. (
6
), especially about the nested commutator Eq. (
8
).
This leads to the better bound in Lemmas
9
and
10
, and the much looser requirement
\tau\in\order{(p_0 kg)^{-1}}
by Eq. (
45
), which is independent of
\Gamma
.

We note that this improvement is essential in Hamiltonian simulation, especially for Hamiltonians with long-range interactions.
For generic Hamiltonians with finite-range interactions, the number of terms
\Gamma
can be
\order{1}
and hence it seems not to be important.
However, when limiting to finite-range interactions, the Haah-Hastings-Kothari-Low (HHKL) algorithm can achieve nearly optimal gate counts
\order{Ngt \times\mr{polylog}(Ngt/\varepsilon)}
, which is better than MPF, based on the Lieb-Robinson bound
Haah et al. 2021
(See also Table
1
).
There is stronger motivation in considering long-range interactions for MPF, where the HHKL algorithm becomes inefficient and the number of terms
\Gamma
generally increases in the number of interactions,
\order{N^k}
.
In that case, the original result for generic cases demands the Trotter step
\tau=t/r\in\order{(\Gamma p_0kg)^{-1}}
, which is not compatible with the good size dependency by Eq. (
71
).
Namely, it is essential to delete the
\Gamma
-dependence from the requirement as Theorem
3
.
Our improvement can ensure that MPF has a cost with good size-dependency, Eq. (
71
), broadly for finite-ranged and long-ranged interacting Hamiltonians.

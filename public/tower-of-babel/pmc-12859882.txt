Surgical management and reconstruction techniques for divided eyelid nevus: a systematic review
Authors: Bahareh Mahdood, Amirmohammad Merajikhah, Zohre Akbari, Maryam Bastami, Arezoo Mohammadi Gazestani
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDivided eyelid nevus represents a congenital melanocytic lesion that carries malignant transformation potential and may compromise both aesthetic appearance and functional integrity of the eyelids. This systematic review examines surgical techniques for the complete excision and subsequent reconstruction of such lesions.Material and methodsA systematic search was conducted across multiple databases including Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, ProQuest, Science Direct, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar, with the search extending until July 7, 2025. The main question of the study was: "What are the outcomes of reconstructive surgery for divided nevus?". No restrictions were applied regarding publication language, geographic location, or time period. Inclusion criteria consisted of original research studies evaluating surgical management of nevi, with particular focus on treatment efficacy and postoperative outcomes. Excluded studies comprised non-original research, qualitative studies, editorials, letters to the editor, and review articles. The methodology followed the PRISMA 2020 guidelines for systematic reviews.ResultsThe systematic review ultimately incorporated 33 studies evaluating various therapeutic modalities for nevus management, including surgical excision with primary closure, flap reconstruction techniques, skin grafting procedures, laser ablation, and cryotherapy. All surgical modalities demonstrated improvements in both aesthetic and functional outcomes; however, flap-based reconstruction yielded superior results compared to skin grafting, attributable to enhanced vascular perfusion. Common complications across techniques included madarosis, trichiasis, recurrence, and residual nevus cells.ConclusionFlap-based reconstruction consistently demonstrates superior functional and aesthetic outcomes compared to skin grafting, primarily due to enhanced vascularity and tissue compatibility. Other techniques have their own benefits and risks.Keywords: Nevus, Surgical procedures, Eyelids, Nose, Face, Plastic surgery procedures

Background

A divided nevus, also called a "kissing nevus," "split ocular nevus," or "panda nevus," is a congenital lesion that affects both the upper and lower eyelids [1]. When the eye is closed, the nevus presents as a continuous mass. According to size, these nevi are categorized into three groups: small (under 1.5 cm), medium (1.5–20 cm), and large (exceeding 20 cm) [2]. Eyelid lesions may extend to conjunctiva, forehead, cheek, or medial canthus [3].

Existing studies report that congenital divided eyelid nevi typically range from small to medium in size [4]. This condition occurs more frequently in Asian and Caucasian populations, with a higher incidence among females [5]. Nevi that span both eyelids can result in ptosis, ectropion, epiphora, or even amblyopia in children. As patients grow older, their primary focus shifts toward improving the aesthetic appearance [6]. Due to risks of malignant change and aesthetic implications, treatment measures such as surgical excision and blepharoplasty remain the primary and efficient treatment options [4].

No established treatment protocol exists for this condition. While malignant transformation is theoretically possible, only a single confirmed case has been documented [7].

The most frequently reported reconstructive method involves surgical removal followed by skin grafting, which may be performed in one or two stages. Different techniques, including musculocutaneous flaps, tarsoconjunctival flaps, and pedicled flaps, have been used to restore the eyelid structure [6].

The pathogenesis involves neural crest-derived melanocyte migration into developing eyelid epithelium during weeks 12–14 of gestation, while the eyelids remain fused (weeks 9–24) [8, 9]. Subsequent separation of the fused eyelids results in division of the nevus across both lid margins [8]. A review of 55 studies shows melanoma risk increases with nevus size: large nevi (> 20 cm) have a 5–40% transformation risk, medium nevi (1.5–19.9 cm) showed no malignant changes in long-term studies, while small nevi (< 1.5 cm) carry less than 1% risk by age 60 [10, 11]. Due to the possibility of cancerous progression and aesthetic considerations, preventive surgical interventions such as complete nevus removal and eyelid reconstruction constitute primary and efficacious treatment modalities. This systematic review evaluates surgical outcomes for eyelid nevus excision and reconstruction.

Materials and methods

Data collection

This research was conducted in accordance with the 2020 PRISMA guidelines [12]. The main question of the study was: " What are the outcomes of reconstructive surgery for divided nevus?".

Search strategy

The relevant literature was systematically retrieved from multiple databases, including Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, ProQuest, Science Direct, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar. The search encompassed all available studies up to July 7, 2025, without restrictions on publication language or date. The following key terms were utilized in the search strategy: nevus or kissing nevus OR split ocular nevus OR panda nevus OR palpebral nevus OR eyelid disease OR eyelid lesion OR eyelid nevus OR dysplastic nevus AND surgery OR excision OR resection OR surgical treatment OR surgical management.

Two independent authors initially screened the references of potentially eligible articles, followed by an evaluation of the titles and abstracts of the retrieved studies. A total of 358 duplicate records were identified. Irrelevant publications and non-original studies were subsequently excluded from further analysis. Key extracted data included publication year, first author, study methodology, patient demographics (sample size, age, sex), nevus characteristics (dimensions, anatomical location), reconstructive techniques employed, and clinical outcomes. All assessments were conducted independently by the reviewers.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria comprised investigations focusing on surgical management or reconstructive techniques for nevus or divided nevus. Exclusion criteria eliminated non-original research, qualitative analyses, letters to the editor, editorials, and review articles. Additionally, papers failing to report surgical outcomes for nevus were omitted from the systematic review. Eligible original research encompassed clinical trials, retrospective analyses, prospective investigations, experimental studies, surgical technique descriptions, case reports, and case series, all of which underwent comprehensive evaluation in this analysis.

Data extraction

The screening of articles was performed independently by two investigators (BM, MB). Any discrepancies between reviewers were resolved through consultation with a third researcher (AM) to reach consensus. For data extraction, a standardized digital form was employed to systematically collect the following parameters: publication year, first author, research methodology, number of patientssize, demographic characteristics (age and sex), nevus dimensions (size and location), reconstructive methods implemented, and treatment outcomes.

Quality assessment

The methodological rigor of included studies was assessed using the Newcastle–Ottawa assessment tools [13]. This evaluation framework examined four key domains: study selection methodology, comparative analyses, treatment protocols, and reported outcomes. Each investigation received a quality score on a 9-point scale, with studies scoring ≥ 7 stars classified as high-quality evidence and those with ≤ 6 stars designated as low-quality evidence. To minimize assessment bias, two independent researchers conducted duplicate evaluations. This systematic quality appraisal process enabled comprehensive identification and evaluation of potential biases across all included studies. Since the present review also includes case reports and case series, the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale was modified accordingly for them.

Assessment of risk of bias for included studies

Two independent investigators (BM, AM) evaluated methodological bias in each included study by applying the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment criteria [14]. This standardized tool enabled systematic classification of potential biases as either high risk, low risk, or indeterminate for each evaluated methodological component.

Results

Our comprehensive search strategy identified an initial pool of 3,820 publications across seven major databases: Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, ProQuest, Science Direct, Cochrane Library, and Google Scholar. Following examination of additional sources, we selected 24 studies meeting our research criteria. The search encompassed all available literature through July 7, 2025. Using EndNote 21, we detected and eliminated 358 duplicate records. Preliminary screening of titles and abstracts resulted in the exclusion of 3,341 articles, while 145 progressed to detailed full-text evaluation. Subsequent assessment led to the removal of 112 papers. Studies that did not specifically address the surgical treatment of nevus or whose full text was unavailable were excluded at this stage. The final selection comprised 33 studies satisfying our predefined inclusion criteria. This included: 1 prospective investigation, 2 randomized controlled trials, 14 retrospective analyses, 14 case reports, and 2 case series (see Fig. 1 and Table 1 for complete details).

Risks of bias of individual studies

Surgical techniques

Various methods can be utilized for the repair of the nevus. In this study, we have investigated surgical techniques for the treatment and repair of this lesion.

Laser ablation

Laser ablation may also be utilized for the removal of nevus lesions. In the present systematic review, two studies employed CO2 laser ablation for nevus excision [4, 24]. The evidence demonstrated that laser ablation can effectively eliminate lesions; however, residual lesional tissue persisted in some cases. The evidence indicated that recurrence was observed only in rare cases. Additionally, complications such as madarosis and trichiasis with associated inflammatory changes were reported in these studies (Table 1). Procedures such as laser ablation and dermabrasion are invasive but non-surgical. However, laser ablation is an invasive yet non-surgical procedure.

Cryosurgery

In the current study, an investigation was conducted on the use of cryosurgery employing a porous and permeable nickel-titanium automated cryoapplicator (Nitinol). The results demonstrated that the lesion healed within 6–8 weeks while maintaining normal eyelid function and marginal anatomy. The study further concluded that porous cryoapplicators achieve optimal clinical-cosmetic outcomes in the treatment of marginal eyelid nevi while preserving the functional structure of the eyelid [20] (Table 1).

Resection and direct closure

The primary treatment for this nevus involves partial or total excision, depending on the affected skin regions. Some lesions involve both eyelids, the tarsus, and even the conjunctiva. In such cases, certain studies recommend partial resection only. Following nevus excision, various reconstruction methods are available, one of which is direct closure. Thus, if the wound edges approximate after excision, primary closure can be performed [16, 17, 29, 31, 38, 39] (Table 1).

Grafts

The use of skin grafts is one of the reconstructive methods employed following the excision of nevus lesions. For the reconstruction of this defect, both split-thickness and full-thickness skin grafts can be employed. In the present systematic review, 13 studies utilized skin grafts. Among these studies, some employed both grafts and flaps.

The reviewed studies employed various reconstructive techniques, including medial canthal skin grafting, anterior lamellar reconstruction using supraclavicular full-thickness skin grafts, and retroauricular full-thickness skin grafts. Additionally, they utilized amniotic membrane transplantation, pentagonal full-thickness skin grafts, and mucosal membrane grafting for bulbar conjunctival and medial canthal reconstruction [4, 16, 24–26, 28, 30, 31, 36, 38, 40, 41, 43] (Table 1).

The aforementioned studies that utilized skin grafting techniques for reconstructing defects following nevus excision demonstrated the following outcomes: complete nevus removal while preserving normal eyelid function, compliance with anatomical requirements, improved aesthetic outcomes, absence of nevus recurrence, no infection, no necrosis, and no complications in wound healing [4, 16, 24–26, 28, 30, 31, 36, 38, 40, 41, 43] (Table 1).

On the other hand, these studies also demonstrated complications including trichiasis, localized madarosis, recurrence of localized melanoma one-year post-grafting, residual pigmentation, epiphora, and eyelid margin reduction [4, 16, 24–26, 28, 30, 31, 36, 38, 40, 41, 43] (Table 1).

Flaps

Flaps are among the most common reconstructive techniques for repairing defects resulting from nevus excision. Flaps can be either free or pedicled. They may be harvested from areas adjacent to the defect or from more distant sites. Flaps, with preserved tissue vascularity and inherent blood supply, can effectively restore both aesthetic contour and functional integrity in defects resulting from nevus excision.

In the present systematic review, 19 studies have employed various types of flaps for the reconstruction of defects resulting from nevus excision. The above studies have reported utilizing various reconstructive flap techniques for nevus excision defects, including: sliding tarsoconjunctival flaps, myocutaneous flaps, local grafts, temporal area expanded flaps, nasolabial flaps, expanded local flaps, modified V–Y orbicularis oculi musculocutaneous flaps, tarsoconjunctival advancement flaps with superior pedicle for posterior lamellar reconstruction, frontal and facial expanded flaps, adjacent and distant island skin flaps, postauricular SMAS-pedicled flaps, reversal superficial temporal artery flaps, complex flap transfers (either forehead or cheek-based), sliding eyelid skin flaps, and transposition flaps [4, 15–18, 23, 25–28, 33, 35, 37, 38, 41, 43–45] (Table 1).

The findings of the present study demonstrated that the use of a flap can be cost-effective while achieving outcomes such as improved eyelid function, defect repair, enhanced aesthetic results, structural support for the contralateral eyelid, and restoration of normal eyelid function.Moreover, flap utilization can provide appropriate symmetry without ptosis, ectropion, raised margins, or keloid formation [4, 15–18, 23, 25–28, 33, 35, 37, 38, 41, 43–45]. Notwithstanding these advantages, current evidence has documented cases of nevus recurrence following flap reconstruction. Furthermore, complications such as madarosis, mild flap edema, and mild pretarsal erythema have been reported in studies utilizing flap reconstruction techniques. In addition, evidence of venous outflow obstruction resulting in localized necrosis following flap reconstruction has been observed [4, 15–18, 23, 25–28, 33, 35, 37, 38, 41, 43–45] (Table 1).

Discussion

This systematic review demonstrated that various reconstructive and therapeutic surgical techniques can be utilized for nevus excision and repair, each associated with distinct advantages and potential complications. The available techniques for nevus excision and reconstruction include surgical resection with direct closure, various flap reconstruction methods, skin grafting, and laser ablation. Each approach offers distinct advantages while carrying specific risks and limitations that must be carefully considered in clinical decision-making.

The utilization of unsuitable skin grafts in tissue repair may result in structural abnormalities or functional impairments of the eyelid. Following lesion excision, surgical restoration of the affected area can be achieved through various techniques, including full-thickness grafts, local advancement flaps, and axial-pattern flaps [46]. Current evidence supports dermabrasion as a viable therapeutic approach for congenital melanocytic nevi. The procedure effectively targets and removes focal collections of nevus cells along with associated pigmentation by selectively removing the superficial epidermal layer and portions of the underlying dermis [18]. However, dermabrasion is an invasive yet non-surgical procedure. Surgical complete resection while preserving optimal aesthetic outcomes presents significant technical challenges, particularly in anatomically sensitive areas like the facial region; because Nevus cells in congenital lesions occupy both epidermal and dermal compartments, with frequent extension into subcutaneous tissues.

When addressing palpebral margin nevi, the lesion paring technique allows for optimal preservation of tarsal architecture. Conjunctival nevus treatment presents notable clinical challenges, as demonstrated by R. Jia et al. (2012), who successfully reconstructed conjunctival defects using buccal mucosal grafts [34]. Also, R. Lu et al. (2015) employed advanced skin flaps integrated with tarsoconjunctival flaps, reporting that all flaps remained viable postoperatively with acceptable donor-site scarring. The sole complication was madarosis, occurring in 5 of 6 patients [27]. On the other hand, in their 2015 study, K. Bhattacharjee et al. demonstrated successful outcomes using amniotic membrane grafts for defect reconstruction following nevus excision. All grafts were fully incorporated without complications such as infection, necrosis, or impaired wound healing. Complete conjunctival regeneration was achieved with no evidence of pigment recurrence or disease relapse. Notably, the series reported no instances of dry eye syndrome, scar hypertrophy, corneal abrasion, or ectropion [30]. Therefore, in addition to skin grafting, amniotic membrane transplantation can also serve as an effective approach for conjunctival defect reconstruction. Furthermore, evidence has shown that Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) can be utilized postoperatively for the repair of surgical wounds [47], because the lack of appropriate wound repair leads to reoperation and an increase in patient hospitalization [48].

The unique anatomical characteristics of eyelid skin—notably its minimal thickness and rich vascular network—create an optimal environment for pedicled flap viability. This enhanced vascular perfusion significantly improves graft survival rates in oculoplastic reconstruction procedures. Skin grafts offer an easy and reliable solution for reconstructing large wounds [4]. However, skin grafting presents several challenges, including color mismatch between donor and recipient sites, scar formation, and potential graft failure.

Therefore, flap reconstruction with preserved vascularity provides superior outcomes compared to grafting. In this regard, X. Li et al. (2025) demonstrated that sliding flap approachescombined with bicanalicular stenting offer an effective and minimally invasive strategy for medial canthal reconstruction [15]. In select cases, expanded flaps are employed to provide additional tissue for surgical reconstruction. A. N. Steblyuk et al. (2022) reported excellent tissue compatibility and optimal color-texture matching with the use of orbicularis musculocutaneous flaps, expanded flaps, and modified V–Y orbicularis oculi musculocutaneous flaps, achieving clinically satisfactory outcomes in all treated cases [23].

Both flaps and grafts present distinct advantages and challenges. D. P. Huang et al. (2009) utilized primary radical resection with complex flap reconstruction (forehead or cheek) and demonstrated superior aesthetic outcomes compared to autologous grafting [38].

In addition to the aforementioned techniques, laser ablation—particularly using CO₂ lasers—can be employed for nevus removal. X. Zhang et al. (2020) demonstrated in their clinical study that while laser treatment achieved complete nevus clearance in some cases, lesion recurrence was also observed [21]. In contrast, H. J. Cho et al. (2019) reported no nevus recurrence following laser therapy; however, they documented complications including trichiasis and madarosis [24].

Our review indicates that cryosurgery may also serve as an effective modality for nevus eradication. A. N. Steblyuk et al. (2022) demonstrated that cryosurgical techniques yield favorable functional and aesthetic eyelid outcomes [20].

In the current review, we have systematically evaluated the outcomes, advantages, and complications associated with various surgical techniques. Therefore, the application of various surgical techniques presents distinct advantages and potential complications. Furthermore, certain outcomes may be influenced by the surgeon's expertise, available resources, and specific case characteristics, which can significantly impact surgical results. The selection of an appropriate surgical technique is contingent upon patient-specific factors.

The present study demonstrates that selecting an appropriate reconstructive approach can significantly influence critical outcomes such as nevus recurrence, cosmetic appearance, and postoperative complications. All the surgical techniques discussed in this study are proven and are still commonly used by surgeons. However, each technique has its own set of pros and cons. Understanding these differences helps surgeons choose the best approach for each patient. The included studies, when considering the potential biases indicated in Fig. 2, demonstrate minor heterogeneity; however, this heterogeneity does not appear to significantly impact the reported outcomes. Additionally, as shown in Fig. 2, some studies employed distinct methodological approaches. The limitation of this study is the use of case reports and case series, which were included due to their high clinical outcomes value. It is recommended that more robust clinical studies be conducted to evaluate reconstructive techniques for this specific type of nevus. Multicenter registries or standardized scoring systems for functional and aesthetic outcomes are also proposed.

Conclusion

Nevi possess malignant potential; therefore, surgical excision of congenital nevi located in periocular regions such as eyelids, conjunctiva and facial areas serves both prophylactic and functional-aesthetic purposes by preventing malignant transformation while improving eyelid appearance and function. The current review highlights that each surgical technique offers unique benefits and drawbacks with the evidence favoring flap procedures over grafts for achieving better aesthetic and functional results adjunctive approaches like cryotherapy and laser ablation can be integrated alongside primary reconstruction methods for combined treatment deployed as secondary options in cases of recurrence or considered as independent surgical alternatives depending on clinical circumstances this multifaceted strategy enables customized treatment plans adapted to each patient's specific needs and lesion characteristics.

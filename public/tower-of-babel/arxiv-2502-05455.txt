Exactly solvable multicomponent spinless fermions


Abstract
By generalising the one to one correspondence between exactly solvable hermitian matrices ℋ = ℋ † \mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\dagger} and exactly solvable spinless fermion systems ℋ f = ∑ x , y c x † ​ ℋ ​ ( x , y ) ​ c y \mathcal{H}_{f}=\sum_{x,y}c_{x}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}(x,y)c_{y} , four types of exactly solvable multicomponent fermion systems are constructed explicitly. They are related to the multivariate Krawtcouk, Meixner and two types of Rahman like polynomials, constructed recently by myself. The Krawtchouk and Meixner polynomials are the eigenvectors of certain real symmetric matrices ℋ \mathcal{H} which are related to the difference equations governing them. The corresponding fermions have nearest neighbour interactions. The Rahman like polynomials are eigenvectors of certain reversible Markov chain matrices 𝒦 \mathcal{K} , from which real symmetric matrices ℋ \mathcal{H} are uniquely defined by the similarity transformation in terms of the square root of the stationary distribution. The fermions have wide range interactions.

2.1
Multivariate Krawtchouk polynomials

The multivariate Krawtchouk polynomials
\{P_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\}
are
defined by two positive integers
N
and
n
(
N>n\geq 2
) and
n
distinct positive numbers

p_{i}>0
,
i=1,\ldots,n
,
on a finite
n

\mathcal{X}
,

\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\quad|x|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i},\quad\mathcal{X}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\ |\,|x|\leq N\}.

(2.1)

The multivariate Krawtchouk
\{P_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\}
are orthogonal with respect to the

multinomial distribution

with probabilities
\{\eta_{i}\}
which are functions of

\{p_{i}\}
,

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}W(\boldsymbol{x},N,\eta)P_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})P_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0,\quad\boldsymbol{m}\neq\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptW(\boldsymbol{x},N,\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{N!}{x_{1}!\cdots x_{n}!x_{0}!}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\eta_{i}^{x_{i}}=\binom{N}{\boldsymbol{x}}\eta_{0}^{x_{0}}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\boldsymbol{x}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102ptx_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}N-|x|,\ \binom{N}{\boldsymbol{x}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{N!}{x_{1}!\cdots x_{n}!x_{0}!},

\displaystyle\quad\eta_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{p_{i}}{1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}},\quad\eta_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}},\quad\sum_{i=0}^{n}\eta_{i}=1,\quad\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\boldsymbol{x}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}^{x_{i}}.

(2.4)

They form a complete set of eigenvectors of a real symmetric
|\mathcal{X}|\times|\mathcal{X}|
matrix
\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle B_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}N-|x|,\quad D_{i}(\boldsymbol{x})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}p_{i}^{-1}x_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\bigl(B_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})+D_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})\bigr)\,\delta_{\boldsymbol{x}\,\boldsymbol{y}}-\sqrt{B_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})D_{j}(\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{e}_{j})}\,\delta_{\boldsymbol{x}+\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\,\boldsymbol{y}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 113.81102pt-\sqrt{B_{j}(\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{e}_{j})D_{j}(\boldsymbol{x})}\,\delta_{\boldsymbol{x}-\boldsymbol{e}_{j}\,\boldsymbol{y}}\right],\qquad\qquad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.6)

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\sqrt{W(\boldsymbol{y},N,\eta)}\,P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{y})=\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})\sqrt{W(\boldsymbol{x},N,\eta)}\,P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.7)

in which
\boldsymbol{e}_{j}
is the
j
-th unit vector,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
The eigenvalue
\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})
has a linear spectrum

\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}\lambda_{j}\geq 0,\qquad\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X},

(I.3.13)

in which
\lambda_{j}>0
is the
j
-th root of a degree
n
characteristic polynomial
\mathcal{F}(\lambda)

of an
n\times n
positive definite symmetric matrix
F(p)
depending on
\{p_{i}\}
,

0=\mathcal{F}(\lambda)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}Det\bigl(\lambda I_{n}-F(p)\bigr),\quad F(p)_{i\,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}1+p_{i}^{-1}\delta_{i\,j}.

(I.3.14)

The multivariate Krawtchouk polynomial
P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})
is a terminating
(n+1,2n+2)
hypergeometric function
of Aomoto-Gelfand
[
6
,
7
,
24
]

\displaystyle P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\leq N\\
(c_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{N}_{0}})\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-x_{i})_{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(-m_{j})_{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}}}{(-N)_{\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}}}\;\frac{\prod(u_{ij})^{c_{ij}}}{\prod c_{ij}!},

(2.8)

in which
M_{n}({\mathbb{N}}_{0})
is the set of square matrices of degree
n
with nonnegative integer
elements.
Here,
(a)_{n}
is the shifted factorial defined for

and a nonnegative integer

(a)_{0}=1

(a)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(a+k)
,
n\geq 1

The
n\times n
matrix

is defined by

u_{i\,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}-p_{i}^{-1}}=\frac{1}{1-p_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}},\quad i,j=1,\ldots,n.

(I.3.16)

With the explicit expression of the multivariate Krawtchouk polynomials (

2.2
)
now reads

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}}W(\boldsymbol{x},N,\eta)P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})P_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{\boldsymbol{m}\,\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}}{\binom{N}{\boldsymbol{m}}(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}})^{\boldsymbol{m}}},\qquad(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}})^{\boldsymbol{m}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\bar{p}_{j}^{m_{j}},

(2.9)

\displaystyle\bar{p}_{j}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}u_{i,j}^{2}-1\Bigr)^{-1}>0,\quad j=1,\ldots,n,

(I.3.18)

leading to the complete set of orthonormal eigenvectors

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{y})=\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x}),\quad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=\delta_{\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}},\quad\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{y})=\delta_{\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y}},\qquad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{m},\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.11)

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sqrt{W(\boldsymbol{x},N,\eta)}P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\sqrt{\bar{W}(\boldsymbol{m},N,\bar{p})},\qquad\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X},

(2.12)

\displaystyle\bar{W}(\boldsymbol{m},N,\bar{p})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\binom{N}{\boldsymbol{m}}(\bar{\boldsymbol{p}})^{\boldsymbol{m}},\qquad\sum_{\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X}}\bar{W}(\boldsymbol{m},N,\bar{p})=\Bigl(1+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{p}_{j}\Bigr)^{N}.

(2.13)

(2.15)

By introducing the momentum space fermion operators

\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}\}
,
\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\}
,
\boldsymbol{m}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})c_{\boldsymbol{x}},\ \ \hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})c_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\dagger}\ \Leftrightarrow\ {c}_{\boldsymbol{x}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}},\ \ {c}_{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\dagger}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},

(2.16)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\Longrightarrow\ \{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}\}=\delta_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\,{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}},\quad\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=0=\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}\},

the fermion Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{f}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{y})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}

(2.19)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\Longrightarrow\ \{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}\}=\delta_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\,{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}},\quad\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=0=\{\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}\},

(3.17)

the fermion Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{f}
is diagonalised

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{f}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}}\in\mathcal{X}}\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x};\beta,{\boldsymbol{u}})\mathcal{H}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{y};\beta,{\boldsymbol{u}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}},{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime},{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x};\beta,{\boldsymbol{u}})\hat{\phi}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{x};\beta,{\boldsymbol{u}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}},

(3.18)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\Longrightarrow\ [\mathcal{H}_{f},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger}]=\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\dagger},\qquad[\mathcal{H}_{f},\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}]=-\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}})\hat{c}_{\boldsymbol{m}}.

(3.19)

This fermion system has nearest neighbour interactions as is clear from the form of
\mathcal{H}
(
3.5
).

Rahman like fermions

Rahman polynomials have long been investigated as typical examples of multivariate orthogonal polynomials,

Two types of Rahman like polynomials introduced here are constructed as eigenvectors of certain

reversible
Markov chain matrices
\mathcal{K}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2

[
9
]
, which will be cited as II.
This method is very different from those of the existing theories and models.
The main results of Rahman like polynomials
[
9
]
of type (1) and type (2) are summarised as follows.
Both types of Rahman like polynomials
\{P_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})\}

depend on two positive integers
N
and
n
(
N>n\geq 2
) and
2n
distinct positive numbers

0<\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}<1
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, with
|\beta|<1
,
on a finite
n
-dimensional integer lattice
\mathcal{X}
,

\mathcal{X}=\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\ |\,|x|\leq N\},\quad\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}),\quad\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}),\ |\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}.

(4.1)

These are the parameters of the binomial (
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
) and multinomial (
\boldsymbol{\beta}
) distributions,

\displaystyle W_{1}(x,y,\alpha)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\binom{y}{x}\alpha^{x}(1-\alpha)^{y-x}>0,\quad\sum_{x=0}^{y}W_{1}(x,y,\alpha)=1,

(4.2)

\displaystyle W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{N!\cdot(1-|\beta|)^{N-|x|}}{x_{1}!\cdots x_{n}!(N-|x|)!}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}^{x_{i}}=\binom{N}{\boldsymbol{x}}\beta_{0}^{x_{0}}\boldsymbol{\beta}^{\boldsymbol{x}}>0,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})=1,\quad x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}N-|x|,\ \beta_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}1-|\beta|.

Two types of Markov chain matrices
\mathcal{K}^{(i)}
,
i=1,2
are constructed
by certain convolutions of the multinomial distribution and
n
-tuple of the binomial distributions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(1)}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\mathcal{X}}W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{z}},N-|z|,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{1}(z_{i},y_{i},\alpha_{i})>0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{(2)}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{{\boldsymbol{z}}\in\mathcal{X}}W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}}-{\boldsymbol{z}},N-|y|,{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\prod_{i=1}^{n}W_{1}(z_{i},y_{i},\alpha_{i})>0.

(4.5)

The convention is that the transition probability matrix per unit time interval

\mathcal{K}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})
on
\mathcal{X}
means
the transition from an initial point
\boldsymbol{y}
to a final point
\boldsymbol{x}
with

\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})>0,\quad\sum_{x\in{X}}\mathcal{K}^{(i)}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})=1,\quad i=1,2.

(4.6)

Positive
\mathcal{K}
means wide range interactions as
every pair of points
\boldsymbol{x}
and
\boldsymbol{y}
is connected.

Reversibility means that
\mathcal{K}
has a reversible distribution
W_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},N,\boldsymbol{\eta})
satisfying

\mathcal{K}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{y})W_{n}(\boldsymbol{y},N,\boldsymbol{\eta})=\mathcal{K}(\boldsymbol{y},\boldsymbol{x})W_{n}(\boldsymbol{x},N,\boldsymbol{\eta}).

(4.7)

This condition determines the probability parameter
\boldsymbol{\eta}

as a function of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
in
type (1) and (2);

\displaystyle\text{type (1)}:\qquad

\displaystyle\eta_{i}=\frac{\beta_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}}\frac{1}{D_{n}},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,\qquad D_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\alpha_{k}\beta_{k}}{1-\alpha_{k}},

(II.2.12)

\displaystyle\text{type (2)}:\qquad

\displaystyle\eta_{i}=\frac{\beta_{i}}{1-\alpha_{i}}\frac{1}{D_{n}},\quad i=1,\ldots,n,\qquad D_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\beta_{k}}{1-\alpha_{k}}.

(II.2.17)

As the reversible distribution is the stationary distribution, both types of
Rahman like polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the stationary distribution
W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\eta}})

with each own
\boldsymbol{\eta}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{X}}W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\eta}})P_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{x}})P_{\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}}({\boldsymbol{x}})=0,\quad\boldsymbol{m}\neq\boldsymbol{m}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X},

(4.8)

\displaystyle W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\eta}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{N!\cdot(1-|\eta|)^{N-|x|}}{x_{1}!\cdots x_{n}!(N-|x|)!}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}^{x_{i}}=\binom{N}{\boldsymbol{x}}\eta_{0}^{x_{0}}\boldsymbol{\eta}^{\boldsymbol{x}}>0,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}W_{n}({\boldsymbol{x}},N,{\boldsymbol{\eta}})=1,\quad x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}N-|x|,\ \eta_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}1-|\eta|.

4.1
Rahman like polynomials type (1)

Rahman like polynomials of type (1) are
left eigenvectors of reversible
Markov chain matrix

\mathcal{K}^{(1)}
with a multiplicative spectrum,

\displaystyle\sum_{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{K}^{(1)}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x};{\boldsymbol{u}})=\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}})P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{y};{\boldsymbol{u}}),\quad\forall{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathcal{X},

(4.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\mathcal{E}({\boldsymbol{m}})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}},

(II.3.25)

in which
\{\lambda_{i}\}

i=1,\ldots,n

the roots of the characteristic equation,

Det\left(\lambda\,I_{n}-F^{(1)}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\right)=0,\quad F^{(1)}({\boldsymbol{\alpha}},{\boldsymbol{\beta}})_{i\,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-\alpha_{i}\beta_{j}+\alpha_{i}\delta_{i\,j},\quad i,j=1,\ldots,n.

(II.3.7)

It should be stressed that
-1<\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})\leq 1
due to Perron-Frobenius theorem of positive matrices.
This is in good contrast with the multivariate Krawtchouk I(3.13)
and Meixner I(4.14) polynomials.
The Rahman like polynomials of type (1)

(n+1,2n+2)
type terminating hypergeometric function of

[

i,j=1,\ldots,n

u_{i,j}=\frac{\alpha_{i}(\lambda_{j}-1)}{\lambda_{j}-\alpha_{i}},

(II.3.16)

\displaystyle P_{\boldsymbol{m}}(\boldsymbol{x};{\boldsymbol{u}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}\leq N\\
(c_{ij})\in M_{n}({\mathbb{N}_{0}})\end{subarray}}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-x_{i})_{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}c_{ij}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}(-m_{j})_{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}c_{ij}}}{(-N)_{\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}}}\;\frac{\prod(u_{ij})^{c_{ij}}}{\prod c_{ij}!}.

(4.11)

and

A Mapping Theorem for Derived Foliations
Victor Alfieri

Abstract
In this paper, we construct in characteristic zero a derived foliation on derived mapping stacks 𝐌𝐚𝐩 ¯ S ​ ( X , Y ) \underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y) , for S S a base derived stack, X X a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection derived stack over S S , and Y Y a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack over S S , when Y Y is equipped with a derived foliation relative to S S . In the process, given a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack Z Z over a derived stack X X , we will first show that the ∞ \infty -category of derived foliations over Z Z relative to X X embeds as a full subcategory of derived stacks over Z Z equipped with extra structure, and describe its essential image explicitly. We will then show that given a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection map of derived stacks f : X → Y f:X\to Y , the push-forward functor f ∗ f_{*} from derived stacks over X X to derived stacks over Y Y preserves the preceding essential images, and thus defines a push-forward, from derived foliations over Z Z relative to X X , to derived foliations over f ∗ ​ Z f_{*}Z relative to Y Y . The aforementioned result on derived mapping stacks is obtained as a special case of this statement. As example applications, given a smooth projective scheme X X equipped with a derived folation, we obtain derived folations on the derived moduli stacks ℝ ​ 𝐌 ¯ g , n ​ ( X ) \mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X) and ℝ ​ 𝐇𝐢𝐥𝐛 l ​ c ​ i ​ ( X ) \mathbb{R}\mathbf{Hilb}^{lci}(X) , which are respectively the derived enhancements of the moduli stack of families of stable curves of genus g g with n n marked points on X X , and of the Hilbert scheme of closed subschemes of X X .

Introduction

This paper aims to prove existence theorems for derived foliations in derived geometry in characteristic zero. Foliations in this context were introduced and studied in a series of papers by Bertrand Toën and Gabriele Vezzosi,
[
TV23
]
and
[
TV20
]
, and later on in their book
[
TV25
]
.

1.1
Classical foliations

In classical differential geometry, a foliation on a differential manifold
M
is morally the data of a partition of
M
into sub-manifolds, called the ”leaves” of the foliation. Given such a foliation
F
and a point
x\in M
, one can define the sub-vector space
T_{x}F\subset T_{x}M
of tangent vectors to
x
in
F_{x}\subset M
, where
F_{x}
is the only leaf of
F
containing
x
. Collectively they form a sub-bundle
TF\subset TM
of the tangent bundle of
M
, called the tangent complex of the foliation, which is moreover stable by the Lie bracket. By dualizing, one obtains instead a quotient of the de Rham algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)\to\Omega^{\bullet}(F)
which inherits an internal differential from the de Rham differential, and
\Omega^{1}(F)
is called the contangent complex of the foliation. Moreover, when properly defined, all these descriptions of foliations turn out to be equivalent to each other. A reference on the subject covering these different definitions and their equivalence is
[
MM03
]
.

In classical algebraic geometry, the latter point of view has been used to define algebraic foliations, which are defined as algebras equipped with a square-zero derivation. This allows in particular to consider foliations on singular varieties which was already not possible in the differential case, but they are difficult to construct and cannot often be transported along morphisms. A reference on modern applications of the theory of foliations in algebraic geometry is
[
CMP16
]
.

1.2
Derived foliations and main results of the paper

In derived geometry, we consider instead a notion of derived foliation which is less rigid and can thus exist in a wider range of contexts, as studied in
[
TV25
]
.
Most notably, in this paper we will prove the following main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a base derived stack in characteristic 0. Let
X
be a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection derived stack over
S
, and
Y
be a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack over
S
equipped with a derived foliation
\mathcal{F}
relative to
S
.
Then the derived mapping stack
\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)
inherits a derived foliation
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
relative to
S
.

Note that in comparison, in the differential case, the statement of
1.1
would already require clarifications on its meaning because
\mathbf{Map}(X,Y)
is an infinite dimensional object. Moreover, even in the case of classical algebraic geometry where the meaning of such a statement is clear, without the tools of derived geometry its conclusion would be false in this generality and require much stronger hypotheses, because this object would be very singular and its de Rham algebra extremely difficult to compute and not bounded.

Moreover, as in classical foliation theory, derived foliations admit tangent complexes, and the tangent complex of the induced derived foliation on
\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)
stated by the above theorem has the following description:

Propositon 1.2
.

In the context of
1.1
, let
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{F}}
(resp.
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}})
denote the tangent complex of
\mathcal{F}
(resp.
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
), and
\mathbb{T}_{X/S}
the tangent complex of
X
relative to
S
. For any
S
-point of
\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)
, which corresponds to an
S
-linear map
g:X\to Y
, there is a canonical equivalence:

\mathbb{T}_{g}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\simeq\Gamma_{S}(X,\mathbb{T}_{X/S}\oplus g^{*}\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{F}})

where
\Gamma_{S}:\textup{QCoh}(X)\to\textup{QCoh}(S)
is the global section relative to the base
S
, i.e. the push-forward of quasi-coherent sheaves along the structural map
X\to S
, which recovers the usual global sections when
S=\textup{Spec}(k)
. More generally, the tangent complex of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
itself is given by:

\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}\simeq\pi_{*}ev^{*}(p_{X}^{*}\mathbb{T}_{X/S}\oplus p_{Y}^{*}\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{F}})

where
ev:X\times_{S}\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)\to X\times_{S}Y
is the evaluation, and
\pi,p_{X},p_{Y}
are the projections in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times_{S}Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times_{S}\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Intuitively, this property means that a leaf of this derived foliation corresponds to a family of maps
X\to Y
such that, at any point in
X
, the family of points in
Y
induced by evaluating this family of maps at this point forms a leaf of the derived foliation on
Y
.

Let us a now give important example applications of this theorem.

Example 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}_{g,n}^{pre}
be the derived enhancement of the universal family of curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points, defined over the derived enhancement of the moduli stack of pre-stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
, and
X
be a smooth projective scheme equipped with a derived folation.
In this case,
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X)
, defined as
\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}_{g,n}^{pre},X\times\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre})
, inherits a derived foliation, and moreover it contains
\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)
as an open substack, which is the derived enhancement of the moduli stack of families of stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points on
X
, which also inherits a derived foliation by restriction. This provides a more systematic construction of the derived foliation that was constructed in section 3.3 of
[
TV25a
]
, in which only the case of derived foliations arising from vector fields was treated, whereas the results of this paper extend to arbitrary derived foliations even when they do not arise from vector fields or maps.
In particular, one could consider a new kind of Gromov-Witten invariants, relative to this derived foliation.

Example 1.4
.

Similarly, let
Y
be a derived Deligne-Mumford stack equipped with a derived foliation. We can form the derived stack
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{Hilb}^{lci}(Y)
, whose
S
-points for
S
a derived scheme are closed immersions
i:Z\hookrightarrow Y
where
Z
is a derived scheme over
S
and
Z\to S
is proper and local complete intersection (lci), thus
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{Hilb}^{lci}(Y)
is canonically embedded in a mapping stack, and inherits a derived foliation from
Y
. Moreover, we expect fixed points of this derived foliation to correspond to closed derived subschemes
Z\subset Y
that are stable by the derived foliation on
Y
, i.e. closed derived subschemes of algebraic leaves of the derived foliation.

For more information on both of these examples, and other applications of the work done in this paper, see
section

5
. Similar results could be obtained for various derived moduli stacks, provided its universal object is proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection over the derived moduli stack in question.

In order to prove
1.1
, we will more generally construct a push-forward of derived foliations, as stated next. Note that for a map of derived stacks
Z\to X
, we will denote by
\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)
the
\infty
-category of derived foliations defined on
Z
relative to
X
, and for a map of derived stacks
f:X\to Y
, we will denote by
f_{*}:\textup{dSt}/X\to\textup{dSt}/Y
the push-forward functor (also called direct image or Weil restriction).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
Z\to X
be a map of derived stacks which is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack, and
f:X\to Y
a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection map of derived stacks.
Then, there is a push-forward functor:

f_{*,\mathcal{F}ol}:\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\to\mathcal{F}ol(f_{*}Z/Y)

In order to construct this push-forward, we will show that
\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)
is equivalent to an
\infty
-category of derived stacks over
Z
equipped with extra structure (see
3.49
), and that the canonical push-forward functor of derived stacks
f_{*}:\textup{dSt}/X\to\textup{dSt}/Y
preserves this structure (see
4.12
).
Moreover, the tangent complex of the push-forward of a foliation admits a description very similar to that of
1.2
.
Finally, note that the following special case of the above theorem yields the main
1.1
.

Example 1.6
.

Let
S
be a derived stack,
X
be a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection derived stack over
S
, and
F
be a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack over
S
equipped with a derived foliation relative to
S
.
Let
Y=S
and
Z=X\times_{S}F
, and note that
X\to Y
is thus proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection, and that the projection
Z\to X
is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack.
Moreover, there is a canonical equivalence
f_{*}Z=\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{S}(X,F)
, so the latter inherits a derived folation by application of the above theorem.

1.3
Plan of the paper

As stated in the abstract, the main theorem will follow from a geometric reinterpretation of derived foliations. Let us give here a more detailed overview of this process.

In
section

2
, we will first recall the algebraic definitions of derived foliations, then rephrase them using geometric objects, by reviewing results from previous papers. The conclusions of this section are summarized in
2.26
.

In
section

3
, this geometric description will be used to construct a functor from the
\infty
-category of derived foliations to an
\infty
-category of derived stacks equipped with extra structure, as summarized in
3.12
. We will then show that this functor is fully faithful, and explicitly describe its essential image, which yields an equivalence of
\infty
-categories between derived folations and this essential image, as stated in
3.40
for the affine case, and generalized to relative derived Deligne-Mumford stacks in
3.49
.

In
section

4
, we will show that the push-forward functor from derived stacks over the base to derived stacks over the target preserves the extra structure of the derived stacks mentioned above, as stated in
4.12
. This will yield both the above
1.5
and finally the main
1.1
, as well as the description of the tangent complex of the resulting derived foliations in
1.2
.

Finally, in
section

5
, we will talk about applications of this paper and future work.

Definitions

Throughout this paper, we will work in characteristic zero, i.e. over a fixed

-algebra
k

is fixed, it will be omitted from all notations.

In this section we will first recall the algebraic definitions of graded mixed algebras, some constructions on them, the properties used to define derived foliations, and discuss their stability under the preceding constructions.

Secondly, we will recall results from

2.1
Algebraic definitions

We will first recall the definition of the structure of a graded mixed algebra over the base field
k
, which we will call the absolute definition. Graded mixed algebras are essentially graded algebras equipped with internal differentials shifting the grading, whose main class of examples is de Rham algebras.

Secondly we will recall the definitions of graded and graded mixed objects defined over other
k

All the definitions in this subsection can be found in
[
Pan+13
]
and
[
TV25
]

.

•

A graded module equipped with a mixed structure is called a graded mixed module.

•

-cdga’s:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A^{\prime}}}

In this situation, there is a canonical morphism
\mathbf{DR}(B/A)\to\mathbf{DR}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
of graded mixed algebras. We can use it to construct a push-forward/pull-back adjunction between
\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
and
\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)
:

•

The push-forward functor
\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})\to\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)
is defined by composing the structural map with this map.

•

The pull-back functor
\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)\to\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
is defined by the derived tensor product
-\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}_{\mathbf{DR}(B/A)}\mathbf{DR}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
.

Some
A
-linear graded mixed
B
-algebras serve as models for derived foliations on
\textup{Spec}(B)
relative to
\textup{Spec}(A)
. We will now discuss the properties defining them.

Definition 2.15
.

An
A
-linear graded mixed
B
-algebra
F
will be called
quasi-free
if the natural map
\textup{Sym}_{F(0)}F(1)\to F
is an equivalence of graded algebras.

We can show that this property is stable by push-forward.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
F^{\prime}\in\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
, and let
F\in\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)
denote its push-forward. If
F^{\prime}
is quasi-free, then so is
F
.

Proof:
We have to show that the natural map
\textup{Sym}_{F(0)}F(1)\to F
is an equivalence, assuming
\textup{Sym}_{F^{\prime}(0)}F^{\prime}(1)\to F^{\prime}
already is. But
F
and
F^{\prime}
are canonically isomorphic as graded algebras (forgetting the respective
B
and
B^{\prime}
-algebra structures, i.e. after taking the push-forward to the point), so in particular
F(0)
and
F^{\prime}(0)
are canonically equivalent as algebras, and
F(1)
and
F^{\prime}(1)
also are canonically equivalent as
F(0)
-modules. Therefore, we can construct this commutative square of graded algebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{Sym}_{F(0)}F(1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{Sym}_{F^{\prime}(0)}F^{\prime}(1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{F^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

and thus the top map is also an equivalence of graded algebras.
\Box

To conclude this section, we give the definition of a derived foliation over
\textup{Spec}(B)
relative to
\textup{Spec}(A)
.

Definition 2.17
.

An
A
-linear graded mixed
B
-algebra
F
will be called a
derived foliation
if it satisfies the following conditions:

1.

The structural map
B\to F(0)
is an equivalence

2.

F
is quasi-free

3.

F(1)
is perfect over
B

We define the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{F}ol(B/A)\subset\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)^{\textup{op}}
as the full subcategory spanned by these objects (note the
op
). Moreover, the
B
-module
F(1)[-1]
will be denoted by
\mathbb{L}_{F}
and is called the cotangent complex of
F
, and condition 1. and 2. imply that
F\simeq\textup{Sym}_{B}(\mathbb{L}_{F}[1])
as
A
-linear graded mixed
B
-algebras.

Remark 2.18
.

Firstly, each of these conditions are stable by pull-back (defined in
2.14
in this paper), so that the the pull-back of graded mixed algebras restricts to a pull-back of derived foliations, as described in
[
TV25
]
. More explicitly, note that in the case of a derived folation, the pull-back along a map
f:B\to B^{\prime}
has the following description: if
F\simeq\textup{Sym}_{B}(\mathbb{L}_{F}[1])
then
f^{*}F\simeq\textup{Sym}_{B^{\prime}}((f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{F}\oplus_{f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{B}}\mathbb{L}_{B^{\prime}})[1])
, where
\oplus
denotes the push-out of
B^{\prime}
-modules, i.e. we obtain the following formula to compute the contangent of the pull-back of a foliation:

\mathbb{L}_{f^{*}F}\simeq\mathbb{L}_{B^{\prime}/A}\oplus_{f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{B/A}}f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{F}

Secondly, the first condition is not stable by push-forward of modules, and thus we cannot construct a push-forward of derived foliations naively as a restriction of the push-forward of graded mixed algebras.
Instead, we will construct a push-forward of foliations in
section

4
using a different approach, through a point of view which we will introduce in the next subsection and develop in
section

3
.
Moreover, note that these conditions only depend on the graded structure of
F
, and not on its mixed structure.

2.2
A geometric reformulation

We will now review results from
[
Mou21
]
(resp.
[
MRT22
]
), which provide equivalences between graded modules (resp. graded mixed modules) over a derived stack
X
and quasi-coherent sheaves on derived stacks associated to
X
. In the next section, this will allow us to provide equivalences between bundles over these stacks and graded algebras (resp. graded mixed algebras) satisfying extra regularity conditions, such as derived folations.

More precisely, in the previous subsection we have introduced the following diagram (see
2.13
):

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\epsilon-dg^{gr}(B/A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{DR}(B/A)-Mod(dg^{gr})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{dg^{gr}(B)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cancel{\epsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-^{gr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-^{B}}

In this section, our goal is to view each
\infty
-category of this diagram as the
\infty
-category of quasi-coherent sheaves over a given stack, and each functor between them as either a pull-back or a push-forward along a given map between those stacks.

Remark 2.19
.

Consider first absolute graded modules and algebras.
As is well known in algebraic geometry, and has been shown in derived geometry in
[
Mou21
]
, there is a canonical equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories
dg^{gr}\simeq\textup{QCoh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})
, which promotes to an equivalence between their
\infty
-categories of commutative algebras.
Moreover, by
[
Lur17
]
theorem 4.5.4.7, the
\infty
-category of commutative algebras in the
\infty
-categorical localization of
dg^{gr}
is equivalent to the
\infty
-categorical localization of
cdga^{gr}
, the latter of which is by definition the
\infty
-category of graded mixed algebras.
In conclusion, we have canonical equivalences of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories:

dg^{gr}\simeq\textup{QCoh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})

cdga^{gr}\simeq\textup{CAlg}(\textup{QCoh}(B\mathbb{G}_{m}))

To obtain a similar equivalence for graded mixed objects, let us now briefly introduce the group scheme called
\mathcal{H}
, by a definition which is only valid since we are working in characteristic zero.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{m}
and
\mathbb{G}_{a}
denote respectively the multiplicative and additive group schemes. Recall that there is a canonical action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by multiplication, which induces a canonical action of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
on
B\mathbb{G}_{a}
after delooping. Note that in derived algebraic geometry, all these group schemes including
B\mathbb{G}_{a}
are affine and of finite type over
k
.
We will call
\mathcal{H}
the semi-direct product
B\mathbb{G}_{a}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
, i.e. there is a canonical split short exact sequence of abelian group schemes:

1\to B\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\mathcal{H}\leftrightarrows\mathbb{G}_{m}\to 1

Moreover, note that
\mathcal{H}
is thus affine and of finite type over
k
too.

Remark 2.21
.

Consider now absolute graded mixed modules and algebras.
In this context,
\mathcal{H}
-actions are equivalent to graded mixed objects.
More precisely,
\mathcal{H}
is closely related to
S^{1,gr}
in
[
MRT22
]
, in the sense that the usual delooping
B\mathcal{H}
is equivalent to
B_{B\mathbb{G}_{m}}S^{1,gr}
the relative delooping of
S^{1,gr}
in the
\infty
-topos
\textup{dSt}/B\mathbb{G}_{m}
(see
[
Rob24
]
, remark 2.3.65), and thus by the main theorem of
[
MRT22
]
there is a canonical equivalence of monoidal
\infty
-categories
\epsilon-dg^{gr}\simeq\textup{QCoh}(B\mathcal{H})
.
By a similar reasoning as above we obtain the following equivalences of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories:

\epsilon-dg^{gr}\simeq\textup{QCoh}(B\mathcal{H})

\epsilon-cdga^{gr}\simeq\textup{CAlg}(\textup{QCoh}(B\mathcal{H}))

We will now refine these equivalences to the relative cases, i.e.
cdga^{gr}(B)
and
\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)
.

Remark 2.22
.

For the graded case, consider first the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\textup{Spec}(B)
: its quotient is
[\textup{Spec}(B)/\mathbb{G}_{m}]=\textup{Spec}(B)\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}
, and similarly as before we have symmetric monoidal equivalences:

dg^{gr}(B)\simeq\textup{QCoh}(\textup{Spec}(B)\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})

cdga^{gr}(B)\simeq\textup{CAlg}(\textup{QCoh}(\textup{Spec}(B)\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}))

For the graded mixed case, let us first introduce an auxiliary derived stack.

Definition 2.23
.

We will call graded loops on
\textup{Spec}(B)
relative to
\textup{Spec}(A)
the mapping stack introduced in
[
MRT22
]
:

\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)=\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{\textup{Spec}(A)}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\textup{Spec}(A),\textup{Spec}(B))

It comes equipped with a canonical
\mathcal{H}
-action by acting on the source (more precisely,
B\mathbb{G}_{a}
acts on the source, and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts on
B\mathbb{G}_{a}
, which induces the
\mathcal{H}
-action), and a canonical map
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)\to\textup{Spec}(B)
by evaluation at the base point of
B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times\textup{Spec}(A)
over
\textup{Spec}(A)
.
Moreover, when both
\textup{Spec}(B)
and
\textup{Spec}(A)
are equipped with the trivial
\mathcal{H}
-actions, we have that:

1.

The induced map
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)\to\textup{Spec}(A)
is
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant.

2.

The canonical map
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)\to\textup{Spec}(B)
is not
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant, but it is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant for the induced actions.

Finally, by the main theorem of
[
MRT22
]
, since we are in the affine case in characteristic zero, there is a canonical equivalence of derived stacks over
\textup{Spec}(B)
:

\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)\simeq\textup{Spec}_{B}(\mathbf{DR}(B/A))

where
\mathbf{DR}(B/A)
is the (graded mixed)
B
-algebra introduced in
2.8
.

Remark 2.24
.

Note that since
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)
is relatively affine over the affine
\textup{Spec}(B)
, it is affine in this case, and moreover the unique map to the point is also
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant since it was already the case over
\textup{Spec}(A)
, and
\textup{Spec}(A)\to*
is
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant for the trivial actions.
It follows that the map
[\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}]\to B\mathcal{H}
on quotient stacks is relatively affine, and so there is a canonical symmetric monoidal equivalence:

\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}])\simeq\mathbf{DR}(B/A)-\textup{Mod}(\textup{QCoh}(B\mathcal{H}))

which promotes to the following equivalences:

\epsilon-dg^{gr}(B/A)\simeq\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}])

\epsilon-cdga^{gr}(B/A)\simeq\textup{CAlg}(\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}]))

Remark 2.25
.

We can give a similar geometric description to the forgetful functor
-^{gr}:\epsilon-dg^{gr}(B/A)\to dg^{gr}(B)
.
On the algebraic side, recall by
2.13
that we have defined the following
\infty
-categories and forgetful functors:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\epsilon-dg^{gr}(B/A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{DR}(B/A)-Mod(dg^{gr})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{dg^{gr}(B)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cancel{\epsilon}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-^{gr}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces-^{B}}

On the geometric side, note that the canonical map
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)\to\textup{Spec}(B)
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant as stated in
2.23
, and that
\mathbb{G}_{m}
embeds as a subgroup of
\mathcal{H}
by the splitting of the short exact sequence in

, so we can form the following span of quotient stacks:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathbb{G}_{m}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{Spec}(B)\times\mathbb{G}_{m}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

Recall that we also have the following equivalences:

•

dg^{gr}(B)\simeq\textup{QCoh}(\textup{Spec}(B)\times B\mathbb{G}_{m})
(see
2.22
)

•

\epsilon-dg^{gr}(B/A)\simeq\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}])
(see
2.24

•

\mathbf{DR}(B/A)-Mod(dg^{gr})\simeq\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathbb{G}_{m}])
(similarly)

Finally, note that under those equivalences, the functor
\cancel{\epsilon}
corresponds to
\pi^{*}:\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathcal{H}])\to\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathbb{G}_{m}])
, and that the functor
-^{B}
corresponds to
p_{*}:\textup{QCoh}([\mathcal{L}^{gr}(B/A)/\mathbb{G}_{m}])\to\textup{QCoh}(\textup{Spec}(B)\times\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

3
Foliations as equivariant perfect linear stacks over graded loop stacks

Compared to the previous section, in the present one we will let
Z=\textup{Spec}(B)
be an affine derived stack over an affine base
X=\textup{Spec}(A)
, and replace all occurrences of
B
(resp.
A
) by
Z
(resp.
X
) in the notations of the previous section.

Our goal in this section is to describe the
\infty
-category of
X
-linear derived foliations over
Z
as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant derived stacks over
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
, the graded loop stack of
Z
over

. We will then globalize this result to the case where
Z\to X
is a relative Deligne-Mumford stack.
In order to prove it, we will follow a strategy similar to
[

]
: we will construct an adjunction between
X
-linear graded-mixed algebras over
Z
and the latter
\infty
-category, show that it is fully faithful, and describe its essential image.
In

, we will use the latter description of foliations to define their push-forward, as a restriction of the push-forward of stacks along a map

satisfying extra conditions, after showing that it does preserve foliations.

Reminder 3.13

Consider a diagram of

Conversely, given a left transformation

The operations

Remark 3.27

Moreover, the following square is canonically commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{F}ol(Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z^{\prime}}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime})-\mathcal{H}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Spec}^{\epsilon-gr}_{Z^{\prime}/X^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\textup{Spec}^{\epsilon-gr}_{Z/X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

Which precisely means that the map:

(Z\to X)\mapsto\left(\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\simeq\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}\right)

is contravariant in
Z\to X
.

Remark 3.42
.

Consider now an arbitrary map of derived stacks
Z\to X
.
Starting from the equivalence of
3.40
, and passing through the limit on the
\infty
-category of commutative squares of the form:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

where
U
and
V
are affines, and using the functoriality described in the above corollary, we always obtain an equivalence of
\infty
-categories:

\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{lim}}\mathcal{F}ol(V/U)\simeq\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{lim}}\textup{PerfLinSt}_{V}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V/U)-\mathcal{H}

Moreover, on the left-hand side, by definition it is always the case that:

\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)=\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{lim}}\mathcal{F}ol(V/U)

However, on the right-hand side, it is not always the case that:

\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\overset{?}{=}\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{lim}}\textup{PerfLinSt}_{V}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V/U)-\mathcal{H}

Instead, since it is always the case that both linear stacks and perfect quasi-coherent sheaves are stable by pull-back, we obtain that:

\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{lim}}\textup{PerfLinSt}_{V}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V/U)-\mathcal{H}=\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\left(\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{colim}}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V/U)\right)-\mathcal{H}

This motivates the following definition:

Definition 3.43
.

We call
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X):=\underset{(V\to U)\to(Z\to X)}{\textup{colim}}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V/U)
, where the colimit is taken over commutative squares where
U
and
V
are affines.
It comes equipped with a canonical
\mathcal{H}
-action, and a canonical
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant map
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
which is not an equivalence in general.

Therefore one always has the following equivalence.

Corollary 3.44
.

For
Z\to X
an arbitrary map of derived stacks, there is a canonical equivalence of
\infty
-categories:

\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\simeq\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}

Remark 3.45
.

In
[
BN12
]
, another object called
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
is defined. Note that this is a distinct object, although we expect them to coincide at least in some cases, but we will not need this fact in this paper.

We will now compare
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}
to
\mathcal{L}^{gr}
, with the final goal of proving that they coincide when
Z\to X
is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack in
3.48
.

Propositon 3.46
.

The functor
(Z\to X)\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)
satisfies étale codescent, and is moreover the co-stackification of
(Z\to X)\mapsto\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
. As such, given any étale covering of any map of derived stacks
Z\to X
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\coprod_{i,j}V_{i,j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\coprod_{i}U_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

where all the
U_{i},V_{i,j}
are affines, we can recover:
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)=\textup{colim}_{i,j}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V_{i,j}/U_{i})
.
Moreover, given any map
u:U\to X
where
U
is affine,
u^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)
.

Proof:
The formula defining
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}
is the standard formula for the Kan extension of the functor
\mathcal{L}^{gr}:Arr(\textup{dAff})\to\textup{dSt}
, therefore it suffices to check that this functor satisfies étale codescent on affines. However in this case, for
\textup{Spec}(B)\to\textup{Spec}(A)
such affines, by the main theorem of
[
MRT22
]
, we have a canonical identification of derived stacks:

\mathcal{L}^{gr}(\textup{Spec}(B)/\textup{Spec}(A))=\textup{Spec}(\mathbf{DR}(B/A))

Consider a commutative square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A^{\prime}}}

in which both
A\to A^{\prime}
and
B\to B^{\prime}
are étale, then by definition of étale we have an equivalence
\mathbf{DR}(B/A)\otimes_{B}B^{\prime}\simeq\mathbf{DR}(B^{\prime}/A^{\prime})
of
B^{\prime}
-algebras, i.e. the canonical map
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(\textup{Spec}(B^{\prime})/\textup{Spec}(A^{\prime}))\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(\textup{Spec}(B)/\textup{Spec}(A))
is an equivalence, so
\mathcal{L}^{gr}
satisfies étale codescent.
As a result, the functor
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}
is a Kan extension of a functor satisfying étale codescent, so it satisfies étale codescent too, and the other assertions of this proposition follow from this fact.
\Box

We want to use this property to show that, when
Z\to X
is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack, the canonical map
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
is an equivalence, which we want to reduce to the case where
X
is affine and
Z
is a derived Deligne-Mumford stack. The next proposition will essentially enable us to do so.

Propositon 3.47
.

Let
Z\to X
be any map of derived stacks, and
u:U\to X
any map of derived stacks. Then
u^{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
as derived stacks over
U
.

Proof:
Let
T\to U\in\textup{dSt}/U
, we have the following chain of equivalences:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Map}_{U}(T,u^{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Map}_{U}(T,u^{*}\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{X}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times X,Z))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(u_{!}T,\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{X}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times X,Z))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(u_{!}T\times_{X}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times X),Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(u_{!}(T\times_{U}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times U)),Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{U}(T\times_{U}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times U),u^{*}Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{U}(T,\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{U}(B\mathbb{G}_{a}\times U,u^{*}Z))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Map}_{U}(T,\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U))

Being natural in
T
, this equivalence proves that
u^{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
. Note that this equivalence is also
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant, although we will not need this fact.
\Box

To conclude this section, we will show the following theorem.

Theorem 3.48
.

Assuming
Z\to X
is a relative Deligne-Mumford stack, the canonical map:

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)

is an equivalence.

Proof:
To check that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
is an equivalence, it suffices to check that the pull-back of this map along any map
u:U\to X
is an equivalence, where
U
is affine.
Let
u:U\to X
be such a map, we will now show that the resulting map after pull-back along
u
is the canonical map
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
. Notice that by assumption, since
Z\to X
is relative derived Deligne-Mumford,
u^{*}Z
is a derived Deligne-Mumford stack, so we will essentially be reduced to proving the statement in the case where
X
is a affine and
Z
is a derived Deligne-Mumford stack.
As in
3.46
, we already know that
u^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\simeq\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)
, and moreover
u^{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
by
3.47
.

Finally, the fact that the following square commutes comes from the fact that
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}
is the Kan extension of
\mathcal{L}^{gr}
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u^{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

In particular, as explained above, it is sufficient to show that the canonical map
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
is an equivalence to conclude.
Since
u^{*}Z
is now a derived Deligne-Mumford stack, there exists an étale effective epimorphism
v:V\to u^{*}Z
where
V
is a derived scheme.
Let us note that by
3.46
, we have the following pull-back diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(V/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{u^{*}Z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}

By pull-back, the top map is an étale epimorphisms too. In particular, if we denote by
V^{\bullet}
the simplicial nerve of
v:V\to u^{*}Z
, we obtain that the following square is commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(u^{*}Z/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{colim }\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(V^{\bullet}/U)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\textup{colim }\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V^{\bullet}/U)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the bottom map is an equivalence since each
\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(V^{i}/U)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V^{i}/U)
is an equivalence because the
V^{i}
are derived schemes and the result has been shown in this case in
[
BN12
]
, Lemma 4.2.
As a consequence, the top map is an equivalence if and only if the map on the right, i.e.
\textup{colim }\mathcal{L}^{gr}(V^{\bullet}/U)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(u^{*}Z/U)
, is an equivalence, which is the case by
[
Fu+25
]
, theorem 2.11. In conclusion, the initial canonical map is an equivalence:

\widetilde{\mathcal{L}^{gr}}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)

\Box

To finish this section, note that combining this result with
3.44
, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 3.49
.

If
Z\to X
is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack, then there is a canonical equivalence of
\infty
-categories:

\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\simeq\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}

4
Push-forward of perfect linear stacks and foliations

Let
Z\to X
be a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack. Let us consider a map
f:X\to Y
along which we will push-forward. Our goal in this section is to construct a functor
f_{*,\mathcal{F}ol}:\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\to\mathcal{F}ol(f_{*}Z/Y)
under some assumptions on
f
, and use it to obtain in particular a derived foliation on some mapping stacks when the target comes equipped with one.

Our plan to construct
f_{*,\mathcal{F}ol}:\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\to\mathcal{F}ol(f_{*}Z/Y)
is to use
3.49
, so we will consider that an object of
\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)
is an
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant stack
\mathbb{V}(E)\to\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
where
E\in\textup{Perf}(Z)
, and to notice that this data is mapped by the functor
f_{*}:\textup{dSt}/X\to\textup{dSt}/Y
to
f_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)\to f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\to f_{*}Z
. In this section, we will prove that
f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(f_{*}Z/Y)
, that
f_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)
is perfect linear over
f_{*}Z
when assuming that
f
is proper schematic and local complete intersection, and finally that
f_{*}Z\to Y
is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford map when adding that
f
is moreover flat, thus by
3.49
we have that
f_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)
corresponds to a derived foliation over
f_{*}Z
relative to
Y
, which yields
1.5
.

Finally, applying this theorem to
1.6
will yield the main
1.1
on mapping stacks.

Let us now follow the steps described above to construct the push-forward of foliations, starting with two remarks on the push-forward along
f
of stacks over
Z
.

Definition 4.1
.

We will denote by:

f^{Z}_{*}:\textup{dSt}/Z\to\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z

the functor mapping a derived stack
T\to Z
, viewed as a map in
\textup{dSt}/X
, to the map
f_{*}T\to f_{*}Z
, providing an object in
\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z
.

Remark 4.2
.

The preceding functor is right adjoint to the functor mapping a derived stack
T^{\prime}\to f_{*}Z
to the composite
f^{*}T^{\prime}\to f^{*}f_{*}Z\to Z
, where the map we will call
ev:f^{*}f_{*}Z\to Z
is the co-unit of
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}:\textup{dSt}/Y\rightleftarrows\textup{dSt}/X
.

This left adjoint will be denoted by:

f_{Z}^{*}:\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z\to\textup{dSt}/Z

Corollary 4.3
.

As a right adjoint, the functor
f^{Z}_{*}:\textup{dSt}/Z\to\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z
commutes with limits and thus has a canonical monoidal structure.

After establishing these results on
f^{Z}_{*}
, the next step in our construction consists in providing a canonical equivalence between
f^{Z}_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
and
\mathcal{L}^{gr}(f_{*}Z/Y)
. Note that graded loop stacks come equipped with canonical
\mathcal{H}
-actions, and since
f^{Z}_{*}
is monoidal,
f^{Z}_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
also inherits an
\mathcal{H}
-action. Therefore, it makes sense to further prove that this canonical map is an equivalence of
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant stacks over
f_{*}Z
, which we will need later on for our construction.

Lemma 4.4
.

There is a canonical identification
f^{Z}_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)\simeq\mathcal{L}^{gr}(f_{*}Z/Y)
as
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant stacks over
f_{*}Z
.

Proof:
We will start by comparing their functors of points simply as stacks over
Y
, where
f^{Z}_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
simply becomes
f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
. Let
T\in\textup{dSt}/Y
, we thus have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Map}_{Y}(T,f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(f^{*}T,\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Map}_{X}(f^{*}T,\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{X}(X\times S^{1,gr},Z))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(f^{*}T\times_{X}(X\times S^{1,gr}),Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(f^{*}T\times_{X}f^{*}(Y\times S^{1,gr}),Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{X}(f^{*}(T\times_{Y}(Y\times S^{1,gr})),Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{Y}(T\times_{Y}(Y\times S^{1,gr}),f_{*}Z)

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{Y}(T,\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{Y}(Y\times S^{1,gr},f_{*}Z))

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Map}_{Y}(T,\mathcal{L}^{gr}(f_{*}Z/Y))

Now, assume that
T
is moreover equipped with the structure of an
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant derived stack over
f_{*}Z
, and let us fix some map
T\to f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
of
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant stacks over
f_{*}Z
. It then suffices to notice that each step of the preceding chain of equivalences of mapping spaces takes the fixed map
T\to f_{*}\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)
to a map of
\mathcal{H}
-equivariant stacks over
f_{*}Z
or over
Z
depending on the step, but this follows solely from unfolding the definitions involved in each step.
\Box

The next step in our construction now consists in proving that, assuming at some point that
f
is proper schematic and local complete intersection, and given
E\in\textup{Perf}(Z)
, the push-forward of
\mathbb{V}(E)\to Z\to X
along
f
is still perfect linear over
f_{*}Z
.
The main difficulty in this situation is that the universal property of
f_{*}
applies after forgetting the structural map
f_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)\to f_{*}Z
along
f_{*}Z\to Y
, while the universal property of linear stacks depends on viewing them over
f_{*}Z
. Therefore, in order to prove our statement, we will first prove that
f^{Z}_{*}
actually coincides with another functor having a universal property over
f_{*}Z
. Later on, we will apply this description to perfect linear stacks while assuming that
f
is proper schematic and local complete intersection to compute the functor of points of
f_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)
over
f_{*}Z
and show that it also corresponds to a perfect linear stack.

More precisely, consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{*}f_{*}Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces ev}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

where
ev:f^{*}f_{*}Z\to Z
is the co-unit of the adjunction
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

In the above situation, the functor
f^{Z}_{*}:\textup{dSt}/Z\to\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z
coincides with the composition
\pi_{*}\circ ev^{*}
.

Proof:
Note that
f^{Z}_{*}
is right adjoint to
f_{Z}^{*}
, while
\pi_{*}\circ ev^{*}
is right adjoint to
ev_{!}\circ\pi^{*}
, so it suffices to check that these left adjoints coincide to conclude. Inspecting the definition of
f_{Z}^{*}
, we can immediately rewrite it as the composite
ev_{!}\circ f_{f^{*}f_{*}Z}^{*}
, where
f_{f^{*}f_{*}Z}^{*}:\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z\to\textup{dSt}/f^{*}f_{*}Z
is the functor mapping a stack
T\to f_{*}Z
to
f^{*}T\to f^{*}f_{*}Z
using the functoriality of
f^{*}
. Therefore, it actually only suffices to check that
f_{f^{*}f_{*}Z}^{*}
coincides with
\pi^{*}
.
Let thus
T\in\textup{dSt}/f_{*}Z
. Consider the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi^{*}T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{*}f_{*}Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}Z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

As a pasting of two cartesian squares, the exterior square is also cartesian, which precisely means that
\pi^{*}T\simeq f^{*}T
as derived stacks over
f^{*}f_{*}Z
, as required.
\Box

Let us now introduce the following definition and lemmas.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\phi:U\to V
be a proper schematic and local complete intersection map of derived stacks, and
E\in\textup{Perf}(U)
. Note that by proper schematic we mean a map
U\to V
such that the pull-back along any derived affine
\textup{Spec}(A)\to V
is a proper derived scheme
U_{A}\to\textup{Spec}(A)
. In particular,
\phi
is a assumed to be a relative derived scheme.
Then, by
[
Toë12
]
,
\phi_{*}:\textup{QCoh}(U)\to\textup{QCoh}(V)
preserves perfect objects, and thus for
E\in\textup{Perf}(U)
we can set:

\phi_{+}E=\phi_{*}(E^{\vee})^{\vee}\in\textup{Perf}(V),

where
-^{\vee}
denotes the dual of a perfect sheaf.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that
\phi_{+}
canonically promotes to a covariant functor
\textup{Perf}(U)\to\textup{Perf}(V)
, which is moreover left adjoint to
\phi^{*}:\textup{Perf}(V)\to\textup{Perf}(U)
. As in
[
Toë12
]
, also note that
\phi_{*}
is the restriction of the push-forward of
\mathcal{O}_{U}
-modules to
\mathcal{O}_{V}
-modules (which preserves quasi-coherent sheaves in this case), and thus it satisfies the base-change formula.

Lemma 4.8
.

In the above setting, there is a canonical equivalence
\phi_{*}\mathbb{V}(E)\simeq\mathbb{V}(\phi_{+}E)
as stacks over
V
.

Proof:
We will compare their functors of points as stacks over
V
. Let
v:T\to V
be any map of derived stacks. We can form the following pull-back diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\phi^{*}T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces v}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

Freely using the fact that pull-back functors of quasi-coherent sheaves are monoidal and thus commute with
-^{\vee}
, we then have:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Map}_{V}(T,\phi_{*}\mathbb{V}(E))

\displaystyle\simeq\mathbf{Map}_{U}(\phi^{*}T,\mathbb{V}(E))

\displaystyle=\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(\phi^{*}T)}(u^{*}E,\mathcal{O}_{\phi^{*}T})

\displaystyle\simeq\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(\phi^{*}T)}(\mathcal{O}_{\phi^{*}T},u^{*}(E^{\vee}))

\displaystyle\simeq\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(\phi^{*}T)}(p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{T},u^{*}(E^{\vee}))

\displaystyle\simeq\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(T)}(\mathcal{O}_{T},p_{*}u^{*}(E^{\vee}))\ \ (*)

\displaystyle\simeq\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(T)}(\mathcal{O}_{T},v^{*}\phi_{*}(E^{\vee})),\textup{ using the base-change formula}

\displaystyle\simeq\textup{Hom}_{\textup{QCoh}(T)}(v^{*}(\phi_{+}E),\mathcal{O}_{T})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Map}_{V}(T,\mathbb{V}(\phi_{+}E))

Moreover, this equivalence is natural in
T\to V
, which concludes the proof.
\Box

Remark 4.9
.

This statement can be interpreted in the context of
3.19
, where conditions on a map were compared to certain canonical adjunction squares to be Beck-Chevalley. In this remark, on the side of derived stacks, only the adjunction
f_{!}\dashv f^{*}
was considered, while on this side there is always a canonical triple
f_{!}\dashv f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
. This is because, in general, there is no corresponding triple on the side of quasi-coherent sheaves, and only
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
can be considered on that side. However, in the above situation, the functor
\phi_{+}
does fit in a similar triple
\phi_{+}\dashv\phi^{*}\dashv\phi_{*}
, and the above statement can be interpreted as a proof that the appropriate extra canonical square that exists in this situation is in fact Beck-Chevalley too, at least when restricted to symmetric algebras. We will neither formally state nor prove this fact in this paper as the above result suffices.

In the same context as above, the equivalence

\mathcal{O}_{\phi^{*}\textup{Spec}(A)}

The definition of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\phi^{*}\textup{Spec}(A)}}

Lemma 4.13

Let

Let us now consider the truncation:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t_{0}(Z)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t_{0}(f_{*}Z)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t_{0}(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{t_{0}(Y)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{0}(f)}

is a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection map of (non-derived) stacks, so we can apply

is schematic and flat, let us now show that

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{Map}_{\textup{St}}(\textup{Spec}(A),t_{0}(f_{*}Z))

Combining these results, we obtain our push-forward of derived foliations.

1.5

Let

be a map of derived stacks which is a relative derived Deligne-Mumford stack, and

a proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection map of derived stacks.
Then, there is a push-forward functor:

f_{*,\mathcal{F}ol}:\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\to\mathcal{F}ol(f_{*}Z/Y)

Proof:
Applying
4.12
, we have a functor:

f_{*}:\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}\to\textup{PerfLinSt}_{f_{*}Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(f_{*}Z/Y)-\mathcal{H}

Moreover, by

\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\simeq\textup{PerfLinSt}_{Z}/\mathcal{L}^{gr}(Z/X)-\mathcal{H}

f_{*,\mathcal{F}ol}:\mathcal{F}ol(Z/X)\to\mathcal{F}ol(f_{*}Z/Y)

\Box

Remark 4.14
.

Being a push-forward, we can wonder whether this functor is a right adjoint. More precisely, remember that this functor is essentially the application of

, which is right adjoint to the functor

, so we can ask if this left adjoint also preserves perfect linear stacks and
\mathcal{H}
-actions and defines a left adjoint to

. Note that the pull-back of derived foliations as defined in
[
TV25
]
does not apply in this context and this would thus be a different construction, since there is no canonical map

making a commutative square with the existing maps

,

and
f_{*}Z\to Y
. However, it is not the case that

preserves neither perfect linear stacks nor their

it can be rewritten as

, where

is the projection, and

is the evaluation map (the co-unit of the adjuction

always preserves this structure, we do not expect

Remark 4.16
.

While we have provided a description of the tangent and thus also of the cotangent complex of a derived foliation induced by
1.1
or
1.5
, we do not provide a description of the induced mixed differential on the corresponding graded mixed algebra. It should be noted first that the mixed differential comes from the
\mathcal{H}
-action on the perfect linear stack corresponding to the induced derived foliation, so the mixed differential would be computed via this action. However, it should also be noted that the explicit computation of mixed differentials for derived foliations is very difficult, as mentioned in
[
TV25
]
. This is because the graded mixed algebra of a derived foliation is only quasi-isomorphic to the symmetric algebra of its cotangent. As such, while the mixed differential of the graded mixed algebra itself may have an explicit description, its homotopical transport along the quasi-isomorphism only induces a weaker structure on the symmetric algebra of its cotangent. This structure is not the data of a differential whose square is equal to zero, but the data of a differential equipped with a homotopy from its square to zero, and moreover with infinitely many higher homotopy coherences. The explicit description of this homotopical data is outside the scope of this paper, but would be required to compute the resulting differential on the derived foliations induced by our theorems. Nonetheless, it is often the case that derived foliations first arise from their tangent bundles rather than their cotangent bundles, in which case the differential on the cotangent bundle corresponds to the dual of the Lie bracket on the tangent of the derived foliation, which moreover often is the restriction of the Lie bracket of the ambient space to this tangent bundle. In those cases, this provides an intuitive description of the mixed differential.

5.1
Applications

Let us start by providing more details to

Reminder 5.1
.

As a first application, let us consider the moduli stacks of pre-stable and stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points. We take the following constructions and properties from
[

]

•

There is a derived stack
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
, called the derived moduli stack of pre-stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points, that comes equipped with a universal family
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}_{g,n}^{pre}
defined over
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
, whose classical truncations coincide with the usual
\mathbf{M}_{g,n}
and

respectively.

•

As such, for

\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X)
as the following derived mapping stack, canonically defined over
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
:

\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X):=\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}}(\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}_{g,n}^{pre},X\times\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre})

Note that points of this stack morally correspond to families of pre-stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points in
X

•

Moreover,
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X)
contains an open sub-stack
\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)
, which constitutes similarly the derived moduli stack of families of stable curves of genus
g
with
n
marked points on
X
, and whose classical truncation also coincides with the usual
\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)

•

Finally, note that the map
\mathbb{R}\mathcal{C}_{g,n}^{pre}\to\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
is proper schematic (i.e. representable by proper derived schemes), flat, and local complete intersection.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth (possibly derived) projective scheme equipped with a derived foliation. Then
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X)
and
\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)
inherit derived foliations.

Proof:
The derived foliation on
X
induces one on
X\times\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}
by pull-back along the projection to
X
. The derived foliation on
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{M}_{g,n}^{pre}(X)
is then obtained as a direct application of
1.1
to this derived foliation, and it induces one on
\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)
by restriction (i.e. pull-back) along the inclusion.
\Box

Remark 5.3
.

In the study of Gromov-Witten invariants, where the new data of a derived foliation could lead to the definition of new invariants relative to it, the space
\mathbb{R}\overline{\mathbf{M}}_{g,n}(X)
is typically considered when
X
is a smooth projective scheme, which is why this result was stated in this case. While our construction would also apply when
X
is a derived Deligne-Mumford stack, Gromov-Witten invariants on such an
X
can involve different definitions and constructions, which we will not discuss in this paper.

Reminder 5.4
.

As a second application, let us now rephrase the construction of
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{Hilb}^{lci}(Y)
from
[
CK02
]
in terms of mapping stacks. Let
Sch_{prop,flat,lci}:\textup{dAff}^{\textup{op}}\to\infty-Grpd
be the functor defined by:

Sch_{prop,flat,lci}(S):=\{\textup{derived schemes }Z\to S\textup{ that are proper, flat and lci over }S\}

This functor is a derived stack, and moreover it is equipped with a universal object
\mathcal{S}\to Sch_{prop,flat,lci}
which is a relative derived scheme, proper schematic, flat, and local complete intersection. Let
Y
be a derived Deligne-Mumford stack, and consider the derived stack:

\underline{\mathbf{Map}}_{Sch_{prop,flat,lci}}(\mathcal{S},Y\times Sch_{prop,flat,lci})

Note that if
Y

5.2
, this derived stack inherits a derived folation. Moreover, for a derived affine
S

S

S
-scheme
Z

S

S
-points consist in those

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
Y
be a derived Deligne-Mumford stack. Then the derived moduli stack of closed derived subschemes of
Y
that are local complete intersection over their base, denoted by
\mathbb{R}\mathbf{Hilb}^{lci}(Y)

As mentioned in the introduction, we expect fixed points of this derived foliation to correspond to closed derived subschemes of
Y
contained in the algebraic leaves of its derived foliation, but a rigorous proof of this result is outside the scope of this paper. More generally, these two examples of applications can be thought of as illustrations of the transfer of derived foliations to a wide class of derived moduli stacks.

5.2

Let us now talk about future work following this paper. In an upcoming paper, we will define the notion of shifted symplectic structures on a derived foliation
\mathcal{F}
, and derived foliations equipped with such structures will be called shifted symplectic derived folations. As expected, they correspond to shifted symplectic forms on
\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{F}}
the cotangent of the derived foliation as in

be a map of derived stacks which is a relative derived Artin stack equipped with an orientation of degree

The weak-type (1,1) estimate of the ℋ \mathcal{H} -Harmonic Bergman projection
Kenan Zhang

Abstract
. In this note, the author recalls the Calderon-Zygmund theory on the unit ball and derives the weak (1,1) boundedness of the projection for ℋ \mathcal{H} -harmonic Bergman space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
42B20,31C05,46E22.

1.
Introduction

For
n\geqslant 2
, let
\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|<1\}
be the unit ball and
\partial\mathbb{B}_{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x|=1\}
be the unit sphere, where
|x|=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{2})^{1/2}
is the standard Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
The hyperbolic ball is the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
equipped with the hyperbolic metric

ds^{2}=\frac{4}{(1-|x|^{2})^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}dx_{i}^{2}.

For
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
, the Möbius transformation
\varphi_{a}
exchanging
a
and
0
in
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is the following self-map on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
:

\varphi_{a}(x)=\frac{a|x-a|^{2}+(1-|a|^{2})(a-x)}{|x-a|^{2}+(1-|a|^{2})(1-|x|^{2})}.

The Laplacian operator
\Delta_{h}
with respect to the hyperbolic metric is the linear operator
such that

(1.1)

(\Delta_{h}f)(a)=\Delta(f\circ\varphi_{a})(0)

for all
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
and for each
a\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
This operator is also known as the Laplace-Beltrami operator or the invariant Laplace operator. By calculation, it is easy to show that

(\Delta_{h}f)(a)=(1-|a|^{2})^{2}\Delta f(a)+2(n-2)(1-|a|^{2})\langle a,\nabla f(a)\rangle,

where

\Delta=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{n}^{2}},\quad\nabla=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\cdots,\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}}\right)

denote the standard Euclidean Laplacian and gradient operators, respectively.

We say that a complex-valued function
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
is
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic if
(\Delta_{h}f)(x)=0
for all
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
. The linear space of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}_{n})
. For further background and basic properties of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions see
[
29
]
and
[
27
]
.

Let
\nu
be the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
normalized so that
\nu(\mathbb{B}_{n})=1.

For
1\leqslant p<\infty
, the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman space
\mathcal{B}^{p}
is the Banach space defined by

\mathcal{B}^{p}=\bigg\{f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{B}_{n}):\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|f(x)|^{p}d\nu(x)<\infty\bigg\}.

It is known that the point evaluation functionals
f\mapsto f(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
are bounded on
\mathcal{B}^{2}

[
29
]
. Therefore,
\mathcal{B}^{2}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. We denote the reproducing kernel by
\mathcal{R}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{B}^{2}
. Then, by the reproducing property, we have

(1.2)

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(y)\overline{\mathcal{R}(x,y)}d\nu(y),\quad\text{for all }f\in\mathcal{B}^{2}.

Since the reproducing kernel
\mathcal{R}(\cdot,\cdot)
is real-valued
[
29
]
, the conjugation in (
1.2
) can be removed. The
\mathcal{H}
-Bergman projection
P:L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)\to\mathcal{B}^{2}
is given by

(Pf)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}f(y)\mathcal{R}(x,y)d\nu(y).

It is straightforward to verify that the
\mathcal{H}
-Bergman projection
P
is well-defined as an integrable operator for each
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
and any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
.

In fact, the invariant Laplace operator can also be defined as (
1.1
) on the Euclidean unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The space of
\mathcal{M}
-harmonic functions, which are annihilated by the invariant Laplace operator on the complex unit ball, has been studied extensively
[
1
,
12
,
21
,
24
]
.
However, functional theory of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic function spaces on the real hyperbolic ball is less well-known.
The concept of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions was first introduced by Jaming in 1999
[
18
]
.
In the same paper, he investigated the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\mathbb{B}_{n}),0<p<\infty,
whose elements are the hyperbolic harmonic extensions of distributions belonging to the Hardy spaces
H^{p}(\partial\mathbb{B}_{n})
of the sphere and obtained an atomic decomposition of these spaces.
In 2003, Liu and Shi studied the invariant mean-value property of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic
[
20
]
.
Furthermore, similar spaces defined for holomorphic functions can also be defined for
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions, such as Hardy-Sobolev spaces, Lipschitz spaces
[
13
]
, and weighted Dirichlet spaces
[
25
]
.
Additionally, Stoll obtained the reproducing kernel of the Hardy space and radial eigenfunctions of the invariant Laplacian
\Delta_{h}

[
26
]
.

This note focuses on the Bergman spaces of
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic functions.
The Bergman projection on reproducing kernel function spaces plays a crucial role in the study of function spaces and their operators.
Among other things, one of the key issues regarding
P
is the study of its Calderón-Zygmund theory, which includes the
L^{p}
-boundedness for all
1\leqslant p\leqslant+\infty
.
In general, related orthogonal projections such as classical Bergman projection on holomorphic and harmonic function space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
are weak-type
(1,1)

[
9
]
, hence, are bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
for
1<p<+\infty
by interpolation.
Indeed, Üreyen
[
29
]
proved that
P
is bounded on
L^{p}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
if and only if
1<p<\infty
and
P:L^{\infty}\to\mathcal{B}
is bounded, where
\mathcal{B}
is the
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bloch space on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, but leaving the case
p=1
open. Here the author proves the weak-type
(1,1)
estimate, closing the gap.

Theorem 1.1
.

The
\mathcal{H}
-harmonic Bergman projection operator
P
is of weak-type (1,1), i.e., there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
f
such that for any
t>0

(1.3)

(Pf)_{*}(t)\leqslant\frac{C}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)},\qquad\text{holds for all $f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)$},

where
f_{*}(t)=\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|f(x)|>t\})

is the distribution function of
f
.

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(or
\mathbb{C}^{n}
), one can see the harmonic (or holomorphic) Bergman spaces
\mathcal{B}^{2}(\Omega)
(or
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
) are reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces with kernel
K_{\Omega}(x,y)
(or
k_{\Omega}(z,w)
).
Denote the harmonic Bergman projection by
P_{\Omega}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}^{2}(\Omega)
and

P_{\Omega}f(x)=\int_{\Omega}f(y)K_{\Omega}(x,y)dy

for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)

(or the holomorphic Bergman projection
P_{\Omega}:L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
and

P_{\Omega}f(z)=\int_{\Omega}f(z)k_{\Omega}(z,w)dw

for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.)

Since
P_{\Omega}
is an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{B}^{2}(\Omega)
(or
\mathcal{B}_{a}^{2}(\Omega)
), it is a bounded operator on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Unlike the case of the unit ball, where the Bergman projection is
L^{p}
-bounded for all
1<p<\infty
, determining the range
1<p_{\Omega}\leqslant q_{\Omega}<\infty
for which
P_{\Omega}
is
L^{p}
-bounded on a general domain
\Omega
is a nontrivial question. By interpolation and duality, one finds that
1/p_{\Omega}+1/q_{\Omega}=1
. Therefore, the
L^{p}
-boundedness problem of the Bergman projection for a given domain
\Omega
reduces to determining the exponent
p_{\Omega}\in(1,2]
such that
P_{\Omega}
extends continuously to
L^{p}(\Omega)
for all
p\in(p_{\Omega},p_{\Omega}^{\prime})
, where
p_{\Omega}^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p_{\Omega}
.

For a class of domains in complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, Solovyov characterizes the boundedness of the Bergman projection on
L^{p}(\Omega)
by geometric properties of the boundary of
\Omega

[
22
,
23
]
, which are automatically fulfilled for
p\in(\frac{4}{3},4)
.
For a simply connected domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{C}
, by conformal mapping, Hedenmalm gives a positive answer for some
p_{0}\in[\frac{4}{3},2)

[
15
]
.
Here
p_{0}
is independent of
\Omega
.
Edholm and McNeal extend Hedenmalm’s result to some
p_{\Omega}
for generalized Hartogs triangles, which are pseudoconvex but non-smooth domains in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

[
10
,
11
]
.
Specifically, Chakrabarti and Zeytuncu show
p_{\Omega}=\frac{4}{3}
when
\Omega
is the classical Hartogs triangle
[
8
]
.
For higher dimensions, such continuous extension of the Bergman projections also holds on monomial polyhedra in
\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
2
]
.

Recall that the classical holomorphic Bergman projection on
\mathbb{D}
is weak-type
(1,1)
.
One may ask how the Bergman projection
P_{\Omega}
behaves on
L^{p_{\Omega}}(\Omega)
and
L^{p^{\prime}_{\Omega}}(\Omega)
, where
p_{\Omega}
is the endpoint of the bounded range of
P_{\Omega}
.
The works of Huo-Wick
[
16
]
and Christopherson-Koenig
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
show that
P_{\Omega}
is not weak-type
(p,p)
at the lower endpoint of boundedness, while it is weak-type
(p,p)
at the upper endpoint for the classical and generalized Hartogs triangles, respectively.
The mapping behavior of the holomorphic Bergman projection on monomial polyhedra at the lower endpoint the is even worse: there is no way to extend the Bergman projection to
L^{p}(\Omega)
using its integral representation
[
3
, Proposition 8.1]
.
In other words, there exists a function
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
such that the integral does not converge.

A similar phenomenon for the harmonic Bergman projection can be observed when
\Omega
is a punctured smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
In such setting, Koenig and Wang show that
P_{\Omega}
is bounded on
L^{p}(\Omega)
if and only if
\frac{3}{2}<p<3

[
19
]
.
For endpoints,
P_{\Omega}
is weak-type
(3,3)
but is not weak-type
(\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2})
.
This limitation is missing when
n\neq 3
, i.e. the harmonic Bergman projection
P_{\Omega}
on such punctured domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geqslant 4
, is always bounded on
L^{p}(\Omega)
for
1<p<\infty
.
Besides,
P_{\Omega}
is not weak-type
(1,1)
for
n=2
, while
P_{\Omega}
is weak-type
(1,1)
for
n\geqslant 4
.

2.
A dyadic decomposition of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}

The classical Calderón-Zygmund decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
relies on a dyadic system in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is a useful tool for establishing the weak (1,1) boundedness of singular integrals.
Similarly, to estimate
P
, we require a dyadic system of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}_{n}
.
A collection of sets

\mathscr{D}=\{Q_{k,i}\subset\mathbb{B}_{n}:1\leqslant k<\infty,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)\}

is called a dyadic system on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
if

(i)

for each integer
k\geqslant 1
,
\{Q_{k,i}\}_{i=1}^{M(k)}
is a disjoint covering of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
, i.e.,

•

\mathbb{B}_{n}=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{M(k)}Q_{k,i}
and

•

for all
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant M(k),Q_{k,i}\cap Q_{k,j}=\emptyset
.

(ii)

if
1\leqslant k\leqslant l
, then either
Q_{l,j}\subset Q_{k,i}
or
Q_{k,i}\cap Q_{l,j}=\emptyset
.

Let
\rho(x,y)=|x-y|
be the Euclidean metric.
For
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n},\ r>0
, denote

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|x-y|<r\}.

We say the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
is associated with a triple of positive constants
(\eta,\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1})
if there exists a point set

\mathcal{P}=\{x_{k,i}\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:1\leqslant k<\infty,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)\}

such that

B(x_{k,i},\kappa_{0}\eta^{k})\subset Q_{k,i}\subset B(x_{k,i},\kappa_{1}\eta^{k})

for each
k\geqslant 1,1\leqslant i\leqslant M(k)
.
Here
x_{k,i}
is called the center of the cube
Q_{k,i}
.
Denote
B(x_{k,i},\kappa_{1}\eta^{k})
by
B(Q_{k,i})
for each
k
and
i
.
By Hytönen and Kairema’s result (
[
17
, Theorem 4.1]
), we have the following result on metric space
(\mathbb{B}_{n},\rho)
:

Theorem 2.1
.

There is a dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
associated with the triple
(1/96,1/12,4)
on
(\mathbb{B}_{n},\rho)
.

Remark 2.1
.

We observe that for each
Q_{k,i}
there exists a collection of sets
\{Q_{k+1,j}\}
such that

Q_{k,i}=\bigcup\limits_{j}Q_{k+1,j},

and each such cube
Q_{k+1,j}
is called child of
Q_{k,i}.

Additionally, there exists a constant
C_{1}>0
satisfying

\nu(Q_{k,i})\leqslant C_{1}\nu(Q_{k+1,j})

for each cube
Q_{k,i}\in\mathscr{D}
and its child
Q_{k+1,j}
.

Now we introduce the Calderón-Zygmund decomposition on
\mathbb{B}_{n}
associated with the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
and
t>0
, there exists a decomposition of
\mathbb{B}_{n}
such that

(i)

\mathbb{B}_{n}=F\cup\Omega,
with
F\cap\Omega=\emptyset
;

(ii)

|f(x)|\leqslant t
on
F
almost everywhere;

(iii)

\Omega
can be expressed as a union of pairwise disjoint cubes from the dyadic system
\mathscr{D}
in Theorem
2.1
.
That is, there exists a collection
G
of such cubes such that

\Omega=\bigcup\limits_{Q\in G}Q.

And for each
Q\in G
, we have

t<\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)|d\nu(x)<C_{1}t.

Here,
C_{1}
is the constant in Remark
2.1
and independent of
f
and
t
.

For the proof, one can see
[
28
, §1, 3.2]
or
[
14
, §5.3]
.

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

For a fixed
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
, the inequality (
1.3
) holds for all
t\leqslant||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}
since

(Pf)_{*}(t)=\nu(\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|(Pf)(x)|>t\})\leqslant 1\leqslant\frac{||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}}{t}.

Thus, it suffices to consider the case
t\geqslant||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

For a fixed
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu),
we decompose
f
into a “good” function
g
and a “bad” function
b
by Lemma
2.2
. Let

g(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f(x)&&x\in F,\\
&\frac{1}{\nu(Q_{j})}\int_{Q_{j}}f(x)d\nu(x)\quad&&x\in Q_{j}\subset\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then
g
is bounded by Lemma
2.2
. Define
b(x)=f(x)-g(x)
. Then

\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|Pf(x)|>t\}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|Pg(x)|>\frac{t}{2}\}\cup\{x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}:|Pb(x)|>\frac{t}{2}\}.

It suffices to prove

(Pg)_{*}(t)\leqslant\frac{C}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(Pb)_{*}(t)\leqslant\frac{C}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

The estimate of
Pg
:

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle||g||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{F}|g(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)+\int_{\Omega}|g(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant t\int_{F}|f(x)|d\nu+\sum\limits_{Q\in G}\int_{\Omega}\left|\frac{1}{\nu(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)d\nu(x)\right|^{2}\chi_{Q}(x)d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant t||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}+C_{1}t\sum\limits_{Q\in G}\int_{Q}\left|f(x)\right|d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant t||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}+C_{1}t\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant(C_{1}+1)t||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}

Since
P
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)
,
P
is of weak-type (2,2).
Hence, we have

(Pg)_{*}(t)\leqslant\frac{1}{t^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}}|g(x)|^{2}d\nu(x)\leqslant\frac{(C_{1}+1)}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

The estimate of
Pb
:

The following theorem from
[
29
]
is required to estimate the distribution of the function
Pb
.

Theorem 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{2}>0
depending only on
n
such that for all
x,y\in\mathbb{B}_{n}
,

(i)

|\mathcal{R}(x,y)|\leqslant\frac{C_{2}}{[x,y]^{n}},

(ii)

|\nabla_{x}\mathcal{R}(x,y)|\leqslant\frac{C_{2}}{[x,y]^{n+1}}.

Here
[x,y]^{2}=|x-y|^{2}+(1-|y|^{2})(1-|x|^{2})
and
\nabla_{x}
denotes the gradient operator with respect to the variable
x
.

We denote the cubes in
G
by
\{Q_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.
Then
\Omega=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}Q_{j}
.
For
Q_{j}\in G
, let
b_{j}(x)=b(x)\chi_{Q_{j}}(x)
. Then
b(x)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{j}(x)
and

\int_{Q_{j}}b(x)d\nu(x)=0.

For each
j
, set
y_{j}
is the center of the cube
Q_{j}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle|(Pb_{j})(x)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q_{j}}\mathcal{R}(x,y)b(y)d\nu(y)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{Q_{j}}(\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j}))b(y)d\nu(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{Q_{j}}|\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j})||b(y)|d\nu(y)

Recall the notion of
B(Q_{j})
introduced in the discussion at the beginning of Section 2.
Let
B_{j}=2B(Q_{j})
and
\Omega^{\prime}=\bigcup\limits_{j}B_{j}
.
By the definition of
B(Q_{j})
and Lemma
2.2
, there exists a constant
C_{3}
independent of
f
and
t
such that

\nu(\Omega^{\prime})\leqslant\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\nu(B_{j})\leqslant C_{3}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\nu(Q_{j})\leqslant\frac{C_{3}}{t}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{j}}|f(x)|d\nu(x)\leqslant\frac{C_{3}}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

Then we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\Omega^{\prime}}|(Pb)(x)|d\nu(x)\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}|(Pb_{j})(x)|d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{j}}|b(y)|\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}|\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j})|d\nu(x)d\nu(y)

Claim 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C_{4}
depending only on
n
such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}|\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j})|d\nu(x)\leqslant C_{4}

for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

By the mean value theorem and Theorem
3.1
, there exists a point
\bar{y_{j}}
on the straight-line segment connecting
y_{j}
and
y
such that

|\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j})|=|\nabla_{y}\mathcal{R}(x,\bar{y_{j}})||y-y_{j}|\leqslant\frac{C_{2}}{[x,\bar{y_{j}}]^{n+1}}|y-y_{j}|.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}|\mathcal{R}(x,y)-\mathcal{R}(x,y_{j})|d\nu(x)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}\frac{C_{2}}{[x,\bar{y_{j}}]^{n+1}}|y-y_{j}|d\nu(x).

Note that
x\in\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}
and
\bar{y_{j}}\in Q_{j}
, we have

|x-y_{j}|\geqslant 2|y_{j}-\bar{y_{j}}|.

Since

[x,\bar{y_{j}}]^{2}=|x-\bar{y_{j}}|^{2}+(1-|\bar{y_{j}}|^{2})(1-|x|^{2}),

we have

[x,\bar{y_{j}}]\geqslant|x-\bar{y_{j}}|\geqslant|x-y_{j}|-|y_{j}-\bar{y_{j}}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|x-y_{j}|.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash B_{j}}\frac{|y-y_{j}|}{[x,\bar{y_{j}}]^{n+1}}d\nu(x)\leqslant 2^{n+1}\int_{|x-y_{j}|\geqslant 2|y-y_{j}|}\frac{|y-y_{j}|}{|x-y_{j}|^{n+1}}d\nu(x)\leqslant C_{4}

for some constant
C_{4}=C_{4}(n)
.
This completes the proof of the claim.
∎

By (
3.4
) and Claim
3.1
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\Omega^{\prime}}|(Pb)(x)|d\nu(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{4}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{j}}|b(y)|d\nu(y)

\displaystyle=C_{4}\int_{\Omega}|b(y)|d\nu(y).

Since
|b(y)|=|f(y)-g(y)|\leqslant|f(y)|+|g(y)|\leqslant|f(y)|+C_{1}t,
we have

\int_{\Omega}|b(y)|d\nu(y)\leqslant\int_{\Omega}|f(y)|d\nu(y)+C_{1}t\nu(\Omega).

By Lemma
2.2
, we have

\nu(\Omega)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\nu(Q_{j})\leqslant\frac{1}{t}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{Q_{j}}|f(x)|d\nu(x)\leqslant\frac{1}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\Omega^{\prime}}|(Pb)(x)|d\nu(x)\leqslant C_{4}\int_{\Omega}|b(y)|d\nu(y)\leqslant C_{4}(1+C_{1})||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

Moreover,

(Pb)_{*}(t)\leqslant\nu\big((\mathbb{B}_{n}\backslash\Omega^{\prime})\cap\{|(Pb)(x)|>t\}\big)+\nu(\Omega^{\prime})\leqslant\frac{C_{4}(1+C_{1})+C_{3}}{t}||f||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{B}_{n},d\nu)}.

This completes the proof of Theorem

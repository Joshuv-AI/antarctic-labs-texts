The impact of melatonin implants on the fertility of superstimulated donor heifers at subsequent insemination after embryo recovery
Authors: Najmeh Davoodian, Ali Kadivar, Majid Gholami, Fatemeh Nasiri-Dashtaki
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSuperstimulation of ovaries results in oxidative stress (OS), which may disturb the subsequent fertility of donor cows. This research was carried out to evaluate whether melatonin implants could alleviate the effect of OS during the follicular development, subsequent to their ovarian superstimulation program, to maintain the oxidative balance and improve pregnancy outcomes. Twenty-four donor heifers (14 of which had received 18 mg/50 kg melatonin implants) were subjected to blood sampling at their first AI (artificial insemination) after ovarian superstimulation and embryo collection. Total Antioxidant Capacity (TAC), and Total Oxidant Status (TOS) in serum were assayed to calculate the oxidative stress index (OSI), and the fertility outcomes were followed up.ResultsThe interval of Flushing to AI was 24.07 ± 0.68 and 25.55 ± 0.95 (p = 0.202), and that to pregnancy was 47.69 ± 7.71 and 63.11 ± 15.40 (p = 0.638) in treatment and control groups respectively. The rate of First AI pregnancy was 42.85% and 30% (p = 0.678), the Final pregnancy was 85.71% and 80% (p = 1.00), and the mean Services per conception was 1.85 ± 0.25 and 2.1 ± 0.31 (p = 0.51) in treatment and control groups respectively. TAC (mmol/L) was 9.13 ± 0.23 in treatment and 6.94 ± 0.39 in control group (p = 0.00). TOS (µmol/L) was 7.13 ± 1.01 in treatment and 11.78 ± 1.4 in control group (p = 0.01), and OSI (AU) was 0.79 ± 0.11 in treatment and 1.72 ± 0.21 in control group (p = 0.00). TOS in control group exhibited significant positive correlation with Flushing to AI (r = 0.688, p = 0.028), and negative correlation with First AI pregnancy (r =—0.800, p = 0.005).ConclusionsMelatonin implants improved TAC, and decreased TOS and OSI during the first insemination subsequent to the ovarian superstimulation and embryo collection in heifer donors; higher TOS in control group was correlated with greater Flushing to AI and lower First AI pregnancy. However, pregnancy outcomes were not significantly influenced by the treatment.Keywords: Melatonin, Fertility, Superstimulation, Oxidative stress, Cattle

Background

The reproductive efficiency of dairy cattle depends on timely pregnancy, which is correlated directly with the regularity of the estrous cycle in which follicles from the ovarian pool grow, and few ovulate to get fertilized to zygotes. In cattle, primordial follicles require approximately 180 days to reach the ovulatory stage. Small antral follicles develop over 40–50 days (equivalent to about two estrous cycles), during which follicle progresses from the growing stage to the selection and dominance phases to reach preovulatory follicle capable of ovulation [1–3]. Overall development of follicles during this interval (follicle activation to ovulation) is affected by oxidative stress, which causes damage to cellular components, leading to apoptosis, follicular loss, and diminished reproductive potential [4]. In fact, OS compromises fertility through its effects on the ovarian environment during follicle growth phases [5]. This highlights the importance of maintaining oxidative balance, enhancing antioxidant defenses, and managing metabolic stressors to counteract these effects [4].

To mitigate OS, antioxidants have been proposed and evaluated extensively. Antioxidants neutralize excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) and maintain equilibrium with endogenous antioxidant defenses within the follicular microenvironment, thereby ensuring proper follicular and oocyte development [6, 7]. In vitro studies have demonstrated that supplementation with antioxidants such as melatonin, ascorbic acid, resveratrol, and astaxanthin enhances the antioxidant capacity of follicles; improves follicle survival, growth, and the oocyte quality; and alleviates oxidative damage [8–11]. During bovine follicle culture, ascorbic acid protects preantral follicles from oxidative damage [12], and Melatonin enhances follicle growth and alleviates oxidative stress [9].

The superstimulation program typically involves repeated administration of FSH (follicle-stimulating hormone) to induce rapid growth of multiple antral follicles. This elevates metabolic and cellular activity, leading to increased production of ROS in the ovary that exceeds the capacity of natural antioxidant defenses, leading to oxidative stress which impairs proper follicle development and oocyte quality [13, 14]. Moreover, superstimulation results in abnormal steroid concentrations in the microenvironment of the uterine tube, where fertilization and early stages of embryo development take place, thus disturbing normal function [15]. On the other hand, during the superstimulation program, oocytes that are scheduled to ovulate in the next estrus phase are in the growth stage; so, the oxidative status of the animal can affect their proper growth and future quality. Therefore, superstimulation not only affects the present estrous cycle but also influences the next cycles, potentially compromising the subsequent fertility of superstimulated donors, and is very important in the overall reproductive efficiency of the herd. This makes a challenge, since it might even result in the elimination of a high-genetic-value donor. Reducing the negative impacts of superstimulation programs on the fertility of donors is thus critical. However, it is unclear whether an antioxidant supplementation during this process could preserve the normal fertility of donors and hasten their return to estrus for fertilization in their subsequent cycles. In our previous research, we employed melatonin implants just before the start of synchronization and superstimulation program in donor heifers to obtain more transferable embryos [16]; these implants have been shown to remain effective for up to 100 days [17]. Therefore, this study was carried out to evaluate whether these implants could mitigate OS during follicular development in the cycle following the ovarian superstimulation program, maintain the oxidative balance at the time of their first insemination after uterine flushing, and improve their subsequent pregnancy outcomes.

Methods

Animals

This study was conducted on Holstein heifers at Zagros Milk and Meat Co. Shahrekord, Iran, between May and July 2024. The study had previously been approved by the Ethics Committee of the Experimental Animals Production and Research Center at Shahrekord University (Approval Number: IR.SKU.REC.1403.016). Prior to study enrollment, informed consent was obtained from the animal owner to use the heifers in our research.

A total of 24 Holstein heifers (14—14.5 months of age, body weight ranges of 360—440 kg, and uniform height of 137.2 cm) were included in the experiment. These heifers had been previously enrolled in our prior study [16] in which the heifers were divided into a treatment group (n = 14; received 18 mg/50 kg melatonin implants, Regulin®, Ceva Santé Animale, France) and a control group (n = 10, no melatonin implants), followed by a MOET (multiple ovulation and embryo transfer) program.

The synchronization and superstimulation of the donor heifers—in the previous study [16]—was performed using estradiol benzoate (Vetastrol, Aburaihan, Iran; 2 mg, IM), progesterone (vetagestrone, Aburaihan, Iran; 75 mg, IM), CIDR (Vetocidr, Aburaihan, Iran), FSH (Cinalf, Cinagen, Iran; 900 IU, twice daily in a decreasing manner over 4 days, IM), PGF2α (Cloprost, Nasr, Iran; 500 µg, two IM injections 12 h apart), and GnRH injection (Vetarolin, Aburaihan, Iran; 50 µg, IM), followed by AI (artificial insemination) with frozen-thawed sexed sperm (× 3,243,355,618). Transcervical flushing of the uterus was then performend to recover embryos, after which PGF2α (500 µg, IM) was injected to induce luteolysis [16]. Epidural anesthesia was induced by the injection of 80 mg Lidocaine HCL 2% (Vetacaine, Aburaihan, Iran) and 5 mg Xylazine 2% (Xylamax, Rooyandaroo, Iran) at the time of uterine flushing to prevent staining and facilitate the safe passage and precise manipulation of the catheter through the cervix during the procedure. After PGF2α administration—upon completion of the previous study [16]—the heifers were monitored for estrus signs; the first estrus was ignored (no insemination). Approximately 21 days later, at the subsequent estrus, the donors were subjected to AI with frozen-thawed sexed sperm (× 3,243,355,618), with blood collection performed concurrently.

Serum sampling

At the time of AI, collection of blood samples was performed from the coccygeal vein (no anesthesia), subjected to centrifugation at 3000 × g for 10 min, and sera were stored at − 80 °C until analysis.

Assays

Serum samples were subjected to ELISA to quantify the levels of Total Antioxidant Capacity (TAC) and Total Oxidant Status (TOS).

The FRAP (Ferric Reducing Antioxidant Power) method was employed to determine TAC, utilizing Naxifer™-Total Antioxidant Capacity Assay Kit-TAC (Novin Navand Salamat Pishtaz Co., Iran), according to the manufacturer’s provided protocol, by measuring the absorbance of serum samples at a wavelength of 593 nm with a microplate reader, MPANM96 (Nano Mabna Iranian Co.). TAC values were determined and presented in millimoles per liter of ferric ions. The coefficients of variation for intra- and inter-assay measurements were 3.7% and 2.5%, respectively.

TOS was determined using an oxidation reaction in which the quantity of amplifying molecules is proportional to the intensity of produced color in the reaction. For this purpose, Natos™ (Novin Navand Salamat Pishtaz Co., Iran) was employed according to the manufacturer’s provided protocol, and the absorbance of serum samples in the reaction was measured at a wavelength of 530 nm with a microplate reader, MPANM96 (Nano Mabna Iranian Co.); the results were standardized using hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂) and reported as μmol H₂O₂ equivalents per liter. The coefficients of variation for intra- and inter-assay measurements were 4.5% and 3.6%, respectively.

The oxidative stress index (OSI), was calculated as the ratio of TOS (μmol Eq H2O2) to TAC (mmol Fe2 +), and expressed in arbitrary unit (AU) [18, 19].

Statistical analysis

All the statistical analyses were performed with IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27 (IBM, NY, USA), with the significance level set at 0.05. Two time intervals were analyzed; the first interval—Flushing to AI—exhibited a normal distribution, allowing for comparison using an independent samples t-test. However, the second interval—Flushing to pregnancy—did not meet the normality assumption; as a result, the Mann–Whitney U test was employed to compare the distributions between the groups. Two categorical variables—First AI pregnancy and Final pregnancy—were compared between groups using the chi-square test. The values for Services per conception did not meet the normality assumption and the Mann–Whitney U test was employed to compare the means between the groups. To compare the oxidative stress indices (TOS, TAC and OSI), the normal distributions of the variables were confirmed. Therefore, independent samples t-tests were conducted to compare their means. The effects of heifer weight, height, and the TOS, TAC, and OSI concentrations on the First AI pregnancy and Final pregnancy were evaluated using logistic regression, as these outcome variables are categorical. In contrast, the influence of these same factors on continuous outcomes—such as Flushing to AI, and Flushing to pregnancy, was analyzed using linear regression. Because Services per conception is a numerical variable, the Poisson regression model was fitted to assess the predictors of Service per conception and compare the treatment and control groups. Spearman’s rank correlation was also used to evaluate the relationships among all parameters in control and treatment groups separately. Moreover, IBM SPSS Statistics, version 27, and G*Power version 3.1.9.2 were used to calculate the power of our study through inputting the effect sizes, sample sizes, and a significance level of 0.05.

However, in linear regression analysis of the time intervals, weight emerged as a significant predictor (Table 2). The power of study

The results of power analysis for Flushing to pregnancy revealed a study power of 0.071. The power was 0.067 for First AI pregnancy, 0.017 for Final pregnancy, 0.99 for TAC, 0.75 for TOS, and 0.982 for OSI. The significance level was established at 0.05.

Discussion

This study aimed to mitigate the effects of oxidative stress on fertility outcomes in donor heifers at their first insemination following embryo recovery. The results revealed that the heifers in the treatment group presented significantly greater TAC, and lower TOS and OSI than did those in the control group. However, the differences in reproductive parameters, including First AI and Final pregnancy rates, and Services per conception did not achieve statistical significance. It was hypothesized in this study that a superstimulation program results in OS due to increased follicular growth activity and elevated ROS production, which could impair proper follicle development and oocyte quality in subsequent cycles unless attention to antioxidant supplementation is provided. Evaluation of oxidative stress status and pregnancy outcomes at subsequent cycle after uterine flushing revealed that, although the antioxidant treatment improved systemic oxidative status, these biochemical changes were not accompanied by parallel improvements in fertility endpoints in these heifers.

Raising replacement heifers represents a critical task for dairy operations, as these animals constitute the future foundation of the milking herd. However, the heifer rearing period remains financially nonproductive [20]. Therefore, management strategies to shorten generation intervals can significantly enhance the overall profitability of dairy farms. This study investigated the fertility outcomes of heifers that previously had been incorporated in MOET program, a reproductive biotechnology that boosts economic profit through accelerated genetic gain, production of higher-value calves, and efficient herd replacement [21]. Nevertheless, assisted reproductive techniques are associated with reduced reproductive performance in dairy heifers and cows; specifically, embryo donors subjected to MOET programs have been demonstrated to exhibit a reduced hazard of first breeding and pregnancy [22]. Along with this, the heifers of the present study exhibited low First AI pregnancy rates of 42.85% and 30%, which was lower than common pregnancy rate per AI in heifers (approximately 74.4%) [23]. Part of this difference may be attributed to the use of sexed semen in the heifers of this study. However, conception at the first service is a crucial reproductive performance factor in dairy farm profit [24]. A decrease in the first service conception increases the age at first calving, and the number of inseminations required for pregnancy [25, 26]. In this study a high Services per conception was also observed. Generally, value of services per conception exceeding 2.0 is considered indicative of poor reproductive performance in cows [27]. Our results in heifers indicated a serious problem with conception. This study tried to connect it with the oxidative stress, and suggested melatonin implants which is demonstrated to elevate antioxidant enzymes GSH-PX and SOD, and has been accompanied by a higher conception rate in dairy cattle [28], however, although the treatment group required approximately 13% fewer services per conception, this reduction was not statistically significant, suggesting failure of antioxidant supplementation in improving this result. On the other hand, pregnancy failure at first insemination increases the age at first calving, thereby raising rearing costs [29]. This parameter was evaluated in our study through determination of Flushing to pregnancy, which did not differ between groups. It’s clear that several factors determine the optimal age at first calving, including management, growth, health, genetics, and environment [30]. Although the present study utilized a uniform group of high-genetic-merit heifers that maintained under identical environmental and management conditions—with melatonin as the sole variable—the lack of significant difference between groups, despite strict monitoring of health, nutrition, and estrus detection, can arise from biological variability, and study design limitations rather than melatonin ineffectiveness. Factors such as embryonic loss or semen quality can create variabilities in fertility that mask modest treatment effects [30–33]. Moreover, oxidative stress which was focused in this study, is not the sole determining factor on this parameter. In this study, estrus detection was accurately controlled, there were no disease and nutritional deficiencies, the population was uniform in age and weight, and an important contributing factor may be the sperm source. Insemination of heifers in the present study was performed using sexed semen. Lately, use of sexed semen for AI of Holstein heifers has increased, however the overall reproductive efficiency remains suboptimal, and conception rate of lower than 55% of conventional semen has been reported [34, 35]. Sexed semen exhibits reduced lifespan in the female genital tract and produces embryos with lower developmental competence [36, 37]. Additionally, the low sperm count per insemination requires precise timing and careful handling [38], where personnel inadvertence may compromise outcomes. On the other hand, in many dairy herds, the use of sexed semen for heifer insemination is determined primarily by economic considerations and is therefore tightly managed. In the herd where the present study was conducted, heifers are routinely inseminated with sexed semen for the first two inseminations, and unsexed semen is used thereafter if conception has not occurred, due to the high cost of sexed semen. This protocol may introduce substantial variability in pregnancy outcomes, paricularly the Final pregnancy rate, which is not informative in the context of our study because insemination type differed beyond the third AI, and the data of oxidative stress status pertained only to the first insemination.

Upon our results, there was an effect from weight on some reproductive parameters of all the heifers. Heavier heifers tended to achieve pregnancy more quickly, had a higher likelihood of becoming pregnant following the first insemination, and required fewer inseminations to achieve pregnancy. This correlation was evidence in both the treatment and control groups. However, these results are logical because weight is a well-known determinant factor in heifer fertility, and could not be attributed with melatonin implants. Similar significant correlations were also observed between the Flushing to AI or pregnancy and the pregnancy rates or Services per conception.

Another compromising factor on successful pregnancy is oxidative stress, which disrupts proper follicular development, ovulation, and corpus luteum formation [39]. During the follicle growth phase, OS adversely affects the ovarian environment, by hindering the follicle development and the ovulation process [40, 41], impairing the quality and viability of oocytes [42], disrupting steroidogenesis and CL function [4, 43–45], disturbing the environment of the uterine tube and uterus, thereby impairing embryo development and implantation [15]. Collectively, these effects can lead to anovulation, conception failure, and embryo loss, ultimately resulting in pregnancy failure. In our study, the issue of oxidative imbalance resulting from the superstimulation program was considered. In superovulated donor cows, TAC in serum is positively correlated with the quality and count of transferable embryos [46, 47]. Moreover, Melatonin implants has significantly increased TAC in donor heifers, accompanied by a higher transferable embryos [16]. The higher TAC observed in melatonin-treated heifers in this study was expected to support optimal ovarian function and enhance the quality of growing oocytes, especially given the simultaneously lower TOS and OSI was recorded. It is well demonstrated that TOS correlates with follicular development, oocytes quality, and fertilization in donor cows [46]. Notably, higher level of TOS in control group correlated with a longer Flushing to AI, and lower First AI pregnancy. In addition TOS was significantly reduced in the heifers treated with melatonin implants but the mentioned correlations or improved pregnancy outcomes were not observed, despite the previously well-established protective effects of melatonin in alleviating oxidative stress effect on bovine oocytes and embryos [48–50]. The lack of statistically significant differences in these parameters indicated that, although antioxidant intervention boosted overall antioxidant levels, it failed to resolve underlying fertility disorders, such as suboptimal oocyte and embryo quality. This could be attributed to the dependence of fertility on multiple biological factors—such as insemination efficiency—beyond systemic OS markers. Therefore, normalizing OS may not translate into obviously improved pregnancy rates under conditions like those in our study, where variability in inseminations was present.

On the other hand, there was a limitation in this study related to sample size, which reduces the power to detect differences. This study aimed to evaluate oxidative stress status in heifers, where these parameters are highly sensitive to temporal and environmental variations. Therefore, the donor heifers of this study were strictly age-matched, and treatments synchronized over a short period of time to control key confounders such as age-related oocyte yield differences and seasonal estrus fluctuations, resulting in a limited number of eligible animals.​ However, this uniformity enhances statistical power despite the low number, as is evidenced by several studies addressing significant antioxidant effects or antioxidant trials in theriogenology [51–53]. In our study, the power analysis indicated ideal power for TAC and OSI, and good power for TOS. This confirms the reliability of the findings related to oxidative stress indices. However, comparing First AI pregnancy between the treatment and control groups revealed a relatively low power of 6.7%, which is primarily due to the small sample size that limits our ability to detect significant differences; the study is underpowered to confidently identify smaller effects. As a result, this finding should be interpreted with caution, and cannot be considered as a reason for ineffectiveness of melatonin implants. This is also true for Final pregnancy, with a very low power of 1.7%. The small sample size was related to the previously mentioned limitations for inclusion of eligible candidates in the study.

Given the economic importance of MOET in dairy heifers and the adverse effects of oxidative stress on fertility, the authors believe that melatonin implants merit further investigation through comprehensive laboratory evaluations, extending beyond clinical outcomes. Moreover, larger, mechanistically oriented trials are warranted to ascertain whether modulation of oxidative status via melatonin implants in MOET candidate heifers confers substantive fertility benefits. Finally, although melatonin implants showed a trend toward improved antioxidant status in these heifers, higher concentrations may yield pronounced clinical effects, as suggested by dose–response patterns reported in some antioxidant supplementation trials. Dose-escalation studies are thus warranted to determine optimal therapeutic levels while assessing potential cytotoxicity.

Conclusion

In conclusion, melatonin implants improved the TAC and decreased TOS and OSI during the first insemination after ovarian superstimulation treatment and the embryo recovery program in donor heifers. Higher level of TOS was correlated with a longer Flushing to AI, and lower First AI pregnancy in the control group. However, pregnancy outcomes, including Flushing to pregnancy, First AI pregnancy, Services per conception and the Final pregnancy, were not significantly influenced by treatment.

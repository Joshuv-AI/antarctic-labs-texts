Schramm-Loewner evolution contains a topological Sierpiński carpet when κ \kappa is close to 8
Haoyu Liu Zijie Zhuang

Abstract
We consider the Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE κ ) for κ ∈ ( 4 , 8 ) \kappa\in(4,8) , which is the regime where the curve is self-intersecting but not space-filling. We show that there exists δ 0 > 0 \delta_{0}>0 such that for κ ∈ ( 8 − δ 0 , 8 ) \kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8) , the range of an SLE κ curve almost surely contains a topological Sierpiński carpet. Combined with a result of Ntalampekos (2021), this implies that in this parameter range, SLE κ is almost surely conformally non-removable, and the conformal welding problem for SLE κ does not have a unique solution. Our result also implies that for κ ∈ ( 8 − δ 0 , 8 ) \kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8) , the adjacency graph of the complementary connected components of the SLE κ curve is disconnected.

Introduction

The Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE
κ
) is a one-parameter family of random fractal curves connecting two boundary points in a simply connected domain. It was introduced by Schramm
[
Sch00
]
to describe the conjectural scaling limits of interfaces in two-dimensional statistical mechanics models. Since then, many such models have been proved to converge to SLE
κ
in the scaling limit, see, e.g.
[
Smi01
,
LSW04
,
CDCH+14
]
. For
\kappa>0
, SLE
κ
in the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
from
0
to
\infty
is defined via a family of conformal maps
(g_{t})_{t\geq 0}
solving the Loewner equation:

\partial_{t}g_{t}(z)=\frac{2}{g_{t}(z)-W_{t}},\quad g_{0}(z)=z,

(1.1)

where
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
and
B_{t}
is a standard Brownian motion. For
z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
, let
\tau(z)=\inf\{t\geq 0:\lim_{s\uparrow t}|g_{s}(z)-W_{s}|=0\}
and let
K_{t}=\{z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}:\tau(z)\leq t\}
for
t\geq 0
, which is an increasing family of compact sets. It was shown in
[
RS05
,
LSW04
]
that
(K_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is almost surely generated by a unique continuous curve
\eta:[0,\infty)\to\overline{\mathbb{H}}
such that for each
t\geq 0
, the set
K_{t}
is the union of
\eta([0,t])
and the bounded connected components of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}\setminus\eta([0,t])
. The parameter
\kappa
determines the roughness of SLE
κ
: the random curve is simple for
\kappa\leq 4
, self-intersecting but not space-filling for
\kappa\in(4,8)
, and space-filling for
\kappa\geq 8

[
RS05
]
. Moreover, the Hausdorff dimension of the range of an SLE
κ
curve is
\min\{1+\kappa/8,2\}

[
RS05
,
Bef08
]
.

Since the introduction of SLE by Schramm, many other representations of SLE
κ
have appeared. For instance, in
[
SS09
,
Dub09
,
MS16a
,
MS17
]
, SLE
κ
curves are realized as flow lines of the Gaussian free field (GFF). Also SLE
κ
curves are characterized by the conformal restriction property and admit rich algebraic structures
[
LSW03
,
Wer08
,
BJ24
]
. In addition, SLE
κ
curves play a central role in the study of Liouville quantum gravity (LQG), which describes the scaling limit of random planar maps
[
She16
,
DMS21
]
. In particular, SLE
κ
arises as the welding interface between two conformally welded LQG disks, as we now recall. Let
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be the Riemann sphere. Suppose
\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
are two copies of the unit disk, and let
\phi:\partial\mathbb{D}_{1}\to\partial\mathbb{D}_{2}
be a homeomorphism. A
conformal welding
with
welding homeomorphism

\phi
corresponds to a Jordan loop
\eta
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and a pair of conformal maps
\psi_{i}
mapping
\mathbb{D}_{i}
to the two connected components of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\eta
such that
\phi=\psi_{2}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}
; see Figure
1
(left). Given a homeomorphism
\phi
, it is not obvious in general whether a conformal welding exists or whether it is unique up to a Möbius transformation. Sheffield
[

]
showed that if one endows

and

with the random geometry of LQG and defines

by identifying the LQG quantum boundary length measures on
\partial\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\partial\mathbb{D}_{2}

κ
-type curve for
\kappa\in(0,4)
.

The conformal removability of the welding interface is a sufficient condition for the uniqueness of conformal welding. Recall that a compact set
K\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be
conformally removable
if every homeomorphism
\phi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that is conformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus K
is conformal on all of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, and thus Möbius. In light of this, it is natural to ask whether SLE
κ
curves are conformally removable for different values of
\kappa
.
In the case
\kappa\in(0,4)
, it was proved in
[
RS05
]
that SLE
κ
is the boundary of a Hölder domain and such boundaries are conformally removable by a criterion of Jones and Smirnov
[
JS00
]
. For
\kappa=4
, SLE
4
does not form the boundary of a Hölder domain and moreover it was shown in
[
KMS21
]
that the condition in
[
JS00
]
for conformal removability does not hold for SLE
4
. Recently,
[
KMS22
]
proved the conformal removability of SLE
4
using a new criterion. For
\kappa\geq 8
, SLE
κ
curves have positive Lebesgue measure and are thus conformally non-removable. In the regime
\kappa\in(4,8)
, which is the remaining case, the complement of an SLE
κ
curve consists of a countable collection of simply connected components, making it more difficult to check the conformal removability
[
She16
, Question 8]
. However, in a recent work
[
KMS23
]
, the authors proved the conformal removability of SLE
κ
for those values of
\kappa
for which the adjacency graph of the complementary connected components is connected (see Theorem
1.6
for details). This condition was shown in
[
GP20
]
to hold for
\kappa\in(4,\kappa_{0})
, where
\kappa_{0}\approx 5.6158
.

In this paper, we will show that SLE
κ
is conformally non-removable when
\kappa
is close to 8. We will use a criterion for conformal non-removability established in
[
Nta21
]
. Recall that a topological Sierpiński carpet
S\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a set that is homeomorphic to the standard Sierpiński carpet. By a result of Whyburn
[
Why58
]
, a set
S\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a topological Sierpiński carpet if and only if
S
has empty interior and can be written as
S=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}Q_{i}
, where
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a family of Jordan domains satisfying:

(a)

\overline{Q}_{i}\cap\overline{Q}_{j}=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

(b)

The spherical diameter of
Q_{i}
converges to 0 as
i\to\infty
.

The following result is taken from
[
Nta21
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Nta21
]
)
.

All topological Sierpiński carpets are conformally non-removable.

In fact, for any two topological Sierpiński carpets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
, Ntalampekos
[
Nta21
]
constructed in a topological way a homeomorphism
\phi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\phi(S_{1})=S_{2}
and
\phi
is conformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus S_{1}
. The construction relies on inductively partitioning Sierpiński carpets into smaller Sierpiński carpets along with finite collections of peripheral disks. At each stage, we assign conformal maps to matching peripheral disks and extend them to a global homeomorphism. These homeomorphisms converge to a limiting homeomorphism that maps each peripheral disk of
S_{1}
conformally onto one of
S_{2}
, thereby satisfying the desired properties. This implies Theorem
1.1
since if
S_{1}
has zero (resp. positive) Lebesgue measure, we may choose
S_{2}
to have positive (resp. zero) Lebesgue measure so that
\phi
cannot be Möbius.

For
\kappa\in(4,8)
, let
\eta
be an SLE
κ
curve from
0
to
\infty
in
\mathbb{H}
. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, the range of the curve
\eta
almost surely contains a topological Sierpiński carpet.

The key intuition in proving Theorem
1.2
comes from Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation model
[
Man82
]
. Mandelbrot introduced this model in 1974 to generate random self-similar fractal sets, which were shown to undergo phase transitions
[
CCD88
,
CCGS91
]
, as we will recall in a moment. Later on, fractal percolation was used to study various random fractal objects—for instance, Brownian motion
[
Per96
]
, Brownian loop soup and CLE
[
SW12
]
, and log-correlated Gaussian fields
[
DGZ24
]
. Our work is the first to apply fractal percolation to non-simple SLE, which is itself a random fractal object. We now introduce the model of fractal percolation in detail. Let
p\in(0,1)
denote the retaining probability. We begin with the unit square
[0,1]^{2}
.
In each subsequent step, every retained box is divided into
3\times 3
disjoint sub-boxes of equal side length, and each sub-box is independently retained with probability
p
. When
p>1/9
, the final retained set—defined as the intersection of the retained sets at all steps—is non-empty with positive probability. In
[
CCD88
]
, a phase transition for the final retained set was established: there exists
p_{c}\in[\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},1)
such that when
p<p_{c}
, the set is almost surely totally disconnected, whereas when
p\in[p_{c},1)
, it contains a horizontal crossing of
[0,1]^{2}
with positive probability. Using the idea from
[
CCD88
]
, one can show that when
p
is sufficiently close to 1, the final retained set contains a topological Sierpiński carpet with positive probability; see Section
2
. We will define a similar fractal percolation process within the framework of the imaginary geometry construction of SLE
κ

[
MS16a
,
MS17
]
, which realizes SLE curves as flow lines of the Gaussian free field, such that the final retained set is contained in the trace of SLE
κ
; see Section
3
. Applying the fractal percolation argument, we show that this final retained set in imaginary geometry contains a topological Sierpiński carpet when
\kappa
is sufficiently close to 8, analogous to the case of
p
close to 1 in Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By definition, if
E\subseteq E^{\prime}
and
E
is conformally non-removable, then
E^{\prime}
is also conformally non-removable. Combining this property with Theorems
1.2
and
1.1
, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, the range of an SLE
κ
curve is almost surely conformally non-removable.

Theorem
1.2
, together with a refined result in
[
Nta21
]
, implies the non-uniqueness of conformal welding for SLE
κ
in the same parameter range.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, the solution to the conformal welding problem for SLE
κ
is almost surely not unique.

Let us mention that the conformal non-removability does not imply the non-uniqueness of conformal welding, so Theorem
1.4
is really not a direct consequence of Theorem
1.3
. We will discuss conformal welding in more detail in Section
7
.
However, we remark that an abstract measurability argument was used in
[
DMS21
]
to show that there is almost surely a unique way to obtain an SLE
κ
-decorated LQG surface from conformal welding for all
\kappa\in(4,8)
. A stronger version of the uniqueness result was proved in
[
MMQ21
]
where it was shown that the conformal welding problem for SLE
κ
has a unique solution if one requires the welding interface to be an SLE
κ
curve.

Remark 1.5
.

(Different domains or variants of SLE)
Since the properties in Theorem
1.2
are topological, an analogous result holds for SLE
κ
in any simply connected domain when
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
. (Note that the topological Sierpiński carpet found in the curve
\eta
from Theorem

can be required to be bounded and have positive distance from

, so Theorem

Theorem

also partially answers the bubble connectivity problem posed in Question 11.2 of

\kappa\in(4,\kappa_{0})

Theorem 1.6
.

For
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, the adjacency graph of the bubbles of
\eta
is almost surely disconnected.

Sierpiński carpet in Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation

As mentioned earlier, the proof of Theorem
1.2
is based on insights from fractal percolation
[
Man82
,
CCD88
]
. To better illustrate the proof idea, we will prove a parallel result for this model in this section.

Mandelbrot’s fractal percolation model is defined as follows. Fix a positive integer
N\geq 2
, and let
p\in[0,1]
. We partition the unit box
[0,1]^{2}
into
N
-adic boxes: for
n\geq 0
, let
\mathfrak{B}_{n}=\{[\frac{i-1}{N^{n}},\frac{i}{N^{n}}]\times[\frac{j-1}{N^{n}},\frac{j}{N^{n}}]:1\leq i,j\leq N^{n}\}
be the collection of boxes of side-length
N^{-n}
, such that each box in
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
contains
N^{2}
sub-boxes in
\mathfrak{B}_{n+1}
. For
B\in\cup_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{B}_{n}
, let
\sigma_{B}
be independent Bernoulli random variables with
\mathbb{P}[\sigma_{B}=1]=1-\mathbb{P}[\sigma_{B}=0]=p
. Let
A_{0}=[0,1]^{2}
. For
n\geq 1
, we inductively define
A_{n}=A_{n-1}\cap(\cup_{B\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}:\sigma_{B}=1}B)
. We may view (modulo some boundary issues) that boxes
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
with
\sigma_{B}=0
as being removed at step
n
, and
A_{n}
is the closed set of points retained after step
n
.

Let
A_{\infty}=\cap_{n\geq 0}A_{n}
be the retained set, which is a random fractal set with Hausdorff dimension
\max\{2+\frac{\log p}{\log N},0\}
. It is easy to see that when
p\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}
, we have
A_{\infty}=\emptyset
almost surely; whereas when
p>\frac{1}{N^{2}}
, the set
A_{\infty}
is non-empty with positive probability. The seminal work
[
CCD88
]
studied the connectivity properties of
A_{\infty}
and proved the existence of a non-trivial phase transition: there exists
p_{c}=p_{c}(N)\in[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}},1)
such that when
\frac{1}{N^{2}}<p<p_{c}
, the set
A_{\infty}
is a.s. totally disconnected, while when
p_{c}\leq p\leq 1
, the set
A_{\infty}
contains a horizontal crossing of
[0,1]^{2}
with positive probability.

1.2
for fractal percolation is the following.

There exists

, with positive probability,

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

For convenience, we will consider
N\geq 6
. Since the case for
N
can be treated by comparing it with the case
N^{\prime}=N^{2}
, the conclusion then holds for all
N\geq 2
.

For each box
B\in\cup_{n\geq 1}\mathfrak{B}_{n}
,
B
is called
0
-good
if
\sigma_{B}=1
; and we always say
[0,1]^{2}
is
0
-good.
B
is called
1
-good
if at least
(N^{2}-1)
of its sub-boxes are
0
-good. For
m\geq 2
,
B
is called
m
-good
if at least
(N^{2}-1)
of its sub-boxes are both
0
-good and
(m-1)
-good. Note that the
m
-good properties of disjoint boxes of the same side-length are independent from each other.
Also observe that for
m\geq 1
, the
m
-good condition becomes sharper as
m
increases (that is, for
1\leq m<m^{\prime}
, if
B
is
m^{\prime}
-good, then
B
is also
m
-good). If
B
is
m
-good for all
m\geq 0
, we say
B
is
\infty
-good
.

We claim that if
[0,1]^{2}
is
\infty
-good, then

\mbox{the retained set }A_{\infty}\mbox{ contains a topological Sierpi\'{n}ski carpet.}

(2.1)

Suppose that
[0,1]^{2}
is
\infty
-good. Let
A_{0}^{\dagger}=[0,1]^{2}
. For
n\geq 1
, we define
A_{n}^{\dagger}\subseteq A_{n}\cap A_{n-1}^{\dagger}
to be the union of boxes in
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
that are
\infty
-good and contained in
A_{n}\cap A_{n-1}^{\dagger}
. For each
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{n}
such that
B\subset A_{n}^{\dagger}
, we see that at least
(N^{2}-1)
of its sub-boxes are retained in
A_{n+1}^{\dagger}
. Typically,
\cap_{n\geq 0}A_{n}^{\dagger}
is not a topological Sierpiński carpet as the closures of its complementary connected components may intersect at the corner. Therefore, we will modify
A_{n}^{\dagger}
to define
A_{n}^{*}
by removing sub-boxes at the corner so that
\cap_{n\geq 0}A_{n}^{*}
, which is contained in
A_{\infty}
, is a topological Sierpiński carpet.

Let
A_{0}^{*}=[0,1]^{2}
, and for
n\geq 1
, we inductively define
A_{n}^{*}\subseteq A_{n}^{\dagger}
as follows. Let
\mathcal{C}_{n}
denote the collection of all complementary connected components of
A_{n-1}^{*}\cap A_{n}^{\dagger}
. Whenever the closures of two components in
\mathcal{C}_{n}
intersect exactly at one point, we further remove from
A_{n-1}^{*}\cap A_{n}^{\dagger}
the other two boxes in
\mathfrak{B}_{n+1}
incident to that point, and denote the closure of the resulting set by
A_{n}^{*}
; see Figure
2
. It is clear that the complementary components of
A_{n}^{*}
have pairwise disjoint closures, and
\{A_{n}^{*}\}_{n\geq 0}
is a decreasing sequence of closed sets.

We now show that

A_{\infty}^{*}:=\cap_{n\geq 0}A_{n}^{*}\subseteq A_{\infty}\mbox{ is a topological Sierpi\'{n}ski carpet,}

(2.2)

which directly implies (
2.1
).
Let
\{Q_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
be the complementary connected components of
A_{\infty}^{*}
, including the unbounded one. We first observe that for any
k\geq 1
and a complementary component
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
of
A_{k}^{*}
, if we denote by
\mathcal{Q}^{k+m}
the complementary component of
A_{k+m}^{*}
that contains
\mathcal{Q}^{k}
, then
\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}^{k+m}
is a complementary component of
A_{\infty}^{*}
. Conversely, for each complementary component
Q_{i}
of
A_{\infty}^{*}
, there exists a positive integer
k
such that
Q_{i}
contains a complementary component of
A_{k}^{*}
. The crucial observation for proving (
2.2
) is that the evolutions of two complementary components of
A_{k}^{*}
can never touch each other. In fact, if
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k}
are two complementary components of
A_{k}^{*}
, then
d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k})\geq N^{-k}(1-2N^{-1})
. This is because before possibly removing some boxes in
\mathfrak{B}_{k+1}
at the corner, the
|\cdot|_{\infty}
-distance between two different components is at least
N^{-k}
, and such removal operation reduces the distance by no more than
2N^{-k-1}
.
For
m\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m}
) be the complementary connected components of
A_{k+m}^{*}
that contains
\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k}
). Recall that for each
B\in\mathfrak{B}_{k}
such that
B\subset A_{k}^{\dagger}
, at most one sub-box of
B
fails to remain in
A_{k+1}^{\dagger}
, so we have

d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+1},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+1})\geq d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k})-2N^{-k-1}-2N^{-k-2}\geq N^{-k}(1-4N^{-1}-2N^{-2}),

where the additional
2N^{-k-2}
comes from the potential removal of some boxes in
\mathfrak{B}_{k+2}
at the corner in the construction of
A_{k+1}^{*}
. In general, we can prove inductively that

d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m})\geq N^{-k}(1-4N^{-1}-4N^{-2}-\cdots-4N^{-m}-2N^{-m-1}),\quad\mbox{for all }m\geq 1.

This implies that
d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m})\geq N^{-k}/5
for all
m\geq 0
(where we used
N\geq 6
), so
\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m}
and
\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m}
have disjoint closures.
Therefore, for different
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
, there exists a positive integer
k
such that
Q_{i}
(resp.
Q_{j}
) contains a
unique
complementary connected component
\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{k}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{k}
) of
A_{k}^{*}
, and hence
Q_{i}=\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{k+m}
and
Q_{j}=\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{k+m}
have disjoint closures.
A similar argument shows that each
Q_{i}
is a simply connected domain whose boundary is a simple continuous curve; hence, each
Q_{i}
is a Jordan domain. Moreover, since
A_{\infty}
has Hausdorff dimension smaller than 2,
A_{\infty}^{*}
has no interior points. By Whyburn’s criterion
[
Why58
]
,
A_{\infty}^{*}
is a topological Sierpiński carpet, which proves (
2.2
). The desired proposition now follows from Proposition
2.2
below.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For each
m\geq 0
, let
\vartheta_{m}
be the probability that
[0,1]^{2}
is
m
-good. Then there exists
p_{0}=p_{0}(N)\in(0,1)
such that
\inf_{m\geq 0}\vartheta_{m}\geq 2/3
for all
p\in(p_{0},1)
.

Proof.

By definition,
\vartheta_{0}=1
, and for
m\geq 1
,
\vartheta_{m}=\phi(\vartheta_{m-1})
, where

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=(px)^{N^{2}}+N^{2}(px)^{N^{2}-1}(1-px)

\displaystyle=N^{2}p^{N^{2}-1}x^{N^{2}-1}-(N^{2}-1)p^{N^{2}}x^{N^{2}}.

Note that
\phi
increases on
[0,1]
. For sufficiently small
\nu>0
and
p>p_{0}:=(1-\nu/2)^{1/N^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\phi(1-\nu)

\displaystyle\geq N^{2}p^{N^{2}-1}(1-(N^{2}-1)\nu)-(N^{2}-1)p^{N^{2}}(1-N^{2}\nu+\tfrac{1}{2}N^{2}(N^{2}-1)\nu^{2})

\displaystyle=N^{2}(p^{N^{2}-1}-p^{N^{2}})+p^{N^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{2}N^{2}(N^{2}-1)^{2}p^{N^{2}}\nu^{2}

\displaystyle\geq p^{N^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{2}N^{2}(N^{2}-1)^{2}\nu^{2}>1-\nu.

The last inequality follows by choosing
\nu
sufficiently small depending on
N
. Then, it follows from simple induction that
\vartheta_{m}>1-\nu>2/3
for all
m\geq 1
and
p\in(p_{0},1)
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

Using a qualitative argument, we can in fact show that for any
p>p_{c}
, the retained set
A_{\infty}
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet with positive probability. Since we do not need this claim in the paper, we only outline the proof strategy. Consider the removed boxes in fractal percolation, i.e., the
N
-adic boxes for which
\sigma_{B}=0
and which are not contained in any other box with
\sigma_{B}=0
. We say that two removed boxes
A
and
B
are

if there exists a finite chain of removed boxes

such that each pair
A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}
shares a non-trivial boundary segment. (Indeed, if
A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}
intersect at exactly one point, there almost surely exists another removed box
A^{\prime}
containing that point such that
A^{\prime}
intersects both
A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}
along a non-trivial boundary segment.) We define
removed box clusters
as the unions of removed boxes that are connected in this way. The final retained set
A_{\infty}
can be equivalently defined by removing from
[0,1]^{2}
the interiors of all removed box clusters.

For a removed box cluster
\mathcal{O}
, its
filling

F(\mathcal{O})
is defined to be the complement of the unbounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, and its
outer boundary

\partial^{o}\mathcal{O}
is defined to be the boundary of
F(\mathcal{O})
. Consider the union of
\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,1]^{2}
and all removed box clusters that intersect the boundary of
[0,1]^{2}
. Define
E
as the event that its complement contains interior points. It follows from the FKG inequality that

occurs with positive probability when

. On the event

, we choose an arbitrary connected component of its interior, denoted by

.
We say that a removed box cluster
\mathcal{O}
contained in

is an outermost removed box cluster if it is not enclosed by the outer boundary of any other removed box cluster contained in
U
. Suppose that for some
p\in[p_{c},1)
, the following holds almost surely on the event

:
\partial U
is a simple loop and for any two outermost removed box clusters
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
contained in
U
,

\partial^{o}\mathcal{O}\mbox{ and }\partial^{o}\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\mbox{ are simple loops and are disjoint from each other and }\partial U.

We claim that for those

satisfying (
2.3
), on the event
E
, the set
A_{\infty}
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet. Consider the fillings
\{F(\mathcal{O}_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
of all outermost removed box clusters that are contained in

. Then we have

, where
(F(\mathcal{O}_{i}))^{\circ}
denotes the interior of
F(\mathcal{O}_{i})
. Moreover, by (
2.3
), there are infinitely many such outermost removed box clusters (since
A_{\infty}
has no interior points). By (
2.3
) and Whyburn’s criterion, the set

Next, we will elaborate that (

) holds for any
p>p_{c}
except for a countable set. In particular, this combined with the monotonicity of
A_{\infty}
in
p
implies that for any
p>p_{c}
, the set
A_{\infty}
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet with positive probability. We first show that except for a countable set of
p
, for any
p\in(0,1)
, it is almost surely the case that

the boundaries of any two different removed box clusters do not intersect.

(2.4)

Fix two
N
-adic boxes
A
and
B
, and consider the removed box clusters
\mathcal{O}_{A}
and
\mathcal{O}_{B}
containing
A
and
B
, respectively. There is a natural increasing coupling of fractal percolation for different

in terms of

. The crucial observation is that there is almost surely at most one value of

for which
\mathcal{O}_{A}\neq\mathcal{O}_{B}
and
\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{A}\cap\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{B}\neq\emptyset
. Specifically, consider the largest

\overline{\mathcal{O}}_{A}

for all

for any

(if

lies on the boundary or at a corner of a removed box, then we can choose at most four removed boxes whose union contains

By a first moment estimate and enumerating over all pairs

We now show that (

A fractal percolation from imaginary geometry

In this section, we describe the proof outline of Theorem
1.2
using imaginary geometry theory
[
MS16a
,
MS17
]
. For convenience, we will work with chordal SLE
κ
processes in the box
\mathbb{B}=(-1,1)^{2}
.

3.1
Setting up the fractal percolation

Let
\kappa\in(6,8)
,
\underline{\kappa}=16/\kappa\in(2,8/3)
, and
\chi=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\underline{\kappa}}}-\frac{\sqrt{\underline{\kappa}}}{2}\in(\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}})
. We first recall the imaginary geometry (IG) construction of the chordal space-filling SLE
κ
and chordal SLE
κ
; see
[
MS16a
,
MS17
]
for details. Let
h
denote the Gaussian free field (GFF) on the box
\mathbb{B}
with the imaginary geometry boundary conditions on
(\mathbb{B},-i,i)
so that the counterflow line of
h
from
-i
to
i
corresponds to a chordal SLE
κ
. For
(\mathbb{H},0,\infty)
, the corresponding GFF is a Dirichlet GFF
\widetilde{h}
with boundary conditions
\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{\kappa}}
on
\mathbb{R}_{-}
and
-\lambda^{\prime}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. If
\psi
is a conformal map from
(\mathbb{H},0,\infty)
to
(\mathbb{B},-i,i)
, then
h
is given by
h=\widetilde{h}\circ\psi^{-1}-\chi\arg(\psi^{-1})^{\prime}
.

For rational points
v\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}
, we can simultaneously define the
\pi/2
and
-\pi/2
angle flow lines of
h
starting from
v
, denoted by
\eta^{L}(v)
and
\eta^{R}(v)
. We orient these flow lines from
-i
to
i
after they hit and merge with the boundary. Since flow lines with the same angle will merge together after hitting, these flow lines form a space-filling tree and a space-filling dual tree. There a.s. exists a unique continuous curve
\widetilde{\eta}
that traces the entire tree, which is the chordal space-filling SLE
κ
from
-i
to
i
. Parameterizing
\widetilde{\eta}
using capacity seen from
i
then yields an ordinary chordal SLE
κ
from
-i
to
i
, which is also the counterflow line of
h
from
-i
to
i
. Let
\eta
denote this chordal SLE
κ
. In other words,
\eta
can be obtained by restricting
\widetilde{\eta}
to the set of times when its tip lies on the frontier of the region
\widetilde{\eta}
has traversed so far.

We record some qualitative properties that follow from
[
MS17
]
and will be used in this paper. We will elaborate Properties (
a
), (

), and (

) in more detail in Section

.

(a)

For
\kappa\in(6,8)
, the
\pi/2
and
-\pi/2
angle flow lines of
h
starting from rational points in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}
are almost surely simple curves and do not cross each other.

(b)

For all
v\in\overline{\mathbb{B}}
, let
\sigma_{v}
denote the first time that
\widetilde{\eta}
hits
v
, and define
\eta^{L}(v)
and
\eta^{R}(v)
as the left and right boundaries of
\widetilde{\eta}([\sigma_{v},\infty))
. For rational points
v
in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}
, these boundaries coincide with the

and
-\pi/2
angle flow lines. Moreover, for any

, there exists a sequence of rational points

such that

as

for

in the sense that for any

,

coincide after the first time exiting

are simple for all

and the flow line interaction rule applies to all

(c)

Let

Almost surely, every point

In light of Lemma

, we define

The Hausdorff dimension of the set of points

To prove Theorem

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that for
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, with positive probability,
\mathcal{K}
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
given Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
. Since Proposition
3.2
is a topological property, it follows from conformal invariance that in any simply connected domain, the trace of a chordal SLE
κ
curve has a uniformly positive probability, denoted by
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{p}(\kappa)
, of containing a topological Sierpiński carpet. To prove Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to find independent copies of chordal SLE
κ
in the trace of
\eta
, as we elaborate below. If each copy independently has a uniformly positive probability of containing a topological Sierpiński carpet, then the trace of
\eta
almost surely contains a topological Sierpiński carpet.

Let
\eta
be a chordal SLE
κ
from
0
to
\infty
in
\mathbb{H}
. We parameterize
\eta
by
[0,\infty)
so that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)=\infty
. Consider
D_{k}=\mathbb{H}\cap(B(0,2^{k+1})\setminus\overline{B(0,2^{k})})
for

. For each
k\geq 1
, let
\tau_{k}
be the first time that
\eta
enters
\overline{D}_{k}
and
\widetilde{\tau}_{k}
be the last time that
\eta
exits
\overline{D}_{k}
.
By the reversibility of SLE
κ

[
MS16b
]
and the domain Markov property, the conditional law of
\eta|_{[\tau_{k},\widetilde{\tau}_{k}]}
given
\eta|_{[0,\tau_{k}]\cup[\widetilde{\tau}_{k},\infty)}
is that of a chordal SLE
κ
from
\eta(\tau_{k})
to
\eta(\widetilde{\tau}_{k})
in the appropriate connected component of
\mathbb{H}\setminus\eta([0,\tau_{k}]\cup[\widetilde{\tau}_{k},\infty))
. It follows that for any increasing sequence
\{k_{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq M}
, conditioned on
E:=\{\tau_{k_{1}}<\widetilde{\tau}_{k_{1}}<\tau_{k_{2}}<\widetilde{\tau}_{k_{2}}<\cdots<\tau_{k_{M}}<\widetilde{\tau}_{k_{M}}\}
, the events that
\eta|_{[\tau_{k},\widetilde{\tau}_{k}]}
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet are independent. Therefore, with conditional probability at least

, at least one of these events occurs, which implies that the trace of
\eta
contains a topological Sierpiński carpet. Since
\eta
is a continuous curve tending to infinity, for any fixed

, we can choose an increasing sequence
\{k_{n}\}_{1\leq n\leq M}
such that

occurs with probability at least

. Taking

to infinity yields the desired result.
∎

3.2
Finding the Sierpiński carpet

In this section, we outline the proof of Proposition

similarly to the proof strategy of Proposition

. The very first idea is that the trace of SLE
κ
is a random self-similar fractal set, whose dimension

[

Let

.

Definition 3.3

(
n
-good)
.

We say that the origin is

\eta^{L}(u)
and

do not intersect after leaving

from

(2)

κ
when

In this section, we analyze the behavior of flow lines and SLE
κ
as
\kappa
tends to 8. First, in Section
4.1
, we will collect the definitions and basic properties of different variants of SLE and GFF; the latter will also be used several times in Section
5
. In Section
4.2
, we provide an overview of the imaginary geometry coupling of SLE and GFF, and the construction of space-filling SLE. Specifically, we will make sense of the flow lines emanating from every point as mentioned in Section

. Finally, we will prove Lemma

.

4.1
Preliminaries on SLE and GFF

As mentioned in (
1.1
), chordal SLE
κ
is a random curve generated by a family of conformal maps
(g_{t})_{t\geq 0}
that solve the Loewner equation with driving function
W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}B_{t}
. In this work, we will also consider variants of SLE
κ
called chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
, in which one keeps track of several additional points, which we refer to as force points.

For
\kappa>0
, SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
processes on the upper half-plane
\mathbb{H}
with force points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
and weights
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{m}
are defined by the same Loewner equation (
1.1
), except that the driving function
W
is given by the solution of

\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{t}=\sqrt{\kappa}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}B_{t}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\rho_{j}}{W_{t}-g_{t}(x_{j})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t,

(4.1)

where the evolutions of force points,
(g_{t}(x_{j}))_{t\geq 0}
, are determined by solving (
1.1
) for
z\in\overline{\mathbb{H}}
. It was proved in
[
MS16a
]
that (
4.1
) has a unique solution up until the continuation threshold
\inf\{t\geq 0:\sum_{j:x_{j}<0,g_{t}(x_{j})=W_{t}}\rho_{j}\wedge\sum_{j:x_{j}>0,g_{t}(x_{j})=W_{t}}\rho_{j}\leq-2\}
, and that SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
is a continuous curve. For a simply connected domain
D
with boundary points
x,y
, the chordal SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
in
D
from
x
to
y
is defined using conformal maps.

Let us now review the definition and basic properties of the two-dimensional Gaussian free field (GFF). We refer the reader to
[
She07
,
Dub09
,
WP21
]
for more details. Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain with harmonically non-trivial boundary. We denote by
H_{0}(D)
the Hilbert closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(D)
under the Dirichlet inner product
\langle f,g\rangle_{\nabla}=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{D}\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x
. Let
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
H_{0}(D)
, and let
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. The random series
\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}f_{n}
converges almost surely in the space of distributions, whose limit is called a
zero-boundary
GFF on
D
. The GFF on
D
with boundary conditions
\xi
is referred to as the sum of a zero-boundary GFF and a (deterministic) harmonic function that agrees with
\xi
on
\partial D
.

The whole-plane GFF is defined in a similar way. For
D=\mathbb{C}
, let
H(D)
be the Hilbert closure of
\{f\in C^{\infty}(D):\int_{D}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=0\}
under the Dirichlet inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\nabla}
. Let
(g_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an orthonormal basis of
H(D)
, and let
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables. Then
\sum_{n\geq 1}\alpha_{n}g_{n}
viewed as a random distribution modulo global additive constant is called a whole-plane GFF. For
s>0
, we can define the whole-plane GFF modulo
s\mathbb{Z}
(so that two distributions are equivalent if they differ by an integer multiple of
s
). We first sample a whole-plane GFF
\mathfrak{h}
modulo global additive constant. Then we independently sample a uniform random variable
U
from
[0,s)
and fix the additive constant of
\mathfrak{h}
by requiring that the average of
\mathfrak{h}
on the unit circle is equal to
U
.

For a random distribution
h
and an open set
V
, we define the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
h|_{V}
as the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the distributional pairing
\phi\mapsto(h,\phi)
, where
\phi
ranges over smooth functions compactly supported in
V
. For
s>0
, we define the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
h|_{V}
modulo
s\mathbb{Z}
as follows. Fix a reference function
\phi_{0}
that is compactly supported in
V
and satisfies
\int\phi_{0}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=1
. Let
(h,\phi_{0})=ns+r
where
n
is an integer and
r\in[0,s)
. The
\sigma
-algebra is generated by the distributional pairing
\phi\mapsto(h-ns,\phi)
, where
\phi
ranges over smooth functions compactly supported in
V
. We extend these notation to a closed set
K
by taking the intersection of the
\sigma
-algebras with respect to
O(K,\epsilon)
for
\epsilon>0
, where
O(K,\epsilon)
is the
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
K
.

We now recall some basic properties of the GFF. The zero-boundary GFF
h
on a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
with harmonically non-trivial boundary satisfies the domain Markov property. Specifically, for any subdomain
U\subset D
with harmonically non-trivial boundary, if
h^{U}
denotes the harmonic extension of
h|_{D\setminus U}
on
U
. Then,
h-h^{U}
is a zero-boundary GFF on
U
and is independent of
\sigma(h|_{D\setminus U})
. We also recall the domain Markov property for the whole-plane GFF from
[
GMS19
, Lemma 2.2]
. We will only need the case for boxes.

Lemma 4.1

(Domain Markov property)
.

For any
B(z,r)\subset B(0,R)
, let
\mathfrak{h}
be a whole-plane GFF normalized so that
\mathfrak{h}_{R}(0)=0
, where
\mathfrak{h}_{R}(0)
is the average of
\mathfrak{h}
over
\partial B_{R}(0)
. let
\mathfrak{h}^{z,r}
be the harmonic extension of
\mathfrak{h}|_{\mathbb{C}\setminus B(z,r)}
on
B(z,r)
, and define
\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}^{z,r}=\mathfrak{h}-\mathfrak{h}^{z,r}
. Then,
\mathring{\mathfrak{h}}^{z,r}
is independent of
\mathfrak{h}|_{\mathbb{C}\setminus B(z,r)}
, and has the same law as a zero-boundary Gaussian free field on
B(z,r)
.

The following estimates will be used repeatedly in the proof.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
s\in(0,1)
, there exists a universal constant
C=C(s)>0
such that the following holds.

1.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a whole-plane GFF normalized so that
\mathfrak{h}_{R}(0)=0
. For any
B(z,r)\subset B(0,R)
, let
\mathfrak{h}^{z,r}
be the harmonic extension of
\mathfrak{h}|_{\mathbb{C}\setminus B(z,r)}
in
B(z,r)
. Then, for any
t\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{u,v\in B(z,sr)}|\mathfrak{h}^{z,r}(u)-\mathfrak{h}^{z,r}(v)|\geq t\Big]\leq Ce^{-t^{2}/C}.

2.

Let
B(z,r)
and
B(y,l)
be two boxes such that
B(z,r)\subset B(y,l)
. Let
h_{y,l}
be a zero-boundary GFF on
B(y,l)
and let
h^{z,r}_{y,l}
be the harmonic extension of
h_{y,l}|_{B(y,l)\setminus B(z,r)}
on
B(z,r)
. Then, for any
t\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{u,v\in B(z,sr)}|h^{z,r}_{y,l}(u)-h^{z,r}_{y,l}(v)|\geq t\Big]\leq Ce^{-t^{2}/C}.

Proof.

By Green’s function estimates and Fernique’s inequality, it is easy to see that the expectation of the supremum is uniformly bounded; see, e.g.,
[
DD20
, Lemma 2.9]
. The tail estimates then follow from the Borell-TIS inequality, see, e.g.,
[
AT07
, Theoerm 2.1.1]
.
∎

We will also rely on the following local absolute continuity property.

Lemma 4.3

(Local absolute continuity)
.

Fix
0<b<b^{\prime}<1
and
T>0
. For any
M>0
and
\alpha>0
, there exists a constant
C=C(b,b^{\prime},T,\alpha,M)>0
such that the following holds. Let
h
be a zero-boundary GFF on
B(z,r)
. Let
g
be any deterministic harmonic function in
B(z,r)
satisfying
\sup_{u,v\in B(z,b^{\prime}r)}|g(u)-g(v)|\leq M
, and let
s\in(0,T)
. Then,
(h+g)|_{B(z,br)}
and
h|_{B(z,br)}
are absolutely continuous with respect to each other viewed as random generalized functions modulo
s\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore, the Radon-Nikodym derivative
H_{g}
of the former with respect to the latter satisfies

\max\{\mathbb{E}[(H_{g})^{-\alpha}],\mathbb{E}[(H_{g})^{\alpha}]\}\leq C.

Proof.

We first replace
g
with
\tilde{g}:=g-ns
for some integer
n
such that
\sup_{u\in B(z,b^{\prime}r)}|\tilde{g}(u)|\leq M+T
. Similar to
[
MQ20
, Lemma 4.1]
, we can explicitly compute the Radon-Nikodym derivative
H_{\tilde{g}}
of
(h+g-ns)|_{B(z,br)}
and
h|_{B(z,br)}
viewed as random generalized functions and show that
\max\{\mathbb{E}[(H_{\tilde{g}})^{-\beta}],\mathbb{E}[(H_{\tilde{g}})^{\beta}]\}\leq C^{\prime}(\beta)
for any
\beta>0
. The desired lemma then follows from Hölder’s inequality.
∎

The following lemma, which will be used in Section
4.4
, is a consequence of Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\kappa\in(6,8)
, let
\mathbb{P}
be the law of a imaginary geometry GFF on
\mathbb{B}
as defined at the beginning of Section

, and
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}
be the law of a whole-plane GFF modulo
2\pi\chi\mathbb{Z}
. Let

and

be an event that is measurable with respect to
h|_{B(z,r)}
modulo
2\pi\chi\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{P}[A]=1-o_{\nu}(1)
as
\nu\to 0
, then
\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}[A]=1-o_{\nu}(1)
with a rate uniformly in
\kappa
and
B(z,r)
; and vice versa.

Proof.

Write
h
for the imaginary geometry GFF on
\mathbb{B}
. Let
h^{\prime}
be the harmonic extension of
h|_{\mathbb{B}\setminus(-31/32,31/32)^{2}}
on
(-31/32,31/32)^{2}
. For
M>0
, let
E_{M}
be the event that

\sup_{u,v\in(-15/16,15/16)^{2}}|h^{\prime}(u)-h^{\prime}(v)|\leq M.

(4.2)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
h|_{\mathbb{B}\setminus(-31/32,31/32)^{2}}
, and let
\widehat{\mathbb{P}}
be the law of a zero-boundary GFF on

. By Lemma
4.3
and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, on the event

\widehat{\mathbb{P}}[A^{c}]=\mathbb{E}[(H_{h^{\prime}})^{-1}\mathbf{1}_{A^{c}}|\mathcal{F}]\leq\mathbb{E}[(H_{h^{\prime}})^{-2}|\mathcal{F}]^{1/2}\cdot\mathbb{P}[A^{c}|\mathcal{F}]^{1/2}\leq c(M)\cdot\mathbb{P}[A^{c}|\mathcal{F}]^{1/2}\mbox{ for some constant }c(M)>0.

By Lemma

, the event

occurs with probability

as

. Sending

and then

gives

. Using the same argument, we get

which concludes the first assertion. The other direction follows from a similar argument.
∎

In this subsection, we provide a further review of imaginary geometry

. For

(4.3)

Let
\eta

-\lambda(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\rho_{i,L})\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in(x_{j+1,L},x_{j,L}]\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j^{\prime}}\rho_{i,R})\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in(x_{j^{\prime},R},x_{j^{\prime}+1,R}]

(4.4)

for

and

, where we set

,

,

,

by convention. By
[

can be coupled with

in the following sense. Let

and

be the family of conformal maps and the driving function associated with

. Then, for each stopping time

of

is a GFF on
\mathbb{H}
with boundary data given by
-\lambda
for

,
\lambda
for

, and

-\lambda(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\rho_{i,L})\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in(f_{\tau}(x_{j+1,L}),f_{\tau}(x_{j,L}])\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda(1+\sum_{i=1}^{j^{\prime}}\rho_{i,R})\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\in(f_{\tau}(x_{j^{\prime},R}),f_{\tau}(x_{j^{\prime}+1,R})]

for

of

.

For
\kappa>4
, SLE
{}_{\kappa}(\underline{\rho})
process
\eta^{\prime}
from 0 to
\infty

5.3
Counting paths in a fractal graph: Proof of Lemma
5.5

All constants

in this subsection may vary from line to line, but depend only on
\varrho
. We assume that

and fix an arbitrary sequence of vertex sets

on

that satisfies Conditions
1
and

.

Definition 5.11

(weight)
.

Let
\beta>0
be a constant to be chosen later. Recalling the definition of self-avoiding connected paths and the collection
\{\mathcal{S}_{m}\}_{m\geq 0}
from Definition
5.4
, we assign a weight
w(a)=\beta N^{-8m}
to each box

for

. For any path

, its weight

is defined as the product of the weights of all boxes along the path.

Lemma 5.12
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\varrho)>0
such that for any
\beta\leq C^{-1}
,
N\geq C
, and any box

\sum_{P:a\to\bullet}w(P)\leq 1,

where the sum is taken over all self-avoiding connected paths starting from

Proof.

For any

, we first show that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\cup_{m=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{S}_{m},\,d(a,b)\leq 40\varrho\end{subarray}}\sqrt{w(a)w(b)}\leq 1/2.

(5.14)

Assume that

. For each

, there are at most
(200\varrho N^{l})^{2}
possible choices of

satisfying

. On the other hand, for each

, Condition
2
implies that there is at most one

with
d(a,b)\leq 40\varrho
. The left-hand side of (

) is therefore bounded above by

\beta\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{l}N^{-4l}\times N^{-4j}\times(200\varrho N^{l})^{2}+\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}N^{-4l}\times N^{-4j}\Big).

For sufficiently small

and sufficiently large

, inequality (
5.14
) holds. The lemma follows from (
5.14
) combined with the fact that
\sqrt{w(a)}\leq 1/2
.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

There exists a constant
C=C(\varrho)\geq 100
such that for all
N\geq 100C
and
k\geq 0
, all self-avoiding connected paths at level
k
with
|\cdot|_{\infty}
-diameter
D\geq N^{k}
have length at least
\frac{1}{C}(1-\frac{C}{N})^{k}D
.

Proof.

We set
C=10000\varrho
and prove the lemma by induction on
k
. The base case
k=0
holds trivially. For the inductive step, assume the statement holds for some
k\geq 0
. To prove the case
k+1
, consider any self-avoiding connected path
(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{L})
at level
(k+1)
with diameter
D\geq N^{k+1}
. We divide it into sub-paths using the boxes from
\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
:

P_{0},y_{1},P_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{M},P_{M},

where
y_{1},\ldots,y_{M}\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
and
P_{0},\ldots,P_{M}
are self-avoiding connected paths at level
k
(with
P_{0}
and
P_{M}
possibly empty). Then,

{\rm diam}(P_{0})+(2\times 40\varrho+20\varrho N^{k})+{\rm diam}(P_{1})+(2\times 40\varrho+20\varrho N^{k})+\cdots+{\rm diam}(P_{M})\geq D.

(5.15)

We claim that for
N\geq 100C
,

\tfrac{C}{2N}({\rm diam}(P_{0})+{\rm diam}(P_{1})+\cdots+{\rm diam}(P_{M}))\geq M(2\times 40\varrho+20\varrho N^{k}).

(5.16)

We separate into two cases
M=1
and
M\geq 2
(the case
M=0
is trivial). If
M\geq 2
, for each
1\leq i\leq M-1
, since the
|\cdot|_{\infty}
-distance between the centers of

and

is at least
\frac{1}{10}\varrho N^{k+1}
(Condition
2
), we have
{\rm diam}(P_{i})\geq\frac{1}{10}\varrho N^{k+1}-20\varrho N^{k}-2\times 40\varrho\geq\frac{1}{20}\varrho N^{k+1}
. This implies that

\tfrac{C}{2N}({\rm diam}(P_{0})+{\rm diam}(P_{1})+\cdots+{\rm diam}(P_{M}))\geq\tfrac{C}{2N}(M-1)\tfrac{1}{20}\varrho N^{k+1}\geq 100M\varrho N^{k}\geq M(2\times 40\varrho+20\varrho N^{k}).

If
M=1
, we have
\min\{{\rm diam}(P_{0}),{\rm diam}(P_{M})\}\geq\frac{1}{3}N^{k+1}
, which leads to

\tfrac{C}{2N}({\rm diam}(P_{0})+{\rm diam}(P_{1})+\cdots+{\rm diam}(P_{M}))\geq\tfrac{C}{2N}\times\tfrac{1}{3}N^{k+1}\geq 2\times 40\varrho+20\varrho N^{k}.

Combining both cases establishes Claim (
5.16
).

Let
A=\frac{1}{C}(1-\frac{C}{N})^{k}
. For
1\leq i\leq M-1
, recall that
{\rm diam}(P_{i})\geq\frac{1}{20}\varrho N^{k+1}\geq N^{k}
. By the induction hypothesis,
{\rm len}(P_{i})\geq A\cdot{\rm diam}(P_{i})
holds for all
1\leq i\leq M-1
. Similarly, we can also show that
{\rm len}(P_{0})+AN^{k}\geq A\cdot{\rm diam}(P_{0})
and
{\rm len}(P_{M})+AN^{k}\geq A\cdot{\rm diam}(P_{M})
. Combining these estimates with (
5.15
) and (
5.16
) yields

\displaystyle AD

\displaystyle\leq A(1+\tfrac{C}{2N})({\rm diam}(P_{0})+{\rm diam}(P_{1})+\cdots+{\rm diam}(P_{M}))

\displaystyle\leq(1+\tfrac{C}{2N})(2AN^{k}+{\rm len}(P_{0})+{\rm len}(P_{1})+\cdots+{\rm len}(P_{M})).

Therefore, the total length
L
satisfies

L\geq\sum_{i=0}^{M}{\rm len}(P_{i})\geq(1+\tfrac{C}{2N})^{-1}AD-2AN^{k}.

Since
C\geq 100
and
N\geq 100C
, this yields
L\geq(1-\frac{C}{N})AD
whenever
D\geq N^{k+1}
, thereby completing the induction.
∎

Next, we upper-bound the fraction of boxes from

Let

. For

be the number of boxes from
\mathcal{S}_{i}
in

.

For fixed

, we divide

into sub-paths using the boxes from
\mathcal{S}_{m}

P_{0},y_{1},P_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{M},P_{M},

where

have

-diameter at most
\frac{1}{4}\epsilon^{k}
.
Therefore, we have

d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+1},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+1})\geq d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k})-2\times\tfrac{1}{4}\epsilon^{k}-2\times\epsilon^{k+2}\geq\epsilon^{k}(1-2\epsilon-\tfrac{1}{2}-2\epsilon^{2}),

where the additional
2\times\epsilon^{k+2}
comes from the potential removal of some
\epsilon^{k+2}
-boxes at the corner in the construction of
\mathcal{K}_{k+1}
. In general, we can prove inductively that

d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m})\geq\epsilon^{k}(1-2\epsilon-(\tfrac{1}{2}+2\epsilon^{2})\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\epsilon^{i}),\quad\mbox{for all }m\geq 1.

This implies that
d(\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m},\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m})\geq\epsilon^{k}/3
for all
m\geq 0
, so
\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{k+m}
and
\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{2}^{k+m}
have disjoint closures.
Therefore, for each
i\neq j
, we can find a positive integer
k
such that
Q_{i}
(resp.
Q_{j}
) contains a
unique
complementary connected component
\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{k}
(resp.
\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{k}
) of
\mathcal{K}_{k}
, and hence
Q_{i}=\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{i}^{k+m}
and
Q_{j}=\cup_{m\geq 0}\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{k+m}
have disjoint closures. A similar argument shows that each
Q_{i}
is a simply connected domain whose boundary is a simple continuous curve; hence, each
Q_{i}
is a Jordan domain.
Moreover, since
\eta
has Hausdorff dimension
1+\kappa/8

[
Bef08
]
, the set
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
has no interior points. By Whyburn’s criterion
[
Why58
]
, the set
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
is a topological Sierpiński carpet. Since
\mathcal{K}\supset\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
(Lemma
3.4
), we conclude the proof. ∎

Conformal welding problem for SLE

In this section, we explain how Theorem
1.2
implies that the conformal welding problem for SLE almost surely does not have a unique solution (Theorem
1.4
).

We first give a precise definition of conformal welding. Let
\mathbb{D}_{1}
be the unit disk and
\mathbb{D}_{2}=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}_{1}
. Given an oriented Jordan loop
\eta
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, let
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
denote the left and right connected components of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\eta
, respectively. For
i=1,2
, let
\psi_{i}
be a conformal map from
\mathbb{D}_{i}
to
D_{i}
. By Carathéodory’s theorem,
\psi_{i}
extends continuously to the boundary of
\mathbb{D}_{i}
, hence
\phi:=\psi_{2}^{-1}\circ\psi_{1}:\partial\mathbb{D}_{1}\to\partial\mathbb{D}_{2}
defines an orientation-preserving homeomorphism from
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to itself. We refer to
\phi
as a
conformal welding homeomorphism
for
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
with interface
\eta
. Note that
\phi
is only identified up to pre- and post-composition with automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
. Moreover, the image of
\eta
under any Möbius transformation would yield the same welding homeomorphism as
\eta
itself. Therefore, the solution to the conformal welding problem is characterized by the map

\mathcal{W}:\mathcal{L}/{\sim_{\text{M\"{o}b}}}\to\mathrm{Homeo}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})/{\sim_{\mathrm{Aut}}},\quad[\eta]\mapsto[\phi],

where
\mathcal{L}/{\sim_{\text{M\"{o}b}}}
is the collection of oriented Jordan loops on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
under the equivalence relation that
\eta_{1}\sim_{\text{M\"{o}b}}\eta_{2}
if
\eta_{2}=\omega(\eta_{1})
for some Möbius transformation
\omega
, and
\mathrm{Homeo}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{1})/{\sim_{\mathrm{Aut}}}
is the set of orientation-preserving homeomorphism from
\mathbb{S}^{1}
to itself under the equivalence relation that
\phi_{1}\sim_{\mathrm{Aut}}\phi_{2}
if
\phi_{1}=\rho_{1}\circ\phi_{2}\circ\rho_{2}
for
\rho_{i}\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D}_{i})
. Note that
\mathcal{W}
is neither surjective nor injective; see, e.g.,
[
Bis07
]
.

Conformal removability is related to the uniqueness of conformal welding, i.e., injectivity of
\mathcal{W}
. It is known that conformal removability of
\eta
implies the uniqueness of conformal welding (see, e.g.,
[
You15
, Corollary 5.24]
). However, it remains an open question whether the converse is true, i.e., whether conformal non-removability guarantees the non-uniqueness of conformal welding. Indeed, if
\eta
is conformally non-removable, then there exists a homeomorphism
f:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
which is conformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\eta
but not on all of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Then, pushing forward
\psi_{i}
by
f
would yield a new solution to conformal welding with interface
f(\eta)
. However, it is still possible that
f(\eta)=\omega(\eta)
for some Möbius transformation
\omega
; see
[
You18
]
.

Pioneered by Sheffield’s work
[
She16
]
, it was shown in
[
AHS23b
,
BJ25
]
that SLE
κ
loop with
\kappa\in(0,4]
arises from conformal welding. Specifically, if one endows
\mathbb{D}_{1}
and
\mathbb{D}_{2}
with
\gamma
-LQG (
\gamma=\sqrt{\kappa}
) quantum disks and defines the homeomorphism
\phi
by identifying the LQG quantum boundary length on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, then a conformal welding exists and the welding interface has the same law as SLE
κ
loop measure. On the other hand, using the conformal removability of SLE
κ

[
JS00
,
RS05
,
KMS22
]
, we have that the conformal welding is almost surely unique for these welding homeomorphisms, i.e., the pre-image of
\phi
under
\mathcal{W}
is almost surely unique. Later, it was shown in
[
DMS21
]
that SLE
κ
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
also arises from conformal welding. Since SLE
κ
for
\kappa\in(4,8)
is a random non-simple curve that touches itself, we need to first extend the definition of conformal welding to beaded domains as defined below.

We call a connected closed set
A\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
a
beaded domain
if
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus A
is simply connected and there exists a continuous non-self-crossing loop
\mathcal{L}
such that
A
is the union of
\mathcal{L}
and the set of points enclosed by
\mathcal{L}
. Moreover, each connected component of the interior of
A
, together with its prime-end boundary, is homeomorphic to a closed disk. For two beaded domains
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, we parameterize their corresponding loops by
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and define a homeomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
. A (non-simple) conformal welding with welding homeomorphism
\phi
consists of a loop
\eta
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
together with two homeomorphisms
\psi_{i}
from the left and right connected components of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\eta
, together with the prime-end boundary, to
B_{i}
such that
\psi_{i}
is conformal on each connected component of its interior and, moreover,
\phi=\psi_{2}\circ\psi_{1}^{-1}
.

It was shown in
[
ACSW24
]
based on
[
DMS21
]
that SLE
κ
loop for
\kappa\in(4,8)
arises from conformal welding for beaded domains. Specifically, define two random beaded domains using the stable looptree
[
CK14
]
obtained by
\alpha
-stable Lévy process with
\alpha=\kappa/4
and replace the interior of each loop with conditionally independent
\gamma
-LQG (
\gamma=\sqrt{16/\kappa}
) quantum disks. If one defines a homeomorphism by identifying the generalized quantum boundary length on these two beaded domains, then a conformal welding exists and the welding interface has the same law as SLE
κ
loop measure.

When
\eta
is an SLE
κ
-type curve for
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
, it is enough to deduce from Theorem
1.2
the non-uniqueness of conformal welding. This is based on an explicit construction of
f
from
[
Nta21
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

It suffices to show that for
\kappa\in(8-\delta_{0},8)
and an SLE
κ
loop
\eta
, there almost surely exists a homeomorphism
f:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
which is conformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\eta
but not on all of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, and
f(\eta)\neq\omega(\eta)
for all Möbius transformation
\omega
. By Theorem
1.2
,
\eta
almost surely contains a topological Sierpiński carpet. Let
S
be such a carpet contained in
\eta
, and let
S^{\prime}
be any topological Sierpiński carpet in
[0,1]^{2}
with positive Lebesgue measure
6
6

6

For instance, in the first step, we divide
[0,1]^{2}
into 9 congruent squares and remove the central one. In the
k
-th step (
k\geq 1
), we divide each remaining square into
(2k+1)^{2}
congruent squares and remove the central one. The final set is a topological Sierpiński carpet with area
\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-1/(2k+1)^{2})=\pi/4
.
. By
[
Nta21

, there exists a homeomorphism

Open problems

In this section, we discuss some open problems.

Fractal percolation

We begin with some problems in fractal percolation that we find fundamental but to the best of our knowledge are not well understood. Recall from Remark
2.3
that we showed that when
p>p_{c}
, the final retained set
A_{\infty}
in fractal percolation contains a topological Sierpiński carpet. We conjecture that this also holds at the critical value
p=p_{c}
.

Problem 8.1
.

Prove that when
p=p_{c}
, the final retained set in fractal percolation contains a topological Sierpiński carpet.

A topological Sierpiński carpet can be found if condition (
2.4
) holds for
p\geq p_{c}
. In that case, removing from
[0,1]^{2}
the fillings of outermost removed box clusters yields the largest possible topological Sierpiński carpet contained in
A_{\infty}
. In Remark
2.3
, we were only able to show that (
2.4
) holds for all
p\in(0,1)
except for a countable set. We conjecture that it actually holds for all
p\in(0,1)
.

Problem 8.2
.

Prove that for any
p\in(0,1)
, a.s., the outer boundaries of any two different removed box clusters do not intersect.

If Problem
8.2
can be solved at
p=p_{c}
, the argument in Remark
2.3
would yield a positive answer to Problem
8.1
.

The Brownian loop soup is an analogous model to fractal percolation and similar problems have been answered affirmatively
[
SW12
]
, as we now recall. Sample a Poissonian random collection of Brownian loops in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with intensity
c>0
. One can view Brownian loops as analogous to removed boxes in fractal percolation. Two Brownian loops are considered connected if they can be linked by a finite chain of intersecting loops, thereby forming Brownian loop soup clusters. It was shown in
[
SW12
]
that when
c>1
, there is a single Brownian loop soup cluster whose closure is
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, while for
c\in(0,1]
, there are infinitely many Brownian loop coup clusters whose boundaries are disjoint. Moreover, if one considers the set of points not surrounded by any Brownian loop—equivalently, the region obtained by removing from
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
the fillings of all outermost Brownian loop soup clusters—then the resulting set has the law of a CLE
κ
carpet for
\kappa\in(8/3,4]
, where
\kappa
is determined by the relation
c=(3\kappa-8)(6-\kappa)/2\kappa
. By
[
SW12
]
and Whyburn’s criterion
[
Why58
]
, CLE
κ
carpet is a topological Sierpiński carpet.

The key properties of Brownian loop soup used in
[
SW12
]
are the domain Markov property and conformal invariance. While the latter is absent in fractal percolation—so the arguments of
[
SW12
]
do not directly apply—their results give support to Problems
8.1
and
8.2
.

Conformal removability and bubble connectivity of SLE

As mentioned in Section
1
, conformal removability (or non-removability) is essential in the study of SLE
κ
via conformal welding.

Problem 8.3
.

Prove Conjecture
1.7
.

Problem 8.4
.

Determine the value of
\kappa_{c}
in Conjecture

One might hope to adapt the argument in Remark

to prove Conjecture

with respect to

.
We hope that the mating-of-trees theory
[
DMS21
]
may help establish such properties; see
[
GP20
]
for its application in proving the connectivity of the adjacency graph of SLE
κ
for
\kappa\in(4,\kappa_{0})
, where
\kappa_{0}\approx 5.6158
. In addition, it would be interesting to determine whether SLE

is conformally removable, as it describes the scaling limit of Bernoulli percolation
[
Smi01
]
and enjoys additional nice properties, for instance, locality. Let us also mention that analogous questions for Brownian motion are also open: Consider a planar Brownian motion with unit time duration. Werner

MP10

asks whether the adjacency graph of the complementary connected components is connected. It is also unknown whether the trace of Brownian motion is conformally removable.

Even if Conjecture

Problem 8.5

Let
\kappa_{c}
be as defined in Conjecture
1.7

In the regime where the adjacency graph of the complementary connected components of SLE

Problem 8.6

Let

If Problem

Connectivity of other random fractals

In a seminal work
[
Per96
]
, Peres showed that planar Brownian motion is intersection-equivalent to the final retained set of a certain fractal percolation process in which a
2^{-k}
-box is retained with probability
1/k
. This property allows one to show that the intersection of two independent planar Brownian motions is intersection-equivalent to the final retained set of a similar fractal percolation process, which in particular contains non-singleton connected components. Based on this, Peres
[
Per
]
posed the following question: For two independent planar Brownian motions run for unit time duration, does their intersection almost surely contain non-singleton connected components?

Motivated by this, we ask an analogous question for SLE
κ
.

Problem 8.7
.

For
\kappa\in(4,8)
and an integer
n\geq 2
, let
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{n}
be
n
independent SLE
κ
curves from 0 to
\infty
in
\mathbb{H}
. Does the intersection
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}
contain non-singleton connected components? Here we view
\eta_{i}
as the trace of SLE
κ
, which is a closed subset of
\overline{\mathbb{H}}
.

We note that when
\kappa\leq 4
, each curve is simple so their intersection is totally disconnected. When
\kappa\geq 8
, the SLE
κ
curves are space-filling, so their intersection has interior points. For the regime
\kappa\in(4,8)
, we first compute the Hausdorff dimension of
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}
. Since the Hausdorff dimension of a single SLE
κ
curve is
1+\kappa/8

[
RS05
,
Bef08
]
, the intersection
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}
is expected to have dimension
\max\{0,2-n(1-\kappa/8)\}
. If the value is smaller than 1, the intersection is totally disconnected by
[
Fal14
, Proposition 3.5]
. This suggests that for
n\geq 3
and
\kappa
close to 4, the intersection of
n
independent SLE
κ
curves should be totally disconnected. When
n=2
and
\kappa
is close is 4, even though the Hausdorff dimension calculation is not sufficient, we still expect the intersection to be totally disconnected, since SLE
κ
converges to the simple curve SLE
4
as
\kappa\to 4
. Finally, let us comment that the fractional percolation strategy in Section
3
can be adapted to show that for any fixed
n\geq 2
and
\kappa
sufficiently close to 8, the intersection
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}
contains non-singleton connected components (possibly even a topological Sierpiński carpet). This reveals another phase transition in the behavior of SLE
κ
within the regime
\kappa\in(4,8)
.

The case
\kappa=6
may be of particular interest, as it describes the scaling limit of Bernoulli percolation
[
Smi01
]
. The Hausdorff dimension calculation above suggests that the intersection of five or more independent SLE
6
curves does not contain non-singleton connected components.

Problem 8.8
.

Does the intersection of two, three, or four independent SLE
6
curves contain non-singleton connected components?

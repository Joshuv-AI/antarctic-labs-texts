Blow up for nonlinear wave-type equations with perturbed derivatives
F. A. Chiarello, G. Girardi, S. Lucente

Abstract
. We investigate semilinear wave-type equations that can be recast as wave equations with derivatives perturbed by zero-order terms. This framework covers several well-studied cases, including the scale-invariant wave equation. In this setting, we refine existing blow-up results for radial initial data with suitable decay, and identify conditions on the zero-order terms that govern the interplay between derivative perturbations, initial data size, and nonlinearity exponent.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we treat the blow-up for nonlinear wave type equation with perturbed derivatives.

1.1.
Main Result

We consider a vector field
A:=(A_{1},\dots A_{n})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
A_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
.
For all
i=1,\dots n
we introduce the following partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
:

\tilde{\partial}_{x_{i}}:=\partial_{x_{i}}+A_{i}(x).

(1.1)

In particular, the second partial derivative is given by

\tilde{\partial}_{x_{i}x_{i}}=(\partial_{x_{i}}+A_{i}(x))(\partial_{x_{i}}+A_{i}(x)),

and the corresponding Laplace operator is
\tilde{\Delta}=\tilde{\partial}_{x_{1}x_{1}}+\dots\tilde{\partial}_{x_{n}x_{n}}
.

Similarly, given
A_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
we introduce the following partial derivative with respect to
t
:

\tilde{\partial}_{t}:=\partial_{t}+A_{0}(t);

(1.2)

then,
\tilde{\partial}_{tt}:=(\partial_{t}+A_{0}(t))(\partial_{t}+A_{0}(t))
.

We set
\mathbb{A}:=(A_{0},A)
and we call
\mathbb{A}
-derivative the perturbed derivatives in (
1.1
) or (
1.2
).

Let
h\in\mathcal{C}^{1}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
and
F\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
positive functions such that

F(s)\geq|s|^{p},

(1.3)

for some
p>1
.
Given
\varepsilon>0,
we consider the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\tilde{\partial}_{tt}v(t,x)-\tilde{\Delta}v(t,x)=h(t,x)v(t,x)+F(\tilde{\partial}_{t}^{j}v(t,x)),\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=0,&\\
v_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon v_{1}(x),&\end{cases}

(1.4)

with
j\in\{0,1\}
and we investigate blow-up results and lifespan estimates with decaying initial data.

Our main assumption is that the vector field
A
is conservative, namely there exists
U\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
such that

\nabla U(x)=A(x),\quad\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n};

(1.5)

moreover, we denote by
G:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
a differentiable function satisfying

G^{\prime}(t)=A_{0}(t),\quad\text{for any }t\geq 0.

(1.6)

We will prove the following result.

Main Theorem
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let us consider
v_{1}
a radial smooth function satisfying

v_{1}(r)\geq\frac{M}{(1+r)^{\alpha+1}},\quad\forall r>0,

(1.7)

for some
\alpha>-1
and
M>0.
Assume that

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left[\Big(\frac{2-j}{p-1}-\alpha\Big)\log(t)-\max_{s\in[0,t]}G(s)+\frac{1}{p}\min_{\xi\in[0,3t]}U\left(\xi\right)-\max_{\xi\in[0,3t]}U\left(\xi\right)\right]=+\infty,

(1.8)

then the classical solution
v
of (
1.4
) with
j=0,1
and radial
h,U,v_{1}
blows up.

1.2.
Motivation for using the perturbed derivatives

Many well-known equations in literature can be rewritten as a wave-type equation with perturbed derivatives properly choosing suitable functions
A_{0},A_{1},\dots A_{n}
in the definition of
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives.
The very general form is

\tilde{\partial}_{tt}v-\tilde{\Delta}v=F,

(1.9)

where

\tilde{\partial}_{t}:=\partial_{t}+A_{0}(t,x),\quad\tilde{\partial}_{x_{i}}:=\partial_{x_{i}}+A_{i}(t,x),\quad i=1,\dots,n.

(1.10)

Assume, for instance,
A_{0}=0
and
A:=(A_{1},\dots A_{n})\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
In this case, the wave-type equation in problem (
1.9
) reads as

\partial_{tt}v-\Delta v-2A\cdot\nabla v-(\mathinner{\mathop{{\rm div}}}_{x}(A)+|A|^{2})v=F,

(1.11)

that is a wave equation with lower order terms. It is well-known that the presence of lower order terms can influence well-posedness results for the corresponding Cauchy problem.

Moreover, it would be interesting to consider the case of complex valued
\mathbb{A}
; in particular, if
A_{0}=0
and
A
is purely imaginary with
\mathinner{\mathop{{\rm div}}}(A)=0
then equation (
1.11
) corresponds to a wave equation with electromagnetic potential and Coulomb gauge.
Many papers treat this equation by obtaining linear estimates towards some global existence or scattering results (see
[
DA
,
DAF
,
FV
,
GYZZ
]
). Up to our knowledge, no blow up result has been obtained in this setting, for this reason we started from the simplest case of real perturbation of the derivatives.

Considering the opposite case
A_{0}=A_{0}(t,x)
and
A=0
, we gain

\partial_{tt}v-\Delta v+2A_{0}\partial_{t}v+(\partial_{t}A_{0}+A_{0}^{2})v=F,

that is, a wave equation with damping and mass term. Conversely, if we take a wave equation with damping and a mass term of the form

\partial_{tt}v-\Delta v+b(t)v_{t}+m^{2}(t)v=F;

this can be rewritten in the form of
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives if and only if

m^{2}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left(b^{\prime}(t)+\frac{b^{2}(t)}{2}\right),

that is, the possibility to write the equation using the
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives is strictly connected with an interaction among the low-order terms.
On the other hand, if
b(t)=\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}
, then we are treating the scale-invariant wave equation that has been very well studied in the last decade starting from the suitable change of variable that preserves the wave shape of the equation (see
[
DALR
,
NPR
,
PT
]
).

In general, the introduction of the
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives allows one to gain some useful shape-preserving properties; in particular, if
A_{0}(t)=-\beta^{\prime}(t)/\beta(t)
for some
\beta\in C^{1}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
, then one can easily see that

\tilde{\partial}_{t}(\beta(t)f(t))=\beta(t)\partial_{t}f(t)

for any
f\in C^{1}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R}).

The application of this identity, together with a suitable change of coordinates, enable us to prove that our non-linear wave-type problem with perturbed derivatives (
1.4
) is equivalent to a classical wave equation with a perturbed non-linearity.
For this reason, we choose to perturb the derivatives splitting the variables as in definitions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), that is
A_{0}
depends only on
t
-variable and
A
depends only on
x
-variables. In this case the following lemma holds true:

Lemma 1
.

Let us assume that
v=v(t,x)
solves the equation (
1.4
)
where
\tilde{\partial}_{x_{i}}
and
\tilde{\partial}_{t}
are defined in (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) respectively; let
U\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
and
G\in C^{2}([0,\infty),\mathbb{R})
given by equations (
1.5
) and, respectively, (
1.6
). Then, the function

u(t,x)=e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}v(t,x),

solves the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)=h(t,x)u(t,x)+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}F\Big(e^{-G(t)}e^{-U(x)}\partial_{t}^{j}u(t,x)\Big),&\\
u(0,x)=0,&\\
u_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon e^{U(x)}v_{1}(x),&\end{cases}

(1.12)

for all
(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

It holds

\partial_{t}u(t,x)=A_{0}(t)u(t,x)+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{t}v(t,x)=e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\tilde{\partial}_{t}v,

and then,

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=A_{0}^{\prime}(t)u(t,x)+A_{0}(t)\partial_{t}u(t,x)+A_{0}(t)e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{t}v(t,x)+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{tt}v(t,x)

\displaystyle=A_{0}^{\prime}(t)u(t,x)+A_{0}(t)^{2}u(t,x)+2A_{0}(t)e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{t}v(t,x)+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{tt}v(t,x).

Moreover, for all
i=1,\dots,n
we have

\partial_{x_{i}}u=e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}(A_{i}(x)v(t,x)+\partial_{x_{i}}v(t,x)),

and then,

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}u=e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}(A_{i}(x)^{2}v(t,x)+\partial_{x_{i}}A_{i}(x)v(t,x)+2A_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}v(t,x)+\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}v(t,x));

we conclude that

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}u(t,x)

\displaystyle-\Delta u(t,x)=A_{0}^{\prime}(t)u(t,x)+A_{0}(t)^{2}u(t,x)+2A_{0}(t)e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\partial_{t}v(t,x)

(1.13)

\displaystyle-e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}\Big(v(t,x)|A(x)|^{2}+\mathinner{\mathop{{\rm div}}}(A(x))v(t,x)+2A(x)\cdot\nabla v(t,x)\Big)

\displaystyle+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}(\partial_{tt}v(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)),

where
\nabla:=(\partial_{x_{1}},\dots,\partial_{x_{n}}
).
On the other hand, it holds

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{tt}v(t,x)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{t}+A_{0}(t))(\partial_{t}v(t,x)+A_{0}(t)v(t,x))

\displaystyle=\partial_{tt}v(t,x)+A_{0}^{\prime}(t)v(t,x)+2A_{0}(t)\partial_{t}v(t,x)+A_{0}(t)^{2}v(t,x),

and similarly,

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{x_{i}x_{i}}v(t,x)=\partial_{x_{i}x_{i}}v(t,x)+\partial_{x_{i}}A_{i}(x)v(t,x)+2A_{i}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}v(t,x)+A_{i}(x)^{2}v(t,x),

for all
i=1,\dots n
; we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\partial}_{tt}v(t,x)-\tilde{\Delta}v(t,x)

\displaystyle=\partial_{tt}v(t,x)+A_{0}^{\prime}(t)v(t,x)+2A_{0}(t)\partial_{t}v(t,x)+A_{0}(t)^{2}v(t,x)

\displaystyle-\Delta v(t,x)-v(t,x)\mathinner{\mathop{{\rm div}}}(A(x))-2A(x)\cdot\nabla v(t,x)-v(t,x)|A(x)|^{2}.

In particular,
v=v(t,x)
solves equation (
1.4
) if and only if,

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}v(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)=

\displaystyle h(t,x)v(t,x)+F(\tilde{\partial}_{t}^{j}v(t,x))

(1.14)

\displaystyle-A_{0}^{\prime}(t)v(t,x)-2A_{0}(t)\partial_{t}v(t,x)-A_{0}(t)^{2}v(t,x)

\displaystyle+v(t,x)\mathinner{\mathop{{\rm div}}}(A(x))+2A(x)\cdot\nabla v(t,x)+v(t,x)|A(x)|^{2}.

Repleacing (
1.14
) in (
1.13
) we get

\displaystyle\partial_{tt}u(t,x)-\Delta u(t,x)=h(t,x)u(t,x)+e^{G(t)}e^{U(x)}F\Big(e^{-G(t)}e^{-U(x)}\partial_{t}^{j}u(t,x)\Big).

\square

1.3.
Radial assumption and critical exponent

Many authors have studied existence theorems and blow up results for the following non-linear classical wave equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}v(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)=|\partial_{t}^{j}v(t,x)|^{p},\quad(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=0,&\\
v_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon v_{1}(x).&\end{cases}

(1.15)

which corresponds to problem (
1.4
) with
\mathbb{A}\equiv 0
,
h\equiv 0
and
j\in\{0,1\}
. In the case of compactly supported initial data the critical exponent for problem (
1.15
) is

p_{j}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{n+1+\sqrt{n^{2}+10n-7}}{2(n-1)}\quad&\text{ if }j=0,\\
\frac{n+1}{n-1}\quad&\text{ if }j=1;\end{cases}

(1.16)

namely, problem (
1.15
) admits a global solution for
small
initial data if
p>p_{j}(n)
, and all the solutions to (
1.15
) blow up in finite time if
1<p\leq p_{j}(n)
. For
j=0
the exponent
p_{0}(n)
is known as
Strauss exponent
and it is the positive root of the polynomial equation
(n-1)p^{2}-(n+1)p-2=0
; whereas for
j=1
the exponent
p_{1}(n)
is known as
Glassey exponent
. A complete bibliography about global existence results can be found in
[
GLS
]
.

It is well-known that if the initial data has noncompact
support, one can find blowing-up solutions even for
p>p_{0}(n)
; in particular, slow decay yields blowing-up solutions while rapid
decay assures global solution.

More in details, suppose that
v_{1}
is radially symmetric and satisfies

\displaystyle v_{1}(x)\geq\frac{M}{(1+|x|)^{\alpha+1}},\quad M>0,\;\alpha>-1;

(1.17)

for
j=0
in
[
T
]
the author proved that any classical solution to (
1.15
) blows up in finite time for any
1<p\leq p_{c}(\alpha)
where

p_{c}(\alpha):=1+\frac{2}{\alpha}

independently on the space dimension
n\geq 2
. Later we will refer to
p_{c}(\alpha)
as
slowing decaying critical exponent
(see Section
2.1
). Similarly, for
j=1
in
[
T
]
has been proven that any classical solution to (
1.15
) blows up in finite time for any
1<p\leq 1+1/(\alpha+1)
.
Additionally, from
[
T
]
we know that the maximal existence time
T(\varepsilon)
of classical solutions to (
1.15
) satisfies the following lifespan estimate

T(\varepsilon)\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{p-1}{2-jp-(p-1)\alpha}},

(1.18)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
t
and
\varepsilon
.
The proof of blow-up results in high space dimension
n\geq 4
requires the assumption of radial symmetry of the initial data, which could be avoided in low space dimension
n=2,3
due to the positivity of the solution to the free wave equation with positive initial data
[
A
,
AT
]
. In our case, the
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives that perturb the wave operator introduce greater complexity to the problem. Therefore, we assume radial symmetry in every dimension. On the other hand, the radial assumption is also necessary in
[
K
]
and
[
KK
]
to prove the existence of slowing decaying radial solutions to (
1.15
) with
j=0
for every
p>p_{c}(\alpha)
.

As already mentioned in Section
1.2
, the scale-invariant equation can be rewritten in the form (
1.9
), and the results related to it in the slowly decaying case have been studied in
[
CGL
]
and
[
GiLu
]
. More precisely, we considered the following problem

{\small\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}v(t,x)-\Delta v(t,x)+\frac{\mu}{(1+t)}v_{t}+\frac{\mu}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}-1\right)\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}v=|\tilde{\partial}_{t}^{j}v(t,x)|^{p},\hskip 9.24994pt(t,x)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
v(0,x)=0,&\\
v_{t}(0,x)=\varepsilon v_{1}(x),&\end{cases}}

(1.19)

which corresponds to problem (
1.4
) with
A_{0}=\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}
,
A\equiv 0
,
h\equiv 0
and
j\in\{0,1\}
.

If
j=0
and
v_{1}
has compact support, then the presence of scale invariant damping and mass in problem (
1.19
) determine a shift of the critical exponent which becomes
p_{0}(n+\mu)
(see
[
PT
]
and the references therein for additional details). In
[
CGL
]
we removed the compact support assumption for
v_{1}
and assume that
v_{1}
is a slowly decaying initial data satisfying (
1.17
), and we proved that any solution to (
1.19
) blows up in finite time for any
1<p\leq p_{c}(\alpha+\mu/2)
. This confirm the shift of the critical exponents. In addition we showed that the lifespan satisfies

T(\varepsilon)\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{4-(\mu+2\alpha)(p-1)}},

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
. A detailed review about global existence results for problem (
1.19
) is provided in
[
CGL
]
.

In
[
PT
]
the authors considered the same scale-invariant damped wave equation with mass (
1.19
), with classical derivative-type nonlinearity
|\partial_{t}u|^{p}
in place of the perturbed derivative-type nonlinearity
|\tilde{\partial}_{t}u|^{p}
; for small initial data
v_{1}
with compact support, they proved that any solution blows up in finite time for any
1<p\leq p_{1}(n+\mu)
. In
[
GiLu
]
we considered problem (
1.19
) with a perturbed derivative-type non-linearity, namely
|\tilde{\partial}_{t}u|^{p}
in place of
|\partial_{t}u|^{p}
; assuming
v_{1}
to be a slowly decay function satisfying (
1.17
) we proved that any classical solution blows up in finite time for any
1<p\leq p_{c}(2+2\alpha+\mu)
and the maximal existence time satisfies

T(\varepsilon)\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{4-2p-(\mu+2\alpha)(p-1)}}.

(1.20)

In this work, we adopt a much broader perspective given by the study of problem (
1.4
), and we recover, as a confirmation, the results in
[
CGL
]
and
[
GiLu
]
.

Although it falls outside the scope of this work, which focuses on blow-up with slowly decaying data, we would like to recall that the case of the scale-invariant wave equation for quasilinear equations was considered in
[
GeLu
]
, where the corresponding
\mathbb{A}
-derivatives appeared at second order.

Blow-up results

v_{1}(x)=v_{1}({\left|x\right|})

Lemma 2

Let
n\geq 2

m=[n/2]
. Given a smooth function
\psi=\psi(r)

\begin{cases}u^{0}_{tt}-u^{0}_{rr}-\frac{n-1}{r}u^{0}_{r}=0,\quad&(t,r)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty),\\

Let
u=u(t,r)
be a solution to the corresponding non-linear Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u_{tt}-u_{rr}-\frac{n-1}{r}u_{r}=H(t,r,u,\partial_{t}u),\quad&(t,r)\in[0,\infty)\times(0,\infty),\\

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{r-t}^{r+t}\lambda^{m}\psi(\lambda)d\lambda,

(2.5)

2.1.
Applying Theorem
1

It is worth noticing that in condition (
2.7
) there is interaction between the decay of the initial datum, the exponent of the nonlinear term and the growth of the potentials of the functions
A_{0}
and
A
appearing in our definition of time and space derivatives. Let us show some examples in which this condition can be written in more explicit form, treating the logarithmic term in (
2.7
) as the leading one. For this reason, we call
slowly decaying critical exponent

p_{c}:=1+\frac{2}{\alpha},

(2.10)

which firstly appears in
[
T
, Theorem 1.2]
with classical derivatives, that is

A_{i}=0
in (
1.1
), and
A_{0}\equiv 0
in (
1.2
). We also say that time or space derivatives are
effective
when they change the critical exponent with respect to
p_{c}
.

We provide several examples that are summarized in Example
4
. This choice allows us to present the various interactions in more detail.

Example 1
.

Denote by
\langle x\rangle=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
. Let us consider the scale invariant case
A_{0}(t)=\frac{\mu}{2(1+t)}
and
A(x)=\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{x}{\langle x\rangle^{2}}.
This means
G(t)=\frac{\mu}{2}\log(1+t)
and
U(x)=\frac{\eta}{2}\log\langle x\rangle
.
Suppose that

\left(\frac{2}{p-1}-\alpha-\frac{\mu}{2}-\frac{\eta}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\right)>0;

H(\tau,\lambda,u,\partial_{t}u)\geq e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p},

and then

u(t,r)\geq\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau\int_{r-t+\tau}^{r+t-\tau}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p}d\lambda.

Exchanging the order of the integrals we get

\displaystyle u(t,r)\geq\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{r-t}^{r}d\lambda

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\lambda-r+t}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p}d\tau

(2.48)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{r}^{r+t}d\lambda\int_{0}^{r+t-\lambda}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{r}^{r+t}d\lambda\int_{0}^{r+t-\lambda}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p}d\tau.

Since
u(0,\lambda)=0
, applying Hölder inequality we may estimate

\displaystyle|u(r+t-\lambda,\lambda)|^{p}\leq e^{(p-1)G(r+t-\lambda)}\int_{0}^{r+t-\lambda}e^{-(p-1)G(\tau)}|\partial_{t}u(\tau,\lambda)|^{p}d\tau.

From (
2.45
) and (
2.48
) this allows to derive:

\displaystyle u(t,r)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8r^{m}}\int_{r}^{r+t}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}e^{-(p-1)G(r+t-\lambda)}|u(r+t-\lambda,\lambda)|^{p}d\lambda

(2.49)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C^{p}}{8r^{m}}\int_{r}^{r+t}\lambda^{m}e^{-(p-1)U(\lambda)}e^{-(p-1)G(r+t-\lambda)}\frac{(r+t-\lambda)^{ap}e^{dp\,\bar{U}(r+t-\lambda,\lambda)}}{\lambda^{mp}(r+t)^{bp}}d\lambda.

Let us define

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{U}}(t,r)

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{U(\xi):r-t\leq\xi\leq r+t\right\},

(2.50)

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{G}}(t)

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{G(\xi):0\leq\xi\leq t\right\}.

Being

it is easy to note that
\bar{U}(r+t-\lambda,\lambda)>\bar{U}(t,r)
and
U(\lambda)<\bar{\bar{U}}(t,r)
; moreover, it holds
G(r+t-\lambda)<\bar{\bar{G}}(t)
and
\lambda^{-m(p-1)}>(r+t)^{-m(p-1)}
.
Thus, we get

\displaystyle u(t,r)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C^{p}e^{-(p-1)\bar{\bar{U}}(t,r)}e^{dp\bar{U}(t,r)}e^{-(p-1)\bar{\bar{G}}(t)}}{8r^{m}(r+t)^{bp+m(p-1)}}\int_{r}^{r+t}(r+t-\lambda)^{ap}d\lambda

(2.51)

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{p}e^{-(p-1)\bar{\bar{U}}(t,r)}e^{dp\bar{U}(t,r)}e^{-(p-1)\bar{\bar{G}}(t)}t^{ap+1}}{8(ap+1)r^{m}(r+t)^{bp+m(p-1)}}.

We can conclude that for any
(t,r)\in\Sigma_{\delta}
the solution

satisfies

Similarly, by induction one can prove that for any

(2.53)

where

\displaystyle a_{k+1}=p^{k}\left(m+1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)-\frac{1}{p-1},

(2.54)

\displaystyle b_{k+1}=p^{k}(\alpha+1+m)-m,

(2.55)

\displaystyle d_{k+1}=p^{k-1},

(2.56)

\displaystyle\ell_{k+1}=p^{k}-1,

(2.57)

\displaystyle C_{k+1}\geq K\frac{C_{k}^{p}}{p^{2k}},

(2.58)

for some constant

independent of
k
.
Following straightforward calculations as in the proof of Theorem
1
, as a consequence of (
2.53
), one can prove that there exists a positive constant

such that

(2.59)

for any
(t,r)\in\Sigma_{\delta}

\displaystyle\log(C_{0})-\tilde{S}_{p,K}+\Big(m+1+\frac{1}{p-1}\Big)\log t-(\alpha+1+m)\log(r+t)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{p}\bar{U}(t,r)-\bar{\bar{G}}(t)-\bar{\bar{U}}(t,r).

The desired blow up result follows if we prove the existence of

such that

Restricting to the line

the condition

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\alpha\right)\log(t)-\bar{\bar{G}}(t)+\frac{1}{p}\bar{U}(t,t(1+\sigma_{n}))-\bar{\bar{U}}(t,t(1+\sigma_{n}))

(2.60)

\displaystyle>\log\left(\frac{e^{S_{p,K}}}{C_{0}}\left(2+\sigma_{n}\right)^{\alpha+1+m}\right).

) (with

\square

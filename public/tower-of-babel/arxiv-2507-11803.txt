Instability of regular planar black holes in four dimensions arising from an infinite sum of curvature corrections Preprint: YITP-25-111, WUCG-25-08
Antonio De Felice 1, Shinji Tsujikawa 2

Abstract
In four-dimensional scalar-tensor theories derived via dimensional regularization with a conformal rescaling of the metric, we study the stability of planar black holes (BHs) whose horizons are described by two-dimensional compact Einstein spaces with vanishing curvature. By taking an infinite sum of Lovelock curvature invariants, it is possible to construct BH solutions whose metric components remain nonsingular at r = 0 r=0 , with a scalar-field derivative given by ϕ ′ ​ ( r ) = 1 / r \phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r , where r r is the radial coordinate. We show that such BH solutions suffer from a strong coupling problem, where the kinetic term of the even-parity scalar-field perturbation associated with the timelike coordinate vanishes everywhere. Moreover, we find that these BHs are subject to both ghost and Laplacian instabilities for odd-parity perturbations near r = 0 r=0 . Consequently, the presence of these pathological features rules out regular planar BHs with the scalar-field profile ϕ ′ ​ ( r ) = 1 / r \phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r as physically viable and stable configurations.



I
Introduction

The existence of black holes (BHs) is ubiquitous in
general relativity (GR) and extended theories of gravity.
In four spacetime dimensions, the vacuum solution of GR
on a spherically symmetric and static (SSS) background
is known as the Schwarzschild geometry
Schwarzschild 1916
.
The Schwarzschild solution has a curvature singularity at the center (
r=0
), where the Kretschmann scalar diverges.
A similar singular behavior also occurs for charged
BHs
Reissner 1916
and rotating BHs
Kerr 1963
in GR.
This is attributed to Penrose’s singularity theorem in GR
Penrose 1965
, which states that, under certain assumptions about spacetime and matter, singularities at the centers of BHs naturally arise as a consequence of
gravitational collapse.

In the presence of specific matter sources, numerous approaches have been proposed to construct nonsingular BH solutions in four dimensions. One well-known example is nonlinear electrodynamics (NED) within GR.
The NED Lagrangian
{\cal L}
is a nonlinear
function of the electromagnetic field strength

F=-F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}/4
, where

F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the Maxwell tensor
associated with a vector field
A_{\mu}
.
With suitable choices of the functional form of

{\cal L}(F)
, it is possible to construct
SSS charged BH solutions that are
free from curvature singularities at their centers
Ayon-Beato and Garcia 1998
;
Ayon-Beato and Garcia 2000
;
Bronnikov 2001
;
Dymnikova 2004
.
However, such nonsingular BHs are ruled out by
angular Laplacian
instabilities of vector-field perturbations near
their centers
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025a
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2025b
.

If we consider a canonical scalar field
\phi

minimally coupled to gravity on the SSS background, the resulting solution is the Schwarzschild BH without scalar hair
Hawking 1972a
;
Bekenstein 1972
.
This no-hair property holds for a broad class of
scalar-tensor theories—including k-essence
Graham and Jha 2014
,
nonminimally coupled scalar fields with
the Ricci scalar
R
of the form

G_{4}(\phi)R

Hawking 1972b
;
Bekenstein 1995
;
Sotiriou and Faraoni 2012
,
and covariant Galileons
Hui and Nicolis 2013
;
Babichev et al. 2016
;
Minamitsuji et al. 2022a
.
A notable exception is the Gauss-Bonnet (GB) curvature invariant

R_{\rm GB}^{2}
coupled to the scalar field
in the form
\xi(\phi)R_{\rm GB}^{2}

Kanti et al. 1996
;
Torii et al. 1997
;
Kanti et al. 1998
;
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018
;
Silva et al. 2018
;
Antoniou et al. 2018
;
Minamitsuji and Ikeda 2019
;
Minamitsuji et al. 2022b
.
In this case, the background geometry is modified from the Schwarzschild solution by the presence of a nonvanishing
scalar-field profile, but a curvature singularity still remains at the center of BHs.

From a geometric perspective, the construction of
regular BHs in spacetime dimensions
d\geq 5

has been investigated.
In the context of quasi-topological gravity
Oliva and Ray 2010
;
Myers and Robinson 2010
;
Dehghani et al. 2012
;
Ahmed et al. 2017
;
Cisterna et al. 2017
, the possibility of eliminating the singularities of higher-dimensional BHs has been explored by incorporating an infinite tower of curvature corrections
Bueno et al. 2025a
;
Konoplya and Zhidenko 2024
;
Estrada and Aros 2025
;
Frolov et al. 2025
;
Bueno et al. 2025b
;
Bueno et al. 2025c
.
In general, this geometric approach induces derivative terms higher than second order, raising concerns about the emergence of Ostrogradsky instabilities
Ostrogradsky 1850
.
Indeed, in some related theories like Einsteinian cubic
gravity
Bueno and Cano 2016

or Weyl gravity
Stelle 1977
, it is known that
the presence of higher-order derivative terms can give rise to
ghost or Laplacian
instabilities
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2023a
;
Beltrán Jiménez and Jiménez-Cano 2024
;
De Felice and Tsujikawa 2023b
.
Moreover, the above prescription of quasi-topological gravity
is applicable only in spacetime dimensions
d\geq 5

Bueno et al. 2023
.

In four-dimensional spacetime (
d=4
), there
have been attempts to extract higher-curvature corrections by employing various
regularization schemes
Glavan and Lin 2020
;
Fernandes et al. 2020
;
Hennigar et al. 2020
;
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Colléaux 2020
;
Fernandes 2025a
.
If we restrict ourselves to local gravitational theories involving derivatives of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
up to second order in order to avoid Ostrogradsky instabilities, the only nonvanishing Lagrangians in four dimensions are the cosmological constant
\Lambda
and the Ricci scalar
R

Lovelock 1971
;
Lovelock 1972
.
The GB term
R_{\rm GB}^{2}
is a specific
combination of quadratic curvature invariants that leads to second-order field equations in arbitrary dimensions, but its contribution to the equations of motion identically
vanishes in four dimensions.
By rescaling the GB coupling constant
\alpha

to
\alpha/(d-4)
and then taking the limit
d\to 4
,
one can extract the contribution
of the GB term
in four dimensions
Glavan and Lin 2020
.
However, this 4-dimensional
Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet (4DEGB) gravity is
plagued by several problems—such as the
divergences in the perturbation
equations
Arrechea et al. 2021
;
Arrechea et al. 2020

and the absence of covariant equations
for a graviton
Gürses et al. 2020
;
Gurses et al. 2020
.

An alternative approach to regularization in
4DEGB gravity is to perform a conformal rescaling of
the metric tensor,
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=e^{-2\phi}g_{\mu\nu}
, where
\phi
is a scalar degree
of freedom
Fernandes et al. 2020
;
Hennigar et al. 2020
.
The resulting four-dimensional action
belongs to a subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski
theories
Horndeski 1974
;
Deffayet et al. 2011
in which
the coupling functions
G_{2,3,4,5}

depend only on the field kinetic term

X=-(1/2)\nabla^{\mu}\phi\nabla_{\mu}\phi
,
where
\nabla_{\mu}
denotes the covariant derivative.
There is also another way of regularization based on
a Kaluza-Klein reduction of the higher-dimensional
EGB theory
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Kobayashi 2020
;
Ma and Lu 2020
.
For a maximally symmetric and spatially flat internal space whose size is characterized by a scalar field, the effective four-dimensional action coincides with that derived via conformal rescaling.
In this scalar-tensor formulation of 4DEGB gravity, there exists a hairy BH solution on the SSS background
Glavan and Lin 2020
;
Lu and Pang 2020
;
Kobayashi et al. 2011
;
Charmousis et al. 2012
.
However, it suffers from a strong coupling problem
associated with a vanishing kinetic term of
the scalar-field perturbation
Tsujikawa 2022
.
A similar strong coupling problem also arises
for the dynamics of perturbations on the isotropic
cosmological background
Kobayashi 2020
.

The conformal regularization performed in 4DEDB gravity
can be extended to the
n
-th order Lovelock
curvature invariants
Lovelock 1971
;
Lovelock 1972

defined by

{\cal R}^{(n)}\equiv\frac{1}{2^{n}}\delta^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}\nu_{n}}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}{R^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}}}_{\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}\,,

(1)

where

\delta^{\mu_{1}\nu_{1}\cdots\mu_{n}\nu_{n}}_{\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}\beta_{n}}\equiv n!\delta^{\mu_{1}}_{[\alpha_{1}}\delta^{\nu_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}\cdots\delta^{\mu_{n}}_{\alpha_{n}}\delta^{\nu_{n}}_{\beta_{n}]}\,,

(2)

is the generalized Kronecker delta, and

{R^{\alpha_{i}\beta_{i}}}_{\mu_{i}\nu_{i}}
is the Riemann
tensor. For each integer value of
n\geq 0
,
the Lagrangian
{\cal R}^{(n)}
leads to
second-order field equations of motion.
We note that the integers
n=0,1,2

correspond, respectively, to

{\cal R}^{(0)}=1
,

{\cal R}^{(1)}=R
, and

{\cal R}^{(2)}=R_{\rm GB}^{2}
.
For the spacetime dimension
d>2n
,
the field equations of motion acquire
contributions from the
n
-th
order Lagrangian
{\cal R}^{(n)}
.
As
d
approaches the critical dimension
2n
,
one can perform a conformal regularization by taking
the following limit at each
order

Fernandes et al. 2020
;
Hennigar et al. 2020
;
Colléaux 2020
;
Fernandes 2025a
:

{\cal L}^{(n)}=\lim_{d\to 2n}\frac{\sqrt{-g}{\cal R}^{(n)}-\sqrt{-\tilde{g}}\tilde{\cal R}^{(n)}}{d-2n}\,,

(3)

where a tilde denotes quantities defined in the
conformally related frame with metric tensor

\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}=e^{-2\phi}g_{\mu\nu}
.

The conformal regularization in 4DEGB gravity
corresponds to the case
n=2
in Eq. (
3
),
yielding the Lagrangian
{\cal L}^{(2)}

in the limit
d\to 4
.
Although we are working in four dimensions, it is still possible
to include an infinite tower of Lagrangians
{\cal L}^{(n)}

with
n=2,3,\ldots
, by taking the limit
d\to 2n

at each order
n

Colléaux 2020
;
Fernandes 2025a
.
In particular, summing over the infinite tower of
{\cal L}^{(n)}

for all
n=2,3,\ldots
leads to a subclass of shift-symmetric
Horndeski theories in four dimensions.

If we apply such theories to the dynamics on an
isotropic and homogenous cosmological background, the initial
big-bang singularity can be replaced by an inflationary solution
characterized by a finite Hubble expansion
rate
H

Fernandes 2025a
.
The background solution in which the time derivative of
the scalar field,
\dot{\phi}
,
equals
H
gives rise to this nonsingular inflationary cosmology.
However, it was recently found that this solution
suffers from a strong coupling problem for the scalar-field perturbation, as well as
Laplacian instabilities in the tensor sector
Tsujikawa 2025
.

In four-dimensional theories containing an infinite sum of curvature corrections, there exist planar BH solutions that are
regular at
r=0

Fernandes 2025a
. The horizon of such BHs has a two-dimensional flat topology.
In GR, planar BHs can be realized in an asymptotically
Anti-de Sitter (AdS) spacetime with a negative
cosmological constant
Lemos 1995a
.
The BHs with a two-dimensional planar topology, which may be interpreted as black membranes, are related to cylindrical BHs (black strings)
Lemos 1995b
and to toroidal BHs (black tori)
Lemos and Zanchin 1996
through the compactification of one and two coordinates, respectively.
Planar BHs can form as a result of the gravitational
collapse of a planar distribution of matter
Lemos 1998
.
Ref.
Cardoso and Lemos 2001
computed the quasinormal modes
of planar, cylindrical, and toroidal BHs in GR, all of which
share the same quasinormal frequencies.

In theories arising from an infinite sum of curvature corrections,
the geometry of planar BHs is modified by the presence of a nonvanishing scalar-field radial derivative of the form

\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r

Fernandes 2025a
. The metric components of such BHs remain regular at
r=0
, but it is not yet clear whether they are stable against linear perturbations. In this paper, we address the linear stability of planar BHs that are regular at
r=0
. Since the two-dimensional topology of these BHs
differs from that of the SSS spacetime,
it is necessary to formulate BH perturbations
on a different background
within the framework of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories.
We compute the second-order actions for both the odd- and even-parity
sectors and derive the linear stability conditions
for the three dynamical degrees of freedom:
one scalar mode and two gravitational modes.
In the odd-parity sector, we show that the BH solutions
with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
suffer from
ghost and Laplacian instabilities near
r=0
.
Moreover, the kinetic term
of the even-parity scalar-field perturbation vanishes
both inside and outside the horizon.
As a result, BHs that are regular at
r=0
are plagued
by an infinitely strong coupling problem.
Thus, these regular planar BHs
are ruled out as stable configurations.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we briefly review the four-dimensional
action of regularized Lovelock gravity obtained
through a conformal rescaling of the metric.
In Sec.
III
, we investigate the properties of
planar BHs that are free from singularities at
r=0
.
In Sec.
IV
, we analyze perturbations of
planar BHs and discuss the issue of gauge choices.
In Sec.
V
, we derive the stability conditions of regular planar BHs under odd-parity perturbations and show that these solutions are generally subject to ghost and
Laplacian instabilities.
In Sec.
VI
, we turn to the analysis of even-parity perturbations
and demonstrate that regular BHs with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
inevitably suffer from a strong coupling problem due to the vanishing kinetic term of the scalar-field perturbation.
Finally, Sec.
VII
is devoted to conclusions.

Throughout this paper, we use units in which the speed of light
c
, the reduced Planck constant
\hbar
, and the reduced Planck mass
M_{\rm Pl}
are all set to 1.

IV
Perturbations around the planar BH

The linear stability of planar BHs can be
analyzed by considering perturbations
on the background (
16
).
We write the metric tensor in the form

g_{\mu\nu}=\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
,
where
\bar{g}_{\mu\nu}
is the background
value and
h_{\mu\nu}
is the metric perturbation.
In general, the components of

h_{\mu\nu}
are given by

\displaystyle h_{tt}=f(r)H_{0}(t,r)Z(x_{2},x_{3}),\quad h_{tr}=H_{1}(t,r)Z(x_{2},x_{3}),

\displaystyle h_{ta}=h_{0}(t,r)\partial_{a}Z(x_{2},x_{3})+Q(t,r)\varepsilon_{ab}\delta^{bc}\partial_{c}Z(x_{2},x_{3})\,,

\displaystyle h_{rr}=h^{-1}(r)H_{2}(t,r)Z(x_{2},x_{3}),

\displaystyle h_{ra}=h_{1}(t,r)\partial_{a}Z(x_{2},x_{3})+W(t,r)\varepsilon_{ab}\delta^{bc}\partial_{c}Z(x_{2},x_{3})\,,

\displaystyle h_{ab}=r^{2}K(t,r)\delta_{ab}Z(x_{2},x_{3})+r^{2}G(t,r)\partial_{a}\partial_{b}Z(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}U(t,r)[{\varepsilon_{a}}^{c}\partial_{c}\partial_{b}Z(x_{2},x_{3})+{\varepsilon_{b}}^{c}\partial_{c}\partial_{a}Z(x_{2},x_{3})],

(45)

where
H_{0}
,
H_{1}
,
h_{0}
,
Q
,
H_{2}
,

h_{1}
,
W
,
K
,
G
,
U
are functions
of
t
and
r
,
Z
is a function of
x_{2}

and
x_{3}
, the subscripts
a
,
b
,
c

denote either
x_{2}
or
x_{3}
,

\partial_{a}Z\equiv\partial Z/\partial a
,
and
\varepsilon_{ab}
is an
antisymmetric symbol with

\varepsilon_{x_{2}x_{3}}=1
.
Since we are considering the two-dimensional flat space with coordinates
(x_{2},x_{3})
, we can use partial derivatives instead of covariant derivatives—for example, writing
\partial_{a}\partial_{b}Z
in place of
\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}Z
.
We also decompose the scalar field

\phi
, as

\displaystyle\phi=\bar{\phi}(r)+\delta\phi(t,r)Z(x_{2},x_{3})\,,

(46)

where
\bar{\phi}(r)
denotes the background component,

H_{0}

H_{1}
,

(47)

is the eigenvalue corresponding to
the wavenumber associated with the two-dimensional
coordinates
(x_{2},x_{3})
.
Namely,
Z
obeys the differential

\partial_{x_{2}}^{2}Z+k^{2}Z=0

\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}Z+k^{2}Z=0

,

,

(49)

We will use this form of

for deriving the second-order action

of

In general, the change of metric

\Delta h_{\mu\nu}=-g_{\mu\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}{}_{,\nu}-g_{\nu\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}{}_{,\mu}-g_{\mu\nu,\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}\,,

where

(\xi_{V})_{a}\equiv\delta_{ab}\xi^{b}_{V}=\varepsilon_{ac}\delta^{cd}\partial_{d}\xi_{V}

Each variable is further decomposed
in terms of the eigenfunction

the two-dimensional Laplacian.
For instance, we have
\xi_{V}=\xi_{V}(t,r)\,Z(x_{2},x_{3})
,
and hence,

\xi_{V}^{a}=\delta^{ab}\varepsilon_{bc}\delta^{cd}\xi_{V}(t,r)\partial_{d}Z\,.

(52)

The change of the
(x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle\Delta h_{ab}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2r\xi^{r}Z\delta_{ab}-2r^{2}\xi_{S}\partial_{a}\partial_{b}Z

(53)

\displaystyle-r^{2}\xi_{V}\left(\varepsilon_{a}{}^{d}\partial_{d}\partial_{b}Z+\varepsilon_{b}{}^{d}\partial_{d}\partial_{a}Z\right)\,,

where
\varepsilon_{a}{}^{d}=\varepsilon_{ab}\delta^{bd}
. Then, the perturbed fields

K
,
G
,
U
,
Q
, and
W

respectively, as

\displaystyle\Delta K=-\frac{2}{r}\xi^{r}\,,\quad\Delta G=-2\xi_{S}\,,\quad\Delta U=-2r^{2}\xi_{V}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta Q=r^{2}\dot{\xi}_{V}\,,\quad\Delta W=r^{2}\xi_{V}^{\prime}\,,

(54)

where a dot represents the derivative
with respect to
t
.
The transformations of
h_{0}
,
h_{1}
,
H_{0}
,

H_{1}
, and
H_{2}
are given, respectively, as

\displaystyle\Delta h_{0}=f\xi^{t}-r^{2}\dot{\xi}_{S}\,,\quad\Delta h_{1}=-\frac{\xi^{r}}{r}-r^{2}\xi_{S}^{\prime}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta H_{0}=2f\dot{\xi}^{t}+f^{\prime}\xi^{r}\,,\quad\Delta H_{1}=f\dot{\xi}^{t}-\frac{\dot{\xi}^{r}}{h}\,,

\displaystyle\Delta H_{2}=\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}\xi^{r}-2(\xi^{r})^{\prime}\,.

(55)

In the following, we impose the gauge conditions

K=0\,,\qquad G=0\,,\qquad h_{0}=0\,,\qquad U=0\,,

(56)

under which the components
\xi^{t}
,
\xi^{r}
,

\xi_{S}
, and
\xi_{V}

conditions adopted in Refs.
Kobayashi et al. 2012
;
Kobayashi et al. 2014
;
Kase and Tsujikawa 2022

Kase and Tsujikawa 2023

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-\frac{h^{2}\phi^{\prime 3}G_{5,X}}{r}\,,

(61)

\displaystyle{\cal F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G_{4}-h\phi^{\prime 2}\left(\frac{1}{2}h^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}+h\phi^{\prime\prime}\right)G_{5,X}\,,

(62)

\displaystyle{\cal G}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2G_{4}+2h\phi^{\prime 2}G_{4,X}-\frac{f^{\prime}h^{2}\phi^{\prime 3}G_{5,X}}{2f}\,.

(63)

The Lagrangian (
60
) is valid for both
the regions
f>0
,
h>0
and
f<0
,
h<0
.

To identify the dynamical degree of freedom,
we consider the Lagrangian in the form

\displaystyle\tilde{{\cal L}}_{\rm odd}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}{\cal H}\left[2\chi\left(\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}\right)-\chi^{2}\right]

(64)

\displaystyle+\frac{k^{2}}{4r^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{h}{f}}\left(\frac{{\cal F}}{h}Q^{2}-f{\cal G}W^{2}\right)\,.

Varying
\tilde{{\cal L}}_{\rm odd}
with
respect to the Lagrange multiplier
\chi
yields

\chi=\dot{W}-Q^{\prime}+\frac{2Q}{r}\,.

(65)

Substituting Eq. (
65
) into Eq. (
64
),
we find that
\tilde{{\cal L}}^{\rm odd}
is equivalent to
{\cal L}^{\rm odd}
.
Varying Eq. (
64
) with respect to
W
and

Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r^{2}{\cal H}}{k^{2}f{\cal G}}\dot{\chi}\,,

(66)

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{r[2rfh(\chi{\cal H}^{\prime}+\chi^{\prime}{\cal H})+(rfh^{\prime}-rf^{\prime}h+4fh)\chi{\cal H}]}{2k^{2}f{\cal F}}.

Substituting Eqs. (
66
), (
V
), and
their
t
and
r
derivatives into
Eq. (
64
) and performing the integration
by parts, the second-order Lagrangian reduces to

\tilde{{\cal L}}_{\rm odd}=K_{\chi}\dot{\chi}^{2}+G_{\chi}\chi^{\prime 2}+M_{\chi}\chi^{2}\,,

(68)

where

\displaystyle K_{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}{\cal H}^{2}}{4k^{2}{\cal G}}\frac{h}{f^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\,,

(69)

\displaystyle G_{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-fh\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal F}}K_{\chi}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle M_{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{f{\cal G}}{r^{2}{\cal H}}K_{\chi}\left(k^{2}+\alpha_{\chi}^{\prime}-\frac{2}{r}\alpha_{\chi}\right)\,,

(71)

where

\alpha_{\chi}=-\frac{r^{2}h{\cal H}}{{\cal F}}\left(\frac{{\cal H}^{\prime}}{{\cal H}}-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2f}+\frac{h^{\prime}}{2h}+\frac{2}{r}\right)\,.

(72)

The reduced Lagangian (
68
) shows that
there is a single dynamical gravitational
perturbation,
\chi
, in the odd-parity sector.

V.1
Stability conditions

V.1.1
Region with
f>0
and
h>0

In the region characterized by
f>0
and
h>0
,
the coordinate
t
plays a timelike role.
The absence of ghosts requires that

K_{\chi}>0
, which translates
into the condition

{\cal G}>0\,.

(73)

To obtain the propagation speed in the radial direction,
we vary Eq. (
68
) with respect to

\chi
and substitute the WKB-form solution

\chi=\chi_{0}e^{-i(\omega t-k_{r}r)}
into the
perturbation equation for
\chi
, where

\chi_{0}
,
\omega
, and
k_{r}
are constants.
In the large
\omega
and
k_{r}
limits, we obtain
the dispersion relation

\omega^{2}K_{\chi}+k_{r}^{2}G_{\chi}\simeq 0
.
The radial propagation speed, which is
defined as
c_{r,{\rm odd}}=h^{-1/2}{\rm d}r/{\rm d}\tau
in proper time

\tau=\int\sqrt{f}\,{\rm d}t
, can be
obtained by substituting
\omega=\sqrt{fh}\,c_{r}k_{r}

into the dispersion relation.
This yields

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}=-\frac{G_{\chi}}{fhK_{\chi}}=\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal F}}\,.

(74)

The angular propagation speed measured by the
proper time is given by

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}=r{\rm d}l/{\rm d}\tau=(r/\sqrt{f})(\omega/k)
.
In the limits of large
\omega
and
k
,
the dispersion relation yields

\omega^{2}K_{\chi}+M_{\chi}\simeq 0
,
with
M_{\chi}\simeq-f{\cal G}K_{\chi}k^{2}/(r^{2}{\cal H})
.
Then, we obtain
the squared angular propagation speed, as

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=-\frac{r^{2}}{f}\frac{M_{\chi}}{k^{2}K_{\chi}}=\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal H}}\,.

(75)

To avoid the Laplacian instability of
odd-parity perturbations,
we require that

{\cal F}>0\,,\qquad{\cal H}>0\,,

(76)

along with the ghost-free condition (
73
).
We note that these stability conditions are
the same as those derived for BHs
on the SSS background
Kobayashi et al. 2012
.

V.1.2
Region with
f<0
and
h<0

In the region characterized by
f<0
and
h<0
,
the coordinate
r
plays a timelike role.
In this regime, the no-ghost condition
corresponds to
G_{\chi}>0
,
which translates to

{\cal F}>0\,.

(77)

To obtain the propagation speeds of
odd-parity perturbations, we substitute
a solution of the form

\chi=\chi_{0}e^{-i(\omega r-k_{r}t)}
into
the perturbation equation for
\chi
.
In the limits of large
\omega
and
k_{r}
,
the dispersion relation yields

k_{r}^{2}K_{\chi}+\omega^{2}G_{\chi}\simeq 0
. The radial propagation speed

c_{r,{\rm odd}}=\sqrt{-f}\,{\rm d}t/{\rm d}\hat{r}

measured in
proper time
\hat{r}=\int(-h)^{-1/2}{\rm d}r

is expressed as
c_{r,{\rm odd}}=\sqrt{fh}\,{\rm d}t/{\rm d}r=\sqrt{fh}\,\omega/k_{r}
.
Then, we obtain

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}=-fh\frac{K_{\chi}}{G_{\chi}}=\frac{{\cal F}}{{\cal G}}\,,

(78)

whose expression is the inverse of
Eq. (
74
).
In the limits of large
\omega
and
k
,
we approximately obtain the dispersion relation

\omega^{2}G_{\chi}+M_{\chi}\simeq 0
,
with
M_{\chi}\simeq(k^{2}/r^{2}h)({\cal F}/{\cal H})G_{\chi}
.
Since the angular propagation speed
is given by

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}=r{\rm d}l/{\rm d}\hat{r}=r\sqrt{-h}\,\omega/k
, we have

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=\frac{r^{2}h}{k^{2}}\frac{M_{\chi}}{G_{\chi}}=\frac{{\cal F}}{{\cal H}}\,,

(79)

whose numerator differs from
that in Eq. (
75
).

If the three conditions
{\cal F}>0
,
{\cal G}>0
,
and
{\cal H}>0
are satisfied, the odd-parity
perturbation is free from both ghost and Laplacian instabilities.
This stability criterion coincides with that in the regime
where
f>0
and
h>0
.

V.2
Application to concrete models

Let us examine the linear stability of regular
planar BHs discussed in Sec.
III
.
In all these models, we have shown the existence of a background solution characterized by
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
. For this solution, the metric functions satisfy
f=h
. Using these properties,
the difference between
{\cal F}
and
{\cal G}

is given by

{\cal F}-{\cal G}=-\frac{h}{r^{4}}\left(2r^{2}G_{4,X}-h\,G_{5,X}\right)\,.

(80)

The functions
G_{4}^{(n)}
and
G_{5}^{(n)}
,
which are given in Eq. (
II
), satisfy

2r^{2}G_{4,X}^{(n)}=hG_{5,X}^{(n)}
.
Then, from Eqs. (
10
) and (
11
),
we obtain the following relation:

2r^{2}G_{4,X}=hG_{5,X}\,,

(81)

irrespective of the choice of coefficients
c_{n}
.
Then, for the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
with

f=h
, we generally have the following property:

{\cal F}={\cal G}=2G_{4}-\frac{G_{4,X}}{r^{2}}(rh^{\prime}-2h)\,.

(82)

This leads to

c_{r,{\rm odd}}^{2}=1\,,

(83)

at any distance
r
, and hence
the radial propagation speed
is always luminal.
The squared angular propagation
speed takes the form

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=\frac{{\cal G}}{{\cal H}}=\frac{{\cal F}}{{\cal H}}=1-\frac{G_{4,X}}{2G_{4}r^{2}}(rh^{\prime}-2h)\,,

(84)

irrespective of the sign of
f
.
Since the values of
{\cal F}~(={\cal G})
and

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}
depend on the
form of the Horndeski functions,
we explicitly compute them for Models 1 and 2.

V.2.1
Model 1

In Model 1, the metric component
h

is given by Eq. (
29
).
Using this solution, we obtain

\displaystyle{\cal F}={\cal G}=-[10M\ell^{2}\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})r^{3}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\,\times[2M\ell^{2}\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}+(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})r^{3}]/(\ell_{\Lambda}^{4}r^{6})\,,

(85)

\displaystyle c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=-\frac{10M\ell^{2}\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})r^{3}}{2M\ell^{2}\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}+(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})r^{3}}\,.

(86)

Expanding these expressions around
r=0
,
we find

\displaystyle{\cal F}={\cal G}=-\frac{20M^{2}\ell^{4}}{r^{6}}-\frac{8M\ell^{2}(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})}{\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}r^{3}}+{\cal O}(r^{0})\,,

(87)

\displaystyle c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=-5+\frac{3(\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}-\ell^{2})}{M\ell^{2}\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}}r^{3}+{\cal O}(r^{6})\,.

(88)

Since the leading-order terms of

{\cal F}
and
c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}

are negative, both ghost and Laplacian
instabilities arise near
r=0
.
This shows that the BH solution (
29
)
with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
is unstable due to the
rapid growth of odd-parity perturbations around
r=0
.

V.2.2
Model 2

In Model 2, we have

\displaystyle{\cal F}={\cal G}=-\frac{\cosh(x)}{r^{3}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\left[12M\ell^{2}\sinh(x)-r^{3}\cosh(x)\right]\,,

(89)

\displaystyle c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=1-\frac{12M\ell^{2}}{r^{3}}\tanh(x)\,,

(90)

where
x
is defined in Eq. (
41
).
Since
M>0
, we have
x\to+\infty

in the limit
r\to+0
.
In this limit, Eqs. (
89
) and (
90
)
approximately behave as

{\cal F}={\cal G}\simeq-\frac{3M\ell^{2}e^{2x}}{r^{3}}\,,\qquad c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}\simeq-\frac{12M\ell^{2}}{r^{3}}\,,

(91)

which are both negative.
Therefore, the BH solution (
40
) is
unstable due to the presence of both ghost
and Laplacian instabilities
near
r=0
. The difference from Model 1 is that

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}
diverges to

-\infty
in the limit
r\to+0
,
indicating that the Laplacian instability is
even more severe in Model 2.

In both Models 1 and 2, we have shown that planar
BHs regular at
r=0
are ruled out due to the
instability of odd-parity perturbations.
In contrast, in GR, we have

G_{4}=1/2
and
G_{5}=0
,
which leads to
{\cal F}={\cal G}=1
and

c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}=1
.
In models involving an infinite sum over

n
, the function
G_{4}(X)
exhibits a nontrivial
dependence on
X
, as seen in Eqs. (
13
) and (
15
).
This specific feature leads to
negative values of
{\cal F}

and
c_{\Omega,{\rm odd}}^{2}
near
r=0
.

and
h>0
, where
the term
2{\cal P}-{\cal F}
appears
in the denominator of Eq. (
107
),
the same term appears in the numerator
of Eq. (

).

VI.3
Strong coupling problem

We apply the stability conditions for
even-parity perturbations to planar BHs
discussed in Sec.
III
.
To this end, we use the background solution

\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
together with
Eqs. (
18
) and (
19
).

Let us consider Model 1 in the region where

f>0
and
h>0
. Computing the quantity

2{\cal P}-{\cal F}
, we find

2{\cal P}-{\cal F}=0\,,

(113)

at any distance
r
. This immediately
implies that

{\rm det}\,{\bm{K}}=0\,.

(114)

Therefore, the BH solution with

\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
suffers from a strong
coupling problem
in any region where
f>0
and
h>0
.
In Model 1, we also find the
following property:

{\rm det}\,{\bm{G}}=0\,,

(115)

along the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
.
Since the terms inside the square brackets
of Eq. (
107
) are proportional to

{\rm det}\,{\bm{G}}
, the value of

c_{r,{\rm even}}^{2}
is undetermined.

If we consider the region
f<0
and
h<0

in Model 1, we find that both properties
(
114
) and (
115
) hold along
the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
.
In this regime, the radial coordinate
r

plays the role of time, so the condition

{\rm det}\,{\bm{G}}=0

implies that the strong coupling problem
is also unavoidable.
In summary, this BH solution suffers from an
infinitely strong coupling problem at any value of
r
.

We have shown the relations (
114
) and (
115
) for Model 1. However, the same properties also hold for models with an arbitrary power
n
along the solution

\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
, namely, for the Horndeski coupling functions given in Eq. (
II
).
This implies that, irrespective of the choices
of coefficients
c_{n}
, the strong coupling problem inevitably arises in all models constructed from the infinite sum of curvature corrections.
Moreover, this problem persists for all values of
r
,
including
r=0
, where the background BH solution itself remains regular.
In this situation, nonlinear perturbations become uncontrollable, which makes the background BH solution physically illegitimate.
We stress that this strong coupling problem originates from the pathological behavior of the scalar-field perturbation
\delta\phi
along the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the linear stability of regular planar BHs in four-dimensional theories constructed by taking an infinite sum of Lovelock curvature invariants, along with a conformal
rescaling of the metric.
The effective four-dimensional action belongs to a subclass of shift-symmetric Horndeski theories, characterized by the coupling functions

G_{2,3,4,5}(X)
given in Eqs. (
8
)-(
11
). The convergence of these Horndeski functions depends on the choice of the coefficients
c_{n}
.
We have considered two examples:
c_{n}=1
(Model 1)
and
c_{n}=[1-(-1)^{n}]/(2n)
(Model 2),
for which the corresponding coupling functions are given by Eqs. (
13
) and (
15
), respectively.

In Sec.
III
, we discussed how planar BH solutions
regular at
r=0
arise on the background given by
Eq. (
16
). The common feature is that the scalar-field
current
J
must vanish everywhere to ensure the finiteness of
\phi^{\prime}(r)
at the horizon.
In both Models 1 and 2, the solution to the scalar-field equation is given by
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
. This leads to metric components
f
and
h
that remain finite at
r=0
,
see Eqs. (
29
) and (
40
) for Models 1 and 2, respectively.
Indeed, the leading-order term of the Kretschmann
scalar expanded around
r=0
has a finite value

24/\ell^{4}
in both models.

To study the linear stability of regular planar BHs,
we consider perturbations on the background line element (
16
). As discussed in Sec.
IV
, the mode functions associated with the two-dimensional flat topology are given by cosine (or sine) functions of the form (
49
).
The perturbations can be decomposed into odd- and even-parity modes under rotations in the two-dimensional

(x_{2},x_{3})
plane.
We discussed the gauge transformation properties
of each perturbed field and imposed the gauge condition given in Eq. (
56
) to fix the components of the transformation vector
\xi^{\alpha}
.

In Sec.
V
, we obtained the linear stability conditions for planar BHs under odd-parity perturbations in shift-symmetric Horndeski theories.
As long as the three inequalities

{\cal G}>0
,
{\cal F}>0
, and
{\cal H}>0
are
satisfied, there are neither ghost nor Laplacian
instabilities in both regions where
f>0
and
f<0
.
In both Models 1 and 2, we showed that the ghost-free condition for odd-parity perturbations is violated for regular BHs with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
near
r=0
.
Moreover, these same BH solutions are prone to Laplacian instability in the angular direction.

In Sec.
VI
, we derived the no-ghost conditions and radial propagation speeds
of even-parity perturbations for planar BHs
by taking the large
\omega
and
k_{r}
limits.
In the even-parity sector, there are two dynamical perturbations
\psi
and
\delta\phi
arising from
the gravitational and scalar-field sectors, respectively.
For regular BHs with
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
, we showed that the determinants of the
2\times 2
matrices

{\bm{K}}
and
{\bm{G}}
in the second-order
Lagrangian (
97
) identically vanish for all values of
r
, independent of the signs of
f
and
h
.
This indicates an infinitely strong coupling problem, thereby excluding the regular BHs as physically legitimate solutions. We note that this issue arises from the pathological behavior of the scalar-field perturbation
\delta\phi
, whose kinetic term vanishes along the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
.

We thus showed that the regular planar BHs in four spacetime dimensions, obtained by taking an infinite sum of Lovelock curvature invariants, suffer from both instabilities under odd-parity perturbations and a strong coupling problem in the even-parity sector. Since the strong coupling issue arises for the solution
\phi^{\prime}(r)=1/r
in models with each
value of
n
, the same problem persists
in models with the infinite sum over
n
,
regardless of the choice of coefficients
c_{n}
.
Note that a similar strong coupling problem also
arises for the scalar-field perturbation on the isotropic and homogeneous cosmological background
Tsujikawa 2025
. It will be of interest to investigate whether the
four-dimensional SSS BHs that may exist in the same theory can remain stable under odd- and even-parity perturbations.
Furthermore, one can extend the linear stability analysis to the
2+1
-dimensional
regular BTZ BHs arising from an infinite tower of curvature corrections
Fernandes 2025b
.
We leave these issues for future work.

Appendix C: Stability of the
planar BH in GR

In GR without a scalar field
\phi
,
the Horndeski functions are given by

G_{2}=-\Lambda\,,\quad G_{3}=0\,,\quad G_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\,,\quad G_{5}=0\,.

(A.1)

Considering the asymptotically AdS spacetime with

\Lambda=-3/\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}<0

has the following metric components

f=h=\frac{r^{2}}{\ell_{\Lambda}^{2}}-\frac{2M}{r}\,,

(A.2)

where
M
is a constant.
We discuss the linear stability of this planar
BH against odd- and even-parity perturbations.

In the odd-parity sector, the dynamical perturbation
corresponds to the field
\chi
, for which

{\cal F}={\cal G}={\cal H}=1
. In this case,
there is no ghost instability, and the propagation speeds
of
\chi
are luminal in both the radial and angular directions.

In the even-parity sector, the only dynamical perturbation
is the field
\psi
, since the scalar field
\phi

is absent. The reduced second-order Lagrangian is
expressed in the form

{\cal L}_{\rm even}=K_{\psi}\dot{\psi}^{2}+G_{\psi}\psi^{\prime 2}+M_{\psi}\psi^{2}\,,

(A.3)

where

\displaystyle K_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}h^{2}}{f}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\frac{1}{(k^{2}-3h-\Lambda r^{2})^{2}}\,,

(A.4)

\displaystyle G_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-r^{2}h^{3}\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\frac{1}{(k^{2}-3h-\Lambda r^{2})^{2}}\,,

(A.5)

\displaystyle M_{\psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-h\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}[hk^{4}-\Lambda r^{2}(\Lambda r^{2}-h)k^{2}+\Lambda r^{2}(\Lambda^{2}r^{4}-9h^{2})]

(A.6)

\displaystyle/(k^{2}-3h-\Lambda r^{2})^{3}\,,

where we have not set

for generality.
In the regime characterized by
f=h>0

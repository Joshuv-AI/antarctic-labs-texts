Information theoretic measures within Schrödinger-Dunkl framework in spherical coordinates
Akash Halder and Amlan K. Roy

Abstract




Abstract

In this article, we have presented analytical solution of Schrödinger-Dunkl equation with Deng-Fan molecular potential in spherical coordinates. The ro-vibrational energy of some selected diatomic molecules ScH, TiH, VH and CrH are obtained under a simple, new approximation to the centrifugal term in presence of three reflection operators and Dunkl parameters. The angular wave functions are obtained in terms of Jacobi polynomial, whereas radial wave functions in terms of hypergeometric function. The analytical results of Shannon entropy, expectation, Heisenberg uncertainty, entropic moment, disequilibrium, Rényi entropy and Tsallis entropy of marginal density (radial
r
, and angular
\theta,\,\phi
) and total density functions are obtained in Schrödinger-Dunkl system with respect to the weighted Lebesgue measure. This has been possible by making use of factorization method for Shannon entropy. The absolute per cent deviation between the analytical and numerical results for all the information theoretic measures remain well within 0.0001%. The effect of reflection operators on angular wave solutions and on information theoretic measures have been investigated. In essence, a number of statistical measures have been reported for Deng-Fan potential in the Dunkl-Schrödinger framework.

Keywords:
Schrödinger-Dunkl equation, Dunkl derivative, Heisenberg uncertainty, Shannon entropy, Rényi entropy, Tsallis entropy, Deng-Fan potential, reflection operator

Introduction

In 1950, a novel quantization method was proposed by Wigner
[
1
]
. A kind of deformation of Heisenberg algebra was introduced, where the product of Wigner parameter
\mu
and parity operator in the traditional position-momentum communication relation, i.e.,
[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}]=i\hbar(1+2\mu\widehat{R})
, with
\widehat{R}f(x)=f(-x)
, does not change the equation of motion. This is done though the Dunkl operator
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
, which is expressed in terms of the differential-difference and reflection operators. The deformation of quantum mechanics has been a subject of considerable research in last few decades. These operators have emerged in a number of important areas of mathematical physics, such as in the analysis of polynomials in multiple variables having discrete symmetry
[
4
,
5
]
, representation of complete set of quantum integrals in terms of Dunkl derivative
[
6
]
, application of quantum Calogero-Sutherland model
[
7
]
in conformal field theory, bosonization of sypersymmetric quantum mechanics
[
8
]
, anyons in (2+1) and (1+1) dimensions
[
9
]
, para-fields and para-statistics
[
10
]
.

In literature, the most studied system is harmonic oscillator, due to its important connection to various quantum systems. The eigenfunctions of 1D Dunkl oscillator has been given in terms of
\nu
-deformed Hermite polynomial
[
11
]
. Solutions for (1+1)-dimensional Dirac-Dunkl oscillator has been found via the
SU(1,1)
algebraic approach
[
12
]
.
Exact
solutions of the
generalized
Dunkl oscillator was presented
[
13
]
, along with the thermodynamic properties (Helmholtz free energy, internal energy, specific heat capacity, entropy). The thermal properties of relativistic Dunkl-Klein-Gordon and Dunkl-Dirac oscillators have been reported recently
[
14
]
. The coherent states for real and complex variables by means of the Segal-Bargmann transformation of Dunkl-type has been published
[
15
]
. The momentum representation with Dunkl derivatives in 1D has been presented by constructing Dunkl-Heisenberg relation
[
16
]
, through the
\nu
-deformed exponential and
\nu
-deformed trigonometric function. Recently various entropic measures such as Rényi entropy, Fisher information, disequilibrium, relative entropies, as well as associated Jensen divergences have been provided
[
17
]
.

The superintegrability of isotropic Dunkl oscillator model in a plane has been investigated in Cartesian and polar coordinates
[
18
]
. The coherent states and its time evolution in a plane was considered in a 2D Dunkl oscillator
[
19
]
. The Dunkl-Klein-Gordon equation in 2D has been solved by an algebraic method
[
20
]
. The Landau levels of (2+1)-dimensional Klein-Gordon oscillator in presence of an external magnetic field were also pursued
[
21
]
. Eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of 2D
generalized
Dunkl-Klein-Gordon relativistic equation has been reported
[
22
]
via a Nikiforov-Uvarov (NU) method. Exact solutions are also reported for 2D Dirac Dunkl oscillator in presence of a uniform magnetic field
[
23
]
.

Likewise, the isotropic 3D Dunkl oscillator is also well studied. In Cartesian, polar (cylindrical) and spherical coordinates, the relevant Schrödinger equation is
exactly
solvable, admits separation of variables and maximally superintegrable
[
24
]
. The eigenfunctions are expressed in terms of generalized Hermite polynomials; the radial and angular solutions are obtained in terms of generalized Hermite, Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials. In parallel, the relativistic Dirac-Klein-Gordon oscillator in Cartesian and spherical coordinates become separable—the eigenfunctions are written in terms of associated Laguerre and Jacobi polynomials
[
25
]
.
Exact
solutions of 3D generalized Dunkl oscillator in Cartesian coordinates are available
[
26
]
.

Apart from harmonic oscillator, only very few works are available on other potentials of interest within the Dunkl framework. Only recently, some model pedagogic systems, such as particle in a 1D box, as well as radial and angular solutions of certain central-field problems, such as free particle spherical wave, pseudoharmonic oscillator and Mie-type potentials
[
27
]
have been considered. The Dunkl-Coulomb problem in a plane is maximally superintegrable and
exactly
solvable (as products of Laguerre polynomial and Dunkl harmonics on circle)
[
28
]
. The 2D Dunkl-Coulomb problem in polar coordinates has been
exactly
solved and Perelomov coherent states are constructed for radial part
[
29
]
. The energy spectrum as well as wave function of the 3D problem are obtained by means of a spectrum generating algebra technique based on so(1,2) Lie algebra
[
30
]
.
Exact
solution of the 2D Dunkl-Klein-Gordon equation has been offered for Coulomb potential
[
20
]
. Relativistic Klein-Gordon equation was solved for generalized Dunkl anharmonic oscillator in 2D
[
22
]
. Eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of Dirac-Klein-Gordon equation for Coulomb potential and Dirac fine structure has been published recently
[
25
]
. Using the Lewis-Riesenfeld method, exact solution of time dependent oscillator was defined within Dunkl formalism
[
31
]
.

From the above consideration, it appears that there is a lack of work on the Dunkl-Schrödinger equation for problems other than the few model potentials mentioned. The purpose of this communication is to offer such a treatment for the important Deng-Fan molecular potential, introduced nearly 70 years ago
[
32
]
. This three-parameter model potential has found considerable relevance in the parlance of molecular vibration. A characteristic feature is that in the limiting values of internuclear distance, this provides a correct qualitative asymptotic value. Attempts have been made to calculate eigenvalues and eigenvalues through a number of attractive elegant methods such as exact solvability approach
[
33
]
, algebraic method
[
34
]
, supersymmetric shape invariance
[

]
, NU method
[

,

, factorization method
[

, generalized pseudospectral method
[
39

, Feynman path integral formalism
[
40
]
, exact quantization rule
[

, etc.

The Dunkl angular momentum operator contains the reflection operators and therefore, reflection operators have significant effecftive role on angular wave solutions
[
24
,
30
,
27
,
31
]
. So, the reflection operators have important contribution on information theoretic measure of angular wave functions.

At first, the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the relevant Dunkl-Schrödinger equation of the Deng-Fan potential are obtained by means of the NU method, utilizing a simple yet effective modified Pekeris approximation for centrifugal term, recently proposed and succesfully applied by us. This is elaborated in Sec.
2
. This will extend the scope of application of the Dunkl operator in physical problems. Then a detailed analysis of energy with respect to Dunkl parameters is given for four diatomic molecules (ScH, TiH, VH, CrH), in ground and low-lying excited states (including both
\ell=0
and
\ell\neq 0
states). Results are presented in tabular and graphical format. Various expectations like
\langle r^{n}\rangle
(for

) as well as Heisenberg uncertainty product is offered in Sec.
3
. Next, using this wave function, expression for radial, angular as well as composite Shannon entropy (

5
. The effect of reflection operators on information theoretic measures are investigated. In all cases representative results are given for same four molecules. Finally a few concluding remarks are made in Sec.
6
.

2
Solution of Schrödinger-Dunkl equation in spherical coordinates

The time-independent Schrödinger-Dunkl equation in three dimensional cartesian coordinates can be written as,

\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2\mu}\nabla_{D}^{2}+V(\mathbf{r})\right]\psi(\mathbf{r})=E\psi(\mathbf{r}),

(1)

where the Dunkl-Laplacian operator is defined by,

\nabla_{D}^{2}=D_{x}^{2}+D_{y}^{2}+D_{z}^{2}.

(2)

The Dunkl derivatives along the three coordinate axes are given by,

\begin{array}[]{ll}D_{x}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\frac{\mu_{x}}{x}(1-\widehat{R}_{x}),D_{y}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\frac{\mu_{x}}{y}(1-\widehat{R}_{y}),D_{z}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\mu_{x}}{z}(1-\widehat{R}_{z}),\end{array}

(3)

where
\widehat{R}
’s denote the reflection operators along
x,y,z
axes, as follows,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{R}_{x}f(x,y,z)=f(-x,y,z),\widehat{R}_{y}f(x,y,z)=f(x,-y,z),\widehat{R}_{z}f(x,y,z)=f(x,y,-z),\end{array}

(4)

with
\mu_{x},\mu_{y},\mu_{z}
signifying the Dunkl parameters. Now using the following relations,

\begin{array}[]{ll}x=r\cos\phi\sin\theta,y=r\sin\phi\sin\theta,z=r\cos\theta,\end{array}

(5)

the Dunkl-Laplacian operator,
\nabla_{D}^{2}
can be written as
[
24
,
27
]
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla_{D}^{2}&=M_{r}-\frac{L_{D}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}r^{2}},\end{array}

(6)

where
L_{D}^{2}=-\hbar^{2}\left(N_{\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}B_{\phi}\right)
represents the angular Dunkl momentum operator, while
N_{\theta}
and
B_{\phi}
are given by the following expressions,

\begin{array}[]{ll}M_{r}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2a}}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^{2a}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\right),~a=1+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+\mu_{z},\\
N_{\theta}&=\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\right)+2\left[(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y})\cot\theta-\mu_{z}\tan\theta\right]\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\frac{\mu_{z}}{\cos^{2}\theta}(1-\widehat{R}_{z}),\\
B_{\phi}&=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\phi^{2}}-2\left(\mu_{x}\tan\phi-\mu_{y}\cot\phi\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}-\frac{\mu_{x}}{\cos^{2}\phi}(1-\widehat{R}_{x})-\frac{\mu_{y}}{\sin^{2}\phi}(1-\widehat{R}_{y}).\end{array}

(7)

Here the reflection operators, in the spherical coordinates, act as given below,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\widehat{R}_{x}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=\psi(r,\theta,\pi-\phi)=s_{1}\psi(r,\theta,\phi),\\
\widehat{R}_{y}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=\psi(r,\theta,-\phi)=s_{2}\psi(r,\theta,\phi),\\
\widehat{R}_{z}\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=\psi(r,\pi-\theta,\phi)=s_{3}\psi(r,\theta,\phi).\end{array}

(8)

Now, let,

\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=\frac{R(r)}{r^{a}}H(\theta)G(\phi)

(9)

be the solution of our desired Schrödinger equation,

H\psi(r,\theta,\phi)=E\psi(r,\theta,\phi).

(10)

Then one obtains,

B_{\phi}G(\phi)=\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}G(\phi),

(11)

\left(N_{\theta}+\frac{\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}}{\sin^{2}\theta}\right)H(\theta)=\epsilon^{(\theta)}_{\ell,m}H(\theta),

(12)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}U}{dr^{2}}+\frac{2a}{r}\frac{dU}{dr}+\left[\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}(E-V)+\frac{\epsilon^{(\theta)}_{\ell,m}}{r^{2}}\right]U(r)=0,~U=\frac{R(r)}{r^{a}}.

(13)

In order to solve the
\phi
equation, Eq. (
11
), we consider a transformation,
p=\cos 2\phi
, leading to,

(1-p^{2})\frac{d^{2}G}{dp^{2}}+\left[\mu_{x}-\mu_{y}-(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+1)p\right]\frac{dG}{dp}-\left[\frac{\mu_{x}(1-\widehat{R}_{x})}{2(1+p)}+\frac{\mu_{y}(1-\widehat{R}_{y})}{2(1-p)}+\frac{\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}}{4}\right]G=0.

(14)

Now let us substitute,

G(\phi)=(1-p)^{l_{1}}(1+p)^{l_{2}}F^{(\phi)}(p),

(15)

in the above equation. This gives the following equation,

(1-p^{2})\frac{d^{2}F^{(\phi)}}{dp^{2}}+\left[\beta_{1}-\alpha_{1}-(\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+2)p\right]\frac{dF^{(\phi)}}{dp}+m(m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1)F^{(\phi)}=0,

(16)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{1}=2l_{1}+\mu_{y}-\frac{1}{2},\beta_{1}=2l_{2}+\mu_{x}-\frac{1}{2},\\
4l_{1}\left(l_{1}+\mu_{x}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\mu_{x}(1-s_{2})=0\implies l_{1}=\frac{1-s_{2}}{4},\\
4l_{2}\left(l_{2}+\mu_{y}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\mu_{y}(1-s_{1})=0\implies l_{2}=\frac{1-s_{1}}{4}.\end{array}

(17)

After some algebraic manipulation, the final solution of the
\phi
problem, Eq. (
11
), can be written as,

G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)=N_{m}^{(\phi)}(1-p)^{l_{1}}(1+p)^{l_{2}}\,P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p),~p=\cos 2\phi,

(18)

and the corresponding angular energy is given by,

\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}=-4(l_{1}+l_{2})(l_{1}+l_{2}+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y})-4m(m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1),

(19)

where
P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(x)
denotes the classical Jacobi polynomial. The normalization constant
[
42
]
,

N_{m}^{(\phi)}=\left[\frac{(2m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1)m!\Gamma(m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1)}{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}}\Gamma\left(m+\alpha_{1}+1\right)\Gamma\left(m+\beta_{1}+1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(20)

is obtained from the orthogonality condition,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)G_{m^{\prime}}^{(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})}(\phi)d\chi_{\phi}=\delta_{m,m^{\prime}}\delta_{s_{1},s_{1}^{\prime}}\delta_{s_{2},s_{2}^{\prime}},~d\chi_{\phi}=|\cos\phi|^{2\mu_{x}}|\sin\phi|^{2\mu_{y}}d\phi.

(21)

If
m\rightarrow m-l_{1}-l_{2}
, then
\epsilon_{m}^{(\phi)}\rightarrow-4m(m+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y})
. In this case,
m\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{N}
, when
s_{1}s_{2}=-1
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
when
s_{1}s_{2}=1
. But if
m=0
, then
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
must be equal to one
[
24
]
.

Similarly, to solve the
\theta
problem, Eq. (
12
), we let
q=\cos 2\theta
, and
H=(1-q)^{l_{3}}(1+q)^{l_{4}}F^{(\theta)}(q)
, to yield,

(1-q^{2})\frac{d^{2}F^{(\theta)}}{dq^{2}}+\left[\beta_{2}-\alpha_{2}-(\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+2)q\right]\frac{dF^{(\theta)}}{dq}+\ell(\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1)F^{(\theta)}=0,

(22)

where

\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{2}=2l_{3}+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y},~\beta_{2}=2l_{4}+\mu_{z}-\frac{1}{2},\\
4l_{3}\left(l_{3}+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}\right)+\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}=0\implies l_{3}=\frac{1}{2}\left[-\mu_{x}-\mu_{y}+\sqrt{(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y})^{2}-\epsilon^{(\phi)}_{m}}\right],\\
4l_{4}\left(l_{4}+\mu_{z}-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\mu_{z}(1-s_{3})=0\implies l_{4}=\frac{1-s_{3}}{4}.\end{array}

(23)

After some simplification, the solutions of Eq. (
12
) can be written as,

H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)=N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}(1-q)^{l_{3}}(1+q)^{l_{4}}\,P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q),~q=\cos 2\theta,

(24)

and the eigenvalues are obtained as,

\epsilon^{(\theta)}_{\ell,m}=-4\ell(\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1)-4(l_{3}+l_{4})\left(l_{3}+l_{4}+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+\mu_{z}+\frac{1}{2}\right),

(25)

whereas, the normalization constant is gotten as
[
42
]
,

N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}=\left[\frac{\left(2\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1\right)\ell!\Gamma(\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1)}{2^{\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1}\Gamma(\ell+\alpha_{2}+1)\Gamma(\ell+\beta_{2}+1)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

(26)

from the following condition,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)H_{\ell^{\prime},m^{\prime}}^{(s_{3}^{\prime})}(\theta)d\chi_{\theta}=\delta_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{s_{3},s_{3}^{\prime}},~d\chi_{\theta}=|\sin\theta|^{2\mu_{x}+2\mu_{y}}|\cos\theta|^{2\mu_{z}}\sin\theta\,d\theta.

(27)

If
\ell\rightarrow\ell-l_{4}
and
m\rightarrow m-l_{1}-l_{2}
, then
\epsilon_{(\ell,m)}^{(\theta)}\rightarrow-4(\ell+m)(\ell+m+\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+\mu_{z}+\frac{1}{2})
and
l_{3}\rightarrow m
. In this case,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
when
s_{3}=1
, and
\ell\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{N}
when
s_{3}=-1

[
24
]
.

Thus the energy of angular momentum operator,
L_{D}^{2}
is
-\hbar^{2}\epsilon_{(\ell,m)}^{(\theta)}
. Then the radial Schrödinger equation becomes,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}R}{dr^{2}}+\left[\frac{2\mu}{\hbar^{2}}(E-V)-\frac{\gamma}{r^{2}}\right]R(r)=0.

(28)

In this work, we have considered the Deng-Fan molecular potential
[
32
,
36
,
38
,
37
,
40
]
, having the form,

V(r)=D_{e}\left(1-\frac{b}{e^{\alpha r}-1}\right)^{2},\ \ \ \ b=e^{\alpha r_{e}}-1,

(29)

where the three positive parameters
D_{e},r_{e},\alpha
represent dissociation energy, equilibrium internuclear distance and radius of the potential well respectively. The
effective
potential in presence of the Dunkl parameter is then given by,

V_{eff}=V(r)+\frac{\hbar^{2}\gamma}{2\mu r^{2}},~\gamma=a(a-1)-\epsilon^{(\theta)}_{\ell,m},

(30)

where
\frac{\hbar^{2}\gamma}{2\mu r^{2}}
is the centrifugal term.
To find the solution of radial Schrödinger equation we use a new approximation to the centrifugal term, recently proposed by us and found to be quite successful in a number of molecular potentials
[
37
,
43
,
44
,
45
]
,

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\approx\alpha^{2}\left(d_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{d_{1}(\lambda)\,s}{1-s}+\frac{d_{2}(\lambda)\,s^{2}}{(1-s)^{2}}\right),~s=e^{-\alpha r}

(31)

where

d_{0}(\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{12}+\frac{(1-\lambda)c_{0}}{u^{2}},~d_{1}(\lambda)=\lambda+\frac{(1-\lambda)c_{1}}{u^{2}},~d_{2}(\lambda)=\lambda+\frac{(1-\lambda)c_{2}}{u^{2}}.

(32)

The coefficients
c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
are obtained by the Pekeris-type approximation
[
46
,
47
,
38
,
40
]
, where the potential is minimum at the point
r=r_{e}
. Thus one obtains,

\begin{array}[]{ll}c_{0}&=\frac{1}{u^{2}}\left[3-3u+u^{2}+\left(2u-6\right)s_{e}+\left(u+3\right)s_{e}^{2}\right],\\
c_{1}&=\frac{2}{s_{e}u^{2}}\left(1-s_{e}\right)^{2}\left((3+u)s_{e}+2u-3\right),\\
c_{2}&=-\frac{1}{s_{e}^{2}u^{2}}\left(1-s_{e}\right)^{3}\left((3+u)s_{e}+u-3\right),\\
s_{e}&=e^{-u},~u=\alpha r_{e}.\end{array}

(33)

Note that
\lambda=1
resembles a Greene-type approximation
[
48
]
having well-behaved-ness near origin, whereas
\lambda=0
corresponds to the Pekeris-type approximation (well behaved near
r=r_{e}
). For
0<\lambda<1
, it remains well behaved in domain
(0,r_{e})
.

3
Expectations and Heisenberg uncertainty

The total density function
\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}
is a product of three independent marginal density functions,
[\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}(r)}{r^{2a}}]^{2}
,
[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)]^{2}
and
[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)]^{2}
, which are defined in
r
,
\theta
and
\phi
spaces over the domain
r\in[0,\infty)=\mathbb{R}^{*}
,
\theta\in[0,\pi]
and
\phi\in[0,2\pi]
. The individual density functions are orthogonal to their respected weighted Lebesgue measures,
d\chi_{r}
,
d\chi_{\theta}
,
d\chi_{\phi}
, and form three weighted
L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{*},d\chi_{r}\right)
,
L_{2}\left([0,\pi],d\chi_{\theta}\right)
,
L_{2}\left([0,2\pi],d\chi_{\phi}\right)
spaces
[
18
]
. Next, the composite (total) density function is orthogonal to the weighted Lebesgue measure product,
d\chi=d\chi_{\phi}d\chi_{\theta}d\chi_{r}
, and the corresponding weighted space is
L_{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{*}\times[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi],d\chi\right)
. The expectation of a function or operator
\hat{f}(r,\theta,\phi)
for a given state represented by
\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)
, is given as,

\left\langle\hat{f}(r,\theta,\phi)\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}=\displaystyle\int\left[\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)\right]^{*}\hat{f}(r,\theta,\phi)\ \psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)d\chi,

(40)

where
\left[\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)\right]^{*}
is the complex conjugate of
\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)
. Due to the central nature of the potential, the expectation of an independent operator,
\hat{f}(r,\theta,\phi)=f^{(r)}(r)f^{(\theta)}(\theta)f^{(\phi)}(\phi)
can be expressed separately as,

\begin{array}[]{lr}\left\langle\hat{f}(r,\theta,\phi)\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}(r)}{r^{a}}\right]^{*}f^{(r)}(r)\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}(r)}{r^{a}}d\chi_{r}\,\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{*}f^{(\theta)}(\theta)H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)d\chi_{\theta}\\
&\quad\displaystyle\int\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{*}f^{(\phi)}(\phi)G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)d\chi_{\phi}.\end{array}

(41)

Therefore, the expectation
\left\langle r^{i}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}
can be written as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle r^{i}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{i}R^{2}_{n,\ell,m}(r)dr.\end{array}

(42)

Using the differentiation under the sign of integral, expectation
\left\langle r^{i}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}
can be expressed as,

\left\langle r^{i}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}=\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{i}[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha^{i+1}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\left[\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{d^{i}}{dp^{i}}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k+p,2L+1)\right].

(43)

Furthermore,
\mathcal{B}(a,b)=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}
is the Beta function,
\Gamma(a)
is the Gamma function,
\Psi^{(d)}(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(x)}\frac{d\Gamma(x)}{dx}
signifies digamma function, whereas
\widetilde{B}_{m,\ell}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m-l+1})
denotes the Bell polynomial defined by
[
49
]
,

\widetilde{B}_{m,\ell}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m-l+1})=\sum\limits_{\widehat{\pi}(m,\ell)}\frac{m!}{j_{1}!j_{2}!\dots j_{m-\ell+1}!}\left(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\right)^{j_{1}}\left(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\right)^{j_{2}}\dots\bigg(\frac{x_{m-\ell+1}}{(m-\ell+1)!}\bigg)^{j_{m-\ell+1}}.

Here
\widehat{\pi}(m,\ell)
refers to the set of partitions, such that,
\displaystyle j_{1}+j_{2}+\dots+j_{m-\ell+1}=\ell,~j_{1}+2j_{2}+\dots+(m-\ell+1)j_{m-\ell+1}=m
, and

c_{i}^{(n)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(-n)_{i}(n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L)_{i}}{(2\bar{\varepsilon}+1)_{i}i!},&i\leq n\\
0,&i>n\end{array}\right\},

(44)

(a)_{i}=a(a+1)...(a+i-1)
signifies the Pochhammer symbol.
For
i=1,2
, the following expectations can be written,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle r\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle-\frac{[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k,2L+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k)-\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L+k+1)\right],\\
\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\frac{[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha^{3}}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k,2L+1)\left[\Psi^{(p)}(1,2\bar{\varepsilon}+k)-\Psi^{(p)}(1,2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L+k+1)\right.\\
&\quad\left.\left\{\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k)-\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L+k+1)\right\}^{2}\right],\end{array}

(45)

where
\Psi^{(p)}(n,x)
represents the Polygamma function, defined by
\Psi^{(p)}(n,x)=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\Psi^{(d)}(x)
, and
\Psi^{(p)}(0,x)=\Psi^{(d)}(x)
. Next, we provide two important expectations of inverse power, i.e.,
r^{-2}
and
r^{-1}
, which are found to be,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r^{2}}R^{2}_{n,\ell,m}(r)dr=\displaystyle\alpha[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+k-1}(1-s)^{2L}\frac{ds}{(\ln[s])^{2}}\\
&=\displaystyle\alpha[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lfloor\rfloor}(-1)^{i}\binom{2L}{i}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k+i)^{2}\ln(2\bar{\varepsilon}+k+i),\end{array}

(46)

where
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\lfloor\rfloor}
is a finite series, if
2L\in\mathbb{N}
; otherwise it is an infinite series. The expectation
\left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle
is given as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle\frac{1}{r}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{r}R^{2}_{n,\ell,m}(r)dr=\displaystyle-[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+k-1}(1-s)^{2L}\frac{ds}{\ln[s]},\\
&=\displaystyle-[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{k+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(k+1)!c_{k}^{(n)}\right)}{(k+2)!}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2L}(-1)^{i-1}\ln[\Gamma(2L-i+2\bar{\varepsilon}+k)],~\mbox{if}~2L\in\mathbb{N}.\end{array}

(47)

Note that, for
2L\notin\mathbb{N}
, it needs to be pursued numerically.
Now, one can find the root mean square (RMS)
\left(\Delta r\right)_{n,\ell,m}=\sqrt{\left\langle r^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}-\left\langle r\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}^{2}}

of
r
.
The expectation of the potential function,
V(r)
, is given as,

\begin{array}[]{lr}\left\langle V(r)\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle D_{e}+\frac{b^{2}D_{e}[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+2,2L-1)\\
&\quad\displaystyle-\frac{2bD_{e}[N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+1,2L).\end{array}

(48)

The radial momentum operator,

p_{r}=\displaystyle-i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{a}{r}\right)

[r,p_{r}]=i\hbar

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle p_{r}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=0,\\

(49)

Then the RMS of radial momentum operator can be expressed as,

\left(\Delta p_{r}\right)_{n,\ell,m}=\sqrt{\left\langle p_{r}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}-\left\langle p_{r}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}^{2}}=\sqrt{\left\langle p_{r}^{2}\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}}.

(50)

(51)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}&=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}^{2}(r)}{r^{2a}}\ln\left[\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}^{2}(r)}{r^{2a}}\right]d\chi_{r}.\end{array}

(53)

This can be further recast as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)}&=\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,1)}-\ln[(N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}]+2(N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}\left[\bar{\varepsilon}\,\mathcal{S}^{(r,2)}+L\,\mathcal{S}^{(r,3)}\right]+2a\left\langle\ln[r]\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,1)}&=-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}-1}(1-s)^{2L}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)\right]^{2}\ln\left(\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)\right]^{2}\right)ds,\end{array}

(56)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}^{(r,3)}&=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}-1}(1-s)^{2L}\left[{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)\right]^{2}\ln[1-s]ds\\
&=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+i,2L+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(2L+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L+i+1)\right].\end{array}

(57)

Following
[
55
,
56
,
57
]

\left\langle\ln[r]\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left\langle\ln[r]\right\rangle_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}[R_{n,\ell,m}^{2}]\ln[r]dr\\

&=\displaystyle\ln[\alpha]+\frac{[N_{n\ell,m}^{(r)}]^{2}}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\lfloor 2L\rfloor}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}(-1)^{j}\binom{2L}{j}\frac{\gamma^{(E)}+\ln[2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+j]}{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+j},\end{array}

where

4.1.1
Factorization method for
\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,1)}

The expression for
\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,1)}
, in Eq. (
55
) can be further simplified from a consideration of the hyper-geometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)
, as follows,

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)=a^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}(s-s_{i}),

(59)

where
a^{(r)}_{n}=\frac{(-n)_{n}(n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L)_{n}}{(2\bar{\varepsilon}+1)_{n}n!}
is the leading term of
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)
and
s_{j}(j=1,2,\cdots,n)
are roots of the equation
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+2\bar{\varepsilon}+2L;2\bar{\varepsilon}+1;s\right)=0
, such that
0\leq s_{1}\leq s_{2}\leq s_{3}\leq\cdots\leq s_{n}\leq 1
. Then the integral in
\mathcal{S}^{(r,1)}_{n,\ell,m}
can be expressed as
[
60
,
61
,
59
,
62
,
63
]
:

\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{S}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,1)}&=\displaystyle(N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)\ln\left(\frac{1-s_{j}}{s_{j}}\right)^{2}}{(i+2)!\,\alpha}\mathcal{B}(s_{j};2\bar{\varepsilon}+i,2L+1)\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{n,\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}}{\alpha}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left[\mathcal{J}_{i,j}^{(r,R)}+\mathcal{J}_{i,j}^{(r,L)}\right]-\displaystyle\ln\left[a^{(r)}_{n}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-s_{j}\right)\right]^{2}\\
\end{array}

(60)

where

\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,R)}=\int_{0}^{s_{j}}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}-1}(1-s)^{2L}\left\{a_{n}^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}(s-s_{i})\right\}^{2}(s-s_{j})^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{j}}\right)^{2}ds,

(61)

\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,L)}=\int_{s_{j}}^{1}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}-1}(1-s)^{2L}\left\{a_{n}^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}(s-s_{i})\right\}^{2}(s-s_{j})^{2}\ln\left(\frac{s-s_{j}}{1-s_{j}}\right)^{2}ds,

(62)

are improper integrals and
\mathcal{B}(s_{j};2\bar{\varepsilon}+i,2L+1)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s_{j}}s^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1}(1-s)^{2L}ds
. The incomplete beta function
\mathcal{B}(z;a,b)
reduces to the complete beta function
\mathcal{B}(a,b)
, if
z=0
, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}(0;a,b)=\mathcal{B}(a,b)
.
From the integrals in Eq. (
61
), it is clear that
s_{j}
is the only point of infinite discontinuity such that,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-(1-s)^{2L}\left\{a_{n}^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}(s-s_{i})\right\}^{2}(s-s_{j})^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{j}}\right)^{2}>0,~\mbox{for~all~}s\in(0,s_{j}),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow s_{j}-}(1-s)^{2L}\left\{a_{n}^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}(s-s_{i})\right\}^{2}(s-s_{j})^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{j}}\right)^{2}=0,\end{array}

(63)

and the product function
-(1-s)^{2L}\left\{a_{n}^{(r)}\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq j}^{n}(s-s_{i})\right\}^{2}(s-s_{j})^{2}\ln\left(1-\frac{s}{s_{j}}\right)^{2}
is continuous in
(0,s_{j})
. Therefore, the integral
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,R)}
exists
[
64
,
59
]
. To find
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,R)}
, we express this integral in an alternative form by using a transformation
s=s_{j}(1-x)
as follows,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,R)}&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2L\rfloor}\frac{4\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}s_{j}^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+k}(1-s_{j})^{2L-k}\binom{2L}{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1}x^{k}\ln x\,dx\\
&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2L\rfloor}\left\{\frac{4\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}s_{j}^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+k}(1-s_{j})^{2L-k}\binom{2L}{k}\right\}\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+i,k+1)[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(2\bar{\varepsilon}+i+k+1)].\end{array}

(64)

Note that,
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2L\rfloor}
is finite in Eqs. (
58
),(
64
) if
2L
belong to
\mathbb{N}
, otherwise it is a convergent infinite series.
Similarly, we can say that the integral
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,L)}
exists
[
64
,
59
]
. To find
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,L)}
, we express this integral in an alternative form by using another transformation of the form, such that
s=s_{j}+(1-s_{j})y
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(r,L)}&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1\rfloor}\frac{4\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}s_{j}^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-k-1}(1-s_{j})^{2L+k-1}\binom{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1}{k}\int_{0}^{1}(1-y)^{2L}y^{k}\ln y\,dy\\
&=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1\rfloor}\left\{\frac{4\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(i+2)!}s_{j}^{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-k-1}(1-s_{j})^{2L+k-1}\binom{2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1}{k}\right\}\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(k+1,2L+1)[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(2L+k+2)].\end{array}

(65)

Note that,
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor 2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1\rfloor}
is finite if
2\bar{\varepsilon}+i-1\in\mathbb{N}
; otherwise it is an infinite series and convergent. This completes the radial Shannon entropy function.

4.2
Shannon entropy of the marginal density function
\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2}

Now,
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}
of the angular density function
\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2}
is given by,

\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2}\ln[\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2}]d\chi_{\theta}.

(66)

This can be expressed as follows,

\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}=\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,1)}-\ln[(N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)})^{2}]+2[N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)}]^{2}\left[l_{3}\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,2)}+l_{4}\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,3)}\right],

(67)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,1)}&=\displaystyle-\frac{(N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)})^{2}}{2^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\ln\left(\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\right)dq,\end{array}

(68)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,2)}&=\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\ln\left[1-q\right]dq\\
&=\displaystyle-\frac{2[(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}]^{2}}{[\ell!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell}\frac{2^{2l_{3}+2l_{4}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}+i+1,\mu_{z}+2l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\left[\ln 2+\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}+i+1\right)-\Psi^{(d)}\left(a+2l_{3}+2l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}+i\right)\right],\end{array}

(69)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,3)}&=-\frac{1}{2^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\ln\left[1+q\right]dq\\
&=\displaystyle-\frac{2[(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}]^{2}}{[\ell!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell}\frac{2^{2l_{3}+2l_{4}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}+i+1,\mu_{z}+2l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\left[\ln 2+\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{z}+2l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\Psi^{(d)}\left(a+2l_{3}+2l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}+i\right)\right],\end{array}

(70)

and

c_{i}^{(\ell)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{(-\ell)_{i}(\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1)_{i}}{i!2^{i}(\alpha_{2}+1)_{i}},&i\leq\ell\\
0,&i>\ell\end{array}\right\}.

(71)

4.2.1
Factorization method for
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,1)}

Let
a_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}=\frac{(-\ell)_{\ell}(\ell+\alpha_{2}+\beta_{2}+1)_{\ell}}{\ell!2^{\ell}(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}}
be the leading term of the Jacobi polynomial,
P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)
and
-1\leq q_{1}\leq q_{2}\leq\cdots\leq q_{\ell}\leq 1
denote the roots of the equation
P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)=0
. Then one can write,

P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)=a_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}(q-q_{k}).

(72)

Using this identity, the integral
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,1)}
, can be written as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,1)}&=-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)})^{2}}{2^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}\left[\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,R)}+\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,L)}\right]-\ln\left[a_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}(1-q_{j})\right]^{2}\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{\ell,m}^{(\theta)})^{2}}{2^{a-\frac{1}{2}}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}\ln\left(\frac{1+q_{j}}{1-q_{j}}\right)^{2}\frac{2[(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}]^{2}}{[\ell!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell}\frac{2^{2l_{3}+2l_{4}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\theta)}\end{array}

(73)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,R)}&=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{q_{j}}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\ln\left(\frac{q_{j}-q}{1+q_{j}}\right)^{2}dq\\
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,L)}&=\displaystyle\int_{q_{j}}^{1}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{\ell}^{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{2})}(q)\right]^{2}\ln\left(\frac{q-q_{j}}{1-q_{j}}\right)^{2}dq\\
\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\theta)}&=\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{q_{j}}(1-q)^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{3}+i}(1+q)^{\mu_{z}+2l_{4}-\frac{1}{2}}dq\end{array}

(74)

By using the transformation
x=\frac{q_{j}-q}{1+q_{j}}
, the last integral
\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\theta)}
can be simplified as,

\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\theta)}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{2}+i\rfloor}\binom{\alpha_{2}+i}{k}(1-q_{j})^{\alpha_{2}+i-k}(1+q_{j})^{\beta_{2}+k+1}\mathcal{B}(k+1,\beta_{2}+1).

(75)

If
(\alpha_{2}+i)
is (+)ve integer, then
\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{2}+i\rfloor}
is a finite series sum over
k=0,1,2,\cdots,\alpha_{2}+i
. Otherwise it is a convergent infinite series. Similarly, one finds that the integrals
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,R)}
,
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,L)}
exist and using the transformations
x=\frac{q_{j}-q}{1+q_{j}}
and
y=\frac{q-q_{j}}{1-q_{j}}
, they can be expressed as:

\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,R)}&=\displaystyle\frac{4[(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}]^{2}}{[\ell!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{2}+i\rfloor}\left[\frac{2^{2l_{3}+2l_{4}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\binom{\alpha_{2}+i}{k}(1-q_{j})^{\alpha_{2}+i-k}(1+q_{j})^{\beta_{2}+k+1}\right]\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(k+1,\beta_{2}+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(\beta_{2}+k+2)\right],\end{array}

(76)

and

\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\theta,L)}&=\displaystyle\frac{4[(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}]^{2}}{[\ell!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}\rfloor}\left[\frac{2^{2l_{3}+2l_{4}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\binom{\beta_{2}}{k}(1-q_{j})^{\alpha_{2}+i+k+1}(1+q_{j})^{\beta_{2}-k}\right]\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(k+1,\alpha_{2}+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(\alpha_{2}+k+2)\right].\end{array}

(77)

The series
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{2}+i\rfloor}
is finite in Eq.(
76
) if
(\alpha_{2}+i)\in\mathbb{N}
and finite in Eq. (
77
) if
\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
; otherwise
\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}\rfloor}
they are convergent infinite series.

4.3
Shannon entropy of the marginal density function
\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2}

Likewise,
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi)}
of the density function
\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2}
can be expressed as,

\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi)}=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2}\ln[\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2}]d\chi_{\phi}.

(78)

This can be rewritten as,

\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi)}=\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,1)}-\ln[(N_{m}^{(\phi)})^{2}]+(N_{m}^{(\phi)})^{2}\left[2l_{1}\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,2)}+2l_{2}\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,3)}\right],

(79)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,1)}&=\displaystyle-\frac{(N_{m}^{(\phi)})^{2}}{2^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-1}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-p)^{\mu_{y}+2l_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}(1+p)^{\mu_{x}+2l_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)\right]^{2}\ln\left(\left[P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)\right]^{2}\right)dp,\end{array}

(80)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,2)}&=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-1}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-p)^{\mu_{y}+2l_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}(1+p)^{\mu_{x}+2l_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)\right]^{2}\ln\left[1-p\right]dp\\
&=-\displaystyle\frac{2[(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}]^{2}}{[m!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m}\frac{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{y}+2l_{1}+i+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{x}+2l_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\left[\ln 2+\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{y}+2l_{1}+i+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1\right)\right],\end{array}

(81)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,3)}&=\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-1}}\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-p)^{\mu_{y}+2l_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}(1+p)^{\mu_{x}+2l_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\left[P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)\right]^{2}\ln\left[1+p\right]dp\\
&=-\displaystyle\frac{2[(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}]^{2}}{[m!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m}\frac{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{y}+2l_{1}+i+\frac{1}{2},\mu_{x}+2l_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\left[\ln 2+\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{x}+2l_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-\Psi^{(d)}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1\right)\right],\end{array}

(82)

and

c_{i}^{(m)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{(-m)_{i}(m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1)_{i}}{i!2^{i}(\alpha_{1}+1)_{i}},&i\leq m\\
0,&i>m\end{array}\right\}.

(83)

4.3.1
Factorization method for
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,1)}

Let
a_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}=\frac{(-m)_{m}(m+\alpha_{1}+\beta_{1}+1)_{m}}{m!\,2^{m}(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}}
be the leading term of the Jacobi polynomial,
P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)
, and
-1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots\leq p_{m}\leq 1
represent the roots of the equation
P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(p)=0
. Then one can write,

P_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}(q)=a_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{\ell}(p-p_{k}).

(84)

One can then express the integral
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,1)}
as below,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,1)}&=-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{m}^{(\phi)})^{2}}{2^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\left[\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\phi,R)}+\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\phi,L)}\right]-\ln\left[a_{m}^{(\alpha_{1},\beta_{1})}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(1-p_{j})\right]^{2}\\
&-\displaystyle\frac{(N_{m}^{(\phi)})^{2}}{2^{\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}-1}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\ln\left(\frac{1+p_{j}}{1-p_{j}}\right)^{2}\frac{2[(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}]^{2}}{[m!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m}\frac{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\phi)},\end{array}

(85)

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\phi,R)}&=\displaystyle\frac{2[(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}]^{2}}{[m!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{1}+i\rfloor}\left[\frac{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\binom{\alpha_{1}+i}{k}(1-p_{j})^{\alpha_{1}+i-k}(1+p_{j})^{\beta_{1}+k+1}\right]\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(k+1,\beta_{1}+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(\beta_{1}+k+2)\right],\\
\mathcal{J}_{j}^{(\phi,L)}&=\displaystyle\frac{2[(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}]^{2}}{[m!]^{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\beta_{1}\rfloor}\left[\frac{2^{2l_{1}+2l_{2}+i+1}\widetilde{B}_{i+2,2}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)}{(i+2)!}\binom{\beta_{1}}{k}(1-p_{j})^{\alpha_{1}+i+k+1}(1+p_{j})^{\beta_{1}-k}\right]\\
&\quad\times\mathcal{B}(k+1,\alpha_{1}+1)\left[\Psi^{(d)}(k+1)-\Psi^{(d)}(\alpha_{1}+k+2)\right],\\
\mathcal{J}_{j,i}^{(\phi)}&=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\lfloor\alpha_{1}+i\rfloor}\binom{\alpha_{1}+i}{k}(1-p_{j})^{\alpha_{1}+i-k}(1+p_{j})^{\beta_{1}+k+1}\mathcal{B}(k+1,\beta_{1}+1).\end{array}

(86)

Note that here,
\alpha_{i}+i\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
.

5.1
Entropic moment, Disequilibrium

The entropic moment of the density function
\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)
for an arbitrary state
\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(r,\theta,\phi)
is given as,

\mathcal{I}^{(\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=\displaystyle\int\left[\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}\right]^{\widetilde{\alpha}}d\chi=\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(r,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}\mathcal{I}^{(\theta,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{\ell,m}(s_{3})\mathcal{I}^{(\phi,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{m}(s_{1},s_{2})

(87)

where
\mathcal{I}^{(r,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}
,
\mathcal{I}^{(\theta,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{\ell,m}(s_{3})
and
\mathcal{I}^{(\phi,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{m}(s_{1},s_{2})
are entropic moments of the marginal density functions. The radial entropic moment of the marginal density function is defined by,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{I}^{(r,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\frac{R_{n,\ell,m}(r)}{r^{a}}\right]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}d\chi_{r}\\
&=\displaystyle-\frac{[N^{(r)}_{n,\ell,m}]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}}{(-\alpha)^{2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)+1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n\widetilde{\alpha}}\frac{(2\widetilde{\alpha})!\widetilde{B}_{2\widetilde{\alpha}+i,2\widetilde{\alpha}}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(2\widetilde{\alpha}+i)!}\int_{0}^{1}s^{2\widetilde{\alpha}\varepsilon+i-1}(1-s)^{2L\widetilde{\alpha}}\left[\ln(s)\right]^{2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)}ds.\end{array}

(88)

In a particular case, if
2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)\in\mathbb{N}
, then we can find the analytical form of
\mathcal{I}^{(r,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}
; otherwise it needs to be treated numerically. The analytical form is given in differentiation form as:

\mathcal{I}^{(r,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}=\displaystyle-\frac{[N^{(r)}_{n,\ell,m}]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}}{(-\alpha)^{2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)+1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n\widetilde{\alpha}}\frac{(2\widetilde{\alpha})!\widetilde{B}_{2\widetilde{\alpha}+i,2\widetilde{\alpha}}\left(c_{0}^{(n)},2!c_{1}^{(n)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(n)}\right)}{(2\widetilde{\alpha}+i)!}\lim\limits_{p\rightarrow 0}\frac{d^{2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)}}{dp^{2a(\widetilde{\alpha}-1)}}\mathcal{B}(2\widetilde{\alpha}\varepsilon+i+p,2L\widetilde{\alpha}+1).

(89)

The angular entropic moment of the marginal density function
\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2}
can be expressed as,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{I}^{(\theta,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{\ell,m}(s_{3})&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[H_{\ell,m}^{(s_{3})}(\theta)\right]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}d\chi_{\theta}\\
&=\displaystyle\left[\frac{(\alpha_{2}+1)_{\ell}N^{(\theta)}_{n,\ell,m}}{\ell}\right]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2\ell\widetilde{\alpha}}\frac{(2\widetilde{\alpha})!\widetilde{B}_{2\widetilde{\alpha}+i,2\widetilde{\alpha}}\left(c_{0}^{(\ell)},2!c_{1}^{(\ell)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(\ell)}\right)}{(2\widetilde{\alpha}+i)!}2^{2\widetilde{\alpha}(l_{3}+l_{4})+i-1}\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{x}+\mu_{y}+2\widetilde{\alpha}l_{3}+1+i,\mu_{z}+2\widetilde{\alpha}l_{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right).\end{array}

(90)

Similarly, the angular entropic moment of the marginal density function
\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{I}^{(\phi,\widetilde{\alpha})}_{m}(s_{1},s_{2})&=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[G_{m}^{(s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)\right]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}d\chi_{\phi}\\
&=\displaystyle\left[\frac{(\alpha_{1}+1)_{m}N^{(\phi)}_{n,\ell,m}}{m}\right]^{2\widetilde{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2m\widetilde{\alpha}}\frac{(2\widetilde{\alpha})!\widetilde{B}_{2\widetilde{\alpha}+i,2\widetilde{\alpha}}\left(c_{0}^{(m)},2!c_{1}^{(m)},\cdots,(i+1)!c_{i}^{(m)}\right)2^{2\widetilde{\alpha}(l_{1}+l_{2})+i+1}}{(2\widetilde{\alpha}+i)!}\\
&\quad\hskip 142.26378pt\displaystyle\times\mathcal{B}\left(\mu_{y}+2\widetilde{\alpha}l_{1}+\frac{1}{2}+i,\mu_{x}+2\widetilde{\alpha}l_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\right).\end{array}

(91)

Now, the Rényi entropy of the density function

(93)

(94)

5.3
Tsallis entropy

Lastly, Tsallis entropy of the density function
\rho_{n,\ell,m}
can be written as
[

\mathcal{T}^{(\widetilde{\alpha})}_{n,\ell,m}(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})=-\displaystyle\int\left[\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}\right]^{\widetilde{\alpha}}\ln_{\widetilde{\alpha}}\left[\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}\right]d\chi,\widetilde{\alpha}>0,\neq 1,

(95)

where
\ln_{\widetilde{\alpha}}
is the
\widetilde{\alpha}
-logarithm function, defined as
\ln_{\widetilde{\alpha}}(x)=\frac{x^{1-\widetilde{\alpha}}-1}{1-\widetilde{\alpha}}
, for
x>0
,
\widetilde{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}-\{1\}

\widetilde{\alpha}\rightarrow 1

\ln_{\widetilde{\alpha}}(x)
reduces to the natural logarithm function
\ln(x)

6
Conclusions

In this article, we have considered Schrödinger-Dunkl equation in presence of Deng-Fan potential in three-dimensional spherical coordinates. The resultant radial equation is solved by the NU method using a simple novel approximation of the centrifugal term. This has been proposed by us recently and shown to be quite useful as well as effective for molecular potentials. The ro-vibrational energies and eigenfunctions are obtained in presence of Dunkl parameters, for arbitrary quantum numbers, in terms of Jacobi polynomials and hyper-geometric functions. To find analytical value of
S
, we have employed the factorization method in the Dunkl-Shrod̈inger framework. These are compared and contrasted with the non-Dunkl situation without having the reflection operators. Then
S
, expectation, Heisenberg uncertainty, entropic moment, disequilibrium, Rényi entropy and Tsallis entropy are obtained in analytic form. In all cases the marginal radial and angular quantities, as well as the total measures are offered. Representative illustrative results for ro-vibrational energies, Shannon entropic and moment generating functions are presented for four diatomic molecules (ScH, TiH, VH, CrH), in both ground and excited states. Numerically calculated values show negligible deviation from the semi-analytical results. For all the calculations in this work, the absolute per cent deviation remains well within 0.0001%. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to deal with the Dunkl-Schrödinger equation for the important case of molecular potentials. In Schrödinger-Dunkl framework, all information theoretic measures are obtained with respect to the weighted Lebesgue measure. In the vanishing limit of
\mu_{x},\mu_{y},\mu_{z}
and
\widehat{R}_{x},\widehat{R}_{y},\widehat{R}_{z}
, the information measures compare with the classical orthogonal polynomials
[
65
,
66
,
68
,
69
,
70
,
67
]
. The operators
\widehat{R}_{x},\widehat{R}_{y},\widehat{R}_{z}
, have direct effect on Dunkl angular momentum operator as well as on angular wave functions
H
and
G
. The corresponding effect on information theoretic measures are seen to be oscillating, while without
\widehat{R}_{x},\widehat{R}_{y},\widehat{R}_{z}
, they appear monotone.

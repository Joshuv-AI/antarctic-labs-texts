Dynamics due to competitive flip cycles in active Potts models
Hiroshi Noguchi

Abstract
Nonequilibrium spatiotemporal patterns have been extensively studied. However, a single oscillator or cyclic loop of states is typically employed at each site in theories and simulations. Here, we investigated how competition among multiple identical cyclic loops at each site alters patterns. We simulated active Potts models with standard Potts interactions between neighboring sites in two-dimensional square lattices. When multiple three-state cycles exist in state flips, such as in octahedral and square-antiprism networks, all types of spiral waves comprising the three states are formed simultaneously at high flip energies. However, at lower energies, only one or a few types emerge and switch stochastically into different types. At even lower energies, cyclic changes in single-state dominant homogeneous phases emerge [homogeneous cycling (HC) mode]. At intermediate flip energies, the spiral wave and HC modes temporally coexist in small systems but do not switch between each other in large systems. Conversely, when multiple four-state cycles exist in six-state and cubic networks, one state remains dominant for the entire range of flip energies, whereas the other states occasionally form domains at intermediate flip energies. Therefore, the number of spatially coexisting states can be controlled using flip networks and energies.

II
Models and Methods

Two-dimensional square lattices with a side length
L
are employed under periodic boundary conditions.
The total number of sites is
N=L^{2}
.
In a
q
-state Potts model, each site possesses a state
s\in[0,q-1]
.
The nearest neighboring sites (
i
and
j
) have contact energies
J_{s_{i}s_{j}}
:
Wu 1982
;
Potts 1952

H_{\mathrm{int}}=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}J_{s_{i}s_{j}}.

(1)

In equilibrium systems, each state may additionally possess self-energy
\varepsilon_{s}
,
and the ratio of the forward and backward flip rates for the flipping of a single site from
s
to
s^{\prime}
is given by
\exp(-\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}})
, where
\Delta H_{s_{i}s^{\prime}_{i}}=\Delta H_{\mathrm{int}}-h_{s,s^{\prime}}
is the energy difference between the two states,

h_{s,s^{\prime}}=\varepsilon_{s}-\varepsilon_{s}^{\prime}
is the flip energy,
and the thermal energy
k_{\mathrm{B}}T
is normalized to unity.
At equilibrium, the sum of flip energies along a flip cycle vanishes.
This model is extended to a nonequilibrium framework with nonzero cyclic sums, while maintaining
h_{ss^{\prime}}=-h_{s^{\prime}s}

Noguchi et al. 2024
.
Therefore, the detailed balance can be locally satisfied for flips between neighboring states, but not globally.
For three-state cycles, this corresponds to the rock–paper–scissors relationship.
These types of dynamics can be realized in reactions on a catalytic surface
Ertl 2008
;
Brär et al. 1994
;
Gorodetskii et al. 1994
;
Barroo et al. 2020
;
Zeininger and et al. 2022
and molecular transport across membranes
Tabe and Yokoyama 2003
;
Miele et al. 2020
;
Holló et al. 2021
;
Noguchi 2023
.
Active Potts models with global coupling were studied by Esposito et al
Herpich et al. 2018
;
Meibohm and Esposito 2024
;
Ptaszyński and Esposito 2025
. They called them driven Potts
Herpich et al. 2018
;
Meibohm and Esposito 2024
and nonequilibrium Potts models
Ptaszyński and Esposito 2025
.
The four-state active vector-Potts model is also called the nonreciprocal Ising
Avni et al. 2025
and nonreciprocal Ashkin–Teller model
Noguchi 2025d
.

In the MC simulations,
a site is randomly selected, and

III
Competition among Three-state Cycles

III.1
Single Three-state Cycle

Before discussing the competition among multiple three-state cycles,
we briefly describe the dynamics induced by the single three-state cycle in the three-state active Potts model under cyclic symmetry
Noguchi et al. 2024
;
Noguchi and Fournier 2024
.
Under thermal equilibrium (
h=0
), one of the states dominantly occupies the entire lattice,
and the three phases
s=0,1,2
are equivalent.
At low
h
(
h>0
), these three homogeneous phases change cyclically as
s=0\to 1\to 2\to 0

through nucleation and growth.
This mode is called homogeneous cycling (HC).
At high
h
, the three states coexist spatially, and these domains form spiral waves.
The spiral centers are the contact points of the three domains.
In this study, we call this mode W3 (W
n
for waves comprising
n
states).
At intermediate
h
, these two modes temporally coexist and stochastically switch
in small systems but do not switch in the available simulation periods in large systems
owing to the exponential increase in the lifetime of the W3 mode with the system size.

III.2.1
Homogeneous Mixed State in the Absence of Interactions

Before simulating the spatiotemporal patterns,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2+\exp(-h)+\exp(-2h)}{1+\exp(-h)+2\exp(-2h)},

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{1}+\rho_{3}}{2\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{[1+\exp(-h)]^{2}}{1+\exp(-h)+2\exp(-2h)},

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{3}}

\displaystyle=

IV.1
Single Four-state Cycle

Before discussing the competition among multiple four-state cycles,
we briefly describe the dynamics induced by the single four-state cycle in the four-state active standard Potts model under cyclic symmetry
Noguchi 2025b
.
At low
h
(
h>0

At high

under the interaction of the standard Potts model

The domain boundaries between neighboring states propagate ballistically, but the boundaries between non-neighboring states move
slowly (driven by diffusion).

At intermediate flip energies,

V
Summary

We studied the spatiotemporal patterns in active Potts models with multiple flip cycles.
For the competition among three-state cycles,
three networks were examined: a four-state network of two cycles (with and without diagonal flips),
a six-state octahedral network, and an eight-state square-antiprism network.
At low flip energy
h
, the changes in single-state dominant phases
along the network bonds occur for all networks (HC mode).
At high
h
, all states spatially coexist as spiral waves in all cycles.
At intermediate
h
, spiral waves of single three-state cycles emerge in the cases of the first two networks,
whereas spiral waves of multiple (but not all) cycles emerge in the case of the antiprism network.
The transition from the HC and wave modes occurs via the temporal coexistence of the two modes in small systems.
However, the dynamics are trapped in either mode via hysteresis in large systems.

Regarding the competition among the four-state cycles,
two networks are examined: a six-state network of two cycles and an eight-state cubic network.
Single-state dominant phases occupy the systems most of the time for all simulation conditions,
so that the steady traveling waves are suppressed by competition.
At intermediate
h
, diagonal states often form domains but subsequently shrink slowly.
This is due to the absence of direct flips between the diagonal states.
In this study, we employed the contact interactions for the standard Potts model.
Spiral waves and other forms of dynamics are likely to emerge when different contact interactions are used in active Potts models of four or more states.

In this study, we used a constant flip energy for all flips (except for the diagonal ones for two three-state cycles).
By adjusting the flip and contact energies in asymmetric networks, spiral waves of specific cycles can be generated selectively.
Hence, multiple cycles and states in wave modes can be controlled by choosing the networks and conditions.

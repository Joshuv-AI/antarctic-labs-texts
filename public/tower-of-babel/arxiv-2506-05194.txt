Abstract
. A k k -star decomposition of a graph is a partition of its edges into k k -stars (i.e., k k edges with a common vertex). The paper studies the following problem: given k ≤ d / 2 k\leq d/2 , does the random d d -regular graph have a k k -star decomposition (asymptotically almost surely, provided that the number of edges is divisible by k k )? Delcourt, Greenhill, Isaev, Lidický, and Postle proved the a.a.s. existence for every odd k k using earlier results regarding orientations satisfying certain degree conditions modulo k k . In this paper we give a direct, self-contained proof that works for every d d and every k < d / 2 − 1 k<d/2-1 . In fact, we prove stronger results. Let s ≥ 1 s\geq 1 denote the integer part of d / ( 2 ​ k ) d/(2k) . We show that the random d d -regular graph a.a.s. has a k k -star decomposition such that the number of stars centered at each vertex is either s s or s + 1 s+1 . Moreover, if k < d / 3 k<d/3 or k ≤ d / 2 − 2.6 ​ log ⁡ d k\leq d/2-2.6\log d , we can even prescribe the set of vertices with s s stars, as long as it is of the appropriate size.

Introduction

For a positive integer

The study of this problem was initiated in

In

Proof method

Using a general result regarding orientations with degree bounds at the vertices, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for a graph

-star decomposition with a prescribed number of stars at each vertex (see Lemma

Subgraphs with prescribed edge-densities

In this paper we write
\mathcal{G}_{N,d}
for a random
d
-regular graph on
N
vertices, that is, a uniform random graph among all
d
-regular simple graphs on the vertex set
\{1,\ldots,N\}

There is a closely related random graph model, obtained via the so-called

, that we denote by
\mathbb{G}_{N,d}

N

d

\mathbb{G}_{N,d}
is

\mathbb{G}_{N,d}
is conditioned to be simple, then we get back
\mathcal{G}_{N,d}
. Moreover, for any
d
, the probability that
\mathbb{G}_{N,d}
is simple converges to a positive

as

\mathbb{G}_{N,d}

With the configuration model one can often easily compute or bound the probability of
\mathbb{G}_{N,d}
having various properties. Such probabilities often decay exponentially in the number of vertices

Note that
\log
means natural logarithm throughout the paper. As usual, let

h(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}-x\log x&\text{if }x\in(0,1];\\
0&\text{if }x=0.\end{cases}

We will also use the shorthand notation:

H(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=h(x)+h(1-x)\quad\text{for }x\in[0,1].

In order to check condition (

) of Lemma

in random graphs, we will need a result regarding the number of edges of induced subgraphs of
\mathbb{G}_{N,d}
. Let

be fixed. Given a subset

of density

(5)

F(x,t)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{2}h\big(tx\big)+h\big((1-t)x\big)+\frac{1}{2}h\big(1-(2-t)x\big)-H(x).

The precise result is the following.

5.1.
Properties of

We start with rigorously proving the properties of the function

listed in Proposition
3.3
.

To see (i) we notice that the probabilities

p_{00}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=xt;\quad p_{01}=p_{10}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=x(1-t);\quad p_{11}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-(2-t)x

define a distribution corresponding to

p_{ij}=\mathbb{P}(X=i\,\&\,Y=j)
;
i,j\in\{0,1\}
, where both marginals are distributed
as

p_{0}=x;\quad p_{1}=1-x.

Therefore the Shannon entropy

H(X,Y)=\sum_{i,j\in\{0,1\}}h\big(p_{ij}\big)=h\big(tx\big)+2h\big((1-t)x\big)+h\big(1-(2-t)x\big)

of the joint distribution of
X,Y
is at most

H(X)+H(Y)=2H(x),

with equality if and only if
X
and
Y
are independent (
p_{00}=p_{0}^{2}
), that is, if
xt=x^{2}
. It follows that
F(x,t)\leq 0
with equality if and only if
x=0
or
x=t
.

For (ii) we need to differentiate
F
w.r.t. the second variable
t
:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}F(x,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}x\bigg(-\log(tx)+2\log((1-t)x)-\log(1-(2-t)x)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}x\log\frac{(1-t)^{2}x}{t\big(1-(2-t)x\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}x\log\left(1-\frac{t-x}{t\big(1-(2-t)x\big)}\right),

which is clearly negative for
t\in(x,1)
, implying monotonicity. We will not need concavity but it would follow easily by considering the second derivative.

As for (iii), first we use the identity
h(ab)=a\cdot h(b)+b\cdot h(a)
to rewrite
F(x,t)
as follows:

F(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}xh(t)+\frac{1}{2}th(x)+\frac{1}{2}h\big(1-(2-t)x\big)+xh(1-t)+(1-t)h(x)-h(x)-h(1-x)\\
=-\frac{1}{2}th(x)+\frac{1}{2}xh(t)+\frac{1}{2}h\big(1-(2-t)x\big)+xh(1-t)-h(1-x).

Note that
x/h(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0+
and
h(1-z)\leq z
. So for any fixed
t\in(0,1)
we have

\frac{F(x,t)}{h(x)}\to-\frac{t}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{H(x)}{h(x)}\to 1\quad\text{as }x\to 0+.

We conclude that

(16)

\lim_{x\to 0+}\frac{F(x,t)}{H(x)}=-\frac{t}{2}.

Now we fix
t\in(0,1)
and a constant
c>0
and consider the function

G(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=F(x,t)+cH(x).

Differentiating (w.r.t.
x
) gives:

G^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{1}{2}t\log(tx)-(1-t)\log((1-t)x)+\frac{1}{2}(2-t)\log(1-(2-t)x)\\
-(1-c)\big(-\log(x)+\log(1-x)\big)\\
=\frac{1}{2}h(t)+h(1-t)+(1-t/2)\log(1-(2-t)x)+\left(t/2-c\right)\log(x)-(1-c)\log(1-x).

Then the second derivative is

\displaystyle G^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\frac{1}{2}(2-t)^{2}}{1-(2-t)x}+\left(\frac{t}{2}-c\right)\frac{1}{x}+(1-c)\frac{1}{1-x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\frac{t}{2}-c\right)-x(2-t)\left(\frac{1}{2}-c\right)}{x(1-x)\big(1-(2-t)x\big)}.

Therefore,
G^{\prime\prime}(x)=0
if and only if
x
is equal to

\hat{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{\frac{t}{2}-c}{\left(\frac{1}{2}-c\right)(2-t)}.

Therefore, if
0<c<t/2
, then
G(x)
is convex on
(0,\hat{x})
and concave on

. Furthermore, on the one hand, due to (

is negative for small

is positive at

as

. It follows that

has a unique root. In other words,

for exactly one

. If

We conclude that

must be a bijection between

and (-t/2,0), and the claim follows from the continuity of

Beforehand, note that

\displaystyle\text{if }k\leq\frac{d}{3}\text{, then }\beta

and
\beta\geq 0.1
. As we pointed out, we may assume that

Using Proposition
5.2
with

and

we get the following estimate:

F\left(\beta,\frac{2\beta}{1+\beta}\right)\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{1+\beta}\left(1-\frac{1+\beta}{2}+\underbrace{\log(1+\beta)}_{\leq\beta}-\log 2\right)\leq\frac{\beta^{2}}{1+\beta}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\log 2+\frac{\beta}{2}\right).

We need to see that this is negative. First we consider the case

. Since

\frac{1}{2}-\log 2+\frac{\beta}{2}<0.5-\log 2+0.05<-1/7.

Also note that

\frac{\beta}{1+\beta}=\frac{2k\beta}{2k(1+\beta)}=\frac{r}{2k+r}=\frac{r}{d}.

It follows from (

d\cdot F\left(\beta,\frac{2\beta}{1+\beta}\right)+H(\beta)<\beta\bigg(-r/7+\log\frac{1}{\beta}+1\bigg)=\beta\bigg(-r/7+\log\frac{d-r}{r}+1\bigg)\\
<\beta\bigg(-r/7+\log\frac{d}{r}+1\bigg)=\beta\big(-r/7+\log d\big)+\beta\big(1-\log r\big),

where both terms are negative provided that

.

Finally, we show our strongest threshold: let

C_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{\log 2-1/2}\approx 5.1774\text{ so that }C_{0}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\log 2\right)+1=0.

The weak condition

x_{-}

Furthermore, let

Using this notation (

For a fixed

First case:

The

t_{y}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=t(y,x-y)=\underbrace{\frac{2r}{d}}_{t_{0}}+\frac{2ky}{dx}.

Then, for any fixed

\eta_{x}(y)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\eta(y,x-y)=d\cdot F(x,t_{y})+g(\alpha_{1},y)+g(\alpha_{2},x-y).

Using the formula for

\partial_{y}\big(d\cdot F(x,t_{y})\big)=\underbrace{d\frac{2k}{dx}\frac{x}{2}}_{=k}\log\left(1-\frac{t_{y}-x}{t_{y}\big(1-(2-t_{y})x\big)}\right).

It is easy to see that the term

\frac{t_{y}-x}{t_{y}\big(1-(2-t_{y})x\big)}=\frac{\frac{1}{x}-\frac{1}{t_{y}}}{\frac{1}{x}-(2-t_{y})}

is monotone increasing in
t_{y}
provided that
x\leq t_{y}\leq 1
, which holds now as
t_{y}\geq t_{0}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq x
. Therefore, we have the largest derivative at
y=0
, that is

\partial_{y}\big(d\cdot F(x,t_{y})\big)\leq k\log c_{0}\text{, where}\quad c_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1-\frac{t_{0}-x}{t_{0}\big(1-(2-t_{0})x\big)}<1.

Consequently,

\eta_{x}(y)\leq d\cdot F(x,t_{0})+(k\log c_{0})y+g(\alpha_{1},y)+g(\alpha_{2},x-y).

To find out the maximum of the right-hand side, we differentiate it w.r.t.
y
:

k\log c_{0}+\log\frac{(\alpha_{1}-y)(x-y)}{y(\alpha_{2}-x+y)},

where we used that

\partial_{x}g(\alpha,x)=-\log(x)+\log(\alpha-x).

So the maximum is attained at the unique positive solution
\widetilde{y}
of the quadratic equation

(22)

Specifically, we have

A=1-c_{0}^{k}>0;\,B=(\alpha_{2}-x)+c_{0}^{k}(\alpha_{1}+x)>0;\,C=-c_{0}^{k}\alpha_{1}x<0.

So one may use the quadratic formula to express

explicitly. We omit this but we conclude that

\max_{(x_{1},x_{2})\in K_{x}}\eta(x_{1},x_{2})=\max_{y}\eta_{x}(y)\leq d\cdot F(x,t_{0})+(k\log c_{0})\widetilde{y}+g(\alpha_{1},\widetilde{y})+g(\alpha_{2},x-\widetilde{y}),

and

Abstract
The flow of incompressible fluid in highly permeable porous media in vorticity - velocity - Bernoulli pressure form leads to a double saddle-point problem in the Navier–Stokes–Brinkman–Forchheimer equations. The paper establishes, for small sources, the existence of solutions on the continuous and discrete level of lowest-order piecewise divergence-free Crouzeix–Raviart finite elements. The vorticity employs a vector version of the pressure space with normal and tangential velocity jump penalisation terms. A simple Raviart–Thomas interpolant leads to pressure-robust a priori error estimates. An explicit residual-based a posteriori error estimate allows for efficient and reliable a posteriori error control. The efficiency for the Forchheimer nonlinearity requires a novel discrete inequality of independent interest. The implementation is based upon a light-weight forest-of-trees data structure handled by a highly parallel set of adaptive mesh refining algorithms. Numerical simulations reveal robustness of the a posteriori error estimates and improved convergence rates by adaptive mesh-refining.



Keywords
: Navier–Stokes–Brinkman–Forchheimer equations; pressure robustness; nonconforming finite element methods; Banach fixed-point theory; a priori and a posteriori error estimates.

The general Navier–Stokes and Forchheimer nonlinearities as well as the Brinkman drag will be included in this paper using fixed-point arguments. In contrast to the works above, we do not use augmentation techniques and treat the problem as a (perturbation of a) perturbed saddle-point problem embedded in another saddle-point problem. The analysis hinges on working on the kernel of the divergence operator. At the discrete level we can perform a fairly similar analysis as long as we use kernel-characterising spaces, such that the divergence of the discrete velocity is zero locally in each cell.
For this we can take for instance the nonconforming Crouzeix–Raviart
finite element pair

,
which is Stokes inf-sup stable and satisfies the required local kernel property.
Pressure robust discretisations achieve velocity errors in the broken
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
norm which are proportional to the best approximation error in the velocity, without dependence on the velocity error.
In order to do so in the present setting, we include a modification in the discrete right-hand side functional and in the nonlinear variational forms using a lowest-order
\mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}},\Omega)

It is important to mention that the nonconformity of the method in combination with the need to control the
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl}\,,\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}},\Omega)

In many problems, solution singularities can cause suboptimal convergence, and adaptive mesh refinement is essential for recovering optimal rates. A number of variants of residual-based a posteriori estimators are available for Crouzeix–Raviart schemes applied to Stokes equations

The a posteriori error estimator contains a residual contribution and a non-conformity contribution. We show that it is reliable and efficient, where the efficiency proof relies upon a novel inverse estimate associated with element bubble functions. We use the estimator as an indicator for adaptive mesh refinement, and this restores optimal convergence in the case of singular solutions (which under uniform mesh refinement yield suboptimal convergence).

The particular adaptive meshes that we leverage in this work are hierarchically-adapted (i.e., nested) non-conforming octree-based meshes endowed with Morton (a.k.a., Z-shaped) space-filling-curves for storage and data partitioning; see, e.g.,
[
8

[
18
]

n

However, as these meshes are non-conforming (in particular they have hanging faces at cell interfaces between cells located at different levels of refinement), one needs to add additional multi-point constraints to the Crouzeix–Raviart finite element spaces in order to have optimal approximability properties. In particular, following

11

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\left(\frac{\partial v_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j=1,3},\quad\mathbf{D}{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\frac{1}{2}({\boldsymbol{\nabla}}{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}{\boldsymbol{v}}^{T}),\quad{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{\partial v_{j}}{\partial x_{j}},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}:={\boldsymbol{\nabla}}\times{\boldsymbol{v}}.

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{0}({\mathrm{div}};\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\,\big\{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\quad{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega){\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\gamma_{\mathbf{n}}=0{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\partial\Omega\big\},

2
Model problem and its weak formulation

2.1
The governing equations

We start with the steady Navier–Stokes–Brinkman–Forchheimer equations in their usual velocity–pressure form. They consist in finding velocity
{\boldsymbol{u}}
and kinematic pressure
P
such that

\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{u}}-\nu{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}{\boldsymbol{u}}+({\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}){\boldsymbol{u}}+\mathsf{F}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}+\dfrac{1}{\rho}\nabla P=\,\boldsymbol{f}{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega,\qquad{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}=0{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega

(2.1)

with
\kappa>0
the permeability of the porous media (assumed constant),
\nu
the kinematic fluid viscosity,
\mathsf{F}>0
the Forchheimer coefficient,
\rho
the fluid mass density, and
\boldsymbol{f}
a given external force.
Problem (
2.1
) can be equivalently set in terms of vorticity, velocity and pressure (similarly as done in, e.g.,
[
7
]
for Brinkman and in
[
3
]
for Oseen equations). For this, we introduce the rescaled vorticity vector

{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=\sqrt{\nu}\,\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}

and use the identity
{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}{\boldsymbol{u}}=\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}\times{\boldsymbol{u}}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla({\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}})
. Then we introduce the rescaled Bernoulli pressure

p:=\frac{1}{\rho}P+\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}-\lambda

for
\lambda
defined as the mean value of
\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{u}}
; and employ the following vector identity

\mathbf{curl}\,\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}=-{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla({\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{u}})

together with the incompressibility constraint. These steps lead to the following equations

\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{u}}+\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}+\mathsf{F}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times{\boldsymbol{u}}\,=\,\boldsymbol{f},\qquad{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}\,=\,{\boldsymbol{0}},\qquad{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}\,=\,0.

(2.2)

Furthermore, we focus on homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for velocity and therefore an additional condition is required to enforce the uniqueness of the Bernoulli pressure. This gives

{\boldsymbol{u}}={\boldsymbol{0}}{\quad\hbox{on}\quad}\Gamma{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\int_{\Omega}p=0.

(2.3)

However, similar results as those shown below are also valid for other types of boundary conditions.

2.2
Mixed weak formulation

First, multiplying the first, second and third equations of (
2.2
) by
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}
,
{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
q\in\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
,
respectively, integrating by parts and utilising the boundary condition, we obtain the problem: Find

(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\kappa^{-1}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}-\int_{\Omega}p\,{\mathrm{div}}{\boldsymbol{v}}-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}({\boldsymbol{u}}\times{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathsf{F}\int_{\Omega}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}},

\displaystyle\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}

\displaystyle=0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}q\,{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}

\displaystyle=0.

We introduce the bounded bilinear forms
a:[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\to\mathbb{R}
,
b:[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
, and, for each
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}
, the bilinear form
c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}:[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})

\displaystyle:=\kappa^{-1}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\theta}},

(2.5a)

\displaystyle b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};q)

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Omega}q\,{\mathrm{div}}{\boldsymbol{v}},

(2.5b)

\displaystyle c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})

\displaystyle:=-\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\times{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathsf{F}\int_{\Omega}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}.

(2.5c)

On the other hand, we define the functional
\mathbf{F}\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]^{\prime}
as

F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}):=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}.

(2.6)

Then, the formulation consists in finding
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, such that:

\begin{array}[]{llll}a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})&+\quad b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};p)&+\quad c^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})&=F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};q)&&&=0,\end{array}

(2.7)

for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

3
Analysis of the coupled problem

The following well-known symmetric and non-symmetric versions of the generalised Lax–Milgram lemma will be used in the forthcoming analysis (for a proof see, e.g.,
[
30
, Theorems 1.3 & 1.2]
).

Lemma 3.1

Let
\mathrm{H}
be a real Hilbert space, and let
A:\mathrm{H}\times\mathrm{H}\to\mathbb{R}
be a symmetric and bounded bilinear form. Assume that

\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}}\frac{A(u,v)}{\|v\|_{\mathrm{H}}}\,\geq\,\widetilde{\alpha}\,\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}}\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}.

(3.1)

Then, for each
F\in\mathrm{H}^{\prime}
there exists a unique
u\in\mathrm{H}
such that

A(u,v)=F(v)\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathrm{H},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}}\leq\dfrac{1}{\widetilde{\alpha}}\|F\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\prime}}.

Lemma 3.2

Let
\mathrm{H}_{1},\mathrm{H}_{2}
be real Hilbert spaces, and let
B:\mathrm{H}_{1}\times\mathrm{H}_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded bilinear form. Assume that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\neq v\in\mathrm{H}_{2}}\frac{B(u,v)}{\|v\|_{\mathrm{H}_{2}}}

\displaystyle\,\geq\,\widehat{\alpha}\,\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}_{1}}\quad\forall\,u\in\mathrm{H}_{1},

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\sup_{u\in\mathrm{H}_{1}}B(u,v)

\displaystyle\,>0\quad\forall\,v\in\mathrm{H}_{2},v\neq 0.

(3.2b)

Then, for each
F\in\mathrm{H}_{2}^{\prime}
there exists a unique
u\in\mathrm{H}_{1}
such that

B(u,v)=F(v)\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathrm{H}_{2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}_{1}}\leq\dfrac{1}{\widehat{\alpha}}\|F\|_{\mathrm{H}_{2}^{\prime}}.

We will combine these results with
the Banach fixed-point theorem to demonstrate the well-posedness of (
2.7
) under a small data assumption.

3.1
Stability properties of a linear problem

First we recall the continuous embedding from
\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)
into
\mathrm{L}^{\mathsf{p}}(\Omega)
, for all
\mathsf{p}\in[1,6]
:

\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\mathsf{p}}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{S}}\,\|w\|_{1,\Omega}\quad\forall\,w\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)

(3.3)

with
C_{\mathrm{S}}>0
depending only on
|\Omega|
and

(see
[
45
, Theorem 1.3.4]
).

Next we easily deduce from from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, the continuity of
a(\bullet,\bullet)
,
b(\bullet,\bullet)
:

\displaystyle\big|a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\big|

\displaystyle\leq(\kappa^{-1}+\sqrt{\nu}+1)(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega})(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}),

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\big|b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};q)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|q\|_{0,\Omega}(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(3.4b)

In turn, using Hölder’s inequality
together with (
3.3
), we readily deduce that

\big|c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\big|\leq C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega})(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(3.5)

Similarly, the linear functional
F(\bullet)
is bounded

\big|F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\big|\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(3.6)

Now, it is straightforward to see that the kernel of the bilinear form

is a closed subspace of
\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

\mathbf{V}_{0}:=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}:\quad{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}=0{\quad\hbox{in}\quad}\Omega\}\,.

(3.7)

Lemma 3.3

The bilinear form
a(\bullet,\bullet)
induces an invertible operator on
\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

We proceed using Lemma
3.1
.
First, from (
3.4a
) we observe that
a(\bullet,\bullet)
is bounded. To show that it also satisfies the inf-sup condition (
3.1
), we proceed as in, e.g.,
[
3
, Section 2.3]
. For all
({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
(see (
3.7
)), we can define
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}:=2{\mathbf{z}}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}:=\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\mathbf{z}}-{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}
, and then immediately assert that

a({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=2\kappa^{-1}\|{\mathbf{z}}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\nu\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\mathbf{z}}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\geq\min\{2\kappa^{-1},\nu\}\|{\mathbf{z}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}.

Furthermore, it is clear that

and
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq(1+\sqrt{\nu})(\|{\mathbf{z}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|_{0,\Omega})
, and from this, we can conclude that

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{a({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}}\,\geq\,\frac{a({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}})}{\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\|_{0,\Omega}}\geq\alpha\,(\|{\mathbf{z}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|_{0,\Omega})\quad\forall\,({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)

(3.8)

with
\alpha:=\dfrac{\min\{2\kappa^{-1},\nu,1\}}{2(3+\sqrt{\nu})}
. Thus, the result follows.

\square

On the other hand, from the equivalence

(3.9)

Let us now define the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}:\big([\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\times\big([\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\big)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{A}\big({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q):=a({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\,+\,b({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};q)\,+\,b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};r).\,

(3.10)

Owing to (
3.4a
) and (
3.4b
), it is clear that
\mathcal{A}(\bullet,\bullet)
is bounded. Moreover, from (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and
[
29
, Proposition 2.36]
it is not difficult to see that the following inf-sup condition holds:

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}\geq\gamma\,\big\|(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\big\|

(3.11)

for all
(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, where
\|(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\|:=\|{\mathbf{z}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|r\|_{0,\Omega}
, and

\gamma=\dfrac{\min\{2\kappa^{-1},\nu,1\}\beta^{2}}{(\beta+\kappa^{-1}+\sqrt{\nu}+2)^{2}}.

(3.12)

3.2
Well-posedness analysis via Banach fixed-point

We proceed similarly to
[
27
]
using a fixed-point strategy to prove the well-posedness of (
2.7
). Let us introduce the bounded set

\mathbf{K}\,:=\,\Big\{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}:\quad\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\dfrac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big\}

(3.13)

with
\gamma
the constant defined in (
3.12
).
Then, we define a fixed-point operator as

{\mathscr{F}}:\mathbf{K}\to\mathbf{K},\quad\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\to{\mathscr{F}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}})={\boldsymbol{u}},

(3.14)

where, given
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{K}
,
{\boldsymbol{u}}
is the first component of
({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}})
, where
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
is the solution of the linearised version of problem (
2.7
): Find
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\begin{array}[]{llll}a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})&+\quad b({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};p)&+\quad c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})&=F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad b({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};q)&&&=0\end{array}

(3.15)

for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. It is clear that
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)
is a solution to (
2.7
) if and only if
{\boldsymbol{u}}
satisfies
{\mathscr{F}}({\boldsymbol{u}})={\boldsymbol{u}}
, and consequently, the well-posedness of (
2.7
) is equivalent to the unique solvability of the fixed-point problem: Find
{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{K}
such that

{\mathscr{F}}({\boldsymbol{u}})={\boldsymbol{u}}.

(3.16)

In this way, in what follows we focus on proving the unique solvability of (
3.16
).

3.3
Well-definiteness of the fixed-point map

Let us first provide sufficient conditions under which the operator
{\mathscr{F}}
(cf. (
3.14
)) is well-defined, or equivalently, the problem (
3.15
) is well-posed.

Lemma 3.4
(Unique solvability of the linearised problem)

Let
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{K}
and assume that

\dfrac{4}{\gamma^{2}}C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq 1

(3.17)

with
\gamma
the positive constant in (
3.12
).
Then, there exists a unique
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
solution to (
3.15
).
In addition, there holds

\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\|\leq\frac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(3.18)

Proof.

We proceed similarly as in the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 3.6]
.
In fact, given
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{K}
, we begin by defining the bilinear form:

\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q):=\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)\,+\,c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)

(3.19)

with
\mathcal{A}(\bullet,\bullet)
and
c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
the forms defined in (
3.10
) and (
2.5c
).
Then,
problem (
3.15
) can be rewritten equivalently as:
Find
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, such that

\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}},p;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)=F({\boldsymbol{\theta}},{\boldsymbol{v}})\quad\forall\,(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega).

(3.20)

Therefore, to prove the well-definiteness of
{\mathscr{F}}
, in the sequel we equivalently prove that problem (
3.20
)
is well-posed by means of Lemma
3.2
.
First, given
(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r),((\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
with
((\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})\neq{\boldsymbol{0}}
, from (
3.5
) we observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}\geq\frac{\big|\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\widetilde{q})\big|}{\|((\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})\|}-\frac{\big|c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}};\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\big|}{\|((\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})\|}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big|\mathcal{A}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\widetilde{q})\big|}{\|((\widetilde{\boldsymbol{v}},\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})\|}-C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}(\|{\mathbf{z}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|_{0,\Omega}).

Together with the global inf-sup condition (
3.11
) and the fact that
((\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\widetilde{q})
is arbitrary, this implies

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}\geq\left(\gamma-C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\right)\|(({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\mathbf{z}}),r)\|.

(3.21)

Hence, from the definition of the set
\mathbf{K}
(cf. (
3.13
)), and assumption (
3.17
), we easily get

C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\dfrac{2}{\gamma}C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\dfrac{\gamma}{2}

(3.22)

and then, combining (
3.21
) and (
3.22
), we obtain

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r;{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}\geq\dfrac{\gamma}{2}\|(({\boldsymbol{\zeta}},{\mathbf{z}}),r)\|.

(3.23)

On the other hand, for a given
(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, we observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)\,+\,c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}};{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}

with the problem definition in the last step. Putting this
together with (
3.5
) implies

\displaystyle\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{A}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}-C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}(\|{\mathbf{z}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(3.24)

Therefore, using the fact that
\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
is symmetric, from (
3.11
) and (
3.3
) we obtain

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)\geq\left(\gamma-C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\right)\|(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\|.

Using also (
3.22
), yields

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q;{\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}},r)\geq\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\|(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\|>0

(3.25)

for all
(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
.

In this way, from (
3.23
) and (
3.25
) we obtain that
\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\bullet,\bullet)
satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma
3.2
,
which allows us to conclude the unique solvability of
(
3.15
), or equivalently, the existence of a unique
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
such that
{\mathscr{F}}({\boldsymbol{u}})={\boldsymbol{u}}
.
Finally, from (
3.23
), with
(({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}),r)=(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)
and (
3.20
), we readily obtain that

\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\leq\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\|\leq\dfrac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}

(3.26)

implying that
{\boldsymbol{u}}
belongs to
\mathbf{K}
and concludes the proof.

\square

3.4
Well-posedness of the continuous problem

Now we provide the main result of this section, namely, the existence and uniqueness of solution of problem (
2.7
). This result is established in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.5
(Unique solvability)

Let
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that

\dfrac{4}{\gamma^{2}}C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}<1

(3.27)

with
\gamma
the positive constant in (
3.12
).
Then,
{\mathscr{F}}
(cf. (
3.14
)) has a unique fixed-point
{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{K}
. Equivalently, problem (
2.7
) has a unique solution
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{K}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, there holds

\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\|\leq\dfrac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(3.28)

Proof.

Recall that
(
3.27
) ensures the well-definiteness of
{\mathscr{F}}
.
Now, let
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}
,
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}
,
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}
,
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\in\mathbf{K}
, be such that
{\mathscr{F}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1})={\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}
and
{\mathscr{F}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2})={\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}
.
According to (
3.14
), it follows that there exist unique
({\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1},p_{1})
,
({\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2},p_{2})

\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, such that for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
, there hold

\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{1},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1},p_{1};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)=F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\,,{\quad\hbox{and}\quad}\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2},p_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)=F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\,.

Then, subtracting both equations, adding
\pm c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)
, and recalling the definition of
\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}}
in (
3.19
), we easily arrive at

\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2},p_{1}-p_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)=c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)-c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)\,.

Therefore, recalling that
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}\in\mathbf{K}
from the latter identity, together with (
3.23
), the inequality
|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}|-|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}|\leq|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}|
, and simple computations, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma}{2}\,\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{\mathcal{B}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2},p_{1}-p_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}},q)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}

\displaystyle=\,\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)}\frac{c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)-c^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2};{\boldsymbol{v}},\theta)}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),q)\|}

\displaystyle\leq\,C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2}\|_{0,\Omega}).

Together with the fact that
({\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{2})
satisfy (
3.18
), this yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\|{\mathscr{F}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1})-{\mathscr{F}}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2})\|_{\mathbf{V}}\,=\,\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\,\leq\,\dfrac{2}{\gamma}C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}\Big(\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\dfrac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}-\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\|_{\mathbf{V}}\,.\end{array}

Combining the previous estimate with (
3.27
) and the Banach fixed-point theorem, readily implies that
{\mathscr{F}}
has a unique fixed-point in
\mathbf{K}
, and so there exists a unique
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
solution to (
2.7
).
Finally, the estimate (
3.28
) is obtained analogously to (
3.26
).

\square

Note that the formulation analysed above can also be defined in 2D. The vorticity is then the scalar
\omega=\sqrt{\nu}\mathrm{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}
, the operator
\mathbf{curl}\,
is to be replaced by
\mathrm{rot}
, and the weak convective term is now written as
-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}\omega{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{v}}
. The space for vorticity is then
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. At the discrete level these considerations also hold, but for sake of conciseness of the presentation we only discuss the 3D case.

4
Galerkin scheme

In this section we introduce the Galerkin scheme associated with problem (
2.2
), and show using Banach’s fixed-point arguments that it admits a unique discrete solution.

4.1
Definition of the non-conforming method

First, let us denote by
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}
a family of non-degenerate simplicial meshes on
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(we simply assume that the domain is polytopal, so that no special treatment of the boundary is needed), and denote by
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the set of all facets (edges in 2D) in the mesh, distinguishing between inner facets
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
and the set of facets lying on
\Gamma
,
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma}
. By
h_{K}
we denote the diameter of the element
K
and by
h_{F}
we denote the length/area of the facet
F
. As usual, by
h
we denote the maximum of the diameters of elements in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. For a smooth vector, scalar, or tensor field
\zeta
defined on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\zeta^{\pm}
denote its traces taken from the interior of
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
, respectively. We also
denote by
{\mathbf{n}}^{\pm}
the outward unit normal vector to
K^{\pm}
(and for boundary faces it points outward of the domain
\Omega
). For any inner facet
F
we define the normal and tangential jumps of any element-wise defined vector function
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
across
F
by

[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}:={\boldsymbol{v}}^{+}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}^{+}+{\boldsymbol{v}}^{-}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}^{-},\quad[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}:={\boldsymbol{v}}^{+}\times{\mathbf{n}}^{+}+{\boldsymbol{v}}^{-}\times{\mathbf{n}}^{-}

with
K^{+}
and
K^{-}
the two elements adjacent to
F
, and use the convention that
[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}:={\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}
and
[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}:={\boldsymbol{v}}\times{\mathbf{n}}
if
F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma}
.
For all meshes we assume that they are sufficiently regular (there exists a uniform positive constant
\eta_{1}
such that each element
K
is star-shaped with respect to a ball of radius greater than
\eta_{1}h_{K}
). It is also assumed that there exists
\eta_{2}>0
such that for each element and every facet
F\in\partial K
, we have that
h_{F}\geq\eta_{2}h_{K}
, see, e.g.,
[
29
]
). For
\ell\geq 0
, by
\mathbb{P}_{\ell}(K)
we denote the space of polynomials of total degree at most
\ell
defined locally on the generic element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.

For the approximation of velocity and pressure we use the nonconforming Crouzeix–Raviart Stokes inf-sup stable element (see

[
28
]
) where the velocity space consists of piecewise vector-valued
d
-linear polynomials on each dimension and continuous at the barycentre of the intra-element facets, the discrete pressure consist of piecewise constant functions, and for sake of inf-sup stability we also need that the curl of the discrete velocity lives in the space of vorticity and so we take piecewise vector-valued constants. This gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)^{d}\ \forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\quad J_{F}([\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}]\!]_{F})=\boldsymbol{0}\ \forall F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h},\quad J_{F}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|_{F})=\boldsymbol{0}\ \forall F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma}\},

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\in\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega):\ {\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)^{d(d-1)/2}\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\},

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{q_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\Omega):\ q_{h}|_{K}\in\mathbb{P}_{0}(K)\quad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.

For any facet
F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
with barycentre
C_{F}
, the nodal functional
J_{F}
is defined by

J_{F}({\boldsymbol{v}})={\boldsymbol{v}}(C_{F})\quad\text{or}\quad J_{F}({\boldsymbol{v}})=\frac{1}{h_{F}}\int_{F}{\boldsymbol{v}}\,\mathrm{d}s

and the degrees of freedom associated with facets on
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\Gamma}
vanish for any
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
. We recall the definition of the Crouzeix–Raviart interpolation
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{CR}}}:\mathbf{V}\to\mathbf{V}_{h}
as

{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{CR}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}(C_{F})=J_{F}({\boldsymbol{v}})\qquad\forall F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.

We also recall that element-wise integration by parts on a given
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
readily gives that
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{CR}}}
preserves the averages of first derivatives.

Next, defining the lowest-order Raviart–Thomas space

\mathbf{RT}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}}\,,\Omega):\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\ \exists\,\mathbf{c}_{K}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},a_{K}\in\mathbb{R}:\ {\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|_{K}(\boldsymbol{x})=\mathbf{c}_{K}+a_{K}\boldsymbol{x}\}

we recall the Raviart–Thomas interpolation
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}:\mathbf{V}\oplus\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathbf{RT}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
as

{\mathbf{n}}_{F}\cdot[{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}](C_{F})=\frac{1}{h_{F}}\int_{F}{\boldsymbol{v}}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}_{F}\qquad\forall F\in\mathcal{E}_{h}.

(4.2)

Note that even if
\mathbf{RT}_{0}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\not\subset\mathbf{V}_{h}
(since the tangential components of Raviart–Thomas functions are not necessarily continuous at each
C_{F}
), the interpolation is well-defined for
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
and we have that (see, e.g.,
[
43
]
)

{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{CR}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}={{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}.

For the subsequent analysis we consider the following broken norm for the space
\mathbf{V}_{h}

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\bigl(\frac{1}{\kappa}\|{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}\|^{2}_{0,K}+\nu\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}+\|{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{0,K}^{2}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}}\frac{{1}}{h_{F}}\bigl(\nu\|[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}\|^{2}_{0,F}+\|[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}\|^{2}_{0,F}\bigr)

(4.3)

as well as the piecewise
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
-seminorm

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}^{2}:=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}{\boldsymbol{v}}|^{2}_{1,K}.

We also have that the Raviart–Thomas interpolator is stable on
\mathbf{V}
and also on
\mathbf{V}_{h}

{\|{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{h}\leq C_{\mathrm{RT}}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{h}\quad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}\cup\mathbf{V}_{h}}.

(4.4)

The following approximability bounds are known for Crouzeix–Raviart and Raviart–Thomas interpolants

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}-{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{CR}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{CR}}h|{\boldsymbol{v}}|_{2,\Omega}\qquad\forall\,{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

(4.5a)

\displaystyle\|{\boldsymbol{v}}-{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{F}h\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\|_{h}\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}\cup\mathbf{V}_{h}

(4.5b)

with (
4.5a
) stated in
[
15
, Section 2.3]
, and the constant
C_{\mathrm{CR}}
depends only on the mesh regularity. In addition, the constant
C_{F}
only depends on the shape of the triangles/tetrahedra (maximum angle) but not on their size (see, e.g.,
[
23
,
35
]
).
Let
\mathcal{P}_{h}
denote the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
projection operator, which satisfies the following approximation property (see
[
30
, Theorem 3.6]
):

\|\mathcal{P}_{h}q-q\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{\mathrm{P}}\,h|q|_{1,\Omega}\qquad\forall\,q\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.6)

Since the method is nonconforming in the velocity space, for the discrete setting we will require the broken curl and broken divergence operators (associated with the non-diagonal part of the bilinear form
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
and the discrete bilinear form
b_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
)

\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}:\mathbf{V}\oplus\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega),\quad{\mathrm{div}}_{h}:\mathbf{V}\oplus\mathbf{V}_{h}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)

in the following sense

(\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})|_{K}:=\mathbf{curl}\,({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|_{K})\quad\text{and}\quad({\mathrm{div}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})|_{K}:={\mathrm{div}}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|_{K})\qquad\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

With these ingredients, we can define element-wise variational forms. The forms that require modification are as follows

\displaystyle a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\kappa}\int_{\Omega}{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\cdot{{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}}\frac{\vartheta}{h_{F}}\int_{F}\bigl(\nu[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}\cdot[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}+[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\cdot{{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}

\displaystyle\quad+\!\!\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}}\frac{\vartheta}{h_{F}}\int_{F}\bigl(\nu[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}\cdot[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}+[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}[\![{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]_{F}\bigr)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\nu}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}+\sqrt{\nu}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\cdot\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-\int_{\Omega}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},

(4.7a)

\displaystyle b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};q_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\int_{\Omega}q_{h}{\mathrm{div}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}q_{h}{\mathrm{div}}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},

(4.7b)

\displaystyle c_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}({\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\times{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h})\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}+\mathsf{F}\int_{\Omega}|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\,{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},

(4.7c)

\displaystyle F_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}

(4.7d)

with
\vartheta>0
a sufficiently large, user specified penalty parameter. The stabilisation in
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
uses normal and tangential jumps across inter-element boundaries, which are needed for controlling the consistency error and in general for the convergence of the scheme, as discussed in, e.g.,
[
33
,
36
]
for elasticity equations (see also for example
[
37
,
48
]
for the case of nonconforming schemes on quadrilaterals). We also provide numerical evidence in Section
7
that if
\vartheta=0
then the method does not converge. Note also that, in
[
16
]
the jumps do not require a penalisation parameter since in that formulation the curl-curl and div-div terms are explicitly present in the continuous and discrete bilinear form (and the jump terms only contribute to maintain consistency). Also, note that the 2D Crouzeix–Raviart space used in
[
16
]
is also element-wise divergence-free, but the underlying continuous space only sets tangential components on boundary edges. Another variant in
[
34
]
imposes continuity only of the tangential components at the edges’ midpoints.

The interpolation of the test velocity in the right-hand side functional (
4.7d
) follows the definition proposed in
[
40
]
, but we stress that one could use any smoother operator such that the velocity error (in the broken norm (
4.1
)) is proportional to the corresponding best approximation error
[
51
]
. We proceed similarly for the convective and Forchheimer nonlinearities in (
4.7c
), as well as for the Brinkman term.

Having introduced the additional notations described above, the nonlinear discrete problem consists in finding
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in(\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h})\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, such that:

\begin{array}[]{llll}a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})&+\quad b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};p_{h})&+\quad c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})&=F_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad b_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};q_{h})&&&=0\end{array}

(4.8)

for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in(\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h})\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
. Note that the interpolated test discrete velocity on the right-hand side functional induce a variational crime approach that maps discretely divergence-free test functions to divergence-free functions in
\mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}},\Omega)
. This can also be regarded as a smoothing approach that permits to have a discrete load
F_{h}
well defined for all continuous functionals on
\mathbf{V}
. This setting has been used extensively in, e.g.,
[
1
,
51
,
40
,
43
]
, with the additional aim of achieving pressure robustness of the formulation. We also recall that interpolated test velocities are used in the convective nonlinearity. Finally, using the Cauchy–Schwarz and Hölder inequalities, it is clear that the bilinear forms
a_{h}
and
b_{h}
are bounded

\displaystyle a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}),

\displaystyle b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};q_{h})

\displaystyle\leq\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})

as well as
c_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}
and
F_{h}
:

\displaystyle|c_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq C_{c_{h}}\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}),

(4.9a)

\displaystyle\big|F_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}(\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})

(4.9b)

with
C_{c_{h}}>0
and
C_{F_{h}}>0
depending on the boundedness constant of the operator
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}
denoted by
C_{\mathrm{RT}}
, as well as on the penalty parameter
\vartheta
.

4.2
Further properties of the discrete problem

Discrete kernel properties.

First, we denote the kernel of the bilinear form
b_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
as
\mathbf{V}^{0}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}
(noting that the discrete vorticity space does not play an active role), and from
[
35
, Lemma 4.62]
we can see that, since the broken divergence of an element-wise affine function is element-wise constant, we can readily choose as test function
q_{h}={\mathrm{div}}_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}
, yielding the characterisation

\mathbf{V}^{0}_{h}:=\{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}:\quad{\mathrm{div}}_{h}\,{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}=0\}\,.

(4.10)

Similarly, we stress that

\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{W}_{h}\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}.

(4.11)

The following lemma corresponds to the discrete version of Lemma
3.3
. It depends on a mesh size smallness assumption, which can easily be avoided – and the proof thus further simplified – either if we have a discrete Körn-type inequality using the broken curl part of the discrete velocity norm (which is indeed valid trivially in the 2D case thanks to a discrete Poincaré inequality for Crouzeix–Raviart elements
[
16
]
), or if the first term in the definition of
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
is symmetric (for example, if it has also the Raviart–Thomas interpolation applied to the trial discrete function). We opt to keep the present form as it makes the a posteriori analysis more straightforward.

Lemma 4.1
(Invertibility on the kernel)

The restriction of
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
to the kernel of
b_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
induces an invertible operator, provided that the mesh size
h
is sufficiently small:

{h\leq\frac{1}{2C_{F}\sqrt{\nu}}.}

(4.12)

Proof.

First, we note that for any
{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
, from the definition (
4.1
), it readily holds

\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\sqrt{\kappa}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}.

(4.13)

Then, we can assert that

\displaystyle({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\kappa}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{\mathbf{z}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}+({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\mathbf{z}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq-\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\geq-C_{F}h\sqrt{\kappa}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{h}+\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}

(4.14)

having used the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, as well as (
4.5b
)
and (
4.13
)
in the last step.

Next, and similarly to the continuous case, it is clear that
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
is bounded. In addition, for all
({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{0}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}
(see (
4.10
)), and owing to (
4.11
), we can define
\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}:=2{\mathbf{z}}_{h}
and
\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}:=\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}
, and invoke (
4.2
), from which we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h};\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})

\displaystyle\quad={\frac{2}{\kappa}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h})_{0,\Omega}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}}\frac{2\vartheta}{h_{F}}\int_{F}\bigl(\nu[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]^{2}_{F}+[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]^{2}_{F}\bigr)+\nu\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,K}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad{\ \geq\frac{2}{\kappa}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}-\frac{2C_{F}h}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{h}}+\sum_{F\in\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{int}}_{h}}\frac{2\vartheta}{h_{F}}\int_{F}\bigl(\nu[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\times{\mathbf{n}}]\!]^{2}_{F}+[\![{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot{\mathbf{n}}]\!]^{2}_{F}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\nu\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,K}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\quad\geq{2\biggl(1-\frac{C_{F}h}{\sqrt{\kappa}}\biggr)}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|^{2}_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}.

Thus, using (
4.12
) together with the fact that
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}=2\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}
and
\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq(1+\sqrt{\nu})(\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})
, we can conclude that

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{0}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}}\frac{a_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}}\,\geq\,\frac{a_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h};\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})}{\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}}\geq\alpha_{h}\,(\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})

(4.15)

for all
({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}^{0}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}
, with
\alpha_{h}:=\frac{1}{2(3+\sqrt{\nu})}
. Thus, the result follows directly from Lemma
3.1
.

\square

Discrete inf-sup conditions.

The discrete inf-sup condition for the discrete divergence operator is satisfied for Crouzeix–Raviart elements
[
28
]
. This is recalled in the following result.

Lemma 4.2

The pair
(\mathbf{V}_{h},\mathrm{Q}_{h})
is inf-sup stable with constant
\beta_{h}>0
independent of the mesh size

0<\beta_{h}:=\inf_{q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}_{h}\setminus\{0\}}\sup_{{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}\setminus\{\boldsymbol{0}\}}\frac{b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};q_{h})}{\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}\|q_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}}.

(4.16)

In addition, we have the following properties, shown in
[
43
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma 4.3

For all
{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
there holds

\displaystyle b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\bullet;q)

\displaystyle=b\,({{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\bullet;q)\qquad\forall q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega),

(4.17a)

\displaystyle b\,({{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\bullet;q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\nabla q\cdot({{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h})\qquad\forall q\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega).

(4.17b)

4.3
Discrete fixed-point arguments

Here we proceed similarly as in the continuous setting. To begin with, we define the bilinear form
\mathcal{A}_{h}:\big([\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}\big)\times\big([\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h})\to\mathbb{R}
as

\mathcal{A}_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},r_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h}):=a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})\,+\,b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};p_{h})\,+\,b_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};q_{h}).\,

(4.18)

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{A}_{h}
is bounded (since
a_{h}
and
b_{h}
are), and furthermore, using (
4.15
), (
4.16
), and
[
29
, Proposition 2.36]
, we have that
\mathcal{A}_{h}
satisfies the following inf-sup condition

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{A}_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},r_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}\geq\widetilde{\gamma}\,\big\|(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\big\|_{h}

(4.19)

for all
(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, where
\|(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\|_{h}:=\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|r_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}
, and
\widetilde{\gamma}>0
is the discrete version of
\gamma
(cf. (
3.12
)).

Let us introduce the following set

\mathbf{K}_{h}\,:=\,\Big\{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}:\quad\|\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}\leq\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big\}

(4.20)

with
\widetilde{\gamma}
the global inf-sup constant defined in (
4.19
).
Then, and again analogously to the continuous case, we define the following fixed-point operator

{\mathscr{F}}_{h}:\mathbf{K}_{h}\to\mathbf{K}_{h},\quad\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\to{\mathscr{F}}_{h}(\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})={\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},

(4.21)

where, given
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{K}_{h}
,
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
is the first component of
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, the solution of the linearised version of problem (
4.8
): Find
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
such that

\begin{array}[]{llll}a_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})&+\quad b_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h};p_{h})&+\quad c_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})&=F_{h}({{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),\\[4.30554pt]
\qquad b_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h};q_{h})&&&=0,\end{array}

(4.22)

for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.

It is clear that
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})
is a solution to (
4.8
) if and only if
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
satisfies
{\mathscr{F}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})={\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}
, and consequently, the well-posedness of (
4.8
) is equivalent to the unique solvability of the fixed-point problem: Find
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{K}_{h}
such that

{\mathscr{F}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})={\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}.

(4.23)

In what follows we focus on (
4.23
).
We start by establishing that
{\mathscr{F}}_{h}
is well-defined.

Lemma 4.4
(Wellposedness of the discrete linearised problem)

Let
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{K}_{h}
and assume that

\dfrac{4}{\widetilde{\gamma}^{2}}C_{c_{h}}C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq 1

(4.24)

with the positive constant
\widetilde{\gamma}
in (
4.19
).
Then, there exists a unique
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
solution to (
4.22
).
In addition, there holds

\|(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\|_{h}\leq\dfrac{2}{\widehat{\gamma}}C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(4.25)

Proof.

Given
\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{K}_{h}
, we proceed as in the proof of Lemma
3.4
and define the bilinear form

\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},r_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h}):=\mathcal{A}_{h}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},r_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})\,+\,c_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},\theta_{h}).

(4.26)

Using (
4.9a
), (
4.19
), (
4.24
) and
[
29
, Proposition 2.36]
we obtain the inf-sup condition

\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\widehat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},r_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}\geq\dfrac{\widetilde{\gamma}}{2}\|(({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h},{\mathbf{z}}_{h}),r_{h})\|_{h}

(4.27)

for all
(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
.
Therefore, owing to the fact that for finite dimensional linear problems surjectivity and injectivity are equivalent, from (
4.27
) and
Lemma
3.2

we obtain that there exists a unique
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
satisfying (
4.22
) with
{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\mathbf{K}_{h}
.
\square

Theorem 4.5

(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{K}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}

\|(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\|_{h}\leq\dfrac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(4.29)

Proof.

Employing (
4.27
) and (
4.25
), along with (

. Further details are omitted.

\square

5
A priori error bounds

Now we turn to the error analysis. First we derive a Strang-type estimate. Then, under a small data assumption we show linear convergence of the method in the energy norm. Finally, we show that the velocity-vorticity error is independent of the pressure error.

Lemma 5.1
(Céa estimate)

Let
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in[\mathbf{V}\times\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)]\times\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
and
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, the solution of
(
2.7
) and (
4.8
), respectively. Then there hold the error estimate

\displaystyle\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\|_{h}\leq\Big(1+\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}\Big)\inf_{(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\dfrac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}},p;{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})-F_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}.

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}

\begin{array}[]{c}{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}={\boldsymbol{u}}-{\mathbf{z}}_{h}+({\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})={\boldsymbol{u}}-{\mathbf{z}}_{h}+{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{\boldsymbol{u}}},\quad{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}={\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}+({\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h})={\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}+{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\\
p-p_{h}=p-r_{h}+(r_{h}-p_{h})=p-r_{h}+\chi_{p}.\end{array}

(5.2)

Then, from (
4.27
), using (
4.8
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\widetilde{\gamma}}{2}\|(({\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{\boldsymbol{u}}},{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),\chi_{p})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{{\boldsymbol{u}}},{\boldsymbol{\chi}}_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\chi_{p};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\leq\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h},p-p_{h};{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}

\displaystyle=\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\mathbf{z}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}),r_{h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{{\boldsymbol{0}}\neq(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\in[\mathbf{V}_{h}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}]\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}}\frac{\mathcal{B}_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}},p;{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})-F_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})}{\|(({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}),q_{h})\|_{h}}.

(5.3)

Finally, (
5.1
) is obtained directly from estimate (
5
) along with the error decomposition (cf. (

\square

Let

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{B}_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}},p;{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h},q_{h})-\int_{\Omega}(\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{u}}+\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}+\mathsf{F}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nabla p+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times{\boldsymbol{u}})\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}|

Theorem 5.2
(Rate of convergence)

Let
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)
and
(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})
solve the continuous and discrete problems (
2.7
) and (
4.8

and that
(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)\in(\mathbf{H}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega))\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)

Then there exists
C_{\mathrm{rate}}>0
, independent of

, such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\|_{h}\leq\,C_{\mathrm{rate}}\,h\Big\{|{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{2,\Omega}\,+\,|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|_{1,\Omega}\,+\,|p|_{1,\Omega}\Big\}.\end{array}

(5.6)

Proof.

To prove the result, we must estimate the two terms on the right-hand side of (
5.1
). For the first term, we can use the properties in (
4.6
) (to estimate the errors for
{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
and
p
) and (
4.5a
) (to estimate the error for
{\boldsymbol{u}}
), while for the second term, we apply estimate (
5
), along with interpolation properties (
4.4
) and (

), and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, to obtain

\displaystyle\|(({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),p)-(({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}),p_{h})\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\,(C_{\mathrm{CR}}+2C_{\mathrm{P}})\Big(1+\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}\Big)\,h\Big\{|{\boldsymbol{u}}|_{2,\Omega}\,+\,|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|_{1,\Omega}\,+\,|p|_{1,\Omega}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{F}(\sqrt{\nu}+1)\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}h\Big\{\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega}\,+\,\|\nabla p\|_{0,\Omega}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}C_{\mathrm{RT}}\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F})(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega})\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}.

From the latest estimate, using the fact that

satisfies estimate (

) and applying hypothesis (

).

\square

The estimate above can be refined to reflect the pressure-robustness of the formulation. Consider the continuous and discrete problems in their reduced form (in the continuous and discrete kernels
\mathbf{V}_{0}
and
\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}
, respectively)

a({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})+c^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}};{\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})=F({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\quad\forall({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}})\in\mathbf{V}_{0}\times\mathbf{W},

and

which are equivalent to (

) and (

\|\bullet\|_{h}
of vector fields from

. In turn, for this as well as for the a posteriori error estimation later on, we will employ the companion operator

(5.8)

that is a

interpolation

, satisfying

\|h_{\mathcal{T}_{h}}({\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}},

as well as other additional

orthogonality properties not needed herein (see the precise design for

Lemma 5.3

For any
{\boldsymbol{v}}\in\mathbf{V}
and
{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}
and
1\leq s\leq 6
in 3D (and
1\leq s<\infty
in 2D), there holds

\|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{s}(\Omega)}+|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\leq C_{\sharp}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{h}

(5.9)

with
C_{\sharp}
depending on
\Omega
and the shape-regularity of the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(and as well on
s
in the 2D case).

Proof.

We first add
\pm\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}
to the left-hand side of (
5.9
), use triangle inequality, and invoke the well-known discrete Sobolev embedding with constant
C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s)>0
:

\|{\boldsymbol{w}}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{s}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s)|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{w}}\in\mathbf{V}

applied on the term
{\boldsymbol{v}}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}
.
Then, we employ the continuous Sobolev embedding (
3.3
), and this gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}_{\eqref{eq:aux-24}}

\displaystyle:=\|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{s}(\Omega)}+|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\leq C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s)|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}+\|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{s}(\Omega)}+|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s))|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}+\|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{s}(\Omega)}+|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s))|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}+(1+C_{\mathrm{S}})|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle\leq(1+C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s))[\|\mathbf{curl}\,({\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\mathrm{div}}({\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}]+(1+C_{\mathrm{S}})|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}

with the equivalence — valid for
{\boldsymbol{v}}+\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}
— between the piecewise norm
|\!|\!|\bullet|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}
and the semi-norm in
\mathbf{H}_{0}(\mathbf{curl}\,,\Omega)\cap\mathbf{H}_{0}({\mathrm{div}},\Omega)

[
31
, Lemma 2.5 & Remark 2.7]
in the last step.

Using next again triangle inequality and the definition of the broken curl, the broken divergence, and the discrete velocity norm
\|\bullet\|_{h}
, from the bounds above we readily get

\displaystyle\mathrm{LHS}_{\eqref{eq:aux-24}}

\displaystyle\leq[(1+C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s))C_{\mathrm{norm}}+(1+C_{\mathrm{S}})]|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1+C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s))[\|\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\mathrm{div}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}]

\displaystyle\leq C_{\sharp}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}\|_{h}

with the following estimate from
[
26
]
in the last step:

|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}-\mathcal{J}{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}\lesssim|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}},

as well as the fact that

\|\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}+\|{\mathrm{div}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}\lesssim|\!|\!|{\boldsymbol{v}}+{\boldsymbol{w}}_{h}|\!|\!|_{\mathrm{pw}}.

Therefore
C_{\sharp}>0
depends on
C_{\mathrm{S}},C_{\mathrm{dS}}(s)
and on the parameter-dependent constant
C_{\mathrm{norm}}>0
.
\square

implies, in particular, that

{\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\sharp}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}.}

(5.10)

Theorem 5.4
(Pressure-robust error bound)

Assume that
({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h})
are the unique solutions to (
5.7a
) and (
5.7b
), respectively. Suppose further that the data satisfies (
5.5
) with
r=1-C_{\mathrm{S}}^{2}C_{\mathrm{RT}}\frac{8}{\gamma\widetilde{\gamma}}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}+\mathsf{F}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}>0
, and that the continuous vorticity is more regular
{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
. Then

\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|_{1,\Omega}+\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}({\boldsymbol{v}}_{h},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h})\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}\\
\alpha_{h}\|{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}=1\end{subarray}}\biggl[\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{r\alpha_{h}}\biggr](\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{v}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(5.11)

Proof.

Let us adopt the notation
\vec{u}:=({\boldsymbol{u}},{\boldsymbol{\omega}}),\ \vec{v}:=({\boldsymbol{v}},{\boldsymbol{\theta}}),\ \vec{z}:=({\mathbf{z}},{\boldsymbol{\zeta}})\in\mathbf{V}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}
and similarly for their discrete counterparts
\vec{u}_{h},\vec{v}_{h},\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}
, denoting the corresponding discrete norm as, e.g.,
\|\vec{z}_{h}\|:=\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}
. First we decompose

\vec{u}-\vec{u}_{h}=\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h}+\vec{v}_{h}-\vec{u}_{h}=\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h}-\vec{y}_{h}

and note that
\vec{y}_{h}:=\vec{u}_{h}-\vec{v}_{h}
belongs to
\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}
. Then, using the inf-sup condition of
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)
, the definition of
\vec{y}_{h}
, and the definition of the discrete problem (
5.7b
), we can write
the following

\displaystyle\alpha_{h}\|\vec{z}_{h}\|\|\vec{y}_{h}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{0\neq\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}a_{h}(\vec{y}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})=\sup_{0\neq\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}a_{h}(\vec{u}_{h}-\vec{v}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}\bigl(a_{h}(\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})+a_{h}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sup_{0\neq\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}\bigl(a_{h}(\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})+\bigl[F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})\bigr]\bigr).

(5.12)

Consequently, dividing through
\alpha_{h}\|\vec{z}_{h}\|
and using the boundedness of
a_{h}(\bullet,\bullet)

as well as the triangle inequality
\|\vec{u}-\vec{u}_{h}\|\leq\|\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h}\|+\|\vec{y}_{h}\|
, we obtain the following estimate

\|\vec{u}-\vec{u}_{h}\|\leq\inf_{\vec{v}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}\biggl(1+\frac{1}{\alpha_{h}}\biggr)\|\vec{u}-\vec{v}_{h}\|+\sup_{\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}\frac{\bigl|F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})\bigr|}{\alpha_{h}\|\vec{z}_{h}\|}

(5.13)

composed by the best approximation in
\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}
and the consistency error.

Note that
even if
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}
is not in
\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\|{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\leq\|{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\boldsymbol{\nabla}}_{h}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq{C_{*}}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h},

(5.14)

thanks to triangle inequality, inverse estimates, and discrete Sobolev properties
[
42
, Theorem 4.12]
.

Next, we proceed to add
\pm c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})
to
the numerator in the consistency error
a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})+c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})
. The
+
will contribute to complete a full residual
a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})+c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})-F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})
, so we need to investigate first the remainder terms as follows, adding and subtracting appropriate terms. Applying Hölder’s inequality, property (
5.14
), and reverse triangle inequality, gives

\displaystyle|c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})|

\displaystyle=\bigl|-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}\times({\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}+\mathsf{F}\int_{\Omega}|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|{({\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}-{\boldsymbol{u}})}\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\int_{\Omega}{({\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})}\times{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-\mathsf{F}\int_{\Omega}[|{\boldsymbol{u}}|-|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|]{{\boldsymbol{u}}}\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq{\frac{C^{2}_{*}}{\sqrt{\nu}}}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+{\mathsf{F}C_{*}^{2}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\qquad+{\frac{C_{*}}{\sqrt{\nu}}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}+\mathsf{F}C_{*}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}}

\displaystyle\lesssim{C_{*}[M_{h}C_{*}+MC_{\sharp}]\biggl(\mathsf{F}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\biggr)}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}

(5.15)

where for the last estimation we have used that
\vec{u},\vec{u}_{h}
are solutions to the continuous and discrete problems featuring a continuous dependence on data that we denote here by
M=\frac{2}{\gamma}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}
and
M_{h}=\frac{2}{\widetilde{\gamma}}C_{F_{h}}\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}
, respectively (cf. (
3.28
) and (
4.29
), respectively); we have also used (
5.10
).

We now look again at the numerator of the consistency error and rewrite
\boldsymbol{f}
in terms of the left-hand side of the momentum balance equation in (
2.2
), use the fact that

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}=0

(see, e.g.,
[
40
]
)
and apply integration by parts on the term
\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,_{h}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\omega}}
, to get

\displaystyle a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})+c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})+[c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})]

\displaystyle\quad=\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}})\cdot{\boldsymbol{\zeta}}_{h}+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot({\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h})+[c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})]

\displaystyle\quad\leq\sqrt{\nu}\|\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{0,\Omega}\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}-{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\qquad+{C_{*}[M_{h}C_{*}+MC_{\sharp}]\biggl(\mathsf{F}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\biggr)}(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{h}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega})\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\biggl[ch|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|_{1,\Omega}+{C_{*}[M_{h}C_{*}+MC_{\sharp}]\biggl(\mathsf{F}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\biggr)}\|\vec{u}-\vec{u}_{h}\|\biggr]\|{\mathbf{z}}_{h}\|_{h}.

Here we have used the estimate in (
5.15
) and the fact that if
\vec{u}
is the exact smooth solution of (

) then
\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}}={\boldsymbol{\omega}}
in
\Omega
, and have also used approximation properties of
{{\mathcal{I}}^{\mathsf{RT}}}
. This yields

\sup_{\vec{z}_{h}\in\mathbf{V}_{h}^{0}\times\mathbf{W}_{h}}\frac{\bigl|F_{h}(\vec{z}_{h})-c_{h}^{{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}}(\vec{u}_{h},\vec{z}_{h})-a_{h}(\vec{u},\vec{z}_{h})\bigr|}{\alpha_{h}\|\vec{z}_{h}\|}\leq Ch|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}|_{1,\Omega}+{C_{*}[M_{h}C_{*}+MC_{\sharp}]\biggl(\mathsf{F}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\biggr)}\|\vec{u}-\vec{u}_{h}\|.

And the proof is complete after combining this estimate, the small data assumption (
5.5
), and (

).

\square

6

\||{\boldsymbol{u}}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}-|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}|\,{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{\mathbf{L}^{1}(K)}\leq(\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,K}+\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K})\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}.

Let us hide several constants in the notation
\lesssim
as before but also
upper bounds of
\|{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}
and
\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\|_{0,K}
to infer

S_{6}\lesssim|K|^{-1/d}\|b_{K}^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{K}\|_{0,K}|\mathsf{F}|\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}.

Let us hide the material constants
|\mathsf{F}|
,
\kappa^{-1}
,
\nu
, and
\nu^{-1}

in the notation
\lesssim

for a summary of the above estimates of
S_{1}
,…,
S_{6}
in
(
6
). After a division of
the factor
\|b_{K}^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{K}\|_{0,K}
(if positive as else there is nothing left to prove)
we infer

|K|^{1/d}\|b_{K}^{1/2}{\boldsymbol{v}}_{K}\|_{0,K}\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|p_{h}-p\|_{0,K}+\textrm{osc}_{k}(\boldsymbol{f},K).

The combination with (
6.4
) concludes the proof of the
local efficiency

\mu_{K}\lesssim\|{\boldsymbol{u}}-{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-{\boldsymbol{\omega}}_{h}\|_{0,K}+\|p_{h}-p\|_{0,K}+\textrm{osc}_{k}(\boldsymbol{f},K)

of the volume contribution in lower order terms. The efficiency proof does not require any smallness assumption neither on the mesh-size nor on the closeness of the exact and discrete solution.

Numerical results

In this section we report and discuss a number of numerical examples to illustrate the performance of the proposed mixed finite element schemes and a posteriori error estimators. The realisation of the numerical methods designed in the paper is conducted with a combination of open source software packages in the so-called
Gridap
ecosystem
[
10
,
49
,
9
]
. In all cases, we use a Newton method with exact Jacobian, setting a tolerance of
10^{-8}
on the
\ell^{2}
norm of the increment and
10^{-12}
on the
\ell^{\infty}
norm of the residual. The linear systems were solved either with UMFPACK (2D cases) or the multifrontal massively parallel sparse direct solver MUMPS (3D cases).

The adaptive mesh refinement procedure follows Algorithm
7.1
. It comprises an standard SOLVE
\rightarrow
ESTIMATE
\rightarrow
MARK
\rightarrow
ADAPT loop. This algorithm can be in principle combined with any kind of adaptive mesh approach. Our implementation of Algorithm
7.1
particularly leverages hierarchically-adapted (i.e., nested) non-conforming octree-based meshes; see, e.g.,
[
8
]
. Forest-of-octrees meshes can be seen as a two-level
decomposition of
\Omega
, referred to as macro and micro level, respectively. The macro level is a suitable
conforming
partition
\mathcal{C}_{h}
of
\Omega
into quadrilateral (
d=2
) or hexahedral cells (
d=3
). This mesh, which may be generated, e.g., using an unstructured mesh generator, is referred to as the coarse mesh. At the micro level, each of the cells of
\mathcal{C}_{h}
becomes the root of an adaptive octree with cells that can be recursively and dynamically refined or coarsened using the so-called
1:2^{d}
uniform partition rule. If a cell is marked for refinement, then it is split into
2^{d}
children cells by subdividing all parent cell edges. If all children cells of a parent cell are marked for coarsening, then they are collapsed into the parent cell. The union of all leaf cells in this hierarchy forms the decomposition of the domain at the micro level, i.e.,
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. While these meshes are made of quadrilaterals or cubes, we split their elements into simplices (2 triangles per mesh quadriteral in 2D, 6 tetrahedra per mesh cube in 3D) to obtain the simplicial meshes for our formulation; see, e.g., Figure
7.3
.

The adaptive meshes resulting from this approach are non-conforming. In particular, they have hanging faces at the interface of neighbouring cells at different levels of refinement. Mesh non-conformity requires special adaptations to the Crouzeix–Raviart finite element space used in our formulation. In particular, following
[
11
]
, we impose a set of linear multi-point constraints for the velocity degrees of freedom located at these non-conforming interfaces such that the average of the averages on the finer children faces equals the average on the coarse parent face (so-called Option C in
[
11
]
). While this approach was mathematically proven in
[
11
]
to lead to optimal convergence rates in the case of the Douglas–Santos–Sheen–Ye finite element, our numerical results confirm that this is also the case for the Crouzeix–Raviart finite element. We used the
GridapP4est.jl

[
44
]
Julia package in order to handle such kind of meshes (including facet integration on non-conforming interfaces as per required by the stabilization terms in the formulation and the computation of the a posteriori error estimator) and finite element space constraints. This package, built upon the
p4est
meshing engine
[
18
]
, is endowed with the so-called Morton space-filling curves, and it provides high-performance and low-memory footprint algorithms to handle forest-of-octrees.

\nu=\mathsf{F}=\kappa=1

\Omega=(-1,1)^{2}\setminus([0,1)\times(-1,0])
(see, e.g.,

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{u}}=r^{\lambda}\begin{pmatrix}(1+\lambda)\sin(\theta)\psi(\theta)+\cos(\theta)\psi^{\prime}(\theta)\\
\sin(\theta)\psi^{\prime}(\theta)-(1+\lambda)\cos(\theta)\psi(\theta)\end{pmatrix},\quad{\boldsymbol{\omega}}=\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{u}},

\displaystyle p=-\nu\frac{r^{\lambda-1}}{1-\lambda}((1+\lambda)^{2}\psi^{\prime}(\theta)+\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(\theta))

in polar coordinates centred at the origin
(r,\theta)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,\frac{3\pi}{2})

\psi(\theta)=\frac{\sin((1+\lambda)\theta)\cos(\lambda w)}{1+\lambda}-\cos((1+\lambda)\theta)-\frac{\sin((1-\lambda)\theta)\cos(\lambda w)}{1-\lambda}+\cos((1-\lambda)\theta).

Here
\lambda=\frac{856399}{1572864}\approx 0.5444837
is the smallest positive solution of
\sin(\lambda w)+\lambda\sin(w)=0
, and we take

. Second-order derivatives for velocity (and first order derivatives for pressure and vorticity) are not square integrable, and therefore these solutions do not have higher regularity (
{\boldsymbol{u}}\notin\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega),\omega,p\notin H^{1}(\Omega)
). Nevertheless, the exact boundary velocity is zero on the reentrant edges (at
\theta=0
and
\theta=\frac{3\pi}{2}
) and so the boundary data oscillation can be considered of high order. Note that the exact velocity and pressure above are such that
\sqrt{\nu}\mathbf{curl}\,{\boldsymbol{\omega}}+\nabla p=\boldsymbol{0}
and so
\boldsymbol{f}=\kappa^{-1}{\boldsymbol{u}}+\nu^{-1/2}{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times{\boldsymbol{u}}+\mathsf{F}|{\boldsymbol{u}}|{\boldsymbol{u}}
. For this test the stabilisation parameter is taken as
\vartheta=10
.

8
Concluding remarks

We have developed and analysed a novel non-augmented vorticity–velocity–Bernoulli formulation for incompressible flow in highly permeable porous media, governed by the Navier–Stokes–Brinkman–Forchheimer equations. The approach avoids least-squares or stabilisation-based augmentation by formulating the problem as a nested saddle-point system and working in divergence-free velocity spaces. This framework enables both continuous and discrete solvability analyses under small data assumptions, relying on fixed-point arguments and inf-sup conditions in divergence-free subspaces. At the discrete level, we employ a Crouzeix–Raviart finite element method enhanced with tangential and normal jump penalisation to control the full
\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{curl}\,)\cap\mathbf{H}({\mathrm{div}})
velocity norm, and we ensure pressure-robustness through a modified variational form using a Raviart–Thomas interpolant. We introduced a fully computable residual-based a posteriori error estimator, which is shown to be reliable and efficient—the latter hinging on a new inverse inequality tailored to the Forchheimer nonlinearity. Numerical experiments validate all theoretical results and demonstrate that adaptive mesh refinement, driven by the proposed estimator, restores optimal convergence even in the presence of solution singularities. The practical implementation leverages a lightweight, parallel octree-based infrastructure capable of generating non-conforming adaptive meshes, where appropriate multipoint constraints allow the recovery of optimal approximation properties. The combined contributions in formulation, analysis, estimator design, and implementation provide a foundation for further development of adaptive solvers for nonlinear porous flow models and illustrate the benefits of structure-preserving discretisations in complex multiphysics regimes.

Possible directions of future work include the extension of the analysis to more general data regimes, particularly beyond the smallness assumptions required for fixed-point arguments, potentially through monotonicity or compactness-based techniques. It would also be valuable to investigate higher-order nonconforming discretisations that retain divergence-free properties and support pressure-robustness. Finally, we want to address the coupling with transport and reaction models within the same porous medium, where interface dynamics or multiphysics constraints may necessitate tailored trace operators and stabilisation.

A new inverse estimate

Let us first recall that the scalar volume-bubble function
{b_{K}}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}({K})
on a simplex
{K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is the product
of all
d+1
barycentric coordinates of the vertices of
K
times a factor
(d+1)^{d+1}

for the normalisation
0\leq b_{K}\leq 1=\max b_{K}
in
K
. Note that typical efficiency estimates rely upon Verfürth’s bubble-function methodology
[
50
]
. For the present case, the Forchheimer nonlinearity requires a non-trivial modification of this approach, presented in the following key inverse estimate.

Lemma A.1
(New inverse estimate)

There exists a universal positive constant
C_{\mathrm{eq}}
, that exclusively depends on

,
such that all affine vector-valued functions
{\boldsymbol{f},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)^{d}}
satisfy

Proof of independence of
K
. At first glance, the most striking aspect of the lemma is the

a constant that depends on the reference simplex
K_{\textrm{ref}}

of volume

But that aspect follows from an elementary affine transformation
\Psi
from
K
to

: All integrals of both sides
of the squared estimate are Lebesgue functions (and no derivative appears) so the transformation shows

\||\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{g}\|_{0,K}=\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{|K_{\textrm{ref}}|}}\,\||\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}|\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{g}}\|_{0,K_{\textrm{ref}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|b^{1/2}_{K}(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{g})\|_{0,K}=\sqrt{\frac{|K|}{|K_{\textrm{ref}}|}}\,\|b^{1/2}_{K_{\textrm{ref}}}(|\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}|\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}+\hat{\boldsymbol{g}})\|_{0,K_{\textrm{ref}}}

for
\hat{\boldsymbol{f}}:=\boldsymbol{f}\circ\Psi

,

. Thus, once the assertion holds on the reference simplex

with the same constant

.
\square

Proof in case

|{\boldsymbol{b}}|\leq r^{*}
, for instance

{\boldsymbol{b}}
vanishes at
\textrm{\rm mid}(K)
. Altogether

r^{*}=\|{\boldsymbol{b}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)^{d}}.

This and the calculation
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}=|{\boldsymbol{b}}|^{2}+(\boldsymbol{a}\cdot{\boldsymbol{b}})^{2}
imply

(r^{*})^{2}=\|{\boldsymbol{b}}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\omega)^{d}}\leq\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq 2\min_{g_{0}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-g_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}

with optimal value
g_{0}=\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}/2
in the last step
(recall that
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}\geq 0
and it vanishes at
\textrm{\rm mid}(K)
).
The choice of
\overline{g}
as the integral mean of
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}
over
\omega

is therefore an upper bound,

\min_{g_{0}\in\mathbb{R}}\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-g_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\overline{g}\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\leq C_{\textrm{inv}}\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\overline{g}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

from another inverse estimate (from equivalence of norms in
\mathbb{P}_{2}(\Omega)
)
in the last step. The key insight starts with the quadratic polynomial

\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\overline{g}
with integral mean zero
over the symmetric (fixed) domain
\omega
such that

\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\overline{g}
is
symmetric along all straight lines through
\textrm{\rm mid}(K)
, while
{\boldsymbol{\xi}}

is asymmetric (with respect to the centre
\textrm{\rm mid}(K)
). Thus the
product
\int_{\omega}(\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-g_{0}){\boldsymbol{\xi}}\,\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{x}={\boldsymbol{0}}
vanishes. This means
that
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-g_{0}
has vanishing
moments up to degree one:
\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-g_{0}
is
L^{2}(\omega)
perpendicular to
all affine functions. Consequently

\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\overline{g}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}=\min_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\omega)}\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-q_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}\leq 2\min_{q_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\omega)}\|\boldsymbol{a}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-q_{1}\|_{b_{K}}\leq 2\min_{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\omega)^{d}}\|\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\|_{b_{K}}

with
b_{K}>1/2
a.e. in
\omega\subset K
and
|\boldsymbol{a}|=1
in the last steps.
Notice that the last term
\min_{\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\omega)^{d}}\|\boldsymbol{g}_{2}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1}\|_{b_{K}}

is equal to
\|(1-P)\boldsymbol{g}_{2}\|_{b_{K}}
. The
combination of the aforementioned inequalities reads

\frac{t^{2}(r^{*})^{2}}{8C_{\textrm{inv}}}\leq\|t^{2}(1-P)\boldsymbol{g}_{2}/2\|_{b_{K}}\leq\|(1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{b_{K}}+C_{0}\,t^{3}

with (
A.1
) and a triangle inequality in the last step.
Under the condition

0<t\leq\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\{1/2,(r^{*})^{2}/(16C_{0}C_{\textrm{inv}})\},

we conclude the proof of the assertion
with
C_{2}=(r^{*})^{2}/(16C_{0}C_{\textrm{inv}})
in the third step.

Step four concludes the proof for
t\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. The previous two steps provide

C_{1}^{-1}C_{2}\|(1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{0,K}\leq t^{2}\,C_{2}\leq\|(1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{b_{K}}.

Given any
\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)^{d}
and
\boldsymbol{f}
as before, a triangle and the last inequality
lead to

\displaystyle\||\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{g}\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{0,K}+\|\boldsymbol{g}+P(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{0,K}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C_{2}^{-1}\|(1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{b_{K}}+C_{\textrm{eq,2}}\|\boldsymbol{g}+P(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f})\|_{b_{K}}

with the aforementioned classical inverse estimate in the last step. A Cauchy inequality
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a Pythagoras identity
in the
b_{K}
weighted Lebesgue norms based on the orthogonality

((1-P)(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}),\boldsymbol{g}+P(|\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}))_{b_{K}}=0
leads to

\||\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{g}\|_{0,K}\leq\sqrt{C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{-2}+C_{\textrm{eq,2}}^{2}}\||\boldsymbol{f}|\boldsymbol{f}+\boldsymbol{g}\|_{b_{K}}.

This concludes the proof of the assertion with

C_{\textrm{eq}}^{2}=C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{-2}+C_{\textrm{eq,2}}^{2}
.

\square

Conclusion of the proof of the lemma
. We depart from the previous definition
of
\varepsilon_{0}
as a universal constant (it exclusively depends on
K=K_{\textrm{ref}}
,
whence solely on
d
) and see that the assertion follows with a constant

C_{\textrm{eq}}=\sqrt{C_{1}^{2}C_{2}^{-2}+C_{\textrm{eq,2}}^{2}}
(that exclusively
depends on
d
) in case
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)^{d}
satisfies

\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{f})<\varepsilon_{0}|\boldsymbol{f}(\textrm{\rm mid}(K))|
.
The other regime is covered by the choice
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{0}
and leads to
the assertion with a constant
C_{\textrm{eq}}(\varepsilon_{0})
in case

\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(K)^{d}
satisfies

\varepsilon_{0}|\boldsymbol{f}(\textrm{\rm mid}(K))|\leq\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{D}\boldsymbol{f})
.
This concludes the proof.

\square

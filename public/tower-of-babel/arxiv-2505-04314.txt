Monotonic normalized heat diffusion for distance-regular graphs with classical parameters of diameter 3 3
Shiping Liu, Heng Zhang

Abstract
We prove the monotonic normalized heat diffusion property on distance-regular graphs with classical parameters of diameter 3 3 . Regev and Shinkar found a Cayley graph for which this property fails. On the other hand, this property has been proved on abelian Cayley graphs, graphs with 3 3 distinct eigenvalues and regular bipartite graphs with 4 4 distinct eigenvalues by Price, Nica and Kubo-Namba, respectively. A distance regular graph with classical parameters of diameter 3 3 has 4 4 distinct eigenvalues and is not necessarily bipartite or vertex transitive.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we study the heat kernel
H:[0,\infty)\times V\times V\to\mathbb{R}
on a simple finite connected graph
G=(V,E)
.
For any two vertices
u,v\in V
and any
t\geq 0
,
H_{t}(u,v)
can be considered as the probability that a continuous-time random walker starting at vertex
u
, is at vertex
v
at time
t
.
For any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
, we consider the function

t\mapsto r_{t}(u,v):=\frac{H_{t}(u,v)}{H_{t}(u,u)}.

Clearly, we have

r_{0}(u,v)=0,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\lim_{t\to\infty}r_{t}(u,v)=1.

(1)

It is natural to ask whether the above function is monotonically non-decreasing or not. This question has been considered by Regev and Shinkar
[
15
]
. We will refer to this property as
the
monotonic normalized heat diffusion property
, or the MNHD property for short, following
[
8
,
10
]
.

Due to (
1
), a necessary condition for the MNHD property is that
r_{t}(u,v)\leq 1
holds for any
t\geq 0
. However, there do exist graphs for which the function
r_{t}(u,v)
exceeds 1 for certain vertices
u,v
at certain time
t
. This is observed by Regev and Shinkar
[
15
, Proposition A.1]
. Regev and Shinkar mentioned that this observation was brought to their attention by Cheeger. They also provided an explicit example which is a
4
-regular graph with
10
vertices
[
15
, Figure 2]
.

A sufficient condition to ensure that
r_{t}(u,v)\leq 1
is a
heat homogeneity
, that is, the heat kernel has constant diagonal, which means that the value of
H_{t}(u,u)
is independent of the choice of
u
(see
[
15
, Section 1]
and
[
10
, Section 1]
). In fact, the heat homogeneity holds if and only if the graph is walk-regular
[
10
, Theorem A.1]
. Recall that a graph is walk-regular if for every
\ell\geq 2
, the number of closed walks of length
\ell

starting from and ending at every vertex is constant. Typical walk-regular graphs includes vertex-transitive graphs, distance-regular graphs, and regular graphs with at most
4
distinct eigenvalues.

Peres asked in 2013 (see
[
15
, Section 1]
) whether the MNHD property holds for all
vertex transitive graphs
or not. Regev and Shinkar
[
15
]

showed that there exists a Cayley graph for which the MNHD property fails. This provides a negative answer to Peres’ question. On the other hand, Price
[
13
]
proved that the MNHD property does hold for all finite
abelian
Cayley graphs.

Nica
[
10
]
showed that the MNHD property holds for graphs with
3
distinct Laplacian eigenvalues. We stress that the result of Nica does not require the graph to be regular. Kubo and Namba
[
8
]
confirmed the MNHD property for regular
bipartite
graphs with
4
distinct eigenvalues. It is natural to ask whether the MNHD property holds for all
regular graphs with at most
4
distinct eigenvalues
.

Distance-regular graphs
are introduced by Biggs
[
1
]
as a natural extension of distance-transitive graphs. A graph is distance-regular if for any two vertices
u,v
at distance
h
, the number of vertices that are at distance
i
to
u
and at distance
j
to
v
only depends on the three numbers
h,i
and
j
. Every distance-regular grpah has diameter
D
if and only if it has
D+1
distinct eigenvalues.

For a regular graph
G
with
4
distinct eigenvalue,
van Dam and Haemers
[
5
]
showed that
G
is distance-regular if and only if the number of vertices at distance
2
from each given vertex satisfies an expression in terms of eigenvalues of
G
.

We identify a class of distance-regular graphs with
4
distinct eigenvalues that are not necessarily
bipartite
, for which the MNHD property holds true. Our main result states as follows.

Theorem 1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a distance-regular graph with classical parameters of diameter
3
. Then
G
has the monotonic normalized heat diffusion (MNHD) property.

Most distance-regular graphs related to classical groups and groups of Lie type have classical parameters. Lots of interesting graphs constructed from algebraic structures are either distance-regular graphs with classical parameters (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
and
[
3
, Tables 6.1 and 6.2]
) or are closely related to distance-regular graphs with classical parameters (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
and
[
6
, Section 3.2]
). We refer to Subsection
2.3
for the precise definition of a distance-regular graph with classical parameters.
Although there exist various classification results for distance-regular graphs with classical parameters of large diameter (see, e.g.
[
6
]
), relatively fewer results are known for the diameter
3
case. We believe that our investigation into the monotonic normalized heat diffusion (MNHD) property sheds new light on the structure of distance-regular graphs with classical parameters.

In
[
2
]
, Biggs classified the distance-regular graphs of diameter 3 into three categories: antipodal, bipartite and primitive. We show in Section
5
that the MNHD property holds for all
antipodal
distance-regular graphs of diameter
3
. Notice that the bipartite case follows from the result of Kubo and Namba
[
8
]
.

Relevance of the MNHD property in other areas

It is very natural to study whether the MNHD property holds on a given graph. In general, it is highly concerned to understand how certain properties of solutions to differential/difference equations depend on the structure of the underlying graphs.
Moreover, the MNHD property is very useful and relevant to other research areas.

It is also very interesting to study the MNHD property in the setting of Riemannian geometry. Similarly, the MNHD property does not always hold for Riemannian manifolds
[
13
, Appendix A]
. In the other direction, it was shown that the MNHD property holds on all flat tori
[
15
]
and some other Riemannian symmetric spaces including the
3
-dimensional hyperbolic space
[
13
, Section 5 and Appendix B]
.
It is still open whether the MNHD property holds on all Riemannian symmetric spaces of compact type and on all hyperbolic spaces.

In a lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the MNHD property reduces to a monotonicity property of the periodic Gaussian function. An extension of this monotonicity property to the non-spherical Gaussian case
[
16
, Proposition 4.2]
can be used to derive a monotonicity property of the normalized heat diffusion on a torus with respect to different flat Riemannian metrics
[
12
,
16
]
. Such an application also leads to a key ingredient in the framework of the so-called numerical cohomology theory
[
11
,
14
]
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect basics about graph Laplacian, heat kernel, and distance-regular graphs with classical parameters.

2.1
Graph Laplacian and heat kernels

Let
d\geq 1
be an integer and
G=(V,E)
a simple finite connected graph with vertex set
V
and edge set
E
. Suppose that
|V|=n\geq 2
. The graph Laplacian
L
is a symmetric
n\times n
matrix
L=(L(x,y))_{x,y\in V}
defined by
L=\mathrm{Deg}-A
, where
\mathrm{Deg}
is the diagonal vertex degree matrix and
A
is the adjacency matrix. The constant vector
\mathbf{1}=(1,1,...,1)^{T}
is an eigenvector of
L
corresponding to the eigenvalue
0
. Since
G
is connected, the eigenvalue
0
is simple by the Perron-Frobenius theorem. Let us denote by
\mathrm{sp}(L)
the set of all distinct eigenvalues of
L
. The graph Laplacian has the following spectral decomposition

L=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{sp}(L)}\lambda P_{\lambda},

(2)

where
P_{\lambda}
is
the projection operator onto the eigenspace
associated with
\lambda
. The projection operator
P_{0}=\langle\cdot,\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\sqrt{n}}\rangle\frac{\mathbf{1}}{\sqrt{n}}

corresponding to the simple eigenvalue
0
is an averaging operator, where we use
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard Euclidean inner product. In matrix form, we have
P_{0}=\frac{1}{n}J
, with
J
being the
n\times n
all-
1
matrix.

The heat kernel
H_{t}
on the graph
G
is defined in matrix form as
H_{t}:=e^{-tL}
. Accordingly,
H_{t}
has the following spectral decomposition

H_{t}=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathrm{sp}(L)}e^{-t\lambda}P_{\lambda}\ ,

(3)

for all
t\geq 0
. For any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
, we define the function

h_{u,v}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows,

h_{u,v}(t):=H_{t}^{\prime}(u,v)H_{t}(u,u)-H_{t}(u,v)H_{t}^{\prime}(u,u).

(4)

Here
H_{t}^{\prime}
stands for the derivative of
H_{t}

with respect to
t
.
The MNHD property is equivalent to that
h_{u,v}(t)\geq 0
for any
t\geq 0
. Nica observed that this is always true at
t=0
.

Lemma 1

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

For any two distinct vertices
u,v
, it holds that
h_{u,v}(0)\geq 0
.

Let us list all
\lambda\in\mathrm{sp}(L)
as follows:

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{s}.

(5)

Then, we have the following expression of
h_{u,v}(t)
, see, e.g.,
[
8
, (3.2)]
.

Lemma 2
.

For any two distinct vertices
u,v
and
t\geq 0
, we have

h_{u,v}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{n}e^{-t\lambda_{i}}\Delta_{i}(u,v)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq s}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})e^{-t(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})}\Delta_{ij}(u,v),

(6)

where for any
i,j=1,\ldots,s
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=P_{\lambda_{i}}(u,u)-P_{\lambda_{i}}(u,v),

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=P_{\lambda_{i}}(u,v)P_{\lambda_{j}}(u,u)-P_{\lambda_{j}}(u,v)P_{\lambda_{i}}(u,u).

Proof.

This follows from (
3
) by a straightforward calculation.
∎

2.2
Heat kernels on regular graphs with four distinct eigenvalues

We consider a simple finite connected
d
-regular graph
G=(V,E)
with
4
distinct Laplacian eigenvalues, i.e.,
s=3
in (
5
).
In this particular setting, we have the following explicit expressions for the functions
\Delta_{i}(u,v)
and
\Delta_{ij}(u,v)
due to Kubo and Namba
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3

(
[
8
, Lemmas 3 and 4]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a simple finite connected
d
regular graph with
4
distinct Laplacian eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}.

Denote by

\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})},\,\,C_{2}=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})},\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,C_{3}=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{3})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3})}.

Then, for any two distinct vertices
u,v\in V
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=C_{1}\{d^{2}+d-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}\},

(7)

\displaystyle\Delta_{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle=C_{2}\{d^{2}+d-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}\},

(8)

\displaystyle\Delta_{3}(u,v)

\displaystyle=C_{3}\{d^{2}+d-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\},

(9)

and for
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
,

\displaystyle\Delta_{ij}(u,v)=C_{i}C_{j}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle\bigg\{\left(d+\frac{1-n}{n}\lambda_{k}\right)L^{2}(u,v)

\displaystyle-\left(d^{2}+d+\frac{1-n}{n}\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)L(u,v)-\frac{1}{n}\left((d^{2}+d)\lambda_{k}-d\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)\bigg\}.

(10)

Proof.

The expressions (
7
)-(
9
) are obtained in
[
8
, Proof of Lemma 3]
and equation (
10
) is a rearrangement of
[
8
, (3.4)]
. For readers’ convenience, we briefly recall the key steps of the proof. Solving the equations
I=P_{0}+P_{\lambda_{1}}+P_{\lambda_{2}}+P_{\lambda_{3}}
and
L^{k}=\lambda_{1}^{k}P_{\lambda_{1}}+\lambda_{2}^{k}P_{\lambda_{2}}+\lambda_{3}^{k}P_{\lambda_{3}}
,
k=1,2
leads to the following expressions for the projection operators:

\displaystyle P_{\lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=C_{1}\big\{L^{2}-(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3})L+\lambda_{2}\lambda_{3}(I-P_{0})\big\},

\displaystyle P_{\lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle=C_{2}\big\{L^{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{3})L+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{3}(I-P_{0})\big\},

\displaystyle P_{\lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle=C_{3}\big\{L^{2}-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})L+\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}(I-P_{0})\big\}.

Then Lemma
3
follows by a straightforward calculation, noticing that
L(u,u)=d
and
L^{2}(u,u)=d^{2}+d
.
∎

2.3
Distance-regular graph with classical parameters

A connected graph
G=(V,E)
of diameter
D
is

distance-regular
if and only if it is regular of degree
d
, and if there exist constants
c_{i},a_{i},b_{i}
, known as intersection numbers, such that for all

i=0,1,\dots,D
, and for any two vertices
x,y\in V
at distance

i=d(x,y)
, among the neighbors of
y
, there are
c_{i}
at
distance
i-1
from
x
,
a_{i}
at distance
i
, and
b_{i}
at
distance
i+1
. It follows that
d=b_{0}
, and
a_{i}=d-b_{i}-c_{i}
for all
i=0,1,\dots,D
. These constants are commonly listed in
the so-called
intersection array

\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{D-1};c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{D}\}.

Observe that
b_{D}=0
and
c_{0}=0
are excluded from this array, while
c_{1}=1

is included, and all numbers in the intersection array are positive
integers. Furthermore, the number of vertices can be derived from the intersection
array. Indeed, by counting edges
yz
with
d(x,y)=i
and
d(x,z)=i+1
for each vertex
x
, it has a constant number of vertices
n_{i}
at a given
distance
i
. Specifically, it follows from
n_{0}=1
and

n_{i+1}=\frac{b_{i}n_{i}}{c_{i+1}}
for all
i=0,1,\dots,D-1
that the number of vertices
n=n_{0}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{D}
.

The
distance-regular graphs with classical parameters

(D,b,\alpha,\beta)
are the distance-regular graphs whose intersection numbers can be expressed by the four
parameters: diameter
D
and numbers
b
,
\alpha
,

\beta
, in the following way,

\displaystyle b_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{bmatrix}D\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}i\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}\right)\left(\beta-\alpha\begin{bmatrix}i\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}\right)\;\;,i=0,1,\dots,D-1,

(11)

\displaystyle c_{i}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}i\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}\left(1+\alpha\begin{bmatrix}i-1\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}\right)\;\;,i=1,2,\dots,D,

(12)

where
\begin{bmatrix}j\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix}=1+b+b^{2}+\cdots+b^{j-1}
is the Gaussian binomial coefficient with basis
b
. To make
b_{i},c_{i}
feasible parameters, there are necessary restrictions on the numbers
b,\alpha,\beta
. For example,
the number
b
must be an integer not equal to
0
or
-1

[
3
, Proposition 6.2.1]
. See also Lemma
4
below.

Proposition 1

(
[
3
, Corollary 8.4.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(D,b,\alpha,\beta)
. Then the eigenvalues of its adjacent matrix are

\theta_{i}=\frac{b_{i}}{b^{i}}-\begin{bmatrix}i\\
1\\
\end{bmatrix},\ \ i=0,1,\cdots,D,

where
b_{i},i=0,1,\ldots,D
are given by (
11
).

For more discussion on distance-regular graphs, we refer to
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
.

2.4
Distance-regular graphs with classical parameters of diameter 3

In this subsection, we collect basics of distance-regular graphs with classical parameters
(D,b,\alpha,\beta)
of diameter
D=3
.

First, we derive from (
11
) and (
12
) the intersection numbers as follows.

\begin{array}[]{ll}b_{0}=d=(1+b+b^{2})\beta,&b_{1}=(b+b^{2})(\beta-\alpha),\\
b_{2}=b^{2}(\beta-\alpha(1+b)),&b_{3}=0,\\
c_{0}=0,&c_{1}=1,\\
c_{2}=(1+b)(1+\alpha),&c_{3}=(1+b+b^{2})(1+\alpha(1+b)),\\
a_{0}=0,&a_{1}=-1+b\alpha+b^{2}\alpha+\beta,\\
a_{2}=(1+b)(-1-\alpha+b^{2}\alpha+\beta),&a_{3}=(1+b+b^{2})(-1-\alpha-b\alpha+\beta).\\
\end{array}

(13)

Then, we calculate the vertex number
n
as below,

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle 1+d+\frac{db_{1}}{c_{2}}+\frac{db_{1}b_{2}}{c_{2}c_{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+d+\frac{db(\beta-\alpha)}{1+\alpha}+\frac{db(\beta-\alpha)b_{2}}{(1+\alpha)c_{3}}.

(14)

Applying Proposition
1
, we obtain the eigenvalues of its adjacent matrix as follows,

\theta_{0}=d,\theta_{1}=(1+b)(\beta-\alpha)-1,\theta_{2}=\beta-(1+b)(1+\alpha),\,\text{and}\,\,\theta_{3}=-(1+b+b^{2}),

(15)

and, accordingly, the non-zero Laplacian eigenvalues
\gamma_{i}=d-\theta_{i}
listed below,

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=1+\alpha+b\alpha+b^{2}\beta,\gamma_{2}=(1+b)(1+\alpha+b\beta),\,\text{and}\,\,\gamma_{3}=(1+b+b^{2})(1+\beta).

(16)

In the following, we derive some necessary constraints from (
13
) on the parameters
b,\alpha,\beta
in case
D=3
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a distance-regular graph with classical parameters
(3,b,\alpha,\beta)
. Then we have the following constraints:

(i)

If
b\geq 1
, then
\alpha\geq 0
.

(ii)

If
b\leq-2
, then
\alpha<-1
.

(iii)

\beta\geq 1+(1+b)\alpha\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since
c_{2}=(1+b)(1+\alpha)>0
, we obtain
\alpha<-1
if
b\leq-2
. Moreover, since
b
is an integer, we read from
c_{2}
that
(1+b)\alpha
is an integer. Via
c_{3}=(1+b+b^{2})(1+\alpha(1+b))>0
, we derive that
\alpha(1+b)>-1
if
b\geq 1
. Therefore, we have
\alpha(1+b)\geq 0
if
b\geq 1
. This proves
(i)
. The constraint
\beta\geq 1+(1+b)\alpha
follows from the facts
a_{3}=(1+b+b^{2})(-1-\alpha-b\alpha+\beta)\geq 0
. Applying
(i)
and
(ii)
, we further deduce
\beta\geq 1+(1+b)\alpha\geq 1
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 1
.

Lemma
4

(i)
is taken from Lv and Koolen
[
9
]
. In fact, Lemma
4

(i)
and
(ii)
hold for any distance-regular graph with classical parameters of diameter
D\geq 3
. The proof of Lemma
4

(i)
and
(ii)
relies only on the expressions of
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
in (
13
), which holds for any diameter
D\geq 3
.

3
The MNHD property for regular graphs with four distinct eigenvalues

In this section, we derive a sufficient condition for the MNHD property to hold on regular graphs with four distinct eigenvalues.

Our main Theorem
2
in this section relies on the following key observation.

Lemma 5
.

For any three distinct numbers
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\{i,j,k\}=\{1,2,3\}
and any numbers
L(u,v),L^{2}(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\Delta_{i}(u,v),i=1,2,3
and
\Delta_{ij}(u,v)
be defined in terms of
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k},L(u,v),L^{2}(u,v)
as in (
7
)-(

). Then it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{n}\Delta_{i}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}}{n}\Delta_{j}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}{n}\Delta_{k}(u,v)

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})(\Delta_{ik}(u,v)+\Delta_{jk}(u,v))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L^{2}(u,v)-L(u,v)(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}).

(17)

Remark 2
.

Notice that in identity (
17
), the three distinct numbers
\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j},\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are not required to be eigenvalues of the graph Laplacian
L
; the two numbers
L(u,v)
and
L^{2}(u,v)
are not required to be obtained from the graph Laplacian
L
and its square
L^{2}
. We only use the formal definitions of
\Delta_{i}
and
\Delta_{ij}
in (
7
)-(

).

Proof.

We break down the calculations into two parts.
First, we consider the terms involving
\Delta_{i}(u,v)
,
\Delta_{j}(u,v)
, and
\Delta_{k}(u,v)
. We calculate by inserting (
7
)-(
9
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{n}\Delta_{i}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}}{n}\Delta_{j}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}{n}\Delta_{k}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\left(d+d^{2}-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k})+\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}\right)}{n(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\left(d+d^{2}-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{k})+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{k}\right)}{n(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})\left(d+d^{2}-L^{2}(u,v)+(L(u,v)-d)(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\right)}{n(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{i})(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(d+d^{2}-L^{2}(u,v))\mathrm{I}+(L(u,v)-d)\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}}{n(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})},

where we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}:=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})+\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{II}:=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{j}^{2}-\lambda_{k}^{2})-\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{k}^{2})+\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{i}^{2}-\lambda_{j}^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{III}:=

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}^{2}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})-\lambda_{i}^{2}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{k})+\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Putting together yields that

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}}{n}\Delta_{i}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{j}\lambda_{i}}{n}\Delta_{j}(u,v)+\frac{\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k})}{n}\Delta_{k}(u,v)

The distance-regular graphs with diameter
3
are classified as antipodal, bipartite, and primitive
[
2
]
. A distance-regular graph with an intersection array
\{d,b_{1},b_{2};1,c_{2},c_{3}\}
is antipodal if
b_{2}=1
and
c_{3}=d
. In fact, the general form of the intersection array for an antipodal distance-regular graph of diameter

\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2};c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}\}=\{d,m\gamma,1;1,\gamma,d\}

[

is primitive if it is neither antipodal nor bipartite.

For the bipartite case, the MNHD property is true by the result of Kubo and Namba
[

]
. For the antipodal case, we show that the MNHD property holds still.

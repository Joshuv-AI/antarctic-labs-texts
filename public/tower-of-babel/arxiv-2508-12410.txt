SRMA-Mamba: Spatial Reverse Mamba Attention Network for Pathological Liver Segmentation in MRI Volumes
Jun Zeng, Yannan Huang, Elif Keles, Halil Ertugrul Aktas, Gorkem Durak, Nikhil Kumar Tomar, Quoc-Huy Trinh, Deepak Ranjan Nayak, Ulas Bagci, Debesh Jha

Abstract
Liver Cirrhosis plays a critical role in the prognosis of chronic liver disease. Early detection and timely intervention are critical in significantly reducing mortality rates. However, the intricate anatomical architecture and diverse pathological changes of liver tissue complicate the accurate detection and characterization of lesions in clinical settings. Existing methods underutilize the spatial anatomical details in volumetric MRI data, thereby hindering their clinical effectiveness and explainability. To address this challenge, we introduce a novel Mamba-based network, SRMA-Mamba, designed to model the spatial relationships within the complex anatomical structures of MRI volumes. By integrating the Spatial Anatomy-Based Mamba module (SABMamba), SRMA-Mamba performs selective Mamba scans within liver cirrhotic tissues and combines anatomical information from the sagittal, coronal, and axial planes to construct a global spatial context representation, enabling efficient volumetric segmentation of pathological liver structures. Furthermore, we introduce the Spatial Reverse Attention module (SRMA), designed to progressively refine cirrhotic details in the segmentation map, utilizing both the coarse segmentation map and hierarchical encoding features. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SRMA-Mamba surpasses state-of-the-art methods, delivering exceptional performance in 3D pathological liver segmentation. Our code is available for public: https://github.com/JunZengz/SRMA-Mamba .



Introduction

Cirrhosis constitutes a significant global health burden, ranking as the 11th leading cause of mortality worldwide in 2019
(
Huang et al. 2023
)
. As the end-stage manifestation of progressive liver fibrosis, it remains a principal cause of death among individuals with chronic liver disease. Cirrhosis most commonly arises from chronic viral hepatitis, metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MAFLD), and alcohol-related liver disease
(
Huang et al. 2023
)
. A hallmark pathological feature is the disruption of normal hepatic architecture, replaced by regenerative nodules. The condition is frequently associated with sinusoidal portal hypertension, which constitutes a major risk factor for hepatocellular carcinoma
(
Bartolomeo, Trerotoli, and Serio 2011
)
. Severe complications, including hepatic encephalopathy, ascites, and hepatorenal syndrome, frequently manifest during the late or decompensated phase of cirrhosis, indicating a substantial decline in patient survival
(
Tapper and Parikh 2023
)
.

Liver biopsy remains a pivotal procedure in the detection and diagnosis of liver diseases, particularly in assessing liver fibrosis and cirrhosis
(
Chowdhury and Mehta 2023
)
. However, it is associated with inherent risks, including hemorrhage and infection, owing to its invasive nature. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), as a non-invasive diagnostic modality, offers superior soft tissue contrast, positioning it as an essential diagnostic modality for the identification of cirrhotic lesions and the assessment of liver pathology. Morphological alterations and variations in signal intensity within MRI volumes play a critical role in the diagnosis and staging of cirrhosis
(
Tonan, Fujimoto, and Qayyum 2010
)
. CirrMRI600+ is a large-scale dataset of cirrhotic MRI volumes to develop automatic segmentation methods with deep learning
(
Jha et al. 2025
)
. Computer-assisted segmentation enables rapid and precise localization of cirrhotic lesions, thereby enhancing clinical decision-making and optimizing treatment planning.

Volumetric segmentation is a critical task in medical image analysis, aiming to delineate the region of interest (ROI) within volumetric data. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs)
(
Çiçek et al. 2016
;
Milletari, Navab, and Ahmadi 2016
;
Oktay et al. 2018
)
for volumetric segmentation commonly utilize an encoder-decoder architecture. Due to the localized nature of convolutional kernels, CNNs struggle to capture long-range dependencies in the volumetric data. In contrast, transformers
(
Cao et al. 2022
;
Hatamizadeh et al. 2022
;
Shaker et al. 2024
)
utilize self-attention mechanisms to model global relationships, enabling them to achieve significant success in volumetric segmentation. However, transformers incur substantial computational costs due to the quadratic complexity of the self-attention mechanism.

Recently, state-space models (SSMs)
(
Gu, Goel, and Ré 2021
;
Smith, Warrington, and Linderman 2022
;
Gu and Dao 2023
)
have demonstrated exceptional performance in capturing long-range dependencies. Mamba
(
Gu and Dao 2023
)
employs a selection mechanism and a hardware-aware algorithm to model global dependencies, achieving state-of-the-art performance in linear-time sequence modeling with linear complexity. To extend Mamba into the vision domain, VMamba
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
performs four distinct traversal path scans to achieve 2D global receptive field. Based on this foundation, Mamba-based methods
(
Ruan, Li, and Xiang 2024
;
Wang et al. 2024
;
Zeng et al. 2025
;
Xing et al. 2024
)
have emerged in the field of medical image segmentation.
RMAMamba
(
Zeng et al. 2025
)
effectively segments pathological liver regions within MRI slices by employing a novel reverse mamba attention mechanism, yet fails to capture the intricate spatial dependencies inherent in volumetric MRI data.
Segmamba
(
Xing et al. 2024
)
takes the whole volumetric data as a linear sequence and explores the forward, reverse, and inter-slice features interaction. However, SegMamba exhibits limitations in capturing complex spatial anatomical relationships, as it linearizes volumetric data into a one-dimensional sequence, thereby disregarding the inherent spatial context, an essential factor for accurate interpretation in clinical applications. To address these issues, we propose SRMA-Mamba, to model the spatial relationships within the complex anatomical structures of MRI volumes for efficient volumetric pathological liver segmentation. The main contributions of the work are as follows:

•

We present a novel spatial reverse mamba attention network, SRMA-Mamba, for efficient volumetric pathological liver segmentation in MRI volumes.

•

We propose a novel Spatial Anatomy-Based Mamba (SABMamba) module to perform scans within
multiple anatomical perspectives, including sagittal, coronal, and axial planes. Additionally, we introduce the Spatial Reverse Mamba Attention (SRMA) module, which progressively refines the segmentation map using hierarchical features and coarse segmentation map.

•

We introduce the Anatomy-Based Selective Scan (ABSS) module to model 3D visual feature representations, enabling direct processing of 3D volumetric data with low computational complexity. This module addresses the gap left by Vision Mamba in the 3D vision domain.

•

We conduct extensive experiments on the CirrMRI600+ T1W and T2W datasets and demonstrate that SRMAMamba achieves state-of-the-art performance in 3D pathological liver segmentation, surpassing existing volumetric segmentation methods.

In the encoder, the input MRI scan is initially divided into embeddings, reducing the resolution to half of the original size. Related work

Volumetric Segmentation

Volumetric (3D) segmentation is a fundamental challenge in medical imaging, enabling precise localization, delineation, and quantification of organs and tumors. This technique is indispensable for various clinical applications, including surgical planning, treatment monitoring, and the assessment of disease progression. Among the various architectures designed for this task, the encoder-decoder framework
(
Ronneberger, Fischer, and Brox 2015
)
has become the dominant approach. In this framework, the encoder progressively extracts hierarchical feature representations, while the decoder sequentially reconstructs these features into binary voxel predictions, aligned with the ground truth labels.

Initially, 3D segmentation was approached by converting volumetric data into 2D image slices, which were then processed using two-dimensional convolutional neural networks. This technique is known as pseudo-3D segmentation. Subsequent research
(
Çiçek et al. 2016
;
Milletari, Navab, and Ahmadi 2016
)
in the field shifted to using 3D convolutional neural networks, enabling direct processing of volumetric data. 3D-UNet
(
Çiçek et al. 2016
)
extends the original U-Net architecture by replacing all 2D operations with their 3D counterparts, allowing the network to capture richer volumetric spatial relationships and effectively learn feature representations from sparse annotations. SegResNet
(
Myronenko 2018
)
leverages ResNet blocks to construct an encoder-decoder network for efficient volumetric segmentation. UX-Net
(
Lee et al. 2022
)
introduces volumetric depth-wise convolutions with large kernel sizes, enabling a receptive field similar to that of hierarchical Transformers. MedNext
(
Arnold et al. 2020
)
, which incorporates the ConvNeXt
(
Liu et al. 2022
)
design into the 3D U-Net framework, is the first architecture fully based on ConvNeXt, specifically tailored for volumetric segmentation. UNETR
(
Hatamizadeh et al. 2022
)
leverages Transformers to encode the features of volumetric data, generating global multi-scale feature representations. SwinUNETR
(
Hatamizadeh et al. 2021
)
utilizes the hierarchical Swin transformer
(
Liu et al. 2021
)
as an encoder to extract multi-scale features for volumetric segmentation. SwinUNETRv2
(
He et al. 2023
)
integrates Stagewise Convolutions into the original SwinUNETR, enhancing its performance in volumetric segmentation.
SegMamba
(
Xing et al. 2024
)
introduces the tri-oriented Mamba module for sequence modeling of flattened volumetric data, effectively capturing long-range dependencies within the volume.

Vision Mamba

Transformers have exhibited remarkable performance across diverse domains. However, their intrinsic quadratic complexity presents considerable challenges, rendering them computationally expensive to train. State Space Models (SSMs) have emerged as a promising approach for sequence modeling
(
Mehta et al. 2022
;
Fu et al. 2022
)
, establishing themselves as a formidable alternative to the Transformer architecture. LSSL
(
Gu et al. 2021
)
leverages HIPPO initialization to demonstrate the feasibility of SSMs in capturing long-range dependencies. S4
(
Gu, Goel, and Ré 2021
)
enhances model performance by diagonalizing the parameter matrix, leading to a substantial reduction in computational costs. Since then, various structured SSM models
(
Gupta, Gu, and Berant 2022
)
have showcased remarkable performance on sequential data, such as in text and speech. However, SSMs still face scalability challenges due to their limited support for parallel computation.

Recently, Mamba
(
Gu and Dao 2023
)
introduces a hardware-aware algorithm and a selective scanning mechanism, with linear complexity and efficient inference. With its superior reasoning speed and global modeling capabilities, Mamba has become a focal point of research across a wide range of fields. To introduce Mamba into the visual domain, Vim
(
Zhu et al. 2024
)
introduces bidirectional Mamba blocks that leverage bidirectional state spaces to compress visual feature representations. MambaVision
(
Hatamizadeh and Kautz 2025
)
redesigns the Mamba formulation and introduces a hybrid Mamba-Transformer backbone architecture to model long-range dependencies in visual tasks effectively. VMamba
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
introduces a 2D Selective Scanning mechanism (SS2D) that performs four-way scanning to achieve comprehensive spatial domain traversal. By stacking multiple visual state-space modules equipped with SS2D, VMamba demonstrates strong feature encoding capabilities, delivering effective hierarchical feature representations for various visual tasks.

Methodology

Overall Architecture of SRMA-Mamba

SRMA-Mamba is an innovative mamba-based network specifically designed for volumetric segmentation
of pathological liver in MRI volumes. The network leverages the Mamba-based encoder for hierarchical feature extraction and integrates a spatial reverse mamba attention module to progressively refine the segmentation map with features from each encoding stage.
As illustrated in Fig 1, the proposed network employs an encoder-decoder architecture.
The encoder extracts different levels of features from the input cirrhotic MRI volume and the decoder incrementally refines the segmentation maps, utilizing the coarse segmentation map alongside the features obtained from the encoding stages.

Specifically, the input MRI volume is fed into the stem block and converted into embedding features. Subsequently, the features are processed through four encoding stages, each consisting of a Gated Spatial Convolution block (GSC)
(
Xing et al. 2024
)
, a SABMamba block, and a downsampling operation, generating hierarchical feature maps
\frac{H}{2^{i}}\times\frac{W}{2^{i}}\times\frac{D}{2^{i}}\times C_{i}
, where
C_{i}\in\left\{48,96,192,384\right\}
and
i\in\left\{1,2,3,4\right\}
. Then the feature map from the final encoding stage is processed by a convolutional layer to produce the coarse segmentation map. After that, the feature maps from each encoding stage, along with the segmentation map from the previous level, are used as inputs to the SRMA module.

Spatial Anatomy-Based Mamba (SABMamba)

Details of the
Anatomy-Based Selective Scan (ABSS)
module

In clinical practice, radiologists typically review MRI volumes from multiple anatomical planes to develop a comprehensive spatial awareness of liver pathology. Inspired by this, we introduce the Spatial Anatomy-Based Mamba module (SABMamba), designed to model complex anatomical relationships between spatial pixels by integrating information from three orthogonal planes.

As shown in Figure 2, SABMamba consists of consecutive residual branches. The first branch includes a Layer Normalization followed by a ABM block, while the second branch contains a Layer Normalization and a multi-layer perception layer. This process can be formulated as follows:

x_{1}=x+ABM(LN(x)),

(1)

x_{2}=x_{1}+MLP(LN(x_{1})),

(2)

where MLP stands for a multi-layer perceptron, and LN refers to Layer Normalization.

In the ABM block, the input features first pass through a linear layer and a depthwise separable convolution. The resulting features are then passed through the SiLU activation function before being fed into an ABSS module. Finally, the features undergo Layer Normalization, and a linear layer is applied to produce the final output of the ABM block.

Anatomy-Based Selective Scan (ABSS)

Vision Mambas have introduced a range of scanning mechanisms for the global modeling of efficient visual data. While these approaches have achieved significant success in the 2D vision domain, their application to 3D vision remains largely unexplored. Xing et al.
(
Xing et al. 2024
)
propose a tri-oriented Spatial Mamba module (TSMamba) to model volumetric data efficiently. However, TSMamba cannot directly process 3D volumetric data. It first serializes the volume before global modeling, disrupting the spatial anatomical relationships between voxels, which are essential for accurate liver segmentation, particularly in the presence of complex liver pathologies. To address these issues, we introduce
Anatomy-Based Selective Scan
(ABSS) to process 3D volumetric data directly, without losing the spatial anatomical relationships within the voxel.

The overall structure of ABSS is illustrated in Figure 3. ABSS is employed to learn the spatial feature representation of cirrhotic liver tissue. It comprises three key components: scan expanding operation, scan merging operation
(
Liu et al. 2024
)
, and the S6 module
(
Gu and Dao 2023
)
. The selective mechanism within the S6 module effectively filters out irrelevant information while preserving relevant features. Specifically, the input MRI volume is initially rearranged into three anatomical planes.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}f_{h}&=\text{Reshape}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times H\times L_{1}},\\
f_{w}&=\text{Reshape}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times W\times L_{2}},\\
f_{d}&=\text{Reshape}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times D\times L_{3}}.\end{array}\right.

(3)

where
L_{1}=W\times D,L_{2}=H\times D,L_{3}=H\times W
.

The scan expanding operation then unfolds the data from these planes into sequences along four distinct directions. Next, the S6 module selectively processes these sequences to extract features from the anatomical planes.
After that, the scan merging operation integrates these features and restores them to their original resolution. This process can be summarized as follows:

Y=\text{CrossMerge}\left(\text{S6}\left(\text{CrossScan}(f)\right)\right)

(4)

where
f\in\{f_{h},f_{w},f_{d}\}
, and
CrossMerge
and
CrossScan
represent the scan expanding and scan merging operations, respectively. Finally, we reshape the resulting features to their original dimensions and apply an addition operation to generate the final fused feature map.

Spatial Reverse Mamba Attention (SRMA)

Reverse attention
(
Chen et al. 2018
)
first utilizes deep features to generate a coarse segmentation map, which offers high semantic confidence but low spatial resolution. It then progressively erases the predicted salient regions using features from other levels. With the help of additional supervision, this process guides the network to gradually refine object boundaries and recover fine details. While this mechanism has been widely adopted and proven effective in 2D image segmentation
(
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Fan et al. 2020
;
Zeng et al. 2025
)
, it remains underexplored in the context of 3D volumetric segmentation.
To address this issue, we introduce the Spatial Reverse Mamba Attention (SRMA) module to demonstrate the efficiency of reverse attention mechanism in 3D volumetric segmentation.

The architecture of SRMA, depicted in Figure 4, consists of SABM and convolutional blocks. SRMA takes the coarse segmentation map and feature map from the encoding stage as input, progressively refining edge details and enhancing feature representation. Specifically, in SABM, the feature map from the current level and the segmentation map from the previous level are first fed into the SRMA module. Then the segmentation map undergoes a Sigmoid function and a reverse attention operation to generate the reverse attention map weights
A_{i}
, which can be formulated as:

A_{i}=1-\mathrm{Sigmoid}(S_{i+1}),

(5)

where
i\in\{1,2,3\}
,
S_{i+1}
denotes the coarse segmentation map. Subsequently, element-wise multiplication is applied between the attention weights and the enhanced feature map. The resulting features
F
are further processed by a SABM module, which is summarized as follows:

F=\delta(Conv(A_{i}\cdot\delta(f_{i})))

(6)

where
\delta(\cdot)
denotes a SABMamba module. Finally, a residual connection is applied, followed by two convolutional layers to generate the output.

Experiments

Dataset

CirrMRI600+
(
Jha et al. 2024
)
is a single-center, multi-vendor, multi-sequence dataset designed to enhance cirrhotic liver research in MRI volumes. The dataset includes both enhanced and non-enhanced scans, encompassing a wide range of liver cirrhosis cases across different disease stages and morphological variations. It also features corresponding segmentation masks, meticulously annotated by radiologists. The dataset comprises two imaging modalities: T1W and T2W MRI. T1W MRI is particularly effective at visualizing anatomical structures, vascular tissue, and fat content, revealing features like liver capsule retraction. In contrast, T2W MRI offers higher sensitivity to soft tissue, making it ideal for detecting changes in cirrhosis-related tissue, such as liver fibrosis. These multimodal characteristics allow researchers to combine T1W and T2W imaging for comprehensive segmentation and classification studies. Specifically, the scans were acquired from 339 patients and meticulously reviewed and annotated by senior radiologists to ensure their high quality and suitability. The dataset includes 628 abdominal MRI scans, consisting of 310 T1-weighted (T1W) images and 318 T2-weighted (T2W) images. For the T1W dataset, 248 images were split for training, 31 for validation, and 31 for testing. Similarly, for the T2W dataset, 256 images were allocated for training, 31 for validation, and 31 for testing.

Implementation details

We implemented our model using the PyTorch framework on a single Tesla V100 GPU with 32GB of memory. During the training phase, each volume is randomly cropped to a size of 224×224×64. The Adam optimizer is employed to optimize the model parameters with the initial learning rate of 1e-4. In our experiments, all models are trained for 500 epochs with an
early stopping patience of 50. The batch size was initiated to
2. The data augmentation techniques, including spatial cropping, flipping, and intensity scaling, were employed to mitigate overfitting. We use a combination of Dice loss and cross-entropy loss to supervise the network training.

Evaluating Metrics

We use the Dice similarity coefficient (mDSC), mean intersection over union (mIoU), recall, precision, Hausdorff distance (HD95), and average symmetric surface distance (ASSD) to evaluate the performance of our network. Among these metrics, mDSC and mIoU evaluate the spatial overlap between the predicted segmentation and the ground truth annotations, accurately reflecting the overall volume estimation of cirrhosis. However, these metrics do not capture boundary segmentation accuracy. HD95 calculates the 95th percentile of the maximum distance between segmentation boundaries, effectively measuring the most significant boundary errors. ASSD computes the average distance between predicted and true boundaries, providing a precise estimate of boundary accuracy. In cirrhotic segmentation tasks, HD95 and ASSD are particularly efficient for evaluating the accuracy of nodular surface delineation associated with liver fibrosis.

Discussion

In this paper, we explored the potential of visual mamba as a visual backbone for volumetric segmentation and demonstrated that the SABMamba encoder exhibits robust spatial visual feature modeling capabilities with reduced resource consumption. The SABMamba encoder adopts a Transformer-like architecture, incorporating residual connections and feed-forward layers to efficiently generate hierarchical feature representations. However, while most visual Mamba methods rely on scanning multi-directional sequences or rearranged regions, most researchers often overlook the clinical significance embedded in medical images. Clinicians and radiologists evaluate MRI and CT scans by analyzing the volumetric data across multiple anatomical planes, integrating their medical knowledge to inform accurate clinical diagnoses. Inspired by this clinical practice, we utilized advanced visual Mamba scanning technology to selectively capture key anatomical planes and integrate these features from MRI volumes, thereby generating spatial visual representations of cirrhotic tissue. Comprehensive experimentation has demonstrated that SRMA-Mamba exhibits superior performance in volumetric segmentation tasks, particularly in the context of pathological liver scans.

However, a notable limitation of SRMA-Mamba lies in the reverse attention mechanism, which is presently constrained to binary classification scenarios encompassing only normal and pathological tissue. Therefore, SRMA-Mamba fails to handle multi-category segmentation tasks, such as brain tumor or organ segmentation. Notably, although we reduce GPU memory usage by employing only one SABMamba Block in each stage, the SABMamba encoder still maintains robust and effective visual modeling capabilities. Additionally, when the input image is downsampled to one-quarter of its original resolution in the stem block, the model’s GPU usage is significantly reduced, while still maintaining robust segmentation performance. Our future work will aim to extend this approach to multi-class volumetric segmentation tasks, while also exploring more effective and efficient methods for volumetric segmentation of pathological liver.

Conclusion

This paper presents a novel spatial reverse mamba attention network, named SRMA-Mamba, for volumetric segmentation of pathological liver in MRI volumes. We introduce the SABMamba module, which performs planar scanning across the three anatomical planes and combines feature information from these planes to construct a spatial representation of liver cirrhosis lesions. Additionally, SRMA-Mamba progressively refines boundary precision through multiple Spatial Reverse Mamba Attention modules, thereby enhancing the overall

We also introduce an Anatomy-Based Selective Scan module that directly processes voxel data to generate 3D feature representations, preserving spatial anatomical information. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SRMA-Mamba achieves state-of-the-art performance on both T1W and T2W modalities of the liver cirrhosis benchmark dataset, surpassing existing methods and highlighting its effectiveness and stability in the volumetric segmentation of pathological liver.

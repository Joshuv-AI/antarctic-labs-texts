Extended 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators, Novikov Yang-Baxter equations and post-Novikov algebras
Jianfeng Yu, Yanyong Hong

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce the definition of extended 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators on a Novikov algebra ( A , ∘ ) (A,\circ) associated to an A A -bimodule Novikov algebra which is a generalization of the definition of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators and show that there are new Novikov algebra structures on the A A -bimodule Novikov algebra obtained from extended 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators. We also introduce the definition of post-Novikov algebras and show that there is a close relationship between post-Novikov algebras and 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators of weight λ \lambda . The tensor form of extended 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators is also investigated which leads to the definition of extended Novikov Yang-Baxter equations, which is a generalization of the notion of Novikov Yang-Baxter equations. The relationships between extended 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operators, Novikov Yang-Baxter equations, extended Novikov Yang-Baxter equations and generalized Novikov Yang-Baxter equations are established.

]
, affine manifolds and affine

[
19
]
, deformation of
associative algebras
[

]
, vertex algebras

The definition of Novikov bialgebras was introduced in
[
10

[

9

, the notion of Novikov Yang-Baxter equations was introduced in

, and the skew-symmetric solutions of the Novikov Yang-Baxter equation (NYBE) can produce Novikov bialgebras.

Motivated by the results of
[

-operators on Novikov algebras). It should be pointed out that such problems for Lie algebras and associative algebras have been investigated in

[

introduced the notion of extended
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras in order to study double Lie algebra structures and nonabelian generalized Lax pairs. Note that extended
\mathcal{O}

-operators on Lie algebras
[
23
,
20
,
1

\mathcal{O}
-operators and extended classical Yang-Baxter equations which are generalizations of classical Yang-Baxter equations was presented. By these results, it is easy to see that a general solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation is equivalent to some extended
\mathcal{O}
-operator (see
[
5
,
18
]
). Moreover, it was shown in
[
4
]
that extended
\mathcal{O}
-operators are also related to generalized classical Yang-Baxter equations which also naturally appeared in the study of Lie bialgebras. Therefore, extended
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Lie algebras are important in the theory of Lie algebras and Lie bialgebras. Similar results for associative algebras were presented in
[
3
]
. Based on these results, in this paper, we plan to introduce the definitions of extended
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Novikov algebras, extended Novikov Yang-Baxter equations and generalized Novikov Yang-Baxter equations, and investigate the relationships between them. We also hope that by studying extended
\mathcal{O}
-operators on Novikov algebras, we can also obtain some new results for the NYBE as in
[
5
]
for the case of Lie algebras.

2.
Post-Novikov algebras, extended
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators and new Novikov algebra structures

In this section, we introduce the definitions of
A
-bimodule Novikov algebras, post-Novikov algebras and extended
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators, where
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra. The relationship between post-Novikov algebras and

{\mathcal{O}}

2.1.
A
-bimodule Novikov algebras

We start with the classical notions of Novikov algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

[
13
]
A
Novikov algebra
is a vector space
A
with a binary
operation
\circ
satisfying

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c-a\circ(b\circ c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b\circ a)\circ c-b\circ(a\circ c),

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\circ c)\circ b,\;\;\;a,b,c\in A.\vskip-2.84544pt

A Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is called
trivial
if for all
a
,
b\in A
,
a\circ b=0
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra. Define another binary operation
\star
on
A
by

\displaystyle a\star b:=a\circ b+b\circ a,\quad\;\;\;~~a,~~b\in A.

Let
L_{A}
,
R_{A}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\bf k}(A)
be the linear maps defined by

\displaystyle L_{A}(a)(b)\coloneqq a\circ b,\quad R_{A}(a)(b)\coloneqq b\circ a,\quad\;\;\;a,~~b\in A.

Define
L_{A,\star}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\bf k}(A)
by
L_{A,\star}=L_{A}+R_{A}
.

Definition 2.2
.

[
22
]
A
bimodule
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
is a triple
(V,l_{A},r_{A})
, where
V
is a vector space and
l_{A}
,
r_{A}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\bf k}(V)
are linear maps satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle l_{A}(a\circ b-b\circ a)v=l_{A}(a)l_{A}(b)v-l_{A}(b)l_{A}(a)v,

(2)

\displaystyle l_{A}(a)r_{A}(b)v-r_{A}(b)l_{A}(a)v=r_{A}(a\circ b)v-r_{A}(b)r_{A}(a)v,

(3)

\displaystyle l_{A}(a\circ b)v=r_{A}(b)l_{A}(a)v,

(4)

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)r_{A}(b)v=r_{A}(b)r_{A}(a)v,

\displaystyle\hskip-76.82234pt\quad\;\;\;a,b\in A,v\in V.\vskip-8.5359pt

Note that
(A,L_{A},R_{A})
is a bimodule of
(A,\circ)
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
(M,\cdot)
be another Novikov algebra. Let
l_{A}
,
r_{A}
:
A\rightarrow\text{End}_{\bf k}(M)
be two linear maps. We call
M

(or the quadruple
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
) an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra
if
(M,l_{A},r_{A})
is a bimodule of
(A,\circ)
and the following equalities hold.

(5)

\displaystyle(l_{A}(a)v)\cdot w-l_{A}(a)(v\cdot w)=(r_{A}(a)v)\cdot w-v\cdot(l_{A}(a)w),

(6)

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)(v\cdot w)-v\cdot(r_{A}(a)w)=r_{A}(a)(w\cdot v)-w\cdot(r_{A}(a)v),

(7)

\displaystyle(l_{A}(a)v)\cdot w=(l_{A}(a)w)\cdot v,

(8)

\displaystyle r_{A}(a)(v\cdot w)=(r_{A}(a)v)\cdot w,

\displaystyle\hskip-76.82234pt\quad\;\;\;a\in A,v,w\in M.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra. Then
(A,\circ,L_{A},R_{A})
is an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra. Moreover, a bimodule
(V,l_{A},r_{A})
of
(A,\circ)
can also be seen as an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra if we regard the vector space
V
as the trivial Novikov algebra. For convenience, in the sequel, we say that
(V,l_{A},r_{A})
is an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra, which means that
(V,l_{A},r_{A})
is a bimodule of
(A,\circ)
and the Novikov algebra structure on
V
is trivial.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra,
M
be a vector space with a binary operation
\cdot

and
l_{A}
,
r_{A}:A\to{\rm End}_{\bf k}(M)
be linear maps.
Define a binary operation
\bullet
on
A\oplus M
by

(a+u)\bullet(b+v)\coloneqq a\circ b+l_{A}(a)v+r_{A}(b)u+u\cdot v,\quad\;\;\;a,b\in A,u,v\in M.

Then
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
is an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra if and only
if
(A\oplus M,\bullet)
is a Novikov algebra.

Proof.

It follows directly from
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

By Proposition
2.5
, if the binary operation
\cdot
on
M
is trivial,
(M,l_{A},r_{A})
is a bimodule of
(A,\circ)
if and only if
(A\oplus M,\bullet)
is a Novikov algebra. In this case, we call
(A\oplus M,\bullet)
the

semi-direct product
of
A
by
(M,l_{A},r_{A})
and denoted by

A\ltimes_{l_{A},r_{A}}M
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
V
be a vector space.
For a linear map
\varphi:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\bf k}(V)
, define a linear map

\varphi^{*}:A\rightarrow\mathrm{End}_{\bf k}(V^{*})
by

\langle\varphi^{*}(a)f,v\rangle=-\langle f,\varphi(a)v\rangle,\;\;\;a\in A,f\in V^{*},v\in V,

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual pairing between
V
and
V^{*}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

[
10
, Proposition 3.3]

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
(V,l_{A},r_{A})
be a
bimodule of
(A,\circ)
. Then
(V^{\ast},l_{A}^{\ast}+r_{A}^{\ast},-r_{A}^{\ast})
is
a bimodule of
(A,\circ)
.

Remark 2.8
.

By Proposition
2.7
, the bimodule
(A,L_{A},R_{A})
of a Novikov algebra
(A,\circ)
gives the bimodule

(A^{\ast},L_{A,\star}^{\ast},-R_{A}^{\ast})
. By Remark
2.4
,
(A^{\ast},L_{A,\star}^{\ast},-R_{A}^{\ast})
is also an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra.

2.2.
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators of weight
\lambda
and post-Novikov algebras

We first introduce the notion of
{\mathcal{O}}
-operators of weight
\lambda
on Novikov algebras.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra,
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
be an
A
-bimodule Novikov algebra and
\lambda\in\bf{k}
. A linear map
\alpha:M\rightarrow A
is called an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
if
\alpha
satisfies

(9)

\displaystyle\alpha(u)\circ\alpha(v)=\alpha\Big(l_{A}(\alpha(u))v+r_{A}(\alpha(v))u+\lambda u\cdot v\Big),\quad\;\;\;u,v\in M.

An
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator
T:A\rightarrow A
of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(A,\circ,L_{A},R_{A})
is called a
Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
, i.e.,

(10)

T(x)\circ T(y)=T\Big(T(x)\circ y+x\circ T(y)+\lambda x\circ y\Big),\quad x,y\in A.

Remark 2.10
.

If
(M,\cdot)
is trivial, then an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
is just an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator on
(A,\circ)
associated to the bimodule
(M,l_{A},r_{A})
defined in
[
10
]
.

Remark 2.11
.

When
\lambda\neq 0
,
\alpha
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight
\lambda
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
if and only if
\alpha/\lambda
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight
1
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})
if and only if
\alpha
is an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight
1
on
(A,\circ)
associated to
(M,\cdot_{\lambda},l_{A},r_{A})
, where
\cdot_{\lambda}
is defined by
u\cdot_{\lambda}v=\lambda u\cdot v
for all
u
,
v\in M
.

Then we introduce the definition of post-Novikov algebras.

Definition 2.12
.

A
post-Novikov algebra
is a quadruple
(A,\circ,\lhd,\rhd)
, where
(A,\circ)
is a Novikov algebra, and
\lhd
and
\rhd
are binary operations such that the following compatibility conditions hold.

(11)

\displaystyle(a\rhd c)\lhd b=(a\rhd b+a\lhd b+a\circ b)\rhd c,

(12)

\displaystyle a\rhd(c\lhd b)-(a\rhd c)\lhd b+(c\lhd a)\lhd b=c\lhd(a\lhd b+a\rhd b+a\circ b),

(13)

\displaystyle(a\rhd b+a\lhd b+a\circ b)\rhd c-(b\rhd a+b\lhd a+b\circ a)\rhd c=a\rhd(b\rhd c)-b\rhd(a\rhd c),

(14)

\displaystyle(a\lhd b)\lhd c=(a\lhd c)\lhd b,

(15)

\displaystyle(a\rhd b)\circ c-a\rhd(b\circ c)=(b\lhd a)\circ c-b\circ(a\rhd c),

(16)

\displaystyle(b\circ c)\lhd a-b\circ(c\lhd a)=(c\circ b)\lhd a-c\circ(b\lhd a),

(17)

\displaystyle(a\rhd b)\circ c=(a\rhd c)\circ b,

(18)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\lhd c=(a\lhd c)\circ b,\vskip-11.38092pt\;\;\;a,b,c\in A.

Remark 2.13
.

(i)

If the binary operation
\circ
in the definition of post-Novikov algebras is trivial, then a post-Novikov algebra is a pre-Novikov algebra defined in
[
10
]
.

(ii)

Let
(A,\circ,\lhd,\rhd)
be a post-Novikov algebra. It is straightforward to verify that
(A,\circledcirc)
is also a Novikov algebra with the following binary operation:

(19)

\displaystyle a\circledcirc b:=a\rhd b+a\lhd b+a\circ b,~a,b\in A.

We say that
(A,\circledcirc)

has a compatible post-Novikov algebra structure
(A,\circ,\lhd,\rhd)
and
(A,\circledcirc)
is
the associated Novikov algebra of
(A,\circ,\lhd,\rhd)
.

Recall
[
21
]
that a
commutative dendriform trialgebra

(A,\cdot,\circ)
is a
vector space
A
with two binary operations
\cdot
and
\circ
, where
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra and they satisfy the following compatibility conditions:

(20)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c=a\circ(b\circ c+c\circ b+b\cdot c),

(21)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\circ b,

(22)

\displaystyle(a\circ b)\cdot c=a\cdot(c\circ b),

(23)

\displaystyle(a\cdot b)\circ c=(a\circ c)\cdot b,\;\;a,b,c\in A.

Next, we present a construction of post-Novikov algebras from commutative dendriform trialgebras with a derivation.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\circ)
be a commutative dendriform trialgebra with a derivation

. Define binary operations
\ast
,
\lhd
and
\rhd:A\times A\rightarrow A
by

a\ast b=a\cdot D(b),\;\;a\lhd b\coloneqq a\circ D(b),\;\;a\rhd b\coloneqq D(b)\circ a\;\;\;a,b\in A.

Then
(A,\ast,\lhd,\rhd)
is a post-Novikov algebra.

Proof.

It is straightforward.

∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
(A,\circ,\lhd,\rhd)
be a post-Novikov algebra. Define two linear maps
L_{\rhd}
,
R_{\lhd}:A\rightarrow{\rm End}_{\bf k}(A)
by
L_{\rhd}(a)b:=a\rhd b
and
R_{\lhd}(b)a:=a\lhd b
for all

(A,\circ,L_{\rhd},R_{\lhd})
is a
(A,\circledcirc)
-bimodule Novikov algebra.

Proof.

It is straightforward.

Theorem 2.16
.

(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
(M,\cdot,l_{A},r_{A})

-bimodule Novikov algebra. Let

be an
{\mathcal{O}}
-operator of weight

(i)

There is a post-Novikov algebra structure on

Denote this post-Novikov algebra by

(ii)

\alpha
is a Novikov algebra homomorphism from

to

, where

(iii)

If

\displaystyle~~~\alpha(x)\circ_{\alpha}\alpha(y):=\lambda\alpha(x\cdot y),~\alpha(x)\rhd_{\alpha}\alpha(y):=\alpha(l_{A}(\alpha(x))y),~\alpha(x)\lhd_{\alpha}\alpha(y):=\alpha(r_{A}(\alpha(y))x),~x,y\in M.

Furthermore,

to

(\alpha(M),\circ_{\alpha},

Proof.

It is straightforward.

Let

(A,\circ,L_{\rhd},R_{\lhd})

3.1.
Extended Novikov Yang-Baxter equation

We first recall the definition of Novikov Yang-Baxter equations given in
[
10
]
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra.
Let
r=\sum_{i}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\in A\otimes A
and

r^{\prime}=\sum_{j}x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{j}^{\prime}\in A\otimes A
. Set

r_{12}\circ r^{\prime}_{13}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\circ x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{i}\otimes y_{j}^{\prime},\;\;r_{12}\circ r^{\prime}_{23}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\circ x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{j}^{\prime},\;\;r_{13}\circ r^{\prime}_{23}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{i}\circ y_{j}^{\prime},

r_{13}\circ r^{\prime}_{12}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\circ x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{i},\;\;r_{23}\circ r^{\prime}_{13}=\sum_{i,j}x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\circ y_{j}^{\prime},

r_{12}\star r^{\prime}_{23}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes y_{i}\star x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{j}^{\prime},\;\;r_{13}\star r^{\prime}_{23}=\sum_{i,j}x_{i}\otimes x_{j}^{\prime}\otimes y_{i}\star y_{j}^{\prime}.

Definition 3.1
.

[
10
]

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
r\in A\otimes A
. The
equation

(46)

\displaystyle r\diamond r\coloneqq r_{13}\circ r_{23}+r_{12}\star r_{23}+r_{13}\circ r_{12}=0

is called the
Novikov Yang-Baxter equation (NYBE)
in
A
.

Proposition 3.2
.

[
4
, Proposition 2.3]

Let
V
and
W
be two vector spaces.

(a)

Define the natural linear isomorphisms as follows:

\displaystyle\wedge=\wedge_{V,W}:V\otimes W\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V^{*},W),\;\;\langle w^{\ast},\hat{r}(v^{\ast})\rangle=\langle v^{\ast}\otimes w^{\ast},r\rangle,

\displaystyle\vee=\vee_{{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V,W)}:{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V,W)\rightarrow V^{*}\otimes W,\;\;\langle\check{\alpha},v\otimes w^{\ast}\rangle=\langle w^{\ast},\alpha(v)\rangle,

where
r\in V\otimes W
,
v^{\ast}\in V^{\ast}
,
w^{\ast}\in W^{\ast}
,
\alpha\in{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V,W)
and

.
Then
\wedge_{V,W}
and
\vee_{{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V^{\ast},W)}
are the inverse of each other.

(b)

For
\alpha\in{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(V^{*},W)
, let
\alpha^{*}:W^{\ast}\rightarrow V^{\ast\ast}\cong V
be the corresponding dual linear map of
\alpha
. Then for any
r\in V\otimes W
, we have
\widehat{\tau(r)}=\hat{r}^{*}
.

Using the above notations, for a vector space
A
, we set

(47)

\displaystyle r_{\pm}:=(r\pm\tau(r))/2,\;\;\alpha_{\pm}:=(\alpha\pm\alpha^{*})/2,\;\;\;\;r\in A\otimes A,~\alpha\in{\rm Hom}_{\bf k}(A^{*},A).

Moreover, for any
r\in A\otimes A
, we have
(\hat{r})_{\pm}=\hat{r_{\pm}}
.

By Proposition
3.2
, we can identify
r\in A\otimes A
with the linear map
\hat{r}:A^{*}\rightarrow A
by

(48)

\langle\hat{r}(a^{*}),b^{*}\rangle=\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*},r\rangle,\quad a^{*},b^{*}\in A.

Similarly define a linear map

by

(49)

\langle a^{*},\hat{r}^{t}(b^{*})\rangle=\langle a^{*}\otimes b^{*},r\rangle,\quad a^{*},b^{*}\in A,

if and only if

\alpha=\hat{r}_{-}=(\hat{r}-\hat{r}^{t})/2,\quad\beta=\hat{r}_{+}=(\hat{r}+\hat{r}^{t})/2,

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(A,\circ)
be a Novikov algebra and
s\in A\otimes A
be symmetric. Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(i)

s is
invariant
, i.e.,

(51)

(L_{A}(x)\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes L_{A,\star}(x))s=0,\quad x\in A.

(ii)

\hat{s}:(A^{*},L_{A,\star}^{*},-R_{A}^{*})\rightarrow A
is balanced.

(iii)

\hat{s}:(A^{*},L_{A,\star}^{*},-R_{A}^{*})\rightarrow(A,L_{A},R_{A})
is an
A
-bimodule homomorphism.

Proof.

First, we show that Item (i) is equivalent to Item (ii). Since
s\in A\otimes A
is symmetric, for all
x\in A
,
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\langle(L_{A}(x)\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes L_{A,\star}(x))s,a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle s,L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle-\langle s,a^{*}\otimes L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\circ\hat{s}(b^{*}),a^{*}\rangle+\langle x\circ\hat{s}(a^{*})+\hat{s}(a^{*})\circ x,b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle R_{A}(\hat{s}(b^{*}))x,a^{*}\rangle+\langle L_{A,\star}\hat{s}(a^{*}))x,b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle x,R_{A}^{*}(\hat{s}(b^{*}))a^{*}\rangle-\langle x,L_{A,\star}^{*}(\hat{s}(a^{*}))b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x,(-R_{A}^{*})(\hat{s}(b^{*}))a^{*}-L_{A,\star}^{*}(\hat{s}(a^{*}))b^{*}\rangle.

Thus
s
is invariant if and only if
\hat{s}:(A^{*},L_{A,\star}^{*},-R_{A}^{*})\rightarrow A
is balanced.

Next, we show that Item (i) is equivalent to Item (iii). For all
x\in A
,
a^{*},b^{*}\in A^{*}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle(L_{A}(x)\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes L_{A,\star}(x))s,a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle s,L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle-\langle s,a^{*}\otimes L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\hat{s}(b^{*}),L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*}\rangle-\langle\hat{s}(L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}),a^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle x\circ\hat{s}(b^{*})-\hat{s}(L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}),a^{*}\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\langle(L_{A}(x)\otimes{\rm id}+{\rm id}\otimes L_{A,\star}(x))s,a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle s,L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*}\otimes b^{*}\rangle-\langle s,a^{*}\otimes L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\hat{s}(L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*}),b^{*}\rangle-\langle\hat{s}(a^{*}),L_{A,\star}^{*}(x)b^{*}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-\hat{s}(L_{A}^{*}(x)a^{*})+x\circ\hat{s}(a^{*})+\hat{s}(a^{*})\circ x,b^{*}\rangle.

Then it is easy to see that
s
is invariant if and only if
\hat{s}:(A^{*},L_{A,\star}^{*},

-R_{A}^{*})\rightarrow(A,L_{A},R_{A})
is an
A
-bimodule homomorphism.
∎

.

Let
(A,\circ,\mathcal{B}(\cdot,\cdot))
be a quadratic Novikov algebra and
\varphi
be the invertible linear map
:A\rightarrow A^{*}

\displaystyle\langle L_{A,\star}^{*}(P_{\beta}(a^{*}))b^{*},z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle L_{A,\star}^{*}(\beta(x))\varphi(y),z\rangle=-\langle\varphi(y),\beta(x)\star z\rangle

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{B}(y,\beta(x)\star z)=\mathcal{B}(z\circ y,\beta(x))=\mathcal{B}(\beta(z\circ y),x),

Prenatal monitoring in primary health care: a design science research-based approach to FHIR interoperability
Authors: Renata D Braga, Plinio S Leitao-Junior, Suse B Castilho, Dandra A Souza, Lismary B Oliveira, Michael L D Oliveira, Luiselena L Esmeraldo, Carlos A G Tibiriçá, Diana F Guimarães, Aline R Nogueira, Livia M Vilela, Rejane F Ribeiro-Rotta, Fabio N Lucena, Juliana P Souza-Zinader

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis research addresses the development and validation of an information model for pregnancy monitoring that, through the Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR) specification, seeks to promote interoperability for the quality of prenatal care by multidisciplinary teams in Brazilian primary healthcare contexts. The Design Science Research (DSR) approach is applied to propose and evaluate the model, through quality strategies, where iterations increase the mode maturity in accordance with healthcare scenarios.ResultsA set of use cases is constructed from user stories in primary care that abstract health concepts for pregnancy monitoring. The information model is presented in a hierarchical structure, with the categorization of related concepts grouped into pillars, in the context of the integration of health professionals who work in a complementary manner. The traceability of the use cases in relation to the semantic pillars of the information model, the implementation of the model from a FHIR perspective, and how the model complies with the ISO 13972:2022 standard (Health informatics – Clinical information models - Characteristics, structures and requirements) are analyzed.ConclusionsThis work makes several key contributions, starting with the development of an information model for pregnancy monitoring. This model integrates different areas of primary care to promote a holistic and personalized approach to prenatal care. To ensure data can be shared effectively, it establishes health information interoperability using the HL7 FHIR R4 standard, which involved mapping resources and creating profiles as specified in an implementation guide. The study rigorously applies the Design Science Research method, utilizing multidisciplinary consensus strategies to scientifically improve and evolve the information model.Keywords: Health information interoperability, Prenatal care, FHIR, Fast health interoperability resources

Background

Digital transformation, the ongoing process of using digital technologies to transform businesses and services, is no longer a future possibility in healthcare but an urgent necessity [1]. An important goal is to promote the sharing and unequivocal interpretation of information to integrate systems, professionals, and organizations [2]. Semantic interoperability enables data sharing between organizations (e.g., healthcare units) and their internal ecosystem without loss of meaning [3]. The search for interoperability is one of the most pressing challenges for health systems and has required studies and research in recent years [4–6].

The lack of semantic interoperability potentially leads to inconsistent understanding of information among health professionals, which negatively impacts individual and community healthcare services [7, 8]. This negative effect is even more evident in extensive networks of health units, where the diversity of formats and recording methods can generate inconsistencies and failures in communication between the different points of care [9].

The search for a uniform representation of heterogeneous data has its specificity in each country, which is a consequence of its legislation and infrastructure [10]. In the case of Brazil, as a federative republic that encompasses 26 states, one federal district and more than 5,000 municipalities, there are hundreds of thousands of health care units, distributed across different regions, with geographic and population capillarity. Interoperability between information systems is urgent in this scenario, includes the availability of unambiguous understanding of information in all health domains, such as prenatal care.

In the Brazilian constitution health is a right granted to each citizen. The Unified Health System (UHS) is the national public system for the provision of health care in all municipalities [11]. From a decentralized administrative point of view, the UHS is implemented by three entities [12]:

Municipalities are responsible for the actual execution of basic care services.

Municipalities are responsible for the actual execution of basic care services.

States support specialized health services and more complex services.

The federal government moderates and defines national health policies, in addition to supporting financial resources.

In practice, these entities have coordinated actions: when one of the entities presents deficiencies in the execution of its competences, the others carry them out.

Given the global demand for health information structural and semantic interoperability, which includes complexities of the Brazilian health system described above, the development of health information models (e.g. the structure and content of health concepts), combined with the adoption of international standards (e.g. HL7 FHIR - Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), has emerged as relevant initiatives for semantic interoperability, promoting the exchange of clinical data between systems and unambiguous interpretation of health information [13–18].

This research stems from a collaboration between the Goiás State Health Department (SES-GO) and the Federal University of Goiás (UFG), Brazil, focused on health interoperability and continuing education. A key objective is developing standardized information architectures, including health information models designed for consensus-based clinical scenarios.

Within this context, prenatal care monitoring is a priority for the UHS, as highlighted by recent research [19–21]. This priority requires the development and implementation of interoperability-focused information models. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), prenatal care encompasses skilled health professionals’ provision of care to pregnant individuals, aiming to optimize health outcomes for both mother and fetus(es). Key components include risk identification, prevention, and management of pregnancy-related or comorbid conditions, health education, and health promotion [22].

This study aimed to develop and validate a standardized HL7 FHIR-based clinical information model for pregnancy monitoring in primary healthcare set tings of SES-GO, Brazil. The model was designed to promote semantic interoperability and integrated multidisciplinary care by harmonizing fragmented data from electronic health records, immunization registries, and laboratory systems. This integration enables longitudinal pregnancy health profiles accessible across care settings. Critically, the design process engaged nurses, physicians, health informaticians, and policymakers to ensure clinical relevance and alignment with real-world workflows, instead of idealized or theoretical ones [23].

Although the literature addresses health information models, such as in [13–18], to the best of our knowledge, this is the first scientific communication that explores pregnancy monitoring and its mapping to the FHIR standard in a context of multidisciplinary primary health care.

Methods

The study applies the Design Science Research (DSR) approach, which is based on the development and validation of artifacts relevant to solving real-world problems [24]. DSR seeks the production of knowledge in the form of design principles, models, research approaches and impacts of the use of technology [25].

DSR has been used in several fields of health [26–28]. Fig. 1 shows a DSR process, which is composed of activities, each with a defined artifact [29].

Information model

The information model addresses key healthcare concepts for primary care teams. We define a ‘health concept’ as a data point in the model, like BMI, or a medication dose. So we use data element and health concept interchangeably throughout the text.

Figure 3 shows a mind map of the model’s data structure. It focuses only on the necessary key elements, considering the set of use cases, so some details (like patient details, dates, times, locations, and organizations) are omitted. The complete list of data elements can be found in the Implementation Guide [23].

Mind map illustrating the information model specifically designed for pregnancy monitoring

The map indicates the model supports the monitoring from one to multiple pregnancies of a patient (Node “1..N Pregnancy”), making it possible to have a comprehensive view of gestational development over time.

As foreseen in the methodology, data elements were categorized into “pillars”, so that each pillar brings together those concepts related in structure and/or purpose, namely clinical encounter, anthropometry, exam, medicinal product, and status.

A pillar represents a core area of prenatal care. It is composed of a set of related health concepts that collectively form a specific perspective. For example, one pillar might be dedicated to the clinical encounter, which includes all information documenting the interaction between the patient and the healthcare professional.

Clinical Encounter pillar summarizes encounters between patients and health professionals for assessment, intervention, and documentation of essential data: care unit, care professional, care specialty, care type, modality of service provision, risk classification, justification for the encounter, and care outcome. It records the longitudinal monitoring among different professionals, e.g., physicians, nurses, psychologists, nutritionists, and dentists.

Anthropometric pillar refers to measuring, recording, and analyzing essential body measurements: height, weight, and body mass index (BMI). These provide objective indicators of the nutritional status and physical development of the pregnant woman and fetus.

Exam pillar allows the communication and evaluation of the results of multidisciplinary exams. Basically, fundamental data elements are recorded, such as the requested test, which precisely identifies the type of clinical investigation required, and the result obtained, which documents the findings in a standardized manner.

We use the term “medicinal product” to represent any substance (or combination of substances) that can be administered to patients for the treatment or prevention of diseases, with the intention of making a medical diagnosis, or to restore, correct, or modify physiological functions. In this sense, a medicinal product may contain one or more manufactured items and one or more pharmaceutical products. In Medicinal Product pillar, immunobiologicals are documented, which precisely identifies the vaccine agent or biological product administered, and the dose, recording the exact quantity of the medication prescribed and administered. This enables safe monitoring of the therapy instituted, allowing the traceability of the patient’s medication history and immunizations.

The final pillar (Status) encapsulates the overall state of the pregnancy. It consolidates key data points, including temporal markers (e.g., last menstrual period, expected delivery date), outcome details, and a synthesis of clinical problems identified in other pillars. This comprehensive overview is crucial for identifying risk factors and tailoring the care plan to the specific needs of the patient and fetus.

By gathering the most important health data for primary care during pregnancy, this model promotes consistent and complete recording of concepts according to use cases. The model also recognizes the integration of care episodes and the biopsychosocial complexity of pregnancy, as well as the need for a coordinated approach by professionals from different areas.

Health concepts represented by coding systems in FHIR aim to have unambiguous interpretations of these concepts. The information model for prenatal monitoring is bound to international code systems and value sets, ensuring semantic interoperability through standardized public coding systems. Some examples are: ICD1 is used for the diagnostic categorization of associated conditions (Clinical Encounter pillar); LOINC2 2 is used for measurement elements, such as height, weight, and BMI, as well as requested laboratory examinations (Anthropometry and Exam pillars); and ICD and ICPC3 are applied to the pregnancy outcome (Status pillar).

A FHIR perspective for the information model

Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (FHIR®) is an international standard developed by the Health Level Seven International (HL7®) to promote the electronic exchange of health information in a standardized manner [34]. In the Brazilian context, the National Health Data Network (NHDN) has adopted FHIR as a basis to ensure the standardization of the exchange of clinical and administrative data [35].

FHIR resources

The core of FHIR is having a resource-oriented model, with each resource representing a healthcare entity such as a patient, a medication, or a clinical condition. In other words, each resource in FHIR is a modular, reusable unit designed to represent essential clinical or administrative data. There are over 150 resources defined in FHIR, seeking to be complete in covering health concepts [35].

Therefore, the understanding of FHIR resources is essential for professionals involved in interoperability and integration projects of health systems. Correct mapping of health concepts and events from the clinical information domain to FHIR resources ensures that the semantics and integrity of data are maintained throughout the electronic transmission of information.

FHIR resource mapping

The expert panel worked together to enable collaborative interaction between clinical concept experts (healthcare professionals) and interoperability experts (information technology and digital health professionals): healthcare professionals provided detailed and validated expertise on the definition and application of key clinical concepts, while FHIR experts used this expertise to accurately map these concepts to FHIR resources.

FHIR implementation guide

A FHIR Implementation Guide (IG) is a technical document that guides developers, analysts, and healthcare professionals in the consistent, interoperable, and secure adoption of FHIR resources in real-world digital health contexts. The full implementation guide for pregnancy monitoring is available [23].

On developing the IG, FHIR resources and attributes were used to define the technical structure of each concept, promoting semantic and syntactic interoperability. Each data element is linked to a value domain, often expressed through standardized terminologies (e.g., LOINC, SNOMED-CT, ICD-11), and implemented using HL7 FHIR Release 4 (R4) structures. FHIR profiling was conducted using tools such as SUSHI4 and SIMPLIFIER5, ensuring alignment with HL7 FHIR R4 and Brazilian terminology artifacts (e.g., imunizacao-gestante6).

Adequacy of the information model according to ISO 13972:2022 standard

ISO 13972:2022 standard (Health informatics – Clinical information models - Characteristics, structures and requirements) describes requirements for content, structure and context of clinical information models and the specification of their data elements, data element relationships, metadata and version control. Compliance with the requirements of the standard promotes the interoperability of healthcare information, in addition to having clinical data represented in a more consistent and structured manner [30].

Regarding the analysis of how the information model and its process adhere to the main key requirements addressed in the standard, it was mainly based on Section 6 of the standard, namely: Clinical Information Model Content, Structure and Requirements [30]. Regarding the development process of information models, the standard states “approaches often used include mind mapping of the domain that needs to be modeled or the preparation of a use case specification for one or more purposes” [30]. We have applied both approaches: use case specification and domain mind mapping, developed collaboratively and refined iteratively through shared editing sessions, to structure and relate key data elements. This dual-method approach supports a shared understanding between clinical and technical stakeholders.

Conclusion

This paper refers to a scientific communication planned within the framework of research cooperation between the academic community and healthcare professionals in the context of primary public health care. The research explores the process and artifacts involved in developing an information model for pregnancy monitoring, from its conception to its implementation:

Definition of use cases → Abstraction of health concepts → Development of the information model and its presentation in the form of a mind map, with its data elements grouped into information pillars → Selection of appropriate FHIR resources for the elements of the information model → Definition of profiles according to the selected FHIR resources, the specification of which is in an implementation guide [23].

Based on “use cases”, the model foresees collaboration between obstetricians, nurses, nutritionists and other professionals, ensuring a holistic and personalized approach to prenatal care.

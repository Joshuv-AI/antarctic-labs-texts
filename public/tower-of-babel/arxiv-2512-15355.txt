MacMahon’s Ω ≥ \Omega_{\geq} operator: A computational framework
Feihu Liu, Guoce Xin, [2mm] School of Mathematical Sciences, [-0.8ex] Capital Normal University, Beijing, 100048, P.R. China

Abstract
MacMahon introduced partition analysis in his book “Combinatory Analysis” as a computational technique for solving problems related to systems of linear Diophantine equations and inequalities. This paper aims to develop a fundamental computational method for MacMahon’s partition analysis. As applications, we present simplified computations for “Han’s formula”, the “ k k -gon partitions problem”, and the “two-dimensional problem”. Moreover, we apply our method to solve a challenging problem.

Introduction

In enumerative combinatorics,
partition analysis
was first introduced by MacMahon in his famous book, “Combinatorial Analysis”
[
20
]
, published in 1915. In this work, MacMahon primarily investigated plane partitions by introducing the omega operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
.

We briefly outline MacMahon’s partition analysis. Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. A formal Laurent series in
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
is defined as a series that can be expressed in the form

\sum_{i_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{k}^{i_{k}},

where each
a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
is an element of
\mathbb{F}
. For such formal Laurent series, the definition of the omega operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
as given in
[
20
]
is clear:

Definition 1.1
.

The operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
acts on a formal Laurent series in
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}
with coefficients
a_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}}
in
\mathbb{F}
by

\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\sum_{i_{1}=-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}\lambda_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{k}^{i_{k}}=\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}=0}^{\infty}a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}.

In particular, if
F(\lambda)
is a rational function in
\lambda
whose series expansion contains no negative power in
\lambda
, then
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}F(\lambda)=F(1)
.

We illustrate the operator with a simple example. Let
\mathbb{N}
denote the set of all non-negative integers. Suppose
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
with
a_{1}\geq a_{2}
. Consider the generating function

F(x_{1},x_{2})=\sum_{a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq 0}x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}.

Introducing a variable
\lambda
, we obtain

F(x_{1},x_{2})=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\geq 0}\lambda^{a_{1}-a_{2}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}x_{2}^{a_{2}}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x_{1}\lambda)(1-\frac{x_{2}}{\lambda})}.

(1)

To compute this, we need to eliminate
\lambda
from (
1
). The method for this calculation will be introduced later.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
12
]
, the coefficients
a_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}
from Definition
1.1
are defined over the field of rational functions in several complex variables over
\mathbb{C}
, with each
\lambda_{i}
restricted to a neighborhood of the circle
|\lambda_{i}|=1
. Furthermore, these coefficients are required to ensure that all series involved are absolutely convergent within their domain of definition.

Note that the operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
is supposed to act only on the Greek letters
\lambda
or
\mu
, or on the corresponding indexed versions such as
\lambda_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
in this paper. The parameters unaffected by
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
will be denoted by letters from the Latin alphabet.

An
Elliott rational function

E
takes the form

\displaystyle E=\frac{L}{(1-M_{1}(X)\Lambda^{c_{1}})(1-M_{2}(X)\Lambda^{c_{2}})\cdots(1-M_{n}(X)\Lambda^{c_{n}})},

(2)

where each
M_{i}(X)
is a monomial in the variables
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
that does not involve
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\}
;
L
is a Laurent polynomial in
X
and
\Lambda
. The
core problem
in MacMahon’s partition analysis is to compute
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}E
, which amounts to eliminating the variables
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}\}
to obtain a rational function in
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
.

2
Fundamental Calculation Method

We now present a fundamental computational method for the
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator.
In
[
22
]
, Xin introduced the field of iterated Laurent series to establish the uniqueness of series expansions for rational functions. Define the working field as
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}((\lambda_{k}))((\lambda_{k-1}))\cdots((\lambda_{1}))
.
We adopt terminology from
[
23
]
.

Note that every monomial
M\neq 1
is comparable to
1
in
\mathbb{K}
according to the following rule: identify the “smallest” variable
\lambda_{j}
that appears in
M
, i.e.,
\deg_{\lambda_{i}}M=0
for all
i<j
. If
\deg_{\lambda_{j}}M>0
, then
M
is said to be
small
, denoted
M<1
; otherwise,
M
is
large
, denoted
M>1
. Consequently, we can determine which of the following series expansions holds in
\mathbb{K}
:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-M}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i\geq 0}M^{i}&\text{if }M<1,\\
\dfrac{1}{-M(1-1/M)}=\sum_{i\geq 0}-\dfrac{1}{M^{i+1}}&\text{if }M>1.\end{cases}

Let
\overline{\mathbb{K}}=\mathbb{K}((x_{1}))\cdots((x_{m}))
. For Equation (
2
), when expanding
E
as a series in
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
, we typically require
E
to be in its
proper form
, meaning that
M_{i}(x)\Lambda^{c_{i}}<1
in
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
for all
i
.

We now employ Xin’s partial fraction decomposition algorithm
[
23
]
to compute
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}E
in
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
with respect to
\lambda
.
For convenience, we express
E
in the following form:

E(\lambda)=\dfrac{L(\lambda)}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})}\quad\text{(not in proper form)},

(3)

where
L(\lambda)
is a Laurent polynomial, each
u_{i}
is a monomial in
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}\cup(\Lambda\setminus\{\lambda\})
, and all
a_{i}
are
positive integers
. Note that each
u_{i}
is free of
\lambda
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that the partial fraction decomposition of
E(\lambda)
is given by

E(\lambda)=P(\lambda)+\frac{p(\lambda)}{\lambda^{s}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{A_{i}(\lambda)}{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}},

(4)

where each
u_{i}
is independent of
\lambda
;
P(\lambda)
,
p(\lambda)
, and
A_{i}(\lambda)
are polynomials,
\deg p(\lambda)<s
, and
\deg A_{i}(\lambda)<a_{i}
for all
i
. Then,

\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}E(\lambda)=P(1)+\sum_{u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}<1}\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}.

(5)

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
(Definition
1.1
), if
u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}<1
, then

\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{A_{i}(\lambda)}{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}=\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}.

If
u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}>1
, we have

\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{A_{i}(\lambda)}{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{\lambda^{-a_{i}}A_{i}(\lambda)}{-u_{i}\left(1-\frac{1}{u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}\right)}=0.

The proof is completed by applying Definition
1.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.2
.

[
23
]

Let
E(\lambda)
be as in (
4
). Then
A_{i}(\lambda)
is uniquely characterized by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}A_{i}(\lambda)\equiv E(\lambda)\cdot(1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})&\mod\langle 1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}\rangle,\\
\deg_{\lambda}A_{i}<a_{i},&\end{array}\right.

(6)

where
\langle 1-u\lambda^{a}\rangle
denotes the ideal generated by
1-u\lambda^{a}
.

The primary objective is to compute
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}E(\lambda)
for
\lambda
as in (
3
) in
\overline{\mathbb{K}}
.
We aim to compute

\mathcal{T}_{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}E(\lambda):=\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}},

where
A_{i}(\lambda)
is characterized by (
6
). Therefore, in this new notation, Theorem
2.1
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}E(\lambda)=P(1)+\sum_{i}\chi(u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}<1)\mathcal{T}_{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}E(\lambda),

(7)

where
\chi(\text{true})=1
and
\chi(\text{false})=0
.

The following result can be used to avoid computing
P(1)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
E(\lambda)
be as in (
3
). If
E(\lambda)
is proper in
\lambda
, i.e., the degree of the numerator is less than that of the denominator, then

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ E(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\chi(u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}<1)\cdot\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}\right).

(8)

If
E(0)=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}E(\lambda)
and
E(1)
exists, then

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ E(\lambda)=E(1)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\chi(u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}>1)\cdot\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}\right).

(9)

Proof.

Assume the partial fraction decomposition given in (
4
).
(i) If
E(\lambda)
is proper in
\lambda
, then
P(\lambda)=0
, and the first equality holds.
(ii) If
E(0)
exists, then
p(\lambda)
must be identically zero. Setting
\lambda=1
and applying (
7
) yields

E(1)=P(1)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ E(\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\chi(u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}>1)\mathcal{T}_{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}E(\lambda).

The second equality follows by subtracting the sum on the right-hand side.
∎

Equation (
9
) can be viewed as a dual form of (
7
). Due to these two formulas, it is convenient to refer to the denominator factor
1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}
as
contributing
when
u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}
is small and as
dually contributing
when
u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}
is large.

For convenience, we introduce the following notation.

Definition 2.4
.

We denote

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \underline{\dfrac{1}{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}}E(\lambda)(1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}})=\mathcal{T}_{1-u_{i}\lambda^{a_{i}}}E(\lambda)=\frac{A_{i}(1)}{1-u_{i}}.

In this notation, when applying
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
in
\lambda
, only the single underlined denominator factor contributes.

Theorems
2.1
,
2.2
, and
2.3
establish the fundamental computational framework for the
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator. Definition
2.4
provides a convenient notational shorthand that streamlines our presentation.
To illustrate the application of our method, we now consider the following example.

Example 2.5
.

[
20
, Page 102]

We compute the identity

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}=\frac{1-xyz}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-zy)}

using two distinct approaches. For clarity, we provide detailed step-by-step explanations to facilitate the reader’s understanding of our notation.

First approach:
We proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\underline{(1-x\lambda)}\underline{(1-y\lambda)}(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}\quad(\text{since both factors contribute})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\underline{(1-x\lambda)}(1-y\lambda)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}+\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)\underline{(1-y\lambda)}(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{\underline{(1-x\lambda)}(1-x^{-1}y)(1-xz)}+\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-xy^{-1})\underline{(1-y\lambda)}(1-yz)}\quad(\text{by Theorem \ref{charAS}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^{-1}y)(1-xz)}+\frac{1}{(1-xy^{-1})(1-y)(1-yz)}\quad(\text{setting }\lambda=1\text{ via \eqref{EE222Omega}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-xyz}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-zy)}.

At the third equality, we apply the operations modulo
\langle 1-x\lambda\rangle
and modulo
\langle 1-y\lambda\rangle
, respectively, as justified by Theorem
2.2
.

Second approach:
By considering the dual contribution, we derive:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)\underline{(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}}\quad(\text{by \eqref{DCE}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{-\lambda z^{-1}}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)\underline{(1-\frac{\lambda}{z})}}\quad(\text{since }a_{i}\text{is a positive integer in \eqref{NEE}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{-1}{(1-xz)(1-yz)\underline{(1-\frac{\lambda}{z})}}\quad(\text{by Theorem \ref{charAS}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\frac{-1}{(1-xz)(1-yz)(1-\frac{1}{z})}\quad(\text{setting }\lambda=1\text{ via \eqref{DCE}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-xyz}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-zy)}.

In fact, the second approach can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)\underline{(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{\lambda}{(1-x\lambda)(1-y\lambda)\underline{(\lambda-z)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{z}{(1-xz)(1-yz)\underline{(\lambda-z)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)}-\frac{z}{(1-xz)(1-yz)(1-z)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-xyz}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-zy)}.

3
Fundamental Formulas

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A\neq 1
be independent of
\lambda
. If
F(\lambda^{-1})
is a rational function in
\lambda^{-1}
that admits a formal power series expansion in
\lambda^{-1}
, then

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{1-A\lambda}\cdot F(\lambda^{-1})=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(A).

Proof.

Method 1: For any positive integer
m
, by Definition
1.1
and the geometric series expansion, we have

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{1-A\lambda}\cdot\lambda^{-m}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda^{k}A^{k}\cdot\lambda^{-m}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\lambda^{k-m}A^{k}=\sum_{k\geq m}A^{k}=\frac{A^{m}}{1-A}.

The result follows from the linearity of the
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator.

Method 2: Using the underlined notation for the contributing factor, we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{1-A\lambda}\cdot F(\lambda^{-1})=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \underline{\frac{1}{1-A\lambda}}\cdot F(\lambda^{-1})=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(A),

which completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
A\neq 1
be independent of
\lambda
. If
F(\lambda)
is a rational function in
\lambda
that admits a formal power series expansion in
\lambda
, then

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{1-\frac{A}{\lambda}}\cdot F(\lambda)=\frac{F(1)-A\cdot F(A)}{1-A}.

Proof.

We proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{1-\frac{A}{\lambda}}\cdot F(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(1)-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \underline{\frac{1}{1-\frac{A}{\lambda}}}\cdot F(\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(1)-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \underline{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-A}}\cdot F(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(1)-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \underline{\frac{A}{\lambda-A}}\cdot F(A)=\frac{1}{1-A}\cdot F(1)-\frac{A}{1-A}\cdot F(A).

Here, the second step rewrites the denominator, and the third step applies the operator to the underlined factor. The final expression yields the desired result.
∎

[

Let

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda x)(1-\frac{y}{\lambda^{s}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-x^{s}y)},

(10)

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda^{s}x)(1-\frac{y}{\lambda})}

(11)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-xyz}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-zy)},

(13)

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda x)(1-\lambda y)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda^{2}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+xyz-x^{2}yz-xy^{2}z}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-zx^{2})(1-zy^{2})},

\displaystyle=\frac{1+xy+xz+xyz}{(1-x)(1-xy^{2})(1-xz^{2})},

(15)

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda^{2}x)(1-\lambda y)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+xz-xyz-xyz^{2}}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-yz)(1-xz^{2})},

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda x)(1-\lambda y)(1-\lambda z)(1-\frac{w}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-xyw-xzw-yzw+xyzw+xyzw^{2}}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-z)(1-wx)(1-wy)(1-wz)},

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\lambda x)(1-\lambda y)(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})(1-\frac{w}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-xyz-xyw-xyzw+xy^{2}zw+x^{2}yzw}{(1-x)(1-y)(1-xz)(1-xw)(1-yz)(1-yw)}.

(18)

Proof.

Proposition 3.4

The following identity holds:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(1-A\lambda_{1})(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-AB)(1-CDE)}{(1-A)(1-B)(1-C)(1-D)(1-CE)(1-DE)}.

Proof.

The operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
acts to eliminate the variables
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
.
We proceed by first eliminating
\lambda_{1}
, followed by
\lambda_{2}
.
By accounting for the dual contribution in
\lambda_{1}
, we derive

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(1-A\lambda_{1})(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{2}}{(1-A)(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{2})(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}})}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(1-A\lambda_{1})(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})\underline{(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}})}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{2}}{(1-A)(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{2})(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}})}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{(1-ABE)\cdot\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}}}{(1-\frac{AE}{\lambda_{2}})(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-CE)(1-DE)(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}})}.

For the first term above, we consider the dual contribution in
\lambda_{2}
, while for the second term, we account for the contribution in
\lambda_{2}
. This yields

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}{(1-A\lambda_{1})(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2})(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-AB}{(1-A)(1-B)(1-C)(1-D)(1-E)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1-AB\lambda_{2}}{(1-A)(1-B\lambda_{2})(1-C\lambda_{2})(1-D\lambda_{2})\underline{(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}})}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{(1-ABE)\cdot\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}}}{(1-\frac{AE}{\lambda_{2}})\underline{(1-B\lambda_{2})}(1-CE)(1-DE)(1-\frac{E}{\lambda_{2}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-AB}{(1-A)(1-B)(1-C)(1-D)(1-E)}-\frac{(1-ABE)E}{(1-A)(1-BE)(1-CE)(1-DE)(1-E)}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\frac{(1-ABE)BE}{(1-ABE)(1-B)(1-CE)(1-DE)(1-BE)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1-AB)(1-CDE)}{(1-A)(1-B)(1-C)(1-D)(1-CE)(1-DE)}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

5.1
Han’s Formula

Han
[
17
]
developed a general algorithm for computing the MacMahon
\Omega_{\geq}
operator and implemented it in the
Maple
package
GenOmega
. Equation (
19
) below is a key result of this algorithm, and its proof occupies a significant portion
[
17
, Theorem 1.4]
. We now need only one step to prove this formula.

Let

A(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-x_{i}t),\quad B(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(1-y_{i}t)

be two polynomials.

Theorem 5.1
.

[
17
, Theorem 1.4]

Let
U(\lambda)
be a Laurent polynomial with
x_{i}\neq 1
and
x_{i}y_{j}\neq 1
for all
i,j
. If
\deg U(\lambda)\leq n-1
, then

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{U(\lambda)}{A(\lambda)B(1/\lambda)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{n-1}U(1/x_{i})}{(1-x_{i})B(x_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}(x_{i}-x_{j})}.

(19)

The formula holds even when the
y_{i}
’s are not all distinct.

Proof.

We compute as follows:

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{U(\lambda)}{A(\lambda)B(1/\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{U(\lambda)}{\underline{(1-x_{1}\lambda)(1-x_{2}\lambda)\cdots(1-x_{n}\lambda)}B(1/\lambda)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{U(1/x_{i})}{(1-x_{i})B(x_{i})\prod_{j\neq i}(1-x_{j}/x_{i})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.3
Two Dimensional Problem

Inspired by the work of
[
18
]
, Andrews, Paule, and Riese investigated the generating function for a two-dimensional problem:

S_{K,L}(x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 0;\\
Km\geq n;\\
Ln\geq m\end{subarray}}x^{m}y^{n},

where
K,L\geq 2
. We now illustrate the application of our method to this problem.

Theorem 5.4
.

[
7
, Section 2]

Let
K,L\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle S_{K,L}(x,y)=\frac{1+xy\frac{(1-x^{L})(1-y^{K})}{(1-x)(1-y)}-xy^{K}-x^{L}y}{(1-xy^{K})(1-x^{L}y)}.

Proof.

By the definition of the
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator and the geometric series summation, we have

S_{K,L}(x,y)=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\frac{1}{(1-\frac{x\lambda_{1}^{K}}{\lambda_{2}})(1-\frac{y\lambda_{2}^{L}}{\lambda_{1}})}.

To apply the
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
operator, we eliminate
\lambda_{1}
first, followed by
\lambda_{2}
.
Considering the dual contribution in
\lambda_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle S_{K,L}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\frac{x}{\lambda_{2}})(1-y\lambda_{2}^{L})}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\frac{x\lambda_{1}^{K}}{\lambda_{2}})\underline{(1-\frac{y\lambda_{2}^{L}}{\lambda_{1}})}}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-\frac{x}{\lambda_{2}})(1-y\lambda_{2}^{L})}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{y\lambda_{2}^{L}}{(1-xy^{K}\lambda_{2}^{LK-1})(1-y\lambda_{2}^{L})}.

The second term can be evaluated directly by setting
\lambda_{2}=1
. For the first term, we consider the dual contribution in
\lambda_{2}
, yielding

\displaystyle S_{K,L}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)}-\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{\underline{(1-\frac{x}{\lambda_{2}})}(1-y\lambda_{2}^{L})}-\frac{y}{(1-xy^{K})(1-y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-y)}-\frac{x}{(1-x)(1-yx^{L})}-\frac{y}{(1-xy^{K})(1-y)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+xy\frac{(1-x^{L})(1-y^{K})}{(1-x)(1-y)}-xy^{K}-x^{L}y}{(1-xy^{K})(1-x^{L}y)}.

This completes the proof.
∎

We begin by recalling the definition of the
q
-shifted factorial
. For a complex number
a
and a positive integer
n
, it is defined as

(a;q)_{0}=1,\quad(a;q)_{n}=(1-a)(1-aq)\cdots(1-aq^{n-1}).

In
[
19
]
, the computation of the following generating function is a difficult problem and has not been solved:

F(x,y,q)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq b_{0}\geq b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{k}\geq 0;\\
b_{0}\geq j\geq c_{1}\geq c_{2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}x^{i}y^{j}q^{b_{0}+b_{1}+\cdots+b_{k}+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}}.

In this subsection, we employ MacMahon’s partition analysis to derive an explicit formula for
F(x,y,q)
.

Theorem 5.5
.

With the notation as above, we have

\displaystyle F(x,y,q)=\frac{1}{(y;q)_{r+1}(x;q)_{k+2}}+\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}yq^{i(i+3)/2}}{(1-x)(1-yq^{i})(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{r-i}(xyq^{i+1};q)_{k+1}}.

Proof.

To establish this result, we calculate the following generating function:

G(x,y,t,z,q)=\sum_{i\geq b_{0}\geq b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{k}\geq 0;\atop b_{0}\geq j\geq c_{1}\geq c_{2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{r}\geq 0}x^{i}y^{j}t^{b_{0}}z^{b_{1}}q^{b_{2}+\cdots+b_{k}+c_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}}.

Note that
F(x,y,q)=G(x,y,q,q,q)
.
Let

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,t,q)=\sum_{i\geq b_{0}\geq j\geq c_{1}\geq c_{2}\geq\cdots\geq c_{r}\geq 0}x^{i}y^{j}t^{b_{0}}q^{c_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}}\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ G_{2}(z,q)=\sum_{b_{1}\geq\cdots\geq b_{k}\geq 0}z^{b_{1}}q^{b_{2}+\cdots+b_{k}}.

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,t,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-xt)(1-xyt)(1-xytq)\cdots(1-xytq^{r})},

\displaystyle G_{2}(z,q)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-z)(1-zq)\cdots(1-zq^{k-1})}.

By Definition
1.1
of the operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
, the generating function
G(x,y,t,z,q)
can be expressed as

G(x,y,t,z,q)=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ G_{1}(x,y,t\lambda,q)\cdot G_{2}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda},q\right).

Applying the operator step by step, we eliminate the variable
\lambda
and obtain:

\displaystyle G(x,y,t,z,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \frac{1}{(1-x)\underline{(1-xt\lambda)(1-xyt\lambda)(1-xytq\lambda)\cdots(1-xytq^{r}\lambda)}(1-\frac{z}{\lambda})(1-\frac{zq}{\lambda})\cdots(1-\frac{zq^{k-1}}{\lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-xt)(1-y)(1-yq)\cdots(1-yq^{r})(1-xzt)(1-xztq)\cdots(1-xztq^{k-1})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\frac{1}{y})(1-xyt)(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{r})(1-xyzt)(1-xyztq)\cdots(1-xyztq^{k-1})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\frac{1}{yq})(1-\frac{1}{q})(1-xytq)(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{r-1})(1-xyztq)(1-xyztq^{2})\cdots(1-xyztq^{k})}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\frac{1}{yq^{2}})(1-\frac{1}{q^{2}})(1-\frac{1}{q})(1-xytq^{2})(1-q)\cdots(1-q^{r-2})(1-xyztq^{2})\cdots(1-xyztq^{k+1})}

\displaystyle+\cdots

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\frac{1}{yq^{r}})(1-\frac{1}{q^{r}})(1-\frac{1}{q^{r-1}})\cdots(1-\frac{1}{q})(1-xyztq^{r})(1-xyztq^{r+1})\cdots(1-xyztq^{k+r-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-xt)(y;q)_{r+1}(xzt;q)_{k}}+\sum_{i=0}^{r}\frac{(-1)^{i+1}yq^{\frac{i(i+3)}{2}}}{(1-x)(1-yq^{i})(1-xytq^{i})(q;q)_{i}(q;q)_{r-i}(xyztq^{i};q)_{k}}.

Substituting
t=q
,
z=q
, and recalling that
F(x,y,q)=G(x,y,q,q,q)
, we arrive at the desired expression. This completes the proof.
∎

5.5
Two Generating Functions

Let
\mathbb{H}
be a ring, such as the ring
\mathbb{Z}
of integers. Consider the bivariate generating function

F(x,y)=\sum_{i\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{i}y^{j},\quad a_{ij}\in\mathbb{H}.

Define the generating functions
G_{1}(x,y,z)
and
G_{2}(x,z)
by

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,z):=\sum_{m\geq i\geq\ell\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{m}y^{\ell}z^{i+j},\ \ \ \ \ G_{2}(x,z):=\sum_{i\geq\ell\geq j\geq 0}a_{ij}x^{\ell}z^{i+j}.

As highlighted in
[
19
]
, these functions are of particular interest. We now derive their expressions via MacMahon’s partition analysis.

Theorem 5.6
.

Following the above notation, we have

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,z)=\frac{F(xz,yz)-yF(xyz,z)}{(1-x)(1-y)},\quad\quad G_{2}(x,z)=\frac{F(z,xz)-xF(xz,z)}{1-x}.

Proof.

By the definition of
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
and the geometric series summation, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \sum_{m,i,\ell,j\geq 0}a_{ij}\lambda_{1}^{m-i}\lambda_{2}^{i-\ell}\lambda_{3}^{\ell-j}x^{m}y^{\ell}z^{i+j}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{ij}\left(\frac{z\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\right)^{i}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{3}}\right)^{j}\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda_{1})(1-\frac{y\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}})}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(\frac{z\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{z}{\lambda_{3}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-x\lambda_{1})(1-\frac{y\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}})}.

The operator
\mathrm{\Omega}_{\geq}
eliminates the variables
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
, and
\lambda_{3}
. We first eliminate
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{3}
, then
\lambda_{2}
. Applying this procedure:

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(\frac{z\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}},\frac{z}{\lambda_{3}}\right)\frac{1}{\underline{(1-x\lambda_{1})}(1-\frac{y\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}})}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(xz\lambda_{2},\frac{z}{\lambda_{3}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-x)\underline{(1-\frac{y\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{2}})}}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(xz\lambda_{2},\frac{yz}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-x)(1-\frac{y}{\lambda_{2}})}.

Note that the power series expansion of
F(xz\lambda_{2},\frac{yz}{\lambda_{2}})
contains only non-negative powers of
\lambda_{2}
. Considering the dual contribution in
\lambda_{2}
, we derive

\displaystyle G_{1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(xz\lambda_{2},\frac{yz}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-x)\underline{(1-\frac{y}{\lambda_{2}})}}=\frac{F(xz,yz)}{(1-x)(1-y)}-\frac{\lambda_{2}\cdot F(xz\lambda_{2},\frac{yz}{\lambda_{2}})}{(1-x)\underline{(\lambda_{2}-y)}}

\displaystyle=\frac{F(xz,yz)-yF(xyz,z)}{(1-x)(1-y)}.

Similarly, for
G_{2}(x,z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle G_{2}(x,z)

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \sum_{i,\ell,j\geq 0}a_{ij}\lambda_{1}^{i-\ell}\lambda_{2}^{\ell-j}x^{\ell}z^{i+j}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ \sum_{i,j\geq 0}a_{ij}(z\lambda_{1})^{i}\left(\frac{z}{\lambda_{2}}\right)^{j}\frac{1}{(1-\frac{x\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}})}

\displaystyle=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(z\lambda_{1},\frac{z}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-\frac{x\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}})}=\underset{\geq}{\mathrm{\Omega}}\ F\left(z\lambda_{1},\frac{xz}{\lambda_{1}}\right)\frac{1}{(1-\frac{x}{\lambda_{1}})}

\displaystyle=\frac{F(z,xz)-xF(xz,z)}{1-x}.

This completes the proof.
∎

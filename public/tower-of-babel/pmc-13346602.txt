Observer-based dynamic gain control method for active vehicle suspension control systems under input delay and disturbance
Authors: Pengfei Zhang, Henghao You, Fengyi Gu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, the adaptive control problem is addressed for active vehicle suspension systems with the unknown nonlinearity as well as the unknown time-varying input delay. A dynamic gain control frame is proposed to suppress vehicle vibration and maintain the required suspension performance. Firstly, a dynamic gain extended state observer (ESO) is constructed to obtain the unknown velocity information and nonlinearity for the implementation of the control scheme. Moreover, a robust control method with a dynamic gain control term and a time delay compensation term is designed to mitigate the effect of unknown input time delay and uncertainty on system stability. By resorting to the Lyapunov–Krasovkii stability theory, the ultimate uniform boundedness of all signals in the closed-loop vehicle active suspension system is guaranteed. Finally, an example is presented as an illustration of the effectiveness of the proposed method.Keywords: Vehicle suspension systems, Unknown input delay, Adaptive control, Extended state observerSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Stability, comfort, and safety in the process of vehicle operation have always been highly concerned issues, and the most effective way to deal with this problem is to control the active vehicle suspension system so as to reduce the vibration of the vehicle body and obtain an excellent riding experience1,2. The active suspension system is an important structural component of the entire vehicle; therefore, it has received a great deal of attention from scholars and engineers. In the last decades, several control methods have been developed, such as control3, neuro-adaptive control4, fuzzy control5, and so on. However, given the complex structure of the active suspension system and external disturbances, how to design an excellent active suspension control strategy is still a hot topic of research.

In practice, it is well known that active suspension systems are difficult to achieve good control performance for the reason that there exist uncertainties on the complex pavement environment as well as mechanical structures6,7. In order to deal with this issue, many anti-disturbance control methods have been proposed8,9. For instance, sliding mode control based on a nonlinear disturbance observer for active suspension has been proposed to estimate and compensate uncertainties from various uncertain masses of passengers10. For strong disturbance action of vehicle active suspension system, a sliding mode control method and an optimised velocity approach law have been designed to ensure driving comfort11. In12, the fault-tolerant control strategy based on an adaptive robust observer has been proposed Markov-type active suspension system under faults and road disturbances. Inspired by the asymmetric structure of X in biological limbs, a prespecified time fault-tolerant control strategy has been proposed, which can effectively deal with system uncertainty, external disturbances and actuator failures in the case of low energy consumption13. In14, an adaptive neural networks fault-tolerant control method has been proposed for pneumatic active suspension. In order to estimate the unknown nonlinear term in pneumatic actuator dynamic, neural networks and fuzzy logic method are utilized as the nonlinear function approximator. For enhancing the robustness of active suspension system, an adaptive fuzzy fault-tolerant control method has been designed to deal with unknown actuator nonlinearities and external disturbances15. Literature16 has analyzed the suppression effect of various heuristic-based PID controllers on external disturbances in the active suspension system. In17, a robust and adaptive integral sliding mode-neural network controller has been designed for attenuation of the vibration response of vehicles traversing off-road terrains. It has to be mentioned that the above anti-disturbance methods proposed in the literature have to assume that the velocity state information is available, which will lead to the failure of the control algorithm in case of velocity sensor failure. Therefore, one of the motivations of this paper is to design an anti-disturbance controller to ensure the control accuracy under the consideration of unknown velocity.

One aspect of this studied in this paper is the input delay, which is usually caused by the generation of hysteresis in the actuator or sensor information for active suspension systems. It is well known that delay is one of the most important factors leading to system instability18,19. Therefore, many researchers have studied control systems with time delays and provided many interesting results20,21. For example, for a class of bioinspired active suspension systems with the constant input delay, a neural network finite-time adaptive controller based on a constructive predictor has been proposed to suppress vibration with much more improved control performance22. An unknown system dynamics estimator and predictor-based compensation control strategy have been designed to dampen body vibration and maintain the desired suspension performance for active suspension systems with the known time-varying delay23. A robust control strategy based on a Proportional-Integral control action, as well as a delay compensator, has been proposed for a class of uncertain nonlinear systems under unknown constant input delay24. An adaptive event-triggered control method based on neural network dynamic surface control and low-gain nonlinear observer has been proposed to achieve output-feedback consensus control of nonlinear multi-agent systems with known time-varying input delay25. For the semi-active suspension systems for vehicles with constant time delay, based on an improved Smith predictor, a Takagi-Sugeno fuzzy control strategy has been proposed to effectively reduce the impact of time delay on suspension systems26. The above study on input delays focuses on constant delays as well as known time-varying delays. However, input delay information is often unknown and time-varying due to, for example, actuator or sensor errors. Therefore, it is of theoretical interest to investigate control strategies for active suspension systems with unknown time-varying time delays. To the best of our knowledge, research into controlling active suspension systems when faced with unknown time-varying delays has been very scattered, which provides another motivation for writing this paper.

In view of the above, the control problem on the active vehicle suspension control systems subject to unknown input time delay and nonlinearities is considered in this paper. In concrete terms, the main contributions can be emphasised as follows:

(1) Compared to the traditional ESO, an dynamic gain ESO under the unknown time-varying input delay is designed to estimate the unknown velocity information as well as nonlinearity while mitigating the peak phenomenon caused by high gain; (2) compared with23, a dynamic gain control method including of the proportional-integral term and the time delay compensation term is proposed for active vehicle suspension systems with the unknown time-varying input delay; (3) under the unknown time-varying input delay, the sufficient conditions are provided to ensure that the dynamic gain ESO and controller are convergent as well as the active vehicle suspension control systems can be stabilized.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. The problem is formulated in Section II. In Section III, the stability conditions of the adaptive ESO and the dynamic gain controller are shown. In Section IV, an example was used to verify the effectiveness of the proposed method. Finally, the conclusion is given in Section V.

Remark 4

The impact of time-varying input delay on the active vehicle suspension system is addressed by introducing a Lyapunov-Krasovskii functional. The presence of time-varying input delay causes the delayed injection of control energy, leading to internal energy accumulation within the active vehicle suspension system, which can result in system instability. From a mathematical perspective, the sign of the cross term cannot be determined. The integral terms R(t), S(t), H(t), and G(t) are utilized to characterize the energy stored in the delayed channel, which can construct a quadratic integral term for the input delay.

By constructing a dynamic gain observer and state transformation, the active vehicle suspension system with the unknown input delay is transformed into a new system with the known input delay. Utilizing the new state , an integral term related to input delay is introduced to mitigate the delay impact on the system.

Conclusion

In this paper, the control problem has been investigated for vehicle active suspension systems with unknown nonlinearities and unknown time-varying input delays. Firstly, in order to estimate the unknown velocity and disturbance, a dynamic gain ESO has been proposed for the estimation of the velocity and nonlinearity under unknown input delay. Moreover, a compound delay compensation control strategy including a dynamic gain control term as well as a nonlinear compensation term is designed to attenuate the effects of input delays as well as vehicle body vibrations. By resorting to the Lyapunov–Krasovkii stability theory, the stability analysis on the vehicle active suspension system has been shown. Finally, an example has been presented as an illustration of the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The next research direction involves conducting experimental verification and exploring improvements to suspension system performance under random delay.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following Moser-Trudinger equations in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}=\lambda u_{\lambda}e^{u_{\lambda}^{2}},\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{2},\\
&u_{\lambda}\to 0,\ \text{when}\ \rho(x)\to\infty,\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{4}
and
\rho(x)=\ln\frac{1+|x|}{1-|x|}
denotes the geodesic distance between
x
and the origin in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
.
We will mainly study the quantization analysis of problem (
1.1
)
and its applications to the existence and uniqueness of critical points of the Moser-Trudinger functional. Quantization analysis of the Moser-Trudinger equations has attracted much attention due to its importance in applications to PDEs and geometric analysis. Let us first present a brief history of the main results in this direction.

Quantization analysis for elliptic equations with the Moser-Trudinger growth dates back to the work of Druet in
[
12
]
, who proved the following result:

Theorem A.

Let
u_{k}
be a family of solutions satisfying the equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{k}=\lambda_{k}u_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}},\quad\quad&x\in\Omega,\\
u_{k}>0,\quad\quad&x\in\Omega,\\
0<\lambda_{k}<\lambda_{1}(\Omega),\\
u_{k}=0,\quad\quad&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.2)

with
u_{k}
being bounded in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. If
u_{k}
blows up, then after passing to a subsequence, one has
\lambda_{k}\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
,
u_{k}\rightharpoonup u_{0}
in
W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
and there exists some integer
N
such that

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\|\nabla u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+4\pi N.

When
\Omega
is a disk, all positive solutions must be radially symmetric through the standard moving-plane method. (see e.g.
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
.) Applying the refined radial asymptotic expansions of
u_{k}
around the origin, Malchiodi and Martinazzi
[
36
]
proved that
u_{0}=0
and
N=1
.
For general bounded domains, Druet and Thizy
[
13
]
furthermore proved that
u_{0}=0
and
N
is equal to the number of concentration points. Further results concerning the existence of solutions
to elliptic equations (see
[
5
,
15
]
) or inequalities (see
[
22
,
32
,
33
,
40
]
)
in the hyperbolic space have been established.
The quantization analysis of the Moser-Trudinger equations in a bounded domain has been also extended by Chen, Lu, Xue and Zhu in
[
8
]
to a hyperbolic ball. Their results can be stated as follows:

Theorem B.

Assume that
u_{\lambda}
are a family of solutions satisfying

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}=\lambda u_{\lambda}e^{u_{\lambda}^{2}},\quad\quad&x\in B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R),\\
u_{\lambda}>0,\quad\quad&x\in B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R),\\
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)),\\
u_{\lambda}=0,\quad\quad&x\in\partial B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R),\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)
denotes the Poincaré disk centered at the origin with the geodesic radius equal to
R<\infty
,
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
,

\lambda_{1}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R))
is the first eigenvalue of the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
with the Dirichlet boundary in
B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)
. Then
u_{\lambda}
is radially symmetric and unique.
Denote by
dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx
the hyperbolic volume element and
\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\Big(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}\Big)^{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}
the gradient operator in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. When
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
, there holds

(i) if
\lambda_{0}=0
, then
u_{\lambda}
blows up at the origin, and
|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
,
\lambda u_{\lambda}^{2}e^{u_{\lambda}^{2}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
;

(ii) if
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\lambda_{1}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)))
, then
u_{\lambda}\rightarrow u_{0}
in
C^{2}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)))
and
u_{0}
is a positive radial solution of the equation

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{0}=\lambda_{0}u_{0}e^{u_{0}^{2}},\quad\quad&x\in B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R),\\
u_{0}>0,\quad\quad&x\in B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R),\\
u_{0}=0,\quad\quad&x\in\partial B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R);\end{cases}

(1.4)

(iii) if
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R))
, then
u_{\lambda}\rightarrow 0
in
C^{2}(B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R))
.

However, the above quantization analysis of the Moser-Trudinger equations in the whole Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
is still unknown. This is because the quantization analysis of the Moser-Trudinger equations in hyperbolic balls is based on the uniqueness of positive solutions to (
1.3
), which is not available for the corresponding equation in the Poincaré disk due to the presence of singularity of Hardy-potential near the boundary.
We are motivated to solve this problem. Our first main result states that

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
u_{k}\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
are a family of positive solutions satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}=\lambda_{k}u_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}},\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{2},\\
&u_{k}\to 0,\ \text{when}\ \rho(x)\to\infty,\\
&\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\leq M_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

where
0<\lambda_{k}<\frac{1}{4}
and
M_{0}>0
is a fixed large constant.
Assuming that
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{0}
, up to some Möbius transformation
T_{a}
(see 2.1 of Section 2) and some subsequence still denoted by
u_{k}
, we have

(i) if
\lambda_{0}=0
, when
k\rightarrow\infty
,
u_{k}
blows up at the origin,
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\rightarrow 4\pi
,
|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
and
\lambda_{k}u_{k}^{2}e^{u_{k}^{2}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
;

(ii) if
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, then
u_{k}\to u_{0}
in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
as
k\to\infty
, where
u_{0}
is a radially symmetric positive solution of the equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{0}=\lambda_{0}u_{0}e^{u_{0}^{2}},\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{2},\\
&u_{0}\to 0,\ \text{when}\ \rho(x)\to\infty;\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

(iii) if
\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{4}
, then
u_{k}\to 0
in
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
as
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Remark 1.2
.

From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we know that when
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{k}=0
, the blow-up behavior of
u_{k}
is uniquely determined. Hence, one needn’t pick subsequences and can directly derive
that the positive solutions
u_{\lambda}
of equation (
1.1
) must blow up at the origin as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
,
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\rightarrow 4\pi
,
|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
and
\lambda u_{\lambda}^{2}e^{u_{\lambda}^{2}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}.

It is very effective to apply the moving plane method or the method of moving spheres to prove the symmetry and uniqueness of solutions (see e.g.
[
6
,
7
,
29
]
). We first adopt the hyperbolic moving-plane method in integral forms initially developed by Li, Lu and Yang
[
26
]
(see also
[
4
,
24
,
25
]
) to show the hyperbolic symmetry of positive solutions (see Proposition 2.1). Up to some Möbius transformation, we can assume that positive solutions are radially decreasing.
Then, by employing the ODE theory and a fixed point argument, we obtain the blow-up behaviors of positive solutions (see Proposition
3.1
), which is helpful to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The key ingredient here is to derive a monotonicity formula (see Lemma
3.4
), which provides us with a crucial decay estimate away from the blow-up point so that we can establish the uniform boundedness of
\lambda_{k}c_{k}^{2}
, where
c_{k}=u_{k}(0)=\max\limits_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}(x)
.
In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we shall discuss three cases respectively. For the case
\lambda_{0}=0
, we first show that the Dirichlet energy has a positive lower bound as
k\to\infty
and then use a contradiction argument to establish that the positive solution must blow up at the origin. This together with Proposition
3.1
completes the study for the first case.
For the case
\lambda_{0}\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
, applying Proposition
3.1
again, we can derive the
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
-convergence by standard elliptic regularity theory. To obtain the convergence in
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
, we need to deal with the singularity of the Hardy-potential near the boundary, where a decay property for radial positive solutions is essential.
For the case
\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{4}
, we can also get the analogous
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
-convergence. The main difficulty here is to show that the limit of
u_{k}
in
C^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
is zero, since the first eigenvalue
\frac{1}{4}
of the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
with the Dirichlet boundary in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
cannot be achieved. To overcome this difficulty, we rely on the decay properties of entire positive solutions, which leads to the non-existence of positive solutions to (
1.1
) when
\lambda=\frac{1}{4}
(see Appendix B).

Exploiting the existence of critical points for the Moser-Trudinger functional
M(u)=\int_{\Omega}\big(e^{u^{2}}-1\big)dx
on the bounded domain under the constraint

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\gamma
for
\gamma>4\pi
has been a challenging problem. By a variational method and the monotonicity of
M(u)
, Struwe
[
42
]
proved that there exists
\gamma^{*}>4\pi
such that
M(u)
has at least two positive critical points for almost every
\gamma\in(4\pi,\gamma^{*})
. Lamm, Robert and Struwe
[
23
]
further introduced the Moser-Trudinger flow and strengthened it to every
\gamma\in(4\pi,\gamma^{*})
. del Pino, Musso and Ruf
[
10
]
characterized some of these critical points when
\gamma
is close to
4\pi
. When
\Omega
is a disk, Malchiodi and Martinazzi
[
36
]
applied a refined blow-up analysis for radial critical points of
M(u)
to derive that
M(u)
does not admit any positive critical point for
\gamma
sufficiently large. It is conjectured that if
\Omega
is a simply connected domain, the above non-existence result still holds. When
\Omega
is a finite hyperbolic ball, Chen, Lu, Xue and Zhu
[
8
]
established the multiplicity and non-existence of positive critical points for
\gamma>4\pi
. We also mention that recently De Marchis, Malchiodi, Martinazzi and Thizy
[
41
]
proved that when
\Omega
is a closed manifold, the Moser-Trudinger functional
M(u)=\int_{\Omega}\big(e^{u^{2}}-1\big)dV_{g}
under the constraint
\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla u|^{2}+|u|^{2}\big)dV_{g}=\gamma
always has a nontrivial positive critical point for any
\gamma>0
.

Theorem
1.1
will be used to investigate the existence of critical points for the Moser-Trudinger functional
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\big(e^{u^{2}}-1\big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}
under the constraint
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\gamma
for
\gamma>4\pi
. We have the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

There holds that the Moser-Trudinger functional

F(u):=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\big(e^{u^{2}}-1\big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}

under the constraint
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\Lambda
has at least one positive critical point for
\Lambda\in(4\pi,+\infty)
up to some Möbius transformation.

Our proof of Theorem
1.3
consists of two main steps.

Step 1.
Applying Theorem
1.1
and Remark
1.2
, we know that, up to a Möbius transformation, the positive solutions
u_{\lambda}
of (
1.5
) (with
\lambda_{k}
replaced by
\lambda
) blow up at
0
as
\lambda\to 0
and satisfy

\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=4\pi.

In this step we aim to prove that the Dirichlet energy approaches
4\pi
from above as
\lambda\to 0
. This will be achieved by expanding the solution near the origin up to order
o(c_{\lambda}^{-4})
(see Lemma
4.3
) and then estimating the energy deficit

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}-4\pi

more precisely (Lemma
4.5
) to determine the sign of the error term of order
c_{\lambda}^{-4}
, where
c_{\lambda}=u_{\lambda}(0)=\max_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}(x)
.

Step 2.
We then prove that

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\frac{1}{4}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=+\infty

for the nonzero least energy solution.
To establish this energy blow-up, we employ a simple contradiction argument combining the endpoint Poincaré–Sobolev inequality with Theorem
1.1
(iii), thereby avoiding any additional blow-up analysis.
In addition, using the continuous dependence of the least–energy branch on
\lambda
for
0<\lambda<1/4
, we can apply the intermediate value theorem. Combining the local result near
4\pi
(from Step 1) with this blow–up at
1/4
and the continuity, we conclude that for every
\Lambda>4\pi
the Moser–Trudinger functional
F(u)
under the constraint

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u|^{2}\,dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\Lambda

possesses at least one positive critical point.

Remark 1.4
.

Since the existence of extremals for the Moser-Trudinger inequality in the Poincaré disk is still unknown,
it is impossible to apply the perturbed method developed by Struwe in
[
42
]
to obtain the existence of critical points for the Moser-Trudinger functional under the constraint
\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=\Lambda
for
\Lambda
sufficiently close to
4\pi
. By a delicate expansion for the deficit
\big(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}-4\pi\big)
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, we overcome this difficulty.

Another application of Theorem
1.1
is the uniqueness of positive solutions to Moser-Trudinger equations, which is an important topic in partial differential equations and geometric analysis. It is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that the positive solutions of Moser-Trudinger equation (
1.1
)
with finite Dirichlet energy are unique up to some Möbius transformation for any
0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}
.

There have been extensive studies on the uniqueness of positive solutions to
elliptic equations with nonlinear polynomial growth
(
[
11
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
) and with exponential nonlinearities (
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
30
]
).
However, much less is known for the
uniqueness results for Moser-Trudinger equation (
1.1
).
In
[
35
]
, Luo, Pan and Peng
studied the uniqueness of the low energy solutions to (
1.1
)
on a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}.

To prove Theorem
1.5
, we mainly apply a contradiction argument and some local Pohozaev identities.
Specifically, we assume that there exist two solutions
u^{(1)}_{\lambda}(x)

and
u^{(2)}_{\lambda}(x)
of (
1.1
).
Note that we can show the
positive solutions of equation (
1.1
) are radially decreasing up to some Möbius transformation (see Proposition
2.1
). By the uniqueness of solutions to the Cauchy initial value problem for ODEs, we only need to prove
u^{(1)}_{\lambda}(0)=u^{(2)}_{\lambda}(0).

To this end, we wish to prove
u^{(1)}_{\lambda}(x)=u^{(2)}_{\lambda}(x)

in a Poincaré disk with a small radius and the center of the disk being at 0.

A pivotal and technically demanding ingredient in our uniqueness proof is the establishment of a precise asymptotic relationship between the parameter
\lambda
and the concentration level
c_{\lambda}:=u_{\lambda}(0)=\max\limits_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{\lambda}(x)
as
\lambda\to 0
(see Proposition
5.8
). This high-order characterization is fundamentally new and substantially transcends the approaches available in the existing literature. We develop a completely different approach based on precise pointwise estimates of solutions both near and away from the concentration point to establish the relationship between the parameter
\lambda
and
c_{\lambda}
. More precisely, we employ a progressive iteration scheme. First, we establish the preliminary relation
\lambda c_{\lambda}^{2}\to 1
(Proposition
5.1
) via a pointwise comparison with the Green’s function (Lemma
5.4
). Then we refine the expansion by proving that
c_{\lambda}^{2}(c_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}-4\pi G)
converges to a limit function
W
in
C^{1,\gamma}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{B}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
(Lemma
5.10
) and further prove that the higher-order residual
c_{\lambda}^{2}[c_{\lambda}^{2}(c_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}-4\pi G)-W]
also converges (Lemma
5.13
). These results provide, for the first time, a clear picture of how
c_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}
deviates from
4\pi G
on the whole Poincaré disk. In this process, the choice of the truncation radius
r_{\lambda}\widetilde{s_{\lambda}}
with
\widetilde{s_{\lambda}}^{2}=e^{(1-\sigma)c_{\lambda}^{2}}
plays an important role, as it allows us to separate the domain into a region where blow-up analysis applies and a region where the Moser-Trudinger inequality provides sufficient control. We point out that such a third-order expansion in Proposition
5.8
is not just a technical refinement but is truly necessary: the local Pohozaev identity used in the uniqueness proof (Proposition
6.6
) involves error terms that can only be controlled when
c_{\lambda}
is expanded up to the order
O(\lambda^{\frac{3}{2}})
.

Theorems

and

are proved in Sections

In Section

\lambda

in Section

.
Finally, we collect some previous results and basic preliminaries in Appendix

and give the decay estimates of positive solutions in Appendix

.

2.
Auxiliary facts

2.1.
Hyperbolic spaces and Möbius transformations

We introduce the ball model
\mathbb{B}^{n}
of the hyperbolic space. Denote
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by the Poincaré ball, which is a unit ball
B^{n}
equipped with the usual Poincaré metric
g=\big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\big)^{2}g_{e}
, where
g_{e}
represents
the standard Euclidean metric. The hyperbolic volume element can be written as
dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\big(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\big)^{n}dx
and the geodesic distance from the origin to
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
is given by
\rho(x)=\ln\frac{1+|x|}{1-|x|}
. Let
B_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(0,R)
denote the hyperbolic ball centered at the origin with the radius equal to
R
. The associated Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and the gradient operator
\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
are given respectively by

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{4}\Big((1-|x|^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}^{2}}+2(n-2)\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big),

and

\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\Big(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{2}\Big)^{2}\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

Denote by
W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
the first order Sobolev space in the Poincaré ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
, i,e. the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
under the norm
\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\cdot|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. In the Poincaré ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}u|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\geq\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{4}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|u|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}.

(2.1)

Under the polar coordinate system, the hyperbolic metric
g
can be decomposed into

g=d\rho^{2}+\sinh^{2}\rho d\sigma,

where
d\sigma
is the standard sphere metric. Then it is not difficult to check that

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
can be written as

\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\rho^{2}}+(n-1)\coth\rho\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}+\frac{1}{\sinh^{2}\rho}\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},\ \ \nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho},\ \frac{1}{\sinh\rho}\nabla_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\right),

respectively. We can also write

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}f(x)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}&=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{S^{n-1}}f(r\xi)r^{n-1}\Big(\frac{2}{1-r^{2}}\Big)^{n}d\xi dr\\
&=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}f\left(\tanh(\frac{\rho}{2})\xi\right)(\sinh\rho)^{n-1}d\xi d\rho\end{split}

(2.2)

under the the polar coordinate system.

For each
a\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
, we define the Möbius transformations
T_{a}
by (see
[
1
]
)

T_{a}(x)=\frac{|x-a|^{2}a-(1-|a|^{2})(x-a)}{1-2x\cdot a+|x|^{2}a^{2}},

(2.3)

where
x\cdot a
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is known that the volume element
dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
is invariant with respect to the Möbius transformations, which deduces that for any
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
,
there holds

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\varphi\circ\tau_{a}|dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}|\varphi|dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}.

Furthermore, the commutativity of Möbius transformations
T_{a}
with the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}
still holds. That is to say that for any
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{B}^{n})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(\phi\circ\tau_{a})(\phi\circ\tau_{a})dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\phi)\circ\tau_{a}\cdot(\phi\circ\tau_{a})dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}\phi\cdot\phi dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}.

Using the Möbius transformations, we can define the geodesic distance from
x
to
y
in
\mathbb{B}^{n}
as follows

\rho(x,y)=\rho(T_{x}(y))=\rho(T_{y}(x))=\ln\frac{1+|T_{y}(x)|}{1-|T_{y}(x)|}.

Also using the Möbius transformations again, we can define the convolution of measurable functions
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{B}^{n}
by (see
[
31
]
)

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}f(y)g(T_{x}(y))dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(y),

where
dV_{\mathbb{B}^{n}}(y)=\big(\frac{2}{1-|y|^{2}}\big)^{n}dy
.

2.2.
Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{B}^{2}

It is well known that for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u|^{2}-\lambda|u|^{2}\big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\geq C_{\lambda,q}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|u|^{q}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q}}

for any
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{4}
and
2\leq q<+\infty
. If we define
f=(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}-\lambda)^{\frac{1}{2}}u
, by duality, the above inequality is equivalent to

\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|f|^{q^{\prime}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}}\geq C_{\lambda,q}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|(-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}-\lambda)^{-\frac{1}{2}}f|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}

with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, which can be written as

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}f(x)G_{\lambda}(x,y)f(y)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(y)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(x)\leq C_{\lambda,q}^{-1}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|f|^{q^{\prime}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{q^{\prime}}},

where
G_{\lambda}(x,y)
is the Green’s function of the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}-\lambda
in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
.
Furthermore, we can also derive that for any
f
and
g\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
, there holds

\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}f(x)G_{\lambda}(x,y)g(y)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(y)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(x)\leq C_{\lambda,q}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}\|g\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}.

If we define
I_{\lambda}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)f(y)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(y)
, then it follows that

\|I_{\lambda}(f)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}\leq C_{\lambda,q}^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}.

2.3.
Totally geodesic lines of two-dimensional hyperboloid model

Let
\mathbb{R}^{2,1}=(\mathbb{R}^{2+1},g)
be the Minkowski space, where the metric

ds^{2}=dx_{1}^{2}+dx_{2}^{2}-dx_{3}^{2}.

The hyperboloid model of hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is the submanifold

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2,1}:x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}^{2}=-1,x_{3}>0\}.

The totally geodesic line
U_{x_{2}}
along
x_{2}
direction through the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be defined as

U_{x_{2}}=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}:x_{2}=0\}
. The general totally geodesic line along
x_{2}
direction can be generated through hyperbolic rotation. More precisely, define
A_{x_{2}}^{t}=Id_{\mathbb{R}^{1}}\bigotimes\widetilde{A}^{t}
, where
\widetilde{A}^{t}
is the hyperbolic rotation on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}^{t}=\bigg(\begin{array}[]{c}\cosh t,\ \sinh t\\
\sinh t,\ \cosh t\end{array}\bigg).

Then
\{U_{x_{2}}^{t}:=A_{x_{2}}^{t}(U_{x_{2}})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is a family of totally geodesic lines along
x_{2}
direction and they are pairwise disjoint and constitute the whole hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
. The totally geodesic line
U_{\nu}
along
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
direction through the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be defined as
U_{\nu}=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}:(x_{1},x_{2})\cdot\nu=0\}
. For any
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we can write
x^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}\cdot\nu)\nu+y^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
is orthogonal to
\nu
.
U_{\nu}^{t}=Id_{\nu^{\perp}}\bigotimes\widetilde{A}^{t}
. Simple calculation gives that

U_{\nu}^{t}=\left(\sinh tx_{3}\nu+y^{\prime},\cosh tx_{3}\right),\ \ (x^{\prime},x_{3})\in U_{\nu}.

It is easy to check that
\{U_{\nu}^{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are pairwise disjoint and also constitute the whole hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{2}
.

2.4.
Totally geodesic lines of Poincaré disk model and reflection

Let
\phi
be the isometric map from two-dimensional hyperboloid model to Poincaré disk. The
\phi
can be obtained by stereographic projection from the hyperboloid to the plane
\{x_{3}=0\}
taking the vertex from which to project to be
(0,0,-1)
.
In fact, we can write the map
\phi
as

\phi:x\in\mathbb{H}^{2}\mapsto\frac{x^{\prime}}{x_{3}+1}\in\mathbb{\mathbb{B}}^{2}
. Under the map
\phi
, by careful calculation, one can check that
the totally geodesic line
U_{\nu}
of hyperboloid
\mathbb{H}^{2}
becomes
\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}:x\cdot\nu=0\}
, which is also a geodesic line of Poincaré disk. Furthermore
\{\phi(U_{\nu}^{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
are pairwise disjoint and constitute Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. Let us recall Möbius transformations in the Poincaré disk. For each
a\in\mathbb{B}^{2}
, we define the Möbius transformations
T_{a}
by (see
[
1
]
)

T_{a}(x)=\frac{|x-a|^{2}a-(1-|a|^{2})(x-a)}{1-2x\cdot a+|x|^{2}a^{2}},

where
x\cdot a
denotes the scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. The Möbius transformation includes rotation and is isometric from
\mathbb{B}^{2}
to
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. It is well known that for any fixed
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
\{T_{a}\left(\phi(U_{\nu})\right)\}_{a\in\mathbb{B}^{2}}
generates all geodesic lines of Poincaré disk.

For the totally geodesic line
\phi(U_{x_{2}})=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{2}:x_{2}=0\}
along
x_{2}
direction in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
, it is easy to define the reflection
I_{x_{2}}
about
\phi(U_{x_{2}})
through
I_{x_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})=(x_{1},-x_{2})
for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{B}^{2}
. Obviously, through the geodesic line equation, one can easily verify that
I_{x_{2}}
maps the geodesic line to the geodesic line. One can similarly define the reflection
I_{\nu}
about
\phi(U_{\nu})
through
I_{\nu}(x_{1},x_{2})=-(x\cdot\nu)\nu+y

x=(x_{1},x_{2})=(x\cdot\nu)\nu+y
and

I_{\nu}(\phi(U_{\nu}))=\phi(U_{\nu})
.

Now, we start to define the reflection about the general totally geodesic line in
\mathbb{B}^{2}
. According to the definition of totally geodesic line, any totally geodesic line can be written as
T_{a}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))
for some
a>0
. The reflection
I_{x_{2}}^{a}
about
T_{a}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))
can be defined through

I_{x_{2}}^{a}(x)=T_{a}\circ I_{x_{2}}\circ T_{a}

since
T_{a}^{-1}=T_{a}

I_{x_{2}}^{a}\left(T_{a}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))\right)=T_{a}\circ I_{x_{2}}\circ T_{a}\left(T_{a}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))\right)=T_{a}\circ I_{x_{2}}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))=T_{a}(\phi(U_{x_{2}}))

2.5.
Symmetry for Moser-Trudinger equations in
\mathbb{B}^{2}

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
is a positive solution of equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u=\lambda ue^{u^{2}},\ x\in\mathbb{B}^{2},\\
&u\to 0,\ \text{when}\ \rho(x)\to\infty,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

where
0<\lambda<\frac{1}{4}
.
Then there exists
P\in\mathbb{B}^{2}
such that
u
is a constant on the geodesic sphere centered at
P
. Furthermore,
u(x)
is strictly decreasing about the geodesic distance
\rho(x)=d(x,P)
.

Proof.

By Green’s formula, we know that

u(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}},

where
G_{\lambda}(x,y)
is the Green’s function of the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}-\lambda
in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
(The Green’s function
G_{\lambda}(x,y)
is a decreasing function about
\rho(x,y)
, one can refer to Lu and Yang
[
34
]
for details). We only need to show the symmetry of positive solutions to this integral equation. We adopt the moving-plane method in integral forms of the hyperbolic space
developed by Li, Lu and Yang
[
26
]
. (see also
[
24
,
25
]
.) To perform the moving-plane arguments, we fix one specific direction
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
and for such direction consider
t>0
, then the hyperbolic hyperplane
\phi(U_{\nu}^{t})
splits the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
into two parts. We denote by
\Sigma_{t}=\bigcup_{s<t}\phi(U_{\nu}^{t})
and
u_{t}(x)=u(I^{t}_{\nu}(x))
.
Direct calculations give that for any
x\in\Sigma_{t}
, there hold

\begin{split}u(x)&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}\backslash\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(x,I_{t}(y))\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}u_{t}(x)&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}+\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}\backslash\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),I_{t}(y))\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}.\end{split}

Since
I_{\nu}^{t}
is an isometry, we have
G_{\lambda}(I_{\nu}^{t}(x),y)=G_{\lambda}(x,I_{\nu}^{t}(y))
and
G_{\lambda}(I_{\nu}^{t}(x),I_{\nu}^{t}(y))=G_{\lambda}(x,y)
. Hence we can derive that

\begin{split}u_{t}(x)-u(x)&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\Big(\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)-\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Sigma_{t}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\Big(\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)-\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(G_{\lambda}(x,y)-G_{\lambda}(I_{\nu}^{t}(x),y)\right)\Big(\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)-\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}.\\
\end{split}

(2.5)

Step 1. We compare the values of
u_{t}(x)
and
u(x)
.
We first show that for
t
sufficiently negative, there holds

u_{t}(x)\geq u(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t}.

(2.6)

Define
\Sigma_{t}^{u}=\{x\in\Sigma_{t}:\ u(x)>u_{t}(x)\}.

By (
2.5
) and the mean-value theorem, we derive

\begin{split}u(x)-u_{t}(x)&=\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left(G_{\lambda}(x,y)-G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\right)\Big(\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)-\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t}^{u}}\left(G_{\lambda}(x,y)-G_{\lambda}(I_{t}(x),y)\right)\Big(\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)-\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t}^{u}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\Big(\lambda u(e^{u^{2}}-1)-\lambda u_{t}(e^{u_{t}^{2}}-1)\Big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\
&\leq\int_{\Sigma_{t}^{u}}G_{\lambda}(x,y)\Big(\lambda(e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\Big)(u-u_{t})dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}.\end{split}

Through Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on the hyperbolic space, we derive that

\begin{split}\|u(x)-u_{t}(x)\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}&\leq C_{\lambda,q}^{-1}\|\Big(\lambda(e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\Big)(u-u_{t})\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}\\
&\lesssim\|\Big((e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\Big)\|_{L^{r}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}\|u-u_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})},\end{split}

(2.7)

where
2\leq q<+\infty
and
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{r}
. By Moser-Trudinger inequality in the Poincaré disk
\mathbb{B}^{2}
(see
[
39
]
and
[
LuTang
]
)

\sup_{u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2}),\ \|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\big(e^{4\pi u^{2}}-1\big)dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}<+\infty,

(2.8)

we know that
(e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)
is
L^{r}
integrable and
\|(e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\|_{L^{r}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}
is sufficiently small for sufficiently negative
t
. This deduces that
\Sigma_{t}^{u}
must be empty for sufficiently negative
t
. Then we accomplish the proof of Step 1.

Step 2. Inequality (
2.6
) provides a starting point to move the plane
\phi(U_{\nu}^{t})
. Define

t_{0}=\sup\{\ t:\ u_{s}(x)\geq u(x),\ s\leq t,\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{s}\}.

We will show that
u_{t_{0}}(x)=u(x)
for any
x\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}
. Obviously

u_{t_{0}}(x)\geq u(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}.

If there exists some point
x_{0}
such that
u_{t_{0}}(x_{0})>u(x_{0})
, then by (
2.5
), we get

u_{t_{0}}(x)>u(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}.

Next, we will show that the plane can be moved further to the right. More precisely, there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\epsilon)
, there holds

u_{t}(x)\geq u(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t}.

Let

\overline{\Sigma_{t_{0}}^{u}}=\{x\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}\ |\ u(x)\geq u_{t_{0}}(x)\}.

Obviously
\overline{\Sigma_{t_{0}}^{u}}
has the measure zero and
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow t_{0}}\Sigma_{t}^{u}\subseteq\overline{\Sigma_{t_{0}}^{u}}
. Then it follows that there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\epsilon)
, the integral
\|\big((e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\big)\|_{L^{r}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}
is sufficiently small. Recall that

\|u(x)-u_{t}(x)\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}\lesssim\|\big((e^{u^{2}}-1)(2u^{2}+1)\big)\|_{L^{r}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}\|u-u_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\Sigma_{t}^{u})}.

This deduces that for any
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\epsilon)
, there holds
u_{t}(x)\geq u(x),\ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t}
, which is a contradiction with the definition of
t_{0}
. Hence we conclude that

u_{t_{0}}(x)=u(x),\ \ \forall x\in\Sigma_{t_{0}}.

Step 3. Since the direction
\nu
is arbitrary, we conclude that there exists
P\in\mathbb{B}^{2}
such that
u(x)
is strictly decreasing about the geodesic distance
\rho(x)=d(x,P)
.
∎

3.
Quantization analysis of Moser-Trudinger equations

From Proposition
2.1
, we know that a positive solution to equation (
1.5
) is radially decreasing about the origin up to some Möbius transformation. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the positive solutions are radially decreasing.
In this section, we are devoted to investigating blow-up behaviors of equation (

[

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
u_{k}\subset W^{1,2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})
are a family of solutions satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}=\lambda_{k}u_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}},\ &x\in\mathbb{B}^{2},\\

&\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\leq M_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(3.1)

where

as

,
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\to 4\pi

|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}

In order to prove Proposition

) into the following equation in the unit ball

of Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by the conformal invariance between
(B_{1},dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}})
and
(B_{1},dx)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u_{k}=\lambda_{k}u_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2},&x&\in B_{1},\\
&u_{k}>0,&x&\in B_{1},\\
&u_{k}=0,&x&\in\partial B_{1}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(3.2)

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r_{k}>0
be such that
\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{2}c_{k}^{2}e^{c_{k}^{2}}=1

as

and

\eta_{k}(x):=c_{k}(u_{k}(r_{k}x)-c_{k})\to\eta_{0}(x):=-\ln(1+|x|^{2})\quad\text{in}\ C_{\rm{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),

(3.3)

and

\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{Rr_{k}}}\lambda_{k}u_{k}^{2}e^{u_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}4e^{2\eta_{0}}dx=4\pi.

(3.4)

Proof.

First, we claim that after passing to a subsequence,
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\geq 4\pi
. Assume by contradiction that
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}<4\pi

\int_{B_{1}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}dx<4\pi.

For any
\delta\in(0,1)
, applying Moser-Trudinger inequality and Sobolev inequality in bounded domains of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
respectively,
we get that

and
u_{k}
are bounded in

for any

. This deduces that

by Moser iteration techniques, which is a contradiction with
u_{k}(0)=c_{k}\to\infty
.

Recalling that
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\leq M_{0}
, so we can choose

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi}{(1-\delta^{2})^{2}}\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{Rr_{k}}^{\delta}\frac{1}{r\ln^{2}(r/r_{k})}dr=0.

On the other hand, since
u_{k}(1)=0
and
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\leq M_{0}
, we get by Hölder inequality that, for any
r\in[\delta,1)
,

\displaystyle u_{k}(r)=\int_{1}^{r}u_{k}^{\prime}(s)ds\leq{}

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{1}|u_{k}^{\prime}(s)|ds

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\left(\int_{r}^{1}2\pi s|u_{k}^{\prime}(s)|^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq M_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This infers that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}\lambda_{k}u_{k}^{2}e^{u_{k}^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx

(3.27)

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}(\delta)}\int_{B_{1}\backslash B_{\delta}}u_{k}^{2}\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}e^{u_{k}^{2}(\delta)}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}\backslash B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,\tilde{\delta})}u_{k}^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}4\lambda_{k}e^{\frac{M_{0}}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{\delta}}\int_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=0,

where
\tilde{\delta}
satisfies
\delta=\frac{e^{\tilde{\delta}}-1}{e^{\tilde{\delta}}+1}
.
Therefore, combining (
3.4
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) yields
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B}^{2})}^{2}\to 4\pi
,
|\nabla_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}u_{k}|^{2}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
and
\lambda_{k}u_{k}^{2}e^{u_{k}^{2}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\rightharpoonup 4\pi\delta_{0}
as

.
∎

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s_{\lambda}}}\left(-\Delta\eta_{0}-\frac{\eta_{0}\Delta\eta_{0}+\Delta w_{0}}{c_{\lambda}^{2}}-\frac{w_{0}\Delta\eta_{0}+\eta_{0}\Delta w_{0}+\Delta z_{0}}{c_{\lambda}^{4}}\right)dx+o(c_{\lambda}^{-5}).

Computing directly yields that

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle o(c_{\lambda}^{-2}),

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s_{\lambda}}}4e^{2\eta_{0}}(w_{0}+\eta_{0}^{2}+\eta_{0}^{3}+2w_{0}\eta_{0})dx+\int_{B_{s_{\lambda}}}(-\Delta z_{0})dx

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle-8\pi-\frac{2\pi^{3}}{3}+o(1)+2\pi\left(6+\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}\right)+o(1)=4\pi+o(1).

According to the above estimates, we obtain

\int_{B_{r_{\lambda}\widetilde{s_{\lambda}}}}\lambda c_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}e^{u^{2}_{\lambda}}dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}=4\pi+\frac{4\pi}{c_{\lambda}^{2}}+O\big(c_{\lambda}^{-4}\big).

Direct computation gives that

(5.44)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}c^{2}_{\lambda}J_{1}=4\pi\varphi(0).\end{split}

(5.45)

Since

5.4

5.1

\begin{split}{}&\lambda c_{\lambda}^{2}\int_{B_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}(0,R)\backslash B_{r_{\lambda}\widetilde{s_{\lambda}}}}(c_{\lambda}u_{\lambda}-4\pi G)e^{u_{\lambda}^{2}}\varphi dV_{\mathbb{B}^{2}}\\

(5.46)

with

(5.47)

with

Since it follows from Proposition

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u_{\lambda}^{(1)}
and
u_{\lambda}^{(2)}
be two positive solutions to equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda ue^{u^{2}}\left(\frac{2}{1-|x|^{2}}\right)^{2},&x&\in B_{1},\\
&u=0,&x&\in\partial B_{1}.\\

Then there exists
\lambda_{0}>0

u_{\lambda}^{(1)}(x)\equiv u_{\lambda}^{(2)}(x),~\mbox{for any}~~\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0}),x\in B_{\delta r_{\lambda}},

where

is a small fixed constant.

To prove Proposition
6.1

u_{\lambda}
from scaling and

Let
u_{\lambda}^{(1)}
and
u_{\lambda}^{(2)}
be two positive solutions to problem (
6.1
).

c_{\lambda}^{(l)}:=u^{(l)}_{\lambda}(0)=\max_{\overline{B_{\delta}}}u^{(l)}_{\lambda}(x)~\mbox{for some small fixed}~\delta>0

Now we have a basic result on
\frac{r^{(1)}_{\lambda}}{r^{(2)}_{\lambda}}.

Lemma 6.2
.

It holds that

\frac{r^{(1)}_{\lambda}}{r^{(2)}_{\lambda}}=1+o_{\lambda}\big(\lambda\big).

Proof.

Firstly, we have

\frac{r^{(1)}_{\lambda}}{r^{(2)}_{\lambda}}=\frac{c^{(2)}_{\lambda}}{c^{(1)}_{\lambda}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\big((c^{(1)}_{\lambda})^{2}-(c^{(2)}_{\lambda})^{2}\big)}.

(6.3)

\begin{split}\big(c^{(1)}_{\lambda}\big)^{2}-\big(c^{(2)}_{\lambda}\big)^{2}&=\big(c^{(1)}_{\lambda}+c^{(2)}_{\lambda}\big)\cdot\big(c^{(1)}_{\lambda}-c^{(2)}_{\lambda}\big)=o_{\lambda}\big(\lambda\big).\end{split}

.

(B.8)

(B.10)

Let

(B.11)

and

\begin{split}\hat{\psi}^{\prime}_{\lambda}-\nu^{-}(T)\hat{\psi}_{\lambda}&=u^{\prime\prime}_{\lambda}-(\nu^{+}(T)+\nu^{-}(T))u^{\prime}_{\lambda}+\nu^{+}(T)\nu^{-}(T)u_{\lambda}\\

(B.12)

B.11

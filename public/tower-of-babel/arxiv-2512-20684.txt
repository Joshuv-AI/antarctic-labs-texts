Remark on a determinant involving prime numbers
Huan Xiao

Abstract
. Pausinger recently investigated a special determinant involving prime numbers. In this short note we point out that this type of determinants was already known in linear algebra and its computation is unrelated to prime numbers.



Let
p_{n}
be the
n
th prime number. Recently Pausinger
[
2
]
investigated a special determinant involving prime numbers. It is the determinant of the
n\times n
matrix whose diagonal contains the first
n
prime numbers and all other elements are ones and thus

D_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}2&1&\cdots&1\\
1&3&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&p_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

It is the sequence A067549 of The On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences
[
1
]
and the first few values of
D_{n}
are
2,5,22,140,1448,17856
. Pausinger
[
2
]
relates this sequence to a
counting problem and obtains a recurrence relation on
D_{n}
, see
[
2
, Lemma 2]
.

Here in this short note we point out that this type of determinants like
D_{n}
was already known in linear algebra. Although at this moment I am unable to give a specific reference, the computation of
D_{n}
is standard and the value of it is well known. Indeed we have

Proposition 0.1
.

Let
a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}\neq 0
, then

d_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}1+a_{1}&1&\cdots&1\\
1&1+a_{2}&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&1+a_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}=\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{a_{k}}\right)a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}.

Proof.

This result is already known in linear algebra and for the completeness we give a proof here. Let
r_{i}
and
c_{i}
denote the
i
-th row and
i
-th column of the matrix in question. The first step is making the elementary transformations
r_{i}-r_{1}
for
i=2,\cdots,n
, then we have

(0.1)

d_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}1+a_{1}&1&\cdots&1\\
-a_{1}&a_{2}&\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
-a_{1}&&&a_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}.

The second step is making the following elementary transformations for
i=2,\cdots,n

c_{1}+\dfrac{a_{1}}{a_{i}}c_{i}.

The resulting determinant is

(0.2)

d_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}b&1&\cdots&1\\
0&a_{2}&\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\
0&&&a_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}

where

(0.3)

b=1+a_{1}+a_{1}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\dfrac{1}{a_{k}}=a_{1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{a_{k}}\right).

By (
0.2
) we have

(0.4)

d_{n}=\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{a_{k}}\right)a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{n}.

∎

Now taking
a_{k}=p_{k}-1
we have

Corollary 0.2
.

(0.5)

D_{n}=\begin{vmatrix}2&1&\cdots&1\\
1&3&\cdots&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&1&\cdots&p_{n}\\
\end{vmatrix}=\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dfrac{1}{p_{k}-1}\right)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(p_{k}-1\right).

The effective phase space and e e -folding of the Starobinsky and extended Starobinsky model of inflation
Theppawan Rukpakawong, Piyabut Burikham

Abstract
For zero spatial curvature, cosmological phase space of Starobinsky and extended Starobinsky inflationary model show three apparent attractors; the fixed angle attractor in the large field limit, the final attractor representing reheating phase in the small field region, and the apparent attractor corresponding to the slow-roll condition connecting between the large-field and small-field region. To consider the total e e -folding likelihood of the model, Remmen-Carroll conserved measure is constructed and normalized. Using the measure, the total e-folding number N N and its expectation value ⟨ N ⟩ \left\langle N\right\rangle are calculated. We found that ⟨ N ⟩ = 62.37 \left\langle N\right\rangle=62.37 for ultraviolet cutoff ϕ UV = 50 ​ M Pl ∗ \phi_{\rm UV}=50M^{*}_{\rm Pl} in the Starobinsky model. For extended Starobinsky model with additional R 3 R^{3} term parametrized by a coupling α \alpha , the expectation value shifted to lower values ⟨ N ⟩ = 4.03 , 61.4 \left\langle N\right\rangle=4.03,61.4 for α = 10 − 4 , 10 − 35 \alpha=10^{-4},10^{-35} respectively.

II
the effective phase space of Inflation

In the simplest model of inflation, we assume the universe is spatially homogeneous and isotropic. The accelerated expansion of the universe is driven by a single canonical scalar field. The action of scalar field coupled to general relativity on an expanding FLRW universe in the Einstein frame is
Burikham et al. 2024

S=\frac{c^{3}}{2\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}s\partial_{\nu}s-V(s)\right].

(1)

Henceforth, we will use the natural unit with
c=\hbar=1
. We also define
1/\kappa\equiv M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}
and the dimensionless scalar field
s\equiv\phi/M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}
. The action now can be written as

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{{M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}^{2}}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V(\phi)\right],

(2)

where
\phi
is an inflaton field. In Hamiltonian analysis, we adopt the Arnowitt-Deser-Misner (ADM) formalism to express the spacetime metric in a 3+1 decomposition. This involves slicing space into constant-time sections and using a lapse function to establish time-evolution
Pebralia et al. 2019
;
Corichi and Núñez 2023
. The FLRW metric with the lapse function can be written as
Remmen and Carroll 2014

ds^{2}=-N^{2}(t)dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right)

(3)

where
N(t)
is the lapse function and the curvature parameter
k\in\mathbb{R}
is fixed for a given FLRW universe. Taking
\phi(t)
as a function of time, the Hamiltonian is given by

\mathcal{H}=N\left[-\frac{p_{a}^{2}}{12a{M^{*}_{\rm Pl}}^{2}}+\frac{p_{\phi}^{2}}{2a^{3}}+a^{3}V(\phi)-3ak{M^{*}_{\rm Pl}}^{2}\right],

(4)

where
p_{a}=-6N^{-1}{M^{*}_{\rm Pl}}^{2}a\dot{a}
,
p_{\phi}=N^{-1}a^{3}\dot{\phi}
, and
p_{N}=0
since
N
is a Lagrange multiplier
Remmen and Carroll 2013
to only impose the constraint. After evaluating Hamilton’s equation with respect to
\phi
, we obtain the equation of motion of the scalar:

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+V^{\prime}(\phi)=0,

(5)

where the Hubble parameter
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
.
Furthermore, varying the action with respect to
N
gives the Hamiltonian constraint, which is equivalent to the Friedmann equation, describing how the inflaton field affects the expansion of our universe:

H^{2}=\frac{1}{3{M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)\right]-\frac{k}{a^{2}}.

(6)

Therefore, the vector field in
\phi-\dot{\phi}
space is

\mathbf{v}=\left(\dot{\phi},-3H\dot{\phi}-V^{\prime}(\phi)\right),

(7)

where
V^{\prime}(\phi)={\rm d}V/{\rm d}\phi
.

However, an analysis of the phase space on cosmic inflation using GHS measure
Gibbons et al. 1987
reveals that the measure diverges with factor
|\Omega_{k}|^{-5/2}
as the spatial curvature,
k
, approaches zero
Carroll and Tam 2010
. Nearly all trajectories correspond to a flat universe. Consequently, analysis of the cosmological phase space focuses on finding a measure in the space of a flat universe, when
k=0
; it leads to the scale factor,
a
, vanishing from the equation of motion (
5
) and (
6
). The equation of motion for inflation is then completely expressed by two variables:
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
.

In cosmology, it is often preferred to describe certain conditions or behaviors as
natural
. In the context of Hamiltonian analysis, this perspective is reflected in the concept of a cosmological
attractor
within phase space. Attractor behavior refers to scenarios where a group of points in phase space converge into a specific region and remain there indefinitely. This means that the evolution of trajectories reaches a certain behavior without requiring finely tuned initial conditions. However, for an attractor to exist, phase space volumes must contract in some regions. This contradicts the Liouville’s theorem on the Hamiltonian system (
4
) which states that the volume of a region in phase space remains constant over time, as discussed in
Gibbons and Turok 2008
;
Corichi and Karami 2011
;
Ashtekar and Sloan 2011
.

To reconcile these two common characteristics in the cosmological phase space: attractor and Liouville’s theorem, Remmen and Carroll
Remmen and Carroll 2013
proposed the formalism for defining the conserved measure on the effective phase space
\Phi
with non-canonical coordinates
\phi
and
\dot{\phi}
for universes with zero spatial curvature. This effective phase space can illustrate the appearance of certain attractor-like behavior by identifying it with the divergence of the measure and/or the negativity of Lyapunov exponents along certain coordinates. Furthermore, they found that the Liouville’s measure
{\rm d}\pi_{\phi}\wedge{\rm d}\phi
on effective phase space is just equivalent to constructing a two-form
f(\phi,\dot{\phi})\,{\rm d}\phi\wedge{\rm d}\dot{\phi}
conserved under Hamiltonian flows
1
1

1

defining the conjugate momentum on
\Phi
as
\pi_{\phi}\equiv\partial\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}/\partial\dot{\phi}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}
is a Lagrangian description of the trajectories on the effective phase space
\Phi
. Ref.
Remmen and Carroll 2013
also shows the condition for the existence of
\mathcal{L}_{\Phi}
.
.

II.1
The probability distribution of different FLRW trajectories for universes

Following Ref.
Remmen and Carroll 2014
, the formalism of effective phase space have been applied in a wide range of potentials to construct a conserved measure and probability distribution of trajectories on the Planck surface (
H=M^{*}_{\rm Pl}
). The
e
-folds average and the probability for particular total
e
-folds in a specific model of inflation can then be evaluated.

The effective phase space for a given potential
V(\phi)
is characterized by coordinates
(x,y)
, where
x,y
are reparametrizing functions of
\phi,\dot{\phi}
variables, respectively; and they can be expressed as

\displaystyle x(\phi)

\displaystyle\equiv\lambda\frac{\sqrt{V(\phi)}}{\sqrt{3}M^{*}_{\rm Pl}}

(8)

\displaystyle y(\dot{\phi})

\displaystyle\equiv\lambda\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\sqrt{6}M^{*}_{\rm Pl}},

(9)

where
\lambda
is defined as an auxiliary parameter with mass dimension
-1
. By the definition of
x,y
in Cartesian coordinates, we also can define polar coordinates
(z,\theta)
in the effective phase space as

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle\equiv\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}=\lambda H,

(10)

\displaystyle\tan\theta

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{y}{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\dot{\phi}}{\sqrt{V(\phi)}}.

(11)

Hence, Hubble parameter
H
is proportional to the radial component in polar coordinates. This setup clearly distinguishes the early universe
(H\gg 1)
from the late universe
(H\ll 1)
.

Considering Liouville’s theorem in this effective phase space, the conserved measure under evolution is a two-form

\boldsymbol{\omega}=\omega(x,y)\,dx\wedge dy,

(12)

which satisfies the conservation condition in context of the Lie derivative,
\pounds_{\mathbf{v}}\boldsymbol{\omega}=0
, where the vector field
\mathbf{v}
is defined as components
(\dot{x},\dot{y})
. Using the property of Lie derivation, we can equivalently write in component form as

\partial_{\mu}\left(\omega v^{\mu}\right)=0,

(13)

The conservation (
13
) will be used to evaluate the function of
\omega
in term of coordinates in the effective phase space.

The probability distribution on the space of trajectories, parametrized by the coordinate
\theta
with which the trajectory intersects the surface of constant
H
, can be expressed as
Remmen and Carroll 2014

P(\theta)|_{H}=\frac{\omega(H,\theta)|\dot{H}|}{\int\omega(H,\theta^{\prime})|\dot{H}|\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}},

(14)

where
\int\omega(H,\theta^{\prime})|\dot{H}|\mathrm{d}\theta^{\prime}
is a normalization factor. This is an essential formula for evaluating the average number of
e
-folds, the fraction of the universe having more than 60
e
-folds, and the probability of inflation resulting in a specific total number of
e
-folds.

II.2
The slow-roll inflation

One of the favored conditions in cosmology that is often considered natural is the slow-roll condition. In the well-defined potential of inflaton, slow-roll behavior
Faraoni 2000
corresponds to an apparent attractor in the effective phase space, where the conserved measure becomes significant. The slow-roll behavior of inflation is determined by two conditions on Eqn. (
5
) and (
6
):

\dot{\phi}^{2}\ll|V(\phi)|\quad\mathrm{and}\quad|\ddot{\phi}|\ll|H\dot{\phi}|,|V^{\prime}(\phi)|.

(15)

It implies that

H^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{3M^{*2}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}V(\phi)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad 3H\dot{\phi}\simeq-V^{\prime}(\phi),

(16)

respectively. With a given potential
V(\phi)
to construct the inflationary model, the slow-roll approximation typically emphasizes the necessity for the following parameters to be small
Liddle et al. 1994
:

\epsilon_{V}\equiv\frac{M^{*2}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}{2}\left[\frac{V^{\prime}(\phi)}{V(\phi)}\right]^{2},\quad\eta_{V}\equiv M^{*2}_{\mathrm{Pl}}\frac{V^{\prime\prime}(\phi)}{V(\phi)}.

(17)

The inflationary period ends when
|\epsilon_{V}|
and
|\eta_{V}|
reach unity, marking the transition to the reheating era
Bassett et al. 2006
. A widely used measure to quantify the total amount of inflation is the number of
e
-foldings, defined by

N\equiv\ln\frac{a_{f}}{a}=\int_{t}^{t_{f}}H{\rm d}t.

(18)

From those slow-roll conditions (
16
), one can obtain

H\mathrm{d}t\simeq\pm\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{V}}M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}.

(19)

We use this expression (
19
) to calculate the
e
-folding number on a given trajectory in the phase space.

(21)

:

1.

\omega
is finite everywhere except on the apparent attractor solution.

2.

At fixed
\theta
trajectories become more squeezed together as the radial component
z
decreases. It implies that
\omega
is inversely proportional to
z
, so
m\leq 9
.

3.

The measure
\omega
is infinitely differentiable everywhere except the apparent attractor solution, there is only one case that satisfies this condition which is
m=5
.

From these conditions, we then select
m=5
as the physical solution. The measure then becomes

\omega(\theta)\propto\frac{1}{z^{4/3}}\frac{(1-\sin\theta)^{2}}{\left|2-3\sin\theta\right|^{7/3}}.

(46)

The next step is to construct the probability distribution over the space of trajectories, parametrized by
\theta
on a surface of constant
H
, in this case, we choose the Planck surface (
H=M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}
). The probability distribution takes a form
\omega(H,\theta)|\dot{H}|
where
\dot{H}
is defined as

\dot{H}=-\frac{\dot{\phi}^{2}}{2M^{*2}_{\mathrm{Pl}}}=-\frac{3}{M^{*2}_{\mathrm{Pl}}\beta}z^{2}\sin^{2}\theta.

(47)

And the probability distribution on the space of trajectories on the Planck surface, where
z=M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}
, is given by

P(\theta)=C\frac{(1-\sin\theta)^{2}}{\left|2-3\sin\theta\right|^{7/3}}\sin^{2}\theta,

(48)

where
C
is a normalization factor.

In order to determine the normalization factor, we focus exclusively on the region within the UV cutoff that satisfies the slow-roll conditions, specifically for
\theta\in(\theta_{\mathrm{UV}},\theta_{\rm end})
, where
\theta_{\mathrm{UV}}
and
\theta_{\rm end}
are defined in Eqn. (
37
) and Eqn. (
38
), respectively.
By imposing
\cos\theta_{0}
,
\cos\theta_{\rm end}\ll 1
, we can simplify the probability distribution
P(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta
. In the interval of
(\theta_{0},\pi/2)
, we use
\sin\theta\simeq 1-\frac{1}{2}\cos^{2}\theta
and obtain

P_{1}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\simeq-\frac{C}{4}\cos^{4}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta.

(49)

In the interval of
(3\pi/2,2\pi-\theta_{0})

\sin\theta\simeq\frac{1}{2}\cos^{2}\theta-1
and obtain

P_{2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta\simeq-\frac{4C}{5^{7/3}}\left(\frac{3}{10}\cos^{2}\theta-1\right)\,\mathrm{d}\cos\theta.

(50)

The requirement that the total probability equals unity leads to the normalization,
\int P(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=1
, it implies that

\int_{\theta_{\mathrm{UV}}}^{\theta_{\rm end}}P_{1}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta+\int_{2\pi-\theta_{\rm end}}^{2\pi-\theta_{\mathrm{UV}}}P_{2}(\theta)\,\mathrm{d}\theta=1,

(51)

over the slow-roll region on the Planck surface. Using the approximation, we express the probability distribution as a function of

as follows:

\displaystyle P(u)

\displaystyle=C\left[\frac{1}{4}u^{4}\cos^{4}\theta_{0}\right.

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\left.-\frac{4}{5^{7/3}}\left(\frac{3}{10}u^{2}\cos^{2}\theta_{0}-1\right)\right]\cos\theta_{0}.

(52)

The normalization then becomes

\int_{u_{\rm end}}^{u_{\mathrm{UV}}}P(u)\,\mathrm{d}u=1,

(53)

and the normalization factor
C
is

\displaystyle C^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{20}\left(u^{5}_{\mathrm{UV}}-u^{5}_{\rm end}\right)\cos^{5}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}-\frac{4}{5^{7/3}}\frac{1}{10}\left(u^{3}_{\mathrm{UV}}-u^{3}_{\rm end}\right)\cos^{3}\theta_{0}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\frac{4}{5^{7/3}}\left(u_{\mathrm{UV}}-u_{\rm end}\right)\cos\theta_{0},

where

The extended Starobinsky Inflationary Model

IV.1
Determining the effective phase space

We now extend the Starobinsky model of inflation to include the higher-order terms in
R
. A simple modification of the Starobinsky model is by adding an
R^{3}

\left(\displaystyle{R+\frac{\beta}{2}R^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{3}R^{3}}\right)

n_{s}
as long as the
R^{3}
term is kept relatively small comparing to the dominating
R^{2}
term, so that
\gamma\ll\beta

Cheong et al. 2020
;
Burikham et al. 2024
;
Ivanov et al. 2022
. By Eqn. (
20

V(\phi)=V_{0}\left[1-\alpha(\sigma(s)-1)\right],

(61)

where

is the potential of the Starobinsky model (
22
) (for
\gamma=0
),

\alpha
is a perturbation parameter given by
\alpha=2\gamma/3\beta^{2}
, and

\sigma(s)
is defined in Eqn. (
21

s=\phi/M^{*}_{\mathrm{Pl}}

\alpha\to 0

As for the Starobinsky model, we define dimensionless Cartesian coordinates for the effective phase space:

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\left(1-\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)\sqrt{1-\alpha(\sigma-1)},

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{6}}\dot{\phi}.

(63)

Again, using Eqn. (

in Eqn. (

The Remmen-Carroll conserved phase space for flat universe has been constructed and explored in the Starobinsky and extended Starobinsky model of inflation. Under the slow-roll condition, the total
e
-folds along each trajectory is computed, the probability distribution of each inflationary trajectory with respect to the
e
-folding number is calculated using the slow-roll
e
-folds and the conserved measure. We find that the expectation value of
e
-folds
\langle N\rangle
of the Starobinsky model depends crucially on the UV field
\phi_{\rm UV}
where inflation starts, and it requires at least
\phi_{\rm UV}>50M^{*}_{\rm Pl}
for
\langle N\rangle>60
. Also
P(N>60)>0
requires
\phi_{\rm UV}>5.5M^{*}_{\rm Pl}
in the Starobinsky model. The probability distribution
P(N)
peaks at
N=0
resulting in the saturation of
P(N>60)=0.0251
for arbitrarily large
\phi_{\rm UV}
.

For extended Starobinsky model with additional
R^{3}
term parametrized by the coupling
\alpha
, the potential plateau where slow-roll occurs is truncated to
\phi<\phi_{\rm max}
as shown in Fig.
5
, resulting in a shorter period of inflation. For example, for
\alpha=10^{-4}
, the expectation value drops to
\langle N\rangle=4.03
while for
\alpha=10^{-35}
,
\langle N\rangle=61.4
converging to the corresponding value in the Starobinsky model. The probability
P(N>60)
is also less than the saturation value
P(N>60)({\rm Starosbinky})=0.0251
in the Starobinsky model for
\alpha>0
. It appears that for the extended Starobinsky model to solve the horizon problem, the
R^{3}
contribution must be extremely small compared to the
R^{2}
term.

As observed in Ref.
Remmen and Carroll 2014
for quadratic and cosine inflaton potentials, there are two apparent attractors in the Remmen-Carroll phase space, one is the slow-roll attractor at large field and another is the small-field reheating attractor. In both Starosbinsky and extended Starobinsky models, there are three apparent attractors found in the stream plot of velocity fields. For large field region, there is a fixed angle attractor at
\theta=\arcsin(2/3)
. For intermediate field, there is slow-roll attractor connecting between ultraviolet field region and the small-field region. In the small field region, there is apparent oscillatory attractor where reheating phase should begin as shown in Fig.
1
and Fig.
6
.

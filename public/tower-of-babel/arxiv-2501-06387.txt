Dispersive decay for the energy-critical nonlinear wave equation
Matthew Kowalski

Abstract
. We prove pointwise-in-time dispersive decay for solutions to the energy-critical nonlinear wave equation in spatial dimension d = 3 d=3 .



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we demonstrate dispersive decay for the
energy-critical nonlinear wave equation
:

(NLW)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u\pm u^{5}=0\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\\
\partial_{t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{cases}

where
u(t,x)
is a real-valued function on spacetime
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}
,
+
represents the defocusing equation, and
-
represents the focusing equation.

For dispersive models, our understanding of the long-time behavior of solutions is naturally tied to
dispersion
: the property that different frequencies propagate at different velocities.
In classical works, dispersion manifested primarily through pointwise-in-time dispersive decay; see e.g.
[
9
,
12
,
13
,
15
,
21
]
. For the linear half-wave equation, this is exemplified by the dispersive estimate

(1.1)

\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim|t|^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\||\nabla|^{(d+1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\quad\text{for }t\neq 0\text{ and }2\leq p<\infty.

For nonlinear models, consistent with the well-posedness theory at that time, these classical dispersive decay estimates required high-regularity initial data.
In contemporary works, dispersion has shifted to appear through Strichartz estimates and the resulting spacetime bounds; see e.g.
[
3
,
10
,
28
]
as well as Theorems
2.2
and
2.3
.

Given recent successes in scaling-critical well-posedness and scattering (see e.g. Theorem
1.1
), a string of authors
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
14
,
18
,
22
]
has returned to these pointwise-in-time estimates and sought to lower the regularity required for dispersive decay.
In
[
4
,
14
]
, these efforts successfully demonstrated dispersive decay for the mass-critical and energy-critical nonlinear Schrödinger equations under minimal hypotheses.
Subsequent progress has focused on the mass-critical gKdV equation in
[
22
]
.
For a full history of dispersive decay, we direct the interested reader to the aforementioned string of authors.

The (non)linear wave equation has a rich history of decay estimates which we do not attempt to fully recount here. For decay estimates of (
NLW
) on nonstationary spacetimes under stronger regularity and decay hypotheses, we direct the reader to the work of Shi-Zhuo Looi in
[
16
,
17
]
and references therein.
For work on dispersive estimates for various wave equations, we direct the interested reader to
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
16
,
19
,
21
]
and references therein.

Before stating our main theorem, let us briefly recall the well-posedness and scattering theory for this model.
The initial-value problem (
NLW
) is
energy-critical
: the scaling symmetry,
u(t,x)\mapsto\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda t,\lambda x)
, preserves the (conserved) energy

E(u,\partial_{t}u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\partial_{t}u|^{2}-\tfrac{1}{3}|u|^{6}dx,

as well as the
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
norm.
Global well-posedness in the energy-space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
was established for the defocusing equation by a culmination of many authors; see
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
23
,
24
,
27
]
and references therein. For the focusing equation, global well-posedness was established by Kenig and Merle in
[
10
]
.
For a history of well-posedness up to their respective times, see
[
25
,
26
]
.
For a recent installment, see
[
28
]
.

We recall the necessary well-posedness results in the following theorem
[
2
,
6
,
7
,
10
,
23
,
24
,
27
]
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let there exist
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In the focusing case, suppose that
(u_{0},u_{1})
satisfies

E(u_{0},u_{1})<E(W,0)\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}<\|W\|_{\dot{H}^{1}},

where

W(x)=\big(1+\tfrac{1}{3}|x|^{2}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

is a stationary solution to (
NLW
).
Then there exists a unique global solution
u\in C_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{3})
to (
NLW
) with initial data
(u_{0},u_{1})
which satisfies

(1.2)

\|u\|_{L_{t,x}^{8}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(E(u_{0},u_{1})).

Moreover, there exist scattering states
(u_{0}^{\pm},u_{1}^{\pm})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
such that

(1.3)

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\Big\|u(t)-\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}^{\pm}-\tfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}^{\pm}\Big\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0.

In the theorem to follow, we show dispersive decay analogous to (
1.1
) for solutions to (
NLW
) in the energy-space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
. We believe that this theorem is the optimal nonlinear analogue of the dispersive decay (
1.1
). Notably, it requires only that initial data lie in the energy-space
\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
, it exhibits a linear dependence on the initial data, and it has constants that depend only on the size of the initial data.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
2<p<\infty
. Given
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\big(\dot{H}^{2-\frac{4}{p},p^{\prime}}\times\dot{H}^{1-\frac{4}{p},p^{\prime}}\big)\cap(\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})(\mathbb{R}^{3})
which satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, let
u(t)
denote the corresponding global solution to (
NLW
). Then

(1.4)

\|u(t)\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}},p\big)|t|^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\Big[\big\||\nabla|^{2-\frac{4}{p}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\big\||\nabla|^{1-\frac{4}{p}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\Big],

uniformly for
t\neq 0
.
A stronger, Besov version of (
1.4
) is shown within the proof; see (
3.1
).

Absent from Theorem
1.2
is decay in
L^{\infty}
. This restriction arises from the high number of derivatives present in the right-hand side of (
1.6
) which prevents the use of Schur’s test to recombine frequencies; see (
3.8
) and the surrounding discussion.
If we restrict our attention to initial data in the stronger Besov space
\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}\times\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}
(see Definition
1.4
), we recover full dispersive decay in
L^{\infty}
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Given
(u_{0},u_{1})\in(\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}\times\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1})\cap(\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1})
which satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, let
u(t)
denote the corresponding global solution to (
NLW
). Then

(1.5)

\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big)|t|^{-1}\Big[\big\|u_{0}\big\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}}+\big\|u_{1}\big\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}}\Big],

uniformly for
t\neq 0
. A stronger version of (
1.5
) is shown within the proof; see (
4.1
).

Our interest in this particular model is to expand the methods presented in
[
4
,
14
]
and inspire continued work in this area.
In contrast to
[
4
,
14
]
, whose arguments are based on spacetime bounds in Lorentz spaces, we instead rely on frequency-localized estimates and careful summation.
These new methods are necessary to address the loss of derivatives in (
1.1
).

It is well known that the linear half-wave equation, and hence the linear wave equation, enjoys a dispersive decay in
L^{p}
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
of the form

(1.6)

\big\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}P_{N}f\big\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim N^{d(1-\frac{2}{p})}(1+|t|N)^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}},

uniformly for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
where
P_{N}
is a Littlewood–Paley projection (see (
1.8
)).
Due to the nonlinearity, frequency-localized decay of the form (
1.6
) is not possible for solutions to (
NLW
); indeed, this fails for any initial data with finite Fourier support. Instead, from the boundedness of the Littlewood–Paley square function (
1.10
) we immediately gain the linear dispersive decay (
1.1
), of which Theorem
1.2
is a nonlinear analogue.

Absent from (
1.1
), and correspondingly from Theorem
1.2
, is dispersive decay in
L^{\infty}
. Though present in (
1.6
), square function estimates are insufficient to recover
L^{\infty}
decay of the form (
1.1
). Instead, (
1.6
) and countable subadditivity imply the weaker result:

(1.7)

\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}f\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\infty,1}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{\frac{d+1}{2}}_{1,1}},

of which Theorem
1.3
is the nonlinear analogue.

Notation and Preliminaries

We use the standard notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate that
A\leq CB
for some universal constant
C>0
that may change from line to line. If both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
then we use the notation
A\sim B
. When the implied constant fails to be universal, the relevant dependencies will be indicated within the text or included as subscripts on the symbol.

We define the
Littlewood–Paley projections
as follows: Let
\varphi
denote a smooth bump function supported on
\{|\xi|\leq 2\}
such that
\varphi(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
. For dyadic numbers
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, we then define
P_{\leq N}
,
P_{>N}
, and
P_{N}
as

(1.8)

\begin{split}\widehat{P_{\leq N}f}&=\varphi(\xi/N)\widehat{f}(\xi)\\
\widehat{P_{>N}f}&=[1-\varphi(\xi/N)]\widehat{f}(\xi)\\
\widehat{P_{N}f}(\xi)&=[\varphi(\xi/N)-\varphi(2\xi/N)]\widehat{f}(\xi).\end{split}

We will often denote
P_{N}f=f_{N}
,
P_{\leq N}f=f_{\leq N}
and
P_{>N}f=f_{>N}
. When a function
f(t,x)
depends on both time and space, we let
f_{N}(t,x)
denote the Littlewood–Paley projection of
f
in only the spatial variable
x
.

As Fourier multipliers, the Littlewood–Paley projections commute with derivative operators and the half-wave propagator
e^{\pm it|\nabla|}
. Moreover, they are bounded on
L^{p}
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and on
\dot{H}^{s}
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. For
1<p<\infty
, we have that

\sum_{N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}}P_{N}f\to f\quad\text{in $L^{p}$}.

In addition,
P_{N}
,
P_{\leq N}
, and
P_{>N}
are bounded pointwise by a constant multiple of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function,

|P_{N}f|+|P_{\leq N}f|\lesssim Mf,

uniformly for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Associated to the Littlewood–Paley projections are the
Bernstein inequalities
, which state

(1.9)

\begin{split}\||\nabla|^{s}P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}&\sim N^{s}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\\
\||\nabla|^{s}P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{p}}&\lesssim N^{s}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}}\\
\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p}},\|P_{\leq N}f\|_{L^{p}}&\lesssim N^{d(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{q}}\end{split}

for all
s\geq 0
,
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
and uniformly for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Additionally, we have the Littlewood–Paley square function estimate which states

(1.10)

\big\|\|f_{N}\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}}\big\|_{L^{p}}\sim\|f\|_{L^{p}},

for
1<p<\infty
.

For Theorem
1.3
, it will be necessary to consider initial data in homogeneous Besov spaces. We recall the definition here:

Definition 1.4
.

Fix
d\geq 1
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. The homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}
is the completion of the Schwartz functions
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,q}}=\big\|N^{s}\|f_{N}(x)\|_{L^{p}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{q}(2^{\mathbb{Z}})}.

We note that the Bernstein inequalities (
1.9
) imply that the factor
N^{s}
acts as
|\nabla|^{s}
.

For our Besov analysis, it will be necessary to consider the Littlewood–Paley projection of the product of functions. We recall two simple paraproduct decompositions in the following lemma:

Lemma 1.5

(Paraproduct decompositions)
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{2}
. Then

(1.11)

\displaystyle|P_{N}(f^{5})|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}M\big[f_{N_{1}}f_{N_{2}}f_{N_{3}}f_{N_{4}}f_{N_{5}}\big]+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}M\big[f_{N_{1}}f_{N_{2}}f_{N_{3}}f_{N_{4}}f_{N_{5}}\big]

(1.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}M\big[f_{N_{1}}f_{N_{2}}f_{N_{3}}f_{N_{4}}f_{N_{5}}\big],

uniformly for
N\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
M
is the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function.

We use
L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(T\times X)
to denote the mixed Lebesgue spacetime norm

\|f\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(T\times X)}=\big\|\|f(t,x)\|_{L^{q}(X,dx)}\big\|_{L^{p}(T,dt)}=\bigg[\int_{T}\bigg(\int_{X}|f(t,x)|^{q}dx\bigg)^{p/q}dt\bigg]^{1/p}.

When
p=q
, we let
L^{p}_{t,x}=L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{p}
. When
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(T)=L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(T\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Similar to this notation, we use
\ell_{N}^{r}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(I\times T\times X)
to denote the mixed Besov spacetime norm

\|f\|_{\ell_{N}^{r}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(I\times T\times X)}=\Big\|\big\|\|f_{N}(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{q}(X,dx)}\big\|_{L_{t}^{p}(T,dt)}\Big\|_{\ell_{N}^{q}(I)}.

When
I=2^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
\ell_{N}^{r}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(T)=\ell_{N}^{r}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(2^{\mathbb{Z}}\times T\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We note that other sources may use the notation
\tilde{L}_{t}^{p}\dot{B}^{0}_{q,r}=\ell_{N}^{r}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}
; see e.g.
[
1
]
.

Motivated by the linear dispersive decay (
1.6
), we define
\langle x\rangle=1+|x|
.
With this, the linear dispersive decay (
1.6
) will be often rewritten as

\big\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}P_{N}f\big\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim N^{d(1-\frac{2}{p})}\langle tN\rangle^{-(d-1)(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|P_{N}f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}.

We note that
\langle x\rangle^{-\alpha}
is integrable over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
\alpha>d
.

2.
Spacetime Bounds

Central to our analysis will be estimates of solutions to (
NLW
) in various mixed spacetime norms. In the usual way, for a range of admissible exponents, control over these mixed spacetime norms will follow from the bound (
1.2
) and Strichartz estimates.

We begin by recalling the admissible exponents and Strichartz estimates.

Definition 2.1

(Wave-admissible)
.

We say that a pair
(p,q)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty)
is
wave-admissible
if

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{p}+\tfrac{1}{q}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}.

Theorem 2.2

(Strichartz estimates)
.

Suppose that
p,q
is a wave-admissible pair such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{1}{2}+\gamma
for some
\gamma
. Then for all spacetime slabs
J\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, the half-wave map satisfies

\|e^{\pm it|\nabla|}|\nabla|^{\gamma}f\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(J)}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Moreover, for all wave-admissible pairs
(\tilde{p},\tilde{q})
with
\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}+\frac{3}{\tilde{q}}=\frac{1}{2}+\tilde{\gamma}
for some
\tilde{\gamma}
,

\bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\partial_{x}^{3}}|\nabla|^{\gamma}F(s)ds\bigg\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}(J)}\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{-\tilde{\gamma}}F\big\|_{L_{t}^{\tilde{p}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\tilde{q}^{\prime}}}.

From the Strichartz estimates, a standard bootstrap argument extends the bound (
1.2
) to all wave-admissible pairs with a suitable number of derivatives. As the proof is standard, we do not recreate it here; instead, we direct the reader to
[
3
,
20
]
for examples of the argument applied to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For the Besov improvement, we similarly direct the reader to
[
14
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(Mixed spacetime bounds)
.

Given
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}
which satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, let
u(t)
denote the corresponding global solution to (
NLW
). Suppose that
p,q
is a wave-admissible pair such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=\frac{1}{2}+\gamma
for some
\gamma
. Then

\big\||\nabla|^{\gamma}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{\gamma}u\big\|_{\ell^{2}_{N}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\big).

Moreover, if
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}
, then

\big\||\nabla|^{\gamma}u\big\|_{\ell^{1}_{N}L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{q}}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We now prove Theorem
1.2
. This proof will follow the overall bootstrap structure of
[
4
,
14
]
. However, in contrast to
[
4
,
14
]
, we do not use Lorentz spaces to control the decay terms; instead, we use the frequency-localized dispersive decay (
1.6
) and careful summation.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

It suffices to work with
t>0
as
t<0
will follow from time-reversal symmetry. By the density of Schwartz functions in
(\dot{H}^{2-\frac{4}{p},p^{\prime}}\times\dot{H}^{1-\frac{4}{p},p^{\prime}})\cap(\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2})
, it suffices to consider Schwartz solutions of (
NLW
).

To take advantage of the additional decay in (
1.6
), we seek to prove the stronger decay result

(3.1)

\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\langle tN\rangle}{N}\big)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}u_{N}\Big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}([0,\infty))}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\big)\Big[\big\||\nabla|^{2-\frac{4}{p}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\big\||\nabla|^{1-\frac{4}{p}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\Big].

For
0<T\leq\infty
, we define the norm

\|u\|_{X(T)}=\Big\|\big(\tfrac{\langle tN\rangle}{N}\big)^{1-\frac{2}{p}}u_{N}\Big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}([0,T))}.

It then suffices to show

(3.2)

\|u\|_{X(\infty)}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\big)\Big[\big\||\nabla|^{2-\frac{4}{p}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\big\||\nabla|^{1-\frac{4}{p}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\Big],

for which we proceed with a bootstrap argument.

Let
\eta>0
denote a small parameter to be chosen later depending only on universal constants. A quick calculation shows that

(q,r)=\Big(\tfrac{3p^{2}}{3p-2},\tfrac{18p^{2}}{3p^{2}-6p+4}\Big)

is a wave-admissible pair with
\gamma=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}-\frac{1}{2}=0
. Proposition
2.3
then implies that we may decompose
[0,\infty)
into
J=J(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H^{1}}\times L^{2}},\eta)
many intervals
I_{j}=[T_{j-1},T_{j})
on which

(3.3)

\big\|u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}<\eta.

For all
j=1,\dots,J
, we aim to show the bootstrap statement:

(3.4)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}&\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{2-\frac{4}{p}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\big\||\nabla|^{1-\frac{4}{p}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+C(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H^{1}}\times L^{2}})\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}+\eta^{4}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}.\end{split}

Choosing
\eta>0
sufficiently small based on the constants in (
3.4
), we could then iterate over the norms
X(T_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,J(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H^{1}}\times L^{2}})
to yield (
3.2
) and conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We therefore focus our attention on (
3.4
). Recall the Duhamel formula:

(3.5)

\begin{split}u(t)&=\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}+\tfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}\mp\int_{0}^{t}\tfrac{\sin((t-s)|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u^{5}(s)ds\\
&=\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}+\tfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}+\text{NL}.\end{split}

By the linear dispersive decay (
1.6
) and the Littlewood-Paley square function estimate, the contribution of the linear term to
\|u(t)\|_{X(T_{j})}
is immediately seen to be acceptable:

(3.6)

\big\|\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}+\tfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}\big\|_{X(T_{j})}\lesssim\big\||\nabla|^{2-\frac{4}{p}}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\big\||\nabla|^{1-\frac{4}{p}}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}.

We thus focus on the nonlinear correction
NL
.

By the linear dispersive decay (
1.6
) and the Bernstein inequality (
1.9
), we may estimate

\begin{split}\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}&\lesssim\bigg\|N^{1-\frac{4}{p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\big\|P_{N}u^{5}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}ds\bigg\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\bigg\|N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\big\|P_{N}u^{5}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds\bigg\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}}.\end{split}

The
\ell^{1}_{N}\hookrightarrow\ell^{2}_{N}
embedding and the simple paraproduct decomposition (
1.12
) then imply that

\displaystyle\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\int_{0}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds.

We now decompose
[0,t)
into
[0,t/2)\cup[t/2,t)
. For
s\in[0,t/2)
, we note that
|t-s|\sim|t|
and for
s\in[t/2,t)
, we note that
|s|\sim|t|
. We may then estimate

\displaystyle\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\int_{0}^{t/2}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\int_{t/2}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\langle sN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds.

Applying Hölder’s inequality and taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T_{j})
, direct calculation then implies that

\displaystyle\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{1-\frac{1}{p}}\big\|\langle sN\rangle^{-(1-\frac{2}{p})}\big\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{p^{2}}{(p-1)(p-2)}}}\bigg\|\langle sN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\bigg\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}([0,T_{j}))}

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\bigg\|\langle sN\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\bigg\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}([0,T_{j}))}.

Bounding
\langle sN\rangle\lesssim\langle sN_{1}\rangle
and then summing over
\{N:N\lesssim N_{1}\}
, we find that

\begin{split}\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}&\lesssim\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}}N_{1}^{\frac{2}{p}-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\Big\|\langle sN_{1}\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\Big\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}([0,T_{j}))}\\
&=\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}}N_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\bigg\|\tfrac{\langle sN_{1}\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{N_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\bigg\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}([0,T_{j}))}.\end{split}

As
N_{1}\geq N_{2}
, this implies that

\displaystyle\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}}N_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\bigg\|\tfrac{\langle sN_{2}\rangle^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}{N_{2}^{1-\frac{2}{p}}}\|u_{N_{2}}\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\bigg\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}([0,T_{j}))}.

We now decompose
[0,T_{j})
into
[0,T_{j-1})\cup I_{j}
. We apply Hölder’s inequality to the sum over
\{N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}:N_{3}\leq N_{2}\leq N_{1}\}
in order to place
u_{N_{2}}(s)
into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j})
for
s\in I_{j}
and into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j-1})
for
s\in[0,T_{j-1})
. We may then estimate

(3.7)

\begin{split}\big\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}&\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\sum_{N_{5}\leq N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{1}}\log\big(\tfrac{N_{1}}{N_{3}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}(I_{j})}\\
&\hskip 11.0pt+\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\sum_{N_{5}\leq N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{1}}\log\big(\tfrac{N_{1}}{N_{3}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{1-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}\big\|_{L_{s}^{\frac{q}{3}}L_{x}^{p^{\prime}}}\\
&=\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\textup{I}+\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\textup{II}.\end{split}

We first focus our attention on
I
. The estimates for
II
will then follow a symmetric argument. By Hölder’s inequality, we may estimate

I

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{5}\leq N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{1}}\log\big(\tfrac{N_{1}}{N_{3}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\|N_{1}u_{N_{1}}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\prod_{i=3}^{5}\big\|u_{N_{i}}\big\|_{L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{\frac{6p}{p-2}}(I_{j})}.

Applying the Bernstein inequality (
1.9
) and then summing over
\{N_{4},N_{5}:N_{5}\leq N_{4}\leq N_{3}
, we find that

(3.8)

\begin{split}\textup{I}&\lesssim\sum_{N_{5}\leq N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{1}}\log\big(\tfrac{N_{1}}{N_{3}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\|N_{1}u_{N_{1}}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\prod_{i=3}^{5}N_{i}^{\frac{2}{3p^{2}}}\big\|u_{N_{i}}\big\|_{L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}\\
&\lesssim\big\|u\big\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{\infty}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}\sum_{N_{3}\leq N_{1}}\log\big(\tfrac{N_{1}}{N_{3}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}N_{3}^{\frac{2}{p^{2}}}N_{1}^{-\frac{2}{p^{2}}}\|N_{1}u_{N_{1}}\|_{L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\big\|u_{N_{3}}\big\|_{L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}.\end{split}

Because
p<\infty
, Schur’s test and standard arguments then imply that

(3.9)

\begin{split}\textup{I}&\;\lesssim\;\big\|u\big\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{\infty}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}\||\nabla|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\big\|u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I_{j})}\;\lesssim\;C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\big)\eta^{4}.\end{split}

Repeating the above analysis for
II
, we may similarly estimate

(3.10)

II

\displaystyle\;\lesssim\;\|u\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{\infty}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\big\||\nabla|u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{s}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\;\lesssim\;C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}\times L^{2}}\big).

Combining the estimates (
3.7
), (
3.9
), and (
3.10
) with the linear estimate (
3.6
) then yields the bootstrap statement (
3.4
). Along with earlier considerations, this concludes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We now prove Theorem
1.3
. This proof will parallel the proof of Theorem
1.2
but will require a more careful paraproduct decomposition.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

It suffices to work with
t>0
as
t<0
will follow from time-reversal symmetry. By the density of Schwartz functions in
(\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}\times\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1})\cap(\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1})
, it suffices to consider Schwartz solutions of (
NLW
).

We seek to prove the stronger decay result:

(4.1)

\Big\|\tfrac{\langle tN\rangle}{N}u_{N}\Big\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}([0,\infty))}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big)\Big[\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}}\Big].

Note that the sum over frequencies is
\ell^{1}_{N}
as opposed to
\ell_{N}^{2}
in (
3.1
).
For
0<T\leq\infty
, we then define the norm

\|u\|_{X(T)}=\Big\|\tfrac{\langle tN\rangle}{N}u_{N}\Big\|_{\ell_{N}^{2}L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p}([0,T))}.

It then suffices to show

(4.2)

\|u\|_{X(\infty)}\leq C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big)\big[\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}}\big],

for which we proceed with a bootstrap argument.

Let
\eta>0
denote a small parameter to be chosen later, depending only on universal constants and
\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}
. Proposition
2.3
implies that we may decompose
[0,\infty)
into
J=J(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}},\eta)
many intervals
I_{j}=[T_{j-1},T_{j})
on which

(4.3)

\big\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{t,x}(I_{j})}+\big\|u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{8}_{t,x}(I_{j})}+\big\|u\big\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{t}^{4}L_{x}^{12}(I_{j})}<\eta.

For all
j=1,\dots,J
, we aim to show the bootstrap statement:

(4.4)

\begin{split}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}}+C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big)\Big[\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}+\eta^{3}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\Big].\end{split}

Choosing
\eta>0
sufficiently small based on the constants in (
4.4
), we could then iterate over the norms
X(T_{j})
for
j=1,\dots,J(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}})
to yield (
4.2
) and conclude the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We therefore focus on (
4.4
). As before, we recall the Duhamel formula (
3.5
) and note that the contribution of the linear term to
\|u(t)\|_{X(T_{j})}
is acceptable:

(4.5)

\big\|\cos(t|\nabla|)u_{0}+\tfrac{\sin(t|\nabla|)}{|\nabla|}u_{1}\big\|_{X(T_{j})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{1,1}}+\|u_{1}\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{1,1}}.

We thus focus our attention on the nonlinear correction
NL
.

We decompose
[0,t)
into
[0,t/2)\cup[t/2,t)
. For the early time interval
[0,t/2)
, we apply the simple paraproduct decomposition (
1.12
) and then the linear dispersive decay (
1.6
). For the late time interval
[t/2,t)
, we apply the full paraproduct decomposition (
1.11
) and consider the two terms separately.
For
N_{1}\gtrsim N
, we apply (
1.6
) directly.
For
N_{1}\sim N
, we first apply the Bernstein inequality (
1.9
) and then apply the conservation of mass. Estimating in this manner, we may bound the nonlinear correction
\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}
by three terms,

\displaystyle\|\text{NL}\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle\int_{0}^{t/2}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-1}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle\int_{t/2}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-1}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\langle tN\rangle\int_{t/2}^{t}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\textup{I}+\textup{II}+\textup{III},

which we will treat separately.

We consider term
I
first. As
s\in[0,t/2)
, we note that
|t-s|\sim|t|
. Summing over
\{N:N\sim N_{1}\}
, then applying Hölder’s inequality, and then noting that
N_{5}\leq N_{4}
, we may estimate
I
as

I

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}}N_{1}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\int_{0}^{t/2}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{2}}\big\|u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L^{1}_{s}L_{x}^{2}([0,T_{j}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||\nabla|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\sum_{N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{2}}\Big\|\tfrac{\langle sN_{5}\rangle}{N_{5}}\tfrac{N_{4}}{\langle sN_{4}\rangle}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\Big\|_{L^{1}_{s}L_{x}^{2}([0,T_{j}))}.

We now decompose
[0,T_{j})
into
[0,T_{j-1})\cup I_{j}
. For
s\in[0,T_{j-1})
, we place
u_{N_{5}}
into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j-1})
. For
s\in I_{j}
, we instead place
u_{N_{5}}
into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j})
. Hölder’s inequality then implies

I

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\sum_{N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{2}}\big\|\tfrac{N_{4}}{\langle sN_{4}\rangle}\big\|_{L_{s}^{2}}\big\|u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s,x}(I_{j})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\sum_{N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{2}}\big\|\tfrac{N_{4}}{\langle sN_{4}\rangle}\big\|_{L_{s}^{2}}\big\|u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\sum_{N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{2}}N_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s,x}(I_{j})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\sum_{N_{4}\leq N_{3}\leq N_{2}}N_{4}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}(s)\big\|_{L^{2}_{s,x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{1/2}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}(I_{j})}\|u\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{8}_{s,x}(I_{j})}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.0pt+\|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{1/2}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}}\|u\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{8}_{s,x}}\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}.

Proposition
2.3
and the smallness condition (
4.3
) then imply that

(4.6)

\textup{I}\lesssim C\big(\|(u_{0},u_{1})\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,1}\times\dot{B}^{0}_{2,1}}\big)\Big[\eta^{3}\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}+\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\Big]

which is acceptable for the bootstrap statement (
4.4
).

We now consider term
II
. We note that for
s\in[t/2,t)
,
|s|\sim|t|
. Hölder’s inequality then implies that

II

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\int_{t/2}^{t}\langle(t-s)N\rangle^{-1}\langle sN\rangle\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N_{2}\gtrsim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|\langle sN\rangle\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\Big\|_{L_{s}^{2}([0,T_{j}))}.

\langle sN\rangle\lesssim\langle sN_{1}\rangle

\{N:N\lesssim N_{1}\}
. Noting that
N_{1}\sim N_{2}

N_{5}\leq N_{1}
, we may then estimate

II

[0,T_{j})
into
[0,T_{j-1})\cup I_{j}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality, we may place
u_{N_{5}}
into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j-1})
for
s\in[0,T_{j-1})
and into
X(T_{j})
for
s\in I_{j}
. Proposition
2.3
and (
4.3
) then imply that

(4.7)

\begin{split}\textup{II}&\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\||\nabla|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}}\|u\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{8}_{s,x}}\\
&\hskip 11.0pt+\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\||\nabla|u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}(I_{j})}\|u\|^{2}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{8}_{s,x}(I_{j})}\\

4.4
).

We finally consider
III
. Summing over
\{N:N\sim N_{1}\}
and noting that

III

\displaystyle\sim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N\\
N_{5}\leq\dots\leq N_{1}\end{subarray}}N_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T_{j})}\int_{t/2}^{t}\tfrac{\langle sN_{1}\rangle}{N_{1}}\big\|u_{N_{1}}u_{N_{2}}u_{N_{3}}u_{N_{4}}u_{N_{5}}(s)\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N_{1}\sim N\\

We now decompose

. By Hölder’s inequality, we may place
u_{N_{5}}
into the bootstrap norm
X(T_{j-1})
for

X(T_{j})

. We then find that

\begin{split}\textup{III}&\lesssim\|u\|_{X(T_{j-1})}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}}\|u\|^{3}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{4}L_{x}^{12}}+\|u\|_{X(T_{j})}\||\nabla|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{\ell_{N}^{1}L^{4}_{s,x}(I_{j})}\|u\|^{3}_{\ell_{N}^{1}L_{s}^{4}L_{x}^{12}(I_{j})}\\

4.4
).

), and (

). Along with earlier considerations, this concludes the proof of Theorem

.
∎

The existence of valuative interpolation
Shijie Bao, Qi’an Guan, Zhitong Mi, Zheng Yuan

Abstract
. In this article, using key tools including Zhou valuations, Tian functions and a convergence result for relative types, we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of valuative interpolations on the rings of germs of holomorphic functions and real analytic functions at the origin in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} and ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , respectively. For the cases of polynomial rings with complex and real coefficients, we establish separate necessary conditions and sufficient conditions, which become both necessary and sufficient when the intersection of the zero sets of the given polynomials is the set of the origin in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . Furthermore, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a valuation to be of the form given by a relative type with respect to a tame maximal weight. We demonstrate a result of Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson on quasimonomial valuations also holds for quasimonomial Zhou valuations. Finally, we obtain a relationship between Zhou valuations and the differentiable points of Tian functions.

13A18, 32B05, 14B05

1.
Introduction

Denote the ring of germs of holomorphic functions at the origin
o
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by
\mathcal{O}_{o}
, and
\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}
.
A
valuation
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
is a non-constant map
\nu\colon\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying the following:

(1)

\nu(fg)=\nu(f)+\nu(g)
;

(2)

\nu(f+g)\geq\min\{\nu(f),\nu(g)\}
;

(3)

\nu(c)=0
, where
c\not=0
is a constant function.

We set
\nu(0)=+\infty
for any valuation
\nu
. We call the valuation
\nu
is
centered
at
o
if
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\coloneqq\{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}\colon\nu(f)>0\}
coincides with the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathcal{O}_{o}
. Similarly, we can define the valuations on the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
.

A natural problem is how to determine the existence of valuative interpolation:

Question 1.1
.

Given any positive integer
m
, any finite elements
\{f_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
in
\mathcal{O}_{o}
(or in
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
), and any finite positive real numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
, can one find a valuation
\nu
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
(or on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
) such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for all
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
?

There are many examples where valuative interpolation fails to exist. For instance, there is no valuation
v
such that
\nu(z_{1})=1
,
\nu(z_{2})=2
, and
\nu(z_{1}+z_{2})=3
.

The valuation theory is closely related to the singularity theory in several complex variables and algebraic geometry (see e.g.
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
9
,
21
,
2
]
).
The concept of
Lelong number
is an analytic generalization of the algebraic notion of multiplicity (see
[
24
,
10
]
), which is defined to be

\nu(u,o):=\sup\big\{c\geq 0:u(z)\leq c\log|z|+O(1)\text{ near }o\big\},

where
u
is a plurisubharmonic function near
o
.
Replacing
\log|z|
by
\log\max_{1\leq j\leq n}|z_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}}
(
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{n}
) in the definition of Lelong numbers, the directional Lelong numbers
\nu_{a}(u,o)
(also known as
Kiselman numbers

[
23
]
) were introduced by Kiselman. Both the Lelong number and Kiselman number are used to measure the singularity of
u
at
o
(see
[
11
]
).
It is clear that Kiselman numbers induce a class of important valuations
\nu_{a}(\log|\cdot|,o)
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
(see
[
14
,
26
]
).

A plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
on a neighborhood of
o
is called a
maximal weight
, if
u(o)=-\infty
,
u
is locally bounded on
U\setminus\{o\}
and
(dd^{c}\varphi)^{n}=0
on
U\setminus\{o\}
(see
[
26
,
9
]
), where
U
is a neighborhood of
o
.

Motivated by the definition of Lelong numbers, Rashkovskii
[
26
]
introduced the
relative type

(1.1)

\sigma(u,\varphi)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\geq 0\colon u\leq c\varphi+O(1)\text{ near }o\big\}

for a plurisubharmonic function
u
with respect to a maximal weight
\varphi
, which also generalizes the Lelong numbers. Nevertheless, restricted to
\mathcal{O}_{o}
, the relative type
\sigma(\log|\cdot|,\varphi)
is not a valuation in general. In this paper, we simply use the notation of (
1.1
) and the name ”relative type” for any plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
near
o
, even if it is not maximal.

In
[
9
]
, Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson established that every quasimonomial valuation on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
can be expressed as a relative type with respect to a tame maximal weight. Furthermore, they established a fundamental result connecting Lelong numbers, multiplier ideal sheaves, relative types, and Demailly’s generalized Lelong numbers. In
[
2
]
, we introduced a class of tame maximal weights called Zhou weights and proved that the relative types with respect to them yield valuations (called Zhou valuations) on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
. We then used these valuations to
measure the singularities of plurisubharmonic functions.

In this article, we study the valuative interpolation problem on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
(see
1.1
) by employing Zhou valuations, Tian functions, and several results on relative types. Here, a Tian function is defined as the function of jumping numbers with respect to the exponents of a holomorphic function or the multiples of a plurisubharmonic function.

Recall that Kiselman numbers (also known as directional Lelong numbers) induce a class of valuations. Both Zhou valuations (see
[
2
]
) and quasimonomial valuations (see
[
9
]
) can be expressed as relative types with respect to certain tame maximal weights (for the definition of ”tame”, see
Section

1.2
). We discuss the relationship between valuations and relative types, and obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for a valuation to be represented by a relative type with respect to a tame maximal weight.

Finally, we show that a result of Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson on quasimonomial valuations
[
9
]
also holds for quasimonomial Zhou valuations, and we establish a relationship between Zhou valuations and the differentiable points of Tian functions.

1.1.
Valuative interpolation

The following theorem establishes a criterion for the valuative interpolation problem on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
m+1
holomorphic functions
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
near
o
,
a_{0}=0
and
m
positive numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
,
the following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a valuation
\nu
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
;

(2)

\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
, where

F\coloneqq\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}f_{j},\quad\varphi\coloneqq\log\Big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}|f_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}}\Big).

Furthermore, if
o
is an isolated point in
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}
and
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
, then there exists a valuation
\nu
and a tame maximal weight
\varphi_{\nu}
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
j
and
\nu(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{\nu})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Note that
\nu(f_{1}f_{2})=0

\Leftrightarrow

\nu(f_{1})=0
and
\nu(f_{2})=0
. Therefore, in the theorem above, it suffices to consider the case where exactly one function
f_{0}
satisfies
\nu(f_{0})=0
. Since
\nu(1)=0
for any valuation
\nu
, in the above theorem, we can actually ignore
f_{0}
when
f_{0}\equiv 1
. This convention allows the case where all interpolated values are nonzero to be naturally incorporated into our framework.

We give some corollaries of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\{f_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
be
n
holomorphic functions near
o
with
\cap_{1\leq j\leq n}\{f_{j}=0\}=\{o\}
. Then for any positive numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
, there exists a valuation
\nu
and a tame weight
\varphi_{\nu}
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
j
and
\nu(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{\nu})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\nu
be a valuation on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
centered at
o
. Then for any finite collection of holomorphic functions
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\}
near
o
with
f_{j}(o)=0
for all
j
, there exists a valuation
\tilde{\nu}
and a tame maximal weight
\varphi_{\tilde{\nu}}
such that
\tilde{\nu}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{\tilde{\nu}})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
, and

\nu(f_{j})=\tilde{\nu}(f_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Let us consider the valuative interpolation problem on the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}\subset\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
,
a_{0}=0
and
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
be
m
positive numbers. Denote
F\coloneqq\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}f_{j}
and
\varphi\coloneqq\log(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}|f_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}})
.

Corollary 1.5
.

If
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
, then there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
.

Conversely, if there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
satisfying that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
and
\nu(z_{l})>0
for every
1\leq l\leq n
, then we have
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
.

When
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}=\{o\}
,
1.5
gives a necessary and sufficient condition for the existence of valuative interpolations on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
.

Corollary 1.6
.

If
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}=\{o\}
, then there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
j
if and only if
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
.

The following example shows that the condition “
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}=\{o\}
” can not be removed.

Example 1.7
.

Let
h_{1}=z_{1},

h_{2}=z_{2}
,
h_{3}=z_{1}z_{2}
,
g_{1}=z_{1}-1
and
g_{2}=z_{2}-1
on
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Take
\{f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},f_{4},f_{5},f_{6}\}=\{h_{1}g_{1},h_{1}g_{2},h_{2}g_{1},h_{2}g_{2},h_{3}g_{1},h_{3}g_{2}\}
and
a_{1}=\ldots=a_{6}=1
. It is clear that

\cap_{1\leq l\leq 6}\{f_{l}=0\}=\{o,(1,1)\}.

As
F=\prod_{1\leq l\leq 6}f_{l}=3\log(|z_{1}-1||z_{2}-1|)+O(1)
near
(1,1)
and
\varphi=\log(\sum_{1\leq l\leq 6}|f_{l}|^{\frac{1}{a_{l}}})=\log(|z_{1}-1|+|z_{2}-1|)+O(1)
near
(1,1)
, we have

\sup\{c\colon\log|F|\leq\varphi+O(1)\text{ near $(1,1)$}\}=6=\sum_{1\leq l\leq 6}a_{l}.

By
1.5
, there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]
such that
\nu(f_{l})=a_{l}
for every
1\leq l\leq 6
. But we have

\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)>6=\sum_{1\leq l\leq 6}a_{l}

since
F=4\log|z_{1}z_{2}|+O(1)
near
o
and
\varphi=\log(|z_{1}|+|z_{2}|+|z_{1}z_{2}|)+O(1)
near
o
. Thus, the condition “
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}=\{o\}
” in
1.6
can not be removed.

1.1.1.
Applications on real cases

Denote the set of all germs of real analytic functions near the origin
o^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
C^{\omega}_{o^{\prime}}
.

There exists an injective ring homomorphism
P\colon C^{\omega}_{o^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{o}
such that

P\Big(\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\Big)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{\alpha}z^{\alpha},

where
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
are the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
respectively, and
\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}}a_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
is the power series expansion of an arbitrarily given real analytic function near
o^{\prime}
. It is clear that, for any
(h,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
, there exists a unique pair of real analytic functions
(h_{1},h_{2})
near
o^{\prime}
such that

h=P(h_{1})+\mathrm{i}P(h_{2})\quad\ \text{near}\ o.

In the following, we discuss the valuative interpolation problem for the real cases.

Corollary 1.8
.

Given
m+1
real analytic functions
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
near
o^{\prime}
,
a_{0}=0
and
m
positive numbers
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
,
the following two statements are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a valuation
\nu
on
C^{\omega}_{o^{\prime}}
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
;

(2)

\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
, where

F\coloneqq\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}P(f_{j}),\quad\varphi\coloneqq\log\Big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}|P(f_{j})|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}}\Big).

Remark 1.9
.

Theorem
1.2
and Corollary
1.8
show that for any valuation
\nu
on
C^{\omega}_{o^{\prime}}
and finite collection of real analytic functions
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}
near
o^{\prime}
, there exists a valuation
\tilde{\nu}
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
such that
\tilde{\nu}(P(f_{j}))=\nu(f_{j})
for all
j
.

Denote the restriction of
P
to
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
also by
P
.
Let
\{f_{j}\}_{0\leq j\leq m}\subset\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
a_{0}=0
and
\{a_{j}\}_{1\leq j\leq m}
be
m
positive numbers. Set
F\coloneqq\prod_{0\leq j\leq m}P(f_{j})
and
\varphi\coloneqq\log(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}|P(f_{j})|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}})
.

Corollary 1.10
.

If
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
, then there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
.

Conversely, if there exists a valuation
\nu
on
\mathbb{R}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
satisfying that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for every
0\leq j\leq m
and
\nu(x_{l})>0
for every
1\leq l\leq n
, then we have
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
.

1.2.
Valuation and relative type

We recall that a plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
near
o
is called a
tame weight
(see
[
9
]
), if
\varphi
has an isolated singularity at
o
,
e^{\varphi}
is continuous, and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(1.2)

c_{o}^{f}(\varphi)\leq\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)+C\quad\forall\,(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}.

Equivalently, the inequality in (
1.2
) requires that for any
t>0
and every
(f,o)\in\mathcal{I}(t\varphi)_{o}
, we have
\log|f|\leq(t-C)\varphi+O(1)
near
o
. Here

c_{o}^{f}(\varphi)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\geq 0\colon|f|^{2}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}

denotes the
jumping number
(see
[
21
,
22
]
), and

\mathcal{I}(t\varphi)_{o}\coloneqq\big\{(g,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\colon|g|^{2}e^{-2t\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}

is the
multiplier ideal
.

As shown in previous works, both Zhou valuations (
[
2
]
) and quasimonomial valuations (
[
9
]
) can be realized as relative types with respect to some tame maximal weight. These facts motivate the following question:

Can one characterize which valuations on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
are induced by a tame maximal weight? More precisely, for a given valuation
\nu
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
centered at
o
, what is a necessary and sufficient condition for
\nu
to be represented as the relative type with respect to a tame maximal weight near
o
?

In this paper, we provide a complete answer to this question.

Theorem 1.11
.

A valuation
\nu
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
centered at
o
satisfies
\nu(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi\nu)
for some tame maximal weight
\varphi_{\nu}
if and only if
\sup_{(f,o)}\big(\lct^{(f)}(\mathfrak{a}^{\nu}_{\bullet})-\nu(f)\big)<+\infty
. Moreover, when this holds, the representing weight
\varphi_{\nu}
satisfies
c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{\nu})=\lct^{(f)}(\mathfrak{a}^{\nu}_{\bullet})
for all
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}
.

It is worthy of noting that
\sup_{(f,o)}\big(\lct^{(f)}(\mathfrak{a}^{\nu}_{\bullet})-\nu(f)\big)
coincides with the notion
\mathscr{A}(\nu)
defined in
[
3
]
, and the
log discrepancy
(see
[
21
]
)
A(\nu)
of
\nu
satisfies
A(\nu)\geq\mathscr{A}(\nu)
. Therefore, the log discrepancy
A(\nu)<+\infty
implies
\sup_{(f,o)}\big(\lct^{(f)}(\mathfrak{a}^{\nu}_{\bullet})-\nu(f)\big)<+\infty
.

1.3.
Zhou valuation and quasimonomial valuation

Let
f_{0}=(f_{0,1},\cdots,f_{0,m})
be a vector,
where
f_{0,1},\cdots,f_{0,m}
are holomorphic functions near
o
.
Denote by
|f_{0}|^{2}=|f_{0,1}|^{2}+\cdots+|f_{0,m}|^{2}
.
Let
\varphi_{0}
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
,
such that
|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}
is integrable near
o
.

Definition 1.12

(
[
2
]
)
.

We call a plurisubharmonic function
\Phi^{f_{0},\varphi_{0}}_{o,\max}
(
\Phi_{o,\max}
for short) near
o
a
local Zhou weight related to
|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}
near
o
,
if the following three statements hold:

(1)

|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}|z|^{2N_{0}}e^{-2\Phi_{o,\max}}
is integrable near
o
for large enough
N_{0}\gg 0
;

(2)

|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{-2\Phi_{o,\max}}
is not integrable near
o
;

(3)

for any plurisubharmonic function
\varphi^{\prime}\geq\Phi_{o,\max}+O(1)
near
o
such that
|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{-2\varphi^{\prime}}
is not integrable near
o
,
\varphi^{\prime}=\Phi_{o,\max}+O(1)
holds.

The existence of local Zhou weights follows from the strong openness property of multiplier ideal sheaves (see
[
2
]
or Remark
2.22
).

Let us recall some properties of Zhou weights (see
[
2
]
). For a local Zhou weight
\Phi_{o,\max}
, we have
\Phi_{o,\max}\geq N\log|z|
near
o
for some
N>0
, and its corresponding
Zhou valuation

\nu(\cdot,\Phi_{o,\max})
is defined by
\nu(f,\Phi_{o,\max})\coloneqq\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{o,\max})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
. If
\Phi_{o,\max}
is a local Zhou weight related to
|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}
near
o
, then
(1+\sigma(\varphi,\Phi_{o,\max}))\Phi_{o,\max}
is a local Zhou weight related to
|f_{0}|^{2}
near
o
.

We give a sufficient condition for a Zhou valuation to be quasimonomial.

Proposition 1.13
.

Let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
with analytic singularities such that
c_{o}^{f_{0}}(\varphi)=1.

Let
\Phi_{o,\max}
be a local Zhou weight related to
|f_{0}|^{2}
near
o
such that
\Phi_{o,\max}\geq\varphi+O(1)
near
o
. Denote by
\nu
the Zhou valuation corresponding to the Zhou weight
\Phi_{o,\max}
. Then
\nu
is a quasimonomial valuation.

In
[
9
]
, Boucksom–Favre–Jonsson established a fundamental result about the relations between multiplier ideal sheaves and relative types by using quasimonomial valuations. In
[
2
]
, we proved that the result also holds for Zhou valuations. Furthermore, the following theorem shows that the result holds for quasimonomial Zhou valuations

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
u,

v
be two plurisubharmonic functions near
o
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

there exists a plurisubharmonic function
\varphi_{0}
near
o
(
\not\equiv-\infty
) and two sequences of numbers
\{t_{i,j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}
(
t_{i,j}\rightarrow+\infty
when
j\rightarrow+\infty
,
i=1,2
) such that
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{t_{1,j}}{t_{2,j}}=1
and

\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{0}+t_{1,j}v)_{o}\subset\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{0}+t_{2,j}u)_{o}

for every
j
;

(2)

for any plurisubharmonic function
\varphi_{0}
near
o
and any
t>0
, we have

\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{0}+tv)_{o}\subset\mathcal{I}(\varphi_{0}+tu)_{o};

(3)

for any local Zhou weight
\Phi_{o,\max}
near
o
whose corresponding Zhou valuation is quasimonomial, we have

\sigma(u,\Phi_{o,\max})\leq\sigma(v,\Phi_{o,\max}).

1.4.
Valuations related to Tian functions

Let
f_{0}
and
\varphi_{0}
be as those defined in
Section

1.3
. Let
\varphi
,
\psi
be any two plurisubharmonic functions defined near
o
. Denote

c_{o}(\varphi,t\psi;f_{0},\varphi_{0})\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\colon|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{2t\psi}e^{-2c\varphi}\ \text{is integrable near}\ o\big\}.

We recall the following notation of
Tian function
(see
[
2
]
):

\Tn(t;f_{0},\varphi_{0},\varphi,\psi)\coloneqq c_{o}(\varphi,t\psi;f_{0},\varphi_{0}),\quad\text{for}\ t\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that we allow
t
to take negative value. We also denote
\Tn(t;f_{0},\varphi_{0},\varphi,\psi)
by
\Tn(t;\psi)
or
\Tn(t)
for short. It is easy to see that
\Tn(t)
is non-decreasing. It follows from the Hölder inequality that
\Tn(t)
is concave in
t
.

If
\varphi
is a local Zhou weight related to
|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-\varphi_{0}}
near
o
, then
\Tn(t)
is differentiable at
t=0
and
\Tn^{\prime}(0)=\sigma(\psi,\varphi)
for any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
near
o
(see
2.24
).

Now we assume
\varphi
is a general plurisubharmonic function near
o
. If
\Tn(t)
is differentiable at
t=0
for any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
, we set

\tilde{\sigma}(\psi,\varphi)\coloneqq\Tn^{\prime}(0)=\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\Tn(t)-\Tn(0)}{t}.

We show that
\tilde{\sigma}(\cdot,\varphi)
satisfies the tropical multiplicativity and tropical additivity properties in the following theorem, which has been proved in
[
2
]
when
\varphi
is a local Zhou weight.

Theorem 1.15
.

Assume that
\Tn(t)
is differentiable at
t=0
for any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
near
o
. Then

\tilde{\sigma}(\psi,\varphi)
defined above satisfies

(1)

for any
c_{1}\geq 0
and
c_{2}\geq 0
,
\tilde{\sigma}(c_{1}\psi_{1}+c_{2}\psi_{2},\varphi)=c_{1}\tilde{\sigma}(\psi_{1},\varphi)+c_{2}\tilde{\sigma}(\psi_{2},\varphi)
;

(2)

\tilde{\sigma}(\max\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\},\varphi)=\min\{\tilde{\sigma}(\psi_{1},\varphi),\tilde{\sigma}(\psi_{2},\varphi)\}
, where
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
are germs of plurisubharmonic functions near
o
;

(3)

\tilde{\sigma}(\log|f_{1}+f_{2}|,\varphi)\geq\min\big\{\tilde{\sigma}(\log|f_{1}|,\varphi),\tilde{\sigma}(\log|f_{2}|,\varphi)\big\}
, where
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Statements
(1)
and
(3)
in Theorem
1.15
show that
\tilde{\sigma}(\log|\cdot|,\varphi)
is a valuation on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
.
For the isolated singularity case, the following proposition shows that the valuation is a Zhou valuation. Thus,
\tilde{\sigma}(\log|\cdot|,\varphi)
is a generalization of Zhou valuation.

Proposition 1.16
.

Assume that there exists
N\gg 0
such that
|f_{0}|^{2}|z|^{2N}e^{-2\varphi_{0}-2\varphi}

o

If
\Tn(t;f_{0},\varphi_{0},\varphi,\log|g|)
is differentiable at

for any holomorphic function

near

, then
\nu(g)\coloneqq\Tn^{\prime}(0;f_{0},\varphi_{0},\varphi,\log|g|)
is a Zhou valuation on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Strong openness property of multiplier ideal sheaves

Let
f
be a holomorphic function near
o
, and let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
.
Let us define the minimal
L^{2}
integral related to multiplier ideal sheaf (see
[
18
,
15
]
)

C_{f,\varphi}(U)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\int_{U}|\tilde{f}|^{2}\colon(\tilde{f}-f,o)\in\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}\ \&\ \tilde{f}\in\mathcal{O}(U)\right\},

where
U\subseteq\Delta^{n}
is a domain with
o\in U
.

Let
\{\phi_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}
be a sequence of negative plurisubharmonic functions on
\Delta^{n}
,
which is convergent to a negative Lebesgue measurable function
\phi
on
\Delta^{n}
in Lebesgue measure.

In
[
18
]
, Guan–Zhou presented the following lower semicontinuity property of plurisubharmonic functions with a multiplier.

Proposition 2.1

(see
[
18
, Proposition 1.8]
)
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function near
o
, and let
\varphi_{0}
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
.
Assume that for any small enough neighborhood
U
of
o
,
the pairs
(f,\phi_{m})

(m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0})
satisfies

(2.1)

\inf_{m}C_{f,\varphi_{0}+\phi_{m}}(U)>0.

Then
|f|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{-2\phi}
is not integrable near
o
.

The Noetherian property of multiplier ideal sheaves (see
[
11
]
) shows that

Remark 2.2
.

Assume that

(1)

\phi_{m+1}\geq\phi_{m}
holds for any
m
;

(2)

|f|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{-2\phi_{m}}
is not integrable near
o
for any
m
.

Then the inequality (
2.1
) holds.

In particular, Proposition
2.1
and Remark
2.2
imply the
strong openness property
(cf.
[
17
]
)

\mathcal{I}(\varphi)=\mathcal{I}_{+}(\varphi)

of plurisubharmonic functions
\varphi
.

2.2.
Some results on plurisubharmonic functions, multiplier ideal sheaves and valuations

Firstly, let us recall a basic result on plurisubharmonic functions.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
27
]
; see also
[
10
]
)
.

For any plurisubharmonic function
\varphi
near
o
, if the Lelong number
\nu_{o}(\varphi)<1
, then
e^{-2\varphi}
is locally integrable near

.

The following is the so-called
Demailly’s approximation theorem
.

Lemma 2.4

(see
[
10
]
)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain, and let
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(D)
. For any positive integer
m
, let
\{\sigma_{m,k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an orthonormal basis of
A^{2}(D,2m\varphi)\coloneqq\big\{f\in\mathcal{O}(D)\colon\int_{D}|f|^{2}e^{-2m\varphi}d\lambda<+\infty\big\}
. Set

\varphi_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2m}\log\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|\sigma_{m,k}|^{2}

on
D
. Then there exist two positive constants
c_{1}
(depending only on
n
and the diameter of
D
) and
c_{2}
such that

(2.2)

\varphi(z)-\frac{c_{1}}{m}\leq\varphi_{m}(z)\leq\sup_{|\tilde{z}-z|<r}\varphi(\tilde{z})+\frac{1}{m}\log\frac{c_{2}}{r^{n}}

for any
z\in D
satisfying
\{\tilde{z}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|\tilde{z}-z|<r\}\subset\subset D
. Especially,
\varphi_{m}
converges to
\varphi
pointwise and in
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
on
D
.

The strong openness property implies that the multiplier ideal sheaf is essentially with analytic singularities.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
. Then
\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}=\mathcal{I}(\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|)_{o}
for large enough
N\gg 0
, where
J_{N+1}\coloneqq\mathcal{I}((N+1)\varphi)_{o}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{M})_{o}
and
|J_{N+1}|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}|f_{j}|^{2}
.

Proof.

For the convenience of readers, we give a proof.

There exists a neighborhood
V
of
o
such that

\begin{split}&\int_{V}e^{-2\varphi}-e^{-2\max\{\varphi,\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\}}\\
=&\ \int_{\{\log|J_{N+1}|\geq N\varphi\}\cap V}e^{-2\varphi}-e^{-2\max\{\varphi,\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\}}\\
\leq&\ \int_{\{\log|J_{N+1}|\geq N\varphi\}\cap V}e^{-2\varphi}|J_{N+1}|^{2}e^{-2N\varphi}\\
\leq&\ \int_{V}|J_{N+1}|^{2}e^{-2(N+1)\varphi}\\
<&\ +\infty,\end{split}

which implies that

(2.3)

\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}=\mathcal{I}\Big(\max\big\{\varphi,\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\big\}\Big)_{o}.

Lemma
2.4
shows that

(2.4)

\varphi\leq\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|+O(1)

near
o
. By the strong openness property, there exists
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

(2.5)

\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}=\mathcal{I}\big((1+\epsilon_{0})\varphi\big)_{o}.

Taking
N\geq\frac{1}{\epsilon_{0}}
, we have
\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\geq(1+\epsilon_{0})\frac{1}{N+1}\log|J_{N+1}|
.
Combining with the equalities (
2.3
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\Big)_{o}&\subset\mathcal{I}\Big(\max\big\{\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|,\varphi\big\}\Big)_{o}\\
&=\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}\\
&=\mathcal{I}\big((1+\epsilon_{0})\varphi\big)_{o}\\
&\subset\mathcal{I}\Big((1+\epsilon_{0})\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\Big)_{o}\\
&\subset\mathcal{I}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|\Big)_{o},\end{split}

which implies
\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}=\mathcal{I}(\frac{1}{N}\log|J_{N+1}|)_{o}
.
Theorem
2.5
holds.
∎

Let us recall two results about the upper envelope of family of plurisubharmonic functions.

and

(see

If all

We recall Skoda’s division theorem.

Theorem 2.9

(see
[
10
, Theorem 12.13]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a complete Kähler open subset of

and
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function on

. Set
m=\min\{n,r-1\}
. Then for every holomorphic function

on

Using the above division theorem, we give some lemmas on valuations.

Lemma 2.10
.

For any valuation
\nu
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
and holomorphic functions
g,f_{1},\ldots,f_{r}
near
o
satisfying
\nu(f_{j})>0
for
1\leq j\leq r
, we have

\sigma(\log|g|,\varphi)\leq\nu(g)
, where
\varphi\coloneqq\log\big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}|f_{j}|^{\frac{1}{\nu(f_{j})}}\big)
.

Proof.

We prove this lemma by contradiction: if not, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that

\log|g|\leq(\nu(g)+\delta_{1})\varphi+O(1)
near
o
. Then we can find some
\delta_{2}>0
and a set of positive integers
\{m_{0},\ldots,m_{r}\}
such that

\log|g|\leq\frac{\nu(g)+\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}\tilde{\varphi}+O(1)\quad\ \text{near}\ o,

where

\tilde{\varphi}\coloneqq\log\big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}|f_{j}^{m_{j}}|\big)
and
\nu(f_{j}^{m_{j}})\geq m_{0}
for
1\leq j\leq r
. For any positive integer
l
, we have

\log|g^{l}|\leq\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}\tilde{\varphi}+O(1)\quad\ \text{near}\ o,

which implies that
|g^{l}|^{2}e^{-2\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}+\epsilon}{m_{0}}\tilde{\varphi}}
is integrable near
o
for small enough
\epsilon>0
(independent of
l
).
Denote by
I
the ideal generated by
\{(f_{j}^{m_{j}},o)\}_{1\leq j\leq r}
in
\mathcal{O}_{o}
. Let
|F|\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}|f_{j}^{m_{j}}|
,
\epsilon_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\epsilon}{m_{0}}
and
m\coloneqq\min\{n,r-1\}
. Since
|g^{l}|^{2}e^{-2\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}+\epsilon}{m_{0}}\tilde{\varphi}}
is integrable on a small open set
U
containing
o
, we have

\begin{split}&\int_{U}|g^{l}|^{2}|F|^{-2(m+1)-2\epsilon_{0}}|F|^{-2\big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-(n+1)\big)}\\
\leq&C\int_{U}|g^{l}|^{2}|F|^{-2(n+1)-2\epsilon_{0}}|F|^{-2\big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-(n+1)\big)}\\
=&C\int_{U}|g^{l}|^{2}e^{-2\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}+\epsilon}{m_{0}}\tilde{\varphi}}<+\infty.\end{split}

By Theorem
2.9
, there exist holomorphic functions
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{r})
on
U
such that
g^{l}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}f_{j}^{m_{j}}h_{j}
and

(2.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{U}|h|^{2}|F|^{-2(m+1)-2\epsilon_{0}}|F|^{-2\big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-1-(n+1)\big)}\\
=&\int_{U}|h|^{2}|F|^{-2m-2\epsilon_{0}}|F|^{-2\big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-(n+1)\big)}<+\infty.\end{split}

Note that
g^{l}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}f_{j}^{m_{j}}h_{j}
implies
(g^{l},o)\in I
. It follows from the estimate (
2.6
) that, using Theorem
2.9
for each
h_{j}

(1\leq j\leq r)
, we can get
h_{j}=\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}h_{j,k}f_{j}^{m_{j}}\in I
(which implies
(g^{l},o)\in I^{2}
) and

\int_{U}\sum_{1\leq k\leq r}|h_{j,k}|^{2}|F|^{-2m-2\epsilon_{0}}|F|^{-2\big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-1-(n+1)\big)}<+\infty.

Hence, using Theorem
2.9
repeatedly, we finally have
(g^{l},o)\in I^{\big\lfloor\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}\big\rfloor-n-1}
for
l\gg 1
. As
\nu(f_{j}^{m_{j}})\geq m_{0}
for
1\leq j\leq r
, we get

\begin{split}\nu(g^{l})&\geq m_{0}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}\Big\rfloor-n-1\Big)\\
&\geq m_{0}\Big(\frac{\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}}{m_{0}}-n-2\Big)\\
&=\nu(g^{l})+l\delta_{2}-m_{0}(n+2),\end{split}

which implies
l\leq\frac{m_{0}(n+2)}{\delta_{2}}
. This is a contradiction since we can choose
l
arbitrarily large. Then we have
\sigma(\log|g|,\varphi)\leq\nu(g)
.
∎

\delta_{1}>0
such that

\log|g|\leq(\nu(g)+\delta_{1})\varphi+O(1)
near

. There exist
\delta_{2}>0

2.3.
Tian function

In this section, we discuss a special condition for Tian functions.

Firstly, let us recall the definition of Tian functions and a useful lemma.
Let
f_{0}
be a vector of holomorphic functions near
o
, and
\varphi
be a plurisubharmonic function near
o
. Let
f
be a holomorphic function near
o
.
Recall the Tian function

\Tn(t;f_{0},f,\varphi)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\colon|f_{0}|^{2}|f|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The Hölder inequality implies the concavity of
\Tn(t;f_{0},f,\varphi)
.

Lemma 2.14

(see
[
2
, Lemma 2.14]
)
.

Assume that
\Tn(t;f_{0},f,\varphi)
is strictly increasing on
(t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta)
for some fixed
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and small
\delta>0
. Then

\Tn\Big(t_{0};f_{0},f,\max\big\{\varphi,\frac{\log|f|}{a}\big\}\Big)=\Tn(t_{0};f_{0},f,\varphi)

holds for any
a\in(0,\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(t_{0};f_{0},f,\varphi)]
.

Example 2.15
.

Let
f=\sum_{\alpha\in I}b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
and
g=\sum_{\alpha\in\tilde{I}}\tilde{b}_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}
be holomorphic functions near
o
, and let

\varphi=\max\{a_{1}\log|z_{1}|,\ldots,a_{r}\log|z_{r}|\},

where
a_{j}>0
, and
I,\tilde{I}\subset\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
b_{\alpha}\not=0
for any
\alpha\in I
and
\tilde{b}_{\alpha}\not=0
for any
\alpha\in\tilde{I}
.
The following Lemma
2.16
shows that

\Tn(m;g,f,\varphi)=\inf_{\alpha\in\tilde{I}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}+m\inf_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}

for any integer
m\geq 0
. Due to the concavity of
\Tn(t;g,f,\varphi)
, we have

\Tn(t;1,f,\varphi)=\inf_{\alpha\in\tilde{I}}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}+t\inf_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}

for any
t\geq 0
. By a direct calculation, we have

\log|f|\leq\left(\inf_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}\right)\varphi+O(1)

near
o
, which shows

\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)\geq\inf_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}.

As
\Tn(t;g,f,\varphi)\geq t\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)
for any
t\geq 0
, we have

\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)=\inf_{\alpha\in I}\sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}}{a_{j}}.

Lemma 2.16

(see
[
16
]
)
.

Let
\varphi=\max\{a_{1}\log|z_{1}|,\ldots,a_{r}\log|z_{r}|\}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then

\mathcal{I}(\varphi)_{o}=\Big\{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\colon b_{\alpha}=0\ \text{if}\ \sum_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\alpha_{j}+1}{a_{j}}\leq 1,\ \text{where}\ f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}b_{\alpha}z^{\alpha}\Big\}.

We say
(f,\varphi;|f_{0}|^{2})
satisfies
condition (T)
if the following statements hold:

(1)

\log|f|\leq a\varphi+O(1)
near

and
\Tn(t;f_{0},f,\varphi)=at+b
for any
t\geq 0
;

(2)

\Tn(t;f_{0},f,\varphi)
is differentiable at
t=0
.

2.15
shows that
(f,\varphi;f)
satisfies condition (T), where
f
is any holomorphic function near
o
and
\varphi
is defined as in
2.15
.

If
(f,\varphi;|f_{0}|^{2})
satisfies condition (T), by definition, we have

. It follows from

. Then we get

The following lemma shows that condition (T) satisfies a multiplicative property.

Lemma 2.17
.

If
(f_{i},\varphi;|f_{0}|^{2})
satisfies condition (T) for

, then we have that
(f_{1}f_{2},\varphi;|f_{0}|^{2})
also satisfies condition (T) and

\sigma(\log|f_{1}f_{2}|,\varphi)=\sigma(\log|f_{1}|,\varphi)+\sigma(\log|f_{2}|,\varphi).

Proof.

Set

A(s,t)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\colon|f_{0}|^{2}|f_{1}|^{2s}|f_{2}|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}.

It is clear that
A(s,0)=\Tn(s;f_{0},f_{1},\varphi)
and
A(0,t)=\Tn(t;f_{0},f_{2},\varphi)
. Denote
a_{i}=\sigma(\log|f_{i}|,\varphi)
for

near

\log|f_{1}f_{2}|\leq(a_{1}+a_{2})\varphi+O(1)

near

.
Note that
\Tn(t;f_{0},f_{1}f_{2},\varphi)
is concave. Then it suffices to prove

\Tn_{-}^{\prime}(0;f_{0},f_{1}f_{2},\varphi)\leq a_{1}+a_{2}.

As
A(s,t)
is concave in
s
for fixed

, we have

(2.7)

\begin{split}\limsup_{x\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(-x,-x)-A(0,-x)}{-x}&\leq\limsup_{x\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(-y,-x)-A(0,-x)}{-y}\\
&=\frac{A(-y,0)-A(0,0)}{-y}\end{split}

for any

.
As
(f_{i},\varphi;|f_{0}|^{2})
satisfies condition (T) for

\begin{split}\Tn_{-}^{\prime}(0;f_{0},f_{1}f_{2},\varphi)&=\limsup_{x\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(-x,-x)-A(0,0)}{-x}\\
&\leq\limsup_{x\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(-x,-x)-A(0,-x)}{-x}+\limsup_{x\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(0,-x)-A(0,-x)}{-x}\\
&\leq\lim_{y\rightarrow 0+0}\frac{A(-y,0)-A(0,0)}{-y}+\Tn^{\prime}(0;f_{0},f_{2},\varphi)\\
&=a_{1}+a_{2}.\end{split}

Thus, Lemma
2.17
holds.
∎

We give a maximal property for

Remark 2.27

\begin{split}\Tn(t+\Delta t)&=\sup\{c:|f|^{2t+\Delta t}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\}\\

\frac{\Tn(t)}{t}
,
\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(t)
and
\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(t)
are decreasing, and

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Tn(t)}{t}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(t).

As
\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi)-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)\big)<+\infty
, there exists
C>0
such that

\Tn(m)=c_{o}^{f^{m}}(\varphi)\leq m\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)+C,\quad\forall\,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1},

which implies

\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Tn(m)}{m}\leq\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi).

As
\Tn(t)/t\geq\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)
for any
t\geq 0
and
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn(t)/t
exists, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn(t)/t=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)
.
∎

Lemma 2.28

For the convenience of readers, we recall the proof.

(f,o)\in\mathcal{I}(a\varphi)_{o}

Proof.

2.6.
Convergence results for valuations and for relative types

Let
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a sequence of valuations on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
satisfying the following conditions:

(1)

There exists a sequence of tame maximal weights
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
near

such that
\nu_{j}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}

(2)

There exists

(3)

\sup_{j}\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\Phi_{j})-\nu_{j}(f)\big)<+\infty
.

We provide a necessary and sufficient condition for statement (1) in Theorem
1.11
.

Remark 2.33
.

Condition (3) implies that
\sup_{j}\nu_{j}(f)<+\infty
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}
. In fact, by definition, we have
c_{o}(\Phi_{j})\leq c_{o}^{f}(\Phi_{j})-\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j})\leq M
for every
j
, which implies the Lelong number
\nu_{o}(\Phi_{j})\geq\frac{1}{M}
by Lemma

, i.e.,
\Phi_{j}\leq\frac{1}{M}\log|z|+O(1)
near

. Then
\nu_{j}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j})\leq M\nu_{o}(\log|f|)
for any

and every

.

We give a convergence result for valuations, which will be used to solve the valuative interpolation problem.

Proposition 2.34
.

There exist a subsequence of
\{\nu_{j}\}
denoted by
\{\nu_{j_{l}}\}
and a tame maximal weight
\varphi_{0}
near
o
, which satisfy that
\{\nu_{j_{l}}\}
converges to a valuation
\nu_{0}
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
and
\nu_{0}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Proof.

Denote
I_{k}^{j}\coloneqq\{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\colon\nu_{j}(f)\geq k\}
for every
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. We prove Proposition
2.34
in three steps.

Step 1.
Find a subsequence
\{I_{k}^{j_{l}}\}
of
I_{k}^{j}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
is maximal for any
k
. It means that for any subsequence
\{I_{k}^{j^{\prime}_{l}}\}
of
\{I_{k}^{j_{l}}\}
,
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j^{\prime}_{l}}\big)=\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
.

Note that

\cup_{j_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{j\geq j_{1}}I_{k}^{j}\big)=\{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\colon\exists\,j_{1}\text{ s.t. $\nu_{j}(f)\geq k$ holds for any $j\geq j_{1}$}\}.

As
\mathcal{O}_{o}
is a Noetherian ring, there exists a subsequence
\{I_{1}^{j^{(1)}(l)}\}
of
I_{1}^{j}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{1}^{j^{(1)}(l)}\big)
is maximal. Similarly, there exists a subsequence
\{I_{1}^{j^{(2)}(l)}\}
of
I_{1}^{j^{(1)}(l)}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{2}^{j^{(2)}(l)}\big)
is maximal. By induction, for any
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there exists a sequence
\{I_{1}^{j^{(r)}(l)}\}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{r}^{j^{(r)}(l)}\big)
is maximal and
\{I_{1}^{j^{(r+1)}(l)}\}
is a subsequence of
\{I_{1}^{j^{(r)}(l)}\}
. Using the diagonal method, we obtain a subsequence
\{I_{k}^{j_{l}}\}
of
I_{k}^{j}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
is maximal for any
k
.

Step 2.

We prove

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)=\nu_{0}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0}),\quad\forall\,(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}.

Firstly, we prove that
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)
exists for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

We prove it by contradiction: if not, there exist two subsequences of valuations
\{\nu_{j^{\prime}_{l}}\}
and
\{\nu_{j^{\prime\prime}_{l}}\}
of
\{\nu_{j_{l}}\}
such that

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j^{\prime}_{l}}(f)=a_{1}<a_{2}=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j^{\prime\prime}_{l}}(f).

Note that
\nu_{j}(f^{m})=m\nu_{j}(f)
. Then, without loss of generality, assume that there exists a positive integer
k_{0}\in(a_{1},a_{2})
. Then we have

(f,o)\in\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k_{0}}^{j^{\prime}_{l}}\big)

and

(f,o)\not\in\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k_{0}}^{j_{l}}\big),

which contradicts to the maximality of
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k_{0}}^{j_{l}}\big)
.
Thus,
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)
exists for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Secondly, we construct the plurisubharmonic function
\varphi_{0}
.

As
A_{1}\log|z|\leq\Phi_{j}+O(1)
near
o
for any
j
, we have
\nu_{j}(f)\geq\frac{N}{A_{1}}
for any
(f,o)\in J_{N}
, where
J_{N}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{o}
is generated by
\{z^{\alpha}\colon|\alpha|=N\}
. Then

J_{\lceil A_{1}k\rceil}\subset\cap_{j}I_{k}^{j},

which implies that
\mathcal{O}_{o}/\big(\cup_{j_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq j_{1}}I_{k}^{j}\big)\big)
is a finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-linear space for any

Denote
I_{k}\coloneqq\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
. It is clear that
I_{k_{1}}\cdot I_{k_{2}}\subset I_{k_{1}k_{2}}
for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. As

there exist finitely many polynomials

which generate
I_{k}
. Denote
|I_{k}|^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq r\leq r_{k}}|f_{k,r}|^{2}
(without loss of generality, we assume
|I_{k}|<1
in the polydisc
\Delta^{n}
).

Let
\varphi_{0}\coloneqq(\max_{k}\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|)^{*}
on
\Delta^{n}
, where
(h)^{*}
denotes the upper semicontinuous regularization of
h
. Then
\varphi_{0}
is a plurisubharmonic function on
\Delta^{n}
and equals almost everywhere to
\max_{k}\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|
.

Thirdly, we prove

\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f),\quad\forall\,(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}.

If
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)>a
for some

, then there exist

such that
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f^{s})>k>sa
,
which implies
(f^{s},o)\in I_{k}
. By the definition of
\varphi_{0}
, we have

\frac{s}{k}\log|f|\leq\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|+O(1)\leq\varphi_{0}+O(1)

near
o
. Then we obtain the
\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\geq\frac{k}{s}>a
, which shows
\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\geq\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)
.

On the other hand, if
\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})>a
for some
a>0
, then
\log|f|\leq a\varphi_{0}+O(1)
near
o
, which shows that
|f|^{2s}e^{-2as\varphi_{0}}
is integrable near
o
for any integer
s>0
. By the strong openness property of multiplier ideal sheaves, there exists
k>0
such that
|f|^{2s}e^{-\frac{2as}{k}\log|I_{k}|}
is integrable near
o
. There exists
l_{1}\geq 1
such that
I_{k}=\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l_{1}}}\subset I_{k}^{j_{l}}
for any
l\geq l_{1}
. Note that

\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}^{j_{l}}|+O(1)\leq\Phi_{j_{l}}+O(1)

near
o
. Then we obtain that for any
s
, there exists
l_{s}
such that for any
l\geq l_{s}
,

|f|^{2s}e^{-2as\Phi_{j_{l}}}

is integrable near
o
. Set

M\coloneqq\sup_{j}\sup_{(h,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{h}(\Phi_{j})-\nu_{j}(h)\big)<+\infty.

Now, we have

\nu_{j_{l}}(f)=\frac{\nu_{j_{l}}(f^{s})}{s}\geq\frac{c_{o}^{f^{s}}(\Phi_{j_{l}})-M}{s}\geq\frac{as-M}{s}

for any
s
and
l\geq l_{s}
. Letting
l\rightarrow+\infty
and
s\rightarrow+\infty
, we get
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)\geq a
, which induces
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)\geq\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})
.

Thus, we have
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)=\nu_{0}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Step 3.
Find a tame maximal weight near
o
.

If
|f|^{2}e^{-2a\varphi_{0}}
is integrable near
o
, by the strong openness property of multiplier ideal sheaves, there exists
k>0
such that
|f|^{2}e^{-\frac{2a}{k}\log|I_{k}|}
is integrable near
o
. There exists
l_{1}\geq 1
such that
I_{k}=\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l_{1}}}\subset I_{k}^{j_{l}}
for any
l\geq l_{1}
. Note that

\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|\leq\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}^{j_{l}}|+O(1)\leq\Phi_{j_{l}}+O(1)

near
o
. Then for any
l\geq l_{1}
,
|f|^{2}e^{-2a\Phi_{j_{l}}}
is integrable near
o
.
By the definition of
M
, we have

\nu_{j_{l}}(f)\geq c_{o}^{f}(\Phi_{j_{l}})-M\geq a-M

for any
l\geq l_{1}
. Letting
l\rightarrow+\infty
, we have

\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f)\geq a-M.

Thus,
c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{0})-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\leq M
when
\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})<+\infty
.
As there exists
(f_{0},o)
such that
\sup_{j}\nu_{j}(f_{0})<+\infty
and
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\nu_{j_{l}}(f_{0})=\nu_{0}(f)=\sigma(\log|f_{0}|,\varphi_{0})
, we have
c_{o}^{f_{0}}(\varphi_{0})<+\infty
, which implies that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that
\varphi_{0}\leq C_{2}\log|z|+O(1)
near
o
. Then we have

\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{0})-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\big)\leq M.

It is clear that there exists
C_{1}>0
such that
\varphi_{0}\geq C_{1}\log|z|+O(1)
near
o
.
Using Lemma
2.29
, Lemma
2.30
and Lemma
2.32
, we can find a tame maximal weight
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
satisfying
\nu_{0}(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.
∎

Remark 2.35
.

Following the above proof, we know that if
\{\nu_{j}\}
does not satisfy conditions
(1)-(3)
, and only satisfies
\sup_{j}\nu_{j}(g)<+\infty
for any holomorphic function
g
near
o
, we can also obtain that: There exists a subsequence of
\{\nu_{j}\}
denoted by
\{\nu_{j_{l}}\}
such that
\{\nu_{j_{l}}\}
converges to a valuation
\nu_{0}
on
\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Similarly, for relative types, we can also obtain the following convergence result.

Let
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}}
be a sequence of tame maximal weights near
o
satisfying the following statements:

(1)

There exists
A_{1}>0
such that
A_{1}\log|z|\leq\Phi_{j}+O(1)
near
o
holds for all
j
;

(2)

M\coloneqq\sup_{j}\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\Phi_{j})-\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j})\big)<+\infty
.

Proposition 2.36
.

There exists a subsequence of
\{\Phi_{j}\}
denoted by
\{\Phi_{j_{l}}\}
and a tame maximal weight
\varphi_{0}
near
o
such that

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\psi,\Phi_{j_{l}})=\sigma(\psi,\varphi_{0})

for any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
near
o
, and

\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{0})-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\big)\leq M.

Proof.

Denote
I_{k}^{j}\coloneqq\{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\colon\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j})\geq k\}
for any
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. Note that

\begin{split}\sigma(\log|f_{1}f_{2}|,\Phi_{j})&\geq\sigma(\log|f_{1}|,\Phi_{j})+\sigma(\log|f_{2}|,\Phi_{j})\\
&\geq\max\{\sigma(\log|f_{1}|,\Phi_{j}),\sigma(\log|f_{2}|,\Phi_{j})\}\end{split}

and

\sigma(\log|f_{1}+f_{2}|,\Phi_{j})\geq\sigma(\log(|f_{1}|+|f_{2}|),\Phi_{j})=\min\{\sigma(\log|f_{1}|,\Phi_{j}),\sigma(\log|f_{2}|,\Phi_{j})\}.

Thus
I_{k}^{j}
is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Following the proof of Proposition
2.34
, we obtain that:

(1)

There exists a subsequence
\{I_{k}^{j_{l}}\}
of
I_{k}^{j}
such that
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
is maximal for any
k
. It means that for any subsequence
\{I_{k}^{j^{\prime}_{l}}\}
of
\{I_{k}^{j_{l}}\}
,
\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j^{\prime}_{l}}\big)=\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
.

(2)

Denote
I_{k}\coloneqq\cup_{l_{1}\geq 1}\big(\cap_{l\geq l_{1}}I_{k}^{j_{l}}\big)
. It is clear that
I_{k_{1}}\cdot I_{k_{2}}\subset I_{k_{1}k_{2}}
for any
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. As
A_{1}\log|z|\leq\Phi_{j}
for any
j
, there exist finitely many polynomials
f_{k,1},\ldots,f_{k,r_{k}}
which generate
I_{k}
. Denote
|I_{k}|^{2}\coloneqq\sum_{1\leq r\leq r_{k}}|f_{k,r}|^{2}
(without loss of generality, we assume
|I_{k}|<1
on the polydisc
\Delta^{n}
).
Let
\varphi_{0}\coloneqq(\max_{k}\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|)^{*}
on
\Delta^{n}
, which is a plurisubharmonic function on
\Delta^{n}
and equal almost everywhere to
\max_{k}\frac{1}{k}\log|I_{k}|
.

(3)

For any
(f,o)
,

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j_{l}})=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})

and

\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{0})-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})\big)\leq M.

(4)

Using Lemma
2.29
, Lemma
2.30
and Lemma
2.32
, we can find a tame maximal weight
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
satisfying
\sigma(\log|f|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{0})
and
c_{o}^{f}(\tilde{\varphi}_{0})=c_{0}^{f}(\varphi_{0})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

In the following, we prove
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\psi,\Phi_{j_{l}})=\sigma(\psi,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})
for any plurisubharmonic function
\psi
near
o
.

Fixed a plurisubharmonic function
\psi
near
o
, for any
m\geq 0
, denote
\psi_{m}\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\log|\mathcal{I}(m\psi)_{o}|
. As
\psi\leq\psi_{m}+O(1)
near
o
, we have
\sigma(\psi_{m},\Phi_{j_{l}})\leq\sigma(\psi,\Phi_{j_{l}})
and
\sigma(\psi_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\leq\sigma(\psi,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})
.

If
\sigma(\psi,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})>a>0
, then
|\mathcal{I}(m\psi)_{o}|^{2}e^{-2ma\tilde{\varphi}_{0}}
is integrable near
o
for any
m
. By
\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\big(c_{o}^{f}(\tilde{\varphi}_{0})-\sigma(\log|f|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\big)\leq M
, we have

\sigma(\psi_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{0})=\frac{1}{m}\sigma(\log|\mathcal{I}(m\psi)_{o}|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\geq\frac{ma-M}{m},

which shows

(2.13)

\sigma(\psi_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\leq\sigma(\psi,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\leq\sigma(\psi_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{0})+\frac{M}{m}

for any
m
.
Similarly, we have

(2.14)

\sigma(\psi_{m},\Phi_{j_{l}})\leq\sigma(\psi,\Phi_{j_{l}})\leq\sigma(\psi_{m},\Phi_{j_{l}})+\frac{M}{m}

for any
m
and
l
.

Since
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|f|,\Phi_{j_{l}})=\sigma(\log|f|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})
holds for any
(f,o)
, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\psi_{m},\Phi_{j_{l}})&=\frac{1}{m}\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|\mathcal{I}(m\psi)_{o}|,\Phi_{j_{l}})\\
&=\frac{1}{m}\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\min_{1\leq k\leq p_{k}}\{\sigma(\log|f_{m,k}|,\Phi_{j_{l}})\}\\
&=\frac{1}{m}\min_{1\leq k\leq p_{k}}\{\sigma(\log|f_{m,k}|,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})\}\\
&=\sigma(\psi_{m},\tilde{\varphi}_{0}),\end{split}

where
\mathcal{I}(m\psi)_{o}
is generated by
\{(f_{m,k},o)\}_{1\leq k\leq p_{k}}
. Combining with (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), we obtain
\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\psi,\Phi_{j_{l}})=\sigma(\psi,\tilde{\varphi}_{0})
.

Thus, Proposition
2.36
holds.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

If there exists a valuation
\nu
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for any
j
, then by Lemma
2.10
,

\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)\leq\nu(F)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq m}\nu(f_{j})=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}.

On the other hand, by the definition of relative type, clearly we also have

\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)\geq\sum_{0\leq j\leq m}\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\varphi)\geq\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}.

Thus,
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
holds.

We prove this theorem in two steps.

Step 1.
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
.

As
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)<+\infty,
we have
f_{j}(o)=0
for any
1\leq j\leq m.

Denote
\varphi_{N}\coloneqq\max\{\varphi,N\log|z|\}
for any
N>0
. Then we have
\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\varphi_{N})\geq\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\varphi)\geq a_{j}
for all
0\leq j\leq m
. As
e^{\varphi}
is Hölder continuous, there exists
C>0
such that
c_{o}^{f}(\varphi)\leq\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi)+C
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
. Thus, for any
k>0
, there exists
N_{k}
such that

\begin{split}\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)&\geq\frac{c_{o}^{F^{k}}(\varphi)-C}{k}\\
&\geq\frac{c_{o}^{F^{k}}(\varphi_{N_{k}})-1-C}{k}\\
&\geq\frac{k\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N_{k}})-1-C}{k}\\
&\geq\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N})-\frac{1+C}{k},\end{split}

where the second “
\geq
” follows from Lemma
2.6
. Then we have

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N})=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi).

Consider the Tian function

\Tn(t)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\geq 0\colon|F|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi_{N}}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}.

As
\varphi_{N}
is a tame weight, it follows from Remark
2.27
that

(3.1)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(t)=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N}).

Then for every positive integer
s
,
|F|^{2s}e^{-2\Tn(s)\varphi_{N}}
is not integrable near
o
(because of the strong openness property of multiplier ideal sheaves, see
[
17
]
), then by
2.22
, there exists a local Zhou weight
\Phi_{N,s}
near
o
related to
|F|^{2s}
such that

\Phi_{N,s}\geq\Tn(s)\varphi_{N}.

Consider the Tian function

\widetilde{\Tn}(t)\coloneqq\sup\Big\{c\geq 0\colon|F|^{2t}e^{-2c\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}}\text{ is integrable near }o\Big\}.

Thus, we have
\widetilde{\Tn}(s)=\Tn(s)
and
\widetilde{\Tn}(t)\geq\Tn(t)
for any
t\geq 0
. It follows from
2.24
that
\widetilde{\Tn}^{\prime}(s)=\sigma\Big(\log|F|,\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big)
. Then we have

(3.2)

\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s)\leq\sigma\Big(\log|F|,\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big)\leq\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(s).

Denote the corresponding Zhou valuation of
\Phi_{N,s}
by
\nu_{N,s}
. Then we have

\Tn(s)\nu_{N,s}(f_{j})=\Tn(s)\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\Phi_{N,s})\geq\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\varphi_{N})\geq a_{j}

for all
0\leq j\leq m
and

\Tn(s)\nu_{N,s}(F)=\Tn(s)\sigma(\log|F|,\Phi_{N,s})=\sigma\Big(\log|F|,\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big).

Note that

\varphi_{N}=\log\Big(\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}|f_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}}+\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}|z_{k}|^{N}\Big)+O(1)

near
o
. By Lemma
2.28
, there exists a constant
C
(independent of
N
) such that
\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}^{*}}(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi_{N})-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{N}))<C
.
As
\widetilde{\Tn}(t)
is a concave function, we have

(3.3)

c_{o}\Big(\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big)=\widetilde{\Tn}(0)\leq\Tn(s)-s\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s).

It follows from the concavity of
\Tn(t)
and equality (
3.1
) that
\Tn(s)-s\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s)\leq c_{o}^{F^{s}}(\varphi_{N})-\sigma(\log|F^{s}|,\varphi_{N})<C
. Then the inequality (
3.3
) implies
c_{o}\big(\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\big)<C
for any
N
and
s
, which shows that the Lelong number
\nu_{o}\big(\frac{\Phi_{N,s}}{\Tn(s)}\big)\geq\frac{1}{C}
by Lemma
2.3
. Thus,

(3.4)

\Tn(s)\nu_{N,s}(f)\leq C\mathrm{ord}_{o}(f)

for any
N
,
s
and
(f,o)
.

By equality (
3.1
), inequalities (
3.2
), (
3.4
), and the proof of Theorem
2.34
(see
2.35
), there exists a subsequence of valuations of
\{\Tn(s)\nu_{N,s}\}_{s}
, which converges to a valuation
\nu_{N}
. Then we have

\nu_{N}(f_{j})\geq a_{j},\quad j=0,1,\ldots,m,

and

\nu_{N}(F)=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N}).

By the same reason, there exists a subsequence of valuations of
\nu_{N}
, which converges to a valuation
\nu
. Then we have

\nu(f_{j})\geq a_{j}
for any
0\leq j\leq m
and

\sum_{0\leq j\leq m}\nu(f_{j})=\nu(F)=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi_{N})=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j},

which implies
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for
j=0,1,\ldots,m
since
a_{0}=0
.

Step 2. If
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}
and
o
is an isolated point in
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}
, there exists a valuation
\nu
and a tame maximal weight
\varphi_{\nu}
such that
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for any
j
and
\nu(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{\nu})
for any
(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}
.

Consider the Tian function

\Tn(t)\coloneqq\sup\big\{c\geq 0:|F|^{2t}e^{-2c\varphi}\text{ is integrable near }o\big\}.

As
\varphi
is a tame weight, we have

(3.5)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(t)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(t)=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi).

As
o
is an isolated point in
\cap_{1\leq j\leq m}\{f_{j}=0\}
, there exists
A_{1}>0
such that
\varphi\geq A_{1}\log|z|+O(1)
near
o
.
Then for any positive integer
s
, by Remark
2.22
, there exists a local Zhou weight
\Phi_{s}
near
o
with respect to
|F|^{2s}
such that

\Phi_{s}\geq\Tn(s)\varphi

and

\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s)\leq\sigma\Big(\log|F|,\frac{\Phi_{s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big)\leq\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(s).

Denote the corresponding Zhou valuation of
\Phi_{s}
by
\nu_{s}
. Then we have

(3.6)

\Tn(s)\nu_{s}(f_{j})=\Tn(s)\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\Phi_{s})\geq\sigma(\log|f_{j}|,\varphi)\geq a_{j}

for any
0\leq j\leq m
and

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Tn(s)\nu_{s}(F)

\displaystyle=\Tn(s)\sigma(\log|F|,\Phi_{s})

\displaystyle=\sigma\Big(\log|F|,\frac{\Phi_{s}}{\Tn(s)}\Big)\in[\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s),\Tn^{\prime}_{-}(s)],

which shows
\sup_{s}\Tn(s)\nu_{s}(F)<+\infty
.

As
\Phi_{s}
is a local Zhou weight with respect to

, by Theorem
2.25
, we have

c_{o}^{f}(\Phi_{s})-\nu_{s}(f)\leq 1-\nu_{s}(F)s

for any

It follows from the concavity of
\Tn(s)
, (

) and (

\Tn(s)-s\Tn(s)\nu_{s}(F)\leq\Tn(s)-s\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s)\leq\Tn(s)-s\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi).

Note that
\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}(c_{o}^{f}(\varphi)-\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi))<\infty
. Then we have

\begin{split}\sup_{s}\sup_{(f,o)\in\mathcal{O}_{o}\setminus\{0\}}\left(c_{o}^{f}(\frac{\Phi_{s}}{\Tn(s)})-\Tn(s)\nu_{s}(f)\right)&\leq\sup_{s}(\Tn(s)-\Tn(s)s\nu_{s}(F))\\
&\leq\sup_{s}(\Tn(s)-s\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi))\\
&<+\infty.\end{split}

Note that
\frac{\Phi_{s}}{\Tn(s)}\geq\varphi\geq A_{1}\log|z|+O(1)
near

for any

, where
\frac{\Phi_{s}}{\Tn(s)}
is the corresponding weight of valuation
\Tn(s)\nu_{s}
.
By Theorem
2.34
, there exist a subsequence of
\{\Phi_{s}\}
denoted by
\{\Phi_{s_{l}}\}
(

), a valuation
\nu
and a tame maximal weight

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn(s_{l})\nu_{s_{l}}(f)=\nu(f)=\sigma(\log|f|,\varphi_{\nu})

for any

. Combining equalities (

), (

) and (

),
we have

\nu(F)=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\Tn^{\prime}_{+}(s_{l})=\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}a_{j}

and

for all

, which implies
\nu(f_{j})=a_{j}
for all

.

Thus, Theorem
1.2
holds.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

By Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to prove that
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}
, where
F\coloneqq\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}f_{j}
and
\varphi\coloneqq\log(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}|f_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}})
. By definition, we have
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)\geq\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}
. In the following, we prove
\sigma(\log|F|,\varphi)\leq\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}
.

Let
G\colon U\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a holomorphic map defined by
G\coloneqq(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
, where
U
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let
\tilde{F}\coloneqq\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}w_{j}
and
\tilde{\varphi}\coloneqq\log(\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}|w_{j}|^{\frac{1}{a_{j}}})
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
is the coordinate system on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By a direct calculation, we have

(3.8)

\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{c_{o}^{\tilde{F}^{l}}(\tilde{\varphi})}{l}=\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}a_{j}.

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)

exist, where
G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)
is the derivative of
G(t,t)
at

.
As

is concave, we have that

(6.2)

\begin{split}G\left(\frac{1}{2}t,\frac{1}{2}t\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}G(t,0)+\frac{1}{2}G(0,t).\end{split}

When

\begin{split}\frac{G(\frac{1}{2}t,\frac{1}{2}t)-G(0,0)}{\frac{1}{2}t}\geq\frac{G(t,0)-G(0,0)}{t}+\frac{G(0,t)-G(0,0)}{t}.\end{split}

Since
G_{t}^{\prime}(0,0)
,
G_{s}^{\prime}(0,0)
and

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)

exist, letting

, we have

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)\geq G_{t}^{\prime}(0,0)+G_{s}^{\prime}(0,0).

When
t<0
, the inequality (
6.2
) implies

\begin{split}\frac{G(\frac{1}{2}t,\frac{1}{2}t)-G(0,0)}{\frac{1}{2}t}\leq\frac{G(t,0)-G(0,0)}{t}+\frac{G(0,t)-G(0,0)}{t}.\end{split}

Letting
t\to 0
, as
G_{t}^{\prime}(0,0)
,
G_{s}^{\prime}(0,0)
and

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)

exist, we have

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)\leq G_{t}^{\prime}(0,0)+G_{s}^{\prime}(0,0).

Thus, we get

(6.3)

G_{1,1}^{\prime}(0,0)=G_{t}^{\prime}(0,0)+G_{s}^{\prime}(0,0).

Combining (
6.1
) with (
6.3
),

1.15

Now we prove the statement (2) of Theorem
1.15
. For

, denote

\displaystyle\int_{U}|f_{0}|^{2}e^{-2\varphi_{0}}e^{2t\max\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\}}e^{-2c\varphi}\leq

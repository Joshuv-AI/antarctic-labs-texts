On the application of a hypergeometric identity to generate generalized hypergeometric reduction formulas
J.L. González-Santander

Abstract
We systematically exploit a new generalized hypergeometric identity to obtain new hypergeometric summation formulas. As a consistency test, alternative proofs for some special cases are also provided. As a byproduct new summation formulas involving finite sums involving the psi function, or a recursive formula for the Bateman’s G function are derived. Finally, all the results have been numerically checked with MATHEMATICA.



Keywords
: Gamma function, psi function, Bateman’s
G
function,
beta function, incomplete beta function, summation formulas for generalized
hypergeometric functions.

Introduction

Mathematical applications of the generalized hypergeometric functions are
abundant in the existing literature. For instance, a variety of problems in
classical mechanics and mathematical physics lead to Picard-Fuchs equations.
These equations are frequently solvable in terms of generalized
hypergeometric functions
[
1
]
. Also, many combinatorial
identities, especially ones involving binomial and related coefficients, are
special cases of hypergeometric identities
[
2
, Sect. 2.7]
.
Another example is given in the calculation of the moments of certain
probability distributions, which are given in terms of generalized
hypergeometric functions
[
3
]
. Therefore, the calculation of
generalized hypergeometric functions for particular values of the argument
and the parameters in terms of more simple functions is of great relevance.
The classical compilation of these summation formulas is found in
[
4
, Chap. 7]
. A revision, as well as an extension of these tables,
was carried out in
[
5
]
. More recently, we found a new compilation
of representations of generalized hypergeometric functions in
[
6
, Chap. 8]
.

In existing literature, we found several papers devoted to the calculation
of hypergeometric summation formulas for a given number of parameters and
particular values of the argument (see i.e.
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,

[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
). However, the number of papers
devoted to the calculation of hypergeometric summation formulas for an
arbitrary number of parameters as well as an arbitrary argument is
relatively scarce (see i.e.
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
). The aim of this
paper is to contribute to this last type of articles.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2

presents the special functions that will be used throughout the paper, as
well as some of their basic properties. Section
3

proves the main hypergeometric identity on which most of the results
obtained in the article are based. In Section
4
we apply this hypergeometric identity to obtain reduction
formulas for particular arguments, while in Sections
5
and
6
we apply it to
arbitrary arguments. Finally, we collect our conclusions in Section
7
.

All the results presented in this paper have been tested with MATHEMATICA.
The corresponding MATHEMATICA notebook is available at
https://shorturl.at/tGOwb
.

Preliminaries

The gamma function is usually defined as
[
14
, Eqn. 1.1.1]

\Gamma\left(z\right):=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}t^{z-1}dt.\quad\mathrm{Re}z>0,

(1)

and satisfies the reflection formula
[
14
, Eqn. 1.2.2]

\Gamma\left(z\right)\,\Gamma\left(1-z\right)=\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi z},\quad z\notin\mathbb{Z}.

(2)

The logarithmic derivative of
\Gamma\left(z\right)
is defined as
[
14
, Eqn. 1.3.1]
:

\psi\left(z\right):=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}\left(z\right)}{\Gamma\left(z\right)},

(3)

and the Bateman’s
G
function is defined in terms of the
\psi\left(z\right)
function as
[
15
, Eqn. 44:13:1]
:

\beta\left(z\right):=\frac{1}{2}\left[\psi\left(\frac{z+1}{2}\right)-\psi\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\right].

(4)

The beta function is given by
[
15
, Eqn. 43:13:1-2]

\mathrm{B}\left(\alpha,\beta\right):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\alpha-1}\left(1-t\right)^{\beta-1}dt=\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)\,\Gamma\left(\beta\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha+\beta\right)},

(5)

and the incomplete beta function
[
16
, Eqn. 8.17.1]
:

\mathrm{B}_{z}\left(\alpha,\beta\right):=\int_{0}^{z}t^{\alpha-1}\left(1-t\right)^{\beta-1}dt.

(6)

The Pochhammer polynomial can be expressed in terms of gamma functions as

\left(\alpha\right)_{k}:=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(\alpha+j\right)=\frac{\Gamma\left(\alpha+k\right)}{\Gamma\left(\alpha\right)}.

(7)

Note that, according to
[
15
, Eqn. 18:5:1]

\left(-\alpha\right)_{k}=\left(-1\right)^{k}\left(\alpha-k+1\right)_{k},

(8)

thus for
m=0,1,2,\ldots
, we have

\left(-m\right)_{k}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{k}m!}{\left(m-k\right)!}.

(9)

Also, applying the reflection formula (
2
), we have

\left(\alpha\right)_{-n}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{\left(1-\alpha\right)_{n}}.

(10)

The generalized hypergeometric function is defined by the hypergeometric
series
[
16
, Eqn. 16.2.1]

{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}\end{array}\right|z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(a_{1}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(a_{p}\right)_{k}\,z^{k}}{k!\,\left(b_{1}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(b_{q}\right)_{k}},

(11)

wherever this series converges, and elsewhere by analytic continuation.
According to
[
16
, Eqn. 16.3.2]
, the following differentiation formula
is satisfied:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[z^{\gamma}{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}\end{array}\right|z\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\gamma-n+1\right)_{n}\,z^{\gamma-n}\,_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma+1,a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},\\
\gamma+1-n,b_{1},\ldots,b_{q},\end{array}\right|z\right),

thus

{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},a+n\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q},a\end{array}\right|z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{1-a}}{\left(a\right)_{n}}\,\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[z^{n+a-1}\,_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q}\end{array}\right|z\right)\right].

Also, the Chu-Vandermonde summation formula is
[
16
, Eqn. 15.4.24]
:

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}-n,a\\
c\end{array}\right|1\right)=\frac{\left(c-a\right)_{n}}{\left(c\right)_{n}}.

(24)

Further, according to
[
16
, Eqn. 15.8.1]
, we have the linear
transformation formula:

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a,b\\
c\end{array}\right|z\right)=\left(1-z\right)^{-a}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a,c-b\\
c\end{array}\right|\frac{z}{z-1}\right).

(25)

Finally, Leibniz’s differentiation formula
[
16
, Eqn. 1.4.2]
is given
by

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[f\left(z\right)\,g\left(z\right)\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\left[f\left(z\right)\right]\frac{d^{n-k}}{dz^{n-k}}\left[g\left(z\right)\right].

(26)

Lemma 26

For
m=0,1,2,\ldots
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the following derivative formula holds true:

\displaystyle\frac{d^{m}}{dz^{m}}\left(\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\left(1-z\right)^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{z^{\alpha-m}}{\left(1-z\right)^{\beta+m}}\left(\alpha-m+1\right)_{m}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}-m,1-m+\alpha-\beta\\

Apply Leibinz’s differentiation formula
[

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[f\left(z\right)\,g\left(z\right)\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{d^{k}}{dz^{k}}\left[f\left(z\right)\right]\,\frac{d^{n-k}}{dz^{n-k}}\left[g\left(z\right)\right],

and the

-th derivative formula
[

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[z^{\alpha}\right]=\left(-1\right)^{n}\left(-\alpha\right)^{n}z^{\alpha-n},

\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left[\left(1-z\right)^{\alpha}\right]=\left(-\alpha\right)^{n}\left(1-z\right)^{\alpha-n},

to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d^{m}}{dz^{m}}\left(\frac{z^{\alpha}}{\left(1-z\right)^{\beta}}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}\frac{d^{\ell}}{dz^{\ell}}\left[\left(1-z\right)^{-\beta}\right]\,\frac{d^{m-\ell}}{dz^{m-\ell}}\left[z^{\alpha}\right]

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m}z^{\alpha-m}}{\left(1-z\right)^{\beta}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}\left(\beta\right)_{\ell}\left(-\alpha\right)_{m-\ell}\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{\ell}.

Now, according to (

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}-m,\beta\\

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\binom{m}{\ell}\frac{\left(\beta\right)_{\ell}\,\left(-z\right)^{\ell}}{\left(1-m+\alpha\right)_{\ell}}.

However, from the definition of the Pochhammer symbol (

\left(-\alpha\right)_{m-\ell}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{m+\ell}\left(\alpha-m+1\right)_{m}}{\left(1-m+\alpha\right)_{\ell}},

7
Conclusions

Throughout the paper, we have systematically used the hypergeometric
identity (
2
), i.e.

{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},\ldots,a_{p},c+n\\
b_{1},\ldots,b_{q},c\end{array}\right|z\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{\,\left(a_{1}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(a_{p}\right)_{k}\,\,z^{k}}{\,\left(b_{1}\right)_{k}\cdots\left(b_{q}\right)_{k}\,}\,_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}+k,\ldots,\,a_{p}+k\\
b_{1}+k,\ldots,\,b_{q}+k\end{array}\right|z\right),

to obtain a set of summation formulas that do not seem to be reported in the
existing literature. In order to see how this method works, consider a
vector of parameters
\vec{\alpha}=\left(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}\right)
and adopt the notation,
\vec{\alpha}+k=\left(\alpha_{1}+k,\ldots,\alpha_{\ell}+k\right)
. If we know the reduction formula
of a generalized hypergeometric function of the form:

{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),\ldots,\,a_{p}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right)\\
b_{1}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),\ldots,\,b_{q}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right)\end{array}\right|z\right),

in such a way that
\forall i=1,\ldots,p
and
\forall j=1,\ldots,q
we
have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}a_{i}\left(\vec{\alpha}+k\right)=a_{i}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right)+k,\\
b_{j}\left(\vec{\alpha}+k\right)=b_{j}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right)+k,\end{array}\right.

then, according to (
7
), we obtain
\forall n=0,1,2,\ldots
a reduction formula for the generalized hypergeometric
function:

{}_{p+1}F_{q+1}\left(\left.\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),\ldots,\,a_{p}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),c+n\\
b_{1}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),\ldots,\,b_{q}\left(\vec{\alpha}\right),c\end{array}\right|z\right).

It is worth noting that during the development of the proofs, we have found
some interesting formulas such as the recursive formula (
60
) or the proof of the conjecture given in (
116
). In addition,
we have proved in (
71
) and (
91
) two finite sums
involving the psi function. Also, with the new reformulation given in (
20
) of the reduction formula given in the literature, i.e.
(
6
), we were able to obtain two new equivalent
reduction formulas in (
23
) and (
24
). Finally, for the special case given in (
27
), which is not included in (
23
), we have
developed a particular proof.

p53 expression and NLR could recognize distinct subtypes of lung adenocarcinoma with high expression of PD-L1 (TPS ≥ 50%), defined with 22C3 antibody
Authors: Ming Li, Mengqi Huang, Yuchen Huang, Xiaoliang Zhang, Haibo Wu, Zhenzhong Feng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) with high expression of PD-L1 (TPS ≥ 50%) is a distinct subset of NSCLC and a potential immunotherapy candidate. An improved understanding of the heterogeneity of PD-L1-high LUAD patients and the recognition of other biomarkers combined with PD-L1 could facilitate more precise use of PD-1 inhibitors in clinical practice.MethodsSurgically resected tumour tissues from 69 LUAD patients with a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50% defined by 22C3 antibody were tested for p53 and IFN-γ protein expression using immunohistochemistry. The densities of CD8 and Granzyme B-positive T cells were evaluated by immunohistochemistry. EGFR mutation was detected by RT‒PCR, ALK rearrangement was examined by a fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assay, and TP53 gene mutations were sequenced using Sanger sequencing.ResultsDifferences in clinicopathological features, including age and spread through the air space (STAS), were observed between patients with higher PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥ 50%, < 70%) and those with the highest PD-L1 expression (TPS ≥ 70%). LUAD patients whose PD-L1 TPS was ≥ 70% and whose p53 protein abnormal expression or NLR was < 5 had higher densities of CD8-positive tumour-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and Granzyme B-positive T cells as well as higher IFN-γ protein expression than those whose TPS was < 70%, those whose p53 protein expression was normal, or those whose NLR was ≥ 5.ConclusionsThe present study is the first to reveal that distinct subtypes of PD-L1-high LUAD can be identified on the basis of p53 protein expression and the NLR. p53 expression and the NLR can recognize a subtype with brisk immune cell cytotoxicity that might potentially respond better to PD-1 inhibitors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-026-05228-1.

Introduction

Lung cancer is the main cause of tumour-related death worldwide, and lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the predominant subtype of lung cancer [1, 2]. The identification of novel biomarkers, such as the EGFR inhibitor for LUAD, is crucial for the precise treatment of malignancies [3, 4]. The development of targeted therapeutics against driver oncogenes has improved the prognosis of non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients. However, few NSCLC patients do not harbour targetable mutations. Antitumour immunotherapy targeting immune checkpoints, such as programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1)/programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1), has become a successful therapeutic modality for NSCLC without oncogene mutation [5]. The expression of PD-L1 on tumour cells is currently considered an important predictive biomarker for treatment with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors. High expression of PD-L1, defined as a tumour proportion score (TPS) ≥ 50% when detected by 22C3 antibody, is associated with improved responses to PD-1 inhibitors in NSCLC [5, 6]. Nevertheless, a proportion of patients with high PD-L1 expression do not have better response to anti-PD-1 therapy [5, 6]. Therefore, PD-L1 positivity alone cannot be considered a rock-solid predictive factor for the response to PD-1/PD-L1 blockade in NSCLC [7–9]. It is of clinical relevance to recognize the subgroup of patients with PD-L1-high NSCLC, who would likely be better responders to PD-1 inhibitors. Thus, identifying other biomarkers beyond PD-L1 is essential.

Our study cohort included LUAD patients whose PD-L1 TPS was ≥ 50%, as defined by 22C3 antibody. The distinct subtypes could be recognized according to the expression pattern of p53 protein and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) in PD-L1-high LUAD. The density of CD8-positive TILs and Granzyme B (GZMB)-positive T cells as well as the protein expression level of IFN-γ, which are associated with the efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors, significantly differed among the distinct subtypes of patients with PD-L1-high LUAD. These results provide a new perspective for better understanding the potential heterogeneity of PD-L1-high LUAD. Our work is the first to reveal that p53 and the NLR could recognize a subset of patients with PD-L1-high LUAD with brisk immune cell cytotoxicity who might potentially respond better to PD-1 inhibitors. This will likely help facilitating more precise PD-1 inhibitor therapy in clinical practice.

Materials and methods

Patients and samples

In total, 69 patients included in the present study underwent curative-intent surgical resection of stage I-III LUAD in the Thoracic Surgery Department of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University and The First Affiliated Hospital of USTC (University of Science and Technology of China) between 2019 and 2021. The inclusion criteria were a pathological diagnosis of primary LUAD and a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50% detected with the 22C3 antibody and adequate formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) blocks with tumour tissue available for further evaluation. All patients had not received any treatment prior to surgery, including the use of corticosteroids, etc. LUAD patients with respiratory inflammatory disease were excluded from the study.

Clinical data were retrospectively collected from medical records. The median age of the 69 patients was 63 years (range: 28–79 years). The patients included 52.17% males and 47.83% females. 67% of the 69 patients were non-smokers. According to the 8th version of the Union for International Cancer Control (UICC) consortium TNM staging, the stages in the LUAD cohort were stage I (34.78%), stage II (33.33%) and stage III (31.89%). All tumour tissue sections were reviewed by two pathologists (ML and ZZF) and classified according to the WHO 2015 classification of LUAD [10]. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of The Second Affiliated Hospital of Anhui Medical University (Ethics Code: YX2025-092). All participants provided written informed consent prior to enrolment in the study, and this study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki.

Neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) measurement

The NLR was measured on the basis of laboratory test data. Laboratory test results of peripheral blood were obtained shortly before surgical resection; that is, a maximum of one day before surgical resection was included in the study. All laboratory tests, including blood collection, processing, and quantification, were performed according to the clinical standard operational protocol. The haematological data analysed were tested using the same testing platform and a unified standard testing method. The cut-off value of the NLR for subgroup analysis was 5 according to a previous study [11].

Immunohistochemical staining

All FFPE tumour samples were subjected to immunohistochemical staining using primary antibodies against CD8 (1:200; clone# SP16; GT211229; GeneTech, Inc., Washington, DC, USA), Granzyme B (1:100; clone# EP230; ZA-0599; ZSGB-BIO Co., Ltd., Beijing, China), IFN-γ (1:300; polyclonal antibody; cat# DF6045; Affinity Biosciences, Jiangsu, China) and p53 (1:100; clone# DO-07; IR616; Dako, CA, USA). Tissue Sect. (4 µM) were deparaffinized, rehydrated, and subjected to microwave antigen retrieval in EDTA buffer (pH = 9) for 20 min, followed by cooling at room temperature. After blocking with 3% hydrogen peroxide for 10 min, the slides were washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) for 15 min and incubated with primary antibodies for 12 h at 4 °C. Immunoreactivity was visualized using the DAB method (GK600705; Genetech, Inc.) after the slides were incubated with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-linked secondary antibodies. Slides stained with PBS instead of primary antibody were considered negative controls.

Detection of the EGFR, ALK and TP53 gene status

EGFR mutations were detected using the Cobas IVD method (Cobas EGFR test; Roche Molecular Systems, Inc., Branchburg, NJ, USA), and ALK rearrangement was examined by the ALK break-apart fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) assay. The Cobas EGFR mutation test can identify mutations in exons 18, 19, 20 and 21 of the EGFR gene, including Exon 19 deletion and Exon 21 L858R. FISH was performed using a break-apart probe (Vysis LSI ALK Dual Colour, break-apart rearrangement probe; Abbott Molecular, Abbott Park, IL, USA) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. FISH signals were observed using an Olympus BX61 fluorescence microscope, and the results were evaluated according to a previous study [12]. With respect to the TP53 gene, exons 1 and 3–8 were sequenced using Sanger sequencing.

Evaluation of immunohistochemical staining

Expression of PD-L1

The expression of PD-L1 in 69 patients was confirmed by two experienced pathologists (ML and ZZF). PD-L1 expression was determined as the percentage of tumour cells with positive membranous staining using 22C3 antibody.

Densities of CD8 + and granzyme B-positive T cells

To evaluate CD8 + T cells, tissue sections were first viewed at low power, and four independent areas containing the greatest abundance of CD8 + cells (termed hot spot areas) were selected. Three views were subsequently randomly selected from each hot spot area at a higher power. CD8 + T cells in the tumour island as well as the tumour stroma adjacent to the parenchyma were counted in 12 high-power fields (HPFs). The average number of CD8 + T cells in 12 high-power fields was calculated as the density of CD8 + T cells per HPF [13]. The density of Granzyme B-positive T cells was also assessed according to the abovementioned method.

Expression of IFN-γ protein

Immunohistochemistry was used to detect IFN-γ protein expression in a previous study, and the H score was used to evaluate its expression level in tumours [14]. The staining intensity score was graded according to four categories: 0 for no staining, 1 for light yellow staining visible only at high magnification, 2 for light brown staining visible at medium magnification, and 3 for dark brown staining that could be observed even at low magnification. The H score was determined by multiplying the percentage of positive cells by the intensity score.

Expression of p53 protein

The expression pattern of the p53 protein, which was characterized as diffusely strong positive or completely absent, was interpreted as abnormal/aberrant/mutation-type [15, 16]. The normal/wild-type expression pattern of p53 protein was characterized by an admixture of negative cells with weakly and strongly positive cells [15, 16]. The three expression patterns of p53 protein and their interpretation require the presence of a positive internal control with staining of lymphocytes, fibroblasts and other nonneoplastic cells. The immunohistochemical sections were reviewed by an additional pathologist when observing equivocal or rare expression patterns of p53 protein. After repeat detection and re-evaluation, the result was considered as equivocal or rare if the staining still remained unchanged.

Statistical analysis

Categorical data were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test when appropriate. Quantitative data are presented as the means ± SDs and were analysed using Student’s t test and ANOVA according to the data distribution. The Mann–Whitney U test was also used to analyse quantitative data when appropriate. A p value less than 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. All analyses were performed using the IBM SPSS software program (IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA), version 26.0.

Results

Baseline clinical and pathological characteristics in LUAD patients with higher expression (TPS ≥ 50%, < 70%) and the highest expression (TPS ≥ 70%) of PD-L1

Among the 69 patients, 37 (53.62%) had higher PD-L1 expression, and 32 (46.38%) had the highest PD-L1 expression. PD-L1 TPS ≥ 70% was observed more frequently in older patients (≥ 65 years old, P < 0.05) as well as in patients who did not have tumour cells that had spread through the air space (STAS) (P < 0.05). Compared with the lepidic and acinar subtypes, a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 70% seems to be more common in LUAD patients with the solid, papillary or micropapillary subtypes. Fifty-eight patients (84.06%) had no classic driver oncogene mutations, including those in EGFR and ALK. ALK fusion was more frequently observed than EGFR mutation in the 11 patients harbouring oncogene mutations (11.59% versus 4.35%), but the difference was not statistically significant. Oncogene status was not significantly different between LUAD patients with higher expression and those with the highest expression of PD-L1. Expression of p53 protein and TP53 gene aberrations in LUAD patients with higher and the highest expression of PD-L1

Reliable abnormal p53 protein expression was observed in 31 patients. All 31 patients had TP53 gene mutations according to Sanger sequencing. Equivocal immunohistochemical results were found in one case. The equivocal mutation was confirmed to be a TP53 mutation through gene sequencing and was ultimately defined as abnormal expression of the p53 protein. All 37 patients who had normal p53 protein expression did not harbour TP53 gene mutations according to gene sequencing. The concordance between abnormal p53 protein expression and TP53 gene mutation was nearly 100%. Abnormal p53 protein expression was detected in 13.51% of the 37 patients whose PD-L1 expression was < 70%. It was more frequently observed in the PD-L1 ≥ 70% LUAD group (84.38%) than in the PD-L1 < 70% group (P < 0.001; Figs. 1 and 2).

Densities of CD8-positive TILs and GZMB-positive T cells in LUAD patients with higher and the highest expression of PD-L1

The average number of CD8-positive TILs was 24 ± 15/HPF (range: 0–74/HPF) in the PD-L1 TPS < 70% group and 57 ± 19/HPF (range: 0–80/HPF) in the PD-L1 TPS ≥ 70% group. A significant difference in the number of CD8 + TILs was observed between LUAD patients with a TPS < 70% and those with a TPS ≥ 70% (P < 0.001; Figs. 3A and 4-a and b). The average number of GZMB-positive T cells was 18 ± 26/HPF (range: 0–117/HPF) in the TPS < 70% group and 52 ± 44/HPF (range: 0–148/HPF) in the TPS ≥ 70% group. LUAD patients whose PD-L1 TPS was ≥ 70% had significantly more GZMB-positive T cells than those whose TPS was < 70% (P < 0.001; Figs. 3A and 5).

Densities of CD8-positive TILs and GZMB-positive T cells in PD-L1-high LUAD with or without mutation-type expression of p53 protein

The average density of CD8-positive TILs was 24 ± 14/HPF (range: 0–65/HPF) in LUAD patients with normal p53 protein expression. It was 57 ± 19/HPF (range: 0–80/HPF) in LUAD patients with mutation-type expression of the p53 protein. The density of CD8 + TILs was significantly greater in the group with abnormal p53 expression than in the group with normal p53 expression (P < 0.001; Figs. 3B and 4-c and d). The average density of GZMB + T cells was 60 ± 43/HPF (range: 0–148/HPF) in LUAD patients with abnormal p53 protein expression and 11 ± 14/HPF (range: 0–66/HPF) in the normal expression group. Significant differences in GZMB + T-cell density were observed between the two subtypes according to p53 protein expression (P < 0.001; Figs. 3B and 5).

Densities of CD8-positive TILs and GZMB-positive T cells in PD-L1-high LUAD patients with an NLR < 5 or ≥ 5

The average density of CD8 + TILs was 51 ± 23/HPF (range: 0–80/HPF) in LUAD patients with an NLR < 5 and 27 ± 16/HPF (range: 1–74/HPF) in LUAD patients with an NLR ≥ 5. The density of CD8 + TILs was significantly greater in the NLR < 5 subgroup than in the NLR ≥ 5 subgroup (P < 0.001; Figs. 3C and 4-e and f). The average number of GZMB + T cells was 45 ± 41/HPF (range: 0–148/HPF) in patients with an NLR < 5 and 22 ± 34/HPF (range: 0–133/HPF) in patients with an NLR ≥ 5. Compared with the NLR ≥ 5 subgroup, the PD-L1-high LUAD subgroup had significantly more GZMB + T cells when the NLR was < 5 (P < 0.05; Figs. 3C and 5-e and f).

The expression of IFN-γ protein in PD-L1-high LUAD patients according to the PD-L1 TPS, p53 protein expression and NLR

The IFN-γ protein was reliably detected in tumour tissues. The average H score of IFN-γ protein was 68 ± 80 (range: 0–270) in the PD-L1 TPS < 70% subgroup and 137 ± 85 (range: 0–265) in the TPS ≥ 70% subgroup. The protein expression level of IFN-γ was significantly greater in the TPS ≥ 70% subgroup than in the TPS < 70% subgroup (P < 0.01; Fig. 6). The average H score was 68 ± 55 (range: 0–270) in patients with mutation-type p53 protein expression. However, it was only 11 ± 14 (range: 0–66) in patients with normal p53 protein expression. Compared with that in the normal expression subset, the expression of IFN-γ in the abnormal p53 protein subset was significantly greater (P < 0.001; Fig. 6). The mean score was 121 ± 93 (range: 2–270) in patients with an NLR < 5. The expression score of IFN-γ in patients with an NLR < 5 was significantly greater than that in patients with an NLR ≥ 5 (78 ± 80; range: 0–263) (P < 0.05; Fig. 6).

Discussion

A PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50% is not more frequently observed in primary LUAD [17]. In clinical practice, PD-L1 expression is not an entirely reliable predictive marker for the efficacy of immunotherapy because the response rate to PD-1 inhibitors in advanced NSCLC patients with strongly positive PD-L1 expression was only 44.8% according to the findings of KEYNOTE-024 and other studies [5, 8, 9]. Previous studies including patients with heterogeneous histological subtypes and different PD-L1 expression levels have explored other biomarkers in addition to PD-L1. All 69 patients in our study underwent surgical resection for LUAD with high expression of PD-L1. Our work is the first to depict the tumour immune microenvironment of different subtypes, defined as p53 and NLR, in PD-L1-high LUAD. p53 and the NLR can recognize a subset of patients with a brisk immune microenvironment in PD-L1-high LUAD, suggesting a potential response to immunotherapy. Our data suggested that there is potential heterogeneity in PD-L1-high LUAD on the basis of p53 expression and the NLR. This finding may underlie clinical observations that not all patients with high PD-L1 expression in LUAD respond better to PD-1 inhibitors [5, 8, 9].

Few studies have explored whether PD-L1-high LUAD can be further classified into PD-L1-higher and PD-L1-highest subsets, and the cut-off for PD-L1 expression was not consistent across different studies [11, 18]. TPS 70% was used as a cut-off for PD-L1 in our work according to Frost’s study [18]. Compared with patients with higher PD-L1 expression, the highest expression of PD-L1 (TPS ≥ 70%) was more frequently observed in older patients (P < 0.05). In addition, a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 70% was more often found in tumours without STAS (P < 0.05). These results suggested that patients with a TPS ≥ 70% or a TPS < 70% could be considered distinct subtypes of PD-L1-high LUAD according to their clinicopathological characteristics.

A growing body of evidence suggests that the response to PD-1 inhibitors is predominant in NSCLC with a brisk tumour immune microenvironment, such as that with a high density of CD8-positive TILs [7, 19–21]. Moreover, antitumour effector T cells are referred to as granzyme B (GZMB)-positive T cells and have been proven to be reliable predictors of a better response to anti-PD-1 blockade [22]. Our data revealed that the densities of CD8 + and GZMB + T cells were significantly greater in tumours with a PD-L1 expression ≥ 70% than in those with a PD-L1 expression < 70%. PD-L1 ≥ 70% and PD-L1 < 70% LUAD could be considered different subtypes on the basis of the tumour immune microenvironment.

TP53 is the most commonly mutated gene in NSCLC [23]. The expression pattern of the p53 protein has been shown to be an accurate surrogate reflecting the underlying TP53 mutation status in human malignancies [24, 25]. The concordance between p53 protein expression and TP53 gene status in our work was nearly 100%. Mutation-type p53 protein expression was more frequently observed in patients with PD-L1 expression ≥ 70% than in patients with PD-L1 expression < 70% in LUAD (p < 0.001). In addition, there was no significant difference in other driver oncogene mutations between patients with a PD-L1 expression ≥ 70% and those with a PD-L1 expression < 70%, including EGFR and ALK mutations. Taken together, these findings further supported that LUAD with a TPS ≥ 70% or a TPS < 70% could be regarded as different subsets according to the expression pattern of the p53 protein.

A strong association has already been revealed between a high density of CD8 + TILs and the TP53 mutation in NSCLC [7, 26]. Biton’s work revealed that GZMB-positive T cells were more abundant in the TP53-mutated LUAD group than in the wild-type group. However, the abundance of GZMB-positive T cells was indirectly evaluated by detecting GZMB gene expression in tumour tissues [26]. Considering the density of GZMB-positive cells as a potential predictor of immune cell cytotoxicity was more accurate. In addition, the subgroup with high expression of PD-L1 was not evaluated in Biton’s study.

Our work included only PD-L1-high LUAD patients. Both CD8 + T cells and effector T cells were more robust in PD-L1-high LUAD patients with mutation-type p53 protein expression than in patients with normal p53 expression. These findings suggested that the expression pattern of the p53 protein could be used to recognize subtypes with different immune microenvironments in PD-L1-high LUAD. As an easily evaluated biomarker in routine clinical practice, p53 could recognize a subset with brisk immune cell cytotoxicity and might be a potential better responder to PD-1 inhibitors.

The neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) is regarded as a surrogate for tumour-associated inflammation, and previous results have suggested that the NLR likely represents the activity of myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), which suppress T-cell proliferation and expansion [27]. These findings indicate that increased accumulation of neutrophils suppresses T-cell activity in lung cancer [28]. Thus, it was concluded that the baseline NLR is likely a potential predictor of immunotherapy efficacy. Banna’s work suggested that the appropriate NLR cut-off for advanced NSCLC patients treated with PD-1 inhibitors was 5 [11]. Advanced PD-L1-high NSCLC with an NLR < 5 is associated with favourable outcomes following pembrolizumab treatment [11], but the mechanisms of these findings are still unclear. A higher density of CD8 + and GZMB-positive T cells was observed in PD-L1-high LUAD patients with an NLR < 5. The NLR < 5 in the PD-L1-high LUAD subtype indicated a brisk immune microenvironment, which may suggest potential responsiveness to PD-1 inhibitors. Although the exact predictive value of the NLR was not clear, our work is the first to reveal that the NLR can be used to recognize a subset with a distinct immune microenvironment in PD-L1-high LUAD. This information will aid in the identification of potentially better responders to PD-1 inhibitors. In addition, the NLR can be measured in peripheral blood by minimally invasive approaches. Our results suggest that the NLR might be a potential easy-to-detect biomarker that is useful for guiding neoadjuvant PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor therapy in early-stage LUAD patients, particularly when appropriate tumour tissue is unavailable.

Previous studies detected the expression of either the gene encoding IFN-γ (IFNG) or IFN-γ downstream genes in NSCLC as a surrogate for the abundance of IFN-γ protein [29, 30]. Compared with patients with low expression, those with high expression of IFNG mRNA benefit more from nivolumab [29]. In addition, IFN-γ signalling deficiency in tumour cells could serve as a mechanism of resistance to PD-1 inhibitors [31–33]. The protein expression of IFN-γ has been detected by immunohistochemistry in melanoma [14]. Our work also evaluated the protein expression of IFN-γ by immunohistochemistry in PD-L1-high LUAD. Increased IFN-γ protein expression was more frequently observed in LUAD patients with a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 70%, an NLR < 5 or abnormal p53 protein expression (p < 0.001). It was suggested that the p53/NLR could recognize not only subtypes with different immune microenvironments but also subtypes with different levels of cytokine expression in PD-L1-high LUAD, which may indicate potential responsiveness to PD-1 inhibitors.

To strengthen the understanding of the association between p53/NLR and tumour immune microenvironment characteristics, the density of CD8/GZMB-positive T cells and IFN-γ expression were compared between patients with and without a smoking history in the subgroups according to PD-L1 expression, p53 protein expression and the NLR. The results suggested that there was no significant difference in tumour microenvironment characteristics among patients with and without a smoking history defined by PD-L1/p53/NLR separately. These findings suggested that smoking history in our cohort did not influence the association between PD-L1/p53/NLR and tumour microenvironment characteristics (the data are shown in Table S1). Moreover, several physiological and psychological stressors prior to surgery cannot be ruled out, which may suggest potential confounders to NLR. It should be acknowledged as a possible limitation of our work. Future studies would incorporate validated tools for assessing stress, anxiety, and quality of life to better understand impact of NLR on the immune microenvironment.

Currently, studies on the subclassification of LUAD with a PD-L1 TPS ≥ 50% and its clinical relevance are rare. The present study is the first to raise the possibility that distinct subtypes could be recognized in PD-L1-high LUAD according to p53 expression and the NLR. Although our exploratory work is not a direct predictive study, our results could characterize the immune microenvironment of different subsets, defined as the p53/NLR in PD-L1-high LUAD patients, suggesting a potential response to immunotherapy. These findings provide practical approaches to help facilitate more precise treatment with PD-1 inhibitors. Future studies with comprehensive prognostic and therapeutic efficacy data are needed to validate these results. In addition, the sample size in this study is relatively small, but several significant differences between subtypes defined by p53/NLR was still observed, suggesting that the main results remain reasonably robust. Future research would include larger samples to enhance the generalizability of the findings.
